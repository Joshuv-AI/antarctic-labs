Constrained Kolmogorov widths
Ronald DeVore, Guergana Petrova, Jonathan W. Siegel, Przemysław Wojtaszczyk

Abstract
The main theme of approximation theory is to understand how well a general function f f can be approximated by a simpler function g g such as a polynomial or spline. In many applications, one wants g g to retain known properties of f f such as its inherent smoothness or a geometrical property such as monotonicity or convexity. Additional requirements on g g of this type are known as constraints. In this paper, we do a systematic study of constrained approximation to understand how the imposition of such constraints limits the efficiency of the approximation. We study constrained approximation in the setting of linear approximation where g g is to be taken from a finite dimensional linear space V V of a fixed dimension n n . Kolmogorov widths describe how well one can approximate when using such linear spaces V V . The first part of this paper introduces and studies several types of constrained widths, including the constrained Kolmogorov widths, and gives comparisons between them. The second part of the paper is restricted to classical settings where the constraint imposes a smoothness requirement on g g . In this case, our results prove that the additional constraint can typically be imposed with no loss in the efficiency of the approximation.



1
Introduction

Kolmogorov widths are a measure of how well we can approximate the elements of a compact set
K
when using linear spaces
V
of a fixed dimension
n\geq 0
. There are numerous generalizations of these widths to other settings of approximation (see e.g.
[
38
,
41
]
). Two prominent examples are linear widths and Gelfand widths. We also have the notions of manifold widths
[
13
]
, stable manifold widths
[
12
]
, and Lipschitz widths
[
37
]
, which replace the linear spaces
V
by more general sets. The theory of
n
widths is important in application domains such as numerical analysis and learning theory since they provide a benchmark for the optimal performance of numerical algorithms in the corresponding application domain.

The present paper is concerned with another type of width, called the
constrained Kolmogorov width
, where the approximation is again required to come from a finite
n
dimensional linear space
V
, but the approximation is subjected to an additional constraint whose exact form depends on the targeted application domain of the approximation process such as numerical PDEs, learning, geometric design, etc.

1.1
Kolmogorov, Gelfand and linear widths

Before formally defining the constrained Kolmogorov widths, we first recall the definition of the standard Kolmogorov widths. Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. We denote the collection of all compact subsets of
X
by

{\cal K}:={\cal K}(X):=\{K:\ K\subset X\ {\rm is\ compact}\},

(1.1)

and by

{\cal K}_{p}\subset{\cal K}(X)

the collection of those subsets of
{\cal K}(X)
that are convex and centrally symmetric about the origin.
Given a compact set
K\in{\cal K}(X)
, the
n
-th Kolmogorov width of
K
is given by

d_{0}(K)_{X}:=\sup_{f\in K}\,\,\|f\|_{X},\quad d_{n}(K)_{X}:=\inf_{\dim(V)\leq n}\,\sup_{f\in K}\,\,\inf_{v\in V}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.2)

where the infimum is taken over all
n
dimensional linear subspaces
V
of
X
. In other words, the Kolmogorov width describes how well the elements in
K
can be approximated by an optimally chosen
n
dimensional
space
V\subset X
. If a subspace
V
of
X
with dimension
n
satisfies

\mathop{\rm dist}(K,V):=\sup_{f\in K}\,\,\inf_{v\in V}\|f-v\|_{X}=d_{n}(K)_{X},

(1.3)

we call
V
a
Kolmogorov subspace
for
K
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. Such a space
V
does not always exist, but for each
\varepsilon>0
there is always a space
V
whose distance to
K
is at most
d_{n}(K)_{X}+\varepsilon
. In this case, we say
V
is an
\varepsilon
-Kolmogorov space.

Remark 1.1
.

When establishing bounds for Kolmogorov widths or the constrained Kolmogorov widths introduced below, it is easier to prove these bounds under the assumption that these widths are attained by an
n
dimensional space
V
. In the absence of such knowledge, one uses spaces
V
that approximate within
\varepsilon>0
of the width, and then takes a limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We want to avoid the constant repetition of this
\varepsilon
argument and so in the proofs given below we assume the existence of a Kolmogorov space. The reader can easily substitute the
\varepsilon
argument to handle the general case. When it is not clear how the
\varepsilon
argument works we provide details.

Two other important widths which we will mention and use later are the
linear
and
Gelfand widths
.
For a compact set
K\subset X
, the linear widths are defined by

\delta_{n}(K)_{X}:=\inf_{A_{n}}\sup_{f\in K}\|f-A_{n}f\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.4)

where the infimum is taken over all linear continuous operators
A_{n}:X\rightarrow X
of rank at most
n
. The linear widths measure how well the elements of
K
can be approximated by a rank (at most)
n
linear map.

The Gelfand widths of a set
K\in{\cal K}_{p}(X)
is defined by

d^{n}(K)_{X}:=\inf_{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in X^{*}}\sup\{\|f\|_{X}:~f\in K,~\lambda_{i}(f)=0,\ i=1,\dots,n\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.5)

where the infimum is taken over any
n
continuous linear functionals from the dual space
X^{*}
.
The Gelfand widths measure how accurately elements of
K
can be recovered from
n
linear measurements
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in X^{*}
. It is easy to see that

d_{n}(K)_{X}\leq\delta_{n}(K)_{X}\quad{\rm and}\quad d^{n}(K)_{X}\leq\delta_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

A significant chapter of approximation theory has been devoted to determining the Kolmogorov, Gelfand, and linear widths of a specified
K\in{\cal K}
and specified Banach space
X
, or at least to determine how fast these widths tend to zero as
n\to\infty
. The set
K
is commonly referred to as a
model class
since it serves as an assumption about the functions
f\in X
that we wish to approximate. A simple example to keep in mind is
the case when
X=C([0,1])
is the space of continuous functions on
\Omega=[0,1]
with the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and the set
K=U({\rm Lip}\ \alpha)
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, is the unit ball of
{\rm Lip}\ \alpha
. This is a compact subset of
X
and its Kolmogorov
n
widths satisfy
d_{n}(K)_{X}\asymp n^{-\alpha}
,
n\geq 1
, with constants of equivalency independent of both
n
and
\alpha
.

1.2
Constrained Kolmogorov widths

Some applications may require more restrictions on the approximating functions than the requirement that they come from a finite dimensional linear space
V\subset X
. Let us denote by
{\cal C}\subset X
a subset of
X
, which we refer to as the
constraining class
. In addition to requiring that the approximating functions come from an
n
dimensional space
V
, we may also want to require that these approximants come from the class
{\cal C}
. This setting is referred to as constrained approximation. The most common constraints relate to some geometric property such as positivity, monotonicity, or convexity.
Another common constraint is simply the requirement that the approximating function be in the model class
K
, in which case we have
{\cal C}=K
. This latter example is
important in learning theory (see
[
7
]
).

Formally, we define the
constrained Kolmogorov widths
of
K
to be

d_{0}(K,{\cal C})_{X}:=\sup_{f\in K}\|f\|_{X},\quad d_{n}(K,{\cal C})_{X}:=\inf_{\dim(V)\leq n}\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V\cap{\cal C}}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.6)

Note that we obviously have

d_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}(K,{\cal C})_{X},

(1.7)

for any
{\cal C}\subset X
.
The constrained Kolmogorov widths (
1.6
) were first introduced in
[
29
]
, where they were called relative widths. It was shown in
[
29
]
that the constrained Kolmogorov widths can be substantially different from the standard Kolmogorov widths for certain classical smoothness classes
K
. Different choices for
{\cal C}
yield different constrained Komogorov widths.

In this paper, we are particularly interested in a special class of constrained widths that arises in learning theory (see
[
7
]
), where one wants the approximant to
be in
K
, or at least be close to being in
K
. Our first example of such a width is

d^{c}_{n}(K)_{X}:=d_{n}(K,K)_{X}:=\inf_{\dim(V)\leq n}\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V\cap K}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.8)

This width corresponds to taking
{\cal C}=K
. For example, if
K
were the unit ball of Lip
1
, we would want the approximating functions to also have this property.

This constrained width is quite demanding and we would like to relax this constraint. To this end, for each real number
\gamma\geq 1
, we introduce the sets

K_{\gamma}:={\displaystyle\bigcup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\gamma}\lambda K}.

(1.9)

Note that when
K
is convex and centrally symmetric, we have
\gamma K=K_{\gamma}
. We define the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov width

d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}:=d_{n}(K,K_{\gamma})_{X}:=\inf_{\dim(V)\leq n}\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V\cap K_{\gamma}}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.10)

When
\gamma=1
, we note that for centrally symmetric convex sets
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
we have
K_{1}=K
, and hence

d^{c}_{n}(K)_{X}=d^{c,1}_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\geq 1.

(1.11)

The widths
d_{n}^{c}
and
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}
have been studied by numerous authors
[
3
,
30
,
34
,
40
]
for particular cases of Banach spaces X.

We next introduce some further widths that are closely related to the concept of constrained approximation.

1.3
Greedy Kolmogorov widths

We define the
greedy Kolmogorov widths
of
K
as

d_{0}^{g}(K)_{X}:=\sup_{f\in K}\,\|f\|_{X},\quad d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}:=\inf_{V={\rm span}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\},f_{i}\in K}\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(1.12)

where the
n
dimensional spaces
V
allowed in the competition are spanned by elements of
K
. This width has been indirectly studied in the context of the construction of greedy bases which have, in turn, been used in the study of reduced modeling (see
[
8
,
6
,
16
]
). In some settings, it is known that the decay rate of
d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}
,
n\to\infty
, is comparable to the decay rate for
d_{n}(K)_{X}
. For example, in the case when
X
is a Hilbert space, if we know that if for some
\alpha>0
, we have
d_{n}(K)_{X}\leq Mn^{-\alpha}
,
n\geq 1
, then it follows that
d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq C(\alpha)Mn^{-\alpha}
,
n\geq 1
(see
[
6
]
).

Notice that the knowledge of
d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}
does not provide us with any direct information about
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}
or
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
since we do not know that the approximants are in
K_{\gamma}
. On the other hand, if

K
is convex and centrally symmetric, then the approximant will be in
K_{\gamma}
if we can provide a bound on the coefficients in the approximation. This approach of bounding coefficients will be studied later in this paper.

1.4
Restricted Kolmogorov widths

Note that one can view
K
not only as a subset of
X
, but also as a subset of other ambient spaces.
For example, given a compact set
K
, let
{\cal X}:={\cal X}(K)
be the closure of the finite linear span
S(K)
of
K
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. We equip
{\cal X}
with the norm in
X
. That is,
\|g\|_{{\cal X}}:=\|g\|_{X}
when
g\in{\cal X}
. Then,
{\cal X}(K)\subset X
is the smallest subspace of
X
which contains
K
. With this notation in hand, we define the
restricted Kolmogorov width
of
K
to be

d_{0}^{r}(K)_{X}:=\sup_{f\in K}\,\|f\|_{X},\quad d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}:=d_{n}(K)_{{\cal X}}=\inf_{V\subset{\cal X},{\rm dim}(V)\leq n}\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V}\|f-v\|_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.13)

This restricted width has been studied in other contexts (see

[
23
,
36
,
35
]
). Note that
the restricted width is a constrained width, namely, it can also be defined by

d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}:=d_{n}(K,{\cal X}(K))_{X}.

The reader should notice that in both the greedy and constrained widths, the norm in which the error of approximation is measured continues to be
\|\cdot\|_{X}
.
The added property imposed in these widths is to limit the
n
dimensional spaces
V
which can be used to do the approximation. In greedy widths, these
n
dimensional spaces are required to be spanned by the elements of
K
, whereas in the restricted widths the spaces
V
must be spanned by elements of
{\cal X}(K)
. It follows therefore that

d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.14)

One may expect that these two widths are the same.
This is indeed the case
for
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, that is
for convex, centrally symmetric sets, as shown in §
4
.

1.5
The goals of this paper

By now, we have presented several different types of widths.
Our main interest in this paper are the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
, while the greedy and restricted Kolmogorov widths,
d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}
and
d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}
, have been introduced as tools to better understand
the behavior of
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
.
The goal of this paper is to investigate the asymptotic behavior of these constrained widths of a compact set
K
as
n\to\infty
. In particular, we want to understand
if and when the addition of the constraint denegrates the rate of approximation.

We obtain results of two flavors. The first type of results are statements for constrained or
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths that hold for any compact set
K
or for any convex centrally symmetric compact set
K
and any Banach space
X
. In this generality, we shall see that there may be a severe loss in the rate of constrained approximation. Result of this type are described and discussed in §2-§4.

The second set of results we obtain (starting in §5) apply to specific classical compact sets
K
and particular Banach spaces
X
such as the
L_{p}
spaces. The compact sets
K
that
arise in classical settings of approximation theory studied in this paper are Besov classes and approximation classes. Our main results for these classical settings will show that
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths have the same asymptotic rate of decay as the Kolmogorov widths.

2
Some properties of Kolmogorov widths

We start this section with the observation that the Kolmogorov widths of a set
K
and its convexification
\widetilde{K}
are the same. More precisely,
for a given a compact set
K\in X
, we denote its symmetrized convex hull by

\widetilde{K}:=\overline{{\rm conv}(-K\cup K)},

(2.1)

where the closure is taken with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{X}
.
It is easy to see that, (see Chapter 13,
[
33
]
),

d_{n}(K)_{X}=d_{n}(\widetilde{K})_{X},\quad n\geq 0.

(2.2)

Indeed, since
K\subset\widetilde{K}
we have that that
d_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}(\widetilde{K})_{X}
. The reverse inequality
holds because whenever a linear space
V
approximates a set
K
to a given accuracy then it approximates

-K\cup K
to the same accuracy. In turn, it approximates the convex hull of
-K\cup K
and thus its closure to the same accuracy.

Note that an equality of this type does not generally hold for the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths. However, it is easy to check that for any compact set
K
we have

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(\widetilde{K})_{X}\leq d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X},\quad\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.3)

We next prove the following theorem for the Kolmogorov widths which we will use later for particular choices of Banach spaces
Y
and
Z
. We consider any Banach space
Z
and any subspace
Y
of
Z
.
Note that this assumption means that
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
is the restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{Z}
onto
Y
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every Banach space
Z
, every subspace
Y
of
Z
, and any compact set
K\in{\cal K}(Y)
,
we have that

d_{n}(K)_{Z}\leq d_{n}(K)_{Y}\leq(1+\sqrt{n})d_{n}(K)_{Z},\quad n\geq 0.

(2.4)

If
Z
is a Hilbert space, then

d_{n}(K)_{Y}=d_{n}(K)_{Z},\quad n\geq 0.

(2.5)

Proof:
Notice that the difference in these two Kolmogorov widths is that
d_{n}(K)_{Z}
allows the
n
dimensional subspaces
V
to come from the larger Banach space
Z
, while
d_{n}(K)_{Y}
requires them to come from the smaller space
Y
. Therefore, the first inequality in (
2.4
) follows trivially

To show the second inequality in (
2.4
), we fix
n
and let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. We
choose
Z_{n}\subset Z
, with
\dim Z_{n}=n
, such that

\sup_{f\in K}\inf_{g\in Z_{n}}\|f-g\|_{Z}\leq d_{n}(K)_{Z}+\varepsilon.

Let us define

\tilde{Z}:=Y+Z_{n}.

Clearly
Y
is a subspace of
\tilde{Z}
of codimension
\leq n
. It follows that for each
\delta>0
, there exists a linear projector
Q_{\delta}:\tilde{Z}\to Y
onto
Y
such that (see Theorem 8 in
[
21
]
),

\|Q_{\delta}\|\leq(1+\sqrt{n}+\delta).

(2.6)

We now consider the linear space
Y_{n}:=Q_{\delta}(Z_{n})\subset X
which has dimension at most
n
.

If
f\in K
, let
g(f)\in Z_{n}
be such that

\|f-g(f)\|_{Y}=\|f-g(f)\|_{Z}\leq d_{n}(K)_{Z}+\varepsilon.

Then
g^{*}(f):=Q_{\delta}(g(f))\in Y_{n}
, and we have

\|f-g^{*}(f)\|_{Y}=\|Q_{\delta}(f-g(f))\|_{Y}\leq\|Q_{\delta}\|\|f-g(f)\|_{Z}\leq(1+\delta+\sqrt{n})(d_{n}(K)_{Z}+\varepsilon).

(2.7)

It follows that

d_{n}(K)_{Y}\leq(1+\sqrt{n}+\delta)(d_{n}(K)_{Z}+\epsilon).

(2.8)

Letting
\epsilon,\delta\to 0
gives (
2.4
).
When
Z
is a Hilbert space the projection from
\tilde{Z}
onto
Y
can be chosen of norm one and therefore (
2.5
) follows. This completes the proof of the theorem.

\Box

3
Elementary properties and comparisons between constrained,
\gamma
-constrained, greedy, and restricted Kolmogorov widths

In this section, we will describe some elementary properties of the constrained Kolmogorov widths. We start with recalling the notion of entropy numbers
\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{X}
of a compact set
K
. Given a
g\in X
and
r>0
, we let
B(g,r):=B(g,r)_{X}
denote the closed ball in
X
of radius
r
centered at
g
.
For every fixed
n\geq 0
, the entropy number
\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{X}
is the infimum over all

\varepsilon>0
for which
2^{n}
balls
with centers from
X
and radius
\varepsilon
cover
K
.

\epsilon_{n}(K)_{X}:=\inf\{\epsilon>0:\,K\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{n}}B(g_{j},\varepsilon),\,g_{j}\in X,\quad j=1,...,2^{n}\}.

(3.1)

In some applications, one wants the centers of the covering of
K
to come from
K
itself. This leads to the definition of the inner entropy numbers

\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(K)_{X}:=\inf\{\epsilon>0:\,K\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{n}}B(f_{j},\varepsilon),\,f_{j}\in K,\,j=1,...,2^{n}\}.

(3.2)

An elementary comparison between these to notions of entropy is (see
[
10
]
),

\epsilon_{n}(K)_{X}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq 2\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{X}.

(3.3)

The following lemma provides some elementary properties and comparisons between the widths we have introduced.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
X
is any Banach space and
K\in{\cal K}(X)
, then the following elementary properties hold for
n=0,1,\dots
.

(i)
If
L\subset K\subset\lambda L
, with
K,L\in{\cal K}_{p}(X)
, and
\lambda\geq 1
, then
d^{c,\lambda\gamma}_{n}(L)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
for all
\gamma>0
.
Secondly, for any
\alpha,\gamma
, we have

d^{c,\alpha\gamma}_{n}(\alpha K)_{X}=\alpha d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
.

(ii)

For any
1\leq\gamma\leq\delta
, we have

d^{c,\delta}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}
.

(iii)
For every
\gamma\geq 1
, we have
d^{c,\gamma}_{n+1}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}
and
d^{c}_{n+1}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c}_{n}(K)_{X}
.

(iv)
For any
\gamma\geq 1
, we have

d_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c,1}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq d^{c}_{n}(K)_{X}.

(v)
We have
d^{c}_{2^{n}}(K)_{X}\leq 2\epsilon_{n}(K)_{X}.

(vi)
We have
d^{c}_{n}(K)_{X}\to 0
, as
n\to\infty
.

Proof:
For the proof of the first statement in (i), let
\delta_{n}:=d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
. We assume that there is an
n
-dimensional space
X_{n}
that
realizes
\delta_{n}
; otherwise one should use the
\varepsilon
-argument of Remark
1.1
. If
f\in L
, then
f\in K
and so there is a
g\in X_{n}
such that
\|f-g\|_{X}\leq\delta_{n}
and
g\in\gamma K
.
It follows that
g\in\lambda\gamma L
and so
d_{n}^{c,\lambda\gamma}(L)_{X}\leq\delta_{n}
as desired. Similarly, for the second statement of (i), let
V
again attain
\delta_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
. If
f\in\alpha K
then
f=\alpha f_{0}
with
f_{0}\in K
. Therefore, there is a
g_{0}\in V
that approximates
f_{0}
to accuracy
\delta_{n}
and
g_{0}\in K_{\gamma}=\gamma K
. Hence,
g:=\alpha g_{0}
is in
V
and approximates
f
to the accuracy
\alpha\delta_{n}
. Moreover,
g\in\alpha\gamma K
. This gives the inequality
d_{n}^{c,\alpha\gamma}(\alpha K)_{X}\leq\alpha d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)
.
If we reverse the roles of
K
and
\alpha K
, we obtain
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}\leq\alpha^{-1}d_{n}^{c,\alpha\gamma}(\alpha K)_{X}
, as desired.

The monotonicity claimed in (ii) follows from the fact that
K_{\gamma}\subset K_{\delta}
, when
\gamma\leq\delta
. Claim (iii) is obvious.

We now turn to the proof of (iv). The first inequality in (iv) follows from the definition of the restricted width. The next inequality was given in (
1.14
).
We next prove that

d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X},\quad\gamma\geq 1,

(3.4)

To prove this, we fix
\gamma\geq 1
and note that
for any
n
dimensional subspace
V\subset X
, we have

V\cap K_{\gamma}\subset{\rm span}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\},

for some
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in K
, and therefore

d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}\leq\sup_{f\in K}\inf_{g\in V\cap K_{\gamma}}\|f-g\|_{X}.

(3.5)

Taking an infimum over all
n
dimensional spaces
V
, we arrive at

d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X},

which is (
3.4
). Next, note that
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
is a monotone decreasing function of
\gamma
, and so we have

d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}\leq\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X},

which is the third inequality in (iv). The remainder of inequalities in (iv) are obvious.

To prove (v),
let us fix
n
and take any
\varepsilon>\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
. Let
f_{j}\in K
,
j=1,\ldots,2^{n}
, be the centers of balls
B(f_{j},\varepsilon)
that cover
K
. Clearly, if
V_{2^{n}}:={\rm span}\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}
, then every
f\in K
can be approximated by at least one of the
f_{j}
’s up to accuracy
\varepsilon
. Therefore, we have

d^{c}_{2^{n}}(K)_{X}\leq\varepsilon
.
Since
\epsilon
can be taken arbitrarily close to
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
, using (
3.3
), we have

d^{c}_{2^{n}}(K)_{X}\leq\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq 2\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{X}.

To prove (vi), we note that
for compact sets
K
we have
\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{X}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, from (v), we know that
d^{c}_{2^{n}}(K)_{X}\to 0
as

n\to\infty
. The statement then follows from the monotonicity of the constrained Kolmogorov widths as stated in (iii).

\Box

We next want to show that part (v) of Lemma
3.1
cannot be improved.
Let us denote by
\ell_{p}
,
0<p\leq\infty
,
the infinite dimensional
\ell_{p}
space with its canonical basis
e_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots
, where each
e_{i}
has coordinate one in the
i
-th position and coordinate zero in all other positions.
We let
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
denote the Banach space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
equipped with the
\ell_{p}
(quasi-)norm when
0<p\leq\infty
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For the Hilbert space
X=\ell_{2}
, there is a compact subset
K\subset X
such that

d^{c}_{2^{n}}(K)_{\ell_{2}}\geq d_{2^{n}}(K)_{\ell_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{\ell_{2}},\quad n\geq 2.

(3.6)

Proof:

To construct such a set
K
, we consider the sequence
(a_{j})_{j\geq 1}
, where
a_{1}:=a_{2}:=1
and

a_{2^{k}+1}=\cdots=a_{2^{k+1}}:=\frac{1}{k},\quad k\geq 1.

(3.7)

We take the canonical basis
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,
for
\ell_{2}
and define the set

K:=\{0,a_{1}e_{1},a_{2}e_{2},\dots\}\subset\ell_{2}.

Since
a_{k}\to 0
, as
k\to\infty
, the set
K
is compact in
\ell_{2}
.

Next, we prove that

\varepsilon_{n+1}(K)_{\ell_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{n},\quad n\geq 1.

(3.8)

Consider the balls of radius
\frac{1}{n}
about the points

0,a_{1}e_{1},a_{2}e_{2},\dots,a_{2^{n}}e_{2^{n}}.

(3.9)

There are
2^{n}+1\leq 2^{n+1}
such balls. We now show that they cover
K
. Certainly, they cover the points listed in (
3.9
). Consider any point
a_{k}e_{k}
with
k\geq 2^{n}+1
. It has norm at most
\frac{1}{n}
and therefore is in the ball
B(0,\frac{1}{n})
. This proves that these balls cover
K

and completes the proof of (
3.8
).

Finally, we can bound
d_{2^{n}}(K)_{X}
from below as follows. Let
\widetilde{K}
be the closure (in
\ell_{2}
) of the convex hull of

-K\cup K
. From (iv) in Lemma
3.1
and (
2.2
), we have

d_{N}^{c}(K)_{\ell_{2}}\geq d_{N}(K)_{\ell_{2}}=d_{N}(\widetilde{K})_{\ell_{2}},\quad N\geq 1.

We next bound
d_{N}(\widetilde{K})_{\ell_{2}}
from below. For this, we recall (see Theorem 3.3 on page 411 of
[
33
]
) that for the unit ball
U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},~\|x\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq 1\}
of
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
in
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we have

d_{N}(U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})))_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\sqrt{1-\frac{N}{m}},\quad 0\leq N\leq m.

(3.10)

Given a value of
m
, we let
\tilde{e}_{1},\dots,\tilde{e}_{m}
be the vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
formed by the first
m
coordinates of
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}
, that is, the vectors
\tilde{e}_{1},\dots,\tilde{e}_{m}
are the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The convex hull
\Lambda_{m}
of
{\pm}a_{m}\tilde{e}_{1},\dots,{\pm}a_{m}\tilde{e}_{m}
is
a_{m}U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
. Since
(a_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a nonincreasing sequence, we have

d_{N}(\widetilde{K})_{\ell_{2}}\geq a_{m}d_{N}(U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}=a_{m}\sqrt{1-\frac{N}{m}},\quad 1\leq N\leq m.

(3.11)

Hence, taking
N=2^{n}
and
m=2^{n+1}
, and using (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle d_{2^{n}}(K)_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{2^{n}}(\widetilde{K})_{\ell_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}a_{2^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}(n-1)}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{n}(K)_{\ell_{2}},\quad n\geq 2,

(3.12)

which proves the statement of the lemma.

\Box

The bound (v) of Lemma
3.1
is very weak but it does hold for a general Banach space
X
and any compact set
K\in{\cal K}(X)
. The main goal of this paper is aimed at giving much stronger rates of convergence for constrained widths when more is known about
X
and
K
. Before formulating these stronger convergence results, we first establish some fundamental comparisons between the different types of constrained Kolmogorov widths and the standard Kolmogorov widths that hold for general compact sets.

4
Comparisons between various constrained Kolmogorov widths

In this section, we derive various comparisons between the Kolmogorov width and the restricted, greedy, and
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths.

4.1
Greedy, restricted, and
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths.

We first prove the following theorem, which shows that the restricted Kolmogorov widths and the greedy Kolmogorov widths are the same, provided
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be any Banach space. If
K\in{\cal K}_{p}={\cal K}_{p}(X)
, then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}=d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}d^{c,\gamma}_{n}(K)_{X}.

(4.1)

Proof:
We fix the value of
n
.
Lemma
3.1
, part (iv), gives that for every
K\in{\cal K}(X)
,

d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}\leq L:=\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.2)

We will now show that
for
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, we have

L\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(4.3)

which will complete the proof of the theorem.

To prove (
4.3
), it is enough to show that for any
0<\epsilon\leq 1
, there exists a
\gamma\geq 1
, which may depend on
\epsilon
, such that

L\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon.

(4.4)

Note that the first part of the inequality, i.e.

L\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
, holds for every
\gamma\geq 1
since
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
is a decreasing function of
\gamma
.

Let
0<\epsilon\leq 1
be arbitrary and fixed, and recall that
S(K)
is the linear span of
K
. From the definition of the restricted width, there exists a subspace
W_{n}=W_{n}(\epsilon):={\rm span}\{w_{1},...,w_{n}\}
with
w_{i}\in{\cal X}:={\cal X}(K)=\overline{S(K)}
,
such that

\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in W_{n}}\|f-v\|_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon/2.

(4.5)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the
w_{i}
’s,
i=1,\dots,n
, are a basis for
W_{n}
. Thus, each
g\in W_{n}
has a unique representation

g=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(g)w_{i}.

(4.6)

From the equivalence of norms on a finite dimensional space, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}(g)|\leq C_{1}\|g\|_{X},\quad g\in W_{n},

(4.7)

where the constant
C_{1}
depends on
W_{n}
.

For each
f\in K
, let
S(f)\in W_{n}
be a best approximation to
f
from
W_{n}
, that is

\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))w_{i}\right\|_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon/2.

(4.8)

It follows that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))w_{i}\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}+d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon/2\leq C_{2},\quad f\in K,

(4.9)

where
C_{2}
is a fixed constant, depending only on
K
.
Going further, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}(S(f))|\leq C_{1}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))w_{i}\|_{X}\leq C,\quad f\in K,\quad C:=C_{1}C_{2}.

(4.10)

Let
\delta>0
. Since each
w_{i}\in\overline{S(K)}
we can choose a
\widetilde{w}_{i}\in S(K)
such that
\|w_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{i}\|_{X}\leq\delta
. Considering the perturbed space
V_{n}:={\rm span}\{\widetilde{w}_{1},...,\widetilde{w}_{n}\}
, we then get for any
f\in K
,

\displaystyle\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))\widetilde{w}_{i}\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f)){w}_{i}\right\|_{X}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}(S(f))|\|w_{i}-\widetilde{w}_{i}\|_{X}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f)){w}_{i}\right\|_{X}+\delta\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}(S(f))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon/2+C\delta.

If
0<\delta<\frac{\epsilon}{2C}
, we obtain for all
f\in K

\left\|f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))\widetilde{w}_{i}\right\|_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon.

(4.12)

We will now show that there is a value of
\gamma
such that for each
f\in K
, the approximant in (
4.12
) is in
K_{\gamma}
. For this, we recall that each
\widetilde{w}_{i}\in S(K)
, and since
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, there exists
\widetilde{\gamma}>0
such that
\widetilde{w}_{i}\in K_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
, i.e.

\widetilde{w}_{i}=\widetilde{\gamma}f_{i}
, for some
f_{i}\in K
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Using (
4.10
) and the fact that
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, we find that
for all
f\in K

\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))\widetilde{w}_{i}=\widetilde{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(S(f))f_{i}\in K_{\gamma},~\text{for}~\gamma=C\widetilde{\gamma},

(4.13)

where
C
is the constant in (
4.10
).
It then follows from (
4.12
) that

\sup_{f\in K}\,\inf_{v\in V_{n}\cap K_{\gamma}}\|f-v\|_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon.

Therefore, we have proven that

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}+\epsilon,

(4.14)

which shows (
4.4
) and completes the proof.

\Box

4.2
Comparisons between restricted, greedy, and standard Kolmogorov widths

It is clear that the standard Kolmogorov width is no larger than the other widths we have introduced. We are interested in how much larger these constraining widths are. We begin with the case of
restricted Kolmogorov width since
this is the least demanding of the constrained widths we have introduce.
The following theorem gives an upper bound on the restricted Kolmogorov width of a compact set
K
by its Kolmogorov width.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every Banach space
X
and every
K\in{\cal K}(X)
we have that

d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq(1+\sqrt{n})d_{n}(K)_{X},\quad{n\geq 0.}

(4.15)

When
X
is a Hilbert space, we have

d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}=d_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\geq 0.

(4.16)

Proof:

We apply Theorem
2.1
with the Banach spaces
Y:={\cal X}(K)\subset X=:Z
. In the case
X
is a Hilbert space, we have that
d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}=d_{n}(K)_{X}
, see (
2.5
). In the case of a general Banach space,
from the definition of restricted widths, this theorem gives

d^{r}_{n}(K)_{X}=d_{n}(K)_{Y}\leq(1+\sqrt{n})d_{n}(K)_{X},

as desired.

\Box

In the case of compact sets
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, the estimate (
4.15
) was given in Proposition 3.2 from
[
44
]
with
d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}
substituted by
d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}
. Combining Theorems
4.2
and
4.1
furnishes an alternative proof. Namely, the following holds.

Theorem 4.3
.

[Proposition 3.2 from
[
44
]
]

For every
compact subset
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
of a Banach space
X
we have

d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq(1+\sqrt{n})d_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\geq 0.

(4.17)

When
X
is a Hilbert space
H
, then

d^{g}_{n}(K)_{H}=d_{n}(K)_{H},\quad n\geq 0.

(4.18)

Proof:
This follows from Theorem
4.2
and the fact that
d_{n}^{g}(K)_{X}=d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}
when
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, see Theorem
4.1
.

\Box

Note that estimate (
4.15
) holds for any
K\in{\cal K}(X)
. In the case when
K\in{\cal K}_{p}
, the greedy and restricted widths are identical, and so we obtain the same bound for greedy widths. However, for a general compact set
K\in{\cal K}(X)
, the situation for greedy widths is different.
Namely, Theorem 3.1 from
[
44
]
(see Theorem 4.1 from
[
6
]
for the result in a Hilbert space) gives
the following result.

Theorem 4.4
.

[Theorem 3.1 from
[
44
]
]

For any
K\in{\cal K}(X)
we have that

d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}\leq(n+1)d_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n=0,1,\ldots.

On the other hand, for every
n
and every
\varepsilon>0
there is a Hilbert space
X
and a compact set
K=K(\varepsilon,n)\in{\cal K}(X)
, such that

d^{g}_{n}(K)_{X}\geq(n-1-\varepsilon)d_{n}(K)_{X}.

We next show that the bound in Theorem
4.2
is sharp.
Before doing this, we gather some preliminary results.
We denote by
C(\Omega)
the space of continuous functions on a compact topological space
\Omega
, equipped with the usual norm

\|f\|_{C(\Omega)}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|,

(4.19)

and present the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be any compact topological space and
K
be any compact, centrally symmetric subset of
C(\Omega)
. Then we have

d_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}\leq\delta_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}=d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)},

(4.20)

where
d^{n}
and
\delta_{n}
denote the Gelfand and linear widths, respectively.

Proposition
4.5
essentially follows from classical results concerning the so called
absolute Kolmogorov widths
(see for instance Theorem 1 in
[
23
]
). A similar result for the corresponding
s
-numbers of operators can be found in
[
42
]
(point (ii) above equation 1.3). For the reader’s convenience, we give in the Appendix, see §
8.1
, a self-contained proof.

We now prove a result that shows that the bound in Theorem
4.2
is sharp.

Theorem 4.6
.

There exists a Banach space
X
and a set
K\in{\cal K}(X)
for which

d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}\geq C\sqrt{n}d_{n}(K)_{X},\quad n\geq 0,

(4.21)

with
C>0
independent of
n
.

Proof:

We start with a set
K\in{\cal K}(X)
and a separable Banach space
X
, such that

d_{n}(K)_{X}\geq C\sqrt{n}d^{n}(K)_{X}.

(4.22)

Examples of such
pairs
K
and
X
are well-known. For example, in going further, we can take

K=U(W^{1}(L_{1}([0,1])),\quad X=L_{2}([0,1]),

(see
[
27
]
or Chapter 14 of
[
33
]
).

We next use the fact (Banach-Mazur theorem) that every separable Banach space
X

embeds isometrically into
C([0,1])

(see Theorem 1.4.3 in
[
2
]
). Let

j:X\rightarrow C([0,1])

denote such an embedding and consider the image
j(K)\subset C([0,1])
. It is clear by definition that

d_{n}^{r}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}\geq d_{n}(j(K))_{j(X)}=d_{n}(K)_{X},

(4.23)

since
\mathcal{X}(j(K))\subset j(X)
. Using the Hahn-Banach theorem, it follows that

d^{n}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}=d^{n}(j(K))_{j(X)}=d^{n}(K)_{X},

(4.24)

since any linear functionals on
j(X)
can be extended to
C([0,1])
.
It follows from (
4.24
) and Proposition
4.5
, which , in this particular case states that
d_{n}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}\leq d^{n}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}
, that

d_{n}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}\leq d^{n}(K)_{X}.

(4.25)

Relations (
4.23
), (
4.25
), and (
4.22
) give that

d_{n}^{r}(j(K))_{C([0,1])}\geq d_{n}(K)_{X}\geq C\sqrt{n}d^{n}(K)_{X}\geq C\sqrt{n}d_{n}(j(K))_{C([0,1])},

(4.26)

that is, (
4.21
) holds for the set
j(K)
and the space
C([0,1])
.

\Box

4.3
Comparison between the constrained Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}
and the Kolmogorov widths

Since the constrained widths
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}\geq d_{n}^{r}(K)_{X}
, the results of the previous section, see Theorem
4.6
, show that in some cases the constrained widths of a compact set
K
can be considerably larger than the Kolmogorov widths of
K
. There is a natural question of whether there is any direct comparison between these two widths that holds for all Banach spaces
X
and sets
K\in{\cal K}(X)
. For example, do there exist sequences
(m_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, and
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, such that

d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}\leq\lambda_{n}d_{m_{n}}(K)_{X},\quad n\geq 1,

(4.27)

holds for all
X
and
K\in{\cal K}(X)
. The next theorem shows that there is no such direct comparison of the form (
4.27
) that holds for Hilbert spaces
X
and convex, centrally symmetric compact subsets
K\subset X
.

Theorem 4.7
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and every
m\geq n\geq 2
, there is a convex centrally symmetric compact set
K=K(\varepsilon,n,m)\subset\ell_{2}
, such that

d_{n}(K)_{\ell_{2}}<\varepsilon,\quad\hbox{and}\quad d^{c}_{m}(K)_{\ell_{2}}>1.

Proof:

We fix
m\geq n\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
, and choose
m+1
vectors

v_{1},\ldots,v_{m+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
{\rm span}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m+1}\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\mathop{\rm dist}(v_{i},\text{conv}(\pm v_{j},j\neq i))>2,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(4.28)

One can construct such a set of vectors by taking quasi uniformly
spaced points on a sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sufficiently large radius. Let
e_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, be the canonical basis for
\ell_{2}
where
e_{j}
has
j
-th coordinate one and all other coordinates zero.
For each
i=1,\dots,m+1
, we define the vectors
w_{i}\in\ell_{2}
with the property

w_{i}\cdot e_{j}=\begin{cases}v_{i}\cdot e_{j},&j\leq n,\\
\epsilon,&j=n+i,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(4.29)

In other words,
w_{i}
has its first
n
coordinates given by
v_{i}
, its entry in coordinate
n+i
is
\varepsilon
, and all of the remaining coordinates are zero.
We then consider the centrally symmetric and convex set

K:=K(\varepsilon,n,m):=\text{conv}\left\{\pm w_{1},\pm w_{2},\ldots,\pm w_{m+1}\right\}\subset\ell_{2}.

(4.30)

We write any element
z\in\ell_{2}
as
z=(x;y)
, where

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector consisting of its first
n
coordinates and
y
is the vector consisting of its remaining coordinates. In particular, any element
z\in K
is of this form with

x=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}c_{i}v_{i},\quad y=(y_{i})_{i\geq 1},\quad\hbox{where}\quad y_{i}=\varepsilon c_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq m+1,\quad y_{i}=0\quad\hbox{for}\quad i>m+1,

(4.31)

and the coefficients
c_{i}
satisfy the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}|c_{i}|^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}|c_{i}|\leq 1.

(4.32)

We define the
n
dimensional subspace
V^{*}:={\rm span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
. Then, from (
4.32
), we have

\mathop{\rm dist}(z,V^{*})_{\ell_{2}}\leq\varepsilon,\quad z\in K,

(4.33)

and therefore

d_{n}(K)_{\ell_{2}}\leq\varepsilon.

(4.34)

We now want to show that the constrained width
d_{m}^{c}(K)_{\ell_{2}}
is large.
Let
V
be any
m
-dimensional subspace of
\ell_{2}
, where
m\geq n
. Observe that the orthogonal projection of
V
onto the space

{\rm span}\{(0;e_{1}),\ldots,(0;e_{m+1})\}
,
where
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is an
m
dimensional space.
If
z=(x;y)\in V
, then there are coefficients
a_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m+1
, depending only on
V
, not all zeroes, such that

a_{1}y_{1}+\ldots+a_{m+1}y_{m+1}=0.

Now, if
z
is also in
K
, then
z=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}c_{i}w_{i}
, where the
c_{i}
’s satisfy (
4.32
). Combining this with (
4.31
) gives that

\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}a_{i}c_{i}=0.

(4.35)

Let
j\in\{1,\dots,m+1\}
be chosen so that
|a_{j}|
is the biggest among all these
|a_{i}|^{\prime}s
. Then it follows from (
4.35
) that

|a_{j}c_{j}|=\left|-\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{m+1}a_{i}c_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{m+1}|a_{i}||c_{i}|\leq|a_{j}|\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{m+1}|c_{i}|,

and therefore

|c_{j}|\leq\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{m+1}|c_{i}|\leq 1-|c_{j}|,

(4.36)

where we have used (
4.32
).
Thus,

|c_{j}|\leq 1/2.

(4.37)

In other words, there is
j\in\{1,\dots,m+1\}
depending only on
V
, such that if
z=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}c_{i}w_{i}\in K\cap V
, then (
4.37
) must hold.

Consider now the approximation of the element
f:=w_{j}\in K
, by any element
z=(x;y)\in K\cap V
. We write
z=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}c_{i}w_{i}
as in
(
4.31
).
We calculate using (
4.28
)

\displaystyle\|f-z\|_{\ell_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|(1-c_{j})v_{j}-\sum_{i=i\neq j}c_{i}v_{i}\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=(1-c_{j})\left\|v_{j}-\sum_{i=1,i\neq j}^{m+1}\frac{c_{i}}{1-c_{j}}v_{i}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(4.38)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-c_{j})\mathop{\rm dist}(v_{j},\text{conv}(\pm v_{i},i\neq j))>1

since
1-c_{j}\geq 1/2
and
\sum_{i\neq j}|c_{i}|\leq 1-|c_{j}|\leq 1-c_{j}
. This proves the theorem.
\Box

The example given in Theorem
4.7
is rather exotic. One may ask what happens for well known compact sets
K
.
The following lemma shows a result of this type when
K
is the unit ball in
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
m\geq 2
and

U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m},~\|x\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq 1\}\subset X=\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

be the unit ball in
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then, for
n<m
, we have that

d_{n}(U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})))_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq C\sqrt{\frac{1+\log(m/n)}{n}},\quad\hbox{while}\quad d_{n}^{c}(U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})))_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\geq 1/2.

(4.39)

Proof:
The estimate for the Kolmogorov width is a classical result, see
[
25
,
26
]
. To prove the result for
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}
, let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be any linear space of dimension
n<m
, and suppose that
v_{i}\in V\cap U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
approximates
e_{i}\in U(\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
,
i=1,\ldots,m,
to accuracy
<1/2
. That is,

\|e_{i}-v_{i}\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}<1/2,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|v_{i}\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq 1,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

Then the components
v_{ij}
of the vectors
v_{i}
satisfy
v_{ii}>1/2
and

\sum_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{m}|v_{ij}|<1/2,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

But this implies that the matrix with rows
v_{i}
is diagonally dominant and thus non-singular. So, the vectors
v_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, are linearly independent, and therefore cannot lie in a subspace of dimension
n<m
.

\Box

Despite the negative results given above, we can still derive some weak comparisons.
By combining Lemma
3.1
with the Carl’s inequality, we obtain the following weak indirect comparison.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
K\in{\cal K}(X)
. Then, for each
\alpha>0
there is a constant
C=C(\alpha)
such that

\max_{k=1,\ldots,n}\left\{k^{\alpha}d^{c}_{2^{k}}(K)_{X}\right\}\leq C(\alpha)\max_{m=1,\ldots,n}\left\{m^{\alpha}d_{m-1}(K)_{X}\right\}.

Proof:
From Carl’s inequality (see e.g.
[
9
]
), we know that for any
\alpha>0
, we have

\max_{k=1,\ldots,n}\left\{k^{\alpha}\varepsilon_{k}(K)_{X}\right\}\leq C(\alpha)\max_{m=1,\ldots,n}\left\{m^{\alpha}d_{m-1}(K)_{X}\right\}.

Lemma
3.1
, part (v) allows us to replace
\varepsilon_{k}(K)_{X}
by
2^{-1}d_{2^{k}}(K)_{X}
and obtain the conclusion of the lemma.

\Box

The results presented so far show that, in general, the constrained Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}
can decay much slower than the classical Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}(K)_{X}
.
This suggests that one needs to put additional requirements on the set
K
in order to achieve a meaningful comparisons between its constrained, or
\gamma
-constrained widths and its Kolmogorov widths.
The remaining sections of this paper present classical settings of Banach spaces
X
and compact sets
K
, where the asymptotic decay of the
\gamma
-constrained widths

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
, for a fixed value of
\gamma
, is the same as that of the Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}(K)_{X}
. In other words, in these settings, there is no essential
loss in the efficiency of approximation when invoking the constraint.

5
Widths of interpolation spaces

A general family of model classes
K
are those generated by interpolation spaces. We study the constrained Kolmogorov widths of these classes in this section. Before going into details, we recall the definition of the interpolation spaces
X_{\theta,q}
,
0<\theta<1
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
in the special case of the real method of interpolation. A comprehensive treatment of interpolation spaces can be found in the books
[
5
,
4
]
.

Throughout this section, we let
X
be a quasi-Banach space with a quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
, and
Y

be a linear subspace of
X
which is itself a space with a quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
. The terminology quasi-Banach space for
X
means that in place of the triangle inequality, we have

\|f+g\|_{X}\leq C^{*}(\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}),\quad f,g\in X,

(5.1)

with
C^{*}
a constant depending only on
X
.
We need this concept below when dealing with Besov spaces.

We assume that the embedding inequality

\|f\|_{X}\leq C_{Y}\|f\|_{Y},\quad f\in Y,

(5.2)

is valid and that the unit ball
U(Y)
is a compact subset of
X
.

The K-functional for the pair
(X,Y)
is defined for each
f\in X
by

K(f,t;X,Y):=\inf_{g\in Y}\left\{\|f-g\|_{X}+t\|g\|_{Y}\right\},\quad t>0.

(5.3)

This is a concave increasing function of
t>0
. By taking
g=0
, we see
that

K(f,t)\leq\|f\|_{X},\quad 0<t<\infty.

(5.4)

The K-functional measures how well
f
can be approximated by elements
g
of
Y
with a control on
\|g\|_{Y}
.
From the compactness of
U(Y)
in
X
, it follows that for each
t>0
, there is a
g_{t}\in Y
such that

\|f-g_{t}\|_{X}+t\|g_{t}\|_{Y}=K(f,t;X,Y),\quad 0\leq t<\infty.

(5.5)

The K-functional can be used to define new Banach spaces
Z
that are intermediate to
X
and
Y
as follows. If
0<\theta<1
and
0<q<\infty
, we define the interpolation space

X_{\theta,q}:=(X,Y)_{\theta,q}
as the set of all
f\in X
for which

\|f\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,q}}:=\left\{\intop\limits_{0}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}K(f,t;X,Y)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right\}^{1/q}

(5.6)

is finite.
When
q=\infty
, we use the same definition with the
L_{q}(\frac{dt}{t})
quasi-norm replaced by the
L_{\infty}
norm.
The expression
\|f\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,q}}
is a norm when
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
are norms and
q\geq 1
and it is a quasi-norm in all cases.

In what follows, we shall use the following known properties of interpolations spaces.

Remark 5.1
.

Whenever
X
and
Y
are as above, we have the embeddings

\|f\|_{X_{\theta,q}}\leq C\|f\|_{X_{\theta^{\prime},q^{\prime}}},

(5.7)

provided
0<\theta<\theta^{\prime}<1
and
0<q,q^{\prime}\leq\infty
, or
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
and
0<q^{\prime}<q\leq\infty
. Here the constant
C
depends only on the parameters
\theta,\theta^{\prime},q
and
q^{\prime}
.

Proof:
See (7.7) of Chapter 6 in
[
14
]
.

\Box

The reiteration theorem (see Theorem 7.3 in Chapter 6 of
[
14
]
, or Theorem 2.4, Chapter 5 in
[
4
]
) shows that the spaces
X_{\theta,q}:=(X,Y)_{\theta,q}
,
0<\theta<1
,
0<q\leq\infty
, are an interpolation family.
We shall use this fact in the following special case.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
0<\theta_{0},\theta<1
and
0<q_{0},q\leq\infty
, then

(X,X_{\theta_{0},q_{0}})_{\theta,q}=X_{\theta^{\prime},q},\quad\theta^{\prime}:=\theta\theta_{0},

(5.8)

with equivalent quasi-norms, where the constants in the equivalency depend on
\theta,\theta_{0},q,q_{0}
.

This lemma was proved in Theorem 2.4, Chapter 5 in
[
4
]
in the case
1\leq q,q_{0}\leq\infty
. The case when either
q
or
q_{0}
is less than one is proved in
[
15
]
.

5.1
Upper bounds for Kolmogorov widths of interpolation spaces

We now explain how the K-functional is useful in proving results in approximation theory. Let
X
and
Y
be as above and suppose that we know the Kolmogorov width
d_{n}(U(Y))_{X}
, where

U(Y):=\{g\in Y:\ \|g\|_{Y}\leq 1\}.

(5.9)

We claim that from this knowledge we can give an upper bound for the Kolmogorov width of the unit ball of any of the interpolation spaces
X_{\theta,q}:=(X,Y)_{\theta,q}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

There is an absolute constant
C>0
, such that for every
0<\theta<1
and
0<q\leq\infty
, we have

d_{n}(U(X_{\theta,q}))_{X}\leq C[d_{n}(U(Y))_{X}]^{\theta},\quad n\geq 0.

(5.10)

In addition, for any
0<\theta<\theta_{0}<1
and
0<q,q_{0}\leq\infty
, we have

d_{n}(U(X_{\theta,q}))_{X}\leq C(\theta_{0},\theta,q,q_{0})[d_{n}(U(X_{\theta_{0},q_{0}}))_{X}]^{\frac{\theta}{\theta_{0}}},\quad n\geq 0.

(5.11)

Proof:

Let us fix
n\geq 0
and denote by

\delta:=d_{n}(U(Y))_{X}.

Then
\delta\leq C_{Y}
because of (
5.2
).
Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional Kolmogorov space for
U(Y)
. If such a Kolmogorov space does not exist, we use the
\varepsilon
argument of Remark
1.1
. Fix any
f\in X
and first choose
g:=g_{\delta}\in Y
satisfying (
5.5
) with
t=\delta
and then a best approximation
v\in V
to
g
. We then have

\|f-v\|_{X}\leq C^{*}(\|f-g\|_{X}+\|g-v\|_{X})\leq C^{*}(\|f-g\|_{X}+\delta\|g\|_{Y})=C^{*}K(f,\delta;X,Y).

(5.12)

Now suppose
\theta\in(0,1)
. If
f\in U(X_{\theta,\infty})
, we have
K(f,\delta;X,Y)\leq\delta^{\theta}
and
\|g\|_{Y}\leq\delta^{\theta-1}
. It follows that for any
0<q\leq\infty
the unit ball of
X_{\theta,q}
satisfies

d_{n}(U(X_{\theta,q}))_{X}\leq Cd_{n}(U(X_{\theta,\infty}))_{X}\leq C\delta^{\theta}=C[d_{n}(U(Y))_{X}]^{\theta},\quad n\geq 0,

where we have used Remark
5.1
to see that
U(X_{\theta,q})
is contained in a ball of radius
C
in
X_{\theta,\infty}
.
This completes the proof of
(
5.10
).

Next, we prove (
5.11
). We fix
\theta_{0}
and
q_{0}
and use the notation
Y_{0}:=X_{\theta_{0},q_{0}}
.
It is known that the unit ball
U(Y_{0})
is a compact subset of
X
, see Theorem 3.8.1 in
[
5
]
.
Therefore, we can use
use (
5.10
) with
Y_{0}
in place of
Y
, and obtain

d_{n}(U((X,Y_{0})_{\alpha,q}))_{X}\leq C[d_{n}(U(Y_{0}))_{X}]^{\alpha}.

(5.13)

On the other hand, we know from Lemma
5.2
that

(X,Y_{0})_{\alpha,q}=(X,Y)_{\alpha\theta_{0},q}

(5.14)

with equivalent norms. If we take
\alpha=\theta/\theta_{0}

and use (
5.14
) in (
5.13
) we have proved (
5.11
).

\Box

5.2
Upper bounds for
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of interpolation spaces

In this section, we extend the results of the previous subsection to the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X,Y
be as above and let
\gamma\geq 1
. Then, for any
0<\theta<1
, and any
0<q\leq\infty
, there are constants
C_{0},C_{1}
depending only on
\theta
and

, such that

d_{n}^{c,\tilde{\gamma}}(U(X_{\theta,q}))_{X}\leq C_{0}(d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U(Y))_{X})^{\theta},\quad n\geq 0,

(5.15)

provided that
\tilde{\gamma}>C_{1}\gamma
.

Proof:
Throughout this proof,
C
denotes a constant that depends at most on
\theta
, and
q
and its value may change from line to line.
We fix
n
and use the abbreviated notation

\delta_{n}:=d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U(Y))_{X}.

It follows from the definition of
\delta_{n}
that given any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a linear space
V
of dimension
n
with the property that for any
h\in Y
, there is a
v\in V\cap Y
such that

\|h-v\|_{X}\leq(\delta_{n}+\varepsilon)\|h\|_{Y},\quad{\rm and}\quad\|v\|_{Y}\leq\gamma\|h\|_{Y}.

(5.16)

To prove (
5.15
), we fix
\theta
and
q
, and begin as in the derivation of (
5.12
). Recall that
X_{\theta,q}
is embedded in

X_{\theta,\infty}
and (see Remark
5.1
)

\|f\|_{X_{\theta,\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{X_{\theta,q}}.

(5.17)

Given any
f\in X_{\theta,q}
, we take
g:=g_{\delta_{n}}
for

and an appropriate

satisfying (
5.16
) for

. This gives

\displaystyle\|f-v\|_{X}

\displaystyle\!\!\!\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C^{*}(\|f-g\|_{X}+\|g-v\|_{X})\leq C^{*}(\|f-g\|_{X}+(\delta_{n}+\varepsilon)\|g\|_{Y})\

(5.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!C^{*}K(f,\delta_{n};X,Y)+{C^{*}}\varepsilon\|g\|_{Y}\leq C\delta_{n}^{\theta}+{C^{*}}\varepsilon\|g\|_{Y}\leq C\delta_{n}^{\theta}+C\varepsilon\delta_{n}^{\theta-1}\leq C\delta_{n}^{\theta},

provided that

is chosen small enough (depending only on

Note that the function

also satisfies

\|v\|_{Y}\leq\gamma\|g\|_{Y}\leq C\gamma\delta_{n}^{\theta-1}.

(5.19)

We want next to bound
\|v\|_{X_{\theta,q}}
, and thus we concentrate on estimating

.
On one hand, we have

K(t)\leq t\|v\|_{Y}\leq C\gamma t\delta_{n}^{\theta-1},\quad\hbox{when}\quad 0<t\leq\delta_{n}.

(5.20)

On the other hand, we also have the bound

\displaystyle K(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v-g_{t}\|_{X}+t\|g_{t}\|_{Y}\leq C^{*}(\|f-g_{t}\|_{X}+\|f-v\|_{X})+t\|g_{t}\|_{Y}

(5.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{*}K(f,t;X,Y)+{C^{*}}\|f-v\|_{X}\leq C^{*}K(f,t;X,Y)+C\delta_{n}^{\theta},\quad\hbox{when}\quad t>\delta_{n},

where we have used (
5.18
).

We can use these two estimates to bound
\|v\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,q}}
as follows.

Case 1:

q=\infty
. In this case, we have
K(f,t;X,Y)\leq t^{\theta}
,
t>0
. Therefore, from (
5.20
) we have

K(t)\leq\gamma t\delta_{n}^{\theta-1}\leq C\gamma t^{\theta},\quad 0<t\leq\delta_{n}.

(5.22)

Also, from (
5.21

K(t)\leq Ct^{\theta}+C\delta_{n}^{\theta}\leq Ct^{\theta},\quad t>\delta_{n}.

(5.23)

Since

, this shows that

, and completes the proof in this case.

Case 2:

0<q<\infty
. We write

\|v\|^{q}_{X_{\theta,q}}=\intop\limits_{0}^{\delta_{n}}[t^{-\theta}K(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}+\intop\limits_{\delta_{n}}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}K(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(5.24)

From (

), we obtain

I_{1}=\intop\limits_{0}^{\delta_{n}}[t^{-\theta}K(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\leq C^{q}\gamma^{q}\delta_{n}^{q(\theta-1)}\intop\limits_{0}^{\delta_{n}}t^{(1-\theta)q}\frac{dt}{t}=C^{q}\frac{\gamma^{q}}{(1-\theta)q}\leq C^{q}\gamma^{q}.

(5.25)

For
I_{2}
, from (
5.21
), we have the bound

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\intop\limits_{\delta_{n}}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}K(t)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\leq\intop\limits_{\delta_{n}}^{\infty}\left[t^{-\theta}K(f,t;X,Y)+Ct^{-\theta}\delta_{n}^{\theta}\right]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

(5.26)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}\intop\limits_{\delta_{n}}^{\infty}[t^{-\theta}K(f,t;X,Y)]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}+(2C)^{q}\delta_{n}^{\theta q}\intop\limits_{\delta_{n}}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{q}\|f\|^{q}_{X_{\theta,q}}+\frac{(2C)^{q}}{\theta q}\leq C^{q}\gamma^{q},

where in the last inequality we recall that

.

Combining the bounds we have for
I_{1}
and

, we arrive at

\|v\|_{X_{\theta,q}}\leq C\gamma,

(5.27)

where now

is a fixed constant depending only on
\theta
and

. This completes the proof of
the theorem.
\Box

Let

be as above, and let

6
Widths of approximation spaces

Approximation classes are a major subject in approximation theory whose aim is to characterize all elements of a quasi-Banach space
X
that are approximated with
a prescribed rate by a specified method of approximation. To describe these approximation classes in the setting of interest to us, we fix the quasi-Banach space
X
, where we measure approximation error, and we fix any sequence
{\cal X}:=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of nested linear subspaces
X_{n}\subset X_{n+1}\subset X
, with
\dim(X_{n})=n
and
\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
dense in
X
. Here, we define

X_{0}:=\{0\}
.
Given any
f\in X
, we have the approximation error

E_{n}(f)_{X}:=E(f,X_{n})_{X}:=\inf_{g\in X_{n}}\|f-g\|_{X},\quad n\geq 0.

(6.1)

Note that
E_{0}(f)_{X}=\|f\|_{X}
.

Consider any non-increasing sequence
{\bf\sigma}:=(\sigma_{n})
of positive numbers
1=:\sigma_{0}\geq\sigma_{1}\geq\ldots
,

\sigma_{n}\searrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(6.2)

Typical examples of such sequence are

\sigma_{n}=(n+1)^{-\alpha}
,
n\geq 0
, with
\alpha>0
, or
\sigma_{n}=a^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, where
0<a<1
.
We define the approximation class

{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X}):={\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})_{X}:=\{f\in X:\,\,E_{n}(f)_{X}\leq M\sigma_{n},\,n\geq 0,\,\,\hbox{for some}\,\,M=M(f)\geq 0\}.

(6.3)

Then,
{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})
is clearly a linear subspace of
X
, which we equip with the
norm

\|f\|_{{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\sigma_{n}^{-1}E_{n}(f)_{X}.

(6.4)

The unit ball

U(\sigma,{\cal X}):=\{f\in X:\,\,\|f\|_{{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})}\leq 1\}

is a compact subset of
X
.

We have the following

Theorem 6.1
.

For any sequence
\sigma=(\sigma_{n})
satisfying
(
6.2
)
and nested linear spaces
{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
,
X_{n}\subset X
, with
\dim(X_{n})=n
, and
\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
dense in
X
, the Kolmogorov width of
U(\sigma,{\cal X})
is

d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=\sigma_{n},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

and
X_{n}
is a Kolmogorov space, i.e., it attains the Kolmogorov width.

Proof:

This theorem was proved in
[
38
]
, see Proposition 1.7 in Chapter 1, in the case of a normed linear space
X
. It also holds for a quasi-Banach space
X
. Indeed, the only part of the proof which required
X
to be a Banach space is the fact that the Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}
of the unit ball of a
k
-dimensional space are
\geq 1
for
n<k
. This is proved using the Borsuk-Ulam theorem for Banach spaces
X
in
[
38
]
, but an alternative proof which also works for quasi-Banach spaces can be found for example in
[
22
]
, Part 2, Section 7.
\Box

We turn now to the
\gamma
-constrained approximation of these compact sets.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
decrease monotonically to zero and let
{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be any sequence of nested linear subspaces of
X
with
\dim(X_{n})=n
and
\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
dense in
X
. Then, we have

d_{n}^{r}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=d_{n}^{c,{2C^{*}}}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=d_{n}^{c,\gamma_{n}}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=\sigma_{n},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.5)

where

\gamma_{n}:={C^{*}(1+\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{n-1}})\leq{2C^{*}}},

(6.6)

and
C^{*}
is the constant in
(
5.1
)
.

Proof:

Let us fix
f\in U(\sigma,{\cal X})
and let
\varphi_{k}=\varphi_{k}(f)\in X_{k}
be such that

E_{k}(f)_{X}=\|f-\varphi_{k}\|_{X},\quad k\geq 0.

From the nestedness of the spaces
X_{k}
,
k\geq 0
, we have

E_{k}(\varphi_{n})_{X}=0,\quad k\geq n.

On the other hand,
for
k<n
,

E_{k}(\varphi_{n})_{X}\leq\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{k}\|_{X}\leq{C^{*}(\|\varphi_{n}-f\|_{X}+\|f-\varphi_{k}\|_{X})\leq C^{*}(\sigma_{n}+\sigma_{k})}.

Thus, we have

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})}=\sup_{k\geq 0}\sigma_{k}^{-1}E_{k}(\varphi_{n})_{X}\leq{C^{*}}\sup_{0\leq k<n}\frac{\sigma_{n}+\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{k}}\leq{C^{*}(1+\frac{\sigma_{n}}{\sigma_{n-1}})=:\gamma_{n}\leq 2C^{*}}.

This means that
\varphi_{n}\in\gamma_{n}U(\sigma,{\cal X})\cap X_{n}
and that
\varphi_{n}
approximates
f
to accuracy
d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}
, according to Theorem
6.1
.
Therefore, we have

d_{n}^{c,\gamma_{n}}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}\leq d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=\sigma_{n}

The latter inequality, Lemma
3.1
, part (iv), and Theorem
6.1
, give that for
n\geq 0
, we have

\sigma_{n}=d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}\leq d_{n}^{r}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}\leq{d_{n}^{c,2C^{*}}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}}\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma_{n}}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}=d_{n}(U(\sigma,{\cal X}))_{X}\leq\sigma_{n}.

Hence, we must have equality everywhere in the above inequalities.

\Box

6.1
Refined approximation spaces

There are generalizations of approximation spaces which capture the decay of approximation error in a more subtle way than the spaces
{\cal A}(\sigma,{\cal X})_{X}
. We next describe the best known of these generalizations. We now let
X
be any quasi-Banach space. In particular, we allow
X
to be an
L_{p}
space with
0<p<1
.

As before, we denote by
{\cal X}:=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, a sequence of nested linear subspaces
X_{n}
with
\dim(X_{n})=n
and
\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
dense in
X
. We define the spaces

{\cal A}_{q}^{r}:={\cal A}_{q}^{r}({\cal X},X),\quad\hbox{for}\quad r>0,\quad 0<q<\infty,

as the set of all functions
f\in X
for which

\|f\|_{{\cal A}_{q}^{r}}:=\left\{\sum_{n\geq 0}[(n+1)^{r}E_{n}(f)_{X}]^{q}(n+1)^{-1}\right\}^{1/q}<\infty.

(6.7)

When
q=\infty
, we define

{\cal A}_{\infty}^{r}({\cal X},X):={\cal A}((n+1)^{-r},{\cal X})_{X},

where
{\cal A}((n+1)^{-r},{\cal X})_{X}
is given by (
6.3
) with its defined norm (
6.4
).

Lemma 6.3
.

We have the embedding inequality

\|f\|_{{\cal A}^{r}_{\infty}}\leq 2^{(r+1/q)}\|f\|_{{\cal A}_{q}^{r}},\quad 0<q<\infty.

(6.8)

Proof:
Fix
f\in{\cal A}_{q}^{r}
and let
E_{k}:=E_{k}(f)_{X}
. Since the sequence
(E_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is non-increasing, for each
n\geq 0
, we have

[(n+1)^{r}E_{n}]^{q}\leq(n+1)2^{rq}[(k+1)^{r}E_{k}]^{q}(k+1)^{-1},\quad n/2\leq k\leq n.

(6.9)

There are at least
(n+1)/2
integers
k\in[n/2,n]
. If we sum (
6.9
) over those integers, we obtain

[(n+1)^{r}E_{n}]^{q}\leq 2^{rq+1}\|f\|^{q}_{{\cal A}_{q}^{r}},\quad n\geq 0.

Taking the
q
-th root of this inequality proves the lemma.
\Box

An important property of approximation spaces is that they are an interpolation family (see
[
19
]
, Corollary 3.4, or
[
14
]
, Chapter 7). Namely, for the real method of approximation, we have that for

0<r_{1}<r_{2}
and any
0<q,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
,
and
0<\theta<1
,

{\cal A}_{q}^{s}({\cal X},X)=({\cal A}_{q_{1}}^{r_{1}}({\cal X},X),{\cal A}_{q_{2}}^{r_{2}}({\cal X},X))_{\theta,q},\quad s=(1-\theta)r_{1}+\theta r_{2},

(6.10)

with equivalent quasi-norms

\|f\|_{{\cal A}^{s}_{q}({\cal X},X)}\asymp\|f\|_{({\cal A}_{q_{1}}^{r_{1}}({\cal X},X),{\cal A}_{q_{2}}^{r_{2}}({\cal X},X))_{\theta,q}},\quad s=(1-\theta)r_{1}+\theta r_{2},\ \ 0<q\leq\infty,

(6.11)

and constants of equivalence depending only on
r_{0}
,
r_{1}
,
q
,
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
, and
\theta
.

One of the main chapters of approximation theory is to characterize the approximation classes
for specific sequences
{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of nested
n
dimensional linear spaces whose union is dense in
X
. We outline the typical way this is accomplished.
Given a sequence
{\cal X}
, suppose that for some value of
r>0
, we can find a quasi-Banach space
Y_{r}\subset X
for which the following two inequalities hold (see
[
19
]
or Chapter 7 of
[
14
]
for details):

Jackson Inequality:

E_{n}(f)_{X}\leq C\|f\|_{Y_{r}}(n+1)^{-r},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.12)

Bernstein Inequality:

\|g\|_{Y_{r}}\leq C\|g\|_{X}(n+1)^{r},\quad g\in X_{n},\ n\geq 0.

(6.13)

Then, for any
0<s<r
and
0<q\leq\infty
, the approximation space
{\cal A}_{q}^{s}:={\cal A}_{q}^{s}({\cal X},X)
is characterized as an interpolation
space, that is

{\cal A}^{s}_{q}=(X,Y_{r})_{\theta,q},\quad\hbox{where}\quad\theta:=s/r.

(6.14)

The approximation space norm is equivalent to the interpolation space norm with the constants of equivalency depending on
s
and
q
.

In particular, see
[
14
]
, Chapter 7, Theorem 9.3, or Corollary 3.3 in
[
19
]
, one can take the space
Y_{r}
in (
6.14
) to be
Y_{r}={\cal A}^{r}_{\tau}
for any
s<r<\infty
and any
0<\tau\leq\infty
, that is,

{\cal A}^{s}_{q}=(X,{\cal A}^{r}_{\tau})_{\theta,q},\ 0<q\leq\infty,\quad\theta:=s/r,

(6.15)

with equivalent norms, and the constants in the norm equivalence
depending only on the parameters.

The following theorem is of the same flavor as Theorem
6.1
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Banach space and
{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of nested subspaces of dimension
n
in
X
for which
\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}X_{n}
is dense in
X
. For any
s>0
,
0<q\leq\infty
, we have that the Kolmogorov widths of the unit ball
U({\cal A}_{q}^{s})_{X}:=U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}({\cal X},X))
satisfy

d_{n}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{X}\asymp(n+1)^{-s},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.16)

where the constants in the asymptotic decay depend only on
s
and
q
.

Proof:
We fix
n
. It follows from Lemma
6.3
and Theorem
6.1
, that

d_{n}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{X}\leq Cd_{n}(U({\cal A}_{\infty}^{s}))_{X}=C(n+1)^{-s},\quad n\geq 0.

(6.17)

We now prove the lower bound in (
6.16
). We already know this lower bound when
q=\infty
from Theorem
6.1
.
Since the approximation spaces are an interpolation family, it follows from (
5.11
) and (
6.14
) that for
s^{\prime}:=3s/4
, we have

(n+1)^{-3s/4}=(n+1)^{-s^{\prime}}=d_{n}(U({\cal A}^{s^{\prime}}_{\infty}))_{X}\leq C_{0}[d_{n}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{X}]^{s^{\prime}/s}=C_{0}[d_{n}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{X}]^{3/4},

(6.18)

where
C_{0}
is the constant in (
5.11
).
Thus, the lower bound in (
6.16
)
holds with the constant
C=C_{0}^{-4/3}
.

\Box

6.2
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of refined approximation spaces

We turn now to analyzing the constrained widths of the unit ball
U({\cal A}_{q}^{s})
and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be a quasi-Banach space and
{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of nested subspaces of dimension
n
in
X
, whose union is dense in
X
.

(i)
For any
s>0
and
0<q\leq\infty
, there is
\bar{\gamma}(s,q,X)\geq 1
such that for all
\gamma\geq\bar{\gamma}(s,q,X)
the constrained Kolmogorov widths of the unit ball
U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}):=U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}({\cal X},X))
satisfy

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{X}\asymp(n+1)^{-s},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.19)

with constants of equivalency depending only on
s,q
.

(ii)

Additionally, if
s^{\prime}<s
and
0<q^{\prime}\leq\infty
, then there is a
\bar{\gamma}(s,s^{\prime},q,q^{\prime},X)\geq 1
so that for all
\gamma\geq\bar{\gamma}(s,s^{\prime},q,q^{\prime},X)
we have

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U({\cal A}_{q}^{s}))_{{\cal A}_{q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}}\asymp(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.20)

with the constants of equivalency depending on
s,s^{\prime},q,q^{\prime}
.

Proof:

It follows from Theorem
6.2
with
\sigma_{n}=(n+1)^{-s}
that

(n+1)^{-s}=d_{n}(U({\cal A}^{s}_{\infty}))_{X}={d_{n}^{c,2C^{*}}}(U({\cal A}^{s}_{\infty}))_{X},\quad s>0.

(6.21)

To prove the upper bound in (
6.19
), we fix
s,q
and use the fact that the
{\cal A}_{q}^{s}
spaces are an interpolation family (see (
6.15
)). This together with
(
6.21
) and Theorem
5.4
gives
for
r=2s
the upper bound

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U({\cal A}^{s}_{q}))_{X}\leq C({d_{n}^{c,2C^{*}}}(U({\cal A}^{r}_{\infty})))^{s/r}_{X}\leq C(n+1)^{-s},\quad n\geq 0,

(6.22)

provided
\gamma
is sufficiently large (depending on
s,q
).

To prove the lower bound in (
6.19
), we know that the
\gamma
-constrained width is always as large as the Kolmogorov width. Hence, from Theorem
6.4
we deduce that for all
\gamma
we have

C(n+1)^{-s}\leq d_{n}(U({\cal A}^{s}_{q}))_{X}\leq d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U({\cal A}^{s}_{q}))_{X},

(6.23)

as desired.

To prove (
6.20
) of (ii), we fix
q,q^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}
, and we replace the space
X
where we measure error by
X_{0}:={\cal A}_{q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}
.
It is known that the approximation spaces satisfy

\tilde{\cal A}_{q}^{s}:={\cal A}_{q}^{s-s^{\prime}}({\cal X},{\cal A}_{q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})={\cal A}_{q}^{s}({\cal X},X),\quad s>s^{\prime}\ {\rm and}\ 0<q,q^{\prime}\leq\infty,

(6.24)

with equivalent norms depending only on the parameters. Indeed, (
6.24
) follows from the fact that the spaces
X_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, satisfy Jackson and Bernstein inequalities
when approximation takes place in
X_{0}
(see Appendix
8.2
), since for any
s_{0}>s
, we have

{\cal A}_{\tau}^{s-s^{\prime}}({\cal X},{\cal A}_{q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}})=({\cal A}^{s^{\prime}}_{q^{\prime}},{\cal A}^{s_{0}}_{q})_{\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{s_{0}-s^{\prime}},\tau}={\cal A}_{\tau}^{s}({\cal X},X),

where we have used Theorem 9.1 and equation (9.7) in Chapter 7
from
[
14
]
.

From what we have already proved in (
6.19
) for this new approximation family

\tilde{\cal A}_{q}^{s}
we know there is a
\tilde{\gamma}
such that

d_{n}^{c,\tilde{\gamma}}(U(\tilde{\cal A}_{q}^{s-s^{\prime}}))_{X_{0}}\asymp(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})},\quad n\geq 0.

(6.25)

The conclusion of the theorem then follows from Lemma
3.1
, part (i) since we have

\mu U(\tilde{\cal A}_{q}^{s})\subset U({\cal A}_{q}^{s})\subset\lambda U(\tilde{\cal A}_{q}^{s})
for some
\lambda,\mu>0
.

\Box

7
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of compact sets defined by smoothness

As an illustration of how the results of this paper can be used to determine constrained widths, we discuss, in this section, the
\gamma
-constrained widths of compact sets described by a smoothness condition, when the approximation error in measured in
L_{p}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

We start by recalling the known results
for constrained widths of smoothness classes in
L_{p}
. These have been obtained exclusively in the one dimensional case

d=1
. Konovalov introduced constrained widths in
[
29
]
and proved that

d^{c}_{n}(U(W^{s}(L_{\infty})))_{L_{\infty}}\geq Cn^{-\min\{s,2\}},\quad s\in\mathbb{N},

(7.1)

where
U(W^{s}(L_{\infty}))
is the unit ball of the one-dimensional periodic Sobolev space
W^{s}(L_{\infty})
. This result implies that there can be a substantial gap between
d_{n}^{c}(K)_{X}
and
d_{n}(K)_{X}
for certain compact sets
K\subset X
for certain Banach spaces
X
, since it is well known that the Kolmogorov widths of
U(W^{s}(L_{\infty}))
behave like
n^{-s}
(see Theorem 1.1 in Chapter VII of
[
38
]
, for instance). On the other hand,
there are Banach spaces
X
and compact sets in
K\subset X
for which the Kolmogorov and constrained Kolmogorov widths have the same behavior. For example, it was shown in
[
7
]

that for

K:=\{f\in W^{1}(L_{p}([0,1])):\,\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{p}([0,1])}\leq 1\}\subset L_{2}([0,1]),\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

we have

d_{n}(K)_{L_{2}([0,1])}\asymp d^{c}_{n}(K)_{L_{2}([0,1])}\asymp n^{-1+(1/p-1/2)_{+}},\quad n\geq 1.

The picture is slightly different for the
\gamma
-constrained widths. In spite of the result (
7.1
) for the constrained widths of
U(W^{s}(L_{\infty}))
, Babenko
[
3
]
showed that for any
\gamma>1
, we have

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U(W^{s}(L_{\infty})))_{L_{\infty}}\leq C(\gamma,s)n^{-s},\quad s\in\mathbb{N}.

(7.2)

His work raises the interesting question of how the constant
C(\gamma,s)
depends on
\gamma
and
s
.
This question was addressed in
[
34
]
, where it was shown that for any finite
\gamma<\infty
,
C(\gamma,s)
must grow with
s
.

In this section, we investigate the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
of unit balls
K
of smoothness spaces in the more general setting of bounded Lipschitz domains
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
.
The classical examples of smoothness spaces on
\Omega

are the Lipschitz, Sobolev, or Besov spaces. For the definition
and properties of these spaces, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
for the univariate case and
[
4
,
39
,
43
]
for the multi-dimensional setting.

We concentrate on Besov spaces, since they are both approximation spaces and interpolation spaces, so that we can apply the theory developed in the previous two sections on the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widthss.
For the most part, we are able to determine the asymptotic decay of these widths. We show that they behave like the ordinary Kolmogorov widths,
provided the parameter
\gamma
is chosen large enough. However, there are some exceptional cases where we do not yet know the asymptotic decay of
d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(K)_{X}
. These exceptional cases occur when the Kolmogorov widths
d_{n}(K)_{X}
of
K
cannot be obtained using classical approximation methods (polynomials, splines, wavelet) and are commonly referred to as the Kashin regime (cases where approximation based on probabilistic methods is needed).

7.1
Besov spaces

We briefly recall the definition of Besov spaces
and some of their properties. The material in this section is taken for the most part from the papers
[
20
,
17
]
. The reader may refer to

[
14
]
for the univariate case when
\Omega
is an interval.

If
r
is a positive integer,
0<p\leq\infty
and
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
, we define the modulus of smoothness
\omega_{r}(f,\cdot)_{p}
of
f
by

\omega_{r}(f,t)_{p}:=\omega_{r}(f,t,\Omega)_{p}:=\sup_{|{\rm\bf h}|\leq t}\|\Delta_{\rm\bf h}^{r}(f,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega_{r{\rm\bf h}})},\quad t>0,

(7.3)

where

\Delta_{\rm\bf h}^{r}(f,\cdot):=(-1)^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{k}\binom{r}{k}f(\cdot+k{\rm\bf h}),

(7.4)

is the
r
-th difference of
f
for
{\rm\bf h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\Omega_{\rm\bf h}:=\{{\rm\bf x}\in\Omega:[{\rm\bf x},{\rm\bf x}+{\rm\bf h}]\subset\Omega\}
. Here

[{\rm\bf x},{\rm\bf x}+{\rm\bf h}]
, with

{\rm\bf x}
,
{\rm\bf h}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denotes the line segment in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
between
{\rm\bf x}
and
{\rm\bf x}+{\rm\bf h}
, and
|{\rm\bf h}|
is the Euclidean norm of
{\rm\bf h}
.

If
s>0
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
, then the Besov space

B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))
is defined as the set of all functions in
f\in L_{p}(\Omega)
for which

|f|_{B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))}:=\left[\intop\limits_{0}^{1}[t^{-s}\omega_{r}(f,t)_{p}]^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{1/q}<\infty,\quad 0<q<\infty,

(7.5)

where
r
can be taken as any integer strictly bigger than
s
. When
q=\infty
, we replace the integral in (
7.5
) by a supremum. This is a (quasi-)semi-norm and we obtain the (quasi-)norm for
B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))

by adding
\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}
to it.
While different choices of
r
result in different (quasi-)semi-norms, the corresponding Besov (quasi-)norms are equivalent, provided
r>s
.
To fix matters, we define the Besov norm by taking the value of
r=r(s)
as the smallest integer strictly larger than
s
.
The (quasi-) norm on this Besov space is then

\|f\|_{B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))}:=|f|_{B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))}+\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

(7.6)

Let us make some remarks on some properties of these spaces. Note that

\|f\|_{B_{q_{1}}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))}\leq C\|f\|_{B_{q_{2}}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))},\quad 0<q_{2}\leq q_{1}\leq\infty,

(7.7)

with the embedding constant
C
depending only on the parameters.
In other words, the Besov spaces get smaller as
q
gets smaller.

The effect of
q
in the definition of the Besov spaces is subtle. We remark that when
p=q
and
s
is not an integer, the space
B^{s}_{p}(L_{p}(\Omega))
is equivalent to the Sobolev space
W^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))
, see
[
1
]
.

7.2
Equivalent description of Besov spaces as interpolation spaces or approximation spaces

When analyzing the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of finite balls of a Besov space, we use two important properties of these spaces.
The first property is that the for any fixed
0<p\leq\infty
, the Besov spaces
B_{q}^{s}(L_{p}(\Omega))
are an interpolation family. Namely, for
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
and
0<q,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
, we have

\left(B_{q_{1}}^{s_{1}}(L_{p}(\Omega)),B_{q_{2}}^{s_{2}}(L_{p}(\Omega))\right)_{\theta,q}=B^{s}_{q}(L_{p}(\Omega)),\quad s=(1-\theta)s_{1}+\theta s_{2},

(7.8)

with equivalent norms.
For a proof of this fact, see Corollary 6.2 in
[
18
]
in the case of
\Omega
being the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and Corollary 6.8 in
[
20
]
for Lipschitz graph domains
\Omega
.
In the case
1\leq p\leq\infty
, for
0<\theta<1
and
0<q\leq\infty
, we have

B_{q}^{\theta r}(L_{p}(\Omega))=\left(L_{p}(\Omega),W^{r}(L_{p}(\Omega))\right)_{\theta,q},

(7.9)

see
[
20
]
.
Moreover, the interpolation space norm is equivalent to the Besov space norm given in (
7.6
).

The second property we will use is that Besov spaces are approximation spaces. Namely, if
X=L_{\tau}(\Omega)
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, there is a family

{\cal X}=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of nested linear spaces, with
\dim(X_{n})=n
, whose union is dense in
L_{\tau}(\Omega)
, such that

B_{q}^{s}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))={\cal A}_{q}^{s/d}({\cal X},L_{\tau}(\Omega)),\quad s>0,\,\ 0<\tau\leq\infty,~0<q\leq\infty.

(7.10)

For example, it is shown in
[
17
]
that one may take
X_{n}
as an
n
dimensional linear space of piecewise polynomials on a partition of
\Omega
, when
\Omega
is the unit cube. For the more general case of bounded Lipschitz graph domains a similar result was given in
[
20
]
. One may also take
X_{n}
to be the linear space spanned by
n
terms of a multiscale (wavelet) basis
[
11
]
under certain assumptions on
\Omega
.

7.3
Upper bounds for
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of Besov balls

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz graph domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We fix
L_{p}(\Omega)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, as the Banach space where approximation is to take place. We use the notation
U^{s}_{\tau,q}:=U(B^{s}_{q}(L_{\tau}(\Omega)))
for the unit ball of the Besov space
B_{q}^{s}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))
for
s>0
and
0<q,\tau\leq\infty
. Then
U^{s}_{\tau,q}
is a compact set in
L_{p}(\Omega)
if and only if

s>\left(\frac{d}{\tau}-\frac{d}{p}\right)_{+}.

(7.11)

Theorem 7.1
.

For the set
U^{s}_{\tau,q}
, where
0<q,\tau\leq\infty
,
s>d(1/\tau-1/p)_{+}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, there is a
\gamma=\gamma(p,q,\tau,s)
such that

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U^{s}_{\tau,q})_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq C(p,q,s,\tau)(n+1)^{-(\frac{s}{d}-(\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{p})_{+})},\quad n\geq 0.

(7.12)

Proof:
Let us first consider the case when
\tau=p
, i.e., the smoothness is measured in the same space
L_{p}
where we measure error. We use the fact that these Besov spaces are approximation spaces, see (
7.10
). The upper bound in this case therefore follows from (
7.10
) and Theorem
6.5
.

In the case
\tau>p
, we have from what we just proved that

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U_{\tau,q}^{s})_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq Cd_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U_{\tau,q}^{s})_{L_{\tau}(\Omega)}\leq C(n+1)^{-\frac{s}{d}},\quad n\geq 0,

(7.13)

provided
\gamma
is sufficiently large. Indeed, let
\delta_{n}:=d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U_{\tau,q}^{s})_{L_{\tau}(\Omega)}
. We know that
\delta_{n}\asymp(n+1)^{-s/d}
,
n\geq 0
. If
f\in U_{q,\tau}^{s}
, let
g\in X_{n}
provide the bound

\|f-g\|_{L_{\tau}}\leq\delta_{n}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|g\|_{B_{q}^{s}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}\leq\gamma\|f\|_{B_{q}^{s}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}.

Then,
\|f-g\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f-g\|_{L_{\tau}(\Omega)}\leq C\delta_{n}
because
\Omega
is bounded. This proves (
7.12
) in this case.

Finally, we consider the case
\tau<p
. Let
s^{\prime}:=d(\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{p})
. In this case, we know from (
7.10
) that

B^{s}_{q}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))={\cal A}^{s/d}_{q}({\cal X},L_{\tau}(\Omega))~~\text{and}~~B^{s^{\prime}}_{1}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))={\cal A}^{s^{\prime}/d}_{1}({\cal X},L_{\tau}(\Omega))

(7.14)

with equivalent norms. Therefore, if
f\in U_{q,\tau}^{s}
, from part (ii) of
Theorem
6.5
for an appropriate
\gamma=\gamma(\tau,p,s,q)
, there is a function
g\in X_{n}
such that

\|f-g\|_{B^{s^{\prime}}_{1}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}\leq C(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})/d},\quad{\rm and}\quad\|g\|_{B^{s}_{q}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}\leq\gamma\|f\|_{B^{s}_{q}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}.

(7.15)

It is standard fact that the Besov space
B^{s^{\prime}}_{1}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))
embeds into
L_{p}
, i.e., that
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{B^{s^{\prime}}_{1}(L_{\tau}(\Omega))}
. This gives

\|f-g\|_{L_{p}}\leq C(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})/d}\leq C(n+1)^{-(\frac{s}{d}-(\frac{1}{\tau}-\frac{1}{p})_{+})},

(7.16)

which proves (
7.12
) in this last case.
\Box

7.4
Lower bounds for the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of Besov classes

Of course, the
\gamma
-constrained Kolmogorov widths of Besov classes in
L_{p}(\Omega)
are always larger than their Kolmogorov widths, i.e.,

d_{n}^{c,\gamma}(U^{s}_{\tau,q})_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\geq d_{n}(U^{s}_{\tau,q})_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

(7.17)

The Kolmogorov widths of many classical smoothness classes are known (see Chapters 13 and 14 of
[
33
]
, or Chapter VII or
[
38
]
).
These unconstrained widths match the upper bound
in Theorem
7.1
when
p\leq\tau
or when
1\leq\tau,p\leq 2
(see for instance Theorem 3.8 in Chapter 14 in
[
33
]
for the one dimensional case and Lipschitz spaces). Outside of this regime, probabilistic methods are necessary to construct optimal Kolmogorov spaces
[
27
]
and we do not know the constrained widths in these cases. We leave this as an interesting open problem.

8.1
Proof of Proposition
4.5

We want to to show that if
\Omega
is a compact topological space and
K
is a compact, centrally symmetric subset of
C(\Omega)
, then we have

d_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}\leq\delta_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}=d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)},\quad n\geq 1,

where
d^{n}
and
\delta_{n}
denote the Gelfand and linear widths, respectively. Since the left inequality is trivial and we also clearly have
d^{n}(K)_{X}\leq\delta_{n}(K)_{X}
for any compact
K\subset X
in any Banach space
X
(see for instance
[
38
]
or Chapter 13 in
[
33
]
), we need only prove that

\delta_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}\leq d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)},\quad n\geq 1.

(8.1)

To prove (
8.1
), we fix
n\geq 1
and let
\delta>0
be arbitrary and fixed. Let

\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in C(\Omega)^{*}
be chosen to realize the Gelfand widths up to error
\delta
, i.e.,

\sup\{\|f\|_{C(\Omega)}:~f\in K~\text{and}~\lambda_{i}(f)=0,\ i=1,\dots,n\}\leq d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}+\delta.

(8.2)

Consider the continuous map
E:K\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
given by

E(f,x):=(\lambda_{1}(f),...,\lambda_{n}(f),f(x)).

(8.3)

For each
x\in\Omega
, the image

E(K,x):=\{E(f,x):~f\in K\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

(8.4)

is a compact, convex, centrally symmetric subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
and let
z:=(d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}+\delta+\epsilon)e_{n+1}
. Observe that by (
8.2
) it follows that
z\notin E(K,x)
. We define

z(x):=\arg\min_{y\in E(K,x)}\|y-z\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}

(8.5)

to be the closest point in
E(K,x)
to
z
. This point exists and is unique since
E(K,x)
is compact and convex.
It follows relatively easily from this that
z(x)
is a continuous function of
x
.

Next, we observe that for every
x\in\Omega
we have

z(x)\cdot e_{n+1}<z\cdot e_{n+1}.

(8.6)

Indeed, since
0\in E(K,x)
for every
x\in\Omega
, by convexity we have that
(0-z(x))\cdot(z-z(x))\leq 0
, and therefore

0\leq z(x)\cdot(z-z(x))=(z\cdot e_{n+1}-z(x)\cdot e_{n+1})(z(x)\cdot e_{n+1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z(x)\cdot e_{i})^{2}.

(8.7)

Since
z(x)\cdot e_{n+1}\geq 0
(since
0\in E(K,x)
would otherwise be closer to
z
) and
z(x)\neq z
(since
z\notin E(K,x)
), (
8.6
) follows from (
8.7
).
Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle(z-z(x))\cdot z(x)\leq((z-z(x))\cdot e_{n+1})(z(x)\cdot e_{n+1}).

(8.8)

For
i=1,...,n
, we now define the functions

f_{i}(x):=\frac{(z-z(x))\cdot e_{i}}{(z-z(x))\cdot e_{n+1}},\quad x\in\Omega.

(8.9)

Note that
f_{i}\in C(\Omega)
since the denominator never vanishes and
z(x)
is a continuous function of
x
.
We next show that the linear operator
{\cal L}:C(\Omega)\to{\rm span}\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
, given by

{\cal L}(f):=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(f)f_{i}\,\,\in C(\Omega)

is a good approximation to
f\in K
. First, for any
f\in K
and
x\in\Omega
, we calculate

\displaystyle f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(f)f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(z-z(x))\cdot e_{n+1}}(z-z(x))\cdot\left(f(x)e_{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(f)e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(z-z(x))\cdot e_{n+1}}(z-z(x))\cdot E(f,x).

Since
E(f,x)\in E(K,x)
, it follows that
(z-z(x))\cdot(E(f,x)-z(x))\leq 0
. Hence, we get

f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(f)f_{i}(x)\leq\frac{(z-z(x))\cdot z(x)}{(z-z(x))\cdot e_{n+1}}\leq z(x)\cdot e_{n+1}<z\cdot e_{n+1}=d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}+\delta+\epsilon,

(8.10)

where we have used (
8.8
) and (
8.6
).
Applying the exact same argument to
-f\in K
, we get

\left|f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(f)f_{i}(x)\right|\leq d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}+\delta+\epsilon,\quad x\in\Omega,\,f\in K.

(8.11)

Hence,

\sup_{f\in K}\left\|f-{\cal L}(f)\right\|_{C(\Omega)}\leq d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}+\delta+\epsilon.

(8.12)

Since
\delta>0
and
\epsilon>0
were arbitrary, this implies
\delta_{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}\leq d^{n}(K)_{C(\Omega)}
, as desired.

\Box

8.2
Jackson and Bernstein inequalities for approximation spaces

In this section, we prove the following Jackson and Bernstein inequalities that were used in the proof of (
6.24
). Let
s>s^{\prime}>0
and
X_{0}={\cal A}_{q^{\prime}}^{s^{\prime}}
and
Y_{0}:={\cal A}^{s}_{q}
.

Jackson inequality
: There is a constant
C>0
, depending only on
s,s^{\prime},q,q^{\prime}
such that for each
f\in Y_{0}
we have

E_{n}(f)_{X_{0}}\leq C(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})}\|f\|_{Y_{0}},\quad n\geq 0.

(8.13)

Proof:

Since
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal A}_{\infty}^{s}}\leq C_{0}\|\cdot\|_{{\cal A}_{q}^{s}}
,
0<q<\infty
(see Lemma
6.3
), it is enough to prove this when
Y_{0}={\cal A}_{\infty}^{s}
.
Similarly, we only have to consider values of
q^{\prime}
that are
0<q^{\prime}<\infty
. Let
f\in U({\cal A}_{\infty}^{s})
and for each
k=0,1,\dots
, let
g_{k}
be a best approximation to
f
from

X_{k}
in the
X
quasi-norm. Then,
\|f-g_{k}\|_{X}\leq(k+1)^{-s}
,
k\geq 0
. It follows that

E_{k}(f-g_{n})_{X}\leq(n+1)^{-s},\quad 0\leq k\leq n,

(8.14)

and

E_{k}(f-g_{n})_{X}\leq\|f-g_{n}-(g_{k}-g_{n})\|_{X}=\|f-g_{k}\|_{X}\leq(k+1)^{-s},\ k>n.

(8.15)

Hence,

\|f-g_{n}\|^{q^{\prime}}_{X_{0}}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}((k+1)^{s^{\prime}q^{\prime}-1}[\max\{k+1,n+1\}]^{-sq^{\prime}}\leq C(n+1)^{-(s-s^{\prime})q^{\prime}},

(8.16)

as desired.
\Box

Bernstein inequality
: If
g\in X_{n}
, then

\|g\|_{Y_{0}}\leq C\|g\|_{X_{0}}(n+1)^{s-s^{\prime}},\quad n\geq 0,

(8.17)

where
C
depend on
s,s^{\prime}q,q^{\prime}
.

Proof:
It is enough to prove this when
X_{0}={\cal A}_{\infty}^{s^{\prime}}
and
q
is small. Let
g\in X_{n}
. Then, we have
E_{k}(g)_{X}=0
,
k\geq n

E_{k}(g)_{X}\leq\|g\|_{X_{0}}(k+1)^{-s^{\prime}}
,

. It follows that

\|g\|^{q}_{{\cal A}_{q}^{s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}[(k+1)^{s}E_{k}(g)_{X}]^{q}(k+1)^{-1}\leq\|g\|_{X_{0}}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{q(s-s^{\prime})-1}\leq C\|g\|_{X_{0}}^{q}(n+1)^{q(s-s^{\prime})},
