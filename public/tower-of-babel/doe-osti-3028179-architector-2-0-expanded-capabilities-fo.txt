Architector 2.0: Expanded Capabilities for Metal Complex Engineering

Michael G. Taylor+, Garrett D. Santis*, Thomas J. Summers*, Enrique R. Batista, Danny Perez,
Ping Yang+

Abstract

Automated three-dimensional molecular construction from two-dimensional graph
representations is critical to high-throughput discovery eIorts. Software capabilities in this
area have accelerated research across ﬁelds ranging from protein design and drug discovery
to transition metal catalyst development. When Architector was ﬁrst introduced, it uniquely
enabled high-throughput, chemically relevant three-dimensional construction of f-element
complexes. Since its introduction, Architector has been applied in large-scale
computational campaigns, targeted studies in critical mineral extraction, and artiﬁcial
intelligence-driven discovery eIorts.

Here, we present expanded capabilities developed since the initial release of Architector.
These include secondary solvation shell sampling, accessible and robust three-dimensional
functionalization routines, and a graphical user interface (GUI) that exposes key
construction parameters and enables interactive visualization of complexes for a broader
user base. Across these developments, we demonstrate scientiﬁc applications in critical
mineral extraction and highly coordinated metal systems.

Introduction

Metal complexes, or coordination compounds, represent a highly diverse class of chemical
systems spanning the periodic table and enabling applications in catalysis, separations, and
pharmaceutical chemistry. Despite their importance, computational construction of three-
dimensional (3D) structures for coordination complexes has historically lagged that of
organic molecules. This challenge arises from increased symmetry complexity, particularly
in systems exceeding six coordination sites, and a relative lack of dedicated software tools.

Accurate 3D structural embeddings are essential for evaluating chemical properties through
potential energy surface exploration using methods such as density functional theory,
machine-learned interatomic potentials, and semi-empirical approaches. EIicient and
chemically meaningful generation of these structures therefore remains a central bottleneck
in computational inorganic chemistry.

Architector was developed to address this challenge by enabling high-throughput
construction of chemically realistic metal complexes. In this work, we extend its capabilities
to include (1) eIicient 3D functionalization of existing complexes, (2) sampling of second-

shell coordination environments, and (3) a graphical user interface that lowers the barrier to
entry for non-expert users and facilitates interactive exploration of generated structures.

Methods

3D Functionalization

We developed a method, functionalized_3D, to construct metal–ligand complexes by
leveraging existing three-dimensional structures from high-level calculations and modifying
only localized regions of the molecular system. This approach preserves core geometries
while enabling systematic exploration of ligand substitutions.

To evaluate this method, we investigated the separation of Nd(III) from Eu(III) using
functionalized BTP and terpyridine-based complexes. A total of 56 extractants were
considered. Initial 1:3 lanthanide complexes with a reference ligand (LRef) were generated
using the build_complex method (De Novo Architector). Structures from Nd(III) and Eu(III)
systems were combined into a dataset of 25 unique complexes and optimized using the UMA
machine learning potential.

Subsequently, 1:3 complexes for the remaining extractants were generated using both 3D
functionalization and De Novo Architector. Calculations were performed in serial on a single
compute node (details to be speciﬁed), although the functionalization workﬂow is trivially
parallelizable. Ligand modiﬁcations were introduced through multiple strategies, including
accessible solvent area (ASA) addition, radial addition, and scripted transformations.

All generated structures were optimized to a maximum force threshold of 0.02 eV/Å using the
UMA 1.2 potential. For comparison, additional structures were generated manually using the
Avogadro graphical user interface, allowing benchmarking of automated versus manual
workﬂows.

A subset of Eu(III) and Am(III) complexes was further reﬁned at a higher level of theory.
Structures generated via De Novo Architector employed lanthanide–actinide substitution
and UFF-based initial geometries. Functionalized structures preserved metal-core
geometries while allowing constrained relaxation of ligand environments. Final
optimizations were performed using density functional theory in implicit solvent.

Second Shell Sampling

To capture eIects beyond the primary coordination environment, Architector was extended
to support construction and sampling of second-shell interactions.

Reference structures for both cationic and anionic lanthanide complexes were ﬁrst
generated and optimized. These reference geometries were then propagated across the
lanthanide series via metal substitution and subsequent relaxation.

Second-shell conﬁgurations were constructed by placing outer-sphere ions around ﬁrst-
shell complexes using a spiral sampling algorithm. Multiple candidate structures were
generated and optimized, followed by cross-metal resampling to reduce bias in accessible
conformational space. This workﬂow enabled the rapid generation and optimization of
thousands of metal–ligand structures spanning the lanthanide series.

Graphical User Interface (GUI)

A major expansion in Architector 2.0 is the introduction of a graphical user interface (GUI)
designed to broaden accessibility and improve usability.

Key GUI capabilities include:

 • Interactive construction of metal complexes from user-deﬁned inputs

 • Exposure of core Architector parameters, including coordination number,
ligand placement strategies, and stochastic sampling controls

 • Real-time visualization of generated 3D structures, enabling rapid inspection
of coordination environments

This interface reduces reliance on command-line workﬂows and enables researchers to
rapidly prototype structures, inspect geometries, and reﬁne inputs in an interactive
environment.

Results

3D Functionalization

Complexes were generated using both 3D functionalization and De Novo Architector.
Generation times were evaluated on a per-structure basis for functionalization and as
averaged values for De Novo workﬂows.

3D functionalization achieved approximately a fourfold speedup relative to De Novo
Architector, largely due to reuse of pre-constructed core geometries. Compared to manual
construction using a GUI-based molecular editor, the functionalization approach yielded an
approximate twenty-ﬁve-fold speedup.

Certain highly substituted ligands posed challenges due to failures in structural sanity
checks. In these cases, relaxed criteria enabled generation of initial structures that could

subsequently converge during optimization. Functionalization proved more robust than De
Novo construction for these diIicult systems.

Optimization using the UMA potential required approximately 65% fewer steps for
functionalized structures relative to De Novo structures. This improvement was most
pronounced for small perturbations to the parent ligand and diminished for more extensive
structural modiﬁcations.

Both approaches sampled similar regions of the potential energy surface, with increasing
divergence observed for highly ﬂexible ligands. Functionalization was limited by its
dependence on prior conformations, while De Novo construction struggled to
simultaneously sample ligand ﬂexibility and core geometry. A combined strategy is therefore
recommended for comprehensive sampling.

For actinide systems, functionalized structures required approximately 60% fewer
optimization steps and exhibited improved convergence behavior. These gains are
particularly signiﬁcant given the high computational cost of ab initio methods.

Application of the functionalization workﬂow to para-substituted ligands revealed
systematic trends in selectivity. Electron-withdrawing groups increased selectivity for Nd
over Eu, while electron-donating groups increased selectivity for Am over Eu, highlighting
diIerences in lanthanide versus actinide behavior.

Second Shell Sampling

Second-shell sampling enabled construction of thousands of structures across the
lanthanide series, capturing both inner- and outer-sphere coordination motifs.

Although absolute reaction energies were dominated by electrostatic contributions, relative
trends revealed two distinct stability regimes across the series. Later lanthanides showed
greater stabilization of outer-sphere nitrate coordination, while earlier lanthanides favored
inner-sphere binding.

The transition between these regimes occurred near the Gd/Tb boundary, consistent with
experimentally observed trends. This result demonstrates that Architector can reproduce
chemically meaningful changes in coordination behavior across a series using automated
structure generation and sampling.

Conclusion and Discussion

Architector 2.0 introduces signiﬁcant advances in the automated construction and
exploration of metal complexes. The addition of 3D functionalization enables rapid
modiﬁcation of existing structures while preserving chemically relevant geometries, leading

to substantial gains in eIiciency and robustness. Second-shell sampling extends the scope
of accessible chemical space by incorporating outer-sphere interactions that inﬂuence
stability and reactivity.

Importantly, the introduction of a graphical user interface transforms Architector from a
primarily programmatic tool into an interactive platform. By exposing key parameters and
enabling real-time visualization, the GUI broadens accessibility to non-expert users while
accelerating iterative design workﬂows for experienced practitioners.

Together, these developments position Architector as a versatile and scalable platform for
studying complex coordination chemistry, with direct applications in critical mineral
separations, catalyst design, and data-driven materials discovery.
