Topological algebra of symplectic geometry of symmetric powers
Vivek Shende, Peng Zhou

Abstract
To a noncompact orientable surface with no closed boundary, we associate the sum of Fukaya categories of (Liouville sectors associated to) its symmetric powers. We construct sectorial covers with the combinatorics of the bar resolution to show this association extends to an open 2d topological field theory – without naming a Lagrangian, let alone a holomorphic disk. In particular, we recover results of Rouquier and Manion on extending Heegaard-Floer theory down to an interval.

Definition 1.1

Clustering rules

One fundamental technical tool of the present article is a system of coordinates on symmetric products which allow us to separate dependence on the normal and tangential directions to the big diagonal. Were the diagonal smooth (it is not), it would suffice to fix a metric and choose a tubular neighborhood.
Let us elaborate on the basic challenge. Given a point on the big diagonal,
\mathbf{z}=\sum n_{i}z_{i}
with
n_{i}>1
, we might consider a class of nearby configurations

\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
, with
|\mathbf{z}_{i}|=n_{i}
and
\mathbf{z}_{i}
centered at
z_{i}
. It is natural to view variation of the
\mathbf{z}_{i}
as tangential to the diagonal, and internal variation constituents of
\mathbf{z}_{i}
(constrainted to remain centered at
z_{i}
) as normal to the diagonal.
Of course, each
\mathbf{z}
admits many expressions of the form

\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
. In this section we will describe a certain class of such decompositions (clusterings), in order to define a class of functions on symmetric powers, which depend only on the center of masses of clusterings.

We proceed with the construction.
Let
M
be a smooth Riemannian manifold with distance function
\dist:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
. Recall that a subset
U\subset M
is said to geodesically convex if any two points in
U
can be connected by a unique geodesic, and the distance square function
\dist(-,x)^{2}
restricts to any length-parametrized geodesic is a convex function.
The radius of convexity at a point
p
is the radius of the largest geodesically convex open ball around
p
; we write
r_{conv}
for the infimum over
M
of the convexity radii. We refer to
[
44
, Ch 5.4]
for properties of the convexity radius. We will assume that
r_{conv}>0
, which will hold for our examples of interest because Liouville manifolds have bounded geometry.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k})\in\Sym^{k}M
, if
\dist(z_{i},z_{j})<r_{conv}
, we say
\mathbf{z}
is
pre-confined
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\Sym^{n}(M)
be pre-confined. Let
g:M\to\mathbb{R}
be the continuous function given by

g(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dist(z_{i},z)^{2}.

Fix

\max_{i,j}(\dist(z_{i},z_{j}))<\rho<r_{conv}
.
Let

.

Proof.

Each ball

is strongly geodesically convex, hence their

is geodesically convex as well: if

, then for each

the unique minimizing geodesic from

to

lies in

, hence
it lies in
U
. The functions

is smooth and strictly (geodesic) convex, hence their sum

is strictly convex. Thus

can have at most one minimum.

Since
\overline{U}
is compact and

is continuous,

attains its minimum

. We claim that

. Suppose instead that

. Then for

, so

.

For pre-confined
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{n}M
, the center of mass of
\mathbf{z}
is the minimum point produced in Lemma
2.2
; we denote it
c(\mathbf{z})
. We write
r(\mathbf{z}):=\max_{i}\dist(z_{i},c(\mathbf{z}))
.

When
V
is a normed vector space,
r_{conv}=\infty
, every
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{n}M
is pre-confined, and the center of mass is the weighted average of the
\{z_{i}\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
clustering rule
is two sequences of positive real numbers,

0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\cdots<r_{N}\ll r_{conv},\quad 0=d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots<d_{N}\ll r_{conv}

such that

r_{k}>d_{k}+r_{k-1},\quad d_{k}>6r_{k-1}.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
a,b\geq 1
, we have

r_{a+b}>d_{a+b}+\max(r_{a},r_{b}),\quad d_{a+b}>3(r_{a}+r_{b})

Lemma 2.6
.

Clustering rules exist.

Proof.

We may choose
d_{2}>0
,
\epsilon>0
, and set
r_{1}=d_{2}/(6(1+\epsilon))
. Then, for
k=2,3,\cdots
, we set

r_{k}=(1+\epsilon)(d_{k}+r_{k-1}),\quad d_{k+1}=(1+\epsilon)6r_{k}.

∎

Definition 2.7
.

Fix a clustering rule. We say
\mathbf{z}
is
confined
if it is pre-confined and
r(\mathbf{z})<r_{|\mathbf{z}|}
. We say
\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}
are
separated
if they are pre-confined and
\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{1}),c(\mathbf{z}_{2}))>d_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}|+|\mathbf{z}_{2}|}
.
We say
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
is a cluster decomposition if each
\mathbf{z}_{i}
is confined and each pair
\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{z}_{j}
are separated. We say the
type
of the decomposition is the partition

|\mathbf{z}|=\sum|\mathbf{z}_{i}|
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}
are confined, but not separated. Then
\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}
is confined.

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle r(\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2})

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}}\dist(z,c(\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}))=\max_{i=1,2}\max_{z\in\mathbf{z}_{i}}\dist(z,c(\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}))

\displaystyle<\max_{i=1,2}\max_{z\in\mathbf{z}_{i}}\dist(z,c(\mathbf{z}_{i}))+\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{i}),c(\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}))

\displaystyle<\max(r_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}|},r_{|\mathbf{z}_{2}|})+d_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}|+|\mathbf{z}_{2}|}

\displaystyle<r_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}|}

∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Every
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{n}(M)
admits a cluster decomposition.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{z}=\sum n_{i}z_{i}
, begin with the decomposition
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
where
\mathbf{z}_{i}=n_{i}z_{i}
.
Iteratively merge pairs
\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{z}_{j}
which are confined but not separated; at each stage, by Lemma
2.8
, we maintain a decomposition into pieces which are confined. By finiteness of
|\mathbf{z}|
the procedure terminates (possibly at
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}
if
\mathbf{z}
itself is confined).
∎

Let
\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}
be two sets of points in
M
. We define their
separation
as

\sep(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}):=\min_{z\in\mathbf{z}_{1},w\in\mathbf{z}_{2}}\dist(z,w).

Lemma 2.10
.

For any cluster decomposition
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
, we have

\sep(\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{z}_{j})>2(r_{|\mathbf{z}_{i}|}+r_{|\mathbf{z}_{j}|})

Proof.

For any
z\in\mathbf{z}_{i},w\in\mathbf{z}_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\dist(z,w)

\displaystyle>\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{i}),c(\mathbf{z}_{j}))-\dist(z,c(\mathbf{z}_{i}))-\dist(w,c(\mathbf{z}_{j}))

\displaystyle>d_{|\mathbf{z}_{i}|+|\mathbf{z}_{j}|}-r_{|\mathbf{z}_{i}|}-r_{|\mathbf{z}_{j}|}

\displaystyle>2r_{|\mathbf{z}_{i}|}+2r_{|\mathbf{z}_{j}|}.

∎

In particular, the disks around the centers of clusters which witness the confinement are disjoint. Even more particularly: if
\mathbf{z}=\sum n_{i}z_{i}
is the sum with multiplicities, then if
z_{i}
appears in some cluster in a cluster decomposition, said cluster in fact contains
n_{i}z_{i}
. Because of this, if
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
are clusters in possibly different cluster decompositions of some given
\mathbf{z}
, it makes unambiguous sense to write
\mathbf{x}\cap\mathbf{y}
,
\mathbf{x}\setminus\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\setminus\mathbf{x}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
are clusters in possibly different cluster decompositions of
\mathbf{z}
. Then either
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
are disjoint, or one contains the other.

Proof.

The assertion is equivalent to asking that not all three of
\mathbf{x}\setminus\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\setminus\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\cap\mathbf{y}
are all nonempty.

Suppose
\mathbf{x}\setminus\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{x}\cap\mathbf{y}
are both nonempty. We have:

2r_{|\mathbf{x}|}>2r(\mathbf{x})>\sep(\mathbf{x}\setminus\mathbf{y},\mathbf{x}\cap\mathbf{y})\geq\sep(\mathbf{x}\setminus\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y})>2r_{|\mathbf{y}|},

where we have used Lemma
2.10
for the final inequality.

Similarly, if
\mathbf{y}\setminus\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}\cap\mathbf{y}
are both nonempty, then
2r_{|\mathbf{y}|}>2r_{|\mathbf{x}|}
.
Thus if all three are nonempty, we arrive at a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Any two cluster decompositions of a given
\mathbf{z}
have a common coarsening.

Proof.

Suppose given cluster decompositions
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{x}_{i}=\sum\mathbf{y}_{j}
. Let
\mathbf{w}_{k}
be the maximal elements for containment amongst the
\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}
. By Lemma
2.11
, in fact
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{w}_{k}
. Since the
\mathbf{w}_{k}
were among the
\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j}
, they are confined; in case they are not separated, we may iteratively apply Lemma
2.8
to eventually obtain the desired coarsening.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Any two cluster decompositions of a given
\mathbf{z}
have a common refinement. In particular, there is a finest cluster decomposition.

Proof.

Suppose given cluster decompositions
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{x}_{i}=\sum\mathbf{y}_{j}
. The claim is that
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{y}_{j}

is a cluster decomposition.
The result is obvious if one of the
\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\mathbf{y}_{j}
decomposition is a refinement of the other; so suppose otherwise.

By Lemma
2.11
, each
\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{y}_{j}
is either
\mathbf{x}_{i}
or
\mathbf{y}_{j}
or empty, hence in particular satisfies confinement.
We check the separation constraint for the
\mathbf{x}_{i}\cap\mathbf{y}_{j}
. Of course it holds already for pairs of clusters from
\mathbf{x}
or
\mathbf{y}
. The remaining possibility is that there is say
\mathbf{x}_{1}\subsetneq\mathbf{y}_{1^{\prime}}
and
\mathbf{x}_{2}\supsetneq\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}
.
We compute:

\dist(c(\mathbf{x}_{1}),c(\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}))>\dist(c(\mathbf{x}_{1}),c(\mathbf{x}_{2}))-\dist(c(\mathbf{x}_{2}),c(\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}))>d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2}|}-r_{|\mathbf{x}_{2}|}>d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2}|}-r_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{x}_{2}|-1}.

Then using
d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{2}|}>3r_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{x}_{2}|-1}
and
r_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{x}_{2}|-1}>d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{x}_{2}|-1}
, we have

\dist(c(\mathbf{x}_{1}),c(\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}))|>2d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{x}_{2}|-1}\geq 2d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}|}>d_{|\mathbf{x}_{1}|+|\mathbf{y}_{2^{\prime}}|}.

∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose
\mathbf{z}_{1}
and
\mathbf{z}_{2}
are each confined, and
(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2})
is separated. Then for any confined
\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{z}_{1}
and
\mathbf{z}_{2}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{z}_{2}
, the pair
(\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{z}_{2}^{\prime})
is separated.

Proof.

Suppose not. Then:

d_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime}|+|\mathbf{z}_{2}^{\prime}|}>\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime}),c(\mathbf{z}_{2}^{\prime}))\geq\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{1}),c(\mathbf{z}_{2}))-\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{1}),c(\mathbf{z}_{1}^{\prime}))-\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{2}),c(\mathbf{z}_{2}^{\prime}))>d_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}|+|\mathbf{z}_{2}|}-r_{|\mathbf{z_{1}}|}-r_{|\mathbf{z}_{2}|}

This is a contradiction (recall
d_{k}>6r_{k-1}>6d_{k-1}
).
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

If
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i}
is a cluster decomposition, and
\mathbf{z}_{i}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i,\alpha}
are cluster decompositions, then
\mathbf{z}=\sum\mathbf{z}_{i,\alpha}
is a cluster decomposition.

More generally:

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a contraction, i.e.
\dist(f(z_{1}),f(z_{2}))\leq\dist(z_{1},z_{2})
for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in M
. Let
f:\Sym^{n}M\to\Sym^{n}N
be the natural extension. Suppose given
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{n}M
and a (not necessarily cluster) decomposition
\mathbf{z}=\sum_{i}\mathbf{z}_{i}
. If
f(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i}f(\mathbf{z}_{i})
is a cluster decomposition, and
\mathbf{z}_{i}=\sum_{\alpha}\mathbf{z}_{i,\alpha}
are cluster decompositions, then
\mathbf{z}=\sum_{i,\alpha}\mathbf{z}_{i,\alpha}

is a cluster decomposition.

For any partition
\tau
, we write
\Clus^{\tau}(M)\subset\Sym^{n}(M)
for the (open) subset of elements admitting a type
\tau
decomposition. We write
\Clus^{\leq\tau}(M)=\cup_{\sigma\leq\tau}\Clus^{\tau}(M)
.

Proposition 2.17
.

For any partition
\tau
,
\Sym^{\tau}(M)\subset\Clus^{\leq\tau}(M)
.

Proof.

Given any
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym V
, we initialize a candidate cluster decomposition, where only coincidental points form a cluster. This initial candidate satisfies confinement constraints, but possibly not the separation constraint.
We will iteratively merge clusters if they are not separated, i.e.
\dist(c(\mathbf{z}_{1}),c(\mathbf{z}_{2}))<d_{|\mathbf{z}_{1}|+|\mathbf{z}_{2}|}
; per Lemma
2.8
, the result consists again of a decomposition into candidate clusters which are all confined.
Eventually we arrive at a cluster decomposition of a type obtained by merging parts of
\tau
, i.e. of a type
\leq\tau
.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

\Sym^{\leq\tau}(M)\subset\Clus^{\leq\tau}(M)
.

Lemma 2.19
.

If
(r_{i},d_{i})
is a set of clustering rules, then there exists another set of clustering rules
(r_{i}^{\prime},d_{i}^{\prime})
, such that
r_{i}^{\prime}>r_{i}
and
d_{i}^{\prime}<d_{i}
for all
i
.

Proof.

For any fixed
k
, if we fix all parameters
(r_{i},d_{i})
except
r_{k}
, there are finitely many open constraints for
r_{k}
, e.g.,

r_{k}>d_{k}+r_{k-1},\quad r_{k+1}>d_{k+1}+r_{k},\quad d_{k+1}>6r_{k}.

Similarly, if we fix all other parameters except
d_{k}
, there are only finitely many open constraints for
d_{k}
. Thus, we can initialize
r_{i}^{\prime}=r_{i},d_{i}^{\prime}=d_{i}
, then adjust them iteratively one by one for
i=1,2,\cdots
, such that
r_{i}^{\prime}>r_{i}
and
d_{i}^{\prime}<d_{i}
for all
i
.
∎

Definition 2.20
.

Fix a clustering rule. A function
f:\Sym^{n}(M)\to X
is a
center of mass function
if there exist functions
f_{\tau}:\Sym^{\tau}(M)\to X
such that for any cluster decomposition

\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{1}+\ldots+\mathbf{z}_{k}
of type
\tau
, we have

f(\mathbf{z})=f_{\tau}(|\mathbf{z}_{1}|c(\mathbf{z}_{1})+\ldots+|\mathbf{z}_{k}|c(\mathbf{z}_{k}))

Note the definition is local on
\Sym^{n}(M)
, so makes sense for functions defined only on some open subset, and indeed such functions form a sheaf. One obvious nonconstant such global function is the total center of mass. We will construct some useful center of mass functions in Lemma
4.10
below.

Remark 2.21
.

Continuous functions on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
which pull back to holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
are already holomorphic on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
(fundamental theorem of symmetric functions), but continuous functions on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
which pull back to smooth functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
need not be smooth. However, a continuous function on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
which is a center of mass function and which pulls back to a smooth function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is already smooth on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
.

3
Kähler potentials

In this section we construct Kähler potentials on symmetric products of surfaces which are well adapted to studying gluing configurations.

The smoothing of Kähler potential on
\Sym^{n}\Sigma
for smooth affine
\Sigma
was considered by Perutz
[
43
, Section 7]
, based on smoothing and patching technique of Richberg
[
45
]
and Varouchas
[
50
]
. Here we use the same tools but exert more control of the smoothing over strip-like regions
T^{*}[-2,2]\subset\Sigma

3.1
Recollections on smoothing plurisubharmonic functions

Recall that a
smooth function
f
is said to be (strictly) subharmonic if

\Delta f\geq 0
(resp.
\Delta f>0
).
8
8

8

Many texts allow also upper semi-continuous
f
, where Laplacian is understood in a distributional sense, ie,
\int\Delta f\cdot g\geq 0
for all compactly supported non-negative test functions
g
. Here we will only need continuous psh functions.

A function on a complex manifold,
\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
, is said to be (strictly) plurisubharmonic for any holomorphic map from a 1-complex-dimensional disk,
i:D\to X
, the pullback
i^{*}\phi
is (strictly) subharmonic. Note that if
\pi:X\to Y
is any finite map of complex manifolds and
\phi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
X
, then
\pi_{*}\phi
is strictly plurisubharmonic on
Y
(we will later apply this to
\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
).

In case
\phi
is smooth, subharmonicity is equivalent to asking that the complex Hessian
\omega_{\phi}:=i\partial\bar{\partial}\phi
is positive semidefinite, and strict plurisubharmonicity
to positive definiteness. A smooth strictly plurisubharmonic function is referred to as a Kähler potential. A discussion of plurisubharmonic functions in the context of symplectic geometry can be found in
[
10
]
. If we define
d^{c}=(\partial-\bar{\partial})/(2i)
, then
dd^{c}=i\partial\bar{\partial}
, and we get

\lambda=d^{c}\phi,\quad\omega=d\lambda=dd^{c}\phi=i\partial\bar{\partial}\phi.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be a continuous
strictly plurisubharmonic function on
X
.
We say
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in(0,t_{0})}
is a smoothing of
\phi
, if for each
t\in(0,t_{0})
,
\varphi_{t}
is smooth strictly plurisubharmonic on
X
and
\|\varphi_{n,t}-\phi_{n}\|_{C^{0}(X)}<t
.

A theorem of Richberg asserts the existences of psh smoothings of continuous psh functions
[
45
]
:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
45
]
,

[
14
, Thm 5.21]
)
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{Psh}(X)
be a continuous function which is strictly
plurisubharmonic on an open subset
\Omega\subset X
, with

Hu\geq\gamma

for some continuous positive Hermitian form
\gamma
on
\Omega
.
For any continuous function
\lambda\in C^{0}(\Omega)
with
\lambda>0
,
there exists a plurisubharmonic function

\tilde{u}\in C^{0}(X)\cap C^{\infty}(\Omega)

such that

u\leq\tilde{u}\leq u+\lambda\quad\text{on }\Omega,

and
\tilde{u}=u
on
X\setminus\Omega
. Moreover,
\tilde{u}
is
strictly plurisubharmonic on
\Omega
and satisfies

H\tilde{u}\geq(1-\lambda)\gamma.

In particular,
\tilde{u}
can be chosen strictly plurisubharmonic on
X

if
u
has the same property.

The regularized max function is useful for patching:

Lemma 3.3
.

[
14
, Lemma 5.18]

Let
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
be a nonnegative function with support in
[-1,1]
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(h)\,dh=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\,\theta(h)\,dh=0
.
For arbitrary
\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{p})\in(0,+\infty)^{p}

M_{\eta}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\max\{t_{1}+h_{1},\ldots,t_{p}+h_{p}\}\prod_{1\leq j\leq p}\theta(h_{j}/\eta_{j})\,dh_{1}\cdots dh_{p}

(5)

possesses the following properties:

is non decreasing in all variables, smooth and convex on

2.

\max\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}\}\leq M_{\eta}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\leq\max\{t_{1}+\eta_{1},\ldots,t_{p}+\eta_{p}\}

4.

if

If

The following is a variant of a lemma of Varouchas

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold,
\phi
a continuous strictly plurisubharmonic function on
X

X=V\cup W
be an open cover,
W^{\prime}=X\backslash\bar{V}
and
V^{\prime}=X\backslash\bar{W}
. Let
\varphi^{V}_{t}
and
\varphi^{W}_{t}
be smoothing family of
\phi|_{V}
and
\phi|_{W}
. Then there exists a smoothing family
\varphi_{t}
on
X
, for small enough

, such that
\varphi_{t}=\varphi^{V}_{t}
on
V^{\prime}
and
\varphi_{t}=\varphi^{W}_{t}
on
W^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
1=\eta^{V}+\eta^{W}
be a smooth partition of unity, so that
\eta^{V}|_{V^{\prime}}=1
and
\eta^{W}|_{W^{\prime}}=1
.
For small enough
s
, and
t(s)
small enough depending on
s
, such that
t(s)<s/4
, and

s\eta^{V}+\varphi^{V}_{t(s)}
and
s\eta^{W}+\varphi^{W}_{t(s)}
are still strictly plurisubharmonic on
V
and

\varphi_{t(s)}(z)=\begin{cases}\varphi^{V}_{t(s)}&z\in\bar{V}^{\prime}\\
M_{s/4}(s\eta^{V}+\varphi^{V}_{t},s\eta^{W}+\varphi^{W}_{t})&z\in V\cap W\\
\varphi^{W}_{t(s)}&z\in\bar{W}^{\prime}\end{cases}

Near
\partial V
, we have

|(s\eta^{V}+\varphi^{V}_{t})-(s\eta^{W}+\varphi^{W}_{t})|=|(-s+\varphi^{V}_{t})-\varphi^{W}_{t}|>s-|\varphi^{V}_{t}-\varphi^{W}_{t}|>s-2t>s/2,

hence

(Lemma

smoothly transit to

3.2
Factorizable Kähler potentials on symmetric powers

Fix a real quadratic form
\phi:\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with positive trace.

Then
\phi
is a strictly plurisubharmonic function. Likewise the function
\phi_{n}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\mapsto\sum\phi(z_{i})
is strictly plurisubharmonic.
Evidently
\phi_{n}
descends to a continuous (not smooth) strictly plurisubharmonic function on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
, which we also denote
\phi_{n}
.

We have the following translation equivariance:

Lemma 3.5
.

\phi_{n}(\mathbf{z})=\phi_{n}(\mathbf{z}-c(\mathbf{z}))+|\mathbf{z}|\cdot\phi(c(\mathbf{z}))
.

Proof.

By the relation between quadratic and bilinear forms:

\phi(z_{i})-\phi(z_{i}-c(\mathbf{z}))-\phi(c(\mathbf{z}))=\langle z_{i}-c(\mathbf{z}),c(\mathbf{z})\rangle_{\phi}

Summing over
i
kills the RHS and gives the result.
∎

That is,
\phi_{n}
is the sum of functions on the factors under the splitting

\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\times\Sym^{n}_{0}\mathbb{C},\quad\mathbf{z}\mapsto(c(\mathbf{z}),\mathbf{z}-c(\mathbf{z})).

Here,
\Sym^{n}_{0}(\mathbb{C})\subset\Sym^{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the locus of points with center of mass at the origin.

Finally, we argue that
\phi_{n}
can be smoothed preserving strict plurisubharmonicity and also certain relevant structural properties:

Proposition 3.6
.

Fix a clustering rule. Then there exist positive numbers
\epsilon_{1}>\epsilon_{2}>\cdots
, and family of smooth strictly plurisubharmonic functions
\varphi_{n,t}
on
\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
,
t\in(0,\epsilon_{n})
, such that

1.

\varphi_{1,t}=\phi
, and
(\varphi_{n,t})_{t\in(0,\epsilon_{n})}
is a smoothing of
\phi_{n}
.

2.

For
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{n}\mathbb{C}
and a cluster decomposition
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{1}+\ldots+\mathbf{z}_{m}
, and
t\in(0,\epsilon_{n})
, we have

\varphi_{n,t}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{|\mathbf{z}_{i}|,t}(\mathbf{z}_{i}).

(6)

3.

\varphi_{n,t}(\mathbf{z})=\varphi_{n,t}(\mathbf{z}-c(\mathbf{z}))+|\mathbf{z}|\cdot\phi(c(\mathbf{z}))

Proof.

We will inductively construct
\varphi_{n,t}
. The case for
n=1
is done, we may set
\epsilon_{1}=1
for concreteness. Assume the cases for
n<n_{0}
are done, and we are considering
n=n_{0}
. For any

4.1

Fix

For

We say

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\mathbf{z}
is well contained in

, then for any decomposition
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{1}+\mathbf{z}_{2}+\cdots+\mathbf{z}_{m}

Proof.

Otherwise, we have

, which implies a contradiction:

∎

Lemma 4.2

For any
\mathbf{z}\in\Sym^{k}\mathbb{R}

\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{z}_{m}

\mathbf{z}

, and the

Proof.

For existence, we begin with the decomposition into one-point parts,

. Each fits into its own box, but the boxes may overlap. If two boxes overlap, we merge the corresponding summands. It is easy to check that the new collection of points are well-contained in its box. We repeat the process until no pair of boxes overlap anymore.

For uniqueness, if there are two such decompositions of

4.2
Recollections on Liouville structures

Given an exact symplectic manifold
(X,\omega=d\lambda)
, the
Liouville vector field

Z
is characterized by
\omega(Z,\cdot)=\lambda
. Then
X
is said to be
Liouville
if there is a compact submanifold-with-boundary
\overline{X}\subset X
such that
Z
is outwardly transverse to
\partial\overline{X}
and the flow of
Z
identifies
X\setminus\overline{X}
with a half-cylinder on
\partial\overline{X}
. In this case
\lambda|_{\partial\overline{X}}
is a contact form, and the corresponding contact manifold is canonically independent of the choice of
\overline{X}
; we correspondingly denote it
\partial_{\infty}X
and term it the
ideal contact boundary
of
X
. The submanifold
\overline{X}
is termed a
Liouville domain
with completion
X
.

We now recall from
[
20
,
22
]
the definitions and some properties of Liouville sectors.

Definition 4.12
.

[
20
]

Let
(X,\lambda)
be a Liouville manifold.
A closed codimension one smooth submanifold
H\subset X
is a
sectorial hypersurface
if there is a function
I:H\to\mathbb{R}
such that (1)
ZI=I
in the complement of a compact set and (2)
dI
is nonvanishing on the characteristic foliation of
H
.

Note that if
H=\{\rho=0\}
with
d\rho|_{H}
non-vanishing, then (2) above is equivalent to asking the Poisson bracket
\{\rho,I\}
to be nowhere zero.

Definition 4.13
.

[
22
, Def. 12.2]

A union of cleanly intersecting sectorial hypersurfaces
H_{i}
with characteristic foliations
\xi_{i}
is
sectorial
if (the functions
I_{i}
admit extensions to neighborhoods of the
H_{i}
so that)
the
\xi_{i}
are symplectically orthogonal over the intersections of the
H_{i}
, the
I_{i}
are in involution (
\{I_{i},I_{j}\}=0
), and
dI_{i}|_{\xi_{j}}=0
when

.

A model and motivating example is that if

is a cover of a smooth manifold by open sets with smooth transverse boundaries, with boundary-defining-functions

, then the

give a sectorial cover of

, with witness functions given e.g. by choosing a metric and taking

to be the function on the cotangent bundle given by evaluation of a covector at

.
