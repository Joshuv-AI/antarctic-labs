Abstract
. We establish quantitative stability estimates for the Trudinger–Moser inequality on smooth, bounded domains of the Euclidean plane. More specifically, we prove that the deficit in the Trudinger–Moser functional quadratically controls the distance to the set of optimizers if either (i) the exponential rate of growth is sufficiently small or (ii) the domain is a round disk. The latter estimate remains valid even in the critical case. Both proofs rely on a new spectral gap that we prove, which may be of independent interest. Additionally, we show the same stability estimate in the nondegenerate case, and show this occurs generically.

1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
be a natural number. The classical Sobolev embedding theorem
states that for each
q\in(1,n)
the Sobolev
space
W^{1,q}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
embeds into
L^{\frac{nq}{n-q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
. Trudinger
[
30
]

showed that this embedding fails in the critical case
q=n
, but that functions
in
W^{1,n}_{0}(\Omega)
are of exponential type for any bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}

with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. Moser
[
28
]
later
sharpened this result, determining the
best possible rate of exponential growth. The combination of these two
results is now widely known as the Trudinger–Moser inequality, which states

(1)

{\rm TM}(n,\alpha,\Omega)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{1,n}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathscr{E}_{n,\alpha,\Omega}(u)=\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\}<\infty\Leftrightarrow 0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{*}(n)=n\omega_{n-1}^{n-1}.

Here
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbf{S}^{n-1}|
is the surface measure of the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere. Observe
that we implicitly define the Trudinger–Moser functional
\mathscr{E}_{n,\alpha,\Omega}

in (
1
).

For any given
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
,
the set of extremal functions (
i.e.,
those functions realizing equality
in (
1
)) is nonempty. Carleson and Chang
[
6
]

demonstrated the existence of extremal functions in the case that
\Omega

is a two-dimensional disk. Subsequently, Flucher
[
17
]
extended
the existence result to any smooth, bounded domain in
\mathbf{R}^{2}
, and finally
Lin
[
24
]
proved the set of extremals is nonempty in
any dimension
n\geq 2
. However, the precise form of the extremal
functions remains unresolved, even in the case of the disk.

1.1.
Main results

In the present manuscript, we investigate the
stability of (
1
)
in dimension two. More precisely, we are interested in quantifying the statement
that if
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1

is close to realizing the supremum in (
1
), then it
must be close to an extremal function. We measure the distance to the set of
extremals as

(2)

{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u):=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad{\rm for}\quad u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is the set of functions realizing equality
in (
1
). Notice that we measure the distance in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

as the
L^{2}
-norm of the gradient; by the Poincaré inequality this is equivalent
to the standard
W^{1,2}
-norm. We also define the deficit

(3)

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u):={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)

and observe that in two dimensions the critical value of
\alpha

is
\alpha_{*}(2)=4\pi
. We denote the unit ball in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

by
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and the unit sphere in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
by
\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
once again with respect to the norm
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
; that is,

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<1\}

and the unit sphere

\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\}.

Note that, since
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}
is strictly increasing along radial
rescalings
u\mapsto u/\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, every optimizer satisfies

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, so that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}

(see (
11
) below).

Our first result states that when
\alpha
is sufficiently small, the deficit
is bounded below by the square of the distance.

Theorem 1

(Small subcritical case)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded set with a smooth boundary.
There exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for all
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, one can find
a constant
C_{*}=C_{*}({\alpha},\Omega)>0
such that

{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq C_{*}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}{\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.

In other words, one has

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\gtrsim_{\alpha,\Omega}{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)^{2}.

Our second main result replaces the small growth hypothesis in Theorem
1

with nondegeneracy.
We recall that the quantitative behavior of the functional near its maximizers
is governed by the second variation of
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}
on the unit sphere

\mathcal{S}_{1}
. We say that a maximizer
u_{*}
is
nondegenerate
if the second
variation of the Trudinger-Moser functional is coercive on
T_{u_{*}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
.
More precisely,
u_{*}
is nondegenerate if there exists
\mu>0
such that

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})[w,w]\geq\mu\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\mathrm{foreach}\quad w\in T_{u_{*}}(\mathcal{S}_{1}),

where
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})
is the quadratic form given by (
44
).

Theorem 2

(Nondegenerate subcritical case)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded set with a smooth boundary and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi)
.
If every optimizer is nondegenerate, then there exists
C^{*}=C^{*}(\alpha,\Omega)>0
such that

{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq C^{*}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}{\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.

In other words, one has

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq C^{*}{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)^{2}.

Moreover, there exists an open dense set of parameters
(\alpha,\Omega)
for which
all optimizers in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
are nondegenerate.

Our last main result is a quantitative stability estimate for the full range

0<\alpha\leq 4\pi
in the case that

\Omega=\mathbf{D}=\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{2}:|x|<1\}.

Theorem 3

(Symmetric critical case)
.

Let
\mathbf{D}\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be the open
(
unit
)
disk and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
There exists a constant
C_{\circ}=C_{\circ}(\alpha)>0
satisfying

{\rm TM}({\alpha},\mathbf{D})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)\geq C_{\circ}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{D})}{\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.

In other words, one has

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)\gtrsim_{\alpha}{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)^{2}.

1.2.
Comparison to other results

Historically, stability questions first arose in
the classical Sobolev inequality. Brezis and Lieb
[
3
]
first asked if one can
quantify the Sobolev inequality, and Bianchi and Egnell
[
2
]
proved this
is possible. Since then, many others have investigated the stability, both
qualitative and quantitative, of a variety of functional inequalities, including
higher-order Sobolev inequalities
[
26
,
10
]
, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev
and log Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequalities
[
5
]
,
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequalities for

p>2

[
16
]
,
the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg inequality
[
31
,
18
]
and
the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality
[
8
]
. Dolbeault, Esteban, Figalli, Frank, and
Loss
[
14
]
recently uncovered the sharp dimension dependence of the
stability constants in the Sobolev and log-Sobolev inequalities. Engelstein, Neumayer,
and Spolaor
[
15
]
extended the Bianchi–Egnell result to a more geometric
setting, proving a quantitative stability inequality for the conformal
Yamabe constant; see
[
1
]
for
an extension of the Engelstein, Neumayer, and Spolaor result
to higher-order curvatures. Other related work investigates the best constants in
the Bianchi–Egnell inequality
[
22
]
and the attainability of the stability
inequality
[
23
]
. This list of recent results is far from complete;
the reader can find a nice overview of this topic in
[
19
]
.

Most of this previous work is quite different from ours, because in
most cases the set of optimizers is well-known, which greatly simplifies
the investigation of the behavior of the corresponding functional on
nearly-optimal functions. As mentioned earlier, the precise form of the
Trudinger–Moser extremals remain unknown, and so we must use substantially different techniques.

1.3.
Outline of the proofs

Our methods rely on several spectral gap inequalities, and so to
begin we state the relevant eigenvalue problems. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded domain with smooth boundary, and let

\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
. The first relevant eigenvalue problem is the
well-studied
first Dirichlet eigenvalue

(4)

\mu_{1}(\Omega)=\inf_{\psi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x},

which is associated with the problem

(
\mathcal{P}_{0,\Omega}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\psi=\mu\psi&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
\psi=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Now let
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
be an
optimizer of (
1
), so that
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)
and
\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
. We define
the first
\alpha
-regularized eigenvalue

(5)

\lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)=\inf_{\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x},

associated with the linearized problem

(
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha,\Omega}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\phi=\lambda(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\phi&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
\phi=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We also define the (nonlinear)
first Trudinger–Moser eigenvalue
as

(6)

\Lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}v^{2}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x},

associated with the nonlinear eigenvalue problem

(
\mathcal{TM}_{\alpha,\Omega}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\Lambda e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}v&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
v=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

We denote the eigenvalues of (

P

α
,
Ω

) and (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

) by

\{\lambda_{j}(\alpha,\Omega)\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\{\Lambda_{j}(\alpha,\Omega)\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}},

respectively.
For simplicity, we omit the domain in the notation when no confusion can arise.
Also, when
\Omega=\mathbf{D}
is the unit disk, we simply denote

(7)

\lambda_{j}(\alpha,\mathbf{D})=\lambda_{j}^{\circ}(\alpha)\quad{\rm and}\quad\Lambda_{j}(\alpha,\mathbf{D})=\Lambda_{j}^{\circ}(\alpha)\quad{\rm for}\quad j\in\mathbf{N}.

In Lemma
11
we will show that

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)=\mu_{1}(\Omega).

The proof of Theorem
1

proceeds by contradiction. We suppose no uniform coercivity constant
exists, and so there is a violating sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that

(
\mathcal{H}_{0}
)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla(v-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})^{2}}=0.

Thus, the compactness of maximizing sequences for
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}
(via Vitali’s
theorem and concentration–compactness) ensures that, up to subsequences,

u_{k}\to u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
converges strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
In addition, the normalized deviations
\{\widehat{w}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, defined as

(8)

\widehat{w}_{k}=\frac{u_{k}-u_{*}}{\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\quad{\rm for}\quad k\in\mathbf{N},

satisfy an asymptotic Euler–Lagrange equation, and the limit

\widehat{w}_{*}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{k}
solves the linearized eigenvalue
problem (

P

α
,
Ω

). We then use Lemma
12
to
conclude that when
\alpha
is sufficiently small this cannot occur.

The proof of Theorem
2
is analogous to the works of
Bianchi and Egnell
[
2
]
for the Sobolev inequality,
and Engelstein, Neumayer, and Spolaor
[
15
]
for the Yamabe problem.
In this situation, the quantitative exponent in our stability estimate depends on the spectral properties
of the second variation at the family of maximizers
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
.
When all critical points are nondegenerate or integrable, a local Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction
implies
quadratic
coercivity, hence the optimal quadratic exponent.
Moreover, such nondegeneracy holds
generically
: for an open dense set of parameters

(\alpha,\Omega)
, all maximizers are nondegenerate.

The proof of Theorem
3
is slightly different and exploits the symmetry of the domain.
On the unit disk
\mathbf{D}
, symmetry and compactness can be restored without smallness in
\alpha
.
Initially, the results of Carleson and Chang
[
6
]
and Gidas, Ni, and
Nirenberg
[
21
]
ensure that every extremal
\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{D})

is positive, radial, and strictly decreasing.
Furthermore, the linearized operator around
u_{*}
separates into angular modes, and the
j=1

nonradial sector introduces a positive centrifugal term
r^{-2}
.
This produces a strict mode gap
\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)<\lambda_{2}^{\circ}(\alpha)
between the radial
eigenvalue of (
6
) and the first nonradial eigenvalues of (

P

α
,
Ω

),
ensuring that the quadratic form associated with the second variation is positive on the entire
tangent space
T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
The same contradiction argument as in the subcritical case then shows that any sequence violating
stability would lead to a sign-definite limit eigenfunction, which is again impossible by orthogonality,
thereby providing a global stability estimate for all
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.

Let us describe the plan for the rest of the manuscript.
§
2
introduces the functional setting and collects basic
properties of the Trudinger–Moser functional, including the asymptotic comparison of
the associated eigenvalues and the statement of the Krein–Rutman theorem, which plays
a central role in the spectral analysis.
In §
3
we establish the quantitative stability in the
subcritical regime.
The argument first derives a local coercivity estimate on the unit sphere
via a contradiction strategy based on the sign of the limiting
eigenfunction, and then extends the inequality to the closed unit ball
using the monotonicity of the Trudinger–Moser functional
along radial rescalings.
§
4
is devoted to the critical case on the unit disk,
where the rotational symmetry allows a decomposition into angular modes and the proof
of a strict spectral gap between the first radial and nonradial eigenvalues, leading
to a direct stability estimate without invoking the contradiction argument.

2.1.
The Krein–Rutman principle

The positivity and simplicity of the first eigenvalue for the linearized problems
(

) and (

T

,
Ω

) follow from the Krein–Rutman theorem,
which extends the Perron–Frobenius theory to compact, strongly positive operators
in ordered Banach spaces. We recall a convenient formulation for our purposes.

For further background on positivity methods in nonlinear elliptic problems, we
refer to de Figueiredo
[

Theorem A

(Krein–Rutman
[
4
]
)
.

Let
X
be a real Banach space ordered by a closed, convex, solid cone
\mathcal{K}\subset X
with
nonempty interior
{\rm int}(\mathcal{K})\neq\varnothing
and
\mathscr{T}:X\to X
be a compact linear
operator.
If
\mathscr{T}(\mathcal{K}\setminus\{0\})\subset{\rm int}(\mathcal{K})
, then, the spectral
radius
\rho(\mathscr{T})>0
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathscr{T}
with an eigenvector

\phi_{1}\in{\rm int}(\mathcal{K})
(strictly positive in the cone ordering),
and no other eigenvalue admits a nonnegative eigenvector.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{S}\subset X
defined as
\mathcal{S}:=\{u\in X:\langle u,\phi\rangle=0\}

is the hyperplane orthogonal to any
continuous positive linear functional
\phi\in\mathcal{K}^{*}
, then

\left\|\mathscr{T}^{k}\big|_{\mathcal{S}}\right\|=\mathrm{o}(\rho(\mathscr{T})^{k})\quad as\quad k\to\infty,

and so
\rho(\mathscr{T})\in\mathbf{R}
is algebraically and geometrically simple and isolated in the spectrum.

Remark 4
.

In our setting,
X=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{K}=\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):u\geq 0\}
, and

\mathscr{T}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is the Green operator associated with
(

P

α
,
Ω

) or (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

).
The Krein–Rutman theorem ensures that
both smallest eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)
and
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)

are positive, simple,
\lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha,\Omega)
and possess
a strictly positive eigenfunction.

2.2.
Compactness of optimizing sequences

Now, we will prove that any maximizing sequence for the Trudinger–Moser constant shall, up to a
subsequence, converge strongly to some extremal.

Lemma 5

(Integral convergence)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
be a sequence and suppose that
there exist
u_{*}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
q>1
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{*}
a.e. in
\Omega
and

(9)

\sup_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha q{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Then one has

(10)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha{|u_{*}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Setting
f_{k}=e^{\alpha|u_{k}|^{2}}
and
f=e^{\alpha{|u_{*}|}^{2}}
we have
that
f_{k}\to f
a.e. in
\Omega
and by (
9
) we get that

\{f_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. In addition, the Trudinger–Moser
inequality (
1
) implies that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, and
so (
10
) follows from
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
.
∎

Lemma 6

(Compactness of maximizing sequences)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain, and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
If
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is a maximizing sequence
for the Trudinger–Moser inequality on the closed unit ball, that is,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x={\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega).

Then, there exists an optimizer
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that,
up to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{*}
strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

First, we note that

(11)

u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\quad{\rm implies}\quad u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}.

In fact, if
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and

0<\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<1
, we obtain
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(v)>\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)
, where
v=\tfrac{u_{*}}{\theta}

with
\theta=\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Since
v\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
, this
yields a contradiction. Hence,
\theta=\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, and so (
11
) holds.

Since
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is uniformly bounded in a Hilbert space,

u_{*}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{*}
weakly

and consequently

(13)

u_{*}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.

Further, from the compact embedding
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega),\;p>1
, up to a subsequence,

(14)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{*}\;\;\mbox{weakly in}\;\;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),\quad u_{k}\to u_{*}\;\;\mbox{in }\;\;L^{p}(\Omega)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;u_{k}\to u_{*}\;\;\mbox{a.e.\ in }\;\;\Omega.

Claim 1:
If
\alpha\in(0,4\pi)
, then
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

In fact, by choosing

(close to
1
) such that

, we
have
\mathscr{E}_{q\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})\leq\mathscr{E}_{4\pi,\Omega}(u_{k})
and the
estimate (
1
) implies that (
9
) holds.
Thus, from (
14
) we can apply Lemma
5
to
obtain

(15)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha{|u_{*}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega).

By combining (
13
) with (
15
), we have
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.
From (
11
), we
obtain
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Claim 2:
If
\alpha=4\pi
, then
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

The critical case
\alpha=4\pi
is more involved. We shall use the
concentration–compactness alternative due to Lions
[
25
]
(see also
[
7
]
). First,
let us denote by
\mathbb{M}(\overline{\Omega})
the space of Radon measures on
\overline{\Omega}
.
As proved in
[
4
, Sec. 3, page 115]
, the boundedness of
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}

in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
implies that, up to a subsequence,

(16)

|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\quad\text{in the weak* topology of }\mathbb{M}(\overline{\Omega}).

By using (
14
) and (
16
), up to a subsequence, we obtain

(17)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{*}\;\;\text{in}\;\;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),\quad u_{k}\to u_{*}\;\;\text{a.e.\ in}\;\;\Omega\;\;\text{and}\;\;|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{M}(\overline{\Omega}).

Hence, according to Lions’ concentration–compactness alternative, there are only three possibilities:

(i)

If
u_{*}\equiv 0
and
\mu=\delta_{x_{0}}
is the Dirac mass concentrated measure at
some point
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, then

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=c+|\Omega|

for some
c\in[0,\infty)
.

(ii)

If
u_{*}\equiv 0
and
\mu
is not a Dirac mass concentrated at one point,
then there is
q>1
such that

\sup_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi q{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

(iii)

If
u_{*}\not\equiv 0
, then there is
q>1
such that

\sup_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi q{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Notice that Lemma
5
implies, for cases (ii) and (iii), that

(18)

{\rm TM}(4\pi,\Omega)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi{|u_{k}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi{|u_{*}|}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
{\rm TM}(4\pi,\Omega)>|\Omega|
, (
18
) excludes the possibility (ii). In order to exclude possibility (i), we recall that
Flucher
[
17
]
(see also
[
12
,
24
]
) proved that the concentration level is strictly lower than
{\rm TM}(4\pi,\Omega)
, namely,

{\rm TM}(4\pi,\Omega)>\sup_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}C_{\Omega}(x),

where

C_{\Omega}(x)=\sup\left\{\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{4\pi|u_{k}|^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}x:\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\;\;|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\delta_{x}\;\;\text{in}\;\;\mathbb{M}(\overline{\Omega})\;\;\text{with}\;\;x\in\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

Since
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
is a maximizing sequence, the concentration case (i) does not occur. Consequently, possibility (iii) must occur, and (
18
) holds as well. From (
11
), (
13
), and (
18
), we must have
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{4\pi}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Claim 3:
If
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
, then
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
.

From Claims 1 and 2, we already have
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
, for any
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
. In addition, from (
12
), it follows that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1=\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By virtue of the Hilbert structure of
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can write

\displaystyle 0\leq\|\nabla(u_{k}-u_{*})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\nabla u_{*}\,\,\mathrm{d}x+\|\nabla u_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla u_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}-2\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\nabla u_{*}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0\quad{\rm as}\quad k\to\infty

due to the weak convergence
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{*}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thus,
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{*}
strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
.
The proof of the lemma is a consequence of the last claim.
∎

Corollary 7

(Qualitative stability)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
If
\{u_{k}\}\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is a maximizing sequence for the Trudinger–Moser supremum
{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)
, then it satisfies

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|\nabla(v-u_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=0.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that the statement is false. Then, there exist a subsequence
\{u_{k_{\ell}}\}_{\ell\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and a constant
\delta>0
such that
\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}}\|\nabla(v-u_{k_{\ell}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\delta
for all
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
. Passing to a further subsequence, by Lemma
6
,
u_{k_{\ell}}\rightarrow u
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
for some
u\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
, which contradicts
\|\nabla(u-u_{k_{\ell}})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}>\delta
.
∎

Corollary 8

(Compactness of optimizing sequences)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
If
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
is an optimizing sequence for
{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)
then there exists an optimizer
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{*}
strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\to\infty
.

2.3.
From the unit sphere to the closed unit ball

The quantitative stability results established so far concern functions normalized
on the unit sphere.
In contrast with the Sobolev functional, which is homogeneous and hence scale-invariant,
the Trudinger–Moser functional does not preserve its value under rescalings of the Dirichlet energy.
Thus, to obtain a formulation valid for arbitrary functions satisfying

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 1
, one must extend the stability inequality from the sphere to the closed unit ball, which is the content of the next lemma.

Before establishing the extension result, let us set some preliminary notation.
For any
u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\setminus\{0\}
, we set

\theta:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\in(0,1]
.
We define the normalized projection
\Pi:\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
as

\Pi(u):=\frac{u}{\theta}
.

Lemma 9

(Extension from the sphere to the ball)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth bounded domain,
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
, and
\zeta\geq 2
.
Assume that

(19)

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\gtrsim_{\alpha,\Omega}{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)^{\zeta}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

Then one has

(20)

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\gtrsim_{\alpha,\Omega,\zeta}{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)^{\zeta}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}.

Proof.

We give the proof in the case
\zeta=2
; the proof for
\zeta>2
is
very similar.
Suppose by contradiction that there exists a sequence
\{\widetilde{u}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset{\rm int}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
,
i.e.,

0<\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<1
and

(21)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}=0.

In addition, notice that
\widetilde{u}_{k}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
must be a maximizing sequence for the Trudinger–Moser inequality, that is,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})={\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)
.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may use Lemma
6
and assume that
\widetilde{u}_{k}\to\widetilde{u}_{*}
converges strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
for some
\widetilde{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
and also

-\infty<\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}<\infty.

Since
{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})\geq{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}\left(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\right)
for
k\gg 1
large, it is not hard to check that
\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\to u_{*}
must also converge strongly in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We distinguish two cases:

Case 1:
There exists
\delta>0
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}>\delta>0
.

By means of (
19
), we have the uniform estimate below:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}\geq\delta^{2}\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}\gtrsim\delta^{2}\quad{\rm for}\quad k\gg 1,

which contradicts (
21
).

Case 2:
One has
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}=0
.

In this case, we observe

{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})\leq{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}\left(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\right)+\left(1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\leq 3{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k}).

From this, we can find
k\gg 1
sufficiently large satisfying

\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\left(1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}.

Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k}))}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}+\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}\left(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\right)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}\left(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\right)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{4}\left[\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}\left(\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\right)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{\left(1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{4}\left[\frac{1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}{\left(1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}}\right]\|\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

and so

\frac{{\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}{{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}(\widetilde{u}_{k})}\geq\frac{\alpha}{4}\left(\frac{1+\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{1-\|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\right)\|\Pi(\widetilde{u}_{k})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\rightarrow\infty\quad{\rm as}\quad k\to\infty,

which contradicts (
21
).
∎

Remark 10

(Extension mechanism)
.

In contrast with the Sobolev inequality, whose sharp quotient is homogeneous and
thus automatically invariant under rescaling of
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
,
the Trudinger–Moser functional lacks this homogeneity.
Nevertheless, the monotonicity of the map

t\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(t\,u)
for
t\in[0,1]

compensates for the absence of scale invariance and guarantees that the stability
inequality valid on the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}_{1}
extends to the closed unit ball

\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
without loss of generality.

and

.

3.1.
First eigenvalue asymptotic comparison

In our next lemma, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the first eigenvalues associated with the linearized problems (

P

α
,
Ω

) and (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

).
Both operators can be regarded as
\alpha
-perturbations of the Dirichlet Laplacian (

P

0
,
Ω

), with exponentially weighted potentials depending on the corresponding extremal
u_{*}
.
The following result shows that, as
\alpha\to 0
, these weights converge uniformly to
1
, so that the associated eigenvalues approach the first Dirichlet eigenvalue
\mu_{1}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, the inequality
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
reflects the fact that the stronger weight
(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}
in (

P

α
,
Ω

) produces a smaller Rayleigh quotient.

Lemma 11

(First eigenvalue asymptotic gap)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain.
Then
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\mu_{1}

and

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\mu_{1}.

In other words, given
\delta\in(0,\mu_{1})
there exists
0<\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(\delta)
,
independent of the optimizer such that
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
implies

\mu_{1}-\delta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\mu_{1}.

Proof.

Let
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
be an optimizer for the Trudinger–Moser constant, that is,

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})={\rm TM}({\alpha},\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1.

A direct computation shows that defining

(22)

\Lambda_{1}({\alpha})=\frac{1}{\int_{\Omega}u_{*}^{2}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x}

implies that
|u_{*}|\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfies

(23)

\begin{cases}-\Delta|u_{*}|=\Lambda_{1}({\alpha})e^{\alpha|u_{*}|^{2}}|u_{*}|&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
|u_{*}|=0&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By the maximum principle,
|u_{*}|>0
in
\Omega
.
Hence
u_{*}
does not change sign; without loss of generality, we assume
u_{*}>0
.
Then
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
solves the Euler–Lagrange equation associated with (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

)

(24)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{*}=\Lambda_{1}({\alpha})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u_{*}=0&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
is the first Trudinger–Moser eigenvalue defined in (
6
). Indeed, by the Krein–Rutman principle in Theorem
A
, the first eigenvalue of (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

) can be characterized as

\Lambda_{1}({\alpha})=\inf_{v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq\inf_{\psi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\psi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}=\mu_{1}(\Omega),

where
\mu_{1}>0
denotes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian.

We now establish a uniform
L^{\infty}
bound for the optimizers
u_{*}
as
\alpha\to 0
.
Recall that
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfies
\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and solves the boundary value problem
(
24
).
By elliptic regularity for the Dirichlet Laplacian, one has

(25)

\|u_{*}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\,\|\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=C_{\Omega}\,\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\|e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We next bound the right-hand side uniformly in
\alpha
.
Since
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\leq\mu_{1}(\Omega)
and
u_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we can apply Hölder’s inequality and the Trudinger–Moser estimate to obtain

\|e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}u_{*}^{2}e^{2\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\|u_{*}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{4\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega}e^{4\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}.

For
\alpha
sufficiently small, say
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
with
\alpha_{0}<\pi
, the last integral is uniformly bounded by the Trudinger–Moser inequality, since
\|\nabla u_{*}\|_{2}=1
and
4\alpha_{0}<4\pi
.
Consequently, uniformly, we get

\|e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega},\quad{\rm for}\quad\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}),

and from (
25
), we obtain

\|u_{*}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}^{\prime}.

Because
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
, the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
(for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
) implies

\|u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}^{\prime\prime}\,\|u_{*}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq M,

for some constant
M>0
depending only on
\Omega
and independent of
\alpha
as long as
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
.
Thus, we obtain the desired uniform bound

(26)

\|u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}).

From the compactness of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
(Corollary
8
), the constant
M
can be taken independent of the extremal
u_{*}
.

Next, we compare the Rayleigh quotients of the three eigenvalue problems (
4
), (
5
), and (
6
).
For any
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, using (
26
) we have

\frac{1}{(1+2\alpha M^{2})e^{\alpha M^{2}}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}.

Taking the infimum over all
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
gives us

\frac{\mu_{1}}{(1+2\alpha M^{2})e^{\alpha M^{2}}}\ \leq\ \lambda_{1}(\alpha)\ \leq\ \Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\ \leq\ \mu_{1}.

Since
(1+2\alpha M^{2})e^{\alpha M^{2}}\to 1
as
\alpha\to 0
, it follows that

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\mu_{1}.

Thus, for every
\delta>0
, there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
implies

\mu_{1}-\delta<\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\mu_{1},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 12

(Spectral gap)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain.
For
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
sufficiently small, one has

\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\lambda_{2}(\alpha).

In particular,
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
is not an eigenvalue of the linearized problem (

P

α
,
Ω

).

Proof.

The strict inequality
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
is established in Lemma
11
.
For the upper bound, we extend the Rayleigh quotient comparison to higher eigenvalues.
By the Courant–Fischer min-max principle (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Chapter VI]
), the
j
-th eigenvalue of (

P

α
,
Ω

) satisfies

\lambda_{j}(\alpha)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\dim V=j\end{subarray}}\;\sup_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\Omega}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}.

Using the uniform bound
\|u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq M
from (

), the same sandwich estimate as in Lemma

yields

\frac{\mu_{j}}{(1+2\alpha M^{2})e^{\alpha M^{2}}}\leq\lambda_{j}(\alpha)\leq\mu_{j}\quad\text{for all }j\in\mathbf{N}.

In particular,
\lambda_{j}(\alpha)\to\mu_{j}
as

for every

Since
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
(the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is simple) and

, there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\tfrac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})<\lambda_{2}(\alpha)\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}).

Combined with Lemma

, this gives the desired spectral gap.
Since the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}(\alpha)\}_{j\geq 1}
are discrete and
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
lies strictly between consecutive eigenvalues, it cannot belong to the spectrum of (

P

α
,
Ω

).
∎

3.2.
Taylor expansion for almost optimizers

We obtain an asymptotic expansion for the Trudinger–Moser functional near optimizers.

Lemma 13

(Expansion near optimizers)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain.
For any
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
one has

(27)

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)=\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\alpha\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w)+\mathcal{O}(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3})\quad{\rm as}\quad\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,

where
w=u-u_{*}
and

(28)

\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w)=\int_{\Omega}w^{2}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Initially, a direct computation implies that the following expansion holds:

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*}+w)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}}{\ell!}(2u_{*}w+w^{2})^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\alpha\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}(2u_{*}w+w^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}(4u_{*}^{2}w^{2}+4u_{*}w^{3}+w^{4})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}(2u_{*}w+w^{2})^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

(29)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})+\alpha\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w)+T_{1}(w)+T_{2}(w)+T_{3}(w),

where
\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}
is defined in (
28
) and

T_{1}(w)=2\alpha\int_{\Omega}u_{*}we^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

T_{2}(w)=\int_{\Omega}\left(2\alpha^{2}u_{*}w^{3}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}w^{4}\right)e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

T_{3}(w)=\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}(2u_{*}w+w^{2})^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)=-T_{1}(w)-\alpha\mathcal{I}^{*}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w)-T_{2}(w)-T_{3}(w).

We analyze each of these terms individually.
We start with the first term, for which we use the first eigenvalue of the boundary problem (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

).

Claim 1:

T_{1}(w)=-\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.

First, observe
that since
u_{*},u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, one has

1=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{*}+\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{*}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{*},\nabla w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which, by rearranging, implies

(30)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-2\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{*},\nabla w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.

Second, since
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
is an extremal of the functional
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}
, it solves the
boundary value problem (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

) with Lagrange multiplier
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)>0
defined in (
22
), namely

(31)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{*}=\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\\
u_{*}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

As a consequence of (
30
) and
(
31
), we get

(32)

\displaystyle T_{1}(w)=\frac{2\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{*},\nabla w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

This proves the first estimate.

Before analyzing the terms
T_{2}(w)
and
T_{3}(w)
,
let us perform some preliminary computations.
First, observe that the convexity of the function
t\mapsto t^{\ell}
implies that
for each
a,b>0
we have

(33)

\left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^{\ell}\leq\frac{a^{\ell}+b^{\ell}}{2}\quad{\rm or}\quad(a+b)^{\ell}\leq 2^{\ell-1}(a^{\ell}+b^{\ell}).

Next, we use the Trudinger–Moser and Hölder
inequalities to bound integrals of powers of
w
.
More precisely, we have

\displaystyle{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\geq\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha w^{2}}{\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2j}}w^{2j}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{j}}{j!\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2j}}\int_{\Omega}w^{2j}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha^{\ell}}{\ell!\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}}\int_{\Omega}w^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\ell}\|w\|_{L^{2\ell}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}}{\ell!\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}}

for any specific
\ell\in\mathbf{N}
. This is justified by the fact that
w^{2}\geq 0
,
so each term in the sum is non-negative. We can rearrange this inequality to read

(34)

\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\frac{\ell!}{\alpha^{\ell}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}.

We can similarly control the integral of
w^{2\ell-1}
using Hölder’s inequality as follows:

(35)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2\ell-1}(\Omega)}^{2\ell-1}

\displaystyle=\|w^{2\ell-1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\chi_{\Omega}\|_{L^{2\ell}(\Omega)}\|w^{2\ell-1}\|_{L^{\frac{2\ell}{2\ell-1}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2\ell}}\left(\int_{\Omega}w^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}=|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{2\ell}}\left(\|w\|_{L^{2\ell}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}\right)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}\frac{(\ell!)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}}{\alpha^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2}}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell-1}.

We can proceed to estimate the second term.

Claim 2:

T_{2}(w)=\mathcal{O}(\|\nabla w\|^{3}_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
as
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
.

As a matter of fact, using (
34
) we see

(36)

\left|\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}w^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)|\Omega|e^{\alpha M^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega,M}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}.

Additionally, by means of (
35
), we get

(37)

\left|2\alpha^{2}\int_{\Omega}u_{*}w^{3}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq 2^{7/4}\alpha^{1/4}{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)^{3/4}Me^{\alpha M^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega,M}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}.

This concludes the proof of the claim.

At last, we can estimate the third term, which is slightly more technical.

Claim 3:

T_{3}(w)=\mathcal{O}(\|\nabla w\|^{3}_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
as
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
.

Now, let
M=\max\{1,\|u_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}
. Using (
33
)
we can bound
T_{3}(w)
by the following three series:

(38)

\displaystyle|T_{3}(w)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}(2u_{*}w+w^{2})^{\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{\alpha M^{2}}\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}(2^{\ell}u_{*}^{\ell}|w|^{\ell}+|w|^{2\ell})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq e^{\alpha M^{2}}\left[\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell-1}2^{4\ell-3}M^{2\ell-1}}{(2\ell-1)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell-1}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell}2^{4\ell-1}M^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha M^{2}}\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle:=e^{\alpha M^{2}}[S_{1}(w)+S_{2}(w)+S_{3}(w)].

Here we set

S_{1}(w):=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell-1}2^{4\ell-3}M^{2\ell-1}}{(2\ell-1)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell-1}\,\mathrm{d}x,

S_{2}(w):=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell}2^{4\ell-1}M^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

S_{3}(w):=\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Next, we estimate each sum individually. First, we use (
34
) to bound the
last sum by a geometric series, so long as

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}<\frac{1}{2}.

More precisely, one has

\displaystyle S_{3}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{\ell-1}}{\ell!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}(2\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})^{\ell}

\displaystyle={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\left(\frac{1}{1-2\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}-1-2\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-4\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\left(\frac{4\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{6}}{1-2\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\right).

From this, we conclude

(39)

|S_{3}(w)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}.

We can also use (
34
) to bound the second sum when
0<\alpha\ll 1
. Also, when
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell}2^{4\ell-1}M^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)|\Omega|\sum_{\ell=3}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{4\ell-1}\ell!M^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\ell}2^{4\ell-1}\ell!M^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}.

One can show, using the ratio test, that this last sum converges; and so

(40)

|S_{2}(w)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega,M}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{4}.

At last, we use (
35
) to bound
S_{1}(w)
.
In this case, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2\ell-1}2^{4\ell-3}M^{2\ell-1}}{(2\ell-1)!}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2\ell-1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2\ell-2}{2}}2^{4\ell-3}M^{2\ell-1}{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}(\ell!)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}}{(2\ell-1)!}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2\ell-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}\sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}\frac{\alpha^{\frac{2\ell-2}{2}}2^{4\ell-3}M^{2\ell-1}(\ell!)^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2\ell}}}{(2\ell-1)!}.

Once again, one can use the ratio test to show that this last sum converges, which in
turn gives us

(41)

S_{1}(w)\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega,M}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}.

Combining (
39
), (
40
) and (
41
)
we now see

(42)

|T_{3}(w)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\Omega,M}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3}.

This concludes the proof of the claim.

Finally, by combining (
32
), (
42
), (
36
),
and (
37
) from Claims 1, 2, 3, respectively, we obtain
the desired asymptotic expansion (
27
).
The lemma is proved.
∎

3.3.
Second variation

Now, we prove some properties of the second variation of the Trudinger–Moser functional.

Let
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
be a fixed optimizer and

\{u_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
be a violating sequence with
u_{k}\to u_{*}
in

W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, that is, (

H
0

) holds. Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
be the sequence of deviations
w_{k}=u_{k}-u_{*}
, and denote by
\{\widehat{w}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
its normalization defined in (
8
).

We introduce the Lagrangian functional

(43)

\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

whose critical points on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
correspond to solutions of the
Euler–Lagrange equation (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

).
The tangent space to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
at
u_{*}
is

T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\Bigl\{\,w\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{*},\nabla w\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=0\,\Bigr\}.

The first variation of
\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}
at
u_{*}
is

\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{\prime}(u_{*})(v)=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{*},\nabla v\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}v\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the second variation is the symmetric bilinear form

\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(u_{*})(w,v)=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla w,\nabla v\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\int_{\Omega}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}wv\,\mathrm{d}x.

We denote this quadratic form by

(44)

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,v):=\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla w,\nabla v\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\,\langle w,v\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega},\quad{\rm with}\quad\langle w,v\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}wv\,\mathrm{d}x.

With these notations, we can summarize the structural properties of the second variation.

Lemma 14

(Second variation kernel)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth bounded domain and
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.
Assume that
v_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
is the first (normalized) eigenfunction of
(

P

α
,
Ω

) and that
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}

is a Trudinger–Moser optimizer.
If
\{u_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
is an optimizing sequence such that

u_{k}\to u_{*}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, then the following properties hold:

(i)

For all
w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
, one has
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)\geq 0

(ii)

For each
\widehat{w}_{*}\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
satisfying

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widehat{w}_{*},\widehat{w}_{*})=0
, it follows that
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widehat{w}_{*},v)=0
for any
v\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
, that is,

(45)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla\widehat{w}_{*},\nabla v\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\int_{\Omega}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\widehat{w}_{*}v\,\mathrm{d}x\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad v\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}.

(iii)

One has
v_{*}\notin T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{v_{*}\}.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps as follows.

(i)
Since
u_{*}
is a global minimizer of
\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}
on the
Hilbert manifold
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, its second variation is nonnegative on

T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
Thus
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)\geq 0
for all

w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

(ii)
From (i),
\widehat{w}_{*}
is a minimizer of

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and
so it is also a critical point, that is,

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(\widehat{w}_{*},v)=0
for any
v\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

(iii)
For the positive first Krein–Rutman eigenfunction
v_{*}>0
of (

P

α
,
Ω

)
(normalized by
\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
), since
v_{*}
solves (

P

α
,
Ω

) we have
\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\lambda_{1}(\alpha)\langle v_{*},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}
, and thus a direct computation gives

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(v_{*},v_{*})=\|\nabla v_{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\langle v_{*},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}=\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}{\lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\right)<0,

where we used
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)
(cf. Lemma
12
).
By (i),
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)\geq 0
for all
w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
hence
v_{*}\notin T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
Since
T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a closed codimension-one subspace of

W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, the direct sum decomposition in (iii) follows.
∎

Lemma 15

(Coercivity on the tangent space)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain.
Let
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
be a Trudinger–Moser optimizer and
let
v_{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
denote the first (positive, normalized) eigenfunction
of the linearized problem (

P

α
,
Ω

).
Then, for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
with
\alpha_{0}>0
given by Lemma
12
,
there exists a constant
c(\alpha)>0
such that

(46)

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)\geq c(\alpha)\,\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}.

In particular, the quadratic form
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}
has trivial kernel on
T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
14
(iii),

v_{*}\notin T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
and

W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\oplus\mathrm{span}\{v_{*}\}
.
We decompose
w=t\,v_{*}+w^{\perp}
with

\langle w^{\perp},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}=0
.
Since
v_{*}
solves (

P

α
,
Ω

) with eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)

and the cross terms vanish by orthogonality, a direct computation gives us

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)

\displaystyle=t^{2}\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\right)\langle v_{*},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{\perp}|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}.

By the Courant–Fischer characterization of
\lambda_{2}(\alpha)
, one has

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w^{\perp}|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\lambda_{2}(\alpha)\,\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega},

which yields

(47)

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)\geq t^{2}\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\right)\langle v_{*},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}+\left(\lambda_{2}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\right)\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}.

We now use the tangent constraint to control the coefficient
t
.
Since
w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
, one has

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{*}\!\cdot\!\nabla w\,\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, which by the
Euler–Lagrange equation (

⁢
T

M

α
,
Ω

) reads

\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}u_{*}\,w\,\,\mathrm{d}x=0
.
We set
f:=u_{*}/(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})
, so that the tangent constraint becomes

\langle w,f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}=0
, from which we obtain

t=-\frac{\langle w^{\perp},f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}}{\langle v_{*},f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}}.

We observe that
\langle v_{*},f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}v_{*}\,u_{*}\,e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\,\mathrm{d}x>0

since
v_{*}>0
and
u_{*}>0
in
\Omega
.
It follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

t^{2}\leq\frac{\langle f,f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}}{\langle v_{*},f\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}^{2}}\,\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}=:C_{0}(\alpha)\,\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}.

Substituting into (
47
), one has

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(w,w)

\displaystyle\geq\left[\lambda_{2}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)+C_{0}(\alpha)\left(\lambda_{1}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\right)\langle v_{*},v_{*}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}\right]\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}

\displaystyle:=C_{1}(\alpha)\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}.

By Lemma
12
and Lemma
11
,
as
\alpha\to 0
one has
\lambda_{j}(\alpha)\to\mu_{j}
for
j=1,2
,

\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\to\mu_{1}
, and
C_{0}(\alpha)\to C_{0}(0)<\infty
.
Therefore, one has the convergence

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}C_{1}(\alpha)=\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}>0,

which is the Dirichlet spectral gap of
\Omega
,
and in particular remains strictly positive for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
with
0<\alpha_{0}\ll 1

sufficiently small.
Since
t^{2}\leq C_{0}(\alpha)\,\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega}
,
it follows that

\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\langle w^{\perp},w^{\perp}\rangle_{\alpha,\Omega},

and estimate (
46
) is proved.
∎

3.4.
General subcritical case

At last, we can present the main result of this section, which is the proof of the stability estimate for small subcritical growth parameters.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The proof is by contradiction.
Suppose that there exists a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
, an optimizing sequence for the Trudinger–Moser inequality, such that
\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
and (

H
0

) holds.
Notice that by Lemma
9
, it is enough to work on the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

From Lemma
6
, up to a subsequence there exists a maximizer
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{*}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and (

H
0

) still hold, that is,

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{\|{\nabla(u_{*}-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}=0.

In particular, since

{\rm dist}_{\alpha}(u_{k},\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega))=\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla(v-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|{\nabla(u_{*}-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}={\rm dist}_{\alpha}(u_{k},u_{*})\quad{\rm for}\quad k\in\mathbf{N},

it follows

\frac{{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla(v-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\geq\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{\|{\nabla(u_{*}-u_{k})}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}\rightarrow 0\quad{\rm as}\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

Again, by the maximum principle, we may assume without loss of generality that
u_{*}>0
. Next, we set
w_{k}=u_{k}-u_{*}
and
\{\widehat{w}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
given by (
8
).
Using Lemma
13
, we can expand the Trudinger–Moser functional near an optimizer to obtain

(48)

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})=\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\mathcal{I}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{*}(w_{k})+\mathcal{O}(\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3})\quad{\rm as}\quad k\to\infty.

Claim 1:
There exists
\widehat{w}_{*}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
such that
\widehat{w}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widehat{w}_{*}
weakly and
\widehat{w}_{*}
satisfies

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{*}(\widehat{w}_{*})=\int_{\Omega}\widehat{w}_{*}^{2}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\quad{\rm for}\quad\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0}).

First, since
\sup_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\infty
, there exists
\widehat{w}_{*}\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
such that
\widehat{w}_{k}\rightharpoonup\widehat{w}_{*}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
up to a subsequence.
Moreover, by the lower semi-continuity of the norm, one has

0\leq\|{\nabla\widehat{w}_{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|{\nabla\widehat{w}_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1.

Next, by the compact Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
applied to (
48
), we get

(49)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{k})}{\|\nabla w_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}=\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}\left(1-\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\int_{\Omega}\widehat{w}_{*}^{2}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)=0,

T

M

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathscr{F}_{\alpha,\Omega}^{\prime\prime}(u_{*})(\widehat{w}_{*},\widehat{w}_{*})

3.5.
Generic nondegenerate case

We present the proof of Theorem
2
, which applies only under the nondegeneracy condition.

For nondegenerate critical points, the kernel of the Hessian is entirely tangential
to the manifold of maximizers. In this situation, a Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction
(see
[
2
,
15
]
)
shows that the Trudinger–Moser functional decreases quadratically in the normal directions,
yielding a local coercivity estimate of second order. We make this precise below.

We recall that for a maximizer
u_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
,
the second variation of
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}
restricted to the tangent
space
T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
is denoted by
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})[w,w]
.
If
u_{*}
is nondegenerate, there exists
\mu>0
such that

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})[w,w]\geq\mu\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad w\in T_{u_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}.

This property provides the spectral gap needed for local quadratic coercivity.

Proposition 16

(Local quadratic coercivity)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a smooth, bounded domain.
If all optimizers of the Trudinger–Moser constant are nondegenerate, then there exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that for any
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
, one can find constants
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,u_{*})>0
and
c=c(\alpha,\Omega,u_{*})>0
such that if
\|\nabla(u-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon
, uniformly on
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
, it holds

(51)

{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq c(\alpha,\Omega,u_{*})\|\nabla(u-u_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

Proof.

By Lemma
11
, we have

\delta(\alpha):=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(\alpha)}>0\quad{\rm for}\quad 0<\alpha\ll 1.

Applying Lemma
13
with
w=u-u_{*}
gives us

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq\alpha\delta(\alpha)\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{3},

for some
C=C(\alpha,\Omega)>0
.
By the nondegeneracy hypothesis, it follows that
\alpha\,\delta(\alpha)\geq\mu>0
.
Therefore, by choosing
\varepsilon>0
such that
2\varepsilon C\leq\mu
, we find

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq\tfrac{\mu}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\mathcal{S}_{1},

which is the desired coercivity estimate.
This proves the desired quadratic coercivity estimate with

c(\alpha,\Omega,u_{*})=\tfrac{\mu}{2}
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,u_{*})
.

Finally, since
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
is compact and
the map
u_{*}\mapsto\mathscr{Q}^{\prime\prime}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u_{*})
is continuous,
the nondegeneracy constants
\mu(u_{*})>0
admit a uniform positive lower bound on

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, from Lemma
9
, one can extend the estimate for optimizers on
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
(stability part).

By Lemma
6
, the set
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}

is compact, and so one finds finitely many balls
\mathcal{B}(u_{*}^{j},\varepsilon_{j})\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
with
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}(u_{*}^{j},\varepsilon_{j})
and

{\rm TM}(\alpha,\Omega)-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq c(\alpha,\Omega,u_{*}^{j})\|\nabla(u-u_{*}^{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\mathcal{B}(u_{*}^{j},\varepsilon_{j}),

where

\mathcal{B}(u_{*}^{j},\varepsilon_{j})=\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega):\|\nabla(u-u_{*}^{j})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon_{j}\}.

Let
C^{*}(\alpha,\Omega)=\min_{1\leq j\leq N}c(\alpha,\Omega,u_{*}^{j})>0
and
\varepsilon_{*}(\alpha,u_{*}):=\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\varepsilon_{j}(\alpha,u_{*})
.
If
u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
satisfies

{\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\leq\varepsilon_{*}
,
then there exists
u_{*}^{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
with

and Proposition

applies.
Otherwise, if

In both cases, the estimate below follows

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)\geq C^{*}(\alpha,\Omega){\rm dist}_{\alpha,\Omega}(u)^{2}.

Thus, the stability part of the result is proved.
∎

Fix a smooth, bounded reference domain

For any

We work on the fixed constraint

Next, notice that critical points of

on

are characterized by the

Let us define the augmented map

Lemma 17

(Regularity and Fredholm property)
.

For
m\geq 3
the map
\mathfrak{F}
is
\mathcal{C}^{m-1}
in
(\alpha,\psi;v,\Lambda)
.
For each

.

The claimed regularity follows from the
\mathcal{C}^{m-1}
-dependence of

and
\det D\psi

and standard composition/chain rules in

and Sobolev spaces.
At a zero, the linearization is

is the second variation (Hessian) of the Lagrangian

, acting as a symmetric elliptic operator

∎

We say that a critical point

Let us consider the projection

and the implicit function

\mathcal{C}^{m-1}

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\psi}
on
\mathcal{S}_{1,\psi}
.

Proposition 18

(Residual set of parameters)
.

The set

\mathcal{G}\ :=\ \Bigl\{(\alpha,\psi)\in\mathcal{P}:\ \text{all critical points of $\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,\psi}$

By Sard–Smale theorem
[
29

, the set of regular values of the

\mathcal{P}
provided

(\alpha,\psi)

the linearization in
(v,\Lambda)

is onto, hence (by index

∎

We now discuss the openness of the transversality map and the passage to geometric parameters.
In fact, residual sets are dense; openness follows from continuous dependence of the Hessian on parameters.

Lemma 19

(Openness)
.

The set
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{P}
is relatively open.

Proof.

We note that nondegeneracy is equivalent to invertibility of the bounded linear operator

, which depends continuously on
(\alpha,\psi)
in the operator norm.
Invertibility is an open property by the bounded inverse theorem, hence
\mathcal{G}
is open.
∎

At last, we can conclude the proof of our second main result.

(genericity part).

We transfer from the diffeomorphism parameter
\psi
to the geometric domain parameter
\Omega
.

is open in the

is open and dense in the set of

with

.

From this, we conclude that for any fixed

with

∎

4.
Critical regime on a disk

In this section, we address the Trudinger–Moser inequality at the critical exponent

\phi_{1}
as

\begin{cases}(r\psi^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{1}{r}\psi=-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)rW_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*})\psi,\\
(r\phi_{1}^{\prime})^{\prime}-\frac{1}{r}\phi_{1}=-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)rW_{\alpha}(u_{*})\phi_{1}.\end{cases}

From this, we get

\int_{0}^{1}\big[\psi(r)(r\phi_{1}^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}-\phi_{1}(r)(r\psi^{\prime}(r))^{\prime}\big]\,\mathrm{d}r=\big(\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)\big)\int_{0}^{1}rW_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*}(r))\psi(r)\phi_{1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

We integrate the left-hand side by parts to find

\big[r(\psi(r)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(r)-\phi_{1}(r)\psi^{\prime}(r))\big]_{0}^{1}=\big(\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)\big)\int_{0}^{1}rW_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*}(r))\psi(r)\phi_{1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

At
r=0
, we have
\psi(0)=\phi_{1}(0)=0
, and at
r=1
we use
\phi_{1}(1)=0
to obtain

0<\psi(1)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(1)=\big(\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)\big)\int_{0}^{1}rW_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*}(r))\psi(r)\phi_{1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Since
\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}((0,1))
is strictly decreasing in
r
, it follows that
\psi=\bar{u}_{*}^{\prime}<0
and
\phi_{1},W_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*})>0
on
(0,1)
, which implies
\psi(1)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(1)>0
and

\int_{0}^{1}rW_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*}(r))\psi(r)\phi_{1}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r<0\quad{\rm for}\quad\alpha\in(0,4\pi].

Therefore,
\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)<0
, which yields

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)>\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)
.
Finally,
\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)>\lambda_{1}^{(0)}(\alpha)
follows from the
fact that
e^{\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2}(r)}<W_{\alpha}(\bar{u}_{*}(r))
.
We conclude the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 21
.

This spectral hierarchy has a direct variational interpretation.
If we write the constrained second variation of the Trudinger–Moser functional at

\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{D})\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
as

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w]=\int_{\mathbf{D}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\int_{\mathbf{D}}(1+2\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2}}w^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

then the decomposition
w=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}w^{(k)}
yields

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(\lambda_{1}^{(k)}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\big)\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\int_{\mathbf{D}}(1+2\alpha u_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha u_{*}^{2}}{w^{(k)}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

In particular, the positive spectral gap

\lambda_{1}^{(1)}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)>0

from Lemma
20

provides a uniform lower bound on the Hessian

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w]\geq\big(\lambda_{2}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\big)\|\nabla w_{\perp}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2},

where
w_{\perp}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w^{(k)}
represents the projection onto all nonradial directions.
This inequality expresses the quadratic coercivity of the Trudinger–Moser functional
restricted to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and is the analytic core of the critical stability
result on the disk.

4.2.
Symmetric critical case

Now, we are ready to prove the main stability result, which is based on Lemma
20
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Initially, we observe that by Lemma
9
, it is enough to work on the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbf{D})
.
We divide the proof into two claims as follows:

Claim 1:
Any optimizer
\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{D})\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
is positive and radially symmetric.

Since
\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{D})\cap\mathcal{S}_{1}
is a critical point of
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}
, it satisfies
\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(\bar{u}_{*})={\rm TM}(\alpha,\mathbf{D})
, or equivalently

(54)

\begin{cases}-\Delta\bar{u}_{*}=\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)e^{\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2}}\bar{u}_{*}&\mbox{in}\quad\mathbf{D}\\
\bar{u}_{*}=0&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\mathbf{D}.\end{cases}

Hence, since
f_{\alpha}(s)=\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)e^{\alpha s^{2}}s
satisfies
f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbf{R})
, by the classical result of Gidas, Ni, and Nirenberg
[
21
, Theorem 1]
, it follows that
\bar{u}_{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbf{D})\cap W^{1,2}_{0,{\rm rad}}(\mathbf{D})
is radially symmetric and nonincreasing,
i.e.,

\partial_{r}\bar{u}_{*}\leq 0
for
r>0
.
Moreover, the positivity
\bar{u}_{*}>0
is a direct consequence of the standard maximum principle.

Claim 2:
For all
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
and
u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, it holds

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)\geq C(\alpha)\,\|\nabla(u-\bar{u}_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}.

Initially, we decompose any
w\in T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
into angular modes

w=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}w^{(k)}
, which are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\mathbf{D})
and in the Dirichlet form.
The quadratic form (
52
) splits modewise as

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w^{(k)}].

Subsequently, we divide the rest of the proof into some steps with respect to the Fourier eigenmodes.

Step 1: Radial sector (
k=0
).

By Lemma
14
(iii), the first eigenfunction
v_{*}>0
of (

P

α
,
Ω

) satisfies
v_{*}\notin T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
Since
v_{*}
is the unique (up to scaling) eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{(0)}(\alpha)<\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)
,
the tangent space constraint projects out the only direction on which
\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}
is negative.
More precisely, by the Courant–Fischer min-max characterization (cf.
[
11
, Chapter VI]
),
the Rayleigh quotient

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(w):=\frac{\int_{\mathbf{D}}|\nabla w^{(0)}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbf{D}}(1+2\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2}}(w^{(0)})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x},

restricted to
T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap W^{1,2}_{0,{\rm rad}}(\mathbf{D})
, which has codimension one in the radial sector, with the removed direction spanned by
v_{*}
satisfies

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(w)\geq\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)
. Next, using that

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w^{(0)}]=\|\nabla w^{(0)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\int_{\mathbf{D}}(1+2\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2})e^{\alpha\bar{u}_{*}^{2}}(w^{(0)})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

one has

(55)

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w^{(0)}]\geq\left(1-\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)}{\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)}\right)\|\nabla w^{(0)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad w^{(0)}\in T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}\cap W^{1,2}_{0,{\rm rad}}(\mathbf{D}),

where
\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)
is the second radial Sturm–Liouville eigenvalue.
Finally, since

\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)\to\mu_{2}^{(0)}=j_{0,2}^{2}\approx 30.47\quad{\rm as}\quad\alpha\to 0,\quad{\rm and}\quad\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\leq\mu_{1}=j_{0,1}^{2}\approx 5.78,

the strict inequality
\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)>\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)
holds for all
\alpha\in(0,4\pi]
.

Step 2: Nonradial sectors (
k\geq 1
).

On each nonradial sector
k\geq 1
, Lemma
20
gives us a uniform spectral gap

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w^{(k)}]\geq\big(\lambda_{1}^{(k)}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)\big)\|\nabla w^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}\geq c_{\circ}(\alpha)\|\nabla w^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2},

with
c_{\circ}(\alpha):=\lambda_{2}^{\circ}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)>0
.

Step 3: Full coercivity.

Combining (
55
) with the nonradial estimate and setting

\tilde{c}(\alpha):=\min\big\{\lambda_{2}^{(0)}(\alpha)-\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha),\,c_{\circ}(\alpha)\big\}>0,

a direct summation yields

(56)

\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}[w]\geq\tilde{c}(\alpha)\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}.

For
u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we write
u=\bar{u}_{*}+w
, with
w\in T_{\bar{u}_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{1}
.
By the Taylor expansion around the optimizer in Lemma
13
, it follows

\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(\bar{u}_{*})-\mathscr{E}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)=\frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_{1}^{\circ}(\alpha)}\mathscr{Q}_{\alpha}[w]+\mathcal{O}\big(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{3}\big)\quad{\rm as}\quad\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}\to 0.

Using (
56
), for
0<\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}\ll 1
sufficiently small one obtains
C_{\circ}(\alpha)>0
such that

{\rm def}_{\alpha,\mathbf{D}}(u)\geq C_{\circ}(\alpha)\|\nabla(u-\bar{u}_{*})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{D})}^{2}\quad{\rm for\ all}\quad u\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

The proof of the theorem is concluded.
∎
