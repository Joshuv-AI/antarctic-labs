0600 EST

17 January 1991

Situation Report #476

SPOT COMMENTARY

CENTRAL INTELLIGENCE AGENCY
DIRECTORATE OF INTELLIGENCE

Iraq-Kuwait:

Irag Widens Reserve Call-up

The Iraqi Defense Ministry today ordered reservists
born in the years 1954, 1955, and 1956 to report within
three days to their recruitment offices, according to
press sources. .

French Join Desert Storm

French Defense Minister Jean-Pierre Chevenement
announced today that 12 French Jaguar fighter-bombers
attacked Al Jaber airfield in Kuwait this morning,
according to press reports. At least four of the aircraft
were hit by ground fire--one plane was seriously damaged--
forcing two to land at alternate airfields in Saudi
Arabia. President Mitterand issued an order earlier in
the day placing French forces in Saudi Arabia under US
operational control.

International Reaction
SBcviet Reaction

According to Chief of the General Staff Moiseyev, the
Soviet armed forces in the southern USSR have been put on
"high military alert." In a taped message, Soviet
President Gorbachev stated that hostilities were provoked
by Iraq’s refusal to withdraw from Kuwait. He reassured
the Soviet people that thus far there were no casualties
among the Soviets left in Iraq. Gorbachev declared that
he had been notified about the attack by President Bush an
hour before it occurred and that he proposed that Bush
contact Husayn directly in a final attempt to resolve the
crisis peacefully. He also claimed to have contacted
numerous other world leaders in an attempt to "localize"
the conflict. He concluded by saying that he would take
all measures possible to end the war quickly. Ee

Official Reaction...

Iraq: Saddam Husayn, ina recorded statement carried by
Baghdad Radio stated that "the treacherous have committed
their treachery...the great duel, the mother of all
pattles has begun and victory is very near." The Iraqi
president was subsequently seen by a CNN reporter at
Baghdad’s Television station--he was reportedly there to
record a statement to the Iraqi people.

Israel: Israeli Defense Minister Arens cautioned against
premature euphoria over the onset of war, noting that it
was “too early to say that the darger has passed." fa

Kuwait: The Crown Prince said in a message to the Kuwaiti
people that the "whole world is witnessing the final
historic moments from victory of right and justice and the
defeat of deception and aggression." BS

Iran: President Rafsanjani stated Iran deplored the
outbreak of the Gulf war as a "heavy blow for human life
and the region’s resources." He added “Iran is closely
following all the movements in the region." He also
voiced regrets over the failure of efforts to find a
peaceful solution to the crisis.

Syria: An official Syrian daily said "The responsibility
for this catastrophic situation in Iraq and the Gulf
region now is borne by Saddam personally and he should pay
the price and the price of all other crimes he ceumitted
against the...Arabs."

PLO: The PLO leadership accused "the US-Israeli-European
coalition" of "cowardly aggression" against Iraq and said
it should be opposed "on all levels."

Libya: Muammar Qadhafi said that allied military action
in the Gulf should not go beyond driving Iraq out of
Kuwait. He was also quoted as saying air raids against
Iraq should stop and that military operations be limited
to Kuwaiti territory.

China: In an official statement China asked the
international community to search for a peaceful
solution--"At this critical moment, the Chinese government
strongly appeals to the warring sides to exercise maximum
restraint and to prevent the war from spilling over and
expanding."

Japan: Prime Minister Kaifu expressed resolute support
for the US-led military assault on Iraq and said Japan
would provide additional cash to help pay for the war.

New Zealand: Prime Minister Jim Bolger described the
attack as inevitable but said he still hoped Iraq would
quit Kuwait voluntarily, "Saddam made the decision to
ignore the deadline and that decision has made today’s
action unavoidable and inevitable." ee

Cuba: President Fidel Castro quickly criticized the war
as unnecessary and said it could have been avoided by
diplomatic means--stating the war is a failure for the
United Nations and for the politicians of our time.

Australia: Prime Minster Bob Hawke said "this tragic
necessity has one cause and one cause only...that is the
invasion and the occupation of Kuwait. Saddam’s
aggression has plunged the world into a terrible and
needless crisis." i

Bouth Korea: The Information Minister stated "we
wholeheartedly support the decision of the United States-
led multinational force to punish Iraq for its continuing
uncivilized acts, with the aim of restoring world peace
and order." He went on to urge Irag to immediately
withdraw from Kuwait and respect the wishes of the
international community. ea

European Community: EC foreign and defense ministers
expressed profound regret over the outbreak of hostilities
in the Gulf, according to a statement issued after an
emergency meeting in Paris this morning. The statement
said only an Iraqi retreat from Kuwait would avoid greater
destruction. a

WATO: In an emergency meeting this morning NATO
ambassadors reiterated their support for the enforcement
of Security Council resolutions and reaffirmed their
commitment to defend Turkey from Iraqi attack. ml

~.-Public Protests and Violence

A group called the "Direct Action Committee Against
the War" claimed responsibility for firebomb attacks
against US and British targets in northern Italy early
Thursday morning, according to press. Other press reports

indicate a wave of anti-war protests swept Australia this
morning, while three anti-US demonstrations were getting
underway in Pakistan, according to the American Consulate
in Peshawar. Some of the demonstrators were reportedly
planning to attack the American Center in Peshawar, but
i police claim they have the crowds under control.
