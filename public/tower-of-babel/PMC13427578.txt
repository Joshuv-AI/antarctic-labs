Development of colloidal gold and carbon immunochromatographic strips for rapid detection of PDCoV
Authors: Chenlin Hao, Mengxiao Xu, Tong Bu, Bingqian Zhang, Zhiyang Ran, Xueshi Niu, Zhihui Chen, Xiaohui Jin, Yue Zhang, Zhanyong Wei, Honglei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryPorcine deltacoronavirus (PDCoV) is an enteric coronavirus that causes diarrhea in suckling piglets, leading to serious economic losses in the swine industry. In this study, we report the development of competitive immunochromatographic strips for PDCoV detection using both colloidal gold and carbon nanoparticles, which also represents the novel reported application of carbon nanoparticles in a viral immunochromatographic assay. The established strips had visual detection limits of 1:128 (gold) and 1:64 (carbon) at 1 × 108 TCID50/mL. Both assays showed excellent specificity, exhibiting no cross-reactivity with porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV), transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV), or other viruses. Testing 137 clinical fecal samples showed that gold strips had 92.7% agreement with both RT-PCR and western blot, while carbon strips reached over 88%. These results demonstrate that gold- and carbon-nanoparticle-based immunochromatographic strips hold great potential for the rapid and sensitive detection of PDCoV, facilitating timely disease diagnosis and prevention.

Introduction

As a novel porcine intestinal coronavirus of the genus Deltacoronavirus, porcine deltacoronavirus (PDCoV) shows a particular severity in newborn piglets, leading to acute diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration, and even death.1,2 Since its emergence in 2012 and a notable outbreak in the United States in 2014, PDCoV has spread to multiple regions including South Korea, Japan, Thailand, and China, resulting in substantial economic damage to the global swine industry.3,4,5,6 Experimental studies have shown that PDCoV can infect mice, chickens, calves, and turkey poults.1,7,8,9 Furthermore, its detection in the plasma of Haitian children in 2021 demonstrated its potential for cross-species transmission.10 PDCoV is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus that encodes four structural proteins: spike (S), membrane (M), envelope (E), and nucleocapsid (N).11,12 Among these, the N protein is highly immunogenic and abundantly expressed during viral infection. Due to its high conservation, it is frequently employed as a target antigen for coronavirus detection.13 Therefore, this study aimed to develop a rapid, highly sensitive, and cost-effective PDCoV detection method based on the N protein to promote its prevention and control.

Epidemiological investigation revealed that the co-infections of PDCoV with porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) and porcine sapelovirus (PSV) were highly prevalent in swine herds.14,15 Such co-infections are likely to aggravate disease severity, complicate clinical presentations, and increase the difficulty of diagnosis and treatment. Currently, PDCoV detection relies on laboratory diagnostic techniques. These methods primarily include molecular biological detection based on viral genetic material analysis, such as RT-PCR and real-time quantitative PCR,16,17 as well as serological detection methods that assess host immune responses, including ELISA and virus neutralization tests.18,19,20 Although RT-PCR remains the preferred method for diagnosing coronavirus infections due to its high sensitivity and broad applicability, it typically requires prolonged detection time, sophisticated bench-top equipment, and professionally trained technicians. These requirements hinder its application in on-site farm detection.21,22

The immunochromatographic assay (ICA) was first proposed by Faulk and Taylor in 1971 and developed as a rapid diagnostic technology in the 1990s.23 With advancements in nanotechnology, various nanomaterials and detection strategies have been successfully incorporated into ICA platforms.24 Gold nanoparticles (Au NPs) are favored for their cost-effectiveness, facile synthesis, operational simplicity, low sample consumption, and stability, establishing them as the most widely employed labels in ICA strips.25,26 Carbon nanoparticles (C NPs) are also effective labels, with the advantages of scalable, low-cost industrial production and clear color development. Gold and carbon nanoparticles have been successfully employed as labels in ICA strips.27 The minimal sample preparation, user-friendly format, low cost, and speed of ICA strips make it a highly promising point-of-care testing (POCT) platform for rapid on-site virus detection.

In this study, we selected the N protein as the detection antigen and employed gold and carbon nanoparticles conjugated with an anti-PDCoV monoclonal antibody (mAb) as probes to construct two types of competitive ICA strips. Key parameters in the ICA process, including the coating concentration of the test line (T-line) protein, antibody labeling amount, and color development time, were optimized. Finally, the developed test strips demonstrated high specificity and sensitivity. This work demonstrates the application of carbon nanoparticle probes for coronavirus detection, offering important data and a theoretical basis for diagnostic kit development.

Results

Purification of recombinant PDCoV N and preparation of anti-PDCoV-N mAb 6B7

Following the principle depicted in the schematic representation of the ICA strip (Figure 1), we first prepared the PDCoV N protein and anti-PDCoV-N mAb 6B7 used in the strip. The recombinant N protein was successfully expressed in the Escherichia coli Rosetta strain. The SDS-PAGE analysis showed that N protein was detected in the supernatant of the IPTG-induced E. coli (Figure 2A, lane 2). The molecular weight of the expressed N protein was 53 KDa, which was consistent with the predicted target protein size. N protein was then purified by Ni-NTA agarose column with the final concentration of 1 mg/mL (Figure 2B, lane 8). Western blot results showed that the recombinant N protein could be specifically recognized by PDCoV positive serum, which had good reactionogenicity (Figure 2C). Characterization of gold nanoparticles

Au NPs were successfully synthesized via the trisodium citrate reduction method. The prepared solution was clear, homogeneous, and exhibited a stable wine red (Figure 3A). UV-visible spectrophotometer showed a single, sharp absorption peak at approximately 521 nm, indicating the formation of monodisperse and stable nanoparticles. Based on the established correlation between the absorption maximum and particle size, the average diameter of the synthesized Au NPs was estimated to be around 20 nm (Figure 3B). The above results show that the gold nanoparticles have been successfully prepared and can be used for subsequent probe preparation.Figure 3Characterization of gold nanoparticles(A) Appearance of Au NPs using trisodium citrate reduction method.(B) UV absorption spectra of Au NPs.(C) Optimization of pH for Au NPs.(D) Optimization of antibody labeling levels for Au NPs-mAb probes.

Optimization of labeling conditions

In general, the nanoprobe is most stable when the pH is at or just above the isoelectric point of the antibody. The optimal pH was determined by adjusting K2CO3 concentrations in the reaction system. As shown in Figure 3C, the addition of 2 μL of 0.1 M K2CO3 obtained the optimal labeling pH.

The minimum amount of anti-PDCoV antibody required to stabilize the colloidal gold nanoparticles was determined by a salt stability assay using 10% NaCl.28 Accordingly, the optimal labeling amount was defined as the smallest antibody volume that sustained a stable wine red. As shown in Figure 3D, the optimal antibody labeling amount was determined to be 8 μL.

Determination of the optimal labeled antibody

MAbs 2E9, 11E5, and 6B7, along with polyclonal antibody (pAb), all against PDCoV, were each conjugated to Au NPs and C NPs.29,30,31 The optimal antibody for labeling was determined by assessing the color intensity of the controlline (C-line) and T-line. Results indicated that mAb 6B7 outperformed the others, producing significantly stronger color signals on the C- and T-lines of both gold and carbon nanoparticle test strips (Figures 4A and 4B). The probe, formed by conjugating an antibody with Au NPs or C NPs, when 8 μL AuNPs-mAb probe and 4 μL C NPs-mAb probe were added, produced clearly visible C- and T-lines on the two test strips (Figure 5B).

The reaction times for the Au NPs and C NPs test strips were optimized. As shown in Figure 5C, clear and stable color development on both C- and T-lines was achieved at 12 min for the Au NPs strips and at 18 min for the C NPs strips. These time points were determined to be optimal by balancing high detection sensitivity with the need for rapid diagnostic turnaround.

Specificity and sensitivity testing

The Au NPs and C NPs test strip results were positive for PDCoV but negative for PEDV, TGEV, PSV, mammalian orthoreovirus (MRV), and Getah virus (GETV), demonstrating no cross-reactivity and high specificity with these common diarrheal viruses (Figure 6A).Figure 6The specificity and sensitivity of two test strips(A) Determination of cross-reactivity of Au NPs and C NPs test strips.(B) Color development of Au NPs and C NPs test strips for the detection of different concentrations of PDCoV standards.

The vLOD for the test strip was defined as the minimum PDCoV concentration that produced a noticeably lighter T-line. The results revealed that the T-line color became significantly lighter at PDCoV of 1:128 for the Au NPs test strip and 1:64 for the C NPs test strip (Figure 6B), indicating vLODs of 1:128 for Au NPs probes and 1:64 for C NPs probes (1 × 108 TCID50/mL).

Clinical fecal sample testing

The clinical performance of the test strips was evaluated using 137 porcine fecal samples with known PDCoV infection status. The results obtained with the Au NPs strip were compared with those from RT-PCR and western blot (Table 1). Relative to RT-PCR, the Au NPs strip showed a diagnostic sensitivity of 83.9% [47/(47 + 9)] and a specificity of 98.8% [80/(1 + 80)]. Compared with western blot, it exhibited a sensitivity of 85.2% [46/(46 + 8)] and a specificity of 97.6% [81/(2 + 81)]. Discussion

PDCoV infection induces diarrhea and dehydration in piglets, leading to high morbidity and mortality.32,33 The clinical symptoms of PDCoV are highly similar to those of other swine diarrheal viruses, preventing visual diagnosis and complicating outbreak control. This has led to significant economic losses in the global swine industry.34,35 Rapid and sensitive detection of PDCoV remains essential for infection control and pandemic prevention, especially with the broad cross-species potential.36,37,38 However, current primary methods like RT-PCR are laboratory-intensive and instrument-dependent, limiting their clinical applicability.39 To enable rapid point-of-care detection of PDCoV, we developed two competitive immunochromatographic assays using anti-PDCoV mAb conjugated with Au NPs and C NPs, respectively. This approach requires no sophisticated instrumentation and shows results within 20 min.

Au NPs, as the conventional colorimetric label, are widely used in virology for their operational simplicity, speed, low cost, and clear visual detection.40,41,42,43 C NPs, on the other hand, are distinguished by their simple conjugation without activation steps and their ability to provide a distinct dark signal, which supports their increasing use in point-of-care diagnostics.44,45 Currently, research on the application of colloidal carbon-based immunochromatographic test strips has primarily focused on the detection of organic compound residues in food,46 leaving their use for viral detection unexplored. Here, this study focuses on the development of competitive immunochromatographic strips for PDCoV detection using Au NPs and C NPs, respectively, demonstrating for the first time the feasibility of employing C NPs as probes in viral detection strips.

The N protein of PDCoV is a highly conserved and immunogenic structural protein, inducing specific antibodies and therefore serving as an ideal detection target.47,48 To obtain test strips with higher specificity, the N protein and anti-PDCoV-N mAb 6B7 were first purified. Using the N protein as the capture antigen and the mAb as the competing antibody, a competitive immunochromatographic assay was established. Subsequently, key reaction conditions for the test strips were optimized, including the coating concentration of N protein on the T-line, the amount of Au NPs-mAb and C NPs-mAb probes used, and the reaction time. After optimization, the test strips obtained accurate and clear visual results, which allowed for effective sample testing.

The accuracy of the results was further evaluated, including specificity, sensitivity, and the concordance rate with fecal sample detection. Both assays demonstrated good specificity, showing no cross-reactivity with PEDV, TGEV, PSV, MRV, and GETV. Furthermore, the vLOD for the Au NPs and C NPs test strips were 1:128 and 1:64, respectively, using PDCoV stock of 108 TCID50/mL. Although both competitive immunochromatographic assays exhibited relatively low sensitivity, the Au NPs test strips showed high coincidence rates, exceeding 92% with both RT-PCR and western blot, while the C NPs test strips reached over 88%. These data demonstrate that the ICA strips are applicable for detecting PDCoV in porcine fecal samples. In practical applications, the Au NPs test strips are more suitable for scenarios requiring higher diagnostic accuracy, whereas the C NPs test strips offer the advantages of lower cost and simpler conjugation without activation steps, making it a more appropriate choice for large-scale field screening.

In conclusion, this study reports two competitive immunochromatographic strips for PDCoV and the use of C NPs as a label in rapid viral diagnostics. Evaluation using 137 clinical fecal samples confirmed the potential of both Au NPs and C NPs strips for rapid screening. With the application of C NPs in viral immunochromatographic strips, this work is expected to provide a valuable reference for detecting other diarrheal viruses in swine populations.

Limitations of the study

First, the sensitivity of both strips, particularly the C NPs test strips, is lower than that of RT-PCR, making them more suitable for screening than confirmatory diagnosis. Second, the assay is qualitative, incorporating a strip reader could reduce subjective bias and enable semi-quantitative analysis. Finally, potential interference from endogenous substances in fecal samples was not systematically evaluated. Addressing these limitations will be the focus of future work.

Experimental model and study participant details

Clinical fecal samples testing

A total of 137 fecal samples were collected from pigs with known infection status using cotton swabs. RT-PCR, western blot and the test strip developed in this study were used to test 137 fecal samples. The results were compared to calculate the positive coincidence rate, negative coincidence rate, and overall coincidence rate.

Ethics oversight

All 137 samples were used and processed with the consent of pig farm owners. The Animal Care and Ethics Committee at Henan Agricultural University reviewed and approved all animal experimentation, and the corresponding approval number has been provided (HNND2025031120).

Method details

Expression and purification of PDCoV N protein

The pET-32a-PDCoV-N recombinant strain was inoculated into LB liquid medium containing 50 μg/mL ampicillin and cultured at 37°C for 8 h. After following induction with 0.8 mmol/L isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at 37°C for 10 h, the bacterial cells were collected. The expression of the His-tagged fusion protein was analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE). Subsequently, the recombinant protein was purified with a Ni-Agarose His purification kit (CWBIO, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Finally, the concentration of the purified N protein was measured using an ultra-micro spectrophotometer (Thermo, China).

Purification and identification of monoclonal antibodies

Monoclonal antibodies were purified from the ascites using octanoic acid-ammonium sulfate precipitation. Antibody reactivity was confirmed by indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA) and western blotting using PDCoV as the antigen and the purified antibody as the primary antibody, following previously reported methods.49

Preparation of gold and carbon nanoparticles

Au NPs were prepared using trisodium citrate reduction method. Under stirring, a mixture of 50 mL ultrapure water and 0.1 mL of 10% HAuCl4·3H2O was heated to boiling in a 250 mL round-bottom flask. Then, 1.5 mL of 1% trisodium citrate solution was rapidly added to the boiling mixture, and stirring was maintained for 10 min until the solution color changed sequentially from colorless to gray, black, purple, and finally to stable wine red.

C NPs were purchased from NanoGen Biotech Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). The commercially obtained 10× carbon nanoparticles solution was first sonicated for 5 min to ensure homogeneity. Subsequently, it was diluted with ultrapure water at a ratio of 1:10.

Optimization of labeling conditions

To determine the optimal labeling pH, 100 μL of Au NPs were aliquoted into microcentrifuge tubes and mixed with varying volumes of 0.1 M K2CO3. Then, 10 μL of anti-PDCoV mAb was added to each tube, followed by thorough mixing and incubation for 10 min. Subsequently, 100 μL of 10% NaCl was added, and the tubes were mixed and incubated for 10 min. The optimal pH was identified based on the observed color change.

Following adjustment of the Au NPs to the optimal labeling pH, 100 μL aliquots were dispensed into separate centrifuge tubes and mixed with varying volumes of anti-PDCoV mAb (1 mg/mL). Subsequently, 100 μL of 10% NaCl was added to each tube. The minimum antibody amount that maintained a stable color was defined as the optimal labeling level for the Au NPs-mAb probes.

Construction of test strip

The test strip is composed of sample pad, absorbent pad, PVC bottom plate, NC membrane. The PDCoV N protein and goat anti-mouse IgG polyclonal antibody (1.0 mg/mL) were dispensed onto the NC membrane as the test line (T-line) and control line (C-line), respectively, using a three-dimensional film spray gold instrument, followed by drying at 37 °C for 3 h. The NC membrane, conjugate pad, sample pad, and absorbent pad were assembled sequentially on a PVC bottom plate and then cut into strips with a width of 3.0 mm. A schematic diagram of the immunochromatographic test strip structure is presented in Figure 1A.

Optimization of the test strip

The dosage of probe was 10 μL and the dosage of goat anti-mouse IgG was 1.0 mg/mL. The spraying amount of PDCoV N was adjusted to 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, and 0.2 mg/mL, respectively. After mixing the PDCoV solution with probe, the test strip was inserted for testing, and the color formation of C-line and T-line was observed after 10 min.

The amount of goat anti-mouse IgG and PDCoV N protein coated on the test line was fixed. Varying volumes (4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 μL) of either AuNP-mAb or C NP-mAb probes were added into the microhole containing 100 μL PDCoV virus solution and thoroughly mixed. The test strip was then inserted, and the color formation of C-line and T-line was observed after 10 min incubation.

The test strips were evaluated at different development times (8, 10, 12, 15, and 18 min), with all other parameters kept consistent.

Strip test interpretation criteria

The results are determined by visualizing the color development on both the T-line and C-line. The detection of test strips followed the competitive principle. With increasing PDCoV concentration, the color of the T-line progressively lightened. Positive (+): T-line is either significantly lighter or absent, while C-line is clearly visible. Weakly positive (±): The T-line is slightly lighter, and the C-line remains distinct. Negative (−): Both T and C-lines are fully developed with equal color intensity. Invalid: No color appears on the C-line, regardless of T-line visibility. Figure 1B shows a schematic diagram of the immunochromatographic test strip detection principle.

Specificity and sensitivity testing

To assess the specificity of the test strip, PDCoV, PEDV, TGEV, PSV, MRV, and GETV were analyzed using strips from the same batch, with PBS buffer serving as the negative control. The results were observed and recorded after 15 min.

The PDCoV virus solution was serially diluted using dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM) to 1:2, 1:4, 1:8, 1:16, 1:32, 1:64, 1:128, 1:256, 1:512, and 1:1024, respectively, and DMEM without PDCoV served as the negative control. The lowest dilution that caused the T-line to become noticeably faint was considered the test strip’s visual limits of detection (vLOD).

Quantification and statistical analysis

There are no quantification or statistical analyses to include in this study.
