Abstract
We study the maximum length of q q -ary codes as a function of alphabet size, code size, and Singleton defect. For an ( n , M , d ) q (n,M,d)_{q} code with dimension κ = log q ⁡ M ≥ 2 \kappa=\log_{q}M\geq 2 and Singleton defect s = n − ⌈ κ ⌉ + 1 − d s=n-\lceil\kappa\rceil+1-d , we establish a maximal-arc-type bound . For M = q k M=q^{k} , we call codes with n = ( s + 1 ) ​ ( q + 1 ) + k − 2 n=(s+1)(q+1)+k-2 length-maximal , and show such codes are necessarily symbol-uniform, have pairwise distances confined to { d } ∪ { n − k + 3 , … , n } \{d\}\cup\{n-k+3,\ldots,n\} , and satisfy the divisibility condition ( s + 2 ) | q ⁡ ( q + 1 ) (s+2)\mid q(q+1) . An equivalent form yields an improved Singleton-type inequality extending a result of Guerrini, Meneghetti, and Sala for binary systematic codes. When s ≥ 2 ​ q s\geq 2q , the bound tightens to n ≤ s ⁡ ( q + 1 ) + k − 1 n\leq s(q+1)+k-1 ; more finely, when α ​ q ≤ s < ( α + 1 ) ​ q \alpha q\leq s<(\alpha+1)q for integer α ≥ 2 \alpha\geq 2 , it tightens to n ≤ ( s + 2 − α ) ​ ( q + 1 ) + α + k − 3 n\leq(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+k-3 , improving on the main bound by ( α − 1 ) ​ q (\alpha-1)q . We identify several conditions under which nonlinear codes satisfy the Griesmer bound, including: d ≤ q 2 d\leq q^{2} ; s ≤ q − 1 s\leq q-1 ; s ≥ β ​ q s\geq\beta q with d ≤ β ​ q 2 d\leq\beta q^{2} ; and a parametric family of binary conditions. We also show that near-length-maximal A 1 A^{1} MDS codes of length k + 2 ​ q − 1 k+2q-1 cannot exist for k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 when q = 2 q=2 , nor for k ≥ 7 k\geq 7 when q = 3 q=3 . For codes of non-integer dimension κ ∈ ( k , k + 1 ) \kappa\in(k,k+1) , an analogous bound holds but is never attained. This forces the corresponding Singleton-type inequality one unit tighter than the integer-dimension case. For rational non-integer κ \kappa , our bounds specialise to a length bound for additive codes of fractional dimension, complementing recent geometric results on additive codes. Throughout, the results parallel the theory of maximal arcs. Whether length-maximal nonlinear codes can exist for parameter ranges within which no linear length-maximal codes exist is the principal open problem raised by this work.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, by an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code

\mathcal{C}
we denote an

{\mathcal{A}}^{n}

is a finite alphabet of size

denotes the standard Hamming distance. Elements of

is a

is said to be a

, and may be denoted an

-code to emphasize linearity. More generally, for an

code
\mathcal{C}
, we define the

of

to be

. The

measuring how far the code falls short of the Singleton bound
[

,

(MDS); a code with

is

(AMDS)
[
11

A central problem in coding theory is to determine, for given

,

, and

code. For

codes, this question connects deeply to finite projective geometry: the columns of a generator matrix form a multiset of points—a

(n,k+s-1)
-arc, that is, an arc meeting the upper bound

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2.

(1)

Maximal arcs are rare. In the planar case (

[
5

[
12
]

[
7
]
shows that planar arcs one step short of maximal, having size
(s+1)(q+1)

(s+2)\mid q

[
3

For

codes the situation has been comparatively moribund: the question of the parameters for which nonlinear codes can match or exceed the lengths achievable by linear codes is largely open. Moreover, the Griesmer bound does not generally hold for nonlinear codes. Regarding this second point, Guerrini, Meneghetti, and Sala
[
14
]
, building on earlier work of Bellini and Meneghetti
[
9
]
, studied

k

S_{q}(k,d)
, and
L_{q}(k,d)
denote the minimum length of a nonlinear, systematic, and linear code, respectively, so that in particular,
N_{q}(q^{k},d)\leq S_{q}(k,d)\leq L_{q}(k,d)
. Their results relevant to nonlinear codes include the following:
the Griesmer bound holds
for nonlinear codes when
M\geq q^{k}
and
q^{k-1}
divides
d
, or when
q=2
and the dimension
k=2,
or
3
. They also provide an improved Singleton-type bound for binary systematic codes:

S_{2}(k,d)\geq k+\left\lceil\tfrac{3}{2}d\right\rceil-2

(2)

In this paper we take a different approach, working directly with the Singleton defect as the primary parameter. In Section
3
, we establish that the maximal arc bound (
1
) holds for all codes of integer dimension
k
, linear or not. We further show that codes achieving this bound—which we call
length-maximal
—are necessarily
symbol-uniform
, meaning every alphabet symbol appears equally often in each coordinate position. Moreover, we show that the nonzero codeword weights of a length-maximal code of integral dimension must take values in
\{d\}\cup\{n-k+3,\ldots,n\}
, and the divisibility condition
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
must also hold. An equivalent expression of the new bound provides the Singleton-type inequality in Lemma
3.12
, which recovers the bound on systematic codes (
2
) of Guerrini et al.
[
14
]
as a special case. This improved Singleton-type bound is further tightened for codes of non-integer dimension in Lemma
5.3
.

For codes of non-integer dimension, an analogous bound holds (Lemma
5.1
), but with strict inequality. In turn, this provides a tighter Singleton-type inequality, one unit stronger than the integer-dimension form (Lemma
5.3
).

When the defect
s
is large, bounds may be tightened further. For example, Corollary
4.15
shows that a code of integer dimension

and defect
s\geq 2q
, satisfies

, and that any such code with

satisfies the classical Griesmer bound. We further demonstrate an improvement (Theorem
4.17
) which grows as
(\alpha-1)q
when
\alpha q\leq s<(\alpha+1)q
for

, thus providing a measure of how the length ceiling descends with the defect; similar behaviour extends to codes of non-integer dimension (Corollary
5.5
).

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2

3
proves the maximal-arc-type bound and establishes the structural properties of length-maximal codes. Section
4

5
treats codes of non-integer dimension: the analogous length bound, the non-existence of length-maximal codes in this setting, tighter Singleton-type inequalities, and defect-preservation results. Section

Preliminaries

In this section we establish notation, recall the fundamental bounds used throughout, and introduce the two key structural concepts of the paper: symbol-uniformity and shortening.

2.1
Code Equivalence

Two codes
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
are

if one can be obtained from the other by a combination of
positional permutations
(permuting the
n
coordinate positions) and

(applying, independently in each coordinate, a bijection of
{\mathcal{A}}
)
[
23
]
. Equivalent codes have identical parameters

,
M
,

. Since an appropriate symbol permutation can map any chosen codeword to the all-zero word, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{C}
, and we adopt this convention throughout. A code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be

if there is a linear code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
that is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}
.

2.2
Fundamental Bounds

The Singleton bound holds for all codes, regardless of linearity or structure.

Theorem 2.1

(Singleton Bound
[
21
,
17
]
)
.

For any
(n,M,d)_{q}
code with dimension
\kappa=\log_{q}M
,

d\leq n-\lceil\kappa\rceil+1.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Singleton defect
of an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code
\mathcal{C}
is

s=n-\lceil\kappa\rceil+1-d\geq 0.

We recall that a code with defect

is an
A^{s}
MDS code. When
M=q^{k}
, this reduces to the standard linear defect
s=n-k+1-d
.

The Plotkin bound gives an upper bound on the size of a code when the minimum distance is large relative to the length. The following
q
-ary form will be our primary tool. Part (1) is due to Plotkin
[
19
]
; part (2), which improves the classical bound

of Berlekamp
[

[

]
.

Theorem 2.3

(

[
19
,
10
,
15
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code.

1.

If
dq>n(q-1)
, then
M\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{dq}{dq-n(q-1)}\right\rfloor
.

2.

If
dq=n(q-1)
, then
M\leq 2(q-1)n
.

For binary codes, a sharper formulation that distinguishes the parity of

Theorem 2.4

(Binary Plotkin Bound

[

,
10
,
17
]
)
.

Let

be a binary

1.

If

.

2.

If

If

If

For linear codes, the Griesmer bound provides a stronger lower bound on length than the Singleton bound.

Theorem 2.5

(Griesmer Bound
[

,

]
)
.

For any linear

The proof of the Griesmer bound relies critically on linearity, and it is known that the bound does not hold for general nonlinear codes

2.3
Symbol-Uniformity

A key structural property of optimal codes, which will emerge naturally from our main bound, is the following.

Definition 2.6
.

A code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}^{n}
is
symbol-uniform in coordinate
j
if every symbol
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
appears equally often,
that is,

|\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\mid c_{j}=a\}|=\frac{M}{q}\quad\text{for each }a\in{\mathcal{A}}.

If
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform in every coordinate, it is called

symbol-uniform
.

Note that symbol-uniformity in any coordinate requires
q\mid M
. The following examples show that the property is common among well-structured
codes.

Example 2.7
.

•

Every linear
[n,k,d]_{q}
code is symbol-uniform.

•

A systematic
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}
code is symbol-uniform in each of its
k
systematic coordinates, by the same argument.

•

Any coset of a linear code is symbol-uniform.

•

The Nordstrom–Robinson code
(16,2^{8},6)_{2}

[
18
,
17
]
is
symbol-uniform.

2.4
Shortening

A standard operation that preserves many code properties is shortening. We use it extensively in our inductive arguments.

Definition 2.8

Let

be an

code. For a coordinate

\mathcal{C}_{i}^{(a)}=\bigl\{(c_{1},\dots,c_{i-1},c_{i+1},\dots,c_{n})\mid\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C},\ c_{i}=a\bigr\}.

The properties in the following lemma are easily established by way of the respective definitions and simple counting arguments.

(Properties of Shortening)
.

Let

be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}

, and let

MDS code of dimension

.

1.

M^{\prime}=|\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\mid c_{i}=a\}|
, and
d^{\prime}\geq d
.

2.

The symbol

can be chosen so that

; consequently there is an

A
{}^{s^{\prime}}
MDS code

and

.

3.

If
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform in coordinate

, then

.

4.

If
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform, then

(and hence

3
Length-Maximal Codes

In this section we prove our main upper bound on the length of a code in terms of its alphabet size
q
, its dimension
\kappa=\log_{q}M
, and its Singleton defect
s
. We then show that codes achieving this bound possess a rigid combinatorial structure: they are necessarily symbol-uniform and their pairwise distances are sharply constrained.

3.1
The Base Case: Codes of Dimension 2

We begin with codes of size
M=q^{2}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{2},d)_{q}
code with Singleton defect
s=n-d-1
. Then

n\leq(s+1)(q+1).

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform and equidistant, that is, every pair of distinct codewords agrees in
exactly
s+1
coordinates.

Proof.

Consider the complete graph on the
q^{2}
codewords of
\mathcal{C}
. For distinct codewords
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}
, let
w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
denote the number of coordinates in which they agree. Since any two distinct codewords agree in at most
n-d=s+1
positions,

\sum_{\{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\}}w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\leq\binom{q^{2}}{2}(s+1).

On the other hand, count the agreements coordinate-wise. For each coordinate
i
, let
n_{i,a}
be the number of codewords with symbol
a\in{\mathcal{A}}
in position
i
, so
\sum_{a}n_{i,a}=q^{2}
. The contribution of coordinate
i
is

A_{i}=\sum_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}\binom{n_{i,a}}{2}.

By convexity of
\binom{\cdot}{2}
,
A_{i}
is minimized when the
n_{i,a}
are as equal as possible, i.e.,
n_{i,a}=q
for all
a
, yielding

A_{i}\geq q\binom{q}{2}=\frac{q^{2}(q-1)}{2},

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform in coordinate
i
.

Summing over all
n
coordinates and combining, we obtain

n\cdot\frac{q^{2}(q-1)}{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\leq\frac{q^{2}(q^{2}-1)}{2}(s+1).

(4)

Dividing through by
\frac{q^{2}(q-1)}{2}
immediately yields
n\leq(s+1)(q+1)
.

Equality in (
4
) forces
A_{i}=\frac{q^{2}(q-1)}{2}
for every
i
, which by convexity requires symbol-uniformity in every coordinate. It also forces
w(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=s+1
for every pair of distinct codewords (equidistance). Conversely, if
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform and equidistant with agreement count exactly
s+1
, then equality holds throughout (
4
), and thus
n=(s+1)(q+1)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

2-dimensional length-maximal codes exist for all
q=p^{t}
,
p
prime: For
s=0
, the bound of Lemma
3.1
gives

, achieved by the
[q+1,2,q]_{q}
MDS simplex code. More generally, an
(q+1,q^{2},q)_{q}
MDS code exists if and only if there is an affine plane of order

(
[

), moreover such a code is equivalent to a linear code if and only if the corresponding plane is isomorphic to PG
(2,q)
(for this, and the more general concept of Desarguesian net-codes, see
[

For

, and

MDS code and concatenating, giving an

and

.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

,

, and

Proof.

Let

be a prime power. From Lemma

, an
(n,q^{2},d)
A

, or equivalently

-A

∎

3.2
The General Bound

Theorem 3.5

(Maximal-Arc-Type Bound)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
,
then

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2.

(5)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

. The base case

is Lemma
3.1
. Assume
k\geq 3
. By part (
2
) of Lemma
2.9
, there exists a shortened code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with parameters
(n-1,q^{k-1},d^{\prime})_{q}
with

and defect

. Applying the induction hypothesis to

gives

n-1\leq(s^{\prime}+1)(q+1)+(k-1)-2\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-3,

which yields the claim.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

A code attaining the bound in Theorem

.

Remark 3.7
.

For a linear

Corollary 3.8

Let

Proof.

From Theorem

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2
. Substituting
s+1=n-k-d+2
yields

n\geq d+\frac{d}{q}+k-2.

(6)

If
k=2
, or if
k\geq 3
and
d\leq q^{2}
then
g_{q}(k,d)=d+\frac{d}{q}+k-2
, so the inequality (
6
) matches the Griesmer bound (
3
).
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We note that Corollary
3.8
subsumes Theorem 4 in
[
14
]
, which proves that systematic codes with
d\leq 2q
satisfy the Griesmer bound.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
C
be an
(n,k,d)_{q}
A
s
MDS code,
k\geq 1
. If
s\leq q-1
then
C
respects the Griesmer bound

Proof.

By definition,
d=n-k+1-s
, so Theorem
3.5
provides

\displaystyle d=n-k+1-s

\displaystyle\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2-k+1-s

\displaystyle=(s+1)(q+1)-(s+1)=q(s+1)\leq q^{2},

and the result follows from the Corollary
3.8
.
∎

The following lemma records a key consequence of a code being sufficiently long: shortening preserves the defect exactly. It will be used repeatedly in the sequel.

Lemma 3.11

(Defect Preservation)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a shortened code with parameters

(n-1,q^{k-1},d^{\prime})_{q}
and defect
s^{\prime}
. If

n\geq s(q+1)+k-1,

then
s^{\prime}=s
and
d^{\prime}=d
.

Proof.

We always have
s^{\prime}\leq s
. Suppose for contradiction that
s^{\prime}\leq s-1
. Applying Theorem
3.5
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
gives

n-1\leq(s^{\prime}+1)(q+1)+(k-1)-2\leq s(q+1)+k-3,

so
n\leq s(q+1)+k-2
, contradicting the hypothesis. Hence
s^{\prime}=s
, and
d^{\prime}=(n-1)-s-(k-1)+1=n-s-k+1=d
.
∎

The maximal-arc-type bound has a direct consequence for the minimum distance: rearranging in terms of
d
yields an improvement on the classical Singleton bound.

3.3
An Improved Singleton-Type Bound

Substituting
s=n-k+1-d
into (
5
) and isolating
d
yields the following which may be viewed as an improvement of the Singleton bound.

Lemma 3.12
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
, then

\left\lceil d\cdot\frac{q+1}{q}\right\rceil\leq n-k+2.

Proof.

From
n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2
and
s=n-k+1-d
, substitution gives
n\leq(n-k+2-d)(q+1)+k-2
, which provides
d\cdot\frac{q+1}{q}\leq n-k+2
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Binary systematic codes satisfy
M=2^{k}
, so Lemma
3.12
gives
\lceil\frac{3}{2}d\rceil\leq n-k+2
, recovering (
2
) as a special case.

We now turn from length bounds to the internal structure of codes that achieve them.

3.4
Structural Consequences of Length-Maximality

We now show that length-maximal codes are highly constrained: they must be symbol-uniform, their pairwise distances take only a restricted set of values, and their defect satisfies a divisibility condition.

Theorem 3.14

(Length-Maximal Codes are Symbol-Uniform)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a length-maximal
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform. Moreover, any code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
of dimension at least
2

obtained by successively shortening
\mathcal{C}
is also symbol-uniform.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. The base case
k=2
is the equality characterisation in Lemma
3.1
.

Assume
k\geq 3
and that
\mathcal{C}
is length-maximal but not symbol-uniform. Some symbol
\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}
appears in at least
q^{k-1}+1
codewords in some coordinate, say coordinate
1
. Consider the shortened code
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(\alpha)}
. Since
|\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(\alpha)}|\geq q^{k-1}+1
, we may choose distinct
u\neq v\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(\alpha)}
.

Since
n=(s+1)(q+1)+k-2\geq s(q+1)+k-1
, Lemma
3.11
applies, so any
q^{k-1}
-subset
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(\alpha)}
has defect
s
, and is length-maximal. By the induction hypothesis,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is symbol-uniform. Thus, by considering any two
q^{k-1}
-subsets of
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{(\alpha)}
with symmetric difference
\{u,v\}
, it follows that
u
and
v
must carry the same symbol in each coordinate, contradicting
u\neq v
. Hence
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform.
∎

Theorem 3.15

(Distance Distribution of Length-Maximal Codes)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a length-maximal
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
, then for any two distinct codewords
u,v\in\mathcal{C}
,

\operatorname{d}(u,v)\in\{d\}\cup\{n,n-1,\ldots,n-k+3\}.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=2
, the result follows from Lemma
3.1
.

Assume
k\geq 3
. By Theorem
3.14
,
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform. Let
u\neq v\in\mathcal{C}
. If
u
and

v
agree in no coordinate then
\operatorname{d}(u,v)=n
. Otherwise, let
i
be a coordinate in which
u_{i}=v_{i}
. The shortened code
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{(u_{i})}
contains both
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
(the shortening of
u
and
v
respectively). By Lemma
3.11
,
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a length-maximal
(n-1,q^{k-1},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code, and the induction hypothesis gives

\operatorname{d}(u,v)=\operatorname{d}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\{d\}\cup\{n-1,\ \ldots,\ (n-1)-(k-1)+3\}=\{d\}\cup\{n-1,n-2,\ldots,n-k+3\},

completing the induction.
∎

Corollary 3.16

(Weight Distribution of Length-Maximal Codes)
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a length-maximal
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
, then (up to code equivalence) every nonzero codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
has weight

w(\mathbf{c})\in\{d\}\cup\{n,n-1,\ldots,n-k+3\}.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
3.15
with
v=\mathbf{0}
, which is in
\mathcal{C}
up to code equivalence.
∎

The following lemma shows that the defect of a length-maximal code satisfies a divisibility condition which closely resembles the conditions
s\leq q-1
, and
(s+2)\mid q
known for linear codes through the results on maximal arcs
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.17
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a length-maximal
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
, then
s\leq q-1
and
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.11
, we may successively shorten
\mathcal{C}
to obtain a length-maximal
(n^{\prime},q^{3},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
n^{\prime}=(s+1)(q+1)+1
. We may assume (perhaps after relabelling) that
\mathbf{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. By Corollary
3.16
, the nonzero codeword weights of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
lie in
\{d\}\cup\{n^{\prime}\}
.

Let
B
denote the set of
q^{2}
codewords of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
whose first entry is the symbol
1
. Since
\mathbf{0}\notin B
, each word in
B
has weight
d
or
n^{\prime}
. A word of weight
d
has

n^{\prime}-d=s+2
zeros, none in the first position. A word of weight
n^{\prime}
has no zeros. Thus every row of
B
contributes either
0
or
s+2
zeros to columns
2,\ldots,n^{\prime}
.

The shortened code
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}_{1}^{(1)}
is a length-maximal
(n^{\prime}-1,q^{2},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code (Lemma
3.11
), so by Theorem
3.14
is symbol-uniform. The total number of zero entries in
B
is therefore
q\cdot(s+1)(q+1)
. Since each row contributes
0
or
s+2
zeros,
(s+2)
divides
q(s+1)(q+1)
, and since
\gcd(s+1,s+2)=1
, we obtain
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
.

For the bound on
s
: the total zero count
q(s+1)(q+1)
is at most
q^{2}(s+2)
(since each of the
q^{2}
rows contributes at most
s+2
zeros), so
q(s+1)(q+1)\leq q^{2}(s+2)
, which simplifies to
s\leq q-1
.
∎

Corollary 3.18
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a length-maximal
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
and
\gcd(s+2,q)=1
, then
(s+2)\mid(q+1)
. In particular, if
q
is prime and
s<q-2
, then
(s+2)\mid(q+1)
.

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.17
.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

For linear length-maximal codes the stronger condition
(s+2)\mid q
is required
[
5
,
3
]
. Lemma
3.17
shows that nonlinear length-maximal codes must satisfy the weaker condition
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
, leaving open whether the stronger linear condition is also
sine qua non
in the nonlinear
setting.

The bounds above are sharp in general. In the next section we show that additional hypotheses such as binary alphabet, large defect, or non-integer dimension, each permit a tighter ceiling.

4
Refinements and Applications

In this section we sharpen the maximal-arc-type bound in two directions. First, for binary codes we obtain a family of length bounds, and extend a bound of Guerrini, Meneghetti, and Sala. Second, for codes whose defect satisfies
s\geq 2q
, we obtain a bound that improves on Theorem
3.5
. We conclude by extending results to codes of non-integer dimension.

4.1
Binary Refinements

We first recall the Hamming (sphere-packing) bound, which will be useful in this section.

Theorem 4.1

(Hamming Bound
[
16
,
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code, where
t=\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor
is the maximum number of errors the code can correct. Then

M\leq\frac{q^{n}}{\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}}.

We now proceed with a preliminary observation that sufficiently long binary codes are forced to be symbol-uniform.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
.
If
n>3s+k-1
, then
\mathcal{C}
is symbol-uniform.

Proof.

The base case
k=2
: suppose
\mathcal{C}
is not symbol-uniform. Then
some coordinate contains a symbol appearing in at least
3
codewords; say

a_{1}=b_{1}=c_{1}=1
for distinct
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
. In each of the remaining
n-1
coordinate positions at
least two of these three codewords must agree, contributing at least one
agreement to each position. Since any two codewords agree in at most

n-d=s+1
positions, we get
n-1\leq 3(s+1)-3=3s
, contradicting

n>3s+1
.

For
k\geq 3
, the induction proceeds in a manner entirely analogous to
the proof of Theorem
3.14
, with
Lemma
3.11
applicable since

n>3s+k-1=s(q+1)+k-1
.
∎

Before discussing further refinements, let us situate the binary case. For
s=0
, the MDS case is completely settled, in that every binary MDS code is equivalent to linear
[
4
]
, and thus satisfies
n\leq k+1
(with equality achievable), and the weight spectra of binary MDS codes are determined explicitly. In particular, no genuinely nonlinear binary MDS codes exist, so binary MDS codes offer no room for nonlinear improvement.

For binary codes with
s=1
, linear length-maximal (
n=k+4
) codes exist if and only if
k\leq 4
, and for
k\geq 5
a linear binary
A^{1}
MDS code has maximum length
k+2
(see e.g. Corollary 4.25 in
[
3
]
). The nonlinear case mirrors that of the linear. Indeed, if a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{1}
MDS code has
n=k+3
, then
d=3
, and the Hamming bound (Theorem
4.1
) gives

2^{k}\leq\frac{2^{n}}{\sum_{i=0}^{1}\binom{n}{i}}=\frac{2^{k+3}}{1+(k+3)}=\frac{8\cdot 2^{k}}{k+4},

which requires
k\leq 4
. Hence no binary
(k+3,2^{k},3)_{2}

A^{1}
MDS code exists for
k\geq 5
, and as such, the maximum length
k+2
of the linear case cannot be exceeded by nonlinear codes. We thus have the following the following:

Lemma 4.3
.

A binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{1}
MDS code satisfies
n\leq k+2
for
k\geq 5
.
In particular, length-maximal binary
A^{1}
MDS codes (of length
k+4
) exist
if and only if
2\leq k\leq 4
. The bound
n=k+2
is achieved for all

k\geq 5
(Corollary 4.25 of
[
3
]
).

The argument extends to
q=3
, and more generally yields a bound in terms of
the
q
-ary Hamming ball volume for all prime powers
q
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{1}
MDS code with
k\geq 2
and

n=k+2q-1
(one step below length-maximal). Then

V(k+2q-1,\,q-1)\leq q^{2q-1},

where
V(n,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}
denotes the volume of the
q
-ary
Hamming ball of radius
t
. Consequently, if
q=2
, then
k\leq 4
, and if
q=3
, then
k\leq 6
.

Proof.

Since
s=1
and
n=k+2q-1
, we have
d=n-k=2q-1
, so

t=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor=q-1
. The Hamming bound
(Theorem
4.1
) gives

q^{k}\leq\frac{q^{n}}{V(n,\,q-1)}=\frac{q^{k+2q-1}}{V(k+2q-1,\,q-1)},

which rearranges to
V(k+2q-1,\,q-1)\leq q^{2q-1}
.

For
q=2
:
V(k+3,1)=1+(k+3)\leq 8
gives
k\leq 4
, recovering
the binary case above.

For
q=3
:
V(k+5,2)=1+2(k+5)^{2}\leq 3^{5}
gives
k\leq 6
, with equality at
k=6
.
∎

Let us now focus on the cases where
s\geq 2
. First, we have the following refinement for 3-dimensional codes.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
t\geq 0
be an integer. If
\mathcal{C}
is a binary
(n,2^{3},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with
s\geq 3t+2
, then

n\leq 3s+2-2t.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
n=3s+3-2t
. From
s=n-3+1-d
we obtain
d=2s+1-2t
is odd, and
s\geq 3t+2
provides

2d+1-n=2(2s+1-2t)+1-(3s+3-2t)=s-2t\geq t+2>0.

The binary Plotkin bound (Theorem
2.4
, part (2)) gives the contradiction:

8=|\mathcal{C}|\leq 2\left\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2d+1-n}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor 2+\frac{2t+2}{s-2t}\right\rfloor\leq 2\left\lfloor 2+\frac{2t+2}{t+2}\right\rfloor=2\left\lfloor 4-\frac{2}{t+2}\right\rfloor\leq 6,

where the second inequality uses
s\geq 3t+2
. So
n\leq 3s+2-2t
.
∎

By leveraging Lemma
3.11
, and shortening, Lemma
4.5
provides an extension to codes of dimension
k\geq 3
.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 3
and
s\geq 2
, then

n\leq 3s+k-1.

Proof.

Since
s\geq 2
, Lemma
4.5
applies with

, establishing the result for
k=3
. For
k>3
, suppose
n\geq 3s+k
. By Lemma
2.9
, successive shortening may be performed to produce an
(n-(k-3),2^{3},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, where
s
is preserved since

, (Lemma
3.11
) . Applying Lemma
4.5
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with

gives
n-(k-3)\leq 3s+2
, hence

, a contradiction.
∎

Utilizing the bound in Corollary

, an argument entirely similar to that in the proof of Corollary
3.8
provides the following.

Corollary 4.7
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with

and
s\geq 2
. If

, binary codes with

respect the Griesmer bound. Pairing this with Corollary

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

MDS code with

\mathcal{C}

Example 4.9
.

The Nordstrom–Robinson code
(16,2^{8},6)_{2}
(see

,

]
) has

,

, and Singleton defect

, and thus this famous code once more stands as a paradigmatic example, meeting the bound of Corollary

with equality.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
t\geq 0
be an integer. If
\mathcal{C}
is a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 3
and
s\geq 3t+2
, then

n\leq 3s-2t+k-1.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.6
, we need only consider
t\geq 1
. The base case
k=3
is Lemma
4.5
. Assume
k\geq 4
and suppose for contradiction
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
n=3s+k-2t
. Take
t\geq 1
maximal such that
s\geq 3t+2
. By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a shortened code
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{(a)}
, and an
(n^{\prime},q^{k^{\prime}},d^{\prime})_{q}
-A
{}^{s^{\prime}}
MDS codewith
n^{\prime}=n-1
,
k^{\prime}=k-1
,
d^{\prime}\geq d
, and
s^{\prime}\leq s
. Let
\alpha=s-s^{\prime}\geq 0
.

Note that by assumption

n^{\prime}=n-1=3s+k-2t-1\geq 3(3t+2)+k-2t-1=k+5+7t\geq(k-1)+13=k^{\prime}+13.

Consequently, (Theorem
3.5
),
s^{\prime}\geq 4
, so Corollary
4.6
may be applied, giving
s^{\prime}\geq 5
, and therefore
0\leq\alpha\leq s-5
.

If
\alpha=0
, then
s^{\prime}=s\geq 3t+2
, and the induction hypothesis gives
n-1\leq 3s+(k-1)-1-2t=3s+k-2t-2
, so

, contradicting

.

If
\alpha\geq 1
, set

. Since

by the maximality of

, we have

, and

. Moreover,

, so the induction hypothesis applies, giving

Hence

, contradicting

.
∎

The following corollary shows that the binary refinement implies the Griesmer bound when
d
is small relative to
s
.

Corollary 4.11

(Griesmer Bound for Binary Codes with Large Defect)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a binary
(n,2^{k},d)_{2}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 3
and
s\geq 3t+2
for some integer
t\geq 0
. If
d\leq 2(t+1)
, then
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the Griesmer bound.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
,

n\leq 3s+k-1-2t.

Substituting
s=n-k+1-d
and rearranging gives

n\geq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil+k-1+t.

On the other hand, for
d\leq 2(t+1)
,

\displaystyle g_{2}(k,d)

\displaystyle=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{2^{i}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle<d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil+(k-2)+\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\frac{d}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil+k-2+\frac{d}{2}

\displaystyle\leq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil+k-1+t,

(2)

since
\frac{d}{2}\leq t+1
. Comparing (1) and (2) completes the proof.
∎

The binary refinements exploit parity of
d
. A different mechanism operates when the defect
s
is large relative to
q
: the general Plotkin bound forces a sharper ceiling for all alphabet sizes.

4.2
Large-Defect Refinements

We now show that when
s\geq 2q
the maximal-arc-type bound tightens by approximately
q
. We establish this first for dimension
3
, and then extend to all
k\geq 3
by induction.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{3},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code. If
s=q-1+t
for some
0\leq t\leq q
, then

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+1-t.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
n=(s+1)(q+1)+2-t
. Then
d=n-(s+2)=sq+q+1-t
, and

dq-n(q-1)=(sq+q+1-t)q-[(s+1)(q+1)+2-t](q-1)=s-q+3-t.

Substituting
s=q-1+t
gives
dq-n(q-1)=2>0
, so the Plotkin bound (Theorem
2.3
) yields
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\lfloor dq/2\rfloor
.

Since
t\leq q
and
s=q-1+t
we have
dq=(sq+q+1-t)q=q^{3}+q+t(q^{2}-q)\leq 2q^{3}-q^{2}+q<2q^{3}
, giving
|\mathcal{C}|<q^{3}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{3},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code. If
s=2q+t
for some
0\leq t\leq q
, then

n\leq s(q+1)+2-t.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
n=s(q+1)+3-t
. Then
d=n-(s+2)=sq+1-t
, and

\displaystyle dq-n(q-1)

\displaystyle=(sq+1-t)q-[s(q+1)+3-t](q-1)

\displaystyle=s-2q+3-t.

Substituting
s=2q+t
gives
dq-n(q-1)=3>0
, so
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\lfloor dq/3\rfloor
. Since
t\leq q<q+1
we have
t(q-1)<(q+1)(q-1)=q^{2}-1
, giving
dq<3q^{3}
and hence

|\mathcal{C}|<q^{3}
, a contradiction.
∎

An interpolating bound incorporating the defect regimes in Lemmas
4.12
and
4.13
follows.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{3},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code. If
\alpha q\leq s<(\alpha+1)q
for some integer
\alpha
with
2\leq\alpha\leq q-2
, then

n\leq(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
n=(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+1
.We have
d=n-(s+2)=(s+2-\alpha)q+\alpha
, so

\displaystyle dq-n(q-1)

\displaystyle=((s+2-\alpha)q+\alpha)q-[(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+1](q-1)

\displaystyle=s+2-\alpha+\alpha q-\alpha q+\alpha-q+1=s-q+3.

Since
s\geq\alpha q\geq 2q
we have
s-q+3\geq q+3>0
, so the Plotkin bound gives
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\lfloor dq/(s-q+3)\rfloor
. Using
s\geq\alpha q
it suffices to show
d<q^{2}(s-q+3)
, which reduces to
\alpha(q^{2}-1)>q(q-2)
. Since
\alpha\geq 2
we have
\alpha(q^{2}-1)\geq 2(q^{2}-1)>q^{2}-2q
for
q\geq 2
, giving the contradiction.
∎

Lemma
4.12
sharpens the bound in Theorem
3.5
in the transitional range
s\in[q-1,2q-1]
, improving it by up to
q
when
t=s-(q-1)
is large, and serves as a stepping stone; Lemma
4.13
gives the finest bound for
s\in[2q,3q)
; and Lemma
4.14
covers all bands
\alpha q\leq s<(\alpha+1)q
for
\alpha\geq 2
, at the cost of coarser divisions within each band. We may extend inductively to
k\geq 3
via shortening.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
k\geq 3
and
s\geq 2q
. Then

n\leq s(q+1)+k-1.

(7)

Consequently, if also
d\leq 2q^{2}
, then
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the Griesmer bound.

Proof.

For the length bound, we induct on
k
. The base case
k=3
is Lemma
4.13
(with
t=0
). For the inductive step, suppose
n>s(q+1)+k-1
. Then
n\geq s(q+1)+k>s(q+1)+(k-1)-1
, so Lemma
3.11
applies and there exists a shortened code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with parameters
(n-1,q^{k-1},d)_{q}
and the same defect

. The induction hypothesis gives
n-1\leq s(q+1)+k-2
, contradicting

.

For the Griesmer bound, substitute

into (
7
) to obtain

n\geq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+k-1.

For

, each term

and is at most

for

, so

g_{q}(k,d)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil\leq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+2+(k-3)=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+k-1\leq n.\qed

Remark 4.16
.

The bound (
7
) improves on Theorem
3.5
by approximately
q
. Lemma
4.14
reveals the finer structure: the improvement is not constant but grows with

\lfloor s/q\rfloor
, as made precise in the following corollary.

Theorem 4.17

(Stratified Length Bound)
.

Let
C
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with dimension
k\geq 3
and Singleton defect
s
. Let
\alpha=\lfloor s/q\rfloor\geq 1
denote the stratification index. Then the length
n
satisfies:

n\leq(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+k-3.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the dimension
k
, with the base case
k=3
established through Theorem
3.5
for
\alpha=1
, and Lemma
4.14
for
\alpha\geq 2
.

Assume the bound holds for codes of dimension
k-1

with stratification index
\alpha^{\prime}\geq 1
. Suppose for contradiction there
exists an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}
-A
s
MDS code, where
\alpha=\lfloor s/q\rfloor\geq 1
, and

n\geq(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+k-2.

(8)

By Lemma
2.9
, there exists an
(n^{\prime},k^{\prime},d^{\prime})
-A
{}^{s^{\prime}}
MDS code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
n^{\prime}=n-1
,
k^{\prime}=k-1
,
d^{\prime}\geq d
, and
s^{\prime}\leq s
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}=\lfloor s^{\prime}/q\rfloor
be the
stratification index of
C^{\prime}
. We consider the cases for
s^{\prime}
:

If
s^{\prime}=s
then
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha
, and the induction hypothesis gives

n^{\prime}=n-1\leq(s^{\prime}+2-\alpha^{\prime})(q+1)+\alpha^{\prime}+(k-1)-3=(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+k-4,

(9)

which after isolating
n
, gives a contradiction to (
8
).

On the other hand,
s=s^{\prime}+t
for some
t\geq 1
, and
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
. From (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle n^{\prime}=n-1

\displaystyle\geq(s+2-\alpha)(q+1)+\alpha+k-3

\displaystyle=(s^{\prime}+t+2-\lfloor\frac{s^{\prime}+t}{q}\rfloor)(q+1)+\alpha+k-3

\displaystyle\geq(s^{\prime}+t+2-\left(\lfloor\frac{s^{\prime}}{q}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{t}{q}\rfloor+1\right))(q+1)+\alpha^{\prime}+k-3

\displaystyle=(s^{\prime}+2-\alpha^{\prime}+\left(t-\lfloor\frac{t}{q}\rfloor-1\right))(q+1)+\alpha^{\prime}+k^{\prime}-2

(10)

If
\alpha^{\prime}=0
, then applying the bound in Theorem
3.5
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
provides a contradiction with (
10
). If
\alpha^{\prime}\geq 1
, then (
10
) contradicts the induction hypothesis.

∎

We note that the inequality
\lfloor(s^{\prime}+t)/q\rfloor\leq\lfloor s^{\prime}/q\rfloor+\lfloor t/q\rfloor+1
utilized above is crude, but sufficient for the required contradiction.

We may now provide two generalized results regarding conditions under which a nonlinear code with sufficiently large defect respects the Griesmer bound.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}
-A
s
MDS code with
s\geq\beta q
for some

. If

, then
\mathcal{C}
respects the Griesmer bound.

Proof.

Applying the bound in Theorem
4.17
, utilizing

, and isolating
n
provides

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\leq(s+2-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3

\displaystyle=(n-k+3-d-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;n\geq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+k+\beta-3.

(11)

On the other hand, if
d\leq\beta q^{2}\leq q^{3}
, then the Griesmer Bound (Theorem
2.5
) provides

g_{q}(k,d)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{2}}\right\rceil+k-3\leq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\beta+k-3

(12)

A comparison of (
11
) and (
12
) provides the result.
∎

Theorem 4.19
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}
-A
s
MDS code
d<q^{r+1}
,
r\geq 1
. If
s\geq\beta q
, where
\beta=\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}
, then
\mathcal{C}
respects the Griesmer bound.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.17
we have
n\leq(s+2-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3.
Substituting
s=n-k+1-d
and isolating
n
provides

n\geq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\beta+k-3

(13)

Since
d\leq q^{r+1}-1
, from (

) we obtain

\displaystyle g_{q}(k,d)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{q^{r+1}-1}{q^{i}}\right\rceil

\displaystyle=d+\left\lceil\frac{d}{q}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=2}^{r+1}\left\lceil\frac{q^{r+1}-1}{q^{i}}\right\rceil+\sum_{i=r+2}^{k-1}\left\lceil\frac{q^{r+1}-1}{q^{i}}\right\rceil

A comparison of (

) and (

) provides the result.
∎

The results of this section apply throughout to codes of integer dimension. We now extend the analysis to codes whose

\kappa=\log_{q}M
is not an integer.

5
Codes of Non-Integer Dimension

The results above admit natural analogues for codes of non-integer dimension, though the picture is starker: rather than merely bounding length, the combinatorial constraints preclude length-maximal codes entirely.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
q^{k}<M<q^{k+1}
,
\kappa=\log_{q}(M)
. Then

n\leq(s+\lceil\kappa\rceil-\lfloor\kappa\rfloor+1)(q+1)+\lfloor\kappa\rfloor-2\;\;(=(s+2)(q+1)+k-2).

Proof.

Recall that
s=n-\lceil\kappa\rceil+1-d
. Choose a subcode
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
with
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|=q^{k}
and minimum distance
d
; this is possible by
selecting any
q^{k}
codewords including two at distance
d
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s^{\prime}}
MDS code with

s^{\prime}=n-k+1-d=s+1
. Applying Theorem
3.5
gives

n\leq(s^{\prime}+1)(q+1)+k-2=(s+2)(q+1)+k-2.\qed

Note that the bound in Lemma
5.1
equates to the main bound (Theorem
3.5
) in the case
\kappa
is an integer. As such, we say any code meeting this bound is length-maximal.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code of non-integer dimension
\kappa>2
, say
q^{k}<M<q^{k+1}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then

n\leq(s+2)(q+1)+\lfloor\kappa\rfloor-3,

where, by definition
s=n-\lceil k\rceil+1-d=n-(k+1)+1-d
.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}
is not length-maximal.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
satisfy the hypothesis, with
n=(s+2)(q+1)+k-2
. Since
M\geq q^{k}+1
there exist at least
q^{k-1}+1
codewords agreeing in coordinate position
1
, and inductively, there exists a
(q^{2}+1)
-subset
S
of codewords agreeing in coordinate positions
1
through
k-2
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the shortened code obtained by deleting the first
k-2
coordinates of each member of
S
. It follows that
\mathcal{S}
is an
(n-k+2,q^{2}+1,d^{\prime})_{q}
code with
d^{\prime}\geq d
. Note that by construction, any two codewords in
\mathcal{S}
agree in at most
n-d-(k-2)=n-k+2-d
coordinates. Since
q\geq 2
, we may let
u\neq v\in\mathcal{S}
, be chosen such that the codes
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\setminus\{u\}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{S}\setminus\{v\}
are
(n^{\prime},q^{2},d^{\prime})
-A
{}^{s^{\prime}}
MDS codes. From Theorem
3.5
, we have
(s+2)(q+1)=n^{\prime}\leq(s^{\prime}+1)(q+1)
, so
s^{\prime}\geq s+1
. By construction,
s^{\prime}=n^{\prime}-2+1-d^{\prime}=n-k+2-2+1-d^{\prime}=n-k+1-d^{\prime}\leq n-k+1-d=s+1
. Thus we have
s^{\prime}=s+1
, so both
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
are length-maximal, and by Theorem
3.14
are both symbol uniform. As such, the coordinate entries in any particular codeword
x\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are uniquely determined by the coordinate entries of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\setminus\{x\}
. Likewise for
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
. As such, we arrive at
u=v
, providing the required contradiction.
∎

The strict bound of Proposition
5.2
tightens the Singleton-type inequality of Lemma
3.12
by one unit in the non-integer case.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
q^{k}<M<q^{k+1}
and
k\geq 2
, then

\left\lfloor d\cdot\frac{q+1}{q}\right\rfloor\leq n-k+1.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.2
the bound of Lemma
5.1
is strict, so
n\leq(s+2)(q+1)+k-3
.
Substituting
s=n-k-d
gives
d(q+1)\leq q(n-k+2)-1
, providing the desired inequality.
∎

The large-defect refinement also extends to non-integer dimension.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
q^{k}<M<q^{k+1}
. If
s\geq 2q-1
, then

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-1.

Consequently, if also
d\leq 2q^{2}
, then
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the Griesmer bound
n\geq g_{q}(k+1,d)
, that is,

n\geq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left\lceil\frac{d}{q^{i}}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Choose
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
with
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}|=q^{k}
and minimum distance
d
as in Lemma
5.1
. Then
s^{\prime}=s+1\geq 2q
, so Corollary
4.15
applied to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
gives

n\leq s^{\prime}(q+1)+k-1=(s+1)(q+1)+k-1.

For the Griesmer bound, substitute
s=n-k-d
(noting
\lceil\kappa\rceil=k+1
) into the length bound to obtain

n\geq d+\lceil d/q\rceil+k
. For
d\leq 2q^{2}
the same calculation as in Corollary
4.15
gives

g_{q}(k+1,d)\leq d+\lceil d/q\rceil+k\leq n
.
∎

Corollary 5.5

(Stratified Bound, Non-Integer Dimension)
.

Let
C
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with non-integer dimension
\kappa=\log_{q}M\in(k,k+1)
for
k\geq 3
and Singleton defect
s\geq 2q-1
. Let
\beta=\lfloor(s+1)/q\rfloor\geq 2
denote the stratification index. Then the length
n
satisfies:

n\leq(s+3-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3.

Proof.

Let
C
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code with
q^{k}<M<q^{k+1}
. By selecting a subcode
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
of size
|C^{\prime}|=q^{k}
that includes two codewords at distance
d
, we obtain an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}
code. The Singleton defect of
C^{\prime}
is given by:

s^{\prime}=n-k+1-d=(n-(k+1)+1-d)+1=s+1.

Since
s\geq 2q-1
, we have
s^{\prime}\geq 2q
. The stratification index
\beta
for the subcode
C^{\prime}
satisfies
\beta q\leq s^{\prime}<(\beta+1)q
by the definition of
\beta=\lfloor s^{\prime}/q\rfloor=\lfloor(s+1)/q\rfloor
.

Applying the Stratified Length Bound (Theorem
4.17
) to the integer-dimension code

, we have:

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\leq(s^{\prime}+2-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3

\displaystyle=((s+1)+2-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3

\displaystyle=(s+3-\beta)(q+1)+\beta+k-3.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The improvement of Corollary
5.5
over the general non-integer bound (Proposition
5.2
) is approximately
(\beta-2)q
. This confirms that the ”stratification” phenomenon—where the maximum achievable length decreases as the defect-to-alphabet ratio grows—is not an artifact of integer dimension, but a fundamental property of the
q
-ary Hamming space. The bound reveals that as
s
moves through successive multiples of
q
, the length ceiling descends in discrete steps of roughly
q
.

5.1
Refining the Non-integer Dimension Bound

Below, we state and prove a sharper bound, utilizing a Plotkin-type argument.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}
code with
M>q
.
Write
M=qb+r
with
b\geq 1
and
0\leq r<q
, and define

L(M)\;:=\;r\binom{b+1}{2}+(q-r)\binom{b}{2}\;=\;q\binom{b}{2}+rb.

1.

\displaystyle n\;\leq\;(n-d)\,\frac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}
,
with equality if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is equidistant and,
in every coordinate, exactly
r
symbols appear
b{+}1
times
and
q{-}r
symbols appear
b
times.

2.

If
b=1
(equivalently,
q<M<2q
), then

\frac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}\;\leq\;\frac{q(q+1)}{2},

with equality if and only if
M=q+1
.

3.

If
b\geq 2
(equivalently,
M\geq 2q
), then

\frac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}\;\leq\;q+\frac{q-1}{b-1},

with equality if and only if
q\mid M
.

4.

If
M\geq q^{2}
(equivalently,
b\geq q
), then

n\leq(n-d)(q+1)
,
with equality if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is equidistant,
symbol-uniform, and
M=q^{2}
.

Proof.

For each coordinate
i
and symbol
a\in\mathcal{A}
, let
f_{i,a}

denote the number of codewords with symbol
a
in position
i
.
Set
A_{i}:=\sum_{a}\binom{f_{i,a}}{2}
and
A:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
;
then
A
counts agreements over all unordered codeword pairs and all
coordinate positions.

Part
1
.

Since any two distinct codewords agree in at most
n-d
positions,

A\leq(n-d)\binom{M}{2}
, with equality iff
\mathcal{C}
is equidistant.
By convexity of
\binom{\cdot}{2}
, among all tuples
(f_{i,a})_{a}

summing to
M
, the quantity
A_{i}
is minimised when
r
entries equal

b+1
and
q-r
equal
b
, giving
A_{i}\geq L(M)
, with equality iff
coordinate
i
achieves this distribution.
Summing and combining:

n\,L(M)\;\leq\;A\;\leq\;(n-d)\binom{M}{2}.

(15)

Since
L(M)>0
for
M>q
, dividing by
L(M)
yields
part
1
, with equality iff both
inequalities (
15
) are equalities simultaneously.

Part
2
.

If
b=1
, then
L(M)=r
(
1\leq r\leq q-1
), so
\dfrac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}=\dfrac{(q+r)(q+r-1)}{2r}=:f(r)
.
For
1\leq r\leq q-2
, a direct computation gives

f(r)-f(r+1)\;=\;\frac{(q+r)(q-1-r)}{2r(r+1)}\;>\;0,

so
f
is strictly decreasing on
\{1,\ldots,q-1\}
. The maximum
f(1)=\dfrac{q(q+1)}{2}
is attained iff
r=1
,
i.e.
M=q+1
.

Part
3
.

If
b\geq 2
, a direct computation yields the identity

\left(q+\frac{q-1}{b-1}\right)L(M)\;-\;\binom{M}{2}\;=\;\frac{r\,\bigl[b(2q-1-r)+(r-1)\bigr]}{2(b-1)}.

(16)

For
r=0
the right-hand side vanishes; for
1\leq r\leq q-1
, since
2q-1-r\geq q>0
and
b\geq 2
, we have
b(2q-1-r)\geq 2q>0
and
r-1\geq 0
, so the right-hand side is strictly positive. Hence
\dfrac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}\leq q+\dfrac{q-1}{b-1}
, with equality iff
r=0
, i.e.
q\mid M
.

Part
4
.

Since
b\geq q
, applying part
3
gives

\frac{\binom{M}{2}}{L(M)}\;\leq\;q+\frac{q-1}{b-1}\;\leq\;q+\frac{q-1}{q-1}\;=\;q+1,

with equality in the second step iff
b=q
, and in the first iff
r=0
(part
3
).
Thus the ratio equals
q+1
iff
M=q^{2}
. Substituting into part
1
gives
n\leq(n-d)(q+1)
.
Equality throughout requires equidistance,
M=q^{2}
, and the balanced frequency distribution of part
1
; with
r=0
and
b=q
, the last condition is exactly symbol-uniformity.
∎

By the pigeonhole principle, in any
(n,M,d)_{q}
code with
q^{k}\leq M
,
each coordinate contains some symbol in at least
\lceil M/q\rceil\geq q^{\kappa-1}
codewords.
Applying this observation iteratively, shortening in
k-2
suitably chosen
coordinates yields a code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with parameters

(n-k+2,\,M^{\prime},\,d^{\prime})_{q}
,
A^{s^{\prime}}
MDS, where
n^{\prime}=n-k+2
,
d^{\prime}\geq d

(so
s^{\prime}\leq s
), and
M^{\prime}\geq q^{\kappa-k+2}\geq q^{2}
.
In particular,
b^{\prime}:=\lfloor M^{\prime}/q\rfloor\geq\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor\geq q\geq 2
,
so Part
3
of Lemma
5.7

applies to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with

q^{k}\leq M<q^{k+1}
,
k\geq 2
, and
\kappa=\log_{q}M
. Then

n-k+2\;\leq\;(n-d-k+2)\!\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\right),

(17)

equivalently,

n\;\leq\;(s+\lceil\kappa\rceil-k+1)\!\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\right)+k-2.

(18)

In the two principal cases this specialises as follows.

1.

If
\kappa\notin\mathbb{Z}
(so
\lceil\kappa\rceil=k+1

and
k=\lfloor\kappa\rfloor
):

n\;\leq\;(s+2)\!\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\!\left\lfloor M/q^{k-1}\right\rfloor-1}\right)+\lfloor\kappa\rfloor-2.

2.

If
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
(so
\kappa=k
and
M=q^{k}
):

n\;\leq\;(s+1)(q+1)+k-2,

recovering Theorem
3.5
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be obtained by
k-2
successive shortenings as described
above, with parameters
(n^{\prime},M^{\prime},d^{\prime})_{q}
where
n^{\prime}=n-k+2
,
d^{\prime}\geq d
,
and
b^{\prime}=\lfloor M^{\prime}/q\rfloor\geq\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor\geq q\geq 2
.
Since
q+\frac{q-1}{b-1}
is decreasing in
b
,
Part
3
of Lemma
5.7
gives

\frac{\binom{M^{\prime}}{2}}{L(M^{\prime})}\;\leq\;q+\frac{q-1}{b^{\prime}-1}\;\leq\;q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\;=:\;F.

Applying Part
1
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
and using

d^{\prime}\geq d
:

\displaystyle n^{\prime}\;\leq\;(n^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\,\frac{\binom{M^{\prime}}{2}}{L(M^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;(n^{\prime}-d^{\prime})\,\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\right)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;(n^{\prime}-d)\,\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;(n-d-k+2)\,\left(q+\frac{q-1}{\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor-1}\right),

which is (
17
). Adding
k-2
to both sides
and substituting
d=n-\lceil\kappa\rceil+1-s
gives (
18
). The two special cases follow by setting

\lceil\kappa\rceil=k+1
(non-integer) and
\lceil\kappa\rceil=k
,

\lfloor q^{\kappa-k+1}\rfloor=q
(integer), respectively.
∎

When
\kappa
is a non-integer rational, the quantity
q^{\kappa-k+1}

is itself an integer, so the floor in the non-integer case of
Lemma
5.8
is redundant.
To see this, write
\kappa=a/b
in lowest terms with
b\geq 2
.
Since
M=q^{a/b}
is a positive integer and
\gcd(a,b)=1
, the
value
r:=q^{1/b}
is necessarily a positive integer, and
q=r^{b}
,

M=r^{a}
.
Then
q^{\kappa-k+1}=r^{a-bk+b}
, which is an integer because

a\geq bk
(from
\kappa\geq k
) gives
a-bk+b\geq b\geq 1
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
(n,M,d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with

q^{k}\leq M<q^{k+1}
and
k\geq 2
.
Suppose
\kappa=\log_{q}M=a/b
is rational, non-integer, and
in lowest terms with
b\geq 2
.
Let
r\geq 2
be the positive integer satisfying
q=r^{b}
and

M=r^{a}
. Then

n\;\leq\;(s+2)\!\left(q+\frac{q-1}{r^{a-bk+b}-1}\right)+k-2.

Proof.

By the discussion above,
q^{\kappa-k+1}=r^{a-bk+b}
is an
integer no less than
r^{b}=q\geq 2
, so the floor in the
non-integer case of Lemma
5.8
is
superfluous, and the bound reduces directly to the stated
inequality.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Corollary
5.9
applies naturally to

additive
codes.
An additive
[n,k/h,d]_{q}^{h}
code, which is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{h}}^{n}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension
k
, has

M=q^{k}
codewords over the alphabet
\mathbb{F}_{q^{h}}
.
Viewed as a code over the alphabet of size
Q=q^{h}
, its dimension
is
\kappa=\log_{Q}M=k/h
.
In the notation of Corollary
5.9
, set

r=q
,
b=h
, and
a=k
; the hypotheses are satisfied and the
bound specialises to

n\;\leq\;(s+2)\!\left(q^{h}+\frac{q^{h}-1}{q^{k\bmod h}-1}\right)+\left\lfloor\frac{k}{h}\right\rfloor-2,

where
k\bmod h
denotes the remainder of
k
upon division by
h
.
Griesmer-type bounds for additive codes have been studied from a
geometric perspective in
[
6
]
; the bound above
provides a complementary combinatorial approach valid for all
additive codes of fractional dimension, without appeal to projective

.

Let
\mathcal{C}

Suppose

This completes the development of bounds and structural results. We now summarise the contributions and provide some open problems.

6
Conclusion

The central contribution of this paper is a purely combinatorial proof that the maximal-arc bound

n\leq(s+1)(q+1)+k-2

holds for all
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS codes with
k\geq 2
, without any appeal to linearity or projective geometry. Codes attaining this bound—which we call length-maximal—are necessarily symbol-uniform (Theorem
3.14
), have pairwise distances confined to
\{d\}\cup\{n-k+3,\dots,n\}
(Theorem
3.15
), and satisfy the divisibility condition
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
(Lemma
3.17
).

An equivalent reformulation yields an improved Singleton-type inequality that recovers and extends the bound of Guerrini, Meneghetti, and Sala for binary systematic codes. For large defect we obtain stratified upper bounds on length whose improvement over the main bound grows linearly with
\lfloor s/q\rfloor
. We further identify several natural regimes in which nonlinear codes nevertheless satisfy the Griesmer bound (summarized in Table

The results parallel the classical theory of maximal arcs in finite projective geometry, yet remain entirely combinatorial. For linear codes the situation is well-understood; for nonlinear codes the picture remains comparatively moribund.

The principal open problem raised by this work is the following.

The odd
q
problem.

For linear codes, the theorem of Ball, Blokhuis, and Mazzocca
[
5
]
shows that non-trivial length-maximal
A^{s}
MDS codes do not exist when
q
is odd and
s<q-2
. The proof is irreducibly geometric. Whether genuinely nonlinear length-maximal codes can exist in this regime—despite satisfying only the weaker divisibility condition
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
—remains open. A positive answer would demonstrate that nonlinear codes can significantly exceed the linear length ceiling; a negative answer would require a new combinatorial obstruction.

Note that even in the intermediate additive setting, some linear-style bounds are already exceeded (longer quasi-MDS codes exist), yet length-maximality for dimensions greater than 3 still appears elusive
[
8
]
. This suggests that the boundary between linear and nonlinear behaviour may be subtle, and that combinatorial obstructions could persist beyond the purely linear case.

Further natural questions include:

Unique extendability.

A classical result of Barlotti
[
7
]
, recently extended to all dimensions
[
3
]
, shows that a linear
A^{s}
MDS code of length
(s+1)(q+1)+k-3
admits a unique extension to a length-maximal code whenever
(s+2)\mid q
and
s<q-2
. The analogous question for nonlinear codes with
(k,s)\neq(2,0)
is entirely open. For
k\geq 4
, does an
(n,q^{k},d)_{q}

A^{s}
MDS code with
n=(s+1)(q+1)+k-3
and
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
admit a unique extension to a length-maximal code? The symbol-uniformity and weight distribution results presented here may prove useful in approaching this problem.

Sharpening the divisibility condition.

Lemma
3.17
establishes
(s+2)\mid q(q+1)
. For linear length-maximal codes the stronger condition
(s+2)\mid q
is required
[
5
,
3
]
. Whether
(s+2)\mid q
holds for all nonlinear length-maximal codes remains open. A positive answer would bring the nonlinear theory into complete structural alignment with the linear one.

AMDS codes and the even-distance case.

Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
use the Hamming bound to show that near-length-maximal
A^{1}
MDS codes of length
k+2q-1
exist only for
k\leq 4
when
q=2
and
k\leq 6
when
q=3
. The even-distance case
n=k+2q-2
is inaccessible to the Hamming bound and remains open for large
k
. More generally, determining the maximum dimension of a near-length-maximal
A^{s}
MDS code for
s\geq 2
and general
q
is open.

Existence of length-maximal nonlinear codes.

In
[
3
]
, the maximum dimension
k
of a length-maximal
A^{s}
MDS code was conjectured to be at most
5
. This conjecture has recently been settled in the affirmative
[
2
]
. For nonlinear codes the analogous question is entirely open.

The necessary conditions established here, including symbol-uniformity, restricted distance spectrum, and the divisibility condition, substantially constrain the search space and may guide future constructions or non-existence proofs. In particular, they suggest that many geometric constraints on maximal arcs persist, at least in weakened form, even after linearity is dropped.

This work provides a combinatorial foundation for the study of length-maximal codes and highlights the intriguing possibility that the boundary between linear and nonlinear behaviour under such conditions may be more subtle than expected.
