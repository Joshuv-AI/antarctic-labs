Local tensor-train surrogates for quantum learning models
Sreeraj Rajindran Nair, Christopher Ferrie

Abstract
A key bottleneck in quantum machine learning is the computational cost of repeated quantum circuit evaluations during the inference phase. To address this, we present a framework for constructing fast, cheap, provably accurate classical tensor-train surrogates of fully trained quantum machine learning models within local patches of their input data space. The approach combines Taylor polynomial approximation with a tensor-train (TT) representation and embeds it in a statistical learning paradigm via empirical risk minimization. In our analysis, the Taylor-TT construction serves as a deterministic error certificate proving that the TT hypothesis class contains a good approximation; empirical risk minimization then provably recovers a surrogate with controlled generalization error and explicit bounds. This translates into three independently controllable error sources: (i) Taylor truncation error controlled by the patch radius r r and polynomial degree p p , (ii) TT approximation error controlled by the bond dimension χ \chi , and (iii) statistical estimation error. While the parameter count scales polynomially in the number of data dimensions N N , i.e., d eff = N ⁡ ( p + 1 ) ​ χ 2 d_{\mathrm{eff}}=N(p+1)\chi^{2} rather than the naive ( p + 1 ) N (p+1)^{N} , the worst-case constants inherit an exponential factor through the tensor-product feature norm during Taylor polynomial embedding onto TT. This cleanly separates representation complexity from feature-induced constants. Our risk bounds and sample complexity depend explicitly on the local patch radius r r .

Framework overview

In this paper, we introduce local tensor-train (TT) surrogates (LTTS) for a broad class of trained quantum learning models
[
20
,
59
,
44
]
. Tensor trains (TT)
[
5
,
12
,
36
,
37
]
, also known in the physics literature as matrix product states (MPS), are a structured tensor network decomposition designed to efficiently represent quantum states with limited entanglement. We treat a trained quantum model as a black-box function and focus on a local patch around a point of interest. Our goal is to construct a local surrogate that is accurate in the patch, efficiently parameterized through the TT structure , and learnable from noisy samples with quantifiable sample complexity. We do not assume any special reconstruction structure intrinsic to the underlying quantum model. This distinguishes our setting from structure-dependent reconstruction frameworks such as those of Schreiber et al.
[
48
]
. Instead, our analysis combines classical approximation theory with probably approximately correct (PAC) learning guarantees
[
51
]
.

The theoretical backbone of the framework is a deterministic error certificate constructed from a local Taylor approximation together with TT compression. The deterministic error terms originating from Taylor truncation are characterized using Taylor remainder terms, while the error term due to TT representation is characterized using the results of Oseledets
[
36
]
. In statistical learning terms, this certificate plays the role of a witness hypothesis: it shows that the constrained TT class contains a good local approximant to the black-box function on the patch. Aldavero et al
[
45
]
had introduced Chebyshev approximation for quantum numerical analyses, wherein they traced out a similar polynomial approximation to TT embedding.

Deterministic certificate

(Theorem
5.1
).
On a local patch of radius
r
, the Taylor–TT construction yields a predictor whose pointwise error over the patch is bounded by the sum of a Taylor truncation term (controlled by
r
and
p
) and a TT compression term
(controlled by
\chi
):

|g(x)-h_{A_{\chi}}(x)|\;\leq\;\underbrace{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Taylor}}(r,p)}_{\text{Taylor truncation}}\;+\;\underbrace{\varepsilon_{TT}}_{\text{TT compression}}\;=:\;E_{\mathrm{det}}.

where
\varepsilon_{TT}
absorbs the feature-norm constant. Both terms are deterministic approximation terms and do not depend on the number of training samples.

We set up a statistical learning task based on empirical risk minimization (ERM)
[
54
,
51
]
, wherein the goal is to learn a TT predictor from a constrained TT hypothesis (parameterized by TT cores and a fixed degree-p tensor-product feature map) class that closely approximates the target black-box function with quantifiable sample complexity. Using pseudo-dimension bounds for tensor-network models
[
22
]
together with a uniform-convergence argument for bounded squared loss
[
52
,
32
,
55
]
, we obtain high-probability excess-risk guarantees for the learned surrogate. Thus, the deterministic certificate controls the approximation side of the problem, while the statistical analysis controls the estimation side. In practice, the learner does not need to compute Taylor coefficients or perform TT-SVD, although the certificate may also be useful as a warm start for the learning pipeline. In our analysis, the explicit dependence of the risk and sample-complexity bounds on the patch radius enters through this Taylor–TT construction. This yields polynomial complexity in the TT model parameters, although worst-case constants can still retain dimension dependence, thus inheriting the curse of dimensionality.

Statistical guarantee

(Theorem
7.4
).
When ERM is performed over the bounded TT hypothesis class, the population risk (expected squared error on unseen points) of the learned surrogate
\hat{h}
satisfies, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

R(\hat{h})\;\leq\;E_{\mathrm{det}}^{2}\;+\;\Delta_{n}(\delta),

where
\Delta_{n}(\delta)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. The statistical term depends on the pseudo-dimension of the TT class, which scales as
d_{\mathrm{eff}}=O(N(p+1)\chi^{2}\log N)
i.e. polynomially in all relevant parameters, in contrast to the exponential
(p+1)^{N}
scaling of an unconstrained polynomial model. The certificate
E_{\mathrm{det}}^{2}
enters as an upper bound on the best achievable risk in the class; ERM finds a hypothesis at most
\Delta_{n}
worse.

Local dependence on the patch radius

(Theorem
7.5
).
Shrinking the patch radius

\Lambda^{\star}(r)

compresses under TT-SVD, which is problem-dependent and can be assessed empirically. Our numerical experiments indicate that, for the quantum models tested, smaller patches are associated with lower approximation error.

2
Truncated Taylor Model

We will begin with formally defining the local patch we intend to surrogate.

Definition 2.1

(Patch and normalized coordinates)
.

Fix a center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and radius
r>0
. Define the
\ell^{\infty}
-hypercube

\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|x-x_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq r\}.

For
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
, define the normalized coordinate
\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}
by

\xi:=\frac{x-x_{0}}{r}\qquad\text{(componentwise)}.

Local tensor-train surrogates (LTTS) are formalized for a locally smooth blackbox function, and we formally define these as follows:

Definition 2.2

(Local function)
.

Let
g:\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)\to\mathbb{R}
be the target function, assumed to be
(p+1)
-times
continuously differentiable on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
. Define the normalized local function

f:[-1,1]^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
by

f(\xi):=g(x_{0}+r\xi).

Also define

C_{p+1}^{(x)}:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)}\max_{|\alpha|=p+1}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g(x)|<\infty

and there exists a finite constant:

C_{\leq p}^{(x)}:=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)}\max_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq p}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g(x)|<\infty.

The local patch function is approximated with a truncated Taylor series, and we present the accrued truncation error bound in this section. We use standard multi-index notation throughout. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}
, let
|\alpha|=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}
,
\alpha!=\prod_{i}\alpha_{i}!
, and
\xi^{\alpha}=\prod_{i}\xi_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Taylor truncation error)
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as in Definition
2.2
. Define the total-degree-
p
Taylor polynomial on the normalized patch:

T_{p}(\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq p}\frac{r^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})\,\xi^{\alpha},\qquad\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}.

Then for all
\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}
,

\bigl|g(x_{0}+r\xi)-T_{p}(\xi)\bigr|\leq\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}.

Proof.

For a scalar function
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that is
(p+1)
-times
continuously differentiable on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
, the Taylor expansion around
x_{0}
up to total degree
p
is

g(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq p}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})\cdot(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}+R_{p}(x),

where
R_{p}(x)
is the remainder term. The standard multivariate remainder bound gives

|R_{p}(x)|\leq\sup_{z\in[x_{0},x]}\max_{|\alpha|=p+1}|\partial^{\alpha}g(z)|\cdot\sum_{|\alpha|=p+1}\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}}{\alpha!},

where
[x_{0},x]
denotes the line segment from
x_{0}
to
x
.

Set
x=x_{0}+r\xi
where
\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}
. Then
x-x_{0}=r\xi
and

|x-x_{0}|^{\alpha}=(r|\xi|)^{\alpha}=r^{|\alpha|}|\xi|^{\alpha}.

Since
|\xi_{i}|\leq 1
, we have
|\xi|^{\alpha}\leq 1
. For
|\alpha|=p+1
:

|R_{p}(x_{0}+r\xi)|\leq C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\sum_{|\alpha|=p+1}\frac{1}{\alpha!}.

Using the multinomial identity
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\frac{1}{\alpha!}=\frac{N^{m}}{m!}
with
m=p+1
yields

|R_{p}(x_{0}+r\xi)|\leq\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}.\qed

3
Embedding Scheme

In order to embed the truncated Taylor polynomial constructed from the local patch into a tensor-train (TT) format, we define a custom TT-compatible feature map wherein the factorial terms of the Taylor polynomial form a factorial feature vector as defined below:

Definition 3.1

(Factorial feature map of degree
p
)
.

Fix an integer
p\geq 0
. For
\xi_{i}\in[-1,1]
, define the factorial feature vector

v(\xi_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1},\qquad v(\xi_{i})_{k}:=\frac{\xi_{i}^{k}}{k!},\quad k=0,1,\ldots,p.

Define the tensor-product feature map

\Phi(\xi):=v(\xi_{1})\otimes v(\xi_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes v(\xi_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)^{N}}.

The feature map
\Phi(\xi)
is naturally indexed by multi-indices
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots,p\}^{N}
(the “box” truncation), with

\Phi(\xi)[\alpha]=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\xi_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{\alpha_{i}!}.

Throughout this paper, we use the following terminology:

•

The
simplex
(or
total-degree index set
) refers to

\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}:|\alpha|\leq p\}
, which indexes
the Taylor polynomial
T_{p}
.

•

The
box
(or
product index set
) refers to

\{0,1,\ldots,p\}^{N}
, the Cartesian product structure required
for tensor-train decomposition.

The Taylor polynomial
T_{p}(\xi)
in Theorem
2.1
uses
simplex truncation
(total degree
|\alpha|\leq p
) which has
\binom{N+p}{p}
terms, whereas the TT-compatible tensor-product feature map
\Phi(\xi)
is indexed by the
box

\{0,\ldots,p\}^{N}
which has
(p+1)^{N}
entries. To express
T_{p}
as an inner product with
\Phi
, we embed the simplex coefficients into the box tensor space by zero-padding. The zero-padding ensures that the “extra” box entries (those with
|\alpha|>p
) do not contribute to the inner product. We provide numerical evidence substantiating that such a zero-padding embedding scheme doesn’t exhibit systematic rank inflation under TT decomposition. This embedding is what enables TT approximation of the corresponding coefficient tensor as defined below, while preserving the Taylor polynomial structure.

Definition 3.2

(Embedded Taylor coefficient tensor)
.

Define the order-
N
tensor
A^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times\cdots\times(p+1)}
indexed by
\alpha\in\{0,1,\ldots,p\}^{N}
via

A^{\star}[\alpha]:=\begin{cases}r^{|\alpha|}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0}),&\text{if }|\alpha|\leq p,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{if }|\alpha|>p.\end{cases}

Equivalently, using the normalized target
f(\xi)=g(x_{0}+r\xi)
and the chain rule identity
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(0)=r^{|\alpha|}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})
:

A^{\star}[\alpha]=\begin{cases}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(0),&\text{if }|\alpha|\leq p,\\[4.0pt]
0,&\text{if }|\alpha|>p.\end{cases}

Note that
A^{\star}
stores the
unnormalized
derivatives; the factorials are carried by the feature map.

Lemma 3.1

(Taylor polynomial as inner product)
.

With
A^{\star}
as in Definition
3.2
and
\Phi
as in Definition
3.1
, for all
\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}
:

T_{p}(\xi)=\left\langle A^{\star},\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle,

where
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
denotes the Frobenius inner product.

Proof.

Expanding the inner product:

\displaystyle\left\langle A^{\star},\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,p\}^{N}}A^{\star}[\alpha]\,\Phi(\xi)[\alpha]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,p\}^{N}}A^{\star}[\alpha]\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\xi_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{\alpha_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq p}r^{|\alpha|}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\xi_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{\alpha_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq p}\frac{r^{|\alpha|}}{\alpha!}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})\,\xi^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=T_{p}(\xi),

where the third equality uses that
A^{\star}[\alpha]=0
for
|\alpha|>p
as per zero-padding, and the fourth uses
\alpha!=\prod_{i}\alpha_{i}!
.
∎

We explicitly bound the feature vector defined in Definition
3.1
and identified the bound as a modified Bessel function. Later, this bound will feature in the TT error term.

Lemma 3.2

(Bessel feature norm bound)
.

Let
I_{0}
denote the modified Bessel function of the first kind (order
0
):

I_{0}(2)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k!)^{2}}.

Define
K:=\sqrt{I_{0}(2)}\approx 1.50983
(numerically). Then for all
\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}
,

\|\Phi(\xi)\|_{2}\leq K^{N}.

Proof.

By definition of
\Phi(\xi)
as a tensor (Kronecker) product and using
\|u\otimes w\|_{2}=\|u\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}
we have:

\|\Phi(\xi)\|_{2}=\|v(\xi_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes v(\xi_{N})\|_{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\|v(\xi_{i})\|_{2},

For each coordinate
i
, since
|\xi_{i}|\leq 1
:

\|v(\xi_{i})\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{p}\left(\frac{\xi_{i}^{k}}{k!}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k!)^{2}}=I_{0}(2).

Thus
\|v(\xi_{i})\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{I_{0}(2)}=K
for each
i
, and multiplying over
i=1,\ldots,N
gives
\|\Phi(\xi)\|_{2}\leq K^{N}
.
∎

K^{N}
grows exponentially in
N
. This exponential factor is a manifestation of the curse of dimensionality for full tensor-product features.

Lemma 3.3

(
r
-dependent upper bound on
\|A^{\star}\|_{F}
)
.

Under Definition
2.2
, the embedded Taylor coefficient tensor satisfies

\|A^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\Lambda^{\star}(r):=C_{\leq p}^{(x)}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{N+m-1}{m}r^{2m}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

For each multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=m\leq p
by Definition
3.2
:

|A^{\star}[\alpha]|=|r^{m}(\partial^{\alpha}g)(x_{0})|\leq r^{m}C_{\leq p}^{(x)}.

For
|\alpha|>p
, we have
A^{\star}[\alpha]=0
by same definition. Therefore:

\|A^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,p\}^{N}}|A^{\star}[\alpha]|^{2}=\sum_{m=0}^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}\\
|\alpha|=m\end{subarray}}|A^{\star}[\alpha]|^{2}\leq(C_{\leq p}^{(x)})^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{p}r^{2m}\cdot\#\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N}:|\alpha|=m\}.

where
\#
represents the cardinality of the set. The number of nonnegative integer solutions to
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{N}=m
is
\binom{N+m-1}{m}
. Taking the square root yields the claim.
∎

4
Tensor-Train (TT) Representation

We formalize a tensor-train using the following definition:

Definition 4.1

(TT-rank and TT tensor class)
.

An order-
N
tensor
A\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times\cdots\times(p+1)}
has TT-rank at most
\chi
if it admits a representation

A[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}]=G_{1}[\alpha_{1}]\,G_{2}[\alpha_{2}]\cdots G_{N}[\alpha_{N}],

where
G_{k}[\alpha_{k}]\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times r_{k}}
are matrices (cores) with
r_{0}=r_{N}=1
and
r_{k}\leq\chi
for
k=1,\ldots,N-1
.

For
\Lambda>0
and
\chi\in\mathbb{N}
, define the constrained tensor class

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi):=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times\cdots\times(p+1)}:\rankTT(A)\leq\chi,\,\|A\|_{F}\leq\Lambda\}.

Definition 4.2

(Best TT approximation error in the constrained class)
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
and a TT-rank budget
\chi\in\mathbb{N}
. Define

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi):=\inf\{\|A^{\star}-A\|_{F}:A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\}.

Lemma 4.1

(TT-SVD error bound)
.

Let
A
be an order-
N
tensor and let
A_{\mathrm{TT}}
be obtained by TT-SVD (left-orthonormal / left-canonical) with uniform rank cap
\chi
. Let
\sigma_{k,j}
denote the singular values of the
k
-th unfolding of
A
. Then

\|A-A_{\mathrm{TT}}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j>\chi}\sigma_{k,j}^{2}.

Proof.

This is the standard TT-SVD quasi-optimality estimate; see Oseledets
[
36
]
, Eq. (2.4).
∎

5
Deterministic Error Bounds

We have all the pieces to put together a deterministic error bound for the Taylor-TT certificate predictor.

Lemma 5.1

(Relation between
x
- and
\xi
-derivative bounds)
.

As defined under the Definition
2.2
, the normalized target
f(\xi)=g(x_{0}+r\xi)
satisfies

C_{p+1}^{(\xi)}:=\sup_{\xi\in[-1,1]^{N}}\max_{|\alpha|=p+1}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi)|\leq r^{p+1}C_{p+1}^{(x)}.

Proof.

By the chain rule,
\partial_{\xi_{i}}=r\,\partial_{x_{i}}
. Thus
\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f(\xi)=r^{|\alpha|}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g)(x_{0}+r\xi)
. Taking
|\alpha|=p+1
and suprema yields the result.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(TT approximation error propagates with
K^{N}
)
.

Let
\xi=(x-x_{0})/r
. Then for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
,

|T_{p}(\xi)-h_{A_{\mathrm{TT}}}(x)|\leq\|A^{\star}-A_{\mathrm{TT}}\|_{F}K^{N}.

Proof.

Recall that
\xi=(x-x_{0})/r
. By Lemma
3.1
,

T_{p}(\xi)=\left\langle A^{\star},\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle=h_{A^{\star}}(x).

Apply Lemma
6.1
with
A=A^{\star}
and
B=A_{\mathrm{TT}}
.
∎

Theorem 5.1

(Deterministic error decomposition (Taylor-TT certificate predictor))
.

Let
g
be defined under Definition
2.2
. Let
A_{\mathrm{TT}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
be arbitrary.
Then for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
, writing
\xi=(x-x_{0})/r
, we have

\bigl|g(x)-h_{A_{\mathrm{TT}}}(x)\bigr|\leq\underbrace{\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}}_{\text{Taylor truncation}}\;+\;\underbrace{\|A^{\star}-A_{\mathrm{TT}}\|_{F}\,K^{N}}_{\text{TT approximation}}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality,

|g(x)-h_{A_{\mathrm{TT}}}(x)|\leq|g(x_{0}+r\xi)-T_{p}(\xi)|+|T_{p}(\xi)-h_{A_{\mathrm{TT}}}(x)|.

Apply Theorem
2.1
to the first term and Lemma
5.2
to the second.
∎

Remark 5.1

(When the deterministic certificate is informative)
.

Theorem
5.1
holds for any
(\Lambda,\chi)
and does
not
assume
that
A^{\star}
is well-compressible in TT format. The bound is quantitatively informative
when the TT approximation term does not dominate over the Taylor truncation term. This condition is problem-dependent and can be assessed empirically by measuring the TT
approximation error of the embedded Taylor tensor
A^{\star}
as a function of
\chi
.

6
Statistical Learning Framework

Now that we have a certificate predictor with deterministic error bounds, we will embed it into a statistical learning framework. For this, we define an appropriate hypothesis class for the learning task.

Definition 6.1

(TT hypothesis class)
.

Define the hypothesis class of TT-parameterized predictors:

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi):=\left\{h_{A}(x)=\left\langle A,\Phi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{r}\right)\right\rangle:A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\right\}.

Equivalently,
h_{A}(x)=\tilde{h}_{A}(\xi)
where
\tilde{h}_{A}(\xi):=\left\langle A,\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle
and
\xi=(x-x_{0})/r
.

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
in Definition
6.1
is
not
restricted to simplex
support: an arbitrary
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
may place mass on
|\alpha|>p
terms.
Our deterministic Taylor-TT bound (Theorem
5.1
) should be interpreted as an

approximation certificate
for the class: it upper-bounds the error of the specific predictor

h_{A_{\mathrm{TT}}}
obtained by TT-approximating the embedded Taylor tensor
A^{\star}
.

Lemma 6.1

(Prediction boundedness and Lipschitz continuity in
A
)
.

For any tensors
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)\times\cdots\times(p+1)}
and any
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
:

|h_{A}(x)|\leq\|A\|_{F}\,K^{N},\qquad|h_{A}(x)-h_{B}(x)|\leq\|A-B\|_{F}\,K^{N}.

For
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
, we have
|h_{A}(x)|\leq\Lambda K^{N}
uniformly over
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
.

Proof.

Recall that
\xi=(x-x_{0})/r\in[-1,1]^{N}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
3.2
:

|h_{A}(x)|=|\left\langle A,\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle|\leq\|A\|_{F}\|\Phi(\xi)\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{F}\,K^{N}.

The Lipschitz bound follows similarly:
|h_{A}(x)-h_{B}(x)|=|\left\langle A-B,\Phi(\xi)\right\rangle|\leq\|A-B\|_{F}\,K^{N}
.
∎

Definition 6.2

(Local TT hypothesis class)
.

Given patch radius
r>0
and bond dimension
\chi\in\mathbb{N}
, define the canonical local hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{\star}(r),\chi)
where
\Lambda^{\star}(r)
is given by Lemma
3.3
. By construction,
A^{\star}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{\star}(r),\chi)
whenever
\rankTT(A^{\star})\leq\chi
.

With the existence of a good hypothesis established via the Taylor-TT certificate with deterministic error decomposition in Theorem
5.1
, we define a
learning task
as a regression problem, where the learner collects input-output samples
(X_{i},Y_{i})
from the patch (where
Y_{i}=g(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}
may include measurement noise) and fits a predictor from a constrained tensor-train (TT) hypothesis class that minimizes the expected squared loss via empirical risk minimization (ERM)
[
54
,
51
]
. The statistical analysis proceeds by using standard theoretical machine learning tools to bound the population/true risk. We use
uniform convergence
[
52
]
of empirical risk to population risk to show that the ERM solution generalizes i.e. low
training error implies low generalization error. We will now elaborate on these steps in the following sections.

Definition 6.3

(Local regression model)
.

We define
local regression model
on the patch
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
as a tuple

(P_{X},\,g,\,\varepsilon,\,G_{\max},\,\sigma)
where:

1.

(X_{i},Y_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
are i.i.d. with
X_{i}\sim P_{X}
(probability distribution)
supported on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
;

2.

Y_{i}=g(X_{i})+\varepsilon_{i}
with

\E[\varepsilon_{i}\mid X_{i}]=0
; (unbiased error/noise)

3.

|g(x)|\leq G_{\max}
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)

and
|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq\sigma
a.s. (bounded signal and noise)

We write
|Y_{i}|\leq Y_{\max}:=G_{\max}+\sigma
a.s.

Definition 6.4
.

Define the population and empirical risks:

L(h):=\E[(h(X)-Y)^{2}],\qquad\hat{L}_{n}(h):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(h(X_{i})-Y_{i})^{2}.

Define the clean (noise-free) risk:

R(h):=\E[(h(X)-g(X))^{2}].

Lemma 6.2

(Excess risk transfers from
L
to
R
)
.

Under
\E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0
:

L(h)=R(h)+\E[\varepsilon^{2}]\qquad\text{for all measurable }h,

so for any hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
:

L(h)-\inf_{h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}}L(h^{\prime})=R(h)-\inf_{h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}}R(h^{\prime}).

Proof.

Expand
L(h)=\E[(h-g-\varepsilon)^{2}]=\E[(h-g)^{2}]+\E[\varepsilon^{2}]-2\E[(h-g)\varepsilon]
. Using the tower property and
\E[\varepsilon\mid X]=0
:

\E[(h-g)\varepsilon]=\E[\E[(h(X)-g(X))\varepsilon\mid X]]=\E[(h(X)-g(X))\cdot\E[\varepsilon\mid X]]=0.\qed

Lemma 6.3

(Compactness of the TT constraint set)
.

For fixed
(N,p,\chi,\Lambda)
, the set
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\subset\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)^{N}}
is compact. Consequently, there exists an empirical risk minimizer (ERM):

\hat{A}\in\argmin_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}\hat{L}_{n}(h_{A}),\qquad\hat{h}:=h_{\hat{A}}\in\argmin_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}\hat{L}_{n}(h).

Proof.

The Frobenius ball
\{A:\|A\|_{F}\leq\Lambda\}
is compact in finite-dimensional Euclidean space (closed and bounded).

The set
\{A:\rankTT(A)\leq\chi\}
is closed: the TT-rank of
A
equals
\max_{k=1,\ldots,N-1}\mathrm{rank}(A^{\langle k\rangle})
, where
A^{\langle k\rangle}\in\mathbb{R}^{(p+1)^{k}\times(p+1)^{N-k}}
is the
k
-th unfolding (matricization) of
A
. The map
A\mapsto A^{\langle k\rangle}
is linear (hence continuous), and the set of matrices with rank
\leq\chi
is closed in the Frobenius topology
[
18
, Sec. 2]
.

Therefore
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
is a closed subset
of a compact set, and is therefore compact.

Continuity of
\hat{L}_{n}(h_{A})
in
A
follows from continuity of
A\mapsto h_{A}(x)=\left\langle A,\Phi((x-x_{0})/r)\right\rangle
for each
x
. A continuous function on a compact set attains its minimum.
∎

7
PAC Bounds via Pseudo-Dimension

Definition 7.1

(Bounded loss class)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
be as in Definition
6.1
.
From Lemma
6.1
we have
|h_{A}(x)|\leq\Lambda K^{N}
.
Under Definition
6.3
,
|Y|\leq Y_{\max}
almost surely,
and
|h(X)|\leq\Lambda K^{N}
almost surely for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
.
Therefore the squared loss
\ell_{h}(x,y):=(h(x)-y)^{2}
is uniformly bounded:

0\leq\ell_{h}(X,Y)\leq M(\Lambda):=\bigl(\Lambda K^{N}+Y_{\max}\bigr)^{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

In the local setting, substituting the canonical choice

\Lambda=\Lambda^{\star}(r)
from Definition
6.2
gives

M(r):=\bigl(\Lambda^{\star}(r)\,K^{N}+Y_{\max}\bigr)^{2},

which decreases as
r\to 0
since
\Lambda^{\star}(r)\to C_{\leq p}^{(x)}
.

We write
h_{A}(x)=\langle A,\Phi((x-x_{0})/r)\rangle
. Although
\Phi
lives in an exponentially
large feature space, the TT constraint restricts
A
to a low-parameter tensor network family. We use pseudo-dimension to quantify complexity of the hypothesis class which is required to prove the uniform convergence of the population and empirical risk classes. For this, we use a standard pseudo-dimension bound for tensor network regression models in terms of the number of real parameters in the network
[
22
]
.

Theorem 7.1

(TN pseudo-dimension via parameter count
[
22
, Thm. 2]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E,\mathrm{dim})
be a tensor-network structure, and let
\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{regression}}_{G}

be the associated real-valued regression hypothesis class parameterized by
N_{G}
real parameters.
Then

\mathrm{Pdim}\!\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{regression}}_{G}\right)\leq 2\,N_{G}\,\log(12|V|).

Corollary 7.1

(TT pseudo-dimension (via restriction of a TN model))
.

Consider TT tensors of order
N
and mode size
m:=p+1
with TT ranks
(r_{k})_{k=1}^{N-1}
,
where
r_{0}=r_{N}=1
. Parameterize such tensors by TT cores
G_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{k-1}\times m\times r_{k}}
.
The total number of real core parameters is

N_{G}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}m\,r_{k-1}r_{k}.

\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{regression}}_{\mathrm{TT}}
denotes the associated (unconstrained) TT regression class

x\mapsto\langle A(G),\Phi((x-x_{0})/r)\rangle
induced by these parameters. By
Theorem
7.1
,

\mathrm{Pdim}\!\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{regression}}_{\mathrm{TT}}\right)\leq 2\,N_{G}\,\log(12N).

By Def
4.1
,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
is a
restriction
of this larger class (imposing the constraints
\|A\|_{F}\leq\Lambda
and
r_{k}\leq\chi
), pseudo-dimension cannot increase under
restriction, hence

\mathrm{Pdim}\!\left(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\right)\leq\mathrm{Pdim}\!\left(\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{regression}}_{\mathrm{TT}}\right)\leq 2\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}m\,r_{k-1}r_{k}\Big)\log(12N)\leq 2\,N\,m\,\chi^{2}\,\log(12N),

where the last inequality uses
r_{k}\leq\chi
for
k=1,\dots,N-1
(and
\chi\geq 1
).

The uniform convergence theorem we apply later on is stated in terms of the pseudo-dimension of the

loss class
and not the
predictor class
as in corollary
7.1
.
Define the squared-loss class

\mathcal{G}:=\left\{(x,y)\mapsto(h(x)-y)^{2}:h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\right\}.

Lemma 7.1

(Squared-loss lifting:
\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})
from
\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{H})
)
.

Assume
|Y|\leq Y_{\max}
a.s. and
|h(X)|\leq B
a.s. for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\{(x,y)\mapsto(h(x)-y)^{2}:h\in\mathcal{H}\}
.
Then

\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}})\;\leq\;4(2\,\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{H})+1)\log_{2}(6).

Writing
d_{H}:=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi))
, we may define

d:=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})\;\leq\;4(2d_{H}+1)\log_{2}(6).

Proof.

Let
d_{H}=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{H})
. By the definition of pseudo-dimension,

\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}})
is the VC-dimension of the thresholded concept class

\mathcal{C}:=\Big\{(x,y,t)\mapsto\mathbf{1}\!\big((h(x)-y)^{2}>t\big)\;:\;h\in\mathcal{H}\Big\}.

If
t<0
then
(h(x)-y)^{2}>t
holds trivially and such coordinates cannot increase VC-dimension.
Thus we may restrict to
t\geq 0
without loss.
Write
\tilde{t}:=\sqrt{t}\geq 0
. Then

(h(x)-y)^{2}>t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\big(h(x)>y+\tilde{t}\big)\ \ \text{or}\ \ \big(h(x)<y-\tilde{t}\big).

Each concept in
\mathcal{C}
corresponds to the set union
S_{h}^{+}\cup S_{h}^{-}
, where

S_{h}^{+}:=\big\{(x,y,\tilde{t}):h(x)>y+\tilde{t}\big\},\qquad S_{h}^{-}:=\big\{(x,y,\tilde{t}):h(x)<y-\tilde{t}\big\}.

Define the set families
\mathcal{F}_{+}:=\{S_{h}^{+}:h\in\mathcal{H}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{-}:=\{S_{h}^{-}:h\in\mathcal{H}\}
.
By restriction arguments analogous to those for the standard threshold class

\mathcal{T}:=\{(x,t)\mapsto\mathbf{1}[h(x)>t]:h\in\mathcal{H}\},

we have
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}_{+})\leq d_{H}
and
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}_{-})\leq d_{H}
.
Define
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{F}_{+}\cup\mathcal{F}_{-}
. By a standard union-growth argument (We give full proof of this inequality in the appendix as Lemma
A.1
),

\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}_{+})+\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F}_{-})+1\leq 2d_{H}+1.

Since
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\cup 2}:=\{A\cup B:A,B\in\mathcal{F}\}
,
Lemma 3.2.3 of
[
4
]
(applied with union parameter
s=2
) yields

\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C})<2\cdot\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{F})\cdot 2\cdot\log_{2}(6)\leq 4(2d_{H}+1)\log_{2}(6),

where we used that
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C})
is an integer in the last step.
Thus,

\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{H}})=\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C})\leq 4(2d_{H}+1)\log_{2}(6)

∎

Remark 7.1

(Explicit TT loss-class bound)
.

Combining Lemma
7.1
with Corollary
7.1
gives

d\;\leq\;4\Big(2\cdot 2\,N\,m\,\chi^{2}\,\log(12N)+1\Big)\log_{2}(6),\qquad m=p+1.

Since
\log_{2}(6)\approx 2.585
, this simplifies to

d\;\lesssim\;41.4\,N\,m\,\chi^{2}\,\log(12N)+10.3\;=\;O\big(N\,m\,\chi^{2}\,\log(N)\big).

7.1
Uniform convergence and ERM

We apply a standard uniform deviation bound for bounded loss classes in terms of pseudo-dimension

[
32
, Thm. 11.8]
.

Theorem 7.2

(Uniform deviation for bounded losses via pseudo-dimension
[
32
, Thm. 11.8]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of measurable loss functions
\ell:\mathcal{Z}\to[0,M]
on
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
,
and let
d:=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})
. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta

over
n
i.i.d. samples
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}\sim P
,

\sup_{\ell\in\mathcal{G}}\left|\E[\ell(Z)]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(Z_{i})\right|\;\leq\;M\sqrt{\frac{2d\log\!\big(\frac{en}{d}\big)}{n}}\;+\;M\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.

Applying Theorem
7.2
to the squared-loss class

\mathcal{G}=\{(x,y)\mapsto(h(x)-y)^{2}:h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\}
gives the usual ERM excess-risk bound.

Theorem 7.3

(ERM excess-risk bound)
.

Assume regression model as defined in Definition
6.3
and let
\hat{h}
be an ERM over
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
.
Let
\mathcal{G}=\{(x,y)\mapsto(h(x)-y)^{2}:h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\}
and set

M=(\Lambda K^{N}+Y_{\max})^{2}
. Let
d=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})
.
Then with probability at least
1-\delta
:

L(\hat{h})\leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}L(h)\;+\;2M\sqrt{\frac{2d\log\!\big(\frac{en}{d}\big)}{n}}\;+\;2M\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.

The same bound holds with
L
replaced by
R
(Lemma
6.2
).

Proof.

For each
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
define
\ell_{h}(Z):=(h(X)-Y)^{2}
, so that

L(h)=\E[\ell_{h}(Z)]
and
\hat{L}_{n}(h)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{h}(Z_{i})
.
By Theorem
7.2
, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}\big|L(h)-\hat{L}_{n}(h)\big|\leq\Delta,\qquad\Delta:=M\sqrt{\frac{2d\log\!\big(\frac{en}{d}\big)}{n}}+M\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.

Then for any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
,

L(\hat{h})\leq\hat{L}_{n}(\hat{h})+\Delta\leq\hat{L}_{n}(h)+\Delta\leq L(h)+2\Delta.

Taking the infimum over
h
yields the claim. The transfer from
L
to
R
follows from
Lemma
6.2
.
∎

7.2
Consolidated learning guarantee for local TT surrogates (LTTS) (Taylor + TT + estimation)

Lemma 7.2

(Uniform pointwise error implies risk bound)
.

If a measurable
h
satisfies
|h(x)-g(x)|\leq E
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
,
then
R(h)=\E[(h(X)-g(X))^{2}]\leq E^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
|h(x)-g(x)|\leq E
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
,
squaring both sides gives
(h(x)-g(x))^{2}\leq E^{2}
for all
x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
.
By Definition
6.3
,
X
is supported on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
.
Therefore,
(h(X)-g(X))^{2}\leq E^{2}
almost surely, and taking expectations yields

R(h)=\E[(h(X)-g(X))^{2}]\leq E^{2}.\qed

Theorem 7.4

(Consolidated PAC bound for local TT surrogates (LTTS))
.

Let
g
and regression model be defined by Definitions
2.2
and
6.3
. Fix
(\Lambda,\chi)
and let
\hat{h}
be an ERM
over
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
. Define

E_{\mathrm{det}}:=\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\;+\;K^{N}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi).

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{(x,y)\mapsto(h(x)-y)^{2}:h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)\}
, set
d=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})
and

M=(\Lambda K^{N}+Y_{\max})^{2}
. Then with probability at least
1-\delta
:

R(\hat{h})\;\leq\;E_{\mathrm{det}}^{2}\;+\;2M\sqrt{\frac{2d\log\!\big(\frac{en}{d}\big)}{n}}\;+\;2M\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}.

Moreover, by Lemma
7.1
and Corollary
7.1
, one may take

d\;=\;\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})\;\leq\;4(2\,\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{H})+1)\log_{2}(6)

which is of the order
O\big(N\,m\,\chi^{2}\,\log(N)\big)
where
m=p+1

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
\tau>0
. By definition of
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
(Definition
4.2
),
there exists
A_{\tau}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
such that

\|A^{\star}-A_{\tau}\|_{F}\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)+\tau.

Apply Theorem
5.1
with
A_{\mathrm{TT}}=A_{\tau}
to obtain a uniform pointwise bound on the patch:

|g(x)-h_{A_{\tau}}(x)|\leq\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}+K^{N}\big(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)+\tau\big)\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r).

Hence, by Lemma
7.2
,

R(h_{A_{\tau}})\leq\left(\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}+K^{N}\big(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)+\tau\big)\right)^{2}.

Therefore,

\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}R(h)\leq R(h_{A_{\tau}}).

Apply Theorem
7.3
with
R
in place of
L
(via Lemma
6.2
) and combine.
Finally let
\tau\downarrow 0
.
∎

As discussed before, the deterministic quantity
E_{\mathrm{det}}
in Theorem
7.4
is an

error certificate
for the
reference predictor

h_{A_{\tau}}
obtained by
(Taylor truncation
\rightarrow
embedding
\rightarrow
TT compression), as established by
Theorem
5.1
. It is not claimed that the same Taylor-remainder term holds
for an arbitrary ERM minimizer
\hat{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)
, which may in principle
place mass on indices with
|\alpha|>p
.
Instead, we use
h_{A_{\tau}}
only to upper bound the approximation term

\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}R(h)\leq R(h_{A_{\tau}})
, and then apply the ERM
uniform-convergence bound to conclude that
\hat{h}
competes with (and may improve upon)
this reference predictor.

Corollary 7.2

(Sample size for excess-risk tolerance
\eta
)
.

Fix
\eta>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. If

n\;\gtrsim\;\frac{(\Lambda K^{N}+Y_{\max})^{4}}{\eta^{2}}\left(d\log\!\Big(\frac{en}{d}\Big)+\log\frac{2}{\delta}\right),

then with probability at least
1-\delta
the ERM satisfies

R(\hat{h})\leq\inf_{h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda,\chi)}R(h)+\eta.

Proof.

Theorem
7.4
yields a risk bound
R(\hat{h})\;\leq\;E_{\mathrm{det}}^{2}\;+\;\Delta_{n}(\delta)
. To obtain a sample complexity statement, we fix a target estimation tolerance
\eta\;>\;0
and require
\Delta_{n}(\delta)\;\leq\;\eta
. Solving this sufficient condition for
n
and substituting for
M=(\Lambda K^{N}+Y_{\max})^{2}
yields the above sample complexity.
∎

7.3
Local TT surrogates (LTTS) guarantee: risk bound and sample complexity

The following theorem is the main statistical result of this paper. It
specializes the general statistical learning machinery of the preceding sections to the
local hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{*}(r),\chi)
, making the full

r
-dependence explicit and quantifying the advantage of local over
global surrogation.

Theorem 7.5

(LTTS guarantee with
r
dependence)
.

Let
g
and the regression model be as in Definitions
2.2

and
6.3
. Let
\hat{h}
be an ERM over the local hypothesis
class
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{*}(r),\chi)
from Definition
6.2
,
with
\Lambda^{\star}(r)
from Lemma
3.3
. Define

M(r)\;:=\;\bigl(\Lambda^{\star}(r)\,K^{N}+Y_{\max}\bigr)^{2},\qquad\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(r,\chi)\;:=\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}\bigl(\Lambda^{\star}(r),\chi\bigr),

where
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{\star}(r),\chi)
is as in
Definition
4.2
, and let
d=\mathrm{Pdim}(\mathcal{G})=O(N\,m\,\chi^{2}\log N)
with
m=p+1
.

(i) Risk bound.

With probability at least
1-\delta
over
n
i.i.d. samples:

R(\hat{h})\;\leq\;\underbrace{\left(\frac{C_{p+1}^{(x)}\,r^{p+1}\,N^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\;+\;K^{N}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(r,\chi)\right)^{\!2}}_{E_{\mathrm{det}}(r)^{2}\ \text{(approximation)}}\;+\;\underbrace{2M(r)\sqrt{\frac{2d\log\!\bigl(\frac{en}{d}\bigr)}{n}}\;+\;2M(r)\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{2n}}}_{\text{statistical terms}}.

(ii) Sample complexity.

Fix excess-risk tolerance
\eta>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
.
To guarantee
R(\hat{h})\leq E_{\mathrm{det}}(r)^{2}+\eta
with
probability at least
1-\delta
, it suffices to take

n\;\gtrsim\;\frac{M(r)^{2}}{\eta^{2}}\left(d\log\!\Big(\frac{en}{d}\Big)+\log\frac{2}{\delta}\right).

Proof.

Apply Theorem
7.4
with
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\star}(r)

from Lemma
3.3
, also

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{TT}}(\Lambda^{\star}(r),\chi)
is non-empty and hence

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TT}}(r,\chi)<\infty
.
Substituting
M=M(r)
into the PAC bound of
Theorem
7.4
gives part (i) directly.
Part (ii) follows from Corollary
7.2
under the same
substitution.
∎

Both the Taylor truncation term in
E_{\mathrm{det}}(r)^{2}
and
M(r)
decrease and saturate as
r\to 0
, so
smaller patches simultaneously reduce approximation error and statistical
complexity. Note that
M(r)
contains a factor of
K^{N}
; for fixed
N

this is a constant, but for large
N
the choice of
r
and
\chi
must
be made carefully to control the overall bound. Smaller patches
require fewer samples to achieve the same excess-risk tolerance
\eta
,
giving a quantitative advantage of local over global surrogation.

8
Application to Quantum Learning Models

The local TT surrogate (LTTS) framework developed in the preceding sections applies to any locally smooth
black-box function with bounded signal and noise. A gate-based quantum learning model typically outputs an expectation value

g(x)=\Tr\!\bigl(O\,U(x)\,\rho\,U(x)^{\dagger}\bigr),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a classical input,
\rho
is an initial quantum state,

U(x)
is a data-dependent unitary circuit, and
O
is a bounded Hermitian
observable. When the input enters through finitely many gates of the form

e^{-ix_{j}H_{j,\ell}}
with finite-dimensional Hermitian generators

H_{j,\ell}
, the map
x\mapsto U(x)
is real-analytic, since the matrix
exponential is an entire function of the scalar parameter and products of
analytic maps are analytic. Because the trace is linear,
g(x)
is
real-analytic in
x
. In particular,
g\in C^{p+1}(\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r))
for any
p

and any compact patch
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
. Since each partial derivative

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g
is continuous and
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
is compact, the supremum
in Definition
2.2
is finite. This reasoning applies broadly: it
covers reuploading models
[
38
,
50
]
, quantum convolutional architectures (as demonstrated in experiment
9.2
) and general hybrid
ansätze with data-dependent rotations.

For bounded observables,
|g(x)|\leq\|O\|_{\infty}
, hence we may take

G_{\max}=\|O\|_{\infty}
. Moreover, under finite-shot estimation of a bounded
observable, each label
Y_{i}
(a sample mean, hence a convex combination of
bounded outcomes) satisfies
|Y_{i}|\leq\|O\|_{\infty}
deterministically.
Therefore the induced noise
\varepsilon_{i}:=Y_{i}-g(X_{i})
is also bounded, with

|\varepsilon_{i}|\leq|Y_{i}|+|g(X_{i})|\leq 2\|O\|_{\infty}
, so
Definition
6.3
holds with
\sigma=2\|O\|_{\infty}
and hence

Y_{\max}=G_{\max}+\sigma\leq 3\|O\|_{\infty}
. In our setting the tighter label
bound
|Y_{i}|\leq\|O\|_{\infty}
is available directly and can be used wherever
only a bound on
|Y_{i}|
is required. Such local smoothness and boundedness are precisely the conditions our statistical analysis requires. Thus, trained quantum models are a natural setting for this framework. The high cost of circuit evaluation makes the speedup from classical surrogation especially valuable.

Discussion

In summary, we proposed a model-agnostic analytical framework for local TT surrogates (LTTS) wherein the total approximation error decomposes into three transparent sources, each independently controllable:

•

Polynomial truncation error
, controlled by the patch radius
r
and polynomial degree
p
;

•

Tensor compression error
, controlled by the TT bond dimension
\chi
;

•

Statistical estimation error
, arising when the surrogate is learned from finite noisy samples with
r
dependence.

Neither the deterministic approximation component nor the statistical component alone suffices: the Taylor-TT certificate without ERM gives no learning algorithm, and ERM without the certificate gives no guarantee that the hypothesis class is rich enough. The final learned surrogate is a TT-format function but is
not
the certificate predictor. ERM is free to find any predictor in the hypothesis class, and may outperform the certificate. The resulting surrogate can be evaluated, differentiated, and integrated at negligible classical cost. Our framework directly addresses a practical concern in the deployment of quantum machine learning: large-scale-trained quantum models still require repeated quantum circuit executions, which are computationally expensive or even infeasible. By decoupling the training phase (which runs on quantum hardware) from the deployment/inference phase (which runs classically), local TT surrogates enable a workflow in which quantum resources are invested once during training and then amortized over an unlimited number of cheap classical queries. Given the explicit
r
-dependence in our guarantees, local surrogates do improve with smaller patch radii. Numerical experiments on quantum convolutional neural networks trained on real-world datasets validate the theoretical predictions while using the Taylor-TT certificate as a warm start.

A natural question is whether one can obtain guarantees similar to classical surrogates in Schriber et al
[
48
]
by sampling
g
on a deterministic grid and reconstructing uniformly on
\mathcal{B}(x_{0},r)
. Such results require the target to belong
a priori
to a finite-dimensional function family with a well-conditioned sampling operator (in their case: bandlimited Fourier models). In essence, there ought to be structural guarantees for the quantum learning model we intend to surrogate. The statistical regression approach sidesteps these issues by foregoing any such design prerequisites and views the target as a black box with local smoothness. For such agnostic targets, the TT constraint controls model complexity, Taylor features control the approximation-to-function link, and i.i.d. sampling yields principled PAC guarantees.

In our framework, while the
parameter count

d_{\mathrm{eff}}
scales polynomially i.e
d_{\mathrm{eff}}=N(p+1)\chi^{2}
rather than the naive
(p+1)^{N}
, the
worst-case constants
in the bounds inherit an exponential factor
K^{N}
through the tensor-product feature norm. As a consequence, the worst-case sample complexity bound scales as
K^{4N}
in Corollory
7.2
, which could be improved upon in future work by utilizing more efficient TT constructions. This separates
representation complexity
(polynomial via TT structure) from
feature-induced constants
(exponential via the embedding), clarifying precisely where the curse of dimensionality enters. On the same note, it should be possible to construct the certificate predictor using other higher-order approximations such as Chebyshev
[
45
]
or Fourier bases, potentially yielding tighter complexity bounds.

Appendix: VC Dimension Union Bound

We provide a self-contained proof of the union bound for VC dimension used in Lemma
7.1
.

Lemma A.1

(VC Dimension of Union)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
be set families over a domain
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C}_{1})=d_{1}
and
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C}_{2})=d_{2}
. Then

\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq d_{1}+d_{2}+1.

Proof.

For a finite set
S\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, let
\mathcal{C}|_{S}:=\{C\cap S:C\in\mathcal{C}\}
and

\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}(n):=\max_{|S|=n}|\mathcal{C}|_{S}|
.

We use the standard growth function argument. For any
S
with
|S|=n
, we have
(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2})|_{S}=\mathcal{C}_{1}|_{S}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}|_{S}
, hence

|(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2})|_{S}|\leq|\mathcal{C}_{1}|_{S}|+|\mathcal{C}_{2}|_{S}|
.
Taking the maximum over
S
gives

\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}}(n)\leq\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(n)+\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{2}}(n).

By Sauer’s lemma
[
47
]
, if
\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C}_{i})=d_{i}
, then for all
n
,

\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}(n)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{d_{i}}\binom{n}{j}.

Assume
d_{1}\leq d_{2}
and set
n=d_{1}+d_{2}+2
. Then

\sum_{j=0}^{d_{2}}\binom{n}{j}=2^{n}-\sum_{j=d_{2}+1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}=2^{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-d_{2}-1}\binom{n}{j}=2^{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}+1}\binom{n}{j},

where we used
\binom{n}{j}=\binom{n}{n-j}
and
n-d_{2}-1=d_{1}+1
.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}\binom{n}{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{d_{2}}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}}\binom{n}{j}+2^{n}-\sum_{j=0}^{d_{1}+1}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle=2^{n}-\binom{n}{d_{1}+1}<2^{n}.

Hence
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}}(n)<2^{n}
, so
\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}
cannot shatter
n
points.
Therefore:

\mathrm{VCdim}(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq n-1=d_{1}+d_{2}+1.

∎
