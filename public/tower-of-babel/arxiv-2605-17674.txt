On the finite transcendence of Frobenius traces for abelian varieties over ℚ \displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
Yuto Tsuruta

Abstract
. The first purpose of this paper is to give the fnite transcendence of Frobenius traces for elliptic curves over ℚ \displaystyle\mathbb{Q} without the assumption of complex multiplication (CM). This result generalizes the previous work by Luca and Zudilin, who obtained similar transcendence results specifically for the CM case. The second purpose is to give the finite transcendence of Frobenius traces for several principally polarized abelian varieties over ℚ \displaystyle\mathbb{Q} , by using Luca–Zudilin’s method.

Transcendence for the Frobenius traces of elliptic curves

In this section, we prove
A
. In
[
6
]
, Luca and Zudilin proved

Serre’s open image theorem

Theorem 2.1

(
[
11
, Section 4.4]
)
.

For any non-CM elliptic curve
\displaystyle E
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
, the
\displaystyle\ell
-adic Galois representation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{E,\ell}\colon\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}}(E[\ell])\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})

is surjective for all but finitely many primes
\displaystyle\ell

Our proof proceeds without using the above theorem, focusing instead on primes that split completely in some finite Galois extensions over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
and applying
Sato–Tate conjecture
. Here we summarize the result. The CM case is due to Hecke and we refer to
[
8
]
. The non-CM case is due to a celebrated work
[
3
]
. For an elliptic curve
\displaystyle E/\mathbb{Q}
and a prime
\displaystyle p
such that
\displaystyle E
has good reduction at
\displaystyle p
, we choose
\displaystyle\theta_{p}\in[0,\pi]
satisfying
\displaystyle\cos{\theta_{p}}:=a_{p}(E)/2\sqrt{p}
. We call such a prime
\displaystyle p
a good prime of
\displaystyle E
.

Theorem 2.2

(Sato–Tate conjecture,
[
3
,
8
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle E
be an elliptic curve over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
.
Then, the following holds.

(1)

When
\displaystyle E
is non-CM, then for
\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\pi
, it holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{p\leq X\ |\ \text{$\displaystyle p$ is a good prime of $\displaystyle E$},\ \alpha\leq\theta_{p}\leq\beta\right\}}{\#\left\{p\ |\ p\leq X\right\}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\sin^{2}{\theta}d\theta.

(2)

When
\displaystyle E
has CM, then for
\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\pi
, it holds

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{\#\left\{p\leq X\ |\ \text{$\displaystyle p$ is a good prime of $\displaystyle E$},\ \alpha\leq\theta_{p}\leq\beta\right\}}{\#\left\{p\ |\ p\leq X\right\}}=\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}d\theta.

Here,
\displaystyle\delta_{0}=\begin{cases}1,\ \mathrm{if}\ \pi/2\in[\alpha,\beta],\\
0,\ \mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

proof of
A
.

We only consider infinitely many good primes. We assume
\displaystyle\bm{a}=(a_{p}(E)\bmod p)_{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{A}}^{0}
(and consequently, deduce a contradiction). By using the formulation in
1.1
-(
3

\displaystyle L/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
\displaystyle d=[L:\mathbb{Q}]
, a map
\displaystyle g\colon\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\to L
, and finite elements
\displaystyle b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in L
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{Im}(g)=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}\ \text{and}\ a_{p}(E)\equiv g(\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})=b_{i}\bmod\mathfrak{P},

for some
\displaystyle i
with
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq k
.
By discarding
\displaystyle b_{i}
if necessary, we may assume that
\displaystyle L
is the Galois closure of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(_{1},\ldots,_{k})
. We set

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{1}=\left\{p\ |\ p\ \text{is completely splited in}\ L\right\}.

Then the natural density is given as
\displaystyle\text{den}(S_{1})=d^{-1}>0
by Chebotarev density theorem. It is important to note that there are infinitely many primes that belong to
\displaystyle S_{1}
.

We fix such
\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}\mid p
. The condition
\displaystyle p\in S_{1}
says
\displaystyle\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}}=\text{id}\in\mathrm{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, we have
\displaystyle b:=g(\text{id})\in\mathbb{Q}
by the definition of
\displaystyle g
. We can assume that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
. Indeed, if
\displaystyle E
is non-CM, then the density of
\displaystyle p
such that
\displaystyle a_{p}=0
is
\displaystyle 0
, also if
\displaystyle E
is CM, then the density of such prime is
\displaystyle 1/2
. In either case, there exist infinitely many primes
\displaystyle p
for which
\displaystyle a_{p}\neq 0
. Since
\displaystyle a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\displaystyle b\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle({}^{\exists}N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\gg 0})
, we have

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{p}\equiv b\bmod p.

Using (
2
) and Hasse bound (cf.
[
14

), we obtain

(3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{p}{N}\leq|a_{p}-b|\leq 2\sqrt{p}+|b|.

(
3
) says that
\displaystyle p
lives in
\displaystyle p<K:=(N+\sqrt{N^{2}+N|b|})^{2}
. Note that
\displaystyle K
is independent of the choice of
\displaystyle p
, so we have

(4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{p}-b=0,\ {}^{\forall}p\in S_{1}\ \text{and}\ p>K.

We define another sets by

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{2}=\{p\ |\ p\ \text{is completely splited in}\ L,\ b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ p>K,\ a_{p}=\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{3}=\{p\ |\ b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ p>K,\ a_{p}=\}.

Then, we have
\displaystyle S_{2}\subseteq S_{3}
.

(i)
\displaystyle E
: non-CM elliptic curve

For any
\displaystyle K^{\prime}>K
and
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}>0
such that
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}\to 0
as
\displaystyle K^{\prime}\to\infty
, the equality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{den}(S_{3})=\lim_{\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}\to 0}\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}+\varepsilon_{K^{\prime}}}\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\theta=0

holds by Sato–Tate conjecture (

-(

(ii)
\displaystyle E

By the condition

from

-(
2

However, in both cases, it contradicts

Thus, we obtain the conclusion.
∎
