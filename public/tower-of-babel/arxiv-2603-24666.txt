Bounds on the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic fibrations
Antonella Grassi 1, Rick Miranda 2, Kapil Paranjape 3, Vasudevan Srinivas 4, Timo Weigand 5

Abstract
. We present two proofs for a bound on the rank of the Mordell-Weil group of some elliptic fibrations. The bounds apply to Calabi-Yau varieties, which are also of interest to the physics of string theory. We prove explicit bounds for Calabi-Yau threefolds, as predicted by physics, and give new explicit bounds for fourfolds under mild assumptions. These results motivate a conjecture for bounds in any dimensions.



ZMP-HH/26-6

1.
Introduction

We prove bounds on the rank of the Mordell-Weil group
\mathrm{MW}(X/B)
of an elliptically fibered variety
X
over a base
B
. We present two types of arguments, which have parallels, but differ in some details and emphasis. Each method leads to possible generalizations in different directions.

For
X
a smooth K3 surface, it is known that the rank of the Mordell-Weil group can take any value
0\leq{\rm rk}\mathrm{MW}(X/B)\leq 18

[
30
,
28
]
.
In higher dimensions, the existence of a bound on
{\rm rk}\mathrm{MW}(X/B)
is guaranteed, for Calabi-Yau varieties, by the effective boundedness of families of elliptic fibrations
[
12
,
3
]
. The currently highest known Mordell-Weil rank for elliptic Calabi-Yau threefolds is obtained for special Schoen manifolds with
{\rm rk}\mathrm{MW}(X/B)=10

[
20
,
11
]
, but based on expectations from physics, higher ranks should be possible.

In this work, we prove three structure theorems for bounds on elliptic fibrations: Theorem
3.1
, Theorem
4.16
and Theorem
4.18
..

Based on these, we furthermore prove the following explicit bounds:

Theorem
.

Let
X\to B
be an elliptic Calabi-Yau threefold with sections and singular fibers.
Then the Mordell-Weil group of

satisfies
{\rm rank}\,\mathrm{MW}(X/B)\leq 28
.

Theorem
.

Let
X\to B
be an elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfold with sections and singular fibers. Then under mild assumptions the Mordell-Weil group of

satisfies
{\rm rank}\,\mathrm{MW}(X/B)\leq 38
.

The precise statements and hypotheses are given in Theorem
5.1
and Theorem

. These theorems and their proofs formalise and generalise previous results from physics
[
35
,
38
,
33
]
.

The same reasoning, with the same assumptions, provides bounds on the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic threefolds fourfolds with

.

1.1.
Bounds from physics

To every elliptic Calabi-Yau
3
-fold
X

one can associate a six-dimensional
N=1
supergravity theory obtained as the low-energy limit of F-theory
[
52
,
43
,
44

compactified on

.
In this context,
the free and torsion part of the Mordell-Weil group of

encode
the continuous and, respectively, discrete abelian gauge symmetries in the effective action (see, for example, the reviews
[
54
,
7
]
for details and references).
Bounds on the rank of the Mordell-Weil group, or in physics terms on the rank

of the abelian gauge group, are motivated by general arguments
suggesting that the number of light degrees of freedom and their interactions in any consistent
quantum gravitational theory are severely constrained.
While gauge and gravitational anomalies alone do admit infinite families of theories with arbitrarily high abelian gauge rank
[
47
]
, taking into consideration constraints from quantum consistency of probe strings
[
26
]
sets tight bounds on the ranks
[
35
,
38
,
33
,
27
]
.

In
[
35
]
bounds on

have been obtained for general

supergravity theories in six dimensions by analysing probe consistency of certain solitonic strings coupling to gravity.
These bounds are given by
r_{\rm U(1)}\leq 22
for theories with
T\geq 1
tensor multiplets (subsequently improved to
r_{\rm U(1)}\leq 20
in
[

]
) and

for

.
In explicit realisations of the supergraviy via F-theory, the solitonic strings arise from

. If the curve

is rational, movable and without base points on

, the associated string couples to all abelian gauge groups because

has a non-zero intersection number with the height pairing divisor associated with the Shioda map. This follows by extending arguments in
[
6
]
to Calabi-Yau threefolds
[
34
]
and led, for smooth base

, to the conservative bounds

{\rm rk}(\mathrm{MW}(X/B))\leq 32

and

[

]
. In
[

these bounds were sharpened
to

(1)

{\rm rk}(\mathrm{MW}(X/B))\leq 10\,C\cdot(-K_{B})-2\,,

which also holds for elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfolds with a smooth base

For elliptic threefolds, (

) implies
the stronger bound
{\rm rk}(\mathrm{MW}(X/B))\leq 28

(leading to
T=0
) and

{\rm rk}(\mathrm{MW}(X/B))\leq 18

The physics derivation of (

[

]
argues for the bound
{\rm rk}(\mathrm{MW}(X/B))\leq 18

\mathrm{MW}(X/B)

1.2.
Outline of our paper

We now give an overview of the main contents of the present work.

In Section
2
we collect the necessary background for our analysis, including results from the Minimal Model Problem applied to elliptic fibrations and properties of the Shioda pairing for rational sections. We also revisit Noether’s formula (in all characteristics).

In Section
3
, we prove the structure theorem
3.1
. While philosophically similar to the procedure of Section
4
, the proof presented in this section differs in two aspects. It uses that the Mordell-Weil rank of an elliptic curve over a field only increases
on base change to a larger field, and that an elliptic curve over a function field of higher transcendence degree may sometimes be viewed as the generic fiber of an elliptic fibration on a smooth proper surface
S_{K}
, which is itself defined over some function field
K
. Then Noether’s formula directly yields a bound for the Picard rank of this surface (and hence our desired elliptic Mordell-Weil rank), without considering degenerate fibers of the elliptic fibration, the Shioda pairing, or heights. This approach uses only elements of the classical minimal model theory for rational surfaces, and avoids difficult results in higher dimensional birational geometry. It is formulated in the scheme language. At the current state this method does not provide an explicit effective bound for fourfolds.

In Section
4
we develop and prove the two structure theorems
4.16
and
4.18
for elliptic fibrations (not necessarily Calabi-Yau).
The proof formalises and generalises the physics approach of
[
34
,
35
,
38
,
33
]
by proving an injection from
\mathrm{MW}(X/B)
to
{\rm MW}(Z)
for a suitable surface
Z
(smooth or birationally a K3 surface) obtained by restricting the elliptic fibration to a smooth curve
C
in the smooth locus of the base
B
; in particular, if the Picard rank of
B
exceeds one,
C
must be movable. The result uses key properties of the Shioda map of
rational sections and its height-pairing (see in particular Lemma
4.2
).

We present the two different approaches, as developed in Section
3
and in Section
4
, to bound the Mordell-Weil rank since each of them has the possibility of generalization in different ways, which could be useful for future work.

In Section
5
, we apply Theorems
3.1
and
4.16
to prove a universal bound, formulated as Theorem
5.1
, on the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic Calabi-Yau threefolds.

In Section
6
, we apply the structure theorems
4.16
and
4.18
to obtain an explicit bound for the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic Calabi-Yau fourfolds, Theorem
6.3
. The proof makes use of general results from the Minimal Model Program (in particular Theorem
2.11
). For the proof we have to make the assumption that the base of the fourfold, in case its rank is one, can be taken to be smooth. Under this hypothesis, we can use the classification of smooth rank-one Fano threefolds
[
22
]
to establish the bound by proving the existence of a curve
C
as required by Theorems
4.16
and
4.18
. Note that unlike for Calabi-Yau threefolds, in some of the cases the curves giving the bound are not movable and are not rational.

Our results naturally motivate a generalisation to any dimension, which we present in the form of a conjecture in Section
7
.

Let

\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Z)=12\,\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z})-K_{Z}^{2},

On the other hand, in characteristic zero, even if the field is not the complex numbers

(5)

\chi_{\mathrm{top}}(Z)=\sum(-1)^{i}b_{i}(Z)=2-4\,h^{0,1}(Z)+2p_{g}+h^{1,1}(Z).

By combining (

) and (

Now assume that

admits an elliptic fibration

\pi\colon Z\to C,

where

is a smooth curve and

By Riemann–Roch and using

\displaystyle p_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{C})+g(C)-1+\deg{\mathcal{L}}_{C}=g(C)-1+\deg\mathcal{L}_{C}\,

2.4.
The Shioda map and the Shioda pairing

Given an elliptic fibration
\pi:X\to B
, we
study the Mordell-Weil group
\mathrm{MW}(X/B)
,
defined as the
group of rational sections of
the elliptic fibration.
Equivalently, we can write the Mordell-Weil group as

for

the function field of

The zero section (the zero element of the group) will be denoted by

. In particular the existence of a zero section implies that there are no multiple fibers.
Also, there is a canonical
identification of groups between
{\rm Pic}^{0}(E_{K})
and the Mordell-Weil
group
E(K)
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
Y\to V
and
X\to B
be birationally equivalent fibrations as in Definition
2.7
. Then
\mathrm{MW}(Y/V)\simeq\mathrm{MW}(X/B)
.

Proof.

For two birationally equivalent fibrations as in Definition
2.7
, the
generic elliptic fibers over the (common)
function field of the two bases are isomorphic, and therefore have the same
Mordell-Weil groups. ∎

Therefore, to derive bounds on the rank of the Mordell-Weil group, we can, without loss of generality, assume that

X
,
B
and
\pi:X\to B
are as in Theorem
2.8
.

In the sequel, we assume that the rank of

is non-vanishing so that there are non-torsion rational sections, i.e.

.

Definition 2.15
.

,
42

For an elliptic fibration
\pi:X\to B

as above, if

, there is a Shioda map from the Mordell-Weil group to the Neron-Severi group

\sigma\;:\;\mathrm{MW}(X/B)\longrightarrow\mathrm{NS}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\,,

which associates to a section

\sigma(S_{a})\;=\;S_{a}-S_{0}-\pi^{*}\!\bigl(\pi_{*}((S_{a}-S_{0})\cdot S_{0})\bigr)+\sum_{i}\sum_{A}\ell^{\,i}_{A}(S_{a})\,E_{i}^{A}.

Here

•

S_{a}
denotes the divisor (section) corresponding to the section

;

•

is the zero section;

•

is a rationally fibered divisor such that

is an irreducible component

meets the same component of a singular fiber as the zero-section).

Remark 2.16
.

()

The Shioda map is in fact a group homomorphism. The term

contains as a factor the intersection multiplicity of

with the general fibre of the
divisors
E^{A}_{i}

In particular, if
S_{a}
intersects the singular fibres in the same component as the zero-section, then all
\ell^{\,i}_{A}(S_{a})=0

()

The restriction on

explicitly done in the literature. This can be deduced from the

()

Theorem

This also accounts for the fact that the Shioda map is a group

()

Finally, the Shioda map is injective after tensoring with

.

3.
Bounds for the Mordell-Weil rank with Functions Fields

In this section we present an arithmetic proof of the following structure theorem bounding the Mordell-Weil rank of elliptic fibrations:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi:X\to B
be a morphism between projective, normal complex algebraic varieties, such that

(i)
X
is Cohen-Macaulay, and its singular locus has codimension at least 3;

(ii)
\pi
is an elliptic fibration with a section;

(iii) the canonical sheaf of
X
is trivial (
\omega_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X})
,
and
h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})=0
.

Suppose that there is an (irreducible) nonsingular complex variety
T
,
together with a smooth morphism

\varphi:T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to B
with dense image,
contained in the smooth locus of
B
.
Regarding this as a family of morphisms
\varphi_{t}:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to B
,
assume further that these are all embeddings,
giving a family
C_{t}\hookrightarrow B
of smooth rational curves, which has no base points
(i.e., for any
b\in B
there exists
t\in T

such that the corresponding rational curve
C_{t}
does not contain
b
).

Then the elliptic curve
E_{F}
over the function field
F={\mathbb{C}}(B)

has Mordell-Weil group
E_{F}(F)
with

(11)

{\rm rk}E_{F}(F)\leq 18+10d=10(-C_{t})\cdot K_{B}-2\,,

where
d
is the degree of the normal bundle of
C_{t}
for any
t\in T
.

Remark 3.2
.

Because of our hypothesis that the family of rational curves
C_{t}
has no base points,
the normal bundle of any such
C_{t}
must be generated by its global sections,
and so
d\geq 0
.

Remark 3.3
.

Movable curves are not necessarily base point free. However, the movable curves we consider in Section
5
are base point free.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Recall that for an elliptic curve
E
over a field
K
,
which has a given rational point
P\in E(K)
,
the Mordell-Weil group
E(K)
of
K
-rational points is naturally identified
with the group
{\rm Pic}^{0}(E)
of isomorphism classes of line bundles on
E
of degree 0,
via the map

Q(\in E(K))\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{E}(Q-P)(\in{\rm Pic}^{0}(E)).

Hence if
K\subset L
is any extension of fields,
then
{\rm Pic}^{0}(E)\to{\rm Pic}^{0}(E_{L})
is injective,
since
E(K)\subset E_{L}(L)
in an obvious way.

With the hypotheses and notation of Theorem
3.1
,
consider the “incidence variety”

Z=(T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})\times_{B}X:=\{(t,u,x)\in T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\times X\mid\varphi(t,u)=\pi(x)\}\,.

The image
\pi(X_{sing})
in
B
of the singular locus of
X
has codimension at least 2 in
B
,
since
X_{sing}
has codimension at least 3, by assumption.
Since
\varphi
is smooth,

\varphi^{-1}(\pi(X_{sing}))
is also of codimension at least
2

in
T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Hence, if we replace
T
by a nonempty Zariski open subset,
we may assume
\varphi(T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})

does not intersect
\pi(X_{sing})
.
Now since
\varphi
is a smooth morphism,
so is the morphism
f:Z\to X
induced by the projection,
and its image is disjoint from
X_{sing}
.
Hence
Z
is nonsingular. It is also clear, since it is a pull-back,
that the morphism
Z\to T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}

is an elliptic fibration with a cross section, since
X\to B
has this structure.

The generic fibre of
f:Z\to T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is
E_{K}=E\times_{F}K
,
where
F={\mathbb{C}}(B)
is the function field of
B
,

K={\mathbb{C}}(T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})

is the function field of
T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
and
F\hookrightarrow K
is the inclusion of fields induced by
\varphi
.
Hence it suffices to prove the stated bounds on the Mordell-Weil rank for the elliptic curve
E_{K}
, since that rank can only increase after a base change of fields.

We next note that, if
L={\mathbb{C}}(T)
is the function field of
T
,
we may consider the elliptic surface
Z_{L}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{L}

obtained as the generic fiber of the composition

g:Z\to T\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}\to T

where the second map is the projection to
T
.
Then
Z_{L}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{L}
is a nonsingular projective surface
defined over the field
L
, which has an elliptic fibration
(the map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{L}
) with a section,
and whose generic fiber is the
same
elliptic curve
E_{K}
.

We also note that for a Zariski open dense subset of
t\in T
,
the fiber
Z_{t}=g^{-1}(t)=\pi^{-1}(C_{t})\subset X
is a nonsingular projective complex surface.

Consider an algebraic closure
\bar{L}
of
L
, and the corresponding surface
Z_{\bar{L}}
. This surface comes equipped with an elliptic fibration

h:Z_{\bar{L}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\bar{L}}

with a cross section, and if
M=\bar{L}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})
is the function field,
then there are field extensions

K\hookrightarrow L\hookrightarrow\bar{L}\hookrightarrow M,

which allows us to identify the generic fiber of
h
with the elliptic curve

E_{M}=E_{F}\times_{F}M,

so that

E(F)\subset E(M)

is a subgroup.
It clearly suffices to prove the bounds stated in Theorem
3.1

for this “new” elliptic curve
E_{M}
,
which is the generic fiber of
h:Z_{\bar{L}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\bar{L}}
.

This elliptic surface
Z_{\bar{L}}
has trivial
H^{1}(\mathcal{O})
,
since it is an elliptic surface over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(see
[
40
]
).
Hence the Picard group is discrete, and equal to its Neron-Severi group;
by application of Theorem
2.25
we therefore have the estimate

{\rm rk}\,E(M)\leq{\rm rk}\,\mathrm{NS}(Z_{\bar{L}})-2\,.

The bound stated in Theorem
3.1
follows immediately from the lemma below.

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, and with the above notations,
if
d
is the degree of the normal bundle of
C_{t}\subset B
, where
t\in T
,

{\rm rk}(\mathrm{NS}(Z_{\bar{L}}))\leq 20+10d\,.

Proof.

We note that if
t\in T
, and
Z_{t}=\pi^{-1}(C_{t})\subset X

is such that
Z_{t}
is a nonsingular complex surface,
with its induced elliptic fibration
\pi_{t}:Z_{t}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
then (using adjunction on
X
, which has trivial canonical bundle) we see that

Z_{t}
has canonical line bundle

\omega_{Z_{t}}={\rm det}{\mathcal{N}_{Z_{t}}}=\pi_{t}^{*}({\rm det}{\mathcal{N}}_{C_{t}}),

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{Z_{t}}
is the normal bundle of
Z_{t}\subset X
,
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{C_{t}}
is the normal bundle of
C_{t}
in
B
.
Since
C_{t}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
,
we have that
{\rm det}{\mathcal{N}}_{C_{t}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(d)

where
d
is the degree of the normal bundle of
C_{t}
.
Similar reasoning shows that

\omega_{Z_{\bar{L}}}\cong h^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\bar{L}}}(d).

Hence the surface
Z_{\bar{L}}
has geometric genus

p_{g}(Z_{\bar{L}})=\dim H^{0}(Z_{\bar{L}},\omega_{Z_{\bar{L}}})=d+1,

and

q(Z_{\bar{L}})=\dim H^{1}(Z_{\bar{L}},{\mathcal{O}}_{Z_{\bar{L}}})=0.

Noether’s formula
2.12
then implies that

{\rm rk}(\mathrm{NS}(Z_{\bar{L}}))\leq 12(1+p_{g})-2-2p_{g}=12(d+2)-2-2(d+1)=10d+20.

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Recall from Proposition
2.12
that Noether’s formula
is also valid for a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field,
when interpreted suitably
[
48
]
; thus the resulting inequality on the rank of the Neron-Severi group is valid in characteristic 0 as given in Lemma
3.4
and a slightly weaker upper bound holds in any characteristic.
Since
\bar{L}
has characteristic

,
this can in fact be deduced from the case of complex surfaces,
together with the assertion that for a surface

over an algebraically closed field

,
and an extension of algebraically closed fields

,
the Neron-Severi groups of
S
and of
S_{L}
are canonically isomorphic.
