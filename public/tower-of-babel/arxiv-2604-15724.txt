Abstract
The charged current ν μ ​ ( ν ¯ μ ) \nu_{\mu}(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}) -induced deep inelastic scattering (DIS) from an Ar 40 {}^{40}\mathrm{Ar} target is studied using a microscopic framework that incorporates nuclear medium effects due to Fermi motion, binding energy, nucleon correlations, mesonic ( π \pi and ρ \rho ) contributions, and nuclear shadowing and antishadowing across the relevant Bjorken- x x region. The nuclear structure functions F i ​ A ​ ( x , Q 2 ) F_{iA}(x,Q^{2}) ( i = 1 ​ – ​ 3 ) (i=1\text{--}3) are evaluated using a relativistic nucleon spectral function ( S h S_{h} ) within the local density approximation employing the free nucleon structure functions, F i ​ N ​ ( x , Q 2 ) F_{iN}(x,Q^{2}) ( i = 1 ​ – ​ 3 ) (i=1\text{--}3) . These F i ​ N ​ ( x , Q 2 ) F_{iN}(x,Q^{2}) ( i = 1 ​ – ​ 3 ) (i=1\text{--}3) are calculated using parton distribution functions (PDFs) from MMHT 2014 parameterization, including higher-order perturbative QCD corrections up to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO), along with nonperturbative target mass corrections (TMC). The resulting nuclear structure functions F i ​ A ​ ( x , Q 2 ) F_{iA}(x,Q^{2}) ( i = 1 ​ – ​ 3 ) (i=1\text{--}3) are subsequently used to compute the differential DIS cross sections for A 40 ​ r {}^{40}Ar nucleus. Numerical results are presented for ν μ ​ ( ν ¯ μ ) \nu_{\mu}(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}) beam energies E = 4 E=4 GeV and E = 6 E=6 GeV for the differential scattering cross sections d 2 ​ σ d ​ x ​ d ​ y \frac{d^{2}\sigma}{dxdy} and d ​ σ d ​ x \frac{d\sigma}{dx} , relevant to ongoing and upcoming liquid-argon neutrino experiments such as DUNE and the Fermilab Short-Baseline Neutrino program.

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}^{2}\;M_{N}\;y}{2\pi}\;\frac{E}{E^{\prime}}\frac{|{\bf k^{\prime}}|}{|{\bf k}|}\left(\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\right)^{2}L_{\mu\nu}\;W^{\mu\nu}_{A}\;.

(16)

The leptonic tensor
L_{\mu\nu}
is already defined in Eq.
5
.
For
\nu_{\mu}(\bar{\nu}_{\mu})-A
DIS process, the nuclear hadronic tensor
W^{\mu\nu}_{A}

is written in terms of the weak nuclear structure functions
W_{iA}(\nu,Q^{2})
(
i=1-5
) as
Zaidi:2019asc
:

\displaystyle W^{\mu\nu}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q^{\mu}q^{\nu}}{q^{2}}-g^{\mu\nu}\right)\;W_{1A}(\nu,Q^{2})+\frac{W_{2A}(\nu,Q^{2})}{M_{A}^{2}}\left(p_{A}^{\mu}-\frac{p_{A}.q}{q^{2}}\;q^{\mu}\right)\left(p_{A}^{\nu}-\frac{p_{A}.q}{q^{2}}\;q^{\nu}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{2M_{A}^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{A\rho}q_{\sigma}~W_{3A}(\nu,Q^{2})+\frac{W_{4A}(\nu,Q^{2})}{M_{A}^{2}}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}+\frac{W_{5A}(\nu,Q^{2})}{M_{A}^{2}}(p^{\mu}_{A}q^{\nu}+q^{\mu}p^{\nu}_{A}),

with
M_{A}
as the nuclear target mass. In the massless lepton limit, terms associated with

W_{4A}(\nu,Q^{2})
and
W_{5A}(\nu,Q^{2})
vanish when contracted with the leptonic tensor.

These nuclear structure functions
W_{iA}(\nu,Q^{2})
; (
i=1-3
) are generally expressed in terms of the dimensionless nuclear structure
functions
F_{iA}(x,Q^{2})
; (
i=1-3
) defined as follows:

\displaystyle F_{1A}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{A}~W_{1A}(\nu,Q^{2})\ ,

(18)

\displaystyle F_{2A}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu~W_{2A}(\nu,Q^{2})\ ,

(19)

\displaystyle F_{3A}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu~W_{3A}(\nu,Q^{2})\ .

(20)

Using the above relations and Eqs.
17
and
5
in Eq.
16
, the differential cross sections is written in terms of the
first three dimensionless nuclear structure functions
F_{iA}(x,Q^{2})
; (
i=1-3
) as
Zaidi:2019asc
:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{dxdy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}^{2}M_{N}E}{\pi}\left(\frac{M_{W}^{2}}{M_{W}^{2}+Q^{2}}\right)^{2}\left[xy^{2}F_{1A}(x,Q^{2})+\left(1-y-\frac{M_{N}xy}{2E}\right)F_{2A}(x,Q^{2})\pm xy\left(1-\frac{y}{2}\right)F_{3A}(x,Q^{2})\right]\;.

(21)

As the interaction takes place with a nuclear target, the nucleons bound inside it are off-shell and continuously moving with non-zero Fermi momentum.
For the present numerical calculations, we choose the three momentum transfer
{\bf q}

to be along the
z
axis, and therefore
q^{\mu}=(q^{0},{\bf q})=(q^{0},0,0,q^{z})
, where
q^{0}
is the energy transferred to the nuclear target. The Bjorken variables for
the nuclear target(
x_{A}
) and the bound nucleon(
x_{N}
) are defined as

\displaystyle x_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q^{2}}{2p_{A}\cdot q}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2M_{A}q^{0}}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2A~M_{N}q^{0}}\;,\hskip 17.07164ptx_{N}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2p\cdot q}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2(p^{0}q^{0}-p^{z}q^{z})}.

(22)

The nucleons bound inside a nucleus may also interact among themselves via strong interaction which we incorporate by taking
the nucleon-nucleon correlations into account using a microscopic field theoretical approach.
The effect of Fermi motion, binding energy and nucleon correlations are included through the relativistic nucleon spectral function (
S_{h}
) which is
obtained by using the Lehmann’s representation for the relativistic nucleon propagator.
The dressed relativistic nucleon propagator in an interacting Fermi sea in the infinite nuclear matter
is calculated by using the technique of many body theory
FernandezdeCordoba:1991wf
.
To obtain the results for a finite nucleus the local density approximation (LDA) is then applied in which
the Fermi momentum of an interacting nucleon is not considered to be a constant quantity but the function of
position coordinate (
r
)
FernandezdeCordoba:1991wf
.
Following our earlier work
Zaidi:2019asc
, the expressions of weak nuclear structure functions which are used to evaluate the differential scattering
cross sections are given by:

\displaystyle F_{1A,N}(x_{A},Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4AM_{N}\int\,d^{3}r\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{M_{N}}{E_{N}({\bf p})}\,\int^{\mu}_{-\infty}dp^{0}~S_{h}(p^{0},{\bf p},\rho(r))~

(23)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{F_{1N}(x_{N},Q^{2})}{M_{N}}+\left(\frac{p^{x}}{M_{N}}\right)^{2}\frac{F_{2N}(x_{N},Q^{2})}{\nu_{N}}\right],

\displaystyle F_{2A,N}(x_{A},Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int\,d^{3}r\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{M_{N}}{E_{N}({\bf p})}\,\int^{\mu}_{-\infty}dp^{0}~S_{h}(p^{0},{\bf p},\rho(r))\left(\frac{M_{N}}{p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma}\right)\times F_{2N}(x_{N},Q^{2})

(24)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{Q^{2}}{(q^{z})^{2}}\left(\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}~-~(p^{z})^{2}}{2M_{N}^{2}}\right)+\frac{(p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma)^{2}}{M_{N}^{2}}\left(\frac{p^{z}~Q^{2}}{(p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma)q^{0}q^{z}}~+~1\right)^{2}\right]~

\displaystyle F_{3A,N}(x_{A},Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4A\int\,d^{3}r\,\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{M_{N}}{E_{N}({\bf p})}\,\int^{\mu}_{-\infty}dp^{0}S_{h}(p^{0},{\bf p},\rho(r))\times\frac{q^{0}}{q^{z}}\left({p^{0}q^{z}-p^{z}q^{0}\over p\cdot q}\right)F_{3N}(x_{N},Q^{2}),

(25)

where
\nu_{N}=\frac{p\cdot q}{M_{N}}=\frac{p^{0}q^{0}-p^{z}q^{z}}{M_{N}}
,
\gamma=\sqrt{1+\frac{4M_{N}^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}}
and
E_{N}
is the relativistic energy
of an on-shell nucleon.
It is notable that
F_{1A,N}(x_{A},Q^{2})
has been evaluated without using the Callan-Gross relation at the nuclear level
Zaidi:2019asc
.
The results obtained by using Eqs.
23
,
24
and
25

are labeled as the results with the spectral function(SF) only.

In the case of bound nucleons which interact among themselves via the exchange of virtual mesons
such as
\pi,~\rho,
etc., there is a finite non-zero probability that intermediate vector boson may interact with these mesons instead of the nucleon.
Therefore, we also incorporate the mesonic contribution by using the many-body field theoretical approach similar to the
case of bound nucleons following Ref.
Marco:1995vb
. The expressions for the mesonic structure functions
F_{1A,i}(x,Q^{2})
and

F_{2A,i}(x,Q^{2})
for pions and rho meson are given by
Zaidi:2019asc

\displaystyle F_{1A,i}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6\times a\times AM_{N}\int d^{3}r\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\theta(p^{0})~\delta ImD_{i}(p)\;2m_{i}

(26)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle~\left[\frac{F_{1i}(x_{i})}{m_{i}}~+~\frac{{|{\bf p}|^{2}~-~(p^{z})^{2}}}{2(p^{0}~q^{0}~-~p^{z}~q^{z})}\frac{F_{2i}(x_{i})}{m_{i}}\right],

\displaystyle F_{2A,i}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6\times a\int\,d^{3}r\,\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\theta(p^{0})~\delta ImD_{i}(p)\;2m_{i}\left(\frac{m_{i}}{p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma}\right)~F_{2i}(x_{i})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{Q^{2}}{(q^{z})^{2}}\left(\frac{|{\bf p}|^{2}~-~(p^{z})^{2}}{2m_{i}^{2}}\right)+\frac{(p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma)^{2}}{m_{i}^{2}}\left(\frac{p^{z}~Q^{2}}{(p^{0}~-~p^{z}~\gamma)q^{0}q^{z}}~+~1\right)^{2}\right],~

where subscript
i=\pi/\rho
,
m_{i}
is the mass of pion/rho meson,
x_{i}=\frac{Q^{2}}{-2p\cdot q}
with an extra minus sign due to the direction
of mesonic four momenta
p
, and
a=1
for pion and
a=2
for rho meson
Marco:1995vb
. Notice that the
F_{2A,\rho}(x,Q^{2})
has
an extra factor of two compared to pionic contribution because it has two transverse polarization states
Baym:1975vb
.
For the numerical calculations, we use the GRV PDFs parameterization
Gluck:1991ey
for both the
pion and rho meson. Recall that parity violating nuclear structure function
F_{3A}(x_{A},Q^{2})
has no
mesonic contribution as it depends mainly on the valence quarks distribution which average to zero
when the three charge states of pion and rho mesons are taken into consideration.

Besides the mesonic contributions, the shadowing and antishadowing effects, which dominate in the low
x
region, have also been taken into account in the present numerical calculations, following the work of Kulagin and Petti
Kulagin:2004ie
based on the Glauber-Gribov multiple scattering theory.
The nuclear structure functions
F_{iA}^{S}(x,Q^{2});~(i=1-3)
with the shadowing effect are given by

F_{iA}^{S}(x,Q^{2})=\delta R(x,Q^{2})\times F_{iN}(x,Q^{2})\;,

(28)

where superscript
S
corresponds to the nuclear shadowing effect and the correction factor
\delta R(x,Q^{2})
is taken from Ref.
Kulagin:2004ie
.

After taking the perturbative corrections up to NNLO
Moch:2004xu
;
Moch:2008fj
;
Vermaseren:2005qc
and nonperturbative TMC
Schienbein:2007gr
effect into account for the evaluation of free nucleon structure functions
which are being used as an input to calculate the nuclear structure functions
F_{iA}(x,Q^{2});~(i=1,2,3)
, the full expression for the
nuclear structure functions
F_{iA}(x,Q^{2})
with the nuclear medium effects is given by:

F_{iA}(x,Q^{2})=F_{iA,N}(x,Q^{2})+\;F_{iA,\pi}(x,Q^{2})+F_{iA,\rho}(x,Q^{2})\;+F_{iA}^{S}(x,Q^{2})\;,\;i=1-2

(29)

and

\displaystyle F_{3A}(x,Q^{2})=F_{3A,N}(x,Q^{2})+F_{3A}^{S}(x,Q^{2}).

(30)

It should be noted that
F_{3A}(x,Q^{2})
has no mesonic contribution.

IV
Summary and Conclusion

In this work, we have studied
\nu_{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
interactions on
{}^{40}Ar
target in the energy region of a few GeV. The numerical results have been
presented for the differential scattering cross sections off free nucleon as well as off the argon nuclear target in the kinematic region of

Q^{2}\geq 1
GeV
2
without and with different cuts on the center of mass energy
W
.
We summarize our findings below:

•

The nuclear medium effects on the differential cross sections for
\nu_{\mu}
-
40
Ar and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
-
40
Ar DIS processes, arising
from the inclusion of spectral functions that incorporate Fermi motion, binding energy, and nucleon correlations, lead to a clear
suppression of the cross sections. Importantly, this reduction is strongly kinematics dependent and is not uniform across the entire
range of Bjorken
x
and
y
, highlighting the nontrivial role of nuclear dynamics in shaping the observed behavior.

•

The mesonic contributions are found to play a decisive role in enhancing the cross sections, particularly in the intermediate region
of
x
, relative to the results obtained with only nuclear structure effects. This enhancement can be as large as

14-20\%
at
x=0.35
and
y=0.5
, and remains at a comparable level of
about
7-10\%
at
x=0.45
and
y=0.5
for
\nu_{\mu}
-induced scattering on the
40
Ar target in the energy region 4 GeV
\leq E\leq 6
GeV. In
the case of antineutrino induced processes, a similarly significant enhancement of
38-48
% and
16-25
%
is observed over the same kinematic region of
x
and
y
, underscoring the crucial impact of mesonic degrees of freedom.

•

The effect of shadowing and antishadowing corrections are very small in the considered kinematic range of
x
and
y
.

•

The effects of shadowing and antishadowing corrections are found to be negligibly small in the considered kinematic
range of
x
and
y
, indicating that their overall impact on the differential cross sections is minimal.

•

The nuclear medium effects are found to be significantly more pronounced in antineutrino-induced processes
than in neutrino-induced processes off the argon nucleus.

•

The imposition of a cut on the center of mass energy is found to significantly
suppress the cross section in the considered kinematic region of
x
and
y
for
4\leq E\leq 6
GeV. Notably, this
suppression becomes increasingly pronounced with rising
x
, while it systematically weakens with increasing beam energy.

•

The reduction in the
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
-
40
Ar differential cross sections due to the imposition of a cut on the
center of mass energy is found to be significantly more substantial than that observed for the
\nu_{\mu}
-
40
Ar
differential cross sections, underscoring a stronger sensitivity of antineutrino-induced processes to this kinematic constraint.

To conclude, nuclear medium effects are found to play a crucial and non-negligible role in the
kinematic regime relevant to DUNE and SBND energies. These effects have a direct and significant
impact on (anti)neutrino energy reconstruction in ongoing and future experiments, thereby influencing
the precision of oscillation measurements. Furthermore, a deeper and more systematic investigation of
nuclear medium effects is essential, particularly in the
Q^{2}
region beyond the domain where perturbative
QCD is reliably applicable. Such studies are indispensable for achieving a comprehensive and accurate
understanding of deep inelastic lepton-nucleus scattering.
