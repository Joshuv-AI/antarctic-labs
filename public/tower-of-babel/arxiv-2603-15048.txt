Homomorphisms of topological rings and change-of-scalar functors
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. We consider homomorphisms of complete, separated right or two-sided linear topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero 𝔣 : ℜ ⟶ 𝔖 \mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S} . Taut maps of right linear topological rings, strongly right taut maps of two-sided linear topological rings, left proflat continuous ring maps, and topological ring epimorphisms are discussed. For a left proflat topological ring epimorphism 𝔣 \mathfrak{f} , we show that the functor of restriction of scalars on the categories of left contramodules 𝔣 ♯ : 𝔖 ​ − − 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗍𝗋𝖺 ⟶ ℜ ​ − − 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗍𝗋𝖺 \mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}} is fully faithful. Assuming that the contramodule-to-module forgetful functor ℜ ​ − − 𝖢𝗈𝗇𝗍𝗋𝖺 ⟶ ℜ ​ − − 𝖬𝗈𝖽 \mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}} is fully faithful and the topological ring map 𝔣 \mathfrak{f} is left proflat, we prove that the commutative square of forgetful functors between the left contramodule and module categories over 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} and ℜ \mathfrak{R} is a pseudopullback diagram. This provides a description of the essential image of 𝔣 ♯ \mathfrak{f}_{\sharp} under the conjunction of the respective assumptions. The left adjoint functor to 𝔣 ♯ \mathfrak{f}_{\sharp} always exists, but is not exact even when 𝔣 \mathfrak{f} is (pro)flat. A right adjont functor to 𝔣 ♯ \mathfrak{f}_{\sharp} does not always exist, but for a left proflat map 𝔣 \mathfrak{f} we construct it explicitly and show that it has good exactness properties. This work is motivated by the theory of contraherent cosheaves of contramodules on formal schemes.



1.
Introduction

We start with several sections explaining the geometric motivation
before passing to the more technical and algebraic parts of
the Introduction.

1.1.
Schemes and quasi-coherent sheaves

Schemes are glued from affine schemes.
Affine schemes are described by their rings of functions.
To any commutative ring
R
, such as the ring of polynomials

R=k[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]
in several variables
x_{1}
, …,
x_{m}

over a field
k
, the affine scheme
\Spec R
is assigned.

Quasi-coherent sheaves on schemes are glued from modules over
commutative rings using the localization functors.
The abelian category of quasi-coherent sheaves on an affine scheme

U=\Spec R
is equivalent to the category of
R
-modules.
Let
V=\Spec S
be an affine open subscheme in
U
.
Then the functor of restriction of quasi-coherent sheaves to
the open subscheme
{-}|_{V}\colon U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Qcoh}}}\longrightarrow V{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Qcoh}}}
is described in
terms of modules as the functor of extension of scalars

S\otimes_{R}{-}\,\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
with respect to the ring
homomorphism
R\longrightarrow S
.

The example of a
principal affine open subscheme
is instructive.
In this case, the commutative
R
-algebra
S
is obtained by
inverting an element
r\in R
, that is
S=R[r^{-1}]
.
The restriction of quasi-coherent sheaves from
\Spec R
to

\Spec S
takes an
R
-module
M
to the
S
-module
M[r^{-1}]
.
This is the localization functor mentioned in the previous paragraph.

1.2.
Formal schemes and quasi-coherent torsion sheaves

Formal schemes are glued from affine formal schemes.
Affine formal schemes are described by their topological rings of
functions, such as the ring of formal power series

\mathfrak{R}=k[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}][[y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n}]]
in several variables

y_{1}
, …,
y_{n}
with coefficients in a ring of polynomials

k[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]
.

The relevant class of topological commutative rings
\mathfrak{R}
is specified
by several kinds of assumptions.
First of all, the topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
must be separated and
complete, and have a
linear topology
.
The latter condition means that open ideals form a base of
neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
.
A further nilpotency condition is imposed
[
7
, Sections 0.7.1
and I.10.1]
.
To any suitable topological commutative ring
\mathfrak{R}
, an affine formal
scheme
\Spf\mathfrak{R}
is assigned.

Quasi-coherent torsion sheaves
on formal schemes are glued from
torsion modules over topological commutative rings.
The abelian category
\mathfrak{U}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}
of quasi-coherent torsion sheaves on
an affine formal scheme
\mathfrak{U}=\Spf\mathfrak{R}
is equivalent to the full
subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
of torsion modules (known also as

discrete modules
) in the category of
\mathfrak{R}
-modules.
An
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
torsion

(or
discrete
) if the annihilator of any element of
\mathcal{M}
is
an open ideal in
\mathfrak{R}
.

What are the affine formal open subschemes of an affine formal
scheme
\mathfrak{U}=\Spf\mathfrak{R}
?
Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a morphism of topological commutative
rings.
Then the related morphism of affine formal schemes
\Spf\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow\Spf\mathfrak{R}
is said to be a
formal open immersion
if, for every
open ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, the closure
\mathfrak{J}
of the ideal

\mathfrak{S}\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\subset\mathfrak{S}
is an open ideal in
\mathfrak{S}
, the ideals
\mathfrak{J}

form a base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{S}
, and the induced maps of
affine schemes
\Spec\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}\longrightarrow\Spec\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
are open immersions.

Once again, the example of a
principal affine formal open
subscheme
is instructive.
In this case, there is an element
r\in\mathfrak{R}
such that
\mathfrak{S}
is
the completion of the commutative ring
\mathfrak{R}[r^{-1}]
with respect to
the topology induced by the topology on
\mathfrak{R}
.

Quasi-coherent torsion sheaves are glued from torsion modules using
the localization functors.
Let
\mathfrak{V}=\Spf\mathfrak{S}
be an affine formal open subscheme in
\mathfrak{U}=\Spf\mathfrak{R}
.
Then the functor of restriction of quasi-coherent torsion sheaves to
the formal open subscheme
{-}|_{\mathfrak{V}}\colon\mathfrak{U}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{V}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}
is
described in terms of modules as the functor of extension of
scalars
\mathfrak{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}_{\mathfrak{R}}{-}\,\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Tors}}}
.
Here the notation
\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}_{\mathfrak{R}}
stands for the topological tensor product
functor, which in this context can be also interprested as the

contratensor product
functor
\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}
.

The key observation is that, for every torsion
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{M}
,
the
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathfrak{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{M}
is also torsion.
This holds because an
\mathfrak{S}
-module is torsion if and only if its
underlying
\mathfrak{R}
-module is torsion.
For example, in the case of a principal affine open subscheme
\mathfrak{V}\subset\mathfrak{U}
corresponding to an element
r\in\mathfrak{R}
, one has

\mathfrak{S}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}[r^{-1}]
.

1.3.
Contramodules

Contramodules over topological rings
[
13
, Section 1.2]
,

[
15
, Section 2.1]
,
[
28
, Sections 1.2 and 5]
,

[
20
, Section 2.7]
,
[
21
, Sections 2.6–2.7]
,

[
29
, Section 6]
are the dual-analogous concept to discrete
or torsion modules.

In the context of a complete, separated topological commutative ring

\mathfrak{R}
with linear topology, an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
is
a set/abelian group/
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathfrak{P}
endowed with
infinite
summation operations

(p_{x}\in\mathfrak{P})_{x\in X}\longmapsto\sum\nolimits_{x\in X}r_{x}p_{x}\,\in\,\mathfrak{P},

assigning an element denoted formally by
\sum_{x\in X}r_{x}p_{x}\in\mathfrak{P}

to every family of elements
(r_{x}\in\mathfrak{R})_{x\in X}
converging to zero
in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
and an arbitrary family of elements

(p_{x}\in\mathfrak{P})_{x\in X}
.
Suitable contraassociativity and contraunitality axioms are imposed.

For any discrete
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{M}
and any
\mathfrak{R}
-module
W
,
the Hom
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\Hom_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{M},W)
has a natural

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure given by the simple formula

(1)

\left(\sum\nolimits_{x\in X}r_{x}h_{x}\right)(m)=\sum\nolimits_{x\in X}h_{x}(r_{x}m)\,\in\,W

for all
h_{x}\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathcal{M},W)
and
m\in\mathcal{M}
.
Here the sum in the right-hand side is finite, because
r_{x}m=0
for
all but a finite subset of indices
x\in X
(as
r_{x}\to 0
in
\mathfrak{R}

and the annihilator of
m
is open in
\mathfrak{R}
).

The category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
of
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is abelian.
It is endowed with an exact, faithful forgetful functor

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
(preserving the infinite products, but

not
the infinite direct sums).

1.4.
Contraherent cosheaves

Contraherent cosheaves
[
14
]
are the dual-analogous concept to
quasi-coherent sheaves.
Contraherent cosheaves on schemes are glued from modules over
commutative rings using the colocalization functors.

The exact category
U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}
of contraherent cosheaves on an affine
scheme
U=\Spec R
is equivalent to the full subcategory of

contraadjusted

R
-modules
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\subset R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
in
the category of
R
-modules.
Here an
R
-module
P
is said to be
contraadjusted
[
14
, Section 1.1]
,
[
17
, Section 2]

if
\Ext_{R}^{1}(R[r^{-1}],P)=0
for all
r\in R
.
There is no need to impose the
\Ext^{i}
vanishing condition for

i\geq 2
here, as the projective dimension of the
R
-module

R[r^{-1}]
never exceeds
1

[
17
, proof of Lemma 2.1]
.

Let
V=\Spec S
be an affine open subscheme in
U
.
Then the functor of restriction of contraherent cosheaves to
the open subscheme
{-}|_{V}\colon U{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}\longrightarrow V{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}
is described in terms
of modules as the functor of
coextension of scalars

\Hom_{R}(S,{-})\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}
.

In the case of a principal affine open subscheme
V=\Spec R[r^{-1}]
,
this means that the restriction of contraherent cosheaves from
U

to
V
takes a contraadjusted
R
-module
P
to the contraadjusted

R[r^{-1}]
-module
\Hom_{R}(R[r^{-1}],P)
.
This is the colocalization functor mentioned in the first paragraph
of this section.

1.5.
Contraherent cosheaves of contramodules

Dual-analogously to the quasi-coherent torsion sheaves, one would like
to define
contraherent cosheaves of contramodules
on formal
schemes (cf.
[
27
, Section 2]
).
But how does one construct the gluing functors?

Let
\mathfrak{U}=\Spf\mathfrak{R}
be an affine formal scheme.
We skip the discussion of the definition of the full subcategory
of
contraadjusted
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
It has been worked out in
[
14
, Section E.4]

and
[
28
, Example 7.12(3)]
, under mild assumptions on
\mathfrak{R}
.
We would like the category
\mathfrak{U}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}
of contraherent cosheaves of
contramodules on
\mathfrak{U}
to be equivalent to
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cta}}
.
But what would the functor of restriction to an affine formal open
subscheme do?

Let
\mathfrak{V}=\Spf\mathfrak{S}
be an affine formal open subscheme in an affine
formal scheme
\mathfrak{U}=\Spf\mathfrak{R}
.
The functor
{-}|_{\mathfrak{V}}\colon\mathfrak{U}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{V}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Ctrh}}}
is supposed to be
described on the level of contramodules as a functor of coextension
of scalars
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
(where
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}
is our notation for
the Hom groups in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
).

Now let
\mathfrak{P}
be a (contraadjusted)
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
How does one construct an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structure on

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
?

There is a natural explicit general formula defining a contramodule
structure on the Hom group
from a torsion module
,
see (
1
).
But
\mathfrak{S}
is
not
a torsion
\mathfrak{R}
-module; it is
an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
There does
not
seem to be a comparable explicit formula
defining a contramodule structure on the group of Hom
from
a contramodule
.

The Hom
into
a contramodule is a contramodule, with infinite
summation operations on functions defined elementwise.
So it is easy to construct an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
structure
on
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
in terms of the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure
on
\mathfrak{P}
.
But how does an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
structure on

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
emerge?

Notice that the functor
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is supposed to be right adjoint to the forgetful functor

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
But a right adjoint functor to the forgetful functor on
the contramodule categories, for a continuous homomorphism of
topological rings
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, does
not
exist
in general, if only because the forgetful functor need not preserve
infinite direct sums
[
21
, Section 2.9]
.
(A more detailed discussion of a specific counterexample of this kind
is presented in
Example
10.1
in this paper.)
Furthermore, a mere existence of such a right adjoint would not be
sufficient for the aims of the theory of contraherent cosheaves
of contramodules.
An explicit construction, allowing to establish exactness and
other properties, is very desirable.

The main aim of the present paper is to work out general techniques
for constructing the desired
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structure on

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
.

1.6.
The full-and-faithfulness approach

Succinctly put, one approach that we suggest works as follows.
There may be no simple formula defining an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
structure on
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
, but there are such simple formulas
making
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule and
an
\mathfrak{S}
-module.

The idea is to prove that one of the forgetful functors

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
or
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is
fully faithful.
Specifically, the functor
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is considered
in detail in this paper.

Under certain assumptions, we not only prove that the functor

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is fully faithful, but also describe
its essential image.
In fact, we show that an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule arises from
an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule if and only if its underlying
\mathfrak{R}
-module
arises from an
\mathfrak{S}
-module.
Then it follows immediately that there is a naturally defined

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structure on
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
, even if
this definition is not quite explicit.

Under less restrictive assumptions, we have no explicit description
of the essential image, but the functor
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is
still provable to be fully faithful.
It follows that the essential image enjoys certain closure properties;
specifically, it is a full subcategory closed under all limits and
finite colimits in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Using such properties, one can show that the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
belongs to the full subcategory of

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

1.8.
Ring epimorphisms

What is entailed in the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

being fully faithful, for a morphism of topological rings

\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
?
The case of abstract (discrete, nontopological) rings
R
and
S
is
relevant.

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a homomorphism of associative rings.
Then the following six conditions are
equivalent
[
34
, Section XI.1]
:

(1)

the forgetful functor
S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
for
the categories of left modules is fully faithful;

(2)

the forgetful functor
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
for
the categories of right modules is fully faithful;

(3)

any one or both of the two maps
S\rightrightarrows S\otimes_{R}S

induced by
f
are isomorphisms (or equivalently, surjective);

(4)

the two maps
S\rightrightarrows S\otimes_{R}S
are equal to each
other;

(5)

the multiplication map
S\otimes_{R}S\longrightarrow S
is an isomorphism
(or equivalently, injective);

(6)

f
is an epimorphism in the category of rings.

If the rings
R
and
S
are commutative, then conditions (1–6)
are also equivalent to the following condition:

(7)

f
is an epimorphism in the category of commutative rings.

Notice that all the three maps
S\rightrightarrows S\otimes_{R}S\longrightarrow S

are ring homomorphisms if the rings
R
and
S
are commutative (but
not in the general case of associative rings).

1.9.
Topological rings

The main results of this paper are stated in the context of
associative, noncommutative rings.

The maximal natural generality for the definition of a contramodule
is that of complete, separated right linear topological rings
\mathfrak{R}
.
The words “right linear” mean that open right ideals form a base of
neighborhoods of zero.
To such a topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
, one assigns the abelian categories
of
discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}
and

left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

A right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
is said to be
discrete
if
the annihilator of any element of
\mathcal{N}
is an open right ideal in
\mathfrak{R}
.
The left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are the sets/abelian groups/left

\mathfrak{R}
-modules with infinite summation operations, similarly to
the discussion in Section
1.3
above.
For every family of elements
(r_{x}\in\mathfrak{R})_{x\in X}
converging to
zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
, an infinite summation operation with
this family of coefficients is defined in any
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
.

The setting in this paper is a bit more restrictive.
For the main results, we make two additional assumptions.
Firstly, our topological rings need to have a
countable
base
of neighborhoods of zero.
Secondly, we assume a base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of
open
two-sided
ideals.

1.10.
Change of scalars and gluing over a formal scheme

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a continuous homomorphism of right linear
topological rings.
Then the forgetful/restriction-of-scalars functor

f_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
is well-defined.
The functor
f_{\diamond}
assigns to a discrete right
S
-module

\mathcal{N}
its underlying discrete right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}
.
The functor
f_{\diamond}
always has a right adjoint functor

f^{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R\allowbreak\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S
, called the functor
of
coextension of scalars

[
21
, Section 2.9]
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a continuous homomorphism of complete,
separated right linear topological rings.
Then the forgetful/restriction-of-scalars functor

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is well-defined.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
assigns to a left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{Q}
its underlying left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
always has a left adjoint functor

\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
, called the functor
of
contraextension of scalars

[
21
, Section 2.9]
.

Why are we so interested in a right adjoint functor to
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
,
which we want to compute as
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
, and which does not
even always exist?
What’s wrong with the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
left adjoint
to
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
, which exists for any morphism of (complete,
separated, right linear) topological rings
\mathfrak{f}
?

The discussion in
Section
1.5
suggests
considering
cosheaves of contramodules
.
This involves gluing contramodules over nonaffine formal schemes
using colocalization functors, and
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
are
the required colocalization functors.

Why not consider
sheaves of contramodules
?
This would mean gluing contramodules over nonaffine formal schemes
using localization functors, and the localization functors
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}

are always available.
The answer is that the functors of contraextension of
scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
do always exist, but even the most stringent
of reasonable assumptions on the morphism
\mathfrak{f}
do
not
make them

well-behaved enough
.

For the glued category of sheaves or cosheaves to be well-behaved
from the homological algebra standpoint, the functors used for
the gluing must be exact, if only on the suitable exact subcategories
of “adjusted” modules or contramodules.
The open immersion morphisms in algebraic geometry are flat, and so
the related localization/extension-of-scalars functors are exact.

But the functors of contraextension of scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
are

not
exact for morphisms of topological commutative rings

\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
corresponding to open immersions of affine formal
schemes
\Spf\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow\Spf\mathfrak{R}
.
A simple counterexample to this effect, involving Noetherian
commutative rings with adic topologies, is presented in
Example
10.8
at the end of
this paper (see also the preceding
Remark
10.7
).

The functor of contraextension of scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
only seems to
be exact on the exact category of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules (see
Lemma
6.2
).
Flat contramodules can indeed be glued over formal schemes using
the functors of contraextension of scalars.
The resulting exact category is known as the category of

flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves
on a formal scheme, or
more generally, on an ind-scheme
[
2
, Section 7.11.3]
,

[
23
, Chapter 3]
.
But flat (contra)modules are relatively rare.

The functors
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
, which we construct in this paper,
are
not
exact on the whole abelian categories of contramodules,
either.
But the functor
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
is exact on the exact category
of
cotorsion left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
whenever
\mathfrak{S}
is
a flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
The latter condition is satisfied for
left proflat
morphisms
of topological rings
\mathfrak{f}
; see
Section
1.11
below.
It is satisfied, in particular, in the case of a morphism of
commutative topological rings
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
corresponding to
an open immersion of affine formal schemes
\Spf\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow\Spf\mathfrak{R}
.
In fact, the functor
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},{-})
is exact on the even wider
exact category of
contraadjusted

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules in
the latter case.
Contraadjusted (contra)modules exist in abundance, and even cotorsion
(contra)modules are numerous enough for many purposes.

See also the discussion in
[
27
, Section 2]
.

1.11.
Classes of morphisms of topological rings

Several classes of morphisms of topological rings are discussed in
detail in this paper.
Borrowing the terminology from
[
9
, Section Tag 0GX1]
, we
consider
taut maps
of two-sided linear topological rings.
All rings in
[
9
]
are generally assumed to be commutative;
in the context of not necessarily commutative two-sided linear
topological rings, we are interested in what we call
strongly
right taut
continuous ring maps.
We use the later concept in order to define a more narrow class of

left proflat
continuous ring maps.
We also discuss
proepimorphisms
of two-sided linear
topological rings.

More explicitly, the left proflat maps of complete, separated
two-sided linear topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods
of zero
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
are described by the following sets of
data.
There is a morphism
f\colon(R_{n})_{n\geq 1}\longrightarrow(S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
projective systems of rings, indexed by the positive integers
n
.
The transition maps
R_{n+1}\longrightarrow R_{n}
and
S_{n+1}\longrightarrow S_{n}

in both the projective systems are surjective ring maps.
The ring maps
f_{n}\colon R_{n}\longrightarrow S_{n}
have the property that they
make
S_{n}
a flat left
R_{n}
-module for every
n\geq 1
.
An important assumption is that the induced
R_{n}
-
S_{n+1}
-bimodule
maps
R_{n}\otimes_{R_{n+1}}S_{n+1}\longrightarrow S_{n}
must be isomorphisms.

The map
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is the projective limit

\mathfrak{f}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}f_{n}
.
The topologies on
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
are the topologies of projective
limit of discrete rings
R_{n}
and
S_{n}
.
Then the map
\mathfrak{f}
is left proflat; and all the left proflat maps
of complete, separated two-sided linear topological rings with
countable bases of neighborhoods of zero can be obtained in this way.
The map
\mathfrak{f}
is a a proepimorphism of topological rings if and only
if the maps
f_{n}
are ring epimorphisms for all
n\geq 1
.

More generally, a continuous homomorphism of two-sided linear
topological rings
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
is called a
topological ring
proepimorphism
if, for every pair of open two-sided ideals

I\subset R
and
J\subset S
such that
f(I)\subset J
, the induced
homomorphism of discrete rings
R/I\longrightarrow S/J
is a ring epimorphism.

Here are our results in this context:

(a)

A strongly right taut continuous map of two-sided linear
topological rings
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
is a topological ring proepimorphism
if and only if the functor of restriction of scalars on the categories
of discrete right modules
f_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R

is fully faithful
(Proposition
7.6
).

(b)

A strongly right taut continuous map of complete, separated
two-sided linear topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods
of zero
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is a topological ring proepimorphism if
and only if the functor of restriction of scalars on the categories of
separated left contramodules
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}
is
fully faithful
(Proposition
7.7
).

(c)

For a morphism of complete, separated right linear topological
rings with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}

such that the functor of restriction of scalars on the categories of
left contramodules
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
takes flat

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules to flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules,
the functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
is fully faithful if and only if the similar
functor on the categories of separated left contramodules

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}
is fully faithful, and if and
only if he similar functor on the categories of flat left contramodules

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Flat}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Flat}}}
is fully faithful
(Theorem
7.4
).

(d)

Consequently, for a left proflat map of complete, separated
two-sided linear topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods
of zero
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, the functor of restriction of scalars on
the categories of left contramodules
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is fully faithful if and only if
\mathfrak{f}
is a topological ring
proepimorphism
(Corollary
7.8
).

Let us make a small comment concerning the comparison between
assertions (b) and (d) above.
All the main results of this paper require the
strong right
tautness assumption
on a ring map
\mathfrak{f}
is the case of
separated

contramodules.
This already presumes that the topological rings
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
are
two-sided linear.

In the context of the whole abelian categories (of
not

necessarily separated) contramodules, the even stronger
left
proflatness
assumption on
\mathfrak{f}
is needed to make our arguments work.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated right linear topological ring,

R
be a ring, and
\rho\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
be a ring homomorphism
whose image is dense in
\mathfrak{R}
.
Endow
R
with the induced topology (where a base of neighborhoods of

\mathfrak{I}
ranges over the open right ideals in
\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

In the case of a complete, separated right linear topological ring

with a countable base of neighorhoods of zero, the two main

(a)

1.13.
Characterizing the essential image

In the context of assertion (d) from
Section
1.11
,
the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is
fully faithful, so the question of describing its essential image
becomes relevant.
We answer this question under the additional assumption that
the contramodule-to-module forgetful
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

is fully faithful, too.

Here is the formulation of our result.
Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a left proflat proepimorphism of
complete, separated two-sided linear topological rings with countable
bases of neighborhoods of zero.
Assume additionally that the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully faithful.
Then a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
arises from
an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule if and only if the underlying left

\mathfrak{R}
-module of
\mathfrak{Q}
arises from an
\mathfrak{S}
-module.
Moreover, the
\mathfrak{S}
-module structure structure on
\mathfrak{Q}
is
unique if it exists.
It follows that the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is fully faithful, too.

Notice that we do
not
claim here that an
\mathfrak{S}
-module
structure extending the given
\mathfrak{R}
-module structure on
a set/abelian group
Q
is always unique if it exists.
This is
not
true, as a left proflat proepimorphism of
topological rings
need not
be an epimorphism of the underlying
abstract rings (see
Example
5.3

and Remark
9.7
).
Rather, the claim is that there is at most one
\mathfrak{S}
-module structure
extending the given
\mathfrak{R}
-module structure
if the given

\mathfrak{R}
-module structure arises from an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure
.

More generally, the topological proepimorphism assumption on
the map
\mathfrak{f}
can be dropped.
Then the functor of restriction of scalars

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is no longer fully faithful.
In this context, we prove the following results, stated in terms of
the commutative square diagrams of forgetful functors.
In all the three assertions, the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is assumed to be fully faithful:

(a)

For a taut continuous map of complete, separated right linear
topological ring with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero

\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, a right
\mathfrak{S}
-module is discrete if and only if
its underlying right
\mathfrak{R}
-module is discrete
(Proposition
9.1
).

(b)

For a strongly right taut continuous map of complete, separated
two-sided linear topological ring with countable bases of neighborhoods
of zero
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, the commutative diagram of forgetful
functors

\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{f}_{*}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}}

is a pseudopullback square (up to category equivalence).
This is our Proposition
9.2
.

(c)

For a left proflat map of complete, separated two-sided linear
topological ring with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero

\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, the commutative diagram of forgetful functors

\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{f}_{*}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}}

This is our Theorem
9.5
.

The horizontal arrows with tails on both the diagrams above show fully
faithful functors.

Once again, similarly to the comments at the end of
Section

,
the assertion (b) (concerning separated contramodules) is provable
under the strong right tautness assumption on
\mathfrak{f}
, while our proof
of the assertion (c) (concerning arbitrary contramodules) requires
the left proflatness assumption on
\mathfrak{f}
.

Constructing the

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{P}

\mathfrak{S}

\mathfrak{S}

\mathfrak{P}

\mathfrak{S}

topology
is open subgroups form a base of neighborhoods of zero in
A
.
All the topological abelian groups in this paper are presumed to have
linear topologies.

The
completion
of a topological abelian group
A
is
the abelian group
\mathfrak{A}=\varprojlim_{U\subset A}A/U
, where
the projective limit is taken over the directed poset of all open
subgroups
U\subset A
.
The abelian group
\mathfrak{A}
is endowed with the topology of projective
limit of the discrete abelian groups
A/U
.
The natural
completion map

\lambda_{A}\colon A\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}
is
a continuous homomorphism of topological abelian groups.

A topological abelian group
A
is said to be
separated
if
the map
\lambda_{A}
is injective, and
complete
if
the map
\lambda_{A}
is surjective.
The completion
\mathfrak{A}
of any topological abelian group
A
is
a complete and separated topological abelian group.

A topological ring
R
is said to be
right linear
(or to have
a
right linear topology
) if open right ideals form a base of
neighborhoods of zero in
R
.
Similarly, the topological ring
R
is said to be
two-sided
linear
(or to have a
two-sided linear topology
) if open
two-sided ideals form a base of neighborhoods of zero.
In the case of a commutative ring
R
, we speak simply of
a
linear ring topology
or a
linear topological ring
.

For any right linear topological ring
R
, there exists a unique
topological ring structure on the completion
\mathfrak{R}
of the topological
abelian group
R
such that the completion map
\lambda_{R}\colon R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}

is a ring homomorphism.
This ring topology makes
\mathfrak{R}
a right linear topological ring.
If the topological ring
R
is two-sided linear, then so is its
completion
\mathfrak{R}
.

Let
R
be a right linear topological ring.
A right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}
is said to be
discrete
if, endowed
with the discrete topology,
\mathcal{N}
becomes a topological
R
-module.
The latter condition means that the action map
\mathcal{N}\times R\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}

is continuous in the given topology on
R
and the discrete topology
on
\mathcal{N}
.
Equivalently, a right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}
is discrete if and only if
the annihilator of every element of
\mathcal{N}
is an open right ideal in
R
.

The full subcategory of discrete right
R
-modules is denoted by

{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R\subset{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
.
The full subcategory
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
is closed under subobjects, quotients,
and infinite direct sums in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
.
In other words, one can say that
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
is a
hereditary
pretorsion class
in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R

[
34
, Sections VI.1 and VI.4]
.
It follows that
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
is a Grothendieck abelian category.
The inclusion functor
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
is exact and preserves
infinite direct sums (but
not
infinite products).

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be the completion of
R
.
Then the
R
-module structure on any discrete right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}

can be extended to a discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module structure on
\mathcal{N}
in
a unique way.
Hence the functor of restriction of scalars
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R

induces an equivalence of categories
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\simeq{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
.

For any abelian group
A
and any set
X
, we denote by
A[X]=A^{(X)}

the direct sum of copies of
A
indexed by the elements of
X
.
The elements of
A[X]
are interpreted as finite formal linear
combinations
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x
of elements of
X
with
the coefficients
a_{x}\in A
.
The word “finite” means that
a_{x}=0
for all but a finite subset of
indices
x\in X
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a complete, separated topological abelian group, and
let
X
be a set.
We put
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]=\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}}(\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{U})[X]
, where
the projective limit is taken over the directed poset of all open
subgroups
\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}
.
The elements of
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
are interpreted as infinite formal linear
combinations
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x
of elements of
X
whose families
of coefficients
a_{x}\in\mathfrak{A}
converge to zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{A}
.
The latter condition means that, for every open subgroup

\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}
, the set of indices
x\in X
for which
a_{x}\notin\mathfrak{U}

is finite.

The assignment
X\longmapsto\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
is a covariant functor

\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
, but we will mostly consider it as a functor

\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
.
Specifically, any map of sets
f\colon X\longrightarrow Y
induces a map of
sets/abelian groups
\mathfrak{A}[[f]]\colon\mathfrak{A}[[X]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}[[Y]]
given by
the rule
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x\longmapsto\sum_{y\in Y}b_{y}y
, where

b_{y}=\sum_{x\in X}^{f(x)=y}a_{x}
.
Here the infinite sum in the latter formula is computed as
the limit of finite partial sums in the topology of
\mathfrak{A}
.
The limit is well-defined and exists due to the completeness and
separatedness conditions on
\mathfrak{A}
and the zero-convergence condition
on the family of elements
(a_{x}\in\mathfrak{A})_{x\in X}
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated right linear topological ring.
Then, for any set
X
, there is a natural map of sets

\epsilon_{X}\colon X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
taking every element
x\in X

to the formal linear combination
\sum_{y\in X}r_{y}y
with
the coefficients
r_{x}=1
and
r_{y}=0
for
y\neq x
.
Furthermore, for any set
X
, there is a natural map of sets/abelian
groups
\phi_{X}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{R}[[X]]]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
, taking a formal
linear combination of formal linear combinations of elements of
X

to a formal linear combination of elements of
X
.
The “opening of parentheses” map
\phi_{X}
is constructed in terms of
the operations of multiplication of pairs of elements in
\mathfrak{R}
and
infinite sums of zero-convergent families of elements in
\mathfrak{R}
.
The conditions that the multiplication map
\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}

is continuous and open right ideals form a base of neighborhoods of zero
in
\mathfrak{R}
guarantee the convergence of the families of elements whose
sums one needs to compute when opening the parentheses.

The natural transformations
\epsilon_{X}
and
\phi_{X}
define
the structure of a
monad
on the category of sets on the functor

\mathfrak{R}[[{-}]]
(we suggest the discussion
in
[
29
, Section 6]
for an introductory exposition).
One usually speaks about “algebras over a monad”, but in our context
we prefer to call them
modules over a monad
.
The category of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is defined
as the category of modules over the monad
\mathfrak{R}[[{-}]]
on
\mathsf{Sets}
.
Explicitly, this means that a
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{P}

is a set endowed with a map of sets
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
,
called the
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contraaction map
.
The usual axioms of (contra)unitality and (contra)associativity,
involving the maps
\epsilon_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
\phi_{\mathfrak{P}}
, are imposed
on the contraaction map
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}
.

For any abstract (nontopological) ring
R
, there is a similar monad
structure on the functor
X\longmapsto R[X]
on the category of sets,
and the category of modules over the monad
R[{-}]
is naturally
equivalent (in fact, isomorphic) to the category of left

R
-modules
[
29
, Section 6.1]
.
For a complete, separated topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
, the set
\mathfrak{R}[X]

is naturally embedded into the set
\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
as the subset of finite
formal linear combinations in the set of zero-convergent infinite ones.
The inclusion
\mathfrak{R}[X]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
is a natural transformation that
is a morphism of monads on
\mathsf{Sets}
, and it induces a forgetful functor

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, which assigns to every
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{P}
its underlying
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathfrak{P}
.
Essentially, the structure of an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is the structure
of infinite summation operations on
\mathfrak{P}
with zero-convergent families
of coefficients from
\mathfrak{R}
; the functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

forgets the infinite summation operations and only keeps
the finite ones.

The category of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is a locally
presentable abelian category with enough projective objects.
The forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is exact and
preserves infinite products (but
not
infinite direct sums).
The projective objects of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
are precisely the direct
summands of the
free left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
,
where
X\in\mathsf{Sets}
.
Here the left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure on
\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
is given
by the contraaction map
\pi_{\mathfrak{R}[[X]]}=\phi_{X}
(this is a special
case of the general construction of the “free algebra/module over
a monad”).
For any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
and any set
X
, there is
a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{R}[[X]],\mathfrak{Q})\simeq\mathfrak{Q}^{X}=\Hom_{\mathsf{Sets}}(X,\mathfrak{Q}),

where
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}({-},{-})
denotes the group of morphisms in the abelian
category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module and
W
be an abelian
group.
Then the group of abelian group maps
\mathfrak{Q}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{N},W)
has
a natural structure of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule given by the formula
similar to the one in Section
1.3
:

\pi\left(\sum\nolimits_{q\in\mathfrak{Q}}r_{q}q\right)(y)=\sum\nolimits_{q\in\mathfrak{Q}}q(yr_{q})\,\in\,W

for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
.
Here
(r_{q})_{q\in\mathfrak{Q}}
is a zero-convergent family of elements
in
\mathfrak{R}
, and the sum in the right-hand side of the formula above
is finite because
yr_{q}=0
in
\mathcal{N}
for all but a finite subset of
indices
q\in\mathfrak{Q}
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module and
\mathfrak{P}
be a left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
Then the
contratensor product

\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}
is an abelian
group constructed as the cokernel of (the difference of) the natural
pair of abelian group maps

\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\,\rightrightarrows\,\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}.

Here the first map
\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}
is
induced by the contraaction map
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
.
The second map
\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}
,
constructed in terms of the discrete right action of
\mathfrak{R}
in
\mathcal{N}
,
is given by the formula

y\otimes\sum\nolimits_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}r_{p}p\,\longmapsto\,\sum\nolimits_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}yr_{p}\otimes p.

The sum in the right-hand side is finite because
yr_{p}=0
in
\mathcal{N}

for all but a finite subset of indices
p\in\mathfrak{P}
.

For any discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
, andy left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, and any abelian group
W
, there is
a natural adjunction isomorphism of abelian groups

\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P},\>W)\simeq\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P},\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{N},W)).

For any discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
and any set
X
, there
is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\simeq\mathcal{N}[X]=\mathcal{N}^{(X)}.

The contratensor product functor

\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{Ab}

preserves all colimits in both of its arguments.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule and
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be
a closed subgroup in the topological abelian group
\mathfrak{R}
.
Then we denote by
\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathfrak{P}
the image of the composition

\mathfrak{A}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathfrak{P}

of the natural inclusion
\mathfrak{A}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]

with the contraaction map
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
.
Denoting by
A\cdot P\subset P
the subgroup spanned by the products

ap\in P
(
a\in A
,
p\in P
) for any given ring
R
,
left
R
-module
P
, and additive subgroup
A\subset R
, one obviously
has
\mathfrak{A}\cdot\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
.

For any closed left ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, the subgroup
\mathfrak{J}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
is
a left
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule in
\mathfrak{P}
.
For any closed right ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any set
X
, one has

\mathfrak{J}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{R}[[X]]=\mathfrak{J}[[X]]\subset\mathfrak{R}[[X]].

For any open right ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, there is a natural
isomorphism of abelian groups

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}).

For any open two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, the left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure on the quotient contramodule

\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})
comes from a left module structure over
the discrete quotient ring
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
.
In this context, for any right
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module

, there is
a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

N\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq N\otimes_{\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}}(\mathfrak{P}/\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}).

3.
Separated and Flat Contramodules

This section is based on the results of
[
14
, Section E.1]

and
[
28
, Section 6]
.
Another exposition can be found in
[
25
, Section 8]
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated right linear topological ring.
A left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is said to be
flat
if
the contratensor product functor

{-}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\mathsf{Ab}

is exact.
Since the direct limit functors are exact in the abelian category

\mathsf{Ab}
, the class of flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under
direct limits in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

For a complete, separated two-sided linear topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
,
all discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules are direct limits of right modules
over the discrete quotient rings of
\mathfrak{R}
, and all short exact sequences
of discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules are direct limits of short exact
sequences of right modules over the discrete quotient rings of
\mathfrak{R}
.
It follows that a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is flat if and only
if the left
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
\mathfrak{F}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{F})
is flat for every open
two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.
It suffices to check this condition for ideals
\mathfrak{I}
belonging to
any chosen base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
consisting of open
two-sided ideals.

For any
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, consider the projective limit of
abelian groups

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})=\varprojlim\nolimits_{\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{R}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})

taken over the directed poset of all open right ideals
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.
The abelian group
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
comes together with the natural

completion map

\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
.
In fact, there is a natural left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure on

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
, making
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
morphism
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
.
Put
\Omega_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})=\ker(\mathfrak{P}\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P}))=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})\subset\mathfrak{P}
.
It follows that
\Omega_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
is an
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule
of
\mathfrak{P}
.

The left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
is said to be
separated
if

\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
is an injective map, and
\mathfrak{P}
is said to be

complete
if the map
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
is surjective.
The left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
is always separated.
The subcontramodule
\Omega_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})\subset\mathfrak{P}
is called
the
nonseparatedness kernel
of
\mathfrak{P}
.

.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated right linear topological ring

Then

(a)
all left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are complete;

(b)

\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}\neq\mathfrak{P}

(c)

Part (a) is
[
28
, Lemma 6.3(b)]

[

, Theorem 5.3]
.
Part (b) is
[

The case of a two-sided linear topological ring

See

Corollary 3.2

Let
\mathfrak{R}

In this setting:

(a)
For any discrete right
\mathfrak{R}

(b)

\mathfrak{R}

yet.

Now under the latter assumption, we have a right exact sequence of

-contramodules
\Omega_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow 0

Part (b): the first assertion follows immediately from part (a).
The second assertion is

(c).

Lemma 3.3

.

4.
Taut and Strongly Right Taut Maps of Topological Rings

We start with the general setting of right linear topological rings.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
and
S
be right linear topological rings, and let

f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a ring map.
Then the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
is continuous;

(2)

the open right ideals
J\subset S
for which the right ideal
I=f^{-1}(J)\subset R
is open in
R
form a base of neighborhoods
of zero in
S
;

(3)

any discrete right
S
-module is also discrete as a right

R
-module.

Proof.

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2) Obvious.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(3) Let
\mathcal{N}
be a discrete right
S
-module
and
y\in\mathcal{N}
be an element.
Let
J
be the annihilator of
y
in
S
; then
J
is an open
right ideal in
S
.
The annihilator of
y
in
R
is the right ideal
f^{-1}(J)\subset R
.
Since
f
is continuous,
f^{-1}(J)
is an open right ideal in
R
.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(1) Let
J\subset S
be an open right ideal.
Then the right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}=R/J
is discrete.
The ideal
J\subset S
is the annihilator of the coset
y=1+J\in\mathcal{N}

in
S
.
Following the previous paragraph,
f^{-1}(J)
is the annihilator of
y

in
R
.
By assumption,
\mathcal{N}
is a discrete right
R
-module; so
f^{-1}(J)

is an open right ideal in
R
.
As open right ideals form a base of neighborhoods of zero in
S

and the preimage of any such ideal is open in
R
, it follows that

f
is a continuous map.
∎

In the following two lemmas, topological right modules over right
linear topological rings are considered.
For this reason, and in order to avoid any ambiguity, we refrain from
relying on our usual terminology of “discrete modules” over
topological rings, and utilize the longer expression “endowed with
the discrete topology, remains/becomes a topological module” instead.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R
and
S
be right linear topological rings, and let

f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a ring map.
Consider the following three conditions:

(1)

for any open right ideal
I\subset R
, the closure
J
of
the right ideal
f(I)S\subset S
is an open right ideal in
S
;

(2)

the open right ideals
I\subset R
for which the closure
J
of
the right ideal
f(I)S\subset S
is an open right ideal in
S
form
a base of neighborhoods of zero in
R
;

(3)

a separated topological right
S
-module remains a topological
right
S
-module when endowed with the discrete topology whenever,
viewed as right
R
-module and endowed with a discrete topology,
it becomes a topological right
R
-module.

The equivalence and implication
(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2)

\Longrightarrow
(3)
always hold.
When the ring
S
is commutative, one has

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2)

\Longleftrightarrow
(3)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2) Obvious.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(3) Let
\mathscr{N}
be a separated topological
right
S
-module and
y\in\mathscr{N}
be an element.
Since the action map
\mathscr{N}\times S\longrightarrow\mathscr{N}
is continuous,
the embedding
S=\{y\}\times S\longrightarrow\mathscr{N}\times S
is continuous,
and
0\in\mathscr{N}
is a closed point, it follows that the annihilator

J^{\prime}\subset S
of
y
in
S
is a closed right ideal in
S
.
Now
I=f^{-1}(J^{\prime})
is the annihilator of
y
in
R
.
By assumption,
I
is an open right ideal in
R
.
We have
f(I)S\subset J^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
is a closed right ideal in
S
,
hence the closure
J
of
f(I)S
in
S
is contained in
J^{\prime}
, that is

J\subset J^{\prime}
.
By (1),
J
is an open right ideal in
S
; hence
J^{\prime}
is an open
right ideal in
S
, too.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(1) Consider the cyclic
S
-module
\mathscr{N}=S/J
,
and endow it with the quotient topology.
Then
\mathscr{N}
is separated topological
S
-module (since

J\subset S
is a closed ideal).
The annihilator of any element
y\in\mathscr{N}
in
S
contains the ideal

J\subset S
, hence the annihilator of
y
in
R
contains
the open ideal
I\subset R
.
Thus, endowed with the discrete topology,
\mathscr{N}
becomes a topological

R
-module.
By (2), it follows that, endowed with the discrete topology,
\mathscr{N}
is
also a topological
S
-module.
Hence the annihilator
J
of the coset
1+J\in\mathscr{N}
is an open ideal
in
S
.
∎

We will say that a ring map

is
taut

[

, Section Tag 0GX1]

the equivalent conditions of Lemma
4.2
(1) or (2).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
R
and
S
be right linear topological rings, and
let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a ring map.
Consider the following four conditions:

(1)

is continuous and taut;

(2)

for any open right ideal
I\subset R
, the closure
J
of
the right ideal
f(I)S\subset S
is an open right ideal in
S
, and
such open right ideals
J\subset S
form a base of neighborhoods
of zero in
S
;

(3)

open right ideals
I\subset R
for which the closure
J
of
the right ideal
f(I)S\subset S
is an open right ideal in
S
form
a base of neighborhoods of zero in
R
, and such open right ideals

J\subset S
form a base of neighborhoods of zero in
S
;

(4)

a separated topological right
S
-module remains a topological
right
S
-module when endowed with the discrete topology if and only
if, viewed as right
R
-module and endowed with a discrete topology,
it becomes a topological right
R
-module.

The equivalences and implication
(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2)

\Longleftrightarrow
(3)

\Longrightarrow
(4)
always
hold.
When the ring
S
is commutative, one has

(1)
\Longleftrightarrow
(2)

\Longleftrightarrow
(3)

\Longleftrightarrow
(4)
.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) Let
J^{\prime}\subset S
be an open right ideal
in
S
.
Then
I=f^{-1}(J^{\prime})\subset R
is an open right ideal in
R

(since
f
is continuous).
Hence
J^{\prime}
contains the closure
J
of the right ideal

f(I)S\subset S
.
The right ideal
J\subset S
is open (since
f
is taut).
Thus the open right ideals
J
form a base of neighborhoods of zero
in
S
.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1) Obviously, (2) implies that
f
is taut.
Now let
J^{\prime}\subset S
be an open right ideal.
By (2), there exists an open right ideal
I\subset R
such that
J^{\prime}

contains the closure
J
of the right ideal
f(I)S\subset S
.
Hence
I
is contained in the right ideal
I^{\prime}=f^{-1}(J^{\prime})\subset R
.
Thus the right ideal
I^{\prime}\subset R
is open, too; so

is continuous.

(2)
\Longleftrightarrow
(3) Obvious.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(4) The condition that
f
is continuous
implies the “only if” implication in (4) by
Lemma
4.1
(1)
\Rightarrow
(3).
The condition that
f
is taut implies the “if” implication in (4)
by Lemma
4.2
(1)
\Rightarrow
(3).

(4)
\Longrightarrow
(1): the condition that

is continuous
follows from the “only if” implication in (4) by

4.1
(3)
\Rightarrow
(1)
(apply the “only if” implication in (4) to a topological right

S
-module with discrete topology).
The condition that

is taut follows from the “if” implication

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
and
S
be right linear topological rings, and
let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S

Let

such
that the closure

of the right ideal

S

J^{\prime\prime}
of the right ideal

f(I^{\prime\prime})S\subset S

R^{\prime}=R/I^{\prime}
,
R^{\prime\prime}=R/I^{\prime\prime}
,

, and

Then the induced
R^{\prime}
-

-bimodule map

Proof.

Denote by
f^{\prime}\colon R^{\prime}\longrightarrow S^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime\prime}\colon R^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow S^{\prime\prime}
the maps of
quotient rings induced by

.
As

is an open two-sided ideal in

.
Hence
S^{\prime}=S^{\prime\prime}/(f^{\prime\prime}(I^{\prime}/I^{\prime\prime})S^{\prime\prime})
.
This means precisely that the map
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R^{\prime\prime}}S^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow S^{\prime}
is
an isomorphism.
∎

(1)

(2)

and

, and, using the notation

-

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) The condition of

It follows that the corresponding open two-sided ideals

is the closure of the right ideal

in

Now it remains to refer to

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(1) We need to prove that

is the closure
of the right ideal
f(I_{n})S
in
S
.
Indeed, denote by
f_{n}\colon R_{n}\longrightarrow S_{n}
the maps of the quotient rings
induced by
f
.
The condition that the map
R_{n}\otimes_{R_{n+1}}S_{n+1}\longrightarrow S_{n}
is
an isomorphism means that
S_{n}=S_{n+1}/(f_{n+1}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})S_{n+1})
,
or equivalently,
J_{n}/J_{n+1}=f_{n+1}(I_{n}/I_{n+1})S_{n}\subset S_{n+1}

for every
n\geq 1
.
It follows that

\begin{split}J_{n}&=J_{n+1}+f(I_{n})S\\
&=J_{n+2}+f(I_{n+1})S+f(I_{n})S\\
&=J_{n+2}+f(I_{n})S\\
&=J_{n+3}+f(I_{n+2})S+f(I_{n})S\\
&=J_{n+3}+f(I_{n})S\\
&=\dotsb\\
&=J_{m}+f(I_{n})S\end{split}

for every
m>n

As the open two-sided ideals
J_{m}
form a base of neighborhoods
of zero in
S
, the desired conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
be complete, separated right linear topological
rings, and let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a taut continuous ring map.
Assume that the topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
has a countable base of
neighborhoods of zero.
Let
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be an open right ideal and
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{S}

be the closure of the right ideal
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{S}
.
Then one has
\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{S}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{I}=\mathfrak{I}_{1}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{2}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{3}\supset\dotsb
be a descending
chain of open right ideals in
\mathfrak{R}
, indexed by the positive integers,
that is a base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
.
For every
n\geq 1
, denote by
\mathfrak{J}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{S}
the closure of
the right ideal
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I}_{n})\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{S}
.
By Lemma
4.3
(2), the right ideals

\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}_{1}\supset\mathfrak{J}_{2}\supset\mathfrak{J}_{3}\supset\dotsb
are open in
\mathfrak{S}

and form a base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{S}
.
Similarly to the discussion in the proof of
Lemma
4.4
, we have
\mathfrak{J}_{n}/\mathfrak{J}_{n+1}=(\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I}_{n})\mathfrak{S}+\mathfrak{J}_{n+1})/\mathfrak{J}_{n+1}\subset\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}_{n+1}
for every

(since
\mathfrak{J}_{n+1}
is an open right ideal in
\mathfrak{S}
).

The inclusion

follows immediately from
the inclusion
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\subset\mathfrak{J}
and the fact that
\mathfrak{J}
is
a closed (in fact, open) right ideal in
\mathfrak{S}
.
To prove the converse inclusion, let

be an element.
Then we have
\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\mathfrak{S}+\mathfrak{J}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{S}

s_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}\mathfrak{f}(r_{i})t_{i}+s_{2}
for some
m_{1}\geq 1
,

r_{1}
, …,
r_{m_{1}}\in\mathfrak{I}
,
t_{1}
, …,
t_{m_{1}}\in\mathfrak{S}
,
and
s_{2}\in\mathfrak{J}_{2}
.
Similarly,
\mathfrak{J}_{2}=\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I}_{2})\mathfrak{S}+\mathfrak{J}_{3}\subset\mathfrak{S}
, hence

s_{2}=\sum_{i=m_{1}+1}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\mathfrak{f}(r_{i})t_{i}+s_{3}
for some
m_{2}\geq 1
,

r_{m_{1}+1}
, …,
r_{m_{1}+m_{2}}\in\mathfrak{I}_{2}
,
t_{m_{1}+1}
, …,

, and

.

Proceeding in this way, we construct a sequence of integers

, a sequence of elements

,

, such that

r_{i}\in\mathfrak{J}_{n}
for all

,
a sequence of elements

, and a sequence of elements

, since

\mathfrak{J}_{n}
form a base of neighborhoods of zero
in
\mathfrak{S}
.
As the sequence of elements

6.
Change-of-Scalar Functors I

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a homomorphism of (abstract, nontopological)
rings.
Then every
S
-module has an underlying
R
-module structure, so
there are exact, faithful functors of restriction of scalars

f_{*}\colon S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and
f_{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
.

The functors
f_{*}
have adjoint functors on both sides.
In particular, for left modules, the functor of
extension of
scalars

f^{*}\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
left adjoint to
f_{*}
is given
by the formula
f^{*}(M)=S\otimes_{R}M
for any left
R
-module
M
.
The functor of
coextension of scalars

f^{!}\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
right adjoint to
f_{*}
is given by the formula

f^{!}(M)=\Hom_{R}(S,M)
for all
M\in R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a continuous homomorphism of right linear
topological rings.
Then, as explained in Section
5

(based on Lemma
4.1
(3)), the functor

f_{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}R
applied to discrete modules provides
a functor of restriction of scalars
f_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
, taking every discrete right
S
-module
\mathcal{N}
to its
underlying discrete right
R
-module
\mathcal{N}
.

The functor
f_{\diamond}
is always exact and faithful, and preserves
infinite direct sums.
So, as any colimit-preserving functor between Grothendieck categories,
the functor
f_{\diamond}
has a right adjoint functor of

coextension of scalars

f^{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S

[
21
, Section 2.9]
.
The functor
f^{\diamond}
is left exact, preserves infinite products,
and takes injective objects to injective objects.
For any discrete right
R
-module
M
, the discrete right

S
-module
f^{\diamond}(M)
can be computed as the maximal discrete

S
-submodule of the right
S
-module
f^{!}(M)=\Hom_{R^{\mathrm{op}}}(S,M)
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a continuous homomorphism of complete,
separated right linear topological rings.
Then, as explained in Section
5
, there is
a functor of restriction of scalars
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
taking every left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
to its
underlying left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
is always exact and faithful, and preserves
infinite products.

The functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
has a left adjoint functor of

contraextension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\allowbreak\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
is right exact, preserves infinite direct

One can construct this functor using the rules that the free

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule spanned by any set

goes to the free

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule spanned by

, that is
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{R}[[X]])=\mathfrak{S}[[X]]

.

For any discrete right
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathcal{N}
and any left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}

[

(2)

\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}(\mathcal{N})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{P}).

As the functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}
is exact, it follows immediately that
the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
takes flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules to flat

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules.

Lemma 6.1
.

By Lemma

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a strongly right taut continuous map
of complete, separated two-sided linear topological rings with

Let
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{S}
be an open two-sided ideal such that

-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
and of its underlying

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}

-contramodule map

is an isomorphism.

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule is separated if and only if its
underlying left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is separated.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case of a separated

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
.
Following the proof of
Lemma
4.6
, we can
pick two descending chains
(\mathfrak{I}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\mathfrak{J}_{n})_{n\geq 1}

of open two-sided ideals in
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
satisfying the conditions
of Lemma
4.6
(2)
in such a way that
\mathfrak{I}_{1}=\mathfrak{I}
and
\mathfrak{J}_{1}=\mathfrak{J}
.
Denote by
R_{n}=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}
and
S_{n}=\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}_{n}
the related discrete
quotient rings.
Then we have a strongly right taut morphism of projective systems
of rings
(f_{n}\colon R_{n}\to S_{n})
such that
\mathfrak{f}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}f_{n}
.

Let
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the left
(S_{n})
-system corresponding to
the separated left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
under
the equivalence of categories from
Lemma
3.4
(b).
By Lemma
6.4
(b),
the underlying

-modules of the

-modules

form
a left
(R_{n})
-system
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.
By Lemma
6.5
(b),
the
(R_{n})
-system
(Q_{n}
) corresponds to the underlying separated

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule of
\mathfrak{Q}
under the same equivalence of

Lemma

(b).
Looking into the construction of the later equivalence of

We start with a simple lemma and an important proposition going
back to the paper

]

These should be understood in the context of

complete cotorsion pairs
in abelian categories;

\mathfrak{R}

10.
Change-of-Scalar Functors II

Let
f\colon R\longrightarrow S
be a continuous homomorphism of right linear
topological rings.
Then, following the discussion in
Section
6
, the exact, faithful functor
of restriction of scalars
f_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R
has
a right adjoint functor of coextension of scalars

f^{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}R\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}S
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a continuous homomorphism of complete,
separated right linear topological rings.
Then, according to Section
6
,
the exact, faithful functor of restriction of scalars

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
has a left adjoint functor
of contraextension of scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

We start with presenting simple counterexamples showing that
the adjoints on the other sides
need not

exist
[
21
, Section 2.9]
.

Example 10.1
.

Let
k
be a field,
\mathfrak{R}=k[x]
be the ring of polynomials in one
variable
x
over
k
endowed with the discrete topology, and

\mathfrak{S}=k[[x]]
be the ring of formal power series in
x
over
k
endowed
with the
x
-adic topology.
Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be the natural inclusion map.
So
\mathfrak{f}
is a continuous homomorphism of complete, separated
commutative linear topological rings.

The category of discrete
\mathfrak{S}
-modules is simply the category of

x
-torsion (i. e.,
x
-power torsion)
k[x]
-modules.
The category of
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules is the category of

k[x]
-modules with
x
-power infinite summation operation,
which is also a full subcategory in
k[x]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

(see
[
13
, Theorem B.1.1 or Lemma B.5.1]
,

[
15
, Section 1.6]
, or
[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Then the exact, fully faithful functor of restriction of scalars

\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}={\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{R}
does
not

have a left adjoint.
The point is that the functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}
does
not
preserve
countable products.
Indeed, the
k[x]
-modules
k[x]/(x^{n})
,
n\geq 1
, are
x
-torsion,
but their countable product
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}k[x]/(x^{n})
, computed
in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}k[x]
, is
not

x
-torsion.
The product of the
x
-torsion
k[x]
-modules
k[x]/(x^{n})
computed
in the category of
x
-torsion
k[x]
-modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}
, is
the submodule of all
x
-power torsion elements in

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}k[x]/(x^{n})
.

Dual-analogously, the exact, fully faithful functor of restriction of
scalars
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
does

not
have a right adjoint.
The point is that the functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
does
not
preserve
countable direct sums.
Once again, the
k[x]
-modules,
n\geq 1
, have
x
-power infinite
summation operations, but their countable direct sum

\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}k[x]/(x^{n})
, computed in
k[x]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
, does

not
have such an operation.
For a construction and discussion of the direct sum of the objects

k[x]/(x^{n})
computed in the category
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
of
k[x]
-modules
with
x
-power infinite summation operation,
see
[
15
, Section 1.5]
.
The direct sum
\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}k[x]/(x^{n})
computed in

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is a nonseparated
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule.

The countable direct sum
\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}k[x]/(x^{n})
also
exists in the category of separated
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}
,
and it is still
not
preserved by the fully faithful forgetful
functor
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Consequently, the latter functor does not have a right adjoint
functor, either.

One illuminating, relevant observation in connection with this series
of examples is that the continuous ring map
\mathfrak{f}
considered here
is
not
taut.

One aim of this section is to show that under certain assumptions
a left adjoint functor to
\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}
and a right adjoint functor
to
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}
do exist, nevertheless.
Another aim is to construct such adjoint functors explicitly.

Notice that, for any complete, separated right linear topological
ring
\mathfrak{S}
, the endomorphism ring of the free left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{S}
is naturally isomorphic to the opposite ring
\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{op}}
to
the ring
\mathfrak{S}
.
In other words, the right action of
\mathfrak{S}
on itself is an action by
left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule endomorphisms.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
be complete, separated right linear topological
rings with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero, and let

\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a taut continuous ring map.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module.
Then the right
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}
is discrete.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\bullet}\colon\mathcal{M}\longmapsto\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}
is left
adjoint to the functor of restriction of scalars

\mathfrak{f}_{\diamond}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}
.

Proof.

Every discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module is a quotient
\mathfrak{R}
-module of
a direct sum of cyclic discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
,
where
\mathfrak{I}
ranges over the open right ideal is
\mathfrak{R}
.
The full subcategory
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}\subset{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{S}
is closed under
quotients and infinite direct sums, and the functor
{-}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{S}
is right exact and preserves infinite
direct sums.
Thus, in order to show that the essential image of the latter functor
is contained in
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}
, it suffices to check that
the right
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{S}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S})
is
discrete for every open right ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.

Indeed, by Lemma
4.2
(1)
or
4.3
(2), the closure
\mathfrak{J}
of
the right ideal
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{S}
is an open right ideal
in
\mathfrak{S}
.
By Lemma
5.4
, we have

\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{S}
.
Thus
\mathfrak{S}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S})=\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}
is a discrete right
\mathfrak{S}
-module.

Furthermore, for every right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
M
and every right

\mathfrak{S}
-module
N
, we have an adjunction isomorphism of abelian groups

\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{op}}}(M\otimes_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S},\>N)\simeq\Hom_{\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{op}}}(M,N)
, which
induces a natural injective map
\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S},\>N)\longrightarrow\Hom_{\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{M},N)
for every discrete right

\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, by precomposing with the natural surjective
right
\mathfrak{S}
-module map
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}
.
We need to show that, for a discrete right
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\mathcal{N}
,
the resulting map of abelian groups

(12)

\Hom_{\mathfrak{S}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S},\>\mathcal{N})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom_{\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

is an isomorphism.

Indeed, every discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module is the cokernel of
a morphism of infinite direct sums of cyclic discrete right

\mathfrak{R}
-modules.
As both the left-hand side and the right-hand side
of (
12
) take colimits in
the discrete
\mathfrak{R}
-module argument
\mathcal{M}
to limits in
\mathsf{Ab}
,
it suffices to check that (
12
) is
an isomorphism for cyclic discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules
\mathcal{M}=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
.
In this case, in the notation above, we have
\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}
,
and the assertion that (
12
) is
an isomorphism simply means that an element
y\in\mathcal{N}
is annihilated
by
\mathfrak{J}
whenever it is annihilated by
\mathfrak{I}
.

Finally, if
y\mathfrak{I}=0
then
y\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\mathfrak{S}=0
in
\mathcal{N}
.
Let
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
be the annihilator of
y
in
\mathfrak{S}
.
Then
\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
is an open right ideal in
\mathfrak{S}
, and we have

\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{I})\mathfrak{S}\subset\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{J}^{\prime}
, so
y\mathfrak{J}=0
, as desired.
∎

We will call the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\bullet}\colon\mathcal{M}\longmapsto\mathcal{M}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{S}

the functor of
discrete module extension of scalars
.
The functor of discrete module extension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\bullet}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}
is well defined for any taut
continuous map of complete, separated right linear topological rings
with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero, as per
Proposition
10.2
.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\bullet}
agrees with the functor of extension
of scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{*}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{S}
whenever the forgetful

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
be complete, separated two-sided linear
topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero, and
let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a strongly right taut continuous ring map.
Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a separated left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
Then the left
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
carries a naturally
defined separated left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structure from which its
left
\mathfrak{S}
-module structure arises.
The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longmapsto\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
is right
adjoint to the functor of restriction of scalars
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}
from
Lemma
6.1
.
The adjunction counit
\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Id}
is the map

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P})\simeq\mathfrak{P}
induced by
the left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathfrak{I}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\mathfrak{J}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be two descending chains of
open two-sided ideals in
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{S}
provided by
Lemma
4.6
(2).
Denote by
R_{n}=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}
and
S_{n}=\mathfrak{S}/\mathfrak{J}_{n}
the related discrete
quotient rings.
Then we have a strongly right taut morphism of projective systems
of rings
(f_{n}\colon R_{n}\to S_{n})
such that
\mathfrak{f}=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}f_{n}
,
as in Section
6
.

Let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the left
(R_{n})
-system of modules
corresponding to the separated
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
.
So we have
\mathfrak{P}\simeq\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}P_{n}
, where the projective
limit is taken in the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
, or equivalently, in
its full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}
.
Hence
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})\simeq\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},P_{n})
.
We compute

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},P_{n})\simeq\Hom_{R_{n}}(\mathfrak{S}/(\mathfrak{I}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S}),\>P_{n})\simeq\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n}),

as
\mathfrak{I}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{J}_{n}
by
Lemma
5.4
.
Now
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})\simeq\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n})

is a projective limit of left
S_{n}
-modules.
All left
S_{n}
-modules are separated left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodules, and
the inclusion/forgetful functors
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
preserve all limits (all limits exist in all the three
categories).
Thus
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
is naturally a left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule.

We have constructed the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
; it remains to establish
the adjunction.
For any left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
, we have

\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}))\simeq\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n})).

We compute

\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n}))\simeq\Hom_{S_{n}}(\mathfrak{Q}/(\mathfrak{J}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{Q}),\>\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n}))\\
\simeq\Hom_{R_{n}}(\mathfrak{Q}/(\mathfrak{J}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{Q}),\>P_{n})\simeq\Hom_{R_{n}}(\mathfrak{Q}/(\mathfrak{I}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{Q}),\>P_{n})\simeq\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},P_{n}),

as
\mathfrak{J}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{I}_{n}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{Q}
by
Corollary
6.6
.
Passing to the projective limit, we finally obtain the isomorphisms

\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom_{R_{n}}(S_{n},P_{n}))\simeq\varprojlim\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},P_{n})\simeq\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P}),

so
\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}(\mathfrak{P}))\simeq\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P})
, as desired.
The description of the adjunction counit follows straightforwardly
from the constructions.
∎

We will call the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longmapsto\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})

separated contramodule coextension of scalars

The functor of separated contramodule coextension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Separ}}}

right taut continuous map of complete, separated right linear
topological rings with countable bases of neighborhoods of zero, as per

The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
agrees with the functor of coextension
of scalars
\mathfrak{f}^{!}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
whenever the forgetful

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{P}

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{S}

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})

\mathfrak{S}

\mathfrak{S}

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longmapsto\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})

\mathfrak{f}_{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{S}
-modules

0\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{C})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{F})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow 0
is exact (by the definition of
a cotorsion left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule).

The flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is separated by
Lemma
3.1
(c), hence
the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{C}
is separated, too.
By
Proposition
10.3
,
it follows that both the
\mathfrak{S}
-modules

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{C})
and
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{F})
carry natural separated left

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structures, and the map
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{C})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{F})
is an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule morphism.
The forgetful functor
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
preserves cokernels.
We endow
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})
with the left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
structure of the cokernel of the
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule morphism

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{C})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{F})
.

Now let
\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime}
be a morphism of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
We need to check that the induced map
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime})
is an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule morphism.
Let
0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0
and

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0
be two short exact
sequences of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules with cotorsion

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime}
and flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}
.

Consider the composition
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
\Ext^{\mathfrak{R},1}(\mathfrak{F}^{\prime},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime})=0
, the morphism
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime}

lifts to an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence we obtain a morphism of short exact sequences of left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
(0\to\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}\to\mathfrak{P}^{\prime}\to 0)\longrightarrow(0\to\mathfrak{C}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime}\to 0)
.
The assertion that the map
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}^{\prime\prime})
is an
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule morphism now follows from
the construction above of the
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule structures involved.

We have constructed the promised functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}
; it remains to
establish the adjunction.
It follows from
Proposition
10.3

and the construction above that the desired adjunction counit

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P})\longrightarrow\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P})\simeq\mathfrak{P}
is a left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism.
For any left
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
, we have the induced map of
abelian groups

(13)

\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}))\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P}).

Viewed as functors of the
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule argument
\mathfrak{Q}
, both
the left-hand side and the right-hand side of (
13
)
take cokernels of morphisms in
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
to kernels of morphisms
of abelian groups.
Hence it suffices to consider the case of a projective left

\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
.
Then the underlying left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule of
\mathfrak{Q}
is flat by
Corollary
6.7
.

Hence, in the notation above, we have short exact sequences of abelian
groups

(14)

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{C}))\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{F}))\\
\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathfrak{Q},\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{S},\mathfrak{P}))\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0

and

(15)

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{C})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{F})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu0.

The natural transformation (
13
) provides
a morphism from the short exact
sequence (
14
) to the short
exact sequence (
15
),
which is an isomorphism on the leftmost and middle terms by
Proposition
10.3
.
Hence this map is also an isomorphism on the rightmost terms of

)
and (

∎

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated commutative linear topological
ring, and let
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{Q}
be
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
Then the abelian group of
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphisms

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P})
has a natural
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure.

Proof.

The
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure on
\mathfrak{H}=\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P})
is simply
induced by the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure on
\mathfrak{P}
.
The contramodule infinite summation operations in
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q},\mathfrak{P})

are constructed as a kind of pointwise summation of
\mathfrak{P}
-valued
functions.
More precisely,

\pi_{\mathfrak{H}}\left(\sum\nolimits_{h\in\mathfrak{H}}r_{h}h\right)(q)=\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}\left(\sum\nolimits_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}\left(\sum\nolimits_{h\in\mathfrak{H}}^{h(q)=p}r_{h}\right)p\right).

Here
(r_{h}\in\mathfrak{R})_{h\in\mathfrak{H}}
is a zero-convergent family of elements
of
\mathfrak{R}
indexed by elements of
\mathfrak{H}
.
The second (inner) summation sign in the right-hand side denotes
the topological limit of finite partial sums in the topology of
\mathfrak{R}
,
while the first (outer) summation sign is the notation for an element
of
\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
to which the contraction map
\pi_{\mathfrak{P}}
is applied.
That element of
\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
is obtained by applying the map

\mathfrak{R}[[g_{q}]]\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{H}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
to the element

\sum_{h\in\mathfrak{H}}r_{h}h\in\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{H}]]
, where
g_{q}\colon\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
is
the map of sets given by the rule
g_{q}(h)=h(q)
.
∎

Remark 10.6
.

10.3
,

Remark 10.7
.

Given a homomorphism of abstract (nontopological) rings

f\colon R\longrightarrow S
, the functor of extension of scalars
f^{*}\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is exact whenever
S
is a flat right
R
-module.
For an arbitrary ring homomorphism
f
, the functor
f^{*}
is exact
on the full subcategory of flat
R
-modules in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
The functor of coextension of scalars
f^{!}\colon R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}

is exact on the full exact subcategory of cotorsion
R
-modules
in
R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
provided that
S
is flat left
R
-module.

What exactness properties do the discrete module and contramodule
functors of extension/coextension/contraextension of scalars have,
for a morphism of complete, separated right linear topological rings

\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
?

The functor of discrete module extension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\bullet}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{S}
from
Proposition
10.2
is exact
whenever
\mathfrak{f}
is a taut continuous ring map and
\mathfrak{S}
is a flat left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule (by construction).
By Corollary
5.5
, these
assumptions hold, in particular, whenever
\mathfrak{f}
is a left proflat
continuous ring map.

The functor of contramodule coextension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\text{\textexclamdown}}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
from
Theorem
10.4
is exact on
the exact category of cotorsion left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules (by
construction) whenever
\mathfrak{f}
is a left proflat continuous ring map.
The functor of contraextension of scalars

\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is always exact
on the exact category of flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules, by
Lemma
6.2
.

The analogy with the module case may suggest that
the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
should be exact on the whole abelian category
of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules under suitable flatness assumptions
on
\mathfrak{f}
.
The following counterexample appears to refute such naïve
expectations decisively.

Example 10.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be the proflat proepimorphism of Noetherian
commutative rings with adic topologies discussed in
Example
5.3

and Remark
9.7
.
So we have
\mathfrak{R}=k[x][[y]]
and
\mathfrak{S}=k[x,x^{-1}][[y]]
.
Since
\mathfrak{S}
is a flat
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule and
\mathfrak{R}
is a Noetherian
commutative ring with adic topology,
\mathfrak{S}
is actually a flat

\mathfrak{R}
-module by
[
13
, Lemma B.9.2]
,

[
17
, Corollary 10.3(a)]
, or
[
36
, Theorem 1.6(2) or 6.11]
.
Nevertheless, the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is
not
exact.

For a counterexample, consider the following module
\mathfrak{Q}
over the ring
of formal power series
k[[x,y]]
.
Put
\mathfrak{Q}=\prod_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}^{i+j\geq 0}kx^{i}y^{j}
.
Here
kx^{i}y^{j}
is a one-dimensional
k
-vector space with the basis
vector denoted formally by
x^{i}y^{j}
, and the infinite direct product
is taken over the integer half-plane
\{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}\mid i+j\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
The ring of formal power series
k[[x,y]]
acts in
\mathfrak{Q}
in the obvious
way.
In fact, the
[x,y]
-power infinite summation operations, in
the sense of
[
17
, Section 4]
, are easily defined on
\mathfrak{Q}
,
making
\mathfrak{Q}
a contramodule over the topological ring
k[[x,y]]

(endowed with the adic topology of the ideal
(x,y)\subset k[[x,y]]
),
cf.
[
13
, Theorem B.1.1 and Section B.6]
.
The restriction of scalars with respect to the continuous identity
inclusion map of rings
\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow k[[x,y]]
endows
\mathfrak{Q}
with
an
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure.

Put
R=k[x,y]
and
S=k[x,x^{-1},y]
; so
\mathfrak{R}
is the
y
-adic
completion of
R
and
\mathfrak{S}
is the
y
-adic completion of
S
.
Then the identity inclusions of rings
R\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}
and

S\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}
induce fully faithful forgetful functors

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
and
\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow R{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
,
by
[
13
, Theorem B.1.1]
(cf.
[
15
, Section 1.6]
).
Denote by
\Delta_{y}=\mathbb{L}_{0}\Lambda_{y}\colon S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

the left adjoint functor to the forgetful/inclusion functor

\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow S{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
A construction of the functor
\Delta_{y}
is spelled out
in
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
or
[
17
, Section 6]
, while
a discussion of the functor
\mathbb{L}_{0}\Lambda_{y}
and its comparison
with
\Delta_{y}
can be found in
[
22
, Section 1 and Remark 3.8]
.

The functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
can be
computed by the rule
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{P})=\Delta_{y}(S\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{P})
for
any
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
.
In particular, for the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
, we observe
that a pair of mutually inverse automorphisms
\frac{x}{y}
and
\frac{y}{x}

is defined on
\mathfrak{Q}
.
Hence the element
y\in S
acts by an invertible map on
the
S
-module
S\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{Q}=\mathfrak{Q}[x^{-1}]
.
In other words, inverting the action of
x
on
\mathfrak{Q}
also makes
the action of
y
invertible.
One has
\Hom_{S}(M,\mathfrak{P})=0
for any
y
-divisible
S
-module
M
and
any
\mathfrak{S}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
(see
[
13
, Theorem B.1.1]

or
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
); so
\Delta_{y}(M)=0
.
Thus, in particular,
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{Q})=\Delta_{y}(S\otimes_{R}\mathfrak{Q})=0
.

On the other hand, there is an obvious injective
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
map
g\colon\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Q}
from the free
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{R}
to
the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{Q}
, given by the rule
g(1)=x^{0}y^{0}
.
Furthermore, we have
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(\mathfrak{R})=\mathfrak{S}\neq 0
.
So
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}(g)\colon\mathfrak{S}\longrightarrow 0
is
not
an injective map,
and the functor
\mathfrak{f}^{\sharp}
is not exact.
