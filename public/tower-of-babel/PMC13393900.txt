Evaluation of equity and efficiency in health resource allocation in underdeveloped county areas: empirical research from Guangxi, China
Authors: Rong Peng, Meng Deng, Zhen Li, Jing Kang, Junyuan Wei, Nan Jiang, Xianjing Qin, Tingting Xu, Jun Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aims to analyze the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped county areas, providing a reference for improving health resource policies in such regions.MethodsFocusing on eight underdeveloped counties in Guangxi, China, this research evaluates the fairness of health resource distribution using the Gini coefficient and Lorenz curve, and assesses allocation efficiency through the Data Envelopment Analysis model.ResultsIn terms of equity in health resource allocation from 2015 to 2021, the Gini coefficients for health resources per capita were between 0.07 and 0.23, indicating the highest level of equity; the fairness in the distribution of healthcare human resources was lower than that of medical institutions and hospital beds, with particularly low equity observed in the distribution of licensed (assistant) physicians. Regarding efficiency, the overall efficiency of health resource allocation averaged between 0.899 and 0.933, with the number of counties achieving efficiency fluctuating between three and five. There were inefficiencies observed, particularly in technical and scale efficiency, with low scale efficiency being a significant factor affecting the efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties.ConclusionThe distribution of health resources based on population and GDP demonstrates greater equity compared to distribution based on geographic areas. The equity of institutions and beds is superior to that of healthcare human resources. Overall, the efficiency of health resource allocation is not high, and there are regional disparities in efficiency. Recommendations include increasing the focus on health resource allocation in underdeveloped areas, optimizing the management of healthcare human resources, and coordinating planning to improve the precision and rationality of resource investment, thereby enhancing the efficiency of health resource utilization.Keywords: Health resource allocation, Equity, Efficiency, Under developed county, Data envelopment analysis

Background

Optimal allocation of healthcare resources is crucial for ensuring access to basic medical and health services and for meeting people’s health needs [1, 2]. Equity and efficiency in the allocation of medical resources are important objectives of public health management and are also fundamental principles advocated by the World Health Organization [3]. As China enters a new era of socio-economic development, the primary contradiction in society has transformed into one between the people’s ever-growing needs for a better life and unbalanced and inadequate development [4]. One of the main challenges faced by the uneven and inadequate development of China’s health sector is structural contradictions. These structural contradictions are primarily manifested in three ways: firstly, a shortage of resources, especially a lack of high-quality supplies [5]; secondly, uneven distribution across urban and rural areas, regions, and populations [6–8]; and thirdly, a structural imbalance, with the allocation of medical resources resembling an ‘inverted triangle‘ [2, 9]. To address these issues of uneven and insufficient development in the health sector, optimizing the equity of health resource allocation and improving the efficiency of the health system are key areas of reform. Currently, the people’s demand for multi-level and diversified medical and healthcare services continues to grow rapidly. Accelerating the improvement of the quality of health supply and enhancing the fairness and efficiency of health resource allocation are the requirements for adapting to the changes in the principal contradiction facing Chinese society and meeting the people’s needs for a better life. At the same time, good health and well-being and the reduction of inequalities are important elements in the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). The evaluation of the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in less developed regions is directly related to the ability of the residents of these regions to receive adequate and quality healthcare services. Equitable allocation of health resources enables more people to enjoy medical services and improve their health, thus achieving high-quality universal health coverage (SDG target 3.8) [10] and also reduces interregional disparities in health levels and accessibility of medical services and promotes social equity (SDG target 10.3) [11].

Counties serve as the fundamental units for socio-economic management and development in China, covering about 90% of the country’s total land area [12, 13]. The permanent population in these counties is approximately 740 million, accounting for over 50% of China’s total population. As China strives to build a moderately prosperous society in all respects and implements the rural revitalization strategy and the construction of a healthy China, the role of counties becomes increasingly important. The current reform of rural medical and health services in China focuses on deepening comprehensive medical reforms at the county level and advancing the construction of closely integrated medical communities. Optimizing the allocation of health resources at the county level is crucial for meeting the growing medical and health service needs of the grassroots population, and for implementing a tiered diagnosis and treatment system [14]. However, current research on health resource allocation in China is mainly concentrated at the national [15, 16], provincial [1, 17, 18], and municipal [19] levels, with limited literature on county-level health resource allocation. Moreover, existing studies on county-level health resource allocation tend to focus on the national context [20, 21], with scant research specifically addressing the allocation of health resources in underdeveloped county areas. Some scholars believe that optimizing the allocation of medical resources and achieving balanced development of services are the key challenges faced by underdeveloped counties and rural areas [22]. Additionally, some scholars also argue that the efficiency of health resource utilization, the prices of medical services, and the structure of health personnel have significant impacts on the hospital performance in underdeveloped counties [23].

Research on developed countries, particularly in Europe, has revealed the existence of a social gradient in health outcomes, where higher socioeconomic status correlates with better health. Consequently, it is crucial to focus on individuals at the lower end of the social gradient and the most vulnerable populations [24]. Specifically, after the implementation of the UK strategy in England, scholars compared the trends in health inequality with those in three countries—Finland, the Netherlands, and Italy. They found that while England has undertaken significant efforts to reduce health inequalities, the changes in health inequality trends were not statistically significant compared to those in the other countries [25]. Germany’s healthcare system is renowned for its efficiency and equity, with substantial investments in health [26]. In Spain, the national health system has long been recognized for its universal accessibility and provision of free services, achieving favorable health outcomes. In recent years, the Spanish government has made efforts to achieve the equity and efficiency of the healthcare system through policy adjustments and resource redistribution [27]. According to World Health Organization data in 2024 [28], the number of physicians and nurses/midwives in China (25.2/10,000 and 35.2/10,000) is lower than that of European countries such as the United Kingdom (31.7/10,000 and 91.5/10,000), France (33.4/10,000 and 93.8/10,000), and Spain (44.8/10,000 and 65.4/10,000). Meanwhile, it is also lower than that of developed countries such as the United States (36.1/10,000 and 118.8/10,000) and Japan (26.1/10,000 and 124.5/10,000). However, it is higher than that of African countries such as South Africa (8.1/10,000 and 10.4/10,000) and Egypt (6.8/10,000 and 18.3/10,000). Guangxi, as a relatively resource-scarce region with a concentrated ethnic minority population in the west, suffers from insufficient medical resources due to historical reasons and economic constraints. In 2021, the number of physicians in Guangxi was 21.7/10,000, and in some underdeveloped counties, it was only 14.9/10,000. Additionally, primary healthcare institutions generally face issues such as weak service capabilities, poor infrastructure, and severe shortages of personnel, equipment, and technical resources [29]. To address the deficiencies in the primary medical and health service system, Guangxi has implemented plans for the medical and health service system, adhering to the principles of ‘ensuring basic services, promoting standards, filling gaps, and strengthening foundations.’ The focus is on county-level areas, where efforts are made to build the capacity of medical and health institutions, overcoming challenges such as an underdeveloped economy, scarce health resources, weak infrastructure, and heavy debt burdens. Therefore, the quantity, quality, and structure of health resource allocation in the underdeveloped counties of Guangxi, and whether these resources can be allocated fairly, accessibly, and efficiently, warrant in-depth analysis and research.

Therefore, this study aims to analyze the health resource allocation in the counties of Guangxi using tools for equity and efficiency analysis. It will assess the fairness and efficiency of health resource allocation, identify factors hindering the development of medical and health affairs in underdeveloped counties, and explore strategies and approaches to optimize the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in these areas. This will help to continuously improve the primary medical and health service system in Guangxi, enhance the service capabilities and levels of medical and health institutions in underdeveloped counties, and ensure that the basic medical and health system as a public good is provided to all. Besides, this study aims to promote the equalization of basic public health services, gradually reduce disparities in basic health services and health levels between urban and rural areas, regions, and populations, and accelerate the realization of universal health coverage.

Materials and methods

Data sources

In 2015, China issued the “Outline of the National Medical and Health Service System Development Plan (2015–2020)” aimed at further optimizing the allocation of medical and health resources in China, enhancing service accessibility, capacity, and resource utilization efficiency. In the same year, China also issued the “Opinions on Fully Implementing the Comprehensive Reform of County-level Public Hospitals,” which proposed to comprehensively implement the reform and optimize the allocation of medical resources in counties. Therefore, this study takes 2015 as the starting year and collects data from the latest available year, which is 2021. The eight underdeveloped counties in Guangxi selected for this study are located in western China with a concentrated ethnic minority population. These counties are geographically remote from cities and have a relatively low per capita disposable income of residents, making them areas with high poverty incidence. By studying these underdeveloped counties, we can gain a detailed understanding of the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped regions.

In the analysis of health resource equity, this study used five indicators: the number of healthcare institutions, beds, health technicians, licensed (assistant) physicians, and registered nurses in healthcare institutions at the county level. For the efficiency analysis, indicators such as the number of beds, health technicians in healthcare institutions at the county level, and the number of visits and inpatient in healthcare institutions at the county level were used. The research data were sourced from the “Guangxi Statistical Yearbook,” “Guangxi Health and Family Planning Statistics Digest,” and “Guangxi Health and Wellness Statistical Yearbook” over the years.

Research methods

Lorenz curve

The Lorenz curve was first introduced by the American economist and statistician M.O. Lorenz in the early 20th century [30]. In health economics research, the Lorenz curve is used to evaluate the equity of health resource distribution and can visually reflect the average level of a specific health indicator [31]. Health resources per capita or per unit area in different regions are arranged in ascending order, with the cumulative percentage of the population or area as the horizontal axis, and the cumulative percentage of health resources as the vertical axis, forming what is known as the Lorenz curve. The line of absolute equality is shown as a diagonal line; when resource allocation is absolutely fair, the two lines coincide. The smaller the curvature of the curve below the diagonal, the greater the equity of the health resource allocation [32, 33].

Gini coefficient

To address the Lorenz curve’s insensitivity to precise quantification of equity, Italian statistician Corrado Gini introduced the Gini coefficient, based on the Lorenz curve [34]. This coefficient represents the ratio of the area enclosed by the actual Lorenz curve and the line of absolute equality to the area between the line of absolute equality and the line of absolute inequality [35]. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 to 1, where a Gini coefficient < 0.2 indicates absolute equity, 0.2–0.3 indicates comparative equity, 0.3–0.4 indicates relative equity, 0.4–0.5 indicates comparative inequity and > 0.5 indicates significant inequity [36, 37].

Data envelopment analysis method

(1) Data envelopment analysis model

Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) is a widely used classic method for evaluating technical efficiency, first introduced by American operations researchers Charnes A., Cooper W.W., and Rhodes E. in 1978. It has since rapidly developed in both the business sector and academia for evaluating economic operational efficiency [38]. DEA constructs a linear programming model corresponding to the data to analyze actual inputs and outputs of the healthcare system and compares the relative efficiency by determining whether each decision-making unit is on the production frontier [39, 40]. The DEA model is particularly suited for complex systems with multiple inputs and outputs, with its most prominent advantage being its greater flexibility and fewer restrictive assumptions [41]. In DEA, the weights of input and output indicators for each decision-making unit (DMU) are not determined subjectively by the evaluator but are the optimal weights derived from the actual data of the DMUs. Therefore, DEA can handle multiple inputs and outputs and assign efficiency scores to each DMU.

Over time, DEA has evolved to include thousands of evaluation models, among which the CCR and BCC models are two classic models [42]. The CCR model assumes constant returns to scale (CRS) and measures overall technical efficiency without separating scale effects. The BCC model, an improvement upon the CCR model, is applied in situations where DMUs operate under variable returns to scale, and it is used to measure technical efficiency, pure technical efficiency, and scale efficiency. This means that the BCC model can more comprehensively assess the efficiency of DMUs and reveal different sources of efficiency [43]. DEA models can be classified into input-oriented and output-oriented models. The input-oriented model measures the degree of inefficiency of the evaluated DMU from the input perspective, focusing on the extent to which various inputs should be reduced to achieve technical efficiency without reducing output. The output-oriented model measures the degree of inefficiency of the evaluated DMU from the output perspective, focusing on the extent to which various outputs should be increased to achieve technical efficiency without increasing input [44]. Because this study aims to maximize output given constant healthcare input, and because, according to the theory of health production, the production technology of the healthcare system exhibits variable returns to scale, the output-oriented BCC model was selected to measure the comprehensive technical efficiency, pure technical efficiency, and scale efficiency of health resource allocation in eight underdeveloped counties in Guangxi.

In this study, DEAP 2.1 software was used for data envelopment analysis of health resources.

(2) Selection of input and output indicators

The selection of input and output indicators for DEA should adhere to the principles of representativeness, availability, relevance, and comparability. According to the conditions for the use of the DEA model and the availability of data and materials, the input indicators for the efficiency analysis were finally determined to be the number of health technicians [45] and the number of beds [46, 47]. The output indicators were set as the number of visits [48] and the number of inpatients [49]. Moreover, it is generally believed that the number of DMUs should not be less than twice the sum of the number of input and output indicators [50–53]. In this study, two input indicators, two output indicators, and eight DMUs were selected, meeting the requirements of the quantitative relationship between indicators and DMUs in the DEA method.

Results

Socio-economic development of counties

In 2021, the eight counties exhibited relatively low levels of economic development, with their GDPs all falling below the average for Guangxi’s counties, and there were significant development disparities among the counties. County PX had the highest GDP per capita, amounting to RMB 65,242, while County NP had the lowest, at RMB 27,612. Among these eight counties, County DX recorded the highest disposable income for residents, which was RMB 39,855, while County NP had the lowest, at RMB 16,130. County DX also recorded the highest urbanization rate, which was 72.86%, whereas County NP had the lowest, at 25.48%. In terms of health resource allocation, County FC had the highest number of health technicians, which was 91.0 per 10,000 people, while County BX had the lowest, at 48.70 per 10,000 people. County JX had the highest number of hospital beds, which was 65.99 per 10,000 people, while County BX had the lowest, at 23.34 per 10,000 people. See Table 1.

Equity of health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties of Guangxi based on the Gini coefficient

From 2015 to 2021, compared to geographic area and economic dimensions, the Gini coefficients for various health resources were lowest in the population dimension. In terms of population, except for the Gini coefficient for licensed (assistant) physicians which was 0.23 in 2015, the Gini coefficient for all health resources from 2016 to 2021 was less than 0.2, indicating a state of absolute equity in health resource allocation, with the allocation of medical and health institutions being the most equitable. In terms of geographic area, the Gini coefficient for the number of medical and health institutions from 2015 to 2021 was less than 0.2, indicating a state of absolute equity; the Gini coefficient for the number of health technicians, licensed (assistant) physicians, and registered nurses were between 0.2 and 0.3, indicating a state of comparative equity. The Gini coefficient for the number of beds was between 0.2 and 0.3 in 2015 and 2016, and less than 0.2 from 2017 to 2021. In terms of the economic dimension, except for a few years where the Gini coefficient was greater than 0.2, the Gini coefficients for medical and health resources in most other years were less than 0.2, indicating a state of absolute equity.

In a longitudinal comparison over time, in the population dimension, various health resources have either moved toward or remained at absolute equity. In the geographic area dimension, medical and health institutions have maintained a state of absolute equity, the number of beds has shifted from comparative to absolute equity, and the numbers of health technicians, licensed (assistant) physicians, and registered nurses have maintained a state of comparative equity. The Gini coefficients for health resource indicators in the economic dimension have shown considerable fluctuation, with the number of healthcare institutions, beds, health technicians, and licensed (assistant) physicians transitioning between states of absolute and comparative equity, while the number of registered nurses has maintained a state of absolute equity. Efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties of Guangxi based on DEA

Comprehensive efficiency of health resource allocation

From 2015 to 2021, the average comprehensive efficiency of health resource allocation in the eight underdeveloped counties of Guangxi ranged from 0.899 to 0.933. Among them, County NP and County PX maintained a comprehensive efficiency of 1 throughout the seven years, indicating that the input-output of medical and health resources in these two counties was quite reasonable, with high allocation efficiency and effective hospital management. The comprehensive efficiency of County FC and County BX was consistently less than 1 over the seven years, with County BX in particular only achieving a comprehensive efficiency between 0.699 and 0.773. This low-efficiency value indicates that the input and output of health resources have not reached a relatively optimal state, showing that compared to other efficient counties, the health resource allocation in County BX only operated at 69.90–77.30% efficiency. Compared to 2015, by 2021, there was an increase of one county achieving comprehensive efficiency, and the average comprehensive efficiency increased by 0.021. See Table 3.

Discussion

Equity of health resource allocation based on population and GDP is superior to geographic area

According to the results calculated by the Gini coefficient, the equity of health resources in the eight underdeveloped counties of Guangxi, whether distributed by geographic area, population, or economic dimensions, was in a state of absolute or relative equity. This indicates that since 2015, especially from the 13th to the early 14th Five-Year Plan periods, Guangxi has centered on people’s health, promoted the equalization of basic public health services, and essentially achieved universal health coverage, which has effectively enhanced the fairness and accessibility of health resource allocation. However, it was noteworthy that while the equity of health resource allocation based on population and GDP continues to improve, there was a declining trend in equity based on geographical area. The problem of unequal geographical distribution of health resource allocation is also severe in developing countries such as Cameroon [54]. The reason for this disparity was mainly due to health administrative departments traditionally using population as the standard for allocating health human resources while overlooking the impact of geographic area on the accessibility of health services [55, 56]. Particularly in sparsely populated underdeveloped minority mountainous areas like County NP, County LZ, and County NM, if the standard metrics such as beds per thousand population and health personnel possession continue to be used without considering geographic environment and service radius, it can lead to the dilemma of health personnel serving too large an area under unique mountainous conditions. This affects the accessibility of high-quality, equitable medical and health services for residents in remote and backward areas, leading to disparities in health conditions and ultimately contributing to social injustice. This is similar to the research findings of scholars such as Wang [57] and Wei [58].

Moreover, as a southwestern border province in China, some counties in Guangxi have increasingly active trade and personnel exchanges with ASEAN countries [59]. Merely considering the current permanent population as the main factor for allocating health resources is insufficient to meet the demands for infectious disease prevention and control, and medical and health resource needs. For some underdeveloped border counties, it is also advisable to consider the actual circumstances of the border areas, which include large geographic areas with low population density, active border trade and tourism, and high personnel mobility. Factors such as border trade turnover, number of border trade personnel, and number of cross-border tourists should be included as important indicators for the allocation of health resources, to guide the distribution of health resources in border areas with a more scientific and rational indicator system.

Equity in the allocation of institutions and beds is superior to that of healthcare human resources

According to the Gini coefficient and Lorenz curve results, the fairness in the allocation of medical and health institutions and beds in the eight counties is superior to that of healthcare human resources, with the fairness of licensed (assistant) physicians being relatively poor. This may be due to the higher mobility of healthcare personnel, especially in underdeveloped remote areas, where the problem of a lack of successors in primary healthcare teams is common. This is similar to the research findings of scholars such as Yang [60] and Liu [61]. Key primary healthcare talents frequently seek transfers to higher-level medical institutions, and the loss of grassroots healthcare personnel weakens the basic medical functions of grassroots medical institutions. The basic health service needs of the public are difficult to meet, resulting in a vicious cycle of “talent shortage - functional contraction - massive personnel loss - exacerbated talent shortage”, which becomes a ‘bottleneck’ hindering the development of grassroots medical institutions and a constraint on the smooth operation of the tiered diagnosis and treatment system [62]. However, following the COVID-19 pandemic, we should focus on health equity and the health issues of vulnerable groups worldwide [63]. At the same time, we should pay even greater attention to the equity of health human resources [64], as only with adequate health human resources can the healthcare system provide good medical services.

For health resources such as the number of beds in underdeveloped areas, solutions like increasing financial investments and optimizing bed layouts can quickly address these issues. However, strengthening the construction and allocation of grassroots personnel is more challenging due to the long training periods and high mobility of personnel, making human resource provisioning much more difficult than hardware investment.

Firstly, it is recommended to accelerate the reform of county-level medical communities focused on primary care, implementing ‘county hires for township use’ and in villages ‘township hires for village use. Secondly, there should be an enhancement in the staffing of primary healthcare institutions, particularly in the allocation of general practitioners, nurses, and other healthcare technicians, to improve the efficiency of staffing quotas. Township health centers should operate under a mechanism that combines ‘specified tasks, budgeted revenues and expenditures, performance assessment subsidies’ with ‘public welfare type one protection and specialized performance incentives.’ Thirdly, it is suggested to strengthen the construction of rural doctor teams, improve their income levels, optimize the income structure of primary healthcare personnel, and increase job satisfaction. Under national policy guidance, adopting salary standards for township health technicians, a reasonable remuneration system should be established for primary healthcare institutions. Additionally, the policy for on-the-job rural doctors to participate in basic pension insurance should be implemented to improve the income and welfare of grassroots medical staff. Last but not least, the continuing education system for staff in primary healthcare institutions should be improved, increasing training and learning opportunities for grassroots doctors to ensure their enthusiasm for learning and reduce professional burnout [65].

Low overall efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties of Guangxi with regional disparities

According to DEA analysis results, from 2015 to 2021, the number of counties in Guangxi with effective comprehensive efficiency in health resource allocation fluctuated between three and five, indicating a prevalence of low efficiency. From a breakdown of efficiency, both technical efficiency and scale efficiency were insufficient, with scale efficiency deficiencies being a significant factor affecting the low efficiency of health resource allocation in these eight counties. Counties with ineffective scale efficiency experienced decreasing returns to scale, indicating that the scale of medical and health resource inputs in these counties was not effectively utilized, with issues such as underutilization of beds and other idle resources.

Comparatively, County BX has the lowest health resource allocation efficiency among the eight counties and is the only one without a Traditional Chinese Medicine hospital. It is also the only county where the number of medical and health institution beds per thousand population is declining. In 2021, the number of beds per thousand population in County BX was 2.33, only 36.8% of the regional average of 6.33 and 34.7% of the national average of 6.70. In the same year, the number of healthcare personnel per thousand population in County BX was 4.87, only 73% of the national average of 6.70 and 62% of the Guangxi average of 7.82. This highlights the insufficiency of medical resources in County BX.

At the same time, the bed utilization rate and the average daily burden of beds per doctor in County BX are among the lowest in the eight counties. The volume of medical consultations shows negative growth, indicating that existing resources are not being fully utilized. The primary reason is that the medical level in County BX is not high enough; however, due to convenient transportation, patients who cannot receive adequate treatment locally often opt to seek better hospitals elsewhere. The decrease in local treatment of complex cases prevents the improvement of medical specialty levels, further exacerbating the vicious cycle of ‘insufficiency of capabilities—lack of trust—seeking treatment elsewhere—lack of improvement leading to further insufficiency of capabilities.’ This is similar to the research findings of scholars such as Wang [66].

To meet the medical service needs of the population in underdeveloped counties, it is essential to plan the scale of health resource investment reasonably while simultaneously strengthening the comprehensive service capabilities of medical and health facilities in these areas, enhancing the efficiency of health resource utilization. On one hand, there should be coordinated planning to improve the precision and rationality of resource investment [67], optimize the equitable distribution of medical and health resources, increase the supply of high-quality medical resources, and promote the decentralization of medical resources from large cities to counties and grassroots levels [68, 69]. This will enhance the spatial supply and service levels of medical and health resources and reduce patient outflow. On the other hand, the introduction of new equipment, new technologies, and new concepts should be considered to improve resource utilization rates [70]. Efforts should be made to accelerate the development of tightly integrated medical communities at the county level, strengthen the construction of clinical specialties in county-level public hospitals, continuously enhance the comprehensive capabilities of county hospitals, and effectively implement the leading role of county hospitals in the county medical service system and their bridging function in the urban-rural medical service system. This will further narrow the gap in medical service levels between urban and rural areas, allowing the population in underdeveloped areas to have nearby access to basic medical and health services [71, 72].

Conclusion

This study collected data on health resources from underdeveloped counties in Guangxi, China, from 2015 to 2021 and utilized tools such as the Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient for analyzing equity from demographic, geographic, and economic perspectives. The allocation equity of health resources from 2015 to 2021 was evaluated; additionally, the efficiency of health resource allocation in these counties was analyzed using the DEA model. The findings indicate that the health resources in the eight counties, whether allocated based on geographic area, population distribution, or economic dimensions, were in a state of either absolute or relative equity. However, a declining trend in the equity of some health resources was observed. Some counties exhibited low efficiency in health resource allocation, with both technical efficiency and scale efficiency being insufficient. In particular, insufficient scale efficiency was a significant factor affecting the low efficiency of health resource allocation in these eight underdeveloped counties.

To address the health resource allocation issues in these eight underdeveloped counties of Guangxi, it is essential to increase focus on the allocation of health resources, with particular attention to the provision of health human resources in underdeveloped areas, to promote fair and accessible distribution of health resources. Additionally, improvements in the medical and health service capabilities and efficiency in these counties can be achieved through the construction of medical information systems and the development of medical alliances and communities.

Unique features and possible innovations

While there is abundant research on health resource allocation at the provincial (autonomous region) and city levels, literature that simultaneously studies the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation at the county level is limited, particularly concerning underdeveloped minority areas. This study analyzes the health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties in Guangxi, which not only helps improve the medical and health service capabilities and service levels in these counties but also promotes the equalization of basic public health services across health domains. This approach gradually reduces the disparities in basic health services and health levels between urban and rural areas, regions, and populations, accelerating the achievement of universal health coverage.

Study limitations

Due to the availability of data, this study only analyzed the equity and static efficiency of health resource allocation in eight underdeveloped counties in Guangxi from 2015 to 2021, without the ability to conduct a longer time series study and lacking an analysis of the dynamic efficiency of health resource allocation. Furthermore, we lack information on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on health resource allocation. In future research, we will conduct in-depth analysis along this theme, supplement and improve data over a longer period of years, attempt to include studies on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on health resource allocation, and add comparisons with health resource allocation in developed counties. This will provide more valuable insights and references for promoting the equity and efficiency of health resource allocation in underdeveloped counties.
