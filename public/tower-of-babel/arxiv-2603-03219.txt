Hardness of the Binary Covering Radius Problem in Large ℓ p \ell_{p} Norms
Huck Bennett, Peter Ly

Abstract
We study the hardness of the γ \gamma -approximate decisional Covering Radius Problem on lattices in the ℓ p \ell_{p} norm ( γ \gamma - GapCRP p \mathrm{GapCRP}_{p} ). Specifically, we prove that there is an explicit function γ ⁡ ( p ) \gamma(p) , with γ ⁡ ( p ) > 1 \gamma(p)>1 for p > p 0 ≈ 35.31 p>p_{0}\approx 35.31 and lim p → ∞ γ ⁡ ( p ) = 9 / 8 \lim_{p\to\infty}\gamma(p)=9/8 , such that for any constant ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , ( γ ⁡ ( p ) − ε ) (\gamma(p)-\varepsilon) - GapCRP p \mathrm{GapCRP}_{p} is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard. This shows the first hardness of GapCRP p \mathrm{GapCRP}_{p} for explicit p < ∞ p<\infty . Work of Haviv and Regev (CCC, 2006 and CJTCS, 2012) previously showed Π 2 \Pi_{2} -hardness of approximation for GapCRP p \mathrm{GapCRP}_{p} for all sufficiently large (but non-explicit) finite p p and for p = ∞ p=\infty . In fact, our hardness results hold for a variant of GapCRP \mathrm{GapCRP} called the Binary Covering Radius Problem ( BinGapCRP \mathrm{BinGapCRP} ), which trivially reduces to both GapCRP \mathrm{GapCRP} and the decisional Linear Discrepancy Problem ( LinDisc \mathrm{LinDisc} ) in any norm in an approximation-preserving way. We also show Π 2 \Pi_{2} -hardness of ( 9 / 8 − ε ) (9/8-\varepsilon) - BinGapCRP \mathrm{BinGapCRP} in the ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} norm for any constant ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . Our work extends and heavily uses the work of Manurangsi (IPL, 2021), which showed Π 2 \Pi_{2} -hardness of ( 9 / 8 − ε ) (9/8-\varepsilon) - LinDisc \mathrm{LinDisc} in the ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} norm.

Related Work

Early work of McLoughlin
[

]
showed that the Covering Radius Problem on error-correcting codes is

-hard by a reduction from a
\Pi_{2}
-hard variant of the
3
-Dimensional Matching Problem, which is in turn shown to be
\Pi_{2}
-hard by a reduction from
\Pi_{2}
-
3
-SAT.
2
2

2

Here and throughout this section we refer to linear error-correcting codes simply as “codes.”

Twenty years later, work of Guruswami, Micciancio, and Regev
[
GMR04
]
studied the complexity of the Covering Radius Problem both on codes and lattices.
It showed a number of results, including that the Covering Radius Problem on codes is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard to approximate to within any constant and
\Pi_{2}
-hard to approximate to within some constant.
(It did not show hardness results for
\mathrm{GapCRP}
on lattices.)

The work
[
GMR04
]
also showed that
2
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{p}
for any
p
is in
\mathsf{AM}
via an elegant protocol.
The fact that
2
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{p}
is in
\mathsf{AM}
provides evidence that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{p}
for
\gamma\geq 2
is unlikely to be
\Pi_{2}
-hard, since if it were the polynomial hierarchy would collapse.
However, being in
\mathsf{AM}
says nothing about the potential
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness of these problems. In particular it could very well be the case that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard for every constant
\gamma\geq 1
as is the case for the Covering Radius Problem on codes.

The factor of
\gamma=2
in the
[
GMR04
]

\mathsf{AM}
protocol follows generically for
\mathrm{GapCRP}
in any norm from the triangle inequality, but it is not always tight.
Follow-up work by Haviv, Lyubashevsky, and Regev
[
HLR09
]
studied the distribution of the (normalized)
\ell_{p}
distance of a random point in a fundamental domain of a lattice to the lattice.
This distribution is closely related to the
[
GMR04
]

\mathsf{AM}
protocol, and
[
HLR09
]
showed that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{2}
for
\gamma>\sqrt{3}\approx 1.732
is in
\mathsf{AM}
under a certain conjecture about this distribution in the
\ell_{2}
norm.
Their conjecture was subsequently proved by Magazinov
[
Mag20
]
.

Understanding the worst-case hardness of
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}
is also motivated by its relevance to cryptography. Indeed,
[
Mic02
]
showed that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}
with sufficiently large polynomial
\gamma=\gamma(n)
can be taken as the starting worst-case problem for a worst-case to average-case reduction to the problems used as the foundation of lattice-based cryptography.

The Haviv-Regev hardness reduction.

The work of Haviv and Regev
[
HR06
]
is the only prior work that studied the worst-case hardness of
\mathrm{GapCRP}
on lattices. As noted above, they showed that for every constant
\varepsilon>0
,
(3/2-\varepsilon)
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{\infty}
is
\Pi_{2}
-hard and that for all sufficiently large
p
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(p)
such that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{p}
is
\Pi_{2}
-hard to approximate. However, the cutoff for “sufficiently large” and the approximation factors
\gamma(p)
for
p<\infty
are non-explicit.

The reduction in
[
HR06
]
is from the Group Coloring Problem on graphs with respect to the group
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, a problem that is
\Pi_{2}
-hard.
3
3

3

Their reduction actually works with
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
for any
q\geq 3
, but they achieve the best hardness of approximation with
q=3
.

The problem is defined as follows. The input is a directed graph
G=(V,E)
. The goal is to decide whether for every assignment
\phi:E\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}
of labels to edges, there exists a coloring
c:V\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}
of vertices such that for every edge
e=(u,v)\in E
,
c(u)-c(v)\neq\phi(e)
. The reduction to
\mathrm{GapCRP}
is as follows. Let
A=A(G)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m\times n}
for
m:=|E|
,
n:=|V|
be the incidence matrix of
G
. That is,
A
is the matrix with two non-zero entries per row defined as

A_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if edge $e_{i}=(v_{j},u)$ for some $u\in V$,}\\
1&\text{if edge $e_{i}=(u,v_{j})$ for some $u\in V$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The reduction then outputs (a basis of) the lattice
\mathcal{L}:=A\cdot\mathbb{Z}^{n}+3\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.
One may also view
A
as the generator matrix of a ternary code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{3}^{m}
, and
\mathcal{L}
as the Construction-A lattice
\mathcal{C}+3\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

To show
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness of
\gamma
-
\mathrm{GapCRP}_{p}
in some
\ell_{p}
norm (for large
\gamma
), one might try to reduce from variants of the
3
-Coloring Problem, which is the
\mathsf{NP}
analog of the
\Pi_{2}
-complete
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-Group Coloring Problem.
Indeed, strong hardness of approximation results are known for Almost-
3
-Coloring—the optimization variant of
3
-Coloring in which the goal is to minimize the number of monochromatic edges—assuming variants of the Unique Games Conjecture
[
DMR06
]
.
However, although an analog of the soundness argument in
[
HR06
]
holds for (Almost-)
3
-Coloring, it is unclear how to show an analog of the completeness argument.

Combinatorial and linear discrepancy.

The (combinatorial)
discrepancy
of a set system
S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}\subseteq\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is defined as
\min_{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{-1,1\}}\max_{i\in[m]}|\sum_{j\in S_{i}}x_{j}|
, and the goal of the
combinatorial discrepancy problem
is, given the set system as input, to find
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
that achieve this minimum.
Equivalently, if
\mathbf{A}\in\{0,1\}^{m\times n}
is the incidence matrix of the input set system, the goal is to find
\boldsymbol{x}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
that minimizes
\lVert\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{x}\rVert_{\infty}
.
Combinatorial discrepancy and other variants of discrepancy are used widely in computer science and math (see, e.g.,
[
CST14
]
).

Linear discrepancy
was originally defined and related to other notions of discrepancy in work of Lovász, Spencer, and Vestergombi
[
LSV86
]
, and it appears as a key ingredient in certain approximation algorithms (see, e.g.,
[
EPR13
,
Rot16
,
HR17
]
).
Formally, we define the linear discrepancy
\lindisc(\mathbf{B})
of a matrix
\mathbf{B}
in the
\ell_{p}
norm as
4
4

4

“Standard” linear discrepancy corresponds to
\lindisc_{\infty}
, and, to the best of our knowledge, our work is the first to extend the definition to finite
\ell_{p}
norms. This extension is useful for our work.

\lindisc_{p}(\mathbf{B}):=\max_{\boldsymbol{w}\in[0,1]^{n}}\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\lVert\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{x})\rVert_{p}\ \text{.}

(2)

This definition is syntactically very similar to the definition of the covering radius in the
\ell_{p}
norm,
\mu_{p}
.
The difference is that in the definition of
\mu_{p}
, the minimum is taken over
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
rather than
\boldsymbol{x}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. See
Sections

2.1
and
2.2
.

Combinatorial discrepancy is captured, up to translation and scaling, by fixing
\boldsymbol{w}:=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{1}
in
Equation

2
instead of taking the max over all
\boldsymbol{w}\in[0,1]^{n}
there.
Linear discrepancy was first investigated from a complexity perspective by Li and Nikolov
[
LN20
]
, who provided both algorithms and hardness results.
In particular, they showed that the exact Linear Discrepancy Problem in the
\ell_{\infty}
norm,
\mathrm{LinDisc}_{\infty}
, is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard and that it is contained in
\Pi_{2}
.
Their hardness result was subsequently improved by Manurangsi
[
Man21
]
, who showed that
\gamma
-
\mathrm{LinDisc}_{\infty}
is
\Pi_{2}
-hard for
\gamma:=9/8-\varepsilon
for any constant
\varepsilon>0
. Our work makes extensive use of

]
.

2.1
Covering Radius and Linear Discrepancy

We call a matrix

For

of a lattice

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{L}):=\max_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\lspan(\mathcal{L})}\mathrm{dist}_{p}(\boldsymbol{t},\mathcal{L})\ \text{,}

where for a discrete set

\mathrm{dist}_{p}(\boldsymbol{t},S):=\min_{\boldsymbol{y}\in S}\lVert\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{y}\rVert_{p}

Because
\mathrm{dist}_{p}(\boldsymbol{t}+\boldsymbol{y},\mathcal{L})=\mathrm{dist}_{p}(\boldsymbol{t},\mathcal{L})

\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and

for a basis

\mu_{p}(\mathcal{L})=\max_{\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\lVert\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{x})\rVert_{p}=\max_{\boldsymbol{w}\in[0,1]^{n}}\min_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\lVert\mathbf{B}(\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{x})\rVert_{p}\ \text{.}

(4)

That is, because the distance of a vector
\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{w}
to

is invariant under shifts by lattice vectors (i.e., vectors
\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{x}
for
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{w}
in the fundamental parallelepiped

to the lattice.

We will also work with
