The theta correspondence over finite fields
Anne-Marie Aubert

Abstract
. This set of lecture notes is an expanded version of a mini-course the author gave in March of 2025 for the program “ Representation Theory & Noncommutative Geometry ” at the Institut Henri Poincaré, Paris. The goal is to provide a survey of the main properties of the theta correspondence over finite fields of odd characteristic, including its compatibility with Harish-Chandra and Lusztig series, and with the Jordan decomposition of representations, as well as its full explicit description.



1.
Introduction

In 1964, André Weil introduced the
Weil representation
(also called
oscillator representation
), initially for adelic and local fields, see
[
42
]
.
Weil showed that this representation encodes deep symmetries linking symplectic and orthogonal structures, and
also observed that the same construction makes sense over finite fields, where everything becomes concrete and combinatorial.

Let
W
be a finite dimensional vector space over a finite field
k
of odd characteristic, let
\langle\;,\;\rangle
be a nondegenerate alternated form on
V
, and let
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
denote the symplectic group attached to

\langle\;,\;\rangle
. The Weil representation is a representation of the group
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
, the construction of which relies on the Stone-von Neumann Theorem for the Heisenberg group associated to the vector space
W
.
In the local fields setting the Weil representation is only a projective representation, which makes necessary to consider a metaplectic cover of the symplectic group (see
[
42
]
or
[
5
]
). The finite fields setting (in the odd characteristic case) is hence simpler in this aspect.

In the 1970s, Roger Howe introduced in
[
16
,
17
]
the concept of
dual pairs
(these are the pairs
(G,G^{\prime})
of reductive subgroups of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
such that
G^{\prime}
is the centralizer of
G
in
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
and vice-versa)
and defined a correspondence between the categories of complex representations of these subgroups.

This correspondence, which is known as the
theta correspondence
or the
Howe correspondence
, is obtained from the restriction of the Weil representation to
G\cdot G^{\prime}
.
The image under the theta correspondence for a dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
of a given irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is a finite set, say
\{\pi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\pi^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}\}
of irreducible representations
\pi^{\prime}_{i}
of
G^{\prime}
, which in general, in contrast with the the local fields setting, is not a singleton.

The explicit description of the theta correspondence over finite fields is a long standing question, which is the main subject of these notes. It has also applications to the theta correspondence over nonarchimedean local fields as shown notably in
[
33
,
20
,
2
]
.

These notes are organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the Weil representation of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
, and section
3
describes the construction of the theta correspondence and some of its basic properties.

Section
4
concerns the “philosophy of cusp forms” of Harish-Chandra and its application to the theta correspondence. More precisely, let
\mathbf{G}
be an arbitrary reductive connected group or a split orthogonal group defined over
k
, and
G
denote its group of
k
-points. The group
G
is a finite group of Lie type.
The category
\mathfrak{R}(G)
of complex representations of
G
decomposes as a direct product of subcategories
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
, where
\mathfrak{s}
is the
G
-conjugacy class of a
cuspidal pair

(L,\sigma)
, that is,
L
is a Levi subgroup of
G
and
\sigma
an irreducible cuspidal representation of
L
. We describe the set
\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
of isomorphism classes of irreducible objects of
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
, called the
Harish-Chandra series
associated to
\mathfrak{s}
, and, in Theorem
4.2.10
(proved in
[
4
]
), we formulate the compatibility of the theta correspondence with the Harish-Chandra series of
G
and
G^{\prime}
.

In section
5
, we focus on Lusztig’s approach of the study of representations of finite groups of Lie type. Let
\check{\mathbf{G}}^{\circ}
denote the reductive connected group with root datum dual to that of the identity component
\mathbf{G}^{\circ}
of
\mathbf{G}
. We consider the partition of the set
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
of isomorphism classes of irreducible complex representations of
G
in
Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}(G,s)
, where
(s)
runs over the
\check{G}^{\circ}
-conjugacy classes of semisimple elements of
\check{G}^{\circ}
.
Let
\mathbf{G}_{s}
be the
endoscopic group
of
\mathbf{G}
associated with
s
, that is, the dual group of the centralizer of
s
in
\check{\mathbf{G}}
. By Lusztig’s results
[
26
,
27
]
(and
[
4
]
for
\mathbf{G}={\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2n}
), the set
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
is in bijection with the
unipotent Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}(G_{s},1)
of
G_{s}
. We show that the description of the theta correspondence reduces to describe its restriction to unipotent representations.

Section
6
describes the irreducible constituents of unipotent Harish-Chandra series for every reductive dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
and expresses the theta correspondence for unipotent representations
as a correspondence between representations of two finite Weyl groups. These latter was proved for the dual pair of general linear groups and for the dual of unitary groups in
[
4
]
, and conjectured in
[
4
]
for ortho-symplectic dual pairs.
The validity of our conjecture has been established independently by S.-Y. Pan in
[
37
]
and by J.-j. Ma, C. Qiu, and J. Zou in
[
28
]
.

The main goal of section
7
is to reformulate, following Pan’s works, the correspondence between representations of Weyl groups in terms of Lusztig’s combinatorics of symbols.
Section
8
describes several manners to extract a one-to-one correspondence from the theta correspondence for arbitrary dual pairs and representations.

Finally, section
9
briefly relates the theta correspondence to the holographic correspondence studies by A. Dymarsky, J. Henriksson and B. McPeak in
[
9
]
.

Let

Let

.
The group of isometries of

(2.0.1)

\operatorname{Sp}(W):=\left\{g\in\mathrm{GL}(W)\;:\;\langle g(w_{1}),g(w_{2})\rangle=\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle\;\text{ for all $w_{1},w_{2}\in W$}\right\}.

The elements of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(k)
admit the following characterization:
Let
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\cr c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2N}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
, with
a,b,c,d\in\operatorname{M}_{2N}(k)

\displaystyle g\in\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(k)

\displaystyle\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;

a^{t}c=c^{t}a
,
b^{t}d=d^{t}b
and
a^{t}d-c^{t}b=\mathrm{I}

\displaystyle\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;

ab^{t}=ba^{t}
,
cd^{t}=dc^{t}
and
ad^{t}-bc^{t}=\mathrm{I}

Definition 2.0.2
.

The Heisenberg group
H(W)
:
As a set, we have

H(W):=W\times k=k^{2N+1}.

The multiplication law is defined by

(w,t)\cdot(w^{\prime},t^{\prime}):=(w+w^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\langle w,w^{\prime}\rangle).

Definition 2.0.3
.

Let

be a finite group.

•

The

of

s.t.
h^{n}=1

\forall h\in H
.

•

The

of

.

•

We denote by

the derived subgroup of

.
If

is a

is

), and

•

The group

|H^{\prime}|=p
.

Let

denote the center of

. It is isomorphic to

.

Remark 2.0.4
.

H=H(W)
is a special
p
-group with exponent

. If

Theorem 2.0.5
.

[Finite analogue of the Stone-von-Neumann Theorem]

For every non-trivial character
\psi
of
{\mathrm{Z}}_{H(W)}
, there exists a unique (up to equivalence) irreducible representation
(\varrho_{\psi},S)
of
H(W)
, known as the Heisenberg representation, such that

\varrho_{\psi}(z)=\psi(z)\,\operatorname{Id}_{S}\;\;\text{for all $z\in{\mathrm{Z}}_{H(W)}$.}

Proof.

See
[
29
, Theorem 2.I.2]
.
∎

The group
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
acts as a group of automorphisms of
H(W)
by the rule:

(2.0.6)

g\cdot(w,t):=(g(w),t)\quad\text{for $g\in\operatorname{Sp}(W)$ and $(w,t)\in H(W)$}.

Let
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(W)
. The action of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
on
H(W)
fixes the elements of its center. Hence, for a fixed character
\psi
of
k
, the representations
\varrho_{\psi}
and
g\cdot\varrho_{\psi}
agree on
{\mathrm{Z}}_{H(W)}
, for any
g\in\operatorname{Sp}(W)
.
Theorem
2.0.5
implies that there is an operator
\omega_{\psi}(g)
verifying

(2.0.7)

\varrho_{\psi}(g\cdot w,t)=\omega_{\psi}(g)\varrho_{\psi}(w,t)\omega_{\psi}(g)^{-1}.

This defines a projective representation
\omega_{\psi}
of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
, which can be lifted to an actual representation of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
, still denoted by
\omega_{\psi}
, since
H^{2}(\operatorname{Sp}(W),\mathbb{C}^{\times})=0
.

Definition 2.0.8
.

The representation
\omega_{\psi}
of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
is the Weil representation (or oscillator representation) determined by the additive character
\psi
.

Remark 2.0.9
.

For
a\in k
, let
\psi_{a}\colon{\mathrm{Z}}_{H(W)}\to\mathbb{C}
be the character defined by
\psi_{a}(x):=\psi(ax)
.

•

For
b\in k^{\times}
, the representations
\omega_{\psi_{a}}
and
\omega_{\psi_{ab^{2}}}
are isomorphic.

•

The representations
\omega_{\psi_{a}}
, as
a
runs over representatives for

, are not isomorphic.

The theta correspondence

3.1.
Reductive dual pairs

Definition 3.1.1
.

A pair of reductive subgroups of
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
, is called a
dual pair
if

\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{Sp}(W)}(G)=G^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{C}_{\operatorname{Sp}(W)}(G^{\prime})=G.

We will focus on irreducible dual pairs, because these are the building blocks of all the others. Such pairs are of the following kinds:

\bullet

pairs of type I:

\circ

(\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(k),{\mathrm{O}}_{n^{\prime}}(k))
with
mn^{\prime}=N
;

\circ

(\mathrm{U}_{n}(k),\mathrm{U}_{n^{\prime}}(k))
with
nn^{\prime}=2N
;

\bullet

pairs of type II:

\circ

(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\mathrm{GL}_{n^{\prime}}(k))
with
nn^{\prime}=2N
.

The dual pairs formed by a symplectic group and an orthogonal group are more precisely described as follows.

•

n
and
n^{\prime}
be two fixed non-negative integers

•

V=V_{n}
a
k
-vector space of (even) dimension
n=2m
, endowed with a non-degenerate alternated

k
-bilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
.
The corresponding group of isometries is the symplectic group
G:=\operatorname{Sp}(V_{n})
.

•

V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
a
k
-vector space of dimension
n^{\prime}
over
k
, endowed with a non-degenerate symmetric

k
-bilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle^{\prime}
.
The orthogonal group
G^{\prime}:={\mathrm{O}}(V_{n^{\prime}})
is the corresponding group of
isometries.

•

(G,G^{\prime})
is a dual pair in
\operatorname{Sp}(W)
where
W:=V_{n}\otimes_{k}V^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
with

\langle\langle v_{1}\otimes v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}\otimes v_{2}^{\prime}\rangle\rangle:=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\cdot\langle v_{2}^{\prime},v_{1}^{\prime}\rangle^{\prime},

for
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
and
v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.1.2
.

A reductive dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
is said to be in the

(with
G^{\prime}

has a totally isotropic subspace of dimension

.

Examples 3.1.3
.

The pair
(\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(k),{\mathrm{O}}^{\epsilon}_{2m^{\prime}}(k))
is in the stable range if and only if
m\geq 2m^{\prime}
.
Consider now a pair
(G_{n},G_{n^{\prime}}^{\prime})=({\mathrm{O}}^{\epsilon}_{2m^{\prime}}(k),\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(k))
, with
{\mathrm{O}}_{2m^{\prime}}(k)={\mathrm{O}}(V_{2m^{\prime}})

Note that, if

We will introduce a few more notation in order to be able consider all possible irreducible reductive dual pairs. Let

Let

For

Let

Note that

3.2.
Definition of the theta correspondence

Let
\psi
be a nontrivial additive character of
k
.
Gérardin introduced in
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
a slightly different version
\omega_{\psi}^{\flat}
of
\omega_{\psi}
.

When
(G,G^{\prime})
is a pair formed by a symplectic and an orthogonal group, the representations
\omega_{\psi}
and
\omega_{\psi}^{\flat}
have the same restriction to
G\cdot G^{\prime}
.
In the other cases, the restrictions of
\omega_{\psi}
and
\omega_{\psi}^{\flat}
differ by a representation of
G
with values in
\{\pm 1\}
.

From now on we suppose that the character
\psi
is fixed. Let
\omega^{\flat}_{G,G^{\prime}}
denote the restriction of
\omega^{\flat}_{\psi}
to
G\cdot G^{\prime}
.

Let
(G,G^{\prime}):=(G(V),G(V^{\prime})
. In
[
28
]
, a modified version
\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}
of
\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}^{\flat}
is used. It is defined as follows:

(3.2.1)

\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}:=\begin{cases}\left((\xi\circ\det_{G})^{\frac{\dim V^{\prime}}{2}}\otimes 1_{\mathbb{G}^{\prime}}\right)\otimes\omega^{\flat}_{G,G^{\prime}}&\text{in cases (2) and (5)}\cr\left(1_{G}\otimes(\xi\circ\det_{G^{\prime}})^{\frac{\dim V}{2}}\right)\otimes\omega^{\flat}_{G,G^{\prime}}&\text{in cases (3) and (4)}.\end{cases}

The representation
\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}
has the form

(3.2.2)

\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}=\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\atop\pi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime})}\mathrm{m}_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}\,\,\pi\otimes\pi^{\prime},\;\;\text{where $\mathrm{m}_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$}.

We set

(3.2.3)

\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}:=\left\{(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)\times\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime})\,:\,\mathrm{m}_{\pi,\pi^{\prime}}\neq 0\right\}.

Hence
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}
defines a
correspondence
between
\operatorname{Irr}(G)
and
\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime})
. This correspondence is
symmetric
, that is,
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}
if and only if
(\pi^{\prime},\pi)\in\Theta_{G^{\prime},G}
.

Definition 3.2.4
.

Let
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
. We say that the representation
\pi
occurs in the theta correspondence for the dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
if there exists
\pi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime})
such that
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}
.

For
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)
, we define its
theta lift

(3.2.5)

\theta_{G^{\prime}}(\pi):=\bigoplus_{\text{$\pi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime})$ such that $(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}$}}\pi^{\prime},

and we extend
\theta_{G^{\prime}}
by linearity into a map

(3.2.6)

\theta_{G^{\prime}}\colon\mathbb{Z}\,\operatorname{Irr}(G)\to\mathbb{Z}\,\operatorname{Irr}(G^{\prime}).

In general, if nonzero,
\theta_{G^{\prime}}(\pi)
is reducible, and the main goal of these notes is to obtain an explicit description of the representation
\theta_{G^{\prime}}(\pi)
.

On the other side, since the correspondence
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}
is in general not one-to-one, a natural question is: can we find an interesting sub-relation of
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}
which is one-to-one.

3.3.
Witt towers of theta correspondences

The non-vanishing question of theta lifts for (Type I dual pairs) can be approached by considering theta lifts in the Witt tower of the the target space.

Let
\mathbb{H}
denote the unique (up to isomorphism)
2
-dimensional
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian vector space containing a non-zero isotropic vector. We call
\mathbb{H}
the hyperbolic
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian plane.

Definition 3.3.1
.

A set
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
of
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian vector spaces is called a
Witt tower
if there is an anisotropic
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian vector space
V^{\prime}_{\mathrm{an}}
such that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
consists of all
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-Hermitian vectors spaces
V^{\prime}(r^{\prime})

satisfying

V^{\prime}(r^{\prime})\simeq V^{\prime}_{\mathrm{an}}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb{H}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{H}}_{r^{\prime}}.

The integer
r^{\prime}
above is called the
split rank
of
V^{\prime}(r^{\prime})
and the parity of
\dim V^{\prime}_{\mathrm{an}}
is called the
parity of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
.

If
k=k_{0}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=1
, we have
{\mathrm{disc}}(V^{\prime}(r^{\prime}))={\mathrm{disc}}(V^{\prime}_{\mathrm{an}})
for every integer
r^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Therefore, we define

(3.3.2)

{\mathrm{disc}}({\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}):={\mathrm{disc}}(V^{\prime}_{\mathrm{an}}),

and call it the discriminant of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}
in this case.

Remark 3.3.3
.

(1)

For even orthogonal groups there are two Witt towers depending if
G^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}={\mathrm{O}}(V_{n^{\prime}})
is split or not. We denote them
{\mathrm{O}}^{+}
and
{\mathrm{O}}^{-}
, respectively, and write
{\mathrm{O}}_{2n}^{\epsilon}(k)
for the corresponding orthogonal group

(2)

For odd orthogonal groups there are two Witt towers as well
\left\{{\mathrm{O}}_{2n+1}^{\epsilon}(k)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
, with
\epsilon=\pm
.

(3)

For unitary groups there are two Witt towers
\mathrm{U}^{+}:=\left\{\mathrm{U}_{2m}(k)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

and
\mathrm{U}^{-}:=\left\{\mathrm{U}_{2m+1}(k)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
. We will set
\mathrm{U}^{+}_{n}(k):=\mathrm{U}_{2m}(k)
if
n=2m
and
\mathrm{U}^{-}_{n}(k):=\mathrm{U}_{2m+1}(k)
if

, and
\mathbf{T}_{m}\simeq\overline{k}^{\times}\times\cdots\times\overline{k}^{\times}
be a
k
-rational maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
. For
s=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\check{\mathbf{T}}_{m}
, we denote by let
\nu_{\lambda}(s)
denote the number of the
\lambda_{i}
’s which are equal to
\lambda
, and by
\langle\lambda\rangle
the set of all roots in
\overline{k}
of the irreducible polynomial of
\lambda
over
k
.

The endoscopic group
\mathbf{G}_{s}
decomposes as a product

(5.3.1)

\mathbf{G}_{s}=\prod_{\langle\lambda\rangle\subset\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\}}\mathbf{G}_{s,\lambda},

where
\mathbf{G}_{s,\lambda}
is a reductive quasi-simple group of rank equal to
\nu_{\lambda}(s)\cdot|\langle\lambda\rangle|
, see for instance
[
4
, §1.B]
.

Let
\mathbf{G}\in\{\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1},\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},{\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2n},\mathrm{U}_{m}\}
. Then the following holds:

(i)

if
\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
, then
\check{\mathbf{G}}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
,
\mathbf{G}_{s,1}\simeq\operatorname{SO}_{2\nu_{1}(s)+1}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{s,-1}\simeq\operatorname{SO}_{2\nu_{-1}(s)+1}
;

(ii)

if
\mathbf{G}=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}
, then
\check{\mathbf{G}}=\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1}
,
\mathbf{G}_{s,1}\simeq\operatorname{Sp}_{2\nu_{1}(s)}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{s,-1}\simeq{\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2\nu_{-1}(s)}
;

(iii)

if
\mathbf{G}={\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2n}
, then
\check{\mathbf{G}}={\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2n}
,
\mathbf{G}_{s,1}\simeq{\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2\nu_{1}(s)}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{s,-1}\simeq{\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2\nu_{-1}(s)}
;

(iv)

if
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{U}_{m}
or if
\lambda\neq\pm 1
, then
\mathbf{G}_{s,\lambda}
is either a general linear group or a unitary group.

5.3.1.
Unitary groups

When
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{U}_{m}
, the endoscopic group
\mathbf{G}_{s}
decomposes as

(5.3.2)

\mathbf{G}_{s}=\mathbf{G}_{s,1}\times\mathbf{G}_{s,\neq 1},

where

(5.3.3)

\mathbf{G}_{s,\neq 1}:=\prod_{\langle\lambda\rangle\subset\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\},\lambda\neq 1}\mathbf{G}_{s,\lambda}.

By composing
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{G}
with the natural bijection

(5.3.4)

\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s})&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq 1})\cr\pi^{\mathrm{u}}&\mapsto&\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{\neq 1}\end{matrix}

we obtain a bijection

(5.3.5)

\begin{matrix}{{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}}_{s}^{G}\colon\mathcal{E}(G,s)&\xrightarrow{1-1}&\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq 1})\cr\pi&\mapsto&\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{\neq 1}.\end{matrix}

By collecting the bijections
{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}_{s}^{G}
for all the semisimple element
s
, we obtain a bjijection

(5.3.6)

{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}^{G}\colon\operatorname{Irr}(G)\longrightarrow\bigcup_{s}\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq 1}).

5.3.2.
Symplectic and orthogonal groups

Let
\mathbf{G}\in\{\operatorname{SO}_{2n+1},\operatorname{Sp}_{2n},{\mathrm{O}}^{\pm}_{2n}\}
. Then the group
\mathbf{G}_{s}
decomposes as a product

(5.3.7)

\mathbf{G}_{s}=\mathbf{G}_{s,1}\times\mathbf{G}_{s,-1}\times\mathbf{G}_{s,\neq},

where

(5.3.8)

\mathbf{G}_{s,\neq}:=\prod_{\langle\lambda\rangle\subset\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\},\lambda\neq\pm 1}\mathbf{G}_{s,\lambda}

is a product of general linear groups and unitary groups.

We write

(5.3.9)

s=s_{1}\times s_{-1}\times s_{\neq},

where
s_{1}
(resp.
s_{-1}
) is the part whose eigenvalues are all equal to
1
(resp.
-1
), and
s_{\neq}
is the part whose
eigenvalues do not contain
1
or
-1
.

By composing
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{G}
with the natural bijection

(5.3.10)

\begin{matrix}\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s})&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,-1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq})\cr\pi^{\mathrm{u}}&\mapsto&\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{-1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{\neq}\end{matrix}

we obtain a bijection

(5.3.11)

\begin{matrix}{{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}}_{s}^{G}\colon\mathcal{E}(G,s)&\xrightarrow{1-1}&\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,-1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq})\cr\pi&\mapsto&\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{-1}\otimes\pi^{\mathrm{u}}_{\neq}.\end{matrix}

By collecting the bijections
{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}_{s}^{G}
for all the semisimple element
s
, we obtain a bjijection

(5.3.12)

{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}}^{G}\colon\operatorname{Irr}(G)\longrightarrow\bigcup_{s}\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,-1})\times\operatorname{Irr}^{\mathrm{u}}(G_{s,\neq}).

Notation 5.3.13
.

Let
s
be a semisimple element in

and let

5.3.3.
The case of

We have

6.
The theta correspondence for unipotent representations

6.1.
Dual pairs of type II

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group defined over
k
, and let
\mathbf{T}
be a maximal torus in
\mathbf{G}
. Let
F
be the Frobenius endomorphism and
W
the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
.

Definition 6.1.1
.

The
F
-conjugation
in
\mathbf{G}
is defined to be the action of
\mathbf{G}
on itself defined for any
g\in\mathbf{G}
by
x\mapsto gx({}^{F}g)^{-1}
.

The
F
-conjugacy class of the image of
g^{-1}{}^{F}g
in
W
is called the
type
of the torus
{}^{g}\mathbf{T}
with respect to
\mathbf{T}
; a representative of this

F
-conjugacy class is called a type of
{}^{g}\mathbf{T}
with respect to
\mathbf{T}
.

By the conjugation action of
g^{-1}
, the torus
{}^{g}\mathbf{T}
, endowed with the action of
F
, is identified with the torus
\mathbf{T}
endowed with the action of
wF
, if
w
is the image of
g^{-1}{}^{F}g
in
W
.
We will denoted by
\mathbf{T}_{w}
this torus.

We now take for
\mathbf{G}
the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
. The unipotent irreducible representations of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
are the irreducible components of
\operatorname{i}_{\mathbf{T},\mathbf{B}}^{\mathbf{G}}(1)
, where
\mathbf{B}
is a Borel subgroup of

\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{T}\subset\mathbf{B}
a maximal torus, and
1
denotes the trivial representation of
T=\mathbf{T}(k)\simeq k^{\times}
.
Hence the set
\operatorname{Irr}_{\mathrm{u}}(G)
is in bijection with
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
, where
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is symmetric group. It follows that the unipotent irreducible representations of
G
are parametrized the partitions of
n
.
We denote by
\pi_{\lambda}
and
E_{\lambda}
the irreducible representation of
G
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, respectively, which corresponds to the partition
\lambda
of
n
.

We consider the
almost character
:

(6.1.2)

R^{G}_{\lambda}:=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\operatorname{Tr}(E_{\lambda})(w)\,R_{\mathbf{T}_{w}}^{\mathbf{G}}(1).

Proposition 6.1.3
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
, the almost character
R_{E_{\lambda}}:=R_{\lambda}
is a unipotent irreducible character of
G
, and each such character is of this form for some partition
\lambda
of
n
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{h})\in\mathcal{P}(n)
and
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{h^{\prime}})\in\mathcal{P}(m)
. We may assume that
h=h^{\prime}
by adding
0
s if necessary.
We define the
union
of
\lambda
and
\mu
, denoted
\lambda\cup\mu
, to be the partition of
n+m
with parts
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{h}
.
The usual order on
\mathcal{P}(n)
is defined by

(6.1.4)

\lambda\leq\lambda^{\prime}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+\cdots+\lambda_{i}^{\prime},\text{ for all $i\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Let
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{h}]
be a partition of
n
. The
height
of
\lambda
, denoted
0pt(\lambda)
, is defined to be the largest
i
with
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
.

For every index
j
, we set

(6.1.5)

\lambda_{j}^{\ast}:=|\left\{i\,:\,\lambda_{i}\geq j\right\}|.

The partition
{}^{t}\lambda=[\lambda^{\ast}_{1},\lambda^{\ast}_{2},\ldots]
is called the
transpose
of
\lambda
.
Flipping a Young diagram of
\lambda
of over its main diagonal (from upper left to lower right), we obtain the Young diagram of the partition
{}^{t}\lambda
.

If
\lambda=[\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{h}]
and
\mu=[\mu_{1}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{h^{\prime}}]
are two partition we write
\mu\subset\lambda

if the followings hold:

0pt(\mu)\leq 0pt(\lambda)
and

\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 0pt(\mu)
.

If we identify
\lambda
and
\mu
with their Young diagrams, this means that the diagram of
\mu
is contained in those of
\lambda
. Removing the boxes of
\lambda
which belong to
\mu
, we obtain a skew diagram which we denote by

\lambda-\mu
.

We will also need to consider the
intersection partition
of of
\lambda
and
\mu
:

\lambda\cap\mu:=[\min(\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}),\ldots,\min(\lambda_{\min(h,h^{\prime})},\mu_{\min(h,h^{\prime})})].

We have
\mu\subset\lambda
if and only if
\lambda\cap\mu=\mu
.

Let
\nu:=[\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{m}]
. Then we denote by
p_{\lambda=\mu}(\nu)
the partition
[\nu_{i}]_{\{i:\lambda_{i}=\mu_{i}\}}
and we put

(6.1.6)

\lambda\cap^{=}\mu:=p_{\lambda=\mu}(\lambda\cap\mu).

The partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
are said to be
close
if for each
i
we have
|\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}|\leq 1
.

We consider the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module:

\mathfrak{R}(\mathfrak{S}):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{R}(\mathfrak{S}_{n}),

and define a map
\theta^{\mathfrak{S}}\colon\mathfrak{R}(\mathfrak{S})\to\mathfrak{R}(\mathfrak{S})
by

(6.1.7)

E_{\lambda}\mapsto\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\atop\text{${}^{t}\lambda^{\prime}$ is close to ${}^{t}\lambda$}}f({}^{t}\lambda\cap^{=}{}^{t}\lambda^{\prime})\,E_{\lambda^{\prime}},

where, if
\mu=[r^{a_{1}},(r-1)^{a_{2}},\ldots,1^{a_{r}}]
, we have put
f(\mu):=\prod_{i}a_{i}
.

Theorem 6.1.8
.

The theta correspondence between unipotent representations of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
and
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{GL}_{n^{\prime}}(k)
is given by the map

R^{G}_{\lambda}\mapsto R^{G^{\prime}}_{\theta^{\mathfrak{S}}(E_{\lambda})}.

Proof.

See
[
4
, Théorème 5.5]
.
∎

Corollary 6.1.9
.

We consider the dual pair
(G,G^{\prime}):=(\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k),\mathrm{GL}_{n^{\prime}}(k))
with
n^{\prime}\leq n
. The unipotent representations of
G
and
G^{\prime}

with characters
R^{G}_{\lambda}
and
R^{G^{\prime}}_{\lambda\cup(n-n^{\prime})}
, where
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
and
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(n^{\prime})
, correspond under the theta correspondence.

Moreover, any representation of
G
which belongs to the image of
R_{\lambda}
under the theta correspondence is of the form

\text{$R_{\mu^{\prime}}$ where $\mu^{\prime}\geq\lambda\cup(n-n^{\prime})$}.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Theorem 3]
.
∎

6.2.
Cuspidal unipotent representations of classical groups

Let
G
be a classical group. It has cuspidal unipotent irreducible representations in the following cases:

\bullet

G=\mathrm{GL}_{1}(k)
has a unique such representation: the trivial representation;

\bullet

G\in\{\operatorname{Sp}_{2(c^{2}+c)}(k),\operatorname{SO}_{2(c^{2}+c)+1}(k),\mathrm{U}^{\epsilon}_{(c^{2}+c)/2}(k)\}

\pi^{G}_{c}

\bullet

G=\operatorname{SO}_{2c^{2}}^{-}(k)
, with
c
a positive odd integer, has a unique such representation, say
\pi^{G}_{c}
;

\bullet

G={\mathrm{O}}_{2c^{2}}^{+}(k)
, with
c
a positive even integer, has two such representations, say
\pi^{G}_{c,+}
and
\pi^{G}_{c,-}=\pi^{G}_{c,+}\otimes\operatorname{sign}
;

\bullet

G={\mathrm{O}}_{2c^{2}}^{-}(k)
, with

a positive odd integer, has two such representations, say
\pi^{G}_{c,+}
and
\pi^{G}_{c,-}=\pi^{G}_{c,+}\otimes\operatorname{sign}

\bullet

G={\mathrm{O}}_{2(c^{2}+c)+1}(k)
, with
c
a positive integer, has two such representations, say
\pi^{G}_{c,+}
and
\pi^{G}_{c,-}=\pi^{G}_{c,+}\otimes\operatorname{sign}
;

\bullet

, with
c
a positive integer such that
\epsilon=(-1)^{\frac{c^{2}+c}{2}}
.

Theorem 6.2.1
.

(1)

We suppose that
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(k)
and

(a)

If
\pi
is a cuspidal irreducible representation of

, then

is a singleton
\{\pi^{\prime}\}
with
\pi^{\prime}
cuspidal irreducible.

(b)

If
\pi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G_{n})
is unipotent then any
\pi^{\prime}\in\theta_{G^{\prime}}(\pi)
is unipotent.

(c)

The representation
\pi_{c}^{G}
of
G=\operatorname{Sp}_{2(c^{2}+c)}(k)
corresponds to the representation
\pi^{G^{\prime}}_{c^{\prime},-}
with
c^{\prime}=c
if
\epsilon
is the sign of
(-1)^{c}
, and to the representation
\pi^{G^{\prime}}_{c^{\prime},+}
with
c^{\prime}=c+1
otherwise.

(2)

We suppose that

and

. The representation
\pi_{c}^{G}
of
G
corresponds to the representation
\pi^{G^{\prime}}_{c^{\prime}}
, where

(a)

if
c=0
, then
c^{\prime}=0
;

(b)

if
c\neq 0
, then
c^{\prime}=c\pm 1
such that
\epsilon^{\prime}=(-1)^{\frac{c^{\prime}(c^{\prime}+1)}{2}}
.

Proof.

See
[
1
]
.
∎

Hence, for
(G,G^{\prime})
an irreducible reductive dual pair, the theta correspondence induces a correspondence between cuspidal unipotent representations of
G
and

7.
Combinatorial description

7.1.
Unitary groups

We define another order on partitions as follows: for
\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}
partitions of possibly different integers, we write

(7.1.1)

\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\lambda^{\prime}_{i+1}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}^{\prime},\text{ for all $i\in\mathbb{N}$.}

In other words,
\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}
if the Young diagram of
\lambda
is contained in the one of
\lambda^{\prime}
and that we can go from the first to the second by adding at most one box per column.

Example 7.1.2
.

The partitions
\lambda=(4,1,1)
of
6
, and
\lambda^{\prime}=(4,4,1,1)
of
10
verify
\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}
.

Remark 7.1.3
.

Two partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{h})
and
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{h})
satisfy
\lambda\preceq\lambda^{\prime}
and
|\lambda|=|\lambda^{\prime}|
, then we have
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

The
Young diagram
of a partition
\lambda
is defined to be the set of points
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
1\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}
.
To each point
(i,j)
in the Young diagram of
\lambda
we associate the
hook
at
(i,j)
, which the set

(7.1.4)

\xi=\xi_{i,j}:=\left\{(i,j^{\prime})\,:\,j\leq j^{\prime}\leq\lambda_{i}\right\}\,\cup\,\left\{(i^{\prime},j)\,:\,i\leq i^{\prime}\leq\lambda^{\ast}_{i}\right\},

The cardinality

|\xi|=\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}^{\ast}-i-j+1

is called the
hook-length
of
\xi
. A hook of length
2
is also a
2
-hook
. A hook

\xi_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
of
\lambda
is said to be

•

above

\xi_{i,j}
if we have
j^{\prime}=j
and
i^{\prime}<i

•

left to

\xi_{i,j}
if
i^{\prime}=i
and
j^{\prime}<j
.

Let
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}]
and
\lambda^{\prime}=[\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{m}]
be
two partitions of
n
such that either

•

there exists an index
i_{0}
such that
\lambda_{i_{0}}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i_{0}}+2
, and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}
for
i\neq i_{0}
;

•

or there exists an index
i_{0}
such that
\lambda_{i_{0}}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i_{0}}+1
,
\lambda_{i_{0}+1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i_{0}+1}+1
, and

\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}
for
i\neq i_{0},i_{0}+1
,

then we said that
\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained from
\lambda
by adding a
2
-hook or
\lambda
is obtained from
\lambda^{\prime}
by removing a
2
-hook.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
. After removing all possible
2
-hooks step by step, the resulting partition is denoted by
\lambda_{\infty}
and called the
2
-core
of
\lambda
.
We observe that
\lambda_{\infty}=[d,d-1,\ldots,1]
for some non-negative integer
d
. A partition
\lambda
is said to be
cuspidal
if
\lambda=\lambda_{\infty}
.

Definition 7.1.5
.

A
\beta
-set
X=\left\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\right\}
is a finite set of non-negative integers with elements written in increasing order, i.e.,
x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{m}
. We define an equivalence relation (denoted by “
\sim
”) on
\beta
-sets generated by

X\sim\{0\}\,\cup\,\left\{x+1|\,:\,x\in X\right\}.

The
rank
of
X
is

\operatorname{rank}(X):=\sum_{x\in X}x-\binom{|X|}{2}.

We have
\operatorname{rank}(X^{\prime})=\operatorname{rank}(X)
if
X^{\prime}\sim X
, and the mapping

(7.1.6)

\left\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\right\}\mapsto[x_{m},x_{m-1}-1,\ldots,x_{2}-(m-2),x_{1}-(m-1)]

defines a bijection from set of equivalence classes of
\beta
-sets of rank
n
onto the set
\mathcal{P}(n)
.

Conversely, we associate to every partition
\lambda
the
\beta
-set
X_{\lambda}
defined by

(7.1.7)

X_{\lambda}:=\begin{cases}\{\lambda_{h},\lambda_{h-1}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{2}+(h-2),\lambda_{1}+h\},&\text{if $\ell(\lambda)+\ell(\lambda_{\infty})$ is even;}\cr\{0,\lambda_{h}+1,\lambda_{h-1}+2,\ldots,\lambda_{2}+(h-1),\lambda_{1}+h\},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is clear that the rank of
X_{\lambda}
is equal to
|\lambda|
. Moreover,
X_{\lambda^{\prime}}\sim X_{\lambda}
if
\lambda^{\prime}
is obtained
from
\lambda
by adding several
0
’s.

We set

(7.1.8)

X_{\lambda}^{0}:=\{x\in X_{\lambda}\,:\,x\equiv 0\text{ (${\mathrm{mod}}2$)}\}\quad\text{and}\quad X_{\lambda}^{1}:=\{x\in X_{\lambda}\,:\,x\equiv 1\text{ (${\mathrm{mod}}2$)}\}.

Definition 7.1.9
.

A
symbol
is an array
\Lambda
of the form

\Lambda=\left(\begin{matrix}A\cr B\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{r}\cr b_{1}&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{s}\end{matrix}\right)

of non-negative integers satisfying

a_{i}<a_{i+1}
and
b_{i}<b_{i+1}
for every
i
.

The
rank
of
\Lambda
is defined to be

\operatorname{rank}(\Lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{r+s-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor.

The
defect
of
\Lambda
is defined as

{\mathrm{def}}(\Lambda):=r-s.

A symbol
\Lambda
of defect
1
is said to be
distinguished
if

a_{1}\leq b_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{r-1}\leq b_{r-1}\leq a_{r}.

Similarly, a symbol
\Lambda
of defect
0
is said to be
distinguished
if

a_{1}\leq b_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq b_{r-1}\leq a_{r}\leq b_{r}.

There is an equivalence relation on symbols generated by the shift

(7.1.10)

\left(\begin{matrix}A\cr B\end{matrix}\right)\sim\left(\begin{matrix}\{0\}\cup\{A+1\}\cr\{0\}\cup\{B+1\}\end{matrix}\right).

The functions
\operatorname{rank}(\cdot)
and
{\mathrm{def}}(\cdot)
are invariant under the shift operation, hence are well-defined on the set
of symbol classes.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{n}
the set of symbol classes of rank

and by
\mathcal{S}_{n,d}
the set of symbols classes of rank

and defect

.
Each symbol class contains a unique symbol
\Lambda
such that

. Such a symbol is called

. From now on, unless otherwise specified, a symbol will always assumed to be reduced.

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
, we denote by
\Lambda^{\ast}
(resp.
\Lambda_{\ast}
) the first row (resp. second row)
of
\Lambda
, that is,
\Lambda^{\ast}:=A
and
\Lambda_{\ast}:=B
.

We associate to
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n)
the symbol
\Lambda_{\lambda}
defined by

(7.1.11)

\Lambda_{\lambda}:=\begin{cases}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X^{1}_{\lambda}\cr X^{0}_{\lambda}\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\text{if $\ell(\lambda_{\infty})$ is even},\\
\\
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}X^{0}_{\lambda}\cr X^{1}_{\lambda}\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\text{if $\ell(\lambda_{\infty})$ is odd}.\end{cases}

By
[
35
, Lemma 2.6]
, we have

(7.1.12)

{\mathrm{def}}(\Lambda_{\lambda})=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}\ell(\lambda_{\infty})&\text{if $\ell(\lambda_{\infty})$ is even};\\
-\ell(\lambda_{\infty})&\text{if $\ell(\lambda_{\infty})$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Let
\Upsilon\colon\mathcal{S}_{n}\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(n)
be the map defined by

(7.1.13)

\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{r}\cr b_{1}&b_{2}&\ldots&b_{s}\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto\left[\begin{matrix}a_{r}-(r-1)&a_{r-1}-(r-2)&\ldots&a_{2}-1&a_{1}\cr b_{s}-(s-1)&b_{s-1}-(s-2)&\ldots&b_{2}-1&b_{1}\end{matrix}\right].

We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{+}

\displaystyle=\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\mid\Upsilon(\Lambda_{*})^{t}\preccurlyeq\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime*})^{t},\ \Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime}_{*})^{t}\preccurlyeq\Upsilon(\Lambda^{*})^{t}\,\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\mid\Upsilon(\Lambda^{*})^{t}\preccurlyeq\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime}_{*})^{t},\ \Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime*})^{t}\preccurlyeq\Upsilon(\Lambda_{*})^{t}\,\};

-{\rm def}(\Lambda)+1&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)\neq 0$}\end{cases}\,\right\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}^{-}_{\mathrm{U},\mathrm{U}}

9.
A holographic duality

Classical error-correcting codes - used to transmit messages over noisy channels – have long been known to have rich connections to other branches of mathematics, including invariant theory, sphere packings, and modular forms.

Narain conformal field theories (CFTs) are a class of two-dimensional CFTs describing the geometry of the spacetime in string theory (see
[
31
,
32
]
). They are characterized by a set of vertex operators whose left- and right-moving momenta span so-called Narain lattices. Narain CFTs are bosonic, non-chiral, and modular invariant when the lattices are even, Lorentzian, and self-dual, respectively. While they are simple theories of free compact bosons specified by the
lattices, they have a rather large continuous moduli space and exhibit a rich structure such as symmetry enhancement and dualities. Recently, they have received renewed interest due to the revelation of their relation to quantum error-correcting codes (QECs). The CFTs whose Narain lattices are the even self-dual Lorentzian lattices built from qubit stabilizer codes are named
Narain code CFTs
.

In
[
9
]
, A. Dymarsky, J. Henriksson and B. McPeak studied the holographic correspondence between 3d “Chern–Simons gravity” and an
ensemble
of 2d Narain code CFTs, showing that the mathematical identity underlying this holographic duality can be understood and rigorously proven using the framework of the theta correspondence over finite fields. The physical properties of the CFT ensemble are linked to concepts from quantum information theory, specifically quantum stabilizer codes and classical even self-dual error-correcting codes. This rephrasing of holographic duality in terms of quantum codes provides new tools and perspectives for studying quantum gravity and how spacetime geometry might emerge from quantum information.
