Hadamard Hypercubes
Amin Bahmanian, Sho Suda

Abstract
. Although Hadamard matrices have been investigated since the nineteenth century, relatively little is known about their higher-dimensional analogues. In this paper, we introduce two constructions of Hadamard hypercubes. The first construction is derived from conference matrices, while the second is recursive, combining Hadamard matrices (and hypercubes) of smaller order with Latin hypercubes. The former approach draws on the theory of association schemes on triples, whereas the latter yields applications to the construction of higher-dimensional symmetric designs.

To construct Hadamard cubes, we study a three-dimensional analogue of the relationship between association schemes and Hadamard matrices. We begin by extending several connections between symmetric ASTs and regular two-graphs to the skew-symmetric setting, namely to skew-symmetric ASTs and regular skew two-graphs.
Note that any symmetric or skew-symmetric conference matrix gives rise to a symmetric or skew-symmetric AST, respectively.
We then determine which linear combinations of the adjacency matrices of these ASTs produce Hadamard cubes.
We remark that the Hadamard cubes constructed in Theorem

Association schemes on triples

We first define (commutative) association schemes

]
.

An association scheme is

Now, we define association schemes on triples which were originally

(I)

\displaystyle R_{0}=\{(x,x,x)\mid x\in X\},

\displaystyle R_{1}=\{(x,y,y)\mid x,y\in X,x\neq y\},

\displaystyle R_{2}=\{(x,y,x)\mid x,y\in X,x\neq y\},

\displaystyle R_{3}=\{(x,x,y)\mid x,y\in X,x\neq y\};

(II)

\displaystyle A_{i}A_{j}A_{k}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}p_{ijk}^{\ell}A_{\ell},\quad\mbox{ for }i,j,k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}.

\displaystyle JJA_{k}=(\delta_{k0}+(v-1)\delta_{k3})(A_{0}+A_{3})+n_{k}^{(3)}\left(J-A_{0}-A_{3}\right),\quad\mbox{ for }k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}.

Regular Two-Graphs and Symmetric ASTs

Two-graphs are 3-uniform hypergraphs in which every 4-subset of points contains an even number of hyeredges; they should not be confused with 2-graphs in graph theory. Two-graphs play a crucial role in this paper. The study of two-graphs was initiated by Higman, motivated by investigations of Conway’s sporadic simple group

Theorem 5.3
.

For

, there exists a line-rainbow

Proof.

For convenience, we assume the set of symbols is

\displaystyle L(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q_{j}n^{j},\quad i_{1},\dots,i_{d}\in[n],

.
Recall that a line is obtained by fixing

\ell
-th coordinate, vary. To show that

is line-rainbow, there are two cases to consider.

•

\ell\in[d-r]
: Suppose, to the contrary, that for
a\neq b
,

Then, we have

Then, we have

\left[a-(i_{1}+\dots+i_{d-r})\right]_{n}n^{d-\ell}=\left[b-(i_{1}+\dots+i_{d-r})\right]_{n}n^{d-\ell},

, so we have

, which is a contradiction.

Now, we show that

is an

contains each symbol equally often. Let

be a symbol. We can write

with

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}q_{j}n^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p_{j}n^{j}.

Equation (

\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(q_{j}-p_{j})n^{j}=
which has a solution if and only if

\displaystyle i_{d-j}-(i_{1}+\dots+i_{d-r})=p_{j}\pmod{n},\quad 0\leq j\leq r-1.

To complete the proof, there are two cases to consider.

•

\ell\in[d-r]

•

\ell\in[d]\setminus[d-r]

An
(n,d)
-orthogonal family
is a collection
\mathcal{S}
of

\pm 1
-hypercubes of order

The following lemma provides a construction of

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
H
be a Hadamard matrix of order
n
, and for
\ell\in[n]
, let
C_{\ell}
be a
d
-dimensional hypercubes with

C_{\ell}(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}H(\ell,i_{j})\quad\text{ for }i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\in[n].

Then
\mathcal{S}:=\{C_{\ell}\mid\ell\in[n]\}
forms an
(n,d)
-orthogonal family.

Proof.

Let
i\in[d]
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
i=1
.
To prove (O1), let
A,B\in\mathcal{S}
, and
a,b\in[n]
. There exist distinct
k,\ell\in[n]
such that
A=C_{k},B=C_{\ell}
.
We have

\displaystyle A_{1;a}\cdot B_{1;b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}C_{k}(a,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\,C_{\ell}(b,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}\Bigl(H(k,a)\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(k,x_{j})\Bigr)\Bigl(H(\ell,b)\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(\ell,x_{j})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=H(k,a)H(\ell,b)\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(k,x_{j})H(\ell,x_{j})

\displaystyle=H(k,a)H(\ell,b)\prod_{j=2}^{d}\sum_{x=1}^{n}H(k,x)H(\ell,x)

\displaystyle=H(k,a)H(\ell,b)(H_{1;k}\cdot H_{1;\ell})^{d-1}=0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
H
is a Hadamard matrix. To prove (O2), let
a,b\in[n]
with
a\neq b
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in\mathcal{S}}A_{1;a}\cdot A_{1;b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}C_{\ell}(a,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})C_{\ell}(b,x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}\Big(H(\ell,a)\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(\ell,x_{j})\Big)\Big(H(\ell,b)\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(\ell,x_{j})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}H(\ell,a)H(\ell,b)\prod_{j=2}^{d}H(\ell,x_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}=1}^{n}H(\ell,a)H(\ell,b)

\displaystyle=n^{d-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}H(\ell,a)H(\ell,b)=0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
H
is a Hadamard matrix.
∎
