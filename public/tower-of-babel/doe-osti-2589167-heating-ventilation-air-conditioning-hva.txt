Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This project proposes to integrate the NREL-developed OpenStudio (OS) and its accompanying
Analytical Framework (OSAF) into Autodesk’s (ADSK’s) market leading suite of building
information modeling (BIM) tools. As a result, BIM users will gain access to advanced (but user-
friendly) building energy modeling (BEM), calibration, and design optimization capabilities.
Following integration, the BIM software will automatically generate a consistent OS-based BEM
model, allowing users to develop projects in an integrated manner, moving back and forth
between architectural and engineering perspectives without redundancy or data loss, saving time
and effort.

 • Adds new capability to the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Enhances the laboratory’s core competencies
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies

Purpose

This project proposes to integrate the NREL-developed OpenStudio (OS) and its accompanying
Analytical Framework (OSAF) into Autodesk’s market leading suite of building information
modeling (BIM) tools. As a result, BIM users will gain access to advanced (but user-friendly)
building energy modeling (BEM), calibration, and design optimization capabilities. Following
integration, the BIM software will automatically generate a consistent OS-based BEM model,
allowing users to develop projects in an integrated manner, moving back and forth between
architectural and engineering perspectives without redundancy or data loss, saving time and
effort. This project will:

 • Alleviate the lack of BIM-BEM integration, identified by DOE’s Buildings Technology
 Office (BTO) as one of the largest market barriers to use of BEM in the design process
 • Help BTO achieve building energy use intensity reduction targets by giving industry
 practitioners the tools they need to design low energy buildings
 • Improve industry confidence in BEM accuracy

Project Plan.

The overall plan for the project is for NREL to work with Autodesk to establish a persistent
connection between the design and analytical environments to support widespread adoption of
building energy modeling. Details of the plan are provided below:

Early Stage Analysis.

At the earliest stages of design, very little is known about a building besides an expected
programming that details the presence of different space types. A variety of building forms may
be considered, different construction types, window-to-wall ratios, lighting power densities,
HVAC systems and so on. For this reason, it will be possible to examine a large portion of the
possible parameter space using the OSAFs sampling capabilities; this analysis will not only
provide a range of Energy Use Intensity (EUIs), but also provide a means to examine which
design parameter choices lead to better energy performance. As the design moves from the
preliminary design (PD), more parameters become known and the range of uncertain parameters
tighten as the design progresses, reducing the project’s likely EUI range. BEM analysis will help
lower the final EUI range as much as possible.

 Figure 3: EUI Projections Throughout Design Phases

The project intends to use existing and new NREL-authored Measures that allow a design team
to customize the parameter space they wish to consider. Of high priority is development of a
series of Measures focused on detailed HVAC analysis. These Measures need to be written,
validated to make sure they produce expected results, and then further tested directly with the
Revit generated models to ensure compatibility between the BIM and BEM models. In addition
to providing a greater off-the-shelf selection of parameters to explore than other commercial
products, the project will allow for users to develop and contribute their own Measures,
providing unlimited extensibility.

Autodesk’s technical focus will be on providing market strategies related to direction and outreach, and
testing the framework to ensure stability of the platform. Autodesk will also be responsible for ensuring
compliance with local data protection requirements for the markets they serve.

 4

Detailed Design.

As stated above, the design BIM model geometry has an analytical analog that encapsulates all
the fields required for simulation while retaining a reference to its equivalent in the design
model. The intent is to carry this pattern throughout the various design objects in Revit, which
will require collaboration between the Autodesk and OpenStudio teams to generate such
mappings for each design object. Once created, this will allow the design model to generate the
analytical model whose results then feedback to the design model for the purposes of mechanical
design. As ductwork, piping and other physical design features of a building are laid out, this
will then allow the parameters of the physical system (i.e. static pressure in the duct work) to
feed to energy model for a more exacting analysis. This detailed design stage will not only
improve the accuracy of analysis for new construction but will enable as built BIM models to
seed the BEM model needed for calibration purposes, which can lead to much more accurate
energy efficiency measure (EEM) analysis.

Task Descriptions

The project team has identified a set of tasks specific to each party, as well as a variety of tasks
that will take collaboration between Autodesk and NREL. The general split is for Autodesk to

Revit Systems Analysis

As a result of this CRADA, Autodesk’s desktop software product Revit added a
feature called Systems Analysis that allows users to run an annual energy
simulation to estimate the energy use of their overall building design, or run
mechanical sizing to estimate the sizes of their HVAC system components. These
features are contained in the OpenStudio Command Line Interface (CLI) for Revit 3,
developed by NREL, that integrates the following into a standalone Windows-compatible
installer that is included with Revit installations.

 • A customized version of EnergyPlus4
 • The OpenStudio CLI from the OpenStudio Software Development Kit (SDK)5
 • OpenStudio Measures6
 • OpenStudio Workflows7

Insight

As a result of this CRADA, Autodesk’s cloud software product Insight added the ability to
perform annual energy simulations using the following DOE tools developed by NREL. Insight
previously used a different simulation engine.

 • OpenStudio SDK8, an unmodified release of the OpenStudio SDK that is compatible
 with Autodesk’s cloud platform.
 • OpenStudio Insight Measures9, a collection of OpenStudio Measures for Insight and an
 OpenStudio Workflow file to run simulations

3.2: Develop measures for systems where they don't exist

NREL developed a single OpenStudio Measure (gbxml_hvac_import)13, based on the prioritized
list of systems in Task 3.1, to enable Revit to produce a corresponding BEM with a user-
specified HVAC system type.

In the early stages of development, NREL relied on Autodesk’s software regression tests to

improvements. Later, NREL developed a unit testing framework that covered all system types
enabled by the gbxml_hvac_import Measure14.

3.4: Develop infrastructure skeleton for parametric analysis from Revit

Autodesk’s Insight cloud product, which connects their desktop product, Revit, to the cloud for

4.1: Deploy OSAF on desired cloud platform

4.2: Parallelize each simulation for faster runtime

Autodesk has a cloud compute platform built on top of AWS that allows for large scaling of

spun back up and the simulations are rerun.

Task 5 Result:

5.1: Perform testing for stability and engineering quality

Autodesk developed automated testing for Systems Analysis to check for stability and quality
whenever there are changes to it. For example, whenever a bug fix, new feature, or new version
is committed to the repository the continuous integration (CI) system automatically runs tests.
The CI system includes the following:

 1. Test fixtures: a suite of approximately 40 models to run the tests on
 2. Integration tests: software tests to test that the parts of the software product work together
 after changes are introduced
 3. Regression tests: software tests to test that the performance of the software product as a
 whole does not significantly change after changes are introduced

5.3: Develop measures to map Revit data to BEM data

NREL developed the following OpenStudio Measures to map Revit data (via the gbXML
schema) to BEM data17. These Measures collectively enable the translation of a Revit BIM to an
OpenStudio BEM, which is not possible using OpenStudio’s built-in gbXML import feature
(contained within the import_gbxml Measure). In particular, the advanced_import_gbxml and
gbxml_hvac_import Measures create complex building attributes in OpenStudio, such as
schedules and HVAC systems, from a gbXML, that enable Revit users to successfully perform
annual energy simulations and mechanical sizing.

 1. import_gbxml
 2. advanced_import_gbxml
 3. gbxml_hvac_import
 4. gbxml_to_openstudio_cleanup
 5. set_simulation_control
