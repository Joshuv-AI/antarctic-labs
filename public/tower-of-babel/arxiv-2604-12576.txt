Detecting entanglement from few partial transpose moments and their decay via weight enumerators
Daniel Miller, Jens Eisert

Abstract
In light of the rapid experimental development of quantum devices, tools for estimating quantum properties, particularly entanglement, must be improved to keep pace. The p 3 p_{3} -PPT criterion is an experimentally viable relaxation of the well-known positive partial transposition (PPT) criterion for the certification of quantum entanglement. Recently, it has been generalized to various families of entanglement criteria based on the PT moments p k = Tr ⁡ [ ( ρ Γ ) k ] p_{k}=\Tr[(\rho^{\Gamma})^{k}] , where ρ Γ \rho^{\Gamma} denotes the partially transposed density matrix of a quantum state ρ \rho . While most of these generalizations are strictly more powerful than the p 3 p_{3} -PPT criterion, their m m -th level versions usually rely on the availability of p k p_{k} for all moment orders k ≤ m k\leq m . Here, we show that one can alternatively compare any three PT moments of orders k < l < m k<l<m , which can significantly reduce experimental overheads. More precisely, we show that any state satisfying p l > p k x ​ p m 1 − x p_{l}>p_{k}^{x}p_{m}^{1-x} must be entangled, where x = ( m − l ) / ( m − k ) x=(m-l)/(m-k) . Using the example of locally depolarized Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states, a family of states that has been used as a test for the quantum character of large-scale states prepared on quantum computers, we identify the most promising versions of these three-moment criteria and compare their performance with a broad range of entanglement criteria. In the case of globally depolarized stabilizer states, we prove that having access to p k p_{k} for k ≤ 5 k\leq 5 is sufficient to reproduce the full PPT criterion. More generally, we show that the Stieltjes- m m criterion is as powerful as the PPT criterion whenever ρ Γ \rho^{\Gamma} has no more than ( m + 1 ) / 2 (m+1)/2 distinct eigenvalues. Finally, we introduce a notion of quantum weight enumerators that capture the decay of p k p_{k} under local white noise for arbitrary quantum states and illustrate this concept for an absolutely maximally entangled (AME) state. Our results contribute to the growing body of literature on higher-moment PPT relaxations and modern applications of weight enumerators in quantum error correction and information theory.

II
A simple three-moment relaxation of the PPT criterion

As a warm-up, let us rederive our
2\times 2
relaxation of the Stieltjes-
m
criterion in Eq. (
6
) from scratch.
Assume that
\rho^{\Gamma}
is PSD.
Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\Tr[K^{\dagger}M]^{2}\leq\Tr[K^{\dagger}K]\Tr[M^{\dagger}M]
to the two matrices
K=(\rho^{\Gamma})^{k/2}
and
M=(\rho^{\Gamma})^{m/2}
yields
p_{(k+m)/2}^{2}\leq p_{k}p_{m}
, which finishes the proof.
Note that the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is a special case (with
r=s=2
) of Hölder’s inequality
(
8
, Eq. (IV.43))
, which states that

\displaystyle\Tr\left[((KM)^{\dagger}(KM))^{q/2}\right]^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq\Tr\left[(K^{\dagger}K)^{{r/2}}\right]^{2/r}

(7)

\displaystyle\times\Tr\left[(M^{\dagger}M)^{{s/2}}\right]^{2/s}

for all
1\leq q,r,s\leq\infty
with
1/q=1/r+1/s
and all matrices
K
and
M
.
This suggests the following generalization of Eq. (
6
).

Theorem 1

(The
(k,l,m)
-PPT criterion)
.

Let
k<l<m
be integers and define
x=(m-l)/(m-k)
.
If
p_{l}>p_{k}^{x}p_{m}^{1-x}
, then
\rho
is NPT entangled.

Proof.

We prove the contraposition. Assume that
\rho
is PPT.
Then, the fractional powers
K=(\rho^{\Gamma})^{k/2}
and
M=(\rho^{\Gamma})^{m/2}
are well defined and Hermitian.
Inserting them—together with
r=2l/k
and
s=2l/m
—into Inequality (
7
)
yields
p_{l}\leq p_{k}^{x}p_{m}^{1-x}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

III
Quantifying entanglement via PT moments

While the emphasis of this work is on refining entanglement criteria as such,
we show in this section that knowledge about PT moments also leads to quantitative estimates of entanglement.
This reasoning follows the intuition that if an entanglement criterion is strongly violated, then the underlying entanglement should also be significant in a quantitative sense.
Such quantitative entanglement witnesses
22
;
7
often provide lower bounds on entanglement measure from experimental data in a substantially more efficient manner than what is achievable via quantum state tomography.

In the context of PT moments, previous work
74
showed that it is possible to derive lower bounds for the logarithmic negativity,

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]=\log_{2}\left(\|\rho^{\Gamma}\|_{1}\right),

(8)

where

\|A\|_{q}=\Tr[(A^{\dagger}A)^{q/2}]^{1/q}

denotes the Schatten-
q
norm of a matrix
A
for
q\geq 1
.
Note that the logarithmic negativity is a (non-convex) entanglement measure that upper bounds the distillable entanglement
89
;
25
;
80
;
64
.
In analogy with
p_{m}=\Tr\left[(\rho^{\Gamma})^{m}\right]
, one defines

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{m}=\|\rho^{\Gamma}\|_{m}^{m}\,.

(9)

By mapping the problem to a classical probability distribution and leveraging the monotonicity of Rényi entropies, one can show
74
that

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]\geq\frac{\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{m})}{2-m}+\frac{1-m}{2-m}\log_{2}(p_{2})\,.

(10)

If
m
is even, then
\tilde{p}_{m}=p_{m}
, and the lower bound in Eq. (
10
) can be efficiently estimated using the
m
-cycle test.
Our next result generalizes this lower bound, which is recovered in the special case of
l=2
.

Theorem 2

(Quantitative lower bounds)
.

Let
l
and
m
be integers and define
x=(m-l)/(m-1)
.
Then

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]\geq\frac{\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{m})}{x}+\frac{1-x}{x}\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{l})\,.

(11)

Proof.

Replacing
\rho^{\Gamma}
in the proof of Thm.
1
by the matrix
|\rho^{\Gamma}|=\sqrt{(\rho^{\Gamma})^{\dagger}\rho^{\Gamma}}
, which is positive for all states
\rho
,
and specializing to the case
k=1

yields
\tilde{p}_{l}\leq\tilde{p}_{1}^{x}\tilde{p}_{m}^{1-x}
.
Thus,
\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{1})\geq\frac{\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{m})}{x}+\frac{1-x}{x}\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{l})
, and

E_{\mathcal{N}}[\rho]=\log_{2}(\tilde{p}_{1})
finishes the proof.
∎

IV
The Stieltjes criterion is equivalent to the PPT criterion

In Ref.
59
,
it has been pointed out that for every
n
-qubit state
\rho
that is NPT entangled across a bipartition
A|B
,
there exists an order
3\leq m\leq 2^{n}
for which the Descartes-
m
criterion detects this entanglement.
On the other hand, the question of whether the Stieltjes-
m
criterion (for a sufficiently large value of
m
) is as strong as the PPT criterion has, to our knowledge, not been addressed before.
Our next result answers this in the affirmative.

Theorem 3

(The Stieltjes criterion detects all NPT states)
.

Denote by
N
the number of distinct eigenvalues of
\rho^{\Gamma}
.
If
\rho
is NPT entangled and
m\geq 2N-1
, then
H_{m}
is not PSD.

Proof.

Denote the eigenvalues of
\rho^{\Gamma}
by
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}
and their multiplicities by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{N}
.
Given
\lambda_{1}<0
, we need to construct
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
, with
\mathbf{c}^{\text{T}}H_{m}\mathbf{c}<0
,
where
M={(m+1)/2}
is the dimension of the Hankel matrix
H_{m}
from Eq. (
4
).
Consider the polynomial
f(x)=\prod_{i=2}^{N}(x-\lambda_{i})

and denote its coefficients by
c_{0},\ldots,c_{N-1}
.
By assumption,
M\geq N
, so we can define the vector
\mathbf{c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{N-1},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
.
With this choice, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\text{T}}H_{m}\mathbf{c}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}c_{i}c_{j}p_{i+j+1}\,.

(12)

Since
p_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mu_{l}\lambda^{k}_{l}
, we can further write

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}^{\text{T}}H_{m}\mathbf{c}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mu_{l}\lambda_{l}\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}c_{i}\lambda^{i}c_{j}\lambda^{j}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\mu_{l}\lambda_{l}f(\lambda_{l})^{2}\,.

(13)

By construction of
f
, we have
f(\lambda_{l})=0
for all
l\geq 2
.
Hence, only one term in Eq. (
13
) survives, namely

\mathbf{c}^{\text{T}}H_{m}\mathbf{c}=\mu_{1}\lambda_{1}f(\lambda_{1})^{2}
.
Since the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}
were chosen to be distinct, we have
f(\lambda_{1})\neq 0
, which implies
f(\lambda_{1})^{2}>0
.
Finally, we have
\lambda_{1}<0
with multiplicity
\mu_{1}\geq 1
,
which implies
\mathbf{c}^{\text{T}}H_{m}\mathbf{c}<0
and finishes the proof.
∎

Let us apply Thm.
3
to show that the Stieltjes-5 criterion is as strong as the PPT criterion in the simple case of a globally depolarized stabilizer state.

\lambda_{0}=\varepsilon/2^{n}

The eigenvalue

Corollary 4

(Low moments suffice for stabilizer states with global noise)
.

The Stieltjes-5 criterion detects entanglement in
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{glob}}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi_{\mathrm{stab}}]
for all values of
\varepsilon<1-1/(2^{n-r}+1)

Proof.

Having already established the spectrum of
\mathcal{D}^{\text{glob}}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi]^{\Gamma}

N=3

As such, Thm.
3
applies with

.
∎

We have just established in Cor.
4
that the case of global white noise is, as usual, rather unspectacular.
As such, let us turn to the more interesting setting of local noise.
We define the single-qubit depolarizing channel,
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}
, of error strength
\varepsilon
by
\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}[\rho]=(1-\varepsilon)\rho+\varepsilon\Tr[\rho]\mathbbm{1}/2
.
As an illustrative example, consider the
n
-qubit

(GHZ) state,
whose state vector is defined as

\ket{\text{GHZ}^n}=\frac{\ket{0}^{\otimes n}+\ket{1}^{\otimes n}}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(14)

Such GHZ states have been used to probe the quantum nature of large-scale superconducting quantum computers involving 120 qubits in the recent past

We denote the corresponding projector by
\Psi_{\text{GHZ}}=\ket{\text{GHZ}^n}\!\bra{\text{GHZ}^n}

For simplicity, we assume that the number of qubits

For the abstract PPT criterion, early work

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\text{max}}^{\text{PPT}}=1-(2^{2-2/n}+1)^{-1/2}\,.

(15)

In our situation, the PPT criterion is known to be both necessary and sufficient for entanglement

In other words,
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi_{\mathrm{GHZ}}]

A|B

\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\text{PPT}}_{\text{max}}

We can invoke Thm.

to establish that the Stieltjes criterion fully recovers this threshold without requiring exponentially many moments of

Corollary 5

(Stieltjes for locally depolarized GHZ states)

For

, the Stieltjes-

criterion detects entanglement in
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\Psi_{\mathrm{GHZ}}]

Since we assume that the number of qubits

is even and that the bipartition
A|B
is balanced, we can directly use the eigenvalue spectrum of

as reported in the literature

The eigenvalues are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n-j}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{j}+\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{j}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n-j}\right)

(16)

with multiplicities
\mu_{j}=\binom{n}{j}-2\delta_{j,n/2}
,
as well as

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n/2}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{n/2}\pm\frac{(1-\varepsilon)^{n}}{2}

(17)

with multiplicities
\mu_{\pm}=1

Thus, we have no more than
N=n+3

and Thm.
3
finishes the proof.

Note that the moment bound in Cor.

For small system sizes, we find that significantly lower moments already suffice to fully reproduce the PPT criterion: for

However, the precise relationship between these and other common entanglement criteria remains unclear.
In this section, we address this question by investigating the important case of locally depolarized GHZ states.
Importantly, applying each criterion (except for the fidelity criterion) does not rely on prior knowledge of the exact form of the state.

As in Cor.

Since GHZ states are well-studied in the literature, we begin our discussion by reviewing various entanglement criteria as well as the experimental state-of-the-art.

Next, we show—for general

-qubit GHZ states—how to compute the theoretical threshold
\varepsilon_{\text{max}}^{\text{purity}}
below which the purity criterion certifies entanglement across a balanced bipartition.
We leverage the concept of Shor-Laflamme enumerators,
which have their origins in the theory of quantum error correction
69

They are defined as

\displaystyle a_{i}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}\\
\mathrm{wt}(P)=i\end{subarray}}\Tr[\rho P]^{2}\,,

(20)

for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}

where the weight
\mathrm{wt}(P)
of a Pauli operator

is defined as the number of non-identity tensor factors.
For a stabilizer state
\Psi=\ket{\psi}\!\bra{\psi}

\mathcal{S}

a_{i}[\Psi]=\#\{S\in\mathcal{S}\ |\ \mathrm{wt}(S)=i\}/2^{n}
,
i.e., the probability that a uniformly random stabilizer element has weight

In particular, we have
a_{i}[\Psi_{\text{GHZ}}]=(\binom{n}{i}\delta_{i,\text{even}}+\delta_{i,n})/2^{n}
for the
n
-qubit GHZ state

.
Since Shor-Laflamme enumerators generally decay as
a_{i}[\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes n}[\Psi]]=(1-\varepsilon)^{2i}a_{i}[\Psi]
,
one can write the global purity of the noisy state as

\displaystyle\Tr\left[(\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes n}[\Psi])^{2}\right]=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}[\Psi](1-\varepsilon)^{2i}\,.

(21)

Similarly, the averaged (over all marginals of size
n/2
) subsystem purity (see Eq. (
19

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{n/2}[\rho]=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{n/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\\
\#A=n/2\end{subarray}}\Tr\left[\rho_{A}^{2}\right]\,,

(22)

which is also known as Rains’ unitary enumerator
66
of order
n/2

can be expressed through Shor-Laflamme enumerators via

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{n/2}\left[\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes n}[\Psi]\right]=\frac{2^{{n}/{2}}}{\binom{n}{{n}/{2}}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n-i}{{n}/{2}}a_{i}[\Psi](1-\varepsilon)^{2i}\,.

(23)

In Fig.

(24)

is entangled across each bipartition
A|B

VI
Weight enumerators for the decay of PT moments under local depolarizing noise

In the previous section, we analyzed the performance of moment-based entanglement criteria in a concrete setting.
We now take a more general perspective and investigate how PT moments evolve under local depolarizing noise.
To this end, we introduce a family of quantum weight enumerators (QWEs) that capture this decay and naturally extend the well-known Shor–Laflamme quantum weight enumerators, which have been first defined in the context of quantum error correction
69
.

Let
\rho
be an
n
-qubit state, and let
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\rho]
denote its evolution under local depolarizing noise.
We can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\rho^{\Gamma}]=\tfrac{1}{2^{n}}\hskip-11.38109pt\sum_{P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}}\hskip-11.38109pt\vartheta(P)\Tr[\rho P](1-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{wt}(P)}P\,,

(26)

where for an
n
-qubit Pauli operator,
P=P^{(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes P^{(n)}
, we define

\displaystyle\vartheta(P)=(-1)^{\#\{\,a\in A\ |\ P^{(a)}=Y\}}\,.

(27)

For multiple Pauli operators,
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}
,
we similarly define

\vartheta(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\vartheta(P_{i})

as well as

\displaystyle\varphi(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\Tr[\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}]\in\{0,\pm 1,\pm\texttt{i}\}\,.

(28)

With this notation at hand, we can write

\displaystyle p_{k}[(\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\rho])^{\Gamma}]=\sum_{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}}\frac{\vartheta(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})\varphi(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})}{2^{n(k-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{wt}(P_{i})}\Tr[P_{i}\rho]\,.

(29)

Stratifying Eq. (
29
) by all possible values that
\vartheta(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
,
\varphi(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})
, and
\mathrm{wt}(P_{i})
can assume, we finally obtain

\displaystyle p_{k}[(\mathcal{D}^{\otimes n}_{\varepsilon}[\rho])^{\Gamma}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\{\pm 1\}\\
\phi\in\{\pm 1,\pm\texttt{i}\}\end{subarray}}\theta\phi\sum_{w=0}^{nk}(1-\varepsilon)^{w}c_{w}^{(k,\theta,\phi)}[\rho]\,,

(30)

where we have introduced the real-valued QWEs

\displaystyle c_{w}^{(k,\theta,\phi)}[\rho]=\frac{1}{2^{n(k-1)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}\\
\vartheta(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})=\theta\\
\varphi(P_{1},\ldots,P_{k})=\phi\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{wt}(P_{i})=w\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Tr[\rho P_{i}]\,,

(31)

which implicitly, via Eq. (
27
), depend on the bipartition
A|B
with respect to which the partial transpose is taken.
For
k=2
, our QWEs are directly related to the well-known
Shor-Laflamme QWEs from Eq. (
20
)
via

c_{w}^{(2,\theta,\phi)}[\rho]=\delta_{\theta,+1}\delta_{\phi,+1}\delta_{w,2i}\times a_{i}[\rho]
,
regardless of the bipartition.
For
k\geq 3
, the QWEs in Eq. (
31
) belong to the general class of local polynomial invariants
67
, which is less well understood than the
k=2
case.

Let us investigate the important special case of stabilizer states in greater detail.
For every
n
-qubit stabilizer state
\Psi_{\text{stab}}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}S/2^{n}
,
where
\mathcal{S}
denotes the stabilizer group,
the condition
-\mathbbm{1}\not\in\mathcal{S}
implies that
c_{w}^{(k,\theta,\phi)}=0
is trivial whenever
\phi\in\{-1,\pm\texttt{i}\}
.
In the opposite case,
we have
c_{w}^{(k,\theta,+1)}=C_{w}^{(k,\theta)}/2^{n(k-1)}
, where

\displaystyle C_{w}^{(k,\theta)}=\#\Biggl\{

\displaystyle S_{2},\ldots,S_{k}\in\mathcal{S}\ \Bigg|\ \vartheta(\textstyle\prod_{i=2}^{k}S_{i},S_{2},\ldots,S_{k})=\theta,

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}(\textstyle\prod_{i=2}^{k}S_{i})+\sum_{i=2}^{k}\mathrm{wt}(S_{i})=w\Biggr\}\,.

(32)

Therefore, Eq. (
30
) simplifies to

\displaystyle p_{k}[(\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes n}[\Psi_{\text{stab}}])]=\sum_{w=0}^{nk}\frac{C_{w}^{(k,+)}-C_{w}^{(k,-)}}{2^{n(k-1)}}(1-\varepsilon)^{w}\,.

(33)

In the important special case of a trivial bipartition
A|B
with
A=\diameter
,
we have
C_{w}^{(k,-)}=0
and, therefore, the moments of
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{D}_{\varepsilon}^{\otimes n}[\Psi_{\text{stab}}]
can be written as

\displaystyle\Tr\left[\rho_{\varepsilon}^{k}\right]=\frac{1}{2^{n(k-1)}}\sum_{w=0}^{nk}C_{w}^{(k,+)}(1-\varepsilon)^{w}\,.

(34)

Using Eq. (
32
), the QWEs
C_{w}^{(k,\theta)}
, which appear in Eq. (
33
), can in principle be computed via an exhaustive search over all
(k-1)
-tuples of stabilizers.
However, this approach leads to an algorithm with runtime that is exponential in both
n
and
k
.
As we show next, by leveraging the theory of Fourier analysis on finite Abelian groups
76
, the exponential runtime in
k
can be avoided.

Consider a fixed stabilizer state with stabilizer group,
\mathcal{S}
, and a fixed bipartition,
A|B
, which determines the function
\vartheta
from Eq. (
27
).
For each
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we define the two functions
f^{\pm}_{z}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, where
f^{+}_{z}(S)=z^{\mathrm{wt}(S)}
and
f^{-}_{z}(S)=\vartheta(S)z^{\mathrm{wt}(S)}
.
Evaluating the
k
-fold discrete convolution of
f_{z}^{\pm}
at the identity element
I\in\mathcal{S}
yields

\displaystyle(f_{z}^{\pm})^{\scalebox{1.0}{$\ast$}k}(I)=\sum_{w=0}^{nk}(C_{w}^{(k,+)}\pm C_{w}^{(k,-)})z^{w}\,.

(35)

The Fourier transform,
\mathcal{F}
, over the finite group
\mathcal{S}
turns convolutions into pointwise products, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}((f_{z}^{\pm})^{\scalebox{1.0}{$\ast$}k})=(\mathcal{F}(f^{\pm}_{z}))^{k}
, where
\widehat{f}^{\pm}_{z}=\mathcal{F}(f^{\pm}_{z})
are functions from the group of characters,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}=\{\chi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\{\pm 1\}\ |\ \chi\,\text{ is a group homomorphism}\}\,,

(36)

to
\mathbb{C}
.
More precisely, for each
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
, we have
\widehat{f}_{z}^{+}(\chi)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\chi(S)z^{\mathrm{wt}(S)}
and
\widehat{f}^{-}_{z}(\chi)=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\chi(S)\vartheta(S)z^{\mathrm{wt}(S)}
.
Note that
\vartheta
itself is not a character (unless
A=\diameter
) because
\vartheta(X_{a})=\vartheta(Z_{a})=1
but
\vartheta(X_{a}Z_{a})=-1
for each qubit
a\in A
.
Let

\{\alpha^{\pm}_{1}(z),\ldots,\alpha^{\pm}_{N^{\pm}}(z)\}=\{\widehat{f}^{\pm}_{z}(\chi)\ |\ \chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}\}

(37)

be the sets of
N^{\pm}
polynomials that occur,
and denote their multiplicities by
m^{\pm}_{i}=\#\{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}\ |\ \widehat{f}^{\pm}_{z}(\chi)=\alpha^{\pm}_{i}(z)\}
.
The inversion formula, see e.g., Thm. 2 in Chap. 10 of Ref.
76
, allows us to write

\displaystyle(f^{\pm}_{z})^{\scalebox{1.0}{$\ast$}k}(S)=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\widehat{f}^{\pm}_{z}(\chi))^{k}\chi(S).

(38)

for each
S\in\mathcal{S}
.
Evaluating Eq. (
38
) at
S=I
finally yields

\displaystyle\sum_{w=0}^{nk}(C_{w}^{(k,+)}\pm C_{w}^{(k,-)})z^{w}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{\pm}}m_{i}^{\pm}\times(\alpha_{i}^{\pm}(z))^{k}\,.

(39)

We can extract
C_{w}^{(k,+)}+C_{w}^{(k,-)}
and
C_{w}^{(k,+)}-C_{w}^{(k,-)}

from Eq. (
39
) by taking the coefficient in front of
z^{w}
.
This allows us to compute
C_{w}^{(k,+)}
and
C_{w}^{(k,-)}
from the
m^{\pm}_{i}
and
\alpha_{i}^{\pm}
, which themselves do not depend on
k
.

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have taken steps to make entanglement detection
more practical and applicable to real-life experimental platforms. Concretely, we have
introduced a conceptually simple generalization of the
p_{3}
-PPT entanglement criterion (Thm.
1
), which we call the
(k,l,m)
-PPT criterion.
This approach allows one to compare arbitrary triples of partial transpose (PT) moments, rather than requiring access to all moments up to order
m
, as in previous approaches.
Then, we have generalized previous lower bounds on the logarithmic negativity, which solely rely on a few PT
moments (Thm.
2
).
While completeness of moment-based criteria has been discussed using Descartes-type conditions (based on Newton identities), it has not been established whether the Stieltjes hierarchy (Hankel positivity) alone is complete at finite order.
Our result (Thm.
3
) shows that for spectra with
N
distinct eigenvalues,
the Stieltjes conditions already detect NPT at order
2N-1
.
As important consequences, we showed that for globally depolarized stabilizer states and locally depolarized GHZ states, respectively, the PPT criterion is equivalent to the Stieltjes-
m
criterion with
m=5
(Cor.
4
) and
m=2n+5
(Cor.
5
).
Through a detailed analysis of locally depolarized GHZ states, we identified optimal choices of moment orders and showed that our
(3,4,5)
-PPT criterion achieves performance comparable to the more demanding Stieltjes-
5
criterion.
For carefully selected triples, we furthermore found that the
(k,l,m)
-criteria retain the favorable noise-scaling behavior of the Stieltjes-
m
criterion (even if
m
is constant), outperforming traditional approaches like fidelity- and purity-based methods in relevant regimes.
In the second part of this paper, we introduced a new notion of quantum weight enumerators as a unifying framework to describe the decay of PT moments under local depolarizing noise, providing both analytical insight and computational advantages.
Overall, our results highlight that meaningful relaxations of the PPT criterion can be achieved with fewer experimental resources than previously expected, while maintaining strong detection capabilities. This opens promising avenues for scalable entanglement certification in near-term quantum devices, particularly in regimes where access to certain high-order moments is possible but expensive.

IX
Outlook

For the examples analyzed in this work, we find that—for a given maximal moment order—the Stieltjes criterion consistently outperforms the Descartes criterion.
It would be interesting to determine whether this holds in general, either by proving its superiority or by constructing a counterexample.
On the computational side, recent tensor-network methods have been developed to efficiently compute Shor–Laflamme weight enumerator distributions
16
;
17
;
13
;
62
.
A complementary line of work leverages generating-function techniques for their analytical computation
79
.
It is conceivable that some of these approaches could be extended to compute the quantum weight enumerators introduced here at larger and more physically relevant system sizes.
From a theoretical perspective, further investigation of the structure of our new quantum weight enumerators and their relation to other families of quantum invariants appears particularly promising.
A deeper understanding of their algebraic properties may not only enable more efficient evaluation of higher-order moments, but also lead to new and more powerful classes of entanglement criteria.
Finally, extending our framework to more general noise models could provide additional insight into the robustness of moment-based PPT relaxations and their applicability in large-scale quantum systems.
