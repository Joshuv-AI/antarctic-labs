Odd-parity perturbations of trace-quadratic f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) black holes with anisotropic matter: admissible branches, axial ringdown, and a coupled-PINN benchmark
Mushtaq Ahmad, M. Farasat Shamir, Adnan Malik, Ahdab K. Althukair

Abstract
We study odd-parity gravitational perturbations of static black holes in trace-quadratic f ⁡ ( R , T ) = R + α ​ T 2 f(R,T)=R+\alpha T^{2} gravity supported by an anisotropic effective fluid with constant closure parameters ( w r , w t ) (w_{r},w_{t}) . From the unreduced axial system and its principal symbol, we identify the sector of parameter space that supports a regular horizon, asymptotic flatness, and hyperbolic odd-sector evolution. Within this closure the admissible branch lies at negative w r w_{r} , while the commonly used positive- w r w_{r} family fails the background regularity test and is kept only as a numerical comparison branch. On static admissible backgrounds the odd sector is exactly equivalent to Einstein gravity coupled to a frozen effective anisotropic fluid, so the physical axial spectrum is governed by a single gauge-invariant master equation. For the anchored branch ( w r , w t ) = ( − 0.2 , 0.15 ) (w_{r},w_{t})=(-0.2,0.15) we compute the fundamental axial ℓ = 2 \ell=2 quasinormal mode with an exact Chebyshev solve. The mass-normalized spectrum differs from Schwarzschild by about 22 % 22\% , whereas no statistically resolved direct α \alpha -dependence appears within the conservative spectral envelope over 0 ≤ α / M 2 ≤ 0.3 0\leq\alpha/M^{2}\leq 0.3 . We also construct a coupled physics-informed neural network for the unreduced two-field eigenproblem and use it to benchmark the inadmissible comparison branch. A closure-level audit of the anchored family shows positive diagnostic combinations associated with the null, weak, and dominant energy conditions, denominator safety in the modified balance law, and an effective exterior mass fraction of about 20 % 20\% , while indicating that the constant- ( w r , w t ) (w_{r},w_{t}) model should be read as an effective anisotropic stress rather than as a microphysical fluid. Within this closure, the main observable imprint in axial ringdown comes from the existence of the matter-supported branch itself, not from direct variation of the trace coupling.

II
Trace-quadratic f(R,T) gravity and the axial perturbation problem

II.1
Field equations and matter prescription

We start from the action

S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,f(R,T)+\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,L_{m}^{\mathrm{eff}},

(1)

with quadratic trace coupling

f(R,T)=R+\alpha T^{2}.

(2)

Because f(R,T) gravity depends explicitly on the matter action through
\Theta_{\mu\nu}
, the matter sector must be specified explicitly. We adopt the effective anisotropic-fluid Lagrangian

L_{m}^{\mathrm{eff}}=p_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(\rho+p_{t})(g^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+1)-\frac{1}{2}(p_{r}-p_{t})(g^{\alpha\beta}\chi_{\alpha}\chi_{\beta}-1),

(3)

where
u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1
,
\chi_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}=1
, and
u_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}=0
. The corresponding stress tensor is

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p_{t})u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{t}g_{\mu\nu}+(p_{r}-p_{t})\chi_{\mu}\chi_{\nu}.

(4)

For this matter prescription,

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-2T_{\mu\nu}+p_{t}g_{\mu\nu}.

(5)

Since
f_{R}=1
identically, all derivative terms involving
f_{R}
vanish and the field equations reduce to

G_{\mu\nu}=(8\pi-2\alpha T)T_{\mu\nu}+\alpha\left(2p_{t}T+\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\right)g_{\mu\nu}.

(6)

Taking the divergence and using
\nabla^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0
yields the modified matter-balance law

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi-\alpha T}\left[T^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,\partial_{\mu}T-T\,\partial_{\nu}p_{t}-\left(p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}T\right)\partial_{\nu}T\right].

(7)

All results below refer to this specific matter prescription; different matter actions would realize the same formal f(R,T) Lagrangian differently.

II.2
Background equations, solver workflow, and consistency constraints

For static spherical symmetry we write the metric as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-N(r)\sigma(r)^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{N(r)}+r^{2}\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2}^{2},

(8)

with

N(r)=e^{-2\nu_{0}(r)},\qquad\sigma(r)=e^{\mu_{0}(r)+\nu_{0}(r)},\qquad e^{2\mu_{0}}=N\sigma^{2}.

(9)

The background matter is specified by the constant phenomenological closure

p_{r0}=w_{r}\rho_{0},\qquad p_{t0}=w_{t}\rho_{0},\qquad T_{0}=q\rho_{0},\qquad q\equiv-1+w_{r}+2w_{t}.

(10)

Defining

\mathcal{C}_{N}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(w_{r}^{2}+8w_{r}w_{t}+2w_{r}+12w_{t}^{2}-3\right),

(11)

Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
) become the first-order system

\displaystyle N^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-N}{r}-8\pi r\rho_{0}+\alpha\mathcal{C}_{N}\,r\rho_{0}^{2},

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{r(1+w_{r})\rho_{0}\left(4\pi-\alpha q\rho_{0}\right)}{N},

(13)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho_{0}\left[(1+w_{r})\left(\frac{N^{\prime}}{2N}+\frac{\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right)+\frac{2(w_{r}-w_{t})}{r}\right]}{w_{r}-\dfrac{\alpha q\rho_{0}(1+w_{r}-6w_{t})}{2\left(4\pi-\alpha q\rho_{0}\right)}}.

(14)

The angular Einstein equation is then a consistency check rather than an independent evolution equation.

Equations (
12
)–(
14
) fully specify the background solver. A practical implementation is:

1.

Fix the horizon scale by setting
r_{H}=1

2.

Start at

\sigma_{H}=1
initially and renormalize to
\sigma(\infty)=1
after the integration.

3.

Integrate the ODE system outward with an adaptive solver. A shooting parameter such as the leading matter amplitude
\rho_{s}
is adjusted until the solution joins the decaying branch and the asymptotic metric is flat within numerical tolerance.

4.

Extract
M

N(r)
and verify a posteriori that the angular Einstein equation and the modified balance law are satisfied along the numerical profile.

The same ODE system also yields the consistency conditions. Let
r=r_{H}+\Delta r
near the horizon and assume a nontrivial matter-supported branch of the form

N(r)=\frac{\Delta r}{r_{H}}+\cdots,\qquad\sigma(r)=\sigma_{H}+\cdots,\qquad\rho_{0}(r)=\rho_{s}\,\Delta r^{s}+\cdots.

(15)

Equation (
14

s=-\frac{1+w_{r}}{2w_{r}}.

(16)

A nontrivial decaying matter branch therefore requires

s\leq 0

(17)

The illustrative positive-
w_{r}

At large radius, let

\rho_{0}(r)\sim\rho_{\infty}r^{-n_{\infty}},\qquad T_{0}(r)\sim q\rho_{\infty}r^{-n_{\infty}},\qquad r\to\infty.

(18)

The leading terms of Eq. (
14

n_{\infty}=\frac{2(w_{r}-w_{t})}{w_{r}}.

(19)

Finite ADM mass requires

(20)

(21)

with

II.3
Axial perturbations and the compactified QNM system

In Regge–Wheeler gauge, the odd-parity metric perturbations are

h_{t\phi}=h_{0}(r)\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}(\theta),\qquad h_{r\phi}=h_{1}(r)\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}(\theta),

(22)

with
S_{\ell}(\theta)=\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}P_{\ell}(\cos\theta)
. The axial fluid amplitude is written as

\delta u_{\phi}=r^{2}\varpi(r)\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}(\theta).

(23)

Odd parity does not perturb the scalar fluid variables,

\delta\rho=\delta p_{r}=\delta p_{t}=0,

(24)

so the only nonvanishing perturbed stress-tensor components are

\displaystyle\delta T_{t\phi}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[-(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})r^{2}\varpi+p_{t0}h_{0}\Bigr]\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell},

(25)

\displaystyle\delta T_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=p_{t0}h_{1}\mathrm{e}^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}.

(26)

A key simplification is that

\delta T=0,\qquad\delta f_{T}=0.

Even so, the
f_{T}
sector does not disappear altogether. As shown in Appendix A, the variation of
f_{T}(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu})
survives through
2\alpha T_{0}(\delta T_{\mu\nu}+\delta\Theta_{\mu\nu})
.

The axial matter equation follows from the modified divergence law, not from the GR conservation equation. The resulting linearized
\phi
-component is

-i\omega(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})\varpi+\Xi_{0}(r)h_{1}=0,

(27)

where

\Xi_{0}(r)=\frac{p_{t0}-p_{r0}}{r}+(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})^{\prime}-\frac{2\alpha p_{t0}T_{0}^{\prime}}{8\pi-2\alpha T_{0}}.

(28)

For
\omega\neq 0
,

\varpi=\frac{\Xi_{0}(r)}{i\omega(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})}\,h_{1}.

(29)

Substituting Eq. (
29
) into the axial Einstein equations yields the coupled system

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\prime\prime}+A_{1}h_{0}^{\prime}+A_{2}h_{0}+A_{3}h_{1}^{\prime}+A_{4}h_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

(30)

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\prime\prime}+B_{1}h_{1}^{\prime}+B_{2}h_{1}+B_{3}h_{0}^{\prime}+B_{4}h_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

(31)

with coefficients

\begin{gathered}A_{1}=\frac{2}{r}-\nu_{0}^{\prime}+\mu_{0}^{\prime},\qquad A_{2}=-e^{2(\nu_{0}-\mu_{0})}\omega^{2}+e^{2\nu_{0}}(R_{0}+\alpha T_{0}^{2}+16\pi p_{t0}),\\
A_{3}=i\omega,\qquad A_{4}=i\omega A_{1}-\frac{2e^{2\nu_{0}}r^{2}(8\pi-2\alpha T_{0})}{i\omega}\,\Xi_{0}(r),\\
B_{1}=\frac{2}{r}+\mu_{0}^{\prime}-\nu_{0}^{\prime},\\
B_{2}=-e^{2(\nu_{0}-\mu_{0})}\omega^{2}+\frac{e^{2\nu_{0}}}{r^{2}}\bigl(2-\ell(\ell+1)\bigr)+e^{2\nu_{0}}(R_{0}+\alpha T_{0}^{2}+16\pi p_{t0}),\\
B_{3}=i\omega,\qquad B_{4}=-i\omega\nu_{0}^{\prime}.\end{gathered}

(32)

The explicit derivation is given in Appendix A.

A complementary rewriting follows directly from Eq. (
6
). Defining

T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}=c(T)\,T_{\mu\nu}+d(T,p_{t})\,g_{\mu\nu},\qquad c(T)\equiv 1-\frac{\alpha T}{4\pi},\qquad d(T,p_{t})\equiv\frac{\alpha}{8\pi}\left(2p_{t}T+\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\right),

(33)

the field equations become exactly

G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}.

(34)

In odd parity the scalar fluid variables do not fluctuate, so
\delta\rho=\delta p_{r}=\delta p_{t}=\delta T=0
and hence
\delta c=\delta d=0
. The odd sector is then identical to the odd-parity problem of Einstein gravity on a static background sourced by the frozen effective anisotropic fluid
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}
.

For a static line element written as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-A(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{B(r)}+C(r)\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2}^{2},

(35)

the odd-parity sector can then be expressed in terms of a gauge-invariant master function
\Psi
obeying
[
48
;
49
]

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\Psi}{\mathrm{d}r_{*}^{2}}+\bigl[\omega^{2}-V_{\rm ax}(r)\bigr]\Psi=0,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}r_{*}}{\mathrm{d}r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{AB}},

(36)

with

V_{\rm ax}(r)=\frac{[\ell(\ell+1)-2]A}{C}+\sqrt{ABC}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left[\sqrt{AB}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left(C^{-1/2}\right)\right].

(37)

For the metric (
8
) one has

A=N\sigma^{2},\qquad B=N,\qquad C=r^{2},\qquad\Psi=N\sigma h_{1},

(38)

and therefore

V_{\rm ax}(r)=\frac{[\ell(\ell+1)-2]N\sigma^{2}}{r^{2}}-\frac{N\sigma}{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}(N\sigma)}{\mathrm{d}r}+\frac{2(N\sigma)^{2}}{r^{2}}.

(39)

This exact master equation provides the production equation used below for the admissible negative-
w_{r}
branch. The unreduced two-field system (
30
)–(
31
) remains the natural benchmark problem for the coupled PINN and for the positive-
w_{r}
comparison branch.

For the unreduced coupled formulation used by the PINN benchmark, we compactify the exterior region with

x=\frac{r_{H}}{r},\qquad x\in[0,1],

(40)

where
x=1
is the horizon and
x=0
is infinity. Let
r_{*}
be the tortoise coordinate,
\mathrm{d}r_{*}/\mathrm{d}r=e^{\nu_{0}-\mu_{0}}
. Then the QNM conditions are purely ingoing at the horizon and purely outgoing at infinity. We factor these universal asymptotics as

h_{a}(x)=\mathcal{F}(x,\Omega)g_{a}(x),\qquad a=0,1,

(41)

with
\Omega=r_{H}\omega
and

\mathcal{F}(x,\Omega)=(1-x)^{-i\gamma_{H}\Omega}x^{-2i\eta_{M}\Omega}\mathrm{e}^{i\Omega/x},\qquad\gamma_{H}\equiv\frac{1}{2\kappa_{H}r_{H}},\qquad\eta_{M}\equiv\frac{M}{r_{H}}.

(42)

The regular functions
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
satisfy a compactified system of the schematic form

\mathcal{R}_{a}[g_{0},g_{1};\Omega](x)=\sum_{b=0}^{1}\sum_{n=0}^{2}\sum_{m}C_{nm}^{(ab)}(x)(i\Omega)^{m}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}g_{b}}{\mathrm{d}x^{n}}=0,\qquad a=0,1,

(43)

where the coefficient arrays are built directly from the background profiles and their derivatives. The Schwarzschild limit is recovered when
\rho_{0}=p_{r0}=p_{t0}=T_{0}=0
, in which case the system collapses to the usual Regge–Wheeler problem.

III
Numerical strategy, validation hierarchy, and uncertainty model

III.1
Numerical scope and validation strategy

We use two complementary numerical routes. The coupled PINN targets the unreduced two-field odd-parity eigenproblem and is employed here for the Schwarzschild benchmark and for the positive-
w_{r}
comparison branch. For the physically admissible negative-
w_{r}
family, the quoted frequencies instead come from an exact solve of the gauge-invariant axial master equation derived in Sec.
II.3
. The coupled PINN and the compactified two-field Chebyshev formulation remain benchmark tools for the unreduced gauge-fixed problem, while the admissible-branch frequencies reported below come from the exact master-equation calculation.

III.2
Coupled PINN architecture and exact horizon regularity

The PINN learns the regularized fields
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
rather than
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
themselves. Its input is the single real coordinate
x\in[0,1]
. A shared encoder with four hidden layers of width 256 and GELU activations feeds two decoders, each with two hidden layers of width 128, which return the real and imaginary parts of the raw complex functions
G_{0}(x)
and
G_{1}(x)
. The complex QNM frequency is represented by two trainable scalars,
\Omega_{R}
and
\Omega_{I}
.

Figure 2:
Coupled PINN architecture for the compactified two-field axial eigenproblem. The shared encoder extracts common radial features, the two decoder branches generate field-specific latent outputs, and the boundary transform enforces Eq. (
47
) to produce the regular fields
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
. The physical perturbations are reconstructed as
h_{a}=\mathcal{F}g_{a}

Because the QNM problem is homogeneous, one overall normalization must be fixed. Writing the near-horizon expansion as

g_{a}(x)=g_{a,H}+g_{a,1}(1-x)+O\!\bigl((1-x)^{2}\bigr),

(44)

substitution into the regularized equations yields a linear relation

\mathcal{H}_{00}(\Omega)g_{0,H}+\mathcal{H}_{01}(\Omega)g_{1,H}=0.

(45)

Defining

\Lambda_{H}(\Omega)\equiv-\frac{\mathcal{H}_{00}(\Omega)}{\mathcal{H}_{01}(\Omega)},

(46)

we impose exact horizon regularity through

\displaystyle g_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=1+(1-x)G_{0}(x),

(47)

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{H}(\Omega)+(1-x)G_{1}(x).

This removes the need for a separate horizon penalty. No asymptotic penalty is required because the QNM behavior has already been factored into
\mathcal{F}(x,\Omega)
.

For the admissible branch, the quoted frequencies come directly from the exact gauge-invariant axial master equation on the anchored backgrounds, with Schwarzschild benchmarks and spectral-resolution checks described below. A full retrained coupled-PINN survey on the same branch would still be a useful cross-check, but the axial conclusions drawn here do not depend on it.

III.3
Loss function and optimization protocol

At a collocation point
x_{i}
, let
\mathcal{R}_{0}(x_{i})
and
\mathcal{R}_{1}(x_{i})
denote the two complex residuals of the compactified system. We use automatic differentiation for the derivatives of
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
and the complex norm
|z|^{2}=(\Re z)^{2}+(\Im z)^{2}
. The equation-residual losses are

L_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\mathcal{R}_{0}(x_{i})|^{2},\qquad L_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\mathcal{R}_{1}(x_{i})|^{2}.

(48)

We also retain a coupling-sensitive regularizer,

L_{\mathrm{coup}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{0}}{\partial g_{0}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{1}}{\partial g_{1}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{0}}{\partial g_{1}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}_{1}}{\partial g_{0}}\right|_{x_{i}}^{2},

(49)

but we now interpret it explicitly as an empirical numerical stabilizer rather than a derived physical condition. To suppress spurious short-wavelength oscillations in the learned regular functions we also include

L_{\mathrm{sm}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(|g_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x_{i})|^{2}+|g_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x_{i})|^{2}\right).

(50)

The total loss is

L_{\mathrm{tot}}=\lambda_{0}L_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(0)}+\lambda_{1}L_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(1)}+\lambda_{c}L_{\mathrm{coup}}+\lambda_{s}L_{\mathrm{sm}},

(51)

with initial weights
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}=1
,
\lambda_{c}=10^{-1}
, and
\lambda_{s}=10^{-4}
. During the final stage of training the relative weights are updated adaptively to keep the four contributions comparable in scale.

Optimization is performed in three stages with Adam
[
60
]
: a warm start with the off-diagonal couplings temporarily suppressed, a coupling ramp in which the full operator is restored gradually, and a final full-coupling stage with lower learning rate and cosine annealing. A typical production schedule is 1000 epochs for the warm start, 2000 for the coupling ramp, and 3000 for the final stage. Transfer learning is used along smooth one-parameter families so that the converged weights and frequency from one point initialize the next. The collocation set is nonuniform and concentrated toward both boundaries; the production value is
N=2000
collocation points.

III.4
Independent matrix method, hyperbolicity screen, and validation tiers

Rather than treating all nonzero-
\alpha
points on the same footing, we assign each result to one of three validation tiers.

1.

Externally validated
: the point satisfies the hyperbolicity criterion
\mathcal{H}_{\min}>10^{-4}
, the final loss obeys
L_{\mathrm{tot}}<0.1
, and an independent Chebyshev spectral solve agrees with the PINN result within the combined numerical uncertainty.

2.

Internally validated
: the point satisfies
\mathcal{H}_{\min}>10^{-4}
and
L_{\mathrm{tot}}<0.1
, and it is stable under seed variation, collocation refinement, architecture variation, and smooth continuation, but no external spectral solve is currently available.

3.

Provisional or marginal
: the point is too close to the hyperbolicity boundary or the loss threshold for a strong quantitative claim. Such points are retained only to illustrate the approach to breakdown.

For the positive-
w_{r}
comparison branch, the independent spectral method discretizes the same compactified two-field operator on a Chebyshev–Gauss–Lobatto grid and solves the resulting matrix eigenproblem. After collocation, the problem takes a polynomial form in
z\equiv i\Omega
,

\bigl[\mathbf{A}_{0}+z\mathbf{A}_{1}+z^{2}\mathbf{A}_{2}\bigr]\mathbf{v}=0,

(52)

which can be linearized to a generalized eigenvalue problem as described explicitly in Appendix D. This deterministic formulation provides the independent spectral cross-check used in the low-coupling regime of the raw coupled system. In practice, direct external comparison is reliable through
\alpha/M^{2}\lesssim 0.1
, where the compactified operator remains well conditioned. At stronger coupling we therefore classify the comparison-branch results by validation tier rather than claim independent spectral confirmation. The admissible branch, by contrast, is quoted from the exact master-equation solve and its own spectral-resolution envelope.

Physical admissibility is determined from the unreduced time-domain odd system, not from the reduced frequency-domain pair. Before any PINN solve is attempted we collect the coefficients of the principal derivatives into the schematic form

\mathbf{K}(r)\,\partial_{t}^{2}\mathbf{q}+2\mathbf{M}(r)\,\partial_{t}\partial_{r}\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{P}(r)\,\partial_{r}^{2}\mathbf{q}+\cdots=0,

(53)

where
\mathbf{q}
denotes the unreduced odd variables. Inserting
\mathbf{q}\propto\exp[-i\omega t+ikr]
gives the principal symbol

\bm{\Pi}(\omega,k;r)=-\omega^{2}\mathbf{K}-2\omega k\mathbf{M}+k^{2}\mathbf{P}.

(54)

The characteristic phase speeds are the roots of

\det\!\bigl[c^{2}\mathbf{K}+2c\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{P}\bigr]=0,\qquad c\equiv\omega/k.

(55)

At each radius we sort the real roots and define
c_{-}^{2}(r)
as the smallest physical branch. The background diagnostic used throughout the paper is

\mathcal{H}_{\min}(\alpha,w_{r},w_{t})\equiv\min_{x\in[0,1]}c_{-}^{2}(x).

(56)

Hyperbolicity requires
\mathcal{H}_{\min}>0
. In practice we adopt the conservative stop criterion
\mathcal{H}_{\min}>10^{-4}
to avoid grazing the boundary where roundoff and branch reordering become troublesome. Appendix D gives the explicit extraction algorithm and the strong-shift validation protocol.

The background analysis fixes the physically admissible sector. For the constant-

V
Discussion

Two points stand out on the admissible branch. First, within the constant-
(w_{r},w_{t})
closure, the axial spectrum is essentially insensitive to direct variation of
\alpha
along the anchored family: over
0\leq\alpha/M^{2}\leq 0.3
, the pointwise drift in
M\omega
stays far below the conservative spectral envelope of the exact master-equation solve. Second, the branch itself is not close to Schwarzschild. Relative to the vacuum baseline, the anchored solution shifts
\Re(M\omega)
and
-\Im(M\omega)
by about
22\%
. Because
(M/r_{H})_{\rm adm}=0.624628
, that shift tracks an effective exterior mass fraction of about
20\%
. In other words, the dominant axial signature comes from the presence of the matter-supported exterior, not from a directly resolved change in the trace coupling along the branch.

That conclusion rests on a direct solve of the exact axial master equation on self-consistent admissible backgrounds, benchmarked against Schwarzschild and tracked across the
\beta
scan. The coupled PINN remains valuable because it solves the unreduced gauge-fixed problem directly and provides a useful benchmark on the comparison branch, while the admissible-branch spectrum itself is taken from the exact master equation rather than from a surrogate reduction or transferred PINN scatter. Within this effective closure, “physical admissibility” means regularity, asymptotic flatness, odd-sector hyperbolicity, positive diagnostic NEC/WEC/DEC/SEC combinations, and denominator safety in the modified balance law. At the same time, the negative formal surrogate
c_{s,r}^{2}=w_{r}
makes it clear that the constant-
(w_{r},w_{t})
prescription should be viewed as an effective anisotropic stress model, not as a literal microphysical fluid. That caveat is intrinsic to f(R,T) gravity, where different matter-sector completions can realize the same formal Lagrangian in different ways.

From an observational standpoint, the fixed-mass shifts in Table
4
are large enough to make the branch interesting, but the fundamental axial mode by itself is not a clean discriminator of the trace-quadratic coupling. The quality factor differs from Schwarzschild by only
0.70\%
, so a Kerr source with slightly adjusted mass and spin can sit close to the same
(f_{220},\tau_{220})
point when only one mode is used
[
53
;
7
]
. A serious spectroscopy statement will therefore require multimode waveform modeling, detector response, parameter covariances, and possibly non-normal effects in black-hole perturbation problems
[
50
]
. The polar sector remains the clearest next target. In odd parity
\delta T=0
, so the trace coupling enters only indirectly; in polar parity
\delta T\neq 0
, direct trace-coupling terms appear already at linear order. On the anchored backgrounds,
\max_{r}|\alpha T_{0}|/(4\pi)\simeq 2.0\times 10^{-6}
at the top of the scan, so any generic polar effect on this branch is more naturally expected at the
10^{-5}
level unless it is enhanced by mode mixing or by a near-degeneracy of the polar operator. An independent time-domain extraction on the same backgrounds would be a useful next check.

VI
Conclusions

We have identified the regular, asymptotically flat, hyperbolic odd-sector branch of the constant-
(w_{r},w_{t})
closure in trace-quadratic
f(R,T)
gravity and derived its axial perturbation problem. The admissible branch lies at negative
w_{r}
; the commonly used positive-
w_{r}
family fails the regularity test and is useful only as a comparison branch. On admissible static backgrounds the odd sector reduces exactly to a single gauge-invariant master equation, which allows a direct solve of the axial spectrum.

For the branch anchored at
(w_{r},w_{t})=(-0.2,0.15)
, the baseline axial
\ell=2
mode is

\Omega_{\rm adm}=0.7310046\pm 0.0000298-(0.1728182\pm 0.0000223)i,

(74)

with

M\omega_{\rm adm}=0.4566061\pm 0.0000186-(0.1079471\pm 0.0000139)i.

(75)

Relative to Schwarzschild, the branch shifts the mass-normalized oscillation frequency and damping rate by
+22.19\%
and
+21.34\%
, respectively, while the direct drift with
\alpha
along the same family remains numerically unresolved within the conservative spectral envelope over
0\leq\alpha/M^{2}\leq 0.3
.

Within the constant-
(w_{r},w_{t})
closure, axial ringdown therefore probes the existence of the admissible matter-supported branch much more readily than it probes the trace-quadratic coupling itself. The natural next steps are an independent time-domain extraction on the same backgrounds and a polar-sector analysis, where direct
\delta T
effects can appear already at linear order.

More broadly, the analysis shows that an admissibility-first treatment can qualitatively reshape ringdown forecasts in matter-coupled modified gravity. Apparent strong-coupling trends on formal branches can disappear once regularity, asymptotic flatness, and odd-sector hyperbolicity are enforced. That lesson is likely to matter beyond the specific closure studied here.

Appendix A
Derivation of the odd-parity perturbation equations in quadratic
f(R,T)
gravity

This appendix derives the axial perturbation equations used in the main text. In particular, it makes explicit how the odd-parity linearization is carried out in the quadratic trace-coupled model

f(R,T)=R+\alpha T^{2},

and how the matter-coupling term proportional to
f_{T}
enters at linear order.

Throughout we use metric signature
(-,+,+,+)
, adopt standard curvature conventions as in Refs.
[
62
;
63
;
64
]
, and let primes denote derivatives with respect to
r
.

A.1
Full field equations and matter-sector identities

We begin from the
f(R,T)
field equations

f_{R}R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}fg_{\mu\nu}+(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})f_{R}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}+f_{T}(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}),

(A1)

with

f_{R}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial R},\qquad f_{T}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial T},\qquad T=g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}.

We adopt the effective anisotropic-fluid Lagrangian

L_{m}^{\mathrm{eff}}=p_{t}-\frac{1}{2}(\rho+p_{t})(g^{\alpha\beta}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+1)-\frac{1}{2}(p_{r}-p_{t})(g^{\alpha\beta}\chi_{\alpha}\chi_{\beta}-1),

with

u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=-1,\qquad\chi_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}=1,\qquad u_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}=0.

For Lagrangians without metric derivatives,

T_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}L_{m}-2\frac{\partial L_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}},

which yields

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p_{t})u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{t}g_{\mu\nu}+(p_{r}-p_{t})\chi_{\mu}\chi_{\nu}.

The tensor
\Theta_{\mu\nu}
is

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=-2T_{\mu\nu}+p_{t}g_{\mu\nu}.

For the quadratic model

f(R,T)=R+\alpha T^{2},

we have

f_{R}=1,\qquad f_{T}=2\alpha T,

so all derivative terms of
f_{R}
vanish. The field equations reduce to

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(R+\alpha T^{2})g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}+2\alpha T(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}).

(A2)

A.2
Background geometry and perturbation ansatz

The static spherically symmetric metric is

ds^{2}=-e^{2\mu_{0}(r)}dt^{2}+e^{2\nu_{0}(r)}dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,d\phi^{2}).

The background stress tensor is

T^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+p_{t0}g_{\mu\nu}+(p_{r0}-p_{t0})\chi_{\mu}\chi_{\nu},

with trace

T_{0}=-\rho_{0}+p_{r0}+2p_{t0}.

We perturb the metric as

g_{\mu\nu}=g^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}.

In Regge–Wheeler gauge, axial perturbations are

h_{t\phi}=h_{0}(r)e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}(\theta),\qquad h_{r\phi}=h_{1}(r)e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}(\theta),

with

S_{\ell}(\theta)=\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}P_{\ell}(\cos\theta).

The axial fluid perturbation is

\delta u_{\phi}=r^{2}\varpi(r)e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}.

The only nonzero perturbed stress components are

\displaystyle\delta T_{t\phi}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[-(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})r^{2}\varpi+p_{t0}h_{0}\Bigr]e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell},

\displaystyle\delta T_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=p_{t0}h_{1}e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell}.

A.3
Vanishing of
\delta T
,
\delta f_{T}
, and
\delta L_{m}^{\mathrm{eff}}

The trace perturbation is

\delta T=\delta(g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu})=-h^{\mu\nu}T^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}+g^{(0)\mu\nu}\delta T_{\mu\nu}.

Both terms vanish in the odd sector, hence

\delta T=0,\qquad\delta f_{T}=0.

Similarly,

\delta L_{m}^{\mathrm{eff}}=0.

These identities are the key simplifications of the axial sector.

A.4
Explicit variation of the
f_{T}
-sector

We compute

\delta\bigl[f_{T}(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu})\bigr].

Since
\delta f_{T}=0
,

\delta[\cdots]=2\alpha T_{0}(\delta T_{\mu\nu}+\delta\Theta_{\mu\nu}).

Using

\delta\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-2\delta T_{\mu\nu}+p_{t0}h_{\mu\nu},

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta T_{t\phi}+\delta\Theta_{t\phi}

\displaystyle=(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})r^{2}\varpi e^{-i\omega t}S_{\ell},

\displaystyle\delta T_{r\phi}+\delta\Theta_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=0.

The trace-coupling therefore contributes only to the
t\phi
equation.

A.5
Linearized odd-parity field equations

Linearizing the field equations yields

\delta R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}(R_{0}+\alpha T_{0}^{2})h_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\delta T_{\mu\nu}+2\alpha T_{0}(\delta T_{\mu\nu}+\delta\Theta_{\mu\nu}).

Using the Ricci perturbations in Regge–Wheeler gauge then yields two coupled equations for
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
before
\varpi
is eliminated.

A.6
Linearized modified divergence law

The matter equation follows from

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\frac{\alpha}{4\pi-\alpha T}\left[T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\partial_{\mu}T-T\partial_{\nu}p_{t}-\left(p_{t}+\frac{1}{2}T\right)\partial_{\nu}T\right].

Linearizing the
\nu=\phi
component yields

-i\omega(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})\varpi+\Xi_{0}(r)h_{1}=0,

with

\Xi_{0}(r)=\frac{p_{t0}-p_{r0}}{r}+(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})^{\prime}-\frac{2\alpha p_{t0}T_{0}^{\prime}}{8\pi-2\alpha T_{0}}.

Hence

\varpi=\frac{\Xi_{0}(r)}{i\omega(\rho_{0}+p_{t0})}h_{1}.

A.7
Closed two-field system

Substituting
\varpi
into the gravitational equations yields

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\prime\prime}+A_{1}h_{0}^{\prime}+A_{2}h_{0}+A_{3}h_{1}^{\prime}+A_{4}h_{1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\prime\prime}+B_{1}h_{1}^{\prime}+B_{2}h_{1}+B_{3}h_{0}^{\prime}+B_{4}h_{0}

\displaystyle=0,

with coefficients given explicitly in Sec. II.

These are the equations solved by the PINN. On static admissible branches the same odd sector also admits the exact gauge-invariant master-equation reduction summarized in Appendix
E
, which is the production variable used for the negative-
w_{r}
scan.

A.8
Closure of the odd sector

The odd sector contains three amplitudes

h_{0}(r),\qquad h_{1}(r),\qquad\varpi(r).

The two gravitational equations and the modified divergence law form a closed system. Eliminating
\varpi
yields the two-field system quoted above.

A.9
Consistency checks

Two limits provide important checks.

GR limit (
\alpha\to 0
):

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=0,

and the system reduces to the GR axial equations.

Vacuum limit:

\rho_{0}=p_{r0}=p_{t0}=T_{0}=0,

so all matter-dependent terms vanish and the system reduces to the Regge–Wheeler equation.

In summary,
f_{R}=1
removes all derivative terms exactly,
\delta T=0
does not eliminate the
f_{T}
sector because it still contributes through
2\alpha T_{0}(\delta T_{\mu\nu}+\delta\Theta_{\mu\nu})
, and the axial matter equation follows from the modified divergence law rather than from GR conservation.

Appendix C
Weak-coupling frequency shift and its discrete implementation

The main text uses the first-order correction
\Omega_{1}
in the small-
\alpha
comparison. For completeness, we derive it here once the compactified operator is organized as

\mathcal{L}(\Omega,\alpha)\mathbf{g}=0,\qquad\mathbf{g}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}g_{0}(x)\\
g_{1}(x)\end{pmatrix},

(C1)

with the exact horizon regularity already built into the ansatz for
\mathbf{g}
. Expand the operator, the eigenfunction, and the eigenvalue around the Schwarzschild point,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\Omega,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\Omega)+\alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{1}(\Omega)+O(\alpha^{2}),

(C2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{g}_{0}+\alpha\,\mathbf{g}_{1}+O(\alpha^{2}),

(C3)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\Omega_{0}+\alpha\,\Omega_{1}+O(\alpha^{2}),

(C4)

where
\Omega_{0}=\Omega_{\mathrm{Schw}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}(\Omega_{0})\mathbf{g}_{0}=0
. The
O(\alpha)
equation is

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\Omega_{0})\mathbf{g}_{1}+\left[(\partial_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{0})_{\Omega_{0}}\,\Omega_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\Omega_{0})\right]\mathbf{g}_{0}=0.

(C5)

Because the QNM problem is non-self-adjoint, the solvability condition is imposed with the adjoint zero mode
\bm{\chi}_{0}
satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{0}^{\dagger}(\Omega_{0})\bm{\chi}_{0}=0.

(C6)

Using the bilinear pairing

\langle\bm{\chi},\mathbf{u}\rangle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\bm{\chi}^{\dagger}(x)\cdot\mathbf{u}(x)\,w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(C7)

with any convenient positive weight
w(x)
on the compactified interval, Eq. (
C5
) yields the Fredholm condition

\Omega_{1}=-\frac{\left\langle\bm{\chi}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{1}(\Omega_{0})\mathbf{g}_{0}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\bm{\chi}_{0},(\partial_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{0})_{\Omega_{0}}\mathbf{g}_{0}\right\rangle}.

(C8)

Equation (
C8
) is the formal weak-coupling correction quoted in the main text.

In practice the external validator works with a Chebyshev collocation discretization. If the discretized problem is written as

\mathbf{A}(\Omega,\alpha)\mathbf{v}=0,

(C9)

then the corresponding first-order correction is

\Omega_{1}=-\frac{\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\dagger}\,\mathbf{A}_{1}(\Omega_{0})\,\mathbf{v}_{0}}{\mathbf{u}_{0}^{\dagger}(\partial_{\Omega}\mathbf{A}_{0})_{\Omega_{0}}\mathbf{v}_{0}},

(C10)

where
\mathbf{v}_{0}
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}
are the right and left null vectors of
\mathbf{A}_{0}(\Omega_{0})
. The weak-coupling comparison used in the main text can therefore be reproduced either from the continuum operator via Eq. (
C8
) or directly from the matrix discretization via Eq. (
C10
).

Two points deserve emphasis. First, the correction includes both the explicit perturbative change of the compactified operator and the first-order change in the background profiles. Second, because the odd sector is genuinely coupled,
\mathbf{g}_{0}
and
\bm{\chi}_{0}
are two-component objects even when the Schwarzschild limit reduces to the vacuum Regge–Wheeler problem in a particular basis.

The hyperbolicity scan is performed on the unreduced time-domain system above rather than on the reduced frequency-domain pair. In practice, the equations are organized schematically as

(D7)

Appendix E
Exact odd-sector reduction on the admissible branch

Starting from Eq. (
6
), define

T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}=c\,T_{\mu\nu}+d\,g_{\mu\nu},\qquad c=1-\frac{\alpha T}{4\pi},\qquad d=\frac{\alpha}{8\pi}\left(2p_{t}T+\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\right).

(E1)

Then the quadratic trace model can be written exactly as

G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm eff}.

(E2)

For the anisotropic-fluid form used in the main text this implies

\rho_{\rm eff}=c\rho-d,\qquad p_{r,\rm eff}=cp_{r}+d,\qquad p_{t,\rm eff}=cp_{t}+d.

(E3)

In odd parity the scalar fluid variables do not fluctuate, so

\delta\rho=\delta p_{r}=\delta p_{t}=\delta T=\delta c=\delta d=0.

(E4)

The odd-parity problem is therefore exactly the odd-parity problem of Einstein gravity on a static background sourced by the frozen effective anisotropic fluid.

For a static line element

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-A(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{B(r)}+C(r)\mathrm{d}\Omega_{2}^{2},

(E5)

the gauge-invariant master variable

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\Psi}{\mathrm{d}r_{*}^{2}}+\bigl[\omega^{2}-V_{\rm ax}(r)\bigr]\Psi=0,\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}r_{*}}{\mathrm{d}r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{AB}},

(E6)

with

V_{\rm ax}(r)=\frac{[\ell(\ell+1)-2]A}{C}+\sqrt{ABC}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left[\sqrt{AB}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left(C^{-1/2}\right)\right].

(E7)

For the metric of Eq. (
