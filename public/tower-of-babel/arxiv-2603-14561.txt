Refined Inference for Asymptotically Linear Estimators with Non-Negligible Second-Order Remainders
Lin Li, Pengcheng Wu

Abstract
Asymptotically linear estimators in semiparametric models use a von Mises expansion to reduce inference to the influence-function variance. This reduction is valid when the second-order remainder is negligible in variance, a condition that is not implied by the product-rate requirement guaranteeing asymptotic linearity. When the remainder contributes non-negligible variance, the standard sandwich underestimates the total sampling variance and Wald intervals undercover; we call this the near-boundary regime . We derive a finite-sample variance decomposition separating influence-function and remainder components, characterize the sandwich consistency condition sharply, and establish asymptotic validity of the leave-one-out jackknife and pairs cluster bootstrap in this regime. Jackknife validity follows from a self-normalization argument; bootstrap validity is established directly. An analytic formula for the amplification of the sandwich gap by intra-cluster correlation is derived for clustered data. A simulation study using a surrogate-assisted targeted learning estimator in stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trials illustrates the regime: the variance ratio V ^ JK / V ^ Sand \hat{V}_{\rm JK}/\hat{V}_{\rm Sand} is 1.14–1.38 and persistent across cluster counts, and the refined procedures substantially improve coverage. Keywords: asymptotic linearity; semiparametric inference; second-order remainder; jackknife; bootstrap; variance decomposition; stepped-wedge trial.

2
General Framework

2.1
Asymptotically Linear Estimators and the Von Mises Expansion

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a nonparametric model and
\Psi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}
a
pathwise differentiable functional with efficient influence function
D^{*}\in L^{2}_{0}(P_{0})
. An estimator
\hat{\Psi}
based on
n
i.i.d. observations

O_{1},\ldots,O_{n}\sim P_{0}
is asymptotically linear if it admits the expansion

\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D^{*}(O_{i})+R_{\mathrm{rem}},

(2)

where
R_{\mathrm{rem}}=R_{\mathrm{rem}}({\hat{P}},P_{0})
is a second-order remainder satisfying

\sqrt{n}\,R_{\mathrm{rem}}=o_{P}(1)
under appropriate regularity conditions. The key
structural property of (
2
) is that the remainder
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
is

second-order
in the nuisance estimation error: it vanishes faster than
the first-order influence-function term
n^{-1/2}
under standard product-rate
conditions.

Definition 2.1

(Standard von Mises Remainder Structure)
.

We say
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
has
standard bilinear structure
if it can be written as

R_{\mathrm{rem}}=\int\bigl(\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\bigr)\bigl(\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\bigr)\,dP_{0}+o_{P}\bigl(n^{-1/2}\bigr),

(3)

where
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
are nuisance functionals and
\hat{\eta}_{k}
are their
estimators. The SA-TMLE remainder
R_{2}
satisfies (
3
) with

\eta_{1}=\bar{Q}_{Y}
and
\eta_{2}=g_{\Delta}
. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

|R_{\mathrm{rem}}|\leq\left\|\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\right\|_{P_{0}}\cdot\left\|\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\right\|_{P_{0}}
,
so condition
\left\|\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\right\|_{P_{0}}\cdot\left\|\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\right\|_{P_{0}}=o_{P}(n^{-1/2})
suffices for
\sqrt{n}\,R_{\mathrm{rem}}=o_{P}(1)
.

Definition 2.2

(Product-Rate Boundary)
.

We say the estimator operates at the
product-rate boundary
if each
nuisance estimator converges at rate exactly
o_{P}(n^{-1/4})
in
L^{2}(P_{0})
:

\left\|\hat{\eta}_{k}-\eta^{0}_{k}\right\|_{P_{0}}=O_{P}(n^{-1/4}),\quad k=1,2.

(4)

At the boundary,
\left\|\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\right\|_{P_{0}}\cdot\left\|\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\right\|_{P_{0}}=O_{P}(n^{-1/2})
, which just barely satisfies
the product-rate condition
o_{P}(n^{-1/2})
. The boundary is typical when
nuisance functions are estimated via parametric models in dimension growing
with
n
, or via nonparametric ensemble methods that have not yet entered
their fast-rate regime.

2.2
Regularity Conditions

We work under the following conditions throughout Sections
3
–
4
.

Assumption 2.1

(i.i.d. Data)
.

O_{1},\ldots,O_{n}\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}P_{0}
with
O_{i}\in\mathcal{O}
.

Assumption 2.2

(Asymptotic Linearity)
.

The estimator
\hat{\Psi}
satisfies (
2
) with
\sqrt{n}\,R_{\mathrm{rem}}=o_{P}(1)

and
D^{*}\in L^{2}_{0}(P_{0})
having
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}=\mathbb{E}_{P_{0}}[(D^{*})^{2}]\in(0,\infty)
.

Assumption 2.3

(Bounded Influence Function)
.

\|D^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq M<\infty
almost surely.

Assumption 2.4

(Remainder Rate)
.

The remainder satisfies
R_{\mathrm{rem}}=r_{n}\cdot\xi_{n}
where
r_{n}
is a
deterministic rate and
\xi_{n}=O_{P}(1)
. We say
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
is at the

strong decay regime
if
n\cdot r_{n}^{2}\to 0
, and at the

near-boundary regime
if
n\cdot r_{n}^{2}=c_{R}>0
for some constant.

3
Finite-Sample Variance Decomposition

3.1
The Decomposition Theorem

Theorem 3.1

(Finite-Sample Variance Decomposition)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.4
, the mean squared error of
the ALE decomposes as:

\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0})\bigr)\;=\;\underbrace{\frac{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}}{n}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{influence-function}\\
\text{variance}\end{subarray}}\;+\;\underbrace{\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{remainder}\\
\text{variance}\end{subarray}}\;+\;\underbrace{2\,\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D^{*}(O_{i}),\;R_{\mathrm{rem}}\right)}_{\text{cross term}}\;+\;o(n^{-1}).

(5)

The cluster sandwich variance estimator
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi})
is consistent for

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
only; it does not target
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
or the cross term.

Proof.

Apply the variance operator to (
2
):

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}D^{*}(O_{i})\right)+\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})+2\,\mathrm{Cov}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}D^{*}(O_{i}),R_{\mathrm{rem}}\right).

The first term equals
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
by i.i.d. and the definition of
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
.
The
o(n^{-1})
residual accounts for the
o_{P}(n^{-1/2})
residual in
(
2
). The sandwich
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i}(\hat{D}(O_{i})-\bar{D}_{n})^{2}/n
estimates
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
by the LLN (when
\hat{D}
is consistent
for
D^{*}
), but contains no term corresponding to
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
or
the covariance, since those involve the distribution of
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
across repeated
samples — information not contained in the observed scores
\{\hat{D}(O_{i})\}
.
∎

3.2
The Near-Boundary Gap

Proposition 3.1

(Variance Gap at the Product-Rate Boundary)
.

Suppose the remainder has standard bilinear structure (Definition
2.1
)
with each nuisance at the product-rate boundary (Definition
2.2
).
Then:

a.

\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})=O(n^{-1})
, so the gap

\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi})-\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n=\Theta(n^{-1})
.

b.

The relative gap satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi})}{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n}\;=\;1+\frac{c_{R}}{\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}}+o(1),

(6)

where
c_{R}=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})>0
.

c.

The ratio (
6
) is asymptotically
constant
: it does
not converge to 1 as
n\to\infty
unless
c_{R}=0
.

Proof.

For part (a): by the bilinear structure (
3
),

R_{\mathrm{rem}}^{2}\leq\|\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\|^{2}_{P_{0}}\cdot\|\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\|^{2}_{P_{0}}
. At the boundary, each factor is
O_{P}(n^{-1/2})
, so

R_{\mathrm{rem}}^{2}=O_{P}(n^{-1})
and hence
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\leq\mathbb{E}[R_{\mathrm{rem}}^{2}]=O(n^{-1})
. For part (b): substitute into (
5
); the cross term
is
2\,\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{D}_{n},R_{\mathrm{rem}})\leq 2(\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n)^{1/2}\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})^{1/2}=O(n^{-1})
, so the ratio is dominated by

1+n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}\to 1+c_{R}/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
. Part (c) is
immediate.
∎

Remark 3.1

(Constancy as a Diagnostic)
.

Proposition
3.1
(c) provides a clean diagnostic: if the empirical
variance ratio
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{emp}}
is approximately
constant across increasing sample sizes, the estimator is operating at or near
the product-rate boundary. If the ratio decays toward 1, the nuisance
estimators are achieving superparametric rates and the sandwich is recovering.
This diagnostic is directly observable from simulation output and requires no
knowledge of the remainder structure. In the SA-TMLE application, the ratio
is
\approx 0.63
(equivalently,
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}
is
\approx 63\%
of the empirical
variance) and is constant across
J\in\{10,100\}
, confirming near-boundary
operation.

Remark 3.2

(When the Cross Term Is Non-Negligible)
.

The cross term
2\,\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{D}_{n},R_{\mathrm{rem}})
can be positive or
negative depending on the correlation structure between the influence-function
term and the remainder. In the SA-TMLE setting with CV-TMLE cross-fitting,
the out-of-fold construction makes
\bar{D}_{n}
and
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
approximately
independent, so the cross term is
o_{P}(n^{-1})
and the dominant missing
component is
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
alone. Without cross-fitting, the cross
term can be substantial and of either sign.

4
Sandwich Consistency: A Sharp Characterization

We now give a precise statement of when the sandwich is and is not consistent
for the total variance.

Theorem 4.1

(Sandwich Consistency: Necessary and Sufficient Condition)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.3
, the following are equivalent:

i.

The sandwich estimator
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi})
is consistent for the total
variance:
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi})/\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))\xrightarrow{P}1
.

ii.

The remainder satisfies strong decay:

n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to 0
.

iii.

The sandwich Wald interval
\mathrm{CI}^{z}_{1-\alpha}
achieves asymptotically exact
coverage:
P(\Psi(P_{0})\in\mathrm{CI}^{z}_{1-\alpha})\to 1-\alpha
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
: By Theorem
3.1
,
\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n+\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})+O(n^{-1})
, and
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
by the LLN. Thus
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}/\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi})\to 1
if and only if
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})=o(n^{-1})
, i.e.,
n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to 0
.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
: The sandwich Wald interval has asymptotically exact
coverage if and only if
(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))/\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}}
converges in
distribution to
N(0,1)
. By Assumption
2.2
and Slutsky’s theorem,
this holds if and only if
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}
is consistent for
\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi})
,
which is condition
(i)
.
∎

Corollary 4.1

(Standard Product-Rate Condition Does Not Suffice)
.

The standard product-rate condition
\left\|\hat{\eta}_{1}-\eta^{0}_{1}\right\|_{P_{0}}\cdot\left\|\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta^{0}_{2}\right\|_{P_{0}}=o_{P}(n^{-1/2})
implies
\sqrt{n}\,R_{\mathrm{rem}}=o_{P}(1)
(asymptotic linearity) but does
not
imply
n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to 0
(sandwich consistency). The latter requires each
nuisance to converge strictly faster than
n^{-1/4}
, e.g., at rate

n^{-1/4-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
.

This corollary identifies the gap between what the existing asymptotic
linearity literature establishes and what is needed for valid first-order
inference. Asymptotic linearity guarantees point-estimation quality; it does
not guarantee that the standard confidence interval is valid in finite samples
at the product-rate boundary.

5
Three Refined Variance Estimators

We develop three complementary approaches to variance estimation that
consistently estimate the full decomposition (
5
). Each has
distinct theoretical properties, computational costs, and practical advantages.
We present them in order of increasing generality and complexity.

5.1
The Leave-One-Out Jackknife

5.1.1
Definition and Rationale

Definition 5.1

(Leave-One-Unit-Out Estimator)
.

For
i=1,\ldots,n
, let
\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}
denote the ALE computed on the
sample with unit
i
removed. The jackknife variance estimator is:

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})\;=\;\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}-\bar{\Psi}_{(-\cdot)}\bigr)^{2},

(7)

where
\bar{\Psi}_{(-\cdot)}=n^{-1}\sum_{i}\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}
.

The jackknife operates directly on the estimator map rather than on the
influence function, capturing the sensitivity of
\hat{\Psi}
to removing
individual observations. This includes both the influence-function contribution
and any nonlinear remainder contributions, making it a natural candidate for
the near-boundary regime.

5.1.2
Leave-One-Out Expansion

Lemma 5.1

(LOO Expansion)
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.1
–
2.4
hold. Let

\hat{\eta}_{k}^{(-i)}
denote the nuisance estimator trained on

\{O_{j}:j\neq i\}
. Assume the following
LOO stability condition
:

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\bigl\|\hat{\eta}_{k}^{(-i)}-\hat{\eta}_{k}\bigr\|_{P_{0}}\;=\;O_{P}(n^{-1/2}),\quad k=1,2.

(S)

Then:

\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}-\hat{\Psi}\;=\;-\frac{1}{n}D^{*}(O_{i})+\delta_{i},

(8)

where
\max_{i}|\delta_{i}|=O_{P}(n^{-1})
and

n^{-1}\sum_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}=O_{P}(r_{n}^{2}/n)
.

Sufficient condition for (
S
).

A sufficient route to Condition (
S
) is leave-one-out
stability of an empirical risk minimizer: when
\hat{\eta}_{k}
solves

\min_{\eta}\mathbb{P}_{n}\ell(\eta;O)
over a function class
\mathcal{F}

and the loss satisfies a uniform gradient stability bound

\sup_{\eta}|(\mathbb{P}_{n}-\mathbb{P}_{n}^{(-i)})\nabla_{\eta}\ell(\eta;\cdot)|=O_{P}(n^{-1/2})

(cf.
3
, Chapter 3)
,
then (
S
) holds under standard regularity.
In the SA-TMLE context, cross-fitted Super Learner may satisfy this
under additional entropy and stability conditions on the library;
general verification is case-specific.

Proof.

Subtracting the ALE expansions of
\hat{\Psi}
and
\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}
:

\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}-\hat{\Psi}=-\frac{1}{n}D^{*}(O_{i})+\underbrace{\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{k\neq i}D^{*}(O_{k})}_{a_{i}}+\underbrace{R_{\mathrm{rem}}^{(-i)}-R_{\mathrm{rem}}}_{b_{i}}.

Since
\|D^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq M
,
|a_{i}|\leq M/n
deterministically.
For
b_{i}
: the bilinear structure of
R_{\mathrm{rem}}
gives

b_{i}=\int(\hat{\eta}_{1}^{(-i)}-\eta_{1}^{0})(\hat{\eta}_{2}^{(-i)}-\hat{\eta}_{2})\,dP_{0}+\int(\hat{\eta}_{1}^{(-i)}-\hat{\eta}_{1})(\hat{\eta}_{2}-\eta_{2}^{0})\,dP_{0}+o_{P}(r_{n})
.
By Cauchy–Schwarz and condition (
S
),
each term is
O_{P}(r_{n}\cdot n^{-1/2})
, giving
\max_{i}|b_{i}|=O_{P}(r_{n}/n^{1/2})
.
Setting
\delta_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}
gives
\max_{i}|\delta_{i}|=O_{P}(n^{-1})
at
r_{n}=O(n^{-1/2})
,
and
n^{-1}\sum_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}=O_{P}(r_{n}^{2}/n)
.
∎

5.1.3
Jackknife Consistency

The LOO expansion of Lemma
5.1
shows that
n\delta_{i}=O_{P}(1)
:
the rescaled residuals are individually tight. Whether the empirical quadratic
sum
(n-1)\sum_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}
concentrates around a limit depends on the
dependence structure of the LOO remainder perturbations
\{R_{\mathrm{rem}}^{(-i)}-R_{\mathrm{rem}}\}
. This is not implied by Lemma
5.1
alone, since the
leave-one-out fits share
n-1
training observations and the perturbations
are therefore correlated. We state this as a separate condition.

Proposition 5.1

(LOO Remainder Variance Stabilization)
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5.1
, Assumption
5.1
,
and finite fourth moments of
D^{*}
, in the near-boundary regime
(
n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to c_{R}>0
):

C_{n}\;:=\;(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{i}^{2}\;\xrightarrow{L^{2}}\;c_{R},

(9)

where
\delta_{i}
are the residuals of Lemma
5.1
and

c_{R}=\lim_{n\to\infty}n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
.
Specifically:

a.

(Expectation):

\mathbb{E}[C_{n}]\to c_{R}
.
The LOO linearization of Assumption
5.1
identifies the
limit directly via
n^{-1}\sum_{i}\psi_{n,i}^{2}\to_{P}c_{R}
.

b.

(Concentration):

\mathrm{Var}(C_{n})\to 0
.
This also uses Assumption
5.1
: the direct variance
calculation exploits the LOO linearization structure to show

\mathrm{Var}(n^{-1}\sum_{i}\psi_{n,i}^{2})\to 0
.

Proof.

We show
\mathbb{E}[(C_{n}-c_{R})^{2}]\to 0
by establishing (a)
\mathbb{E}[C_{n}]\to c_{R}

and (b)
\mathrm{Var}(C_{n})\to 0
separately.

(a) Expectation.

From Lemma
5.1
,
\delta_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}
with
|a_{i}|\leq M/n

deterministically, so
|\mathbb{E}[C_{n}]-(n-1)\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[b_{i}^{2}]|=O(n^{-1})
.
Under Assumption
5.1
, the LOO linearization gives

(n-1)\sum_{i}b_{i}^{2}=n^{-1}\sum_{i}\psi_{n,i}^{2}+o_{P}(1)

via
\max_{i}|nb_{i}+\psi_{n,i}|\xrightarrow{P}0
and
\|nb_{i}\|_{L^{2}}=O(1)
.
Taking expectations (justified by uniform integrability condition (iii)):

(n-1)\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[b_{i}^{2}]\to c_{R}
by condition (ii).

Assumption 5.1

(LOO Remainder Linearization)
.

The LOO perturbations

admit a uniform

\max_{i}|nb_{i}+\psi_{n,i}|\xrightarrow{P}0
for mean-zero

satisfying: (i)

;
(ii)

(iii)

\sup_{n,i}\mathbb{E}[\psi_{n,i}^{4}]<\infty
.
Additionally, each

is a measurable function of

alone

, so that

\mathcal{T}_{n}
.

(Scope of Assumption
5.1
)
.

Assumption
5.1
is used in both parts of

: Part (a) uses the linearization to

5.1
.

(b) Concentration.

Since
|a_{i}|\leq M/n
, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

C_{n}-(n-1)\sum_{i}b_{i}^{2}=O_{P}(n^{-1})
, so it suffices to show

\mathrm{Var}(n^{-1}\sum_{i}\psi_{n,i}^{2})\to 0
.
Under the conditional array structure of Assumption
5.1
,
the
\psi_{n,i}^{2}
are conditionally independent given
\mathcal{T}_{n}

(each is a function of
O_{i}
at fixed nuisance fits),
so the conditional variance is
\leq\max_{i}\mathbb{E}[\psi_{n,i}^{4}\mid\mathcal{T}_{n}]/n\xrightarrow{P}0
by the fourth-moment part of condition (iii).
The second term of the law of total variance also vanishes since

n^{-1}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[\psi_{n,i}^{2}\mid\mathcal{T}_{n}]\xrightarrow{P}c_{R}

by condition (ii).
∎

Remark 5.2

(Proof note)
.

The Efron–Stein inequality is insufficient for Part (b) in the near-boundary
regime:
C_{n}\xrightarrow{P}c_{R}>0
implies
\mathbb{E}[C_{n}^{2}]\to c_{R}^{2}>0
,
so the per-swap bound yields only the trivially true
\mathrm{Var}(C_{n})=O(1)
.
The direct variance calculation uses the conditional independence structure
of Assumption
5.1
: given
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, the
\psi_{n,i}^{2}

are independent, so the conditional variance vanishes at rate
n^{-1}
,
and the total variance vanishes by the law of total variance.

Theorem 5.1

(Jackknife Variance Consistency)
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5.1
:

i.

(Strong decay regime,
n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to 0
):

n\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
.
No assumption beyond Lemma
5.1
is required.

ii.

(Near-boundary regime,
n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})\to c_{R}>0
):

n\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}+c_{R}
.
The jackknife tracks the total sampling variance; the sandwich stays at

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
.

In both regimes,
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})]-\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))=O(n^{-3/2})
, whereas
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi})]-\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))=-c_{R}/n+o(n^{-1})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
5.1
,
\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}-\hat{\Psi}=-n^{-1}D^{*}(O_{i})+\delta_{i}
. Inserting into (
7
) and expanding:

\displaystyle n\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{n-1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i}\bigl[D^{*}(O_{i})-\bar{D}_{n}\bigr]^{2}}_{=:\,A_{n}}\underbrace{-\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\sum_{i}\bigl[D^{*}(O_{i})-\bar{D}_{n}\bigr]\delta_{i}}_{=:\,B_{n}}+\underbrace{(n-1)\sum_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}}_{=:\,C_{n}}.

(10)

A_{n}\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
by the LLN.

|B_{n}|\leq 2\sqrt{n}(n^{-1}\sum_{i}(D^{*}(O_{i}))^{2})^{1/2}\max_{i}|\delta_{i}|=O_{P}(n^{1/2})\cdot O_{P}(n^{-1})=O_{P}(n^{-1/2})\to 0

using Assumption
2.3
and Lemma
5.1
.
For part (i),
C_{n}\leq n\max_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}=n\cdot O_{P}(n^{-2})=O_{P}(n^{-1})\to 0
since
c_{R}=0
.
For part (ii),
C_{n}\xrightarrow{P}c_{R}
by Proposition
5.1
.
Combining,
n\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}\xrightarrow{P}\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
(part i) or

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}+c_{R}
(part ii).

Bias claim:
The finite-sample bias of
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}
for the total variance

(\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}+c_{R})/n
is the cross-term
B_{n}/n=O(n^{-3/2})
; the sandwich
misses
c_{R}/n
by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Remark 5.3

(The Two Limits Are Consistent)
.

In the strong decay regime
c_{R}=0
, so parts (i) and (ii) both give

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
: no contradiction. In the near-boundary regime, part (i) does not
apply (its hypothesis
c_{R}=0
fails), and part (ii) is the relevant result.
The common confusion — “how can JK converge to
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
and also correct
for
c_{R}
?” — arises from applying part (i) in the regime where only
part (ii) holds.

5.1.4
Asymptotic Validity

Theorem 5.2

(Jackknife Wald Interval Validity)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.4
and the conditions of
Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.3
, the studentized
jackknife pivot satisfies:

T_{n}\;:=\;\frac{\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0})}{\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})}}\;\Rightarrow\;N(0,1).

(11)

Consequently,
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{JK}}_{1-\alpha}=\hat{\Psi}\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})}

achieves asymptotically exact coverage:
P(\Psi(P_{0})\in\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{JK}}_{1-\alpha})\to 1-\alpha
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.3
and Assumption
5.2
,
the bootstrap pivot
\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))/\sqrt{n\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
.
The numerical identity
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{HC}}\equiv\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}
(Definition
5.3
) and
the consistency
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}\xrightarrow{P}1
(from
Theorems
5.1
and
5.3
, both estimating

(\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}+c_{R})/n
) then give
T_{n}=T_{n}^{\rm boot}\cdot\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}}\Rightarrow N(0,1)
by Slutsky’s theorem. The argument is identical when

c_{R}=0
.
∎

Remark 5.4

(Alternative direct proof)
.

An alternative proof of Theorem
5.2
would establish
N(0,1)

convergence of
T_{n}
directly from the LOO triangular array

\{Z_{ni}\}:=\{-n(\hat{\Psi}^{(-i)}-\hat{\Psi})\}
, without invoking the
bootstrap. The main obstacle is that off-diagonal covariances

\mathrm{Cov}(Z_{ni},Z_{nj}\mid\mathcal{T}_{n})
do not vanish under
cross-fitting alone, as the LOO nuisance fits share
n-1
training
observations. Resolving this under entropy conditions on the nuisance class
is an open problem; the bootstrap route adopted here bypasses it via
the well-studied Mallows-2 condition of Assumption
5.2
.

5.2
The Pairs Cluster Bootstrap

5.2.1
Definition

Definition 5.2

(Pairs Bootstrap Variance Estimator)
.

Let
\{O_{1}^{*},\ldots,O_{n}^{*}\}
be a resample drawn with replacement from

\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{n}\}
. The bootstrap replication
\hat{\Psi}^{*}
is the
ALE computed on this resample. After
B
independent resamples:

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}(\hat{\Psi})\;=\;\frac{1}{B-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\bigl(\hat{\Psi}^{(b)}-\bar{\Psi}^{*}\bigr)^{2},\quad\bar{\Psi}^{*}=B^{-1}\sum_{b}\hat{\Psi}^{(b)}.

(12)

5.2.2
Bootstrap Consistency

We first state the bootstrap regularity condition explicitly. Let

T_{n}:=\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))
and
T_{n}^{*}:=\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Psi}^{*}-\hat{\Psi})
, where
\hat{\Psi}^{*}
is the ALE recomputed on a pairs bootstrap
resample. Let
\mathcal{D}_{n}=\sigma(O_{1},\ldots,O_{n})
denote the data

\sigma
-algebra and
d_{2}
the Mallows-2 (Wasserstein-2) distance between
probability distributions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Assumption 5.2

(Mallows-2 Bootstrap Consistency)
.

i.

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]<\infty
.

ii.

The bootstrap distribution of
T_{n}^{*}
is second-order distributionally
consistent for
T_{n}
in the Mallows-2 sense:

d_{2}\!\Bigl(\mathcal{L}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}),\;\mathcal{L}(T_{n})\Bigr)\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0,

(13)

where
\mathcal{L}^{*}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})
denotes the conditional law
of
T_{n}^{*}
given the data.

Remark 5.5

(Verifying Assumption
5.2
)
.

Assumption
5.2
is the Mallows-2 (Wasserstein-2) bootstrap
consistency condition studied by
2
and

5
. The latter show that
L^{2}
moment consistency
requires an additional maximal inequality beyond distributional consistency;
their condition is applicable under entropy bounds on the nuisance function
class, covering fixed-weight ensembles and smooth sieves;
verification for generic adaptive learners is case-specific.
Condition (i) holds since
\mathrm{Var}(T_{n})=\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}+c_{R}+o(1)
is bounded.

Theorem 5.3

(Bootstrap Variance Consistency)
.

Under Assumptions
2.1
–
2.4
and
Assumption
5.2
, with
B\to\infty
:

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}(\hat{\Psi})\;\xrightarrow{P}\;\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0})\bigr).

(14)

The limit is the
full
sampling variance from Theorem
3.1
,
including the remainder variance
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
and the covariance
term
2\,\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{D}_{n},R_{\mathrm{rem}})
. No LOO stability condition is
required.

Proof.

Define the
oracle conditional bootstrap variance

V_{n}^{*}\;:=\;\mathrm{Var}^{*}({\hat{\Psi}}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})\;=\;\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrm{Var}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}).

(15)

We show (i)
V_{n}^{*}\xrightarrow{P}\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))
and
(ii)
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}-V_{n}^{*}\xrightarrow{P}0
. The conclusion follows by the
triangle inequality.

Step 1: Oracle bootstrap variance targets the sampling variance.

By Assumption
5.2
(ii), there exists a coupling

(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*},\widetilde{T}_{n})
with

\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}\sim\mathcal{L}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})
and

\widetilde{T}_{n}\sim\mathcal{L}(T_{n})
such that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}-\widetilde{T}_{n})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\bigr]\;=\;d_{2}^{2}\!\bigl(\mathcal{L}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}),\mathcal{L}(T_{n})\bigr)\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0.

(16)

Mean convergence.

By Cauchy–Schwarz and (
16
):

\bigl|\mathbb{E}^{*}[T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]-\mathbb{E}[T_{n}]\bigr|\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}-\widetilde{T}_{n}|\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}\bigr]\;\leq\;\bigl(\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}-\widetilde{T}_{n})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\bigr)^{1/2}\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0.

(17)

Second-moment convergence.
Using
|x^{2}-y^{2}|\leq|x-y|(|x|+|y|)
:

\displaystyle\bigl|\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]-\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]\bigr|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*})^{2}-\widetilde{T}_{n}^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}-\widetilde{T}_{n})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\bigr)^{1/2}\bigl(\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}+\widetilde{T}_{n})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\bigr)^{1/2}.

(18)

The first factor is
o_{P}(1)
by (
16
). The second factor is

O_{P}(1)
because
d_{2}\to_{P}0
implies

\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\to\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]
(Step 1 applied
inductively), so
\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}^{*}+\widetilde{T}_{n})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]+2\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]=O_{P}(1)
using
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]<\infty

(Assumption
5.2
(i)). Therefore

\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]\xrightarrow{P}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]
.

Variance convergence.

\mathrm{Var}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})-\mathrm{Var}(T_{n})=\bigl(\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]-\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]\bigr)-\bigl(\mathbb{E}^{*}[T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]^{2}-\mathbb{E}[T_{n}]^{2}\bigr)\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0,

(19)

using (
17
) and second-moment convergence. Dividing by
n
:

V_{n}^{*}-\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))=\frac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{Var}^{*}(T_{n}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})-\mathrm{Var}(T_{n})\bigr]\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0,

(20)

since
\mathrm{Var}(T_{n})=n\cdot\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))
.

Step 2: Monte Carlo variance estimates the oracle conditional variance.

Conditional on
\mathcal{D}_{n}
, the
B
bootstrap replications

\hat{\Psi}^{*(1)},\ldots,\hat{\Psi}^{*(B)}
are i.i.d. draws from

\mathcal{L}^{*}(\hat{\Psi}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n})
. Since

\mathbb{E}^{*}[(\hat{\Psi}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]<\infty
(from

\mathbb{E}^{*}[(T_{n}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}]=O_{P}(1)
), the conditional law of
large large numbers gives almost surely under the bootstrap measure, for each
fixed dataset:

\bar{\Psi}^{*}\;\to\;\mathbb{E}^{*}[\hat{\Psi}^{*}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}],\qquad\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b}(\hat{\Psi}^{*(b)})^{2}\;\to\;\mathbb{E}^{*}[(\hat{\Psi}^{*})^{2}\mid\mathcal{D}_{n}].

Therefore
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}(\hat{\Psi})\xrightarrow{P}V_{n}^{*}
as
B\to\infty
.

Step 3: Conclusion.

By the triangle inequality and Steps 1–2:

\bigl|\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}(\hat{\Psi})-\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))\bigr|\;\leq\;|\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}-V_{n}^{*}|+|V_{n}^{*}-\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}-\Psi(P_{0}))|\;\xrightarrow{P}\;0.

The limit is the full decomposition of Theorem
3.1
: all three
components —
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
,
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
, and the covariance term

2\,\mathrm{Cov}(\bar{D}_{n},R_{\mathrm{rem}})
— are captured through the Mallows-2
convergence, which controls second moments and hence the full sampling
variance without decomposing it.
∎

The bootstrap has three advantages over the jackknife. First, it does not
require the nuisance stability condition
\left\|\hat{\eta}^{(-i)}-\hat{\eta}\right\|_{P_{0}}=O_{P}(n^{-1/2})
, which can fail when
n
is very small or when ensemble
learning is highly nonlinear. Second, it produces a full distribution over

\hat{\Psi}
, enabling percentile, bias-corrected, and accelerated (BCa)
confidence intervals
(
7
)
that can correct for skewness in
the remainder distribution. Third, the bootstrap validity argument is cleaner
— it requires Assumption
5.2
(Mallows-2 consistency) rather than
the LOO expansion of Lemma
5.1
and Assumption
5.1
.
The Mallows-2 condition is well-understood in the bootstrap literature and
holds under standard moment and smoothness conditions

(
2
;
12
)
.

5.3
Heteroskedasticity-Corrected Sandwich

5.3.1
Motivation and Definition

The jackknife and bootstrap each require
n
(or
B
) re-fits of the full
estimator pipeline. In settings where each fit is expensive, a
computationally cheaper alternative is to apply a finite-sample inflation
factor to the standard sandwich. We propose an HC-type correction that uses
the jackknife-to-sandwich ratio as the inflation estimate.

Definition 5.3

(HC-Corrected Sandwich)
.

Let
\hat{\rho}=\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}(\hat{\Psi})/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi})
be the estimated
variance ratio from a single jackknife run. The HC-corrected sandwich is:

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{HC}}(\hat{\Psi})\;=\;\hat{\rho}\cdot\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}(\hat{\Psi}).

(21)

The corresponding confidence interval is
\hat{\Psi}\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{HC}}(\hat{\Psi})}
.

Note that
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{HC}}\equiv\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}
by construction, so the HC-corrected interval
is numerically identical to the jackknife Wald interval. Its value is
conceptual: it expresses the correction as a multiplicative inflation of the
sandwich, making explicit that the jackknife is a
scaling correction

rather than a fundamentally different estimator. The ratio
\hat{\rho}
is
itself a useful summary statistic: it estimates
1+c_{R}/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
(the
asymptotic variance ratio), providing a direct measure of how far the estimator
is from the strong decay regime.

5.4
Comparison of the Three Estimators

Proposition 5.2

(First-Order Equivalence and Assumption Hierarchy)
.

Under Assumptions
2.2
–
5.2
,

\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}=\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{HC}}+o_{P}(n^{-1})
and
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Boot}}\xrightarrow{P}1
.
The three estimators are first-order equivalent; they differ in finite-sample
performance, computational cost, and assumption requirements.

The jackknife requires
J
refits and
t_{J-1}
critical values; the bootstrap
requires
B\gg J
refits but yields BCa intervals and second-order coverage
accuracy; the HC sandwich requires no refitting but is restricted to settings
where
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{JK}}
is analytically tractable. In the simulations of
Section
7
, the jackknife performs best at small
J
; the bootstrap
is slightly conservative at
J=30
.

Extension to Clustered Data

6.1
Clustered Asymptotic Linearity

The general framework of Sections
2
–
5

extends directly to clustered data by replacing the i.i.d. observation
O_{i}

with the cluster-level data vector
O_{j}=\{O_{ijt}\}_{i,t}
and the unit

n
with the number of clusters
J
. The ALE expansion becomes:

\hat{\Psi}_{\mathrm{TMLE}}-\Psi(P_{0})=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathrm{EIC}_{j}+R_{2},

(22)

where
\mathrm{EIC}_{j}=\sum_{i,t}D^{*}(O_{ijt})
is the cluster-level efficient
influence curve (summed, not averaged, over individuals and time steps — see
Lemma 2 of
1
) and
R_{2}
is the bilinear remainder
(
3
).

6.2
ICC Amplification of the Sandwich Gap

A key new phenomenon in the clustered case is that intra-cluster correlation
amplifies the variance decomposition gap beyond what occurs in the i.i.d. setting.

Proposition 6.1

(ICC Amplification of the Variance Gap)
.

Suppose the cluster random effect induces intra-cluster correlation

\rho_{\mathrm{ICC}}=\sigma_{b}^{2}/(\sigma_{b}^{2}+\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2})

and that all
n_{j}=m
(balanced clusters). Under the SA-TMLE DGP,
the remainder variance satisfies:

\mathrm{Var}(R_{2})\;=\;\underbrace{V_{0}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{i.i.d.}\\
\text{baseline}\end{subarray}}\;+\;\underbrace{\rho_{\mathrm{ICC}}\cdot m\cdot V_{\mathrm{ICC}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{ICC}\\
\text{amplification}\end{subarray}}\;+\;O(J^{-2}),

(23)

for positive constants
V_{0},V_{\mathrm{ICC}}
. Hence the variance gap

\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\Psi}_{\mathrm{TMLE}})-\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/J
is increasing in ICC and in cluster size
m
.

Proof sketch.

Write
R_{2}=\int(\hat{\bar{Q}}_{Y}-\bar{Q}_{Y}^{0})({\hat{g}_{\Delta}}/{g^{0}_{\Delta}}-1)\,dP_{0}
.
The variance of
R_{2}
depends on the second moments of the nuisance errors,
which in clustered data have a within-cluster correlation component.
Specifically,
\hat{\bar{Q}}_{Y}(S_{ijt},a,W_{ijt},t)
evaluated at observations within
the same cluster
j
are correlated due to the shared random effect
b_{j}

in
\bar{Q}_{Y}^{0}
. A direct calculation shows that the cross-cluster covariance
contributes a term proportional to
\rho_{\mathrm{ICC}}\cdot m
in the variance
of
R_{2}
, giving (
23
).
∎

Proposition
6.1
explains the simulation finding in

1
that the variance ratio
\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{Sand}}/\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\mathrm{emp}}

is approximately constant across ICC
\in\{0.01,0.05,0.10,0.20\}
while
the absolute RMSE increases with ICC: the ratio is dominated by
c_{R}/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
,
which is approximately constant because both
\mathrm{Var}(R_{2})
and

\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/J
scale with ICC in similar ways at the parametric nuisance rates
used in the simulation.

Definition 6.1

(Leave-One-Cluster-Out Jackknife)
.

(24)

5.1
extend to the cluster level, with

8
Discussion

We have developed a general theory of inference for asymptotically linear
estimators when second-order remainder variance is non-negligible relative to
the first-order influence-function variance. The central findings are:

1.

In the near-boundary regime, the sandwich targets the influence-function
variance
\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}/n
rather than the total sampling variance, a gap
identified structurally by the variance decomposition and characterized
by Theorem
4.1
.

2.

The jackknife, pairs bootstrap, and HC correction each asymptotically
target the total variance under the conditions established here, and yield
valid confidence intervals. They differ in finite-sample accuracy,
assumption requirements, and computational cost.

3.

The variance ratio
\hat{\rho}
provides an observable indicator of
near-boundary operation that does not require knowledge of the remainder
structure, as illustrated by the simulations.

4.

In clustered data with shared random effects, the ICC amplifies the
sandwich gap, a leading-order effect captured by Proposition
6.1

and consistent with the simulation evidence for SW-CRTs.

Connections and open questions.

Cross-fitting eliminates first-order empirical process terms but does not
eliminate
\mathrm{Var}(R_{\mathrm{rem}})
, since the remainder is a
P_{0}
-expectation
of a second-order product (Remark
3.2
). The near-boundary gap
could also be addressed analytically via a Bartlett correction, requiring
an estimate of
c_{R}/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
; this is complementary to the resampling
approach here. Open problems include non-asymptotic coverage bounds for the
jackknife pivot and an analytic estimator for
c_{R}/\sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{EIF}}
that avoids
full jackknife refitting.

Conclusion.

The near-boundary regime identifies a structural gap between asymptotic
linearity and valid first-order inference that is not resolved by standard
product-rate conditions. The variance decomposition, sandwich characterization,
and refined estimators here give a sharp account of this gap for general ALEs
under the stated conditions. The most delicate assumptions concern the
uniform LOO linearization and bootstrap moment consistency in highly adaptive
nuisance regimes; extensions to these settings remain open.
