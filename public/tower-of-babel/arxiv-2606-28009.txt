Uniqueness, analyticity and mixing for Gibbs point processes via spectral gaps
Andreas Göbel, Matthew Jenssen, Marcus Michelen, Marcus Pappik, Will Perkins, Leon Schiller

Abstract
. A Gibbs point process is a model for particles interacting in the continuum through a potential. Among the most classical examples is the hard-sphere model, where given an activity parameter λ \lambda , a radius r r , and a bounded set Λ ⊂ ℝ d \Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} one samples a Poisson process of intensity λ \lambda in Λ \Lambda conditioned on the points forming the centers of a packing of spheres of radius r r . We prove uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measure, analyticity of the pressure, and various notions of spatial and temporal mixing for activities up to what we define as the spectral threshold λ spec \lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}} of the potential. For each fixed dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , this improves the uniqueness and analyticity bounds for the hard-sphere model. As d → ∞ d\to\infty , we obtain not only the first bound with a growing improvement over the classical bounds, but one whose improvement grows exponentially fast. We also prove an optimal mixing time bound for heat bath dynamics for the hard-sphere model up to an expected density of Θ ⁡ ( d / 2 d ) \Theta(d/2^{d}) , which is the first result that asymptotically matches the maximum density for rapid mixing predicted by Parisi and Zamponi. We also exhibit repulsive, radial pair potentials for which λ spec = + ∞ \lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}=+\infty , thereby showing that there are Gibbs point processes with such potentials that provably exhibit no phase transition at any activity λ > 0 \lambda>0 . Further, in dimensions 8 8 and 24 24 we exhibit such a potential with no phase transition for which the work of Cohn-Kumar-Miller-Radchenko-Viazovska proves that the unique ground state at any fixed density is given by the E 8 E_{8} and Leech lattices, respectively. Our work builds upon a 2013 work of Kondratiev–Kuna–Ohlerich which implicitly defined λ spec \lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}} and demonstrated a spectral gap for a Glauber-like continuum birth-death dynamics. Our main work consists of showing that such a spectral gap implies several strong notions of absence of phase transition, along with analyzing the behavior of λ spec \lambda_{\mathrm{spec}} for some interesting potentials.



1.
Introduction

A Gibbs point process, or classical gas, is a model for particles interacting in the continuum through a potential. For a pair potential given by a symmetric function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we define the energy of a finite configuration
\mathbf{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
via

H(\mathbf{X}):=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset\mathbf{X}}\phi(x,y)\,.

For a bounded, measurable region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an activity
\lambda>0
, the
partition function
is defined by

Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\Lambda^{k}}e^{-H(\mathbf{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,.

The
finite-volume Gibbs measure
is defined by

\mu_{\Lambda,\lambda}(A)=\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\int_{\Lambda^{k}}\bm{1}{\{\mathbf{x}\in A}\}\cdot e^{-H(\mathbf{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,.

One of the central thermodynamic quantities is the pressure, first defined by defining the
finite-volume pressure
at activity
\lambda
by

p_{\Lambda}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)}\cdot\log Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)

and subsequently defining
p(\lambda)
via
\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\Lambda}(\lambda)
over a sequence of sets
\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
such as increasing cubes.

In statistical physics, the central questions for these models concern properties of the infinite-volume limits: the uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measures, analyticity of the pressure, and spatial decay of correlations. In the computer science literature, the central questions concern sampling and approximation in finite volume: mixing times for Markov chains such as Glauber dynamics and approximation algorithms for the partition function. Essentially, each of these properties listed says something about the model behaving in a “gas-like” or “fluid” fashion; in fact, strategies that prove one of these properties for a specific potential
\phi
and activity
\lambda
often prove many others simultaneously. For many choices of pair potential
\phi
, it is expected that the model undergoes a
phase transition
: one definition of this is that for small
\lambda
there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure while at other
\lambda
there is more than one such measure.

The most well-studied choice of potential
\phi
is the
hard-sphere model
, where one sets
\phi(x,y)=+\infty
if
\|x-y\|_{2}<2r
and
0
otherwise. The hard-sphere model is supported on pointsets that are
2r
-separated, meaning that the points form the centers of a sphere packing of balls of radius
r>0
.
It is widely believed in the physics literature that in dimensions
2
and
3
there is a phase transition for the hard-sphere model going back at least to the seminal 1957 work of Alder and Wainwright
[
1
]
; various physics works suggest phase transitions in other dimensions (see e.g.
[
21
]
for a treatment up to
d=50
) as well as asymptotically as
d\to\infty

[
26
,
52
]
. Dimension
d=2
is particularly subtle; here Richthammer
[
54
]
proved a Mermin-Wagner type theorem, showing that all infinite-volume Gibbs measures are translation invariant. This omits the possibility of crystallization, but does not rule out the possibility of phase transition breaking rotational order, which has been predicted by recent works in physics
[
5
]
. See
[
43
]
for a survey on the physics literature on the hard sphere model.

Classical techniques for understanding when a Gibbs point process is in the fluid phase—and in particular for showing uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measure and analyticity of the pressure—include cluster expansion methods: one expands
\log Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)
as a power series for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and proves analyticity of
\log Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)
in a complex disk
|\lambda|<\lambda_{0}
for some fixed
\lambda_{0}>0
uniformly in the domain
\Lambda
. For these techniques, the relevant quantity is the
temperedness constant
defined by

(1)

C_{\phi}=\sup_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|1-e^{-\phi(x,y)}|\,\mathrm{d}y

which is always assumed to be finite.
In the case where
\phi
is non-negative, we say that it is
repulsive
. For repulsive potentials Groeneveld, Penrose, and Ruelle
[
30
,
53
,
55
]
showed in various forms that one can take
\lambda_{0}=1/(eC_{\phi})
, which shows analyticity and uniqueness for
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{0})
.

Traditional routes to proving uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measures go through the Kirkwood–Salsburg equations, a hierarchy of equestions for the correlation functions satisfied by infinite-volume Gibbs measures. Approaches that directly analyze the Kirkwood–Salsburg equations are similar in spirit to cluster expansion approaches, and such results prove uniqueness up to
1/(eC_{\phi})
.
We refer the reader to Ruelle’s classic book for further context on this approach
[
56
]
. A 1970 work of Meeron
[
44
]
proved uniqueness and analyticity up to
1/C_{\phi}
using a novel interpolation approach along with a variant of the Kirkwood–Salsburg equations. A sequence of works by Michelen–Perkins
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
proved uniqueness and analyticity along with various spatial and temporal mixing up to
e/C_{\phi}
as well as
e/\Delta_{\phi}
, where
\Delta_{\phi}<C_{\phi}
is a quantity introduced in
[
48
]
called the potential-weighted connective constant; these works were built upon tree-like integral recursions and were inspired by work of Weitz
[
59
]
in an analogous discrete setting. Other approaches to proving uniqueness involve Dobrushin uniqueness
[
33
]
, disagreement percolation
[
7
,
16
,
32
]
, and Markov chain mixing
[
31
]
.

Our contribution is to establish uniqueness and analyticity through a different approach. A remarkable but little-noticed work by Kondratiev, Kuna and Ohlerich
[
39
]
shows that under a certain positive-definiteness condition on an integral operator defined in terms of
\phi
and
\lambda
, a continuous-time Markov chain known as
spatial birth-death dynamics
exhibits a spectral gap.
In
[
39
]
, the authors used this to show that for certain potentials there is a spectral gap for all activities and the corresponding models “should not show any phase transition…at any activity.”
Our main work proves this in a very strong form, showing that this spectral gap condition for the dynamics implies uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measure, analyticity of the pressure, and various strong forms of spatial and temporal mixing. These implications are reminiscient of the implications in Dobrushin and Shlosman’s theory of complete analyticity in the setting of bounded-range lattice spin models
[
18
]
; the novelty is in the weakness of the assumption (the spectral gap of birth–death dynamics) and the generality to continuum models with unbounded range potentials.

Using these implications (and analyzing specific potentials), we will prove that for certain pair potentials there is uniqueness and analyticity at
all
activities, thus providing the first rigorous proof of the existence of a Gibbs point process with a non-trivial, radial pair potential that lacks a phase transition. As we will see below, we will exhibit such a pair potential for which the breakthrough work of Cohn-Kumar-Miller-Radchenko-Viazovska
[
13
]
proves that the ground state at any fixed density is uniquely given by the
E_{8}
and Leech lattices in dimension
8
and
24
respectively.
Additionally, for classical examples such as the hard-sphere model, our approach demonstrates dramatically improved bounds for the fluid regime for every dimension
d\geq 2
, with improvements that increase exponentially as the dimension grows.
To our knowledge, this is the first asymptotically growing improvement in the literature.

1.1.
Main results

To state our results, as well as those of
[
39
]
,
for a repulsive, tempered potential
\phi
, define the
spectral threshold

\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}
via

(2)

\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}:=\left(\sup_{f\in L^{2}:\|f\|_{2}=1}\iint f(x)f(y)(e^{-\phi(x,y)}-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{-1}

where the supremum is over real-valued functions
f
and if the supremum on the right-hand side is
0
then we interpret
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}=+\infty
. In the special case where
\phi
is translation invariant, we may define
g(x)=1-\exp(-\phi(x,0))
and we will have

(3)

\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=\left(\esssup_{\xi}(-\widehat{g}(\xi))\right)^{-1}

where
\widehat{g}
is the Fourier transform of
g
(see Lemma
8.1
for a proof). We will also assume that
\phi
decays exponentially, meaning
1-\exp(-\phi(x,y))\leq\exp(-\Omega(\mathrm{dist}(x,y)))
(see Definition
2.1
for a precise definition).

Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially. Then for all
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
, there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure and the infinite-volume pressure is analytic on
[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}})
.

To illustrate the uses of Theorem
1.1
, we give several examples, computing or bounding
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
for specific potentials and deducing the consqeuences.

1.1.1.
Hard sphere model

A first example is the
hard sphere model
, perhaps the most studied specific potential, and one that illustrates the quantiative improvements given by Theorem
1.1
. For an interaction radius
2r_{d}
we define

(4)

\phi(x,y)=\begin{cases}+\infty&\text{ if }\|x-y\|_{2}\leq 2r_{d}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

where we often take
r_{d}
to be the radius of the ball of volume
1
in which case the temperedness constant is given by
C_{\phi}=2^{d}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

For the case of the hard sphere potential, as
d\to\infty
we have

\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=\left(\frac{e}{2}+o(1)\right)^{d/2}\cdot C_{\phi}^{-1}\,.

In particular, we obtain not only the first bound for uniqueness and analyticity thresholds for the hard sphere model that allows
\lambda C_{\phi}
to increase with higher dimensions, but also an exponential improvement over previous results.
We in fact compute the leading order asymptotics of
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
(see (
75

d

\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
in terms of Bessel functions and their zeros below.

One may also parameterize the hard sphere model in terms of the expected density; for a region
\Lambda
and activity
\lambda
, define
\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)
by

(5)

\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\lambda}[|\mathbf{X}|]}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)}\,.

A basic calculation shows that
\lambda\mapsto\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)
is monotone increasing for all
\Lambda
(see e.g.
[
36

).
For repulsive potentials such as the hard sphere model, the random point process
\mathbf{X}
is always stochastically dominated by a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda
(see Lemma
2.2
). In particular, this implies that we always have the upper bound

(6)

\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\leq\lambda\,.

All prior results for the hard sphere model obtained results for
\lambda=\Theta(2^{-d})
, which translates to
\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\Theta(2^{-d})
(see
[
47
]
for a lower bound on the implicit constant).

Outside of the trivial upper bound (
6
), bounding the density is often challenging. A theorem of Jenssen–Joos–Perkins
[
36
, Theorem 2]
shows that for the hard sphere model, for
\lambda\geq 3^{-d/2}
one has

(7)

\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\geq(1+o_{d}(1))\frac{\log(2/\sqrt{3})d}{2^{d}}\,.

Since Proposition
1.2
shows

\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}\geq\left(\frac{e^{1/2}}{2^{3/2}}+o(1)\right)^{d}\geq 3^{-d/2}\,,

this shows that in fact when parametrizing in terms of the density, our results hold up to
\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\Theta(d/2^{d})
, a factor of
d
denser than the reach of previous results. As we will see below, this includes showing that we efficiently sample from the hard-sphere model using a Markov chain at expected density
\Theta(d/2^{d})
in high dimensions. A physics prediction due to Parisi and Zamponi
[
52

suggests that Markov chains should mix slowly at density above
\approx 4.8\cdot d/2^{d}
. This shows that Parisi and Zamponi predict our result is sharp up to a constant factor of the density.
We also note that the spectral threshold yields an improvement in the uniqueness and analyticity thresholds for the hard sphere model in low dimensions as well.

For the case of hard spheres in fixed dimension

\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
in terms of Bessel functions. In particular, setting

C_{\phi}\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=\operatorname{Vol}(B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1))(2\pi)^{-\nu}(j_{\nu+1,1})^{d/2}(-J_{\nu}(j_{\nu+1,1}))^{-1}

1.1.2.
Gibbs point processes with no phase transition

We next give examples of repulsive Gibbs point processes for which we prove there is no phase transition. In particular, if
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}=+\infty
then one obtains uniqueness and analyticity for all
\lambda>0
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially. Assume
\phi
is translation invariant and define
g(x)=1-e^{-\phi(x,0)}
. If
\widehat{g}(\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
then for all
\lambda\geq 0
there is a unique infinite volume Gibbs measure and the infinite volume pressure is analytic on
[0,\infty)
.

We include a short Fourier argument that shows that it is impossible for
\phi
to satisfy the hypotheses of Corollary
1.3
while simultaneously having a hard-core (Lemma
7.4
).

One can ask if potentials satisfying the conditions of Corollary
1.3
exist, and if so, whether they avoid phase transitions by not possesing sufficiently ordered ground states that might drive crystallization. We will in fact show that there are potentials with no phase transition whose ground states are ordered in the strongest sense.
Consider the potential
\phi
defined by

(8)

\phi(x,y)=\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(-\|x-y\|^{2})}\right)\,.

In this case, we see that
g(x)=\exp(-\|x\|^{2})
and so
\widehat{g}(\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and so Corollary
1.3
implies that the potential
\phi
does not exhibit a phase transition at any
\lambda>0
. While there are many choices for potential
\phi
which yield
\widehat{g}(\xi)\geq 0
for all
\xi
, the potential (
8
) is in fact a completely monotonic function of distance squared as can be seen by the expansion

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\log\left(\frac{1}{1-\exp(-\|x-y\|^{2})}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{e^{-k\|x-y\|^{2}}}{k}\,.

As such, the breakthrough work of Cohn-Kumar-Miller-Radchenko-Viazovska
[
13
]
shows that the potential
\phi
defined in (
8
) has a unique ground state in dimensions
8
and
24
up to isometries; the ground states are the
E_{8}
and Leech lattices, respectively, which were shown to be the unique optimal sphere packing configurations in work by Viazovska
[
57
]
and Cohn-Kumar-Miller-Radchenko-Viazovska
[
12
]
. More precisely, this means that if we fix any density
\rho
, the unique energy minimizer over point configurations at density
\rho
in dimensions
8
and
24
are given by a rescaled version of the
E_{8}
and Leech lattice respectively. Despite the potential having highly structured and unique ground states at each density, Corollary
1.3
shows that at
all
activities the Gibbs point process remains gas-like.

It is conjectured that in dimension
2
completely monotonic potentials have unique ground state given by the hexagonal lattice—which is the unique optimizer for the sphere packing problem—but this remains open (see Montgomery
[
50
]
for a proof that the hexagonal lattice is optimal among lattices, and
[
13
]
for further context and references).

1.1.3.
Gaussian core model

The Gaussian core model is another widely studied potential for which we obtain a significant quantitative improvement.
The Gaussian core model is given by the potential

\phi(x,y)=\beta e^{-\|x-y\|_{2}^{2}/2}

where
\beta>0
is an inverse temperature parameter. We will work in the regime where
\beta
is fixed and
d\to\infty
. In this setting, work of Cohn and de Courcy-Ireland
[
11
]
show that the approximate ground states at each fixed density are given by the point configurations of random lattices.

In the regime where
\beta
is fixed and
d\to\infty
we obtain an exponential improvement over prior results. In particular, defining the temperedness constant

C_{\phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}1-e^{-\phi(x,0)}\,\mathrm{d}x

then prior results
[
47
,
48
]
showed uniqueness and analyticity up to
\lambda\leq e/C_{\phi}
. We will see in Lemma
8.4
that there is a constant
C_{\beta}>0
so that
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}\geq C_{\beta}2^{d}/C_{\phi}
.

1.2.
Temporal and spatial mixing

We also prove optimal mixing (i.e., asymptotically optimal mixing times) of a natural Markov chain known as
block dynamics
or
heat bath dynamics
. Block dynamics with update radius
L
on a bounded measurable set
\Lambda
is the Markov chain where given a configuration
X
, the next configuration is obtained by choosing a point
x\in\Lambda
uniformly at random and updating the configuration in
B_{L}(x)
according to the finite-volume Gibbs measure associated to
\lambda,\phi
subject to the boundary condition
X\cap B_{L}(x)^{c}
(see Definition
6.1
for the formal details). We prove optimal mixing of block dynamics up to
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially. Then for all
\lambda<\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}
, there is some
L_{0}=L_{0}(\lambda,\phi)>0
so that for all activity functions
{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\lambda
and block size
L\geq L_{0}
, the block dynamics with update radius
L
have mixing time
O(\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)\log\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda))
for all boxes
\Lambda=[-n,n]^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

While the connection between a spectral gap and mixing bounds for the corresponding dynamics is classical, it often provides a sub-optimal mixing bound. In particular, using only the information of the spectral gap provided by
[
39
]
, one would obtain
1
1

1

In the case where the potential does not have a hard core, one can bound the spectral gap of block dynamics to that of the birth-death dynamics by a comparison argument.
a mixing time of
O(\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)^{2})
rather than our bound of
O(\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)\log\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda))
, which is optimal.

Underpinning all of our results is the use of a notion of spatial mixing. In the case
\phi
is finite-range, the notion of strong spatial mixing is sufficient to capture the change in a Gibbs measure under the inclusion of an additional point. Since our potentials may have infinite-range, we introduce an additional notion of spatial mixing to capture exponential decay of the influence of including one additional point. We call this notion
single-site Strong Spatial Mixing
(single-site SSM).

To formalize this, for a given measurable function
{\bm{\lambda}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\lambda]
, we let
\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
denote the Gibbs distribution on
\Lambda
with activity function
{\bm{\lambda}}
(see Section
2.1
for a precise definition of
\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
).
For a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and activity function
{\bm{\lambda}}
, define
{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\lambda]
via
{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}(v)={\bm{\lambda}}(v)e^{-\phi(x,v)}
(see Section
2.2
for our more general definition of an activity function with pinnings). For a Borel set
\Delta
, a bounded set
\Lambda
, and activity functions
{\bm{\lambda}}
and
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}
we define the projected total variation distance
\|\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}\|_{\Delta}
as the total variation distance between the pushforwards of
\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
and
\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}
under the map
X\mapsto X\cap\Delta
for a point set
X
.

Definition 1.5

(Single-site Strong Spatial Mixing)
.

A potential
\phi
satisfies
single-site Strong Spatial Mixing
(single-site SSM) up to some activity
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
if there exist constants
C<\infty
and
m>0
such that, for all bounded measurable
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all activity functions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq\lambda
it holds that

\|\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{x}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\leq C\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^{\prime})},

where for the distance
\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^{\prime})
we take
\inf\{\|x-y\|_{2}\mid y\in\Lambda^{\prime}\}
.

We also recall the notion of Strong Spatial Mixing (SSM) for point measures, introduced in
[
46
]
.

Definition 1.6

(Strong Spatial Mixing)
.

A potential
\phi
satisfies
Strong Spatial Mixing
(SSM) up to some activity
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
if there exist constants
C>0
and
m>0
so that for all bounded and measurable
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all activity functions
{\bm{\lambda}},{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}\leq\lambda
it holds that

\|\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\leq C\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda^{\prime},\mathrm{supp}({\bm{\lambda}}-{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}))}\,.

We note that when
\phi
has finite-range, SSM implies single-site SSM. In general, the two are not immediately equivalent.
We will show that both occur up to
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
when
\phi
decays exponentially, which yields various algorithmic implications discussed below.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially. Then
\phi
satisfies SSM and single-site SSM up to
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
.

In fact, we will show that in the setting of Theorem
1.7
, single-site SSM implies SSM (see Proposition
3.10
) but this appears to be non-trivial and uses the machinery built for SSM in
[
46
]
. A similar argument shows that SSM implies single-site SSM in the setting of Theorem
1.7
.

As a final notion of spatial mixing, we will see that the
k
-point correlation functions of the infinite-volume measure (which is unique by Theorem
1.1
) enjoy exponential decay. For an activity function
{\bm{\lambda}}
for which there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure, let
\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
denote the
k
-point correlation function for the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure.

We will see exponential decay of
k
-point correlation functions up to
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially. For each

We then move in Section

In the analogous discrete case—or similarly when the potential is of finite range—uniqueness follows essentially immediately from strong spatial mixing along with the DLR equations. Our setting is a bit trickier, and in fact served as one of the main motivations of our definition of single-site SSM. To show uniqueness, the DLR equations state that for an infinite-volume Gibbs measure, the point configuration on a finite set

subject to the boundary conditions of the point configuration outside of

We have now arrived at the most technical section of the paper, Section

As in the case of Dobrushin–Shlosman, our proof is by induction and deals
with ratios of partition functions applied for complex activities, but we have two serious obstacles: first, since we are working in the continuum, the nature of such an induction is significantly less clear; second, our interactions need not have bounded range. This requires us to introduce various intermediate steps that must be proven simultaneously in our inductive proof in order to overcome these two challenges. The core of our inductive proof is Proposition
5.2
containing four statements concerning zero-freeness of partition functions along with approximations of the influence of altering the activity function
{\bm{\lambda}}
by introducing a single point. We then prove Proposition
5.2
with a fairly involved four-part strong inductive step (see Figure

[

[

In Section

[

Finally, in Section

2.1.
Finite-volume Gibbs measure

A repulsive pair potential on

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}

With some abuse of notation, we extend this definition to finite tuples

We will impose the following decay property on the potential
\phi

Definition 2.1

(Exponential decay of the potential)

We say that a repulsive potential

1-e^{-\phi(x,y)}\leq Be^{-\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}\text{ for }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Fix some repulsive potential

\Lambda

The right-hand side of the expression above is normalized by
\frac{e^{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)}}{Z_{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}

is called the partition function on
\Lambda
, and where we abbreviate
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathbf{x}}\coloneqq\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(x_{j})

We write
\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[f]

with respect to
\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
.
Lastly, if the activity function is constant

2.4.
Birth-death dynamics

Spatial birth-death dynamics are a natural Markov process associated with a potential
\phi

\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

For a function
f

\mathcal{N}

D_{x}^{+}
and
D_{x}^{-}

D_{x}^{+}f(X)\coloneqq f(X\cup\{x\})-f(X)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptD_{x}^{-}f(X)\coloneqq f(X\setminus\{x\})-f(X)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},X\in\mathcal{N}.

Given a repulsive potential
\phi

\boldsymbol{\lambda}

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(16)

L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f(X)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in X}D_{x}^{-}f(X)+\int_{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(x)e^{-D_{x}^{+}H(X)}D_{x}^{+}f(X)\,\mathrm{d}x

L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
with dense domain

\mathcal{D}_{0}(L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\coloneqq\{f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\mid\exists C\geq 0:\left\lvert f\right\rvert\leq C\text{ and }f(X)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{N}\text{ with }\left\lvert X\right\rvert>C\}.

The Dirichlet form associated with
L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is given by

(17)

\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)=\left\langle-L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f,g\right\rangle_{\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\text{ for }f,g\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}),

and we write
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)=\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,f)
.
The following useful properties of
L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
were proven in
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
.

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is closable, and its closure is associated with a self-adjoint extension of
L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
with respect to
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
.
By abuse of notation, we write
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and
L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
for the extensions and denote their respective domains by
\mathcal{D}(L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.

(2)

for every
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
,
P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\coloneqq e^{tL_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defines a Markov semi-group on
L^{p}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.

(3)

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.

Moreover, we have the following useful connection between a Poincaré inequality for
L_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
and contraction of the semi-group
(P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
3
, Theorem 4.2.5]
)
.

Suppose there is some
\gamma<\infty
such that
{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)\leq\gamma\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
.
Then, for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
and
t\geq 0
, it holds that

\left\lVert P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f-\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[f]\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}\leq\sqrt{{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)}\ e^{-t/\gamma}.

Furthermore, the following explicit representation of the Dirichlet form will be useful.

Lemma 2.5
.

For all
f,g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)=\int_{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[e^{-D_{x}^{+}H}D_{x}^{+}fD_{x}^{+}g\big]\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

By definition of the generator
L
, we have

\displaystyle-\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\lambda}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}g(\mathbf{X})D_{x}^{-}f(\mathbf{X})\Big]+\int_{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[e^{-D_{x}^{+}H(\mathbf{X})}g(\mathbf{X})D_{x}^{+}f(\mathbf{X})\big]\mathrm{d}x.

Applying the GNZ equation (
12
) to the first term, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\lambda}\Big[\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}g(\mathbf{X})D_{x}^{-}f(\mathbf{X})\Big]=\int_{\Lambda}\boldsymbol{\lambda}(x)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[e^{-D_{x}^{+}H(\mathbf{X})}g(\mathbf{X}\cup\{x\})D_{x}^{-}f(\mathbf{X}\cup\{x\})\big]\mathrm{d}x.

Noting that
D_{x}^{-}f(\mathbf{X}\cup\{x\})=-D_{x}^{+}f(\mathbf{X})
and plugging the result back into our expression for
-\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)
then yields the lemma.
∎

2.5.
Elementary inequalities and Poisson moments

We will make frequent use of the following standard elementary inequality:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be complex numbers with
|z_{j}|,|w_{j}|\leq\theta
for all
j
. Then,

\left|\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right|\leq\theta^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|w_{j}-z_{j}|.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
, noting the
n=1
case is trivial. For tuples
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n+1}
and
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n+1}
with
|z_{j}|,|w_{j}|\leq\theta
for all
j\leq n+1
define
Z=\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}
and
W=\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}
and note

\left|\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}z_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}w_{j}\right|\leq|z_{n+1}|\cdot|Z-W|+|W|\cdot|z_{n+1}-w_{n+1}|\,.

The proof follows by induction.
∎

We will also make frequent use of various exponential moment identities for Poisson random variables due to our frequent use of Poisson domination (Lemma
2.2
).

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\gamma\geq 0
and let
\xi\sim\textup{Pois}(\gamma)
.
For all
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
K\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it holds that:

(1)

\mathbb{E}[e^{b\xi}]=\exp((e^{b}-1)\gamma)
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\xi>K}e^{b\xi}]=\exp((e^{b}-1)\gamma)\cdot\mathbb{P}(\xi^{\prime}>K)
, where
\xi^{\prime}\sim\textup{Pois}(\gamma e^{b})
.

(2)

\mathbb{E}[\xi e^{b\xi}]=\gamma e^{b}\exp((e^{b}-1)\gamma)
.

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi^{2}e^{b\xi}]=(\gamma e^{b}+\gamma^{2}e^{2b})\exp((e^{b}-1)\gamma)
.

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi^{3}e^{b\xi}]=(\gamma e^{b}+3\gamma^{2}e^{2b}+\gamma^{3}e^{3b})\exp((e^{b}-1)\gamma)
.

Proof.

For
1
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{\xi>K}e^{b\xi}]=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{k>K}\frac{\gamma^{k}}{k!}e^{bk}=e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma e^{b}}\cdot\mathbb{P}(\xi^{\prime}>K).

To compute
\mathbb{E}[e^{b\xi}]
we follow the same calculation without the indicator.

For
2
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi e^{b\xi}]=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\gamma^{k}}{k!}ke^{bk}=\gamma e^{b}e^{-\gamma}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\gamma^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}e^{b(k-1)}=\gamma e^{b}e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma e^{b}}.

Next, for
3
, we first write
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{2}e^{b\xi}]=\mathbb{E}[\xi(\xi-1)e^{b\xi}]+\mathbb{E}[\xi e^{b\xi}]
.
For calculating the second term, we use
2
.
For the first term, we have

\mathbb{E}[\xi(\xi-1)e^{b\xi}]=e^{-\gamma}\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\gamma^{k}}{k!}k(k-1)e^{bk}=\gamma^{2}e^{2b}e^{-\gamma}e^{\gamma e^{b}}.

Lastly, for
4
, we write
\xi^{3}=\xi(\xi-1)(\xi-2)+3\xi(\xi-1)+\xi
and then proceed just like before.
∎

3.
Spatial mixing from spectral gap

In this section, we prove that a uniform Poincaré inequality for the spatial birth-death dynamics implies single-site SSM.
The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially with constants
B
and
\alpha
(see Definition
2.1
).
Suppose there is a constant
\gamma<\infty
such that, for all activity functions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq\lambda
and all bounded, measurable
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)\leq\gamma\ \mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}).

Then
\phi
satisfies single-site SSM up to
\lambda
for constants
C\coloneqq C(\gamma,\lambda,B,\alpha,d)<\infty
and
m\coloneqq m(\gamma,\lambda,B,\alpha)>0
(see Definition
1.5
).

Recall that single-site SSM is ultimately a statement at projected total variation distance. In particular, recall that given
\Delta\in\mathcal{B}
we define the projected total variation distance
\|{P-Q}\|_{\Delta}
between probability distributions
P,Q
on
(\mathcal{N},\mathfrak{R})
as the total variation distance between the pushforwards of
P
and
Q
under the map
\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N},X\mapsto X\cap\Delta
. To simplify notation throughout this section, we will write
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{p}
for
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})}
for every
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
whenever the region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
are clear from the context.

At the core of our proof of Theorem
3.1
is the derivation of a correlation-decay result that generalizes that in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
in two ways.
Firstly, it applies without assuming a bounded range of the potential and only needs an exponential decay as in Definition
2.1
. Secondly, instead of only applying to local functions, it applies to functions
f,g
on
\mathcal{N}
that are “approximately local” on a region
\Delta
in the sense that
|f(X\cup\{x\})-f(X)|
decays exponentially in the distance
\mathrm{dist}(x,\Delta)
for all
x\notin\Delta
and
X\in\mathcal{N}
. By contrast, an (ordinary)
\Delta
-local function
f
is required to satisfy
|f(X\cup\{x\})-f(X)|=0
whenever
x\notin\Delta
.

Definition 3.2

(Approximately local functions)
.

Given a bounded region
\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
K,\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leq K<\infty
and
\kappa>0
, we say that a function
f
on
\mathcal{N}
is
(\Delta,\kappa,K)
-local if for all
x\notin\Delta
,

\|D_{x}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\leq Ke^{-\kappa\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,\Delta)}\,.

We note that a
\Delta
-local function is
(\Delta,\kappa,0)
-local for any choice of
\kappa>0
, and that a
(\Delta,\kappa,K)
-local function, it is also
(\Delta^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime},K^{\prime})
-local for every
\Delta^{\prime}\supseteq\Delta
,
\kappa^{\prime}\leq\kappa
and
K^{\prime}\geq K
.

To introduce our correlation decay result, we further need to introduce the semi-norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
.
Given a measurable function
f
on
\mathcal{N}
, we define

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\coloneqq\int\|D_{x}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\,dx\,.

Our correlation decay result now reads as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given an activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq\lambda
, a repulsive potential
\phi
satisfying exponential decay for constants
\alpha>0
and
B<\infty
(see Definition
2.1
), and a bounded measurable region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, suppose there is a constant
\gamma<\infty
such that

{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)\leq\gamma\ \mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}})

then, for each
\kappa>0,K\in[0,\infty)
, there are constants
m_{0}=m_{0}(\gamma,\lambda,\alpha,B,\kappa)>0
and
C_{0}=C_{0}(\gamma,\lambda,\alpha,B,\kappa)<\infty
so that the following holds.
For all bounded, measurable
\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g}\subset\Lambda
with
\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})>0
and all measurable functions
f,g
that are
(\Lambda_{f},\kappa,K)
- and
(\Lambda_{g},\kappa,K)
-local, we have

\left\lvert\operatorname{Cov}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)\right\rvert\leq C_{0}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+K({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})+\sqrt{{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)}\sqrt{{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(g)})e^{-m_{0}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}\,.

The main difference between our proof of Proposition
3.3
and that of
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
lies in proving the following lemma, which generalizes
[
6
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

In the setting of Proposition
3.3
, there are constants
M=M(\lambda,\alpha,B,d)<\infty
,
C_{1}=C_{1}(\lambda,B,\alpha,\kappa,d)<\infty
and
m_{1}=m_{1}(\alpha,\kappa)>0
such that, for all
t\geq 0

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)-P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\big]\big|\leq C_{1}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+K({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}))\cdot e^{tM-m_{1}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}.

We first deduce Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.1
and then prove Lemma
3.4
.

3.1.
Proving Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.1

The proofs of Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.1
using Lemma
3.4
will be similar to the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
. We begin with Proposition
3.3
:

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[f]=\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]=0
.
For every
t\geq 0
, using that
P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is self-adjoint with respect to
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
, we then get

\operatorname{Cov}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)=\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[fg]=\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)].

Using the triangle inequality and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)]\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g]+\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)-P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g]\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big\|P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\big\|_{2}\big\|P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\big\|_{2}+\big|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)-P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\big]\big|.

Using the fact that
P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
satisfies a Poincaré inequality with constant
\gamma
, we can apply Lemma
2.4
to the first term, and Lemma
3.4
to the second term to obtain

\left\lvert\operatorname{Cov}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{2}e^{-2t/\gamma}+C_{1}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+K({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}))e^{tM-m_{1}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}.

Choosing
t=\frac{m_{1}}{2M}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})
,
m_{0}=\min(\frac{m_{1}}{M\gamma},\frac{m_{1}}{2})
and
C_{0}=\max(1,C_{1})
concludes the proof.
∎

We use Proposition
3.3
to prove the main theorem of this section. The presence of the term
\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}
on the right-hand side of Proposition
3.3
will yield a bound of the form

\|\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\leq C(\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})+C)e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda^{\prime})}

which will ostensibly only yield single site SSM for sets
\Lambda^{\prime}
of volume bounded below. We will see that using the strategy of

46
]
will upgrade single site SSM to all sets
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\phi
satisfies single site strong spatial mixing up to some activity
\lambda
for all bounded measurable
\Lambda^{\prime}

satisfies single site strong spatial mixing up to

\Lambda^{\prime}
.

.
We now prove Theorem

:

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Lemma
3.5
we may assume that
\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})\geq 1
.
Note that

\|{\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{x}}-\mu_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\sup_{A\in\mathfrak{R}}\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{x}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}]\right\rvert.

Given an event
A\in\mathfrak{R}
, we then write

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{x}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\left\lvert\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}\exp(-\sum_{y\in\mathbf{Y}}\phi(x,y))]}{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[\exp(-\sum_{y\in\mathbf{Y}}\phi(x,y))]}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A}]\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert\operatorname{Cov}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f,g)\right\rvert}{\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]},

where

.

Thus, by Poisson domination (Lemma

), the Laplace functional of Poisson point processes and the exponential decay of

(18)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}[g]\geq\exp\left(-\lambda\int_{\Lambda}1-e^{-\phi(x,y)}\mathrm{d}y\right)\geq e^{-\lambda BC_{\alpha,d}},

.

.

\sqrt{{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(f)}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1

\sqrt{{\operatorname{Var}}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(g)}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 1

Since

\Lambda^{\prime}
-local, it holds that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 2\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})
.

\phi

\|D^{+}_{y}g\|_{\infty}\leq 1-e^{-\phi(x,y)}
.

further satisfies exponential decay according to Definition

-local, implying that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq BC_{\alpha,d}
.
Applying Proposition
3.3
and choosing

large enough and

small enough then proves the theorem.

3.2.
Proving Lemma
3.4

We begin with an identity proven in
[
6
, Eq. (4.3)]
, which may be understood as a standard identity for the carré-du-champ operator associated to
L_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
:

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
f,g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}})
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)-P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\big]=-2\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(P_{s}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f,P_{s}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Proof.

We expand on the proof of
[
6
, Eq. (4.3)]
. We approximate
L_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
by an
L^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mu)
bounded operator where we write
\mu=\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
. For each
k>0
define
L_{k}
via
L_{k}=-\int_{0}^{k}s\mathrm{d}E_{s}
where
\{E_{s}:s\in[0,\infty)\}
are the spectral projections associated to
-L_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
guaranteed by the spectral theorem. Define
P^{k}:=e^{tL_{k}}
. Define
\Phi(s)=P^{k}_{t-s}(P_{s}^{k}f\cdot P_{s}^{k}g)
and note that since
L_{k}
is bounded,
\Phi(s)
is differentiable. By the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

\displaystyle P_{t}^{k}(f)P_{t}^{k}(g)-P_{t}^{k}(fg)

\displaystyle=\Phi(t)-\Phi(0)=\int_{0}^{t}\Phi^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}^{k}\left[L_{k}(P_{s}^{k}f\cdot P_{s}^{k}g)-P_{s}^{k}g\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}f-P_{s}^{k}f\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}g\right]\mathrm{d}s

where we use that
P_{s}^{k}
and
L_{k}
commute. Using the fact that
L_{k}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\left[P_{t-s}^{k}\left[L_{k}(P_{s}^{k}f\cdot P_{s}^{k}g)-P_{s}^{k}g\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}f-P_{s}^{k}f\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}g\right]\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[L_{k}(P_{s}^{k}f\cdot P_{s}^{k}g)-P_{s}^{k}g\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}f-P_{s}^{k}f\cdot L_{k}P_{s}^{k}g\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\langle L_{k}(P_{s}^{k}f\cdot P_{s}^{k}g),1\rangle_{\mu}-\langle P_{s}^{k}g,L_{k}P_{s}^{k}f\rangle_{\mu}-\langle P_{s}^{k}f,L_{k}P_{s}^{k}g\rangle_{\mu}

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\mathcal{E}_{k}(P_{s}^{k}f,P_{s}^{k}g)

where
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is the Dirichlet form associated to
L_{k}
. Taking
k\to\infty
completes the proof.
∎

Combining Lemma
2.5
and
3.6
shows

(19)

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(fg)-P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\ P_{t}^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}g\big]\big|\leq 2\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Lambda}F_{s}(x)G_{s}(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

where
F_{t}(x)\coloneqq\big\|D_{x}^{+}P^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{t}f\big\|_{\infty}
and
G_{t}(x)\coloneqq\big\|D_{x}^{+}P^{\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{t}g\big\|_{\infty}
. We upper bound
F_{t}
, analogously to
[
6
, Eq. (4.8)]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

In the notation above we have

F_{t}(x)\leq F_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\left(F_{s}(x)+\int_{\Lambda}\lambda\big\|D_{x}^{+}e^{-D_{y}^{+}H}\big\|_{\infty}F_{s}(y)\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Proof.

This bound is implicit in
[
6
]
. In particular, if we let
T_{x}
denote the operator defined by
T_{x}f(\omega)=f(\omega\cup\{x\})
and apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to
P_{t-s}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(D_{x}^{+}P_{s}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}f)
to obtain

D_{x}^{+}P_{t}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}f=P_{t}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}D_{x}^{+}f+\int_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\left[-D_{x}^{+}P_{s}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}f+\int_{\Lambda}{\bm{\lambda}}(y)(D_{x}^{+}e^{-D_{y}^{+}H})T_{x}D_{y}^{+}P_{s}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}f\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s

which is precisely
[
6
, Eq. (4.7)]
. Since
P_{t}^{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}
is a contraction in
L^{\infty}(d\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}})
we obtain the stated bound.
∎

Using that
\big\|D_{x}^{+}e^{-D_{y}^{+}H}\big\|_{\infty}\leq 1-e^{-\phi(x,y)}
for repulsive
\phi
, we get

(20)

F_{t}(x)\leq F_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\left[F_{s}(x)+(\Gamma F_{s})(x)\right]\mathrm{d}s,

where
\Gamma
is the Hilbert–Schmidt operator with kernel
\gamma(x,y)=\mathds{1}_{y\in\Lambda}\ \lambda(1-e^{-\phi(x,y)})
defined by

(\Gamma F)(x)=\int_{\Lambda}F(y)\gamma(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\,.

Iterating (
20
) then yields the following convenient bound on
F_{t}
in terms of
F_{0}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Iterating the inequality (
20
) yields

(21)

\displaystyle F_{t}(x)\leq e^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}(\Gamma^{k}F_{0})(x).

Proof.

Set
G=I+\Gamma
and note that (
20
) can be rewritten as

F_{t}(x)\leq F_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(GF_{s})(x)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

By induction this implies that for each
k\geq 1
we have

F_{t}(x)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}(G^{j}F_{0})(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(t-s)^{k}}{k!}(G^{k+1}F_{s})(x)\,ds\,.

Taking
k\to\infty
and using that
\|G^{k+1}F_{s}\|_{\infty}\leq(1+|\Lambda\lambda|)^{k+1}\|F_{s}\|_{\infty}
shows that

F_{t}(x)\leq e^{tG}F_{0}(x)=e^{t}e^{t\Gamma}F_{0}(x)=e^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}(\Gamma^{k}F_{0})(x)\,.\qed

We proceed by observing that
\Gamma^{k}
is again a Hilbert–Schmidt operator with kernel
\gamma^{k}
, i.e. recursively defined by

(\Gamma^{k}F)(x)=\int_{\Lambda}\gamma^{k}(x,y)F(y)\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{ where }\quad\gamma^{k}(x,y)=\int_{\Lambda}\gamma^{k-1}(x,z)\cdot\gamma(z,y)\mathrm{d}z.

Our last stop before proving Lemma
3.4
is to bound
\gamma^{k}(x,y)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
\phi
is repulsive and decays exponentially (see Definition
2.1
) with constants
B<\infty
and
\alpha>0
, then there is some
b\coloneqq b(\lambda,B,\alpha,d)<\infty
so for all
k\geq 1
we have

\gamma^{k}(x,y)\leq b^{k}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}

Proof.

Set

b\coloneqq\max\{\lambda B,\lambda B\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\mathrm{d}x\}.

We prove the claim by induction on
k
.
The case
k=1
follows immediately from the exponential decay of the potential, which yields

\gamma(x,y)=\lambda(1-e^{-\phi(x,y)})\leq\lambda Be^{-\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}.

For the induction step, we use the induction hypothesis and the exponential decay of the potential to get

\displaystyle\gamma^{k}(x,y)=\int_{\Lambda}\gamma^{k-1}(x,z)\gamma(z,y)\mathrm{d}z\leq b^{k-1}\lambda B\int_{\Lambda}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)}e^{-\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\mathrm{d}z

By the triangle inequality, we have
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)+\mathrm{dist}(y,z)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)
and hence

e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)}e^{-\alpha\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\leq e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}

for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which then yields

\gamma^{k}(x,y)\leq b^{k-1}\lambda Be^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}\int_{\Lambda}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\mathrm{d}z\leq b^{k}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}.\qed

In order to apply Lemma
3.9
to bound the expansion in (
21
), we will need to isolate the
k=0
term. With this in mind, define the operator
\mathcal{K}
by

(\mathcal{K}h)(x)=\int_{\Lambda}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}h(y)\mathrm{d}y\,.

We are now ready to prove Lemma
3.4
:

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Substituting the bound from Lemma
3.9
into (
21
) yields

(22)

\displaystyle F_{t}(x)\leq e^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\int_{\Lambda}b^{k}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}F_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y\leq e^{t(1+b)}(F_{0}(x)+(\mathcal{K}F_{0})(x))

and similarly for
G_{t}
. We bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Lambda}F_{s}(x)G_{s}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Lambda}e^{2(1+b)s}(F_{0}(x)+(\mathcal{K}F_{0})(x))(G_{0}(x)+(\mathcal{K}G_{0})(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

When multiplying out, there are four terms to integrate, namely
F_{0}\cdot G_{0}
,
(\mathcal{K}F_{0})\cdot G_{0}
,
F_{0}\cdot(\mathcal{K}G_{0})
and
(\mathcal{K}F_{0})\cdot(\mathcal{K}G_{0})
. We show how to bound the latter term, as the others are similar (or in fact simpler).

We bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Lambda}e^{2(1+b)s}(\mathcal{K}F_{0})(x)(\mathcal{K}G_{0})(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{2(1+b)s}\iiint_{\Lambda^{3}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dx\,dy\,dz\,ds

\displaystyle\leq e^{2(1+b)t}\iiint_{\Lambda^{3}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dx\,dy\,dz\,.

We then note that, for
C_{\alpha,d}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\mathrm{d}x<\infty
,

(23)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}\mathrm{dist}(x,z)}\,dx\leq C_{\alpha,d}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}

which follows from bounding
\mathrm{dist}(x,y)+\mathrm{dist}(x,z)\geq\mathrm{dist}(y,z)
so
\mathrm{dist}(x,y)+\mathrm{dist}(x,z)\geq\frac{\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}{2}
.
It remains to bound

\iint_{\Lambda^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dy\,dz.

To this end, we split the integral into two cases: either
y
is close to
\Lambda_{f}
and
z
is close to
\Lambda_{g}
, or at least one of them is far away from their respective region.
Formally, set
A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda_{f})\leq\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})/3\}
and
B=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\mathrm{dist}(x,\Lambda_{g})\leq\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})/3\}
.
We note that
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}=(A\times B)\cup(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times B^{c})\cup(A^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
y\in A
and
z\in B
, we have
\mathrm{dist}(y,z)\geq\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})/3
.

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{A}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dy\,dz

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\alpha}{12}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D_{y}^{+}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D_{z}^{+}g\right\rVert_{\infty}\mathrm{d}z\right)

(24)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\alpha}{12}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

For
z\notin B
, using that
g
is
(\Lambda_{g},\kappa,K)
-local yields

\displaystyle\int_{B^{c}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dy\,dz

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{-\frac{\kappa}{3}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lVert D_{y}^{+}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}y

(25)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha,d}Ke^{-\frac{\kappa}{3}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Analogously

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{A^{c}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dy\,dz\leq C_{\alpha,d}Ke^{-\frac{\kappa}{3}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Summing (
24
), (
25
) and (
26
) yields

\iint_{\Lambda^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\mathrm{dist}(y,z)}\|D_{y}^{+}f\|_{\infty}\|D_{z}^{+}g\|_{\infty}\,dy\,dz\leq C_{\alpha,d}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+K({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}))e^{-m_{1}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}

for
m_{1}=\min(\alpha/12,\kappa/3)
.
Consequently,

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Lambda}F_{s}(x)G_{s}(x)\,dx\,ds\leq C_{\alpha,d}^{2}e^{2(1+b)t}({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\cdot{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+K({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|g\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}))e^{-m_{1}\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda_{f},\Lambda_{g})}

and substituting this bound back into (

) proves the lemma for
C_{1}=2\lambda C_{\alpha,d}^{2}
and
M=2(b+1)
.
∎

3.3.
Correlation functions and strong spatial mixing

In this subsection we will prove Lemma
3.5
, and also show that SSM holds. In particular, we will show:

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
and
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially with constants
B
and
\alpha
(see Definition
2.1
). Suppose
\phi
satisfies single-site SSM for some constants
C_{0}
and
m_{0}
. Then
\phi
satisfies SSM up to
\lambda
for constants
C=C(\lambda,B,\alpha,C_{0},m_{0},d)>0
and
m=m(m_{0},\alpha)>0
.

We will first prove Lemma
3.5
and then prove Proposition
3.10
in a similar manner. Both proofs will follow the strategy in
[
46
]
and make use of correlation functions. Each proof will begin by showing that one-point correlation functions are close (see Lemma
3.16
).
We begin by truncating the sum over
\mathbf{X}
using exponential decay of
\phi
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\lambda
have compact support
\Lambda
. Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially with constants
B
and
\alpha
(see Definition
2.1
). Then there is a constant
C:=C(d,B,\alpha)
so that for all
T\geq 1
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}\cap B_{T}(v)}\phi(v,x)\right)\right|\leq C\lambda e^{-\alpha T/2}\,.

Proof.

Note that by Lemma
2.6
and Poisson domination (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}\cap B_{T}(v)}\phi(v,x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\left[\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}\cap B_{T}(v)^{c}}(1-e^{-\phi(v,x)})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\int_{x\in B_{T}(v)^{c}}(1-e^{-\phi(v,x)})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lambda B\int_{x\in B_{T}(v)^{c}}e^{-\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}(v,x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Bounding the integral by
C\lambda e^{-\alpha T/2}
for some
C
depending on
B,\alpha,d
completes the proof.
∎

We now show that one can prove decay of one-point correlation functions:

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\lambda
have compact support contained in
\Lambda
. Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially with constants
B
and
\alpha
(see Definition
2.1
) and that satisfies single-site SSM (see Definition
1.5
) with constants
C_{0},m_{0}>0
for all bounded measurable
\Lambda^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})\geq 1
. Then there are constants
C:=C(C_{0},m_{0},d,B,\alpha,\lambda)
and
m=m(m_{0},\alpha,d)
so that for all
v
and
x
we have

\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}(v)\left(1-Ce^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(v,x)}\right)\leq\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(v)e^{-\phi(v,x)}\leq\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}(v)\left(1+Ce^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(v,x)}\right)\,.

Proof.

Recall that by (
13
) we have

\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(v)={\bm{\lambda}}(v)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)

and note
{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}(v)={\bm{\lambda}}(v)e^{-\phi(v,x)}.

By (
18
) we have
\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(v)\geq c{\bm{\lambda}}(v)
for some
c=c(d,B,\alpha,\lambda)>0
so it is sufficient to bound

\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)\right|\,.

Set
T=\mathrm{dist}(v,x)
and note that by adjusting
C
we may assume that
T
is large enough so that
\operatorname{Vol}(B_{T/2}(v))\geq 1
. Letting
C_{1}
be the constant from Lemma
3.11
we may bound

\displaystyle\Bigg|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}}\phi(v,x)\right)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}e^{-\alpha T/4}+\Bigg|\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}\cap B_{T/2}(v)}\phi(v,x)\right)-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}\exp\left(-\sum_{x\in\mathbf{X}\cap B_{T/2}(v)}\phi(v,x)\right)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}e^{-\alpha T/4}+\|\mu_{{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}\|_{B_{T/2}(v)}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{1}e^{-\alpha T/4}+C_{0}e^{-mT/2}

where the last inequality is by single-site SSM.
∎

It will now be easy to upgrade to decay of

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\lambda
have compact support contained in
\Lambda
. Let

for all bounded measurable

and

so that for all

and

\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}(\mathbf{v})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1-Ce^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(v_{j},x)}\right)\leq\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v})e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\phi(v_{j},x)}\leq\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}(\mathbf{v})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left(1+Ce^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(v_{j},x)}\right)\,.

Proof.

This follows immediately from combining (

∎

We obtain strong spatial mixing by writing the projected measure of an event as a sum of non-negative terms involving correlations. For an event
A\in\mathfrak{R}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
we define

A_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\{\mathbf{X}:\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\in A\}\,.

Lemma 3.14

(Lemma 12,
[
46
]
)
.

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
be compact sets,
x_{0}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
and
{\bm{\lambda}}
be a bounded activity function. For a tempered repulsive potential
\phi
we have

\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(A_{\Lambda^{\prime}})=\int_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{(\Lambda^{\prime})^{k}}\bm{1}_{\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}\in A}\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\exp\left(-\int_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\rho_{\Lambda,\widehat{{\bm{\lambda}}}_{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{k}

where

\widehat{{\bm{\lambda}}}_{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}(y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }|y-x_{0}|<|x-x_{0}|\text{ and }y\in\Lambda^{\prime}\\
{\bm{\lambda}}(y)\prod_{i=1}^{k}e^{-\phi(y,x_{i})}&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}\,.

We now complete the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

We wish to show that

\|\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{w}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\leq C\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda^{\prime},w))}\,.

By Corollary
3.13
, we have constants
C_{0},\alpha_{0}
so that for all
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
we have

\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\leq\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{x}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\left(1+C\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime})e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda^{\prime},w)}\right)^{k}\,.

Set
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}={\bm{\lambda}}_{w}
. Define
{\varepsilon}=C_{0}e^{-\alpha_{0}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\Lambda^{\prime},w)}
and assume
\delta:=(\lambda+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda^{\prime}){\varepsilon}\leq 1
as otherwise there is nothing to show. Defining

h_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{x})=\exp\left(-\int_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\rho_{\Lambda,\widehat{{\bm{\lambda}}}_{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,,

if we assume that
\delta\leq 1
then we may use Lemma
3.12
to bound

(27)

\exp\left(-\int_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\rho_{\Lambda,\widehat{{\bm{\lambda}}}_{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq\exp\left(-\int_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\rho_{\Lambda,\widehat{{\bm{\lambda}}}^{\prime}_{x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)(1+2\delta)

(see
[
46
, Corollary 16]
for more details).
As such, we see

\displaystyle\|\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}-\mu_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{(\Lambda^{\prime})^{k}}|\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{x})h_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{x})-\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})h_{{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x})|\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{(\Lambda^{\prime})^{k}}\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(x)h_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(x)\left[(1+{\varepsilon})^{k}(1+2\delta)-1\right]\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,.

Lemma
3.14
shows

4.
Infinite-volume Gibbs measures and thermodynamic limits

We prove thermodynamic properties along with uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measure.

4.1.
Uniqueness

In this section, we show that single-site SSM implies that there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The main result of this section is the following uniqueness theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that satisfies single-site SSM (see Definition
1.5
) up to
\lambda
.
Then, for every activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq\lambda
, there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is compatible with
\phi
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

We note that by a standard compactness argument there is always at least one Gibbs measure (see, e.g.,
[
35
, Theorem B.1]
) and so the content of Theorem
4.1
is that there is precisely one Gibbs measure.

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we show that single-site SSM implies that the following uniqueness criterion applies.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix an activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
and a repulsive pair potential
\phi
, and write
\Lambda_{n}=[-n,n]^{d}
.
Suppose for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all infinite-volume Gibbs measures
\mu
compatible with
\phi
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu}\left[\left\|\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\right]=0,

then there is at most one infinite-volume Gibbs measure compatible with
\phi
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We will use the following standard fact that distributions of point processes are characterized by their finite-volume projections:
If, for two probability measures
P,Q
on
(\mathcal{N},\mathfrak{R})
it holds, for every bounded measurable
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the pushforwards of
P
and
Q
under
X\mapsto X\cap\Lambda
are identical, then
P
and
Q
must be identical (this follows from, e.g.
[
14
, Lemma 6.1.III]
).

Suppose
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are two infinite-volume Gibbs measures.
Our goal is now to show that, for all bounded measurable
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all events
A\in\mathfrak{R}
, it holds that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]
.
It follows that
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
, and consequently, there is at most one Gibbs measure.

To this end, choose
k
large enough such that
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{k}
.
Since
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
satisfy the DLR equations, it holds that, for all
n>k
,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\big]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\big]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}\left[\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}\left[\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\right\rvert\right]

Since
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{k}
, we further have

\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{X\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{Y}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\right\rvert\leq\left\|{\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{X\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}

for all
X\in\mathcal{N}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}[\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{X}\cap\Lambda\in A}]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{1}}\left[\left\|{\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\mu_{2}}\left[\left\|{\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{X}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\right]

and taking
n\to\infty
concludes the proof.
∎

We now use Lemma
4.2
to show Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
n>k
and
Y\in\mathcal{N}
.
Since
Y
is a locally finite point set, it must be countable.
For any enumeration
y_{1},y_{2},\dots
of the points in
Y\setminus\Lambda_{n}
, let
Z_{j}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{j}\}
, and set
Z_{0}=\emptyset
.
Note that
Z_{j}
and
Z_{j-1}
differ by only a single point.
By the triangle inequality and single-site SSM, we then have

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{Y\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\|{\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{Z_{j}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{Z_{j-1}}}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\leq C\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{k})\sum_{y\in Y\setminus\Lambda_{n}}e^{-m\kappa\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\Lambda_{k})}.

Note that by the DLR equations (
11
) and Poisson domination (Lemma
2.2
), for every compact set
K
we have

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{Y}\cap K\setminus\Lambda_{n}}e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\Lambda_{k})}\leq\lambda\int_{K\setminus\Lambda_{n}}e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\Lambda_{k})}\mathrm{d}y\,.

By monotone convergence we obtain

(29)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\mu}\left[\left\|{\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\mathbf{Y}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}}-\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}\right\|_{\Lambda_{k}}\right]\leq C\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{k})\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Lambda_{n}}e^{-m\kappa\cdot\mathrm{dist}(y,\Lambda_{k})}\mathrm{d}y.

For fixed
k
, the right-hand side goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
, which concludes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Decay of correlations

We will show that the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure enjoys exponential decay of correlations. For this, we recall that for an infinite-volume Gibbs measure
\mu
compatible with
\phi
and
{\bm{\lambda}}
the
k
-point correlation function satisfies (
15
); when the infinite-volume Gibbs measure is unique, we will simply write
\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}=\rho_{\mu,{\bm{\lambda}}}
. Under the assumption of uniqueness, we will see that one has convergence of finite volume correlation functions to their infinite-volume analogue.

Fact 4.3
.

Suppose that
\phi
is a repulsive potential and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
so that for all
{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\lambda
there is a unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure compatible with
\phi
and
{\bm{\lambda}}

we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{\Lambda_{n},{\bm{\lambda}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})=\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})\,.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{n}
denote the finite-volume Gibbs measures associated to
(\Lambda_{n},{\bm{\lambda}})
and
\mu
the infinite-volume Gibbs measure. The sequence
\mu_{n}
is tight and so has a convergence subsequence; each convergent subsequence must converge to a Gibbs measure (see e.g.
[
34

) and so
\mu_{n}
converges to
\mu
. Equation (
14
) implies

\rho_{\Lambda_{n},{\bm{\lambda}}}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\bm{\lambda}}(v_{j})e^{-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\phi(v_{i},v_{j})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{Y}\sim\Lambda_{n},{\bm{\lambda}}}\left[\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{y\in\mathbf{Y}}\phi(v_{j},y)\right)\right]

and so taking
n\to\infty
and using (
15
) completes the proof.
∎

Approximate factorization of correlation functions follows quickly.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially with constants

and
\alpha
(see Definition

) and satisfies single-site SSM (see Definition

) with constants

Then for each

and

\left|\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v})\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq C^{k+\ell}e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})}

where we write
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=\min_{i,j}\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},w_{j})
and
\rho_{{\bm{\lambda}}}
refers to the correlation functions of the unique infinite-volume Gibbs measure compatible with
\phi
and
{\bm{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

By Fact
4.3

(30)

\left|\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v})\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq C^{k+\ell}e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})}

for all bounded and measurable
\Lambda
. We note that

\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})=\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v})e^{-\sum_{i,j}\phi(v_{i},w_{j})}\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{\mathbf{v}}}(\mathbf{w})\,.

Letting

(31)

\left|\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})-\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{v})\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}}(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq\left(\lambda+1\right)^{k+\ell}\left(1-e^{-\sum_{i,j}\phi(v_{i},w_{j})}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}}(\mathbf{w})-\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{i-1}}(\mathbf{w})\right|\right)\,.

For the latter term in (

\displaystyle\left|\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{i}}(\mathbf{w})-\rho_{\Lambda,{\bm{\lambda}}_{i-1}}(\mathbf{w})\right|\leq(\lambda+1)^{\ell}\left[\left(1-e^{-\sum_{a,b}\phi(v_{a},w_{b})}\right)+\left|1-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\left(1+C_{1}e^{-m\cdot\mathrm{dist}(w_{j},v_{i})}\right)\right|\right]\,.

Recall that we have

5.
Zero-freeness from spatial mixing

In this section, we show that single-site SSM implies zero-freeness and analyticity for repulsive interactions with exponential decay.
Given a repulsive potential
\phi
and a bounded measurable region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)>0
, recall that the finite-volume pressure at activity
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is given by

p_{\Lambda}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)}\cdot\log(Z_{\Lambda}(\lambda)).

We are interested in identifying activity regimes such that the limit
p=\lim_{\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{\Lambda}
, called the infinite-volume pressure, is an analytic function.
For studying this question, it is beneficial to study the partition function
Z_{\Lambda}
not only for real activities, but to extend its domain to complex inputs.
In particular, analyticity of
p
on some interval in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is closely related to the question of whether
Z_{\Lambda}
is non-vanishing in some uniform neighborhood of the interval as
\Lambda\nearrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Our main result in this section is the following relationship between single-site SSM and analyticity and zero-freeness.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially (see Definition
2.1
) and that satisfies single-site SSM (see Definition
1.5
) up to
\lambda
.
Then there exists some
\delta>0
such that the following holds:

(1)

For every bounded measurable region
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
Z_{\Lambda}\neq 0
for all complex activities in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
[0,\lambda]
.

(2)

There is a constant
M
so that for
\Lambda_{n}=[-n,n]^{d}
there is an analytic branch of
\log Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(w)
for
w
in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
[0,\lambda]
so that
\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})}|\log Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(w)|\leq M
.

(3)

Given a sequence of bounded measurable regions
(\Lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
p=\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{\Lambda_{n}}
exists pointwise on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, it holds that
p
has an analytic continuation in the
\delta
-neighborhood of
[0,\lambda]
.

Theorem
1.1
follows quickly now:

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Single-site SSM holds for all
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
by Theorem
1.7
. Theorem
4.1
implies uniqueness of infinite-volume Gibbs measure, and Theorem
5.1
3
shows analyticity of the pressure.
∎

We will prove Theorem
5.1
by a fairly involved inductive step involving ratios of partition functions.
For a repulsive potential
\phi
, activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, bounded measurable
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{C}
, we let

F_{\Lambda}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\big[(1+\varepsilon)^{\left\lvert\mathbf{X}\right\rvert}\big]=\frac{Z_{\Lambda}((1+\varepsilon)\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{Z_{\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}.

The main work of proving Theorem
5.1
will be the following technical proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially (see Definition
2.1
) with constants
B<\infty
and
\alpha>0
, and that satisfies single-site SSM (Definition
1.5
) with constants
C
and
m
up to some activity
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
For all
R>0
and
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
, there are
\beta=\beta(\alpha)>0
,
D=D(\lambda,\alpha,B,d)<\infty
,
\delta=\delta(\lambda,\alpha,B,d)>0
and
\varepsilon^{*}=\varepsilon^{*}(d,\lambda,\kappa,R,\alpha,B,m,C)>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{C}
with
\left\lvert\varepsilon\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon^{*}

Moreover, for Part 3 of the induction step, we use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
t>0
, and let

f\colon[0,t]\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
be absolutely continuous. It holds
that

\frac{f(t)}{f(0)}=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{f(s)}\mathrm{d}s\right),

where the integral should be understood as a Lebesgue integral and
f^{\prime}
is almost everywhere a derivative of
f
.

Proof.

Since
f
is continuous and non-zero, we have that
1/f
is bounded and continuous. In particular, we have that
f^{\prime}/f\in L^{1}([0,t])
and so
I(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t
is absolutely continuous and
I^{\prime}(u)=\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u)}
almost-everywhere. Compute

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\left(f(u)e^{-I(u)}\right)=f^{\prime}(u)e^{-I(u)}-I^{\prime}(u)f(u)e^{-I(u)}=0

almost everywhere. Hence
f(u)e^{-I(u)}
is constant almost-everywhere on
[0,t]
. Since
f(u)e^{-I(u)}
is absolutely continuous, it is in fact constant, implying
f(t)e^{-I(t)}=f(0)
and completing the proof.
∎

Definition 6.1

(Block dynamics)

Recall that for a pair of Borel probability measures

Instead, we argue that after some short “burn-in” period, the block dynamics from any initial configuration with sufficiently high probability reach a state within some bounded (expected) distance from a random state drawn from the stationary distribution.
This is done by combining the triangle inequality with the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(\mathbf{X}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a block dynamics chain with some activity function
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\leq\lambda
, repulsive potential
\phi
and update radius
L>0
on a box
\Lambda=[-n,n]^{d}
.
There are
t_{0}\in\Theta(\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)\cdot\log(\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)/\varepsilon))
and
s\in\Theta(\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)\cdot\log(1/\varepsilon))
such that for all
t\geq t_{0}
it holds that
\mathbb{P}[\left\lvert\mathbf{X}_{t}\right\rvert\geq s]\leq\varepsilon
, where the implicit constants only depend on
L
,
\lambda
and
d
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. First sample
y_{1},\ldots,y_{T}
which will be the centers of the block updates. Define
B_{j}=B_{L}(y_{j})
and
S=\bigcup_{j=1}^{T}B_{j}
. Since
\phi
is repulsive, we may use Poisson domination (Lemma
2.2
) and induct on
T
to note that
\mathbf{X}_{T}\cap S
is stochastically dominated by
\mathbf{Y}\cap S
where
\mathbf{Y}
is a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda
. Then we see for each
T
and
s>0
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|\mathbf{X}_{T}|\geq s)\leq\mathbb{P}(\Lambda\not\subset S)+\mathbb{P}(|\mathbf{Y}\cap S|\geq s)\,.

Note that by standard bounds on the coupon collector problem, there is a constant
C=C_{L,\lambda,d}>0
so that for
T\geq C\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)\cdot\log(\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)/{\varepsilon})
we have
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda\not\subset S)\leq{\varepsilon}/2
. Similarly, by a standard tail bound on Poisson random variables, we can find
C^{\prime}=C_{L,\lambda,d}^{\prime}>0
so that for
s\geq C^{\prime}\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)\cdot\log(1/{\varepsilon})
we have
\mathbb{P}(|\mathbf{Y}\cap S|\geq s)\leq{\varepsilon}/2
, completing the proof.
∎

Using Lemma

and Lemma

Proof of Theorem

.

For

,

For

and

(1)

(3)

If

, draw
(\mathbf{Z}_{1},\mathbf{Z}_{2})
from a coupling of

(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})
of

As such, using (

), we have the bound

W_{\Delta}(P^{y}(X,\cdot),P^{y}(Y,\cdot))\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})]\,.

We proceed by bounding the right-hand side of (

) for every choice of

along the same case distinction used for constructing the coupling.

Case

:
\mathrm{dist}(y,x)\leq L
.

Case

Our goal is to show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y})]\leq 1+q(L)\cdot e^{-m\cdot(\mathrm{dist}(x,y)-L)/2}

for some polynomial

that only depends on
\lambda
,

,

and

We first note that, for

We then use that

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbf{y})>L+\ell}\cdot\left(1+q(L)\cdot e^{-m\cdot(\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbf{y})-L)/2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+q(L)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbf{y})>L+\ell}\cdot e^{-m\cdot(\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathbf{y})-L)/2}\right]

7.
The canonical ensemble

In this section we prove Theorem
1.9
and Corollaries
1.10
and
1.11
.
Recall that for
\alpha>0
we define

\psi(\alpha):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})}\log\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lfloor\alpha\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})\rfloor)

where

\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k)=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\Lambda^{k}}e^{-H(\mathbf{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\,.

Our main technical result is to show the limit
\psi
is given in terms of the grand canonical pressure
p
(Proposition
7.1
). We note that equivalence of ensembles has been established in various settings. For instance see
[
56
, Theorem 3.4]
for various statements about equivalence of ensembles under various differentiability assumptions.

Recall that for
\lambda>0
we define the limiting density

\alpha(\lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda_{n},\lambda}[|\mathbf{X}|]}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})}\,.

We will show that for
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}})
, this limit exists and moreover,
\alpha(\lambda)
is strictly increasing on this interval. We may therefore define the limit

\alpha_{{\mathrm{spec}}}\coloneqq\lim_{\lambda\uparrow\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}}\alpha(\lambda)\,.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive potential that decays exponentially (see Definition
2.1
) Then for all
\lambda<\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
, the limit defining
\alpha(\lambda)
exists and
\alpha
is nondecreasing on the interval
(0,\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}})
. Moreover, we have the identity

\psi(\alpha(\lambda))=p(\lambda)-\alpha(\lambda)\log\lambda.

We start by showing a basic bound of approximate flatness of the canonical partition functions
\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k)
.

Lemma 7.2
.

For a repulsive potential
\phi
and all
k,\ell\geq 0
we have

\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k+\ell)\leq\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)^{\ell}\frac{k!}{(k+\ell)!}\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k)\,.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k+\ell)

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{(k+\ell)!}\cdot\frac{1}{k!}\int_{\Lambda^{k+\ell}}e^{-H(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{k+\ell})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq\frac{k!}{(k+\ell)!}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)^{\ell}}{k!}\int_{\Lambda^{k}}e^{-H(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)^{\ell}\frac{k!}{(k+\ell)!}\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda}(k)\,.\qed

We need the fact that the variance for the number of points in a sample grows at least linearly. This was proven for repulsive potentials by Ginibre
[
27
]
and more generally by Dereudre–Flimmel
[
17
]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

For fixed
\lambda>0
and repulsive, tempered potential
\phi
there is a constant

so that

{\operatorname{Var}}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\lambda}(|\mathbf{X}|)\geq c\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)\,.

We now prove Proposition
7.1
.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Note that for
\lambda>0
and
\Lambda
that

\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda,\lambda}|\mathbf{X}|}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda)}=\lambda p_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\alpha_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\,.

By Theorem
1.7
and Theorem
5.1
2
, we have that for
\Lambda_{n}=[-n,n]^{d}
and each
\lambda_{0}\in[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}})
, the sequence of analytic functions
\lambda\mapsto\alpha_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lambda)
converge uniformly on a ball containing
\lambda_{0}
in the complex plane. In particular, this implies that we may swap limits and derivatives

(67)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}\sim\Lambda_{n},\lambda}|\mathbf{X}|}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})}=\lambda p^{\prime}(\lambda)=\alpha(\lambda)\,.

We note that
\alpha(\lambda)
is strictly increasing since for each
\Lambda
we have

(68)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}(p_{\Lambda}(\lambda e^{t}))\big|_{t=0}=\frac{{\operatorname{Var}}_{X\sim\Lambda,\lambda}(|\mathbf{X}|)}{\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda)}\geq c>0

where the latter is by Lemma
7.3
.

We now turn to proving the identity in the statement of the proposition.
Fix
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}})
and choose
\lambda_{n}
approaching
\lambda
. First
let
\mathbf{X}_{n}
be a sample from
\mu_{\Lambda_{n},\lambda_{n}}
and set
X_{n}=|\mathbf{X}_{n}|
. Define

and
\sigma_{n}^{2}={\operatorname{Var}}(X_{n})
. Choose
\lambda_{n}
so that

and note that by (

) we have
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda
as

. Further, we note that
\lambda_{n}
is unique due to Lemma
7.3
.

Using the uniform bound on the complex logarithm of the partition function guaranteed by Theorem
5.1
2
, a central limit theorem of Michelen-Sahasrabudhe
[

, Theorem 1.8]
shows that, e.g.

(69)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{n}-m_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}\in(0,1)\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x>\frac{1}{3}\,.

Lemma

shows that

while Cauchy’s integral formula along with Theorem

\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\log Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lambda_{n}e^{t})\bigg|_{t=0}\leq C

for some constant

depending only on
M
and
\delta
from Theorem

. For

sufficiently large we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{X_{n}-m_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}\in(0,1)\right)\leq\frac{\lambda_{n}^{m_{n}}}{Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lambda_{n})}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\sigma_{n}}\lambda_{n}^{\ell}\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda_{n}}(m_{n}+\ell)\leq\left(\frac{\lambda_{n}^{m_{n}}\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda_{n}}(m_{n})}{Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(\lambda_{n})}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\sigma_{n}}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})^{\ell}}{(m_{n}+\ell)_{\ell}}\right)

denotes the falling factorial and
in the first inequality we used (

. If we write

then note that

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\sigma_{n}}\frac{V_{n}^{\ell}}{(m_{n}+\ell)_{\ell}}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\sigma_{n}}\left(\frac{V_{n}}{m_{n}}\right)^{\ell}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\sigma_{n}}\left(\alpha(\lambda_{\ast})^{-1}+o(1)\right)^{\ell}\leq\exp(O(\sqrt{V_{n}}))

Proof of Theorem

Corollary

Lemma 7.4

Let
\phi
be a repulsive, translation invariant potential that decays exponentially. If
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=+\infty
then for all
r>0
,

\esssup_{\|x\|\geq r}\phi(x,0)<\infty\,.

Proof.

Let
g(x)=1-e^{-\phi(x,0)}
.
Since
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=+\infty
,
g
is positive definite and measurable and therefore continuous (modifying
g
on a null set if necessary, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 3.10.20, page 221]
). Thus Bochner’s theorem implies that there is a measure
\mu
so that

g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)\,.

Since
g(0)=1
, we in fact have that
\mu
is a probability measure. Note that

g(h)\leq g(0)=1

for all
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
g(h)=1
then we must have that
e^{-i\langle h,\xi\rangle}=1
for
\mu
-almost every
\xi
. We then see that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x-h,\xi\rangle}e^{i\langle h,\xi\rangle}\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x-h,\xi\rangle}\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)=g(x-h)\,.

Since
g(x)\to 0
as
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\to\infty
(since
\phi
decays exponentially) we conclude that
h=0
. Since
g
is continuous and
g(x)\to 0
as
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\to\infty
we have

\esssup_{\|x\|\geq r}g(x)<1\,.

The result follows.
∎

We now show that the canonical pressure cannot equal
-\infty
when
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}=+\infty
. Note that in the case when
\phi
has a hard core, one in fact does have
\psi(\alpha)=-\infty
for all
\alpha
sufficiently large.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\phi
be a repulsive, translation invariant potential that decays exponentially. If
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=+\infty
then
\psi(\alpha)>-\infty
for all
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Fix a density
\alpha>0
. Choose a lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
density
\eta>\alpha
i.e. so that

M_{n}:=|\mathcal{L}\cap\Lambda_{n}|=(\eta+o(1))\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})\,.

Let
s>0
be the minimum distance between two distinct elements in
\mathcal{L}
. Let
\rho=s/4
so that the balls
B(z,\rho)
,
z\in\mathcal{L}
, are disjoint and have mutual distance at least
s/2
.
Let

k_{n}=\lfloor\alpha\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})\rfloor

and note that
M_{n}\geq k_{n}
for large
n
since
\eta>\alpha
.

Suppose now that
X\subseteq\Lambda_{n}
is a configuration of size
k_{n}
such that
|X\cap B(z,\rho)|=1
for exactly
k_{n}
elements
z\in\mathcal{L}
. Note that
\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\geq s/2
for all distinct
x,y\in X
.
By Lemma
7.4
and the fact the
\phi
decays exponentially we have

H(X)\leq Ck_{n}\,,

for some constant
C
depending only on
s,\phi
.
Consequently,

\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda_{n}}(k_{n})\geq\binom{M_{n}}{k_{n}}\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,\rho))^{k_{n}}e^{-Ck_{n}}.

Taking logarithms, dividing by
\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})
, and sending
n\to\infty

gives a finite lower bound for
\psi(\alpha)
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.11
.

Since
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}=\infty
, Theorem
5.1
gives
analyticity of the infinite-volume pressure
p(\lambda)
for every

\lambda>0
. Thus

\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda p^{\prime}(\lambda)

is analytic for every
\lambda>0
.
By Theorem
1.9
it suffices to show that

\alpha(\lambda)\to\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Let
P(t):=p(e^{t})
and note that
P^{\prime}(t)=\alpha(e^{t})
. Moreover, by (
68
) we see that
P
is a limit of convex functions and is therefore convex.

Fix
a>0
. For
k_{n}=\lfloor a\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})\rfloor
, the
grand-canonical partition function satisfies

Z_{\Lambda_{n}}(e^{t})\geq e^{tk_{n}}\widehat{Z}_{\Lambda_{n}}(k_{n}).

Taking logarithms, dividing by
\mathrm{Vol}(\Lambda_{n})
and sending
n\to\infty
, we obtain

(71)

\displaystyle P(t)\geq at+\psi(a)\,,

where
\psi(a)>-\infty

by Lemma
7.5
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\alpha(e^{t})=P^{\prime}(t)
does not tend to

\infty
. Since
P
is convex, this would imply that
P^{\prime}(t)\leq A
for all sufficiently large
t
, for
some finite
A
. Consequently,

P(t)\leq P(t_{0})+A(t-t_{0})\qquad\text{for all }t\geq t_{0}.

Choosing
a>A
, this contradicts
(
71
) for large
t
.
∎

8.
Computing
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}

We begin with a proof that in the case of a translation invariant potential, one can write
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
in terms of the Fourier transform of the associated function
g
:

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose that
\phi
is translation invariant, i.e.
\phi(x,y)=h(x-y)
for some
h
. Define
g(x)=1-\exp(-h(x))
and assume that
\phi
is tempered and repulsive. Then

\sup_{f\in L^{2}:\|f\|_{2}=1}\iint f(x)f(y)(e^{-\phi(x,y)}-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\esssup_{\xi}(-\widehat{g}(\xi))\,.

Proof.

We will show each inequality. Let
f\in L^{2}
with
\|f\|_{2}=1
. Since
g\in L^{1}
and
f\in L^{2}
we may apply Parseval’s theorem to see

(72)

\iint f(x)f(y)g(x-y)\,dx\,dy=\int f(x)(g*f)(x)\,dx=\int\widehat{g}(\xi)|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\geq\essinf_{\xi}\widehat{g}(\xi)

where in the last bound we used that
\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}=1
by Plancherel’s theorem. Multiplying by
-1
shows the left hand side is at most the right-hand side in the lemma. For the reverse, for each
{\varepsilon}>0
we want to find
f\in L^{2}
with
\|f\|_{2}=1
so that

\iint f(x)f(y)(-g(x-y))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\geq\esssup_{\xi}(-\widehat{g}(\xi))-{\varepsilon}\,.

Let
A\subset\{\xi:\widehat{g}(\xi)\leq\essinf\widehat{g}+{\varepsilon}\}
be a finite volume measurable set which we may assume to be symmetric since
g
is symmetric. Define
f
via
\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathds{1}_{A}/\operatorname{Vol}(A)^{1/2}
. Note that by Parseval we have that
\|f\|_{2}=1
. Apply (
72
) to see

\iint f(x)f(y)g(x-y)\,dx\,dy=\int\widehat{g}(\xi)|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Vol}(A)}\int_{A}\widehat{g}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\essinf_{\xi}\widehat{g}(\xi)+{\varepsilon}

completing the proof.
∎

We now deduce Corollary
1.3
:

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

This follows from combining Theorem
1.1
with Lemma
8.1
.
∎

We will use the criteria Lemma
8.1
in order to identify
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
for the hard sphere potential and to bound
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
for the Gaussian core potential.

8.1.
The hard sphere potential

Given the hard sphere potential defined by (
4
) we first note that one can write
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
in terms of the fourier transform of the unit ball. In particular, if we let
h_{d}
denote the indicator function of the ball of volume
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
a_{d}=-\min_{\xi}\widehat{h}_{d}(\xi)
then we obtain the identity

(73)

2^{d}\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=a_{d}^{-1}

using Lemma
8.1
. We first show that one can write
a_{d}
in terms of Bessel functions:

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
h_{d}
denote the indicator function of the ball of volume
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
a_{d}=-\min_{\xi}\widehat{h}_{d}(\xi)
. Then writing
\nu=d/2
we have

(74)

a_{d}=\operatorname{Vol}(B_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1))^{-1}(2\pi)^{\nu}(j_{\nu+1,1})^{-\nu}(-J_{\nu}(j_{\nu+1,1}))

where
J_{\nu}
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
\nu
and
j_{\nu+1,1}
is the first positive zero of
J_{\nu+1}
.

We then find the asymptotic behavior as
d
tends to
\infty
:

Lemma 8.3
.

As
d\to\infty
we have

a_{d}=(1+O(d^{-1/3}))2^{4/3}e^{-1}\sqrt{\pi}(\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(-\alpha_{1}))d^{-1/6}\left(\frac{2}{e}\right)^{d/2}\exp(-2^{2/3}\alpha_{1}d^{1/3})

where
\mathrm{Ai}
is the Airy function and
\alpha_{1}=\min\{\alpha>0:\mathrm{Ai}(-\alpha)=0\}
is the magnitude of its first negative root.

We will prove both Lemma
8.2
and
8.3
in Appendix
A
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Combining (
73
) with Lemma
8.3
shows

(75)

2^{d}\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}=(1+O(d^{-1/3}))\frac{e}{2^{4/3}\sqrt{\pi}(\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(-\alpha_{1}))}d^{1/6}\exp(2^{2/3}\alpha_{1}d^{1/3})\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{d/2}\,.\qed

8.2.
Gaussian core model

Recall that the Gaussian core model is given by the potential

(76)

\phi(x,y)=\beta e^{-\left\|x-y\right\|_{2}^{2}/2}

where
\beta>0
is an inverse temperature parameter. We will be interested in the regime where
\beta
is fixed and
d\to\infty
. In this regime, we observe that the temperedness constant may be computed asymptotically:

(77)

C_{\phi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\exp\left(-\beta\exp(-\|x\|_{2}^{2}/2\right))\,\mathrm{d}x=(1+o(1))\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\|x\|_{2}^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}x=(1+o(1))\beta(2\pi)^{d/2}\,.

We will show that
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}
is exponentially large as
d\to\infty
.

Lemma 8.4
.

For fixed
\beta>0
, there is a constant
C_{\beta}>0
so that
\lambda_{{\mathrm{spec}}}\geq C_{\beta}2^{d}/C_{\phi}
for the Gaussian core model (
76
).

Proof.

Letting
\psi(z)=\beta e^{-\|z\|^{2}/2}
, we may write
g(z)=1-e^{-\psi(z)}
as

\displaystyle g(z)=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{n!}e^{-n\|z\|^{2}/2}

and so

\displaystyle-\widehat{g}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{d/2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{n!}n^{-d/2}e^{-2\pi^{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}/n}\,.

Relabeling
2\pi^{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}=x
, we will throw away all odd terms for
n\geq 3
and bound

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{n!}n^{-d/2}e^{-x/n}\leq-\beta e^{-x}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}2^{-d/2}e^{-x/2}+\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\beta^{2k}}{(2k)!}(2k)^{-d/2}e^{-x/2k}\,.

We note that

\max_{x\geq 0}\left(-\beta e^{-x}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}2^{-d/2}e^{-x/2}\right)=\frac{\beta^{3}}{16}2^{-d}

while

\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\beta^{2k}}{(2k)!}(2k)^{-d/2}e^{-x/2k}\leq 2^{-d}\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{\beta^{2k}}{(2k)!}\leq 2^{-d}\cosh(\beta)\,.

Recalling
C_{\phi}\sim\beta(2\pi)^{d/2}
, applying Lemma
8.1
completes the proof.
∎

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma

F_{\nu}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\nu+1}{t}\cdot F_{\nu}^{\prime}+F=0\,.
