Viscosity Solutions of Stochastic Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman Equations with Jumps Thanks: Submitted to the editors August 10, 2026.
Dunxiang Liang, Qingxin Meng

Abstract
This paper studies the stochastic optimal control of jump-diffusion processes and the associated fully nonlinear backward stochastic Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (BSHJB) equations. We establish the dynamic programming principle (DPP) via backward semigroups to characterize the value function. To handle non-local integro-differential operators and polynomial growth, we introduce a stochastic viscosity solution framework based on semimartingale test functions and global tangency conditions. Existence is proved using the measurable selection theorem and the generalized Itô–Kunita formula. Finally, under a super-parabolicity condition, we establish a weak comparison principle and prove global uniqueness via localized bounding envelopes and backward induction.

Introduction

The optimal control of stochastic systems in random environments has led to the extensive development of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs)
[
18
]
and the comprehensive theory of forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs)
[
15
,
16
]
. A central object arising from such studies is the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation, which characterizes the value function of a stochastic optimal control problem. In the classical Markovian framework with deterministic coefficients, the HJB equation is a deterministic second-order PDE whose well-posedness under smoothness conditions is well understood. When the coefficients are random, however, the associated HJB equation becomes a backward stochastic partial differential equation (BSPDE) whose solution is a random field. The additional presence of non-local integral operators from jump processes and polynomial growth in the coefficients introduces profound analytical challenges that render both classical smooth solutions and standard Sobolev-space techniques inapplicable.

The theory of stochastic HJB equations with random coefficients has been developed along two complementary lines: classical/weak solutions in Sobolev spaces, and viscosity solutions. Along the first line, Peng
[
19
]
studied the stochastic HJB equation and established the existence and uniqueness of a Sobolev weak solution under the assumption that the diffusion coefficient does not depend on the control. Subsequently, Peng
[
20
]
introduced the backward stochastic semigroup method, which provides a powerful tool for characterizing the value function via dynamic programming in stochastic optimal control problems. More recently, Meng et al.
[
17
]
extended this Sobolev framework to jump-diffusion systems with random coefficients, proving that the value function is a classical solution of the associated stochastic HJB equation under suitable regularity assumptions. While these results provide rigorous foundations, they inherently require sufficient Sobolev regularity of the solution, a condition that is generally not satisfied in fully nonlinear settings with non-smooth value functions.

The viscosity solution approach, pioneered by Crandall and Lions
[
9
]
, offers an alternative that accommodates non-smooth solutions. For deterministic jump-diffusion systems, Li and Peng
[
14
]
extended the viscosity solution theory to HJB equations with non-local integral operators, proving that the value function is a viscosity solution of the associated nonlocal HJB integro-partial differential equation. In the stochastic setting with random coefficients, Buckdahn and Ma
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
developed the concept of stochastic viscosity solutions for fully nonlinear SPDEs via a Doss–Sussmann-type transformation, later unified with rough path theory
[
6
,
5
]
. Building on this foundation, Qiu
[
21
]
introduced an alternative and more direct framework based on semimartingale test functions that directly incorporate the stochastic dynamics of the controlled system, proving that the value function is the maximal viscosity solution of the associated stochastic HJB equation, with uniqueness established under a super-parabolicity condition. Qiu and Wei
[
22
]
further proved uniqueness under standard Lipschitz assumptions for stochastic HJ equations, and Qiu and Zhang
[
23
]
extended the theory to zero-sum two-player stochastic differential games. However, these stochastic viscosity solution frameworks are developed exclusively for continuous diffusions and do not account for jumps.

In parallel, the viscosity solution theory was extended to path-dependent settings by Ekren, Touzi, and Zhang
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, who established a rigorous analytical foundation for fully nonlinear path-dependent PDEs. Within this framework, Tang and Zhang
[
24
]
characterized the value functional as the unique viscosity solution of the associated path-dependent Bellman equation. Zhou
[
27
]
extended this theory to second-order path-dependent HJB equations, highlighting applications to backward stochastic HJB equations, and Zhou, Touzi, and Zhang
[
28
]
further extended the theory to mean field control with common noise. These works provide conceptual tools for managing randomness in viscosity solutions, albeit within a path-dependent infinite-dimensional setting rather than the finite-dimensional Markovian framework of the present paper.

A critical gap emerges from the above review: the existing literature covers three dimensions of difficulty—random coefficients, non-local jump operators, and viscosity solutions—but no single work addresses all three simultaneously. More specifically, (i) the classical/weak solution approach
[
19
,
17
]
handles jumps and random coefficients but requires Sobolev regularity; (ii) the deterministic viscosity solution theory
[
14
]
accommodates jumps but not random coefficients; (iii) the stochastic viscosity solution theory
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
handles random coefficients but not jumps. The non-local nature of the Lévy measure necessitates global (rather than local) tangency conditions in the definition of viscosity solutions, substantially altering the analytical framework. Moreover, polynomial growth in the coefficients prevents standard compactness arguments. The recursive cost considered in this paper is defined via a BSDE, introducing a backward structure that is absent in the forward stochastic HJB equations studied by Qiu
[
21
]
and Buckdahn–Ma
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we overcome these structural difficulties and establish a complete well-posedness theory for the stochastic viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear backward stochastic HJB (BSHJB) equations with jumps and polynomial growth. Our approach extends the semimartingale test function framework of
[
21
]
(originally developed for continuous diffusions) to handle jump processes. This extension is nontrivial: unlike the local touching conditions in
[
21
]
, the presence of the Lévy measure requires global tangency conditions over the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to ensure that the non-local integro-differential operator is well-defined when applied to test functions. We also incorporate the backward semigroup method
[
20
]
to characterize the recursive cost via dynamic programming, and the generalized Itô–Kunita formula
[
8
]
to handle the interplay between spatial derivatives and jump dynamics.

The main contributions of the paper are threefold. First, we establish the Dynamic Programming Principle (DPP) for the recursive cost functional driven by jump-diffusions via the backward semigroup approach, showing that the DPP holds in the presence of non-local jumps and polynomial growth. Second, we prove the existence of a stochastic viscosity solution using the measurable selection theorem and the generalized Itô–Wentzell (Itô–Kunita) formula for jump diffusions, alongside carefully constructed global tangency conditions and exit-time estimates that account for the non-local nature of the jump measure. Third, under a super-parabolicity condition (deterministic diffusion coefficient), we establish a weak comparison principle and prove global uniqueness via localized bounding envelopes and a backward induction scheme.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the probabilistic framework and the recursive optimal control problem. Section 3 provides a priori estimates and proves the DPP. The BSHJB equation is formally derived in Section 4. Section 5 defines stochastic viscosity solutions and proves existence. Section 6 establishes the weak comparison principle and uniqueness. The Appendix collects technical estimates used in the main proofs.

Preliminaries and Notation

2.1
Probability Space and Driving Processes

Let
T>0
be a fixed finite time horizon. We work on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
equipped with a right-continuous,
\mathbb{P}
-complete filtration
\mathbb{F}:=\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{T}=\mathcal{F}
. We denote by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
the expectation under
\mathbb{P}
, by
\mathcal{P}
the predictable
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega\times[0,T]
associated with
\mathbb{F}
, and by
\mathcal{B}(E)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of a topological space
E
.

Let
W(\cdot):=\big(W_{1}(t),\dots,W_{d}(t)\big)^{\top}_{t\in[0,T]}
be a
d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion. Let
(E,\mathcal{B}(E),\nu)
be a measure space with finite measure
\nu(E)<\infty
, and
\eta
be a mutually independent stationary Poisson point process taking values in
E
with characteristic measure
\nu
. We assume
\mathbb{F}
is exactly the
\mathbb{P}
-augmentation of the natural filtration generated by
W
and
\eta
. The induced counting measure
\mu
and its compensated random martingale measure
\tilde{\mu}
are cohesively defined as:

\displaystyle\mu\big((0,t]\times A\big)

\displaystyle:=\#\{s\leq t\mid\eta(s)\in A\},

(1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(dt,de)

\displaystyle:=\mu(dt,de)-dt\nu(de),\quad\text{for }t>0,A\in\mathcal{B}(E).

Let
\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
denote the set of all stopping times taking values in
[0,T]
. For any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
, we define
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}:=\{\theta\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}\mid\theta\geq\tau\ \text{a.s.}\}
. The closed stochastic interval associated with two stopping times
\tau,\gamma\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
is defined by:

[\tau,\gamma]:=\big\{(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\mid\tau(\omega)\leq t\leq\gamma(\omega)\big\},

(2)

with the open and half-open stochastic intervals
[\tau,\gamma)
,
(\tau,\gamma]
, and
(\tau,\gamma)
defined accordingly.

2.2
Notation and Abstract Function Spaces

Let
(B,\|\cdot\|_{B})
be a Banach space. For
t\in[0,T]
, let
L^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t};B)
denote the space of
B
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variables, and
L^{q}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{t};B)
(
q\geq 1
) be its subspace with
\mathbb{E}[\|\xi\|^{q}_{B}]<\infty
. To handle non-local jumps,
L^{2}_{\nu}(E;\mathbb{R}^{k})
denotes the Hilbert space of Borel measurable functions
r:E\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
equipped with the norm:

\|r\|_{\nu}:=\left(\int_{E}|r(e)|^{2}\nu(de)\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

(3)

For any
q\geq 1
, we introduce the following Banach spaces of stochastic processes (which reduce to classical FBSDE spaces when
B
is Euclidean):
\mathcal{S}^{q}(B)
for
\mathbb{F}
-adapted càdlàg processes
X
,
\mathcal{H}^{q}(B)
for predictable processes
Z
, and
\mathcal{H}^{q}_{\nu}(B)
for
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(E)
-measurable processes
K
. Their respective norms are defined as:

\displaystyle\|X\|_{\mathcal{S}^{q}(B)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{t}\|^{q}_{B}\bigg]\right)^{1/q}<\infty,

(4)

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\mathcal{H}^{q}(B)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|Z_{t}\|^{2}_{B}dt\right)^{q/2}\bigg]\right)^{1/q}<\infty,

(5)

\displaystyle\|K\|_{\mathcal{H}^{q}_{\nu}(B)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{E}\|K_{t}(e)\|^{2}_{B}\nu(de)dt\right)^{q/2}\bigg]\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

(6)

Let
C^{k}(\mathcal{O})
denote the space of functions with bounded and continuous derivatives up to order
k
on a domain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and write
C(\mathcal{O}):=C^{0}(\mathcal{O})
. The space of locally continuous random fields is defined by:

\mathcal{S}^{q}(C_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})):=\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}^{q}\big(C(B_{N}(0))\big).

(7)

By standard convention, we identify random fields with their pointwise representatives. Furthermore, whenever a process admits a modification with better path regularity (e.g., a càdlàg version), we systematically adopt this regular modification without altering the notation.

2.3
The Controlled State Equation

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a non-empty compact control domain. For any
t\in[0,T]
, the set of admissible controls
\mathcal{U}[t,T]
consists of all
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes
u:[t,T]\times\Omega\to U
. For any stopping time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
, initial state
\xi\in L^{p^{2}}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
p\geq 2
, and control
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]
, the state process
X(\cdot)\equiv X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\cdot)
is governed by the following jump-diffusion stochastic differential equation (SDE):

\begin{cases}dX(s)=b(s,X(s),u(s))ds+\sigma(s,X(s),u(s))dW(s)\\
\quad\quad\quad+\int_{E}g(s,e,X(s-),u(s))\tilde{\mu}(ds,de),\quad s\in[\tau,T],\\
X(\tau)=\xi.\end{cases}

(8)

We impose the following standard assumptions on the coefficients:

Assumption 1
.

(i)

The mappings
b,\sigma
are
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(U)
-measurable, and
g
is
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(E)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(U)
-measurable. They are continuous in
(x,u)
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
and all
t\in[0,T]
.

(ii)

There exist a constant
C>0
and a deterministic nonnegative function
\rho(e)
on
E
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, all
t\in[0,T]
,
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
u,u^{\prime}\in U
:

\displaystyle|b(t,x,u)-b(t,x^{\prime},u^{\prime})|+\|\sigma(t,x,u)-\sigma(t,x^{\prime},u^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|u-u^{\prime}|\big),

(9)

\displaystyle|g(t,e,x,u)-g(t,e,x^{\prime},u^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(e)\big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|u-u^{\prime}|\big),

(10)

\displaystyle|b(t,x,u)|+\|\sigma(t,x,u)\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|+|u|),

(11)

\displaystyle|g(t,e,x,u)|

\displaystyle\leq\rho(e)(1+|x|+|u|).

(12)

(iii)

The deterministic function
\rho(e)
satisfies the exponential integrability condition:
\int_{E}\exp\{\rho(e)\}\nu(de)<\infty
.

Remark 2
.

The exponential moment condition in Assumption
1
(iii) is a technical simplification to uniformly bound the Hamiltonian, as it guarantees the finiteness of
\int_{\{\rho\geq 1\}}\nu(de)
and
\int_{\{\rho<1\}}\rho(e)^{2}\nu(de)
. While this excludes heavy-tailed jumps, our framework can be extended under a weaker polynomial condition
\int_{E}\rho(e)^{p^{2}}\nu(de)<\infty
at the expense of lengthier moment estimates.

Remark 3
.

Assumption
1
(iii) strengthens the conventional
L^{2}
-integrability. Since
\int_{E}\exp\{\rho(e)\}\nu(de)<\infty
implies
\int_{E}\rho(e)^{q}\nu(de)<\infty
for every
q\geq 1
, together with the linear growth bound
|g|\leq\rho(e)(1+|x|+|u|)
, we immediately obtain high-order moment bounds for any
p\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\int_{E}|g(t,e,x,u)-g(t,e,x^{\prime},u^{\prime})|^{p^{2}}\nu(de)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\big(|x-x^{\prime}|^{p^{2}}+|u-u^{\prime}|^{p^{2}}\big),

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{E}|g(t,e,x,u)|^{p^{2}}\nu(de)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\big(1+|x|^{p^{2}}+|u|^{p^{2}}\big).

(14)

Consequently, classical SDE theory ensures the forward equation admits a unique strong solution
X^{u}_{\tau,\xi}(\cdot)\in\mathbb{S}^{p^{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.4
The Recursive Cost Functional

We characterize the recursive cost functional via a decoupled backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE). For any admissible pair
(u(\cdot),X(\cdot))
on
[\tau,T]
, consider the following BSDE with jumps:

\begin{cases}-dY(s)=f\left(s,X(s),u(s),Y(s),Z(s),\int_{E}K(s,e)l(s,e)\nu(de)\right)ds\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad-Z(s)dW(s)-\int_{E}K(s,e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de),\quad s\in[\tau,T],\\
Y(T)=h(X(T)).\end{cases}

(15)

Here,
l:[0,T]\times E\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a deterministic, non-negative weight function. To guarantee the well-posedness of this system under polynomial growth, we impose the following assumptions on the coefficients
f,h
, and
l
:

Assumption 4
.

(i)

The generator
f
is
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(U)\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
-measurable; the weight function
l
is
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(E)
-measurable; and the terminal cost
h
is
\mathcal{F}_{T}\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-measurable.

(ii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
\omega\in\Omega
, all
t\in[0,T]
, and valid arguments
(x,u,y,z,k)
and
(x^{\prime},u^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},k^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle|f(t,x,u,y,z,k)-f(t,x^{\prime},u^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime},k^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p-1}+|x^{\prime}|^{p-1}+|u|^{p-1}+|u^{\prime}|^{p-1}\big)\big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|u-u^{\prime}|\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C\big(|y-y^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|+|k-k^{\prime}|\big),

(16)

\displaystyle|h(x)-h(x^{\prime})|\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p-1}+|x^{\prime}|^{p-1}\big)|x-x^{\prime}|.

(17)

(iii)

The generator and terminal cost satisfy the following growth bounds:

|f(t,x,u,y,z,k)|\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p}+|u|^{p}+|y|+|z|+|k|\big),\quad|h(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{p}).

(18)

(iv)

The mapping
k\mapsto f(t,x,u,y,z,k)
is non-decreasing. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C_{l}>0
such that
0\leq l(t,e)\leq C_{l}(1+|e|)
for all
(t,e)
.

Given the assumptions above, for any initial data
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}\times L^{p^{2}}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and control
u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]
, the BSDE (
15
) admits a unique solution
(Y_{\tau,\xi}^{u},Z_{\tau,\xi}^{u},K_{\tau,\xi}^{u})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 5
.

For any stopping time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
and initial state
\xi\in L^{p^{2}}(\Omega,\allowbreak\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the stochastic recursive cost functional is defined as the
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
-measurable random variable:

\mathbb{J}(\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)):=Y_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\tau),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(19)

where
(Y_{\tau,\xi}^{u},Z_{\tau,\xi}^{u},K_{\tau,\xi}^{u})
is the unique solution to the recursive system (
15
) on the stochastic interval
[\tau,T]
.

3.3
A Priori Estimates for the Value Function

Taking the essential infimum over

Lemma 12

Let Assumption

p\geq 2

3.4
Dynamic Programming Principle

The Dynamic Programming Principle (DPP) is characterized via the backward semigroup. For
(\tau,\xi)
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{T}_{[\tau,T]}
,
u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
-measurable

G_{s,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)}[\eta]:=Y(s),\quad s\in[\tau,\gamma],

(30)

where
(X,Y,Z,K)
is the unique strong solution to the following truncated forward-backward system on
[\tau,\gamma]
:

\begin{cases}dX(s)=b(s,X(s),u(s))ds+\sigma(s,X(s),u(s))dW(s)\\
\quad\quad\quad+\int_{E}g(s,e,X(s-),u(s))\tilde{\mu}(ds,de),\\
-dY(s)=f\left(s,X(s),u(s),Y(s),Z(s),\int_{E}K(s,e)l(s,e)\nu(de)\right)ds\\
\quad\quad\quad-Z(s)dW(s)-\int_{E}K(s,e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de),\\
X(\tau)=\xi,\quad Y(\gamma)=\eta.\end{cases}

(31)

The main result of the dynamic programming principle is formulated in the following theorem.

Theorem 13
.

Under Assumption
1
and Assumption
4
, the value function
\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)
obeys the following dynamic programming principle: for any initial time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
, intermediate stopping time

, and initial state

\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)}\Big[\mathbb{V}\big(\gamma,X^{\tau,\xi;u}(\gamma)\big)\Big],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

We define the family of admissible costs at initial data
(\tau,\xi)
as:

\Lambda(\tau,\xi):=\big\{\mathbb{J}(\tau,\xi;u(\cdot))\mid u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]\big\}.

Lemma 14
.

For any initial data
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}\times L^{p^{2}}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the family

satisfies the following
\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(i)

is downward directed: for any

(ii)

There exists a sequence of admissible controls

such that

(iii)

For any sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\tau}

\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)\big|\mathcal{G}\big]=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{J}(\tau,\xi;u)\big|\mathcal{G}\big].

Proof of
theorem

13
.

Step 1: The Lower Bound (
\geq
).

By the definition of
\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)
and the flow property of the backward semigroup, we have
\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]}G_{\tau,T}^{\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)}\big[h(X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(T))\big]

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,T]}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)}\big[\mathbb{J}(\gamma,X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\gamma);u(\cdot))\big].

Since
\mathbb{J}(\gamma,X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\gamma);u(\cdot))\geq\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\gamma))
holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. by definition, and the semigroup operator
G
is monotone with respect to terminal conditions, we obtain:

\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)\geq\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u(\cdot)}\big[\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\tau,\xi}^{u}(\gamma))\big],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(32)

Step 2: The Upper Bound (
\leq
).
Fix an arbitrary
u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]
. Consider the family of random variables evaluated at time
\gamma
:

\Lambda:=\bigl\{\mathbb{J}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u};u^{\prime}):u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}[\gamma,T]\bigr\}.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is stable under concatenation,
\Lambda
is directed downward. Standard properties of the essential infimum guarantee a sequence
\{u_{n}^{\prime}\}\subset\mathcal{U}[\gamma,T]
whose costs
Y_{n}:=\mathbb{J}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u};u_{n}^{\prime})
monotonically decrease to the value function:

Y_{n}\downarrow\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}[\gamma,T]}\mathbb{J}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u};u^{\prime})=\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u}),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. as }n\to\infty.

Applying
G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}[\cdot]
to this sequence, standard BSDE stability and the Dominated Convergence Theorem (since
Y_{n}\geq\mathbb{V}\in L^{p}

G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}[Y_{n}]\downarrow G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}\bigl[\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u})\bigr],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

By the flow property of the backward semigroup and the definition of

, taking the limit as

G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}\bigl[\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u})\bigr]=\lim_{n\to\infty}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}[Y_{n}]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{J}(\tau,\xi;u\oplus u_{n}^{\prime})\geq\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi).

Taking the essential infimum over arbitrary

\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}\bigl[\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u})\bigr]\geq\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi).

Combining the two inequalities, we conclude

\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)=\operatorname*{essinf}_{u\in\mathcal{U}[\tau,\gamma]}G_{\tau,\gamma}^{\tau,\xi;u}\bigl[\mathbb{V}(\gamma,X_{\gamma}^{u})\bigr],\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

The proof is complete.

4
Stochastic HJB Equation with Jumps

Following the framework established in
[
17
]
, and assuming sufficient smoothness of the value field
V(t,x)
, the dynamic programming principle formally implies that
V(t,x)
satisfies a backward stochastic partial integro-differential equation (BSHJB). We first define the generalized Hamiltonian
H:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times U\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. In the definition below,
y
corresponds to
V(t,x)
,
p
to
DV(t,x)
,
q
to
Z(t,x)
,
Q
to
DZ(t,x)
,
A
to
D^{2}V(t,x)
, and
k
to the integral term
\int_{E}K(t,e)l(t,e)\nu(de)
.

\displaystyle H(t,x,u,y,p,q,Q,A,k)

\displaystyle:=f\big(t,x,u,y,\sigma^{\top}(t,x,u)p+q,k\big)+\langle p,b(t,x,u)\rangle

(33)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Tr}\big(Q\sigma^{\top}(t,x,u)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\big(A\sigma\sigma^{\top}(t,x,u)\big).

For a predictable triplet of random fields
(V,Z,K)
, the fully nonlinear BSHJB equation takes the following differential form on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\begin{cases}-dV(t,x)=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\in U}\Bigg\{H\Big(t,x,u,V,DV,Z,DZ,D^{2}V,L(t,x,u,V,K)\Big)\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{E}\Big[I_{V}(t,e,x,u)-\langle g(t,e,x,u),DV(t,x)\rangle+I_{K}(t,e,x,u)\Big]\nu(de)\Bigg\}dt\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-Z(t,x)dW(t)-\int_{E}K(t,e,x)\tilde{\mu}(dt,de),\\
V(T,x)=h(x),\end{cases}

(34)

where the non-local difference operators
L
and
I
are defined by:

\displaystyle L(t,x,u,\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{E}\Big(I_{\phi}(t,e,x,u)+\psi\big(t,e,x+g(t,e,x,u)\big)\Big)l(t,e)\nu(de),

\displaystyle I_{\phi}(t,e,x,u)

\displaystyle:=\phi\big(t,x+g(t,e,x,u)\big)-\phi(t,x).

To compactify the notation, we encapsulate the essential infimum and all predictable characteristics into a global non-linear drift operator
F
:

\displaystyle F(t,x,V,Z,K)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\in U}\Bigg\{H\Big(t,x,u,V,DV,Z,DZ,D^{2}V,L(t,x,u,V,K)\Big)

(35)

\displaystyle+\int_{E}\Big[I_{V}(t,e,x,u)-\langle g(t,e,x,u),DV\rangle+I_{K}(t,e,x,u)\Big]\nu(de)\Bigg\}.

The BSHJB equation can thus be compactly written as:

\begin{cases}-dV(t,x)=F(t,x,V,Z,K)dt-Z(t,x)dW(t)-\int_{E}K(t,e,x)\tilde{\mu}(dt,de),\\
V(T,x)=h(x).\end{cases}

(36)

Definition 17
.

Let
p\geq 2
be the polynomial growth exponent from Assumption
4
. A triplet of random fields
(V,Z,K)
is called a predictable classical solution to the BSHJB equation
eq.

36
if it satisfies the following:

(i)

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
V(\cdot,x)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted càdlàg process,
Z(\cdot,x)
is predictable, and
K(\cdot,\cdot,x)
is
\mathcal{P}\otimes\mathcal{B}(E)
-measurable, where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the predictable
\sigma
-algebra.

(ii)

For
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
(t,\omega)
,
V(t,\cdot,\omega)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
Z(t,\cdot,\omega)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, for
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}\otimes\nu(de)
-a.e.
(t,\omega,e)
,
K(t,e,\cdot,\omega)\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(iii)

The triplet
(V,Z,K)
satisfies
eq.

36
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Stochastic Viscosity Solutions and Existence

Since the global spatial
C^{2}
smoothness required for classical solutions is generally unattainable in fully nonlinear stochastic optimal control, this section develops the framework of stochastic viscosity solutions tailored for our
p
-th order recursive system, building upon the foundational concepts established in
[
21
,
22
]
.

5.1
The Space of Test Functions

Definition 18
.

We denote by
\mathcal{T}^{p}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of all random fields
\varphi:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\varphi(\cdot,x)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted càdlàg semimartingale admitting the backward representation:

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(T,x)+\int_{t}^{T}\alpha(s,x)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\beta(s,x)dW(s)

(37)

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{E}\gamma(s,e,x)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de),

where
\alpha,\beta
, and
\gamma
are appropriately integrable predictable processes.

(ii)

For
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
(t,\omega)
, we have
\varphi(t,\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\alpha(t,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\beta(t,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, for
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}\otimes\nu(de)
-a.e.
(t,\omega,e)
,
\gamma(t,e,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(iii)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for
dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
(t,\omega)
and all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle|\varphi|+|\alpha|+|\beta|+\|\gamma\|_{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p}\big),

\displaystyle|D\varphi|+|D\alpha|+|D\beta|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p-1}\big),

\displaystyle|D^{2}\varphi|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p-2}\big),

where
\|\cdot\|_{\nu}
denotes the norm in
L^{2}(E,\mathcal{B}(E),\nu)
.

5.2
Stochastic Tangency Spaces

We now define the sets of test functions that act as stochastic super- and sub-tangents for the value function. For any stopping time
t\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
, let
\mathcal{T}_{[t,T]}
denote the set of all stopping times valued in
[t,T]
, and define the strict subset
\mathcal{T}_{[t,T]}^{+}:=\{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[t,T]}\mid\tau>t\text{ a.s.}\}
. For any
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{[0,T]}
and event
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
, we denote by
L^{p^{2}}(A,\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
the space of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued,
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
-measurable random variables with finite
p^{2}
-th moments over
A
. For any test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{T}^{p}
with the semimartingale decomposition given in
definition

18
, we denote its predictable drift rate
\alpha(s,x)
by the shorthand notation
-d_{s}\varphi(s,x)
.

Definition 19
.

For a given random field
V\in\mathbb{S}^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, each touching time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
, event
\Omega_{\tau}\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\tau})>0
, and spatial random variable
\xi\in L^{p^{2}}(\Omega_{\tau},\mathcal{F}_{\tau};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define the super-tangent test function space
\mathcal{G}^{+}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau})
as:

\mathcal{G}^{+}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \varphi\in\mathcal{T}^{p}:\;&(\varphi-V)(\tau,\xi)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}=0\\
&=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{\bar{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}}\mathbb{E}\left[\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi-V)(\bar{\tau}\wedge\hat{\tau},y)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}\\
&\text{for some }\hat{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{\tau}\end{aligned}\right\}.

Symmetrically, the sub-tangent test function space
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau})
is defined as:

\mathcal{G}^{-}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau}):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \varphi\in\mathcal{T}^{p}:\;&(\varphi-V)(\tau,\xi)\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}}=0\\
&=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\bar{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi-V)(\bar{\tau}\wedge\hat{\tau},y)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\tau}},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}\\
&\text{for some }\hat{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{\tau}\end{aligned}\right\}.

Note that if either
\mathcal{G}^{+}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau})
or
\mathcal{G}^{-}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau})
is non-empty, it intrinsically implies
0\leq\tau<T
on the event
\Omega_{\tau}
.

Remark 20
.

Due to the non-local nature of the jump measure
\nu(de)
, the tangency conditions are formulated globally on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
rather than locally. This global extremum ensures that the integro-differential operator evaluating the test function remains well-defined.

For any test function

\displaystyle+\int_{E}\Big[I_{\varphi}(t,e,x,u)-\langle g,D\varphi\rangle+I_{\gamma}(t,e,x,u)\Big]\nu(de)\Bigg\}.

To avoid ambiguity in the limiting procedures involving the conditional expectation, we adopt the following equivalent formulation based on sequences.

Part I: Viscosity Subsolution.

-a.e. on
[\tau,\tau+\tilde{\delta}]\times\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}
:

\alpha(s,\xi)\geq\varepsilon+H\big(s,\xi,u^{*}_{s},\varphi,D\varphi,\beta,D\beta,D^{2}\varphi,\mathcal{L}(u^{*}_{s})\big)+\int_{E}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}-\langle g,D\varphi\rangle+\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\big]\nu(de).

(45)

Here
\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}(s,e,x,u):=\varphi(s,x+g(s,e,x,u))-\varphi(s,x)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(s,e,x,u):=\gamma(s,e,x+g(s,e,x,u))
.

Since the test function
\varphi
and the coefficients
(b,\sigma,g)
are continuous in
x
with at most polynomial growth, the functions
\alpha
,
H
,
D\varphi
,
D\beta
,
D^{2}\varphi
,
\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}
are uniformly continuous on the compact stochastic cylinder
Q_{\tilde{\delta}}^{+}(\tau,\xi)\cap\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}
. Consequently, the strict inequality established at the point
\xi
by the measurable selection theorem extends to the entire neighborhood
Q_{\tilde{\delta}}^{+}(\tau,\xi)
after possibly shrinking
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\delta}
. More precisely, for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}
and every
(s,x)\in Q_{\tilde{\delta}}^{+}(\tau,\xi)
, we have

\alpha(s,x)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+H\big(s,x,u^{*}_{s},\varphi,D\varphi,\beta,D\beta,D^{2}\varphi,\mathcal{L}(u^{*}_{s})\big)+\int_{E}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}-\langle g,D\varphi\rangle+\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\big]\nu(de),

(46)

where the spatial arguments in the right-hand side are evaluated at
(s,x)
. Since the controlled trajectory
X_{s}
remains within
Q_{\tilde{\delta}/2}^{+}(\tau,\xi)
up to the exit time
\tilde{\tau}
, substituting
x=X_{s}
is legitimate on
[\tau,\tau_{h}]
.

We define the exiting stopping time of the state process from the spatial neighborhood:

\tilde{\tau}:=\inf\left\{s>\tau:\big|X_{s}^{\tau,\xi;u^{*}}-\xi\big|\geq\frac{\tilde{\delta}}{2}\right\}.

(47)

Setting
\tau_{h}:=(\tau+h)\wedge\tilde{\tau}
for a sufficiently small
h>0
, standard moment estimates via Lemma
8
(iii) and Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion guarantee:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+h}|X^{\tau,\xi;u^{*}}_{s}-\xi|^{p^{2}}\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\leq K(1+|\xi|^{p^{2}})h^{p^{2}/4}.

(48)

Applying Chebyshev’s inequality yields the conditional early-exit probability:

\mathbb{P}\big(\tilde{\tau}<\tau+h\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big)\leq\frac{2^{p^{2}}}{\tilde{\delta}^{p^{2}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\tau\leq s\leq\tau+h}\big|X_{s}^{\tau,\xi;u^{*}}-\xi\big|^{p^{2}}\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\leq\frac{2^{p^{2}}K}{\tilde{\delta}^{p^{2}}}\big(1+|\xi|^{p^{2}}\big)h^{p^{2}/4}.

(49)

Consequently, the expected elapsed time satisfies:

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{h}-\tau\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big]\geq h\mathbb{P}\big(\tilde{\tau}\geq\tau+h\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big)\geq h-\frac{2^{p^{2}}K}{\tilde{\delta}^{p^{2}}}\big(1+|\xi|^{p^{2}}\big)h^{1+p^{2}/4}.

(50)

This ensures that
\mathbb{E}[\tau_{h}-\tau|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\geq h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, providing the exact analytical order required for the asymptotic contradiction. Applying the generalized Itô-Kunita formula to
\varphi(s,X_{s})
along the controlled trajectory
X_{s}:=X^{\tau,\xi;u^{*}}_{s}
on
[\tau,\tau_{h}]
yields:

\displaystyle d\varphi(s,X_{s})

\displaystyle=\bigg\{-\alpha(s,X_{s})+\langle D\varphi(s,X_{s}),b(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s})\rangle

(51)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\big(D^{2}\varphi(s,X_{s})\sigma\sigma^{\top}(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s})\big)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Tr}\big(D\beta(s,X_{s})\sigma^{\top}(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s})\big)

\displaystyle+\int_{E}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}-\langle D\varphi,g\rangle+\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\big]\nu(de)\bigg\}ds

\displaystyle+\big(\beta(s,X_{s})+\sigma^{\top}(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s})D\varphi(s,X_{s})\big)dW_{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{E}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}(s,e,X_{s-},u^{*}_{s})+\gamma\big(s,e,X_{s-}+g(s,e,X_{s-},u^{*}_{s})\big)\big]\tilde{\mu}(ds,de).

Recasting this differential into the canonical backward form
-d\varphi(s,X_{s})=\hat{f}_{s}ds-\hat{Z}_{s}dW_{s}-\int_{E}\hat{K}_{s}(e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de)
uniquely identifies the martingale coefficients:

\displaystyle\hat{Z}_{s}

\displaystyle:=\beta(s,X_{s})+\sigma^{\top}(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s})D\varphi(s,X_{s}),

(52)

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{s}(e)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}(s,e,X_{s-},u^{*}_{s})+\gamma\big(s,e,X_{s-}+g(s,e,X_{s-},u^{*}_{s})\big).

(53)

By isolating
\alpha(s,X_{s})
and substituting the strict inequality established via measurable selection, the equivalent drift
\hat{f}_{s}
satisfies:

\hat{f}_{s}\geq f\left(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s},\varphi(s,X_{s}),\hat{Z}_{s},\int_{E}\hat{K}_{s}(e)l(s,e)\nu(de)\right)+\varepsilon,\quad\text{a.e. in }[\tau,\tau_{h}].

(54)

Let
Y_{s}^{u^{*}}
be the solution to the standard BSDE associated with control
u^{*}
and terminal condition
V(\tau_{h},X_{\tau_{h}})
. We define the difference processes
\Delta Y_{s}:=\varphi(s,X_{s})-Y_{s}^{u^{*}}
,
\Delta Z_{s}:=\hat{Z}_{s}-Z_{s}^{u^{*}}
, and
\Delta K_{s}:=\hat{K}_{s}-K_{s}^{u^{*}}
, whose dynamics satisfy:

-d(\Delta Y_{s})=\Big(\hat{f}_{s}-f\big(s,X_{s},u^{*}_{s},Y_{s}^{u^{*}},Z_{s}^{u^{*}},K_{s}^{u^{*}}\big)\Big)ds-\Delta Z_{s}dW_{s}-\int_{E}\Delta K_{s}\tilde{\mu}(ds,de).

(55)

Applying the mean-value theorem to the uniformly Lipschitz generator
f
produces bounded predictable processes
a_{s}
,
b_{s}
, and
c_{s}(e)>-1
, linearizing the drift inequality into:

\displaystyle-d(\Delta Y_{s})

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\varepsilon+a_{s}\Delta Y_{s}+\langle b_{s},\Delta Z_{s}\rangle+\int_{E}c_{s}(e)\Delta K_{s}(e)\nu(de)\bigg)ds

(56)

\displaystyle-\Delta Z_{s}dW_{s}-\int_{E}\Delta K_{s}\tilde{\mu}(ds,de).

To absorb the difference terms, we introduce a process
\Gamma_{s}
governed by the linear forward SDE:

d\Gamma_{s}=\Gamma_{s-}\left(a_{s}ds+\langle b_{s},dW_{s}\rangle+\int_{E}c_{s}(e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de)\right),\quad\Gamma_{\tau}=1.

(57)

Applying Itô’s product rule to
\Gamma_{s}\Delta Y_{s}
on
[\tau,\tau_{h}]
and taking the conditional expectation, the local martingales vanish. Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}_{V}^{+}
guarantees a non-negative terminal difference
\Delta Y_{\tau_{h}}\geq 0
, dropping this term and utilizing the strictly positive adjoint bound
\Gamma_{s}\geq c>0
yields:

\Delta Y_{\tau}\geq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\tau}^{\tau_{h}}\Gamma_{s}ds\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\right]\geq c\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{h}-\tau\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big].

(58)

At the contact point
\tau
, the fundamental touching condition enforces
\Delta Y_{\tau}=0
. Substituting the asymptotic exit time estimate
\mathbb{E}[\tau_{h}-\tau|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\geq h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
, we construct the ultimate algebraic contradiction:

0\geq c\varepsilon h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})>0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(59)

for a sufficiently small
h>0
. This strict inequality
0>0
completely falsifies the initial assumption, rigorously proving that
V
is a stochastic viscosity subsolution to the BSHJB equation.

Part II: Viscosity Supersolution.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
V
is not a viscosity supersolution. Then there exist
\tau,\Omega_{\tau},\xi,\varphi\in\mathcal{G}^{-}_{V}(\tau,\xi;\Omega_{\tau})
and constants
\varepsilon,\tilde{\delta}>0
,
\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}\subset\Omega_{\tau}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau})>0
, such that

\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{(s,x)\in\mathcal{Q}^{+}_{\tilde{\delta}}(\tau,\xi)}\mathbb{E}\big[\alpha(s,x)-F^{\varphi}(s,x)\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big]\leq-2\varepsilon,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. on }\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}.

(60)

Because
F^{\varphi}
is defined as the essential infimum over
U
, the upper bound
-2\varepsilon
is universal for
every
control. Hence, for any
u\in\mathcal{U}
,
ds\otimes d\mathbb{P}
-a.e. on
[\tau,\tau+\tilde{\delta}]\times\Omega^{\prime}_{\tau}
,

\alpha(s,\xi)\leq-2\varepsilon+H\big(s,\xi,u_{s},\varphi,D\varphi,\beta,D\beta,D^{2}\varphi,\mathcal{L}(u_{s})\big)+\int_{E}\big[\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}-\langle g,D\varphi\rangle+\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\big]\nu(de).

(61)

For a fixed
u
, define
\tilde{\tau}^{u}
and
\tau_{h}^{u}
analogously; the moment estimates give
\mathbb{E}[\tau_{h}^{u}-\tau|\mathcal{F}_{\tau}]\geq h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
uniformly in
u
. The same Itô-Kunita decomposition leads to

\hat{f}^{u}_{s}\leq f\big(s,X_{s},u_{s},\varphi,\hat{Z}^{u}_{s},\int_{E}\hat{K}^{u}_{s}(e)l(s,e)\nu(de)\big)-2\varepsilon,\quad\text{a.e. in }[\tau,\tau_{h}^{u}].

(62)

Let
Y^{u}
be the BSDE solution with terminal condition
V(\tau_{h}^{u},X_{\tau_{h}^{u}})
. By the sub-tangency property
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}_{V}^{-}
, we have
Y^{u}_{\tau_{h}^{u}}-\varphi(\tau_{h}^{u},X_{\tau_{h}^{u}})\geq 0
. Applying the linearisation and the adjoint process to
\Delta Y_{s}:=Y^{u}_{s}-\varphi(s,X_{s})
yields

Y^{u}_{\tau}-\varphi(\tau,\xi)\geq 2c\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{h}^{u}-\tau\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\big]\geq 2c\varepsilon\big(h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})\big).

(63)

Taking
\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\in\mathcal{U}}
on the left-hand side, the DPP gives
\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u}Y^{u}_{\tau}
, while the touching condition gives
\varphi(\tau,\xi)=\mathbb{V}(\tau,\xi)
on
\Omega_{\tau}
. Thus the left-hand side becomes zero, leading to

0\geq 2c\varepsilon h-\mathcal{O}(h^{2})>0,

(64)

a contradiction. Hence
V
is a stochastic viscosity supersolution.

Conclusion.
Parts I and II, together with the terminal condition
V(T,x)=h(x)
, rigorously establish
V
as a stochastic viscosity solution to the fully nonlinear BSHJB equation, completing the proof of
theorem

24
.

5.5
Maximal Viscosity Subsolution and Weak Comparison

To establish the weak comparison principle, we construct smooth approximations for the coefficients. To accommodate the
p
-th order polynomial growth, uniform convergence is evaluated under the standard supremum norm for the Lipschitz forward coefficients, and under the weighted norm
w_{p}(x):=(1+|x|^{p})^{-1}
for
f
and
h
. Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the standard non-negative symmetric mollifier:

\rho(x):=\begin{cases}\tilde{c}\exp\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}-1}\right),&\text{if }|x|<1,\\
0,&\text{if }|x|\geq 1,\end{cases}

(65)

where
\tilde{c}
is the normalization constant such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho(x)dx=1
. For each
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, define the scaled sequence
\rho_{l}(x):=l^{n}\rho(lx)
. We define the spatial convolutions for the state equation coefficients and the terminal condition as follows:

\displaystyle b_{l}(t,x,u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{l}(x-y)b(t,y,u)dy,\quad

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(t,x,u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{l}(x-y)\sigma(t,y,u)dy,

(66)

\displaystyle g_{l}(t,e,x,u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{l}(x-y)g(t,e,y,u)dy,\quad

\displaystyle h_{l}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{l}(x-y)h(y)dy.

For the generator
f
, the convolution is performed exclusively with respect to the spatial variable
x
:

f_{l}(t,x,u,y,z,k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{l}(x-y)f(t,y,u,y,z,k)dy.

(67)

Under Assumptions
1
and
4
, standard mollifier properties ensure the following uniform and weighted convergences as
l\to\infty
:

\|h-h_{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})}+\sup_{u\in U}\big(\|b-b_{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}+\|\sigma-\sigma_{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(L^{\infty})}+\|g-g_{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(L^{\infty}_{\nu})}\big)\to 0,

(68)

where
L^{\infty}_{\nu}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};L^{2}(\nu))
. Moreover, the state-independent Lipschitz continuity of
f
in
(y,z,k)
guarantees that its weighted convergence is uniform across all backward variables:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{u\in U,y,z,k}\|f(\cdot,\cdot,u,y,z,k)-f_{l}(\cdot,\cdot,u,y,z,k)\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}(L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})}=0.

(69)

Since the mollified coefficients possess bounded spatial derivatives of all orders locally, classical well-posedness theory for stochastic partial differential equations applies directly.

Proposition 25
.

For each integer
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and fixed admissible control
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists a unique predictable classical solution
V^{l,u}\in\mathcal{T}^{p}
to the following smoothed BSHJB equation governed by the mollified coefficients and the
fixed
control
u
:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -dV^{l,u}(t,x)&=F^{l,u}\big(t,x,V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u}\big)dt-Z^{l,u}(t,x)dW(t)\\
&\quad-\int_{E}K^{l,u}(t,e,x)\tilde{\mu}(dt,de),\end{aligned}\\
V^{l,u}(T,x)=h_{l}(x),\end{cases}

(70)

where
F^{l,u}
is defined analogously to (
35
) but using the mollified coefficients and the fixed control
u
. Furthermore, for any initial data
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the evaluated processes along the forward trajectory
X_{s}\equiv X_{s}^{t,x;u,l}
, defined by

\displaystyle Y_{s}

\displaystyle:=V^{l,u}(s,X_{s}),

(71)

\displaystyle Z_{s}

\displaystyle:=Z^{l,u}(s,X_{s-})+\sigma_{l}^{\top}(s,X_{s-},u_{s})DV^{l,u}(s,X_{s-}),

\displaystyle K_{s}(e)

\displaystyle:=K^{l,u}\big(s,e,X_{s-}+g_{l}(s,e,X_{s-},u_{s})\big)+I_{V^{l,u}}(s,e,X_{s-},u_{s}),

satisfy the associated smoothed FBSDE system on
[t,T]
.

To quantify the global approximation, we define the following space-invariant stochastic error processes:

\displaystyle\delta h_{l}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|h_{l}(x)-h(x)\big|w_{p}(x),

\displaystyle\delta f_{l}(t)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u\in U,y,z,k}\big|f_{l}(t,x,u,y,z,k)-f(t,x,u,y,z,k)\big|w_{p}(x),

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda_{l}(t)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u\in U}\Big(|b_{l}-b|+|\sigma_{l}-\sigma|+\|g_{l}-g\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\Big)(t,x,u).

Let
C_{V}
be a constant such that for all smooth approximations
V^{l,u}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[|DV^{l,u}(x)|+(1+|x|)\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{N}(x,E)}\|D^{2}V^{l,u}(\xi)\|\Big]w_{p}(x)\leq C_{V},

which exists due to the uniform
p
-th order polynomial growth of the smoothed solutions. Then define
(Y^{l},Z^{l},K^{l})\in\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{H}_{\nu}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as the unique solution to the bounding BSDE:

Y_{t}^{l}=\delta h_{l}+\int_{t}^{T}\big(\delta f_{l}(s)+C_{V}\delta\Lambda_{l}(s)\big)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{l}dW(s)-\int_{t}^{T}\int_{E}K_{s}^{l}(e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de).

(72)

By standard a priori estimates for linear BSDEs, the convergence of the smoothed coefficients implies that the driving terms of (
72
) vanish, yielding:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\|Y^{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=0.

(73)

Lemma 26
.

For each fixed admissible control
u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}
, setting our weighted patched performance index as

\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,x):=V^{l,u}(t,x)+Y^{l}_{t}\big(w_{p}(x)\big)^{-1},

(74)

we have
\hat{J}^{u}_{l}\in\mathcal{T}^{p}
with the global uniform limit in the weighted functional space:

\lim_{l\to\infty}\big\|\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,x)-J(t,x;u)\big\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}(L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})}=0,

(75)

and it algebraically satisfies, with respect to the frozen-control drift operator
F^{l,u}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname*{ess\,liminf}_{(s,x)\to(t^{+},y)}\mathbb{E}\Big[-\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)-F^{l,u}\big(s,x,\hat{J}^{u}_{l},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u}\big)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\geq 0,

(76)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. }\forall(t,y)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
F^{l,u}
uses the mollified coefficients without the infimum, we have
F^{l,u}\geq F
, where
F
is the original infimum operator, because replacing the exact coefficients by approximations introduces a controllable error that is absorbed by
Y^{l}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\hat{J}^{u}_{l}\in\mathcal{T}^{p}
follows directly from
proposition

25
, the moment estimates of
Y^{l}_{t}
, and the polynomial weight
w_{p}^{-1}
. Standard a priori estimates for jump diffusions yield the trajectory bound for any
(s,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[s,T]}|X^{s,x;u,l}_{t}-X^{s,x;u}_{t}|^{2}\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{s}^{T}\delta\Lambda_{l}(r)^{2}dr\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right].

(77)

To bound the performance gap, we evaluate
V^{l,u}
along the true trajectory
X^{s,x;u}_{t}
using its associated integro-differential generator
\mathcal{L}^{u}
, defined for
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{u}\phi(x)

\displaystyle:=\langle b(x,u),D\phi(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\big(\sigma\sigma^{\top}(x,u)D^{2}\phi(x)\big)

(78)

\displaystyle+\int_{E}\big[\phi\big(x+g(x,u,e)\big)-\phi(x)-\langle g(x,u,e),D\phi(x)\rangle\big]\nu(de),

with
\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}
denoting its mollified counterpart. Applying Itô’s formula to
V^{l,u}(r,X^{s,x;u}_{r})
and substituting the smoothed BSPDE
-(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l})V^{l,u}=f_{l}
, the drift term simplifies directly to:

-(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}=f_{l}\big(r,X^{s,x;u}_{r},u_{r},V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u}\big)+(\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}-\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}.

(79)

Applying standard stability estimates for Lipschitz BSDEs directly yields:

\displaystyle|V^{l,u}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[|h_{l}(X^{s,x;u}_{T})-h(X^{s,x;u}_{T})|

(80)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}\Big(\big|f_{l}(r,X^{s,x;u}_{r},u_{r},V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})

\displaystyle-f(r,X^{s,x;u}_{r},u_{r},V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})\big|

\displaystyle+\big|(\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}-\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}\big|\Big)dr\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\bigg].

Decomposing the generator residual, the drift and diffusion differences scale proportionally with
\delta\Lambda_{l}
and the linear growth of the coefficients. For the non-local jump operator, applying the mean value theorem and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields:

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}-\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E}\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{N}(x,e)}\|D^{2}V^{l,u}(\xi)\|\cdot(|g_{l}|+|g|)\cdot|g_{l}-g|\nu(de)

(81)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{N}(x,e)}\|D^{2}V^{l,u}(\xi)\|\bigg(\int_{E}2(|g_{l}|^{2}+|g|^{2})\nu(de)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg(\int_{E}|g_{l}-g|^{2}\nu(de)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the linear growth of
g
, the integral involving squared magnitudes is bounded by
C(1+|x|^{2})
, while the
L^{2}(\nu)
-norm of the coefficient difference is controlled by
\delta\Lambda_{l}
. Combining the residuals for drift, diffusion, and jumps, we obtain the total operator discrepancy:

|(\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}-\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}(x)|\leq C\delta\Lambda_{l}\big(|DV^{l,u}(x)|+(1+|x|)\sup_{\xi}\|D^{2}V^{l,u}(\xi)\|\big).

(82)

By the definition of
C_{V}
, the following pointwise bound holds along the trajectory:

|(\mathcal{L}^{u}_{l}-\mathcal{L}^{u})V^{l,u}(r,X^{s,x;u}_{r})|\leq C_{V}\delta\Lambda_{l}(r)\cdot\big(w_{p}(X^{s,x;u}_{r})\big)^{-1}.

(83)

Under Assumption
1
, standard moment bounds yield
\mathbb{E}[w_{p}(X^{s,x;u}_{r})^{-1}|\mathcal{F}_{s}]\leq C(1+|x|^{p})=Cw_{p}(x)^{-1}
. Substituting this into the stability estimate gives:

V^{l,u}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)\leq Cw_{p}(x)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\delta h_{l}(X^{s,x;u}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}\big(\delta f_{l}(r)+C_{V}\delta\Lambda_{l}(r)\big)dr\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{s}\right].

(84)

Recognizing the expectation term as the solution to the auxiliary BSDE (
72
) with vanishing martingale parts, the pointwise bound simplifies directly to:

V^{l,u}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)\leq\tilde{C}w_{p}(x)^{-1}Y^{l}_{s},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(85)

By the triangle inequality and the previous pointwise bound, we have:

|\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)|\leq|V^{l,u}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)|+Y^{l}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}\leq(\tilde{C}+1)Y^{l}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

(86)

Multiplying by
w_{p}(x)
and taking the supremum over
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
yields:

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)-J(s,x;u)|w_{p}(x)\leq(\tilde{C}+1)Y^{l}_{s}.

(87)

Since
\lim_{l\to\infty}\|Y^{l}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=0
, the global weighted convergence follows immediately. For the subsolution inequality, by construction,

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}V^{l,u}-\partial_{s}Y^{l}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}

(88)

\displaystyle=F^{l,u}(V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})+\big(\delta f_{l}(s)+C_{V}\delta\Lambda_{l}(s)\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

Because
F^{l,u}
uses the mollified coefficients and no infimum, the error bounds give

F^{l,u}(\hat{J}^{u}_{l},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})\leq F^{l,u}(V^{l,u},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})+L_{y}Y^{l}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1},

and therefore

-\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}-F^{l,u}(\hat{J}^{u}_{l},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})\geq\big(\delta f_{l}(s)+C_{V}\delta\Lambda_{l}(s)-L_{y}Y^{l}_{s}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

Taking the essential limit inferior as
(s,x)\to(t^{+},y)
preserves the inequality, and the right-hand side tends to
0
with
l
. This yields the desired property.

To localize the maximum point against the
\mathcal{O}(|x|^{p})
growth of the value functions, we introduce the strictly convex penalty function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

\chi(x):=\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}-1.

(89)

Direct differentiation yields the following bounds:

\displaystyle|D\chi(x)|

\displaystyle=(p+2)|x|\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C\big(1+|x|^{p+1}\big),

(90)

\displaystyle D^{2}\chi(x)

\displaystyle=(p+2)\Big[\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}I+p\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}xx^{\top}\Big]\geq(p+2)\big(1+|x|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}I.

(91)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the penalty term
-\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})
strictly dominates the polynomial growth of
w-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}
. Indeed, by Lemma
12
(i) and the construction of
\hat{J}^{u}_{l}
, we have
|w(t,x)|+|\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{p})
uniformly in
t
. Since
\chi(x-\bar{x})\geq\frac{1}{2}|x|^{p+2}-C_{\bar{x}}
for large
|x|
, the difference

w(t,x)-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,x)-\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})\leq C(1+|x|^{p})-\varepsilon\big(\tfrac{1}{2}|x|^{p+2}-C_{\bar{x}}\big)\to-\infty

as
|x|\to\infty
, uniformly in
\omega
on the full-measure set where the growth bounds hold. Consequently, the global maximum of this functional is attained
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. within a compact ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
whose radius
R=R(\varepsilon,\kappa,\omega)
depends only on
\varepsilon
,
\kappa
, and the growth constants, and not on the particular realization of
\omega
outside a null set. This compactness is essential for the application of the stochastic Ishii’s lemma.

Theorem 27
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
4
hold. Let
w\in\mathcal{T}^{p}
be a stochastic viscosity subsolution of the BSHJB equation
eq.

36
. It holds
\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}
that
w(t,x)\leq V(t,x)
for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
V
is the value function.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists a point
(t,\bar{x})\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
w(t,\bar{x})>\mathbb{V}(t,\bar{x})
with positive probability. By the approximation established in
lemma

26
, we can select a control
u\in\mathcal{U}
and an integer
l\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that:

\mathbb{P}\Big(w(t,\bar{x})-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,\bar{x})>\kappa\Big)>0,\quad\text{for some }\kappa>0.

(92)

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we penalize the difference and utilize the measurable selection theorem to find an
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variable
\xi_{t}
such that:

\alpha:=w(t,\xi_{t})-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,\xi_{t})-\varepsilon\chi(\xi_{t}-\bar{x})=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[w(t,x)-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(t,x)-\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})\Big]\geq\kappa,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(93)

Since the penalty
\chi
has growth order
p+2>p
, the maximum is attained a.s. within a compact ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
uniformly in
\varepsilon
, with
\xi_{t}\to\bar{x}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For
s\in(t,T]
, we select an
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable maximizer
\xi_{s}
satisfying:

\Big(w(s,\xi_{s})-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,\xi_{s})-\varepsilon\chi(\xi_{s}-\bar{x})\Big)^{+}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(w(s,x)-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)-\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})\Big)^{+}.

(94)

We introduce the bounding generator
g
based on the Lipschitz constants
L_{y},L_{z},\allowbreak L_{k}
of
f
:

g(y,z,k):=L_{y}y+L_{z}|z|+L_{k}\|k\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}.

(95)

Let
(Y,Z^{Y},K^{Y})
be the unique solution to the reflected BSDE (RBSDE) with generator
g
and lower obstacle
\Theta
:

\Theta_{s}:=\Big(w(s,\xi_{s})-\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,\xi_{s})-\varepsilon\chi(\xi_{s}-\bar{x})\Big)^{+}+\frac{\alpha(s-t)}{2(T-t)}.

(96)

Define the optimal stopping time
\tau:=\inf\{s\geq t:Y_{s}=\Theta_{s}\}
. On
[t,\tau]
,
Y
satisfies the unreflected BSDE:

dY_{s}=-g(Y_{s},Z^{Y}_{s},K^{Y}_{s})ds+Z^{Y}_{s}dW_{s}+K^{Y}_{s}d\tilde{\mu}.

(97)

By the comparison principle,
Y_{t}\geq\Theta_{t}=\alpha
. Since
Y_{T}=\Theta_{T}=\alpha/2<\alpha
, we inherently have
\mathbb{P}(\tau<T)>0
and
Y_{\tau}=\Theta_{\tau}\geq\alpha/2
. Furthermore, define the stopping time
\hat{\tau}:=\inf\Big\{s\geq\tau:\Theta_{s}\leq\frac{\alpha(s-t)}{2(T-t)}\Big\}
, which is the first time after
\tau
when the penalty-compressed error reduces to the linear baseline; by continuity
\Theta_{\tau}>\frac{\alpha(\tau-t)}{2(T-t)}
, so
\hat{\tau}>\tau
a.s., i.e.
\hat{\tau}\in\mathcal{T}^{+}_{\tau}
, and
\mathbb{P}(\tau<\hat{\tau})>0
. Define the test function
\phi\in C^{1,2}_{\mathbb{F}}
on
[t,\tau]
:

\phi(s,x):=\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)+\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})-\frac{\alpha(s-t)}{2(T-t)}+Y_{s},

(98)

where the dynamics of
\hat{J}^{u}_{l}
are given by:

d\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)=\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}(s,x)ds+Z^{l,u}(s,x)dW_{s}+K^{l,u}(s,x)d\tilde{\mu}.

(99)

By the obstacle condition,
(w-\phi)
attains its maximum of
0
at
(\tau,\xi_{\tau})
. Applying the viscosity subsolution property of
w
at this point yields:

0\geq\liminf_{(s,x)\to(\tau^{+},\xi_{\tau})}\mathbb{E}\Big[-\partial_{s}\phi(s,x)-F(s,x,\phi,Z^{\phi},K^{\phi})\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\Big].

(100)

Direct computation gives
-\partial_{s}\phi=-\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}+\frac{\alpha}{2(T-t)}+g(Y_{s},Z^{Y}_{s},K^{Y}_{s})
alongside

(Z^{\phi},K^{\phi})=(Z^{l,u}+Z^{Y}_{s},K^{l,u}+K^{Y}_{s}).

Using the Lipschitz continuity of
f
and the bound
F\leq F^{l,u}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle f(s,x,\phi,Z^{\phi},K^{\phi})

\displaystyle\leq f(s,x,\hat{J}^{u}_{l},Z^{l,u},K^{l,u})

(101)

\displaystyle+L_{y}\big(\varepsilon\chi(x-\bar{x})+Y_{s}\big)+L_{z}|Z^{Y}_{s}|+L_{k}\|K^{Y}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}.

By the polynomial growth of
\chi
, the nonlocal terms evaluated at
\phi
deviate from those at
\hat{J}^{u}_{l}
by at most
O(\varepsilon)
. Substituting
\phi
into the viscosity inequality and applying the subsolution condition
-\partial_{s}\hat{J}^{u}_{l}-F^{l,u}\geq 0
(
lemma

26
), the
(Y_{s},Z^{Y}_{s},K^{Y}_{s})
terms exactly cancel with
g
while the
O(\varepsilon)
difference is absorbed by
\varepsilon C_{R}
, leaving:

0\geq\frac{\alpha}{2(T-t)}+\operatorname*{ess\,\liminf}_{(s,x)\to(\tau^{+},\xi_{\tau})}\mathbb{E}\Big[-\varepsilon(L^{u}\chi+L_{y}\chi)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\Big].

(102)

Recall from the operator expansion:

L^{u}\chi+L_{y}\chi=\langle b,D\chi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma\sigma^{\top}D^{2}\chi)+\int_{E}\Big[\chi(x+g)-\chi(x)-\langle D\chi,g\rangle\Big]\nu(de)+L_{y}\chi.

(103)

The
(p+2)
-th order growth of
\chi
confines the maximizer
\xi_{\tau}
to a bounded ball
B_{R}(\bar{x})
, where the radius
R
is independent of
\varepsilon
. Since the coefficients satisfy the linear growth conditions (Assumption
1
) and
\xi_{\tau}\in B_{R}(\bar{x})
, there exists a constant
C_{R}>0
depending only on
R
such that:

|L^{u}\chi(\xi_{\tau})+L_{y}\chi(\xi_{\tau})|\leq C_{R},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(104)

Recall that
\alpha\geq\kappa>0
and
\tau\leq T
. Substituting the bound yields:

0\geq\frac{\alpha}{2(T-t)}-\varepsilon C_{R}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2T}-\varepsilon C_{R}.

(105)

Choosing
\varepsilon\in\big(0,\frac{\kappa}{2TC_{R}}\big)
yields a strict contradiction. Therefore, our initial assumption is false, which implies
w(t,\bar{x})\leq\mathbb{V}(t,\bar{x})
.

5.6
Uniqueness under Super-Parabolicity

To establish uniqueness via the weak comparison principle, we assume that the controlled diffusion coefficient
\sigma(t,x,u)
is deterministic (super-parabolicity). Conditioning on the driving noise then locally reduces the BSHJB equation to a parametrized deterministic integro-PDE on each small stochastic interval.

Lemma 29
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
4
hold. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=T
, a finite-dimensional noise projection
H^{N}_{t}
capturing the Brownian and Poisson histories up to time
t
, and smooth approximations
(h^{N},f^{N},b^{N},g^{N})\in C^{\infty}
with respect to
(H^{N},x)
that preserve the Lipschitz and growth conditions of the original coefficients. Denote by
\mathcal{P}^{N}_{t}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
H^{N}_{t}
. Define the weighted error processes:

\displaystyle\delta h^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|h^{N}(H^{N}_{T},x)-h(x)\big|w_{p}(x),

(107)

\displaystyle\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{x,u,y,z,k}\big|f^{N}(H^{N}_{t},t,x,u,y,z,k)-f(t,x,u,y,z,k)\big|w_{p}(x),

(108)

\displaystyle\delta b^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{x,u}\frac{\big|b^{N}(H^{N}_{t},t,x,u)-b(t,x,u)\big|}{1+|x|},

(109)

\displaystyle\delta g^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle:=\operatorname*{ess\,\sup}_{x,u}\frac{\|g^{N}(H^{N}_{t},t,x,u,\cdot)-g(t,x,u,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}}{1+|x|}.

(110)

These approximations satisfy the following
\mathcal{L}^{2}
-bound:

\|\delta h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\delta f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|\delta b^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}+\|\delta g^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}<\varepsilon.

(111)

Consequently, letting
\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{t}
denote the aggregated error bounding the forward dynamics, we have
\|\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The rigorous construction extends the finite-dimensional approximation of
[
22
]
to accommodate non-local Poisson jump measures. The complete proof is deferred to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 30
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. For the polynomial weight function
\varphi(x):=w_{p}(x)^{-1}\allowbreak=1+|x|^{p}
(
p\geq 2
), there exists a universal constant
C_{\varphi}>0
such that
\sup_{u\in U}\mathcal{L}^{u}\varphi(x)\leq C_{\varphi}\varphi(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The result follows from Taylor’s expansion with an integral remainder and the moment conditions on the Lévy measure. To maintain the flow of the main argument, the complete analytical proof is deferred to Appendix
B
.

Theorem 31
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
4
hold, and suppose the controlled diffusion coefficient
\sigma(t,x,u)
is deterministic. Then, the stochastic viscosity solution to the fully nonlinear BSHJB equation
eq.

36
is unique within the weighted stochastic functional space
\mathbb{S}^{p}(L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})
.

Proof of
theorem

31
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{p}(L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})
be two stochastic viscosity solutions of
eq.

36
. We prove
u_{1}=u_{2}
by backward induction on the partition
\{t_{k}\}_{k=0}^{N}
from Lemma
29
.

Preliminary: Conditional reduction on each subinterval.

Fix a subinterval
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
. Conditioning on the cylinder
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{P}^{N}_{t_{k-1}}
reduces the BSHJB equation along a fixed path of
H^{N}
to a deterministic parabolic integro-PDE. Specifically, for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
and
\mathbf{h}:=H^{N}_{t_{k-1}}(\omega)
, we define the conditional coefficients:

\displaystyle b^{\mathbf{h}}(s,x,u)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[b^{N}(s,x,u)\mid H^{N}_{t_{k-1}}=\mathbf{h}],

\displaystyle f^{\mathbf{h}}(s,x,u,y,z,k)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[f^{N}(s,x,u,y,z,k)\mid H^{N}_{t_{k-1}}=\mathbf{h}],

and analogously for
\sigma,g,h
. On
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
, the conditional BSHJB takes the deterministic form:

\begin{split}-\partial_{s}V^{\mathbf{h}}(s,x)&=\inf_{u\in U}H^{\mathbf{h}}\big(s,x,u,V^{\mathbf{h}},DV^{\mathbf{h}},0,0,D^{2}V^{\mathbf{h}},L^{\mathbf{h}}(V^{\mathbf{h}})\big),\\
V^{\mathbf{h}}(t_{k},x)&=\mathbb{E}[h(x)\mid H^{N}_{t_{k-1}}=\mathbf{h}].\end{split}

(112)

The martingale representation terms vanish
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. on
(t_{k-1},t_{k}]
under this conditioning (see Lemma
32
below). Equation (
112
) is a fully nonlinear parabolic integro-PDE with smooth coefficients and uniformly elliptic diffusion
\sigma^{\mathbf{h}}(\sigma^{\mathbf{h}})^{\top}\geq\delta I
. By classical regularity theory
[
1
]
, it admits a unique classical solution
V^{\mathbf{h}}\in C^{1,2}((t_{k-1},t_{k}]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
depending smoothly on
\mathbf{h}
.

Uniform a priori bounds.

Standard parabolic estimates for (
112
) yield a constant
\tilde{C}>0
, independent of
k
and
\mathbf{h}
, such that

|DV^{\mathbf{h}}(s,x)|\leq\tilde{C}\big(1+|x|^{p-1}\big),\quad\|D^{2}V^{\mathbf{h}}(s,x)\|\leq\tilde{C}\big(1+|x|^{p-2}\big).

(113)

Lifting back to the stochastic setting via
V_{N}(s,x,\omega):=V^{H^{N}_{t_{k-1}}(\omega)}(s,x)
, we have for any
u\in U
:

|DV_{N}(s,x)|+(1+|x|)\|D^{2}V_{N}(s,x)\|\leq C_{1}w_{p}(x)^{-1},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(114)

To bound the non-local jump variation
\mathcal{I}_{\text{jump}}
, we partition it into low-intensity (
\mathcal{I}_{\text{low-int}}
) and high-intensity (
\mathcal{I}_{\text{high-int}}
) regimes based on
\rho(e)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{jump}}(s,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}\Big|V_{N}(s,x+g_{N})-V_{N}(s,x)-\langle DV_{N}(s,x),g_{N}\rangle\Big|\nu(de)

(115)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\rho(e)<1\}}\Big|V_{N}(s,x+g_{N})-V_{N}(s,x)-\langle DV_{N}(s,x),g_{N}\rangle\Big|\nu(de)

\displaystyle+\int_{\{\rho(e)\geq 1\}}\Big|V_{N}(s,x+g_{N})-V_{N}(s,x)-\langle DV_{N}(s,x),g_{N}\rangle\Big|\nu(de)

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{I}_{\text{low-int}}(s,x)+\mathcal{I}_{\text{high-int}}(s,x).

Applying Taylor’s theorem with integral remainder to
\mathcal{I}_{\text{low-int}}
yields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{low-int}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\{\rho(e)<1\}}\sup_{\theta\in[0,1]}\|D^{2}V_{N}(s,x+\theta g_{N})\|\cdot|g_{N}|^{2}\nu(de)

(116)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{C}\int_{\{\rho(e)<1\}}\Big(1+\big(|x|+\rho(e)(1+|x|+|u|)\big)^{p-2}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\rho(e)^{2}(1+|x|+|u|)^{2}\nu(de)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

For
\mathcal{I}_{\text{high-int}}
, the triangle inequality gives:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{high-int}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\rho(e)\geq 1\}}\Big(|V_{N}(s,x+g_{N})|+|V_{N}(s,x)|+|\langle DV_{N}(s,x),g_{N}\rangle|\Big)\nu(de)

(117)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\int_{\{\rho(e)\geq 1\}}\Big[2+\big(|x|+\rho(e)(1+|x|+|u|)\big)^{p}+|x|^{p}

\displaystyle+\big(1+|x|^{p-1}\big)\rho(e)(1+|x|+|u|)\Big]\nu(de)

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

Combining these bounds yields a uniform constant
C_{V}>0
(independent of
N,\varepsilon
) such that

|DV_{N}(s,x)|+(1+|x|)\|D^{2}V_{N}(s,x)\|+\mathcal{I}_{jump}(s,x)\leq C_{V}w_{p}(x)^{-1},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(118)

Step 1: Terminal interval
[t_{N-1},t_{N}]
.

Let
V_{N}^{(N)}
be the unique classical solution of (
112
) on
(t_{N-1},t_{N}]
with terminal data
h_{N}:=\mathbb{E}[h\mid\mathcal{P}^{N}_{t_{N-1}}]
. Define the conditional operator
F_{N}
as
F
but with coefficients
(b_{N},\sigma_{N},g_{N},f_{N},h_{N})
and without the essential infimum (it is a frozen-control operator on each cylinder path). The conditional classical solution satisfies:

-\partial_{s}V_{N}^{(N)}=F_{N}\big(s,x,V_{N}^{(N)},0,0\big),\quad V_{N}^{(N)}(t_{N},x)=h_{N}(x).

(119)

We now upgrade Lemma
29
from
L^{2}
convergence to
L^{\infty}_{w_{p}}
-weighted convergence. By the martingale representation theorem for jump-diffusions and the density of cylinder functions in
L^{2}
, the
L^{2}
bounds of Lemma
29
imply the existence of a subsequence such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Big(\|\delta h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\delta f^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega;L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})}+\|\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\Omega)}\Big)=0

(120)

after passing to a common subsequence and applying the Borel–Cantelli lemma. More precisely, for
\varepsilon=2^{-m}
, set
A_{m}:=\{\|\delta h^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\cdots>m^{-1}\}
; by Chebyshev and the
L^{2}
bounds,
\sum\mathbb{P}(A_{m})<\infty
, so
\mathbb{P}(\limsup A_{m})=0
. Hence, on a full-measure set, the weighted errors vanish uniformly. In the sequel we work on this full-measure set.

To bound the local errors and absorb the spatial derivatives of the polynomial weight
w_{p}(x)^{-1}
, we utilize the Lyapunov-type condition from Lemma
30
:
\sup_{u\in U}\mathcal{L}^{u}(w_{p}(x)^{-1})\leq C_{\varphi}w_{p}(x)^{-1}
. Introduce an auxiliary linear BSDE
(Y^{\varepsilon,(N)},\allowbreak Z^{\varepsilon,(N)},\allowbreak K^{\varepsilon,(N)})
on
[t_{N-1},t_{N}]
with terminal condition
Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{t_{N}}=\delta h^{\varepsilon}
:

\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}

\displaystyle=-\Big(\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{s}+C_{V}\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{s}+(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}+L_{z}|Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|

(121)

\displaystyle+L_{k}\|K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\Big)ds+Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}dW_{s}+\int_{E}K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}(e)\tilde{\mu}(ds,de).

Standard BSDE estimates ensure
\|Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{\infty}}\leq K_{0}\varepsilon
on the full-measure set identified above. Define the bounding envelopes on
[t_{N-1},t_{N}]
:

\displaystyle\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}(s,x)

\displaystyle:=V_{N}^{(N)}(s,x)+Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1},

(122)

\displaystyle\underline{V}_{N,\varepsilon}(s,x)

\displaystyle:=V_{N}^{(N)}(s,x)-Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

Since
V_{N}^{(N)}
is
C^{1,2}
and
Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}
is a semimartingale with no spatial derivatives, for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
and each
x
, the predictable drift rate of
\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}
is:

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{s}V_{N}^{(N)}+\big(\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{s}+C_{V}\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{s}+(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}

(123)

\displaystyle+L_{z}|Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|+L_{k}\|K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

The critical step is to compare
F
evaluated at the upper envelope with
F_{N}
evaluated at
V_{N}^{(N)}
. Since
F
contains the essential infimum over
u\in U
, we cannot directly take differences term-by-term across two different infimum operations. Instead, we use the following pointwise bound: for any
\phi,\psi
with
|\phi-\psi|\leq\delta
pointwise,

\displaystyle\Big|\inf_{u\in U}H\big(t,x,u,\phi,D\phi,q,Q,D^{2}\phi,k\big)-\inf_{u\in U}H\big(t,x,u,\psi,D\psi,q,Q,D^{2}\psi,k\big)\Big|

(124)

\displaystyle\leq L\big(|\phi-\psi|+|D\phi-D\psi|+\|D^{2}\phi-D^{2}\psi\|\big),

where
L
is the Lipschitz constant of
H
in its arguments
(y,p,q,Q,A,k)
uniformly in
u
, which follows from Assumption
4
(ii) and the linear growth of
(b,\sigma,g)
. Indeed, for each
u
,
H(u,\cdot)

Now evaluate the nonlinear operator

at the upper envelope

Importantly, the weight

for each fixed

\displaystyle D\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}

\displaystyle=DV_{N}^{(N)}+Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}D(w_{p}^{-1}),

\displaystyle D^{2}\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}

\displaystyle=D^{2}V_{N}^{(N)}+Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}D^{2}(w_{p}^{-1}),

with no spatial derivatives applied to
Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}
or
K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}

The correct inequality to verify is

(126)

Since

satisfies a uniform Lipschitz condition in

with constants

(inherited from

via the infimum; see (

(127)

Applying this with
\phi=\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}
,

,

and using (

\displaystyle F\big(s,x,\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N},Z^{\overline{V}},K^{\overline{V}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq F(s,x,\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N},0,0)+\big(L_{z}|Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|+L_{k}\|K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

We now estimate

\displaystyle\big|F(s,x,\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N},0,0)-F(s,x,V_{N}^{(N)},0,0)\big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq L_{y}|Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|w_{p}(x)^{-1}+C_{V}|Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|\Big(|D(w_{p}^{-1})|

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+|x|)\|D^{2}(w_{p}^{-1})\|\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}.

(129)

Since

and

only differ by their coefficients, the

Combining (

123
), (
128
), (
129
) and (
130

\displaystyle-\partial_{s}\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}-F\big(s,x,\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N},Z^{\overline{V}},K^{\overline{V}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq-\partial_{s}\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N}-F(s,x,\overline{V}^{\varepsilon}_{N},0,0)-\big(L_{z}|Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|+L_{k}\|K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{s}+C_{V}\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{s}+(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}+L_{z}|Z^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}|+L_{k}\|K^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq-\big(\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{s}+C_{V}\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{s}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}-(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\delta f^{\varepsilon}_{s}+C_{V}\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}_{s}+(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}\big)w_{p}(x)^{-1}

Appendix A
Finite-Dimensional Projection for Jump Measures

Proof of Lemma 5.12.

The proof extends the density arguments for Brownian filtrations (
[
22
]
) to the jump-diffusion setting by explicitly partitioning the mark space. We outline the construction for the generator
f
.

Since the progressively measurable coefficient
f
can be approximated in
L^{2}
by time-step functions
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}\mathbf{1}_{(t_{j-1},t_{j}]}(t)
with
\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{t_{j-1}}
, the core task is to approximate
\varphi_{j}
using a finite-dimensional noise projection. Because the characteristic measure is finite (
\nu(E)<\infty
), the jump process exhibits finitely many jumps almost surely. We partition the mark space
E
into
M
disjoint measurable subsets
E_{1},\dots,E_{M}
, and define the finite-dimensional projection
H^{N,M}_{t}
comprising both Brownian increments and discrete Poisson counts:

H^{N,M}_{t}:=\Big(W(t_{1}\wedge t),\dots,W(t_{N}\wedge t),\mu\big((0,t_{1}\wedge t]\times E_{1}\big),\dots,\mu\big((0,t_{N}\wedge t]\times E_{M}\big)\Big).

By the density of cylinder functions, any
\mathcal{F}_{t_{j-1}}
-measurable random variable can be approximated in
L^{2}
by a measurable mapping of
H^{N,M}_{t_{j-1}}
. Crucially, although the jump components of
H^{N,M}
take values in the discrete lattice
\mathbb{N}
, such mappings can be smoothed as follows. For each coordinate
k
corresponding to a Poisson count, let
\psi_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the piecewise linear interpolation on
\mathbb{N}
extended by zero outside
\mathbb{N}
, then set
\tilde{\psi}_{k}:=\psi_{k}*\rho_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\rho_{\delta}
is a standard mollifier supported on
(-\delta,\delta)
with
\delta\in(0,1/4)
. This
\tilde{\psi}_{k}
coincides with the original mapping on
\mathbb{N}
and preserves the global Lipschitz constant
C
(since convolution with a positive mollifier of mass 1 does not increase the Lipschitz constant). Moreover, the linear growth condition is preserved because
|\tilde{\psi}_{k}(t)|\leq C(1+|t|)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Applying this smoothing coordinate-wise and composing with the cylinder mapping yields a smooth cylinder function
\tilde{G}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
evaluated on
H^{N,M}_{t_{j-1}}
that approximates the target random variable. Aggregating these smooth interpolations globally yields the approximation
f^{N,M}
, which strictly preserves the required Lipschitz continuity and linear growth conditions.

Upgrading
L^{2}
convergence to
L^{\infty}_{w_{p}}
convergence.

The
L^{2}
bounds in Lemma
29
are sufficient for the envelope construction after a Borel–Cantelli argument. Take
\varepsilon_{m}=2^{-m}
and let
E_{m}
be the event that the total weighted error exceeds
m^{-1}
. By Chebyshev’s inequality,
\mathbb{P}(E_{m})\leq Cm^{2}2^{-m}
, whence
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(E_{m})<\infty
. The Borel–Cantelli lemma implies
\mathbb{P}(\limsup_{m\to\infty}E_{m})=0
, so on a full-measure set
\Omega_{0}
, there exists
M(\omega)
such that for all
m\geq M
the weighted error is bounded by
m^{-1}
. Consequently, along the subsequence
\varepsilon_{m}
, the convergence is uniform in
\omega
on
\Omega_{0}
, i.e. in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{\infty}_{w_{p}})
. The same argument applies to
\delta\Lambda^{\varepsilon}
via the pathwise stability of the forward SDE. Hence, for all practical purposes in the uniqueness proof, we may work with
L^{\infty}_{w_{p}}
convergence.

Appendix B
Proof of the Lyapunov Condition for the Jump Operator

Proof of Lemma
30
.

Direct differentiation yields
|D\varphi(x)|\leq p|x|^{p-1}
and
\|D^{2}\varphi(x)\|\leq p(p-1)|x|^{p-2}
. For the local drift and diffusion terms, the linear growth of
b
and boundedness of
\sigma
immediately yield
\langle b,D\varphi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\sigma\sigma^{\top}D^{2}\varphi)\leq C_{1}(1+|x|^{p})=C_{1}\varphi(x)
.

For the non-local jump operator, Taylor’s expansion with integral remainder gives

\varphi(x+g)-\varphi(x)-\langle D\varphi(x),g\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)\langle D^{2}\varphi(x+\theta g)g,g\rangle d\theta.

Using the elementary inequality
|x+\theta g|^{p-2}\leq 2^{(p-3)^{+}}(|x|^{p-2}+|g|^{p-2})
and the linear growth
|g|\leq\rho(e)(1+|x|)
, the integral remainder is bounded by
C_{p}|g|^{2}(|x|^{p-2}+|g|^{p-2})\leq C^{\prime}_{p}(\rho(e)^{2}+\rho(e)^{p})\varphi(x)
.

Integrating this upper bound over the mark space
E
yields

\int_{E}\big[\varphi(x+g)-\varphi(x)-\langle D\varphi(x),g\rangle\big]\nu(de)\leq C^{\prime}_{p}\varphi(x)\int_{E}\big(\rho(e)^{2}+\rho(e)^{p}\big)\nu(de).

By Assumption
1
, the exponential moment condition ensures
\int_{E}\big(\rho(e)^{2}\allowbreak+\rho(e)^{p}\big)\,\nu(de)\allowbreak<\infty
. Summing the local and non-local bounds completes the proof with
C_{\varphi}=C_{1}+C^{\prime}_{p}\int_{E}(\rho^{2}+\rho^{p})\,d\nu
.

Weighted Hessian bound for
w_{p}(x)^{-1}
.

For completeness, we record the estimate used in (
129
). Let
\psi(x):=w_{p}(x)^{-1}=1+|x|^{p}
. Direct computation gives:

\displaystyle D\psi(x)

\displaystyle=p|x|^{p-2}x,

\displaystyle D^{2}\psi(x)

\displaystyle=p|x|^{p-2}I+p(p-2)|x|^{p-4}xx^{\top},

so
|D\psi(x)|\leq C_{p}(1+|x|^{p-1})
and
\|D^{2}\psi(x)\|\leq C_{p}(1+|x|^{p-2})
. The Lyapunov condition from Lemma
30
applied to
\varphi=\psi
yields
\mathcal{L}^{u}\psi(x)\leq C_{\varphi}\psi(x)/2
(the factor
1/2
is for convenience). Hence,

|D\psi(x)|+(1+|x|)\|D^{2}\psi(x)\|+\mathcal{L}^{u}\psi(x)\leq C_{\varphi}\psi(x).

(136)

This justifies the absorption of the spatial derivative terms in (
129
) into
(L_{y}+C_{\varphi})Y^{\varepsilon,(N)}_{s}w_{p}(x)^{-1}
.
