Analysis of salt change and desalination effect of saline soil in Xinjiang under the influence of multiple measures
Authors: Ling Du, Daqian Zhang, Yingying Xu, Yin Sun, Jianjiang Li, Ankang Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo evaluate the remediation strategies for newly reclaimed, heavily salinized farmland in Xinjiang province, China, a three-year field experiment was conducted comparing different treatments, such as agricultural improvement measures alone, and agricultural measures combined with root zone salt separation, subsurface pipe drainage, or chemical amendments, alongside a natural control. Result findings indicated that the artificial irrigation, evapotranspiration, and groundwater depth were significantly correlated with soil salinity, with irrigation during the growth period showing the strongest negative correlation with plough layer salinity (r = -0.871). However, the use of agricultural measures alone resulted in salt accumulation in the surface layer, whereas the subsurface pipe and chemical treatments successfully shifted salt to the bottom layer. Temporally, desalination efficiency peaked in the second year, particularly for root zone separation and pipe drainage treatments (rates > 44%), before declining in the third year as the total salt efficiency decreased. Overall, the root zone salt separation and subsurface pipe drainage treatments achieved the best performance, with desalination rates of 61.33 and 59.37% in the 0–80 cm profile, respectively. While chemical improvement outperformed agricultural measures in the 0–40 cm layer (55.32% vs. 45.42%), there was no significant difference between them across the complete 0–80 cm profile. Consequently, from the perspective of soil desalination, combining engineering measures, especially root-zone salt isolation or subsurface pipe drainage, with agricultural practices, such as deep tillage and organic fertilizer application may provide an effective approach for improving severely salinized farmland in arid regions. However, large-scale applications should further consider construction cost, maintenance requirements, and long-term economic benefits in years to come.Keywords: Saline soil, De-salination factor, Agricultural improvement, Water and salt migration, Soil profile improvementSubject terms: Engineering, Environmental sciences, Hydrology

Introduction

Saline soil, as a special soil type formed under the combined action of specific climate, topography, soil, and hydrogeological conditions, is widely distributed in the extremely arid Xinjiang region, China. The strong evaporation and limited precipitation in this area have aggravated surface accumulation and secondary soil salinization, making Xinjiang a typical distribution area of saline soil in China1–3. There are various types of saline soil in Xinjiang, and the degree of salt accumulation is deep. It is known as the “World Saline-Alkali Soil Museum”4–6. From an engineering perspective, when the mass fraction of soluble salts in the soil exceeds 0.3%, it is classified as a saline soil. This kind of soil is sensitive to water-salt migration and phase change reaction, and its structural stability is poor. It is easy to cause engineering geological problems, such as salt expansion, subsidence, and corrosion, due to changes in temperature and humidity, posing a continuous threat to infrastructure durability7,8. Under the environmental conditions of drought and alternating severe cold and drought, the water and salt transport driven by the temperature gradient is particularly active, further promoting the surface accumulation and redistribution of soil salinity4,9. The resulting high-salt environment, coupled with secondary salinization caused by unreasonable irrigation, has become a major obstacle to the sustainable development of agriculture8,10–12.

As a land resource with potential utilization value, the management and improvement of saline soil has always been of concern to academic circles at national and international levels, and its effectiveness is directly related to regional ecological security and grain production capacity13. The current governance approaches mainly include comprehensive measures, such as physics, chemistry, water conservancy projects, agriculture, and biological improvement. Studies have shown that agricultural measures, such as straw mulching combined with winter irrigation, can enhance soil aggregate stability, promote salt leaching, and improve crop yield and quality during the freeze-thaw period14–16. Hydraulic engineering measures are often regarded as the key strategy for treating saline soil because they can regulate groundwater levels over the long term, inhibit the upward movement of salt, and improve soil structure17–20. In addition, the application of organic fertilizer, straw returning, deep tillage, soil loosening, and other agronomic approaches has also been proven to alleviate soil salinization. Organic fertilizer can not only promote the formation of an aggregate structure but also improve soil nutrient levels and water-holding capacity (WHC), thereby improving crop growth under salt stress. In recent years, with the development of remote sensing and numerical simulation technologies, collaborative management of water and salt has become a research frontier. Zhang et al.21 discussed the allocation of water resources in inland river basins from the perspective of blue-green water.

Wang et al.22 applied the electromagnetic induction and machine learning to reveal the temporal and spatial variation in salt concentrations in the root zone, providing a advance approach to fine-scale management of saline soil. The process above shows that soil salinization has long-term, complex characteristics, and that a single improvement model is usually difficult to achieve lasting results. Therefore, the organic integration of water conservancy projects and agricultural measures is the key to achieving long-term management of saline soil23–26. However, most existing research focuses on the single measure. Because of significant differences in soil parent materials, climate, and hydrology across regions, it is difficult to directly compare the desalination effects of different measures, which greatly limits the optimization and promotion of comprehensive management models. Therefore, it is of great significance to conduct long-term comparative empirical research across different regions to assess efficient improvement models suitable for local conditions.

The present study selected newly reclaimed farmland characterized by high groundwater levels, poor water quality, and severe salinization in the arid region of Bachu County, Xinjiang province, China as the research objective. Building on conventional agricultural improvement measures, systematic treatments involving root-zone salt separation, subsurface pipe salt discharge, and chemical amendment were implemented. Through long-term, site-specific observation, the dynamic changes in soil salinity and the corresponding desalination effects under different treatments were compared and analyzed, to provide a theoretical basis and technical knowledge for the precise amelioration and sustainable utilization of saline affected area.

Materials and methods

Experimental location

The study area is located in Bachu County, Xinjiang Province, which is the part of Kashi area of the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, China. It is located in the southwest of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, located in the southern foot of the Tianshan Mountains and the northwest margin of the Tarim Basin. The geographical coordinates are between 77° 22′ 30′′ −79° 56′ 15′′ E and 38° 47′ 30′′ −40° 17′ 30′′ N. The terrain is inclined from southwest to northeast. The main geomorphological features are desert, mountain, and alluvial plain. The test area is located in the Gobi area between Bachu County and Sanchakou Town. The southwest of the experimental site is high, with a cotton field reclaimed for 5–10 years. Due to differences in management practices among planting farmers, some cotton fields are still irrigated by flood irrigation. There is a drainage channel on the northeast side of the test area.

Due to siltation in the channel, the depth is about 1 m, and the northeast side of the channel is a highway. The local soil layer is thick, the groundwater level is high, the annual groundwater depth is between 0.5 and 3.0 m, the groundwater salinity exceeds 30 g/L, and soil salinization is severe. The soil texture in the study area is mainly medium loam. The initial salt content is higher in the surface and bottom layers, while the middle layer has lower salt content. The salt content in the 0–80 cm soil layer is 19.83–24.25 g/kg. Based on the salt distribution, the salt content is higher in the northwest and southeast, while the middle area is relatively low. This spatial heterogeneity was taken into account when interpreting the effects of different treatments on desalination. Due to the shortage of water resources and shallow groundwater depth in the study area, the shaft irrigation and drainage measures have been implemented for many years. The shaft is located 1–2 km from the experimental site on the southwest and southeast sides. The location of the research area is shown in Fig. 1.

The experimental area typically refers to designated physical zones where researchers manipulate variables to study outcomes. T1 - T5 represent the agricultural improvement, root zone salt separation, subsurface drainage, chemical improvement, and natural control, respectively.

Climatic condition

The agroclimatic factors of the test area come from the Bachu County Meteorological Station of the Xinjiang Meteorological Bureau. The meteorological station is located near the experimental area in Bachu County, Kashgar Prefecture. The annual precipitation was monitored from 2023 to 2025 (173, 147, and 165.3 mm), respectively. The precipitation is mainly concentrated from April to August. The maximum precipitation occurs from June to August, accounting for 65.9, 63.3, and 87.4% of the annual total, respectively. The optimum temperature occurs from June to July, and the average temperature from April to August of > 22 °C. The precipitation concentration time is basically consistent with the time of excess ambient air temperature (Fig. 2).

Experimental design

The commonly used saline-alkali soil improvement measures at regional and international levels were selected. Four improvement measures were set up in the same newly reclaimed land with shallow groundwater depth and severe soil salinization, which were agricultural improvement measures (T1), root zone salt separation treatment with agricultural improvement measures (T2, referred as root zone salt separation), subsurface pipe salt discharge treatment with agricultural improvement measures (T3, referred as subsurface pipe salt discharge), chemical improvement treatment with agricultural improvement measures (T4, referred as chemical improvement), and natural control (CK, no improvement measures, tillage and planting).

Agricultural improvement measures were selected as a local cotton varieties of plastic film mulching planting, farming operations in accordance with the local field management commonly used, cotton annual application of pure nitrogen, pure phosphorus (P2O5), potassium (K2O) were 450,150,75 kg/hm2, 20% of the nitrogen (N) fertilizer and phosphorus and potassium fertilizer into a one-time as base fertilizer, 80% of the N fertilizer with water in the last 6 times drip with water to do fertilizer into. After harvest, deep plowing, straw returning, and organic fertilizer (cattle manure) at 40 m3/hm2 were applied. The first water in the growth period was irrigated in early and mid-May, and the last water in the growth period was irrigated on August 25. The irrigation capacity was 450–675 m3/hm2 each time. Generally, the water was irrigated once every 15 days, for a total of 8 irrigation frequency. The irrigation capacity during the growth period was 4800 m3/hm2 (Table 1). Due to the optimum temperature in the study area from July to August and the vigorous crop growth, irrigation could not fully suitable for crop requirement. The irrigation frequency, irrigation duration and amount in the experiment were consistent with the local farmland practices. The irrigation source was reservoir water, and the salinity level was 0.4–1.0 g/L. Cotton planting method is film mulching cultivation, 1 film 4 lines, row spacing 30 + 60+30 cm, planting density 22.2 × 104 plants/hm2. The irrigation method is drip irrigation under film, i.e., 1 film, 2 tubes, dripper spacing, 40 cm, and dripper flow 2.6 L/h.

Root zone salt separation treatment was set up a salt-isolation layer below the soil plough layer. The isolation layer can inhibit the increase of capillary water, promote the downward infiltration of gravity water, and increase the soil desalination coefficient above the salt isolation layer. The project uses a layered excavation of 1 m deep earthwork, according to the natural slope of the south high north low to ensure the bottom slope of 1:500, after drying the bottom of the soil pit is covered with shade cloth, the side is covered with waterproof plastic cloth, and then 15 cm thick, 2–4 cm particle size of the stone, the stone is covered with a layer of geotextile, and finally 0–80 cm excavated earthwork is backfilled layer by layer to ensure that the soil depth of the plough layer is approximately 80 cm.

The treatment of underground pipe salt discharge of a D110 (pipe diameter 110 mm) corrugated drainage pipe is laid north-south; the corrugated pipe surface is perforated, and the geotextile is wrapped. Based on the theoretical formula of underground pipe embedding27 and the technical specification of farmland drainage engineering (SL/T4-1999), it is determined that the buried depth of the underground pipe in the test area is 0.8–1.0 m, the interval is 10 m, the slope is 1:500, the southern end of the drainage pipe is connected with the D50 (pipe diameter 50 mm) exhaust valve, the surface is exposed to the drainage pipe, the northern end is connected with the D160PVC collector pipe, the gate valve, control the connection and the catchment pool is built. It can be directly discharged into the north side of the experimental area.

The national patent product, saline-alkali soil conditioner (acid-base balance nursing solution), developed independently by Beijing Hongdu Jiaye Science and Technology Development Company, China was used. The product had a good application effect in different areas of Xinjiang. In this study, the recommended application amount was 30 kg/hm2 according to the soil salt content in the test area, and the application method was applied with drip irrigation with water. After sowing, water was dripped into the seed hole at the edge of the film, and then the acid-base balance nursing agent was diluted 1:150 per hectare and added to the drip fertigation tank for 90–120 min28. According to the product technical specification, this conditioner belongs to an acid-regulating chemical amendment for saline-alkali soil. Its main functions are to regulate the soil solution’s acid-base environment, promote ion exchange and complexation, reduce the activity of harmful ions in the root zone, and enhance salt leaching under drip irrigation. The treatment was applied with irrigation water, so its effect was expected to be stronger in the shallow cultivated layer and the main wetted zone of drip irrigation. Because the exact composition and concentration of commercial conditioners may vary among products, the present study evaluated the field desalination performance of this conditioner under the recommended dosage rather than the reaction effect of a single chemical component.

The test area is 70 m wide in the north and south, about 550 m long in the east and west, with a total area of 3.85 hm2 and an altitude of 380.1–381.2 m. There are observation wells for groundwater depth in each treatment test site. The area of T1, T2, T3, and T4 treatment plots is 100 × 70 m, and the control is 150 × 70 m. The arrangement of each treatment is shown in Fig. 1. It should be noted that this experiment was conducted as a field-scale, long-term positioning observation trial rather than a strictly randomized block experiment with independent replicated plots. This design was adopted because some improvement measures, especially root-zone salt isolation and subsurface pipe drainage, require relatively large and continuous engineering plots to maintain construction integrity, drainage slope, and hydraulic connectivity. To reduce the influence of field heterogeneity, all treatment plots were arranged within the same newly reclaimed farmland, with similar soil texture, shallow groundwater conditions, and unified crop cultivation and irrigation management. Groundwater observation wells were set in each treatment plot, and ten soil sampling points were arranged in each treatment to characterize the spatial variation of soil salinity within the plot. Therefore, the comparison among treatments was mainly based on the relative changes in soil salinity before and after treatment, and three-year temporal variation, rather than absolute salinity values alone.

Collection of samples

From 2023 to 2025, samples were collected in different improvement-measures test areas throughout the crop growth period (March-September), and photographs were taken to observe phenotypic changes in surface soil salinity, crop growth, and water status (surface water, groundwater, and subsurface pipe drainage). Soil samples were collected per treatment (10), and the soil depth was divided into different depths, such as 0–20, 20–40, 40–60, and 60–80 cm. An indoor conductivity meter (DDS-307, Shanghai Leici, China) was used to monitor the conductivity of the 1:5 soil-water mass ratio extract. Water sample were collect from each treatment groundwater observation well; measure the groundwater depth; record the collection date and corresponding treatment in details; collect irrigation water and subsurface pipe drainage water samples; if there is no drainage from the subsurface pipe, record it as 0.

Data measurement and processing

The climatic data values were observed on the monthly average ambient air temperature, precipitation, and evaporation from the local Mosuowan meteorological station. The amount of irrigation water was measured by the water meter for each irrigation. The salt content of soil and water samples was determined by a conductivity meter. The total salt content and soil water content of the samples were determined by the drying method. The groundwater salinity was determined by the gravimetric method. The initial soil salt and the salt at the end of the growth period were measured before and after cotton sowing, respectively. Soil desalination rate32(%) = (Initial soil salt - the salt at the end of the growth period)/Initial soil salt × 100%.

Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate the relationships between soil salinity and the main environmental or management factors, including monthly average temperature, precipitation, evaporation, irrigation amount, groundwater depth, and mineralization. The significance of the correlation coefficients was tested using a two-tailed test. The significance levels were set at *p < 0.05, and **p < 0.01, respectively. Because the field treatments were arranged as engineering-scale plots without independent, replicated treatment plots, no formal multiple-comparison test was conducted to assess desalination effects among treatments. Therefore, the comparison among treatments was mainly based on descriptive statistics and temporal changes in soil salinity and desalination rate.

Results

Influencing factors of soil salinity

The accumulation and change of salt in the soil is the product of specific climate, soil parent material, topography, hydrology, and other natural conditions, and man-made farming, irrigation, and other agricultural activities31,33, which is the reflection of the superposition of various environmental factors34. The climate and groundwater observation data for 3 years of agricultural improvement measures were collected, and the main factors affecting soil salt transport were analyzed (Table 2). Pearson correlation analysis showed that the soil salinity was mainly related to evaporation, irrigation amount, and groundwater depth. Soil salinity was significantly negatively correlated with irrigation amount and groundwater depth, and significantly positively correlated with monthly evaporation. The correlation coefficient with irrigation amount is −0.871, and monthly evaporation is significantly positively correlated, indicating that local soil salinity is strongly affected by artificial irrigation and groundwater level.

The correlation between groundwater depth and monthly precipitation is not significant because precipitation in the test area is low. The average temperature of the month with the highest precipitation is also higher, and monthly evaporation is high, which has little effect on groundwater depth. Due to the relatively low terrain of the test area, a large number of high-lying flood-irrigated farmlands have runoff and deep leakage during the irrigation season, resulting in a significant negative correlation between groundwater depth and irrigation volume. There was a significant positive correlation between monthly average temperature and evaporation, but the correlation between soil salt content and average temperature was not significant. The main reason of irrigation was more frequent in the month with higher temperatures, and irrigation during the growth period had a greater impact on soil salt content.

Analysis of soil salt content changes under different improvement measures

As shown in Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7, the changes in soil salt content under different improvement measures were found. The soil salt content in the T1, T2, T3, and T4 treatments decreased gradually from 2023 to 2025. The annual average salt content in the 0–80 cm soil layer was 22.67, 17.23, and 15.37 g/kg (T1), 19.96, 11.88, and 9.73 g/kg (T2), 20.04, 12.49, and 8.7 g/kg (T3), 19.47, 14.63, and 11.79 g/kg (T4), respectively. The salt content of the control, 0–80 cm soil layer in the normal area was 23.83, 21.83, and 20.56 g/kg. In 2023, soil salt content decreased slightly during the early growth stage and increased slightly during the later growth stage. On the one hand, because of the shallow depth of groundwater, high salinity and large evaporation, the deep soil salt was strongly evaporated by the surface and moved up to the plough layer with water35,36; on the other hand, it is due to the high background value of soil salinity in the first year of reclamation farmland, poor physical and chemical properties, poor permeability, unobvious leaching effect, and many surface water accumulation phenomena in the process of dripping. The salt content of 0–20 cm in the surface layer of T1 treatment changed greatly, and the salt content of > 40–80 cm changed more gently.

With the decrease in groundwater depth in June, the salt content in the 0–20 and 60–80 cm layers increased. The high salt content in the 0–20 cm layer is a typical form of the original distribution of soil salt in areas where evaporation exceeds rainfall37. The high salt content in the bottom layer is mainly due to high salinity and shallow groundwater depth38. The salt content in the > 20–40 cm soil layer does not increase, perhaps because the bottom-layer salt has not moved up into the soil layer during sampling. The salt content of the 0–40 cm soil in the surface layer of the T2 treatment was low and showed a clear decreasing trend throughout the growth period. The salt was mainly concentrated in the 40–80 cm soil layer. Different from the T1 treatment, the shallow groundwater depth in June increased the salt content in the bottom layer. The salt content in the 0–40 cm soil layer remained reduced, indicating that the use of root-zone salt separation measures can effectively block the connection between deep soil water and salt and the plough layer39.

The upper-layer salt is leached into the bottom layer with irrigation water, and the bottom-layer salt is partially discharged below the isolation layer. The trend in salt content in the T3 treatment was similar to that in the T2 treatment. The salt content in the 60–80 cm soil layer was the highest, and soil salt was clearly accumulated at the bottom. The soil layer above the subsurface pipe could reduce soil salt content through drainage, but the soil salt content near the subsurface pipe was higher40. The decrease in salt content in the 0–40 cm soil layer of the T4 treatment was more pronounced than other treatments. The highest difference was that the salt content in the 0–40 cm soil layer decreased continuously, while that in the 40–80 cm soil layer increased continuously, which may be due to the poor water and air permeability of newly reclaimed farmland soil. It was difficult to apply chemical modifiers to deep soil with water; this may be related to the acid-regulating and ion-complexing effect of the chemical conditioner in the shallow wetted zone. Under drip irrigation, the conditioner may promote ion exchange, regulate the soil solution environment, and enhance leaching of soluble salts from the 0–40 cm soil layer37. The soil salt content in 2024 is significantly lower than 2023. On the one hand, the groundwater level is low, and the high-salinity groundwater has little effect on the topsoil’s salt content. On the other hand, the farmland tillage measures increase the content of organic matter and various nutrients, improve the soil structure and physical and chemical properties. Low irrigation amount and high-frequency drip irrigation under film can effectively leach soil salts into deeper soil layers41.

The salt content of the T1 treatment showed decreasing, increasing, and decreasing trend, and the salt content of each soil layer decreased to varying degrees; the bottom layer (40–80 cm) decreased more slowly. The salt content of the 0–80 cm soil layer in T2 treatment decreased rapidly from April to September. The salt content of the 0–20 cm soil layer was higher than other soil layers, which was quite different from the characteristics of salt oligomerization in 2011, indicating that the salt separation measures in the root zone effectively blocked the soil capillary in this year. The soil salt in the topsoil was only leached downward without upward recharge from the bottom layer. The evapotranspiration from farmland drove soil salt to accumulate with water in the upper soil; the soil salt content during the growth period of the T3 treatment also decreased greatly. On August 23, the soil salt content in each layer increased, which may be due to the high temperature and long-term non-irrigation at this time, resulting in salt upward42.

After irrigation on August 25, the soil salt was underwater, and the highest salt content in the > 60–80 cm soil layer indicated that salt accumulation zone was formed near the buried depth of the subsurface pipe. The 0–20 cm and > 20–40 cm soil layers of T4 treatment decreased from 18.1 to 15.58 g/kg in early April to 7.22 and 6.77 g/kg in September, respectively, with the most obvious decrease in the surface layer. The 0–40 cm soil layer showed a trend of decrease-increase-decrease, while the > 40–80 cm soil layer showed a trend of increase-slow decrease. It may be that the implementation of agricultural improvement measures has gradually improved soil structure, promoted the formation of aggregate structure, increased soil porosity and permeability43, and increased the application of chemical modifiers in the soil. It can gradually act on deep soil by replacing and complexing harmful ions in soil44.

In 2024, the changes in salt content resulting from root-zone salt separation and subsurface pipe salt-discharge measures are largely stable. The original natural salt balance is broken, and it tends toward a new balance. Because irrigation water itself contains salt and evaporation always occurs, soil salt in the field cannot be completely washed away. Under frequent irrigation and continuous evaporation, the soil salt content is eventually stabilized at a lower level and fluctuates slightly. The salt content of agricultural improvement showed a slight reduction in fluctuation, and the salt content of chemical improvement in the 0–60 cm soil layer showed a decreasing trend this year. The test results showed that soil salt content can be reduced over the years of improvement, but the soil salt state remains unstable due to groundwater conditions, and the topsoil is at risk of salt return (Fig. 3a).

From 2023 to 2025, the salt content in the natural control area changed slightly, and it remained relatively stable across layers. The salt content in the surface and bottom layers of the soil profile was higher, and the salt content in the 0–20 cm soil layer was optimum. The soil salt mass fractions over the three years were 25.09, 23.58, and 20.81 g/kg, respectively. This is directly related to the high average temperature and the lower rainfall in the study area from March to September, indicating a strong surface accumulation characteristic. The salt content of the bottom soil is mainly affected by groundwater conditions. Overall, different treatments showed different vertical salt redistribution patterns. T1 showed relatively high salinity in the surface and bottom layers, indicating that agricultural improvement alone could not fully prevent salt accumulation in shallow groundwater under strong evaporation. T2 reduced soil salinity in the 0–40 cm layer, while part of the salt tended to move toward the > 40–80 cm layer or below the salt isolation layer. T3 showed a relatively clear salt accumulation zone near the subsurface pipe depth, especially in the > 60–80 cm layer. T4 mainly reduced salinity in the 0–40 cm layer, whereas its effect in the > 40–80 cm layer was limited. These results indicate that the improvement measures changed not only the total salt content but also the vertical redistribution pattern of soil salinity.

Effects of various measures on soil desalination rate

The desalination effects of different treatments varied greatly across soil layers and years (Table 3). In 2023, the salt content of the T2, T3, and T4 treatments in the 0–20 cm soil layer decreased, and the T2 treatment achieved the highest desalination rate of 21.42%, slightly higher than T3 and T4. The differences between T3 and T4 treatments were 16.45 and 16.24%, respectively. The desalination rate for the T1 treatment was − 4.96%, indicating surface salt accumulation. The desalination rate of T2 treatment in the > 20–40 cm soil layer was still higher than other treatments. The desalination rate for the T1 treatment was 9.29%, and the salt content decreased. The desalination rate of T3 and T4 treatments in the > 20–40 cm soil layer was lower than 0–20 cm soil layer. The desalination rate of T1 treatment in the > 40–80 cm soil layer was stable at about 9%, whereas T2 treatment was significantly lower than 0–40 cm layer. The salt content of the T3 and T4 treatments in the > 60–80 cm soil layer increased, and the desalination rates were − 1.71 and − 18%, respectively.

In 2024, desalination effects in each soil layer were significantly better than 2023. The desalination rates of T2 and T3 in the 0–20 cm soil layer reached 68.82 and 72.07%, respectively, which were higher than T1 and T4, but the difference between the two treatments was relatively small. The desalination rate of > 20–40 cm soil layer was T2 > T4 > T3 > T1, and the salt reduction in this layer was slightly lower than 0–20 cm soil layer. T2, T3, and T4 treatments still showed better desalination effects in the 40–60 cm soil layer, with desalination rates > 40%, while the T1 treatment showed a 11.86% decrease in desalination rate. The desalination rate for each treatment in the > 60–80 cm soil layer was lower than that in the upper soil layer. The average desalination rate of the 0–80 cm soil layer was highest in the T2 treatment (64.14%), higher than other treatments, and no obvious difference was observed between T1 and T4. In 2025, the desalination rate per treatment was lower than previous year, and the upper soil still had a higher desalination rate than the bottom soil. This decrease did not necessarily indicate that the improvement measures failed. It was partly related to the lower initial salt content after two years of improvement and the gradual stabilization of soil salinity at lower level.

The desalination rates of T4 treatment in the 0–20 and 20–40 cm soil layers were 21.63 and 16.21%, respectively, which were higher than other treatments, mainly because the initial salt content was lower in T2 and T3 in 2024. By comparing the salt content at the end of the growth period in 2023 with the initial salt content in 2024, the desalination rates for the T2 and T3 treatments in each soil layer exceeded 53%. The desalination rates at 0–80 cm were 61.33 and 59.37%, respectively, which were better than other treatments. The difference in desalination rate between the two treatments was relatively small, indicating that root-zone salt separation and subsurface pipe salt discharge, combined with agricultural improvement measures based on drainage, fertilization, and soil improvement are effective. The combination of water conservancy measures and agricultural improvement can quickly and effectively control soil salinization42. The desalination rate of the 0–40 cm soil layer was 55.32%, and the desalination rate of the > 40–80 cm layer was 26.21%. The desalination effect of the upper soil was better than lower soil. The desalination rate of T1 in the 0–40 cm soil layer was 45.42 lower than other treatments, and the desalination rate in > 40–80 cm soil layer was 37.14% higher than T4 treatment. It also showed that desalination in the upper soil was better than lower soil.

In summary, from the perspective of desalination rate, the treatments combining agricultural measures with root-zone salt isolation, subsurface pipe drainage, or chemical amendment showed better desalination performance than agricultural measures alone under the field conditions of this study. Among them, root-zone salt isolation and subsurface pipe drainage showed relatively better performance in the 0–80 cm soil profile, with desalination rates of 61.33 and 59.37%, respectively. However, this comparison was mainly based on soil salinity reduction and desalination rate. Engineering cost, construction difficulty, maintenance requirements, and long-term operational stability were not included in the quantitative evaluation. After 3 years of improvement testing, the soil in the root zone salt separation and subsurface pipe salt discharge test area changed from severe salinization to moderate salinization (< 10 g/kg), and the soil salinity in the agricultural improvement and chemical improvement areas was also effectively reduced. The difference in desalination rate between years may be due to the agricultural improvement measures in the first year not producing a significant improvement in the physical and chemical properties of the salinized soil, and to poor soil aeration and water permeability, resulting in unsatisfactory desalination effect in the first year. On the other hand, it also shows that agricultural improvement measures can effectively increase land productivity, reduce soil salinization and alkalinization obstacles, promote crop growth, and lay a solid physical and chemical foundation for other improvement measures to exert their effects.

Discussion

Saline soil forms under specific natural conditions, such as climate, topography, soil, and hydrogeology. Human activities have a significant impact on the occurrence and development of soil salinization27. The delta oasis of the Weigan-Kuqa River in Xinjiang, and thought that the influencing factors of soil salinization were multi-factor coexistence, and the influence of human activities was the key factor. Some scholars believe that the salt content of saline soil is mainly affected by the groundwater level27,28,32, salinity, soil nutrient status32,45 and artificial tillage20. On the regional scale, topographic factors also influence the spatial distribution of soil salinity. In this study, the climate in the experimental area is dry, with less rainfall, evapotranspiration in farmland is high, the terrain is low-lying, groundwater depth is shallow, and higher salinity. Regional unreasonable irrigation results from the combined action of typical natural factors and human factors. Soil salinity is significantly correlated with irrigation volume, groundwater depth, and farmland evapotranspiration, consistent with previous research36,46. Different saline-alkali soil improvement measures directly affect the soil profile salt distribution characteristics. Horton et al.37 showed that the soil salt content of 0–20, > 20–40, > 40–60, > 60–80, > 80–100 cm soil layer decreased by 90.51, 82.86, 65.73, 39.23, and 15.94%, respectively. It showed that the effect of soil desalination decreased significantly with increasing soil depth. In this study, the desalination rates of T3 treatment at 0–20, 20–40, 40–60, and 60–80 cm were found 69.24, 55.8, 58.6, and 53.83%, respectively, from 2023 to 2025.

The surface desalination effect was the best, which was consistent with other research results. However, the desalination rate of > 20–80 cm in this study was also > 50%, which may be due to improvements in soil physical and chemical properties after 3 years of treatment. Irrigation during the growth period caused deep leaching of salt in the 0–80 cm soil layer, and the salt was discharged through the dark tube or into deeper soil. The application of chemical amendments in saline-alkali soil has the greatest effect on improving the upper soil, consistent with the conclusion of this study. The root zone salt barrier blocks the hydraulic connection between the upper and lower soil layers, which makes the soil moisture stagnate when it runs to the lower interface of the isolation layer, so that the accumulation of the upper soil of the salt barrier layer is reduced, which can slow down the salt accumulation to a certain extent40. In this study, the main reason for the increase in salt content in the cultivated layer of the root zone salt barrier may be that the root zone salt barrier layer was set at > 80–100 cm in the experiment. The decline in desalination rate in the third year and the resalinization in some soil layers can be explained by the combined effects of the decreasing initial salt load, irrigation regime, evaporation, and groundwater dynamics.

After 2 years of improvement, the salt content in the cultivated layer had already decreased significantly, so the remaining available salt for leaching was lower in the third year. As a result, the calculated desalination rate decreased, even though the soil salinity remained relatively low. In addition, the irrigation water itself contained specific amount of salt, and strong evaporation persisted during the growth period. When the irrigation interval was long under high-temperature conditions, upward water movement driven by evaporation could bring salts from lower soil layers or shallow groundwater back into the upper soil layer, causing local resalinization. Deep salt accumulation was also related to the structural characteristics of the improvement measures. In the root-zone salt-isolation treatment, the isolation layer blocked upward capillary movement of groundwater but also promoted downward redistribution of salts toward the lower part of the cultivated layer or below the isolation layer. In the subsurface pipe drainage treatment, drip irrigation leached salts downward, and the drainage pipe created a preferential zone for water-salt convergence near the burial depth. Therefore, higher salinity was observed in the 60–80 cm layer before part of the salt was discharged through drainage system. These phenomena indicate that the improvement measures did not simply reduce total soil salt, but changed the vertical migration and redistribution pattern of soil water and salt under field conditions. Because some nearby farmland still uses flood irrigation, groundwater depth becomes shallower12,41,47.

In addition, the error in the construction of the root zone salt barrier project causes the groundwater to contact the bottom of the cultivated layer, and the salt content of the cultivated layer increases in some months due to the groundwater depth < 0.8 m. In the experiment, agricultural improvement measures, root-zone salt separation treatment, subsurface pipe salt discharge treatment, and chemical improvement treatment can effectively improve severely salinized soil26,45,46. In saline-alkali wasteland and heavy saline-alkali land, leveling land, planting green manure, and returning straw to the field can assist farmland projects to reduce soil salinity faster, not only increase soil fertility, improve soil structure, increase crop yield, but also play the benefits of soil improvement by farmland water conservancy projects, which is consistent with the results of this study42. The effect of agricultural improvement combined with other improvement measures on saline soil improvement is better than single agricultural improvement measures. In the actual application process, root-zone salt separation or subsurface pipe salt-discharge measures can be selected based on soil salt levels and production needs. Before the implementation of improvement, the focus is on agricultural improvement measures, such as deep tillage48,49, film mulching, straw returning43, and application of organic fertilizer44 to improve soil structure and physical and chemical properties, and pay full attention to the effect of salt separation or subsurface pipe salt discharge in the root zone, and quickly and lastingly reduce the salinity of severely salinized soil.

For crops with shallow roots, agricultural improvement combined with chemical improvement measures is also an optional approach that can yield better improvement effects in shallow soil. This paper compares only the desalination effects of various improvement measures for salinized soil and does not analyze their economic benefits. Based on the data on desalination rates, the farmland water conservancy project, combined with agricultural improvement measures, remains an effective method for treating severely salinized soil in Xinjiang. However, this study still has some limitations. First, because the improvement measures were implemented at the field-engineering scale, independent replicated plots and a randomized block design were not established for each treatment. Therefore, the comparison among treatments may be affected by terrain conditions, groundwater depth, and the spatial heterogeneity of initial soil salinity. Second, the analysis of influencing factors was mainly based on field monitoring data, correlation analysis, and simplified multivariate analysis, which cannot fully quantify the independent contribution and causal pathway of each factor. Third, the engineering promotion value of different measures was evaluated primarily based on desalination rate, while construction cost, maintenance cost, crop yield, service life, and input-output benefits were not quantitatively analyzed. Fourth, the chemical improver used in this study was a commercial conditioner, and its detailed composition and long-term environmental effects were not fully evaluated. Therefore, future studies should further verify these findings using replicated field trials, larger datasets, long-term monitoring, chemical characterization of amendments, environmental risk assessment, and economic benefit analysis.

Conclusion

In this study, on the severely salinized soil in the same area, the comprehensive application of agricultural improvement measures and root zone salt separation, subsurface pipe salt discharge, and chemical improvement based on agricultural improvement measures were used to compare and analyze the changes of soil salt content in each improvement measure upto 3 years. Soil salinity was significantly correlated with irrigation amount, groundwater depth, and farmland evapotranspiration. Especially, irrigation during the growth period had the greatest impact on soil salinity, with a correlation coefficient of −0.871. Groundwater depth was closely correlated with changes in soil salinity. Irrigation amount showed the strongest negative correlation with soil salinity. Shallow groundwater, high groundwater salinity, and strong evaporation increased the risk of salt return in the test area. Different improvement measures changed the vertical redistribution pattern of soil salinity. Agricultural improvement alone reduced soil salinity to some extent, but the surface and bottom soil layers still showed a relatively high risk of salt accumulation. Root-zone salt isolation reduced salinity in the cultivated layer and promoted downward redistribution of salt. Subsurface pipe drainage formed a relatively clear salt accumulation zone near the pipe depth, especially in the > 60–80 cm layer. Chemical improvement mainly reduced salinity in the 0–40 cm soil layer, while its effect in the deeper soil layer was relatively limited.

From the perspective of desalination rate, root-zone salt isolation and subsurface pipe drainage showed better desalination performance in the 0–80 cm soil profile. The desalination rates for the two treatments were above 50% from 2023 to 2025, and the difference between them was relatively limited. However, these result findings mainly reflect soil salinity reduction under the field conditions of the present study. The engineering promotion of these measures should also consider construction costs, construction difficulty, maintenance requirements, drainage stability, crop yield response, and long-term economic returns.
