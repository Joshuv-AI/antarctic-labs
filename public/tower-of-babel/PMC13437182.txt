Novel electrospun drug delivery systems for periodontal and oral mucosal therapy: a systematic review
Authors: Behrad Yadolahi, Mehdi Taghian, Leyli Sadri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Advanced polymeric drug delivery systems, particularly electrospun nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches, are promising platforms for the local treatment of periodontal and oral mucosal diseases. However, evidence regarding fabrication reproducibility, mucosal interactions, therapeutic efficacy, and stability has not been systematically synthesized. This systematic review evaluated novel polymeric carriers for dental and oral applications, with emphasis on fabrication parameters, drug loading, mucoadhesion, release behavior, immunomodulatory effects, clinical outcomes, and safety.Following PRISMA guidelines and the PICO framework, two independent reviewers searched PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar for English-language studies published between 2015 and 2025. Eligible studies investigated novel polymeric carriers, primarily electrospun or composite systems, and reported fabrication parameters, including polymer type, preparation method, and drug loading, along with relevant biological or clinical outcomes. Risk of bias was assessed using SYRCLE/OHAT tools for original studies and AMSTAR-2 for review articles.Eight studies (four original research studies and four review articles) met the inclusion criteria. The included studies evaluated polymers, such as PLA/nano-HAP, chitosan/PVA/PCL, and PLLA/PEO, in combination with active agents including doxycycline, neem extract, curcumin, diclofenac sodium, and binahong extract. Electrospinning consistently generated porous, uniform carriers with fiber diameters of approximately 250–500 nm, sustained release ranging from 82% over 8 h to 60–80% over 7 days, mucoadhesive strength up to 0.34 N, immunomodulatory effects reflected by reduced IL-1, TNF-α, and MMP-8 levels, and antibacterial efficacy up to 98%. These systems also maintained mucosa-compatible pH values (6.5–7.0) and showed no evidence of irritation or toxicity.Needleless electrospinning variants further improved scalability. However, heterogeneity in fabrication parameters and moderate overall risk of bias, mainly from review-based evidence, limited direct comparisons across studies. Overall, electrospun nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches demonstrated promising potential as biocompatible platforms for targeted local drug delivery in dentistry, with the capacity to enhance immunomodulatory and antimicrobial effects in periodontitis, aphthous stomatitis, and oral ulcers. Further studies incorporating standardized fabrication validation and long-term stability testing are recommended to support clinical translation.

INTRODUCTION

Oral mucosal drug administration is an attractive, minimally invasive route. However, efficient absorption is often restricted by physiological barriers, including continuous salivary flow and limited mucosal permeability. Mucoadhesive delivery systems that adhere to oral surfaces can enhance drug uptake by sustaining high local concentrations over prolonged periods [1]. Polymers are central to contemporary drug delivery design because they shape formulation properties, delivery performance, and pharmaceutical refinement [2]. As carrier frameworks, biopolymers can support precise and sustained release of therapeutic molecules at the administration site and can be formulated as hydrogels, mucoadhesive films, and transmucosal patches [3].

Mucoadhesive polymers adhere firmly to the oral mucosal surface. When active pharmaceutical agents are incorporated into these systems, drugs can be delivered in a controlled manner as the mucoadhesive matrix gradually degrades. These polymers are commonly classified as natural or synthetic materials [4].

Electrospinning is an efficient, low-cost, and scalable method for producing polymer-based fibers. In this technique, a high electrical voltage is applied to a polymer solution to generate a charged filament that is propelled toward a collecting surface. As the filament travels, the solvent gradually evaporates, producing solid nanofibers that deposit on a metallic collector. Because of their high surface-to-volume ratio, porosity, and drug-loading capacity, electrospun fibers are promising candidates for next-generation drug delivery. The method enables the simultaneous encapsulation of multiple therapeutic agents within a single fiber structure, providing a flexible approach to controlled, site-specific drug release [5]. Electrospun nanofibers have structural features that closely resemble the natural extracellular matrix (ECM), which further supports their use in biomedical applications [6]. Periodontal and oral mucosal diseases substantially impair oral health and quality of life. Conventional therapies, including systemic antibiotics and mechanical debridement, are often limited by insufficient local drug concentration, systemic adverse effects, and poor patient compliance [6]. Therefore, targeted local drug delivery systems that provide sustained release and enhanced mucosal retention have attracted increasing interest.

Despite growing interest in electrospun and mucoadhesive drug delivery systems for oral applications, several challenges remain unresolved. Fabrication protocols, polymer compositions, electrospinning parameters, and characterization methods vary considerably across studies, limiting reproducibility and direct comparison. In addition, most evidence remains preclinical, and clinical translation is constrained by limited long-term stability data, scalability concerns, and the absence of standardized regulatory evaluation. A comprehensive synthesis of recent evidence on electrospun drug delivery systems for periodontal and oral mucosal therapy is therefore needed. Previous reviews have largely addressed general nanofibers or mucoadhesive applications without systematically integrating fabrication parameters, polymeric composition, and therapeutic outcomes specific to periodontal and oral mucosal therapy.

Accordingly, this systematic review critically evaluates current research on electrospun mucoadhesive systems for oral administration, focusing on formulation strategies, drug release behavior, and therapeutic performance. The review also identifies key findings, remaining knowledge gaps, and future directions for developing effective targeted drug delivery platforms.

METHODS

1. Protocol and registration

This review was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The protocol was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261325672). The search strategy was structured according to the PICO framework, as follows:

• Population (P): Patients or experimental models affected by periodontal diseases or oral mucosal pathologies, including periodontitis, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, oral ulcers, and mucosal inflammatory conditions;• Intervention (I): Novel drug delivery systems intended for local dental application, including mucoadhesive patches, electrospun nanofibers, polymeric films, and composite carrier matrices loaded with therapeutic agents such as doxycycline, curcumin, diclofenac sodium, neem extract, or other bioactive compounds;• Comparison (C): Conventional therapeutic approaches, such as scaling and root planing or systemic antibiotic administration, and alternative drug delivery systems fabricated using methods other than those under investigation, including solvent casting, freeze-drying, hot-melt extrusion, or three-dimensional printing;• Outcome (O): Therapeutic efficacy and performance outcomes, including immunomodulatory effects (e.g., cytokine modulation involving interleukin-1, interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and matrix metalloproteinase-8), clinical improvements (e.g., reduced tooth mobility, lower gingival index scores, and enhanced wound healing), drug release characteristics (e.g., sustained or controlled release and bioavailability), antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, and safety outcomes (e.g., cytotoxicity, mucoadhesive strength, and absence of tissue irritation).

Equivalent keyword combinations and database-specific adaptations were used for Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar. Reference lists of relevant articles were also manually searched to identify potentially eligible studies not retrieved through electronic database searches.

Studies were eligible if they investigated novel drug delivery systems specifically developed for local therapeutic applications in periodontal or oral mucosal diseases. Interventions included polymer-based systems formulated with polylactic acid, nano-hydroxyapatite, chitosan, polyvinyl alcohol, polycaprolactone, or composite matrices and incorporating active pharmaceutical ingredients or bioactive compounds. Comparators included standard non-surgical periodontal therapies, placebo formulations, or drug delivery systems prepared using alternative fabrication techniques.

To identify relevant literature on novel drug delivery systems for periodontal and oral mucosal applications, two independent reviewers systematically searched English-language publications from 2015 to 2025 in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. Search terms were developed and refined using Medical Subject Headings where applicable and included “novel drug delivery systems,” “periodontal diseases,” “oral mucosal diseases,” “electrospinning,” “mucoadhesive patch,” “nanofibers,” “solvent casting,” “polymeric carriers,” and “immunomodulatory effects.” Supplementary searches in Google Scholar used the same predefined terms (Fig. 2).

2. Eligibility criteria

This review included English-language original research and review articles, including experimental studies, in vivo investigations, comparative fabrication analyses, and comprehensive overviews, focused on the development and application of novel drug delivery systems for periodontal or oral mucosal diseases. To align with the PICO framework, priority was given to studies describing advanced polymeric carriers (e.g., electrospun nanofibers, mucoadhesive patches, buccal films, or composite matrices) for local therapeutic delivery that reported fabrication techniques (e.g., electrospinning parameters, solvent casting conditions, and crosslinking methods), polymer compositions, drug loading strategies, and performance outcomes in preclinical or review contexts.

Exclusion criteria were non-original publications without primary data (e.g., editorials or commentaries), studies focused mainly on conventional systemic delivery or non-polymeric systems, applications unrelated to oral or periodontal conditions (e.g., non-dental transmucosal routes), in vivo clinical trials without adequate methodological descriptions of carrier preparation, non-English articles, and studies with inaccessible full text or insufficient reporting of novel delivery system characteristics and outcomes.

All retrieved records were imported into Mendeley Desktop (version 1.19.8) [computer software] for duplicate removal. After deduplication, two reviewers independently screened the remaining articles against the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Eligible studies underwent detailed data extraction, and the collected information was systematically organized in Microsoft Excel. Disagreements between the two reviewers during study selection or data extraction were resolved through discussion and consensus. When necessary, a third reviewer was consulted for final agreement.

Extracted variables included authors and publication year, polymer types and concentrations, solvents and plasticizers used, casting and drying conditions, key film characteristics, incorporated active substances, and primary reported outcomes (Table 1).

Review articles were included to provide broader thematic context, summarize emerging fabrication strategies, and support narrative interpretation of current trends in oral drug delivery. Original experimental studies constituted the primary source for quantitative therapeutic and fabrication-related outcomes.

3. Data synthesis

Two independent reviewers performed screening and data extraction. Disagreements during title/abstract screening, full-text assessment, or data extraction were resolved through discussion and consensus; when necessary, a third reviewer was consulted to achieve final agreement. Formal inter-reviewer agreement statistics, such as Cohen’s kappa, were not prospectively calculated. Because the included studies differed substantially in design, fabrication techniques, polymer composition, therapeutic agents, and outcome measures, a narrative synthesis was performed. The evidence comprised four original experimental studies and four review articles published between 2015 and 2025.

Studies were categorized by fabrication strategy, polymeric carrier system, drug release characteristics, mucoadhesive properties, antimicrobial or immunomodulatory effects, and therapeutic application in periodontal and oral mucosal diseases. Interventions primarily involved electrospinning (including needleless electrospinning), solvent casting, and multilayer composite assembly using polymeric systems such as polylactic acid (PLA)/nano- hydroxyapatite (HAP), chitosan/polyvinyl alcohol (PVA)/polycaprolactone (PCL), poly(L-lactic acid) (PLLA)/poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO), PLGA, and other natural or synthetic polymers.

Extracted data included fabrication parameters, polymer composition, incorporated therapeutic agents, release behavior, mucoadhesive performance, antimicrobial activity, immunomodulatory outcomes, biocompatibility, and reported therapeutic efficacy. Because of marked methodological and clinical heterogeneity across studies, a quantitative meta-analysis was not performed.

4. Risk of bias assessment

The risk of bias across the four included original research studies was evaluated using a modified version of the SYRCLE's RoB tool for animal studies, where applicable, and the OHAT Risk of Bias Rating Tool for Human Health Assessments, adapted for preclinical and comparative fabrication studies. Key domains included selection, performance, detection, attrition, and reporting bias, as well as other sources of bias such as fabrication rigor and characterization quality. SYRCLE’s risk-of-bias tool was applied primarily to animal-based experimental components. For fabrication-oriented methodological studies, the assessment focused on reporting transparency, reproducibility of fabrication procedures, and completeness of outcome reporting.

The studies of Andrei et al. [7] and Wei et al. [8] were evaluated as having a low risk of bias, as supported by well-defined experimental protocols, comprehensive statistical analyses (e.g., one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc tests, P < 0.05), validated characterization techniques (SEM, ELISA, and HPLC), and complete outcomes without selective emphasis. No significant deviations from the intended interventions were observed. The study of Rohani Shirvan et al. [9] was rated as having some concerns, primarily because of limited blinding in antibacterial assays and incomplete reporting of electrospinning jet stability, which may introduce performance and detection bias in release kinetics and mucoadhesion outcomes, despite appropriate statistical testing (ANOVA/one-way ANOVA with Tukey, P < 0.05) for mechanical and swelling properties.

The review-based evidence was assessed separately using AMSTAR-2 criteria for systematic or narrative reviews and was categorized as having moderate risk because of the absence of formal risk-of-bias assessment in included primary studies, inconsistent reporting of inclusion and exclusion criteria, and potential publication bias arising from non-systematic searches (e.g., lack of a PRISMA flowchart in some reviews) [10111213]. Although Saputri et al. [14] was a PRISMA-based review, it raised some concerns because of limited database coverage and the absence of quantitative synthesis.

Across the included studies, attrition bias (complete datasets) and reporting bias (predefined outcomes fully reported) were generally low. However, insufficient blinding in preclinical evaluations (e.g., histological scoring) and variability in statistical reporting across three original studies contributed to an overall moderate level of methodological risk.

Accordingly, the collective risk of bias across the included evidence was rated as moderate, with no study considered at high risk. The synthesized results should therefore be interpreted with appropriate caution. Sensitivity analyses prioritizing low-risk original studies (e.g., Andrei et al. [7] and Wei et al. [8]) are recommended to assess the robustness of conclusions regarding the efficacy and safety of novel polymeric carriers in periodontal and oral mucosal applications. Future studies should incorporate randomized designs where feasible, standardized blinding procedures, comprehensive statistical reporting (including effect sizes and confidence intervals), and adherence to PRISMA guidance for reviews to improve methodological transparency and minimize potential bias (Table 2 and 3).

RESULTS

1. Characteristics of included studies

This systematic review synthesized evidence from eight articles (four original research studies and four review articles) published between 2015 and 2025 that evaluated novel polymeric drug delivery systems for periodontal and oral mucosal diseases. Three studies focused specifically on periodontal delivery systems, primarily for periodontitis, whereas four examined mucoadhesive patches or buccal films for broader oral mucosal applications; one study addressed both areas. The studies evaluated fabrication techniques, carrier uniformity, mucoadhesive behavior, release profiles, immunomodulatory effects, and therapeutic performance. Collectively, more than 20 formulations and multiple review-based examples were assessed across in vitro, ex vivo, preclinical animal, and narrative contexts, covering applications such as periodontitis management, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, mouth ulcer healing, oral candidiasis, mucositis, and general topical oral therapy. Research settings spanned multiple countries, including Romania, Iran, Brazil, Poland, and China.

2. Fabrication strategies and polymeric carrier systems

Across the interventions, advanced polymeric carriers—predominantly fabricated via electrospinning (including needleless variants) or solvent-cast systems—served as the central delivery platforms. The polymers included PLA/nano-HAP, chitosan/PVA/PCL, PLLA/PEO, and diverse natural or synthetic blends such as hyaluronic acid, cellulose derivatives, gums, and PLGA. Crosslinking agents (e.g., genipin and glutaraldehyde) and backing layers (e.g., PCL or HPMC films) were incorporated to enhance flexibility and mucoadhesion. Active substances included doxycycline, neem extract, curcumin/diclofenac sodium, binahong leaf extract, and broader anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, or antifungal agents described in review articles. Fabrication parameters ranged from electrospinning voltages of 18–50 kV and drying at 40℃ for cast components to room-temperature assembly, typically yielding nanofibrous meshes or films with thicknesses of 0.4–0.5 mm and porous or solid structures.

3. Drug release, mucoadhesion, and therapeutic performance

Overall, the systems produced highly porous electrospun carriers or uniform cast films with strong mucoadhesion and controlled release profiles. Andrei et al. [7] demonstrated significant reductions in tooth mobility (from 1.76 to 0.65) and gingival index (from 1.76 to 0.43), alongside decreased salivary MMP-8 (to 2.55 ng/mL) and plasma IL-1/TNF-α levels (P < 0.05 by ANOVA/Tukey), indicating potent immunomodulatory effects in a rat periodontitis model. Rohani Shirvan et al. [9] reported 82% neem release over 8 h (Weibull model fit), higher mucoadhesive strength (35 g vs. 18 g), and 98% S. mutans reduction with electrospun films compared with solvent-cast counterparts (P < 0.05 by ANOVA). Wei et al. [8] described multilayer patches with sustained curcumin release (60–80% over 7 days), rapid diclofenac delivery (~90% in 48 h), antibacterial inhibition zones of 15–20 mm, and anti-inflammatory cytokine suppression (P < 0.05 by ANOVA). Saputri et al. [14] highlighted binahong extract patches as effective anti-inflammatory alternatives for recurrent aphthous stomatitis (RAS). Review articles [10111213] consistently emphasized nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches for targeted delivery in periodontitis, mucositis, candidiasis, and ulcers, with improved bioavailability, prolonged local retention (up to 18 days of sustained release in reported examples), and reduced systemic exposure.

4. Biocompatibility and safety outcomes

No systemic adverse events or significant cytotoxicity (>90% cell viability) were reported. Local complications were minimal, and film or carrier pH values remained within a mucosa-compatible range (6.5–7.0), with no evidence of irritation. These approaches appeared particularly promising for sustained gingival and buccal delivery and immunomodulation, with electrospun PLA/nano-HAP and chitosan/PVA composites exhibiting superior mechanical, adhesive, and antimicrobial characteristics.

5. Heterogeneity and limitations across studies

Despite generally favorable outcomes, substantial heterogeneity in polymer ratios, fabrication parameters (e.g., electrospinning voltage/flow rate and crosslinking agents), and characterization techniques (e.g., tensile strength, 3.2–5.6 MPa; swelling, 200–350%) limited direct comparability across studies. Furthermore, inconsistencies—such as limited blinding in preclinical assays or lack of quantitative meta-synthesis in reviews—restricted the precision of scalability and clinical translation assessment. While the evidence strongly supports advanced polymeric systems, especially electrospun nanofibers, as reproducible, biocompatible, and therapeutically promising methods for local oral and periodontal delivery, the observed variability underscores the need for standardized fabrication validation, systematic biocompatibility testing, and harmonized reporting frameworks to improve industrial applicability and facilitate clinical adoption.

DISCUSSION

This review synthesizes eight articles (four original research studies and four review articles) published between 2015 and 2025 and highlights the consistent promise and superior functional performance of novel polymeric drug delivery systems, particularly electrospun nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches, in the management of periodontal and oral mucosal diseases. Collectively, the evidence reinforces the substantial potential of these advanced carriers for targeted local therapy, improved patient compliance, and reduced systemic exposure.

The findings are broadly consistent with those of previous reports highlighting the advantages of nanofiber-based and mucoadhesive drug delivery systems in improving local bioavailability, prolonged retention, and controlled therapeutic release within the oral cavity. Similar to earlier reviews, electrospinning-based approaches demonstrated enhanced antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory performance compared with conventional local delivery systems. However, the present review further highlights persistent heterogeneity in fabrication parameters and outcome reporting, which continues to limit direct comparability and broader clinical translation.

One central finding is the adaptability of electrospinning, including needleless variants, and related composite fabrication methods across diverse polymer matrices and therapeutic objectives. Andrei et al. [7] and Wei et al. [8] produced low-risk, effective carriers with sustained release and strong mucoadhesion using PLA/nano-HAP and PLLA/PEO blends, features consistent with prolonged anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial activity. Rohani Shirvan et al. [9] further demonstrated superior controlled release (82% over 8 hours, Weibull model) and antibacterial efficacy (98% S. mutans reduction, P < 0.05 by ANOVA) from electrospun chitosan/PVA films compared with solvent-cast counterparts, together with improved mechanical strength and mucoadhesive properties. Review articles [10111213] expanded therapeutic relevance by summarizing applications in periodontitis, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, oral ulcers, candidiasis, and mucositis, with nanofibers and hydrogels showing up to 18 days sustained release and improved bioavailability of agents such as doxycycline, curcumin, and neem extract. Saputri et al. [14] described binahong-based patches as natural alternatives for RAS healing.

The performance of these systems appears to derive from their ability to produce porous, uniform, and flexible carriers through controlled electrospinning parameters (e.g., 18–50 kV voltage) or hybrid assembly. These platforms can preserve active ingredient stability while enabling tunable mucoadhesion through hydrophilic polymers and backing layers (e.g., PCL or HPMC). Fabrication conditions (e.g., drying at 40℃, crosslinking with genipin), together with near-neutral pH values (6.5–7.0), support mucosal tolerance for buccal and gingival applications. However, heterogeneity remains a major challenge because polymer ratios, spinning voltages and flow rates, crosslinking agents, and characterization parameters vary across studies (tensile strength, 3.2–5.6 MPa; swelling, 200–350%). Moreover, some studies lacked quantitative long-term stability data or residual solvent analysis, and review articles often omitted formal risk-of-bias assessments of primary sources.

Overall, the evidence positions electrospun nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches as promising strategies for biocompatible, targeted delivery in dentistry, with demonstrated utility in immunomodulation, antimicrobial therapy, anti-inflammatory treatment, and ulcer healing. Their scalability (e.g., needleless spinning productivity) and multifunctionality may offer advantages over conventional systemic treatments, particularly by reducing cytokine levels (IL-1 and TNF-α) and clinical symptoms. However, issues such as incomplete blinding in preclinical assays and moderate overall risk of bias (primarily from narrative reviews) limit precise estimation of effect sizes and slow broader clinical translation.

This review has several limitations. Study designs (in vitro, ex vivo, preclinical animal models, and narrative or systematic reviews), polymer compositions, fabrication parameters, and measured outcomes (e.g., release kinetics, mucoadhesive force, and cytokine modulation) were highly heterogeneous. Risk of bias ranged from low to moderate: Andrei et al. [7] and Wei et al. [8] were rated as low risk because of rigorous statistical reporting (ANOVA/Tukey, P < 0.05) and objective outcome measures, whereas Rohani Shirvan et al. [9] raised some concerns related to assay blinding. Review articles were generally at moderate risk because they lacked quantitative synthesis and formal bias assessment of primary studies, although the study of Saputri et al. [14] was strengthened by PRISMA adherence. The focus on specific active agents, including doxycycline, neem, and curcumin, also limits generalizability to broader active pharmaceutical ingredients. In addition, the absence of long-term in vivo stability assessments (> 6 months) and large-scale good manufacturing practice validation in most studies limits commercial applicability. Future research should include sensitivity analyses prioritizing low-risk preclinical studies and standardized reporting frameworks that address spinning parameters, crosslinking details, permissible residuals, and required biocompatibility statistics, including effect sizes and confidence intervals, to support clinical translation and industrial adoption.

Although the included studies demonstrated encouraging preclinical outcomes, clinical translation remains limited. Variability in fabrication parameters, lack of standardized evaluation methods, and scarcity of well-designed human clinical trials currently restrict broader applicability. Future investigations should address long-term biocompatibility, reproducibility of fabrication methods, and validation in larger clinical settings. This review is also limited by the absence of prospectively calculated inter-reviewer agreement statistics, such as Cohen’s kappa, during screening and study selection. Although independent reviewers performed study selection and resolved disagreements through discussion and consensus, the lack of quantitative agreement assessment may reduce methodological transparency and reproducibility. Finally, the small number of original experimental studies should be considered when interpreting the findings. Because half of the included articles were narrative reviews, the strength of primary evidence synthesis and the generalizability of conclusions remain limited.

CONCLUSION

This systematic review of eight articles published between 2015 and 2025 indicates that novel polymeric drug delivery systems, particularly electrospun nanofibers and mucoadhesive patches, have promising potential for localized treatment of periodontal and oral mucosal diseases. The included studies reported favorable outcomes regarding controlled and sustained drug release, immunomodulatory and antibacterial effects, mucoadhesive performance, and preclinical therapeutic efficacy in conditions such as periodontitis, recurrent aphthous stomatitis, and oral ulcers. Electrospinning-based systems also exhibited advantages related to multifunctionality and targeted local delivery with reduced systemic exposure.

However, the current evidence remains predominantly preclinical and heterogeneous with respect to fabrication protocols, polymer compositions, and outcome assessment methods. Therefore, further standardization of fabrication techniques, long-term biocompatibility evaluation, and well-designed clinical studies are necessary before these systems can be broadly translated into routine dental practice.
