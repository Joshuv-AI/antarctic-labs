Setting of import tolerance for folpet in bananas and modification of the existing maximum residue level in honey
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant ADAMA Agriculture BV submitted a request to the competent national authority in Austria to set an import tolerance for the active substance folpet in bananas. Within the submitted application the applicant requested as well to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for folpet in honey. The data submitted in support of the requests were found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal for bananas according to the current residue definition for enforcement. For honey however, the submitted trials were not compliant with good agricultural practice (GAP) and therefore not appropriate to derive an MRL proposal. Adequate analytical methods for enforcement, based on liquid and gas chromatography, are available to quantify residues of folpet and phthalimide in bananas and honey with lowest validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg (both compounds). Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of folpet residues expected in bananas and honey is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health. However, uncertainties remain regarding the long‐term exposure assessment due to missing residue data for phthalamic acid (in root crops) and phthalic acid (in all commodities except bananas and honey).Keywords: bananas, consumer risk assessment, folpet, honey, import tolerance, MRL, pesticide

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, ADAMA Agriculture BV submitted an application to the competent national authority in Austria (Rapporteur Member State (RMS)) to set import tolerance for the active substance folpet in bananas. Within the submitted application the applicant requested as well to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for folpet in honey.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 2 October 2024. The appointed RMS Austria assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 12 December 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 31 January 2025 to 21 February 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 23 May 2025. The RMS proposed to establish MRL for bananas imported from Ecuador and El Salvador at the level of 0.9 mg/kg and MRL for honey at the level of 0.7 mg/kg according to the existing residue definition for enforcement (i.e. ‘Folpet (sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet)’). In addition, the RMS derived MRLs in accordance with the new residue definition for enforcement proposed in the framework of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b). Under this residue definition (‘folpet’), MRLs of 0.7 mg/kg for bananas and 0.07 mg/kg for honey were derived. However, the RMS did not support the use of this new residue definition, as folpet rapidly degrades to phthalimide during frozen storage and therefore considered more appropriate to maintain the current residue definition for enforcement.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 20 June 2025 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified a point which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address it. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 30 March 2026, which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, and the additional data provided by the RMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of folpet following foliar application was investigated in crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops, root crops and cereals/grass crops.

Residues in rotational crops are not expected since according to the soil degradation studies evaluated during the peer review conclusion for the renewal of the approval of folpet, the DT90 values of folpet, phthalimide and the soil metabolites phthalic acid and phthalamic acid are all below the trigger value of 100 days.

Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of folpet (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that the active substance completely degrades to phthalimide, predominantly under conditions of pasteurisation and further to phthalic acid under conditions simulating boiling/brewing/baking and sterilisation.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies, hydrolysis studies, the toxicological relevance of metabolites and/or degradation products and the performance of the method of analysis, the following residue definitions were proposed for plant products in the framework of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of the approval of folpet:

Residues in rotational crops are not expected (EFSA, 2023b). Residue definitions for enforcement, risk assessment 1a (fruit crops) and 2 are applicable to honey. The proposed residue definition for enforcement differs from the one currently set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, i.e. ‘Folpet (sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet)’.

EFSA concluded that for the crops assessed in this application, metabolism of folpet in primary crops and the possible degradation in processed products has been sufficiently addressed and that the previously derived residue definitions are applicable.

Sufficiently validated analytical methods, based on high performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS), gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) and gas chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (GC–MS/MS), are available to quantify residues in high‐water content commodities according to the enforcement residue definitions (current and new). The methods enable quantification of residues of folpet and phthalimide at or above the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg (both compounds using HPLC–MS/MS and phthalimide using GC–MS/MS), 0.02 mg/kg (phthalimide, using GC–MS) and 0.05 mg/kg (folpet, using GC–MS) in bananas.

The available residue trials are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.9 mg/kg for bananas according to the existing residue definition for enforcement. No MRL proposal could be derived based on the new residue definition for enforcement proposed in the context of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of the approval of folpet due to an insufficient number of residue trials with samples stored for periods supported by demonstrated storage stability of folpet in bananas.

Specific studies investigating the magnitude of folpet residues in processed commodities are not required, as significant residues are not expected in the edible portion of the raw agricultural commodity (RAC), with levels in the pulp remaining below 0.1 mg/kg.

Residues of folpet in commodities of animal origin were not assessed since the commodities under consideration in this MRL application are normally not fed to livestock.

For honey, sufficiently validated analytical methods, based on LC–MS/MS, are available to quantify residues of folpet and phthalimide in honey at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. EFSA could not derive an MRL proposal in honey, as the selected critical GAP (cGAPs) on wine grapes (northern Europe (NEU)) and ornamental plants (NEU) were not considered to be sufficiently supported by the available semi‐field (tunnel) trials, due to inconsistencies between the application patterns used in the trials compared to those defined in the selected cGAPs. In contrast, the Applicant and the RMS considered the cGAPs to be adequately supported and reiterated an MRL proposal of 0.7 mg/kg based on the existing residue definition for enforcement. Further risk management considerations are required. The available monitoring programme datasets (i.e. dataset 1, including 2705 samples analysed according to the existing residue definition for enforcement in the period from 2017 to 2025; dataset 2, including 4916 samples analysed according to the newly proposed residue definition for enforcement in the period from 2009 to 2025) indicate that honey placed in the EU market complies with the existing MRL (defined for folpet); furthermore, it would also comply with the MRL established under the new residue definition for enforcement.

Since folpet is not fat soluble, investigation of the nature and magnitude of residues in fish is in principle not required according to SANCO/11187/2013. Furthermore, the commodities under consideration in this MRL application are normally not used as fish feed item.

The toxicological profile of folpet was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.1 mg/kg bw per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.6 mg/kg bw. The metabolites phthalimide and phthalamic acid, are of similar toxicity as the parent active substance. The toxicological profile of the metabolite phthalic acid was assessed in the framework of the MRL review on the active substance dithianon and the data were sufficient to derive an ADI of 0.113 mg/kg bw per day and an ARfD of 1.34 mg/kg bw.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). The risk assessment was conducted separately for RA‐RD1 (i.e. ‘folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’ (fruit crops and cereal/grass crops, foliar application); ‘folpet and phthalamic acid, expressed as folpet’ (root crops, foliar application)) and RA‐RD2 (i.e. ‘phthalic acid’ (all crops, foliar application)). The tentative risk assessment values for honey were included in the calculations despite the potential overestimation, in order to allow risk managers to take an informed decision.

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed for the commodities assessed in this application and did not exceed the ARfD for both RA‐RD1 and RA‐RD2, accounting for 0.5% of the ARfD for bananas (both RA‐RD1 and RA‐RD2), and 0.2% of the ARfD (RA‐RD1) and < 0.01% of the ARfD (RA‐RD2) for honey.

The maximum estimated long‐term dietary intake was 8% of the ADI according to RA‐RD1. The contribution of residues expected in bananas to the overall long‐term exposure accounted for up to 0.16% of the ADI and 0.02% of the ADI for honey according to RA‐RD1. it is noted that for root commodities, i.e. potatoes, radishes and salsifies, STMR values were based on the previously established RA‐RD, i.e. ‘sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’ and not according to the RA‐RD1 as recently defined for root crops in the context of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of the approval of folpet, i.e. ‘sum of folpet and phthalamic acid, expressed as folpet’, which introduced a degree of uncertainty in the consumer risk assessment.

Due to unavailability of residue data (and STMR values) for phthalic acid (RA‐RD2) for any of the commodities with reported GAPs, except for bananas and honey, the chronic consumer risk assessment could not be finalised according to RA‐RD2. EFSA only estimated the contribution to the ADI from bananas and honey, using STMR values derived from the trials submitted for these commodities in the current MRL application. The contribution of residues expected in bananas to the overall long‐term exposure accounted for up to 0.24% of the ADI for bananas and < 0.01% of the ADI for honey according to RA‐RD2.

For RA‐RD1, EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of folpet resulting from the existing and the authorised uses of folpet on bananas in Ecuador and El Salvador and the residues in honey resulting from the authorised uses on melliferous crops will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. For RA‐RD2, due to the lack of STMR values according to RA‐RD2 for the commodities with reported GAPs, with the exception of bananas, a high degree of uncertainty remains but the contribution from the commodities under assessment is very minor.

ASSESSMENT

The EFSA received an application to set an import tolerance for the active substance folpet in bananas and to modify the existing MRL for folpet in honey. The detailed description of the existing uses of folpet authorised in Ecuador and El Salvador in bananas, which is the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.1. Concerning the request for the modification of the MRL for folpet in honey, the current MRL application is not linked to one specific GAP but is related to the existing uses in crops and plants that might be attractive to bees and that are a potential source for residues of folpet in honey. The critical GAPs identified by the applicant is reported in Appendix A.2.

Folpet is the ISO common name for N‐(trichloromethylthio)phthalimide (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix E.

Folpet was evaluated in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 1 with Austria designated as RMS for the representative outdoor uses as a foliar treatment on wheat, barley, wine grapes and as both glasshouse and outdoor uses on tomatoes.

The renewal assessment report (RAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2023a). The approval of folpet was renewed 2 for the use as fungicide on 4 September 2024.

The EU MRLs for folpet are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 3 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been performed (EFSA, 2014) and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued several reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for folpet. The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations. 4

In accordance Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381 5 , the applicant ADAMA Agriculture BV submitted on 2 October 2024 an application to the competent national authority in Austria, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format. The appointed RMS Austria assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 12 December 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 31 January 2025 to 21 February 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 23 May 2025. The RMS proposed to establish MRL for bananas imported from Ecuador and El Salvador at the level of 0.09 mg/kg and MRL for honey at the level of 0.07 mg/kg.

The European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA on 20 June 2025 to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. EFSA identified a point which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address it. The applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 30 March 2026 (Austria, 2025), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Austria, 2025), the RAR (Austria, 2018, revised in 2023) prepared under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009, the Commission review report on folpet (European Commission, 2024), the conclusion on the peer review conclusion for the renewal of the approval of the active substance folpet (EFSA, 2023b), as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on folpet (2017, 2021, 2022; EFSA, 2014), including the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation No 396/2005.

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 283/2013 6 and the guidance documents applicable at the date of submission of the IUCLID application are applicable (European Commission, 2010, 2018, 2023a, 2023b, 2023c; OECD, 2007a, 2007b, 2007c, 2007d, 2007e, 2007f, 2007g, 2007h, 2008a, 2008b, 2009, 2014, 2016, 2021). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 7 A selected list of end points of the studies assessed by EFSA in the framework of this MRL application including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Austria, 2025) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA PRIMo are made publicly available as background documents to this reasoned opinion. 8

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of folpet in primary crops was investigated in the framework of peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b). Studies were conducted on commodities belonging to the groups of fruit crops (avocados, grapes), root crops (potatoes) and cereals/grass crops (wheat) following foliar application using 14C‐phenyl labelled folpet. Under the same framework, an additional metabolism study was provided on tomato plants following soil application, which was considered as supporting information only.

In the crops tested, the parent compound is degraded to phthalimide which is further hydrolysed to phthalamic acid, phthalic acid and related conjugates. The main residues in mature grapes, wheat straw and grain were folpet and phthalimide, in potato tuber phthalamic acid and in ripe avocado phthalic acid, which was also the major metabolite in all edible crop parts and feed. It was noted that phthalic acid occurs ubiquitously and it was suggested to collect data on background levels of this compound in raw agricultural commodities for all proposed uses to estimate and consider the contribution of these background levels compared to the residue levels resulting from folpet treatment according to the representative uses (EFSA, 2023b).

For the intended use, the metabolic behaviour in primary crops is sufficiently addressed.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

As folpet and phthalimide are not regarded as soil persistent (half‐lives below 3 days), metabolism studies in rotational crops are not required (EFSA, 2023b).

Nature of residues in processed commodities

The effect of processing on the nature of folpet was investigated in the framework of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of the active substance (EFSA, 2023b).

Two hydrolysis experiments were submitted and assessed. Both studies indicate a rapid and complete hydrolysis of folpet. The results of the two studies are comparable for pasteurisation and baking, brewing and boiling conditions. Folpet completely degraded predominantly to phthalimide (pasteurisation: 91% and 98% applied radioactivity (AR); baking, brewing/boiling: 58% and 56% AR; results given for both studies, respectively), while levels of phthalic acid increased with temperature and pH (pasteurisation: 8% and 1%, baking, brewing/boiling: 42% and 41% AR; sterilisation 81% and 45% AR). In the second study, high amounts of phthalamic acid were observed under sterilisation conditions (33% AR). In addition, 2‐cyanobenzoic acid was found under sterilisation conditions (11% AR) which is not in line with the first study. This incomplete degradation might have been caused by a different buffer system in the sterilisation experiments of this study. Overall, the main residues are formed by metabolites already identified in the plant metabolism studies (EFSA, 2023b).

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

Analytical methods for the determination of folpet and phthalimide residues were assessed during the MRL review, two previous MRL applications and the peer review for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2014, 2017, 2022, 2023b). These are based on HPLC–MS/MS and were fully validated in all commodity groups with a LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for both folpet and phthalimide (EFSA, 2023b). GC–MS methods also exist for the determination of folpet and phthalimide residues with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for both folpet and phthalimide in high‐oil content commodities (EFSA, 2023b), LOQs of 0.01 mg/kg and 0.05 mg/kg for folpet and phthalimide, respectively, in high acid content and dry commodities (EFSA, 2023b), LOQs of 0.05 mg/kg and 0.02 mg/kg for folpet and phthalimide, respectively, in high‐water content commodities (EFSA, 2014; EFSA, 2017), and LOQs of 0.05 mg/kg and 0.1 mg/kg for folpet and phtalimide, respectively, in hops (EFSA, 2014, 2023b); GC–MS/MS is also available for the determination of phthalimide in high‐water content commodities at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2022).

In addition, a new study for the validation of an analytical method was submitted with the current MRL application (Austria, 2025). The method, based on LC–MS/MS, was validated, including confirmation using two ion transitions and independent laboratory validation (ILV), for the quantification of folpet and phthalimide at or above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each analyte in high‐water content (lettuce), high‐acid content (orange), high‐oil content (oilseed rape) and dry (wheat grain) commodities. For wheat straw (dry commodity), the validated LOQs are 0.01 mg/kg and 0.05 mg/kg for folpet and phthalimide, respectively.

The methods are sufficiently validated for residues of folpet and phthalimide in the plant commodity under consideration.

The extraction efficiency of the solvent system used in the analytical method for enforcement of folpet (according to both the current Mo‐RD, ‘sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’ and the new Mo‐RD, ‘folpet’) in high‐water content commodities (to which the commodity under assessment belongs). The assessment was based on a comparison of the amounts of residues extracted from wheat whole plant (high‐water content matrix, cereals) using (i) the solvent system of the method for enforcement under assessment and (ii) the solvent system used in the metabolism study on potato tubers (high‐water content matrix, root crops). Due to the different metabolic pattern observed in fruit/cereal crops and root crops (see Section 1.1.1), this cross‐validation can only be regarded as indirect. Ideally, the cross‐validation should have been conducted using the solvent system applied in the metabolism study on a crop belonging to the same matrix group and showing a comparable metabolic pattern, which is not the case for root crops. A cross‐validation comparing residues extracted using the solvent systems of (i) the method for enforcement under assessment, (ii) the metabolism study in cereals and (iii) the metabolism study in potato tubers would have allowed a thorough evaluation of the extraction efficiency of the analytical method for enforcement in high‐water content commodities. EFSA acknowledges that the Applicant provided evidence demonstrating acceptable performance of the solvent system used in the metabolism study on potato tubers (high‐water content matrix, root crops) for the quantification of spiked residues of folpet, phthalimide and phthalic acid in wheat whole plant (high‐water content matrix, cereal crop). However, this evidence alone is not sufficient to demonstrate efficient extraction of incurred residues, in accordance with SANTE/2017/10632 rev. 5 (European Commission, 2023c).

Based on the considerations above, the extraction efficiency of the analytical method for enforcement in high‐water content commodities is considered to be only partially demonstrated, resulting in some uncertainty for the present assessment. To satisfy the current criteria of the guidance, further investigation on this matter would be required. EFSA would therefore recommend re‐assessing the extraction efficiency in the framework of future MRL applications or the review of existing authorised uses.

Storage stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of folpet and the metabolite phthalimide in plant commodities stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the framework of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b). Further information on the stability of folpet, phthalimide and, additionally, phthalic acid and phthalamic acid, in plant commodities stored under frozen conditions was submitted with the current application (Austria, 2025). Although the present assessment focuses solely on high‐water content commodities, the storage stability data submitted for other commodity groups were also assessed.

In high‐water content commodities, storage stability of folpet was demonstrated for at least 12 months (onions, tomatoes). However, it could not be demonstrated for such a period in other commodities such as cucumber (at least 1 month, limited by the shorter duration of the storage stability study), apple (at least 5 months, limited by the shorter duration of the storage stability study) and lettuce (up to 6 months, due to degradation of folpet at longer storage periods). The storage stability of phthalimide was demonstrated for at least 24 months in lettuce and for at least 12 months in onions and tomatoes, but only for up to 1 month in homogenised tomatoes, due to degradation of phtalamide at longer storage periods. The storage stability of the sum of folpet and phthalimide (expressed as folpet), phthalic acid and phthalamic acid was demonstrated for at least 24 months (lettuce), when stored at or below −18°C. For the commodity assessed in the framework of the current application, which belongs to the high‐water content commodity group, storage stability of residues according to the existing residue definition for enforcement (current Mo‐RD) and the relevant residue definitions for risk assessment (see Section 1.1.6) is sufficiently investigated. A comprehensive overview of all storage stability data, including matrices other than high‐water content, can be found in Appendix B.1.1.2.

It should be noted that, for folpet alone (new Mo‐RD; see Section 1.1.6), no consistent trend in storage stability across high‐water‐content commodities could be determined. This is due to the rapid degradation of folpet observed in certain commodities (i.e. lettuce), as well as to differences in the storage period tested across studies. FAO & WHO (2025) suggest that storage stability within this commodity group appears to be pH‐dependent, with folpet being stable under frozen storage conditions for at least 12 months in commodities with low pH levels (e.g. tomato), while quickly degrading to phthalimide in commodities with higher pH levels (e.g. lettuce). In the absence of storage stability data on bananas, a conclusion regarding storage stability of folpet alone in the commodity under assessment cannot be reached. It may only be tentatively assumed that the storage stability period of folpet in this commodity could reach 12 months (optimistic scenario); however, this assumption is associated with additional non‐standard uncertainty.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the MRL application, the applicant submitted 12 independent and GAP‐compliant residue trials performed in bananas.

The trials were conducted in Costa Rica (4), Ecuador (4), Colombia (2) and Mexico (2) in the 2022 growing season. The trials consisted of 12 foliar broadcast treatments at a nominal application rate of 750 g a.s./ha (687–809 g a.s./ha), performed at BBCH 71–80 at last application and with an interval between applications of 4–8 days. Four trials were conducted as decline trials with samples collected at 0 (corresponding to PHI), 1, 3, 5 and 7 days after the last treatment. Decline of residues over time was observed in all decline trials. At harvest, samples were collected from both bagged and unbagged banana commodities. Only residue data for bagged banana samples were presented in the evaluation report, reflecting the standard commercial practice in Ecuador, one of the exporting countries.

The samples were analysed for folpet (new Mo‐RD), sum of folpet and phthalimide (expressed as folpet) (current Mo‐RD and RA‐RD1(fruit crops)) and phthalic acid (RA‐RD2(all crops)). In addition, residues of phthalamic acid were also measured (for information purpose only). According to the assessment of the RMS, the method used, based on liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS), was sufficiently validated and fit for purpose and is able to quantify residues at the LOQs of 0.01 mg/kg (folpet, phthalimide) and 0.05 mg/kg (phthalic acid, phthalamic acid) in high‐water content commodities (Austria, 2025). The extraction efficiency of the analytical method applied for data generation in high‐water content commodities was investigated via indirect cross‐validation, by comparing the amounts of residues of folpet, phthalimide and phthalic acid extracted from wheat whole plant (high‐water content matrix, cereals) using (i) the solvent system of the analytical method under assessment (which is identical to the analytical method for enforcement) and (ii) the solvent system used in the metabolism study on potato tubers (high‐water content matrix, root crops). Based on the data provided, the extraction efficiency of the analytical method for data generation in bananas (high‐water content commodities) is considered to be only partially demonstrated, resulting in additional uncertainty for the present assessment (see also Section 1.1.4).

Existing Mo‐RD and associated RA‐RDs

The samples of the residue trials were stored under conditions for which stability of the residues in high‐water content commodities has been demonstrated, according to the current residue definition for enforcement (i.e. ‘sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’) and the residue definitions for risk assessment in fruit crops (i.e. RA‐RD1: sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet; RA‐RD2: phthalic acid) (Austria, 2025).

The dataset indicates concentrations of the sum of folpet and phthalimide (expressed as folpet) ranging between < 0.03 and 0.52 mg/kg in whole banana fruit and < 0.03 mg/kg in banana pulp. Phthalic acid residues ranged between < 0.05 and 0.07 mg/kg in banana pulp.

The submitted data are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.9 mg/kg according to the existing residue definition for enforcement (i.e. sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet).

New Mo‐RD

For folpet only (the only compound included in the residue definition for enforcement proposed in the context of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b)), no consistent trend in storage stability across high‐water‐content commodities could be determined (see Section 1.1.5). In the absence of storage stability data specifically for bananas, it cannot be confirmed whether banana samples, which were stored for 159–484 days (corresponding to 5–16 months), were maintained under conditions ensuring folpet stability. Even under the optimistic assumption of a 12‐month storage stability period in high‐water content commodities, 6 out of 12 trials had samples stored beyond this duration and cannot therefore be considered suitable for MRL calculations.

It is noted that, based on the full residue dataset, an MRL option (0.7 mg/kg) in line with the new residue definition for enforcement (i.e. folpet; EFSA, 2023b) was proposed by the RMS (Austria, 2025). However, EFSA does not support this proposal, due to an insufficient number of trials with samples stored under conditions ensuring stability of folpet in bananas (6). Consequently, an MRL for bananas according to the new residue definition for enforcement could not be derived.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

As the proposed uses of folpet is on permanent crops, investigations of residues in rotational crops are not required.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

However, since residue data on bananas were provided for whole fruit, peel and pulp (Austria, 2025), it was possible to calculate peeling factors. The number and quality of these data are sufficient to derive robust peeling factors. 9

Proposed MRLs

In Section 4, EFSA assessed whether residues in banana pulp resulting from the uses authorised in Ecuador and El Salvador are likely to pose a risk to consumer health.

Regarding the new residue definition for enforcement proposed in the context of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b) (RD‐Mo: folpet), no MRL could be derived due to the lack of a sufficient number of valid trials.

RESIDUES IN HONEY

Nature of residues in honey

Honey is produced by bees from sugary secretions of plants (floral nectar mainly) through regurgitation, enzymatic conversion and water evaporation and followed by storage in the bee hives for a certain time period.

In the absence of specific metabolism studies with honey bees, studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops and rotational crops (where available) and studies investigating the degradation during pasteurisation should be considered to determine the nature of residues in honey (European Commission, 2018). It is likely that the nature of residues in pollen and nectar collected from primary and rotational crops, as well as in honey (resulting from the residues in floral nectar), is the same as in primary and rotational crops.

Considering that sufficient data investigating the metabolic profile in primary crops and the degradation of the active substance under standard hydrolysis conditions are available, no further information is required for the current application according to the guidelines.

Analytical methods for enforcement in honey

In the framework of the present assessment, the applicant submitted a new method for enforcement of folpet residues in honey (Austria, 2025). The method, based on LC–MS/MS, is fully validated, including confirmation and ILV, and allows quantification of residues of folpet and phthalimide at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.

Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for enforcement of residues in honey was not provided and is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023c).

Storage stability of residues in honey

The storage stability of residues of folpet and its metabolites phthalimide, phthalic acid and phthalamic acid in honey samples stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the current MRL application (Austria, 2025). In addition, the storage stability of residues of phthalic anhydride was also investigated in honey samples stored under frozen conditions.

It was demonstrated that residues of folpet, phthalimide, phthalic acid and phthalamic acid were stable for at least 4 months when stored at or below −18°C in honey. Residues of phthalic anhydride were stable for at least 3.5 months when stored at or below −18°C in honey.

Proposed residue definitions

In the absence of specific metabolism studies on honey, the studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops and the studies investigating the degradation of the active substance during pasteurisation are considered to derive the residue definitions for honey. Taking into account the information from the residue trials provided with this application, showing quantifiable residues of folpet and its metabolites phthalimide and phthalic acid and no quantifiable residues of phthalamic acid and phthalic anhydride (see Section 3.2), the same residue definitions applicable to fruit crops are considered appropriate for honey (i.e. current Mo‐RD (Reg (EU) 2023/1042): folpet (sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet); new Mo‐RD (EFSA, 2023b): folpet; RA‐RD1a (fruit and cereal/grass crops only): folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet; RA‐RD2: phthalic acid) (Austria, 2025).

Magnitude of residues in honey

In support of the MRL application in honey, the Applicant submitted four independent semi‐field (tunnel) trials conducted with Phacelia tanacetifolia as a surrogate crop. The EU critical GAPs selected by the applicant are on wine grapes (NEU) and ornamental plants (NEU). The NEU GAP on wine grapes consists of up to 8 foliar applications at a maximum nominal application rate of 1.6 kg a.s./ha, applied up to BBCH 89, with a minimum interval between applications of 7 days; the NEU GAP on ornamental plants consists of up to 4 applications at a nominal application rate of 1.92 kg a.s./ha, applicable at any BBCH stage, with an interval between applications of 8 days.

The trials were performed in Germany (2), Spain (1) and southern France (1) in 2023. Each trial consisted of two plots, one treated and one untreated. In the treated plot, folpet was applied twice as a foliar spray during flowering of the surrogate crop. Applications were made at a nominal application rate of 3 kg a.s./ha, with a 7‐day interval between applications (first treatment at BBCH 60–61 and second treatment at BBCH 63–65). Honey was sampled between 8 and 21 days after application. The amount of honey collected from the four trials ranged from 35 to 128 grams and all samples had a water content below 20%. Although a sample weight of 100 g is recommended by the European Commission (2018), it is acknowledged that achieving this target under semi‐field conditions can be challenging. Therefore, the lower sample weights of 35 and 51 grams obtained from two treated plots are only considered a minor deviation.

Samples were analysed for folpet and its metabolites phthalimide, phthalic acid, phthalamic acid and phthalic anhydride. No residues of folpet, phthalimide, phthalamic acid and phthalic anhydride were detected at or above the limit of detection (LOD) in samples from the untreated plots. Residues of phthalic acid were measured slightly above the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg in a single untreated plot (0.054 mg/kg), which is attributable to background levels in the environment. In treated samples, residues of folpet ranged from < 0.01 to 0.035 mg/kg; residues of phthalimide ranged from 0.017 to 0.16 mg/kg; residues of phthalic acid ranged from < 0.05 to 0.06 mg/kg; residues of phthalamic acid and phthalic anhydride were below the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg in all samples.

According to the assessment of the RMS, the methods used, based on HPLC–MS/MS, are sufficiently validated for the quantification of residues of folpet and its metabolites with LOQs of 0.01 mg/kg for folpet and phthalimide and 0.05 mg/kg for phthalic acid, phthalamic acid and phthalic anhydride. Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for data generation in honey samples is not available and is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023b). The samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which integrity of the samples has been demonstrated.

The RMS considered the trials submitted in the current application suitable to support the cGAPs on wine grapes and ornamental plants and, based on the available dataset, proposed MRLs of 0.7 and 0.07 mg/kg according to the existing (Reg. (EU) 2023/1042) and the new (EFSA, 2023b) residue definitions for enforcement, respectively.

EFSA, however, does not consider the honey residue trials appropriate to support the selected cGAPs and therefore does not recommend an MRL modification for honey. In EFSA's view, the trials fail to mimic the selected cGAPs due to deviations in the application pattern used. Specifically, the individual application rate in the residue trials deviate by more than 25% (+56–88%). Overall, EFSA considers that the trials were overdosed as regards to the individual application rate compared with the authorised application pattern, which may lead to an overestimation of residue levels and, consequently, of the MRL proposal derived by the RMS, which would not be in line with the ALARA principle. This issue was raised by EFSA by means of a request for additional data (clock‐stop). The RMS and the applicant did not provide additional data but provided an argumentation based on the total application rate during the flowering period. They indicate that, for both GAPs, the total rate applied in the trials (6 kg a.s./ha, based on 2 × 3 kg a.s./ha) falls within ±25% of the total application rate during flowering of wine grapes (4.8 kg a.s./ha, based on 3 × 1.6 kg a.s./ha) and ornamental plants (7.68 kg a.s./ha, based on 4 × 1.92 kg a.s./ha), and that the trials are therefore acceptable. However, according to the Technical Guidelines on pesticide residues in honey (European Commission, 2018), ‘the test substance should be applied in a realistic worst‐case scenario with respect to residues in honey’ using an application rate ‘consistent with (the) GAP’. This is because honey residue levels depend not only on the total application rate during flowering, but also on how this rate is distributed in terms of both the number of applications and the individual application rates. Bee foraging on crops treated with 2 × 3 kg a.s./ha during flowering may result in a different honey residue profile compared with foraging on crops treated with 3 × 1.6 kg a.s./ha or 4 × 1.92 kg a.s./ha, even if the total application rate during flowering falls within the ±25% tolerance. Application patterns involving higher individual application rates and a lower number of applications may result in higher peak residues in nectar, and, consequently, higher residue levels in honey, compared to patterns in which the same total amount is applied through lower individual rates distributed over more applications. Overall, the application pattern used in the honey trials is not considered representative of the selected cGAPs and may result in overestimated residue concentrations in honey. Therefore, the trials are not considered acceptable to support an MRL modification.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The risk assessment was conducted separately for RA‐RD1 (i.e. ‘folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’ (fruit crops and cereal/grass crops, foliar application); ‘folpet and phthalamic acid, expressed as folpet’ (root crops, foliar application)) and RA‐RD2 (i.e. ‘phthalic acid’ (all crops, foliar application)).

RA‐RD1

The toxicological reference values for folpet used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI and ARfD values) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2023b; European Commission, 2024). The metabolites phthalimide and phthalamic acid, included in the first residue definition for risk assessment (RA‐RD1) were considered to be of similar toxicity than the parent compound (EFSA, 2023b).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology and focused on the commodities assessed in this application (FAO, 2016). For bananas, the calculations were based on the HRpulp derived from supervised field trials assessed in the present opinion. For honey, indicative calculations were based on the HR derived from semi‐field (tunnel) residue trials not compliant with the cGAP, as the individual application rates in the trials exceeded those of the authorised GAPs. Therefore, the dietary risk assessment for this commodity is likely overestimated (risk management decisions required; see Section 3.2.1). The input values used in the exposure calculations are reported in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for the commodities assessed in this application, accounting for 0.5% of the ARfD for bananas and 0.2% of the ARfD for honey.

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the MRL review a comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment was performed, taking into account the existing uses at EU level (EFSA, 2014). EFSA updated the calculation with the relevant supervised trials median residue (STMR) values derived in EFSA opinions published after the MRL review (EFSA, 2017, 2021, 2022) and from the residue trials for bananas and honey submitted in the current MRL application (Austria, 2025). For bananas the STMRpulp was considered. For honey, the STMR values used were derived from semi‐field (tunnel) residue trials not compliant with the cGAP, as the individual application rates in the trials exceeded those of the authorised GAPs. Therefore, the dietary risk assessment for this commodity is likely overestimated (risk management decisions required; see Section 3.2.1). Furthermore, for root crops with reported GAPs (i.e. potatoes, radishes and salsifies), STMR values were derived according to the previously established RA‐RD, i.e. ‘sum of folpet and phthalimide, expressed as folpet’ (EFSA, 2014) and not according to the RA‐RD1 as recently defined for root crops in the context of the peer review conclusion for the renewal of approval of folpet, i.e. ‘sum of folpet and phthalamic acid, expressed as folpet’ (EFSA, 2023b). As indicated in the metabolism study on potato tubers, phthalamic acid represents the major residue component in root crops (24.6% total radioactive residue (TRR), corresponding to 0.27 mg parent equivalent/kg), while folpet and phthalimide occur only at low levels (0.1% TRR for folpet, corresponding to 0.001 mg parent equivalent/kg; 0.5% TRR for phthalimide, corresponding to 0.01 mg parent equivalent/kg) (Austria, 2018, 2023; EFSA, 2023b). As a consequence, the use of STMR values based on the previous RA‐RD is expected to underestimate the consumer exposure and to introduce an additional degree of uncertainty in the chronic consumer risk assessment which cannot be accurately quantified with the available data.

The complete list of input values is presented in Appendix D.1.

The maximum estimated long‐term dietary intake was 8% of the ADI according to RA‐RD1. The contribution of residues to the overall long‐term exposure accounted for 0.16% of the ADI for bananas (NL toddler diet) and 0.02% of the ADI for honey (DE child diet) (see Appendix B.4).

As mentioned above, for RA‐RD1, the consumer risk assessment is affected by uncertainties related to the STMR values used for honey (commodity under assessment) and root crops (commodities assessed previously). For root crops, EFSA recommends that residue data generated in accordance with the revised residue definition for risk assessment are provided in the framework of future assessment to allow an accurate estimation of consumer exposure. Nevertheless, considering the overall margin of safety calculated for the chronic dietary risk assessment, EFSA concludes that the long‐term intake of residues of folpet resulting from the intended and the authorised uses is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health and that the uncertainties identified above are not considered to affect the conclusions of the current assessment.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

RA‐RD2

The toxicological reference values for phthalic acid, which is included in the second residue definition for risk assessment (RA‐RD2), used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI and ARfD values) were derived in the framework of the MRL review on the active substance dithianon (EFSA, 2023a; European Commission, 2024). For phthalic acid, EFSA derived higher TRVs than for the parent compound folpet which indicates a lower toxicological concern compared with the parent. The compound is unlikely to be genotoxic (EFSA, 2023b).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The short‐term exposure assessment was performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology and focused on the commodities assessed in this application (FAO, 2016). For bananas, the calculations were based on the HRpulp derived from supervised field trials. For honey, the calculations were based on the HR derived from semi‐field (tunnel) residue trials not compliant with the cGAP, as the individual application rates in the trials exceeded those of the authorised GAPs. Therefore, the dietary risk assessment for this commodity is likely overestimated (risk management decisions required; see Section 3.2.1). The input values used in the exposure calculations can be found in Appendix D.1.

The short‐term exposure did not exceed the ARfD for the commodities assessed in this application, accounting for 0.5% of the ARfD for bananas and < 0.01% of the ARfD for honey.

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

Due to the recent introduction of the RA‐RD2 in the context of the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b), risk assessment values according to the RA‐RD2 are currently only available for the commodities assessed in the present MRL application. As a consequence, a comprehensive chronic dietary exposure assessment for phthalic acid could not be performed. EFSA only estimated the contribution to the ADI from bananas and honey, using STMR values derived from the residue trials submitted for these commodities in the current MRL application (Austria, 2025). For bananas the STMRpulp was considered. For honey, the STMR values used were derived from semi‐field (tunnel) residue trials not compliant with the proposed cGAPs, as the individual application rates in the trials exceeded those of the authorised GAPs. Therefore, the dietary risk assessment for this commodity is likely overestimated (risk management decisions required; see Section 3.2.1). The list of input values is presented in Appendix D.1.

Despite the lower toxicity of phthalic acid compared to folpet, phthalic acid may occur at higher levels than the parent compound in plant commodities. It represents a major metabolite (EFSA, 2023b), accounting for a high proportion of the TRR in certain plant commodities (e.g. 81.9% TRR in avocados, 55.1% TRR in potato tubers; Austria, 2018, 2023). Therefore, the availability of residue data for all relevant commodities is essential to perform a consumer risk assessment. Confirmatory information should be provided and assessed in the framework of future MRL applications to determine phthalic acid residue levels resulting from the use of folpet, taking also into account its background levels in raw agricultural commodities during residue field trials (European Commission, 2024).

The contribution of residues to the overall long‐term exposure from the commodities under assessment is very low, accounting for 0.24% of the ADI for bananas (NL toddler diet) and < 0.01% of the ADI for honey (DE child diet) (see Appendix B.4).

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of PRIMo is presented in Appendix C for RA‐RD1. A screenshot of PRIMo for RA‐RD2 is not provided due to the lack of residue data for commodities other than banana and honey. Nevertheless, the results of the chronic exposure assessment for these two commodities are reported in Section B.4. and the full PRIMo file is provided for information, as background document to this reasoned opinion.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The dataset on bagged bananas submitted in support of this MRL application was found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.9 mg/kg for bananas, according to the currently applicable residue definition for enforcement. This MRL proposal is supported by EFSA.

For honey, an MRL of 0.7 mg/kg was proposed by the RMS based on the submitted residue trials. However, as these trials were not compliant with either of the selected cGAPs on wine grapes and ornamental plants, this proposal could not be supported by EFSA. The RMS's proposal is however reported for transparency and for consideration by risk managers. Available EU monitoring data on honey were assessed as supplementary information and indicate that honey placed in the EU market generally complies with the existing MRL, which is currently set at the LOQ of 0.05* mg/kg.

Based on the datasets on bagged bananas and honey, the RMS also derived alternative MRL options of 0.7 mg/kg for bananas and 0.07 mg/kg for honey, according to the new residue definition for enforcement (i.e. ‘folpet’) proposed in the framework of the renewal of approval of folpet (EFSA, 2023b), which is not currently implemented. EFSA does not support these alternative MRL options considering that the dataset for bananas lacks a sufficient number of residue trials with samples stored for durations for which stability of folpet has been demonstrated (in bananas or high‐water content commodities) and that the second MRL option for honey is based on residue trials that are not compliant with the relevant cGAPs and therefore cannot be used for MRL calculation.

EFSA concluded that the authorised South American use of folpet on bananas does not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. For honey, no consumer risk is indicated based on the conservative (likely overestimated) exposure estimates derived from the available non‐GAP‐compliant trials. For all other uses assessed in the context of the MRL review and previous applications (EFSA, 2014, 2017, 2021, 2022), uncertainties remain regarding the exposure to phthalamic acid from root crops, due to the lack of residue data measured in accordance with the recently established RA‐RD1b, and regarding exposure to phthalic acid for all commodities with authorised uses of folpet, with the exception of bananas and honey. In particular, due to the uncertainties related to phthalic acid, the long‐term consumer risk assessment according to the residue definition for risk assessment RA‐RD2 could not be finalised.

The MRL recommendations are summarised in Appendix B.4.
