Abstract
. We study preservation theorems for modal logics over finite structures with respect to three fundamental semantic relations: embeddings, injective homomorphisms, and homomorphisms. We focus on classes of pointed Kripke models that are invariant under bounded unravellings, a natural locality condition satisfied by modal logics and by graph neural networks (GNNs). We show that preservation under embeddings coincides with definability in existential graded modal logic; preservation under injective homomorphisms with definability in existential positive graded modal logic; and preservation under homomorphisms with definability in existential positive modal logic. A key technical contribution is a structural well-quasi-ordering result. We prove that the embedding relation on classes of tree-shaped models of uniformly bounded height forms a well-quasi-order, and that the bounded-height assumption is essential. This well-quasi-ordering yields a finite minimal-tree argument leading to explicit syntactic characterisations via finite disjunctions of (graded) modal formulae. As an application, we derive consequences for the expressive power of GNNs. Using our preservation theorem for injective homomorphisms, we obtain a new logical characterisation of monotonic GNNs, showing that they capture exactly existential-positive graded modal logic, while monotonic GNNs with MAX aggregation correspond precisely to existential-positive modal logic.

Introduction

Preservation theorems are a central theme in model theory
and theoretical computer science. They characterise
closure properties of classes of structures—such as preservation under embeddings or homomorphisms—with definability in specific logical fragments.
Łoś-Tarski, Lyndon, and Homomorphism
Preservation theorems are three classical examples
(
38
;
22
;
19
;
31
)
. Each states that
a particular fragment of first-order logic—existential,
positive, and existential-positive, respectively—is, up to logical equivalence, exactly as expressive as the class of first-order formulae whose models are preserved under a corresponding relation between structures: embeddings, surjective homomorphisms, and homomorphisms.

While these classical preservation theorems hold over arbitrary structures, many of them fail when restricted to finite structures.
Standard proofs rely on compactness and other infinitary techniques
from classical model theory
(
35
)
, which are unavailable in the finite setting
(
20
;
28
;
15
)
. Only a limited number of classical tools, most notably Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games, are applicable also to finite model theory, but they are insufficient to
bridge the gap left by the failure of compactness. As a consequence, important preservation theorems such as Łoś-Tarski and Lyndon are known to fail over finite structures
(
28
;
20
)
. By contrast, in a breakthrough result, Rossman showed that the Homomorphism Preservation Theorem does remain valid over finite structures
(
29
)
.

Preservation theorems have also been extensively investigated in the setting of modal logics. Unlike first-order logic, standard modal logics are
inherently local and enjoy the tree-model property
(
5
;
10
;
6
)
.
Modal semantics is invariant under bisimulation and admits canonical tree representations via unravelling.
Van Benthem’s classical characterisation theorem shows that, over arbitrary structures, first-order properties invariant under bisimulation are exactly those definable in the basic modal logic
\mathcal{ML}
. Rosen subsequently showed that this correspondence continues to hold over finite structures, establishing that bisimulation invariance suffices for modal definability even in the absence of compactness
(
27
;
28
)
.

Furthermore,
27
proved a finite modal analogue to Łoś-Tarski’s preservation theorem
showing that, over finite pointed models,
\mathcal{ML}
-formulae
preserved under embeddings are definable in the existential fragment of
\mathcal{ML}
. More recently,

2
developed
a unifying framework for homomorphism preservation theorems exploiting
category-theoretic methods and game comonads
(
3
;
1
)
. In particular, they show that for
\mathcal{ML}
, as well as for graded modal logic
\mathcal{GML}
, preservation under homomorphisms is equivalent to definability in the existential-positive fragment, with no increase in modal depth. Their results apply to finite and infinite structures without reliance on compactness.

In this paper, we study modal preservation theorems from a
finite model-theoretic perspective. We work with classes of finite pointed Kripke models that are invariant under bounded unravellings, a natural locality condition in modal logic. Within this setting, we establish preservation theorems for embeddings, injective homomorphisms, and homomorphisms, obtaining exact characterisations in terms of existential and existential-positive fragments of
\mathcal{GML}
and
\mathcal{ML}
. Our results complement Rosen’s finite preservation theorems and the categorical framework of Abramsky and Reggio by providing syntactic characterisations. In particular, we obtain new preservation theorems for embeddings and injective homomorphisms in the setting of
\mathcal{GML}
. Our results imply also the results over finite models by Rosen as well as Abramsky and Reggio.

An important technical ingredient underlying our approach is a structural result concerning well-quasi-orders (wqos). In
Section

3
we show that the embedding relation on classes of tree-shaped models of uniformly bounded height forms a well-quasi-order (
Section

3
), and that this bounded-height assumption is essential: without it, none of the considered relations is guaranteed to yield a wqo (
Section

3
). Our use of wqos is closely related in spirit to the framework
developed by
21
, which studies finite preservation theorems via minimal models, wqos and Noetherian topologies.
The two approaches, however, address different settings: Lopez’s results rely on hereditary classes of finite structures, an assumption that fails for unravelling-invariant classes of Kripke models (see
Section

3
), and hence require different techniques.

In
Section

4
, building on our results for wqos, we establish general preservation theorems for classes
\mathcal{C}
of finite pointed Kripke models invariant under bounded unravellings. From any such class
\mathcal{C}
preserved under one of the semantic relations discussed above, we extract a finite set of minimal tree-shaped models. Each such minimal tree is definable by a modal formula of bounded depth and the finite disjunction of these formulae yields a defining formula for
\mathcal{C}
.
Concretely, we show that

\mathcal{C}
is preserved under embeddings if and only if it is definable in existential
\mathcal{GML}
(
Section

4.1
); that
\mathcal{C}
is preserved under injective homomorphisms if and only if it is definable in existential-positive
\mathcal{GML}

(
Section

4.2
); and that
\mathcal{C}
is preserved under homomorphisms if and only if it is definable in existential-positive
\mathcal{ML}
(
Section

4.3
).
From these results, we derive corresponding preservation theorems for
\mathcal{GML}
and
\mathcal{ML}
formulae, summarised in

As we show in
Section

5
, beyond their intrinsic interest in logic, our preservation theorems have direct implications for the expressive power of graph neural networks (GNNs)
(
14
)
—machine learning models for graph data—by enforcing structural invariants such as node permutation invariance (i.e. invariance under graph isomorphism)
(
17

11

30
)
.
In their most common form, GNNs are message-passing architectures that compute node representations by iteratively aggregating information from neighbouring nodes. Their expressive power—the classes of graph properties they can represent—is thus tied to logical notions of locality and unravelling
(
8
)
. A growing body of work has established correspondences between classes of GNN architectures and logic, including basic and graded modal logic, two-variable fragments, and Datalog
(
8

16
;
18
;
32
;
34
;
25

12

monotonic GNNs using only MAX aggregation can be represented without inequalities
(
32
;
33

Our results yield a new logical characterisation of monotonic GNNs. Using the preservation theorem for injective homomorphisms, we show that monotonic GNNs have exactly the expressive power of existential-positive
\mathcal{GML}
whereas monotonic-MAX GNNs have the expressive power of existential-positive
\mathcal{ML}

More broadly, our approach illustrates how tools from finite model theory, such as unravelling-invariance and wqos, can be fruitfully applied to questions in the theoretical analysis of machine learning models.

Regarding invariance, we focus on classes of pointed models that are invariant

Classes invariant under unravelling have been extensively studied in the literature. Crucially for our results, both modal logic formulae and graph neural networks induce only classes of models that are invariant under unravelling, a fact that we exploit throughout the paper.

Well-quasi-orders.

Our results exploit well-quasi-orders (wqos) and their standard properties, which we recall. Let
A
be a set and let
\preceq
be a binary relation on
A
.
The pair
(A,\preceq)
is a
well-quasi-order
if it is a quasi-order (that is, reflexive and transitive) and each infinite sequence
a_{1},a_{2},\dots

of elements
of
A
contains indices
i<j
such that
a_{i}\preceq a_{j}
.
Equivalently,
\preceq
is a wqo if it is well-founded and has no infinite antichains.
In particular, in a wqo every subset has finitely many minimal elements, since an infinite set of minimal elements forms an infinite antichain.

We will use the following basic properties of wqos. If
(A,\preceq)
is a wqo, then
(A^{\prime},\preceq)
, with
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
is also a wqo.
Moreover,
if
\preceq^{\prime}
is a quasi-order on
A
extending
\preceq
, then
(A,\preceq^{\prime})
is again a wqo.

Another key property of wqos used in this paper is Higman’s Lemma.
Let
A^{*}
be the set of all finite sequences with elements in
A
and
let
\preceq^{*}
be the
subsequence order
, defined by
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\preceq^{*}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
if there exists a strictly increasing map

f:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
a_{i}\preceq b_{f(i)}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Lemma
\thethm
(Higman’s Lemma).

If
(A,\preceq)
is a wqo, then
(A^{*},\preceq^{*})
is also a wqo.

Well-quasi-orders of Tree-Shaped Models of Bounded Height

In this section we establish structural results that form the technical core of the paper. We study the embedding, injective homomorphism, and homomorphism relations on classes of tree-shaped pointed Kripke models, with particular attention to the role played by bounded height.
Throughout the paper, we work in finite model theory; accordingly, all models considered are finite.

We begin by highlighting the general strategy underlying our preservation results. Let
\preceq
denote one of the embedding, injective homomorphism, and homomorphism relations.
Given a class

Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{T},v_{1})
.
Since
(\mathfrak{T},v_{1})
has a neighbour
u
of the root satisfying
p
, while
w^{\prime}
itself does not satisfy
p
(as
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
), it follows that

\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
must contain a world reachable from
w^{\prime}
in one step that satisfies
p
.
Moreover, since
(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
must contain a self-loop
(w^{\prime},w^{\prime})
.

As a consequence,
\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})

contains distinct paths of lengths
1,\dots,L
from the root to distinct nodes satisfying
p
. In particular,

\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
contains

L
distinct nodes satisfying
p
. By contrast,

(\mathfrak{T},v_{1})
contains exactly one world satisfying
p
.
Since
L>1
, we obtain that
\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M}^{\prime},w^{\prime})
cannot be isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{T},v_{1})
, contradicting
our assumption.
∎

Therefore, we cannot assume that the classes
\mathcal{T}
of unravellings are preserved under embeddings.
To overcome this obstacle and ensure the finiteness of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
,
we establish a stronger structural result.
Specifically, we show that the embedding relation forms a well-quasi-order on any class of tree-shaped models of uniformly bounded height.
Since
L
-unravellings have height at most
L
, this result applies directly to the
classes
\mathcal{T}
considered above.

This theorem, however, goes beyond the setting of
unravellings: it applies to arbitrary classes
of tree-shaped models, independently of any preservation properties.
In particular, it complements the framework developed by Lopez
(
21
)
, which relies on hereditary classes of
finite structures. As shown in Proposition
3
, unravelling-invariant classes of pointed Kripke models are not
hereditary and hence the results in
(
21
)

do not apply in our setting.

Theorem
\thethm
.

The embedding relation on any class of pointed tree-shaped models whose height is uniformly bounded by a fixed constant
is a well-quasi-order.

Proof.

Let
L
be the uniform bound on the height of the models under consideration, and let
\preceq
be the embedding relation between pointed models.
Clearly,
\preceq
is a quasi-order.
We prove by induction on
\ell\leq L
the following claim:
for every class
\mathcal{T}
of pointed tree-shaped models of height at most
\ell
,
the quasi-order
(\mathcal{T},\preceq)
is a wqo.

Base case (
\ell=0
)
. A tree-shaped model of height
0
consists of a single root labelled by some set

S\subseteq\mathsf{PROP}
. Since the propositional signature
\mathsf{PROP}
is finite, there are finitely many non-isomorphic models of this form.
Hence, for any class of pointed tree-shaped models of height
0
, the quasi-order
induced by
\preceq
is a wqo. Note that the finiteness of

\mathsf{PROP}
is essential for this argument.

Inductive step
. Let
\ell\geq 1
and assume that the claim holds for
\ell-1
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be an arbitrary class of pointed tree-shaped models of height at most
\ell
.
We show that
(\mathcal{T},\preceq)
is a wqo.

We begin by partitioning
\mathcal{T}

into subclasses
\mathcal{T}^{S}
indexed by
S\subseteq\mathsf{PROP}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{S}
consists of all pointed models in
\mathcal{T}
whose root satisfies exactly the propositions in
S
.
Since
2^{\mathsf{PROP}}
is finite and a finite disjoint union of wqos is also a wqo,
it suffices to show that each
(\mathcal{T}^{S},\preceq)
is a wqo.

Fix
S\subseteq\mathsf{PROP}
. For each
(\mathfrak{M},w)
in
\mathcal{T}^{S}
, let

\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{n})

be a finite sequence enumerating its immediate subtrees rooted at the children of
w
.
As the children are unordered, we fix an arbitrary but canonical ordering on models
to obtain a well-defined enumeration.
The embedding relation
\preceq
on pointed models induces the subsequence order relation
\preceq^{*}
on their finite sequences, defined as in Higman’s Lemma (Lemma
2
).
To account for the arbitrary ordering of children,
we consider the permutation-closed variant
\preceq^{*}_{\pi}

defined by
\tau\preceq^{*}_{\pi}\tau^{\prime}
if and only if
\tau\preceq^{*}f(\tau^{\prime})
for some permutation
f
.
We claim that for any
(\mathfrak{M},w),(\mathfrak{N},v)\in\mathcal{T}^{S}
,
(\mathfrak{M},w)\preceq(\mathfrak{N},v)

if and only if
\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\preceq^{*}_{\pi}\tau_{\mathfrak{N},v}
.
Indeed, an embedding from
(\mathfrak{M},w)
into
(\mathfrak{N},v)
must map each child of
w
injectively
to a distinct child of
v
, such that the corresponding subtree embeds recursively.
This yields
\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\preceq^{*}_{\pi}\tau_{\mathfrak{N},v}
.
Conversely, if
\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\preceq^{*}_{\pi}\tau_{\mathfrak{N},v}
then
each subtree in
\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}
embeds into a distinct
subtree in
\tau_{\mathfrak{N},v}
, yielding an embedding from
(\mathfrak{M},w)
into
(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Thus, we show that
\bigl(\{\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{T}^{S}\},\preceq^{*}_{\pi}\bigr)
is a wqo.
To this end, let
\mathcal{I}
be the set of all pointed tree-shaped models of
height at most
\ell-1
.
By the inductive hypothesis,
(\mathcal{I},\preceq)
is a wqo. By Higman’s Lemma (Lemma
2
), the set of finite sequences
\mathcal{I}^{*}
ordered by the subsequence relation
\preceq^{*}
is also a wqo.
Since wqos are closed under taking subsets, and

(\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{T}^{S})
is a subset of
\mathcal{I}^{*}
,
it follows that
((\tau_{\mathfrak{M},w}\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{T}^{S}),\preceq^{*})
is a wqo. Moreover, as
\preceq^{*}
is a subrelation of
\preceq^{*}_{\pi}
and
\preceq^{*}_{\pi}
is a quasi-order, it follows that
\preceq^{*}_{\pi}
is also a wqo on this set. This completes the inductive step.
∎

As an immediate consequence, the preceding theorem extends to the other semantic relations considered in this paper. Since wqos are closed
under taking super-relations, and since the injective homomorphism relation and the homomorphism relation extend the embedding relation, the same result holds for these relations.

Corollary
\thethm
.

The injective homomorphism relation, as well as the homomorphism relation, on any class of tree-shaped models whose
height is uniformly bounded by a constant is a well-quasi-order.

As a result, for
\preceq
equal to the embedding, injective homomorphism, or homomorphism
relation, and for any class
\mathcal{T}
of pointed tree-shaped models of uniformly bounded height, the quasi-order

(\mathcal{T},\preceq)
is a wqo.
In particular,
\mathcal{T}
admits only finitely many
\preceq
-minimal elements. This finiteness property underpins the minimal-model arguments used in the subsequent preservation theorems.

We conclude this section by showing that the bounded-height assumption
is essential: if height is unbounded, the well-quasi-order property fails.

4.
Preservation Theorems for Unravelling-Invariant Classes and Modal Logics

We now use the results from the previous section—most notably,
Section

3
—to establish preservation theorems for classes of models that are invariant under unravelling.
These results will subsequently be applied
to obtain preservation theorems for modal logics and, in turn, for graph neural networks.

This section is organised into three main parts, which
relate unravelling-invariant classes of models preserved under embeddings, injective homomorphisms, and homomorphisms, with definability in
\exists\mathcal{GML}
,
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
, and
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
, respectively.

All three preservation theorems
follow the same general proof strategy.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of models invariant under
L
-unravelling and preserved under a relation
\preceq
(namely, embedding, injective homomorphism, or homomorphism).
We first construct the class
\mathcal{T}
of
L
-unravellings of models in
\mathcal{C}
.
We then let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
be the set of
\preceq
-minimal models in
\mathcal{T}
.
For each model
(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
we
construct a characteristic formula
\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}
such that, for every pointed model
(\mathfrak{M},w)
,
(\mathfrak{M},w)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}

if and only if
(\mathfrak{T},v)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M},w)
.
Finally, we define a formula
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}
as a disjunction over all
\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}
with
(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
.
By construction, the formula
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}
defines the class
\mathcal{C}
.

Finiteness of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}

is crucial for this strategy.
If
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
were infinite, the resulting disjunction would be infinite.
Importantly, the finiteness of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
is guaranteed by
Section

3
.
The modal language to which the characteristic formulae

\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}
belong
depends on the chosen relation
\preceq
: for
\preceq
being the
embedding, injective homomorphism, and homomorphism, the formulae

\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}
belong, respectively, to the fragments
\exists\mathcal{GML}
,
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
, and
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
.

4.1.
Preservation Under Embeddings

Our aim is to show that any class of models preserved under embeddings and invariant under
L
-unravelling is definable by a
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formula. Moreover, we show
that such a formula can be chosen to have depth at most
L
.
To this end, we introduce characteristic formulae
in
\exists\mathcal{GML}
. These formulae are obtained as a modification of the standard characteristic formulae for graded modal logic from the literature
(
26
;
12
)
.
The key difference is that our characteristic formulae in
\exists\mathcal{GML}
do not contain negated modal operators.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
be a pointed model with
\mathfrak{M}=(W,R,V)
.
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
a
characteristic
\exists\mathcal{GML}
formula
,
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}
of depth
\ell
inductively as follows.
For
\ell=0
, let

\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}:=\bigwedge\{p\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\models p\}\land\bigwedge\{\neg p\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\not\models p\}.

For
\ell+1
, partition the
R
-successors of
w
into equivalence classes
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
according to the relation

\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{\ell}
.
For each
C_{j}
, choose an arbitrary representative
w_{j}\in C_{j}

and define

\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell+1}\ :=\ \varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\Diamond^{\geq|C_{j}|}\,\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}^{\ell}.

For full
\mathcal{GML}
, a characteristic formula of depth
\ell
for a pointed model

(\mathfrak{M},w)
holds in
(\mathfrak{N},v)
if and only if there exists a graded
\ell
-bisimulation between these two pointed models. Over
finite models, this is equivalent to
(\mathfrak{M},w)\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v)

(
26
)
.
The characteristic formulae defined above for
\exists\mathcal{GML}
are strictly weaker, and
these equivalences no longer hold.
Instead, the
\exists\mathcal{GML}
characteristic formulae ensure a one-way structural property: the
\ell
-unravelling of the model used to construct the characteristic formula embeds into the
\ell
-unravelling of any model satisfying the formula.
This property is captured by the following lemma, which plays a central role in the
preservation theorem for embeddings.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
(\mathfrak{N},v)
be pointed models and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
We have
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}

if and only if
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w)
embeds into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Proof.

We prove the equivalence by induction on
\ell
. Let
\preceq
be the
embedding relation between pointed models.

Base case (
\ell=0
)
. Formula
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}
is a conjunction of all literals (propositions and their negations) satisfied at
(\mathfrak{M},w)
.
Thus,
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}
iff
(\mathfrak{M},w)\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.
Since
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
consist single worlds, it holds iff

\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Inductive step.
Assume that the claim holds for
\ell\geq 0
.
We prove that it also holds for
\ell+1
.
Recall that

\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell+1}\ :=\ \varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}\Diamond^{\geq|C_{j}|}\,\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}^{\ell}
, where

C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
are the equivalence classes of

R
-successors of
w

under

\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{\ell}
, and
w_{j}\in C_{j}
is an
arbitrary representative. For each
j
, we also fix an
enumeration

C_{j}=\{w_{j,1},\ldots,w_{j,|C_{j}|}\}
.

(
\Rightarrow
)

Assume that
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell+1}
.
Then,
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}
, and hence
(\mathfrak{M},w)\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.
Moreover, for each
j
, there exist
|C_{j}|
distinct successors

v_{j,1},\ldots,v_{j,|C_{j}|}
of
v
such that

(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}^{\ell}
.

By the inductive hypothesis, for each such
j
and
k
, there exists an embedding
f_{j,k}
from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j})
into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})
.
Since
w_{j,k}\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{\ell}w_{j}
, the unravellings

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k})
and
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j})
are isomorphic.
Composing this isomorphism with
f_{j,k}
yields an embedding of

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k})
into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})
.

We now construct a global embedding
f

from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
as follows.
Function
f
maps the root of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to the root of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
. For each
j
and
k
,
f
coincides with the embedding constructed above on the

w_{j,k}
.
Since the successors
v_{j,k}
are pairwise distinct, the images of these embeddings are
disjoint. It follows that
f
is an embedding of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Conversely, assume that
f
is an embedding of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
into
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.
Then,
f
maps the root of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to the root of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
, and therefore,
(\mathfrak{M},w)\equiv^{\mathcal{GML}}_{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.
Thus,
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}
.
Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
By the definition of unravelling,
the root of
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
has exactly
|C_{j}|

distinct nodes at depth
1
corresponding to the successors

w_{j,1},\dots,w_{j,|C_{j}|}
of
w
.
Since
f
is injective, it maps these nodes
to
|C_{j}|
distinct nodes at depth
1
in
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.
Each such node corresponds to a
successor

v_{j,k}
of
v
in
\mathfrak{N}
, and the subtree below this node is precisely

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})
.
Restricting
f
to the corresponding subtrees yields embeddings, so

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k})\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})

for
k=1,\dots,|C_{j}|
.
By the inductive hypothesis, this implies
(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})\models\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k}}^{\ell}
.
Since each
w_{j,k}\in C_{j}
, we obtain that
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k}}^{\ell}
is identical to
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}^{\ell}
.
Moreover, the worlds
v_{j,k}
are pairwise distinct successors
of
v
. Hence,

(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\Diamond^{\geq|C_{j}|}\,\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}^{\ell}
, as required.
∎

Equipped with
Section

4.1
and
Section

3
, we are now ready to prove the preservation theorem for classes of models preserved under embeddings and invariant under
L
-unravelling.
As we show below, these are exactly the classes definable by
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formulae of modal depth at most
L
.

Theorem
\thethm
.

The following are equivalent for any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models and any
L\in\mathbb{N}
:

•

\mathcal{C}
is invariant under
L
-unravelling and preserved under embeddings,

•

\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists\mathcal{GML}
formula of depth at most
L
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)

Let
\mathcal{T}=\{\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M},w)\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{C}\}
and let
\preceq
be the embedding relation.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under

L
-unravelling, we have that
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
By
Section

3
,
(\mathcal{T},\preceq)
is a wqo and therefore admits
only finitely many
\preceq
-minimal elements up to isomorphism. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
contain exactly
one representative for each such minimal isomorphism type.
For each
(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
, we construct the
\exists\mathcal{GML}
characteristic formula
\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}^{L}
and define

\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}}\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}^{L}
.
Since the disjunction is finite and each
\varphi_{\mathfrak{T},v}^{L}
is an
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formula,
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}
is a (finite)
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formula.
We show that
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}}
defines
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{C}
. Then

.

Conversely, suppose that

. Then,

, this implies
\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{T},v)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M},w)

(
\Leftarrow
)
Suppose that
\mathcal{C}

-formula

4.2.
Preservation Under Injective Homomorphisms

We next characterise unravelling-invariant classes that are preserved under injective homomorphisms.
Recall that every embedding is an injective homomorphism, but not conversely.
This suggests that the logical language defining classes preserved under injective homomorphisms should be
strictly weaker than the one required for preservation under embeddings.
By
Section

4.1
, the latter language is
\exists\mathcal{GML}
.
As we show next, the appropriate language in the case of injective homomorphisms is
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
, the existential-positive fragment of
\mathcal{GML}
.

We first define characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formulae, obtained from the characteristic
\exists\mathcal{GML}
formulae by deleting all negative literals.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
be a pointed model with

\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formula

\bigwedge\{\neg p\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\not\models p\}

\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formula.

While

guarantees the existence of an embedding between
\ell
-unravellings of models, the formula
\psi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}
guarantees the existence of an injective homomorphism between these unravellings. This is captured by the following lemma.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
(\mathfrak{N},v)
be pointed models and let

. Then,

(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\psi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}

if and only if there exists an injective homomorphism from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
\ell
and closely parallels the proof of Lemma
4.1
;
we highlight only the points where injective homomorphisms replace embeddings.
Throughout this proof, let
\preceq

be the injective homomorphism relation.

Base case (
\ell=0

The formula

(\mathfrak{M},w)
.
Since
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)

\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Inductive step.

Assume that the claim holds for some
\ell\geq 0
, and consider
\ell+1
.
We use the same notation as in the proof of
Section

4.1
.

(
\Rightarrow
)

Assume that
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\psi^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
. Then
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\psi^{0}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
, and for each equivalence

, there exist

of

, these homomorphisms can be combined together into a single injective homomorphism from

Since the targets lie in pairwise disjoint subtrees rooted at distinct depth-

.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Conversely, suppose that there exists an injective homomorphism

f
from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
. Then

maps the root to the root, which implies

Fix

. Injectivity of

\mid C_{j}\mid

Each such node corresponds to a successor

of

in
\mathfrak{N}
.
Restricting

to the corresponding subtrees yields injective homomorphisms from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j,k})

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j,k})

By the induction hypothesis we obtain

for all

are pairwise distinct successors of

is invariant under

-unravellings, we have

\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.

Let
\preceq
denote the injective homomorphism relation. By
Section

3
,
(\mathcal{T},\preceq)
is a wqo and hence admits finitely many
\preceq
-minimal elements up to isomorphism. Let

\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
contain one representative for each such minimal isomorphism type, and let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
consist of the prunings of the models in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
.

By
Section

4.2
, each pruning is

L
-bisimilar to the original tree. Since
\varphi
is an
\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth
\ell
, the class
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under

\ell
-bisimulations, and hence
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is finite because
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
is finite.

For each pruned tree
(\mathfrak{T},t)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
consider its
characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formula

\psi_{\mathfrak{T},t}^{\ell}
. By construction, these formulae
are positive and, by
Section

4.2
,
each
\psi_{\mathfrak{T},t}^{\ell}
is in fact an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula.
Let

\Psi\;=\;\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{T},t)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\psi_{\mathfrak{T},t}^{\ell}.

Then
\Psi
is a finite disjunction of
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formulae of depth at most
\ell
.
By construction,
\Psi
holds in each model in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

and hence in each model in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
.
By
Section

4.2
, it holds in all models in
\mathcal{T}
and since
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under
\ell
-unravellings, it follows that
\Psi

defines
\mathcal{C}
.

(
\Leftarrow
)
Let
\varphi
be equivalent to an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula. Since
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}\subseteq\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
,
\varphi
is also equivalent to an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formula. By
Section

4.2
, it is preserved under injective homomorphisms.
∎

4.3.
Preservation Under Homomorphisms

We now turn to classes preserved under homomorphisms.
We begin by showing that if such a class is invariant under unravelling, then it is definable by existential-positive modal logic (
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
).
Our proof strategy follows the same general pattern
as in the cases of embeddings and injective homomorphisms.
In particular, we define an appropriate form of characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formulae.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
be a pointed model with
\mathfrak{M}=(W,R,V)
, the
characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula

\chi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}
of depth
\ell
is defined as the formula obtained from
the characteristic
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formula
\varphi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}

(see
Section

4.1
) by removing all negative literals
\neg p
and replacing each graded modal operator
\Diamond^{\geq k}
with the basic modal operator
\Diamond
.

Characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formulae are related to homomorphisms between unravellings by the following lemma.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Let
(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
(\mathfrak{N},v)
be pointed models and let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\chi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{\ell}

if and only if
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v)
, where

\preceq
denotes the homomorphism relation.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
\ell
.

Base case (
\ell=0
).

The formula

\chi^{0}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
is a conjunction of propositional
symbols satisfied at
w
. Thus,
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\chi^{0}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
if and only if
(\mathfrak{N},v)
satisfies at least the same
propositional symbols as
(\mathfrak{M},w)
. Since
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)
and
\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
each consist of a single world, this holds if and only if

\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{M},w)\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{0}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

Inductive step.

Assume that the claim holds for some
\ell\geq 0
, and consider
\ell+1
.
We use the same notation as in the proof of
Section

4.1
.

(
\Rightarrow
)

Let
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\chi^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
. Then,
for each equivalence class
C_{j}
of successors of
w
in
\mathfrak{M}
,
there exists at least one successor
v_{j}
of
v
in
\mathfrak{N}
such that

(\mathfrak{N},v_{j})\models\chi^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}
. By the inductive hypothesis, for each
j
,

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j})\preceq\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j})
.
The unravelling
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
consists of the root
together with subtrees rooted at depth-
1
nodes corresponding
to the successors of
w
. Since homomorphisms need not be injective,
all depth-
1
nodes corresponding to successors in the same class

C_{j}
may be mapped to the same depth-
1
node corresponding to

v_{j}
. Mapping the root to the root and using the homomorphisms above on the corresponding subtrees yields
a homomorphism from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Let
f
be a homomorphism from
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{M},w)
to
\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell+1}(\mathfrak{N},v)
. Then,
f
maps
the root to the root, implying
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\chi_{\mathfrak{M},w}^{0}
.
Fix an equivalence class
C_{j}
. Every depth-
1
node corresponding to a successor
w_{j}
of
w
must be mapped by
f
to some
depth-
1
node corresponding to a successor
v_{j}
of
v
. Restricting

f
to the corresponding subtree yields a homomorphism

\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{M},w_{j})\to\mathsf{Unr}^{\ell}(\mathfrak{N},v_{j})
and by the
inductive hypothesis we obtain
(\mathfrak{N},v_{j})\models\chi^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}
.
Hence,
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\Diamond\chi^{\ell}_{\mathfrak{M},w_{j}}
for each
j
and therefore
(\mathfrak{N},v)\models\chi^{\ell+1}_{\mathfrak{M},w}
.
∎

The preceding result allows us to establish the homomorphism preservation theorem for unravelling-invariant classes.

Theorem
\thethm
.

The following are equivalent for any class
\mathcal{C}
of pointed models and any
L\in\mathbb{N}
:

•

\mathcal{C}
is preserved under homomorphisms and invariant under
L
-unravelling,

•

\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same general strategy
as that of
Section

4.1
, but now uses
Section

4.3
in place of
Section

4.1
.

(
\Rightarrow
)

Let

\mathcal{T}=\{\,\mathsf{Unr}^{L}(\mathfrak{M},w)\mid(\mathfrak{M},w)\in\mathcal{C}\,\}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under
L
-unravelling, we have

\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
be a set of representatives of the
homomorphism-minimal elements of
\mathcal{T}
. By
Section

3
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
is finite (alternatively, finiteness also follows from the fact that there are
only finitely many
\mathcal{ML}
-inequivalent formulae of depth at most
L
).
For each

(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
, let
\chi^{L}_{\mathfrak{T},v}
be its characteristic
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula and define

\chi_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{T},v)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}}\chi^{L}_{\mathfrak{T},v}
, which is a finite
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
.

By construction,
\chi_{\mathcal{C}}
holds in every model in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}

and by
Section

4.3
it therefore holds in all models in
\mathcal{T}
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is invariant under
L
-unravelling, it follows that
\chi_{\mathcal{C}}
defines
\mathcal{C}
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula
\varphi
of depth at most
L
. Since
\varphi
is positive, the
standard translation yields an existential-positive first-order logic formula.
Such formulae are preserved under homomorphisms
(
29
)
.
Moreover, since
\varphi
has depth at most
L
, it is invariant under
L
-unravelling.
∎

We note that
Section

4.3
yields,
as immediate corollaries, equirank homomorphism
preservation theorems for modal logics. Results of this form were previously obtained
by
2
in a general category-theoretic framework. In our setting, they follow directly
from unravelling invariance. Indeed,
every
\mathcal{GML}
(and
\mathcal{ML}
) formula of depth at most
L
is invariant under

L
-unravelling and hence
Section

4.3
applies to the class of its models.

Corollary
\thethm
(
(
2
)
, Theorem 5.20).

The following are equivalent for any
\mathcal{GML}
-formula
\varphi
of depth at most
L
:

•

\varphi
is preserved under homomorphisms;

•

\varphi
is equivalent to an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
.

4.4.
Recovering Rosen’s Embedding Preservation Theorem for
\mathcal{ML}

We conclude the preservation results by observing that our framework also
recovers the classical embedding preservation theorem for
\mathcal{ML}

in the finite setting due to
27
.
This yields an alternative proof based on well-quasi-orders and minimal models, rather than Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games.

Theorem
\thethm
(
(
27
)
, Proposition 6).

The following are equivalent for any
\mathcal{ML}
-formula
\varphi
of depth at most
L
:

•

\varphi
is preserved under embeddings,

•

\varphi
is equivalent to an
\exists\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Assume that
\varphi
is equivalent to an
\exists\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
. Since

\exists\mathcal{ML}\subseteq\exists\mathcal{GML}
, it follows from
Section

4.1
that
\varphi
is preserved under embeddings.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\varphi
be preserved under embeddings, and
let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of pointed models of
\varphi
.
Since
\varphi\in\mathcal{ML}
, the class
\mathcal{C}
is invariant
under
L
-unravellings.
By
Section

4.1
,
\mathcal{C}
is definable by a finite disjunction of characteristic formulae

\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{T},t)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}}\varphi^{L}_{\mathfrak{T},t}
, where

\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
consists of
\preceq
-minimal
tree-shaped models of height
\leq L
, for
\preceq
the embedding relation.
Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
consist of the pruning of each tree model in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
.
By
Section

4.2
, each

(\mathfrak{T},t)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathsf{min}}
is
L
-bisimilar to its pruning
and hence satisfies the same
\mathcal{ML}
-formulae of depth
\leq L
.
In particular,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is finite, since pruning retains at most one
representative of each
\mathcal{ML}_{k}
-type for
k\leq L
.
By
Section

4.2
, formula
\psi=\bigvee_{(\mathfrak{T},w)\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\varphi^{L}_{\mathfrak{T},w}
is an
\exists\mathcal{ML}
-formula.
The same argument as in the proof of
Section

4.1
shows that
\psi
defines
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

5.
Preservation Theorems for Graph Neural Networks

In this section we turn our attention to graph neural networks (GNNs)—machine learning models for graph-structured data.
We focus on one of the flagship classes of GNNs, namely aggregate-combine GNNs
(
8
)
.
When such a trained GNN
\mathcal{N}
is used for binary classification of nodes in a graph, it defines (analogously to a logical formula) a class of pointed models that it accepts.

Classes of pointed models definable by GNNs with
L
layers are preserved under
L
-unravelling
(
37
;
23
;
8
)
.
There are also striking connections between GNNs and modal logics
(
8
;
25
;
9
;
12
)
.
In particular, existing results show that a first-order property is definable by an aggregate-combine GNN if and only if
it is definable in
\mathcal{GML}

(
8
)
.
Consequently, our results on unravelling-invariant classes apply directly to GNNs yielding the following preservation theorem.

Corollary
\thethm
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be an aggregate-combine GNN with
L
layers and let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of pointed models defined by
\mathcal{N}
. Then the following
equivalences hold:

•

\mathcal{C}
is preserved under embeddings if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists\mathcal{GML}
-formula of depth at most
L
;

•

\mathcal{C}
is preserved under injective homomorphisms if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
-formula of depth at most
L
;

•

\mathcal{C}
is preserved under homomorphisms if and only if
\mathcal{C}
is definable by an
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
-formula of depth at most
L
.

In the following subsections, we show that our preservation theorems can be used to give exact logical characterisations of
the expressive power of two classes of GNNs recently studied in the literature
(
33
;
34
;
32
;
24
)
.
In particular, we show that monotonic GNNs have exactly the expressive power of
\exists^{+}\mathcal{GML}
(
Section

5.2
)
while monotonic GNNs with MAX aggregation have exactly the expressive power of
\exists^{+}\mathcal{ML}
(
Section

5.2
).

5.1.
Graph Neural Networks

We consider finite, undirected, simple, and node-labelled graphs
G=(V,E,\lambda)
, where
V
is a finite set of nodes,
E
a set of undirected edges without self-loops, and
\lambda:V\to\{0,1\}^{d}
assigns to each node a
binary vector of a fixed dimension
d
(the
dimension
of
G
).
A
pointed graph
is a pair
(G,v)
consisting of a graph and a distinguished node.
Pointed graphs of dimension
d
correspond to Kripke models over a propositional signature of size
d
in the standard way:
nodes correspond to worlds, each undirected edge
\{u,v\}
induces accessibility in both directions, and binary vectors correspond to
valuations, where
i
th vector position at a node
v
determines the truth value of the
i
th proposition at
v
.
Under this correspondence, the notions of embedding and homomorphism (and preservation thereof) extend seamlessly from Kripke models
to pointed graphs.

A
node classifier

is a function mapping
pointed graphs to
\mathsf{true}
(accepted) or
\mathsf{false}
(rejected).
Two classifiers are
equivalent
if they compute the same function.
Two families
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of classifiers have the same expressive power, written
\mathcal{F}\equiv\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, if each classifier in
\mathcal{F}
(respectively,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
) has an equivalent classifier in

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
(respectively,
\mathcal{F}
).
We consider GNN node classifiers with

aggregate-combine
layers
(
9
;
8
)
.
A layer is a pair
(\mathsf{agg},\mathsf{comb})
, where
\mathsf{agg}
is an
aggregation function
mapping multisets of vectors into single vectors and
\mathsf{comb}
is a
combination function
mapping vectors to vectors.
Applying a layer to
G=(V,E,\lambda)
yields a graph
G^{\prime}=(V,E,\lambda^{\prime})
with the same nodes and edges, but with an updated labelling function

\lambda^{\prime}
.
For each node
v\in V
, the updated label
\lambda^{\prime}(v)
is defined by

(1)

\mathsf{comb}\Big(\lambda(v),\mathsf{agg}(\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}\lambda(w)\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}_{w\in N_{G}(v)})\Big),

where
N_{G}(v)=\{w\mid\{v,w\}\in E\}
denotes the
neighbours of
v
.

Each aggregation and combination function has a fixed input and output dimension.
For the application of a layer to be well defined, these dimensions must be compatible:
if
\mathsf{agg}
has input dimension
d
and output dimension
d^{\prime}
, then
\mathsf{comb}
has input dimension
d+d^{\prime}
.
A
GNN classifier

\mathcal{N}
of dimension
d
consists of
L
aggregate-combine layers and a
classification function
\mathsf{cls}
mapping vectors to truth values.
The input dimension of the first layer is
d
, so that the output dimension of layer
\ell
matches the input dimension of layer
\ell+1
.
We write
\lambda^{(\ell)}(v)
for the vector associated with node
v
after applying layer
\ell
, with
\lambda^{(0)}(v)=\lambda(v)
and
\lambda^{(L)}(v)
the final representation.
The output of
\mathcal{N}
on a pointed graph
(G,v)
is then defined as
the truth value
\mathcal{N}(G,v)=\mathsf{cls}\bigl(\lambda^{(L)}(v)\bigr)
.

5.2.
Expressive Power of Monotonic GNNs

For vectors
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the same dimension, we write

\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
if
x_{i}\leq x_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in[d]
.
For multisets
M
and
M^{\prime}
of vectors of the same dimension, we write
M\leq M^{\prime}
if there exists an injection
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{x}\leq f(\mathbf{x})
, for every
\mathbf{x}\in M
.
For example,

\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}[-1,2],[0,1]\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}\;\leq\;\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}[-1,2],[0,3],[-1,-1]\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}},

as witnessed by the injection
f
defined as
f([-1,2])=[-1,2]
and
f([0,1])=[0,3]
.

A function
f
mapping vectors or multisets to vectors or numbers—such as combination, aggregation, or classification—is
non-decreasing
if
x\leq y
implies
f(x)\leq f(y)
for all admissible arguments
x,y
.

A GNN is
monotonic
if all its aggregation functions, combination functions, and its
final classification function are non-decreasing.

A
positive-weight GNN
is a GNN where each layer
\ell
has the form:

ReLU\Big(\mathbf{b}_{\ell}+\lambda(v)\mathbf{A}_{\ell}+\mathsf{agg}_{\ell}\big(\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}\lambda(w)\mid w\in N_{G}(v)\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}\big)\mathbf{C}_{\ell}\Big)

where
\mathbf{b}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\ell}}
,
\mathbf{A}_{\ell},\mathbf{C}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d_{\ell-1}\times d_{\ell}}
,

\mathsf{agg}_{\ell}
is one of
\mathrm{SUM}
,
\mathrm{MAX}
, or
\mathrm{max}\text{-}k\text{-}\mathrm{SUM}
,
and the classification function
\mathsf{cls}
is a threshold function of the form

\mathsf{cls}(\mathbf{x})=1
iff
x_{i}\geq t
(or
x_{i}>t
) for all
i
, for some threshold
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition
\thethm
.

Positive-weight GNNs using MAX, SUM, or max-k-sum aggregation are monotonic. In contrast, positive-weight GNNs using
MEAN aggregation are not monotonic.

Proof.

In all these GNNs, the combination function is monotonic, since the ReLU activation function
is monotonic and, for each layer
\ell
, all entries of the weight matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\ell}
and
\mathbf{C}_{\ell}
are non-negative.
The aggregation functions MAX, SUM, and max-k-sum are also monotonic with respect to the multiset order.
In contrast, MEAN is not monotonic. Indeed,

\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}1\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}\leq\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}1,0\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}
, but

MEAN(\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}1\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}})=1\not\leq 0.5=MEAN(\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}1,0\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}})
.
∎

Theorem
\thethm
.

Monotonic GNNs are preserved under injective homomorphisms, but not under arbitrary homomorphisms.

Proof.

Let
h\colon(G,v)\to(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be an injective homomorphism between pointed graphs, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a monotonic GNN such that
\mathcal{N}(G,v)=1
.
We show that
\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})=1
.

Since the classification function
\mathsf{cls}
is non-decreasing, it suffices to show that
\lambda^{(L)}(v)\leq\lambda^{(L)}(v^{\prime})
, for
L
the number of layers of
\mathcal{N}
.
We prove by induction on
\ell\leq L
that
\lambda^{(\ell)}(w)\leq\lambda^{(\ell)}(h(w))
, for every node
w
in
G
.

The base case
(\ell=0)
holds because
h
preserves node labels. For the inductive step, assume the claim
holds for
\ell<L
. Since
h
is an injective homomorphism, it induces an injection from the multiset of
neighbours of
w
in
G
to the multiset of neighbours of
h(w)
in
G^{\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis, this
yields
\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}\lambda^{(\ell)}(u)\mid u\in N_{G}(w)\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}\leq\mathopen{\{\mkern-3.0mu|}\lambda^{(\ell)}(u^{\prime})\mid u^{\prime}\in N_{G^{\prime}}(h(w))\mathclose{|\mkern-3.0mu\}}
.
By monotonicity of the aggregation and combination functions, we conclude that
\lambda^{(\ell+1)}(w)\leq\lambda^{(\ell+1)}(h(w))
.
Hence,
\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})=1
.

To see that monotonic GNNs are not preserved under arbitrary homomorphisms, let
G=(V,E,\lambda)
with
V=\{v,u,w\}
,
E=\{\{v,u\},\{v,w\}\}
,
\lambda(v)=\lambda(u)=\lambda(w)=1
, and let
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
with
V^{\prime}=\{v^{\prime},u^{\prime}\}
,
E=\{\{v^{\prime},u^{\prime}\}\}
, and
\lambda^{\prime}(v)=\lambda^{\prime}(u)=1
.
Define
\mathcal{N}
with a single layer, aggregation function SUM, parameters
\mathbf{b}=0
and
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{C}=1
, and classification function

\mathsf{cls}(x)=1
iff
x\geq 3
.
Then,
\mathcal{N}(G,v)=1
, but
\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})=0
.
However, the function
f
defined by
f(v)=v^{\prime}
,
f(u)=u^{\prime}
and
f(w)=u^{\prime}
is a (non-injective) homomorphism from
(G,v)
to
(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})
.
∎

The above subsumes the result that monotonic max-
k
-sum GNNs are closed under injective homomorphisms
(
33
, Proposition 7)
.

A
monotonic-MAX GNN
is a monotonic GNN using only MAX as aggregation function.
The following theorem isolates MAX aggregation as a case in which injectivity is no longer required

Theorem
\thethm
.

Monotonic-MAX GNNs are preserved under arbitrary homomorphisms.

Proof.

The proof follows the same general structure as that of
Section

5.2
.
Let
h\colon(G,v)\to(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})
be a homomorphism between pointed graphs, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a monotonic-MAX GNN with
\mathcal{N}(G,v)=1
.
We show that
\mathcal{N}(G^{\prime},v^{\prime})=1
.
As before, since the classification function
\mathsf{cls}
is non-decreasing, it suffices to show that

\lambda^{(L)}(v)\leq\lambda^{(L)}(v^{\prime})
, where
L
is the number of layers in
\mathcal{N}
.

To this end, we prove by induction on

Conclusions

In this paper, we developed a uniform approach to preservation theorems for classes of models that are invariant under unravelling,
covering both (graded) modal logics and graph neural networks.
Our results establish a correspondence between semantic preservation properties and logical definability:
preservation under embeddings coincides with definability in existential graded modal logic,
preservation under injective homomorphisms with definability in existential-positive graded modal logic, and
preservation under homomorphisms with definability in existential-positive modal logic.
Technically, our approach relies on a structural well-quasi-ordering result for tree-shaped models of uniform bounded height.
This framework allows us to obtain new preservation theorems for graded modal logic, to recover and subsume classical results for basic modal
logic over finite structures, and to provide alternative proofs avoiding compactness and Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé games.
Furthermore, our results on GNNs place recent expressive power analyses of GNNs in a unified logical framework using elegant techniques
from finite model theory.

Our work opens several directions for future research.
On the logical side, it is natural to investigate whether similar preservation theorems can be established for extensions of modal logic, such as
logics with nominals, global modalities, or fixed-point operators, as well as for restricted fragments, including Horn or core variants.
On the algorithmic and machine-learning side, an important open question is whether our proof strategy—based on unravelling invariance, well-quasi-orders, and minimal models—can be adapted to analyse the expressive power of other classes of graph neural networks, such as architectures
with attention, normalization, or higher-order message passing.
We believe that further interaction between finite model theory and the theory of GNNs will continue to yield fruitful insights in both areas.
