Association between lactate to albumin ratio and 28-day all-cause mortality in patients with extracorporeal membrane oxygenation: a retrospective multi-cohort study
Authors: Jin Zhao, Chenyang Xu, Yuan Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe relationship between lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR) and mortality in patients with cardiogenic etiology undergoing veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO) remains unclear. Against this backdrop, the present study is designed to explore the correlation between LAR and 28-day mortality among patients supported by VA-ECMO.MethodsThis study was a multicenter retrospective study that included patients undergone VA-ECMO from Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care-IV (MIMIC-IV) database and the First People’s Hospital of Kunshan in China. The primary outcome was 28-day in-hospital mortality. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, multivariable Cox regression, and restricted cubic splines (RCS) were used to assess the association between LAR and mortality. Model performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Subgroup analyses were used to test interactions. ResultsIn total, 154 patients (78 patients in Chinese database and 76 patients in MIMIC database) participated in this study. LAR were associated with 28-days mortality significantly as a continuous variable (HR: 1.16, 95% CI: 1.05 ~ 1.28, P < 0.01) or categorical variable (HR 5.67, 95% CI: 1.58 ~ 17.23, P < 0.01) in Chinese database. The same result was also observed in the MIMIC group. RCS analysis model illustrated the linear relationship between higher LAR and 28-day mortality in patients with VA-ECMO. ROC analysis suggest that LAR holds less predictive value, when combined with APACHII or APSIII scores the improvement is also small.ConclusionsLAR is associated with 28-day mortality in patients with cardiogenic etiology undergone VA-ECMO which offers valuable prognostic marker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12872-026-06112-0.

Introduction

Veno-arterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA-ECMO) has emerged as a pivotal therapeutic modality for patients with refractory cardiogenic shock or cardiac arrest (ECPR) [1, 2]. By providing temporary circulatory and respiratory support, VA-ECMO creates a critical time window for treating the underlying disease and facilitating cardiac function recovery. Despite ongoing advancements in ECMO technology, anticoagulation protocols, and patient management strategies, in-hospital mortality rates among VA-ECMO supported patients remain alarmingly high, ranging from 40% to 60% [3]. The substantial medical costs and limited intensive care resources underscore the importance of early and accurate high-risk patient identification, optimized resource allocation, and individualized treatment implementation.‌

Lactate (Lac) and albumin (Alb) are two classical and pathophysiologically distinct markers in critical care medicine [4]. Lac, a key end product of tissue hypoperfusion and anaerobic metabolism, serves as a crucial indicator of tissue oxygenation and the effectiveness of circulatory resuscitation. Its dynamic changes have been robustly associated with mortality in conditions such as sepsis and cardiogenic shock [5, 6]. Alb, the primary plasma protein, reflects the severity of acute inflammatory response, capillary leakage, and deterioration in nutritional status [7], low albuminemia is not merely a consequence of disease; it can also significantly exacerbate the clinical outcomes in critically ill patients.‌ In VA-ECMO patients, low Alb levels signal a state of severe systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and compromised physiological reserve, factors directly linked to worse clinical outcomes and mortality [8]. Lac, primarily reflects the severity of tissue hypoxia and the degree of circulatory failure. Alb,‌ Reflects the magnitude of the inflammatory host response and endothelial injury. In recent years, there has been a growing body of research focused on prognostic biomarkers for patients with cardiogenic shock supported by VA-ECMO, such as NT-proBNP [9]. However, the NT-proBNP indicator is limited by its singularity and susceptibility to factors such as renal function, age, and gender. Recently, studies have proposed combining these two markers into the lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR), aiming to integrate the pathological processes of impaired tissue perfusion and systemic inflammation, malnutrition as a composite prognostic index. Preliminary evidence suggests that LAR exhibits independent prognostic value in patients with sepsis, severe pancreatitis, and trauma [10–12], potentially offering greater sensitivity than either marker alone. LAR’s superiority stems from its ability to concurrently assess the ‌double hit‌ of critical illness in VA-ECMO patients. Thus, ‌LAR contextualizes tissue hypoperfusion within the framework of the patient’s inflammatory and synthetic status, potentially offering a more holistic and sensitive reflection of overall physiological derangement.‌ In the specific, high-stakes context of VA-ECMO, where patients are exceptionally vulnerable to the combined effects of shock induced hypoperfusion and the profound systemic insult triggering ECMO initiation, a biomarker like LAR that captures this integrative pathophysiology is particularly compelling. However, LAR has not been systematically investigated in patients supported by VA-ECMO, whose condition is primarily driven by acute cardiogenic dysfunction and characterized by extreme hemodynamic instability.‌

This study aims to address this critical knowledge gap. We hypothesize that among patients receiving VA-ECMO support for cardiogenic causes, LAR is associated with 28-day all-cause mortality. To test this hypothesis, we conducted a multicenter retrospective study using data from the publicly available MIMIC-IV database in the United States and clinical records from Kunshan First People’s Hospital in China. The findings of this study may provide clinicians for early risk stratification, allowing for more precise identification of high-risk VA-ECMO patients with poor prognosis. This could guide adjustments in support strategies and optimize therapeutic decision-making, with the ultimate goal of improving survival outcomes in this vulnerable population through precise prognostic assessment.

Method

Data sources

This multi-center study utilized data from the Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care IV (MIMIC-IV 3.1) between 2008 and 2022, Kunshan First People’s Hospital in China from April 2018 to January 2026. The MIMIC database is a publicly available critical care repository jointly developed by Massachusetts Institute of Technology researchers and clinical investigators at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center [13]. Data extraction was conducted by certified author Jin Zhao (Certification ID: 67544010) in full compliance with HIPAA Safe Harbor provisions

Study populations

Patients diagnosed with cardiogenic shock who need VA-ECMO to maintain vital signs were selected in the study. Cardiogenic shock is a life threatening clinical syndrome characterized by severe failure of the heart’s pumping function, leading to a significant decrease in cardiac output, inability to maintain basic systemic circulation, and consequently, severe hypoperfusion of tissues and organs throughout the body. The etiology includes acute myocardial infarction (AMI), myocarditis and valvular heart disease‌‌‌‌. Patients in the MIMIC database were selected based on ICD-9 and ICD-10. Exclusion criteria include: under 18 years old, VA-ECMO and ICU stay duration less than 24 h, severe irreversible organ failure (excluding the heart), such as malignant tumors or other organ dysfunction or failure, and loss of Lac and Alb. Ultimately, we included 76 patients in MIMIC and 78 patients in Chinese database (Fig. 1).

Variable extraction

We use PostgreSQL software to extract the variables from MIMIC IV 3.1. Demographic characteristics include gender, age; vital signs include heart rate. Comorbidities: Hypertension, Diabetes. Complications include acute kidney injury (AKI), acute myocardial infarction (AMI). Treatment interventions included:‌ Renal Replacement Therapy (RRT), Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR). Laboratory data include PH, blood Lactate (Lac, mmol/L), White Blood Cell (WBC, 10^9/L), Neutrophilic Granulocyte (NE, 10^9/L), Red Blood Cell (RBC, 10^9/L), Platelet count (PLT, 10^9/L), Hemoglobin‌ (HB, g/dL), Albumin (Alb, g/L), Prothrombin Time (PT, s), Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time (APTT, s), International Normalized Ratio (INR), Creatinine (Cr, mg/L‌‌)), ‌fasting blood glucose (FBG, mg/dL), aspartate aminotransferase (AST, IU/L), alanine aminotransferase (ALT, IU/L), doses of vasoactive agents (Vasoactive-Inotropic Score, VIS). Etiology: AMI, myocarditis and valvular heart disease‌‌‌‌. All data were obtained from the first recorded values within the initial 24-hour period following ICU admission. LAR was calculated as: LAR = Lac (mmol/L) /Alb (g/L). Clinical outcome include length of stay in ICU and hospital, and 28-day in-hospital mortality status. Severity scores include acute physiology score III (APSIII) and Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and groups compared with one-way ANOVA, other variables as median (IQR) and compared with Kruskal-Wallis test. Categorical variables are expressed as percentages and compared with Chi-square or Fisher’s exact. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards (PH) models estimated LAR’s link to 28-day mortality. We used Schoenfeld residual test and logarithmic negative log survival curve to verify the PH hypothesis of Cox PH model. Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 was adjusted for gender, age; Model 3 was additionally adjusted for Hypertension, Diabetes, AKI, RRT, WBC, RBC, PLT, VIS. Nonlinear trends‌ in the LAR mortality association were ‌examined using restricted cubic splines‌. Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curve is utilized to evaluate‌ how the LAR mortality association varies among different subpopulations. Subgroup analysis was conducted to verify the consistency of the association between LAR and mortality. Discrimination capacity of LAR‌ was determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, with Area Under the Curve (AUC) used as the summary metric. Variables with missing data exceeding 5% were excluded‌ to ensure data quality and analytical validity. Missing values in variables with ≤ 5% missing data were filled using multiple imputation‌, generating several plausible datasets to reflect uncertainty (sTable1 and 2). All analyses were performed using R software version 4.5.1 within RStudio. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Baseline characteristics

The study population comprised 78 patients in Chinese database and 76 patients in MIMIC database meeting selection criteria (Fig. 1), categorized into survival and non-survival groups. In the Chinese database, the mortality rate of VA-ECMO patients was 64%, while in the MIMIC database it was 56%. ROC curve analysis

We conducted a comparative evaluation of the prognostic utility of LAR against Lac and Alb levels in predicting the risk of 28-day mortality in different groups. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that LAR achieved a significantly higher AUC than both Lac and Alb. As an independent predictor, LAR demonstrated an AUC of 0.67 (95%CI: 0.54 ~ 0.80) in assessing 28-day mortality in Chinese database, APACHE II is 0.81 (95%CI: 0.71 ~ 0.91), APACHE II + LAR is 0.82 (95%CI: 0.72 ~ 0.91). The best cutoff is 1.31. In the Mimic database, the AUC of LAR alone is 0.61 (95%CI: 0.48 ~ 0.74), APACHII alone is 0.70 (95%CI: 0.58–0.82), for combine LAR is 0.71 (95%CI: 0.59 ~ 0.82). The best cut-off is 0.70. While LAR demonstrates limited standalone predictive value for 28-day mortality, its inclusion as a composite component may improve predictive mortality, but the improvement is small. Subgroup analysis

The link between LAR and 28-day mortality was examined across diverse demographic and clinical subgroups to confirm the stability of the findings. No evidence of interaction were detected for in gender, age, AKI, CPR, AMI, Hypertension, Diabetes group (all P for interaction > 0.05, Fig. 5). In age groups, patients were stratified into two groups based on the median age. However, a statistically significant interaction was observed between LAR and RRT group (P for interaction < 0.05), with differing mortality risk patterns emerging between patients who did and did not receive RRT in MIMIC database while it’s not occur in Chinese database. This finding is exploratory and warrants validation in independent cohorts.

Discussion

Based on a comprehensive multi-center retrospective analysis, the present study demonstrates that LAR is associated with 28-day mortality in patients receiving VA-ECMO support for cardiogenic etiologies. Stability of the association was further affirmed through subgroup analyses and interaction tests, indicating consistent findings across different patient populations within a multicenter framework.

In the Chinese database, the mortality rate of VA-ECMO patients was 64%, while in the MIMIC database it was 56%. This difference may reflect variations in healthcare systems, patient baseline characteristics, clinical management strategies, or data inclusion criteria. For instance, the Chinese cohort might include a higher proportion of high risk or late referred patients, whereas the MIMIC database, as a publicly database in the United States, may represent a population with better access to resources, higher rates of early intervention, or more optimized comorbidity management. Additionally, differences between the two cohorts in etiological composition such as proportions of AMI, myocarditis, timing of ECMO initiation, anticoagulation strategies, and levels of multi-organ support could also influence the final mortality outcomes [14, 15]. Despite this 8% point gap, the independent association between LAR and mortality remains robust in both cohorts, suggesting the potential value of this biomarker across different clinical settings.

LAR has been confirmed by multiple high-quality studies as a robust prognostic biomarker across diseases and populations, and validated in multiple critical care fields. Liu. et al. found that elevated LAR is positively correlated with 90-day mortality in patients with cardiogenic shock [16]. LAR also has a certain predictive value for short-term mortality, and An’s research shows that high LAR is associated with 30-day mortality in sepsis patients [17]. In our study, through multivariable Cox regression analysis, when LAR treated as a categorical variable, patients with higher levels had significantly increased mortality risk. The optimal cut-off value for LAR was higher in Chinese database (1.31) compared to the MIMIC cohort (0.70), which aligns with the observed 8% higher mortality rate in the Chinese database. This finding further supports that elevated LAR is associated with greater mortality risk in VA-ECMO patients. Differing healthcare system contexts, single center Chinese database and multi-center MIMIC database, clinical management practices, such as VA-ECMO implantation criteria, fluid management strategies, and rates of early RRT initiation, likely contributed to variations in the composition of illness severity and treatment responses, indirectly affecting the distribution of physiological biomarkers. However, despite the difference in optimal cut-offs, LAR as a ‌continuous‌ risk indicator demonstrated a ‌monotonic, linear positive association‌ with 28-day mortality in ‌both‌ cohorts. Furthermore, even when dichotomized using cohort-specific thresholds, the high-LAR group maintained a statistically significant association with increased mortality risk in both cohorts. Therefore, the discrepancy in cut-off values ‌does not negate the consistency of LAR’s core prognostic value. Therefore, we have demonstrated through various statistical methods that elevated LAR is associated with increased mortality in VA-ECMO patients and can serve as a reliable predictor for evaluating the prognosis of this population. The potential mechanisms include: (1) VA-ECMO patients often suffer from severe cardiogenic shock, leading to a sharp decline in cardiac output and inadequate tissue perfusion [18]. Cells are forced to rely on glycolysis for energy, resulting in massive lactate production and accumulation [19]. Reduced albumin synthesis and capillary leakage‌: Systemic inflammatory response, elevated IL-6 and CRP [20], suppresses hepatic albumin synthesis [21], while endothelial damage in capillaries leads to plasma protein extravasation, causing a significant decrease in Alb levels [22]. Albumin itself possesses protective functions such as anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and maintenance of colloid osmotic pressure [23–25]. Its reduction further aggravates tissue edema and organ perfusion impairment. LAR as an integrative marker of metabolic, inflammation imbalance‌. Elevated LAR signifies the collapse of the body’s compensatory mechanisms. Higher LAR is associated with worse prognosis‌. However, ‌the relationship is not universally linear or absolute‌. VA-ECMO implantation is a response to profound cardiogenic shock, a state of extreme circulatory failure. Yuan Z et al.‘s study shows that the combination of VA-ECMO and IABP can significantly improve myocardial function in patients with fulminant myocarditis and reduce in-hospital mortality [26]. From a hemodynamic perspective, these patients exhibit severely depressed cardiac output, leading to systemic hypoperfusion, a core driver of elevated lactate due to tissue-level anaerobic metabolism. The magnitude of this lactate elevation reflects the severity of the circulatory insult and the extent of tissue injury [27]. Conversely, hypoalbuminemia in this cohort stems from a multifactorial insult. The profound systemic inflammatory response, leads to widespread endothelial dysfunction and capillary leakage [8]. This results in the rapid loss of albumin into the interstitial space. Unlike conventional shock indicators, LAR encapsulates this double-hit pathophysiology, where poor perfusion damages organs while simultaneously, systemic inflammation and vascular leak exacerbate that damage and hinder recovery. This pathophysiological basis underscores LAR’s value in VA-ECMO patients. There are specific contexts where a higher LAR does not directly translate to higher mortality. Studies have shown that when LAR > 2.15, it is not associated with 28-day mortality in patients with AMI in eICU-CRD database [28]. In this study, RCS curve analysis revealed a significant overall linear correlation between LAR and 28-day mortality, further supporting the reliability of LAR as a prognostic indicator for VA-ECMO patients.

Subgroup analysis demonstrated a statistically significant effect of RRT on the association between LAR and 28-day mortality in the MIMIC database. In the Non-use subgroup, the hazard ratio (HR) was 1.39 (95% CI: 1.12 ~ 1.72), with a P-value < 0.01 and a P-value for interaction of 0.01, indicating a robust positive association. In contrast, the use subgroup did not exhibit a significant association (HR: 1.05, 95% CI: 0.89 ~ 1.24, P-value = 0.12). The mortality rate of patients receiving RRT during VA-ECMO is significantly higher, which is consistent with previous research results [29]. However, this differential effect was not replicated in the Chinese database [30]. These disparities in patient population and healthcare access could partly explain the differences in results between the Chinese and MIMIC databases. Furthermore, our findings underscore the importance of a ‌proactive, protocol driven approach to Acute Kidney Injury‌ and volume management‌ in VA-ECMO patients to potentially mitigate the need for emergent RRT or optimize the timing of its initiation. The observed subgroup interaction, although not replicated in the Chinese cohort, offers a hypothesis generating avenue for future research. Future prospective studies should specifically examine how ‌dynamic LAR values before and after initiation of RRT‌, in conjunction with hemodynamic parameters and fluid balance, might modify prognostic associations.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, this is a retrospective study, despite incorporating patient data from multiple centers, the relatively small sample size, a total of 154 patients may impact statistical power and the reliability of subgroup analyses. Secondly, the study did not account for several potentially important variables that could influence patient outcomes, such as details on pre-ECMO support, specific anticoagulation protocols, the use of concomitant organ support therapies like IABP or Impella [31, 32], and underlying genetic or biomarker factors. Finally, the calculation of LAR relied on single-point measurements of Lac and Alb, failing to capture their dynamic trajectories, which might contain more informative prognostic value. All data were obtained from the first recorded values after ICU admission. There is a time interval between ICU admission and VA-ECMO initiation‌, but we can’t recollect data before VA-ECMO initiation in critically ill patients, as it would delay life saving treatment. In further, large-scale, multi-center prospective cohort studies are urgently needed to dynamically collect lactate and albumin data at strictly predefined time points such as 0, 6, 12, and 24 h after ECMO initiation in order to establish the optimal predictive model and clinical cut-off values for dynamic LAR.

Conclusion

Our findings reveal a linear association between LAR and 28-day all-cause mortality in VA-ECMO patients, with higher ratios indicating worse outcomes. Future dynamic monitoring of LAR is needed to optimize its predictive value.
