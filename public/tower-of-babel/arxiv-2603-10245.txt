Over-the-Air Consensus-based Formation Control of Heterogeneous Agents: Communication-Rate and Geometry-Aware Convergence Guarantees
Michael Epp, Fabio Molinari, Jörg Raisch

Abstract
This paper investigates the formation control problem of heterogeneous, autonomous agents that communicate over a wireless multiple access channel. Instead of avoiding interference through orthogonal node-to-node transmissions, we exploit the superposition property of the wireless channel to compute, at each receiver, normalized convex combinations of simultaneously broadcast neighbor signals. At every communication instant, agents update their reference positions from these aggregates, and track the references in continuous time between updates. The only assumption on the agent dynamics is that each agent tracks constant reference positions exponentially, which accommodates a broad class of platforms. Under this assumption, we analyze the resulting jump-flow system under time-varying communication graphs and unknown channel coefficients. We derive a communication-rate based sufficient condition that guarantees convergence to a prescribed formation. We then provide a geometry-aware refinement showing how favorable tracking transients can relax the required condition. Simulations with unicycle agents illustrate the theoretical results and demonstrate a substantial reduction in the number of required orthogonal transmissions compared to interference-avoiding node-to-node communication protocols.



I
Introduction

Autonomous multi-agent systems (MAS) have gained significant attention in science and engineering, motivated by applications such as cooperative robotics, unmanned vehicle teams, and networked sensing.
A key driver is that many collective objectives can be achieved without centralized coordination by using distributed control laws that rely only on local information exchange, while remaining scalable and robust to failure and topology changes
[
14
,
12
,
8
]
.

Formation control is a central coordination problem in MAS, where the objective is to achieve and maintain a prescribed spatial arrangement using only local interaction rules.
Two broad paradigms are commonly considered.
Inter-agent measurements can be used to encode the desired formation in relative measurements between neighboring agents, without a global reference variable
[
4
,
6
]
.
On the other hand, in centroid-based formation control, agents agree on a common reference point and realize the formation through prescribed displacement vectors with respect to that reference
[
13
,
19
]
.
In the latter case, distributed consensus mechanisms are a natural tool to establish agreement on the shared reference point.
Consensus algorithms under directed and time-varying interaction topologies have been extensively studied, providing conditions that connect connectivity properties of the communication graph to convergence guarantees
[
12
,
2
,
17
]
.

In many robotic systems, wireless communication resources are limited.
Traditional multi-agent coordination controls often presume that pairwise communication can be implemented reliably, implicitly relying on interference-avoidance mechanisms.
In dense swarms, however, allocating orthogonal channels to all links is costly and can introduce latency that degrades closed-loop performance.
An alternative is leveraging the superposition property of wireless signals, a concept known as over-the-air (OTA) computation
[
7
]
.
This approach, identified as a promising candidate technology for 6G wireless communication systems
[
18
]
, turns traditional interference challenges into a communication advantage.

For MAS, OTA computation naturally enables communication-efficient consensus control.
In particular,
[
10
]
proposed a consensus protocol that uses simultaneous broadcasts and receiver-side normalization so that each agent can obtain a convex combination of its neighbors’ values without decoding individual transmissions, reducing the need of orthogonal channels and offering robustness to unknown channel gains.
Building on this principle, consensus-based formation control for single-integrator agents was studied in
[
10
]
and later extended to include collision avoidance in
[
5
]
.
The consensus-based formation control of non-holonomic agents was studied in
[
1
]
, which established sufficient conditions under which a swarm of agents establishes the formation.

This paper develops an OTA, consensus-based formation control scheme for heterogeneous agents under sampled wireless communication.
Rather than assuming identical agent dynamics, we require only that each agent’s position is exponentially stabilizable to any constant reference, covering a broad range of platforms, including unicycle and differential drive models
[
3
]
and car-like models
[
16
]
.
For the resulting jump-flow system, we derive a communication-rate based sufficient condition for convergence to the desired formation and provide a geometry-aware refinement that captures how the tracking transient can relax the required communication-rate bound, thereby generalizing earlier formation control results in
[
10
]
and
[
1
]
.

In Section

II
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{N}=\{1,\dots,n\}
index a set of heterogeneous autonomous agents moving on a two-dimensional plane.
Agent
i
has position
p_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and control input
u_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
, with
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, and evolves according to

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{i}\left(t,x_{i}(t),u_{i}(t)\right),\quad x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}

(2)

\displaystyle p_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=g_{i}(t,x_{i}(t)),

(3)

where
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are functions of appropriate dimensions.
This formulation allows heterogeneous agent dynamics, while the formation objective described in terms of the position output
p_{i}(t)
.
Moreover, we assume the agents are dynamically independent in the sense that (
2
) and (
3
) do not depend on other agents’ states.
Inter-agent coupling is solely introduced through the feedback law used to generate
u_{i}(t)
.
Additionally, we impose the following assumption on the controller.

Assumption 1
.

For each agent
i\in\mathcal{N}
there exist constants
C_{i},\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that, for any constant reference position
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there exists an input signal
u_{i}(t)
for which the corresponding solution satisfies, for all
t\geq\tau\geq 0
,

\|p_{i}(t)-r_{i}\|\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}(t-\tau)}\|p_{i}(\tau)-r_{i}\|.

(4)

Remark
.

At this point, we only impose exponential stability of an agent’s position to its reference.
This assumption is satisfied by many common mobile robot models such as differential drive and car-like kinematics under regulation control
[
3
,
16
]
.

However, we do not impose any additional geometric properties on the resulting trajectories.
In Section
V
, we derive convergence conditions that admit a geometric interpretation, indicating how properties of the tracking behavior affect consensus.

The agents exchange information at sampling times
t_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, with uniformly bounded inter-sampling times, i.e., there exist
\check{T},\hat{T}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, such that

\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},~\check{T}\leq t_{k+1}-t_{k}\leq\hat{T}.

(5)

We aim to achieve a prescribed formation described by displacement vectors
d_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
This is achieved asymptotically if,

\forall i\in\mathcal{N},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}p_{i}(t)=c(t)+d_{i},

(6)

where
c(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the so-called centroid of the formation.

III
Communication Model

This study adopts the WMAC model to describe the superposition property (interference) of the wireless medium, i.e., the effect of multiple signals being simultaneously transmitted in the same frequency band.
Interference has traditionally been avoided by using more wireless resources, e.g., by creating orthogonal transmission (each agent is assigned its own time or frequency slot).
Instead of avoiding interference, however, we let agents broadcast simultaneously, giving rise to the following definition.

Definition 1

(Wireless Multiple Access Channel)
.

Let all agents in
\mathcal{N}
simultaneously broadcast
\alpha_{j}(t_{k})\in\mathbb{R}
.
The obtained superimposed value at the receiving agent is

y_{i}(t_{k})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}(t_{k})}\xi_{ij}(t_{k})\alpha_{j}(t_{k}),

(7)

where
\xi_{ij}(t_{k})\in(0,1]
are unknown time-varying channel coefficients.
Since the received superimposed value is a result of power modulation, the channel coefficients are assumed to be positive.

For normalization purposes,
[
11
]
proposed to additionally broadcast a known value, e.g.,
1
via an orthogonal channel (i.e., in a different time slot or frequency range).
To transmit a vector
\mu_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we apply the WMAC model element-wise and allocate an orthogonal channel to each component.
If we use TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access), we assume that the delays between the individual broadcasts of the entries of the vector
\mu_{j}
and the normalization signal are so small that they can be considered to occur at the same time
t_{k}
, i.e., the channel coefficients will not change between these broadcasts.

According to the WMAC model and using the notation
\nu_{i}(t_{k})=\nu_{i,k}
, all agents
i\in\mathcal{N}
then receive for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle\nu_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i,k}}\xi_{ij,k}\mu_{j,k}

(8)

\displaystyle\nu_{i,k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i,k}}\xi_{ij,k}.

(9)

In the following, we will assume that
\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\forall i\in\mathcal{N},i\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
, i.e.,
\xi_{ii,k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Therefore
\nu_{i,k}^{\prime}
is positive and the vector

\zeta_{i,k}\coloneqq\frac{\nu_{i,k}}{\nu_{i,k}^{\prime}}

(10)

is well defined.
Clearly,

\zeta_{i,k}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i,k}}h_{ij,k}\mu_{j,k},

(11)

where

h_{ij,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{\xi_{ij,k}}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i,k}}\xi_{ij,k}}&\text{if }(j,i)\in\mathcal{A}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(12)

are the normalized channel coefficients.

By construction,
h_{ij,k}\in[0,1]
, and

\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}=1.

(13)

Hence,
\zeta_{i,k}
is in the convex hull of the
\mu_{j,k}
,
j\in\mathcal{N}
.
It encapsulates aggregated information from neighboring agents.

Corollary 1
.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the matrix
H_{k}
, with
[H_{k}]_{ij}=h_{ij,k}
, is row-stochastic.

Assumption 2
.

There exist
L\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}>0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\tau_{1}(H_{k+L-1}\cdots H_{k})\leq 1-\mu.

(14)

Remark
.

Assumption
2
is a standard connectivity requirement for consensus over time-varying networks.
Within every block of
L
consecutive updates, the product
H_{k+L-1}\cdots H_{k}
is sufficiently ergodic to ensure a uniform contraction of disagreement.
The assumption is satisfied, for example, under joint strong connectivity of the communication graphs together with a uniform positive lower bound on the nonzero weights of
H_{k}
.
These conditions are classical in distributed control and exclude switching patterns that keep the network essentially disconnected over arbitrarily long periods.

IV
Control Strategy

In this section we present the reference-update rule induced by the communication model described in Section
III
.
For each communication instant
t_{k}
, let
p_{i,k^{+}}
and
r_{i,k^{+}}
denote, respectively, the position of agent
i
and its reference immediately after the update.
The resulting closed-loop system consists of jump and flow dynamics, accounting for the discrete-time communication instants and the continuous-time motion in between them.

Jump Dynamics

At each communication instant
t_{k}
, agents broadcast their displacement-shifted position
p_{i,k}-d_{i}
.
The reference position for agent
i
is then updated as, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

r_{i,k^{+}}=d_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}(p_{j,k}-d_{j}).

(15)

Since the physical position does not jump at communication instants, we have, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

p_{i,k^{+}}=p_{i,k}.

(16)

Flow Dynamics

Between two adjacent communication instants, the references are held constant, i.e., for all
t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}],k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

r_{i}(t)=r_{i,k^{+}},

(17)

and in particular
r_{i,k+1}=r_{i,k^{+}}
.
Under Assumption
1
, the agents then converge exponentially to their individual reference positions, i.e., for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\|p_{i,k+1}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|.

(18)

V
Convergence Analysis

In this section we first provide a general bound on
\check{T}
under which the system achieves convergence and establishes the desired formation.
We then continue the analysis by exploiting additional geometric information about the tracking transient.

General Convergence Condition

We start by introducing the displacement-shifted state variables
\tilde{p}_{i}(t)\coloneqq p_{i}(t)-d_{i}
and
\tilde{r}_{i,k}\coloneqq r_{i,k}-d_{i}
.
The jump-flow system in (
15
) to (
18
) can then be rewritten as

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}\tilde{p}_{j,k},

(19)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{i,k^{+}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{p}_{i,k}

(20)

at jumps for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}},

(21)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}_{i,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|

(22)

during flow intervals for all
t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]
.
By (
6
), the desired formation is therefore achieved if and only if
\forall i,j,\,\tilde{p}_{i}(t)=\tilde{p}_{j}(t)
.

Additionally, we denote stacked vectors by omitting the agent related subscript, i.e., we have
\tilde{p}_{k}=[\tilde{p}_{1,k}^{\top},\dots,\tilde{p}_{n,k}^{\top}]^{\top}
and
\tilde{r}_{k}=[\tilde{r}_{1,k}^{\top},\dots,\tilde{r}_{n,k}^{\top}]^{\top}
.

In order to analyze the disagreement in the system, we define the seminorm

\Delta(x)=\max_{i,j}\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|,

(23)

for any stacked vector
x=[x_{1}^{\top},\dots,x_{n}^{\top}]^{\top}
,
\forall i,\,x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
It satisfies the usual seminorm properties

\displaystyle\Delta(x)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(24)

\displaystyle\Delta(\alpha x)

\displaystyle=|\alpha|\Delta(x),\,\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

(25)

\displaystyle\Delta(x+y)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta(x)+\Delta(y),

(26)

and in addition

\Delta(x)=0\iff\forall i,j,\,x_{i}=x_{j}.

(27)

The seminorm (
23
) can therefore be used as a measure of disagreement in a system and is zero exactly when that system reaches consensus.
Additionally, for any row-stochastic matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we have by
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
,

\Delta((A\otimes I_{2})x)\leq\tau_{1}(A)\Delta(x).

(28)

With these definitions at hand, we are able to state the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied.
Then there exists a constant
\check{T}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, such that if
\check{T}>\check{T}^{*}
, i.e.,

\forall k,\,t_{k+1}-t_{k}>\check{T}^{*},

(29)

the jump-flow system in (
15
) to (
18
) achieves the desired formation in the sense of (
6
).

Proof.

By substituting (
19
) in (
22
), we obtain, for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{p}_{i,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}\bigg\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}\tilde{p}_{j,k}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}\check{T}}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}),

(30)

where we used that

\displaystyle\bigg\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}\tilde{p}_{j,k}\bigg\|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{l}\bigg\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{ij,k}\tilde{p}_{l,k}\bigg\|

\displaystyle=\max_{l}\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\tilde{p}_{l,k}\|\leq\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})

(31)

and
e^{-\lambda_{i}(t_{k+1}-t_{k})}\leq e^{-\lambda_{i}\check{T}}
.
We now define

\beta_{i}=C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}\check{T}}

(32)

and
\hat{\beta}=\max_{i}\beta_{i}
to obtain, for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
,

\|\tilde{p}_{i,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|\leq\hat{\beta}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}).

(33)

By defining
w_{k}=\tilde{p}_{k+1}-\tilde{r}_{k^{+}}
and making use of the subadditivity property of
\|\cdot\|
, we hence obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(w_{k})

\displaystyle=\max_{i,j}\|(\tilde{p}_{i,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}})-(\tilde{p}_{j,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{j,k^{+}})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{i}\|\tilde{p}_{i,k+1}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\hat{\beta}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}).

(34)

Using (
19
), we can write
\tilde{p}_{k+1}=(H_{k}\otimes I_{2})\tilde{p}_{k}+w_{k}
.
By iterating this recursion over an
L
-step window, applying
\Delta(\cdot)
, and making use of its properties, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k+L})

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{1}\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{L}H_{k+L-\ell}\bigg)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tau_{1}\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i-1}H_{k+L-\ell}\bigg)\Delta(w_{k+L-i})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{L}\Delta(w_{k+L-i})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+2\hat{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k+L-i})

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+2\hat{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{L}(1+2\hat{\beta})^{i-1}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+\big((1+2\hat{\beta})^{L}-1\big)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}),

(35)

where in the first inequality we used the convention
\prod_{\ell=1}^{i-1}H_{k+L-\ell}=H_{k+L-1}\cdots H_{k+L-i+1}
and for
i=0
this product is empty and therefore equal to the identity matrix.
In the third inequality we used the one-step relation

\displaystyle\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{1}(H_{k})\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+\Delta(w_{k})

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\hat{\beta})\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}).

(36)

In order to establish convergence to the formation, it therefore suffices to require

(1-\mu)+\big((1+2\hat{\beta})^{L}-1\big)<1

(37)

or equivalently

\hat{\beta}<\frac{1}{2}\big((1+\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1\big).

(38)

Since
\hat{\beta}\leq\hat{C}e^{-\check{\lambda}\check{T}}
, where
\hat{C}=\max_{i}C_{i}
and
\check{\lambda}=\min_{i}\lambda_{i}
, a sufficient condition for convergence is

\check{T}>\check{T}^{*}\coloneqq-\frac{1}{\check{\lambda}}\ln\frac{(1+\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1}{2\hat{C}}.

(39)

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

The jump-flow system in (
15
) to (
18
) therefore converges if the time in between communication instants is sufficiently large.
However, the requirement in (
39
) can be conservative in practice since increasing the inter-communication time may be undesirable, as in many settings the sampling schedule is fixed, and increasing
\check{T}
might lead to reduced responsiveness of the system.
In the following, we therefore show how the geometric features of the tracking transient affect the convergence.

Geometry-Aware Convergence Condition

We start by relaxing Assumption
1
.

Assumption 3
.

For each agent
i\in\mathcal{N}
there exist constants
C_{i},\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and scalar
\sigma_{i}\in(0,1]
such that the following holds.
For any constant reference position
r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any initial time
\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

s_{i}(\tau)=(1-\sigma_{i})p_{i}(\tau)+\sigma_{i}r_{i}.

(40)

Then there exists an input signal
u_{i}(t)
for which the corresponding solution satisfies, for all
t\geq\tau

\displaystyle\|p_{i}(t)-s_{i}(\tau)\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}(t-\tau)}\|p_{i}(\tau)-s_{i}(\tau)\|.

(41)

This assumption relaxes Assumption
1
by requiring exponential convergence only to a point
s_{i}(\tau)
on the line segment joining the initial position
p_{i}(\tau)
and the reference
r_{i}
, rather than to the reference itself.
The parameter
\sigma_{i}
determines how far that point lies toward
r_{i}
and the case
\sigma_{i}=1
recovers Assumption
1
.

Evaluating (

) at

t_{k+1}-t_{k}\geq\check{T}
, we obtain

\|p_{i,k+1}-s_{i,k^{+}}\|\leq\beta_{i}\|p_{i,k}-s_{i,k^{+}}\|,

(43)

where
\beta_{i}=C_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}\check{T}}
as in (
32
).

Similar to the shifted variables
\tilde{p}_{i,k}
and
\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}
, we define
\tilde{s}_{i,k^{+}}=s_{i,k^{+}}-d_{i}
and the stacked vector
\tilde{s}_{k^{+}}=[\tilde{s}_{1,k^{+}}^{\top},\dots,\tilde{s}_{n,k^{+}}^{\top}]^{\top}
.
Using (
42
) and the jump update
\tilde{r}_{k^{+}}=(H_{k}\otimes I_{2})\tilde{p}_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{k^{+}}

\displaystyle=\big((I_{n}-\Sigma_{k})\otimes I_{2}\big)\tilde{p}_{k}+\big(\Sigma_{k}\otimes I_{2}\big)\tilde{r}_{k^{+}}

\displaystyle=\big((I_{n}-\Sigma_{k})\otimes I_{2}\big)\tilde{p}_{k}+\big((\Sigma_{k}H_{k})\otimes I_{2}\big)\tilde{p}_{k}

\displaystyle=\big(H_{k}^{\sigma}\otimes I_{2}\big)\tilde{p}_{k},

(44)

where
\Sigma_{k}=\diag(\sigma_{1,k},\dots,\sigma_{n,k})
and

H_{k}^{\sigma}=I_{n}-\Sigma_{k}+\Sigma_{k}H_{k}.

(45)

Note that
H_{k}^{\sigma}
is row-stochastic since
H_{k}
is row-stochastic and each row of
H_{k}^{\sigma}
is a convex combination of the corresponding rows of
I_{n}
and
H_{k}
.
The following lemma quantifies how this modification degrades the ergodicity coefficient of
L
-step products.

Lemma 1
.

Under Assumption
2
and the definition of
H_{k}^{\sigma}
in (
45
), it holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
that

\tau_{1}(H_{k+L-1}^{\sigma}\cdots H_{k}^{\sigma})\leq 1-\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu,

(46)

where
\check{\sigma}=\inf_{i,k}\sigma_{i,k}
and
L,\mu
as given in Assumption
2
.

Proof.

We begin with the entry-wise inequalities

H_{k}^{\sigma}\geq\Sigma_{k}H_{k}\geq\check{\sigma}H_{k}.

(47)

Consequently,

H_{k+L-1}^{\sigma}\cdots H_{k}^{\sigma}\geq\check{\sigma}^{L}H_{k+L-1}\cdots H_{k}.

(48)

Let
\Pi_{k}=H_{k+L-1}\cdots H_{k}
and
\Pi_{k}^{\sigma}=H_{k+L-1}^{\sigma}\cdots H_{k}^{\sigma}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\min_{i,j}\sum_{s=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\min\{[\Pi_{k}^{\sigma}]_{is},[\Pi_{k}^{\sigma}]_{js}\}

\displaystyle\geq\check{\sigma}^{L}\min_{i,j}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\min\{[\Pi_{k}]_{is},[\Pi_{k}]_{js}\}.

(49)

By
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, the coefficient of ergodicity of any row-stochastic matrix
A
can equivalently be expressed as

\tau_{1}(A)=1-\min_{i,j}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\min\{[A]_{is},[A]_{js}\}.

(50)

By that and Assumption
2
, we have

\min_{i,j}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\min\{[\Pi_{k}]_{is},[\Pi_{k}]_{js}\}\geq\mu.

(51)

Combining the above yields

\tau_{1}(H_{k+L-1}^{\sigma}\cdots H_{k}^{\sigma})\leq 1-\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu.

(52)

∎

Lemma
1
therefore shows that relaxing Assumption
1
, and thereby enlarging the required end-of-interval region by allowing
\sigma_{i,k}\leq 1
, reduces the guaranteed disagreement contraction from
\mu
to
\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose Assumptions
2
and
3
are satisfied.
Then, the jump-flow system defined by (
15
)-(
17
), (
42
), and (
43
) achieves the desired formation in the sense of (
6
), if for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,k}\beta_{i}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\big((1+\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1\big).

(53)

Proof.

The proof of this theorem largely follows the proof of Theorem
1
.

By (
42
), we have
\|p_{i,k}-s_{i,k^{+}}\|=\sigma_{i,k}\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|
, and therefore the bound in (
43
) yields

\|p_{i,k+1}-s_{i,k^{+}}\|\leq\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|.

(54)

Since
\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|=\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\tilde{r}_{i,k^{+}}\|
, applying (
31
) gives,

\|p_{i,k+1}-s_{i,k^{+}}\|\leq\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}).

(55)

By defining
v_{k}=\tilde{p}_{k+1}-\tilde{s}_{k^{+}}
, we can therefore establish the bound

\displaystyle\Delta(v_{k})\leq 2\hat{\delta}\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}),

(56)

with
\hat{\delta}=\sup_{i,k}\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}
.
The steps to obtain this bound are analogous to (
34
).

Using the definitions of
v_{k}
and
\tilde{s}_{k^{+}}
in (
44
), we can write the one-step recursion

\tilde{p}_{k+1}=(H_{k}^{\sigma}\otimes I_{2})\tilde{p}_{k}+v_{k}.

(57)

Iterating this over an
L
-step window, applying the seminorm
\Delta
, and using Lemma
1
gives

\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k+L})\leq(1-\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k})+\big((1+2\hat{\delta})^{L}-1\big)\Delta(\tilde{p}_{k}).

(58)

Convergence to the formation in the sense of (
6
) is therefore ensured whenever

(1-\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)+\big((1+2\hat{\delta})^{L}-1\big)<1

(59)

or equivalently

\hat{\delta}<\frac{1}{2}\big((1+\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1\big).

(60)

Since by definition
\forall i,\,\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}\leq\hat{\delta}
, it suffices to require

\sigma_{i,k}\beta_{i}<\frac{1}{2}\big((1+\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1\big),

(61)

which is the stated sufficient condition.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem
2
permits a geometric interpretation.
The bound in (
54
) states that, at the end of each flow interval, the position
p_{i,k+1}
lies inside a ball centered at
s_{i,k^{+}}
with radius

R_{i,k}=\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|.

(62)

Thus, the product
\beta_{i}\sigma_{i,k}
acts as a radius multiplier that quantifies how far the agent may deviate from the segment point
s_{i,k^{+}}
.

The parameter
\sigma_{i,k}
can be interpreted as a certificate of the tracking control on flow interval
k
.
It certifies that the closed-loop motion in each flow interval admits exponential convergence to a point on the line segment between the position at the last communication instant
p_{i,k}
and the received reference
r_{i,k^{+}}
.
In this view, agents continue to track
r_{i,k^{+}}
, however, when an agent cannot track
r_{i,k^{+}}
sufficiently fast, this analysis may certify convergence to a closer point on the segment, corresponding to a smaller
\sigma_{i,k}
.

On the other hand,
\beta_{i}
represents the contraction factor to the segment point itself.
Since the condition in Theorem
2
has to hold for
\check{\sigma}
as well, we can define

\beta^{*}=\frac{1}{2\check{\sigma}}\big((1+\check{\sigma}^{L}\mu)^{\tfrac{1}{L}}-1\big).

(63)

Consequently, by Theorem
2
, convergence is assured if
\sigma_{i,k}\beta_{i}<\check{\sigma}\beta^{*}
is satisfied for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Substituting this into the definition of the radius in (
62
) yields

R_{i,k}<\check{\sigma}\beta^{*}\|p_{i,k}-r_{i,k^{+}}\|,

(64)

which is constant in each flow interval for any choice of
\sigma_{i,k}
.

This yields the following picture.
Instead of requiring every agent to end each interval inside a ball centered at
r_{i,k^{+}}
, it suffices that they end up within the distance of
R_{i,k}
to an arbitrary point in between
(1-\check{\sigma})p_{i,k}+\check{\sigma}r_{i,k^{+}}
and
r_{i,k^{+}}
, which can be geometrically interpreted as a rounded tube around that segment with radius
R_{i,k}
.
An example of this consequence is illustrated in Figure
1
by the yellow shaded region, where to simplify the illustration we take the radius of the dashed circle to be
\beta^{*}
.

Importantly, the ability to certify
\sigma_{i,k}<1
depends on the shape of the tracking transient.
Controllers that keep the agent’s position near the line segment joining
p_{i,k}
and
r_{i,k^{+}}
, make it easier to certify the bound on
\sigma_{i,k}\beta_{i}
.
In this sense, beyond enlarging
\check{T}
, the tracking controller itself provides a second lever for guaranteeing consensus and thereby convergence to the desired formation.

Consequently, Theorem
2
generalizes not only Theorem
1
, but also the findings in
[
10
]
, where the tracking control satisfied
\forall k,\forall i,\,\sigma_{i,k}\beta_{i}=0
for any choice of
\sigma_{i,k}\in(0,1]
, i.e., the agents moved along the line connecting
p_{i,k}
and
r_{i,k^{+}}
, which immediately satisfies the condition in Theorem
2
.

VI
Simulation Results

In this section, we present numerical simulations of swarms implementing different control laws to underline the results in Theorems
1
and
2
.
To this end, we consider a swarm of
n=6
agents with unicycle dynamics given by

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{i}(t)=v_{i}(t)\cos\theta_{i}(t)\\
\dot{y}_{i}(t)=v_{i}(t)\sin\theta_{i}(t)\\
\dot{\theta}_{i}(t)=\omega_{i}(t),\end{cases}

(65)

with position
p_{i}(t)=[x_{i}(t),y_{i}(t)]^{\top}
and inputs
v_{i}(t)
and
\omega_{i}(t)
.
In order to let the agents converge toward their reference positions during flow intervals
t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]
, we use a feedback linearization control similar to the one described in
[
9
]
.
We write
e_{i}(t)=[\cos\theta_{i}(t),\sin\theta_{i}(t)]^{\top}
and define the point

q_{i}(t)=p_{i}(t)+\varepsilon_{i}(t)e_{i}(t)

(66)

with
\varepsilon_{i}(t)=\varepsilon(p_{i}(t))=\kappa_{\varepsilon}\|p_{i}(t)-r_{i,k^{+}}\|
,
\kappa_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
.
We then define the variable
z_{i}(t)\coloneqq q_{i}(t)-r_{i,k^{+}}
and

F_{i}(z_{i})\coloneqq-\gamma_{i}z_{i}+\mu_{i}Jz_{i},

(67)

where
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are control gains and
J=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)
.
The control inputs are then calculated by

\displaystyle v_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{i}(t)^{\top}F_{i}(z_{i}(t))}{1+\kappa_{\varepsilon}e_{i}(t)^{\top}\frac{p_{i}(t)-r_{i,k^{+}}}{\|p_{i}(t)-r_{i,k^{+}}\|}}

(68)

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}(t)}e_{i}(t)^{\top}F_{i}(z_{i}(t)).

(69)

Note that the first term in (
67
) is a contracting feedback toward
z_{i}=0
, whereas the second term induces a rotation around the origin through
J
.
The gain
\mu_{i}
therefore controls the induced circular motion.
Consequently, for smaller values of
\mu_{i}
, the agent takes a more direct path towards its reference.
By arguments analogous to
[
9
]
, the agent’s position
p_{i}(t)
remains exponentially stable around the reference
r_{i,k^{+}}
for any
\gamma_{i}>0
.

In the following, we run three simulations implementing the reference updates described in Section
IV
together with the agent dynamics introduced above.
We generate a random sequence of strongly connected matrices
H_{k}
satisfying Assumption
2
and consider a fixed flow interval length, i.e.,
\check{T}=\hat{T}=T
.
For each agent, we sample
a_{i}\sim\mathcal{U}(0.4,1)
independently and set
\gamma_{i}=-10\ln a_{i}
.
Furthermore, in all experiments we set
\forall i,\,\mu_{i}=\mu
.

The initial positions
p_{i}(0)
and headings
\theta_{i}(0)
are drawn uniformly in
[-5,5]\times[-5,5]
and
[0,2\pi)
, respectively.
The desired formation is a regular hexagon of radius
5
, i.e.,
d_{i}=5\left[\cos\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{6},\sin\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{6}\right]^{\top}
.

For the first two simulations we fix
T=$0.1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
.
In the first run we choose
\forall i,\,\mu=0
, while in the second run we set
\mu=\tfrac{\pi}{2T}
.
The third simulation also uses the rotational gain
\mu=\tfrac{\pi}{2T}
, but increases the interval length to
T=$1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
.

To quantify the convergence to the desired formation, we compute at each communication instant the mean squared error

\mathrm{MSE}(\tilde{p}_{1,k},\dots,\tilde{p}_{n,k})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{p}_{i,k}-\bar{p}_{k}\|^{2},

(70)

where
\bar{p}_{k}
is the arithmetic mean of
\tilde{p}_{1,k},\dots,\tilde{p}_{n,k}
.
The formation is achieved when this error converges to zero.

Figure
2
shows the formation error over time for the three simulation runs.

Fig. 2:
Evolution of the formation error for varying choices of
\mu
and
T
.

In the first simulation (
\mu=0
,
T=$0.1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
), the agents reach consensus and the swarm converges to the desired formation.
In contrast, in the second simulation (
\mu=\tfrac{\pi}{2T}
,
T=$0.1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
) the formation error diverges.
This behavior is consistent with the geometric interpretation of Theorem
2
.
Increasing
\mu
introduces a stronger tangential component, causing trajectories to deviate further from the line segment between the previous position and the updated reference, which in turn makes it harder to satisfy the inequality in Theorem
2
.

However, convergence is recovered in the third experiment by increasing the time in between communication instants (
\mu=\tfrac{\pi}{2T}
,
T=$1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
).
This matches the prediction of Theorem
1
, which guarantees convergence for sufficiently long flow intervals.

Lastly, we analyze the communication efficiency of the proposed protocol.
For
T=$0.1\text{\,}\mathrm{s}$
, the
30\text{\,}\mathrm{s}
simulation contains
300
communication instants.
Since all agents transmit simultaneously and three scalars must be communicated at each update, the communication protocol requires three orthogonal channels per update, which amounts to
900
orthogonal transmissions in total.

For comparison, under a node-to-node protocol each active communication link would require two orthogonal channels.
Counting all links over the simulation horizon then yields
6018
orthogonal transmissions.
Therefore, the employed protocol provides a substantial reduction in the number of required orthogonal channels.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper we proposed a consensus-based formation control scheme for heterogeneous agents.
Instead of imposing explicit assumptions on the agent dynamics, we only require exponential stabilizability of the position with respect to a constant reference.
We derived sufficient conditions that guarantee convergence to the desired formation.
In particular, convergence can be ensured by choosing the time in between communication updates sufficiently large.
Moreover, convergence can also be established through an appropriate choice of tracking control laws that yield favorable transient geometry.
In this way we extend the results in
[
1
]
and
[
10
]
.
Numerical simulations illustrate the theoretical findings.

A key element of the approach is the OTA broadcast protocol.
The protocol exploits the superposition property of the wireless channel to compute normalized convex combinations from simultaneous transmissions.
This yields a substantial reduction in the required number of orthogonal channels when compared to traditional interference-avoiding node-to-node communication schemes.

In future work, we plan to incorporate additional adverse communication effects, including additive noise and half-duplex communication.
In addition, we will study the impact of imperfect position measurements and other sensing limitations as well as explicit agent constraints, such as collision avoidance, on the convergence guarantees.
