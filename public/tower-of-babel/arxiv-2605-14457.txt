Stateful Reasoning via Insight Replay
Bin Lei, Caiwen Ding, Jiachen Yang, Ang Li, Xin Eric Wang

Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has become a foundation for eliciting multi-step reasoning in large language models, but recent studies show that its benefits do not scale monotonically with chain length: while longer CoT generally enables a model to tackle harder problems, on a given problem, accuracy typically increases with CoT length up to a point, after which it declines. We identify a major cause of this phenomenon: as the CoT grows, the model’s attention to critical insights produced earlier in the trace gradually weakens, making those insights progressively less accessible when they are most needed. Therefore, we propose InsightReplay , a stateful reasoning approach in which the model periodically extracts critical insights from its reasoning trace and replays them near the active generation frontier, keeping them accessible as the reasoning scales. Extensive experiments on a × × 𝟒 \mathbf{2}\!\times\!\mathbf{3}\!\times\!\mathbf{4} benchmark grid, covering model scales { 8B , 30B } \{\text{8B},\text{30B}\} , model families { Qwen3.5 , DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen , Gemma-4 } \{\text{Qwen3.5},\text{DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen},\text{Gemma-4}\} , and reasoning benchmarks { AIME , HMMT , GPQA Diamond , LiveCodeBench v5 } \{\text{AIME},\text{HMMT},\text{GPQA Diamond},\text{LiveCodeBench v5}\} , show that 3-round InsightReplay yields accuracy gains across all 24 settings , with an averaged improvement of + 1.65 \mathbf{+1.65} points over standard CoT, and a largest single-setting gain of + 9.2 \mathbf{+9.2} points on R1-Distill-32B’s LiveCodeBench v5 subset. Our results suggest that the effectiveness of test-time scaling depends not only on how much a model reasons, but also on whether critical intermediate insights remain accessible throughout long reasoning trajectories. Introduction

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is a key mechanism for eliciting reasoning in large language models. By explicitly generating intermediate steps, CoT decomposes difficult objectives into smaller sub-problems and has become a foundation for test-time scaling: increasing reasoning-token budgets often improves performance on difficult tasks
[
25
,
24
,
7
,
12
]
.

However, recent work shows that the benefits of additional reasoning tokens do not scale monotonically. On fixed-difficulty problems, accuracy typically follows an inverted-U relationship with CoT length (see Figure
1
): performance first improves as the model reasons longer, reaches a peak, and then declines as the trajectory becomes excessively long
[
26
,
4
,
5
]
. Harder problems shift this peak rightward, requiring longer reasoning before degradation appears.

What drives this decline? Prior work has identified that not all
reasoning tokens contribute equally: a small subset of sentences disproportionately determines the final
answer, and certain attention heads consistently focus on them

[
3
]
. We complement this line of
work by asking a different question:
how does the model’s access to such critical insights evolve as the reasoning chain grows?

In Section
2.2
, we directly measure the
pre-softmax attention from answer tokens to critical insights as
a function of token distance, and find that it decays monotonically.
As a result, later reasoning is conducted with progressively degraded access to the very intermediate conclusions that matter most.
This observation suggests a broader view of test-time scaling: increasing reasoning depth alone is insufficient unless important reasoning state can also remain accessible over time.

This echoes a classical challenge in sequence modeling: preserving long-range information across time. LSTM
[
8
]
addressed this by introducing an explicit memory cell that persists and is continually updated as the sequence unfolds. A natural question therefore arises for long reasoning chains:
can we equip the growing reasoning chain with an analogous mechanism that keeps critical insights accessible over time?

To answer this question, we introduce
stateful reasoning
for CoT, where
the reasoning state at any point is the cumulative set of

insights
the model has generated so far, compressed
abstractions of prior reasoning. We instantiate this with

InsightReplay
, which periodically generates new insights
and replays them near the active generation frontier. Keeping
insights close to the decoding position preserves the model’s
attention to them. This mitigates the attention decay that critical
intermediate content suffers in long trajectories, raising the
achievable peak accuracy and expanding the regime in which extended
reasoning translates into higher accuracy.

Figure
1
previews this property. The Baseline curve exhibits the characteristic inverted-U pattern: accuracy first rises with CoT length, peaks at around
15
K tokens, and then declines as the chain grows further.
InsightReplay behaves differently. It operates precisely in the long CoT regime where additional reasoning already degrades the Baseline, yet each additional replay round (
1
,
3
,
5
) achieves progressively higher mean accuracy.
By
3
replay rounds, InsightReplay’s mean accuracy already exceeds the Baseline’s peak; by
5
rounds, performance continues to improve further. In effect, InsightReplay turns the Baseline’s degradation regime into a continued-growth regime,
extending the range of token budgets over which additional reasoning remains productive.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

•

We empirically study critical insights in CoT and identify two key properties:
(I)
critical insights and the surrounding reasoning trace are complementary;
(II)
attention to critical insights decays as CoT grows, making them progressively less accessible to the model.

•

Building on these observations, we propose
InsightReplay
, a stateful reasoning approach that periodically extracts critical insights and replays them near the active generation frontier. We further provide a theoretical analysis showing that InsightReplay can shift the optimal reasoning length rightward and raise the achievable peak accuracy.

•

We validate InsightReplay in a purely inference-time setting and show that replaying critical insights yields consistent improvements across a broad range of models and reasoning benchmarks, without any additional training.

•

We further reinforce this reasoning pattern through post-training, demonstrating that InsightReplay improves both training stability and reasoning performance over vanilla CoT under same training settings.

Properties of Critical Insights in Long CoT

2.1
Critical Insights and the Reasoning Trace are Complementary

Related Work.

Recent work provides evidence that information in CoT is not uniformly distributed:
compression methods
show that many reasoning tokens can be pruned with minimal accuracy loss
[
27
,
11
,
31
,
14
]

summarize-and-restart methods
first generate a reasoning segment, then summarizes it into a compact form, discards the original segment, and resumes thinking from the summary
[
28
,
29
,
16
]
; and
importance-attribution methods
identify a small number of sentences or tokens that disproportionately influence the final answer
[
3
,
15
]

2.2
Critical Insights Become Less Accessible as Reasoning Chains Grow

Related Work.

While prior work has
identified important reasoning steps and the heads that attend
to them
[
3
]
, and while
positional encodings such as RoPE

[
22
]
are known to
bias attention toward nearby tokens, empirical attention
patterns are ultimately shaped by learned query-key interactions
rather than positional geometry alone

[
2
]
. Whether attention to
critical insights actually decays with distance therefore
requires direct measurement. We take critical insights as the
unit of analysis and measure how attention from answer tokens
to these insights varies with token distance.

Experimental Setting.

On
60
AIME problems with
Qwen3-8B
and

Gemma-4-31B-it
, the model first generates a CoT
trace, after which
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B
identifies
5
–
7

critical insights, each localized to a token span
c
ending at
position
p_{e}
. We use a stronger extractor here because attention measurement requires
precise token boundaries that smaller models do not produce
reliably.
To isolate the effect of distance, we insert semantically neutral
filler tokens at sentence boundaries between the insight
and the answer. Each filler unit is
"\n "

(newline + space), chosen for its minimal semantic impact. Let

p_{a}
denote the answer position, so the baseline distance is

d=p_{a}-p_{e}
. We sweep an insertion ratio

\rho\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}
, inserting
N=\lfloor\rho d\rfloor
filler units total, distributed uniformly at random
across the sentence boundaries between insight and answer. The
resulting total distance is
(1+\rho)d
regardless of placement.
We use one sample at
\rho=0
and
10
random allocations at
each non-zero
\rho
, yielding
41
variants per insight. Since the insight and all preceding tokens are untouched, any
change in attention is primarily attributable to distance.
For each variant, we compute the per-layer

pre-softmax
attention score from the answer token to the insight span:

s^{(\ell)}(c,a)\;=\;\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h}\;\frac{1}{|c|}\sum_{t\in c}\;\frac{\mathbf{q}_{p_{a}}^{(\ell,h)}\cdot\mathbf{k}_{t}^{(\ell,h)}}{\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{head}}}},

where
c
denotes the set of token positions covered by the insight span (with
|c|
its size and
t
ranging over its elements),
\ell
is the layer index,
h
indexes attention heads,
\mathbf{q}
and
\mathbf{k}
are post-RoPE query and key vectors,
|H|
is the number of heads, and
d_{\mathrm{head}}
is the per-head dimension. We use pre-softmax scores rather than post-softmax weights because softmax normalization over the full preceding context mechanically squashes weights as context length grows, confounding the effect of distance with that of sequence length. Full implementation details are in Appendix
B
.

Results & Conclusion

Figure
2
reports the relative
decline in attention to critical insights from
\rho{=}0
to

\rho{=}0.4
:
19.2\%
for
Qwen3-8B
(final layer) and
3.3\%

for
Gemma-4-31B-it
(final global-attention layer).
We draw two conclusions:

(I)
Within each model, attention to critical insights
decays monotonically as the insight is pushed away from the
generation frontier, and the decline is highly significant
(
p<0.001
).

(II)
The decay is not specific to a single architecture
or RoPE configuration: it persists in Gemma-4-31B-it, which uses
partial-rotary RoPE on only the first quarter of head dimensions
and a different attention scaling, suggesting the phenomenon
reflects a general property of trained attention rather than a
feature of any specific positional encoding.

3
InsightReplay

3.1
Method Overview

Figure
3
illustrates the core idea
of InsightReplay. Reasoning unfolds as an interleaving of two
types of content:
reasoning chunks
(
R_{t}
), in which the
model performs step-by-step thinking, and
insights

(
I_{t}
), short distilled conclusions the model itself produces
to summarize prior reasoning. As the trajectory progresses, the
sequence of insights
\{I_{t}\}
acts as a continuously updated,
compressed abstraction of what has been concluded so far, kept
near the active generation frontier so it remains accessible to
subsequent reasoning. One specific example is shown in Appendix
D.6
.

Figure 3:
Illustration of InsightReplay.

: grouping chunks into a logical state. The dashed arrows group chunks into two logical roles: insights collectively form the
Insight State
, and reasoning chunks form the
Reasoning Stream
. These groupings reflect logical roles only, and all chunks reside in the same conversation history.

Method Description.

The process begins from the user question
Q
. We initialize

I_{0}\!=\!Q
, treating the question itself as a “goal
insight” that anchors all subsequent reasoning, consistent
with Section
2.1
. The model then enters a
periodic loop. At each round
t
, conditioned on the full
history
(Q,R_{1},I_{1},\ldots,R_{t-1},I_{t-1})
, the model:
(i) generates a reasoning chunk
R_{t}
, and
(ii) generates a new insight
I_{t}
via a summarization prompt that asks the model to consolidate the conclusions reached so far, with the thinking content
R_{1},\ldots,R_{t}
retained in context.
Both
R_{t}
and
I_{t}
are appended to the history in order.
Crucially, because
I_{t}
is generated in the presence of all
prior insights
I_{0},\ldots,I_{t-1}
, each new insight can
reflect on and supersede earlier ones—incorporating
new conclusions, correcting prior errors, or refining
imprecise statements. The cumulative trace of insights thus
forms an evolving abstraction of the reasoning so far, rather
than a flat concatenation of independent summaries. After
N

rounds (a hyperparameter; we ablate
N\!\in\!\{1,3\}
in
Section
4
), the model produces the final
answer
A
from the complete history. By construction, the
most recent
I_{t-1}
always sits immediately before
R_{t}
,
keeping the latest distilled conclusions close to the decoding
position of subsequent reasoning.

3.2
Theoretical Analysis

In this section, we formalize the observations from Sections
2.1
and
2.2
, and prove that InsightReplay provably shifts the optimal CoT length rightward and raises the peak accuracy.

3.2.1
Modeling

Following
26
, for a task
q
with difficulty
T
and model
\theta
, the probability of an
N
-step CoT producing the correct final answer is:

P(a_{\mathrm{final}}|q,\theta,N)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\underbrace{P(q_{i}|H_{i-1},q,\theta,N)}_{\text{sub-question}}\;\underbrace{P(a_{i}|q_{i},H_{i-1},q,\theta,N)}_{\text{sub-answer}},

(1)

where
H_{k}:=[t_{1},a_{1},\dots,t_{k},a_{k}]
denotes the reasoning history up to step
k
.

They parameterize the sub-question accuracy as
(1-\sigma(T))
, where
\sigma(T)
denotes the probability that the model fails to generate the correct next sub-question for a task of total difficulty
T
, and the sub-answer accuracy as
(1-E(N,M,T))
with a linear error model
E(N,M,T)=T/(NM)
, where
M
represents the model’s reasoning capability. Thus, the accuracy of
N
-step reasoning is:

A_{0}(N)=\alpha\,\bigl[(1-\sigma(T))(1-T/(NM))\bigr]^{N},

(2)

where
\alpha
is a constant independent of
N
.
Crucially, their parameterization
drops the dependence on the history

H_{i-1}
, implicitly assuming each step’s accuracy is independent of its position in the chain.

However, our Section
2.2
shows: as
i
increases, the model’s ability to access critical insights from earlier steps diminishes due to attention decay.
We restore this missing dependence by introducing an
insight accessibility function

\Phi(i)
into the sub-question term.
Specifically, we model the sub-question accuracy at step
i
as
(1-\sigma(T))\cdot\Phi(i)
, where
\Phi(i)
captures how effectively the model can access prior insights when formulating the
i
-th sub-question. We apply
\Phi(i)
to the sub-question term because generating the correct next sub-question requires synthesizing prior insights, whereas answering a sub-question, once correctly posed, depends primarily on the sub-question itself.

Assumption 1
.

The insight accessibility function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to(0,1]
satisfies:

1.

\Phi(0)=1

(zero distance
\Rightarrow
full accessibility);

2.

\Phi
is strictly monotonically decreasing (Section
2.2
)
(farther
\Rightarrow
less accessible);

3.

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\Phi(i)=0

(infinite distance
\Rightarrow
inaccessible).

Under this modeling, we define two accuracy functions:

Real-world CoT vs. InsightReplay.

We define two accuracy functions, where

p(N):=(1-\sigma(T))(1-E(N,M,T))
denotes the per-step
accuracy without decay. The real-world CoT accuracy
A_{\Phi}
accounts
for insight decay via
\Phi(i)
, while InsightReplay relocates
critical insights to a fixed distance
d_{0}\geq 0
from the generation
frontier at every step, replacing
\Phi(i)
with the constant

\Phi(d_{0})
:

A_{\Phi}(N)=[p(N)]^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Phi(i),\qquad A_{\mathrm{IR}}(N)=[p(N)\cdot\Phi(d_{0})]^{N}.

(3)

3.2.2
Main Results

We first state two structural lemmas that underlie our main results.
Let
g(N):=\frac{d}{dN}[N\ln p(N)]
.

Lemma 1

(Strict concavity)
.

\ln A_{0}(N)
is strictly concave for
N>T/M
, and consequently
g
is
strictly decreasing on this domain.

Lemma 2

(Uniqueness of optima)
.

A_{\Phi}
and
A_{\mathrm{IR}}
each admit a unique maximizer on

(0,\infty)
, characterized by

g(N_{\Phi}^{*})=-\ln\Phi(N_{\Phi}^{*}),\qquad g(N_{\mathrm{IR}}^{*})=-\ln\Phi(d_{0}).

(4)

Building on these lemmas, our two main results show that
InsightReplay both shifts the optimal CoT length and raises the
achievable peak accuracy.

Theorem 1

(InsightReplay shifts the optimal length rightward)
.

N_{\mathrm{IR}}^{*}>N_{\Phi}^{*}
.

Theorem 2

(InsightReplay raises peak accuracy)
.

A_{\mathrm{IR}}(N_{\mathrm{IR}}^{*})>A_{\Phi}(N_{\Phi}^{*})
.

Proof intuition.

Theorem
1
follows because InsightReplay places
insights adjacent to the generation frontier (
d_{0}\ll N_{\Phi}^{*}
), so

\Phi(d_{0})>\Phi(N_{\Phi}^{*})
. Combined with the optimality conditions
in Eq. (
13
) and the strict monotonicity of
g

(Lemma
1
), this forces
N_{\mathrm{IR}}^{*}>N_{\Phi}^{*}
.
Theorem
2
follows because
\Phi(d_{0})>\Phi(i)
for
all
i\geq 1
implies
A_{\mathrm{IR}}(N)>A_{\Phi}(N)
pointwise, and
maximizing both sides preserves the inequality.
Full proofs are deferred to Appendix
C
.

Setup.

We use GRPO
[

[

training. The actor is trained on
DAPO-Math-15K

[
30
]

with a maximum response length of
30{,}720
tokens, a rollout

8
per prompt, and a global batch size of
256

Rollout Protocol.

The two settings differ only in how rollouts are generated. In
the
Baseline
setting, the policy
generates a single thinking trace until it emits the
EOS token or reaches the maximum response length.
In the

setting, once the policy emits
the EOS token, we append a fixed continuation cue
asking the model to extract and replay the insights
(exact text in Appendix
G
). Generation then
continues under the same length budget, so the total response
length matches the baseline exactly.
The cue is inserted with
\mathrm{response\_mask}{=}0
which isolates the effect of the
InsightReplay reasoning pattern from any auxiliary supervision
signal.

We also experimented with a more elaborate scheme that explicitly

we document this attempt in

(1) Training stability.

The standard CoT baseline exhibits a pronounced late-training

, all three metrics decline. The

. In

200
onward,
\mathrm{mean@32}
stays
within the

–

band, with no monotonic decline
observed up to step
600
.

(2) Peak accuracy.
InsightReplay also achieves higher peak validation accuracy across
all three metrics. On
\mathrm{maj@32}
, InsightReplay peaks at

38.8\%
versus
34.1\%
for the baseline, an absolute improvement of
+4.7
points; on

\mathrm{mean@32}

28.8\%

+0.9

(3) The advantage is not an initialization effect.

For the first
\sim 200

. This rules out alternative

Together, the three observations show that the InsightReplay reasoning
pattern can be reinforced through RL training rather than only used at
inference time. Beyond improving peak accuracy, it stabilizes long-CoT
GRPO training in a regime where the standard CoT baseline suffers a
pronounced late-training degradation, and does so without relying on
any early-learning advantage. This positions InsightReplay as a
reasoning pattern compatible with both inference-time and training-time
use.

6
Limitation

Although InsightReplay can deliver substantial absolute gains where
the base model has substantive room to grow, with the largest
single-cell improvement reaching
+9.2
points on

R1-Distill-32B
’s LiveCodeBench v5 subset (lifting
25.8\%
to

35.0\%
,
+35.7\%
relative), the gains are bounded by each
benchmark’s available headroom. On near-saturated settings the
marginal improvement is modest: on
Qwen3.5-35B-A3B
’s HMMT
subset, IR3 improves over an
88.5\%
baseline by only
+0.1
points.
This pattern is consistent with our theoretical framework, in which

\Phi(d_{0})
relocation cannot exceed the ceiling imposed by

p(N)
itself. We therefore expect InsightReplay to provide the
largest practical value on hard reasoning tasks where the underlying
model is far from saturation.

7
Conclusion

We introduced InsightReplay, a stateful reasoning approach that periodically
extracts critical insights from the CoT trace and replays them near the active generation
frontier. By mitigating the attention decay that critical insights suffer in long
trajectories, InsightReplay reshapes the inverted-U between CoT length and
accuracy, shifting the peak rightward and lifting it upward, thereby expanding the
regime in which extended reasoning translates into higher accuracy. We support this
through both theoretical analysis and
empirical validation: positive gains across all
24
cells of our
scale
\times
family
\times
benchmark grid in the pure-inference setting, and
consistent improvements in training stability and peak accuracy when used as a rollout
protocol for post-training.

Appendix A
Experimental Details for Property I

Dataset.

We use
60
problems from the American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME) spanning the
2024
and
2025
competitions.

Thinking trace generation.

For each problem, we generate
16
independent chain-of-thought samples using
Qwen3-8B
in its native thinking mode, and report results averaged over these samples. We use the Qwen3 chat template and prompt the model with:

Solve the following math competition problem step by step. Show your detailed reasoning, then give your final answer as a single integer inside
\boxed
{}
.

Generation is performed via vLLM
[
13
]
with sampling (
temperature
{}=0.6
,
top_p
{}=0.95
), tensor parallelism across
2
GPUs,
max_model_len
{}=30{,}000
,
dtype=bfloat16
, and a maximum of
30{,}000
new tokens. The model’s output follows the
<think>...</think>
convention; we parse the content inside the tags as the thinking trace and the content outside as the final response. For traces that reach the
30{,}000
-token limit before producing a closing
</think>
tag, we treat the content up to the closing tag as the thinking trace if it appears, and otherwise treat the entire generated output as the thinking trace. All seven conditions in Section
2.1
are evaluated on the same set of
60\times 16=960
generated traces.

Insight extraction.

For each thinking trace, we use
Qwen3-8B
to extract a list of
5
–
7
key insights. The extraction model receives the original question and the full thinking trace, and is instructed to:

Extract exactly
5
to
7
key insights from this thinking, in the order they were derived. Each insight should be one concrete sentence containing specific numbers, equations, or results—not vague descriptions.

The model produces a numbered list (e.g.
1. The area of
\triangle ABC
is
6\sqrt{14}
by Heron’s formula.
), which we parse with a regex-based extractor that captures each enumerated entry. We cap the result at
7
insights per problem in the rare cases where the model produces more. This phase uses vLLM with greedy decoding,
max_tokens
{}=8192
, and the same tensor parallelism configuration as the trace generation phase. Across all
60
problems and
16
samples per problem, the mean number of extracted insights is
6.9
, with all extractions falling within the requested
5
–
7
range.

Probability scoring.

We load
Qwen3-8B
via HuggingFace Transformers in
bfloat16
precision on a single GPU.
For each problem, we construct a prefix string following the Qwen3 chat template:

<|im_start|>user
{user_prompt}<|im_end|>
<|im_start|>assistant
<think>
{THINK_CONTENT}
</think>

\boxed{

where
THINK_CONTENT
varies across conditions (see below).
We then append the ground-truth answer tokens
a_{\mathrm{gt}}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{K})
(the tokenized integer answer) to the prefix and perform a single forward pass over the concatenated sequence. The score is the joint log-probability of the answer tokens conditioned on the prefix:

\log P_{\theta}(a_{\mathrm{gt}}\mid x_{\mathrm{prefix}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log P_{\theta}\!\bigl(a_{k}\mid x_{\mathrm{prefix}},a_{<k}\bigr),

(5)

where
x_{\mathrm{prefix}}
denotes the prompt up to and including the opening token sequence
\boxed{
, and
K
is the number of tokens in the tokenized ground-truth answer.

Condition definitions.

Let
\mathbf{q}
denote the problem statement,
\mathbf{t}
the full thinking trace generated in the previous paragraph, and
\mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{K})
the insights extracted by the extraction step (formatted as a numbered list). Let
\mathbf{r}
denote a sequence of pseudo-random tokens whose token count matches that of
\mathbf{t}
for the same problem. The seven conditions differ only in
THINK_CONTENT
:

(A)

Full thinking (baseline)
:
\mathbf{t}

(B)

Repeated question + insights
:
Question:

\mathbf{q}
, followed by
\mathbf{f}

(C)

Insights only
:
\mathbf{f}

(D)

Full thinking + repeated question + insights
:
\mathbf{t}
, then
Question:

\mathbf{q}
, followed by
\mathbf{f}

(E)

Full thinking + insights
:
\mathbf{t}
, followed by
\mathbf{f}

(F)

Random tokens + repeated question + insights
:
\mathbf{r}
, then
Question:

\mathbf{q}
, followed by
\mathbf{f}

(G)

Random tokens + insights
:
\mathbf{r}
, followed by
\mathbf{f}

Adjacent components are concatenated with two newline characters (
\n\n
). To construct
\mathbf{r}
, we sample strings of the form
xq{0--9999}
with a fixed random seed of
42
for reproducibility, then truncate or pad at the token level to match
|\mathrm{tokenize}(\mathbf{t})|
exactly.

Software and hardware.

All experiments use vLLM
0.19.0
for trace generation and insight extraction, and HuggingFace Transformers
5.6.0
for probability scoring. Models are served in
bfloat16
precision. Trace generation and insight extraction each run on
2{\times}
NVIDIA GPUs with tensor parallelism; probability scoring runs on a single GPU.

Models.

: 36 layers, 32 query heads, 8 KV heads via GQA, head dim
128
, full RoPE, with QK-norm.
Gemma-4-31B-it

; we extract scores only from global layers (head_dim
=512
) so the attention pattern is not truncated by the sliding window. The insight-extraction model is
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B
.

CoT generation.

Each target model generates a complete CoT trace via vLLM with greedy decoding (
max_new_tokens
=
38,000
,
temperature
=
1.0
,
do_sample
=
False
,
bfloat16
). The user prompt is:

Solve the following math problem step by step. Show your complete reasoning, then give the final answer inside
\boxed
{}
.

<think>...</think>
; for Gemma-4 we use the native thought block. The ground-truth answer is appended as
\boxed{<answer>}
at the fixed position

(the first token after
\boxed{
).

Insight extraction and token-level localization.

We prompt
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B
to list
5
–
7

(start_token_idx, end_token_idx)
for the insight span
c
. Insights whose verbatim span cannot be recovered, or whose sentence position leaves fewer than one downstream sentence before
p_{a}
, are discarded. This yields an average of
{\sim}6

Filler unit calibration.

The filler unit
"\n "
is calibrated per tokenizer at runtime to determine its token cost
\tau
. The number of filler units actually inserted at ratio
\rho
is
N=\lfloor\lfloor\rho\cdot d\rfloor/\tau\rfloor
, and the realized distance after insertion is
d^{\prime}=d+N\cdot\tau
. Random allocation of fillers across sentence boundaries uses a seeded RNG (
seed
=
42
plus problem-id hash) for reproducibility.

Attention-score extraction.

We run a layer-by-layer forward pass with
bfloat16
. At each layer
\ell
we recompute the post-RoPE query vector
\mathbf{q}^{(\ell,h)}_{p_{a}}
and post-RoPE key vectors
\mathbf{k}^{(\ell,h)}_{t}
for
t\in c
, and store the per-layer score
s^{(\ell)}(c,a)
together with the realized distance
d^{\prime}
. For GQA models we map each query head to its shared KV head via integer division by the GQA group size.

Model-specific handling.

Qwen3-8B
applies standard full-dimension RoPE and QK-norm; we replicate the ordering q-proj
\to
q-norm
\to
RoPE, and likewise for keys.
Gemma-4-31B-it
uses head_dim
=512
on global layers; to avoid materializing the full attention matrix at this size we use PyTorch SDPA for
head_dim
>
256
and fall back to
flash_attention_2
elsewhere. Only the explicit
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{k}
computation for the (answer, insight) pair enters the logging path.

Aggregation and reporting.

Scores are aggregated per
(\ell,\rho)
by first averaging across samples, then across insights within a problem, then across problems. For distance-based plots, we bin by realized distance
d^{\prime}
with bin size
500
tokens. We additionally identify candidate “receiver” heads as the top-
32
heads ranked by score kurtosis across variants.

Compute.

Attention extraction uses
8
-way data parallelism across NVIDIA H200 GPUs. Each worker loads one full model instance (
Qwen3-8B
on one GPU;
Gemma-4-31B-it
sharded across multiple GPUs via
device_map
). All intermediate artifacts (CoTs, insights, token locations, variants, raw scores) are persisted to JSONL/CSV and released with the code.

Appendix C
Proofs of Theoretical Results

C.1
Preparation

Define the baseline log-accuracy term

\ln A_{0}(N):=N\ln p(N),\qquad g(N):=\frac{d}{dN}\bigl[N\ln p(N)\bigr].

(6)

C.1.1
Proof of Lemma
1

Proof.

Let
c:=T/M
. From Eq. (
2
), we can write

\ln A_{0}(N)=\ln\alpha+N\ln(1-\sigma)+N\ln\!\left(1-\frac{c}{N}\right).

(7)

The first term is constant and the second is linear in
N
, so it suffices to study

h(N):=N\ln\!\left(1-\frac{c}{N}\right),\quad h^{\prime}(N)=\ln\!\left(1-\frac{c}{N}\right)+\frac{c}{N-c},\quad h^{\prime\prime}(N)=-\frac{c^{2}}{N(N-c)^{2}}<0.

(8)

Hence
(\ln A_{0})^{\prime\prime}(N)<0
for all
N>T/M
, proving strict concavity. Since
g(N)=(\ln A_{0})^{\prime}(N)
, it follows immediately that

g^{\prime}(N)=(\ln A_{0})^{\prime\prime}(N)<0\qquad\text{for all }N>T/M.

(9)

When
N\leq T/M
, we have
E=T/(NM)\geq 1
, which would imply a per-step error rate of at least
100\%
and is therefore outside the meaningful regime of the model.
∎

C.1.2
Proof of Lemma
2

Proof.

For analytical convenience, we approximate
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ln\Phi(i)
by
\int_{1}^{N}\ln\Phi(x)\,dx
, so that

\frac{d}{dN}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ln\Phi(i)\;\approx\;\ln\Phi(N).

(10)

Taking logarithms of Eq. (
3
),

\ln A_{\Phi}(N)=\ln A_{0}(N)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ln\Phi(i),\qquad\ln A_{\mathrm{IR}}(N)=\ln A_{0}(N)+N\ln\Phi(d_{0}).

(11)

Differentiating and applying Eq. (
10
) and Eq. (
6
),

(\ln A_{\Phi})^{\prime}(N)=g(N)+\ln\Phi(N),\qquad(\ln A_{\mathrm{IR}})^{\prime}(N)=g(N)+\ln\Phi(d_{0}).

(12)

Setting these to zero gives the optimality conditions

g(N_{\Phi}^{*})=-\ln\Phi(N_{\Phi}^{*}),\qquad g(N_{\mathrm{IR}}^{*})=-\ln\Phi(d_{0}).

(13)

We prove the claim for

has at most one solution, so the maximizer is unique.

To jointly characterize the relationship between compute budget and accuracy for the length sweep in Section

1.0

p

0.95

Appendix H
Alternative Training Method

This appendix documents an earlier attempt to train the multi-round
InsightReplay structure directly, rather than inducing it at
rollout time with a fixed continuation cue. The pipeline has two stages:
we first construct supervised multi-round traces, and then optimize the
policy with a training procedure that explicitly mirrors the round-wise
visibility pattern used at inference time.

H.1
Method Overview

Base model and goal.

We start from
Qwen3.5-9B-Base

[
18
]
. The goal of this
alternative design is to make the multi-round reasoning structure
explicit during training: each round exposes only the question, the
currently visible reasoning context, and the replayed intermediate
insights that should be accessible at that round.

Stage 1: Supervised multi-round trace construction.

To obtain SFT data in the target format, we sample
2{,}000
math
problems from
DAPO-Math-17K

[
30
]
. For each problem, a strong
teacher model,
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B
, generates a full solution,
which we then decompose into a sequence of reasoning steps and compact
intermediate insights. We convert these into multi-round targets where
all non-final rounds contain a reasoning segment together with an
intermediate insight, and the last round produces the final answer. This
procedure yields
1{,}892
valid problems and
8{,}102
SFT training
entries. A concrete example appears in Appendix
H.3
.

Stage 2: Direct multi-round RL training.

Our RL training procedure consists of two steps, illustrated in
Figure
8
.

In
Step 1
, we start from the standard causal mask and add a
round-level visibility mask over the concatenated multi-round response.
This makes the intended computation explicit: at each round, the model
can attend only to the original question, the relevant reasoning tokens,
and the replayed insights that should be visible in that round, while
all other tokens are masked out on top of the causal constraint.
However, this alone creates a RoPE mismatch: masked tokens become
invisible under attention, but they still occupy positions during
training, so the relative distance between two visible tokens no longer
matches the rollout setting in which those masked tokens are physically
absent.

In
Step 2
, we regroup each training case by round. For every
round, we delete all masked-out tokens, keep only the tokens visible in
that round, and then bundle the resulting per-round traces from the same
case for joint optimization. This yields contiguous

position_ids
that match rollout exactly. For the advantage,
standard GRPO uses a sample-wise normalized quantity within a prompt
group, e.g.,

A_{i}^{\mathrm{GRPO}}=\frac{R_{i}-\mathrm{mean}(\{R_{j}\})}{\mathrm{std}(\{R_{j}\})}.

In our alternative design, we instead assign the same case-level reward
to every round from the same case and use
A_{r}=R-b
for all
rounds
r
, reusing the same shared advantage across the bundled
round-level traces.

This construction is methodologically faithful to the intended
multi-round computation, but in practice it is substantially more
expensive than the rollout-time cue-injection protocol used in the main
paper and did not produce comparable gains on AIME 2025.
