Uniqueness of addition in Lie rings 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) \mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K) and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) \mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K) Thanks: This article is an output of a research project (HSE-BR-2025-22) implemented as part of the Basic Research Program at HSE University.
Gennadiy Sosnov

Abstract
. We prove that for any Lie ring ℜ \mathfrak{R} and any commutator-preserving bijection α : 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) → ℜ \alpha:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{R} , the map α \alpha is additive on 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) \mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K) , where n ⩾ 2 n\geqslant 2 and K K is an arbitrary field. Using this result we find criteria for Lie rings 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) \mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K) and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ​ ( K ) \mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K) to be unique addition. We also show that any commutator-preserving injection of Lie rings β : 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ ( K ) → 𝔖 \beta:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S} is additive. This is the first result on additivity of commutator-preserving injections.

1.
Introduction

An associative ring
(R,+,\cdot)
is called a unique addition ring, or UA-ring for short, if for any ring
(S,\oplus,*)
every isomorphism
\alpha:R\rightarrow S
of the multiplicative semigroups
(R,\cdot)
and
(S,*)
is an isomorphism of rings. This concept traces back to Rickart’s works. In
[
8
]
he proved that every Boolean ring is a UA-ring. Later, Stephenson in
[
9
]
showed that every matrix ring
M_{n}(R)
is a UA-ring, where
n\geqslant 2
and
R
is an arbitrary associative ring with unit.

However, this theory is not well suited for non-associative rings. The first results on uniqueness of addition for Lie rings were obtained by Arzhantsev. In
[
2
]
it was proven that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
is a UA-Lie algebra for any field
K
with
\Char K\neq 2
. A year later, it was shown in
[
3
]
that every semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero is a UA-ring. Mayorova
[
7
]
proved that the uniqueness of addition holds in the case of Lie algebras of Chevalley type over rings with
1/2
and
1/3
.

Stronger results were obtained by Dolinar, Kuzma and Marovt
[
5
]
. They explicitly described the structure of commutator-preserving bijections from
M_{n}(K)
to themselves. Namely, for any commutator-preserving bijection
\varphi:M_{n}(K)\rightarrow M_{n}(K)
, where
K
is a field, there exists an invertible matrix
T\in M_{n}(K)
, a field automorphism
\sigma:K\rightarrow K
and a function
\psi:M_{n}(K)\rightarrow K
, where
\psi(A)=0
for all matrices of trace zero such that

(1)

for
n\geqslant 3
and
K
with at least
2^{n-1}
elements, either

\varphi(A)=TA^{\sigma}T^{-1}+\psi(A)E\quad\text{for all}\quad A\in M_{n}(K),

or

\varphi(A)=-T(A^{\sigma})^{\top}T^{-1}+\psi(A)E\quad\text{for all}\quad A\in M_{n}(K);

(2)

for
n=2
and
\Char K\neq 2
,

\varphi(A)=TA^{\sigma}T^{-1}+\psi(A)E\quad\text{for all}\quad A\in M_{n}(K).

Here
E
is the identity matrix and
A^{\sigma}
is the matrix obtained by applying
\sigma
to all matrix entries.

This article is devoted to the case of matrix Lie algebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
over arbitrary fields. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denote a finite field with
q
elements.

Theorem A
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Then the ring
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
is a UA-ring if and only if
n
is odd and
K={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

This result answers the question posed in
[
4
, Problem 1]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Then the ring
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is not a UA-ring if and only if
\Char K=2
and
n=2
.

We also consider a natural generalization of the concept of UA-rings to the case of injective maps.

Theorem C
.

Let
K
be a field with
\Char K\neq 2
and
\mathfrak{S}
be a Lie ring. Then any commutator-preserving injection
\beta:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is additive.

We prove Theorem A, Theorem B and Theorem C in Sections
3
,
5
,
4
, respectively. Section
2
contains basic definitions and preliminary results.

The author is grateful to Ivan Arzhantsev for posing the problem and useful suggestions throughout this work.

2.
Preliminary results

We start with the main definition for this work.

Definition 2.1
.

A Lie ring
\mathfrak{R}
is called a
unique addition Lie ring
or a
UA-Lie ring
if for any Lie ring
\mathfrak{S}
and any commutator-preserving bijection
\alpha:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, the map
\alpha
is additive, i.e. the condition
\alpha([a,b])=[\alpha(a),\alpha(b)]
for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{R}
implies
\alpha(a+b)=\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)
.

Let us define
C(a)=\{b\in\mathfrak{R}\mid[a,b]=0\}
to be the centralizer of an element
a
and
Z(\mathfrak{R})=\{x\in\mathfrak{R}\mid\forall y\in\mathfrak{R}:[x,y]=0\}
to be the center of a Lie ring
\mathfrak{R}
. Also, let us fix a commutator-preserving bijection
\alpha:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
. Clearly,
\alpha(0)=\alpha([0,0])=[\alpha(0),\alpha(0)]=0
. Now we consider the properties of
\alpha
in terms of the center and centralizers.

Lemma 2.2
.

The equality
\alpha(Z(\mathfrak{R}))=Z(\mathfrak{S})
holds.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z(\mathfrak{R})
. Then for all
x\in\mathfrak{R}
we have
0=\alpha([x,z])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(z)]
, therefore
\alpha(z)\in Z(\mathfrak{S})
. Hence,
\alpha(Z(\mathfrak{R}))\subseteq Z(\mathfrak{S})
. Applying this argument to the commutator-preserving bijection
\alpha^{-1}
, we obtain the required equality.
∎

Let us recall a definition that simplifies the study of UA-Lie rings.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be any commutator-preserving map of Lie rings. The map
\Delta:\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
that sends each pair
(a,b)
to the element
\alpha(a+b)-\alpha(a)-\alpha(b)
is called the
defect
of
\alpha
.

In
[
3
, Lemma 1]
it was proved that for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{R}
we have

\Delta(a,b)\in\alpha(C(a)\cap C(b)).

Moreover, a stronger statement holds.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
a,b\in\mathfrak{R}
we have

\Delta(a,b)\in\alpha(Z(C(a))\cap Z(C(b))).

Proof.

Let
\alpha(a+b)=\alpha(a)+\alpha(b)+\alpha(c)
. It is enough to show that
c\in Z(C(a))\cap Z(C(b))
. Let
x
be any element in
C(a)
. Since

\alpha([b,x])=\alpha([a+b,x])=[\alpha(a),\alpha(x)]+[\alpha(b),\alpha(x)]+[\alpha(c),\alpha(x)]=\alpha([b,x])+\alpha([c,x]),

we have
\alpha([c,x])=0
, therefore
[c,x]=0
and
c\in Z(C(a))
. Similarly, we obtain
{c\in Z(C(b))}
.
∎

A natural generalization of a UA-Lie ring is the concept of an
iUA
-ring.

Definition 2.5
.

A Lie ring
\mathfrak{R}
is called an
iUA-Lie ring
if for any Lie ring
\mathfrak{S}
and any commutator-preserving injection
\beta:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, the map
\beta
is additive.

It is easy to show that Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
fail in the case of injections. Nevertheless, the defect still has some interesting properties. Let us fix a commutator-preserving injection of Lie rings
\beta:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
and its defect
\Delta
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
x,y\in\mathfrak{R}
be elements such that there exist
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{R}
with

x=[a,[b,c]]\quad\text{and}\quad y=[b,[c,a]].

Then
\Delta(x,y)=0
.

Proof.

By the Jacobi identity we have

x+y=[a,[b,c]]+[b,[c,a]]=[[a,b],c],

so

\beta(x+y)=[[\beta(a),\beta(b)],\beta(c)]=[\beta(a),[\beta(b),\beta(c)]]+[\beta(b),[\beta(c),\beta(a)]]=\beta(x)+\beta(y).

Therefore,
\Delta(x,y)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{R}
we have

[\beta(x),\Delta(y,z)]=\Delta([x,y],[x,z]).

In particular,
\Delta([x,y],[x,z])=0
provided
\Delta(y,z)=0
.

Proof.

By using the commutator-preserving property, we obtain

[\beta(x),\Delta(y,z)]=[\beta(x),\beta(y+z)]-[\beta(x),\beta(y)]-[\beta(x),\beta(z)]=\Delta([x,y],[x,z]).

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

For any
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{R}
we have

\Delta(x+y,z)+\Delta(x,y)=\Delta(x,y+z)+\Delta(y,z).

Proof.

The left hand side is

\beta(x+y+z)-\beta(x+y)-\beta(z)+\beta(x+y)-\beta(x)-\beta(y)=\beta(x+y+z)-\beta(x)-\beta(y)-\beta(z),

and the right hand side is

\beta(x+y+z)-\beta(x)-\beta(y+z)+\beta(y+z)-\beta(y)-\beta(z)=\beta(x+y+z)-\beta(x)-\beta(y)-\beta(z).

∎

3.
UA-properties for the general linear Lie algebra

Let
E
be the identity matrix and
E_{ij}
be a matrix unit. It is shown in
[
4
, Corollary 2, Remark 2]
that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
is not a UA-Lie ring if either
K\neq{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
or
K={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and
n
is even. For the convenience of the reader, we provide a short proof of this result.

Proposition 3.1
.

The algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
is not a UA-Lie ring if either
K\neq{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
or
K={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and
n
is even.

Proof.

If the field
K
consists of more than
2
elements, then we can construct the bijection
\alpha:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
.

\alpha(X+tE)=X+\psi(t)E,\quad\text{for all}\quad X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K),t\in K,

where
\psi
is an arbitrary non-additive field bijection. It is obvious that the map
\alpha
preserves the commutator and is not additive.

On the other hand, if
K={\mathbb{F}}_{2}
and
n
is even, take a matrix
A
with non-zero trace. Clearly,
A\not\in[\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K),\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)]
and
A+E\not\in[\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K),\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)]
, therefore the map
\alpha:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
that swaps the elements
A
and
A+E
preserves the commutator but is not additive.
∎

Let us fix a commutator-preserving bijection
\alpha:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
, where
n\geqslant 3
(the case
n=2
is considered in Proposition
4.4
) with defect
\Delta
. Although
\alpha
is not additive in general, it is almost additive. More precisely, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K
be a field and
\alpha:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a commutator-preserving bijection. Then
\alpha
is additive on
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
, i.e.
\alpha(A+B)=\alpha(A)+\alpha(B)
for any
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
.

Before proving this proposition, we need the following lemmas. It is important to remember that
n\geqslant 3
; some of these proofs do not work in the case
n=2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any matrix unit
E_{ij}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
we have
Z(C(E_{ij}))=\langle E_{ij},E\rangle
.

Proof.

If
i=j
then
C(E_{ii})=\langle E_{ii}\rangle\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{n-1}(K)
. Therefore,
Z(C(E_{ii}))=\langle E_{ii},E\rangle
. Otherwise, if
i\neq j
then for all
s\neq j
and
t\neq i
we have
E_{st}\in C(E_{ij})
. Since
n>2
, these conditions ensure that
Z(C(E_{ij}))=\langle E_{ij},E\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
be a diagonal matrix. Then the set
Z(C(A))
consists of diagonal matrices.

Proof.

Let
X\in Z(C(A))
. Clearly,
E_{ii}-E_{jj}\in C(A)
for any
i\neq j
. Therefore, the equality
[X,E_{ii}-E_{jj}]=0
holds, so we have
X_{ik}=X_{ki}=X_{jk}=X_{kj}=0
for all
k\neq i
and
k\neq j
. Since
n>2
, the previous observation shows that
X
is a diagonal matrix.
∎

It is known that the center
Z(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K))
is the set of all scalar matrices. Let us denote it as
\mathfrak{z}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
be two diagonal matrices. Then
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
.

Proof.

Since the matrix units
E_{ii}
span the space of diagonal matrices, it is sufficient to show that
\Delta(A,\lambda E_{ii})\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
for all
i
and
\lambda\in K
by Lemma
2.8
. If
Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))=\mathfrak{z}
, then our lemma follows from Lemma
2.4
. Otherwise,
Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))\subseteq Z(C(A))
, so
[A,\lambda E_{ii}]=0
. Let
C=E_{ik}
, where
k\neq i
. Then
[\lambda E_{ii},A+C]=[\lambda E_{ii},C]\neq 0
. Therefore, the equality
Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))\cap Z(C(A+C))=\mathfrak{z}
holds. Since
A+\lambda E_{ii}
and
A
are diagonal matrices, the sets
Z(C(A+\lambda E_{ii}))
and
Z(C(A))
consist of diagonal matrices. Hence,

Z(C(A+\lambda E_{ii}))\cap Z(C(C))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(C))=\mathfrak{z}.

Applying Lemmas
2.4
and
2.8
, we obtain

\Delta(A,\lambda E_{ii})=\Delta(\lambda E_{ii},A+C)+\Delta(A,C)-\Delta(A+\lambda E_{ii},C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
and
B
be a diagonal matrix. Then
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
.

Proof.

Again, it is sufficient to prove the statement in the case
B=\lambda E_{ii}
. If
A
is a diagonal matrix, then the lemma follows from Lemma
3.5
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume that
A
is not a diagonal matrix. Let
s,t
be such different indices that the inequality
A_{st}\neq 0
holds. If
Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))=\mathfrak{z}
then the lemma follows from Lemma
2.4
. Otherwise,
Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))\subseteq Z(C(A))
, so
[A,\lambda E_{ii}]=0
. Since
t\neq i
, we have
[\lambda E_{ii},A+E_{ti}]\neq 0
,
[A,E_{ti}]\neq 0
and
[A+\lambda E_{ii},E_{ti}]\neq 0
. Therefore,

Z(C(A+\lambda E_{ii}))\cap Z(C(E_{ti}))=Z(C(\lambda E_{ii}))\cap Z(C(A+E_{ti}))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(E_{ti}))=\mathfrak{z}.

Hence,

\Delta(A,\lambda E_{ii})=\Delta(\lambda E_{ii},A+E_{ti})+\Delta(A,E_{ti})-\Delta(A+\lambda E_{ii},E_{ti})\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
, then
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the lemma for
B=\lambda E_{ij}
. The case
i=j
was proved in the previous lemma. So, without loss of generality, we can assume that
i\neq j
. Let
C
be a diagonal matrix such that
[A+C,B]\neq 0
. Then

\Delta(A+B,C),\Delta(B,A+C),\Delta(A,C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

Applying Lemma
2.8
, we obtain

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+C)+\Delta(A,C)-\Delta(A+B,C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The defect
\Delta(A,B)=0
for any diagonal matrices
A
and
B
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
.

Proof.

Let
J
be a nilpotent Jordan block, so
J=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i,i+1}
. The image of the adjoint operator of
J
contains the space of all diagonal matrices of trace zero. Therefore, for any diagonal matrices
A,B
there exist matrices
C,D
such that
A=[J,C]
and
B=[J,D]
. From Lemma
3.7
it follows that
\Delta(C,D)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
, hence

\Delta(A,B)=[\alpha(J),\Delta(C,D)]=0.

∎

Let us fix a standard basis
\mathcal{B}=\{E_{ij}\mid i\neq j\}\cup\{E_{11}-E_{ii}\mid i\neq 1\}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
. The key observation of the whole proof is the theorem proved by Albert and Muckenhoupt
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Any matrix in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is a commutator of two matrices in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
. Then
\Delta(\lambda_{1}X,\lambda_{2}X)=0
.

Proof.

Since
X=[A,B]
for some matrices
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
and
\Delta(\lambda_{1}B,\lambda_{2}B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
, it follows from Lemma
2.7
that

\Delta(\lambda_{1}X,\lambda_{2}X)=\Delta([A,\lambda_{1}B],[A,\lambda_{2}B])=[\alpha(A),\Delta(\lambda_{1}B,\lambda_{2}B)]=0.

∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
X
be any matrix in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
and
X=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}E_{ij}+\sum_{i}\beta_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii})
be the decomposition of
X
in the basis
\mathcal{B}
. Then

\alpha(X)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha(\beta_{ij}E_{ij})+\sum_{i}\alpha(\beta_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii})).

Proof.

Let
X=[A,B]
for some matrices
A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
and

A=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}E_{ij},\quad B=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}E_{ij}

be their decompositions. Then

\alpha(X)=[\alpha(A),\alpha(B)]=\sum_{i,j,k,s}\alpha([a_{ij}E_{ij},b_{ks}E_{ks}]).

Since
[a_{ij}E_{ij},b_{ks}E_{ks}]
is proportional to a basis element in
\mathcal{B}
or is equal to a diagonal matrix, it is sufficient to prove additivity for diagonal matrices, which we already have done in Lemma
3.8
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let us consider two arbitrary matrices
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
with decompositions in
\mathcal{B}

A=\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}E_{ij}+\sum_{i}\beta_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii})

and

B=\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}E_{ij}+\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii}).

Then the sum
A+B
has the following decomposition

A+B=\sum_{i,j}(\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij})E_{ij}+\sum_{i}(\beta_{i}+\gamma_{i})(E_{11}-E_{ii}),

so applying Lemma
3.11
we obtain

\alpha(A+B)=\sum_{i,j}\alpha((\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij})E_{ij})+\sum_{i}\alpha((\beta_{i}+\gamma_{i})(E_{11}-E_{ii}))=\\
=\sum_{i,j}\left[\alpha(\beta_{ij}E_{ij})+\alpha(\gamma_{ij}E_{ij})\right]+\sum_{i}\left[\alpha(\beta_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii}))+\alpha(\gamma_{i}(E_{11}-E_{ii}))\right]=\alpha(A)+\alpha(B).

∎

With this proposition we can obtain criteria for Lie ring
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
to be unique addition.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

The negative results were provided in Proposition
3.1
, so it is sufficient to prove that the ring
\mathfrak{gl}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is a UA-Lie ring for any
n\geqslant 1
. Let
\alpha
be a commutator-preserving bijection from
\mathfrak{gl}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
to the Lie ring
\mathfrak{S}
. Since
\alpha(E)+\alpha(E)\neq\alpha(E)
and
|Z(\mathfrak{S})|=2
, we have
\alpha(E)+\alpha(E)=0=\alpha(E+E)
. Therefore, we only need to prove that
\alpha(A+E)=\alpha(A)+\alpha(E)
for any matrix
A
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2n+1}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
. Assume the contrary, then
\alpha(A+E)=\alpha(A)+\alpha(E)+\alpha(T)
for some matrix
T\in Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(E))=\mathfrak{z}
and
T\neq 0
. Thus,
T=E
and
\alpha(A+E)=\alpha(A)
. We obtain a contradiction, so the map
\alpha
is additive.
∎

4.
Commutator-preserving injections

Let
\beta:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a commutator-preserving injection between two Lie rings with defect
\Delta
. Our goal is to determine when
\beta
is additive.

The article
[
4
]
states the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.1
.

Every semisimple Lie algebra is an iUA-Lie ring.

The same article proves the following negative result
[
4
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
K
be a field with at least
3
elements and
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over
K
such that
\mathfrak{g}\neq[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Then there exists a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
and a non-additive commutator-preserving injection
\beta:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{s}
.

This lemma can be easily generalized.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a Lie ring with at least
2
elements and
\mathfrak{R}\neq\{[x,y]\mid x,y\in\mathfrak{R}\}
. Then the ring
\mathfrak{R}
is not an iUA-Lie ring.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{S}=\mathfrak{R}\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
is an abelian Lie ring with exactly three elements. Also, let
y
be a fixed element in
\mathfrak{R}\setminus\{[x,y]\mid x,y\in\mathfrak{R}\}
. Then the desired injection
\beta:\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is defined as

\beta(x)=\begin{cases}(x,0),\quad x\neq y\\
(x,1),\quad x=y\end{cases}.

The injection
\beta
preserves the commutator because

\beta([a,b])=([a,b],0)=[\beta(a),\beta(b)],\quad\text{for all}\quad a,b\in\mathfrak{R}.

However,

(y+y,0)=\beta(y+y)\neq\beta(y)+\beta(y)=(y+y,2),

hence
\beta
is not additive.
∎

Therefore, every iUA-Lie ring has bracket width equal to
1
. For semisimple Lie algebras the question of maximal bracket width is open (see
[
6
]
). However, the article
[
6
]
constructs an infinite-dimensional simple Lie algebra with bracket width strictly greater than
1
, so there are simple Lie algebras that are not iUA-Lie rings.

We will prove Conjecture
4.1
in the case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
, where
\Char K\neq 2
. For the proof, we need to fix some notation. Let
e,f,h
be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
, so

e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
K
be a field with arbitrary characteristic. Then any commutator-preserving injection of Lie rings
\beta:\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is additive on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
.

To do this, we need to prove several lemmas.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
we have
\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}e)=0
and
\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}f)=0
.

Proof.

The case
\lambda_{1}=0
or
\lambda_{2}=0
is obvious. Therefore, we will consider the case
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\neq 0
. Applying Lemma
2.6
to the matrices

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda_{2}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},

we obtain
\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}e)=\Delta([A,[B,C]],[B,[C,A]])=0
. Since the identity

[A^{\top},[B^{\top},C^{\top}]]=[A,[B,C]]^{\top}

holds, we get the equality
\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}f)=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
we have

\Delta(\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{2}e)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\lambda_{1}f,\lambda_{2}f)=0.

Proof.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}&-\lambda_{2}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
[A,[B,C]]=\lambda_{1}e
and
[B,[C,A]]=\lambda_{2}e
. Applying Lemma
2.6
, we obtain the equality
\Delta(\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{2}e)=0
. The equality
\Delta(\lambda_{1}f,\lambda_{2}f)=0
is obtained by transposition.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For any
\lambda\in K
we have
\Delta(\lambda h,-\lambda h)=0
.

Proof.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
\lambda&0\end{pmatrix},\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
[A,[B,C]]=\lambda h
and
[B,[C,A]]=-\lambda h
. Applying Lemma
2.6
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

In fact, for any field
K
with
\Char K\neq 2
and any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K\setminus\{0\}
with
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\neq 0
, there are no matrices
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(K)
such that

\lambda_{1}h=[A,[B,C]],\quad\lambda_{2}h=[B,[C,A]].

Because otherwise
\lambda_{3}h=-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})h=[C,[A,B]]
and since
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(K)=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)\oplus\langle E\rangle
we can assume that
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
. If
\varkappa
is the Killing form on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
, then

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda_{1}h,A)

\displaystyle=\varkappa([A,[B,C]],A)=\varkappa([C,B],[A,A])=0\Rightarrow A\in\langle h\rangle^{\perp},

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda_{2}h,B)

\displaystyle=\varkappa([B,[C,A]],B)=\varkappa([A,C],[B,B])=0\Rightarrow B\in\langle h\rangle^{\perp},

\displaystyle\varkappa(\lambda_{3}h,C)

\displaystyle=\varkappa([C,[A,B]],C)=\varkappa([B,A],[C,C])=0\Rightarrow C\in\langle h\rangle^{\perp}.

But
\langle h\rangle^{\perp}=\langle e,f\rangle
, therefore
[B,C]\in\langle h\rangle
and
[A,[B,C]]\in\langle e,f\rangle
. We obtain a contradiction.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
we have
\Delta(\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{2}f)=0
.

Proof.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\lambda_{2}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\lambda_{1}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad C=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
[A,[B,C]]=-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{1}e
and
[B,[C,A]]=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{2}f
. Therefore,

0=\Delta(-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{2}f)=\beta(\lambda_{1}e+\lambda_{2}f)-\beta(-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{1}e)-\beta(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{2}f).

Applying Lemmas
4.5
and
4.7
, we obtain

\beta(\lambda_{1}e+\lambda_{2}f)-\beta(-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{1}e)-\beta(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h+\lambda_{2}f)=\\
=\beta(\lambda_{1}e+\lambda_{2}f)-\beta(-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h)-\beta(\lambda_{1}e)-\beta(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}h)-\beta(\lambda_{2}f)=\Delta(\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{2}f).

Therefore,
\Delta(\lambda_{1}e,\lambda_{2}f)=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
we have
\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}h)=0
.

Proof.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda_{2}\\
-\lambda_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Applying Lemmas
4.9
and
2.7
, we obtain

\Delta(\lambda_{1}h,\lambda_{2}h)=\Delta([A,e],[A,f])=[\beta(A),\Delta(e,f)]=0.

∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in K
we have

\Delta(\lambda_{1}h+\lambda_{2}e,\mu_{1}h+\mu_{2}f)=0.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{2}=0
, then applying Lemmas
4.5
and
4.10
, we obtain the required result. The case
\mu_{2}=0
is considered similarly. So we can assume
\lambda_{2}\neq 0
and
\mu_{2}\neq 0
. Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\\
-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then applying Lemmas
4.9
and
2.7
, we obtain

\Delta(\lambda_{1}h+\lambda_{2}e,\mu_{1}h+\mu_{2}f)=\Delta([A,\lambda_{2}e],[A,-\mu_{2}f])=[\beta(A),\Delta(\lambda_{2}e,-\mu_{2}f)]=0.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let

A=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&-a_{1}\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&-a_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows from Lemma
4.11
that

\beta(A+B)=\beta(a_{1}h+(b_{1}+b_{2})e)+\beta(a_{2}h+(c_{1}+c_{2})f).

Therefore, applying Lemma
4.5
, we obtain

\beta(a_{1}h+(b_{1}+b_{2})e)+\beta(a_{2}h+(c_{1}+c_{2})f)=\beta(a_{1}h+b_{1}e)+\beta(b_{2}e)+\beta(a_{2}h+c_{2}f)+\beta(c_{1}f).

Again, it follows from Lemma
4.11
that

\beta(a_{1}h+b_{1}e)+\beta(c_{1}f)=\beta(A),\quad\beta(a_{2}h+c_{2}f)+\beta(b_{2}e)=\beta(B),

so
\beta(A+B)=\beta(A)+\beta(B)
.
∎

With this proposition we can easily prove Theorem
C
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

Let
\beta:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a commutator-preserving injection of Lie rings. Let
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{S}\oplus Kz
be the Lie ring with bracket
[\mathfrak{S},Kz]=0
and
\beta^{\prime}:\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
be the map sending
A+kE
to
\beta(A)+kz
, where
A\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
and
k\in K
. The map
\beta^{\prime}
preserves the commutator, therefore
\beta^{\prime}
is additive on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
. Since
\beta^{\prime}|_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)}=\beta
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Using a computer, one can check that the map

\varphi:\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}\rightarrow[\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{R}]\times[\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{R}],\quad(a,b,c)\mapsto([a,[b,c]],[b,[c,a]])

is surjective for
\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{gl}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
. Therefore, applying Lemma
2.6
and modifying the proof of Theorem
C
, we can show that the ring
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}({\mathbb{F}}_{2})
is an iUA-Lie ring. From Remark
4.8
it follows that the map
\varphi
is not surjective for
\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(K)
, where
\Char K\neq 2
.

Problem 4.12
.

Is
\varphi
surjective for
\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)
, where
n\geqslant 3
?

5.
UA-properties for the special linear Lie algebra

First, let us prove the negative result.

Lemma 5.1
.

The Lie ring
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
is not a UA-Lie ring if
\Char K=2
.

Proof.

In this case
[\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K),\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)]=\langle E\rangle
, where
E
is the identity matrix. Therefore, we can construct a bijection
\alpha:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
in the following way

\alpha(x)=\begin{cases}e+E,&x=e\\
e,&x=e+E\\
x,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Clearly, it preserves the commutator and is not additive.
∎

For
\Char K\neq 2
we know from Theorem
C
that the algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
is an iUA-Lie ring, so it is definitely a UA-Lie ring. So we only need to prove that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is a UA-Lie ring for every
n\geqslant 3
and an arbitrary field
K
. Let the map
\alpha:\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a commutator-preserving bijection of Lie rings and
\Delta
be the defect of
\alpha
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Char K\nmid n
and
n\geqslant 3
. Then the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is a UA-Lie ring.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)\oplus\langle E\rangle
, we can extend the map
\alpha
to
\alpha^{\prime}:\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
by defining
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}
as
\mathfrak{S}\oplus Kz
, where
z
is a central element and

\alpha^{\prime}(A+kE)=\alpha(A)+kz.

Clearly, the map
\alpha^{\prime}
preserves the commutator. Applying Proposition
3.2
, we obtain that
\alpha^{\prime}
is additive on
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}|_{\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)}=\alpha
, we get the required result.
∎

So we can assume that
\Char K\mid n
. It is known from the work of Thompson
[
10
]
that any matrix
A\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
can be written as
A=[X,Y]
for some
X,Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
, except when
\Char K=2
and
n=2
. So the bracket width of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is equal to
1
for an arbitrary field
K
and
n\geqslant 3
. Let us denote
Z(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K))
as
\mathfrak{z}=\langle E\rangle
. Now it is clear from the proof of Proposition
3.2
that we only need to prove that
\Delta(A,B)\in\mathfrak{z}
for any
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E_{ij}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
be a matrix unit, where
i\neq j
. Then
Z(C(E_{ij}))=\langle E_{ij},E\rangle
.

Proof.

For all
s\neq j
and
t\neq i
we have
E_{st}\in C(E_{ij})
. Since
n>2
, these conditions ensure that
Z(C(E_{ij}))=\langle E_{ij},E\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
A\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
be a diagonal matrix. Then the set
Z(C(A))
consists only of diagonal matrices.

Proof.

Let
X\in Z(C(A))
. Clearly,
E_{ii}-E_{jj}\in C(A)
for any
i\neq j
. Therefore, the equality
[X,E_{ii}-E_{jj}]=0
holds, so we have
X_{ik}=X_{ki}=X_{jk}=X_{kj}=0
for all
k\neq i
and
k\neq j
. Since
n>2
, the previous observation shows us that
X
is a diagonal matrix.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
i\neq j
the following holds

Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj}))=\langle E_{ii}-E_{jj},E\rangle.

Proof.

From Lemma
5.4
we know that if
X\in Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj}))
, then
X
is diagonal. Let
s
and
t
be two indices not equal to
i
and
j
. Since
[X,E_{st}]=0
, we conclude that
X_{ss}=X_{tt}
for all such
s
and
t
. Assume that
\dim Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj}))\geqslant 3
. Then we can find linearly independent matrices

E_{ii}-E_{jj},E,Y\in Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj})).

Therefore, we can assume that
Y_{ss}=0
for all
s\neq i
. Since
\operatorname{tr}(Y)=0
, we conclude that
Y=0
. We obtain a contradiction, so
\dim Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj}))\leqslant 2
. Since the inclusion
\langle E_{ii}-E_{jj},E\rangle\subseteq Z(C(E_{ii}-E_{jj}))
holds, we get the required result.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
be two diagonal matrices. Then
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
.

Proof.

Since the matrices
E_{ii}-E_{jj}
span the space of diagonal matrices, it is sufficient to prove the lemma for
B=\lambda(E_{ii}-E_{jj})
, where
i\neq j
and
\lambda\in K
. If
Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(B))=\mathfrak{z}
, then the lemma follows from Lemma
2.4
. Otherwise,
Z(C(B))\subseteq Z(C(A))
, hence the equality
[A,B]=0
holds. Let
C=E_{ik}
, where
k\neq i
and
k\neq j
. Then
[B,A+C]\neq 0
, therefore
Z(C(B))\cap Z(C(A+C))=\mathfrak{z}
. Since
A+B
and
A
are diagonal matrices, we conclude that the sets
Z(C(A+B))
and
Z(C(A))
consist of diagonal matrices. Hence,

Z(C(A+B))\cap Z(C(C))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(C))=\mathfrak{z}.

Applying Lemmas
2.4
and
2.8
, we obtain

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+C)+\Delta(A,C)-\Delta(A+B,C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
and
B
be a diagonal matrix. Then
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
.

Proof.

Again, it is sufficient to prove the statement in the case
B=\lambda(E_{ii}-E_{jj})
. If
A
is a diagonal matrix, then the lemma follows from Lemma
3.5
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume that
A
is not a diagonal matrix. Let
A_{st}\neq 0
, where
s\neq t
. If
Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(B))=\mathfrak{z}
, then the lemma follows from Lemma
2.4
. Otherwise,
Z(C(B))\subseteq Z(C(A))
, so
[A,B]=0
.

Case 1.
Let
s\in\{i,j\}
,
t\in\{i,j\}
and
k\not\in\{i,j\}
. Since
[B,A+E_{ks}]=[B,E_{ks}]\neq 0
,
[A,E_{ks}]\neq 0
and
[A+B,E_{ks}]\neq 0
, we have

Z(C(A+B))\cap Z(C(E_{ks}))=Z(C(B))\cap Z(C(A+E_{ks}))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(E_{ks}))=\mathfrak{z}.

Hence,

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+E_{ks})+\Delta(A,E_{ks})-\Delta(A+B,E_{ks})\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

Case 2.
Let
s\neq i
and
s\neq j
. Since
[B,A+E_{is}]=[B,E_{is}]\neq 0
,
[A,E_{is}]\neq 0
and
[A+B,E_{is}]\neq 0
, we have

Z(C(A+B))\cap Z(C(E_{is}))=Z(C(B))\cap Z(C(A+E_{is}))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(E_{is}))=\mathfrak{z}.

Hence,

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+E_{is})+\Delta(A,E_{is})-\Delta(A+B,E_{is})\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

Case 3.
Let
t\neq i
and
t\neq j
. Since
[B,A+E_{ti}]=[B,E_{ti}]\neq 0
,
[A,E_{ti}]\neq 0
and
[A+B,E_{ti}]\neq 0
, we have

Z(C(A+B))\cap Z(C(E_{ti}))=Z(C(B))\cap Z(C(A+E_{ti}))=Z(C(A))\cap Z(C(E_{ti}))=\mathfrak{z}.

Hence,

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+E_{ti})+\Delta(A,E_{ti})-\Delta(A+B,E_{ti})\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Let us again fix a standard basis
\mathcal{B}=\{E_{ij}\mid i\neq j\}\cup\{E_{11}-E_{ii}\mid i\neq 1\}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The defect
\Delta(A,B)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z})
for any
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove this lemma for matrix
B
that is proportional to an element in
\mathcal{B}
. The case
B=\lambda(E_{ii}-E_{jj})
was proved in the previous lemma. So, without loss of generality, we can assume that
B=\lambda E_{ij}
, where
i\neq j
. Let
C
be a diagonal matrix such that
[A+C,B]\neq 0
. Then

\Delta(A+B,C),\Delta(B,A+C),\Delta(A,C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

Applying Lemma
2.8
, we obtain

\Delta(A,B)=\Delta(B,A+C)+\Delta(A,C)-\Delta(A+B,C)\in\alpha(\mathfrak{z}).

∎

Since the algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
has commutator width
1
, we can repeat the proof of Proposition
3.2
, but for the convenience of the reader we summarize the whole proof of Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

From Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
C
we obtain that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(K)
is a UA-Lie ring if and only if
\Char K\neq 2
. From Lemma
5.2
we conclude that
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
is a UA-Lie ring if
\Char K\nmid n
.

Now let
n\geqslant 3
,
\Char K\mid n
and
\alpha:\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be a commutator-preserving bijection of Lie rings with defect
\Delta
. From Lemma
5.8
we obtain that
\Delta(A,B)\in Z(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K))
for any
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
. Similarly to what we did in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, we obtain that
\Delta(A,B)=0
for any diagonal matrices
A,B\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
. Since the Lie ring
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
has bracket width
1
, we get that
\Delta(\lambda_{1}X,\lambda_{2}X)=0
for any
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in K
and
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(K)
. Therefore,
\alpha
preserves decompositions with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
, so
\alpha
is additive.
∎
