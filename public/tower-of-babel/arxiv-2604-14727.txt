Abstract
To quantify the geometric expressivity of transformers, we introduce a tropical geometry framework to characterize their exact spatial partitioning capabilities. By modeling self-attention as a vector-valued tropical rational map, we prove it evaluates exactly to a Power Voronoi Diagram in the zero-temperature limit. Building on this equivalence, we establish a combinatorial rationale for Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA): via the Minkowski sum of Newton polytopes, multi-head aggregation expands the polyhedral complexity to 𝒪 ⁡ ( N H ) \mathcal{O}(N^{H}) , overcoming the 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \mathcal{O}(N) bottleneck of single heads. Extending this to deep architectures, we derive the first tight asymptotic bounds on the number of linear regions in transformers ( Θ ⁡ ( N d model ​ L ) \Theta(N^{d_{\text{model}}L}) ), demonstrating a combinatorial explosion driven intrinsically by sequence length N N , ambient embedding dimension d model d_{\text{model}} , and network depth L L . Importantly, we guarantee that this idealized polyhedral skeleton is geometrically stable: finite-temperature soft attention preserves these topological partitions via exponentially tight differential approximation bounds.

III-A

Geometric Expressivity and Linear Regions

Before introducing the algebraic framework, we first formalize the concept of
expressivity
investigated in this work. In the theoretical analysis of deep learning, expressive power quantifies a network’s structural capacity to approximate complex, highly non-linear functions. For networks utilizing piecewise-linear activation functions (such as MLPs with ReLU), the network computes a CPWL function. The expressivity of such models is classically quantified by the number of maximal linear regions they partition the input space into
[
31
,
35
,
18
,
43
]
.

Definition III.1

(Maximal Linear Region
[
31
,
35
]
)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}}
be a CPWL mapping representing a neural network, where
d
and
d_{\text{out}}
denote the input and output dimensions, respectively. A
maximal linear region

\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as a non-empty, connected open subset such that the restriction
\Phi|_{\omega}
is an affine function (i.e.,
\Phi(x)=Ax+b
for some
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}\times d}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}}}
), and for any strictly larger
connected
open set
\omega^{\prime}\supsetneq\omega
, the restriction
\Phi|_{\omega^{\prime}}
is not affine.

Why Linear Regions Measure Expressivity:
The number of maximal linear regions serves as a fundamental structural measure for the expressivity of CPWL networks
[
31
]
. Within each such region, the network’s mapping restricts to a local affine transformation. The global non-linearity and complex decision boundaries emerge from the interactions across these polyhedral boundaries. Therefore, a higher count of regions corresponds to a finer tessellation of the input domain, characterizing the network’s capacity to approximate functions with complex topographies or high-frequency oscillations
[
35
,
18
]
.

Definition III.2

(Zaslavsky’s Theorem)
.

Let
R(n,d)
denote the number of regions induced by an arrangement of
n
hyperplanes in general position in a space of dimension
d

[
46
]
:

R(n,d)\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{n}{j}.

For
n\gg d
, this sum is dominated by the highest-order term:
R(n,d)\approx\frac{1}{d!}n^{d}
.

Example III.3

(1-layer and 2-layer ReLU MLP of linear regions)
.

Consider a 2-layer ReLU MLP mapping
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{out}}
with ambient dimension
d=2
and
n=3
neurons per layer. According to
Definition
III.2
, the first hidden layer induces exactly
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{n}{j}=\binom{3}{0}+\binom{3}{1}+\binom{3}{2}=7
maximal linear regions. In
Figure
2
, deep composition facilitates recursive shattering: the second layer’s hyperplanes intersect the folded image of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, subdividing each pre-existing region. This multiplicative interaction yields a theoretical maximum of
\mathcal{N}_{2}=\mathcal{N}_{1}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{n}{j}=49
regions. This geometric escalation provides a classical measure of expressivity, which this work extends to the non-piecewise-linear self-attention mechanism.

The Obstruction in Transformers:
While the exact counting of linear regions has matured for classical Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs)
[
31
,
35
,
18
]
, extending this topological metric to the transformer architecture inherently faces a mathematical challenge. The self-attention mechanism, characterized by the softmax function, is smooth and non-piecewise-linear. Therefore, it does
not trivially
induce discrete linear regions, rendering classical hyperplane arrangement theories inapplicable.

To bridge this gap and quantify the continuous spatial partitioning capabilities of attention mechanisms, we must adopt a geometric framework capable of translating smooth log-sum-exp operations into piecewise-linear polytopes. This necessitates the introduction of

III-B

Tropical Algebra and Geometry

We operate over the
max-plus semiring
(also known as the tropical semiring), denoted by
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\},\oplus,\otimes)

[
30
,
25
]
.

Definition III.4

(Tropical Semiring Operations)
.

For any scalars
a,b\in\mathbb{T}
, the tropical addition and multiplication are defined as
[
26
]
:

a\oplus b:=\max(a,b),\quad a\otimes b:=a+b.

The identity elements for
\oplus
and
\otimes
are
-\infty
and
0
, respectively.

These operations extend to matrices and vectors in the standard manner. If
A,B\in\mathbb{T}^{n\times n}
, their product is
(A\otimes B)_{ij}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}(A_{ik}\otimes B_{kj})
, which exactly corresponds to standard matrix multiplication evaluated in the
(\max,+)
algebra
[
39
]
.

Definition III.5

(Tropical Polynomial and Newton Polytope)
.

A tropical polynomial
P:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function of the form
[
36
]
:

P(x)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\left(c_{j}\otimes x^{\otimes\alpha_{j}}\right)=\max_{j=1,\dots,m}\left(c_{j}+\langle\alpha_{j},x\rangle\right),

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the input vector,
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are scalar coefficients, and
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are exponent vectors. The geometric structure of
P
is completely characterized by its
Newton Polytope
, denoted as
\text{Newt}(P)
, which is defined as the convex hull of the exponent vectors
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
47
,
37
]
.

Geometrically, a tropical polynomial defines a convex CPWL function whose graph forms a convex polyhedron. Projecting this graph onto the input space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
yields a regular subdivision into maximal linear regions, fully determined by
\text{Newt}(P)
and the coefficients
c_{j}
. Since tropical division corresponds to standard subtraction (
\oslash
), this extends to tropical rational functions, as defined in
Definition
III.6
.

Definition III.6

(Tropical Rational Map)
.

A tropical rational function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as the tropical quotient (i.e., standard subtraction) of two tropical polynomials, expressed as
F(x)=P(x)\oslash Q(x)=P(x)-Q(x)

[
30
,
47
]
. Extending this, a
vector-valued tropical rational map

\mathbf{F}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{out}}
is a mapping where each coordinate function
F_{c}(x)
takes the form
P_{c}(x)\oslash Q_{c}(x)
. We define it as
structured
if all output coordinates strictly share a common denominator
Q_{c}(x)=Q(x)
.

Geometrically, while tropical polynomials strictly represent convex CPWL functions, tropical rational maps represent Difference-of-Convex (DC) CPWL functions, which are capable of forming complex, non-convex spatial decision boundaries
[
37
]
.

Definition III.7

(Normal Fan and Tropical Hypersurface)
.

For a tropical polynomial
P(x)=\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,m\}}(\langle\alpha_{j},x\rangle+c_{j})
, the
tropical hypersurface

\mathcal{H}(P)
is the set of points where the maximum is attained by at least two distinct terms
[
25
]
:

\mathcal{H}(P)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\exists i\neq j,\,\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+c_{i}=\langle\alpha_{j},x\rangle+c_{j}=P(x)\}.

The
normal fan

\Sigma(P)
is the collection of polyhedral cones
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
that partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where each
d
-dimensional cone
\sigma_{j}
is defined by the optimality of the
j
-th term
[
48
]
:

\sigma_{j}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle\alpha_{j},x\rangle+c_{j}\geq\langle\alpha_{i},x\rangle+c_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\}.

The union of all lower-dimensional faces of these cones constitutes
\mathcal{H}(P)
. For a structured rational map
\mathbf{F}
, the global decision boundary
\mathcal{H}
is defined as the union of hypersurfaces across all coordinates:
\mathcal{H}=\bigcup_{c}\mathcal{H}(P_{c})\cup\mathcal{H}(Q)
.

Definition III.8

(Common Refinement
[
13
,
48
]
)
.

Given two polyhedral fans
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, their
common refinement
, denoted as
\Sigma_{1}\wedge\Sigma_{2}
, is the fan obtained by taking all possible intersections of cones from each:
\Sigma_{1}\wedge\Sigma_{2}=\{\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}\mid\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}\}
. This operation corresponds to the superimposition of the spatial partitions induced by both fans.

III-C

Maslov Dequantization

To bridge standard neural network operations with tropical geometry, we utilize
Maslov dequantization

[
24
]
. This process treats standard arithmetic as a continuous deformation of tropical arithmetic, controlled by a temperature parameter
\tau>0
.

Definition III.9

(Deformed Semiring Operations)
.

For a given temperature
\tau>0
, we define the smooth operations:

a\oplus_{\tau}b:=\tau\log(e^{a/\tau}+e^{b/\tau}),\quad a\otimes_{\tau}b:=a+b.

The operator
\oplus_{\tau}
is the standard
LogSumExp
(LSE) function scaled by
\tau
. It is a foundational property that
\lim_{\tau\to 0^{+}}(a\oplus_{\tau}b)=\max(a,b)=a\oplus b
. Consequently, standard matrix multiplication involving the LSE operation asymptotically converges to tropical matrix multiplication as
\tau\to 0
. This strict asymptotic boundary is referred to as the
tropical limit
or
zero-temperature limit
.

III-D

The Tropical Formulation of Self-Attention

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{\text{model}}}
denote the sequence of
N
input tokens, where each row
x_{i}
is a
d_{\text{model}}
-dimensional embedding. For a single attention head, we define the query, key, and value matrices as
Q=XW_{Q}
,
K=XW_{K}
, and
V=XW_{V}
, where
W_{Q},W_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{model}\times d_{k}}
and
W_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{model}\times d_{v}}
are learnable projections. Specifically, these matrices are composed of row vectors
Q=[q_{1},\dots,q_{N}]^{\top}
,
K=[k_{1},\dots,k_{N}]^{\top}
, and
V=[v_{1},\dots,v_{N}]^{\top}
, where
q_{i},k_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
and
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}
represent the embeddings for the
i
-th token. Furthermore, we denote the
j
-th element of the
i
-th value vector
v_{i}
as
v_{i,j}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,d_{v}\}
.

Identifying the temperature parameter
\tau
with the inverse scaling factor (typically
1/\sqrt{d_{k}}
), the
i
-th row of the standard dot-product attention output,
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d_{v}}
, is computed as
[
19
]
:

z_{i}=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\langle q_{i},k_{j}\rangle/\tau}v_{j}}{\sum_{l=1}^{N}e^{\langle q_{i},k_{l}\rangle/\tau}}.

For the purpose of spatial partitioning analysis, we represent the attention mechanism as a continuous functional mapping
Z:\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}
. For any arbitrary query vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}
, the output is given by
Z(q)=\sum_{j}\text{softmax}(\langle q,k_{j}\rangle/\tau)v_{j}
, such that the
i
-th row of the output matrix is simply
z_{i}=Z(q_{i})
.

To analyze this strictly under the tropical framework, we evaluate the computation in the logarithmic domain, which corresponds to the native space of the tropical semiring. Since standard division corresponds to tropical subtraction (denoted as
\oslash
), the log-domain output acts as a difference of two functions.

Definition III.10

(Log-Lifted Tropical Representation of Attention)
.

Let
c\in\{1,\dots,d_{v}\}
denote a specific output channel dimension. To preserve value information under the tropical limit, we introduce a formal algebraic device called
log-lifting
, parameterizing strictly positive standard values as
v_{j,c}=e^{\tilde{v}_{j,c}/\tau}
, where
\tilde{v}_{j,c}\in\mathbb{R}
is the log-lifted value. As
\tau\to 0
, the log-domain attention output for the
c
-th channel of the
i
-th token converges to:

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau\log(z_{i,c})=P_{\text{num},c}(q_{i})\oslash P_{\text{denom}}(q_{i}),

where the numerator and denominator converge to distinct tropical polynomials:

P_{\text{num},c}(q_{i}):=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\left(\langle q_{i},k_{j}\rangle\otimes\tilde{v}_{j,c}\right),\quad P_{\text{denom}}(q_{i}):=\bigoplus_{l=1}^{N}\langle q_{i},k_{l}\rangle.

Remark III.11

(Log-Lifted Values and Asymptotic Degeneracy)
.

The log-lifting parameterization
v_{j,c}=e^{\tilde{v}_{j,c}/\tau}
is introduced as a formal algebraic device to express the numerator within the tropical semiring under Maslov dequantization. For any fixed temperature
\tau>0
, this constitutes a reparameterization of the same numerical value matrix. However, as a
\tau
-indexed family approaching zero temperature, it defines a distinct asymptotic scaling regime whose limit behavior differs from that of a fixed value matrix.

To clarify the necessity of this device, consider instead a standard, temperature-independent value matrix
V
. For the purpose of log-domain evaluation assume
v_{j,c}>0
(in the standard architecture values may be signed, in which case the tropical lifting is not directly defined). Then

\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau\log v_{j,c}=0.

Consequently, the tropical polynomial associated with the numerator coincides identically with that of the denominator:

\max_{j}\big(\langle q,k_{j}\rangle+0\big)=\max_{j}\langle q,k_{j}\rangle.

Their tropical subtraction therefore degenerates to the neutral element
0
in the log-domain. Hence, without log-lifting, the tropical abstraction collapses to a piecewise constant selector over the Voronoi partition induced by the Query-Key interaction. Log-lifting is therefore introduced to avoid this algebraic collapse, enabling a non-degenerate tropical rational representation while preserving the same routing hypersurfaces
\mathcal{H}
. Importantly, while Log-Lifting is required to preserve the
value information
within the tropical algebraic structure, the
topological partitioning
of the input space is determined exclusively by
\langle q,k_{j}\rangle
, which are independent of
V
.

In a standard transformer where the value matrix
V
is fixed and independent of temperature (i.e., no log-lifting), the zero-temperature limit
\tau\to 0
does not imply a zero output in the linear arithmetic domain; rather, it reflects the concentration of the exponential weights onto the single dominant index. Specifically, the softmax distribution degenerates into a hard
one-hot
vector selecting the index
j^{*}=\arg\max_{j}\langle q,k_{j}\rangle
. Consequently, the mapping
Z(q)
converges strictly to the unmodified value vector
v_{j^{*}}
:

Z(q)\xrightarrow{\tau\to 0}v_{j^{*}},\quad\text{where }j^{*}=\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,max}}_{j}\langle q,k_{j}\rangle.

Thus, the tropical framework (via Log-Lifting) captures the boundaries of the function (the
where
), while the standard formulation determines the magnitude (the
what
) as a constant mapping within each polytope. The difference is only a scaling factor in the limiting definition, not a topological distortion, ensuring that the geometric complexity bounds derived from the tropical skeleton remain valid for standard transformers.

Remark III.12

(CPWL and Normal Fan Refinement)
.

Since the tropical polynomials
P_{\text{num},c}
and
P_{\text{denom}}
are polyhedral convex functions, their difference

IV
The Geometric Structure of Self-Attention

In this section, before analyzing the spatial partitioning induced by self-attention, we formalize the geometric concepts of standard and weighted tessellations from computational geometry.

Definition IV.1

(Standard Voronoi Diagram
[
14
]
)
.

Let
S=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of distinct generator points (sites). The
Standard Voronoi Diagram
partitions
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into
N
convex polyhedral regions
\{V_{1},\dots,V_{N}\}
. Each cell
V_{j}
is defined by the standard Euclidean metric, encompassing all points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are closer to
c_{j}
than to any other site
c_{l}

[
44
]
:

V_{j}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\|x-c_{j}\|\leq\|x-c_{l}\|,\quad\forall l\neq j\right\}.

Geometrically, the bounding hyperplanes between adjacent cells are the exact perpendicular bisectors of the line segments joining the sites.

While the standard Voronoi diagram relies on unweighted Euclidean distances, assigning individual scalar weights to the generator sites fundamentally alters the boundary geometry, yielding the Power Voronoi Diagram.

Definition IV.2

(Power Voronoi Diagram
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\hat{S}=\{(c_{1},w_{1}),\dots,(c_{N},w_{N})\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
be a set of generating sites with associated scalar weights. The
power distance
(or Laguerre distance) from a spatial point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to a weighted site is defined compactly as
d_{\text{pow}}(x,c_{j})\coloneqq\|x-c_{j}\|^{2}-w_{j}
. The
Power Voronoi Diagram
is the cellular complex
\{P_{1},\dots,P_{N}\}
partitioning
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where each cell
P_{j}
is defined by the strict optimality of this power distance:

P_{j}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid d_{\text{pow}}(x,c_{j})\leq d_{\text{pow}}(x,c_{l}),\quad\forall l\neq j\right\}.

Unlike the standard diagram, bounding hyperplanes are shifted orthogonally away from the midplanes based on weight differences. Therefore, a power cell
P_{j}
can be topologically empty if its site is heavily dominated by adjacent larger weights.

Building upon these strict geometric structures, we analyze input space partitioning by self-attention in the zero-temperature limit (
\tau\to 0
). We show that attention divides the projected query space into convex polyhedra, and as softmax collapses to a one-hot indicator, the routing becomes algebraically equivalent to a

, with cells determined by the key vectors’ metric properties.

(Voronoi Routing Theorem)

away from standard midplanes, where the full-dimensional cells are defined by:

C_{j}=\left\{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}\mid\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}-w_{j}\leq\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}-w_{l},\quad\forall l\neq j\right\}.

Uniform Function Approximation.
The function value converges exponentially to the tropical limit:

VIII
Conclusion

We introduced a tropical geometry framework to quantify transformer expressivity. By proving an equivalence between zero-temperature self-attention and Power Voronoi diagram, for the first time, we show that the tight maximal number of linear regions scales as
\Theta(N^{d_{\text{model}}L})
, with per-layer complexity growing as
\mathcal{O}(N^{H})
through the Minkowski sum of multi-head Newton polytopes. Stability bounds further ensure that this structure persists at finite temperatures. Proof.

We aim to establish the set equality
\mathcal{R}_{j}^{\text{Attn}}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\text{Power}}
by showing that the defining condition of the Attention Region is algebraically equivalent to the defining condition of the Power Voronoi Cell under the specific weight assignment
w_{l}=\|k_{l}\|^{2}
.

Consider an arbitrary query vector
q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By definition,
q\in\mathcal{R}_{j}^{\text{Attn}}
if and only if the dot product with key
k_{j}
is maximal:

\langle q,k_{j}\rangle\geq\langle q,k_{l}\rangle,\quad\forall l\neq j.

(1)

We utilize the polarization identity to express the inner product in terms of Euclidean norms. For any vector
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the squared Euclidean distance is expanded as:

\|x-y\|^{2}=\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}-2\langle x,y\rangle.

Solving for the inner product term
\langle x,y\rangle
:

\langle x,y\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}-\|x-y\|^{2}\right).

(2)

Substituting the identity Eq. (
2
) into the inequality Eq. (
1
) for both the left-hand side (
x=q,y=k_{j}
) and the right-hand side (
x=q,y=k_{l}
):

\frac{1}{2}\left(\|q\|^{2}+\|k_{j}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|q\|^{2}+\|k_{l}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}\right).

Since the scalar factor
\frac{1}{2}
is positive, we multiply both sides by
2
:

\|q\|^{2}+\|k_{j}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}\geq\|q\|^{2}+\|k_{l}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}.

Subtracting the common term
\|q\|^{2}
(which is non-negative and independent of the index
l
) from both sides:

\|k_{j}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}\geq\|k_{l}\|^{2}-\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}.

We rearrange the terms to isolate the distance metrics. Subtracting
\|k_{j}\|^{2}
and
\|k_{l}\|^{2}
from their respective sides and multiplying the entire inequality by
-1
(which reverses the inequality direction from
\geq
to
\leq
):

\displaystyle-\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}+\|k_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}+\|k_{l}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\quad\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}-\|k_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}-\|k_{l}\|^{2}.

Let us define the weight for the
m
-th site as
w_{m}:=\|k_{m}\|^{2}
. The inequality becomes:

\|q-k_{j}\|^{2}-w_{j}\leq\|q-k_{l}\|^{2}-w_{l}.

This is precisely the defining condition for the Power Voronoi Cell
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\text{Power}}
centered at site
s_{j}=k_{j}
with weight
w_{j}=\|k_{j}\|^{2}
.
Since the derivation consists of a chain of ”if and only if” algebraic manipulations, we conclude:

q\in\mathcal{R}_{j}^{\text{Attn}}\iff q\in\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\text{Power}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{R}_{j}^{\text{Attn}}=\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\text{Power}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof.

Let
d=d_{\text{model}}
denote the ambient dimension. For a single attention head, the tropical potential is

P_{\mathrm{single}}(q)=\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,N\}}\langle q,k_{j}\rangle.

By definition of the Newton polytope of a tropical polynomial,

\mathrm{Newt}(P_{\mathrm{single}})=\mathrm{Conv}\{k_{1},\dots,k_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since the vertex set of a convex hull is a subset of its generating set,

V_{\mathrm{single}}=|\mathrm{Vert}(\mathrm{Newt}(P_{\mathrm{single}}))|\leq N.

For head
h
,

f_{h}(q)=\max_{j}\langle W_{Q}^{(h)}q,k_{j}^{(h)}\rangle.

Using the adjoint property of the inner product,

\langle Ax,y\rangle=\langle x,A^{\top}y\rangle,

we obtain

f_{h}(q)=\max_{j}\langle q,(W_{Q}^{(h)})^{\top}k_{j}^{(h)}\rangle.

Define

\tilde{k}_{j}^{(h)}=(W_{Q}^{(h)})^{\top}k_{j}^{(h)}.

Then

P_{h}=\mathrm{Newt}(f_{h})=\mathrm{Conv}\{\tilde{k}_{1}^{(h)},\dots,\tilde{k}_{N}^{(h)}\}.

Hence

|\mathrm{Vert}(P_{h})|\leq N.

In the tropical abstraction, the multi-head potential is

F(q)=\bigotimes_{h=1}^{H}f_{h}(q)=\sum_{h=1}^{H}f_{h}(q).

A fundamental property of Newton polytopes of tropical polynomials yields

\mathrm{Newt}(F)=P_{1}+\cdots+P_{H},

where the Minkowski sum is

P_{1}+\cdots+P_{H}=\{v_{1}+\dots+v_{H}\mid v_{h}\in P_{h}\}.

We now bound

V_{\mathrm{multi}}=|\mathrm{Vert}(P_{1}+\cdots+P_{H})|.

A classical result due to
[
15
]
states that for polytopes
P_{1},\dots,P_{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

f_{0}(P_{1}+\cdots+P_{H})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom{H-1}{k}\max_{|I|=k+1}\prod_{h\in I}f_{0}(P_{h}),

where
f_{0}(\cdot)
denotes the number of vertices. Since
f_{0}(P_{h})\leq N
for all
h
,

f_{0}(P_{1}+\cdots+P_{H})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom{H-1}{k}N^{k+1}.

If
H\leq d
, then

V_{\mathrm{multi}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\binom{H-1}{k}N^{k+1}.

Factor out
N
:

V_{\mathrm{multi}}=N\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\binom{H-1}{k}N^{k}.

By the binomial theorem,

\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\binom{H-1}{k}N^{k}=(1+N)^{H-1}.

Therefore,

V_{\mathrm{multi}}\leq N(1+N)^{H-1}=\mathcal{O}(N^{H}).

If
H>d
, then

V_{\mathrm{multi}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom{H-1}{k}N^{k+1}.

Using the bound

\binom{H-1}{k}\leq(H-1)^{k},

we obtain

V_{\mathrm{multi}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(H-1)^{k}N^{k+1}.

Factor out
N
:

V_{\mathrm{multi}}=N\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}((H-1)N)^{k}.

For fixed
d
, the truncated geometric series satisfies

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}((H-1)N)^{k}=\mathcal{O}(((H-1)N)^{d-1}).

Hence

V_{\mathrm{multi}}=\mathcal{O}(N(HN)^{d-1})=\mathcal{O}((NH)^{d}).

Independently of
H
, the Minkowski sum is a
d
-dimensional polytope whose vertex count cannot exceed the McMullen Upper Bound Theorem for a
d
-polytope generated by at most
\sum_{h=1}^{H}|\mathrm{Vert}(P_{h})|\leq NH
points. Therefore,

V_{\mathrm{multi}}\leq\mathcal{O}\!\left((NH)^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\right).

Combining the two regimes,

V_{\mathrm{multi}}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{O}(N^{H}),&H\leq d,\\
\mathcal{O}((NH)^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}),&H>d.\end{cases}.

Substituting
d=d_{\text{model}}
completes the proof.

By the standard duality between convex hulls and normal fans

, the collection

constitutes the
outer normal fan

. There is a bijection between the full-dimensional (

A fundamental identity in convex geometry

The FFN layer

introduces a partition

induced by the arrangement of hyperplanes

[

|\mathcal{P}_{\text{FFN}}|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom{d_{ff}}{k}\leq\left(\frac{ed_{ff}}{d}\right)^{d}:=\mathcal{M}_{\text{FFN}}.

The total partition

|\mathcal{S}_{l+1}|=|\mathcal{P}_{\text{MHSA}}\wedge\mathcal{P}_{\text{FFN}}|\leq\mathcal{M}_{\text{MHSA}}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\text{FFN}}.

Recurrence Relation.
Let
\mathcal{R}_{l}
denote the collection of maximal linear regions induced by the first

. Since the class of CPWL functions is closed under composition, the cumulative map

is strictly CPWL. Consequently, for any region
\Omega\in\mathcal{R}_{l}
, the restriction
\Phi_{l}|_{\Omega}
coincides with an affine map, explicitly
\Phi_{l}(x)=A_{\Omega}x+b_{\Omega}
. The sub-partition of
\Omega
by the subsequent layer
l+1
is formed by the preimage intersections:

\text{Refine}(\Omega)=\{\Omega\cap\Phi_{l}^{-1}(S)\mid S\in\mathcal{S}_{l+1},\,\Omega\cap\Phi_{l}^{-1}(S)\neq\emptyset\}.

Since
\Phi_{l}
is affine, preimages of convex sets are convex; hence intersections remain convex regions. The cardinality is strictly bounded by
|\text{Refine}(\Omega)|\leq|\mathcal{S}_{l+1}|
.
The total number of regions at layer
l+1
is obtained by summing the complexity contribution from each region in the previous layer:

N_{l+1}=\sum_{\Omega\in\mathcal{R}_{l}}|\text{Refine}(\Omega)|\leq\sum_{\Omega\in\mathcal{R}_{l}}|\mathcal{S}_{l+1}|=N_{l}\cdot|\mathcal{S}_{l+1}|.

Substituting the bound for the single-layer partition complexity, we obtain the multiplicative recurrence:

N_{l+1}\leq N_{l}\cdot(\mathcal{M}_{\text{MHSA}}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{\text{FFN}}).

With the base case
N_{0}=1
, the total complexity after
L

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T}(L,H,d_{\text{model}},d_{\text{ff}}))=N_{L}\leq\left(C_{d}N^{\min(H,d)}\cdot\left(\frac{ed_{ff}}{d}\right)^{d}\right)^{L}.

In the saturated regime where

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T}(L,H,d_{\text{model}},d_{\text{ff}}))=\mathcal{O}\left((N^{d})^{L}\right)=\mathcal{O}(N^{dL}).

This completes the proof.

Lemma F.1

Lemma F.2

(Explicit Exact Sawtooth via ReLUs)
.

Let

. The continuous piecewise linear sawtooth function

with
w
uniform teeth, forming

Evaluate

on an arbitrary segment

for an integer

\displaystyle=2wx+4w\sum_{m=1}^{k}(-1)^{m}\left(x-\frac{m}{2w}\right)

\displaystyle=2wx+4wx\sum_{m=1}^{k}(-1)^{m}-\frac{4w}{2w}\sum_{m=1}^{k}m(-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=2wx\left(1+2\sum_{m=1}^{k}(-1)^{m}\right)-2\sum_{m=1}^{k}m(-1)^{m}.

We evaluate the discrete sums based strictly on the parity of

If

If

\sum_{m=1}^{k}m(-1)^{m}=\frac{k-1}{2}-k=-\frac{k+1}{2}
