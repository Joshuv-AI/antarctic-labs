Comparative diagnostic accuracy of image-enhanced endoscopy modalities and artificial intelligence-assisted endoscopy for gastric intestinal metaplasia and gastric dysplasia: protocol for a systematic review and diagnostic test network meta-analysis
Authors: Rongcan Xia, Yingzhan Yang, Wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM) and gastric dysplasia are critical precursors in the Correa cascade of gastric carcinogenesis. Although multiple image-enhanced endoscopy (IEE) modalities and artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted systems have been developed to improve their detection, the comparative diagnostic accuracy of these technologies remains unclear. Direct comparative studies are limited and conventional pairwise meta-analyses do not provide a coherent framework for comparing multiple diagnostic technologies.Methods and analysisThis protocol follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) and PRISMA-Network Meta-Analysis guidelines. We will search MEDLINE (Ovid), Embase (Ovid), the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL; Cochrane Library/Wiley), Web of Science Core Collection (Clarivate), Scopus (Elsevier), ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform from inception, without date or language restrictions. Eligible studies will be diagnostic accuracy studies evaluating IEE modalities (including narrow-band imaging with or without magnification, blue laser imaging, linked colour imaging and iScan) or AI-assisted endoscopy against histology. Comparative studies will form the primary evidence base for the diagnostic test network meta-analysis; single-arm studies will be summarised separately and will not contribute to the primary network estimates. The coprimary diagnostic parameters will be sensitivity and specificity for GIM and dysplasia. These will be analysed jointly using a hierarchical bivariate diagnostic test network meta-analysis with binomial likelihoods and correlated random effects. Diagnostic OR and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve will be treated as secondary summary measures. Two reviewers will independently screen, extract data and apply Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2. Heterogeneity, transitivity and inconsistency will be assessed using prespecified clinical and methodological effect modifiers, model-based diagnostics and sensitivity analyses. The certainty of evidence will be assessed using Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis, with interpretation adapted to diagnostic accuracy outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThis study does not require ethical approval as it relies on secondary analysis of published literature. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420261363078.

Introduction

Gastric intestinal metaplasia (GIM) and gastric dysplasia occupy a central place in the Correa cascade, the well-established histological pathway linking chronic gastritis to gastric atrophy, intestinal metaplasia and ultimately intestinal-type gastric adenocarcinoma.1 2 Gastric cancer continues to rank among the leading causes of cancer death worldwide. In 2022 alone, gastrointestinal cancers accounted for approximately 4.91 million new cases and 3.32 million deaths, contributing significantly to the global burden of disease.3 4 By 2050, the annual number of incident gastrointestinal cancer cases and deaths is forecast to rise by 85% and 93%, respectively, relative to 2022 levels, reaching an estimated 9.06 million new incident cases and 6.42 million deaths. The global prevalence of GIM, a key precursor lesion, is estimated at 17.5% (95% CI 14.6% to 20.8%), with substantially higher rates in high-incidence regions and among individuals with gastro-oesophageal reflux or persistent Helicobacter pylori infection.5

Accurate endoscopic detection and characterisation of GIM and dysplasia are therefore fundamental to risk stratification, surveillance planning and timely endoscopic resection—steps that can interrupt disease progression and substantially improve long-term outcomes. Current international guidelines place strong emphasis on high-quality endoscopy for this purpose. The 2025 American College of Gastroenterology Clinical Guideline on Gastric Premalignant Conditions and the updated European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy MAPS III guideline both endorse systematic biopsy protocols based on the updated Sydney system and recommend routine use of image-enhanced endoscopy (IEE) to improve detection of subtle lesions beyond what is possible with conventional white-light endoscopy.6 7

Over the past two decades, several IEE technologies have been introduced to enhance mucosal visualisation and highlight preneoplastic changes.8 These include narrow-band imaging (NBI) with or without magnification,9 blue laser imaging (BLI),10 linked colour imaging (LCI)11 and iScan. More recently, artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted systems (computer-aided detection (CADe) and computer-aided diagnosis (CADx)) have shown promising results,12 13 with high sensitivity and specificity for both GIM and dysplasia.14 15 However, much of the evidence is derived from studies of single technologies, and direct head-to-head comparisons between modalities remain limited.

Among endoscopic classification systems for GIM, the Endoscopic Grading of Gastric Intestinal Metaplasia (EGGIM) is particularly relevant. EGGIM is an NBI-based semiquantitative score ranging from 0 to 10, derived from endoscopic assessment of five gastric areas: the lesser and greater curvature of the antrum, the incisura, and the lesser and greater curvature of the corpus. In the multicentre validation study by Esposito et al, EGGIM showed high diagnostic performance for identifying extensive GIM (operative link on gastric intestinal metaplasia III/IV), with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) of 0.96 and sensitivity and specificity of 89% and 95%, respectively, using a cut-off >4.16

The 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis by Rodríguez-Carrasco et al provided the pooled estimates of diagnostic accuracy for IEE in gastric preneoplastic conditions.8 However, its pairwise approach could not rank multiple technologies simultaneously, did not include recent AI developments17 and was unable to generate the kind of comparative hierarchies that are increasingly needed for clinical decision-making.

Diagnostic test network meta-analysis (NMA) offers a practical solution to these challenges.18 19 Sensitivity and specificity are correlated within studies and may vary with diagnostic threshold; therefore, they should not be treated as independent univariate outcomes.2023 In this protocol, diagnostic OR (DOR) and area under the ROC curve (AUC/ROC) will be considered secondary summaries because single-number rankings may obscure clinically important trade-offs between sensitivity and specificity.

The primary objective of this systematic review and diagnostic test network meta-analysis is therefore to compare the diagnostic accuracy of IEE modalities and AI-assisted endoscopy systems for detecting GIM and gastric dysplasia in adults undergoing upper gastrointestinal endoscopy. Secondary objectives include assessment of examination time, biopsy requirements, adverse events and (where reported) cost-effectiveness, together with exploration of potential effect modifiers such as magnification use, high-risk populations and specific endoscopic descriptors.

Methods

Protocol registration and reporting

This protocol is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) statement and the PRISMA extension for NMA (PRISMA-NMA).24 25 It has been prospectively registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number CRD420261363078). The review is planned to start on 1 July 2026, with completion of searches, screening, analysis and preparation of the final review manuscript planned by 31 December 2026. Any substantive amendments will be recorded with the date, rationale and details of the change and will be updated on PROSPERO.

Types of studies

We will include all diagnostic accuracy studies that evaluate one or more index tests against a histopathological reference standard. Eligible designs include prospective and retrospective cohort studies, cross-sectional diagnostic accuracy studies, randomised diagnostic studies and paired or tandem comparative studies reporting diagnostic performance data. To be included in quantitative synthesis, a study must provide or allow reconstruction of a 2×2 table (true positives (TP), false positives, false negatives and true negatives (TNs)) for at least one eligible index test. Comparative studies, defined as studies evaluating two or more eligible index tests in the same participants or in comparable participant groups, will form the primary evidence base for the diagnostic test NMA.

Single-arm studies will be eligible for the systematic review but will not contribute to the primary network estimates or rankings. When sufficient data are available, they will be summarised descriptively or analysed in supplementary modality-specific bivariate syntheses. This approach retains relevant diagnostic evidence while maintaining the comparative structure required for the primary NMA. We will exclude conference abstracts without sufficient data after author contact, case reports, case series with fewer than 10 participants, animal or in vitro studies, and studies without histological confirmation. For cross-over or tandem endoscopy studies, we will extract data from the first phase or from analyses that avoid carry-over effects.

Types of participants

Adults aged 18 years or older undergoing upper gastrointestinal endoscopy for any clinical indication will be eligible. Indications may include routine screening, surveillance of high-risk individuals or investigation of symptoms suggestive of gastric pathology.26 27 No restrictions will be applied with respect to sex, ethnicity or comorbid conditions. Studies will be excluded if the participant cohort is limited to individuals with gastric lesions attributable to non-gastric or non-premalignant aetiologies or if the presence or absence of GIM or dysplasia cannot be verified by histology.

Index tests

Eligible index tests include NBI (with or without optical magnification), BLI, LCI, iScan and equivalent virtual chromoendoscopy platforms. AI-assisted endoscopy systems, defined as CADe or CADx algorithms applied to white-light or IEE-derived images, will also be included. Index tests may be performed with or without magnification. We will extract the diagnostic criteria, classification system and threshold used for each index test, including EGGIM, versus classification, light-blue crest sign or other prespecified descriptors where applicable. Studies restricted to conventional white-light endoscopy alone will not form index-test nodes, although white-light endoscopy may be extracted as a comparator within otherwise eligible comparative studies.

Reference standard

The reference standard will be histopathological assessment of gastric mucosal biopsy or resection specimens. We will not require all studies to have followed the updated Sydney biopsy protocol or to have explicitly reported assessment by an expert gastrointestinal pathologist because doing so would exclude a substantial part of the diagnostic accuracy literature. Instead, we will extract the biopsy protocol (systematic Sydney-based sampling, targeted biopsy or mixed/unclear sampling), number and location of biopsies and pathologist expertise where reported. Studies using histology as the reference standard will be eligible; studies without histological confirmation will be excluded. The influence of biopsy protocol and pathologist expertise will be examined through Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2 (QUADAS-2), subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses restricted to studies using Sydney-based systematic biopsies and/or expert gastrointestinal pathology review.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The coprimary diagnostic parameters are sensitivity and specificity for the detection of GIM and gastric dysplasia.28 29 These parameters will be modelled jointly, rather than as independent univariate outcomes, to preserve the within-study correlation between sensitivity and specificity and to account for threshold-related trade-offs.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary diagnostic outcomes will include DOR, positive and negative likelihood ratios, AUC/ROC or summary ROC parameters where estimable and comparative ranking measures derived from the joint distribution of sensitivity and specificity. Clinical and implementation outcomes will include examination time, number of biopsies obtained, procedure-related adverse events and cost-effectiveness estimates where reported.

Data sources and search strategy

We will systematically search MEDLINE (Ovid), Embase (Ovid), the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL; Cochrane Library/Wiley), Web of Science Core Collection (Clarivate), Scopus (Elsevier), ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform from database inception to the search date. The planned initial search date is 1 July 2026, and the search will be updated before the final analysis. No date or language restrictions will be applied. Non-English reports will be translated where necessary. The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary and free-text terms for (1) IEE modalities and endoscopic GIM classification terms, including NBI, BLI, LCI, iScan and magnifying endoscopy, together with EGGIM and related endoscopic GIM grading terminology; (2) AI-assisted systems, including CADe, CADx, deep learning and convolutional neural networks and (3) gastric premalignant conditions, including GIM, atrophic gastritis and gastric dysplasia. The complete platform-specific search strategies, including limits and planned search procedures, are provided in online supplemental appendix 1.

Grey-literature searching will include trial registries, Google Scholar screening of the first 200 records sorted by relevance and targeted screening of recent conference proceedings from major gastroenterology and endoscopy meetings when accessible. Reference lists of included studies and relevant systematic reviews will be checked manually. Conference abstracts will be considered for full-text retrieval or author contact; abstracts that do not provide sufficient data for 2×2 reconstruction will be excluded from quantitative synthesis.

Study selection

All retrieved records will be imported into EndNote 2025 for deduplication.

Two reviewers (RX and YY) will independently screen titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria. The same reviewers will assess the full text of potentially eligible studies. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion or, when required, by consultation with a third reviewer (WZ). The selection process will be reported in a completed PRISMA flow diagram in the final review; the placeholder flow diagram has been removed from the protocol.

Data extraction and management

Two reviewers (RX and YY) will independently extract data using a standardised Microsoft Excel form piloted before full extraction. Extracted items will include study characteristics, participant demographics, clinical setting, disease prevalence, index test specifications (modality, magnification, AI algorithm version, diagnostic criteria, endoscopic classification system and diagnostic threshold), reference standard details, 2×2 tables and QUADAS-2 items. Data presented only in graphical form will be extracted using WebPlot Digitizer. When reporting is incomplete, we will contact the corresponding author by email. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus or by adjudication by WZ.

Risk of bias assessment

The methodological quality of included studies will be appraised using the QUADAS-2 tool. Two reviewers (RX and YY) will independently evaluate patient selection, index test, reference standard and flow and timing. Each domain will be judged as low, high or unclear risk of bias; applicability concerns will also be assessed. Discrepancies will be resolved by consensus or consultation with WZ. Risk-of-bias judgments will inform sensitivity analyses and the certainty assessment.

Data synthesis and analysis

Analyses will be conducted separately for GIM and dysplasia where data permit. We will first describe the evidence network by test modality, diagnostic criteria and reference-standard characteristics. Pairwise diagnostic meta-analyses will use hierarchical bivariate random-effects or hierarchical summary ROC models when sufficient studies are available for a given comparison; otherwise, findings will be summarised narratively.20 30

The primary comparative analysis will be a diagnostic test NMA that jointly models sensitivity and specificity. We will use a hierarchical bivariate model with binomial likelihoods for the number of TPs among participants with disease and the number of TNs among participants without disease. The model will include correlated random effects for logit sensitivity and logit specificity and will account for the within-study comparison of multiple tests where studies evaluate more than one eligible modality. The primary model will be restricted to comparative studies. Test nodes will be grouped into clinically interpretable categories: NBI with magnification, NBI without magnification, BLI, LCI, iScan, AI-assisted white-light endoscopy and AI-assisted IEE. More granular nodes will be explored in sensitivity analyses if data are sufficient.

The primary model will be implemented in R using a Bayesian Markov chain Monte Carlo framework through JAGS/rjags or Stan/RStan. Convergence will be assessed with trace plots, effective sample size and the Gelman-Rubin statistic. Vague or weakly informative priors will be used for test effects, and alternative priors for variance components will be examined in sensitivity analyses. The netmeta package will not be used as the primary inferential model for diagnostic accuracy outcomes; it may be used only for network visualisation or descriptive displays when appropriate.31 32

When thresholds or diagnostic criteria vary across studies, we will extract the threshold prespecified by the study authors. If multiple thresholds are reported for the same index test, the clinically recommended or prespecified primary threshold will be used in the main analysis, with alternative thresholds examined in sensitivity analyses. Results will be presented separately for sensitivity and specificity and, where appropriate, as joint probabilities of reaching clinically relevant sensitivity and specificity. Rankings based solely on DOR or AUC will not be used as the main basis for clinical interpretation.

Measures for transitivity assumption

Transitivity will be assessed before quantitative synthesis by comparing the distribution of potential effect modifiers across comparisons. These will include disease prevalence, mean participant age, study design, clinical indication, population risk profile, magnification use, diagnostic criteria or threshold, endoscopist expertise, biopsy protocol, pathologist expertise and risk-of-bias profile. Comparisons with marked imbalance in key modifiers will be interpreted cautiously, and network estimates will not be pooled when the transitivity assumption is judged implausible.

Measures for heterogeneity

Heterogeneity will be assessed using between-study variance parameters from the hierarchical bivariate model and, for descriptive pairwise analyses, the I2 statistic where applicable. I2 values will be interpreted cautiously because this statistic may not fully reflect heterogeneity in both sensitivity and specificity in diagnostic accuracy meta-analysis.

Measure for inconsistency

Inconsistency between direct and indirect evidence will be evaluated when the network contains closed loops and sufficient comparative data. We will compare consistency and inconsistency models globally and, where data permit, apply local approaches such as node-splitting or loop-specific assessment.3335 Potential sources of inconsistency will be explored in relation to diagnostic threshold, magnification use, study design, reference standard and risk of bias.

Subgroup analyses and meta-regression

Where sufficient data are available, we will perform meta-regression to explore potential sources of heterogeneity. Prespecified covariates include: (1) use of optical magnification (yes vs no), (2) study population risk stratum (high-risk surveillance vs unselected screening), (3) application of a formal endoscopic classification scheme (EGGIM vs classification, light-blue crest sign or other prespecified diagnostic criteria), (4) biopsy protocol (Sydney-based systematic sampling vs targeted or mixed sampling), (5) pathologist expertise (expert gastrointestinal pathologist vs not reported/other) and (6) overall risk of bias (low vs high/unclear).

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses will include exclusion of studies at high risk of bias, industry-funded studies, studies published before 2015, studies with unclear diagnostic thresholds, studies not using Sydney-based systematic biopsies and AI studies lacking external validation. We will also examine the influence of single-arm studies in supplementary modality-specific bivariate syntheses; these studies will not be incorporated into the primary network estimates. Small-study effects will be assessed cautiously using diagnostic test-specific approaches, such as Deeks’ funnel plot asymmetry test, when at least ten studies are available for a modality-specific synthesis.3638

Certainty assessment

The certainty of evidence for network estimates will be appraised using the Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis framework.

The assessment will consider within-study bias, reporting bias, indirectness, imprecision, heterogeneity and incoherence. For each comparison, certainty will be categorised as high, moderate, low or very low, with explicit consideration of the paired interpretation of sensitivity and specificity.

Discussion

GIM and gastric dysplasia are critical premalignant conditions that significantly increase the risk of gastric adenocarcinoma, one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide.39 Although multiple IEE modalities and AI-assisted systems have been developed to improve detection and characterisation, their comparative diagnostic performance has not been assessed using a prespecified diagnostic test NMA framework. This protocol describes a review designed to compare sensitivity and specificity across major IEE modalities and AI-assisted systems, updating and substantially expanding the 2020 pairwise meta-analysis by Rodríguez-Carrasco et al.8 The analytical plan addresses methodological issues specific to diagnostic accuracy evidence synthesis. Sensitivity and specificity will be modelled jointly using hierarchical bivariate methods rather than analysed separately. DOR and AUC will be treated as secondary measures because they may mask different sensitivity-specificity trade-offs across modalities.

Nevertheless, several limitations must be acknowledged. Heterogeneity is expected across endoscopic systems, magnification use, AI algorithm versions, diagnostic criteria including EGGIM-based criteria, study populations and biopsy protocols. Restricting the primary NMA to comparative studies may reduce the amount of data available for network estimation, but it should improve the interpretability of indirect comparisons. Single-arm studies will be retained as supplementary evidence to describe the wider literature without determining the primary network estimates. Variation in the reference standard will be handled by extracting biopsy protocol and pathologist expertise and assessing their influence through QUADAS-2, subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses. EGGIM-based studies will be evaluated as a prespecified endoscopic classification category in subgroup analyses or meta-regression when data permit.

This protocol provides a prespecified approach for synthesising diagnostic accuracy evidence on gastric premalignant conditions. The resulting review may support evidence-based endoscopic practice and identify areas where direct comparative studies or external validation of AI-assisted systems are still needed.4042

Ethics and dissemination

This work involves no primary data collection and relies on the analysis of published literature; ethical approval is therefore unnecessary. Dissemination will occur through peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations. Any significant amendments will be documented and updated on the PROSPERO register.
