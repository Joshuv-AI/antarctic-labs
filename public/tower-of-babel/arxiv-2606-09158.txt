Abstract
The interest in studying the size of the intersection of multiple q q -ary Hamming balls has grown due to the recent advances in DNA-based data storage systems. We present an exact formula for the cardinality of the intersection of s s Hamming balls of varying radii over a q q -ary alphabet. It is known that the distances between the center points of the Hamming balls are not enough, in general, to determine the size of the intersection. Based on our formula, we are able to find more refined structural properties of the center points for determining the exact size of the intersection. Moreover, we also analyze the size of the intersection for sufficiently large n n . When s = 3 s=3 , we give the necessary and sufficient conditions (for all q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , q ≠ 6 q\neq 6 and sufficiently large n n ) to obtain the maximum size of the intersection when the center points of the Hamming balls have a given minimum distance and demonstrate how to compute it using our general formula.



Keywords:
Substitution errors, Hamming balls, Intersection of balls, Levenshtein sequence reconstruction, DNA-memory, Information Retrieval

However, it should be noted that in the results mentioned above regarding

In what follows, we briefly discuss the Levenshtein sequence reconstruction problem and its application to DNA-storage.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
be a code. In the Levenshtein sequence reconstruction model, a word
\mathbf{x}\in S
is transmitted through
N
channels and, due to the errors introduced in each channel, a set of output words
Y=\{\mathbf{y}_{1},\mathbf{y}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{y}_{N}\}
is obtained. The output of each channel is assumed to be distinct and the number of errors occurring in a channel is at most
t
. Let
T(Y)\subseteq S
denote the set of codewords that may produce the output set
Y
when sent through the
N
channels. In other words,
T(Y)
can be considered as a list of possible transmitted codewords which the decoder can deduce based on the output words
Y
. The maximum value
N_{t}^{q}(s,d)
is essential in the reconstruction model since if
|Y|\geq N_{t}^{q}(s,d)+1
, then the length of the list
T(Y)
is bounded from above by
s-1
. On the other hand, if
|Y|\leq N_{t}^{q}(s,d)
, then it is possible that
|T(Y)|\geq s
.

The original motivation for the Levenshtein sequence reconstruction model came from molecular biology and chemistry
[
3
]
. Recently, the topic has regained popularity due to its connections to advanced memory storage technologies such as associative memories
[
4
]
, racetrack memories
[
11

and, especially, polymer-based memories such as ones based on DNA
[
12
]

The relationship between DNA-based memory storage systems and the Levenshtein reconstruction model can be stated as follows: In such memories, the data is stored directly in the DNA by synthesizing suitable strands. This leads to multiple redundant copies of each strand which are stored into a pool of freely floating DNA. When the information is read, we pick strands from this pool (possibly obtaining multiple erroneous copies of the strand in which the original information was stored). These strands are multiplied in the reading process (called sequencing). Finally, we end up with many copies of a strand, each with its own errors. We may apply the Levenshtein reconstruction model to this problem by considering each strand as an output word in
Y
based on which we can determine the originally stored information
\mathbf{x}
(see
[
13

14
]

The larger alphabets (with

Beyond the study of intersections of multiple Hamming balls, several works have focused specifically on the intersection of two balls, motivated by a range of applications.

Let

Furthermore, the

In the following theorem, we give an exact formula for the number of words in the intersection of balls of radii

respectively centered at
\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2},\dots,\mathbf{c}^{s}
based on the values

.

Theorem 3
.

Let
S=\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2},\dots,\mathbf{c}^{s}\}
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
and
T=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{s})
a list of nonnegative integers, where
s\geq 2
. Let
j_{k,i}^{l}
be nonnegative integers, where
k\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,s\}\}
,
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\mathcal{S}(s,k)\}
and
l\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,k+1\}\}
, satisfying the following conditions: (i)
\sum_{l=1}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}j_{k,i}^{l}=p_{k,i}
and (ii) for each
\mathbf{c}^{m}\in S
,

\sum_{k=1}^{\min\{q,s\}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\left(p_{k,i}-j_{k,i}^{f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{m})}\right)\leq t_{m}\text{.}

Let
\mathcal{J}
denote all the tuples formed by
j_{k,i}^{l}
which satisfy the conditions (i) and (ii). The formula for the size of the intersection divides into the following two cases depending on whether (a)
q>s
or (b)
q\leq s
:

(a)

If
q>s
, then

|I_{T}(S)|=\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\right)\text{,}

(3)

where the sum goes through all the tuples of
\mathcal{J}
.

(b)

If
q\leq s
, then

|I_{T}(S)|=\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{q-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,q)}\binom{p_{q,i}}{j_{q,i}^{1},j_{q,i}^{2},\dots,j_{q,i}^{q}}\right)\text{,}

(4)

where the sum goes through all the tuples of
\mathcal{J}
.

Proof.

Since the Hamming distance counts coordinatewise the differences of words (regardless of the actual different symbols in the words), we may assume, without loss of generality, that the words of
S
are such that within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
their symbols in the coordinates belong to
[0,k-1]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, i.e., the
k
blocks of the partition
P_{k,i}
are formed by the
k
smallest elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. More formally, we may assume that a word
\mathbf{c}^{m}\in S
is such that for each
k\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,s\}\}
and
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\mathcal{S}(s,k)\}
, its value in the coordinates of
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
is equal to the element of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
corresponding to
f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{j})-1
; in particular, the symbols of
\mathbf{c}^{m}\in S
within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
belong to
[0,k-1]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. Thus, the word
\mathbf{c}^{m}\subseteq[0,\min\{q,s\}-1]^{n}
. Let
\mathbf{u}
be a word of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
. For
k\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,s\}\}
,
i\in\{1,2,\dots,\mathcal{S}(s,k)\}
and
l\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,k+1\}\}
, the nonnegative integers
j_{k,i}^{l}
may be considered as the distribution of symbols of
\mathbf{u}
among the coordinate positions of
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
as follows:

•

If
q>k
, then for each
l\in[1,k]
, the value
j_{k,i}^{l}
corresponds to the number of coordinates of
\mathbf{u}
equal to
l-1
within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
and
j_{k,i}^{k+1}
to the number of coordinates belonging to
[k,q-1]
.

•

If
q\leq k
, then for each
l\in[1,q]
, the value
j_{k,i}^{l}
corresponds to the number of coordinates of
\mathbf{u}
equal to
l-1
within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
.

Thus, as
j_{k,i}^{l}
represents the distribution of symbols of
\mathbf{u}
in the coordinates indicated by
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
, the condition (i)
\sum_{l=1}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}j_{k,i}^{l}=p_{k,i}
is satisfied. Furthermore, for each
\mathbf{c}^{m}
, we have

d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{c}^{m})=\sum_{k=1}^{\min\{q,s\}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\left(p_{k,i}-j_{k,i}^{f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{m})}\right)\text{.}

Indeed, the previous equality holds since
p_{k,i}-j_{k,i}^{f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{m})}
coordinate positions contribute to the distance
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{c}^{m})
among
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
. In what follows, we show that
\mathbf{u}\in I_{T}(S)
if and only if the conditions (i) and (ii) are satisfied.

(
\Rightarrow
)

If
\mathbf{u}\in I_{T}(S)
, then by the previous discussion the conditions (i)
\sum_{l=1}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}j_{k,i}^{l}=p_{k,i}
and (ii)
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{c}^{m})\leq t_{m}
for all
\mathbf{c}^{m}\in S
are satisfied for all
\mathbf{c}^{m}\in S
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

Assume that the word
\mathbf{u}
satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii). Firstly, by (i), it follows that the values
j_{k,i}^{l}
give a proper distribution of symbols of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
in the coordinates positions of
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
. Secondly, the condition (ii) guarantees that
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{c}^{m})\leq t_{m}
for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{u}\in I_{T}(S)
.

Hence, in order to prove the claim, we count the number of words
\mathbf{u}
satisfying (i) and (ii) based on the tuples of
\mathcal{J}
.

For this purpose, assume that the values
j_{k,i}^{l}
form a fixed tuple of
\mathcal{J}
, i.e.,
j_{k,i}^{l}
satisfy (i) and (ii). Next we count the number of words
\mathbf{u}
corresponding to these choices of
j_{k,i}^{l}
. The number of ways to choose the coordinates corresponding to
j_{k,i}^{l}
within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
, where
l\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,k+1\}\}
, is equal to the multinomial coefficient

\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}}\text{.}

(5)

Furthermore, if
k+1\leq q
, there are
(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}
choices for the symbols contributing to
j_{k,i}^{k+1}
. Therefore, if
q>s
, then
k+1\leq q
holds for all
k\in\{1,2,\dots,\min\{q,s\}\}
and, by going through all the choices for
k
and
i
, we end up with the term in the formula for the case (a). Thus, the claim (a) follows by iterating over all the the tuples of
\mathcal{J}
. For the case (b) (when
q\leq s
), we notice that the condition
k+1\leq q
is satisfied for
k\in[1,q-1]
. Furthermore, if
k\in[q,\min\{q,s\}]=\{q\}
, i.e.,
k=q
, then the value
j_{k,i}^{k+1}
does not exist (since there are no symbols left in
[k,q-1]=\emptyset
) and the different choices within
\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
can be counted similarly as in (
5
). Thus, the number of words
\mathbf{u}
corresponding to the current choice of
j_{k,i}^{l}
is equal to

\prod_{k=1}^{q-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,q)}\binom{p_{q,i}}{j_{q,i}^{1},j_{q,i}^{2},\dots,j_{q,i}^{q}}\text{.}

Therefore, the claim (b) also follows by iterating over all the tuples of
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Note that although the formula of the theorem can seem somewhat complicated at first glance, it can actually be efficiently computed, for example, when

is constant, as described in Remark

In the following remark, we observe that the formulas of the cases (a) and (b) of the previous theorem can be combined.

Remark 4

In the previous theorem, we can in fact use the formula

in both cases. Indeed, if

, then the claim immediately follows by the case (b) of the theorem. Hence, we may assume that

\begin{split}\quad&\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{q-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,q)}\binom{p_{q,i}}{j_{q,i}^{1},j_{q,i}^{2},\dots,j_{q,i}^{q}}\right)\\

=&\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{s}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\right)\cdot 1\right)\text{.}\end{split}

(8)

contributes to the distance

if and only if the coordinates of

and

\mathcal{P}_{k,i}
. Since each pair of words belonging to

corresponds to an equation of type (
8
), we may form a system of
\binom{s}{2}

P_{q}(s)-1

(excluding

In what follows, we focus more closely on the linear independence of the system

Lemma 11
.

The rows of the matrix
M_{q,s}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Observe first that for

are linearly independent. Indeed, this follows since it is straightforward to verify (by computer) that the rows of their submatrices

corresponding to the partitions

are distinct integers of

, and based on these columns, construct an

is equal to an adjacency matrix of the Johnson graph

Observe that the adjacency of the distinct vertices

and

of

can be equivalently formulated as follows:

In the following remark, we briefly discuss the asymptotic behavior of

Remark 13
.

In order to determine the asymptotic behavior of

\mathcal{S}(s,k)\sim\frac{e^{s}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\text{,}

that is,
\mathcal{S}(s,k)/(\frac{e^{s}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}})\overset{s\to\infty}{\rightarrow}1
, where

Therefore, when

P_{q}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{q}\mathcal{S}(s,k)\sim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\frac{e^{s}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}}\in\Theta(e^{s})

Moreover, we have
\binom{s}{2}\sim\frac{s^{2}}{2}
.
Hence, the requirement for additional parameters grows rapidly with respect to

. Recall by Theorem

can be non-zero due to the fact that

. This also restricts the number of non-zero

’s in Theorem
3
.

Assume then that
s
is a constant and
q
increases. While
q<s
, increasing
q
also makes
P_{q}(s)
larger; namely, for
2<q<s
, we have
P_{q-1}(s)<P_{q}(s)
. However, if
q\geq s
, then
P_{q}(s)=P_{s}(s)
stays constant. Therefore, we have
P_{q}(s)-1-\binom{s}{2}\in\Theta(1)
.

The main focus of the section has been to study the theoretical and structural aspects of the intersection
I_{T}(S)
and its cardinality. However, in the following remark, we briefly discuss the computational complexity of applying Theorem
3
and compare it to the complexity of a ‘brute-force’ style algorithm, when the size
q
of the alphabet and the number
s
of the balls are bounded by constants; such assumptions are rather natural concerning applications such as DNA-storages.

Remark 14
.

Let
q
and
s
be constants. For computing the formula of Theorem
3
, we first observe by (
2
) that
P_{q}(s)
is constant. Therefore, the number of parameters
j_{k,i}^{l}
is upper bounded by a constant
qP_{q}(s)
. For efficiently determining the set
\mathcal{J}
of tuples formed by
j_{k,i}^{l}
, we denote
t_{\min}=\min{T}
and the corresponding center by
\mathbf{c}^{\min}
. Note that the length of any tuple in
\mathcal{J}
is determined by the number of parameters
j_{k,i}^{l}
and is at most
qP_{q}(s)
. As each
j_{k,i}^{l}\leq n
, any tuple in
\mathcal{J}
can be presented using
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}n)
bits. Related to Theorem
3
, consider then the condition (ii) for
t_{\min}
and, by substituting the condition (i)
p_{k,i}=\sum_{l=1}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}j_{k,i}^{l}
for all pairs of
k
and
i
, obtain the inequality

\sum_{k=1}^{\min\{q,s\}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{\min})\end{subarray}}^{\min\{q,k+1\}}j_{k,i}^{l}\leq t_{\min}\text{.}

(9)

Denote the tuples of
j_{k,i}^{l}
satisfying this inequality by
\mathcal{J^{\prime}}
. Observe that
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{J^{\prime}}
since the values
f_{k,i}(\mathbf{c}^{\min})
(not involved in the inequality) are fixed by the condition (i). By a rather standard ‘stars-and-bars’ argument (for example, see
[
31
, Section 1.5]
), we obtain that

|\mathcal{J^{\prime}}|=\binom{t_{\min}+\beta}{\beta}\leq\binom{t_{\min}+(q-1)P_{q}(s)}{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\in\mathcal{O}\left(t_{\min}^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\right)\text{,}

where
\beta
stands for the number of parameters
j_{k,i}^{l}
appearing in Inequality (
9
). It is also rather immediate that the tuples of
\mathcal{J^{\prime}}
can be listed using
\mathcal{O}\left(t_{\min}^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\cdot\log_{2}{n}\right)
time since each tuple in
\mathcal{J}
can be presented using
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}n)
bits. In order to determine which tuples of
\mathcal{J^{\prime}}
actually belong to
\mathcal{J}
, we need to check which of them satisfy the inequalities in (ii) for the other values of
t_{m}
. Notice first that the left-hand side of each inequality is at most
n
since
\sum_{k=1}^{\min\{q,s\}}\sum_{i=1}^{\mathcal{S}(s,k)}p_{k,i}=n
. Therefore, calculating the left-hand side requires approximately
P_{q}(s)
additions and subtractions of integers with
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{n})
bits. Hence, due to the assumption
t_{m}\leq n
(made in the introduction), the time complexity of checking each of the
s-1
other inequalities is
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{n})
. Thus, based on
\mathcal{J^{\prime}}
, the set
\mathcal{J}
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}\left(t_{\min}^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\cdot\log_{2}{n}\right)
time. In conclusion, the total time complexity of determining
\mathcal{J}
is
\mathcal{O}\left(t_{\min}^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\cdot\log_{2}{n}\right)
.

After
\mathcal{J}
has been determined, for (
3
) and (
4
), we need to calculate
P_{q}(s)
multinomial coefficients and powers
(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}
for each tuple of
\mathcal{J}
. Each multinomial coefficient

\binom{p_{k,i}}{j_{k,i}^{1},j_{k,i}^{2},\dots,j_{k,i}^{k+1}}=\frac{p_{k,i}!}{j_{k,i}^{1}!\cdot j_{k,i}^{2}!\cdots j_{k,i}^{k+1}!}

can be calculated in polynomial time with respect to
n
. Indeed, by
[
32
]
, the coefficient can be computed using
\mathcal{O}(p_{k,i})=\mathcal{O}(n)
multiplications (and divisions). Furthermore, each multiplication (and division) involves
\mathcal{O}(n\log_{2}{n})
-bit integers since by Stirling’s formula,
n!
can be represented using
b=\log_{2}(n!)=n\log_{2}{n}-n\log_{2}{e}+\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{n})=\mathcal{O}(n\log_{2}{n})
bits. Furthermore, the multiplication (and division) of two
b
-bit integers can be performed in
\mathcal{O}(b^{2})
time using schoolbook algorithms, and there also exist more sophisticated algorithms working, for example, in time
\mathcal{O}(b\log_{2}{b})
(
[
33
]
), which are assumed to be used in the following discussions. Thus, the multinomial coefficient can be calculated using
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}(\log_{2}{n})^{2})
bit operations. Furthermore, each power
(q-k)^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\leq q^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}
can be computed using
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{n})
multiplications based on the exponentiation by squaring, where the multiplied integers have at most
\mathcal{O}(n)
bits (as
q^{j_{k,i}^{k+1}}\leq q^{n}=|\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}|
). Hence, the power can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(n(\log_{2}{n})^{2})
time. Thus, for each tuple of
\mathcal{J}
, the products in (
3
) and (
4
) can be computed using at most
2P_{q}(s)
multiplications of integers, which can be represented using
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{q^{n}})=\mathcal{O}(n)
bits, and the sums using approximately
P_{q}(s)
additions of
\mathcal{O}(n)
-bit integers. Therefore, the time complexity of computing
|I_{T}(S)|
is
\mathcal{O}\left(t_{\min}^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\cdot n^{2}(\log_{2}{n})^{2}\right)
.

Consider next the brute-force method, where we first compute the intersection
I_{T}(S)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
and then calculate its size. For this purpose, assume for simplicity that
t_{\min}<n/2
. In this approach, we first construct the ball
B_{t_{\min}}(\mathbf{c}^{\min})
, which by the previous assumption is a smallest one, and then check whether its words belong to the other balls. The cardinality of the ball
B_{t_{m}}(\mathbf{c}^{m})
satisfies

|B_{t_{\min}}(\mathbf{c}^{\min})|=\sum_{k=0}^{t_{\min}}\binom{n}{k}(q-1)^{k}\geq\binom{n}{t_{\min}}(q-1)^{t_{\min}}\geq\left(\frac{n}{t_{\min}}\right)^{t_{\min}}(q-1)^{t_{\min}}\text{.}

(10)

Therefore, the construction of
B_{t_{\min}}(\mathbf{c}^{\min})
takes at least
n(n(q-1)/t_{\min})^{t_{\min}}
bit operations. Let us divide into the following cases based on the size of
t_{\min}
:

•

If
t_{\min}
is a constant, then
\mathcal{J}
can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(\log_{2}{n})
time and, hence, calculating the formula of Theorem
3
takes
\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2}(\log{n})^{2}\right)
time. In comparison, the time complexity of the brute-force method is at least
n(n(q-1)/t_{\min})^{t_{\min}}\in\Omega(n^{t_{\min}+1})
. Thus, in general, computing
|I_{T}(S)|
using the formula of Theorem
3
is faster than the brute-force method.

•

If
t_{\min}
depends linearly on
n
, say
t_{\min}=\tau n

for some fixed positive real number
\tau<1/2
, then the complexity

n\left(\frac{n}{t_{\min}}\right)^{t_{\min}}(q-1)^{t_{\min}}\geq\left(\frac{q-1}{\tau}\right)^{\tau n}

of the brute-force method is exponential on
n
. In comparison, the computation of the formula can be performed in polynomial time
\mathcal{O}\left((\tau n)^{(q-1)P_{q}(s)}\cdot n^{2}(\log{n})^{2}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2+(q-1)P_{q}(s)}(\log{n})^{2}\right)
.

Thus, in both cases, Theorem
3
leads to a faster algorithm than the brute-force method.

III
Asymptotic Results for the Intersections

We begin the section by introducing some additional definitions and notations. For a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
and a word
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
, the smallest integer
r\geq 0
such that
S\subseteq B_{r}(\mathbf{v})
is denoted by
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)
. In other words, we have
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)=\max\{d(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{s})\mid\mathbf{s}\in S\}
. Observe that
\mathbf{v}\in I_{t}(S)
if and only if
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)\leq t
.
We further denote by
C_{d}(S)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
the set of words whose maximum distance to words in
S
is exactly
d
, that is,
C_{d}(S)=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}\mid d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)=d\}
.
Furthermore, we call
d(S)=\min\{d_{M}(\mathbf{x},S)\mid\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}\}
the
radius of the set

S
. Moreover, the set
C(S)
of
central words
consists of the words
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
such that
d(S)=d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)
, that is,

C(S)=\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}\mid d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)=d(S)\}.

In the following lemma, we determine (asymptotically with respect to
n
) the main term of the cardinality
|I_{t}(S)|
.

Lemma 15
.

Let
c,t
and
q
be fixed constants.
Given a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
with at most
c
words
such that
t\geq d(S)
, then

|I_{t}(S)|=|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}\binom{n}{t-d(S)}+E(n,t,d(S))

where
|E(n,t,d(S))|\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)-1})
and
|C(S)|\in\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Proof.

Let us fix constants
c,t
and
q
and let
S=\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}^{c^{\prime}}\}
, for
c^{\prime}\leq c
, where
d(S)\leq t
. Due to
d(S)\leq t
, the intersection
I_{t}(S)
is nonempty. Thus, as
t
is a constant, the maximum distance
d
between words in
S
is also upper bounded by some constant; namely, by
2t
.
Let
D\subseteq[1,n]
denote the coordinate positions in which some of the words of
S
differ (pairwise); in other words, all the words of
S
agree on the coordinates
[1,n]\setminus D
. Since a pair of words in
S
differs in at most
d
coordinate positions and there are
\binom{c^{\prime}}{2}
word pairs, we have
|D|\leq d\binom{c^{\prime}}{2}
. Thus,
|D|
is bounded from above by a constant.

Let
\mathbf{v}
be an arbitrary word of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
. We may write the word as a sum
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{1}+\mathbf{v}^{2}
, where
\mathbf{v}^{1},\mathbf{v}^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
are words such that

•

the coordinates of
\mathbf{v}^{1}
have the same value as all the words
\mathbf{c}^{j}
outside of
D
, that is,
v_{i}^{1}=c_{i}^{j}
for each
i\in[1,n]\setminus D
and
j\in[1,c^{\prime}]
, while the symbols within the coordinate positions in
D
are unrestricted, and

•

each nonzero symbol of
\mathbf{v}^{2}
(if any) is outside the coordinates of
D
, that is,
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{v}^{2})\subseteq[1,n]\setminus D
.

Observe that we have
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)=d_{M}(\mathbf{v}^{1},S)+w(\mathbf{v}^{2})
. Hence, we have
\mathbf{v}\in I_{t}(S)
if and only if
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t-d_{M}(\mathbf{v}^{1},S)
. This further implies that
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t-d(S)
. In the following, we split our considerations into the cases with
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})=t-d(S)
and
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t-d(S)-1
. We will show that the main term
|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}\binom{n}{t-d(S)}
follows from the first case and that the error term
E(n,t,d(S))
contains the cases with
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t-d(S)-1
together with the possible overflow from the first case.

Let us first consider the case with the largest possible weight
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})=t-d(S)=t^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{v}^{2}
. It is obtained if and only if
we have
\mathbf{v}^{1}\in C(S)
. Furthermore, there exist exactly

|C(S)|(q-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{n-|D|}{t^{\prime}}=|C(S)|(q-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}\frac{(n-|D|)!(n-t^{\prime})!}{n!(n-|D|-t^{\prime})!}

such words
\mathbf{v}
. We may estimate

\frac{(n-|D|)!(n-t^{\prime})!}{n!(n-|D|-t^{\prime})!}\leq\frac{(n-|D|)^{t^{\prime}}}{(n-t^{\prime})^{t^{\prime}}}=\left(\frac{n-|D|}{n-t^{\prime}}\right)^{t^{\prime}}=\left(1+\frac{t^{\prime}-|D|}{n-t^{\prime}}\right)^{t^{\prime}}\leq\left(1+\frac{2t^{\prime}-|D|}{n}\right)^{t^{\prime}}\leq 1+t^{\prime}\frac{2t^{\prime}-|D|}{n}+\frac{p^{\prime}}{n^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{p}{n}

for some constants
p^{\prime}
and
p\geq 0
when
n
is large enough. Indeed, the second inequality is due to the mediant inequality and the third inequality follows from the binomial expansion. Moreover, we have

\frac{(n-|D|)!(n-t^{\prime})!}{n!(n-|D|-t^{\prime})!}\geq\frac{(n-t^{\prime}-|D|)^{t^{\prime}}}{n^{t^{\prime}}}=\left(1-\frac{t^{\prime}+|D|}{n}\right)^{t^{\prime}}\geq 1-t^{\prime}\frac{t^{\prime}+|D|}{n}\geq 1-\frac{b}{n}

by Bernoulli’s inequality for some constant
b\geq 0
and sufficiently large
n
(ensuring that
(t^{\prime}+|D|)/n\leq 1
). Therefore,

|C(S)|(q-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}\left(1-\frac{b}{n}\right)\leq|C(S)|(q-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{n-|D|}{t^{\prime}}\leq|C(S)|(q-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{p}{n}\right).

We note that since
\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t^{\prime}})
, we have
\frac{b}{n}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t^{\prime}-1})
and the same is true for
\frac{p}{n}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}
. Hence, both
\frac{b}{n}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}
and
\frac{p}{n}\binom{n}{t^{\prime}}
are absorbed into the error term
E(n,t,d(S))
.

Let us then consider the words
\mathbf{v}
with
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t^{\prime}-1
. There are at most
V_{q}(n,t^{\prime}-1)
such words
\mathbf{v}
in
I_{t}(S)
for each
\mathbf{v}^{1}
. Since there are at most
q^{d\binom{c^{\prime}}{2}}
ways to choose the word
\mathbf{v}^{1}
, there are
\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)-1})
words
\mathbf{v}
with
w(\mathbf{v}^{2})\leq t^{\prime}-1=t-d(S)-1
in
I_{t}(S)
. Hence, in total, we have
|I_{t}(S)|=|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}\binom{n}{t-d(S)}+E(n,t,d(S))
where
|E(n,t,d(S))|\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)-1})
. Finally, in order to determine the cardinality of
C(S)
, we observe that if
\mathbf{v}\in C(S)
, then
\mathbf{v}^{2}=\mathbf{0}
since
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)=d_{M}(\mathbf{v}^{1},S)+w(\mathbf{v}^{2})
and
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}^{1}
. Hence, as the number of ways to choose
\mathbf{v}^{1}
is bounded from above by a constant, we have
|C(S)|\in\mathcal{O}(1).

∎

As a corollary of the previous result, we obtain the following lemma determining the asymptotic size of
I_{t}(S)
(with respect to
n
).

Lemma 16
.

Let us fix constants
c,t
and
q
.
Given a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
with at most
c
words
such that
t\geq d(S)
, then
|I_{t}(S)|\in\Theta(n^{t-d(S)})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
, we have
|I_{t}(S)|=|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}\binom{n}{t-d(S)}+E(n,t,d(S))
where
|E(n,t,d(S))|\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)-1})
and
|C(S)|\in\mathcal{O}(1)
. Since
t\geq d(S)
and
t
is a constant, we have
\binom{n}{t-d(S)}\in\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)})
. Hence,
|I_{t}(S)|=|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}\binom{n}{t-d(S)}+E(n,t,d(S))\in\mathcal{O}(|C(S)|(q-1)^{t-d(S)}n^{t-d(S)}+E(n,t,d(S))=\mathcal{O}(n^{t-d(S)})
.
∎

When
t=d(S)
, we obtain the following simplification of Lemma
15
. Observe that the following lemma allows us to compute the exact number of central words with Theorem
3
.

Lemma 17
.

Given a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}

such that
t=d(S)
, then

I_{t}(S)=C(S).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in I_{t}(S)
. Then we have
d_{M}(\mathbf{v},S)\leq t
. Hence,
I_{t}(S)\subseteq C(S)
. Moreover, we have
C(S)\subseteq I_{t}(S)
since each vertex in
C(S)
has distance of at most
d(S)=t
to words in
S
.
∎

As Lemma
16
suggests, given a sufficiently large
n
, to find a set of words
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
for which the intersection
|I_{t}(S)|
achieves its maximum value, we are interested in finding the radius
d(S)
of the set
S
and the central words
C(S)
. Notice that in the following lemma, if
|S|=1
, then we consider the pairwise distance of words in
S
to be
0
.

Lemma 18
.

Let us fix constants
c
and
d
and consider a nonempty set
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
with at most
c
words with pairwise distances exactly equal to
d
.

1.

If
n\geq d
, then
d(S)\geq\lceil d/2\rceil
.

2.

If
n\geq 1+(c+1)\lfloor d/2\rfloor
and we have
q\geq c
or
d
is even, then there exists a set
S
attaining the bound in 1).

Proof.

Notice that if
|S|=1
, then
d=0
and the claim follows. Let
\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2}\in S
and
\mathbf{y}\in C(S)
. Due to the triangular inequality, we have
d=d(\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{c}_{2})\leq d(\mathbf{c}_{1},\mathbf{y})+d(\mathbf{c}_{2},\mathbf{y})\leq 2d_{M}(\mathbf{y},S)=2d(S)
. Therefore,
d(S)\geq d/2
.

Let us next consider point 2) and construct
S
so that we attain the lower bound
\lceil d/2\rceil
. Let us first consider the case with even
d
. Let
S=\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}^{c}\}
where
c_{j}^{i}\in\{0,1\}
for each

and
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{i})=[1,d/2]\cup[i\cdot d/2+1,(i+1)\cdot d/2]
for each

. Notice that the pairwise distance of all words in

is exactly

. Let
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
be such that
w(\mathbf{w})=d/2
and
w_{j}=1
for exactly
j\in[1,d/2]
. Now, we have

.

Let us next consider the case with odd
d
and

. Let
S=\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}^{c}\}
where

for each

and

,

and

for each

. Note that since

is

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}

and

Intersection of Three Substitution Balls

In this section, we consider the cardinality of an intersection of three
q
-ary substitution (Hamming) balls with
q\geq 3
centered at words
\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}
with pairwise distances
d=d(\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1})=d(\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{2})=d(\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2})
(recall that the case of three balls with
q=2
is known); in particular, we focus on determining the maximum cardinality of the intersection. We first concentrate on the equidistant case as we later manage to show that it leads to the maximum size intersection in Theorem
25
. Note that as we are only interested in the size of the intersections, by applying the symmetries of the Hamming space, we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\mathbf{c}^{0}=\mathbf{0}
,
\mathbf{c}^{1}=1^{d}0^{n-d}
and
c_{j}^{2}\in\{0,1,2\}
for each
j
. These assumptions can be made by first subtracting
\mathbf{c}_{0}
from each word, then observing that from the point-of-view of distance, the ordering of coordinate positions does not matter and finally by permuting the symbols within each coordinate position. We further denote by
S_{d}^{A}=\{\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}\}
a triple of words such that
d(\mathbf{c}^{i},\mathbf{c}^{j})=d
for each
i\neq j
and
|\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|=A
.
Recall that in Example
1
, we have
A=1
in the case of the set
S
, while
A=2
in the case of
S^{\prime}
.
Note that
A\geq d/2
. Indeed, otherwise we would have
d(\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2})>d
.

In the following lemma, we show that
d
and
A
determine the size of the intersection of three Hamming balls of radius
t
each at distance
d
from each other.

Lemma 19
.

Given
t\geq d(S_{d}^{A})
,
d<n
and
q\geq 3
, the size of intersection of three
t
-radius Hamming balls in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
with centers at distances
d
from each other is within the set

\{|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|\mid A\in[\lceil d/2\rceil,d]\}.

Proof.

Consider a triple of codewords
\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}
at pairwise distance
d
from each other. As we have discussed at the beginning of this section, when we consider the cardinality of
|B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{0})\cap B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\mathbf{c}^{0}=\mathbf{0}
,
w(\mathbf{c}^{1})=w(\mathbf{c}^{2})=d
,
c_{i}^{1}=1
for

and
c_{j}^{2}\in\{0,1,2\}
for each

by

. Consequently, this implies that
|B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{0})\cap B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap B_{t}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|=|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|
. Hence, we only need to show that

We have

and since

∎

Since
\mathbf{c}^{0}=\mathbf{0}

w(\mathbf{c}^{1})=w(\mathbf{c}^{2})=d
,

|\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\setminus\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|=|\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})\setminus\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})|=d-A\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. Furthermore, let us denote by
A_{1}
the number of coordinates in
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})
in which the symbols of
\mathbf{c}^{1}
and
\mathbf{c}^{2}
agree and by
A_{2}
the number of coordinates in
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})
in which the symbols of
\mathbf{c}^{1}
and
\mathbf{c}^{2}
disagree. Thus,

. Notice that since

, we have

In the following lemma, we give exact value for the radius of the set

, where the pairwise distances of its words are equal to

Lemma 20
.

Given
d\leq n
,

and

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{w}\in C(S_{d}^{A})
be a central word of
S_{d}^{A}
such that firstly the sum
d_{\mathbf{w}}=d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{0})+d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{1})+d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{2})
is minimum among the words in
C(S_{d}^{A})
and secondly, among the central words attaining the minimum sum
d_{\mathbf{w}}
, the distance
d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{0})
is minimal.
Consider the distance between the word
\mathbf{w}
and the words
\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1}
and
\mathbf{c}^{2}
. Let us denote
a_{1}=|\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{w})\cap\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|
. Note that
a_{1}\leq A_{1}
. Denote by
a_{2}^{1}
(resp.
a_{2}^{2}
) the number of symbols of
\mathbf{w}
which agree only with
\mathbf{c}^{1}
(resp.
\mathbf{c}^{2}
) but not with
\mathbf{c}^{2}
(resp.
\mathbf{c}^{1}
) in
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\cap\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})
. Note that
a_{2}^{1}+a_{2}^{2}\leq A_{2}
. Furthermore, we denote by
b_{1}
(resp.
b_{2})
the number of symbols of
\mathbf{w}
which agree with exactly
\mathbf{c}^{1}
(resp.
\mathbf{c}^{2}
) in
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})\setminus\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})
(in
\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})\setminus\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{1})
). By the choice of
\mathbf{w}
, we may assume that
w(\mathbf{w})=a_{1}+a_{2}^{1}+a_{2}^{2}+b_{1}+b_{2}
as no other non-zero symbol can decrease the distances between
\mathbf{w}
and the words in
S^{A}_{d}
while they would increase the sum of the distances
d_{\mathbf{w}}
. Hence, we may observe the following

\displaystyle d_{0}=d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{0})

\displaystyle=a_{1}+a_{2}^{1}+a_{2}^{2}+b_{1}+b_{2};

\displaystyle d_{1}=d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{1})

\displaystyle=d-a_{1}-a_{2}^{1}-b_{1}+b_{2};

(11)

\displaystyle d_{2}=d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{2})

\displaystyle=d-a_{1}-a_{2}^{2}+b_{1}-b_{2}.

Since
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are in symmetrical positions in Equations (
III-A
), we may assume without loss of generality that
b_{1}\geq b_{2}
. As
\mathbf{w}
is a central word, it minimizes the maximum distance to
S^{A}_{d}
, that is, minimizes the value
\max\{d_{0},d_{1},d_{2}\}
. Hence, we may assume that
b_{1}=B
and
b_{2}=0
. Indeed, if
b_{1}=B+B^{\prime}
and
b_{2}=B^{\prime}
, then decreasing both values by

would not change the values
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
while it would decrease
d_{0}
by Equations (
III-A
). This is a contradiction to
\mathbf{w}
minimizing the sum
d_{\mathbf{w}}=d_{0}+d_{1}+d_{2}
. This justifies the assumption
b_{2}=0
.

Next, we show
d_{1}=d_{2}
. Suppose to the contrary that
d_{i}>d_{j}
for some
\{i,j\}=\{1,2\}
. If
d_{2}-d_{1}>0
, then
a_{2}^{1}+2b_{1}-a^{2}_{2}>0
. If
d_{1}>d_{2}
, then
d_{1}-d_{2}>0
and hence,
a^{2}_{2}-a_{2}^{1}-2b_{1}>0
. Consider a word
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by decreasing
a_{2}^{2}
by 1 if

and by decreasing either
a_{2}^{1}
or
b_{1}
by 1 if
d_{2}>d_{1}
. Note that
d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{0})=d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{0})-1
. Moreover, if
d_{1}>d_{2}
, then
d_{M}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}\})=d_{1}

=d_{M}(\mathbf{w},\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}\})
and if
d_{2}>d_{1}
, then
d_{M}(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}\})\leq d_{2}=d_{M}(\mathbf{w},\{\mathbf{c}^{1},\mathbf{c}^{2}\})
. Thus,
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
is a central word. Hence, the sum of the distances from
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
to
\mathbf{c}^{0},\mathbf{c}^{1}
and
\mathbf{c}^{2}
is at least
d_{\mathbf{w}}
, that is,
d_{\mathbf{w}}\leq d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{0})+d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{1})+d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{2})
. Indeed, we have
d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{0})+d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{1})+d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{2})

\leq d_{0}-1+(d_{1}+d_{2}+1)=d_{\mathbf{w}}
. However, since
d(\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\mathbf{c}^{0})<d(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{c}^{0})
, we have a contradiction with the minimality of
d_{0}
.
Hence, we may assume from now on that
d_{1}=d_{2}
. Together with
b_{2}=0
, this implies that
a_{2}^{2}=a_{2}^{1}+2b_{1}
.

We continue by showing that
b_{1}=0
. Suppose to the contrary that
b_{1}=B>0
. Consider a word obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by decreasing
b_{1}
and
a_{2}^{2}
by
B
and increasing
a_{2}^{1}
by
B
. Now the distances
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
remain the same while
d_{0}
is decreased by
B
. Again, we obtain a contradiction to the minimality of
d_{\mathbf{w}}
.
Hence, we have
b_{1}=0
and thus,
a_{2}^{2}=a_{2}^{1}
.
This implies

d_{1}=d-a_{1}-a_{2}^{1}=d_{2}

(12)

and

d_{0}=a_{1}+2a_{2}^{1}.

(13)

Note that
a_{1}\leq A_{1}=d-A\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor
. Next, we show that
a_{1}=A_{1}
. Suppose to the contrary that
a_{1}=A_{1}-A^{\prime}
for some positive integer
A^{\prime}
. We have
d_{1}=d-a_{1}-a_{2}^{1}=A+A^{\prime}-a^{1}_{2}\geq\lceil d/2\rceil+A^{\prime}-a^{1}_{2}
by Equation (
12
) and
d_{0}=A_{1}-A^{\prime}+2a_{2}^{1}\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor-A^{\prime}+2a^{1}_{2}
by Equation (
13
). We first show that
a_{2}^{1}\geq 1
. For this purpose, suppose to the contrary that
a_{2}^{1}=0
. In this case, we have
d_{0}\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor-A^{\prime}\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1
and
d_{1}=d_{2}\geq\lceil d/2\rceil+1
. However, now we can obtain word
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
from
\mathbf{w}
by decreasing
A^{\prime}
by one. The resulting word
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
has

, a contradiction to
\mathbf{w}
being a central word. Hence, we may assume that
a_{2}^{1}\geq 1
.

Consider a word
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
obtained from
\mathbf{w}
by decreasing both
A^{\prime}
and
a^{1}_{2}
by one. This change decreases the distance to
\mathbf{c}^{2}
while the other two distances remain equal. Thus,
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
is also a central word but has a smaller sum of distances to the words in
S^{A}_{d}
contradicting the minimality of the sum

. Hence, we have

and
a_{1}=A_{1}
.
Thus, we obtain

d_{1}=d_{2}=d-A_{1}-a_{2}^{1}=A-a_{2}^{1}

(14)

and

d_{0}=A_{1}+2a_{2}^{1}=d-A+2a_{2}^{1}.

(15)

We may consider
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
as linear functions with parameter
a_{2}^{1}
, that is, as an increasing and as a decreasing line, respectively.
If we had
a_{2}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
then the minimum value of the maximums would be obtained when the lines cross each other. However, as
a_{2}^{1}
is an integer, we are interested in comparing the first integer value below and above the intersection point.
Consider first when
d_{0}=d_{1}
. By Equations (
14
) and (
15
), this requires that
A_{1}+2a_{2}^{1}=d-A_{1}-a_{2}^{1}
, that is,
3a_{2}^{1}=d-2A_{1}=2A-d
and
a_{2}^{1}=\frac{2A-d}{3}
.
When
2A-d
is divisible by 3, we have for
a_{2}^{1}=\frac{2A-d}{3}
that
d_{0}=d_{1}=d_{2}=\frac{d+A}{3}
by Equations (
14
) and (
15
). Since increasing or decreasing

would increase

or
d_{1}
, the maximum of the three distances would also increase. Therefore, in this case, we obtain
d(S_{d}^{A})=\frac{d+A}{3}
.

Let us next consider the case when

is not divisible by three. In this case, the three distances cannot be equal. We consider the cases where
a_{2}^{1}\in\{\lfloor\frac{2A-d}{3}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{2A-d}{3}\rceil\}

for some integer

. We have
a_{2}^{1}\in\{\frac{2A-d+2}{3},\frac{2A-d-1}{3}\}

) and

). If
a_{2}^{1}=\frac{2A-d-1}{3}

by Equation (
15
) and

). Notice that the latter case obtains a smaller maximum value. Therefore, in this case, we obtain

when

.

Consider then the case with

. We have

. If
a_{2}^{1}=\frac{2A-d+1}{3}

by Equation (

) and

by Equation (

). If

, then

) and

). Notice that both cases obtain the same maximum value

. Hence, we obtain

. To conclude, we have

.∎

Due to the previous results, we obtain the following lemma which gives the asymptotic size of the intersection based on the parameter

.

Lemma 21
.

Given
t\geq d(S_{d}^{A})
,
d<n
,
A\in[\lceil d/2\rceil,d]
and

, we have

|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|\in\Theta(n^{t-\lceil\frac{d+A}{3}\rceil}).

Proof.

By Lemma
16
we have
|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|\in\Theta(n^{t-d(S_{d}^{A})})
. Hence, the claim follows from Lemma
20
.
∎

As we have seen in the previous lemma, given a
q
-ary
e
-error-correcting code with minimum distance
d=2e+2
and a codeword triple at pairwise equal distances
d
with
A=\frac{d}{2}
, we obtain
|I_{e+1}(S_{d}^{A})|\in\Theta(n^{e+1-\lceil\frac{3e+3}{3}\rceil})=\Theta(1)
. However, if we forbid triples with

, then we would have
A>\frac{d}{2}
and

. As we see in the following remark, this gives list-error-correcting codes, i.e., such error-correcting codes that (instead of a single codeword) the decoder gives a list of codewords including the transmitted one (for further detail see
[
17
]
), since
I_{e+1}(S_{d}^{A})=\emptyset
.

Remark 22
.

Let
S
be an
e
-error-correcting code with minimum distance
d=2e+2
with the following additional property: For each triple
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}
with
d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})=d(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c})=d(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c})=d
we never have
|\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})\cap\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a})|=\frac{d}{2}
. From the perspective of Lemma
21
, this implies that we forbid minimal
A=\frac{d}{2}
(or in other words maximal
A_{1}
) for codeword triples with pairwise distances exactly
d
.

Consider a ball of radius
e+1
containing the codewords
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{c}
. We may assume that the ball is centered around the
\mathbf{0}
-word. Due to the minimum distance
2e+2
, we have
w(\mathbf{a})=w(\mathbf{b})=w(\mathbf{c})=e+1
and the supports of these words cannot intersect. However, now
|\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})\cap\textrm{vsupp}(\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{a})|=\frac{d}{2}
, a contradiction. Thus,
S
is an
e
-error-correcting code with the property
|B_{e+1}(\mathbf{w})\cap S|\leq 2
for each
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
. In other words, if obtain a word
\mathbf{w}
from
\mathbf{x}\in S
with at most
e+1
errors, then we can decode
\mathbf{w}
into a list of length
2
containing
\mathbf{x}
.

1

In this subsection, we consider how to obtain the

from each other.
In Lemma

Lemma 23
.

Given
q\geq 3
,
t\geq d(S_{d}^{A})
and

, the maximum cardinality of intersection

|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|\in\Theta\left(n^{t-\lceil d/2\rceil}\right)

for an even

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have

for

.
Observe that

is a decreasing function for the argument

. Consider first an even

Let us next consider odd

For
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}

Theorem 24
.

Given

,

, a sufficiently large

, the value

Assume first that

is even. By Lemma
21
, the maximum value of
|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|

A\in\{\lceil d/2\rceil,\lceil d/2\rceil+1\}
.

Hence, in the following, we concentrate on comparing
|I_{t}(S_{d}^{A})|
for an odd

and values

. Consider first the case
|I_{t}(S_{d}^{\lceil d/2\rceil})|
with

. Now, we have
A_{1}=d-A=\lceil d/2\rceil-1
and
A_{2}=2A-d=1
. Recall that
\mathbf{c}^{0}=\mathbf{0}
,
c_{i}^{1}=1
exactly for

and
w(\mathbf{c}^{2})=d
. We may further assume that

and

exactly for

for each

and each other

.

Consider next a word
\mathbf{w}_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}_{q}
and
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
. By Lemma
18
, we have
d(\mathbf{w}_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor},S^{A}_{d_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}})

\geq\lceil d/2\rceil
. Notice that if
d(\mathbf{w}_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor},S^{A}_{d_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}})=\lceil d/2\rceil+d^{\prime}
for some integer

, then we may choose the rest of the symbols in
\mathbf{w}
in
[d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor,n]
in
\Theta(n^{t-\lceil d/2\rceil-d^{\prime}})

|C_{\lceil d/2\rceil}(S^{A}_{d_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}})|
.
Let us assume that
\mathbf{v}\in C_{\lceil d/2\rceil}(S^{A}_{d_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}})=I_{\lceil d/2\rceil}(S^{A}_{d_{|d+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}})
.

Let us first show that
v_{i}=0
for each

.
Suppose to the contrary that

for some

.
Assume that

\lceil d/2\rceil\geq d(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{c}^{2})\geq 1+d-|\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{v})\cap\textrm{supp}(\mathbf{c}^{2})|

As

for both

Another interesting question is whether there exist some universal conditions for the center points of the Hamming balls which obtain the maximum size intersection when we consider more than three balls (instead of exactly three). With the help of our Theorem
3
, such description could lead to a general formula for the value
N^{q}_{t}(s,d)
which would be of interested as it is related to DNA-storage as mentioned in the introduction. Further lines of research include describing the algorithmic complexity of the problem in particular when the number of balls
s

3
, the complexity of any related algorithm is unclear as we have to solve a system of non-constant number of linear inequalities.
