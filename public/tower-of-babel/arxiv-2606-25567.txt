Convergence of the Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz constraint qualification for Constrained Composite Optimization
Christian Kanzow, Jannis Krüger

Abstract
In this work we provide theoretical and practical results of the Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method (SALM) for constrained composite optimization problems whose objective is the sum of a smooth and a nonsmooth function. We obtain global convergence results to an M-stationary point for SALM under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz constraint qualification (PŁCQ). For this result the boundedness of the Lagrange multipliers is crucial, and this is shown under the assumption that the nonsmooth part of the objective function is locally Lipschitz continuous. A counterexample shows that one cannot expect to get bounded multipliers without such an assumption. The performance of the algorithm is evaluated numerically on a set of sparse portfolio optimization problems with two different regularization terms, one being Lipschitz and the other one being non-Lipschitz. The results are significantly better for the Lipschitz sparsity term, whereas the underlying method generates seemingly unbounded multipliers in many instances when using the non-Lipschitz sparsity function.

Introduction

We consider constrained optimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\varphi(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad g(x)\leq 0,\quad h(x)=0

(1.1)

under the following assumptions:

Assumption 1.1
.

(i)

The functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
are continuously differentiable.

(ii)

\varphi
is a locally Lipschitz continuous function.

Problems of this kind arise in a wide range of topics such as engineering, machine learning, optimal control or signal processing, with tasks like image restoration
[
6
]
, low-rank matrix completion
[
8
]
, compressed sensing
[
41
]
, clustering
[
39
]
, principal component analysis
[
29
]
and model predictive control
[
13
]
.

The theoretical difficulty for solving problems of such composite nature is the non-smooth function in the objective, as this requires an extension of differentiability and stationarity concepts that may lack the nice properties of smooth problems.

In this paper we consider the Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method (SALM) as a suitable method for numerically solving composite constrained problems. Building on the classical Augmented Lagrangian Method by Powell
[
35
]
, Hestenes
[
22
]
, and Rockafellar
[
36
]
, the safeguarded version introduced for the
ALGENCAN
solver
[
1
]
is a powerful and versatile choice for smooth constrained optimization due to its algorithmic simplicity and extensive global convergence theory, see also
[
7
]
.

As Augmented Lagrangian methods require solving an unconstrained minimization problem in each step, a practically useful extension of SALM to composite problems necessitates the existence of subproblem solvers which can at least find subdifferentially stationary points of unconstrained composite problems satisfying Assumption
1.1
. More recently, suitable results for the Proximal Gradient Method
[
24
]
and Proximal Quasi Newton Methods
[
17
,
12
]
have been established. This gave the opportunity to develop a global convergence theory for SALM and constrained composite problems in
[
16
]
, using even a more general problem setting via geometric constraints, and only requiring
\varphi
to be proper and
lower semicontinuous (lsc). In the latter paper, the authors use AM-regularity as a constraint qualification in order to show subsequential convergence of the SALM sequence to a M-stationary point for the more general problem.

We want to follow a different approach here. As we consider a more specialized setting with explicit equality and inequality constraints, we are looking to use the least strict constraint qualification tailored to this constraint setting that also still ensures a global convergence result of the iterates. For this approach, we mainly follow the considerations of Andreani et al. in
[
2
]
. In that paper, the authors introduce a constraint qualification (PŁCQ) using a Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, show that it is in fact equivalent to an Error Bound Condition (EB), and also show that this is the least restrictive constraint qualification for smooth problems that still ensures boundedness of the multiplier sequences, which is in return pivotal in establishing convergence of the SALM iterates to an M-stationary point.

In view of these results cited above,
\varphi
being locally Lipschitz continuous may seem like a quite restrictive assumption for the regularization term. However, this restriction is necessary for the required multiplier boundedness, as illustrated in Section
4.1
.

The rest of this work is organized as follows: Section
2

summarizes some basic facts from variational analysis.
In Section
3
, we extend the global convergence theory of SALM under the PŁCQ from
[
2
]
to our composite optimization setting (
1.1
) under
Assumption
1.1
. We then provide a counterexample that
demonstrates the necessity of the local Lipschitz assumption for
\varphi

in Section
4
. We present some numerical results of SALM applied to
sparse portfolio optimization comparing Lipschitz and non-Lipschitz regularization functions in Section
5
, and conclude the paper with some
final remarks in Section
6
.

\Omega

\displaystyle I(x^{*}):=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:g_{i}(x^{*})=0\}.

The sets of non-negative and positive real numbers are denoted by
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

, respectively. For
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write

\displaystyle v_{+}:=(\max\{0,v_{1}\},\ldots,\max\{0,v_{n}\})^{T}

for the projection of
v
onto the nonnegative orthant
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
The symbol
\|\cdot\|
always denotes the Euclidean norm, and
B_{r}(x)
and
\overline{B}_{r}(x)
the corresponding open and closed balls
with radius
r
centered at
x
, respectively. Finally,
\operatorname{dist}(x,S)
denotes the Euclidean distance
\inf_{w\in S}\|x-w\|
from
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2
Mathematical Background

The set of extended real numbers is denoted by

\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.
We write
x\to^{f}x^{*}
if
x\to x^{*}
and
f(x)\to f(x^{*})
, also called
f
-attentive convergence
. For
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
we write
\operatorname{dom}(f):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}
and say that
f
is proper, if
\operatorname{dom}(f)\neq\emptyset
and
f(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For our convergence analysis we recall some definitions and classical results from nonsmooth analysis as presented in the standard sources
[
30
]
and
[
37
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc and
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
.

(a)

The set

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}f(x):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\biggm|\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{f(x)-f(\bar{x})-v^{T}(x-\bar{x})}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0\right\}

is called the
Fréchet subdifferential
of
f
at
x
.

(b)

The set

\displaystyle\partial f(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\{x^{k}\},\{v^{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{k}\to^{f}x,v^{k}\to v,v^{k}\in\hat{\partial}f(x^{k})\,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\}

is called the
limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential
of
f
at
x
.

By the definitions of the subdifferentials it is apparent that

\hat{\partial}f(x)\subseteq\partial f(x)

(2.1)

holds for all
x\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
.

These subdifferentials admit a generalized Fermat rule
[
30
, Proposition 1.30 (i)]
, which leads to a natural extension of stationarity for an unconstrained non-smooth setting.

Lemma 2.2

(Subdifferential Fermat rule)
.

Let
f
be proper and lsc and let
f
have a local minimum in
x^{*}
. Then

0\in\hat{\partial}f(x^{*})

(2.2)

and

0\in\partial f(x^{*}).

(2.3)

Points satisfying these conditions are called
F- or S-stationary
(
2.2
) (S for strong) and
M-stationary
(
2.3
) respectively. We can relax the stationarity condition by introducing
\varepsilon
-stationarity. Here, we only introduce this terminology for the Mordukhovich subdifferential.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper and lsc. A vector
x^{*}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
is called an
\varepsilon
-stationary
point, if there exists an
\eta\in\partial f(x^{*})
with
\|\eta\|\leq\varepsilon
.

In the following we will list the properties of the Mordukhovich subdifferential used in the convergence analysis. The first result is the robustness of the Mordukhovich subdifferential, which serves as a non-smooth substitute for gradient continuity.

Theorem 2.4

(Robustness of the Limiting Subdifferential)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc, and
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
be given. Then

\displaystyle\partial f(\bar{x})=\Limsup_{x\to^{f}\bar{x}}\partial f(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\{x^{k}\}\to^{f}\bar{x},\exists\{v^{k}\}\to v:v^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})\ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

The next important result is the sum rule, which essentially allows us to split our composite problem and deal with the differentiable and non-differentiable part separately.

Theorem 2.5

(Sum Rule)
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc, and let
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)\cap\operatorname{dom}(g)
be given. Then

(a)

If
f
is differentiable in
\bar{x}
, then
\hat{\partial}(f+g)(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+\hat{\partial}g(\bar{x})
.

(b)

If
f
is differentiable in a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
and continuously differentiable in
\bar{x}
, then
\partial(f+g)(\bar{x})=\nabla f(\bar{x})+\partial g(\bar{x})
.

If we have local Lipschitz continuity around a given point, the Mordukhovich subdifferential has the following useful boundedness property, cf.
[
37
, Theorem 9.13]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be locally Lipschitz around some point
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
with constant
L=L(\bar{x})>0
. Then
\|v\|\leq L
for all
v\in\partial f(\bar{x})
.

3
The Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method

Recall that the
Lagrangian function
of the optimization problem (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\varphi(x)+\lambda^{T}g(x)+\mu^{T}h(x),

whereas the (Hestenes-Powell-Rockafellar)
augmented Lagrangian function
with penalty parameter
\rho>0
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(x,\lambda,\mu)=f(x)+\varphi(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left(\left\|\left(g(x)+\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\right)_{+}\right\|^{2}+\left\|h(x)+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\right\|^{2}\right)

for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and certain vectors
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
usually called the
Lagrange multipliers
.

We define the Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method (SALM) similar to
[
2
]
as follows.

Algorithm 3.1
.

(Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method)

(S.0)

Let
u_{\max}>0
,
v_{\min}<v_{\max}
,
\{u_{k}\}\subseteq[0,u_{\max}]^{m}
and
\{v_{k}\}\subseteq[v_{\min},v_{\max}]^{p}
be bounded sequences. Let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
with
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
. Choose a starting point
(x^{0},\lambda^{0},\mu^{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
and a
\rho_{0}>0
as well as parameters
\tau\in(0,1),\gamma>1
and set
k:=0
.

(S.1)

If a suitable termination criterion holds for
(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})
: STOP.

(S.2)

Compute
x^{k+1}
as an
\varepsilon_{k}
-stationary point of

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{k}}(x,u^{k},v^{k}).

(S.3)

Set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{i}^{k+1}&:=(u_{i}^{k}+\rho_{k}g_{i}(x^{k+1}))_{+}\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,m,\\
\mu_{j}^{k+1}&:=v^{k}_{j}+\rho_{k}h_{j}(x^{k+1})\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,p.\end{split}

(3.1)

(S.4)

If either
k=0
or the two conditions

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|h(x^{k+1})\|&\leq\tau\|h(x^{k})\|\quad\text{and}\\
\left\|\min\left\{-g(x^{k+1}),\frac{u^{k}}{\rho_{k}}\right\}\right\|&\leq\tau\left\|\min\left\{-g(x^{k}),\frac{u^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}\right\}\right\|\end{split}

(3.2)

hold, set
\rho_{k+1}:=\rho_{k}
, otherwise set
\rho_{k+1}:=\gamma\rho_{k}
.

(S.5)

Set
k\leftarrow k+1
and go to (S.1)

The safeguarding mechanism, which is to use the bounded sequences
\{u^{k}\},\{v^{k}\}
in the augmented Lagrangian for unconstrained subproblems, yields better global convergence properties, as discussed in
[
25
]
.

The multiplier update in (
3.1
) is done such that the following relation between Lagrangian and augmented Lagrangian holds in each step.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It holds

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})=\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{k-1}}(x^{k},u^{k-1},v^{k-1})

for the iterates of the safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method.

Proof.

By the sum rule for the Mordukhovich subdifferential and the continuous differentiability of
f,g,h
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{M}\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{k-1}}(x^{k},u^{k-1},v^{k-1})

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x^{k})+\partial_{x}\varphi(x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(u_{i}^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{p}(v_{j}^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k}))\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})

\displaystyle=\nabla f(x^{k})+\partial_{x}\varphi(x^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k}),

where the penultimate identity follows from the definitions of the updated
multipliers
\lambda_{i}^{k}
and
\mu_{j}^{k}
in Algorithm
3.1
.
∎

In smooth constrained minimization problems, we are aiming for convergence of our algorithm to a point satisfying the KKT conditions. These conditions are necessary for local minima under a suitable constraint qualification, and are also algorithmically verifiable in a reasonable way. For our potentially non-smooth setting, the counterpart of the KKT conditions is the notion of M-stationarity that is applicable to our case. We do this by adapting the generalized definition of composite M-stationarity from
[
16
]
to our explicit constraint setting, which leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

The triple
(x^{*},\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
is called an
M-stationary point
of problem (
1.1
) if the following
conditions hold:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\in\nabla f(x^{*})+\partial\varphi(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{*}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu^{*}_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*})\quad\text{(stationarity)},

\displaystyle h(x^{*})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(equality feasibility)},

\displaystyle g(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq 0\quad\text{(inequality feasibility)},

\displaystyle(\lambda^{*})^{T}g(x^{*})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(complementarity)}.

In our subsequent convergence analysis, we only consider the case that the SALM Algorithm runs indefinitely and produces an infinite series of iterates. In practice, we want to employ a termination criterion in (S.1) that checks for (approximate) stationarity, for more details we refer to Section
5
.

Ensuring the boundedness of the sequences generated by SALM is already sufficient for convergence to an M-stationary point on a subsequence, as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\big\{(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})\big\}
be an infinite sequence generated by Algorithm
3.1
. Then every accumulation point of this sequence is an M-stationary
point of the program (
1.1
).

Proof.

Let
(x^{*},\lambda,\mu)
be an accumulation point of the sequence

\big\{(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})\big\}
, and let

\big\{(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})\big\}_{K}
be a subsequence converging
to this accumulation point. We proceed by showing that the M-stationarity
conditions hold in
(x^{*},\lambda,\mu)
.

To this end, we begin with the feasibility of
x^{*}
. Taking the multiplier
update (
3.1
) and rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k})_{+}\leq(u_{i}^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}=\lambda_{i}^{k}

for
i=1,\ldots,m

and

\displaystyle\rho_{k-1}|h_{j}(x^{k})|

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sign}(h_{j}(x^{k}))\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{sign}(h_{j}(x^{k}))(\mu^{k}_{j}-v_{j}^{k-1})

\displaystyle\leq|\mu_{j}^{k}|+\max\big\{|v_{\min}|,|v_{\max}|\big\}

for
j=1,\ldots,p
. By the boundedness of the multiplier sequences on
K
,
the right-hand sides of the respective equations are bounded for
k\in K
, hence there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k})_{+}\leq c,\quad\rho_{k-1}|h_{j}(x^{k})|\leq c

(3.3)

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,
j=1,\ldots,p
,
k\in K
.

We first assume that the sequence
\{\rho_{k}\}
is unbounded. Due to the
updating of the penalty parameter, this already implies
\{\rho_{k}\}\to\infty
. In particular, we therefore have
\lim_{k\in K}\rho_{k-1}=\infty
.
Then (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\lim_{k\in K}g_{i}(x^{k})_{+}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{k\in K}|h_{j}(x^{k})|=0

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,p
. Since
\lim_{k\in K}x^{k}=x^{*}
and
g,h
are continuous, we therefore obtain
g(x^{*})\leq 0
and
h(x^{*})=0
,
which immediately shows feasibility of
x^{*}
.

On the other hand, if
\{\rho_{k}\}
is bounded, this sequence is eventually
constant. Thus the condition (
3.2
) cannot be violated infinitely
many times, and in infinitely many iterations we have a decrease of at least
\tau\in(0,1)
. This immediately yields
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|h(x^{k})\|=0
and thus
h(x^{*})=0
. Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\min\left\{-g(x^{k}),\frac{u^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}\right\}=0.

(3.4)

Assume there exists an index
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
with
g_{i}(x^{*})>0
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\min\left\{-g_{i}(x^{k}),\frac{u_{i}^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}\right\}\leq-g_{i}(x^{k})\leq-\frac{1}{2}g_{i}(x^{*})<0

for
k
sufficiently large, which produces a contradiction by taking the limit
k\to\infty
. Thus we must have
g_{i}(x^{*})\leq 0
for all
=1,\ldots,m
, and feasibility of
x^{*}
holds also in this case.

We next verify the stationarity of the limit
(x^{*},\lambda,\mu)
.
In Step 2 of the SALM, we compute
x^{k}
as an
\varepsilon_{k}
-stationary point of the augmented Lagrangian, hence there exists an
\eta_{k}\in\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{k-1}}(x^{k},u^{k-1},v^{k-1})
with
\|\eta_{k}\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}
. By Lemma
3.2
we have
\eta_{k}\in\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}(x^{k},\lambda^{k},\mu^{k})
. As all functions except
\varphi
in the Lagrangian function are continuously differentiable, this is equivalent to the existence of a
z^{k}\in\partial_{x}\varphi(x^{k})
with

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f(x^{k})+z^{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\right\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}.

We now define

E^{k}:=\nabla f(x^{k})+z^{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})

(3.5)

and

z^{*}:=-\nabla f(x^{*})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*})
. Then

\displaystyle\|z^{k}-z^{*}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|z^{k}+\nabla f(x^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\right.

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla f(x^{k})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\nabla f(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|E^{k}\right\|+\|\nabla f(x^{*})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{k}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\right\|+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*})-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{i}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{k}+\|\nabla f(x^{*})-\nabla f(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{k}_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\right\|+\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*})-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{i}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\right\|

Taking the limit
k\to\infty
,
k\in K
and using
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
as well as the continuous differentiability of
f,g,h
, the right-hand side vanishes, and we have
\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in K}z^{k}=z^{*}
. By the robustness of the Mordukhovich subdifferential, this shows
z^{*}\in\partial\varphi(x^{*})
and thus

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla f(x^{*})+\partial\varphi(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}\nabla h_{j}(x^{*}),

so that the triple
(x^{*},\lambda,\mu)
satisfies the stationarity condition.

We finally establish satisfaction of the complementarity property.
To this end, consider an arbitrary index
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. If

g_{i}(x^{*})=0
, we are already finished. Hence, let
g_{i}(x^{*})<0
. If

\lim_{k\to\infty}\rho_{k-1}=\infty
, we obtain by the boundedness of
\{u^{k}\}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\in K}u^{k-1}_{i}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k})=-\infty.

In particular, for
k\in K
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{k}=(u^{k-1}_{i}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}=0,

and thus
\lambda_{i}=0
in the limit
k\in K
. We now assume that
\rho_{k}
is bounded, hence, eventually constant. By the same reasoning that led to (
3.4
) and
g_{i}(x^{*})<0
, we must have

\displaystyle\min\left\{-g_{i}(x^{k}),\frac{u_{i}^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}\right\}=\frac{u_{i}^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}

for sufficiently large
k\in K
. As
\{\rho_{k-1}\}
is eventually constant,
taking the limit on
K
yields
\lim_{k\in K}u_{i}^{k-1}=0
. For all
k\in K
sufficiently large, we therefore have

\displaystyle u_{i}^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k})<0

and thus by (
3.1
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{k}=(u_{i}^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}=0.

In the limit
k\in K
, we obtain
\lambda_{i}=0
. Hence, for all
i=1,\ldots,m
,
we have shown
\lambda_{i}g_{i}(x^{*})=0
.
∎

Note that in the proof we did not have to rely on the Lipschitz-continuity of
\varphi
, hence this result is also valid if
\varphi
is merely continuous.
However, we need the Lipschitz property of
\varphi
to establish sufficient conditions for multiplier boundedness, and this is also necessary in the sense that applications of SALM on non-Lipschitz functions can lead to unbounded multipliers, as discussed in Section
4.1
.

We can ensure multiplier boundedness if certain constraint qualifications hold in the limit point
x^{*}
, which are taken from
[
2
]
and defined as follows.

Definition 3.5
.

We define the infeasibility measure
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
as

\displaystyle\Phi(x):=\frac{1}{2}(\|g(x)_{+}\|^{2}+\|h(x)\|^{2}).

(3.6)

We say that
\Phi
meets the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ) inequality
at
x^{*}\in\Omega
when there are constants
\nu>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Phi(x)}\leq\nu\|\nabla\Phi(x)\|\quad\forall x\in B_{\delta}(x^{*}).

(3.7)

We call
\nu
a
Polyak-Łojasiewicz constant
at
x^{*}
. If
\Phi
satisfies the PŁ inequality at
x^{*}
we say that the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz constraint qualification (PŁCQ)
holds at
x^{*}
.

This type of inequality was first studied by Polyak in
[
34
]
, and was identified as a special instance of the Łojasiewicz inequality
[
28
]
. Different to our application here, it was formulated for an optimality measure
\Theta(x):=f(x)-f^{*}
, where
f^{*}
is the optimal value of an unconstrained minimization problem with objective
f
. This is why, since its introduction, the inequality has been mainly used to prove convergence rate results for unconstrained optimization methods like gradient descent and
proximal gradient methods, see also
[
5
,
26
]
.

In the approach by Andreani et al.
[
2
]
,
the PŁ inequality for the infeasibility measure
\Phi
as a constraint qualification yields a sufficient condition for multiplier boundedness. Additionally, Andreani et al. show in
[
2
, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 4.1]
that, in the case of the constraint functions
g,h
being also locally Lipschitz continuous, the PŁCQ is equivalent to their own Relaxed Quasi-Normality (RQN) constraint qualification from
[
3
]
as well as the commonly used error bound constraint qualification (EB). The latter is defined as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

We say that a feasible
x^{*}\in\Omega
satisfies the
Error Bound (EB) constraint qualification
or
Error Bound condition
if there exist constants
\kappa>0
and
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(x,\Omega)\leq\kappa\sqrt{\Phi(x)}\quad\forall x\in B_{\delta}(x^{*})

where
\kappa
is called the
local error bound constant
for
\Omega
at
x^{*}
relative to the infeasibility measure
\Phi
.

The error bound condition was first introduced in
[
23
]
for systems of linear inequalities. Similar to the PŁCQ, it has often been used in unconstrained minimization to connect an optimality measure in a point
x
to the distance of
x
to a set of minimizers
S
in order to obtain results about convergence rates (cf.
[
18
,
31
]
). However, there are also instances in the literature where the EB condition is applied to an infeasibility measure and the distance to the feasible set, like in
[
32
]
. In the latter, the EB condition is used to derive an exact penalty result for a constrained problem.

In the following, we show that if the PŁCQ holds in an accumulation point of a SALM sequence, we obtain multiplier boundedness. In the case of locally Lipschitz gradients of the constraint functions, this result can also be extended to the equivalent EB and RQN constraint qualifications. In the latter setting, we also note that on the contrary, if any of the three equivalent CQs is violated, SALM can produce unbounded multiplier sequences even for completely smooth objective functions, as shown in
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
, which is why these CQs can be seen as the minimally restrictive ones for ensuring multiplier boundedness.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
x^{*}
be feasible and assume that
g,h
are continously differentiable. Let additionally
\varphi
be locally Lipschitz continuous in a neighborhood of
x^{*}
. If the PŁCQ holds at
x^{*}
and there exists an infinite subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that the sequence
\{x^{k}\}
generated by the SALM converges on
K
to
x^{*}
, then the associated dual subsequences
\{\lambda^{k}\}_{k\in K}
and
\{\mu^{k}\}_{k\in K}
are bounded.

Proof.

Let
\{x^{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{\rho_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be infinite sequences generated by the safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method. By the definition of the algorithm and similar reasoning as in the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we have that there exists
z^{k}\in\partial\varphi(x^{k})
and
\{E^{k}\}
as defined in (
3.5
) such that

\displaystyle\|E^{k}\|\leq\varepsilon_{k}

(3.8)

for all
k\in K
. By
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
, we obtain
\lim_{k\to\infty}E^{k}=0
. As
\varphi
is locally Lipschitz at
x^{*}
, there exist
L>0
and
\delta>0

such that

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|\leq L\|x-y\|

for all
x,y\in B_{\delta}(x^{*})
. Proposition
2.6
now implies
\|z^{k}\|\leq L
for all
k\in\mathbb{K}
sufficiently large. This again implies by the above results and the continuous differentiability of
f
that

\|\nabla f(x^{k})+z^{k}-E^{k}\|\leq\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+\|z^{k}\|+\|E^{k}\|\leq\|\nabla f(x^{*})\|+L+1

(3.9)

for
k\in K
sufficiently large. We define
M_{1}:=\max\{u_{\max},|v_{\min}|,|v_{\max}|\}
. By continuous differentiability of
g,h
, there exists a constant
M_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\|\leq M_{2}\text{ and }\|\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\|\leq M_{2}

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,p
as well as
k\in K
sufficiently large.
Altogether we have

\displaystyle\rho_{k-1}\|\nabla\Phi(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{k-1}(g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k})\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\rho_{k-1}(g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}-\lambda_{i}^{k}]\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{p}[\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k})-\mu_{j}^{k}]\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})+E^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})-z^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\rho_{k-1}(g_{i}(x^{k}))_{+}-(\rho_{k-1}g_{i}(x^{k})+u_{i}^{k-1})_{+}]\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{p}[\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k})-(\rho_{k-1}h_{j}(x^{k})+v_{j}^{k-1})]\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})+E^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})-z^{k}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}^{k-1}\|\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})\|+\sum_{j=1}^{p}|v_{j}^{k-1}|\|\nabla h_{j}(x^{k})\|+\|E^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})-z^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}M_{1}M_{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}M_{1}M_{2}+\|\nabla f(x^{*})\|+L+1

\displaystyle=(m+p)M_{1}M_{2}+\|\nabla f(x^{*})\|+L+1=:S

for sufficiently large
k\in K
, where the first inequality follows from
the nonexpansiveness of the projection operator. By assumption, the
PŁ-inequality holds in
x^{*}
. By combining it with the above inequality,
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{k-1}\sqrt{\Phi(x^{k})}\leq\nu\rho_{k-1}\|\nabla\Phi(x^{k})\|\leq\nu S

for
k\in K
sufficiently large, where
\nu>0
denotes the
PŁ constant. For all
k\in K
large enough, we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\|(u^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g(x^{k}))_{+}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(u^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}g(x^{k}))_{+}-\rho_{k-1}g(x^{k})_{+}\|+\rho_{k-1}\|g(x^{k})_{+}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|u^{k-1}\|+\rho_{k-1}\sqrt{2\Phi(x^{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{m}M_{1}+\sqrt{2}\nu S

and, analogously,

\displaystyle\|\mu^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\|v^{k-1}+\rho_{k-1}h(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{k-1}\|+\rho_{k-1}\|h(x^{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v^{k-1}\|+\rho_{k-1}\sqrt{2\Phi(x^{k})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{p}M_{1}+\sqrt{2}\nu S.

This shows the boundedness of the multiplier sequences
\{\lambda^{k}\}
and
\{\mu^{k}\}
on
K
.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

is feasible and there exists sequences

and

with

for all
i\in I(x^{*})
and
\lambda_{i}^{k}=0
for all

(a)

x^{k}\to x^{*}
.

(b)

For all

we have

.

(c)

\|\eta^{k}\|\to 0
.

(d)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i\in I(x^{*})}|\lambda_{i}^{k}g_{i}(x^{k})|+\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\mu_{j}^{k}h_{j}(x^{k})|=0

Corollary 3.9
.

Let

be feasible, fulfilling PŁCQ and there exists an infinite subset

such that the sequence

generated by the SALM converges on

to

. Then

Proof.

If we consider

, property (a) holds by assumption. By the SALM algorithm, we solve the subproblems as
\varepsilon_{k}
-stationary, which in turn implies the existence of a sequence

satisfying properties (b) and (c).
Lastly, by Theorem

, the PŁCQ in

guarantees that the correspoonding Lagrange multiplier subsequences generated by SALM stay bounded, which already implies (d). Also, by the reasoning from the complementarity section of the proof of Theorem

, we have
\lambda^{k}_{i}=0
for

and

sufficiently large. Hence all requirements of the sequences

and
\{\mu^{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
are fulfilled by the SALM-multipliers.
∎

4
On the Local Lipschitz Assumption of
\varphi

For some applications of the SALM it would be desirable, not to demand the local Lipschitz property of
\varphi
in Theorem
3.7
. This issue arises for example when considering sparsity optimization: In order to obtain sparse solutions of a smooth optimization problem, it is popular to set
\varphi
to a
\ell^{0}
-regularization-term and consider the composite problem. The
\ell^{0}
-pseudonorm is defined by first setting

\displaystyle|a|_{0}:=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }a=0,\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(4.1)

for
a\in\mathbb{R}
and then setting

\displaystyle\|x\|_{0}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|_{0}

which is essentially counting the number of non-zero entries of the vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that despite the notation, this does not define a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The function is also obviously not continuous in zero, and hence also not locally Lipschitz continuous there.
However,
\|\cdot\|_{0}
is proper and lsc.

The following example shows that requiring
\varphi
to be only proper and lsc does not necessarily lead to bounded Lagrange multipliers under the PŁCQ which would be required for the applicability of Theorem
3.4
. The core observation is that without local Lipschitz continuity, we cannot rely on
Lemma
2.6
and may get arbitrarily large
Mordukhovich subgradients, which in turn impact the Lagrange multipliers.

The above example illustrates that the convergence theory we established in this paper is not directly applicable to optimization problems with
\ell_{0}
-sparsity-regularization. However, there exist ways of circumventing this issue. One of them is to introduce surrogate sparsity functions with similar behavior as the
\ell_{0}

\psi_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{\delta_{i}}(x_{i}).

The approximation is done such that

satisfies the following three conditions.

(R1)

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\psi_{\delta}(t)=|t|_{0}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
(pointwise).

(R2)

For all
\delta>0
,
\psi_{\delta}
is symmetric on
\mathbb{R}
(i.e.,
\psi_{\delta}(-t)=\psi_{\delta}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
) as well as concave and non-decreasing on
[0,\infty)
.

(R3)

\psi_{\delta}(t)=1
for all
t
with
|t|\geq\delta
.

5.2
Sparse Portfolio Optimization

The test problem from the field of mathematical finance is the sparse formulation of a portfolio optimization problem, specifically the Markowitz mean-variance model (cf.
[
10
]
). The problem in its original form is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{x}\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Qx\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mu^{T}x\geq\varrho,\quad\mathbf{1}_{n}^{T}x=1,\quad x\geq 0.

The vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is seen as the amount invested in a collection of
n
financial assets. Expected risk
x^{T}Qx
and return
\mu^{T}x
are modeled by determining the covariance matrix
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and a vector
\mu
of mean values of each asset, which have been calculated from past stock market performance. The value
\varrho>0
indicates a minimal return threshold. We assume to have a budget of
1
. The equality constraint then indicates that the whole budget must be spent on assets. Finally, we want to invest money, hence only allowing positive spending.

Splitting the investment onto a broad range of assets with potentially small amounts per position may not be feasible in practice due to for example fixed transaction costs for each purchase
[
9
]
. Thus, we modify the original problem in order to obtain a sparse solution to the portfolio optimization:

\displaystyle\min_{x}\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Qx+\beta\varphi(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mu^{T}x\geq\varrho,\quad\mathbf{1}_{n}^{T}x=1,\quad x\geq 0,

(5.1)

with a sparsity promoting function
\varphi
and regularization factor
\beta>0
, we obtain a composite formulation this way. A natural choice would be
\varphi=\|\cdot\|_{0}
. However, in view of the presented convergence theory, we chose the
\ell_{0}
-norm surrogate
\varphi=\varphi_{\delta}^{\mathrm{MCP}}
from
Example
4.2
with
\delta=0.1
for testing our SALM algorithm. Determining the proximal operator of the MCP function is an elementary, but quite arduous task, we refer to
[
38
]
for more details, where the prox-operator is determined for a slightly modified version of MCP.

We additionally ran the problem with
\ell_{p}^{p}
-regularization for
0<p<1
. This function is in particular not locally Lipschitz in zero, which we use to demonstrate the numerical differences in the algorithmic performance of SALM, especially in view of the Lipschitz properties discussed in Section
4
. The
\ell_{p}^{p}
-quasinorm admits analytical expressions for its proximal operator if
p=\frac{1}{2}
or
p=\frac{2}{3}

[
27
]
. We chose
p=\frac{1}{2}
for our numerical experiments.

6
Conclusions

We presented the Safeguarded Augmented Lagrangian Method as an algorithm capable of performing optimization on composite constrained problems with a locally Lipschitz continuous regularization function. We obtained a global convergence result to an M-stationary point under the minimally restrictive PŁCQ, equivalently under the error bound condition. The boundedness of the Lagrange multipliers played a central role in the convergence analysis, and not imposing a Lipschitz property onto
\varphi
may lead to unbounded multipliers, as demonstrated in the counterexample. Our numerical findings underline our theoretical considerations about the choice of a regularization function.

Recently, De Marchi et al. introduced the Elastic SALM in
[
15
]
, a modification of the SALM algorithm that allows the safeguards to grow in a controlled way. This approach has the advantage of unifying the good global convergence guarantees of SALM with some desirable properties of the classical ALM when applied to convex problems.
As to the best of our knowledge, it is not yet clear, if Elastic SALM also has a global convergence property for potentially non-convex problems under EB/PŁCQ, which might be a topic for further research.

Appendix

This proof for CAM-stationarity as a necessary optimality condition is a direct extension of
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
to the composite setting.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
x^{*}
be a local minimizer of (
1.1
). Then
x^{*}
is CAM-stationary.

Proof.

We denote
\Psi:=f+\varphi
. As
x^{*}
is a local minimizer, there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\Psi(x^{*})\leq\Psi(x)
for all
feasible
x\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x^{*})
. We consider the auxiliary problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad g(x)\leq 0,\quad h(x)=0,\quad x\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x^{*}).

(A.1)

By construction,
x^{*}
is the unique solution of Problem (
A.1
). For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define another auxiliary problem as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Psi(x)+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\left[\|g(x)_{+}\|^{2}+\|h(x)\|^{2}\right]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x^{*}).

(A.2)

As we have a continuous objective function on a compact set, there exists a solution
x^{k}
to (
A.2
) for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By the feasibility of
x^{*}
and the optimality of each
x^{k}
with respect to (
A.2
), we have the property

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\left[\|g(x^{k})_{+}\|^{2}+\|h(x^{k})\|^{2}\right]\leq\Psi(x^{*}).

(A.3)

Furthermore, as the sequence
\{x^{k}\}\subseteq\overline{B}_{\delta}(x^{*})
is bounded, there exists an infinite set
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
z^{*}\in\overline{B}_{\delta}(x^{*})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in K}x^{k}=z^{*}.

We can now divide expression (
A.3
) by
k
and take the limit
k\to\infty
for
k\in K
. This yields

\displaystyle\|g(z^{*})_{+}\|^{2}+\|h(z^{*})\|^{2}=0,

showing feasibility of
z^{*}
for problem (
A.1
). Additionally, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq\Psi(x^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{k}{2}\left[\|g(x^{k})_{+}\|^{2}+\|h(x^{k})\|^{2}\right]\leq\Psi(x^{*}),

and taking the limit
k\to\infty
,
k\in K
yields

\displaystyle\Psi(z^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{*}-x^{*}\|\leq\Psi(x^{*}),

which implies
z^{*}=x^{*}
by the optimality of
x^{*}
for Problem (
A.1
), thus showing

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in K}x^{k}=x^{*}.

(A.4)

We now define the sequences
\lambda^{k}:=kg(x^{k})_{+}
,
\mu^{k}:=kh(x^{k})
and
\eta^{k}:=x^{*}-x^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{K}
. Clearly,
\lambda^{k}\geq 0
and for
i\notin I(x^{*})
, by the continuity of
g
we have
g_{i}(x^{k})\leq 0
and hence
\lambda_{i}^{k}=0
for
k
sufficiently large. We take
K_{1}\subseteq K
such that
\lambda_{i}^{k}=0
for all
i\notin I(x^{*})
and
k\in K_{1}
and show
that all properties of CAM-stationarity hold for the above defined sequences on the index set
K_{1}
.

In (
A.4
) we showed property (a) of CAM-stationarity for the sequence
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in K_{1}}
. By definition,
\{\eta^{k}\}_{k\in K_{1}}
fulfills (c).

By local optimality with respect to the auxiliary problem (
A.2
), the generalized Fermat rule from Lemma
2.2
and the Mordukhovich subdifferential sum rule yields

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla f(x^{k})+\partial\varphi(x^{k})+x^{k}-x^{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}kg_{i}(x^{k})_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}kh_{j}(x^{k})\nabla h_{j}(x^{k}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\eta^{k}=x^{*}-x^{k}\in\nabla f(x^{k})+\partial\varphi(x^{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k}\nabla g_{i}(x^{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\mu_{j}^{k}\nabla h_{j}(x^{k}),

which shows property (b), whereas (c) holds by definition of
\eta_{k}

and (a).

Once again considering (
A.3
) and taking the limit
k\to\infty
for
k\in K_{1}
yields

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in K_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}k(g_{i}(x^{k})_{+})^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}kh_{j}(x^{k})^{2}=0.

As

\displaystyle|\mu^{k}_{j}h_{j}(x^{k})|=|kh_{j}(x^{k})^{2}|=kh_{j}(x^{k})^{2}

and

\displaystyle|\lambda_{i}^{k}g_{i}(x^{k})|=|kg_{i}(x^{k})_{+}g_{i}(x^{k})|=|k(g_{i}(x^{k})_{+})^{2}|=k(g_{i}(x^{k})_{+})^{2},

as well as
\lambda_{i}^{k}=0
for all
k\in K_{1}
and
i\notin I(x^{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in K_{1}}\sum_{i\in I(x^{*})}|\lambda_{i}^{k}g_{i}(x^{k})|+\sum_{j=1}^{p}|\mu^{k}_{j}h_{j}(x^{k})|=0,

which shows the last property (d).
