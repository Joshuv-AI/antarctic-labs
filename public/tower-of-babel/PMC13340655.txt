Dorsal onlay buccal mucosal graft urethroplasty in a female patient: a case report from Vietnam

ABSTRACT

Introduction

Urethral stricture in women is a rare and complex condition, often presenting significant challenges in both diagnosis and treatment. The incidence of female urethral stricture accounts for a small proportion of all cases of lower urinary tract obstruction, with estimates ranging from 4% to 13%[1]. The causes of urethral stricture in women include trauma, post-surgical or medical interventions, infections, malignant conditions, and idiopathic factors. Historically, urethral dilation was the primary method for managing this condition. However, this approach has a relatively low success rate, often below 50%, and can lead to further damage to the urethra[1,2]. Over time, urethral reconstructive surgery has been increasingly recognized as an effective and durable treatment option for recurrent or complex urethral strictures, with favorable success rates and acceptable complication profiles. Several different techniques have been described for female urethroplasty surgery, utilizing flaps or grafts, with various approaches. Among these, dorsal urethroplasty with buccal mucosa grafts has demonstrated promising outcomes, with minimal complications, particularly regarding stricture recurrence and urinary incontinence[3]. Case presentation

A 60-year-old female patient presented to our Reconstructive Urology Department with a long-standing history of dysuria. She had previously undergone multiple urethral dilations. During her examination, she underwent a voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG), a retrograde urethrogram, and a combined cystoscopy, which confirmed a 2 cm urethral stricture (Fig. 1). The patient underwent urethral reconstruction with a buccal mucosa graft. The surgical procedure was performed with the patient in the lithotomy position and followed a cystostomy that had been conducted 3 months earlier. An inverted U-shaped incision was made on the dorsum of the urethra, extending from the 9 o’clock to the 3 o’clock position, allowing for the easy passage of a 30 French bougie. A segment of buccal mucosa measuring 5–6 cm in length and 2–3 cm in width was harvested. The buccal mucosa graft was sutured to both lateral edges of the urethra using 4-0 polydioxanone sutures. The reconstructed dorsum of the urethra was then attached to the body of the clitoris, covering the exposed urethral surface (Fig. 2). Confirmation of the newly reconstructed urethra was achieved by passing a flexible cystoscope through the cystostomy opening. A 14 Fr silicone Foley catheter was placed through the new urethra. After 3 weeks, a follow-up VCUG was performed, leading to the removal of the Foley catheter and the cystostomy (Fig. 3). The patient was scheduled for a follow-up appointment 4 months later. At that visit, a flexible cystoscopy demonstrated easy passage through the urethra, and the patient’s maximum urinary flow rate (Qmax) was recorded at greater than 15 mL/s, indicating normal urinary function. In addition to uroflowmetry, postoperative functional assessment in this case included post-void residual volume, symptom evaluation, and continence status. The patient demonstrated minimal post-void residual urine, substantial symptomatic improvement, and preserved continence without de novo stress urinary incontinence or urgency incontinence. Discussion

Urethral stricture in women is a rare but clinically significant condition. Traditional management, which primarily includes urethral dilation or internal urethrotomy, often fails to provide a definitive resolution, with reported recurrence rates exceeding 50%. This condition can lead to further trauma to the urethra and surrounding tissues, resulting in additional fibrosis. As a result, repeat dilations are often associated with persistent symptoms and poor long-term outcomes. Urethroplasty has shown significantly higher success rates and lower recurrence compared to endoscopic treatments, making it the preferred choice for managing female urethral stricture disease[5].

Oral mucosa, especially buccal mucosa, has become a widely accepted graft material for urethral reconstruction. It offers several favorable properties: autologous tissue with excellent elasticity, hair-free epithelium, rich vascularity, and a robust graft “take” with minimal donor-site morbidity from the harvesting process. Dorsal oral mucosa graft urethroplasty has been increasingly reported to yield good functional outcomes and low complication rates. A narrative review by Tao et al confirmed that dorsal oral mucosa urethroplasty is an effective option for managing female urethral strictures, demonstrating high anatomical and functional success with a low risk of restenosis or incontinence[6].

When selecting a surgical approach, both ventral and dorsal techniques have been described. The dorsal approach offers specific advantages. First, it minimizes the risk of urethrovaginal fistula formation by preserving ventral tissues. Additionally, dorsal graft fixation provides a rich blood supply, promoting reliable healing and reducing the risk of restenosis[7]. Current literature does not clearly demonstrate the absolute superiority of the dorsal approach over the ventral approach in female buccal mucosal graft urethroplasty. In this case, the dorsal technique was selected based on anatomical considerations, including stable graft support, a well-vascularized graft bed, and preservation of the ventral urethrovaginal tissues. Preserving the ventral periurethral tissue also maintains anatomical support for the anterior vaginal wall, which may be beneficial if future continence procedures are necessary[8].

The benefits of the dorsal approach are supported by existing literature. In contrast, ventral approaches may carry a higher theoretical risk of fistula formation and could have a more considerable impact on the anterior vaginal wall. Although high-level comparative data are limited, available evidence suggests that dorsal placement optimizes outcomes while preserving critical anatomy[7].

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first clinical case in Vietnam where buccal mucosa was used for urethral reconstruction in a female patient. After the surgery, the patient experienced complete relief from dysuria, with no evidence of donor site complications such as bleeding, infection, or pain after 4 months. Furthermore, this was confirmed by post-operative VCUG, which showed a patent urethra. Cystoscopy through the urethra was easily performed, and the post-surgical Qmax measured greater than 15 mL/s. In addition to uroflowmetry, postoperative functional assessment in this case included post-void residual volume, symptom evaluation, and continence status. The patient demonstrated minimal post-void residual urine, substantial symptomatic improvement, and preserved continence without de novo stress urinary incontinence or urgency incontinence. These findings support the feasibility and favorable short-term efficacy of dorsal onlay buccal mucosal graft urethroplasty in this patient. However, long-term follow-up (>12–24 months) is required to confirm durability and exclude late stricture recurrence.

Conclusion

Dorsal onlay oral mucosa graft urethroplasty is an effective and viable method for treating female urethral strictures. However, further studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up are needed to confirm the long-term efficacy and safety of oral mucosa grafts for urethral reconstruction in females.
