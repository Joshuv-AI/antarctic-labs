Birkhoff rigidity from a covariant optical seed
D. A. Easson

Abstract
We present a local seed-to–Kerr–Schild route to Birkhoff rigidity in four-dimensional spherical vacuum gravity. On the two-dimensional orbit space, the areal radius r r determines a scalar F := − ( ∇ r ) 2 F:=-(\nabla r)^{2} , and the reduced vacuum equations imply F ⁡ ( r ) = 1 − 2 ​ M / r F(r)=1-2M/r . We show that the normalized one-forms d ​ r / F dr/F and ( ∗ d r ) / F (*dr)/F are closed, so that the null combinations F − 1 ( d r ± ∗ d r ) F^{-1}(dr\pm*dr) are exact null seed forms. Integrating these yields local Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates in which the metric takes Kerr–Schild form over a flat background. We then prove the corresponding uniqueness statement in the stationary optical sector: spherical symmetry forces the inverse optical seed ℛ \mathcal{R} to equal ± r \pm r , equivalently the optical seed ρ \rho to equal ∓ 1 / r \mp 1/r , and the resulting seed data reconstruct the Schwarzschild family. Thus, Birkhoff rigidity is paired with a spherical converse theorem in the stationary optical framework: Schwarzschild is the unique spherically symmetric stationary vacuum Kerr–Schild geometry generated by a nowhere-vanishing optical seed.

This formulation touches several familiar viewpoints while remaining distinct from
each of them. It shares ingredients with coordinate-free arguments, dual-null
formulations, and Kodama-time constructions, but its organizing feature is the
exact null seed-form packaging of the reduced vacuum equations.

Our goal is not merely to recover Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates in a new
notation, but to show that, in spherical vacuum gravity they arise as integrals of
covariantly defined exact null seed forms extracted directly from the reduced
field equations on the orbit space. In this way, the Kerr–Schild structure
emerges as an intrinsic consequence of the orbit-space vacuum system itself,
rather than being imposed only after Schwarzschild has been recognized in a
preferred chart. Hence the connection to the stationary optical
framework is clearly established: the forward orbit-space construction and the optical converse are
linked by a common seed-based organization of the geometry.

II
Orbit-space seed equations

We work with mostly-minus signature and consider a spherically symmetric spacetime
with two-dimensional Lorentzian orbit space
Q
, equipped with metric
h_{ab}
,
so that

ds^{2}=h_{ab}(x)\,dx^{a}dx^{b}-r(x)^{2}\,d\Omega_{2}^{2},

(1)

where
r(x)
is the areal radius and
d\Omega_{2}^{2}
is the unit round metric on

S^{2}
. All contractions, covariant derivatives, and Hodge duals below refer to

h_{ab}
.

Let
\epsilon_{ab}
denote the volume form on
Q
, normalized by

\nabla_{a}\epsilon_{bc}=0,\qquad\epsilon_{ab}\epsilon^{ab}=-2.

(2)

For any one-form
\alpha_{a}
, define its Hodge dual by

(*\alpha)_{a}:=\epsilon_{a}{}^{b}\alpha_{b}.

(3)

On one-forms in two-dimensional Lorentzian signature one has

\alpha\cdot(*\alpha)=0,\qquad(*\alpha)^{2}=-\alpha^{2},\qquad*(*\alpha)=\alpha.

(4)

We will also use

d(*df)=-(\Box f)\,\epsilon

(5)

for any scalar
f
, and

\alpha\wedge*\beta=-(\alpha\cdot\beta)\,\epsilon

(6)

for any one-forms
\alpha,\beta
.

Proposition 1

(Seed ODE)
.

Let (
1
) be vacuum,

R_{\mu\nu}=0,

(7)

with nonconstant areal radius
r
. Then on
Q
,

\displaystyle R_{ab}[h]-2r^{-1}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}r

\displaystyle=0,

(8)

\displaystyle 1+(\nabla r)^{2}+r\Box r

\displaystyle=0.

(9)

Since
Q
is two-dimensional,

\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}r=\lambda\,h_{ab},\qquad\lambda:=\frac{1}{2}\Box r.

(10)

Defining

F:=-\,(\nabla r)^{2},

(11)

one finds, locally wherever
dr\neq 0
,

1-F-rF^{\prime}(r)=0,

(12)

and hence

F(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}

(13)

for some constant
M
.

Proof.

For the warped product (
1
), the vacuum Einstein equations
reduce to (
8
) and (
9
). Since
Q
is
two-dimensional,

R_{ab}[h]=\frac{1}{2}R[h]\,h_{ab},

(14)

so (
8
) implies (
10
).

Now differentiate (
11
):

\nabla_{a}F=-2\nabla^{b}r\,\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}r=-2\lambda\,\nabla_{a}r.

(15)

Thus
F=F(r)
locally wherever
dr\neq 0
, and

F^{\prime}(r)=-2\lambda=-\Box r.

(16)

Substituting (

) into (
9
) gives
(

), which integrates to (

III
Closed normalized seed forms

The seed equation for
F(r)
has an immediate differential consequence.

Lemma 2

(Closed normalized seed forms)
.

On any open set where
dr\neq 0
and
F\neq 0
, define

dR_{*}:=\frac{dr}{F},\qquad dT:=\frac{*dr}{F}.

(17)

Then both one-forms are closed:

d(dR_{*})=0,\qquad d(dT)=0.

(18)

Hence, on a simply connected local region, there exist functions
R_{*}
and
T

such that (
17
) holds.

Proof.

Since
F=F(r)
,

dF=F^{\prime}(r)\,dr.

(19)

Therefore

d(dR_{*})=d(F^{-1}dr)=-\,F^{-2}dF\wedge dr=0.

(20)

For
dT
,

\displaystyle d(dT)

\displaystyle=d(F^{-1}*dr)

\displaystyle=-\,F^{-2}dF\wedge*dr+F^{-1}d(*dr)

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{F^{\prime}}{F^{2}}\,dr\wedge*dr-\frac{\Box r}{F}\,\epsilon.

(21)

Using (
6
),

dr\wedge*dr=-(dr)^{2}\,\epsilon=F\,\epsilon,

(22)

and then (
16
), we obtain

d(dT)=-\,\frac{F^{\prime}}{F}\,\epsilon-\frac{\Box r}{F}\,\epsilon=0.

(23)

Thus both forms are closed.
∎

Corollary 3

(Diagonal orbit-space form)
.

On the same local region, the orbit-space metric may be written as

h=F(r)\,\bigl(dT^{2}-dR_{*}^{2}\bigr)=F(r)\,dT^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{F(r)}.

(24)

Proof.

From (
17
) and (
4
),

\displaystyle(dT)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(*dr)^{2}}{F^{2}}=\frac{1}{F},

(25)

\displaystyle(dR_{*})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(dr)^{2}}{F^{2}}=-\frac{1}{F},

(26)

\displaystyle dT\cdot dR_{*}

\displaystyle=0.

(27)

Hence

h^{-1}=F^{-1}\bigl(\partial_{T}^{2}-\partial_{R_{*}}^{2}\bigr),

(28)

or equivalently,

h=F(r)\,\bigl(dT^{2}-dR_{*}^{2}\bigr).

(29)

Since
dr=F\,dR_{*}
, the second equality in (
24
) follows immediately.
∎

Corollary 4

(Kodama one-form and Killing symmetry)
.

Define the Kodama one-form on
Q
by

K^{\flat}:=*dr.

(30)

Then

K^{\flat}=F\,dT,\qquad K^{2}=F,

(31)

and the corresponding vector field
K^{a}:=h^{ab}K_{b}
is Killing:

\nabla_{(a}K_{b)}=0.

(32)

Proof.

The relation
K^{\flat}=F\,dT
follows directly from (
17
). For the
Killing property,

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}K_{b}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{a}(\epsilon_{b}{}^{c}\nabla_{c}r)=\epsilon_{b}{}^{c}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{c}r

\displaystyle=\lambda\,\epsilon_{b}{}^{c}h_{ac}=\lambda\,\epsilon_{ba},

(33)

using (
10
). Hence

\nabla_{(a}K_{b)}=0.

(34)

Finally,

K^{2}=(*dr)^{2}=-\,(dr)^{2}=F.

(35)

∎

IV
Exact null seed forms

The null combinations of the normalized seed forms are the primary objects in our proof. They are the point at which the present formulation most clearly differs from
more standard static-coordinate, Kodama, and dual-null treatments of local
Birkhoff rigidity.

Theorem 5

(Exact null seed forms in spherical vacuum)
.

On any simply connected local region of a nontrivial spherically symmetric vacuum
spacetime for which
dr\neq 0
and
F\neq 0
, define

k_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{F}\bigl(dr\pm*dr\bigr).

(36)

Then:

\displaystyle k_{\pm}

\displaystyle=dR_{*}\pm dT,

(37)

\displaystyle k_{\pm}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(38)

\displaystyle dk_{\pm}

\displaystyle=0,

(39)

\displaystyle k_{\pm}\cdot dr

\displaystyle=-1.

(40)

The associated null vectors

\ell_{\pm}^{a}:=h^{ab}(k_{\pm})_{b}

(41)

are geodesic and affinely parametrized,

\ell_{\pm}^{b}\nabla_{b}(\ell_{\pm})_{a}=0,

(42)

with

\ell_{\pm}^{a}\nabla_{a}r=-1.

(43)

Thus
r
serves as an affine parameter along the radial null geodesics, up to
sign.

Proof.

Equation (
37
) is immediate from (
17
). Closure
(
39
) then follows from Lemma
2
.

To prove nullness, use (
4
) and
(dr)^{2}=-F
:

(dr\pm*dr)^{2}=(dr)^{2}+(*dr)^{2}\pm 2\,dr\cdot*dr=-F+F=0,

(44)

hence
k_{\pm}^{2}=0
. Likewise,

k_{\pm}\cdot dr=\frac{1}{F}\bigl((dr)^{2}\pm*dr\cdot dr\bigr)=-\frac{F}{F}=-1.

(45)

Since
dk_{\pm}=0
,
\nabla_{a}(k_{\pm})_{b}=\nabla_{b}(k_{\pm})_{a}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\ell_{\pm}^{b}\nabla_{b}(k_{\pm})_{a}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\pm}^{b}\nabla_{a}(k_{\pm})_{b}=\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{a}(k_{\pm}^{2})=0.

(46)

Since
(\ell_{\pm})_{a}=(k_{\pm})_{a}
, this is (
42
). Finally,

\ell_{\pm}^{a}\nabla_{a}r=k_{\pm}\cdot dr=-1,

(47)

which is (
43
).
∎

Corollary 6

(Double-null orbit-space form)
.

Let

v:=T+R_{*},\qquad u:=T-R_{*},

(48)

so that

dv=k_{+},\qquad du=-\,k_{-}.

(49)

With this convention,
v
is the advanced null coordinate and
u
is the retarded null coordinate.

Then the orbit-space metric may be written equivalently as

h=F(r)\,\bigl(dT^{2}-dR_{*}^{2}\bigr)=F(r)\,du\,dv.

(50)

Proof.

From (
48
),

dT=\frac{1}{2}(dv+du),\qquad dR_{*}=\frac{1}{2}(dv-du).

(51)

Then

dT^{2}-dR_{*}^{2}=du\,dv,

(52)

which gives (
50
).
∎

V
Emergent Eddington–Finkelstein and Kerr–Schild structure

The main payoff of the exact null seed forms is that their integration gives local
Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates immediately, and with them a local
Kerr–Schild decomposition over a flat background.

Theorem 7

(From exact null seed forms to local Eddington–Finkelstein and Kerr–Schild form)
.

On any simply connected local region where
dr\neq 0
and
F\neq 0
, the exact
null seed forms of Theorem
5
integrate to local null
coordinates for which the metric takes the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein form

ds^{2}=F(r)\,dv^{2}-2\,dv\,dr-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},\qquad F(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r},

(53)

or the outgoing form

ds^{2}=F(r)\,du^{2}+2\,du\,dr-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}.

(54)

Equivalently, in the ingoing chart,

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2V\,n_{\mu}n_{\nu},\qquad V=-\frac{M}{r},\qquad n_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=dv,

(55)

with our mostly-minus signature convention, the Schwarzschild Kerr–Schild profile
appears as
V=-M/r
.
Here, the flat background is

\eta=dv^{2}-2\,dv\,dr-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}.

(56)

Hence the spacetime is locally Schwarzschild.

Proof.

Starting from (
24
), use
v=T+R_{*}
and
dr=F\,dR_{*}
:

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=F(dT^{2}-dR_{*}^{2})

\displaystyle=F(dv-dR_{*})^{2}-F\,dR_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle=F\,dv^{2}-2F\,dv\,dR_{*}

\displaystyle=F\,dv^{2}-2\,dv\,dr.

(57)

This gives (
53
). The outgoing form follows analogously from

u=T-R_{*}
.

Now define

\eta:=dv^{2}-2\,dv\,dr-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}.

(58)

Setting

t:=v-r

(59)

gives

\eta=dt^{2}-dr^{2}-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},

(60)

so
\eta
is Minkowski space in spherical coordinates. Therefore

g-\eta=\bigl(F(r)-1\bigr)\,dv^{2}=-\frac{2M}{r}\,dv^{2}.

(61)

Since
dv
is null with respect to
\eta
, this is a Kerr–Schild decomposition,

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2V\,n_{\mu}n_{\nu},\qquad V=-\frac{M}{r},\qquad n_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=dv.

(62)

The distinctive feature here is that the local Kerr–Schild form is obtained directly
from the exact seed forms, rather than being introduced only after the local
Schwarzschild geometry has already been identified by other means.
∎

VI
Collapse to the real monopole seed

The orbit-space construction of Secs.
II
–
V

reaches Schwarzschild directly. The purpose of the present section is complementary:
to record the corresponding uniqueness and converse statements internal to the
stationary optical sector itself. The first statement concerns the stationary
optical scalar system on a flat background; the second combines it with the
converse machinery of Ref.
Easson:2026xod
.

Proposition 8

(Spherical uniqueness of the stationary optical seed)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
be a connected
SO(3)
-invariant
region, with origin chosen at the center of symmetry. Let

\mathcal{R}:U\to\mathbb{C}
be a nowhere-vanishing function and define

\rho:=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}.

(63)

Assume that
\mathcal{R}
is spherically symmetric,

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(r),\qquad r:=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}},

(64)

and that it satisfies the stationary optical scalar system

(\nabla\mathcal{R})^{2}=1,\qquad\mathcal{R}\,\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}=2,

(65)

where
\nabla
and
\nabla^{2}
are the flat Euclidean gradient and Laplacian on

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then there exists
\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}
such that

\mathcal{R}=\sigma r,\qquad\rho=-\frac{\sigma}{r}.

(66)

Thus, up to the choice of null branch, the stationary optical seed collapses to the
unique real monopole.

Moreover, the reconstructed spatial congruence is

\mathbf{k}=\sigma\,\nabla r,

(67)

and the corresponding optical scalars are

\theta=\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad\omega=0.

(68)

Proof.

Because
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(r)
, the eikonal equation in
(
65
) gives

\left(\frac{d\mathcal{R}}{dr}\right)^{2}=1.

(69)

Since
d\mathcal{R}/dr
is continuous and takes values in the discrete set

\{+1,-1\}
, it is constant on each connected radial interval. Hence there exist

\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\mathcal{R}(r)=\sigma r+c.

(70)

For any radial function
f(r)
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
,

\nabla^{2}f=f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2}{r}f^{\prime}.

(71)

Applying this to (
70
) gives

\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}=\frac{2\sigma}{r}.

(72)

Hence

\mathcal{R}\,\nabla^{2}\mathcal{R}=(\sigma r+c)\frac{2\sigma}{r}=2+\frac{2\sigma c}{r}.

(73)

Imposing the second equation in (
65
) forces

c=0.

(74)

Therefore

\mathcal{R}=\sigma r,\qquad\rho=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}}=-\frac{\sigma}{r},

(75)

which proves (
66
).

Because
\mathcal{R}
is real and
(\nabla\mathcal{R})^{2}=1
, the stationary
reconstruction reduces to

\mathbf{k}=\nabla\mathcal{R}=\sigma\,\nabla r.

(76)

Finally, since
\rho=-\theta-i\omega
, one finds

\theta=\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad\omega=0.

(77)

∎

The orbit-space derivation up to this point is self-contained. The converse
statement below is conditional only on the local reconstruction formalism of
Ref.
Easson:2026xod
, which we use in the minimal form stated explicitly in
Theorem
9
.

Theorem 9

(Spherical converse in the stationary optical sector)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a nowhere-vanishing spherically symmetric solution of the
stationary optical scalar system on a connected
SO(3)
-invariant region

U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, and reconstruct the stationary optical data
and vacuum Kerr–Schild metric by the local converse of
Ref.
Easson:2026xod
. Then

\mathcal{R}=\sigma r,\qquad\rho=-\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad\mathbf{k}=\sigma\,\nabla r,\qquad\sigma\in\{+1,-1\},

(78)

and the reconstructed stationary vacuum Kerr–Schild metric is locally
Schwarzschild. Equivalently, up to the choice of null branch, the only
spherically symmetric solution of the stationary optical system reconstructs the
Schwarzschild family.

Proof.

By Proposition
8
,

\mathcal{R}=\sigma r,\qquad\rho=-\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad\mathbf{k}=\sigma\,\nabla r,\qquad\omega=0.

(79)

The converse theorem of Ref.
Easson:2026xod
then reconstructs the
stationary vacuum Kerr–Schild data with

V=m\,\Re\rho=-\frac{m\sigma}{r}

(80)

for some real constant
m
. Defining

M:=m\sigma,

(81)

one obtains

V=-\frac{M}{r}.

(82)

The associated null Kerr–Schild one-form is

n_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=dt+\sigma\,dr,

(83)

so the reconstructed metric is

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+2V\,n_{\mu}n_{\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}-\frac{2M}{r}\,n_{\mu}n_{\nu}.

(84)

Since the flat background is

\eta=dt^{2}-dr^{2}-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2},

(85)

this gives

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-dr^{2}-r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}-\frac{2M}{r}(dt+\sigma\,dr)^{2}.

(86)

For
\sigma=+1
, Eq. (
86
) is the ingoing
Eddington–Finkelstein Kerr–Schild form of Schwarzschild; for
\sigma=-1
, it is
the outgoing form. Hence the reconstructed stationary vacuum Kerr–Schild metric is
locally Schwarzschild.
∎

For the ingoing branch singled out by Theorem
7
, one has

\sigma=+1
, and therefore
\mathcal{R}=r
and
\rho=-1/r
. In this sense, the spherical vacuum sector isolates a common monopole seed whose
distinct classical dressings yield the Schwarzschild/Newton potential on the
gravitational side and the Coulomb potential on the gauge side.

Remark 10

(Locality and horizons)
.

The construction is local and applies on any region where
dr\neq 0
and

F=1-2M/r\neq 0
. At a nondegenerate horizon
F=0
, the normalized seed forms

dT
and
dR_{*}
become singular, but the corresponding ingoing and outgoing
Eddington–Finkelstein charts extend regularly across the horizon. Global extensions
are obtained by patching neighboring local regions.

VII
Discussion

We have presented a local seed-to–Kerr–Schild route to Birkhoff rigidity in
four-dimensional spherical vacuum gravity. The reduced orbit-space vacuum equations
give rise to exact null seed forms whose integration yields the local
Eddington–Finkelstein and Kerr–Schild descriptions of Schwarzschild. The stationary optical sector provides the complementary converse statement: spherical symmetry collapses the inverse optical seed to
the real monopole branches, and the associated Kerr–Schild reconstruction is
Schwarzschild. The same monopole profile admits a natural double-copy interpretation:
gravitational Kerr–Schild dressing yields Schwarzschild, while electrostatic
dressing yields Coulomb.

Several questions remain open. Beyond the spherical case, it would be
interesting to see if the present covariant null seed-form route, arising
directly from the reduced orbit-space vacuum equations, admits a comparably
direct analogue in less symmetric settings, and in particular beyond the
stationary sector in which genuinely complex seeds already occur, as in the
Kerr solution. It is also natural to ask whether the rigidity interpretation
isolated in the spherical case, which singles out the real monopole sector,
extends in a useful form to genuinely complex stationary seeds. A particularly
immediate extension concerns the
\Lambda\neq 0
case: one may ask whether the
present covariant seed-form route admits a Schwarzschild–(A)dS analogue, and
whether its unique spherical branch matches the curved-background deformation of
the optical-seed picture.
