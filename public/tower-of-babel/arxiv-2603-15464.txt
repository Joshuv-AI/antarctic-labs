Variations of Demuškin groups that are not absolute Galois groups
Simone Blumer, Claudio Quadrelli

Abstract
. We construct two families of examples of pro- p p groups, with rather elementary presentations, that do not complete into 1-cyclotomic oriented pro- p p groups. These provide brand new examples of pro- p p groups that do not occur as maximal pro- p p Galois groups of fields containing a root of unity of order p p — and thus, as absolute Galois groups. Moreover, we show that these pro- p p groups may not be ruled out as maximal pro- p p Galois groups employing other cohomological properties that are known to hold for all maximal pro- p p Galois groups, such as the triple Massey vanishing property, or the quadraticity of 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -cohomology.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Framework

Let
p
be a prime number, and, given a field
\mathbb{K}
, let
\mathbb{K}(p)
denote its
p
-closure
, i.e.,
\mathbb{K}(p)
is the compositum of all finite Galois
p
-extensions of
\mathbb{K}
.
The
maximal pro-
p
Galois group
of the field
\mathbb{K}
, denoted by
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
, is the Galois group of the Galois extension
\mathbb{K}(p)/\mathbb{K}
: it is a pro-
p
group and it is the maximal pro-
p
quotient of the
absolute Galois group
of
\mathbb{K}
.

Detecting which pro-
p
groups occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups is one of the major pursuits in current research in Galois theory (see, e.g.,
[
friedjarden
, § 25.16]
and
[
birs
, § 2.2]
).
Already the production of new concrete examples of pro-
p
groups which do not occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups is considered a remarkable achievement (see, e.g.,
[
BLMS
,
rogel
,
cq:chase
,
BQW
]
).
In recent years — especially after the proof of the Norm Residue Theorem — the research on maximal pro-
p
Galois groups focused on the investigation of their cohomology, and of associated graded algebras, and this led to the discovery of new obstructions for the realization of pro-
p
groups as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups, and to the formulation of new conjectures — see, e.g.,
[
posi
,
cq:bk
,
EM:Massey
,
MPQT
,
mt:Massey
,
HW
]
and references therein.

One of the cohomological properties that provided new obstructions for the realization of pro-
p
groups and maximal pro-
p
Galois groups is
1-cyclotomicity
.
A pro-
p
group is said to be 1-cyclotomic if there exists a continuous
G
-module
M
,
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as an abelian pro-
p
group, such that for every closed subgroup
H
of
G
, and for every
n\geq 1
, the natural map

(1.1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,M_{H}/p^{n}M_{H})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,M_{H}/pM_{H}),

— here
M_{H}
denotes the restriction of
M
as an
H
-module —,
induced by the epimorphism of
H
-modules

M_{H}/p^{n}M_{H}\to M_{H}/pM_{H}
, is surjective.
By Hilbert 90, maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of fields containing a root of unity of order
p
are 1-cyclotomic, as observed first by J. Labute (see Section
3.2
below).
In the last years this property has been put under the spotlight by several authors, see, e.g.,
[
eq:kummer
,
qw:cyc
,
IlirSlobo
,
cq:chase
,
DCF
,
MerScaH90
]
.

1.2.
Main results: new examples

The goal of this work is to produce two families of pro-
p
groups,
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
, that are “variations” of
Demuškin groups
— namely, they have very similar presentations —, which are not 1-cyclotomic.
Demuškin groups are Poincaré duality pro-
p
groups of dimension
2
— hence, they have a single defining relation: for example, if
p\neq 2
a pro-
p
group
G
is a Demuškin group if and only if it has a minimal pro-
p
presentation

(1.2)

G=\left\langle\>x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}\>\mid\>x_{1}^{q}[x_{1},y_{1}]\cdots[x_{d},y_{d}]=1\>\right\rangle_{\hat{p}}

for some
p
-power
q=p^{k}
with
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
(here we adopt the convention
p^{\infty}=0
), and
d\geq 1
, see, e.g.,
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4.5]
.
Demuškin groups were introduced by S.P. Demuškin (see
[
demushkin0
,
demushkin1
,
demushkin2
]
): these pro-
p
groups show up in Galois theory as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a
p
-adic local field containing a root of unity of order
p
is a Demuškin group (see, e.g.,
[
serre:galc
, Ch. II, § 5.6]
).
Moreover,
all
Demuškin groups — and their cohomology, and their associated graded algebras — satisfy all properties that are known, or conjectured, to hold for maximal pro-
p
Galois groups (which we will recall in §
1.3
below). In particular, all Demuškin groups are 1-cyclotomic.
Nevertheless, it is still not known whether
any
Demuškin group occurs as the maximal pro-
p
group of a field (see, e.g.,
[
etc_new
, § 11.2]
).

The family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
consists of pro-
p
groups with minimal pro-
p
presentation

(1.3)

G=\left\langle\>x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}\>\mid\>[x_{1}^{q},y_{1}]\cdots[x_{d},y_{d}]=1\>\right\rangle_{\hat{p}}

for some
d\geq 2
and some
p
-power
q=p^{k}
with
k\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and
k\geq 2
if
p=2
— hence
q\neq 0
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Every pro-
p
group
G
in the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is not 1-cyclotomic.
In particular,
G
does not occur as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field containing a root of unity of order
p

(
and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
p=2
)
.

The family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
consists of pro-
p
groups with minimal pro-
p
presentation

(1.4)

G=\left\langle\>x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}\>\mid\>x_{1}^{q}[x_{1},y_{1}]\cdots[x_{d},y_{d}]=[z_{1},z_{2}]=1\>\right\rangle_{\hat{p}}

for some
p
-power
q=p^{k}
with
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
(and
k\geq 2
if
p=2
),
d\geq 2
, and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\{x_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}
such that
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
and
\{z_{1},z_{2}\}\neq\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
for any
1\leq i\leq d
.
In other words, a pro-
p
group
G
in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is the quotient of a Demuškin group with minimal presentation (
1.2
) over the commutator of two distinct generators that are not paired in a commutator of the Demuškin defining relation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Every pro-
p
group
G
in the family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is not 1-cyclotomic.
In particular,
G
does not occur as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field containing a root of unity of order
p

(
and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
p=2
)
.

Thus, the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are genuine new examples of pro-
p
groups that do not — but are very “close” to — occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups.
We underline that, although Demuškin groups are some of the (extremely few) examples of pro-
p
groups which are known to be 1-cyclotomic, the presence of a further, elementary and apparently harmless “variation” (in particular in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
) is enough to lose 1-cyclotomicity.

1.3.
Galois-like properties

Next, we study the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
, focusing on several important theorems and conjectures on maximal pro-
p
Galois groups: the
Norm Residue Theorem
, the
Massey Vanishing Conjecture
, and the
Koszulity Conjectures
, which we briefly recall below.

(1)
The
Norm Residue Theorem
(proved by M. Rost and V. Voevodsky, with a “patch” by Ch. Weibel, for an overview see, e.g.,
[
HW:book
]
) implies that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra

\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{n}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p}),

with
\mathbb{K}
a field containing a root of unity of order
p
, endowed with the cup-product, is a
quadratic

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra (see Section
2.2
below): i.e., it is generated in degree 1, and its defining relations are generated in degree 2 (see, e.g.,
[
qsv:quadratic
]
and references therein).

(2)
For
n\geq 2
and
G
a pro-
p
group, the
n
-fold Massey product
is a multi-valued product which associates a sequence of elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
to a subset

\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})

(see Section
2.3
below).
E. Matzri proved that if
G=G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
with
\mathbb{K}
a field containing a root of unity of order
p
, then every 3-fold Massey product contains 0 (see
[
eli:Massey
,
EM:Massey
]
), and it was conjectured by J. Minač and N.D. Tân that every
n
-fold Massey product contains 0 for
n\geq 3
(see
[
mt:conj
]
).

For an overview on Massey products in Galois cohomology see
[
mt:Massey
]
.

(3)

Koszul algebras
are a particular kind of quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras: a graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
, with
A_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, is Koszul if the trivial
A
-module
M=\mathbb{F}_{p}
admits a linear free resolution, that is an exact sequence

(1.5)

\dots\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0,

where
F_{i}
is a free
A
-module generated by elements of degree
i
. Koszul algebras boast a very nice behavior — e.g., a “small” projective resolution may easily be computed, and they are completely determined by their
\ext
-algebra
\ext_{A}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

It was conjectured by L. Positselski that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
, with
\mathbb{K}
a field containing a root of unity of order
p
, is Koszul; and later, it was conjectured by J.Minač et al. that such an algebra satisfies even a stronger condition, called
universal Koszulity
. An
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A
is universally Koszul if there exists a linear free resolution as in (
1.5
), where
M
is any quotient
A/I
, where
I
is a (left) ideal of
A
generated in degree
1
.

Moreover, a strengthening of Positselki’s conjecture was proposed by Th. Weigel
[
thomas:koszul
]
who predicted that also the graded group
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra

\mathrm{gr}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)]\!]=\coprod_{n\geq 0}I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1},

induced by the augmentation ideal
I_{G}\lhd\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)]\!]
(see Section
2.4
below), is quadratic, Koszul, and it is the
quadratic dual
of
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

For an overview of Koszulity in Galois cohomology see
[
posi
,
MPQT
,
expo
,
MPPT
]
.

We prove that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
matches the aforementioned theorems and conjectures.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group lying in the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
or in the family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.

(1)

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic.

(2.a)

If
G\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
n\leq q
, then
G
satisfies a strong variant of the
n
-fold Massey vanishing property.

(2.b)

If
G\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
, then
G
satisfies the
3
-fold and the 4-fold Massey vanishing properties.

(3)

The algebras
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathrm{gr}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
are Koszul, and they are quadratic dual to each other. Moreover, the former is universally Koszul.

A key point for the proof of Proposition
1.3
–(3) is the fact that the pro-
p
group in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
are
mild
.
Mild pro-
p
groups were introduced by J.P. Labute in
[
labute:mild
]
to study the Galois groups of pro-
p
extensions of number fields with restricted ramification — for an overview on mild pro-
p
groups see also
[
Jochen
, § 2–3]
,
[
qsv:quadratic
, § 2.5]
and
[
expo
, § 4]
.

Altogether, the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
“behave” pretty much like maximal pro-
p
Galois groups, apart from 1-cyclotomicity.
This suggests that 1-cyclotomicity provides a rather refined and powerful tool to study maximal pro-
p
Galois groups, and it succeeds in detecting pro-
p
groups which do not occur as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups when other methods fail.

1.4.
Graded restricted Lie algebras

Finally, we focus on the graded restricted Lie algebras
\mathrm{gr}\>G
, with
G\in\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
, induced by the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration of
G
, which may be seen as “linearizations” of the pro-
p
groups
G
.
On the other hand, they are interesting objects on their own.
Since the pro-
p
groups in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
are mild, the associated graded restricted Lie algebras may be described easily.
Explicitly, for
G
in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, respectively
G
in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\mathrm{gr}\>G
has presentation

\mathrm{gr}\,G=\begin{cases}\langle\>X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d}\>\mid\>[X_{2},Y_{2}]+\ldots+[X_{d},Y_{d}]=0\>\rangle,\\
\langle\>X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d}\>\mid\>[X_{1},Y_{1}]+\ldots+[X_{d},Y_{d}]=[Z_{1},Z_{2}]=0\>\rangle\end{cases}

respectively — in the latter
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\{X_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}\}
and
\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}\neq\{X_{i},Y_{i}\}
for any
1\leq i\leq d
, in accordance with
z_{1},z_{2}\in G
.

The notion of Koszul algebra may be transposed to the setting of graded restricted Lie algebras (see
[
simone:koszul
, §1.3]
and §
2.4
below).
For
G
in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
or in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
the associated restricted Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}\>G
satisfies a stronger version of Koszulity, as stated by the following.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group lying in the family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
or in the family
\mathcal{F}_{2}
.
If
p\neq 2
the restricted Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}\,G
is Bloch-Kato, namely, every subalgebra of
\mathrm{gr}\,G
generated in degree 1 is Koszul.

Proposition
1.3
–(1) raises a question inspired also by Proposition
1.4
.
A pro-
p
group is said to be
Bloch-Kato
if the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of every closed subgroup is quadratic (see
[
cq:bk
]
and Section
2.2
below) — so, Bloch-Kato-ness is a “hereditary” property, like 1-cyclotomicity.
Proposition
1.3
–(1) states that
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic for
G
in
\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}
, but it says nothing about the subgroups of
G
.
Hence, similarly to Proposition
1.4
, it is natural to ask the following.

Question 1.5
.

Are the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
Bloch-Kato pro-
p
groups?

In order to prove Theorems
1.1
–
1.2
we studied some open subgroups of the pro-
p
groups in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
, which turned out to have quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology — see Remarks
4.3
–
5.5
–
5.8
—, so answering Question
1.5
will require a more thorough investigation.

Although all known examples indicate a close connection between the Bloch–Kato property of a pro-p group and that of its associated Lie algebra, it remains open whether a pro-p group with quadratic cohomology and Bloch–Kato associated Lie algebra is necessarily Bloch–Kato.

1.5.
An open question

We conclude with a question on another variation of Demuškin groups, which has not been studied in this work.
Consider the pro-
p
group
G
with minimal presentation

(1.6)

G=\langle\>x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}\>\mid\>[x_{1},y_{1}]=[x_{2},y_{2}]=\ldots=[x_{d},y_{d}]\>\rangle_{\hat{p}},

with
d\geq 3
.
Namely,
G
is the amalgamated free pro-
p
product

G=G_{1}\amalg_{H_{1}}G_{2}\amalg_{H_{2}}\cdots\amalg_{H_{d-2}}G_{d-1}

of
d-1
isomorphic Demuškin groups on four generators

G_{i}=\langle\>x_{i},y_{i},x_{i+1},y_{i+1}\>\mid\>[x_{i},y_{i}]=[x_{i+1},y_{i+1}]\>\rangle_{\hat{p}}\qquad i=1,\ldots,d-1

with amalgams the intersection
H_{i}=G_{i}\cap G_{i+1}=\langle x_{i+1},y_{i+1}\rangle
.

Question 1.6
.

Is the pro-
p
group
G
in (
1.6
)
1-cyclotomic?

We suspect that the above question has negative answer, like for the pro-
p
groups in
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
, and thus it does not occur as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of a field containing a root of unity of order
p
.

Last, but not least, we deem the pro-
p
groups in the families
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
good showcases, useful to display the techniques that may be employed to study, in a concrete way, some cohomological features of pro-
p
groups relevant from a Galois-theoretic point of view, such as 1-cyclotomicity and Massey products.
Also for this reason, we hope that our work may be appreciated — in particular — by students and young researchers who are approaching Galois cohomology of pro-
p
groups.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Henceforth, every subgroup of a pro-
p
group will be tacitly assumed to be closed with respect to the pro-
p
topology, and sets of generators, and presentations, of pro-
p
groups will be intended in the topological sense.
Given two elements
x,y\in G
of a pro-
p
group
G
, we adopt the notation

y^{x}=x^{-1}\cdot y\cdot x\qquad\text{and}\qquad[x,y]=(y^{-1})^{x}\cdot y=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy.

Given a positive integer
n
, one has the normal subgroups

G^{p^{n}}=\left\langle\>x^{p^{n}}\>\mid\>x\in G\>\right\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad G^{\prime}=\left\langle\>[x,y]\>\mid\>x,y\in G\>\right\rangle.

More in general, if
H_{1},H_{2}
are subgroups of
G
,
[H_{1},H_{2}]
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by the elements
[x,y]
where
x\in H_{1}
and
y\in H_{2}
.
Finally, the Frattini subgroup of
G
is
\Phi(G)=G^{p}\cdot G^{\prime}
.

Later on, we will make use of the following fact.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a subgroup of
G
.
Then
\mathcal{X}
is a minimal generating set of
G
if and only if
\bar{\mathcal{X}}=\{xG^{\prime}\mid x\in\mathcal{X}\}
is a minimal generating set of the abelianization
G^{\ab}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

2.2.
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of pro-
p
groups

For an account on
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of pro-
p
groups, we refer to
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4]
and
[
nsw:cohn
, Ch. III, § 9]
.

Given a pro-
p
group
G
, consider the abelian group
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as a trivial
G
-module.
For
n\geq 0
, we write simply
\mathrm{H}^{n}(G)
for the
n
th cohomology group of
G
with coefficients in the trivial
G
-module
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We recall that for a finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
one has the following:

(a)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)=\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and there is an isomorphism of finite
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)\simeq(G/\Phi(G))^{\ast},

where
\textvisiblespace^{\ast}
denotes the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dual — in particular,
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G))
is the cardinality of a minimal generating set of
G
— (cf., e.g.,
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4.2]
);

(b)

\dim(\mathrm{H}^{2}(G))
is the cardinality of a minimal set of defining relations of
G
(cf., e.g.,
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4.3]
).

In particular, if
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
is a minimal generating set of a finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
, then
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
has a basis
\{x_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,x_{d}^{\ast}\}
, where
x_{i}^{\ast}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
, and moreover
\alpha=\alpha(x_{1})x_{1}^{\ast}+\ldots+\alpha(x_{d})x_{d}^{\ast}
for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space

\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{n}(G),

endowed with the graded-commutative cup-product, is a graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra (for the definition and the properties of the cup-product see, e.g.,
[
nsw:cohn
, Ch. I, § 4–6]
).
Recall that
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
is said to be quadratic if the homomorphism of graded algebras

(2.1)

\mu\colon\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G),

which extends linearly
\alpha_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{m}\mapsto\alpha_{1}\smallsmile\cdots\smallsmile\alpha_{m}
,
is surjective, and its kernel is generated as a graded ideal by a subspace of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)^{\otimes 2}

(cf., e.g.,
[
qsv:quadratic
, § 2.1]
).

We will make use of the following proposition, which recaps the procedure to sort out the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology ring of a pro-
p
group with at most two defining relations, from the shape of these relations (cf.
[
cq:onerel
, Prop. 4.2]
and
[
cq:2relUK
, Exam. 2.7–2.8 and § 3.2–3.3]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group with

\dim(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G))=d\geq 2\qquad\text{and}\qquad 1\leq\dim(\mathrm{H}^{2}(G))\leq 2.

Suppose that, for some ordered minimal generating set
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
of

\begin{split}1=&\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left[x_{i},x_{j}\right]^{a_{ij}}\cdot r_{1}^{\prime}\qquad\text{with }0\leq a_{ij}\leq p-1\\
1=&\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left[x_{i},x_{j}\right]^{b_{ij}}\cdot r_{2}^{\prime}\qquad\text{with }0\leq b_{ij}\leq p-1\end{split}

— the latter occurring if
\dim(\mathrm{H}^{2}(G))=2
—, with
a_{1,2}\neq 0
,
b_{1,2}=0
, and
b_{hk}\neq 0
for at least a pair
(h,k)\neq(1,2)

r_{1}^{\prime},r_{2}^{\prime}\in\begin{cases}G^{p}\cdot[G^{\prime},G],&\text{if }p\neq 2,\\
G^{4}\cdot(G^{\prime})^{2}\cdot[G^{\prime},G],&\text{if }p=2.\end{cases}

Then one has the following:

(a)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
has a basis
\{x_{1}^{\ast}\ldots,x_{d}^{\ast}\}
such that

\mathcal{B}=\left\{\>x_{1}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{2}^{\ast},\text{ and }x_{h}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{k}^{\ast}\text{ if }\dim(\mathrm{H}^{2}(G))=2\text{ and }b_{hk}\neq 0\right\}

is a basis of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G)
, and one has

x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{j}^{\ast}=\frac{a_{ij}}{a_{1,2}}\left(x_{1}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{2}^{\ast}\right)+\frac{b_{ij}}{b_{hk}}\left(x_{h}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{k}^{\ast}\right)\qquad\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq d;

(b)

\cd(G)=2
— i.e.,
\mathrm{H}^{n}(G)=0
for
n\geq 3
—, and
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
is a universally Koszul (hence quadratic)
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra.

Remark 2.3
.

The pro-
p
groups in families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition
2.2
, as we shall see in §

and §

(with a single defining relation).

Recall from the introduction that a pro-

group

is said to be a Bloch-Kato pro-

-cohomology algebra

By the Norm Residue Theorem, proved by V. Voevodsky and M. Rost (with the contribution of Ch. Weibel), if

[

).

The following are well-known to be Bloch-Kato pro-

Example 2.4
.

(a)

A free pro-

). Indeed, a free pro-

(b)

2.3.
Massey products in Galois cohomology

For the formal definition and properties of Massey products in the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of pro-
p
groups, we direct the reader to
[
vogel
,
mt:Massey
,
cq:massey
]
and references therein.
Here we recall the few things needed to prove Proposition
1.3
–(2).

Given a pro-
p
group
G
, consider a sequence
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of length
n\geq 2
of (non-necessarily distinct) elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
.
The associated
n
-fold Massey product, which is a subset of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G)
, is denoted by
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
.
The
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is said
to be
defined
, if
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle\neq\varnothing
, and to
vanish
if
0\in\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
.
Finally,
G
is said to satisfy the
n
-fold Massey vanishing property
if every
n
-fold Massey product vanishes whenever it is defined.

Massey products enjoy the following properties (cf., e.g.,
[
vogel
, Prop. 1.2.3–1.2.4]
and
[
mt:Massey
, Rem. 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group, and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
a sequence of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
of length
n
, for
n\geq 3
.

(a)

If
\alpha_{i}=0
for some
i
, then the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes.

(b)

If the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is defined, then
\alpha_{1}\smallsmile\alpha^{\prime},\alpha_{n}\smallsmile\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle

for every
\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
.

(c)

If the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
is defined, then

(2.2)

\alpha_{1}\smallsmile\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{2}\smallsmile\alpha_{3}=\ldots=\alpha_{n-1}\smallsmile\alpha_{n}=0

If the
n
-fold Massey product
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes whenever the associated sequence of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
of length
n
satisfies (
2.2
), then
G
satisfies the
strong

n
-fold Massey vanishing property (cf., e.g.,
[
pal:Massey
, Def. 1.2]
or
[
cq:massey
, Def. 2.4]
).

Example 2.6
.

(a)

If
G
is a Demuškin group,
G
satisfies the strong
n
-fold Massey vanishing property for every
n\geq 3
(cf., e.g.,
[
pal:Massey
, Thm. 3.5]
).

(b)

If
\mathbb{K}
is a field containing a root of unity of order
p
, then
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
satisfies the
3
-fold Massey vanishing property (cf.
[
eli:Massey
,

,

]
), and it is conjectured to satisfy the

-fold Massey vanishing property for every

(cf.
[

]
).
It has been proved that this conjecture holds in several relevant cases (cf., e.g.,
[
mt:Massey
,
HW
,
palquick:Massey
,
cq:massey
]
).

Now let
n
be a positive integer.
For
1\leq i,j\leq n
let
E_{ij}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
denote the matrix with every entry equal to 0 but the
(i,j)
-entry, equal to 1, and let
I_{n}
denote the identity matrix.
Let

\mathbb{U}_{n+1}=\left\{\>I_{n}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}E_{ij}\mid a_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\right\}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

be the (multiplicative)
p
-group of upper unitriangular matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
The center of
\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
is
\Zen(\mathbb{U}_{n+1})=\{I_{n}+aE_{1,n}\>\mid\>a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
We put
\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}=\mathbb{U}_{n+1}/\Zen(\mathbb{U}_{n+1})
.

Now consider a pro-
p
group
G
, and let
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
be a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups.
For every
i=1,\ldots,n
the
(i,i+1)
-entry of
\rho
, denoted by
\rho_{i,i+1}
, is a homomorphism

, and thus it may be considered as an element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
.
Analogously, if
n\geq 2
and
\bar{\rho}\colon G\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
is a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups, then for every
i=1,\ldots,n
the
(i,i+1)
-entry of
\bar{\rho}
, denoted by
\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}
, is a homomorphism
G\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and thus it may be considered as an element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
as well.
The following is the pro-
p
translation of W. Dwyer’s interpretation (cf.
[
dwyer
]
) of Massey products in terms of upper unitriangular representations (see, e.g.,
[

, § 8]
or
[

, Lemma 9.3]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group, and let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
a sequence of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
of length
n
, for
n\geq 2
.

(a)

If there exists a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups
\bar{\rho}\colon G\to\bar{\mathbb{U}}_{n+1}
satisfying
\bar{\rho}_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for every

, then

is defined.

(b)

If there exists a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
satisfying
\rho_{i,i+1}=\alpha_{i}
for every

, then
\langle\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\rangle
vanishes.

We will make use of the following well-known property of upper unitriangular matrices.

Fact 2.8
.

Let
A=(a_{ij}),B=(b_{ij})\in\mathbb{U}_{n}
such that
a_{ij}=0
for if

, and

if

, for some

Then

.

2.4.
Graded group algebras and associated Lie algebras

For an introduction on restricted Lie algebras, we refer the reader to the original paper of Jacobson
[
jacobson
]
on this topic. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a restricted Lie
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with
p
-operation
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, then its restricted universal envelope, denoted
u(\mathfrak{g})
, is the quotient of the associative algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}):=T^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})/(X\otimes Y-Y\otimes X-[X,Y]:X,Y\in\mathfrak{g})
by the ideal generated by the elements
X^{[p]}-X^{\otimes p}
, for
X
belonging to the image of
\mathfrak{g}
into
U(\mathfrak{g})
.

Given a pro-
p
group
G
, denote by
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
the complete group algebra
\invlim_{U\lhd_{o}G}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G/U]
, and let
I_{G}
be its augmentation ideal. The graded group algebra is the graded algebra associated with the
I_{G}
-adic filtration of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, that is,

\gr\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![{G}]\!]:=\coprod_{n\geq 0}I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1}.

By a well known result of S.A. Jennings, the subgroups
G_{n}:=\left\{{g\in G}\ \big|\ {g-1\in I_{G}^{n}}\right\}
coincide with the subgroups of the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration of
G
. It follows that the graded group algebra
\gr\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![{G}]\!]
is the restricted universal envelope of the restricted Lie algebra

\gr G:=\coprod_{n\geq 1}G_{n}/G_{n+1}

(cf., e.g.,
[
ddsms
, Ch. 12]
, see also
[
expo
, § 3]
).

Usually, describing the restricted Lie algebra
\gr G
associated to a pro-
p
group
G
, and its restricted universal envelope
\gr\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![{G}]\!]
, is a challenging task.
By Labute’s work
[
labute:mild
]
, if
G
is mild, then it admits a presentation — where the relations satisfy a condition of being independent in a maximal possible way — from which a presentation
of the associated restricted Lie algebra may be deduced directly, cf.
[
labute:mild
, Thm. 1.2–(a)]
.
For instance, infinite Demuškin pro-
p
groups are mild. Moreover, Proposition
2.2
and
[
expo
, Prop. 1.4]
imply the following.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group in one of the families
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then
G
is mild.

A positively graded restricted Lie
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
quadratic
(resp.
Koszul
) if so is its restricted envelope
u(\mathfrak{g})
with respect to the induced grading, that is,

\ext^{i,j}_{u(\mathfrak{g})}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})=0\quad\text{for $i,j\in\{1,2\}$, $i\neq j$ (resp. $\forall i\neq j$)}.

We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
Bloch-Kato
(resp.
weakly Bloch-Kato
) if all of its restricted subalgebras generated in degree one are Koszul (resp. quadratic).
If
p
is an odd prime, then
[
simone:koszul
, Cor. 2.3]
shows that weakly Bloch-Kato restricted Lie algebras of characteristic
p
are automatically Bloch-Kato.

Moreover, one has the following (cf.
[
simone:kurosh
, Thm. A]
)

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a positively graded restricted Lie algebra generated in degree one. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is Bloch-Kato if and only if the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is universally Koszul.

In particular, the Lie theoretic version of Minač’s universal Koszulity conjecture holds true.

As an example, if
p
is an odd prime and
G
is a Demuškin group as in (
1.2
), then the associated restricted Lie algebra admits presentation (see
[
labute:onerel
]
)

\gr G=\left\langle\>{x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{d},y_{d}}\>\mid\>{\sum_{i=1}^{d}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\>\right\rangle

and is Bloch-Kato.

Indeed, all the proper subalgebras generated in degree one are free restricted Lie algebras (see
[
simone:kurosh
, §1.4]
for the ordinary case and
[
simone:koszul
, §1.3, §4.1]
for restricted Lie algebras). We call
\gr G
the Demuškin Lie algebra of rank
2d
; if
d=1
, then the Lie algebra is a free-abelian restricted Lie algebra.

These Lie algebras play a special role in the theory of Bloch-Kato Lie algebras, because any Bloch-Kato restricted Lie algebra that is neither free nor abelian contains a Demuškin Lie algebra of positive rank as a subalgebra generated in degree one (
[
simone:koszul
, §4]
).
The group theoretic counterpart of this phenomenon is only conjectural, and part of I. Efrat’s Elementary Type Conjecture on maximal pro-
p
Galois groups (see, e.g.,
[
etc_new
]
).

Quadratic restricted Lie algebras with a single relation are free products of a free restricted Lie algebra and a Demuškin Lie algebra, and hence they are all Bloch-Kato. Actually, the class of quadratic Lie algebras which are automatically Bloch-Kato is much bigger, as the following result shows.
Most Lie algebras considered in the present paper are instances of this class.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quadratic restricted Lie algebra with at most two minimal relations, that is
\dim_{\,\mathbb{F}_{p}}\ext^{2}_{u(\mathfrak{g})}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\leq 2
.
Then,
\mathfrak{g}
is Bloch-Kato.

A proof of this result can be found in
[
simone:koszul
, Thm. 4.3]
, where the first named author applies techniques developed by the second named author in
[
cq:2relUK
]
.

The cohomology of a pro-
p
group can be approximated via a spectral sequence discovered by J.P. May
[
may:SS
]
involving the restricted cohomology of the associated graded restricted Lie algebra, that is,

E_{1}^{i,j}=\ext^{i+j,j}_{u(\gr G)}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\implies\mathrm{H}^{i+j}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
G
be a pro-
p
group whose associated restricted Lie algebra is Koszul. Then, the natural map
\hom(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\hom_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G/\Phi(G),\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\hom_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\gr_{1}G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
induces an isomorphism
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\ext^{\bullet}_{u(\gr G)}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. In particular,
G
is an
\mathrm{H}
-Koszul pro-
p
group.

Proof.

The differential of the
r
th page of May’s spectral sequence is

d_{r}^{i,j}:\ext_{u(\gr G)}^{i+j,j}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to\ext_{u(\gr G)}^{i+j+1,j-r+1}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Since
\gr G
is Koszul, for all
r\geq 1
, either the source or the target of
d_{r}
are zero, and hence
d_{r}=0
, that is, the spectral sequence collapses at the first page
E_{1}=E_{\infty}
. Finally, Theorem 7.1 of
[
pp:quadrAlg
]
implies that
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
is Koszul, and hence isomorphic to
\ext_{u(\mathfrak{g})}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.
∎

3.
1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
groups

3.1.
Orientations of pro-
p
groups

Let

1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\{\>1+p\lambda\>\mid\>\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\>\}

denote the multiplicative group of principal units of the ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Given a pro-
p
group
G
, a homomorphism of pro-
p
groups
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is called an orientation of
G
.
Analogously, a pair
(G,\theta)
, with
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
an orientation, is called an oriented pro-
p
group.

An oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
gives rise to the continuous
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as an abelian pro-
p
group, and with
G
-action

x.\lambda=\theta(x)\cdot\lambda\qquad\text{for every }x\in G\text{ and }\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta).

Since the action of
G
on the quotient
G
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)
is trivial, we may identify
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)=\mathbb{F}_{p}
as trivial
G
-modules.

Conversely, if a pro-
p
group
G
comes endowed with a continuous
G
module
M
, isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as an abelian pro-
p
group, then the action gives rise to an orientation
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
x.v=\theta(x)v
for every
x\in G
and
v\in M
.
Therefore, one may reformulate the definition of 1-cyclotomicity as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

An oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
is said to be Kummerian if the natural map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(G),

induced in cohomology by the epimorphism of continuous
G
-modules

\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 36.16516pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-36.16516pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 60.16516pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern-3.0pt\lower 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 60.16516pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)/p\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta)=\mathbb{F}_{p}}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces,

is surjective.
Moreover,
(G,\theta)
is said to be 1-cyclotomic if
(H,\theta|_{H})
is a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group for every subgroup
H
of
G
.

3.2.
Maximal pro-
p
Galois group

Oriented pro-
p
groups arise naturally in Galois theory.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a root of unity of order
p
, and let
\mu_{p^{\infty}}\subseteq\mathbb{K}(p)^{\times}
denote the group of the roots of 1 of order a power of
p
lying in the maximal pro-
p
Galois extension
\mathbb{K}(p)
of
\mathbb{K}
.
The action of the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
on
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
induces the pro-
p
cyclotomic character

\theta_{\mathbb{K}}\colon G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)\longrightarrow 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p},

satisfying
g(\zeta)=\zeta^{\theta_{\mathbb{K}}(g)}
for all
g\in G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
and
\zeta\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}
.
Then
\Img(\theta_{\mathbb{K}})=1+p^{f}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, where
f\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is maximal such that

\mathbb{K}
contains a root of unity of order
p^{f}
(if
f=\infty
, we set
p^{\infty}=0
).
Then
\theta_{\mathbb{K}}
is a torsion-free orientation if
p\neq 2
or if
p=2
and
\sqrt{-1}\in\mathbb{K}
.

The
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
-module
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta_{\mathbb{K}})
coincides with the 1st Tate twist
\mathbb{Z}_{p}(1)
(cf.
[
nsw:cohn
, Def. 7.3.6]
), and moreover for every
n\geq 1
one has the equality of continuous
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
-modules

\mathbb{Z}_{p}(\theta_{\mathbb{K}})/p^{n}=\left\{\>\zeta\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}\>\mid\>\zeta^{p^{n}}=1\>\right\}.

By Kummer theory, the oriented pro-
p
group
(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\theta_{\mathbb{K}})
is Kummerian — as shown
ante litteram
by J.P. Labute, see
[
labute:demushkin
, p. 131]
.

Observe that if
H
is a subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
, then
H=G_{\mathbb{L}}(p)
for some algebraic pro-
p
extension
\mathbb{L}/K
(and thus clearly
\mathbb{L}
contains the roots of 1 of order
p
), and moreover the pro-
p
cyclotomic character
\theta_{\mathbb{L}}\colon H\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the restriction of
\theta_{\mathbb{K}}
to
H
, so that
(H,\theta_{\mathbb{K}}|_{H})
is Kummerian, and thus
(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\theta_{\mathbb{K}})
is 1-cyclotomic.

3.3.
Detecting 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
groups

An oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
has a distinguished normal subgroup

K_{\theta}(G)=\left\langle\>y^{x}\cdot y^{-\theta(x)^{-1}}\>\mid\>x\in G,y\in\Ker(\theta)\>\right\rangle

(cf., e.g.,
[
eq:kummer
, § 3]
or
[
qw:bogomolov
, p. 2]
).
We remark the following:

(a)

K_{\theta}(G)\subseteq\Ker(\theta)
;

(b)

moreover,
K_{\theta}(G)\supseteq\Ker(\theta)^{\prime}
, so that
\Ker(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
is abelian;

(c)

finally, if
\theta=\mathbf{1}_{G}
— i.e.,
\theta
is constantly equal to 1 — then
K_{\theta}(G)
is the derived subgroup
G^{\prime}
.

One has the following group-theoretic characterization of Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group (cf.
[
eq:kummer
, Thm. 5.6]
, see also
[
qw:bogomolov
, Prop. 2.6]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a finitely generated oriented pro-
p
group, and suppose that
\Img(\theta)\subseteq 1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
p=2
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

(G,\theta)
is Kummerian;

(b)

G/K_{\theta}(G)
is a torsion-free pro-
p
group;

(c)

\Ker(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
is a free abelian pro-
p
group;

(d)

there is a minimal presentation
\pi\colon F\twoheadrightarrow G
— i.e.,
F
is a free pro-
p
group and
\Ker(\pi)\subseteq\Phi(F)
— such that

\Ker(\pi)\subseteq K_{\theta\circ\pi}(F).

In particular, if
\theta
is constantly equal to 1, then
(G,\theta)
is 1-cyclotomic if and only if
H^{\ab}
is a free abelian pro-
p
group for any closed subgroup
H
of
G
.
Pro-
p
groups with this property are called
absolutely torsion-free pro-
p
groups
, and were introduced by T. Würfel in
[
wurfel
]
.
Therefore, it is straightforward to deduce the following (cf., e.g.,
[
cq:galfeat
, Rem. 2.3]
)

Example 3.3
.

An oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta)
, with
G
a free abelian pro-
p
group, is 1-cyclotomic if and only if
\theta
is constantly equal to 1.

3.4.
Demuškin pro-
p
groups and their canonical orientation

J.P. Labute showed in his PhD thesis that every Demuškin group
G
comes endowed with a canonical orientation
\theta_{G}\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that the associated oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta_{G})
is Kummerian, and this is the only one completing
G
into a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group (cf.
[
labute:demushkin
, Thm. 4]
).
In particular, if
G
has a presentation (
1.2
), then

\theta_{G}(y_{1})=(1-q)^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{G}(x_{1})=\ldots=\theta_{G}(x_{d})=\theta_{G}(y_{2})=\ldots=\theta_{G}(y_{d})=1.

Hence, a Demuškin group is determined — up to isomorphism — by the image of its canonical orientation together with the number of generators (cf.
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4.5, Rem. 2.a]
).

Moreover, one may show that the oriented pro-
p
group
(G,\theta_{G})
is also 1-cyclotomic (cf.
[
qw:cyc
, Thm. 5.5]
).
Altogether, one has the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a Demuškin group.
The canonical orientation
\theta_{G}\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the only orientation completing
G
into a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
group.

4.
The family
\mathcal{F}_{1}

In this section we focus on the first family of pro-
p
groups
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, and
G
will denote a pro-
p
group with presentation

G=\left\langle\>x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{d}\>\mid\>\left[x_{1}^{q},y_{1}\right]\cdots[x_{d},y_{d}]=1\>\right\rangle

for some

4.1.
Cohomology of
G

As underlined in Remark
2.3
,

satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
2.2
, considering
\{x_{2},y_{2},x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}
as an ordered minimal generating set.
In particular,
\{x_{1}^{\ast},\ldots,y_{d}^{\ast}\}
is a basis of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
and
\{x_{2}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{2}^{\ast}\}
a basis of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G)

x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{i}^{\ast}=x_{2}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{2}^{\ast}\qquad\text{for all }i=3,\ldots,d,

while

\begin{split}&x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{j}^{\ast}=y_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{j}^{\ast}=0\qquad\text{for all }1\leq i<j\leq d,\\
&x_{1}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{1}^{\ast}=x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{j}^{\ast}=0\qquad\text{for all }i\neq j.\end{split}

Also, the Euler characteristic of

is

E(G)=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\dim\left(\mathrm{H}^{n}(G)\right)=1-2d+1=2(1-d).

Remark 4.1
.

Since
\cd(G)=2
, also
\cd(U)=2
for every open subgroup
U
of
G
(cf.
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 3.3, Prop. 14]

Therefore, in order to check whether
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(U)
is quadratic (and thus, whether

is Bloch-Kato), it suffices to study
\mathrm{H}^{2}(U)
, and hence the defining relations of
U
.

4.2.
The only candidate orientation

Let
H
be the subgroup of
G
generated by
z=x_{1}^{q}
, and by
y_{1},x_{2},\ldots,y_{d}
.
Then
H
is the Demuškin group

H=\langle\>z,y_{1},x_{2},\ldots,y_{d}\>\mid\>[z,y_{1}][x_{2},y_{2}]\cdots[x_{d},y_{d}]=1\>\rangle.

Suppose that
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is an orientation such that
(G,\theta)
is a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
group.
Then, also
(H,\theta|_{H})
is a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
group, and hence necessarily

\theta(z)=\theta(y_{1})=\ldots=\theta(y_{d})=1

(cf. §
3.4
).
Since
z=x_{1}^{q}
, one deduces that
\theta(x_{1})^{q}=1
, and since
1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
if
p\neq 2
, and
1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
p=2
, are torsion-free abelian pro-
p
groups, one deduces that
\theta(x_{1})=1
.

Altogether, the orientation
\theta
must be constantly equal to 1, and therefore

must be absolutely torsion-free (cf. §
3.3
).

4.3.
A subgroup of index
p
and its abelianization

Let
\phi\colon G\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the homomorphism defined by
\phi(y_{1})=1
and

\phi(x_{1})=\phi(x_{2})=\ldots=\phi(x_{d})=\phi(y_{d})=0.

Then
U:=\ker\phi
is a normal subgroup of
G
of index
p
.
Now put
u=y_{1}^{p}
, and
z_{h}=x_{1}^{y_{1}^{h}}
,
x_{i}(h)=x_{i}^{y_{1}^{h}}
, and
y_{i}(h)=y_{i}^{y_{1}^{h}}
for
h=0,\ldots,p-1
;
i=2,\ldots,d
.
Since
G/U=\{y_{1}^{h}U\>\mid\>h=0,\ldots,p-1\}
, the set

\mathcal{X}_{U}=\left\{\>u,z_{h},x_{i}(h),y_{i}(h)\>\mid\>h=0,\ldots,p-1;\>i=2,\ldots,d\>\right\}

generates
U
as a pro-
p
group.
More precisely,
U
is the pro-
p
group generated by
\mathcal{X}_{U}
and subject to the
p
relations obtained conjugating the defining relation (
1.3
) by
y_{1}^{h}
for all
h=0,\ldots,p-1
.
Applying the equalities
[x_{1}^{q},y_{1}]=z_{0}^{-q}\cdot z_{1}^{q}
and

[x_{1}^{q},y_{1}]^{y_{1}^{p-1}}=z_{p-1}^{-q}\cdot y_{1}^{-p}\cdot x_{1}^{q}\cdot y_{1}^{p}=z_{p-1}^{-q}\cdot z_{0}^{q}\cdot[z_{0}^{q},u],

one obtains the
p
relations

(4.1)

\begin{split}&z_{0}^{-q}z_{1}^{q}[x_{2}(0),y_{2}(0)]\cdots[x_{d}(0),y_{d}(0)]=1\\
&z_{1}^{-q}z_{2}^{q}[x_{2}(1),y_{2}(1)]\cdots[x_{d}(1),y_{d}(1)]=1\\
&\vdots\\
&z_{p-1}^{-q}z_{0}^{q}[z_{0}^{q},u][x_{2}(p-1),y_{2}(p-1)]\cdots[x_{d}(p-1),y_{d}(p-1)]=1.\end{split}

By Remark
4.1
, one has also
\mathrm{H}^{n}(U)=0
for
n\geq 3
.
Observe that

E(U)=1-|\mathcal{X}_{U}|+p=1-(1+p+2p(d-1))+p=p(2-2d),

and indeed
E(U)=p\cdot E(G)=[G:U]\cdot E(G)

(cf.
[
serre:galc
, Ch. I, § 4.1, Exer. (b)]
).
Altogether, one has the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

The pro-
p
group
G
is not absolutely torsion-free.

Proof.

Consider the open subgroup
U
of
G
, and for any element
w\in\mathcal{X}_{U}
, let
\bar{w}=wU^{\prime}
denote the corresponding coset in
U^{\ab}
.
Then
U^{\ab}
is the abelian pro-
p
group generated by

\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{U}=\left\{\>\bar{u},\bar{z}_{h},\overline{x_{i}(h)},\overline{y_{i}(h)}\>\mid\>h=0,\ldots,p-1,\>i=2,\ldots,d\>\right\},

and, by (
4.1
), subject to the
p
relations

\left(\bar{z}_{0}^{-1}\bar{z}_{1}\right)^{q}=\left(\bar{z}_{1}^{-1}\bar{z}_{2}\right)^{q}=\ldots=\left(\bar{z}_{p-1}^{-1}\bar{z}_{0}\right)^{q}=1,

as
[z_{0}^{q},u],[x_{i}(h),y_{i}(h)]\in U^{\prime}
for every
h=0,\ldots,p-1
and
i=2,\ldots,d
.
Hence,
U^{\ab}
is not torsion free — in particular, it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2(d-1)p+1}\times(\mathbb{Z}/q)^{p}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Since
\mathrm{H}^{n}(U)=0
for
n\geq 3
, one may show — employing
[
qsv:quadratic
, Prop. 2.4–2.5 and Rem. 2.6]
— that the cohomology algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(U)
is a quadratic algebra: therefore, the subgroup
U
may not be used to prove that
G
is not Bloch-Kato.

4.4.
Massey products

Proposition 4.4
.

If
n\leq q
then
G
satisfies the strong
n
-fold Massey vanishing property.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be a sequence of length
n
of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
satisfying (
2.2
).
Since
n\leq q
, for any matrix
A\in\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
one has
A^{q}\in\Zen(\mathbb{U}_{n+1})
.
Therefore, if
A_{1},B_{1}\in\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
such that their
(h,h+1)
-entries, for
h=1,\ldots,n
, are equal to
\alpha_{h}(x_{1})
and
\alpha_{h}(y_{1})
respectively, then
[A_{1}^{q},B_{1}]=I_{n+1}
.
We will find matrices
A_{2},\ldots,B_{d}\in\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
such that

(4.2)

[A_{2},B_{2}]\cdots[A_{d},B_{d}]=I_{n+1},

and for all
i=2,\ldots,d
the
(h,h+1)
-entries of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are equal to
\alpha_{h}(x_{i})
and
\alpha_{h}(y_{i})
respectively,
so that the assignment
x_{i}\mapsto A_{i}
and
y_{i}\mapsto B_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, yields a morphism
\rho\colon G\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
satisfying the properties prescribed in Proposition
2.7
–(b).

Let
N
be the normal subgroup of
G
generated by
x_{1},y_{1}
as a normal subgroup, set
\bar{G}=G/N
— namely,
\bar{G}
is the Demuškin group

\bar{G}=\left\langle\,\bar{x}_{2},\,\bar{y}_{2},\ldots,\,\bar{x}_{d},\,\bar{y}_{d}\>\mid\>[\bar{x}_{2},\bar{y}_{2}]\cdots[\bar{x}_{d},\bar{y}_{d}]=1\>\right\rangle,

where
\bar{w}
denotes the coset
wN\in\bar{G}
, for
w\in G
—
and let
\pi\colon G\to\bar{G}
denote the canonical orientation.
Also, for
\beta\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
, set

\bar{\beta}=\beta(x_{2})\bar{x}_{2}^{\ast}+\beta(y_{2})\bar{y}^{\ast}_{2}+\ldots+\beta(x_{d})\bar{x}_{d}^{\ast}+\beta(y_{d})\bar{y}^{\ast}_{d}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\bar{G}).

Since
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
satisfy (
2.2
), one has

0=\alpha_{h}\smallsmile\alpha_{h+1}=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{d}\left(\alpha_{h}(x_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(y_{i})-\alpha_{h}(y_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(x_{i})\right)\right)(x_{2}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{2}^{\ast}),

and hence the sum above is 0.
Since
\bar{G}
is a Demuškin group,

\begin{split}\bar{\alpha}_{h}\smallsmile\bar{\alpha}_{h+1}&=\sum_{i=2}^{d}\left(\alpha_{h}(x_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(y_{i})-\alpha_{h}(y_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(x_{i})\right)(\bar{x}_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile\bar{y}_{i}^{\ast})\\
&=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{d}\left(\alpha_{h}(x_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(y_{i})-\alpha_{h}(y_{i})\alpha_{h+1}(x_{i})\right)\right)(\bar{x}_{2}^{\ast}\smallsmile\bar{y}_{2}^{\ast}),\end{split}

and hence also the sequence
\bar{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\alpha}_{n}
of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\bar{G})
satisfies (
2.2
).
Therefore, by Proposition
2.7
–(b) there exists a homomorphism
\eta\colon\bar{G}\to\mathbb{U}_{n+1}
such that

\eta_{h,h+1}=\bar{\alpha}_{h}\qquad\text{for all }h=1,\ldots,n-1,

as the Demuškin group
\bar{G}
has the strong
n
-fold Massey vanishing property, cf. Example
2.6
–(a).
Now set
A_{i}=\eta(\bar{x}_{i})
and
B_{i}=\eta(\bar{y}_{i})
for
i=2,\ldots,d
.
Then these matrices satisfy (
4.2
), and this completes the proof.
∎

4.5.
The graded group algebra

Consider the graded group algebra
\gr\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
, generated by
X_{1},Y_{2}\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d}
.
The initial form of the defining relation of

— i.e., the image in

— is the Lie polynomial

(X_{2}Y_{2}-Y_{2}X_{2})+\ldots+(X_{d}Y_{d}-Y_{d}X_{d}).

Since

is a mild pro-

,

[

implies that

\begin{split}\gr\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![{G}]\!]&\simeq\frac{T^{\bullet}(X_{1},Y_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d})}{([X_{2},Y_{2}]+\dots+[X_{d},Y_{d}])}\\
&=T^{\bullet}(X_{1},Y_{1})\sqcup\frac{T^{\bullet}(X_{2},Y_{2},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d})}{([X_{2},Y_{2}]+\dots+[X_{d},Y_{d}])},\end{split}

where the latter is a free product of quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras.
In particular, Proposition
1.3
–(3) follows by
[
expo
, Thm. 1.3]
.

By Jennings’ theorem, the restricted Lie algebra
\gr G
is the free product of a free Lie algebra of rank
2
— generated by
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
— and a
Demuškin Lie algebra
on

generators, namely, the restricted Lie algebra associated to a Demuškin group on

generators (with

). By
[
simone:kurosh
, Thm. 4.3]
and
[
simone:koszul

, the restricted Lie algebra

is Bloch-Kato, with cohomology
\ext^{\bullet}_{u(\gr G)}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})\simeq\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G)
.
This proves Proposition
1.4
for
\mathcal{F}_{1}
.

In this section we focus on the pro-

, and

— here we set

5.1.
Cohomology of

As underlined in Remark

satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition

, considering
\mathcal{X}_{G}=\{x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}

In particular,

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G)
and
\{x_{2}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{2}^{\ast},z_{1}^{\ast}\smallsmile z_{2}^{\ast}\}
a basis of
\mathrm{H}^{2}(G)
, and one has

x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{i}^{\ast}=x_{1}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{1}^{\ast}\qquad\text{for all }i=2,\ldots,d,

while

x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile x_{j}^{\ast}=y_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{j}^{\ast}=x_{i}^{\ast}\smallsmile y_{j}^{\ast}=0\qquad\text{for all }i\neq j

Remark 5.1
.

As observed in Remark

for
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, since
\cd(G)=2
, also
\cd(U)=2
for every open subgroup

of

.
Therefore, in order to check whether
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(U)
is quadratic (and thus, whether

\mathrm{H}^{2}(U)
, and hence the defining relations of

.

5.2.
The only candidate orientation

Since
G
is a quotient of the Demuškin group
\tilde{G}
, if
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is an orientation turning

into a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-

group, then such an orientation must be “inherited” from
\tilde{G}
, as shown by the following.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group, and let
p:\tilde{G}\to G
be a Frattini cover, i.e.,
\ker(p)\subseteq\Phi(\tilde{G})
. Then,
(\tilde{G},\theta\circ p)
is Kummerian.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\pi}:F\to\tilde{G}
be a minimal presentation of
\tilde{G}
, and consider the composite
\pi=p\circ\tilde{\pi}:F\to G
, which is a minimal presentation for
G
.
By Kummerianity of
(G,\theta)
, one has
\ker(\pi)\subseteq K_{\theta\circ\pi}(F)
, and hence, by construction,

\ker(\tilde{\pi})\subseteq\ker(\pi)\subseteq K_{\theta\circ\pi}(F)=K_{\theta\circ p\circ\tilde{\pi}}(F),

which implies that
(\tilde{G},\theta\circ p)
is Kummerian.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

If
\theta\colon G\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is an orientation such that
(G,\theta)
is a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
p
group with
G\in\mathcal{F}_{2}
, then
\theta\circ\pi\colon\tilde{G}\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the canonical orientation
\theta_{\tilde{G}}
of the Demuškin group
\tilde{G}
.
Moreover,
z_{1},z_{2}\neq y_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the subgroup of
G
generated by
z_{1},z_{2}
.
Since
[z_{1},z_{2}]=1
,

is an abelian pro-
p

On the other hand,
\cd(G)\leq 2<\infty
, so that

— and thus

— is torsion-free.
Altogether,

is a free abelian pro-
p
group, and thus
\theta|_{H}
is constantly equal to 1 by Example

.

Now set
\theta_{0}=\theta\circ\pi
.
Then
z_{1},z_{2}
— considered as elements of
\tilde{G}
— lie in
\Ker(\theta_{0})
, and hence
[z_{1},z_{2}]\in K_{\theta_{0}}(\tilde{G})
.
Thus,

\Ker(\theta_{0})/K_{\theta_{0}}(\tilde{G})\simeq\Ker(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G),

and the latter is a free abelian pro-
p
group by Proposition
3.2
, as
(G,\theta)
is Kummerian.
Consequently, also
(\tilde{G},\theta_{0})
is a Kummerian oriented pro-
p
group, again by Proposition
3.2
.

Therefore,
\theta_{0}
must be the canonical orientation
\theta_{\tilde{G}}\colon\tilde{G}\to 1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of the Demuškin group
\tilde{G}
(cf. §
3.4
).
Hence,

\theta(y_{1})=(1-q)^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta(x_{1})=\ldots=\theta(x_{d})=\theta(y_{2})=\ldots=\theta(y_{d})=1.

In particular,
z_{1},z_{2}\neq y_{1}
, as
z_{1},z_{2}\in\Ker(\theta)
.
∎

5.3.
A subgroup of index
p

Let

Proposition 5.14
.

Let

be a pro-
p

(

Then, the derived subalgebra

∞

Proof.

If

belongs to the class
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, then

is the free product of a surface Lie algebra and a free Lie algebra of rank

[

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a free Lie algebra.

Now let
G
be a group of the family
\mathcal{F}_{2}

Up to isomorphism,
\mathfrak{g}
admits the following presentation as an ordinary Lie algebra:

\left\langle\>{X_{1},Y_{1},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d}}\>\mid\>{\sum_{i=1}^{d}[X_{i},Y_{i}],[Y_{1},Y_{2}]}\>\right\rangle.

Let
\phi:\left\langle{Y_{1}}\right\rangle\to\mathfrak{h}=\left\langle{Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d}}\right\rangle
be the derivation defined by
\phi(Y_{1})=\sum_{i=2}^{d}[Y_{i},X_{i}]
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is Bloch-Kato, the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
generated by
Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d}
is quadratic, and hence it has presentation
\left\langle\>{Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\dots,X_{d},Y_{d}}\>\mid\>{[Y_{1},Y_{2}]}\>\right\rangle
.
In particular,
\mathfrak{h}
has free derived subalgebra.
Now, by
[
hnnLS
]
, since
\left\langle{Y_{1}}\right\rangle\cap\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=0
, we deduce that
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a free Lie algebra.

By
[
bassSerreLie
, Prop. 5.8]
,
\mathfrak{g}
is locally of type FP, that is,
\ext^{n}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p})

∎

The finitely generated closed subgroups of a Bloch-Kato pro-
p
group have quadratic cohomology, and, in particular, they admit a finite presentation. This means that Bloch-Kato pro-
