Uniform estimates and Brezis-Merle type inequalities for the k k -Hessian equation Thanks: This research is partially supported by National Key R & \& D Program of China 2023YFA009900 and NSFC Grant 12271008.
Jie Deng, Haibin Wang, Bin Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a Brezis-Merle type inequality for k k -convex functions vanishing on the boundary. As an application, we establish an Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci type estimate for the intermediate Hessian equation. Furthermore, we establish a concentration-compactness principle for the blow-up behavior of solutions to the Liouville type k k -Hessian equations.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
{\bf\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
,
k=1,\dots,n
, the
k
-Hessian
operator
S_{k}
is

\displaystyle S_{k}[u]=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

where
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
. When
k=1
, it is the Laplacian operator.
Let

\Phi^{k}(\Omega)=\{u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\,|\,S_{j}[u]\geq 0,\forall j=1,...,k\}

be the sets of smooth
k
-convex functions.
k
-convex functions are admissible functions
for
S_{k}
to be elliptic.
The notion of
k
-convexity can be extended to general upper semi-continuous functions.
An upper semi-continuous function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty)
is called
k
-convex
in
\Omega
if
S_{k}[q]\geq 0
for all quadratic polynomials
q
for which the diﬀerence
u-q
has a finite local maximum in
\Omega
.
In a series of papers
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
, Trudinger-Wang introduced the Hessian measures for general
k
-convex functions and
developed the weak solution theory for the
k
-Hessian equation

(1.1)

S_{k}[u]=f.

A key property shown in
[
14
]
is the weak continuity of
S_{k}[u]
. Namely, if there is a sequence
\{u_{m}\}
in
\Phi^{k}(\Omega)
converging locally in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
to
u
and
S_{k}[u_{m}]
converges weakly to the
k
-Hessian measure
\mu_{k}[u]
in the sense of measures.
The uniform estimate for the
k
-Hessian equations was established by
[
5
]
under the assumption that

f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
where
p>\frac{n}{2k}
if
k\leq\frac{n}{2}
or
p=1
if
k>\frac{n}{2}
,
based on the Hessian Sobolev inequalities
[
17
,
18
]
.

In a famous paper
[
4
]
, the uniform estimates and blow-up behavior
for equation

-\triangle u=V(x)e^{u}

were investigated based on the following basic inequality:

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left[\frac{(4\pi-\delta)|u(x)|}{\|\triangle u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}\right]\,dx\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\delta}(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{2}

for all
\delta\in(0,4\pi)
and
u
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with vanishing boundary value. Subsequently, in higher dimensions, Aguilar and Peral
[
1
]
established the inequality

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left[\frac{(n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}-\delta)|u(x)|}{\|f\|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}}\right]\,dx\leq\frac{n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}{\delta}(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{2}

for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\delta\in(0,n^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n-1}})
, where
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
u
vanishes on the boundary with
-\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{n-2}\nabla u)=f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
.
An inequality of similar form for the complex Monge-Ampère operator were obtained by
[
7
,
2
,
3
]
.
In this paper, we study the analogous theory of
k
-Hessian equations.

Let
\Phi^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
be the subset of functions in
\Phi^{k}(\Omega)
with vanishing boundary value.
When
\partial\Omega
is
(k-1)
-convex, i.e., there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\leq n-1}\kappa_{i_{1}}(x)\cdots\kappa_{i_{j}}(x)\geq c_{0}

for all
x\in\partial\Omega
and
1\leq j\leq k-1
, where
\kappa_{1}(x),\dots,\kappa_{n-1}(x)
are the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
at
x
, we have
\Phi^{k}_{0}(\Omega)\neq\emptyset
.
We define the
k
-Hessian integral

I_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega}(-u)S_{k}[u]\,dx

and the
k
-Hessian mass

\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega}S_{k}[u]\,dx.

Then

||u||_{\Phi^{k}_{0}(\Omega)}:=I_{k}(u)^{\frac{1}{k+1}}

is a norm on
\Phi^{k}_{0}(\Omega)
. As we mentioned above, the
k
-Hessian measure can be defined for
general
k
-convex functions. We denote by
\mathcal{F}^{k}(\Omega)
the set of all
k
-convex functions
u

such that
\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)=\mu_{k}[u](\Omega)<+\infty
and
\displaystyle\lim_{y\to x}u(y)=0
for any
x\in\partial\Omega
.

The first result of this paper is the following Brezis-Merle type inequality for the
k
-Hessian equations.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{F}^{k}(\Omega)
.

(1)

If
1\leq k<n/2
, then for
1\leq p\leq\frac{kn}{n-2k}
, there exists
C=C(n,k,p,|\Omega|)
such that

||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}.

(2)

If
k=\frac{n}{2}
, then for
0<\lambda<\alpha_{0}
and
1\leq\beta\leq\beta_{0}
, there exists
C=C(n)
such that the following Brezis–Merle type inequality holds:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\lambda\left(\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{1}{k}}}\right)^{\frac{k\beta}{k+1}}\right)\,dx:u\in\mathcal{F}^{k}(\Omega),\,0<||u||_{\Phi_{0}^{k}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\}\leq\frac{\alpha_{0}C(n)}{\alpha_{0}-\lambda}|\Omega|,

where

(1.3)

\alpha_{0}=n\left[\omega_{n}\binom{n}{k}\right]^{2/n},\quad\beta_{0}=\frac{n+2}{n}.

With the Brezis-Merle type inequality in the intermediate case(
k=\frac{n}{2}
), we can improve the uniform estimate and get an ABP type estimate.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
(k-1)
-convex smooth domain,
k=n/2
and
u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
. Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a positive increasing function with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi^{-1/k}(t)\,dt\leq\Lambda<\infty.

Then there exist constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(n,|\Omega|),c_{2}=c_{2}(n,\Lambda)>0
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u+c_{1}+c_{2}\left(\int_{\Gamma^{-}}S_{k}[-u]\Phi(\log(S_{k}[-u]))\,dx\right)^{1/k},

where
\Gamma^{-}=\{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)>0\text{ and }-S_{k}[u](x)>0\}
.

We can also apply the Brezis-Merle type inequality to study the concentration-compactness principle for the intermediate Hessian equation.
Let
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty}
be a sequence of nonnegative functions and

\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty}
be a sequence of weak solutions of

S_{k}[u_{i}]=V_{i}e^{-u_{i}}

in a domain
\Omega
with

||V_{i}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1},\ ||e^{-u_{i}}||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}

for some
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and some
1<p\leq\infty
. Here
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
.
Define the blow-up set

S=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ \text{there exists a sequence}\;\{x_{i}\}\in\Omega\;\text{such that }x_{i}\to x\;\text{and }u_{i}(x_{i})\to-\infty\}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.3
.

There exists a subsequence
\{u_{i_{j}}\}
satisfying the following alternative:

either (i)
\{u_{i_{j}}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega)

or (ii)
\{u_{i_{j}}\}\to+\infty
uniformly on every compact subset of
\Omega

or (iii) the blow-up set
S
of
{u_{i_{j}}}
is finite and nonempty, and
u_{i_{j}}\to+\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega\setminus S
.
Moreover, in case (iii), the measures
V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}
converge in the sense of measure on
\Omega
to

\mu=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}
, where
S=\bigcup_{i}{\alpha_{i}}
and each coefficient satisfies
\alpha_{i}\geq\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{k}
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the proof of Brezis-Merle type inequality(Theorem
1.1
). Section
3
is devoted to a Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci type estimate for the
k
-Hessian equation. Finally, in the last section, we apply the Brezis-Merle type inequality to study the concentration-compactness principle.

2.
The Brezis-Merle type inequality

We make use of the isocapacitary inequalities for the
k
-Hessian equations to establish the Brezis-Merle type inequalities.

Recall the capacity for the
k
-Hessian equation
[
15
,
11
]

\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(K,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{K}S_{k}[u]\,dx:u\;\text{is k-convex},-1<u<0\right\}.

A non-negative Borel measure
\mu
on
\Omega
is said to be
continuous with respect to the
k
-Hessian capacity
if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any open set
E\subset\Omega
with
\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega)<\delta
, we have
\mu(E)<\varepsilon
.
It follows from
[
15
]
that
k
-Hessian measure
\mu_{k}[u]
is continuous with respect to capacity if either
u
is bounded in
\Omega
or there exist constants
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
such that

\mu_{k}[u](B_{r}(x))\leq Cr^{n-2k+\varepsilon}

whenever
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
. We will use the following isocapacitary inequalities.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
19
, Theorem 1.2]

(i)

If
1\leq k<\frac{n}{2}
and
1\leq q\leq\frac{n(k+1)}{n-2k}
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,k,q,|\Omega|)>0
such that for any
E\subset\Omega
,

|E|\leq C\cdot\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega)^{\frac{q}{k+1}}.

(ii)

If
k=\frac{n}{2}
and
1\leq\beta\leq 1+\frac{1}{k}
, there exists a constant
C(n)>0
such that for any
E\subset\Omega
,

|E|\cdot\exp\left(\alpha_{0}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(E,\Omega)^{\frac{\beta}{k+1}}}\right)\leq C(n)|\Omega|,

where
\alpha_{0}
is given in (
1.3
).

In order to establish the Brezis-Merle inequality for general
k
-convex functions, we need the following comparison principle.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
15
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and let
u,v
be
k
-convex functions such that
u=v=\phi
continuously on
\partial\Omega
. If the
k
-Hessian measures
\mu_{k}[u]
and
\mu_{k}[v]
are both continuous with respect to the
k
-Hessian capacity, then

\mu_{k}[u]\bigl(\{u<v\}\bigr)\geq\mu_{k}[v]\bigl(\{u<v\}\bigr).

Remark 2.3
.

The theorem also holds if the condition “
u=v=\phi
continuously on
\partial\Omega
” is replaced by
u\geq v
on
\partial\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Define
K_{t}:=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)<-t\}
,
t>0
. Let
u_{K_{t}}
be the extremal function with respect to
\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega)
and let
u_{j}\in\Phi_{0}^{k}(\Omega)
be a sequence converging to
u
decreasingly. Choose
\delta>0
such that there exists a compact set
B_{\delta}
satisfying

\{u+\delta\leq tu_{K_{t}}\}\subset B_{\delta}\subset\Omega.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have

\int_{\{u_{j}+\delta<tu_{K_{t}}\}}S_{k}[u_{j}]\,dx\geq\int_{\{u_{j}+\delta<tu_{K_{t}}\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx.

Note that
u_{j}\geq u
. Hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{j}\int_{\{u_{j}+\delta<tu_{K_{t}}\}}S_{k}[u_{j}]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j}\int_{\{u+\delta\leq tu_{K_{t}}\}}S_{k}[u_{j}]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j}\int_{B_{\delta}}S_{k}[u_{j}]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}}S_{k}[u]\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}S_{k}[u]\,dx.

Since
u_{j}
decrease and
\{u+\delta<-t\}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\{u_{j}+\delta<-t\}
, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{j}\int_{\{u_{j}+\delta<tu_{K_{t}}\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j}\int_{\{u_{j}+\delta<-t\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{u+\delta<-t\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx.

Letting
\delta\to 0
, then

\int_{\{u+\delta<-t\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx\to\int_{\{u<-t\}}S_{k}[tu_{K_{t}}]\,dx.

Therefore we obtain

\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)=\int_{\Omega}S_{k}[u]\,dx\geq t^{k}\int_{K_{t}}S_{k}[u_{K_{t}}]\,dx=t^{k}\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega),\quad\forall\,t>0,

that is,

(2.1)

\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega)\leq\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}{t}\right)^{k}.

The rest of the proof is divided into two cases:

(1) Let
1\leq k<n/2
.
By Theorem
2.1
(i), for
1\leq q\leq\frac{n(k+1)}{n-2k}
, it holds

|K_{t}|\leq C\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}{t}\right)^{\frac{kq}{k+1}}.

Then for any
1\leq p\leq\frac{kq}{k+1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}\right)^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{\{\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}\leq 1\}}\cdot\left(\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}\right)^{p}+\chi_{\{\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}>1\}}\cdot\left(\frac{-u}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}\right)^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|+\int_{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}^{\infty}pt^{p-1}\frac{|K_{t}|}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{p/k}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|+Cp\int_{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k}}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{q}{k+1}-\frac{p}{k}}t^{p-1-\frac{kq}{k+1}}\,dt

\displaystyle=|\Omega|+Cp\left(\frac{kq}{k+1}-p\right)^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{q}{k+1}-\frac{p}{k}}\cdot(\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{1/k})^{p-\frac{kq}{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq C.

(2) Let
k=\frac{n}{2}
. By Theorem
2.1
(ii),

|K_{t}|\leq C(n)|\Omega|\cdot\exp\left(-\alpha_{0}\frac{1}{\operatorname{Cap}_{k}(K_{t},\Omega)^{\frac{\beta}{k+1}}}\right)\leq C(n)|\Omega|\cdot\exp\left(-\alpha_{0}\frac{t^{\frac{k\beta}{k+1}}}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta}{k+1}}}\right).

Then for every
0<\lambda<\alpha_{0}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp\left(\lambda\frac{(-u)^{\frac{k\beta}{k+1}}}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta}{k+1}}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{j}}{j!}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(-u)^{\frac{k\beta j}{k+1}}}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta j}{k+1}}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{j}}{j!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|K_{t}|}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta j}{k+1}}}\,d(t^{\frac{k\beta j}{k+1}})

\displaystyle\leq C(n)|\Omega|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{j}}{j!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\alpha_{0}\frac{t^{\frac{k\beta}{k+1}}}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta}{k+1}}}\right)\,d\left(\frac{t^{\frac{k\beta j}{k+1}}}{\mathcal{M}_{k}(u)^{\frac{\beta j}{k+1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(n)|\Omega|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{j}}{\alpha_{0}^{j}}=\frac{\alpha_{0}C(n)}{\alpha_{0}-\lambda}|\Omega|.

∎

3.
An ABP type estimate

The proof is inspired by a recent iterative method using an auxiliary Monge–Ampère equation in the study of the complex
Monge–Ampère equation
[
9
,
12
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
\sup_{\partial\Omega}u=0
.
Let
\{e^{G_{i}}\}
be a sequence of smooth positive functions approximating
\chi_{\Gamma^{-}}S_{k}[-u]
from above. Define

N_{i}:=\int_{\Omega}e^{G_{i}}\Phi(G_{i})\,dx.

Then
N_{i}\to\int_{\Gamma^{-}}S_{k}[-u]\Phi(\log(S_{k}[-u]))\,dx
as
i\to\infty
.

Consider the auxiliary
k
-Hessian equation for
\psi_{1}
,

\begin{cases}S_{k}[\psi_{1}]=\frac{e^{G_{i}}\Phi(G_{i})}{N_{i}}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi_{1}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

By Theorem
1.1
, there exists a constant
\alpha(n)>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\exp(-\alpha(n)\psi_{1})\,dx\leq C(n)|\Omega|.

Let
q>1
and let
\alpha>0
be the constant from the inequality above. Define
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as

h(s)=-\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{N_{i}^{1/k}}{\Phi^{1/k}(t)}\,dt.

Since
\Phi
is increasing,
h
is a concave, increasing function.
Define the function
\psi=-h(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})
.
A direct computation shows

D^{2}\psi=\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})D^{2}\psi_{1}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{q^{2}}h^{\prime\prime}D\psi_{1}\otimes D\psi_{1}.

We can get

S_{k}[\psi]\geq\left(\frac{\alpha}{q}h^{\prime}(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})\right)^{k}S_{k}[\psi_{1}]=\left(\frac{\alpha}{q}\right)^{k}(h^{\prime})^{k}\frac{e^{G_{i}}\Phi(G_{i})}{N_{i}}.

By the definition of
h
, we have
(h^{\prime}(s))^{k}=\frac{q^{k}}{\alpha^{k}}\frac{N_{i}}{\Phi(s)}
. So

S_{k}[\psi]\geq\frac{e^{G_{i}}\Phi(G_{i})}{\Phi(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})}.

If
G_{i}>-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}
, then
\Phi(G_{i})>\Phi(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})
, thus
e^{G_{i}}\leq S_{k}[\psi]
. If
G_{i}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}
,
e^{G_{i}}\leq\exp(-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1})
.
In summary, we get

(3.2)

\displaystyle e^{G_{i}}\,dx\leq S_{k}[\psi]+F\,dx\quad\text{with}\quad F=\min\{\exp\{-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}\},e^{G_{i}}\}.

We consider the auxiliary
k
-Hessian equation

\begin{cases}S_{k}[\psi_{s,j}]=\frac{\eta_{j}(u+\psi-s)F}{A_{s,j}}&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi_{s,j}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where

A_{s,j}=\int_{\Omega}\eta_{j}(u+\psi-s)F\,dx

and
\eta_{j}
is a sequence of smooth positive functions approximating
\max\{x,0\}
from above uniformly.

Consider the function
\Psi=u+\psi-s-\epsilon(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{k}{k+1}}
, where

\epsilon=\left(\frac{k+1}{k}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}A_{s,j}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}.

Let
s_{0}=-h(0)\leq q\alpha^{-1}N_{i}^{1/k}\Lambda
. We claim that
\Psi\leq 0
for
s\geq s_{0}
. On the boundary, we have
\Psi\leq 0
when
s\geq-h(0)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the maximum of
\Psi
is attained at
x_{0}\in\Omega_{s}=\{x\in B_{1}\mid u(x)+\psi(x)-s>0\}
. At
x_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq D^{2}u+D^{2}\psi+\epsilon\frac{k}{k+1}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{-1}{k+1}}D^{2}\psi_{s,j}.

Then we get

S_{k}[-u]\geq S_{k}[\psi]+\left(\epsilon\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{-k}{k+1}}_{i}S_{k}[\psi_{s,j}].

Using the inequality (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle S_{k}[\psi]+F

\displaystyle\geq e^{G_{i}}\geq S_{k}[-u]

\displaystyle\geq S_{k}[\psi]+\left(\epsilon\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{k}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{-k}{k+1}}S_{k}[\psi_{s,j}].

By the definition of
\psi_{s,j}
, we get

\eta_{j}(u+\psi-s)\leq A_{s,j}\left(\epsilon\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{-k}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{k}{k+1}}.

From the definition of
\epsilon
, it follows
\Psi\leq 0
.

Let
\phi(s):=\int_{\Omega_{s}}F\,dx
, where
\Omega_{s}=\{u+\psi>s\}
and
F=\min(e^{-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}},e^{G_{i}})
.
Let

A_{s}:=\int_{\Omega_{s}}(u+\psi-s)F\,dx.

For
t>0
, we have
(u+\psi-s)\geq t
on
\Omega_{s+t}
. Thus,

t\cdot\phi(s+t)=t\int_{\Omega_{s+t}}F\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{s+t}}(u+\psi-s)F\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega_{s}}(u+\psi-s)F\,dx=A_{s}.

For
s\geq s_{0},\Psi\leq 0
, we have
u+\psi-s\leq\epsilon(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{k}{k+1}}
. Then

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{s}}(u+\psi-s)F\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{k}{k+1}}F\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{kp}{k+1}}F\,dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}F\,dx\right)^{1-1/p}.

Letting
j\to\infty
, we have
A_{s,j}\to A_{s}
. Note that
\epsilon=c(n)A_{s,j}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}\to c(n)A_{s}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}
, then we get

A_{s}\leq c(n)\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{kp}{k+1}}F\,dx\right)^{\frac{k+1}{kp}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{s}}F\,dx\right)^{(1-1/p)\frac{k+1}{k}}.

For
p>k+1
, let
\delta=(1-1/p)\frac{k+1}{k}-1>0
.
By (
3.1
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{s}}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{kp}{k+1}}F\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}(-\psi_{s,j})^{\frac{kpq^{*}}{k+1}}\,dx\right)^{1/q^{*}}||F||_{L^{q}}\leq c(n,|\Omega|).

Here
q^{*}
sasisfies
1/q^{*}+1/q=1
.
Hence

A_{s}\leq C(n)\cdot\phi(s)^{1+\delta}.

This gives

t\phi(s+t)\leq C\phi(s)^{1+\delta},\quad\forall s\geq s_{0}\;\text{and}\;t>0.

The following is a classic lemma due to De Giorgi
[
6
]
, which was also used in
[
10
,
8
,
9
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(De Giorgi’s lemma)
.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a monotone decreasing function such that for some
\delta>0
and any
s\geq s_{0}
,
t>0
,

t\,\phi(s+t)\leq C_{0}\,\phi(s)^{1+\delta}.

Then
\phi(s)=0
for any
s\geq\dfrac{2C_{0}\,\phi(s_{0})^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}+s_{0}
.

Applying De Giorgi’s lemma, we obtain
\phi(s)=0
for all
s\geq s_{\infty}
, where

s_{\infty}=\frac{2C\phi(s_{0})^{\delta}}{1-2^{-\delta}}+s_{0}.

This means
u+\psi\leq s_{\infty}
, then

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\Omega}(u+\psi)\leq s_{\infty}.

Note that

s_{0}=-h(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{q}{\alpha}\frac{N_{i}^{1/k}}{\Phi^{1/k}(t)}\,dt=c_{2}(n,\Lambda)N_{i}^{1/k}

and

\phi(s_{0})\leq\int_{\Omega}F\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{q}\psi_{1}}\,dx\leq c_{1}(n,|\Omega|).

Hence, we obtain

s_{\infty}\leq c_{1}+c_{2}N_{i}^{1/k}.

Letting
i\to\infty
, we have

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq c_{1}+c_{2}\left(\int_{\Gamma^{-}}S_{k}[-u]\Phi(\log(S_{k}[-u]))\,dx\right)^{1/k}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Concentration-compactness principle

In this section, we prove the concentration compactness principle for the
k
-Hessian equation with
k=\frac{n}{2}
.

First, we apply Brezis-Merle type inequality.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty}
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1}^{+\infty}
be as in Theorem
1.3
. Suppose there exists
C_{0}<(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{k}
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V_{i}e^{-u_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}.

Denote
u_{i}^{-}:=\min\{u_{i},0\}
.
Then
\{u_{i}^{-}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{i}^{-}=\{x\in\Omega\ |\ u_{i}(x)<0\}
and
v_{i}=u_{i}^{-}\chi_{\Omega_{i}^{-}}
. We have
v_{i}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{i}^{-}
.
By Theorem
1.1
and (
4.1
), we have
e^{-v_{i}}
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
and so
V_{i}e^{-v_{i}}
is bounded in
L^{q}
for some
q>1
. Using the
L^{\infty}
estimate in
[
16
]
or
[
5
]
, we get
||v_{i}||_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded. Hence,
||u_{i}^{-}||_{L^{\infty}}
is bounded.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We can also apply Theorem
1.2
with
u=-v_{i}
,
\Phi(t)=e^{\delta t}
for some
\delta>0
to get
||v_{i}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is bounded.

By the assumptions,
V_{i}e^{-u_{i}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then we may choose a subsequence such that
V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}
converges to some nonnegative bounded measure
\mu
in the sense of measures. For every
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}\phi\to\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\mu.

Similarly as in
[
4
]
, we say a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
is
regular
if there is a
\phi\in C_{c}(\Omega)
,
0\leq\phi\leq 1
with
\phi=1
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, such that

\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\mu<\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}}\right)^{k}.

Let
\Sigma
be the set of nonregular points in
\Omega
. Then some simple conclusions follow:

(1)

By Lemma
4.1
, if
x_{0}
is regular, then there is some
R_{0}>0
such that
\{u_{i_{j}}^{-}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))
.

(2)

By definition
x_{0}\in\Sigma
iff
\mu(x_{0})\geq(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{k}
.

(3)

Since
\mu(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\,d\mu\leq C_{1}C_{2}
, it follows that
\Sigma
is finite.

Moreover, we have

Lemma 4.3
.

S=\Sigma
.

Proof.

If
x_{0}
is regular, by (1) above,
\{u_{i_{j}}^{-}\}
is locally uniformly bounded near
x_{0}
. So
S\subset\Sigma
.

On the other hand, if
x_{0}\in\Sigma
, then we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to+\infty}||u^{-}_{i_{j}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}=+\infty.

Otherwise there exist some
R^{\prime}>0
and a subsequence such that
||u^{-}_{i_{j}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{0}))}\leq C
. Hence, we have

||e^{-u_{i_{j}}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{0}))}\leq C
and

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}\,dx\leq CC_{1}R^{n/p^{\prime}}

for
R<R^{\prime}
. Letting
R\to 0
, we get
x_{0}
is regular. This is a contradiction.
Now we choose
R>0
small enough so that
\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
does not contain any other point of
\Sigma
. Let
x_{i_{j}}\in\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
such that

u^{-}_{i_{j}}(x_{i_{j}})\leq\inf\limits_{\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}}u^{-}_{i_{j}}+\ep\to-\infty.

After choosing a subsequence, we have
x_{i_{j}}\to\bar{x}\in\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
. By the choice of
R
,
\bar{x}\notin\Sigma\setminus\{x_{0}\}
. If
\bar{x}\neq x_{0}
, then
\bar{x}
is regular. There is
R_{1}>0
such that
||u^{-}_{i_{j}}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R_{1}}(\bar{x}))}\leq C
. This contradicts with
u^{-}_{i_{j}}(x_{i_{j}})\to-\infty
. Therefore
\bar{x}=x_{0}
and
x_{0}\in S
.
∎

We also need the following Harnack inequality for the
k
-Hessian equation.

Theorem 4.4
.

[
11
]
Let
u\in\Phi^{k}(B_{R}),1\leq k\leq n/2
,
u\leq 0
,
S_{k}[u]=\mu
and there exist constants
M>0,\ep>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,r))\leq Mr^{n-2k+\ep}

for all
x\in B_{R/10},0<r<R/10
, then there exist positive constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,k)
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(n,k,M,\ep)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r}}|u|\leq C_{1}\inf_{B_{r}}|u|+C_{2}r^{\ep/k}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If
S=\emptyset
, then every point in
\Omega
is regular. Hence, by Lemma
4.1
,
{u_{i_{j}}^{-}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, and consequently
V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}
is bounded in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. We now show that if case (ii) does not occur, then case (i) holds. Suppose there exist
x_{0}\in\Omega
, a constant
C>0
, and a subsequence such that
u_{i_{j}}(x_{0})\leq C
. For a ball
B_{10r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}\leq||V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}||_{L^{p}(B_{r})}\cdot r^{n/p^{\prime}}.

By Theorem
4.4
,

\sup_{B_{r}}|u_{i_{j}}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}|u_{i_{j}}(x_{0})|+C_{2}^{\prime}.

Hence

\sup_{B_{r}}|u_{i_{j}}|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}(C+||u_{i_{j}}^{-}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})})+C_{2}^{\prime}.

Since
{u_{i_{j}}^{-}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, the right-hand side is uniformly bounded, and therefore
{u_{i_{j}}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, i.e., case (i) holds.

If
S\neq\emptyset
, from above we have
\{u^{-}_{i_{j}}\}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus S)
and
V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}
is bounded in
L^{p}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus S)
. From Theorem
4.4
, we get either

(4.3)

\displaystyle\text{a subsequence}\;\{u_{i_{j}}\}\text{\ is bounded in }\;L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus S)

or

\displaystyle\{u_{i_{j}}\}\;\text{converges to }+\infty\;\text{uniformly on compact subsets of}\;\Omega\setminus S.

We claim that (
4.3
) does not happen. Choose
x_{0}\in S
and
R>0
such that
x_{0}=S\cap\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
. Assume (
4.3
) holds. Then there exists
C>0
such that
||u_{i_{j}}||_{L^{\infty}(\partial B_{R}(x_{0}))}\leq C
. Let
\{z_{i_{j}}\}
be the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}S_{k}[z_{i_{j}}]&=V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}\quad\text{in}\;B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
z_{i_{j}}&=C\qquad\text{on}\;\partial B_{R}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

By maximum principle,
u_{i_{j}}\leq z_{i_{j}}
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Thus

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}e^{-p^{\prime}z_{i_{j}}}\,dx\leq\int_{B_{R}}e^{-p^{\prime}u_{i_{j}}}\,dx\leq C_{2}^{p^{\prime}}.

On the other hand,
z_{i_{j}}\to z
uniformly on compact sets of
B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus\{x_{0}\}
, where
z
is the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}S_{k}[z]&=\mu\quad\text{in}\;B_{R}(x_{0}),\\
z&=C\qquad\text{on}\;\partial B_{R}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

Since
x_{0}
is not a regular point, we have
\mu(\{x_{0}\})\geq(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{k}
. Thus
\mu\geq(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{k}\delta_{x_{0}}
. Let
w(x)=\log|x-y|
be the fundamental solution to the
k
-Hessian operator satisfying

\mu_{k}[w]=\tbinom{n}{k}\omega_{n}\delta_{y}

with
k=\frac{n}{2}
.
By comparision, we get

z\leq\frac{n}{p^{\prime}}\log|x-x_{0}|+O(1).

Therefore

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}e^{-p^{\prime}z}\,dx\geq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{\,dx}{|x-x_{0}|^{n}}=+\infty.

From (
4.4
), we get contradiction.

So
\{u_{i_{j}}\}
converges to
+\infty
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega\setminus S
. Thus
V_{i_{j}}e^{-u_{i_{j}}}\to 0
in
L^{p}_{loc}(\Omega\setminus S)
and
\mu
is supported on S. We get
\mu=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}
with
S=\cup_{i}\{a_{i}\}
and
\alpha_{i}\geq(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{p^{\prime}})^{k}
.
∎
