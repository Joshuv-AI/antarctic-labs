Prevalence and risk factors of echocardiographically suspected pulmonary hypertension in maintenance hemodialysis patients: a retrospective study
Authors: Yan Jiang, Zongyang Liu, Chaojiang Su, Yan Chen, Xingde Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPulmonary hypertension (PH) is a prevalent complication in maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) patients, contributing to increased cardiovascular morbidity. Introduction

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) face a profoundly elevated risk of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality, with cardiovascular events accounting for approximately half of all deaths in this population [1, 2]. In recent years, pulmonary hypertension (PH) has emerged as a frequent and severe complication among MHD patients, independently predicting reduced exercise capacity, frequent hospitalizations, and significantly higher all-cause mortality [3, 4]. However, the reported prevalence of PH in this population varies unacceptably widely—ranging from 18% to over 70% [5–7]. This massive discrepancy is largely driven by a historical reliance on subjective echocardiographic reporting, varying population characteristics, and inconsistent diagnostic thresholds across different centers [8].

Pathophysiologically, PH in the ESRD population is predominantly classified as World Health Organization (WHO) Group 2, resulting directly from left heart disease and chronic volume overload [9, 10]. Consequently, objective markers of left-sided cardiac remodeling, such as enlarged left atrial diameter and reduced ejection fraction, are critical underlying mechanisms. Furthermore, the role of vascular access complications is increasingly recognized; high-output arteriovenous fistulas, recurrent access thromboses, and repeated endovascular interventions (such as percutaneous transluminal angioplasty or thrombectomy) are hypothesized to contribute to pulmonary vascular remodeling and right ventricular strain through altered hemodynamics and potential microembolization [11, 12]. Because right-sided structural changes can be a consequence rather than a cause of the disease, evaluating these risk factors requires strict adherence to standardized, objective hemodynamic estimates—such as a pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) > 35 mmHg derived from tricuspid regurgitant velocity—rather than subjective chamber enlargement [13, 14].

Despite growing clinical recognition and updated international guidelines, large-scale data comprehensively evaluating the independent contributions of vascular access history and left-sided cardiac parameters to PH risk in Chinese MHD patients remains limited [15, 16]. Furthermore, previous predictive models have frequently been confounded by circular reasoning, utilizing the same right-heart structural changes used to suspect the disease as predictors of the disease itself. Therefore, this large single-center retrospective study aimed to determine the prevalence of PH using standardized, objective echocardiographic criteria (PASP > 35 mmHg) and to identify robust, independent clinical, laboratory, and left-sided structural risk factors in a Chinese MHD cohort, with a particular focus on the impact of vascular access interventions.

Methods

Study design and setting

This was a single-center, retrospective cross-sectional study conducted at Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Guizhou Medical University between January 2023 and December 2024. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Guizhou Medical University (Approval No.: FZ 2025-10-271). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study.

Patients

During the study period, a total of 1050 patients were receiving maintenance hemodialysis at our center. Of these, 948 consecutive adult patients (≥ 18 years) who had undergone routine transthoracic echocardiography for clinical care and met all criteria were included in the final analysis. Patients with acute coronary syndrome, severe valvular heart disease, congenital heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, connective tissue disease, pulmonary embolism, or incomplete echocardiographic or clinical data were excluded.

Data collection

Demographic and clinical data were retrospectively collected from the hospital’s electronic medical records and hemodialysis database. Recorded variables included age, sex, dialysis vintage (months), body height, body weight, primary vascular access type, access history (central vein stent placement, thrombectomy, percutaneous transluminal angioplasty/balloon dilation, catheter infection, catheter dysfunction), and comorbidities (hypertension and diabetes mellitus).

Laboratory parameters obtained within 1 month of echocardiography were recorded, including hemoglobin, serum albumin, uric acid, calcium, phosphorus, beta-2 microglobulin, serum iron, unsaturated iron-binding capacity, total iron-binding capacity, transferrin saturation, intact parathyroid hormone, ferritin, and brain natriuretic peptide (BNP).

Echocardiography and definition of echocardiographically suspected pulmonary hypertension

Transthoracic echocardiography was performed as part of routine clinical care by experienced ultrasound physicians using Philips iE33 or GE Vivid E9 systems. To minimize the immediate hemodynamic effects of ultrafiltration, echocardiograms were typically performed prior to the dialysis session. Key structural parameters measured included left atrial diameter (LAD), right atrial transverse and longitudinal diameters, and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF, using Simpson’s biplane method).

When tricuspid regurgitation was present and adequately visualized, pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) was estimated using continuous-wave Doppler to measure the maximum tricuspid regurgitation jet velocity. According to the simplified Bernoulli equation, the tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient was calculated as ΔP = 4 × Vmax², and PASP was derived as PASP = 4 × Vmax² + RAP, where RAP is the estimated right atrial pressure.

In this study, echocardiographically suspected pulmonary hypertension was primarily defined using standardized objective echocardiographic criteria, specifically an estimated PASP > 35 mmHg [3]. In a subset of patients where the tricuspid regurgitant jet was inadequate for reliable continuous-wave Doppler measurement, the diagnosis of PH was made based on established qualitative echocardiographic markers of right heart strain, primarily significant right atrial enlargement (right atrial longitudinal diameter > 50 mm) combined with supportive signs such as right ventricular dilatation or abnormal septal motion as evaluated by the experienced ultrasound physicians [14].

It is important to note that echocardiography provides only a non-invasive estimate of pulmonary pressures and right heart loading conditions. It does not constitute a definitive hemodynamic diagnosis of pulmonary hypertension, which requires right heart catheterization (RHC). The terminology “echocardiographically suspected pulmonary hypertension” is therefore used throughout this manuscript to accurately reflect the surrogate nature of the assessment.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, and as median with interquartile range (IQR) for highly skewed data (e.g., BNP). Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (%). Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. Prior to analysis, the dataset underwent rigorous data cleaning. A sensitivity analysis was performed by excluding physiologically implausible data entry errors and extreme outliers (e.g., in BMI and phosphorus) to verify the stability of the key predictors and ensure model robustness.

Group comparisons between patients with and without PH were conducted using independent samples t-tests, Mann-Whitney U tests, or chi-square tests, as appropriate. Univariate binary logistic regression analyses were performed to identify potential risk factors for PH. To avoid circular reasoning, echocardiographic markers of right heart strain (right atrial transverse and longitudinal diameters) were strictly excluded from all predictive modeling, as they were utilized in the diagnostic criteria for a subset of patients.

To determine independent predictors, a prespecified multivariable binary logistic regression model was constructed. Core clinical confounders (age, sex, dialysis vintage, diabetes, and hypertension) were forced into the model to ensure robust adjustment, alongside variables that showed significance (p < 0.05) in the univariate analysis. Prior to modeling, multicollinearity was assessed using the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). Iron saturation was excluded from the multivariable model to prevent perfect collinearity, as it is mathematically derived from serum iron. All variables retained in the final model demonstrated a VIF < 2.0.

Model calibration and fit were evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and McFadden’s pseudo-R². Missing data were handled using complete-case analysis for the regression models. A total of 870 complete cases were included in the multivariable model. Odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were reported. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 26.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). Figures were generated using GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population

This single-center cross-sectional study included 948 maintenance hemodialysis patients. The mean age was 53.33 ± 13.72 years, with a male proportion of 49.6%. The average dialysis vintage was 46.34 ± 39.55 months. Hypertension was the most common comorbidity, affecting 75.3% of patients, followed by diabetes in 22.9%. The majority of patients used an arteriovenous fistula (AVF) as their current vascular access (71.8%). Approximately 4.1% had a history of central vein stent placement, 5.8% had undergone thrombectomy, and 26.3% had received percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (PTA) or balloon dilation.

Anthropometric measurements showed a mean body mass index (BMI) of 21.99 ± 3.65 kg/m². Key laboratory parameters included hemoglobin (111.43 ± 23.99 g/L), serum albumin (42.66 ± 4.69 g/L), and phosphorus (1.87 ± 0.69 mmol/L). Brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) demonstrated a highly skewed distribution with an overall median of 166.40 pg/mL (Interquartile Range [IQR]: 60.83–516.56). Echocardiographic findings revealed a mean left atrial diameter (LAD) of 37.67 ± 4.99 mm, right atrial transverse diameter of 35.28 ± 5.20 mm, right atrial longitudinal diameter of 45.66 ± 5.61 mm, and left ventricular ejection fraction (EF) of 56.46 ± 7.26%. Prevalence of Pulmonary Hypertension

In this cohort of 948 maintenance hemodialysis patients, the overall prevalence of echocardiographically suspected PH was 29.7% (282 patients). Stratification by key subgroups showed variations in prevalence. Patients older than the median age of 53 years had a higher prevalence (31.4%, n = 472) compared to those aged 53 years or younger (28.2%, n = 476). The prevalence was slightly higher in patients with diabetes (32.7%, n = 217) versus those without (28.9%, n = 731), and in those with hypertension (30.3%, n = 714) compared to those without (28.2%, n = 234). The prevalence was higher in patients with more than the median dialysis vintage of 36 months (31.6%, n = 411) versus those with 36 months or less (28.3%, n = 537). Comparison of clinical and laboratory parameters between patients with and without pulmonary hypertension

Patients with suspected PH exhibited several notable differences compared to those without suspected PH. The suspected PH group had significantly lower hemoglobin levels (107.48 ± 23.70 vs. 113.10 ± 23.93 g/L, p = 0.001), lower serum albumin (41.79 ± 5.22 vs. 43.03 ± 4.40 g/L, p < 0.001), lower serum iron (10.85 ± 5.74 vs. 12.26 ± 6.84 µmol/L, p = 0.002), and markedly higher median BNP levels (414.30 [IQR: 132.46–1466.23] pg/mL vs. 124.25 [IQR: 50.30–338.54] pg/mL, p < 0.001). Echocardiographically, patients with suspected PH had larger LAD (41.46 ± 4.93 vs. 36.06 ± 4.07 mm, p < 0.001) and lower EF (52.10 ± 8.56% vs. 58.30 ± 5.71%, p < 0.001). Central vein stent placement (6.7% vs. 3.0%, p = 0.014), thrombectomy history (8.9% vs. 4.5%, p = 0.013), and PTA history (33.7% vs. 23.1%, p = 0.001) were more prevalent in the suspected PH group. Discussion

In this large single-center cross-sectional study of 948 Chinese maintenance hemodialysis patients, the prevalence of echocardiographically suspected PH, defined by standardized echocardiographic criteria, was 29.7%. This rate aligns with contemporary echocardiographic estimates ranging from 18% to 50% in similar cohorts [17, 18], though it remains lower than some reports from non-Asian populations [19]. After strict multivariable adjustment—and the intentional removal of potentially circular right-heart parameters—we identified larger left atrial diameter (LAD), lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and a history of thrombectomy or PTA as robust, independent predictors of suspected PH.

The independent associations between suspected PH and both enlarged LAD and reduced LVEF highlight the central role of left-sided cardiac remodeling in this population. suspected PH in hemodialysis patients is predominantly classified as WHO Group 2 (pulmonary venous hypertension) [9, 10]. Enlarged LAD frequently serves as a surrogate marker for chronic volume overload and left ventricular diastolic dysfunction, which have been consistently linked to elevated pulmonary pressures. Similarly, a lower LVEF reflects systolic impairment that can retrogradely exacerbate pulmonary venous congestion. Importantly, by explicitly excluding right atrial dimensions from our predictive models, we avoided the circular reasoning of predicting a disease using its own supportive diagnostic criteria, thereby confirming that left-heart disease is a primary structural correlate of suspected PH in this cohort. Although the fundamental association between left-sided cardiac remodeling and elevated pulmonary pressures (WHO Group 2 physiology) is well recognized in patients with chronic volume overload, our study contributes several incremental advances. First, it provides contemporary large-scale data (n = 948) from a Chinese maintenance hemodialysis population, for which robust multivariable analyses using strictly objective left-heart predictors and deliberate avoidance of circular reasoning have been limited. Second, history of thrombectomy (OR 2.45) and percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (OR 1.51) emerged as independent predictors even after adjustment for left atrial diameter and ejection fraction. These vascular access interventions may serve as clinical markers of patients exposed to high-output arteriovenous access physiology, recurrent endothelial shear stress, or more severe systemic vasculopathy—factors that could contribute to pulmonary vascular remodeling. This aspect extends prior observations and warrants further mechanistic investigation.

A notable finding is the significant association between vascular access complications and suspected PH. Specifically, a history of thrombectomy emerged as a strong independent predictor (OR 2.450), with PTA demonstrating a similar independent association (OR 1.511). While previous literature has hypothesized that thrombectomy procedures might physically promote suspected PH through the dislodgement of microemboli and subsequent chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) [20], our cross-sectional design strictly precludes causal interpretations. Instead of a direct mechanical cause, we hypothesize that a history of recurrent thrombectomy or PTA more likely serves as a clinical marker for a broader, severe vascular disease burden or access dysfunction. Patients requiring frequent endovascular interventions may experience highly fluctuating, high-output arteriovenous flows that chronically induce endothelial shear stress and subsequent cardiopulmonary remodeling [11, 12]. This clinical hypothesis is supported by experimental models demonstrating that high pulmonary blood flow and inflammatory pathways directly drive progressive pulmonary vascular remodeling and associated fibrotic changes [21, 22].

Although univariate analysis revealed associations with lower hemoglobin, serum albumin, and serum iron, these nutritional and hematological variables did not retain independence in the forced-confounder multivariable model. This suggests that while anemia and malnutrition-inflammation complexes are highly prevalent in ESRD [23], their relationship with suspected PH may be heavily mediated or confounded by the primary structural cardiac and vascular abnormalities. Nevertheless, the fundamental role of iron metabolism in pulmonary hemodynamics remains a critical area of investigation, as recent evidence highlights its causal impact on hypoxic signaling and right-heart outcomes [24].

This study has several important limitations. First, the retrospective cross-sectional design inherently precludes causal inference; we can only report associations, and future longitudinal follow-up is required to understand the temporal sequence of access interventions and suspected PH development. Second, the identification of suspected PH relied on echocardiographic estimation of PASP rather than the gold-standard right heart catheterization (RHC). Although we applied a standardized, objective threshold (estimated PASP > 35 mmHg) and guideline-supported qualitative criteria when jets were unmeasurable, echocardiography remains an indirect surrogate influenced by volume status, acoustic windows, tricuspid regurgitation jet quality, and assumptions about right atrial pressure. This introduces potential misclassification bias. Consequently, our results identify factors associated with echocardiographic markers of elevated right-sided pressures and right heart strain probability, rather than confirmed pulmonary vascular disease or hemodynamically defined PH. Prospective studies incorporating selective RHC validation in high-risk subsets are needed to confirm the hemodynamic phenotype and to refine risk prediction. Third, unmeasured temporal confounders—such as contemporaneous arteriovenous fistula flow rates, strict volume status via bioimpedance analysis, and exact dialysis adequacy (Kt/V) at the precise time of the echocardiogram—were unavailable and may contribute to residual confounding. Finally, because our cohort consisted exclusively of maintenance hemodialysis patients, our data reflects cardiac status after the initiation of renal replacement therapy. A highly clinically relevant question for future research is whether at-risk patients can be identified prior to dialysis initiation. Prospective longitudinal studies incorporating pre-dialysis echocardiographic and hemodynamic baseline assessments are necessary to disentangle the effects of advanced uremia from the subsequent hemodynamic burdens introduced by arteriovenous access creation and maintenance dialysis. The single-center setting may also limit the generalizability of these findings.

From a clinical perspective, these results support a phenotype-based approach to risk stratification. Maintenance hemodialysis patients who exhibit left atrial enlargement, reduced ejection fraction, or a history of complex vascular access interventions represent a high-risk group that may benefit from intensified volume management, closer surveillance for progressive right heart strain, optimization of anemia and blood pressure control, and timely input from cardiology or vascular access specialists. In selected symptomatic patients, these echocardiographic findings could also prompt consideration of further diagnostic evaluation. However, because this study is cross-sectional, it cannot establish whether routine echocardiographic monitoring or phenotype-guided interventions improve hard clinical outcomes. Dedicated prospective cohort studies and interventional trials are essential to translate these associations into actionable strategies and to quantify their impact on hospitalization rates, quality of life, and survival.

Conclusion

In conclusion, echocardiographically suspected PH affects approximately 30% of Chinese maintenance hemodialysis patients. It is independently associated with objective markers of left-sided cardiac remodeling and a history of recurrent vascular access interventions. These factors identify a high-risk clinical phenotype that may warrant closer monitoring and optimized management of volume status and vascular access. Prospective studies are required to determine whether targeted interventions informed by this phenotype can improve patient outcomes.
