The impact of blood ammonia after interventional therapy on the prognosis of patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Zhou Liu, Zhong-Bin Li, Yun Zhao, Chang Guo, Meng-Qi Sun, Yi-Zhe Zhang, Jia-Min Cheng, Yi-Ming Fu, Yin-Ying Lu, Dong Ji, Xiao-Xia Niu, Jian-Jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and aimsHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors with a dismal prognosis. Interventional therapy serves as a main therapeutic approach for unresectable HCC. This study aimed to investigate the impact of post-interventional blood ammonia (BLA) levels on the prognosis of patients with unresectable HCC, to provide a reference for clinical risk stratification and individualized treatment.MethodsA retrospective study was conducted, enrolling patients with unresectable HCC admitted to Senior Department of Hepatology, Chinese PLA General Hospital from August 2022 to September 2025. According to the BLA levels within 1 week after interventional therapy, the patients were divided into the BLA < 50 μmol/L group and the BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L group. The clinical characteristics, interventional treatment modalities (hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy [HAIC] or transcatheter arterial chemoembolization [TACE]), and laboratory indicators of the patients were collected. The Kaplan-Meier method was used for survival analysis, and the Cox proportional hazards regression model was applied to analyze the prognostic factors.ResultsA total of 209 patients were enrolled in this study, including 178 males (85.2%); the mean age was 56.6 ± 9.5 years. By the last follow-up, 110 patients were still alive, and 99 had died. The objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) in the BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L group were significantly lower than those in the BLA < 50 μmol/L group after interventional therapy (ORR: 12.2% vs. 24.4%, p = 0.046; DCR: 28.1% vs. 48.1%, p = 0.004). Survival analysis showed that the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in the BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L group were significantly shortened (p < 0.05). Multivariable Cox regression analysis revealed that BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L (HR = 1.572, 95% CI: 1.049–2.356, p = 0.029) was an independent risk factor for prognosis, while treatment with HAIC (HR = 0.648, 95% CI: 0.423–0.993, p = 0.046) was an independent protective factor.ConclusionElevated BLA (≥ 50 μmol/L) after interventional therapy is an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in patients with unresectable HCC, and HAIC treatment may improve the survival of such patients.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a globally prevalent malignant tumor with insidious onset and rapid progression, which seriously threatens human health.1 As a high-prevalence area of hepatitis B virus infection, China accounts for more than half of the global incidence of HCC. Due to atypical early symptoms and the lack of specific screening indicators, more than 50% of patients with HCC are diagnosed at an advanced stage and lose the opportunity for radical surgical resection.2

For patients with unresectable advanced HCC, interventional therapies such as transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) and hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) are the preferred local treatment methods in clinical practice. They can accurately block tumor blood supply, inhibit tumor proliferation, effectively control the disease, prolong patient survival, and play a core role in comprehensive treatment.3, 4, 5 However, in clinical practice, there are significant differences in treatment efficacy and prognosis among patients after receiving the same interventional regimen. Some patients experience treatment resistance, recurrence, and metastasis, so there is an urgent need for simple and efficient early prognostic biomarkers to provide a basis for individualized treatment.

In recent years, in addition to traditional factors such as tumor size and alpha-fetoprotein, indicators reflecting liver function and systemic metabolism have attracted much attention. Blood ammonia (BLA) is mainly cleared through the hepatic urea cycle. In patients with chronic liver disease, due to impaired hepatocytes, BLA clearance is disturbed, leading to elevated BLA levels, which is the core inducement of hepatic encephalopathy. Recent studies have shown that BLA plays a complex role in the tumor microenvironment: tumor cells can use BLA to support proliferation and induce treatment resistance,6,7 and can also inhibit anti-tumor immunity and affect treatment efficacy.8

Interventional therapy (especially TACE) may aggravate hepatocyte damage due to the toxicity of chemotherapeutic drugs and the effect of embolization, affect BLA metabolism, and lead to dynamic changes in early post-interventional BLA levels. However, at present, there is a lack of systematic research evidence on the changing rules of early BLA after interventional therapy and its prognostic value for objective response rate, disease control rate, and long-term survival in patients with unresectable HCC. Based on this, this study retrospectively analyzed the correlation between early BLA levels after interventional therapy and the efficacy and prognosis of patients, so as to provide new ideas for clinical prognostic evaluation and individualized management.

Material and methods

Study subjects

This was a retrospective observational cohort study. We screened the medical records of patients with unresectable HCC admitted to Senior Department of Hepatology, Chinese PLA General Hospital from August 2022 to September 2025. Inclusion criteria: (1) Diagnosed as hepatitis B virus (HBV)-related HCC by clinical diagnostic criteria according to the “Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer” (2022 Edition)9 or confirmed by pathology; (2) Patients with BCLC B/C stage who had not received systemic treatment before, or had disease progression after first-line systemic treatment, or could not tolerate first-line systemic treatment; (3) Child-Pugh A/B class without hepatic encephalopathy; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group-performance status (ECOG-PS) score of 0‒2.10 (5) HBVDNA undetectable or < 102 IU/mL. Exclusion criteria: (1) Mixed HCC, cholangiocellular carcinoma, or combined with other active malignant tumors except HCC; (2) Complicated with severe dysfunction of important organs such as heart and kidney; (3) Complicated with other etiologies, such as hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, autoimmune liver diseases, drug-induced liver injury, alcoholic liver diseases, metabolic-associated fatty liver disease, or in the active stage of infection; (4) Incomplete important clinical data or missing follow-up records. This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital (No. KY-2025-11-206-1). The written informed consent for data collection was waived due to the retrospective study design. All study procedures were in line with the relevant requirements of medical ethics.

Sample size estimation

According to a previous research,3 the treatment response rate was 46% in the HAIC group or 18% in the TACE group. The significance level‌ was 0.05, the power of the test was 0.9, the ratio of the two groups was 1:1, and the number of cases in each group required 57 cases. Considering the 15% of lost rate, at least 68 cases in each group were needed.

Data collection

Baseline clinical data of enrolled patients were collected through the hospital electronic medical record system, including sex, age, body mass index (BMI), tumor-related characteristics (tumor diameter, number of lesions, extrahepatic metastasis, portal vein tumor thrombus), and interventional treatment methods (HAIC, TACE). Laboratory examination indicators included alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), albumin (ALB), total bilirubin (TBIL), prothrombin time (PT), platelet (PLT), BLA levels before interventional treatment, and within 1 week after treatment. BLA was detected by the dry chemistry method, with ‌a reference range of 0‒30 μmol/L.

Treatment regimens

All patients received HAIC or TACE interventional treatment according to their condition. HAIC was performed by percutaneous femoral artery puncture and catheterization, with the catheter placed in the tumor-feeding artery for continuous perfusion of chemotherapeutic drugs. TACE was performed by injecting a mixture of chemotherapeutic drugs and embolic agents into the tumor-feeding artery through a catheter to achieve a synergistic effect of chemotherapy and embolization. The selection and dosage of chemotherapeutic drugs for the two treatment regimens were individually formulated according to the patient's liver function, performance status, and other conditions. Specifically, HAIC was administered using the FOLFOX regimen (oxaliplatin/leucovorin/fluorouracil), whereas conventional TACE was performed using anthracyclines combined with platinum-based chemotherapeutic agents emulsified in ultra-fluid lipiodol for embolization of the tumor-feeding arteries, with supplementary proximal embolization using gelatin sponge particles when necessary. Some patients were treated with drug-eluting beads.

Follow-up and efficacy evaluation

Patients were followed up regularly every 3 months. The zero time was the day of interventional treatment, and the endpoint was the time of patient death or the last follow-up (February 2026). The main follow-up contents included patient survival status and tumor progression. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), which was defined as the time from interventional treatment to patient death or last follow-up; the secondary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS), which was defined as the time from interventional treatment to tumor progression, patient death, or last follow-up. The efficacy of interventional treatment was also evaluated according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST),11 which was divided into complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD).

Sensitivity analyses

To further confirm the research results, a sensitivity analysis was conducted using the propensity score matching (PSM) method. Firstly, the patients were grouped according to the BLA level or treatment modalities, respectively. Secondly, parameters with statistical differences between the two groups were matched by PSM (at a 1:1 ratio) to achieve a balance between the two groups. Lastly, the time-event analyses (Kaplan-Meier curve and Log-rank test) were conducted.

Statistical analyses

R software (version 4.0.4, R Foundation Inc., Vienna, Austria. https://www.r-project.org/) was used for data processing and analysis. Measurement data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and an independent samples t-test was used for comparison between groups; measurement data not conforming to normal distribution were expressed as median [interquartile range], and a Mann-Whitney U test was used for comparison between groups. Count data were expressed as frequency (percentage) [n(%)], and the χ2 test was used for comparison between groups. The Cochran-Armitage trend test was adopted to analyze the mRECIST data. The maximum selected rank statistics (MSRS) were used to determine the optimal prognostic cut-off value of post-interventional BLA. Kaplan-Meier curve, Log-rank test, and Cox proportional hazards regression. The ANOVA and pairwise comparisons were performed to analyze the repeated measures data. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to draw survival curves, and the Log-rank test was used to compare survival differences between different groups. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to analyze the risk factors affecting the prognosis of patients. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Treatment efficacy by mRECIST criteria

The Cochran-Armitage trend test showed that patients in the BLA < 50 μmol/L group (p for trend = 0.006) or HAIC treatment group (p for trend = 0.016) achieved significantly better radiological response assessed by mRECIST criteria (Fig. 3A). The objective response rate (ORR) or disease control rate (DCR) in the BLA < 50 μmol/L group were 24.4% or 48.1%, respectively, which was significantly higher than 12.2% or 28.1% in the BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L group (ORR: p = 0.046; DCR: p = 0.006). Regarding interventional treatment, the ORR or DCR in the HAIC group were 26.7% or 51.1%, which was also significantly higher than 14.3% or 31.9% in the TACE group (ORR: p = 0.040, DCR: p = 0.008, Fig. 3B and C).Fig. 3Treatment Efficacy by mRECIST Criteria. Cox regression analyses

Based on the preliminary comparison results of the above clinical characteristics, we further used the Cox proportional hazards regression model to systematically analyze the key factors that may affect the prognosis of HCC patients after interventional treatment from both univariable and multivariable levels, to exclude the interference of confounding factors and identify independent prognostic factors. The results showed that post-interventional BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L was an independent risk factor affecting the prognosis of patients (HR = 1.572, 95% CI: 1.049–2.356, p = 0.029), indicating that patients with elevated post-interventional BLA levels had a poorer prognosis; while receiving HAIC treatment was an independent protective factor for patient prognosis (HR = 0.648, 95% CI: 0.423–0.993, p = 0.046), suggesting that HAIC treatment may help improve the survival outcome of patients. TBIL, PT, and ALB were not associated with prognosis in such a clinical situation. Discussion

Regarding treatment of HCC, interventional therapies represented by TACE and HAIC have become the cornerstone treatment options for unresectable HCC.1,12, 13, 14 This study focused on the relationship between metabolic changes after interventional treatment and prognosis through a retrospective analysis. A core finding was that the early-elevated BLA level after HAIC or TACE was significantly associated with poor prognosis, and HAIC might improve the survival of such patients compared to TACE. Most traditional baseline characteristics, such as sex, age, BMI, and tumor diameter, did not show significant prognostic predictive value in this cohort.

This study observed that although there was no difference in baseline BLA between the survival group and the death group, within one week after interventional treatment, the BLA level in the death group was significantly higher than that in the survival group, and the proportion of patients with BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L was significantly higher in the death group (52.5% vs. 28.2%). This phenomenon suggests that elevated post-interventional BLA may be a dynamic event related to treatment response or systemic stress. Subsequent survival analysis confirmed that BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L after interventional treatment was an independent risk factor for shortened OS and PFS in patients with unresectable HCC (HR = 1.572, 95% CI: 1.049–2.356, p = 0.029). Efficacy evaluation also showed that the ORR and DCR in the high BLA group were significantly lower than those in the low BLA group. These consistent results indicated that post-interventional BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L was closely associated with poorer short-term efficacy and long-term survival.

Ammonia is a nitrogenous waste product produced by amino acid metabolism, which is mainly detoxified through the hepatic urea cycle.15 In cases of impaired liver function, such as cirrhosis, ammonia clearance disorders can lead to hyperammonemia, which in turn causes complications such as hepatic encephalopathy.16 However, the association between hyperammonemia and poor prognosis observed in this study may go beyond the scope of traditional liver insufficiency. Recent studies have shown that ammonia plays a more complex role in the tumor microenvironment. Some malignant tumors can reuse ammonia and integrate it into biosynthetic pathways, thereby promoting tumor growth and inducing treatment resistance.17, 18, 19 In addition, ammonia can induce T cell metabolic reprogramming, leading to their functional exhaustion, which may further weaken the efficacy of immunotherapy.20,21 In HCC, ammonia is also considered to be related to the maintenance of tumor stemness and the enhancement of metastatic potential.22,23 A 2024 study also suggested that elevated BLA can serve as a predictive indicator for high incidence risk of HCC.24 Meanwhile, hyperammonemia exhibits potent hepatotoxicity, which exacerbates hepatocellular damage and inflammatory necrosis of the hepatic parenchyma induced by interventional embolization and chemotherapeutic agents, thereby contributing to progressive liver function deterioration and disrupting hepatic metabolic and detoxifying homeostasis. Furthermore, elevated BLA impairs gastrointestinal peristalsis and intestinal flora stability. Combined with stress-induced hyperactive protein catabolism and increased endogenous ammonia production post-interventional, a vicious cycle of “liver injury-hyperammonemia-progressive hepatic dysfunction” is formed, which continuously depletes the residual functional reserve of the liver, which struggles to cope with interventional stress, making high-risk patients more vulnerable. Therefore, the post-interventional hyperammonemia observed in this study may not only be a passive reflection of insufficient liver function compensation, but also an active signal of aggressive biological behavior of tumors and an imbalanced host anti-tumor immune metabolic microenvironment, which provides a potential pathophysiological basis for explaining why it is associated with a lower treatment response rate and shorter survival time.

Another important conclusion of this study is that HAIC treatment was an independent protective factor for prognosis (HR = 0.648, p = 0.044) when compared with TACE. This advantage was consistently reflected in efficacy and survival data: the ORR, DCR, OS, and PFS of the HAIC group were significantly better than those of the TACE group. Although TACE is currently the mainstream local therapy, it often faces problems such as uneven efficacy and post-embolization injury.25 Its mechanism mainly relies on the embolization of tumor-feeding arteries, while HAIC can maintain a high and sustained local drug concentration in tumors through continuous arterial perfusion, which may be more advantageous for patients with portal vein tumor thrombus or multiple lesions.26 Recent studies have also supported that oxaliplatin-based HAIC combination regimens can improve the survival of patients with advanced HCC.27 The beneficial mechanism of HAIC may lie in its ability to achieve potent local treatment while minimizing embolic injury to normal liver tissue, thereby better protecting residual liver function and metabolic reserve.28

From the perspective of clinical practice, for patients with preoperative liver function in a critical state (such as lower preoperative ALB and higher TBIL in the death group of this study) or complicated with obvious portal hypertension, prioritizing the HAIC regimen, which has a milder impact on liver function, may help reduce the risk of metabolic disorders such as post-interventional liver insufficiency and hyperammonemia. This clinical selection idea is consistent with the better efficacy and survival outcomes of patients in the HAIC group in this study, which is worthy of further exploration and verification.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the single-center retrospective observational design may introduce selection bias, the preliminary results of the current merely provided the clues for further research, whether targeted interventions aiming to reduce hyperammonemia can effectively improve clinical prognosis remains undetermined and requires further validation through prospective interventional studies; secondly, the sample size is limited, especially in the high BLA subgroup, making it impossible to construct a prognostic prediction score; thirdly, changes in AFP and blood urea were not detected, making deep analysis not impossible; lastly, although the intrinsic mechanism links between interventional treatment and BLA metabolism was negative in the current study, their interaction is still needed to be explored.

Conclusions

This study shows that early BLA ≥ 50 μmol/L after interventional treatment is a strong independent predictor of poor prognosis in patients with unresectable HCC, which is significantly associated with a low treatment response rate and short survival time. At the same time, HAIC treatment shows potential advantages over traditional TACE in improving patient efficacy and survival. In clinical practice, it is recommended to routinely monitor post-interventional BLA levels in HCC patients receiving interventional treatment, and for patients who reach or exceed this threshold, comprehensive interventions, including nutritional management and drug ammonia reduction, should be actively adopted.
