Exceptional Points as Manifestations of Topological-Charge Breakdown in a Non-Hermitian Skyrmion
Kejun Liu

Abstract
The integer topological charge of a magnetic skyrmion is the standard emblem of topological protection. We ask what happens to that protection when the magnet is made non-Hermitian, with balanced gain and loss or a 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} -symmetric anisotropy. A non-Hermitian skyrmion carries two charges that coincide in the Hermitian limit but part ways under deformation. The charge built from the right state alone is homotopy-protected: the 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} flow reduces exactly to a Gilbert-type relaxation on the target sphere, so it cannot change under smooth evolution. The charge built from the biorthogonal left–right pair is complex, loses quantization as soon as the gain/loss is turned on, and breaks down at the exceptional point of the local generator—a ring on the skyrmion’s equator, where the biorthogonal Bloch field itself diverges. Topological protection of a skyrmion is therefore not a single statement once the dynamics is non-Hermitian: it splits at an exceptional point. This is the real-space topological counterpart of the analyticity breakdown a causal response function suffers at an exceptional point, both manifestations of the same non-Hermitian degeneracy.

The right-state charge is homotopy-protected.
—Consider the canonical

This does not preserve

The two charges, equal in the Hermitian limit, thus have opposite fates at the EP: the right-state
charge of a smooth time-evolved texture is homotopy-protected, while the biorthogonal eigenstate
charge loses quantization where its underlying Bloch field diverges. “The skyrmion charge is
topologically protected” is no longer unambiguous once the magnet is non-Hermitian—it depends on
which charge one means, and the physical, biorthogonal one is fragile precisely where the texture

Discussion.
—The picture sharpens what “topological protection” means for an open or
non-Hermitian magnet. Homotopy protects the right-state charge of a smooth time-evolved texture: a
degree of a map that never leaves the sphere cannot change. The biorthogonal charge behaves
differently—it equals that integer only when Hermitian, drifts off as soon as gain/loss is turned
on, and loses quantization where left and right states fail to be independent. The skyrmion’s
equator, where the magnetization turns in plane, is the first place this happens, switched on when
the gain/loss reaches the texture’s own scale,
\gamma=h
. For the CP
N-1
fractonic conservation
laws, this means the right-state charge—and the fractonic immobility built on it—survives full
non-Hermiticity, while the biorthogonal sector is where new physics, such as EP-mediated single-
skyrmion nucleation at a threshold gain/loss, could enter
1
.

The result completes a parallel worth stating. In the ’t Hooft model an imaginary
\mathcal{PT}

deformation sends a causal response function to an EP where its analyticity, and the
Kramers–Kronig relations that encode causality, break down
15
; here the same kind
of deformation sends a skyrmion to an EP where its biorthogonal charge loses quantization. A
response function losing analyticity and a texture losing its quantized charge are two
manifestations of one EP mechanism—one in the analytic structure of a Green’s function, the other
in the homotopy class of a real-space field.

Experimental observability.
—Each charge maps to a different handle. The right-state charge

Q_{R}
is the standard winding number, read out by Lorentz microscopy, magneto-optical Kerr, or
polarized neutrons. The exceptional ring of Fig.
1
(a) is a spatially resolved spectral
feature, predicting a locally resolvable collapse of the phase rigidity on the equator—an anomaly
in the local density of states or spin-resonance frequency detectable by spin-polarized STM or a
nitrogen-vacancy sensor. The biorthogonal charge
Q_{B}
needs both eigenstates; reciprocity relates
the left eigenstate to the far-field radiation pattern, so
Q_{B}
is in principle accessible through
far-field polarimetry, as in recent measurements of the non-Hermitian Berry curvature and quantum
geometric tensor
25
;
10
. Concretely, optical-skyrmion microcavities and finite
non-Hermitian photonic lattices reach the EP when the per-mode gain/loss equals the texture exchange
scale,
\gamma\simeq h

7
;
24
;
12
; in magnonic
\mathcal{PT}
films this is a
damping/anisotropy ratio of order unity
5
. In the polariton case the linear model used
here is the small-signal limit of a nonlinear system
17
. The explicit results are for
CP
1
; the general CP
N-1
statements, a fully dynamical account, and a lattice realization of
the equatorial ring are natural next steps.
