BV quantization of ϕ 3 \phi^{3} -theory on λ \lambda -Minkowski space: Tree-level correlation functions
R. J. Szabo

Abstract
We review the quantization of scalar field theory on λ \lambda -Minkowski space using the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism. We consider ϕ 3 \phi^{3} -theory in two different quantization schemes: standard and braided. While standard BV quantization is based on an ordinary L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra, braided BV quantization is based on a braided L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra. We compare the tree-level three-point and four-point correlation functions in the two approaches. For the four-point function, standard quantization leads to two inequivalent classes of diagrams with different noncommutative contributions, whereas braided quantization yields only a single class of diagrams with noncommutativity entering solely through an overall phase factor depending on the external momenta.

1
Introduction

A systematic way to construct noncommutative deformations of spacetime together with their deformed symmetry structures is provided by the Drinfel’d twist formalism
[
1
]
. In this approach, the twist deforms the Hopf algebra of spacetime symmetries by modifying its coproducts, and hence the Leibniz rule for the action of symmetry generators on products of fields. At the same time, it induces a deformation of the algebra of functions through a star-product. The twist formalism allows one to formulate noncommutative field theories with well-controlled twisted symmetries. Besides the canonical Moyal deformation, it also naturally accommodates other noncommutative spacetimes with twisted Poincaré invariance
[
2
]
.

One of the most prominent characteristic features of noncommutative quantum field theories is the appearance of ultraviolet/infrared (UV/IR) mixing, whereby ultraviolet divergences reemerge as infrared singularities in loop diagrams
[
3
,
4
]
. This phenomenon, first observed for scalar field theories on Moyal space, remains one of the central obstacles in the construction of renormalizable noncommutative quantum field theories.

In this contribution we focus on
\lambda
-Minkowski space, which arises from an angular Drinfel’d twist and provides an example of a Lie algebraic noncommutative deformation of Minkowski space
[
5
]
. Scalar field theory on this spacetime was previously studied using standard Feynman quantization and the conventional plane wave basis for the Fourier decomposition of fields, where noncommutativity manifests itself through a deformed momentum composition law and leads to nontrivial quantum effects, including UV/IR mixing
[
6
]
.

More recently, an alternative algebraic approach to quantization based on homotopy algebra methods and the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formalism was developed for noncommutative field theories
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. A key outcome of this framework is that the same classical noncommutative scalar theory can be encoded by two different homotopy algebraic structures
[
12
]
: an ordinary
L_{\infty}
-algebra, leading to standard BV quantization, and a braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra, leading to braided BV quantization. In standard BV quantization, one recovers the usual noncommutative scalar field theory with nonplanar diagrams and UV/IR mixing. In braided BV quantization, noncommutativity is incorporated directly into the braided homotopy algebraic structure, leading to the absence of nonplanar diagrams and therefore the absence of the UV/IR mixing. These two quantization schemes were investigated in detail for
\phi^{3}
-theory on
\lambda
-Minkowski space in
[
11
]
.

Here we present a brief review of this construction, concentrating on the simplest correlation functions that already capture the differences between the two quantization schemes. We discuss the two
L_{\infty}
-algebraic descriptions of the noncommutative
\phi^{3}
-theory, comparing the resulting tree-level three-point and four-point correlation functions. These examples complement the one-loop analysis of the two-point function from
[
11
]
. They provide a transparent illustration of how the standard and braided BV formalisms result in two different quantum field theories, even though they originate from the same classical action.

The rest of this contribution is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review the two
L_{\infty}
-algebra descriptions of
\phi^{3}
-theory on
\lambda
-Minkowski space and explain how they lead to the standard and braided BV quantization schemes. In Section
3
we compute the tree-level three-point and four-point correlation functions in both approaches, and discuss their properties. In Section
4
we conclude by summarizing the main results and comment on possible directions for future work.

2
\boldsymbol{\phi^{3}}
-theory on
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
-Minkowski space

Consider four-dimensional Minkowski space
\mathbbm{R}^{1,3}
with Cartesian coordinates
(t,x,y,z)
, where
t
is time. For a deformation parameter
\lambda\in\mathbbm{R}
, the
\lambda
-Minkowski space is defined by the angular Drinfel’d twist
[
5
]

{\cal F}=\exp\left(-\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda}{2}\,(\partial_{z}\otimes\partial_{\varphi}-\partial_{\varphi}\otimes\partial_{z})\right)\ .

(2.1)

The commuting vector fields
\partial_{z}
and
\partial_{\varphi}=x\,\partial_{y}-y\,\partial_{x}
are the generators of translations in the
z
-direction and rotations around the
z
-axis, respectively.

The pointwise product between functions
\mu(f\otimes g)=f\cdot g
is replaced by the noncomutative star-product, defined via

\displaystyle\begin{split}f\star g&=\mu\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}(f\otimes g)\\[4.0pt]
&=f\cdot g+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\bigg(\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda}{2}\bigg)^{n}\ \sum_{k=0}^{n}\,\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!\,(n-k)!}\,\partial_{z}^{n-k}\,\partial_{\varphi}^{k}f\cdot\partial_{\varphi}^{n-k}\,\partial_{z}^{k}g\ .\end{split}

(2.2)

The braided commutativity of the star-product (
2.2
) is expressed via the triangular
R
-matrix

\displaystyle f\star g

\displaystyle=\mu\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}\,{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(g\otimes f)=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(g)\star\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(f)\ ,

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{2}=:\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ .

(2.4)

The twist (
2.1
) deforms the standard Poincaré Hopf algebra of symmetries into the twisted Poincaré Hopf algebra. In particular, the coproduct of translation generators, i.e. of Cartesian momentum operators
P_{\mu}
, is deformed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm{\Delta}}_{\cal F}(P_{t})&=P_{t}\otimes 1+1\otimes P_{t}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
{\rm{\Delta}}_{\cal F}(P_{z})&=P_{z}\otimes 1+1\otimes P_{z}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
{\rm{\Delta}}_{\cal F}(P_{x})&=P_{x}\otimes\cos\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)+\cos\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)\otimes P_{x}+P_{y}\otimes\sin\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)-\sin\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)\otimes P_{y}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
{\rm{\Delta}}_{\cal F}(P_{y})&=P_{y}\otimes\cos\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)+\cos\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)\otimes P_{y}-P_{x}\otimes\sin\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)+\sin\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,P_{z}\big)\otimes P_{x}\ .\end{split}

(2.5)

We will see later that this deformation results in a deformation of the conservation of momentum in vertices and correlation functions.

Using the star-product (
2.2
) instead of the pointwise product leads to the action for the standard noncommutative cubic scalar field theory on four-dimensional Minkowski space, which can be written as

S_{\rm cl}=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,{\rm d}^{4}x\ \Big(\frac{1}{2}\,\phi\,\big(-\square-m^{2}\big)\,\phi-\frac{g}{3!}\,\phi\star\phi\star\phi\Big)\ .

(2.6)

In what follows we show that this action functional is reproduced by two different cyclic
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

2.1
Standard
\boldsymbol{L_{\infty}}
-algebra

The underlying vector space of the standard
L_{\infty}
-algebra of noncommutative scalar field theory with cubic interaction is the graded vector space
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, consisting of the space of fields
\phi\in V_{1}=C^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}^{4})
and the space of antifields
\phi^{+}\in V_{2}=C^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}^{4})
. This vector space is equipped with the pair of nontrivial brackets

\displaystyle\mu_{1}:V_{1}\longrightarrow V_{2}\quad,\quad\mu_{2}:V_{1}\otimes V_{1}\longrightarrow V_{2}\ ,

(2.7)

acting on elements
\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in V_{1}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{1}(\phi)&=-(\Box+m^{2})\,\phi\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mu_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})&=\frac{g}{2}\,(\phi_{1}\star\phi_{2}+\phi_{2}\star\phi_{1})\ .\end{split}

(2.8)

The binary bracket
\mu_{2}
is strictly symmetric,
\mu_{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\mu_{2}(\phi_{2},\phi_{1})
. The noncommutative deformation enters through the star-product between the fields
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
.

To formulate the action functional and the action principle, a strictly graded symmetric cyclic pairing of degree
-3
is defined by the integral

\langle\phi,\phi^{+}\rangle=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,{\rm d}^{4}x\ \phi\star\phi^{+}=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,{\rm d}^{4}x\ \phi\cdot\phi^{+}\ .

(2.9)

Using this data, we can write the classical action as the homotopy Maurer–Cartan functional

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{\rm cl}&=\frac{1}{2!}\,\langle\phi,\mu_{1}(\phi)\rangle-\frac{1}{3!}\,\langle\phi,\mu_{2}(\phi,\phi)\rangle\\[4.0pt]
&=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,{\rm d}^{4}x\ \Big(\frac{1}{2}\,\phi\,\big(-\square-m^{2}\big)\,\phi-\frac{g}{3!}\,\phi\star\phi\star\phi\Big)\ ,\end{split}

(2.10)

and the corresponding equation of motion as the Maurer–Cartan equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{\phi}&=\mu_{1}(\phi)-\frac{1}{2!}\,\mu_{2}(\phi,\phi)\\[4.0pt]
&=\big(-\square-m^{2}\big)\,\phi-\frac{g}{2}\,\phi\star\phi=0\end{split}

(2.11)

of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra.
As expected, the standard noncommutative action (
2.6
) is recovered.

Next we choose the plane wave basis for the Fourier decomposition of fields and antifields in
V
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathtt{e}_{k}(x)&=\mathtt{e}_{(E,k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})}(t,x,y,z):={\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot x}\ \in\ V_{1}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathtt{e}^{k}(x)&=\mathtt{e}_{(-E,-k_{x},-k_{y},-k_{z})}(t,x,y,z):={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k\cdot x}\ \in\ V_{2}\ .\end{split}

(2.12)

These basis vectors are dual with respect to the cyclic structure (
2.9
):

\displaystyle\langle\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\,,\,\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\rangle=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}x\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,(k_{1}-k_{2})\cdot x}=(2\pi)^{4}\ \delta(k_{1}-k_{2})\ .

Following
[
11
]
the corresponding interacting BV master action is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int}=\int_{k_{1}}\ \int_{k_{2}}\ \int_{k_{3}}\ V_{\tt pl}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\ \mathtt{e}^{k_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{k_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{k_{3}}\ ,

(2.13)

with the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\int_{k}:=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ \ ,

(2.14)

and the vertex

\displaystyle V_{\tt pl}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=-\frac{g}{3!}\ (2\pi)^{4}\ \delta(k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2}+\star k_{3})\ .

(2.15)

The deformed momentum conservation laws implied by (
2.5
) are implemented by the Dirac distribution

\displaystyle\delta(k_{1}+_{\star}\cdots+_{\star}k_{n})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\langle\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}},\mathtt{e}_{k_{2}}\star\cdots\star\mathtt{e}_{k_{n}}\rangle=\int_{\mathbbm{R}^{4}}\,\frac{{\rm d}^{4}x}{(2\pi)^{4}}\ \mathtt{e}_{k_{1}}\star\cdots\star\mathtt{e}_{k_{n}}\ .

(2.16)

By the cyclicity property of (
2.9
), it satisfies

\displaystyle\delta(k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2}+_{\star}\cdots+_{\star}k_{n})=\delta(k_{2}+_{\star}\cdots+_{\star}k_{n}+_{\star}k_{1})=\delta\big(k_{1}+(k_{2}+_{\star}\cdots+_{\star}k_{n})\big)\ .

(2.17)

In particular,
\delta(k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2})=\delta(k_{1}+k_{2})
. Then it is clear that the vertex (
2.15
) is cyclic:

V_{\tt pl}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=V_{\tt pl}(k_{2},k_{3},k_{1})=V_{\tt pl}(k_{3},k_{1},k_{2})\ .

(2.18)

Explicitly, the star-sums of two and three momenta read as
[
6
]

\displaystyle k+_{\star}p

\displaystyle={\rm{\Lambda}}(p_{z})\,k+{\rm{\Lambda}}(-k_{z})\,p\ ,

(2.19)

\displaystyle k+_{\star}p+_{\star}q

\displaystyle={\rm{\Lambda}}(p_{z}+q_{z})\,k+{\rm{\Lambda}}(-k_{z}+q_{z})\,p+{\rm{\Lambda}}(-k_{z}-p_{z})\,q\ ,

(2.20)

where the rotation matrix

{\rm{\Lambda}}(p_{z})={\small\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&\cos\big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{z}\big)&\sin\big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{z}\big)&0\\
0&-\sin\big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{z}\big)&\cos\big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{z}\big)&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)}\normalsize

(2.21)

implements a rotation through angle
\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{z}
in the
(x,y)
-plane of momentum space. While the star-sum does not affect the components
(E,k_{z})
, it modifies the addition law of the components
(k_{x},k_{y})
.

The plane wave basis (
2.12
) is not a natural basis for the twist (
2.1
). Namely, the star-product of two plane waves is no longer a plane wave:

{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{1}\cdot x}\star{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2}\cdot x}=\mathtt{e}_{k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2}}(x)={\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,(k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2})\cdot x}\ ,

(2.22)

with
k_{1}+_{\star}k_{2}
defined in (
2.19
). Since the BV quantization of
\phi^{3}_{\star}
-theory will be based on the standard
L_{\infty}
-algebra (
2.8
), there is no preferred basis and we can continue working with plane waves. This will change in the braided BV quantization of
\phi^{3}_{\star}
-theory.

To prepare all the necessary tools for calculating correlation functions, in addition to the interacting BV master action (
2.13
) we now calculate the tree-level two-point function, i.e. the propagator, in the BV formalism following
[
11
]
. Starting from the Poincaré-invariant Feynman propagator and its momentum space representation,

\mathsf{G}=-\frac{1}{\square+m^{2}+{\mathrm{i}}\,\epsilon}\quad,\quad\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(k)=\frac{1}{k^{2}-m^{2}-{\mathrm{i}}\,\epsilon}\ ,

(2.23)

with
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
and
k^{2}=E^{2}-k_{x}^{2}-k_{y}^{2}-k_{z}^{2}
, we define the contracting homotopy
{\sf h}:V_{2}\longrightarrow V_{1}
by

\displaystyle{\sf h}(\mathtt{e}^{k})=-\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(k)\ \mathtt{e}^{k}\ .

(2.24)

The tree-level two-point function
\widetilde{G}_{2}(p_{1},p_{2})^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}
follows as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{G}_{2}(p_{1},p_{2})^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}&=-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\\[4.0pt]
&=-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\langle\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\,,{\sf h}(\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}})\rangle\\[4.0pt]
&={\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,(2\pi)^{4}\ \delta(p_{1}+_{\star}p_{2})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{1})\\[4.0pt]
&={\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,(2\pi)^{4}\ \delta(p_{1}+p_{2})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{1})\ ,\end{split}

(2.25)

where
{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}
is the BV Laplacian and
\mathsf{H}
denotes the “thickened” extension of
{\sf h}
to symmetric products of antifields, see
[
11
]
for details.

Using (
2.13
) and (
2.25
), in Section
3
we will calculate the tree-level three-point and four-point correlation functions.

2.2
Braided
\boldsymbol{L_{\infty}}
-algebra

As in the case of the standard
L_{\infty}
-algebra of
\phi^{3}_{\star}
-theory, the underlying braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra of the scalar field theory with cubic interaction is the graded vector space
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
, consisting of the space of fields
V_{1}=C^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}^{4})
and the space of antifields
V_{2}=C^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}^{4})
. Again, the only nontrivial action of brackets is on fields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{1}^{{\mathcal{R}}}(\phi)&=\mu_{1}(\phi)=-(\Box+m^{2})\,\phi\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mu_{2}^{{\mathcal{R}}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})&=g\,\phi_{1}\star\phi_{2}\ ,\end{split}

(2.26)

but the binary bracket is now braided symmetric,
\mu_{2}^{{\mathcal{R}}}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=\mu_{2}^{\mathcal{R}}\big(\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\phi_{2}),\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\phi_{1})\big)
. More details on the construction and properties of braided
L_{\infty}
-algebras can be found in
[
13
,
12
]
.

The strictly graded symmetric pairing is the same as in (
2.9
), and the classical action and the corresponding equation of motion are the same as in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
).

However, the braided
L_{\infty}
-algebra formalism is adapted to the braided (deformed) underlying Poincaré symmetry, and the plane wave basis is no longer suitable since it does not diagonalize the twist (
2.1
): the star-product of two plane waves is no longer a plane wave, cf. (
2.22
). The basis adapted to the deformed Poincaré symmetry is written in cylindrical coordinates and is given by the cylindrical harmonics

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathtt{c}_{k}(x)&=\mathtt{c}_{(E,\alpha,\ell,k_{z})}(t,r,\varphi,z):=J_{\ell}(\alpha\,r)\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell\,\varphi}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,E\,t+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{z}\,z}\ \in\ V_{1}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathtt{c}^{k}(x)&=\mathtt{c}_{(-E,\alpha,-\ell,-k_{z})}(t,r,\varphi,z):=J_{\ell}(\alpha\,r)\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell\,\varphi}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,E\,t-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{z}\,z}\ \in\ V_{2}\ ,\end{split}

(2.27)

where
J_{\ell}
are the cylindrical Bessel functions.
The variables
k=(E,\alpha,\ell,k_{z})
have the following physical interpretation:
E\in\mathbbm{R}
is the energy and
k_{z}\in\mathbbm{R}
is the linear momentum in the
z
-direction, just as before. The variable
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
is the magnitude of the radial momentum in the
(x,y)
-plane, and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
is the angular momentum about the
z
-axis, that is, an eigenvalue of the operator
P_{\varphi}=-{\mathrm{i}}\,\partial_{\varphi}
.

Using the orthogonality relation for cylindrical Bessel functions

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,r\,\mathrm{d}r\ J_{\ell}(\alpha_{1}\,r)\,J_{-\ell}(\alpha_{2}\,r)=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\alpha_{1}}\ \delta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})\ ,

(2.28)

it is easy to check that the basis vectors (
2.27
) are indeed dual:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mathtt{c}_{k_{1}}\,,\mathtt{c}^{k_{2}}\rangle&=\int_{\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}}\,{\rm d}t\ {\rm d}z\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\,r\,{\rm d}r\ \int_{0}^{2\pi}\,{\rm d}\varphi\ J_{\ell_{1}}(\alpha_{1}\,r)\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{1}\,\varphi}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,E_{1}\,t}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{1z}\,z}\\[-8.0pt]
&\hskip 170.71652pt\times\,J_{\ell_{2}}(\alpha_{2}\,r)\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{2}\,\varphi}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,E_{2}\,t}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{2z}\,z}\\[4.0pt]
&=(2\pi)^{3}\ \delta(E_{1}-E_{2})\ \delta(k_{1z}-k_{2z})\ \delta_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\ \frac{\delta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{\alpha_{1}}\ .\end{split}

(2.29)

Following
[
11
]
the corresponding braided interacting BV master action is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\rm int}^{{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{k_{1}}\hskip-14.22636pt\mbox{$\sum$}\ \ \int_{k_{2}}\hskip-14.22636pt\mbox{$\sum$}\ \ \int_{k_{3}}\hskip-14.22636pt\mbox{$\sum$}\ \ V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\ \mathtt{c}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{k_{3}}\ ,

(2.30)

where we use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\int_{k}\hskip-11.38109pt\mbox{$\sum$}\ :=\int_{\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}}\,\frac{{\rm d}E\,{\rm d}z}{(2\pi)^{2}}\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\,\alpha\,{\rm d}\alpha\ \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\ \ ,

(2.31)

and we introduced the braided symmetric tensor product
\odot_{\mathcal{R}}=\odot\circ\mathcal{F}^{-1}
with

\mathtt{c}^{k_{1}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{k_{2}}=\mathrm{R}_{\alpha}(\mathtt{c}^{k_{2}})\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}(\mathtt{c}^{k_{1}})={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda\,(k_{1z}\,\ell_{2}-k_{2z}\,\ell_{1})}\ \mathtt{c}^{k_{2}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{k_{1}}\ .

(2.32)

The vertex
V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
is defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})&=-\frac{g}{3!}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda}{2}\,\sum\limits_{a<b}\,(k_{az}\,\ell_{b}-k_{bz}\,\ell_{a})}\ (2\pi)^{3}\ \delta(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})\ \delta(k_{1z}+k_{2z}+k_{3z})\\[-5.0pt]
&\hskip 170.71652pt\times\delta_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3},0}\ \mathtt{F}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\ .\end{split}

(2.33)

The function
\mathtt{F}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
is defined as an integral of the product of three cylindrical Bessel functions:

\mathtt{F}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,r\,{\rm d}r\ J_{\ell_{1}}(\alpha_{1}\,r)\ J_{\ell_{2}}(\alpha_{2}\,r)\ J_{\ell_{3}}(\alpha_{3}\,r)\ .

(2.34)

This integral can be computed explicitly
[
15
,
14
]
provided that
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}=0
, which is satisfied here due to angular momentum conservation, and it is nontrivial when the radial momenta satisfy the triangle condition. The interaction vertex is formally similar to the braided interaction vertex in the Moyal case
[
9
]
, due to the choice of basis adapted to the symmetry of the twist. The vertex (
2.33
) is braided symmetric:

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda\,(k_{1z}\,\ell_{2}-k_{2z}\,\ell_{1})}\ V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{2},k_{1},k_{3})={\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda\,(k_{2z}\,\ell_{3}-k_{3z}\,\ell_{2})}\ V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{3},k_{2})\ ,

(2.35)

and cyclic:

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle=V_{\mathcal{R}}(k_{2},k_{3},k_{1})\ .

(2.36)

The cylindrical harmonics also diagonalize the Klein–Gordan operator and hence the Feynman propagator (
2.23
),

\displaystyle{\sf h}(\mathtt{c}^{k})=-\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(k)\ \mathtt{c}^{k}\ ,

(2.37)

with
k^{2}=E^{2}-\alpha^{2}-p_{z}^{2}
.
The tree-level two-point function is now given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{C}_{2}(p_{1},p_{2})^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}&=-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{c}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{c}^{p_{2}})\\[4.0pt]
&=-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,\langle\mathtt{c}^{p_{1}}\,,{\sf h}(\mathtt{c}^{p_{2}})\rangle\\[4.0pt]
&={\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,(2\pi)^{3}\,(-1)^{\ell_{1}}\ \delta(E_{1}+E_{2})\ \delta(p_{1z}+p_{2z})\ \delta_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2},0}\ \frac{\delta(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{\alpha_{1}}\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{1})\ .\end{split}

(2.38)

3
Tree-level correlation functions

In this contribution we focus on tree-level correlation functions. We present and compare results for the tree-level three-point and four-point functions in two different BV formalisms: the standard BV quantization and braided BV quantization. These computations complement the one-loop calculations performed in
[
11
]
, where more details can be found.

3.1
Standard Batalin–Vilkovisky quantization

Since the standard BV quantization is based on the standard
L_{\infty}
-algebra, there are no restrictions on the basis of fields we should use and we continue working with the plane wave basis, defined in (
2.12
).

Three-point function.

We start from the definition of the tree-level three-point correlation function

\widetilde{G}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})^{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}=\mathsf{P}\,\big(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}-\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\big)^{3}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{3}})\ ,

(3.1)

where
\mathsf{P}
is the projection to
\mathbbm{C}
and
\{-,-\}
denotes the BV antibracket, see
[
11
]
for details. Extracting only one-particle irreducible contributions

\widetilde{G}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}_{{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}}=-(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\big\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{3}})\big\}\,\Big|_{{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}}\ ,

(3.2)

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{G}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}_{{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}}&=({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar)^{2}\,\Big(-\frac{g}{3!}\Big)\,(2\pi)^{4}\ \delta(p_{1}+_{\star}p_{2}+_{\star}p_{3})\\
&\hskip 19.91684pt\times\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{1})\,\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{2})\,\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{3})\ .\end{split}

(3.3)

The star-sum of momenta in the Dirac distribution is defined in (
2.16
) and it encodes the deformed conservation of the Cartesian momenta. In this basis, the noncommutative deformation enters differently compared to results with the Moyal deformation where it appears as a phase factor.

To better understand this deformed conservation law, we express plane waves as Fourier series in cylindrical harmonics,

\displaystyle\mathtt{e}_{k}=\sum_{\ell_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}}\,{\mathrm{i}}^{\,\ell_{k}}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{k}\,\vartheta_{k}}\ \mathtt{c}_{k}\ ,

(3.4)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathtt{e}_{k}(x)&=\mathtt{e}_{(E_{k}\,,\,k_{x}\,,\,k_{y}\,,\,k_{z})}(t,x,y,z)={\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,E_{k}\,t+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{x}\,x+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{y}\,y+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{z}\,z}\ ,\\[4.0pt]
\mathtt{c}_{k}(x)&=\mathtt{c}_{(E_{k}\,,\,\alpha_{k}\,,\,\ell_{k}\,,\,k_{z})}(t,r,\varphi,z)=J_{\ell_{k}}(\alpha_{k}\,r)\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{k}\,\varphi}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,E_{k}\,t+{\mathrm{i}}\,k_{z}\,z}\ ,\end{split}

(3.5)

where
\alpha_{k}=\big(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
and
\vartheta_{k}=\tan^{-1}({k_{y}}/{k_{x}})
. Motivated by the relation (
3.4
) as well as explicit calculations at low multiplicity and loop level, in
[
11
]
it is conjectured that the all orders
n
-point correlation functions in these two bases are related by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{n}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}}\ {\mathrm{i}}^{\,\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{n}}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{1}\,\vartheta_{1}-\cdots-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{n}\,\vartheta_{n}}\ \widetilde{C}_{n}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\ .

(3.6)

The correlation functions
\widetilde{C}_{n}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
are calculated in a completely analogous way as the corresponding correlators
\widetilde{G}_{n}(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
, but with insertions of the cylindrical harmonic states (
2.27
) instead of the plane wave states (
2.12
)
[
11
]
.

Let us consider the correlator (
3.3
) in the special case where
p_{1}=(E_{1},0,0,p_{1z})
, i.e. for purely axial momentum
\alpha_{1}=0
. Starting from (
3.3
) and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{C}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})_{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}&=({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar)^{2}\,\frac{g}{3!}\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{1})\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{2})\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{3})\ {\mathrm{e}}^{\,\frac{{\mathrm{i}}\,\lambda}{2}\,(p_{1z}\,\ell_{2}-p_{2z}\,\ell_{1})}\ \delta_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3},0}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times(2\pi)^{3}\ \delta(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})\ \delta(p_{1z}+p_{2z}+p_{3z})\ \mathtt{F}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\ ,\end{split}

(3.7)

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{G}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})_{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}}\big|_{\alpha_{1}=0}&=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\ {\mathrm{i}}^{\,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}\ {\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{1}\,\vartheta_{1}-{\mathrm{i}}\ell_{2}\,\vartheta_{2}-{\mathrm{i}}\,\ell_{3}\,\vartheta_{3}}\ \widetilde{C}_{3}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})_{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}^{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}\big|_{\alpha_{1}=0}\\[4.0pt]
&=({\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar)^{2}\,\frac{g}{3!}\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{1})\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{2})\ \widetilde{\mathsf{G}}(p_{3})\,(2\pi)^{3}\ \delta(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})\\
&\qquad\times\delta(p_{1z}+p_{2z}+p_{3z})\ \frac{\delta(\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{3})}{\alpha_{2}}\ \delta\big(\vartheta_{2}-\vartheta_{3}-\pi+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{1z}\big)\ .\end{split}

(3.8)

We conclude that the deformed conservation of momentum encoded by the Dirac distribution
\delta(p_{1}+_{\star}p_{2}+_{\star}p_{3})\big|_{\alpha_{1}=0}
can be interpreted as a modification of momentum conservation in the
(p_{x},p_{y})
-plane, namely as a shift of the relative polar angle between the momenta by
\frac{\lambda}{2}\,p_{1z}
. In this way, the noncommutative deformation modifies the momentum conservation law.

Four-point function.

The tree-level four-point correlation function is defined as

\widetilde{G}_{4}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})^{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}=\mathsf{P}\,\big(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}-\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\big)^{5}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{3}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{4}})\ .

(3.9)

The one-particle irreducible contribution can be found in terms of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\widetilde{G}_{4}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})^{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}_{\mbox{\tiny 1PI}}\\[4.0pt]
&\qquad=(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar)^{3}\,({\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{2}\,\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}\,\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{3}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{4}})\,\big|_{{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H})^{3}\,\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\,\{\mathcal{S}^{\tt pl}_{\rm int},-\}\,\mathsf{H}\,(\mathtt{e}^{p_{1}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{2}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{3}}\odot\mathtt{e}^{p_{4}})\,\big|_{{\textrm{\tiny 1PI}}}\ .\end{split}

(3.10)

Performing a long but straightforward calculation, the final result contains three sets of diagrams corresponding to the all three
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels.

The
s

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{G}_{4}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})_{\mbox{\tiny 1PI}}^{{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}s}&=(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar)^{3}\,\left(\frac{g}{3!}\right)^{2}\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{1})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{2})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{3})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}(p_{4})\ {\widetilde{\mathsf{G}}}\big((-p_{2})+_{\star}(-p_{1})\big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times(2\pi)^{4}\ \Big(\delta\big((-p_{3})+_{\star}(-p_{4})+_{\star}(-p_{2})+_{\star}(-p_{1})\big)\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt+\,\delta\big((-p_{4})+_{\star}(-p_{3})+_{\star}(-p_{2})+_{\star}(-p_{1})\big)\Big)\ .\end{split}

\widetilde{C}^{{\mathcal{R}}}_{n}(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4})^{\textrm{\tiny$(0)$}}=\mathsf{P}\,\big(-{\mathrm{i}}\,\hbar\,{\Delta}_{\textrm{\tiny BV}}\,\mathsf{H}-\{\mathcal{S}^{{\mathcal{R}}}_{\rm int},-\}_{{\mathcal{R}}}\,\mathsf{H}\big)^{5}\,(\mathtt{c}^{p_{1}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{p_{2}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{p_{3}}\odot_{\mathcal{R}}\mathtt{c}^{p_{4}})\ .

4
Final remarks

In this contribution we discussed two different quantization schemes in the algebraic BV formalism: the standard (Feynman) quantization and braided quantization. While the standard quantization can be performed in any basis, braided quantization naturally selects a basis adapted to the Drinfel’d twist defining the noncommutative deformation. More precisely, in the braided BV formalism all maps entering a homotopy equivalence must be covariant with respect to the deformed Poincaré symmetry induced by the twist. In our analysis, we carried out the standard quantization in the plane wave basis (
2.12
), whereas the braided quantization uniquely selects the basis of cylindrical harmonics (
2.27
).

As expected from previous work
[
6
,
16
,
17
,
11
]
, the standard quantization leads to two inequivalent classes of diagrams in tree-level four-point functions, with one diagram in each of the
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels for each class. On the other hand, the braided quantization yields only a single class of diagrams, again with one diagram in each of the
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels, and the noncommutativity enters only through a phase factor depending on the external momenta.

In
[
11
]
and in this contribution we have considered only the scalar
\phi^{3}
-theory on
\lambda
-Minkowski space. It would be very interesting to extend this analysis to gauge theories on
\lambda
-Minkowski space and to understand the consequences of the deformed Poincaré symmetry as well as the corresponding deformed conservation laws in that setting. Another important open problem is to understand how to systematically extract the one-particle irreducible diagrams from the full set of diagrams (disconnected, reducible and irreducible) that arise from the definition of correlation functions using homological perturbation theory, see for example (
3.9
), and how to construct scattering amplitudes within this formalism based on a homotopy algebraic version of the LSZ reduction theorem in textbook quantum field theory.
