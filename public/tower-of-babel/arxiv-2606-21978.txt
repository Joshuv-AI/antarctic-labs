Weighted sum formulas for finite multiple mixed values
Zhonghua Li and Zhenlu Wang[1mm] a. School of Mathematical Sciences,Key Laboratory of Intelligent Computing and Applications (Tongji University),Ministry of Education, Tongji University, Shanghai 200092, Chinab. School of Artificial Intelligence,Taizhou University, Taizhou 318000, Zhejiang, China

Abstract
In this paper, we employ the iterated integral expression of multiple polylogarithms to establish a weighted sum formula for finite multiple mixed values. As applications, we derive various relations among level-two variants of finite multiple zeta values.



Keywords
finite multiple zeta values; finite multiple mixed values; weighted sum formula

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11M32; 05A19

1
Introduction

1.1
Multiple zeta values and level-two variants

A finite sequence of positive integers is called an index. An index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
is called admissible if
k_{r}\geq 2
. The weight, depth and height of
\boldsymbol{k}
are defined by
\operatorname{wt}(\boldsymbol{k}):=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
,
\operatorname{dep}(\boldsymbol{k}):=r
, and
\operatorname{ht}(\boldsymbol{k}):=|\{i|k_{i}\geq 2\}|
respectively.

For any admissible index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, Hoffman
[
3
]
and Zagier
[
17
]
independently defined the multiple zeta value (MZV)
\zeta(\boldsymbol{k})
by

\displaystyle\zeta(\boldsymbol{k}):=\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

These values have featured prominently in both number theory and physics, attracting considerable attention and interest over the past three decades. One of the main points of interest in this area is to find as many relations among MZVs as possible and further explore the structure of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by MZVs. There are many linear relations among MZVs. A well-known example is the sum formula
[
3
]

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k\atop k_{r}\geq 2}\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\zeta(k),\quad(k\geq 2).

Ohno and Zudilin
[
10
]
proved the following weighted sum formula for double zeta values:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k\atop k_{2}\geq 2}2^{k_{2}}\zeta(k_{1},k_{2})=(k+1)\zeta(k),\quad(k\geq 3).

Guo and Xie
[
2
]
generalized the above formula to arbitrary depth. Many other types of weighted sum formulas among MZVs exist, see Zhao’s monograph
[
14
]
for further details.

In recent years, a number of variants of MZVs have been introduced and studied. Among them, a family known as level-two MZVs arises from restricting the summation in the definition of MZVs to a prescribed parity pattern. Prominent examples include Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values (MtVs)
[
4
]
, Kaneko-Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values (MTVs)
[
7
]
, and Xu-Zhao’s multiple
S
-values (MSVs)
[
13
]
. For any admissible index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we give their respective definitions as follows:

\displaystyle t(\boldsymbol{k})=t(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=2^{r}\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}\atop\forall m_{i}:odd}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}},

\displaystyle T(\boldsymbol{k})=T(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=2^{r}\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}\atop m_{i}\equiv i\pmod{2}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}},

\displaystyle S(\boldsymbol{k})=S(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=2^{r}\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}\atop m_{i}\equiv i-1\pmod{2}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

Note that the definition of multiple
t
-values adopted in this paper coincides with that in
[
4
]
, with an extra multiplicative factor of
2^{r}
.

To reformulate the definitions of these level-two MZVs, Xu and Zhao
[
13
]
introduced the multiple mixed values (MMVs, or multiple M-values). For any admissible index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and any finite sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, the finite multiple mixed value
M(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})
is defined by

\displaystyle M(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}):=\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\cdots(1+\varepsilon_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}=2^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}\\
2\mid m_{j}\text{ if }\varepsilon_{j}=1\\
2\nmid m_{j}\text{ if }\varepsilon_{j}=-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

By applying Chen’s theory of iterated integrals, one can define a shuffle algebra that reflects the multiplication rule of two MZVs in terms of these integrals. For any differential 1-forms
f_{1}(u)du,\dots,f_{n}(u)du
, the iterated integral is defined by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}f_{1}(u)du\cdots f_{n}(u)du=\int\limits_{0<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n}<z}f_{1}(u_{1})du_{1}\cdots f_{n}(u_{n})du_{n}.

To investigate the shuffle structure of MMVs, we define the following 1-forms:

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(u)=\frac{du}{u},\quad\omega_{1}(u)=\frac{2udu}{1-u^{2}},\quad\omega_{-1}(u)=\frac{2du}{1-u^{2}}.

As established in
[
13
]
, MMVs admit the following integral representations:

\displaystyle M(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\dots,\varepsilon_{r})=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}\omega_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2}}\omega_{0}^{k_{2}-1}\cdots\omega_{\varepsilon_{r-1}\varepsilon_{r}}\omega_{0}^{k_{r}-1}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}\omega_{0}^{k_{1}-1}\omega_{\varepsilon_{2}}\omega_{0}^{k_{2}-1}\cdots\omega_{\varepsilon_{r}}\omega_{0}^{k_{r}-1}=M(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2},\dots,\varepsilon_{1}\cdots\varepsilon_{r}).

1.2
Finite multiple zeta values and level-two variants

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of primes. Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\Big/\bigoplus_{p\in\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}).

For any index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, the finite multiple zeta value (FMZV)
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})
was introduced by Kaneko and Zagier (see
[
5
,
6
]
) via the definition

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k}):=\left(\sum_{0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<p}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}\pmod{p}\right)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

Kaneko and Zagier proposed a central conjecture on finite multiple zeta values, which predicts a deep connection between finite and classical multiple zeta values. Similarly, there are many linear relations among finite multiple zeta values. It is known that FMZVs satisfy the reversal formula

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{1}).

Saito and Wakabayashi
[
12
]
proved a general identity, from which the following analogue of the classical sum formula can be derived as a special case:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}=k\atop k_{r}\geq 2}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\left(1+(-1)^{r}\binom{k-1}{r-1}\right)Z(k),

where for an integer
k\geq 2
,
Z(k)\in\mathcal{A}
is defined by

\displaystyle Z(k)_{(p)}:=\frac{B_{p-k}}{k}\pmod{p}\quad\text{for}\;p>k,\quad(B_{p-k}:\text{Bernoulli number}).

Using the iterated integral expression for multiple polylogarithms, Kamano
[
8
]
derived some weighted sum formulas for FMZVs.

In this paper, we focus on the level-two finite multiple zeta values. For any index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, any finite sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, and any positive integer
n
, Zhao
[
16
]
defined the
n
-th partial sum of multiple mixed value
M_{n}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})
by

\displaystyle M_{n}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}):

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<n}\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\cdots(1+\varepsilon_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=2^{r}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}<n\\
2\mid m_{j}\text{ if }\varepsilon_{j}=1\\
2\nmid m_{j}\text{ if }\varepsilon_{j}=-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

and the finite multiple mixed value (FMMV)
M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})
by

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}):=\Bigl(M_{p}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})\pmod{p}\Bigr)_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{A}.

Finite multiple mixed values serve as a common generalization of the following four level-two variants of finite multiple zeta values. For any index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, the finite multiple
t
-value (FMtV), the finite multiple
E
-value (FMEV), the finite multiple
T
-value (FMTV), and the finite multiple
S
-value (FMSV) are defined by

\displaystyle t_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=t_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=M_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};\{-1\}^{r}),

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=E_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=M_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};\{1\}^{r}),

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=T_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=M_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};-1,1,\dots,(-1)^{r-1},(-1)^{r}),

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=S_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r}):=M_{\mathcal{A}}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};1,-1,\dots,(-1)^{r-2},(-1)^{r-1}),

respectively, where
\{a\}^{n}
denotes the string
a,\ldots,a
with
n
repetitions. We remark that our definitions of these level-two variants match those in
[
16
]
up to a multiplicative factor of
2^{r}
. Furthermore, FMEVs satisfy
E_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k})=2^{\operatorname{dep}(\boldsymbol{k})-\operatorname{wt}(\boldsymbol{k})}\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k})
, where
\zeta_{\mathcal{A}}^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k})
was recently introduced by Kaneko et al.
[
9
]
, and they also presented a parity result and some sum formulas.

Zhao
[
15
,
16
]
studied these level-two variants of FMZVs, establishing their stuffle, reversal, and linear shuffle relations, which can be used to compute generating sets in weights one and two and to analyze the dimensions of the various subspaces generated by these values. For any tuple
\boldsymbol{d}
and any real number
c
, let
\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{d}}
denote the reversal of
\boldsymbol{d}
, and let
c\boldsymbol{d}
denote the tuple formed by multiplying each components of
\boldsymbol{d}
by
c
. In particular,
-\boldsymbol{d}
denote the tuple obtained by negating all components of
\boldsymbol{d}
. Similarly, the FMMVs admit the following reversal relation
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{k}};-\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})=(-1)^{\operatorname{wt}(\boldsymbol{k})}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}).

(1.1)

In this paper, we provide a weighted sum formula for finite multiple mixed values. The following is our main theorem, where the notation
\sum_{|\boldsymbol{k}|_{r}=N}
indicates that the sum is taken over all indices
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
satisfying
k_{1}+\cdots k_{r}=N
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a,b
be non-negative integers, and let
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\tau\in\{\pm 1\}
. For all integers
m,n
satisfying
0\leq m\leq a
and
0\leq n\leq b
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq a\atop 0\leq j\leq b}\binom{i}{m}\binom{j}{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{a-i+1}=a+b-i-j+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i+1}=i+j+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{a-i},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-1},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{0\leq i\leq a\atop 0\leq j\leq b}\binom{i}{a-m}\binom{j}{b-n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{a-i+1}=a+b-i-j+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i+1}=i+j+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{2},\delta_{2}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-1},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{a-m+1}=a+b-m-n+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{m+1}=m+n+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{m+n+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{a-m},-\delta_{2}\tau^{m},-\delta_{2}\tau^{m-1},\ldots,-\delta_{2}\tau,-\delta_{2}).

This theorem is proved by using the iterated integral expression for multiple polylogarithms. The same method has been used to give various weighted sum formulas for classical MZVs and FMZVs (see
[
2
,
11
,
8
]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we prove our main theorem. In Sec.
3
, we discuss some special cases of our main result and give some formulas involving level-two variants of FMZVs with depth-two, height-zero and height-one.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For any index
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and any finite sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
, we define the multiple polylogarithm by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon};z):=\sum\limits_{0<m_{1}<\dots<m_{r}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\cdots(1+\varepsilon_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})z^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

For any prime
p
, as defined in
[
8
]
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear operator
\mathcal{C}_{p}:\mathbb{Q}[[z]]\to\mathbb{Q}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{p}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}:=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{p-1}a_{n}.

Lemma 2.1
.

For indices
\boldsymbol{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}),\boldsymbol{l}=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{s})
, and any finite sequences
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
,
\boldsymbol{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{s})\in\{\pm 1\}^{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon};z)\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{l};\boldsymbol{\delta};z)\right)\equiv(-1)^{\operatorname{wt}(\boldsymbol{l})}M_{p}(\boldsymbol{k},\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{l}};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},-\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\delta}})\pmod{p}.

(2.1)

Proof.
The left-hand side of (
2.1
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}\\
0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{s}\\
m_{r}+n_{s}<p\end{subarray}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon_{1}(-1)^{m_{1}})\cdots(1+\varepsilon_{r}(-1)^{m_{r}})(1+\delta_{1}(-1)^{n_{1}})\cdots(1+\delta_{s}(-1)^{n_{s}})}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}n_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots n_{s}^{l_{s}}}.

By changing the variables as
n_{j}\to p-n_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{p}\left(\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon};z)\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{l};\boldsymbol{\delta};z)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}\\
0<p-n_{1}<\cdots<p-n_{s}\\
m_{r}<n_{s}\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigl(1+\varepsilon_{i}(-1)^{m_{i}}\bigr)\prod_{j=1}^{s}\bigl(1+\delta_{j}(-1)^{p-n_{j}}\bigr)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{k_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}(p-n_{j})^{l_{j}}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}<n_{s}<\cdots<n_{1}<p\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1+\varepsilon_{i}(-1)^{m_{i}})\prod_{j=1}^{s}(1-\delta_{j}(-1)^{n_{j}})}{(-1)^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{s}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{k_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}n_{j}^{l_{j}}}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\operatorname{wt}(\boldsymbol{l})}M_{p}(\boldsymbol{k},\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{l}};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},-\overleftarrow{\boldsymbol{\delta}}),

and we obtain (
2.1
).
∎

We define the following integrals:

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}(u_{1},u_{2}):=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\omega_{0}(u)=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\frac{du}{u},

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}):=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\omega_{1}(u)=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\frac{2udu}{1-u^{2}},

\displaystyle\Omega_{-1}(u_{1},u_{2}):=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\omega_{-1}(u)=\int_{u_{1}}^{u_{2}}\frac{2du}{1-u^{2}}.

We may deduce the following lemma via a similar approach to that employed in
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
8
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For a positive integer
n
, let
i_{m},j_{m}
be non-negative integers (
1\leq m\leq n
) and
\tau_{m},\delta_{m}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i_{1}!\cdots i_{n}!j_{1}!\cdots j_{n}!}\int\limits_{0<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{n}<z}\Omega_{\tau_{1}}(u_{1},u_{2})^{i_{1}}\Omega_{\tau_{2}}(u_{2},u_{3})^{i_{2}}\cdots\Omega_{\tau_{n}}(u_{n},z)^{i_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\Omega_{0}(u_{1},u_{2})^{j_{1}}\Omega_{0}(u_{2},u_{3})^{j_{2}}\cdots\Omega_{0}(u_{n},z)^{j_{n}}\omega_{\delta_{1}}(u_{1})\cdots\omega_{\delta_{n}}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}_{m}|_{i_{m}+1}=i_{m}+j_{m}+1\\
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{m}=\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{m}\tau_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\tau_{m-1}^{i_{m-1}}(1,\tau_{m},\ldots,\tau_{m}^{i_{m}})\\
m=1,\ldots,n\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{k}_{n};\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{n};z).

Combining the integral and series representations of FMMVs, we prove the following theorem using the lemmas established above.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
be parameters. Then for any non-negative integers
a,b
and any
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\tau\in\{\pm 1\}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{2}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{2},\delta_{2}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{2}}\beta_{1}^{j_{1}}\beta_{2}^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i_{2}+j_{2}+1}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{i_{1}},-\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{2}},-\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{2}-1},\ldots,-\delta_{2}).

(2.2)

Proof.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{p}F(z))_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\

i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{2}}\beta_{1}^{j_{1}}\beta_{2}^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i_{2}+j_{2}+1}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{i_{1}},-\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{2}},-\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{2}-1},\ldots,-\delta_{2}).

On the other hand, by partitioning the region of integration, we can rewrite
F(z)
as

\displaystyle F(z)=\frac{1}{a!\,b!}\left(\displaystyle\int_{0<u_{1}<u_{2}<z}+\displaystyle\int_{0<u_{2}<u_{1}<z}\right).

(2.3)

We denote the first integral in (
2.3
) by
F_{1}(z)
and the second by
F_{2}(z)
.
It is clear that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{1},z)+\alpha_{2}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{2},z)=\alpha_{1}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{1},u_{2})+(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\Omega_{\tau}(u_{2},z),

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\Omega_{0}(u_{1},z)+\beta_{2}\Omega_{0}(u_{2},z)=\beta_{1}\Omega_{0}(u_{1},u_{2})+(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\Omega_{0}(u_{2},z).

Then we have

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{1}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}}{i_{1}!\,i_{2}!\,j_{1}!\,j_{2}!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u_{1}<u_{2}<z\end{subarray}}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{1},u_{2})^{i_{1}}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{2},z)^{i_{2}}\Omega_{0}(u_{1},u_{2})^{j_{1}}\Omega_{0}(u_{2},z)^{j_{2}}\omega_{\delta_{1}}(u_{1})\omega_{\delta_{2}}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{1}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}};z).

By operating
\mathcal{C}_{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{p}F_{1}(z))_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{1}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}}).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha_{2}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{2}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}}{i_{1}!\,i_{2}!\,j_{1}!\,j_{2}!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u_{2}<u_{1}<z\end{subarray}}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{2},u_{1})^{i_{1}}\Omega_{\tau}(u_{1},z)^{i_{2}}\Omega_{0}(u_{2},u_{1})^{j_{1}}\Omega_{0}(u_{1},z)^{j_{2}}\omega_{\delta_{1}}(u_{1})\omega_{\delta_{2}}(u_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{2}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Mi}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{2},\delta_{2}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}};z)

and

\displaystyle(\mathcal{C}_{p}F_{2}(z))_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}=a\\
j_{1}+j_{2}=b\\
i_{1},i_{2},j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\alpha_{2}^{i_{1}}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{i_{2}}\beta_{2}^{j_{1}}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\boldsymbol{k}|_{i_{1}+1}=i_{1}+j_{1}+1\\
|\boldsymbol{l}|_{i_{2}+1}=i_{2}+j_{2}+1\end{subarray}}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{l};\delta_{2},\delta_{2}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+1},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{i_{1}+i_{2}}).

Hence, we finished th proof.
∎

Then, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

By comparing the coefficients of
\alpha_{1}^{a-m}\alpha_{2}^{m}\beta_{1}^{b-n}\beta_{2}^{n}
on both sides of (
2.2
), we get the desired result.
∎

3
Some special cases

3.1
Weighted sum formulas for depth-two FMMVs

Let
a=0
in Theorem
1.1
, then we get the following weighted sum formula for depth-two FMMVs.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
b
be a non-negative integer, and let
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then for any integer
n
satisfying
0\leq n\leq b
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{n}M_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1;\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\delta_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{b-n}M_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1;\delta_{2},\delta_{1}\delta_{2})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}M_{\mathcal{A}}(b-n+1,n+1;\delta_{1},-\delta_{2}).

(3.1)

By considering all possible combinations of
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
in (
3.1
), we get the following weighted sum formulas, where the integer
n
satisfies
0\leq n\leq b
. Let
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(1,1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\left(\binom{j}{n}+\binom{j}{b-n}\right)E_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=(-1)^{n+1}S_{\mathcal{A}}(b-n+1,n+1).

(3.2)

Let
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(1,-1)
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{n}S_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{b-n}t_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}E_{\mathcal{A}}(b-n+1,n+1).

(3.3)

Let
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(-1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{n}t_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\binom{j}{b-n}S_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}t_{\mathcal{A}}(b-n+1,n+1).

(3.4)

Let
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(-1,-1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}\left(\binom{j}{n}+\binom{j}{b-n}\right)T_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=(-1)^{n+1}T_{\mathcal{A}}(b-n+1,n+1).

(3.5)

Note that (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) are equivalent by the reversal relation.

Moreover, setting
n=0
in (
3.2
), (
3.3
),
(
3.4
) and (
3.5
) yields the following sum formulas below, respectively:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}E_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=-S_{\mathcal{A}}(b+1,1)-E_{\mathcal{A}}(1,b+1),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}S_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=-E_{\mathcal{A}}(b+1,1)-t_{\mathcal{A}}(1,b+1),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}t_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=-t_{\mathcal{A}}(b+1,1)-S_{\mathcal{A}}(1,b+1),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{b}T_{\mathcal{A}}(b-j+1,j+1)=-T_{\mathcal{A}}(b+1,1)-T_{\mathcal{A}}(1,b+1).

3.2
Relations for height-zero FMMVs

Let
b=0
in Theorem
1.1
, then we get a weighted sum formula for height-zero FMMVs.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
a
be a non-negative integer, and let
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\tau\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then for any integer
m
satisfying
0\leq m\leq a
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{a}\binom{i}{m}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{a-i},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-1},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{a}\binom{i}{a-m}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};\delta_{2},\delta_{2}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-i},\ldots,\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a-1},\delta_{1}\delta_{2}\tau^{a})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+1}M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\tau,\ldots,\delta_{1}\tau^{a-m},-\delta_{2}\tau^{m},-\delta_{2}\tau^{m-1},\ldots,-\delta_{2}\tau,-\delta_{2}).

(3.6)

Considering
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\tau)=(1,1,1)
in (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};\{1\}^{a-m+1},\{-1\}^{m+1})=(-1)^{m+1}\binom{a+2}{m+1}E_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2}),\quad(0\leq m\leq a).

It is clearly that
E_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2})=(-1)^{a}t_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2})
from the reversal relation (
1.1
), then we have

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};\{1\}^{a-m+1},\{-1\}^{m+1})=(-1)^{a+m+1}\binom{a+2}{m+1}t_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2}),\quad(0\leq m\leq a).

Considering
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\tau)=(-1,-1,-1)
in (
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2};-1,1,\ldots,(-1)^{a-m+1},(-1)^{m},(-1)^{m-1},\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+1}\binom{a+2}{m+1}T_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a+2}),\quad(0\leq m\leq a).

Assuming that
a
is an even integer in the above formula, we have
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{a})=0
, see also
[
15
, Proposition 7]
. Zhao
[

, Conjecture 5]
conjectured that

for all odd integers
a
.

\displaystyle\binom{a+1}{m+1}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{a+1}t_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{j},2,\{1\}^{a-j+1})+(-1)^{m}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{a-m}t_{\mathcal{A}}(\{1\}^{j},2,\{1\}^{a-j+1})

(3.7)

b=1

n=0

Corollary 3.4
