Non-invertible symmetries and modular invariance in lattice models
Yacine Ikhlef

Abstract
We consider classical 2d lattice models with face interactions defined in terms of a fusion category. The symmetries of such models typically include an algebra of topological operators sitting on a closed path in the lattice. In the case when the face interactions obey the Temperley–Lieb (TL) relations, we present a generic algorithm to determine the decomposition of the transfer-matrix space of states as a direct sum of simple TL modules. We apply this approach to several examples, and analyse the action of topological operators. As an application, we compute the modular transformation of the irreducible TL characters at primitive roots of unity.

Introduction

In any discrete or continuous model of 2d Statistical Mechanics, a topological operator is defined by the insertion of modified conditions for the local degrees of freedom along a given contour, so that the resulting partition function only depends on the homotopy class of this contour. This was first studied systematically for rational Conformal Field Theories (CFTs) in the seminal works of Petkova and Zuber
[
1
,
2
]
, where the algebra of topological operators was derived in terms of the modular
S
-matrix of conformal characters, and it was related to the Ocneanu algebra. The lattice analogs of these operators were constructed
[
3
,
4
]
in the corresponding integrable face models, using specific “braid limits” of the commuting transfer matrices. The results of
[
1
,
2
]
also found many applications to Topological Quantum Field Theories, especially after the work of
[
5
,
6
]
.

A new perspective on the symmetries of lattice models was introduced with the concept of anyon chains associated to fusion categories
[
7
]
. The latter consist in 1d quantum systems where the quantum states of the chain live in a space of homomorphisms of the category, rather than a tensor product of spin representations as in usual quantum spin chains. The prototype of this construction is the “golden chain”
[
8
]
, but it equally applies to 2d statistical models
[
9
,
10
]
. A lot of progress occurred in the understanding and classification of symmetries and dualities of these models, using the tensor-network formalism – see
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
for recent advances.

One of the major advantages of the approach based on fusion categories is that it provides a natural construction of the algebra of topological operators and their structure constants, directly in terms of the basic data of the underlying fusion category, namely the
F
-symbols.
A particularly tractable case is when the interaction of the lattice model is given by the projector of two neighbouring “anyons” onto the identity object: in this case, one can show that the local operators which encode this interaction satisfy the relations of the Temperley–Lieb (TL) algebra
[
14
]
. It turns out that many exactly solved models fit in this framework, such as the Ising, three-state Potts, Restricted Solid-On-Solid
[
15
]
and ADE
[
16
,
17
]
lattice models. In some of these cases, it is possible to construct part of the algebra of topological operators using the braid limit of the TL transfer matrices
[
18
,
19
]
.

In the present work, we consider a generic 2d statistical model with configurations based on a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, and face interactions obeying the TL algebra. We describe the space of states of the transfer matrix, with open or closed boundary conditions, and we show that it forms respectively a module over the ordinary or affine TL category, in the sense of
[
20
]
. Then, we present a recursive algorithm to calculate analytically the decomposition of the space of states as a direct sum of simple TL modules. This algorithm is largely inspired from previous work
[
21
]
on the ADE lattice models, and it is based on
seed states
. The latter are states of fixed size, which generate a simple TL module when acted upon by the TL operators. For open boundary conditions, the decomposition takes the form (
2.38
), and the multiplicities are encoded in a sequence of matrices
n_{k}
given by Chebyshev polynomials of the defining adjacency matrix, analogously to the “fused adjacency matrices” introduced by
[
22
,
23
]
in the context of boundary CFT. For closed boundary conditions, the algorithm is similar, except that the decomposition (
2.49
) relies on the spectrum of the cyclic translation operator
\Omega
, and the loop operator
f
.

We then apply the above approach to three examples of critical lattice models: the Fibonacci, Ising and three-state Potts models. In each example, we write the decomposition of the space of states for fixed, periodic and twisted periodic boundary conditions, and we obtain the action of the topological operators on each term of the decomposition. In these three cases, we observe that the algebra of TL endomorphisms of the space of states is generated by the topological operators associated to the simple objects and the automorphisms of
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, we discuss the scaling limit in terms of bulk CFT. Besides these examples, we consider a non-critical model based on the psu(2)
5
fusion rules (see
[
24
]
), where the space of states takes the form of an infinite direct sum of simple TL modules.

Finally, similarly to the Virasoro modules, we define modular characters as traces on the affine TL simple modules. A natural question is then to determine how these characters transform under the modular group. To answer this question when the loop weight is of the form
\beta=2\cos(\pi/h)
where
h
is an integer, we first analyse the unitary RSOS models of
[
15
]
with twisted periodic boundary conditions, and determine the decomposition of the space of states and the eigenvalues of the topological operators. By reversing the logic of
[
1
]
, we use these results to derive the modular transformation of the irreducible TL characters.

, where we have used the braid relations to move the line with label
p
to the top of the diagrams.
For
p=1
, we have
Y_{1}=\overline{Y}_{\!\!1}=\mathbf{1}
.
The braid relations also yield the composition rules

Y_{p}\cdot Y_{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{p^{\prime\prime}=1}^{n}N_{pp^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime\prime}}\,Y_{p^{\prime\prime}}\,,\qquad\overline{Y}_{\!\!p}\cdot\overline{Y}_{\!\!p^{\prime}}=\sum_{p^{\prime\prime}=1}^{n}N_{pp^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime\prime}}\,\overline{Y}_{\!\!p^{\prime\prime}}\,,\qquad Y_{p}\overline{Y}_{\!\!p^{\prime}}=\overline{Y}_{\!\!p^{\prime}}Y_{p}\,.

(2.29)

If one assumes moreover that the fusion rules are such that
a\otimes a=\mathbf{1}\oplus a^{\prime}
, where
a^{\prime}
is a simple object, then one can show that the matrices
R_{aa}
and
\overline{R}_{aa}
are of the form

R_{aa}=y\,\mathbf{1}_{aa}+y^{-1}\,\psi_{1}^{aa}\circ\psi_{aa}^{1}\,,\qquad\overline{R}_{aa}=y^{-1}\,\mathbf{1}_{aa}+y\,\psi_{1}^{aa}\circ\psi_{aa}^{1}\,,

(2.30)

where
y\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is such that
y^{2}=q
or
y^{2}=q^{-1}
.
In this case, the operators
Y_{a},\overline{Y}_{\!\!a}
coincide respectively with the
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
braid transfer matrices
F,\overline{F}
(
B.27
) acting on
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,\mathbf{1}}(N)
, and hence they have eigenvalues given by (
B.28
) on the simple submodules of
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,\mathbf{1}}
.

Fixed boundary conditions.

Similarly to the case of periodic boundary conditions, we define for any simple object
p
, the operator
Y_{p}:\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)\to\mathcal{V}_{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(N)
as

Y_{p}\cdot u:=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{Yp.bc.u2.pdf}}

(2.31)

which yields a topological operator by construction.
The action of
Y_{p}
on the basis states of
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
reads

Y_{p}\cdot[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}]=\sqrt{\frac{d(c)d(p)}{d(c^{\prime})}}\sum_{x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{N-1}}\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{x^{\prime}},\boldsymbol{x}}^{(p)}\,[b^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{N-1},c^{\prime}]\,,

(2.32)

where
(x_{0},x_{N})=(b,c)
and
(x^{\prime}_{0},x^{\prime}_{N})=(b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
.
The composition rules are

Y_{p^{\prime}}\cdot Y_{p}=\sum_{p^{\prime\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{d(p)d(p^{\prime})}{d(p^{\prime\prime})}}\left(F_{b^{\prime\prime}}^{bpp^{\prime}}\right)_{p^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime}}\,\left(F_{c^{\prime\prime}}^{cpp^{\prime}}\right)_{c^{\prime}p^{\prime\prime}}^{*}\,Y_{p^{\prime\prime}}\,.

(2.33)

For any automorphism
L
such that
L(a)=a
, one defines the operator
Q_{L}:\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)\to\mathcal{V}_{L(b)L(c)}(N)
as in (
2.20
).

Like in the case of periodic twisted boundary conditions, if
\mathcal{C}
is a braided category then one can construct a subalgebra of topological operators which obey the fusion rules of
\mathcal{C}
.
We introduce operators acting in
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
as

X_{p}\cdot u=\quad\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{Xp.u2.pdf}}

(2.34)

These operators are topological by construction. Moreover, they decompose over the operators
Y_{m}
as

X_{p}=\sum_{m}\sqrt{\frac{d(m)}{d(p)d(\bar{p})}}\,x_{pmb}^{*}\,x_{pmc}\,Y_{m}\,,\qquad\text{where}\quad x_{pmc}=\sum_{r}\sqrt{\frac{d(p)d(r)}{d(m)d(c)}}\,\left(F_{c}^{c\bar{p}p}\right)^{*}_{mr}\,R_{r}^{\bar{p}c}\,\overline{R}_{r}^{c\bar{p}}\,.

(2.35)

Using the braid relations, we obtain the commutation rules

X_{p}\cdot X_{p^{\prime}}=\sum_{p^{\prime\prime}}N_{pp^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime\prime}}\,X_{p^{\prime\prime}}\,.

(2.36)

Additionally, if (
2.30
) holds, then using the braid relations, one can move the lines indexed by
p,\bar{p}
to the top of the diagram (similarly to the operators (
2.28
)), and thus show that
X_{a}
coincides with the braid transfer matrix
G
(
B.26
). Hence, in this case,
X_{a}
has eigenvalues given by (
B.28
) on the simple submodules of
\mathcal{V}_{bc}
.

2.3
Decomposition over simple Temperley–Lieb modules

In this section, we refer to the classification of simple Temperley–Lieb modules found in
[
20
]
: the corresponding material is reviewed in Appendix
B
.
When studying the face model associated to a pair
(\mathcal{C},a)
, we consider two distinct cases, depending on the value of the quantum dimension
d(a)
.

1.

If
0<d(a)<2
then one can show that
d(a)
is of the form
2\cos(\pi/h)
, where
h
is a positive integer. We then set
q=e^{i\pi(h-1)/h}
. Moreover, we assume that all the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix
G=\widehat{N}(a)
are of the form
\lambda_{j}(a)=2\cos(\pi m_{j}/h)
, with
m_{j}\in\{1,2,\dots,h-1\}
. The integers
m_{j}
are called the exponents of
G
.

2.

If
d(a)\geq 2
, then
d(a)
is of the form
2\cosh\gamma
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. We set
q=-e^{\gamma}
, and
h=\infty
by convention. We also parameterise the eigenvalues of
\widehat{N}(a)
as
\lambda_{j}(a)=2\cos(\pi\theta_{j})
, with
\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.

In both cases, we have
d(a)=-q-q^{-1}
.

Fixed boundary conditions.

Consider the space of states
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
with fixed boundary conditions, in the face model defined by
(\mathcal{C},a)
.
The spaces
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(0),\mathcal{V}_{bc}(1),\mathcal{V}_{bc}(2)\dots
equipped with the action of
c_{j},c^{\dagger}_{j}
(
2.7
) form a
\mathsf{TL}
module. This means that the diagrams
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
obtained by composing operators of the form
c_{j}
and
c^{\dagger}_{j}
act from
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N^{\prime})
to
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
.
Moreover, the inner product on
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
defined by

\langle{[x_{0},\dots,x_{N}],[y_{0},\dots,y_{N}]}\rangle:=d(a)^{N/2}\,\delta_{x_{0},y_{0}}\dots\delta_{x_{N},y_{N}}

(2.37)

is invariant, namely it satisfies
\langle{c_{j}u,v}\rangle=\langle{u,c_{j}^{\dagger}v}\rangle
for any
u\in\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N),\ v\in\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N-2)
and
j=1,\dots,N-1
.

Based on these properties, let us show that the module
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
decomposes as

\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)\equiv\bigoplus_{k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{h}{2}-1}(n_{k})_{bc}\,I_{k}(N)\,.

(2.38)

Here,
I_{k}
is the simple module with
2k
legs, and the multiplicities are given by the “fused adjacency matrices”
[
22
]
defined as

n_{k}=\begin{cases}U_{2k}[\widehat{N}(a)/2]&\text{if }k\in\{0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{h}{2}-1\}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(2.39)

where
U_{2k}
is the
(2k)
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind (see Appendix
C
). Note that the direct sum in (
2.38
) is finite if
d(a)<2
, and infinite if
d(a)\geq 2
. However, in the latter case, for any value of
N
, all terms with
k>N/2
vanish.

The first ingredient to prove (
2.38
) is the equality of dimensions:

\dim\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)=\sum_{k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{h}{2}-1}(n_{k})_{bc}\,\dim I_{k}(N)\,.

(2.40)

In order to establish this equality, we use the orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\widehat{N}(a)
, given by the normalised fusion characters

w_{j}(a):=\frac{\lambda_{j}(a)}{|\lambda_{j}|}\,,\qquad|\lambda_{j}|:=\sqrt{\sum_{a=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}(a)|^{2}}\,.

(2.41)

We write the LHS of (
2.40
) as

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)

\displaystyle=\left[\widehat{N}(a)^{N}\right]_{bc}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,\lambda_{j}(a)^{N}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,h-\frac{1}{2}}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,D_{k}(N)\,\cos\frac{2\pi km_{j}}{h}&\text{if }d(a)<2\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/2}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,d_{k}(N)\,\cos 2k\theta_{j}&\text{if }d(a)\geq 2\end{cases}

(2.42)

where we have used the binomial identities (
C.3
)–(
C.4
).
If
d(a)<2
, the RHS of (
2.40
) reads

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{h}{2}-1}(n_{k})_{bc}\,\dim I_{k}(N)=\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{h}{2}-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,U_{2k}(\lambda_{j}(a)/2)\,[D_{k}(N)-D_{k+1}(N)]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,\Bigg[\sum_{k=1,\frac{3}{2}\dots,\frac{h}{2}-1}(U_{2k}-U_{2k-2})\left(\cos\frac{\pi m_{j}}{h}\right)\,D_{k}(N)+D_{0}(N)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2\cos\frac{\pi m_{j}}{h}\,D_{1/2}(N)+\cos\pi m_{j}\,D_{h/2}(N)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,h-\frac{1}{2}}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,D_{k}(N)\,\cos\frac{2\pi km_{j}}{h}\,.

(2.43)

where we have used the property (
C.2
).
If
d(a)\geq 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2}(\mu_{k})_{bc}\,\dim I_{k}(N)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,U_{2k}(\lambda_{j}(a)/2)\,[d_{k}(N)-d_{k+1}(N)]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,\left[\sum_{k=1,\frac{3}{2},2,\dots\infty}(U_{2k}-U_{2k-2})(\cos\theta_{j})\,d_{k}(N)+d_{0}(N)+2\cos\theta_{j}\,d_{1/2}(N)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{j}(b)^{*}w_{j}(c)\,d_{k}(N)\,\cos 2k\theta_{j}\,.

(2.44)

Hence, in both cases, the formula (
2.40
) is proved.

The next key ingredient to prove (
2.38
) consists in exploiting the fact that the simple modules are generated by the action of
\mathsf{TL}
diagrams on a single state, i.e.

I_{k}(N)=\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot u_{k}\,,

(2.45)

where
u_{k}
is the unique basis state of
I_{k}(2k)=W_{k}(2k)
.
We say that
u_{k}
is a seed state for
I_{k}
.
Similarly, for any
\mathsf{TL}
-module
A
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2
, a seed state of type
k
in
A
is defined as a nonzero state
\xi\in A(2k)
such that
c_{1}\cdot\xi=c_{2}\cdot\xi=\dots=c_{2k-1}\cdot\xi=0
.

For instance, a seed state of type
k=1
in
\mathcal{V}_{bc}
is a nonzero state of the form
\xi=\sum_{x}\alpha_{x}[b,x,c]
in
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(2)
, where
\alpha_{x}\in\mathbb{C}
, and such that
c_{1}\cdot\xi=0
. Given the expression (
2.8
) of
c_{j}
in
\mathcal{V}_{bc}
, we have

c_{1}\cdot[b,x,c]=\delta_{bc}\,\sqrt{\frac{d(x)d(a)}{d(b)}}\,[b,c]\,.

Hence, if
b\neq c
then any basis state
[b,x,c]
of
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(2)
is a seed state of type
k=1
. If
b=c
then any nonzero solution to the linear equation
\sum_{x}\alpha_{x}\sqrt{d(x)}=0
yields a seed state of type
k=1
.

In Appendix
B
we show that, if the loop weight
\beta
is real and the module
A
is equipped with an invariant inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
then

\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi\equiv I_{k}(N)\,,

(2.46)

for any seed state
\xi
of type
k
in
A
. Moreover, if
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
are two seed states respectively of types
k,k^{\prime}
in
A
, with
\langle{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=0
in the case
k=k^{\prime}
, then
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi
is orthogonal to
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi^{\prime}
.

Let us now prove (
2.38
) by constructing the seed states recursively.
More specifically, let us construct, for any
k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{h}{2}-1
, an orthonormal
basis
(\xi_{k,1},\dots,\xi_{k,(n_{k})_{bc}})
of seed states of type
k
in
\mathcal{V}_{bc}
, such that for any
N
, the space
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
decomposes as

\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)=\bigoplus_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{N}{2}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{(n_{k})_{bc}}\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi_{k,i}\,.

(2.47)

•

For
N=0
, we have
(n_{0})_{bc}=\delta_{bc}
. On the other hand,

\mathcal{V}_{bc}(0)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{span}\,[b]&\text{if }b=c\\
0&\text{if }b\neq c\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
b=c
, the state
[b]
is automatically a seed state of type zero. Hence (
2.47
) holds in any case.

•

For
N=1
, similarly, we have
(n_{1/2})_{bc}=N_{ab}^{c}
, whereas

\mathcal{V}_{bc}(1)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{span}\,[b,c]&\text{if }N_{ab}^{c}=1\\
0&\text{if }N_{ab}^{c}=0\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
N_{ab}^{c}=1
, the state
[b,c]
is automatically a seed state of type
\tfrac{1}{2}
. Hence (
2.47
) holds in any case.

•

For
N\geq 2
, we assume that (
2.47
) holds for
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N-2)
, and we consider the submodules of
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N):=\mathsf{TL}(N,N-2)\cdot\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N-2)\,,

(2.48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N):=[\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N)]^{\perp}=\{u\in\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)\ |\ \forall v\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N)\,,\langle{u,v}\rangle=0\}\,.

For any
(k,i)
with
2k\leq N-2
, we have

\mathsf{TL}(N,N-2)\cdot\mathsf{TL}(N-2,2k)\cdot\xi_{k,i}=\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi_{k,i}\equiv I_{k}(N)\,,

which yields

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N)\equiv\bigoplus_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{N}{2}-1}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{(n_{k})_{bc}}I_{k}(N)\,.

Hence, using (
2.40
) we obtain

\dim\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N)=\dim\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)-\dim\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N)=(n_{N/2})_{bc}\,.

If
N>h-2
then
\dim\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N)=0
, and thus
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)=\mathcal{V}_{bc}^{\prime}(N)
satisfies (
2.47
).

Let us treat the case
N\leq h-2
.
For any
v\in\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N-2)
and
j\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
,
the state
c_{j}^{\dagger}\,v
belongs to
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{bc}(N)
.
Thus, for any
u\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N)
we have
\langle{c_{j}\,u,v}\rangle=\langle{u,c_{j}^{\dagger}\,v}\rangle=0
. Hence, the state
c_{j}\,u
is orthogonal to the module
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N-2)
, and therefore
c_{j}\,u=0
. This proves that any nonzero element of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N)
is a seed state of type
N/2
. Let
(\xi_{N/2,1},\dots,\xi_{N/2,(n_{N/2})_{bc}})
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}_{bc}(N)
. Since the submodules
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi_{k,i}
are all orthogonal to each other, they are in direct sum, which proves (
2.47
) for
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
.

Twisted periodic boundary conditions.

The simple
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules are of the form
I_{k,z}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
(see Appendix
B
).
Let us present a general method to find the decomposition of
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}
in the form

\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(N)\equiv\bigoplus_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{h}{2}}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\nu_{k}(m,K)}I_{k,z_{k,j}}(N)\,,

(2.49)

with non-negative integers
\nu_{k}(m,K)
, and parameters
z_{k,j}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
|z_{k,j}|=1
.

As a first step, using a similar argument to the one for
\dim\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
, one obtains the dimension formulas

\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(N)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\,D_{0}(N)+2\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2},1,\dots,\frac{h-1}{2}}\alpha_{k}\,D_{k}(N)+\alpha_{\frac{h}{2}}\,D_{\frac{h}{2}}(N)&\text{if }d(a)<2\,,\\
\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\,d_{0}(N)+2\sum_{k=\frac{1}{2},1,\dots,\frac{N}{2}}\alpha_{k}\,d_{k}(N)&\text{if }d(a)\geq 2\,,\\
\end{cases}

(2.50)

where

\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{k}(m,K)=\mathrm{tr}\left[\widehat{K}\widehat{N}(m)T_{2k}\left(\frac{\widehat{N}(a)}{2}\right)\right]

(2.51)

and
T_{2k}
is the
2k
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, whereas the dimensions
d_{k}(N)
and
D_{k}(N)
are defined in (
B.11
) and (
B.20
).

One has
\alpha_{0}=\mathrm{tr}[\widehat{K}\widehat{N}(m)]\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and one then shows by recursion that
2\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, if
d(a)<2
, using the relation (
2.50
) at
N=h
, we get
\alpha_{h/2}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, (
2.50
) yields
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(N)
as a linear combination of
D_{0}(N),\dots,D_{h/2}(N)
[resp.
d_{0}(N),\dots,d_{N/2}(N)
] with integer coefficients if
d(a)<2
[resp.
d(a)\geq 2
].

Next, we shall exploit the fact that any simple module over
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
is generated by a single state:

I_{k,z}(N)=\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)\cdot u_{k}\,,

(2.52)

where
u_{k}
is the unique basis state of
I_{k,z}(2k)=W_{k,z}(2k)
.
We define the seed states for
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules as follows. Let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-module,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. A seed state of type
(k,z)
in the module
A
is a non-zero element
\xi\in A(2k)
such that

\begin{cases}c_{1}\cdot\xi=0&\text{if }k\geq 1\,,\\
\Omega\cdot\xi=z\,\xi&\text{if }k\geq 1/2\,,\\
f\cdot\xi=(z+z^{-1})\,\xi&\text{if }k=0\,.\end{cases}

(2.53)

Like for the case of
\mathsf{TL}
modules, if the loop weight is real and
A
admits an invariant inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
then the following properties hold:

•

For any seed state
\xi
of type
(k,z)
with
|z|=1
, one has
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)\cdot\xi\equiv I_{k,z}(N)
.

•

For any pair of seed states
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
of types
(k,z),(k^{\prime},z^{\prime})
, with
\langle{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=0
in the case
k=k^{\prime}
, the submodules
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)\cdot\xi
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k^{\prime})\cdot\xi^{\prime}
are orthogonal.

Based on these ingredients, we obtain the decomposition (
2.49
) by a recursive argument similar to the case of
\mathcal{V}_{bc}(N)
. Throughout this recursion, the parameters
z_{k,j}
are determined by the eigenvalues of
f
and
\Omega
respectively on
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(0)
and
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(1),\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(2),\dots,\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(h)
.

•

For
N=0
, we have
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(0)=\alpha_{0}=\mathrm{tr}[\widehat{K}\widehat{N}(m)]
.
Consider any orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
f
in
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(0)
. By definition, if we parameterise the corresponding eigenvalues as
\varphi_{j}=z_{0j}+z_{0j}^{-1}
with
z_{0j}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, then each of these eigenvectors is a seed state of type
(0,z_{0,j})
. We thus have

\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(0)\equiv\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\nu_{0}(m,K)}I_{0,z_{0j}}(0)\,,\qquad\text{with}\quad\nu_{0}(m,K)=\alpha_{0}\,.

(2.54)

Note that, if
(m,K)=(1,\mathbf{1})
, we have

f_{a}\cdot[x]=\sum_{y}N_{ax}^{y}\,[y]\,,

(2.55)

and thus the eigenvalues of
f_{a}
are
\varphi_{j}=\lambda_{j}(a)
.
If
m\neq 1
and
K=\mathbf{1}
, we still have the fusion relation

f_{a}\cdot f_{a^{\prime}}=\sum_{a^{\prime\prime}}N_{aa^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime\prime}}\,f_{a^{\prime\prime}}\,,

(2.56)

and thus the eigenvalues of
f_{a}
form a subset of
\{\lambda_{1}(a),\dots,\lambda_{n}(a)\}
.

•

For
N=1
, we have
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(1)=2\alpha_{1/2}=\mathrm{tr}[\widehat{K}\widehat{N}(m)\widehat{N}(a)]
. Any orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
\Omega
in
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(1)
, with eigenvalues
z_{1/2,j}
, yields a basis of seed states of type
(1/2,z_{1/2,j})
.
We thus have

\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(1)\equiv\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\nu_{1/2}(m,K)}I_{1/2,z_{j,1/2}}(1)\,,\qquad\text{with}\quad\nu_{1/2}(m,K)=2\alpha_{1/2}\,.

(2.57)

•

For
N\geq 2
, we assume that the decomposition (
2.49
) holds for
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(N-2)
, and we define the submodules of
\widehat{\cal V}_{m,K}(N)
:

[n]\times[a]=[n+1-a]
.
The only category automorphism is the identity.

Using the relation (
A.7

\widehat{N}(2)\,\widehat{N}(b)=\begin{cases}\widehat{N}(b-1)+\widehat{N}(b+1)&\text{if }2\leq b\leq n-1\\
\widehat{N}(2)&\text{if }b=1\\
\widehat{N}(n-1)&\text{if }b=n-1\,.\end{cases}

(4.2)

Hence, the fusion matrices for

\widehat{N}(b)=U_{b-1}[\widehat{N}(2)/2]
, where
U_{m}
is the
m

The fusion characters are
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}

\lambda_{j}(m)=\frac{\sin\frac{mj\pi}{h}}{\sin\frac{j\pi}{h}}=U_{m-1}\left[\frac{\lambda_{j}(2)}{2}\right]\,,\qquad h=n+1\,.

(4.3)

In particular, the quantum dimensions read

d(m)=\lambda_{1}(m)=\frac{\sin\frac{m\pi}{h}}{\sin\frac{\pi}{h}}=U_{m-1}\left[\frac{d(2)}{2}\right]\,.

(4.4)

The
F
-symbols are given in
[

– see also
[

.
The category is braided, and one has
R_{22}=q^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{22}+q^{-1/2}\psi_{1}^{22}\circ\psi_{22}^{1}
.
The

S_{ab}=S_{ba}=\frac{\lambda_{a}(b)}{|\lambda_{a}|}=\frac{\lambda_{b}(a)}{|\lambda_{b}|}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{h}}\sin\frac{\pi ab}{h}\,,\qquad h=n+1\,,

(4.5)

and the twist operator is

T=\mathrm{diag}(\theta_{s})_{s=1,\dots,h-1}\,,\qquad\theta_{s}=e^{i\pi(1-s^{2})/(2h)}\,.

(4.6)

The operators

Decomposition of the space of states.

We consider the case of twisted periodic boundary conditions, and we use the short-hand notation
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}:=\widehat{\cal V}_{m,\mathbf{1}}
for any
m=1,\dots,n
. Due to the bipartition of the adjacency graph (
4.7
), the space
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)
is non-zero only if
(m+N)
is odd. We shall then argue that the following decomposition holds

\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)\equiv\bigoplus_{k=-\frac{m-1}{2}}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\bigoplus_{\ell=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{h-\frac{m+1}{2}}\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(N)\,,

(4.9)

where
\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}:=I_{k,(-1)^{\ell+k}q^{\ell}}
as in (
B.23
).

Let us first show that the dimensions of the LHS and RHS of (
4.9
) agree. For the LHS, we use the dimension formula (
2.50
), which can be written

\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)=\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,h-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{k}(m)\,D_{k}(N)\,,\qquad\alpha_{k}(m)=\mathrm{tr}\left[\widehat{N}(m)T_{2k}\left(\frac{\widehat{N}(2)}{2}\right)\right]\,,

(4.10)

where
T_{2k}
denotes the
2k
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind.
Using simple trigonometric calculations, we obtain for any
k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,h-\tfrac{1}{2}

\alpha_{k}(1)=h\delta_{k,0}-1\,,\qquad\alpha_{k}(2)=h(\delta_{k,\frac{1}{2}}+\delta_{k,h-\frac{1}{2}})-2\,,

(4.11)

and

\alpha_{k}(m+1)-\alpha_{k}(m-1)=h(\delta_{k,\frac{m}{2}}+\delta_{k,h-\frac{m}{2}})-2\qquad\text{if }m\geq 2\,.

(4.12)

For
m=1
and
m=2
, this yields

\displaystyle\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{1}(N)=(h-1)D_{0}(N)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{h-1}D_{s}(N)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{h-1}\dim\mathcal{I}_{\ell\ell}(N)\,,

(4.13)

\displaystyle\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{2}(N)=2(h-2)D_{1/2}(N)-2\sum_{\ell=3/2}^{h-3/2}D_{s}(N)=\sum_{k=\pm 1/2}\sum_{\ell=3/2}^{h-3/2}\dim\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(N)\,.

For
m\geq 2
we get the recursion relation

\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m+1}(N)-\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m-1}(N)=2h\,D_{m/2}(N)-2\sum_{k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,h-\tfrac{1}{2}}D_{k}(N)\,.

(4.14)

On the other hand, the dimension of the RHS of (
4.9
) reads

\displaystyle\rho_{m}(N):=\sum_{k=-\frac{m-1}{2}}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{\ell=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{h-\frac{m+1}{2}}\dim\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\frac{m-1}{2}}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{\ell=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{h-\frac{m+1}{2}}[D_{k}(N)-D_{\ell}(N)]

\displaystyle=(h-m)\sum_{k=-\frac{m-1}{2}}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}D_{k}(N)-m\sum_{\ell=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{h-\frac{m+1}{2}}D_{\ell}(N)

(4.15)

After some simple algebra, using the symmetries
D_{k}=D_{-k}=D_{h+k}
, we find for any
m\geq 2

\rho_{m+1}(N)-\rho_{m-1}(N)=2h\,D_{m/2}(N)-2\sum_{k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,h-\tfrac{1}{2}}D_{k}(N)\,.

(4.16)

Comparing with (
4.14
), we see that
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)
and
\rho_{m}(N)
obey the same recursion. Since their initial values (
4.13
) at
m=1,2
also coincide, this proves that

\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)=\sum_{k=-\frac{m-1}{2}}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{\ell=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{h-\frac{m+1}{2}}\dim\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(N)

(4.17)

for any
m=1,2,\dots,n
.

As a next step to establish the decomposition (
4.9
), we need to compute the eigenvalues of
f
(resp.
\Omega
) on the space
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(0)
[resp. the spaces
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(1),\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(2),\dots,\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(m-1)
]. Note that these operators are given explicitly in (
2.13
) in terms of the
F
-symbols.

For
m=1
, recall that
f
acts as
\widehat{N}(2)
on
\widehat{\cal V}(0)
. Its eigenvalues read

\lambda_{1}(2),\lambda_{2}(2),\dots,\lambda_{n}(2)=2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{h}\right),2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{h}\right),\dots,2\cos\left(\frac{n\pi}{h}\right)\,,

(4.18)

and hence its eigenvectors are insertion states of type
(0,-q),(0,q^{2}),\dots,(0,(-q)^{n})
, since
-q=e^{-i\pi/h}
. Hence, the argument of Sec.
2.3
yields (
4.9
) for
\widehat{\cal V}(N)
.

For
m=3,5,7\dots
as argued in Sec.
2.3
, the eigenvalues of
f
on
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(0)
are all of the form
\lambda_{\ell}(2)=\cos(\pi\ell/h)
with
1\leq\ell\leq h-1

4.17
) yields
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(0)=h-m

on
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(0)
among the possible values of

(h-m)\times(h-m)

\left\{2\cos\frac{\pi\ell}{h}\,,\quad\ell=\frac{m+1}{2},\dots,h-\frac{m+1}{2}\right\}\,.

(4.19)

Similarly, Eq. (
4.17
) yields
\dim\widehat{\cal V}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(2k)=2(h-m)
for
k=1,2,3\dots
where
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}^{\prime\prime}

(4.20)

These spectra of

4.9
) for
\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)

For
m=2,4,6\dots

\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)=0

is even, we only need to consider the spectrum of

\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(1),\widehat{\cal V}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(3),\widehat{\cal V}_{m}^{\prime\prime}(5)\dots

4.2
Temperley-Lieb modular characters

Let us define the modular characters associated to a
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
module
A
. Let
M,N
be two non-negative integers, and
\mu
an element of
\mathsf{TL}(M+N)
. We represent
\mu
in a rectangle with
M
nodes on the vertical sides, and
N
nodes on the horizontal sides

The nodes on the left and bottom sides are interpreted as the incoming nodes, and those on the top and right sides are the outgoing nodes.
For instance,
(\mathbf{1}+ve_{1})
corresponds to a single face,
(\mathbf{1}+ve_{2})(\mathbf{1}+ve_{1})
corresponds to a system of size
2\times 1
, etc.
For any
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
module
A
and any
\mu\in\mathsf{TL}(M+N)
we define the characters

\displaystyle\chi_{A}(\mu,M,N):=\mathrm{tr}_{A(N)}\left[c_{N+1}c_{N+2}\dots c_{N+M}\cdot\mu\cdot(c_{0}^{\dagger})^{M}\right]\,,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}_{A}(\mu,M,N):=\mathrm{tr}_{A(M)}\left[c_{1}c_{2}\dots c_{N}\cdot\mu\cdot(c_{0}^{\dagger})^{N}\right]\,.

In the following, to lighten the notation, we shall write
\chi_{A}(\mu)
and
\widetilde{\chi}_{A}(\mu)
instead of
\chi_{A}(\mu,M,N)
and
\widetilde{\chi}_{A}(\mu,M,N)
.
Note that
c_{N+1}c_{N+2}\dots c_{N+M}\mu(c_{0}^{\dagger})^{M}
[resp.
c_{1}c_{2}\dots c_{N}\mu(c_{0}^{\dagger})^{N}
] is an element of
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N)
[resp.
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(M)
], obtained by connecting together the left and right (resp. top and bottom) sides of
\mu
. For any
\mu\in\mathsf{TL}(M+N)
, we define the diagram
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathsf{TL}(M+N)
as

\widetilde{\mu}:=c_{N+M+1}c_{N+M+2}\dots c_{N+2M}\cdot(\mathbf{1}_{M}\otimes\mu\otimes\mathbf{1}_{M})\cdot c^{\dagger}_{M}c^{\dagger}_{M-1}\dots c^{\dagger}_{1}\,.

(4.25)

With this definition, we obtain
\widetilde{\chi}_{A}(\mu,M,N)=\chi_{A}(\widetilde{\mu},N,M)
.
Thus, graphically, for instance with
N=4
and
M=3
, we have

\chi_{A}(\mu)=\mathrm{tr}_{A(N)}\left[\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{annulus.hor2.pdf}}\right]\,,\qquad\chi_{A}(\widetilde{\mu})=\mathrm{tr}_{A(M)}\left[\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{annulus.ver2.pdf}}\right]\,,

(4.26)

where the dotted lines indicate periodic boundary conditions.

In the following, we consider the algebra
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
of loop weight
\beta=-q-q^{-1}
, with
q=e^{i\pi(h-1)/h}
, where
h
is a positive integer. The simple modules are of the form

\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}=I_{k,(-1)^{\ell+k}q^{\ell}}\,,\qquad|k|\leq\frac{h}{2}-1\,,\quad 1\leq\ell\leq h-1\,,\quad\ell+k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(4.27)

and we use the short-hand notation
\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\mu):=\chi_{\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}}(\mu)
for the TL modular characters.

In the
A_{n}
model, since the twisted boundary condition
(m,\mathbf{1})
and the topological operators
Y_{m},\overline{Y}_{\!\!m}
consist in inserting the weights (
2.16
) respectively on a vertical and horizontal closed path, we obtain the relation

\chi_{\widehat{\cal V}_{m}}(Y_{p}\cdot\mu)=\chi_{\widehat{\cal V}_{p}}(\overline{Y}_{\!\!m}\cdot\widetilde{\mu})\,,

(4.28)

for any
\mu\in\mathsf{TL}(M+N)
and any pair of integers
(m,p)
with
1\leq m,p\leq n
.
We note that the decomposition (
4.9
) can be written as

\widehat{\cal V}_{m}(N)\equiv\bigoplus_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle|k|\leq\frac{h}{2}-1\\[-4.0pt]
\scriptstyle 1\leq\ell\leq h-1\\[-4.0pt]
\scriptstyle\ell+k\in\mathbb{Z}\end{array}}N_{\ell+k,\ell-k}^{m}\,\mathcal{I}_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(N)\,.

(4.29)

Hence, using (
4.23
) we obtain

\sum_{\ell,k}N_{\ell+k,\ell-k}^{m}\,\lambda_{\ell+k}(p)\,\chi_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(\mu)=(-1)^{MN}\sum_{i,j}N_{i+j,i-j}^{p}\,\lambda_{i-j}(m)\,\chi_{i+j,i-j}(\widetilde{\mu})\,.

(4.30)

We fix two integers
s,\bar{t}
such that
1\leq s,\bar{t}\leq n
, we multiply the above relation by
the factor

\frac{\lambda_{s}(p)\lambda_{\bar{t}}(m)}{|\lambda_{s}|^{2}|\lambda_{\bar{t}}|^{2}}

and we sum over
m
and
p
. This yields

\sum_{\ell+k=s}\frac{\lambda_{\bar{t}}(\ell-k)}{|\lambda_{\bar{t}}|}\,\chi_{\ell+k,\ell-k}(\mu)=(-1)^{MN}\sum_{i-j=\bar{t}}\frac{\lambda_{s}(i+j)}{|\lambda_{s}|}\,\chi_{i+j,i-j}(\widetilde{\mu})\,.

(4.31)

where we have used (
4.5
), (
A.8
) and (
A.10
).
We now fix an integer
\bar{s}
such that
1\leq\bar{s}\leq n
, we multiply by
\lambda_{\bar{s}}(\bar{t})/|\lambda_{\bar{s}}|
, and we sum over
\bar{t}
, which gives

\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\mu)=(-1)^{MN}\sum_{1\leq t,\bar{t}\leq n}\frac{\lambda_{s}(t)\lambda_{\bar{s}}(\bar{t})}{|\lambda_{s}||\lambda_{\bar{s}}|}\,\chi_{t,\bar{t}}(\widetilde{\mu})\,.

(4.32)

Recall that
\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}(N)
is nonzero only if
(N+s+\bar{s})
is even. Hence the above relation can be written as

\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\mu)=\sum_{t,\bar{t}=1}^{h-1}\mathcal{S}_{(s,\bar{s}),(t,\bar{t})}\,\chi_{t,\bar{t}}(\widetilde{\mu})\,,\qquad\mathcal{S}_{(s,\bar{s}),(t,\bar{t})}=(-1)^{(s+\bar{s})(t+\bar{t})}S_{st}\,S_{\bar{s}\bar{t}}\,,

(4.33)

where
(S_{st})
is the
S
-matrix of the
A_{n}
category, given in (
4.5
).

The twist operator
\mathcal{T}=\Omega^{N}
has eigenvalue
[(-1)^{s}q^{(s+\bar{s})/2}]^{s-\bar{s}}
on
\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}(N)
. Comparing with (
4.6
), we obtain

\chi_{s,\bar{s}}\left(\Omega^{N}\mu\right)=\theta_{s,\bar{s}}\,\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\mu)\,,\qquad\theta_{s,\bar{s}}=e^{-i\pi(s+\bar{s})^{2}/2}\,\theta_{s}\,\theta_{\bar{s}}^{*}\,.

(4.34)

After some simple algebra, using the properties of
S
and
T
, we obtain

\mathcal{S}^{2}=\mathbf{1}\,,\qquad(\mathcal{ST})^{3}=\mathbf{1}\,,

(4.35)

where
\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{diag}(\theta_{s,\bar{s}})
. Hence, the modular characters
\chi_{s,\bar{s}}
form a representation of the modular group.

Applying the Verlinde formula to the
\mathcal{S}
-matrix, we obtain the fusion numbers

\mathcal{N}_{(s,\bar{s}),(t,\bar{t})}^{(u,\bar{u})}:=\sum_{1\leq v,\bar{v}\leq h-1}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{(s,\bar{s}),(v,\bar{v})}\mathcal{S}_{(t,\bar{t}),(v,\bar{v})}\mathcal{S}_{(u,\bar{u}),(v,\bar{v})}}{\mathcal{S}_{(1,1),(v,\bar{v})}}=N_{st}^{u}\,N_{\bar{s}\bar{t}}^{\bar{u}}\,,

(4.36)

where
N_{ab}^{c}
is given in (
4.1

This suggests that the fusion rules for the simple modules under the
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
fusion product
[
29

are given by

\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{t,\bar{t}}\equiv\sum_{1\leq u,\bar{u}\leq h-1}N_{st}^{u}\,N_{\bar{s}\bar{t}}^{\bar{u}}\,\mathcal{I}_{u,\bar{u}}\,.

(4.37)

Scaling limit.

The monodromy matrix
L_{N}\in\mathsf{TL}(N+1)
and the row-to-row transfer matrix
t_{N}\in\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N)
are defined as

L_{N}:=(\mathbf{1}+ve_{N})\dots(\mathbf{1}+ve_{2})(\mathbf{1}+ve_{1})\,,\qquad t_{N}:=c_{N+1}\cdot L_{N}\cdot c^{\dagger}_{0}\,,

(4.38)

For instance, in any face model defined as in Sec.

(4.39)

To make contact with Virasoro characters, one considers the following collection of TL characters. For any
\tau\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathrm{Im}\,\tau>0
and any non-negative integer

M_{1}(N,\tau):=\lfloor\mathrm{Re}(N\tau)/2\rfloor\,,\qquad M_{2}(N,\tau):=\lfloor\mathrm{Im}(N\tau)/2\rfloor\,,

where

From the scaling (
B.31

\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\tau,m_{1},m_{2})=\sum_{r=1}^{h^{\prime}-1}(-1)^{(r+s)(m_{1}+m_{2})}\chi^{\rm Vir}_{r,s}(\tau)\,\chi^{\rm Vir}_{r,\bar{s}}(\tau)^{*}

(4.42)

where
\chi^{\rm Vir}_{r,s}
is the Virasoro character of the irreducible module with heighest weight

.

For simplicity, we restrict to the case when

As
N\to\infty

\displaystyle\chi_{s,\bar{s}}(\tau,0,m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{h^{\prime}-1}(-1)^{(r+s)m}\chi^{\rm Vir}_{r,s}(\tau)\,\chi^{\rm Vir}_{r,\bar{s}}(\tau)^{*}

(4.43)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho=1}^{h^{\prime}-1}\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}\scriptstyle t,\bar{t}=1\\[-4.0pt]
\scriptstyle m+t+\bar{t}\text{ even}\end{array}}^{h-1}(-1)^{(s+\bar{s})(\rho+t+m)}S_{st}\,S_{\bar{s}\bar{t}}\,\chi^{\rm Vir}_{\rho,t}(-1/\tau)\chi^{\rm Vir}_{\rho,\bar{t}}(-1/\tau)^{*}\,,

which is consistent with the well-known expression of the

\mathcal{M}(h,h^{\prime})
minimal CFT

S^{\rm Vir}_{(r,s),(\rho,\sigma)}=\sqrt{\frac{8}{hh^{\prime}}}(-1)^{(r+s)(\rho+\sigma)}\sin\frac{\pi r\rho}{h^{\prime}}\sin\frac{\pi s\sigma}{h}\,.

(4.44)

5
Conclusion

The decomposition of the space of states under the spectrum-generating algebra is a cornerstone for the symmetry analysis of any discrete or continuous model. In rational CFTs, the Hilbert space is usually determined, following
[
33
]
or
[
34
]
, by consistency conditions on the modular partition functions. In the present work, for any face model with TL interactions associated to a fusion category, we have presented a systematic way of computing this decomposition from first principles. Moreover, applying this approach to the unitary series of RSOS models of
[
15
]
, we have obtained the modular transformation of TL characters, which is a fundamental result for the representation theory of the affine TL algebra.

On the three specific examples of critical models we have considered, the topological operators of the form
Y_{p}
and
Q_{L}
generate the algebra of
\mathsf{TL}
or
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
endomorphisms, say
\mathrm{End}(\widehat{\cal V})
for periodic boundary conditions. In contrast, for face models with a loop weight
\beta\geq 2
such as the psu(2)
5
face model, the dimension of
\mathrm{End}(\widehat{\cal V})
is infinite, whereas the operators
Y_{p}
and
Q_{L}
can only generate a finite-dimensional algebra. It would be interesting to find the concrete form of the other endomorphisms in such cases.

Another perspective for future work is concerned with the formula (
4.36
) for the fusion numbers among simple
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules
\mathcal{I}_{s\bar{s}}
. Indeed, the validity of the Verlinde relies on a set of conditions on the category of
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules generated by the
\mathcal{I}_{s\bar{s}}
, which one would need to verify. Alternatively, one could think of calculating from first principles the decomposition of the fusion product
\mathcal{I}_{s\bar{s}}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{t,\bar{t}}
, and check if it agrees with the fusion numbers (
4.36
).

Appendix A
Fusion categories

Fusion category.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
consists essentially in the following data
[
7
]
:

1.

a finite set of simple objects
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, equipped with some associative fusion rules

a\otimes b=\bigoplus_{c=1}^{n}N_{ab}^{c}\,c\,,

(A.1)

where
N_{ab}^{c}
is a non-negative integer, and such that the object
1
is the neutral element, i.e.
N_{1a}^{b}=N_{a1}^{b}=\delta_{ab}
,

2.

for any triplet
a,b,c
of simple objects, two vector spaces
V_{ab}^{c}
and
V_{c}^{ab}
, encoding the morphisms
(a\otimes b)\to c
and
c\to(a\otimes b)
respectively, with
\dim V_{ab}^{c}=\dim V_{c}^{ab}=N_{ab}^{c}
, and an antilinear involution
{\dagger}
between
V_{ab}^{c}
and
V_{c}^{ab}
,

3.

an involution on simple objects
a\leftrightarrow\bar{a}
, such that
N_{a\bar{a}}^{1}=N_{\bar{a}a}^{1}=1
(
\bar{a}
is called the dual of
a
), and for each pair
(a,\bar{a})
, a pair of morphisms
\xi_{a}\in V_{1}^{a\bar{a}}
and
\eta_{a}\in V_{1}^{\bar{a}a}
such that

(\mathbf{1}_{a}\otimes\eta_{a}^{\dagger})\circ(\xi_{a}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{a})=(\mathbf{1}_{a}\otimes\xi_{a}^{\dagger})\circ(\eta_{a}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{a})=\mathbf{1}_{a}
and
\xi_{a}^{\dagger}\circ\xi_{a}=\eta_{a}^{\dagger}\circ\eta_{a}:=d(a)
– the number
d(a)
is called the quantum dimension of
a
,

4.

a collection of
F
-symbols
F_{u}^{abc}
encoding the associativity isomorphism
\oplus_{x}(V_{x}^{ab}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{c})\circ V_{u}^{xc}\to\oplus_{y}(\mathbf{1}_{a}\otimes V_{y}^{bc})\circ V_{u}^{ay}
,
and obeying the pentagon equation.

For simplicity, in the present work we make the following assumptions:

1.

the fusion numbers are symmetric, i.e.
N_{ab}^{c}=N_{ba}^{c}
for any
a,b,c
,

2.

the fusion rules are multiplicity-free, i.e.
N_{ab}^{c}\in\{0,1\}
for any
a,b,c
,

3.

the category
\mathcal{C}
is unitary, i.e.
d(a)>0
for any
a
.

Then every morphism space
V_{ab}^{c}
(resp.
V_{c}^{ab}
) is spanned by a single state
\psi_{ab}^{c}
[resp.
\psi_{c}^{ab}=(\psi_{ab}^{c})^{\dagger}
], and the states can be normalised so that

\psi_{ab}^{c}\circ\psi_{c}^{ab}=\sqrt{\frac{d(a)d(b)}{d(c)}}\,\mathbf{1}_{c}\,,\qquad\mathbf{1}_{ab}=\sum_{c=1}^{n}\sqrt{\frac{d(c)}{d(a)d(b)}}\,\psi_{c}^{ab}\circ\psi_{ab}^{c}\,.

(A.2)

The quantum dimensions obey the relation

d(a)d(b)=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}\,d(c)\,.

(A.3)

The associativity relations (also called
F
-moves) read

(\psi_{x}^{ab}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{c})\circ\psi_{u}^{xc}=\sum_{y=1}^{n}\left(F_{u}^{abc}\right)_{yx}\,(\mathbf{1}_{a}\otimes\psi_{y}^{bc})\circ\psi_{u}^{ay}\,.

(A.4)

Furthermore, to simplify the calculations, we assume that

\eta_{a}=\xi_{\bar{a}}\,,\qquad(\eta_{b}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{c})\circ(\mathbf{1}_{\bar{b}}\otimes\psi_{a}^{bc})=\psi_{\bar{b}a}^{c}\,,\qquad(\mathbf{1}_{b}\otimes\xi_{c})\circ(\psi_{a}^{bc}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\bar{c}})=\psi_{a\bar{c}}^{b}\,,

(A.5)

i.e. we suppose that the Schur-Frobenius indicators and the index raising and lowering factors (denoted
\kappa_{a},A_{a}^{bc},B_{a}^{bc}
in
[
7
]
) are all equal to one – these factors can be easily restored to recover the general case.

We use the following conventions for graphical calculus:

\psi_{ab}^{c}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{psi.ab.c2.pdf}}\qquad\psi_{c}^{ab}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{psi.c.ab2.pdf}}\qquad\xi_{a}=\psi_{1}^{a\bar{a}}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{psi.1.aa2.pdf}}\qquad\xi_{a}^{\dagger}=\psi_{a\bar{a}}^{1}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{psi.aa.12.pdf}}

(A.6)

Fusion algebra.

For each simple object
a
, one defines the
n\times n
adjacency matrix
[\widehat{N}(a)]_{bc}:=N_{ab}^{c}
. The relation (
A.3
) means that
(d(1),\dots,d(n))
is an eigenvector of
\widehat{N}(a)
, with eigenvalue
d(a)
. By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, the quantum dimension
d(a)
is thus equal to the dominant eigenvalue (i.e. the eigenvalue with maximal modulus) of
\widehat{N}(a)
.

Using the associativity of the fusion rules (
A.1
), one shows that

\widehat{N}(a)\cdot\widehat{N}(b)=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}\,\widehat{N}(c)\,.

(A.7)

In particular, the matrices
\widehat{N}(a)
commute with one another, and since
\widehat{N}(\bar{a})=\widehat{N}(a)^{\dagger}
, they are normal matrices. Thus, they can be diagonalised simultaneously in an orthogonal basis
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
. Let
\lambda_{j}(a)
be the eigenvalue of
\widehat{N}(a)
associated to
v_{j}
. The relation (
A.7
) yields

\lambda_{j}(a)\,\lambda_{j}(b)=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}\,\lambda_{j}(c)\,,

(A.8)

and thus
(\lambda_{j}(1),\dots,\lambda_{j}(n))
is an eigenvector of
\widehat{N}(a)
, with eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}(a)
. Thus, we can choose the basis of common eigenvectors of the matrices
\widehat{N}(a)
as

\lambda_{j}=(\lambda_{j}(1),\dots,\lambda_{j}(n))\,,\qquad j=1,\dots,n\,.

(A.9)

These vectors are called the fusion characters of the algebra (
A.1
). They are orthogonal to one another:

\sum_{a=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(a)^{*}\lambda_{j}(a)=\delta_{ij}\,|\lambda_{j}|^{2}\,,\qquad|\lambda_{j}|:=\sqrt{\sum_{a=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}(a)|^{2}}\,.

(A.10)

By convention, we set
\lambda_{1}=(d(1),\dots,d(n))
.

An automorphism of
\mathcal{C}
is a permutation
a\mapsto K(a)
of the simple objects, such that

N_{K(a)K(b)}^{K(c)}=N_{ab}^{c}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad\left(F_{K(u)}^{K(a)K(b)K(c)}\right)_{K(y)K(x)}=\left(F_{u}^{abc}\right)_{yx}\,.

(A.11)

Let us introduce the matrix
\widehat{K}
, with coefficients
\widehat{K}_{ab}:=\delta_{a,K(b)}
. We then have
\widehat{N}[K(a)]=\widehat{K}\widehat{N}(a)\widehat{K}^{-1}
, and hence
d[K(a)]=d(a)
.

Braiding.

If the category
\mathcal{C}
is braided, for each pair of simple objects
(a,b)
, there exists a pair of braid operators
R_{ab},\overline{R}_{ab}
acting on morphisms as

R_{ab}\circ\psi_{c}^{ab}=R_{c}^{ab}\,\psi_{c}^{ba}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{R2.pdf}}\qquad\overline{R}_{ab}\circ\psi_{c}^{ab}=\overline{R}_{c}^{ab}\,\psi_{c}^{ba}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{Rb2.pdf}}

(A.12)

with
R_{c}^{ab},\overline{R}_{c}^{ab}\in\mathbb{C}
, and obeying the hexagon equations.

Temperley-Lieb diagrams and algebras.

Consider the Temperley-Lieb algebra
\mathsf{TL}(N)
with loop weight
\beta

e_{1},\dots,e_{N-1}
, subject to (

e_{j}^{2}=\beta\,e_{j}\,,\qquad e_{j}e_{j\pm 1}e_{j}=e_{j}\,,\qquad e_{j}e_{k}=e_{k}e_{j}\quad\text{if }|j-k|>1\,.

(B.1)

The operator
e_{j}
is depicted as the diagram

e_{j}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{ej2.pdf}}\,\,.

(B.2)

Following
[
20
]

\mathsf{TL}(N)

\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})

N

N^{\prime}
nodes on the bottom boundary.
Moreover, we define

\lambda^{\dagger}\in\mathsf{TL}(N^{\prime},N)

The diagram space
\mathsf{TL}(N-2,N)
[resp.
\mathsf{TL}(N,N-2)

(resp.

c_{j}:=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{cj2.pdf}}\,,\qquad c^{\dagger}_{j}:=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{cdagj2.pdf}}\,.

(B.3)

Here, by convention, the operators act from bottom to top.
The defining algebraic relations for these diagrams are

\begin{cases}c_{j}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,c_{j+1}^{\dagger}=c_{j+1}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{1}\\
c_{j}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}=\beta\,\mathbf{1}\end{cases}\qquad\begin{cases}c_{j}c_{k}=c_{k}c_{j+2}\\
c^{\dagger}_{k}c^{\dagger}_{j}=c^{\dagger}_{j+2}c^{\dagger}_{k}\\

N

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{j}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,c_{j+1}^{\dagger}=c_{j+1}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\,c_{j}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{1}&\text{if }j\geq 1\\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{j}c_{k}=c_{k}c_{j+2}\\
c^{\dagger}_{k}c^{\dagger}_{j}=c^{\dagger}_{j+2}c^{\dagger}_{k}\\
c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{k}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}=c_{k}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c^{\dagger}_{j+2}\end{cases}\quad\text{if }1\leq k\leq j

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{0}c_{j}=c_{j-1}c_{0}\\
c_{0}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c^{\dagger}_{j}=c^{\dagger}_{j-1}c_{0}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\\
c_{j}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c_{0}^{\dagger}=c_{0}^{\dagger}c_{j-1}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}\\
c^{\dagger}_{j}c_{0}^{\dagger}=c_{0}^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}_{j-1}\end{cases}\quad\text{if }2\leq j\leq N-2

c_{0}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c_{1}^{\dagger}\cdot c_{1}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c_{0}^{\dagger}=c_{1}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c_{0}^{\dagger}\cdot c_{0}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}c_{1}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{1}\end{cases}

where by convention, the rightmost

(resp.

\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N-2,N)
[resp.
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,N-2)
].
One recovers the affine Temperley-Lieb algebra

, by forming the generators

\displaystyle e_{j}=c^{\dagger}_{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}\,,\quad j=0,\dots,N-1

(B.6)

\displaystyle\Omega=c_{1}c_{0}^{\dagger}\,,\quad\Omega^{\dagger}=c_{0}c_{1}^{\dagger}\,,

\displaystyle\text{if }N\geq 1\,,

\displaystyle f=c_{1}c_{0}^{\dagger}=c_{0}c_{1}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\text{if }N=0\,.

Here, the shift operators
\Omega,\Omega^{\dagger}
correspond to the diagrams

\Omega=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{Omega2.pdf}}\,\,,\qquad\Omega^{\dagger}=\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{Omega.dag2.pdf}}\,\,,

(B.7)

whereas

Modules over
\mathsf{TL}
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
.

Following
[
20
]
, one defines a
\mathsf{TL}
-module
as a collection

A=(A(0),A(1),A(2),\dots)

of modules over
\mathsf{TL}(0),\mathsf{TL}(1),\mathsf{TL}(2),\dots
with an action
\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime}):A(N^{\prime})\to A(N)
obeying the relations (
B.4
). Since
\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})=0
for any
N,N^{\prime}
with different parities, any
\mathsf{TL}
-module decomposes as

A=A^{\rm even}\oplus A^{\rm odd}=(A(0),0,A(2),0,A(4),\dots)\oplus(0,A(1),0,A(3),0,A(5),\dots)\,.

(B.8)

For any pair of modules
(A,B)
, a morphism
\phi:A\to B
is a collection
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots)
where
\phi_{N}
is a linear map from
A(N)
to
B(N)
, and

\phi_{N}(\lambda\cdot u)=\lambda\cdot\phi_{N^{\prime}}(u)\,,

(B.9)

for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
and
u\in A(N^{\prime})
.
The same definitions apply to
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-modules.

Standard modules.

The standard modules (also called Weyl modules)
[
20
]
are an important class of
\mathsf{TL}
or
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules, which play a role analogous to heighest-weight modules of Lie algebras. Here, we review some basic facts on these modules – see
[
20
]
or
[
30
]
for more details.

We denote by
W_{k}
the standard modules over
\mathsf{TL}
, with
2k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The basis states of
W_{k}(N)
are connectivity states on
N
nodes sitting on the boundary of a half-strip, with
2k
legs and
(N/2-k)
arches, and the
\mathsf{TL}
diagrams act on them by connecting the loop segments (if
N-2k
is odd or
N<2k
, then
W_{k}(N)=0
).
The dimension of
W_{k}(N)
reads

\dim W_{k}(N)=d_{k}(N)-d_{k+1}(N)\,,

(B.10)

where for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}/2

d_{k}(N):=\begin{cases}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}N\\
\frac{N}{2}-k\end{array}\right)&\text{if }|k|\leq\frac{N}{2}\text{ and }N+2k\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(B.11)

At
N=2k
, the module
W_{k}(2k)
is one-dimensional, with a single state made of
2k
legs, and denoted
u_{k}
. For any system size
N
, the action of
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)
on
u_{k}
generates
W_{k}(N)
:

W_{k}(N)=\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot u_{k}\,.

(B.12)

The Gram form on
W_{k}
, denoted
\langle\!\langle{\,,\,}\rangle\!\rangle
, is defined as the unique bilinear form

W_{k}(N)\times W_{k}(N)\to\mathbb{C}
which is invariant, i.e.

\langle\!\langle{u,\lambda\,v}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle{\lambda^{t}\,u,v}\rangle\!\rangle

for any
u\in W_{k}(N),\ v\in W_{k}(N^{\prime})
and
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
, and such that
\langle\!\langle{u_{k},u_{k}}\rangle\!\rangle=1
.
Here the transposition is defined as the linear operator which maps any diagram
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
to its image
\lambda^{t}\in\mathsf{TL}(N^{\prime},N)
under the reflection around the horizontal axis (note that this is similar to
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{\dagger}
, except that the latter is antilinear).

We denote by
W_{k,z}
the standard modules over
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
, where
2k
is the number of legs, and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the twist parameter. The space of states is similar to that of
W_{k}
, except that the arches connect nodes living on the boundary of a half-cylinder. The
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
diagrams act by connecting the loop segments, and produce twist factors
z^{\pm 1}
(resp.
z+z^{-1}
) as a leg crosses the boundary conditions (resp. as a non-contractible loop is formed).
In particular, the operator
\Omega^{N}
acts as
z^{2k}\mathbf{1}
in
W_{k,x}
.
The dimension of
W_{k,z}(N)
reads

\dim W_{k,z}(N)=d_{k}(N)\,,

(B.13)

where
d_{k}(N)
is given in (
B.11
).
Like for
W_{k}
, the module
W_{k,z}(2k)
is one-dimensional, and we denote by
u_{k}
its unique basis state. We then have

W_{k,z}(N)=\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)\cdot u_{k}\,.

(B.14)

Similarly to
W_{k}
, one defines the Gram form on
W_{k,z}
as the unique invariant bilinear form

W_{k,1/z}(N)\times W_{k,z}(N)\to\mathbb{C}
.

Simple modules.

A simple module is a non-zero module which does not admit any non-trivial submodule.
Recall that a maximal submodule
A^{\prime}
of
A
is a submodule such that
A/A^{\prime}
is simple, and the radical of
A
, denoted
\mathrm{rad}(A)
, is defined as the intersection of all its maximal submodules. A central result of
[
20
]
is the following property on standard modules: the radical
\mathrm{rad}(W_{k})
[resp.
\mathrm{rad}(W_{k,z})
] is equal to the kernel of the Gram form in
W_{k}
(resp.
W_{k,z}
), i.e.

\displaystyle\mathrm{rad}[W_{k}(N)]=\big\{v\in W_{k}(N)\ |\ \langle\!\langle{u,v}\rangle\!\rangle=0\text{ for any }u\in W_{k}(N)\big\}\,,

(B.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{rad}[W_{k,z}(N)]=\big\{v\in W_{k,z}(N)\ |\ \langle\!\langle{u,v}\rangle\!\rangle=0\text{ for any }u\in W_{k,1/z}(N)\big\}\,.

Using this result,
[
20
]
showed that the simple modules over
\mathsf{TL}
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
are all the modules of the form, respectively

I_{k}:=W_{k}\big/\mathrm{rad}(W_{k})\qquad\text{with }2k\in\mathbb{Z}

(B.16)

and

I_{k,z}:=W_{k,z}\big/\mathrm{rad}(W_{k,z})\qquad\text{with }2k\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,.

(B.17)

To describe these modules more completely, it is convenient to parameterise the loop weight as

\beta=-q-q^{-1}\,,\qquad q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\,.

(B.18)

We then distinguish two cases.

1.

If
q
is a root of unity, then we write
q=e^{i\pi h^{\prime}/h}
where
h,h^{\prime}
are two integers such that
0<h^{\prime}<h
and
\mathrm{gcd}(h,h^{\prime})=1
. In this case, we shall restrict our attention to particular subsets of
\mathsf{TL}
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
simple modules. For
k=0,\tfrac{1}{2},\dots,\tfrac{h}{2}-1
, one has
W_{k}\equiv[I_{k}\to I_{h-k-1}]

[
31
]
, and thus the dimensions of the corresponding simple modules read

\dim I_{k}(N)=D_{k}(N)-D_{k+1}(N)\,,

(B.19)

where

D_{k}(N):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}d_{k+jh}(N)\,.

(B.20)

For
k,m\in\mathbb{Z}/2
and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}
with
0\leq k<m<h-k
and
m-k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the module
W_{k,\varepsilon q^{m}}
has the double-ladder structure (see
[
30
]
)

W_{k,\varepsilon q^{m}}\equiv\left[\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[]{ladder2.pdf}}\right]

(B.21)

and similarly for
W_{k,\varepsilon q^{-m}}
, up to the change
q\to q^{-1}
.
The dimensions of the corresponding simple modules read

\dim I_{k,\varepsilon q^{\pm m}}(N)=D_{k}(N)-D_{m}(N)\,.

(B.22)

For the particular choice of sign
\varepsilon=(-1)^{m+k}
, we shall denote these simple modules as

I_{k,(-1)^{m+k}q^{m}}:=\mathcal{I}_{m+k,m-k}\,.

(B.23)

2.

If
q
is not a root of unity then, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2
, the module
W_{k}
is simple, and hence
I_{k}=W_{k}
.
For the
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
standard modules, there are two subcases. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}/2
, if
z
is of the form
z=\varepsilon q^{\sigma m}
with
\varepsilon,\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}
and
m\in k+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, then one has
W_{k,\varepsilon q^{\sigma m}}\equiv[I_{k,\varepsilon q^{\sigma m}}\to I_{m,\varepsilon q^{\sigma k}}]
. Otherwise,
W_{k,z}
is simple, and hence
I_{k,z}=W_{k,z}
.

Braid transfer matrices.

Let
y\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
y^{2}=q
or
y^{2}=q^{-1}
.
The operators
b_{j},\bar{b}_{j}
defined as

b_{j}:=y\,\mathbf{1}+y^{-1}\,e_{j}\,,\qquad\bar{b}_{j}:=y^{-1}\,\mathbf{1}+y\,e_{j}

satisfy the braid relations

b_{j}b_{j+1}b_{j}=b_{j+1}b_{j}b_{j+1}\,,\qquad\bar{b}_{j}\bar{b}_{j+1}\bar{b}_{j}=\bar{b}_{j+1}\bar{b}_{j}\bar{b}_{j+1}\,,\qquad b_{j}\bar{b}_{j}=\bar{b}_{j}b_{j}=\mathbf{1}\,.

(B.25)

The braid transfer matrices for
\mathsf{TL}(N)
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N)
read, respectively

G=c_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\dots b_{N+1}b_{N+1}\dots b_{3}b_{2}c^{\dagger}_{1}=c_{1}\bar{b}_{2}\bar{b}_{3}\dots\bar{b}_{N+1}\bar{b}_{N+1}\dots\bar{b}_{3}\bar{b}_{2}c^{\dagger}_{1}\,,

(B.26)

and

F=c_{N+1}\bar{b}_{N}\bar{b}_{N-1}\dots\bar{b}_{1}\,c^{\dagger}_{0}\,,\qquad\overline{F}=c_{N+1}b_{N}b_{N-1}\dots b_{1}\,c^{\dagger}_{0}\,,

(B.27)

The operators
G
and
F,\overline{F}
are central elements of
\mathsf{TL}(N)
and
\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N)
respectively, and they act in simple modules as

G\big|_{I_{k}(N)}=(-q^{2k+1}-q^{-2k-1})\,\mathbf{1}\,,\qquad\begin{cases}F\big|_{I_{k,z}(N)}=(y^{2k}z+y^{-2k}z^{-1})\,\mathbf{1}\,,\\
\overline{F}\big|_{I_{k,z}(N)}=(y^{2k}z^{-1}+y^{-2k}z)\,\mathbf{1}\,.\end{cases}

(B.28)

B.2
Scaling limit

The scaling limit considered in this paper consists in setting all the coupling constants to
v=1
in (
2.1
), and computing partition functions on an
N\times M
lattice in the limit
M,N\to\infty
. Equivalently, this limit is encoded in the low-energy spectrum of the Hamiltonian

H=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_{j}

as
N\to\infty
.
The following results derive from the isomorphism
[
32
]
between the standard module
W_{k,w}
and the vector space with magnetisation
S^{z}=k
in the XXZ spin chain with anisotropy parameter
\Delta=\beta/2
, and diagonal twisted boundary conditions
(\sigma^{x}\pm i\sigma^{y})\to w^{\pm 1}(\sigma^{x}\pm i\sigma^{y})
and
\sigma^{z}\to\sigma^{z}
.

If
q
is a root of unity, we write
q=e^{i\pi h^{\prime}/h}
as above.
The related XXZ spin chain has
\Delta=\cos(\pi h^{\prime}/h)
and thus it is in the critical phase.
The scaling limit of the simple
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-modules is then described in terms of CFT, with the Kac parameterisation of the central charge and conformal weights

c=1-\frac{6(h-h^{\prime})^{2}}{hh^{\prime}}\,,\qquad\Delta_{rs}=\frac{(rh-sh^{\prime})^{2}-(h-h^{\prime})^{2}}{4hh^{\prime}}\,.

(B.29)

Let
s,\bar{s}
be two integers such that
1\leq s,\bar{s}\leq h-1
. As
N\to\infty
, the low-energy part of
\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}(N)
(
B.23
) scales to

\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}(N)\to\bigoplus_{r=1}^{h^{\prime}-1}[\Delta_{rs},\Delta_{r\bar{s}}]\,,

(B.30)

where
[\Delta,\bar{\Delta}]
denotes the irreducible
(\mathrm{Vir}\otimes\overline{\rm Vir})
module with heighest weights
\Delta
and
\bar{\Delta}
, and
(\mathrm{Vir},\overline{\rm Vir})
are two copies of the Virasoro algebra of central charge
c
.
Moreover, on the states of
\mathcal{I}_{s,\bar{s}}
which scale to
[\Delta_{rs},\Delta_{r\bar{s}}]
, the transfer matrix and shift operator act as

t_{N}\to(-1)^{r+s}\,\exp\left[-\frac{2\pi}{N}(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{c}{12})\right]\,,\qquad\Omega\to(-1)^{r+s}\,\exp\left[-\frac{2i\pi}{N}(L_{0}-\bar{L}_{0})\right]\,.

(B.31)

The other case which is considered in the present work is when the loop weight
\beta
is greater than or equal to
2
. The related XXZ spin chain has
\Delta>1
and thus it is in the gapped phase. The face model is not critical, and the correlation length remains finite in the scaling limit.

B.3
Seed states

Let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}
-module. A seed state
\xi
of type
k
in
A
is a nonzero state
\xi\in A(2k)
such that
c_{1}\,\xi=\dots=c_{2k-1}\,\xi=0
, i.e. the action of a diagram on
\xi
is zero if this diagram connects two nodes of
\xi
. In other words, the state
\xi
behaves like
u_{k}\in W_{k}(2k)
under the action of
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)
. Then, one can show that the
insertion map

\varphi:\lambda\cdot u_{k}\mapsto\lambda\cdot\xi

(B.32)

is well-defined for all
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)
, and yields a morphism of modules from
W_{k}
to
A
.

Similarly, let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-module. A seed state of type
(k,z)
in
A
is a non-zero element
\xi\in A(2k)
such that

\begin{cases}c_{1}\cdot\xi=0&\text{if }k\geq 1\,,\\
\Omega\cdot\xi=z\,\xi&\text{if }k\geq 1/2\,,\\
f\cdot\xi=(z+z^{-1})\,\xi&\text{if }k=0\,.\end{cases}

(B.33)

Then the map (
B.32
) is well-defined for all
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)
, and yields a morphism of
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-modules from
W_{k,z}
to
A
.

In the following, we suppose furthermore that the
\mathsf{TL}
-module (resp.
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
-module)
A
is equipped with an inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
which is invariant, i.e.
\langle{u,\lambda\,v}\rangle=\langle{\lambda^{\dagger}\,u,v}\rangle
for any
u\in A(N),\ v\in A(N^{\prime}),\ \lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
[resp.
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,N^{\prime})
]. We then prove three properties of seed states.
We present only the proofs for
\mathsf{TL}
modules, because the ones for
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
modules are perfectly similar.

Property 1.

Suppose that the loop weight
\beta
is real.
Let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}
-module equipped with an invariant inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
and
\xi
be seed state of type
k
in
A
. Then one has

\langle{\varphi(u^{*}),\varphi(v)}\rangle=\langle{\xi,\xi}\rangle\,\langle\!\langle{u,v}\rangle\!\rangle\,,

(B.34)

for any
u,v\in W_{k}(N)
, where
u^{*}
denotes the complex conjugate of
u
in the link state basis of
W_{k}(N)
, whereas
\varphi:W_{k}\to A
is the insertion map associated to
\xi
, and
\langle\!\langle{\,,\,}\rangle\!\rangle
denotes the Gram form in
W_{k}
.

Similarly, let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
module equipped with an invariant inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
and
\xi
be seed state of type
(k,z)
in
A
, with
|z|=1
. If
\beta
is real, then (
B.34
) holds for any
u\in W_{k,z^{*}}(N)
and
v\in W_{k,z}(N)
. In this case,
u^{*}
is considered as an element of
W_{k,z}(N)
.

Proof.
For any
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
, denote by
\lambda^{*}
the complex conjugate of
\lambda
in the basis of diagrams.
Since
\beta
is real, all the diagrams of
\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
have real matrix elements in the link state bases of
W_{k}(N),W_{k}(N^{\prime})
, and we thus have

\lambda^{*}\cdot u^{*}=(\lambda\cdot u)^{*}
for any
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
. We introduce the bilinear form
f:W_{k}(N)\times W_{k}(N)\to\mathbb{C}
defined as
f(u,v)=\langle{\varphi(u^{*}),\varphi(v)}\rangle
, where
\varphi
is the morphism (
B.32
) associated to
\xi
. For any
u\in W_{k}(N),\ v\in W_{k}(N^{\prime})
and
\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(N,N^{\prime})
, one has

f(u,\lambda\,v)=\langle{\varphi(u^{*}),\varphi(\lambda\,v)}\rangle=\langle{\varphi(\lambda^{\dagger}\,u^{*}),\varphi(v)}\rangle=\langle{\varphi((\lambda^{t}\,u)^{*}),\varphi(v)}\rangle=f(\lambda^{t}\,u,v)\,,

and thus
f
is an invariant bilinear form.
On the other hand, we can write
f(u_{k},u_{k})=\langle{\xi,\xi}\rangle
. Hence, using the uniqueness of the Gram form, we obtain (
B.34
).

Property 2.
Under the conditions of Property 1, one has

\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi\equiv I_{k}(N)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{TL}^{a}(N,2k)\cdot\xi\equiv I_{k,z}(N)\,,

(B.35)

respectively for a seed state
\xi
of type
k
and
(k,z)
in
A
.

Proof.
The submodule
L_{\xi}(N):=\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi
is the image of the insertion map:

L_{\xi}(N)=\varphi(W_{k}(N))\,.

Since
\varphi
is a morphism, we have
L_{\xi}\equiv W_{k}/\mathrm{Ker}\,\varphi
. Let
v\in\mathrm{rad}(W_{k}(N))
. For any
u^{\prime}\in L_{\xi}(N)
, we can write
u^{\prime}=\varphi(u)
with
u\in W_{k}(N)
, which yields

\langle{u^{\prime},\varphi(v)}\rangle=\langle{\varphi(u),\varphi(v)}\rangle=\langle{\xi,\xi}\rangle\langle\!\langle{u^{*},v}\rangle\!\rangle=0\,,

and hence
\varphi(v)=0
. This shows that
\mathrm{rad}(W_{k})\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}\,\varphi
. Moreover, since
\varphi(u_{k})=\xi\neq 0
, we have
\mathrm{Ker}\,\varphi\neq W_{k}
. Hence, we obtain
\mathrm{Ker}\,\varphi=\mathrm{rad}(W_{k})
, which proves (
B.35
).

Property 3.

Let
A
be a
\mathsf{TL}
-module equipped with an invariant inner product
\langle{\,,\,}\rangle
, and
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
be two seed states of type
k,k^{\prime}
, respectively, with
\langle{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=0
in the case
k=k^{\prime}
.
Then the modules
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi
and
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)\cdot\xi^{\prime}
are orthogonal.
Similarly, the property holds if
A
is a
\mathsf{TL}^{a}
module and
\xi,\xi^{\prime}
are seed states of types
(k,z),(k^{\prime},z^{\prime})
, respectively, with
\langle{\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=0
in the case
k=k^{\prime}
.

Proof.
Let us write
u=\lambda\,\xi
and
v=\mu\,\xi^{\prime}
, where
\lambda
is a diagram of
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k)
, and
\mu
is a diagram of
\mathsf{TL}(N,2k^{\prime})
. Then

\langle{\lambda\,\xi,\mu\,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle{\mu^{\dagger}\lambda\,\xi,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=\langle{\xi,\lambda^{\dagger}\mu\,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle\,.

(B.36)

If
k>k^{\prime}
, we have
\mu^{\dagger}\lambda\in\mathsf{TL}(2k^{\prime},2k)
, and hence
\mu^{\dagger}\lambda\,\xi=0
. Similarly, if
k<k^{\prime}
, we obtain
\lambda^{\dagger}\mu\,\xi^{\prime}=0
. If
k=k^{\prime}
, we have
\mu^{\dagger}\lambda\,\xi=0
, unless
\mu^{\dagger}\lambda
is propotional to the diagram
\mathbf{1}
. In every case, we obtain
\langle{\lambda\,\xi,\mu\,\xi^{\prime}}\rangle=0
.

Appendix C
Chebyshev polynomials and binomial formulas

Chebyshev polynomials.

The Chebyshev polynomial of the first and second kind
T_{m}
and
U_{m}
are defined by the relations

T_{m}(\cos\theta)=\cos m\theta\,,\qquad U_{m}(\cos\theta)=[\sin(m+1)\theta]/\sin\theta

or, equivalently, by the recursion formulas

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)=1\,,

\displaystyle T_{1}(x)=x\,,

\displaystyle T_{m+1}(x)=2x\,T_{m}(x)-T_{m-1}(x)\,,

(C.1)

\displaystyle U_{0}(x)=1\,,

\displaystyle U_{1}(x)=2x\,,

\displaystyle U_{m+1}(x)=2x\,U_{m}(x)-U_{m-1}(x)\,.

Moreover, they obey the property:

U_{m}(x)-U_{m-2}(x)=2T_{m}(x)\,.

(C.2)

Binomial formulas.

Using the Newton binomial formula, one easily derives the identity:

(2\cos\theta)^{N}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/2}d_{k}(N)\,\cos 2k\theta\,,

(C.3)

where
d_{k}(N)
is defined in (
B.11
).
For any non-zero integers
h,m
we obtain from the above relation

\left(2\cos\frac{\pi m}{h}\right)^{N}=\sum_{k=0,\frac{1}{2},\dots,h-\frac{1}{2}}D_{k}(N)\,\cos\frac{2\pi km}{h}\,,

(C.4)

where
D_{k}(N)
is defined in (
B.20
).
