Procalcitonin-platelet ratio as a predictor of severity of necrotising enterocolitis in preterm infants
Authors: Adhariana Kaddas, Andi Dwi Bahagia Febriani, Ema Alasiry, Besse Sarmila
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA retrospective cohort study included preterm infants (<37 weeks of gestation, <2500 g) diagnosed with necrotising enterocolitis (NEC) admitted to Wahidin Sudirohusodo Hospital’s neonatal intensive care unit from January 2023 to December 2024. Forty-seven infants were analysed; procalcitonin (PCT) levels were significantly higher, and platelet (PLT) counts were significantly lower in the severe group compared with the mild-moderate group (p<0.05). The procalcitonin-to-platelet (PCT/PLT) ratio was notably elevated in severe NEC cases (p<0.05). Receiver operating characteristic analysis indicated area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.786 for PCT, 0.126 for PLT and 0.916 for the PCT/PLT ratio. The sensitivity and specificity of the PCT/PLT ratio are 0.862 and 0.833, respectively.

Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC) is a life-threatening gastrointestinal disease and a major cause of morbidity and mortality in preterm infants, with an incidence of 2% to 5% in neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) populations.1 NEC frequently manifests with an aggressive clinical progression. Therefore, prompt assessment of disease severity and initiation of appropriate treatment are essential to improve neonatal outcomes and quality of life.2 Our study focuses on identifying practical, accessible and simple indicators that predict NEC severity, using commonly available clinical parameters suitable for primary healthcare settings with limited laboratory facilities. Complete blood count parameters such as platelet (PLT) count and procalcitonin (PCT) are routinely performed tests across hospitals of all levels. To our knowledge, no previous study has simultaneously assessed the predictive value of PCT and PLT for NEC severity. This combined approach may support earlier risk stratification and inform clinical decision-making.

This retrospective cohort study included preterm infants with NEC admitted to the NICU of Wahidin Sudirohusodo Hospital between January 2023 and December 2024. NEC severity was classified using Bell’s criteria (stages I–II: mild–moderate; stage III: severe). Infants with major anomalies, severe sepsis, haematologic disorders or early spontaneous intestinal perforation were excluded to minimise potential confounding factors. Clinical and laboratory data, including PLT count and PCT, were obtained at the time of NEC diagnosis, defined by radiological confirmation. This time point corresponds to the time when NEC was first radiologically confirmed. If unavailable, values within ±2 days were used. PLT and PCT were collected at the same time point, and the PCT/PLT ratio was calculated. Diagnostic performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, with p value<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Ethical approval

This study involves human participants and was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Faculty of Medicine, Komite Etik Penelitian Fakultas Kedokteran Universitas Hasanuddin (KEP-FKUH), Hasanuddin University, with approval number UH25100853.

Table 1 summarises the baseline characteristics of the study population and compares PCT, PLT and the procalcitonin-to-platelet (PCT/PLT) ratio between the two groups. Of 192 premature infants, 47 diagnosed with NEC were included in the study. Among them, 18 infants were classified as having mild-to-moderate NEC and 29 as having severe NEC. There was a significant difference in PCT and PLT between the two groups. When these two parameters were combined into a PCT/PLT ratio, a significant difference was observed, with higher levels in the severe NEC group compared with the mild-moderate NEC group. The ROC analysis showed AUC values of 0.786 for PCT alone, 0.874 for PLT alone and 0.916 for the PCT/PLT ratio. These findings indicate that the PCT/PLT ratio provides superior discriminatory ability for predicting NEC severity in preterm infants. A cut-off value of 96.36×10⁻⁶ yielded a sensitivity of 0.862 and a specificity of 0.833 (figure 1).

This study focused on identifying effective predictors of NEC severity using common and relatively affordable laboratory markers, particularly in developing countries. PCT levels rise markedly in severe infections and outperform other inflammatory markers in predicting infection severity and organ dysfunction. In NEC, infection is common, and severe disease may progress to sepsis.3 4 Markedly elevated PCT levels are observed in severe infections, while mild infections cause only slight increases. Compared with other inflammatory indicators, PCT demonstrates higher predictive accuracy for infection severity and organ dysfunction.5 6

Our study demonstrated significantly higher PCT levels and markedly lower PLT counts in infants with severe NEC compared with those with mild–moderate disease. These findings suggest that the combined PCT/PLT ratio may serve as a relevant predictor of NEC severity in preterm infants. The PCT/PLT ratio showed superior predictive performance (AUC=0.916), indicating its potential value as a composite biomarker and offering greater discriminatory ability than either parameter alone for assessing disease severity.
