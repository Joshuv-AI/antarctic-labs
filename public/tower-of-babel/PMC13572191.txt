Salmonella Pullorum strain SPullorum-YN-07 from dead embryos of Yanjin black-bone chickens: Complete genome with IncFII(S) and Col(pVC) plasmids and pathogenicity
Authors: Junyi Tao, Limei Zhang, Zhongwen Zhang, Shan Wang, Bo Zhou, Wenwen Yang, Liangyu Yang, Bin Xiang, Huixin Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Salmonella Pullorum is a host-adapted pathogen that causes Pullorum disease in chickens and can be vertically transmitted via eggs, leading to embryonic mortality. The susceptibility and vertical transmission of S. Pullorum may vary among chicken breeds, yet genomic characterization of strains from dead embryos of indigenous breeds remains limited. This study isolated and characterized a Gram-negative short rod, designated Salmonella Pullorum strain SPullorum-YN-07, from dead embryos of Yanjin black-bone chickens, a native breed in Yunnan, China. The strain formed colorless colonies on MacConkey agar and red, non-H2S colonies on XLD agar, with biochemical reactions consistent with the genus Salmonella. Whole-genome sequencing using Illumina and PacBio platforms generated a complete genome consisting of one circular chromosome and four circular plasmids; plasmid replicon types IncFII(S) and Col(pVC) were identified in two of the plasmids. On the chromosome, a total of 340 virulence-associated genes were detected, including those involved in secretion systems, adhesion, motility, and immune modulation. Resistance gene analysis identified the acquired aminoglycoside resistance gene aac(6′)-Iaa, alongside multiple intrinsic resistance determinants related to efflux pumps and target alteration. Multilocus sequence typing (MLST) assigned the strain to sequence type ST92, and core-genome phylogenetic analysis confirmed its clustering within the Salmonella Pullorum lineage. In a chick infection model, the strain induced depression, white diarrhea, and growth retardation, with clinical scores peaking at 10 days post-infection and a mortality rate of 10%. Bacterial colonization was highest in the cecum, and histopathological lesions were observed in the liver, spleen, and cecum. This study provides the first complete genomic characterization and pathogenicity assessment of an S. Pullorum strain isolated from dead embryos of Yanjin black-bone chickens, offering a foundation for understanding host-pathogen interactions in indigenous breeds and assessing cross-transmission risks to commercial poultry populations.

Introduction

Salmonella enterica serovar Pullorum is a Gram-negative bacterium belonging to the family Enterobacteriaceae and is the causative agent of Pullorum disease in chickens (Li et al., 2019). The pathogen primarily affects young chicks, causing high mortality and systemic infection (Barrow and Freitas Neto, 2011), whereas adult birds often become asymptomatic carrier, serving as long-term reservoirs of infection (Wigley, et al., 2005; Zhang, et al., 2025). Notably, S. Pullorum can be transmitted vertically through contaminated eggs, posing a major challenge to eradication programs in breeding flocks (Wigley, et al., 2001). Pullorum disease remains one of the most significant bacterial diseases in poultry production worldwide, resulting in septicemia, growth retardation, reduced performance, dead embryos, and substantial economic losses.

In China, Salmonella infection is widely prevalent in poultry, with complex epidemiological characteristics and diverse serovar distributions (Sun, et al., 2021b; Wang, et al., 2024). Due to regional differences in farming practices, biosecurity levels, and management strategies, dominant strains and their molecular characteristics vary considerably. Yunnan Province, located in southwestern China, is an important poultry production region with extensive broiler and layer farming. However, the overall level of farm management remains heterogeneous, and biosecurity measures are often suboptimal, leading to a high risk of Salmonella infection (Li, et al., 2018a). To date, studies on the epidemiological characteristics and molecular typing of Salmonella Pullorum in poultry from this region remain limited. Moreover, the improper use of antimicrobials in poultry production has contributed to the emergence and dissemination of resistant strains, further complicating the control and eradication of Pullorum disease in local flocks (Sun, et al., 2021a).

Accurate identification and molecular characterization of pathogens are essential for effective disease prevention and control. In recent years, whole-genome sequencing (WGS) technologies have enabled taxonomic identification and phylogenetic analysis at the genomic level, providing powerful tools for source tracking, epidemiological investigation, and the design of control strategies (Croucher and Didelot, 2015; Didelot, et al., 2012). Recent advances in whole-genome sequencing and comparative genomics of S. Pullorum have uncovered key information regarding its evolution, host adaptation, antimicrobial resistance, virulence profiles, population architecture and genomic characteristics (Cheng, et al., 2024a; Cui, et al., 2024b; Hu, et al., 2019). In particular, genomic characterization of strains derived from dead embryos or associated with vertical transmission is still scarce. Furthermore, the role of host-pathogen interactions in the adaptation of Salmonella has attracted increasing attention. Previous studies have demonstrated that Salmonella Pullorum has undergone genome degradation, including the inactivation of genes such as fimbrial operons, which is associated with enhanced host specificity, vertical transmission, and systemic infection (Feng, et al., 2013). This process has been described as a form of regressive evolution facilitating host adaptation. Indigenous chicken breeds, such as the Yanjin black-bone chicken in Yunnan, possess unique genetic backgrounds and are commonly raised under traditional management systems with relatively low levels of biosecurity. These factors may influence the epidemiology and evolutionary dynamics of Salmonella Pullorum. However, whether strains circulating in such local breeds have acquired distinct genomic characteristics that contribute to host adaptation or cross-population transmission remains largely unclear.

Therefore, this study aimed to isolate and characterize a Salmonella Pullorum strain from dead embryos of Yanjin black-bone chickens, a native breed in Yunnan, China. We used a combination of phenotypic identification, complete genome sequencing (Illumina + PacBio), and bioinformatic analysis to determine its genomic features, including the complete chromosome and plasmid profiles. In addition, a chick infection model was used to evaluate its pathogenicity. This study fills a gap in the genomic characterization of Salmonella Pullorum from dead embryos of an indigenous chicken breed and provides a scientific basis for epidemiological surveillance, cross-breed transmission risk assessment, and disease control in regions with rich poultry genetic resources.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Dead chicken embryos were collected on March 29, 2025, from a commercial poultry farm located in Yanjin County, Zhaotong City, Yunnan Province, China, where an increased incidence of embryonic mortality was observed during the late incubation period. The affected embryos exhibited poor development and incomplete yolk absorption. Based on these clinical signs, a bacterial infection, including possible Salmonella infection, was suspected. Liver and intestinal tissues from the dead embryos were aseptically sampled and used for subsequent analyses. All samples were placed in a portable ice box and transported to the laboratory under cold conditions immediately after collection for bacterial isolation and further examination.

Bacterial isolation and purification

MacConkey agar, Xylose Lysine Deoxycholate (XLD) agar, Buffered Peptone Water (BPW), Selenite Cystine (SC) broth, and Luria–Bertani (LB) agar (Huankai, Guangdong, China) were used for bacterial isolation and culture.

Tissue samples were aseptically homogenized, and the homogenates were first pre-enriched in BPW at 37°C with shaking for 12–16 h. Subsequently, the pre-enriched cultures were transferred into SC broth for selective enrichment under the same conditions for 24 h. The selectively enriched cultures were then streaked onto MacConkey agar and XLD agar plates and incubated at 37°C for 24 h. Suspected Salmonella colonies were preliminarily identified based on colony morphology. Single colonies were selected and streaked onto LB agar plates for purification. After three consecutive rounds of streaking, a pure isolate was obtained and designated as SPullorum-YN-07. The purified strain was preserved in LB broth supplemented with 20% glycerol and stored at −80°C for further use.

Growth curve determination by CFU counting

The isolate SPullorum-YN-07 was inoculated into LB broth and incubated overnight at 37°C with shaking at 220 rpm. The overnight culture was then diluted 1:100 into fresh LB broth to obtain an initial bacterial concentration of approximately 1 × 10^6 CFU/mL and incubated under the same conditions. Samples were collected at 2 h intervals after inoculation. Each sample was serially diluted 10-fold in sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and appropriate dilutions were spread onto Plate Count Agar (PCA) plates (Huankai, Guangdong, China). After incubation at 37°C for 24 h, colonies were counted, and the viable bacterial counts were expressed as colony-forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL). All experiments were performed in triplicate. The values were log10-transformed, and a growth curve was constructed accordingly.

Morphological observation and biochemical identification

The purified isolate SPullorum-YN-07 was subjected to Gram staining, and its cellular morphology and staining characteristics were observed under a light microscope. Biochemical identification was performed using a commercial Salmonella identification kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The tested biochemical characteristics included carbohydrate fermentation and conventional biochemical reactions, such as hydrogen sulfide production, urease activity, lysine decarboxylation, indole production, malonate utilization, and potassium cyanide tolerance. In addition, TSI agar was used to determine glucose fermentation, gas production, and hydrogen sulfide formation. All results were interpreted according to the manufacturer’s guidelines, and the isolate was preliminarily identified as Salmonella Pullorum based on the combined biochemical characteristics.

Genomic DNA extraction and whole-genome sequencing

The strain SPullorum-YN-07 was cultured in LB broth at 37°C with shaking at 220 rpm for 12 h. Genomic DNA was extracted from the bacterial culture according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, and DNA samples with an A260/A280 ratio of 1.8-2.0 and a concentration greater than 50 ng/µL were considered acceptable for sequencing. DNA integrity was assessed by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis.

Whole-genome sequencing was performed by Shanghai Personal Biotechnology Co., Ltd. A PacBio library with an insert size of approximately 10 kb and an Illumina paired-end (PE) library with an insert size of approximately 400 bp were constructed. Single-molecule real-time sequencing was conducted on the PacBio Revio platform, and short-read sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq platform (2 × 150 bp).

Bioinformatics analysis

Raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control prior to downstream analysis. Illumina reads were filtered using fastp (v0.23.1) to remove adapter sequences and low-quality reads (Chen, 2023). PacBio long reads were filtered based on read length and quality to obtain high-quality datasets. De novo genome assembly was performed using Flye (v2.9.1-b1781) based on the PacBio long reads (Kolmogorov, et al., 2019). The initial assembly was further corrected and polished using Illumina high-quality reads with Pilon (v1.24), resulting in a complete genome sequence (Walker, et al., 2014).

For species identification, the assembled genome sequence was compared against the NCBI database using BLAST (v2.13.0) to determine its taxonomic identity (Altschul, et al., 1990). Gene prediction and functional annotation were performed using GeneMarkS (v4.32), and genomic structural elements were annotated accordingly (Besemer, et al., 2001). Plasmid replicon types were identified using PlasmidFinder.

Virulence genes were predicted by comparison with the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB), and antimicrobial resistance genes were identified using the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) (v2024.03) and ResFinder (v4.5.0).

Secretion systems were identified using MacSyFinder (v2.1.2).

In addition, multilocus sequence typing (MLST) was conducted for strain typing (Larsen, et al., 2012). A phylogenetic tree was constructed based on the core genome to analyze the genetic relationships and evolutionary characteristics of the isolate.

Animal pathogenicity experiment

A total of 40 healthy 2-day-old Sanhuang chicks (Guangxi Fufeng Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Group, China), confirmed free of Salmonella Pullorum maternal antibodies by pre-experimental serological screening, were randomly divided into a challenged group (n = 20) and a control group (n = 20). Chicks were housed in a controlled environment at 32-34°C, with relative humidity maintained at 55-65% and a 23 h light/1 h dark lighting schedule. Feed and water were provided ad libitum.

The SPullorum-YN-07 strain was cultured to mid-log phase in LB broth at 37°C, washed twice with sterile PBS, and adjusted to 5.0 × 108 CFU/mL. Chicks in the challenged group were orally inoculated with 0.2 mL of Salmonella Pullorum strain SPullorum-YN-07 (1.0 × 10⁸ CFU per bird), while the control group received an equal volume of sterile saline (Yin, et al., 2015). After inoculation, chicks were monitored daily for clinical signs and body weight changes until 15 days post-infection (dpi). Necropsy sampling was performed at 5, 10, and 15 dpi, with six chicks from each group randomly selected and euthanized at each time point. Target organs, including the liver, spleen, bursa of Fabricius, and intestine, were collected for organ index determination, bacterial load quantification, and histopathological examination using hematoxylin and eosin staining. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with relevant ethical guidelines and were approved by the institutional animal care and use committee (Approval No. 202305011). Euthanasia was performed by cervical dislocation following deep anesthesia with 5% isoflurane inhalation, in compliance with the AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2020 Edition).

Clinical observation and scoring criteria

Chicks were continuously monitored following challenge. Clinical signs were recorded daily from 0 to 15 dpi, and clinical scoring was performed every 24 h. During the experiment, dead chicks were subjected to necropsy, and tissue samples, including the liver, spleen, and cecal contents, were collected for DNA extraction. The presence of Salmonella Pullorum was confirmed by real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) targeting the SEP gene. The primer sequences were as follows: forward primer (5′-CCCGGATTGGACCTCAAGTG-3′) and reverse primer (5′-ATGTTACGGGACGAGTGGGT-3′) (Cui, et al., 2023). The qPCR reaction was carried out in a total volume of 20 µL, containing 10 µL of 2 × dib SYBR qPCR SuperMix Plus, 0.4 µL of each primer (10 µM), 1 µL of template DNA, and 8.2 µL of nuclease-free water. The amplification conditions were as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 1 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 20 s and 60°C for 1 min. Each sample was analyzed in triplicate, and no-template controls were included in each run. Samples showing amplification with a specific melting curve and a Ct value ≤ 35 were considered positive. Combined with necropsy findings, the cause of death was determined.

Quantification of bacterial load by real-time quantitative PCR

At 5, 10, and 15 dpi, liver, spleen, and cecal tissues were collected. Approximately 100 mg of each tissue sample was homogenized in 900 µL of sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Total DNA was extracted from the homogenates using a tissue genomic DNA extraction kit (Servicebio, G3633) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were measured using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, and the DNA samples were stored at −20°C until use. qPCR was performed targeting the SEP gene specific to Salmonella Pullorum. A recombinant plasmid containing the SEP target fragment was constructed and used as a standard for absolute quantification. The plasmid was serially diluted in a 10-fold gradient to generate a series of standard solutions. These standards were subjected to qPCR under the same conditions as the samples to construct a standard curve. The copy number of the target gene in each sample was calculated based on the standard curve. The results were normalized according to the homogenization volume and tissue weight and expressed as log10 (copies/g).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (version 9.0, GraphPad Software, USA). Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Clinical scores were analyzed by repeated-measures ANOVA, body weight by independent samples t-test, organ indices and bacterial loads by two-way ANOVA, and mortality by Fisher’s exact test. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Isolation and morphological identification of the bacterium

A suspected Salmonella strain was isolated from dead embryo tissue samples collected from a chicken farm in Yunnan Province and designated as SPullorum-YN-07. On MacConkey agar, the isolate formed colorless, transparent, circular colonies with smooth surfaces and well-defined edges. On XLD agar, the colonies appeared red, semi-transparent, and smooth, without black centers (non-H₂S-producing), with diameters of approximately 1.0-1.5 mm.

Gram staining revealed that the isolate was composed of Gram-negative short rods, arranged singly or in pairs. Whole-genome sequencing data quality control

Illumina NovaSeq sequencing generated a total of 7,667,904 raw reads, comprising 1,157,853,504 bp, with a GC content of 51.96%. The Q20 and Q30 values were 99.23% and 96.64%, respectively. After quality filtering using fastp, 7,473,086 high-quality reads were retained, accounting for 97.46% of the raw data, with an estimated sequencing depth of approximately 233× .

PacBio Revio sequencing produced 230,586 long reads with a total length of 2,779,231,921 bp, an N50 of 12,459 bp, and a GC content of 52.05%. These results indicate that the sequencing data were of high quality and suitable for subsequent genome assembly and analysis (Supplementary Table 1).

Genome assembly and genomic features

Hybrid assembly based on PacBio long reads combined with Illumina short-read correction generated a complete genome sequence of strain SPullorum-YN-07 (accession number: JBWLIV000000000.1). The genome consisted of one circular chromosome and four circular plasmids, with a total length of approximately 5.08 Mb. Species identification

The genome sequence of strain SPullorum-YN-07 was compared against the NCBI database using BLAST, and the results showed high sequence similarity to members of the genus Salmonella. Taxonomic classification based on the GTDB database assigned the isolate to the domain Bacteria, phylum Pseudomonadota, class Gammaproteobacteria, order Enterobacterales, family Enterobacteriaceae, and genus Salmonella, and further classified it as Salmonella enterica.

Combined with the morphological and biochemical characteristics, the isolate was confirmed to belong to the genus Salmonella. The specific serovar designation was further determined based on subsequent molecular typing and phylogenetic analyses.

Plasmid replicon typing analysis

PlasmidFinder analysis revealed that, among the four plasmids of strain SPullorum-YN-07, plasmid1 harbored an IncFII(S) replicon (97.71% sequence identity), and plasmid3 carried a Col(pVC) replicon (98.96% sequence identity), whereas no known replicon types were detected in plasmid2 and plasmid4. These results suggest that the latter two plasmids may represent novel or uncharacterized plasmid types (Douarre, et al., 2020; Orlek, et al., 2017).

Further sequence homology analysis showed that all four plasmids exhibited high similarity to Salmonella-associated plasmid sequences.

Virulence gene profiling

Virulence gene prediction based on the VFDB database showed that virulence-associated genes of strain SPullorum-YN-07 were predominantly located on the chromosome. A total of 340 virulence-related genes were identified on the chromosome and classified into 13 functional categories, including effector delivery system (n = 103), adherence (n = 79), motility (n = 52), and immune modulation (n = 40). The remaining genes were assigned to the nutritional, regulation, antimicrobial activity, biofilm, stress survival, exotoxin, invasion, others, and exoenzyme categories (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Functional classification of virulence-associated genes in the chromosome of strain SPullorum-YN-07 based on the VFDB database. In contrast, only a small number of virulence-related genes were detected in plasmids. Specifically, plasmid1 contained five virulence-associated genes belonging to antimicrobial activity, effector delivery system, exotoxin, and adherence categories, whereas no virulence genes were identified in plasmid2, plasmid3, or plasmid4.

Antimicrobial resistance-related determinants and acquired resistance genes

Antimicrobial resistance analysis was performed using both CARD and ResFinder databases. CARD analysis identified 130 antimicrobial resistance-associated determinants on the chromosome of strain SPullorum-YN-07, involving seven resistance-related mechanisms, including antibiotic efflux (n = 60), antibiotic target alteration (n = 46), and reduced permeability to antibiotics (n = 13). Other predicted categories included antibiotic inactivation, target protection, target replacement, and resistance by absence.

In contrast, ResFinder analysis identified only one acquired antimicrobial resistance gene, aac(6′)-Iaa (96.35% identity), which is associated with aminoglycoside resistance. No acquired antimicrobial resistance genes were detected on any of the four plasmids.

Secretion systems, molecular typing, and phylogenetic analysis

Genome analysis using MacSyFinder identified multiple secretion systems in strain SPullorum-YN-07, including type I secretion system (T1SS), type III secretion system (T3SS), type IV pilus system (T4aP), and type Va secretion system (T5aSS, autotransporter system). Notably, several effector proteins associated with the T3SS, likely corresponding to Salmonella pathogenicity islands (SPI-1 and SPI-2), were detected.

Further analysis using core genome MLST (cgMLST) classified the strain as cgST 58380, with a locus match rate of 88.44%, indicating a high level of genomic similarity to reference Salmonella Pullorum strains.

Phylogenetic analysis based on core genome sequences demonstrated that strain SPullorum-YN-07 clustered within the Salmonella enterica serovar Pullorum lineage and showed a close evolutionary relationship with reference Pullorum strains. The isolate was clearly separated from other Salmonella serovars, including S. Enteritidis and S. Typhimurium, while remaining closely related to S. Results of the animal pathogenicity experiment

Clinical signs and clinical scoring. Throughout the experimental period, the control group remained in good condition, with normal feeding behavior and activity, and no apparent clinical abnormalities; the clinical score remained at 0. In contrast, chicks in the challenged group gradually developed typical clinical signs after infection, including depression, reduced activity, decreased feed and water intake, as well as varying degrees of white or watery diarrhea, fecal contamination around the cloaca, lethargy, and growth retardation. With progression of infection, the severity of clinical signs increased, and some chicks developed marked weakness or died. Clinical scoring results showed that scores in the challenged group gradually increased from 0 dpi, reached a peak at approximately 10 dpi, and then slightly declined, remaining relatively stable during the later stage (Fig. 6). During the experiment, mortality was observed only in the challenged group, with a mortality rate of 10% (2/20), whereas no deaths occurred in the control group. Necropsy and qPCR detection confirmed the presence of Salmonella Pullorum in the dead chicks, supporting the association between mortality and SPullorum-YN-07 infection.Fig. 6Clinical scores of chicks following challenge with Salmonella Pullorum strain SPullorum-YN-07. Histopathological changes by hematoxylin and eosin staining. Histopathological examination showed normal histological architecture in the control group, whereas obvious pathological lesions were observed in the liver, spleen, and intestine of chicks in the challenged group (Fig. 10). In the liver of infected chicks, multifocal necrosis, hepatocellular degeneration, and inflammatory cell infiltration were observed, accompanied by mild hepatocellular swelling and vacuolar degeneration. Discussion

In this study, a Salmonella Pullorum strain was successfully isolated and identified from dead embryo tissue samples collected from a chicken farm in Yunnan Province. This finding is consistent with previous reports indicating that Salmonella Pullorum is capable of vertical transmission, leading to embryonic infection, and further supports the possibility that Pullorum disease in this farm may be associated with vertical transmission (Geng, et al., 2017; Wu, et al., 2025). In addition, this isolate contributes to the available strain resources for epidemiological studies of Salmonella Pullorum in chickens in Yunnan. The morphological and biochemical characteristics of the isolate were consistent with the typical features of the genus Salmonella. Furthermore, whole-genome sequencing combined with phylogenetic analysis confirmed its taxonomic identity and serovar classification. These results provide a reliable basis for accurate diagnosis and targeted control of Pullorum disease in the affected farm.

The phenotypic characteristics of the isolate were consistent with the typical features of Salmonella Pullorum, supporting the reliability of conventional bacteriological identification (Ghazy Hawal and Talib Bakr, 2023). Unlike many H₂S-producing Salmonella serovars, Salmonella Pullorum may show absent, weak, or variable H₂S production during routine biochemical testing. Therefore, the absence of H₂S production can only provide supportive evidence for preliminary identification and should not be used as a standalone diagnostic criterion (Bentum, et al., 2025). Although molecular methods are increasingly applied for pathogen identification, conventional phenotypic approaches remain valuable, particularly in field settings and resource-limited laboratories, where rapid and cost-effective screening is required. Notably, the isolation of this strain from dead embryos further supports its ability to infect embryos at early developmental stages, reflecting its pathogenic potential (Cui, Li, Huang, Lin, Li, Cao, Hao and Sun, 2023).

Plasmids play an important role in the evolution and adaptation of Salmonella by facilitating horizontal gene transfer (Baumler, 1997). In particular, IncF-type plasmids have been implicated in Salmonella virulence, particularly through spv genes involved in systemic infection (McMillan, et al., 2020; Wiesner, et al., 2016), whereas Col-type plasmids are often linked to interbacterial competition and ecological fitness (Cascales, et al., 2007; Riley and Gordon, 1999). The identification of these plasmid types in the present isolate suggests that plasmid-mediated gene transfer may contribute to its adaptation and survival within the host (McMillan, Jackson and Frye, 2020). Such mechanisms may enhance bacterial persistence and transmission potential, thereby influencing the epidemiological characteristics of Salmonella Pullorum in poultry production systems (Bakkeren, et al., 2019).

Virulence-associated genes provide important insights into the pathogenic potential of Salmonella Pullorum. In particular, genes related to adhesion, motility, and invasion are essential for successful colonization and dissemination within the host (Shaji, et al., 2023). Among these, the type III secretion system (T3SS) represents a key virulence determinant in Salmonella, enabling the translocation of effector proteins into host cells to modulate cellular signaling pathways, suppress immune responses, and promote intracellular survival and replication (Foley, et al., 2013; Wang, et al., 2021; Zhou, et al., 2023). Previous studies have demonstrated that T3SS plays a central role in epithelial cell invasion and systemic dissemination (McGhie, et al., 2009; Radtke, et al., 2010). The virulence gene profile identified in this study is consistent with the clinical manifestations and tissue lesions observed in the challenged chicks, including growth retardation, organ damage, and bacterial colonization. This concordance between genomic features and in vivo phenotypes further supports the pathogenic potential of this isolate.

Antimicrobial resistance analysis indicated that this isolate carries a limited number of confirmed resistance determinants, including the aminoglycoside resistance gene aac(6′)-Iaa, while also harboring multiple genes associated with general resistance-related mechanisms such as efflux systems, target alteration, and permeability regulation (Punchihewage-Don, et al., 2024; Yamasaki, et al., 2023). Notably, the number of resistance-associated genes predicted by the CARD database was substantially higher than that identified by ResFinder (Papp and Solymosi, 2022). This discrepancy is likely attributable to differences in database scope, as CARD includes a broader range of resistance-related genes and mechanisms, whereas ResFinder primarily focuses on the detection of acquired antimicrobial resistance genes (Centner, et al., 2026). This pattern is consistent with previous genomic studies in which only a limited number of acquired resistance genes were identified in Salmonella Pullorum isolates (Cheng, et al., 2024b). Although the presence of resistance-related mechanisms suggests a certain level of adaptive potential to antimicrobial pressure, the actual resistance phenotype of this isolate requires further validation through antimicrobial susceptibility testing.

Molecular typing and phylogenetic analysis further clarified the genetic background of this isolate. The clustering pattern observed in the core genome phylogeny supports its classification within the Salmonella Pullorum lineage and highlights its genetic distinction from other closely related Salmonella serovars (Feng, Johnston, Liu and Liu, 2013). The sequence type ST92 identified in this study has been commonly associated with Salmonella Pullorum in previous reports, providing additional support for its taxonomic assignment (Cheng, Zhang, Huang, Luo, Zhang and Zhou, 2024b; Cui, et al., 2024a). In addition, cgMLST provides a high-resolution tool for strain typing, which is particularly valuable for epidemiological investigations, including source tracing and transmission analysis (Yan, et al., 2021). The application of such genomic approaches may facilitate more precise monitoring and control of Salmonella Pullorum in poultry production systems.

Although previous genomic studies have characterized S. Pullorum isolates from different regions and hosts, complete genome assemblies from embryo-derived field isolates of native chicken breeds are still limited (Cheng, Zhang, Huang, Luo, Zhang and Zhou, 2024a; Kipper, et al., 2024). In the present study, SPullorum-YN-07 was isolated from dead embryos of Yanjin black-bone chickens and completely assembled into one circular chromosome and four circular plasmids using hybrid PacBio–Illumina sequencing. Plasmid replicon analysis detected IncFII(S) and Col(pVC) replicons in two plasmids, whereas the remaining two plasmids could not be assigned to any established replicon classification. Importantly, the combination of complete genome sequencing and in vivo pathogenicity assessment allows comprehensive characterization of both genomic profiles and virulence potential of this isolate. However, several limitations should be noted. First, only one single embryonic isolate was subjected to whole-genome sequencing, so the genomic landscape of local S. Pullorum populations in Yunnan cannot be comprehensively represented. Second, the biological functions of the two novel unclassified plasmids were solely inferred from genomic annotation, lacking in vitro cell infection and gene knockout validation. Even with these constraints, our high-quality complete genome resource serves as a valuable baseline for future comparative genomic, epidemiological and pathogenicity research focusing on S. Pullorum.

The in vivo infection results demonstrated that SPullorum-YN-07 was pathogenic to chicks under the experimental conditions used, as evidenced by clinical signs, growth retardation, bacterial colonization, and histopathological lesions. Bacterial colonization patterns suggested that the cecum served as an important site of colonization, from which the pathogen may disseminate to systemic organs such as the spleen and liver. This pattern is consistent with a possible infection process involving intestinal colonization followed by systemic spread (Matulova, et al., 2013). Histopathological findings further revealed disruption of intestinal integrity and lesions in multiple organs, indicating that the isolate was able to induce tissue damage in infected chicks. In parallel, changes in organ indices suggested a dynamic host response, characterized by sustained enlargement of the liver and spleen, together with a transient increase followed by a decline in the bursa of Fabricius, which may reflect early immune activation and subsequent functional impairment. Taken together, these observations support the pathogenicity of SPullorum-YN-07 in chicks. However, no known pathogenic reference strain of Salmonella Pullorum was included as a parallel positive control, and the median lethal dose (LD₅₀) was not determined. Therefore, the present study cannot assess whether the pathogenicity of SPullorum-YN-07 is relatively strong or weak compared with standard or previously characterized Salmonella Pullorum strains. The results should be interpreted as evidence of pathogenicity under the experimental conditions used, rather than as a quantitative assessment of relative virulence. Future studies should include standard reference strains or well-characterized field isolates for parallel comparison and LD₅₀ determination.

In this study, Sanhuang chickens were used for the in vivo challenge experiment rather than Yanjin black-bone chickens, from which the isolate was originally obtained. This choice was mainly due to practical limitations, as Yanjin black-bone chickens are locally distributed indigenous chickens and standardized experimental birds of this breed are difficult to obtain. The use of a widely available commercial breed under controlled experimental conditions allowed a more standardized assessment of the pathogenicity of strain SPullorum-YN-07. Importantly, the ability of a strain isolated from dead embryos associated with an indigenous chicken population to induce clinical signs, bacterial colonization, and multi-organ lesions in Sanhuang chickens suggests that Salmonella Pullorum strains circulating in local breeds may also pose a potential risk to commercial breeds (Li, et al., 2018b; Pinheiro, et al., 2001). Yunnan Province is characterized by rich indigenous chicken genetic resources and substantial diversity among local chicken populations, and several Chinese indigenous chicken breeds have been included in national livestock and poultry genetic resource catalogues (Huo, et al., 2014). Previous studies have also shown that Salmonella Pullorum remains highly prevalent in Chinese hatchery settings, with ST92 reported as a dominant sequence type, indicating that hatcheries may represent an important point for dissemination of widely circulating Pullorum lineages (Wu, Deng, Chen, Peng, Wu, Chen, Chen, Liao, Xu and Zhang, 2025). In this context, the ST92 assignment of SPullorum-YN-07 suggests that this isolate belongs to a lineage with epidemiological relevance in poultry production. These findings have practical implications for Pullorum disease control in regions where indigenous breeds and commercial poultry production systems coexist (Cui, et al., 2021). Strengthening biosecurity and routine surveillance in indigenous breed farms is important to reduce the risk of pathogen persistence and spillover (Neelawala, et al., 2024). In addition, movement of birds, eggs, equipment, and personnel between indigenous and commercial production systems should be carefully managed. From a breeding and disease-control perspective, the establishment of standardized disease-free or well-monitored indigenous chicken populations would be valuable for future homologous challenge studies and for evaluating potential breed-specific differences in susceptibility to Salmonella Pullorum (Li, Nie, Zhang, Wang, Shao, Zhao, Chen, Wang, Li, Jiao, Li, Qin, He, Jia, Ning and Qu, 2018b).

In summary, this study systematically characterized the phenotypic features, genomic structure, and pathogenic mechanisms of Salmonella Pullorum strain SPullorum-YN-07, providing valuable insights for the epidemiological surveillance and control of this pathogen. Future studies should focus on functional validation of key virulence genes and expand to larger-scale investigations to better elucidate the molecular epidemiology and antimicrobial resistance evolution of Salmonella Pullorum. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. In particular, no reference strain was included for comparative analysis, and the virulence of the isolate was not quantitatively assessed. Further studies are therefore required to address these aspects and to provide a more comprehensive understanding of its pathogenic characteristics.

Conclusion

In this study, a Salmonella Pullorum strain (SPullorum-YN-07) was successfully isolated from dead embryo samples collected from a chicken farm in Yunnan Province and systematically identified using morphological, biochemical, and whole-genome sequencing approaches. Genomic analysis revealed the presence of multiple virulence-associated genes and secretion systems, as well as the aminoglycoside resistance gene aac(6′)-Iaa, suggesting a certain level of adaptive potential to antimicrobial pressure. Molecular typing and phylogenetic analysis further confirmed its classification within the Salmonella Pullorum lineage and its genetic distinction from other Salmonella serovars. In vivo experiments demonstrated that this isolate is pathogenic to chicks, with the ability to colonize the intestine, disseminate to systemic organs such as the liver and spleen, and induce immune organ damage and histopathological changes. Notably, the pathogenicity of a strain originating from a local indigenous chicken-associated source in Sanhuang chickens suggests that Salmonella Pullorum strains circulating in indigenous breeds may pose a potential risk to commercial poultry populations. Overall, this study provides a comprehensive characterization of the phenotypic, genomic, and pathogenic features of this isolate, offering valuable insights into the pathogenesis of Salmonella Pullorum and supporting future epidemiological surveillance and control strategies in poultry production systems.
