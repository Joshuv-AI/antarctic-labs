Emergent gravity from nonlinear perturbation of spherical accretion with variable adiabatic index Preprint: APS/PRD-Template
Rohit Ghosh, Souvik Ghose, Biplab Raychaudhuri, Apashanka Das, Tapas K. Das

Abstract
The main aim of the present work is to demonstrate that the analogue gravity phenomena is not an artifact of linear perturbation, rather gravity-like effects emerge through the non linear higher order perturbation of transonic fluid as well. To establish that fact, a spherically accreting astrophysical system has been considered where the hydrodynamic accretion with a relativistic, multi-component equation of state with position dependent adiabatic index onto compact astrophysical objects has been considered. rate. By extending the acoustic metric formalism beyond the linear regime, it has been shown that the aforementioned perturbations satisfy a covariant wave equation in an effective acoustic spacetime with non-linear corrections, making the analogue geometry dynamical. As a consequence, the acoustic horizon can shift (inward or outward), depending on the relative amplitudes of density, temperature, and mass accretion-rate fluctuations. This provides a more realistic framework to investigate the dynamics of the non-linear analogue spacetime in astrophysically relevant accretion flows.



I
Introduction

Emergent/Analogue gravity provides a framework in which perturbations of transonic fluid flows can be described as scalar fields propagating in an effective curved spacetime containing acoustic horizons. Since the pioneering work of Unruh
Unruh 1981
, it has been understood that sound waves in moving fluids can mimic several kinematical properties of black hole spacetimes. This idea was further developed by Visser
Visser 1998
and later reviewed extensively by Barceló, Liberati and Visser
Barcelo et al. 2011
. Such systems allow certain aspects of horizon physics to be studied in fluid dynamical settings. Astrophysical accretion flows provide particularly important analogue systems because they are naturally transonic. The stability of spherical accretion flows under perturbations was first studied by Moncrief
Moncrief 1980
, and subsequent works by Bilić
Bilić 1999
and Das and collaborators
Das 2004
;
Dasgupta et al. 2005
;
Abraham et al. 2006
;
Das et al. 2007
established accreting black hole systems as natural analogue gravity laboratories containing both gravitational and acoustic horizons. Most of these studies, however, considered linear perturbations and simplified thermodynamic descriptions such as polytropic or isothermal equations of state.

Realistic accretion flows near compact objects may involve relativistic temperatures and multi-species composition, requiring more sophisticated thermodynamic descriptions. In this context, relativistic equations of state developed by Ryu and collaborators
Ryu et al. 2006
and further applied to accretion problems by Chattopadhyay and collaborators
Kumar et al. 2013
;
Chattopadhyay and Chakrabarti 2011
provide a more realistic description of relativistic fluid thermodynamics. More recently, Paul
et al.

Paul et al. 2025
investigated analogue gravity phenomena in multicomponent relativistic accretion flows using such equations of state. The adiabatic index
\Gamma
is essentially a function of the absolute temperature for this case. Another important extension concerns the role of non-linear perturbations. While linear perturbations lead to stationary acoustic geometries, recent work by Fernandes, Maity and Das
Fernandes et al. 2022
has demonstrated that non-linear perturbations can generate dynamical analogue spacetimes in which the effective acoustic metric evolves and the acoustic horizon may shift depending on the relative behaviour of density and mass accretion rate perturbations.

Motivated by these developments, in this work we study non-linear perturbations of the mass accretion rate in spherically symmetric accretion flows described by a relativistic multi-component equation of state. By extending the acoustic metric construction beyond the linear regime, we show that the perturbations satisfy a covariant wave equation in an effective acoustic spacetime with non-linear corrections, thereby rendering the analogue geometry dynamical. We further examine how non-linear effects influence the behaviour of the acoustic horizon, thus extending previous analogue gravity studies toward more realistic relativistic accretion scenarios.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section (
II
), we describe the relativistic equation of state and define the associated adiabatic sound speed. In Section (
III
), we derive the acoustic metric and the wave equation arising from the fluid flow. Section (
IV
) describes the non-linear perturbations in details. Section (
V
) describes the variation of the acoustic horizon. In Section (
VI
), we analyze the stability of standing and travelling waves. In Section (
VII
), we estimate the asymptotic sound speed from the wave equation. Section (
VIII
) includes the graphical analysis and verification of the results with observational data. Finally, Section (
IX
) summarizes our conclusions.

II
A brief on multi-species equation of state

We consider a multispecies accretion flow consisting of electrons (
e^{-}
), positrons (
e^{+}
), and protons (
p^{+}
) of proportion parametrized by
\xi
. The number density (n) of the accreting matter is given by:

n=\sum_{i}n_{i}=n_{e^{-}}+n_{e^{+}}+n_{p^{+}},

(1)

where,
n_{e^{-}}
,
n_{e^{+}}
, and
n_{p^{+}}
are the electron, positron, and
proton number densities, respectively. If we further demand that the
overall charge neutrality is always maintained, we have:

n_{e^{-}}=n_{e^{+}}+n_{p^{+}},

(2)

which gives:

n=2n_{e^{-}},\qquad n_{e^{+}}=n_{e^{-}}(1-\xi),

(3)

where,
\xi\equiv\dfrac{n_{p^{+}}}{n_{e^{-}}}
.

The mass density is expressed as:

\rho=\sum_{i}n_{i}m_{i}=n_{e^{-}}m_{e^{-}}\alpha=\rho_{e^{-}}\alpha,

(4)

where,
\eta=\frac{m_{e^{-}}}{m_{p^{+}}}
,
m_{e^{-}}
, and
m_{p^{+}}
are the electron and proton masses, respectively, and
\alpha=2-\xi\left(1-\frac{1}{\eta}\right)
. The EoS for multispecies flow, as given in
Chattopadhyay and Ryu 2009
, is:

\bar{e}=\sum_{i}e_{i}=\sum_{i}n_{i}m_{i}c^{2}+p_{i}\left(\dfrac{9p_{i}+3n_{i}m_{i}c^{2}}{3p_{i}+2n_{i}m_{i}c^{2}}\right),

(5)

where,
\bar{e}
is the energy density.

The isotropic pressure can be defined with respect to the Boltzmann constant
K_{B}

and absolute temperature
T
as:

p=\sum_{i}p_{i}=2n_{e^{-}}K_{B}T.

(6)

We define the dimensionless temperature as:

\Theta=\frac{K_{B}T}{m_{e^{-}}c^{2}}.

(7)

Using Eq. (
7
), the expression for the isotropic pressure
takes the form:

p=2n_{e^{-}}m_{e^{-}}c^{2}\Theta=2\rho_{e^{-}}c^{2}\Theta=2\frac{\rho}{\alpha}c^{2}\Theta.

(8)

The EoS given in Eq. (
5
) can be written as:

\bar{e}=n_{e^{-}}m_{e^{-}}c^{2}\Xi=\rho_{e^{-}}c^{2}\Xi=\frac{\rho}{\alpha}c^{2}\Xi,

(9)

where,
\Xi=(2-\xi)\left[1+\Theta\left(\frac{9\Theta+3}{3\Theta+2}\right)\right]+\xi\left[\frac{1}{\eta}+\Theta\left(\frac{9\Theta+\frac{3}{\eta}}{3\Theta+\frac{2}{\eta}}\right)\right]
.

The specific enthalpy can be written as:

h=\frac{\bar{e}+p}{\rho}=\frac{c^{2}}{\alpha}(\Xi+2\Theta).

(10)

The polytropic index is conveniently given by
Chattopadhyay and Ryu 2009
:

N=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}\Xi}{\text{d}\Theta}.

(11)

Similarly, the adiabatic index can be written as:

\Gamma=1+\frac{1}{N}.

(12)

The adiabatic sound speed is then given by:

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{2\Gamma\Theta}{\alpha}.

(13)

III
Wave Equation and Inverse Acoustic Metric

The general form of the continuity equation is given by:

\dot{\rho}+\overrightarrow{\nabla}.(\rho\overrightarrow{v})=0.

(14)

The Euler equation is given by:

\dot{\overrightarrow{v}}+\left(\overrightarrow{v}.\overrightarrow{\nabla}\right)\overrightarrow{v}+\frac{1}{\rho}\overrightarrow{\nabla}p+\overrightarrow{\nabla}\Phi=0

(15)

In these equations, overdots denote time derivatives and

\vec{\nabla}
is the spatial derivative. The density, pressure, and velocity
of the fluid are respectively denoted by
\rho
,
p
, and
\vec{v}
,
while
\Phi=\Phi(r)
is the potential of an external force.
We will consider spherically symmetric and barotropic flows.
The barotropic condition
p=p(\rho)
allows us to define a scalar function

H(\rho)
such that
\vec{\nabla}H=\frac{1}{\rho}\vec{\nabla}p
.
With the sound speed
c_{s}
defined by
c_{s}^{2}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}
,
we find:

\frac{\partial H}{\partial r}=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\implies\frac{\partial H}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial r}=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial r}\implies\frac{\partial H}{\partial\rho}=\frac{c_{\text{s}}^{2}}{\rho}.

(16)

Spherical symmetry implies a spatial dependence on the radial distance and a non-vanishing radial velocity component, which we denote by
v
. While, the fields also depend on time, we assume a time-independent external potential
\Phi=\Phi(r)
. Hence, Eq. (
14
) and (
15
) simplify to the following two-dimensional equations:

\dot{\rho}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(r^{2}\rho v)^{\prime}=0

(17)

\text{and }\dot{v}+vv^{\prime}+H^{\prime}+\Phi^{\prime}=0,

(18)

where, primes denote derivatives with respect to
r
in Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
).

We define the mass accretion rate:

f=\rho vr^{2}

(19)

and consider the system in terms of
f
and
\rho
.

\therefore\,\,v=\frac{f}{\rho r^{2}}.

(20)

In the case of Eq. (
17
), we find from direct substitution:

\dot{\rho}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{r^{2}}=0\text{.}

(21)

Partially differentiating Eq. (
20
) with respect to
t
and using Eq. (
21
) gives:

\dot{v}=\frac{\dot{f}}{\rho r^{2}}-\frac{f\dot{\rho}}{\rho^{2}r^{2}}=\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}}\left(\dot{f}-\frac{f\dot{\rho}}{\rho}\right)=\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}}\left(\dot{f}+\frac{ff^{\prime}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)\text{.}

(22)

In order to arrive at a wave equation, we partially differentiate Eq. (
18
) with respect to
t
to get:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\dot{v}+\partial_{t}\left(vv^{\prime}\right)+\partial_{t}H^{\prime}+\cancelto{0}{\partial_{t}\Phi^{\prime}}=0

or,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left\{\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}}\left(\dot{f}+\frac{ff^{\prime}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)\right\}+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}v^{2}\right)+\partial_{r}\dot{H}=0

[using Eq. (
22
)]

or,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\dot{f}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{ff^{\prime}}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}v^{2}}{2}\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\rho}\dot{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt=0

or,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\dot{f}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{ff^{\prime}}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}\right)+\partial_{r}\left(v\dot{v}\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{c_{\text{s}}^{2}\dot{\rho}}{\rho}\right)=0

[using Eq. (
16
)]

or,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\dot{f}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{ff^{\prime}}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{f\dot{f}}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{f^{2}f^{\prime}}{\rho^{3}r^{6}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 135.15059pt+\partial_{r}\left(-\frac{c_{\text{s}}^{2}f^{\prime}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)=0

[using Eqs. (
20
), (
21
) and (
22
)]

or,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}}\partial_{t}f\right)+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{f}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}\partial_{r}f\right)+\partial_{r}\left(\frac{f}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}\partial_{t}f\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\partial_{r}\left\{\left(\frac{f^{2}}{\rho^{3}r^{6}}-\frac{c_{\text{s}}^{2}}{\rho r^{2}}\right)\partial_{r}f\right\}=0

(23)

By defining the inverse metric components,

g^{tt}=\frac{1}{\rho r^{2}},\quad g^{tr}=\frac{f}{\rho^{2}r^{4}}=g^{rt},\quad g^{rr}=\frac{f^{2}}{\rho^{3}r^{6}}-\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{\rho r^{2}},

(24)

we find that Eq. (
III
) takes the suggestive form of the following wave equation:

\partial_{\mu}(g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}f)=0.

(25)

Equation (
25
) will describe fluctuations on a curved background only after perturbatively expanding about a stationary solution. The solutions of Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
) are generally not the same as those for Eqs. (
21
) and (
25
), since Eq. (
25
) results from a time derivative of Eq. (
18
). However, boundary conditions specify a unique stationary solution of eqs. (
17
) and (
18
) that manifestly satisfy Eqs. (
21
) and (
25
). Subsequently, a perturbative solution for
f
from the original fluid equations will be one of the solutions of Eq. (
25
) through a consistently chosen boundary condition in time.

We can further express Eq. (
25
) as [see
Visser 1998
]:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-G}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-G}G^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}f\right)=0,

(26)

where,
\sqrt{-G}G^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}
.
For two-dimensional flows, this is achieved by introducing additional spatial metric components, such as
g^{\theta\theta}
and
g^{\phi\phi}=\frac{g^{\theta\theta}}{\sin^{2}\theta}
that respect spherical symmetry without modifying Eq. (
25
). As this transformation does not affect the causal structure or dynamics, we will consider the unique two-dimensional inverse effective metric
g^{\mu\nu}
with components given in Eq. (
24
).

V
Acoustic Metric and Horizon Variations

The inverse metric to all orders can be compactly represented using Eq. (
24
), with the order
n
expression resulting from Eq. (
IV
). Inverting Eq. (
24
) provides the following acoustic metric:

\displaystyle[g_{\mu\nu}]=\begin{bmatrix}g_{tt}&g_{tr}\\
g_{rt}&g_{rr}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}gg^{rr}&-gg^{rt}\\
-gg^{tr}&gg^{tt}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\rho r^{2}(1-\beta^{2})&\dfrac{f}{c_{s}^{2}}\\
\dfrac{f}{c_{s}^{2}}&-\dfrac{\rho r^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}}\end{bmatrix},

(51)

where,
g=-\dfrac{\rho^{2}r^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}}=-\dfrac{\alpha\rho^{2}r^{2}}{2\Gamma\Theta}
is the determinant of the metric and

\beta=\dfrac{f}{c_{s}\rho r^{2}}
is the fluid velocity to sound speed ratio.
In units with the speed of light
c=1
, we have
0<v=\dfrac{f}{\rho r^{2}}<1
and
0<\beta<\dfrac{1}{c_{s}}
. The condition
g_{tt}=0
, or equivalently
g^{rr}=0
, determines the horizon radius
r_{H}

r_{H}^{4}=\frac{f^{2}}{\rho^{2}c_{s}^{2}}=\frac{\alpha f^{2}}{2\Gamma\Theta\rho^{2}}.

(52)

Taking logarithmic derivative of Eq. (
52
) gives:

\displaystyle 4\frac{\delta r_{H}}{r_{H}}=2\frac{\delta f}{f}-2\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}-\frac{\delta c_{s}^{2}}{c_{s}^{2}}

or,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta r_{H}}{r_{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta f}{f}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}-\frac{1}{4}\frac{\delta\Theta}{c_{s}^{2}}\frac{\partial c_{s}^{2}}{\partial\Theta}

\displaystyle\hskip 19.0633pt=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta f}{f}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{\text{d}\Gamma}{\text{d}\Theta}\right)\delta\Theta

\displaystyle\hskip 19.0633pt=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\delta f}{f}-\left[\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{\text{d}\Gamma}{\text{d}\Theta}\right)\delta\Theta\right]\right].

(53)

Here,
f
,
\rho
,
\Theta
and
r_{H}
denote values at a specific order in perturbation, while
\delta f
,
\delta\rho
,
\delta\Theta
and
\delta r_{H}
describe their relative variations to the next order. Using Eq. (
21
), we deduce:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}+\frac{f^{\prime}}{r^{2}}=0

or,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}=-\dot{\rho}r^{2}

or,

\displaystyle f=-\int\text{d}rr^{2}\dot{\rho}

or,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta f}{f}=\frac{\int\text{d}rr^{2}\delta\dot{\rho}}{\int\text{d}rr^{2}\dot{\rho}}

(54)

The relative mass accretion rate change
\frac{\delta f}{f}
is thus related to a spatially averaged change in energy and is expected to be positive. The relative density change
\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}
and the relative temperature change
\frac{\delta\Theta}{\Theta}
however are not constrained. In particular, Eq. (
V
) leads to the following two cases:

\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{\text{d}\Gamma}{\text{d}\Theta}\right)\delta\Theta>\frac{\delta f}{f}

(55)

for a receding acoustic horizon and

\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Theta}+\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{\text{d}\Gamma}{\text{d}\Theta}\right)\delta\Theta<\frac{\delta f}{f}

(56)

for an advancing acoustic horizon.

VI
Stability Analysis of Standing and Travelling Waves

Since the explicit radial and temporal dependency of the acoustic metric components (
g_{\mu\nu}
) is not known in general, we now focus exclusively on the stability analysis of
1^{\text{st}}
order perturbations.
Consider a trial wave function as a solution to the
1^{\text{st}}
order wave equation [Eq. (
36
)]:

f_{1}=\tilde{f}_{\omega}(r)e^{-i\omega t}.

(57)

Therefore, Eq. (
36
) becomes:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[g^{tt}_{(0)}\,\partial_{t}f_{1}\right]+\partial_{t}\left[g^{tr}_{(0)}\,\partial_{r}f_{1}\right]+\partial_{r}\left[g^{rt}_{(0)}\,\partial_{t}f_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\partial_{r}\left[g^{rr}_{(0)}\,\partial_{r}f_{1}\right]=0

or,

\displaystyle g^{tt}_{(0)}\,\partial_{t}^{2}f_{1}+g^{tr}_{(0)}\,\partial_{t}\partial_{r}f_{1}+\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\,\partial_{t}f_{1}+g^{rt}_{(0)}\,\partial_{r}\partial_{t}f_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 103.85237pt+\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\,\partial_{r}f_{1}+g^{rr}_{(0)}\,\partial_{r}^{2}f_{1}=0

or,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}+2i\omega g^{tr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}+i\omega\left[\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\right]\tilde{f}_{\omega}-\left[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 172.13925pt-g^{rr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}_{\omega}=0

or,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}+i\omega\left[2g^{tr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}+\tilde{f}_{\omega}\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\right]-[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}]\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 173.56198pt-g^{rr}\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}_{\omega}=0.

(58)

VI.1
Standing Wave Analysis

If the accreting object has a clearly defined physical surface (such as a neutron star), it is expected that any perturbation must vanish both at large distances from the object and at its boundary. In such cases, a standing-wave type solution becomes relevant. However, in a transonic flow, once the fluid crosses the sonic point and becomes supersonic, it can return to a subsonic state only through a discontinuous shock. Since a standing wave represents a continuous solution, this situation is incompatible with transonic behaviour. Therefore, for a standing-wave solution to exist, the flow must remain subsonic everywhere.

We will now solve the quadratic equation in
\omega
, given by Eq. (
VI
). Let the two boundaries be
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
(
>r_{1}
), such that:

\tilde{f}_{\omega}(r_{1})=\tilde{f}_{\omega}(r_{2})=0.

(59)

Multiplying Eq. (
VI
) by
\tilde{f}_{\omega}(r)
and integrating w.r.t.
r
from
r_{1}
to
r_{2}
gives:

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\text{d}r+i\omega\left[2\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{tr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}\tilde{f}_{\omega}\text{d}r+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\text{d}r\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt-\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\left[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]\tilde{f}_{\omega}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}\text{d}r-\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{rr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime\prime}_{\omega}\tilde{f}_{\omega}\text{d}r=0

or,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\text{d}r-\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\tilde{f}_{\omega}\partial_{r}\left[g^{rr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}\right]\text{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt+i\omega\cancelto{0}{\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\partial_{r}\left[\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}g^{rt}_{(0)}\right]\text{d}r}=0

or,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\text{d}r-\cancelto{0}{\Big[\tilde{f}_{\omega}g^{rr}_{(0)}\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega}\Big]_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}}+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{rr}_{(0)}\,(\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega})^{2}dr=0

or,

\displaystyle\omega^{2}=-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{rr}_{(0)}(\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega})^{2}\text{d}r}{\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}g^{tt}_{(0)}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\text{d}r}.

(60)

Now,
g^{tt}_{(0)}=\dfrac{v_{0}}{f_{0}}
and
g^{rr}_{(0)}=\dfrac{v_{0}(v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2})}{f_{0}}
. Therefore, Eq. (
VI.1
) becomes:

\omega^{2}=-\frac{\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}v_{0}(v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2})(\tilde{f}^{\prime}_{\omega})^{2}\text{d}r}{\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}v_{0}\tilde{f}_{\omega}^{2}\text{d}r}.

(61)

Since the flow is subsonic everywhere (
v_{0}<c_{s0}
),
\omega
is necessarily real in this region, leading to an oscillatory standing-wave solution even in the presence of a divergent term. Hence, the standing waves are stable under linear perturbations.

VI.2
Travelling Wave Analysis

If the accretor is a black hole, there is no solid surface, and any flow reaching the event horizon becomes supersonic. In this case, the analysis presented in the previous subsection no longer applies. The wave behaves as a travelling wave, with a wavelength much smaller than the radius of the accretor. Employing the Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin (WKB) approximation method, we can approximate the solution of Eq. (
VI
) in the following power series of
\omega
:

\tilde{f}_{\omega}(r)=\exp\left[\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}(r)}{\omega^{n}}\right].

(62)

Therefore,
\partial_{r}\tilde{f}_{\omega}=\tilde{f}_{\omega}\displaystyle\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}\tilde{f}_{\omega}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{r}\tilde{f}_{\omega})\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}+\tilde{f}_{\omega}\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}_{\omega}\left[\left\{\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\left\{\sum_{l=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{l}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{l}}\right\}+\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}\right].

Hence, Eq. (
VI
) becomes:

\displaystyle\omega^{2}g^{tt}_{(0)}+i\omega\left\{2g^{tr}_{(0)}\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}+\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\right\}

\displaystyle-\left[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}

\displaystyle-g^{rr}_{(0)}\left[\left\{\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\left\{\sum_{l=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{l}^{\prime}(r)}{\omega^{l}}\right\}+\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{n}^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\omega^{n}}\right]=0.

(63)

Equating the coefficients of
\omega^{2}
,
\omega
,
\omega^{0}
and
\omega^{-1}
in Eq. (
VI.2
) give:

\displaystyle g^{tt}_{(0)}+2ig^{tr}_{(0)}k^{\prime}_{-1}-g^{rr}_{(0)}(k^{\prime}_{-1})^{2}=0

or,

\displaystyle\dfrac{v_{0}(v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2})}{f_{0}}(k^{\prime}_{-1})^{2}-2i\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{f_{0}}\,k^{\prime}_{-1}-\frac{v_{0}}{f_{0}}=0

or,

\displaystyle\bigl\{(v_{0}+c_{s0})k^{\prime}_{-1}-i\bigr\}\bigl\{(v_{0}-c_{s0})k^{\prime}_{-1}-i\bigr\}=0

or,

\displaystyle k^{\prime}_{-1}=\frac{i}{v_{0}\pm c_{s0}}

or,

\displaystyle k_{-1}=i\int\frac{\text{d}r}{v_{0}\pm c_{s0}},

(64)

\displaystyle i\left[2g^{tr}_{(0)}k^{\prime}_{0}+\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]k^{\prime}_{-1}

\displaystyle-g^{rr}_{(0)}\left(2k^{\prime}_{-1}k^{\prime}_{0}+k^{\prime\prime}_{-1}\right)=0

\displaystyle\text{or, }\left[2ig^{tr}_{(0)}-2g^{rr}_{(0)}k^{\prime}_{-1}\right]k^{\prime}_{0}=

\displaystyle\left[k^{\prime}_{-1}\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}+k^{\prime\prime}_{-1}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle-i\partial_{r}g^{rt}_{(0)}

\displaystyle\text{or, }k^{\prime}_{0}=-\frac{\partial_{r}\left[k^{\prime}_{-1}g^{rr}_{(0)}-ig^{tr}_{(0)}\right]}{2\left[k^{\prime}_{-1}g^{rr}_{(0)}-ig^{tr}_{(0)}\right]}

\displaystyle\text{or, }k^{\prime}_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\left[\ln\left(\frac{c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\text{or, }k_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}\right),

(65)

\displaystyle 2ig^{tr}_{(0)}k^{\prime}_{1}-\left[\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}\right]k^{\prime}_{0}-g^{rr}_{(0)}\left[2k^{\prime}_{-1}k^{\prime}_{1}+(k^{\prime}_{0})^{2}+k^{\prime\prime}_{0}\right]=0

or,

\displaystyle\pm i\frac{8c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}k^{\prime}_{1}-2\partial_{r}\left[\left(\frac{v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}\right)\partial_{r}\left[\ln\left(\frac{c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}}\right)-2\ln r\right]\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 38.41139pt+\left[\partial_{r}\left[\ln\left(\frac{c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}}\right)-2\ln r\right]\right]^{2}\left(\frac{v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}\right)=0

or,

\displaystyle k^{\prime}_{1}=\pm\frac{i}{c_{s\infty}r^{2}}

or,

\displaystyle k_{1}=\mp\frac{i}{c_{s\infty}r}

(66)

and

\displaystyle-2ig^{tr}_{(0)}k^{\prime}_{2}+k^{\prime}_{1}\partial_{r}g^{rr}_{(0)}+g^{rr}_{(0)}\left(k^{\prime}_{-1}k^{\prime}_{2}+2k^{\prime}_{0}k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime\prime}_{1}\right)=0

or,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-v_{0}\pm c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}r^{2}}\right)k^{\prime}_{2}\pm\partial_{r}\left(\frac{v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}r^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 55.48277pt\mp\left(\frac{v_{0}^{2}-c_{s0}^{2}}{\rho_{0}r^{4}}\right)\partial_{r}\left[\ln\left(\frac{c_{s0}}{\rho_{0}}\right)-2\ln r\right]=0

or,

\displaystyle k^{\prime}_{2}=\mp\frac{2c_{s\infty}}{r^{3}}

or,

\displaystyle k_{2}=\pm\frac{c_{s\infty}}{r^{2}},

(67)

respectively. Here we have evaluated Eqs. (

\rho_{0}\big|_{r\to\infty}\rightarrow\rho_{\infty}

Therefore, it follows that:

k_{-1}\sim ir,\,k_{0}\sim\ln r,\,k_{1}\sim\frac{i}{r}\text{ and }k_{2}\sim\frac{1}{r^{2}}.

Hence, the odd and the even modes are phase related and amplitude related, respectively.

We see that, for

\displaystyle\omega^{2}|k_{-1}|\gg\omega|k_{0}|\gg\omega^{0}|k_{1}|\gg\omega^{-1}|k_{2}|\gg\cdots

\omega^{-n}|k_{n}(r)|\gg\omega^{-(n+1)}|k_{(n+1)}(r)|.

(70)

VII
Asymptotic Sound Speed from Wave Equation

In the asymptotic limit, we have:

g^{tt}_{(0)}\big|_{r\to\infty}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\infty}r^{2}},

(71)

g^{tr}_{(0)}\big|_{r\to\infty}=g^{rt}_{(0)}\big|_{r\to\infty}=0

(72)

\text{and}\quad g^{rr}_{(0)}\big|_{r\to\infty}=-\frac{c_{s\infty}^{2}}{\rho_{\infty}r^{2}}.

(73)

Hence, we get:

c_{s\infty}^{2}=\left|\frac{g^{rr}_{(0)}}{g^{tt}_{(0)}}\right|_{r\to\infty}.

(74)

The above result is similar to what one obtains for flat spacetime using the following wave equation for a scalar field,
\Psi(r,t)
:

-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial^{2}_{t}\Psi+\partial^{2}_{r}\Psi=0.

(75)

IX
Conclusion

Only the expressions related to
g^{rr}
change if one non-linearly perturbs a system with relativistic EOS instead of a non-relativistic one. This occurs due to the presence of
c_{s}
in
g_{rr}
.
c_{s}
and
\Gamma
depend explicitly on
\Theta
for relativistic EOS. Horizon variations depend on both
\Theta
and
\rho
.
\Theta
can have any arbitrary temporal dependency in general. The stability of stationary solutions for a general order of perturbation is inconclusive. This is due to the presence of non-zero time-dependent terms in general. Linear perturbations with relativistic EOS are stable for both standing and travelling waves. In particular, the travelling waves have two types of components — phase related and amplitude related. The results obtained from the plots related to the stationary background metric are in agreement with the observational data.
