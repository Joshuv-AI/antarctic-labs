B-complex manifolds with generalized corners. I. Newlander–Nirenberg Theorems
Hülya Argüz, Dominic Joyce

Abstract
We generalize complex manifolds to manifolds with corners X X , and to manifolds with generalized corners (g-corners) in the sense of the second author [ 8 ] , using complex structures on the b-tangent bundle (log tangent bundle) T b ​ X {}^{b}TX . We prove a formal Newlander–Nirenberg type theorem showing that along each corner stratum of X X , the b-complex structure agrees with a standard model to infinite order. In the sequel [ 1 ] we show that if S S is a log smooth log ℂ {\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}} -scheme, or log smooth log complex analytic space, then the Kato–Nakayama space S KN S^{\rm KN} has the structure of a b-complex manifold with g-corners. Using our Newlander–Nirenberg theorem we give necessary and sufficient conditions for a b-complex manifold with g-corners to be a Kato–Nakayama space.



1
Introduction

1.1
Overview

Manifolds with corners, locally modelled on
[0,\infty)^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m-k}
, provide a natural extension of smooth manifolds that accommodates boundary and corner strata. They are particularly useful in situations where non-compact manifolds admit compactifications by adding boundary faces with controlled geometric structure, allowing one to study asymptotic behaviour within a differential-geometric framework. Manifolds with generalized corners (g-corners), introduced by the second author
[
8
]
, further extend this notion by allowing more flexible local models governed by ‘weakly toric monoids’.

The aim of this paper is to extend the theory of complex manifolds to the setting of manifolds with corners and, more generally, manifolds with g-corners. Classically, complex manifolds may be defined either via holomorphic coordinate charts or via integrable almost complex structures, with the equivalence of these approaches ensured by the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem.

In this work, we develop a theory of b-complex manifolds with g-corners following the latter perspective, based on b-almost complex structures and their integrability. Our main result establishes a formal analogue of the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem in this context.

A sequel
[
1
]
applies these ideas to log geometry: we show that if
S
is a log smooth log
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-scheme or log complex analytic space then the ‘Kato–Nakayama space’
S^{\rm KN}
is a b-complex manifold with g-corners, and characterizes the b-complex manifolds which arise this way.

This paper will prove Theorems

Manifolds with (generalized) corners

Manifolds with corners
are analogues of smooth manifolds which are locally modelled on
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}_{k}=[0,\infty)^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m-k}
for
0\leqslant\penalty k\leqslant\penalty m
. They include ordinary manifolds, locally modelled on
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}_{0}={\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}
, and
manifolds with boundary
, locally modelled on
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}_{1}=[0,\infty)\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m-1}
. They were introduced by Cerf
[
3
]
and Douady
[
5
]
in the early 1960s, and studied by many people including Melrose
[
14
,
15
]
,
[
11
, §1]
and the second author
[
7
,
8
]
. There are several different notions of smooth map between manifolds with corners in the literature; our definition in §
2.1
follows Melrose
[
14
,
15
]
, who calls them
b-maps
, and the second author
[
8
]
. Manifolds with corners and smooth maps form a category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}
.

We will be interested in an extension introduced by the second author
[
8
]
, called
manifolds with generalized corners
, or
manifolds with g-corners
, explained in §
2.2
. These were inspired by the ‘interior binomial varieties’ of Kottke–Melrose
[
11
, §9]
. They form a category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
containing
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}
as a full subcategory. The local models for manifolds with g-corners are spaces
X_{P}
depending on a
weakly toric monoid

P
, where
X_{P}\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}_{k}
when
P\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{m-k}
.

We will discuss only topics that we need later. In particular, we will not define the boundary
\partial X
or corners
C(X)=\coprod_{k=0}^{\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits X}C_{k}(X)
of a manifold with corners
X
, though these are very important.

and the second author

]

2.1.1
The definition of manifolds with corners

Definition 2.1
.

Use the notation

2.1.2
(B-)tangent bundles and (b-)cotangent bundles

Manifolds with corners

\displaystyle TX|_{U}

(2.1)

\displaystyle{}^{b}TX|_{U}

\displaystyle\cong\Bigl\langle x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k+1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{m}}\Bigr\rangle_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}},

2.2
Manifolds with g-corners

Manifolds with g-corners were introduced by the second author
[
8
]
, building on ideas in Kottke–Melrose
[
11
, §9]
. Much of the theory of manifolds with corners — in particular, b-(co)tangent bundles
{}^{b}TX,{}^{b}T^{*}X\rightarrow X
— extends nicely to manifolds with g-corners. Manifolds with g-corners behave better in some ways, e.g. transverse fibre products exist in the category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
of manifolds with g-corners
[
8
, §4.3]
, but they exist in
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}
only under restrictive conditions.

Manifolds with g-corners arise naturally in some problems. For example, Ma’u and Woodward
[
13
]
define moduli spaces
{\mathbin{\smash{\,\,\overline{\!\!\mathcal{M}\!}\,}}}_{n,1}
of ‘stable
n
-marked quilted discs’. As in
[
13
, §6]
, for
n\geqslant 4
these are not manifolds with corners, but have an exotic corner structure; in the language of
[
8
]
, the
{\mathbin{\smash{\,\,\overline{\!\!\mathcal{M}\!}\,}}}_{n,1}
are manifolds with g-corners. We will show in the sequel
[
1
]
that if
S
is a log smooth log
{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
-scheme or complex analytic space then the ‘Kato–Nakayama space’
S^{\rm KN}
is a b-complex manifold with g-corners, but it may not be a manifold with corners.

2.2.1
Monoids

Definition 2.8
.

A
monoid
is a set
P
with an associative binary operation, often denoted additively by
+
, with an identity element
0
. Throughout this paper, all monoids are assumed to be commutative. An element of
P
is called a
unit
if it has an inverse. The set of units of
P
, which itself is a submonoid of
P
, is denoted by
P^{\times}
. The
Grothendieck group

P^{\mathrm{gp}}
of a monoid

is an abelian group with a monoid morphism
\iota\colon P\rightarrow P^{\mathrm{gp}}
, with the universal property that for any morphism
f:P\rightarrow A
from
P
to an abelian group
A
, there is a unique group morphism
g:P^{\mathrm{gp}}\rightarrow A
such that
f=g\circ\iota
. A monoid

is called:

(i)

integral
if the cancellation property holds, or equivalently if
P\rightarrow P^{\mathrm{gp}}
is injective.

(ii)

fine
if it is finitely generated and integral.

(iii)

saturated
if it is integral and
P=\{m\in P^{\mathrm{gp}}:nm\in P\subseteq P^{\mathrm{gp}}
for some
n\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}_{>0}\}
.

(iv)

sharp
if
P^{\times}=\{0\}
,

(v)

torsion-free
if it is integral and
P^{\mathrm{gp}}
is torsion-free,

(vi)

weakly toric
if it is fine, saturated and torsion free.

(vii)

toric
if it is weakly toric and sharp.

Every weakly toric monoid is isomorphic to a submonoid of some
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}
.

Some authors, such as Ogus
[
21
]
, call our weakly toric monoids ‘toric’.

If
P
is a weakly toric monoid then
P\cong Q\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n}
where
Q
is a toric monoid. We have
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n}=P^{\times}
and
Q\cong P/P^{\times}
, so the exact sequence
0\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n}\rightarrow P\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0
is natural. If
P
is a monoid, the
dual monoid
is
P^{\vee}=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P,{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}})
. There is a natural morphism
\eta(P):P\rightarrow(P^{\vee})^{\vee}
given by
\eta:p\mapsto\bigl(\mu\mapsto\mu(p)\bigr)
for
p\in P
and
\mu\in P^{\vee}
. This is an isomorphism if
P
is toric.

The next definition is taken from Ogus
[
21
, §1.4]
.

Definition 2.9
.

An

of a monoid

is a subset

such that for all
i\in I
and

we have

. An ideal

is called

if

and

or

.

A submonoid

is called a

of

if

and

and

. If is easy to see that

is a face of

if and only if

is a prime ideal in

. This gives a bijection

between faces

of

and prime ideals

in

.

The

of a face

is the rank of the abelian group

, which is defined when

is finitely generated. If

.

2.2.3
The category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
of manifolds with g-corners

We can now define the category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
of
manifolds with generalized corners
, or
g-corners
, extending Definition
2.2
for the case of ordinary corners.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
X
be a second countable Hausdorff topological space. An
m
-dimensional generalized chart
, or
g-chart
, on
X
is a triple
(P,U,\phi)
, where
P
is a weakly toric monoid with
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits P=m
, and
P
is a submonoid of
{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{k}
for some
k\geqslant 0
, and
U\subseteq X_{P}
is open, for
X_{P}
as in §
2.2.2
, and
\phi:U\rightarrow X
is a homeomorphism with an open set
\phi(U)
in
X
.

Let
(P,U,\phi),(Q,V,\psi)
be
m
-dimensional g-charts on
X
. We call
(P,U,\phi)
and
(Q,V,\psi)

compatible
if
\psi^{-1}\circ\phi:\phi^{-1}\bigl(\phi(U)\cap\psi(V)\bigr)\rightarrow\psi^{-1}\bigl(\phi(U)\cap\psi(V)\bigr)
is a diffeomorphism between open subsets of
X_{P},X_{Q}
, in the sense of Definition
2.10
.

An
m
-
dimensional generalized atlas
, or
g-atlas
, on
X
is a family
\{(P^{i},U^{i},\phi^{i})\!:\!i\!\in\!I\}
of pairwise compatible
m
-dimensional g-charts on
X
with
X\!=\!\bigcup_{i\in I}\phi^{i}(U^{i})
. We call such a g-atlas
maximal
if it is not a proper subset of any other g-atlas. Any g-atlas
\{(P^{i},U^{i},\phi^{i}):i\in I\}
is contained in a unique maximal g-atlas, the family of all g-charts
(P,U,\phi)
on
X
compatible with
(P^{i},U^{i},\phi^{i})
for all
i\in I
.

An
m
-
dimensional manifold with generalized corners
, or
g-corners
, is a second countable Hausdorff topological space
X
with a maximal
m
-dimensional g-atlas. Usually we refer to
X
as the manifold, leaving the g-atlas implicit. By a
g-chart
(P,U,\phi)
on

X
, we mean an element of the maximal g-atlas. Write
\mathop{\rm dim}\nolimits X=m
.

The
interior

X^{\circ}
of an
m
-manifold with g-corners
X
is the dense open subset
X^{\circ}\subset X
such that if
(P,U,\phi)
is a g-chart on
X
then
\phi^{-1}(X^{\circ})=U^{\circ}
, where
U^{\circ}\subseteq U\subseteq X_{P}
is as in Definition
2.10
, so
(P,U^{\circ},\phi)
is a g-chart on
X^{\circ}
.

Let
X,Y
be manifolds with g-corners, and
f:X\rightarrow Y
a continuous map of the underlying topological spaces. We say that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is
smooth
if for all g-charts
(P,U,\phi)
on
X
and
(Q,V,\psi)
on
Y
, the map

\psi^{-1}\circ f\circ\phi:(f\circ\phi)^{-1}(\psi(V))\longrightarrow V

(2.8)

is a smooth map between the open subsets
(f\circ\phi)^{-1}(\psi(V))\subseteq U\subseteq X_{P}
and
V\subseteq X_{Q}
, in the sense of Definition
2.10
. We say that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a
diffeomorphism
if it is a bijection, and both
f:X\rightarrow Y
,
f^{-1}:Y\rightarrow X
are smooth.

We say that a smooth map
f:X\rightarrow Y
is
interior
if
f(X^{\circ})\subseteq Y^{\circ}
. Equivalently,
f
is interior if the maps (
2.8
) are interior in the sense of Definition
2.10
for all
(P,U,\phi),(Q,V,\psi)
.

Manifolds with g-corners and smooth maps, or interior maps, form a category. Write
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
for the category with objects manifolds with g-corners
X,Y
and morphisms smooth maps
f:X\rightarrow Y
, and
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}_{in}}}\subset{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
for the (non-full) subcategory with morphisms interior maps.

By Example
2.12
, we may identify the category
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}
of manifolds with corners with the full subcategory
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}\subset{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
of manifolds with g-corners

which may be covered by charts
(P,U,\phi)
with
P\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{m-k}
for
0\leqslant\penalty k\leqslant\penalty m
. Then we have subcategories
{\mathop{\bf Man}}\subset{\mathop{\bf Man^{b}}}\subset{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}\subset{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
and
{\mathop{\bf Man^{c}_{in}}}={\mathop{\bf Man^{c}}}\cap{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}_{in}}}
in
{\mathop{\bf Man^{gc}}}
.

Let

If

3
B-complex manifolds with g-corners

3.1
B-complex structures on manifolds with g-corners

We generalize some important definitions in complex geometry, including Definition
1.1
, to manifolds with (g-)corners.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a manifold of with corners or g-corners, of real dimension
2n
. A
b-almost complex structure

J
on
X
is a morphism of vector bundles
J:{}^{b}TX\rightarrow{}^{b}TX
such that

(i)

J^{2}=-{\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits}_{{}^{b}TX}
, and

(ii)

For all
0\leqslant\penalty k\leqslant\penalty 2n
, we have the depth stratum
S^{k}(X)\subset X
as in §
2.1.3
and §
2.2.5
, and from the exact sequences (
2.3
) and (
2.12
) we have a rank
k
vector subbundle
{}^{b}i\bigl({}^{b}N_{S^{k}(X)}\bigr)\subseteq{}^{b}TX|_{S^{k}(X)}
. We require that in subbundles of
{}^{b}TX|_{S^{k}(X)}
we have the transversality condition

{}^{b}i\bigl({}^{b}N_{S^{k}(X)}\bigr)\cap J\bigl[{}^{b}i\bigl({}^{b}N_{S^{k}(X)}\bigr)\bigr]=0.

This is only possible if
S^{k}(X)=\emptyset
when
n<k\leqslant\penalty 2n
. It holds automatically if
k=0
or 1.

We call
(X,J)
a
b-almost complex manifold
.

The usual proof of the existence of Nijenhuis tensors
[
10
, §IX.2]
also works for b-(co)tangent bundles. Thus, a b-almost complex structure has a
Nijenhuis tensor

N_{J}\in\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{*}X\otimes{}^{b}T^{*}X\otimes{}^{b}TX)
characterized uniquely by the fact that for all b-vector fields
v,w\in\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}TX)
, we have

N_{J}(v,w)=[v,w]+J\bigl([Jv,w]+[v,Jw]\bigr)-[Jv,Jw],

(3.1)

where
[\,,\,]
is the Lie bracket of b-vector fields from §
2.1.2
and §
2.2.4
. We call
J

integrable
if
N_{J}=0
. If
J
is integrable we call it a
b-complex structure
, and we call
(X,J)
a
b-complex manifold
.

Equivalently, the complexification
{}^{b}TX\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
has a decomposition

{}^{b}TX\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}={}^{b}T^{1,0}X\oplus{}^{b}T^{0,1}X

(3.2)

into the
i
-eigenspace
{}^{b}T^{1,0}X
and
(-i)
-eigenspace
{}^{b}T^{0,1}X
of
J
. Then
J
is integrable if and only if
\bigl[\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{1,0}X),\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{1,0}X)\bigr]\subseteq\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{1,0}X)\subseteq\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}TX\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
. Dual to (
3.2
), we have a decomposition

{}^{b}T^{*}X\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}={}^{b}T^{*\,1,0}X\oplus{}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X,

(3.3)

where
{}^{b}T^{*\,1,0}X
is the complex vector bundle dual to
{}^{b}T^{1,0}X
, and
{}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X
is dual to
{}^{b}T^{0,1}X
. Let
f:X\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
be smooth, so that
{}^{b}{\rm d}f\in\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{*}X\otimes_{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
. Write
{}^{b}{\rm d}f={}^{b}\partial f\oplus{}^{b}\bar{\partial}f
in the splitting (
3.3
), where
{}^{b}\partial f
is the component in
{}^{b}T^{*\,1,0}X
and
{}^{b}\bar{\partial}f
the component in
{}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X
. We call
f

holomorphic
if
{}^{b}\bar{\partial}f=0
.

As in standard complex geometry,
{}^{b}\bar{\partial}
naturally extends to maps

{}^{b}\bar{\partial}\colon\Gamma^{\infty}\bigl(\Lambda^{r}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X)\bigr)\longrightarrow\Gamma^{\infty}\bigl(\Lambda^{r+1}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X)\bigr),

for
r=0,1,\ldots,n-1
, satisfying the graded Leibniz rule:

{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(\alpha\wedge\beta)={}^{b}\bar{\partial}\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^{k}\alpha\wedge{}^{b}\bar{\partial}\beta\,,

for
\alpha\in\Gamma^{\infty}\bigl(\Lambda^{k}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X)\bigr)
and
\beta\in\Gamma^{\infty}\bigl(X,\Lambda^{l}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X)\bigr)
for
k,l\geqslant 0
with
k+l<n
. Then
J
is integrable if and only if
{}^{b}\bar{\partial}^{2}={}^{b}\bar{\partial}\circ{}^{b}\bar{\partial}=0
.

Let
(X,J_{X}),(Y,J_{Y})
be b-complex manifolds with g-corners and
f:X\rightarrow Y
be an interior map. We call
f

holomorphic
if for all
x\in X
with
y=f(x)\in Y
, so that
{}^{b}{\rm d}f|_{x}\colon{}^{b}T_{x}X\rightarrow{}^{b}T_{y}Y
is a linear map, we have

{}^{b}{\rm d}f|_{x}\circ J_{X}|_{x}=J_{Y}|_{y}\circ{}^{b}{\rm d}f|_{x}.

Remark 3.2
.

(a)
For manifolds with boundary, which are equivalent to manifolds with (g-)corners
X
with
S^{k}(X)=\emptyset
for
k>1
, b-(almost) complex structures were defined and studied by Mendoza
[
16
,
17
]
. See also Melrose
[
14
, §6.3]
who discusses Riemann surfaces with boundary.

(b)
Definition
3.1
(ii) ensures good behaviour of b-(almost) complex manifolds at their corner strata. We have decided to include it from the beginning, as it seems fundamental to the theory, and is necessary for Theorems
3.6
–
3.7
below. It does not appear in
[
16
,
17
,
14
]
, as it is trivial for manifolds with boundary.

Here is an important example.

Example 3.3
.

Let
P
be a weakly toric monoid, of rank
n
. Then Example
2.14
defines an
n

, where if

then

is a manifold with corners. As in §

, the b-tangent bundle

is canonically trivial with fibre
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\cong{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}
.

We will define a b-complex structure on the product

, which has real dimension

. Since
{}^{b}T\bigl(X_{P}\times\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\bigr)

\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\oplus\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
, we define

in matrix form by

J=\begin{pmatrix}0&&-{\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits}\\
\\
{\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits}&&0\end{pmatrix}\,:\,\,\begin{matrix}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\\
\oplus\\
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\end{matrix}\,\longrightarrow\,\begin{matrix}\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\\
\oplus\\
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}).\!\end{matrix}

(3.4)

As in Definition
2.18
, the trivialization
{}^{b}TX_{P}\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
gives b-vector fields

on

for

with
[v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}]=0
for all

. Write

for the vector field on the vector space
\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
corresponding to translation in direction
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
. Then
[w_{\alpha},w_{\beta}]=0
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})
. Write
v_{\alpha}^{\prime},w_{\alpha}^{\prime}
for the vector fields on

obtained by lifting

from the factors

. Then
[v^{\prime}_{\alpha},v^{\prime}_{\beta}]=[v^{\prime}_{\alpha},w^{\prime}_{\beta}]=[w^{\prime}_{\alpha},w^{\prime}_{\beta}]=0
for all

J(v_{\alpha}^{\prime})=w_{\alpha}^{\prime},\quad J(w_{\alpha}^{\prime})=-v_{\alpha}^{\prime}\quad\text{for $\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})$.}

In the expression (
3.1
) for the Nijenhuis tensor

, we find that

if

are
v^{\prime}_{\alpha},v^{\prime}_{\beta},w^{\prime}_{\alpha}
or
w^{\prime}_{\beta}
, as all four terms on the right hand side are zero. Hence

, and

is integrable. Thus

is a b-complex manifold. We call

the

on

and also write it as

.

As in Definition
2.10
if
p\in P
we have a smooth function
\lambda_{p}:X_{P}\rightarrow[0,\infty)\subset{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
. Define
\theta_{p}:\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}
by
\theta_{p}(\alpha)=\alpha(p)
. Combine these to give a function

\lambda_{p}e^{i\theta_{p}}=(\lambda_{p}\circ\Pi_{X_{P}})\cdot e^{i\theta_{p}\circ\Pi_{\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P,{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})}}:X_{P}\times\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(P^{\mathrm{gp}},{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}})\longrightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}},

where

.

On

we have

for

by Definition

, and on

. Thus lifting to

and the

-schemes and log smooth log complex analytic spaces.

3.2
Newlander–Nirenberg type theorems for

b-complex manifolds with (g-)corners

Mendoza
[
17

proves the following:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(X,J)
be a b-complex manifold with boundary of real dimension
2n
for
n\geqslant 1,
and suppose
x\in\partial X=S^{1}(X).
Then there exist local coordinates
(\lambda,\theta,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{2n-1}
defined on an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
in
X,
where
\lambda
takes values in
[0,\infty),
with
\partial X
locally defined by
\lambda=0
and
\lambda(x)=0,
such that

{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(\lambda e^{i\theta}),{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(x_{2}+iy_{2}),\ldots,{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(x_{n}+iy_{n})\in I_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\cdot\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X|_{U}),

(3.5)

where
I_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}
is the ideal of smooth functions
U\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
vanishing to infinite order along the boundary hypersurface
\lambda=0
. On
\partial X\cap U,
the complex
1
-forms in
(
3.5
)
are a basis of sections of
{}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}X|_{\partial X\cap U}
.

Here
\lambda e^{i\theta},x_{2}+iy_{2},\ldots,x_{n}+iy_{n}
may not be holomorphic, but they are at least ‘holomorphic to infinite order at
\partial X
’. We can regard Theorem
3.4
as a
formal Newlander–Nirenberg Theorem
, as it basically says that the
formal completion
of
X
along
\partial X=S^{1}(X)
is locally standard.

Remark 3.5
.

Barron–Francis
[
2
, Th. 1.2]
claim a stronger result, that one can choose coordinates with
{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(\lambda e^{i\theta})={}^{b}\bar{\partial}(x_{2}+iy_{2})=\cdots={}^{b}\bar{\partial}(x_{n}+iy_{n})=0
, that is,
\lambda e^{i\theta},\ldots,x_{n}+iy_{n}
are holomorphic functions on
U
. Unfortunately there is a mistake in the proof on
[
2
, p. 12]
(in arXiv version 1, 2023), in the sentence ‘…there is no harm in assuming the deformation terms to be
\tilde{\sigma}
-invariant’. Barron and Francis have told the authors they hope to post a corrected version.

Here is our first main result, proved in §
3.3
. It shows that on each depth stratum

of a b-complex manifold with (g-)corners

, the action of

on the Lie algebroid

has a standard local model.

(X,J)

of

satisfying

(i)

Using the notation of
(
2.3
)
and
(
2.12
),

are a basis of sections of

The following lemma describes the successive quotients
I^{N}/I^{N+1}
explicitly.

Lemma 3.11
.

For any
N\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}
, the following holds:

(i)

There is an isomorphism

I^{N}/I^{N+1}\cong\bigoplus_{q\in Q_{N}\setminus Q_{N+1}}C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})\mu^{\star}_{q},

(3.32)

of free
C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
-modules of finite rank, where
Q_{N}\setminus Q_{N+1}
is finite.

(ii)

Given
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in C^{\infty}(W,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
and distinct
q_{1},\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak q_{l}\allowbreak\in Q_{N}\setminus Q_{N+1}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mu^{\star}_{q_{i}}f_{i}\in I^{N+1},
then
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in I
.

Proof.

For (i),
Q_{N}\setminus Q_{N+1}
is finite as in Definition
3.9
, and as
C^{\infty}(W,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})/I\cong C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
both sides of (
3.32
) are
C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
-modules, so the right hand side of (
3.32
) is a finite rank
C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
-module. Write

Q_{1}\setminus Q_{2}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\}\,.

As in Definition
3.9
,
\phi^{\star}:U_{1}^{\star}\times U_{2}^{\star}\rightarrow W
is a diffeomorphism, where
\delta_{0}\in U_{1}^{\star}\subseteq X_{Q}
and
U_{2}^{\star}\subset{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{2n-k}
are open. Apply Proposition
2.11
to
Q
with generators
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
and a generating set of relations for
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}
of the form

a_{1}^{j}q_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}^{j}q_{m}=b_{1}^{j}q_{1}+\cdots+b_{m}^{j}q_{m}\quad\text{in $Q$ for $j=1,\ldots,l,$}

where
a_{i}^{j},b_{i}^{j}\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,l
. Then
\lambda_{q_{1}}\times\cdots\times\lambda_{q_{m}}:X_{Q}\hookrightarrow[0,\infty)^{m}
identifies

with

in (

). Pick an open set

such that
Y\cap X_{Q}^{\prime}=\lambda_{q_{1}}\times\cdots\times\lambda_{q_{m}}(U_{1}^{\star})
. Then

C^{\infty}(W,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})\cong C^{\infty}(Y\times U_{2}^{\star})/K,

(3.33)

where
K
is the ideal in

with
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
coordinates on

. Write

for the ideal of functions vanishing on

. Then the isomorphism (
3.33
) identifies

, and so it identifies

, and

I^{N}/I^{N+1}\cong\frac{(\tilde{I}^{N}+K)/K}{(\tilde{I}^{N+1}+K)/K}\cong\frac{\tilde{I}^{N}+K}{\tilde{I}^{N+1}+K}\,\ \cong\frac{\tilde{I}^{N}}{\tilde{I}^{N+1}+(K\cap\tilde{I}^{N})}.

So, we have a surjective map

(3.34)

As smooth functions on

have Taylor expansions at
(0,\ldots,0)
, we can identify the quotient
\tilde{I}^{N}/\tilde{I}^{N+1}
with the set of homogeneous polynomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
of total degree
N
with coefficients in
C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
, as
V\cong U_{2}^{\star}

\tilde{I}^{N}/\tilde{I}^{N+1}\cong\bigoplus_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{m}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=N}C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a_{i}}.

(3.35)

With this identification, the map
\varphi:\tilde{I}^{N}/\tilde{I}^{N+1}\twoheadrightarrow I^{N}/I^{N+1}
is defined by

\varphi\bigl(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a_{i}}\bigr)=\mu^{\star}_{\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}q_{i}}+I^{N+1}.

We can describe the kernel of
\varphi
in (
3.34
) in terms of the presentation (
3.35
) as follows. Firstly, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{m}

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=N

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}q_{i}\in Q_{N+1}
then
\varphi(\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a_{i}})=0
. Secondly, if
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}),(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{m}

then
\varphi(\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a_{i}})=\varphi(\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a^{\prime}_{i}})
. Thus, quotienting (
3.35
) by the kernel of
\varphi
is equivalent to deleting those
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
in the sum with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}q_{i}\in Q_{N+1}
, and identifying those
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}),(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m})\in{\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}^{m}
in the sum with

, and then we can replace

by
\mu^{\star}_{q}
. Part (i) of the lemma follows.

For part (ii), given
f_{1},\ldots,f_{l}\in C^{\infty}(W,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
and distinct
q_{1},\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak q_{l}\allowbreak\in Q_{N}\setminus Q_{N+1}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mu^{\star}_{q_{i}}f_{i}\in I^{N+1},
we have
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mu^{\star}_{q_{i}}f_{i}=0
in
I^{N}/I^{N+1}
. By (i),
\mu^{\star}_{q_{i}}
for

are linearly independent over
C^{\infty}(V,{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}})
in
I^{N}/I^{N+1}
, so
f_{i}|_{V}=0
, that is,
f_{i}\in I
, for all
1\leq i\leq l
. This completes the proof.

Here is a version of Borel’s Theorem for manifolds with g-corners.

Proposition 3.12
.

For every formal power series

along

. That is,

for all

.

Proof.

In the proof of Lemma
3.11
we explained how to relate smooth functions such as

on

\mu_{q}=\mu_{q}^{\star}\cdot e^{\sum_{a=1}^{k}\bar{v}_{a}(q)\mathop{\rm Re}g_{a}},\quad\theta_{q}=\theta_{q}^{\star}+\sum_{a=1}^{k}\bar{v}_{a}(q)\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits g_{a},\quad z_{j}=z_{j}^{\star}+h_{j}.

(3.46)

Then
\mu_{q+q^{\prime}}=\mu_{q}\cdot\mu_{q^{\prime}}
and
\theta_{q+q^{\prime}}=\theta_{q}+\theta_{q^{\prime}}
for all
q,q^{\prime}\in Q
. We have
\mu_{q}e^{i\theta}=\mu^{\star}_{q}e^{i\theta^{\star}_{q}}\cdot e^{\sum_{a=1}^{k}\bar{v}_{a}(q)g_{a}}
.

From (
3.27
), (
3.45
) and (
3.46
) we deduce that for
q\in Q
and
j=k+1,\ldots,n

{}^{b}\bar{\partial}(\mu_{q}e^{i\theta_{q}}),{}^{b}\bar{\partial}z_{j}\in I^{\infty}\cdot\Gamma^{\infty}({}^{b}T^{*\,0,1}W).

(3.47)

That is,
\mu_{q}e^{i\theta_{q}}
and
z_{j}
are ‘
J
-holomorphic to infinite order in
I
’. We regard
\mu_{q},\theta_{q},z_{j}
as ‘corrected’ versions of
\mu^{\star}_{q},\theta^{\star}_{q},z^{\star}_{j}
.

Recall that
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}:W\rightarrow X_{Q}\times({\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k})
is a diffeomorphism with the open subset
U_{1}^{\star}\times U_{2}^{\star}\subset X_{Q}\times({\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k})
, where the
U_{1}^{\star}
-component
W\rightarrow X_{Q}
of
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}
is determined by the maps
\mu^{\star}_{q}:W\rightarrow[0,\infty)
for
q\in Q
with
\mu^{\star}_{q+q^{\prime}}=\mu^{\star}_{q}\cdot\mu^{\star}_{q^{\prime}}
, and the
U_{2}^{\star}
-component
W\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k}
of
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}
is
(\theta_{1}^{\star},\ldots,\theta_{k}^{\star},z_{k+1}^{\star},\ldots,z_{n}^{\star})
. The corrected versions
\mu_{q},\theta_{q},z_{j}
also define a smooth map
\phi^{-1}:W\rightarrow X_{Q}\times({\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k})
given on the
X_{Q}
component by the maps
\mu_{q}:W\rightarrow[0,\infty)
for
q\in Q
with
\mu_{q+q^{\prime}}=\mu_{q}\cdot\mu_{q^{\prime}}
and on the
{\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k}
component by
(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{k},z_{k+1},\ldots,z_{n})
.

We think of
\phi^{-1}
as a deformation of
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}
which agrees with
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}
to leading order at
V\subset W
, since
g_{a}|_{V}=h_{j}|_{V}=0
, so in particular
\phi^{-1}|_{V}=(\phi^{\star})^{-1}|_{V}
and
{}^{b}T(\phi^{-1})|_{V}={}^{b}T((\phi^{\star})^{-1})|_{V}
. Since
(\phi^{\star})^{-1}
is a diffeomorphism from
W
to
U_{1}^{\star}\times U_{2}^{\star}\subset X_{Q}\times({\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k})
, it follows that
\phi^{-1}
is also a diffeomorphism near
V
in
W
. Thus, making
V,W
smaller if necessary, we can suppose that
U=\phi^{-1}(W)
is open in
X_{Q}\times({\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}}^{k}\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}^{n-k})=X_{P}
and
\phi^{-1}:W\rightarrow U
is a diffeomorphism, with an inverse
\phi:U\rightarrow W
which is a diffeomorphism with an open set
W\subseteq X
. Hence
(P,U,\phi)
is a g-chart on
X
, as in Theorem
3.7
.

The construction of
\phi
implies that if
p\in P
corresponds to
(q,(a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n}))
under
P=Q\times{\mathbin{\mathbb{Z}}}^{n-k}
then
\phi_{*}(\lambda_{p}e^{i\theta_{p}}):W\rightarrow{\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}}
in Theorem
3.7
is

\phi_{*}(\lambda_{p}e^{i\theta_{p}})=\mu_{q}e^{i\theta_{q}}\cdot e^{\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}a_{j}z_{j}}
. Therefore equation (
3.9
) follows from (
3.47
). This completes the proof of Theorem
3.7
.
