Morse-Novikov theory for links
L. Chen, H. Endo, A. Pajitnov

Abstract
. For a compact 3-manifold W. Thurston introduced a norm on the first cohomology group of the manifold. The unit ball 𝑩 B of this norm is a polyhedron and the set of cohomology classes that are representable by fibrations over a circle is a union of cones on some of the open faces of 𝑩 B . In the present paper we study the fibred faces of the Thurston polyhedra of exteriors of links in 𝑺 𝟑 S^{3} . Our approach is based on the non-abelian Novikov homology associated with the universal covering of the exterior of the link. We prove in particular that for a 2-bridge 2-component link 𝑳 L a cohomology class 𝝃 ∈ 𝑯 𝟏 ​ ( 𝑬 ​ ( 𝑳 ) ) \xi\in H^{1}(E(L)) can be represented by a fibration over a circle if and only if its 2-variable Alexander polynomial is 𝝃 \xi -monic. We compute the Morse-Novikov numbers for a majority of 2-component prime links with number of crossings ⩽ 𝟖 \leqslant 8 .

Introduction

In
[
26
]

W. Thurston introduced a norm on the cohomology
H^{1}(M)

of any compact 3-manifold
M

has many applications in geometry and topology, in particular to
study of fibrations of 3-manifolds over a circle.
Namely, the unit ball

of Thurston’s norm is a polyhedron
and the set of deRham cohomology classes

\xi\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})

representable by
a non-singular 1-form
\omega

is the union of cones on some of
the open faces of
B
.

In the present paper we study the fibred faces of Thurston polyhedron
for complements of links in
S^{3}
.
We use the fibering obstructions
provided by Morse-Novikov theory.

Let
M
be a compact manifold, and
\xi\in H^{1}(M)\approx[M,S^{1}]

a cohomology class. Novikov theory provides obstructions for
\xi
to
be represented by a
C^{\infty}
fibration over a circle (such class will
be called

with structure group

such that
p^{*}(\xi)
is
homologous to

H_{*}(M,\xi)=H_{*}({\widehat{\Lambda}}_{\xi}\underset{\Lambda}{\otimes}C_{*}(\bar{M}))

\xi

Vanishing of this homology is a necessary condition for

In general vanishing of these obstructions is not sufficient
for fibering. However in dimension

a profound theorem due to R. Bieri, W. Neumann and R. Strebel

[

[

In the present paper we apply this result to the case when

of a link

in
S^{3}
.
In Section 1 we deal with the case of 2-bridge 2-component links. We prove that

the link in

of

In Section 2 we consider connected sums of links.

2.
Novikov rings and Novikov homology

In this section we recall the basic facts from Novikov theory.
Let
G
be a group; we denote by
\Lambda
the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}G
.
Let
\xi:G\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a homomorphism.
Intuitively, the Novikov completion
{\widehat{\Lambda}}_{\xi}

of the ring
\Lambda
consists of some special infinite linear combinations
of the elements of
G
, namely the linear combinations that are infinite
in the direction of decreasing of
\xi
. To give a precise definition
let
\widehat{\widehat{\Lambda}}
be the set of all formal linear combinations
(infinite in general)

\lambda=\sum_{g\in G}n_{g}g,\ n_{g}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For
\lambda\in\widehat{\widehat{\Lambda}}
put
\text{\rm supp }\lambda=\{g~|~n_{g}\not=0\}
.
For
C\in{\mathbb{R}}
put

[\lambda]_{\xi,C}=\{g\in\text{\rm supp }\lambda~|~\xi(g)\geqslant C\}.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
19
]
,
[
25
]
)
The set

(5)

\{\lambda\in\widehat{\widehat{\Lambda}}~|~\forall C{\rm\ \ the\ \ set\ \ }[\lambda]_{\xi,C}{\rm\ \ is\ \ finite\ \ }\}

is called
Novikov ring

and denoted by
{\widehat{\Lambda}}_{\xi}
.

When
G={\mathbb{Z}}
and
\xi:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is the identity homomorphism
the ring
{\widehat{\Lambda}}_{\xi}
is isomorphic to the Laurent power series ring in one variable with integer coefficients having only finite number of negative powers of
t
.

Let
X
be a connected space, and
\xi\in H^{1}(X)
.
Let
{\mathscr{C}}
be a regular covering

(
[

)
.

Let

\xi\in H^{1}(M)

.

A Morse function on a compact manifold has necessarily a maximum.
This is not the case for a Morse map to a circle belonging to
a non-zero cohomology class.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let

M
be a connected compact manifold with
\partial M\not=\varnothing
,
and
\xi
be a regular class in

.
Then

Proof.
Pick a minimal Morse function
f
on
(M;\partial M)
.
Pick any
C^{\infty}
function

belonging to
\xi

such that

is a fibration.
We can assume that
the function
g
is constant on some neighbourhood

of
S(f)
.
Consider the closed 1-form
\omega_{C}=dg+Cdf
where
C>0
.
Observe that
\omega
equals
Cdf
in

, and outside

the minimum of
\text{\rm grad}(Cdf)
becomes
arbitrarily large with

. Therefore
for

\omega_{C}
has no zeeros in

and we have

m_{i}(\omega_{C})=m_{i}(f)

Fix such a number

The cohomology class of

\omega_{C}=dh

\xi

for every

.
The Morse map

has one critical point of index

, and this point can be cancelled. The proof of the next two lemmas is done by standard rearrangement and cancellation arguments, similarly to
Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 of
[

]
, and will be omitted.

Lemma 5.8
.

There is a regular Morse map

.

Assume that

8.
The case of closed 1-forms

Morse-Novikov theory has a natural generalization to the case
of Morse forms, that is, closed 1-forms with non-degenerate zeros.
The main aim of this section are Theorems
8.4
and
5.12

which generalize Theorems
5.7
and
8.5

to the case of Morse forms. The generalization of other results
of the previous sections is beyond the scope of the present paper.

We need some more definitions.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
M
be a compact manifold with boundary
\partial M
.
A closed 1-form
\omega
on
M
is called
a Morse form

if all its zeros are non-degenerate and belong to
M\setminus\partial M
,
and the restriction of
\omega
to
\partial M
is a non-singular 1-form
on
\partial M
.

For any Morse map
f:M\to S^{1}
the differential
df
is a Morse form.
Such forms will be caled
integral
.
A Morse form
\omega
is called
rational
, if
\omega=C\eta

where
C\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\eta
is an integral form.
It is easy to see that a Morse form is integral, resp. rational
if and only if its deRham cohomology class

[\omega]

is in the image
of

H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
, resp.
H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
.

For a Morse form
\omega
we denote by
m_{i}(\omega)
the number of zeros of
\omega
of index
i
, and by
m(\omega)
the number of all its zeros.

A class
\xi\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
is called
regular
if its restriction to
\partial M

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(M)
be a Morse form,
belonging to a regular class
\xi\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})\approx{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

Then in every neighbourhood of
\xi\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})

there is a regular rational cohomology class
\eta\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
, such that
some integer multiple
N\eta
is representable by a Morse
map

\phi:M\to S^{1}

having the same Morse numbers as
\omega
.

Proof.
Choose a basis
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}

in the group

and represent
each

by a closed 1-form

having the following properties:

1)

The restriction of
\alpha_{i}
to
\partial M
is non-singular.

2)

The form
\alpha_{i}
equals
0
in some neighbourhood
U
of
S(\omega)
.

For a vector
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

consider a 1-form

\lambda(x)=\omega+\sum_{i}x_{i}\alpha_{i}.

For every
x
the 1-form
\lambda(x)
equals
\omega

in
U
.
If

\lambda(x)
has no zeros in

\lambda(x)~|~\partial M
has no zeros either. Therefore
\lambda(x)

we have

for every

sufficiently small. Write
\xi=\sum_{i}\xi_{i}a_{i}

\hfill\square
