The stable set of associated primes of a complementary edge ideal
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. We explicitly determine the associated primes of every power of a complementary edge ideal, prove that they satisfy the persistence property, and compute the v-function. In the course of the proofs, we completely describe the homological properties of all powers of squarefree monomial ideals generated in degrees large relative to the number of variables defining them.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13D02, 13C05, 13A02; Secondary 05E40

Introduction

In 1979, Brodmann
[
4
]
proved that the set of associated primes
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
of the powers of an ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring eventually becomes stable: there exists an integer
k_{0}>0
such that
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k_{0}})
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. The smallest integer
k_{0}
for which this property holds is called the
index of ass stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\operatorname{astab}(I)
. Similarly, the set
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k_{0}})
is called the
stable set of associated primes
of
I
, and it is denoted by
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
, and let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then, the associated primes of
I
are monomial prime ideals. Let
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. A monomial prime ideal is an ideal of the form
P_{F}=(x_{i}:\ i\in F)
for a subset
F\subset[n]
. A very general and effective criterion for determining when
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
was given in
[
1
,
2
]
, see also
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
. Since there are only finitely many subsets of
[n]
, determining
\operatorname{astab}(I)
and
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
for a monomial ideal
I\subset S
are feasible problems to address.

In general, if
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
it is not necessarily true that
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k+1})
. We say that
I
satisfies the
persistence property
if

\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I^{3})\subset\cdots.

In this case
\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
. Not all monomial ideals satisfy the persistence property. On the other hand, Martínez-Bernal, Morey, and Villarreal proved that squarefree monomial ideals generated in degree 2 satisfy the persistence property, see
[
28
, Theorem 2.15]
. These ideals are known as
edge ideals

[
31
]
. Given a finite simple graph
G
on the vertex set
V(G)=[n]
with edge set
E(G)
, the
edge ideal
of
G
is the squarefree monomial ideal of
S
defined as

I(G)\ =\ (x_{i}x_{j}:\ \{i,j\}\in E(G)).

Any squarefree monomial ideal generated in degree 2 is an edge ideal. In
[
26
]
, Lam and Viet Trung described the set
\operatorname{Ass}(I(G)^{k})
for any graph
G
and all
k\geq 1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal equigenerated in degree
n-2
. As noted in
[
15
,
25
]
,
I
can be seen as the
complementary edge ideal

I_{c}(G)\ =((x_{1}\cdots x_{n})/(x_{i}x_{j}):\ \{i,j\}\in E(G))

of a finite simple graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
. There is a rich interplay between the combinatorics of
G
and the algebraic properties of
I_{c}(G)
as highlighted by the results in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
25
,
27
]
.

Given a finite simple graph
G
and
i\in V(G)
, let
\deg_{G}(i)=|\{j:\ \{i,j\}\in E(G)\}|
. Then
i\in V(G)
is an isolated vertex if and only if
\deg_{G}(i)=0
. Denote by
\widetilde{b}(G)
the number of bipartite connected components of
G
having more than one vertex. We denote by
G|_{F}
the induced subgraph of
G
on
F\subset[n]
. Inspired by the remarkable behaviour of the set
\operatorname{Ass}(I(G)^{k})
(see
[
26
,
28
]
), in this paper we prove:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
[n]
with
n\geq 3
. Then
I_{c}(G)
satisfies the persistence property:

\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{2})\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{3})\subset\cdots.

Moreover,

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))=\{P_{\{i\}}:\ \deg_{G}(i)=0\}\cup\{P_{F}:\ |F|>1,\,\widetilde{b}(G|_{F})=0\}.

and
\operatorname{astab}(I_{c}(G))\leq n-2
.

To prove this result, we adopt the following strategy. Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal with minimal monomial generating set
\mathcal{G}(I)
. Given
F\subset[n]
non-empty, we put
S_{F}=K[x_{i}:i\in F]
. We define the
K
-linear map
\varphi_{F}:S\rightarrow S_{F}
by setting
\varphi_{F}(x_{i})=x_{i}
for
i\in F
, and
\varphi_{F}(x_{j})=1
otherwise. The monomial ideal
I(P_{F})=\varphi_{F}(I)\subset S_{F}
is called the
monomial localization
of
I
at
P_{F}
. Concretely,
I(P_{F})
is the monomial ideal of
S_{F}
obtained by replacing in
I
all variables
x_{j}
by
1
for all
j\notin F
.

We have
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
if and only if
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})=0
. In view of this fact, Theorem
A
will be proved as a consequence of the following result. It is well-known to the experts (see
[
24
]
), but for the sake of record, we state it here explicitly.

Theorem B
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of monomial ideals of
S
satisfying the following two properties.

(a)

I(P_{F})\in\mathcal{F}
, for all
I\in\mathcal{F}
and all
F\subset[n]
.

(b)

The function
k\mapsto\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}
is non-increasing, for all
I\in\mathcal{F}
.

Then, all ideals
I\in\mathcal{F}
satisfy the persistence property:

\operatorname{Ass}(I)\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I^{2})\subset\operatorname{Ass}(I^{3})\subset\cdots.

Furthermore

\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\{P_{F}:\ \operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})^{k}=0\ \text{for some}\ k\}.

Using this result, one can easily prove that polymatroidal ideals satisfy the persistence property, see
[
24
, Proposition 2.3]
.

In order to prove Theorem
A
, we employ Theorem
B
together with the following Theorem
C
. This theorem significantly extends several results proved in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

To state this result, we need more notation.

Given a finite simple graph
G
, we denote by
c(G)
the number of connected components of
G
having more than one vertex.

Let
I\subset S
be any ideal. Brodmann
[
5
]
proved that the function
k\mapsto\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}
is eventually constant. The least integer
k_{*}>0
such that
\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k_{*}}
for all
k\geq k_{*}
is called the
index of depth stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\operatorname{dstab}(I)
.

Given a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
and an integer
j\geq 0
, we denote by
I_{\langle j\rangle}
the homogeneous ideal generated by all homogeneous polynomials of degree
j
belonging to
I
. We say that
I
is componentwise linear if
I_{\langle j\rangle}
has linear resolution for all
j\geq 0
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. We say that
I
has
linear quotients
if there exists an order
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
of
\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
(u_{1},\dots,u_{j-1}):u_{j}
is generated by variables, for all
j=1,\dots,m
. A monomial ideal with linear quotients is componentwise linear
[
22
, Theorem 8.2.15]
but the converse is not necessarily true.

Now, we are in a position to state Theorem
C
.

Theorem C
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degrees
\geq n-2
. Then, the following statements hold.

(a)

The function
k\mapsto\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}
is non-increasing.

(b)

\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq n-1
.

(c)

(i)

If
I
is equigenerated in degree
d\in\{n-1,n\}
, then

\operatorname{reg}I^{k}\ =\ dk,\quad\textit{for all}\ k\geq 1.

(ii)

If
I
is equigenerated in degree
n-2
, let
G
be the unique simple graph on
[n]
such that
I=I_{c}(G)
, and put
c=c(G)
. Then

\operatorname{reg}I^{k}\ =\ \begin{cases}(n-1)k&\textit{for}\ 1\leq k\leq c-2,\\
(n-2)k+c-1&\textit{for}\ k\geq c-1.\end{cases}

(iii)

If
I
is minimally generated in degrees
n-2
and
n-1
, then

\operatorname{reg}I^{k}\ =\ (n-1)k,\quad\textup{for all}\ k\geq 1.

(d)

The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

I^{k}
is componentwise linear for all
k\geq 1
.

(iii)

Either
I_{\langle n-2\rangle}=0
or
I_{\langle n-2\rangle}=I_{c}(G)\neq 0
and
c(G)=1
.

(e)

The graded Betti numbers of
I^{k}
do not depend on
K
, for all
k\geq 1
.

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section
1
we give the proof of Theorem
B
and as a consequence we show that all squarefree monomial ideals of
S
generated in degrees
\geq n-2
satisfy the persistence property. Section
2
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
C
. Next, in Section
3
, we prove Theorem
A
.

Section
4
contains some applications of Theorem
A
. In Corollary
4.1
, we give a simple proof of the main result of
[
30
]
. Namely, we classify the complementary edge ideals whose ordinary and symbolic powers coincide. In Theorem
4.2
, we compute the
\operatorname{v}
-function of any complementary edge ideal.

1.
Criteria for the persistence property

In this short section, we prove Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Let
I\in\mathcal{F}
and
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}(I^{k})
for some
F\subset[n]
and
k\geq 1
. Then
P_{F}
is the maximal ideal of
S_{F}
and
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S_{F}}(I^{k}(P_{F}))=\operatorname{Ass}_{S_{F}}(I(P_{F})^{k})
. This is equivalent to the fact that
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})^{k}=0
. By property (a),
I(P_{F})\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that
\operatorname{depth}S/I(P_{F})^{k}=\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})^{k}+n-|F|
. By property (b), the depth function
k\mapsto\operatorname{depth}S/I(P_{F})^{k}
is non-increasing. It follows that
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})^{k}
is non-increasing too. Hence,
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I(P_{F})^{\ell}=0
, and so
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S_{F}}(I(P_{F})^{\ell})=\operatorname{Ass}_{S_{F}}(I^{\ell}(P_{F}))
, for all
\ell\geq k
. Therefore
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}(I^{\ell})
for all
\ell\geq k
, and so
I
satisfies the persistence property. This argument also proves the second assertion.

∎

Theorem
B
can be used to show that some classes of monomial ideals satisfy the persistence property. For instance, polymatroidal ideals, see
[
24
, Proposition 2.3]
.

As the main new application of Theorem
B
, we show

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degrees
\geq n-2
. Then
I
satisfies the persistence property.

Proof.

Recall that the
support
of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is defined as the set
\operatorname{supp}(I)=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\operatorname{supp}(u)
, where the support of a monomial
u\in S
is defined as the set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{i:\ x_{i}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
.

Let us define the family

\mathcal{F}\ =\ \bigg\{I\subset S\,\,\bigg|\,\,\begin{subarray}{c}\displaystyle I\ \textup{is a squarefree monomial ideal}\\[3.0pt]
\displaystyle\textup{generated in degrees}\ \geq\ |\operatorname{supp}(I)|-2\end{subarray}\,\bigg\}.

In view of Theorem
B
, our assertion follows once we show that the properties (a) and (b) in Theorem
B
are satisfied by the family
\mathcal{F}
.

For a non-empty subset
A\subset[n]
, we put
{\bf x}_{A}=\prod_{i\in A}x_{i}
. Let
I\in\mathcal{F}
and put
G=\operatorname{supp}(I)\subset[n]
. Let
i,j\in G
be distinct elements. Let
F\subset[n]
. We note that

\varphi_{F}({\bf x}_{G})\ =\ {\bf x}_{G\cap F},\quad\quad\quad\varphi_{F}({\bf x}_{G\setminus\{i\}})\ =\ \begin{cases}\,{\bf x}_{G\cap F}&\textup{if}\ i\notin G,\\
\,{\bf x}_{(G\cap F)\setminus\{i\}}&\textup{if}\ i\in G,\end{cases}

\varphi_{F}({\bf x}_{G\setminus\{i,j\}})\ =\ \begin{cases}\,{\bf x}_{G\cap F}&\textup{if}\ i,j\notin G,\\
\,{\bf x}_{(G\cap F)\setminus\{i\}}&\textup{if}\ i\in G,\,j\notin G,\\
\,{\bf x}_{(G\cap F)\setminus\{j\}}&\textup{if}\ i\notin G,\,j\in G,\\
\,{\bf x}_{(G\cap F)\setminus\{i,j\}}&\textup{if}\ i,j\in G.\end{cases}

Since
\operatorname{supp}(I(P_{F}))\subset\operatorname{supp}(I)\cap F=G\cap F
, the above computations show that
I(P_{F})
is a squarefree monomial ideal generated in degrees
\geq|G\cap F|-2\geq|\operatorname{supp}(I(P_{F}))|-2
. Hence
I(P_{F})\in\mathcal{F}
and the property (a) is satisfied.

The property (b) follows from statement (a) of Theorem
C
.

∎

2.
Squarefree monomial ideals generated in big degrees

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
with
n\geq 3
. Our goal is to prove Theorem
C
. To this end, note that for a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S
generated in degrees
\geq n-2
only one of the following possibilities occurs:

(i)

I
is generated in degree
n-2
, in which case it is a complementary edge ideal,

(ii)

I
is generated in degree
d\in\{n-1,n\}
in which case it is a matroidal ideal,

(iii)

I
is minimally generated in two degrees
n-2
and
n-1
.

In the case (i), all the claims of Theorem
C
follow from the results in
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
. The case (ii) is trivial as well, because then
I
is a matroidal ideal, see
[
23
,
24
]
. Only the case (iii) remains to be addressed. This is accomplished next.

Given a monomial ideal
I\subset S
and
j\geq 0
, let
\mu_{j}(I)=|\{u\in\mathcal{G}(I):\deg(u)=j\}|
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal minimally generated in two degrees
n-2
and
n-1
. Let
J=I_{\langle n-2\rangle}
. The following statements hold.

(a)

\operatorname{reg}I^{k}=(n-1)k
for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

The function
k\mapsto\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}
is non-increasing.

(c)

The graded Betti numbers of
I^{k}
do not depend on
K
, for all
k\geq 1
.

(d)

\operatorname{depth}S/I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}S/J^{k}-\mu_{n-1}(I)
for all
k\geq\operatorname{dstab}(J)+\mu_{n-1}(I)
.

(e)

\operatorname{dstab}(I)\leq n-2
.

(f)

The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

I^{k}
has linear quotients for all
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

I^{k}
is componentwise linear for all
k\geq 1
.

(iii)

J=I_{c}(G)
with
c(G)=1
.

Let
I,I_{1},I_{2}\subset S
be monomial ideals such that
\mathcal{G}(I)=\mathcal{G}(I_{1})\sqcup\mathcal{G}(I_{2})
. Following
[
21
, Definition 1.1]
, we say that
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
is a
Betti splitting
if

\beta_{i,j}(I)\ =\ \beta_{i,j}(I_{1})+\beta_{i,j}(I_{2})+\beta_{i-1,j}(I_{1}\cap I_{2}),\ \ \ \text{for all}\ i,j\geq 0.

(1)

In this case, by
[
21
, Corollary 2.2]
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}S/I\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\operatorname{depth}S/I_{1},\,\operatorname{depth}S/I_{2},\,\operatorname{depth}S/(I_{1}\cap I_{2})-1\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}I\

\displaystyle=\ \max\{\operatorname{reg}I_{1},\,\operatorname{reg}I_{2},\,\operatorname{reg}I_{1}\cap I_{2}-1\}.

Consider the natural short exact sequence
0\rightarrow I_{1}\cap I_{2}\rightarrow I_{1}\oplus I_{2}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0
. By
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
, equation (
1
) holds, that is
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
is a Betti splitting, if and only if, the induced maps

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I_{1}\cap I_{2})\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I_{1})\oplus\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(K,I_{2})

are zero for all
i\geq 0
. In this case, we say that the maps
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\rightarrow I_{1}
and
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\rightarrow I_{2}
are
\operatorname{Tor}
-vanishing
. We quote
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
(or also
[
29
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
J,L\subset S
be non-zero monomial ideals with
J\subset L
. Suppose there exists a map
\varphi:\mathcal{G}(J)\rightarrow\mathcal{G}(L)
such that for any
\emptyset\neq\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{G}(J)
we have

\operatorname{lcm}(u:u\in\Omega)\in\mathfrak{m}\cdot(\operatorname{lcm}(\varphi(u):u\in\Omega)),

where
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then the inclusion map
J\rightarrow L
is
\operatorname{Tor}
-vanishing.

Given a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, let
\partial I
be the ideal generated by the elements
u/x_{i}
, with
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
i\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
. The next result follows from
[
29
, Proposition 4.4]
due to Nguyen and Vu. See also
[
8
, Lemma 1.3 and Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
J,L\subset S
be non-zero monomial ideals. Suppose that
\partial J\subset L
. Then
J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}L
and there exists a map
\varphi:\mathcal{G}(J)\rightarrow\mathcal{G}(L)
satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
. In particular, the inclusion map
J\rightarrow L
is
\operatorname{Tor}
-vanishing.

The next two basic facts will be used without reference in what follows.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
I\subset S
be a graded ideal and let
f\in S
be a homogeneous element. Then
\operatorname{reg}fI=\operatorname{reg}I+\deg(f)
and
\operatorname{depth}S/(fI)=\operatorname{depth}S/I
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
I_{x}\subset S_{x}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}]
and
I_{y}\subset S_{y}=K[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]
be proper monomial ideals in polynomial rings over disjoint sets of variables. Then, we have
\operatorname{reg}I_{x}I_{y}=\operatorname{reg}I_{x}+\operatorname{reg}I_{y}
and
\operatorname{depth}S/(I_{x}I_{y})=\operatorname{depth}S_{x}/I_{x}+\operatorname{depth}S_{y}/I_{y}+1
. Moreover, if
I_{y}=(1)
but
I_{x}\neq(1)
, then
\operatorname{depth}S/(I_{x}I_{y})=\operatorname{depth}S_{x}/I_{x}+q
. Similarly, if
I_{x}=(1)
but
I_{y}\neq(1)
, then
\operatorname{depth}S/(I_{x}I_{y})=\operatorname{depth}S_{y}/I_{y}+p
.

For a monomial
u\in S
and an integer
i\in[n]
, the
x_{i}
-degree of
u
is defined as

\deg_{x_{i}}(u)\ =\ \max\{j:\ x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}.

Now, we are in a position to prove Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Note that
I_{\langle n-2\rangle}=I_{c}(G)
is the complementary edge ideal of some graph
G
. Since
I
is minimally generated in the degrees
n-2
and
n-1
, we can find a minimal monomial generator
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
of degree
n-1
. Then
u={\bf x}_{[n]}/x_{i}
for some
i\in[n]
. We claim that
i
is an isolated vertex of
G
. Otherwise, we would have
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
for some
j
, and then
{\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{i}x_{j})\in I_{\langle n-2\rangle}
would divide
u
against the fact that
u
is a minimal generator. Let
F=\{i:\ {\bf x}_{[n]}/x_{i}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\}
. Then
F
is a subset of the isolated vertices of
G
, and therefore
{\bf x}_{F}
divides all the monomial generators of
I_{c}(G)
. Up to relabeling, we may assume that
F=[p]
for some
p\geq 1
. Let
H=G|_{[n]\setminus[t]}
. Then
I_{c}(G)={\bf x}_{[p]}I_{c}(H)
. For our convenience, we relabel the variables
\{x_{p+1},\dots,x_{n}\}
by
\{y_{1},\dots,y_{q}\}
. Our discussion then shows that we can write

I\ =\ {\bf x}_{[t]}I_{c}(H)+{\bf y}_{[q]}({\bf x}_{[t]}/x_{i}:\ i\in[t]).

We put
I_{y}=I_{c}(H)
and
I_{x}=({\bf x}_{[t]}/x_{i}:\ i\in[t])
. Then

I^{k}\ =\ \sum_{h=0}^{k}{\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-h}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{h}I_{y}^{k-h}I_{x}^{h}

for all
k\geq 1
. Fix an integer
k\geq 1
, and set
J_{\ell}=\sum_{h=0}^{\ell}{\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-h}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{h}I_{y}^{k-h}I_{x}^{h}
for
0\leq\ell\leq k
.

Notice that
J_{k}=I^{k}
. For each
0<\ell\leq k
, we claim that

J_{\ell}\ =\ J_{\ell-1}+{\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-\ell}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{\ell}I_{y}^{k-\ell}I_{x}^{\ell}

(2)

is a Betti splitting of
J_{\ell}
. First, we note that

\mathcal{G}(J_{\ell})\ =\ \mathcal{G}(J_{\ell-1})\sqcup\mathcal{G}({\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-\ell}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{\ell}I_{y}^{k-\ell}I_{x}^{\ell}).

To this end, let
u\in\mathcal{G}(J_{\ell-1})
and
v\in\mathcal{G}({\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-\ell}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{\ell}I_{y}^{k-\ell}I_{x}^{\ell})
. Then
u\in{\bf x}_{[p]}^{k-h}{\bf y}_{[q]}^{h}I_{y}^{k-h}I_{x}^{h}
for some
0\leq h<\ell
. A quick computation shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[p]}\deg_{x_{i}}(u)\ =\ pk-h\

\displaystyle>\ pk-\ell\ =\ \sum_{i\in[p]}\deg_{x_{i}}(v),

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in[q]}\deg_{y_{j}}(u)\ =\ (q-2)k+2h\

\displaystyle<\ (q-2)k+2\ell\ =\ \sum_{j\in[q]}\deg_{y_{j}}(v).

Hence
u
can not divide
v
and
v
can not divide
u
. This proves our claim. Next, we compute the intersection

3.
The stable set of associated primes of
I_{c}(G)

In this section, we prove the theorem that gives the title of the paper. We begin with the following observation.

Proposition 3.1

\,{\bf x}_{F}/x_{j}&\textup{if}\ i\notin F,\,j\in F,\\
\,{\bf x}_{F}/{\bf x}_{e}&\textup{if}\ i,j\in F.\end{cases}

∎

B
,
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))
if and only if
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/(I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F}))=0
for all
k\gg 0
. By Proposition
3.1
, since we assumed that
G
does not contain isolated vertices (hence
\bigcup_{e\in E(G)}e=[n]
), we have

I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F})\ =\ (I_{c}(G)(P_{F}))^{k}\ =\ \Big(I_{c}(G|_{F})+({\bf x}_{F}/x_{i}:\ \deg_{G|_{F}}(i)=0)\Big)^{k}.

Now, we distinguish the two possible cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that
\widetilde{b}(G|_{F})=b(G|_{F})
. Then
G|_{F}
does not contain isolated vertices, and so
I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F})=I_{c}(G|_{F})^{k}
. By
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
,
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I_{c}(G|_{F})^{k}=b(G)=\widetilde{b}(G)
for all
k\geq|F|-2
. Hence,
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))
if and only if
\widetilde{b}(G)=0
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
\widetilde{b}(G|_{F})\neq b(G|_{F})
. Then
G|_{F}
contains some isolated vertex. In this case, Theorem
2.1
(d) and
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
imply that

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/(I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F}))\

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{depth}S_{F}/I_{c}(G|_{F})^{k}-\mu_{|F|-1}(I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F}))

\displaystyle=\ b(G)-|\{i\in F:\ \deg_{G|_{F}}(i)=0\}|

\displaystyle=\ \widetilde{b}(G).

Hence, once again
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))
if and only if
\widetilde{b}(G)=0
.

It remains to prove that
\operatorname{astab}(I_{c}(G))\leq n-2
. To this end, let
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))
. If
F=\{i\}
is a singleton, then
i
is an isolated vertex of
G
, and so
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Otherwise, let
|F|\geq 2
. Then
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/(I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F}))=0
for all
k\gg 0
. By Theorem
2.1
(e) and
[
16
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have
\operatorname{depth}S_{F}/(I_{c}(G)^{k}(P_{F}))=0
for all
k\geq|F|-2
. Hence

\operatorname{astab}(I_{c}(G))\ \leq\ \max\{1,\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I_{c}(G))\\
|F|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\{|F|-2\}\}\ \leq\ n-2,

as desired.

∎

The proof of the theorem gives the following additional information.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on
[n]
. Let
F\subset[n]
such that
|F|\geq 2
and
\widetilde{b}(G|_{F})=0
. Then

P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k}),\quad\text{for all}\ k\geq\max\{1,|F|-2\}.

4.
Consequences

Given an integer
t
and a graph
H
, we define
tH
to be the graph consisting of
t
disjoint copies of
H
. The following three graphs will play a crucial role.

(i)

The complete graph on
n
vertices is the graph
K_{n}
with
V(K_{n})=[n]
and edge set
E(K_{n})=\{\{i,j\}:\ 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
.

(ii)

The path on
n
vertices is the graph
P_{n}
with
V(P_{n})=[n]
and edge set
E(P_{n})=\{\{i,i+1\}:\ 1\leq i<n\}=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\dots,\{n-1,n\}\}
.

(iii)

The cycle on
n
vertices is the graph
C_{n}
with
V(C_{n})=[n]
and edge set
E(C_{n})=\{\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\dots,\{n-1,n\},\{n,1\}\}
.

Recall that the
k
-th symbolic power of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
is defined as

I^{(k)}\ =\ \bigcap_{P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)}I^{k}(P_{F})S

We always have
I^{k}\subset I^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
, but equality may fail.

When
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, then
I^{k}=I^{(k)}
for some
k\geq 1
if and only if
\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, see the proof of
[
22
, Theorem 1.4.6]
. As a consequence of this simple fact and Theorem
A
we can give an alternative proof of
[
30
, Theorem 2.9]
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on
[n]
. The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
for all
k\geq 1
.

(b)

I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
for some
k\geq 2
.

(c)

G
is isomorphic to one of the graphs
K_{2}
,
K_{3}
,
P_{3}
,
2K_{2}
,
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
with
(
possibly
)
some isolated vertices.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) is true for any ideal.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) Suppose that
I_{c}(G)^{k}=I_{c}(G)^{(k)}
for some
k\geq 2
. As we explained before, this holds if and only if
\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))
. It is a simple exercise in graph theory to show that if
G
does not satisfy the statement (c), then
V(G)
contains a subset
F
with
|F|=4
such that
\widetilde{b}(G|_{F})=0
. Suppose for a contradiction that (c) does not hold. Then we can find such a
4
-subset
F\subset[n]
. By Corollary
3.2
,
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{\ell})
for all
\ell\geq 2
. Hence,
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})
. By
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
we have
P_{F}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})\setminus\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))
. A contradiction. Hence (c) follows.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a) It is straightforward to see that if
G
satisfies the statement (c) then
\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})=\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence (a) follows.

∎

Our next application regards the computation of the
\operatorname{v}
-function of
I_{c}(G)
.

Following
[
7
]
, given a homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
and a prime
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, the local
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
at
P
is defined as the least degree of a homogeneous element
f\in S
such that
I:f=P
, see also
[
3
,
6
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
. The global
\operatorname{v}
-number of
I
is then defined as

\operatorname{v}(I)\ =\ \min_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)}\operatorname{v}_{P}(I).

Let
P\in\operatorname{Ass}^{\infty}(I)
. Let
\operatorname{astab}_{P}(I)
be the least integer
k_{P}>0
such that
P\in\operatorname{Ass}(I^{k})
for all
k\geq k_{P}
. Then one can consider the function
\operatorname{v}_{P}(I^{k})
for
k\geq k_{P}
. This is called the (local)
\operatorname{v}_{P}
-function of
I
at
P
. The (global)
\operatorname{v}
-function of
I
is simply the function
k\mapsto\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
. It is proved independently in
[
6
]
and
[
17
]
, that the local
\operatorname{v}
-functions and the
\operatorname{v}
-function of a homogeneous ideal
I
are eventually linear functions. Moreover, in
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
, it is proved that the slope of the
\operatorname{v}
-function of
I
is the initial degree of
I
. The least integer
k_{0}>0
such that
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})
is a linear function for all
k\geq k_{0}
, is called the
index of
\operatorname{v}
-stability
of
I
and is denoted by
\operatorname{vstab}(I)
.

It is expected (
[
17
]
and
[
11
, Conjecture 2.6]
) that

(i)

\operatorname{v}(I^{k})<\operatorname{reg}I^{k}
for any homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
and all
k\gg 0
.

(ii)

If
I\subset S
is a monomial ideal with linear powers, then
\operatorname{v}(I^{k})=\operatorname{reg}I^{k}-1
for all
k\geq 1
.

Both conjectures (i) and (ii) have been proved for several classes of monomial ideals, including the family of edge ideals (see the results contained in the paper
[
11
]
, and
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
,
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
12
, Corollary 5.7]
for the proofs of the conjectures (i) and (ii) in the case of edge ideals).

Next, we establish conjectures (i) and (ii) for complementary edge ideals.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on
[n]
. The following statements hold.

(a)

For all
k\geq 1
,

\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})\ =\ \begin{cases}(n-2)k&\text{if}\ G=tK_{2},\ \text{with}\ t\geq 2,\\
(n-2)k-1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(b)

\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})<\operatorname{reg}I_{c}(G)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

(c)

If
I_{c}(G)
has linear powers, then
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})=\operatorname{reg}I_{c}(G)^{k}-1
for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

(a) By
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
, it follows that
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})\geq(n-2)k-1
for all
k\geq 1
. Therefore, when
G\neq tK_{2}
to prove that equality holds for all
k\geq 1
, it suffices to find a monomial
u_{k}
, for each
k\geq 1
, such that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})
and
\deg(u_{k})=(n-2)k-1
.

Assume that
G
contains an isolated vertex
i
. Then
(x_{i})\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G)^{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
e\in E(G)
, then
i\notin e
. We consider the monomial
u_{k}=({\bf x}_{[n]\setminus e})^{k}/x_{i}
for all
k\geq 1
. Note that
x_{i}u_{k}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. Now, let
v
be a monomial with
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset[n]\setminus\{i\}
. Then
\deg_{x_{i}}(vu_{k})=k-1
. Hence
vu_{k}\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}
. We conclude that
I_{c}(G):u_{k}=P_{\{i\}}=(x_{i})
, as desired.

Suppose now that
G
does not contain isolated vertices. Let
G^{c}
be the complementary graph of
G
. That is, the graph with the same vertex set of
G
whose edges are the non-edges. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{T}(G)
be the set of triangles (also called
3
-cliques) of
G
. By
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
, since
G
does not have isolated vertices, we have

\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))\ =\ \{P_{e}:\ e\in E(G^{c})\}\cup\{P_{T}:\ T\in\mathcal{T}(G)\}.

First, suppose that
G
contains a triangle
T=\{p,q,r\}
. Consider the monomial
u_{k}=({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}x_{r}))({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}))^{k-1}
with
k\geq 1
. Note that
\deg(u_{k})=(n-2)k-1
. We claim that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}=P_{T}
. It is clear that
P_{T}\subset I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. For instance,

x_{p}u_{k}\ =\ ({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{q}x_{r}))({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}))^{k-1}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}.

To prove the reverse inclusion
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}\subset P_{T}
, we show that if
v
is a monomial with
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset[n]\setminus\{p,q,r\}
, then
v\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. Suppose on the contrary that
v\in I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. Then, there exist edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in E(G)
such that

w\ =\ \prod_{i=1}^{k}{\bf x}_{[n]\setminus e_{i}}\quad\text{divides}\quad vu_{k}\ =\ v({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}x_{r}))({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}))^{k-1}.

(9)

It follows that

\deg_{x_{p}}(w)\ =\ k-|\{j\in[k]:\ p\in e_{j}\}|\ \leq\ \deg_{x_{p}}(v)+\deg_{x_{p}}(u_{k})\ =\ 0,

because
x_{p}
does not divide
v
and
u_{k}
. Since
|\{j\in[k]:p\in e_{j}\}|\leq k
, the above inequality implies that
|\{j\in[k]:p\in e_{j}\}|=k
. Hence
p\in e_{j}
for all
j\in[k]
. Similarly,
q\in e_{j}
for all
j\in[k]
. Hence
e_{1}=\dots=e_{k}=\{p,q\}
. It follows that
\deg_{x_{r}}(w)=k
. This
x_{r}
-degree should be less than or equal to
\deg_{x_{r}}(vu_{k})=\deg_{x_{r}}(v)+\deg_{x_{r}}(u_{k})=k-1
, because
r\notin\operatorname{supp}(v)
. But this is not possible. Hence
v\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. We conclude that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}=P_{T}
.

Now, assume that
G
contains no triangles. Since
G
does not have isolated vertices, either
G
contains an induced
3
-path
P_{3}
or else
G=tK_{2}
with
t\geq 2
(since
n\geq 3
).

Suppose first that
G
contains an induced
3
-path. Say, that
\{p,q\},\{q,r\}\in E(G)
, but
e=\{p,r\}\notin E(G)
. Hence
P_{e}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I_{c}(G))
. Consider again the monomial
u_{k}=({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}x_{r}))({\bf x}_{[n]}/(x_{p}x_{q}))^{k-1}
with
k\geq 1
. Note that
\deg(u_{k})=(n-2)k-1
. We claim that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}=P_{e}
. As before,
P_{e}\subset I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. Conversely, we show that if
v
is a monomial with
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset[n]\setminus\{p,r\}
, then
v\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. Suppose on the contrary this was the case. Then, equation (
9
) would be again valid. From this equation, we derive that all edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
contain
p
. Since
\{p,r\}\notin E(G)
, it follows that
\deg_{x_{r}}(w)=k
. But this
x_{r}
-degree should be less than or equal to
\deg_{x_{r}}(vu_{k})=k-1
, which is absurd. We conclude that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}=P_{e}
.

Now, let
G=tK_{2}
with
t\geq 2
. Say
E(G)=\{\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\dots,\{2t-1,2t\}\}
. First we show that there is no monomial
u\in S
of degree
(2t-2)k-1
such that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
is a monomial prime ideal. Suppose for a contradiction that such a monomial
u
exists. Then
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
should be generated by variables. Up to relabeling, we may assume that
x_{1}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
. Then
x_{1}u\in I_{c}(G)
. Since
\deg(x_{1}u)=(2t-2)k
, then
x_{1}u\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G)^{k})
. Therefore, there exist edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in E(G)
such that
x_{1}u=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus e_{i}}
. Since
x_{1}
divides this monomial, we have
1\notin e_{i}
for at least one index
i\in[k]
. Say
e_{i}=e_{1}=\{3,4\}
. Therefore
u={\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,3,4\}}(\prod_{i=2}^{k}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus e_{i}})
. Note that
x_{3}x_{4}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
because
x_{3}x_{4}u=x_{2}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,2\}}(\prod_{i=2}^{k}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus e_{i}})\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
. We claim that
x_{3}\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
and
x_{4}\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
. This shows that
x_{3}x_{4}
is a minimal generator of
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
and so
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
is not generated by variables. If
x_{3}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
, then we should have

x_{3}u\ =\ {\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,4\}}(\prod_{i=2}^{k}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus e_{i}})\ =\ {\bf x}_{[2t]}^{k}/((x_{1}x_{4}){\bf x}_{e_{2}}\cdots{\bf x}_{e_{k}})\in I_{c}(G)^{k}.

Since
\deg(x_{3}u)=(2t-2)k
, this is equivalent to
w=(x_{1}x_{4}){\bf x}_{e_{2}}\cdots{\bf x}_{e_{k}}\in I(G)^{k}
. Let
w^{\prime}={\bf x}_{e_{2}}\cdots{\bf x}_{e_{k}}
. Since
\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G))=\{{\bf x}_{\{1,2\}},\dots,{\bf x}_{\{2t-1,2t\}}\}
, each minimal monomial generator of
v\in I(G)^{k}
is such that
\deg_{x_{1}}(v)+\deg_{x_{2}}(v)
is an even integer. On the other hand,
\deg_{x_{1}}(w)+\deg_{x_{2}}(w)=1+\deg_{x_{1}}(w^{\prime})+\deg_{x_{2}}(w^{\prime})
is an odd integer, because
w\in\mathcal{G}(I_{c}(G)^{k-1})
. Thus
w\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}
, and so
x_{3}\notin I_{c}(G)^{k}:u
, as desired.

Summarizing our discussion thus far, we proved that
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})\geq(2t-2)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Now, we prove that equality holds. To this end, consider the monomial
u_{k}={\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,3\}}({\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,2\}})^{k-1}
. Note that
x_{1}u_{k}=x_{4}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{3,4\}}({\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus\{1,2\}})^{k-1}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
. Similarly
x_{3}u_{k}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
. Hence
(x_{1},x_{3})\subset I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}
. To prove the reverse inclusion, consider a monomial
v\in S
with
\operatorname{supp}(v)\subset[2t]\setminus\{1,3\}
. Suppose for a contradiction that
vu_{k}\in I_{c}(G)^{k}
, then there exist edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in E(G)
such that
w=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\bf x}_{[2t]\setminus e_{i}}
divides
vu_{k}
. Since
\deg_{x_{1}}(vu_{k})=0
it follows that
\deg_{x_{1}}(w)=0
, and so
e_{1}=\dots=e_{k}=\{1,2\}
. Hence
\deg_{x_{3}}(w)=k
. However, this
x_{3}
-degree should be less than or equal to
\deg_{x_{3}}(vu_{k})=k-1
, an absurd. It follows that
I_{c}(G)^{k}:u_{k}=(x_{1},x_{3})
as desired. We conclude that
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})=(2t-2)k
for all
k\geq 1
, and statement (a) follows.

(b) Let
c=c(G)
. By
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
or also Theorem
C
(c)(ii) in this paper, it follows indeed that
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})<\operatorname{reg}I_{c}(G)^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
.

(c) Assume that
I_{c}(G)
has linear powers. Then
\operatorname{reg}I_{c}(G)^{k}=(n-2)k
for all
k\geq 1
. Moreover,
[
14
, Theorem B]
(or also Theorem
C
(c)(ii)) yields
c(G)=1
. Part (a) implies that
\operatorname{v}(I_{c}(G)^{k})=(n-2)k-1=\operatorname{reg}I_{c}(G)^{k}-1
for all
k\geq 1
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

We have
\operatorname{vstab}(I_{c}(G))=1
for any graph
G
.

It would be of great interest to determine explicitly all the local
\operatorname{v}
-functions of a complementary edge ideal, as well as those of an edge ideal.

We conclude the paper with the following remark.

An ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring is said to satisfy the
strong persistence property
if
I^{k+1}:I=I^{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. When
I
satisfies the strong persistence property, then it satisfies the persistence property as well. Edge ideals satisfy the strong persistence property, see
[
28
, Lemma 2.12]
.

Besides Theorem
B
, other methods are available to prove that an ideal satisfies the persistence property. For instance, Herzog and Qureshi
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
proved that if the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}t^{k}\subset S[t]
of a monomial ideal
I\subset S
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
, then
I
satisfies the (strong) persistence property.

It is expected that the Rees algebra of a complementary edge ideal is always Cohen-Macaulay (
[
16
, Conjecture 4.7]
). If this is true, then any complementary edge ideal would satisfy the strong persistence property, and this would yield another proof of Theorem
A
. At present, whether
I_{c}(G)
always satisfies the strong persistence property is an open question.
