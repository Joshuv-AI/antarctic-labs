Public Executive Summary
The future of Laser-Based Inertial fusion energy requires the development of efficient diode-pumped
laser drivers that can be scaled to megajoule class output operating at ≥ 10-Hz repetition rate. ARPA-E requested
a study of the range of possibilities for a 100kJ/10-Hz laser system with >15% wall-plug efficiency operating
below 360 nm. The Laser system must exhibit broad-bandwidth in order to enable beam smoothing, temporal pulse
shaping and the suppression of Laser Plasma Instabilities (LPI) in both direct drive and indirect drive fusion targets. The
study documented herein finds that ~12% Electrical to Optical Efficiency to the third harmonic is achievable today.
While 15% Electrical to Optical Efficiency is not currently achievable, such an efficiency can be achieved with specific
and straight-forward technical development. The conceptual design of a compact, 1 kJ class module is presented and
analyzed for performance relative to the objectives along with a roadmap to demonstrate the technology

Accomplishments and Objectives
Over the past 20 years, there have been dramatic advances in diode-pumped laser systems for
weapon applications. The recent demonstration of 300 kW class average power systems required
new classes of laser diodes, coatings, optics and materials. At the most fundamental level, this
study was performed to determine if the technologies developed for these weapon class lasers
could impact the design, development and demonstration of the high pulse energy, diode-pumped
lasers required for Inertial Fusion Energy.

Three critical technologies were shown to have a dramatic impact on potential IFE lasers: 1) High-
brightness, high-efficiency laser diode arrays, 2) Liquid cooled, solid-state lasers, and 3) Ion
Beam Sputtered, multilayer dielectric coatings. The impact of these technologies was examined
and analyzed to determine the quantitative value. This “value” was defined as the impact on
electro-optic efficiency.

We were able to show that a liquid-cooled, solid-state laser could achieve an Electrical to Optical
Efficiency of 12% at the third-harmonic (3ω) today and up to 15% with straightforward advances
in diode efficiency.

At the request of ARPA-E, we provided a specific conceptual design of a 1 kJ class demonstrator
system utilizing an extension of our currently operating laser amplifiers.
