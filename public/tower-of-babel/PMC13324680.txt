Lactobacillus acidophilus improves memory and attenuates neuroinflammation in streptozotocin- and aluminum chloride–exposed rats
Authors: Reza Yousefi, Niloufar Darbandi, Majid komijani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionCognitive impairment associated with neurodegenerative conditions is frequently driven by oxidative stress and neuroinflammation arising from metabolic and environmental insults. Streptozotocin (STZ) and aluminum chloride (AlCl₃) are widely used to induce experimental models of cognitive dysfunction through distinct pathogenic mechanisms. This study evaluated the neuroprotective effects of the probiotic Lactobacillus acidophilus in these two models.Materials and MethodsAdult male Wistar rats were divided into five groups: control, STZ, AlCl₃, probiotic + STZ, and probiotic + AlCl₃. Rats received intracerebroventricular STZ or saline, AlCl₃ in drinking water, and daily oral gavage of Lactobacillus acidophilus for 14 days. Behavioral performance was assessed using the novel object recognition and passive avoidance tests. Aluminum levels, oxidative stress markers, inflammatory cytokines, and hippocampal neuronal integrity were evaluated using biochemical and histological analyses.ResultsSTZ and AlCl₃ administration induced significant memory impairment, increased oxidative stress and pro-inflammatory cytokines, and reduced the number of healthy hippocampal neurons. Probiotic treatment significantly improved memory performance, reduced lipid peroxidation and inflammatory markers, enhanced antioxidant defenses, decreased aluminum accumulation, and preserved neuronal integrity in the hippocampusConclusionOral administration of Lactobacillus acidophilus effectively attenuates cognitive impairment, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress induced by both metabolic and metal-related neurotoxic insults. These findings suggest that probiotic supplementation may represent a promising preventive strategy against cognitive dysfunction associated with diverse neurotoxic conditions.

Introduction

Memory and learning are complex functions of the nervous system, with the hippocampus playing a central role in emotional regulation, long-term memory consolidation, and resistance to memory extinction (Yassa, 2021). Disruption of hippocampal integrity is closely linked to cognitive decline and neurodegenerative disorders. Alzheimer’s disease (AD), the most prevalent age-related neurodegenerative condition, is characterized by progressive memory loss and higher cognitive deficits (Kareva, 2025, FallahBagheri and Akan, 2025). Experimental models have been extensively used to investigate AD pathophysiology and evaluate potential therapies. Among them, intracerebroventricular (ICV) administration of streptozotocin (STZ) is widely employed to mimic sporadic AD by inducing central insulin resistance, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and cholinergic dysfunction (Akhtar et al., 2021, da Silva et al., 2024, Gáspár et al., 2022). This model primarily reflects metabolic and inflammatory disturbances associated with cognitive impairment.

In parallel, increasing attention has focused on the neurotoxic effects of environmental heavy metals. Heavy metals are non-degradable, tend to accumulate in biological tissues, and cause long-term cellular dysfunction (Fu and Xi, 2020). Aluminum, one of the most abundant industrial metals, is used in construction, food packaging, pharmaceuticals, cosmetics, and water treatment (Harmaji et al., 2024). Human exposure occurs via ingestion, inhalation, and dermal absorption, with an estimated daily intake of ∼25 mg (Ganhör et al., 2024). Due to its physicochemical similarity to iron, aluminum can cross the blood–brain barrier and accumulate in neural tissues (Renke et al., 2023).

Within the CNS, aluminum induces neurotoxicity through multiple mechanisms, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, disruption of the Bcl−2/Bax balance, and activation of apoptotic pathways (Singla and Dhawan, 2015, Kawahara et al., 2021). Moreover, aluminum promotes amyloid precursor protein (APP) processing and amyloid-β aggregation, contributing to synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and Alzheimer-like cognitive decline (Mocanu et al., 2020). Therefore, AlCl₃ exposure represents a model of direct metal-induced neurotoxicity, mechanistically distinct from metabolic models such as STZ.

Probiotics, defined as live microorganisms conferring health benefits to the host, have emerged as potential modulators of cognitive function through their effects on inflammation, oxidative stress, neurotransmitter systems, and gut–brain axis signaling (Moghaddam et al., 2021). Lactobacillus acidophilus, a prominent intestinal microbe, exhibits antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties and can modulate immune responses and neuronal signaling pathways (Nobakht et al., 2021). Despite growing evidence supporting probiotic benefits in neurodegenerative conditions, most studies focus on single disease models, leaving it unclear whether probiotic efficacy depends on the underlying pathogenic mechanism.

Neuroinflammation is a shared pathological feature of both metabolic and metal-induced models of cognitive dysfunction. Pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin−6 (IL−6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) play pivotal roles in neuroinflammatory responses and are consistently elevated in AD as well as in STZ- and aluminum-induced experimental models (Tayanloo-Beik et al., 2022, Kazmi et al., 2024). Therefore, assessing IL−6 and TNF-α expression provides a relevant molecular framework for evaluating model-specific inflammatory responses and therapeutic interventions.

This study investigates the neuroprotective potential of Lactobacillus acidophilus against memory impairment, hippocampal neuronal loss, and neuroinflammation induced by STZ and AlCl₃ in male rats. The novelty of this work lies in providing the first comparative evidence that oral administration of this probiotic can attenuate oxidative stress and inflammatory responses in mechanistically distinct models of cognitive dysfunction, highlighting its potential as a preventive or therapeutic strategy against neurodegeneration.

Materials and methods

Animals

A total of 60 male Wistar rats, weighing 220–250 g, were obtained from the Pasteur Institute (Tehran, Iran). The animals were housed in clean, transparent Plexiglas cages, with 3–4 rats per cage, under a 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle. Food and water were provided ad libitum, except during experimental procedures. Room temperature was maintained at 22 ± 2 °C, with lights on from 7:00 pm to 7:00 am. Rats were acclimated to laboratory conditions for 7 days before surgical procedures. To minimize variability due to circadian rhythms, all behavioral tests were performed between 9:00 am and 2:00 pm. All experimental procedures were approved by the local Ethics Committee of the School of Biology, University of Arak (Approval Code: IR.ARAKMU.REC.1401.137) and conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines for laboratory animal care (NIH publication) and relevant national animal protection regulations.

Surgery

One week after acclimation to the laboratory environment, the rats were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of a ketamine (10%, Alfasan, Woerden, Holland) and xylazine (2%, Alfasan, Woerden, Holland) cocktail at doses of 50 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg, respectively. Bilateral implantation of two 22-gauge stainless steel guide cannulas into the lateral ventricles was performed using the bregmatic and lambdoid sutures of the skull as anatomical landmarks (Paxinos et al., 2020). The coordinates for implantation were 0.8 mm posterior to the bregma, ±1.4 mm lateral to the sagittal suture, and 3.4 mm ventral from the skull surface. The injection needle extended 0.5 mm beyond the tip of the guide cannula. Drug administration was carried out over 60 s, followed by a 1-minute pause to ensure complete delivery.

After surgery, rats were monitored twice daily. Based on the short surgery duration (10–15 min) and absence of severe pain signs using a standardized score sheet, the Ethics Committee approved the protocol without additional postoperative analgesia. Humane endpoints (≥20% weight loss, complete anorexia >48 h, seizures, severe behavioral abnormalities) were predefined, and no animal reached these criteria.

Drugs

An experimental model of sporadic (late-onset) Alzheimer's disease in rats was induced using streptozotocin (Akhtar et al., 2021, da Silva et al., 2024, Gáspár et al., 2022). In the groups receiving streptozotocin, the drug (3 mg/kg) was freshly prepared by mixing with sterile 0.9% saline and injected into the lateral ventricles on the first and third days after cannulation. In the other groups, intracerebroventricular injection of saline was performed on the same days. Each injection was administered using polyethylene tubing connected to a 25 μl Hamilton syringe. A 10 μl solution was infused into both the left and right ventricles (totaling 20 μl per rat) over approximately 3 min (Darbandi et al., 2018).

Rats received an AlCl3·6H2O (Sigma Aldrich, USA) solution in their drinking water for 14 days, provided fresh daily. The AlCl3 concentrations in the drinking water were 0 g/L for the Control, STZ, and Pro + STZ groups, and 0.8 g/L for the AL and Pro + Al groups. Individual water intake averaged 18 ml per day (range: 16–19 ml), corresponding to an AlCl3 dose of approximately 64 mg/kg body weight, equivalent to one twentieth of the compound's median lethal dose (LD₅₀; 1283 mg/kg) (Liu et al., 2016). This low dose was administered over a semi-subchronic exposure period of 14 days.

Lactobacillus acidophilus probiotic (ATCC 4356) was procured as a lyophilized ampoule from the National Genetic and Biological Resource Center of Iran and reconstituted according to the manufacturer's instructions. In all experimental groups, either normal saline or the probiotic suspension at a standardized turbidity of 0.5 McFarland (approximately 1.5 ×10⁸ CFU/ml) was administered daily via oral gavage at a volume of 1 ml for 14 consecutive days (Yarullina et al., 2024).

Experimental procedure and treatments

Five experimental groups (n = 12) were considered: control, streptozotocin, aluminum chloride, probiotic with streptozotocin, and probiotic with aluminum chloride. Intracerebroventricular injection of streptozotocin or saline was performed on the first and third days of cannulation in all relevant groups. Probiotic or saline gavage started two days before cannulation and continued for 14 days. In the groups receiving aluminum chloride, treatment as a solution in drinking water also started two days prior to cannulation and lasted 14 days. The 2‑day pretreatment period was selected empirically based on pilot studies from our laboratory (data not shown). This duration allowed for sufficient gut colonization by L. acidophilus before disease induction, while longer pretreatments increased handling stress. Furthermore, in vitro studies have demonstrated that L. acidophilus can induce cytokine expression in immune cells within hours of exposure (O'Flaherty and Klaenhammer, 2012, Weiss et al., 2011) and colonization studies in rodents have confirmed that L. acidophilus can establish itself in the gut within days of oral administration (Buddington et al., 2010).

Following the completion of treatments, each animal underwent behavioral testing. The sequence of tests was as follows: open field test (OFT), novel object recognition test (NORT), and passive avoidance test (PAT), with a minimum interval of 24 h between each test (Galeano et al., 2014). The sequence of behavioral tests was designed from least to most stressful, consistent with established guidelines for behavioral test batteries (McIlwain et al., 2001). A 24‑hour inter‑test interval was maintained between all tests, which has been shown to have no major effect on overall behavioral performance (Paylor et al., 2006). Animals were subsequently randomly assigned into two sets: half were assessed for serum biochemical parameters and aluminum levels in serum and brain tissue, and the other half underwent brain perfusion and tissue analyses.

Open field test

The Open Field Test (OFT) was conducted to evaluate the effects of individual drugs or their combinations on locomotor activity (Alijanpour et al., 2022). On the fifteenth day, one day after completing treatments, each rat underwent a 5-minute OFT using equipment from Borj Sanat Co., Iran. The apparatus measured 40 × 40 × 40 cm and was equipped with 16 infrared photocells spaced at 2.5 cm intervals. Each rat was gently placed in the center of the arena, and the photocell system recorded the number of beam interruptions. The apparatus was thoroughly cleaned after each session. All tests were conducted in a quiet, dimly lit environment to minimize external disturbances.

Novel object recognition test

The Novel Object Recognition (NOR) test was used to evaluate long-term non-spatial recognition memory. The apparatus consisted of a white Plexiglas box measuring 40 × 40 × 50 cm (Bevins and Besheer, 2006). The test was conducted in a quiet, isolated room with consistent, dim lighting. The NOR task was performed over three consecutive days (days 17–19). On the first day (habituation session), animals were allowed to freely explore the empty box for 5 min.

On the second day (training session), rats were placed in the box and presented with two identical objects (A1 and A2 – two nuts) similar in texture, color, and size, positioned in two corners of the box 5 cm from the walls. Animals explored the objects and the environment for 15 min. After a 24-hour retention period in their home cages, rats were reintroduced to the box for 5 min to explore one familiar object (A1) and one novel object (T1 – one bolt) during the recognition session. The objects were large and heavy enough to prevent displacement by the animals, and resistant to rusting to avoid external influences on behavior.

Following each trial, both objects and the apparatus were cleaned with 70% ethanol to eliminate olfactory cues. Animal behaviors were recorded by a camera positioned above the test chamber. Exploration was defined as sniffing or touching the objects with the nose and/or forepaws. In the NOR task, the Preference Index (PI) on Day 2 (training) was used to assess any side bias, while PI on Day 3 (test) reflected the animal’s preference for the novel object (Griffin et al., 2023).

Passive avoidance test

The step-through passive avoidance task was employed to assess contextual long-term memory over two consecutive days (days 21 and 22). The inhibitory avoidance apparatus comprised two equally sized compartments: one illuminated and one dark, separated by a guillotine door. The light compartment was constructed from white Plexiglas, with walls and floor illuminated by a 20-watt lamp positioned 50 cm above the floor. The dark compartment had black Plexiglas walls and a metal grid floor composed of bars spaced 1 cm apart with a diameter of 0.5 cm. This grid was connected to a shock generator capable of delivering a 3-second, 50 Hz electric shock at 1 mA (Darbandi et al., 2023).

On the training day, rats were first allowed a 30-minute habituation period to familiarize themselves with the apparatus. Each rat was then placed individually in the light compartment for 10 s, after which the door was lifted and the latency to enter the dark compartment was recorded. After a 2-minute interval, the rat was returned to the light compartment. Once the rat fully entered the dark compartment, the door was closed and an electric foot shock was delivered for 3 s. The rat was then removed from the apparatus. This procedure was repeated after another 2-minute interval. Rats that failed to enter the dark compartment within 120 s were considered successfully trained and removed. Each animal underwent a maximum of two training trials.

Twenty-four hours later, during the retention test, rats were placed in the light compartment, and after 5 s the door was opened. The latency to enter the dark compartment (step-through latency, STL) and the total time spent in the dark compartment (TDC) were recorded for up to 300 s without any foot shock, serving as measures of inhibitory avoidance memory (Darbandi et al., 2023).

Preparation and staining of brain tissue samples

Following the completion of behavioral tests, half of the animals in each group were randomly selected for tissue sampling and histological evaluation. Rats were anesthetized with 3.5% chloral hydrate (35 mg per 100 g body weight, intraperitoneally) and transcardially perfused through the left ventricle with 0.1 M phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4), followed by 4% paraformaldehyde in chilled physiological saline. Brains were carefully removed and immersed in the same fixative for 24–48 h.

Tissue processing was performed using a Leica tissue processor (Germany), and specimens were embedded in paraffin. Serial coronal sections (7 µm thick) were prepared from the dorsal hippocampus and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histological examination. Based on the Paxinos atlas (Paxinos et al., 2020), all brain sections were obtained from the area ranging from −3.8 to −4.3 mm relative to the bregma, encompassing the clearly identifiable hippocampus. Four evenly spaced sections were selected for neuron counting, and the average was used to represent the neuronal number for each sample (Darbandi et al., 2018).

Hippocampal tissue was examined histologically with a light microscope (BX40, Olympus, New York, USA) fitted with a digital camera (Olympus, DP12). High-resolution images were captured from the CA1, CA2, and CA3 regions and transferred to ImageJ software (NIH, USA) for quantitative analysis. The "Cell Counter" plugin in ImageJ was used for manual counting of pyramidal neurons. Healthy neurons were identified based on clear morphological criteria: intact round nuclei, visible nucleoli, and uniform cytoplasmic staining. Degenerated or unhealthy neurons were characterized by pyknotic nuclei, dark or shrunken cytoplasm, and disrupted morphology. Cell counts were performed in a defined field of view (×400 magnification), and results were expressed as the mean number of healthy neurons per section in each group.

Measurement of aluminum chloride in blood serum and brain tissue

After completion of behavioral assessments, half of the animals in each group were randomly selected for measurement of serum biochemical parameters and aluminum levels in blood serum and brain tissue. Rats were anesthetized with chloroform until death, and blood samples were collected from the right ventricle. Aluminum chloride levels were measured using the ICP-OES method (Analytik Jena GmbH - PlasmaQuant 9100, Germany). To measure aluminum chloride in brain tissue, rats were sacrificed after anesthesia, and their brains were carefully extracted and placed in a Petri dish. Each brain was weighed and then dried in an oven at 105°C for 72 h. Once dried, the brain tissues were ground into a fine, homogeneous powder. For digestion, 1 g of each powdered sample was treated with 10 ml of concentrated nitric acid and 5 ml of hydrogen peroxide, as described in (Yeganeh et al., 2018). Aluminum chloride concentration in the resulting solution was determined using an atomic absorption spectrophotometer (SP UV−21/Soc-Tech, Germany).

Biochemical analysis

Blood serum samples were centrifuged at 4°C using a refrigerated centrifuge (Universal Device, Germany) at 13,300 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant aliquots were then stored at −20°C until biochemical analyses were performed. Lipid peroxidation (Buege and Aust, 1978), total plasma antioxidant capacity (Benzie and Strain, 1996), superoxide dismutase enzyme activity (Marklund and Marklund, 1974), and catalase enzyme activity (Aebi, 1984) were assessed through spectrophotometric measurements using a PG/T80 device (UK). In all groups, sensitive rat ELISA kits were used to measure tumor necrosis factor-alpha (Catalog No. RTA00, R&D systems [bio techne], USA) and interleukin−6 (Catalog No. CSB-E04640r, CUSABIO, USA) levels, following the manufacturers' instructions

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software version 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to verify normality of the data distribution. Results are expressed as mean ± SEM. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was used to determine statistical significance, set at P < 0.05.

Comparison of novel object recognition test in experimental groups

One-way ANOVA showed no significant differences in the preference index among the experimental groups during the training session (Day 2) [F (4, 56) = 0.46, P > 0.05] (Fig. 5C), indicating no inherent side bias in object exploration. During the test session (Day 3), a significant difference in the preference index was observed [F (4, 56) = 65.05, P < 0.001] among groups. Rats treated with STZ or aluminum chloride exhibited a significant reduction in preference index compared to the control group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5D), indicating impaired recognition memory. Administration of Lactobacillus acidophilus for 14 days in STZ- or aluminum chloride-treated groups significantly increased the preference index compared to the STZ or aluminum chloride groups (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5D). These results suggest that probiotic treatment effectively mitigates the recognition memory deficits induced by STZ or aluminum chloride, restoring performance to levels comparable with the control group.

Comparison of open field test among the experimental groups

Analysis of locomotor activity using the Open Field Test revealed no significant differences among the experimental groups [F (4, 56) = 2.49, P > 0.05] (Fig. 5E). This indicates that neither intracerebroventricular injection of STZ, nor consumption of aluminum chloride in drinking water, nor daily gavage with Lactobacillus acidophilus for 14 days, had a significant effect on general locomotor activity.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that oral administration of Lactobacillus acidophilus effectively attenuated cognitive impairment, hippocampal neuronal loss, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation induced by streptozotocin (STZ) and aluminum chloride (AlCl₃) in rats. Both experimental models produced qualitatively similar behavioral deficits and histopathological alterations, supporting the concept that metabolically and environmentally induced neurotoxic insults converge on shared pathological pathways underlying cognitive dysfunction (Akhtar et al., 2021, da Silva et al., 2024, Gáspár et al., 2022, Singla and Dhawan, 2015, Kawahara et al., 2021, Mocanu et al., 2020). Nevertheless, it should be noted that the two models differed considerably in exposure duration and route of administration (STZ: ICV injection on days 1 and 3; AlCl₃: daily oral intake for 14 days), which limits direct quantitative comparisons of the protective efficacy between them.

Consistent with previous reports, AlCl₃ exposure significantly increased aluminum accumulation in serum and brain tissue, likely due to its ability to cross the blood–brain barrier and interfere with metal homeostasis (Ganhör et al., 2024, Renke et al., 2023). Elevated aluminum levels have been reported in the blood and brain of patients with neurodegenerative disorders and are associated with oxidative damage and neuronal dysfunction (Jafari et al., 2022). In the present study, probiotic administration markedly reduced aluminum accumulation, suggesting a potential role for L. acidophilus in modulating aluminum absorption or promoting metal clearance (Feng and Wang, 2020, Hoffmann et al., 2019).

Both STZ and AlCl₃ significantly increased lipid peroxidation and reduced antioxidant defenses, as reflected by elevated MDA levels and decreased FRAP, SOD, and CAT activities. These findings are consistent with previous studies showing that STZ induces oxidative stress via central insulin resistance and impaired glucose metabolism (Darbandi et al., 2016, Bagaméry et al., 2020, Hajizadeh Moghaddam et al., 2020) whereas aluminum exposure promotes reactive oxygen species generation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disruption of antioxidant systems (Tackenberg et al., 2020, Skalny et al., 2021). Importantly, L. acidophilus administration significantly restored antioxidant capacity and reduced oxidative damage, supporting its protective role against redox imbalance (Feng and Wang, 2020, Abu-Elfotuh et al., 2023).

Neuroinflammation is a key pathological feature shared by both models. Increased serum levels of TNF-α and IL−6 observed following STZ and AlCl₃ exposure are consistent with prior reports linking these cytokines to synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and memory impairment (Tayanloo-Beik et al., 2022, Kazmi et al., 2024, Nedosugova et al., 2022, Kumar et al., 2019). Probiotic treatment significantly reduced TNF-α and IL−6 levels, in line with evidence that probiotics modulate immune signaling, suppress inflammatory cascades, and influence gut–brain axis communication (Moghaddam et al., 2021, Naomi et al., 2021, Athari Nik Azm et al., 2018).

Behavioral assessments further confirmed the neuroprotective effects of L. acidophilus. STZ and AlCl₃ impaired recognition and avoidance memory without affecting locomotor activity, indicating specific cognitive deficits rather than nonspecific motor dysfunction (Bevins and Besheer, 2006, Griffin et al., 2023, Darbandi et al., 2023). Probiotic administration significantly improved memory performance in both paradigms, suggesting preservation of hippocampal-dependent learning processes (Carabotti et al., 2015, Sherwin et al., 2016).

Histological findings corroborated the behavioral data, showing marked neuronal degeneration in the CA1, CA2, and CA3 regions following STZ or AlCl₃ exposure. These alterations are likely mediated by oxidative stress–induced apoptosis, inflammatory signaling, and disruption of neurotrophic support (Agrawal et al., 2020, Hira et al., 2019, Mishra et al., 2018, Inan-Eroglu and Ayaz, 2018, Weng et al., 2020). Probiotic treatment preserved hippocampal neuronal integrity, indicating a protective effect at the cellular level, potentially through regulation of apoptotic pathways and maintenance of redox balance (Abu-Elfotuh et al., 2023, El Sayed et al., 2021).

It should be noted that the present study did not include a control group receiving Lactobacillus acidophilus alone without any disease or stress model. Therefore, we cannot completely rule out the possibility that some of the observed effects might be attributed to non-specific actions of the probiotic itself rather than its specific interaction with the pathological condition. However, a previous reported that probiotic administration alone did not significantly affect novel object recognition or passive avoidance memory in non-sleep-deprived healthy mice, suggesting that at least for certain memory modalities, probiotics do not exert non-specific cognitive enhancement (Griffin et al., 2023).

It is worth noting that Alzheimer's disease is approximately twice as prevalent in women as in men, and sex differences in neuroinflammatory responses have been documented. Therefore, future studies should include female cohorts to determine whether the neuroprotective effects of L. acidophilus observed here are comparable across sexes.

Conclusion

Overall, the present findings demonstrate that Lactobacillus acidophilus exerts protective effects against cognitive impairment induced by both metabolic (STZ) and metal-related (AlCl₃) neurotoxic insults. Although direct quantitative comparison between the two models is limited by differences in exposure duration and route of administration, the probiotic consistently attenuated oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and neuronal loss in both models. These results suggest that probiotic supplementation may represent a promising preventive strategy against cognitive dysfunction associated with diverse neurotoxic conditions. However, further studies with standardized protocols are needed to directly compare the efficacy of L. acidophilus across different models and to explore its long-term effects and translational relevance.

Limitations

One limitation is the absence of a probiotic‑only control group. Thus, we cannot fully exclude the possibility that some cognitive or inflammatory changes reflect non‑specific effects of L. acidophilus rather than specific interactions with disease pathology. Although previous evidence has shown that probiotic administration alone does not alter memory performance in healthy, non‑sleep‑deprived mice (Griffin et al., 2023), direct evidence regarding locomotor activity and baseline cytokine levels (e.g., TNF‑α, IL‑6) in healthy rodents is currently lacking. Future studies should include a probiotic‑only control to confirm the specificity of our findings. We acknowledge this limitation and have interpreted our results with caution.

Additionally, the optimal pretreatment duration for L. acidophilus in this specific model has not been systematically established. The 2‑day period was selected based on pilot studies and published evidence of rapid immunomodulation (O'Flaherty and Klaenhammer, 2012, Weiss et al., 2011, Buddington et al., 2010). Future studies should systematically investigate the optimal pretreatment duration.

One potential concern is that the OFT, despite being brief and non‑aversive, may induce acute stress. However, the absence of group differences in locomotor activity suggests that any such stress was evenly distributed across groups. Future studies may consider counterbalancing test order or using a separate cohort for each behavioral test to completely eliminate order effects.

One limitation of this study is the use of only male rats. This decision was made to avoid the confounding influence of the estrous cycle on memory performance and inflammatory responses in this exploratory study. However, given the higher prevalence of Alzheimer's disease in women and evidence of sex differences in neuroinflammation, our findings may not fully generalize to females. Future studies should include both sexes to address this gap.

Finally, this study was limited to short‑term interventions (14 days) and only two neurotoxic models (STZ and AlCl₃). Long‑term effects and other Alzheimer's‑related pathological pathways were not explored. Therefore, caution is required when translating these findings to humans, and further clinical studies with longer intervention periods and additional disease‑relevant models are needed.
