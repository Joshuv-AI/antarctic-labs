Perturbative semiclassical entropy of dynamical black holes
Avinandan Mondal, Kartik Prabhu

Abstract
We consider perturbative quantum gravity as a quantum field theory of linearized metric perturbation on an asymptotically flat spacetime with a bifurcate Killing horizon. We include the perturbative gravitational constraints into the algebra of observables restricted to the right half of the future horizon of the spacetime. We use the boundary charge, associated to the horizon Killing field, as an auxiliary “observer” degree of freedom. The observables “dressed” with the additional charge are invariant under the Killing symmetry and generate a Type- II ∞ \text{II}_{\infty} von Neumann factor. We compute the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix of a classical-quantum coherent state constructed from the metric perturbations and the “observer wavefunction”. This von Neumann entropy satisfies an analogue of the first law of thermodynamics. We further show that this entropy is related to Hollands-Wald-Zhang entropy of the (second order) perturbed dynamical black hole through the flux of perturbations through the horizon and future null infinity.

Introduction

Interplay between gravity, thermodynamics and quantum information has long been a key area of investigation to get insights about quantum nature of gravity in the contexts of black hole mechanics and holographic correspondence. In the area of black hole thermodynamics, some of the fundamental insights came from the discoveries that black holes should be assigned an entropy

These have been done by employing powerful techniques of Tomita-Takesaki modular theory and some important results in mathematical physics connected to it: like the type of local QFT algebras and crossed product factors

4.2
Matter fields.

The entire analysis in the paper is unchanged if semi-classical matter is taken into account. The states will now incorporate matter perturbations as tensor products with linearized gravity states. For massless matter fields the flux terms at both the horizon and null infinity will get an additional contribution due the matter fields. For massive fields, there will be no contribution at null infinity but the “boundary charge”

Using the fact that
\ket{\omega_{h}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}

4.8

Gautam

:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}=\frac{1}{\beta}e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}f\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta\boldsymbol{X}}{2}}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}e^{-\frac{\beta\boldsymbol{X}}{2}}f^{*}\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\right)e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}

(A.1)

The derivation of
eq.

A.1
is computationally involved and it involves computing the modular operator for the state
|\hat{\omega}_{h}\rangle
in the crossed product factor
\mathfrak{A}^{\rm ext}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}
. Then one can seen that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}
fatorizes into an element of the algebra
\mathfrak{A}^{\rm ext}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
and an element of its commutant algebra
\mathfrak{A}^{\rm ext}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})^{\prime}
in
\mathscr{H}^{\rm ext}
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}^{\prime})^{-1}
where
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}\in\mathfrak{A}^{\rm ext}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
is identified with the density matrix corresponding to the state
|\hat{\omega}_{h}\rangle
in
\mathfrak{A}^{\rm ext}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
. We shall not show the details of the construction which can be found in Appendix E of
Sorce
.

Now note that
f(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta})
and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}
do not commute:

\displaystyle\Big[f(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}),\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\Big]=f^{\prime}(\mathbf{X})\Big[\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{X})\Big[\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}^{2}}{\beta^{2}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\Big]+\ldots

(A.2)

and hence the logarithm of
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}
in
eq.

A.1
will not distribute additively over the terms in the R.H.S. However, here we make a simplifying assumption that the function
f
is slowly varying, i.e. derivatives of
f
are vanishingly small (more precisely the derivatives are much smaller than the corresponding commutators in the
eq.

A.2
). In this slowly varying approximation, we have:

\displaystyle\left[f\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\right),\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\right]\approx 0

(A.3)

Thus now we can take a logarithm of
eq.

A.1
easily as the log will distribute additively over all the commuting operators and we have:

\displaystyle-\log{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}}=e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}\left[\log\beta+\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}+\beta\boldsymbol{X}-\log\left|\,f\left(\boldsymbol{X+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}}\right)\,\right|^{2}\right]e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}

(A.4)

Now,
[\boldsymbol{X},\boldsymbol{p}]=i\boldsymbol{1}
implies
e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}\boldsymbol{X}e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}=\boldsymbol{X}-\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}
and, thus:

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}\left|\,f\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\right)\,\right|^{2}e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}=\left|\,f(\boldsymbol{X})\,\right|^{2}

(A.5)

Furthermore,
e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}(\beta\boldsymbol{X})e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}=\beta\boldsymbol{X}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}
. Now, using two equivalent definitions of
Connes cocycles
(see Appendix C of
Sorce
) we have
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}=\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}
, where
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}
is the modular Hamiltonian corresponding to the state
|\omega_{h}\rangle
. Thus,
eq.

A.4
simplifies to

\displaystyle-\log{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}}=e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}(\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}})e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}+\beta\boldsymbol{X}-\log\left|\,f(\boldsymbol{X})\,\right|^{2}+\log\beta

(A.6)

Now note that separately
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}},\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}\notin\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
. However
(\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}})\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
and it follows from the fact that the operator is proportional to the derivative at identity of the Connes flow relating modular flows of
|\omega_{0}\rangle
and
|\omega_{h}\rangle
. See Appendix C of
Sorce
for more details. Furthermore, for the state
|\omega_{h}\rangle
in
\mathcal{P}^{\#}
one has
j_{\omega_{0}}(\boldsymbol{U})\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{0}}j_{\omega_{0}}(\boldsymbol{U}^{*})=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}

HI
. So,
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}=j_{\omega_{0}}(\boldsymbol{U})\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}j_{\omega_{0}}(\boldsymbol{U}^{*})
Now as
j_{\omega_{0}}(\boldsymbol{U})\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})^{\prime}
, so clearly the flows generated by
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}}
and
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}
match on
\mathfrak{A}(\mathcal{H}^{+}_{R},\omega_{0})
. Hence
eq.

A.6
can be written as:

\displaystyle-\log{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}}=e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}+\beta\boldsymbol{X}-\log\left|\,f(\boldsymbol{X})\,\right|^{2}+\log\beta

(A.7)

Now recall that the von Neumann entropy of the classical-quantum state
\ket{\hat{\omega}_{h}}
is:

S_{\text{vN}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}})=-\Tr(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}\log\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}})=-\braket{\hat{\omega}_{h}|\log\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}|\hat{\omega}_{h}}

(A.8)

Since the function
f

X

p=0

\boldsymbol{p}|f\rangle\approx 0
. Using this and that
\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}\ket{\omega_{h}}=0
, as every state has zero energy with respect to its modular Hamiltonian, we have in the slowly varying
f

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{\omega}_{h}|e^{-i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{h}}e^{i\frac{\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}}{\beta}\boldsymbol{p}}|\hat{\omega}_{h}}\approx 0

(A.9)

Now:

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{\omega}_{h}|(-\boldsymbol{H}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}})|\hat{\omega}_{h}}=\braket{\omega_{h}|\log\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}|\omega_{h}}=\braket{\omega_{h}|-\log\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}|\omega_{h}}=-S(\omega_{h}|\omega_{0})

(A.10)

where the first equality is trivial and in the second equality we have used the fact that
|\omega_{h}\rangle
is in
\mathcal{P}^{\#}
and hence
\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}^{-1}
(which follows because for
|\omega_{h}\rangle\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}

\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}}
and the fact that
\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}\boldsymbol{J}_{\omega_{h}|\omega_{0}}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\omega_{0}|\omega_{h}}^{-1}
. See sec. V.2 of
Haag

3.28

A.8

\displaystyle S_{\text{vN}}(\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}})=-S(\omega_{h}|\omega_{0})+\beta\braket{\boldsymbol{X}}_{\hat{\omega}_{h}}+S(f)+\log\beta
