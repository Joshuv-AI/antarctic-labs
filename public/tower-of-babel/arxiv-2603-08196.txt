Algorithm with variable coefficients for computing matrix inverses
Mihailo Krstić, Marko D. Petković, Kostadin Rajković, Marko Kostadinov

Abstract
We present a general scheme for the construction of new eficient generalized Schultz iterative methods for computing the inverse matrix. These methods have the form X k + 1 = X k ​ ( a 0 ( k ) ​ I + a 1 ( k ) ​ A ​ X k ) , k ∈ ℕ , X_{k+1}=X_{k}(a_{0}^{(k)}I+a_{1}^{(k)}AX_{k}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}, where A A is square real matrix and a 0 ( k ) a_{0}^{(k)} and a 0 ( k ) a_{0}^{(k)} are dynamical coefficients. We are going to present basic case of the problem, while formulas are derived analogically in other cases but are more complicated. Constructed method is optimal, meaning that coefficients are chosen in optimal way in terms of Frobenius norm. We have done some numerical testing that confirm theoretical approach. Through construction and numerical testing of method we have considered numerical stability as well. In the end, constructed method in it’s final form is numerically stable and optimal. Key words: Iterative methods, Regular Matrix, Convergence Inverses of regular matrix; Convergence analysis; Iterative methods; Frobenius norm; Quadratic optimization; Scaled Hyper-power method.

Introduction and preliminaries

The problem of computing the inverse of a matrix has played a central role in the development of numerical linear algebra. Since the early days of matrix theory, researchers have sought efficient and numerically stable procedures for determining matrix inverses, particularly for large-scale problems arising in scientific computing, engineering, and applied mathematics.

Classical direct methods, such as Gaussian elimination, were among the earliest approaches used to compute matrix inverses. However, with the rapid growth of computational applications in the twentieth century, iterative techniques became increasingly important due to their suitability for large matrices and parallel computation. Among the earliest iterative approaches are those based on power series expansions and polynomial approximations of matrix functions.

A particularly influential idea was the use of the Neumann series expansion for matrix inversion. This approach inspired a variety of iterative methods, including the well-known Schultz (or Newton) iteration introduced in the mid-twentieth century. Later developments generalized this idea into the broader class of Hyper-Power methods, which achieve higher orders of convergence by incorporating higher-degree polynomial corrections.

Further research expanded these techniques to compute not only classical matrix inverses but also generalized inverses such as the Moore–Penrose and Drazin inverses, which are essential in the analysis of singular or rectangular matrices. Over time, these developments led to the formulation of generalized Schultz-type methods based on polynomial iterations, providing a flexible framework for constructing high-order algorithms.

One of the fundamental tasks in numerical linear algebra is the computation of the inverse matrix
A^{-1}
for a given invertible matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. The vector space of all real matrices of dimension
n\times m
is denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
. For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, we denote by
\mathcal{R}(A)
its image (range) and by
\mathcal{N}(A)
its kernel (null space). In the special case when
m=n
, the symbols
\textup{tr}(A)
and
\sigma(A)
represent the trace and the spectrum of the matrix
A
, respectively. The spectral radius of
A
is defined by

r(A)=\max\{|\lambda|\,:\,\lambda\in\sigma(A)\}.

A commonly used norm on the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is the Frobenius norm
\|\cdot\|_{F}
, which is given by

\|A\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{ij}^{2}}\,\,\,\text{for every}\,\,\,A=[a_{ij}]_{i,j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}.

(1.1)

This norm is particularly convenient in numerical linear algebra applications because it can be computed directly from (
1.1
) with computational complexity
\mathcal{O}(nm)
. Such efficiency is not typical for many other matrix norms, including the spectral norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, which is defined only for square matrices.

Iterative procedures for determining the inverse matrix
A^{-1}
have been studied extensively since the pioneering work
[
7
]
. These approaches rely on truncating the infinite series expansion

(I-M)^{-1}=I+M+M^{2}+M^{3}+\cdots

which is valid for any invertible matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
whose spectral radius satisfies
r(M)<1
. From this expansion, one derives the well known Hyper-Power iterative scheme

X_{k+1}=X_{k}(I+F_{k}+\cdots+F_{k}^{d}),\,\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(1.2)

for any integer
d>1
, where
F_{k}=I-AX_{k}
denotes the residual matrix. The method converges provided that the initial matrix
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
satisfies
\rho(F_{0})<1
, and its order of convergence equals
d+1
.

A special case of (
1.2
) is obtained by choosing
d=1
. This yields the classical Schultz (Newton) iteration (see
[
7
]
)

X_{k+1}=X_{k}(2I-AX_{k}),\,\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1.3)

which will be referred to as
HP2
. Furthermore, the Hyper-Power scheme corresponding to
d=2
will be denoted by
HP3
. It has been demonstrated that the Schultz iteration (
1.3
) can also be used for computing various generalized inverses of matrices that may be singular or rectangular (see, for instance,
[
2
,
8
]
). Related results for the general Hyper-Power method (
1.2
) are presented in
[
1
,
9
,
5
]
.

A broader family of methods, known as generalized Schultz iterations of the form
X_{k+1}=X_{k}P(AX_{k})
, where
P
is a real polynomial of degree
d
, was introduced and analyzed by Petković in
[
6
]
. In that work, the necessary conditions for convergence toward a regular or generalized matrix inverse are established, together with several explicit examples of such methods.

Recently, in
[
4
]
is derived new method with variable coefficients for computing the inverse
A^{-1}
. In this paper we use the idea presented in
[
4
]
to derive method with better performances.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, a new iterative method for matrix inversion is introduced, together with Algorithms
2
and
3
. An optimization-based procedure for determining optimal coefficients of the proposed method is presented in Section
1
. The convergence analysis and error bounds are established in Section
4
. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing schemes in terms of both computational speed and accuracy.

2
Construction of the algorithm

In literature regarding computing matrix inverses have so far been considered methods of form

X_{k+1}=X_{k}P(AX_{k}),

where
P
is polynomial with constant coefficients. In this paper we are going to consider idea that coefficients may vary through iterative process in the following way:

X_{k+1}=X_{k}(a_{0}^{(k)}I+a_{1}^{(k)}AX_{k}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.1)

We are trying to minimize norm of residual matrix
F_{k}
, where:

F_{k}=I-AX_{k},

(2.2)

We have the next computations:

\displaystyle||F_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=||I-AX_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=||I-AX_{k}(a_{0}^{(k)}I+a_{1}^{(k)}F_{k})||^{2}_{F}

(2.4)

\displaystyle=||I-(I-F_{k})(a_{0}^{(k)}I+a_{1}^{(k)}F_{k})||^{2}_{F}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=||(1-a_{0}^{(k)})I+(a_{0}^{(k)}-a_{1}^{(k)})F_{k}+a_{1}^{(k)}F_{k}^{2}||^{2}_{F}.

(2.6)

Now will introduce introduce new variables
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
as

\alpha_{k}:=a_{0}^{(k)}-a_{1}^{(k)}
and
\beta_{k}:=a_{1}^{(k)}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,

F_{k+1}=(1-(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}))I+\alpha_{k}F_{k}+\beta_{k}F_{k}^{2}

(2.7)

and we get the following equation:

\displaystyle||F_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}=||(1-(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}))I+\alpha_{k}F_{k}+\beta_{k}F_{k}^{2}||^{2}_{F}.

(2.8)

Let us denote

F_{k}=\left[f_{i,j}^{(k)}\right]_{n\times n}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,F^{2}_{k}=\left[g_{i,j}^{(k)}\right]_{n\times n},

where
n
is dimension of matrix
A
, so the previous equation now looks like

||F_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left[\alpha_{k}f^{(k)}_{i,j}+\beta_{k}g^{(k)}_{i,j}\right]^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[(1+(f^{(k)}_{i,i}-1)\alpha_{k}+(g^{(k)}_{i,i}-1)\beta_{k}\right]^{2}.

(2.9)

A minimization of previous expression by variables
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
is now required to be done. The minimization process is performed analogically as in the first chapter, when we did not apply the change of the variables. The equations and the method itself become significantly simpler and numerically stabile, which will be clear from the rest of the paper.
The authors emphasize that minimization is done in each iteration of the method, which is very timing expensive operation. Since the function that we are minimizing has convenient form we can calculate the values for
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
explicitly, which enables the iterative process to execute pretty fast in each iteration. Therefore, we are solving the following problem
\min\{||F_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}:\alpha_{k},\ \beta_{k}\in\mathbb{R}\}
for all fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For this purpose we are going to consider the value
||F_{k+1}||^{2}_{F}
as
G(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k})
, where
G
is function of two variables
defined as

G(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left[xf^{(k)}_{i,j}+yg^{(k)}_{i,j}\right]^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[(1+(f^{(k)}_{i,i}-1)x+(g^{(k)}_{i,i}-1)y\right]^{2}\,\,\,\,(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

By finding the minima of function
G\in\mathcal{C^{(\infty)}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we are finding the coefficients
a_{0}^{(k)}
and
a_{1}^{(k)}
for our algorithm.
Therefore, now we are going to solve system

\frac{\partial G\left(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}\right)}{\partial x}=0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{\partial G\left(\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}\right)}{\partial y}=0,

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By grouping coefficients with variables
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
from previous two equations, we have the next system of linear equations:

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}(f_{i,j}^{(k)})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)^{2}\right]\alpha_{k}+\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}f_{i,j}^{(k)}g_{i,j}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)\right]\beta_{k}=n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i,i}^{(k)},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}f_{i,j}^{(k)}g_{i,j}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)\right]\alpha_{k}+\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}(g_{i,j}^{(k)})^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1)^{2}\right]\beta_{k}=n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i,i}^{(k)}.

(2.11)

By solving above linear system of equations, just as previously, we will get explicit equations that define the optimal coefficients

and
\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i,i}^{(k)},

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}f_{i,j}^{(k)}g_{i,j}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f_{i,i}^{(k)}-1\right)\left(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1\right),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i,i}^{(k)},

(2.15)

\displaystyle C_{10}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}f_{i,j}^{(k)}g_{i,j}^{(k)}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f_{i,i}^{(k)}-1\right)\left(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1\right),

(2.16)

\displaystyle C_{11}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(g_{i,j}^{(k)}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(g_{i,i}^{(k)}-1\right)^{2}.

(2.17)

Notice that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
following applies

This fact is convenient for more effective calculation and saves time in each iteration unlike the previous method. After solving the previous system, we get solution like as explicit formulas:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=-\frac{C_{11}^{(k)}\alpha_{k}-C_{01}^{(k)}\beta_{k}}{C_{01}^{(k)}C_{10}^{(k)}-C_{00}^{(k)}C_{11}^{(k)}},\quad\beta_{k}=-\frac{-C_{10}^{(k)}\alpha_{k}+C_{00}^{(k)}\beta_{k}}{C_{01}^{(k)}C_{10}^{(k)}-C_{00}^{(k)}C_{11}^{(k)}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.18)

On base of previous derivation we have a new method

, from Stable Scaled Hyper-power method of degree 2, described in following pseudo-code. Firstly we will provide an pseudo-code of function for determing our coefficients

(3.7)

Convergence analysis

Throughout this section we assume that we work with real matrices and that the initial iteration is of the form
X_{0}=\alpha A^{T}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a suitable constant. The main point is that the convergence analysis of our method can be treated in terms of the eigenvalues of the matrix
F_{k}
. Notice that for such a choice of
X_{0}
the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
consists of real and symmetric, implying that the eigenvalues of
F_{k}
are real. In our analysis we will consider the equivalent and simpler form of the method.

Denote by
\sigma(F_{k})=\{\lambda_{i,k}:i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

From (
2.7
) we get (see Theorem 1.1.6 in
[
3
]
):

\lambda_{i,k+1}=1-(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k})+\alpha_{k}\lambda_{i,k}+\beta_{k}\lambda_{i,k}^{2}\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(4.1)

We will now repeat the process of obtaining
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
with these new relations. We have already commented that
\|F_{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}=f_{k}(x,y)
is a quadratic function of two real variables
x
and
y
, which can be written as

\displaystyle f_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-(x+y))+x\lambda_{i,k}+y\lambda_{i,k}^{2}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+x(\lambda_{i,k}-1)+y(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)\right)^{2}.

(4.2)

The graph of
f_{k}
will be an elliptic paraboloid, so the local extrema will be local minima. Differentiating
f_{k}(x,y)
with respect to
x
and
y
, respectively, we get:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\left(1+x(\lambda_{i,k}-1)+y(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)\right)(\lambda_{i,k}-1)

\displaystyle=2\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}-1)+x\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}-1)^{2}+y\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)(\lambda_{i,k}-1)\right]

\displaystyle=2\left[\textup{tr}(F_{k}-I)+x\|F_{k}-I\|_{F}^{2}+y\textup{tr}\left((F_{k}+I)(F_{k}-I)^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\left(1+x(\lambda_{i,k}-1)+y(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)\right)(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=2\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)+x\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}-1)^{2}(\lambda_{i,k}+1)+y\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lambda_{i,k}^{2}-1)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\left[\textup{tr}(F_{k}^{2}-I)+x\textup{tr}\left((F_{k}-I)^{2}(F_{k}-I)\right)+y\|F_{k}^{2}-I\|_{F}^{2}\right].

The values of
x
and
y
that minimize (
4.2
) are found by solving the system

\displaystyle\|F_{k}-I\|_{F}^{2}\cdot x

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\textup{tr}\left((F_{k}+I)(F_{k}-I)^{2}\right)\cdot y

\displaystyle=-\textup{tr}(F_{k}-I)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\textup{tr}\left((F_{k}-I)^{2}(F_{k}-I)\right)\cdot x

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|F_{k}^{2}-I\|_{F}^{2}\cdot y

\displaystyle=-\textup{tr}(F_{k}^{2}-I)

The solution to this system, which will give us the desired values
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
is

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\|F_{k}^{2}-I\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(F_{k}-I)+\textup{tr}(F_{k}^{2}-I)\textup{tr}((F_{k}^{2}-I)(F_{k}-I))}{\|F_{k}-I\|_{F}^{2}\|F_{k}^{2}-I\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((F_{k}^{2}-I)(F_{k}-I))\right)^{2}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})-\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))}{\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))\right)^{2}},

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\|F_{k}-I\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(F_{k}^{2}-I)+\textup{tr}(F_{k}-I)\textup{tr}((F_{k}^{2}-I)(F_{k}-I))}{\|F_{k}-I\|_{F}^{2}\|F_{k}^{2}-I\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((F_{k}^{2}-I)(F_{k}-I))\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})-\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))}{\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))\right)^{2}},

provided that
\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))\right)^{2}\neq 0
, otherwise
\alpha_{k}=0
,
\beta_{k}=1
. For simplicity, we will denote the denominator in ((
4.4
)) by
D_{k}
, and the numerators by
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
, respectively.
We must again emphasize that the formulas (
4.4
) are equivalent to (
2.18
), and were derived primarily for the convergence analysis of the method.

The following result provides some basic properties of the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be an invertible matrix. The following holds for the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:

(a)

If
D_{k}\neq 0
, then
\textup{tr}(F_{k+1}(I-F_{k}))=0
and
\textup{tr}(F_{k+1}(I-F_{k}^{2}))=0
;

(b)

If
D_{k-1}\neq 0
, then
\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\textup{tr}(F_{k})
and
\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

(c)

If
D_{k-1}\neq 0
, then
\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=n
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

(d)

\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}-\|F_{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}=(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
D_{k}\neq 0
;

(e)

if
D_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N_{0}}
then
(\|F_{k}\|_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}})
is a non-increasing sequence,
\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}
is a non-decreasing sequence and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}=\sqrt{n-L^{2}}.

Proof.

(a)

From (
2.7
) and the fact that
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
are unique solutions to the system of equations (
4.3
) we have that:

\displaystyle\textup{tr}(F_{k+1}(I-F_{k}))

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}((I-\alpha_{k}(I-F_{k})-\beta_{k}(I-F_{k}^{2}))(I-F_{k})

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})-\alpha_{k}\textup{tr}((I-F_{k})^{2})-\beta_{k}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})-\underbrace{(\alpha_{k}\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\beta_{k}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))}_{=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})}=0,

\displaystyle\textup{tr}(F_{k+1}(I-F_{k}^{2}))

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}((I-\alpha_{k}(I-F_{k})-\beta_{k}(I-F_{k}^{2}))(I-F_{k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})-\alpha_{k}\textup{tr}((I-F_{k})(I-F_{k}^{2}))-\beta_{k}\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})^{2})

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})-\underbrace{(\alpha_{k}\textup{tr}((I-F_{k})(I-F_{k}^{2}))+\beta_{k}\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}}_{=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})}=0.

(b)

From part
(a)
it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(F_{k}(I-\alpha_{k-1}(I-F_{k-1})-\beta_{k-1}(I-F_{k-1}^{2})))

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(F_{k})-\alpha_{k-1}\underbrace{\textup{tr}(F_{k}(I-F_{k-1})}_{=0}-\beta_{k-1}\underbrace{\textup{tr}(F_{k}(I-F_{k-1})^{2}))}_{=0}=\textup{tr}(F_{k})

and

\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\textup{tr}((I-F_{k})^{2})=\textup{tr}(I-2F_{k}+F_{k}^{2})=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}),

provided that
D_{k-1}\neq 0
.

(c)

From the previous part we have that

\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\textup{tr}(F_{k})+\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})=\textup{tr}(I)=n.

(d)

Using the result from part
b)
we get:

\displaystyle\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}-\|F_{k+1}\|_{F}^{2}=\textup{tr}(F_{k})-\textup{tr}(F_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(F_{k})-\textup{tr}(I-\alpha_{k}(I-F_{k})-\beta_{k}(I-F_{k}^{2}))

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}(-(I-F_{k})+\alpha_{k}(I-F_{k})+\beta_{k}(I-F_{k}))

\displaystyle=\textup{tr}((\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)(I-F_{k}))=(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})=(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}

(e)

By construction of the method we have that the sequence
(\|F_{k}\|_{F})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is non-increasing, and from part
c)
we see that
\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=n-\|F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}
which implies that
(\|I-F_{k}\|_{F})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is non-decreasing, and we have the equality
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}=\sqrt{n-L^{2}}
.

The results of Theorem
4.1
are in the case when
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
can be determined uniquely, which may now always be the case. Before we proceed with a more detailed analysis of the convergence of the method
SSHP2
let us examine the critical case when
D_{k}=0
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Let
k_{0}
be the first non-negative integer for which
D_{k}=0
.
By construction, we have that

\|F_{k_{0}}\|_{F}\leq\|F_{k_{0}-1}\|_{F}\leq\dots\leq\|F_{0}\|\leq n\cdot r(F_{0}).

From the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality it follows that
D_{k_{0}}
will be equal to
0
if and only if
I-F_{k_{0}}
and
I-F_{k_{0}}^{2}
are co-linear, that is if
I-F_{k_{0}}^{2}=c(I-F_{k_{0}})
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. This further implies that
1-\lambda_{i,k_{0}}^{2}=c(1-\lambda_{i,k_{0}})
for
i=1,\dots,n
from which it follows that

\lambda_{i,k_{0}}=c-1\text{ or }\lambda_{i,k}=1,\quad i=1,\dots,n.

This means that there exists a (possibly trivial) orthogonal projection
P
such that

F_{k_{0}}=(c-1)P+I-P.

However, since
D_{k_{0}-1}\neq 0
, it follows by part
b)
of Theorem
4.1
that
\textup{tr}(I-F_{k_{0}}^{2})=\textup{tr}(I-F_{k_{0}})
, which in turn implies that
c=1
, so

F_{k_{0}}=I-P.

From the definition of the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, it follows that all subsequence iterations will also be equal to the projection
I-P
.

This analysis leads us to the following Theorems:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be an invertible matrix and suppose that
D_{k}=0
for some

. Then the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to an idempotent matrix. If
1\not\in\sigma(F_{k})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
the iterative method
SSHP2
converges.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be an invertible matrix. The following holds for the sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:

(a)

The sequence
\|F_{k}\|_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is non-increasing and convergent.

(b)

\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}\geq 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k})=1
.

Proof.

(a)

This result follows directly from Theorem
4.1
and the analysis of the case when
D_{k}=0
.

(b)

From part
(d)
of Theorem
4.1
and the fact that
(\|F_{k}\|_{F})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a non-increasing sequence we see that
(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}
is non-negative when
D_{k}\neq 0
, so
\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}\geq 1
when
D_{k}\neq 0
. If
D_{k_{0}}=0
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
then
\alpha_{k}+\beta_{1}=1
for all
k\geq k_{0}
.
We conclude that if there exists a non-negative integer
k_{0}
such that
D_{k_{0}}=0
then
(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
trivially converges to
1
, and if
D_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it follows follows from part
(a)
that
(\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1)\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}
converges to
0
, and since
\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}
is positive and bounded it follows that
\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k}-1\to 1
as
k\to\infty
.

Remark 4.1
.

Because of Theorem
4.2
we will primarily study the iterative process
SSHP2
with the additional condition that
D_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
without loss of generality.

Notice that the formulas (
4.4
) can be rewritten in a form that will allow us to analyze their behaviour in a different way. For simplicity, let
A_{k}
denote the denominator of
\alpha_{k}
,
B_{k}
the numerator of
\beta_{k}
and
D_{k}
their common denominator (and
D_{k}\geq 0
due to the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality). Notice that

\displaystyle A_{k}=\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})-\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})\cdot\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))=

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})^{2}-(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j.k}^{2})\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})-(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k}(1+\lambda_{j,k}-1-\lambda_{i,k})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j.k}-\lambda_{i,k}).

If we denote the
(i,j)
-th element of the sum above by
C_{i,j}
we see that

A_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(C_{i,j}+C_{j,i}).

Since

\displaystyle C_{j,i}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{i,k})(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})=-(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{i,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k}),

we see that

\displaystyle C_{i,j}+C_{j,i}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j.k}-\lambda_{i,k})-(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{i,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})\left((1-\lambda_{j,k})(1+\lambda_{j,k}-(1-\lambda_{i,k)(1-\lambda_{i,k}}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})\left(-\lambda_{j,k}^{2}+\lambda_{i,k}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})(\lambda_{j,k}^{2}-\lambda_{i,j}^{2})

\displaystyle=-(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}.

From our calculations we conclude that

A_{k}=-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}.

(4.5)

Also, if sum over all indices
i
and
j
each summand from (
4.5
) will be counted twice, and since the sum above is symmetric with respect to
i
and
j
we get the equivalent formula

A_{k}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}.

(4.6)

Next, let us rewrite
B_{k}
as well:

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\textup{tr}(I-F_{k}^{2})-\textup{tr}(I-F_{k})\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}-(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})\left[(1+\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})-(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}(1+\lambda_{i,k}-1-\lambda_{j,k})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k}).

Let
E_{ij}
denote the
(i,j)
-th element of the sum above, so

B_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(E_{i,j}+E_{j,i}).

As

E_{j,i}=(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k})=-(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i.k}-\lambda_{j,k}),

it stands that

\displaystyle E_{i,j}+E_{j,i}

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})-(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i.k}-\lambda_{j,k})

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}-(1-\lambda_{i,k}))

\displaystyle=(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}.

From the previous calculations we conclude that

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{i,k}-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}.

(4.7)

Finally, we can express
D_{k}
as:

\displaystyle D_{k}=\|I-F_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\|I-F_{k}^{2}\|_{F}^{2}-\left(\textup{tr}((I-F_{k}^{2})(I-F_{k}))\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})^{2}-(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,j})(1-\lambda_{j,k})^{2}\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})-(1-\lambda_{i,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{j,k})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})\left[(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1+\lambda_{j,k})(1+\lambda_{j,k})-(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1+\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})(1-\lambda_{i,k})(1-\lambda_{j,k})(1+\lambda_{j,k}-1-\lambda_{i,k})=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i,k})^{2}(1-\lambda_{j,k}^{2})(1+\lambda_{j,k})(\lambda_{j,k}-\lambda_{i,k}).

If

5
Conclusion and further research

Constructed method presents a new approach in computing of matrix inverses. Construction of the method is based on optimization of a non-linear function. As that function is quadratic in this case, we could derive explicit expressions for computing the coefficients. Some of the questions are still open, like:

•

How to transform the method so it works on generalized inverses?

•

Is it possible to create general scheme for construction of methods with any number of coefficients and how would those methods work in practice, while having in mind that expressions are complicated even in the simplest case?

•

Is it possible to avoid the use of the heuristic in the method?

•

Which method from the presented class of methods is optimal?

The last question is naturaly imposed, since with adding coefficients we are increasing speed of convergence while the complexity and number of calculations in each iteration is increased as well. However, from the theoretical point of view, the following problem is definitely interesting for future research. This and many other questions regarding the presented class of methods open
great possibilites for further research.
