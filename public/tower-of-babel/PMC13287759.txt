Cross-attention guided explainable deep transformer model for multi-level classification of rare neurological disorders using MRI images
Authors: Preethi Veerlapalli, E. Laxmi Lydia, K. Radhika, Kancherla R. Krishna Naga Surya Alekhya, Faten Khalid Karim, Mohamad Khairi Ishak, Samih M. Mostafa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Neurological disorders (ND) impact a significant number of the population all over the world, affecting the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves. These disorders are classified as NeuroDegenerative, NeuroBiological, and NeuroDevelopmental disorders, which vary from common to rare disorders. Traditional deep learning methods often fail to generalize efficiently in these settings and are developed for common or single-label disease classification. Also, the insufficient interpretability in these models can decrease the medical professionals’ trust and utilization. Therefore, this study introduces a Cross-Attention Guided Explainable Deep Transformer Model for Multi-Level Classification of Rare Neurological Disorders (CAXDT-MLCRND) model. The proposed model involves the integration of convolutional neural network for local feature representation with transformer-based network to capture global contextual dependencies. Besides, a new cross-attention strategy is applied for enabling dynamic interaction among local and global representations, enabling the model to effectively distinguish visually similar rare disease patterns. Moreover, the derived features are fused with complementary representations from convolutional neural networks within a soft voting-based ensemble model, which integrates a Graph Neural Network (GNN), Deep Belief Network (DBN), and Stacked Autoencoder (SAE) to boost the robustness and generalization of the classification process. Additionally, Bayesian optimization (BO) is leveraged to fine-tune the hyperparameters of the DL approach for improved performance. For assuring clinical reliability, explainability is incorporated using Eigen Class Activation Mapping (EigenCAM), offering visual understanding into model prediction and improving clinical interpretability. The experimental results of the CAXDT-MLCRND system are validated utilizing open access MRI dataset, and the results report the enhanced multi-label classification performance on the simultaneous prediction of co-existing neurological conditions. Therefore, the proposed model is found to be a robust and interpretable approach to assist clinical applications for rare neurological disorder detection in resource-limited environments. Moreover, the source code used in this study is publicly available at: https://github.com/researcher010-debug/NeurologicalDisorderClassification.git.

Introduction

Neurological disorders (ND) encompass an extensive range of conditions that impacts the central and peripheral nervous systems, varying from the most prevalent to the rarest diseases. The majority of these disorders show significant cognitive, sensory, and motor instabilities1. Specific cases are moderately fast progression, whereas others grow almost gradually over a period of years. Especially rare ND give unique diagnostic complexity because of their lower occurrence and generally early noticeable medical indications2. This may result in diagnostic delay, thus treatments, and progressive decline in patient outcomes and life quality. Therefore, earlier identification of these medical conditions is highly significant in the effective diagnosis and also diminishes the impact on the congested medical field that can be complicated with an improved requirement for specific medical diagnostics3. The Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is main analytical tool present in the ND that permits the origin of comprehensive assessments on brain structures without depending on invasive methods4. It can exhibit the minor variations in brain structure that are vital to identify the earlier medial indication of ND, which might barely be analyzed through the alternative methods. Alternatively, the MRI image analysis needs wide-ranging proficiency and is also time-consuming5. The manual type of MRI analysis also presents a level of inconsistency over the explanations, impacted by the expertise of the radiologist. In rare ND, this inter-observer inconsistency could complicate errors and delays, particularly in conditions where imaging patterns are uncertain or unfamiliar because of inadequate preceding reports of the patients6.

Newly, an innovative concept of an automatic computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) technique is used for neurologists to identify the ND from larger clinical data7. The method of substantial CAD model is designed by employing the pattern recognition methods and schemes, and accordingly, the CAD is ranked as one of the pattern recognition methods. The algorithm is utilized in the CAD systems, namely data classification, feature extraction, and pre-processing8. The CAD outcomes support medical experts in efficiently analyzing the massive amount of clinical data, thus increasing the diagnosis accuracy and constancy whereas decreasing the diagnostic duration9. The CAD technique is employed by medical specialists in the identification and treatment of ND as a decision-supportive approach. The gained biomedical imagery originates from the medical image that must be processed at the phase of pre-processing to eliminate the noise and decrease the computational time of CAD methods10. The most crucial components of the CAD system are the feature representation approach that can be used to acquire the bio-markers. The acquired feature vectors have been employed as input in the classifier for assigning the candidate to the accessible classes that depend on the classification model for CAD systems11. Deep learning (DL) plays an important part in the domain of ND, mainly in neuroimaging methods. The most prevalent techniques that could be utilized to process the medical images by implementing the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and must be leveraged for evaluating the medical images, include the MRI scanning system12.

Research contribution

This study projects a Cross-Attention Guided Explainable Deep Transformer Model for Multi-Level Classification of Rare Neurological Disorders (CAXDT-MLCRND) model. The goal of this study is to classify rare neurological disorders using biomedical images. The proposed CAXDT-MLCRND model offers a novel unified framework which incorporates cross-attention-driven CNN–Transformer feature fusion with a heterogeneous, Bayesian-optimized ensemble, enabling dynamic interaction between local and global representations. This synergistic design considerably improves discriminative ability and interpretability, addressing critical limitations in existing neuroimaging-based deep learning models for rare disorder classification. Extensive evaluations verify that the proposed CAXDT-MLCRND model significantly outperforms recent models. The following are key contributions of this paper.

Existing research works on rare neurological disorders

This section presents an overview of prior studies on rare neurological disorders. Ness et al.13 presented the AI-based techniques for identifying, categorizing, and analyzing the genetic types of ND by employing the multi-omics data. This method shows that the usage of AI techniques, particularly in Graph-Based and Deep Learning models, unswervingly exceeds the standard statistical techniques in recognizing disease-related genomic mutations for disease risk prediction, and computing the correlation among phenotype and genotype. Incorporation of various omics has been indicated to majorly increase the diagnostic precision and biological insight related to one form of omics, thus permitting higher disease categorization and timely detection. Almasi14 designed cutting-edge progress in machine-vision pipelines for quantification, classification, and identification of movement patterns relevant to ND. The key technological elements include markerless pose assessment model and feature extraction methods (spectral, temporal kinematic), and ML methods. Later, the evaluation of disorder-based applications and their assessment metrics, datasets, and medical validation procedures. Nife et al.15 developed a self-supervised CNN architecture for the earlier identification and identification of rare ND under the MRI images gained from the brain. Earlier identification of these diseases are significant for efficient interventions and increases the patient lives. This technique utilizes self-supervised learning to improve the capability of the model for extracting useful aspects from brain MRI images, even with inadequate labeled cases. The pipeline comprises the phases, namely training, model compilation, data preprocessing, and augmentation, which support increasing the accuracy of the model and generalization.

Alatawi et al.16 inspected the contribution of ferroptosis in rare ND mechanisms and concentrated on its role in neuronal dysfunction and oxidative damage. The recent medical developments, like antioxidant-based approaches, lipid peroxidation blockers, and iron chelators, can be used to target ferroptosis. Although such methods exhibit optimism, complexities like smaller patient cohorts, inadequate analytical tools, and disease heterogeneity development. Pan et al.17 implemented an innovative prediction architecture that depends on the DL to recognize the possible drug treatments for Alzheimer’s disease (AD). These prediction techniques are used to make a DTP model reliant on the numerous targeted aspects. In18, an advanced combined technique was introduced that integrates the benefits of phase space reconstruction and DNN. This can be used to access openly accessible EEG datasets, modifying the intrinsic biases of select datasets and, more precisely, the intricate EEG patterns related to depression. Concurrently, the combination of a DNN model ensures precise, unified classification and optimum effectiveness.

Iqbal et al.19 implemented the DL, especially concentrated on multimodal neuroimaging systems, and a field that has experienced fast development and gathered major systematic attention. This method classifies and significantly evaluates various DL techniques, namely LSTM-CNN, VGG, and GAN CNNs, to comprehend their outcome over diverse forms of ND. Additionally, the efficiency of DL in real-time medical situations highlights its ability to transform ND identification and treatments. Sachidananda Murthy and Prasad20 introduced a new methodology that aggregates blockchain (BT) technology with DL strategies to improve the identification of ND. This technique uses the decentralized and clear type of BT to firmly store and exchange clinical data, thus allowing unified collaboration amongst medical providers, patients, and research workers. This architecture confirms privacy, data integrity, and availability, thereby addressing the major issues in clinical data management.

Wu et al.21 explored the traditional methods like logistic regression, SVM, and basic neural networks offer baseline performance but fight with difficult feature extraction. Recent methods using CNNs and attention-based algorithms have meaningfully better accuracy by capturing spatial and contextual data. The research in22 focused on DL based frameworks to overcome the limitations of traditional diagnostic approaches. Attention mechanisms and mixture-of-experts techniques became extensively accepted to better feature demonstration and classification accuracy. Dynamic routing strategies further improved method flexibility by choosing the best feature pathways. These progressive architectures have established strong performance over several benchmark database. Furthermore, tasks like dataset variability, method complexity, and generalization through clinical environments still remain, indicating the need for more scalable and robust solutions. The authors in23 leveraged machine learning and deep learning models to develop early diagnosis using MRI data. Hybrid architectures merging DenseNet and residual networks take exposed strong performance by taking both low-level and high-level features. Attique Khan et al.24 integrated CNN-based methods with attention mechanisms, have shown better performance in removing discriminative features. In25, attention mechanisms and dynamic routing are employed to better feature illustration and classification accuracy. Multi-stage architectures with auxiliary classifiers have greater early-stage detection capabilities. While such algorithms reach high performance over several datasets, they often include maximum computational cost and longer inference time. This limits their applicability in real-time clinical environments, indicating the need for additional efficient and scalable solutions26.

Summary of methods on rare neurological disorders.

Recent state of art studies utilized deep learning methods, mainly CNNs and hybrid CNN-transformer algorithms, for automated diagnosis. These models syndicate local feature extraction with global attention mechanisms to enhance classification accuracy. In addition, existing approaches often lack an effective mechanism to dynamically combine multi-scale representations that is essential to differentiate subtle and visually overlapping patterns in rare neurological disorders. Moreover, most existing frameworks do not incorporate optimized hybrid architectures with explainability and robust ensemble learning in a unified manner. For resolving these drawbacks, the proposed model employs cross-attention-guided fusion strategy, which allows adaptive interaction between local and global features, combined with an optimized ensemble framework to boost classification accuracy and clinical interpretability. Research design

The proposed CAXDT-MLCRND approach follows a comprehensive framework for identifying rare neurological disorders that blends the process of preprocessing, feature extraction, classification, optimization, and explainability. Figure 1 presents the complete overview of the proposed CAXDT-MLCRND framework. The proposed model begins with image preprocessing, where a bilateral filter (BF) and CLAHE are enhancing the image quality. Then, the feature extraction is performed using CNN-based transformer with cross-attention technique. The extracted features are passed to soft voting-based ensemble classification that incorporates a GNN for capturing relational and structural patterns, a DBN learns the hierarchical features, and SAE to perform dimensionality reduction. Furthermore, the BO is used to adjust the hyperparameters of model. Finally, the EigenCAM is leveraged to highlight the most relevant regions of input images and influence the model’s prediction.

Cross attention-based feature extraction

The cross-attention guided CNN-Transformer is employed for feature extraction, integrating CNN and transformer with cross-attention, where the CNN captures local patterns and the transformer models global dependencies, allowing the combination of complementary features from multiple sources.

CNN: The CNN model provides major benefits over conventional image processing techniques by automatically learning appropriate features using the input images29. In contrast to manual feature extraction, the CNNs have gained intricate spatial patterns and decreased the noise efficiently. This framework generally comprises convolutional layers for representing features, pooling layers for lessening dimensions, and fully connected (FC) layers for final model. Batch normalization (BN) increases the training stability and convergence speed.

Model interpretability via Eigen-CAM

Eigen-CAM highlights the critical regions of an image that drive a model’s predictions. This visualization improves interpretability by revealing the features most relevant to the classification outcome. This method aims to exhibit the image parts that are more important for a particular class predictive provided by the CNN model35. The basic idea of Eigen-CAM is to utilize the eigenvectors of the last feature maps of convolutional layer to measure the weights essential to create the heatmap. The eigenvectors have been determined by applying the covariance matrix of feature maps that denotes the correlation among different feature mapping. Conclusion

This study has introduced a CAXDT-MLCRND model for identifying rare neurological disorders utilizing medical images to assist in precise diagnosis and improve patient care. Primarily, the proposed model starts with preprocessing using a BF and CLAHE to enhance the input image quality. Then, the convolutional neural network-based transformer with a cross-attention mechanism is applied to capture both local and global patterns and integrate complementary information for improved classification. Moreover, the soft voting-based ensemble model integrates a GNN, DBN, and SAE for improving the generalization of the classification process. Furthermore, BO is applied to modify the hyperparameters of the DL technique for enhanced performance. To ensure clinical reliability, EigenCAM is integrated to highlight important regions in the input images. The simulation outcomes of the proposed model are evaluated on an open-access MRI dataset, with results indicating superior multi-label classification for predicting multiple co-existing neurological disorders. Consequently, the proposed model provides a robust and interpretable solution for clinical applications, facilitating the detection of rare neurological disorders in resource-limited settings.

Although the proposed method exhibits effective performance of the, more than a few limitations exist. The database hurts from class imbalance owing to rarity of certain neurological disorders, which may lead to unfair predictions toward common classes. Moreover, the limited accessibility of high-quality annotated MRI data can affect the algorithm’s generalization across diverse clinical settings. The integration of CNN, transformer, and ensemble components also increases computational complexity, which may restrict deployment in real-time or resource-constrained environments. Though EigenCAM recovers interpretability, it may not completely capture all aspects of the model’s decision-making process. Future work will focus on addressing class imbalance problem using advanced approach such as data augmentation, cost-sensitive learning, and synthetic data generation. Further validation on multi-center and real-world datasets will be carried out to develop robustness and generalizability. Moreover, more progressive explainability approaches and the combination of multimodal clinical data will be explored to improve diagnostic accuracy and clinical applicability.
