Hierarchical semantic extraction and heterogeneous graph neural networks for event-driven time series forecasting
Authors: Caichun Cen, Hongyan Liu, Beigu Hu, Shang Cao, Ziqi Qu, Yanyan Liang, Zeling Xu, Yuntao Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEvent-driven time series forecasting constitutes a fundamental challenge across multiple scientific domains. Existing deep learning approaches rely on the assumption of historical pattern continuity and thus exhibit inadequate responsiveness to exogenous shocks, while mainstream text-enhanced methods encode documents as singular dense vectors, causing the compression of fine-grained semantic information such as entity stances, event types, and causal propagation relationships. To address these limitations, this study proposes a tripartite methodological framework building upon multimodal graph neural networks and the Temporal Fusion Transformer architecture. The first component employs large language models to perform hierarchical semantic extraction that decomposes unstructured text into four structured layers comprising entity stance quantification, event ontology mapping, inter-event causal chain reasoning, and aggregate sentiment indicators, thereby preserving prediction-relevant information that would otherwise be lost through flattened vectorization. The second component constructs heterogeneous information graphs containing multiple node types and relation types to explicitly model cumulative effects, propagation effects, and synergistic effects among events through relational graph convolutional networks with relation-aware attention mechanisms. The third component utilizes semantic analysis to achieve rapid environmental state identification, enabling dynamic recalibration of feature importance weights according to prevailing conditions and thereby addressing the non-stationarity inherent in event-driven prediction tasks. Experimental validation using crude oil price forecasting as a representative scenario demonstrates that the proposed framework achieves 17.7% improvement over baseline methods in overall prediction accuracy, with improvement reaching 34.7% during event-intensive periods, while ablation studies confirm independent contributions from each component. The proposed methodology possesses domain generality and can be transferred to other event-driven prediction tasks including epidemic spread forecasting, supply chain risk assessment, and financial market analysis.Keywords: Event-driven time series forecasting, Hierarchical semantic extraction, Heterogeneous graph neural networks, Dynamic feature selection, Large language models, Multimodal information fusionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Time series forecasting constitutes a fundamental predictive task across diverse scientific and engineering domains, yet conventional approaches constructed upon the assumption of historical pattern continuity exhibit inherent limitations when confronted with exogenous shock events that disrupt established temporal dynamics1. Deep learning architectures including long short-term memory networks, gated recurrent units, and Transformer-based models have achieved substantial advances in capturing nonlinear dependencies and long-range temporal correlations2,3, demonstrating considerable improvements over traditional statistical methods across multiple evaluation metrics4,5. However, the prediction logic of these approaches fundamentally relies on the continuity of historical patterns, rendering them inadequately responsive to sudden environmental changes triggered by external events such as geopolitical conflicts, policy shifts, and systemic disruptions6,7, while precisely such events constitute critical drivers of dramatic fluctuations in numerous real-world prediction scenarios, and this methodological bottleneck constrains the practical applicability of pure time series models in event-sensitive domains.

Integrating unstructured textual information represents a promising paradigm for addressing this limitation, as news reports and official announcements carry rich semantic signals about impending events8. Pre-trained language models such as FinBERT and the GPT series have substantially advanced textual semantic analysis through deep contextual understanding acquired from massive corpora9,10, yet the effective transformation of textual semantic information into prediction-relevant features remains insufficiently established, with three fundamental challenges persisting across current methodological approaches.

The first challenge concerns semantic information compression during text vectorization. Mainstream methods encode documents as singular dense vectors, wherein fine-grained semantic elements including entity stances, event categories, impact directions, and severity gradations are compressed and lost during the encoding process10, rendering models incapable of distinguishing event types that share surface-level semantic similarity yet differ substantially in their downstream impacts. The second challenge involves the inadequate modeling of network effects among events. Real-world event dynamics exhibit complex interactive patterns wherein multiple events produce cumulative effects through temporal clustering, propagation effects through dependency networks, and synergistic effects through mutual amplification or offset11, yet existing approaches treat events as isolated inputs and struggle to capture such structured interaction mechanisms. The third challenge pertains to feature importance dynamics under non-stationary conditions. The predictive value of different information sources varies significantly according to environmental states, with technical indicators dominating during stable periods while event-related features become paramount during turbulent periods6,7, and static feature fusion strategies employed by current methods represent suboptimal compromises across heterogeneous states rather than adaptive optimization tailored to prevailing conditions.

These three challenges expose complementary blind spots in the two dominant families of prior methods. Pure time-series deep learning models such as Informer, Autoformer, and the Temporal Fusion Transformer derive their predictions from the continuity of historical numerical patterns and therefore cannot respond to exogenous shocks before any statistical anomaly emerges in the price series; augmenting these models with a flattened BERT or FinBERT sentiment scalar partially mitigates this gap but discards the entity-, event-category-, severity-, and causal-chain-level information that distinguishes high-impact from low-impact events10. Pure graph-based methods such as MAGNN instead encode static entity associations, capturing who is structurally related to whom but not how shocks propagate through time, with what lag, and through which causal mechanism11,12. Neither family provides a mechanism to recalibrate feature importance across market regimes, despite empirical evidence that the predictive value of technical and event-related features moves in opposite directions across these regimes6,7,13.

Crude oil price forecasting provides a representative validation scenario that simultaneously encompasses the three aforementioned challenges. The formation mechanism of crude oil prices involves nonlinear coupling among supply-demand fundamentals, geopolitical dynamics, financial speculation, and macroeconomic policies6,7, with price series exhibiting pronounced non-stationarity, heavy-tailed distributions, and volatility clustering1. Geopolitical events in energy markets demonstrate complex semantic structures wherein production announcements from different oil-producing nations carry order-of-magnitude differences in marginal market impacts, multi-hop causal chains connect initial triggers through intermediate transmission nodes to ultimate price effects, and market states transition between calm periods characterized by fundamental-driven pricing and crisis periods dominated by risk sentiment14. These characteristics render crude oil price prediction an ideal testbed for validating methodological innovations addressing semantic information preservation, event network modeling, and adaptive feature selection.

To address the three identified challenges, this study proposes a tripartite methodological framework building upon the Metapath Aggregated Graph Neural Network (MAGNN)12 and the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT)15, as illustrated in Fig 1. At the semantic extraction level, a four-layer hierarchical architecture leverages large language models to decompose unstructured text into structured representations comprising entity stance quantification, event ontology mapping with severity assessment, inter-event causal chain reasoning, and aggregate sentiment indicators, thereby preserving fine-grained information that flattened vectorization would otherwise discard. At the graph modeling level, a heterogeneous information graph containing multiple node types representing information sources, institutional actors, and discrete events along with multiple edge types encoding temporal associations, causal propagation, and structural dependencies enables explicit characterization of cumulative, propagation, and synergistic effects through relational graph convolutional networks with relation-aware attention mechanisms. At the feature fusion level, a semantic-driven state identification module combining large language model analysis with hidden Markov temporal modeling achieves rapid environmental state recognition, enabling dynamic recalibration of feature importance weights and adaptive selection of prediction-relevant information subsets for input to the Temporal Fusion Transformer. The contribution of this study lies not in any individual building block, all of which build upon established prior work, but in the joint formulation of these three components as an integrated framework for event-driven forecasting, whose empirical value is substantiated by the ablation, cross-asset, and state-of-the-art comparison experiments reported in Section "Experiments and results" and Supplementary Sections S1 through S3. The three component innovations are transferable to other event-driven prediction tasks including epidemic spread forecasting, supply chain risk assessment, and broader financial market analysis.

Related work

Financial text semantic representation methods

Financial texts contain rich market sentiment and event information, and their effective extraction is of significant value for energy price forecasting. Early studies employed the Loughran-McDonald financial sentiment lexicon to annotate news texts, constructing sentiment indices through statistical analysis of positive and negative word frequencies and combining them with traditional machine learning methods such as support vector machines to achieve joint modeling of sentiment features and price series, with related work validating the feasibility of integrating textual information into time series prediction and establishing methodological foundations for subsequent research16. The emergence of pre-trained language models has brought paradigmatic innovation to financial text analysis, as BERT acquired deep semantic understanding capabilities through masked language modeling on large-scale corpora17, domain-adapted models such as FinBERT achieved substantial accuracy improvements over traditional methods in financial sentiment classification tasks9, and semantic features extracted based on pre-trained models demonstrated stable performance gains across multiple prediction tasks including crude oil prices and stock returns18, with related achievements having been widely applied and validated in energy economics and financial engineering domains. However, document-level vectorization compresses fine-grained semantic elements such as entity stances and event types into a single representation19, motivating recent interest in leveraging the structured-output capabilities of large language models to recover this hierarchical semantic information20.

Multimodal graph neural network prediction methods

Complex interdependencies exist among entities in financial markets, and effective modeling of these relationships is of significant importance for prediction performance improvement. Knowledge graph technology provides effective tools for structured representation of entity relationships, organizing knowledge scattered across unstructured texts into computable graph structures by representing entities as nodes and relationships as edges21. Graph neural networks perform information aggregation on graph structures through message passing mechanisms22, graph convolutional networks achieve weighted fusion of neighborhood features using spectral or spatial domain convolutions23, and graph attention networks adaptively learn importance weights of neighboring nodes through attention mechanisms24, with both approaches achieving notable success in financial risk assessment, credit scoring, and supply chain analysis tasks. Multimodal heterogeneous graph neural network methods further integrate the aforementioned techniques, wherein the MAGNN framework achieves unified representation of multi-source information by constructing heterogeneous graph structures that fuse historical price series, news text embeddings, and entity knowledge graphs12, employing metapath-based neighborhood aggregation strategies to capture high-order semantic correlations among different types of nodes25, and demonstrating favorable prediction performance and interpretability in stock trend prediction and financial event detection tasks, thereby providing a referenceable technical framework for multimodal financial forecasting. Existing graph methods predominantly rely on static entity associations and provide limited representation of the temporal and causal evolution of event impacts26. Introducing causal and temporal edges within heterogeneous graph frameworks therefore offers a direction for strengthening event network modeling.

Non-stationary market adaptive prediction methods

Financial markets exhibit significant non-stationary characteristics, with feature importance and optimal prediction strategies dynamically varying according to market states, rendering adaptive modeling methods of considerable value for addressing this challenge. Markov regime-switching models introduce discrete hidden state variables and allow model parameters to switch between different states, enabling characterization of structural changes in prediction mechanisms when markets transition from calm periods to volatile periods13, and achieving superior performance compared to static models in exchange rate forecasting and volatility modeling. Time-varying parameter methods and online learning strategies continuously update model parameters to adapt to gradual drift in data distributions, further enriching the technical repertoire for adaptive modeling in non-stationary environments27. The Temporal Fusion Transformer architecture integrates the aforementioned adaptive concepts with deep learning, performing gated screening of input features through variable selection networks to achieve adaptive allocation of feature importance, capturing sequence dependencies across different temporal scales through interpretable multi-head attention mechanisms, and simultaneously outputting predictions at multiple quantile points through quantile regression to characterize prediction uncertainty15, with this architecture achieving notable advances in multi-step prediction tasks such as energy prices and electricity load4, and the feature weights output by its variable selection mechanism providing interpretability support for understanding the predictive contributions of different input variables. The variable selection mechanism of TFT, however, operates on numerical statistics of input features and exhibits response latency to sudden geopolitical shocks3, motivating semantic-driven state recognition for earlier responsiveness28.

Building upon the MAGNN heterogeneous-graph framework and the TFT adaptive feature-selection architecture, this paper proposes three targeted improvements: structured-output prompting of large language models for hierarchical semantic extraction, introduction of causal and temporal edges within heterogeneous graphs, and semantic-driven state recognition for accelerated feature reweighting. These improvements aim to further exploit the predictive potential of existing frameworks rather than to reconstruct them.

Methods

LLM-based hierarchical semantic extraction

Event-driven time series forecasting tasks require extracting semantic features relevant to prediction targets from unstructured text. However, existing methods generally encode text into a single dense vector, causing fine-grained information such as event triggers, affected entities, impact directions, and causal propagation to be compressed and lost during vectorization. This representational information loss is particularly pronounced in prediction scenarios with complex semantic structures. When text involves stance differentiation among multiple parties, heterogeneous impacts of different event types, and cross-event causal propagation chains, traditional flattened representations struggle to distinguish semantically similar but impact-divergent event types. Taking oil price prediction as an example, in geopolitical news, production cut announcements from Saudi Arabia and Venezuela have order-of-magnitude differences in their marginal market impacts. Economic sanctions typically involve supply chain adjustment lags of several days, while military conflicts often trigger panic buying that drives sharp price movements within 24 hours. Traditional BERT-based sentiment analysis compresses these differentiated pieces of information into a single scalar, resulting in reduced model responsiveness to complex events as in Fig 2.

Large language models acquire extensive knowledge about various event mechanisms through deep pre-training on massive multi-domain corpora. This knowledge enables models to understand differences in impact lags and propagation paths across event types, and to reason about how events affect prediction targets through multi-hop causal chains. More critically, large language models possess few-shot learning capabilities. Even when facing event types with sparse samples in training data, they can still provide reasonable semantic parsing through contextual reasoning and knowledge transfer. These characteristics make them effective tools for addressing semantic sparsity problems in event-driven prediction.

Based on the above analysis, this paper designs a four-layer pyramid semantic extraction architecture. The first layer is the entity stance extraction layer, which identifies key entities from text and quantifies their influence tendencies on prediction targets. For each entity , it outputs a stance score and a relevance weight . The second layer is the event type recognition layer, which constructs domain-relevant event ontologies and determines event type and severity . This lag is later used in the temporal gating mechanism of Section "Heterogeneous information graph construction and neural propagation modeling" to enforce that a cause precedes its effect during message passing. The fourth layer is the sentiment quantification layer, which extracts sentiment indicators relevant to prediction targets. In the oil price prediction task, the first layer extracts policy stances of oil-producing countries, international organizations, and energy companies. The event ontology in the second layer includes categories such as military actions, economic sanctions, diplomatic negotiations, and supply changes. The fourth layer extracts three market psychological indicators: fear index, uncertainty index, and bullish ratio.

Heterogeneous information graph construction and neural propagation modeling

In event-driven prediction tasks, multiple events often exhibit complex interactive relationships rather than isolated effects. This network effect manifests in three forms: cumulative effects where consecutively occurring similar events produce nonlinearly growing impacts; propagation effects where local events spread to broader ranges through dependency networks; and synergistic effects arising from mutual enhancement or offset when multiple actors act simultaneously. Traditional methods simply sum event impacts and struggle to fully capture these structured interaction patterns. Geopolitical conflicts in oil price prediction represent a typical scenario for validating network effects. The Russia-Ukraine conflict case shows that after military actions were triggered, financial sanctions, pipeline suspensions, and reserve adjustments formed cascading reactions within a short period, ultimately driving substantial price increases. Such multi-event network dynamics are far beyond what simple superposition can characterize.

Graph neural networks implicitly learn these synergistic patterns through multi-layer nonlinear transformations, with performance gains positively correlated with network clustering coefficients and node centrality.

This paper constructs a heterogeneous information graph to characterize complex interactive relationships among events. In the general definition, the node set contains three types of entities: information source nodes, subject nodes, and event nodes. The edge set contains three types of relationships: temporal association edges, causal propagation edges, and structural dependency edges. In the oil price prediction task, information source nodes correspond to news reports, subject nodes correspond to countries and international organizations, and event nodes correspond to specific events. Structural dependency edges are instantiated as political alliance relationships and trade dependence relationships. The node feature matrix is initialized differently according to type: information source nodes are assigned hierarchical semantic embeddings from the previous module, subject nodes are assigned domain feature vectors, and event nodes are assigned combinations of type encoding and historical impact statistics.

Graph construction integrates automatic extraction and domain knowledge. Temporal edges connect information sources reporting on the same entity within time windows, with weights using temporal decay . Causal edges combine intra-document causal chains with cross-document reasoning, utilizing document-level causal graphs from the previous module and identifying cross-document causal relationships through zero-shot reasoning by large language models. Structural dependency edges are constructed based on external knowledge bases, including oil-producing country alliance membership and bilateral trade dependence in oil price prediction.

Semantic-driven dynamic feature selection for non-stationary environments

Prediction tasks in non-stationary environments commonly exhibit state-switching phenomena, where the predictive value of features differs significantly across states. Static feature selection methods are essentially compromises across all states and are suboptimal in any single state. Let the state space be , and the state at time t be . Each state has an optimal feature set that minimizes loss in that state. In oil price prediction, market states can be divided into three categories: calm periods, conflict outbreak periods, and stalemate transition periods. Prior empirical studies in energy economics and time-series finance have established that the predictive value of different feature classes varies systematically with market regime: technical indicators tend to dominate during calm periods driven by supply-demand fundamentals, while geopolitical risk indices and news-derived sentiment become the most important factors during conflict outbreaks, and the predictive value of transportation costs and inventory levels increases during stalemate transition periods6,7,13. The dynamic feature selection mechanism proposed in this section is motivated by these prior findings rather than by our own experimental results. Joint training strategy and interpretability analysis

Jointly training the three improvements of hierarchical semantic extraction, graph neural propagation, and dynamic feature selection with the Temporal Fusion Transformer faces the challenge of coordinating optimization across heterogeneous components. Large language models have massive parameter scales with fixed pre-trained weights, graph neural networks depend on dynamically changing graph structures, feature selection involves discrete optimization, and the Temporal Fusion Transformer requires stable gradient flow. The differences among these components in optimization objectives and learning dynamics make simple end-to-end backpropagation prone to training instability.

This paper adopts a multi-task learning strategy to coordinate different optimization objectives. The overall loss function integrates point prediction loss, interval prediction loss, and regularization loss:

Point prediction loss uses mean squared error, interval prediction loss uses weighted sums of quantile losses, and regularization loss includes weight decay and sparsity constraints. The scalar weights α, β, γ in Eq. (12) are loss-balancing coefficients applied at the training-objective level3, and dynamically adjust according to environmental states, with the weight on interval prediction loss increased during periods of high uncertainty.

Joint training adopts a three-stage strategy to mitigate optimization conflicts. The first stage is semantic extraction adaptation, which freezes main parameters of the large language model and only trains low-rank adapter layers. Adapters insert low-rank matrices at each Transformer layer, significantly reducing trainable parameter count. The loss function combines multiple auxiliary tasks including event classification, stance regression, and sentiment prediction. The second stage is graph structure pre-training, which uses graphs constructed from historical data for self-supervised learning. Tasks include node type prediction, link prediction, and subgraph contrastive learning, enabling graph neural networks to obtain good initialization before formal training. The third stage is end-to-end fine-tuning, which unfreezes all modules for joint optimization. At this point, each component already possesses basic functionality, and fine-tuning mainly adjusts inter-module interfaces and weight allocation. Hierarchical learning rates are adopted to reflect parameter scale differences across components, and gradient clipping and accumulation strategies are used to stabilize the training process.

This paper analyzes model interpretability from two perspectives: internal mechanisms and external attribution. Internal interpretability utilizes the variable selection network and temporal attention mechanism of the Temporal Fusion Transformer. Variable selection weights reflect marginal contributions of features, and temporal attention weights reveal the influence of historical moments on predictions. External interpretability performs feature attribution through Shapley values. Shapley values satisfy axiomatic properties such as efficiency, symmetry, and monotonicity, enabling fair quantification of each feature’s contribution to predictions. This paper computes rank correlation coefficients between variable selection weights and Shapley values to verify consistency between the two interpretability methods.

Counterfactual analysis is used to deepen understanding of prediction mechanisms, answering how prediction results would change if certain types of information were removed. Counterfactual prediction is implemented through graph structure intervention. After removing specific types of nodes or edges from the graph neural network, graph embeddings are recomputed and predictions are generated. Causal effects are quantified as the difference between actual predictions and counterfactual predictions. Multi-factor contribution decomposition attributes overall prediction changes to different information sources. Each factor’s contribution is obtained by progressively removing corresponding features and computing prediction changes. For cases with strong interactions, Shapley interaction indices are used to quantify pairwise synergistic effects.

Interpretability analysis relies on causal inference assumptions such as unconfoundedness and positivity. Unconfoundedness is approximately satisfied by integrating multimodal information. Positivity is difficult to satisfy when certain event configurations have not historically occurred. In such cases, analogical reasoning based on similar historical cases can be performed through the knowledge base of large language models. The above interpretability analysis methods are applicable to various multimodal prediction tasks, helping users understand prediction bases and evaluate contributions from different information sources. In oil price prediction, these analyses provide quantitative evidence for understanding market price formation mechanisms.

Experiments and results

Experimental setup

Dataset construction

This study uses daily data from January 2021 to July 2024 for experimental validation. Oil price data adopts Brent crude oil spot prices provided by the U.S. Energy Information Administration, covering 870 trading days spanning before and after the Russia-Ukraine conflict. News data was collected through web scraping from Reuters and Oilprice.com, filtered using keywords including Russia, Ukraine, oil, sanctions, and energy, yielding approximately 4,500 geopolitically relevant reports. Auxiliary economic variables include the Geopolitical Risk Index, Economic Policy Uncertainty Index, U.S. Dollar Index, S&P 500 Index, crude oil inventory, and OPEC production data, all sourced from publicly available databases.

Data was divided chronologically into three non-overlapping subsets. The training set covers January 2021 to December 2022, comprising 70% of the total samples, including both the pre-conflict baseline period and the conflict outbreak period, used for model parameter learning. The validation set spans January 2023 to June 2023, comprising 15%, used for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping decisions. The test set covers July 2023 to July 2024, comprising 15%, strictly reserved for final performance evaluation to ensure unbiased results.

Evaluation metrics

Prediction performance is quantified through four complementary metrics. Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) measures the overall deviation between predicted and actual values, being more sensitive to large errors and suitable for capturing extreme volatility. Mean Absolute Error (MAE) provides a linear measure of error, more robust to outliers. Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) expresses relative error in percentage form, facilitating cross-period comparisons. The coefficient of determination measures the model’s ability to explain price variance, with values closer to 1 indicating better fit. All metrics are calculated on the test set without any post-processing or adjustments.

Baseline model selection

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method, four representative baseline models were selected. ARIMA represents traditional econometric methods, modeling through the autocorrelation structure of time series. LSTM, as a typical representative of recurrent neural networks, excels at capturing long-range dependencies in sequential data. The BERT combined with sentiment analysis and LSTM hybrid model represents current mainstream text-enhanced prediction methods, where BERT extracts flattened sentiment scalars from news as additional inputs to LSTM. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) model without news information uses only numerical features, representing a relatively advanced pure time series modeling architecture. These four baseline models cover the technical evolution path from simple to complex, from unimodal to multimodal.

Implementation details

All deep learning models were trained on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU using the PyTorch 2.0 framework and Python 3.9 environment. The large language model uses the GPT-4 API for hierarchical semantic extraction, with processing time of approximately 1.5 seconds per news article. The relational graph convolutional network is configured with 3 layers, hidden dimension of 256, using basis decomposition to reduce parameter count. Model training employs the Adam optimizer with learning rate set to 1e-4, batch size of 32, maximum 50 training epochs, and early stopping strategy to prevent overfitting. Hyperparameters were determined through grid search on the validation set, and all experimental results are averages of 5 independent runs with standard deviation controlled within 5%.

Discussion

Experimental results validate the effectiveness of the three proposed improvement measures. The overall performance improvement of 17.7% demonstrates that targeted improvements on existing multimodal fusion frameworks can yield substantial prediction accuracy gains. The 34.7% improvement during event-intensive periods indicates that hierarchical semantic extraction and dynamic feature selection show more pronounced advantages in scenarios with rich textual information and rapidly changing markets. The improvement in prediction stability also warrants attention: the error ratio between event-intensive and baseline periods decreased from 3.53 times to 2.12 times, indicating that the proposed method maintains relatively robust performance across different market states. Ablation experiments reveal independent contributions and synergistic mechanisms among modules. Hierarchical semantic extraction’s single-step improvement of 6.4% validates the importance of fully preserving fine-grained textual information. Heterogeneous graph modeling and dynamic feature selection contribute 3.8% and 3.7% respectively. The three components form a complete chain from information extraction to relationship modeling to flexible utilization, with synergistic effects enabling overall performance improvement to exceed the simple sum of individual module contributions.

The effectiveness of hierarchical semantic extraction stems from thorough preservation of textual information. Traditional flattened methods inevitably lose structured information when compressing complex text into single scalars, while hierarchical representation explicitly separates and encodes entity differences, event types, and causal relationships through a four-layer architecture. The pre-trained knowledge of large language models enables reasonable parsing even for event types with sparse samples in training data. The effectiveness of heterogeneous graph modeling stems from explicit characterization of network effects among events. Graph structure encodes complex interaction patterns such as cumulative effects, propagation effects, and synergistic effects through topological relationships of nodes and edges. Relation-aware attention mechanisms enable the model to dynamically adjust the importance of different relation types according to environmental states. The effectiveness of dynamic feature selection stems from directly addressing non-stationarity. The globally optimal feature set selected by static methods is essentially a compromise across all states, while dynamic methods decompose global optimization into state-specific local optimization through state identification. Semantic-driven state identification bypasses the lag of numerical statistical methods, enabling faster model response to environmental changes.

Although this paper uses oil price prediction as a validation case, the three proposed improvement measures possess good generality at the methodological level. Hierarchical semantic extraction methods are applicable to any prediction scenario requiring extraction of structured features from unstructured text, applicable to epidemic spread prediction, supply chain risk warning, financial market analysis, and other tasks. Different application scenarios require designing corresponding semantic hierarchy structures according to domain characteristics, but the overall architecture can be reused. Heterogeneous graph modeling methods are applicable to any prediction task where complex dependencies exist among entities and event impacts propagate through networks. Financial risk contagion, social media sentiment propagation, supply chain disruption prediction, and other scenarios can all draw upon the heterogeneous graph modeling approach. Dynamic feature selection methods are applicable to any non-stationary prediction environment with state switching. Economic cycles, epidemic propagation, market sentiment, and other domains all exhibit state-dependent characteristics of feature importance.

Current methods have several limitations requiring future research improvements. Regarding computational efficiency, reliance on large language model APIs for semantic extraction poses cost and latency constraints. Future work may explore domain-specific lightweight language models or hybrid architectures to reduce computational overhead. Regarding graph construction, automatically extracted causal relationships may contain noise, and structural dependency edges have low update frequency. Future work may develop continuous learning mechanisms to enable dynamic graph evolution. Regarding state modeling, discrete state space partitioning may lose information about gradual transitions between states. Future work may explore continuous state spaces or soft state assignment methods. Regarding validation scope, current experiments are conducted on a single asset only, and method effectiveness in other assets and domains awaits further verification.

Conclusion

Event-driven time series forecasting faces three persistent challenges: insufficient utilization of textual semantic information, inadequate modeling of network effects among events, and dynamic feature importance under non-stationary environments. To address these, this study proposes three improvement measures building upon multimodal graph neural network and Temporal Fusion Transformer frameworks: LLM-based hierarchical semantic extraction that decomposes text into entity stances, event types, causal chains, and sentiment indicators; heterogeneous graph neural propagation modeling that characterizes cumulative, propagation, and synergistic effects among events; and semantic-driven dynamic feature selection that adaptively recalibrates feature weights according to environmental states. Validated on Brent crude oil price forecasting, the framework improves 17.7% over the Temporal Fusion Transformer baseline, with improvement reaching 34.7% during event-intensive periods. Ablation experiments confirm independent contributions of each module, namely 6.4% from hierarchical semantic extraction, 3.8% from heterogeneous graph modeling, and 3.7% from dynamic feature selection. Dynamic feature weight analysis together with factor contribution decomposition further demonstrate the model’s interpretability capabilities.

The three improvement measures possess methodological generality beyond the validation scenario and can provide reference for related tasks such as epidemic spread prediction, supply chain risk warning, and financial market analysis. Future research will extend toward computational efficiency optimization, dynamic graph construction, continuous state modeling, and cross-domain validation.
