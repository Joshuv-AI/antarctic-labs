Abstract
. Ternary coherent configurations are, on the one hand, a special case of multidimensional coherent configurations introduced by L. Babai (2016), and, on the other hand, a natural generalization of association schemes on triples introduced by D. M. Mesner and P. Bhattacharya (1990). A ternary coherent configuration 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} is said to be circulant if the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( 𝔛 ) \aut({\mathfrak{X}}) of 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} has a regular cyclic subgroup, and schurian if the classes of 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} are the orbits of the componentwise action of the group Aut ⁡ ( 𝔛 ) \aut({\mathfrak{X}}) on triples of points of 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} . It is proved that any circulant ternary coherent configuration 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} of prime degree p p is schurian with the possible exception of the case when 𝔛 {\mathfrak{X}} is an association schemes on triples and either Aut ⁡ ( 𝔛 ) = AGL 1 ⁡ ( p ) \aut({\mathfrak{X}})=\AGL_{1}(p) and p = ± 1 ( mod 8 ) p=\pm 1\pmod{8} , or Aut ⁡ ( 𝔛 ) ≨ AGL 1 ⁡ ( p ) \aut({\mathfrak{X}})\lvertneqq\AGL_{1}(p) .



1.
Introduction

It is a common principle in algebraic combinatorics that objects with rich automorphism group are easier to analyze. In the most symmetric case, such objects can be described entirely in terms of relations of varying arity, on which the automorphism group acts transitively. However, this paradigm does not always apply: sometimes the object under study has small automorphism group. A classical example is given by the so-called Schur rings introduced by I. Schur in 1933. As H. Wielandt later observed in
[
16
]
, Schur believed that all Schur rings should arise naturally from their automorphism groups. Wielandt’s counterexamples, presented in his monograph on permutation groups
[
17
, Theorem 26.4]
, disproved this expectation and led to the formulation of the Schurity problem for Schur rings: to determine which Schur rings arise from their automorphism groups in the sense envisioned by Schur.

In this paper, we address the Schurity problem for multidimensional
coherent configurations
. These were introduced by L. Babai
[
1
]
(see also
[
10
]
) in connection with the graph isomorphism problem, as a natural extension of the binary coherent configurations originally defined by D. Higman in his study of permutation groups
[
11
]
. The motivation for considering multidimensional coherent configurations is twofold. On the one hand, they arise naturally in the context of the graph isomorphism problem and the Weisfeiler–Leman method (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). On the other hand, unlike in the binary case – where the automorphism groups are precisely the
2
-closed groups in Wielandt’s terminology
[
16
]
– the automorphism groups of multidimensional coherent configurations can be arbitrary permutation groups.

By definition (see Section
2
), an
m
-ary coherent configuration

{\mathfrak{X}}
on a finite set
\Omega
is precisely the partition of the Cartesian power
\Omega^{m}
, constructed by the
m
-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman (WL) algorithm; the number
|\Omega|
is called the
order
of
{\mathfrak{X}}
. We say that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is
schurian
if
{\mathfrak{X}}=\orb_{m}(G)
is the set of all orbits of a group
G\leq\sym(\Omega)
with respect to the componentwise action on
\Omega^{m}
. Nonschurian
m
-dimensional coherent configurations can serve as examples of graphs or higher-arity relational structures that are hard instances for isomorphism testing using the WL algorithm. At present, however, the only known hard examples are the graphs constructed by Cai, Fürer, and Immerman
[
4
]
and their modifications.

An interesting special case of ternary coherent configurations –
association schemes on triples
– was introduced and studied in
[
14
]
; in contrast to general case, the projection of an association scheme on triples to any two coordinates is a partition with exactly two classes: the diagonal of Cartesian square and its complement. The schurity of such ternary coherent configuration implies that its automorphism group is
2
-transitive. Schurian ternary coherent configurations corresponding to some
2
-transitive groups were further investigated in
[
2
]
.

The nonschurian case appears considerably more intricate; here, of special interest are those nonschurian ternary coherent configurations whose projection onto the first two coordinates is a schurian binary coherent configuration. In this setting, known examples are largely limited to association schemes on triples described in
[
14
]
and based on specific designs. The constructions presented in
[
18
]
suggest that the examples we are interested in can also be found among
circulant
ternary coherent configurations, that is, those that admit a regular cyclic automorphism group. The case of circulant association schemes on triples was studied in
[
3
]
. It turned out that even in this rather special class, it is difficult to describe those circulant association schemes on triples whose “essential” intersection numbers are
1
, the so-called thin circulants in terminology of
[
3
]
.

In the present paper, we restrict ourselves to circulant ternary coherent configurations
{\mathfrak{X}}
of prime order. Our first main result shows that the most difficult case here arises if
{\mathfrak{X}}
is an association scheme on triples.

Theorem 1.1
.

Any circulant ternary coherent configuration of prime order, other than an association scheme on triples, is schurian.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
, presented in Subsection
6.1
, is more or less direct. Indeed, let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a circulant ternary coherent configuration of prime order. The assumption that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is not an association scheme on triples implies that the projection of
{\mathfrak{X}}
to any two coordinates is a nontrivial cyclotomic association scheme of prime degree. In accordance with
[
9
, Theorem 1.2(2)]
, the latter scheme has a combinatorial base at most
2
. This enables us to determine the classes of
{\mathfrak{X}}
explicitly. It turns out that each of them is an orbit of the group
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
acting on the triples, which means that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is schurian.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a (circulant) association scheme on triples, that has prime order
p
and admits a
2
-transitive automorphism group. Then
{\mathfrak{X}}
is schurian unless
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})=\AGL_{1}(p)
and
p=\pm 1\ (\text{\rm mod}\hskip 2.0pt8)
.

We believe that Theorem
1.2
is true without any assumption on the prime
p
. At least, we have no counterexamples and, moreover, our proof can easily be extended to cover the case where
p-1=2^{a}r^{b}
, where
a\geq 1
and
b\geq 0
are integers.

We briefly outline the proof of Theorem
1.2
, which is presented in Subsection
6.2
. By the Burnside theorem on transitive groups of prime degree, the group
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
is either
\AGL_{1}(p)
or non-solvable (see also the explicit classification in
[
12
]
). In the non-solvable case, the stabilizer of two distinct points has at most two nonsingleton orbits, which rules out the existence of a nonschurian ternary coherent configuration with
2
-transitive automorphism group.

The most difficult case
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})=\AGL_{1}(p)
is considered in Section
3
(Theorem
5.1
). The proof there is based on the fact that a certain residue of the ternary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
determines a Schur ring over a cyclic group. Such rings admit a more or less detailed classification
[
13
,
9
]
, explained also in Section
3
. Using this classification and the assumption that
2
is a quadratic non-residue modulo
p
, we can first prove that the Schur ring in question is either trivial or a group ring, and then deduce from this that the ternary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
is schurian. The application of the classification exploits several number-theoretic properties of finite fields of prime order; we have collected them in Section
4
.

As an immediate corollary of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we obtain the following statement which shows where nonschurian circulant ternary coherent configurations of prime order can hide.

Corollary 1.3
.

Any circulant ternary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
of prime order is schurian unless
{\mathfrak{X}}
is an association scheme on triples such that either
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})=\AGL_{1}(p)
with
p=\pm 1\ (\text{\rm mod}\hskip 2.0pt8)
, or
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})\lvertneqq\AGL_{1}(p)
.

All facts and concepts concerning multidimensional coherent configurations that are needed to understand the paper are presented in Section
2
.

2.
Multidimensional coherent configurations

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
denotes a finite set and
m\geq 1
an integer. Set
M=\{1,\dots,m\}
. For a tuple
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\Omega^{m}
, we write
x_{i}
for its
i
th coordinate,
i\in M
. Denote by
\rho(x)
the equivalence relation on
M
given by

i\sim j\iff x_{i}=x_{j}.

The number of classes of this equivalence relation is denoted by
\|\rho(x)\|
.

Denote by
\mon(M)
the monoid of all maps from
M
to itself (they act on the set of coordinates); for
\sigma\in\mon(M)
, we put

x^{\sigma}=(x_{1^{\sigma}},\dots,x_{m^{\sigma}}).

For
x\in\Omega^{m}
,
i\in M
, and
\alpha\in\Omega
, denote by
x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}
the tuple obtained from
x
by replacing the
i
th coordinate with
\alpha
.

Let
I\subseteq M
be a nonempty set of coordinate indices and write
\pr_{I}:\Omega^{m}\to\Omega^{I}
for the projection onto the coordinates in
I
. When
I=\{1,\ldots,k\}
, we abbreviate
\pr_{I}=\pr_{k}
. For
X\subseteq\Omega^{m}
and
u\in\Omega^{I}
, we set

X_{u}=\{\pr_{M\setminus I}(x):\ x\in X\text{ and }\pr_{I}(x)=u\}.

Clearly, if
J\subsetneq M
is a nonempty subset disjoint with
I
, then
(X_{u})_{v}=X_{uv}
for all
u\in\Omega^{I}
and
v\in\Omega^{J}
, where
uv\in\Omega^{I\cup J}
is the unique tuple for which
\pr_{I}(uv)=u
and
\pr_{J}(uv)=v
.

We use standard notation:
\sym(\Omega)
for the symmetric group on
\Omega
,
C_{p}
for the cyclic group of order
p
, and
\AGL_{1}(p)=\AGL_{1}({\mathbb{F}})
for the
1
-dimensional linear affine group over a field
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}
with
p
elements.

2.2.
Definition

A partition
{\mathfrak{X}}
of
\Omega^{m}
is called an
m
-ary coherent configuration
on
\Omega
if the following conditions are satisfied for all
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}
:

(C1)

\rho(x)=:\rho(X)
does not depend on the choice of
x\in X
,

(C2)

X^{\sigma}\in{\mathfrak{X}}
for all
\sigma\in\mon(M)
,

(C3)

for any
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\in{\mathfrak{X}}
, the number

n_{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}}^{X}=|\{\alpha\in\Omega:\;x_{i\leftarrow\alpha}\in X_{i}\ \text{for all }i\in M\}|

does not depend on the choice of
x\in X
.

For
m=2
, this definition reproduces the classical notion of a coherent configuration
[
6
]
. Let
m=3
and for any equivalence relation
\rho\subseteq M\times M
with at most two classes, there is exactly one
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}
for which
\rho(X)=\rho
. Then the
3
-ary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
is called an
association scheme on triples
, see
[
14
]
.

2.3.
Automorphisms and schurity

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be an
m
-ary coherent configuration on
\Omega
.
The
automorphism group

\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
is the subgroup of
\sym(\Omega)
consisting of all permutations
f
such that
X^{f}=X
for every
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}
, where the action is defined
componentwise on
\Omega^{m}
:

x^{f}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{f}=(x_{1}^{f},\dots,x_{m}^{f})

for all
x\in\Omega^{m}
. Thus the group
\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
acts on
\Omega
and leaves each class of the partition
{\mathfrak{X}}
fixed. It is not difficult to verify that
(x^{\sigma})^{f}=(x^{f})^{\sigma}
for all
x\in\Omega^{m}
,
\sigma\in\mon(M)
, and
f\in\sym(\Omega)
; in particular,
(X^{\sigma})^{f}=(X^{f})^{\sigma}=X^{\sigma}
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{X}}
and
f\in\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
.

Let
G\leq\sym(\Omega)
be a permutation group. If
G\leq\aut({\mathfrak{X}})
, then we say that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is
invariant
with respect to
G
. Any set of the form
x^{G}=\{x^{f}:\ f\in G\}
with
x\in\Omega^{m}
is called an
m
-orbit
of
G
. The partition

\orb_{m}(G)=\{x^{G}:\ x\in\Omega^{m}\}

of the Cartesian power
\Omega^{m}
into the
m
-orbits of
G
is an
m
-ary coherent configuration. A coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
is called
schurian
if
{\mathfrak{X}}=\orb_{m}(G)
for some
G\leq\sym(\Omega)
. Note that in general, the group
G
with this property is not a unique one.

2.4.
Galois correspondence

The
m
-ary coherent configurations on
\Omega
are partially ordered by refinement: we write

{\mathfrak{X}}\leq{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}
if every class of
{\mathfrak{X}}
is a union of classes of
{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}
. It is straightforward to verify the monotonicity relations

(1)

{\mathfrak{X}}\leq{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}\ \Longrightarrow\ \aut({\mathfrak{X}})\geq\aut({\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}),\qquad G\leq G^{\prime}\ \Longrightarrow\ \orb_{m}(G)\geq\orb_{m}(G^{\prime}),

for partitions
{\mathfrak{X}},{\mathfrak{X}}^{\prime}
of
\Omega^{m}
and subgroups
G,G^{\prime}\leq\sym(\Omega)
. Moreover, the two maps

{\mathfrak{X}}\longmapsto\aut({\mathfrak{X}}),\qquad G\longmapsto\orb_{m}(G)

form a Galois connection between the lattice of
m
-ary coherent configurations (ordered by refinement) and the lattice of permutation subgroups of
\sym(\Omega)
(ordered by inclusion). In particular, for every
m
-ary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
and every group
G
, we have the following identities:

\aut(\orb_{m}(\aut({\mathfrak{X}})))=\aut({\mathfrak{X}}),\qquad\orb_{m}(\aut(\orb_{m}(G)))=\orb_{m}(G).

The Galois closed objects under this correspondence are the schurian
m
-ary coherent configurations and
m
-closed permutation groups, see
[
16
]
.

2.5.
Projections and residues

Let
I\subseteq M
be a nonempty subset and
y\in\Omega^{M\setminus I}
. In accordance with
[
1
,
10
]
, the sets

\pr_{I}{\mathfrak{X}}=\{\pr_{I}X:\ X\in{\mathfrak{X}}\},\qquad{\mathfrak{X}}_{y}=\{X_{y}:\ X\in{\mathfrak{X}},\ X_{y}\neq\varnothing\}

are
k
-ary coherent configurations on
\Omega
, where

; they are called the

of
{\mathfrak{X}}
to
I
, and the
residue
of
{\mathfrak{X}}
with respect to

, respectively. Clearly,

{\mathfrak{X}}_{y}\geq\pr_{I}{\mathfrak{X}}.

The lemma below immediately follows from the definitions.

Lemma 2.1
.

A ternary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
is an association scheme on triples if and only if
\pr_{2}{\mathfrak{X}}
is a trivial coherent configuration.

1

1

A binary coherent configuration is said to be trivial if it consists of at most
2

Taking projections and residues respects the automorphism groups in the following sense. First, given
I\subseteq M
and

, one has the inclusions

(2)

\aut(\pr_{I}{\mathfrak{X}})\geq\aut({\mathfrak{X}}),\qquad\aut({\mathfrak{X}}_{y})\geq\aut({\mathfrak{X}})_{y}.

Second, if
{\mathfrak{X}}=\orb_{m}(G)

The projections

, are called

of the

-ary coherent configuration

. It is not hard to verify that if

, is an

3.
Schur partitions

Let
G
be a group. Following
[
15
, Definition 4.1]
, a partition
\Pi
of
G
is called a
Schur partition
if
\{1\}\in\Pi
, the set
X^{-1}=\{x^{-1}:\ x\in X\}
is a class of
\Pi
for all
X\in\Pi
, and the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module

(3)

{\cal A}=\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{{\underline{X}}:\ X\in\Pi\}

is a subring of the group ring
{\mathbb{Z}}G
, where
{\underline{X}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}G
is the formal sum of the elements of
X
. The subrings of the form (
3
) corresponding to the Schur partitions are exactly the Schur rings over the group
G
. The statement below is an immediate consequence of
[
6
, Corollary 2.4.18]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a binary coherent configuration on the elements of a group
G
. Assume that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is invariant with respect to the group induced by right multiplications of
G
. Then the residue
{\mathfrak{X}}_{(1)}
with respect to the tuple
(1)
is a Schur partition of
G
.

Two obvious examples of the Schur partition
\Pi
are the
discrete
and
trivial
partitions of
G
: in the first case,
|\Pi|=|G|
and the classes of
\Pi
are singletons, whereas in the second case,
|\Pi|\leq 2
and the classes of
\Pi
are
\{1\}
and
G\setminus\{1\}
(if
G
is not the identity group). For any subgroup
K\leq\aut(G)
, the partition
\orb(K,G)
of
G
into the orbits of
K
is a Schur partition; it is called
orbit
(or
cyclotomic
) partition.

The Schur rings over cyclic group were completely classified in
[
13
]
. In Section
5
, we will need a refinement of this classification, given in
[
9
]
. We formulate it in terms of Schur partitions, not in terms of Schur rings as was done there. Below a subset
X
of a cyclic group
G
is said to be
highest
if
X
contains a generator of
G
; the subgroup of all
y\in G
such that
yX=Xy=X
is called the

radical
of
X
and is denoted by
\rad(X)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Pi
be a Schur partition of a cyclic group
G
. Then exactly one of the following alternatives holds.

(a)

\rad(X)\neq\{1\}
for each highest class
X\in\Pi
.

(b)

There exist a direct product decomposition
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{k}

(k\geq 1)
and a Schur partition
\Pi_{i}
of the group
G_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, such that

(b1)

\Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{k}
are trivial partitions except for at most one being orbit;

(b2)

\Pi=\{X_{1}\times\cdots\times X_{k}:\ X_{1}\in\Pi_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\Pi_{k}\}
.

Proof.

The statement is essentially a reformulation of the known classification of Schur rings over cyclic groups (see
[
13
,
9
]
). Denote by
{\cal A}
the Schur ring associated with the partition
\Pi
. By
[
9
, Corollary 6.4]
, either the radical of
{\cal A}
is nontrivial, or
{\cal A}
is a tensor product of a normal Schur ring with trivial radical and Schur rings of rank
2
. The first possibility is equivalent to statement (a): the radical of
{\cal A}
is just the
\rad(X)
for any highest class of
\Pi
. The second possibility is equivalent to statement (b): the decomposition of
{\cal A}
in the tensor product means the existence of the direct product decomposition
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{k}
satisfying condition (b2), the Schur partition associated with normal Schur ring is always orbit
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
, and the Schur rings of rank
2
are associated with trivial Schur partitions.
∎

4.
Arithmetic properties of finite fields of prime order

In this section, we establish two number-theoretic facts about finite fields of prime order. These auxiliary results will be used in the classification arguments of Section 5, especially in the case of circulant ternary coherent configurations invariant with respect to one-dimensional affine linear group of a prime field.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p>2
be a prime. Then there exists a primitive root
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
such that
1-x
is also a primitive root.

Proof.

It was proved in
[
8
]
that for any prime power
q>61
and for arbitrary nonzero
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, there is always one representation of the form
a=bx+cy
, where
x
and
y
are primitive roots of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Taking
q=p
and
a=b=c=1
, we get the required result for all
p>61
. For the remaining primes, the statement can be verified by a straightforward computation.
∎

Let
p
be a prime and
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
be a nonempty subset not containing the element
1=1_{\mathbb{F}}
. We say that
X
is of
group type
if the union
X\cup\{1\}
is a multiplicative subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}
. Clearly,
X
is of group type only if
\sum_{x\in X}x=-1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the above notation, the following statements hold:

(1)

if
\rad(X)\neq\{1\}
, then
\rad(1-X)=\{1\}
,

(2)

if
X
is of group type, then
1-X
is of group type only if
X={\mathbb{F}}^{\times}\setminus\{1\}
.

Proof.

Obviously,

(4)

\sum_{x\in X}x+\sum_{y\in 1-X}y=|X|\cdot 1.

Also, if
\rad(X)\neq 1
, then
X
is a union of full cosets of
\rad(X)
in
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
. As the sum of elements in each coset is equal to
0
, the sum of elements in
X
is
0
.

Now if
\rad(1-X)\neq 1
, then by equality (
4
), we have
|X|\cdot 1=0
in
{\mathbb{F}}
. This is impossible since
1\leq|X|\leq p-1
. This proves statement (1).

If both
X
and
1-X
are of group type, then by (
4
) we have
-2=|X|\cdot 1
in
{\mathbb{F}}
. This yields that
|X|+2=p
. Thus,
|X|=p-2
, which proves statement (2).
∎

5.
Ternary coherent configurations invariant with respect to AGL
{}_{1}(p)

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem

below. It represents a special case of Theorem

and, in a sense, forms a key part of the entire argument.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{X}}
be a ternary coherent configuration on a field
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of prime order

. Assume that
{\mathfrak{X}}
is invariant with respect to the group

{\mathfrak{X}}=\orb_{3}(G),

where

or
\AGL_{1}({\mathbb{F}})

The proof of Theorem
5.1
is given at the end of the section. In what follows, we fix a ternary coherent configuration
{\mathfrak{X}}
invariant with respect to the group
\AGL_{1}({\mathbb{F}})

{\mathfrak{X}}_{(0,1)}

{\mathfrak{X}}
with respect to the pair
(0,1)\in{\mathbb{F}}\times{\mathbb{F}}

It is easily seen that
{\mathfrak{X}}_{(0,1)}
is a partition of
{\mathbb{F}}
that contains two singleton classes,
\{0\}
and
\{1\}

\Pi=\{X\in{\mathfrak{X}}_{(0,1)}:\ X\neq\{0\}\}

of the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}

\{1\}
is a class of
\Pi
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The partition
\Pi
is a Schur partition of the group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
. Moreover, if
X\in\Pi
and
1\notin X
, then
1-X\in\Pi

Proof.

The residue
{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}={\mathfrak{X}}_{(0)}
is a binary coherent configuration. By the assumption, it is invariant with respect to the stabilizer of
0
in the group
\AGL_{1}({\mathbb{F}})
. Moreover,
{\mathfrak{X}}_{0}
