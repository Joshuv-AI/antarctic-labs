Blow-up of solutions to the Euler-Poisson-Darbox equation with critical power nonlinearity Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Mengting Fan, Ning-An Lai, Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
In our recent precious work, we established the finite time blow up result and upper bound of lifespan estimate to the singular Cauchy problem of semilinear Euler-Poisson-Darboux equation in 𝐑 n \mathbf{R}^{n} with subcritical power type nonlinearity. By introducing an improved test function, we obtain an enhanced lower bound for the functional including the spacetime integral of the nonlinear term (see ( 71 ) below) with an additional logarithmic growth, which finally yields the blow up result and upper bound of lifespan estimate for the corresponding Cauchy problem with "critical" nonlinear power. And this gives some partial answer to the 𝐨𝐩𝐞𝐧 ​ 𝐩𝐫𝐨𝐛𝐥𝐞𝐦 ​ 𝟏 \mathbf{open~problem~1} posed by D’Abbicco (J. Differential Equations 286 (2021), 531-556).

In our recent work

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}-\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{t}u_{t}=t^{\alpha}|u|^{p},~~~~t>0,x\in\mathbf{R}^{n},\\

Definition 1.1

We say that
u
is an energy solution of (
1
) with Cauchy data

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T),H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,T),L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))\cap L^{p}_{loc}((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n})

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\{-u_{t}(s,x)\phi_{t}(x,s)+\nabla u(s,x)\cdot\nabla\phi(s,x)\}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\frac{\mu u_{t}(s,x)}{s}\phi(s,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}s^{\alpha}|u(s,x)|^{p}\phi(s,x)dx

with any

Employing the integration by parts in the above equality and letting

, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n}}u(s,x)\bigg\{\phi_{tt}(s,x)-\Delta\phi(s,x)-\Big(\frac{\mu\phi(s,x)}{s}\Big)_{s}\bigg\}dxds

(9)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\frac{\mu u_{t}(s,x)}{s}\phi(s,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}u_{0}(x)\phi_{t}(0,x)dx+\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}s^{\alpha}|u(s,x)|^{p}\phi(s,x)dx,

which is exactly the definition of the weak solution to (

The main result is stated as

Theorem 1.2

Let

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\alpha\geq 0,~~~n\geq 3,\\
&\alpha>0,~~~~n=2\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

0<\mu\leq\mu^{*}(n,\alpha),p=p_{S}(n+\mu,\alpha),

\displaystyle\mu^{*}(n,\alpha)=\frac{2n^{2}+(n+2+\alpha)(n\alpha+2+\alpha)}{(n+2+\alpha)(2+\alpha)}.

(11)

Assume the initial data

is positive and has compact support

\displaystyle supp~u_{0}(x)\subset\{|x|\leq 1\},

(12)

then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(u_{0},n,p,\mu,\alpha)>0

T(\varepsilon)\leq\exp\left(C\varepsilon^{-p(p-1)}\right),

(13)

where

Local existence and finite speed propagation

In this section we will prove the local existence of solution and finite speed propagation property to the Cauchy problem (
1
), by using the method similar to that in Section 5 in
[
23
]
. Assuming that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &1<p\leq\frac{n}{n-2},~~~n\geq 3,\\
&1<p<\infty,~~~~n=2.\end{aligned}\right.

(14)

Define the function space

\displaystyle B_{T}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{u\in C\big([0,T),H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T),L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\big):

(15)

\displaystyle supp~u\subset\big\{(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n}:|x|\leq t+1\big\},\|u\|_{B_{T}}<\infty\},

where
T
is a positive constant and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{B_{T}}:=\mathop{sup}\limits_{t\in[0,T)}E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t),~~~E_{u}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2})dx.

(16)

It is easy to see that
(B_{T},\|\cdot\|_{B_{T}})
is a Banach space.

Consider the following Cauchy problem for
v\in B_{T}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u_{tt}-\Delta u+\frac{\mu}{t}u_{t}=t^{\alpha}|v|^{p}=:F_{v}(t,x),~~~~~(0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n},\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=0,~~~~x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}.\end{aligned}\right.

(17)

For fixed
T>0
, noting the assumption (
14
), by Sobolev embedding inequality it holds that

\displaystyle F_{v}(t,x)\in L^{2}\big((0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\big).

(18)

We are going to show that the map

\displaystyle M:v\mapsto u=Mv,~v\in B_{T}

(19)

is a contracting mapping. For
v\in B_{T}
, by Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and Poincaré type inequality, it holds

\displaystyle||v||_{L^{2p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C||v||_{L^{2}{(\mathbf{R}^{n})}}^{1-\theta}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}{(\mathbf{R}^{n})}}^{\theta},~~\theta:=n\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\right)

(20)

and

||v||_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(1+t)||\nabla v||_{L^{2}{(\mathbf{R}^{n})}},

plugging which into (
20
), we get

\displaystyle||v||_{L^{2p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(1+t)^{1-\theta}||\nabla v||_{L^{2}{(\mathbf{R}^{n})}}\leq C(1+t)^{1-\theta}E_{v}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(21)

We first show the finite speed propagation property of the energy solution. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u(t,x)\in C^{1}([0,T);C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n}))
. In the general case, we may approximate
u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
and
F_{v}(t,x)\in L^{2}\big([0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n}\big)
by the sequence
\{u_{0}^{k}\}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
and
\{F_{v}^{k}\}\subset C^{1}\left([0,T);C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\right)
, and denote by
u^{k}
the corresponding solutions to (
17
). For
(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
\tau\in[0,t)
, define the truncated cone

\displaystyle C_{\tau}:=\Big\{(s,y):|y-x|\leq t-s,0\leq s\leq\tau\Big\}.

(22)

Multiplying the equation in (
17
) with
u_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)-\nabla\cdot(\nabla u\nabla u_{t})+\frac{\mu}{t}u_{t}^{2}=F_{v}u_{t},

(23)

integrating which over
C_{\tau}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{|x-y|\leq t-\tau}\left(u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)(\tau,y)dy-\frac{1}{2}\int_{|x-y|\leq t}\left(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)(0,y)dy

(24)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{|y-x|=t-\tau}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{s}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)+\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\cdot\nabla uu_{s}\right]d\sigma ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{|y-x|\leq t-\tau}\frac{\mu}{s}u_{s}^{2}dyds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{|y-x|\leq t-s}F_{v}u_{s}dyds.

It is easy to see the third surface integral term in the left hand side is nonnegative by using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\left|\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\cdot\nabla uu_{t}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{t}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right).

(25)

Denoting the local energy by

E_{l}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{|x-y|\leq t-\tau}\left(u_{\tau}^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)(\tau,y)dy,

then it follows from (
24
) that

\displaystyle E_{l}(\tau)\leq E_{l}(0)+C\max_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|F_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\int_{0}^{\tau}E_{l}(s)ds,

(26)

which yields by Gronwall’s inequality

\displaystyle E_{l}(\tau)\leq E_{l}(0)\exp\left(C\max_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|F_{v}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\tau\right).

(27)

Hence if
u(0,y)=0
for
|y-x|\leq t
, then
u(\tau,y)=0
for
|y-x|\leq t-\tau,\tau\in[0,t)
. This implies if
u(0,x)=0
for
|x|>1
, then
u(t,x)=0
for
|x|\geq t+1
.

Next, we show that
||Mv||_{B_{T}}<\infty
.
By (
21
) it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|v|^{p}|u_{t}|dx

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}|v|^{2p}dx\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{2}E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

(28)

\displaystyle\leq C(t+1)^{p(1-\theta)}E_{v}^{\frac{p}{2}}(t)E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t).

Integrating (
23
) over
[0,t]\times\mathbf{R}^{n},t\in(0,T]
and using the divergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{u}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}\frac{\mu}{s}u_{s}^{2}dxds

(29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E_{u}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}s^{\alpha}(s+1)^{p(1-\theta)}E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(s)ds,

which yields further by Bihari’s inequality

\displaystyle E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}s^{\alpha}(s+1)^{p(1-\theta)}ds.

(30)

\displaystyle\leq E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)+C(1+T)^{p(1-\theta)}T^{\alpha+1},

hence for a fixed
T
we have
E_{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)<\infty
.

Finally, we show the contraction of the map
M
. Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in B_{T}
and

\displaystyle u_{1}=Mv_{1},~~~u_{2}=Mv_{2}

(31)

with the same initial data and

\overline{u}=u_{1}-u_{2},~~\overline{v}=v_{1}-v_{2}.

Then
\overline{u}
satisfies the following Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\overline{u}_{tt}-\Delta\overline{u}+\frac{\mu}{t}\overline{u}_{t}=t^{\alpha}|v_{1}|^{p}-t^{\alpha}|v_{2}|^{p},~~~~in~(0,T)\times\mathbf{R}^{n},\\
&\overline{u}(0,x)=0,\quad\overline{u}_{t}(0,x)\equiv 0,~~~~x\in\mathbf{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(32)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big(\overline{u}_{t}^{2}+|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}\big)\Bigg)+\frac{\mu}{t}\left(\overline{u}_{t}\right)^{2}=\nabla\cdot(\overline{u}_{t}\nabla\overline{u})+t^{\alpha}(|v_{1}|^{p}-|v_{2}|^{p})\overline{u}_{t}.

(33)

By (
21
) and Hölder inequality, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}t^{\alpha}\big||v_{1}|^{p}-|v_{2}|^{p}\big||\overline{u}_{t}|dx

(34)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n}}t^{\alpha}|v_{1}-v_{2}|(|v_{1}|+|v_{2}|)^{p-1}|\overline{u}_{t}|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\alpha}\left\||\overline{v}|(|v_{1}|+|v_{2}|)^{p-1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\|\overline{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\alpha}\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\Big(\|v_{1}\|_{L_{(\mathbf{R}^{n})}^{2p}}^{p-1}+\|v_{2}\|_{L^{2p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}^{p-1}\Big)\|\overline{u}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\alpha}(t+1)^{p(1-\theta)}E_{\overline{v}}^{\frac{1}{2}}E_{\overline{u}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, integrating (
33
) over
[0,T]\times\mathbf{R}^{n}
, exploiting again the Bihari’s inequality and proceeding similarly as above, we obtain

\displaystyle||\overline{u}||_{B_{T}}\leq C(1+T)^{p(1-\theta)}T^{1+\alpha}\|\overline{v}\|_{B_{T}},

(35)

which implies
M
is a contraction map by choosing
T
small enough.
The proof of the local existence and finite speed propagation are completed.

We may compute

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\lambda t)

\displaystyle=
