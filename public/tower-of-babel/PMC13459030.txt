Natural radionuclide measurement and radioactivity assessment of soil samples in Gansu Province (China)
Authors: Fang Wang, Yanjun Wang, Hanyu Zhang, Yun Wang, Qian Wang, Ruiying Wang, Jialong Wu, Limei Niu, Xiangru Qu, Jie Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis investigation assessed the activity concentrations of natural radionuclides (Th-232, Ra-226, and K-40) within soil matrices across Gansu Province, China. A total of 115 soil specimens were gathered from agricultural lands and uncultivated sites. Samples were analyzed by a low-background and high-purity germanium (HPGe) γ-ray spectrometer. The activity concentration of Th-232, Ra-226, and K-40 ranged from 9.7 to 56.0 Bq/kg, 19.4 to 99.3 Bq/kg, 349.5 to 713.1 Bq/kg, with mean values of 27.4 ± 6.3 Bq/kg, 45.9 ± 10.5 Bq/kg, 566.7 ± 64.8 Bq/kg, respectively. Derived radiological parameters included external hazard index (Hex) of 0.37, absorbed gamma dose rate (D) of 63.8 nGy/h, and annual effective dose rate (E) of 78.5 μSv/a. This is the first time to evaluate the risk of natural radionuclides in soil in this area and provides essential information on the levels of background radiation in Gansu. The results indicate that the radioactivity level in the soil of Gansu is similar to that in Sichuan province, which borders Gansu and shares similar characteristics with the area. The natural radionuclide measurement and radioactivity assessment of soil samples in Gansu province show that they will not cause a significant threat to local residents.

Introduction

Soil serves as a primary reservoir and migration pathway for radionuclides within the surface environment [1, 2]. Its inherent radioactivity, determined by the types and concentrations of contained radionuclides, directly reflects the ambient radiation levels. Agricultural soils present dual exposure pathways, internal exposure through radionuclide uptake into the food chain [3], and external exposure from gamma radiation emitted at the surface. The concentration of natural radionuclides in the soil is influenced by various factors, including geology, geography (e.g. altitude), and agricultural practices [4–6]. It is very important to quantify the distribution and concentration of radionuclides for evaluating migration patterns and potential radiation effects [7].

Geology is the main controller, the source and initial distributor of geography. The initial reserves are determined by the geochemical composition of the bedrock. For example, acid igneous rocks (such as granite- and phosphate-rich sedimentary rocks) usually contain high concentrations of uranium and thorium radionuclides. In contrast, basic rocks like basalt and limestone generally have much lower activity concentrations [8].

Geography, particularly altitude and climate, acts as a powerful modifier of radiation levels. The dose rates in the high-altitude Antarctic plateau, are considerably greater than the background at sea level [9]. Furthermore, geographic features influence erosional and depositional patterns.

Agricultural activity is an important human factor, which can greatly change the natural distribution of radionuclides [10]. The type of crops planted influences the uptake of specific elements. For example, root vegetables and tobacco are known to accumulate high levels of radionuclides [11]. More profoundly, the application of phosphate fertilizer, which can be rich in uranium and its decay products, is the main mechanism for introducing and redistributing radionuclides throughout the agricultural landscapes [10].

The main natural radionuclides in soil include Thorium-232 (Th-232), Radium-226 (Ra-226), and Potassium-40 (K-40). Radionuclides in soil of 86 counties/districts in Gansu province were monitored in this study. The analysis of natural isotopes Th-232, Ra-226, and K-40 in soil samples was based on the Chinese national standard GB/T 16145-2022 [12]. It is time-consuming to measure radioactive concentration with gamma-ray spectrum. However, it is non-destructive, so gamma-ray spectroscopy is adopted by many researchers in this field.

The objectives were to determine regional radioactivity levels, assess terrestrial gamma dose rates, and establish baseline background data crucial for radiological risk assessment and radiation protection initiatives.

Material and methods

Study area

Gansu Province is located in the northwest of China (see Fig. 1), with an area of 425 800 km2 and an east–west extension of ~1600 km. It lies between latitude from 32°11′ N to 44°57′ N and longitude from 92°13′ E to 108°46′ E, with elevations predominantly exceeding 1000 m [13]. The province includes five major natural regions: Hexi Desert Oasis, Longdong Loess Plateau, Longzhong Loess Plateau, Longnan Mountainous Area, and Gannan Plateau. Soil types transition from southeast to northwest, including yellow-brown soil, mountain-brown soil, clayey black calcareous soil, gray calcareous soil, and gray-brown desert soil.

(a) The distribution map of sampling points. (b) The distribution map of sampling points-2.

According to the 2023 Gansu Provincial National Economic and Social Development Statistical Bulletin published by the Gansu Provincial Bureau of Statistics [14], the permanent resident population is 24.65 million, and the urban population is 13.68 million (urbanization rate of 55.49%). The population density of the study area is 57.9 people/km2.

Sampling and sample preparation

Sampling sites were strategically selected based on population density and geographic representation. Within each 10 m × 10 m grid, five sub-samples were collected following the plum blossom method and homogenized into a single composite sample. Approximately 3 kg of soil was collected from the 0–10 cm depth layer, avoiding proximity to rivers, roads, buildings, and trees [12, 15]. Visible organic debris and roots were removed. Samples were mixed thoroughly, placed in labeled airtight polyethylene bags, and transported to the Radiation Hygiene Laboratory at Gansu Center for Disease Control and Prevention (GSCDC) for processing. Measures were taken to prevent contamination, damage, or moisture during transit and storage. Processing commenced promptly upon arrival.

One hundred fifteen samples were collected in 2023–24, including 108 agricultural soil samples and 7 uncultivated soil samples. Agricultural soil samples were distributed as follows: Hexi Desert Oasis (24), Longdong Loess Plateau (17), Longzhong Loess Plateau (49), Longnan Mountainous Area (12), and Gannan Plateau (6) (Fig. 1). Uncultivated soil samples (7) were collected in Hexi. Before collecting soil, the environmental absorbed dose rates (D) were measured five times with a portable HY-425 multifunctional x/γ radiometer (China) at a height of 1 m, and the average value was recorded. Geographic coordinates were obtained using the Yuandao GPS Camera app.

In the laboratory, samples were screened to eliminate gravel and coarse plant debris, then oven-dried at 105°C for 8 h. Dried samples were crushed, passed through a fine nylon sieve, and put into cylindrical polyethylene containers (diameter 75 mm, height 70 mm). Container and sample mass were determined using an electronic balance (0.01 mg sensitivity). The average mass of the samples used for measurements was 0.315 kg. The sample containers were sealed with electrical tape to minimize the escape of Rn-222 [17]. Samples were stored for 4 weeks to reach radioactive balance [18, 19]. Strict protocols were taken to prevent cross-contamination and external radiation interference.

Measurement

Analysis was adopted with a high-resolution gamma-ray spectrometer system, which is characterized by a coaxial P-type HPGe detector (50% relative efficiency and 1.73 keV FWHM resolution at 1332 keV). The detector was coupled to a Multi-Channel Analyzer (MCA) and a computer. The standard sources (Am-241, Cd-109, Co-57, Ce-139, Cr-51, Sn-113, Sr-85, Cs-137, Mn-54, Y-88, Zn-65, Co-60) used for energy calibration can be traced back to the British National Physical Laboratory. The system calibration was certified by the Chinese Academy of Metrology Science (Certificate No.: Dyhg 2006-6030, factory No. 1, 2, 3, 4). The background spectra were obtained, and they were subtracted from the sample spectra. Both the counting time of sample and background ranged from 24 to 48 h. Energy calibration was verified using U-238 (63.3 keV) and K-40 (1460 keV) peaks. Peak identification and boundary channel determination were performed for quantification [12].

Data analysis

Statistical methods

Radiation measurement results in this study were statistically analyzed by using the program tool IBM SPSS Statistics. The data were analyzed by using IBM SPSS version 22 software, and the results showed that the specific activity of soil radioactivity in Gansu Province was not a Gaussian distribution. Kruskal–Wallis H test and the Mann–Whitney U test were used for statistical inference. P < .05 is considered statistically significant.

Basic statistics

Kurtosis measures the peakedness of a probability distribution compared to a normal distribution. Positive kurtosis indicates a sharper peak, whereas negative kurtosis suggests a flatter distribution. In this particular investigation, the kurtosis values for Ra-226 (9.6), Th-232 (3.3), and K-40 (0.8) pointed to distributions with relatively pronounced peaks.

Compared to global averages (30 Bq/kg for Th-232, 35 Bq/kg for Ra-226, 400 Bq/kg for K-40) [27], the mean levels of Th-232 and K-40 in Gansu agricultural soils were higher, whereas Ra-226 levels were lower. A recent global review (2008–2022) [11], reported average concentrations of 144.80 ± 750.50 Bq/kg for Th-232, 408.56 ± 253.04 Bq/kg for Ra-226, and 508.78 ± 496.05 Bq/kg for K-40 across 44 countries. Malaysian paddy soils exhibited higher concentrations, averaging 83.85 ± 40.39 Bq/kg for Th-232, 108.11 ± 28.44 Bq/kg for Ra-226, 403.76 ± 224.8 Bq/kg for K-40 [11] .

Compared to Yangjiang, a high background natural radiation area of China, the mean levels of Th-232 and Ra-226 in Gansu agricultural soils were lower, whereas K-40 levels were higher. The average activity concentrations of Ra-226, Th-232, and K-40 in the soil samples of Yangjiang were 66, 109, and 211 Bq/kg, respectively [28].

Sichuan Province, which borders Gansu, shares similar characteristics with the area. Both regions are located in the transitional zone between the first and second levels of China’s topography, featuring complex terrain and climate. A study on the activity concentrations of natural and artificial radionuclides in the eastern Sichuan province revealed that the average radioactivity concentrations of Ra-226, Th-232, and K-40 in soil samples were 26, 49, and 440 Bq/kg, respectively [29].

For uncultivated soils (n = 7) collected in Hexi, the concentrations were 15.5–31.8 Bq/kg for Ra-226, 19.4–50.3 Bq/kg for Th-232, and 349.5–567.2 Bq/k for K-40. The mean values (±SD) were 21.7 ± 6.2 Bq/kg for Ra-226, 31.5 ± 10.6 Bq/kg for Th-232, and 462.9 ± 86.3 Bq/kg for K-40. The specific activities of natural radionuclides in uncultivated soil of Gansu Province were reported in the 1990s, and the concentrations were 14.4–65.3 Bq/kg for Ra-226, 16.4–105.5 Bq/kg for Th-232, and 115.5–807.3 Bq/kg for K-40 [16].

Statistically significant differences (P < .05) were found between agricultural and uncultivated soils for all three radionuclides (Ra-226: H = 6.11, P = .01; Th-232: H = 8.26, P = .04; K-40: H = 8.67, P = .003). Reclamation activities, irrigation activities, and agricultural habits may affect the content of natural radionuclides in agricultural soils [10, 11]. However, the sample size for uncultivated soil (7) is extremely small compared to agricultural soil (108), the statistical power for this particular comparison is limited. The comparative analysis of natural radionuclides in agricultural soil and uncultivated soil deserves further investigation and research.

The mean absorbed dose rate (D) calculated from soil radionuclide concentrations was 63.8 nGy/h (Fig. 5). The result was comparable to that in the east region of Sichuan, which was 60 nGy/h [29]. This exceeds the global average of 59 nGy/h [27, 30]. Field measurements with the HY-425 instrument at the sampling sites yielded a higher average D of 98.0 nGy/h. The calculated dose rate (63.8 nGy/h) was lower than the measured field dose rate (98.0 nGy/h). The discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that the measured results included not only the contribution of radionuclides in soil but also the radioactive contribution of cosmic rays and other substances in the environment. Moreover, besides these three natural radionuclides (Ra-226, Th-232, and K-40) mentioned in this study, there were other natural radionuclides (such as U-238 and U-235) that were not calculated. There was no statistically significant regional variation in the calculated D value (H = 9.01, P = .06). Although the significance is marginal (P = .06), it is worth noting regional trends.

The distribution map of the studied area for the mean value of absorbed dose rate (D).

Visually looking at Figs 2–5, it can be observed that there is a geographical trend with increasing activity and dose moving from north to southeast. Table 3 shows that Jinchang (82.3 nGy/h) and Wuwei (70.3 nGy/h) in the Hexi region have a higher absorbed dose rate (D), followed by the Longnan region (69.7 nGy/h). These variations likely reflect distinct geological backgrounds. Longnan features a stony mountainous terrain. Longzhong has deep loess and soft red soil. Gannan is characterized by gravel and sandy basement, while Hexi contains gray-brown desert soil, slate, fluvo-aquic soil, and sandy soil. Different soils show different degrees of inherent radioactivity. In the report of 1990s [16], the concentration of Ra-226 was higher in black chernozem soil and cinnamon soil, the concentration of Th-232 was higher in cinnamon soil and neoaccumulation soil, and the concentration of K-40 was higher in black soil and irrigated desert soil. The contents of natural radionuclides in different soils are shown in Table 4. Longnan and the corridor from Honggu (Lanzhou) to Wuwei show relatively high radionuclide content. The mean annual effective dose (E) of Gansu soil samples was 78.5 μSv/a, substantially lower than the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) public dose limit of 1 mSv/a3 [30]. So, the radioactivity of soil Samples in Gansu province does not pose a significant harm to local residents and farmers. But it is crucial to recognize that total radiation exposure encompasses additional components beyond terrestrial gamma rays, including cosmic radiation, inhalation of radon and its progeny, ingestion of natural and artificial radionuclides via food and water, and exposures from medical or occupational sources [27].

Conclusion

This study measured natural radionuclides (Ra-226, Th-232, K-40) in soils from Gansu Province using gamma spectrometry. The mean activity concentrations in both agricultural and uncultivated soils were similar to those in neighboring provinces in China, within typical global ranges. A comprehensive radiological hazard assessment (Hex, D, E) showed that all derived parameters remained below internationally recommended safety thresholds (UNSCEAR, ICRP). It will not cause significant harm to local residents and farmers.

These findings establish essential baseline data for ongoing environmental monitoring, radiological risk assessment, and radiation protection planning in this region. This study uncovers the distribution and concentration of natural radioactive substances in a specific environment, contributing valuable insights for environmental protection and public health. In summary, this specific research provides not only essential information on the levels of background radiation in Gansu, China, but also valuable knowledge and resources for global scientific research and practical applications.
