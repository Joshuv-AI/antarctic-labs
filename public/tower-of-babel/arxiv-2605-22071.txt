Duality theory for categorical theories
Lingyuan Ye

Abstract
. We have generalised the notion of categorical theory in model theory to the context of coherent theories. We prove a duality result between the full sub-2-category of pretopoi which are categorical, and the 2-category of profinite monoids. We also study the geometry of profinite monoids via the classifying topos construction, and show it identifies them as a full sub-2-category of the 2-category of topoi. Keywords. duality, model theory, categoricity, coherent logic, profinite monoid.

1.1.
A Logical Motive: Spectrum of First-Order Theories

From a logical perspective, the main motivation for obtaining such a result takes root in the general study of the duality theory for first-order logic. Gödel’s completeness theorem states that the
category
of models of a first-order theory can
separate
the syntax of a theory, viz. if two formulas are interpreted identically over all models, then they are provably equivalent over this theory. A duality result, furthermore, asks for
reconstruction
of the original theory from its models. The central question is what additional information on the category of models allows us to recover the original first-order theory up to equivalence.

In the propositional case, we have the Stone and Priestly duality that answers this problem. More precisely, for a distributive lattice, the additional information on its poset of models that allows us to reconstruct it consists of a certain
topology
, which is called the Priestley topology. Any distributive lattice determines a Priestley topology on its poset of models, and the original distributive lattice is recoverable from this topological poset up to isomorphism.

For first-order theories,
14
proved his celebrated
conceptual completeness
result, which shows that a pretopos is recoverable by the
ultracategorical
structure on its category of models. Roughly speaking, the ultracategory structure records the operation of ultraproducts of models, which in some sense is a categorification of convergence of ultranets in a topological space.
12
has then reformulated the theory of ultracategories à la Makkai and provided an alternative proof for conceptual completeness. Following his efforts, more recently there have been a proliferation of works on ultracategories
(
22
;
19
;
8
)
that are much more categorical in nature. From a quite different perspective,
5
describes the ultrastructures of models of pretopoi as a
Kan-injectivity
condition in the 2-category of topoi. Extending this idea,
4
has provided similar characterisations for other subfragments of geometric logic.

However, unlike Stone or Priestley duality for propositional theories where the additional structures on posets of models are described by topological data, the notion of an ultracategory is much harder to work with. In most of the previously mentioned approaches towards ultracategories, a ultracategory in general involves a
large
amount of data, i.e. the ultrastructure specifies for
each
ultrafilter
\mu
on
each
set
X
how to take ultraproduct w.r.t. an
X
-indexed family of models. However, the ultracategories we care about in practice intuitively only involves only a
small
amount of information, since the ultrastructure should be completely determined by the syntax, which is encoded by a small pretopos. The notable exception is the approach set out in
5
;
4
, which characterises the ultrastructure as a
property
of topoi. We would also like to mention the approach of
2
, which constructs a the spectrum of first-order theory much as a stack of coherent categories on certain topological spaces, akin to the spectrum construction in algebraic geometry.

The initial aim of this paper is to find a special class of pretopoi, where the additional ultrastructure on their categories of models can in fact be described again by a suitable
topology
. Of course, any distributive lattice can be viewed as a first-order theory, thus we are not interested in propositional examples where the category of models form a poset. A non-trivial example has already been shown in
23
, where it shows a duality result for
essentially finite
theories. A theory is essentially finite if its category of models is equivalent to a
finite
category. There is a dual equivalence between Cauchy complete finite categories and essentially finite first-order theories. However, in this case the ultrastructure is trivial, since any ultraproduct on a finite family of points simply selects some model in that family. In other words, the ultrastructure on finite Cauchy complete categories are given by the
discrete topology
.

From this perspective, here we have established the first non-trivial example of a class of first-order theories, such that there exist genuine topological structures on their categories of models that allow for a duality reconstruction theorem. This class is the collection of categorical coherent theories, and the topologies are certain profinite topologies. We will introduce the correct notion of categorical pretopos in
Section

2
, and study some of their properties.
Section

3
will then explain the associated profinite monoid for a categorical pretopos, which constructs a 2-functor
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon}
. Our proof that this constructions gives an equivalence of 2-categories will be based on studying the
classifying topoi
of profinite monoids, which connects to another general motivation of this paper.

1.2.
A Geometric Motive: Classifying Topoi of Profinite Monoids and Semi-Galois Theory

The results in this paper might be independently interesting to the readers without a logical motivation. In particular, for a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
, its
classifying topos

\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
can be viewed as the category of continuous left
\mathcal{M}
-sets. We will recall in
Section

4
that the classifying topos of a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
is a coherent topos. We will show that this construction is 2-functorial, i.e. it exists a 2-functor
\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}
. Here
\mathbf{CTopoi}
is the 2-category of coherent topoi, with 1-cells being coherent geometric morphisms, i.e. those whose inverse images preserve coherent objects.

In
Section

5
, we will show that this 2-functor is
2-fully faithful
. In fact, we will show this 2-functor is also 2-fully faithful when post-composed with the (non-full) inclusion
\mathbf{CTopoi}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Topoi}
, i.e. any geometric morphism between topoi of the form
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
for a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
is automatically coherent (cf.
Theorem

5.4
). This shows we can identify profinite monoids as a full sub-2-category of the 2-category of topoi,

\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Topoi}

and thus implies that
\mathbf{Topoi}
is a good place to study the geometry of profinite monoids, and also the associated logical structure of categorical theories. This result alone suffices to characterise the category of points of the classifying topos
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
of profinite monoids (cf.
Corollary

5.5
). We will use this characterisation to finally prove the main duality theorem in
Section

6
.

Though not being the main focus of this paper, these results have a deep connection to the study of
semi-Galois theory
in the sense of
21
. In fact,
loc. cit.
has introduced the notion of semi-Galois theories, and established a similar duality result between profinite monoids. The results in this paper then provide a new characterisation of semi-Galois theories, i.e. they are exactly categorical pretopoi (equipped with the canonical model). Dually, this also provides an alternative characterisation of classifying topoi of profinite monoids: they are exactly the categorical coherent topoi, i.e. those coherent topoi which has a point where all the points are retract of it.

We mention that the duality result established in this paper is a two-fold generalisation of the one obtained in
loc. cit.
. Firstly, we consider a wider class of maps between profinite monoids, viz. continuous
semigroup
morphisms, which does not necessarily preserve the identity. From the perspective of the geometry of Galois theory, this means our result provides a duality theory accounting for maps not necessarily preserves the base points of the underlying space. From a logical perspective, this means we consider all coherent functors between the categorical pretopoi, instead of those that commutes with the canonical model. Secondly, our duality is
2-categorical
in nature, where the 2-categorical structure is important from the logical perspective, since they account for natural transformation between coherent functors. We believe that this perspective will provide a bridge between model theory and semi-Galois theory. This in turn has a deep connection with the theory of regular languages and finite automata, which is explained in
21
.

categorical

For a non-Boolean pretopos, in general there will be non-trivial retracts of models. Hence, the more appropriate notion of categoricity would be as follows:

Definition 2.2

(Categorical coherent theory)
.

A pretopos
\mathcal{C}
is
categorical
, if
\mathcal{C}
has one model up to retract, i.e. there exists a model
\star
where every model is a retract of
\star
.

For a categorical theory
\mathcal{C}
,
\star
will be called the
canonical model
of
\mathcal{C}
. However, this model will
not
be canonical in the sense of having a universal property. We will see in
Proposition

2.7
that indeed two canonical models of a categorical theory will be
isomorphic
to each other, but such an isomorphism is by no means unique.

The aim of the remaining of this section is to establish various properties of categorical pretopoi, using elementary tools from model theory. Our first observation is that the canonical model
\star
of
\mathcal{C}
contains all the logical information of
\mathcal{C}
, in the sense that it is a
conservative
model:

Lemma 2.3
.

For a categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
, the canonical model
\star
is conservative, i.e.
\star:\mathcal{C}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Set}
is a conservative functor.

Proof.

By completeness (cf. Theorem
A.1
), a mono
\psi\rightarrowtail\varphi
is an isomorphism iff for all models
M
of
\mathcal{C}
,
M\models\varphi\vdash\psi
. By Lemma

, this happens iff
\star\models\varphi\vdash\psi
, i.e.
\star\psi\rightarrowtail\star\varphi
is an isomorphism. Thus,
\star
is conservative.
∎

\star:\mathcal{C}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Fin}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Set}.

Proof.

By Löwenheim–Skolem theorem (cf. Theorem
A.3
), if
\star(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
is infinite, then there exist models
M
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
M\varphi
has arbitrarily large cardinality. In particular, not all such models can be retracts of
\star
since the cardinality of
\star\varphi
is bounded. Hence,
\star(\varphi)
must be a finite set for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

For a categorical pretopos, all of its models are finite.

Proof.

Retracts of finite models are by definition finite.
∎

Remark 2.6

(Theories with only finite models)
.

It is not true that if a pretopos
\mathcal{C}
only has finite models, then
\mathcal{C}
will be categorical. For instance, if
\mathcal{C}
is induced by a propositional theory, i.e.
\mathcal{C}
is the pretopos completion of a distributive lattice, then models of
\mathcal{C}
will also be finite.

As an immediate consequence, we show that the canonical model of a categorical theory
\mathcal{C}
is unique up to isomorphisms (though not up to unique isomorphism):

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a categorical pretopos. If there are two models
M,N
of
\mathcal{C}
such that all models are both retracts of
M
and of
N
, then
M,N
are isomorphic.

Proof.

By assumption,
M
is a retract of
N
, and
N
is a retract of
M
. Composing the two sections we get an endomorphism of
M\rightarrowtail M
, which is pointwise injective. For any
\varphi
, this induces an injection
M\varphi\rightarrowtail M\varphi
. But since
M\varphi
is finite by
Lemma

2.4
, and any injective endomorphism on a finite set must be bijective, the map
M\rightarrowtail M
must be an isomorphism. Similarly for
N
. Thus,
M,N
are isomorphic.
∎

Using these results, we show that categorical pretopoi have many special properties:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a categorical pretopos. Then we have:

(1)

It is two valued, i.e.
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}}(1)\cong 2
;

(2)

It is of finite type, i.e.
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}}(\varphi)
is finite for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
;

(3)

It is locally finite, i.e.
\mathcal{C}(\varphi,\psi)
is a finite set for any
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}
;

(4)

It admits finite colimits, and
\star
preserves them.

Proof.

1: Since
\star
is conservative,
\mathrm{Sub}(1)
must be a subposet of
\mathcal{P}(1)\cong 2
. Since
\mathcal{C}
has a model, it cannot be degenerate, hence
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}}(1)\cong 2
.

2: Similarly,
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{C}}(\varphi)
must be a subposet of
\mathcal{P}(\star\varphi)
, which is finite.

3: A conservative coherent functor must also be
faithful
, thus
\mathcal{C}(\varphi,\psi)
is a subset of
\mathbf{Fin}(\star\varphi,\star\psi)
, which is finite.

4: It suffices to show
\mathcal{C}
has pushout and they are preserved by
\star
. Since any pretopos is
adhesive
(cf.
6
), pushouts of monos always exist and are preserved by any coherent functor. Hence, it suffices to show pushouts of epis exist. Consider an epi
q:\varphi\twoheadrightarrow\psi
and a map
f:\varphi\to\chi
. Let
R\rightrightarrows\varphi
be the kernel pair of
q
. Now since
\mathrm{Sub}(\chi\times\chi)
is finite, there exists a smallest equivalence relation
Q
on
\chi
such that the composite
R\rightarrowtail\varphi\times\varphi\to\chi\times\chi
factors through
Q
. It is then routine to show that the effective coequaliser of
Q\rightrightarrows\chi
is the pushout of
q
along
f
. Furthermore, from this description it is evident that such pushouts are preserved by coherent functors, since they preserve finite limits, finite unions of subobjects, and effective quotients.
∎

Remark 2.9

(Categorical pretopoi as semi-Galois theories)
.

21
has introduced a notion of
semi-Galois category
, which is a pair
(\mathcal{C},F)
where
\mathcal{C}
is a category and
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Fin}
is a functor, such that:

•

\mathcal{C}
has finite limits, finite colimits, and image factorisation;

•

F
preserves finite limits and finite colimits, and is conservative;

By
Proposition

2.8
, for any categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
,
(\mathcal{C},\star)
forms a semi-Galois category in this sense.

Remark 2.10

(Categoricity in model theory)
.

In model theory, a better studied notion of categoricity is that of
\kappa
-categoricity
for some infinite cardinal
\kappa
. A theory is
\kappa
-categorical if up to isomorphism it only has one model of cardinality
\kappa
. By the Ryll–Nardzewski theorem,
\omega
-categorical theories also have finite types (cf.
9
),. In the future we will study these more general theories from a duality perspective.

We can indeed characterise the entire category of models of a categorical theory. Recall that any small category
\mathcal{C}
has a
Cauchy completion

\overline{\mathcal{C}}
, which is the universal category where every idempotent in
\mathcal{C}
splits. Concretely,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
can be identified as the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Psh}(\mathcal{C})
whose objects are retracts of representables. This way, we will show that a pretopos is categorical iff its category of models is the Cauchy completion of the classifying category of a monoid.

Definition 2.11

(Classifying category of a monoid)
.

For any monoid
M
, we use
BM
to denote its
classifying category
, which is a one object category with endomorphism monoid
M
, where the composition is given by the product in
M
. The unique object in
BM
will be denoted as
\star
.

Proposition 2.12
.

A pretopos
\mathcal{C}
is categorical iff there is a monoid
\mathcal{M}
that
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

By construction, every object in
\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
is a retract of
\star
. Thus if
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
, then
\mathcal{C}
will be categorical by definition. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{C}
is categorical, let
\mathcal{M}
be the endomorphism monoid of its canonical point
\star
,

\mathcal{M}\coloneq\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})(\star,\star).

\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
is Cauchy complete by
Lemma

2.1
, thus the fully faithful inclusion
B\mathcal{M}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
induces a fully faithful embedding
\overline{B\mathcal{M}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is categorical, this functor is also essentially surjective, thus we have an equivalence
\overline{B\mathcal{M}}\simeq\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

Remark 2.13

(A concrete description of the Cauchy completion of a monoid)
.

For any monoid
M
, the Cauchy completion
\overline{BM}
of its classifying category can be concretely described as follows:

•

Objects are of the form
\star_{e}
where
e\in M
is an idempotent, i.e.
ee=e
;

•

A morphism
f:\star_{e}\to\star_{d}
is an element
f\in M
such that
fe=f=df
.

The composition of morphisms in
\overline{BM}
is inherited from the product of
M
, and
BM\hookrightarrow\overline{BM}
is included in
\overline{BM}
by sending
\star
to
\star_{1}
. For any idempotent
e
,
\overline{BM}(\star_{e},\star_{e})\cong eMe
.

As a special case, the category of models of a
Boolean
categorical pretopos will be equivalent to the classifying category of a group:

Corollary 2.14
.

For a Boolean categorical pretopos
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{B})\simeq B\mathcal{G}
for a group
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

As mentioned at the start of
Section

2
, if
\mathcal{B}
is Boolean then all morphisms between models of
\mathcal{B}
must be
injective
. Since the canonical model
\star:\mathcal{B}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Fin}
is finite, any injective endomorphism between finite sets is automatically a bijection, which implies the endomorphism monoid of
\star
in fact forms a group
\mathcal{G}
. Furthermore,
B\mathcal{G}
is already Cauchy complete, since an idempotent in
\mathcal{G}
is necessarily the identity. This way, Proposition
2.12
implies we have an equivalence
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{B})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{G}}\simeq B\mathcal{G}
.
∎

Remark 2.15

(Theories with a groupoid of models)
.

In
Section

6
, by the duality result we will see that
Corollary

2.14
in fact describes an if and only if condition, i.e. a categorical pretopos is Boolean iff its category of models is equivalent to a group. This raises an interesting question of when the category of models of a coherent theory is a
group(oid)
. We comment that if
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
is a groupoid for a pretopos
\mathcal{C}
, then again by Löwenheim–Skolem all models of
\mathcal{C}
must be finite, since if a model
M
of
\mathcal{C}
is infinite then there exists an embedding
M\rightarrowtail M^{\prime}
of
M
into a model of strictly larger cardinality. In the context of topos theory, this corresponds to
grouplike
topoi (cf.
(
11
, C3.6)
); they have been briefly studied in
4
.

We end this section by exhibiting an example of a categorical theory. For a one sorted classical first-order theory, since finite cardinality is definable in this language, all models will have a fixed finite cardinality
n
. Thus the automorphism group of the canonical model will be a subgroup of the permutation group
S_{n}
, which is discrete. The following examples allow one to see how more interesting structures can arise when one consider theories with infinitely many sorts:

Example 2.16
.

Let
N
be a classical first-order theory with countably many sorts
\{\,A_{n}\,\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For each sort
A_{n}
,
N
has an axiom stating that the sort has exactly
n
-many elements:

\exists x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\!:\!A_{n}.\,\left(\bigwedge_{i\neq j}x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right)\wedge\forall x\!:\!A_{n}.\,\bigvee_{i\leq n}x=x_{i}.

The theory
N
has exactly one model
\star
up to isomorphism, where
\star
interprets the sort
A_{n}
to an
n
-element set. However, the automorphism group of this model is highly non-trivial. Since any permutation of an
n
-element set induces an isomorphism on the interpretation at
A_{n}
, the automorphism group of
\star
will be the following product,

\mathrm{Aut}(\star)\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n},

Since each
S_{n}
is a finite group, the product
\mathrm{Aut}(M)
has a natural
profinite topology
, which is the product topology, making it into a profinite group.

We have already shown that a categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
uniquely determines a monoid
\mathcal{M}
, such that
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
. However, just like the case for propositional logic, the categorical structure of models does not provide a full reconstruction for the logical theory. In the next section we will see that the monoid
\mathcal{M}
is naturally equipped with a
profinite topology
. The duality result then shows
\mathcal{C}
is determined by
\mathcal{M}
as a profinite monoid.

3.
The Profinite Monoid for a Categorical Theory

For this section, let us fix a categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
with the canonical model
\star
, whose endomorphism monoid is
\mathcal{M}
. The goal of this section is to observe that
\mathcal{M}
is equipped with a natural profinite topology, and in fact the construction
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{M}
gives us a natural 2-functor

\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon},

from the dual of the 2-category of categorical pretopoi, to the 2-category of profinite monoids which we will define shortly.

The profinite topology on
\mathcal{M}
can be deduced from the finiteness of the canonical model
\star
. Let us first consider the following product space

\mathcal{S}\coloneq\prod_{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{Fin}(\star\varphi,\star\varphi).

An element
f
in
\mathcal{S}
is a family of functions
(f_{\varphi})_{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}}
indexed by objects in
\mathcal{C}
.
\mathcal{S}
has a natural product topology which is profinite, i.e. compact Hausdorff and totally disconnected, since it is a product of finite discrete spaces. We will denote the projection maps as

\pi_{\varphi}:\mathcal{S}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{Fin}(\star\varphi,\star\varphi)

for any object
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
. For any subset
U\subseteq\prod_{i\in n}\mathbf{Fin}(\star\varphi_{i},\star\varphi_{i})
, its inverse image under the projection maps by construction is clopen,

\mathcal{S}_{U}\coloneq(\prod_{i\in n}\varphi_{i})^{\mathrm{-1}}(U)=\{\,f\in\mathcal{S}\,\mid\,(f_{\varphi_{i}})_{i\in n}\in U\,\}.

In particular, for any function
g:\star\varphi\to\star\varphi
for some
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{S}_{g}=\mathcal{S}_{\{\,g\,\}}
is clopen in
\mathcal{S}
, and these subspaces form a subbasis of the profinite topology on
\mathcal{S}
.

In this case, there is a natural inclusion
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
of the endomorphism monoid
\mathcal{M}
of
\star
into the product space
\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{M}
carves out those family of functions satisfying the naturality condition for
\mathcal{C}
. This makes
\mathcal{M}
into a profinite monoid:

Lemma 3.1
.

The inclusion
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
identifies
\mathcal{M}
as a closed subspace of
\mathcal{S}
, thus
\mathcal{M}
also has a profinite topology. In this topology,
\mathcal{M}
is a profinite monoid.

Proof.

An element
f
in
\mathcal{M}
by definition is an element in
\mathcal{S}
satisfying the naturality condition for all morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
. The naturality condition on a morphism
\alpha:\varphi\to\psi
in
\mathcal{C}
is indeed
local
in the sense that it only involves
\varphi
and
\psi
. Thus, we may define the clopen subspace
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
as follows,

\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}\coloneq\{\,f\in\mathcal{S}\,\mid\,\star\alpha\circ f_{\varphi}=f_{\psi}\circ\star\alpha\,\},

and
\mathcal{M}=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
denotes the set of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
, will thus be a
closed
subspace of
\mathcal{S}
, hence has a profinite topology.

To show
\mathcal{M}
is a profinite monoid, we need to show the multiplication on
\mathcal{M}
, which is given by composition
\circ
, is continuous. Since subspaces of the form
\mathcal{S}_{g}
for some
g:\star\varphi\to\star\varphi
form a subbasis of
\mathcal{S}
, subspaces of the form
\mathcal{M}_{g}\coloneq\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{S}_{g}
also form a subbasis of
\mathcal{M}
. Hence, it suffices to consider their preimage under
\circ
. Given such a
g
, by definition

\circ^{\mathrm{-1}}(\mathcal{M}_{g})=\{\,(f,h)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,f_{\varphi}\circ h_{\varphi}=g\,\}=\bigcup_{u\circ v=g}\mathcal{M}_{u}\times\mathcal{M}_{v}.

Since there are only finitely many possible decompositions
u\circ v=g
as
\mathbf{Fin}(\star\varphi,\star\varphi)
is finite, the above union is finite, thus
\circ^{\mathrm{-1}}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
is again clopen in
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}
, which implies
\circ
is continuous for the profinite topology.
∎

The construction
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{M}\coloneq\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})(\star,\star)
thus associates a categorical pretopos with the profinite monoid of the endomorphism monoid of its canonical model. Our remaining goal is to establish the 2-functoriality of this construction. Suppose we have two categorical theories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
, with the associated profinite monoid
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
, respectively. Then any coherent functor
\theta:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
will induces a functor

\theta^{*}:\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{D}),

by precomposing with
\theta
. Since we have equivalences
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
and
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{D})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{N}}
by Proposition
2.12
, this equivalently induces a functor

\theta^{*}:\overline{B\mathcal{N}}\to\overline{B\mathcal{M}}.

We thus first provide a concrete description of the category of functors between the Cauchy completions of the classifying categories of two monoids.

Given monoids
M,N
, recall that a
semigroup morphism

f:M\to N
is a map between them that preserves multiplication, but not necessarily the unit. A
conjugate

a:f\to g
between semigroup morphisms
f,g:M\to N
is an element
a\in N
, such that
af(1)=a=g(1)a
. These data organise into a 2-category, which we denote as
\mathbf{Mon}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For monoids
M,N
, we have an equivalence of categories,

\mathbf{Cat}(\overline{BM},\overline{BN})\simeq\mathbf{Mon}(M,N).

Proof Sketch.

Here we provide the data of such an equivalence; for a complete proof showing it is an equivalence, see
17
. By the universal property of Cauchy completion, we have

\mathbf{Cat}(\overline{BM},\overline{BN})\simeq\mathbf{Cat}(BM,\overline{BN}).

By the description of the Cauchy completion
\overline{BN}
in
Remark

2.13
, a functor
BM\to\overline{BN}
can then be equivalently described as a pair
(e,f)
, where
e\in N
is an idempotent and
f:M\to eNe
is a monoid morphism. It is well-known that this is exactly the data of a semigroup map
f:M\to N
, with
e=f(1)
; see e.g.
20
. By the description of morphisms in
\overline{BN}
, it is also easy to see that natural transformations corresponds to conjugates.
∎

Let us then consider the 2-category
\mathbf{ProMon}
of profinite monoids, where now morphisms are given by
continuous
semigroup morphisms and 2-cells are again conjugates between them. The functoriality of the construction given in this section can be described as follows. Let
\mathbf{PT}_{c}
denote the 2-category of categorical pretopoi.

Theorem 3.3
.

The associated profinite monoid of a categorical pretopos extends to a 2-functor

\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon}.

Proof.

We already know that post-composition with coherent functors between two categorical theories
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
, whose associated profinite monoids are
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
respectively, gives us the following functors

\mathbf{PT}_{c}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})\to\mathbf{Cat}(\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C}),\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{D}))\simeq\mathbf{Cat}(\overline{B\mathcal{M}},\overline{B\mathcal{N}})\simeq\mathbf{Mon}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}).

Since
\mathbf{ProMon}
is a locally full sub-2-category of
\mathbf{Mon}
, it suffices to show that for any coherent functor
\theta:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
, the induced semigroup morphism
\theta^{*}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is continuous. Concretely, this map is given as follows. We know that
\theta^{*}\star_{\mathcal{C}}
is a retract of
\star_{\mathcal{D}}
, which gives the a pair of 2-cells
(r,s)
in the following diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Fin}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\star_{\mathcal{D}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta^{*}\star_{\mathcal{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\star_{\mathcal{C}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

Then for any
f\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

\theta^{*}(f)=s(f\circ\theta)r.

To show this map is continuous, consider the clopen
\mathcal{N}_{g}
of
\mathcal{N}
for some
g:\star_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi\to\star_{\mathcal{D}}\varphi
with
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}
. By construction,

(\theta^{*})^{\mathrm{-1}}(\mathcal{N}_{g})=\{\,f\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,s_{\varphi}\circ f_{\theta\varphi}\circ r_{\varphi}=g\,\},

This is a local condition on
\mathcal{M}
, in the sense that it is the inverse image
\pi_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{-1}}(U)
for some subset
U\subseteq\mathbf{Fin}(\star_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi,\star_{\mathcal{C}}\varphi)
, hence is clopen. Since clopens of the form
\mathcal{N}_{g}
form a subbasis, it follows that
\theta^{*}
is continuous.
∎

When building the 2-functor
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon}
we have made the implicit assumption that we have chosen a canonical model for each categorical theory in
\mathbf{PT}_{c}
, and this 2-functor is subject to such a choice. There is no canonical way of building such a 2-functor without choice. However, we will see that there is a 2-functor from profinite monoids to categorical theories which can be canonically constructed, and this will be the focus of the next section.

4.
Classifying Topoi of Profinite Monoids

In this section, we will describe the inverse of the 2-functor
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon}
constructed in
Section

3
. However, instead of directing constructing a categorical pretopos from a profinite monoid, our approach is to study the
classifying topos
for a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
. In particular, we will see that the classifying topos construction induces a 2-functor

\mathbf{Set}[-]:\mathbf{ProMon}\to\mathbf{CTopoi}

from the 2-category of profinite monoids to the 2-category of coherent topoi and coherent geometric morphisms. Later in
Section

5
we will see that this 2-functor is 2-fully faithful, and its image lies in
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}
under the equivalence
\mathbf{CTopoi}\simeq\mathbf{PT}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

The classifying topos of a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
is the topos
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
of
continuous left
\mathcal{M}
-sets
. Explicitly, an object in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is a set
X
equipped with a left
\mathcal{M}
-action
\mathcal{M}\times X\to X
, which is a continuous map when
X
is viewed as a discrete space. Morphisms are simply functions that preserves the
\mathcal{M}
-action.

We start by recalling some basic properties of
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
; for an extensive investigation of topoi induced by topological monoids, we refer the readers to
17
. By forgetting the topology, the monoid
\mathcal{M}
also induces a presheaf topos
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
of arbitrary left
\mathcal{M}
-sets. We have an evident fully faithful functor
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]\hookrightarrow[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
. If
X\in[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
lies in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
, we will say it is
continuous
. We can characterise continuous left
\mathcal{M}
-sets as follows:

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
X\in\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is continuous.

(2)

Any subset
I^{f}_{x}=\{\,g\,\mid\,fx=gx\,\}
for any
x\in X,f\in\mathcal{M}
is (cl)open.

(3)

Any relation
\operatorname{\sim}_{x}=\{\,(f,g)\,\mid\,fx=gx\,\}
for any
x\in X
is (cl)open.

Proof.

This is well-known; for a proof see e.g.
18
.
∎

We observe that the above characterisation of when
X\in[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
is continuous is local on points in
X
, thus it will in particular imply that
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is closed under subobjects in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
. In fact, it is the inverse image part of a hyperconnected geometric morphism
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
(cf.
18
), thus is also closed under quotients.

Our first goal is to show
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is a coherent topos, which we proceed by studying its generating objects. The presheaf topos
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
is generated by the unique representable object, which as a left
\mathcal{M}
-set is the monoid
\mathcal{M}
itself equipped with the canonical
\mathcal{M}
-action.
\mathcal{M}
in general is not continuous, unless
\mathcal{M}
has the discrete topology. However, it will be true that quotients of
\mathcal{M}
which lie in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
generate the latter topos. Let us first characterise continuous quotients of
\mathcal{M}
:

Lemma 4.2
.

Given any surjection
q:\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow N
in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
,
N
is continuous iff the left congruence
\sim_{N}
on
\mathcal{M}
given by
f\sim_{N}g\Leftrightarrow q(f)=q(g)
is open.

Proof.

Suppose
N
is continuous. Consider
x=q(1)
. For any
f\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

I^{f}_{x}=\{\,g\,\mid\,f\cdot x=g\cdot x\,\}=\{\,g\,\mid\,q(f)=q(g)\,\}=\{\,g\,\mid\,f\sim_{N}g\,\}.

In particular, we have that

\operatorname{\sim}_{N}=\bigcup_{f\in\mathcal{M}}I^{f}_{x}\times I^{f}_{x},

which implies
\sim_{N}
is open. On the other hand, suppose
\sim_{N}
is open. For any
x\in N
, suppose
q(h)=x
. Then by definition,

I^{f}_{x}=\{\,g\,\mid\,f\cdot x=g\cdot x\,\}=\{\,g\,\mid\,fh\sim_{N}gh\,\}.

First observe that the following new left congruence is also open

f\sim^{h}_{N}g\coloneq fh\sim_{N}gh,

since it is the preimage of
\sim_{N}
under the continuous map

(-)\cdot h\times(-)\cdot h:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}.

This way, if
f\sim^{h}_{N}g
, then we can find some open neighbourhoods
U_{f},U_{g}
of
f,g
that
U_{f}\times U_{g}\subseteq\operatorname{\sim}^{h}_{N}
. It then follows that

I^{f}_{x}=\bigcup_{f\sim^{h}_{N}g}U_{g},

which is thus open.
∎

The above fact applies to any topological monoid. For a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
, it can be equivalently described as a cofiltered limit of finite monoids (cf.
10
), thus we can write it as follows,

\mathcal{M}\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}M_{i}.

In this case the topology on
\mathcal{M}
is induced by identifying itself as a subspace
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
of the product space. In this case, continuous images of
\mathcal{M}
are governed by its finite continuous quotients
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow M_{i}
:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}M_{i}
be a profinite monoid. For any quotient
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow N
in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
,
N
is continuous iff
N
factors through some
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow N
.

Proof.

Since the congruence
\sim_{i}
induced by the quotient
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow M_{i}
is clopen in
\mathcal{M}
, by Lemma
4.2

M_{i}
is continuous, which implies any quotients of
M_{i}
are also continuous. On the other hand, suppose we have a continuous quotient
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow N
inducing an open equivalence relation
\sim_{N}
on
\mathcal{M}
. By
(
10
, Prop. VI.2.9)
,
\sim_{N}
contains a clopen congruence
\sim_{i}
on
\mathcal{M}
, which means we have a factorisation
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow N
.
∎

Recall that an object
X
in a topos
\mathcal{E}
is
compact
if
X\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{E}}(X)
is a compact object. Equivalently, any jointly surjective family
\{\,Y_{i}\to X\,\}_{i\in I}
contains a finite family which is already jointly surjective.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}M_{i}
be a profinite monoid. For any
X
in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

X
is a compact object in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
;

(2)

X
has a finite underlying set;

(3)

X
can be written as a quotient
\coprod_{i\in n}M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow X
.

Proof.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) and (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) are evident. For (1)
\Rightarrow
(3), suppose
X
is compact. Since
\mathcal{M}
is the only representable in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
, there is a jointly epic family of maps from
\mathcal{M}
to
X
. Given any morphism
\mathcal{M}\to X
, we may factor it as follows,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X_{i}}}

The factorisation
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow X_{i}\rightarrowtail X
is the image factorisation in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
. Since
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is closed under subobjects,
X_{i}
is also continuous, and thus the factorisation
\mathcal{M}\twoheadrightarrow M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow X_{i}
is induced by Lemma
4.3
. Since
X
is compact, it is a finite union
X=\bigcup_{i\in n}X_{i}
, which implies
\coprod_{i\in n}M_{i}\twoheadrightarrow X
.
∎

In particular, Proposition
4.4
implies that compact objects in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
coincide with
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
, the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
having a finite underlying set. Recall that an object
X
is
coherent
in a topos if it is compact, and if any map
Y\to X
from a compact domain has a compact kernel
Y\times_{X}Y
. A topos is coherent if its full subcategory of coherent objects is left exact and forms a separating family (cf.
(
11
, D3.3)
).

Proposition 4.5
.

For any profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is a coherent topos, where its coherent objects are exactly
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
.

Proof.

By Yoneda, any object
X\in\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is covered by a family of morphisms from
\mathcal{M}
in
[BM,\mathbf{Set}]
. Then by the same proof as in Proposition
4.4
, we obtain a cover of
X
by compact objects of the form
M_{i}
. This means that
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
is separating in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
. Hence by
Proposition

4.4
, it suffices to show
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
is left exact, since in this case the kernel of a map
f:Y\to X
in
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
lies in
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
as well, thus will be compact as well. But this holds trivially since finite limits in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is computed as in
[B\mathcal{M},\mathbf{Set}]
, and thus commutes with the forgetful functor
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]\to\mathbf{Set}
. In particular, a finite limit in
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
again has a finite underlying set, thus is contained in
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
as well.
∎

The remaining goal of this section is to describe the 2-functoriality of the construction
\mathcal{M}\mapsto\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
. For any continuous semigroup morphism
\theta:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
between two profinite monoids, let
e=\theta(1)
. We define the base change functor

\theta^{*}:\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}]\to\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],

by sending any continuous
\mathcal{N}
-set
X
to the following
\mathcal{M}
-set,

\theta^{*}X=eX\coloneq\{\,x\in X\,\mid\,\exists y\in X.\,ey=x\,\}.

For any
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
x\in\theta^{*}X
, the action on
\theta^{*}X
is simply

f\cdot x\coloneq\theta(f)\cdot x.

Notice that this is a well-defined action: Associativity is evident; For unity,

1\cdot x=e\cdot x=eey=ey=x.

Furthermore,
\theta^{*}X
is indeed a continuous
\mathcal{M}
-set,

I^{f}_{x}\coloneq\{\,g\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,fx=gx\,\}=\{\,g\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,\theta(f)x=\theta(g)x\,\}=\theta^{\mathrm{-1}}(I^{\theta f}_{x}).

Thus, if
\theta
is continuous,
I^{f}_{x}
must also be open for any
x\in X
and
f\in\mathcal{M}
. This shows that
\theta^{*}
is well-defined on objects.

For morphisms, suppose we have
u:X\to Y
in
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}]
.
\theta^{*}
also does not change the underlying function, viz.
\theta^{*}u=u
. To show this is well-defined, notice that if
x\in\theta^{*}X
, then

u(x)=u(ey)=eu(y)\in\theta^{*}Y,

thus we indeed have a morphism
u:\theta^{*}X\to\theta^{*}Y
. This construction gives us a 2-functor from
\mathbf{ProMon}
, the 2-category of profinite monoids, to
\mathbf{CTopoi}
, the 2-category of coherent topoi and coherent geometric morphisms. Recall that a geometric morphism
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{F}
between two coherent topoi is coherent, if the inverse image
f^{*}
preserves coherent objects.

Theorem 4.6
.

The above construction gives us a 2-functor.

\mathbf{Set}[-]:\mathbf{ProMon}\to\mathbf{CTopoi}.

Proof.

The fact that
\theta^{*}
is the inverse image part of a geometric morphism, and that any conjugacy also induces 2-cells in
\mathbf{Topoi}
, are established by
17
. Since for any
X
,
\theta^{*}X
is a subset of
X
, it follows by Proposition
4.4
that
\theta^{*}
restricts to a functor on coherent objects, thus it lands in
\mathbf{CTopoi}
.
∎

To show the 2-functor in
Theorem

4.6
describes an inverse to
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{ProMon}
constructed in
Section

3
, we need to show, in particular, that
\mathbf{Set}[-]:\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}
is 2-fully faithful and lands in the full subcategory
\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}
. Now in fact
17
has already shown that this 2-functor is
locally fully faithful
, i.e. for any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\mathbf{ProMon}
, the induced functor

\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}])

is fully faithful. In the next section we will prove this is also essentially surjective, thus induces an equivalence.

5.
Profinite Monoids as a Sub-2-Category of Topoi

In this section we will show that the 2-functors given in Theorem
4.6
is in fact 2-fully faithful, and restricts to coherent topoi coming from categorical pretopoi,

\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}\simeq\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}.

It is well-known that the classifying topos construction provides a duality
\mathbf{PT}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{CTopoi}
, and here we use
\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}
to denote the full subcategory of
\mathbf{CTopoi}
corresponding to
\mathbf{PT}_{c}
under this duality. In particular, we may call a coherent topos
categorical
, if its corresponding pretopos is categorical. In fact, we will even show that this 2-functor is still 2-fully faithful when post-composed with the (non-full) inclusion
\mathbf{CTopoi}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Topoi}
, thus we can identify
\mathbf{ProMon}
as a full sub-2-category of topoi.

To show that a coherent topos
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
for a profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
is categorical, we start with the case of
\mathcal{M}
is a
finite discrete
monoid:

Lemma 5.1
.

If
M
is a finite discrete monoid, then
\mathbf{Fin}[M]
is a categorical pretopos, such that

\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbf{Fin}[M])\simeq\overline{BM}.

In other words,
M
can be identified as the endomorphism monoid of the canonical model of
\mathbf{Fin}[M]
. In this case the profinite topology on
M
given in
Lemma

3.1
is discrete.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.5
, the classifying topos of
\mathbf{Fin}[M]
is exactly
\mathbf{Set}[M]
. Since
M
is discrete, in this case
\mathbf{Set}[M]\simeq[BM,\mathbf{Set}]
is a presheaf topos. This way, we have

\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbf{Fin}[M])\simeq\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set}[M])\simeq\mathbf{Flat}(BM^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set})\simeq\mathbf{Ind}(BM)\simeq\overline{BM}.

Here the first equivalence holds by definition; the second equivalence is given by Diaconescu’s theorem (cf.
(
11
, B3.2)
); the third equivalence holds by construction of ind-objects; while the last holds since the ind-completion of a finite category is its Cauchy completion (cf.
(
1
, Prop. 2.6)
). Hence by
Proposition

2.12
,
\mathbf{Fin}[M]
is categorical, and in fact
M
is the endomorphism monoid of the canonical model. Any profinite topology on a finite set is discrete, thus the topology on
M
given in
Lemma

3.1
must be discrete.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Models as idempotents)
.

Notice that a functor
BM^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}
is equivalently a
right
M
-set
, i.e. a set equipped with a right
M
-action. Under the equivalence in
Lemma

5.1
, any an object
\star_{e}\in\overline{BM}
, viz. an idempotent
e\in M
, corresponds to the right
M
-set
eM
, equipped with the canonical right
M
-action.

As a first application, we can characterise the geometric morphisms into
\mathbf{Set}[N]
for a finite discrete monoid
N
:

Proposition 5.3
.

For any profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
and finite discrete monoid
N
, we have

\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},N)\simeq\mathbf{CTopoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N]).

In fact this is still an equivalence if we replace
\mathbf{CTopoi}
with
\mathbf{Topoi}
.

Proof.

As mentioned at the end of Section
4
, it suffices to show that any coherent functor is induced by a continuous semigroup map. By Diaconescu’s theorem again, we have

\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N])\simeq\mathbf{Flat}(N^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]).

where for any geometric morphism
\theta:\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]\to\mathbf{Set}[N]
, the corresponding flat functor is obtained by composing with the Yoneda embedding
BN^{\mathrm{op}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Set}[N]\to\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
. Concretely, it sends the representable object
N
to
\theta^{*}N
.

Now since
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
has a surjective point, whose inverse image is the forgetful functor

\left|-\right|:\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]\to\mathbf{Set},

a functor
F:BN^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is flat iff
\left|-\right|\circ F
is flat. But we have classified all flat functors from
BN^{\mathrm{op}}
to
\mathbf{Set}
in
Lemma

5.1
,

\mathbf{Flat}(BN^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set})\simeq\overline{BN}.

Thus by
Remark

5.2
, we have
\left|\theta^{*}N\right|\cong eN
for some idempotent element
e\in N
, which is finite. Hence, any such
\theta
is automatically coherent, and we have

\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N])\simeq\mathbf{CTopoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N]).

Furthermore, by construction
\theta^{*}N
has an
\mathcal{M}
-action, where now we can define a semigroup homomorphism
\theta:\mathcal{M}\to N
by

\theta(f)\coloneq f\cdot e.

To show this is a semigroup morphism, consider
f,g\in\mathcal{M}
. By definition,
\theta(f)=f\cdot e=ea
and
\theta(g)=g\cdot e=eb
for some
a,b\in N
. This way, since acting on the right for any element in
N
is a morphism of
\mathcal{M}
-set on
\theta^{*}N
, we have

\theta(fg)=fg\cdot e=f\cdot(g\cdot e)=f\cdot(eb)=(f\cdot e)b=eab.

By the same reasoning, acting on the right by
e
gives us

eae=(f\cdot e)e=f\cdot(ee)=f\cdot e=ea.

It follows that

\theta(fg)=eab=eaeb=\theta(f)\theta(g),

which implies
\theta
thus defined is indeed a semigroup morphism. To show
\theta
is continuous, it suffices to consider inverse images of a point
x\in\theta^{*}N
. By definition,

\theta^{\mathrm{-1}}(x)=\{\,f\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,f\cdot e=x\,\}.

If
\theta^{\mathrm{-1}}(x)
is empty, then evidently it is open. If not, then we can choose
f\in\theta^{\mathrm{-1}}(x)
, and observe that

I^{f}_{e}=\{\,g\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,f\cdot e=g\cdot e=x\,\}=\theta^{\mathrm{-1}}(x),

hence is open as well.

It is then straightforward to verify that the geometric morphism induced by the semigroup morphism
\theta:\mathcal{M}\to N
given in
Theorem

4.6
agrees with
\theta^{*}
on the generator
N\in\mathbf{Set}[N]
. This implies they coincide as geometric morphisms since
\mathbf{Set}[N]
is generated by
N
under colimits. This implies that

\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},N)\to\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N])

is essentially surjective, thus gives an equivalence as explained at the end of
Section

4
.
∎

Using this, we can now show the main result of this section:

Theorem 5.4
.

The 2-functor
\mathbf{Set}[-]
constructed in
Theorem

4.6
is 2-fully faithful,

\mathbf{Set}[-]:\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}.

It is still 2-fully faithful when post-composed with the inclusion
\mathbf{CTopoi}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Topoi}
.

Proof.

Again, as mentioned at the end of Section
4
, it suffices to show any geometric morphism is induced by a semigroup morphism between profinite monoids. The crucial observation is that, given any profinite monoid
\mathcal{N}
, by viewing it as a cofiltered limit of finite monoids
\mathcal{N}\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}N_{i}
, we then have an induced family of inclusions of categories,

\{\,\mathbf{Fin}[N_{i}]\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{N}]\,\}_{i\in I}.

Notice that each inclusion
\mathbf{Fin}[N_{i}]\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{N}]
is indeed fully faithful, with their union being
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{N}]
. It follows that
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{N}]\cong\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbf{Fin}[N_{i}]
is the filtered colimit of these pretopoi. By the duality
\mathbf{PT}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{CTopoi}
, we have

\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}]\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbf{Set}[N_{i}],

in the 2-category
\mathbf{CTopoi}
. Thus, we must have

\displaystyle\mathbf{CTopoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}])

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbf{CTopoi}(\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}],\mathbf{Set}[N_{i}]))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},N_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}N_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{ProMon}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

In fact, it follows from
7
that cofiltered limits of coherent topoi and coherent geometric morphisms are computed in
\mathbf{Topoi}
, thus the limit
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{N}]\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\mathbf{Set}[N_{i}]
is also a cofiltered limit in
\mathbf{Topoi}
. Also by Proposition
5.3
, any geometric morphism from
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
to
\mathbf{Set}[N]
is automatically coherent, thus the above equivalences also hold when we replace
\mathbf{CTopoi}
with
\mathbf{Topoi}
.
∎

As a corollary, we can show that the embedding
\mathbf{Set}[-]:\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}
indeed factors through
\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}
:

Corollary 5.5
.

Any coherent topos of the form
\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}]
is categorical. More concretely, we have

\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}])\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}.

Proof.

Notice that
\mathbf{Set}\simeq\mathbf{Set}[1]
for the terminal monoid
1
. Hence, by Theorem
5.4
we have the equivalence

\mathbf{Topoi}(\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set}[\mathcal{M}])\simeq\mathbf{ProMon}(1,\mathcal{M})\simeq\overline{BM}.

The final equivalence is due to the fact that semigroup morphisms from
1
to
\mathcal{M}
can be identified with idempotents in
\mathcal{M}
. Also, conjugacies are exactly morphisms in the Cauchy completion.
∎

6.
Duality for Categorical Theories

The goal in this section is to complete the proof of the following duality,

\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}\simeq\mathbf{ProMon}.

For this, we need to show that the 2-fully faithful embedding
\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}
we have obtained in
Section

5
is also essentially surjective. We will see in this section that this can be obtained as a corollary of
weak conceptual completeness
of pretopoi.

Recall that Section
3
has shown any categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
has a canonical profinite monoid
\mathcal{M}
associated to it, where
\mathcal{M}
is the endomorphism monoid for the canonical model
\star
for
\mathcal{C}
. Notice that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
the canonical model evaluated at
\varphi
, viz. the set
\star\varphi
, has a natural left
\mathcal{M}
-action, since
\mathcal{M}
by definition is the endomorphism monoid of
\star
. In fact, this lifts to a functor as follows:

Lemma 6.1
.

For a categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
with canonical model
\star
and
\mathcal{M}\coloneq\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})(\star,\star)
, there is a coherent functor
U:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
making the following diagram commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Fin}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\star}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left|-\right|}

Proof.

For any
x\in\star\varphi
and any
f\in\mathcal{M}
, by definition

I^{f}_{x}\coloneq\{\,g\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,fx=gx\,\}=\{\,g\in\mathcal{M}\,\mid\,f_{\varphi}x=g_{\varphi}x\,\},

which is clopen in
\mathcal{M}
. Hence,
\star\varphi
with the
\mathcal{M}
-action induced by endomorphisms of
\star
belongs to
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}]
, which we will denote as
U\varphi
.
U
is coherent because
\star
preserves the coherent structures and
\left|-\right|
creates them.
∎

To show that
U
is an equivalence of categories, we recall the weak conceptual completeness established in
15
: a coherent functor
\theta:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between two pretopoi is an equivalence iff
\theta^{*}:\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{D})\to\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})
is an equivalence of categories. We use this to show that
U
in
Lemma

6.1
is an equivalence:

Proposition 6.2
.

For any categorical pretopos
\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{M}
the associated profinite monoid, the functor constructed in
Lemma

6.1
is an equivalence,

U:\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}].

Proof.

Consider the functor on models induced by
U
,

U^{*}:\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{M}])\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}\to\mathbf{Mod}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\overline{B\mathcal{M}}.

By the commutativity of
U
given in Lemma
6.1
,
U^{*}
sends the canonical point in
\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
to the canonical point in
\overline{B\mathcal{M}}
, thus
U^{*}
is essentially surjective. Fully faithfulness is evident, since by construction endomorphisms on
\star
and on
\left|-\right|
coincide when precompose with
U
. By the weak conceptual completeness,
U
itself is an equivalence.
∎

Finally, we can state the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 6.3
.

There is a duality between the 2-category of categorical pretopoi and the 2-category of profinite monoids. Furthermore, it restricts to a duality between Boolean categorical pretopoi and profinite groups,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{BPT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{ProGrp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{ProMon}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Proof.

By Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.5
, there is a 2-fully faithful embedding

\mathbf{ProMon}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{CTopoi}_{c}\simeq\mathbf{PT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}.

Proposition
6.2
then implies that this is also essentially surjective, thus an equivalence. Now by Corollary
2.14
, if the categorical theory is Boolean then its associated profinite monoid is in fact a profinite group. On the other hand, for a profinite group
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathbf{Fin}[\mathcal{G}]
is indeed a Boolean pretopos. Hence, this induces a duality
\mathbf{BPT}_{c}^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathbf{ProGrp}
.
∎

Appendix A
Some results on model theory

For coherent logic, the completeness theorem can be seen as a consequence of Deligne’s theorem, that coherent topoi have enough points (cf.
7
):

Theorem A.1

(Completeness)
.

For any pretopos
\mathcal{C}
and any
\varphi\rightarrowtail\psi
in
\mathcal{C}
, if for all model
M
of
\mathcal{C}
, the image of this mono under
M
is an isomorphism, then itself must be an isomorphism.

A useful reformulation of completeness is compactness (cf.
9
):

Theorem A.2

(Compactness)
.

Given a first-order theory
T
in the sense of specifying sorts, function symbols, relation symbols, and axioms, if any finite subset of
T
has a model, then
T
also has a model.

Using compactness, we can prove Löwenheim–Skolem theorem:

Theorem A.3

(Löwenheim–Skolem)
.

For any
\varphi
in a pretopos
\mathcal{C}
, if there is a model
M
such that
M\varphi
is infinite, then for any infinite cardinal
\kappa
, there exists a model of
\mathcal{C}
that interprets
\varphi
to a set of size at least
\kappa
.

Proof Sketch.

The usual model-theoretic proof can be easily transferred to this case. From a pretopos
\mathcal{C}
we may canonically associate a classical first-order theory
T_{\mathcal{C}}
, by making each object in
\mathcal{C}
a sort, each morphism a function symbol and each subobject a relation symbol. The axioms are those that are true in
\mathcal{C}
. For any cardinality
\kappa
, we may add
\kappa
many new constants of sort
\varphi
, and the axioms that they are distinct (we need negation of equalities to express this). This new theory
T^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
is finitely satisfiable, since we have assumed there is a model
M
of
\mathcal{C}
interpreting
\varphi
to an infinite set. Thus by compactness,
T^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
also has a model, which must be a model of
\mathcal{C}
interpreting
\varphi
to a set of size at least
\kappa
.
∎
