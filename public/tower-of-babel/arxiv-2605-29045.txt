Abstract
. Spherical caps play a crucial role in establishing a criterion for the existence of solutions to the Yamabe problem on a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, similar to the role played by the standard sphere in the problem on a closed Riemannian manifold. This problem is expressed in terms of a nonlinear boundary-value problem, where both the nonlinearity and the boundary condition are critical in the Sobolev sense. This work focuses on the existence of multiple solutions to the Yamabe problem on spherical caps. We show that if the spherical cap is contained in a hemisphere of the standard n n -sphere and n = 5 n=5 or n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 , the Yamabe problem has infinitely many sign-changing solutions. Our approach takes advantage of symmetries and is based on a careful analysis of the loss of compactness of the variational problem.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the critical problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u&\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}=-b|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
,
a_{n}:=n(n-2)
,
n\geq 3
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

This problem plays a crucial role in the question of whether a given compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with boundary has a metric conformally equivalent to
g
that has constant scalar curvature in
M
and for which the boundary has constant mean curvature. For a positive manifold
M
this is equivalent to the existence of a positive solution to the Yamabe problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u+c_{n}R_{g}u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }M,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{g}}+d_{n}h_{g}u=b|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }\partial M,\end{cases}

where
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplacian,
R_{g}
is the scalar curvature of
M
,
h_{g}
is the mean curvature of its boundary and
\nu_{g}
is the outward unit normal on
\partial M
with respect to the metric
g
,
c_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}
and
d_{n}:=\frac{n-2}{2}
. A positive solution
u
to (
1.2
) gives rise to a conformal metric
\widetilde{g}:=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
with the desired properties. In
[
9
,
10
]
Escobar established the existence of a positive solution to (
1.2
) for a large class of manifolds if
|b|
is sufficiently small. Subsequently, Han and Li showed in
[
12
,
13
]
that, in many cases a positive solution exists for every
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
13
]
Han and Li gave a condition for the existence of a positive solution to (
1.2
), similar to that established by Aubin in
[
2
]
for the Yamabe problem on a closed Riemannian manifold. The role of the standard sphere in Aubin’s condition is played by the the spherical cap

B_{b}:=\pi_{b}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),

given by the stereographic projection
\pi_{b}
from the north pole
N_{b}:=(0,\ldots,0,-\frac{b}{n-2},1)
of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
centered at
\xi_{b}:=(0,\ldots,0,-\frac{b}{n-2},0)
; see Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
Spherical cap

The spherical cap plays also a crucial role on the existence of a least energy sign-changing solution to (
1.2
), as was recently shown in
[
4
]
.

The stereographic projection
\pi_{b}
establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the solutions of (
1.1
) and those of problem (
1.2
) on
B_{b}
. The positive solutions to (
1.1
) are the
bubble

U(x):=\left(\frac{1}{1+x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}^{2}+(x_{n}+\frac{b}{n-2})^{2}}\right)^{(n-2)/2},\qquad x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}},

and its dilations
U_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{2-n}{2}}U\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\big)
,
\varepsilon>0
. It is easy to see that (
1.1
) does not have a least energy sign-changing solution. Therefore, it is natural to ask whether it has higher energy sign-changing solutions. As far as we know, this question has not yet been answered.

Many results concerning the existence of nodal solutions to the Yamabe equation

(1.3)

-\Delta u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u\qquad\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n}

in the whole Euclidean space are already well known. The first one was obtained by W.Y. Ding in
[
8
]
, where he established the existence of infinitely many sign-changing solutions taking advantage of the fact that there are groups of isometries on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
whose orbits are all infinite. These isometries translate into conformal diffeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
through the stereographic projection. Other types of nodal solutions to (
1.3
) were subsequently exhibited in
[
6
,
7
,
5
,
3
,
11
,
14
]
.

Ding’s method cannot be used for our problem because the spherical caps are not invariant under the action of the groups that he considered. In
[
6
,
7
]
del Pino, Musso, Pacard and Pistoia obtained another type of solutions using the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method. Their approach takes advantage of the invariance of (
1.3
) under the Kelvin transform, which is not available for problem (
1.1
).

However, as we will explain below, it is possible to adapt some ideas used in
[
5
]
to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
n=5
or
n\geq 7
, then there exists a decreasing sequence
(\beta_{k})
of positive numbers such that, if
b\geq-\beta_{k}
, the problem (
1.1
) has at least
k
different sign-changing solutions.

In particular, (
1.1
) has infinitely many sign-changing solutions for every
b\geq 0
.

Our strategy is as follows. The main difficulty in addressing problem (
1.1
) using variational methods lies in the lack of compactness produced by its invariance under dilations. To maintain control over the blow-up points we use groups of linear isometries whose fixed-point space is the
x_{n}
-axis and whose remaining orbits have infinite cardinality. Furthermore, we require that the group admits a surjective homomorphism
\phi
onto the group
\{1,-1\}
. This allows us to generate functions that are sign-changing by construction, which are called
\phi
-equivariant. Such groups exist only in dimensions
n=5
and
n\geq 7
.

Next, we introduce an auxiliary mixed boundary-value problem (
3.1
) on the half-ball, which does not have a least-energy
\phi
-equivariant solution, and we analyze the behavior of minimizing sequences for it. We observe two different phenomena. Concentration can occur either on the boundary, in which case the blow-up profile is a least-energy
\phi
-equivariant solution to (
1.1
), or it occurs in the interior of the half-ball, in which case the blow-up profile is a least-energy
\phi
-equivariant solution of the Yamabe problem (
1.3
) in the whole space. A detailed description is given in Theorem
3.3
. Furthermore, we see that for
b\geq 0
and for
b
negative sufficiently close to
0
only the first behavior is possible and therefore a
\phi
-equivariant solution for problem (
1.1
) must exist; see Theorem
3.4
. Considering different symmetries we obtain the multiplicity result.

We note that the existence of a least-energy
\phi
-equivariant solution to the Yamabe problem (
1.3
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was already proven in
[
5
]
. Using a result from that paper we obtain the following improvement of Theorem
1.1
for
b=0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
n\geq 5
and
b=0
, the problem (
1.1
) has infinitely many sign-changing solutions.

The existence of a sign-changing solution to the problem (
1.1
) when
b<0
remains an interesting and challenging open problem.

To complete the picture, it is worth mentioning that, for the scalar flat problem obtained by replacing
a_{n}
with
0
and taking
b=1
in the equations (
1.1
), Almaraz and Wang
[
1
]
proved the existence of infinitely many sign-changing solutions for
n\geq 4
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present the symmetric variational setting and compare the least energy level of the problem (
1.1
) with that of (
1.3
) in that setting. In Section
3
we introduce the mixed boundary-value auxiliary problem, we describe the behavior of its
\phi
-equivariant minimizing sequences, and derive an abstract existence result for problem (
1.1
). Finally, in Section
4
we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2.
The symmetric variational setting

Set
p:=\frac{2n}{n-2}
and
q:=\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}
. Let
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):=\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
with its usual norm

(2.1)

\|u\|:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The solutions to the problem (
1.1
) are the critical points of the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-functional
J_{b}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.2)

J_{b}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{p}-\frac{b}{q}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{q},

whose derivative at
u
is

(2.3)

J^{\prime}_{b}(u)v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{p-2}uv-b\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{q-2}uv,\qquad v\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

We are interested in critical points that possess a particular type of symmetries, which we describe below.

Let
G
be a closed subgroup of the group
O(n-1)
of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and let
\phi:G\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{1,-1\}
be a continuous homomorphism of groups. We denote the
G
-orbit of the point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
by

Gy:=\{gy:g\in G\}

and its cardinality by
\#Gy
. We assume that
G
and
\phi
have the following properties:

(G_{1})

\#Gy=\infty
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

(G_{2})

There exists
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
such that
Gy_{0}\neq Ky_{0}
, where
K:=\ker\phi
.

We extend the action of
G
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by setting

gx:=(gx^{\prime},t)\qquad\text{where \ }x=(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The fixed-point space of this action is the set

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:gx=x\text{ \ for all \ }g\in G\}=\{(0,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\},

and the
G
-orbit of every point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that does not belong to this set has infinite cardinality. A subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
G
-invariant if
Gx\subset X
for every
x\in X
, and a function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
will be called
\phi
-equivariant
if

(2.4)

u(gx)=\phi(g)u(x)\quad\text{for every \ }g\in G,\ x\in X.

Property
(G_{2})
implies, in particular, that the homomorphism
\phi
is surjective. Therefore, every nontrivial
\phi
-equivariant function changes sign.

We are interested in finding
\phi
-equivariant solutions to the problem (
1.1
). Note that the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is
G
-invariant. Property
(G_{2})
guarantees that the space

D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):u\text{ \ is \ }\phi\text{-equivariant}\}

has infinite dimension. It is the fixed-point space of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
under the action of
G
given by
(gu)(x):=\phi(g)u(g^{-1}x)
. Hence, by the principle of symmetric criticality
[
15
, Theorem 1.28]
, the
\phi
-equivariant solutions to problem (
1.1
) are the critical points of the restriction of the functional
J_{b}
defined above, to the space
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
. Abusing notation, we denote this restriction by
J_{b}
.

The nontrivial critical points of
J_{b}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}\to\mathbb{R}
belong to the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

\displaystyle:=\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}:u\neq 0\text{ and }J^{\prime}(u)u=0\}

\displaystyle\,=\Big\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}:u\neq 0\text{ and }\|u\|^{2}=F_{b}(u)\Big\},

where

(2.5)

F_{b}(u):=a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{p}+b\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u|^{q}.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

There exists
c_{0}>0
such that
\|u\|\geq c_{0}
for all
u\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

(b)

\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
is a Hilbert submanifold of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
of
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
and a natural constraint for
J_{b}
.

(c)

If
u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\smallsetminus\{0\}
, then there exists a unique
t_{u}\in(0,\infty)
such that
t_{u}u\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. The function
t\mapsto J(tu)
is strictly increasing in
[0,t_{u}]
and strictly decreasing in
[t_{u},\infty)
.

Proof.

This is proved with the same argument used in
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Set

\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}J_{b}(u).

Next, we compare this value with the corresponding one for the Yamabe problem in the whole space

(2.6)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u,\\
u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\phi},\end{cases}

where
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\phi}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\text{ \ is \ }\phi\text{-equivariant}\}
. We write
J_{\infty}:D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\phi}\to\mathbb{R}
for the functional

J_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u|^{p},

associated to this problem, and set

\mathcal{N}_{\infty}^{\phi}:=\{u\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\phi}:u\neq 0\text{ and }J^{\prime}_{\infty}(u)u=0\}

and

\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{\infty}}J_{\infty}(u).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
and
\phi
satisfy
(G_{1})
and
(G_{2})
. Then, the following hold.

(i)

There exists
\bar{\omega}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
\bar{\omega}
solves (
1.1
) for
b=0
and
2J_{0}(\bar{\omega})=\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}
.

(ii)

If

(2.7)

b\geq-\frac{2(n-1)}{n(2n-3)}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla\bar{\omega}|^{2}}{\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\bar{\omega}|^{q}},

then
\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}>\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

(i):
It is shown in
[
5
, Corollary 3.4]
that
\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}
is attained at a function
\omega\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{\infty}
that satisfies
\omega(x^{\prime},t)=\omega(x^{\prime},-t)
for every
(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}(x^{\prime},0)=0
. Therefore, its restriction
\bar{\omega}:=\omega|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
solves (
1.1
) for
b=0
. So it belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and

\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}=J_{\infty}(\omega)=2J_{0}(\bar{\omega}).

(ii):
Set

\bar{c}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla\bar{\omega}|^{2}={a_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\bar{\omega}|^{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bar{d}:=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\bar{\omega}|^{q}

and let
t\in(0,\infty)
be such that
t\bar{\omega}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Then,

(2.8)

\bar{c}t^{2}=\bar{c}t^{p}+b\bar{d}t^{q}.

It follows that

\displaystyle 2J_{0}(t\bar{\omega})-J_{b}(t\bar{\omega})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bar{c}t^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\bar{c}t^{p}+b\frac{1}{q}\bar{d}t^{q}=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\bar{c}t^{p}+b\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}\Big)\bar{d}t^{q}

\displaystyle=t^{q}\Big(\frac{p-2}{2p}\bar{c}t^{p-q}+b\Big(\frac{q+2}{2q}\Big)\bar{d}\Big).

If
b\geq 0
, this equality yields
2J_{0}(t\bar{\omega})>J_{b}(t\bar{\omega})
. On the other hand, if
b<0
, then (
2.8
) implies that
t>1
and using assumption (
2.7
) we obtain

\frac{p-2}{2p}\bar{c}t^{p-q}+b\Big(\frac{q+2}{2q}\Big)\bar{d}>\frac{1}{n}\bar{c}+b\Big(\frac{2n-3}{2n-2}\Big)\bar{d}\geq 0.

Therefore,
2J_{0}(t\bar{\omega})>J_{b}(t\bar{\omega})
if
b
satisfies (
2.7
). Statement
(i)
and Lemma
2.1
(c)
yield

\mu_{\infty}^{\phi}=2J_{0}(\bar{\omega})\geq 2J_{0}(t\bar{\omega})>J_{b}(t\bar{\omega})\geq\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}),

as claimed.
∎

3.
An auxiliary problem

Throughout this section we assume that
G
and
\phi
are as described in Section
2
and satisfy
(G_{1})
and
(G_{2})
.

Let
\mathbb{B}^{n}
be the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:|x|<1\}=\mathbb{B}^{n}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

be the open half-ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. This set is
G
-invariant. We denote by

\Gamma_{0}:=\{x\in\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}:|x|=1\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gamma_{1}:=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

the inner and outer parts of its boundary, and consider the mixed boundary-value problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u&\text{in \ }\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+},\\
u=0&\text{on \ }\Gamma_{0},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{n}}=-b|u|^{\frac{2}{n-2}}u&\text{on \ }\Gamma_{1}.\end{cases}

Let
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
be the space of functions in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
whose trace vanishes on
\Gamma_{0}
. Note that
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\subset D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
via trivial extension. Abusing notation, we denote by
J_{b}
the restriction to
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
of the functional defined in (
2.2
). Set

\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}):=\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})}J_{b}(u).

Lemma 3.1
.

\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
is not attained by
J_{b}
on
\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\subset\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, we have that
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\leq\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
. To prove the opposite inequality, let
\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
be such that
\varphi_{k}
has compact support and
J_{b}(\varphi_{k})\to\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Choose
\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that the support of
\bar{\varphi}_{k}(x):=\varepsilon_{k}^{(2-n)/2}\varphi_{k}(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}x)
is contained in
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}\smallsetminus\overline{\Gamma}_{0}
. Then
\bar{\varphi}_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
and
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\leq J_{b}(\bar{\varphi}_{k})=J_{b}(\varphi_{k})
for all
k
. Therefore,
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\leq\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.

If
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
were attained by
J_{b}
at some
u\in\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
, the trivial extension of
u
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
would be a solution to (
1.1
), contradicting the unique continuation principle.
∎

Our next goal is to describe the behavior of minimizing sequences for
J_{b}
on
\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
. The following lemma highlights the role of assumption
(G_{1})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
satisfies
(G_{1})
then, any given sequences
(\varepsilon_{k})
in
(0,\infty)
and
(y_{k})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
contain subsequences such that one of the following statements holds:

(i)

either there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|y_{k}|<C_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(ii)

or, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
such that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|g_{i}y_{k}-g_{j}y_{k}|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
for any
i\neq j
,
1\leq i,j\leq m
.

Proof.

After passing to a subsequence, we have that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|y_{k}|\to a\in[0,\infty]
.

If
a<\infty
, then
(i)
holds true.

If
a=\infty
, passing to a subsequence we have that
y_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

\frac{y_{k}}{|y_{k}|}\to y\quad\text{in \ }\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

By
(G_{1})
, for any given
m\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
such that
g_{i}y\neq g_{j}y
if
i\neq j
. Let
3d:=\min_{i\neq j}|g_{i}y-g_{j}y|
, and let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

\big|\frac{y_{k}}{|y_{k}|}-y\big|<d
if
k\geq k_{0}
. Then,

\displaystyle d\leq\Big|\frac{g_{i}y_{k}}{|y_{k}|}-\frac{g_{j}y_{k}}{|y_{k}|}\Big|\qquad\text{if \ }i\neq j\text{ \ and \ }k\geq k_{0}.

Therefore,
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|g_{i}y_{k}-g_{j}y_{k}|\geq\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|y_{k}|d\to\infty
if
i\neq j
. This shows that, if
a=\infty
, then
(ii)
holds true.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(u_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
such that
J_{b}(u_{k})\to\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
. Then, after passing to a subsequence, one of the following statements holds true:

(I)

There exists a sequence of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{k})
and a nontrivial solution
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
to the problem (
1.1
) such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}w\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon_{k}}\right)\right\|=0

and
J_{b}(w)=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
.

(II)

There is a sequence of positive numbers
(\varepsilon_{k})
, a sequence of points
\xi_{k}=(0,t_{k})
in
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}
and a nontrivial solution
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\phi}
to the problem (
2.6
) such that

\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{k},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\to\infty,\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}w\left(\frac{\cdot-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\right)\right\|=0

and
\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}\leq J_{\infty}(w)=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
.

Proof.

In what follows, we consider
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\subset D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
by means of trivial extension.

Since
u_{k}\in\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
we have that

J_{b}(u_{k})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}-\frac{b}{q}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{q}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{q}F_{b}(u_{k})=\frac{q-2}{2q}\|u_{k}\|^{2},

with
\|\cdot\|
and
F_{b}
as defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.5
). Since
J_{b}(u_{k})\to\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
, the sequence
(u_{k})
is bounded and, passing to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
. Using Ekeland’s variational principle, we may assume that
J^{\prime}_{b}(u_{k})\to 0
in
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})^{\prime}
. Then, for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
,

o(1)=J^{\prime}_{b}(u_{k})\varphi=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p-2}u_{k}\varphi-b\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{q-2}u_{k}\varphi,

and, passing to the limit, yields
J_{b}^{\prime}(u)=0
in
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})^{\prime}
. We claim that
u=0
. Indeed, using the Brezis-Lieb lemma we see that

(3.2)

\displaystyle J_{b}(u_{k})

\displaystyle=J_{b}(u_{k}-u)+J_{b}(u)+o(1),

\displaystyle J^{\prime}_{b}(u_{k})u_{k}

\displaystyle=J^{\prime}_{b}(u_{k}-u)[u_{k}-u]+J^{\prime}_{b}(u)u+o(1)=J^{\prime}_{b}(u_{k}-u)[u_{k}-u]+o(1),

and, as a consequence,

(3.3)

J_{b}(u_{k}-u)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{q}F_{b}(u_{k}-u)=\frac{q-2}{2q}\|u_{k}-u\|^{2}+o(1).

Equations (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply that

(3.4)

J_{b}(u_{k})\geq J_{b}(u)+o(1).

So, if
u\neq 0
, we would have that
u\in\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
and
J_{b}(u)=\mu_{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
, contradicting Lemma
3.1
. This shows that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
.

Consider the concentration function

Q_{k}(r):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}\Big\},

where
B_{r}(x)
is the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Lemma
2.1
(a)
,

0<c_{0}\leq\|u_{k}\|^{2}=F_{b}(u_{k})+o(1)\leq a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\max\{b,0\}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}+o(1).

Hence, there exists
\kappa_{0}>0
such that, after passing to a subsequence,

(3.5)

0<\kappa_{0}<\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}\qquad\text{for all \ }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\kappa\in(0,\min\{1,\kappa_{0}\})
, to be fixed later. Then, there exist
\varepsilon_{k}>0
and
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(3.6)

\kappa=Q_{k}(\varepsilon_{k})=\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{k})\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{k})\cap\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}.

We claim that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
.

To prove this claim, we argue by contradiction. Assume there is
0<\widehat{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon_{k}
for all
k
and let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(z))
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
u_{k}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
, using (
3.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla(\varphi u_{k})|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2}u_{k})+o(1)

\displaystyle=a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}\varphi^{2}|u_{k}|^{p}+b\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\varphi^{2}|u_{k}|^{q}+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,b}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p-2}|\varphi u_{k}|^{2}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q-2}|\varphi u_{k}|^{2}\Big)+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,b}\left[\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}\cap B_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(z)}|u_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi u_{k}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{1}\cap B_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(z)}|u_{k}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\Big(\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|\varphi u_{k}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q}}\right]+o(1)

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\overline{C}_{n,b}\Big[\kappa^{\frac{2}{n}}+\kappa^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\Big]\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla(\varphi u_{k})|^{2}+o(1).

So choosing
\kappa
sufficiently small we get that
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla(\varphi u_{k})|^{2}=o(1)
and, hence, that

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|\varphi u_{k}|^{q}=o(1)\qquad\text{for all \ }\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(z)),\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Taking
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}\subset B_{\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}}{2}}(z_{1})\cup\cdots\cup B_{\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}}{2}}(z_{\ell})
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\widehat{\varepsilon}}(z_{i}))
such that
\varphi_{i}(z)=1
if
|z-z_{i}|\leq\frac{\widehat{\varepsilon}}{2}
yields

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi_{i}u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|\varphi_{i}u_{k}|^{q}\Big)=o(1),

which contradicts (
3.5
). This shows that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
.

Our next task is to replace the points
x_{k}
with more convenient concentration points
\xi_{k}
. To this end, first, we
write
x_{k}=(y_{k},t_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and recall that Lemma
3.2
gives two alternatives. We claim that the alternative
(ii)
is impossible. Indeed, arguing by contradiction, assume that, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
such that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|g_{i}y_{k}-g_{j}y_{k}|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
for any
i\neq j
. Then, for
k
large enough,
B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(g_{i}x_{k})\cap B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(g_{j}x_{k})=\emptyset
and, as a consequence,

m\kappa=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(g_{i}x_{k})\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{k}}(g_{i}x_{k})\cap\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}\Big)\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}

which is impossible because
(u_{k})
is bounded in
V(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
. Hence, the alternative
(i)
must hold, that is, after passing to a subsequence, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|y_{k}|<C_{0}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
\zeta_{k}=(0,t_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then, (
3.6
) yields

(3.8)

\kappa\leq\int_{B_{(C_{0}+1)\varepsilon_{k}}(\zeta_{k})\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{(C_{0}+1)\varepsilon_{k}}(\zeta_{k})\cap\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}.

Now we claim that, after passing to a subsequence, there exist points
\xi_{k}
and
C_{1}>1
such that

(3.9)

\kappa\leq\int_{B_{C_{1}\varepsilon_{k}}(\xi_{k})\cap\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{C_{1}\varepsilon_{k}}(\xi_{k})\cap\Gamma_{1}}|u_{k}|^{q}

and one of the following statements holds true:

(1)

\xi_{k}=(0,0)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

\xi_{k}=(0,t_{k})\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{k},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\to\infty
.

(3)

\xi_{k}=(0,1)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Indeed, after passing to a subsequence there are three possibilities: If
(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|\zeta_{k}|)
is bounded, we set
\xi_{k}:=(0,0)
. Then, (
3.9
) follows from (
3.8
). If
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\zeta_{k},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\to\infty
we set
\xi_{k}:=\zeta_{k}
. Since
\mathrm{dist}(\zeta_{k},\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\leq(C_{0}+1)\varepsilon_{k}
we have that
\zeta_{k}\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}
and (
3.9
) is simply (
3.8
). Finally, if
(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|\zeta_{k}|)
is unbounded and
(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}|\zeta_{k}-(0,1)|)
is bounded we set
\xi_{k}:=(0,1)
and (
3.9
) follows from (
3.8
).

Set

D_{k}:=\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}-\xi_{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}:=\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}(\Gamma_{1}-\xi_{k}),

and define

w_{k}(z)=\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{k}(\varepsilon_{k}z+\xi_{k})\qquad\text{if \ }z\in D_{k}.

Since
\xi_{k}
is a fixed-point of
G
, the function
w_{k}
is
\phi
-equivariant, and it follows from (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\kappa=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\{\int_{B_{1}(z)\cap D_{k}}|w_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{1}(z)\cap\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}}|w_{k}|^{q}\Big\}\leq\int_{B_{C_{1}}(0)\cap D_{k}}|w_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{C_{1}}(0)\cap\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}}|w_{k}|^{q}.

Furthermore, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, setting
\varphi_{k}(z):=\varphi\big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)
we see that

(3.11)

\int_{D_{k}}\nabla w_{k}\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2}w_{k})-a_{n}\int_{D_{k}}\varphi^{2}|w_{k}|^{p}-b\int_{\widehat{\Gamma}_{k}}\varphi^{2}|w_{k}|^{q}=J_{b}^{\prime}(u_{k})[\varphi_{k}^{2}u_{k}]=o(1).

Next, we analize the behavior of
(w_{k})
in each of the three cases stated above.

Case
(1)
:
\xi_{k}=(0,0)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this case,
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}D_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
w_{k}\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
. Since the sequence
(w_{k})
is bounded in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, a subsequence satisfies
w_{k}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,
w_{k}\to w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
w_{k}\to w
in
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, and
w
is
\phi
-equivariant. We claim that
w\neq 0
. Otherwise, we get a contradiction as follows: Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z))
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\subset D_{k}
for every large enough
k
. So following the argument in (
3
), this time using (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi w_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi w_{k}|^{q}=o(1)\qquad\text{for every \ }\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z)),\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Taking
z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
B_{C_{1}}(0)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{1})\cup\cdots\cup B_{\frac{1}{2}}(z_{\ell})
and
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z_{i}))
such that
\varphi_{i}(z)=1
if
|z-z_{i}|\leq\frac{1}{2}
we obtain

\int_{B_{C_{1}}(0)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|w_{k}|^{p}+\int_{B_{C_{1}}(0)\cap\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|w_{k}|^{q}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi_{i}w_{k}|^{p}+\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\varphi_{i}w_{k}|^{q}\Big)=o(1),

which contradicts (
3.10
). This shows that
w\neq 0
.

Furthermore, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\subset D_{k}
for all large enough
k
, and setting
\varphi_{k}(z):=\varphi\big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)
we see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla w_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|w_{k}|^{p-2}w_{k}\varphi-b\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|w_{k}|^{q-2}w_{k}\varphi=J_{b}^{\prime}(u_{k})[\varphi_{k}|_{\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}]=o(1).

Passing to the limit yields
J_{b}^{\prime}(w)=0
in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\prime}
, that is,
w
is a nontrivial
\phi
-equivariant solution to (
1.1
). Now, using the Brezis-Lieb lemma and arguing as we did at the beginning of the proof, we see that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})+o(1)

\displaystyle=J_{b}(w_{k})=J_{b}(w_{k}-w)+J_{b}(w)+o(1)\geq J_{b}(w_{k}-w)+\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})+o(1),

(3.13)

\displaystyle J_{b}(w_{k}-w)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|w_{k}-w\|^{2}-\frac{1}{q}F_{b}(w_{k}-w)=\frac{q-2}{2q}\|w_{k}-w\|^{2}+o(1),

and, using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}w\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon_{k}}\right)\right\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|w_{k}-w\|=0

and
J_{b}(w)=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
. Hence, in this case, statement
(I)
holds true.

Case
(2)
:
\xi_{k}=(0,t_{k})\in\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{k},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})\to\infty
.

In this case,
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}D_{k}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the extension operator
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\to D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

, where

v^{*}(x^{\prime},t):=\begin{cases}v(x^{\prime},t)&\text{if \ }t>0,\\
v(x^{\prime},-t)&\text{if \ }t<0,\end{cases}

and define
\widehat{w}_{k}\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\widehat{w}_{k}(z):=\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{k}^{*}(\varepsilon_{k}z+\xi_{k}).

Note that
\widehat{w}_{k}
is
\phi
-equivariant and
\widehat{w}_{k}(z)=w_{k}(z)
if
z\in D_{k}
. Since
(\widehat{w}_{k})
is bounded in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, a subsequence satisfies
\widehat{w}_{k}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\widehat{w}_{k}\to w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\widehat{w}_{k}\to w
in
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
w
is
\phi
-equivariant. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z))
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset D_{k}
for every large enough
k
and following the argument in (
3
), using (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we see that, if
w=0
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\varphi w_{k}|^{p}=o(1)\qquad\text{for every \ }\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z)),\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
B_{C_{1}}(0)\subset D_{k}
for sufficiently large
k
, arguing as in Case (1) we obtain

\int_{B_{C_{1}}(0)}|w_{k}|^{p}=o(1).

This contradicts (
3.10
) and shows that
w\neq 0
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset D_{k}
for all large enough
k
, and setting
\varphi_{k}(z):=\varphi\big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)
we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\widehat{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{w}_{k}|^{p-2}w_{k}\varphi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla w_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w_{k}|^{p-2}w_{k}\varphi

\displaystyle=J_{b}^{\prime}(u_{k})[\varphi_{k}]=o(1).

Passing to the limit yields
J_{\infty}^{\prime}(w)=0
in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{\prime}
. Hence,
w
is a nontrivial
\phi
-equivariant solution to (
2.6
).

Fix a radial cut-off function
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\chi(z)=1
if
|z|\leq 1
and
\chi(z)=0
if
|z|\geq 2
and set

\chi_{k}(z):=\chi\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{k}z}{r_{k}}\Big)\qquad\text{where \ }r_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(\xi_{k},\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}).

Note that
w\chi_{k}\in D^{1,2}_{0}(D_{k})
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla(w(\chi_{k}-1)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|z|\geq\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}}|\nabla w|^{2}+\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big)^{2}\int_{\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\leq|z|\leq\frac{2r_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}}w^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{|z|\geq\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}}|\nabla w|^{2}+\Big(\int_{|z|\geq\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}}w^{p}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\right)=o(1).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla(w_{k}-w\chi_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}-2\int_{D_{k}}\nabla w_{k}\cdot\nabla(w\chi_{k})+\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla(w\chi_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\widehat{w}_{k}\cdot\nabla(w\chi_{k})+\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla(w\chi_{k})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}+o(1).

It is also easy to see that

\int_{D_{k}}|w_{k}-w\chi_{k}|^{p}=\int_{D_{k}}|w_{k}|^{p}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p}+o(1).

As a consequence,

\displaystyle J_{b}(u_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla w_{k}|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{D_{k}}|w_{k}|^{p}-\frac{b}{q}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|u_{k}|^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla(w_{k}-w\chi_{k})|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{D_{k}}|\nabla(w_{k}-w\chi_{k})|^{p}-\frac{b}{q}\int_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+}}|v_{k}|^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla w|^{2}-\frac{a_{n}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w|^{p}+o(1)

(3.14)

\displaystyle=J_{b}(v_{k})+J_{\infty}(w)+o(1),

where

v_{k}(x):=u_{k}(x)-\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}w\Big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\Big)\chi\Big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{r_{k}}\Big).

Similarly,

(3.15)

o(1)=J_{b}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}=J_{b}^{\prime}(v_{k})v_{k}+J_{\infty}^{\prime}(w)w+o(1)=J_{b}^{\prime}(v_{k})v_{k}+o(1).

Note that
v_{k}
is
\phi
-equivariant. It follows from (
3.15
) that
v_{k}\to 0
strongly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
, as, otherwise,
J_{b}(v_{k})+o(1)\geq\mu_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
, contradicting (
3
). Therefore,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|u_{k}-\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{2-n}{2}}w\Big(\frac{\cdot-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\Big)\right\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|=0

and
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})=J_{\infty}(w)\geq\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}
. Hence, in this case, we obtain statement
(II)
.

Case
(3)
:
\xi_{k}=(0,1)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

In this case,
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}D_{k}=\mathbb{H}:=\{(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:t<1\}
and the functions

\widehat{w}_{k}(z):=\varepsilon_{k}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}u_{k}^{*}(\varepsilon_{k}z+\xi_{k})

belong to
D^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{H})
. A subsequence satisfies
\widehat{w}_{k}\rightharpoonup w
weakly in
D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\widehat{w}_{k}\to w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\widehat{w}_{k}\to w
in
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
w\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{H})
. If
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(z))
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}-\xi_{k})=\{(x^{\prime},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}:t>-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\}
for large enough
k
and, arguing as in Case (2), we show that
w\neq 0
.

On the other hand, if
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H})
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset D_{k}
for large enough
k
, and setting
\varphi_{k}(z):=\varphi\big(\frac{x-\xi_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}}\big)
we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{H}}\nabla w_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi-a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{H}}|w_{k}|^{p-2}w_{k}\varphi=J_{b}^{\prime}(u_{k})[\varphi_{k}]=o(1).

Passing to the limit shows that
w
is a nontrivial solution to the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=a_{n}|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u,\\
u\in D^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{H}),\end{cases}

in the half-space
\mathbb{H}
. It is well known that this problem does not have a nontrivial solution. Therefore, Case (3) cannot occur.

This completes the proof.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
and
\phi
satisfy
(G_{1})
and
(G_{2})
. If
b
satisfies (
2.7
) then the problem (
1.1
) has a least energy
\phi
-equivariant solution.

Proof.

Let
(u_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{N}_{b}^{\phi}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
such that
J_{b}(u_{k})\to\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})
. By Lemmas
3.1
and
2.2
,
\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{B}^{n}_{+})=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})<\mu^{\phi}_{\infty}
. Hence statement
(I)
in Theorem
3.3
must be true. In particular, there exists a solution
w\in D^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})^{\phi}
to the problem (
1.1
) such that

J_{b}(w)=\mu^{\phi}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
.
∎

4.
Multiple nodal solutions

We begin by giving some examples of
G
and
\phi
that satisfy
(G_{1})
and
(G_{2})
.

For
n\geq 5
we write
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\equiv\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-5}
and a point in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
as
(z,y)
with
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n-5}
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the group
\widehat{G}_{m}
of linear isometries of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
generated by
\mathbb{S}^{1}\cup\{\varrho_{m}\}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the group of unit complex numbers acting by

\zeta z:=(\zeta z_{1},\overline{\zeta}z_{2}),\quad\text{for \ }\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\ \ z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C},

and
\varrho_{m}
is given by

\varrho_{m}z:=\left(\Big(\cos\frac{\pi}{m}\Big)z+\Big(\sin\frac{\pi}{m}\Big)\tau z\right)\qquad\text{for \ }z\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

where
\tau(z_{1},z_{2}):=(-\overline{z}_{2},\overline{z}_{1})
and
\overline{\zeta}
is the complex conjugate of
\zeta
. The
\widehat{G}_{m}
-orbit of any point
z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is a finite union of circles, so it has infinite cardinality.

Let
O(n-5)
be the group of linear isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n-5}
and let
G_{m}:=\widehat{G}_{m}\times O(n-5)
act on
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
by

(4.1)

gx^{\prime}:=(\gamma z,\theta y),

where
g=(\gamma,\theta)\in\Gamma_{m}\times O(n-5)
and
x^{\prime}=(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-5}
. Then, the
G_{m}
-orbit of
x^{\prime}
is

G_{m}x^{\prime}=\widehat{G}_{m}z\times S^{n-6}_{|y|},

where
S^{k-1}_{r}
is the sphere of radius
r
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, centered at the origin. Note that
G_{m}
satisfies
(G_{1})
if and only if
n\neq 6
.

Now we define
\phi_{m}:G_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\{1,-1\}
to be the homomorphism of groups given by

\phi_{m}(\gamma)=1\text{ \ if \ }\gamma\in\mathbb{S}^{1},\qquad\phi_{m}(\varrho_{m})=-1,\qquad\phi_{m}(\theta)=1\text{ \ if \ }\theta\in O(n-5).

Since
\varrho_{m}
has order
2m
this homomorphism is well defined and it clearly satisfies
(G_{2})
.

We note the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\ell=jm
with
j
even and
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
are nontrivial functions that satisfy

u(\varrho_{\ell}x)=-u(x)\quad\text{and}\quad v(\varrho_{m}x)=-v(x)\quad\text{for every \ }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

then
u\neq v
.

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, assume that
u=v
. Since
\varrho_{\ell}^{j}=\varrho_{m}
and
j
is even, we have that

u(\varrho_{m}x)=u(\varrho_{\ell}^{j}x)=u(x)=v(x)=-v(\varrho_{m}x)=-u(\varrho_{m}x)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. This implies that
u
and
v
are trivial, contradicting our assumption.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For each
m
, we apply Lemma
2.2
to
G_{m}
and
\phi_{m}
, as defined above, to obtain a
function
\bar{\omega}_{m}\in\mathcal{N}^{\phi_{m}}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
such that
2J_{0}(\bar{\omega}_{m})=\mu^{\phi_{m}}_{\infty}
, and we set

\beta(G_{m},\phi_{m}):=\frac{2(n-1)}{n(2n-3)}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\nabla\bar{\omega}_{m}|^{2}}{\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\bar{\omega}_{m}|^{q}}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\beta_{k}:=\min_{i=0,\ldots,k-1}\beta(G_{2^{i}},\phi_{2^{i}}).

If
b\geq-\beta_{k}
, then
b
satisfies (
2.7
) for each
G_{2^{i}}
,
\phi_{2^{i}}
with
i=0,\ldots,k-1
, and Theorem
3.4
yields a least energy
\phi_{2^{i}}
-equivariant solution
w_{2^{i}}
to the problem (
1.1
). This solution changes sign by construction.
By Lemma
4.1
, all functions
w_{1},\,w_{2},\ldots,w_{2^{k-1}}
are different.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For each
m\geq 5
, we consider the action of the group
\widehat{G}_{m}
, defined above, on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

gx:=(\gamma z,y),\qquad\text{for all \ }\gamma\in\widehat{G}_{m},\quad x=(z,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-4}.

Let
\psi_{m}:\widehat{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the restriction of
\phi_{m}
to
\widehat{G}_{m}
. Then,
[
5

establishes the existence of a
\psi_{m}
-equivariant solution

to the Yamabe problem (

if

. This implies, in particular, that

) with

, all functions

∎
