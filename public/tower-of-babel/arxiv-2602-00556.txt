Fully discrete approximation of the semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere
David Cohen, Stefano Di Giovacchino, Annika Lang

Abstract
. The semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere driven by multiplicative Gaussian noise is discretized by a stochastic trigonometric integrator in time and a spectral Galerkin approximation in space based on the spherical harmonic functions. Strong and almost sure convergence of the explicit fully discrete numerical scheme are shown. Furthermore, these rates are confirmed by numerical experiments.

2.
Framework

In this section, we introduce notation, the Wiener process on the sphere and
define the considered semilinear stochastic wave equation.
To do this, we mainly follow the work
[
8
]
on the linear case. This preparation is then used to show existence and smoothness of solutions to the considered SPDE.

2.1.
Setting and Wiener process on the sphere

We denote a complete filtered probability space by
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions. We consider the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\|x\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}=1\}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}=(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}})^{1/2}
denotes the Euclidean norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. On the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we consider the geodesic metric given by
d(x,y)=\arccos(\langle x,y\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}})
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Furthermore, we identify Cartesian coordinates
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
with spherical coordinates
(\vartheta,\varphi)\in[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)
by the transformation

x=(\sin(\vartheta)\cos(\varphi),\sin(\vartheta)\sin(\varphi),\cos(\vartheta))
.

The generalization of the Laplace operator to functions defined on the sphere is called the spherical Laplacian or the Laplace–Beltrami operator. In terms of spherical coordinates, this operator
is defined as

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\sin(\vartheta)^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial_{\vartheta}}\left(\sin(\vartheta)\frac{\partial}{\partial_{\vartheta}}\right)+\sin(\vartheta)^{-2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial_{\varphi}^{2}},

see
[
30
, Section 3.4.3]
for instance.
The eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami operator are for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}Y_{\ell,m}=-\ell(\ell+1)Y_{\ell,m}

with the eigenfunctions given by the spherical harmonics
\mathcal{Y}=(Y_{\ell,m})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m\in\{-\ell,\ldots,\ell\}}
, where
Y_{\ell,m}\colon[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{C}
is

Y_{\ell,m}(\vartheta,\varphi)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_{\ell,m}(\cos(\vartheta))\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\varphi}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
m=0,\ldots,\ell
, and
(\vartheta,\varphi)\in[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)
, and

Y_{\ell,m}=(-1)^{m}\overline{Y_{\ell,-m}}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
m=-\ell,\ldots,-1
. Here,
P_{\ell,m}
are the associated Legendre polynomials, see
[
31
, Theorem 2.13]
for details.

Let
\sigma
denote the Lebesgue measure on the sphere.
The subspace
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathbb{R})
of real-valued functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathbb{C})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product defined by

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\langle f(y),g(y)\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

for
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. It is well-known that the spherical harmonics form an orthonormal basis of the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, see e.g.
[
22
, Chapter 7]
.

It is now time to define the driving noise of the considered stochastic wave equation on the sphere.
We refer to
[
28
]
for details. Let
T\in(0,\infty)
and define the time interval
\mathbb{T}=[0,T]
.
In the work
[
28
]
, it is shown that an isotropic
Q
-Wiener process
(W(t),t\in\mathbb{T})
, taking values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, can be expressed by the series expansion

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}W(t,y)&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}a^{\ell,m}(t)Y_{\ell,m}(y)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt{A_{\ell}}\beta_{1}^{\ell,0}(t)Y_{\ell,0}(y)+\sqrt{2A_{\ell}}\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}(\beta_{1}^{\ell,m}(t)\text{Re}Y_{\ell,m}(y)+\beta_{2}^{\ell,m}(t)\text{Im}Y_{\ell,m}(y))\right)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt{A_{\ell}}\beta_{1}^{\ell,0}(t)L_{\ell,0}(\vartheta)+\sqrt{2A_{\ell}}\sum_{m=1}^{\ell}L_{\ell,m}(\vartheta)(\beta_{1}^{\ell,m}(t)\cos(m\varphi)+\beta_{2}^{\ell,m}(t)\sin(m\varphi))\right),\end{split}

where
(\beta_{i}^{\ell,m},i=1,2,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},m=0,\ldots,\ell)

is a sequence of independent, real-valued Brownian motions, with

\beta_{2}^{\ell,0}=0
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in\mathbb{T}
. In addition, for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
m=1,\ldots,\ell
, and
\vartheta\in[0,\pi]
we have set

L_{\ell,m}(\vartheta)=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}P_{\ell,m}(\cos(\vartheta)).

The covariance operator
Q
is similarly characterized as in the introduction of
[
25
]
by

\displaystyle QY_{\ell,m}

\displaystyle=A_{\ell}Y_{\ell,m}

for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell
, meaning that the eigenvalues of
Q

are represented by the given angular power spectrum
(A_{\ell},\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
,
and the eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonic functions.

2.2.
The semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere

We now have the necessary material in place to define the stochastic wave equation on the sphere, which we will numerically discretize in the next sections.

We consider the semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}

driven by an isotropic
Q
-Wiener process

(2)

\partial_{tt}{u}(t)=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}u(t)+f(u(t))+g(u(t))\dot{W}(t),\qquad\text{for $t\in(0,T]$}

with initial values
u(0)=u_{0}
and
\partial_{t}{u}(0)=v_{0}
. The notation
\dot{W}
denotes the formal derivative of the
Q
-Wiener process
W
given by equation (
1
). Here, the given mappings
f,g(u)\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, for
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, are deterministic and verify some smoothness assumptions given below.

We start by writing the stochastic wave equation (
2
) as the system of
stochastic evolution equations

(3)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X(t)=AX(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+F(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+G(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t),\qquad X(0)=X_{0},

where

X(t)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
v(t)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
\partial_{t}u(t)\end{pmatrix},\>A=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},\>F(X(t))=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f(u(t))\end{pmatrix},\>G(X(t))=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
g(u(t))\end{pmatrix},\text{ and }\>X_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
v_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

To show existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
3
), we define the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-s/2}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and their product spaces
\mathbb{H}^{s}=\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=H^{s}\times H^{s-1}

with the norms

(4)

\left\|X\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{s}}=\left\|u\right\|_{s}^{2}+\left\|v\right\|_{{s-1}}^{2}

for
X=(u,v)^{T}\in\mathbb{H}^{s}
. Here, we have used the norms

\|u\|_{s}=\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

for
u\in H^{s}
. For
s=0
, we set
H^{0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{H}^{0}=H^{0}\times H^{-1}
. Note that
\mathbb{H}
is a separable Hilbert space.

Finally, we denote by
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s})}
the usual operator norm in the space
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s};\mathbb{H}^{s})
of bounded linear operators from
\mathbb{H}^{s}
to
\mathbb{H}^{s}
. Similarly, the space
\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{s})
denotes the space of the Hilbert–Schmidt operator from
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
to
H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

The mild solution to the stochastic evolution equation (
3
), as for instance defined in
[
12
]
, reads

(5)

X(t)=E(t)X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}E(t-s)F(X(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}E(t-s)G(X(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)

for all
t\in\mathbb{T}
. Here, the
C_{0}
-group
E(t)=e^{tA}
is given by

E(t)=\begin{pmatrix}C(t)&\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t)\\
-\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}S(t)&C(t)\end{pmatrix},

where we set, for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
C(t)=\cos\left(t\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
and

S(t)=\sin\left(t\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)
for the cosine and sine operators.

We now collect some results on the above cosine and sine operators on the sphere.
These results are very similar to the corresponding results from the Euclidean setting given in
[
23
,
9
,
36
]
for instance. However, since one eigenvalue of the Laplace–Beltrami operator is zero, the proofs of these results on the sphere need to be adapted. First, using the expansions of the cosine and sine operators in spherical harmonics together with the trigonometric identity
\cos(t)^{2}+\sin(t)^{2}=1
, one can get the following estimates.

Lemma 1
.

For any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
,
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
v\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the elements of the group matrix
E(t)
are bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|C(t)u\right\|_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u\right\|_{s},\quad\left\|\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t)v\right\|_{s}\leq\max\left(|t|,\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left\|v\right\|_{{s-1}},\quad\left\|\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}S(t)u\right\|_{{s-1}}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{{s}}.

These bounds yield the group estimate

\left\|E(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s})}\leq 2\max\left(|t|,\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\right).

Proof.

Let us denote by
\{u^{\ell,m}\}_{\ell=0,m=-\ell}^{\infty,\ell}
the coefficients of
u
in the basis given by the spherical harmonics. By definition of the norms and orthonormality of the spherical harmonics, we have the estimates

\displaystyle\left\|C(t)u\right\|^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\cos(t(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}})u\right\|^{2}_{0}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s}\cos^{2}(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{\frac{1}{2}})|u^{\ell,m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s}|u^{\ell,m}|^{2}=\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{s}.

This shows the first estimate in the lemma.

Let us now denote by
\{v^{\ell,m}\}_{\ell=0,m=-\ell}^{\infty,\ell}

the coefficients of
v
in the basis given by the spherical harmonics. As above, by definition of the norms, we obtain analogously

\displaystyle\left\|\left(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t)v\right\|^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle=\left\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{s}{2}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sin(t(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}})v\right\|^{2}_{0}=\left\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{s}{2}}t\text{sinc}(t(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}})v\right\|^{2}_{0}

\displaystyle=t^{2}|v^{0,0}|^{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s}(\ell(\ell+1))^{-1}\sin^{2}(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{\frac{1}{2}})|v^{\ell,m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}|v^{0,0}|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s-1}|v^{\ell,m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left(t^{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s-1}|v^{\ell,m}|^{2}=\max\left(t^{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{s-1}.

This proves the second estimate of the lemma. The proof of the last estimate of the lemma is done similarly and is thus omitted. Combining the above bounds for the cosine and sine operators yields the bound for
E
and concludes the proof.
∎

We collect further estimates on the cosine and sine operators in the next lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\in[0,1]
,
t\in\mathbb{T}
,
u\in H^{s+q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, and
v\in H^{s-1+q}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the sine and cosine operators satisfy the bounds

\displaystyle\|(C(t)-I)u\|_{s}\leq 2t^{q}\|u\|_{s+q},\quad\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t)v\|_{s}\leq\max\left(T,\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{2}}\right)t^{q}\|v\|_{s-1+q},

\displaystyle\left\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{\frac{1}{2}}S(t)u\right\|_{s-1}\leq t^{q}\left\|u\right\|_{s+q}.

These estimates bound the group
E
by

\|E(t)-I\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{s+q};\mathbb{H}^{s})}\leq\max(4,2T)t^{q}.

Proof.

With the same expansion as in the proof of Lemma
1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(C(t)-I)u\|_{s}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}(1+\ell(\ell+1))^{s}(\cos(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2})-1)^{2}|u^{\ell,m}|^{2}.

We observe that the summand for
\ell=0
is zero and that for
\ell\neq 0
on the one hand

(\cos(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2})-1)^{2}\leq 4

and on the other hand

(\cos(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2})-1)^{2}=\left(\int_{0}^{t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2}}\sin(r)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}\leq t^{2}\ell(\ell+1)\leq t^{2}(1+\ell(\ell+1)),

since
|\sin(\cdot)|
is bounded by
1
. Plugging these bounds into the series expansion therefore yields

\|(C(t)-I)u\|_{s}^{2}\leq\begin{cases}4\|u\|_{s}^{2}&\text{for }u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),\\
t^{2}\|u\|_{s+1}^{2}&\text{for }u\in H^{s+1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).\end{cases}

The claim follows by interpolation.

To show the second claim, we use the bound from Lemma
1
and observe further, with the boundedness of the cosine function, that

\sin^{2}(t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2})=\left(\int_{0}^{t(\ell(\ell+1))^{1/2}}\cos(r)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}\leq t^{2}\ell(\ell+1),

which plugged into the series expansion in the proof of Lemma
1
yields

\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t)v\|_{s}^{2}\leq\begin{cases}\max\left(t^{2},\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\|v\|_{s-1}^{2}&\text{for }v\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}),\\
t^{2}\|v\|_{s}^{2}&\text{for }v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2}).\end{cases}

The claim follows by interpolation and by bounding
t
by
T
.
The last claim follows analogously, and its proof is therefore omitted.
∎

In order to treat the nonlinearities in the stochastic wave equation on the sphere (
2
) or equivalently the abstract SPDE (
3
), we make the following assumptions

Assumption 3
.

For any
u,u_{1},u_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the nonlinear mappings

f
and
g
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left\|\left(g(u_{1})-g(u_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}\leq L_{1}\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})},

\displaystyle\left\|f(u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left\|g(u)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}\leq L_{2}\left(1+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right),

for some constants
\delta,L_{1},L_{2}\in(0,\infty)
.

The finiteness of
\|g(u)Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}
gives conditions on the smoothness of the noise. For additive noise, i. e.,
g=I
, the condition simplifies to
\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}=\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{(\delta-1)/2}Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{0})}=\operatorname{Tr}((I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{(\delta-1)}Q)<+\infty
. For
\delta=1
, we are thus in the setting of trace class noise.

With this at hand, we can prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Assume that the stochastic wave equation (
2
) satisfies Assumption
3
. Then, there exist positive constants
\tilde{L}_{1}
and
\tilde{L}_{2}
, such that, for any
X=(u,v)^{T}
,
X_{1}=(u_{1},v_{1})^{T}
, and
X_{2}=(u_{2},v_{2})^{T}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left\|F(X_{1})-F(X_{2})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\left\|\left(G(X_{1})-G(X_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}\leq\tilde{L}_{1}\left\|X_{1}-X_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\left\|F(X)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\left\|G(X)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}\leq\tilde{L}_{2}\left(1+\left\|X\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\right),

where
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})=\mathcal{L}_{2}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2});\mathbb{H})
.

Proof.

By definitions of the mappings
F
and
G
, and of the norm in the product space
\mathbb{H}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|F(X_{1})-F(X_{2})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\left\|\left(G(X_{1})-G(X_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}

\displaystyle=\left\|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})\right\|_{{-1}}+\left\|\left(g(u_{1})-g(u_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\left\|f(u_{1})-f(u_{2})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\left\|\left(g(u_{1})-g(u_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}.

Using Assumption
3
, one then obtains the estimates

\displaystyle\left\|F(X_{1})-F(X_{2})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\left\|\left(G(X_{1})-G(X_{2})\right)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}\left(\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}+\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\right)\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\tilde{L}_{1}\left\|X_{1}-X_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}

with
\tilde{L}_{1}=L_{1}(\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}+\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})})
. This shows the first result of the lemma.

Similarly, one shows the second part of the statement as follows

\displaystyle\left\|F(X)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}+\left\|G(X)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}

\displaystyle=\left\|f(u)\right\|_{-1}+\left\|g(u)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\left\|f(u)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\left\|g(u)Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}

\displaystyle\leq L_{2}\left(\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}+\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\right)\left(1+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right)\leq\tilde{L}_{2}\left(1+\left\|X\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\right),

which concludes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Well-posedness and regularity for the exact solution

After the above preparations, we can establish the following well-posedness result
for the semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere (
3
).

Theorem 5
.

Given Assumption
3
, suppose further that
X_{0}\in L^{2p}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{H}\right)
for some
p\geq 1
. Then, for any
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists, up to a modification, a unique continuous mild solution (
5
) to the semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere (
3
) defined on
[0,T]
. Moreover, the mild solution satisfies

(6)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\|X(t)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\leq c\left(\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})}^{2p}+1\right).

In addition, if
X_{0}\in L^{2p}\left(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\right)
for some
p\geq 1
and for some
\beta\geq 0
, then, for
T\in(0,\infty)
, the mild solution (
5
) satisfies

(7)

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left\|X(t)\right\|^{2p}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}}\bigg]\leq c\left(\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta})}^{2p}+1\right),

with
\gamma\in[0,\min(\beta,\delta,1)]
.

The existence and uniqueness of the mild solution (
5
) as well as the moment bound (
6
) follow from Lemma
4
and
[
12
, Theorem 7.2]
, see also
[
3
,
36
]
in the Euclidean setting. The second bound can be obtained by following the proof of
[
36
, Proposition 3.1]
.

3.
Spatial semi-discretization

In this section, we consider the spectral method used in
[
8
]
for the linear stochastic wave equation on the sphere and apply it to the semilinear SPDE (
2
), or its equivalent abstract formulation (
3
). We first show properties of the semi-discrete solution and then prove its rate of strong convergence.

Since the spherical harmonics build a basis for the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, one can express the two components of the exact solution
X(t)
of the SPDE (
3
) as the following series expansions

(8)

\displaystyle u(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}u^{\ell,m}(t)Y_{\ell,m}\quad\text{and}\quad v(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}v^{\ell,m}(t)Y_{\ell,m},

where
t\in\mathbb{T}
. Here, one has
u^{\ell,m}(t)=\langle u(t),Y_{\ell,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
and
v^{\ell,m}(t)\langle v(t),Y_{\ell,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
for the coefficients of the series expansions.
We set
X^{\ell,m}(t)=\left(u^{\ell,m}(t),v^{\ell,m}(t)\right)^{T}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
m=-\ell,\dots,\ell
and denote (with a slight abuse of notation)

(9)

X(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}{X}^{\ell,m}(t)Y_{\ell,m}.

In order to numerically approximate solutions to the SPDE (
3
) in space, we truncate the series expansion (
8
) at a fixed integer
\kappa\geq 1
. The semi-discrete solution is thus given by similar series expansions

(10)

\displaystyle u^{\kappa}(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{u^{\ell,m}}(t)Y_{\ell,m}\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\kappa}(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{v^{\ell,m}}(t)Y_{\ell,m}

or

(11)

X^{\kappa}(t)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{X^{\ell,m}}(t)Y_{\ell,m}.

Similarly, one has
\widehat{u^{\ell,m}}(t)=\langle u^{\kappa}(t),Y_{\ell,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
and
\widehat{v^{\ell,m}}(t)=\langle v^{\kappa}(t),Y_{\ell,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}
.
Observe that the semi-discrete solution (
11
) verifies the stochastic evolution equation

(12)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X^{\kappa}(t)=A^{\kappa}X^{\kappa}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F(X^{\kappa}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}G(X^{\kappa}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(t),\qquad X^{\kappa}(0)=\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X_{0},

where
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
is the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}u=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}u^{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}
,
for
u=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}u^{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Observe also that we have an abuse of notation and set
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X=\left(\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}x_{1},\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}x_{2}\right)^{T}
for
X=(x_{1},x_{2})^{T}
. The linear operator
A^{\kappa}
is defined as

A^{\kappa}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{\kappa}\\
\Delta^{\kappa}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}

with
I_{\kappa},\Delta^{\kappa}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})\to\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

defined as
I_{\kappa}=I\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}I
and

\Delta^{\kappa}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
with
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})=\text{span}(Y_{\ell,m}:\ell=0,\ldots,\kappa\>\text{and}\>m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell)
.
Since
I_{\kappa}=I,\Delta^{\kappa}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
and
A^{\kappa}=A
when acting on elements from the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, below, we will use both notations when appropriate.

It is a direct implication that the semi-discrete problem (
12
) has the mild formulation

(13)

X^{\kappa}(t)=E_{\kappa}(t)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}E_{\kappa}(t-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F(X^{\kappa}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}E_{\kappa}(t-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}G(X^{\kappa}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s),

where the group
E_{\kappa}
is defined as
E_{\kappa}(t)=E(t)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}E(t)
and similarly for the discrete cosine and sine operators. Since
E(t)=E_{\kappa}(t)
, and simlarly for the cosine and sine operators, when acting on
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, we may use both notations below.

Note that the operators
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
commute. Furthermore, we will make use of the following result on the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 6
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the linear operators
\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
and
I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
satisfy

(14)

\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}w\right\|_{s}\leq\left\|w\right\|_{s}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)w\right\|_{s}\leq\left\|w\right\|_{s}.

Furthermore, for any
s\geq 0,\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and
W=(w_{1},w_{2})^{T}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}
, the linear operator
I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}
satisfies

(15)

\left\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa})W\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\kappa^{-s}\left\|W\right\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{s}},

where, in an abuse of notation, we set
\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)W=\left(\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)w_{1},\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)w_{2}\right)^{T}
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of the first claim in (
14
).

Let us write
w
as the series expansion
w=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}w^{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}
.
The definition of the norms and the orthonormality of the spherical harmonics yield

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}w\right\|^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}w\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}=\left\|\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}w^{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{s}\big|w^{\ell,m}\big|^{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{s}\big|w^{\ell,m}\big|^{2}=\|w\|_{s}^{2},

which shows the first claim.

The second bound in (
14
) can be shown similarly. The proof is thus omitted.

We continue with the proof of (
15
) and expand first
w_{i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}w_{i}^{\ell,m}Y_{\ell,m}
,
i=1,2
. Using similar arguments as above, we thus obtain the estimates

\displaystyle\left\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa})W\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa})w_{1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\left\|\left(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa})w_{2}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=\kappa+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{-s}\left(\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{s}\big|w_{1}^{l,m}\big|^{2}+\left(1+\ell(\ell+1)\right)^{s-1}\big|w_{2}^{l,m}\big|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-2s}\left(\left\|\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)w_{1}\right\|^{2}_{s}+\left\|\left(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\right)w_{2}\right\|^{2}_{s-1}\right)\leq\kappa^{-2s}\left\|W\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{s}},

where we have used the second estimate in (
14
) in the last inequality.
∎

As a first step in the convergence analysis of the spectral method, we state the well-posedness of the semi-discrete solution
X^{\kappa}
. The proof of this result is similar to the proof of Theorem
5
. We thus omit it.

Proposition 7
.

Given Assumption
3
and
X_{0}\in L^{2p}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{H}\right)
for some
p\geq 1
, for any
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists, up to modification, a unique mild solution (
13
) to the semi-discrete problem (
12
). Moreover, such a solution satisfies

(16)

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\ \mathbb{E}\bigg[\left\|X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\leq c\left(\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})}^{2p}+1\right).

Furthermore, if
X_{0}\in L^{2p}\left(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}\right)
, for some
\beta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, then, one has the estimates

(17)

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\ \mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left\|X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|^{2p}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}}\bigg]\leq c\left(\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta})}^{2p}+1\right)

with
\gamma\in[0,\min\left(1,\beta,\delta\right)]
.

The next step in showing strong convergence of the spectral method is to prove
time regularity of the first component of the semi-discrete solution (
10
).

Proposition 8
.

Given Assumption
3
and
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta})
, for some
\beta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, there exists a positive constant
c
such that, for any
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
, the semi-discrete solution
u^{\kappa}
satisfies

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\big\|u^{\kappa}(t_{2})-u^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{\min(1,\beta,\delta)}.

Proof.

Set
\gamma=\min(1,\beta,\delta)
. From the definition of the mild solution (
13
), we can split

\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|u^{\kappa}(t_{2})-u^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right]\leq c\left(J_{1}^{\kappa}+J_{2}^{\kappa}+J_{3}^{\kappa}+J_{4}^{\kappa}\right),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}^{\kappa}=\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|\left(C(t_{2}-t_{1})-I\right)u^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right],\quad J_{2}^{\kappa}=\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|\big(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-t_{1})v^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right],

\displaystyle J_{3}^{\kappa}=\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}f(u^{\kappa}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right],

\displaystyle J_{4}^{\kappa}=\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}g(u^{\kappa}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\right].

We will now bound these four terms uniformly in
\kappa
.

We start with using Lemma
2

and the uniform boundedness of
X^{\kappa}
in Proposition
7
to obtain

J_{1}^{\kappa}\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}\big\|u^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\Omega;H^{\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|^{2p}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big]\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}.

For the second term,
J_{2}^{\kappa}
, similar arguments give the uniform bound

J_{2}^{\kappa}\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}\big\|v^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\Omega;H^{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|^{2p}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big]\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{2p\gamma}.

In order to bound the term
J_{3}^{\kappa}
uniformly, we first use the commutativity of the Laplace–Beltrami operator and the projection operator, Lemma
6
, and Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle J_{3}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)f(u^{\kappa}(s))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)f(u^{\kappa}(s))\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{4p-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\big\|f(u^{\kappa}(s))\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg],

where we made use of Lemma
2
with
q=1
in the last step. Next, we use the linear growth assumption on the coefficients of the SPDE from Assumption
3
and Proposition
7

to get the bounds

\displaystyle J_{3}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{4p}+c_{2}\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{4p-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X^{\kappa}(s)\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{4p}\left(1+\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\left|t_{2}-t_{1}\right|^{4p}.

Finally, bounds for the term
J_{4}^{\kappa}
are obtained using a Burkholder–Davis–Gundy type inequality (see
[
24
, Prop. 2.12]
and
[
12
, Theorem 4.36]
), Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
6
, Lemma
2
with
q=\min(\delta,1)
and Assumption
3
. Since
\min(\delta-1,0)-(\delta-1)\leq 0
, this implies

\displaystyle J_{4}^{\kappa}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\bigg\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)g(u^{\kappa}(s))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{0})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg\|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}S(t_{2}-s)g(u^{\kappa}(s))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{0})}\bigg]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p-1+2p\min(\delta,1)}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg\|g(u^{\kappa}(s))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\min(\delta-1,0)})}\bigg]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg\|\big(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big)^{\frac{\min(\delta-1,0)-(\delta-1)}{2}}\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p-1+2p\min(\delta,1)}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg\|g(u^{\kappa}(s))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}\bigg]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p-1+2p\min(\delta,1)}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[1+\bigg\|X^{\kappa}(s)\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

This yields, thanks to Proposition
7
, the uniform bound

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}J_{4}^{\kappa}\leq c|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p+2p\min(\delta,1)}.

Gathering all the obtained estimates gives

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|u^{\kappa}(t_{2})-u^{\kappa}(t_{1})\big\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\big]\leq c\left(|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2p\gamma}+|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{4p}+|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{p+2p\min(\delta,1)}\right)

and therefore the desired result.
∎

We conclude this section with the following result on the strong and almost sure convergence of the spectral method (
13
) when applied to the semilinear stochastic wave equation on the sphere (
3
).

Theorem 9
.

Given Assumption
3
and
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
for some
p\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 0
, there exists a positive constant
c
such that, for any
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
, the error between the spatial approximation
X^{\kappa}
in (
13
) and the solution to (
3
) is bounded by

\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq c\left(1+\left\|X_{0}\right\|_{L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))}\right)\kappa^{-\min\left(1,\beta,\delta\right)}.

In addition, if
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
for all
p\geq 1
, then asymptotically for any
t\in\mathbb{T}

\big\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\kappa^{-r},\qquad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.},

for any
r<\min(1,\beta,\delta)
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\min(1,\beta,\delta)
. We start by splitting the error into

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\leq c\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|\big(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big)X(t)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\right\}.

For the first term, we apply Lemma
6
and Proposition
5
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|\big(I-\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big)X(t)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-2p\gamma}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\big\|X(t)\big\|^{2p}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{\gamma}}\right]\leq c\bigg(1+\left\|X_{0}\right\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta})}\bigg)\kappa^{-2p\gamma}.

To bound the second term, we observe that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\left\{\left\|\int_{0}^{t}E_{\kappa}(t-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\left(F(X(s))-F(X^{\kappa}(s))\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}E(t-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\left(G(X(s))-G(X^{\kappa}(s))\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=c\,\left(I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t)\right).

We bound the term
I_{1}(t)
applying Hölder’s inequality, and Lemmas
1
and
6
, and Lemma
4
. This yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}I_{1}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|E(t-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big(F(X(s))-F(X^{\kappa}(s))\big)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\big\|F(X(s))-F(X^{\kappa}(s))\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\big\|X(s)-X^{\kappa}(s)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Using the boundedness of the operator
E
, the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy type inequality from
[
12
, Theorem 4.36]
, and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain for the second term that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}I_{2}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\|E(t-T)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H})}^{2p}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}E(T-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\left(G(X(s))-G(X^{\kappa}(s))\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\|E(T-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\left(G(X(s))-G(X^{\kappa}(s))\right)Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}^{2p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|X(s)-X^{\kappa}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}\right]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|X(s)-X^{\kappa}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}\right]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

where we applied in the later steps as for
I_{1}(t)
Lemmas
1
,
6
, and
4
.

Finally, inserting the obtained bounds into (
3
), we get the estimates

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\leq c_{1}\left(1+\left\|X_{0}\right\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta})}\right)\kappa^{-2p\gamma}+c_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left\|X(s)-X^{\kappa}(s)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Since all expressions are finite by Propositions
5
and
7
, an application of Grönwall’s inequality then yields the claimed
L^{2p}
error estimate.

The bound on
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. convergence follows from the
p
independent strong convergence rates. Indeed, for any
r<\min(1,\beta,\delta)
, Chebyschev’s inequality yields

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}\geq\kappa^{-r}\right)\leq\kappa^{2pr}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}}\left\|X(t)-X^{\kappa}(t)\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]\leq c\kappa^{2pr}\kappa^{-2p\gamma}.

For all
2p\geq 1/(|\gamma-r|)
, the series
\sum_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\kappa^{(r-\gamma)2p}<+\infty
converges, and an application of the Borel–Cantelli lemma concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Temporal discretization

Now, we are ready to provide a temporal discretization of the semi-discrete solutions
X^{\kappa}(t)
in (
13
), for
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
. In this section, we propose and study the following stochastic exponential integrator also called stochastic trigonometric integrator in the present setting:

(18)

X_{n}^{\kappa}=E(h)X_{n-1}^{\kappa}+hE(h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F(X_{n-1}^{\kappa})+E(h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}G(X_{n-1}^{\kappa})\Delta W_{n-1},\quad n=1,2,\dots,N,

where
Nh=T
, with time step size
h>0
and
\Delta W_{n-1}=W(t_{n})-W(t_{n-1})
. By iteration and since
E
is a
C_{0}
-group, the time integrator (
18
) can be rewritten as

(19)

X_{n}^{\kappa}=E(t_{n})\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}X_{0}+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F\big(X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor}\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}G(X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor})\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s),

where
t_{n}=nh
and
\lfloor s/h\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
s/h
.

The following result provides a uniform bound for the numerical solution (
18
) of the stochastic wave equation on the sphere (
3
). This result is shown by following the proof of Theorem
5
and
[
36
, Proposition 3.1]
. Its proof is therefore omitted.

Proposition 10
.

Given Assumption
3
and
X_{0}\in L^{2p}\left(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}\right)
, for some
\beta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, the fully discrete approximation (
19
) is uniformly bounded by

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\ \mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\left\|X^{\kappa}_{n}\right\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}^{\gamma}}\bigg]\leq c\bigg(\left\|X_{0}\right\|^{2p}_{L^{2p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}^{\beta})}+1\bigg),

with
\gamma\in[0,\min(\beta,\delta,1)]
.

We now state and prove the main result of the paper: the strong and almost-sure rates of convergence of the stochastic trigonometric integrator (
19
).

Theorem 11
.

Given Assumption
3
and
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;\mathbb{H}^{\beta})
with
\beta\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, the error between the fully discrete solution (
19
) and the spatial approximation (
13
) is uniformly bounded by

\sup_{\kappa\in\mathbb{N}}\ \bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{n})-X^{\kappa}_{n}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq ch^{\min\left(1,\beta,\delta\right)}.

In addition, if
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
for all
p\geq 1
, then asymptotically for any
0\leq t_{n}\leq T

\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{n})-X^{\kappa}_{n}\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq h^{r},\qquad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.},

for any
r<\min(1,\beta,\delta)
.

Proof.

Using the mild formulations of the semi-discrete and fully discrete solutions, (
13
) and (
19
), we split the error in six terms that we bound separately

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{n})-X_{n}^{\kappa}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\leq c\left(J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}\right),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\bigg(F(X^{\kappa}(s))-F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\bigg)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right],

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\big(E(t_{n}-s)-E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right],

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big(F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)-F(X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor})\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right],

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-s)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\left(G(X^{\kappa}(s))-G\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h))\right)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\big(E(t_{n}-s)-E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big(G\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\big)\,\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right],

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}\big(\big(G\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)-G(X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor})\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\big)\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right].

Since
\|(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})^{-r}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\leq 1
for all
r>0
, the first term
J_{1}
is uniformly bounded in
\kappa
by Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
1
, Lemma
6
, Assumption
3
, and Proposition
8
:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\bigg\|f(u^{\kappa}(s))-f(u^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h))\bigg\|^{2p}_{-1}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\big\|\big(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\bigg\|f(u^{\kappa}(s))-f(u^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h))\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg\|u^{\kappa}(s)-u^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\bigg]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}.

To bound
J_{2}
, we first observe that

(20)

\|E(t_{n}-s)-E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\min(\beta,\delta,1)};\mathbb{H})}\leq\|E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H})}\|E(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h-s)-I\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H}^{\min(\beta,\delta,1)};\mathbb{H})}\leq ch^{\min(\beta,\delta,1)}

by Lemmas
1
and
2
.
Therefore, together with Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
6
, Assumption
3
, and Proposition
7
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\bigg\|\big(E(t_{n}-s)-E(t_{n}-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}^{\min(\beta,\delta,1)}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}\big\|\big(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big)^{\frac{\min(\beta,\delta,1)-1}{2}}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg\|f\big(u^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\bigg\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\big\|X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\bigg]\right)

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}.

Similar computations as above yield for
J_{3}
the bounds

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\big\|F\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)-F(X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor})\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]\leq ch\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{0\leq l\leq j}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{l})-X^{\kappa}_{l}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right].

The term
I_{1}
is bounded with a Burkholder–Davis–Gundy type inequality in the same way as
I_{2}(t)
in the proof of Theorem
9
, which yields together with Assumption
3
and Proposition
8

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|(G(X^{\kappa}(s))-G(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)))Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H})}^{2p}\right]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c\big\|\big(I-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\big)^{-\frac{\delta}{2}}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}(H^{0})}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\|(g(u^{\kappa}(s))-g(u^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2p}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(H^{\delta-1})}]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\mathbb{E}[\|u^{\kappa}(s)-u^{\kappa}(t_{j})\|^{2p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})}]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq ch^{2p\ \min(\beta,\delta,1)}.

Combining the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy type inequality with (
20
) on
\mathbb{H}^{\min(\delta,1)}
yields with Lemmas
1
and
6
for
I_{2}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\|E(t_{n}-T)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{H})}^{2p}\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}E(T-\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)(E(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h-s)-I)\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}G\big(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)\big)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W(s)\,\bigg\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq ch^{2p\min(\delta,1)}\int_{0}^{T}\big\|G(X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h))Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathbb{H}^{\min(\delta,1)})}^{2p}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where the last integral is uniformly bounded by Assumption
3
and Proposition
7
.

Bounding
I_{3}
in the same way as
I_{2}
in the proof of Theorem
9
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|X^{\kappa}(\lfloor s/h\rfloor h)-X^{\kappa}_{\lfloor s/h\rfloor}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2p}\right]\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq ch\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{0\leq l\leq j}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{l})-X^{\kappa}_{l}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\right].

Finally, gathering all the above estimates, we end up with the estimates

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{n})-X_{n}^{\kappa}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\leq ch^{2p\min(\beta,\delta,1)}+ch\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{0\leq l\leq j}\big\|X^{\kappa}(t_{j})-X^{\kappa}_{j}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg].

Since all quantities are finite by Propositions
7
and
10
and uniform in
\kappa
, an application of a discrete Grönwall inequality as in
[
24
, Lemma A.4]
yields the claim.

With the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
9
, one can show
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. convergence of the fully discrete solution. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Having Theorem
9
and Theorem
11
available, the convergence of the fully discrete scheme to the solution
X
of the semilinear stochastic wave equation (
3
) follows by the triangle inequality.

Corollary 12
.

Under the assumptions of Theorems
9
and
11
with
\kappa=N
and
h=T/N
, the error of the fully discrete approximation (
19
) of the semilinear stochastic wave equation (
3
) is bounded by

\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{n=1,\dots,N}\big\|X(t_{n})-X^{\kappa}_{n}\big\|^{2p}_{\mathbb{H}}\bigg]\bigg)\leq c\left(\kappa^{-\min(1,\beta,\delta)}+h^{\min(1,\beta,\delta)}\right)\leq cN^{-\min(1,\beta,\delta)}.

In addition, if
X_{0}\in L^{2p}(\Omega;{\mathbb{H}}^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{2}))
for all
p\geq 1
, then asymptotically for any
0\leq t_{n}\leq T

\big\|X(t_{n})-X^{\kappa}_{n}\big\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq(\kappa^{-1}+h)^{r},\qquad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.},

for any
r<\min(1,\beta,\delta)
.
