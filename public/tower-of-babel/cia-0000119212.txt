Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)

concern in DIA and the Department of Defense (DoD). DIA DC-2

cooperation from all components of the Intelligence Community on
POW/MIA matters; cooperation with the Joint Casualty Resolution
Center (JCRC) by HUMINT representatives in the field is

Department of State

Since late 1982, US Embassies in Southeast Asia have on
several occasions reviewed and, where necessary, adjusted their
procedures for screening Indochinese refugees for information on ;
POW/MIA matters. The Department message that initiated the —_
latest review, in early 1984, reemphasized that the matter was of
highest national priority and that every possible effort must be
made to screen Indochina refugees for POW/MIA information.
Coordination between the Embassy officers and JCRC is excellent.

.

In early 1984, the Department of State instructed posts in
friendly countries with missions in Hanoi to convey background
information on the POW/MIA issue to these governments with the
request that they send such information to their missions in
Hanoi and alert them to the US Government's interest in any
POW/MIA information they might encounter.

In late 1983 and early 1984, using high-level approaches, the
US Government obtained Chinese agreement to receive a JCRC
briefing team to explain US interest in collecting POW/MIA
information from former residents of Vietnam now in China. (S)

In early 1984, the Department of State, in connection with an
Australian team's planned trip to Vietnam to discuss Australian
MIAs, reemphasized to the Australian Government US interest in
unaccounted-for Americans in Vietnam. )
