Sublinear growth of 1 1 -cocycles and uniform convexity
Andreas Thom

Abstract
. Let G be a finitely generated group, let π : G → GL ⁡ ( ℰ ) \pi\colon G\to{\rm GL}(\mathcal{E}) be a uniformly bounded c 0 c_{0} -representation on a superreflexive Banach space ℰ \mathcal{E} , and let b : G → ℰ b\colon G\to\mathcal{E} be a 1 1 -cocycle for π \pi . Then b b has sublinear growth with respect to the word length. As a corollary we obtain the corresponding Hilbert space statement for strongly mixing unitary representations.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with a fixed finite symmetric generating set, and let
|g|
denote the associated word length. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Banach space. Let

\pi\colon G\to{\rm GL}(\mathcal{E})

be a representation by linear isomorphisms. The representation
\pi
is called
uniformly bounded
if there exists
C>0
such that
\left\lVert\pi(g)\right\rVert\leq C
for every
g\in G
. We say that
\pi
is a
c_{0}
-representation
if for every
\xi\in\mathcal{E}
and every
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
one has

\varphi(\pi(g)\xi)\to 0\qquad\text{as }g\to\infty.

Equivalently, every orbit vector tends weakly to
0
at infinity; this terminology is standard in
[
1
,
7
]
.
An
affine isometric action
with linear part
\pi
is encoded by a
1
-cocycle

b(gh)=b(g)+\pi(g)b(h)\qquad(g,h\in G).

The
modulus of convexity
is defined for a Banach space
\mathcal{E}
by

\delta_{\mathcal{E}}(\varepsilon):=\inf\Bigl\{1-\Bigl\lVert\frac{x+y}{2}\Bigr\rVert:\left\lVert x\right\rVert=\left\lVert y\right\rVert=1,\ \left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\geq\varepsilon\Bigr\}\qquad(0<\varepsilon\leq 2).

A Banach space is called
uniformly convex
if
\delta_{\mathcal{E}}(\varepsilon)>0
for every
\varepsilon>0
; see, for example, Benyamini–Lindenstrauss
[
3
]
. A Banach space
\mathcal{E}
is called
superreflexive
if it admits an equivalent uniformly convex norm; see, for example,
[
1
,
3
]
for background.

The purpose of this note is to show that for uniformly bounded
c_{0}
-representations on superreflexive Banach spaces, every
1
-cocycle has sublinear growth along the word length, see Theorem
1
.

In the Hilbert space setting, the corresponding class of unitary (or more generally, uniformly bounded) representations are called
strongly mixing
. Growth and rigidity properties of
1
-cocycles into unitary representations are intertwined with affine isometric actions, reduced cohomology, and harmonic cocycles; classical references include Guichardet
[
10
]
, Shalom
[
12
]
, and the monograph of Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette
[
2
]
. More recently, Cornulier–Tessera–Valette
[
6
]
analyzed the relation between sublinear growth and almost coboundaries, while Chifan–Sinclair
[
4
]
and Ozawa
[
11
]
obtained vanishing and weak sublinearity results for cocycles into weakly mixing representations of amenable groups; see also Gournay
[
9
]
for related mixing/cohomological phenomena.

To the best of our knowledge, even the Hilbert space statement for strongly mixing representations (stated here as Corollary
2
) does not seem to be explicitly recorded in the literature, despite the fact that quantitative and qualitative control of cocycles remains an active topic of current research.

Our main result is the following theorem. It will be proved in the next section.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group,
\mathcal{E}
be a superreflexive Banach space,

\pi\colon G\to{\rm GL}(\mathcal{E})

be a uniformly bounded
c_{0}
-representation, and
b\colon G\to\mathcal{E}
be a
1
-cocycle. Then

\limsup_{|g|\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert}{|g|}=0.

Equivalently, every such
1
-cocycle has sublinear growth.

Applications to the setting of Hilbert spaces are discussed in the third section, where we also show that the weaker notion of weak mixing does not force sublinear growth of
1
-cocycles, by constructing a weakly mixing representation of the free group
F_{2}
together with a cocycle of linear growth along a sequence, see Proposition
3
.

A final section records a consequence of Theorem
1
for vanishing of reduced cohomology for finitely generated amenable groups admitting controlled Følner sequences, see Corollary
5
. This extends results of Tessera
[
13
]
, who proved the vanishing of reduced cohomology for the
L^{p}
-representation associated with a mixing measure preserving actions
G\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
, where
\mu
is an infinite measure and
1<p<\infty
.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of Theorem
1
.

At first, we use
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
to replace the given norm on
\mathcal{E}
by an equivalent uniformly convex norm that is preserved by
\pi
. Hence, we may assume that
\mathcal{E}
is uniformly convex and
\pi
is an isometric representation. Passing to the underlying real Banach space, we may assume that
\mathcal{E}
is real.
Assume for contradiction that

\alpha:=\limsup_{|g|\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert}{|g|}>0.

For
g\neq e
set

q(g):=\frac{\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert}{|g|}.

Step 1.

Fix

\Delta:=\delta_{\mathcal{E}}(1/2)>0

and choose
\eta>0
so small that

0<\eta<\frac{\alpha\Delta}{8}.

By the definition of
\limsup
, there exists
R\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|x|\geq R
implies
q(x)\leq\alpha+\eta.

Equivalently,

|x|\geq R\implies\left\lVert b(x)\right\rVert\leq(\alpha+\eta)|x|.

Step 2.

For
n\geq 1
, let

\alpha_{n}:=\max\{\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert:|g|=n\}.

Since spheres of radius
n
are finite, the maximum exists. The sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is subadditive: if
|g|=n+m
and
g=xy
is obtained by cutting a geodesic word for
g
after
n
letters, then
|x|=n
,
|y|=m
, and

\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert b(x)\right\rVert+\left\lVert b(y)\right\rVert\leq\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{m}.

Hence Fekete’s lemma gives the existence of

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n}.

Since
\alpha_{n}/n=\max_{|g|=n}q(g)
, this limit equals
\alpha
. In particular, for every
n
there exists
g_{n}\in G
such that

|g_{n}|=n\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\lVert b(g_{n})\right\rVert=\alpha_{n}\geq\alpha n.

Fix a geodesic word

g_{n}=s_{1}^{(n)}\cdots s_{n}^{(n)}

for each
n
.

Step 3.

For
n
large enough, partition the geodesic word for
g_{n}
into consecutive geodesic subwords

g_{n}=h_{1}^{(n)}h_{2}^{(n)}\cdots h_{m_{n}}^{(n)}

such that for every
i
,

R\leq\ell_{i}^{(n)}:=|h_{i}^{(n)}|\leq 2R,

and

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}\ell_{i}^{(n)}=n.

Fix
n
and suppress the superscript
(n)
from the notation. For
1\leq i\leq m_{n}
define the prefix

t_{i}:=h_{1}\cdots h_{i-1},

with
t_{1}=e
, and set

v_{i}:=\pi(t_{i})b(h_{i}).

Repeatedly applying the cocycle identity gives

b(g_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}v_{i}.

Since
\pi(t_{i})
is an isometry,

\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert=\left\lVert b(h_{i})\right\rVert.

As
|h_{i}|\geq R
, estimate (1) yields

\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert\leq(\alpha+\eta)\ell_{i}.

Because of (2),
b(g_{n})\neq 0
. Choose a norming functional
j_{n}\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
with

\left\lVert j_{n}\right\rVert=1
and
j_{n}(b(g_{n}))=\left\lVert b(g_{n})\right\rVert.

Step 4.

For each block define

d_{i}:=(\alpha+\eta)\ell_{i}-j_{n}(v_{i}).

Since
j_{n}(v_{i})\leq\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert\leq(\alpha+\eta)\ell_{i}
by (5), we have
d_{i}\geq 0
. Summing and using (2), (3), and (4), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}d_{i}

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\eta)\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}\ell_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}j_{n}(v_{i})

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\eta)n-j_{n}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}v_{i}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\eta)n-j_{n}(b(g_{n}))

\displaystyle=(\alpha+\eta)n-\left\lVert b(g_{n})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\eta n.

Thus

\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}d_{i}\leq\eta n.

Call an index
i

good
if

d_{i}\leq 4\eta\,\ell_{i}.

Let
G_{n}
be the set of good indices. Then

\sum_{i\notin G_{n}}4\eta\,\ell_{i}<\sum_{i\notin G_{n}}d_{i}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}d_{i}\leq\eta n,

so

\sum_{i\notin G_{n}}\ell_{i}\leq\frac{1}{4}n.

Therefore

\sum_{i\in G_{n}}\ell_{i}\geq\frac{3}{4}n.

Step 5.

Fix
i\in G_{n}
. Since
d_{i}\leq 4\eta\ell_{i}
, we have

j_{n}(v_{i})\geq(\alpha-3\eta)\ell_{i}.

Combining (5) and (7), we get

\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert\geq j_{n}(v_{i})\geq(\alpha-3\eta)\ell_{i}>0.

Hence the normalized vector

w_{i}:=\frac{v_{i}}{\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert}\in S_{\mathcal{E}}

is well defined, and

j_{n}(w_{i})=\frac{j_{n}(v_{i})}{\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert}\geq\frac{(\alpha-3\eta)\ell_{i}}{(\alpha+\eta)\ell_{i}}=1-\frac{4\eta}{\alpha+\eta}>1-\Delta.

Now fix
i,j\in G_{n}
. Since
\left\lVert w_{i}\right\rVert=\left\lVert w_{j}\right\rVert=1
and
\left\lVert j_{n}\right\rVert=1
, we get

\Bigl\lVert\frac{w_{i}+w_{j}}{2}\Bigr\rVert\geq j_{n}\Bigl(\frac{w_{i}+w_{j}}{2}\Bigr)=\frac{j_{n}(w_{i})+j_{n}(w_{j})}{2}>1-\Delta.

By the definition of
\Delta=\delta_{\mathcal{E}}(1/2)
, this forces
\left\lVert w_{i}-w_{j}\right\rVert<\frac{1}{2}
, hence

\left\lVert w_{i}-w_{j}\right\rVert<\frac{1}{2}.

Step 6.

Let

\mathcal{A}_{R}:=\{h\in G:R\leq|h|\leq 2R\}.

This is a finite set; write
M_{R}:=|\mathcal{A}_{R}|
. Since each good block has length at most
2R
, estimate (6) implies

2R\,|G_{n}|\geq\sum_{i\in G_{n}}\ell_{i}\geq\frac{3}{4}n,

hence

|G_{n}|\geq\frac{3}{8R}n.

Among the good blocks, some element of
\mathcal{A}_{R}
occurs at least
|G_{n}|/M_{R}
times. Therefore there exist
h^{(n)}\in\mathcal{A}_{R}
and a subset
I_{n}\subset G_{n}
such that

h_{i}=h^{(n)}\quad(i\in I_{n}),

and

|I_{n}|\geq\frac{3}{8RM_{R}}n.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
h^{(n)}=h
is independent of
n
. Set

X_{n}:=\{t_{i}:i\in I_{n}\}.

Since the prefixes
t_{i}
are distinct, we have

|X_{n}|=|I_{n}|\geq\frac{3}{8RM_{R}}n,

so
|X_{n}|\to\infty
.

For
t\in X_{n}
, the corresponding vector is

\pi(t)b(h)=v_{i}

for the unique
i\in I_{n}
with
t=t_{i}
. By (8),

\left\lVert b(h)\right\rVert=\left\lVert v_{i}\right\rVert\geq(\alpha-3\eta)|h|>0.

Moreover, if
t,t^{\prime}\in X_{n}
correspond to good indices
i,j\in I_{n}
, then (9) gives

\lVert\pi(t)b(h)-\pi(t^{\prime})b(h)\rVert<\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert b(h)\right\rVert.

Step 7.

Since
|X_{n}|\to\infty
and balls in
G
are finite, the difference sets
X_{n}^{-1}X_{n}
cannot stay inside one fixed finite subset of
G
. Thus, we may choose

\gamma_{n}\in X_{n}^{-1}X_{n}\qquad\text{with}\qquad|\gamma_{n}|\to\infty.

Write
\gamma_{n}=t_{n}^{-1}s_{n}
with
s_{n},t_{n}\in X_{n}
. Then by (11),

\left\lVert\pi(\gamma_{n})b(h)-b(h)\right\rVert=\left\lVert\pi(t_{n})^{-1}(\pi(s_{n})b(h)-\pi(t_{n})b(h))\right\rVert<\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert b(h)\right\rVert.

Choose a norming functional
\psi\in\mathcal{E}^{\ast}
for
b(h)
, so
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert=1
and
\psi(b(h))=\left\lVert b(h)\right\rVert
. By (12),

\psi(\pi(\gamma_{n})b(h))\geq\psi(b(h))-\left\lVert\pi(\gamma_{n})b(h)-b(h)\right\rVert>\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert b(h)\right\rVert\qquad(n\geq 1).

On the other hand, since
\pi
is a
c_{0}
-representation and
|\gamma_{n}|\to\infty
, we must have

\psi(\pi(\gamma_{n})b(h))\to 0,

contradicting (13). This contradiction proves the theorem.
∎

3.
The setting of Hilbert spaces

Corollary 2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group, let
\pi\colon G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a strongly mixing unitary representation on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and let
b\colon G\to\mathcal{H}
be a
1
-cocycle. Then

\limsup_{|g|\to\infty}\frac{\left\lVert b(g)\right\rVert}{|g|}=0.

Proof.

Every Hilbert space is uniformly convex. Moreover, for unitary representations on Hilbert space, the condition of being
c_{0}
is exactly the usual strong mixing condition, since the dual can be identified with the Hilbert space itself through the inner product. Therefore Corollary
2
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
.
∎

A weaker notion of mixing is that of weak mixing, which is equivalent to the absence of non-zero finite-dimensional subrepresentations. In this section we show that weak mixing does not force sublinear growth of
1
-cocycle; a fact that is surely known to experts. More precisely, we construct a weakly mixing representation of the free group
F_{2}
together with a cocycle of linear growth along a sequence.

Proposition 3
.

There exists a finitely generated group
G
, a weakly mixing unitary representation
\pi\colon G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
, and a
1
-cocycle
b\colon G\to\mathcal{H}
such that

\limsup_{|g|\to\infty}\frac{\|b(g)\|}{|g|}=1.

Proof.

Let
F_{2}=\langle x,y\rangle
, and identify
\mathbb{Z}
with the subgroup generated by
x
. We claim that the quasi-regular representation

\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}\colon F_{2}\to\mathcal{U}(\ell^{2}(F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}))

is weakly mixing, and there exists a
1
-cocycle

b\colon F_{2}\to\ell^{2}(F_{2}/\mathbb{Z})

such that

b(x^{n})=n\,\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
n\geq 1
.
Since
\mathbb{Z}
has infinite index in
F_{2}
, the representation
\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}
is weakly mixing. One way to see this is that weak mixing of
\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}
is equivalent to the absence of non-trivial invariant vectors in
\ell^{2}(F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}\times F_{2}/\mathbb{Z})
with respect to the diagonal
F_{2}
-action, and this is equivalent to the absence of finite
F_{2}
-orbits in

F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}\times F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}.

Since
F_{2}
is the free group on the generators
x
and
y
, a
1
-cocycle for a unitary representation of
F_{2}
is uniquely determined by its values on
x
and
y
, and these values can be prescribed arbitrarily. We apply this to the representation
\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}
and choose

b(x)=\delta_{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad b(y)=0.

Thus there exists a unique
1
-cocycle

b\colon F_{2}\to\ell^{2}(F_{2}/\mathbb{Z})

with these values.

Since
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, the coset
\mathbb{Z}
is fixed by left multiplication with
x
, so

\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}(x)\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}=\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Using the cocycle identity, it follows by induction that for every
n\geq 1
,

b(x^{n})=b(x^{n-1})+\lambda_{F_{2}/\mathbb{Z}}(x^{n-1})b(x)=b(x^{n-1})+\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}=n\,\delta_{\mathbb{Z}}.

This proves the claim, and hence the proposition.
∎

.

It is an interesting open question to decide if an example as in Proposition
3
can be found for an amenable group, see
[
4
,
6
,
11
]
.

4.
Vanishing of reduced cohomology for amenable groups

In this section we record a consequence of Theorem
1
for finitely generated amenable groups admitting controlled Følner sequences. Here, a
controlled Følner sequence
is a Følner sequence
(F_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that there exists
C>0
with

|\partial F_{n}|\leq\frac{C|F_{n}|}{\mathrm{diam}(F_{n})}

for every
n\geq 1
, where
\mathrm{diam}(F_{n})
is the diameter of
F_{n}
with respect to the word metric; see, for example, Tessera
[
13
]
.

Cornulier and Tessera proved that for uniformly bounded Banach
G
-modules, sublinear 1-cocycles are almost coboundaries whenever the group admits a controlled Følner sequence; more precisely, this is Proposition 5.6 in
[
5
]
. Therefore we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 5
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated amenable group admitting a controlled Følner sequence. Let
\pi
be a uniformly bounded
c_{0}
-representation of
G
on a superreflexive Banach space
\mathcal{E}
. Then, the first reduced cohomology vanishes,
H^{1}_{\rm red}(G,\pi)=0.
Equivalently, every 1-cocycle
b\colon G\to\mathcal{E}
is an almost coboundary.

Corollary
5
appears to be new at this level of generality. Indeed, Tessera proved in
[
13
]
the vanishing of reduced first cohomology for mixing
L^{p}
-representations,
1<p<\infty
, for a class of amenable groups with controlled Følner sequences. Since
L^{p}
-spaces are uniformly convex for
1<p<\infty
, the above corollary extends this vanishing statement from mixing
L^{p}
-representations to arbitrary isometric
c_{0}
-representations on uniformly convex Banach spaces.

Let us recall some classes of amenable groups known to admit controlled Følner sequences. Tessera studied controlled Følner sequences in
[
13
]
and recorded that such exist for polycyclic groups and several solvable groups of exponential growth, including semidirect products of the form
\mathbb{Z}[1/mn]\rtimes_{m/n}\mathbb{Z}
(hence in particular the solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups
\mathrm{BS}(1,m)
). Finally, groups of polynomial growth also admit controlled Følner sequences, for instance by taking word balls.
+

Remark 6
.

We are not aware of an explicitly recorded example in the literature of a finitely generated amenable group that does not admit a controlled Følner sequence; see the discussion in
[
4
]
. However, such examples follow immediately from the following observation: a finitely generated amenable group admitting a controlled Følner sequence has Følner function at most exponential along a subsequence. If
(F_{n})
is a controlled Følner sequence, then
|\partial F_{n}|/|F_{n}|\leq C/\operatorname{diam}(F_{n})
, so after setting
k_{n}\asymp\operatorname{diam}(F_{n})
one obtains
\operatorname{Føl}(k_{n})\leq|F_{n}|\leq\exp(C^{\prime}k_{n})
. In particular, groups with superexponential Følner function cannot admit controlled Følner sequences. By Erschler’s theorem
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, this applies for instance to the wreath product
\mathbf{Z}\wr\mathbf{Z}
.
