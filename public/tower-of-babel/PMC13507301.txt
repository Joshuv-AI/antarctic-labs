IGF2BP3 promotes epithelial ovarian cancer progression by regulating FASN expression
Authors: Danting Sun, Yuance Xu, Jiaze Gao, Lin Cheng, Yapei Su, Yan Xu, Junqi He, Teng Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOvarian cancer (OC) is the most lethal disease among female reproductive system tumors, particularly epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC). N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant internal modification in eukaryotic RNA and is involved in gene expression regulation. In OC, high expression of the m6A reader protein IGF2BP3 predicts a poor prognosis, but the target molecules and mechanisms underlying this association remain unclear. This study demonstrates that IGF2BP3 promotes EOC progression by recognizing and stabilizing m6A-modified FASN mRNA, thereby activating the WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway. This activation enhances lipid synthesis, increases mitochondrial membrane potential, shortens S-phase duration, and promotes cell proliferation and metastasis. Mechanistically, IGF2BP3 binds to m6A-modified FASN mRNA to enhance its stability, and pharmacological inhibition of FASN by orlistat reverses IGF2BP3-mediated oncogenic effects and WNT/β-catenin activation. In vivo and in vitro experiments confirm that knocking down either IGF2BP3 or FASN reverses these malignant phenotypes. These findings highlight a novel m6A-dependent IGF2BP3–FASN–WNT axis that drives EOC progression, providing a potential biomarker for targeted therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-57059-3.Keywords: N6-methyladenosine, EOC, IGF2BP3, FASNSubject terms: Cancer, Cell biology, Molecular biology, Oncology

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OC) ranks as the second most frequent cause of gynecologic cancer-related mortality among women globally, and its incidence is on an annual upward trajectory1. Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) accounts for over 90% of ovarian malignancies2. At diagnosis, 70% of cases are already advanced, with the five-year survival rate persistently hovering around 30%3,4. The lack of early symptoms, effective screening strategies, and frequent emergence of chemoresistance are major contributors to the poor prognosis of EOC5,6. Despite the incorporation of PARP inhibitors and anti-angiogenic agents into treatment guidelines, resistant clones emerge rapidly, with median progression-free survival extending by less than six months7,8. Therefore, identifying key molecules driving EOC progression and elucidating their mechanisms of intervention represents a critical breakthrough for overcoming therapeutic plateaus.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant internal modification of eukaryotic mRNA, and its “reader” proteins directly determine the fate of target mRNAs9. As a member of the m6A reader family, IGF2BP3 activates invasive signaling in various solid tumors, including cervical and bladder cancers, by stabilizing oncogenic transcripts10–12. However, the expression profile, clinical significance, and downstream metabolic targets of IGF2BP3 in EOC remain poorly characterized, particularly its cross-regulation with lipid metabolic reprogramming, which has not been previously reported.

Existing EOC-m6A research primarily focuses on writer/eraser enzymes such as METTL3 or FTO13,14, whereas the role of the m6A reader IGF2BP3 remains largely unexplored in EOC, particularly regarding its downstream targets and functional mechanisms. This leaves several gaps: there is a lack of large-scale validation of its independent prognostic value; its specific m6A-dependent target mRNAs remain unidentified; and it remains unclear whether it reshapes the tumor microenvironment through metabolic pathways. Given that lipid metabolism abnormalities underpin the rapid proliferation of EOC cells15,16, investigating the IGF2BP3-mediated lipid metabolic axis may help bridge these knowledge gaps.

This study integrates clinical cohorts, multi-omics data, and in vitro/in vivo models to systematically elucidate the expression characteristics and prognostic relevance of IGF2BP3 in EOC. It identifies FASN as its direct m6A target. It validates that this axis accelerates proliferation and metastasis by activating the WNT/β-catenin pathway, promoting lipid synthesis, reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS), and shortening the S-phase. Finally, xenograft models confirm that targeting the IGF2BP3-FASN axis reverses malignant phenotypes. These findings expand the theoretical frontier of m6A reader regulation in metabolic reprogramming, providing novel combined therapeutic targets and biomarkers for EOC.

Materials and methods

Clinical specimens

Clinical specimens were obtained from surgically excised tissues of patients. All EOC patients had not received any antineoplastic therapy before surgery. Our study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of Qingdao University Hospital. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects and/or their legal guardians.

Cell culture and lentiviral transfection

All EOC cell lines used in this research were obtained from the laboratory cell bank. IOSE80, OVCAR3, SKOV3, and A2780 cells were maintained in DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, VivaCell) at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Lentiviral vectors carrying IGF2BP3-shRNA and IGF2BP3-OE were purchased from GENE CHEM (Shanghai, China). Cells were transfected according to the instructions, and stably transfected EOC cell lines were obtained. Relevant sequences are listed in the Supplementary Table (Supplementary Table A1).

Colony formation assays

Distribute the cells evenly in a 6-well plate. After incubating in DMEM medium containing 2% FBS for 14 days, fix the cells with 4% PFA for half an hour, then stain with 0.1% crystal violet for 15 min, and count the number of colonies with more than 50 cells.

Cell viability assay

Add the prepared CCK8 solution (YENSEN, China) to a 96-well plate seeded with cells, and incubate in a cell culture incubator for 0, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h to detect cell proliferation capacity. Measure the absorbance at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Wound healing assay

Distribute the transfected cells evenly across the 6-well plate to form a fused monolayer. Use the tip of the pipette to create consistent diameter scratches in each well and incubate the cells with DMEM containing 2% FBS. Images were captured using a microscope at 0,12 and 24 h, respectively.

Migration and invasion assays

Cells were seeded in the upper Transwell chamber, with 10% FBS medium in the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After 36 h, non-migrated cells were removed with a cotton swab. Migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and counted under a microscope. The invasion assay was similar but included a matrix gel in the upper chamber.

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using Trizol reagent (TaKaRa, Japan), and the reverse transcription reaction (G592, abm) was performed using a reverse transcription kit to obtain cDNA. The expression levels of relevant genes were assessed using RT-qPCR (G891, abm), and GAPDH was utilized as the internal reference gene for normalization purposes. The primers used are listed in the supplementary table (Supplementary Table A2).

Western blotting (WB) assays

Total protein was extracted from cells and tissues using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime). Proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and subsequently transferred onto PVDF membranes. Blots were probed with anti-IGF2BP3 (AB177477, Abcam), anti-FASN (R381582, Zenbio), anti-β-catenin (ET1601-5, HuaBio), anti-P-GSK3β (ET1607-60, HuaBio), anti-GSK3β (ET1607-71, HuaBio), anti-HIF-1α (YP-mAb-01776, UpingBio), anti-MYC (002-203-001, alpvhhs), and anti-GAPDH (A00227-1, BOSTER) antibodies. The membrane was subsequently washed and incubated with secondary antibody (511203, Zenbio) for 1 h at room temperature. Addition of chemiluminescent substrate (KF005, affbiotech) and exposure to color development with an imager in the darkroom.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RIP was detected using the RNA Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Bes5101, Bersinbio). Cells were collected and lysed, and a mixture of specific IGF2BP3 antibody (AB177477, Abcam) and magnetic beads was added and incubated to capture RNA-binding protein complexes. Subsequently, the magnetic beads were washed with RNase-free reagent, and reverse transcription and RT-qPCR analyses were performed after elution of RNA to determine the expression level of FASN.

RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP) assay

MeRIP was conducted using the m6A RNA Methylation Immunoprecipitation Kit (BersinBio, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Polyadenylated RNA was isolated from total cellular extracts, fragmented to approximately 100–200 nucleotides, and incubated with magnetic beads pre-conjugated to anti-m6A antibody. After washing, methylated RNA fragments were eluted, purified, and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. Enrichment of target transcripts was quantified by RT-qPCR, with results normalized to input controls and IgG isotype controls.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The interaction between IGF2BP3 and FASN was assessed using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (UElandy, China). The FASN coding sequence (CDS) spanning putative m6A sites was subcloned into the pmirGLO vector (GENE CHEM, China). Cells seeded in 24-well plates were co-transfected with the firefly reporter construct and pRL-TK Renilla plasmid using Lipofectamine 3000. After 48 h, luciferase activities were quantified sequentially. Firefly luminescence was normalized to Renilla signal to account for transfection efficiency, with results expressed as fold change relative to the empty vector control. The primers used are listed in the supplementary table (Supplementary Table A3).

mRNA stability assay

Infected cells were placed in 6-well plates and exposed to 10 µg/mL actinomycin D (ACD, HY-17559, MCE) for 0, 3, 6, 9, and 12 h. RNA was then extracted at the corresponding time points to assess the half-life of FASN mRNA.

Quantitation of total cholesterol (TC) and free fatty acid (FFA)

TC concentrations were measured using the Total Cholesterol Assay Kit (BC1985, Solarbio), and FFA concentrations were measured using the Free Fatty Acid Assay Kit (S0215S, Beyotime), following the manufacturer’s protocols.

ROS assay

The Reactive Oxygen Assay Kit (MA1301-1, MeilunBio) was used to assess ROS levels in cells. Cells were cultured to the logarithmic growth phase, and after washing the cells with PBS, the DHE probe working solution was incubated for 30 min away from light.

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) assay

The Mitochondrial Membrane Potential Assay Kit containing JC-1 (C2003S, Beyotime) was used to assess mitochondrial membrane potential in EOC cell lines following virus transfection. JC-1 staining working solution was added, and the cells were incubated at 37 °C for 30 min in a cell incubator. After washing thrice with JC-1 staining buffer, DMEM was added and subsequently photographed by laser confocal microscopy. Fluorescence intensity was then observed using a fluorescence microscope.

Animal experiments

The Animal Experiment Center of Qingdao University approved the animal experiments. All procedures were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, and all methods were reported following the ARRIVE guidelines. Tumor formation experiments were performed using 5-week-old female BALB/C nude mice (n = 5). The animals were purchased from Shandong Pengyue Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. and housed in the SPF facility of Qingdao University Affiliated Hospital. A cell suspension (1 × 10⁶ cells/100 µL) was injected subcutaneously into the right axilla of the nude mice. The tumor’s growth was measured weekly over a period of four weeks using calipers. After inducing the nude mice with 4% isoflurane-oxygen mixture until the righting reflex disappeared, 1%–1.5% isoflurane inhalation was maintained. The mice were immediately euthanized by cervical dislocation, and tumor tissues were excised for subsequent analysis. The volume of the tumor was calculated using the formula: length × width2 × 0.5.

Immunohistochemical staining (IHC)

Paraffin-embedded sections of mouse tumor tissue and ovarian cancer tissue specimens were prepared. Sections were deparaffinized with xylene and then hydrated with graded ethanol solutions. The sections were placed in a pH 6.0 citrate buffer, microwaved for 15 min, cooled, and rinsed with PBS. The sections were immersed in hydrogen peroxide solution for 10 min, then rinsed with PBS. Subsequently, 5% BSA was added, and the sections were incubated at room temperature for 30 min. Anti-IGF2BP3 (14642-1-AP, Proteintech) and anti-FASN (R381582, Zenbio) antibodies are incubated overnight at 4 °C. The secondary antibody (PV-9000, Zhongshan Jinqiao) is added to each sample and incubated at room temperature for 30 min. The third antibody (PV-9000, Zhongshan Jinqiao) was added to each sample and incubated for 30 min. Subsequently, samples were stained with the DAB kit (Beyotime) at room temperature for 3–5 min. Staining was terminated by rinsing with distilled water. The tissue was then fixed with hematoxylin and sealed with neutral resin.

Hematoxylin-eosin staining (HE)

Paraffin sections were dewaxed and hydrated in xylene and graded ethanol. They were stained in hematoxylin for 5 min, differentiated with 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol for 10 s, and counterstained with weak alkaline water. Sections were then stained in eosin for 1 min. Finally, they were dehydrated in graded ethanol, clarified with xylene, and mounted with neutral resin.

RNA sequencing

We conducted RNA sequencing on RNA samples from untreated OVCAR3 cells and from OVCAR3 cells overexpressing IGF2BP3. Differential gene expression and KEGG signaling pathway enrichment were subsequently analyzed and identified17.

Expression level analysis

The expression levels of IGF2BP3 and FASN in OC tissues were analyzed using the GEPIA dataset (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/), and their correlation was detected. Protein expression levels of these genes in OC were analyzed using the Human Protein Atlas database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/). Additionally, the prognostic value of IGF2BP3 and FASN was assessed using the Kaplan-Meier plotter tool (https://kmplot.com/analysis/).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with GraphPad Prism 9.0. Experiments were performed at least three times, and results are shown as mean ± SD. Statistical significance was determined by t-tests or ANOVA, with *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, and ****p < 0.0001 indicating significance.

Results

IGF2BP3 is highly expressed in EOC and is associated with poor prognosis in EOC patients

GEPIA database analysis revealed high IGF2BP3 expression in OC tissues compared to healthy tissues (Fig. 1A). We obtained data from the HPA database showing that IGF2BP3 protein is highly expressed in OC tissue (Fig. 1B). Using IHC methods, we further examined the expression levels of IGF2BP3 in human EOC tissue, normal ovarian tissue, and normal fallopian tube tissue. The results showed that, compared with normal ovarian and fallopian tube tissues, IGF2BP3 expression was significantly upregulated in EOC tissue (Fig. 1C). Relevant studies have shown that high expression of IGF2BP3 is closely related to the poor prognosis of patients with EOC, and multivariate Cox regression analysis has confirmed that it is an independent prognostic factor for progression-free survival18. Subsequently, Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis showed that high IGF2BP3 expression was associated with a poor prognosis in OC patients (Fig. 1D). We then verified the above findings by RT-qPCR and WB in specimens from EOC patients. Indeed, mRNA and protein expression of IGF2BP3 was significantly increased in tumor tissues compared with normal ovarian tissues (Fig. 1E, F). RT-qPCR and WB results showed that IGF2BP3 expression was also up-regulated in 3 EOC cell lines (OVCAR3, SKOV3, A2780) compared with IOSE80 (Fig. 1G, H). In addition, among all selected EOC cell lines, IGF2BP3 expression was higher in OVCAR3 and SKOV3 cells than in A2780 cells; therefore, OVCAR3 and SKOV3 cells were selected for subsequent experiments.

IGF2BP3 affects the viability of EOC cells in vitro

To deeply explore the potential role of IGF2BP3 in EOC, we established OVCAR3 and SKOV3 cell lines stably overexpressing IGF2BP3, as well as stably transfected cells using three lentiviruses carrying IGF2BP3 knockdown (Sh-IGF2BP3-1, Sh-IGF2BP3-2, Sh-IGF2BP3-3). And the transfection efficiencies of IGF2BP3 overexpression and knockdown were assessed using RT-qPCR and WB assays (Fig. 2A- D). Two lentiviruses with high knockdown efficiency, Sh-IGF2BP3-1 and Sh-IGF2BP3-2, were selected. To further delineate the functional consequences of IGF2BP3 dysregulation, we conducted a series of in vitro assays.CCK-8 assays revealed that IGF2BP3 overexpression enhanced the proliferation rate of OVCAR3 and SKOV3 cells (Fig. 2E), whereas IGF2BP3 knockdown significantly inhibited cell proliferation (Fig. 2F). Wound healing assays showed that IGF2BP3 overexpressing cells had a higher migration rate than NC cells (Fig. 2G), whereas IGF2BP3 knockdown significantly inhibited cell migration (Fig. 2H). In addition, overexpression of IGF2BP3 significantly increased the proliferation, migration, and invasion abilities of EOC cells, as revealed by cell colony formation assays (Fig. 2I) and trans-well assays (Fig. 2K, L); however, these effects were reversed by knockdown of IGF2BP3 (Fig. 2J, M, N). In summary, these findings indicate that IGF2BP3 affects the proliferation, migration, and invasion of EOC cells.

IGF2BP3 regulates FASN expression in an m6A-dependent manner

Analysis of the GEPIA databases indicated a positive correlation between IGF2BP3 and FASN expression (Fig. 3A). Previous studies have shown that high FASN expression is closely associated with poor prognosis in ovarian cancer patients19. Additionally, GEPIA database analysis revealed high expression of FASN in OC (Fig. 3B). Further validation showed that FASN mRNA and protein levels were significantly higher in tumor tissues than in normal ovarian tissues (Fig. 3C, D). Additionally, RT-qPCR and WB analyses revealed higher FASN expression in EOC cell lines compared to IOSE80 (Fig. 3E, F). The HPA database confirmed higher FASN expression in OC tissues compared to normal ovarian tissues (Fig. 3G). Using IHC methods, we further examined the expression levels of FASN protein in human EOC tissue, normal ovarian tissue, and normal fallopian tube tissue. The results indicated that, compared with normal ovarian and fallopian tube tissues, FASN protein expression was significantly upregulated in EOC tissue (Fig. 3H). We found that IGF2BP3 overexpression enhanced FASN expression at both protein and mRNA levels (Fig. 3I, J), and similarly, FASN expression was significantly downregulated after IGF2BP3 knockdown (Fig. 3K, L). The binding of IGF2BP3 to FASN mRNA was evaluated by RIP and RT-qPCR. Results indicated that anti-IGF2BP3 antibody-enriched FASN mRNA compared to the control, confirming their direct interaction (Fig. 3M). To determine whether this binding is m6A-dependent, MeRIP assays were performed, demonstrating that IGF2BP3 regulates FASN mRNA through m6A modification (Fig. 3N). This regulatory mechanism was further confirmed by dual-luciferase reporter assays. IGF2BP3 overexpression markedly enhanced the luciferase activity of the wild-type FASN CDS reporter, whereas this effect was abolished when the m6A modification sites were mutated (Fig. 3O). To directly assess the impact of IGF2BP3 on FASN mRNA stability, actinomycin D chase assays were conducted. Indeed, overexpression of IGF2BP3 resulted in significantly higher stability of FASN mRNA (Fig. 3P). Meanwhile, knockdown of the IGF2BP3 gene restored the FASN mRNA stability promoted by overexpression of IGF2BP3 (Fig. 3Q). These findings indicate that IGF2BP3 stabilizes FASN expression in an m6A-dependent manner and positively regulates its expression, thereby promoting the progression of EOC.

Effects of IGF2BP3 on lipid metabolism and mitochondrial function in EOC cells

Given that FASN is involved in lipid metabolism, we measured the levels of FFA and TC in EOC cell lines after lentiviral transfection. We found that FFA increased with IGF2BP3 overexpression (Fig. 4A) and decreased after IGF2BP3 knockdown (Fig. 4B). Similarly, a similar trend was observed for TC content (Fig. 4C, D). To further reveal the potential mechanism of IGF2BP3 in EOC development, we performed RNA-seq analysis on IGF2BP3-overexpressing and control OVCAR3 cells. Results revealed 175 differentially expressed genes between OVCAR3-NC and IGF2BP3-overexpressing cells, with 95 upregulated and 80 downregulated in OVCAR3-NC cells (Fig. 4E). KEGG pathway analysis showed that IGF2BP3 was associated with the WNT pathway, and the GO analysis suggested that IGF2BP3 may be involved in biological processes such as lipid metabolic processes (Supplementary Figure S1) (Fig. 4F-H). To verify whether IGF2BP3 induces malignant progression of EOC through lipid metabolism mediated by the WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway, we conducted a correlation validation. Results indicated that IGF2BP3 overexpression elevated the protein levels of WNT signaling-related biomarkers β-catenin, P-GSK-3β (Ser-9), GSK-3β, HIF-1α, and MYC (Fig. 4I), and showed the opposite trend when IGF2BP3 was knocked down (Fig. 4J). These results are consistent with a model in which IGF2BP3-induced FASN upregulation is associated with enhanced expression of WNT/β-catenin signaling components, suggesting a potential metabolic-epigenetic axis that may facilitate EOC progression. The above experiments suggest that IGF2BP3 overexpression promotes the process of fatty acid oxidation by enhancing lipid metabolism. This process occurs in the mitochondria of the cell, which in turn leads to an elevated MMP, manifested by increased JC-1 fluorescence, showing the opposite trend when IGF2BP3 is knocked down (Fig. 4K). Enhanced mitochondrial function increased the efficiency of cellular energy metabolism while decreasing electron leakage in the electron transport chain, which reduced the generation of ROS and resulted in downregulation of ROS levels; knockdown of IGF2BP3 restored ROS levels suppressed by overexpression of IGF2BP3 (Fig. 4L). Enhanced lipid metabolism and reduced ROS levels together provided more favorable conditions for cell cycle progression, reducing the interference of oxidative stress on DNA replication and making DNA synthesis more efficient, which in turn led to the shortening of the S-phase of the cell cycle; similarly, knockdown of the IGF2BP3 gene significantly prolonged the S-phase of the cell cycle (Fig. 4M, N). These results further revealed the interaction between lipid metabolism and mitochondria and their effect on EOC cell proliferation. Collectively, these data suggest that IGF2BP3-driven FASN up-regulation coincides with enhanced WNT/β-catenin signaling markers, prompting us to explore the possible metabolic-epigenetic crosstalk underlying this observation.

Orlistat counteracts IGF2BP3-promoted tumor aggressiveness via lipogenesis suppression

Orlistat, an irreversible inhibitor of lipases and FASN, suppresses tumor progression in multiple cancers. To evaluate its therapeutic potential in EOC, we determined the IC50 of Orlistat in OVCAR3 (7 µM) and SKOV3 (22 µM) cells (Fig. 5A). At these concentrations, Orlistat effectively reduced FASN expression after 72-hour treatment (Fig. 5B-C) and significantly inhibited EOC cell proliferation, colony formation, migration, and invasion (Fig. 5D-F). Notably, Orlistat suppressed WNT/β-catenin signaling without altering IGF2BP3 protein levels (Fig. 5G), suggesting that lipogenesis acts downstream of IGF2BP3.

(B,C) RT-qPCR and WB analysis of FASN expression following 72-hour Orlistat treatment at indicated concentrations. (D,H) Cell proliferation assessed by CCK-8 assay in control and IGF2BP3-overexpressing cells ± Orlistat. (E,I) Representative images and quantification of colony formation assays. (F,J) Transwell migration and invasion assays (400× magnification) with corresponding quantification. (G,K) WB analysis of WNT/β-catenin pathway components and IGF2BP3. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, and ****p < 0.0001.

To test whether IGF2BP3 promotes EOC progression through FASN-dependent lipogenesis, we performed rescue experiments in IGF2BP3-overexpressing cells. Orlistat treatment abrogated the enhanced proliferative, migratory, and invasive capacities induced by IGF2BP3 overexpression (Fig. 5H-J) and restored WNT/β-catenin signaling activity to baseline (Fig. 5K). These findings indicate that FASN-mediated lipogenesis functions upstream of WNT pathway activation and that pharmacological FASN inhibition reverses IGF2BP3-mediated oncogenic effects in EOC cells.

IGF2BP3 affects EOC cell viability in vivo

To investigate the in vivo effects of IGF2BP3 on EOC cells, a xenograft tumor model was established in BALB/c nude mice using IGF2BP3-overexpressing EOC cells. Tumor volume was significantly larger in the IGF2BP3 overexpression group than in the control group (Fig. 6A-C). Tumor volume was measured by an investigator blinded to the experimental groups. Body weight change curves in nude mice indicated that rapid tumor growth led to weight loss 21–28 days after subcutaneous tumor transplantation. No significant adverse effects were observed in terms of body weight loss or behavioral changes. However, the IGF2BP3 overexpression group exhibited less weight loss (Fig. 6D). Immunohistochemical staining determined that in IGF2BP3 overexpressing tumors, the expression of the IGF2BP3 gene and its downstream molecule, FASN, was upregulated, along with upregulation of the proliferative marker, Ki-67 (Fig. 6E). In contrast, knockdown of the IGF2BP3 gene reduced the viability of EOC cells in vivo (Fig. 6F-J). In summary, these results confirm that IGF2BP3 can influence EOC progression in vivo.

Discussion

EOC comprises approximately 90% of ovarian malignancies and remains among the most lethal gynecologic cancers. Despite incremental therapeutic advances, five-year survival rates have improved minimally over three decades—a stagnation attributable primarily to the asymptomatic nature of early-stage disease and the frequent emergence of chemoresistance. These clinical realities underscore the urgent need to identify molecular drivers of EOC progression that can inform novel therapeutic strategies. The present study establishes IGF2BP3 as one such driver, demonstrating that it promotes EOC progression through m6A-dependent stabilization of FASN mRNA, thereby activating lipogenesis and downstream WNT/β-catenin signaling.

The m6A modification is one of the most abundant internal RNA modifications in eukaryotes, which is involved in the regulation of gene expression, mRNA stability, and translational efficiency20,21. IGF2BP3 is an RNA-binding protein whose structure contains an RNA-binding structural domain, an RRM structural domain, and a protein-interacting structural domain22. It regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival by binding to mRNA and promoting its stability and translation process23. In addition, IGF2BP3, as a m6A reading protein, recognizes m6A-modified GC sequences, affects the stability and translation of mRNAs of downstream target genes, and plays an important regulatory role in cell physiology and pathology24,25. Some relevant studies have found that IGF2BP3 exhibits pro-tumorigenic effects in a variety of cancers. It has been reported that IGF2BP3 can promote the development of cervical cancer by mediating the m6A modification of FSCN126. In nasopharyngeal carcinoma, IGF2BP3 activates the Notch3 signaling pathway by stabilizing NOTCH3 mRNA, thereby promoting metastasis and stemness phenotype of tumor cells27. In bladder cancer, high expression of IGF2BP3 promotes tumor cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and chemoresistance by regulating HMGB1 and its downstream signaling pathways, such as NF-κB and JNK11. However, its function in lipid metabolism remains unclear.

Our findings address this gap by demonstrating that IGF2BP3 is markedly upregulated in EOC tissues and correlates with adverse clinical outcomes. Mechanistically, we provide multiple lines of evidence supporting direct, m6A-dependent binding of IGF2BP3 to FASN mRNA. RNA immunoprecipitation and methylated RNA immunoprecipitation assays confirmed this interaction, while dual-luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that mutation of predicted m6A sites within the FASN coding sequence abolished IGF2BP3-mediated enhancement of reporter activity. Functionally, IGF2BP3 overexpression significantly extended FASN mRNA half-life and increased protein abundance, whereas genetic knockdown produced opposite effects. These results establish FASN as a bona fide m6A-modified target of IGF2BP3 in EOC.

Lipid metabolism plays an important role in the development and progression of cancer28. Cancer cells alter lipid metabolic pathways to increase lipid synthesis and storage in order to meet their rapid proliferation needs29,30. In addition, lipid metabolites can regulate cellular signaling pathways, promoting tumor invasion and metastasis. In addition, tumor cells resist oxidative stress by regulating lipid metabolism to maintain intracellular redox balance31,32. Xuenong Li’s team at Southern Medical University found that the lipid metabolism-related gene ECI2 may inhibit colorectal cancer progression by affecting neutrophil recruitment and activity in the tumor microenvironment, as well as regulating lipid metabolism and IL-8 transcription33,34. In addition, some studies have shown that lipid metabolism plays an important role in the development of cervical cancer, and fatty acid and cholesterol metabolism are two aspects of lipid metabolism that are most closely related to cervical cancer10,35,36. For example, high expression of FASN in cervical cancer cells is associated with lymph node metastasis and can induce migration and invasion in vitro37,38. Since FASN is an important regulator of lipid metabolism, the fatty acids it synthesizes provide the necessary membrane lipids and energy for the rapid proliferation of tumor cells39,40. For example, in breast cancer, high expression of FASN is closely associated with the proliferation and invasion ability of tumor cells41,42.

Building upon this established framework, we assessed whether IGF2BP3-mediated FASN upregulation similarly remodels lipid metabolism in EOC. Our results demonstrate that IGF2BP3 overexpression not only elevates FASN expression but also significantly increases intracellular FFA and TC levels, while genetic knockdown reduces these lipid species. These metabolic alterations were accompanied by enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced reactive oxygen species, and accelerated cell cycle progression—collectively indicating that the IGF2BP3-FASN axis induces a pro-tumorigenic metabolic environment. Importantly, pharmacological inhibition of FASN by orlistat reversed the oncogenic effects of IGF2BP3 overexpression, including WNT/β-catenin pathway activation, confirming that lipogenesis functions downstream of IGF2BP3 in this regulatory hierarchy.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size of EOC patients was relatively small, precluding multivariate survival analysis; future studies with larger cohorts are needed to validate IGF2BP3 and FASN as independent prognostic factors. Second, while orlistat treatment established that FASN-mediated lipogenesis is required for WNT/β-catenin activation, the precise molecular link—such as lipid rafts or palmitoylation of WNT components—remains to be determined. Third, the specific m6A site(s) on FASN mRNA and their quantitative contribution to IGF2BP3 binding require further characterization. Finally, although orlistat-mediated FASN inhibition attenuated the IGF2BP3-driven malignant phenotype in vitro, no in vivo gene rescue experiments have been conducted to verify whether targeting the IGF2BP3-FASN axis can reverse tumor progression in animal models; this limits the translational significance of the present study’s findings. Future studies will conduct such experiments to validate the therapeutic potential of this axis in ovarian cancer.

In summary, this study delineates a novel m6A-dependent IGF2BP3-FASN-WNT signaling axis that drives EOC progression through coordinated metabolic reprogramming and proliferative signaling activation. While the precise m6A site architecture on FASN mRNA and the mechanistic details of lipid-mediated WNT pathway modulation await further investigation, our findings provide compelling rationale for therapeutic targeting of IGF2BP3 or FASN in EOC management, particularly as a strategy to overcome chemoresistance in advanced disease.
