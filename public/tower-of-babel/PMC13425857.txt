Introduction

Valsalva retinopathy is a well-recognized cause of sudden, unilateral, painless visual loss resulting from preretinal (subhyaloid or sub–internal limiting membrane) hemorrhage following a transient increase in intrathoracic or intra-abdominal pressure [1]. It typically affects otherwise healthy young individuals and presents as a dense premacular hemorrhage obscuring the fovea [2, 3].

The condition is closely associated with the Valsalva maneuver, which causes a sudden rise in venous pressure and subsequent rupture of superficial perifoveal capillaries. Numerous precipitating factors have been described, including forceful coughing, vomiting, heavy lifting, strenuous physical exercise, constipation and straining during bowel movements, sexual activity, pregnancy, labor, and general anesthesia [1–8]. More recently, atypical triggers such as yoga breathing exercises, habitual ear-popping behavior, COVID-19 infection, non-invasive ventilation, recreational drug use, and handstands have also been reported [9–14]. Although spontaneous resolution may occur, large premacular hemorrhages can result in prolonged visual impairment and may increase the risk of retinal toxicity or epiretinal membrane formation.

Management strategies range from observation for small hemorrhages to intervention for visually significant premacular hemorrhages. Nd laser hyaloidotomy has emerged as an effective, minimally invasive treatment that facilitates rapid drainage of blood into the vitreous cavity and accelerates visual recovery [15, 16].

Khat (Catha edulis), a widely used psychoactive substance in East Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, contains cathinone, a sympathomimetic alkaloid capable of inducing transient increases in blood pressure and heart rate [17]. Although khat has not been established as a direct cause of Valsalva retinopathy, its cardiovascular effects may theoretically increase vascular susceptibility when combined with activities that generate substantial Valsalva stress.

We report a rare case of Valsalva retinopathy occurring after brief hanging from a window in a habitual khat user. The patient was successfully treated with Nd laser hyaloidotomy, resulting in complete anatomical resolution and visual recovery.

Case presentation

A 23-year-old previously healthy male presented to the ophthalmology clinic with a 2-day history of sudden, painless visual loss in the left eye. The visual symptoms developed immediately following an episode during which he was chewing Catha edulis (khat) and briefly hanging from a window. During this event, he experienced dizziness, lost his grip, and sustained a short fall. He denied direct ocular trauma, head injury, loss of consciousness, or any other significant bodily injury associated with the incident.

The patient reported chronic habitual khat chewing and confirmed that this was not his first exposure to the substance. He had no history of ocular surgery or systemic disease, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, bleeding disorders, anticoagulant use, or hematologic disorders. Furthermore, there were no clinical features suggestive of retinal vascular disease, systemic coagulopathy, or other known causes of premacular hemorrhage. He denied ocular pain, redness, discharge, photophobia, or any previous episodes of visual loss.

On examination, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was 6/6 in the right eye and counting fingers at 1 m in the left eye. Because visual acuity in the affected eye was measurable at the counting-fingers level, light perception and projection testing were not routinely performed. Intraocular pressure measured by applanation tonometry was within normal limits in both eyes (17 mmHg in the right eye and 15 mmHg in the left eye). Anterior segment examination by slit-lamp biomicroscopy was unremarkable bilaterally.

Dilated fundus examination of the right eye demonstrated a healthy optic disc with distinct margins, normal retinal vasculature, an intact macula, and an unremarkable peripheral retina. In contrast, the left eye revealed a large, well-circumscribed premacular (subhyaloid) hemorrhage involving the macular region and obscuring the fovea (Fig. 1A). Corresponding infrared fundus imaging and optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated a large dome-shaped premacular hemorrhagic cavity beneath the posterior hyaloid face, producing marked posterior shadowing of the underlying retinal layers (Fig. 2A). No additional retinal abnormalities, including retinal vascular occlusion, diabetic retinopathy, retinal tears, or peripheral retinal pathology, were identified.

Based on the clinical history and ophthalmic findings, a diagnosis of left-eye subhyaloid hemorrhage secondary to Valsalva retinopathy was established. Given the severity of visual impairment and the premacular location of the hemorrhage, Nd: YAG laser hyaloidotomy was performed. A Q-switched Nd: YAG laser was applied using energy settings ranging from 2 to 4 mJ. Approximately 3 to 5 laser pulses were delivered to the inferior dependent portion of the hemorrhage, away from the foveal center, to facilitate gravitational drainage of blood into the vitreous cavity while minimizing the risk of retinal injury. A 90-D contact lens was used to provide stabilization and precise laser focusing.

Immediate post-procedural examination confirmed successful drainage of the hemorrhage into the vitreous cavity without procedure-related complications. Color fundus photography obtained 2 h after treatment demonstrated partial drainage of the premacular hemorrhage into the vitreous cavity with inferior layering of blood and improved visualization of the macula (Fig. 1B). Corresponding infrared fundus imaging and OCT demonstrated partial collapse of the premacular hemorrhagic cavity following successful laser drainage, with improved visualization of the underlying retina (Fig. 2B).

The patient was scheduled for routine postoperative follow-up visits; however, he did not attend the early follow-up appointments and was subsequently difficult to contact. Consequently, no clinical assessments were available between the day of treatment and the one-month follow-up visit.

At the one-month follow-up visit, BCVA had improved to 6/6 in the left eye. Fundoscopic examination demonstrated complete resolution of the premacular hemorrhage with restoration of the foveal anatomy (Fig. 1C). Corresponding infrared fundus imaging and OCT demonstrated complete resolution of the premacular hemorrhage with restoration of the foveal contour, subtle internal limiting membrane wrinkling, and mild epiretinal membrane formation at the vitreoretinal interface, without evidence of retinal distortion or visual impairment (Fig. 2C). Continued observation was recommended because of the potential for progression of vitreoretinal interface changes, although the overall anatomical and visual outcomes remained favorable.

Discussion

Valsalva retinopathy is a distinct clinical entity first described by Thomas Duane and is characterized by a sudden increase in intraocular venous pressure leading to rupture of superficial retinal capillaries and accumulation of blood within the preretinal space.¹ The condition predominantly affects young, otherwise healthy individuals and typically presents with acute, painless visual loss, as observed in the present case. Valsalva retinopathy is commonly precipitated by activities that cause an abrupt increase in intrathoracic or intra-abdominal pressure, resulting in transmission of elevated venous pressure to the retinal circulation.

The pathophysiology is closely linked to the Valsalva maneuver, during which forced expiration against a closed glottis produces a rapid rise in intrathoracic and intra-abdominal pressures [2, 3]. The resulting increase in venous pressure is transmitted to the perifoveal capillary network, which is particularly susceptible to rupture. The premacular location of the hemorrhage accounts for the marked visual impairment frequently observed despite the absence of widespread retinal pathology [2, 3].

Accurate differentiation between subhyaloid and sub-internal limiting membrane (ILM) hemorrhage is clinically important because both entities may present as premacular hemorrhages with similar funduscopic appearances. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) plays a crucial role in determining the anatomical location of the hemorrhage by demonstrating its relationship to the posterior hyaloid face and the ILM. In the present case, OCT findings, together with the characteristic clinical appearance, confirmed a subhyaloid hemorrhage located between the posterior hyaloid face and the ILM. Recognition of the hemorrhage plane was clinically relevant in guiding management, as Nd laser hyaloidotomy facilitated successful drainage of the hemorrhage and complete visual recovery.

Valsalva retinopathy has been reported following a wide range of activities associated with sudden increases in intrathoracic or intra-abdominal pressure. Classical triggers include forceful coughing, vomiting, heavy lifting, strenuous physical exercise, constipation and straining during bowel movements, sexual activity, pregnancy, and labor [1–7]. Cases associated with twin pregnancy [6], postcoital activity [7], and general anesthesia [8] have also been reported. Additional unusual associations include yoga breathing exercises [9], habitual ear-popping behavior [10], COVID-19 infection [11], non-invasive ventilation [12], recreational drug use [13], and handstands [14]. Leite et al. described a case following a vomiting episode with rapid visual recovery after Nd laser treatment [5], while Miyaki et al. reported Valsalva retinopathy induced by a handstand [14]. Similarly, Temkar et al. documented Valsalva retinopathy in a child receiving non-invasive ventilation, further illustrating the diverse clinical settings in which Valsalva-related retinal hemorrhage may occur [12].

In the present case, the retinal hemorrhage occurred following a brief hanging episode, an uncommon trigger of Valsalva retinopathy. The hanging episode was considered the most likely precipitating event because it would be expected to generate substantial Valsalva stress through physical exertion and breath-holding, resulting in an acute increase in intrathoracic and retinal venous pressures. Although the patient was a habitual khat user and cathinone may theoretically increase vascular susceptibility through its sympathomimetic cardiovascular effects [17], a direct causal relationship between khat use and Valsalva retinopathy cannot be established. This case therefore expands the spectrum of atypical triggers associated with Valsalva retinopathy.

The differential diagnosis of premacular hemorrhage includes proliferative diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, retinal arterial macroaneurysm, Terson syndrome, ocular trauma, blood dyscrasias, leukemia, thrombocytopenia, and other hematologic disorders [1]. In the present case, the patient had no history of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coagulation abnormalities, anticoagulant use, or hematologic disease. Furthermore, fundus examination revealed no evidence of retinal vascular pathology, neovascularization, or other retinal abnormalities that could account for the hemorrhage. The close temporal relationship between symptom onset and the hanging episode strongly supported the diagnosis of Valsalva retinopathy.

Management of Valsalva retinopathy remains controversial and depends on the size, location, and duration of the hemorrhage [1–3, 14, 18–20]. Observation is often appropriate for small hemorrhages because spontaneous resolution may occur over weeks to months. However, large premacular hemorrhages may result in prolonged visual impairment and can lead to complications such as epiretinal membrane formation or toxic retinal damage secondary to prolonged exposure to hemoglobin and iron.

Nd laser hyaloidotomy facilitates rapid drainage of blood from the subhyaloid space into the vitreous cavity and has been shown to accelerate visual recovery compared with conservative management [19, 20]. In the present case, the procedure resulted in complete visual recovery within one month without procedure-related complications. Potential adverse events reported in the literature include macular hole formation, retinal injury, retinal detachment, and incomplete drainage, none of which were observed in our patient.

Pars plana vitrectomy (PPV) represents an alternative therapeutic option for dense premacular hemorrhage, particularly when the hemorrhage is longstanding, fails to resolve, or is associated with significant vitreoretinal interface abnormalities [14, 18–20]. Modern small-gauge vitrectomy techniques provide excellent anatomical outcomes through direct evacuation of the hemorrhage and management of associated retinal surface changes. However, PPV is more invasive than Nd laser hyaloidotomy and carries the inherent risks of intraocular surgery. In the present case, Nd laser hyaloidotomy was selected because of the recent onset of symptoms, the favorable configuration of the hemorrhage, the young age of the patient, and the limited availability of vitreoretinal surgical services. This minimally invasive approach achieved complete visual recovery while avoiding the need for surgical intervention.

Follow-up OCT demonstrated mild retinal surface remodeling characterized by subtle ILM wrinkling and a mild epiretinal membrane. Similar vitreoretinal interface changes have been reported following premacular hemorrhage and Nd laser hyaloidotomy and are thought to result from retinal surface remodeling after hemorrhage resolution. Although these findings were not associated with visual symptoms or reduced visual acuity in our patient, continued observation is warranted because secondary epiretinal membrane progression may occur in some cases.

A limitation of this report is the absence of intermediate postoperative assessments because the patient did not attend the scheduled early follow-up visits. Consequently, detailed documentation of short-term anatomical and visual recovery following Nd laser hyaloidotomy was unavailable. Nevertheless, the favorable findings observed at one month demonstrated complete visual recovery and satisfactory anatomical resolution.

This case highlights several important clinical implications. Clinicians should consider Valsalva retinopathy in young patients presenting with sudden visual loss following activities that increase intrathoracic pressure, even when the trigger is unconventional. The report also underscores the importance of thorough history-taking and demonstrates that timely intervention with Nd laser hyaloidotomy can result in rapid anatomical and visual recovery in appropriately selected patients.

In conclusion, this report describes a rare presentation of Valsalva retinopathy following brief hanging from a window, successfully managed with Nd laser hyaloidotomy. It highlights an uncommon precipitating factor and supports the role of timely intervention in achieving excellent visual outcomes.

Conclusion

This case describes an unusual presentation of Valsalva retinopathy following brief hanging from a window, an activity capable of generating substantial Valsalva stress. Although khat use may have contributed through its sympathomimetic cardiovascular effects, the hanging episode was considered the most likely precipitating factor. This report expands the spectrum of recognized triggers of Valsalva retinopathy and highlights the importance of thorough history-taking in patients presenting with sudden visual loss. Early diagnosis and timely treatment with Nd laser hyaloidotomy can result in excellent anatomical and visual outcomes in selected patients with dense premacular hemorrhage.
