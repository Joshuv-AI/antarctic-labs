Abstract
. We study the rapid stabilization of general linear systems, when the differential operator 𝒜 \mathcal{A} has a Riesz basis of eigenvectors. We find simple sufficient conditions for the rapid stabilization and the construction of a relatively explicit feedback operator. We use an F F -equivalence approach relying on Fredholm transformation to show a stronger result: under these sufficient conditions the system is equivalent to a simple exponentially stable system, with arbitrarily large decay rate. In particular, our conditions improve the existing conditions of rapid stabilization for non-parabolic operators such as skew-adjoint systems.



1.
Introduction

Stabilization is one of the three main problems in control theory, together with controllability and optimal control. The goal is to drive a system of differential equations to a given long-term behavior by acting on it with a control, with the specificity that the control has to be a function of the state. This creates a feedback loop (the control influences the system which in turn influences the control), which can make the achieved stability relatively robust. However, this feedback loop makes the mathematical problem of finding a suitable control difficult and even the well-posedness can be a challenge.
From a practical point of view, stabilization has many applications
[

]
, in engineering
[
3
]
, transportation
[

[

[

From an abstract point of view, the stabilization problem in a linear framework is the following: given a system

(1)

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{A}u+Bw(t),

where
\mathcal{A}
is a differential operator and
B
is a given operator, we would like to find a control feedback law

such that the system (

A more ambitious problem is to require that for any
\lambda>0
there exists a control feedback law

such that all solutions of the system converge exponentially quickly to 0 with decay rate at least
\lambda>0

(or

is finite-dimensional (in particular this implies that

3.4.
Application to a Gribov operator in the
Bargmann
space

The Hilbert space
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
considered in this section is the following Bargmann space

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}:=\left\{f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\ \text{holomorphic}\ \;|\;\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-|z|^{2}}|f(z)|^{2}dz<\infty\ \text{and}\ f(0)=0\right\},

endowed with the scalar product:

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-|z|^{2}}f(z)\bar{g}(z)dz,\quad\forall f,g\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X},

where
dz
denotes the
2-
dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}.

Let us consider the operators
U
and
V
defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}U:D(U)\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\rightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\\
f\mapsto Uf=\frac{df}{dz}\\
D(U)=\{f\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\;|\;Uf\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\},\end{cases}\ \text{and}\ \begin{cases}V:D(V)\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\rightarrow{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\\
f\mapsto Vf=zf\\
D(V)=\{f\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\;|\;Vf\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}\}.\end{cases}

The operator
U
is called the annihilation operator and
V
the creation operator. The nonself-adjoint Gribov operator (
[
1
]
,
[
49
]
) is constructed as a polynomial of the operators
U
and
V
defined in the Bargmann space
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
as follows:

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=(VU)^{3}+\varepsilon V(U+V)U+\varepsilon^{2}(VU)^{3d_{2}}+\cdots+\varepsilon^{k}(VU)^{3d_{k}}+\cdots,

where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{C}
and
(d_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a strictly decreasing sequence with strictly positive terms such that
d_{0}=2d_{1}=1.
This operator appears in the Reggeon field theory which was introduced by Gribov in
[
43
]
to study strong interactions between protons and neutrons
among
other less stable
particles
. Notice that this operator is neither self-adjoint nor skew-adjoint. So the stability analysis results stated in
[
39
]
,
[
40
]

cannot
cover this class of operator. In view of
[
35
]
, this operator can be studied on the domain
\mathcal{D}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=D((VU)^{3d_{2}})\cap D(V(U+V)U).

Consider now the following evolution equation associated to it:

(45)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-\mathcal{A}u+\phi w(t)

where
\phi
is a given function and
w
is the control of the system. Based on
[
35
, section 4.2]
, for small value of
\varepsilon,
there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n}^{i})_{n,i}
such that the system of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{A}
forms a Riesz basis in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
which can be decomposed in entire
series
as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{n}}{n!}+p\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{i}\varphi_{n}^{i}(z),

for any
p\in(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}).
So there exists a Riesz basis
(\widetilde{\varphi_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
of
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
which is a bi-orthogonal family of
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}.
Applying our Corollary
3.5
, the following holds:

COROLLARY 3.16
.

Let
\gamma\in[0,1),
and
r\in(-5/2+\gamma,5/2-\gamma).
If
\phi
is such that there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0
satisfying

c_{1}n^{r}\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}}e^{-|z|^{2}}\phi(z)\widetilde{\varphi_{n}}(z)dz\right|\leq c_{2}n^{r+\gamma},

then if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, for any
\lambda\in(0,+\infty),
there exists a feedback law
w(t)=K(u(t,\cdot))
with
K\in\mathcal{L}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{2}-r},\mathbb{C})

such that
the system (
45
) is exponentially stable in the
Bargmann
space
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
with decay rate
\lambda.

Proof.

All we need to show is that: the Gribov operator
-\mathcal{A}
generates a
C^{0}
semigroup and the eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{A}
satisfy assumptions (
6
) and (
7
) and then concludes by Corollary
3.5
. Based on
[
35
, Section 4.2, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists small
\varepsilon
and a sequence
(\lambda_{n}^{i})_{n,i}
such that the eigenvalues of
-\mathcal{A}
are simple and can be decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}=-n^{3}+O_{\varepsilon}(1),

where
O_{\varepsilon}(1):=\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varepsilon^{i}\lambda_{n}^{i}\simeq O(|\varepsilon|,1/n).

So the conditions (
6
) and (
7
) hold with
\alpha=3
. The fact that
-\mathcal{A}
generates a
C^{0}
semigroup is a consequence of this and the existence of a Riesz basis of eigenvectors: one can easily check that there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that
\lambda_{n}-\omega<0
and therefore
-\mathcal{A}-\omega
is dissipative and similarly thanks to the Riesz basis of eigenvectors
-\mathcal{A}-\lambda_{0}
is surjective for any

and we can apply for instance Lumer-Phillips theorem to conclude to the existence of a

semigroup.

∎

Strategy and outline

To prove our main result, the main challenge is to show the
F
-equivalence in each of the spaces
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}_{i}
separately, more precisely:

PROPOSITION 4.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
satisfies
(A_{1})
, (
6
) and (
7
). Let
\beta=(\beta_{1},...,\beta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{m})\in[0,(\alpha_{1}-1)/2)\times...\times[0,(\alpha_{m}-1)/2)
. For any
i\in\{1,...,m\}
, assume that
B_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}^{\beta_{i}-\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}}
satisfies (

Then for any

, where
\mathcal{N}=\{\lambda_{n}^{i}-\lambda_{p}^{i}\;|\;(n,p)\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\;i\in\{1,...,m\}\}

there exists a bounded linear operator

K_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\beta_{i}+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{i};\mathbb{C})

and a linear mapping

which is an isomorphism from

to the system,

(47)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v=\mathcal{A}v-\lambda v,\;\;v\in\mathcal{H}^{r}_{i}.\end{array}

The idea is that if Proposition
4.1
holds, then a candidate feedback to obtain the exponential stability with decay rate
\lambda
for the total system is
K=(K_{1},....,K_{m})^{T}
. To do so, the main step is to show that the system with feedback
K=(K_{1},...,K_{m})^{T}
is well-posed, that is

PROPOSITION 4.2
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
3.3
,
and with
K=(K_{1},...,K_{m})^{T}
where the

are given by Proposition
4.1
, for any
\vec{r}
satisfying (

\mathcal{A}+BK
generates a semigroup on

and in particular the system (
11

.

If Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
hold, then Theorem
3.3
(and hence Theorem
3.1
) follows by choosing
T=T_{1}+...+T_{m}
. This is detailed in Section
5.8
, while the proof of Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
are done respectively in Sections
5.1
–
5.6
and Section
5.7
. In the remaining of this section, we present the strategy to show the main Proposition
4.1
and the spirit of the
F
-equivalence
approach.

Principle of the approach.
Formally, having
T_{i}
mapping the system (
46
) to the system (
47
) with
v=T_{i}u
means that
T_{i}
is an isomorphism which satisfies the following operator equality

(48)

T_{i}(\mathcal{A}+B_{i}K_{i})=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda)T_{i},

in some sense. Indeed, still formally, if (
48
) holds and
v=T_{i}u
, then

(49)

\partial_{t}v=T_{i}\partial_{t}u=T_{i}(\mathcal{A}+B_{i}K_{i})u=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda)T_{i}u=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda)v,

and conversely, since
T_{i}
is an isomorphism. Because the operator equality (
48
) has no uniqueness in the solutions
(T,K)
–note that if
(T,K)
is a solution, then
(aT,K)
is still a solution for any
a\neq 0
– it is tempting to add a kind of normalization on
T
which would simplify (
48
). In this regards, a good approach is often to formally add a condition of the form

(50)

T_{i}B_{i}=B_{i},

again in a sense to be defined (note that since
B_{i}
can be unbounded and not belong to the spaces
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{i}
on which we consider
T_{i}
, this inequality may have to be considered in a weak sense). With (
50
), the operator equality (
48
) becomes (formally)

(51)

T_{i}\mathcal{A}+B_{i}K_{i}=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda)T_{i}.

This operator equation is easier to solve than (
48
) since it is linear in
(T,K)
. When
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
is finite-dimensional there exists a unique solution to (
50
)–(
51
) if
(\mathcal{A},B_{i})
is controllable in
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}_{i}
(see
[
30
,
24
]
) which is an additional motivation to adding the condition (
50
). When
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}X}
is infinite-dimensional, the situation is much less clear, but
(
51
) can be translated in terms of actions of
T
on
(\varphi^{i}_{n})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Indeed,
projecting the operator equality (
51
) on the eigenvector
\varphi_{n}^{i}
of
\mathcal{A},
we get

(52)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{i}T_{i}\varphi_{n}^{i}+B_{i}K_{i}(\varphi_{n}^{i})=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda I)T_{i}\varphi_{n}^{i}.

We set
h_{n}^{i}:=T_{i}\varphi_{n}^{i};\ K_{n}^{i}:=K_{i}(\varphi_{n}^{i})
and we project (
52
) on a bi-orthogonal family
(\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i})_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
associated to

to obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{i}\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle+K_{n}^{i}\langle B_{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{A}h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle-\lambda\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lambda_{p}^{i}\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle-\lambda\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle

So we obtain, assuming that
\lambda
is chosen such that

for any

,

\displaystyle\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle=-K_{n}^{i}\frac{\langle B_{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle}{\lambda_{n}^{i}-\lambda_{p}^{i}+\lambda}.

This leads to the following

(53)

T_{i}\varphi_{n}^{i}=h_{n}^{i}=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\langle h_{n}^{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle\varphi_{p}^{i}=-K_{n}^{i}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\langle B_{i},\widetilde{\varphi_{p}}^{i}\rangle}{\lambda_{n}^{i}-\lambda_{p}^{i}+\lambda}\varphi_{p}^{i}.

Hence, if
T_{i}
is a solution to (
51
), it has to have the decomposition (
53
). In particular, as soon as
K_{i}
is chosen, the candidate transform
T_{i}
is fixed and we aim to show that it is indeed an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}^{r}_{i}
to itself and maps the system (
46
) to the system (
47
).

\displaystyle S_{c}a=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}n^{-r}\left(\sum\limits_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{\varphi_{p}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right)=\sum\limits_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{p}\left(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{a_{n}n^{-r}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right).

Thus,

\begin{split}\left\|S_{c}a\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r+\varepsilon}}^{2}&=\left\|\sum\limits_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{p}\left(\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{a_{n}n^{-r}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r+\varepsilon}}^{2}\\
&=\sum\limits_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{2r+2\varepsilon}\left|\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{a_{n}n^{-r}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

Hence, we can use Lemma
5.3
and perform exactly as in

to get the following

LEMMA 5.4
.

Consider

For any

-independence of

Let us introduce

We first claim the following

LEMMA 5.5
.

The adjoint of the operator

in

(69)

\tilde{S}:\varphi_{n}\mapsto\widetilde{q_{n}}.

Proof.

Indeed, consider any

The following holds

\displaystyle\langle Sf,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}f_{n}q_{n},\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}g_{n}\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}f_{n}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\varphi_{p}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda},\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}g_{n}\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}\varphi_{p}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{f_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda},\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}g_{n}\varphi_{n}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N^{*}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\overline{g_{p}}f_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}.

In the same way, we have the following

\displaystyle\langle f,\tilde{S}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}f_{n}\varphi_{n},\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}g_{n}\widetilde{q_{n}}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}

∎

In view of (

), (

),

is
\omega
-independent in
{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
or

is
\omega
-independent in
{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
. With a classical argument (see for instance

]

. So,

into itself, it suffices to show

LEMMA 5.6
.

(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is
{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
-dense
\Leftrightarrow

(\widetilde{q_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is
\omega
-independent in
{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
.

Proof.

Assume that
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is not
{\mathcal{H}^{0}}
-dense. Then there exists a non trivial

such that for any

where

\displaystyle\langle q_{n},f\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{0}}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\overline{f_{p}}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{f_{p}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}}=0

Thus,
\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}f_{p}\widetilde{q_{p}}=\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}f_{p}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\varphi_{n}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}}=\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{f_{p}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}}=0,

-independent.

.

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}f_{p}\widetilde{q_{p}}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{f_{p}}{\overline{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}}=0

Thus by setting

meaning that
(q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

-dense.
∎

\displaystyle\left\|\left(p^{\varepsilon}\lambda\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right)_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{2\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\left|\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{C(\lambda)^{2}}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{2\varepsilon}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{2\varepsilon+2(1-\alpha)}\log^{2}(p),

and this converges for
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-3/2).
So if
\alpha>3/2,
the left hand side of (
79
) holds in
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon})
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-3/2).

Consider now the right hand side of (
79
). We have that

(80)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}k_{n}\tau q_{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}k_{n}n^{\varepsilon}\tau(n^{-\varepsilon}q_{n}).

Recalling that
(n^{-\varepsilon}q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-3/2),
it holds by the isomorphism property of
\tau
that
\tau(n^{-\varepsilon}q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a Riesz basis of
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{\varepsilon}).
Thus in view of (
79
) and (
80
),
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-3/2),

\displaystyle(k_{n}n^{\varepsilon})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2}.

In particular, we conclude that if
\alpha>3/2
,
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{\infty}.

In view of (
77
), we have
K_{n}b_{n}=-(\lambda+k_{n})
and since
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
belongs to
l^{\infty},
we get that

(81)

\displaystyle(K_{n}b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{\infty}\quad\forall\alpha>3/2.

Consider now
1<\alpha\leq 3/2.
In this case the gain of regularity in (
79
) is not enough to deduce the boundedness of
K_{n}b_{n}.
However we are able to show that the equality (
79
) holds in
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{-\varepsilon}),
for
\varepsilon>3/2-\alpha.
Indeed, similarly to the previous case, the following holds

\displaystyle\left\|\left(p^{-\varepsilon}\lambda\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\right\|^{2}_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{-2\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\left|\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}}{C(\lambda)^{2}}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{-2\varepsilon}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}p^{-2\varepsilon+2(1-\alpha)}\log^{2}(p).

This converges for
\varepsilon>3/2-\alpha
and then the left hand side of (
79
) belongs to
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{-\varepsilon}).

Similarly as before and in view of (
79
),

(82)

\displaystyle(k_{n}n^{-\varepsilon})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2}\quad\forall\varepsilon\in\left(\frac{3}{2}-\alpha,\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right).

We can see that we obtain
(\alpha-1)
gain of regularity between (
82
) and (
78
). So in the following, we will make an iterative principle to gain at each order this
(\alpha-1)
regularity in order to prove Lemma
5.8
. So let us go back to the equation (
76
) and expressing
\tau q_{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}-K_{n}b_{n}\left(\frac{b_{n}\varphi_{n}}{\lambda}+\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{b_{p}\varphi_{p}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}b_{n}\varphi_{n}.

Replacing
-K_{n}b_{n}
by
(\lambda+k_{n})
in the first term only and using Fubini’s Theorem in
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon})
we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{k_{n}b_{n}}{\lambda}\varphi_{n}-\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}b_{p}\varphi_{p}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{p\}}\frac{K_{n}b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}=0.

Since
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is a Riesz basis, we have by identification that

\displaystyle\frac{k_{m}b_{m}}{\lambda}=b_{m}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{K_{n}b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Thanks to the fact that
b_{m}\neq 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
K_{n}b_{n}=-(\lambda+k_{n}),
this implies that

\displaystyle k_{m}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{\lambda+k_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Let us now set
\lambda=e_{n}^{0}
and
k_{n}=k_{n}^{0}.
It holds that

(83)

\displaystyle k_{m}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{0}+k_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Thus
k_{m}
can be rewritten as
k_{m}=e_{m}^{1}+k_{m}^{1}
where

\displaystyle e_{m}^{1}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda},\quad k_{m}^{1}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

And using Lemma
5.3
, we have that

(84)

\displaystyle|e_{m}^{1}|\lesssim m^{1-\alpha}\log m+m^{-\alpha}\lesssim 1.

So, we focus on the regularity of
k_{n}^{1}.
We define
\varepsilon_{0}:=\frac{3}{2}-\alpha
and we have

\displaystyle\|(m^{-\varepsilon}k_{m}^{1})_{m}\|_{l^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}m^{-2\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\right)^{2}.

Using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Lemma
5.2
, we get

\displaystyle\|(m^{-\varepsilon}k_{m}^{1})_{m}\|_{l^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}m^{-2\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{|k_{n}^{0}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}|}\right)\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}|}\right).

Then applying Lemma (
5.3
) and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|(m^{-\varepsilon}k_{m}^{1})_{m}\|_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}m^{-2\varepsilon+1-\alpha}\log m\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{|k_{n}^{0}|^{2}}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}|}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|k_{n}^{0}|^{2}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{m^{-2\varepsilon+1-\alpha}\log m}{|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}|}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|k_{n}^{0}|^{2}n^{-2(\varepsilon-1+\alpha)+\sigma},

for any
\sigma>0
. As
k_{n}^{0}=k_{n}
and in view of (
82
), for any
\varepsilon-(1-\alpha)\in\left(3/2-\alpha,\alpha-1/2\right)
(i.e.
\varepsilon\in\left(5/2-2\alpha,1/2\right)
) there exists
\sigma>0
such that this converges. Thus for
\alpha\in(1,3/2]
, the following holds

(85)

\displaystyle(n^{-\varepsilon}k_{n}^{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2},\quad\forall\varepsilon\in\left(\frac{5}{2}-2\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right).

If
\alpha>\frac{5}{4},
we have
\varepsilon_{1}=2\alpha-\frac{5}{2}>0
and then
(n^{\delta}k_{n}^{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2}
for all
\delta\in[0,\varepsilon_{1}).
This ensures that
(k_{n}^{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is uniformly bounded. And combining with (
84
), it ensures that
(k_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
also is uniformly bounded. Therefore
(K_{n}b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is uniformly bounded because
K_{n}b_{n}=-(\lambda+k_{n}).

If
1<\alpha\leq\frac{5}{4},
the uniform boundedness of
K_{n}b_{n}
cannot be immediately deduced, but we need to iterate again. So using the definition (
83
), we have from
k_{n}^{0}=k_{n},
that

\displaystyle k_{m}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{0}+k_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{0}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\left(-\lambda\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{e_{p}^{0}+k_{p}^{0}}{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{n}+\lambda}\right).

Referring to the definitions of
e_{n}^{1}
and
k_{n}^{1},
it holds that

\displaystyle k_{m}

\displaystyle=e_{m}^{1}-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{1}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}^{1}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}

(86)

\displaystyle=e_{m}^{1}+e_{m}^{2}+k_{m}^{2},

where

\displaystyle e_{m}^{2}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{e_{n}^{1}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda},\quad k_{m}^{2}=-\lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}^{1}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Since
k_{m}=e_{m}^{1}+k_{m}^{1},
we have in view of (
86
) that
k_{m}^{1}=e_{m}^{2}+k_{m}^{2}.
This means that if
(e_{m}^{2})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(k_{m}^{2})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
are uniformly bounded, then
(k_{m}^{1})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is and consequently
(k_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}.
Let us first notice that combining (
84
) and Lemma
5.3
, we have

\displaystyle|e_{m}^{2}|\lesssim m^{1-\alpha}\log n+m^{-\alpha}\lesssim 1.

For the
(k_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}},
we use again Lemma (
5.3
) and Fubini’s Theorem to have, similarly as previously:

\displaystyle\|(m^{-\varepsilon}k_{m}^{2})_{m}\|_{l^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}m^{-2\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{m\}}\frac{k_{n}^{1}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}+\lambda}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|k_{n}^{1}|^{2}n^{-2(\varepsilon-1+\alpha)+\sigma},

for any
\sigma>0
.
In view of (
85
), there exists
\sigma>0
such that this converges for any
\varepsilon-(1-\alpha)\in\left(\frac{5}{2}-2\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\right)
meaning for

Thus, for
\alpha\in(1,3/2],

\displaystyle(n^{-\varepsilon}k_{n}^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2},\quad\forall\varepsilon\in\left(\frac{7}{2}-3\alpha,\frac{3}{2}-\alpha\right).

If
\alpha>7/6,
then
\varepsilon_{2}=3\alpha-\frac{7}{2}>0
and
(n^{\delta}k_{n}^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2},
for any
\delta\in[0,\varepsilon_{2}).
This ensures that
(k_{n}^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is uniformly bounded and then
(k_{n}^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is. Thus,
\left(K_{n}b_{n}=-(e_{n}^{0}+e^{1}_{n}+e_{n}^{2}+k_{n}^{2})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is uniformly bounded.

If
1<\alpha\leq\frac{7}{6},
we can continue the induction and have

k_{n}^{i}=e_{n}^{i+1}+k_{n}^{i+1}
for all

where

\displaystyle e_{n}^{i+1}=-\lambda\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{e_{m}^{i}}{\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n}+\lambda},\quad k_{n}^{i+1}=-\lambda\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{k_{m}^{i}}{\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n}+\lambda}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},i\in\mathbb{N}.

And for any

\displaystyle K_{n}b_{n}=-\left(k_{n}^{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{i}e_{n}^{j}\right),\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}

Thus, based on the previous computations, we obtain by induction the following:

(87)

\displaystyle|e_{n}^{i}|\lesssim n^{1-\alpha}\log n+n^{-\alpha}\lesssim 1,

(88)

\displaystyle\|(n^{-\varepsilon}k_{n}^{i+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\|_{l^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|k_{n}^{i}|^{2}n^{-2(\varepsilon-1+\alpha)+\sigma}\log^{2}n

for any
\sigma>0
and (
88
) converges for
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{i},-\varepsilon_{i-2})
with
\varepsilon_{i}=(\alpha-1)i-\varepsilon_{0}
, with
\varepsilon_{0}=1/2
, for all

It is worth stressing that there exists a finite

such that

In this case we have
(n^{\delta}k_{n}^{i_{0}+1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2}
for any

meaning that

Combining this with (

) we conclude that

(89)

\displaystyle\left(K_{n}b_{n}=-\left(k_{n}^{i_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{i_{0}}e_{n}^{j}\right)\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{\infty}\quad\forall\alpha\in(1,3/2].

Therefore we conclude Lemma
5.8
from (
81
) and (

).
∎

5.5.
Step 5:

T
is bounded from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\mathcal{H}^{r}
and satisfies the operator equality

In this section, we would like to prove that the
F
-equivalence
transformation
T
is bounded and the operator equality (
48
) holds at least in some
\mathcal{H}^{s}
space. More precisely, we prove the following Lemma:

LEMMA 5.12
.

The operator
T
given by (
55
) is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\mathcal{H}^{r}
for any
r\in(1/2-\alpha+\gamma,\alpha-1/2).
Moreover, we have the following operator equality,

(90)

\displaystyle T(\mathcal{A}+BK)=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda I)T\quad\text{in}\ \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha/2+s},\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha/2+s}),\quad\forall s\in\left(-\frac{\alpha-1}{2}+\gamma,\frac{\alpha-1}{2}-\gamma\right).

Proof.

We recall here that for any
r\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha,\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)
–and therefore for any
r\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha+\gamma,\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)
– the operator
T
is defined as
T:\ n^{-r}\varphi_{n}\mapsto-n^{-r}K_{n}\tau q_{n}
with
\tau
an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})
and
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
uniformly bounded. Since the space
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})
is neither equal nor included in
\mathcal{H}^{r}
a priori, what we are able to say is that
T
is defined from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r}).
Here we will exploit the regularity on
K_{n}b_{n}
to first show that
T
is well defined from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to itself and then deduce that it is bounded.

Let
r\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha+\gamma,\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)
and

a=\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}n^{-r}\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{r},
the following holds

\displaystyle Ta

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\tau q_{n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{b_{p}\varphi_{p}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}b_{n}\varphi_{n}-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\setminus\{n\}}\frac{b_{p}\varphi_{p}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}+\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}b_{n}n^{-r}\varphi_{n}-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\tau S_{c}(\varphi_{n}).

By setting

\displaystyle T_{1}a=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}b_{n}n^{-r}\varphi_{n},\quad T_{2}a=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\tau S_{c}(\varphi_{n}),

one has

Notice that since
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{2}
and
(K_{n}b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\in l^{\infty},
the term
T_{1}a
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{r}
and in particular we have:

(91)

\displaystyle\|T_{1}a\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}.

So, it suffices to show that the second term
T_{2}a
belongs to
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to conclude that
Ta\in\mathcal{H}^{r}
.
We have the following claim.

CLAIM 5.13
.

For any
r\in\left(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha,\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\right)
and
s\geq\gamma
, the space
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+s})
endowed with the norm

(92)

\|f\|_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+s})}:=\|\tau^{-1}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r+s}}

is a Hilbert space, continuously embedded in
\mathcal{H}^{r}
.

Proof.

For any
q\in\mathbb{R}
, since
\tau
is an isomorphism from the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{q}
into
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{q})
, the space
\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+s})
is a Hilbert space with the norm (
92
). Consider

there exists
f=\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}f_{n}n^{-r-s}\varphi_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{r+s}
such that
g=\tau(f)
, thus

\displaystyle g=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}(f_{n}b_{n}n^{-s})n^{-r}\varphi_{n}.

From (
13
) (with
\beta=0
) and the fact that
\gamma\leq s
, we have

and we observe that

\displaystyle\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}=\|(f_{n}b_{n}n^{-s})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\|^{2}_{l^{2}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|f_{n}b_{n}n^{-s}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|f_{n}|^{2}=\|g\|^{2}_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+s})}.

and this concludes the Claim
5.13
.
∎

Going back to the norm of
T_{2}a
now, we have
based on Claim
5.13
,

\displaystyle\|T_{2}a\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}=\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}n^{-r}\tau S_{c}(\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}\tau S_{c}(n^{-r}\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+\gamma})}.

From (

) and Lemma
5.4
,

is a continuous operator from
\mathcal{H}^{r}
to
\mathcal{H}^{r+\varepsilon}
for any

. Since

and

and

is a continuous operator
from

to
\mathcal{H}^{r+\gamma}
and, using the isomorphism property of

and (

),
one has

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}\tau S_{c}(n^{-r}\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r+\gamma})}\lesssim\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}\tau(n^{-r}\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})}.

Using that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}a_{n}K_{n}\tau(n^{-r}\varphi_{n})\right\|_{\tau(\mathcal{H}^{r})}\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|a_{n}K_{n}|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}|a_{n}|^{2}=\|a\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}.

and combining this with (

), it holds that,

\displaystyle\|Ta\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}}\lesssim\|a\|_{\mathcal{H}^{r}},

and thus

\mathcal{H}^{r}
to itself.

It remains now to prove the operator equality (

) to conclude the lemma. As, from Lemma

, the equality (

for any

.
Thus proving (

) amounts to show that for any

\displaystyle T\mathcal{A}+BK=(\mathcal{A}-\lambda I)T\quad\text{in}\ \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha/2+s},\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha/2+s}).

Observe first that all terms make sense : indeed,

is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha/2+s}
to

6.
Conclusion and perspectives

In this paper, we extended the
F
-equivalence approach (
sometimes called
generalized backstepping), to a wide class of linear systems. This includes systems that are not necessarily skew-adjoint or self-adjoint provided that the differential operator involved has a Riesz basis of eigenvectors (hence not necessarily orthonormal) and is of order larger than one. We obtain conditions that are less restrictive than the ones proposed in
[
39
,
40
]
and we show that we can get nice rapid stabilization properties even when the system is not exactly controllable and the control operator is not admissible
in the space of stabilization
.
We illustrate these results with the rapid stabilization of several examples such as the Schrödinger equation, a general diffusion equation,
Burgers equation
, and a system that is neither self nor skew adjoint. For Schrödinger equation and Burgers’ equation, in particular, we obtain less restrictive conditions on the control operator than what was obtained in
[
22
,
68
,
40
]
.
This result makes the
F
-equivalence one step closer to a general theory. Nevertheless, it is still a young approach and there are many questions that remain open and that would be interesting to consider

•

If
\mathcal{A}
only has a generalized basis of eigenvectors (and not a Riesz basis of eigenvectors which we assume here), is it still possible to extend this method? This is interesting in particular for systems of PDE, where
\mathcal{A}
does not always have a Riesz basis of eigenvectors.

•

What happens if the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
are infinite-dimensional? In particular, this could be useful for multidimensional systems. While Theorem
3.1
and
3.3
are not limited to 1D systems, in several multidimensional systems the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
may have infinite multiplicity (think of the heat equation for instance).
What happens if
\alpha=1
? In this case, only a few
F
-equivalence results exist and only in particular cases
[
90
,
24
]
, while there is a large literature on the controllability and stabilization of several such systems, for instance the wave equations
[
50
,
83
]
.

•

Can these linear results be extended to (very) nonlinear (i.e. quasi-linear) systems? This is all the more important that for infinite-dimensional systems the stability of the linearized system does not necessarily imply the stability of the nonlinear system, even locally
[
28
]
when they are at least quasi-linear, and there is so far no general method for stabilizing these systems.

•

Is it possible to further relax the conditions on
B
?

•

Can the method be used for systems with a bilinear control (such as in
[
2
]
) ?

•

What happens if we have only partial knowledge of the eigenvectors and eigenvalues and we can only construct in practice
\hat{K}
that depends only on a finite-number of eigenvalues and
b_{n}
? If the system is parabolic, because high frequencies are stable, one can expect the system with control feedback law
\hat{K}
to still be stable. When the system is not parabolic however, the question is more complicated.

\displaystyle|\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{p}|

\displaystyle=|cn^{2}-cp^{2}+O(1)|.

Since

\lim\limits_{p^{2}+n^{2}\rightarrow+\infty,p\neq n}|n^{2}-p^{2}|\geq\lim\limits_{p^{2}+n^{2}\rightarrow+\infty}|p+n|=+\infty,

there exists

\displaystyle\geq\eta|(n+p)(n-p)|

\displaystyle\geq\eta n|n-p|,

which makes (

\alpha_{i}=\alpha=2
