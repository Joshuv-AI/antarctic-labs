Development validation of a ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults
Authors: Li Huang, Yanli Zhao, Shen Xu, Taiping Lin, Deying Kang, Ningxiu Li, Yadong Liu, Ning Ge, Jirong Yue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesWe aimed to develop and validate a brief sarcopenia screening tool to improve case finding in community settings.DesignA cross-sectional study.ParticipantsCommunity-dwelling older adults in western China.MethodsWe systematically searched the PubMed and Embase databases to establish an initial item pool. The items were further selected and refined using the Delphi method and based on Classical Test Theory (CTT), resulting in the formal version of the sarcopenia screening instrument. Development and validation samples were recruited from three community settings in China. community-dwelling older adults in Chengdu and Hangzhou, China, were enrolled between May 2021 and December 2022. The gold standard for sarcopenia diagnosis was based on AWGS 2019 and EWGSOP 2018 criteria.ResultsA consensus was reached on the final 6-item ultra-brief screening tool with a unidimensional structure. Cut-off scores of ≥10, ≥15, ≥20 were evaluated to find the optimal sensitivity and specificity. At the cut-off value of ≥10, this ultra-brief screening tool had the best sensitivity of 86.9% compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria, and best sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria. Overall, the AUC of the screening instrument ranged from 0.798 to 0.822 based on Asian standards, compared to 0.840 to 0.922 based on European standards.ConslucionsThis instrument has good psychometric properties, high sensitivity; therefore, it may be considered a suitable instrument for sarcopenia screening in community settings.Keywords: Development and validation, Sarcopenia, Screening instrument, Classical test theory

Introduction

Sarcopenia, defined by the loss of muscle mass, strength and function, is common common geriatric disorder among adults of older age. In terms of longer-term clinical outcomes, Sarcopenia leads to a high risk of falls, frailty, functional decline, disability, hospitalization, and death [1]. According to 2019 Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia(AWGS) Consensus, the epidemiology of sarcopenia prevalence ranged from 5.5% to 25.7% in Asian countries [2]. However, there are challenges in clinical diagnostic approaches, as most cases of sarcopenia remain undetected in the community and home settings at an early stage. Currently, a formal diagnosis of sarcopenia requires the application of validated muscle mass measurement modalities, including magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry and bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) [3]. Yet, these instruments are often unavailable in community settings and are costly and time-consuming [4,5].

Recent emphasis has been placed on the early identification and prevention of sarcopenia, a timely and accurate screening tool is essential. In the past decades, AWGS 2019 and updated 2018 European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People have recommended the use of SARC-F for sarcopenia screening. SARC-F was developed by Professor Morley in 2013 using data from the Hong Kong Mr and Ms Os study [6]. It comprises five items: Slowness, Assistance walking, Rising from chair, Climbing stairs, and Falls. Each item scores 0,1 or 2 points. SARC-F is not perfect because of its high specificity but low sensitivity (3.8%∼9.9%) [6]. Low sensitivity indicates that only severe cases may be found at the early stage of sarcopenia, resulting in a high risk of missed diagnosis. Recently, more screening tools such as the Mini Sarcopenia Risk Assessment (MSRA) and the Ishii score are validated in geriatric clinical settings. Some evidence have demonstrated that the MSRA seems have a better sensitivity level than the SARC-F for screening sarcopenia. However, firm conclusions cannot be reached, because the findings for the two instruments were derived from two different study populations [7]. Yet, there is no consensus on the best screening tool.

There is a need for a novel, simple, validated screening instrument to accurately identify sarcopenia in clinical practice. The objective of this research was to develop and validate an ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia among Chinese community-dwelling older adults, and to evaluate its diagnostic test accuracy. We systematically assessed the psychometric properties of this tool, with the aim of providing insights into improving the sensitivity of sarcopenia screening in community settings.

Study samples

We conducted a cross-sectional, community-based research, between 2021 and 2022 across 3 community settings, 2 in Chengdu, 1 in Hangzhou. Our study was registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2100046367) at http://www.chictr.org.cn/index.aspx; Date of clinical trial registration was 15/05/2021. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of West China Hospital, Sichuan University; the approval number is 2021(96). Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) community residents aged over 60 years; (2) had lived in current residence for 3 years; (3) participants volunteered as subjects for our study, and also complied with relevant ethical regulations. Exclusion criteria were: (1) MMSE below 18 points; (2) visual impairment, hearing loss, or dementia inability to complete the assessment; (3) psychiatric disorders. All eligible participants signed an informed consent; and our study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of West China Hospital, Sichuan University.

Pilot study sample- the initial 14-item scale development sample included 458 community-dwelling older adults from Chengdu, Sichuan province in China recruited between May 2021 and July 2021. To form the final ultra-brief screening tool, 458 individuals were chosen to evaluate the reliability, construct validity, and criterion validity. After we removed the redundants items or inappropriate entries, the well-established validity and reliability of screening instrument was confirmed. A consensus was reached on the final 6-item ultra-brief screening tool.

Validation study sample- once we selected the final 6-item ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia, we assessed its diagnostic accuracy with another 2000 community-dwelling residents aged 60 or more. We recruited participants from Hangzhou city, in the Zhejiang province, and Chengdu city, in the Sichuan province in China between Oct 1, 2022 and Dec 30, 2022. Sensitivity, specificity of 10, 15, 20 cutoff points in the ultra brief sarcopenia screener were respectively calculated in comparsion to the 2019 sarcopenia reference standard. A total of 1777 community-dwelling participants aged over 60 years in Gongping Community, Wenjiang District, Chengdu, Sichuan Province and Hedong Road Community, Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province in China finished final 6-item sarcopenia ultra-brief screening tool.

Sarcopenia reference standard

The gold standard of sarcopenia diagnosis in this study was based on 2019 Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS): low muscle strength was identified as handgrip <28.0 kg for males and <18.0 kg for females, we used the spring-type dynamometer (CAMRY EH101). Gait speed <1 m/s was defined as low physical performance. The cut-off value for low skeletal muscle mass via Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA) are <7.0 kg/m2 in men and BIA <5.7 kg/m2 in women. In 2018, the European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP) defined low handgrip strength as <27.0 kg for males and <16.0 kg for females, with low physical performance set at a gait speed of <0.8 m/s. Their BIA-derived cut-offs for low muscle quantity (ASM/height2) were <7.0 kg/m2 for men and <6.0 kg/m2 for women.

Development and validation of a ultra-brief screening tool

4-stage process was conducted to develop a ultra-brief screening tool: (1) Content and item generation; (2) Scale item evaluation; (3) Scoring of the ultra-brief screening tool; (4) Validation study and diagnostic accuracy. Recruitment and sampling procedures in each of the four stages is reported in detail here. Stage 1 and 2 were performed between March 1, 2021 and October 15, 2021. Stage 3 and 4 were conducted between March 10, 2022 and December 30, 2022.

Stage 1. Content and item generation

To generate a pool of items for sarcopenia screening conceptual framework, we conducted a multistep process. At first, we systematicaly searched all available literatures, and summarized all existing sarcopenia screening instruments. A pool of 74 items comprised the initial item bank.

Stage 2. Scale item evaluation

We used a Delphi consensus process for this study, 17 geriatric experts and 5 experts in nutrition, exercise, public health, and evidence-based medicine were were assembled prior to initiating the Delphi model. A three-round Delphi process gauges opinions through email online platform from the panel of 22 experts. The process was performed in 2021, with a three-week interval between the first and second rounds. After two-round Delphi process, 58 items were removed because of redundancy or lack of effectiveness for sarcopenia screening. In the third round, we conducted 90-minute focus groups to refine the content validity and reliability of the items.

After a three-round Delphi expert consultation, three dimensions with 14 items within the item pool were retained to form the preliminary version of the sarcopenia screening instrument. The items were further subjected to selection using discrete trend method, factor analysis, correlation coefficient, and Cronbach's α coefficient based on Classical Test Theory (CTT), The formal sarcopenia screening instrument comprises six items in a single dimension was finalized.

Stage 3. Scoring of the scale and pilot study

We created a short-form screening tool with six items in one dimension, Each items of sarcopenia screening was rated as absent (0), mild (5 score) or marked (10 score). The full score of the formal sarcopenia screening instrument was 70. Calf circumference (CC) <34 cm for men and <33 cm for women was rated as 10. severe diminution of CC <31 cm for both sexes was rated as 20. With the Classical Test Theory (CTT) used to simplify the items further, 458 individuals were recruited to evaluate the reliability, construct validity, and criterion validity. Finally, the formal version of sarcopenia screening instrument with 6 items within a single dimension was finalized. Our study protocol and participants’ informed consent were approved by the West China Hospital Institutional Review Board.

Stage 4. Validation study and diagnostic accuracy

For the validation process, we assessed its diagnostic accuracy with another 2000 community-dwelling residents aged 60 or more. We recruited community participants aged over 60 years from Hangzhou city, in the ZheJiang province, and Chengdu city, in the SiChuan province in China between Oct 1, 2022 and Dec 30, 2022. The full score of the formal sarcopenia screening instrument was 70. sensitivity, specificity at cut-off values 10, 15, 20 were estimated to determine the best cut-point values based on the 2019 Asian sarcopenia reference standard. In addition, we also calculate the AUC under the Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to identify the best diagnostic accuracy.

Statistical analyses

We performed descriptive statistical analysis to report the demographic and clinical characteristics of the pilot study and validation study. All descriptive statistics were reported separately for men and women. The prevalence of chronic diseases was expressed as frequencies and percentages.

To assess the reliability and validity of the ultra-brief screening tool, we conducted Pearson correlation analysis and calculated Cronbach’s α coefficients. The structural dimensions of the scale and its subscales were delineated via exploratory factor analysis (EFA), utilizing principal component extraction with an oblique (oblimin) rotation. Sampling adequacy and data suitability were verified using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. Factor retention was determined by a loading threshold of 0.40 in conjunction with a visual inspection of the scree plot. Furthermore, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed using AMOS 24.0 to validate the scale’s hypothesized factor structure. Criterion validity was evaluated by comparing sensitivity and specificity against the AWGS 2019 and 2018 EWGSOP consensus. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and areas under the curve (AUC) were compared using the DeLong test. An AUC of 0.90 or greater was categorized as demonstrating high diagnostic accuracy, whereas an AUC between 0.70 and 0.90 reflects moderate diagnostic capability.

Results

Characteristics of participants

In the pilot study, 458 participants in Huayang District, Chengdu city, Sichuan province, in China finished the pilot 14-item questionnaire, 185 individuals were excluded due to missing bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) data. A total of 273 completed the 2019 AWGS sarcopenia assessment. Approximately 80% of subjects were aged 60–79 years, and around 60% were female.

In the validation study, a total of 1777 community-dwelling participants aged over 60 years in Gongping Community, Wenjiang District, Chengdu city, Sichuan Province and Hedong Road Community, Hangzhou city, Zhejiang Province in China finished final 6-item sarcopenia ultra-brief screening tool. After excluding 182 individuals due to a lack of BIA data, 1,595 participants ultimately completed the sarcopenia assessments according to both the 2019 AWGS and 2018 EWGSOP criteria. Approximately 70% of the participants were female. In terms of education, individuals with a primary school education or illiterate accounted for 47%∼48%, 41%∼45% had completed middle or high school, and only 6%∼9% had reached college or university. Sarcopenia prevalence

In the pilot study, 51 of the 273 participants met the AWGS 2019 criteria for sarcopenia, yielding a prevalence of 18.6%. Some individuals were ineligible for BIA assessment due to contraindications such as cardiac pacemakers, metal plates, or prosthetic joints. Within the study population, hypertension (17.0%) and diabetes (10.6%) were the predominant chronic conditions, with a notably low prevalence of other morbidities. Subsequently, in the validation phase, 137 of the 1,595 individuals were identified with sarcopenia by AWGS criteria, representing a prevalence of 8.5% in this cross-sectional survey. The prevalence of hypertension ranged from 10% to 14%, while other chronic conditions were negligible. Approximately 80% of subjects were female, no significant differences were observed in height, weight, or age across the various subgroups. More details are shown in Table 3.

Reliability and validity

The item pool of sarcopenia obtained through a literature search was composed of 74 items across five dimensions. After a three-round expert consultation using the Delphi method, three dimensions with 14 items within the item pool were retained to form the preliminary version of the sarcopenia screening instrument. With the CTT used to simplify the items further, the formal sarcopenia screening instrument with 6 items within a single dimension was finalized. A final ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia was shown in Table 2. In the validation set of 458 individuals, the formal sarcopenia screening instrument showed good internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s α coefficient = 0.805) and excellent test-retest reliability (ICC: 0.723−0.855). The results of confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) showed good structural validity (χ2/df = 1.117, GFI = 0.986, CFI = 0.997, SRMR = 0.029, RMSEA = 0.023).

Diagnostic accuracy

We examined diagnostic accuracy of the ultra-brief sarcopenia screening tool in Table 4. 1595 participants completed this study. The full score of the formal sarcopenia screening instrument was 70. Cut-off scores of ≥10, ≥15, ≥20 were estimated to find the optimal sensitivity and specificity. The overall diagnostic accuracy was moderate to high, area under the ROC curve (AUC) ranged from 0.798 (95%CI: 0.768−0.829) to 0.922 (95%CI: 0.894−0.949). At the cut-off value of ≥10, this ultra-brief screening tool has the best sensitivity of 86.9% compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria, and best sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria. As expected, the specificity increased with the cut-off scores, in contrast, the sensitivity decreased.

Discussion

Principal findings

Sarcopenia is an important skeletal muscle disorder to detect. In community settings, most cases of sarcopenia go undiagnosed in the early stage, because most screening instruments are not accurate. This study provides a new ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults, and rigorously describes its development and validation in two independent samples. We confirmed the usefulness and validity of this screening instrument, which has good psychometric properties, satisfactory reliability and validity, and notably, a significant relationship with diagnostic accuracy related to sarcopenia.

We assessed the diagnostic accuracy and observed that the cut-off value of ≥10, this ultra-brief screening tool has the best sensitivity of 86.9% compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria, and best sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria. The novel screening tool demonstrated high diagnostic performance in our study. Using the AGWS standard with cut-off scores of ≥10, ≥15, and ≥20, this instrument achieved AUC values of 0.798 (95%CI: 0.768−0.829), 0.830 (95%CI: 0.797−0.864), and 0.822 (95%CI: 0.784−0.860), respectively. In addition, we found the diagnostic performance is higher under European reference standards (AUC: 0.798 ∼0.822) compared to Asian standards (AUC: 0.840 ∼0.922). Highlights of this study include the high sensitivity of a novel screening tool and its rigorous prospective validation across two independent samples.

In our study, the prevalence of sarcopenia was 18.6% in the pilot phase and 8.5% in the validation phase, both of which fall within the reported range for Asian populations (5.5%–25.7%). The PPV of 20%∼30% observed in this study is consistent with the performance of similar community screening instruments. Conversely, the Negative Predictive Value (NPV) exceeded 97%, demonstrating the scale’s superior capacity for ruling out non-sarcopenic individuals. These results suggest that our scale is more suitable as a first-stage screening tool rather than a definitive diagnostic instrument.

Comparison with other screening instruments

SARC-F serves as a classical screening instrument for sarcopenia, and Exhibits high specificity for the detection rate of sarcopenia among community-dwelling older adults. Huang et al. [3] reported the pooled sensitivity of SARC-F was 36%(95% CI: 28%–44%) and pooled specificity was 87% (95%CI: 0.82%-0.90%) across 35 studies involving 19707 individuals. A systematic review published in JAMDA in 2019 evaluated the SARC-F and its modified versions, reporting that SARC-CalF achieved low to moderate sensitivity (45.9%–57.2%) and high specificity (87.7%–91.3%) [8]. Low to moderate sensitivity of the SARC-F renders it less than optimal for early-phase sarcopenia screening, as it carries a high risk of missed sarcopenia cases. The high specificity of the SARC-F minimizes false positives, making it an effective tool for selecting individuals to undergo further confirmatory testing for sarcopenia. According to the consensus of the European expert panel, SARC-F primarily serves as a filter for detecting severe cases of sarcopenia [9]. In this study, the newly developed ultra-brief screening scale was compared with the SARC-F and SARC-CalF among 770 participants in the Chengdu region. However, due to study design constraints, this comparative analysis was restricted to the single-center sample from Chengdu. It was not conducted as a synchronous, face-to-face, head-to-head comparison across the entire multicenter validation cohort (e.g., Hangzhou). Consequently, a comprehensive evaluation of the overall performance differences between the new scale and established classic instruments remains limited at this stage.

The MSRA, a seven- or five-item brief, was developed by professor Andrea P. Rossi for Italian community dwelling elderly subjects in 2017 [10]. The MSRA questionnaire includes several risk factors for muscle mass and strength loss, such as physical activity level, dietary intake assessment, age, hospitalizations, and weight loss. Both SARC-F and MSRA are brief self-reported questionnaires, can be easily implemented through face-to-face interviews, telephone, or the Internet [7]. Yang et al. reported that MSRA-5 demonstrated a high sensitivity of 90.2% and a specificity of 70.6% based on the AWGS criteria among 384 community older adults in Chengdu, China. In comparison, the MSRA-7 yielded a sensitivity of 86.9% and a lower specificity of 39.6% [7]. As a whole, the MSRA-5 has a better sensivity and diagnostic accuracy for sarcopenia screening compared with MSRA-7. It is worth mentioning that both SARC-F and MSRA were originally developed in non-Asian populations. The dietary intake items in the MSRA were designed for Italian and Mediterranean populations. However, European eating habits were markedly from those in China, most chinese people do not consume dairy products on a daily basis, and some do not consume dairy products at all [11].

According to the current guidelines, sarcopenia diagnosis is time-consuming and requires devices to measure muscle strength, muacle mass, and physical performance. SARC-F, MSRA, the ultra-brief screening tool may serve as the first step to identify low muscle strength, low muscle mass, and low physical function. The item “How much difficulty do you have in lifting and carrying 10 lb?” represents muscle strength, it is unfortunately limited by a ceiling effect. The vast majority of individuals can pick up something that weighs 10 pounds, which contributes to the low sensitivity of the SARC-F. Handgrip strength below 28 kg in men and below18 kg in women is defined as low muscle strength in the AWGS 2019 criteria. However, the 28 kg and 18 kg diagnostic thresholds cannot be used to replace the existing 10 lb metric currently employed in screening scales. Lifting 10 pounds and performing a grip strength test involve different force generation patterns, joint movements, and upper limb muscle groups. Among existing sarcopenia screening instruments, very few items assess muscle strength, and no other reports on this topic have been published in the current literature. The items “How much difficulty do you have walking across a room?” and “How much difficulty do you have transferring from a chair or bed?” are reflections of poor physical performance, and also have a ceiling effect. Indeed, most community-dwelling individuals aged 60∼70 years do not use canes and do not require hand support when rising from chairs. This leads to low sensitivity of the SARC-F and makes sarcopenia screening more challenging. Sarcopenia prevalence is estimated at 11%∼50% among people aged over 80 years [12]. The oldest individuals aged over 80 years experience a sharp decline in physical, social, daily living, and cognitive functions, and are consequently, the group most in need of health care and assistance [13]. Therefore, we believe that the items regarding cane use and the ability to stand up from a chair without assistance are more suitable for screening individuals over 80 years old or those with severe sarcopenia. Falls serve as a clinical indicator and adverse consequence of sarcopenia. The item “fall in the past year” is recommended be excluded based on Classical Test Theory. Research indicates that falls had a weaker association with SPPB, IADL, grip strength, gait speed, and 5- time chair stand test compared to other items of the SARC-F [14].

In this study, we strictly followed the Patient-Reported Outcome (PRO) Instrument in accordance with the consensus-based standards for the selection of health measurement instruments (COSMIN) checklist. Using a process of 22 experts focus group and an online Delphi consultation, we modified the items of sarcopenia screening. The new ultra-brief screening tool showed good psychometric properties under Classical Test Theory frameworks. The items “perform physical activities” and “Walking more than 1,000 meters” are reflection of muscle function, and are predictors of sarcopenia. Health-related quality of life may be impaired in the presence of sarcopenia. The item “Feeling of muscle weakness” can compromise quality of life, and is useful in screening for sarcopenia and sarcopenic obesity in older women. The item “feeling of walking slower” reflects poor gait speed. It was inspired by the SarQol questionnaire, including items such as “feeling of fatigue when walking” and “have difficulties to cross a road fast enough”.

To our knowledge, this is the first literature to develop and validate a ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults. Our study indicated that this new sarcopenia screening instrument have good diagnostic performance. Diagnostic accuracy is typically interpreted as high, moderate, or low when AUCs fall within the ranges of >0.9, 0.7 to 0.9, and 0.5 to 0.7, respectively. Overall, the AUC of the screening instrument ranged from 0.798 to 0.822 based on Asian standards, compared to 0.840 to 0.922 based on European standards.

Strengths and limitations

In clinical practice, both high sensitivity and high specificity are crucial for screening individuals at risk of sarcopenia. Our study developed and validated a new ultra-brief screening tool for sarcopenia in Chinese community-dwelling older adults, which achieved a sensitivity of 86.9% when compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria and achieved a sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria at the cut-off value of ≥10. A major strength of this study is rigorous design, this new instrument have good psychometric properties, high sensitivity compared with the AWGS sarcopenia criteria and EWGSOP criteria. The reduction and revision of the item pool, as well as the development and validation of the instrument, were performed under the guidance of experts in evidence-based medicine and geriatrics. Our instrument is convenient, user-friendly and requires no special equipment, making it more suitable for large-scale community screening. In addition, we conducted two prospective validations with a large sample size among Chinese community-dwelling older adults. The subjects were healthy, community-dwelling older adults with almost no chronic diseases.

We acknowledge that several limitations should be considered in this study. First, comprehensive evaluation of the overall performance differences between the new scale and established classic instruments remains limited at this stage. Second, we observed that the cut-off value of ≥10, this ultra-brief screening tool has the best sensitivity of 86.9% compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria, and best sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria. This instrument achieved its highest specificity at a cut-off value of ≥20, making It difficult to draw conclusions about the appropriateness of the cut-off value. Furthermore, the present study was a single center study limited to Chinese community settings, validation in nursing homes and hospitals has not yet been performed. Considering BIA is free of x-ray exposure and is a more convenient and inexpensive choice in the community, we used BIA for assessing skeletal muscle mass in this study. Finally, like other screening tools, this new instrument cannot be a surrogate for the gold standard in diagnosing sarcopenia.

Conclusions

This new ultra-brief screening tool have good psychometric properties, high sensitivity compared with the AWGS sarcopenia criteria and EWGSOP criteria. We observed that the cut-off value of ≥10, this ultra-brief screening tool has the best sensitivity of 86.9% compared with AWGS sarcopenia criteria, and best sensitivity of 97.3% compared with EWGSOP criteria.
