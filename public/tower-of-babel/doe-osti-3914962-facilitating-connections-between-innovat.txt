Executive Summary
In 2018, the U.S. Department of Energy established the American-Made program to offer prizes
and competitions as a government funding mechanism in addition to traditional funding options
like grants. The American-Made program is a U.S. Department of Energy (DOE)-funded
program that works to advance domestic energy innovation. The program is broken into two
branches, which work together to achieve this mission: American-Made Challenges and the
American-Made Network. American-Made Challenges are prizes and competitions that award
non-dilutive funding and other incentives to startups, entrepreneurs, students, and beyond for
solving a defined energy innovation challenge. The American-Made Network is an ecosystem of
energy industry partners representing a wide breadth of energy and business expertise, including
national laboratories, incubators, manufacturers, law firms, test facilities, business development
experts, and more. Those competing in challenges are encouraged to leverage the network as
they seek partners and vendors to test, validate, and grow their businesses during and beyond the
prize, bringing them closer to commercialization.

The primary goal of the network funding secured for this scope of work was to accelerate the
development, commercialization, and adoption of innovation in energy technologies. This
project expanded the network to create an ecosystem of over 500 organizations across the nation
that are primed to quickly support DOE programs. American-Made staff funded by this project
evaluate all organizations prior to membership acceptance to ensure they can provide valuable,
consistent support. Within the network is a subset of organizations known as Power Connectors,
which are companies contracted with the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) to provide
specific services and complimentary support to applicants in select prizes and competitions.

1 Network Management
1.1 Network Growth (Orientations and Insightly)
To support a growing network, NLR developed and hosted a twice a month onboarding
process, which supported the addition of approximately 10-20 new organizations per month.
The onboarding process existed in three distinct phases: intake, orientation, and follow-up.
Intake was done via an interest form that an interested organization could fill out on the
American-Made website. Orientations included an overview of the program’s history,
network member success stories, and opportunities for network members to support
competitors. Finally, the post-orientation follow-up included the presentation slide deck and
directions to join the private American-Made Network LinkedIn group.

In FY23, NLR continued efforts with the Network Matching Tool, which was created in FY22 to
match organizations with prize competitors’ specific needs. While the original project budget
included funding to create the tool, a there was a lack of additional funds to maintain the effort.
Consequently, the impact of the tool was reduced as network members did not respond to
competitor requests. The team continued offering bi-weekly orientations for new members,
informed members on program growth, and shared prize advancements to multiple DOE offices.
In total, the team vetted and approved more than 80 network applications per fiscal year.
Given the upkeep challenges of the network matching tool, the team successfully proposed a
longer-term solution with less maintenance requirements. In FY24, NLR successfully
transitioned from the Network Matching Tool to a new customer relationship management
platform called Insightly, which allowed the team to easily vet and approve over 200 new
organizations—the largest growth of the network in a single fiscal year. By early 2025, the
network had grown to include a total of 596 organizations that had expressed interest in being
participants in the American-Made Network.

1.2 Audience Impact (Communication)
The NLR team integrated network announcements within the suite of communication tools
used by the American-Made program. Through 52 editions of the American-Made bi-weekly
newsletter, NLR was able to effectively promote 244 network-related events or prize
deadlines. The American-Made communications team ran a sign-up campaign leading up to
each of the annual network summit events. These saw an average open rate of 50% and an
average click rate of 11% across the two campaigns in 2023, and 2024.

Within the bi-weekly newsletter, the “Connection Corner” section was dedicated to
promoting relevant network member-led events. Every other week, administrators would
email the network to collect submissions for events relevant to the prize, innovator, or
entrepreneur communities. These events were then vetted and promoted to the full
distribution list of the American-Made newsletter. Since 2022, 244 non-prize related network
member events were submitted, eliciting 1,412 clicks from readers.

2 Prize Support
2.1 Recognition Rewards
 Each prize in the American-Made program has access to the network, to add value for their
competitors. In some cases (depending on the individual program rules and guidelines),
network members, excluding Power Connectors, are eligible to receive monetary Recognition
Rewards for their documented support in helping competitors achieve goals and win
competition phases. This incentivizes members to proactively spread awareness about prizes,
connect with businesses that need their support, and form early partnerships.

Eight prizes used Recognition Rewards from September 2022 to March 2025, including Solar
Prize Rounds 6 and 7; the Solar Desalination Prize; the Geothermal Geophone Prize; the
Geothermal Manufacturing Prize; the Perovskite Startup Prize; the Envelope Retrofit
Opportunities for Building Optimization Technologies (E-ROBOT) Prize; and the Equitable
and Affordable Solutions to Electrification (EAS-E) Prize. In all, Recognition Rewards have
totaled $2.7 million across three DOE funding offices (buildings, geothermal, and solar). The
average award amount was just over $12,000.
These cash funds serve as incentives for network members to provide high-quality support to
competitors. The competitors benefit from the support and the DOE funding office benefits
from higher-quality submissions and increased innovation speed. The exact rules vary by
challenge, but usually, competitors that have won a phase are allocated a set amount of cash
to be awarded to network members that supported their application.

Recognition Rewards incentivize the network members to provide pro bono support with the
opportunity to win a cash prize to accelerate technology development, commercialization,
and adoption; expand the number of prize applicants; and strengthen connections of
companies across the U.S. that are engaging in DOE programs.
The chart below shows how many network members assisted teams in each round of the
Solar Prize Ready! Contest. Many network members supported multiple teams. For example,
in Solar Prize Round 7, 46 different Network members were listed on 69 submissions. 1

organizations and DOE to complete a one-to-many, or network-of-network approach to
dissemination and support of applicants without becoming an official part of a team seems to
improve quality of applications and long-term relationships.

2.2 Power Connectors
Power Connectors are a subset of network members that provide outreach and in-depth
support to competitors at a level that other network members may not be able to provide.
Organizations are selected as Power Connectors through a competitive request for proposal
process and receive a subcontract with NLR to] support the American-Made Program. As of
March 2025, the network has placed more than 140 work orders for a total of over $3.9
million across 56 competitions and 11 DOE offices. Of that, Power Connectors primarily
provide recruitment, outreach, and mentorship support for select prizes. NLR has worked
with three cohorts of Power Connectors: 6 organizations were contracted in 2021, another 14
in 2022, and finally, 17 in March 2024.

The most recent group, consisting of 10 organizations from the prior year and seven new
organizations, helped American-Made expand its potential reach to more than 100,000+
professionals and organizations. These organizations have professional services that include
marketing, communications, financial modeling, business strategy development,
commercialization services, and more.
Due to their contract status with NLR, Power Connectors are uniquely suited to provide
support for technology prizes. Their support can increase the quality of submissions that each
prize receives as well as the quality of work and businesses that are active in the energy
industry.

Power Connectors benefit DOE beyond just supporting prize competitors. DOE benefits from
leveraging increased visibility for their programs through the network-of-networks ecosystem
provided by Power Connectors. The access to expertise different than those accessed through

2.3 Examples of Network Support
As one of the American-Made program’s most engaged Power Connectors, Jade Garrett of
Positive Deviancy has supported over 450 teams participating in 63 different American-Made
prizes over the past seven years. Positive Deviancy has helped teams win roughly $14.8
million in cash prizes, with 14 of those teams being grand prize winners. Those competitors
remain active in the energy industry and have raised millions more in private investment,
hired additional employees, and brought innovative products to market.
Since joining the network as a Power Connector in 2020, ADL Ventures has supported
competitors in more than 30 prizes. One particular prize, the Perovskite Startup Prize,
engaged ADL Ventures to identify and strengthen connections to the perovskite industry. By
screening more than 2,000 innovators and providing targeted outreach to 64 high potential
candidates, the organization supported efforts to build a vast competitor pool that, in many
cases, did not have prior experience applying for or receiving federal funding. Their
comprehensive support—from connecting teams with mentors to hosting specialized
workshops—is intended to help teams strengthen both technical and business outcomes.

3 Leveraging American-Made to Connect Energy
  Industry Organizations and Innovators
3.1 American-Made Network Innovation Summit 2023
On Nov. 7–8, 2023, network administrators held the first in-person American-Made Network
Innovation Summit at NLR. Over 100 people attended, including network members, industry
experts, prize alumni, and DOE and NLR staff. Attendees gathered to review successes and
discuss opportunities for programmatic growth. The event featured a keynote address from
American-Made program founders; panels featuring highly engaged network members and
Power Connectors; presentations from prize alumni; an alumni “startup jam session”; a
session on government and private industry collaboration opportunities; and working sessions
on network-related challenges.
The summit resulted in new relationships and education around prize initiatives. One
participant reflected on their favorite part of the summit, saying, “Every aspect of the summit
was excellent. That being said, the opportunity to network in person was the most valuable
aspect of the experience.” Another individual expressed a similar note saying, “More than
other conferences, I felt like this was engaging and fostered collaboration/relationship
building very well.” In all, the American-Made Network Innovation Summit 2023 fostered
interactions between industry and prize alumni while creating direction for the upcoming
year of network activities.

3.2 American-Made Network Innovation Summit 2024
The 2024 summit was held on Oct. 22-23, 2024, at the University of Tulsa in Tulsa,

and utility of the network; status updates from regional hub leaders; a session on maximizing
engagement in the network; a panel on energy impacts from data center expansion; and
breakout sessions to help inform annual network planning. DOE leadership spoke about how
the network is helping achieve DOE’s goals, by integrating competitions and projects
integration into the broader American energy innovation system. Event partner Rose Rock
Bridge also hosted two networking events for summit attendees to further engage and
connect outside of the event.
Feedback on the previous summit indicated that participants were interested in developing a
more systematic approach to the network. One individual shared about the 2023 summit:
“(I’d like to) have breakout sessions with ecosystem builders so that best practices can be
discussed further.” The 2024 breakout brainstorming sessions worked to incorporate this
feedback. Session questions included:

 • What could the American-Made Network learn from other communities or successful
 networks you are a part of?

 • What metrics are important for the network to measure?
These discussions spurred conversations around having regular meetups on a regional scale
and collecting impact metrics from prize participants. From these discussions, network
administrators incorporated themes into strategic initiatives for FY24 including continuing
regional hubs, tracking initial network member engagement via Insightly, and beginning to
develop standardized metric questions to incorporate in prize submission forms.

3.3 Regional Hubs – Fostering Connections Across the Country
In FY24, NLR established technology-specific regional hubs to tap into established regional
networks of expertise. NLR noticed within Insightly datasets that organizations related to
certain topics tended to cluster in specific cities or regions within the United States. To
explore the potential of regional hubs, a meeting was held in each area, including Detroit,
Michigan; Raleigh, North Carolina; Tulsa, Oklahoma; and Portland, Oregon. The hubs
focused on the following areas:

 • Detroit (primary partner: Centropolis Accelerator at Lawrence Technological
 University): Advanced manufacturing and hard-tech expertise in transportation and
 design.

 • Raleigh (primary partners: RIoT and the Research Triangle Institute): Academic and
 research communities in cyber, Internet of Things, and battery/storage.

 • Tulsa (primary partners: VentureWell and Tulsa Innovation Labs): Wind, solar, and
 geothermal.

 • Portland (primary partner: Lemelson Foundation): Battery/storage and hydropower.

These were small, collaboration-focused meetings emphasized vision-casting for how each
region was interested in supporting prizes and the American-Made Network as a whole. The
typical attendance was approximately 75 attendees at each and consisted of startups,
investors, accelerators, incubators, and energy industry associations. The goal of each of
these convenings was to cement connections to organizations in each region that could
execute future industry, broader, convenings in their cities. The host organiations of
VentureWell, Tulsa Innovation Labs, Research Triangle Institute, and Centropolis continue
as strong advocates for energy innovation and the prize programs as a means to achieve
increased commercialization and small business engagement.
Attendees of the regional conveners indicated, anecdotally, stronger relationships between
related opportunities and likely collaboration on future projects. Some attendees have
indicated they learned of other opportunities, such as regional pitch events, accelerators, or
incubators at the regional convenings, to which they then applied. However, due to the
absence of multi-year funding and scope, impact-reporting following the events was not
collected. By inviting network members, organizations, and investors with a particular
interest in the region, the likelihood that work would continue beyond the convening was
increased. Reviews of attendee lists across multiple years indicate correlation between
continued engagement and success in American-Made prizes as well as related energy
industry innovation programs. It is notable that the areas of focus for the convenings such as
advanced manufacturing, cyber security, batteries, geothermal, and hydropower continue to
remain as priorities at the time of this final technical report. For future related efforts, we
would recommend a follow-up tasks 3 or 6 months following a convening to reconnect with
attendees and understand the impact of their attendance on their commercialization and
success.

3.4 Non-American-Made DOE Program Support
In addition to supporting competitors in the American-Made program, DOE has leveraged
the network in other government funding opportunities. DOE’s Federal Energy Management
Program (FEMP) Assisting Federal Facilities with Energy Conservation Technologies
(AFFECT) program offers grants to federal agencies that are developing energy and water
efficiency projects and processes at government-owned facilities. In FY24, FEMP benefitted
from the American-Made Network when the project team was able to commit to providing a
list of at least 80 organizations that could review grant proposals. After reaching out to the
network to gauge interest, NLR provided FEMP with 103 total organizations that were
interested in participating. FEMP selected 62 reviewers, of which they approved 56. This
project created and oversaw the structure that enabled the efficient and timely distribution of
56 honorarium payments to participating organizations. This opportunity allowed another
DOE program to benefit from the investment of building the network. It also gave a
significant number of network members a chance to expand their connections within the
federal space and play a critical role in the FEMP AFFECT program.

4 Conclusion and Next Steps
Connection is at the core of the American-Made Network. The funding provided for this project
allowed the NLR team to establish the network as a clearly structured, consistently utilized,

 6

technology-agnostic ecosystem of innovators, professionals, investors, mentors, associations, and
researchers that, collectively, can increase the speed and scale of energy innovation. Since its
inception, network administrators have continued to build an ecosystem of strong technical and
business resources. Regularly scheduled convenings, a systematic onboarding process, a data-
focused customer relationship management system, and continued communication efforts have
resulted in the network’s robust support of the American-Made program and the wider energy
ecosystem.

Because of the network, prize competitors have access to accelerators, incubators, investors,
subject matter experts, facilities, researchers, and related contacts that will help to maintain the
company’s momentum and impact on the energy industry.

A well-funded American-Made Network maximizes the impact of prizes and other DOE funded
programs. It ensures that each prize receives a sufficient number of quality applicants and that
progress made during the prize is impactful.

With continued multi-year funding, future iterations of the network are expected to provide more
sustained support throughout a team’s prize journey, helping innovators navigate the valleys of
death on the path to commercialization. Administrators will provide ongoing American-Made
alumni support, strengthening pipelines of high-potential innovators, and building a durable,
insight-driven ecosystem that enhances commercialization success, reinforcing long-term
knowledge sharing, and positioning the American-Made Network as a national hub for
innovation, collaboration, and energy-sector advancement.
