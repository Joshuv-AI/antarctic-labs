Introduction

1.1
Context and problem statement

The study of rational periodic points of polynomial maps over number fields lies at the intersection of arithmetic dynamics, Diophantine geometry, and the arithmetic of moduli spaces. For maps defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, each individual map has only finitely many rational preperiodic points, but effective and uniform descriptions remain difficult in general; see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Even for low-degree families, questions about rational periodic points quickly lead to curves of positive genus and to substantial Diophantine input
[
5
,
6
]
.

In this paper we study rational
2
-cycles for the cubic family

f_{b,a}(x)=x^{3}+bx+a,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

(1)

We are interested in the following arithmetic question: if
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is fixed, for which values of
b\in\mathbb{Q}
does the map
f_{b,a}
admit a rational point of exact period
2
? Here a rational
2
-cycle means an ordered pair
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
satisfying

f_{b,a}(x_{1})=x_{2},\qquad f_{b,a}(x_{2})=x_{1},\qquad x_{1}\neq x_{2}.

Over
\mathbb{Q}
, every cubic polynomial is linearly conjugate to one of two standard normal forms
[
7
]
. For period
2
, the odd normal form has a rational parameterization, whereas the second normal form has an elliptic parameter space. The family (
1
) is not itself a normal form, but for each fixed
a\neq 0
it is linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{Q}
to the square-coefficient slice of normal form II. This observation reduces the existence of rational
2
-cycles for
f_{b,a}
to the arithmetic of a one-parameter family of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
.

To keep the notation uniform throughout the paper, we write

\mathcal{E}_{t}:\quad Y^{2}=X^{3}+4X^{2}+16t^{2}

for the generic elliptic family and reserve

E_{a}:=\mathcal{E}_{t}\big|_{t=a}

for the specialization attached to the dynamical family (
1
). This separation of the generic parameter
t
from the dynamical parameter
a
is important when the discussion moves back and forth between torsion in the elliptic family and arithmetic consequences for
f_{b,a}
.

1.2
Previous work

The arithmetic-dynamical background comes from the study of rational periodic and preperiodic points for polynomial and rational maps over global fields
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

\mathbb{Q}
, Benedetto, Dickman, Joseph, Krause, Rubin, and Zhou
[

emphasize the role of the two cubic normal forms and show that the period-

The most important precursor for our purposes is Ingram’s paper

2

2

2

3

[
8

[

What is new here is the arithmetic study of the specialized slice arising from the family

On the elliptic-curve side, our methods belong to the arithmetic of torsion and Mordell–Weil groups in families. Mazur’s theorem gives the possible rational torsion groups over
\mathbb{Q}

2
Cubic normal forms and the specialized period-2 slice

2.1
Minimal normal-form background

Over
\mathbb{Q}
, every cubic polynomial is linearly conjugate to one of the two standard normal forms

\text{(NF I)}\quad\phi(z)=uz^{3}+vz,\qquad\text{(NF II)}\quad\psi(z)=Az^{3}+Bz+1,

(2)

with
u,A\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
v,B\in\mathbb{Q}
; see
[
7
, Section 2]
. The first form is odd, while the second is not. Their period-
2
loci therefore behave differently: in NF I the period-
2
locus is rational, whereas in NF II it is elliptic; the latter elliptic moduli description is the one appearing in
[
8
, Section 4]
.

The family studied in this paper,

f_{b,a}(x)=x^{3}+bx+a,

is not itself a normal form. However, for each fixed
a\neq 0
it is linearly conjugate to the square-coefficient slice of NF II. Because our goal is to study rational
2
-cycles for fixed
a
and varying
b
, only the NF II picture is needed in what follows. We therefore record the NF II period-
2
description explicitly and defer the rational parametrization of NF I, which plays no role in the later arithmetic arguments, to
B
.

2.2
Normal Form II and the period-2 elliptic description

Let

\psi(z)=Az^{3}+Bz+1,\qquad A\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times},\quad B\in\mathbb{Q}.

Suppose that
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
is a rational
2
-cycle for
\psi
, so

\psi(z_{1})=z_{2},\qquad\psi(z_{2})=z_{1},\qquad z_{1}\neq z_{2}.

Introduce the symmetric parameters

s:=z_{1}+z_{2},\qquad p:=z_{1}z_{2},\qquad w:=z_{1}-z_{2},

so that
w^{2}=s^{2}-4p
.

Subtracting the two cycle equations and dividing by
z_{1}-z_{2}\neq 0
gives

A(z_{1}^{2}+z_{1}z_{2}+z_{2}^{2})+B=-1.

(3)

Equivalently,

A(s^{2}-p)+B=-1.

(4)

Adding the cycle equations yields

s=A(z_{1}^{3}+z_{2}^{3})+Bs+2=A\,s(s^{2}-3p)+Bs+2.

The case
s=0
is impossible, since the displayed equation would then read
0=2
. Therefore
s\neq 0
, and dividing by
s
gives

A(s^{2}-3p)+B=1-\frac{2}{s}.

(5)

Subtracting (
4
) from (
5
) gives

p=\frac{1-s}{As}.

(6)

Using
w^{2}=s^{2}-4p
, we obtain

w^{2}=\frac{As^{3}+4s-4}{As}.

(7)

Now set

X:=\frac{1}{s},\qquad Y:=AXw.

(8)

Then (
7
) becomes

E^{(A)}:\quad Y^{2}=A(-4X^{3}+4X^{2}+A).

(9)

The discriminant of this cubic is

\Delta\bigl(E^{(A)}\bigr)=-16A^{5}(27A+16),

so (
9
) defines an elliptic curve for
A\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with
27A+16\neq 0
. This is the elliptic period-
2
model appearing in the cubic normal-form analysis and, in particular, in
[
8
, Section 4, Remark 4.9]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
A\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with
27A+16\neq 0
. Then the set of triples

(B,z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}^{3}
satisfying
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
and

Az_{1}^{3}+Bz_{1}+1=z_{2},\qquad Az_{2}^{3}+Bz_{2}+1=z_{1}

is in bijection with points
(X,Y)\in E^{(A)}(\mathbb{Q})
satisfying

X\neq 0
and
Y\neq 0
. The maps are

(B,z_{1},z_{2})\longmapsto\left(\frac{1}{z_{1}+z_{2}},\,A\,\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{1}+z_{2}}\right)

(10)

and

(X,Y)\longmapsto\left(X-2-\frac{A}{X^{2}},\,\frac{1}{2X}+\frac{Y}{2AX},\,\frac{1}{2X}-\frac{Y}{2AX}\right).

(11)

The corresponding parameter
B
is recovered from
X
by

B=X-2-\frac{A}{X^{2}}.

(12)

Proof.

The forward map is exactly the construction above. Conversely, let
(X,Y)\in E^{(A)}(\mathbb{Q})
with
X,Y\neq 0
and set

s=\frac{1}{X},\qquad w=\frac{Y}{AX}.

Since
(X,Y)
satisfies (
9
), reversing the calculation above shows that (
6
) and (
7
) hold with

p=\frac{X-1}{A}.

Defining

z_{1}=\frac{s+w}{2},\qquad z_{2}=\frac{s-w}{2},

and taking
B
from (
12
) gives (
11
). Moreover, (
4
) yields (
12
), and substitution into
\psi(z)=Az^{3}+Bz+1
shows that
\psi(z_{1})=z_{2}
and
\psi(z_{2})=z_{1}
. The two constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

2.3
Conjugating the cubic family to the square-coefficient slice

We now return to the family

f_{b,a}(x)=x^{3}+bx+a,\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

(13)

Proposition 2.2
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, the map
f_{b,a}
is linearly conjugate over
\mathbb{Q}
to

\psi_{a,b}(z)=a^{2}z^{3}+bz+1,

which is in NF II with parameters
A=a^{2}
and
B=b
.

Proof.

With
M(z)=az
, one has

M^{-1}\circ f_{b,a}\circ M(z)=\frac{f_{b,a}(az)}{a}=a^{2}z^{3}+bz+1.

∎

Because the conjugacy is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, it preserves rational periodic points and exact periods. Thus, for fixed
a\neq 0
, rational
2
-cycles of
f_{b,a}
are governed by the curve (
9
) with
A=a^{2}
.

For later use it is convenient to put this specialized curve into the simpler model

E_{a}:\quad Y^{2}=X^{3}+4X^{2}+16a^{2}.

(14)

If

E^{(a^{2})}:\quad Y_{0}^{2}=a^{2}(-4X_{0}^{3}+4X_{0}^{2}+a^{2}),

then the change of variables

X=-4X_{0},\qquad Y=\frac{4}{a}Y_{0}

(15)

identifies
E^{(a^{2})}
with
E_{a}
. The discriminant of (
14
) is

\Delta(E_{a})=-4096a^{2}(27a^{2}+16),

which is nonzero for every
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
.

3
Birational dictionary for rational 2-cycles

3.1
From rational points on Ea to rational 2-cycles

Combining Proposition
2.1
with Proposition
2.2
gives an explicit dictionary between rational points on
E_{a}
and rational
2
-cycles of maps in the one-parameter family (
13
) with fixed nonzero

.

Theorem 3.1

(Birational dictionary)
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. There is a bijection between

(1)

rational points
(X,Y)\in E_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
with
X\neq 0
and
Y\neq 0
, and

(2)

ordered triples
(b,x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}^{3}
such that
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
and

f_{b,a}(x_{1})=x_{2},\qquad f_{b,a}(x_{2})=x_{1}.

The forward map is

b=-\frac{X}{4}-2-\frac{16a^{2}}{X^{2}},\qquad x_{1}=\frac{-4a-Y}{2X},\qquad x_{2}=\frac{-4a+Y}{2X}.

(16)

The inverse map is

(x_{1},x_{2})\longmapsto\left(X,Y\right)=\left(-\frac{4a}{x_{1}+x_{2}},\,\frac{4a(x_{1}-x_{2})}{x_{1}+x_{2}}\right).

(17)

Proof.

Let
(X,Y)\in E_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
with
X,Y\neq 0
and set

X_{0}=-\frac{X}{4},\qquad Y_{0}=\frac{aY}{4}.

By (
15
), the point
(X_{0},Y_{0})
lies on
E^{(a^{2})}(\mathbb{Q})
and still satisfies
X_{0},Y_{0}\neq 0
. Applying Proposition
2.1
with
A=a^{2}
gives a rational
2
-cycle

z_{1}=\frac{1}{2X_{0}}+\frac{Y_{0}}{2a^{2}X_{0}},\qquad z_{2}=\frac{1}{2X_{0}}-\frac{Y_{0}}{2a^{2}X_{0}}

for the map
\psi_{a,b}(z)=a^{2}z^{3}+bz+1
, where

b=X_{0}-2-\frac{a^{2}}{X_{0}^{2}}.

Multiplying by
a
and simplifying yields (
16
). Since
f_{b,a}=M\circ\psi_{a,b}\circ M^{-1}
with
M(z)=az
, the pair
(x_{1},x_{2})=(az_{1},az_{2})
is a rational
2
-cycle of
f_{b,a}
.

Conversely, suppose that
f_{b,a}
admits a rational
2
-cycle
(x_{1},x_{2})
. First note that
x_{1}+x_{2}\neq 0
: if
x_{2}=-x_{1}
, then adding the equations

x_{2}=f_{b,a}(x_{1}),\qquad x_{1}=f_{b,a}(x_{2})

would give
0=2a
, contradicting
a\neq 0
. Set

z_{1}=\frac{x_{1}}{a},\qquad z_{2}=\frac{x_{2}}{a}.

Then
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a rational
2
-cycle for
\psi_{a,b}
. Proposition
2.1
therefore gives a point
(X_{0},Y_{0})\in E^{(a^{2})}(\mathbb{Q})
with

X_{0}=\frac{1}{z_{1}+z_{2}}=\frac{a}{x_{1}+x_{2}},\qquad Y_{0}=a^{2}\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{1}+z_{2}}=a^{2}\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{x_{1}+x_{2}}.

Applying (
15
) gives (
17
). The two constructions are inverse to each other.
∎

The inverse map in (

.

4.1
The distinguished section

For

To study its order, we translate (

Theorem 4.1
.

For every

Discriminant, j-invariant, and nonsingularity

From (

5.3
Rational 2-torsion and the partial torsion analysis

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{t}(\mathbb{Q})
has a nontrivial rational point of order
2
if and only if there exists
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
such that

t=\frac{u(u^{2}+4)}{4}.

(27)

In that case the unique nontrivial rational point of order
2
is

T_{u}=\bigl(-(u^{2}+4),0\bigr),

(28)

and therefore

\mathcal{E}_{t}(\mathbb{Q})[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

If no such
u
exists, then
\mathcal{E}_{t}(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is trivial.

Proof.

A rational point on (
18
) has order
2
if and only if it has the form
(r,0)
with

r^{3}+4r^{2}+16t^{2}=0.

(29)

Equivalently,

16t^{2}=-r^{2}(r+4).

(30)

Since
t\neq 0
, we also have
r\neq 0
, and (
30
) gives
-(r+4)=(4t/r)^{2}
. Write

-(r+4)=u^{2},\qquad u\in\mathbb{Q}.

Then

r=-(u^{2}+4),

and substituting into (
30
) gives

16t^{2}=u^{2}(u^{2}+4)^{2}.

Hence

t=\pm\frac{u(u^{2}+4)}{4}.

Replacing
u
by
-u
if necessary yields (
27
).

Conversely, if (
27
) holds, then substituting
X=-(u^{2}+4)
into the right-hand side of (
18
) gives

X^{3}+4X^{2}+16t^{2}=-(u^{2}+4)^{3}+4(u^{2}+4)^{2}+u^{2}(u^{2}+4)^{2}=0,

so (
28
) is indeed a rational point of order
2
.

It remains to prove uniqueness. The polynomial

F_{t}(X):=X^{3}+4X^{2}+16t^{2}

has discriminant

\operatorname{disc}(F_{t})=-256t^{2}(16+27t^{2}),

which is negative for every
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
. Therefore the real cubic
F_{t}
has exactly one real root. Hence it has at most one rational root, and
\mathcal{E}_{t}(\mathbb{Q})
has at most one nontrivial rational
2
-torsion point. Thus the rational
2
-torsion subgroup is either trivial or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
isolates the only possible rational
2
-torsion in the family. The broader torsion problem is to determine whether any torsion beyond this possible single point of order
2
can occur.

By Mazur’s theorem
[
9
,
10
]
, the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is isomorphic either to
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
with
1\leq N\leq 10
or

, or to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}
with

. Proposition
5.1
rules out full rational
2
-torsion in the family, so only the cyclic groups remain; moreover, an element of order
6
,
9
,
10
, or
12
would produce an element of order
3
or
5
. The torsion classification for (
18
) therefore reduces to the odd prime orders
3
,
5
, and
7
, which Sections
6
–
8
exclude for every
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, and to the
2
-primary orders, which can occur at most on the locus of Proposition
5.1
and which we do not pursue further; see Corollary
8.5
.

Theorem 7.2

.

Assume for contradiction that

has a rational point

of order

for some

with a rational point

of order

.

For every
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, the elliptic curve
\mathcal{E}_{t}

Proof.

8.1
, any rational

\mathcal{E}_{t}

, the only possible

.

Therefore

∎

Appendix B
The normal form I period-2 locus

For completeness we record the rational parametrization of the period-
2
locus of normal form I,

\phi(z)=uz^{3}+vz,\qquad u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times},\ v\in\mathbb{Q},

referred to in Section
2
. This material is classical and independent of the rest of the paper; in contrast to normal form II, the period-
2
locus here is rational, so it contributes no elliptic-curve arithmetic.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
be a rational
2
-cycle of
\phi
with
x\neq y
, and set
s=x+y
,
p=xy
,
w=x-y
, so that
w^{2}=s^{2}-4p
. Adding the two cycle equations gives
u\,s(s^{2}-3p)+vs=s
, and subtracting them gives
u(s^{2}-p)+v=-1
.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
\phi(z)=uz^{3}+vz
with
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
v\in\mathbb{Q}
. A rational
2
-cycle
(x,y)
with
x\neq y
occurs in exactly one of the following two families:

(i)

(Symmetric branch)
there exists
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
with

v=-1-\frac{u\,r^{2}}{4},\qquad(x,y)=\Bigl(\frac{r}{2},-\frac{r}{2}\Bigr);

(ii)

(Generic branch)
there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{Q}
with
mn\neq 0
and
m^{2}\neq n^{2}
such that

u=\frac{4}{n^{2}-m^{2}},\qquad v=-2\,\frac{m^{2}+n^{2}}{n^{2}-m^{2}},\qquad(x,y)=\Bigl(\frac{m+n}{2},\frac{m-n}{2}\Bigr).

Conversely, every choice of parameters in
(i)
or
(ii)
yields a rational
2
-cycle of
\phi
.

Proof.

If
s=0
, then
y=-x
; writing
r=2x\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and using
u(s^{2}-p)+v=-1
with
p=-x^{2}=-r^{2}/4
gives
v=-1-ur^{2}/4
, which is (i). If
s\neq 0
, dividing the sum relation by
s
gives
u(s^{2}-3p)+v=1
; together with
u(s^{2}-p)+v=-1
this yields
p=-1/u
and
us^{2}+v=-2
. Put
m=s
and
n=w=x-y
; then
mn\neq 0
(as
s\neq 0
and
x\neq y
) and

p=\frac{m^{2}-n^{2}}{4}=-\frac{1}{u},

so
m^{2}\neq n^{2}
and
u=4/(n^{2}-m^{2})
, while
v=-2-um^{2}=-2(m^{2}+n^{2})/(n^{2}-m^{2})
. Also
x=(m+n)/2
and
y=(m-n)/2
, which is (ii). Conversely, substituting either family into
\phi(z)=uz^{3}+vz
gives
\phi(x)=y
and
\phi(y)=x
with
x\neq y
.
∎

In particular, for each fixed
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
the symmetric branch alone already yields infinitely many
v\in\mathbb{Q}
for which
\phi
admits a rational
2
-cycle, in contrast with the elliptic behaviour of normal form II.
