Wei Chen, Yong Jiao, Xingyan Quan, Lian Wu

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of quantitative weighted norm estimates for martingale square functions in both scalar-weighted and matrix-weighted settings. In particular, we introduce the martingale square functions S W S_{W} via matrix weights W W , and then use the matrix A p A_{p} condition, introduced in our previous work [ 8 ] , to characterize the L p L_{p} estimate for S W S_{W} . Our proof mainly relies on the idea of sparse dominations, which leads to the explicit information on the characteristic of the matrix weight involved. For the range 1 < p ≤ 2 1<p\leq 2 , our result is sharp in terms of the characteristic of the matrix weight. With some modification on the arguments, we can further improve the result in scalar settings by obtaining the optimal exponent of the characteristic of the weight involved for all indices 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , addressing a fundamental problem from the classical martingale theory.

1.
Introduction

Square functions and inequalities associated with them play a significantly important role in the development of probability, harmonic analysis, ergodic theory and many other areas of mathematics.
Inequalities for martingale square functions, referred to as Burkholder-Gundy inequalities, lie at the heart of these developments.
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space equipped with a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
whose union generates
\mathcal{F}
. For each
k\geq 1
, denote by
\mathbb{E}_{k}
the conditioned expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. Given a martingale
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, the associated difference sequence
df=(d_{n}f)_{n\geq 1}
is given by
d_{1}f=f_{1}
and
d_{n}f=f_{n}-f_{n-1}
for
n\geq 2
, and the square function of
f
is defined as
S(f)=(\sum_{k\geq 1}|d_{k}f|^{2})^{1/2}
. The well known inequality, developed by Burkholder and Gundy in the 1960s, asserts that for
1<p<\infty
there is a constant
c_{p}
depending only on
p
such that

(1.1)

\|S(f)\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\leq c_{p}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

For
p=1
, Burkholder
[
7
]
(see also Osękowski
[
32
]
) established a weak type
(1,1)
version of (
1.1
).
These profound results provide a probabilistic analogue of the Littlewood-Paley theory. They serve as crucial tools in many different areas of mathematics. There are numerous generalizations of (
1.1
); see for instance Pisier
[
34
]
for the extension of (
1.1
) to vector-valued martingales for which the deep connection between vector-valued martingale inequalities and geometric properties of Banach spaces was found, and Johnson-Schechtman
[
25
]
for a far reaching formulation of (
1.1
) in the context of rearrangement invariant spaces. For further details on martingale inequalities associated with square functions, see the monograph of Weisz
[
39
]
.

The theory of weights has draw much attention in recent years. It all begun with the introduction of
A_{p}
weights and the establishment of the weighted Hardy-Littlewood inequalities by Muckenhoupt
[
30
]
. Izumisawa and Kazamaki
[
23
]
extended the
A_{p}
condition to the probabilistic context. Under this condition and the extra reverse Hölder assumption on weights, they established the weighted Doob maximal inequality. The extra reverse Hölder assumption was later successfully removed by Jawerth
[
24
]
. Let us briefly recall this result. By a weight on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
we mean a positive function belonging to
L_{1}(\Omega)
. Given
1<p<\infty
,
it was shown in
[
24
]
that the Doob maximal operator is bounded on
L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)
if and only if
w\in A_{p}
, i.e., the
A_{p}
characteristic

[w]_{A_{p}}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\mathbb{E}_{n}(w)\mathbb{E}_{n}(w^{\frac{1}{1-p}})^{p-1}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

is finite. This
A_{p}
condition is useful in describing weighted norm estimates of other classical operators as well, see
[
2
,
1
,
16
,
14
]
and extensive bibliographies therein.
In
[
4
]
, Bonami and Lépingle considered the weighted version of (
1.1
). Given
1<p<\infty
and
w\in\widehat{A}_{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{S}
(c.f.
[
4
]
or
[
42
, Definitions 2.6, 5.17]
for the introduction of these two conditions), their result can be reformulated as follows:

(1.2)

\|S(f)\|_{L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)}\leq c_{p,w}\|f\|_{L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)}

where
c_{p,w}
is a constant depending only on
p
and
w
.
It is natural to ask whether the
A_{p}
condition is sufficient for the characterization of the above estimate. One may turn to the recent work
[
14
]
of Domelevo, Petermichl and Škreb for an affirmative answer.

In addition, there is a very interesting aspect of the weight theory, concerning the extraction of the optimal dependence of the constants involved on the characteristic of a weight. This topic firstly appeared in Buckley’s work
[
6
]
, where the best exponent of the characteristic of the weight involved in weighted Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequalities was determined. A similar result on weighted Doob inequalities can be found in Tanaka and Terasawa
[
35
, Corollary 4.5]
. Hytönen
[
21
]
addressed the famous
A_{2}
conjecture for general Calderón-Zygmund operators. See also
[
2
,
26
,
28
]
and references therein for similar results regarding other operators.

Our main concern of this paper is a sharp version of (
1.2
) under the
A_{p}
condition. More precisely, writing
c_{p,w}
as
c_{p}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\kappa_{p}}
in (
1.2
), we are interested in the following fundamental problem:

Given
\textsf{w}\in A_{p}
, what is the optimal choice
\kappa_{p}
such that (
1.2
) holds true ?

Surprisingly, though the study of weighted inequalities has a rich history, it seems that the above problem has never really been satisfactorily resolved! There are substantial discussion on this problem in some special contexts. For the dyadic square function defined via the Haar system, Buckley
[
6
]
provided a quantitative weighted
L_{2}
bound with
\kappa_{2}=3/2
. Later, Hukovic
[
19
]
and Hukovic-Treil-Volberg
[
18
]
improved Buckley’s result by showing that the optimal choice of
\kappa_{2}
is actually
1
rather than
3/2
. See also Wittwer
[
41
]
and Petermichl and Pott
[
33
]
for a different proof. In
[
12
]
, Cruz-Uribe, Martell and Pérez received a considerable progress by establishing (
1.2
) for dyadic square functions with
\kappa_{p}=\max\{1/2,(p-1)^{-1}\}
; moreover, they proved that this exponent is the best possible. For continuous-time martingales with continuous-path, Bañuelos and Osękowski
[
2
]
obtained (
1.2
) for
p=2
by using the so-called Bellman function method. Their result is sharp in terms of the dependence on
[w]_{A_{2}}
. But, the additional continuous-path hypothesis prevents us deducing the corresponding result for discrete-time martingales. We should also mention the paper
[
13
]
, containing the discussion of (
1.2
) for
p=2
in the special case that the filtration is atomic. Recently, Domelevo, Petermichl and Škreb
[
14
]
obtained a sharp weighted
L_{p}
estimate for the maximal operator
Y^{*}
of
Y
with respect to
X
, where
Y
and
X
are uniformly integrable càdlàg Hilbert space valued martingales and
Y
is differentially subordinate to
X
via the square bracket process. As a direct consequence of
[
14
, Corollary 1]
, one can infer (
1.2
) with
\kappa_{p}=\max\{1,(p-1)^{-1}\}
which, however, is only optimal when
1<p\leq 2
.

The present paper solves the problem discussed above in full generality. One of our principal results is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a scalar weight
w\in A_{p}
, we have

\|Sf\|_{L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}^{w}(\Omega).

The estimate is sharp in terms of the dependence on
[w]_{A_{p}}
.

Actually, we will study the above statement from a much wider matrix-weighted perspective. This is a newly emerging area, although the investigation can be tracked back to Wiener and Masani
[
40
]
, where the matrix-weighted
L_{2}
space was introduced and applied on the prediction theory for multivariate stochastic processes. In 1997, Treil and Volberg
[
36
]
generalized the
A_{2}
condition to the matrix-weighted setting and used this condition to describe the matrix-weighted
L_{2}
boundedness of Hilbert transforms. By using the language of metrics and their averagings, Volberg
[
38
]
introduced the martix
A_{p}
condition and proved that the Hilbert transform is bounded on the matrix-weighted
L_{p}
space if and only if the matrix weight belongs to the matrix
A_{p}
class. See also Nazarov and Treil
[
31
]
for the introduction of matrix
A_{p}
weights. These works pave the way towards the consideration of matrix-weighted inequalities, after which lots of papers have been devoted to this area, see for instance,
[
3
,
5
,
11
,
15
,
17
]
.

In the recent article
[
8
]
, the authors extended the concept of matrix
A_{p}
condition to the probabilistic context.

The main contribution of the paper
[
8
]
is twofold: the first is to construct reducing matrix-valued functions in the probabilistic context so that the matrix
A_{p}
condition can be properly formulated and the second is to show that under this condition the matrix-weighted Doob maximal inequalities hold true for vector-valued martingales.

Based on
[
8
]
, we consider in this paper the matrix-weighted estimates for martingale square functions. To state the result clearly, we fix some notations. Let
1\leq d<\infty
. A matrix weight
W:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is a
d\times d
self-adjoint matrix function on
\Omega
(with integrable entries) such that
W(x)
is positive definite for almost all
x\in\Omega
. Given a matrix weight
W
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the matrix-weighted square function by

S_{W}f=\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}d_{k}(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued martingales. Note that in sharp contrast with the scalar-weighted setting, one needs to define
the square functions
S_{W}
via matrix weights
W
. This phenomenon
was already uncovered by Christ and Goldberg
[
11
]
and Goldberg
[
17
]
while establishing
matrix-weighted versions of Hardy-Littlewood inequalities. Our second main result of this paper, concerning the extension of (
1.2
) to the matrix-weighted setting, reads as follows.

Theorem B
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

\|S_{W}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p-1)},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

For
1<p\leq 2
, the exponent of
[W]_{A_{p}}
equals to
1/(p-1)
which is sharp.

This result has been investigated for dyadic square functions by some authors. Hytönen, Petermichl and Volberg
[
20
]
addressed the matrix
A_{2}
conjecture for the dyadic square function. Isralowitz
[
22
]
refined the sparse domination from
[
20
]
and then utilized it to prove sharp matrix-weighted type inequalities for dyadic square functions in the range
1<p\leq 2
. Recently, Treil
[
37
]
generalized the result of Hytönen, Petermichl and Volberg to the non-homogeneous situation.
Note that, for
1<p\leq 2
, the exponent of the characteristic of the weights in Theorem
B
is the same with that of the dyadic setting, and thus is sharp. Also, note that for
2<p<\infty
, the exponent is most likely not optimal and we leave it as a problem to the interested reader

Our approach to Theorem
B
depends on the idea of sparse domination. As a powerful tool, sparse domination nowadays has been widely used in analytic settings, especially in the study of weighted norm estimates. It is Lacey
[
27
]
who firstly applied this technique to investigate probabilistic object, the discrete-time martingale transform. Tanaka and Terasawa
[
35
]
introduced the notion of
principal sets for martingales and applied it to provide a Sawyer type characterization for a generalized Doob maximal
operator. The first two authors
[
9
,
10
]
found
the conditional sparsity of principal sets and enhanced the quantitative weighted estimate in
[
35
]
.
Based on this property, the authors
[
8
]
developed the theory of matrix-weighted martingales without recourse to the reverse Hölder inequality of matrix weights or any regularity conditions on the underlying filtration. In the present paper, we further advance the conditional sparsity of principal sets and the technique of sparse domination to derive quantitative (or sharp) weighted estimates for martingale square functions, marking a substantial improvement on previous results in the literature.

As mentioned above, Hytönen, Petermichl and Volberg
[
20
]
and Isralowitz
[
22
]
only proved Theorem
B
for martingales adapted to the dyadic filtration on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and it is nontrivial to generalize their results to even the case of dyadic lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The extension to martingales associated with a general filtration on an abstract probability space requires completely new ideas. In particular, a new construction of a dominating sparse operator, tailoring to general martingales, was needed. Moreover, the argument in
[
20
,
22
]
relies on the reverse Hölder inequality for
A_{\infty}
weights, which is unapplicable to our situation here.

The proof of Theorem
A
follows the same pattern with that of Theorem
B
. Due to this, we will just outline the proof of Theorem
A
by pointing out the main difference.

The paper is organized as follows. The next section contains some basic properties of matrix
A_{p}
weights and some fundamental results.
Section
3
is where we present the detailed construction of principal sets and display some important properties that will be used in the proof of our main results. In Section
4
, we introduce the sparse operators
\mathcal{T}_{W,r}
and establish an
L_{p}
estimate for
\mathcal{T}_{W,2}
.
The domination between
S_{W}
and
\mathcal{T}_{W,2}
, and also the proof of Theorem
B
will be presented in Section
5
. In the last section, we sketch the proof of Theorem
A
.

Throughout, we use
d\in\mathbb{N}
to denote the dimension and the letter
c
denotes a constant, which (unless otherwise specified) probably depends on the dimension, but it may change from occurrence to occurrence. We write
A\gtrsim_{\delta}B
if there is a constant
c_{\delta}>0
depending only on
\delta
such that
A\geq c_{\delta}B
and we use the notation
A\approx_{\delta}B
when
A\lesssim_{\delta}B
and
A\gtrsim_{\delta}B
hold simultaneously.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic properties of martingale matrix
A_{p}
weights obtained in our precious work
[
8
]
and some facts on vector-valued martingales.

2.1.
Matrix
A_{p}
weights

Assume that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space. For
1\leq d<\infty
and
1\leq p<\infty
, the unweighted vector-valued Lebesgue space
L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
consists of all measurable functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that

\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\|f(t)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{p}d\mathbb{P}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

A matrix weight
W:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{M}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is a
d\times d
self-adjoint matrix-valued function on
\Omega
(with integrable entries) such that
W(x)
is positive definite for almost all
x\in\Omega
. Given a matrix weight
W
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we define the corresponding weighted Lebesgue space
L_{p}(W)
to be the space of all measurable functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that the norm

\|f\|_{L_{p}(W)}:=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}(t)f(t)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{p}d\mathbb{P}\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

Obviously, if
W\equiv\mbox{Id}_{\mathbb{M}_{d}}
, then
L_{p}(W)
goes back to
L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
; and if
d=1
, then
L_{p}(W)
reduces to the usual weighted Lebesgue spaces
L_{p}^{w}(\Omega)
.

Let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an increasing sequence of
\sigma
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{F}=\sigma(\cup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{F}_{n})
and let
(\mathbb{E}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be the associated conditional expectations defined for scalar-valued functions. Given
n\geq 1
, we may apply the
n
-th conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{n}
to
d
-dimensional vector-valued functions by allowing it to act separately on each coordinate function. More precisely, let
f=(f^{1},f^{2},\ldots,f^{d})
be a vector-valued function defined on
\Omega
. The
n
-th conditional expectation of
f
is given by

\mathbb{E}_{n}f=(\mathbb{E}_{n}(f^{1}),\mathbb{E}_{n}(f^{2})\ldots,\mathbb{E}_{n}(f^{d})).

For any vector-valued function
h\in L_{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, it is clear that

\|\mathbb{E}_{n}h\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\leq\mathbb{E}_{n}\|h\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}},\quad n\geq 1.

This elementary fact of conditional expectations will be frequently used in the sequel. Similarly, for any matrix weight
W=(w^{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
, the
n
-th conditional expectation of
W
is given by

\mathbb{E}_{n}W=(\mathbb{E}_{n}(w^{i,j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}.

As we know, for scalar weights
w
, two objects are essential in defining
A_{p}
conditions which are
[\mathbb{E}_{n}w]^{1/p}
and
[\mathbb{E}_{n}(w^{-p^{\prime}/p})]^{1/{p^{\prime}}}
. Here and what follows,
p^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate number of
p
. Given a matrix weight
W
, we recall below the reducing matrix-valued functions
\widetilde{W}_{n}
,
\widehat{W}_{n}
and some relevant properties proved in
[
8
, Lemma 2.4, Lemma 2.5]
. Notably, these functions play the roles of
[\mathbb{E}_{n}w]^{1/p}
and
[\mathbb{E}_{n}(w^{-p^{\prime}/p})]^{1/{p^{\prime}}}
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
W
be a matrix weight. There exists a sequence of matrix-valued functions
(\widetilde{W}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that for every
n
, the entries of
\widetilde{W}_{n}
are adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and

(2.1)

\|\widetilde{W}_{n}e\|^{p}_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\thickapprox_{d}\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}e\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{p}\big),\quad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widetilde{W}_{n}^{-1}\|^{p}\big)=\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|\widetilde{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}\|^{p}\big)\lesssim_{p,d}1.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
W
be a matrix weight. There exist a sequence of matrix-valued functions
\widehat{W}_{n}
such that for every
n
, the entries of
\widehat{W}_{n}
are adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and

(2.3)

\|\widehat{W}_{n}e\|^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\thickapprox_{d}\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}e\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{p^{\prime}}),\quad\forall e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}.

Moreover, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(2.4)

\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}\|^{p^{\prime}}\big)=\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|^{p^{\prime}})\lesssim_{p,d}1.

We now recall the definition of matrix
A_{p}
condition introduced in
[
8
, Definition 2.6]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
1<p<\infty
and a matrix weight
W
, we say that
W
satisfies the
A_{p}
condition (denoted by
W\in A_{p}
) if

(2.5)

[W]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\widetilde{W}_{n}\widehat{W}_{n}\|^{p}<\infty.

Similarly, we say that
W
satisfies the
A_{1}
condition (denoted by
W\in A_{1}
) if

(2.6)

[W]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|\widetilde{W}_{n}W^{-1}\|<\infty.

The number
[W]_{A_{p}}
is called the
A_{p}
characteristic of
W
.

The following result is taken from
[
8
, Corollary 2.11]
, which provides equivalent descriptions of the characteristic of the matrix
A_{p}
weight.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

(2.7)

[W]_{A_{p}}\approx_{p,d}\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|\widehat{W}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}\|^{p}\big)\approx_{p,d}\sup_{n\geq 1}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big(\|\widetilde{W}_{n}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|^{p^{\prime}})\Big)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}.

Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we call
V=W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
the dual weight of
W
. It is easy to see that the reducing matrix operators associated with
V
can be taken as
\widehat{V}_{n}=\widetilde{W}_{n}
,
\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widehat{W}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we have the following basic property of dual weights.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, the dual weight
V=W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\in A_{p^{\prime}}
and

[V]_{A_{p^{\prime}}}\approx_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{p^{\prime}-1}.

2.2.
Vector-valued martingales

A sequence
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
is called a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
if
f_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable and

\mathbb{E}_{n}(f_{n+1})=f_{n},\quad n\geq 1.

For a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued martingale
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we define its martingale difference by setting
d_{1}f=f_{1}
and

d_{n}f=f_{n}-f_{n-1},\quad n\geq 2.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, if
f_{n}\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
for all
n\geq 1
, then
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is called a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued
L_{p}
martingale. In this case, if we further have

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|f_{n}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})}<\infty

then
f
is called a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued
L_{p}
-bounded martingale. As is well known, for
1<p<\infty
, the martingale
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is
L_{p}
-bounded if and only if there exists a function
f_{\infty}\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that
f_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{n}(f_{\infty})
. Hence, in this case, we may identify a martingale
f
with its final value
f_{\infty}
. We refer to
[
29
,
34
]
for more information on vector-valued martingales.

Similarly, for
1<p<\infty
and a scalar weight
w
, one could define
L_{p}^{w}
-bounded martingales. The above identification remain valid for
L_{p}^{w}
-bounded martingales; namely, for
1<p<\infty
, the martingale
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is
L_{p}^{w}
-bounded if and only if there exists a function
f_{\infty}\in L_{p}^{w}
such that
f_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{n}(f_{\infty})
.

Given
1<p<\infty
and a matrix weight
W
, we now recall a vector-valued maximal operator introduced in
[
8
, Page 22]
:

{M^{\prime}_{W}f=\sup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}},}

where
f=(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued
L_{p}
-bounded martingale. The following estimate for
M^{\prime}_{W}
will play a crucial role in the proof of our main result; see

[
8
, Proposition 4.8]
for the proof.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

\|M^{\prime}_{W}f\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p(p-1)}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})},\quad\forall\,f\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

3.
Principal sets and related properties

In this section, we provide the detailed construction of principal sets that will be used in the proof of our main result. We start our consideration with a special family of functions. Let
1<p<\infty
and let
W
be a matrix weight. Given a
\mathbb{C}^{d}
-valued function
f
, for
m>n
, we define

\gamma_{1}(n,m):=\frac{\left(\sum_{i=n+1}^{m}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}d_{i}(W^{-1/p}f)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}},

\gamma_{2}(n,m):=\frac{\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{m}(W^{-1/p}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}};

and for
m\leq n
, we put
\gamma_{i}(n,m)=0
for
i=1,

2
. Now set

\gamma(n,m):=\max\left\{\gamma_{1}(n,m),\,\gamma_{2}(n,m)\right\}.

We collect below two useful properties of
\{\gamma(n,m)\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The functions
\{\gamma(n,m)\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}

satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

For every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma(n,m)
is adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{n\vee m}
.

(ii)

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C_{\gamma}
such that for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
,

\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)>C_{\gamma}\}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)\leq C_{\gamma}\}\Big).

Proof.

Indeed, (i) can be easily verified. Hence, we only check (ii) here. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
A\in\mathcal{F}_{n}
and for arbitrary
\delta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\big(\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>\delta\}\cup\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{2}(n,m)>\delta\}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{2}(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)

Let us first deal with the term involving
\gamma_{1}
. Set

\begin{cases}d_{m}F=0,&\mbox{if}\,\,m\leq n,\\
d_{m}F=\frac{\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}d_{m}(W^{-1/p}f)\chi_{A}}{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}},&\mbox{if}\,\,m>n.\end{cases}

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big(\{S(F)>\delta\}\big),

where
S(F)=(\sum_{m}\|d_{m}F\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{2})^{1/2}
. Applying the vector-valued analogue of weak-type estimate of square functions (cf.
[
32
]
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{e}}{\delta}\int_{\Omega}\|F\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{e}}{\delta}\int_{A}\frac{\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}(W^{-1/p}f-\mathbb{E}_{n}(W^{-1/p}f))\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{e}}{\delta}\int_{A}\frac{\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}}d\mathbb{P}=\frac{\sqrt{e}}{\delta}\mathbb{P}(A).

Hence, we may choose
C_{\gamma,1}
such that

\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>C_{\gamma,1}\}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{P}(A).

We turn to deal with
\gamma_{2}
. Set

\tau=\inf\{m>n:\gamma_{2}(n,m)>\delta\}.

It follows from the Chebyshev inequality that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{2}(n,m)>\delta\}\Big)\\
&\leq\sum_{m>n}\int_{A\cap\{\tau=m\}}\frac{\gamma_{2}(n,m)}{\delta}d\mathbb{P}\\
&=\sum_{m>n}\int_{A\cap\{\tau=m\}}\frac{\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}\mathbb{E}_{m}(W^{-1/p}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}}}{{(\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}})\delta}d\mathbb{P}\\
&\leq\sum_{m>n}\int_{A\cap\{\tau=m\}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{m}(\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}})\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}}}{{(\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}})\delta}d\mathbb{P}\\
&=\int_{A}\frac{(\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}})\chi_{\{\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}>0\}}}{{(\mathbb{E}_{n}\|\widehat{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{-1/p}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}})\delta}d\mathbb{P}\leq\delta^{-1}\mathbb{P}(A).\end{split}

Thus, we are able to choose
C_{\gamma,2}
depending only on
d
,
p
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{2}(n,m)>C_{\gamma,2}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{P}(A).

Setting
C_{\gamma}=\max\{C_{\gamma,1},C_{\gamma,2}\}
and putting all the above together, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)>C_{\gamma}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{1}(n,m)>C_{\gamma,1}\}\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma_{2}(n,m)>C_{\gamma,2}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(A),

which implies that

\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)>C_{\gamma}\}\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(A\cap\{\sup_{m>n}\gamma(n,m)\leq C_{\gamma}\}\Big).

This verifies that
\{\gamma(n,m)\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the condition (ii).
∎

We now introduce the principal sets associated with
\{\gamma(n,m)\}_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
. For any fixed
n,
define

\tau_{1}:=\inf\left\{\ell>n:\gamma(n,\ell)>0\right\}.

For every
j>n
, denote by

P_{1,j}:=\{\tau_{1}=j\}

and

\kappa_{1}\left(P_{1,j}\right):=n,\quad\kappa_{2}\left(P_{1,j}\right):=j.

We call
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\left\{P_{1,j}:\penalty\ j>n\right\}
the first generation of principal sets. Without risk of confusion, we will write
P_{1}
in place of
P_{1,j}
in the sequel.

Let us get the second generation. For any
P_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
, define

\tau_{P_{1}}:=\inf\left\{{\ell>\kappa_{2}\left(P_{1}\right)}:\gamma(\kappa_{2}(P_{1}),\ell)\chi_{P_{1}}>C_{\gamma}\right\}.

For every
j>\kappa_{2}(P_{1})
, denote by

P_{2,j}:=\{\tau_{P_{1}}=j\}

and

\kappa_{1}\left(P_{2,j}\right):=\kappa_{2}\left(P_{1}\right),\quad\kappa_{2}\left(P_{2,j}\right):=j.

We call
\mathcal{P}(P_{1}):=\left\{P_{2,j}:\penalty\ j>\kappa_{2}(P_{1})\right\}
the collection of the principal sets generated by
P_{1}.
Moreover, letting
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\bigcup_{P_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\mathcal{P}(P_{1}),
we call
\mathcal{P}_{2}
the second generation of principal sets.

The next generations can be defined by induction. For any fixed
P_{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{m},
define

\displaystyle\tau_{P_{m}}:=\inf\{

\displaystyle l>\kappa_{2}\left(P_{m}\right):\gamma(\kappa_{2}\left(P_{m}\right),\ell)\chi_{P_{m}}>C_{\gamma}\}.

For every
j>\kappa_{2}(P_{m})
, we denote

P_{m+1,j}:=\{\tau_{P_{m}}=j\}

and

\kappa_{1}\left(P_{m+1,j}\right):=\kappa_{2}\left(P_{m}\right),\quad\kappa_{2}\left(P_{m+1,j}\right):=j.

Let
\mathcal{P}(P_{m})=\left\{P_{m+1,j}:\penalty\ j>\kappa_{2}(P_{m})\right\}
and we call it the collection of the principal sets generated by
P_{m}.
Moreover, letting
\mathcal{P}_{m+1}=\bigcup_{P_{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{m}}\mathcal{P}(P_{m}),

we call
\mathcal{P}_{m+1}
the
(m+1)\hbox{-th}
generation of principal sets.

Define the collection of principal sets
\mathcal{P}
by

\mathcal{P}:=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}.

Then, by
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
, we find the following properties of principal sets.

Lemma 3.2
.

For the principal sets introduced above, we have

(a)

The sets
E(P):=P\cap\{\tau_{P}=\infty\}
, where
P\in\mathcal{P}
, are disjoint.

(b)

For every
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
P\in\mathcal{F}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}
.

(c)

For every
P\in\bigcup_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}
,

\Big(\sum_{\kappa_{1}(P)<i\leq\kappa_{2}(P)-1}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}^{-1}d_{i}(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,P

and

\sup_{\kappa_{1}(P)<i\leq\kappa_{2}(P)-1}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{i}(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\leq C_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{1}(P)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,P.

(d)

For every
P\in\bigcup_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}
,

\Big(\sum_{i>\kappa_{2}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}^{-1}d_{i}(W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\quad\mbox{on}\quad E(P)

and

\sup_{i>\kappa_{2}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{i}(W^{-1/p}f)\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\leq C_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\quad\mbox{on}\quad E(P).

(e)

For every
P\in\bigcup_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(P)\leq 2\mathbb{P}(E(P))

and

\chi_{P}\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(\chi_{E(P)}).

(f)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\bigcup_{P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{N}}P)=0.

4.
An
L_{p}
estimate for the sparse operator
\mathcal{T}_{W,2}

The purpose of this section is to introduce the sparse operators
\mathcal{T}_{W,r}
and establish an
L_{p}
estimate for
\mathcal{T}_{W,2}
. Let
W
be a matrix weight. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the principal sets constructed in the preceding section. For
r\geq 1
, we introduce the sparse operators
\mathcal{T}_{W,r}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{W,r}f:=\Big(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\|^{r}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{r}\chi_{P}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

The main result of this section reads as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,2}f\right\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p(p-1)},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

In preparation for Proposition
4.1
, we need several lemmas. The first lemma shows that the operator
\mathcal{T}_{W,p}
is bounded on
L_{p}
for
1<p<\infty
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. If
W\in A_{p}
, then

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,p}f\right\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

Proof.

By Property (
b
) in Lemma
3.2
, we have
P\in\mathcal{F}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}
for every
P\in\mathcal{P}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,p}f\right\|_{L_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\big(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|^{p}\big)\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\chi_{P}d\mathbb{P}.

Since
W\in A_{p}
, it follows from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,p}f\right\|_{L_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\chi_{P}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=[W]_{A_{p}}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\chi_{P}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\quad+[W]_{A_{p}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\chi_{P}d\mathbb{P}=:\mbox{I}+\mbox{II}.

Note that principal sets from the same generation are disjoint. Thus,

\displaystyle\mbox{II}\leq[W]_{A_{p}}\|M_{W}^{\prime}f\|_{L_{p}}^{p}.

One the other hand, by Property (
e
) in Lemma
3.2
, we get

I

\displaystyle\lesssim[W]_{A_{p}}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(\chi_{E(P)})d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=[W]_{A_{p}}\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)^{p}\chi_{E(P)}d\mathbb{P}.

Hence, it follows from Property (
a
) in Lemma
3.2
that

I

\displaystyle\lesssim[W]_{A_{p}}\|M_{W}^{\prime}f\|_{L_{p}}^{p}

Putting the above estimates together and then applying Proposition
2.6
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,p}f\right\|_{L_{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}\|M_{W}^{\prime}f\|_{L_{p}}^{p}\leq[W]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})}^{p},

which is the desired result.
∎

The lemma below could be viewed as one type of weighted maximal inequality.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

(4.1)

\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left[\mathbb{E}_{n}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{n}\big\|g\right)\right]^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{p^{\prime}}\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}},\quad\forall\,0\leq g\in L_{p^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Let
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{d})
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}.
Clearly, for
0\leq g\in L_{p^{\prime}}
, we have

\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{n}\big\|g=\big\|\widehat{W}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}\big\|g\leq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big\|\widehat{W}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}.

Hence, it follows from the
A_{p}
condition that

(4.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{n}\big\|g\right)\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big\|\widehat{W}_{n}\widetilde{W}_{n}\widetilde{W}_{n}^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{p}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}\\
&\leq[W]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big\|\widetilde{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}.\end{split}

Now, denote by
V
the dual weight of
W
(i.e.,
V=W^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
). By Lemma
2.5
,
V\in A_{p^{\prime}}
and
[V]_{A_{p^{\prime}}}\approx_{d,p}[W]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
. Moreover,

\widehat{V}_{n}=\widetilde{W}_{n},\quad\quad\widetilde{V}_{n}=\widehat{W}_{n}.

Therefore, for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots d\}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big\|\widetilde{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big\|\widehat{V}^{-1}_{n}V^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\|M^{\prime}_{V}(ge_{i})\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}.

Applying Proposition
2.6
(for
p^{\prime}
,
V

and
ge_{i}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sup_{n}\left(\mathbb{E}_{n}\big\|\widetilde{W}^{-1}_{n}W^{\frac{1}{p}}ge_{i}\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle{\lesssim_{p,d}}[V]_{A_{p^{\prime}}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1}}\|ge_{i}\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})}^{p^{\prime}}=[W]_{A_{p}}\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}^{p^{\prime}}.

Substituting this into (
4.2
), we obtain the desired estimate (
4.1
).
∎

By Lemma
4.3
and duality, one can establish the following
L_{p}
estimate for
\mathcal{T}_{W,1}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a matrix weight
W\in A_{p}
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,1}f\right\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim_{p,d}[W]_{A_{p}}^{1+\frac{1}{p(p-1)}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}).

Proof.

By duality and Property (
b
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,1}f\right\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)\chi_{P}gd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\Big(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|g\Big)\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\chi_{P}\Big)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big[\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|g\right)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{P}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|g\right)d\mathbb{P}\Big].

According to Property (
e
),
\chi_{P}\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(\chi_{E(P)})
for every
P\in\bigcup_{m=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{m}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,1}f\right\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big[\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|g\right)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\Big(\big\|\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}^{-1}W^{-\frac{1}{p}}f\big\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}\left(\big\|W^{\frac{1}{p}}\widehat{W}_{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}\big\|g\right)d\mathbb{P}\Big].

In view of Property (
a
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{W,1}f\right\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\

We are now ready to show Proposition

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

The proof shall be divided into two cases:

. The case

is rather easy. Indeed, since

, it follows that

Thus, by Lemma

The case

\frac{1}{2}=(1-\theta)+\frac{\theta}{p}

Using Hölder’s inequality again, we get

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\|}T_{W,2}f\|_{L_{p}}

4.4

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{W,2}f\|_{L_{p}}

6.
Proof of Theorem
A

This section devotes to the sharp weighted norm estimate for martingale square functions in the scalar setting. With some proper modification on the previous arguments, we show that in the scalar setting the exponent of the weight in Theorem
B
can be improved to
\max\{1/2,1/(p-1)\}
which is the best possible. Surprisingly, it seems that this result previously has been only known for dyadic square functions (
[
12
,
19
,
18
,
33
,
41
]
), continuous-time martingales with continuous
path (
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
), and discrete-time martingales associated with atomic filtration (
[
13
, Theorem 3.6]
).

Suppose that
w
is a classical weight on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Now the reducing operators
\widetilde{w}_{n}
,
\widehat{w}_{n}
can be taken as

\widetilde{w}_{n}=(\mathbb{E}_{n}w)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\widehat{w}_{n}=\big(\mathbb{E}_{n}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

For
1<p<\infty
, we say that
w
satisfies the
A_{p}
condition (denoted by
w\in A_{p}
) if

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{n}\|\widetilde{w}_{n}\widehat{w}_{n}\|_{\infty}^{p}
is finite. Denote by
\mathbb{E}_{n}^{w}
the conditional expectation, with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and the measure induced by
w
. Then we have the following simple fact:

(6.1)

\mathbb{E}_{n}^{w}(f)=\mathbb{E}_{n}(wf)(\mathbb{E}_{n}(w))^{-1},\quad\forall f\in L_{1}^{w}(\Omega).

For
r\geq 1
, we modify the sparse operators as follows:

\mathcal{T}_{w,r}f=\Big(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}w^{\frac{r}{p}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\chi_{P}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
1<r<p<\infty
. Given a scalar weight
w\in A_{p}
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{w,r}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,d}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
s=p/r
. By duality and Property (
b
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{w,r}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{r}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}\leq 1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}w^{\frac{r}{p}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\chi_{P}gd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\chi_{P}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\Big[\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)d\mathbb{P}\Big],

where the last estimate is due to Property (
e
),
\chi_{P}\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(\chi_{E(P)})
for every
P\in\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
Then by Hölder’s inequality, we find

(6.2)

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{w,r}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{r}\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\footnotesize{\mbox{ is positive}}\\
\|g\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}{\rm I}\cdot{\rm II}\cdot{\rm III},

where

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\Big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{P})\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{-r}\Big\|_{L_{s}(\Omega)}^{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\Big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{r}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{-r}\Big\|_{L_{s}(\Omega)}^{s}\Big)^{1/s},

\displaystyle{\rm II}

\displaystyle:=\Big(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{P})\big)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)^{-1}\Big\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\Big\|\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)^{-1}\Big\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{s^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/s^{\prime}},

\displaystyle{\rm III}

\displaystyle:=\max\bigg\{\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{P})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{P})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)\chi_{P}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\bigg\}

Let us first deal with
\rm I
. Note that

\displaystyle{\rm I}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{-p}w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}d\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|w^{-\frac{1}{p}}f|)\big)^{p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{-p}w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}d\mathbb{P}

Denote by
v
the dual weight of
w
(i.e.,
v=w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}
) and set
w^{-1/p}f=v\widetilde{f}
. Then by (
6.1
) and the unweighted Doob inequality, we get

\displaystyle{\rm I}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|v\widetilde{f}|)\big)^{p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(v)\big)^{-p}vd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(|v\widetilde{f}|)\big)^{p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(v)\big)^{-p}vd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\big(\sup_{n}|\mathbb{E}_{n}^{v}(\widetilde{f})|\big)^{p}vd\mathbb{P}+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\big(\sup_{n}|\mathbb{E}_{n}^{v}(\widetilde{f})|\big)^{p}vd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\big(\sup_{n}|\mathbb{E}_{n}^{v}(\widetilde{f})|\big)^{p}vd\mathbb{P}\lesssim_{p}\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{f}|^{p}vd\mathbb{P}=\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

Similarly, set
w\widetilde{g}=w^{r/p}g
. Using again (
6.1
) and the unweighted Doob inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm II}^{s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\int_{P}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)\big)^{s^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)^{-s^{\prime}}wd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\int_{E(P)}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{\frac{r}{p}}g)\big)^{s^{\prime}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)^{-s^{\prime}}wd\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\big(\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{n}^{w}(\widetilde{g})\big)^{s^{\prime}}wd\mathbb{P}\lesssim_{s}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{g}^{s^{\prime}}wd\mathbb{P}=\|g\|_{L_{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{s^{\prime}}.

It remains to handle
\rm III
. For convenience, let us denote

\displaystyle{\rm III}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{1}}\big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{P})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{P})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)\chi_{P}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle{\rm III}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}.

By the
A_{p}
condition, we have

\displaystyle{\rm III}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\big\|\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big)\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\|\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\big\|\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big]^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)\big]^{\frac{1}{s}}\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\big\|\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\big]^{\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{1-p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\big]^{\frac{1}{s(p-1)}}\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Note that

\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\geq 1

and

\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{1-p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\geq 1.

Therefore,

(6.3)

\begin{split}{\rm III}_{2}&\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{s}}\sup_{P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}}\big\|\big[\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\\
&\qquad\cdot\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{1-p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\big]^{{}^{\max\{\frac{r}{p(p-1)},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\}}}\chi_{E(P)}\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

For every
P\in\bigcup_{i=2}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{i}
, by Property (
e
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{E(P)}\leq\chi_{P}\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}}}(\chi_{E(P)})\leq\big(\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}}}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}\left(P\right)}}}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

which implies

\chi_{E(P)}\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}(P)}}}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big(\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}(P)}}}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{p-1}.

Hence,

\begin{split}&\big(\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}(P)}}}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{1-p}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\big(\mathbb{E}_{{{\kappa_{2}(P)}}}(w\chi_{E(P)})\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\chi_{E(P)}\\
&\quad\lesssim\big(\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}})\big)^{p-1}\mathbb{E}_{\kappa_{2}(P)}(w)\leq[w]_{A_{p}}.\end{split}

Plugging this estimate into (
6.3
), we get

\displaystyle{\rm III}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{s}}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{r}{p(p-1)},\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\}}=[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{r}{p-1},1\}}.

Similarly, one can show that

{\rm III}_{1}\leq[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{r}{p-1},1\}}.

Combining the above estimates of
\rm I,II,III
with (
6.2
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{T}_{w,r}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}.

The proof is complete.
∎

It is easy to see that Lemma
4.2
still holds for
\mathcal{T}_{w,p}
. As a consequence of this result and Lemma
6.1
, the assertion in Proposition
4.1
can be enhanced as follows.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Given a scalar
weight
w\in A_{p}
, we have

(6.4)

\left\|\mathcal{T}_{w,2}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,d}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Proof.

Clearly, for
2<p<\infty
, (
6.4
) is just a special case of Lemma
6.1
. As for
1<p\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{w,2}f\leq\mathcal{T}_{w,p}f.

The estimate (
6.4
) now follows from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A
.

Note that Proposition
5.1
remains valid for
S_{w}
and
\mathcal{T}_{w,2}
. That is,

S_{w}f\lesssim_{p}\mathcal{T}_{w,2}f,\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

This domination together with Corollary
6.2
gives

\|S_{w}f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\max\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1}\}}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\Omega)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\Omega).

Replacing

by

, we obtain the desired result.
