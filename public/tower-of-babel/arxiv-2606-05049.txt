Generalized Heisenberg algebra from o ⁡ ( 2 , 4 ) o(2,4) Yang model Dedicated to the memory of Professor Jerzy Lukierski
Tea Martinić Bilać, Stjepan Meljanac, Salvatore Mignemi

Abstract
It is well known that the algebra o ⁡ ( 2 , 4 ) o(2,4) generates the conformal group, but it can also be used to define some variants of the Yang model of noncommutative geometry on a curved spacetime. Starting from these examples, we construct a new physical model based on o ⁡ ( 2 , 4 ) o(2,4) , that can be interpreted as a generalization of the Heisenberg algebra on phase space, with flat positions and momenta, but nontrivial commutation relations between positions and momenta and with the Planck constant promoted to an operator.



1
Introduction

A given group and its related Lie algebra are often used to describe the symmetries of different physical models. For example, the hydrogen atom enjoys the same SO
(4)
symmetry as a
3
-sphere. Another example is given by the SO
(2,4)
group. It is well known that the
o(2,4)
algebra generates the conformal group. However also the Yang model of noncommutative geometry on a curved background is based on the same algebra, for some values of its parameters. Clearly the physical interpretation of the two models is different, but one can use known representations of one of the algebras to get new representations of the other.

We recall that the conformal algebra is an extension of the Poincaré algebra that describes the symmetries of spacetime under dilatations and conformal transformations and is the spacetime symmetry associated to massless particles
[
1
,
2
]
. The Yang model is instead a quantum phase space with noncommutative positions and momenta
[
3
]
. The noncommutativity of position operators can be associated to a noncommutative geometry and then to a curved momentum space. Analogously, noncommutativity of momenta is usually associated to curved spacetimes.

The Yang model depends on two parameters,
\alpha
and
\beta
proportional to
1/R^{2}
and to
1/M^{2}
, respectively, where
M
is a mass parameter of the order of Planck mass and
R
is a length parameter, usually related to the cosmological constant. When
\alpha
and
\beta
have opposite sign, the Yang algebra is isomorphic to the
o(2,4)
algebra, which describes either a Snyder space on an anti-de Sitter background or an anti-Snyder space on a de Sitter background. In recent years several aspects of the theory and some generalizations have been investigated
[
4
]
-
[
15
]
.

In this letter, we give another interpretation of
o(2,4)
. Starting from the Yang
o(2,4)
model, we construct two generalizations of the Heisenberg algebra that depend on one parameter
1/MR
, and whose commutation relations are identical to those of the conformal algebra for
MR=1
. The Planck constant of the Heisenberg algebra is generalized to an operator that corresponds to the dilatation generator in the conformal algebra. The physical interpretations of the generalized Heisenberg algebra, the Yang model and the conformal algebra are different and relations between them are presented.

2
Generalized Heisenberg algebras

Let us start with the Yang algebra isomorphic to
o(2,4)

[
3
,
7
]

\left[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{x}_{\nu}\right]=\frac{i\epsilon}{M^{2}}M_{\mu\nu},\quad\left[\hat{p}_{\mu},\hat{p}_{\nu}\right]=\frac{-i\epsilon}{R^{2}}M_{\mu\nu},

(1)

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\hat{x}_{\lambda}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\lambda}\hat{x}_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\lambda}\hat{x}_{\mu}\right),\quad\left[M_{\mu\nu},\hat{p}_{\lambda}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\lambda}\hat{p}_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\lambda}\hat{p}_{\mu}\right),

(2)

\left[\hat{x}_{\mu},\hat{p}_{\nu}\right]=i\eta_{\mu\nu}\hat{h},\quad\left[\hat{h},\hat{x}_{\mu}\right]=\frac{i\epsilon}{M^{2}}\hat{p}_{\mu},\quad\left[\hat{h},\hat{p}_{\mu}\right]=-\frac{i\epsilon}{R^{2}}\hat{x}_{\mu},

(3)

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\hat{h}\right]=0,

(4)

\left[M_{\mu\nu},M_{\rho\sigma}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\rho}M_{\nu\sigma}-\eta_{\mu\sigma}M_{\nu\rho}-\eta_{\nu\rho}M_{\mu\sigma}+\eta_{\nu\sigma}M_{\mu\rho}\right),

(5)

where
\epsilon^{2}=1
and all generators are Hermitian. The operators
\hat{x}_{\mu}
and
\hat{p}_{\mu}
are interpreted as phase space coordinates, while the
M_{\mu\nu}
generate the Lorentz algebra
o(1,3)
.

We consider a special case of new generators linear in
\hat{x}_{\mu},\;\hat{p}_{\mu}\;
and
M_{\mu\nu}
,

\tilde{X}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{x}_{\mu}+\frac{R}{M}\hat{p}_{\mu}\right),\quad\tilde{P}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\hat{p}_{\mu}-\frac{M}{R}\hat{x}_{\mu}\right),

(6)

with inverse transformations given by

\hat{x}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\tilde{X}_{\mu}-\frac{R}{M}\tilde{P}_{\mu}\right),\quad\hat{p}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\tilde{P}_{\mu}+\frac{M}{R}\tilde{X}_{\mu}\right).

(7)

The generators
\tilde{X}_{\mu},\;\tilde{P}_{\mu},\;M_{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{H}
generate a new class of Lie algebras isomorphic to the initial Yang algebra. These algebras are defined by

\left[\tilde{X}_{\mu},\tilde{X}_{\nu}\right]=\left[\tilde{P}_{\mu},\tilde{P}_{\nu}\right]=0,

(8)

\left[\tilde{X}_{\mu},\tilde{P}_{\nu}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}\tilde{H}-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}M_{\mu\nu}\right),

(9)

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\tilde{X}_{\lambda}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\lambda}\tilde{X}_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\lambda}\tilde{X}_{\mu}\right),

(10)

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\tilde{P}_{\lambda}\right]=i\left(\eta_{\mu\lambda}\tilde{P}_{\nu}-\eta_{\nu\lambda}\tilde{P}_{\mu}\right),

(11)

where
\tilde{H}=\hat{h}
and

\left[M_{\mu\nu},\tilde{H}\right]=0,\quad\left[\tilde{H},\tilde{X}_{\mu}\right]=\frac{i\epsilon}{MR}\tilde{X}_{\mu},\quad\left[\tilde{H},\tilde{P}_{\mu}\right]=\frac{-i\epsilon}{MR}\tilde{P}_{\mu}.

(12)

We interpret
\tilde{X}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{P}_{\mu}
as the coordinates of a new flat phase space, with
\tilde{H}
a operatorial generalization of the Planck constant,
and we call this algebra generalized Heisenberg algebra. In fact, it can be considered as a deformation of the Heisenberg-Lorentz algebra by a parameter
{1\over MR}
, which reduces to the standard algebra when
{1\over MR}\to 0
.
For
\epsilon=-1
and
MR=1
the commutation relations are identical to those of the conformal algebra, reported in sect. 4.

Inspired by the standard realization of the conformal algebra
[
1
]
, we construct new Hermitian realizations of the generators
\tilde{X}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{P}_{\nu}

in terms of canonical phase space operators
x_{\mu}
and
p_{\nu}
that satisfy the Heisenberg algebra

\left[x_{\mu},x_{\nu}\right]=\left[p_{\mu},p_{\nu}\right]=0,\quad\left[x_{\mu},p_{\nu}\right]=i\eta_{\mu\nu}.

(13)

Then

M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu},

(14)

\tilde{X}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}-\frac{\epsilon}{2MR}\left(x_{\mu}x\cdot p-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}p_{\mu}+h.c.\right),

(15)

\tilde{P}_{\mu}=p_{\mu},

(16)

satisfying (
8
). In analogy with
[
10
]
, eq. (
9
) is then satisfied by choosing the "Planck operator"
\tilde{H}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{H}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\epsilon}{2MR}\left(x\cdot p+p\cdot x\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}\left(\tilde{X}\cdot\tilde{P}+\tilde{P}\cdot\tilde{X}\right)+\frac{1}{2M^{2}R^{2}}M_{\mu\nu}M^{\mu\nu}+\frac{4}{M^{2}R^{2}}}.

(18)

Note that in this representation

\hat{h}=\sqrt{1+\frac{\epsilon}{R^{2}}\hat{x}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{M^{2}}\hat{p}^{2}+\frac{1}{2M^{2}R^{2}}M_{\mu\nu}M^{\mu\nu}+\frac{4}{M^{2}R^{2}}}.

(19)

The last expression differs from the one of ref.
[
10
]
by last term
\frac{4}{M^{2}R^{2}}
.

Defining the action of the operators as

P_{\mu}\rhd 1=0,\qquad\tilde{f}(\tilde{X})\rhd 1=f(x),\qquad\tilde{g}(\tilde{X})\rhd 1=g(x),

(20)

one has

f(x)\star g(x)=\tilde{f}(\tilde{X})\tilde{g}(\tilde{X})\rhd 1=\tilde{g}(\tilde{X})\tilde{f}(\tilde{X})\rhd 1=g(x)\star f(x)\neq f(x)g(x).

(21)

Hence, the star product related to the generalized Heisenberg algebra is commutative, but not pointwise.
Moreover, the coproduct is nontrivial.

\Delta p_{\mu}\neq p_{\mu}\otimes 1+1\otimes p_{\mu}.

(22)

More details on the coproduct and the Hopf algebra will be presented elsewhere.

3
Yang algebra

We also propose an exact realization of the Yang algebra (
1
)-(
5
) alternative to those pesented in ref.
[
7
]
, which can be written in terms of new variables

\xi_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{\mu}-\frac{R}{M}p_{\mu}\right),\quad\pi_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(p_{\mu}+\frac{M}{R}x_{\mu}\right),

(23)

with inverse

x_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\xi_{\mu}+\frac{R}{M}\pi_{\mu}\right),\quad p_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\pi_{\mu}-\frac{M}{R}\xi_{\mu}\right).

(24)

The simplest realization of this kind is

\hat{x}_{\mu}=\xi_{\mu}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(\xi_{\mu}\left(-\frac{3}{8}u+\frac{1}{8}v-\frac{1}{4}z\right)+\frac{R}{M}\left(-\frac{1}{8}u+\frac{3}{8}v+\frac{1}{4}z\right)\pi_{\mu}+h.c.\right),

(25)

\hat{p}_{\mu}=\pi_{\mu}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(\left(-\frac{1}{8}u+\frac{3}{8}v+\frac{1}{4}z\right)\pi_{\mu}+\frac{M}{R}\xi_{\mu}\left(-\frac{3}{8}u+\frac{1}{8}v-\frac{1}{4}z\right)+h.c.\right),

(26)

\hat{h}=1-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left(u-v\right),\quad M_{\mu\nu}=\xi_{\mu}\pi_{\nu}-\xi_{\nu}\pi_{\mu},

(27)

where

u=\frac{1}{M^{2}}\pi^{2},\quad v=\frac{1}{R^{2}}\xi^{2},\quad z=\frac{1}{MR}\xi\cdot\pi,\quad(u)^{\dagger}=u,\quad(v)^{\dagger}=v,\quad(z)^{\dagger}\neq z,

(28)

From (
25
) and (
26
) it follows that

M\left(\hat{x}_{\mu}-\xi_{\mu}\right)=R\left(\hat{p}_{\mu}-\pi_{\mu}\right).

(29)

4
Conformal algebra

In this section we summarize the main properties of the conformal algebra, for comparison with the previous results. This is the symmetry algebra of Minkowski spacetime, and is
generated by Lorentz generators
M_{\mu\nu}
, generators of translations
P_{\mu}
, the dilatation generator
D
and conformal generators
K_{\mu}
obeying the following commutations relations

\left[D,K_{\mu}\right]=-iK_{\mu},

(30)

\left[D,P_{\mu}\right]=iP_{\mu},

(31)

\left[K_{\mu},P_{\nu}\right]=2i\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}D+M_{\mu\nu}\right),

(32)

\left[P_{\mu},P_{\nu}\right]=\left[K_{\mu},K_{\nu}\right]=0,

(33)

giving rise to an
o(2,4)
algebra.
Choosing
MR=1,\epsilon=-1
and making the substitutions
\tilde{X}_{\mu}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}K_{\mu},\;\tilde{P}_{\mu}\rightarrow P_{\mu},\;\tilde{H}\rightarrow D
in (
8
)-(
12
), we observe that the generalized Heisenberg algebra (
8
)-(
12
) becomes the conformal algebra (
30
)-(
33
).

The standard Hermitian realizations of the conformal algebra on phase space is

\displaystyle M_{\mu\nu}=x_{\mu}p_{\nu}-x_{\nu}p_{\mu},\quad D=\frac{1}{2}\left(x\cdot p+p\cdot x\right),\quad P_{\mu}=p_{\mu},

(34)

\displaystyle K_{\mu}=x_{\mu}x\cdot p-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}p_{\mu}+h.c.,

(35)

where
x
and
p
are canonical variables satisfying
(
13
).

The relations between the generators of the generalized Heisenberg algebra (
15
)-(
18
) and the conformal algebra are given by

\tilde{P}_{\mu}=P_{\mu}=p_{\mu},\quad\tilde{X}_{\mu}=x_{\mu}-\frac{\epsilon}{2MR}K_{\mu},\quad\tilde{H}=1-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}D,

(36)

while the relations between the generators of the Yang model and the conformal algebra are

\hat{x}_{\mu}=\xi_{\mu}-\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{2}}{4MR}K_{\mu},\quad\hat{p}_{\mu}=\pi_{\mu}-\frac{\epsilon\sqrt{2}}{4R^{2}}K_{\mu},\quad\hat{h}=1-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}D.

(37)

5
Modified uncertainty relations

Of course, the modified Heisenberg algebra of sect. 2 gives rise to a deformation of the standard uncertainty principle, as in similar cases
[
11
,
16
]
.

The Robertson-Schrödinger argument gives in our case, for vanishing
\langle\tilde{X}\rangle
and
\langle\tilde{P}\rangle
,

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{X}_{\mu}\Delta\tilde{P}_{\nu}\geq{\hbar\over 2}\left|\langle[\tilde{X}_{\mu},\tilde{P}_{\nu}]\rangle\right|={\hbar\over 2}\left|\langle\eta_{\mu\nu}\tilde{H}-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}M_{\mu\nu}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\eta_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{1-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}\langle\tilde{X}\tilde{P}+\tilde{P}\tilde{X}\rangle+\frac{1}{2M^{2}R^{2}}\langle M_{\rho\sigma}M^{\rho\sigma}\rangle+\frac{4}{M^{2}R^{2}}}-\frac{\epsilon}{MR}\langle M_{\mu\nu}\rangle\right|.

(38)

This expression is very complicated and it is not clear whether these modified uncertainty relations
imply the existence of minimal lengths or momenta, as in simpler cases
[
11
]
.

6
Final remarks

The
o(2,4)
algebra is at the basis of several different physical models, like the conformal symmetries of Minkowski space and some variants of the Yang model
of noncommutative geometry on curved spacetime. In this letter we have proposed still another application of
o(2,4)
, defining a generalization of the Heisenberg algebra related to a deformation of the quantum phase space, with an operator-valued Planck constant.

We have discussed some algebraic relations between these models, and outlined some implications of the generalized Heisenberg algebra, but a more thorough investigation of these would be worthy, in particular the details of the Hopf algebra and the consequences of the deformation of the Heisenberg uncertainty relations. We plan to discuss these topics in a future publication.
