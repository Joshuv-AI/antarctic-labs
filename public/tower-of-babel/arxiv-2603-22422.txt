Preparing Fermions via Classical Sampling and Linear Combinations of Unitaries Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-26-0155-T
Erik J. Gustafson, Henry Lamm

Abstract
We present an extension of the Evolving density matrices on Qubits (E ρ \rho OQ) framework that enables efficient fault-tolerant preparation of fermionic quantum states. The original method circumvents state preparation by stochastic sampling, but faces a sign problem in fermionic systems leading to a large number of circuits necessary. We resolve this by combining classical stochastic sampling with a linear combination of unitaries method that avoids the exponential circuit scaling that plagued naïve implementations. The resulting algorithm requires 𝒪 ⁡ ( M 2 ) \mathcal{O}(M^{2}) R Z R_{Z} rotations for circuit preparation, where M M is the number of retained basis states. We validate the method for ground and excited states in the Thirring model, including by computing two-point correlation functions relevant to scattering. In this model for fixed accuracy ε \varepsilon , M M is found to scale empirically as M ∝ 1 m ​ g ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / g ) ​ log ⁡ ( 1 / m ) M\propto\frac{1}{mg}\log(1/g)\log(1/m) .

Theory.
Quantities of interest from quantum computers can be written as matrix elements of time-dependent operators
\mathcal{O}(t)
between states
|\Psi_{P}\rangle
,
|\Psi_{Q}\rangle

P
,

\displaystyle\left<\Psi_{P}|\mathcal{O}(t)|\Psi_{Q}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{\Tr P^{{\dagger}}e^{-\beta H}Q\mathcal{O}(t)}{\Tr e^{-\beta H}}\equiv\frac{\langle Q\mathcal{O}(t)P^{{\dagger}}\rangle_{\rho}}{\langle\delta_{mn}\rangle_{\rho}}

(1)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\rho}
denotes expectation values sampled from

\rho_{mn}
, and the normalization
\langle\delta_{mn}\rangle_{\rho}
can be
computed if needed
52

\rho_{mn}
are
obtained via Euclidean path integral Monte Carlo with open boundary

80

P
,

and

\mathcal{O}(t)
introduces negativity into the classical sampling
83
i.e. the real-time sign problem and therefore a classical computer alone is insufficient for these matrix elements.

E
\rho
OQ offers a solution by using a hybrid approach: the classical computer obtains weights by
sampling, while a quantum computer determines matrix
elements between easily prepared basis states
|\psi_{m}\rangle

|\Psi_{P}\rangle

|\Psi_{Q}\rangle

Classically, E

\displaystyle\rho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}=e^{-\beta H}\propto\sum_{n,m}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\psi_{m}|\rho_{n,m}(\beta;H)

On the quantum side, one must evaluate

The modification of our algorithm works as follows:

1.

Using a classical method, generate a set of
M
bitstrings
|\psi_{m}\rangle
that form
the dominant contributions to an eigenstate
|\Psi\rangle
. For each bitstring, extract the corresponding probability
amplitude
\alpha_{m}=\langle\psi_{m}|\Psi\rangle
.

2.

Construct a LCU circuit where
Prep
loads
\absolutevalue{\alpha_m}
into an ancilla register, preparing the state

|\phi\rangle=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}|\alpha_{m}||\psi_{m}\rangle\right)/\left(\sum_{m=1}^{M}\alpha_{m}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}

(4)

This requires
\lceil\log_{2}(M)\rceil
ancilla qubits and
approximately
\mathcal{O}(M^{2})

R_{Z}
rotations. The success
probability of this preparation is
\|\alpha\|^{2}/\lambda^{2}
where

\lambda=\sum_{i}|\alpha_{i}|
.

3.

Use the ancilla register as a control for the
Select

circuit, apply multicontrolled gates to the working
register initialized in
|0\rangle
to load the corresponding
basis states. The phase information from the signs of

\alpha_{m}
is reintroduced at this stage.

The benefit of this is that we develop a sampling improvement over standard E
\rho
OQ. By extracting approximate weights of the bit strings we can now load linear combination states and their normalization factors. Additional benefits are we can iteratively increase the bond-dimension of the
|\Psi\rangle
to improve the accuracy of the state and perform extrapolations on the coefficients themselves as a function of the bond dimension to extend the reach beyond what might be feasible with a classical computer.

Systematic error in truncation.
The state loaded onto the quantum computer,

|\phi\rangle
, approximates the target state as
|\Psi\rangle=\alpha|\phi\rangle+\varepsilon|\phi^{\perp}\rangle
, where
|\alpha|^{2}+|\varepsilon|^{2}=1
and
|\phi^{\perp}\rangle
is the unimplemented
component. The expectation value of any observable then decomposes as

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|O|\Psi\rangle=|\alpha|^{2}\langle\phi|O|\phi\rangle+|\varepsilon|^{2}\langle\phi^{\perp}|O|\phi^{\perp}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left(\alpha\varepsilon^{*}\langle\phi^{\perp}|O|\phi\rangle\right).

(7)

Applying the triangle inequality with
|\langle\phi|O|\chi\rangle|\leq\|O\|_{2}

to the difference
|\langle\Psi|O|\Psi\rangle-|\alpha|^{2}\langle\phi|O|\phi\rangle|
,
the systematic uncertainty is bounded by

\displaystyle\delta O_{\mathrm{sys}}\leq\left(|\varepsilon|^{2}+2|\varepsilon||\alpha|\right)\|O\|_{2},

(8)

where
\|O\|_{2}
is the operator 2-norm. This error is improvable by increasing

Outlook.
A
\mathcal{O}(M^{2})
state preparation algorithm has been developed for fermionic theories. This method utilizes the LCU to load a weighted selection of bitstrings states computed classically onto a quantum computer. The quantum method is agnostic to the method of sampling. The required number of states depends upon physical parameters and has a polynomial scaling in volume.

Our method is well-suited to the fault-tolerant regime for two reasons. First, it pairs naturally with phase estimation, which can extract eigenvalues with high probability, a key subroutine in computing
n
-point correlation functions. Second, as the number of retained bitstrings increases and the prepared state better approximates the true eigenstate, the overlap approaches unity, directly improving the success probability of phase estimation.

Future work in the field of LCU, approximate quantum loaders
