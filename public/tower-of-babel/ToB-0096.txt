## What Is This?

This is the complete collected works of Johann Weyer (*Opera Omnia*), first published in 1563 (De Lamiis) and here in a 1660 edition. It contains *De Lamiis Liber* (1563) — the text that single-handedly launched the medical/skeptical tradition in witchcraft studies.

## Who Was Johann Weyer?

Weyer (1515–1588) was a Dutch physician and pupil of Cornelius Agrippa. He served as personal physician to the Duke of Cleves and was one of the first physicians in history to apply clinical reasoning to the phenomenon of witchcraft. He was a contemporary of Jean Bodin but came to the exact opposite conclusion.

## De Lamiis Liber (1563) — The First Skeptical Text

*De Lamiis Liber* (Book on the Lamias/Witches) made a radical argument:

- **Supposed witches are mentally ill** — Weyer identified what we would today call psychosis, depression, and hallucination in the descriptions of witch confessions.
- **The devil exploits human weakness** — Rather than recruiting powerful agents, Satan manipulates the gullible and mentally unstable.
- **Confessions are symptoms, not evidence** — Witch confessions describe delusions that match known mental illnesses.
- **The witch hunt is a tragedy, not a triumph** — Entirely innocent people — often elderly women — were being executed based on fantasies produced by torture.

## Weyer vs. Bodin

The conflict between Weyer and Bodin defined the intellectual landscape of witchcraft debate:

| Bodin | Weyer |
|-------|-------|
| Witches choose to serve Satan | The "witch" is a victim of mental illness |
| Execution is the answer | Medical care is the answer |
| Denial of witchcraft is heresy | Witch persecutions are criminal |
| Devil's power is real and active | Devil's "power" exploits human credulity |

## Why It Matters## Why It Matters

Weyer invented the psychological approach to witchcraft. He is the intellectual ancestor of every modern historian who asks "what was really happening to these people?" rather than accepting either the demonological or the purely skeptical position.
