ISOTROPIC POSITIVE DEFINITE FUNCTIONS ON SPHERES
Han Feng, Yan Ge

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the relationship between positive definite functions on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} and on the Euclidean space ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} . For the dimension d d to be odd, a new technique is developed to establish the inheritance of positive (semi-)definite property from ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} to 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} and the converse. For d = 2 d=2 , it is proved that a function defined by f θ , δ ​ ( t ) = ( θ − t ) + δ , δ ≥ d + 1 2 f_{{\theta},\delta}(t)=({\theta}-t)_{+}^{\delta},\quad\delta\geq\frac{d+1}{2} is positive definite on the unit sphere 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} by restricting θ {\theta} in an absolute range. Our results can verify a conjecture proposed by R.K. Beatson, W. zu Castell, Y. Xu and a sharp Pólya type criterion for positive definite functions on spheres.

1.
Introduction

Given a metric space
(\Omega,\rho)
, an isotropic function
g
is called positive definite (positive semi-definite) on
\Omega
if for any integer
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\mathbf{X}_{N}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\subset\Omega
, the corresponding
N\times N
symmetric matrix

g[\mathbf{X}_{N}]=\Bigl\{g\Bigl(\rho(x_{i},x_{j})\Bigr)\Bigr\}_{i,j=1}^{N}

is positive definite (positive semi-definite), which means

\mathbf{c}^{T}g[\mathbf{X}_{N}]\mathbf{c}>0(\geq 0),\quad\text{for all nonzero}\ \mathbf{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Positive definite functions play important roles in many applications, like scatter interpretation, kernel method and spatial statistics (see
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
17
]
).
The concern of construction proper positive definite functions, therefore, has been attracting interest.
In the past, such a problem has been extensively studied for the case
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the Euclidean space, see
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
. The remarkable Bochner’s criterion
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
, states that
a continuous function
g
is isotropic positive definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if and only if it can be the Fourier transform of a nonnegative finite-valued Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
As a typical example, with the positivity of Bessel integral
[
16
]
, it can be proved that for any
\theta>0
, the function defined by

f_{\theta,\delta}(t):=(\theta-t)_{+}^{\delta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\theta-t)^{\delta},&t\leq\theta\\
0,&t>\theta\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

is isotropic positive definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
when
{\delta}\geq\frac{d+1}{2}
.

For
x=\left(x_{1},...,x_{d+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we write
|x|
as the norm. On unit spheres
\mathbb{S}^{d}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}:|x|=1\}
,
d\geq 1
, Schoenberg established a parallel result of Bochner criterion in his classical paper
[
21
]
. He characterized that the isotropic continuous semi-positive definite functions on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
as those
functions of the Gegenbauer expansion

g({\theta})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(\cos\theta),\ \ \theta\in[0,\pi],\ \ {\lambda}:=\frac{d-1}{2}

in which all of the coefficients

a_{n}:=\int_{0}^{\pi}g({\theta})C^{\lambda}_{n}(\cos{\theta})\sin^{2{\lambda}}{\theta}d{\theta}\geq 0,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1.2)

and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(1)<\infty
.
The positive definite functions on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
came somewhat later in
[
10
]
and were characterized as functions with
a_{n}>0
for infinitely many even and infinitely many odd indices in (
1.2
).

Unfortunately, such criterion is often hard to verify when confronted with a particular example. The difficulty is to evaluate the integrals of (
1.2
).
Instead, a Pólya criterion on the spheres was formulated due to Beatson, Castell and Xu
[
5
]
, which provides a simple and efficient way to determine positive definite. Recently, Buhmann and Jäger
[
9
]
established the Pólya criterion on the spheres for conditional positive definite functions. However, the proof in
[
5
]
relies on
a conjecture about the positive definite property of (
1.1
), which can be equivalently stated as the following by adding the convergence of the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(1)<\infty
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\delta\geq{\lambda}+1
and let
\lambda=\frac{d-1}{2}
. Then for any
{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
, the function

f_{{\theta},\delta}(t)=({\theta}-t)_{+}^{\delta}

is isotropic positive definite on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

This conjecture was proved for
d=3,5,7
in
[
5
]
. Related recent work on this conjecture for the higher dimensional case can be found in the papers
[
26
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
19
]
.
In this paper, we first consider the case when
d
is odd and shall provide a new technique to show that the positive definite property can be inherited from Euclidean space to the unit sphere, which will immediately verify the conjecture for all odd dimensions. Our first main theorem states as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d
be an odd integer
\geq 3
. Suppose that
g
is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)
with compact support on
[0,\pi]
. If
g
is isotropic positive definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then so it is on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Some remarks are worthwhile to list.

Remark 1.3
.

The condition of compact support is necessary. A counterexample can be
g(t)=\exp(-t^{2})
, which is isotropic positive define on
{\mathbb{R}}
but not on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Remark 1.4
.

The result was claimed as well by Nie and Ma
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
. However, we find it is difficult to follow the proof of Lemma 3.4 in
[
20
]
which plays a crucial role in the proof of the main theorem. There exists one example sequence
2^{k}n+2^{k}j_{0}+2^{k-1}j_{1}+...+j_{k}
where
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j_{0},j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}
are bounded nonnegative integers, which can not be covered by Lemma 3.4 by taking
n=0
and

j_{i}=\begin{cases}0&{0\leq i\leq\frac{k}{2}-1}\\
1&{\frac{k}{2}\leq i\leq k}.\end{cases}

In this example,

2^{k}\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{0}:=2^{k}n+2^{k}j_{0}+2^{k-1}j_{1}+...+j_{k}

with
\gamma_{0}=0
and

\gamma_{k}=n+j_{0}+2^{-1}j_{1}+\cdots+2^{-k}j_{k}=2^{-\frac{k}{2}+1}-2^{-k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, which implies that the conditions in Lemma 3.4(i) are not satisfied. On the other hand,

2^{k}\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{0}=2^{k}(2^{-\frac{k}{2}+1}-2^{-k})=2^{k/2+1}-1\to\infty,\ \text{as}\ k\to\infty.

Lemma 3.4(ii) can not be used since
2^{k}n+2^{k}j_{0}+2^{k-1}j_{1}+...+j_{k}
is unbounded at this moment.
Besides
[
20
]
, another related and similar result was obtained due to Ma and Lu
[
19
]
, in which the positivity of isotropic covariance matrices was proved to be preserved from Euclidean spaces to unit spheres when the dimensions are odd.

Remark 1.5
.

In contrast with the proof in
[
20
]
, we shall first proceed our proof to functions with high regularity, which allows us to simplify our problem by applying integration by parts. Then through inducing a proper identity approximation operator, additional regular assumptions will be avoided.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
d
be odd and let
g
be an isotropic continuous positive semi-definite function on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
with compact support on
[0,\pi]
. Then
the function

g(t)\left(\frac{\sin t}{t}\right)^{d-1}

is an isotropic positive semi-definite function on both
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

The converse of Theorem
1.2
will be considered for the positive semi-definite functions.
In this situation, the restriction on dimensions can be removed. Precisely, we state the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose that
g
is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)
with compact support on
[0,\pi]
. Let
d>1
. If
g({\theta}^{-1}\cdot)
is isotropic positive semi-definite on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
for
{\theta}\in(0,1)
, then
g
is isotropic positive semi-definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Remark 1.8
.

In the published version, the statement of Theorem
1.7
should be corrected to require that

g(\theta^{-1}\cdot)
is isotropic positive semi-definite on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
for all
\theta\in(0,1)
, as stated above. The proof in the published paper proceeds under this condition and therefore remains valid.

Next, we consider the conjecture in the even dimensional case.
In this situation, the
approach of Theorem
1.2
does not seem to be extendable due to
the fact that there is no such an extensional relation Lemma
2.3
in even dimensional cases.
Our work will focus
on the case
d=2
, and a restriction on the parameter
{\theta}
. Then we shall partly prove the conjecture.
Our main result in this case can be stated as follow:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
d=2
and let
\delta\geq\frac{d+1}{2}
. There exists a constant
c
such that the function

f_{{\theta},\delta}(t)=({\theta}-t)_{+}^{\delta}

is isotropic positive definite on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
whenever
0\leq{\theta}\leq c
,
where
c
is an absolute constant
0<c<\pi
.

To the best of our kownledge, there is no result for even dimensional cases. We will present a detailed discussion of the proof in Section
5
.

The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 contains preliminary results. We will prove an extensional relation about the Jacobi polynomials in Lemma
2.3
and present a known asymptotic relationship between Jacobi polynomial and Bessel function in Lemma
2.4
. They are most often used in our proof. In Section 3, we present the proof of our main result Theorem
1.2
, which can be done in three steps. In particular, in step 2, we divide the estimation into three cases. The proof of the Corollary
1.6

1.7
are given in Section 4. Section 5 is devoted to the problem of Conjecture

and

2.
Preliminaries and Lemmas

In this section, we first prove an extensional relation Lemma
2.3
on the Gegenbauer polynomials that will be useful in the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We also provide the asymptotic relation formula between Jacobi polynomials and Bessel functions that we will use several times in the sequel. Finally, we will recall the fact that the Fourier transform of isotropic functions can be represented by Bessel functions.

Let us start with some notations. We use the standard notation of
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x)
for the Jacobi polynomials where parameters
{\alpha},{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and use
C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)
for the Gegenbauer polynomials of degree
n
. The Gegenbauer polynomial is a special case of the Jacobi polynomial with the well-known relation

C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\Gamma({\lambda}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n+2{\lambda})}{\Gamma(2{\lambda})\Gamma(n+{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2})}P_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2})}(x),\ \ \ {\lambda}>-\frac{1}{2}.

For later applications, we denote
R_{n}^{\lambda}(x):=\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)}{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)}
as normalized Gegenbauer polynomials.

We first recall the following two results on Jacobi polynomials.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following formula holds:
[
24
, (4.5.4)]

P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta}+1)}(x)=\frac{2}{2n+{\alpha}+{\beta}+2}\frac{(n+{\beta}+1)P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x)+(n+1)P_{n+1}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(x)}{1+x}.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following formula holds:
[
24
, (4.1.5)]

P_{2n}^{({\alpha},{\alpha})}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(2n+{\alpha}+1)\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+{\alpha}+1)\Gamma(2n+1)}P_{n}^{({\alpha},-\frac{1}{2})}(2x^{2}-1).

By using the above two formulas, we can establish an explicit expression for
R_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\cos\frac{t}{2})^{2{\lambda}}
. That is, we can write
R_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\cos\frac{t}{2})^{2{\lambda}}
in terms of a linear combination of the terms
R_{2j}^{{\lambda}}(\cos\frac{t}{2})
, with
j=n,...,n+{\lambda}
, and more importantly, all the coefficients are nonnegative. This relation plays an
important role in our proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
{\lambda}
is a positive integer, then there exists a sequence
\{a_{n,j}^{\lambda}\}_{j=n}^{2n+2{\lambda}}
of positive numbers such that for any
t\in[0,\pi]
,

R_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\cos\frac{t}{2})^{2{\lambda}}=\sum_{j=n}^{n+{\lambda}}a_{n,j}^{\lambda}R_{2j}^{{\lambda}}(\cos\frac{t}{2}).

(2.1)

Proof.

First, applying the Lemma
2.1
, substituting
x
by
\cos(\frac{t}{2})
,
{\alpha}
by
{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}
, and
{\beta}
by
{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}
, we get for
t\in[0,\pi]

\displaystyle(\cos\frac{t}{2})^{2}P_{n}^{({\alpha}-\frac{1}{2},{\beta}+\frac{1}{2})}(\cos t)=A_{n}^{{\alpha},{\beta}}P_{n}^{({\alpha}-\frac{1}{2},{\beta}-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos t)+B_{n}^{{\alpha},{\beta}}P_{n+1}^{({\alpha}-\frac{1}{2},{\beta}-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos t),

(2.2)

where

A_{n}^{{\alpha},{\beta}}=\frac{n+{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}{2n+{\alpha}+{\beta}+1},\ \ B_{n}^{{\alpha},{\beta}}=\frac{n+1}{2n+{\alpha}+{\beta}+1}.

Thus,
using (
2.2
)
{\lambda}
times, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(\cos t)(\cos\frac{t}{2})^{2{\lambda}}=\sum_{j=n}^{n+{\lambda}}{\gamma}_{j}P_{j}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos t),

(2.3)

where for all

n\leq j\leq n+\lambda
,
\gamma_{j}
are some nonnegative coefficients. Next, to complete the proof, we will use the Lemma
2.2
with substituting
x
by
\cos(\frac{t}{2})
,
{\alpha}
by
{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}
, and get for
t\in[0,\pi]
and
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
,

P_{n}^{({\alpha}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos t)=\frac{\Gamma(n+{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(2n+1)}{\Gamma(2n+{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(n+1)}P_{2n}^{({\alpha}-\frac{1}{2},{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2})}(\cos\frac{t}{2}).

(2.4)

Combining the equations (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain the desired result (
2.1
).
∎

The following well-known relation connecting Jacobi polynomial and Bessel function is crucial for our proof (See
[
24
]
,
[
15
]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

For
{\alpha}>-\frac{1}{2}
, we have for
t\in(0,\pi)
,

\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\alpha})}(\cos t)}{P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\alpha})}(1)}

\displaystyle=2^{\alpha}\Gamma({\alpha}+1)\Bigl(\frac{t}{\sin t}\Bigr)^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl[j_{\alpha}(Nt)+O(n^{-1})\Bigr],

where
N=n+{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}
,
j_{\alpha}(z)=z^{-{\alpha}}J_{\alpha}(z)
, and here and in what follows
J_{\alpha}(z)
is the Bessel function of the first kind,

J_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{v=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{v}(\frac{z}{2})^{{\alpha}+2v}}{v!{\Gamma(v+{\alpha}+1)}}.

The
O
-term is uniform with respect to
t\in[0,\pi-\varepsilon]
,
\varepsilon
being an arbitrary positive number.

In particular, when
{\alpha}={\beta}=0
,
[
15
, (4.37), (4.38)]
provides a useful consequence in Legendre polynomial case: for
t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
,

\displaystyle\Bigl|{P_{n}}(\cos t)-\left(\frac{t}{\sin t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}J_{0}(Nt)\Bigr|\leq\frac{0.1711}{n}.

(2.5)

Finally, let us recall the Fourier transform
\widehat{f_{d}}
of an isotropic function
{f_{d}}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As we have known in
[
23
]
, it
turns out to be isotropic as well. An important fact is that the Fourier transform of isotropic functions can be represented in terms of the Bessel functions.

Proposition 2.5
.

For the isotropic function
f_{d}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a one dimension function
f
defined on
[0,\infty)
such that
{f_{d}}(x)={f}(|x|)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\displaystyle\widehat{f_{d}}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(u)j_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(|\xi|u)u^{d-1}du,\ \ \ \ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
, that is, for an odd integer
d
and a continuous function
g
on
[0,\infty)
with compact support on
[0,\pi]
, if
g
is isotropic positive definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, so it is on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. According to Schoenberg’s theorem, we need to prove that the Gegenbauer coefficients of
g

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi}g(t)C^{\lambda}_{n}(\cos t)\sin^{2{\lambda}}tdt>0,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(1)<\infty,\ \ \ where\ \ \lambda=\frac{d-1}{2}.

(3.2)

We first verify the validity of
(
3.2
)
. The proof follows a standard argument in
[
21
]
. The series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)
is Abel-summable for every
t\in[0,\pi]
. Hence, for
t=0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)\leq\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)r^{n}<\infty.

Thus we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)
converges.

We now embark on proving equation (
3.1
), which will be carried out in three steps.

3.1.
Step 1: Reduction

Let us begin with a series of reductions. In order to prove equation (
3.1
), we claim that it is enough to prove a slightly stronger statement: for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
and
{\theta}\in(0,\pi]
,

\int_{0}^{{\theta}}g(\frac{t\pi}{{\theta}})C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}}\,dt>0,

(3.3)

and it is obvious to see (
3.1
) is exactly a particular case when
{\theta}=\pi
. We now continue to deal with the claim (
3.3
). Without loss of generality, in (
3.3
), we may replace
f(\frac{t}{\theta})
with
g(\frac{t\pi}{\theta})
, i.e.
f(t)=g(\pi t)
, where
f
is isotropic continuous positive definite on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with compact support on
[0,1]
. Hence, showing the claim (
3.3
) is equivalent to show for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

{\theta}\in(0,\pi]
,

\int_{0}^{\theta}f\Bigl(\frac{t}{\theta}\Bigr)C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}}\,dt>0.\

For simplicity in the proof, we will deal with the following integral and verify its positivity:

I_{n}(\theta):=\theta^{-2{\lambda}-1}\int_{0}^{\theta}f\Bigl(\frac{t}{\theta}\Bigr)R_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}}\,dt>0,\

(3.4)

where
{\theta}\in(0,\pi]
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Thus, by the arguments above, the previous claim (
3.1
) reduces to show
I_{n}(\theta)>0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
\theta\in(0,\pi]
. Furthermore, with the help of Lemma
2.3
, we would restrict our analysis on the integral (
3.4
) on a small range of
{\theta}
. More precisely, we have if
I_{n}(\theta)>0
, where
\theta\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, and
\varepsilon_{0}
is a sufficiently small positive parameter, then
I_{n}(\theta)>0
for every
{\theta}\in(0,\pi]
. Indeed, this claim follows from the fact that for every
{\theta}\in(0,\pi]
, there exists a
\theta_{0}\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, where
\varepsilon_{0}
is a sufficiently small positive parameter, such that
I_{n}({\theta})
can be written as a sum of
I_{n}({\theta}_{0})
with positive coefficients, which establishes the desired claim.

Thus, given the whole reduction arguments above, the proof of the claim (
3.1
) is finally reduced to show the following Lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d
be an odd integer
\geq 3
and let
f\in C[0,\infty)
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset[0,1]
. If
f
is an isotropic positive definite function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then

I_{n}(\theta)=\theta^{-2{\lambda}-1}\int_{0}^{\theta}f\Bigl(\frac{t}{\theta}\Bigr)R_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}}\,dt>0\

(3.5)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
\theta\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, where
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
is a sufficiently small positive parameter.

Now, we will give the proof of the Lemma
3.1
in the following step 2 and 3.

3.2.
Step 2:

In this step, we shall prove Lemma
3.1
for the functions with high regularity, that is, under the additional assumptions that
f\in C^{{\lambda}+3}[0,\infty)
, and
f^{\prime}(0)<0
. We will divide the proof of (
3.5
) into the following three cases: (i)
n\theta\geq A
; (ii)
n\theta\leq B
; (iii)
B\leq n\theta\leq A
, where
A>1
and
B\in(0,1)
are certain parameters depending only on
f
.

3.2.1.
Case (i).

In this case, we shall prove that there exists a constant
A>1
depending only on
f
such that (
3.5
) holds whenever
n\theta\geq A
.

First, we need a definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\lambda}
be a positive integer. For
j=1,\cdots,{\lambda}
, we define

F_{0}(t)=f(\theta^{-1}t)(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}}\ \ and\ \ F_{j}(t)=\Bigl(\frac{F_{j-1}(t)}{\sin t}\Bigr)^{\prime}.

Clearly, for
0\leq j\leq{\lambda}
,
F_{j}\in C^{{\lambda}-j+3}[0,\infty)
,
F_{j}(t)=0
for
t\geq\theta
, and
F_{j}(t)=O(t^{2({\lambda}-j)})
as
t\to 0^{+}
. The functions
F_{j}(t),j=1,2,...,{\lambda},
have the following decomposition.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
0\leq t\leq{\theta}
and
\ell=1,2,\cdots,{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle F_{\ell}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\theta^{-j}f^{(j)}(\theta^{-1}t)\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{\ell-j}{2}]}{\alpha}_{\ell,k}^{(j)}(\sin t)^{2{\lambda}-2\ell+j+2k}(\cos t)^{\ell-j-2k},

(3.6)

where the
{\alpha}_{\ell,k}^{(j)}
are constants,

\displaystyle{\alpha}_{\ell,0}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(2{\lambda}-1)(2{\lambda}-3)\cdots(2{\lambda}-2\ell+1),

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\alpha}_{\ell+1,0}^{(1)}

\displaystyle={\alpha}_{\ell,0}^{(0)}+2({\lambda}-\ell){\alpha}_{\ell,0}^{(1)},\ \ {\alpha}_{1,0}^{(1)}=1.

(3.8)

In particular,

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\Bigl[(2{\lambda}-1)!!\cos^{\lambda}t+\sum_{k=1}^{[\frac{{\lambda}}{2}]}{\alpha}_{{\lambda},k}^{(0)}(\sin t)^{2k}(\cos\ t)^{{\lambda}-2k}\Bigr]f(\theta^{-1}t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda}\theta^{-j}f^{(j)}(\theta^{-1}t)\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{{\lambda}-j}{2}]}{\alpha}_{{\lambda},k}^{(j)}(\sin t)^{j+2k}(\cos t)^{{\lambda}-j-2k}.

(3.9)

Proof.

The proof uses induction by
\ell
.
When

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\theta}}({\theta}-t)^{a}\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{b}g(u)dudt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{\theta}}g(u)\int_{u}^{\theta}(t-u)^{b}({\theta}-t)^{a}dtdu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{\theta}}g(u)\int_{0}^{1}({\theta}-u)^{a+b+1}(1-s)^{a}s^{b}dsdu

\displaystyle=B(b+1,a+1)\int_{0}^{{\theta}}g(u)({\theta}-u)^{a+b+1}du,

where
B(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}dt
is the Beta function. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\theta}}g(u)({\theta}-u)^{a+b+1}du=\frac{1}{B(b+1,a+1)}\int_{0}^{{\theta}}({\theta}-t)^{a}\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{b}g(u)dudt.

Now for
\delta_{1}>\delta_{2}
, let
a={\delta}_{1}-{\delta}_{2}-1
,
b=\delta_{2}
, and
g(u)=C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos u)(\sin u)^{2{\lambda}}
. It follows immediately that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\theta}}({\theta}-u)^{\delta_{1}}

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos u)(\sin u)^{2{\lambda}}du

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B(\delta_{2}+1,\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})}\int_{0}^{{\theta}}({\theta}-t)^{\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-u)^{\delta_{2}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(\cos u)(\sin u)^{2{\lambda}}dudt.

Hence, we will focus on the boundary case.

Our main result is to show that Conjecture
5.1
is true when the dimension
d=2
under the restriction when
{\theta}
is small. By the argument above, it suffices to consider the boundary case
\delta=\frac{3}{2}
. More precisely, we state the main theorem below.

Theorem 5.2
.

The function

f_{{\theta},\delta}(t)=({\theta}-t)_{+}^{\frac{3}{2}}

is isotropic positive definite on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
whenever
0\leq{\theta}\leq c
,
where
c
is an absolute constant
0<c<\pi
.

Remark 5.3
.

In the proof, we give an upper estimate of
c
and it can be taken at most
1.2644\times 10^{-21}
. We believe this value could be improved.

For the proof of Theorem
5.2
, by the above analysis according to the Schoenberg’s theorem, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}P_{n}(\cos t)\sin tdt>0,\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(5.1)

The proof of the positivity in (
5.1
) consists of three cases: (i)
n{\theta}\geq A
; (ii)
n{\theta}\leq B
; (iii)
B\leq n{\theta}\leq A
.
Next, we will give detailed proofs of the three cases.

5.1.
Case (i).

In this case, we shall prove that there exists a constant
A>1
such that (
5.1
) holds whenever
n\theta\geq A
. The proof is long and will
be divided into several subsections.

5.1.1.
Decomposition of the integral.

We first give the following decomposition on the integral (
5.1
):

Lemma 5.4
.

For
{\theta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{4n(n+1)}{3}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}P_{n}(\cos t)\sin t\,dt=I_{n,1}({\theta})+R_{n,1}({\theta})+R_{n,2}({\theta})-R_{n,3}({\theta}),

where

\displaystyle I_{n,1}({\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\cos t}{\sin^{2}t}\,dt,

\displaystyle R_{n,1}({\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt,

\displaystyle R_{n,2}({\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{1}{(\sin t)\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\geq 0,

\displaystyle R_{n,3}({\theta})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt.

Proof.

Using (
A.2
) and integration by parts, we obtain that

\displaystyle A_{n}:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}P_{n}(\cos t)\sin t\,dt=-\frac{2}{n}\int_{0}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(P_{n+1}^{(-1,-1)}(\cos t)\Bigr)^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n+1}^{(-1,-1)}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt=\frac{3}{4n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\sin^{2}t({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt,

where we used (
A.2
) in the first step, and (
A.4
) in the third step.
Applying integration by parts to this last integral once again yields

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr)^{\prime}\sin t\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt+\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cos t\,dt

\displaystyle=:A_{n,1}+A_{n,2}.

For the term
A_{n,2}
, we use integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle A_{n,2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{n^{2}(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl(P_{n+1}^{(-1,-1)}(\cos t)\Bigr)^{\prime}({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cot t\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{n^{2}(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n+1}^{(-1,-1)}(\cos t)\Bigl[\frac{1}{2}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cot t+\frac{({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sin^{2}t}\Bigr]\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8n^{2}(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt+\frac{3}{4n^{2}(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt,

where the third step uses (
A.4
). Putting the above together, we then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{4n(n+1)}{3}A_{n}=-\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt+\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=-R_{n,3}({\theta})+R_{n,1}({\theta})+\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt.

For the last integral, we apply integration by parts again to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt=\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{({\theta}-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sin t}\Bigl(P_{n}(1)-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr)^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\cos t}{\sin^{2}t}\,dt+\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{1}{\sin t\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt

\displaystyle=I_{n,1}({\theta})+R_{n,2}({\theta}).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.1.2.
Estimates of
I_{n,1}({\theta})
,
R_{n,3}({\theta})
, and
R_{n,1}({\theta})

In this subsection, we will give the upper bounds for

and

(see Lemma

Lemma 5.5
.

If
n{\theta}\geq 5
and
{\theta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, then

\displaystyle|R_{n,1}({\theta})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\Bigr|\leq C(n{\theta})^{-2}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}},

where the constant
C
can be taken to be
42.6909
.

Proof.

We break the integral
\int_{0}^{\theta}
into two parts
\int_{{\theta}-n^{-1}}^{\theta}+\int_{0}^{{\theta}-n^{-1}}
.
For the first part, we use estimate (
A.8
),
\arctan x\leq x
, and the condition
n{\theta}\geq 5
to get

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{1}{2n}\int_{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}^{\theta}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\Bigl|

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{2.821\times\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2n(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\int_{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}^{\theta}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{2.821\times\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2n(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\cdot 2\arctan\left(\frac{\frac{1}{n}}{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{2.821\times\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{n(n-1)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\frac{1}{n}}{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2.821\times\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\times\left(\frac{5}{4-\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-2}{\theta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 37.9275\times n^{-2}{\theta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For the second part, we use (
A.2
), integration by parts, triangle inequality, (
A.7
), and
n{\theta}\geq 5
to obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{1}{2n}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t\cos t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\Bigl|=\Bigl|\frac{-1}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\cos t)\,d\Bigl(P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr)\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigl|P_{n}(\cos({\theta}-\frac{1}{n}))\frac{\cos({\theta}-\frac{1}{n})}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}}\Bigr|+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}P_{n}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\cos t)dt\Bigr|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}P_{n}(\cos t)({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sin t)dt\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-\frac{1}{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}dt+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{{\theta}-\frac{1}{n}}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}tdt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{2(\frac{\pi}{2})^{2}}{3n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{4.7634}{n^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Therefore, combining the above two parts, we get the desired estimate.

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

If
n{\theta}\geq 5
and
{\theta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, then

\displaystyle|R_{n,3}({\theta})|=\left|\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\ \right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{n{\theta}}\sin(N{\theta})\Bigl(\frac{\sin{\theta}}{{\theta}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}+C\times(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}},

where
N=n+\frac{1}{2}
, and the constant
C
can be taken to be
92.1237.

Proof.

We will need a function
\eta\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
where

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\frac{1}{2}\leq x\cr\frac{1}{2}\cos(4\pi x)+\frac{1}{2},&\frac{1}{4}\leq x<\frac{1}{2}\cr 0,&x<\frac{1}{4}\end{cases}.

Then,

\int_{0}^{\theta}P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt=\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}(1-\eta({\theta}^{-1}t))P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt+\int_{\frac{{\theta}}{4}}^{{\theta}}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt.

For the first integral, by (
A.5
) and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}(1-\eta({\theta}^{-1}t))P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}_{0}(1-\eta({\theta}^{-1}t))\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}d\Bigl(\frac{\sin^{2}t}{2n}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4n}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}g(t)dt,

where
g(t):=1-\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)-2{\theta}^{-1}({\theta}-t)\eta^{\prime}({\theta}^{-1}t).

Using (
A.8
) and noting that
|g(t)|\leq 4\pi+2
, we get

\displaystyle\Bigl|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}(1-\eta({\theta}^{-1}t))P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}dt\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\frac{1}{4n}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}P_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(\cos t)(\sin t)^{2}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}g(t)\,dt\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2.821\cdot\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4n}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}\frac{1}{(n-1)^{1/2}t^{3/2}}(\sin t)^{2}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}|g(t)|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2.821\cdot\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(4\pi+2)}{4n(n-1)^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}\left(\frac{\sin t}{t}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2.821\cdot\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(4\pi+2)}{4n(n-1)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{{\theta}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{{\theta}}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2.821\cdot\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot(4\pi+2)\cdot\sqrt{2}}{4n^{3/2}},

(5.2)

where the last inequality follows by
n\geq 2
when
{\theta}
is small.

Next, we estimate the second integral. We claim that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\frac{{\theta}}{4}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{n{\theta}}\sin(N{\theta})\Bigl(\frac{\sin{\theta}}{{\theta}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\Bigl(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{2}{\pi})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+10.4160\Bigr)(n{\theta})^{-3/2},

(5.3)

where
N=n+\frac{1}{2}
.
To see this, we use (
A.6
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{{\theta}}{4}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle P_{n}(\cos t)\frac{\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)\sin t}{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1/2}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\theta}/4}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)\cos(Nt-\frac{\pi}{4})(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{2}{\pi})^{\frac{3}{2}}}(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

We then write

\displaystyle 2^{1/2}

\displaystyle\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\theta}/4}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)\cos(Nt-\frac{\pi}{4})(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=2^{-1/2}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{{\theta}/4}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)\Bigl[e^{i(Nt-\frac{\pi}{4})}+e^{i(-Nt+\frac{\pi}{4})}\Bigr](\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=:I_{n}^{+}+I_{n}^{-}.

Using Proposition (
B.1
) and (
B.2
) with
\phi(t)=\eta(\frac{t}{{\theta}})(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

{\theta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}),

{\alpha}=\frac{{\theta}}{4}
,
{\beta}={\theta}
,
{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2}
,
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
, and
v=1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{{\theta}}{4}}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{iNt}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{iN{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\pi i}\eta(1)(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{13.0552}{N},

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{{\theta}}{4}}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}e^{-iNt}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-iN{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\pi i}\eta(1)(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{13.0552}{N}.

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi i}{4}}\int_{{\theta}/4}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)e^{iNt}(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi i}{4}}\Big[\frac{\Gamma(1/2)}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{iN{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\pi i}\eta(1)(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+13.0552\times N^{-1}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-1}e^{iN{\theta}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}i}(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}i}\cdot 5.2080\times(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}},

and

\displaystyle I_{n}^{-}

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi i}{4}}\int_{{\theta}/4}^{\theta}\eta({\theta}^{-1}t)e^{-iNt}(\sin t)^{\frac{1}{2}}({\theta}-t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-1}e^{\frac{\pi i}{2}}e^{-iN{\theta}}(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}i}\cdot 5.2080\times(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle|I_{n}^{+}+I_{n}^{-}|\leq 2^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{-1}\sin(N{\theta})(\sin{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}+10.4160\times(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

This implies (
5.3
).

By equations (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we thus conclude the desired estimate for
R_{n,3}({\theta})
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

For
n{\theta}\geq 5
and
{\theta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{n,1}({\theta})=\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\cos t}{\sin^{2}t}dt\geq\frac{3}{2n}\sqrt{{\theta}}-30.1067\times(n{\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim the following lower estimate.

I_{n,1}({\theta})\geq\frac{3}{4}\frac{2\sqrt{{\theta}}}{n^{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt-\frac{3}{2n^{2}}{\theta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(n{\theta})\left(\frac{4}{\pi}(4+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}})+4\right).

(5.4)

Indeed, since that

\frac{\cos t}{\sin^{2}t}=\frac{1-2\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}=\frac{-2\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{\sin^{2}t}-1\right)+\frac{1}{t^{2}},

we may rewrite the integral
I_{n,1}({\theta})
to

\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}I_{n,1}({\theta})=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{2\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\Bigl[\frac{t^{2}}{\sin^{2}t}-1\Bigr]\,dt+\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt.

Then we will prove that

\displaystyle\frac{n(n+1)}{2}I_{n,1}({\theta})

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{{\theta}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt-\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\theta}}+\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t}\,dt-2(\frac{2}{\pi})^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\theta}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt-\left(\frac{4}{\pi}(4+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}})+2\right){\theta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(n{\theta})-2(\frac{2}{\pi})^{2}{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\theta}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt-{\theta}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(n{\theta})\left(\frac{4}{\pi}(4+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}})+4\right).

(5.7)

The first inequality (
5.5
) follows that

\displaystyle\Big|

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{2\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}\,dt+\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{\sin^{2}t}-1\Bigr)\,dt+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\theta}\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt-\sqrt{{\theta}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\,dt+\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\theta}}+\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t}\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|-\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\frac{2\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}\,dt+\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t^{2}}\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{\sin^{2}t}-1\Bigr)\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{\theta}\Bigl[1-P_{n}(\cos t)\Bigr]\sqrt{{\theta}-t}\Big(\frac{-2\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}t}-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Big)\,dt\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\Big|\frac{-2\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}}{\sin^{2}t}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}t}-\frac{1}{t^{2}}\Big|\,dt\leq 2{\theta}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\frac{2}{\pi})^{2}.

To show the second inequality (
5.6
), we need to split the integral
\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\theta}}+\sqrt{{\theta}-t}}\frac{1-P_{n}(\cos t)}{t}\,dt
into two parts:
\int_{0}^{1/n}+\int_{1/n}^{{\theta}}
. For the first part, we use representation (
A.9
).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
n=2m
(The case when
n=2m-1
can be treated similarly). Then we can obtain that

\displaystyle P_{2m}(\cos t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\Bigl(\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(m+1)}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\Gamma(m-j+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(m+j+\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(m-j+1)\Gamma(m+j+1)}\cos(2jt).

(5.8)

By the identity
