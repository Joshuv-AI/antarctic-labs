Boundary epsilon regularity for incompressible Navier–Stokes equations via weak-strong uniqueness
Siran Li

Abstract
. We show that finite-energy weak solutions to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on a three-dimensional bounded smooth domain are regular up to the boundary, provided that the L t 4 ​ L x 4 L^{4}_{t}L^{4}_{x} -norm of the solution is smaller than a constant depending only on the domain. This answers a problem raised in [D. Albritton, T. Barker, and C. Prange, J. Math. Fluid Mech. 25 (2023), Paper No. 49]. Our proof relies on a new slicing construction near the boundary of the domain.

.

Let

be a smooth bounded domain. There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0

Then

\|U\|_{C^{\infty}\left(]-1,0]\times{\overline{\Omega}}\right)}\leq C^{\prime}\left\|U\right\|_{L^{4}\left(]-1,0[\times\Omega\right)}

depending only on

The key tool for the proof of Theorem

is a new spatial slicing construction in Proposition
8

and fix any

\mathscr{V}_{0}
enclosed by the paraboloid from below and some spherical cap from above. If we contract it to

such that each folium

Step 7: Fixed point arguments.
Having obtained the linear Stokes estimate, namely Equation (

Consider the perturbed Stokes system:

(22)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}V-\Delta V+\nabla P=-{\rm div}\left[\left({\overline{U}}+V\right)\otimes\left({\overline{U}}+V\right)\right]\qquad\text{in }]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R},\\

V=0\qquad\text{on }]t_{0},0[\times\partial\mathscr{R},\\
V\big|_{t=t_{0}}=0\qquad\text{on }\{t_{0}\}\times\mathscr{R}.\end{cases}

We construct a fixed point for the mild solution

(23)

V(t,x)=-\int_{t_{0}}^{0}e^{s{\mathbf{A}}}{\rm div}\,\left[\left({\overline{U}}+V\right)\otimes\left({\overline{U}}+V\right)\right]\,{\rm d}s\equiv{\mathscr{B}}\left[{\overline{U}}+V;{\overline{U}}+V\right],

where

is a bilinear operator given by

\displaystyle{\mathscr{B}}[D;E]:=-\int_{t_{0}}^{0}e^{s{\mathbf{A}}}{\rm div}\,(D\otimes E)\,{\rm d}s.

We shall also use the linear operator:

\displaystyle L[D](t,\bullet):=-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{s{\mathbf{A}}}{\rm div}\left[D\otimes{\overline{U}}+{\overline{U}}\otimes D\right]\,{\rm d}s.

From the well-known estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|e^{s{\mathbf{A}}}{\rm div}(D\otimes E)\right\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\leq\frac{C_{7}}{(-s)^{3/4}}\|D(t,\bullet)\|_{L^{6}_{x}}\|E(t,\bullet)\|_{L^{6}_{x}},

together with the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\big\|B[D;E]\big\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{6}_{x}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}\right)}\leq C_{8}\|D\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{6}_{x}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}\right)}\|E\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{6}_{x}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}\right)}

as well as

. It then holds by Gallagher–Iftimie–Planchon

Step 7: Weak-strong uniqueness.
We show that

Observe by Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathscr{R}}|Z(t,x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathscr{R}}|\nabla Z(t^{\prime},x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{\mathscr{R}}\left[Z\otimes\left(V+{\overline{U}}\right)\right]:\nabla Z\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}t^{\prime}

From the previous steps in this proof,

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{6}_{x}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}\right)}\leq C_{11}\left\{\|a\|_{L^{4}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\partial\mathscr{R}\right)}+\|b\|_{L^{4}_{x}\left(\{t_{0}\}\times\left[{\bf B}_{32r_{0}}(x_{\star})\cap\Omega\right]\right)}\right\}\leq C_{12}\varepsilon_{0},

x_{\star}
. By bootstrap arguments in the previous paragraph, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{6}_{t}L^{6}_{x}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}^{\prime}\right)}\leq M(1+\varepsilon)\qquad\text{whenever }0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.

Here we denote

\begin{cases}\varepsilon:=\|a\|_{L^{4}\left(]t_{0},0[\times\partial\mathscr{R}\right)}+\|b\|_{L^{4}_{x}\left(\{t_{0}\}\times\left[{\bf B}_{32r_{0}}(x_{\star})\cap\Omega\right]\right)},\\
\|U\|_{\left(L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}\dot{W}^{1,2}_{x}\right)\left(]-1,0[\times\Omega\right)}\leq M,\\
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}=\mathscr{R}\cap{\bf B}_{\kappa r_{0}}^{+}(x_{\star})\qquad\text{with a constant $\kappa\in\left]0,8\right]$ depending only on the geometry of $\mathscr{R}$.}\end{cases}

Therefore, we may bootstrap again as in
[
33
,
30
,
23
,
5
,
32
]
(see Theorem
7
) to deduce that

(31)

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(]t_{0},0[\times\mathscr{R}^{\prime\prime})}\leq\wp(M,\varepsilon)\qquad\text{whenever }0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},

where
\wp(M,\varepsilon)
is a polynomial in
M
and
\varepsilon
, and

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}^{\prime\prime}=\mathscr{R}\cap{\bf B}_{\frac{\kappa r_{0}}{2}}^{+}(x_{\star}).

In particular, the region
\overline{\mathscr{R}^{\prime\prime}}
intersects the boundary
{\partial{\Omega}}
tangentially at
x_{\star}
, and the bound in Equation (
31
) is independent of the choice of
x_{\star}
. By varying
x_{\star}
through
{\partial{\Omega}}
and invoking the interior regularity theorem (see
[
2
, Theorem B]
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(]t_{0},0[\times\Omega)}\leq C_{13}(M,\varepsilon_{0})\qquad\text{whenever }0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},

where
\varepsilon_{0}
depends only on the
C^{2}
-geometry of
\Omega
.

It is classically known that essentially bounded weak solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations are
C^{\infty}

[
33
]
. Hence, the proof of Theorem
3
is now complete. ∎

4.
Discussions

Several concluding remarks are in order below.

(1)

Theorem
3
remains valid for an unbounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with compact smooth boundary
{\partial{\Omega}}
.

(2)

The existence and uniqueness for very weak solutions in
L^{4}_{t}L^{6}_{x}
with boundary data in
L^{4}_{t}L^{4}_{x}
(
i.e.
, Proposition
5
taken from
[
14
,
13
]
) require the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
to be of regularity
C^{2,1}
. For future investigations, we shall explore how to lower the regularity assumptions for
\Omega
. The recent paper
[
8
]
by Breit has proved an epsilon boundary regularity theorem for local suitable weak solutions (see
[
9
,
27
]
) over low-regularity domains.

(3)

Our boundary epsilon regularity Theorem
3
imposes no condition on pressure. This is in stark contrast to the epsilon regularity theorems for the “local suitable weak solutions”
à la
Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg
[
9
]
. In our case, the control for pressure is implicitly contained in the estimates for the Stokes semigroup in the very weak solutions.

(4)

The
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
-bound for
U
in Theorem
3
depends only on
M
and the
C^{2}
-geometry of
\Omega
. The dependence on
C^{2}
-geometry enters the proof via the injectivity radius of the boundary
{\partial{\Omega}}
in
\overline{\Omega}
(
i.e.
, the parameter
r_{0}
in the proof of Theorem
3
). Only the higher-regularity bounds for
U
may depend on the
C^{2+}
-geometry of
\Omega
.
