Strategic Facility Location with p p -Norm Social Costs
Jabari Hastings

Abstract
We consider the strategic facility location problem in ℓ q ​ ( ℝ d ) \ell_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) spaces where the social cost is defined by an arbitrary p p -norm of the individual costs. While the optimal approximation ratios for deterministic strategyproof mechanisms are well established in the d = 1 d=1 setting, the guarantees for multi-dimensional spaces under an arbitrary p p -norm are less understood. In this work, we analyze the well-studied, strategyproof coordinate-wise median (CM) mechanism and provide approximation guarantees for these generalized social costs. • For d = 2 d=2 , we establish tight approximation ratios for all p , q ≥ 1 p,q\geq 1 . In particular, we show that the CM mechanism is a 2 1 − 1 / max ⁡ ( p , q ) 2^{1-1/\max(p,q)} -approximation, resolving a conjecture of Goel and Hann-Caruthers (Social Choice and Welfare, 2023). • Furthermore, for d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , we give upper bounds on the approximation ratio of the CM mechanism for arbitrary p p -norm social costs, generalizing the recent result of Gravin and Jia (STOC, 2025) for the utilitarian social cost. Remarkably, we show that this approximation ratio never exceeds 3, regardless of the dimension.

Theorem 1

Preliminaries

In the strategic facility location problem, the true locations of agents
[n]
are denoted by the location profile
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}
. The space
\mathds{R}^{d}
is equipped with an
\ell_{q}
-norm, denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
. The individual
cost
of a facility location
f\in\mathds{R}^{d}
for an agent located at
x_{i}
is the distance
\|f-x_{i}\|_{q}
. When a mechanism
\mathcal{M}
takes these reported locations as input, it produces a facility location
\mathcal{M}(x)\in\mathds{R}^{d}
. The cost of this chosen location for an agent with true location
x_{i}
is the distance
\|\mathcal{M}(x)-x_{i}\|_{q}
.

Definition 1
.

A mechanism
\mathcal{M}
is (individually)
strategyproof
if no agent can strictly decrease their cost by reporting a false location. Formally, for any agent
i\in[n]
and any two location profiles
x,x^{\prime}\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}
that differ only in the
i
-th agent’s location (i.e.,
x_{j}=x_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j\neq i
),

\|\mathcal{M}(x)-x_{i}\|_{q}\leq\|\mathcal{M}(x^{\prime})-x_{i}\|_{q}.

If a mechanism is strategyproof, reporting a truthful location is a dominant strategy. Thus, we can safely assume that such mechanisms operate on the agents’ true locations.

Approximation Ratio.

The
p
-norm
social cost
of a facility
f
aggregates the individual costs using the
\ell_{p}
-norm, defined for finite
p
as

\mathrm{SC}_{p}(f,x)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|f-x_{i}\|_{q}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

When
p=\infty
, this becomes the maximum individual cost among all agents,
\max_{i\in[n]}\|f-x_{i}\|_{q}
.
Given a set of locations
x\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}
, we define the
approximation ratio
of a mechanism
\mathcal{M}
to be the worst-case ratio of the mechanism’s social cost compared to the optimal social cost:

\alpha_{p}(\mathcal{M})=\sup_{x\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}}\frac{\mathrm{SC}_{p}(\mathcal{M}(x),x)}{\min_{f\in\mathds{R}^{d}}\mathrm{SC}_{p}(f,x)}.

Coordinatewise Median.

In this work, we focus on the coordinatewise median (CM) mechanism. For a given location profile
x
, this mechanism calculates the standard one-dimensional median for each of the
d
coordinates independently.
2
2

2

When
n
is even, the mechanism can break ties arbitrarily (in favor of either the larger or the smaller value), but consistently across dimensions. In this work, we assume that ties are broken in favor of the smaller value.

If
x_{i,j}
denotes the
j
-th coordinate of agent
i
’s location, the mechanism outputs a facility location
m=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})\in\mathds{R}^{d}
, where

m_{j}=\operatorname{median}(x_{1,j},\dots,x_{n,j})\quad\text{for each}\ j\in[d].

It is well-known that the CM mechanism is strategyproof and
anonymous
(i.e., for any permutation
\pi
of the agents
\{1,\dots,n\}
, it holds that
\mathcal{M}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\mathcal{M}(x_{\pi(1)},\dots,x_{\pi(n)})
).

Program for the CM Mechanism

Our analysis of the CM mechanism is based on a generalization of a mathematical program introduced by
26
. In this section, we develop this program and explain how it allows us to derive upper bounds on the approximation ratio. In what follows, we let
\mathcal{M}(z)
denote the output of the mechanism.

Fix a tuple of reported locations
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}
and let
f\in\mathds{R}^{d}
denote the optimal facility location under the
p
-norm social cost objective. We make a sequence of transformations that do not affect the ratio of the social costs
\mathrm{SC}_{p}(f,z)
and
\mathrm{SC}_{p}(\mathcal{M}(z),z)
.

1.

Duplication:
If
n
is not even, we can consider the instance where each location appears twice (i.e., the tuple
y=(z_{1},z_{1},z_{2},z_{2},\dots,z_{n},z_{n})
). The optimal facility location for this new instance is still
f
and we also have
\mathcal{M}(y)=\mathcal{M}(z)
, so the approximation ratio is unchanged. Thus, we assume without loss of generality that
n
is even.

2.

Translation:
We uniformly translate all points so that
\mathcal{M}(z)=(0,\dots,0)
. Since
\ell_{q}
-norms are translation invariant, the approximation ratio is preserved.

3.

Symmetry (Non-negative Orthant):
Following the translation, we desire the optimal facility
f
to lie in the non-negative orthant. For any instance
z
in which the optimal facility has a negative coordinate, there exists an equivalent instance
y
yielding the exact same approximation ratio, but where the optimal facility lies in the non-negative orthant.
3
3

3

Equivalently, one can flip the signs of the coordinate axes.

4.

Normalization:
If
f=\mathcal{M}(z)=0
, then the mechanism is optimal on this instance. So, we can assume that
f\neq\mathcal{M}(z)
. We can normalize so that
\|f\|_{q}=1
. This does not affect the approximation ratio since all distances are scaled by the same factor.

Now, for a fixed facility location
f\neq 0
in the nonnegative orthant and any parameter
\lambda
, we can ask what is the smallest possible value of
\sum_{i\in[n]}\|x_{i}-f\|_{q}^{p}-\lambda^{p}\|x_{i}\|_{q}^{p}

among any set of points
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}
satisfying
\mathcal{M}(x)=0
. If this value is nonnegative, then
\mathrm{SC}_{p}(\mathcal{M}(z),z)\leq(1/\lambda)\cdot\mathrm{SC}_{p}(f,z)
and thus
\mathcal{M}
is a
(1/\lambda)
-approximation on the original instance.

Our goal therefore is to fix a facility location and find the largest value
\lambda\in(0,1)
such that the following program has a nonnegative minimum value.

\displaystyle\min_{x\in(\mathds{R}^{d})^{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(x_{i}),\qquad\text{where }g(v):=\|v-f\|_{q}^{p}-\lambda^{p}\|v\|_{q}^{p}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}=1,\quad f\geq 0

CM Approximation Guarantees for
\ell_{q}(\mathds{R}^{2})

We start by restating the initial program to handle the case when

Recall that when the CM mechanism is given an input

\displaystyle S_{++}=\{x_{i}\mid\sigma(x_{i})=(+1,+1)\},\qquad

\displaystyle S_{-+}=\{x_{i}\mid\sigma(x_{i})=(-1,+1)\},

\displaystyle S_{--}=\{x_{i}\mid\sigma(x_{i})=(-1,-1)\},\qquad

\displaystyle S_{+-}=\{x_{i}\mid\sigma(x_{i})=(+1,-1)\}.

Using these defined sets, our objective (

\displaystyle\min_{x\in(\mathds{R}^{2})^{n}}\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{++}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{--}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{+-}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{-+}}g(x_{i}).

(2a)

\displaystyle|S_{++}|+|S_{+-}|

\displaystyle=|S_{-+}|+|S_{--}|,

\displaystyle|S_{--}|+|S_{+-}|

\displaystyle=|S_{-+}|+|S_{++}|,

\displaystyle|S_{++}|+|S_{+-}|+|S_{-+}|+|S_{--}|

\displaystyle=n.

By simply adding and subtracting the first two equations, it becomes immediately apparent that the system is equivalent to the following:

\displaystyle|S_{++}|

\displaystyle=|S_{--}|,

(2b)

\displaystyle|S_{-+}|

\displaystyle=|S_{+-}|,

(2c)

\displaystyle|S_{++}|+|S_{+-}|

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}.

To finish specifying our program, we maintain constraint (

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}=1,\quad f\geq 0.

(2e)

4.1
Optimization over Opposing Orthants

We begin by analyzing the points in the opposing orthants. Because of the balance constraints established in (
2b
) and (
2c
), we can group the terms of our objective (
2a
) into paired components:

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{++}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{--}}g(x_{i})\right)+\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{+-}}g(x_{i})+\sum_{x_{i}\in S_{-+}}g(x_{i})\right).

For each component, the equal sizes of the opposite sets allow us to form a convenient bijection. By pairing each point
a\in S_{++}
with another point
b\in S_{--}
, and similarly for
S_{+-}
and
S_{-+}
, we effectively rewrite the entire objective as a sum of pairwise contributions. As a result, determining when (
2a
) is nonnegative reduces to lower bounding the joint cost
g(a)+g(b)
of any such opposite pair
(a,b)
. We establish this bound using the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f\geq 0
be a facility location. For any pair of points
(a,b)
from opposite orthants, i.e.,
(a,b)\in(S_{+-}\times S_{-+})\cup(S_{++}\times S_{--})
, the following statements hold:

1.

If
1\leq q\leq p
, then
\|a-f\|_{q}^{p}+\|b-f\|_{q}^{p}\geq 2^{1-p}\left(\|a\|_{q}^{p}+\|b\|_{q}^{p}\right)
.

2.

If
1\leq p<q
, then
\|a-f\|_{q}^{p}+\|b-f\|_{q}^{p}\geq 2^{p/q-p}\left(\|a\|_{q}^{p}+\|b\|_{q}^{p}\right)
.

Proof.

We first note that since
a
and
b
are in opposing orthants, they have opposite signs in each coordinate (or are zero). Thus, for any coordinate
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have
|a_{i}-b_{i}|=|a_{i}|+|b_{i}|
.
This guarantees that for any
r\geq 1
, the
r
-norm of their difference simplifies to:

\|a-b\|_{r}^{r}=(|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|)^{r}+(|a_{2}|+|b_{2}|)^{r}.

We will use this observation when proving each of the statements.

We begin with the first statement.
Suppose that
1\leq q\leq p
. We can derive that

\displaystyle\|a-f\|_{q}^{p}+\|b-f\|_{q}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{1-p}\left(\|a-f\|_{q}+\|b-f\|_{q}\right)^{p}

(Jensen’s inequality)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{1-p}\|(a-f)+(f-b)\|_{q}^{p}

(Minkowski’s inequality)

\displaystyle=2^{1-p}\|a-b\|_{q}^{p}

\displaystyle=2^{1-p}\left(\|a-b\|_{q}^{q}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle=2^{1-p}\left((|a_{1}|+|b_{1}|)^{q}+(|a_{2}|+|b_{2}|)^{q}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{1-p}\left(|a_{1}|^{q}+|b_{1}|^{q}+|a_{2}|^{q}+|b_{2}|^{q}\right)^{p/q}

\displaystyle=2^{1-p}\left(\|a\|_{q}^{q}+\|b\|_{q}^{q}\right)^{p/q}

Proposition 14
.

If
p/q\in(0,1)
then
\mathtt{UB}(p,q)\leq 1+2^{1/p}

Proof.

By definition, we have

\mathtt{UB}(p,q)=\begin{cases}1/\delta_{2}&\text{if }p=1,\\

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(2+2^{1-1/p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\gamma^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

\displaystyle=\left(\left(1+2^{1/p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(2^{1/p}+1\right)\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(1+2^{1/p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle=1+2^{1/p}.

(23)

\displaystyle\delta_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{r\delta_{1}(1-a^{*})^{r-1}}{2-r(a^{*})^{r-1}}

It remains to lower bound the constant

Recall we defined
\delta_{1}=\left(1-\gamma^{\frac{p}{1-r}}\right)^{1-r}

.
Furthermore, raising a value in

strictly increases it. Thus, we have

\delta_{1}>(1-\gamma^{p})^{1-r}>1-\gamma^{p}.

Substituting

1-\gamma^{p}=1-\frac{1}{1+2^{1/p}}=\frac{2^{1/p}}{1+2^{1/p}}=\frac{2}{2+2^{1-1/p}}.

Therefore,

Conclusion

6.1
Discussion and Future Directions

Our work illustrates that the median mechanism is highly effective at providing an approximately optimal facility location while incentivizing agents to be truthful.
We provide its exact approximation ratio in
\ell_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and bound the approximation ratio by
3
in
\ell_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p
-norm social costs.
There are several directions for future work and open questions. We outline them below.

Is the median mechanism optimal among deterministic strategyproof mechanisms for
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under an arbitrary
p
-norm social cost? This is indeed the case when
d\in\{1,2\}

(
17
;
25
)
, but our understanding beyond this is considerably limited. While there are well-known characterizations for strategyproof mechanisms in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

(
9
;
37
)
, we do not have similarly crisp characterizations for higher dimensions. New tools may need to be developed for the more general setting.

Another enticing direction is determining the extent to which randomized strategyproof-in-expectation mechanisms can achieve better approximation ratios than deterministic strategyproof mechanisms.
5
recently introduced a randomized strategyproof mechanism with a better approximation ratio than the CM mechanism in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for the utilitarian social cost.
For each
p
-norm social cost, does there exist a randomized strategyproof-in-expectation mechanism with a better approximation ratio than the CM mechanism in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
? Is there a single randomized strategyproof-in-expectation mechanism that
simultaneously
achieves a better approximation for all
p
-norm social costs in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
? Does the same hold in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
?
