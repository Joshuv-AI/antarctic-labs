ABSTRACT: MQ resins are highly branched organosiloxane polymers used to formulate silicone elastomers, coatings, adhesive,
and cosmetics. The tetra functional units (Q) form a matrix that is capped with mono functional units (M). Due to the competitive
growth of the sol matrix (Q-Q bonding) and end-capping condensation (M-Q bonding) controlled, reproducible synthesis of MQ
resins is challenging. Additionally, the properties of the MQ resin are very sensitive to small changes in reaction and workup
conditions. This work explores the effects that various synthetic conditions such as reaction temperature, reagent equivalences, and
reaction time have on the resulting MQ resin. The goal was to create a liquid MQ resin that was reproducible. MQ resins were
compared based on their qualitative properties as well as M:Q ratio as determined by NMR. Ongoing work includes further
investigation into the effects of other reaction conditions as well as MQ resin characterization using thermogravimetric analysis,
differential scanning calorimetry, and rheological techniques.

INTRODUCTION
MQ copolymers, or MQ resins are organosiloxane polymers
consisting of mono- (M) and tetra (Q) functional units. MQ
resins have a complete absence of insoluble crosslinked
structures after prolonged high-temperature condensation, and
they have good solubility in both organic solvents and PDMS1.
This makes them an excellent modifier for silicone polymers.
MQ resins are widely used to formulate silicone elastomers,
coatings, adhesives, and cosmetics 2.
The ratio of M to Q units is an important structural parameter
and is closely associated with the quantity of organic substitutes
as well as the topological structures of MQ resins3. An ideal
structure of MQ copolymer at a ratio of M:Q = 1:1 is a cubane,
but the actual structure typically has more complexity and
defects (Figure 1) 1.
For a single MQ resin, three fractions with various properties
can be observed (Figure 1)1. One model describes the MQ-
copolymer system as a molecular composite where individual
fractions are mixed with each other 1. In this model, high-
molecular formations act as a filler, middle fractions act as a
polymer matrix, and low-molecular copolymers act as a
