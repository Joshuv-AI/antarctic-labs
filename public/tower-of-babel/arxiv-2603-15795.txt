On the central derivatives of L-functions and modularity of Heenger cycles
Tuoping Du, Zhifeng Peng

Abstract
. This paper establishes an arithmetic intersection formula for central L L -derivatives in higher weight. We prove that for a general cusp form (extending the previous result for newforms), the derivative is represented by the global height pairing between higher Heegner cycles. This result provides a framework for the Gross–Zagier–Zhang formula and its generalizations. Furthermore, we investigate the modularity of the generating series of Heegner cycles, proving a weak version of the conjecture and reducing the full modularity to a vanishing conjecture, for which we provide supporting evidence.

Introduction

The study of central derivatives of
L
-functions lies at the heart of modern number theory. A cornerstone of this field is the Gross–Zagier formula
[
GZ
]
, which connects the first derivative of an elliptic curve’s
L
-function at the central point to the height of a Heegner point. This result has since become a fundamental tool in the study of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer (BSD) conjecture, providing a powerful geometric approach to understanding ranks of elliptic curves. Zhang’s later extension
[
Zh
]
of this framework to higher-weight newforms further broadened its scope, expressing central derivatives of
L
-functions in terms of heights of Heegner cycles.

Building upon this geometric foundation, Bruinier and Yang
[
BY
]
pioneered a new direction by studying the central derivatives of
L
-functions associated with more general cusp forms. Later, a new arithmetic representation conjecture was proposed in both
[
BEY
]
and
[
Du
]
. In this paper, we focus on the case of the trivial character, where the underlying structure allows for a more explicit analysis.

Within this setting, we make three main contributions. First, we formulate a precise modularity conjecture for an arithmetic generating function. Second, we prove a weak version of this conjecture, showing that the full modularity property follows from the vanishing of certain Heegner cycles. Third, we establish an explicit formula that provides direct evidence for a related vanishing conjecture. By exploring these three interconnected questions, we aim to advance the understanding of central
L
-derivatives and their role in the broader arithmetic landscape.

We define the quadratic space

(0.1)

V=\{w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}w_{1}&w_{2}\\
w_{3}&-w_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}):\operatorname{tr}(w)=0\},

equipped with the quadratic form
Q(w)=N\cdot\det w
. We then define the lattice

(0.2)

L=\big\{w=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}b&\frac{a}{N}\\
c&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}\subset V.

L^{\sharp}
denote the dual lattice of

, and let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be the full inverse image of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
under the two-fold metaplectic cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

The Weil representation
\rho_{L}
acting on the finite-dimensional group algebra
\mathbb{C}[L^{\sharp}/L]
, which has a standard basis
\{e_{\mu}\mid\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L\}
.

Let
H_{k,\rho_{L}}
be the space of harmonic weak Maass forms of weight
k
with respect to
\rho_{L}
. The differential operator

\xi_{k}:H_{k,\rho_{L}}\rightarrow S_{2-k,\overline{\rho}_{L}}

maps such forms to cusp forms of weight
2-k
and contragredient representation
\overline{\rho}_{L}
.

For each

and each positive rational number

on the modular curve

]

[

The Gross–Zagier formula
[

was extended to higher weight by Zhang
[

, who showed that for any eigenform

Let

denote the Heegner cycle over

in

. In

for higher Heegner cycles, which generalizes the Gillet–Soulé height introduced in
[

The

For any negative definite two-dimensional subspace

is defined analogously.

Given

where

Let

denote the space of Heegner cycles on

\mathrm{Pin}

[

Let

\xi_{3/2-\kappa}(f)=\frac{1}{\langle g,~g\rangle_{Pet}}g.

We now define the generating function

\phi_{\kappa}=\sum_{m>0,\mu}Z_{\kappa}(m,\mu)q^{m}e_{\mu}\in\mathrm{Heeg_{{\kappa}}(X)}\otimes\mathbb{C}[[q]].

(Modularity)

Plan of the Proof

In Section
1
, we review foundational results on modular forms and Heegner divisors.

In Section
2
, we introduce the regularized theta lift, with particular emphasis on the lift associated to non-holomorphic Hejhal-Poincaré series. This construction yields higher Green functions for higher Heegner cycles.

In Section
3
, we recall the moduli stacks of Heegner divisors and the height pairing of Heegner cycles, as developed by Zhang
[

]
. This global pairing decomposes as a sum of local intersection numbers.

In Section

, we compute the local intersection numbers
\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle_{p}
at each prime
p
, including both archimedean and non-archimedean contributions.

In Section
5
, we combine the local results to complete the proof of Theorem
0.6
, thereby obtaining an explicit formula for the global height pairing
\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle
.

In Section
6
, we investigate the modularity of Heegner cycles and prove Theorem
0.3
. Furthermore, we reduce Conjecture
0.1
to Conjecture
0.4
, establishing that the modularity property hinges on the vanishing of
Z_{\kappa}(f)
for all weakly holomorphic modular forms
f
.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the metaplectic double cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, consisting of pairs
(g,\phi(g,\tau))
, where
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\ c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi(g,\tau)
is a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
satisfying
\phi(g,\tau)^{2}=j(g,\tau)=c\tau+d
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}=\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be the preimage of
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
in
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\Gamma^{\prime}
is generated by

S=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\sqrt{\tau}\right)\quad T=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),1\right).

Let
(V,Q)
be a quadratic space of signature
(p,q)
and
L\subset V
an even lattice. Denote by
L^{\sharp}
the dual lattice of
L
. The quadratic form on
L
induces a
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
-valued quadratic form on the discriminant group
L^{\sharp}/L
. Let
{e_{\mu}\mid\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L}
be the standard basis of the group algebra
S_{L}=\mathbb{C}[L^{\sharp}/L]
. The Weil representation
\rho_{L}
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\sharp}/L]
(see
[
Bo1
]
) is defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(T)e_{\mu}

\displaystyle=e(Q(\mu))e_{\mu},

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(S)e_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{e(-\frac{p-q}{8})}{\sqrt{|L^{\sharp}/L|}}\sum\limits_{\mu^{\prime}\in L^{\sharp}/L}e(-(\mu,\mu^{\prime}))e_{\mu^{\prime}}.

1.1.
Modular Forms

A twice continuously differentiable function
f:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[L^{\sharp}/L]
is called a weak Maass form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and representation
\rho_{L}
if it satisfies:

(1)

f\mid_{k,\rho_{L}}\gamma=f
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}
;

(2)

There exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Delta_{k}f=\lambda f
;

(3)

There exists
C>0
such that
f(\tau)=O(e^{Cv})
as
v\rightarrow\infty
, uniformly in
u
.

The slash operator is defined by:

f\mid_{k,\rho_{L}}\gamma(\tau)=\phi(\tau)^{-2k}\rho_{L}^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime})f(\gamma\tau).

When
\lambda=0
,
f
is called a harmonic weak Maass form. Denote by
H_{k,\rho_{L}}
the space of such forms. The principal part of
f
is defined as:

P_{f}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{n\leq 0}c(n,\mu)q^{n}e_{\mu}.

Every
f\in H_{k,\rho_{L}}
admits a unique decomposition
f=f^{+}+f^{-}
with Fourier expansions:

(1.2)

\displaystyle f^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c^{+}(n,\mu)e(n\tau)e_{\mu},

(1.3)

\displaystyle f^{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{n<0}c^{-}(n,\mu)\Gamma(1-k,2\pi|n|v)e(n\tau)e_{\mu},

where
\Gamma(a,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{a-1}dt
is the incomplete Gamma function.

For any field
F
, let
H_{k,\rho_{L}}(F)
denote the subspace of forms whose principal part has coefficients in
F
.

The
\xi
-operator
\xi_{k}:H_{k,\overline{\rho}_{L}}\rightarrow S_{2-k,\rho_{L}}
is defined by:

\xi_{k}(f)=2iv^{k}\frac{\overline{\partial f}}{\partial\bar{\tau}},

giving the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow M_{k,\overline{\rho}_{L}}^{!}\rightarrow H_{k,\overline{\rho}_{L}}\xrightarrow{\xi_{k}}S_{2-k,\rho_{L}}\rightarrow 0,

where
M_{k,\overline{\rho}_{L}}^{!}
is the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms.

The Maass lowering and raising operators are defined as:

L_{k}=-2iv^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\tau}},\quad R_{k}=2i\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+kv^{-1}.

The standard scalar product on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\sharp}/L]
is:

\left\langle\sum_{\mu}f_{\mu}e_{\mu},\sum_{\mu}g_{\mu}e_{\mu}\right\rangle=\sum_{\mu}f_{\mu}g_{\mu}.

For
f,g\in M_{k,\rho_{L}}
, the Petersson scalar product is:

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{Pet}}=\int_{\mathcal{F}}\langle f(\tau),\overline{g(\tau)}\rangle v^{k}\,\mu(\tau),

where
\mu(\tau)=\frac{du\,dv}{v^{2}}
is the hyperbolic measure.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}
be a congruence subgroup and
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. For
f\in M_{k}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
and
g\in M_{l}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})
, the
j
-th Rankin-Cohen bracket is:

[f,g]_{j}=\sum_{s=0}^{j}(-1)^{s}\binom{k+j-1}{s}\binom{l+j-1}{j-s}f^{(j-s)}g^{(s)}\in M_{k+l+2j}(\Gamma^{\prime\prime}),

where
g^{(s)}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{s}}\frac{\partial^{s}g}{\partial\tau^{s}}
.

1.2.
Eisenstein Series

Let
V
be a quadratic space of dimension
m
. Define the character

\chi_{V}(x)=(x,(-1)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\det(V))_{\mathbb{A}},

where
\det(V)
denotes the Gram determinant of
V
.

For any standard section
\Phi(g,s)
in the induced representation
I(s,\chi_{V})
, define the Eisenstein series by

E(g,s,\Phi)=\sum_{\gamma\in P(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})}\Phi(\gamma g,s),

where
P(\mathbb{Q})
is the parabolic subgroup.

There exists an equivalent map

S(V)\rightarrow I(s_{0},\chi_{V}),\quad s_{0}=\frac{m}{2}-1,

such that

\Phi(g,s_{0})=\omega(g)\varphi(0),

where
\omega
denotes the Weil representation.

Let
\Phi^{l}_{\infty}
be the unique archimedean standard section satisfying

\Phi^{l}_{\infty}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},s\right)=e^{il\theta},\quad\theta\in[0,2\pi].

For
\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L
, define
\varphi_{\mu}=\text{char}(L_{\mu})\in S(V(\mathbb{A}_{f}))
. The weight
l
Eisenstein series is defined by

E_{L}(\tau,s;l)=v^{-\frac{l}{2}}\sum_{\mu}E(g_{\tau},s,\Phi^{l}_{\infty}\otimes\lambda(\varphi_{\mu}))\varphi_{\mu}.

Identifying
\varphi_{\mu}
with
e_{\mu}
, it follows from
[
BY
, Section 2]
that

E_{L}(\tau,s;l)=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\infty}^{\prime}\backslash\Gamma^{\prime}}\left(v^{\frac{s+1-l}{2}}e_{0}\right)\mid_{l,\rho_{L}}\gamma^{\prime}.

When
V
is a quadratic space of signature
(0,2)
, the central value
E_{L}(\tau,s;1)
vanishes at
s_{0}=0
, while
E_{L}(\tau,s;-1)
is holomorphic at
s_{0}
. These Eisenstein series satisfy the relation

L_{1}E_{L}(\tau,s;1)=\frac{s}{2}E_{L}(\tau,s;-1),

where
L_{l}=-2iv^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\tau}}
is the Maass lowering operator of weight
l
.

The derivative

E^{\prime}_{L}(\tau,s_{0};1)=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L}\sum_{n}b_{\mu}(n,v)q^{n}e_{\mu}

is a harmonic weak Maass form of weight
1
. Denote its holomorphic part by

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}_{L}(\tau)=\sum_{\mu}\sum_{n\geq 0}\kappa(n,\mu)q^{n}e_{\mu}.

The coefficients are given explicitly (see
[
Ku
,
Sch
]
) by

\kappa(n,\mu)=\begin{cases}\lim_{v\to\infty}b_{0}(0,v)-\log v,&\text{if }n=0\text{ and }\mu=0,\\
\lim_{v\to\infty}b_{\mu}(n,v),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that
\kappa(n,\mu)=0
for
n<0
, and
\kappa(0,\mu)=0
for
\mu\neq 0
. These coefficients have been computed via local methods in
[
BY
]
.

1.3.
Heegner Divisors

In this work, we consider the quadratic space

V=\left\{w=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}\\
x_{3}&-x_{1}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\right\},

equipped with the quadratic form
Q(w)=N\cdot\det(w)
. Define the lattice

L=\left\{w=\begin{pmatrix}b&-\frac{a}{N}\\
c&-b\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,\middle|\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},

and its dual lattice

L^{\sharp}=\left\{w=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{b}{2N}&-\frac{a}{N}\\
c&-\frac{b}{2N}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,\middle|\,a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

We identify

\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}\cong L^{\sharp}/L,\quad r\mapsto\mu_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{r}{2N}&0\\
0&-\frac{r}{2N}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the Hermitian domain of oriented negative 2-dimensional subspaces of
V(\mathbb{R})
. We identify
\mathbb{D}
with
\mathbb{H}\cup\overline{\mathbb{H}}
via

z=x+iy\mapsto\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathfrak{R}\begin{pmatrix}z&-z^{2}\\
1&-z\end{pmatrix}+\mathbb{R}\cdot\mathfrak{I}\begin{pmatrix}z&-z^{2}\\
1&-z\end{pmatrix}.

For
w=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{b}{2N}&-\frac{a}{N}\\
c&-\frac{b}{2N}\end{pmatrix}\in L^{\sharp}
, the associated CM point is

z(w)=\frac{b}{2Nc}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4Nac}}{2N|c|}\in\mathbb{H}.

For
\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L
and
m\in Q(\mu)+\mathbb{Z}
with
m>0
, let

L_{\mu}=L+\mu,\quad L_{\mu}[m]=\{w\in L_{\mu}\mid Q(w)=m\}.

The Heegner divisor on
X_{0}(N)
is defined by

(1.5)

Z(m,\mu):=\sum_{w\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\setminus L_{\mu}[m]}z(w),

which is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Now let
U\subset V
be a quadratic subspace of signature
(0,2)
, identified with an imaginary quadratic field
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{0}})
. The two points
\{z_{U}^{\pm}\}
correspond to the two orientations of
U(\mathbb{R})
.

The CM divisor is defined as

Z(U)=k^{\times}\backslash\{z_{U}^{\pm}\}\times\mathbb{A}_{k,f}^{\times}/\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{k}^{\times}\cong\{z_{U}^{\pm}\}\times\mathrm{Cl}_{k},

where
\mathrm{Cl}_{k}
denotes the ideal class group of
k
.

When
D_{0}=-4Nm_{0}
is a fundamental discriminant, we have

Z(U)\simeq Z(m_{0},\mu_{0}),

so
Z(U)
is also a Heegner divisor.

2.
Regularized theta lift

2.1.
Theta Functions

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have the orthogonal decomposition

V(\mathbb{R})=z\oplus z^{\perp}.

For any
w\in V(\mathbb{R})
, write
w=w_{z}+w_{z^{\perp}}
accordingly.

Define the associated majorant

(w,w)_{z}=(w_{z^{\perp}},w_{z^{\perp}})-(w_{z},w_{z}),

which is a positive definite quadratic form on
V(\mathbb{R})
. Consider the Gaussian

\varphi_{\infty}(w,z)=e^{-\pi(w,w)_{z}}\in S(V(\mathbb{R})).

For
\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{H}
, let
g_{\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}v^{1/2}&0\\
0&v^{-1/2}\end{pmatrix}
. Under the Weil representation, define

\varphi(w,\tau,z)=v^{1/4}\omega(g_{\tau})\varphi_{\infty}(w,z)=v\cdot e\left(Q(w_{z^{\perp}})\tau+Q(w_{z})\bar{\tau}\right).

The Siegel theta function is defined by

\Theta(\tau,z)=\sum_{\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L}\sum_{w\in L_{\mu}}\varphi(w,\tau,z)e_{\mu}=v\sum_{\mu\in L^{\sharp}/L}\sum_{w\in L_{\mu}}e\left(Q(w_{z^{\perp}})\tau+Q(w_{z})\bar{\tau}\right).

This is a modular form of weight
-\frac{1}{2}
, satisfying

\Theta(\gamma\tau,z)=\phi(\tau)^{-1}\rho_{L}(\gamma^{\prime})\Theta(\tau,z).

More generally, for a quadratic space
V
of signature
(n,2)
, one can define a Siegel theta function of weight
\frac{n}{2}-1
.

For
z=x+yi\in\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle-\sqrt{D_{0}D_{1}}^{\,2j+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\equiv r_{1}r_{0}\pmod{2N}\\
n^{2}\leq D_{0}D_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{0}}}{D_{0}}\kappa\left(\frac{D_{0}D_{1}-n^{2}}{4N|D_{0}|},\frac{\tilde{2}n}{\sqrt{D_{0}}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{4N|D_{0}|}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{s=0}^{j}\binom{j+1/2}{s}\binom{j}{s}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{D_{0}D_{1}}}\right)^{2j+1-2s}\left(\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{D_{0}D_{1}}}\right)^{2}-1\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m_{1}^{\frac{2j+1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\equiv r_{1}r_{0}\pmod{2N}\\
n^{2}\leq D_{0}D_{1}\end{subarray}}\kappa\left(\frac{D_{0}D_{1}-n^{2}}{4N|D_{0}|},\frac{\tilde{2}n}{\sqrt{D_{0}}}\right)\beta_{2j+1}\left(\frac{n}{\sqrt{D_{0}D_{1}}}\right).

Since
\beta_{2j+1}(-x)=-\beta_{2j+1}(x)
, the
(m_{1},-\mu_{1})

∎

5.1.
Height Pairings

For any CM point
x
in
X_{0}(N)
, the Heegner cycle
S_{\kappa}(x)
was defined by Zhang
[
Zh
]
. He constructed an arithmetic cycle on
\mathcal{Y}
as

\hat{S}_{\kappa}(x)=(S_{\kappa}(\bar{x}),g_{\kappa}(x)).

The height pairing is defined by

\langle S_{\kappa}(x),S_{\kappa}(y)\rangle=(-1)^{\kappa}\langle\hat{S}_{\kappa}(x)\cdot\hat{S}_{\kappa}(y)\rangle_{\text{GS}},

and decomposes into local contributions:

(5.1)

\langle S_{\kappa}(x),S_{\kappa}(y)\rangle=\sum_{p\leq\infty}\langle S_{\kappa}(x),S_{\kappa}(y)\rangle_{p}.

Moreover,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle S_{\kappa}(x),S_{\kappa}(y)\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}G_{N,\kappa}(x,y),

(5.3)

\displaystyle\langle S_{\kappa}(x),S_{\kappa}(y)\rangle_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\kappa}(S_{\kappa}(\bar{x})\cdot S_{\kappa}(\bar{y}))_{p},

where
G_{N,\kappa}(x,y)
is the higher Green function.

For the cycles
Z_{\kappa}(f)
and
Z_{\kappa}(m_{0},\mu_{0})
, we have:

(5.4)

\displaystyle\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(m_{0},\mu_{0})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\kappa}\langle\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\kappa}(f),\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\kappa}(m_{0},\mu_{0})\rangle_{\text{GS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle_{\infty}+\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle_{\text{fin}}.

We now compute both terms.

Lemma 5.1
.

The Green currents for the Heegner cycles
Z_{\kappa}(m,\mu)
and
Z_{\kappa}(f)
are given by

(-1)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{j}(z,F_{m,\mu})\quad\text{and}\quad(-1)^{\kappa}\Phi^{j}(z,f),

respectively.

Proof.

By equation (
5.2
), the Green current of a Heegner cycle is given by the higher Green function.

For
Z_{\kappa}(m,\mu)
, the multiplicity
m^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}
implies that the current equals

m^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}G_{N,\kappa}(x,Z(m,\mu)).

From Proposition
2.2
, we have

m^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}G_{N,\kappa}(x,Z(m,\mu))=(-1)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{j}(z,F_{m,\mu}).

Since
Z_{\kappa}(f)=\sum_{m>0}c^{+}(-m,\mu)Z_{\kappa}(m,\mu)
, linearity gives the Green current of
Z_{\kappa}(f)
as
(-1)^{\kappa}\Phi^{j}(z,f)
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The archimedean contribution is given by

\displaystyle\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\kappa}\frac{m_{0}^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}\deg(Z(U))}{2}CT_{f}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\Gamma(\kappa-\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{N}\,L^{\prime}(Sh_{m_{0},\mu_{0}}(\xi_{3/2-\kappa}f),\kappa)}{(4\pi)^{\kappa-1}\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have

\langle Z_{\kappa}(f),Z_{\kappa}(U)\rangle_{\infty}=(-1)^{\kappa}\cdot\frac{m_{0}^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}}{2}\Phi^{j}(Z(U),f).

According to Theorem
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{j}(Z(U),f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg(Z(U))CT_{f}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\kappa}\frac{2^{4-2\kappa}\Gamma(\kappa-\frac{1}{2})\sqrt{N}}{m_{0}^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{1}{2}+\kappa}}L^{\prime}(Sh_{m_{0},\mu_{0}}(g),\kappa).

Substituting and simplifying yields the result.
∎

5.2.

.

For any

and Heegner cycle

By equation (
