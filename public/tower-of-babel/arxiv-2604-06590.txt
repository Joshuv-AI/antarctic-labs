When Majority Fails: Tight Bounds for Correlation Distillation Conjectures
Pritish Kamath, Ravi Kumar, Pasin Manurangsi

Abstract
We study two conjectures posed in the analysis of Boolean functions f : { − 1 , 1 } n → { − 1 , 1 } f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\} , in both of which, the Majority function plays a central role: the “Majority is Least Stable” ( Benjamini et al. 1999 ) and the “Non-Interactive Correlation Distillation for Erasures” ( Yang 2004 ; O’Donnell and Wright 2012 ) . While both conjectures have been refuted in their originally stated form, we obtain a nearly tight characterization of the noise parameter regime in which each of the conjectures hold, for all n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . Whereas, for n = 3 n=3 , both conjectures hold in all noise parameter regimes. We state refined versions of both conjectures that we believe captures the spirit of the original conjectures.

When the conjectures are false?

2.1
Majority is Least Stable (MLS)

Conjecture 3

(MLS,

)
.

For any odd integer
n

, it holds that
\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[f]\geq\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]
.

Gopi 2013
proposed a counterexample to the conjecture for all odd
n\geq 5
. To define this counterexample, let
h=(n-1)/2
, and partition
[n]
into
A=[h+1]=\{1,\ldots,h+1\}
and
B=[n]\smallsetminus A=\{h+2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
e=\mathds{1}_{A}\circ-\mathds{1}_{B}
. Define
g_{n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
as

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(x)&x\in\{e,-e\},\\
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(x)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2)

In other words,
g_{n}
is almost the same as
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}
except at two antipodal points at the boundary that are negated. As noted in
(
Gopi 2013
)
,

is indeed an unbiased LTF, as it can be expressed as
\mathsf{sign}\left(a\cdot\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}+b\cdot\sum_{i\in B}x_{i}\right)
for any odd positive integers
a,b
such that
\frac{b}{a}\in\left(1+\frac{1}{h},1+\frac{1}{h-2}\right)
. (For example,

and

Theorem 4

(

)
.

For every odd

, there is
\rho_{n}>0

\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[g_{n}]<\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]
for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{n})
.

Later,
Biswas and Sarkar 2023
show that the MLS conjecture holds for all
\rho\in(0,1)
when
n\leq 3
.
Thus, the above counterexample is minimal.
Additionally, they also independently discovered a different counterexample and proved a statement similar to
Theorem

4
. Nevertheless, an interesting question remains: Could the counterexample be extended to hold for all
\rho\in(0,1)
?
Gopi 2013
partially answered this question, by showing that, for any fixed
n
, there exists a small neighborhood of
\rho
around 1 in which the MLS conjecture holds:

Theorem 5

(
Gopi 2013
)
.

For every odd

, there exists
\zeta_{n}>0
such that, for all
\rho\in[1-\zeta_{n},1]

\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[f]\geq\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]

However, this does not answer, e.g., if for a fixed

Theorem 9

Let

.

(i)

\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[g_{n}]<\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]

(ii)

\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]>\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]

4.1
Useful Properties of Binomials

We start by a couple of useful lemmata on binomial distributions. First is a lemma that relates
\mathrm{Bin}(h+1,p)
and
\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)
:

Lemma 12
.

For any
p\in[0,1]
and
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\Ex_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h+1,p)\\
u^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)\end{subarray}}\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[u>u^{\prime}]}\right]=(1-2p)\Pr_{v,v^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)}[v=v^{\prime}]=(1-2p)\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,1-p)}[w=w^{\prime}]

Proof.

Write
u=v+m
where
v\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)
and
m\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)
are independent. Since
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
are identically distributed, we have

\displaystyle\Ex_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h+1,p)\\
u^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)\end{subarray}}\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[u>u^{\prime}]}\right]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,u^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)\\
m\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)\end{subarray}}\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[v+m>u^{\prime}]}\right]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,u^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)\\
m\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left((-1)^{\mathds{1}[v+m>u^{\prime}]}+(-1)^{\mathds{1}[u^{\prime}+m>v]}\right)\right].

Notice that the term
(-1)^{\mathds{1}[v+m>u^{\prime}]}+(-1)^{\mathds{1}[u^{\prime}+m>v]}=0
unless
u^{\prime}=v
. Thus, the RHS equals

\displaystyle\Pr_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}[u^{\prime}=v^{\prime}]\cdot\Ex_{m\sim\mathrm{Ber}(p)}\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[m>0]}\right]=\Pr_{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}[u^{\prime}=v^{\prime}]\cdot(1-2p),

which yields the first equality.

The second equality follows simply by substituting
w=h-v,w^{\prime}=h-v^{\prime}
.
∎

The second lemma gives a lower bound on the probability that two i.i.d. binomial random variables are equal. The form of the lower bound might look peculiar, but this is exactly what we will need subsequently.

Lemma 13
.

For any
h\in\mathbb{N}
such that
h\geq 2
and any
q\in\left(\frac{1}{(h-1)^{2}+2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle(1-2q)\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,q)}[w=w^{\prime}]+q^{2h+1}-(1-q)^{2h+1}>0.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,q)}[w=w^{\prime}]=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\Pr_{w}[w=i]\cdot\Pr_{w^{\prime}}[w^{\prime}=i]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{h}\left(\binom{h}{i}q^{i}(1-q)^{h-i}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-q)^{2h}+h^{2}q^{2}(1-q)^{2h-2}+q^{4}(1-q)^{2h-4},

where the inequality follows by considering only
the terms corresponding to
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, and using the rough lower bound
\binom{h}{2}\geq 1
.

Using
q\leq 1/2
and the above inequality, we can thus bound the desired quantity as follows:

\displaystyle(1-2q)\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,q)}[w=w^{\prime}]+q^{2h+1}-(1-q)^{2h+1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2q)\left((1-q)^{2h}+h^{2}q^{2}(1-q)^{2h-2}+q^{4}(1-q)^{2h-4}-\sum_{j=0}^{2h}q^{j}(1-q)^{2h-j}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-2q)\left((h^{2}-1)q^{2}(1-q)^{2h-2}-q(1-q)^{2h-1}-(2h-3)q^{3}(1-q)^{2h-3}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-2q)\left((h^{2}-2h+2)q^{2}(1-q)^{2h-2}-q(1-q)^{2h-1}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-2q)q(1-q)^{2h-2}\left((h^{2}-2h+3)q-1\right)

\displaystyle>0,

where the last inequality is due to our assumption that
q\in\left(\frac{1}{(h-1)^{2}+2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
.
∎

4.2
Characterization of the Gaps

We now establish a simple formula of the differences
\Phi_{q}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{q}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]
and
\mathsf{Stab}_{1-2q}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]-\mathsf{Stab}_{1-2q}[g_{n}]
based on binomial distributions. In fact, the two quantities are equal, up to an appropriate scaling, as stated below.

Theorem 14
.

For all odd
n\geq 3
and
q\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle 2^{n-2}\cdot(\Phi_{q}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{q}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}])

\displaystyle=2^{n-3}\cdot(\mathsf{Stab}_{1-2q}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]-\mathsf{Stab}_{1-2q}[g_{n}])

\displaystyle=(1-2q)\Pr_{z,z^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(\frac{n-1}{2},q\right)}\left[z=z^{\prime}\right]+q^{n}-(1-q)^{n}.

Note that
Theorem

9
immediately follows from
Theorem

14
and
Lemma

13
(using
h=\frac{n-1}{2}
).

Proof of
Theorem

14
.

We will separately derive the formula for
\mathsf{Stab}
and
\Phi
. Let
\delta_{n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-2,0,2\}
be given as
\delta_{n}(\bm{x})=\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-g_{n}(\bm{x})
. From definition of
g_{n}
(
2
), it follows that
\delta_{n}(e)=2
,
\delta_{n}(-e)=-2
and
\delta_{n}(\bm{x})=0
for all other
\bm{x}
.

Derivation for
\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}
.

Let
\rho=1-2q
. For
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\sim\mathsf{DSBS}_{\rho}
, we can write the difference between stabilities as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]-\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[g_{n}]

\displaystyle=\Ex\left[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})-g_{n}(\bm{x})g_{n}(\bm{y})\right]

\displaystyle=\Ex\left[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})-\left(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\right)\left(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})-\delta_{n}(\bm{y})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=2\Ex[\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})]-\Ex[\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\delta_{n}(\bm{y})].

Since
\delta_{n}
is only supported at
e
and
-e
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\Ex[\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\delta_{n}(\bm{y})]=\frac{1}{2^{n-3}}\left(\left(\frac{1+\rho}{2}\right)^{n}-\left(\frac{1-\rho}{2}\right)^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{2^{n-3}}\left((1-q)^{n}-q^{n}\right).

To compute the first expectation, using the fact that both
\delta_{n}
and
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}
are odd functions, we have

\displaystyle\Ex[\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})]

\displaystyle=\Ex[\delta_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})\mid x_{1}=1]=\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\Ex[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})\mid\bm{x}=e]\,,

where the second inequality is due to the fact that, conditioned on
x_{1}=1
,
\delta_{n}(\bm{x})
is only non-zero when
\bm{x}=e
. Now, let
n_{A}=|\{i\in A\mid y_{i}=1\}|
and
n_{B}=|\{i\in B\mid y_{i}=-1\}|
.
We have that
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{y})=(-1)^{\mathds{1}[n_{A}>n_{B}]}
. Combining all these, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]-\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[g_{n}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-3}}\left(\Ex\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[n_{A}>n_{B}]}\right]+q^{n}-(1-q)^{n}.\right)

Notice that, conditioned on
\bm{x}=e
, we have
n_{A}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(h+1,\frac{1+\rho}{2}\right)=\mathrm{Bin}\left(h+1,1-q\right)
and
n_{B}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\left(h,\frac{1+\rho}{2}\right)=\mathrm{Bin}\left(h,1-q\right)
. Thus, from
Lemma

12
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]-\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[g_{n}]=\frac{1}{2^{n-3}}\left((1-2q)\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,q)}[w=w^{\prime}]+q^{n}-(1-q)^{n}\right),

as desired.

Derivation for
\Phi_{p}
.

We are interested in
\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]
for
p=q
. Let
S\subseteq[n]
be generated by including each element of
[n]
independently with probability
p
, and let
\bm{x}_{S}\in\{-1,1\}^{S}
be uniformly at random. We also write
\overline{S}
to denote
[n]\smallsetminus S
. Note that
\Phi_{p}
can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}[f]:=\Ex_{S}\left[\Ex_{\bm{x}_{S}}\left[\left|\Ex_{\bm{x}_{\bar{S}}}[f(\bm{x})]\right|\right]\right]

Thus, we can write the desired quantity as

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{S}\Ex_{\bm{x}_{S}}\left[\frac{1}{2^{n-|S|}}\cdot h_{S}(\bm{x}_{S})\right],

where

\displaystyle\textstyle h_{S}(\bm{x}_{S}):=\left|\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}\ g_{n}(\bm{x}_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})\right|-\left|\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}\ \mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})\right|.

For notational convenience, write
e_{S}
as the restriction of
e
on
S
.
Now, if
\bm{x}_{S}\notin\{e_{S},-e_{S}\}
, then we immediately have
g_{n}(\bm{x}_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})=\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})
, and
h_{S}(\bm{x}_{S})=0
. Furthermore, notice that if
S=\emptyset
, we always have
h_{S}(\bm{x}_{S})=0
. Thus, the above term can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{S}\left[\frac{1}{2^{n}}\cdot\left(h_{S}\left(e_{S}\right)+h_{S}\left(-e_{S}\right)\right)\right]=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\Ex_{S}\left[h_{S}(e_{S})\right],

(3)

where in the second equality, we use the fact that
g_{n}
and
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}
are odd and thus
h_{S}
is even.

Finally, to determine the value of
h_{S}(e_{S})
, observe that, unless
S=\emptyset
, we have

\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}g_{n}(e_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})=-2+\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(e_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}).

This means that
h_{S}(e_{S})
is completely determined by the sign of
\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})
:

\displaystyle h_{S}(e_{S})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }S=\emptyset\text{ or }S=[n],\\
-2&\text{ if }\sum_{\bm{x}_{\overline{S}}\in\{-1,1\}^{\overline{S}}}\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(e_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})>0,\\
2&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
n_{A}:=|S\cap A|,n_{B}:=|S\cap B|
.
Finally, notice that
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(e_{S}\circ\bm{x}_{\overline{S}})
is simply
\mathsf{sign}\left(n_{A}-n_{B}+\sum_{i\in\overline{S}}x_{i}\right)
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle h_{S}(e_{S})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }S=\emptyset\text{ or }S=[n],\\
2\cdot(-1)^{\mathds{1}[n_{A}>n_{B}]}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Plugging this back into (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]=\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\left(\Ex\left[(-1)^{\mathds{1}[n_{A}>n_{B}]}\right]+p^{n}-(1-p)^{n}\right).

Notice that
n_{A}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h+1,p),n_{B}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)
are independent. Thus, applying
Lemma

12
, we get

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}[g_{n}]-\Phi_{p}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]=\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}\left((1-2p)\Pr_{w,w^{\prime}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(h,p)}[w=w^{\prime}]+p^{n}-(1-p)^{n}\right).

5
When the conjectures are true? (Proof of
Theorem

10
)

For any function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, let
E_{f}:=\{\bm{x}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}\mid f(\bm{x})\neq\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})\}
and
\mu_{f}:=\Pr_{\bm{x}\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}[f(\bm{x})\neq\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})]=\frac{|E_{f}|}{2^{n}}
.

5.1
Useful Lemmata on Gaps

We first obtain a lower bound on the difference
between the first-level Fourier coefficients of
Majority and
f
in terms of their disagreement.
We state the lemma below without defining Fourier coefficients for simplicity.

Lemma 15
.

For any function
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, it holds that

\sum_{i\in[n]}\ \Ex_{\bm{x}\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left[x_{i}\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))\right]\geq 2\cdot\mu_{f}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}\Ex_{\bm{x}\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left[x_{i}\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))\right]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{\bm{x}\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left[\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\right)\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))\right]

\displaystyle\geq 2\cdot\Pr_{\bm{x}\sim\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}\right]=2\cdot\mu_{f},

where in the inequality we use the fact that
\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\right)
and
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(x)
have the same sign,
(and that the former is a non-zero integer),
which means that
\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}x_{i}\right)\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(x)-f(x))\geq 2
if
f(x)\neq\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(x)
.
∎

We next prove a simple algebraic fact that we will use later.

Lemma 16
.

For
n\geq 5
,

q<\frac{4}{(n+7)(n-1)}\implies\left(2(1-q)^{n-1}-q(1-q)^{n-2}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}\right)>0.

Proof.

From
q<\frac{4}{(n+7)(n-1)}
, we have

\displaystyle 2(1-q)^{n-1}-q(1-q)^{n-2}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{n-2}\left(2(1-q)-q\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\right)-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}

\displaystyle=(1-q)^{n-2}\left(2-q\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{2}\right)-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\geq}}(1-(n-2)q)\left(2-q\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{2}\right)-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}

\displaystyle=2-q\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{2}-2q(n-2)+q^{2}(n-2)\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}

\displaystyle=2-q\cdot\frac{(n+7)(n-1)}{2}+q^{2}\cdot(n-2)\cdot\left(\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{>}}2-q\cdot\frac{(n+7)(n-1)}{2}>0,

where in (*), we applied Bernoulli’s inequality,
(1-q)^{n-2}\geq 1-(n-2)q
, to the first term and
in (**), we used that the numerator
of the dropped term is positive
for
n\geq 5
.
∎

For any
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
we will consider the “gap” between
f
and
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}
that has a very specific form. In particular, the gap will have the following binomial-expansion-like form:

\displaystyle G_{q}[f]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[\textstyle q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\cdot\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}G_{S}(f)\right],

(4)

for some
G_{S}(f)\in\mathbb{R}
.
We prove a generic bound on
G_{q}[f]
in terms of the subset expressions
G_{S}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 17
.

Suppose there is a constant
c>0
such that:

(i)

For
i\in[n]
,
G_{\{i\}}(f)=c\cdot\Ex_{\bm{x}}[x_{i}\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))]
.

(ii)

For
i\neq j\in[n]
,

G_{\{i,j\}}(f)\geq c\cdot\left(\frac{1}{4}\Ex_{\bm{x}}[(x_{i}+x_{j})(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))\mid x_{i}=x_{j}]-\Pr[\bm{x}\in E_{f}\mid x_{i}\neq x_{j}]\right).

(iii)

For
S
such that
|S|\geq 3
,
G_{S}(f)\geq-2c\cdot\Pr[\bm{x}\in E_{f}]
.

Then,
G_{q}[f]>0
for
q<\frac{4}{(n+7)(n-1)}
.

Proof.

We lower bound the LHS using our assumptions on each term on the RHS. For the
k=1
term, we sum over all singletons and apply
Lemma

15
to obtain

\sum_{i\in[n]}G_{\{i\}}(f)=c\cdot\sum_{i\in[n]}\Ex_{\bm{x}}[x_{i}\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))]\geq 2c\cdot\mu_{f}.

Thus, the contribution to
G_{q}[f]
from the
k=1
term is at least
2c\cdot\mu_{f}\cdot q(1-q)^{n-1}
.

For the term corresponding to
k=2
, we sum over all
\binom{n}{2}
pairs. Note that the bound in our assumption has two components. Considering
the first component, we obtain

\displaystyle c\cdot\sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{4}\Ex_{\bm{x}}[(x_{i}+x_{j})(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))\mid x_{i}=x_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\cdot\sum_{i<j}\Ex_{\bm{x}}[(x_{i}+x_{j})(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))]

\displaystyle=c\cdot\frac{n-1}{2}\sum_{i\in[n]}\Ex_{\bm{x}}[x_{i}(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))]

\displaystyle\geq c(n-1)\mu_{f},

where the first equality follows by observing that if
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
then
x_{i}+x_{j}=0
, so we can drop the conditioning on
x_{i}=x_{j}
by gaining a factor of
2
, and the last step follows from
Lemma

15
.
Now we consider the second component.

\displaystyle-c\cdot\sum_{i<j}\Pr\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}~\middle|~x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-2c\cdot\sum_{i<j}\Pr\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}\mbox{ and }x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-2c\binom{n}{2}\cdot\Pr_{\bm{x},S=\{i,j\}}\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}\mbox{ and }x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-2c\binom{n}{2}\cdot\Pr_{\bm{x},S=\{i,j\}}\left[x_{i}\neq x_{j}~\middle|~\bm{x}\in E_{f}\right]\cdot\mu_{f}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{c}{2}(n^{2}-1)\cdot\mu_{f},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that,
for any
\bm{x}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, the probability (over random
\{i,j\}\in\binom{[n]}{2}
) that
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
is at most
\frac{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\binom{n}{2}}=\frac{(n^{2}-1)}{4\binom{n}{2}}
.

Thus, the contribution to
G_{q}[f]
from the
k=2
term is at least

q^{2}(1-q)^{n-2}\left(c(n-1)\mu_{f}-c\frac{n^{2}-1}{2}\mu_{f}\right)=-c\cdot q^{2}(1-q)^{n-2}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\mu_{f}.

For the terms corresponding to
k\geq 3
, we lower bound the contribution to
G_{q}[f]
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{n}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}G_{S}(f)

\displaystyle\geq-2c\sum_{k=3}^{n}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}\Pr[x\in E_{f}]

\displaystyle\geq-2c\cdot\mu_{f}\sum_{k=3}^{n}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}

\displaystyle\geq-2c\cdot\mu_{f}q^{3}\binom{n}{3},

where the last inequality is due to the union bound:
\sum_{k=3}^{n}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}
is the probability that there are at least 3 successes from
n
i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables with success probability
q
.

Summing the above contributions and applying

Lemma

16
with the choice of
q
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{q}[f]

\displaystyle\geq c\cdot\mu_{f}\cdot q\left(2(1-q)^{n-1}-q(1-q)^{n-2}\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}-q^{2}\cdot 2\binom{n}{3}\right)>0.\qed

5.2
Bounds on the Gaps

Derivation for
\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}
.

We assume here that
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
is monotone (but not necessarily unbiased). Let
\rho=1-2q
. Recall that

\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[f]-\mathsf{Stab}_{\rho}[\mathsf{Maj}_{n}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}q^{k}(1-q)^{n-k}\sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{k}}G_{S}(f),

where

\displaystyle G_{S}(f)

\displaystyle=\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[f(\bm{x})f(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})\right]

\displaystyle=\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[|\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|-|f(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|\right].

Here, we are using that sampling
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\sim\mathsf{DSBS}_{\rho}^{\otimes n}
is equivalent to sampling
\bm{x}
uniformly, a subset
S
by including each element with probability
\frac{1-\rho}{2}=q
and setting
\bm{y}=\bm{x}^{\oplus S}
(flipping all coordinates in
S
).
We wish to apply
Lemma

17
to this expansion. We have the following bounds.

•

For
|S|=1
, let
S=\{i\}
. We have
G_{\{i\}}(f)=\Ex_{\bm{x}}[2x_{i}(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))]
, since for any monotone function
h:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, it holds that
\Ex_{\bm{x}}[|h(\bm{x})-h(\bm{x}^{\oplus i})|]=\Ex_{\bm{x}}[2x_{i}h(\bm{x})]
. Thus
G_{\{i\}}[f]
satisfies
Lemma

17
(i) with
c=2
.

•

For
|S|=2
, let
S=\{i,j\}
; we have two cases. If
x_{i}=x_{j}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[|\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|-|f(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|~\middle|~x_{i}=x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[(x_{i}+x_{j})\left(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})\right)-(x_{i}+x_{j})\left(f(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})\right)~\middle|~x_{i}=x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[(x_{i}+x_{j})\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))~\middle|~x_{i}=x_{j}\right],

where the first step uses the monotonicity of
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}
and
f
.
If
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
, notice that
\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})=\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[|\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|-|f(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}^{\oplus S})|~\middle|~x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\Pr_{\bm{x}}\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}\mbox{ or }\bm{x}^{\oplus S}\in E_{f}~\middle|~x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-2\Pr_{\bm{x}}\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}~\middle|~x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right].

Putting these bounds together,

G_{\{i,j\}}(f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\Ex_{\bm{x}}\left[(x_{i}+x_{j})\cdot(\mathsf{Maj}_{n}(\bm{x})-f(\bm{x}))~\middle|~x_{i}=x_{j}\right]-2\Pr_{\bm{x}}\left[\bm{x}\in E_{f}~\middle|~x_{i}\neq x_{j}\right].

This satisfies
Lemma

17
(ii) also with
c=2
.

•

For
|S|\geq 3
