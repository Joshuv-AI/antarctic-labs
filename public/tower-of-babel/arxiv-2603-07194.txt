Shape phase transition, coexistence and mixing in the 98-106 Ru isotopes Preprint: APS/123-QED
R. Budaca 1,2 , P. Buganu 1,∗ , F. El Ouardi 3 , A. Lahbas 3,4

Abstract
The deformation properties within the 98-106 Ru even-even isotopic chain, are investigated by means of the Covariant Density Functional Theory with a Density-Dependent Point-Coupling X parametrization. The considered nuclei are found to exhibit very shallow prolate and triaxial ground state deformation. This information is used to ascertain their dynamical behavior within prolate γ \gamma -stable and γ \gamma -unstable instances of a phenomenological Bohr-Mottelson Hamiltonian with an octic potential in the axial deformation variable. The comparative study of the low-lying collective states, revealed the presence of a shape phase transition from low to high deformation, as well as evidence of shape coexistence and mixing between spherical vibrator, γ \gamma -unstable or prolate configurations in ground and excited states. It is also shown that the effect of shape coexistence and mixing on the γ \gamma -band states can account to some extent for the typical γ \gamma -unstable staggering even in prolate γ \gamma -stable conditions.

Introduction

The nuclear shape phase transitions

;
Cejnar et al. 2010

;

;
Dieperink et al. 1980

\gamma
-unstable

;
Bés 1959

\gamma
-stable prolate

;
Iachello 2001
and triaxial configuration
Davydov and Chaban 1960
;
Bonatsos et al. 2005

\beta
and
\gamma

;
Heyde et al. 1983
;
Bontasos et al. 2023
. The shape coexistence implies that a low excited state, most often the first excited
0^{+}
state

al. 2020a

. Moreover, also the entire bands manifest configurations of the states which correspond to different deformations

. For example, one can have a spherical vibrator or
\gamma
-unstable configuration of the states for the ground band and a prolate (oblate) configuration for the
\beta

;
Naz et al. 2018
and multiple shape coexistence

al. 2019
;

;
Martinou et al. 2023

;

II
Description of the models

II.1
Covariant Density Functional Theory with Density-Dependent Point-Coupling interaction

The microscopic description of nuclear ground-state properties in this work is based on Covariant Density Functional Theory (CDFT) formulated within the relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov (RHB) framework using the density-dependent point-coupling interaction DD-PCX
Vretenar et al. 2005
;
Nikšić et al. 2011
;
Yüksel et al. 2019
.
In this approach, nucleons are described as Dirac particles moving in self-consistent mean fields generated by zero-range four-fermion contact interactions, ensuring Lorentz covariance and an accurate treatment of spin degrees of freedom
Ring 1996
;
Nikšić et al. 2011
.

II.1.1
Point-coupling Lagrangian density

The effective Lagrangian density of the density-dependent point-coupling model is given by

Yüksel et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m)\psi-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{S}(\rho)(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\psi)

(1)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{V}(\rho)(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{TV}(\rho)(\bar{\psi}\vec{\tau}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\vec{\tau}\gamma_{\mu}\psi)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{S}(\partial_{\nu}\bar{\psi}\psi)(\partial^{\nu}\bar{\psi}\psi)-e\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}\frac{1-\tau_{3}}{2}\psi,

where
\psi
denotes the nucleon Dirac field with mass
m
.
The derivative term proportional to
\delta_{S}
simulates finite-range effects essential for reproducing nuclear surface properties
Nikšić et al. 2011
.

The density dependence of the coupling functions is parametrized as
Yüksel et al. 2019
:

\alpha_{i}(\rho)=a_{i}+(b_{i}+c_{i}x)e^{-d_{i}x},\qquad(i=S,V,TV),

(2)

where
x=\rho/\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}
, while
\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}
denotes the saturation density of symmetric nuclear matter. The indices S, V and TV refer to the isoscalar-scalar, isoscalar-vector and isovector-vector interactions between nucleons and
\sigma
,
\rho
and
\omega
mesons. In the DD-PCX formulation, the point-coupling CDFT model depends on ten coupling constants governing the scalar, vector, and isovector channels, as reported in Table
1
.

II.1.2
Relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov formalism and pairing correlations

For open-shell nuclei, pairing correlations are treated self-consistently within the
relativistic Hartree–Bogoliubov (RHB) framework
Vretenar et al. 2005
;
Nikšić et al. 2011
.
The total energy density functional reads:

E_{\mathrm{RHB}}[\rho,\kappa]=E_{\mathrm{RMF}}[\rho]+E_{\mathrm{pair}}[\kappa],

(3)

where
\rho
is the normal density matrix and
\kappa
is the pairing tensor.

The pairing energy is given by
Vretenar et al. 2005

E_{\mathrm{pair}}[\kappa]=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n_{1}n^{\prime}_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}n^{\prime}_{2}}\kappa^{*}_{n_{1}n^{\prime}_{1}}\langle n_{1}n^{\prime}_{1}|V^{PP}|n_{2}n^{\prime}_{2}\rangle\kappa_{n_{2}n^{\prime}_{2}},

(4)

where
V^{PP}
denotes the pairing interaction and the indices label the Dirac spinor quantum numbers.

The pairing force is chosen in separable form in momentum space and reads in coordinate space
Tian et al. 2009

V^{PP}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})=-G\,\delta(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})P(\mathbf{r})P(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(5)

where

\mathbf{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2}),\qquad\mathbf{r}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}),

(6)

and the Gaussian form factor is defined as

P(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{(4\pi a^{2})^{3/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2a^{2}}\right).

(7)

This pairing interaction preserves translational invariance and provides an accurate description of pairing properties across the nuclear chart
Tian et al. 2009
.

II.1.3

The potential energy surfaces are obtained by constrained RHB calculations using the method of quadratic constraints

(8)

where

is the total energy of the system,
\hat{Q}_{20}=2z^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}
and
\hat{Q}_{22}=x^{2}-y^{2}

q_{2\mu}
are the constrained values, and
C_{2\mu}
are stiffness constants.
This procedure allows mapping the potential energy surface as a function of quadrupole deformation parameters
(\beta_{2},\gamma)

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2B}\left[\frac{1}{\beta^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\beta^{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}\sin 3\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\sin 3\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\right]

II.2.1

\varepsilon_{ISW;n,\nu}=\frac{z_{n,\nu}^{2}}{\beta_{\omega}^{2}},\,f_{n,\nu}(\beta)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\beta_{\omega}}\frac{\beta^{-\frac{3}{2}}J_{n,\nu}\left(\frac{z_{n,\nu}}{\beta_{\omega}}\beta\right)}{J_{\nu+1}(z_{n,\nu})}.

It is preferable to introduce the following change of variable

\displaystyle I_{mn}^{(\nu,i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{J_{\nu+1}(z_{m,\nu})J_{\nu+1}(z_{n,\nu})}\times

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}J_{\nu}(z_{m,\nu}x)J_{\nu}(z_{n,\nu}x)x^{2i+1}dx,

\gamma

\gamma

\gamma

\gamma_{0}=0^{o}

\gamma

:

v_{2}(\gamma)=2q^{2}(1-\cos 3\gamma)+d,

(36)

where

and

are free parameters. The separation of the
\gamma

\theta_{i}
is achieved by considering the wave function
\psi(\gamma,\theta_{i})=\eta(\gamma)D_{M,K}^{(L)}(\theta_{i})
and the zero order approximation around
\gamma_{0}=0^{o}
for the rotational energy:

\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{\hat{L}_{k}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\left(\gamma-\frac{2\pi}{3}k\right)}\approx\frac{\hat{L}^{2}}{3}+\hat{L}_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4\sin^{2}\gamma}-\frac{1}{3}\right).

(37)

Here,

are the rotational Wigner functions
Wigner 1959
, which are eigenstates for the above rotational operator, with the indices
L
,

and

denoting quantum numbers for the eigenvalues of the total angular momentum and its projections on the third body fixed axis and the laboratory z-axis, respectively. The action of the operator, given by Eq. (
37

\frac{L(L+1)-K^{2}}{3}+\frac{K^{2}}{\sin^{2}\gamma},

(38)

\gamma

\gamma\approx 0^{o}

\gamma

\left[-\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}+\frac{K^{2}}{4\gamma^{2}}+(3q)^{2}\gamma^{2}\right]\eta(\gamma)=\epsilon_{\gamma}\eta(\gamma),

(39)

where
Budaca et al. 2019a
,

\epsilon_{\gamma}=6q(n_{\gamma}+1)=\Lambda-\frac{L(L+1)-K^{2}}{3}-d

(40)

Here,

is the quantum number associated with the vibrations in the
\gamma

n_{\gamma}=2n+\frac{|K|}{2},\;n=0,1,2,...

(41)

Further, by considering

as in

\Lambda=\frac{L(L+1)-K^{2}}{3}+6qn_{\gamma}.

(42)

This allow us to calculate the index

of the Bessel functions:

\nu=\sqrt{\Lambda+\frac{9}{4}}=\sqrt{\frac{L(L+1)-K^{2}}{3}+6qn_{\gamma}+\frac{9}{4}},

(43)

respectively the energies:

E_{\xi,n_{\gamma},L,K}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2B}\varepsilon_{\xi,n_{\gamma},L,K}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},q),

(44)

and of the wave functions (
21

R_{\xi,n_{\gamma},L,K}=\frac{E_{\xi,n_{\gamma},L,K}-E_{1,0,0,0}}{E_{1,0,2,0}-E_{1,0,0,0}}.

(45)

Reassembling the total wave function, taking into account all five variables, one obtains:

\Psi_{\xi,n_{\gamma},L,K}^{(stable)}(\beta,\gamma,\theta_{i})=F_{\xi,\nu}(\beta)\eta_{n_{\gamma},L,K}(\gamma)\Theta_{L,M,K}(\theta_{i}),

(46)

where,

\displaystyle\Theta_{L,M,K}(\theta_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2L+1}{16\pi^{2}(1+\delta_{K,0})}}\times

(47)

\displaystyle\left[D_{M,K}^{(L)}(\theta_{i})+(-1)^{L}D_{M,-K}^{(L)}(\theta_{i})\right].

\displaystyle B(E2;\xi,n_{\gamma}LK\rightarrow\xi^{\prime}n_{\gamma}^{\prime}L^{\prime}K^{\prime})=\left(\frac{3R^{2}Ze}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\beta_{M}^{2}\times

The constants in front of the above equation are defined in the previous subsection. Here, by

The

Maya-Barbecho et al. 2023

IV
Conclusions

The Bohr-Mottelson Hamiltonian with an octic potential in the
\beta
variable is numerically solved in a basis of Bessel functions of the first kind for axially-symmetric deformed nuclei. This approach, conventionally called Octic
\&
prolate, is able to describe a phase transition between the spherical vibrator and the prolate rotor, as well as shape coexistence and mixing between these two limiting symmetry configurations. The Octic
\&
prolate approach is applied, in the present study, together with the previously proposed solution of the Bohr-Mottelson Hamiltonian with octic potential for
\gamma
-unstable symmetry (Octic
\&

\gamma
-unstable) to investigate the low-lying quadrupole collective states of the
98-106
Ru even-even isotopes. Additional calculations are done for the ground state properties of these isotopes within the Covariant Density Functional Theory with a Density-Dependent Point-Coupling X parametrization. The latter results, which are compared to indirect measurements of the
\beta_{2}
deformations, serve as a guide in interpreting the most appropriate description offered by the two approaches involving the Bohr-Mottelson Hamiltonian with octic potential. After a thorough analysis of the numerical results in relation to the experimental data of these isotopes, an evident conclusion emerges. It is very hard to attribute only one of the two descriptions to the considered isotopes, while the complementary view of the data has proved to offer a more complete understanding of their structure of the states.
In other words, there are states of a band corresponding to a spherical vibrator symmetry, other states to a
\gamma
-unstable symmetry or to prolate one. Moreover, shape phase transition, shape coexistence and mixing between these three configurations are also present within this isotopic chain, respectively shape fluctuations and dynamical shape transitions. Another interesting behavior observed in these isotopes, is the staggering of the
\gamma
band states which is well reproduced by the Octic
\&

\gamma
-unstable approach, while the Octic
\&
prolate approach follows closely this grouping tendency of the states. If for the
\gamma
-unstable approach this is a typical signature, in the case of the Octic
\&
prolate approach the deviation from the regular prolate behavior is related to the presence of the shape coexistence with mixing, respectively to the shape fluctuation phenomena.
