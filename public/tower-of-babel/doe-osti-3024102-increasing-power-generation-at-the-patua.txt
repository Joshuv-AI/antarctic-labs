Executive Summary:
 In 2020, Cyrq Energy LLC, and PI Cladouhos submitted the proposal “Increasing
Power Generation at the Patua Nevada Geothermal Field through Targeted and
Adaptive EGS”, in response to the DOE’s Geothermal Wells of Opportunity Funding
Opportunity Announcement. The primary goal of the project was to convert an idle well,
Patua 16-29, to an active producer and generate at least an additional 5 MWe at the
Patua plant. The project was intended to provide an example of a reproducible
methodology for well stimulation in Enhanced Geothermal Systems
 The project was proposed to be performed in three phases over a period of
performance of four years.
 Phase 1, Pre-Stimulation (Q1–Q6) consisting of; a) identifying and executing actions
 to have the wellbore and site ready for stimulation including installation b)
 development of a thorough stimulation and monitoring plan, and c) full environmental
 compliance (i.e., permitting, induced seismicity mitigation plan).
 Phase 2, Reservoir Creation and Characterization (Q7–Q11) involving a) activities
 supporting reservoir stimulation including transport of carbon dioxide, and chemical
 additives on site, arrangement and preparation of pumping equipment, installation of
 a zonal isolation string; b) reservoir stimulation employing stimulation approaches
 pre-defined during Phase 1 and following a sequential approach as described in the
 detailed stimulation plan; and c) simultaneous reservoir monitoring and data
 collection during injection and production tests.
 Phase 3, Validation and Power Generation (Q12–Q16), starting with installation of a
 production pump and pipeline in the case of a clear +5 MWe decision from the prior
 phase. Otherwise additional well work or conversion to an injection well would be
 executed.
A first project go/no-go decision was made at the end of Phase 1 based on feasibility of
the wellbore, site, and ability to realize a 5 MWe benefit from the project. Several factors
were recognized that led to a no-go decision at this phase:
  Electrical needs for placing the stimulated well on production were not
 considered in the initial scoping. Costs of pipeline costs were also
 underestimated in the original cost share. Easements and rights-of-way to
 establish a geothermal pipeline underneath or across an interstate highway were
 also found to be intractable on the time scale of the project.
  The plant is injection limited, and no design or cost estimate was in place to
 handle the additional production needed to realize a 5 Mwe benefit. A new
 injection well would need to be drilled in addition to the other facility
 improvements to bring in the stimulated well.
Cyrq Energy attempted to locate additional wells of opportunity that could realize the
SOPOs goals, but was unsuccessful. As a result, this project is being terminated at the
end of Phase 1.

Background:

The proposed project intended to demonstrate novel zonal isolation and stimulation techniques in
idle well 16-29 at Patua. These proposed techniques had a long history of development in the
industry or at national labs. Summaries of the relevant technologies and literature have been
updated from the originally submitted technical volume in the following sections.

Zonal Isolation
With funding from the DOE, HERO and partners EGI at the University of Utah and Swell X
Limited, had developed a zonal isolation system utilizing thermally degradable expanding
materials which would be placed around a downhole tool (e.g., frack string). The system is
designed to isolate specific zones within an open well bore hole to allow controlled EGS
stimulations in isolated zones with a differential pressure up to 6000 psi for a few days to a few
weeks. Once the expanding agents degrade, the packer systems can be removed. This Thermally
Degradable Swellable Elastomer (TDSE) system was to be lab tested in Phase 1 and, if ready for
field-deployment at, deployed in Phase 2. The TDSE lab validation was not performed.

StimuFrac - CO2 fluid system
PNNL had developed StimuFrac (Fernandez et al., 2019), a non-toxic stimuli-responsive
fracturing fluid, by combining CO2 with a CO2-reactive aqueous polymer, which has been shown
at the lab scale to consistently fracture diorite rock cores and non-porous fused silica at
significantly lower net pressures and generate higher conductivity fractures compared to water-
based and waterless fracturing fluids in a range of representative of geothermal
pressure/temperature conditions. A first of its kind high-temperature true triaxial stimulation
system developed by PNNL with funding from GTO (Jian et al., 2020) was in use at the time of
the proposal, and may have been used to validate StimuFrac’s viability for the Patua application.

Energetic stimulation
SNL developed a method (Knox et al., 2016) to stimulate wells by igniting a novel energetic
material which rapidly creates a gas pressure pulse downhole, capable of initiating and driving
fractures at least 10 m radially away from the wellbore. In Dec. 2017, this method was tested at
four depths between 3957 and 5000 ft TMD and at temperatures between 250 °F and 275 °F in an
idle, low-permeability well at the Blue Mountain geothermal field in Nevada, then owned by
AltaRock, and owned by Cyrq at the time of this project’s proposal.

Proppant Testing
Zonal isolation using either TDSE or conventional packers was expected to provide the option to
create a tensile fracture, and pump proppant into a targeted zone. The technical team intended to
perform literature and market review of proppants (McLennan et al., 2015; Childers et al., 2017),
and using the PNNL triaxial frame, test proppants for durability at stress and temperature
conditions expected in well 16-29.

High volume moderate pressure stimulation
Geothermal operators are familiar with many examples in which long-term injection at well head
pressure (WHP) below the fracture pressure in the open hole has improved the injectivity index by
an order of magnitude or more (e.g., Bradford et al., 2015), and in some cases allowed an idle well
to be brought online as a producer (e.g., Lovekin et al. 2017). After the other stimulation methods
have been completed and the rig needed for those methods has been demobilized, AltaRock’s
centrifugal pumps (originally used on the Newberry EGS Demonstration) was to be used to inject
plant injectate at a WHP of up to 3000 psi for at least 2 weeks. Tracers were to be injected and
sampled in wells near 16-29 to measure changes in reservoir connectivity resulting from the
stimulation.

Project Objectives:

The primary goal of the proposed project is to convert an idle well, 16-29, to an active producer
and generate at least an additional 5 MWe at the Patua plant. This aligned with the stated goal of
the FOA and Cyrq’s goals as a power producer. Achieving this goal was to also provide an
example of a reproducible methodology for well stimulation in Enhanced Geothermal Systems,
supporting national goals of energy independence.

Technical Scope Summary
The project was to be performed in three phases over a period of performance of four years.

Phase 1, Pre-Stimulation (Q1–Q6) was to consist of three main activities; a) identifying and
executing actions to have the wellbore and site ready for stimulation including installation of a
water supply pipeline, initial planning/tools setup and testing, b) development of a thorough
stimulation and monitoring plan, and c) full environmental compliance (i.e., permitting, induced
seismicity mitigation plan).

A first project go/no-go decision was to be made at the end of Phase 1 based on feasibility of
the wellbore, site, technological readiness for field deployment of the innovative stimulation
methods being proposed (each with individual go/no go decisions), stimulation design to ensure
successful execution during phase 2, and sufficiency of the budget for Phase 2 plans.

Phase 2, Reservoir Creation and Characterization (Q7–Q11) was to involve a) activities
supporting reservoir stimulation including transport of carbon dioxide, and chemical additives on
site, arrangement and preparation of pumping equipment, installation of a zonal isolation string;
b) reservoir stimulation employing stimulation approaches pre-defined during Phase 1 and
following a sequential approach as described in the detailed stimulation plan; and c)
simultaneous reservoir monitoring and data collection during injection and production tests.

A second go/no-go decision was to be made at the end of Phase 2 based on whether a measured
improvement in injectivity and/or productivity index indicates that the goal of +5 MWe of
electricity production can be achieved and thus the expenditure (by Cyrq as cost-share) of

installation of equipment to convert the well to production can be justified. As an alternative,
additional well work may be proposed for phase 3 or conversion to an injection well.

Phase 3, Validation and Power Generation (Q12–Q16) was to start with installation of a
production pump and pipeline in the case of a clear +5 MWe decision from the prior phase.
Otherwise additional well work or conversion to an injection well was to be executed. Analysis
of post-stimulation data logs, tracer tests, production rates and temperature was to be used to
document and validate long-term heat-extraction rates. Success was to be measured by the
potential to sustainably generate +5MWe geothermal electric power which was to be
communicated to GTO in the form of a detailed report at the end of the project.

Task 1.2 Well Evaluation
Two production flow tests were performed on 16-29 in 2011 (see Appendix A); however, 10
years of sitting idle has likely changed the well’s productivity. In addition, there are questions
about the accuracy of the 2011 tests. Therefore, an air compressor was to be mobilized and used
to air-lift the well and measure a baseline productivity. Once the flow test is complete, the tubing
hanging in the hole (left since 2011) was to be removed so that further logging can be performed.
The mechanical integrity of the cased and cemented portion of the well (above 5518 ft) was to be
evaluated using a logging program developed in conjunction with the BLM and NDOM, the
regulatory agencies that oversee injection permits. The most likely logs to be run for MIT are
radioactive tracer and gamma ray, cement-bound log, image logging and injecting PTS. The
open hole from 5518 to 11850 ft was to be logged to gather information critical to stimulation
planning, including PTS (static and injecting), Quad Combo, dipole sonic, Formation Micro
Imager (FMI), and if budget allows formation lithoscanner and modular dynamic tester.

Task 1.3: Development of Stimulation Plan

Subtask 1.3.1: Stress & Fracture Model
The borehole logs was to be used to select zones for rock mechanics testing on core material.
The rock mechanics tests (Subtask 1.3.2) was to measure the quasi-static elastic properties and
development of a correlation between the quasi-static properties and the dynamic properties
measured by the sonic logs. This information was to be used, together with a 3D geologic
conceptual model and stress indicators to construct the 3D stress model.
The FMI log, together with observations from core, was to be used to develop/improve on a
model of the fracture sets observed in the well. Using the output of the stress model the fracture
sets that are most favorable for shearing was to be selected for laboratory testing (Subtask 1.3.2).
Both the estimated stress field as well as the measured rock and fracture properties was to be
used to simulate various stimulation depths and strategies using PNNL’s STOMP and OU’s
GeoFrac. The results of these stimulation models was to be used to make the selection of zones
to be isolated for each stimulation strategy.

Subask 1.3.2: Rock Mechanics & Petrology & Geochemistry
As discussed above, open hole logs was to be used to select zones for detailed testing of core
material. Rock mechanics measurements was to be made on this material to inform the stress and
stimulation models. Additionally, the most favorable natural fracture sets was to be selected for
testing, which was to include toughness, friction, initial and sheared permeability. For each of the
zones selected for rock mechanics testing thin sections was to be prepared and a petrographic
description of each zone was to be performed. Rock mechanical tests was to determine static and
dynamic elastic properties, Biot’s effective stress coefficient, natural fracture properties and how
they respond to injection (e.g., dilation angle, etc.).

Subtask 1.3.3: Reprocessing and interpretation of legacy geophysical data sets
During exploration and development of the Patua field, geophysical data such as active seismic,
passive seismic, MT, airborne magnetic, and gravity was collected. The geophysical data sets
was to be re-evaluated and if deemed useful, re-processed, to provide better input to a revised

Subtask 1.3.4: Update Geothermal Conceptual Model
A new geothermal conceptual model was developed in 2016 based on analysis of well data,
production and injection records, tracer results, and geophysical data (Cladouhos et al., 2017;
Appendix A). Since then Cyrq has built a comprehensive 3D geologic model and supported a

Subtask 1.3.5: Update Reservoir Model.
In 2016, a new reservoir model was also developed (Murphy et al, 2017) using Tetrad, the
commercial code most commonly used in the geothermal industry for projections and planning.
This model did not include 16-29 since it was idle. Using the results of the flow test of 16-29
performed in Task 1.2 and all other new available data and interpretations, the numerical
reservoir model was to be updated. was to be updated with inputs from other models (STOMP
and GeoFrac). The reservoir model(s) was to provide estimates of the volume of rock expected to
be stimulated and connectivity to natural permeability; that is a prediction of the expanded
volume and increased power output potential of the natural geothermal reservoir at Patua.

Subtask 1.3.6: Advanced Geophysical Monitoring plan
The three tasks above and updated 3D models was to inform the advanced geophysical
monitoring plan which was to include ERT, CSEM and CSAMT. In this task, ERT modeling was
to estimate the response of various possible ERT electrode array configurations which include 1)
a string of electrodes suspended in the open-hole section of well 16-29, 2) use of the casing
string in well 16-29 as an electrode, 3) Electrodes mounted in two adjacent wells (corehole 27-29
and idle well 24-29, and 4) Surface electrode/downhole electrode combinations. A series of
CSEM and CSAMT forward and inverse modeling studies was to be performed to optimize the
design of proposed geophysical imaging transmitter/receiver array deployment. A dense array of
electric-field receivers on the surface as well as in the borehole(s) and magnetic-field receivers
on the surface operating continuously prior, during, and after the stimulation was to be designed
to monitor changes in subsurface response due to fluid incursion.
The surface electric and magnetic field sensor array was to serve to detect the signal from all of
the proposed CSEM and CSAMT configurations. The classical MT array configuration combines
three-axis orthogonal vector magnetic field sensors with orthogonal horizontal electric dipole
receivers of 30 m -100 m length, with stations separated by multiple dipole lengths in a grid of
surface measurement locations. Currently we expect that the best results was to be obtained with
a continuous X-Y grid spanning the area of the stimulation zone, with electric current waveform
injected at the surface (and in boreholes), In this way an electric field induced at ground level can
be used to mao injected stimulation fluid with the 3-D geoelectric fabric of the subsurface and
any changes due to stimulation detected using the combination of CSEM and (CS)AMT. By
alternating periods by which an artificial signal is generated and periods when passive AMT
signals can be recorded, the single surface array can be used for multiple purposes.

Subtask 1.3.7.2: StimuFrac-CO2
PNNL was to evaluate two main injection strategies for deployment of StimuFrac-CO 2 fluid
system under the specified stress and temperature conditions described above. These are:
Self-Diversion Strategy: This injection strategy takes advantage of the unique property of this
binary fluid system to reduce short-circuiting. We was to apply a polymer aqueous solution that
can generate high (and reversible) changes in viscosity upon reaction with CO 2. The fracture
network created at this phase would likely be prone to developing just a few dominant flow
paths. While the pressure is maintained constant to keep the polymer crosslinked with CO 2, a
new low viscosity polymer solution was to be pumped that was to not be able to penetrate the
original fractures due to the high viscosity crosslinked polymer plugs, generating then new flow
paths filled with low viscosity fluid. From this point, pressure pulses generated by pressurizing /
depressurizing across the polymer crosslinking pressure threshold was to be carried out to further
propagate the fracture network. PNNL is currently evaluating this injection strategy under
different P/T regimes on granite.

WAG Strategy: this injection approach takes advantage of the difference in viscosity and surface
tension between the polymer solution and CO2. First, we inject the polymer solution that can
generate a new fracture as a conventional hydraulic fluid would do. Second, we inject CO 2,
which was to react with the polymer aqueous solution expanding the size of the fracture and
promoting excess CO2 migration to the tip of the fracture and surroundings of the developing
fracture. This injection was to be followed by a new injection of fresh polymer solution to react
with excess CO2 from the previous injection at the fracture front to further propagate the fracture.
This polymer solution/CO2 alternating cycle was to be repeated to propagate a single fracture as
well as possibly generate secondary fractures further away from the wellbore. PNNL is currently
evaluating this injection strategy under different P/T regimes on granite.

Subtask 1.3.7.3: Energetic stimulation methods
If lab safety protocols can be met, PNNL and Cyrq was to evaluate energetic downhole
stimulation methods such as injection of ethylene and nitrous oxide as high-energy propellants

The lead of the SNL experiment, Dr. Mark Grubelich has recently retired from SNL and was to
be available as a consultant to the project for Phase 1 design and feasibility work.

Subtask 1.3.7.4: Proppant Testing - Using the PNNL triaxial frame, proppants was to be tested

half foot rock cube. Standard proppants was to be incorporated in this surrogate fracture. Flow-
through experiments was to be carried out at representative confining T/P conditions
representative of well 16-29’s depth. The main goal is to learn about the conductivity, stability
and durability of proppants.

Task 1.3.8: Preparation of detailed stimulation plan
Based on the results of the well evaluation and downhole logging (Task 1.2) and data
interpretation and modeling (Subtasks 1.3.1 – 1.3.5), zones for stimulation was to be targeted and
stimulation methods adapted to create a deployment plan with lowest risk and maximum chance
of success. Depending upon performance of the zonal isolation and individual stimulation
methods, a Go/No Go decision was to be made for each method and a deployment plan was to be
designed describing:
 A) Zonal isolation approach: open hole packer targeting 3-5 reservoir zones
 B) Stimulation methods selected and the strategy for their individual deployment,
 C) The sequential order of deployment of the stimulation methods which was to be based on
 1) Input from stress and fracture models as well as geological and geochemical models,
 2) individual performance of the stimulation method in terms of short versus long-range
 fracture creation and propagation, 3) compatibility of stimulation chemistries as related to
 either a) anticipated fracturing performance reduction by the previously deployed
 stimulation method, or b) anticipated synergies between the methods towards
 improvement of fracture propagation and fracture network complexity, volumes, and
 overall hydraulic conductivity.

Task 2.1: Install additional geophysical and hydrologic monitoring
To collect high quality background data on microseismicity, seismic monitoring equipment was
to be installed in Phase 1 in cooperation with LBNL and SNL (see § 1.3). Additional monitoring
instruments was to be installed in the target well, at the surface, in nearby idle wells, and in
operating geothermal wells determined by planning and modeling in Phase 1. Expected
instruments include ERT, CSEM and CSAMT sources and receivers, precision pressure
monitoring, and supplemental seismometers.
If the performance modeling and feasibility study in Phase 1 warrants field application, a custom
downhole electrode cable was to be designed and constructed for well 16-29 and adjacent wells.
The electrode cable was to be constructed of materials capable of withstanding EGS conditions,
including stainless steel electrodes attached to a PEEK spine in the open interval, designed to
facilitate efficient wellbore installation.

Task 2.2 Rig mobilization and Operations
This task was to require a drilling rig for well workover activities. Cyrq was to look for
opportunities to combine this work with other well work in NV to reduce the costs of
mobilization which can be a significant portion of workover costs.

Subtask 2.2.1 Packer test – After rig set-up a drill bit was to be run to TD (total depth of well)
to confirm that the hole is open at expected gauge. A conventional drillable packer was to be
installed off the bottom to isolate the deepest, hottest zone in the well, and an extended leak-off
test (XLOT) was to be performed to determine the stress magnitude.

Subtask 2.2.3 Calibration shot: In order to provide a robust 3D velocity model for the MSA, a
calibration shot at the bottom of the hole was to be performed.

Subtask 2.2.4 Installation of HERO Zonal packers - This procedure was to isolate discrete
zones targeted in Subtask 1.3.1 for stimulation by selected methods. In short:
 1) A packer with water-activated swellable elastomers (TDSE packers) was to be placed in
 the open hole above or below the zones targeted for isolation. This packer was to hold a
 6000 psi differential pressure up to 250 °C and allow stimulation either above or below
 the sealed interval.
 1) If the stimulation target is a discrete zone that ends above the bottom of the well, or if
 multiple zones are targeted for stimulation at the same time, multiple TDSE packers can
 be placed in series.
 2) Once stimulation(s) are complete, the elastomers was to break down due to temperature
 and degrade with time allowing the straddle packer(s) to be removed from the well bore
 to provide unimpeded flow into or out of the well.

Subtask 2.2.5 Stimulation with Rig – Stimulation methods requiring mechanical zonal
isolation installed in subtask 2.2.4, was to be performed during this subtask.

Subtask 2.2.6 Installation of Casing: The current open hole in 16-29 starts at 5518 ft with a
static temperature of 274 F. A string of 9 5/8” cemented casing was to be installed from 5400 ft
to 8500 ft which was to result in an open hole with a temperature range from 340 to 410 °F more
suitable to the power plant operations. A risk analysis was to be performed in Phase 1 to
determine the order of this subtask relative to other rig activities

Task 2.3 Massive hydraulic stimulation- After flow zones have been initiated near (~100 m)
the well bore, and the rig demobilized, centrifugal pumps designed for long-term continuous
operations without downtime (in contrast to positive displacement rig pumps), was to be set up
and to inject plant injectate at a WHP of up to 3000 psi (200 bars) for at least 2 weeks. A
conservative tracer (i.e. a naphthalene disulfonate) was to be added to the injectate and sampled
for in producers in the operating portion of the Patua field. To enhance imaging resolution for the
geophysical monitoring methods (Subtask 1.3.6) a saline tracer was to be injected to determine
the depth and extent of flow away from the borehole.

Task 2.4 Preliminary Stimulation Analysis
A preliminary analysis of the results was to be prepared. The field’s numerical reservoir model
was to be updated with the results of the 16-29 stimulation and used to make projections of flow,
temperature changes and pressure support to determine the cost-benefit of converting 16-29 to a
producer. These conclusions was to be presented to DOE and other stakeholders to provide an
opportunity to evaluate the results and decide on the best course of action for Phase 3.

Task 2.5 Communications
During field operations, the team was to engage the local population through the ISMP with
online information about planned activities and information on the potential for felt microseismic
events.

Task 2.6: Reporting and Publications
During the Phase 2 field activities, the team was to send out daily stimulation reports and host
weekly calls with the DOE and other stakeholders. At the end of Phase 2 a report was to be
submitted to the DOE that compiles the daily stimulation reports, stimulation data, formation
response data for both pre- and post-stimulation, and pumping and related data to evaluate the
stimulation. Following preliminary analysis and presentation of the data collected (Task 2.4), a
go/no-go decision point was to be provided. A “Go” decision would mean that in Phase 3 Cyrq
was to install a production pump and pipeline to 16-29 as cost-share to the project. A technical
paper was to be presented at the Stanford Geothermal WS and/or Annual GRC meeting to
present the preliminary results and get input from industry experts.

Phase 3 – Validation and Power Generation
The goal of Phase 3 was to be to bring 16-29 online as a production well and assess the long-
term flow and temperature change rates.

Task 3.1: Monitoring, Analysis, and Modeling
The seismic, geophysical, and hydrologic monitoring was to continue in Phase 3, with
modifications as dictated by Phase 2 experience. These robust data sets was to continue to be
analyzed and used to refine and update the stress, geothermal conceptual and reservoir models
developed in Phase 1

Task 3.2: Conversion of 16-29 to a production well and post stimulation data collection
Subtask 3.2.1 Install Production Pump and Pipeline - If a “Go” decision is made at the end of
phase 2, then Cyrq was to install a pump to 16-29 and pipe the fluid to the plant. The
productivity index and expected flow temperature of the well was to determine the pump type
selected.

Subtask 3.2.2 Collect Data and supply to GDR
On a quarterly basis, data on well flow rates, down hole pressures, production temperatures,
chemistry of produced fluid, and results of tracer tests was to be provided to the GDR. This data
was to be compared to the updated numerical reservoir model.

Task 3.3: Final Report
The project’s final report was to provide analysis of a year’s worth of data from the stimulated
well 16-29 as well as the rest of the field with a focus on sustainability and heat extraction. The
final report was to also detail lessons learned and best practices for geothermal well stimulation.

Project Results and Discussion:

After Cyrq Energy LLC (Cyrq) and it’s co-applicants were awarded DE-EE0009182, initial work
to characterize the subsurface around well 16-29 primarily towards subtasks 1.1, Site work
towards installation of a water supply, and 1.13, legacy geophysical data evaluation. During this
period Cyrq was acquired by Macquarie Infrastructure Partners. Subsequently, the principal
investigator on the award left Cyrq, and much of the Resource team turned over. Following the
re-structuring, the new team evaluated the WOO proposal and found the following issues:
 • The proposed well of opportunity, Patua 16-29, would need to flow approximately 2000
 GPM at 300 F to add 5 MWe gross generation
 • The Patua plant is currently injection limited, and no design or cost estimate was in place
 to handle the additional 2000 GPM of flow. The temperature improvement from
 replacing an existing well with 16-29 would not result in the goal of 5 MWe.
 • Electrical needed for the 16-29 production pump were not considered in the initial
 scoping. Costs of pipeline were also underestimated in the original cost share.
 • As a result of the much higher than expected costs to Cyrq to complete the project, and
 the infeasibility of returning the promised generation, Cyrq reported to the DOE that the
 project as proposed could not go forward.
 • The DOE agreed to discuss re-scoping the grant, assuming it addresses the funding
 opportunities key goals, increasing generation from existing wells by 5 MW using novel
 techniques in EGS.
 • Cyrq located another well in its portfolio that would have been a suitable target for the
 methods described in the original SOPO, Blue Mountain 13-11, but that asset was sold
 before the updated proposal could be approved.
 • Without a well suitable for the award, Cyrq elected to terminate the award at Milestone 1.

Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

This award was terminated due to infeasibility of the selected site to deliver the FOA’s
stated goal of adding 5 MWe of generation, and the inability of the host organization to
provide an alternative site.

Path Forward:

Single-well stimulation techniques remain a valuable area of technology development
for the Geothermal industry. Depending on the technique, many of which were
proposed in this study, stimulations may reduce near-well bore damage or propagate
fractures deep into formation to connect wells that were near misses up to conventional
reservoirs. Tests of the methods described in this study are still a viable path forward if
wells of opportunity with a clearer path to commercial operation can be identified.
