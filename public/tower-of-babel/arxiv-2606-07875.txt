Hypothetical Singularity of 3D Navier Stokes in Clay Institute set up Reduces to Axisymmetric with Swirl class
Rishad Shahmurov

Abstract
We prove a singular-endpoint reduction for the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes large-data regularity problem in the smooth finite-energy class. The result is a reduction theorem: any hypothetical first singularity of a general three-dimensional solution generates, after scale normalization, threshold selection, and endpoint extraction, a terminal singular endpoint in the axisymmetric-with-swirl class. Thus a proof of the companion axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint theorem closes the corresponding full three-dimensional finite-energy regularity statement. The proof is organized around the scalar vorticity-amplitude identity. Let ω = ∇ × u \omega=\nabla\times u , A = | ω | A=|\omega| , ξ = ω / | ω | \xi=\omega/|\omega| , and S = sym ⁡ ∇ u S=\sym\nabla u . The regularized identity ( ∂ t + u ⋅ ∇ − ν Δ ) A = A ( ξ ⋅ S ξ − ν | ∇ ξ | 2 ) (\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)A=A\bigl(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2}\bigr) splits the endpoint into a zero-production branch and a positive-production branch. The zero-production branch is closed by a Nash–Liouville argument after the amplitude-tail alternatives are removed. In the positive-production branch, the vortex-stretching density is first rebased onto comparable active donors. The central point of the present formulation is that this rebase is signed and stable: positive-signed donor mass is selected by a Carleson procedure, rebased into an exact orthogonal solenoidal hull, and then either produces a nonzero finite-band hull energy or activates a routed output. Nonclosed mechanisms are not hidden. High-frequency conveyors, no-overcount Carleson failures, projection defects, spectral leakage, hull nonclosure, macro/tail forcing, motion, pressure-current errors, and fragmentation are all represented as selected outputs. The selected-output packing/profile theorem states that infinitely many such outputs either spend a finite global currency, generate a strict active descendant, or extract a selected-output ancient profile. The selected-output termination theorem makes this routing well founded by combining exact active-scale compactness, selected-output measure lower semicontinuity, Mosco/frame convergence of maximal hulls, Carleson failure structure, and a finite rank order. Thus no selected-output profile is a new genuinely three-dimensional terminal branch: it either spends currency, selects a strict descendant, decreases rank, becomes flat two-dimensional, or becomes axisymmetric with swirl. Closed orthogonal hulls are ruled out by kinetic-energy skew-symmetry, viscosity, and finite-band coercivity. Finally, field-level one-axis vorticity equivariance is upgraded to velocity equivariance by the curl/divergence recovery. Hence the only non-regular terminal endpoint left by the three-dimensional front end is the axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint.



Keywords.
Navier–Stokes equations; Millennium problem; axisymmetric flows with swirl; large data; vorticity; vortex stretching; flux-closed active hulls; Nash inequality.

Introduction: the reduction problem

Proof map and scope

The paper proves a reduction theorem rather than an independent proof of the axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint. The logical chain is

\text{first singularity}\Longrightarrow\text{singularity-forcing packet}\Longrightarrow\text{amplitude-production alternative}\Longrightarrow\text{active hull or selected output}\Longrightarrow\text{flat 2D or axisymmetric swirl}.

The three-dimensional part of the argument is closed inside this manuscript. The reduced endpoint is the companion statement
(\mathrm{AS})
, namely exclusion of an axisymmetric-with-swirl singular endpoint; the compatibility of the endpoint with
(\mathrm{AS})
is recorded in Theorem
12.4
.

The positive-production part uses the following repaired sequence. First, the active-envelope currency is the scale-invariant spacetime
L^{4}
quantity. Second, comparable donors are selected by positive signed production, not by unsigned absolute mass. Third, selected donors are rebased into an exact orthogonal solenoidal finite-band hull; projection errors, spectral leakage, and no-overcount failures are outputs rather than discarded errors. Fourth, selected outputs are packed or converted into ancient selected-output profiles, and those profiles are closed by a well-founded rank theorem with explicit compactness, lower-semicontinuity, and hull-convergence inputs. These four points are the main additions in the present assembly.

The Clay Millennium Navier–Stokes problem asks for a proof of global existence and smoothness, or for a counterexample, for smooth finite-energy three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes flows
[
1
]
. This paper proves a front-end singularity reduction: if a smooth finite-energy three-dimensional solution develops a first singularity, then the endpoint selection and flux-hull analysis force an axisymmetric-with-swirl singular endpoint. The axisymmetric-with-swirl regularity theorem is not reproved here; it is the endpoint problem to which the general singularity is reduced.

For orientation, we introduce here the notation used in the informal description of the proof. Let

\omega=\curl u,\qquad A=|\omega|,\qquad\xi=\omega/|\omega|,\qquad S=\sym\nabla u,

with the usual regularization at points where
\omega=0
. A threshold packet is denoted

Q_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\times I_{R},\qquad I_{R}=(t_{0}-R^{2},t_{0}),

and an active hull
\mathcal{H}
is a finite collection of comparable-frequency, spatially localized, solenoidal pieces selected inside such a packet. We write
U_{\mathcal{H}}
for the velocity carried by the hull. The quantity

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})=\int_{I_{R}}\left|\left\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(u\cdot\nabla)u]-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}}],U_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right|\,dt

measures the nonlinear kinetic-energy flux between the hull and its complement. The normalization

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})=\int_{I_{R}}\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt+R^{-2}\int_{I_{R}}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\,dt

measures the corresponding dissipative scale. Thus

\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}\to 0

means that the hull is closed at the energy scale.

The result is most naturally read in the contrapositive form. Suppose a general smooth finite-energy solution develops a first-threshold singular endpoint. The analysis below eliminates every endpoint that is genuinely three-dimensional: zero amplitude production is killed by Nash–Liouville; nonclosed positive production becomes an output or a strict descendant; and closed positive production is killed by the kinetic-energy identity. What remains is an axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint. Thus excluding axisymmetric-with-swirl singular endpoints excludes all first singularities.

The guiding problem is to identify the mechanism that funds the first critical packet. The only scalar source capable of increasing the vorticity amplitude is

A^{2}(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2}).

The proof therefore does not begin by classifying all vortex geometries. It splits the endpoint into two cases. If the positive part of this source vanishes, the vorticity amplitude is subcaloric and a Nash–Liouville argument gives a contradiction. If the positive part is nonzero, it is rebased onto comparable active frequency blocks. These positive-production blocks are not studied one by one. Instead, they are enlarged by a stopping-time construction to a flux-closed active hull. More precisely, the construction gives the following dichotomy:

\text{either an output/active descendant is selected,}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}\to 0.

In the second case the hull is asymptotically closed under the Navier–Stokes nonlinearity. Its internal nonlinear energy transfer is skew-symmetric, while viscosity is strictly dissipative; indeed,

\frac{d}{dt}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+2\nu\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}=o(1),\qquad\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus a nonzero bounded ancient flux-closed active hull cannot persist. The central terminal-exclusion theorem then says that a zero-output, no-descendant ancient endpoint cannot remain genuinely non-axisymmetric: after the flat two-dimensional regular class is removed, the only unresolved endpoint is axisymmetric with swirl.

The staircase

The proof has the following linear structure.

Step 1.

Select a singularity-forcing packet in a threshold band
e_{\rm low}\leq\mathcal{Q}(Q)\leq e_{\rm sing}
.

Step 2.

Write the regularized amplitude identity for
A=|\omega|
.

Step 3.

If the positive production
\mathfrak{P}_{+}
vanishes,
A
is subcaloric. With finite amplitude mass and zero tail flux, Nash’s inequality implies
A\equiv 0
.

Step 4.

If
\mathfrak{P}_{+}
is positive, dominate it by
(\omega\cdot S\omega)_{+}
and perform a signed Littlewood–Paley decomposition.

Step 5.

Nonactive terms are controlled in absolute
L^{1}
. If any is nonperturbative, it is one of the finite outputs or selects an active descendant.

Step 6.

The remaining positive production lives in comparable active components. Starting from the receiver component, build the flux-closed active hull by adding every comparable donor whose boundary flux is non-negligible.

Step 7.

If the hull construction fails to close, the failure is shell, tail, macro, pressure/motion, passive, temporal, fragmentation output, or an active descendant. If it closes, the nonlinear energy contribution inside the hull vanishes by skew-symmetry, while viscosity strictly dissipates comparable frequencies. A nonzero bounded ancient closed active hull is impossible.

Step 8.

The only endpoint alternatives are flat two-dimensional or axisymmetric with swirl. The flat case is regular. The axisymmetric case is precisely the reduced problem.

2
Equations and the singular-endpoint reduction theorem

We consider smooth finite-energy solutions of

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u,\qquad\diver u=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1)

with vorticity
\omega=\curl u
. The vorticity equation is

\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=S\omega+\nu\Delta\omega,\qquad S=\sym\nabla u.

(2)

The Navier–Stokes scaling is

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\qquad\omega_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}\omega(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

Definition 2.1

(Large-data regularity statements)
.

Let
(\mathrm{G})
denote the full three-dimensional smooth finite-energy large-data regularity statement: every smooth finite-energy solution of (
1
) remains smooth globally in time. Let
(\mathrm{AS})
denote the same statement restricted to smooth finite-energy axisymmetric solutions with arbitrary swirl.

Theorem 2.2

(Singular-endpoint reduction to axisymmetric swirl)
.

Let
u
be a smooth finite-energy solution of (
1
) on its maximal time interval. If a finite-time first singularity occurs, then there exists a normalized terminal first-threshold endpoint in the axisymmetric-with-swirl class. More precisely, after parabolic rescaling around a singularity-forcing packet and after the output/descendant selection described below, every non-axisymmetric endpoint alternative is eliminated: the zero-production branch is subcaloric and vanishes, nonclosed positive-production hulls generate a named output or a strict active descendant, and closed positive-production hulls are ruled out by the kinetic-energy Liouville theorem. The remaining terminal endpoint is axisymmetric with swirl.

Corollary 2.3

(Reduction of global regularity to the axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint)
.

If smooth finite-energy axisymmetric Navier–Stokes solutions with arbitrary swirl are globally regular, then smooth finite-energy three-dimensional Navier–Stokes solutions are globally regular. Conversely, any axisymmetric-with-swirl counterexample is automatically a three-dimensional counterexample, since the axisymmetric class is a subclass of the full class.

3
Packets, threshold bands, and outputs

Definition 3.1

(Packets)
.

A packet is a parabolic cylinder

Q_{R}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-R^{2},t_{0}).

It comes with a smooth spatial cutoff
\phi_{R}
, a time cutoff
\eta_{R}
, a core
B_{R/2}
, a collar
B_{R}\setminus B_{R/2}
, and a comparable frequency window
\Lambda_{R}=\{N:c_{0}R^{-1}\leq N\leq C_{0}R^{-1}\}
.

Definition 3.2

(Critical score)
.

The packet score is the scale-invariant local enstrophy quantity

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{R}):=\sup_{t\in I_{R}}R\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\phi_{R}^{2}|\omega(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+R^{-1}\iint_{Q_{R}}\phi_{R}^{2}|\omega|^{2}\,dxdt.

(3)

Both terms are invariant under the Navier–Stokes scaling. The first term is the instantaneous first-threshold score; the second is the corresponding spacetime dissipation score. In sections where only the instantaneous threshold is used, the first term alone is denoted by
\mathcal{Q}_{\sup}(Q_{R})
.

Theorem 3.3

(Local vorticity-score regularity criterion)
.

There exists a universal number
\varepsilon_{\rm reg}>0
with the following property. Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution in
Q_{R}(x_{0},t_{0})
. If

R^{-1}\iint_{Q_{R}(x_{0},t_{0})}|\omega|^{2}\,dxdt<\varepsilon_{\rm reg},

then
(x_{0},t_{0})
is a regular point, quantitatively regular in a smaller parabolic cylinder. Consequently, after decreasing
\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
by a controlled cutoff constant, the stronger packet condition
\mathcal{Q}(Q_{R})<\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
implies regularity in the core of
Q_{R}
.

Proof.

This is the
p=q=2
vorticity case of the interior regularity criteria of Gustafson–Kang–Tsai. Their criterion applies to suitable weak solutions and gives regularity when the scaled mixed norm of the vorticity is sufficiently small; in the present case that scaled norm is
R^{-1/2}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}(Q_{R})}
, equivalently the square
R^{-1}\iint_{Q_{R}}|\omega|^{2}
. Thus the displayed smallness condition is exactly the squared scale-invariant
L^{2}_{x,t}
vorticity smallness required by the criterion. The packet score
\mathcal{Q}
dominates this spacetime term on the core where the cutoff equals one; shrinking the cylinder and the threshold absorbs the cutoff constants. The instantaneous term in
\mathcal{Q}
is not needed for the cited regularity criterion; it is retained for compactness and lower semicontinuity in the terminal selection.
∎

We therefore distinguish a warning level from a singularity-forcing level. The warning level is used only to keep descendants visible; the terminal endpoint is always selected at the singularity-forcing level
e_{\rm sing}=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
. A first singularity supplies arbitrarily small packets with
\mathcal{Q}\geq\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
; otherwise Theorem
3.3
would make the putative singular point regular.

Definition 3.4

(Warning band and singularity-forcing threshold)
.

Fix constants

0<e_{\rm low}<e_{\rm warn}<e_{\rm sing}:=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}

with
e_{\rm low}\leq c_{\rm desc}\delta_{0}^{2}e_{\rm sing}
, where
c_{\rm desc}
is the finite-overlap descendant constant and
\delta_{0}
is the nonperturbative-output threshold. A packet is
active
if
\mathcal{Q}(Q)\geq e_{\rm low}
, a
warning packet
if
\mathcal{Q}(Q)\geq e_{\rm warn}
, and
singularity-forcing
if
\mathcal{Q}(Q)\geq e_{\rm sing}
. A terminal packet is selected among singularity-forcing packets. The warning band is used only to track descendants between singularity-forcing crossings; a warning-level descendant that never reaches
e_{\rm sing}
cannot by itself be the terminal singular packet.

Remark 3.5

(Why two levels are necessary)
.

A descendant estimate usually gives

\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq c\delta^{2}\mathcal{Q}(Q),

not
\mathcal{Q}(Q^{\prime})\geq\mathcal{Q}(Q)
. Thus one should not force every descendant to remain singularity-forcing. The lower active and warning levels keep funding channels visible. The upper level
e_{\rm sing}=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
is reserved for the terminal endpoint and is the level forced by a genuine singularity. If an active or warning descendant later reaches
e_{\rm sing}
, the singularity-forcing selection is repeated at a smaller or better packet. If it never reaches
e_{\rm sing}
, it may feed an output or a hull, but it cannot itself be the terminal singular endpoint.

Lemma 3.6

(Singularity-forcing packet selection)
.

If a smooth finite-energy solution develops a finite-time first singularity at
(x_{*},T)
, then there are radii
R_{k}\downarrow 0
and packets
Q_{R_{k}}(x_{k},t_{k})
with
(x_{k},t_{k})\to(x_{*},T)
such that

\mathcal{Q}(Q_{R_{k}})\geq e_{\rm sing}=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}.

Moreover, among such packets one may choose a terminal sequence by the lexicographic order used below. Any strict descendant selected below
e_{\rm sing}
is kept only as an active funding channel; it must either re-cross
e_{\rm sing}
, become perturbative, produce a named output, or be absorbed into an energy-killed flux-closed hull.

Proof.

If no such sequence existed, then for all sufficiently small packets entering a neighborhood of
(x_{*},T)
the score (
3
) would be below
\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
, and the local vorticity regularity criterion would give smoothness at
(x_{*},T)
, contradicting the definition of first singularity. The lexicographic terminal selection is made only among packets at or above
e_{\rm sing}
. Descendants below this level are not discarded; the threshold band records them as possible funding channels. Since the final terminal packet is selected at the regularity threshold, the endpoint produced by this construction is singularity-forcing rather than merely warning-level active.
∎

Definition 3.7

(Output ledger)
.

The named outputs are:

(O1)

cap, collar, cutoff, or moving-boundary flux;

(O2)

amplitude-tail or vorticity-mass escape;

(O3)

shell, low-frequency, or macro/harmonic strain;

(O4)

pressure-current or moving-frame work;

(O5)

passive strain and high-low paraproduct output;

(O6)

fragmentation or active descendant;

(O7)

temporal transfer through the active window;

(O8)

scale-gap, inner-boundary, or normalized shape-jump conveyor output;

(O9)

axisymmetric orbit endpoint, which is the reduced statement
(\mathrm{AS})
.

A terminal zero-output sequence is one for which every normalized output above tends to zero.
For later reference we record the normalized pressure-current output in a form that is actually used in the flux-hull estimates. If
Q_{R}=B_{R}\times I_{R}
and
p=p_{\rm loc}+p_{\rm ext}
is the usual local/nonlocal pressure decomposition with

-\Delta p_{\rm loc}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\bigl((\chi_{4R}u_{i})(\chi_{4R}u_{j})\bigr),

then the pressure-current output controls

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}(Q_{R}):=\inf_{c(t)}\int_{I_{R}}R^{-1}\|p_{\rm ext}(\cdot,t)-c(t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2R})}\|u\|_{L^{3}(B_{2R})}\,dt

together with the analogous local cross-pressure pieces generated by a hull/complement splitting. Thus a nonzero pressure work term is never hidden inside the word “motion”: it is either bounded by the displayed pressure-current norm, absorbed into the hull dissipation, or recorded as output (O4).

The amplitude identity

Because
\xi=\omega/|\omega|
is undefined at zeros of vorticity, set

A_{\varepsilon}=(|\omega|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2},\qquad\xi_{\varepsilon}=\omega/A_{\varepsilon}.

Lemma 4.1

(Regularized amplitude identity)
.

For smooth solutions,

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)A_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\omega\cdot S\omega}{A_{\varepsilon}}-\nu\frac{|\nabla\omega|^{2}}{A_{\varepsilon}}+\nu\frac{|\nabla A_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{A_{\varepsilon}}.

(4)

As
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, this gives in the distributional sense

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)A=A\bigl(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2}\bigr).

(5)

Proof.

Differentiate
A_{\varepsilon}
using (
2
). The identity

|\nabla\omega|^{2}=|\nabla A|^{2}+A^{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}

holds away from
\{A=0\}
and is obtained in the limit by lower semicontinuity. This yields (
5
).
∎

\mathfrak{P}_{+}(Q_{R})=R\iint_{Q_{R}}\eta_{R}\phi_{R}^{2}A^{2}\bigl(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2}\bigr)_{+}\,dxdt.

5
Zero production and Nash–Liouville

Proposition 5.1

(Subcaloric zero-production limit)
.

Let a normalized ancient endpoint have zero cap/tail/cutoff outputs and suppose
\mathfrak{P}_{+}(Q_{R})\to 0
on every fixed compact ancient cylinder. Then its vorticity amplitude satisfies

(\partial_{\tau}+U\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)A\leq 0,\qquad\diver U=0.

Definition 5.2

(Amplitude-tail output)
.

For an ancient endpoint define

\mathfrak{M}_{A}(L)=\sup_{\tau\leq 0}\int_{|y|>L}A(y,\tau)\,dy.

Failure of
\mathfrak{M}_{A}(L)\to 0
is an amplitude-tail output.

Lemma 5.3

(Tail to finite mass)
.

If
A\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}
and
\mathfrak{M}_{A}(L)\to 0
, then

M:=\sup_{\tau\leq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}A(y,\tau)\,dy<\infty.

Proof.

On every fixed ball,
\int_{B_{L}}A\leq|B_{L}|^{1/2}\|A\|_{L^{2}(B_{L})}
. The local
L^{2}
bound comes from the normalized enstrophy score. The exterior part is controlled by
\mathfrak{M}_{A}(L)
.
∎

Theorem 5.4

(Nash–Liouville)
.

Let
A\geq 0
be a bounded ancient transported heat subsolution with
\diver U=0
and

M=\sup_{\tau\leq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}A(y,\tau)\,dy<\infty.

If
F(\tau)=\int A^{2}(y,\tau)dy
is bounded on
(-\infty,0]
, then
A\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Multiplying by
A
and integrating gives

\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\tau)+\nu\|\nabla A\|_{2}^{2}\leq 0.

Nash’s inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

\|A\|_{2}^{10/3}\leq C\|A\|_{1}^{4/3}\|\nabla A\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence

F^{\prime}(\tau)+c\nu M^{-4/3}F(\tau)^{5/3}\leq 0.

A nonzero bounded ancient solution of this differential inequality is impossible: solving backward from any time with
F>0
forces finite-time blow-up. Thus
F\equiv 0
.
∎

6
Positive production and active rebase

The zero-production branch is now closed. Assume
\mathfrak{P}_{+}
remains positive. The key point is the pointwise domination

A^{2}\bigl(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2}\bigr)_{+}\leq(\omega\cdot S\omega)_{+}.

(7)

Decompose

\omega=\sum_{N}\omega_{N},\qquad S=\sum_{N}S_{N},\qquad S_{N}=S[\omega_{N}],

where
P_{N}
are smooth Littlewood–Paley projections. Then

\omega\cdot S\omega=\sum_{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}}\omega_{N_{1}}\cdot S[\omega_{N_{2}}]\omega_{N_{3}}.

(8)

The active density
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}
is the sum of the comparable, on-core terms with
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}\sim R^{-1}
. All other terms are placed into
\mathcal{K}_{\rm out}
and the commutator/cutoff remainder
\mathcal{R}_{R}
.

Proposition 6.1

(Positive production rebase)
.

For a normalized first-threshold sequence,

\mathfrak{P}_{+}(Q_{R})\leq R\iint_{Q_{R}}(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}\,dxdt+R\iint_{Q_{R}}|\mathcal{K}_{\rm out}|\,dxdt+R\iint_{Q_{R}}|\mathcal{R}_{R}|\,dxdt.

(9)

Along a zero-output sequence,

R\iint|\mathcal{K}_{\rm out}|+R\iint|\mathcal{R}_{R}|=o(1).

(10)

Therefore positive production is carried by
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}
.

Proof.

Equation (
7
) and (
8
) give the signed decomposition. The scalar inequality

(a+b+c)_{+}\leq a_{+}+|b|+|c|

yields (
9
). The nonactive classes are: high-low paraproducts, off-core comparable packets, far or harmonic strain, and cutoff commutators. Bernstein, Calderon–Zygmund, and finite-overlap estimates control their absolute
L^{1}
contributions by the named outputs. In a zero-output sequence these contributions are
o(1)
.

∎

Lemma 6.2

(Active-seed extraction)
.

Let
Q_{R_{k}}
be a normalized first-threshold sequence for which

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mathfrak{P}_{+}(Q_{R_{k}})\geq p_{*}>0

and all non-active outputs in the sense of Definition
3.7
vanish. Then, after passing to a subsequence, there is an admissible comparable-frequency active atom
\alpha_{k}
with

R_{k}\iint_{I_{R_{k}}\times B_{R_{k}}}(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act},\alpha_{k}})_{+}\,dxdt\geq c_{0}p_{*}.

(11)

Here
c_{0}>0
depends only on the finite-overlap constants of the spatial and frequency partitions.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
and the zero-output hypothesis,

R_{k}\iint(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}^{(k)})_{+}\,dxdt\geq p_{*}/2

for all sufficiently large
k
. Decompose
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}^{(k)}
using the finite core cover and the finitely many annular frequency windows
N\simeq R_{k}^{-1}
. Since the overlap multiplicity is bounded by a universal constant
C_{\rm ov}
, either one atom carries at least
c_{0}p_{*}
, or the mass is distributed among many essentially disjoint active atoms. The latter is precisely the fragmentation output in the output ledger; it is excluded in a terminal zero-output sequence. Therefore an atom satisfying (
11
) exists.
∎

7
Flux-closed active hulls

The positive-production rebase locates the packet’s funding in comparable active pieces. The remaining task is not to classify every possible resonant triad, but to determine whether the positive-production piece is dynamically closed. We therefore build the active object by a stopping rule: every comparable, spatially tight, time-stable component that feeds the current active set by non-negligible kinetic-energy flux is added to the active hull. Components that are not comparable, not tight, or not time-stable are not added; they are precisely the already named low-frequency, macro, shell, tail, pressure/motion, fragmentation, temporal-transfer, or descendant outputs.

The theorem below is the final bookkeeping step of the reduction. It is a stopping-time statement: every non-negligible active feeder is either incorporated into the hull or recorded by one of the output channels.

The hull is allowed to absorb only comparable-frequency, localized, time-stable active donors. Everything else remains in the output ledger.

7.1
Solenoidal hull operators and active hulls

Let
Q_{R}=I_{R}\times B_{R}
be a normalized threshold-band packet. The hull must be an object for which the kinetic-energy test is legitimate. We therefore do not use a merely formal sum of localized pieces. We use a self-adjoint, solenoidal, almost-orthogonal operator.

Choose real cutoffs
\chi_{\alpha}
from the finite core/collar cover and smooth annular Fourier multipliers
P_{N_{\alpha}}
, with

c_{0}R^{-1}\leq N_{\alpha}\leq C_{0}R^{-1}.

For an admissible label
\alpha
, set

\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f:=\Pi P_{N_{\alpha}}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}P_{N_{\alpha}}\Pi f,\qquad U_{\alpha}:=\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}u.

(12)

Here
\chi_{\alpha}
denotes multiplication by
\chi_{\alpha}
. Since
\Pi
and
P_{N}
are self-adjoint Fourier multipliers and
\chi_{\alpha}
is real,
\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}=\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}^{*}
on
L^{2}
. Moreover
\diver U_{\alpha}=0
. For a finite hull
\mathcal{H}
, define

\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}}\mathsf{P}_{\alpha},\qquad U_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}u.

(13)

The bounded-overlap of the cover gives
\|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2\to 2}\leq C
, uniformly in the terminal sequence. The difference between
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
and the simpler expression
\Pi\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}}P_{N_{\alpha}}(\chi_{\alpha}u)
consists only of cutoff/Leray commutators and finite-overlap cross terms; these are estimated in Lemma
7.3
and are part of the collar, tail, motion, or temporal-transfer outputs.

The labels in
\mathcal{H}
are allowed to be added only when they are comparable-frequency, spatially tight, and time-stable on
I_{R}
. Low-frequency strain, far harmonic drift, rapidly moving windows, and non-comparable donors are deliberately
not
absorbed into the hull, because absorbing them would destroy the frequency gap used in the final energy argument.

Lemma 7.1

(Operator bounds and almost-orthogonality)
.

Let the family of labels in a hull have bounded spatial overlap and all frequencies in the window
c_{0}R^{-1}\leq N_{\alpha}\leq C_{0}R^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-1}\|f\|_{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\|\nabla g\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq cR^{-1}\|g\|_{2}\quad\text{for every }g\in\operatorname{Ran}(\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}),

(16)

up to the collar and low-frequency outputs already named in the ledger. Moreover cross terms between atoms whose cutoffs are separated by more than a fixed multiple of
R
are rapidly decaying; atoms with overlapping supports occur only with uniformly bounded multiplicity.

Proof.

The operator
\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}=\Pi P_{N_{\alpha}}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}P_{N_{\alpha}}\Pi
is an order-zero pseudolocal operator whose kernel is supported at scale
R
up to Schwartz tails. More explicitly, if
K_{N}
is the kernel of
P_{N}
, then for each
M
one has

|K_{N}(x)|\leq C_{M}N^{3}(1+N|x|)^{-M}.

Thus
\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}\mathsf{P}_{\beta}
has operator norm
O_{M}((1+\operatorname{dist}(\supp\chi_{\alpha},\supp\chi_{\beta})/R)^{-M})
unless the supports overlap at scale
R
. Cotlar–Stein almost-orthogonality, together with bounded overlap of the packet cover and the finite annular frequency window, gives (
14
). The upper
H^{1}
bound follows similarly because the left output frequency is
\simeq R^{-1}
. Finally every output
g
of
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
has Fourier support in a finite union of annuli
\{cR^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq CR^{-1}\}
except for terms created by the physical cutoffs. Those cutoff leakage terms are exactly the collar/low-frequency outputs; after they vanish, Bernstein gives (
16
).
∎

7.2
Normalized boundary flux

For a finite hull
\mathcal{H}
, define

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I):=\int_{I}\left|\left\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(u\cdot\nabla)u]-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}}],U_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right|\,dt.

(17)

The normalization is the active dissipation scale

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I):=\int_{I}\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt+R^{-2}\int_{I}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\,dt.

(18)

A hull is
flux-closed
on
I_{R}
if

\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}\to 0

(19)

along the terminal sequence. This is the dimensionless form of closure used below. In the normalized variables
R=1
, it is equivalent to the informal statement that the boundary flux is
o(\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}^{2})
.

7.3
Absolute flux decomposition

All estimates in this subsection are absolute estimates. No signed cancellation from the complement is used. Write

u=U_{\mathcal{H}}+W_{\mathcal{H}},

and decompose

W_{\mathcal{H}}=W_{\mathrm{low}}+W_{\mathrm{high}}+W_{\mathrm{comp},off}+W_{\mathrm{comp},on}^{\perp}+W_{\mathrm{far}}+W_{\mathrm{harm}}+W_{\mathrm{mov}}+W_{\rm com}.

The terms denote low-frequency, high-frequency, comparable off-core, comparable on-core but not yet in the hull, far-field, harmonic/macro, moving-window, and commutator pieces.

Lemma 7.2

(Absolute flux decomposition)
.

With the above notation,

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)\leq\sum_{X}\int_{I}\left|\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{N}}_{X},U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle\right|\,dt,

(20)

where
X
ranges over the eight complement classes and
{\mathcal{N}}_{X}
is the sum of all terms in

(u\cdot\nabla)u-(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}}

with at least one factor from
W_{X}
, together with the corresponding projection/cutoff commutator terms.

Proof.

Expand

(u\cdot\nabla)u-(U\cdot\nabla)U=(U\cdot\nabla)W+(W\cdot\nabla)U+(W\cdot\nabla)W,

insert the complement splitting, apply
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and use the triangle inequality before passing to the terminal limit. This proves (
20
). The estimate is deliberately wasteful: the proof keeps
\int|\cdot|
, because the positive-production rebase cannot rely on cancellations between active and nonactive pieces.
∎

7.4
Commutator and complement routing

The estimates that make the routing quantitative are as follows. Let
N\simeq R^{-1}
. For a low-frequency coefficient
a_{\leq N/C}
and an annular function
b_{N}
, the Bony commutator estimate gives

\int_{I}\bigl|\langle[P_{N},a_{\leq N/C}\cdot\nabla]b_{N},u_{N}\rangle\bigr|\,dt\lesssim\|\nabla a_{\leq N/C}\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}(I)}\|b_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I)}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}(I)}.

(21)

Thus a small low-frequency gradient is perturbative, while a non-small one is exactly the low-frequency/passive/macro/moving-frame output.

For high-low and high-high off-diagonal pieces, Bernstein gives a gain away from the comparable window. Schematically,

\int_{I}\bigl|\langle P_{N}[(u_{N^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla)v_{M}],u_{N}\rangle\bigr|dt\lesssim 2^{-\sigma|\log_{2}(N^{\prime}/N)|}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm high/desc}(I),

(22)

for some
\sigma>0
, unless the high-frequency factor carries threshold-band score at a smaller scale; in that case it is a high-frequency descendant or fragmentation output.

For cutoff and Leray commutators, the Calderon–Zygmund kernel gives

\|[\Pi,\chi]F\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\operatorname{collar})}+\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{-3m/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}(A_{m})},

(23)

where
A_{m}
are dyadic exterior annuli. The first term is collar/cutoff output; the annular sum is tail, far-field, harmonic pressure-current, or motion output. If the packet center or the hull labels vary in time, differentiating the cutoffs or the active index set produces
\partial_{t}\chi
and
\partial_{t}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
terms; those are by definition temporal-transfer or motion output.

Lemma 7.3

(Commutator routing)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
be the self-adjoint hull operator (
13
). In normalized units,

\displaystyle\int_{I}\bigl|\langle[\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}},u\cdot\nabla]u,U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle\bigr|dt+\int_{I}\bigl|\langle[\Pi,\chi]F,U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle\bigr|dt

(24)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)+C\bigl(\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm low}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm desc}\bigr).

Consequently these commutator fluxes are
o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
on a terminal zero-output/no-descendant sequence.

Proof.

Apply (
21
) to the low-frequency commutators, (
22
) to off-diagonal interactions, and (
23
) to the Leray/cutoff terms. The estimates are all in absolute value. If the right-hand side is not perturbative, the corresponding named output or descendant is positive. Otherwise the commutator flux is
o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
.
∎

7.5
Frequency closure and positive kinetic energy

The closed-hull energy argument needs two facts: a spectral gap and a nonzero kinetic-energy carrier.

Lemma 7.4

(Comparable-frequency closure)
.

Let
U_{\mathcal{H}}
be a flux-tight active hull built from atoms satisfying
c_{0}R^{-1}\leq N_{\alpha}\leq C_{0}R^{-1}
. Then

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq cR^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}-C\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar/cutoff}-C\mathfrak{O}_{\rm low}-o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}.

(25)

In a zero-output sequence,

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(c+o(1))R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

For each annular atom, Bernstein gives
\|\nabla U_{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}\geq cR^{-2}\|U_{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}
. Almost-orthogonality of the finite cover and frequency windows transfers this to the finite sum. Any loss of mass to
\ll R^{-1}
frequencies is the low/passive/macro output; any loss through the spatial cutoff is collar/cutoff output; projection commutators have already been routed by Lemma
7.3
.
∎

Lemma 7.5

(Positive production gives nonzero hull energy)
.

If the hull carries a fixed positive fraction of the active production on a terminal sequence, then its kinetic energy is nonzero on the normalized ancient limit. More precisely, for annular hulls,

\|\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\simeq R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2},

and positive active vorticity score implies
\liminf_{k}R_{k}^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}>0
on a subinterval of the normalized window.

Proof.

The active production density is a trilinear expression involving comparable annular vorticity factors and the Biot–Savart strain of comparable annular velocity. The local Calderon–Zygmund and Bernstein inequalities imply that a fixed positive amount of active production cannot occur with vanishing active vorticity score. Since the hull is annular,
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}=\curl U_{\mathcal{H}}
satisfies
\|\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}\simeq R^{-1}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}
, up to the already routed cutoff outputs. Thus the limiting hull has nonzero kinetic energy.
∎

7.6
Flux-hull stopping

The stopping rule is a finite-overlap selection. Its only delicate case is a cloud of many subthreshold donors whose individual fluxes are small but whose total flux is not.

Lemma 7.6

(Finite subcollection or fragmentation)
.

Let a comparable, on-core donor family produce total absolute flux at least
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
into the current hull. Then either a finite subcollection of donors, with cardinality bounded in terms of
\eta
and the overlap constants, carries at least
c\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
of this flux, or the flux is distributed over many essentially disjoint packet elements. In the second case the fragmentation/shell finite-overlap output or an active descendant is positive.

Proof.

Cover the core and collar by the bounded-overlap packet elements and group donors by comparable frequency and time label. If one bounded group carries a fixed fraction of the flux, take it as the finite subcollection. If no bounded group does, then a positive amount of flux is spread over arbitrarily many essentially disjoint elements. The finite-overlap descendant lemma then selects one subpacket with threshold-band score, or else the averaged spread is precisely the fragmentation/shell output. This is the same pigeonhole mechanism used in the shell and fragmentation descendant lemmas.
∎

Lemma 7.7

(Carleson donor cascades are not silent)
.

Let
\{\mathcal{D}_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
be a family of comparable, on-core, time-stable donor packets feeding a current hull
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that, for some fixed
\eta>0
, their total incoming absolute flux satisfies

\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathfrak{F}_{\mathcal{D}_{j}\to\mathcal{H}}\geq\eta\,\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},

(26)

but no bounded finite subcollection carries a fixed fraction of the left-hand side. Then the donor family cannot remain invisible in a terminal zero-output sequence. One of the following alternatives occurs:

(i)

a subpacket has threshold-band score at least
e_{\rm low}
, hence is an active descendant;

(ii)

the donors escape in normalized space, hence shell or tail output is positive;

(iii)

the donors escape in normalized time, hence temporal-transfer or motion output is positive;

(iv)

the donors stay in a bounded normalized region but occupy arbitrarily many essentially disjoint packet elements, hence fragmentation output is positive.

Consequently, under zero named outputs and no active descendant, a non-negligible donor cascade must be absorbed by a finite enlargement of the hull.

Proof.

Normalize
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}=1
. If a bounded finite subcollection carries a fixed fraction of (
26
), that subcollection is admitted to the hull and the lemma is finished. Otherwise, for every integer
M
, the donor family outside each admissible subcollection of at most
M
labels still carries a fixed positive fraction of the incoming flux. The bounded-overlap packet cover has only four possible ways to accommodate such a tail: it escapes in scale, escapes in space, escapes in time, or contains arbitrarily many essentially disjoint packets in a bounded normalized spatial-frequency-time region.

Scale escape is already one of the scale alternatives in the descendant/output ledger: high-frequency escape gives an active descendant, while low-frequency escape gives low/macro output. Space escape is shell/tail output, and time escape is temporal-transfer or motion output. In the remaining case, a positive amount of incoming flux is distributed over arbitrarily many disjoint packets in a bounded normalized region. If one packet has threshold-band score, it is an active descendant. If no packet has threshold-band score, then a positive amount of normalized funding is spread across disjoint subthreshold packets and is, by definition, fragmentation output. Therefore a terminal zero-output/no-descendant sequence admits no silent donor cascade.
∎

Proposition 7.8

(Flux-hull stopping)
.

Fix a small flux threshold
\eta>0
after the threshold-band constants have been chosen. Start from an active receiver atom carrying a fixed fraction of
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}
. Iteratively add comparable, spatially tight, time-stable donor atoms whose absolute flux into the current hull is nonperturbative. Then exactly one of the following occurs:

(i)

the process terminates after finitely many additions with a hull satisfying
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
;

(ii)

a non-comparable, far, low, harmonic, moving, or off-core donor has nonperturbative flux, hence an existing output is positive;

(iii)

infinitely many comparable donors are needed, hence fragmentation, shell/collar, temporal-transfer, or active descendant output is positive.

Letting
\eta\downarrow 0
along a terminal zero-output/no-descendant sequence gives a flux-closed hull.

Proof.

A donor admitted at threshold
\eta
either carries a definite share of the active flux, or the many-small-donor alternative of Lemma
7.6
applies. A finite amount of threshold-band score and bounded spatial overlap permit only finitely many admitted tight comparable donors unless they merge into a single enlarged finite hull. Scale drift gives high-frequency descendant or low/macro output. Center drift gives shell, tail, or motion output. Time-label drift gives temporal-transfer output. Hence silent infinite enlargement is impossible. If none of these alternatives occurs, the residual absolute flux is below
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and the terminal limit followed by
\eta\downarrow 0
gives closure.
∎

Theorem 7.9

(Flux-closed active hull theorem)
.

Let
Q_{k}
be a normalized terminal threshold-band sequence with positive active production, zero named outputs, and no active descendant. Then there exists a comparable-frequency, spatially tight, time-stable, solenoidal hull
U_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}
carrying a fixed positive fraction of the active production such that

\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(I_{k})}{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(I_{k})}\to 0.

(27)

Moreover,

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(c+o(1))R_{k}^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}\|_{2}^{2},

and the hull has nonzero limiting kinetic energy.

Proof.

Lemma
6.2
selects an active receiver atom. Apply Proposition
7.8
. Outcomes (ii) and (iii) contradict the terminal zero-output and no-descendant hypotheses. Hence a flux-closed hull exists. Lemma
7.3
shows that solenoidal localization and hull projection create no hidden flux. Lemma
7.4
supplies the comparable-frequency spectral lower bound. Lemma
7.5
gives nonzero limiting kinetic energy.
∎

8
Closed active energy Liouville

Theorem 8.1

(Closed active hull Liouville)
.

A nonzero bounded ancient limit of the flux-closed active hulls supplied by Theorem
7.9
cannot exist.

Proof.

Project Navier–Stokes using the self-adjoint hull operator and test against
U_{\mathcal{H}}
. The internal pressure associated with the solenoidal hull vanishes after Leray projection; every pressure term created by localization, the hull/complement split, or the moving window is part of the routed error
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}
and is estimated in Appendix
L.4
. The internal nonlinear term is skew-symmetric in kinetic energy:

\langle(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}},U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle=0,

because
\diver U_{\mathcal{H}}=0
. All discrepancies between the full projected equation and the closed internal equation are contained in
\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}=o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
and the routed commutators. Hence

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Using Lemma
7.4
and passing to normalized time gives

\frac{d}{d\tau}E_{\mathcal{H}}+c\nu E_{\mathcal{H}}\leq o(1)E_{\mathcal{H}}.

A bounded ancient solution with
E_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau_{0})>0
would grow exponentially backward in time, contradicting the bounded active-energy ledger. Thus the limit is zero, contradicting Lemma
7.5
.
∎

9
No silent descent and terminal non-axisymmetric exclusion

The preceding sections eliminate zero production and closed positive-production hulls. The last possible loophole is a terminal sequence that repeatedly selects active-band descendants or coherent direction fields without ever producing a new singularity-forcing packet, a named output, or a flux-closed hull. This section records the two facts that close this loophole. They are not additional hypotheses; they are the terminal form of the threshold-band and flux-hull stopping mechanisms.

Definition 9.1

(Strict terminal order)
.

A descendant is called
strict
if it improves the selected packet in at least one of the following well-founded coordinates, in the indicated order:

\text{scale}\quad\prec\quad\text{terminal time}\quad\prec\quad\text{spatial lattice position}\quad\prec\quad\text{active-hull rank}.

The scale coordinate is dyadic; the time and spatial coordinates are chosen on the normalized packet lattice; the active-hull rank is the finite-overlap admission number in the hull construction. A terminal sequence is selected so that no strict descendant remains available after passing to a subsequence.

Definition 9.2

(Active conveyors, shape jumps, and scale gaps)
.

An
active conveyor
is an infinite descendant chain

Q_{k,0}\succ Q_{k,1}\succ Q_{k,2}\succ\cdots

inside the warning band, with each link carrying nonperturbative positive active production into the next selected packet. Write

Q_{k,j}=Q_{R_{k,j}}(x_{k,j},t_{k,j}),\qquad u_{k,j}(y,s)=R_{k,j}u(x_{k,j}+R_{k,j}y,t_{k,j}+R_{k,j}^{2}s).

For two normalized packets define a compact local shape distance

d_{\rm sh}(v,w)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{-m}\min\Bigl\{1,\ \|v-w\|_{L^{3}(Q_{m})}+\|\curl v-\curl w\|_{L^{2}(Q_{m})}+\|p_{v}-p_{w}\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{m})}\Bigr\},

where pressures are normalized by subtracting their space averages on each ball. This distance is measured only after the classical terminal translation, Galilean correction, time normalization, and rigid rotation have been applied.

The conveyor has
shape-jump output
if the corrected normalized profiles fail to be Cauchy in the local active topology. More precisely, set

\operatorname{osc}_{\rm sh}(N):=\sup_{j,\ell\geq N}d_{\rm sh}(u_{k,j},u_{k,\ell})

after applying the terminal translations, Galilean corrections, rigid rotations, pressure normalizations, and the natural Navier–Stokes rescalings to a common unit packet. Then

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\limsup_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{osc}_{\rm sh}(N)>0.

This tail-oscillation definition is stronger than merely measuring consecutive jumps: it rules out slow drift with
d_{\rm sh}(u_{k,j+1},u_{k,j})\to 0
but no compact recurrent limit.

The conveyor has
scale-gap output
if the ratios

\lambda_{k,j}:=R_{k,j}/R_{k,j+1}

leave every compact subset of
(1,\infty)
, fail to have a terminal Cauchy subsequence after the chosen normalization, or if an inner-boundary energy/current term remains nonperturbative between the parent and child scales. The corresponding normalized outputs are denoted by

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape},\qquad\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}.

A terminal zero-output sequence is henceforth understood to satisfy

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}=0

as well as the outputs in Definition
3.7
.

Proposition 9.3

(Active conveyor alternative)
.

Let
Q_{k,j}
be an active conveyor issued from a terminal singularity-forcing packet. Then, after passing to a subsequence, one of the following alternatives holds:

(i)

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}>0
;

(ii)

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}>0
;

(iii)

shell, tail, temporal, motion, pressure-current, fragmentation, or axis-equivariance output is positive;

(iv)

a strict singularity-forcing descendant is selected;

(v)

the conveyor is compact recurrent: the ratios
\lambda_{k,j}
converge to some
\lambda\in(1,\infty)
, the normalized centers and times converge in the terminal frame, and the normalized profiles form a Cauchy family in
L^{3}_{\rm loc}
for velocity,
L^{2}_{\rm loc}
for vorticity, and
L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}
for pressure. In particular, a nonzero ancient suitable recurrent limit exists.

Proof.

If the normalized shapes do not form a Cauchy family after terminal frame correction, then the first alternative holds by the tail-oscillation definition of
\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}
. If the scale ratios leave compact subsets of
(1,\infty)
, fail to have a terminal Cauchy subsequence, or if nonperturbative current crosses the annular region between parent and child scales, the second alternative holds. If centers, time labels, axes, pressure labels, or active-frame labels drift, then the corresponding shell, tail, temporal, motion, pressure, fragmentation, or axis-equivariance output is positive by the output ledger. If a child re-crosses the singularity-forcing level
e_{\rm sing}
with a smaller scale or better terminal order, then a strict descendant is selected. If none of these alternatives occurs, all normalized parameters stay in a compact set, the scale ratios and terminal frames are Cauchy, and the shapes are Cauchy on every compact cylinder. The local energy bounds and pressure normalization give the asserted compact recurrent subsequence and a nonzero ancient suitable limit because the singularity-forcing score is lower semicontinuous under the terminal compactness topology. These alternatives are exhaustive.
∎

Lemma 9.4

(Zero conveyor outputs give strong recurrent compactness)
.

Assume an active conveyor satisfies

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}=\mathfrak{F}_{\rm frag}=0.

Then, after passing to a subsequence and applying the terminal frame corrections, there is a ratio
\lambda\in(1,\infty)
and an ancient suitable limit
(U,P)
such that

u_{k,j}\to U\quad\text{in }L^{3}_{\rm loc},\qquad\curl u_{k,j}\to\curl U\quad\text{in }L^{2}_{\rm loc},

with the pressures converging in
L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}
after subtracting averages. Moreover the active hull energies and dissipations are lower semicontinuous and no positive singularity-forcing vorticity score is lost in the limit.

Proof.

Zero shape output is precisely the Cauchy property in the local active topology. Zero scale output gives a terminal Cauchy subsequence of the scale ratios with limit
\lambda\in(1,\infty)
. Zero motion and temporal outputs fix the centers and time origins in the terminal frame. Zero fragmentation prevents the active mass from splitting into separated compactness classes, and zero tail output prevents loss of the active score through spatial infinity. Hence the normalized profiles are Cauchy on every compact cylinder in the topology defining
d_{\rm sh}
. Completeness gives the stated strong local limits. Lower semicontinuity of the scale-invariant score and the singularity-forcing lower bound give nontriviality of the limit; any missing positive score would be exactly a tail, fragmentation, or time-face output, all of which are excluded.
∎

Lemma 9.5

(Zero flux outputs give a closed scale-period energy identity)
.

Let the compact recurrent limit from Lemma
9.4
be taken over one normalized scale period. If

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm macro}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shell}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm passive}=0,

then the recurrent active hull
\mathcal{H}
satisfies

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}(s)|\,ds\leq o(1)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}(s)\,ds,\qquad\bar{T}=\log\lambda,

after the terminal limit. Consequently the hull energy obeys the closed scale-period inequality

E_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau+\bar{T})+\nu\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}(s)\,ds\leq E_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)+o(1)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}(s)\,ds.

Proof.

Apply the master absolute flux-routing inequality (
41
) on the recurrent hull. The terms in
\mathfrak{O}_{\rm rout}
vanish by assumption. The remaining comparable on-core flux is internal to the recurrent finite hull: if it were external, finite-overlap admission would enlarge the hull; if it were diffuse, fragmentation output would be positive; if it crossed the inter-scale annulus, scale-gap output would be positive. Thus
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}=o(D_{\mathcal{H}})
on the scale period. Letting the terminal sequence run and then

Theorem 9.6

(Compact recurrent active conveyor Liouville)
.

Theorem 9.8

(No silent active descent)
.

Let
Q_{k}
be a terminal threshold-band sequence. Suppose an infinite chain of active descendants remains in the active band,

e_{\rm low}\leq\mathcal{Q}(Q_{k,j})\leq e_{\rm sing},

and suppose that along the chain no named output is positive and no flux-closed active hull is obtained. Then the chain cannot fund the terminal singularity-forcing packet. More precisely, one of the following alternatives must occur:

(i)

the chain re-crosses the high threshold

at a strict descendant, contradicting terminality;

(ii)

the active score drops below
e_{\rm low}
on every compact normalized subpacket, so the chain cannot supply the first-threshold funding;

(iii)

the chain escapes in scale, space, or time, producing high-frequency descendant, low/macro, shell/tail, motion, or temporal-transfer output;

(iv)

the descendants remain comparable, tight, and time-stable, in which case the flux-hull stopping theorem absorbs them into a closed active hull or gives fragmentation output.

Thus no infinite active-band cascade is silent on a terminal zero-output/no-descendant sequence.

Proof.

Normalize each descendant to the unit packet. If the chain has a subsequence with score at least
e_{\rm sing}
, the well-founded order in Definition
9.1
selects a strict singularity-forcing descendant, contrary to terminality. If no such subsequence exists and the score eventually falls below
e_{\rm low}
on every compact subpacket, then the chain has lost the active-band funding needed to support the terminal score.

It remains to consider a chain whose score stays in the active band on bounded normalized packets. If the centers, time labels, or scales escape, the corresponding shell/tail, temporal/motion, or scale-descendant output is positive by the output definitions. If there is no escape, the descendants lie in a bounded comparable spatial-frequency-time region. Bounded overlap gives a locally finite packet cover. Either a finite subcollection carries a fixed amount of the active flux, in which case it is admitted to the hull, or Lemma
7.7
gives fragmentation or a named output. If the admitted finite hull becomes flux-closed, Theorem
8.1
kills it. If it does not, Proposition
7.8
supplies another strict descendant or an output. Equivalently, any remaining chain is an active conveyor. Corollary
9.7
eliminates such a conveyor outside the flat two-dimensional and axisymmetric-with-swirl classes. Hence an infinite silent active descent is impossible.
∎

Lemma 9.18

(One-axis equivariant vorticity implies axisymmetric swirl)

10
Final active-rebase and selected-output assembly

This section records the final form of the active-rebase package used in the proof of the singular-endpoint reduction. It replaces two unsafe shortcuts. First, absolute donor mass is not enough, because the production density is signed-positive. Second, a selected donor family does not automatically survive passage to an exact orthogonal hull. The definitions and theorems below keep these two issues visible.

10.1
Scale-correct active envelope

Let
Q_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-R^{2},t_{0})

Lemma 10.1

(Scale-correct active envelope)
.

\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}|T(\chi_{4}P_{>K}\omega)|\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{A}\|P_{>K}\omega\|_{L^{2}(Q_{4})}+\text{spectral/cutoff error}.

For the exterior term the kernel bound
|K(x-y)|\lesssim|x-y|^{-3}
gives the dyadic far-field/tail currency. Therefore

\mathfrak{E}_{>K}\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{A}\|P_{>K}\omega\|_{L^{2}(Q_{4})}+\mathcal{M}_{A}\mathfrak{T}_{>K}^{\rm far}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm spec/cut}.

If the double-limit high-frequency tail does not vanish, decompose
P_{>K}\omega^{(k)}=\sum_{q>K}\Delta_{q}\omega^{(k)}
and cover
Q_{4}
by parabolic boxes
P_{q,\alpha}
of radius
2^{-q}
and time length
2^{-2q}
, with bounded overlap. Define

\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}^{(k)}=2^{q}\iint_{P_{q,\alpha}^{*}}|\Delta_{q}\omega^{(k)}|^{2}\,dxdt.

A box with
\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}^{(k)}\geq\eta
is a strict descendant. If no such box exists while the tail remains nonzero, the mass is distributed through many boxes/scales; the corresponding positive measure

\mathfrak{C}_{\rm hf}^{(k)}(K)=\sum_{q>K}\sum_{\alpha}2^{-q}\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}^{(k)}

is the conveyor currency. Bounded-overlap occurrences spend finite currency. Failure of bounded-overlap depletion gives concentration in scale-depth; rescaling a concentration sequence gives an ancient high-frequency conveyor profile. This proves the alternatives.
∎

10.3
Positive-signed comparable donor selection

Comparable strain is decomposed into wave-packet donors:

S_{\rm cmp}=\sum_{G}\sum_{\alpha\in G}S[u_{\alpha}]+S_{\rm res},

where
G
ranges over coherence cells. For a cell define the positive signed donor mass

b_{G}^{+}:=\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}\left[\sum_{\alpha\in G}\xi_{A}\cdot S[u_{\alpha}]\xi_{A}\right]_{+}\,dxdt.

This is different from the unsigned mass
\sum_{\alpha}\iint A^{2}|\xi_{A}\cdot S[u_{\alpha}]\xi_{A}|
, which can be large even when the signed production cancels.

Lemma 10.3

(Positive-signed comparable donor selection)
.

Assume

\mathfrak{P}_{\rm cmp}:=\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}(\xi_{A}\cdot S_{\rm cmp}\xi_{A})_{+}\,dxdt\geq p_{1}>0,

and

\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}|S_{\rm res}|\,dxdt\leq p_{1}/8.

Then either signed-cancellation/oscillation output occurs, or

\sum_{G}b_{G}^{+}\geq cp_{1}.

Moreover, from this lower bound one obtains either a bounded-overlap selected family
\mathcal{G}_{*}
with

\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{*}}b_{G}^{+}\geq c^{\prime}p_{1},

or fragmentation, strict descendant/scale-depth output, or no-overcount Carleson output.

Proof.

Since
[\sum_{G}f_{G}]_{+}\leq\sum_{G}[f_{G}]_{+}
, positive comparable production is dominated by the sum of the positive signed cell masses, up to the residual error. If the grouped signed strain fails to approximate the comparable strain, the failure is exactly the signed-cancellation/oscillation output. Define the donor measure
\nu(G)=b_{G}^{+}
. A tree
\mathcal{T}
of comparable donors is required to satisfy the Carleson no-overcount inequality

\nu(\mathcal{T})\leq C\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{T}),

where
\mathfrak{B}
is the finite combination of active score, positive production currency, hull-flux currency, and selected-output currency attached to the tree. Failure of this inequality is the no-overcount output. Under the inequality, the classical maximal selection over packet cores gives a bounded-overlap family carrying a fixed fraction of
\nu
. If this is impossible, the mass either splits into separated spatial/frequency/frame/signed-coherence classes, which is fragmentation, or descends through scales, which is a strict descendant or scale-depth output.
∎

10.4
Stable orthogonal rebase and spectral hull coercivity

Let
\mathcal{G}_{*}
be the selected donor family. Define the selected solenoidal donor field by

W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{*}}P_{\sim 1}\mathbb{P}_{\rm Leray}u_{\alpha}.

Let

\mathcal{H}:=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{P_{\sim 1}\mathbb{P}_{\rm Leray}u_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathcal{G}_{*}\}}^{L^{2}}\subset P_{\sim 1}L^{2}_{\sigma},

and let
P_{\mathcal{H}}
be the exact
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection. Put
U_{\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}u
.

Lemma 10.4

(Stable orthogonal rebase)
.

Assume the selected donor family satisfies the Riesz/Bessel stability bounds associated with the preceding Carleson selection. Then either projection-defect/hull-nonclosure output occurs, or

\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}\left[\xi_{A}\cdot S[W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}]\xi_{A}\right]_{+}\,dxdt\leq C\iint_{Q_{1}}A^{2}|S[U_{\mathcal{H}}]|\,dxdt+o(1).

Proof.

Write
u=W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}+u_{\rm rem}
. Since
W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}\in\mathcal{H}
,

U_{\mathcal{H}}=P_{\mathcal{H}}u=W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}+P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}.

Hence

S[W_{\mathcal{G}_{*}}]=S[U_{\mathcal{H}}]-S[P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}].

Multiplying by
A^{2}
and integrating gives the stated inequality, except for

\iint A^{2}|S[P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}]|.

This term is bounded by
\mathcal{M}_{A}\|\nabla P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}\|_{L^{2}}
. If it is not perturbative it is, by definition, the projection-defect/hull-nonclosure output. The Riesz/Bessel condition prevents the selected representation from degenerating; failure of that condition is already the no-overcount or projection-defect output.
∎

Lemma 10.5

(Spectral hull coercivity)
.

If
\mathcal{H}\subset P_{\sim 1}L^{2}_{\sigma}
, then

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq c\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

If a selected hull contains nonperturbative
(I-P_{\sim 1})
-leakage, then spectral leakage output occurs.

Proof.

The first statement is the Bernstein lower bound on the finite annulus
|\xi|\simeq 1
. The second is the definition of the spectral leakage alternative after the hull is constructed inside
P_{\sim 1}L^{2}_{\sigma}
.
∎

Theorem 10.6

(Positive production to orthogonal hull or routed output)
.

If a normalized packet satisfies
\mathfrak{P}_{+}(Q_{1})\geq p_{0}>0
, then one of the following occurs:

(i)

active-envelope concentration or spectral/cutoff output;

(ii)

low, macro, harmonic, far-field, pressure, motion, commutator, collar, tail, or high-frequency output;

(iii)

fragmentation, signed-coherence, no-overcount, projection-defect, spectral-leakage, or hull-nonclosure output;

(iv)

a strict active descendant;

(v)

an exact orthogonal solenoidal finite-band hull
\mathcal{H}
such that

\iint_{Q_{1}}|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\,dxdt\geq c(p_{0})>0.

Proof.

Decompose the strain into comparable and non-comparable pieces. If low, high, far, macro, motion, pressure, commutator, collar, tail, or spectral pieces are not perturbative, the corresponding output is selected. Otherwise positive production survives in the comparable part. Lemma
10.3
selects positive-signed comparable donors or a routed alternative. Lemma
10.4
rebases the selected donors into the exact orthogonal hull, up to projection-defect output. If no output occurs, then

cp_{0}\leq C\iint A^{2}|S[U_{\mathcal{H}}]|.

By Holder and Lemma
10.1
,

\iint A^{2}|S[U_{\mathcal{H}}]|\leq\mathcal{M}_{A}\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

The active envelope is bounded on the terminal no-output branch, hence
\int\!\!\int|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\geq c(p_{0})>0
. Spectral hull coercivity then makes the hull nontrivial at the kinetic level.
∎

10.5
Selected-output packing and profile routing

The outputs produced by Theorem
10.6
are not allowed to remain as unexamined terminal branches. The next two theorems are the final assembly lock for the reduction.

Theorem 10.7

(Selected-output packing or profile)
.

Let
Q_{k}
be a normalized terminal first-threshold sequence and let
\mathfrak{O}
be one of the selected output types: high-frequency conveyor, no-overcount Carleson failure, projection defect, spectral leakage, or hull nonclosure. If
\mathfrak{O}(Q_{k})\geq\delta>0
along a subsequence, then one of the following holds:

(i)

the selected outputs have bounded overlap and deplete a finite global currency;

(ii)

a strict active descendant occurs;

(iii)

after rescaling, a nonzero ancient selected-output profile is extracted, with all lower-priority outputs absent.

Proof.

For each selected output define its positive measure: the conveyor measure
\sum_{q,\alpha}2^{-q}\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}\delta_{(q,\alpha)}
, the Carleson donor measure
\sum_{G}b_{G}^{+}\delta_{G}
, the projection-defect density
A^{2}|S[P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}]|\,dxdt
, the spectral-leakage energy density of
(I-P_{\sim 1})U_{\mathcal{H}}
, and the hull-nonclosure flux density
|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm nl}|+|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm diff}|+|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm mot}|
. If selected packets have bounded overlap, these measures spend the corresponding finite global currency. If bounded-overlap packing fails, choose a concentration sequence and normalize it to unit scale. The terminal bounds and lower semicontinuity give a nonzero ancient profile. If compactness fails during this extraction, the failure is a strict descendant, macro/tail output, active-envelope loss, or spectral leakage, all already listed in the alternatives.
∎

Theorem 10.8

(Selected-output ancient profile routing)
.

No selected-output ancient profile is a new genuinely three-dimensional non-axisymmetric terminal branch. More precisely, every such profile either produces a strict active descendant, spends finite selected-output currency, is routed to another lower-priority selected output, is killed by the closed-hull energy Liouville theorem, or reduces to the flat two-dimensional or axisymmetric-with-swirl endpoint class.

Proof.

The proof is by the priority order

\text{strict descendant}>\text{macro/tail/high/motion}>\text{projection/spectral/no-overcount}>\text{Campanato terminal class}.

A high-frequency conveyor profile either concentrates in a child packet or loses active-scale compactness; these are a descendant or a spectral/noncompact selected output. A no-overcount Carleson profile is not a new PDE profile: same-core same-orientation donors aggregate, different orientations give signed fragmentation, and frame degeneration gives projection defect. A projection-defect profile means
P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}
carries comparable hull content; it either enlarges the hull or gives hull nonclosure. Spectral leakage splits into low leakage, which is macro/harmonic, and high leakage, which is a high-frequency descendant or conveyor. Hull nonclosure is one of nonlinear flux, diffusion leakage, or motion flux. A comparable nonclosed component is admitted to the maximal hull; a low, high, far, moving, or collar component is the corresponding output. At fixed scale a maximal hull stopping rule prevents infinite same-scale enlargement; across scales the preceding alternatives produce a strict descendant or selected-output profile of lower priority. Thus the iteration is well founded. The only compact coherent terminal classes left after the selected outputs vanish are the fixed-direction flat class and the one-axis equivariant class, which are handled in Section
9
.
∎

10.6
Well-founded selected-output termination

The preceding packing and profile statements are useful only if selected-output routing is a well-founded operation. We now record the final compactness and rank package in a form that does not rely on direct weak convergence of nonlinear products. The principle is: a selected-output measure either survives as a positive defect measure in the limit, or the loss of measure is itself a stricter output, a strict descendant, or a finite-currency event.

Definition 10.9

(Clean selected-output rank)
.

A profile of selected-output rank
j
is called clean if every selected output of rank strictly larger than
j
has already vanished or has exited to rank zero. Rank zero denotes one of the already terminal alternatives: finite currency depletion, a strict active descendant, flat two-dimensional regularity, axisymmetric-with-swirl reduction, closed-hull contradiction, or a macro/tail/motion/collar output already handled by the global ledger. The selected-output ranks are

\rho(\mathfrak{O}_{C})=4,\qquad\rho(\mathfrak{O}_{P})=3,\qquad\rho(\mathfrak{O}_{H})=2,\qquad\rho(\mathfrak{O}_{S})=1,

where
\mathfrak{O}_{C}
is Carleson/no-overcount failure,
\mathfrak{O}_{P}
is projection defect,
\mathfrak{O}_{H}
is hull nonclosure, and
\mathfrak{O}_{S}
is spectral/high-frequency leakage.

Lemma 10.10

(Terminal active-scale compactness with double limits)
.

Let
\omega_{A}^{(k)}
be normalized active vorticity profiles on a compact cylinder
K\Subset Q
. Assume

\sup_{k}\|\omega_{A}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(K)}<\infty,

and assume the spatial, high-frequency, and low-frequency tightness conditions

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\omega_{A}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(K\cap\{|x|>R\})}=0,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|P_{>N}\omega_{A}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(K)}=0,\qquad\lim_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|P_{<N^{-1}}\omega_{A}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(K)}=0.

Then a subsequence converges strongly in
(C^{1}(K))^{*}
. If one of the tightness hypotheses fails along a terminal sequence, the failure is, respectively, a tail/collar output, a spectral/high-frequency output or strict descendant, or a low-frequency macro/harmonic output.

Proof.

Fix
\eta>0
. Choose
R
and then
N
so that the spatial tail and both frequency tails have
L^{2}
-size at most
\eta
in the indicated double limits. Decompose

\omega_{A}^{(k)}=\chi_{R}P_{[N^{-1},N]}\omega_{A}^{(k)}+r_{R,N}^{(k)},\qquad\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|r_{R,N}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\eta.

The first term has fixed compact support and fixed frequency band. Bounded sets of this class are compact in
H^{-s}(K)
for every
s>0
. Since
C^{1}(K)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(K)
for
0<s<1
, compactness in
H^{-s}
implies compactness in
(C^{1}(K))^{*}
. Letting
\eta\downarrow 0
gives the claim. The listed failure alternatives are precisely the negations of the three double-limit tightness assumptions, together with the boundedness assumption.
∎

Lemma 10.11

(Selected-output defect-measure compactness)
.

Let
\mathfrak{O}\in\{\mathfrak{O}_{S},\mathfrak{O}_{C},\mathfrak{O}_{P},\mathfrak{O}_{H}\}
, and let
\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{(k)}
be the corresponding positive selected-output measure. Suppose

\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{(k)}(Q_{1})\geq\delta>0.

Then, after passing to a subsequence and, if necessary, replacing
Q_{1}
by
Q_{1-\varepsilon}
, one of the following alternatives holds:

\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{(k)}\rightharpoonup^{*}\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{\infty}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{\infty}(Q_{1-\varepsilon})\geq c_{\varepsilon}\delta,

or a stricter alternative occurs: a strict descendant, spatial tail escape, macro/low-frequency escape, active-envelope concentration, spectral leakage, or projection/hull instability.

Proof.

The measures are nonnegative Radon measures on the physical cylinder together with the compactified output labels. Banach–Alaoglu gives weak-* subsequential limits. If a definite amount of mass does not remain inside
Q_{1-\varepsilon}
, then the missing mass has escaped through the parabolic boundary, to high frequency, to low frequency, to a smaller parabolic scale, or through instability of the moving projection/hull. These are exactly the tail/collar, spectral/high-frequency, macro/harmonic, strict-descendant, and projection/hull-output alternatives.

For product-type outputs one does not identify the limit measure only with the product of weak limits. For instance,

A_{k}^{2}\bigl|S[P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}u_{\rm rem}^{(k)}]\bigr|\,dxdt

may converge to a positive defect measure. If this measure is realized by

A_{\infty}^{2}\bigl|S[P_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}}u_{\rm rem}^{\infty}]\bigr|\,dxdt,

the rank proof uses the realized expression. If it is not realized, the discrepancy is a concentration, oscillation, or projection-instability defect; by definition this is a stricter selected output or a strict descendant. The same realization-or-rerouting convention is used for hull-nonclosure flux measures.
∎

Lemma 10.12

(Product-by-product defect realization or rerouting)
.

Let
\mu_{\mathfrak{O}}^{(k)}
be one of the product-type selected-output measures appearing in Lemma
10.11
. Then every positive weak-* limit of these measures has one of the following two forms. Either it is realized by the corresponding limiting fields and limiting hull, or its non-realized part is itself a stricter selected output, a strict descendant, or a finite-currency loss already present in the selected-output ledger.

More explicitly:

1.

For projection defect,

\mu_{P}^{(k)}=A_{k}^{2}\bigl|S[P_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}u_{\rm rem}^{(k)}]\bigr|\,dxdt.

If the active multiplier
A_{k}^{2}
is compact in the active testing topology and the projected strains converge in the Mosco-hull weak topology, then the limiting defect is realized by

A_{\infty}^{2}\bigl|S[P_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}}u_{\rm rem}^{\infty}]\bigr|\,dxdt.

Loss of multiplier compactness is an active-envelope, high-frequency, or tail output. Loss of projected-strain convergence is projection defect or spectral leakage. Concentration into a smaller parabolic cylinder is a strict descendant.

2.

For hull nonclosure,

\mu_{H}^{(k)}=|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm nl}^{(k)}|+|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm diff}^{(k)}|+|\mathfrak{F}_{\rm mot}^{(k)}|+\mathfrak{J}_{\rm jump}^{(k)}.

The nonlinear part is realized when one factor has strong active-scale compactness and the other has weak hull convergence. Failure of this strong–weak product passage is a descendant, spectral leakage, projection defect, or tail/macro output. The diffusion part is realized when the hull and complement gradients converge in the Mosco splitting; failure is projection defect, spectral leakage, or collar loss. The motion and jump parts are positive measures by construction; disappearance through time faces or moving frames is exactly motion output or strict descendant.

3.

For Carleson/no-overcount measures, weak-* loss can occur only through noncompact label directions: scale-depth escape, separated signed-coherence classes, or excessive multiplicity. These are, respectively, strict descendant or spectral output, signed fragmentation, or frame/no-overcount output.

4.

For spectral/high-frequency measures, weak-* loss is high-frequency escape, low-frequency macro escape, spatial tail escape, or smaller-scale concentration. These are
\mathfrak{O}_{S}
, macro/tail output, or strict descendant.

Consequently the selected-output rank proof never uses an identity obtained from two unrelated weak limits. It uses realized defect measures when they are realized, and otherwise routes the non-realized defect by the selected-output ordering.

Proof.

All measures in the statement are positive Radon measures after localization to a compact cylinder and after adjoining the compactified label space. Banach–Alaoglu gives weak-* subsequential limits. The product structure is used only to classify how a weak-* limit can fail to be represented by the product of the limiting fields. Loss of compactness of the active multiplier is exactly loss of the active-scale compactness hypotheses in Lemma
10.10
. Loss of hull convergence is exactly failure of the frame/Mosco alternatives in Lemma
10.15
. Loss by concentration produces a smaller selected packet. Loss by spatial, frequency, or label escape is one of the named selected outputs. These alternatives are disjoint from the realized product case and exhaust all possible ways positive mass can fail to be represented by the limiting expression.
∎

Lemma 10.13

(High-frequency conveyor descent or finite currency)
.

Let
\omega^{\infty}
be an ancient terminal profile. If

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\|P_{>N}\omega^{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}>0,

then either a strict smaller-scale active descendant exists, a nonoverlapping high-frequency conveyor currency is spent, or terminal active-scale compactness fails. Thus a clean rank-one high-frequency selected-output profile cannot persist as a compact terminal active-scale profile.

Proof.

For each dyadic
q
, cover
Q_{1}
by parabolic boxes

P_{q,\alpha}=B_{2^{-q}}(x_{q,\alpha})\times I_{2^{-2q}}(t_{q,\alpha})

with bounded overlap, and define

\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}=2^{q}\iint_{P_{q,\alpha}^{*}}|\Delta_{q}\omega^{\infty}|^{2}\,dxdt.

If
\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}\geq\eta_{0}
for some
(q,\alpha)
, then
P_{q,\alpha}
is a strict active descendant. If no child crosses the threshold while high-frequency mass persists, define the conveyor currency

\mathfrak{C}_{\rm hf}(N)=\sum_{q>N}\sum_{\alpha}2^{-q}\mathcal{Q}_{q,\alpha}.

Under bounded overlap, this currency is dominated by the selected high-frequency square-function ledger of the terminal family; each definite conveyor event spends at least a fixed positive amount. Hence infinitely many such events are impossible while the ledger is finite. If bounded overlap fails, concentration compactness extracts an ancient high-frequency conveyor profile. Such a profile violates the high-frequency tightness hypothesis in Lemma
10.10
, unless it is reselected at a smaller scale. Therefore the rank-one branch exits to a descendant, finite conveyor depletion, or terminal compactness failure.
∎

Lemma 10.14

(Frame-failure dichotomy for maximal hulls)
.

Let

e_{\alpha}^{(k)}=P_{\sim 1}\mathbb{P}_{\rm Leray}u_{\alpha}^{(k)}

be a selected solenoidal finite-band donor family. On every selected coherent finite subfamily exactly one of the following alternatives holds.

1.

The uniform frame bounds

c_{R}\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}^{(k)}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{B}\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}

hold with constants independent of
k
.

2.

The upper Bessel bound fails. Then the selected donors have excessive multiplicity or nonorthogonal overlap at the same active scale; this is a no-overcount/Carleson output or spectral leakage.

3.

The lower Riesz bound fails. Then a nontrivial signed combination of selected donors collapses after Leray projection, frequency projection, or localization; this is projection-defect output or signed-coherence fragmentation.

Thus Mosco convergence of maximal hulls is asserted only in the first alternative. In the other alternatives the failure is itself a selected output already ordered by the rank theorem.

Proof.

If the upper estimate fails, bounded coefficients produce a selected sum whose
L^{2}
-mass is too large relative to the counted donor weights. This is precisely the failure of the Carleson/no-overcount bookkeeping, unless the excess is created by leakage outside the finite-band window, in which case it is spectral leakage. If the lower estimate fails, a nonzero selected combination is annihilated or nearly annihilated by the solenoidal/frequency/localized reconstruction. Such cancellation can only be a projection defect or a signed-coherence fragmentation, because the individual donors have positive signed contribution before the projection. If neither failure occurs, the uniform frame bounds hold and the hull construction may be treated by the Mosco compactness lemma.
∎

Lemma 10.15

(Mosco/frame compactness for maximal hulls)
.

Let
\{e_{\alpha}^{(k)}\}_{\alpha}
be the selected countable donor family

e_{\alpha}^{(k)}=P_{\sim 1}\mathbb{P}_{\rm Leray}u_{\alpha}^{(k)},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{k}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{e_{\alpha}^{(k)}\}}^{L^{2}}\subset P_{\sim 1}L^{2}_{\sigma}.

Assume the uniform Bessel bound and lower Riesz bound on every selected coherent finite subfamily,

\left\|\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}^{(k)}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{B}\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|^{2},

c_{R}\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}\leq\left\|\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}^{(k)}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

By Lemma
10.14
, if either frame bound fails, no-overcount, projection-defect, signed-fragmentation, or spectral-leakage output is selected. If the bounds hold uniformly, then a subsequence satisfies Mosco convergence
\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
. Consequently the orthogonal projections converge strongly on strongly convergent data, provided no projection-defect output occurs.

Proof.

The selected labels are countable and satisfy a uniform Bessel bound, so finite linear combinations are uniformly controlled and the hull closure is well-defined. Signed-label compactness and terminal active-scale compactness allow a diagonal extraction on a countable dense family of persistent labels. Define
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
as the closed span of their strong local
L^{2}
-limits. The upper Bessel bound prevents repeated donors from producing false multiplicity, while the lower Riesz bound prevents nontrivial selected combinations from collapsing. If either bound fails, the failure is exactly no-overcount, projection-defect, or spectral leakage.

For Mosco condition M2, approximate any
h\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
by a finite linear combination of limiting donors and lift that finite combination to
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. For M1, a weak limit of vectors
h_{k}\in\mathcal{H}_{k}
belongs to the weak closure of the span of persistent donor limits, hence to
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
. Mosco convergence of closed subspaces gives strong convergence of orthogonal projections on strongly convergent vectors.
∎

Lemma 10.16

(Profile-level maximal hull closure)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{k}\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
be as in Lemma
10.15
. Suppose a limiting donor
v^{\infty}
is comparable, finite-band, positive-signed, and has nonzero selected contribution. Then
v^{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
, unless a stricter output or strict descendant occurs.

Proof.

The contribution is detected by finitely many active tests and a positive signed donor mass. Approximating donors
v^{(k)}
therefore carry contribution bounded below for all large
k
. Since the terminal sieve threshold tends to zero, each
v^{(k)}
is selected and belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{k}
. Mosco M1 passes membership to the limit. If the approximating donors change scale, change core, or disappear weakly while keeping selected contribution, the event is respectively spectral/high-frequency output or descendant, tail/macro output, or selected-output defect measure; these are the stated stricter alternatives.
∎

Lemma 10.17

(Carleson failure structure)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{k}
be comparable donor trees with positive signed donor measure

\nu_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}b_{G}^{+,(k)}.

If

\nu_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})>C_{k}\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{T}_{k}),\qquad C_{k}\to\infty,

then, after normalization and profile extraction, one of the following occurs: same-core same-sign collapse contradicting maximal aggregation, signed fragmentation, or frame degeneration giving projection-defect/no-overcount output.

Proof.

Normalize
\nu_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{k})=1
. The donor-label space is compact after signed-label compactness, so
\nu_{k}
has a weak limit. If the limit is supported in one core/sign/frame cell, maximal positive-signed grouping should have aggregated it into one selected donor, contradicting the sieve. If the limit charges separated signed-coherence classes, signed fragmentation is selected. If neither alternative occurs, the labels collapse while the multiplicity remains excessive; the finite-frame map
(c_{\alpha})\mapsto\sum c_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}
then violates the Bessel or lower Riesz bound. That is frame degeneration, hence no-overcount or projection-defect output.
∎

Theorem 10.18

(Well-founded selected-output rank termination)
.

Use the clean-rank convention of Definition
10.9
. Every clean selected-output ancient profile either exits to rank zero or strictly decreases selected-output rank. Consequently no infinite selected-output cycle is possible.

Proof.

For rank four, Lemma
10.17
gives aggregation contradiction or signed fragmentation, both rank zero, or projection defect, rank three.

For rank three, projection defect is carried by
P_{\mathcal{H}}u_{\rm rem}
. If the effect is comparable and positive-signed, Lemma
10.16
places it in the limiting hull. Since it is simultaneously a projected remainder, orthogonality gives a contradiction unless hull nonclosure is present, which is rank two. If the effect is non-comparable, it is spectral, macro, tail, or motion output, hence rank one or zero.

For rank two, hull nonclosure is nonlinear flux, diffusion leakage, motion flux, or jump cost. A comparable positive-signed responsible component is admitted to the maximal hull by Lemma
10.16
; orthogonality contradicts its role as an exterior complement. Non-comparable components are spectral, macro, tail, collar, or motion outputs, so the rank decreases to one or exits to zero. Projection instability cannot appear as a clean rank-two profile, because clean rank two assumes all higher-rank projection defects have been removed.

For rank one, low-frequency leakage is macro/harmonic rank zero. High-frequency leakage or conveyor is handled by Lemma
10.13

The endpoint step is an exhaustion statement, not an additional geometric black box. The preceding sections have eliminated every non-axisymmetric mechanism that can fund a terminal first-threshold packet. Lemma 11.1

Appendix A
First-threshold selection in full detail

This appendix expands the compactness and minimality step used in Section

A.1
Admissible packets and terminal normalization

Fix a possible terminal time
T
. For each scale

\mathcal{Q}_{R}(T)=\{B_{R}(x)\times(T-R^{2},T):x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\}.

For a packet

E(Q)=\sup_{t\in(T-R^{2},T)}R\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{R}^{2}|\omega(x,t)|^{2}\,dx.

If the solution remains regular at

Lemma A.1

(Minimal singularity-forcing packet selection)
.

Assume that a first singular time exists. Then there is a sequence
Q_{R_{k}}(z_{k})
,

, such that

(i)

, with

;

(ii)

every admissible predecessor packet of strictly larger scale or earlier terminal time has score below the singularity-forcing level, up to
o(1)

(iii)

if a fixed positive fraction of the score leaves every bounded normalized subcylinder, then the tail/fragmentation output is positive.

By the local regularity criterion, a singular point has arbitrarily small packets with score at least
\varepsilon_{\rm reg}=e_{\rm sing}
. Among all such packets choose one with minimal dyadic scale, then earliest terminal time, then a center in a fixed normalized lattice. This gives (i)–(ii) after allowing the controlled
o(1)

If no compact subcylinder captures a fixed fraction of the score, then the score either escapes to spatial infinity in normalized coordinates or breaks into separated components. Lower active descendants selected during the proof are retained as funding channels but are not terminal singular packets unless they re-cross

A.2
Why the score is the right normalization

The instantaneous score
R\int|\omega|^{2}
is critical because

\omega^{(R)}(y,s)=R^{2}\omega(x_{0}+Ry,t_{0}+R^{2}s),\qquad dx=R^{3}dy,

so

\int_{B_{1}}|\omega^{(R)}(y,s)|^{2}dy=R\int_{B_{R}}|\omega(x,t)|^{2}dx.

The spacetime enstrophy component is critical with the opposite factor:

\iint_{Q_{1}}|\omega^{(R)}|^{2}dy\,ds=R^{-1}\iint_{Q_{R}}|\omega|^{2}dx\,dt.

This explains the two terms in (
3

Remark A.2

(No hidden use of global enstrophy)
.

The global energy inequality controls

and

, not a uniform global-in-time bound for

up to a hypothetical singular time. The proof therefore uses the first-threshold normalization rather than pretending that vorticity dissipation has a global finite budget near blow-up.

\mathcal{Q}^{3D}(Q^{\prime})\geq 4e_{\rm low},

so the descendant remains inside the active band. The theorem is therefore used to pass from a singularity-forcing packet to an active descendant, not necessarily to another singularity-forcing packet in one step.

Proof.

Apply Lemmas

,

Corollary C.6

(No infinite silent descent)
.

A singularity-forcing packet cannot generate an infinite chain of nonperturbative active descendants without either producing a smaller singularity-forcing crossing, lowering the selection order until the finite list terminates, or activating a named output.

Proof.

The descendant theorem gives active packets, not necessarily singularity-forcing packets. If an active descendant reaches
e_{\rm sing}
at a later normalized time, continuity of
t\mapsto\mathcal{Q}^{3D}
on the smooth approximating sequence selects the first such time and hence a new singularity-forcing packet with smaller selection order. If it never reaches
e_{\rm sing}
, it cannot be the terminal singularity-forcing singular packet and its contribution is strictly subcritical. The selection order is lexicographic: endpoint time, active scale, number of separated components, and residual-channel localization. Endpoint time and scale are discretized by the singularity-forcing construction, the number of separated components is a nonnegative integer, and the residual channel belongs to a finite list. Hence there is no infinite strictly descending chain inside the admissible threshold band.

Appendix D
Paraproduct rebase without overcounting

The proof of Proposition
6.1
has one delicate point: positive parts do not commute with summation. This appendix explains the convention that avoids overcounting.

D.1
Signed decomposition

The localized stretching density is a signed scalar:

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\phi_{R}^{2}\omega\cdot S\omega.

Littlewood–Paley decomposition gives a signed expansion

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\sum_{\Theta}\mathcal{S}_{R,\Theta}+\mathcal{R}_{R},

where
\Theta
ranges over dyadic frequency triples and packet positions. We do not define the active production by

\sum_{\Theta}(\mathcal{S}_{R,\Theta})_{+}.

That expression can be much larger than
(\sum_{\Theta}\mathcal{S}_{R,\Theta})_{+}
because positive and negative triads may cancel.

Instead we first remove the non-active pieces at the signed level:

\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}+\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{O}}+\mathcal{R}_{R}.

Then we define the active carrier from
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}
. Since

(a+b)_{+}\leq a_{+}+|b|,

we get

(\mathcal{S}_{R})_{+}\leq(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}+|\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{O}}|+|\mathcal{R}_{R}|.

The last two terms are outputs or summable errors. This is the precise meaning of the rebase theorem.

Lemma D.1

(Positive-part rebase estimate)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}+\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{O}}+\mathcal{R}_{R}
be the signed decomposition above. Then

R\iint(\mathcal{S}_{R})_{+}\leq R\iint(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}+R\iint|\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{O}}|+R\iint|\mathcal{R}_{R}|.

Assume in addition the absolute error control

R\iint|\mathcal{K}_{\mathfrak{O}}|+R\iint|\mathcal{R}_{R}|=o(1),

which is proved for the nonactive classes in Appendix
E
. Then, along a zero-output sequence,

R\iint(\mathcal{S}_{R})_{+}\leq R\iint(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}})_{+}+o(1).

Proof.

This is the scalar inequality
(a+b+c)_{+}\leq a_{+}+|b|+|c|
, integrated and multiplied by the critical factor
R
. The zero-output hypothesis sends the output and remainder terms to zero.
∎

Appendix E
Expanded paraproduct constants and active rebase estimates

This appendix records the estimates behind Proposition

E.1
Calderon–Zygmund and Bernstein normalization

For each dyadic

\|S[\omega_{N}]\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|\omega_{N}\|_{L^{p}},\qquad 1<p<\infty,

and, on a ball of radius

\|P_{\leq N}\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}\lesssim N^{3/2}\|P_{\leq N}\omega\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}+\text{tail/collar terms}.

for a fixed

Comparable off-core terms

After the active signed density

Appendix F
Biot–Savart coefficients for the active density

The Biot–Savart symbol is used in this paper to identify the active resonant stretching coefficients. The endpoint closure does not require a classification of all equality cases of a pointwise active-symbol majorant. The proof instead separates active transfer into leakage, which is recorded by an output or descendant, and flux closure, which is ruled out by the kinetic-energy identity.

First, if an active transfer is one-way, leaks out of the active hull, or exchanges energy with nonactive modes, the boundary-flux theorem routes it to a named output or to an active descendant. Second, if all such boundary fluxes vanish, the active hull is energetically closed. Then the Navier–Stokes nonlinearity is skew-symmetric in kinetic energy and viscosity gives the closed-active-block Liouville theorem. Thus the coefficient calculation below defines the active density used in the positive-production branch.

For a vorticity mode
\widehat{\omega}(k)=a
,
a\perp k
, the velocity mode is

\widehat{u}(k)=i\frac{k\times a}{|k|^{2}}.

The associated strain symbol is

\widehat{S[a]}(k)=\frac{i}{2}\left(k\otimes\frac{k\times a}{|k|^{2}}+\frac{k\times a}{|k|^{2}}\otimes k\right).

The active trilinear coefficient for three resonant modes
k_{i}+k_{j}+k_{k}=0
is

B_{ijk}=\frac{1}{2|k_{j}|^{2}}\left[(a_{i}\cdot k_{j})((k_{j}\times a_{j})\cdot a_{k})+(a_{i}\cdot(k_{j}\times a_{j}))(k_{j}\cdot a_{k})\right].

The positive active density
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{act}}
is assembled first as a signed resonant sum using these coefficients, and only then is the positive part taken. This avoids overcounting competing triads.

Appendix G
Flat endpoint details

Suppose the active measure is supported on
\mathcal{Z}_{2D}
. Then the limiting vorticity direction is a fixed vector. After rotation,

\Omega=(0,0,\alpha).

Since
\diver\Omega=0
,

\partial_{3}\alpha=0.

Let
U
be the Biot–Savart velocity reconstructed from
\Omega
. The non-harmonic part of
U
is independent of
x_{3}
. Any remaining harmonic gradient has zero curl. If it is constant or affine in space, it is a moving-frame or pressure gauge; if it is not removable, it is an output (O6). Under the zero-output hypothesis, it is removed. Therefore

U=U(x_{1},x_{2},t),\qquad U^{3}=0

up to the controlled gauge. The resulting equation is the two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equation for the scalar vorticity
\alpha
. The classical two-dimensional enstrophy identity gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int|\alpha|^{2}+\nu\int|\nabla_{x_{1},x_{2}}\alpha|^{2}=0.

Thus a nonzero terminal blow-up endpoint is impossible in the flat class.

Appendix H
Additional terminal endpoint classification theorem

This appendix records a self-contained form of the endpoint classification used in Sections
9
–
11
. The statement is useful because it separates the compactness assumptions of a terminal endpoint from the ledger mechanisms that eliminate the genuinely non-axisymmetric alternatives.

Theorem H.1

(Standalone terminal endpoint classification)
.

Let
(u,p)
be a nonzero ancient suitable Navier–Stokes endpoint on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(-\infty,0]
obtained from a normalized first-threshold sequence. Assume:

(a)

the endpoint has nontrivial active vorticity mass on
Q_{1}
;

(b)

the sequence is terminal in the threshold-band order, so no strict active descendant remains;

(c)

all tail, collar, shell, macro, pressure-current, motion, temporal, passive, and fragmentation outputs vanish;

(d)

the active rebase, flux-hull stopping, Carleson donor, no-silent-descent, and closed-hull energy alternatives hold as stated in Sections
6
–
9
.

Then
(u,p)
is either flat two-dimensional/two-and-a-half-dimensional after a rigid motion and Galilean transformation, or axisymmetric with swirl around one fixed spatial axis. In particular, no genuinely non-axisymmetric, non-flat, zero-output terminal ancient endpoint exists.

Proof.

The proof is the ledger exhaustion written without reference to the order of presentation in the main text. Let
A=|\omega|
. The regularized amplitude identity gives

(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)A=A(\xi\cdot S\xi-\nu|\nabla\xi|^{2})

in the limiting sense. If the positive part of the right-hand side vanishes on the terminal packet, then
A
is subcaloric. The amplitude-tail alternative supplies finite
L^{1}
mass or else produces a tail output. The tail output is excluded, so Nash–Liouville rules out a nonzero first-threshold endpoint in the zero-production branch, except for the classical flat two-dimensional regular class.

Therefore a non-flat terminal endpoint must have positive production. The active rebase bounds this production by the assembled positive active density plus absolute non-active errors. The non-active errors are precisely low-frequency, far-field, shell/collar, macro, pressure/motion, passive, temporal, fragmentation, or residual outputs. Since these outputs vanish, the positive active density has positive mass. By finite overlap, either a comparable active atom carries threshold-band mass, or the mass is spread among separated atoms and hence gives fragmentation output. Fragmentation is excluded, so an active seed exists.

Starting from this seed, the flux-hull stopping theorem gives three possibilities. If the incoming comparable flux can be closed after finitely many admissions, one obtains a flux-closed active hull
\mathcal{H}
with

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}/\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0.

The closed-hull energy identity then gives

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}+\nu\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}=o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}),

and the comparable-frequency Bernstein bound gives

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

A nonzero bounded ancient closed hull is impossible. If the hull does not close, the stopping theorem gives a named output, an active descendant, or a Carleson donor cascade. Outputs and descendants are excluded by terminality. The Carleson donor alternative is also excluded, because it is fragmentation, shell/tail escape, temporal/motion escape, scale output, or an active descendant. Hence no positive-production genuinely non-axisymmetric funding mechanism survives.

It remains to classify the degenerate coherent endpoints. The coherence-or-fragmentation theorem says that the terminal active direction measure is supported on a single coherence class; otherwise fragmentation output or a strict descendant occurs. If the coherence class is fixed-direction, rotate so that the direction is
e_{3}
. Then
\omega=(0,0,\alpha)
on the active region. Since
\diver\omega=0
, one has
\partial_{3}\alpha=0
. Consequently
\partial_{3}\omega=0
. Moreover

\curl(\partial_{3}u)=\partial_{3}\omega=0,\qquad\diver(\partial_{3}u)=\partial_{3}\diver u=0.

Thus
\partial_{3}u
is both curl-free and divergence-free; it is a harmonic gradient. The no-macro and no-moving-frame output hypotheses remove this harmonic gradient, and so
\partial_{3}u=0
. The endpoint is flat two-dimensional/two-and-a-half-dimensional.

If the coherence class is not fixed-direction, then terminal coherence leaves only the one-axis class. Corollary
9.15
shows that either its failure to be fully equivariant is a named output/descendant, or the endpoint vorticity itself is equivariant under the
SO(2)
-action around one fixed axis. The first alternative is excluded by terminality. In the second alternative, Lemma
9.18
upgrades vorticity-field equivariance to velocity-field equivariance modulo harmonic, pressure-current, moving-frame, and shell/tail defects, all of which are excluded. Thus, in cylindrical coordinates around that axis,

u=u^{r}(r,z,t)e_{r}+u^{\theta}(r,z,t)e_{\theta}+u^{z}(r,z,t)e_{z},

with all components independent of the polar angle. This is the axisymmetric-with-swirl class. If the terminal class has only directional rotation but fails equivariance of amplitude, support, phase, pressure-current, or frame data, that failure is a named output, already excluded. These alternatives exhaust the terminal zero-output/no-descendant endpoint.
∎

Appendix I
The axisymmetric reduced problem

The present paper proves the full three-dimensional singular-endpoint reduction to the axisymmetric-with-swirl class. The reduced endpoint statement is precisely
(\mathrm{AS})
: every smooth finite-energy axisymmetric solution with arbitrary swirl is globally regular, or, in endpoint language, no axisymmetric-with-swirl ancient first-threshold endpoint carries a nonzero terminal vorticity score.

No estimate in the full three-dimensional front end uses special axisymmetric coordinates. Axisymmetry enters only after the amplitude-production, output, flux-hull, and closed-energy mechanisms have eliminated every genuinely three-dimensional endpoint. Thus a separate proof of
(\mathrm{AS})
closes the reduced endpoint and, by Corollary
2.3
, proves the full smooth finite-energy three-dimensional statement. Conversely, any axisymmetric counterexample is automatically a full three-dimensional counterexample because the axisymmetric class is a subclass of the full class.

Proof synopsis

For quick reference, the proof steps are:

Step 1.

Step 2.

A=|\omega|
.

Step 3.

.

Step 4.

Step 5.

Appendix K
Flux-closed active hull construction in expanded form

This appendix gives the detailed estimates behind the flux-hull package. The guiding rule is that no new endpoint category is introduced. Every nonclosed term is either absorbed into the comparable active hull or is one of the corresponding typed outputs: low-frequency/passive, shell/collar, tail, harmonic/macro, pressure/motion, temporal-transfer, shape/scale conveyor defect, fragmentation, or active descendant.

K.1
Self-adjoint hull operators

For every admissible packet label
\alpha
, define

\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f=\Pi P_{N_{\alpha}}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}P_{N_{\alpha}}\Pi f,\qquad U_{\alpha}=\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}u.

This particular symmetrization is important. The unsymmetrized expression
\Pi P_{N}\chi P_{N}\chi\Pi
is not literally self-adjoint because
P_{N}
and
\chi
do not commute. The expression above has the form
A^{*}MA
, with
A=P_{N_{\alpha}}\Pi
and
M=\chi_{\alpha}^{2}
, followed by the commuting Leray projection, so it is self-adjoint and positive on
L^{2}
. Its range is solenoidal and annular. For a hull
\mathcal{H}
,

\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}}\mathsf{P}_{\alpha},\qquad U_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}u.

Bounded overlap and the kernel estimate

|K_{N}(x)|\leq C_{M}N^{3}(1+N|x|)^{-M}

show that
\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
is uniformly bounded on
L^{2}
, and that cross terms between separated labels are rapidly decaying. Consequently, the hull energy may be tested without hidden nonsymmetric terms. The only residual operator errors are cutoff or Leray commutators; they are estimated below.

K.2
The exact boundary-flux expansion

Write
U=U_{\mathcal{H}}
,
W=u-U
. Since

(u\cdot\nabla)u-(U\cdot\nabla)U=(U\cdot\nabla)W+(W\cdot\nabla)U+(W\cdot\nabla)W,

the boundary flux is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}|\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(U\cdot\nabla)W],U\rangle|dt+\int_{I}|\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(W\cdot\nabla)U],U\rangle|dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{I}|\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}[(W\cdot\nabla)W],U\rangle|dt+\mathfrak{B}_{\rm com}(I).

Decompose the complement into

W=W_{\mathrm{low}}+W_{\mathrm{high}}+W_{\mathrm{comp},off}+W_{\mathrm{comp},on}^{\perp}+W_{\mathrm{far}}+W_{\mathrm{harm}}+W_{\mathrm{mov}}.

Each summand is inserted in the three displayed nonlinear expressions. All estimates are absolute estimates; no cancellation between different complement classes is used.

K.3
Low-frequency and passive strain terms

Let
a_{\leq N/C}
be a low-frequency velocity or strain coefficient and let
b_{N},u_{N}
be annular at frequency
N\simeq R^{-1}
. The kernel identity

[P_{N},a\cdot\nabla]b(x)=\int K_{N}(x-y)(a(x)-a(y))\cdot\nabla b(y)dy

and the mean-value theorem give

\|[P_{N},a_{\leq N/C}\cdot\nabla]b_{N}\|_{2}\lesssim\|ablaa_{\leq N/C}\|_{\infty}\|b_{N}\|_{2}.

After integration in time,

\int_{I}|\langle[P_{N},a_{\leq N/C}\cdot\nabla]b_{N},u_{N}\rangle|dt\lesssim\|ablaa_{\leq N/C}\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|b_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

If the low-frequency gradient factor is below the terminal tolerance, the term is
o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
. If not, it is exactly the low-frequency/passive/macro output already in the ledger. Thus low-frequency feeding is never absorbed into the hull.

K.4
High-frequency and off-diagonal terms

For an off-diagonal interaction with one factor at
N^{\prime}\gg N
, Bernstein and paraproduct estimates give, for some
\sigma>0
,

\int_{I}|\langle P_{N}[(u_{N^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla)v_{M}],u_{N}\rangle|dt\lesssim 2^{-\sigma|\log_{2}(N^{\prime}/N)|}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm high/desc}(I).

If the high-frequency factor does not carry threshold-band score, the dyadic gain is summable and perturbative. If it does carry such score, the threshold-band selection gives a high-frequency active descendant. A comparable-frequency factor outside the core is covered by the finite shell/collar cover; a large absolute flux in one such element gives a finite-overlap descendant, while a spread over many such elements is the shell/fragmentation output.

K.5
Leray, cutoff, and moving-window commutators

The Leray commutator with a spatial cutoff is estimated from the Calderon–Zygmund kernel. For every vector field
F
,

\|[\Pi,\chi]F\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\operatorname{collar})}+\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{-3m/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}(A_{m})}.

The collar term is the cutoff/collar output. The exterior annular sum is tail/far-field output, or, after harmonic expansion on the core, pressure-current/macro/motion output. If the packet center, hull labels, or cutoffs depend on time, the differentiated energy identity contains
\partial_{t}\chi
and
\partial_{t}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}
. The terms are bounded by

\int_{I}|\langle(\partial_{t}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}})u,U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle|dt\lesssim\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}(I)+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}(I),

by the definition of the temporal-transfer and moving-frame outputs. Hence no time-dependent reselection term is hidden in the energy identity.

K.6
Comparable on-core donors and the stopping rule

Only comparable-frequency, on-core, time-stable donors are eligible to enter the hull. Suppose the total eligible absolute flux into the current hull is at least
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Cover the core by bounded-overlap packet elements and group donors by frequency window and time label. If a bounded subcollection carries a fixed fraction of the flux, add those donors to the hull. If no bounded subcollection carries a fixed fraction, then the flux is distributed over arbitrarily many essentially disjoint packet elements. By the finite-overlap descendant lemma, either one of these elements carries threshold-band score and becomes an active descendant, or the averaged spread is exactly the fragmentation/shell output. Thus a terminal zero-output sequence cannot have a non-negligible cloud of many small donors.

Repeating this stopping step cannot produce an infinite silent comparable hull. If the added donors remain in a fixed comparable spatial-frequency-time window, finite overlap merges them into a finite enlarged hull. If the scale drifts, we obtain a high-frequency descendant or low/macro output. If the center drifts, we obtain shell, tail, or motion output. If the time label drifts, we obtain temporal-transfer output. Consequently the stopping procedure terminates with a flux-closed hull whenever all named outputs and active descendants vanish.

K.7
Frequency closure and nonzero hull energy

Since the leftmost multiplier in each
\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}
is annular with
N_{\alpha}\simeq R^{-1}
, every element in the range of a zero-output hull satisfies Bernstein:

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(c+o(1))R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

The
o(1)
term accounts only for collar leakage, low-frequency leakage, and projection commutators, all already routed above. Moreover, on annular divergence-free fields,

\|\curl U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}\simeq R^{-1}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}.

Thus a fixed positive amount of active vorticity production cannot be carried by a hull with vanishing kinetic energy. Positive active production therefore gives a nonzero limiting kinetic-energy carrier, which is the quantity killed by the closed-hull energy Liouville theorem.

K.8
Conclusion of the flux-hull package

Combining the preceding estimates gives the precise final implication used in the main proof:

\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}{\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})}\not\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{one of the existing outputs is positive, or an active descendant is selected.}

Therefore, under the terminal zero-output/no-descendant hypotheses, the hull is flux-closed. The internal nonlinearity is then skew-symmetric in kinetic energy, viscosity supplies strict comparable-frequency dissipation, and a nonzero bounded ancient closed active hull cannot exist.

Appendix L
Detailed estimates for the flux-hull package

This appendix expands the four estimates that are most likely to be checked line by line: almost-orthogonality of hull atoms, the many-small-donors stopping alternative, commutator routing, and the preservation of the comparable-frequency gap. The appendix does not introduce a new endpoint or a new output. It only verifies that every nonclosed flux term is already in the output ledger used in the main proof.

L.1
A symmetric hull operator and exact almost-orthogonality

Recall that for an admissible label
\alpha
we use

\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f=\Pi P_{N_{\alpha}}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}P_{N_{\alpha}}\Pi f,\qquad N_{\alpha}\simeq R^{-1}.

This operator is self-adjoint on
L^{2}
, maps into solenoidal fields, and has output frequency contained in a fixed annulus
\{cR^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq CR^{-1}\}
. We record the quantitative almost-orthogonality statement used in Lemma
7.1
.

Lemma L.1

(Line-by-line almost-orthogonality)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hull whose labels have bounded spatial overlap and frequencies in a fixed dyadic annulus. There is a constant
C_{\rm ov}
, depending only on the overlap convention and the cutoffs, such that for every
f\in L^{2}
,

\Big\|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}}\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{\rm ov}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{H}}\|\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{\rm ov}\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

(30)

Moreover, if
g=\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}f
and the collar/low-frequency leakage outputs vanish along the terminal sequence, then

\|\nabla g\|_{2}^{2}\geq cR^{-2}\|g\|_{2}^{2}-o(R^{-2}\|g\|_{2}^{2}).

(31)

Proof.

Write

\Big\|\sum_{\alpha}\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\Big\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha}\|\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{\alpha<\beta}\langle\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f,\mathsf{P}_{\beta}f\rangle.

Let
d_{\alpha\beta}=\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\alpha},\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\beta})
. The kernel of
P_{N}
satisfies, for every
M
,

|K_{N}(x)|\leq C_{M}N^{3}(1+N|x|)^{-M}.

Since
N\simeq R^{-1}
, Schur’s test gives

|\langle\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f,\mathsf{P}_{\beta}f\rangle|\leq C_{M}(1+d_{\alpha\beta}/R)^{-M}\|\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}\|\mathsf{P}_{\beta}f\|_{2}.

(32)

For neighboring labels with
d_{\alpha\beta}\lesssim R
, bounded overlap gives at most
C_{\rm ov}
neighbors for each
\alpha
. For separated labels, choose
M
large enough so that

\sup_{\alpha}\sum_{\beta}(1+d_{\alpha\beta}/R)^{-M}<\infty.

Using
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, the cross terms are bounded by a constant multiple of
\sum_{\alpha}\|\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f\|_{2}^{2}
. Finally, each
\mathsf{P}_{\alpha}f
has annular output frequency. If a physical cutoff produces leakage outside the annulus, that leakage is supported in the collar or in low frequencies and is one of the named outputs. Removing this output in a terminal zero-output sequence leaves the annular Bernstein inequality, which proves (
31
).
∎

L.2
The many-small-donors alternative as a finite measure lemma

The stopping construction admits a donor only when its absolute flux into the current hull is non-negligible. The only possible loophole is a cloud of individually tiny donors with non-negligible total flux. The following lemma is the precise pigeonhole form used in Proposition
7.8
.

Lemma L.2

(Finite subcollection or fragmentation, quantitative form)
.

Let
\{\beta\}
be a bounded-overlap family of comparable, on-core, time-stable donor labels and let
F_{\beta}\geq 0
be the absolute flux from
\beta
into a current hull
\mathcal{H}
. Assume

\sum_{\beta}F_{\beta}\geq\eta\,\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R}).

(33)

Fix a small number
0<\theta<1
. Then either

(a)

there is a subcollection
B
of cardinality at most
\lceil\theta^{-1}\rceil
such that

\sum_{\beta\in B}F_{\beta}\geq\theta\eta\,\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R}),

(34)

so the labels in
B
are added to the hull; or

(b)

the flux is spread over more than
\theta^{-1}
essentially disjoint packet elements, in which case the fragmentation/shell output is positive, or one of the elements carries threshold-band score and is an active descendant.

Proof.

Order the donors so that
F_{\beta_{1}}\geq F_{\beta_{2}}\geq\cdots
. If the first
m=\lceil\theta^{-1}\rceil
donors carry at least
\theta\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
, then (
34
) holds. Otherwise at least
(1-\theta)\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
of the incoming flux is carried by donors outside every bounded subcollection of size
m
. By bounded overlap, one can extract from this tail a subfamily of essentially disjoint packets that still carries a fixed fraction of this flux. If one of these disjoint packets has score at least
e_{\rm low}
, the threshold-band selection makes it an active descendant. If no such packet has threshold-band score, then the total flux is spread over many disjoint subthreshold packets; this is exactly the fragmentation/shell output in the output ledger, because a positive amount of the normalized funding is distributed outside every bounded active subcollection. These alternatives exhaust (
33
).
∎

Remark L.3

(Why this is not a new endpoint)
.

The second alternative in Lemma
L.2
is the fragmentation or shell output already included in the output ledger. The lemma makes explicit the pigeonhole mechanism that prevents a diffuse family of small donors from feeding the hull without detection.

L.3
Commutator routing with absolute estimates

We spell out the commutator estimates used in Lemma
7.3
. All bounds are absolute bounds and are compared with
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
. No signed cancellation is used.

Low-frequency transport.

Let
a_{\leq N/C}
be a velocity coefficient with frequency
\leq N/C
, and let
b_{N},u_{N}
be annular at
N\simeq R^{-1}
. The identity

[P_{N},a\cdot\nabla]b(x)=\int K_{N}(x-y)(a(x)-a(y))\cdot\nabla b(y)\,dy

and the mean-value theorem give

\|[P_{N},a_{\leq N/C}\cdot\nabla]b_{N}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\nabla a_{\leq N/C}\|_{\infty}\|b_{N}\|_{2}.

Therefore

\int_{I}|\langle[P_{N},a_{\leq N/C}\cdot\nabla]b_{N},u_{N}\rangle|\,dt\lesssim\|\nabla a_{\leq N/C}\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\|b_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}.

(35)

If
\|\nabla a_{\leq N/C}\|_{L^{1}L^{\infty}}
is below the terminal tolerance, the term is
o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
. If it is not, this is the low-frequency/passive/macro output. Thus low-frequency feeding is never hidden inside the hull.

Off-diagonal high-frequency factors.

For
N^{\prime}\gg N
, the paraproduct estimate gives, for some
\sigma>0
,

\int_{I}|\langle P_{N}[(u_{N^{\prime}}\cdot\nabla)v_{M}],u_{N}\rangle|\,dt\lesssim 2^{-\sigma|\log_{2}(N^{\prime}/N)|}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I)+\text{high-frequency descendant output}.

(36)

The first term is summable over off-diagonal dyadic ratios. If the second term is present, the threshold-band selection gives a smaller-scale descendant. Thus high-frequency feeding is either perturbative or a descendant, not a new terminal case.

Cutoff and Leray projection.

The Leray commutator satisfies

\|[\Pi,\chi]F\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\lesssim R^{-1}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\operatorname{collar})}+\sum_{m\geq 0}2^{-3m/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}(A_{m})}.

(37)

The first term is collar/cutoff output. The annular tail is tail/far-field output, or after harmonic expansion on the core, pressure-current/macro/motion output. Similarly,

\|[P_{N},\chi]F\|_{2}\lesssim N^{-1}\|\nabla\chi\|_{\infty}\|F\|_{2}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}(\operatorname{collar})},

(38)

which is again collar output. If
\chi=\chi(t,x)
, then

\int_{I}|\langle(\partial_{t}\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}})u,U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle|\,dt\lesssim\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}(I)+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}(I).

(39)

This is precisely the temporal-transfer/moving-window output. No term involving the time dependence of the hull operator is left in the closed energy identity.

L.4
Pressure-current and absolute flux routing

The pressure term is the most common place where a local active-hull argument can accidentally hide a force. We therefore state the estimate used in the main proof in the same absolute form as the other commutators. Let

u=U_{\mathcal{H}}+W,

where
U_{\mathcal{H}}
is the solenoidal active hull and
W
is the complement. Decompose the local pressure on
B_{4R}
as

p=p_{HH}+p_{HW}+p_{WW}+p_{\rm ext}+h,

where

-\Delta p_{HH}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(U_{\mathcal{H}}^{i}U_{\mathcal{H}}^{j}),\quad-\Delta p_{HW}=2\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(U_{\mathcal{H}}^{i}W^{j}),\quad-\Delta p_{WW}=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(W^{i}W^{j}),

with cutoffs on
B_{4R}
, while
p_{\rm ext}+h
is harmonic on the core up to tail errors. Then for every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{R}}\left|\left\langle\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla p-\mathsf{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\nabla p_{HH},U_{\mathcal{H}}\right\rangle\right|dt

(40)

\displaystyle\leq\eta\,\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})+C_{\eta}\bigl(\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm macro}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm passive}\bigr)+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R}).

The internal pressure
p_{HH}
does not create a new force in the closed hull energy identity: because
U_{\mathcal{H}}
is solenoidal, its contribution either vanishes after integration by parts in the ideal hull or is one of the cutoff/Leray commutators already estimated in (
37
)–(
38
).

Proof.

The local pressure pieces are Calderon–Zygmund transforms of the indicated quadratic products. On the core,

\|p_{HW}\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2R})}\lesssim\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{3}(B_{4R})}\|W\|_{L^{3}(B_{4R})},\quad\|p_{WW}\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2R})}\lesssim\|W\|_{L^{3}(B_{4R})}^{2},

with analogous bounds for the localized exterior pressure after subtracting a time-dependent constant. Testing against the hull energy current gives

R^{-1}\int_{I_{R}}\|p_{HW}+p_{WW}+p_{\rm ext}-c(t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2R})}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{3}(B_{2R})}\,dt.

By the scale-invariant Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on
B_{2R}
,

\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\lesssim\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{2}}+R^{-1}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

so Young’s inequality gives an
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
contribution plus the corresponding complement norms. If those complement norms are small, the term is absorbed. If not, they are exactly pressure-current, passive/complement, tail, macro, or motion output by Definition
3.7
. The harmonic part is expanded on the core; its constant and affine pieces are pressure-current/macro/motion outputs if not removable, and higher harmonic remainders are controlled by tail/collar norms. This proves (
40
).
∎

L.5
Master absolute flux-routing inequality

Combining the low-frequency, high-frequency, off-core, Leray, cutoff, moving-window, and pressure-current estimates gives the single form used throughout the main proof. If
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the full nonclosed contribution to the projected hull energy identity outside the internal term
(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}}
, then for every
\eta>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{I_{R}}|\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\eta\,\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm rout}(I_{R})+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}(I_{R})+\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}(I_{R}),

(41)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{O}_{\rm rout}:={}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{O}_{\rm low}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm macro}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shell}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}

\displaystyle+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm passive}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm fragmentation}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}.

Here
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}
is the remaining comparable-frequency, on-core, time-stable incoming flux. This is the only term eligible to be added to the hull. Lemma
L.2
and the stopping rule below show that it either enters the hull through a finite donor subcollection, produces fragmentation/shell output, or selects an active descendant. Thus (
41
) is not an additional assumption: it is a compact restatement of the displayed estimates in this appendix.

Proof.

The low-frequency and passive terms are bounded by (
35
); the high-frequency and off-diagonal terms by the dyadic gain estimates in Subsections
K
and
L
; the Leray, cutoff, and moving-window errors by (
37
)–(
38
); and the pressure contribution by (
40
). In each case Young’s inequality gives an
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
term plus the corresponding named output. The only class not routed by these estimates is the comparable, on-core, time-stable complement; by definition this is
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}
.
∎

L.6
A fully explicit stopping proof

Combining Lemma
L.2
with the output ledger gives the stopping argument. Begin with one active receiver atom selected by positive production. At stage
j
, let
\mathcal{H}_{j}
be the current hull. If

\mathfrak{B}_{\mathcal{H}_{j}}(I_{R})\leq\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}_{j}}(I_{R}),

then the hull is
\eta
-closed. If not, decompose the complement into the eight classes used in Lemma
7.2
. Low, high, off-core, far, harmonic, moving, and commutator pieces are routed by the estimates above. The only remaining nonperturbative part is comparable, on-core, time-stable incoming flux. Lemma
L.2
either supplies finitely many donors to add to the hull, or gives fragmentation/shell output or an active descendant.

The iteration cannot continue silently. If all admitted donors remain in a fixed normalized spatial-frequency-time region, bounded overlap implies that after finitely many admissions the union is a finite enlarged hull; repeated admission of the same bounded set changes the hull only by bounded multiplicity. If the admitted labels drift in scale, we obtain high-frequency descendant or low/macro output. If they drift in center, we obtain shell, tail, or motion output. If they drift in time labels, we obtain temporal-transfer output. Thus, under the terminal zero-output/no-descendant assumptions, the algorithm terminates with an
\eta
-closed hull. Letting the terminal sequence run first and then
\eta\downarrow 0
gives the flux-closed hull used in the main theorem.

L.7
Frequency closure and nonzero energy carrier

For every zero-output hull, Lemma
L.1
gives

\|\nabla U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(c+o(1))R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Also, on annular divergence-free fields,

\|\curl U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}\simeq R^{-2}\|U_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence a fixed positive amount of active vorticity score or positive production cannot be carried by a vanishing kinetic-energy hull. In particular, the closed-hull energy identity rules out the actual carrier of positive production, not merely an auxiliary projection.

L.8
Four remaining line-checks: local threshold, routing, recurrence, and period closure

This subsection records the last referee-sensitive implications as explicit line-check lemmas. The point is not to create new output classes; it is to make clear exactly where the scale-invariant hypotheses enter.

Lemma L.4

(Scale and exponent check for the local threshold)
.

The score
\mathcal{Q}
in (
3
) is scale-invariant. Moreover, the singularity-forcing level
e_{\rm sing}=\varepsilon_{\rm reg}
is chosen so that a terminal packet with
\mathcal{Q}<e_{\rm sing}
cannot contain a singular point.

Proof.

Under the Navier–Stokes scaling

u^{(R)}(y,s)=Ru(x_{0}+Ry,t_{0}+R^{2}s),\qquad\omega^{(R)}(y,s)=R^{2}\omega(x_{0}+Ry,t_{0}+R^{2}s),

one has

\int_{B_{1}}|\omega^{(R)}(y,s)|^{2}dy=R\int_{B_{R}}|\omega(x,t)|^{2}dx,

and

\iint_{Q_{1}}|\omega^{(R)}|^{2}dyds=R^{-1}\iint_{Q_{R}}|\omega|^{2}dxdt.

Thus both parts of
\mathcal{Q}
are critical. Theorem
3.3
gives the regularity threshold for the spacetime part; the additional instantaneous part only strengthens the smallness assumption and is used for terminal compactness and lower semicontinuity. Hence a singularity-forcing terminal packet must satisfy
\mathcal{Q}\geq e_{\rm sing}
.
∎

Lemma L.5

(Term-by-term absolute routing table)
.

Every noninternal term in the projected active-hull energy identity is either absorbed into
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
, recorded in a named output, or retained as comparable on-core flux
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}
. More precisely, for each
\eta>0
the following schematic estimates hold with constants independent of the terminal scale:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{low/passive strain}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm low/passive}+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{high/off-diagonal paraproduct}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm high/off}+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{Leray/cutoff/collar commutator}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{moving-window/time-face term}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}(\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal})+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{tail/harmonic/macro term}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}(\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm macro})+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{pressure-current term}&\leq&\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}},\\[5.69054pt]
\text{shape/scale conveyor term}&\leq&C(\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}+\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}),\end{array}

while the only unabsorbed, non-output contribution is the comparable-frequency, on-core, time-stable flux
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}
. Consequently (
41
) follows.

Proof.

All estimates are critical under the same scaling as
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
. The low/passive and high/off-diagonal lines are the Bony and Bernstein estimates already displayed in the preceding subsections. The Leray and cutoff lines use the order-zero boundedness of
\Pi
and the commutator estimate
\|[P_{N},\chi_{R}]F\|_{2}\lesssim(NR)^{-1}\|F\|_{2}
on the core plus collar support for the remainder. The moving-window line follows from
\partial_{t}\chi_{R}+\dot{x}(t)\cdot\nabla\chi_{R}
, whose coefficient is precisely the normalized motion/time-face output. The tail and harmonic terms are separated by the local/nonlocal pressure and Biot–Savart decomposition; nonzero affine or harmonic parts are macro/tail outputs. For pressure, decompose

p=p_{\rm loc}+p_{\rm cross}+p_{\rm far}+p_{\rm harm}

using
-\Delta p=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u_{i}u_{j})
. Calderon–Zygmund bounds control
p_{\rm loc}
by the local velocity factors; cross, far, and harmonic parts are exactly pressure, passive, tail, or macro outputs. The sign of pressure is never used. Young’s inequality gives the
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}
contribution. The shape and scale terms are not analytic commutators: they measure failure of Cauchy recurrence or hidden inner-boundary transfer and are therefore already positive outputs. After all these classes are removed, what remains is by definition the comparable on-core flux eligible for hull enlargement.
∎

Lemma L.6

(Zero conveyor outputs imply strong recurrent compactness: line-check form)
.

Assume an active conveyor satisfies

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shape}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm scale}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm motion}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm temporal}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm tail}=\mathfrak{F}_{\rm frag}=0.

Then, after passing to a subsequence and applying the terminal frame corrections, the normalized profiles are Cauchy in the active topology on every compact cylinder. In particular they converge strongly locally in

u^{(j)}\to U\quad\text{in }L^{3}_{\rm loc},\qquad\omega^{(j)}\to\Omega\quad\text{in }L^{2}_{\rm loc},

and the pressure normalizations converge in
L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}
. The limit keeps the singularity-forcing lower bound unless a tail, time-face, or fragmentation output is positive.

Proof.

The strengthened shape output is the tail oscillation

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{j,\ell\geq N}d_{\rm sh}(u^{(j)},u^{(\ell)}).

The metric
d_{\rm sh}
is the active topology used for terminal compactness: it contains the local
L^{3}
velocity distance, the local
L^{2}
vorticity distance, and the normalized local
L^{3/2}
pressure distance. Therefore its vanishing is exactly the Cauchy property needed for strong local convergence, not an additional compactness assumption. Zero scale output prevents degeneration of the scale ratios and excludes nonperturbative energy/enstrophy crossing the parent-child annulus. Zero motion and temporal outputs fix the centers and time origins. Zero fragmentation prevents the active measure from splitting into two positive-mass compactness classes. Zero tail output prevents loss of the critical vorticity score to spatial infinity. Completeness of the local topology gives the stated strong local convergence. Lower semicontinuity of the spacetime vorticity score and the retained instantaneous part give the nontrivial lower bound; any missing amount is exactly one of the excluded tail, time-face, or fragmentation defects.
∎

Lemma L.7

(Closed scale-period energy: line-check form)
.

Let a compact recurrent conveyor satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
L.6
. If additionally

\mathfrak{O}_{\rm pressure}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm macro}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm collar}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm shell}=\mathfrak{O}_{\rm passive}=0,

then over one normalized recurrence period
[\tau,\tau+\bar{T}]
the active hull satisfies

E_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau+\bar{T})+\nu\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}(s)\,ds\leq E_{\mathcal{H}}(\tau)+o(1)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}(s)\,ds.

Proof.

By Lemma
L.5
, all noninternal terms in the projected hull identity are bounded by
\eta\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+C_{\eta}\mathfrak{O}_{\rm rout}+o(1)\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}}+\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}
. The named routing outputs vanish by assumption. The remaining comparable on-core flux cannot be external to the recurrent hull: a finite nonzero part would be admitted into the hull, a diffuse part would give fragmentation, and an inter-scale part would give scale-gap output. Thus
\mathfrak{F}_{\rm comp,on}=o(\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{H}})
on the period. Testing against
U_{\mathcal{H}}
leaves no internal contribution because
\langle(U_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\nabla)U_{\mathcal{H}},U_{\mathcal{H}}\rangle=0
. The recurrence period is measured after the common terminal normalization. Since zero shape, scale, tail, and time-face outputs exclude loss of hull energy between the two representatives of the period, the endpoint energy at the two period faces differs by
o(1)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+\bar{T}}D_{\mathcal{H}}
. Letting the terminal sequence pass and then
\eta\downarrow 0
gives the stated closed scale-period inequality.
∎
