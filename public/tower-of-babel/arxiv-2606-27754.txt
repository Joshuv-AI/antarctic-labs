Introduction

The classification of compact complex manifolds is still a widely open problem. In particular, the classification of compact non-Kähler manifolds is a very active field of research.

The first and most relevent results up to-day are in complex dimension
2
, basically due to Kodaira in the 60’s. Nonetheless, a complete classification is still missing, as the understanding of the surfaces in the so-called
Kodaira class

{\rm VII_{0}}
with
b_{2}>1
, is still unclear.

However, for non-Kähler compact surfaces with algebraic codimension one, the picture is clear (
[
BHPV
, Proposition 5.1]
,
[
VVO
]
): they are all
elliptic fibrations
, meaning that for any such surface
S
there exists a smooth projective curve
B
and a holomorphic surjective map
f:S\longrightarrow B
whose general fibers are all smooth elliptic (such a map is usually called
an elliptic pencil
).

In fact, the situation is even more precise: any non-Kähler compact surface with algebraic codimension one can be obtained starting from an elliptic principal bundle over a smooth projective curve by taking unramified quotients and blow-ups (see e. g.
[
Bri1
, Lemma 1, Lemma 2]
; also
[
Bri2
, Proposition 3.17, Lemma 3.18]
).

Remark 1.1:
In particular, all the smooth fibers of an elliptic pencil
f:S\longrightarrow B
on a non-Kähler compact surface
S
with algebraic codimension one are isomorphic to some fixed elliptic curve
F
. Moreover, if all the fibers of
f
are reduced, then all elliptic curves on
S
are isomorphic to the general fiber
F
.

It is a natural question to ask if the above result could be generalized to higher dimensions. An immediate example to see that the verbatim extension is hopeless is by taking the product
X=M\times S
, where
M
is a Moishezon non-Kähler manifold, and
S
is an elliptic
K3
surface of algebraic codimension one. The manifold
X
is obviously non-Kähler and its algebraic codimension is one; yet there are infinitely many non-isomorphic elliptic curves on
X
.

However, there exists a class of non-Kähler manifolds for which a similar result can be proven in arbitrary dimensions, namely the class of
Locally Conformally Kähler
manifolds (LCK manifolds, for short; see
2.1
). This class is wide enough: for instance, it contains
all
non-Kähler surfaces with algebraic dimension one and many manifolds obtained starting with an arbitrary algebraic cone of some projective space, by deleting the vertex and acting by a cyclic infinite group (see Subsection
2.1
).

To state the first result of the paper (
1
), we need to recall the following notion.

Definition 1.2:
An
isotrivial
family of curves is a surjective holomoprhic map
f:X\longrightarrow B
between complex manifolds, with
\mathrm{dim}(X)=\mathrm{dim}(B)+1
such that all its smooth fibers are isomorphic.

Theorem 1.3:
Let
M
be a non-Kähler compact complex LCK manifold with algebraic codimension one. Then
M
is bimeromorphic to a manifold
M^{a}
which has an isotrivial family of elliptic curves
f^{a}:M^{a}\longrightarrow B^{a}
over a smooth projective manifold
B^{a}.

Proof.
See Section
3

Unfortunately, it seems difficult to produce an elliptic bundle bimeromorphic to
M
, as in the case of surfaces. The main difficulty in the case when
\mathrm{dim}(M)\geqslant 3
comes from the fact that
f^{a}
may have fibers of dimensions at least two.

A first attempt in this direction was done in
[
APV
]
where it was proven that any compact complex LCK threefold of algebraic codimension one can be obtained from elliptic principal bundles over projective smooth bases, up to alterations.

However, the main result of
[
APV
]
, namely Theorem 2.12, is valid only under two assumptions, seemingly true, but still not proven up to now: (1) the Strong Factorization Conjecture, and (2) the existence of minimal smooth models for elliptic non-Kähler compact threefolds.

In the present paper, we succeed to avoid the use of the above two conjectural assumptions and clarify the structure of compact LCK manifolds of arbitrary dimension and algebraic codimension one.

To fix the terminology, we shall use the following terms:

•

A
modification
(see e. g.
[
Ue
, Definition 2.1]
) is a proper, surjective, holomorphic bimeromorphic map
f:X\longrightarrow Y
between complex analytic spaces.

•

An
alteration
(see
[
DeJ
]
,
[
AO
]
) is a proper, surjective, generically finite map
f:X\longrightarrow Y
between complex analytic spaces. Notice that any modification is a particular case of alteration.

We can now give the the precise statement of the main result of the present paper.

Main Theorem:

Let
M
be a non-Kähler LCK smooth compact manifold with algebraic codimension one. Then there exists an alteration
X\longrightarrow M
(with
X
smooth) and a projective manifold
B
such that
X
is a modification of an elliptic principal bundle over
B
.

The proof (see Section
4
) is mainly based on algebraic methods in analytic spaces, and goes on the following lines. Through a proper modification of our LCK manifold
M
, one can produce an algebraic reduction,
f^{a}\ :\ M^{a}\longrightarrow B^{a}
, whose general fibers are smooth elliptic curves (
[
Ue
, Theorem 12.4]
). Since not all the fibers of the algebraic reduction are of dimension one, we are forced to apply the Hironaka flattening (
[
Hi1
]
) to
f^{a}
. We then obtain a map
f\ :\ X\longrightarrow B
, where
X
a complex space (possibly singular), which is a modification of
M^{a}
,
B
is a smooth projective proper modification of
B
, and all fibers of
f
are of dimension one. After passing, if necessarily, to finite covers of
X
and
B
, we can assume that
f
has reduced fibers. Blowing-up convenient subspaces of
X
, we can assume
X
smooth. At this point, the LCK structure of
M
becomes essential and leads to a clear description of the fibers of
f
: they all consists of a smooth elliptic curve plus some trees of rational curves. Eventually, we prove that the manifold
X
is a proper modification of an elliptic principal bundle.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we shall briefly review several basic notions concerning the geometric and analytic aspects of the manifolds we are interested in.

2.1
Locally Conformally Kähler geometry

In this subsection we introduce the
basic notions of locally conformally Kähler
(LCK) geometry. For details and proofs we
refer to
[
OV
]
.

Definition 2.1:

A Hermitian manifold

(M,I,g,\omega)
is called

locally conformally Kähler
(LCK) if
there exists a closed 1-form
\theta
such that
d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
.
The 1-form
\theta
is called the
Lee form
.

Remark 2.2:
If the Lee form is exact,
\theta=df
, then the manifold is
Globally Conformally Kähler
(GCK), since the metric
e^{-f}g
is Kähler.

Proposition 2.3:

A Hermitian manifold

(M,I,g,\omega)
is LCK if and only if
it admits a Kähler cover
(\tilde{M},\tilde{\omega}){\>\longrightarrow\>}M
whose deck group
\Gamma

acts on the Kähler metric
\tilde{\omega}

by holomorphic homotheties.

Remark 2.4:
Since all smooth compact curves are Kähler, the LCK geometry is not interesting in dimension one. The examples in dimensions greater than one are abundant (see
[
OV
]
), e. g. : almost all compact complex surfaces, all Hopf manifolds (either linear or non-linear), Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds, Kato manifolds.

Theorem 2.5:
(Vaisman,
[
Va
]
)
A compact LCK manifold with non-exact Lee form does not
admit any Kähler metric.

We proved in
[
OVV
, Corollary 2.14]
that the LCK class is not well-behaved with respect to blow-ups. Indeed, the blow up at points preserves the LCK class, whilst the blow-up along a submanifold is LCK if and only if the restriction of the Lee form to the submanifold is exact. This motivated a natural extension of the LCK definition, such as the larger class be closed to blow-up.

Definition 2.6:
(
[
APV
]
)
A
weak locally conformally Kähler
(WLCK) structure
on a complex manifold
(M,I)
is given by a (1, 1)-form
\omega
and a real 1-form

\theta
such that:

•

d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
and
d\theta=0
;

•

\omega
is strictly positive definite outside a proper analytic subset (called the
degeneracy locus
of
\omega
).

Remark 2.7:
If
f\ :\ X\longrightarrow Y
is an alteration between manifolds
X
and
Y
, and if
Y
is WLCK, then
X
is WLCK too (and the Lee form on
X
is the pull-back of the Lee form on
Y
). Notice also that if
X
is a WLCK manifold and
Y\subset X
is a submanifold such that
Y
is not included in the degeneracy locus of the WLCK structure of
X
, then
Y
inherits a WLCK structure too. Moreover, Vaisman’s theorem
2.1
holds in WLCK geometry too.

The following is an useful tool, firstly proved in LCK geometry, but which subsists in WLCK geometry too.

Proposition 2.8:
(
[
OPV
,
APV
]
)
(“Lemma on fibrations”). Let
X
and
B
be complex manifolds
with
{\rm dim}X>{\rm dim}B
. Let
f:X\longrightarrow B
be a surjective proper holomorphic map
with connected fibres. Let
(\omega,\theta)
be a WLCK structure on
X
. If the Lee class

[\theta]=f^{*}[\alpha]
is in the image of the pull-back
f^{*}:H^{1}(B)\longrightarrow H^{1}(X)
induced by
f
in cohomology,
then
[\theta]=0
.

2.2
Algebraic reduction of complex manifolds

We begin by recalling the concept of
algebraic reduction
of a compact complex manifold
M
(see
[
Ue
]
for more details).

By a classical result of Siegel, the space
{\mathcal{M}}(M)
of global meromorphic functions on
M
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{C}}-
algebra.

Definition 2.9:
The transcendence degree of
{\mathcal{M}}(M)
is called the
algebraic dimension
,
a(M)
, of
M
and the difference
\dim(M)-a(M)
is called
algebraic codimension
.

Construction and Definition 2.10:
Let
V
be the affine algebraic variety associated to
{\mathcal{M}}(M)
(that is, its field of rational global functions is
{\mathcal{M}}(M)
) and
\Phi\ :\ M\dashrightarrow V
a meromorphic dominant map. Replacing
V
by its projective closure
\overline{V}
and possibly resolving the singularities of
\overline{V}
, we obtain a smooth projective manifold
B^{a}
and a meromorphic dominant map
\varphi\ :\ M\dashrightarrow B^{a}.
Resolving the indeterminacies of
\varphi
, produces a proper modification
c:M^{a}\longrightarrow M
of
M
and a holomorphic map
f^{a}\ :\ M^{a}\longrightarrow B^{a}.
The triple
(M^{a},f^{a},B^{a})
is called
an algebraic reduction
of
M
(some authors call
B^{a}
alone the algebraic reduction). Notice that the algebraic reduction is only defined up to bimeromorphisms.

Remark 2.11:
If
M
has an LCK structure, then
M^{a}
is a modification of
M
, and it has an WLCK structure. The Lee form of this structure is the pull-back by
c
of the Lee form on
M.

The algebraic reduction is the main tool used by Kodaira in the classification of non-projective compact complex surfaces. For non-Kähler surfaces
S
with
a(S)=1
, the algebraic reduction implies that
S
is bimeromorphic to an elliptic fibration
\hat{S}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
onto some smooth projective curve
B
whose smooth fibers are
isomorphic
elliptic curves. Let
S_{\min}
be the minimal model of
S
, then any singular fiber of the map
S_{\min}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
admits a smooth reduction, which is still an elliptic curve (see e. g.
[
Bri1
, Lemma 1]
). Taking a suitably chosen (ramified) cover of
B^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B,
and letting
S^{\prime}\longrightarrow S
the induced (étale) cover, one can prove that the induced map
f^{\prime}\ :\ S^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B^{\prime}
has only smooth fibers (see
[
Bri1
, Lemma 2]
), which are smooth isomorphic elliptic curves. It follows that
S^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B^{\prime}
is a locally trivial elliptic bundle. Eventually, the non-Kählerianity of
S^{\prime}
combined with a Kodaira’s projectivity criterion for surfaces implies that
S^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B^{\prime}
is an elliptic principal bundle (see e. g.
[
VVO
]
). Summing up, one has the following description of the analytic structure of any non-Kähler surface:

Theorem 2.12:

Let
S
be a non-Kähler smooth compact surface with algebraic codimension one. Then there exists an alteration (in fact, even a unramfied cover)
\eta:S^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}S
with
S^{\prime}
smooth, and a projective smooth curve
B
such that
S^{\prime}
is a blow-up of an elliptic principal bundle
X^{\prime}\longrightarrow B.

Remark 2.13:
Since every non-Kähler elliptic surface admits an LCK metric (see
[
Be
,
Tr
,
VVO
]
), in
2.2
we can replace “non-Kähler” by “LCK”.

2.3
Flat morphisms and flattening

We recollect some basic facts about flat morphisms that will be used in the sequel. In what follows,
X,Y...
will denote complex analytic spaces.

Definition 2.14:
Let
X,Y
be analytic spaces, and
x\in X
. A map
f\ :\ X\longrightarrow Y
is called
flat at
x
if the local ring
{\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}
is a flat module over
{\mathcal{O}}_{f(x),Y}.
The map
f
is called
flat
if it is flat at all points in
X.
The set of points
y\in Y
such that
f
is flat in any point of the preimage
f^{-1}(y)
is called
the flat locus
of
f
.

Remark 2.15:
It is not hard to show that flatness commutes vith base change, that is, for any flat map
f:X\longrightarrow B
and any map
f^{\prime}:B^{\prime}\longrightarrow B
, the map induced by base-change
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}=X\times_{B}B^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
is also flat.

Remark 2.16:
Flat maps are
equidimensional
(see e. g.
[
GrPe
, Proposition 2.11]
, that is, for any point
y\in Y
the fiber
X_{y}:=X\times_{Y}\{y\}
has the same dimension. The converse does not hold in general. Still, an important result in this direction is
2.3
. Before stating it, we need to recall some basic notions of commutative algebra.

Definition 2.17:
(
[
BrHe
]
) Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local noetherian ring. A
system of parameters
for
A
is a sequence
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
t_{1}
is not a zero divisor in
A
and for
i\geqslant 2
,
t_{i}
is not a zero-divisor in
A/(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1}).
The
depth
of
A
is the maximal length of a system of parameters. The ring
A
is called
Cohen-Macaulay
(CM, for short) if its depth equals its Krull dimension,
\mathrm{depth}(A)=\dim(A).
An analytic space
X
is called CM if all its local rings
{\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}
are CM.

For a proof, see e. g.
[
BrHe
, Theorem 2.2.4]
, (resp.
[
BrHe
, Theorem 2.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.18:
Let
A
be a local noetherian ring and
t\in A
not a zero divisor. If
A/(t)
is regular (resp. Cohen-Macaulay) then
A
is regular (resp. Cohen-Macaulay).
In particular, if
X_{C}
is smooth (resp. Cohen-Macaulay) then
X
is smooth (resp. Cohen-Macaulay) in all points of
X_{C}
.

Proposition 2.19:
(
Miracle flatness
,
[
Ma
, Theorem 23.1]
) Let
X,Y
be analytic spaces, with
X
Cohen-Macaulay and
Y
smooth. Then any equidimensional surjective morphism
f\ :\ X\longrightarrow Y
is flat.

Remark 2.20:
A situation when the flatness of a morphism
f:X\longrightarrow Y
can be easily proven is when
X
is locally irreducible of dimension
2
,
Y
is smooth of dimension one and
f
is a surjective morphism
f:X\longrightarrow Y
. In this case for any
x\in X
the ring
A={\mathcal{O}}_{x,X}
is a domain and the ring
R={\mathcal{O}}_{f(x),Y}
is a discrete valuation ring (DVR), since
Y
is smooth. But this immediately implies that
f
is flat, since as
A
is a domain, it is a torsion-free
R-
module, and since
R
is a DVR, torsion free
R-
modules are flat (see e. g.
[
Ei
, Corollary 6.3]
.

The typical example of a non-flat map is the blow-up map, as it is non equidimensional. In particular, non-flat maps arise usually as soon as one does a resolution of singularities. It is thus natural to ask whether one can turn a non-flat map into a flat one, by performing modifications. The cornerstone result is due to Hironaka (first proven by M. Raynaud and L. Gruson,
[
RaGr
]
, in the context of algebraic geometry):

Theorem 2.21:
(
[
Hi1
]
, Hironaka flattening) Let
f:X\longrightarrow Y
be a proper surjective morphism of complex spaces. Then there exists modifications
m_{X}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow X
,
m_{Y}:Y^{\prime}\longrightarrow Y
and a proper surjective holomorphic map
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow Y^{\prime}
such that the following diagram is commutative

\begin{CD}X@<{m_{X}}<{}<X^{\prime}\\
@V{f}V{}V@V{}V{f^{\prime}}V\\
Y@<{}<{m_{B}}<Y^{\prime}\end{CD}

and
f^{\prime}
is flat.

A more concrete description of the flattening is given in
[
Hi1
, Corollary 1.1]
which we quote verbatim:

Theorem 2.22:
For
f:X\longrightarrow Y
of the theorem, there exists a projective
bimeromorphic morphism
\pi:Y^{\prime}\longrightarrow Y
and a closed complex subspace
D
of
Y
such
that:

(i)
D
is nowhere dense in
Y
,
\pi^{-1}(D)
is nowhere dense in
Y^{\prime}
, and
\pi

induces an isomorphism
Y^{\prime}\setminus\pi^{-1}(D)\simeq Y\setminus D
.

(ii) if
X^{\prime}
is the closure of
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}\setminus(\pi\circ f)^{-1}(D)
in
X\times_{Y}Y^{\prime}
then
f^{\prime}

induces a flat morphism
X^{\prime}\longrightarrow Y^{\prime}
.
In particular, the fibres
(f^{\prime})^{-1}(y^{\prime})
with
y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}
have the same dimension over
each connected component of
Y^{\prime}
.

Remark 2.23:
In
2.3
, the space
X^{\prime}
is obtained from
X
by performing a finite sequence of well-chosen blow-ups. Unfortunately, there is no control on the singularities of

. Still, by the commutativity of the flattening with the base-change, and using the Hironaka resolution of singularities

is smooth.

Example 2.24:
(i) Let

, and

and

and

(ii) A more illuminating example appears in
[

and let

and let

A flattening of

to be the blow-up of

of

and defining

to be the induced map.

For further use we recall the following well-known-fact.

Lemma 2.25:
Let

be a local noetherian ring and

is regular (resp. Cohen-Macaulay).

@V{f^{a}}V{}V@V{}V{f}V\\
B^{a}@<{}<{m_{B}}<B\end{CD}

is commutative.

The issue is that Hironaka flattening does not guarantee that
X
is smooth. To get a smooth
X
, we procced as follows.

In a first step, in
4.1
we perform alterations of
X
and
B
to eliminate the possible non-reduced fibers. As a result, we obtain a diagram

\begin{CD}X@<{r_{X}}<{}<X^{\prime}\\
@V{f}V{}V@V{}V{f^{\prime}}V\\
B@<{}<{r_{B}}<B^{\prime}\end{CD}

(4.1)

with
B^{\prime}
smooth projective and
f^{\prime}
flat with all its fibres reduced.

In a second step, we use the LCK structure of
M
to show (
4.1
) that any fiber of
f^{\prime}
consists of an elliptic curve plus some trees of rational curves (this will be also useful in the last part of the paper).

Eventually, using
4.1
, we show that there exist the alterations
r_{X^{\prime}}:\hat{X}\longrightarrow X^{\prime}
and
r_{B^{\prime}}:\hat{B}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
with both
\hat{X}
and
\hat{B}
smooth, and an equidimensional map
\hat{f}:\hat{X}\longrightarrow\hat{B}
such that the diagram

\begin{CD}X^{\prime}@<{r_{X}^{\prime}}<{}<\hat{X}\\
@V{f^{\prime}}V{}V@V{}V{\hat{f}}V\\
B^{\prime}@<{}<{r_{B}^{\prime}}<\hat{B}\end{CD}

is commutative.

We now provide the details of the proof.

We start with the following lemma wich is a weak version of the “Weak semi-stable reduction” theorem (see e. g.
[
AK
]
,
[
DeJ
]
). Here we only consider the case of equidimensional families of curves, and we only ask that the resulting fibers be reduced (we do not pay attention to their semi-stability). For convenience of the reader, we include a proof for our context.

Lemma 4.2:
Let
f:X\longrightarrow B
be a flat surjective morphism, with
B
smooth projective and
{\rm dim}(X)={\rm dim}(B)+1
. Then there exist alterations
r_{X}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow X
, and
r_{B}:B^{\prime}\longrightarrow B
(with
B
smooth projective) and a surjective flat holomorphic map
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
such that the diagram

\begin{CD}X@<{r_{X}}<{}<X^{\prime}\\
@V{f}V{}V@V{}V{f^{\prime}}V\\
B@<{}<{r_{B}}<B^{\prime}\end{CD}

is commutative and such that all the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are reduced.

Proof:
Let
Z\subset X
be the locus of the fibers with nilpotent elements and
D=f(Z).
Using Hironaka resolution of singularities, there exists a composition of blow-ups
c:\hat{B}\longrightarrow B
with
\hat{B}
smooth and such that the inverse image
\hat{D}\subset\hat{B}
of
D
is a divisor in simple normal crossing. Let
\hat{D}=\sum_{i}m_{i}C_{i}
be the decomposition of
\hat{D}
into irreducible components. We apply the Kawamata trick (
[
Ka
, Theorem 17]
) to
\hat{B}
with respect to
(m_{i},C_{i})
. This way, we produce a new manifold
B^{\prime}
and a covering
r:B^{\prime}\longrightarrow\hat{B}
, ramified of order
m_{i}
along each
C_{i}
. Let
\hat{X}:=X\times_{B}\hat{B}
, and
X^{\prime}
the normalization of
\hat{X}\times_{\hat{B}}B^{\prime}
. With a computation similar to the one in
[
APV
, Lemma 1.4]
, one can see that the induced map
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
has only reduced fibers. Notice that the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are curves, since the normalization map
X^{\prime}\longrightarrow\hat{X}\times_{\hat{B}}B^{\prime}
is finite and the fibers of
\hat{X}\times_{\hat{B}}B^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
are curves, and hence
f^{\prime}
is flat.

To proceed, we need to introduce the followibg definition.

Definition 4.3:
Let
f:X\longrightarrow B
be a morphism of complex spaces and let
C\subset B
be a curve. The
slice
of
f
along
C
is the subspace
X_{C}=X\times_{B}C
of
X
, that is
\mid X_{C}\mid=f^{-1}(C)
with ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{J}}_{X_{C}\mid X}=f^{*}\left({\mathcal{J}}_{C\mid B}\right)
, where
{\mathcal{J}}_{C\mid B}
is the sheaf of ideals of
C
in
B.

Lemma 4.4:
Let
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
be a flat surjective morphism with reduced fibers with
B^{\prime}
a projective manifold and
{\rm dim}(X^{\prime})={\rm dim}(B^{\prime})+1.
Assume that the general fibers of
f^{\prime}
are smooth and connected. Then, for a general curve
C\subset B
we have:

(i) All the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are connected.

(ii) The slice
X^{\prime}_{C}
is normal.

(iii) If
X^{\prime}
is an alteration of an LCK manifold, then any desingularization
\widehat{X^{\prime}_{C}}
of the slice
{X^{\prime}_{C}}
is a non-Kähler (elliptic) surface.

(iv) If
X^{\prime}
is an alteration of an LCK manifold, any fiber of
f^{\prime}
consists of a smooth elliptic curve plus some trees of rational curves. Moreover, all elliptic curves contained in the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are isomorphic (to a fixed elliptic curve
F
).

Proof:
(i) Consider the Stein factorization of
f^{\prime}=h\circ f
given by
X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\longrightarrow}}B^{\prime}
with
f
a connected morphism and
h
a finite morphism. Since the general fibers of
f^{\prime}
are connected,
h^{-1}(b)
is a single point for general
b\in B^{\prime}.
Therefore
h
is a bimeromorphism, and hence by Zariski Main Theorem
h^{-1}(b)
is connected for all
b\in B^{\prime}.
The finiteness of
h
implies that
h^{-1}(b)
is a single point for all
b\in B,
and thus
h
is an isomorphism (because
B^{\prime}
is normal).

(ii) First, we notice that
X^{\prime}_{C}
is irreducible: indeed,
C
is smooth and connected (being general), and also the general fiber of the fibration
X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
is smooth and connected.
Next, we notice that since the fibers of the induced map
f{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{X^{\prime}_{C}}}\right.}:X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
are one-dimensional and reduced, they are Cohen-Macaulay (see e. g.
[
BD
]
). Therefore,
2.3
implies that
X^{\prime}_{C}
is CM. As there are finitely many singular points in the fibers, using again
2.3
we deduce that
X^{\prime}_{C}
has singularities at most at the points where the fibers are singular. In particular,
X^{\prime}_{C}
has finitely many singular points. The normality of
X^{\prime}_{C}
now follows from
Serre’s criterion for normality (see e. g
[
Ma
, Theorem 23.8]
) which in our context reads: “An analytic space of dimension
2
is normal if and only if it is Cohen-Macaulay and it has at most finitely many singular points”.
This proves (ii).

(iii) We denote by
\sigma:\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow X^{\prime}_{C}
a desingularization of
X^{\prime}_{C}.

Let
S_{C}\subset M
be the image of
X^{\prime}_{C}
under the composition of alterations
X^{\prime}\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow M^{a}\longrightarrow M
. Since
C
is general,
S_{C}
is a surface. We thus obtain a proper, surjective, generically finite morphism
s:\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow S_{C}
. Pulling-back the LCK structure from
M
to
\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}
we see that
\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}
has a WLCK structure with Lee form
s^{*}(\theta)
(
2.1
).

Assume, by absurd, that
\hat{X^{\prime}}_{C}
is Kähler. Recall that Vaisman theorem also holds for WLCK structures (
2.1
), therefore the class
s^{*}([\theta])=0.
This implies that the restriction of
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
to the general fibers of
X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
is also zero. Since
C
is general, by moving it we can cover the whole
B
, and hence the restriction of
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
to the general fibers of
X\longrightarrow B
vanishes. It follows that the restriction of
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
to the preimage of the set
\operatorname{Reg}(f)
of regular values of
f
is the pull-back of a class
\alpha\in H^{1}(\operatorname{Reg}(f))
. This class
\alpha
can be seen as a cohomology class on
B
, since
H^{1}(B)
and
H^{1}(\operatorname{Reg}(f))
are canonically isomorphic.

We conclude that the restriction of
\alpha
to iterated hyperplane sections vanishes, and hence, by Lefschetz,
\alpha=0
. Therefore
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]=0
which implies
[\theta]=0,
absurd, hence
\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}
is non-Kähler. This proves (iii).

The statement (iv) follows from (ii) and (iii). Indeed, all the fibers of
f^{\prime}
are also fibers of general slices
X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
. On the other hand,
X^{\prime}_{C}
is normal, and hence the fibers of
X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
are obtained by contracting some curves in the fibers of the desingularization
f^{\prime}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{X^{\prime}_{C}}}\right.}\circ\sigma:\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C.
Since
\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}
is non-Kähler, its fibers are smooth elliptic curve plus trees of rational curves
[
VVO
]
.

The elliptic component is not contracted in any fiber
\left(X^{\prime}_{C}\right)_{c}=({f^{\prime}}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{{X^{\prime}_{C}}}}\right.})^{-1}(c)
(
c\in C
). Indeed, since contracting rational curves from a tree yields a tree, such a singular fiber would be simply-connected. This implies that there exists some open neiborhood
V
of
\left(X^{\prime}_{C}\right)_{c}
which is simply-connected. It follows the the restriction of the class
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
of the Lee form to any fiber of
X^{\prime}_{C}\longrightarrow C
contained in
V
vanishes, hence the restriction of
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
to the general fibers of
X_{C}\longrightarrow C
vanishes, which implies that
[\theta_{X^{\prime}}]
is a pull-back from
C
. But then
\hat{X}^{\prime}_{C}
would be Kähler by
2.1
.
Eventually, the fact that all elliptic curves contained in the fibers are isomorphic follows from the fact that this is true for the fibers of any non-Kähler elliptic surfaces. This proves (iv).

For the last step of the proof of
4.1
, we introduce a numerical invariant as a measure of singularities of
X.

Definition 4.5:
Let
f:X\longrightarrow B
be a flat surjective morphism with reduced fibers, with
B
a projective manifold, and
{\rm dim}(X)={\rm dim}(B)+1
. Assume that the general fibers of
f
are smooth.

(i) Fix an arbitrary point

. For an arbitrary general curve
C\subset B
through

we define

the defect

{\operatorname{Def}}(X_{C})
of
X_{C}
as the number of irreducible components of the fiber
F_{b}
which are contracted (i. e. mapped to points) to
X_{b}
under the minimal desingularization
\sigma:\hat{X}_{C}\longrightarrow X_{C}
of
\hat{X}_{C}
.

(ii)
The defect

\operatorname{Def}(b)
of a point

is the minimum of
{\operatorname{Def}}(X_{C})
, where
C\subset B
is a general curve through

(iii)
The defect

\operatorname{Def}(X)
of
X
is defined as
\operatorname{Def}(X):=\max_{b\in B}\operatorname{Def}(b).

Remark 4.6:
Suppose
{\operatorname{Def}}(X_{C})=0
. Then
\sigma
is an isomorphism, hence
{X}_{C}
is smooth. It follows that

is smooth along

(see
2.3
).
In particular, if

is a point such that if

, then

is smooth along

Lemma 4.7:
Let

be a flat surjective morphism with reduced fibers with

a projective manifold and
{\rm dim}(X^{\prime})={\rm dim}(B^{\prime})+1
. Assume that the general fibers of

are smooth. Then there exists a modification

, a modification

with

a smooth projective manifold, and a flat holomorphic map

such that the following diagram is commutative

\begin{CD}X^{\prime}@<{m_{X}}<{}<X^{\prime\prime}\\
@V{f^{\prime}}V{}V@V{}V{f^{\prime\prime}}V\\

and such that
{\operatorname{Def}}(X^{\prime\prime})<{\operatorname{Def}}(X^{\prime}).

Proof:
Let
S(X^{\prime})
be the union of singular loci of the fibers of
f^{\prime},

S(X^{\prime})=\bigcup_{b\in B}\mathrm{Sing}(X^{\prime}_{b}).

Since any fiber has at most finitely singular points, we obtain that
{\rm dim}S(X^{\prime})={\rm dim}f^{\prime}(S(X^{\prime}))
.
Since the general fibers of
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow B^{\prime}
are smooth, we see that
f^{\prime}(S(X^{\prime}))
is a proper subspace of

, hence
{\rm dim}f^{\prime}(S(X^{\prime}))<{\rm dim}(B^{\prime})
.
If
{\rm codim}(f(S(X^{\prime}))\geqslant 2
, by blowing-up

and possibly desingularizing

(using

]
) we get a modification

. Doing base change:

\begin{CD}X^{\prime}@<{b_{X}}<{}<X_{1}\\
@V{f^{\prime}}V{}V@V{}V{f_{1}}V\\
B^{\prime}@<{}<{b_{B}}<B_{1}\end{CD}

where
X_{1}=X^{\prime}\times_{B}B_{1}
, we may assume that

is of codimension

.
Notice that since we are doing base-change, the morphism

is still flat and, for any point

, the fibers of

are isomorphic to the fibers of

. In particular,

Let

be the blow-up of

along

(considered with the reduced structure). Since

is of codimension

, then for any

the fiber of the map

over

over

plus some newly created curve which is the fiber of the natural map from the exceptional divisor of

to

in

. Since the defect counts the number of contracted curves, this construction decreases the defect. Note that blowing-up along a reduced space does not create nilpotent elements, and hence all the fibers of

are reduced. Therefore

This comppletes the proof of

.

4.2
The proof of the main theorem

Let
M
be LCK. Using the algebraic reduction (
2.2
), there exists a modification
M^{a}\longrightarrow M
such that
M^{a}
sends a surjective holomorphic map
f^{a}:M^{a}\longrightarrow B^{a}
onto a projective smooth manifold. Using Hironaka flattening (
2.3
), there exists a modification
X_{1}
of
M^{a}
and a flat morphism
f:X_{1}\longrightarrow B_{1}
onto some smooth projective manifold
B_{1}.
Using
4.1
, we produce an alteration
X_{2}\longrightarrow X_{1}
such that there exists a morphism
X_{2}\longrightarrow B_{2}
as in diagram (
4.1
) having only reduced fibers. Making repeated use of
4.1
, we produce a new modification
X
of
X_{2}
which is smooth and a flat map

f:X\longrightarrow B
onto a projective manifold such that all its fibers
X_{b}
are composed of an elliptic curve
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
plus some trees of rational curves. Notice that there exists a smooth non-Kähler slice
X_{C}
through every point of
X
because
{\rm Def}(X)=0
.

Eventually, to produce an elliptic bundle
{\mathcal{P}}\longrightarrow B
such that
X
is a modification of
{\mathcal{P}}
, we argue as follows.

Recall the context. We have compact complex manifolds
X
and
B
, a fixed smooth curve
F
of genus one, and a holomorphic surjective map
f:X\longrightarrow B
such that, any fiber
X_{b}
of
f
consists of a genus one curve
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
isomorphic to
F
plus some trees
\{T^{i}_{b}\}_{i=1,\ldots m(b)}
of rational curves attached to
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
at the points
R^{i}_{b},{i=1,\dots m(b)}
. We call
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}

the stem of the fiber
, and
R^{i}=R_{T^{i}}
the
root points
of the fiber
X_{b}
. Notice that each stem
{\mathcal{E}}_{b}
is isomorphic to a fixed elliptic curve
F
. Moreover, for any point
b\in B
such that the fiber
X_{b}
is singular, there exists a smooth curve
C\subset B
such that its preimage
X_{C}=X\times_{B}C
is a smooth non-Kähler surface.
We shall also denote by
\Delta\subset B
the
discriminant
of
f
, that is, the set of critical values of
f
. By Sard Lemma,
\Delta

is a proper subvariety of
B
. Let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset X
be the set of all root points contained in the fibers of
f
; one can that
{\mathcal{R}}
is of codimension at least
2.

Choose a local section
s:U\longrightarrow X
of
f:X\longrightarrow B
. For an appropriate choice of
s
, its image
\Sigma=s(U)
contains no root point of any fiber. Let
D=f^{-1}(U)
and consider the linear map
\varphi:D\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(f_{*}\left({\mathcal{O}}_{D}(3\Sigma)\right)
; call
P
the image of
\varphi
(see the diagram below).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

For any point
b\in U
, the restriction of the map
\varphi
to the fiber

contracts all the rational curves and maps isomorphically the stem

of the fiber to the fiber

of

at

.
In particular, all the fibers of the map

are isomorphic to

.

by the assumption on

p:P\longrightarrow U

Now cover
