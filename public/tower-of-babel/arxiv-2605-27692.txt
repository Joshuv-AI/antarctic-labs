Abstract
. We give a complete classification of the Jordan types occurring in the nilpotent commutator of a nilpotent matrix whose Jordan type is a hook partition. As a consequence, we also show that two partitions with the same generic commuting Jordan type need not commute with each other.

Primary 15A27. Secondary 05A17, 13E10, 14A10.

1.
Introduction

The question of which partitions of a given natural number can occur as the Jordan types of two commuting nilpotent matrices has attracted substantial interest in recent decades. We refer the readers to surveys
Kha-S
;
Kos-S
for more information. Since the nilpotent commutator of a nilpotent matrix with Jordan type
P
is an irreducible variety (as proved by Basili in
(
Bas-2
, Lem. 2.3)
), its intersection with one of the nilpotent orbits is a dense subset. The partition corresponding to the dense orbit studied by Panyushev in
Pan
for the first time, is denoted by
\mathcal{D}(P)
. It is a super-distinct or a Rogers-Ramanujan partition, that is, it is a partition with parts differing by at least
2

(
BI
, Thm. 1.12)
. The largest and the smallest part of
\mathcal{D}(P)
were given by Oblak
Obl-1
and Khatami
Kha-2
, respectively. The map
\mathcal{D}
is idempotent
(
KO
, Thm. 6)
. The authors of
(
IKvSZ
, Conj. 4.11)
formulated the Box Conjecture on the structure of the inverse image
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(Q)
of a Rogers-Ramanujan partition
Q
. The conjecture was recently proved in
(
IKM
, Thm. 1)
.

It is known that every nilpotent commutator intersects all nilpotent orbits of Jordan type
P=(2^{k},1^{l})
, where
k
and
l
are integers (see
(
Obl-3
, Thm. 2.4)
or
(
BW
, Thm. 4.11)
). Such partitions are called universally commuting. On the other hand, if the Jordan type of a matrix
B
is a Rogers-Ramanujan partition then no other nilpotent orbit corresponding to a Rogers-Ramanujan partition intersects the nilpotent commutator of
B

(
Kos-S
, Thm. 5.3)
.

The authors in
(
BDKOS
, Thm. 3)
described the nilpotent orbits that intersect the commutator of a nilpotent matrix
A
of Jordan type
(n,n)
. In this paper, we consider a nilpotent matrix whose Jordan type is a hook partition
(n,1^{m})
, where
n\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
, and describe all the nilpotent orbits that intersect its nilpotent commutator. Note that then
\mathcal{D}(n,1^{m})
is a Rogers-Ramanujan partition with two parts. Let us also mention that the special case of our result for
m=1
was proved by Britnell and Wildon in
(
BW
, Prop. 4.9)
.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we introduce the notation and our setting, and in section
3
we present and prove our main results. We describe all partitions that commute with a hook partition
(n,1^{m})
. In particular, we show that a necessary condition for a partition to commute with
(n,1^{m})
is to contain a subpartition that is an almost rectangular partition of either
n
,
n-1
or
n-2
. In section
4
, we discuss consequences of the main result and provide examples. Among these are examples that show that, in general, the hook partitions in the inverse image
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(Q)
need not commute with every other partition in
\mathcal{D}^{-1}(Q)
.

2.
Preliminaries

We assume that
\mathbb{F}
is an infinite field of characteristic
0
and we denote by
\operatorname{Mat}_{N}(\mathbb{F})
the set of all
N\times N
matrices over
\mathbb{F}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}(N)
the set of all partitions of a natural number
N
. A partition
P\in\mathcal{P}(N)
is written as
P=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, where
\lambda_{i}
are arranged in nonincreasing order,
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}=N
.

Suppose
B
is a nilpotent matrix and
P=P_{B}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\in\mathcal{P}(N)
is its Jordan type, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{k}>0
are sizes of its Jordan blocks. The nilpotent commutator
\mathcal{N}_{B}
of
B
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{B}=\{A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{N}(\mathbb{F}):\,BA=AB,A^{N}=0\}.

We are interested in partitions
Q\in\mathcal{P}(N)
such that the nilpotent orbit
\mathcal{O}_{Q}
, that is the set of all nilpotent matrices of Jordan type
Q
, intersects
\mathcal{N}_{B}
nontrivially, i.e., such that
\mathcal{O}_{Q}\cap\mathcal{N}_{B}\neq\emptyset
.

In connection with commuting Jordan types, two classes of partitions play an important role. A partition
Q=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k})
is called
super-distinct or Rogers-Ramanujan
if its parts differ by at least
2
:
\mu_{i}-\mu_{i+1}\geq 2
for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.

On the opposite side, a partition
Q=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{k})
is called
almost rectangular
if its largest and smallest parts differ by at most
1
:
\mu_{1}-\mu_{k}\leq 1
. Note that if
J_{N}
is the
N\times N
Jordan nilpotent matrix with one Jordan block of size
N
, then the Jordan types of its powers
J_{N}^{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,N,
are exactly all almost-rectangular partitions. We denote by
[N]^{k}
the Jordan type of
J_{N}^{k}
.

3.
Commuting with a hook partition

In this section, we characterize all possible Jordan types in the nilpotent commutator of a hook partition
\Gamma_{n,m}=(n,1^{m})
. We write
N=n+m
. Let
B
be the nilpotent Jordan matrix with Jordan type
\Gamma_{n,m}
, and let
\mathcal{U}_{B}
be the maximal nilpotent subalgebra of the nilpotent commutator
\mathcal{N}_{B}
as in
BaIK2010
. Then
\mathcal{U}_{B}
intersects every nilpotent orbit that intersects
\mathcal{N}_{B}

(
BaIK2010
, Lem. 2.2)
, and consists of all matrices of the following form in
\operatorname{Mat}_{N}(\mathbb{F})
, the space of all
N
by
N
matrices over the field
\mathbb{F}
.

(1)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc|ccccc}0&h_{1}&h_{2}&\cdots&h_{n-1}&u_{1}&u_{2}&\cdots&u_{m-1}&u_{m}\\
0&0&h_{1}&\cdots&h_{n-2}&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&h_{1}&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&0&0&\cdots&d_{1}&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&d_{2}&v_{12}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&0&\cdots&d_{3}&v_{13}&v_{23}&0&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&&&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&d_{m}&v_{1m}&v_{2m}&\cdots&v_{m-1,m}&0\\
\end{array}\right)

Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}^{n}
. Then we can write

(2)

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}H&e_{1}u^{T}\\
\hline\cr de_{n}^{T}&V\end{array}\right),

where
H=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}h_{i}J_{n}^{i}
, vectors
u
and
d
are both in
\mathbb{F}^{m}
, and
V
is a strictly lower triangular matrix in
\operatorname{Mat}_{m}(\mathbb{F})
.

Lemma 1
.

The hook partition
\Gamma_{n,m}=\left(n,1^{m}\right)
commutes with any partition
Q
of
m+n
having one of the following forms:

(a)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n]^{k}\right)
, where
\mu
is an arbitrary partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
.

(b)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-1]^{k}\right)
, where
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
is a partition of
m+1
, and
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{1}}\leq k\leq n-1
.

(c)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-2]^{k}\right)
, where either
\mu=(m+2)
and
\frac{n-1}{m+1}<k\leq n-2
, or
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})
is a partition of
m+2
such that
\mu_{2}\geq 2
, and
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{2}}<k\leq n-2
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we use the notations established in (
1
) and (
2
) above.

Part (a).
Let
\mu
be a partition of
m
, and assume that
1\leq k\leq n
. Let
Q
be the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n]^{k}\right)
. In
\mathcal{U}_{B}
, we get Jordan type
Q
by choosing
H=J_{n}^{k}
,
V=J_{\mu}^{T}
, and
u=d=0
in (
2
) above.

Part (b).
Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})
be a partition of
m+1
, and assume that
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{1}}\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
Q
be the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-1]^{k}\right)
.

If
\mu=\left(1^{m+1}\right)
, then
Q
is the partition
\left(1^{m+n}\right)
, which corresponds to the zero matrix in
\mathcal{U}_{B}
. For the rest of the proof of this part we assume that
2\leq\mu_{1}\leq m+1
.

Let
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}-1,\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})
. Using the expression (
2
) for an element
A\in\mathcal{U}_{B}
, set
H=J_{n}^{k}
,
V=J_{\mu^{\prime}}^{T}
,
u=\delta e^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}-1}
and
d=e_{1}^{\prime}
, where
\{e_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,e^{\prime}_{m}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}^{m}
, and
\delta\in\mathbb{F}
. We reorganize the block decomposition of
A
to get

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n}^{k}&\delta e_{1}\left(e^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}-1}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr e_{1}^{\prime}e_{n}^{T}&J_{\mu^{\prime}}^{T}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-1}^{k}&f_{n-k}\left(f^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{T}+\delta f_{1}\left(f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr 0&J_{\mu}^{T}\end{array}\right),

where
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\}
and
\{f^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{m+1}\}
are the standard bases of
\mathbb{F}^{n-1}
and
\mathbb{F}^{m+1}
, respectively.

Thus for a positive integer
l
, we have

A^{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-1}^{kl}&J_{n-1}^{k(l-1)}f_{n-k}(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}+\delta f_{1}\left(f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}}\right)^{T}(J_{\mu}^{l-1})^{T}\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right).

Case 1.
Assume that
\frac{n}{\mu_{1}}\leq k
. In this case, let
\delta=0
.

For
1\leq l<\frac{n}{k}
, since
l<\mu_{1}
, the block
\left(J^{T}_{\mu}\right)^{l}
is non-zero and has a row equal to
(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}
. Therefore, for such
l
, we have

\rank(A^{l})=\rank\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-1}^{kl}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right).

On the other hand, for
l\geq\frac{n}{k}
we have

A^{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right),

Therefore, in this case
A
has the desired Jordan type.

Case 2.
Now assume that
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{1}}\leq k<\frac{n}{\mu_{1}}
. This is possible only if
n-1
is divisible by
\mu_{1}
and so
n-1=k\mu_{1}
. Let
\delta=-1
.

For
1\leq l\leq\mu_{1}-1
, we have
kl<n-1
, so that the block
J_{n-1}^{kl}
has a column equal to
f_{1}
and the block
\left(J^{T}_{\mu}\right)^{l}
has a row equal to
(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}
. Thus, for such
l
, we have

\rank(A^{l})=\rank\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-1}^{kl}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right).

For
l=\mu_{1}
, we have

A^{\mu_{1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&f_{1}(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}-f_{1}\left(f^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr 0&0\end{array}\right)=0=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-1}^{k\mu_{1}}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right),

and hence
A^{l}=0
also for
l>\mu_{1}
.

Therefore, in this case
A
has the desired Jordan type as well.

Part (c).
Assume that
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})
is a partition of
m+2
such that either
\mu_{2}=0
or
\mu_{2}\geq 2
.

If
\mu_{2}=0
, let
\mu^{\prime}=(m)
, and let
k
be such that
\frac{n-1}{m+1}<k\leq n-2
. If
\mu_{2}\geq 2
, let
\mu^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}-1,\mu_{2}-1,\mu_{3},\ldots,\mu_{s})
and
k
be such that
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{2}}<k\leq n-2
.

We first note that if
\mu_{1}\leq 2
, then in fact
\mu_{1}=2
and all parts of the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-2]^{k}\right)
are at most 2. We already know that such partition commutes with any partition (see
(
Obl-3
, Thm. 2.4)
). So for the rest of this proof we assume that
\mu_{1}\geq 3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{U}_{B}
, written as in (
2
), be such that
H=J_{n}^{k}
,
V=\left(J_{\mu^{\prime}}\right)^{T}
,
u=e^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-2}
, and
d=e_{1}^{\prime}
.
In other words,

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n}^{k}&e_{1}\left(e^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-2}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr e_{1}^{\prime}e_{n}^{T}&J_{\mu^{\prime}}^{T}\end{array}\right).

In the case
\mu=(m+2)
, for simplicity, we also write
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
to mean
m+2
.

Let
\bar{A}=P^{-1}AP
where
P
is the base change matrix permuting the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}^{n+m}
by cycle
(1\to 2\,\to\ldots\to n+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-2)
and leaves other vectors fixed (if any). We can then reorganize the block decomposition of
\bar{A}
to write

\bar{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-2}^{k}&f_{n-k-1}\left(f^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}f_{k}^{T}&J_{\mu}^{T}\end{array}\right).

Here,
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-2}\}
and
\{f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{m+2}\}
are the standard bases of
\mathbb{F}^{n-2}
and
\mathbb{F}^{m+2}
, respectively.

Using the notation
\delta_{i,j}
to denote the Kronecker delta, for a positive integer
l
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{A}^{l}&=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-2}^{kl}&J_{n-2}^{k(l-1)}f_{n-k-1}\left(f^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}f_{k}^{T}J_{n-2}^{k(l-1)}&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}+\delta_{kl,n-1}f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}\end{array}\right)\\
\\
&=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-2}^{kl}&f_{n-kl-1}\left(f^{\prime}_{1}\right)^{T}\\
\hline\cr f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}f_{kl}^{T}&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}+\delta_{kl,n-1}f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}\end{array}\right).\end{array}

We will show that
A
has the desired Jordan type by showing that for every positive integer
l
,

\rank\bar{A}^{l}=\rank\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n-2}^{kl}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}\end{array}\right).

Case I.
Assume that
\mu_{2}\geq 2
. If
l
is such that
1\leq l<\mu_{2}
, then the
(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-l)
-th column of
\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}
is equal to
f^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}
. Therefore, for such
l
, we can use an elementary column operation to remove the non-zero entry in the block
\bar{A}^{l}_{21}
, which will be in row
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
and column
kl
of the block, as well as the extra term in the block
\bar{A}^{l}_{22}
, which will be in row
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
and column
1
of the block. Moreover, the
(l+1)
-st row of
\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}
is equal to
(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}
and therefore a nonzero entry in the block
\bar{A}^{l}_{12}
, which will be in row
n-kl-1
and column
1
of that block, can be eliminated using an elementary row operation.

On the other hand, for
l\geq\mu_{2}
, since
kl>n-1
, both blocks
\bar{A}^{l}_{12}
and
\bar{A}^{l}_{21}
, as well as the extra term in the block
\bar{A}^{l}_{22}
, are 0.

Case II.
Now, assume that
\mu=(m+2)
and that
\frac{n-1}{m+1}<k\leq n-2
. We note that in this case, as long as
l\leq m
, the
(l+1)
-st row of
\left(J^{l}_{\mu}\right)^{T}
is
(f^{\prime}_{1})^{T}
, and its
(m+2-l)
-th column is
f^{\prime}_{m+2}
. Since
l+1\leq m+1
and
m+2-l\geq 2
, a nonzero entry in
\bar{A}^{l}_{12}
or
\bar{A}^{l}_{21}
, or an additional term in
\bar{A}^{l}_{22}
can be eliminated through appropriate elementary column and row operations.

On the other hand, for
l\geq m+1
, we have
lk>n-1
and therefore both
\bar{A}^{l}_{12}
and
\bar{A}^{l}_{21}
, as well as the extra term in
\bar{A}^{l}_{22}
are 0.

Therefore, in both cases, for every positive integer
l
, we have the desired rank conditions, implying that
A
has Jordan type
([n-2]^{k},\mu)
.

Remark 2
.

In part (c) of Lemma (
1
), we made the lower bounds for
k
sharp, but partitions that come from the inclusive lower bounds for
k
are also among the commuting partitions covered in earlier parts of Lemma
1
. If
\mu=(m+2)
and
k=\frac{n-1}{m+1}
, then
([n-2]^{k},m+2)=(m,(m+1)^{k-1},m+2)=([n]^{k},m)
, which is already covered in Part (a). On the other hand, if
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})

Theorem 3
.

Partition
\Gamma_{n,m}=\left(n,1^{m}\right)
commutes with a partition
Q
of
m+n
if and only if
Q
is one of the following partitions:

(a)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n]^{k}\right)
, where
\mu
is an arbitrary partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
.

(b)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-1]^{k}\right)
, where
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
is a partition of
m+1
, and
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{1}}\leq k\leq n-1
.

(c)

Q
is the partition obtained from
\left(\mu,[n-2]^{k}\right)
, where either
\mu=(m+2)
and
\frac{n-1}{m+1}<k\leq n-2
, or
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{s})
is a partition of
m+2
such that
\mu_{2}\geq 2
, and
\frac{n-1}{\mu_{2}}<k\leq n-2
.

Proof.

A generic matrix
A
in
\mathcal{U}_{B}
as in (
1
) is written in the block form as in (
2
). We know that
H
is similar to
J_{n}^{k}
for some power
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
V
is similar to a nilpotent Jordan matrix with Jordan type
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{t})
. Then we have
H=S^{-1}J_{n}^{k}S
and
V=T^{-1}J_{\nu}T
for two invertible matrices
S
and
T
. Since
H
is strictly upper-triangular matrix we may choose
S
such that
Se_{1}=\alpha e_{1}
and
S^{T}e_{n}=\beta e_{n}
for two nonzero scalars
\alpha
and
\beta
. Then, we have

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}S^{-1}J_{n}^{k}S&e_{1}u^{T}\\
\hline\cr de_{n}^{T}&T^{-1}J_{\nu}T\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}S^{-1}&0\\
\hline\cr 0&T^{-1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n}^{k}&e_{1}a^{T}\\
\hline\cr be_{n}^{T}&J_{\nu}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}S&0\\
\hline\cr 0&T\end{array}\right),

where we write
u=\alpha^{-1}T^{T}a
and
d=\beta T^{-1}b
.
Thus
A
is similar to

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n}^{k}&e_{1}a^{T}\\
\hline\cr be_{n}^{T}&J_{\nu}\end{array}\right)

and we will consider
B
hereafter.

In order to find the Jordan type of
B
we will compute its powers. Inductively, we prove that

B^{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}J_{n}^{kl}+\left(a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-2}b\right)J_{n}^{n-1}&e_{1}a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-1}\\
\hline\cr J_{\nu}^{l-1}be^{T}_{n}&J_{\nu}^{l}\end{array}\right)

for any
l\geq 2
. As long as
kl\leq n-2
, the off diagonal blocks do not affect the rank of
B^{l}
and we have
\rank(B^{l})=\rank(J_{n}^{kl})+\rank(J_{\nu}^{l})
.

Consider next the case
kl=n-1
. We omit the first
n-1
consecutive columns and
n-1
consecutive rows, starting with the second row, in
B^{l}
that are all zero to obtain

(3)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}1+\left(a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-2}b\right)&a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-1}\\
\hline\cr J_{\nu}^{l-1}b&J_{\nu}^{l}\end{array}\right).

We consider several cases depending on the largest part
\nu_{1}
of partition
\nu
.

Case I.

\nu_{1}\leq l-2
.

In this case we have
J_{\nu}^{l-2}=0
, and thus
\rank B^{l}=1
. We also have
\rank B^{s}=0
for all
s>l
. The Jordan type of
B
is equal to

\left(\nu,[n]^{k}\right)
where
\nu
is a partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
. So it belongs to the case (a) of Lemma
1
.

Case II.

\nu_{1}=l-1.

Now, we have
J_{\nu}^{l-1}=J_{\nu}^{l}=0
. So,
\rank B^{l}
is either
0
or
1
, depending on
1+a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-2}b
being equal to
0
or not, respectively. Suppose first that
\rank B^{l}=1
. Then, it follows that
\rank B^{s}=0
for all
s>l
. Hence
\rank(B^{l})=\rank(J_{n}^{l})+\rank(J_{\nu}^{l})
for all
l
and the Jordan type of
B
is equal to

\left(\nu,[n]^{k}\right)
where
\nu
is a partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
. The Jordan type of
A
in this subcase belongs to the case (a) of Lemma
1
.

Suppose next that
\rank B^{l}=0
. Since
\nu_{1}=l-1
and
kl=n-1
it follows that the Jordan type of
B
is equal to
\left(\nu^{\prime},[n-1]^{k}\right)
, where
\nu^{\prime}=(\nu_{1}+1,\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\ldots,\nu_{t})=(l,\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{t})
and
[n-1]^{k}=(l^{k})
. Since
\nu_{1}+1=l=\frac{n-1}{k}
, it follows that the Jordan type in this case belongs to type (b) of Lemma
1
.

Case III.

\nu_{1}\geq l.

Now the rank of
B^{l}
can be equal to one of
3
possible values:
\rank J_{\nu}^{l}
,
\rank J_{\nu}^{l}+1
or
\rank J_{\nu}^{l}+2
, depending on
a
and
b
. We consider the three cases separately.

(i)

\rank B^{l}-\rank J_{\nu}^{l}=0
: Then
1+a^{T}J_{\nu}^{l-2}b=0
,
J_{\nu}^{l-1}b\in\im J_{\nu}^{l}
and
\left(J_{\nu}^{l-1}\right)^{T}a\in\im\left(J_{\nu}^{l}\right)^{T}
. We have
\rank B^{j}=n-jk+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,l-1
, and
\rank B^{l}=\rank J_{\nu}^{l}
. Since
n=kl+1
it follows that the Jordan type of
B
is equal to
([n-1]^{k},\nu^{\prime})
, where
\nu^{\prime}
is a partition of
m+1
and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}\geq\nu_{1}\geq l=\frac{n-1}{k}
.

So the Jordan type of
B
belongs to type (b) of Lemma
1
.

(ii)

\rank B^{l}-\rank J_{\nu}^{l}=1
: Then
\rank B^{j}=\rank J_{n}^{kj}+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
for all
j\leq l
. Since
J_{n}^{kl+1}=J_{n}^{n}=0
, the equality
\rank B^{j}=\rank J_{n}^{kj}+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
holds also for all
j>l
. So, the Jordan type of
B
is equal to
\left(\nu^{\prime},[n]^{k}\right)
where
\nu^{\prime}
is a partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
. It belongs to the case (a) of Lemma
1
.

(iii)

\rank B^{l}-\rank J_{\nu}^{l}=2
: Then
\rank B^{j}=\rank J_{n}^{kj}+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
for all
j\leq l-1
and
\rank B^{l}=\rank J_{\nu}^{l}+2=\rank J_{n}^{n-1}+\rank J_{\nu}^{l}+1
. Therefore, the Jordan type of
B
is equal to
([n-1]^{k},\nu^{\prime})
, where
\nu^{\prime}=(\nu^{\prime}_{1},\nu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\nu^{\prime}_{t})
is a partition of
m+1
and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}\geq\nu_{1}+1\geq l+1\geq\frac{n-1}{k}
. It belongs to the case (b) of Lemma
1
.

It remains to consider the case when there is no
l
such that
kl=n-1
, i.e., we assume that the remainder of
n
when divided by
k
is not equal to
1
. We write
\ell=\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{k}}\right\rfloor
. Then

\rank B^{j}=\rank J_{n}^{kj}+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
for all
j\leq\ell
.

(i)

If
\nu_{1}\leq\ell
then
B^{j}=0
for

and the Jordan type of

is equal to
\left([n]^{k},\nu\right)
where
\nu
is a partition of
m
and

. It belongs to the case (a) of Lemma
1
.

(ii)

Assume next that
\nu_{1}\geq\ell+1
, and that

,
J_{\nu}^{\ell}b\in\im J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}
and
\left(J_{\nu}^{\ell}\right)^{T}a\in\im J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}
. Then, we have
\rank B^{j}=\rank J_{n}^{kj}+\rank J_{\nu}^{j}
for all

and the Jordan type of
B
is equal to
\left([n]^{k},\nu\right)
where
\nu
is a partition of
m
and
1\leq k\leq n
. It belongs to the case (a) of Lemma
1
.

(iii)

Assume next that
\nu_{1}\geq\ell+1
, and that

, but
J_{\nu}^{\ell}b\in\im J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}
and
\left(J_{\nu}^{\ell}\right)^{T}a\in\im J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}

for all

\rank B^{\ell+1}=\rank J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}+1

([n-1]^{k},\nu^{\prime})

\nu^{\prime}=(\nu^{\prime}_{1},\nu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\nu^{\prime}_{t})
is a partition of

and
\nu_{1}^{\prime}\geq\nu_{1}\geq\ell+1\geq\frac{n-1}{k}

.

(iv)

\left(J_{\nu}^{\ell}\right)^{T}a\in\im J_{\nu}^{\ell+1}

([n-1]^{k},\nu^{\prime})

\nu^{\prime}=(\nu^{\prime}_{1},\nu^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\nu^{\prime}_{t})

(v)

Corollary 5

The following example shows that partitions with the same generic commuting Jordan type do not necessarily commute themselves. In other words, there are noncommuting partitions

and

\mathcal{D}(\Gamma)=\mathcal{D}(P)
