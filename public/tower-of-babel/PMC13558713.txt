Metaheuristic-driven LSTM framework for advanced cyberthreat detection and response using a hybrid firefly–whale–grey wolf optimization pipeline
Authors: Sreekanth Rallapalli, Weiwei Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Conventional intrusion detection systems (IDSs) based on static, signature-based rules are becoming insufficient in response to more advanced, dynamic cyber threats like advanced persistent threats (APTs), zero-day exploits and polymorphic malware The paper presents a new three-step hybrid optimization model based on the combination of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, which is a gated form of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) specifically developed to learn sequential patterns, with three complementary bio-inspired metaheuristic algorithms, each contributing to the specific role of optimization. Firefly algorithm (FA) is used to select the features subset and decrease the input dimensionality, whale optimization algorithm (WOA) is used to optimize LSTM parameters such as hidden units, learning rate, dropout rate, and batch size, and grey wolf optimizer (GWO) to perform weight optimization at the ensemble level to improve the robustness of the classification. The framework has been tested on the NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017 benchmarks on a stringent protocol of 30 independent runs. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test (p < 0.001) and paired t-test (p < 0.001) prove statistical significance. The hybrid model is 98.62% (standard deviation 0.21) on NSL-KDD and 99.1% (standard deviation 0.18) on CICIDS2017 with a false positive rate (FPR) of 1.2 and an area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC) of 0.992. The suggested framework has a high potential of being used as the basis of detecting near-real-time cyberthreats, though it will require additional validation on real-life network settings.

Introduction

The contemporary digital environment is marked by a continuous growing interconnection of critical infrastructure, cloud services, and devices of the Internet of Things (IoT), which increase the attack surface available to malicious actors. The degree of cyber threats has become significantly more advanced, prevalent, and extensive: the cost of cybercrime worldwide is expected to reach USD 10.5 trillion per year by 20251. Routine examples of threats that do not fall under the traditional security controls are advanced persistent threats (APTs), zero-day vulnerabilities, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) campaigns, and polymorphic malware. The classic intrusion detection systems (IDSs) which are mainly rule based or signature driven in nature are inherently reactive: they can only detect attack patterns which are listed in the catalog and cannot handle new or obfuscated attack vectors. This is a critical weakness in the contemporary cybersecurity infrastructure and it requires more adaptive proactive solutions.

The artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning (DL) have become revolutionary technologies in proactive threat detection. The deep neural architecture is capable of learning hierarchical features representations that are directly based on the network traffic data, which allows detecting new patterns that have never been previously seen. Gated versions of a recurrent neural network (RNN) also known as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks which solve the issue of the vanishing gradient are highly suitable in modeling the sequential character of network traffic flows. Autoencoders are a complement to the LSTM architectures where the compact structural representation is learned to detect anomalies. These benefits notwithstanding, deep learning models are highly vulnerable to architectural decisions and hyperparameter settings, and their performance has to be carefully fine-tuned to be able to generalize to a variety of threat settings.

Bio-inspired metaheuristic optimization algorithms provide a conceptual method to the hyperparameter tuning and feature selection difficulties of deep learning-based IDS. Firefly algorithm (FA), whale optimization algorithm (WOA) and grey wolf optimizer (GWO) emulate the collective behaviours of nature to search non-convex, multidimensional search spaces. Nevertheless, the current literature indicates that there is a research gap: the existing studies are largely based on the application of a single metaheuristic optimizer (e.g., GWO-tuned LSTM2, WOA-tuned LSTM3, or FA-guided feature selection4 without taking the complementary benefits of multiple optimizers into account and using them in an integrated pipeline. In particular: (1) single-optimizer models tend to converge prematurely and lack search diversity; (2) the sequential combination of FA (discrete combinatorial searching feature selection), WOA (continuous spiral-based search hyperparameter tuning), and GWO (hierarchical rank-based ensemble optimization) has not been systematically studied in the literature as a three-stage pipeline; and (3) statistically validated repetitions of experiments have not been done on most studies of IDS metaheuristics.

The paper presents a new three-phase hybrid metahybrid-optimized LSTM model of enhanced cyberthreat detection. The framework allocates each optimizer a different, non-overlapping task according to its natural strengths: FA is feature subset selection in a discrete search space; WOA is LSTM hyperparameter optimization in a continuous search space using its spiral bubble-net dynamics; and GWO is ensemble-level weight optimization using its 3-level 3-leaders hierarchical leadership model. It is this architectural division of concerns and the addition of a multi-objective fitness function to combine accuracy, F1-score, and FPR which make up the fundamental innovation of the proposed approach.

Literature review

Deep learning architectures for intrusion detection

Intrusion detection has been utilizing deep neural architectures to learn more intricate time and structure variations of network traffic. Singh and Jang-Jaccard5 suggested the idea of a multi-scale CNN-LSTM autoencoder that learns spatio-temporal dependencies in an unsupervised approach, which has proven to be more effective on NSL-KDD and UNSW-NB15 databases. Al-Kahtani et al.6 improved the performance of the IDS in wireless sensor networks by combining gated recurrent units (GRUs) with LSTM networks and showed their effectiveness in a variety of attack types. Hussein et al.7 demonstrated that LSTM networks with SMOTE-based rebalancing demonstrated a considerable enhancement in intrusion detection performance on both CICIDs2017 and NSL-KDD, especially on unrepresented attack classes. The LSTM autoencoders and stacked bi-directional models of LSTM designed by Kumar et al.8 were used to identify IoT intrusions and performed better on UNSW-NB15 and Bot-IoT datasets than classical machine learning baselines. Gueriani et al.9 made a CNN-LSTM hybrid architecture tested on the current CICIoT2023 dataset with 98.42% accuracy, proving the practicability of the deep hybrid structures in the modern IoT threat context.

Metaheuristic-optimized IDS frameworks

Bio-inspired metaheuristic algorithms and deep learning of IDS have been actively studied. Elsaid et al.10 introduced the optimized grey wolf-based deep learning hybrid (LSTM-GRU) IDS that has a higher detection accuracy. In a CNN-LSTM IDS, Aljanabi and Kumaran11 used the grey wolf optimizer to select features, and they achieved better precision and recall. Abdel-Husseini et al.12 used whale optimization algorithm to optimize LSTM classifiers hyperparameters, and the highest accuracy was 99.6% on NSL-KDD. Khan et al.13 came up with an explainable LSTM-based IDS, which is optimized using the firefly algorithm in an IoT environment, where LIME and SHAP are used to provide model transparency and a model with an accuracy of 98.42. In network traffic, Kumar and Sinha14 used a firefly algorithm-adjusted LSTM to identify anomalies, showing it to be superior in NSL-KDD and UNSW-NB15. Vijayanand et al.15 presented a wrapper feature selection method of IDS, which uses a whale optimization algorithm. Mojtahedi et al.16 showed that whale optimization algorithm that is used in conjunction with a genetic algorithm and KNN outperforms in feature selection to IDS on KDD Cup 1999. Kunang et al.17 introduced whale and remora-inspired algorithms to a CNN-based IDS with a better detection rate and reduced false positives. Sharma and Tripathi18 suggested an enhanced ensemble model with swarm intelligence that employs a metaheuristic, demonstrating an improvement in using a combination of traditional and deep learning classifiers.

Recent advances in metaheuristic-tuned ML/DL for cybersecurity (2023–2025)

A number of literature published in recent times also enlighten the development of the proposed framework. According to Mabrouk et al.19, hybrid CNN-XGBoost IDS fine-tuned using a modified sine cosine algorithm have proven the effectiveness of metaheuristic tuning on boosted tree classifiers on cloud security datasets. A two-level IoT/metaverse intrusion detection framework that was suggested by Dahou et al.20 and applies metaheuristic optimization to independently tune feature selection and classification phases - a design rationale that is similarly close to the separation of stages offered herein. A survey of metaheuristic optimization was conducted by Slimani et al.21 on the topic of IoT/IIoT intrusion detection, with feature selection and hyperparameter optimization being the most significant optimization objectives. Ghosh et al.22 used a CatBoost classifier that was optimized using an adapted reptile search algorithm of IDS and performed competitively on CICIDS2017. Using boosting-based smart city IDS which was optimized by metaheuristics Ibrahim et al.23 suggested that ensemble techniques could be improved by optimizer-coordinated weight coordination, which is of direct interest to the GWO ensemble optimization step in the suggested framework. Mabrook et al.24 used NLP-based insider threat detection as an implementation of the metaheuristic optimization to NLP-based optimization of network-flow data, which is not limited to network-flow data.

Other supporting works are: Growth optimizer and whale optimization algorithm were integrated in deep learning IDS by Rajasekhar and Ghosh25 when using cloud/iot datasets; CNN-based IDS were applied by Rani and Das26 using hybrid filter techniques; Arjunan and Suresh27 developed a variational autoencoder-cnn hybrid IDS; and Jain et al.28 created a whale optimization algorithm-CNN solution to malware classification. Taken together, these publications both affirm the topicality of the offered framework and point out the particular gap that remains unexploited: no previous study has implemented FA, WOA, and GWO in a pipeline separated by stages with complete statistical validation of all of the independent runs.

Recent advancements in ensemble-based intrusion detection for cyber-physical systems further highlight the importance of coordinated optimization strategies. Ji et al.29 proposed cascading ensemble techniques for IDS, while Ji et al.30 provided a comprehensive review of intrusion detection in cyber-physical systems. Hybrid ensemble optimization approaches such as CNN-GWO-voting models31 and feature selection frameworks32 demonstrate improved detection performance. Furthermore, meta-learning-based ensemble strategies33 and quantum-inspired secure frameworks34 indicate the growing importance of adaptive and hybrid optimization techniques in modern cybersecurity environments.

Research objectives

The main aim of the proposed study is to develop, deploy and strictly test a new 3-phase hybrid metaheuristic-optimized LSTM model of high-level cyberthreat detection. The particular aims are: (1) to design a stage-separated pipeline with FA, WOA, and GWO solving a different optimization sub-problem according to their natural computational capabilities; (2) to evaluate the detection performance of the framework on NSL-KDD and CICIoT2017 in terms of accuracy, macro-averaged F1-score, Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), per-class precision and recall, FPR, and AUC-ROC; (3) to test statistical significance testing across 30 independent runs so that performance improvement.

The proposed framework can be distinguished by three key aspects of the previous works (GWO-LSTM10, WOA-LSTM12, FA-LSTM13: (1) role specialization - FA is used to trial combinatorial feature selection and WOA is used to trial continuous hyperparameter optimization and GWO is used to trial ensemble weight coordination - has not been studied in the previous literature as an integrated three-stage pipeline; (2) the multi-objective fitness function that is used to optimize accuracy, F1-score, and FPR simultaneously offers.

Research datasets

The proposed framework is tested on two benchmark datasets NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017. The primary benchmark is NSL-KDD which is necessary to allow making a direct comparison with the enormous amount of previous literature on IDS. The more recent categories of attacks are evaluated at CICIDS2017. It is clearly admitted that NSL-KDD is based on network captures of 19,981,999, thus, it does not represent current encrypted traffic, zero-day activities, or IoT-specific attack patterns. Although more recent, CICIDS2017 is pre-2020 in terms of cyberthreats. Future confirmation on CICIoT20239- including modern patterns of IoT attacks such as MQTT-based attacks and advanced reconnaissance - is quoted as a high-priority extension of this work.

NSL-KDD dataset

NSL-KDD is an improved version of KDD Cup 1999, which is used to overcome the problems of duplicate records and class imbalance35. It has 41 items (38 numerical, 3 categorical) that are network connection characteristics, denoted by the term normal and in the four attack types: Denial of Service (DoS), Probe, Remote-to-Local (R2L), and User-to-Root (U2R). The training (KADDTrain +) has 125,973 instances and the test set (KADDTest +) has 22,544 instances. The dataset has issues of class imbalance especially with U2R (< 0.1% of training instances) which is resolved by using SMOTE-based oversampling as part of the preprocessing pipeline (Fig. 1, Fig. 2 and Fig. 3).

CICIDS2017 dataset

The dataset of CICIDS201736 includes records of network flows during a period of 5 days of monitored traffic which consists of 79 features of each flow obtained with the help of CICFlowMeter, and which have labels Benign traffic and 14 attack categories. The flow-based design of the dataset and the presence of real timestamps allow constructing sequences to train LSTM more principled compared to NSL-KDD. It also has a substantially different feature space, as well as an attack distribution, which makes it useful in assessing cross-dataset generalization.

Methodology

Data preprocessing — NSL-KDD

The preprocessing pipeline for NSL-KDD comprises: (1) Data sanitization: removal of duplicate records, imputation of missing values, and label consistency verification. (2) Categorical encoding: the three categorical features (protocol_type, service, flag) are one-hot encoded. (3) Label mapping: normal traffic → 0; attack traffic → {DoS, Probe, R2L, U2R} for multi-class classification. (4) Normalization: Min-Max scaling to [0,1] applied to all numerical features using training-set statistics only.

Data preprocessing — CICIDS2017

CICIDS2017 preprocessing includes: (1) flow-based feature aggregation grouping packets with matching source-destination IP-port tuples; (2) timestamp processing converting raw timestamps to inter-arrival delta features; (3) attack label generalization mapping fine-grained labels (e.g., DoS-Slowloris) to coarse categories (DoS, Brute Force, DDoS); and (4) SMOTE-based oversampling to address class imbalance for underrepresented categories such as Heartbleed and Infiltration.

Sequence construction for LSTM input

The LSTM networks demand sequential input in the format (samples, timesteps, features). NSL-KDD is not sequential and is tabular in nature, which requires synthesized sequences. KDDTrain+ records are indexed by their original row ID which is a rough approximation of time-ordering since the data were compiled by recording network captures in time. It uses a sliding window of size T = 10 and a stride S = 1, which generates overlapping sequences, S i = {x i, x i + 1, x i + 2, and so on}, in which x j is the preprocessed feature vector of the i th record. The label of sequence S i is the label of the last record x i + T -1. This will give N records as N -T + 1 sequences. It is clearly stated that this method presupposes that there is local temporal locality among the records at nearby location - a flawed assumption when using tabular data. In order to evaluate the value of temporal modeling by LSTM, non-sequential baselines (Random Forest, SVM) that are trained on the same feature subset that FA selected are also presented in comparative assessment. In the case of CICIDS2017, the pseudo time per-flow timestamps allow constructing chronological sequences, and the method is more principled in the case of this dataset.

Class imbalance handling

SMOTE generates synthetic minority-class samples by interpolating between existing minority instances in feature space. Class weights inversely proportional to class frequency are additionally applied in the LSTM loss function to further compensate for residual imbalance, particularly for U2R instances (Fig. 4 and Table 1).

Results

The presented FA + WOA+GWO+LSTM model shows repeatedly better results on NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017 data sets, confirmed by 30 experimental runs and confirmed by stringent statistical analysis. On NSL-KDD, the model has mean accuracy 98.62% + 0.21% - statistically significant + 4.42% better than standalone LSTM (94.2% + 0.47; W = 428, p < 0.001; paired t(29) = 18.63, p < 0.001). The FPR has been minimized to 1.2% +0.16, which is 4.1% points lower than standalone LSTM, which is essential in reducing alert fatigue in the Security Operations Centers (SOCs). On the CNN-LSTM baseline (0.965), the AUC-ROC of 0.992 is significantly lower, which proves the discriminative power. The Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC = 0.971) ensures that the balanced performance is strong in all classes even the minority attacks types.

Super-additive synergy is confirmed by the fact that each of the optimizer stages adds incrementally: FA alone (+ 1.6%), WOA alone (+ 2.5%), GWO alone (+ 2.2%), the three stages of the pipeline altogether (+ 4.4%), and it is super-additive. It is noteworthy that non-sequential baselines (RF: 89.7% SVM: 85.4) with the same subset of features selected by FA prove that the temporal modeling of LSTM does add real value to feature quality by itself.

DoS and Probe have a high detection rate (99.2 and 98.5 respectively) with attack-type analysis. U2R detection stands at 93.7% - worse since the difference in classes is extreme. To some extent, SMOTE-based oversampling addresses this; cost-sensitive learning is a priority in the future. In CICIDS2017, the model gets an accuracy of 99.1% with a ± error of 0.18% which proves the use of reasonable cross-dataset generalization.

With respect to computing properties: 1,850 s training in the given hardware (Intel i7-11800 H, RTX 3060) is a one-time offline expense. The time of the inference is invariably lower than 50 ms per batch on the same machine. It is clearly mentioned that this inference time is platform specific and might vary on resource constrained or embedded deployment platforms. Benchmarking on live-traffic throughput and memory profiling must go through before responsibly making a production deployment claim Fig. 7 and Fig. 8.

Discussion

The given FA + WOA+GWO+LSTM framework proves that the results of the synergistic combination of three independent complementary metaheuristic optimizers in a stage-separated pipeline are statistically significant and their effect is superior to that of individual-optimizer models as well as to traditional deep learning benchmarks. The ablation experiment (Table 4) makes sure that there are non-redundant, distinct contributions of every optimization phase. The fact that the full pipeline (super-additive 4.4- vs. single-optimizer 2.5-performance) operates better than the strong single-optimizer performance, is evidence that the synergy is real rather than due to combination of redundant processes.

The difference between the FPR of 5.3 to 1.2 is operationally important. In a network with a high throughput of 10,000 connections per second, a 4.1% FPR decrease would mean 410 false alarms/sec, which is a significant enhancement to the efficiency of SOC operation. This improvement is statistically sound as shown by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (p < 0.001) and the paired t-test (p < 0.001) in 30 independent runs.

The reduced U2R detection rates (93.7) are evidence of the inherent difficulty in extreme class imbalance, and are in line with the rest of the IDS literature7,13, and prompts further research on cost-sensitive learning. The cross-dataset CICIDS2017 (99.1) results indicate that generalization is reasonable, however, the distribution shift between datasets should be interpreted with care.

The 1,850 s training time is significantly greater than conventional baselines. This is a cost that is incurred once in the offline optimization stage. The latency of inference, of less than 50 ms (measured on Intel i7-11800 H/RTX 3060) is also consistent with near-real-time to classify data in the laboratories. Nevertheless, according to the requirements of the responsible deployment claims, production environment validation must be provided.

Conclusion

The paper has suggested a new three-step hybrid metaheuristic-optimized LSTM architecture to detect advanced cyberthreats, with the firefly algorithm (FA) to select the best feature subset, the whale optimization algorithm (WOA) to optimize LSTM hyperparameters, and the grey wolf optimizer (GWO) to optimize the weight of the ensemble in a stage-based pipeline. The main results are the following: (1) the full FA + WOA+GWO+LSTM pipeline yields 98.62% ± 0.21% mean accuracy on NSL-KDD, and 99.1% ± 0.18% mean accuracy on CICIDS2017, and is statistically significantly greater than all of the baselines using Wilcoxon signed-rank and paired t-tests (p < 0.001) across 30 independent runs; (2) all of the optimizer components have a statistically significant Weaknesses such as age of benchmark dataset, assumptions on synthetic sequence construction, high cost of training and lack of live-traffic testing is explicitly recognized. These will be covered in the future by CICIoT2023 assessment, adversarial robustness, and explainability method combination implementation to deploy production SOC.

Future work

Potential extensions of the proposed framework are: (1) testing on CICIoT2023 and CIC-Bell-DNS-2021 datasets to test its performance against current attack patterns; (2) using Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO) or Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to explore the optimization space further; (3) provide explainability with SHAP and LIME to interpret the SOC alerts; (4) edge deployment optimization with model compression and quantization to support IoT/5G edge deployments; (5) adversarial robust.
