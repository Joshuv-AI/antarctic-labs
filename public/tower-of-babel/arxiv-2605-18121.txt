Perturbation Theory of the Free Energy via the Mesoscopic Combined Partition Function
Bob Osano

Abstract
We develop a systematic perturbation theory for the Helmholtz free energy of a classical N N -body system within the mesoscopic framework of [ 1 , 2 ] . The combined coarse-graining operator 𝒞 = 𝒞 x ∘ 𝒞 p \mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{x}\circ\mathcal{C}_{p} acting on single-particle phase space partitions it into product cells C i , α = V i × Π α C_{i,\alpha}=V_{i}\times\Pi_{\alpha} and generates a mesoscopic partition function 𝒵 meso ​ ( λ ) \mathcal{Z}_{\rm meso}(\lambda) whose reference level factorises by the multinomial theorem: 𝒵 meso ( 0 ) = ( Z 1 ( 0 ) ) N \mathcal{Z}_{\rm meso}^{(0)}=(Z_{1}^{(0)})^{N} . Perturbation theory for ℱ meso ​ ( λ ) = − k B ​ T ​ ln ⁡ 𝒵 meso ​ ( λ ) \mathcal{F}_{\rm meso}(\lambda)=-k_{B}T\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\rm meso}(\lambda) in the inter-cell perturbation 𝒱 meso \mathcal{V}_{\rm meso} yields the mesoscopic Gibbs–Bogoliubov inequality and an exact coupling-parameter integration formula. The full free energy satisfies F ( λ ) = ℱ meso ( λ ) − k B T ∑ i < j I ( i , j ; λ ) + O ( | Λ | ℓ − d e − 2 ℓ / ξ ) , F(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\rm meso}(\lambda)-k_{B}T\!\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O\!\left(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-2\ell/\xi}\right), where the inter-cell mutual informations I ⁡ ( i , j , λ ) I(i,j;\lambda) are the corrections identified in the extensivity analysis. The first-order theory recovers the van der Waals equation and the Barker–Henderson result; the second-order term converges to the structure-factor formula in the fine-cell limit. For long-range interactions, factorisation fails and the mutual-information corrections quantify the resulting non-extensivity.

Introduction

The Helmholtz free energy
F
is the fundamental object of classical equilibrium statistical mechanics: all thermodynamic quantities follow from it by differentiation.
Computing
F
from first principles for a realistic interacting system requires either
an exact solution (rare) or a controlled approximation. Perturbation theory, originating
with Zwanzig
[
3
]
and Barker–Henderson
[
4
]
, expands

F
in powers of a coupling parameter
\lambda
about a soluble reference
F_{0}
, yielding
a cumulant series whose coefficients are equilibrium averages with respect to the reference
Gibbs state.

Independently,
[
2
]
we have shown that the intensive–extensive classification of thermodynamic variables is itself emergent: it arises
precisely when a combined coarse-graining operator
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{x}\circ\mathcal{C}_{p}
, acting jointly on physical space and momentum space, produces mesoscopic variables that factorise across spatial cells. The coarse-grained entropy is additive if and only if inter-cell mutual information vanishes—a condition equivalent to exponential decay of correlations, which holds under the stability and temperedness of the pair potential.

In this paper, we unite these two lines of work. The central object is the
mesoscopic partition function

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
, constructed directly from the
combined coarse-graining framework of
[
2
]
: given the product-cell partition
\{C_{i,\alpha}\}
of single-particle phase space and the cell-averaged
Hamiltonians
\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}
and inter-cell interactions
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}
, one sums the Boltzmann weight
e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}}
over all occupation-number configurations weighted by the phase-space volume
\mathcal{W}
. The full perturbation theory of
F
is then derived at the mesoscopic level: each ingredient—the cumulant expansion, Gibbs–Bogoliubov bound, and coupling-parameter integration—has a precise mesoscopic
counterpart formulated in terms of
\{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\}
and
\{\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}\}
.

This paper present the following main new results are:

(i)

A
rigorous definition
of the mesoscopic partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)

from the combined coarse-graining operator of
[
2
]
, and its
exact factorisation at
\lambda=0
into
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=(Z_{1}^{(0)})^{N}
.

(ii)

A
mesoscopic cumulant expansion
of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
in terms of
occupation-number cumulants of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
with respect to the reference multinomial
distribution.

(iii)

A
mesoscopic Gibbs–Bogoliubov inequality
and coupling-parameter integration
formula, with explicit convergence to the standard continuum results as cells shrink.

(iv)

A
connection formula

F(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O(\left|\Lambda\right|\ell^{-d}e^{-2\ell/\xi})
,
showing that the mutual information from the extensivity analysis is precisely the corrections between the mesoscopic and full partition functions.

(v)

For systems with
long-range interactions
, where factorisation fails, a systematic correction to the mesoscopic free energy expressed in terms of non-vanishing inter-cell mutual information.

2
The Combined Coarse-Graining Framework

We recall the key elements of
[
2
]
, fixing notation for the rest
of the paper.

2.1
The N-Body System and Reduced Densities

Consider
N
identical classical particles of mass
m
in a domain

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
of volume
V=|\Lambda|
, with pairwise interaction

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
. The full Hamiltonian is

H_{N}(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{|\bm{p}_{k}|^{2}}{2m}+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq N}\phi(\bm{q}_{k}-\bm{q}_{l}),\quad\gamma=(\bm{q}_{1},\dots,\bm{q}_{N},\bm{p}_{1},\dots,\bm{p}_{N})\in\Gamma_{N}.

(1)

We split
H_{N}=H_{0}+\lambda V
, where
H_{0}
is a soluble reference Hamiltonian and

V
is the perturbation, with
\lambda\in[0,1]
. The canonical Gibbs state at coupling

\lambda
is
\varrho_{N,\lambda}=Z(\lambda)^{-1}e^{-\beta(H_{0}+\lambda V)}
.

The
reduced one-particle density
is defined by integrating out
N-1
particles:

f^{(1)}_{\lambda}(z)=N\int_{\Gamma_{N}}\varrho_{N,\lambda}(\gamma)\prod_{k=2}^{N}\,\mathrm{d}z_{k},\qquad z=(q,p)\in\mathcal{M}:=\Lambda\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

(2)

normalised to
\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}z=N
. For a pair-potential perturbation,
the
reduced two-particle density

f^{(2)}_{\lambda}(z_{1},z_{2})
satisfies the Ursell
decomposition

f^{(2)}_{\lambda}(z_{1},z_{2})=f^{(1)}_{\lambda}(z_{1})\,f^{(1)}_{\lambda}(z_{2})+u^{(2)}_{\lambda}(z_{1},z_{2}),

(3)

where the pair Ursell function
u^{(2)}_{\lambda}
encodes connected two-body correlations and
obeys
\int_{\mathcal{M}}u^{(2)}_{\lambda}(z_{1},z_{2})\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}=0
for all
z_{1}
.

2.2
The Combined Coarse-Graining Operator

Following
[
2
]
, we partition the spatial domain
\Lambda
into disjoint
cells
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
of volume
\ell^{d}
and momentum space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into cells

\{\Pi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of volume
\nu_{\alpha}
. The product cells

C_{i,\alpha}:=V_{i}\times\Pi_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{M}

(4)

partition
\mathcal{M}
. The spatial cell diameter
\ell
satisfies the scale separation
condition of
[
2
]
:

\xi\ll\ell\ll L:=\mathrm{diam}(\Lambda),

(5)

where
\xi
is the correlation length and
L
is the system size.

Definition 2.1

(Combined coarse-graining operator,
[
2
]
)
.

The
combined coarse-graining operator

\mathcal{C}
maps
f^{(1)}_{\lambda}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{M})
to
a piecewise-constant function:

(\mathcal{C}f^{(1)}_{\lambda})(z)=\sum_{i,\alpha}\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)\,\mathbf{1}_{C_{i,\alpha}}(z),\quad\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{|C_{i,\alpha}|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}f^{(1)}_{\lambda}(z^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}.

(6)

Definition 2.2

(Mesoscopic probability,
[
2
]
)
.

The dimensionless
mesoscopic probability
of cell
C_{i,\alpha}
at coupling

\lambda
is

\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda):=\frac{|C_{i,\alpha}|}{N}\bar{f}_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{N}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}f^{(1)}_{\lambda}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z.

(7)

These satisfy
\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)\geq 0
and
\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)=1

for all
\lambda
. At
\lambda=0
we write
\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}:=\pi_{i,\alpha}(0)
.

Definition 2.3

(Coarse-grained entropy,
[
2
]
)
.

The coarse-grained Boltzmann entropy at coupling
\lambda
is

S_{\mathrm{CG}}(\lambda)=-k_{\mathrm{B}}\sum_{i,\alpha}\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)\,\ln\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda).

(8)

The extensivity theorem of
[
2
]
asserts: under stability, temperedness,
and exponential cluster decomposition with correlation length
\xi
, the entropy satisfies

S_{\mathrm{CG}}(\lambda)=\sum_{i}S_{i}(\lambda)+O(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-\ell/\xi})
,
with equality (exact additivity) if and only if the mesoscopic probabilities factorise
across spatial cells:

\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}(\lambda)\approx\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)\,\pi_{j,\beta}(\lambda)\quad\text{up to corrections }O(N^{-1}e^{-|x_{i}-x_{j}|/\xi}).

(9)

3
The Mesoscopic Partition Function

3.1
Cell-Averaged Hamiltonians

Given the decomposition
H_{N}=H_{0}+\lambda V
with
V=\sum_{k<l}v(\bm{q}_{k}-\bm{q}_{l})
,
we define cell-averaged energies through the one-particle reduced density.

Definition 3.1

(Cell-averaged reference energy)
.

The
cell-averaged single-particle reference energy
in cell
C_{i,\alpha}
is

\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}:=\frac{1}{|C_{i,\alpha}|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}\varepsilon_{0}(z)\,\,\mathrm{d}z,\quad\varepsilon_{0}(q,p):=\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m}+u_{0}(q),

(10)

where
u_{0}(q)
is the single-particle external potential in
H_{0}
. For the pair-potential
perturbation, the
cell-averaged inter-cell pair interaction
between cells

C_{i,\alpha}
and
C_{j,\beta}
(with
i\neq j
or
\alpha\neq\beta
) is

\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}:=\frac{1}{|C_{i,\alpha}|\,|C_{j,\beta}|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}\int_{C_{j,\beta}}v(|\bm{q}_{1}-\bm{q}_{2}|)\,\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}.

(11)

Both quantities are well-defined, finite, and depend only on the geometry of the cells
and the form of the potentials.

3.2
The Mesoscopic Hamiltonian

Definition 3.2

(Mesoscopic Hamiltonian)
.

The
mesoscopic Hamiltonian
for an occupation-number configuration

\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}:=\{\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{|\mathcal{I}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|}:\sum_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}=N\}
at coupling
\lambda
is

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\},\lambda):=\underbrace{\sum_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}\,\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}_{\text{reference (intra-cell)}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)\end{subarray}}n_{i,\alpha}\,n_{j,\beta}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}}_{\text{inter-cell perturbation}}.

(12)

The occupation number
n_{i,\alpha}\in\{0,1,\dots,N\}
counts the particles occupying
cell
C_{i,\alpha}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The first sum in (
12
) captures the kinetic energy and single-particle
reference potential, coarse-grained to the cell level. The second sum captures
pair interactions
between
distinct cells at coupling
\lambda
; the factor

1/2
avoids double-counting. Intra-cell pair interactions
(
n_{i,\alpha}(n_{i,\alpha}-1)/2
pairs within a single cell) are absorbed into

\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}
in the reference, reflecting the choice of

H_{0}
as already capturing short-range repulsion within each cell.

3.3
Phase-Space Volume Weight

Each occupation-number configuration
\{n_{i,\alpha}\}
is compatible with a number of
distinct microstates in the original phase space
\Gamma_{N}
: we must count how many ways to
assign
N
distinguishable particles to the cells.

Definition 3.4

(Phase-space volume factor)
.

The
phase-space volume factor
for configuration
\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}
is

\mathcal{W}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\}):=\frac{N!}{\prod_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}!}\prod_{i,\alpha}|C_{i,\alpha}|^{n_{i,\alpha}}.

(13)

The multinomial coefficient
N!/\prod n_{i,\alpha}!
counts the number of ways to
assign
N
distinguishable particles to the cells;
|C_{i,\alpha}|^{n_{i,\alpha}}
is
the phase-space volume available to the
n_{i,\alpha}
particles assigned to cell

C_{i,\alpha}
.

3.4
Definition of the Mesoscopic Partition Function

Definition 3.5

(Mesoscopic partition function)
.

The
mesoscopic (combined) partition function
at coupling
\lambda
is

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda):=\sum_{\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\mathcal{W}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\})\,e^{-\beta\,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\},\lambda)}.

(14)

It is a discrete sum over all occupation-number configurations consistent with total
particle number
N
, with each configuration weighted by both its phase-space volume
and its Boltzmann factor. The mesoscopic free energy is

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda):=-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
.

Remark 3.6

(Connection to the coarse-graining operator)
.

The sum (
14
) can be read as the partition function of the system in which
the combined coarse-graining operator
\mathcal{C}
has replaced the microscopic density

\varrho_{N}
by its cell-average
(\mathcal{C}\varrho_{N})
: integrating
e^{-\beta H_{N}}
over the
coarse-grained description of phase space precisely yields

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
when the cell-averaged energy is used. This is the explicit partition
function of the coarse-grained state introduced in
[
2
]
.

3.5
Factorisation of the Reference Mesoscopic Partition Function

Proposition 3.7

(Reference factorisation)
.

At
\lambda=0
, the mesoscopic partition function factorises into the
N
-th power of
the single-cell partition function:

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}:=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(0)=\left(\sum_{i,\alpha}|C_{i,\alpha}|\,e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}\right)^{N}=:\bigl(Z_{1}^{(0)}\bigr)^{N},

(15)

where
Z_{1}^{(0)}=\sum_{i,\alpha}|C_{i,\alpha}|\,e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}

is the single-cell partition function of the reference.

Proof.

At
\lambda=0
, the mesoscopic Hamiltonian reduces to

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=\sum_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}
,
so the exponent factorises over cells:

e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}}=\prod_{i,\alpha}e^{-\beta n_{i,\alpha}\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\frac{N!}{\prod_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}!}\prod_{i,\alpha}\!\bigl(|C_{i,\alpha}|e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}\bigr)^{n_{i,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\sum_{i,\alpha}|C_{i,\alpha}|\,e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}\biggr)^{N}=\bigl(Z_{1}^{(0)}\bigr)^{N},

(16)

where the second equality is the multinomial theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.8

(Reference mesoscopic free energy and probabilities)
.

The reference mesoscopic free energy is

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=-Nk_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln Z_{1}^{(0)},

(17)

and the reference mesoscopic probability of cell
C_{i,\alpha}
is

\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}=\frac{|C_{i,\alpha}|\,e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}}{Z_{1}^{(0)}},

(18)

which coincides with the mesoscopic probability of
definition

2.2
at
\lambda=0
:

\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}=\pi_{i,\alpha}(0)
. The occupation numbers follow the reference
multinomial distribution

P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\})=\frac{N!}{\prod_{i,\alpha}n_{i,\alpha}!}\prod_{i,\alpha}(\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha})^{n_{i,\alpha}},

(19)

with mean
\langle n_{i,\alpha}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=N\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}
and variance

\langle(\delta n_{i,\alpha})^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=N\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}(1-\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha})
.

Proof.

Equation (
17
) follows from (
15
). For (
18
),
the expected occupation
\langle n_{i,\alpha}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=N\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}
together with the
factorisation
Z_{1}^{(0)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}=|C_{i,\alpha}|e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{(0)}}

identifies
\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}
as the fraction of the total phase-space weight in cell

C_{i,\alpha}
at the reference level. Dividing by
N
gives the mesoscopic probability
per particle, recovering
definition

2.2
.
∎

4
Mesoscopic Perturbation Theory

4.1
The Mesoscopic Moment Generating Function

Separating the reference and perturbation in (
14
):

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\sum_{\{n_{i,\alpha}\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\mathcal{W}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\})\,e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}}\,e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}},

(20)

where
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\}):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}n_{i,\alpha}n_{j,\beta}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}
is the inter-cell perturbation.
Therefore

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}\cdot\langle e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)},

(21)

and the mesoscopic free energy is

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln\langle e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}.

(22)

The
mesoscopic moment generating function

M_{\rm meso}(\lambda):=\langle e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}
takes the form

M_{\rm meso}(\lambda)=\sum_{\{n_{i,\alpha}\}}P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\})\,e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\{n_{i,\alpha}\})}.

(23)

4.2
The Mesoscopic Cumulant Expansion

Define the mesoscopic cumulants of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
with respect to the reference distribution
(
19
):

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}

\displaystyle:=\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)},

(24)

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}

\displaystyle:=\langle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}-\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)},

(25)

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}

\displaystyle:=n\text{-th cumulant of }\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\text{ under }P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}.

(26)

The mesoscopic cumulant generating function is

K_{\rm meso}(\lambda)=\ln M_{\rm meso}(\lambda)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\beta\lambda)^{n}}{n!}\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}
, giving the

mesoscopic cumulant expansion of the free energy
:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}-\frac{\beta\lambda^{2}}{2}\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}+\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda^{3}}{6}\kappa_{3}^{\rm meso}-\cdots

(27)

This is the mesoscopic analogue of the standard cumulant
expansion
[
3
,
4
]
. At
\lambda=1
it gives the
perturbation series for the full mesoscopic free energy
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)
.

Remark 4.1

(Extensivity of the mesoscopic cumulants)
.

Under the multinomial distribution (
19
), the occupation numbers

n_{i,\alpha}
are independent with mean
N\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
is a sum
of bilinear terms
n_{i,\alpha}n_{j,\beta}
, each cumulant
\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}
is

O(N^{2})
for the first cumulant (mean) and
O(N^{2})
for the variance, both
growing extensively with system size. This is consistent with the linked-cluster
theorem (
section

8
), which guarantees that each free-energy correction is

O(N)
.

4.3
The Mesoscopic Gibbs–Bogoliubov Inequality

Theorem 4.2

(Mesoscopic Gibbs–Bogoliubov inequality)
.

For any coupling
\lambda\geq 0
,

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)},

(28)

with equality iff
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
is constant on the support of
P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}
.

Proof.

Identical in structure to the standard proof
[
11
]
: Jensen’s inequality
applied to the convex function
x\mapsto e^{x}
and the probability distribution

P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}
gives

M_{\rm meso}(\lambda)=\langle e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}\geq e^{-\beta\lambda\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}}
.
Taking logarithms and multiplying by
-k_{\mathrm{B}}T>0
:

-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln M_{\rm meso}(\lambda)\leq\lambda\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}
.
Adding
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}
gives (
28
).
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

The second mesoscopic cumulant
\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}\geq 0
, so the term

-(\beta\lambda^{2}/2)\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}\leq 0
in (
27
) is always
non-positive: inter-cell fluctuations always lower the mesoscopic free energy below
the Gibbs–Bogoliubov bound.

4.4
Mesoscopic Coupling-Parameter Integration

Theorem 4.4

(Mesoscopic Hellmann–Feynman and coupling integration)
.

The derivative of the mesoscopic free energy with respect to the coupling parameter is

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}=\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda},

(29)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda}
denotes the average with respect to the
mesoscopic distribution at coupling
\lambda
. Integrating gives the exact result

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)-\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda}\,\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(30)

Proof.

Differentiate
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
:

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}}{\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\cdot\frac{-\beta\sum_{\{n\}}\mathcal{W}\,\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\,e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}}}{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}}=\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda}.

Integrating over
\lambda\in[0,1]
gives (
30
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Equation (
30
) is the
mesoscopic analogue of the
Kirkwood coupling-parameter integration

[
5
]
: the exact free energy
difference equals the integral of the thermally averaged inter-cell perturbation energy
over the full coupling range. Taylor-expanding
\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda}
in

\lambda
and integrating term-by-term reproduces the cumulant
series (
27
). This identity holds at every scale set by the
coarse-graining partition and requires no approximation.

5
Connection between the Mesoscopic and Full Free Energies

The mesoscopic partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
approximates the full partition function

Z(\lambda)
with corrections controlled by the inter-cell mutual information of the
extensivity analysis
[
2
]
.

5.1
The Connection Formula

Theorem 5.1

(Mesoscopic–full connection)
.

Under the conditions of
[
2
]
(stability, temperedness, exponential
cluster decomposition with length
\xi
, scale separation
\ell\gg\xi
), the full
Helmholtz free energy satisfies

F(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\!\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O\!\left(\frac{|\Lambda|}{\ell^{d}}\,e^{-2\ell/\xi}\right),

(31)

where
I(i,j;\lambda)\geq 0
is the mutual information between spatial cells
i
and
j

at coupling
\lambda
:

I(i,j;\lambda)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}(\lambda)\ln\frac{\pi_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}(\lambda)}{\pi_{i,\alpha}(\lambda)\,\pi_{j,\beta}(\lambda)}.

(32)

Proof.

By definition,
\ln Z(\lambda)=-\beta F(\lambda)
and

\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=-\beta\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
. The difference

\ln Z(\lambda)-\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
equals the correction to the log-partition function
from inter-cell correlations.

The full log-partition function can be expressed via the multi-information expansion
(proved in
[
2
]
, §4.2 for the entropy; the analogous result for

\ln Z
follows from the same cluster-expansion argument):

\ln Z(\lambda)=\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)+\sum_{s=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{s}\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}}\kappa_{s}^{\rm MI}(i_{1},\dots,i_{s};\lambda),

(33)

where
\kappa_{s}^{\rm MI}
is the
s
-cell connected mutual information. At the pair
level,
\kappa_{2}^{\rm MI}(i,j;\lambda)=-I(i,j;\lambda)
(mutual information is the
correction from the factorised approximation). Under exponential cluster decomposition,

I(i,j;\lambda)\leq Ce^{-|x_{i}-x_{j}|/\xi}
(proved in
[
2
]
), and the sum over all
s\geq 3
is bounded by

O(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-2\ell/\xi})
(the
s=3
term involves products of two
exponentially small factors). Hence

\ln Z(\lambda)=\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)+\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O\!\left(\frac{|\Lambda|}{\ell^{d}}\,e^{-2\ell/\xi}\right).

(34)

Multiplying by
-k_{\mathrm{B}}T
gives (
31
).
∎

5.2
Interpretation

Equation (
31
) has four immediate consequences:

(i)

Mesoscopic free energy as leading approximation.

For
\ell\gg\xi
(scale separation (
5
)), all inter-cell mutual
informations satisfy
I(i,j;\lambda)\leq Ce^{-|x_{i}-x_{j}|/\xi}
, and the total
correction
-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)=O(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-\ell/\xi})
is
exponentially suppressed. Thus
F(\lambda)\approx\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
with exponentially
small error.

(ii)

Mutual information as the correction.

The inter-cell mutual information
I(i,j;\lambda)
is precisely the object whose vanishing is equivalent to extensivity in
[
2
]
. Thus, the same
quantity that controls entropy additivity also controls the accuracy of the mesoscopic partition function as an approximation to the full one.

(iii)

Sign.

Since
I(i,j;\lambda)\geq 0
, the correction
-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)\leq 0
: the full free energy is always
lower than or equal to
the mesoscopic free
energy. Statistical dependence between cells lowers the free energy, as expected from the second-order Gibbs–Bogoliubov argument.

(iv)

Long-range interactions.

For non-tempered potentials (
|\phi(r)|\sim r^{-s}
,
s\leq 3
), the mutual information

I(i,j;\lambda)\sim|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-s}
does not decay exponentially, the correction

\sum_{i<j}I(i,j)
diverges, and the mesoscopic free energy is
not
a good
approximation to the full one. One must then either include the mutual-information
corrections explicitly or use a larger cell size to absorb the long-range effects.

6
First-Order Mesoscopic Perturbation Theory

We can now develop the perturbation theory.

6.1
The Mesoscopic First-Order Free Energy

At first order in
\lambda
, (
27
) gives

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}.

(35)

Substituting (
24
):

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\frac{\lambda N(N-1)}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)\end{subarray}}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\,\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}.

(36)

This is the
mesoscopic mean-field free energy
: the correction is the
occupation-probability-weighted average of the cell-averaged pair interaction, summed
over all distinct inter-cell pairs.

6.2
Continuous Limit

As the cell diameter
\ell\to 0
, the cell averages become exact:

\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}\to v(|\bm{x}_{i}-\bm{x}_{j}|)
and

\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\to f^{(1)}_{0}(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{p}_{\alpha})\,|C_{i,\alpha}|/N
.

Proposition 6.1

(Recovery of standard first-order theory)
.

In the limit of infinitesimally small cells with scale separation maintained,

\lim_{\ell\to 0}\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}(1)=F_{0}+\frac{N\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)\,g_{0}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}r=F_{0}+\langle V\rangle_{0},

(37)

where
g_{0}(r)
is the pair distribution function of the reference system at
\lambda=0
.

Proof.

In the continuous limit, the double sum over cell pairs in (
36
)
converges to a double integral:

\displaystyle\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}

\displaystyle\to\frac{N(N-1)}{2N^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{1})f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{2})\,v(|q_{1}-q_{2}|)\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{1})f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{2})\,v(|q_{1}-q_{2}|)\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}+O(N^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{N\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)\,g_{0}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r+O(N^{-1}),

(38)

where in the last line we used the definition of
g_{0}
via the two-particle reduced density

f^{(2)}_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})\approx f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{1})f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{2})
(disconnected part only at the
first-order level, since the Ursell correction is of higher order) and integrated out
the momenta. Adding
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}\to F_{0}
completes the proof.
∎

6.3
Van der Waals from the Mesoscopic Perspective

Take the reference system to be hard spheres of diameter
\sigma
and the perturbation
to be a purely attractive tail
v_{\rm attr}(r)
for
r\geq\sigma
. The hard-sphere
x{\rm HS}
is determined by the cell-averaged kinetic energy
and the hard-sphere configurational weight. At the coarse-grained level, cells separated
by more than one diameter
\sigma
have
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}=v_{\rm attr}(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)
, while cells within one diameter contribute only to the reference
H_{0}^{\rm HS}
.

At the mesoscopic first order, (
36
) becomes

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}\big|_{\lambda=1}=F_{\rm HS}+\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,\alpha),(j,\beta):\\
|x_{i}-x_{j}|>\sigma\end{subarray}}{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm HS}{\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}}^{\rm HS}\,v_{\rm attr}(|x_{i}-x_{j}|).

(39)

In the continuous limit, using
g_{\rm HS}(r)\approx 1
for
r>\sigma
at low density,
this gives
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}\to F_{\rm HS}-Na\rho
with the van der Waals constant

a=-(1/2)\int_{\sigma}^{\infty}v_{\rm attr}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r
, reproducing the van der
Waals equation of state directly from the mesoscopic first-order theory.

7
Second-Order Mesoscopic Perturbation Theory

We aim to develop a perturbation theory that may, in future work, be extended to gravitational settings, such as those considered in
[
16
]
. In the present work, however, we restrict attention to non-relativistic perturbations.

7.1
The Mesoscopic Second-Order Correction

The second-order mesoscopic correction is

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(2)}=-\frac{\beta}{2}\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}=-\frac{\beta}{2}\langle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}-\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}.

(40)

We compute
\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}
using the multinomial statistics of

\{n_{i,\alpha}\}
under
P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}
.

Proposition 7.1

(Decomposition of the mesoscopic variance)
.

The mesoscopic second cumulant decomposes as

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{N(N-1)}{4}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}^{2}}_{\text{diagonal pair contribution}}

(41)

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{\frac{N(N-1)(N-2)}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i,\alpha),(j,\beta),(k,\gamma)\\
\text{distinct cells}\end{subarray}}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\pi^{(0)}_{k,\gamma}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(k,\gamma)}}_{\text{three-cell contribution}}

(42)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{N(N-1)}{2}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}\right)^{2}\cdot O(N^{-2}),

(43)

where the last term is the subtracted mean-squared contribution.

Proof.

Expand
(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}})^{2}
by squaring the double sum
\sum_{(i,\alpha)<(j,\beta)}n_{i,\alpha}n_{j,\beta}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}
and use the multinomial cumulant formula:

\langle n_{i,\alpha}^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=N\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}(1-\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha})+N^{2}(\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha})^{2}

and
\langle n_{i,\alpha}n_{j,\beta}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=N(N-1)\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}
for

(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)
. Collecting terms and subtracting
(\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)})^{2}

yields (
41
)–(
43
).
∎

7.2
Continuous Limit and the Structure Factor

In the continuous limit, the leading terms of
\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}
converge to their
continuum counterparts.

Proposition 7.2

(Recovery of the structure-factor formula)
.

As
\ell\to 0
, the diagonal contribution (
41
) converges to

\frac{N\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)^{2}\,g_{0}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

(44)

and the three-cell contribution (
42
) converges to the
three-body correlation integral, so that the total mesoscopic second-order correction
converges to the structure-factor formula:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(2)}\xrightarrow{\ell\to 0}-\frac{\beta N\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{v}(k)^{2}\,\bigl[S_{0}(k)-1\bigr]\,\frac{4\pi k^{2}\,\mathrm{d}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}-\frac{\beta N\rho}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}v(r)^{2}\,g_{0}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=F^{(2)},

(45)

where
S_{0}(k)=1+\rho\int[g_{0}(r)-1]e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{r}
is the reference
structure factor.

Proof.

As
\ell\to 0
, the cell sums converge to spatial integrals:

\frac{N(N-1)}{4}\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\bar{v}^{2}\to\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\int\!\!\int f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{1})f^{(1)}_{0}(z_{2})\,v^{2}(|q_{1}-q_{2}|)\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}\approx\frac{N\rho}{4}\int v(r)^{2}\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Substituting the pair structure
g_{0}
and the three-body structure
g_{0}^{(3)}
for the
respective integrals, and using Parseval’s theorem to rewrite the cross-term in Fourier
space, reproduces (
45
) exactly as in the continuum theory.
∎

8
The Linked-Cluster Theorem at the Mesoscopic Level

Theorem 8.1

(Mesoscopic linked-cluster theorem)
.

In the thermodynamic limit, the mesoscopic free energy
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
receives
contributions only from
connected
clusters of cells in the mesoscopic Mayer
expansion. Explicitly:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{\text{connected }G}\frac{1}{|{\rm Aut}(G)|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(i_{1},\alpha_{1}),\dots,(i_{n},\alpha_{n})\\
\text{distinct}\end{subarray}}\prod_{(a,b)\in G}\bigl(e^{-\beta\lambda\bar{v}_{(i_{a},\alpha_{a})(i_{b},\alpha_{b})}}-1\bigr)\prod_{k}\pi^{(0)}_{i_{k},\alpha_{k}},

(46)

where the outer sum is over connected graphs
G
on
n
vertices and
|{\rm Aut}(G)|

is the automorphism group order. Disconnected graphs cancel in
\ln\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Expand
e^{-\beta\lambda\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}}=\prod_{(i,\alpha)<(j,\beta)}e^{-\beta\lambda n_{i,\alpha}n_{j,\beta}\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}}
and write

e^{-\beta\lambda nn^{\prime}\bar{v}}=1+(e^{-\beta\lambda nn^{\prime}\bar{v}}-1)
. Expanding over
all cell pairs generates a sum over graphs, where each edge
(i,\alpha)-(j,\beta)

carries a factor
(e^{-\beta\lambda\bar{v}}-1)
. Summing over occupation numbers
under
P_{\rm meso}^{(0)}
(using independence of distinct cells at
\lambda=0
) and
taking the logarithm exactly retains only connected graphs, via the same combinatorial
argument as the standard Mayer linked-cluster theorem
[
6
,
7
]
.
∎

Corollary 8.2

(Extensivity of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}
)
.

Each connected graph on
n
vertices contributes a term
O(N)
to
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
,
because the sum over
n
-tuples of cells gives one factor of
N
from translational
invariance (one cell is fixed) and
n-1
factors from the remaining sums, which converge
geometrically for tempered
\bar{v}
. Hence
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=O(N)
at every order,
confirming the extensivity of the mesoscopic free energy under the conditions
of
[
2
]
.

9
Applications to Reference Systems

9.1
Ideal Gas: Exact Mesoscopic Results

For the ideal-gas reference (
\phi\equiv 0
),
f_{0}^{(1)}(x,p)=\rho\,f_{\rm MB}(p)

(uniform and Maxwell–Boltzmann in momentum), and the cell averages are:

\bar{\varepsilon}_{i,\alpha}^{\rm IG}=\frac{1}{|C_{i,\alpha}|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{\bar{p}_{\alpha}^{2}}{2m},\quad{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm IG}=\frac{|V_{i}|}{|\Lambda|}\cdot\frac{|\Pi_{\alpha}|\,e^{-\beta\bar{p}_{\alpha}^{2}/2m}}{Z_{p}},

(47)

where
Z_{p}=\sum_{\alpha}|\Pi_{\alpha}|e^{-\beta\bar{p}_{\alpha}^{2}/2m}
. Since
f_{0}^{(1)}

factorises in
x
and
p
, so do
{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm IG}
:

{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm IG}=\pi_{i}^{\rm sp}\cdot{\pi_{\alpha}}^{\rm mom}
, and the Ursell
function
u^{(2)}_{0}\equiv 0
.

For a pair-potential perturbation
v(r)
:

\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}\big|_{\rm IG}=\frac{N^{2}\rho^{2}}{2}\int\!\!\int{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm IG}{\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}}^{\rm IG}{\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}}^{\rm IG}v(|x_{i}-x_{j}|)\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}\,\mathrm{d}x_{j}\xrightarrow{\ell\to 0}\frac{N\rho}{2}\int v(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r,

(48)

recovering the ideal-gas first-order correction with
g_{0}(r)\equiv 1
. The
second-order term (
45
) with
S_{0}(k)\equiv 1
gives

F^{(2)}_{\rm IG}=-(\beta N\rho/2)\int v(r)^{2}\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r
, the high-temperature
expansion coefficient
[
3
]
.

9.2
Hard-Sphere Reference and Barker–Henderson Theory

For the hard-sphere reference of diameter
\sigma
, cells
C_{i,\alpha}
with

|x_{i}-x_{j}|<\sigma
have
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}=0
(the perturbation

v_{\rm attr}
vanishes for
r<\sigma
by construction). For cells with

|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\sigma
:

\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}^{\rm BH}=\frac{1}{|C_{i,\alpha}||C_{j,\beta}|}\int_{C_{i,\alpha}}\int_{C_{j,\beta}}v_{\rm attr}(|q_{1}-q_{2}|)\,\mathrm{d}z_{1}\,\mathrm{d}z_{2}.

(49)

The reference mesoscopic probabilities are

{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm HS}=|C_{i,\alpha}|e^{-\beta\bar{\varepsilon_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm HS}}/{Z_{1}}^{\rm HS}
,
where the hard-sphere cell energy
\bar{\varepsilon}^{\rm HS}
accounts for the excluded
volume at the level of the effective Carnahan–Starling equation of state
[
9
]
.

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0+1)}\to F_{\rm HS}+(N\rho/2)\int v_{\rm attr}(r)\,g_{\rm HS}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r
,
where the HS pair distribution function
g_{\rm HS}(r)=\lim_{\ell\to 0}{\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}}^{\rm HS}{\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}}^{\rm HS}/\rho^{2}
emerges naturally from the
mesoscopic probability product.

9.3
WCA Reference and Optimal Cell Partition

The Weeks–Chandler–Andersen (WCA) decomposition
[
8
]
cuts the Lennard-Jones
potential at its minimum
r_{\rm min}=2^{1/6}\sigma
. In the mesoscopic framework,
the WCA choice is
optimal
in the following sense: it minimises the mesoscopic
second cumulant
\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}
at fixed cell partition, thereby minimising the
departure of the mesoscopic free energy from its Gibbs–Bogoliubov bound.

Proposition 9.1

(WCA as mesoscopic variance minimiser)
.

Among all splits
\phi=\phi_{0}+v_{1}
with
\phi_{0}
repulsive, the WCA split

(\phi_{0}^{\rm WCA},v_{1}^{\rm WCA})
minimises

\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}[v_{1}]=\langle(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{v_{1}}-\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{v_{1}}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)})^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}

Sketch.

\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}
is a quadratic functional of
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}

with a positive-definite kernel (it is a sum of squared terms weighted by

\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}>0
). Minimising over the choice of split point
r^{*}

shows that the optimal
r^{*}
is the point where
v(r)
changes sign (the minimum of

\phi
), since this is where the perturbation
v_{1}=\phi-\phi_{0}
has the smallest

L^{2}
norm with respect to the reference distribution. For the LJ potential, this
minimum is at
r_{\rm min}=2^{1/6}\sigma
, which is exactly the WCA choice.
∎

10
Thermodynamic Properties

All thermodynamic properties follow from the mesoscopic free energy

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}-(\beta/2)\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}+\cdots
.

10.1
Pressure

The mesoscopic pressure is
P_{\rm meso}=-(\partial\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}/\partial V)_{T,N}
. The
volume dependence enters through both
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}
(via
Z_{1}^{(0)}
, which depends on
the cell sizes
|V_{i}|=V/M
where
M
is the number of spatial cells) and the
cell-averaged interactions
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}
:

P_{\rm meso}=P_{0}-\frac{N(N-1)\rho^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\!\left[\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\pi^{(0)}_{j,\beta}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}\right]_{\pi^{(0)}\text{ fixed}}+O(\bar{v}^{2}).

(50)

In the continuous limit (
37
), this recovers the standard first-order
pressure correction:

P\to P_{0}-(\rho^{2}/2)\int v(r)\,g_{0}(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r
.

10.2
Chemical Potential

Differentiating
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)=-Nk_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln Z_{1}^{(0)}+N\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}/N+\cdots
with
respect to
N
at fixed
V,T
:

\mu_{\rm meso}=-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln Z_{1}^{(0)}-k_{\mathrm{B}}T+\rho\sum_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}\pi^{(0)}_{i,\alpha}\,\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}+O(\bar{v}^{2}).

(51)

10.3
The Mesoscopic Euler Relation

The mesoscopic Euler relation follows from the extensivity of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}
established
in
section

8
.

Corollary 10.1

(Mesoscopic Euler relation)
.

Under stability and temperedness
[
2
]
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)
is homogeneous of
degree one in
(N,V)
in the thermodynamic limit, so the mesoscopic Euler relation holds:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}=TS_{\mathrm{CG}}-P_{\rm meso}V+\mu_{\rm meso}N,

(52)

where
S_{\mathrm{CG}}
is the coarse-grained entropy of
definition

2.3

from
[
2
]
. In the thermodynamic limit with
\ell/\xi\to\infty
,
the connection formula (
31
) gives
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}\to F
and
S_{\rm CG}\to S
,
so (
52
) converges to the standard Euler relation
F=TS-PV+\mu N
.

11
Convergence of the Mesoscopic Perturbation Series

11.1
Convergence of the Mesoscopic Cumulant Series

Proposition 11.1

(Convergence radius of the mesoscopic series)
.

The mesoscopic cumulant series (
27
) converges absolutely for

|\lambda|<R_{\rm meso}
where

R_{\rm meso}=\left(\beta\max_{(i,\alpha)\neq(j,\beta)}|\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}|\right)^{-1}.

(53)

For
\ell\gg\xi
, the cell-averaged interaction satisfies

\max|\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}|\leq\max_{r>\ell}|v(r)|<\max_{r}|v(r)|
,
so
R_{\rm meso}>R_{\rm full}=(\beta\,{\rm ess\,sup}|v|)^{-1}
:

coarse-graining enlarges the radius of convergence
.

Proof.

The occupation numbers satisfy
|n_{i,\alpha}|\leq N
, so

|\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}|\leq N^{2}\max|\bar{v}|/2
. The cumulant bound

|\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}|\leq n!\,(N^{2}\max|\bar{v}|/2)^{n-1}
then gives a geometric
series with ratio
\beta\lambda N^{2}\max|\bar{v}|/(2N)=\beta\lambda N\max|\bar{v}|/2
,
which converges for
\lambda<2/(\beta N\max|\bar{v}|)
. Normalising per particle
(each
\kappa_{n}^{\rm meso}=O(N)
) removes one factor of
N
, giving
(
53
). The inequality
\max|\bar{v}_{ij}|<\max|v|
follows from
Jensen’s inequality applied to
|\bar{v}|=|\text{cell average of }v|\leq\text{cell average of }|v|\leq\max|v|
, with strict inequality for cells of
positive measure.
∎

11.2
Practical Validity Criterion

The mesoscopic perturbation series is reliable when:

(i)

\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}/(\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso})^{2}\ll 1
: the relative fluctuation
of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}
is small. This holds whenever
\bar{v}_{(i,\alpha)(j,\beta)}
varies little
across cell pairs, i.e., the interaction is smooth at the coarse-graining scale.

(ii)

\ell\gg\xi
: the scale separation (
5
) ensures that
the connection formula (
31
) has exponentially small corrections,
so
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)\approx F(\lambda)
.

(iii)

I(i,j;\lambda)\approx 0
: inter-cell mutual information is negligible, which
is guaranteed by the extensivity conditions of
[
2
]
.

When condition (iii) fails (long-range interactions), the mutual-information
corrections in (
31
) must be included, and the mesoscopic cumulant series
provides a systematic expansion around the factorised (independent-cell) approximation.

12
Discussion

The mesoscopic partition function as a bridge.

The mesoscopic partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
defined in
definition

3.5
is the exact
bridge between the coarse-graining framework of
[
2
]
and standard
perturbation theory. Its reference part (
15
) factorises into

(Z_{1}^{(0)})^{N}
because the combined coarse-graining produces statistically
independent cells—this is precisely the factorisation condition that

[
2
]
identifies with extensivity. The perturbed part introduces
inter-cell interactions that break factorisation, and the degree of this breaking is
measured by the mutual informations
I(i,j;\lambda)
, which are the same objects that
control entropy additivity in
[
2
]
.

Extensivity as a precondition for perturbation theory.

The connection formula (
31
) shows that perturbation theory at the
mesoscopic level is trustworthy precisely when the system is extensive:

F(\lambda)\approx\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
iff
I(i,j;\lambda)\approx 0
iff

S_{\rm CG}\approx\sum_{i}S_{i}
(extensivity). Thus the conditions under which the
standard perturbation theory is valid—stability, temperedness, short-range
interactions—are exactly those that guarantee extensivity in the sense
of
[
2
]
. The two theories are not merely analogous; they are
logically equivalent characterisations of the same microscopic condition.

Non-extensive systems.

For gravitational or other long-range systems,
I(i,j;\lambda)\not\to 0
and the
mesoscopic free energy
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)
overestimates
F(\lambda)
by the total mutual
information
k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j)
. Equation (
31
) provides a
systematic correction: one can include the pair mutual information term explicitly,
using the Ursell-function bound
I(i,j)\leq C|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{-s}
for potentials decaying
as
r^{-s}
, to obtain a controlled non-extensive free energy perturbation theory.

Cell-size dependence and the continuum limit.

The mesoscopic theory depends on the choice of cell partition
\{C_{i,\alpha}\}
.
As
\ell\to 0
,
propositions

6.1
and
7.2
show that the mesoscopic
cumulants converge to their continuum counterparts and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}\to F
. For finite
\ell
,
the mesoscopic theory is an approximation whose accuracy improves monotonically as

\ell\to 0
(assuming
\ell\gg\xi
is maintained). The optimal cell size balances
computational tractability (few cells for large
\ell
) against accuracy (small
\ell

for convergence to the continuum);
proposition

11.1
shows that larger cells enlarge
the convergence radius of the cumulant series.

13
Conclusion

We have developed a complete perturbation theory for the Helmholtz free energy grounded
in the mesoscopic coarse-graining framework of
[
2
]
. The principal
results are:

(i)

Mesoscopic partition function
(
section

3
).
The combined coarse-graining operator
\mathcal{C}
of
[
2
]
defines a
mesoscopic Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}
(cell-averaged kinetic and reference energies plus
cell-averaged inter-cell pair interactions) and a phase-space volume weight

\mathcal{W}
. Their combination gives the mesoscopic partition function

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\sum_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}\mathcal{W}\,e^{-\beta\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{meso}}}
, whose reference part
factorises by the multinomial theorem:
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=(Z_{1}^{(0)})^{N}
.

(ii)

Mesoscopic cumulant expansion
(
section

4
).
The mesoscopic free energy satisfies

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}-(\beta\lambda^{2}/2)\kappa_{2}^{\rm meso}+\cdots
,
with the Gibbs–Bogoliubov inequality
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}\leq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}+\lambda\kappa_{1}^{\rm meso}

and the exact coupling-parameter integration

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(1)-\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}^{(0)}=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{meso}}\rangle_{\rm meso,\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
.

(iii)

Connection formula
(
section

5
).

F(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{meso}}(\lambda)-k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{i<j}I(i,j;\lambda)+O(|\Lambda|\ell^{-d}e^{-2\ell/\xi})
.
The corrections are precisely the inter-cell mutual information from the extensivity
analysis of
[
2
]
, and they vanish exponentially for
\ell\gg\xi
.

(iv)

Continuous limit
(
sections

6
and
7
).
The mesoscopic first-order theory recovers
F_{0}+\langle V\rangle_{0}
in the
\ell\to 0
limit;
The second-order theory recovers the structure-factor formula. The WCA decomposition
is identified as the split that minimises the mesoscopic second cumulant.

(v)

Unified framework
(
sections

10
and
12
).
The conditions for the validity of perturbation theory (stability, temperedness,
short-range
v
) are exactly the conditions for extensivity in
[
2
]
: both reduce to the vanishing of inter-cell mutual information. The mesoscopic Euler
relation (
52
) connects the perturbation expansion directly to the
generalised Euler relation of
[
2
]
.
