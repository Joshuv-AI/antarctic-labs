Harmonic morphisms and minimal submanifolds
Oskar Riedler

Abstract
Harmonic morphisms, maps which preserve Laplace’s equation, are intimately connected to the topic of minimal submanifolds. In this article we first characterise harmonic morphisms between Riemannian manifolds as the weakly horizontally conformal maps that preserve the equation for minimal submanifolds of co-dimension 2 2 . We further derive additional reduction properties of harmonic morphisms for minimal submanifolds of other co-dimensions. These theorems are then applied in an example case, yielding a novel family of degree 4 4 area-minimising hypercones in ℝ m \mathds{R}^{m} , m ≥ 32 m\geq 32 .



1
Introduction

A map
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
between two Riemannian manifolds is called a
harmonic morphism
if it preserves Laplace’s equation, i.e. for any locally defined function germ
f:N\to\mathds{R}
one has

\Delta_{N}f=0\implies\Delta_{M}(f\circ\varphi)=0.

Here
\Delta_{M},\Delta_{N}
denote the Laplacians of
M,N
respectively.

While the above definition is purely analytic and originates in potential theory
[
CC65
]
, the geometric theory of harmonic morphisms turns out to be very rich. The investigation of harmonic morphisms in Riemannian geometry begins with the work of Fuglede
[
Fug78
]
and Ishihara
[
Ish79
]
, who found a geometric characterisation of these objects. They showed that a map is a harmonic morphism if and only if it is both
weakly horizontally conformal
and
harmonic
.

The notion of a harmonic morphism has turned out to be quite fertile and has since been studied in many contexts. We mention the settings of Riemannian polyhedra
[
EF01
]
, metric graphs
[
Ura00
,
ABBR15
]
, Weyl spaces
[
LP06
,
LP10
]
, and probability theory
[
BCD79
,
CFO90
]
.

In this article we will be concerned with the connections between harmonic morphisms and
minimal submanifolds
. In particular we wish to use harmonic morphisms to generate such submanifolds. In the case that the co-domain
N
is a surface a celebrated theorem of Baird and Eells
[
BE81
]
shows that
\varphi
fibres the regular points
M\setminus\{d\varphi=0\}
by minimal co-dimension
2
submanifolds:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
BE81
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a submersive horizontally conformal map with
\dim(N)=2
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is harmonic and thus a harmonic morphism.

(ii)

\varphi^{-1}(\{p\})
is a minimal co-dimension
2
submanifold of
M
for all
p\in N
.

It is natural to ask if a similar characterisation using minimal submanifolds is possible also for higher dimensional co-domains. In the following we characterise harmonic morphisms as those weakly horizontally maps preserving the equation for minimality of co-dimension
2
submanifolds at its regular locus.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a submersive horizontally conformal map. The following are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is harmonic and thus a harmonic morphism.

(ii)

For all minimal co-dimension
2
submanifolds
B\subset N
the pre-image
\varphi^{-1}(B)\subset M
is a minimal co-dimension
2
submanifold.

Harmonic morphisms are then closely tied to the geometry of co-dimension
2
submanifolds in the co-domain. We are also interested in submanifolds of other dimensions, in particular in hypersurfaces, i.e. co-dimension
1
submanifolds. In this direction Baird and Gudmundsson
[
BG92
]
derive results for so-called
p
-harmonic morphisms. Specialising to the case of a harmonic morphism, their statement treats again maps to a surface.

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Corollary 3.4 of
[
BG92
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a submersive harmonic morphism with
\dim(N)=2
and let
B\subset N
be a minimal submanifold of co-dimension
1
(i.e. a geodesic). The following are equivalent:

(i)

d\varphi(\nabla\|d\varphi\|^{2}(x)\,)
is tangent to
B
for all
x\in\varphi^{-1}(B)
.

(ii)

\varphi^{-1}(B)
is a minimal co-dimension
1
submanifold of
M
.

We extend this to arbitrary co-domains as follows:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a submersive harmonic morphism and let
B\subset N
be a minimal submanifold of co-dimension
p
with
p\neq 2
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

d\varphi(\nabla\|d\varphi\|^{2}(x)\,)
is tangent to
B
for all
x\in\varphi^{-1}(B)
.

(ii)

\varphi^{-1}(B)
is a minimal co-dimension
p
submanifold of
M
.

We remark that these theorems can be viewed as reduction theorems for minimal submanifolds, in the spirit of
[
HL71
,
Pal79
]
using group actions and
[
PT86
]
using Riemannian submersions. From this perspective they are useful tools for generating new examples of minimal submanifolds. We mention that the main technical step in showing our statements,
Lemma

3.3
below, can be applied to a wider class of settings and reduction problems than indicated by the above theorems (such as prescribed mean curvature problems in the setting of
p
-harmonic morphisms).

In the second part of the paper we use the above results in order to find a novel family of area-minimising cones in
\mathds{R}^{m}
. To do this we will be finding minimal cones by applying
Theorem

1.4
to
polynomial harmonic morphisms
between two euclidean spaces. Polynomial harmonic morphisms are an important class of harmonic morphisms, interesting in their own right
[
Bai83
,
EY95
,
ABB99
,
Rie25
]
. Since much about their general structure or classification still remains unknown, we mainly restrict ourselves to degree two polynomials, classified by Ou and Wood
[
OW96
,
Ou97
]
.

This classification makes use of so-called Clifford systems, see
4.4
below for their definition. Clifford systems seem well-adapted to the construction of area-minimisers, see
[
Cui26
]
for recent work constructing area-minimising co-dimension
2
cones. They also appear in the
g=4
case for the area-minimisers constructed by Wang
[
Wan94
]
. We show:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
m,n\in\mathds{N}
,
m\geq 32
and
n\geq 4
. For
A_{1},...,A_{2n}\in M_{m\times m}(\mathds{R})
a Clifford system, the cone

C^{4}_{m,n}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathds{R}^{m}\ \middle|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,A_{i}x\rangle^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,A_{n+i}x\rangle^{2}\right\}

is an area-minimising hypersurface in
\mathds{R}^{m}
.

Remark 1.6
.

For all values of
(m,n)
the mean curvature of
C^{4}_{m,n}
vanishes at its regular points, i.e.
C^{4}_{m,n}\setminus\{x\in\mathds{R}^{m}\mid\langle x,A_{i}x\rangle=0\ \forall i\in\{1,...,2n\}\,\}
is a minimal conical submanifold of
\mathds{R}^{m}
.

Example 1.7
.

We give the examples
C^{4}_{8,2}
,
C^{4}_{16,4}
, and
C^{4}_{32,4}
explicitly. Up to an isometry of the domain these cones are uniquely determined by the values of
m,n
.

\displaystyle C^{4}_{8,2}=\bigl\{(z_{1},z_{2},u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathds{C}^{4}\,\big|\,\|z_{1}u_{1}+z_{2}u_{2}\|^{2}=\|\overline{z_{2}}z_{1}-\overline{u_{2}}u_{1}\|^{2}\bigr\},

\displaystyle C^{4}_{16,4}=\bigl\{(z_{1},z_{2},u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathds{H}^{4}\,\big|\,\|z_{1}u_{1}+z_{2}u_{2}\|^{2}=\|\overline{z_{2}}z_{1}-\overline{u_{2}}u_{1}\|^{2}\bigr\},

\displaystyle C^{4}_{32,4}=\biggl\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})\in\mathds{H}^{8}\ \bigg|\ \Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{4}z_{i}u_{i}\Bigr\|^{2}=\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{2}\overline{z_{2i}}z_{2i-1}-\overline{u_{2i}}u_{2i-1}\Bigr\|^{2}\biggr\}.

Here the multiplications and conjugations are in the appropriate (skew-) field
\mathds{C}
or
\mathds{H}
.

Remark 1.8
.

Of the listed examples
C^{4}_{8,2}
is not an area-minimiser,
C^{4}_{32,4}
however is by
Theorem

1.5
. For
C^{4}_{16,4}
it remains open as to whether or not it minimises area – indeed
C^{4}_{16,4}
is the only cone in the family
C^{4}_{m,n}
for which it is not clear if it is an area-minimiser or not (cf.
Remark

4.13
in the main text).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we review basic definitions and relations that we will use. For an overview on the general theory and literature related to harmonic morphisms we recommend both the book
[
BW03
]
and the regularly updated online bibliography
[
Gud
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A map
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
between two Riemannian manifolds is called a
harmonic morphism
if for every
U\subseteq N
open and
f\colon U\to\mathds{R}
harmonic the composition
f\circ\varphi\colon\varphi^{-1}(U)\to\mathds{R}
is again harmonic.

Originally this class of maps was investigated in the potential-theoretic setting of Brelot harmonic spaces
[
CC65
]
. The geometric theory begins with the following theorem, found independently by Fuglede and Ishihara:

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Fug78
,
Ish79
]
)
.

A map
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
is a harmonic morphism if and only if it is both weakly horizontally conformal and harmonic.

We now define the terms appearing in
Theorem

2.2
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a smooth map between two Riemannian manifolds.

1.

\varphi
is
weakly horizontally conformal
if for all points
x\in M
one has either
d\varphi(x)=0
or that
d\varphi(x)
maps the horizontal space
\ker(d\varphi)^{\perp}
conformally onto
T_{\varphi(x)}N
.

2.

\varphi
is
harmonic
if it is a stationary point of the energy functional
\varphi\mapsto E_{2}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\|d\varphi\|^{2}\,d\Vol_{g}
. Here
\|d\varphi\|^{2}=\Tr(d\varphi^{T}d\varphi)
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
d\varphi
.

Remark 2.4
.

1.

If
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
is weakly horizontally conformal there eixsts a function
\lambda\colon M\to\mathds{R}
, called the
conformality factor
, so that
\lambda^{2}g(v,w)=h(d\varphi\,v,d\varphi\,w)
for all horizontal vectors
v,w\in\ker(d\varphi)^{\perp}
.

2.

If
e_{i}
denotes a local orthonormal frame on
M
, then
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
is harmonic if and only if the
tension field

\tau(\varphi)\vcentcolon=\Tr(\nabla d\varphi)=\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{e_{i}}(d\varphi(e_{i}))-\sum_{i}d\varphi(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}e_{i})

(1)

vanishes, here
\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
N
pulled back to
\varphi^{*}(TN)
and
\nabla^{M}
the Levi-Civita connection of
M
.

Another relevant operator we will make use of is the
p
-tension field, it generalises the tension field and is the variation of the functional
E_{p}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\|d\varphi\|^{p}\,d\Vol_{g}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds and
p\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
. The
p
-tension field
of
\varphi
is the section of
\varphi^{*}(TN)
given by

\tau_{p}(\varphi)\vcentcolon=\|d\varphi\|^{p-2}(\tau(\varphi)+(p-2)d\varphi(\nabla\ln\|d\varphi\|)\,).

(2)

We give some basic examples of harmonic morphisms.

Example 2.6
.

1.

Riemannian submersions with minimal fibres are harmonic morphisms. Classical examples are the Hopf fibrations
S^{2m-1}\to S^{m}
,
m\in\{1,2,4,8\}
, quotient maps
G/H\to G/K
for
H\subset K
closed subgroups of a Lie group
G
equipped with a bi-invariant metric, and the projection
(TM,g_{\textrm{Sasaki}})\to(M,g)
where
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold and
g_{\textrm{Sasaki}}
is the Sasaki metric on the tangent bundle.

2.

For
(M,g)
,
(N,h)
Riemannian manifolds and
f\colon M\to\mathds{R}
smooth, the projection of the warped product
(M\times N,g+f^{2}h)\to(N,h)
is a harmonic morphism. Classical examples include quotient maps to projective spaces
\mathds{K}^{m+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathds{KP}^{m}
for
\mathds{K}\in\{\mathds{R},\mathds{C},\mathds{H}\}
and the projection
S^{m+1}\setminus\{(\pm 1,0,...,0)\}\to S^{m}
to the latitudes.

Many specialised examples and constructions exist. In
Section

4
we will be using polynomial harmonic morphisms in order to apply our reduction theorems. These form a rich and important class of harmonic morphisms, see also Chapter 5 of
[
BW03
]
. The cases of co-domain
\mathds{R}^{2}
(
\cong\mathds{C}
) and
\mathds{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, are very different.

Polynomial harmonic morphisms have a special role in the general theory of harmonic morphisms, since a general harmonic morphism is modelled by a polynomial harmonic morphism at its critical points:

Theorem 2.7

(
[
Fug78
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to(N,h)
be a harmonic morphism, not constant on any connected component of
M
. Let
x\in M
be a critical point of
\varphi
, then the order of
\varphi
at
x
is finite and the symbol of
\varphi
at
x
is a homogeneous polynomial harmonic morphism
T_{x}M\to T_{\varphi(x)}N
.

In this article we will work with polynomial harmonic morphisms of degree
2
, which are classified by work of Ou and Wood
[
OW96
,
Ou97
]
. Few examples with co-domain
\mathds{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and degree
d\geq 3
are known, although some general constructions using Clifford systems are possible.

We close this section by noting the following statements about harmonic morphisms to euclidean spaces:

Theorem 2.8

(
[
ABB99
]
)
.

1.

If
n\geq 3
then every harmonic morphism from
\mathds{R}^{m}
to
\mathds{R}^{n}
is a polynomial mapping.

2.

If
n\geq 2
then every weakly horizontally conformal polynomial map
P:\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
is harmonic and thus a harmonic morphism.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
Fug78
]
)
.

Let
\varphi\colon(M,g)\to\mathds{R}^{n}
be smooth. Then
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},...,\varphi_{n})
is weakly horizontally conformal if and only if

\langle\nabla\varphi_{i}(x),\nabla\varphi_{j}(x)\rangle=\delta_{ij}\,\|\nabla\varphi_{i}(x)\|^{2}

for all
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}
.

3
Reduction theorems

In order to show the reduction theorems given in the introduction we first define what it means for a map to be weakly horizontally conformal to first order at some point:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(N,h)
be two Riemannian manifolds,
x\in M
. A submersive map
\varphi:M\to N
is said to be
weakly horizontally conformal to first order at
x
if for all non-zero eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}
of the first fundamental form
\varphi^{*}(h)
one has:

\lambda_{1}(x)=...=\lambda_{n}(x),\qquad\nabla\lambda_{1}(x)=...=\nabla\lambda_{n}(x).

(3)

This notion allows for an infinitesimal Koszul type formula, due to Gudmundsson
[
Gud92
]
, relating the Levi-Civita connections of
M
and
N
. We give the formula below and for completeness we include a proof.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
Gud92
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(N,h)
be Riemannian manifolds,
\varphi:M\to N
a submersive map. Let
X,Y,Z
be vector fields on
N
and denote their horizontal lifts to
M
by
\widehat{X},\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}
. Then at all points
x\in M
at which
\varphi
is weakly horizontally conformal to first order one has:

h(\nabla^{N}_{X}Y,Z)=\lambda^{2}\,g(\nabla^{M}_{\widehat{X}}\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})+\widehat{X}(\ln\lambda)\,h(Y,Z)+\widehat{Y}(\ln\lambda)\,h(X,Z)-\widehat{Z}(\ln\lambda)\,h(X,Y),

(4)

where
\nabla^{M},\nabla^{N}
denote the Levi-Civita connections on
M,N
respectively and
\lambda
the conformality factor of
\varphi
at
x
.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi
is weakly horizontally conformal to first order at
x\in M
, meaning that the one-jet of the first fundamental form
\varphi^{*}(h)
agrees with
\lambda^{2}\,g\circ\mathcal{H}
, where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the orthogonal projection to the horizontal bundle (cf. Defintion
3.1
). Note that
X=d\varphi(\widehat{X})
,
Y=d\varphi(\widehat{Y})
,
Z=d\varphi(\widehat{Z})
and then one has at
x
:

X(h(Y,Z))\circ\varphi=\widehat{X}(\varphi^{*}(h)\,(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}))=\widehat{X}(\lambda^{2}g(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}))=\widehat{X}(\lambda^{2})g(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})+\lambda^{2}\widehat{X}(g(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}))

with similar expressions holding for other compositions. Applying the Koszul formula for
\nabla^{N}
then gives:

\displaystyle 2h(\nabla^{N}_{X}Y,Z)=

\displaystyle X(h(Y,Z))+Y(h(X,Z))-Z(h(X,Y))+h([X,Y],Z)-h([X,Z],Y)-h([Y,Z],X)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&\lambda^{2}\left(\widehat{X}(g(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}))+\widehat{Y}(g(\widehat{X},\widehat{Z}))-\widehat{Z}(g(\widehat{X},\widehat{Y}))+g([\widehat{X},\widehat{Y}],\widehat{Z})-g([\widehat{X},\widehat{Z}],\widehat{Y}])-g([\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z}],\widehat{X}\right)\\
&\quad+2\widehat{X}(\ln\lambda)\lambda^{2}g(\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})+2\widehat{Y}(\ln\lambda)\lambda^{2}g(\widehat{X},\widehat{Z})-2\widehat{Z}(\ln\lambda)\lambda^{2}g(\widehat{X},\widehat{Y})\end{split}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda^{2}g(\nabla^{M}_{\widehat{X}}\widehat{Y},\widehat{Z})+2\widehat{X}(\ln\lambda)h(Y,Z)+2\widehat{Y}(\ln\lambda)h(X,Z)-2\widehat{Z}(\ln\lambda)h(X,Y),

which is the desired relation.∎

The key infinitesimal calculation for deriving the reduction statements is now the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(N,h)
be Riemannian manifolds,
\varphi:M\to N
submersive,
B\subset N
a co-dimension
p
submanifold. Suppose
\varphi
is weakly horizontally conformal to first order at
x\in\varphi^{-1}(B)
, then for all
Z\in T_{\varphi(x)}N
with
Z\perp T_{\varphi(x)}B
:

h(\frac{\tau_{p}(\varphi)\,(x)}{\|d\varphi\|^{p-2}},Z)=h(d\varphi(H_{\varphi^{-1}(B)}),Z)-h(\lambda^{2}H_{B},Z),

(5)

where
\lambda
is the conformality factor of
\varphi
at
x
and
H_{B},H_{\varphi^{-1}(B)}
denote the mean curvatures of
B
,
\varphi^{-1}(B)
at
\varphi(x)
,
x
respectively.
\tau_{p}(\varphi)\,(x)
is the
p
-tension field of
\varphi
evaluated at
x
, recall equation (
2
).

Proof.

Let
y=\varphi(x)
, we let

\bullet

v_{1},...,v_{m-n}
be an orthonormal basis of the fibre
T_{x}\varphi^{-1}(\{y\})
.

\bullet

b_{1},...,b_{n-p}
an orthonormal basis of
T_{y}B
and
\widehat{b}_{1},...\widehat{b}_{n-p}
their horizontal lifts to
T_{x}\varphi^{-1}(B)
.

\bullet

p_{1},...,p_{p}
an orthonormal basis of
(T_{y}B)^{\perp}
and
\widehat{p}_{1},...,\widehat{p}_{p}
their horizontal lifts to
T_{x}M
.

Note that

v_{1},...,v_{m-n},\lambda\widehat{b}_{1},...,\lambda\widehat{b}_{n-p},\lambda\widehat{p}_{1},....,\lambda\widehat{p}_{p}

is an orthonormal basis of
T_{x}M
. For
Z
orthogonal to
B
we denote its horizontal lift by
\widehat{Z}
. By the definition of the tension field
\tau
(recall equation (
1
)):

\displaystyle h(\tau(\varphi),Z)

\displaystyle=h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda b_{i}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda p_{i}-d\varphi(\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{v_{i}}v_{i}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{b_{i}}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{p_{i}}),Z)

\displaystyle=h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda b_{i}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda p_{i},Z)-\lambda^{2}g(\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{v_{i}}v_{i}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{b_{i}}+\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{p_{i}},\widehat{Z}).

(6)

Here
\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}
denotes the pullback connection of
\nabla^{N}
to
\varphi^{*}(TN)
. Note that
Z\perp b_{i}
so that

h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda{b}_{i},Z)=\lambda^{2}h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{N}_{b_{i}}b_{i},Z)=-\lambda^{2}h(H_{B},Z).

Further since
\widehat{Z}
is orthogonal to
\varphi^{-1}(B)
one has:

\lambda^{2}g(-\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{v_{i}}v_{i}-\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{b}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{b}_{i},\widehat{Z})=\lambda^{2}g(H_{\varphi^{-1}(B)},\widehat{Z})=h(d\varphi(H_{\varphi^{-1}(B)}),Z).

Two terms remain unevaluated on the right-hand side of equation (
6
). For these we note first that:

h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{\varphi^{*}}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda p_{i},Z)-\lambda^{2}g(\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}}\lambda\widehat{p}_{i},\widehat{Z})=\lambda^{2}h(\sum_{i}\nabla^{N}_{p_{i}}p_{i},Z)-\lambda^{2}\cdot\lambda^{2}g(\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\widehat{p}_{i}}\widehat{p}_{i},\widehat{Z}).

In the final calculation we apply Proposition
3.2
to get:

\displaystyle-\lambda^{4}g(\sum_{i}\nabla^{M}_{\widehat{p}_{i}}\widehat{p}_{i},Z)

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}h(\sum_{i}\nabla_{p_{i}}^{N}p_{i},Z)+2\sum_{i}\lambda^{2}\widehat{p}_{i}(\ln\lambda)h(p_{i},Z)-\lambda^{2}\widehat{Z}(\ln\lambda)\sum_{i}h(p_{i},p_{i})

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}h(\nabla^{N}_{p_{i}}p_{i},Z)-(p-2)h(d\varphi(\nabla\ln\lambda),Z).

Here we used that
\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}(\lambda)h(p_{i},Z)=g(\nabla\ln\lambda,\lambda\widehat{p}_{i})\lambda^{2}g(\lambda\widehat{p}_{i},\widehat{Z})
, summing this over
i
gives
\lambda^{2}g(\nabla\ln\lambda,\widehat{Z})
since
\widehat{Z}
is a linear combination of the
\lambda\widehat{p}_{i}
.

Combining all terms in (
6
) then gives:

h(\tau(\varphi)\,(x)+(p-2)d\varphi(\nabla\ln\lambda)\,(x),Z)=h(d\varphi(H_{\varphi^{-1}(B)}),Z)-h(\lambda^{2}H_{B},Z),

which is the desired identity (
5
).
∎

We now prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

The implication (i)
\implies
(ii) follows immediately from equation (
5
) of
Lemma

3.3
. Similarly (ii)
\implies
(i) follows from
Lemma

3.3
and the fact that for any
y\in N

Remark 3.4

The (purely local) statement that any tangent space of

Observe that the following reduction type theorem is an immediate consequence of equation (

4
Examples: area-minimising cones of
\mathds{R}^{m}

In this section we will apply the results of
Section

3
in order to generate interesting examples of minimal submanifolds. The harmonic morphisms we will consider for this will be homogeneous polynomials of degree
2
, classified by Ou and Wood
[
OW96
,
Ou97
]
. The data determining these maps is essentially given by so-called
Clifford systems
. The relevant class of quadratic polynomial harmonic morphisms which, in arbitrary co-dimension, pull back minimal cones to minimal cones are the
umbillic
ones. Pulling back
P^{-1}(V)
for
V\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
a subvectorspace by an umbillic quadratic harmonic morphism
P\colon\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
recovers classical families of minimal submanifolds, namely cones over the focal varieties of FKM isoparametric foliations of
S^{m-1}

[
FKM81
]
.

While we develop the remarks of the above paragraph in
Section

4.1
, the main part will be the construction of a novel family of area-minimising co-dimension
1
cones in
\mathds{R}^{m}
in
Section

4.2
. These are built by pulling back the Simons cone

S_{n}\vcentcolon=\{(x,y)\in\mathds{R}^{2n}\mid\|x\|^{2}=\|y\|^{2}\}

by an umbilic quadratic harmonic morphism
P\colon\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{2n}
. We show that if
m\geq 32
and
2n\geq 8
these singular cones are area-minimisers, cf.
Theorem

4.11
.

4.1
Umbillic polynomial harmonic morphisms

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a homogeneous polynomial harmonic morphism
P\colon\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
is
umbillic
if there exists a function
\eta\colon\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}
so that

dP(\nabla\|dP\|^{2}\,(x))=\eta(x)\,P(x)

for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{m}
.

An immediate application of
Theorem

1.4
, and our motivation for this definition, is that an umbillic harmonic morphism pulls back minimal cones to minimal cones:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
P\colon\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
be an umbillic homogeneous polynomial harmonic morphism.

1.

For any minimal conical submanifold
C\subset\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
the pre-image
P^{-1}(C)\subset\mathds{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
is a minimal conical submanifold.

2.

For any minimal submanifold
B\subset S^{n-1}
of
S^{n-1}
the pullback
P^{-1}(\mathds{R}_{>0}\cdot B)\cap S^{m-1}
is a minimal submanifold of
S^{m-1}
.

Proof.

Point 1 follows from
Theorem

1.4
by noting that
dP(\nabla\|P\|^{2}(x))=\eta(x)\cdot P(x)
is proportional to the radial vector, which is always tangent to any cone. Point 2 follows from noting that a submanifold
B\subset S^{n-1}
is minimal in
S^{n-1}
if and only if the cone
\mathds{R}_{>0}\cdot B
is minimal in
\mathds{R}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The submanifold
P^{-1}(\mathds{R}_{>0}\cdot B)\cap S^{m-1}
of point 2. in
Corollary

4.2
is not necessarily closed, even if
B
is closed.

We wish to apply

4.2
in order to generate interesting examples of minimal cones. In what follows we will briefly describe the classification of umbillic polynomial harmonic morphisms of degree

. For completeness we include a short proof.

Theorem 4.6

(

)
.

Let
P:\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
be a homogeneous polynomial harmonic morphism of degree

. Then

is a weighted sum of Clifford systems. That is up to an isometry of the domain:

\mathds{R}^{m}=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{k}\mathds{R}^{m_{\ell}}\oplus\mathds{R}^{m_{0}},\qquad P((x_{1},...,x_{k}),x_{0})=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\left(\langle x_{\ell},A^{(\ell)}_{1}x_{\ell}\rangle,...,\langle x_{\ell},A^{(\ell)}_{n}x_{\ell}\rangle\right)

for Clifford systems
(A_{1}^{(\ell)},...,A_{n}^{(\ell)})
on
\mathds{R}^{m_{\ell}}
and
\lambda_{\ell}>0
.
P
is umbillic if and only if all weights
\lambda_{\ell}
agree.

Proof.

Since
P
is a homogeneous quadratic polynomial there are symmetric matrices
A_{1},...,A_{n}\in M_{m\times m}(\mathds{R})
so that

P(x)=(\langle x,A_{1}x\rangle,...,\langle x,A_{n}x\rangle).

If
P_{i}
denotes the
i
-th component of
P
, then the condition that
P
is weakly horizontally conformal means
\langle\nabla P_{i},\nabla P_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\|\nabla_{i}P\|^{2}
, recall
Lemma

2.9
. Since the
A_{i}
are symmetric, this means

\langle x,\frac{1}{2}(A_{i}A_{j}+A_{j}A_{i})x\rangle=\langle A_{i}x,A_{j}x\rangle=\delta_{ij}\langle x,A_{i}^{2}x\rangle

for all
x\in\mathds{R}^{m}
, i.e.

A_{i}A_{j}+A_{j}A_{i}=2\delta_{ij}\,A_{i}^{2}

(7)

for all
i,j\in\{1,...,n\}
. Let
\lambda_{1}^{2},...,\lambda_{k}^{2}
be the non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{i}^{2}
and
E_{1},..,E_{k}
the associated eigenspaces. Note that this decomposition does not depend on
i
since
A_{i}^{2}=A_{j}^{2}
for all
i,j
. Then each
A_{i}
decomposes as a direct sum
A_{i}=\bigoplus_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}A_{i}^{(\ell)}
, where
A_{i}^{(\ell)}
are symmetric endomorphisms of
E_{\ell}
. Equation (
7
) then becomes

A_{i}^{(\ell)}A_{j}^{(\ell)}+A_{j}^{(\ell)}A_{i}^{(\ell)}=2\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{id}_{E_{\ell}},

from which the first part of the theorem follows. The second part follows from

dP_{i}(\nabla\|\nabla P_{i}\|^{2})\,(x)=8\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\langle x,(A_{i}^{(\ell)})^{3}x\rangle=8\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}^{3}\langle x,A_{i}^{(\ell)}x\rangle.

Taking the quotient by
P_{i}(x)=\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\langle x,A_{i}^{(\ell)}x\rangle
gives a function independent of

if and only if the
\lambda_{\ell}
all agree.
∎

4.2
Minimal submanifolds from quadratic harmonic morphisms

We briefly describe how pulling back subvectorspaces
V\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
by umbillic quadratic harmonic morphisms recovers classical minimal cones. For simplicity we assume the harmonic morphism is full:

Definition 4.8
.

A map
f:\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
is called
full
if

\spn\{(\nabla\langle f,v\rangle)\,(x)\mid x\in\mathds{R}^{m},v\in\mathds{R}^{n}\}=\mathds{R}^{m},

i.e.
f
does not factor over any subvectorspace of
\mathds{R}^{m}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
V\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be a subvectorspace of dimension
k
and
P:\mathds{R}^{m}\to\mathds{R}^{n}
a full umbillic quadratic harmonic morphism. Then
P^{-1}(V)\cap S^{m-1}
is a closed minimal submanifold of
S^{m-1}
of co-dimension
n-k
.

Proof.

P^{-1}(V)\setminus\{x\in\mathds{R}^{m}\mid\|dP\|^{2}(x)=0\}
is a minimal submanifold by
Theorem

1.4
. Since
P
is full one has
\|dP\|^{2}(x)=0
if and only if
x=0
from
Theorem

4.6
(recall that the
A_{i}
are invertible). Then
P^{-1}(V)\setminus\{0\}
is a minimal conical submanifold with closure
P^{-1}(V)
. It follows that
P^{-1}(V)\cap S^{m-1}
is a closed minimal submanifold.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

Note that if
n\geq 2
then
m
is always even by

1
. We assume this to be the case, then:

1.

If
\dim(V)=m-1
then

is congruent to the Simons cone

. If

then

is an area-minimising cone in

[

]
.

2.

If

then

is congruent to the complex quadric, that is the cone

over the Stiefel-manifold of two frames

, since it is calibrated by a power of the Kähler form. See also
[

]
for a proof using Lawlor’s curvature criterion
[

.

3.

More generally

is the cone

over a focal manifold of an isoparametric foliation of

of FKM-type
[

]
.

We now give the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
m\geq 32
and
n\geq 4
. For
A_{1},...,A_{2n}\in M_{m\times m}(\mathds{R})
a Clifford system the cone

C^{4}_{m,n}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in\mathds{R}^{m}\ \middle|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,A_{i}x\rangle^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,A_{n+i}x\rangle^{2}\right\}

is an area-minimising hypersurface in
\mathds{R}^{m}
.

Remark 4.12
.

The cone

defined in

4.11
is equal to

, the pullback of the Simons cone

by a full umbillic quadratic harmonic morphism

. As such the mean curvature of

vanishes at all points of

, regardless of the values of
(m,n)
.

Remark 4.13
.

1.

The singular set of
P^{-1}(S_{n})
is given by

, which is
m-2n
dimensional. Since the singular set of an area-minimising hypersurface of
\mathds{R}^{m}
is at most of dimension

[

]
, the condition

in

is clearly a necessary condition for

to be an area-minimiser.

2.

There exists a Clifford system
A_{1},...,A_{2n}\in M_{m\times m}(\mathds{R})
if and only if

for some

, here

is the number from
Table

. Since
\underline{m}(2\cdot 4)=16
and

if

, it follows that

exhausts all possibilities except

.

The simplest way to prove

is to use the method of subcalibrations. This method was developed by De Philippis and Paolini
[

]
to provide a simple proof that the Simons cone is area-minimising. For convenience of the reader we briefly review the relevant definitions and theorems in

.

4.3
The method of subcalibrations

Definition 4.14
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathds{R}^{m}
be open and
E\subset\Omega
be open.

1.

The
perimeter
of
E
in
\Omega
is

P(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\mathrm{div}(g)\ \middle|\ g\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{m}),\sup_{x}\|g(x)\|\leq 1\right\}

2.

E
is
minimal
if for all

bounded and open one has

P(E\cap A,\Omega\cap A)\leq P(F\cap A,\Omega\cap A)\quad\text{whenever $(F\cup E)\setminus(F\cap E)\Subset A$}.

3.

E
is
subminimal
if for all
A\subseteq\Omega
bounded and open one has

P(E\cap A,\Omega\cap A)\leq P(F\cap A,\Omega\cap A)\quad\text{whenever $F\subseteq E$, $E\setminus F\Subset A$}.

4.

Suppose
\partial E
has
C^{2}
regularity. A vectorfield
\xi:\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{m}
is called a
subcalibration
of
E
if

(i)

\xi(x)
is equal to the exterior normal of
\partial E
for all
x\in\partial E
.

(ii)

\mathrm{div}(\xi)\,(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in E
.

(iii)

\|\xi(x)\|\leq 1
for all
x\in\Omega
.

Remark 4.15
.

E
is subminimal if and only if
\partial E
is area-minimising with respect to variations that go into the set
E
, i.e. inward variations.

(
[

)
.

Let

A\cap(\{f<0\}\setminus\{f<-\epsilon\})
converges to
0
by monotone continuity of measures.
∎

We now complete the proof:

Proof of
Theorem

4.11
.

Combining
Lemmas

4.18
and
4.19
we find that
\{f<0\}
is subminimal. Just as in
Lemma

4.17
one proves that
\mathrm{div}(\frac{-\nabla f}{\|\nabla f\|})
is a subcalibration of
\{f>\epsilon\}
, repeating the argument of
Lemmas

4.18
and
4.19
then shows that
\{f>0\}
is also subminimal.
Theorem

4.11
then follows from point 2. of
Theorem

4.16
.
∎

Appendix A
Local existence of minimal submanifolds

Theorem A.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold,
p\in M
, and
V\subset T_{x}M
a subvectorspace. Then there is a minimal submanifold
B\subset M
with
x\in M
and
T_{x}B=V
.

The proof is essentially identical to a proof given by Fuglede (Lemma A.1.1 of the Appendix of
[
BW03
]
), showing the existence of harmonic functions with prescribed derivatives any point in a Riemannian manifold. While the mean curvature equation is non-linear and significantly more complicated than the Laplacian, both have the same linearisation. Local solutions can then be found by a zooming-in procedure and the Banach space implicit function theorem.

We take a coordinate chart of the form

B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k}\subset\mathds{R}^{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k}=\mathds{R}^{k}

so that
p
corresponds to the point
(0,0)
and
V
to the plane
\mathds{R}^{m-k}\times\{0\}
. Without loss of generality one may assume that the coordinate frame is synchronous at
(0,0)
, i.e. the metric is of the form
g=g_{\textrm{euc}}+O(\|x\|^{2})
.

Definition A.2
.

1.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\ell\geq 2
. We define

\displaystyle E_{1}=\{F\colon B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\to\mathds{R}^{k}\in C^{\ell,\alpha}\mid F(0)=0,dF(0)=0,d^{2}F(0)=0\},

\displaystyle E_{2}=\{F\colon B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\to\mathds{R}^{k}\in C^{\ell-2,\alpha}\mid F(0)=0\}.

2.

For
\epsilon\in(-1,1)
,
x\in B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k}
we let
g_{\epsilon}(x)\vcentcolon=g(\epsilon x)
.

3.

For
F\in E_{1}
we let
\Gamma(F)
denote the graph
\{(x,F(x))\mid x\in B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\}
. For
\epsilon\in(-1,1)
we let
H(\epsilon,F)\in E_{2}
denote
m-k+1
,…,
m
components of the mean curvature vector of
\Gamma(F)
with respect to
g_{\epsilon}
.

Remark A.3
.

1.

Since
F(0)=0
,
dF(0)=0
for all
F\in E_{1}
one has
(0,0)\in\Gamma(F)
and
T_{(0,0)}\Gamma(F)=\mathds{R}^{m-k}\times\{0\}
. The condition
d^{2}F(0)=0
further implies that the second fundamental form of
\Gamma(F)
vanishes at
(0,0)
.

2.

E_{i}
are Banach spaces when equipped with the
C^{\ell,\alpha}
resp.
C^{\ell-2,\alpha}
norms coming from the euclidean metric of
B_{1}(0)_{m-k}
.

3.

For
\epsilon\neq 0
one has that
(B_{1}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k},g_{\epsilon})
is isometric to
(B_{|\epsilon|}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k},\frac{g}{\epsilon^{2}})
, and thus has the same minimal submanifolds as
g\lvert_{B_{|\epsilon|}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k}}
.
g_{0}
is the euclidean metric.

4.

H\colon(-1,1)\times E_{1}\to E_{2}
is continuously differentiable, since
H(\epsilon,F)\,(x)
is given by a (complicated) expression depending smoothly on the arguments
F(x)
,
dF(x)
,
d^{2}F(x)
, and
g_{\epsilon}(x,F(x))=g(\epsilon x,\epsilon F(x))
,
dg_{\epsilon}
,
d^{2}g_{\epsilon}
.

Note that

1.

H(\epsilon,F)=0
if and only if the graph
\Gamma(F)
is minimal with respect to
g_{\epsilon}
.

2.

H(0,\mathbf{0})=0
for the constant function
\mathbf{0}
.

3.

More generally if
H(\epsilon,F)^{\alpha}
denotes the
\alpha
-component of
H(\epsilon,F)
for
\alpha\in\{1,..,k\}
one has

H(0,F)^{\alpha}=\sum_{ij=1}^{m-k}L^{ij}\partial_{ij}F^{\alpha}-\sum_{abij=1}^{m-k}\sum_{\beta=1}^{k}L^{ij}\partial_{ij}F^{\beta}F^{\beta}_{a}L^{ab}F^{\alpha}_{b},

here
L=(\mathds{1}_{(m-k)\times(m-k)}+dF^{T}\cdot dF)^{-1}
is the first fundamental form of
\Gamma(F)
. The complicated second summand arises from the fact that the mean curvature arises from taking the projection of
\sum_{ij}L^{ij}\partial_{ij}F^{\alpha}\,e_{\alpha}
to the normal bundle of
\Gamma(F)
, here
e_{\alpha}
denotes the standard basis of
\mathds{R}^{k}
.

4.

Denoting with
D_{2}H(\epsilon,F)\,K
the Gateux-derivative of
H
at the point
(\epsilon,F)
in the direction
K\in E_{1}
, one then finds:

D_{2}H(0,\mathbf{0})\,K=\sum_{i=1}^{m-k}\partial_{ii}K.

Using the Green’s function of the Laplacian of the unit ball
B_{1}(0)_{m-k}
one can construct a right-inverse for the Laplacian
E_{1}\to E_{2}
,
K\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m-k}\partial_{ii}K
. See e.g. the proof of Lemma A.1.1 in
[
BW03
]
for an explicit formula. This is where the spaces
C^{\ell,\alpha}
, rather than just
C^{\ell}
, are required.

E_{1}
then splits as a direct sum of the kernel of the Laplacian and the image of the right-inverse, which is also a Banach space. The Banach space implicit function theorem shows the equation
H(\epsilon,\cdot)=0
admits solutions for
\epsilon
in some neighbourhood of
0
. These solutions are then minimal
C^{\ell,\alpha}
-submanifolds of
(B_{|\epsilon|}(0)_{m-k}\times\mathds{R}^{k},g)\subset(M,g)
. Since the Riemannian metric is smooth these manifolds are also smooth by elliptic regularity, cf. Theorem 4.4 of Chapter 14 in
[
Tay23
]
. This completes the proof of
Theorem

A.1
.∎
