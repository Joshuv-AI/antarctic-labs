A Banach space with an unconditional basis which is not slicely countably determined
Marcus Lõo, Yoël Perreau

Abstract
. In this note, we study the geometry of the unit ball of the Banach space generated by the adequate family of all subsets of branches of the infinite binary tree, and answer several open questions related to slicely countably determined Banach spaces. Our main result is that the binary tree space is an example of a Banach space with an unconditional basis which fails to be slicely countably determined. In particular, it provides an example of a non slicely countably determined separable Banach space which contains no isomorphic copy of a space with the Daugavet property. We also exhibit some other geometric features of this space: we prove that its unit ball is dentable, that it has numerical index 1, and that the points of continuity of its unit ball form a weakly dense set. Finally, we show that the binary tree space contains a non-convex subset which is slicely countably determined, but does not admit a countable π \pi -base for its relative weak topology, and that there is a 2-equivalent renorming of this space whose unit ball fails to be slicely countably determined.

Primary 46B04; Secondary 46B20, 46B22, 46B25

1.
Introduction

Slicely countably determined Banach spaces (SCD spaces for short) were introduced in
[
AKM+10
]
to provide a class of separable Banach spaces that would contain both spaces with the Radon–Nikodým property (RNP for short) and spaces not containing an isomorphic copy of the space
\ell_{1}
, as well as other natural spaces constructed out of these such as
\ell_{1}(c_{0})
or
c_{0}(\ell_{1})
(see Section
2
for definitions). Incidentally, the corresponding property for sets and operators also provided a powerful tool for the study of some classical properties of Banach spaces such as the Daugavet and alternative Daugavet properties, numerical index 1, or spear operators. Given a (real or complex) Banach space
X
, we denote its topological dual as
X^{*}
, and its unit ball and unit sphere as
B_{X}
and
S_{X}
, respectively. Recall that
X
has the
Daugavet property
if every rank 1 operator
T:X\to X
satisfies the equation

\left\lVert\Id+T\right\rVert=1+\left\lVert T\right\rVert,

and the
alternative Daugavet property
if every rank 1 operator
T:X\to X
satisfies the equation

\max_{\theta\in\mathbb{T}}\left\lVert\Id+\theta T\right\rVert=1+\left\lVert T\right\rVert,

where
\Id
denotes the identity operator of
X
and
\mathbb{T}
the set of modulus one scalars. Also, the Banach space
X
is said to have
numerical index 1
if every bounded linear operator
T:X\to X
has numerical radius 1, i.e. satisfies the equation

\left\lVert T\right\rVert=\sup\{\left\lvert f(Tx)\right\rvert\colon x\in S_{X},\ f\in S_{X^{*}},\ f(x)=1\}.

Spaces with numerical index 1 satisfy the alternative Daugavet property, but the converse is known to be false in general. However, when the unit ball
B_{X}
of the considered space
X
is an SCD set, then the latter two properties coincide (and in fact coincide with a stronger geometric property called lushness, see
[
AKM+10
]
for more details). We refer to the monographs
[
KMMP18
,
KMSRZW25
]
for a detailed study of these properties and their applications to the study of bounded linear operators and the geometry of Banach spaces.

So far, the only known property preventing a separable Banach space from being SCD is the Daugavet property. In particular, the following questions were raised in
[
AKM+10
]
and subsequently in
[
KPW18
]
.

Question 1.1
.

Let
X
be a separable Banach space which fails to be SCD. Does
X
have to contain an isomorphic copy of a Banach space with the Daugavet property?

Question 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with an unconditional basis. Is
X
necessarily an SCD space?

It is a well known fact that spaces with the Daugavet property do not embed isomorphically into Banach spaces with an unconditional basis, so a negative answer to Question
1.2
would also answer Question
1.1
. The authors of
[
AKM+10
]
provided many examples for which the answer to the latter question was positive (e.g. shrinking and boundedly complete unconditional sums of SCD spaces), and it was later proved in
[
KMMW13
]
that if
X
is a Banach space with a 1-unconditional basis, then the unit ball
B_{X}
of
X
is an SCD set. In particular, numerical index 1 and the alternative Daugavet property coincide in this context. Nevertheless, we will provide a negative answer to Question
1.2
, hence also to Question
1.1
, by producing an example of a Banach space with a 1-unconditional basis whose positive unit ball is not an SCD set.

Our target will be the so-called binary tree space
X_{T}
, which can be seen as the Banach space generated by the adequate family of subsets of branches of an infinite binary tree
T
in the sense of Talagrand (see
[
Tal79
]
and
[
Tal84
]
). There are several reasons that make this space particularly relevant to Question
1.2
. First and foremost, this space is connected dually to the stopping time Banach space, introduced by Rosenthal in an unpublished manuscript, and which was shown by Bang and Odell in
[
BO89
]
to be universal for spaces with a 1-unconditional basis and isometric embeddings. In particular, the latter implies that the existence of a Banach space with an unconditional basis which would fail to be SCD is actually equivalent to the fact that
X_{T}
fails to be SCD. Second, a modified version
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
of the binary tree was shown in
[
ALMT21
]
to have some striking geometric properties, namely it has a 1-unconditional basis and contains a weakly dense subset of Daugavet points (see
[
AHLP20
]
for the definition of Daugavet points), pointing towards a potential non-SCDness in the space. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

The positive unit ball
B_{X_{T}}^{+}
of the binary tree space is not an SCD set. In particular,
X_{T}
is a space with an unconditional basis which fails to be SCD.

We will also show that the symmetrization of the set
B_{X_{T}}^{+}
fails to be SCD, which in particular yields the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a Banach space
Y\simeq X_{T}
with a 2-unconditional basis such that
B_{Y}
is not an SCD set.

Last, we obtain a few results related to the notion of countable
\pi
-bases (see Section
2
for the definition). As observed in
[
AKM+10
]
, if a convex bounded non-empty subset
A
of a Banach space
X
has a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology, then the set
A
is SCD. On the other hand, if
A
is separable and if
(A,w)
fails to have a countable
\pi
-base, then
A
must contain a sequence equivalent to the canonical basis of
\ell_{1}
. It is an open question, asked in
[
AKM+10
]
and
[
KPW18
]
, whether every convex bounded set which is SCD also has a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, it is also unknown whether or not the unit ball of a space with a 1-unconditional basis has a countable
\pi
-base for its relative weak topology. We will show that the strengthening of the alternative Daugavet property introduced in
[
LLM+25
]
naturally prevents the existence of a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology of the unit ball of the considered space. In particular, all these spaces have to contain an isomorphic copy of
\ell_{1}
, which was left as an open question in
[
LLM+25
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the super alternative Daugavet property. Then
(B_{X},w)
fails to have a countable
\pi
-base. In particular,
X
contains a copy of
\ell_{1}
.

Using similar ideas, we will also exhibit a non-convex subset
\Sigma
of
B_{X_{T}}
which is SCD, but has no countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. On the other hand, we will show that
B_{X_{T}}=\cconv(\Sigma)
has a countable
\pi
-base for its relative weak topology. We show analogous results for the modified binary tree space and the countably branching tree space
X_{T_{\infty}}
, obtaining the following.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X=X_{T},X_{\mathfrak{M}}
or
X_{T_{\infty}}
. Then
(B_{X},w)
has a countable
\pi
-base.

Let us end this section with a quick description of the structure of the paper. In Section
2
, we introduce all the necessary definitions, and provide a short proof of the fact that the unit ball of a space with the Daugavet property fails to be SCD. Then we show how a similar scheme leads to Theorem
1.5
. Our main results are presented in Section
3
, where we conduct a detailed study of the geometric properties of the unit ball of the binary tree space. In Subsection
3.1
, we show that
B_{X_{T}}
is dentable, and more precisely, that it is the closed convex hull of its strongly exposed points. As a consequence, we obtain that
X_{T}
has the alternative Daugavet property, hence numerical index 1. In Subsection
3.2
, we prove Theorem
1.3
by showing that the positive unit ball of the space
X_{T}
behaves geometrically like a “Daugavet set”, and exhibit a non-convex SCD subset of
B_{X_{T}}
which has no countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. In Subsection
3.3
, we recall the definition of SCD points, and study these in
B_{X_{T}}^{+}
and its symmetrization
C:=\cconv(B_{X_{T}}^{+}\cup B_{X_{T}}^{-})
to show that the latter set also fails to be SCD. In particular, we obtain a 2-unconditional equivalent renorming of
X_{T}
whose unit ball is not SCD, yielding Theorem
1.4
. Finally, in Subsection
3.4
, we prove that the points of continuity of
B_{X_{T}}
and of
B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}}
are weakly dense, and infer that the relative weak topologies of these two sets admit a countable
\pi
-base. We extend the latter result to the countably branching tree space in Section
4
. The unit ball of this space has no points of continuity, but we obtain nonetheless the existence of a
\pi
-base by using a reduction argument which shows that every functional on
X_{T_{\infty}}
is essentially supported on a finitely branching subtree. We end the paper with some open questions and a few additional miscellaneous results related to the topic in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

We will use standard Banach space notation following
[
FHH+11
]
. For simplicity, we will consider real Banach spaces only. Let
X
be a Banach space and
A
a bounded non-empty subset of
X
. We denote respectively by
\conv(A)
and
\cconv(A)
the convex hull of
A
and its closure, by
\spn(A)
and
[A]
the linear span of
A
and its closure. A
slice
of
A
is a non-empty intersection of
A
with an open half-space, or in other words a subset of
A
of the form

S(A,f,\delta):=\{x\in A\colon f(x)>\sup f(A)-\delta\},

where
f\in X^{*}
and
\delta>0
. We say that
x\in A
is

(1)

an
extreme point
of
A
(writing
x\in\ext(A)
) if
x
does not belong to the interior of any segment of
A
;

(2)

a
point of continuity
of
A
(writing
x\in\PC(A)
) if the identity mapping
\Id\colon(A,w)\to(A,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
is continuous at
x
(that is, if
x
is contained in relatively weakly open subsets of
A
of arbitrarily small diameter);

(3)

a
denting point
of
A
(writing
x\in\dent(A)
) if
x
is contained in slices of
A
of arbitrarily small diameter;

(4)

a
strongly exposed point
of
A
(writing
x\in\strexp(A)
) if there exists
f\in X^{*}
such that for all sequences
(x_{n})\subseteq A
,
f(x_{n})\rightarrow f(x)
if and only if
x_{n}\rightarrow x
in norm.

We say that a subset
G
of
A
(respectively a family
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
of elements of
A
) is
generating
for
A
if
A\subseteq\cconv(G)
(respectively
A\subseteq\cconv\{x_{i}\colon i\in I\}
). By the Hahn–Banach theorem,
G
is generating for
A
if and only if every slice of
A
contains an element of
G
. In particular, a subset
G
of
B_{X}
is generating for
B_{X}
if and only if it is norming for
X^{*}
. We say that a collection
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of non-empty subsets of
A
is
determining
for
A
if every
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
is generating for
A
. Equivalently, the collection
\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is determining for
A
if and only if every slice of
A
contains one of the sets
A_{i}
. The set
A
is said to be
slicely countably determined
(SCD set for short) if it admits a countable determining collection of slices.

In
[
AKM+10
, Remark 2.7]

[
KMMP18
, Proposition 7.20]
it was observed that a set
A
is SCD if and only if
\cconv(A)
is SCD. Furthermore, it follows from Bourgain lemma (see
[
GGMS87
, Lemma II.1]
) that if
A
is assumed to be convex, then SCDness is equivalent to the existence of a countable determining collection of relatively weakly open subsets. Prototypical examples of SCD sets are separable dentable sets, i.e. sets
A
for which
\dent(A)
is generating, and separable sets not containing a sequence equivalent to the canonical basis of
\ell_{1}
. This notion was first introduced in
[
AKM+10
]
for convex sets, and was later extended to non-convex sets in
[
KPW18
]
. A separable Banach space
X
is said to be
slicely countably determined
(SCD space for short) if every bounded non-empty subset of
X
is SCD. Considering the examples of SCD sets mentioned before, we have that separable Banach spaces with the RNP (spaces where every bounded convex and closed subset is dentable) and separable spaces not containing an isomorphic copy
\ell_{1}
(in particular, spaces with a separable dual) are SCD. A thorough study of SCD sets and spaces is presented e.g. in
[
KMSRZW25
, Chapter 10]
.

Concerning the Daugavet property and the alternative Daugavet property, let us recall the following well known geometric characterizations via slices of the unit ball.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
KSSW00
]
,
[
MO04
]
)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Then

•

X
has the Daugavet property if and only if
\underset{y\in S}{\sup}\;||x+y||=2
for every
x\in S_{X}
and every slice
S
of
B_{X}
;

•

X
has the alternative Daugavet property if and only if
\underset{y\in S}{\sup}\;\underset{\theta\in\mathbb{T}}{\max}\;||x+\theta y||=2
for every
x\in S_{X}
and every slice
S
of
B_{X}
.

It is known that spaces with the Daugavet property are never SCD (see
[
AKM+10
, Example 2.13]
). Using the characterization above, we provide a short sketch proof below for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the Daugavet property. Then
B_{X}
fails to be SCD.

Proof.

Since
X
has the Daugavet property, then given any countable collection
\{S_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of slices of
B_{X}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we can find
(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\lVert x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\right\rVert>n-\varepsilon
.

More precisely, let
(\varepsilon_{n})
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\varepsilon_{n}<\varepsilon
. Then we can construct inductively the sequence
(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
in such a way that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\lVert x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\right\rVert>n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}.

Indeed, we can first pick an arbitrary
x_{1}\in S_{1}
, and assuming that
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
have been constructed in such a way, then we can just apply the Daugavet property to the point
x=\frac{x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}}{\left\lVert x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\right\rVert}
, the slice
S_{n+1}
and
\xi=\frac{\varepsilon}{\left\lVert x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}\right\rVert}
to get the point
x_{n+1}
we were looking for.

Now this means that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
f_{n}\in S_{X^{*}}
such that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have
f_{n}(x_{i})>1-\varepsilon
. So if
f\in B_{X^{*}}
is a cluster point of the sequence
(f_{n})
for the weak
∗
topology, then we have that every
x_{n}
belongs to the (closed and convex) set
\{x\in B_{X}\colon f(x)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon\}
. In particular,
0\notin\cconv\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, and
\{S_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not determining for
B_{X}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let us note that in the above proof, we would get the exact same conclusion if we were to replace the unit ball of the space
X
by some non-empty closed convex subset
A
which contains
0
and satisfies the following condition: for every
\varepsilon>0
,
a\in A\cap S_{X}
, and
S
slice of
A
, there exists
b\in S
such that
\left\lVert a+b\right\rVert\geqslant 2-\varepsilon
. Surprisingly, we will show in the next section that such “Daugavet sets” may be found in the unit ball of Banach spaces with a 1-unconditional basis.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space. Recall that a collection
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\tau
of non-empty open subsets of
X
is a called a
\pi
-base
for
(X,\tau)
if every non-empty open subset of
X
contains one of the open sets
V_{i}
. Equivalently, the collection
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a
\pi
-base for
(X,\tau)
if and only if every
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}V_{i}
is
\tau
-dense in
X
, i.e. is such that
X=\overline{\{x_{i}\colon i\in I\}}^{\tau}
.

In particular, let
X
be a Banach space and
A
be a bounded non-empty subset of
X
. If
(A,w)
has a countable
\pi
-base, then
A
admits a countable determining collection of non-empty relatively weakly open subsets. As previously mentioned, if
A
is additionally assumed to be convex, then the latter property implies that
A
is SCD. On the other hand, let us point out that the example from
[
KMSRZW25
, Proposition 10.7.6]
actually also shows that for non-convex sets, the existence of a countable
\pi
-base does not always implies SCDness. Prototypical examples of sets which admit a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology are separable sets in which points of continuity are weakly dense, and separable sets not containing a sequence equivalent to the canonical basis of
\ell_{1}
. The latter is a consequence of some deep topological studies from Todorčević (see the proof of
[
AKM+10
, Theorem 2.22]
for more details). To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is actually the only known way of proving that a separable set which fails to be SCD has to contain such a sequence. Let us also point out that it was proved in
[
AKM+10
, Section 6.2]
that every non-empty bounded convex subset of
c_{0}(\ell_{1})
or
\ell_{1}(c_{0})
has a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology.

From the above we know that the unit ball of a Banach space with the Daugavet property fails to have a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. It turns out that we can extend this result to all spaces with the super alternative Daugavet property, introduced in
[
LLM+25
]
. Recall that an infinite-dimensional Banach space
X
has the
super alternative Daugavet property
(super ADP for short) if for every
x\in S_{X}
and every non-empty relatively weakly open subset
W
of
B_{X}
we have

\underset{y\in S}{\sup}\;\underset{\theta\in\mathbb{T}}{\max}\;||x+\theta y||=2.

In
[
LLM+25
]
it is proved that the super ADP lies strictly between the Daugavet property and the alternative Daugavet property. The following theorem shows, in particular, that a Banach space with the super ADP contains an isomorphic copy of
\ell_{1}
, solving an open question from
[
LLM+25
]
. Let us point out that it is still currently open whether or not the unit ball of such a space can be SCD.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the super ADP. Then
(B_{X},w)
does not have a countable
\pi
-base. In particular,
X
contains an isomorphic copy of
\ell_{1}
.

Proof.

As above, it is easy to prove that since
X
has the super ADP, then given any countable collection
\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of non-empty relatively weakly open subsets of
B_{X}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we can find a sequence
(v_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}
and a sequence
(\theta_{n})\subseteq\{-1,1\}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\lVert\theta_{1}v_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}v_{n}\right\rVert>n-\varepsilon
. In particular, we can find
f_{n}\in S_{X^{*}}
such that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
f_{n}(\theta_{i}v_{i})>1-\varepsilon
. So if
f\in B_{X^{*}}
is a cluster point of the sequence
(f_{n})
for the weak
∗
topology, then we have that every
v_{n}
belongs to the (weakly closed) set
\{x\in B_{X}\colon\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert\geqslant 1-\varepsilon\}
. In particular,
0\notin\overline{\{v_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w}
, and
\{V_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is not a
\pi
-base for
(B_{X},w)
.

For the second part of the statement, recall that it was proved in
[
LLM+25
]
that there is a separable subspace
Y
of
X
(in fact an almost isometric ideal) which also has the super ADP. By the above,
(B_{Y},w)
fails to have a countable
\pi
-base, and it follows that
Y
has to contain a copy of
\ell_{1}
(see
[
KMSRZW25
, Theorem 10.1.21]
).
∎

3.
The binary tree space

Recall that a
tree
is a preordered set
T:=(T,\preceq)
with the property that all the sets of the form
\{s\preceq t\colon s\in T\}
with
t\in T
are well ordered. We say that an element
t\in T
is a
successor
of
s\in T
if
s\prec t
(that is,
s\preceq t
and
s\neq t
), and we say that
t
is an
immediate successor
of
s
if
s\prec t
and if there is no
r\in T
such that
s\prec r\prec t
. A
chain
of
T
is a well ordered non-empty subset of
T
, and an
antichain
of
T
is a non-empty subset of
T
composed of pairwise incomparable elements for
\preceq
. We also call
branch
of
T
any maximal chain in
T
.

In this section,
T:=\{\emptyset\}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{0,1\}^{n}
will be the infinite binary tree with its natural preorder
\preceq
. For every
t\in T
, we denote by
\left\lvert t\right\rvert
the length of the sequence
t
(with the convention that
\left\lvert\emptyset\right\rvert=0
) and by
t_{\lvert k}
the sequence obtained by taking the first
k
elements of
t
(
k\leqslant\left\lvert t\right\rvert
). Recall that
s\preceq t
if and only if
s=t_{\lvert k}
for some
k\leqslant\left\lvert t\right\rvert
. Given
s,t\in T
, we denote by
s\smallfrown t
the concatenation of the sequences
s
and
t
, i.e. the sequence of length
\left\lvert s\smallfrown t\right\rvert=\left\lvert s\right\rvert+\left\lvert t\right\rvert
obtained by taking first the
\left\lvert s\right\rvert
elements of
s
and then the
\left\lvert t\right\rvert
elements of
t
. We usually write
n:=(n)
the length-one elements of
T
and
s\smallfrown n:=s\smallfrown(n)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the collection of all chains in
T
. We consider the real Banach space
X:=X_{T}
obtained by taking the completion of the space
c_{00}(T)
of all finitely supported functions on
T
equipped with the norm given by

\left\lVert x\right\rVert:=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{t\in A}\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert

for every
x\in c_{00}(T)
. Observe that the collection
\mathcal{A}
forms an adequate family of subsets of
T
(see Section
5
for more details). In particular, for every
x\in X
, we can find a maximal subset
A
of
\mathcal{A}
(that is a branch of
T
) such that
\left\lVert x\right\rVert=\sum_{s\in A}\left\lvert x_{s}\right\rvert
. Furthermore, if
e_{t}
denotes the function on
T
taking value 1 at
t
and
0
elsewhere, then the family
(e_{t})_{t\in T}
is a 1-unconditional basis for
X
. Also note that for every branch
\beta
of
T
, we have that the sequence
(e_{t})_{t\in\beta}
is isometrically equivalent to the canonical basis of
\ell_{1}
, and that for every infinite antichain
\alpha
of
T
, we have that the sequence
(e_{t})_{t\in\alpha}
is isometrically equivalent to the canonical basis of
c_{0}
.

Throughout the text, we will use the following notation. Given any subset
A
of
X
, we denote by
A^{+}
the intersection of
A
with the positive cone
X^{+}
of
X
, and by
A^{-}
the intersection of
A
with the negative cone
X^{-}
of
X
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

L_{n}:=\{s\in T\colon\left\lvert s\right\rvert=n\},\quad T_{n}:=\{s\in T\colon\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leqslant n\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\perp_{n}:=T\setminus T_{n}=\{s\in T\colon\left\lvert s\right\rvert>n\}.

Also, for every
s\in T
, we set

T(s):=\{t\in T\colon t\succeq s\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{f}
the collection of all finite chains in
T
. Given
A\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
and
S\subseteq T
, we write

A\prec S

if
s\succ\max_{\preceq}(A)
for every
s\in S
, or in other words if
S\subseteq T(\max_{\preceq}(A))\setminus\{\max_{\preceq}(A)\}
.

Given a non-empty subset
S
of
T
, we denote by
P_{S}
the projection on the subspace
[e_{s}]_{s\in S}
. The following lemma is straightforward, and we will use it repeatedly without mention in the rest of the text.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-empty set,
\{S_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a family of pairwise totally incomparable subsets of
T
for
\preceq
, and
S:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}S_{\gamma}
. Then the set
\Gamma
is countable, and we have the
M
-decomposition

[e_{s}]_{s\in S}=\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}[e_{s}]_{s\in S_{\gamma}}\bigg)_{c_{0}}.

3.1.
The unit ball of the binary tree space is dentable

Since the space
X
has a 1-unconditional basis, then it follows from
[
KMMW13
, Theorem 3.1]
that
B_{X}
is an SCD set. In this subsection, we will prove that the strongly exposed points of
B_{X}
are generating, which implies that
B_{X}
is even dentable. As an application, we will see that the space
X
also has the alternative Daugavet property, hence numerical index 1.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x\in S_{X}
. Then
x
is a strongly exposed point of
B_{X}
if and only if there exists a finite maximal antichain
\alpha
of
T
such that
\left\lvert x(s)\right\rvert=1
for every
s\in\alpha
. In particular,
\ext(B_{X})=\strexp(B_{X})
.

Proof.

Clearly, a point
x\in S_{X}
is an extreme point of
B_{X}
if and only if the above condition is satisfied. Furthermore, assuming that this condition is satisfied, then we have that every branch of
T
intersects the support of
x
, and therefore the norm one functional

f=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert}\sum_{s\in\alpha}\sign(x(s))e^{*}_{s}

is strongly exposing at
x
.
∎

Let
\Pi
be the set of all strongly exposed points of
B_{X}
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

X_{n}:=\spn\{e_{t}\}_{t\in T_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{n}:=\Pi\cap X_{n}.

Lemma 3.3
.

The set
\Pi_{n}
is generating for
B_{X_{n}}
.

Proof.

Again, it is straightforward to check that
\Pi_{n}=\ext(B_{X_{n}})
. Since
\dim(X_{n})<\infty
, the conclusion immediately follows from the Minkowski theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The set
\Pi
is generating for
B_{X}
. In particular,
B_{X}
is dentable, hence SCD.

Proof.

This immediately follows from the previous result and the fact that
X=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}X_{n}}
. The in particular part holds because
\Pi\subseteq\dent(B_{X})
.
∎

With the latter result at hand, we can now easily show that the space
X
enjoys the alternative Daugavet property, hence numerical index 1.

Theorem 3.5
.

The space
X
has the alternative Daugavet property. In particular, it has numerical index 1.

Proof.

We will make use of Proposition
2.1
. Let
x\in S_{X}
and
S
be a slice of
B_{X}
. By the above result,
S
contains a strongly exposed point
y
of
B_{X}
. By Proposition
3.2
, we can find a finite maximal antichain
\alpha
such that
\left\lvert y(s)\right\rvert=1
for every
s\in\alpha
. Let
\beta
be a branch of
T
such that
\left\lVert x\right\rVert=\sum_{s\in\beta}\left\lvert x(s)\right\rvert
. Since
\alpha
is maximal, there exists (a unique)
t\in T
which belongs to both
\alpha
and
\beta
. Let
\theta:=\frac{\sign(x(t))}{\sign(y(t))}
. Then
\left\lvert x(t)+\theta y(t)\right\rvert=1+\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert
, and therefore

\left\lVert x+\theta y\right\rVert\geqslant\sum_{s\in\beta}\left\lvert x(s)+\theta y(s)\right\rvert=1+\sum_{s\in\beta}\left\lvert x(s)\right\rvert=2.

The conclusion follows.

For the second part of the statement, recall that it was proved in
[
AKM+10
, Theorem 4.4]
that every Banach space with the alternative Daugavet property whose unit ball is an SCD set satisfies a stronger geometric condition called lushness, and that this property is known to imply numerical index 1.
∎

3.2.
The binary tree space is not an SCD space

In the last subsection, we saw that the unit ball of
X
is an SCD set. In turns out however that its positive unit ball is not, which means that
X
is not an SCD space.

Theorem 3.6
.

The positive unit ball
B_{X}^{+}
of
X
is not an SCD set. In particular,
X
is a Banach space which fails to be SCD, but contains no isomorphic copy of a Banach space with the Daugavet property.

Proof.

We will prove that for every
x\in S_{X}^{+}
and every slice
S
of
B_{X}^{+}
, we can find an element
y\in S
such that
\left\lVert x+y\right\rVert=2
. In particular,
B_{X}^{+}
is a “Daugavet set” in the sense of Remark
2.3
, and thus it is not an SCD set. So take
x
and
S
in this way, and fix a finitely supported element
y\in S
. Wlog,
x
is also finitely supported. Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
be such that
\sum_{t\in A}x(t)=1
. Additionally, let
B\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
be such that
B\succ A
and
y(s)=0
for every
s\succ\max_{\preceq}(B)
. Then, select an infinite antichain
\alpha:=(\alpha(n))\succ B
of
T
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

y_{n}:=y+(1-\lambda)e_{\alpha(n)},

where
\lambda:=\sum_{t\in A\cup B}y(t)
. By construction,
y_{n}\in B_{X}^{+}
, and since
(e_{\alpha(n)})
is weakly null (it is equivalent to the canonical basis of
c_{0}
),
the sequence
(y_{n})
converges weakly to
y
. So there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
y_{n_{0}}
belongs to
S
. Now we have

\left\lVert x+y_{n_{0}}\right\rVert\geqslant\sum_{t\in A\cup B\cup\{\alpha(n_{0})\}}\left\lvert x(s)+y_{n_{0}}(s)\right\rvert=\sum_{t\in A}x(t)+\sum_{t\in A\cup B}y(t)+y(\alpha(n_{0}))=1+\lambda+(1-\lambda)=2.

The conclusion follows.

The second part of the statement follows from the fact that a Banach space with the Daugavet property cannot embed linearly into a Banach space with an unconditional basis (see
[
KSSW00
, Corollary 2.7]
).
∎

Using similar ideas, we can produce a (non-convex) subset of
B_{X}
which is SCD, but has no countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first known example of such a set.

Proposition 3.7
.

Consider the subset
\Sigma
of
B_{X}
given by

\Sigma:=\{x\in B_{X}\colon\forall s\in T,\ x(s)\in\{-1,0,1\}\}.

Then
\Sigma
is an SCD set, but
(\Sigma,w)
has no countable
\pi
-base.

Proof.

As already mentioned, to show that the set
\Sigma
is SCD, it is sufficient (in fact equivalent) to prove that its closed convex hull is an SCD set. Since
\Sigma
contains all the strongly exposed points of
B_{X}
, we get from Corollary
3.4
that
\cconv(\Sigma)=B_{X}
, which is indeed SCD.

To show that
\Sigma
has no countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology, we will establish that given any sequence
(V_{n})
of non-empty relatively weakly open subsets of
\Sigma
, we can find
(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V_{n}
and
(\theta_{n})\subseteq\{-1,1\}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left\lVert\theta_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}\right\rVert=n.

The conclusion then follows as in the proof of Theorem
2.4
. So fix such a sequence
(V_{n})
. We will construct the sequence
(x_{n})
inductively. For the first step, simply note that 0 is not an isolated point of
\Sigma
, because given any infinite antichain
\alpha:=(\alpha(n))
of
T
, we have that the sequence
(e_{\alpha(n)})
converges weakly to 0. So we can pick any non-zero
x_{1}\in V_{1}
and
\theta_{1}\in\{-1,1\}
. Now assume that we have constructed
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
and
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
such that
\left\lVert\theta_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}\right\rVert=n
. Then we can find
A\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
such that

\sum_{t\in A}\left\lvert\theta_{1}x_{1}(t)+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}(t)\right\rvert=n,

and
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\supp(x_{i})
. Observe that the former condition means that for every
t\in A
, all the non-zero
\theta_{i}x_{i}(t)
have the same sign. We claim that there exist
x_{n+1}\in V_{n+1}
and
s\in T
such that
\left\lvert x_{n+1}(s)\right\rvert=1
and
A\cup\{s\}\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
. Indeed, if an element
y\in V_{n+1}
would be such that
y(s)=0
for every such
s
, then we could simply pick an antichain
\alpha:=(\alpha(n))\succ A
and find
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
y+e_{\alpha(n_{0})}
would belong to
V_{n+1}
. Then
x_{n+1}=y+e_{\alpha(n_{0})}
would satisfy the required condition with
s=\alpha(n_{0})
. Now given such
x_{n+1}
and
s
, we are left with two cases to consider. If
s\notin A
, then, taking
\theta_{n+1}=1
, we have

\left\lVert\theta_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}+\theta_{n+1}x_{n+1}\right\rVert\geqslant\sum_{t\in A\cup\{s\}}\left\lvert\theta_{1}x_{1}(t)+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}(t)+x_{n+1}(t)\right\rvert=n+1,

so we are done. Otherwise, let
\theta_{n+1}\in\{-1,1\}
be such that
\theta_{n+1}x_{n+1}(s)
has the same sign as all the non-zero
\theta_{i}x_{i}(s)
. Then

\left\lvert\theta_{1}x_{1}(s)+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}(s)+\theta_{n+1}x_{n+1}(s)\right\rvert=\left\lvert\theta_{1}x_{1}(s)+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}(s)\right\rvert+1,

and it immediately follows that
\left\lVert\theta_{1}x_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}x_{n}+\theta_{n+1}x_{n+1}\right\rVert=n+1
, as we wanted.
∎

3.3.
A renorming of the binary tree space whose unit ball is not SCD

Our aim here is to prove that the set
C:=\cconv(B_{X}^{+}\cup B_{X}^{-})
is also not SCD. As a corollary, we will get an example of a Banach space with a 2-unconditional basis whose unit ball is not SCD.

First, let us observe that the set
\Pi^{+}
is not generating for
B_{X}^{+}
. Indeed, it is easy to show that if
\Omega
denotes the set
\Sigma\setminus\Pi
, then no point in
\Omega^{+}
belongs to the closed convex hull of
\Pi^{+}
. However, the set
\Sigma^{+}=\Omega^{+}\cup\Pi^{+}
is generating for
B_{X}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The set
\Sigma^{+}
is generating for
B_{X}^{+}
.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Sigma_{n}:=\Sigma\cap X_{n}
. Then it is straightforward to check that
\Sigma_{n}^{+}=\ext(B_{X_{n}}^{+})
. So the conclusion follows using the exact same argument as in Subsection
3.1
.
∎

Let us recall the concept of slicely countably determined points, which was introduced in
[
LLMR24
]
as a localization or point-wise version of the global SCD property to study some SCD phenomena in the non-separable setting. Given a bounded convex subset
A\subseteq X
, we say that
x\in A
is a
slicely countably determined point of
A
(SCD point of
A
for short, writing
x\in\SCD(A)
), if there is a countable collection of slices
\{S_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
A
such that every sequence
(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
is generating for the point
a
, meaning that
a\in\cconv\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. We refer to
[
LLMR24
]
for background on SCD points. In particular, it was proved there that the set
\SCD(A)
is always closed and convex, and that if
A
is additionally assumed to be separable, then
A
is SCD if and only if every element of
A
is an SCD point of
A
. In Theorem
3.6
, we have essentially proved that 0 is not an SCD point of the positive unit ball of
X
. We can actually produce more of these points.

Proposition 3.9
.

No element
x\in\Omega^{+}
is an SCD point of
B_{X^{+}}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega^{+}
and let
(S_{n})
be a sequence of slices of
B_{X}^{+}
. Then we can find
A\in\mathcal{A}_{f}
for which the set
S:=A\cup T(\max(A))
does not intersect the support of
x
. Let
P_{S}
be the norm one projection from
X
onto
[e_{s}]_{s\in S}
. Using the exact same argument as the one presented in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we can construct inductively a sequence
(x_{n})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{n}
of finitely supported elements satisfying

\left\lVert P_{S}(x_{1}+\dots+x_{n})\right\rVert=n

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, there exists
f\in S_{X^{*}}
supported on
S
(meaning that
f(e_{t})=0
for every
t\in T\setminus S
) such that
f(x_{n})=1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
S\cap\supp(x)=\emptyset
, we have
f(x)=0
, hence
x\notin\cconv\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
x\in S_{X}^{+}
. If
x
is an SCD point of
C
, then
x
is an SCD point of
B_{X^{+}}
. In particular,
C
is not an SCD set.

Proof.

Let
(S_{n})
be a sequence of slices of
C
which is determining for some element
x\in S_{X^{+}}
, and let
\mathbb{M}
be the set of all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for which
S_{n}
intersects
B_{X}^{+}
. Then the sequence
(S_{n}\cap B_{X}^{+})_{n\in\mathbb{M}}
is also determining for
x
. Indeed, let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{M}}\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{M}}S_{n}\cap B_{X}^{+}
, and fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathbb{M}
, pick an arbitrary
x_{n}\in S_{n}
. Since
(S_{n})
is a determining sequence for
x
in
C
, we can find
y\in\conv\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{M}\}
,
z\in\conv\{x_{n}\colon n\notin\mathbb{M}\}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
such that

\left\lVert x-(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)z)\right\rVert<\varepsilon.

Let
\beta
be a branch of
T
such that
\sum_{t\in\beta}x(t)=1
. Since
z\in B_{X}^{-}
, we have

1-\lambda\leqslant 1-\lambda\sum_{t\in\beta}y(t)-(1-\lambda)\sum_{t\in\beta}z(t)\leqslant\left\lVert x-(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)z)\right\rVert<\varepsilon.

Therefore,

\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert\leqslant\left\lVert x-(\lambda y+(1-\lambda)z)\right\rVert+2(1-\lambda)<3\varepsilon,

which means that
x\in\cconv\{x_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{M}\}
, as we wanted.

The last part of the claim follows from Proposition
3.9
, yielding that no
x\in\Omega^{+}
is an SCD point of
C
. By
[
LLMR24
, Lemma 2.8]

C
cannot be an SCD set.
∎

As a corollary, we the following.

Theorem 3.11
.

There exists a Banach space
Y
with a 2-unconditional basis whose unit ball is not SCD.

Proof.

Let
Y:=(X,{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})
, where
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is the equivalent norm on
X
given by the gauge (a.k.a. Minkowski functional) of the set
C
. Since
\frac{1}{2}B_{X}\subseteq C\subseteq B_{X}
, we have that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert\leqslant{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant 2\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. In particular, the family
(e_{t})_{t\in T}
is 2-unconditional in
Y
. Now by the above,
B_{Y}=C
is not an SCD set, so we are done.

∎

3.4.
The unit ball of the binary tree space has a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology

In this subsection, we show that the points of continuity of
B_{X}
are weakly dense in
B_{X}
. In particular,
(B_{X},w)
has countable
\pi
-base.

Proposition 3.12
.

A point
x\in S_{X}
is a point of continuity of
B_{X}
if and only if
\left\lVert P_{\beta}(x)\right\rVert=1
for every branch
\beta
of
T
.

Proof.

First, assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a branch
\beta
of
T
such that
\left\lVert P_{\beta}(x)\right\rVert<1-\varepsilon
. Since
x
belongs to the closure of
c_{00}(T)
, we can find
s\in\beta
such that
\left\lVert P_{T(s)}(x)\right\rVert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Let
\alpha:=(\alpha(n))\succ s
be an infinite antichain of
T(s)
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
x_{n}:=x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{\alpha(n)}
. By construction,
x_{n}\in B_{X}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
(x_{n})
converges to
x
weakly, and
\left\lVert x-x_{n}\right\rVert\geqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so
x
is not a point of continuity of
B_{X}
.

Second, assume that
\left\lVert P_{\beta}(x)\right\rVert=1
for every branch
\beta
of
T
, pick a net
(x_{a})
in
B_{X}
which converges weakly to
x
, and fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then take
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\left\lVert P_{\perp_{n}}(x)\right\rVert<\varepsilon
. Note that because of our assumption, this means that
\sum_{t\preceq s}\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert>1-\varepsilon
for every
s\in L_{n}
. Since
(x_{a})
converges weakly to
x
, we can find
a
in the corresponding directed set such that for every
b\geqslant a
and for every
s\in T_{n}
, we have
\left\lvert x_{b}(s)-x(s)\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{n+1}
. In particular,
\left\lVert P_{T_{n}}(x_{b}-x)\right\rVert<\varepsilon
. Furthermore, for every
s\in L_{n}
, we have

\sum_{t\preceq s}\left\lvert x_{b}(t)\right\rvert>\sum_{t\preceq s}\left\lvert x(t)\right\rvert-\varepsilon>1-2\varepsilon.

Therefore,
\left\lVert P_{\perp_{n}}(x_{b})\right\rVert<2\varepsilon
, and it immediately follows that
\left\lVert x_{b}-x\right\rVert<4\varepsilon
. So
(x_{a})
converges in norm to
x
, and
x
is a point of continuity of
B_{X}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the set of all branches of
T
. For every
\beta\in\mathcal{B}
and every
f\in X^{*}
, we set

l_{\beta}(f):=\limsup_{t\in\beta}\left\lvert f(e_{t})\right\rvert.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 3.13
.

For every
f\in X^{*}
, the series
\sum_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}l_{\beta}(f)
converges. In particular,
l_{\beta}(f)=0
for all but countably many
\beta
in
\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Let
(\beta_{j})_{j=1}^{k}
be a finite sequence in
\mathcal{B}
. Then we can find
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that every sequence
(s_{j})_{j=1}^{k}
in
\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\beta_{j}\cap\perp_{N})
is an antichain of
T
. In particular, for every such sequence, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left\lvert f(e_{s_{j}})\right\rvert=\left\langle f,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sign(f(e_{s_{j}}))e_{s_{j}}\right\rangle\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert.

Therefore,

\sum_{j=1}^{k}l_{\beta_{j}}(f)\leqslant\left\lVert f\right\rVert,

and conclusion follows.
∎

From this, we deduce that every functional
f\in X^{*}
has small values outside of a set of the form
T_{n}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}
in the following sense.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
f\in X^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
s\in\perp_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}
, we have
|f(e_{s})|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, there exists
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\in\mathcal{B}
such that
l_{\beta}(f)<\varepsilon
for every
\beta\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k}\}
. Suppose that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find
s\in\perp_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}
such that
\left\lvert f(e_{s})\right\rvert\geqslant\varepsilon
. Then we can construct inductively a sequence
(s_{i})
in
T\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{j}
such that
\left\lvert s_{i+1}\right\rvert>\left\lvert s_{i}\right\rvert
and
\left\lvert f(e_{s_{i}})\right\rvert\geqslant\varepsilon
for every
i
. Now observe that by the latter condition, there can be at most
\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}{\varepsilon}
of these points which are incomparable for
\preceq
. Therefore, we can find a branch
\beta
of
T
which contains infinitely many of the
s_{i}
. By construction,
\beta\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}\}
, and
l_{\beta}(f)\geqslant\varepsilon
, contradicting our initial claim.
∎

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
in the interval
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, we can always choose inductively
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}a_{i}
belongs to
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
for every
m\leqslant n
. The following lemma allows us, given
k
such sequences of points
a_{1}^{j},\dots,a_{n}^{j}\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
,
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, to select a common sequence of signs
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
for which all the sums
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}a_{i}^{j}
will belong to the interval
(-2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k}\varepsilon)
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
n>2
. For every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, let
a^{j}:=(a_{i}^{j})_{i=1}^{n}\in B_{\ell_{\infty}^{n}}
. Then there exists
\theta:=(\theta_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have

\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}a^{j}_{i}\right\rvert\leqslant 2^{k}.

Proof.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We will prove the statement by induction on
n
. First, notice that if
n\leqslant 2^{k}
, then every sequence of signs gives us what we want simply by the triangle inequality. Next, assume that the statement holds for
n\geqslant 2^{k}
, and fix
k
vectors
a^{1},\dots,a^{k}
in
B_{\ell_{\infty}^{n+1}}
. Notice that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
, the sequence
(\sign(a_{i}^{j}))_{j=1}^{k}
defines an element of
\{-1,1\}^{k}
(setting
\sign(0):=1
). Since by assumption
n+1>2^{k}=\left\lvert\{-1,1\}^{k}\right\rvert
, there are at least two such indices which define the same sequence. In other words, we can find
i_{1}<i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
such that
\sign(a^{j}_{i_{1}})=\sign(a^{j}_{i_{2}})
for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Set
\omega_{1}:=1
and
\omega_{2}:=-1
. Then we have
|\omega_{1}a^{j}_{i_{1}}+\omega_{2}a^{j}_{i_{2}}|\leqslant 1
for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Now define vectors
b^{j}=(b^{j}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\ell_{\infty}^{n}
by setting

b^{j}=\big(\omega_{1}a^{j}_{i_{1}}+\omega_{2}a^{j}_{i_{2}},a^{j}_{1},\dots,a^{j}_{i_{1}-1},a^{j}_{i_{1}+1},\dots,a^{j}_{i_{2}-1},a^{j}_{i_{2}+1},\dots,a^{j}_{n+1}\big),\;\;j\in\{1,\dots,k\}.

By construction, we have
||b^{j}||_{\infty}\leqslant 1
for every
j
. So by assumption, there exists
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
such that for every
j
, we have

\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}b^{j}_{i}\right\rvert\leqslant 2^{k}.

Let
\theta=(\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{i_{1}},\rho_{1}\omega_{1},\rho_{i_{1}+1},\dots,\rho_{i_{2}-1},\rho_{1}\omega_{2},\rho_{i_{2}},\dots,\rho_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n+1}
. Then, for every
j
, we have

\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\theta_{i}a^{j}_{i}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}b^{j}_{i}\right\rvert\leqslant 2^{k}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

With these tools at hand, we can now prove the main result of this subsection. First, we show that
0
belongs to the weak closure of the set
\PC(B_{X})
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Every neighborhood of
0
in the realtive weak topology of
B_{X}
contains a point of continuity of
B_{X}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in S_{X^{*}}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and

W=\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\{y\in B_{X}\colon|f_{j}(y)|<\varepsilon\}.

By Lemma
3.14
, we can find branches
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{l}\in\mathcal{B}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
so that for every
s\in\perp_{N}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i}
and every
j=\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have
|f_{j}(e_{s})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}
. Wlog, the points
\beta_{1}(N),\dots,\beta_{l}(N)
are incomparable for
\preceq
. Furthermore, by compactness, we can find
n_{2}>n_{1}>N
in
\mathbb{N}
such that for every
i,j
, we have

\left\lvert f_{j}(e_{\beta_{i}(n_{2})})-f_{j}(e_{\beta_{i}(n_{1})})\right\rvert<\varepsilon/l.

For every
i\in\{1,\dots,l\}
, let

S_{i}:=L_{n_{2}}\cap T(\beta_{i}(n_{1}))\setminus\{\beta_{i}(n_{2})\}=\{s\in L_{n_{2}}\setminus\{\beta_{i}(n_{2})\}\colon s\succeq\beta_{i}(n_{1})\}

and
S:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}S_{i}
. Also, let

R:=L_{n_{2}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}T(\beta_{i}(n_{1})).

By assumption, for every
t\in S\cup R
and
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have
\left\lvert f_{j}(e_{t})\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.15
, we can find
(\theta_{t})_{t\in S\cup R}\subseteq\{-1,1\}
such that for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have

\left\lvert\sum_{t\in S}\frac{1}{2}\theta_{t}f_{j}(e_{t})+\sum_{t\in R}\theta_{t}f_{j}(e_{t})\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

So consider the point
x
in
X
given by

x:=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{1}{2}(e_{\beta_{i}(n_{2})}-e_{\beta_{i}(n_{1})})+\sum_{t\in S}\frac{1}{2}\theta_{t}e_{t}+\sum_{t\in R}\theta_{t}e_{t}.

By construction,
x\in B_{X}
, and for every branch
\beta
of
T
, we have
\left\lVert P_{\beta}(x)\right\rVert=1
, so it follows from Proposition
3.12
that
x
is a point of continuity of
B_{X}
. Furthermore, by the above, we have that for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,

\left\lvert f_{j}(x)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lvert f_{j}(e_{\beta_{i}(n_{2})})-f_{j}(e_{\beta_{i}(n_{1})})\right\rvert+\left\lvert\sum_{t\in S}\frac{1}{2}\theta_{t}f_{j}(e_{t})+\sum_{t\in R}\theta_{t}f_{j}(e_{t})\right\rvert<\varepsilon.

So
x\in W
, as we wanted.
∎

For every
t\in T
, let
S_{t}
be the linear isometry from
X
onto
[e_{s}]_{s\in T(t)}
defined by the formula

S_{t}(e_{s}):=e_{t\smallfrown s}

for every
s\in T
, and extended by linearity and density to the whole space
X
. Using these shift operators and the previous result, we can now prove the following.

Theorem 3.17
.

The set
PC(B_{X})
is weakly dense in
B_{X}
. In particular,
(B_{X},w)
has a countable
\pi
-base.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.16
, there exists a net
(x_{a})
in
\PC(B_{X})
which converges weakly to 0. So let
y\in B_{X}
be a non-zero finitely supported element and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that such that
\supp(y)\subseteq T_{n-1}
. For every
t\in L_{n}
, let

\lambda_{t}:=\sum_{s\preceq t}\left\lvert y(s)\right\rvert,

and define

y_{a}:=y+\sum_{t\in L_{n}}(1-\lambda_{t})S_{t}(x_{a}).

for every
a
. By construction,
y_{a}\in B_{X}
, and
\left\lVert P_{\beta}(y_{a})\right\rVert=\lambda_{\beta(n)}+1-\lambda_{\beta(n)}=1
for every branch
\beta
of
T
, so
y_{a}\in\PC(B_{X})
by Proposition
3.12
. Since the
S_{t}
are bounded linear operators, the net
(y_{a})
converges weakly to
y
, so we are done.
∎

Remark 3.18
.

In
[
ALMT21
, Section 4]
, the authors produced another adequate family
\mathfrak{M}
on the unrooted binary tree
T^{*}=T\setminus\{\emptyset\}
by adding to
\mathcal{A}^{*}:=\{A\cap T^{*}\colon A\in\mathcal{A}\}
all the
\lambda
-segments and their subsets, i.e. subsets of
T^{*}
of the form
\{t\in A\colon t\preceq s\}\cup\{s\smallfrown 0,s\smallfrown 1\}
where
s
is an arbitrary point of
T
and
A\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. By this process, they not only removed all the strongly exposed points from the unit ball of
X
, but actually obtained a Banach space
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
with some remarkable geometric properties: this space has a 1-unconditional basis, the set
D
of all Daugavet points of
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
is weakly dense in
B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}}
(in particular,
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
has the slice diameter 2 property), and the set
D_{B}:=D\cap\PC(B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}})
is generating for
B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}}
(in particular,
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
fails the diameter 2 property). We would like to point out that the proof we presented in this section can easily be adapted to this setting to show that actually the set
D_{B}
is also weakly dense in
B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}}
(and consequently,
(B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}},w)
also has a countable
\pi
-base). Indeed, in order to prove that
\PC(B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}})
is weakly dense in
B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}}
, then the only part in the above scheme that requires non-trivial adjustments is the proof of Proposition
3.16
, because the elements we construct there are not in the unit ball of
X_{\mathfrak{M}}
. This can be fixed by initially asking

\left\lvert f_{j}(e_{s})\right\rvert<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{2k+1}}

for every
s\in\perp_{N}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{l}\beta_{i}
and every
j=\{1,\dots,k\}
, and then by replacing for every
t\in S\cup R
the point
e_{t}
which appears in the definition of the point
x
there by the point

y_{t}:=\sum_{s\in T^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}}\omega_{t,s}e_{t\smallfrown s},

where the
\omega_{t,s}
are signs which are obtained level by level from Lemma
3.15
in such a way that

\left\lvert f_{j}(y_{t})\right\rvert\leqslant\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\left\lvert\sum_{\left\lvert s\right\rvert=n}\omega_{t,s}f_{j}(e_{t\smallfrown s})\right\rvert\leqslant 2^{k}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{2k+1}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-n}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}

for every
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then we chose the sequence
(\theta_{t})_{t\in S\cup R}
by calling a second time to Lemma
3.15
to get

\left\lvert\sum_{t\in S}\frac{1}{2}\theta_{t}f_{j}(y_{t})+\sum_{t\in R}\theta_{t}f_{j}(y_{t})\right\rvert<2^{k}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We leave the details to the reader. To conclude, it remains to see that actually
D_{B}=\PC(B_{X_{\mathfrak{M}}})
. This is easily seen by combining several of the observations already made in
[
ALMT21
]
, but let us point out that this actually also immediately follows from the weak density and norm closedness of the set
D
. Indeed, if a point
x\in\PC(B_{X})
belongs to the weak closure of some subset
A
of
B_{X}
, then it actually belongs to the norm closure of
A
, so we get the desired result.

4.
The countably branching tree space

It is clear that the results from the previous section generalize to any infinite finitely branching tree (i.e. a tree such that every element of it has only finite amount of immediate successors). However, the situation is different when one allows infinite branching, because one then loses the weak density of the points of continuity in the unit ball of the associated Banach space. In particular, let

T_{\infty}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{N}^{k}
be the countably branching tree and
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
be the collection of all chains of
T_{\infty}
. In this case, the Banach space
X_{T_{\infty}}
generated by the family
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
has the strong diameter 2 property, meaning that all convex combination of slices (hence, by Bourgain lemma, all non-empty relatively weakly open subsets) of
B_{X_{T_{\infty}}}
have diameter 2. In particular,
B_{X_{T_{\infty}}}
has no point of continuity. Nevertheless, we will prove in this section that
(B_{X_{T_{\infty}}},w)
has a countable
\pi
-base. The key idea for this is the following lemma, which shows that every functional on
X_{\infty}
is essentially supported on a finitely branching tree.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in X_{T_{\infty}}^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
. There exists a finitely branching tree
T_{0}\subseteq T_{\infty}
such that
||P^{*}_{T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}}f||<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Fix a non-zero
f\in X_{T_{\infty}}^{*}
and
\varepsilon>0
. For convenience, given two subsets
A,B
of
T_{\infty}
, we will write
A\preceq B
if every element of
B
is a successor of some element of
A
. First, we construct inductively a sequence
(R_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
of finite subsets of
T_{\infty}\cup\{\emptyset\}
with
R_{0}=\{\emptyset\}
and such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the following conditions are met.

(1)

R_{k}\subseteq L_{k}
;

(2)

R_{k}\succeq R_{k-1}
;

(3)

||P^{*}_{S_{k}}f||<\varepsilon/2^{k}
, where
S_{k}=\bigcup_{s\in L_{k}\setminus R_{k}}T(s)
.

Indeed, let
k=0
or assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have found
R_{k}
satisfying the conditions (1), (2) and (3). Observe that

||P^{*}_{\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in L_{k+1}\\
s\succeq R_{k}\end{subarray}}T(s)}f||=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in L_{k+1}\\
s\succeq R_{k}\end{subarray}}||P^{*}_{T(s)}f||,

so we can find a finite subset
R_{k+1}\succeq R_{k}
of
L_{k+1}
such that

\sum_{s\in R_{k+1}}||P^{*}_{T(s)}f||>||P^{*}_{\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in L_{k+1}\\
s\succeq R_{k}\end{subarray}}T(s)}f||-\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}}.

In particular,

||P^{*}_{S_{k+1}}f||=||P^{*}_{\bigcup_{s\in L_{k+1}\setminus R_{k+1}}T(s)}f||=\sum_{s\in L_{k+1}\setminus R_{k+1}}||P^{*}_{T(s)}f||<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k+1}},

so
R_{k+1}
satisfies all the required conditions. Finally, let
T_{0}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}R_{k}
. Then
T_{0}
is a finitely branching subtree of
T_{\infty}
, and

T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\bigcup_{s\in L_{k}\setminus R_{k}}T(s)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}S_{k}.

Since the
S_{k}
are totally incomparable, it follows that

||P^{*}_{T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}}f||=||P^{*}_{\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}S_{k}}f||=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}||P^{*}_{S_{k}}f||<\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{k}}=\varepsilon,

as we wanted.
∎

With this result at hand, we can prove the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

The set
(B_{X_{T_{\infty}}},w)
has a countable
\pi
-base.

Proof.

We will show that every non-empty relatively weakly open subset
W
of
(B_{X_{T_{\infty}}},w)
contains a subset of the form

W(x_{0},\delta_{0}):=\bigcap_{s\in\supp(x_{0})}\Big\{y\in B_{X_{T_{\infty}}}\colon\left\lvert x_{0}(s)-y(s)\right\rvert<\delta_{0}\Big\},

where
x_{0}\in X_{T_{\infty}}
is a finitely supported element and
\delta>0
. Take
W
of the form

W:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\{y\in B_{X}\colon|f_{i}(x-y)|<\varepsilon\},

where
x\in X_{T_{\infty}}
is a finitely supported element,
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in S_{X^{*}}
and
\varepsilon>0
. By the previous lemma, we can find a finitely branching subtree
T_{0}
of
T_{\infty}
such that
||P^{*}_{T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}}f_{i}||<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Wlog,
\supp(x)\subseteq T_{0}
. Let
X_{0}:=[e_{s}]_{s\in T_{0}}
and

W_{0}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\Big\{z\in B_{X_{0}}\colon|f_{i}(x-z)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big\}.

As already mentioned, we can use the scheme from the previous section with the finitely branching tree
T_{0}
to show that
W_{0}
contains a finitely supported point of continuity
x_{0}
of
B_{X_{0}}
. A quick look at the proof of Proposition
3.12
will convince the reader that in this case, the diameter of the set
W(x_{0},\delta)\cap B_{X_{0}}
goes to 0 as
\delta
goes to 0. Pick
\delta_{0}>0
such that
\diam(W(x_{0},\delta_{0})\cap B_{X_{0}})<\varepsilon/4
and pick
y\in W(x_{0},\delta_{0})
. Note that since
\supp(x_{0})\subseteq T_{0}
, we have that
P_{T_{0}}y\in W(x_{0},\delta_{0}),
and thus
\left\lVert x_{0}-P_{T_{0}}y\right\rVert<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Hence, for every
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{i}(x-y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|f_{i}(P_{T_{0}}(x-y))|+|f_{i}(P_{T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}}(x-y))|

\displaystyle\leqslant|f_{i}(x-x_{0})|+|f_{i}(x_{0}-P_{T_{0}}y)|+2||P^{*}_{T_{\infty}\setminus T_{0}}f_{i}||

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\left\lVert f_{i}\right\rVert+2\frac{\varepsilon}{4}=\varepsilon.

Therefore
W(x_{0},\delta_{0})\subseteq W
. The conclusion follows.
∎

5.
Additional comments and open questions

As previously mentioned, it is currently unknown whether or not every convex SCD set admits a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology. In particular, the following also seems to be open.

Question 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with a 1-unconditional basis. Does
B_{X}
admit a countable
\pi
-base in the relative weak topology?

One natural candidate in regard to this latter question is the stopping time Banach space that we already mentioned in the introduction, and which can be seen as the Banach space generated by the family of all antichains in the binary tree (see
[
BO89
]
for more details). This space also has a 1-unconditional basis, but the reader can easily check that its unit ball contains no points of continuity. We do not know whether or not the unit ball of this space admits a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology.

One of the main difficulties when facing this sort of problems is that there are very few tools at our disposal to show the non-existence of a
\pi
-base. In Section
2
, we have seen that the super alternative Daugavet property naturally prevents the existence of a countable
\pi
-base for the relative weak topology of the unit ball of the underlying Banach space. So far, there are few known examples of Banach spaces which satisfy this property while failing the Daugavet property. In particular, the following is currently unknown (see
[
LLM+25
, Section 6]
for more details), and would be another very natural approach to the above question.

Question 5.2
.

Does there exist a Banach space
X
with the super alternative Daugavet property whose unit ball is an SCD set? In particular, does there exist a Banach space with a 1-unconditional basis with the super alternative Daugavet property?

A direct consequence of our results is that both the binary tree and the countably branching tree spaces fail to have the super alternative Daugavet property. We would like to point out that this is actually also the case for every Banach space
X
generated by an adequate family. Recall that a collection
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
is called an
adequate family
if

(1)

\mathcal{A}
contains all the singletons: for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\{n\}\in\mathcal{A}
;

(2)

\mathcal{A}
is hereditary: if
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\subseteq A
, then
B\in\mathcal{A}
;

(3)

\mathcal{A}
is compact with respect to the topology of pointwise convergence: for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, if
B\in\mathcal{A}
for every finite
B\subseteq A
, then
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

Given such a family, we consider the Banach space
h_{\mathcal{A},1}
obtained as the completion of the space
(c_{00},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
, where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
is the norm on
c_{00}
given by

\left\lVert\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}e_{n}\right\rVert=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{n\in A}\left\lvert a_{n}\right\rvert

for every
(a_{n})\in c_{00}
. Then the canonical basis
(e_{n})
of
c_{00}
form a 1-unconditional basis for
h_{\mathcal{A},1}
. Spaces generated by adequate families include e.g.
c_{0}
,
\ell_{1}
,
c_{0}(\ell_{1})
or the Schreier space, as well as the tree-spaces considered in the previous two sections. We refer to
[
ABC21
,
ALM22
]
and references therein for more information on these spaces. With respect to the latter question, in this setting, we can provide a negative answer.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an adequate family of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. Then the space
h_{\mathcal{A},1}
fails the super Alternative Daugavet property.

Proof.

Let
X:=h_{\mathcal{A},1}
. If
\mathcal{A}
contains only the singletons, then
X
is isometrically isomorphic to the space
c_{0}
, and therefore fails to have the super ADP (this follows e.g. from
[
LLM+25
, Theorem 3.10]
). Otherwise, note that we can find
m<n
in
\mathbb{N}
such that
\{m,n\}\in\mathcal{A}
. We will show that the point
x:=\frac{1}{2}(e_{m}+e_{n})
is not a super alternative Daugavet point in the sense of
[
LLM+25
]
. Indeed, let
y:=\frac{1}{2}(e_{m}-e_{n})
and let
(y_{\alpha})
be a net in
B_{X}
which converges weakly to
y
. Then fix
\varepsilon>0
to be chosen later. By assumption, we can find
\alpha_{0}
such that for every
\alpha\succeq\alpha_{0}
, we have

\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(m)-\frac{1}{2}\right\rvert<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(n)+\frac{1}{2}\right\rvert<\varepsilon.

Take
\alpha\succeq\alpha_{0}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
. There are four cases to consider.

Case 1.
Assume that
m,n\in A
. On one hand, since
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lVert y_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leqslant 1.

On the other, since
\alpha\succeq\alpha_{0}
, we have

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert>1-2\varepsilon+\sum_{k\in A\setminus\{m,n\}}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert.

Hence,

\sum_{k\in A\setminus\{m,n\}}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert<2\varepsilon,

and it follows that

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert x(k)+\theta y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2}(\left\lvert 1+\theta\right\rvert+\left\lvert 1-\theta\right\rvert)+4\varepsilon.

Case 2 and 3.
Assume that
m\in A
and
n\notin A
. Again,

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert>\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon+\sum_{k\in A\setminus\{m\}}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert,

and thus

\sum_{k\in A\setminus\{m\}}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon.

It follows that

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert x(k)+\theta y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert 1+\theta\right\rvert+\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon\leqslant\frac{3}{2}+2\varepsilon.

The case
n\in A
and
m\notin A
is analogous.

Case 4.
Assume that
m,n\notin A
. Then

\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert x(k)+\theta y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert=\sum_{k\in A}\left\lvert y_{\alpha}(k)\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lVert y_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leqslant 1.

Summing up, we have shown that

\left\lVert x+\theta y_{\alpha}\right\rVert\leqslant\max\{\frac{3}{2}+2\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}(\left\lvert 1+\theta\right\rvert+\left\lvert 1-\theta\right\rvert)+4\varepsilon\}\leqslant\max\{\frac{3}{2}+2\varepsilon,\sqrt{2}+4\varepsilon\}.

So if
\varepsilon
was initially chosen small enough, we would get that this quantity is strictly smaller than 2, and therefore
x
is not a super alternative Daugavet point, as claimed.
∎

In Subsection
3.3
, we produced an example of a Banach space with a 2-unconditional basis
Y
whose unit ball fails to be SCD. To the best of our knowledge, the following is unknown.

Question 5.4
.

Can we produce a Banach space with a
k
-unconditional basis for
k\in(1,2)
whose unit ball is not SCD?

Also, observe that the unit ball of the latter space
Y
does contain many SCD points (since
\cconv(\Pi^{+}\cup\Pi^{-})\subseteq\SCD(B_{Y})
). So it is natural to ask whether it is possible to do a little bit better.

Question 5.5
.

Does there exist a Banach space with an unconditional basis whose unit ball has no SCD point (equivalently, such that 0 is not an SCD point in this set)?

The alert reader might wonder what would happen if we were simply to remove the strongly exposed points from the above set, or in other words to consider the symmetrization
D
of the set
\Omega^{+}=\Sigma^{+}\setminus\Pi^{+}
instead of
C
. It turns out that 0 is still SCD in this new unit ball.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
D:=\cconv(\Omega^{+}\cup\Omega^{-})\subseteq B_{X_{T}}
. Then
0\in\SCD(D)
.

Proof.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
x^{(n)}:=\frac{2^{n}-1}{2^{n}}\sum_{t\in L_{n}}e_{t}
. Then, for every
s\in L_{n}
, consider the norm-one functional
g_{s}:=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{t\in L_{n}\setminus\{s\}}e_{t}^{*}
. It is straightforward to check that for every
(x_{s,k}^{(n)})\in\prod_{s\in L_{n}}\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}S(D,g_{s},\frac{1}{k})
, we have

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert x^{(n)}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{s\in L_{n}}x_{s,k}^{(n)}\right\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Hence, for every
(x_{s,k,\theta}^{(n)})\in\prod_{s\in L_{n}}\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{\theta\in\{-1,1\}}S(D,\theta g_{s},\frac{1}{k})
, we have

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{s\in L_{n}}x_{s,k,1}^{(n)}+\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\sum_{s\in L_{n}}x_{s,k,-1}^{(n)}\right\rVert\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{n-2}}.

Therefore,
0\in\cconv\{x_{s,k,\theta}^{(n)}\}
for every
(x_{s,k,\theta}^{(n)})\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{s\in L_{n}}\prod_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\prod_{\theta\in\{-1,1\}}S(D,\theta g_{s},\frac{1}{k})
. The conclusion follows.
∎
