A middle-aged man with progressive facial swelling and generalized violaceous plaques
Authors: Sirikorn Densrisereekul, Jarukit Tantipisit, Suthep Jerasutus, Suthinee Rutnin, Chutima Seree-aphinan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis report describes a patient with AIDS-related angiomatous Kaposi sarcoma (KS) presenting with facial lymphoedema. Angiomatous KS is a rare histological subtype of KS that can mimic benign haemangiomas clinically and histologically. Furthermore, the patient did not have prior history or other clinical clues of HIV infection at the initial presentation, making the diagnosis challenging. Although lymphoedema has not previously been reported in association with angiomatous KS, this case demonstrates that lymphoedema may develop in patients with this histological subtype of KS, reflecting the multistep oncogenic nature of KS. Recognition of this clinical presentation of KS variant is vital as tumour-associated lymphoedema is associated with poorer prognosis and may warrant systemic chemotherapy in addition to highly active antiretroviral therapy.

Case report

Clinical findings

A 42-year-old previously healthy man presented to various hospitals with gradually worsening facial swelling and no other physical abnormalities for a month. He was prescribed a few short courses of low-dose prednisolone and antihistamines for possible angioedema, without any improvement. One month after the onset of facial swelling, he developed multiple asymptomatic erythematous nonscaly plaques on the trunk, forearms and tip of his nose (Figure 1a, b), which became violaceous with perilesional ecchymoses and involved the hard palate after 3 weeks (Figure 1c, d). Complete physical examination and computed tomography scan of the chest and abdomen were otherwise unremarkable except for cervical, thoracic and intra-abdominal lymphadenopathy. Baseline laboratory investigations including full blood count, urea, electrolytes, liver function tests and lactate dehydrogenase levels were within the normal ranges. Serological tests for occult infection screening revealed HIV infection (viral load 114 543 copies mL–1). His CD4 count was 11 cells mm–3. No other opportunistic infection was detected.

Histopathological findings

Skin biopsy taken from the forearm lesion during the initial presentation of the rash revealed abnormal lobular capillary proliferation in the dermis surrounding eccrine structures (Figure 2a). The capillaries were composed of bland-looking, plump, ­spindle-shaped endothelial cells and demonstrated scattered intracytoplasmic hyaline globules, occasional mitoses, as well as perilesional extravasated erythrocytes, hemosiderin deposits and lymphoplasmacytic inflammation (Figure 2b–d). The tumour had a low proliferative index with endothelial differentiation clearly demonstrated by CD31, CD34 and erythroblast transformation specific related gene (Figure 3). Immunohistochemistry staining for latent nuclear antigen 1 was positive in the endothelial cells (Figure 2e).

Diagnosis

The patients was diagnosed with AIDS-related angiomatous Kaposi sarcoma (KS) with facial lymphoedema.

Discussion

Angiomatous KS is a rare histological variant of KS that can easily be mistaken for benign haemangiomas, both clinically and histologically. Previous case reports have described angiomatous KS lesions as patches, papules or plaques.1–3 Microscopically, the tumours appear as a lobular capillary proliferation with preserved collagen bundles between nodular foci. Histological clues for classic KS (e.g. slit-like vascular proliferation with jagged borders and promontory sign) are rarely seen in this type of KS. However, other suggestive features, such as perilesional lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates, extravasated erythrocytes, haemosiderin depositions and intracytoplasmic hyaline globules, may serve as clues that prompt further investigation for KS using a human herpesvirus-8-specific latent nuclear antigen 1 immunohistochemistry study.

Lymphoedema, commonly presenting as facial swelling, may be an early symptom in patients with AIDS-related KS.4 It may manifest before or after the appearance of cutaneous KS lesions and is considered one of the predictors of poor clinical outcome.4,5 In addition to classic KS, lymphangiomatous-like KS is an atypical histological subtype that has been reported in association with lymphoedema.3 Lymphoedema occurring in conjunction with angiomatous KS has not been previously reported; however, our case demonstrates that lymphoedema may develop in patients with any histological subtypes of KS, probably due to the multistep oncogenic nature of KS leading to proliferation of lymphoendothelial tumour cells.5 The presence of tumour-associated ­lymphoedema indicates the need for systemic chemotherapy in addition to highly active antiretroviral therapy; liposomal doxorubicin and paclitaxel have been shown to significantly improve facial swelling and quality of life in patients with AIDS-related KS with lymphoedema.6

Our patient achieved a marked reduction in facial swelling after two cycles of weekly paclitaxel, given concurrently with highly active antiretroviral therapy, with complete resolution of facial swelling after completion of chemotherapy. This case has important educational value in demonstrating an early manifestation of KS associated with a ‘benign-looking’ angiomatous subtype, which physicians should recognize to prompt appropriate investigation and treatment.
