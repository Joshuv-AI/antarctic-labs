Proteomic profiling reveals structural and adhesion pathways regulated by the inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp variant in human neural progenitor cells
Authors: Natalie Erickson, Ivanna Ihnatovych, Andras Szabados, Kinga Szigeti, Nadine Kabbani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The CHRNA7 gene, located on chromosome 15q13.3, encodes the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7 nAChR) subunit and lies within a genomic region characterized by high recombination rates and associations with multiple neuropsychiatric disorders. Within this region, the human specific gene CHRFAM7A arose through partial duplication, rearrangement, and fusion between CHRNA7 and FAM7A. The direct CHRFAM7A allele has been shown to negatively regulate α7 nAChR function. The inverted allele (CHRFAM7AΔ2bp), harboring a two-base pair deletion in exon 6, is linked to schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and other psychiatric conditions. In this study, we investigated how the presence of the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele alters the cellular proteome. Using high-throughput proteomic analysis by liquid chromatography-electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS), we characterized protein expression in neuronal progenitors differentiated from isogenic human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) lines representing CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A-null genotypes. Comparative analysis identified 129 differentially expressed proteins enriched in pathways related to extracellular matrix organization, collagen biosynthesis, and cell adhesion. Functional assays further demonstrated differences in matrix adhesion between CHRFAM7A-null and CHRFAM7AΔ2bp-derived progenitors. These results suggest that the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp variant influences cellular structure through modulation of adhesion matrix-interaction proteins. This work provides insight into molecular mechanisms that may underlie increased neurodisease vulnerability associated with this genotype.

Introduction

The 15q13.3 region of the human chromosome harbors the human-specific CHRFAM7A gene, a negative regulator of the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7 nAChR) encoded by the nearby CHRNA7 gene [11, 30]. CHRFAM7A originated from a partial duplication of CHRNA7 (exons 5–10) and its fusion with FAM7A/ULK4 (exons A-F) [30, 34]. This fusion gene is expressed in the immune and nervous system [6, 20, 34] and mutations at this genetic site have been implicated in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [23, 37, 40]. Multiple CHRFAM7A variants exist in the human population, differing in copy number and genomic orientation. A common inverted allele, marked by a two-base pair deletion in exon 6 and termed CHRFAM7AΔ2bp, occurs in approximately 42% of Caucasians and 14% of African Americans [35]. Expression of the inverted allele shows considerable variability, with reported frequencies ranging from 8 to 66% across diverse populations [8]. These population-level genetic differences underscore the importance of considering CHRFAM7A variants when investigating human diseases linked to the 15q13.3 locus.

The α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7 nAChR) is a calcium-permeable ligand-gated ion channel that plays a critical role in the cholinergic regulation of neuronal development, synaptic connectivity, and glial signaling [33, 47]. The human-specific CHRFAM7AΔ2bp variant was initially thought to encode a structurally deficient α7-like subunit [2, 44]; however, accumulating evidence indicates that the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele is not translated [6, 20]. Despite this, the variant exerts biologically meaningful effects and is associated with increased risk for generalized idiopathic seizures [31], inflammation following spinal cord injury [24], schizophrenia [35], and bipolar disorder [17]. Together, these findings indicate that CHRFAM7AΔ2bp contributes to neural system dysregulation and can contribute to neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disease vulnerability.

We employed an unbiased shotgun proteomics approach to identify proteins affected by the presence of the inverted variant (CHRFAM7AΔ2bp) using an isogenic iPSC model differentiated into medial ganglionic eminence (MGE) progenitors from CHRFAM7A-null and CHRFAM7AΔ2bp lines. High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) coupled with electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (ESI-MS/MS) was used to compare the proteomic profiles between the CHRFAM7A-null line and an isogenic line carrying the inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele. Differential proteomic and bioinformatic analyses revealed significant alterations in protein networks associated with cell adhesion, extracellular matrix organization, and structural signaling. Orthogonal validation and functional assays were performed and support our proteomic findings that the inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp gene modulates neuronal cell interaction with the ECM.

Methods

Cell line generation and culture

Human iPSCs were derived from skin fibroblasts obtained through WNYSTEM at the University at Buffalo using a non-integrating episomal system as previously established [19]. To generate isogenic CHRFAM7AΔ2bp (inverted) lines, the CHRFAM7A-null donor iPSC line (UB068) was edited using TALEN-based genome targeting, as previously established and validated [38]. All iPSC lines were thoroughly evaluated for pluripotency and three-germ-layer potential using Scorecard™ analysis and immunocytochemistry (ICC). For the genome-edited clones, individual colonies were expanded, screened by PCR to verify the engineered insertion, and further analyzed using a breakpoint-specific TaqMan assay and RT-qPCR to quantify copy number and expression. Whole-genome sequencing confirmed correct integration and excluded detectable off-target changes.

Cells were maintained on irradiated mouse embryonic fibroblasts in “iPSC medium”: DMEM/F12-Glutamax supplemented with 20% KnockOut Serum Replacement, 1% non-essential amino acids, 0.1% 2-mercaptoethanol and 4 ng/ml bFGF (all Thermo Fisher). Cultures were kept at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ and passaged every 4–6 days using Dispase (Thermo Fisher).

Neuronal differentiation

To generate medial ganglionic eminence (MGE) progenitors, iPSCs were differentiated following a previously validated workflow [19]. Undifferentiated CHRFAM7A-null and CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells were detached with Dispase on Day 0, transferred to 6-well plates with ultra-low attachment (Corning) and continued to flow in iPSC medium without bFGF to form Embryoid Bodies (EB). On Day 4, the medium was replaced with neural induction medium (NIM): DMEM/F12 + Glutamax, 1% N2, 1% NEAA, and 1% pen/strep. EB were attached in 6-well plates on Day 7, and by Day 10 neural rosettes were present, indicating the development of primitive neuroepithelia. To ventralize primitive neuroepithelia, purmorphamine (1.5 µM) was added to NIM from Day 10 to Day 25. On day 16, neural tube-like rosettes were detached and transferred into T-25 Flasks in NIM with 2% B27 (Thermo Fisher) to form neurospheres. The cultures were fed every other day. On day 25, the neural progenitor cells were dissociated with Accutase (Thermo Fisher) and the resulting MGE progenitors were re-plated for characterization, expansion, and for continued neuronal maturation or experimental procedures.

Proteomic analysis

Liquid chromatography–electrospray ionization tandem mass spectrometry (LC-ESI-MS/MS) was performed to quantify protein expression, following previously established protocols [13, 15, 32]. Protein concentrations were determined using a Bradford assay, and 50 µg of protein per sample was processed for MS analysis. Samples were subjected to denaturation, reduction, and alkylation using 8 M urea, 1 M dithiothreitol (DTT), and 0.5 M iodoacetamide (IA) in ammonium bicarbonate. Proteins were digested with trypsin at 37 °C for approximately 5 h, followed by C18 ZipTip (Millipore) cleanup. Peptides were dehydrated via SpeedVac and reconstituted in 0.1% formic acid prior to analysis. Samples were analyzed on an Exploris Orbitrap 480 mass spectrometer coupled to an Easy-nLC 1200 HPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Peptide separation was achieved using a PepMap RSLC C18 column (75 μm i.d. × 15 cm, 2 μm particle size; Thermo Fisher Scientific) at a flow rate of 300 nL/min with a gradient elution of 0.1% formic acid and 80% acetonitrile between runs. Each sample (2 µL injection volume) was analyzed in triplicate technical replicates.

Protein identification and quantification were performed using Proteome Discoverer v2.4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) against the NCBI Homo sapiens (2018) reference database. Precursor ion mass tolerance was set to 2 ppm, and fragment ion tolerance to 0.05 Da. Only proteins detected in at least two of three replicates were included for downstream analysis. False discovery rate (FDR) cutoff was set to 1% for peptide spectral matches (PSMs). Post-translational modifications included in the analysis consist of full tryptic cleavage constraints with static cysteine alkylation by iodoacetamide and variable methionine oxidation. Abundance ratios were calculated in Proteome Discoverer by comparing the inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cell line to the CHRFAM7A-null control, and p < 0.05 (by unpaired t-test) was considered statistically significant. Differentially expressed proteins were analyzed for functional enrichment using the STRING database [41] following the established pipeline [13]. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses were conducted via the Database for Annotation, Visualization, and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) using an EASE threshold of 0.05, count threshold of 2, and Benjamini-adjusted p-value < 0.05. Fold enrichment was calculated by comparing the proportion of genes in the input dataset associated with a given GO term to the proportion of genes associated with that same GO term in the background population. Processed data were filtered, curated, and visualized using Microsoft Excel and R.

Immunocytochemistry (ICC) and microscopy

MGE progenitors plated on Matrigel-coated glass coverslips and cultured for 72 h were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Mallinckrodt Baker) for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (Mallinckrodt Baker) for 10 min and blocked with blocking buffer (5% BSA in PBS) for 1 h at room temperature (RT). Cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary anti-Vinculin and/or anti-pFAK (Tyr397) antibodies (Proteintech, raised in rabbit, 1:200 dilution) and for 1 h at RT with secondary antibodies (Donkey anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor 594, Jackson ImmunoResearch). Both primary and secondary antibodies were diluted in a blocking buffer. Actin was visualized with ActinGreen 488 ReadyProbes (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Coverslips were incubated with NucBlue (DAPI) for 5 min at RT and mounted with a ProLong® Gold Antifade reagent (Life Technologies). Fluorescent images were taken with ECHO Revolve Microscope (40x) (Discover Echo). Confocal images were captured by using Leica TCS SP8 confocal microscope system (63x) paired with LAS X software and processed using Fiji ImageJ software.

Adhesion assay

Adhesive properties of the CHRFAM7A-null and CHRFAM7AΔ2bp MGE progenitors were quantified according to previously validated method [3]. Briefly, 96-well plates were coated for 1 h at 37 °C with 10 µg/ml of either collagen I (Advanced BioMatrix), Geltrex, or Matrigel (both Thermo Fisher Scientific) and blocked for 1 h at RT with 0.5% BSA/PBS. Subsequently, 20,000 MGE progenitors/well were seeded and incubated for 30 min at 37 °C, fixed with 4% PFA/PBS, and stained with Crystal Violet. Adhesion was quantified colorimetrically at an absorption wavelength of 570 nm. Experiments were performed in quadruplicates. Values are expressed as mean ± SD as indicated in the figure legends. A Mann-Whitney test with Bonferroni correction was performed, based on a non‑normal distribution in some groups, with pairwise comparisons made across matrix conditions and between the two cell lines (GraphPad Prism).

Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from MGE progenitors using Trizol (Thermo Fisher) according to manufacturer’s protocol. cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using iScript™ cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad). For quantitative gene expression, standard RT-qPCR was performed using the primers (IDT): ANXA2 (F: TCGGACACATCTGGTGACTTCC,

R: CCTCTTCACTCCAGCGTCATAG), COL1A1 (F: GATTCCCTGGACCTAAAGGTGC,

R: AGCCTCTCCATCTTTGCCAGCA), ICAM-1 (F: GGCCTCAGTCAGTGTGA,

R: CAGCTCCAGACACACACA), IGFBP5 (F: CGTGCTGTGTACCTGCCCAATT,

R: ACTTGTCCACGCACCAGCAGAT), MMP2 (F: ATGACAGCTGCACCACTGAG, R: ATTTGT TGCCCAGGAAAGTG). qPCR was performed using the SsoAdvanced Universal SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad) and run on Bio-Rad CFX Opus 96 Cycler. Samples were assayed with 3 technical replicates, and data was analyzed using the ∆∆CT method and normalized to GAPDH expression. Data is presented as the average of three independent experiments ± standard deviation.

Results

CHRFAM7AΔ2bp expression reshapes the structural and adhesion proteome in human neural progenitor cells

To investigate the effects of the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp variant, we generated an isogenic cell model by introducing an inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele into a CHRFAM7A-null iPSC line using TALEN-mediated gene editing followed by MGE progenitor differentiation. Comparative proteomic profiling and bioinformatic analyses were then performed to identify molecular and structural adaptations associated with CHRFAM7AΔ2bp expression relative to the CHRFAM7A-null. The use of an isogenic system enabled a direct assessment of protein-level changes attributable to the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp variant when expressed within the CHRFAM7A-null line (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1The experimental framework used for defining CHRFAM7AΔ2bp-dependent proteomic signatures. Donor cells were subjected to quantitative proteomic profiling. Differential protein abundance was assessed and integrated with computational and network-based analyses to identify genotype-dependent molecular pathways and interaction networksWe first compared total proteome profiles between CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A-null cell lines using label-free LC-ESI-MS/MS analysis. A total of 3,410 proteins were identified in the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cell lysate and 3,365 proteins in the CHRFAM7A-null cell lysate (Fig. 2A). Comparative analysis revealed strong overall similarity between the two proteomes, with 3,265 shared proteins representing ~ 93% overlap between the two cell lines. Despite this global similarity, 129 proteins showed significant differential abundance between the two cell lines (p < 0.05).

The experimental framework used for defining CHRFAM7AΔ2bp-dependent proteomic signatures. Donor cells were subjected to quantitative proteomic profiling. Differential protein abundance was assessed and integrated with computational and network-based analyses to identify genotype-dependent molecular pathways and interaction networks

Further analysis showed that among these 129 differentially altered proteins, 70 proteins were downregulated and 59 proteins were upregulated in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells relative to the CHRFAM7A-null (Fig. 2B). These proteins along with their gene symbol, FDR adjusted p-value, and fold-change in expression are listed in Tables 1 and 2. GO enrichment and bioinformatic analyses of the 129 differentially altered proteins revealed a significant enrichment in functional pathways associated with cell adhesion, migration, extracellular exosome function, plasma membrane components, and diverse extracellular matrix (ECM) binding activities (Fig. 2C). Notably, downregulated proteins included annexin A2 (ANXA2), collagen α-1 chain (COL1A1), intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM1), and protein S100-A10 (S100A10), proteins broadly linked to cell-matrix interaction and cytoskeletal integrity. In contrast, upregulated proteins comprised HLA class II histocompatibility components (HLA-DRA and CD74), neuronal membrane glycoprotein M6-b (GPM6B), excitatory amino acid transporter 1 (SLC1A3), and brain fatty acid-binding protein (FABP7), suggesting an increase in antigen presentation, neuroglial communication, and metabolic remodeling in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells. These results indicate that introduction of CHRFAM7AΔ2bp alters protein networks involved in cellular adhesion and extracellular organization.

Given the robust enrichment of cell adhesion and ECM-related proteins across both STRING and GO analyses, we expanded our analysis on this subset. We found that approximately a third (33%) of the significantly altered proteins in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp relative to CHRFAM7A-null cells encompassed a sub-proteome for cytoskeletal organization, ECM remodeling, and cell-matrix interaction (Fig. 5A). Within this sub-proteome, 14 proteins were upregulated and 28 were downregulated. An integrative pathway based on Cytoscape and WikiPathways was used to visualize components of the structural and adhesion proteome. As shown in Fig. 5B, CHRFAM7AΔ2bp gene insertion alters the abundance of proteins across these interconnected pathways. Network mapping revealed coordinated changes in integrin binding components (ITGB5, SLC3A2, ICAM1), collagen isoforms (COL1A1, COL6A2), and extracellular regulators (ICAM1, CD44, ANXA2), along with cytoskeletal and adhesion-associated proteins such as ACTN1, SYNM, and TAGLN2. Pathway modeling also highlighted several upstream signaling intermediates including PRKCA, RHOG, and CHUK as differentially expressed, suggesting remodeling of focal adhesion kinase and Rho GTPase-mediated pathways.

These pathway analyses provide a systems-level perspective, highlighting a shift in cellular interactions with the ECM. Collectively, these changes suggest that the presence of the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele impacts cell structure and ECM interaction.

CHRFAM7AΔ2bp insertion alters cell-matrix adhesion

To functionally validate proteomic findings, we examined the adhesive properties of MGE progenitors differentiated from CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A-null lines across three standard ECM substrates: Matrigel, Geltrex, and collagen I. Cell attachment was quantified using crystal violet staining, followed by absorbance measurement. MGE progenitors differentiated from the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp line demonstrated: (1) a significantly lower adhesion to collagen I compared to the null (Fig. 6A and B; p = 0.0027, Bonferroni corrected); (2) significantly reduced adherence to collagen I compared to Matrigel (p = 0.0027, Bonferroni corrected) and to Geltrex (p = 0.036, Bonferroni corrected). On the other hand, CHRFAM7A-null progenitors showed similar adhesion to all three matrices (Fig. 6B). No statistically significant difference was observed between the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A-null lines when comparing their adhesion to Matrigel or Geltrex substrates. These results indicate genotype-specific functional alterations in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp progenitors that are supported by adhesion assays and translational changes in target proteins identified in our proteomic analysis.

To confirm the cellular basis of altered adhesion suggested by the proteomic and ECM functional attachment assays, we examined morphological changes in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A-null MGE progenitors plated on Matrigel. Cells were immunostained for the focal adhesion marker proteins phosphorylated focal adhesion kinase (pFAK) and vinculin, which reveal sites of integrin-mediated attachment, adhesion signaling, and actin cytoskeletal anchoring. ECHO imaging revealed morphological differences between the two cell lines (Fig. 7). CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells exhibited a more branched actin network, whereas CHRFAM7A-null cells displayed compact actin bundles concentrated around the cell body. Additionally, CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells appear less ameboid in morphology and less clustered, with interactions occurring primarily through elongated processes rather than tightly interconnected cell bodies, in contrast to the more clumped morphology observed in the CHRFAM7A-null group.

Discussion

Approximately 42% of the general population carries the human gene inverted CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele [38], suggesting strong selective pressure [39]. Because CHRFAM7AΔ2bp may not be translated into protein, its mechanism of action remains unclear, although RNA-mediated effects have been proposed [20]. The allele has been associated with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder [34], highlighting its relevance to neuropsychiatric disease. Understanding the biological consequences of CHRFAM7AΔ2bp is therefore important for defining uniquely human genetic contributions to brain function and disease. In this study, we provide new insight into protein-level changes associated with this allele using an isogenic iPSC model.

Using a high‑throughput, unbiased proteomic approach, we found that ~ 7% of all detected proteins differed in abundance between CHRFAM7AΔ2bp and CHRFAM7A‑null cell lines. Although proteomic data alone cannot define functional outcomes, the differentially abundant proteins clearly converged onto pathways for cell adhesion and ECM interaction. This pathway-level consistency provides a hypothetical framework for interpreting proteomic results and strongly suggests that CHRFAM7AΔ2bp influences interaction between the cell and its extracellular environment (Fig. 9).

Prominent components of this adhesion-related remodeling are ECM proteins, particularly collagens and their regulatory enzymes. CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells show decreased abundance of multiple collagen alpha chains (COL1A1, COL6A2, COL11A1, COL5A1, COL18A1), which contribute to ECM composition during neuronal development and can be impacted during neurodisease [36, 45]. These changes are accompanied by reduced levels of enzymes required for collagen stabilization, including P4HA2, P3H1, P3H2, and LOXL2 [26, 46]. In contrast, the collagen degrading enzyme MMP2 appears upregulated and underscores the impact of CHRFAM7AΔ2bp on the collagen composition of the ECM. Because collagen-rich matrices provide mechanical stability and anchoring cues for neurons, these changes may weaken cell-matrix attachment while promoting more plastic and adaptable interactions necessary for structural plasticity during learning [1, 9, 27].

Consistent with this hypothesis, we identify changes in key adhesion-associated proteins that can contribute to cytoskeletal organization and important for cell-matrix interaction dynamics including focal adhesion contact sites. Specifically, ICAM1, ITGB5, ANXA2, S100A10, LGALS3, and LGALS3BP, which participate in cytoskeletal organization, were altered in CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells [7, 14, 16, 28, 48, 49]. The actin-stabilizing proteins (ACTN1, ACTN2), focal adhesion-associated adaptor proteins (PDLIM1, PDLIM5) are decreased while the filopodia associated motor protein MYO10 is increased [18, 42]. Interestingly, upregulation of NOTCH1 and its interactor JAG1 implicate an effect of CHRFAM7AΔ2bp on neurodevelopmental signaling [49]. Taken together our findings provide compelling proteomic evidence for this gene in cell-matrix interactions that should be validated in future studies in more physiological systems for the study of neuronal structure and activity.

Our functional assays indicate that CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells exhibit preferential adhesion to basement membrane-like matrices (i.e., Matrigel and Geltrex) compared to collagen I substrates. Basement membrane matrices are enriched in laminin, collagen IV, and proteoglycans that support dynamic cell-matrix interactions typical of developing or remodeling neural environments [5, 10, 12, 22]. In contrast, reduced adhesion to collagen I, a more rigid structural ECM component, indicates decreased affinity for stable, interstitial matrices. This substrate preference is consistent with a more motile CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cellular phenotype (Fig. 9) that is weaker in attachment to rigid ECM structures and putatively more responsive to migratory cues [43].

Consistent with this, our ECHO imaging experiments indicate that CHRFAM7A-null cells exhibit more prominent actin structures, consistent with stable substrate anchoring, whereas CHRFAM7AΔ2bp cells exhibit patterns indicative of dynamic adhesion remodeling. In these experiments, we propose that the preferential colocalization of vinculin and pFAK at leading edges (e.g., growth cones) supports integrin-mediated signaling for motility [29]. Indeed, these morphological changes are characteristic of cells undergoing active adhesion turnover and cytoskeletal reorganization [4].

In summary, our data support a model in which CHRFAM7AΔ2bp enhances the turnover of proteins important for cell adhesion and ECM interaction, resulting in a more structurally dynamic and mobile cellular state. Supported by strong proteome-scale evidence, the CHRFAM7AΔ2bp allele may contribute to an individual genetic predisposition to a more labile cell wiring state and in the context of neurons influences dendritic growth, synapse formation, and circuit stability [21, 25]. Such effects may contribute to both adaptive and maladaptive clinical outcomes, impacting learning and memory and increasing the risk of neuropsychiatric disease. Ongoing studies integrating spatial analysis, functional assays of adhesion and motility, and neuronal network modeling will help determine how these molecular changes translate into circuit-scale function and identify potential therapeutic targets.
