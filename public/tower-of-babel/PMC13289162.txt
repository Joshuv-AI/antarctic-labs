Can sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibition preserve renal structure and function in de novo kidney transplant recipients? Protocol for a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial assessing the efficacy of dapagliflozin on kidney structure, function and safety in kidney transplant recipients in Norway—the DEAK study
Authors: Ingrid Charlotte Kongerud, Karsten Midtvedt, Marit D Solbu, Marius A Øvrehus, Øystein Eikrem, Ivar A Eide, Kristian Heldal, Kåre Birkeland, Anders Åsberg, Trond G Jenssen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction Recent studies have shown that sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors slow the progression of chronic kidney disease in people at high cardiovascular risk, with or without type 2 diabetes. To date, all published studies have excluded kidney transplant recipients (KTRs). The Dapagliflozin Early After Kidney Transplantation (DEAK) study aims to prospectively evaluate the effects of the SGLT2 inhibitor dapagliflozin on kidney function, histopathology and metabolic outcomes among de novo KTRs.Methods and analysesThis nationwide, investigator-initiated, single-transplant-centre, randomised, placebo-controlled, prospective clinical trial with two parallel groups aims to enrol 330 de novo adult KTRs (aged 18–75 years) with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of at least 25 mL/min/1.73 m². Participants are enrolled 6–8 weeks after transplantation and randomised 1:1 to receive dapagliflozin 10 mg/day or placebo for 3 years. Recipients with conditions that may intermittently affect kidney function, such as recent acute rejection or ongoing infection, are ineligible for inclusion.The primary endpoint of the study is the difference in the chronic eGFR slope between treatment groups over 3 years, estimated using the European Kidney Function Consortium equation. Secondary and safety endpoints include changes in measured GFR (iohexol clearance), urinary albumin/creatinine ratio, blood pressure, infections and safety clinical chemistry. Exploratory endpoints evaluated after 1.5 years include changes in body composition and glucose tolerance; between-group differences in urinary metabolomics; transplant kidney biopsy inflammation and fibrosis scores (n=140) and kidney biopsy messenger RNA and protein expression (n=50). The protocol also includes a 10-year poststudy registry follow-up of eGFR slope, cardiovascular events and graft and patient survival.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol (EudraCT number: 2022-002428-10) has been approved by the Norwegian Medical Products Agency, the Regional Committee for Medical Research Ethics in Southeast Norway (REK Southeast number 426076) and by the data protection offices at the participating hospitals. The main results will be published in international, peer-reviewed scientific journals.Trial registration numberNCT05788276.

Background

Kidney transplantation is the preferred treatment for patients in need of kidney replacement therapy.1 Transplantation extends lifespan and enhances quality of life compared with long-term dialysis.2 Improved immunosuppressive therapy has reduced early rejection rates from approximately 30% to below 10% over the past decades.3 However, unresolved challenges remain, such as low-grade chronic allograft rejection, graft fibrosis and probably haemodynamic mechanisms and glomerular hyperfiltration in a denervated single kidney, which may lead to accelerated decline in glomerular filtration rate (GFR). The average half-life of kidney grafts from donation after brain death ranges from 11 to 17 years,4 5 and the annual death rate for patients with a functioning kidney graft is 3% to 4%,2 but ranges from 5% to 15% after graft loss.3 Therefore, any intervention that extends graft survival will probably also improve post-transplant patient survival. Diabetes, either pre-existing or post-transplant diabetes mellitus (PTDM),6 7 increases the risk of patient death and graft loss even further.8 9 Death in kidney transplant recipients (KTRs) is mainly due to cardiovascular disease, malignancies and infections, with cardiovascular disease dominating in the early post-transplant phase.10 This might reflect the patient’s prior history of end-stage kidney disease and perhaps other comorbidities such as hypertension, diabetes mellitus and hyperparathyroidism, causing acceleration of vascular calcification that promotes cardiovascular disease.11 Kidney transplantation is associated with a lower cardiovascular risk compared with remaining on dialysis treatment, reflecting the benefit of a functioning kidney graft on cardiovascular disease and death.12 Any treatment that improves graft survival, ie, keeping the patient off dialysis, will improve patient survival.

The limited lifespan of kidney grafts has prompted the transplant community to explore novel kidney-protective strategies beyond conventional immunosuppression to prolong graft and patient survival after kidney transplantation.13 Nephron loss or hyperglycaemia tends to upregulate sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) activity in the proximal tubule, leading to afferent arteriolar dilatation in the glomeruli, which increases glomerular pressure and causes hyperfiltration. Inhibiting SGLT2 reduces sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubule, thereby increasing sodium delivery to the salt-sensing macula densa cells in the distal tubule. This again reverses afferent arteriolar dilatation and restores more normal intraglomerular pressure. This mechanism has been shown to preserve kidney function in non-transplanted kidneys.13 Furthermore, sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubules is ATP-dependent, and reduced reabsorption due to SGLT2 inhibition will improve energy balance in this part of the nephron.14 Although it is reasonable to believe that SGLT2 inhibition may also help maintain graft function in transplanted kidneys, its efficacy has not yet been evaluated in prospective, placebo-controlled intervention trials. Transplanted kidneys differ from native kidneys in that they are denervated and exposed to immunosuppressive drugs, specifically calcineurin inhibitors, which are known to affect kidney haemodynamics by afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction, thereby lowering intraglomerular pressure and also causing toxic effects on tubular cells.15 It is unknown how SGLT2 inhibition will interact with the known arteriolar effects of calcineurin inhibitors. The Dapagliflozin Early After Kidney Transplantation (DEAK) study is designed to test the hypothesis that treatment with the SGLT2 inhibitor dapagliflozin is superior to placebo in preserving kidney function when initiated early after transplantation.

Methods and design

Study design

The DEAK study is a nationwide, prospective, randomised, double-blind, parallel-group, placebo-controlled trial that will enrol 330 patients. The graphical abstract is shown in online supplemental figure 1 and the trial flow chart is shown in online supplemental figure 2. All participants will be recruited at the time of transplantation at Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet, Norway’s only transplant centre. Participants are enrolled 6–8 weeks after transplantation and randomised 1:1 to receive either dapagliflozin 10 mg/day or placebo for 3 years. Eight weeks after transplantation, the recipients undergo an in-depth investigation before they are transferred to their local hospital for routine surveillance. After 1.5 years, the in-depth investigation is repeated at the transplant centre, and after 3 years, this is partly repeated at their regional hospital.

Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet, performs approximately 270 kidney transplants annually and serves the entire Norwegian population. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the recruitment period for the 330 participants is estimated to last approximately 3 years.

Study population and eligibility criteria

The trial participants are de novo adult KTRs aged 18–75 years who have received a kidney graft within the past 8 weeks, regardless of proteinuria, with an estimated GFR (eGFR) ≥25 mL/min/1.73 m². The upper age limit is set to exclude recipients with older donors, whose kidney grafts may be more vulnerable due to age matching. Inclusion and exclusion criteria are listed in boxes1 2.

Patient and public involvement

A patient research partner (PRP) with a history of kidney transplantation was requested during the study planning phase. The PRP has been involved in research planning, has provided input on the study protocol design and continues to provide input during study meetings as the study progresses.

Ethics and dissemination

The study protocol (EudraCT number: 2022-002428-10; ClinicalTrials.gov registration number: NCT05788276 (https://clinicaltrials.gov)) has been approved by the Norwegian Medical Products Agency, the Regional Committee for Medical Research Ethics in Southeast Norway (REK Southeast number 426076) and by the data protection offices at the participating hospitals. We confirm that donated organ/tissue has been sourced ethically, and that no organ/tissue has been sourced from executed prisoners, prisoners of conscience or other vulnerable groups. The sponsor plans to communicate important protocol modifications to relevant parties continuously throughout the study. The main results of the study will be published in international, peer-reviewed scientific journals.

Patients eligible for study enrolment are recruited from the kidney transplantation outpatient clinic at Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet, where they are monitored after discharge from the surgical unit following transplantation. The nephrologist in the ward or outpatient clinic provides study information, and a written informed consent is obtained after the patient has read, understood and reflected on this information for at least 24 hours. The study will be carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study participants are covered by a study-specific insurance throughout the study, in accordance with Norwegian law.

Once follow-up and data processing are complete, the study data will be securely stored at the institution. Data will be kept in a de-identified format, with each participant recognised only by a unique trial subject number. The ID key will be stored securely in the Medinsight system at Oslo University Hospital, separate from the study data. Additionally, the data will be retained until the end of 2054 (or for at least 25 years after the final study report is submitted, in accordance with current European regulations).

Adverse events

All serious adverse events (SAEs) and all adverse events (AEs) leading to premature discontinuation of the study drug will be recorded. In addition, given the extensive experience with dapagliflozin, only selected AEs will be documented. Selected AEs include urinary tract infections, genital infections, acute kidney injury, transplant rejection episodes, volume depletion, severe hypoglycaemia, fractures, diabetic ketoacidosis, opportunistic infections and leucopenia.

SAEs are reported to the sponsor within 24 hours. The sponsor maintains detailed records of all SAEs reported by investigators and evaluates them for causality and expectedness. When appropriate, the sponsor will determine whether the study risk-benefit ratio has changed.

Suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) are reported in the EudraVigilance system as soon as possible but no later than 15 days after the sponsor’s first knowledge. The sponsor will ensure that all relevant information about fatal or life-threatening SUSARs is promptly recorded and reported to EudraVigilance and, in any case, no later than 7 days after the sponsor becomes aware of such cases. All SUSARs will also be reported to the Norwegian Competent Authority and Ethics Committee as soon as possible.

Randomisation and blinding

A randomisation list and sequentially numbered envelopes are generated using the Blockrand package for R (10.32614/CRAN.package.blockrand) by a person not directly involved in the study. Randomisation is performed using block sizes of four to eight patients.

Included recipients and all study personnel remain blinded to treatment assignments, except for one independent person with access to the randomisation list if unblinding becomes necessary. Study drugs (dapagliflozin 10 mg and placebo) are packaged identically and have a consistent appearance (supplied by AstraZeneca, Mölndal, Sweden). Patients are instructed to take their study medication in the morning, at approximately the same time each day throughout the study. The study medication is dispensed at randomisation for the first 1.5 years, and a delivery supplement is provided at the transplant centre during the 1.5-year follow-up visit.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the DEAK study is to evaluate differences in the chronic eGFR slope between the two groups over the entire study period, from baseline at 6 (±2) weeks up to 3 years after transplantation. The effects of dapagliflozin on urinary albumin/creatinine and protein/creatinine ratios (U-ACR/U-PCR), attended office blood pressure and 24-hour blood pressure measurements will be investigated over 3 years. Additionally, the postdip chronic measured GFR (mGFR) slope will be evaluated using iohexol-based mGFR measurements16 at 2 weeks after initiation of the study drug, at 1.5 years and at 3 years. Urine metabolomics; lean, fat and bone mass measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) and glucose metabolism assessed by oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) will be evaluated as exploratory endpoints at 1.5 years.

An additional exploratory endpoint will be evaluated in the first 140 enrolled patients, using representative kidney graft biopsies at baseline and follow-up to assess changes in inflammation and fibrosis scores over the first 1.5 years of treatment. A subset of the first 50 patients with high-quality baseline and follow-up biopsies (two complete diagnostic biopsy cores)17 will be analysed to assess changes in messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein expression patterns between the two groups and elucidate the biological mechanisms underlying the treatment response.

Sample and data collection

Patients are randomised and begin study medication approximately 6 weeks after transplantation. Before starting the study medication, eGFR is assessed using the European Kidney Function Consortium (EKFC) creatinine formula, and patients undergo an OGTT to diagnose presence of glucose intolerance and assess the patients at risk of early PTDM. If there are no contraindications, a protocol transplant biopsy is obtained. After enrolment, in-person protocol visits are scheduled after 1 week and 2 weeks. During the 2-week study visit, mGFR and DXA measurements are performed. Patients are discharged 8 weeks after transplant for routine long-term follow-up by their local nephrologist, starting with weekly visits and gradually increasing the intervals (with at least four visits annually after the first year), in accordance with the Norwegian guidelines for kidney transplant follow-up. Each local follow-up visit includes a collection of information regarding potential AEs, changes in concomitant therapies and adherence to the study drug. Additionally, vital signs are recorded, and plasma creatinine and urine samples for ACR/PCR are collected. A 24-hour blood pressure measurement is performed locally within 2–3 months after transplantation, after 1.5 years and on study termination after 3 years.

At 1.5 years post-transplantation, a comprehensive evaluation, including eGFR, kidney graft biopsy, OGTT, blood pressure, mGFR and DXA, is conducted at the transplant centre. A final study closeout visit will take place at 3 years post-transplantation, with at least the mGFR test repeated. This will occur at the patient’s regional nephrology centre or the transplant centre, whichever is most appropriate for the patient. Sampling for mGFR will be performed via a finger-prick test,18 19 and the samples will be sent to the transplant centre for centralised analysis.

Temporary study drug discontinuation and withdrawals

Temporary discontinuation of the study drug is recommended for patients experiencing acute illness, hospitalisation, fasting before surgery or other procedures (such as endoscopy), significant volume depletion, hypotension or unexpected kidney function deterioration.

Discontinuing the study drug is necessary for patients who develop diabetic ketoacidosis. Adequate contraception is mandatory, but if the patient, despite this, becomes pregnant, discontinuation of the study drug is necessary. If the study drug is temporarily paused for >4 months or for a total of >6 months, the patient will be excluded from the per-protocol (PP) analysis.

Patients may also choose to stop the study drug at any time. In cases of discontinuation, patients are asked to remain in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis.

Investigations

eGFR will be calculated using the EKFC creatinine formula.20 Plasma/Serum creatinine levels will be analysed locally.

mGFR will be measured at the Laboratory for Renal Physiology, Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet, using the iohexol serum clearance method with the Bayesian 4-point estimate,16 approximately 2 weeks after baseline, as well as after 1.5 and 3 years. Serum samples will be collected at 10 min, 30 min, 2 hours and 5 hours after intravenous administration of 5 mL of Omnipaque (300 mg I/mL).

Kidney transplant biopsy collection and analyses will be centralised. Light microscopic evaluation of formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded kidney graft biopsies, performing a semi-quantitative assessment of the percentage of graft interstitial fibrosis in the kidney cortex and inflammation outside fibrotic scars, will be conducted.21 Graft samples will be deparaffinised, heated in an antigen-retrieval solution and incubated with validated primary antibodies for markers of premature ageing and capillary density. Samples will be digitally scanned for morphometric evaluation using the software 3D Histech, Analysis Pro and QuPath. Digital software will estimate glomerular volume and mesangial volume fraction with QuPath. All evaluations will be performed by a single dedicated pathologist blinded to participants’ identities and clinical data. Changes from 6 weeks to 1.5 years post-transplant will be assessed. The exploratory assessment of histology parameters has not been evaluated with a formal power calculation.

Molecular analyses of kidney biopsy material will be performed by the Renal Research Group at Haukeland University Hospital and the University of Bergen, Norway. The studies, including mRNA and proteomics, will be performed on a subgroup of patients (n=25 in the intervention group and n=25 in the control group), matched for age, sex and mGFR at the same two time points. In total, 50 biopsies will be processed for RNA sequencing and proteomics.

An OGTT will be performed in individuals not taking glucose-lowering medication other than the study drug, by ingesting 75 g glucose dissolved in water. Blood samples for measuring glucose, insulin and C-peptide will be collected at 0, 30 and 120 min, respectively. The 2-hour glucose value evaluates glucose tolerance, while first-phase insulin release is primarily indicated by the insulin level at 30 min, which is included in the Stumvoll equation that estimates both first-phase and second-phase insulin release.22 Insulin sensitivity will be calculated using the Matsuda equation.23

Plasma/Serum creatinine, U-PCR and U-ACR will be measured at each visit at the local follow-up. Only urine dipsticks, without glucose, are considered appropriate due to the risk of unblinding if a combined dipstick detects glucosuria.

Blood pressure will be measured in accordance with local standard procedures. The same methods will be used to measure blood pressure over time at the local hospitals. Twenty-four hour ambulatory blood pressure measurements will be performed using the device already in clinical use at each local hospital.

DXA measurements will be conducted at the Section of Specialised Endocrinology, Department of Endocrinology, Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet. Total body composition will be measured with a narrow fan-beam Lunar Prodigy Densitometer (GE Healthcare), and all scans will be analysed using enCORER software V.14.10 (GE Medical Systems Lunar, Madison, Wisconsin, USA). The automated software can differentiate between subcutaneous adipose tissue and visceral adipose tissue within the android region of interest (ROI).24

Urine and serum samples for metabolomics analyses, collected at the 6-week and the 1.5-year visit, will be analysed at St Olav’s Hospital, Trondheim, Norway. Samples will be shipped in larger batches on dry ice. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry methodology will be used for the analysis, employing both a commercial kit (Biocrates) and an in-house method developed and validated at St Olav’s Hospital and the Norwegian University of Science and Technology, Trondheim, Norway.25 Additionally, ELISA methodology will be applied if verification analyses are needed. This exploratory analysis will focus on markers of fibrosis, inflammation and secretion function in the kidney tubules.

Adherence and concomitant medication

Adherence will be monitored through pill counting. Patients are instructed to keep their study medication boxes and return any remaining tablets at the 1.5-year follow-up. The tablets will be manually counted, and the remaining tablets will be recorded before a new supply is provided for the next 1.5 years. At the end of the study, the remaining medication will also be counted at Oslo University Hospital, Rikshospitalet.

Concomitant medication is recorded before randomisation and at the 1.5-year and 3-year follow-up; thereafter, it is at the investigator’s discretion, in accordance with local clinical practice and guidelines.

Study oversight

An external monitor will closely monitor the trial, and a Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) has been established. Safety evaluations are conducted after the first 40 patients, then after 140 patients have been followed for 6 months and subsequently at 1.5 and 3 years. The DSMB may request data whenever necessary. The treatment code will remain blinded unless disclosure is critical to the participants’ well-being.

Sample size justification and calculation

The Dapagliflozin chronic kidney disease (Dapa-CKD) study enrolled patients with CKD with an eGFR of 25–75 mL/min/1.73 m². The average rate of decline in GFR was 3.6 mL/min/1.73 m²/year in the placebo group and 1.7 mL/min/1.73 m²/year in the treatment group, with a difference of 1.9 mL/min/1.73 m²/year.26 The EMPEROR-Reduce study, which investigated empagliflozin in patients with heart failure, showed a 1.7 mL/min/1.73 m²/year difference in the slope of eGFR between the treatment and placebo groups.27 In the EMPA-Kidney trial, in which patients with CKD were treated with empagliflozin versus placebo, the difference in the rate of decline was 1.4 mL/min/1.73 m²/year, with the placebo group experiencing an annual decrease in eGFR of 2.8 mL/min/1.73 m². The average (±SD) mGFR among transplant recipients at our transplant centre is 53.4±15.3 mL/min/1.73 m² at 8 weeks post-transplantation (unpublished data from the Norwegian Kidney Transplant Registry).

We estimate a difference of 1.8 mL/min/1.73 m² per year in the decline of eGFR between the treatment groups. Anticipating an SD of approximately 4.6 mL/min/1.73 m²/year for the between-group difference, 134 patients per group are required to achieve 90% power at a 5% alpha level. To account for a 20% dropout rate in the PP analysis, 330 patients (165 per group) will be enrolled.

Efficacy assessment: primary and secondary analysis

The primary analysis is conducted in the ITT population, which comprises all subjects randomised to a treatment group and who have received at least one dose of the study medication.

Additionally, an analysis will be conducted on the PP population, which includes all subjects who have been treated with the study drug according to the protocol and have not experienced any AEs that may have deteriorated kidney function, such as acute rejection, severe infections, graft loss or similar issues. A list of subjects withdrawn from the PP population, preferably with reasons for withdrawal, will be prepared.

The safety population includes all subjects who have received at least one dose of the study drug, also known as the ITT population.

An initial analysis of the secondary endpoints assessed only during the first 1.5 years will be conducted once all included participants have completed the 1.5-year assessments. The primary endpoint and the other secondary endpoints will be assessed once all included participants have completed the 3-year evaluation.

Detailed statistical analyses are described in a statistical analysis plan. Briefly, the primary endpoint will be the difference in the chronic eGFR slope between groups over 3 years, estimated using mixed-effects modelling. The secondary endpoints will be evaluated as differences between the groups after 1.5 and 3 years, respectively. Missing data will not be imputed.

The primary endpoint will also be reanalysed in the PP population. Secondary endpoints will be examined in both the ITT and PP populations. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and normality plots will be used to assess normal distribution. The difference in mGFR delta values between baseline (2 weeks after starting treatment, ie, 8 weeks after transplantation) and the end of the study for the dapagliflozin and control groups will be evaluated using a mixed-effect model and a Student’s t-test for normally distributed data and the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data, whichever is most appropriate. A two-sided p value of <0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

The transplant biopsies will be examined for changes observed under light microscopy before and after treatment. They will be assessed using a semiquantitative approach to estimate the percentage of graft inflammation and interstitial fibrosis in the kidney cortex, excluding scar tissue. This analysis will be performed on the PP population. Data from the two groups will be tested for normality.

Discussion

In patients with non-transplant CKD and significant proteinuria despite receiving optimised standard-of-care protective therapy, SGLT2 inhibitors have been shown to reduce kidney and cardiovascular events.26 The effects are explained by improved glycaemic control due to glucosuria, restoration of tubuloglomerular feedback leading to afferent vasoconstriction in the glomerulus and, probably, a reduction in single-nephron hyperfiltration. A concern is that inhibiting SGLT2 in the proximal tubule causes glucose and sodium reabsorption to shift to downstream nephron segments, including deeper cortical and medullary regions. This could raise overall kidney oxygen demand since sodium transport efficiency varies along the nephron, with the early proximal tubule being more efficient than the distal parts. Nonetheless, shifting oxygen use to the outer medulla may simulate systemic hypoxia and stimulate erythropoiesis.28 A recent meta-analysis of SGLT2 inhibitors in >75 000 patients with non-transplant CKD showed that protection against kidney disease progression was evident across all levels of albuminuria and independent of diabetes status, suggesting that this drug class provides universal kidney benefits.29

Although the transplanted kidney differs in many ways from native kidneys, it shares certain pathological features that may justify the use of an SGLT2 inhibitor. We know that proteinuria is associated with graft loss and death.30 31 We also know that there is an increasing prevalence of pre-existing diabetes and PTDM related to poor patient and graft outcomes.3234

Haemodynamic mechanisms in a denervated single kidney may cause glomerular hyperfiltration, which promotes inflammation and fibrosis and is believed to play a key role in impaired long-term outcomes. Given its role in reducing renal hyperfiltration, an SGLT2 inhibitor may represent a new approach to preserving kidney function after transplantation.30 31 In 2019, a pilot study involving 44 patients with type 2 diabetes who had undergone kidney transplantation randomised patients to receive either empagliflozin or placebo.32 The study noted an initial drop in eGFR in the intervention group, suggesting a possible haemodynamic effect in denervated kidneys as well, without raising safety concerns. A 5% risk of genital fungal infection was observed, but there was no increase in urinary tract infections. Conclusions about kidney outcomes could not be drawn due to the small sample size and short observational period. Earlier, a prospective international observational study of glucose metabolism suggested that empagliflozin could be safely used as an add-on therapy in PTDM when patients are closely monitored.35 In 2022, an extensive retrospective, case-control cohort study from South Korea examined the safety and effectiveness of SGLT2 inhibitors in KTRs with diabetes. It suggested cardiovascular and kidney-protective benefits, similar to those observed in patients with CKD, along with a favourable safety profile.36 A Spanish multicentre observational study reported similar findings, including reductions in blood pressure and proteinuria, and noted a slightly increased risk of urinary tract infections, especially in female recipients and patients with a history of urinary tract infections.37 There is one ongoing randomised, placebo-controlled trial evaluating whether 18 months of daily SGLT2i (10 mg dapagliflozin) preserves kidney function in non-diabetic transplant recipients.38 Another large, ongoing randomised, placebo-controlled trial is evaluating the effect of an SGLT2 inhibitor, including recipients transplanted >6 months prior to inclusion.39 To the best of our knowledge, both of the studies are still recruiting, and no results have been published.

Introducing SGLT2 inhibition early after a kidney transplant might help protect the kidney graft from initial and ongoing hyperfiltration-induced damage, which can lead to atrophy and fibrosis. This will be assessed through protocol biopsies performed before and 1.5 years after initiation of the study drug, including proteomic analyses of kidney tissue. The protocol biopsies will also help distinguish the potential rise in creatinine levels after starting this medication from early graft rejection. A protocol biopsy is not performed in patients with a high risk of bleeding after biopsy and in those who are unwilling to undergo this procedure. Indication-based biopsies will be performed according to standard clinical procedures and will replace protocol biopsies when obtained shortly after a planned protocol biopsy.

In the Dapa-CKD trial, patients were prescribed a renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) blocker, either an ACE inhibitor (ACEi) or an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB), as their standard-of-care kidney-protective therapy. RAAS blockers, like SGLT2 inhibitors, have been shown to reduce renal hyperfiltration. However, the mechanism differs from that of SGLT2 inhibitors, as the RAAS blockers work by blocking angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction of the efferent arteriole. This dilatation lowers intraglomerular pressure and initially decreases GFR; however, over time, it helps preserve kidney function and reduce albuminuria.40 The efficacy and safety of ACEi and ARBs in KTRs are associated with smaller decreases in GFR and reduced proteinuria, and with a higher incidence of hyperkalaemia compared with placebo or no treatment.41 A randomised, placebo-controlled trial from 2001 involving 154 KTRs with hypertension (diastolic blood pressure ≥95 mm Hg) during the first 3 weeks after transplantation randomly assigned patients to receive double-blind nifedipine or lisinopril. The study found that both nifedipine and lisinopril were safe and effective for treating hypertension in kidney transplant patients receiving ciclosporin. However, patients receiving nifedipine, but not lisinopril, showed an improvement in mGFR over 2 years.42 Because of this, RAAS blockers are not the first-line treatment of early post-transplant hypertension at our transplant centre. Only a small number of patients are prescribed RAAS blockers in the early weeks of follow-up. When analysing unpublished data from the Norwegian Renal Registry for the periods 2015–2019 and 2020–2024, we found that in both periods, 67% of patients were prescribed a RAAS blocker within the first 5 years after transplantation. We assume that this pattern reflects the study patient cohort and that cases will be evenly distributed between the two treatment groups.
