Coarsening in the long-range Persistent Voter Model
Jeferson J. Arenzon, F. Corberi, W. G. Dantas, L. Smaldone

Abstract
We investigate the coarsening kinetics in a long-range variant of the Persistent Voter Model in space dimension d = 1 d=1 and 2. In this model agents can hold two confidence levels, normal and zealot. If normal, agents take the opinion of others chosen at distance r r with probability P ⁡ ( r ) ∝ r − α P(r)\propto r^{-\alpha} , with α > d \alpha>d . While in the zealot state, agents keep their own opinion. Normal (zealot) agents can become zealots (normal) if their opinion is equal (different) to that of the chosen neighbour. Through numerical simulations we show that, for any values of α \alpha , the model belongs to the same universality class of the long-range Ising model quenched to a small (non-zero) temperature, similarly to what was already known for the nearest-neighbor case. For the one-dimensional case, we further develop an analytical treatment, which reproduces the α \alpha -dependence of the correlation length and the functional form of the correlation function. These results not only confirm that the introduction of opinion inertia mitigates the strong interfacial noise present in the voter model, thus reinstating the basic kinetic mechanism of the Ising model, but also expand the applicability of this correspondence.



I
Introduction

Coarsening is a defining feature of systems undergoing an ordering process
Bray 1994
, and arises in contexts as diverse as liquid crystals
Sicilia et al. 2008
;
Almeida 2023
;
Almeida and Arenzon 2025
, superconductors
Prozorov et al. 2008
, and a variety of biological phenomena
McNally et al. 2017
;
Smerlak 2018
;
Siteur et al. 2023
;
Grober et al. 2023
, among many other examples
Scheucher and Spohn 1988
;
Cox and Griffeath 1986
.
In such systems, the space is partitioned into domains whose typical size grows over time.
Within the Model A universality class, scalar, non-conserved order parameter dynamics, the scaling regime is characterized by a single growing length scale, such as the average domain radius, which usually increases as
{\cal L}(t)\sim t^{1/2}
in systems with short range interactions.

This scaling behavior is captured by systems both with or without an interfacial energy cost such as, e.g., the quenched Ising model (IM)
Bray 1994
;
Arenzon et al. 2007
and the Voter model (VM)
Holley and Liggett 1975
;
Redner 2019
, respectively.
These two models represent important limiting cases of the system studied here.
Albeit equivalent in one dimension, for
d>1
their domain morphologies are qualitatively different due to the distinct mechanisms driving interface evolution: curvature-driven surface tension in the IM versus interfacial noise in the VM.
As a result, in
d=2
the VM sustains rough interfaces, and the fraction of active sites (those at the interfaces between opposite states) has a much slower decay than in Ising-like systems
Dornic et al. 2001
.
For
d\geq 3
, surface noise halts coarsening altogether for the VM
Corberi et al. 2024
while for the IM
Lipowski 1999
;
Spirin et al. 2001a
;
Spirin et al. 2001b
;
Olejarz et al. 2011
;
Arenzon et al. 2015
, the growth is interrupted at very low temperatures due to the large number of flat interfaces.

The original VM offers a minimal description of opinion dynamics approaching an absorbing consensus state, in which one opinion eventually dominates.
Spins on a lattice adopt the state of a randomly chosen nearest neighbor (nn).
Numerous generalizations have been explored
Stark et al. 2008a
;
Castellano et al. 2009
, including intermediate states between the two original opinions
Volovik and Redner 2012
;
Velásquez-Rojas and Vazquez 2018
;
Vazquez et al. 2003
;
Lambiotte et al. 2009
;
Wang et al. 2014
;
Balenzuela et al. 2015
;
Svenkeson and Swami 2015
.
Such intermediate states may represent complete indecision or more nuanced, moderate viewpoints that, together with other mechanisms that slow down state changes
Stark et al. 2008a
;
Stark et al. 2008b
;
Peralta et al. 2020
;
Vieira et al. 2024
, introduce inertia in the switching process.
A common outcome of several of these variants is the emergence of an effective surface tension, which drives the system toward consensus with power-law scaling that is similar, though not identical, to the IM.
The original VM is exactly solvable in any dimension, lattice, or interaction form
Frachebourg and Krapivsky 1996
;
Ben-Naim et al. 1996
;
Corberi and Castellano 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024a
;
Corberi et al. 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024b
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024c
, owing to the linearity of the moment equations for its probability distribution.
In contrast, because non-Markovian variants with intermediate states are usually analytically intractable (except in special cases that can be mapped onto general field-theoretic descriptions
Dall’Asta and Galla 2008
), most of the insights come from numerical simulations.

In the Persistent Voter Model (PVM), introduced in Refs.
Latoski et al. 2022
;
Latoski et al. 2024
, each opinion can be held with two confidence levels: normal or extreme (zealots).
Zealots are agents that resist opinion changes regardless of neighbors influence
Mobilia 2003
;
Mobilia et al. 2007
;
Galam and Jacobs 2007
;
Martins and Galam 2013
;
Colaiori and Castellano 2016
, a persistence that here is transient because confidence can be reset, returning agents to the normal voter state.
Such transient zealotry, known to optimize the time to consensus in some cases, mirrors critical thinking
Amaral and Arenzon 2018
;
Amaral et al. 2020
, where a sufficient amount of information may turn an opinion.
In opposition, while a normal voter is too volatile, changing its opinion with much less evidence available, the permanent zealot is an irrational agent whose opinion does not change despite the evidences.
Such stubbornness may also be implemented through agents that revert to their original opinion at random times
Grange 2023
.
Since the transition from a normal voter to a zealot depends on a continuated built up of confidence, i.e., on the past states of an agent, the general PVM formulation is non-Markovian.
Recently
de Almeida et al. 2025
, we have introduced a simplified, Markovian variant, demonstrating that the most distinctive features of the PVM do not require explicit memory.
Concentrating on 1D and 2D lattices with short range interactions, we have shown that the density
\rho(t)
of active sites decays with the same power-law exponent as in the Model A universality class to which the low-temperature IM belongs, i.e.,
\rho(t)\sim t^{-1/2}
.

Here we investigate the effect of including interactions with distant agents in the PVM.
The presence of zealots in the PVM offers the possibility to model the formation of extremists and, in this work, the differential role of distant and close contacts.
As detailed in the next section, a focal agent and a neighbor at distance
r
are chosen and the former, if not a zealot, may change its opinion or become a zealot depending on the opinion of its neighbor.
On the other hand, if it is a zealot it either remains in this state or become a normal voter.
Two different scenarios are considered.
First, the unrestricted case, no constraint is imposed on the distance
r
.
In the second case, the transition to and from the zealot state is restricted to be a consequence of the interactions with nearest-neighbors (
r=1
) while opinion changes remain distance-independent.

We perform simulations for several values of
\alpha
showing that, in both scenarios, the PVM falls within the universality class of the IM with analogous long-range interactions, i.e. with a coupling constant
J(r)
among spins decaying algebraically with distance as
r^{-\alpha}
.
However, the unrestricted case presents strong finite-size effects and a slower convergence to the asymptotic regime when
d<\alpha\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{SR}}
.
We also provide an analytical treatment in 1D, computing the two-point correlation functions and the correlation length for any value of
\alpha
.

IV

V

II
The model

We consider
N=L^{d}
agents located on a
d
-dimensional square lattice of length
L
.
In the VM with long-range interactions
Corberi and Castellano 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024a
;
Corberi et al. 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024b
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024c
, a randomly selected agent (spin)
S_{i}
at site
i
adopts the state
S_{j}
of another agent at site
j\neq i
, chosen according to the probability distribution

P(r)\ =\ \frac{r^{-\alpha}}{Z},

(2)

where
r
is the distance between
i
and
j
, and

Z\ =\ \sum_{p}n_{p}\,\ell_{p}^{-\alpha},

(3)

where
p
is an integer labeling the possible distances
\ell_{p}
(e.g., in
d=2
,
\ell_{p}=1,\sqrt{2},2,\sqrt{5},\ldots
for
p=1,2,3,4,\ldots
), and
n_{p}
is the number of sites located at distance
\ell_{p}
from a given site.
In the nn VM, Eq. (
2
) reduces to
P(r)=\zeta^{-1}\delta_{r,1}
, where
\zeta
is the coordination number.

The PVM
Latoski et al. 2022
;
Latoski et al. 2024
introduces inertia into the VM: an agent with sufficiently strong confidence in its opinion (a zealot) does not change state.
Whenever two agents with the same opinion interact, their confidences
\eta_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
increase by a positive increment
\Delta\eta
.
The PVM is thus non-Markovian, since
\eta_{i}
must grow over multiple interactions before reaching the zealot threshold
\eta_{i}\geq 1
.
In the VM, domain growth is driven by interfacial noise, and domain boundaries are rough.
In contrast, in the PVM, zealots tend to form inside the domains, while normal voters accumulate near the interfaces in a boundary layer whose width depends on
\Delta\eta
.
This interplay results in a reduced interfacial noise and in an emergent curvature-driven domain growth, similar to the IM.

In Ref.
de Almeida et al. 2025
, we proposed a simplified nn PVM that still reproduces this key feature of the original model, i.e. the emergent IM-like dynamics.
Differently from the original PVM, in this simplified version only the confidence of the focal agent is affected by the exchanges between neighbors.
Moreover, since for
\Delta\eta=1
the process becomes Markovian, the confidence
\eta_{i}
may be replaced by a binary variable
\theta_{i}
that is
+1
for zealots and
-1
for normal voters.
A normal voter becomes a zealot after a single interaction with a like-minded neighbor (
S_{i}=S_{j}\ \Rightarrow\ \theta_{i}=1
), and reverts to a normal voter after interacting with an opponent (
S_{i}\neq S_{j}\ \Rightarrow\ \theta_{i}=-1
).
In the present work we only deal with this simplified, Markovian version of the PVM.
The probability for a zealot to change its status in the nn PVM is

w(\theta_{i})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{S_{i}\theta_{i}}{2d}\sum_{\delta}S_{i+\delta}\right),

(4)

where the sum runs over the nn of site
i
. This remains valid in the restricted scenario mentioned above for which analytical results will be obtained (in the unrestricted case, results from numerical simulations will be presented instead).

In the long-range VM, the flipping probability for spin
S_{i}
is
Corberi and Castellano 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024a
;
Corberi et al. 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024b
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024c

w_{\rm LR}(S_{i})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}(1-S_{i}S_{k}),

(5)

where
k
labels the
n_{p}
sites at distance
\ell_{p}
from
i
.
In both scenarios discussed above, opinions follow a long-range, Markovian PVM
and Eq. (
5

w(S_{i})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1-\theta_{i}}{2}\right)\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}(1-S_{i}S_{k}).

(6)

Reference
de Almeida et al. 2025
showed that the nn version of the simplified PVM belongs to the IM universality class, rather than the VM class.
This will be expanded here to include its long-range counterpart as well.
Moreover, as discussed in Sec.
IV
, the restricted case where
\theta_{i}
is updaded only for
r=1

IV
Analytical results

The time evolution of the correlator
\langle\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}\rangle
, where
\alpha_{i}
may be either
S_{i}
or
\theta_{i}
, is given by

\frac{d}{dt}\langle\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}\rangle=-2\left\langle\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdots\alpha_{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(\alpha_{i})\right\rangle.

(7)

To simplify the notation, we define

C_{i,j,k,...}^{n,p,q,...}\equiv\langle S_{i}S_{j}S_{k}\cdots\theta_{n}\theta_{p}\theta_{q}\cdots\rangle.

(8)

where subscript and superscript indices are for the
S
and
\theta
components, respectively.
Notice that for the restricted case considered here, the equation for the correlator with only upper (zealots) indices are the same as in the nn model, and were already analyzed in Ref.
de Almeida et al. 2025
.

Starting from the 1-point function
C_{i}
,

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{i}}{dt}=\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})n_{p}\left(C_{i}^{i}-C_{i}\right)+\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}\left(C_{k}-C^{i}_{k}\right)\,.

(9)

Summing over
p
and
k
and using that
\sum_{p}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}P(\ell_{p})=\sum_{p}n_{p}P(\ell_{p})=1
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{i}}{dt}\ =\ -C_{i}+C_{i}^{i}+\sum_{p}\,P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}\,\left(C_{k}-C^{i}_{k}\right)\,.

(10)

Defining the discrete, long-range Laplacians

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2d\,\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{k}-2d\,C_{i}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2d\,\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{k}^{i}-2d\,C_{i}^{i}\,,

(12)

the above equation can be rewritten in the simplified form

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{i}}{dt}\ =\ \frac{1}{2d}\left(\Delta_{i}-\Delta_{i}^{i}\right)\ .

(13)

When the system is quenched from an uncorrelated, random initial condition,
C_{i}=C_{j}
,
C^{i}=C^{j}
and the above equation just states that
C_{i}
is constant (in that case both
\Delta_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}^{i}
are identically zero).

We thus move to the two-point correlation function for the spins, using
w_{\rm LR}(S_{j})
as defined in Eq. (
5
):

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{ij}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\left[w_{\rm LR}(S_{i})+w_{\rm LR}(S_{j})\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\left[\theta_{i}w_{\rm LR}(S_{i})+\theta_{j}w_{\rm LR}(S_{j})\right]\right\rangle\,.

(14)

Apart from the
1/2
factor, the first term also appears in the standard long-range VM (see Refs.
Corberi and Castellano 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024a
;
Corberi et al. 2024
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024b
;
Corberi and Smaldone 2024c
). Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{ij}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-C_{ij}+\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{jk}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle S_{i}S_{j}\left(\theta_{i}w_{\rm LR}(S_{i})+\theta_{j}w_{\rm LR}(S_{j})\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C_{ij}+\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{jk}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}\left(C^{i}_{ij}-C^{i}_{jk}\right)\,,

(15)

where we used the space-translation invariance of the system.
Grouping terms and summing over
p
and
k
one arrives at

\displaystyle\frac{dC_{ij}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\left(C_{ij}-C_{ij}^{i}\right)+\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}\left(C_{jk}-C_{jk}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2d}\left(\Delta_{ij}-\Delta_{ij}^{i}\right)\,,

(16)

with the following definitions for the operators

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=2d\,\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{kj}-2d\,C_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}^{i}

\displaystyle=2d\,\sum_{p}P(\ell_{p})\sum_{k=1}^{n_{p}}C_{kj}^{i}-2d\,C_{ij}^{i}\,.

In the following we will focus on the case
d=1
, where Eqs. (
) read

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=2\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{N/2}P(\ell)\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}C_{i+\sigma\ell,j}-2\,C_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}^{i}

\displaystyle=2\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{N/2}P(\ell)\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}C_{i+\sigma\ell,j}^{i}-2\,C_{ij}^{i}\,.

As in the nn VM, the equations for the one- and two-point correlations basically share the same structure, i.e. the one of a diffusion equation, here made more complicated by the presence of zealots and the long-range interactions among spins.
Eq. (
IV

The relevant intervals are

IV.1
\alpha>3

For sufficiently large values of

For the latter, a simple geometrical argument was put forward in Ref.

We argue that a similar relation, with a possible

This can be checked numerically and the results are shown in Fig.

Plugging this assumption into Eq. (

C(r,t)=f\left(\frac{r}{{\cal L}(t)}\right),

(19)

(20)

f(x)\propto x^{-\alpha},

(22)

for sufficiently large
x\gtrsim 1
.
The numerical results in Fig.
6
suggests that the same applies in the PVM, what will later be checked for consistency.
With this scaling, Eq. (
IV
) reads

\frac{\partial C_{ij}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{ij}-\Delta_{ij}^{i})=-xf^{\prime}(x)\frac{\dot{\cal L}}{{\cal L}}\propto\frac{x^{-\alpha}}{{\cal L}^{z}}\,,

(23)

where
\dot{\cal L}\equiv d{\cal L}(t)/dt
and we have assumed an algebraic growth
{\cal L}(t)\propto\rho^{-1}\propto t^{1/z}
.

We have seen in the previous section that
\kappa\to 1
as
\alpha\to 3^{+}
. This means that the two Laplacians in Eq. (
23
) differ only by subleading terms, which are difficult to calculate.
Because of that, the exponent
z
cannot be easily computed explicitly. Therefore, in the following,
we can only provide a physically-oriented, heuristic argument to determine it.
The typical size
\xi
over which correlations spread can be defined as

\xi(t)=\frac{\sum_{r=1}^{N/2}r\,C(r,t)}{\sum_{r=1}^{N/2}C(r,t)}.

(24)

Letting
N\to\infty
this quantity is finite only if
\alpha>2
. We can expect that, in this case, when
{\cal L}
has grown much larger than
\xi
, namely for large
times, the growth mechanism remains the same as in the short range case, at least in its universal features. This would imply
z=2
, for any
\alpha>2
. It remains to describe what occurs for
1<\alpha\leq 2
.
Notice that in the limit
\alpha\to 1^{+}
,
P(r)
becomes non-integrable. This means that single interfaces are aware of the whole system configuration and, hence, move deterministically in the most favorable direction, implying a ballistic behavior with
z=1
. This is observed both in the IM
Corberi et al. 2017
;
Rutenberg and Bray 1994
and in the VM
Corberi and Castellano 2024
in
d=1
. Assuming that the growth exponents are continuous functions of
\alpha
(which is always observed in coarsening models), the simplest ansatz interpolating between
z(\alpha=2)=2
and
z(\alpha=1)=1
is
z=\alpha
. The above argument reproduces the IM-like behavior observed in numerical simulations of the PVM (see Sec.
III
).

V
Conclusions

We have studied the coarsening kinetics of a long-range variant of the PVM, in which agents can be either normal voters or transient zealots and whose interactions decay with a power-law of the distance,
r^{-\alpha}
.
This Markovian limit of the PVM is interesting not only because it is more approachable analytically, but mainly because it has the same overall behavior of the original version. Allowing the confidence level to change only via nn interactions reduces the finite-size effects in the simulations and makes the analytical approach possible. By means of numerical simulations in
d=1
and
d=2
for various values of
\alpha
, we have shown that the long-range PVM belongs to the same universality class as the long-range IM (with analogous interactions), as already observed in the nn case
de Almeida et al. 2025
. Indeed, the interface density decays with growth exponents consistent with those of the long-range IM quenched to a small but finite temperature,
T\neq 0
. Furthermore, for the one-dimensional case we have provided an analytical treatment based on one- and two-point correlation functions, reproducing the
\alpha
-dependence of the correlation length and of the correlation function. These results seem to confirm that the introduction of opinion inertia qualitatively modifies the dynamics of the VM, reducing interfacial noise and inducing an IM curvature-driven growth mechanism even in the presence of long-range interactions.
