Decision-Flux Optimization-based energy management for battery-supercapacitor hybrid storage in electric vehicles: battery lifetime and efficiency enhancement
Authors: Zhen Wen, Maroun Kuoki, Leren Qian, Mohammad Khishe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Battery-supercapacitor hybrid energy storage systems can reduce the transient loading of lithium-ion batteries in electric vehicles, but their benefit strongly depends on the energy-management parameters used to distribute power between the two storage devices. This study proposes Decision-Flux Optimization (DFO), a simulation-driven feasible-descent framework for calibrating the parameters of a battery/supercapacitor energy management strategy under full driving-cycle constraints. DFO uses induced operational trace of a candidate decision vector which comprises of battery state of charge, supercapacitor state of charge, power limits, voltage limits and end-of-cycle recovery constraints. Constraint satisfaction is considered as an admissibility condition and not as a term in the objective function. The objective is a combination of normalized RMS battery power, normalized energy-loss indicators, and only accepted updates are feasible trace-level transitions. The optimized parameters are computed offline and then deployed in the real-time control layer. Evaluation of the method is performed on ARTEMIS driving cycle through simulation and laboratory-scale controller validation. Compared with GA, PSO, RB-EMS, MPC, SRCPO, DSCEO, and ADRL-EMS benchmarks, DFO produced the lowest RMS battery power in the reported case study and maintained feasibility across repeated runs. The aging analysis indicates a model-estimated lifetime of 3720 equivalent cycles before the 80% capacity threshold, which should be interpreted as a comparative model-based durability estimate rather than a direct long-term lifetime measurement. Results indicate that trace level feasibility gating can be used to enhance repeatability, battery stress mitigation, and energy management for efficiency.

Introduction

The electrification of transportation on the roads has gone at a rapid pace, and so has the need for energy management strategies that not only improve the efficiency, durability, and operational reliability of electric vehicles (EVs)1,2. While lithium-ion batteries are still the dominant onboard energy storage technology because of their high energy density and maturity3, their degradation under high power transients, frequent charge–discharge cycling and regenerative braking is still a major barrier to cost-effective and sustainable EV deployment4,5. Hybrid energy storage systems (HESS), usually consisting of lithium-ion batteries and supercapacitors, have become a viable architectural solution to separate energy and power demands to reduce the stress on batteries and prolong service life6–9.

However, the benefits of HESS are strongly dependent on the effectiveness of the energy management strategy (EMS). Poorly designed EMSs can offset the potential benefits of hybridization, resulting in unnecessary complexity, mass and marginal improvement in lifetime10. As a result, the joint optimization of energy management and system configuration has emerged as a major research problem in EV energy systems.

From a computational point of view, hybrid energy management is a well-constrained, nonlinear and simulation-driven optimization problem11. Decision variables that are related to power split, control gains, and storage utilization interact throughout the vehicle dynamics, converter limitations, and state constraints while feasibility regions are often narrow and fragmented. Conventional methods of computational intelligence—even if they are very powerful in unconstrained or weakly constrained environments—are faced with fundamental challenges when applied to such cyber-physical systems.

Population-based metaheuristic algorithms are still widely used because of their gradient-free characteristic and flexibility12,13. Nevertheless, because of their reliance on stochastic exploration and penalty-based handling of constraints, casual feasibility, slow convergence and sensitivity to parameter tuning is often the case when constraints are the dominant aspects of the solution space14–16. Model predictive control (MPC) approaches provide a systematic way to handle constraints, however, the effectiveness of MPC highly depends on the model fidelity, horizon tuning and computational resources thus limiting their scalability in joint sizing and management problems17. More recently, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has been investigated for hybrid energy management but training instability, poor generalization and unguaranteed feasibility issues prevent it from being reliable for safety–critical EV applications18,19.

These limitations imply there may not be enough refinements of the existing optimization frameworks. Instead, there is a new computational intelligence paradigm need that addresses the dominance of constraints, dynamic feasibility and system-level stability in a unified way, without the need for stochastic population evolution, predictive horizons and extensive offline training20.

Motivated by this gap, this paper proposes Decision-Flux Optimization (DFO), which is a novel optimization framework that reformulates hybrid energy management optimization to be a process of stabilizing decision-induced operational responses over full driving cycles. Rather than exploring the solution space using evolutionary or predictive processes, DFO inhibits unstable decision transitions based on their observed system-level effects with feasible and high-performance configurations emerging intrinsically. The validity of the proposed framework is demonstrated for a lithium-ion battery/supercapacitor HES on the ARTEMIS driving cycle, achieving excellent feasibility consistency, battery stress reduction and lifetime improvement compared to state-of-the-art methods.

The objective of this work is therefore not to optimize the physical sizing of the battery and supercapacitor modules, but to calibrate the parameters of a HESS energy-management policy under cycle-level feasibility constraints. The new scope is optimization of EMS parameters given a fixed battery/supercapacitor architecture. This scope is in line with the mathematical formulation, experimental validation and aging oriented comparison presented in the following sections.

Related work

Hybrid energy storage system and energy management

Battery-supercapacitor hybrid energy storage systems have been widely investigated as a solution to enhance the transient power capability and regenerative energy recovery capability in EVs21. Recent surveys highlight that although hardware configurations are fairly well established, the EMS is a dominant factor that determines the lifetime benefits and overall system efficiency22. Rule-based EMSs are still attractive for real-time implementation for their simplicity but are limited in adaptability with respect to driving conditions and battery aging states23.

To overcome these limitations, optimization-based EMSs have been proposed, e.g. dynamic programming, Pontryagin-based formulations24. Although these approaches offer theoretical optimality they are usually limited to off-line analysis and fixed system configurations, thus they are not suited for joint design and real-time deployment.

Computational intelligence and metaheuristic optimization

Metaheuristic algorithms such as particle swarm optimization, genetic algorithms, marine predators algorithm and Harris hawks optimization have been widely used in HESS sizing and energy management because of their flexibility and derivative-free nature25. Recent research in 2024–2026 is still proposing better or hybrid versions to improve the convergence speed and robustness26,27.

Despite their popularity, several studies report that metaheuristics have problems with constraint-dominated optimization, where feasible regions are small compared to the search space28–30. Penalty functions are themselves difficult to tune and frequently tend to distort the search to some extent, seeking the feasible solution that is not optimal; at the same time, the repair mechanisms add more computational overhead and bring more randomness. Such problems are especially pronounced in simulation-based EV applications, in which the fitness evaluation requires time domain vehicle and storage dynamics.

Predictive and learning based approaches

Model predictive control has been widely studied for hybrid energy management because of its explicit consideration of constraints and predictive capabilities31. However, the performance of MPC deteriorates under mode uncertainty and requires repeated optimization at every time step, which is a limitation for the use of MPC in co-design problems with repeated long horizon simulations.

Deep reinforcement learning has recently attracted attention as a data-driven alternative that is capable of approximating complex control policies32. Nevertheless, DRL approaches usually need a lot of training data, they are not interpretable and offer no built-in guarantees of constraint satisfaction during exploration. Recent reviews emphasize that the safety and feasibility of DRL-based energy management is still an open challenge33.

Research gap and motivation

The existing literature shows that although the current computational intelligence techniques can achieve performance improvements under certain conditions, the optimization principles they are based on are not inherently suited to constraint-dominated, cyber-physical energy systems. Most approaches of solving constraint’s view constraint violations as numerical artifacts to be penalized or corrected for rather than as manifestations of unstable decision-system-interactions.

This observation is the motivation for an alternative optimization paradigm that incorporates feasibility and system stability directly into the optimization mechanism. The proposed Decision-Flux Optimization framework overcomes this gap by operating on decision induced operational responses instead of abstract solution vectors that enable robust convergence without relying on population evolution, predictive horizons or learning policies.

Decision-Flux Optimization for HESS EMS parameter calibration

Novelty and relevance to possible direct-search techniques: The proposed DFO should be viewed as a trace-level feasible-descent framework that is specific to full-cycle HESS EMS parameter calibration. It is novel neither in the use of finite differences alone nor in the concept of feasibility screening, which is well established in constrained optimization. The contribution is the way these elements are combined for HESS energy management: each decision vector is assessed by an entire operational trace, constraint violation is treated as a hard admissibility condition and not as an additive penalty, and the update is only accepted if the induced trace is still feasible, and if it leads to a decrease of a dimensionless stress-efficiency objective. Unlike population-based metaheuristics, DFO does not keep a stochastic population or penalty coefficients to rank the infeasible individuals. Unlike MPC, DFO does not perform a receding horizon optimization problem at every control instant, but rather the EMS parameters are calibrated offline and executed in real-time. In contrast to learning-based EMS, no reward shaping or exploratory unsafe actions or neural network training is required with DFO. The approach can thus be regarded more as a feasible-descent deterministic simulation calibration method for constraint dominated HESS EMS problems than as a new optimization theory.

Feasible-descent interpretation and convergence limitation

The DFO update can be interpreted as a feasibility-gated local descent process. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal{X}}$$\end{document} be compact, and assume that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J({\mathbf{x}})$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V({\mathbf{x}})$$\end{document} are continuous over \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal{X}}$$\end{document} because the HESS model and EMS law are continuous under the imposed saturation limits. Assume further that the initial point \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{x}}^{0}$$\end{document} is feasible, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V({\mathbf{x}}^{0} ) \le \varepsilon_{v}$$\end{document}, and that the finite-difference directional score has bounded error relative to the local objective variation in the tested coordinate directions. Under the acceptance rule in Eq. (12), every accepted iterate satisfies \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$V({\mathbf{x}}^{k + 1} ) \le \varepsilon_{v}$$\end{document} and therefore remains in the admissible set \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal{A}}$$\end{document}. Moreover, the sufficient-decrease condition gives \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J({\mathbf{x}}^{k + 1} ) \le J({\mathbf{x}}^{k} )$$\end{document}, so the accepted objective sequence is monotone non-increasing and bounded below by zero. Consequently, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$J({\mathbf{x}}^{k} )$$\end{document} converges to a finite value along accepted iterates.

This argument does not lead to a global optimum. It is only feasible under the stated assumptions and is objective monotone. When the number of backtracking trials is permitted, if no feasible improving perturbation is found, the algorithm stops at a point that is locally stationary with regard to the tested admissible perturbation set. Thus, in the current study, convergence is reported objectively in the form of convergence trajectories, feasibility rates and run-to-run dispersion, not as a claim of global convergence.

Experimental validation

Experimental setup

In order to rigorously evaluate the performance of the proposed DFO framework, extensive simulation-based experiments were performed using a nonlinear HESS model implemented in the software tool of the Matlab/Simulink. The experimental design was designed to test performance under the constraint-dominated conditions, reproducibility and also fair comparison results with representative classical and state-of-the-art optimization methods including recent 2025 developments.

Modeling of hybrid energy storage system

The considered HESS is composed of a lithium-ion battery pack and a supercapacitor module by means of bidirectional DC-DC converters. The lithium-ion battery is modelled by a second order equivalent circuit model (ECM), with open circuit voltage nonlinearity and SOC dependent internal resistance34. Battery state-of-charge changes relating to current integration and the voltage response to changing current is transient polarization effect35. The supercapacitor is modeled with an RC dynamic representation to represent the variation of voltage under high frequency power exchange. The converter efficiency characteristics are included by nonlinear efficiency maps.

The system operates under the ARTEMIS Urban Driving Cycle which has frequent acceleration and regenerative braking events, which impose strong transient power demands36. The total driving horizon is set to 1200, with a sampling time of 0.1. Each optimization iteration requires a complete cycle dynamic simulation in order to ensure trajectory level feasibility check.

Optimization variables and objective function

The decision vector of the proposed DFO method has six continuous variables that control the hybrid energy management, such as power-sharing coefficients, SOC reference offsets, supercapacitor participation ratios, and update scaling parameters. All decision variables are bounded according to physical limitations of the storage devices and converters37.

The scalar objective function is a combination of battery stress mitigation and energy efficiency with weighting coefficients, alpha = 0.7 and beta = 0.3. The increased weight on battery RMS power is an indication of the importance of degradation-aware optimization for electric vehicle applications.

Practical deployment and computational complexity

The proposed framework consists of two computational layers. The offline DFO calibration stage is the first, where the EMS parameter vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{x}}$$\end{document} is optimized by using full cycle HESS simulations. This stage is not performed for all vehicle-control sampling instances. The second stage is the online EMS execution stage in which the optimized parameter vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathbf{x}}^{ * }$$\end{document} is fixed and the controller calculates the battery/supercapacitor power split based on the measured states and demanded traction power. Therefore, the online controller does not perform finite-difference simulations, population evolution, or policy training.

With n decision variables, K DFO iterations, and up to B backtracking trials, the number of full cycle simulations is upper bounded by K(n + 1 + B). A maximum of 12 full-cycle simulations per iteration are possible in the present implementation, with n = 6, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(Tn_{z} )$$\end{document}, where T is the number of time samples and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n_{z}$$\end{document} is the number of stored state/output channels. The computational cost for online execution is O(1) per sampling instant as only algebraic power-split calculations, measurement of the states and saturation checks are needed.

As the state-representation becomes denser, the offline cost is increased via the cost function \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{f}$$\end{document}, as well as trace-storage dimension, while the number of decision variables dictates the finite-difference evaluations. The cost increases roughly linearly with T, where T is the driving horizon. Therefore, DFO is best suited for offline calibration and supervisory-tuning for embedded EMS deployment, and the resulting EMS can be deployed directly on automotive controller for real-time implementation since the online layer does not need iterative optimization.

Data-source classification and experimental platform

This study distinguishes three data sources. First, the optimization and benchmark comparison is performed by MATLAB/Simulink full-cycle HESS simulation with the same ARTEMIS demand profile and constraints for all methods. Second, real-time validation involves the controller executing the optimized EMS on the laboratory-scale HESS platform or controller-in-the-loop setup and reporting to the user the data for current tracking, voltage response, and SOC regulation. Third, the lifetime curves are aging predictions determined by the model and calculated from the reduced order aging model described in “Experimental validation and assessment in durability oriented” section. To prevent mixing measured and extrapolated results, figures and tables have been renamed as Simulation, Real-time validation, or Aging-model estimate.

Experimental validation and assessment in durability oriented

This section confirms the feasibility of the proposed Decision-Flux Optimization based intelligent energy management strategy (DFO-IEMS) in terms of practical feasibility and durability. The evaluation is performed with the use of a laboratory-scale lithium-ion battery/supercapacitor hybrid energy storage system (HESS) driving under ARTEMIS driving cycles. The investigation is conducted in three steps: (i) validation of dynamic performance in the real time, (ii) electrochemical aging evaluation, and (iii) comparison of the model-estimated lifetime for cross-configurations.

Reported lifetime values are model estimates of equivalent cycle values, not long-term cycling measurements. A reduced order stress model was used to estimate capacity fade and resistance growth. The coefficients were determined by calibration based on the degradation data available as well as the lithium-ion aging trends reported in the literature. This model is for relative simulation under the same duty cycle, and cannot be seen as a universal predictor of absolute lifetime based on the chemistry of the lamp.

Real time intelligent energy management validation

Therefore, the first step of validation is to investigate if the optimized power allocation obtained by DFO can be implemented correctly in hardware with less electrochemical stress compared to conventional strategies.

The battery current tracking performance was measured experimentally in a typical ARTEMIS segment. Comparative analysis with advanced strategies of optimization and control

In order to fully assess the proposed DFO framework for hybrid energy storage energy management, a comparative analysis is conducted with seven representative strategies: RB-EMS, GA, PSO, MPC, SRCPO, DSCEO and ADRL-EMS. These benchmarks cover basically different paradigms, such as rule-based allocation, evolutionary search, swarm intelligence, model-based predictive control, constrained optimization, cooperative evolutionary optimization and adaptive deep reinforcement learning.

All methods are tested under the same conditions using the same LII-BAT/SUP-CAP configuration, ARTEMIS driving cycle, operational constraints and objective function formulation. This ensures that performance differences are those of inherent decision mechanism of each strategy and not parameter inconsistencies or scenario bias. Discussion

The experimental validation and comparative analyses prove as a whole that the proposed DFO framework is able to provide consistent and structural improvements in the performance of HESS. The results confirm not only are the observed gains not simply the result of parameter tuning, but indeed the result of the intrinsic formulation of the decision-flux mechanism which directly controls the power feasibility of the battery and transient redistribution. By introducing the awareness of constraints to the update law, DFO makes sure that power allocation decisions are always physically admissible while actively suppressing electrochemical stress drivers.

The consolidated experimental signals reveal the operational mechanism that is responsible for the stress reduction. The current in the supercapacitor follows the commanded reference trajectory very closely and there is no high frequency ripple in the battery SOC change. This means that transient load components are basically diverted to the supercapacitor and the battery is used to provide lower frequency load demands. Since battery degradation is very dependent on RMS current, peak C-rate and magnitude of voltage excursion, the attenuation of these stress factors can directly account for the model-estimated lifetime extension observed in the aging analysis. In contrast, the adaptive shaping capability is not available in rule-based EMS, and therefore stress levels are increased. Evolutionary approaches such as GA and PSO are less stressful than heuristic control but have an oscillatory allocation behavior due to the stochastic exploration. Adaptive reinforcement learning methods exhibit dynamic adaptability but come with the variability in the policy updates and training instability. Model-based predictive control and structured constrained optimization is the way to give smother allocation, but it has still limitations of the prediction horizons and local optimization. DFO is fundamentally different in that it maintains feasibility at all updates of the decision and controls the magnitude of the flux to avoid overshoot and thus both the average stress and variance are lower among runs.

The results of the convergence analysis also highlight structural advantages of the proposed framework. DFO provides faster stabilization and converges to a lower objective value as compared with GA, PSO, DSCEO and ADRL-EMS. The evolutionary and swarm-based methods have typical oscillations during the exploration phases, while reinforcement learning has fluctuation due to the adaptive policy improvement. Predictive methods such as MPC and SRCPO converge better but stabilize at slightly higher objective levels because of the use of local prediction windows. The statistical distribution of RMS battery power can be used to verify that DFO not only produces the lowest median stress, but also the least dispersion in repeated trials. This robustness is particularly important for the real-world deployment of HEVs, where repeatability and predictable thermal loading are important for the good operation of the system.

The extension of lifespan by DFO can be explained in terms of cumulative stress moderation, and not peak reduction. Battery aging is non-linear with respect to RMS current and voltage exposure; this means that long-term benefits are gained from sustained moderate stress reduction. The estimated improvement over the model-estimated lifetime compared to RB-EMS, GA, PSO, and even MPC based approaches is an estimation of the effectiveness of systematic transient redistribution. Although the model of aging used does capture the temperature and voltage dependence without having to explicitly use rainflow-based cycle counting, all the methods were tested under the same duty cycles and constraints, which preserves the validity of relative performance comparisons.

Energy efficiency improvements noted with DFO provide additional support for the structural effectiveness of the DFO. Reduced resistive losses with lower RMS current as well as the controlled use of supercapacitors are responsible for the higher overall efficiency. Unlike static buffering approaches which can over-cycle the supercapacitor, DFO has a dynamic approach to control the level of engagement of the supercapacitor depending on energy availability and constraint margins. This will balance utilization and avoid useless cycling, which will help achieve good peak shaving effect.

Several limitations should be mentioned. The aging model does not explicitly include depth-of-discharge accumulation and multi-stressor coupling effects and thermal gradients within the battery pack are not modeled. Furthermore, validation has been done under a standardized driving cycle and broader real-world variability need to be investigated. Future research efforts should involve a combination of explicit cycle counting methodologies, multi-cycle scenarios optimization and hardware-in-the-loop validation that can be used to enhance generalizability and industrial-readiness.

Overall, the results confirm that DFO is a structurally different and practical energy management framework. By incorporating the feasibility directly into the optimization update process and dynamically controlling the magnitude of the decision flux, the proposed approach achieves reduced electrochemical stress, greater convergence stability, robustness, estimated model-estimated lifetime and energy efficiency. These characteristics combine to make DFO a good candidate for a durability-oriented energy management in next generation hybrid electric vehicles.

Conclusion

In this study, an energy management framework for lithium-ion battery (LIB)/supercapacitor (SC) hybrid energy storage system in electric vehicles (EVs) based on Decision-Flux Optimization (DFO) was proposed. DFO solves the EMS tuning problem as a trace based constrained optimization problem, with the full driving-cycle response being used to assess each candidate decision. The method is used for offline EMS parameter optimization, while real-time operation refers to executing the optimized EMS parameters on the control platform.

DFO moved high frequency power demand to the supercapacitor to alleviate battery side transient stress under the ARTEMIS driving cycle. The quantitative results indicated a reduction in the peak battery current, current ripple, voltage drop, SOC fluctuation and RMS battery power. The DFO-HESS obtained a mean RMS battery power of 1.16 kW/cell, which is a 19% decrease from the mono-source configuration.

Comparative analysis revealed DFO to be a stable method in terms of stress reduction, feasibility preservation, convergence consistency, and computational efforts. It has demonstrated 100% feasibility under the benchmark protocol adopted, but it is not suggested to be the best in each individual run or each metric. The efficiency results also confirm the overall energy efficiency improvement.

The aging assessment predicted DFO-HESS to have a battery life of 3720 equivalent cycles. This value is considered to be a comparative estimate based on the models and not an experimental measurement of long-term lifetime. Future research will expand the method to other driving scenarios, a more comprehensive electrochemical-thermal degradation model, as well as hardware-in-the-loop or embedded automotive applications.

In conclusion, DFO is a feasible and comprehensible framework for constrained EMS optimization, which can enhance the battery stress mitigation, feasibility consistency, energy-efficiency performance of hybrid energy storage systems.
