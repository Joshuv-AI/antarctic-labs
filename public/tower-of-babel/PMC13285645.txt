TRABD2A promotes osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells by modulating TNF-α and IL-1β
Authors: Huibing Lu, Xiongjun Xu, Yuefei Guo, Li Tang, Jingxia Chen, Shigeng Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeriodontitis is a chronic inflammatory condition characterized by destruction of periodontal support tissues and dysregulated immune responses, particularly involving cytokines like tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β). Identifying molecules that regulate both inflammation and tissue regeneration is essential for targeted therapies. Following preliminary transcriptomic evidence on periodontal tissues, this study aimed to define the specific immunomodulatory and osteogenic regulatory effects of TRAB domain-containing protein 2A (TRABD2A).MethodsRNA sequencing was performed on periodontal tissues from patients with periodontitis and periodontally healthy individuals to identify differentially expressed genes and conduct functional enrichment analysis. TRABD2A expression and its effects on cell proliferation, inflammation, osteogenic markers expression, and mineralization were assessed in human periodontal ligament stem cells (hPDLSCs) treated with recombinant TRABD2A, Porphyromonas gingivalis lipopolysaccharide (LPS), or a TRABD2A inhibitor (o-Phenanthroline).ResultsRNA sequencing revealed significant TRABD2A upregulation in periodontitis tissues, along with elevated bone-related genes runt-related transcription factor 2 (Runx2) and receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB Ligand (RANKL) expression. In vitro experiments demonstrated that TRABD2A reduced the secretion of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) and promoted the proliferation of hPDLSCs. Furthermore, TRABD2A upregulated Runx2 expression and suppressed RANKL expression, while Alizarin Red staining results further confirmed its role in promoting osteogenic differentiation. The underlying mechanism may be associated with the enhancement of β-catenin expression.ConclusionTRABD2A exerts dual functions by promoting osteogenic differentiation and modulating inflammatory responses, highlighting its potential as an immunoregulatory and pro-regenerative target for periodontitis treatment.

Introduction

Periodontitis is one of the most common chronic inflammatory diseases worldwide, affecting a substantial proportion of the adult population and contributing to significant oral health and systemic complications. 1–4 Periodontitis is defined as the periodontal supporting tissues destruction, 5 which can lead to oral dysfunction and diminished quality of life if inadequately treated. This may result in tooth loss, affecting nutrition and systemic health, ultimately increasing medical expenses. 6–9 Several new and potentially efficacious adjunctive treatments have been investigated for periodontal treatment. Chlorhexidine (CHX) with sodium DNA reduces CHX-induced cytotoxicity and metabolic stress while maintaining antimicrobial activity. 10 Supportive periodontal care (SPC) prevents periodontitis recurrence/progression after successful completion of active periodontal therapy, which yielded better results compared to oral prophylaxis alone in reducing bleeding on probing (BoP). 11

The immune system regulates bone development, including both bone resorption and formation, to maintain bone homeostasis. 12 , 13 Additionally, inflammation is initiated as a protective response to infection or injury, serving as a signaling mechanism. 14–16 In periodontitis, this inflammation evolves into a chronic response as pathogenic bacteria persist and evade the acute immune response, ultimately leading to the destruction of bone and other periodontal supporting tissues. 16 When the immune system's regulation is imbalanced, cytokines play a crucial role in maintaining the homeostasis of periodontal supporting tissues through immune responses and cell signaling. Various inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin (IL)-17, tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, and IL-1β, significantly influence the pathogenesis of periodontitis. IL-1β directly reflects the severity of the disease, as evidenced by its gingival fluid correlating (GCF) levels correlating with the intensity of periodontitis. 17 Similarly, pro-inflammatory TNF-α serves a comparable function, indicating the progression of periodontitis. 18 Despite advances in periodontal therapy, the underlying molecular mechanisms driving the imbalance between inflammation and tissue regeneration remain incompletely understood.

Preliminary transcriptomic analysis of periodontal tissues from patients with periodontitis revealed that TRAB domain-containing protein 2A (TRABD2A) was significantly upregulated. This study aims to investigate the effects of TRABD2A on inflammation and osteogenic differentiation in human periodontal ligament stem cells (hPDLSCs) and to elucidate the underlying mechanisms, offering a potential therapeutic target for periodontitis management.

Materials and methods

Clinical sample acquisition

Periodontal tissue samples (3–5 mm³) were aseptically collected from 16 participants, comprising 8 patients diagnosed with chronic periodontitis and 8 periodontally healthy controls. Healthy control samples were obtained from free and attached gingiva in the anterior aesthetic zone during crown lengthening, while periodontitis samples were obtained from gingival tissue surrounding teeth extracted due to periodontitis (buccal/lingual free gingiva, attached gingiva, or interdental papilla). All participants were recruited from The Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University between January and March 2024.

Patients who met the diagnostic criteria for chronic periodontitis were selected, requiring at least 16 natural teeth in the oral cavity, with at least two non-adjacent sites exhibiting clinical attachment loss (CAL) ≥ 3 mm and probing depth (PD) ≥ 5 mm, and no oral mucosal lesions. In contrast, the healthy control group had PD ≤ 3 mm, no detectable CAL, no radiographic evidence of bone loss, and negative bleeding on probing (BOP) at all sites, as well as no oral mucosal lesions.

The cohort included 5 males and 11 females, aged 35–62 y, with equal sex distribution between groups. Exclusion criteria encompassed recent antibiotic use (within 3 months), smoking, pregnancy, systemic inflammatory or autoimmune diseases, and any periodontal treatment within the past 6 months. All samples were obtained during routine dental procedures by experienced dentists, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at –80 °C until RNA extraction. The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. II2024-216-01). All methods were carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Transcriptomic profiling and bioinformatic analysis

Total RNA was extracted from cells using FreeZol Reagent (Vazyme, China) according to the provided information sheet, and the Qubit 4 Fluorometer was employed for qualification. The Universal V8 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit for Illumina (Vazyme, China) was then used to construct the transcriptome library for the Illumina high-throughput sequencing platform. Following the kit's instructions, 1–4 µg of total RNA was input, and magnetic beads were utilized to capture mRNA, which was subsequently eluted and fragmented. The RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA, followed by a ligation reaction and library amplification. After circular amplification, the libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq™ X Plus high-throughput sequencer (Illumina, USA) using a 150 bp paired-end read length, generating raw sequencing data in FASTQ format. Filter-fastp (v0.23.2) was used to analyze the extracted gene expression matrix data [17], which was imported into the Bulk RNA-seq cloud analysis platform (https://rna.eanalyse.cn/) for further analysis. The DESeq2 (v1.38.3) R package was used to compare differentially expressed genes between the two groups and before and after treatment. The clusterProfiler (v4.6.0) R package was employed for enrichment analysis of these differentially expressed genes, with gene set databases primarily sourced from MSigDB v2023.2, including Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) gene sets. GO analysis was mainly used to compare changes in cellular components (CC), molecular functions (MF), and biological processes (BP). KEGG analysis focused on annotating the functional roles of the genes and identifying the main regulatory pathways that were affected.

Cell culture and treatment

Primary hPDLSCs were purchased from Guangzhou Jenniobio Biotechnology Co. and cultured in HyCyte® Human Bone Marrow Mesenchymal Stem Cells Complete Medium (Cos9X, BMHX-G101) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Cells were stimulated with Porphyromonas gingivalis lipopolysaccharide (LPS; Invivogen, tlrl-pglps) at 10, 50, and 100 μg/mL for 48 h. Recombinant human TRABD2A protein (YuBo, YB744101) was applied at 1, 10, and 30 ng/mL for 48 h. O-Phenanthroline (MCE, HY-W004544) was used at 20 μM for 48 h.

Cell proliferation assay

After 48 h of drug treatment, cells were harvested using trypsin (HyClone, SH30042.02) to create a single-cell suspension. The cells were then diluted and seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 10³ cells per well, with 10 replicates per group. The cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ for 24 h and 48 h. At the designated time points, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for 1 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader to assess cell viability.

Osteogenic differentiation

hPDLSCs were inoculated in six‐well plates with about 1 × 105 cells per well. After the cells reached the appropriate degree of fusion, osteogenic differentiation was induced by osteogenic induction solution (Pricella, PD‐014). Then the osteogenic induction fluid was renewed every 3 d. After 7 d of induction, the genes and proteins related to osteogenesis were verified by qPCR.

RT-qPCR

The RNA concentration was measured using a spectrophotometer (HIPIE, Aurora-900). cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (VAHTS, R423). Quantitative PCR was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (QIAGEN, 4993626) on a real-time PCR system. Relative gene expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCt method, normalized to GAPDH. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

A double-antibody sandwich ELISA was used to evaluate the levels of TNF-α and IL-1β in cell metabolites. The ELISA kits were purchased from MEIMIAN Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The assay was performed according to the kit instructions. Briefly, samples, standards, and HRP-labelled detection antibodies were sequentially added to pre-coated antibody wells, followed by incubation and thorough washing. TMB substrate was used for color development, and absorbance (OD value) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. A standard curve was generated by plotting the concentration of standards on the x-axis against the corresponding OD values on the y-axis. Sample concentrations were calculated using the curve equation.

Alizarin red staining

The third‐generation hPDLSCs were cultured in 6‐well plates and were treated with osteogenic induction solution (Pricella, PD‐014) when the cell fusion reached 80%. On Day 7 of osteogenic induction, calcium nodules were stained with ARS (Solarbio, G1450).

Data analysis

Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 24.0. Differences between the two groups were analyzed using t-tests, and one-way ANOVA was used with LSD and Tamhane's T2 tests for multiple group comparisons. A p-value of less than 0.05 or an adjusted p-value was considered statistically significant. Values are reported as mean ± SEM unless otherwise specified.

Results

Upregulation of TRABD2A in periodontitis revealed by transcriptomic profiling

To explore gene expression alterations associated with periodontitis, transcriptome sequencing was performed on periodontal tissue samples obtained from patients with periodontitis and periodontally healthy controls. Principal component analysis revealed distinct clustering between the two groups, indicating clear transcriptomic separation (Figure 1A). Differential gene expression analysis revealed a significant upregulation of TRABD2A in the periodontitis group, along with increased expression of the osteogenic transcription factor runt-related transcription factor 2 (Runx2) and the osteoclastogenic mediator receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB Ligand (RANKL), suggesting active bone remodeling in the disease state (Figure 1B). Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes indicated that biological processes associated with inflammation, immune activation, and skeletal system development were prominently affected (Figure 1C). These findings implicate TRABD2A as a potential immunoregulatory factor involved in the pathophysiology of periodontitis and suggest a possible link to the modulation of bone metabolism within the periodontal microenvironment.

TRABD2A attenuates LPS-induced inflammatory cytokine secretion in hPDLSCs

To investigate the role of TRABD2A in modulating inflammatory responses, an in vitro periodontitis model was established by treating hPDLSCs with LPS at varying concentrations (10, 50, and 100 μg/mL). Exogenous TRABD2A protein was applied at varying concentrations (1, 10, and 30 ng/mL) to assess its regulatory effect. Inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β were significantly upregulated in the LPS only group in a concentration-dependent manner, confirming effective induction of inflammation; while TRABD2A alone also increased TNF-α and IL-1β secretion in a dose-dependent manner, co-treatment with LPS and TRABD2A significantly reduced their secretion compared to the LPS only condition, suggesting that TRABD2A exerts a protective, immunomodulatory role under inflammatory stress (Figure 2A and B). TRABD2A expression was modestly elevated in the LPS only group, although not significantly different from control; while TRABD2A expression was markedly upregulated following exogenous administration, particularly at 10 ng/mL and 30 ng/mL, confirming successful induction (Figure 2C). Interestingly, LPS co-treatment appeared to suppress TRABD2A expression in a dose-dependent manner. To further validate this anti-inflammatory effect, the hPDLSCs were treated with the TRABD2A inhibitor o-phenanthroline. As expected, inhibition of TRABD2A resulted in elevated levels of TNF-α and IL-1β relative to TRABD2A-treated groups (Figure 3A and B), which was also corroborated at the mRNA level via qPCR (Figure 3C). These findings indicate that TRABD2A modulates cytokine dynamics and may protect periodontal cells from excessive inflammation.

TRABD2A modulates proliferation of hPDLSCs under inflammatory conditions

To investigate the effect of TRABD2A on the proliferation of hPDLSCs, cells were treated with varying concentrations of LPS, TRABD2A protein, or their combination, and proliferation was assessed after 24 h using the CCK-8 assay. In the LPS only group, cell proliferation was significantly reduced in a dose-dependent manner, confirming the cytotoxic and pro-inflammatory effects of LPS exposure (Figure 4A). Conversely, treatment with exogenous TRABD2A alone led to a marked increase in proliferation at lower concentrations. Interestingly, higher doses of TRABD2A resulted in a modest reduction in proliferation, suggesting a biphasic effect, where excessive TRABD2A may exert a suppressive influence on cell growth. In the LPS plus TRABD2A co-treatment group, cell proliferation was significantly enhanced compared to the LPS only group, indicating that TRABD2A can counteract LPS-induced cytotoxicity and support cell viability under inflammatory stress. Furthermore, when TRABD2A activity was inhibited using o-phenanthroline, a notable reduction in proliferation was observed (Figure 4B), reinforcing the role of TRABD2A as a protective factor promoting cell survival in hPDLSCs.

TRABD2A enhances osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells via WNT/β-catenin signaling

To elucidate the role of TRABD2A in periodontal bone remodeling, the expression of CTNNB1 (β-catenin), a downstream effector of the WNT signaling pathway involved in osteogenic differentiation, was examined. TRABD2A treatment significantly enhanced CTNNB1 expression in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 5A), and LPS stimulation alone also elevated CTNNB1 expression (Figure 5A), suggesting that β-catenin signaling is activated under both inflammatory and non-inflammatory conditions, potentially promoting the proliferation and differentiation of periodontal stem cells. In contrast, inhibition of TRABD2A led to a marked reduction in CTNNB1 levels (Figure 5B). A similar trend was observed in the expression of Runx2, another osteogenic marker (Figure 5C and D), further supporting the role of TRABD2A in promoting bone formation under inflammatory conditions.

Discussion

Periodontitis remains one of the most widespread chronic inflammatory diseases worldwide, marked by the progressive destruction of periodontal supporting tissues and alveolar bone loss that can lead to tooth loss and systemic complications. 19 The pathogenesis involves a complex interaction between pathogenic microbes, host immune response, and cellular processes governing tissue homeostasis. Despite advances in understanding the microbial contributors to periodontitis, the molecular regulators orchestrating inflammatory signaling and tissue regeneration remain incompletely defined. This study revealed that TRABD2A upregulation enhanced the secretion of TNF-α and IL-1β while promoting cell proliferation and osteogenic differentiation in hPDLSCs, potentially via the activation of the β-catenin signaling pathway. These findings indicate its role in regulating inflammation and promoting osteogenic differentiation in periodontitis.

The identification of biomarkers in the pathogenesis of periodontitis may hold significant value for both diagnosis and clinical follow-up. For example, studies have reported increased local levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-1β, TNF, IL-6, IL-17, and IL-23, in patients with periodontitis. 20 Salivary exosomal microRNAs could be used as a diagnostic biomarker in predicting periodontitis severity and cancer progression. 21 However, further studies are required to validate them.

TRABD2A is a member of the metalloendopeptidase family, reported to possess enzymatic activity that modulates Wnt protein processing and signaling. 22 , 23 The Wnt/β-catenin pathway is a regulator of osteoblast differentiation and stem cell fate decisions. 24 In this study, TRABD2A positively influences osteogenic markers, including CTNNB1 and Runx2, indicating enhanced commitment of periodontal ligament stem cells to the osteoblast lineage. Interestingly, inflammatory stimulation by LPS also elevated β-catenin expression, aligning with prior studies showing that inflammation can activate reparative Wnt signaling as an adaptive response. 25 The synergistic effect of TRABD2A under inflammatory conditions suggests it may fine-tune the balance between destructive inflammation and reparative bone formation in the periodontal microenvironment.

This study identifies the immunomodulatory role of TRABD2A, particularly its regulation of the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-1β, which are important mediators of periodontal inflammation and bone resorption. 26 The literature reflects the complexity of cytokine effects on osteogenesis, with TNF-α reported to inhibit osteoblast differentiation via NF-κB signaling in mesenchymal stem cells, 27 yet paradoxically capable of stimulating osteogenic transcription factors like OSX under certain contexts. 28 Similarly, IL-1β has been shown to suppress osteoblastic markers by antagonizing Wnt signaling pathways. 29 In this study, TRABD2A reduces LPS-induced secretion of TNF-α and IL-1β while simultaneously enhancing osteogenic differentiation, suggesting that it modulates inflammatory signaling to allow bone formation to proceed. Conversely, inhibition of TRABD2A led to increased cytokine secretion and reduced osteogenic markers, confirming its role as a molecular switch balancing inflammation and regeneration. Furthermore, TRABD2A suppressed RANKL expression, an essential osteoclastogenic factor that promotes bone resorption. 30 This dual regulation of osteoblast promotion and osteoclast inhibition highlights the potential of TRABD2A to maintain alveolar bone homeostasis and prevent bone loss in periodontitis. It is plausible that TRABD2A modulates Wnt signaling either by direct cleavage of Wnt ligands or receptors or through indirect mitochondrial regulation affecting downstream pathways. 31 Dissecting these mechanisms requires advanced approaches such as Wnt reporter assays, co-immunoprecipitation studies, and mitochondrial bioenergetics analyses. Moreover, in vivo validation using animal models of periodontitis with TRABD2A overexpression or knockout would be instrumental in confirming its therapeutic potential and systemic effects.

The identification of TRABD2A as a modulator of multiple cellular processes provides a potential therapeutic target that could be leveraged to simultaneously manage inflammation and promote tissue regeneration. This dual functionality is noteworthy, as current treatments often focus predominantly on infection control without directly promoting tissue repair. This study benefits from combining transcriptomic data with mechanistic in vitro validation, enhancing its translational relevance. However, several limitations offer directions for future investigation. First, the absence of in vivo periodontal disease models restricts understanding of TRABD2A's role within the complex tissue environment and systemic interactions. Second, heterogeneity among patient samples—such as age, sex, systemic health, and disease severity—was not fully accounted for, potentially influencing gene expression variability. Third, other relevant periodontal cell populations like immune cells and fibroblasts were not studied, yet likely interact with TRABD2A-expressing stem cells to shape disease outcomes. Lastly, the precise signaling cascades downstream of TRABD2A require deeper exploration to uncover direct molecular targets and pathways involved. Future studies should address these gaps by employing animal models to validate therapeutic efficacy and safety, as well as single-cell transcriptomics to resolve cell-type specific functions. Investigating TRABD2A’s role in immune cell modulation and cross-talk within the periodontium may reveal additional regulatory mechanisms. Furthermore, development of pharmacological agents targeting TRABD2A activity could offer innovative strategies for periodontitis treatment that balance anti-inflammatory effects with enhanced tissue regeneration.

The clinical relevance of these findings lies in the potential of TRABD2A to address the existing limitations of conventional periodontitis treatments. Current clinical approaches primarily emphasize infection control and the removal of pathogenic biofilms. Given that TRABD2A concurrently attenuates the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and promotes the osteogenic differentiation, it may represent a target for adjuvant therapies designed to foster tissue repair. Furthermore, the observed upregulation of TRABD2A in diseased periodontal tissues suggests its possible utility as a clinical biomarker for monitoring disease progression and assessing the reparative state of the alveolar bone.

Conclusion

Overall, the significant upregulation of TRABD2A in periodontitis tissues, suggests its potential as a clinical biomarker for monitoring the evolution of periodontal impairment and assessing disease progression. TRABD2A mitigates LPS-induced inflammatory cytokine secretion while simultaneously promoting osteogenic differentiation of periodontal ligament stem cells. These dual actions position TRABD2A as a promising therapeutic target that could shift current treatment paradigms from solely controlling infection to actively fostering tissue repair and bone regeneration. Future translational efforts focusing on the modulation of TRABD2A activity may pave the way for innovative, regenerative therapies to improve clinical outcomes in periodontitis patients.
