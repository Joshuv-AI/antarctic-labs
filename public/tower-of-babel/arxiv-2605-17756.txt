Linear independence of periods related to polylogarithms
Makoto Kawashima

Abstract
This paper provides the first criteria for the linear independence of multiple polylogarithm values over algebraic number fields. In particular, we derive novel results regarding the linear independence of products of polylogarithms at distinct points over an algebraic number field. Our approach is based on the explicit construction of Padé-type approximants tailored for multiple polylogarithms.



Key words
: Multiple polylogarithm, linear independence, Padé approximation, Rodrigues formula.

1
Introduction

Multiple polylogarithms are fundamental functions that arise naturally as periods of families of mixed Hodge structures (cf.
[
40
,
22
]
).
Their special values encompass multiple zeta values, whose arithmetic properties, such as irrationality and linear independence, have been extensively studied yet remain largely shrouded in mystery (see
[
8
]
).
In this paper, we establish a new criterion for the linear independence of multiple polylogarithms evaluated at algebraic points over number fields in both complex and
p
-adic settings.
As a corollary, we derive a novel linear independence criterion for products of polylogarithms at distinct algebraic points.

A primary tool for investigating the arithmetic of these values is the theory of Padé approximation (see
[
29
,
30
]
).
The classical origins of this approach date back to the work of C. Hermite
[
21
]
on the transcendence of
e
.
In
1782
, Legendre introduced the orthogonal polynomials that now bear his name, and in
1816
, Rodrigues discovered a remarkably elegant expression for them, subsequently termed the
Rodrigues formula
by Hermite (cf.
[
3
]
for the history of the formula).
Legendre polynomials naturally yield Padé approximants for the logarithmic function.

The application of Legendre-type polynomials has led to profound arithmetic consequences. Notably, this approach yielded the irrationality of logarithms (K. Alladi and M. L. Robinson
[
1
]
) and the irrationality of Riemann zeta values
\zeta(2)
and
\zeta(3)
(F. Beukers
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
).
These methods were further enhanced and refined by many authors, including G. V. Chudnovsky
[
9
]
, D. V. Chudnovsky and G. V. Chudnovsky
[
10
,
11
]
, G. Rhin and P. Toffin
[
31
]
, M. Hata
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
, V. N. Sorokin
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
, R. Marcovecchio
[
26
]
, and C. Viola and W. Zudilin
[
39
]
, primarily through the discovery of more sophisticated Legendre-type polynomials and their associated integral representations.
In a related framework, S. David, N. Hirata-Kohno, and the author
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
established linear independence criteria for polylogarithms by deriving Rodrigues-type formulas for their Padé-type approximants.

The objective of the present paper is to provide a unified framework for the construction of these Padé approximants.
To this end, we introduce the concept of the
Rodrigues ideal
, which allows us to understand the existence of Rodrigues-type formulas from a purely algebraic perspective.
By leveraging this algebraic structure, we clarify the essential link between the non-vanishing of certain determinants and the linear independence of the involved functions over the function field.

The present paper is divided into two parts. In the first part, we establish a formal general theory of Padé-type approximants for holonomic Laurent series. Following earlier ideas from
[
25
]
(see also
[
13
,
24
,
23
]
), we associate with a given Laurent series
f
a linear map called the
formal
f
-integration transform
, denoted by
\varphi_{f}
(see equation (
2
)). We show that the explicit construction of Padé-type approximants for
f
is governed by the structure of the kernel of
\varphi_{f}
, which can be analyzed through the polynomial coefficient differential operators annihilating
f
(Corollary
4.3
).

A central pillar of this general theory is the introduction of the
Rodrigues ideal
associated with a polynomial coefficient differential operator
L
. We prove that any nonzero element of this ideal yields Padé-type approximants for holonomic Laurent series whose image under
L
is a polynomial (Proposition
5.7
). This concept unifies several earlier constructions and provides a transparent algebraic machinery that bypasses the ad-hoc analytic or combinatorial identities often found in the classical literature. By utilizing the algebraic properties of the Rodrigues ideal, we provide a systematic guideline for the explicit construction of approximants, effectively handling the structural complexity of functions such as the multiple polylogarithms treated in Part II.

Furthermore, we address a question of fundamental importance in transcendental number theory: the criteria for the non-vanishing of the determinants of matrices formed by Padé-type approximants. We show that, provided there exists a suitable nonzero element in the Rodrigues ideal, the non-vanishing of such a determinant is equivalent to the linear independence of the corresponding family of functions over the function field (Proposition
6.1
). This equivalence provides a structural explanation of the determinant method underlying many Padé-type arguments, establishing a rigorous link between the existence of Rodrigues-type formulas and the functional independence of the series involved.

In the second part, we apply the general theory to the case of
multiple polylogarithms
.
We explicitly construct Padé-type approximants for these functions and derive, as an arithmetic application, a
new linear independence criterion for values of multiple polylogarithms
over an algebraic number field (Theorem
2.2
).
As a corollary, we establish the linear independence of products of polylogarithms at distinct points (Corollary
2.3
).

Furthermore, while Theorem
2.2
can be viewed as a generalization of the work of Sorokin
[
36
,
37
]
, it should be noted that his papers, particularly
[
37
]
, did not provide explicit statements regarding the linear independence of multiple polylogarithms or products of polylogarithms at distinct points.
Specifically, Sorokin’s approach heavily relied on the analytic properties of the underlying functions, known as the Nikishin system (defined in
[
27
]
), to establish the non-vanishing of certain crucial determinants.
In contrast, one of the key contributions of the present paper is to eliminate these analytic constraints by introducing a purely formal treatment of Padé-type approximations.
By characterizing the approximation properties via intrinsic algebraic structures, we are able to extend the linear independence assertions unconditionally over arbitrary algebraic number fields.

By applying the methodology of this paper to other Legendre-type polynomials, it is also possible to construct and analyze Padé-type approximants for other functions related to multiple polylogarithms.
As an example, in Section
10
, we provide new Padé-type approximants for products of logarithms.
As observed by the Chudnovsky brothers (cf.
[
12
, Sections 4, 5, and 6]
), these Padé-type approximants are expected to satisfy linear recurrence relations of Poincaré type.
If the asymptotic behavior of the magnitude of these linear recurrences can be determined using the Poincaré-Perron theorem,
it will allow for a precise analysis of the linear independence measures of multiple polylogarithm values.

Example 5.6

Proposition 5.7

(i)

{\rm(i)}

{\rm(ii)}

{\rm{deg}}\,R_{n}^{*}\cdot Q=dn+{\rm{deg}}\,Q\leq d(n+1)-1,

whereas

{\rm{deg}}\,R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot P=d(n+1)+{\rm{deg}}\,P\geq d(n+1),

a contradiction.
Thus
\Theta_{n}\neq 0
, proving
(b)
.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
.
Assume
(b)
holds and suppose that
1,f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
are linearly dependent over
K(z)
.
Then there exist a positive integer
n
and polynomials

(P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{d})\in K[z]^{d+1}\setminus\{\bm{0}\},\qquad\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\{{\rm{deg}}P_{j}\}=n+1,

such that

P_{0}(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(z)f_{j}(z)=0.

Write
P_{j}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}p_{j,k}z^{k}
.
From the above identity,

(19)

\pi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}z^{n+1}f_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{j,k}\,\pi(z^{k}f_{j})\in V_{n+1}(L).

Put
\bm{p}=(p_{1,n+1},\ldots,p_{d,n+1})\in K^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
Then

\bm{p}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{f_{1}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot 1)&\cdots&\varphi_{f_{1}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot t^{d-1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{f_{d}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot 1)&\cdots&\varphi_{f_{d}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot t^{d-1})\end{pmatrix}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}\,\varphi_{f_{j}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot t^{\ell})\right)_{0\leq\ell\leq d-1}.

By the
K
-isomorphism
\Phi
from (
3
) and Proposition
4.2
,

\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}\,\varphi_{f_{j}}(t^{n+1}R_{n+1}^{*}\cdot t^{\ell})=\varphi_{\pi\left(R_{n+1}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}z^{n+1}f_{j}\right)}(t^{\ell})=0.

The last equality follows from (
19
) together with

\displaystyle\pi\left(R_{n+1}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}z^{n+1}f_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\left(R_{n+1}\cdot\pi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j,n+1}z^{n+1}f_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\left(R_{n+1}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{j,k}\,\pi(z^{k}f_{j})\right)=0,

since the morphism
\pi\circ R_{n+1}
annihilates the element of
V_{n+1}(L)
.
Thus the matrix defining
\Theta_{n+1}
has a nontrivial kernel, contradicting
\Theta_{n+1}\neq 0
by assumption
(b)
.
Hence
1,f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}
must be linearly independent over
K(z)
.
∎

7
Padé-type approximants for multiple polylogarithms

Keeping the notation from Section
5
, we fix positive integers
m,r
and a field
K
of characteristic
0
.
Let us fix
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in K^{\times}
which are pairwise distinct.
Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{r}
the set of indices

\mathcal{S}_{r}=\{(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\cup_{k=1}^{r}\left(\mathbb{N}^{k}\times\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\}^{k}\right)\,;\,|\bm{s}|\leq r\}

and set

M_{r}=\#\mathcal{S}_{r}=(m+1)^{r}-1,

where no confusion may arise, we write
\mathcal{S}_{r}=\mathcal{S}
and
M_{r}=M
for simplicity.

For
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}
with
\bm{a}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{k}})
, we denote the following multiple polylogarithm

f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(z):=\mathrm{Li}_{\bm{s}}(\alpha_{i_{1}}/\alpha_{i_{2}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{k}}/z).

For a positive integer
N
, we define the differential operator

L_{N}=\frac{1}{N!}z^{N}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})^{N}\partial^{N}_{z}\in K[z,\partial_{z}].

One easily verifies that
{\rm{ord}}_{(1,-1)}(L_{N})=mN
and that
L_{N}
satisfies property
(P)
(see Definition
5.8
).
Let
r
be a positive integer. We define the differential operator
L
by

L=L_{(m+1)^{r-1}}L_{(m+1)^{r-2}}\dots L_{m+1}L_{1}.

The aim of section is to construct the Padé-type approximants of the multiple polylogarithms
(f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(z))_{(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}}
.

The following is the crucial functional properties of the multiple polylogarithms
(f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(z))_{(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

V_{n}(L)={\rm{Span}}_{K}\{\pi(z^{k}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}})\mid 0\leq k\leq n-1,(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}\}
.

(ii)

The functions
(f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(z))_{(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}}
together with
1
are linearly independent over
K(z)
.

(iii)

The differential operator

R_{n}=L_{(m+1)^{r-1}n}L_{(m+1)^{r-2}n}\dots L_{(m+1)n}L_{n}\in K[z,\partial_{z}]

belongs to the left ideal
I_{n}(L)

(
see Definition
\ref{Rodrigues ideal})
.

In order to prove Proposition
7.1
, we first establish several auxiliary results.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
k
be an integer with
0\leq k\leq n-1
.
For any
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
, we have

L_{n}\cdot z^{k}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}\in K[z]+\sum_{(\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{a}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{S}_{r-1}}K[z]_{\leq(m+1)n-1}f_{\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{a}^{\prime}}(z).

Proof..

Let
(\bm{s},\bm{a})=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k},\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k}})
.
A straightforward computation yields

\partial_{z}\cdot f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{1}{z}f_{\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{a}}&\text{if }s_{k}>1,\\
-\dfrac{\alpha_{i_{k}}}{z(z-\alpha_{i_{k}})}f_{\bm{s}^{\prime},\bm{a}^{\prime}}&\text{if }s_{k}=1,\end{cases}

where
\bm{s}^{\prime}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k}-1)
if
s_{k}>1
, and
\bm{s}^{\prime}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1})
,
\bm{a}^{\prime}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{k-1}})
if
s_{k}=1
. The desired relation then follows from the Leibniz rule.
∎

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
K
-vector space

V_{n,r}=\mathrm{Span}_{K}\{\pi(z^{k}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}})\mid 0\leq k\leq n-1,({\bm{s},\bm{a}})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}\}.

Note that
L_{n}
belongs to the left ideal
I_{n}(L_{1})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
(cf. Example
5.6
with
a_{1}(z)=z\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})
and
a_{0}(z)=0
). By applying Lemma
7.2
repeatedly, we obtain

\displaystyle L\cdot f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}

\displaystyle=L_{(m+1)^{r-1}}(L_{(m+1)^{r-2}}\dots L_{m+1}L_{1}\cdot f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}})

\displaystyle\in L_{(m+1)^{r-1}}\left(K[z]+\sum_{i=1}^{m}K[z]_{\leq(m+1)^{r-1}-1}f_{1,\alpha_{i}}\right)\subset K[z].

This implies
V_{1,r}\subseteq V_{1}(L)
and, more generally,

(20)

V_{n,r}\subseteq V_{n}(L)\quad\text{for any }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma
7.2
ensures that the following
K
-homomorphism is well-defined:

\pi\circ L_{n}:V_{n,r}\longrightarrow V_{(m+1)n,r-1}.

Lemma 7.3
.

For any
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 2
, the morphism
\pi\circ L_{n}:V_{n,r}\rightarrow V_{(m+1)n,r-1}
is surjective.

Proof..

We proceed the proof by induction on
r
.
Let
a(z)=z\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})
.
First, consider the base case
r=2
for any
r=2
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Fix
1\leq i\leq m
.
Lemma
7.2
and the Leibniz rule imply for
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
and
1\leq k\leq m
:

\displaystyle\pi\circ L_{n}(z^{\ell}f_{2,\alpha_{i}})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{a^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-1}{z}\right)^{(n-j-1)}f_{1,\alpha_{i}}\mod K[z],

\displaystyle\pi\circ L_{n}(z^{\ell}f_{(1,1),(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{k})})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{a^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-\alpha_{k}}{z(z-\alpha_{k})}\right)^{(n-j-1)}f_{1,\alpha_{i}}\mod K[z],

where
(a)_{j}
is Pochhammer symbol, given by
(a)_{0}=1
and
(a)_{j}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+j-1)
for
j\geq 1
, and
(-1/z)^{(j)}=\partial^{j}_{z}(-1/z)
.
Since the
K
-space
V_{(m+1)n,1}
is spanned by the set

\{\pi(z^{k}f_{1,\alpha_{i}})\mid 0\leq k\leq(m+1)n-1,1\leq i\leq m\},

the surjectivity
\pi\circ L_{n}:V_{n,2}\rightarrow V_{(m+1)n,1}
is equivalent to

(*)

The
K
-space
K[z]_{\leq(m+1)n-1}
is spanned by the
(m+1)n
polynomials

\displaystyle a^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-1}{z}\right)^{(n-j-1)},

\displaystyle a^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-\alpha_{k}}{z(z-\alpha_{k})}\right)^{(n-j-1)}

for
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
and
1\leq k\leq m
.

Taking into account the identity
\tfrac{-\alpha_{k}}{z(z-\alpha_{k})}=\tfrac{1}{z}-\tfrac{1}{z-\alpha_{k}}
and the fact that
\alpha_{k}
are pairwise distinct, the partial fraction expansion yields:

\biggl\{\dfrac{P(z)}{a^{n}(z)}\Bigm|P(z)\in K[z]_{\leq(m+1)n-1}\biggr\}={\rm{Span}}_{K}\biggl\{\left(\dfrac{-1}{z}\right)^{(\ell)},\left(\dfrac{-\alpha_{k}}{z(z-\alpha_{k})}\right)^{(\ell)}\Bigm|0\leq\ell\leq n-1,1\leq k\leq m\biggr\}.

This confirms that assertion
(*)
holds, establishing the base case.

Now, assume the statement holds for some
r\geq 2
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
r+1
, the inductive hypothesis implies it is sufficient to prove the surjectivity of the induced morphism:

(21)

\displaystyle\pi\circ L_{n}:V_{n,r+1}/V_{n,r}\longrightarrow V_{(m+1)n,r}/V_{(m+1)n,r-1}.

Fix
(\bm{s},\bm{a})=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k},\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{k}})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
.
By Lemma
7.2
and the Leibniz rule, for
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle\pi\circ L_{n}(z^{\ell}f_{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}+1),\bm{a}})

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{a^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-1}{z}\right)^{(n-j-1)}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}\mod V_{(m+1)n,r},

\displaystyle\pi\circ L_{n}(z^{\ell}f_{(\bm{s},1),(\bm{a},\alpha_{k})})

\displaystyle\equiv\dfrac{a^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{n}{j}(\ell-j+1)_{j}\left(\dfrac{-\alpha_{k}}{z(z-\alpha_{k})}\right)^{(n-j-1)}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}\mod V_{(m+1)n,r}.

Since the space
V_{(m+1)n,r}/V_{(m+1)n,r-1}
is spanned by the set

\Bigl\{\pi(z^{k}f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}})\mod V_{(m+1)n,r-1}\mid 0\leq k\leq(m+1)n-1,({\bm{s},\bm{a}})\in\mathcal{S}_{r}\Bigr\},

the same argument used in the
r=2
case ensures the surjectivity of (
21
).
This completes the induction.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

(i) By the definition of

, we have
{\rm{dim}}_{K}V_{n}(L)\leq nM_{r}
. In view of (

), it suffices to show that

{\rm{dim}}_{K}V_{n,r}=nM_{r}\quad\text{for all }n,r\in\mathbb{N}.

We proceed by induction on

.
For
r=1
, since the
\alpha_{i}
are pairwise distinct, the functions

.

Next, assume that (

) holds for

7.3
implies the following chain of inequalities:

\displaystyle nM_{r+1}\geq{\rm{dim}}_{K}V_{n,r+1}

(ii) This follows immediately from Lemma

(iii) By item (i) and the definition of the ideal

8
Estimates

We keep the notation in Section
7
.
We fix positive integers
m,r
and an algebraic number field
K
.
Let
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})\in(K^{\times})^{m}
be a vector whose coordinates are pairwise distinct.
Recall that
M=(m+1)^{r}-1
and the morphism
\varphi_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}
defined in (
23
) for
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}
.

For
0\leq\ell\leq M
and
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}
, Corollary
7.4
states that the polynomials

P_{n,\ell}(z)=\mathrm{Eval}_{t=z}(R^{*}_{n}\cdot z^{\ell}),\quad Q_{n,\bm{s},\bm{a},\ell}(z)=\varphi_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}\left(\frac{P_{n,\ell}(z)-P_{n,\ell}(t)}{z-t}\right)

form Padé-type approximants to
(f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(z))_{(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}}
.

In this section, we describe the asymptotic behavior as
n\to\infty
of the polynomials
P_{n,\ell}(z)
and
Q_{n,\bm{s},\bm{a},\ell}(z)
evaluated at
\beta\in K
, as well as the Padé-type approximation

P_{n,\ell}(\beta)f_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(\beta)-Q_{n,\bm{s},\bm{a},\ell}(\beta)

for
\beta\in K
with
|\beta|_{v}>H_{v}(\bm{\alpha})
.

Furthermore, we fix the following notation. Let
v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}
.
Recall
\varepsilon_{v}=1
if
v\mid\infty
and
\varepsilon_{v}=0
if
v\nmid\infty
.
For a polynomial
P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p_{k}z^{k}\in K[z]
, we denote by
\|P\|_{v}
the maximum
v
-adic absolute value of its coefficients:

\|P\|_{v}:=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\{|p_{k}|_{v}\}.

For
\beta\in K
, using the triangle inequality when
v\in\mathfrak{M}^{\infty}_{K}
(resp. the strong triangle inequality when
v\in\mathfrak{M}^{f}_{K}
), we obtain

(25)

\log|P(\beta)|_{v}\leq\varepsilon_{v}\log({\rm{deg}}P+1)+\log\|P\|_{v}+{\rm{deg}}P\cdot h_{v}(\beta).

Let
n
be a positive integer. We denote by
d_{n}
the least common multiple of
1,\dots,n
. In the following, we define the differential operator

\mathcal{L}_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\partial^{n}_{z}z^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})^{n}\in K[z,\partial_{z}].

Note that

P_{n,\ell}(z)=(-1)^{\tfrac{M}{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n}\mathcal{L}_{(m+1)n}\ldots\mathcal{L}_{(m+1)^{r-1}n}\cdot z^{\ell}.

In the following, we refer the symbols
o(1)
and
o(n)
refer to the limit as
n\to\infty
.

We begin by establishing the following fundamental lemma, which provides the necessary estimates for the
v
-adic norms of our constructions.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
v\in\mathfrak{M}_{K}
,
P\in K[z]
be a polynomial of degree
N
, and
n
be a positive integer. The following assertions hold
:

(i)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n!}\partial^{n}_{z}\cdot z^{n}P\right\|_{v}\leq\binom{n+N}{n}^{\frac{d_{v}}{d}\varepsilon_{v}}\|P\|_{v}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})^{n}\right\|_{v}\leq(n+1)^{m\varepsilon_{v}}\cdot 2^{mn\frac{d_{v}}{d}\varepsilon_{v}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}H_{v}(\alpha_{i})^{n}
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{n}\cdot P\|_{v}\leq(mn+N+1)^{(m+1)\varepsilon_{v}}\left(2^{mn}\binom{(m+1)n+N}{n}\right)^{\frac{d_{v}}{d}\varepsilon_{v}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}H_{v}(\alpha_{i})^{n}\|P\|_{v}
.

(iv)

For
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

|\varphi_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}(P(t))|_{v}\leq(N+1)^{(r+1)\varepsilon_{v}}|d_{N+1}^{r}|_{v}^{\varepsilon_{v}-1}H_{v}(\bm{\alpha})^{N+1}\|P\|_{v}.

(v)

For
(\bm{s},\bm{a})\in\mathcal{S}
, let
Q(z)=\varphi_{\bm{s},\bm{a}}\left(\frac{P(z)-P(t)}{z-t}\right)
. Then

\|Q\|_{v}\leq(N+1)^{(r+1)\varepsilon_{v}}|d_{N+1}^{r}|_{v}^{\varepsilon_{v}-1}H_{v}(\bm{\alpha})^{N+1}\|P\|_{v}.

Proof..

Write
P=\sum_{k=0}^{N}p_{k}z^{k}
.

(i) A direct computation yields the identity

\frac{1}{n!}\partial_{z}^{n}\cdot z^{n}P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\binom{j+n}{n}p_{j}z^{j}.

By the definition of the
v
-adic norm, we obtain

\left\|\frac{1}{n!}\partial_{z}^{n}\cdot z^{n}P(z)\right\|_{v}\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\{\left|\binom{j+n}{n}\right|_{v}|p_{j}|_{v}\right\}\leq\binom{n+N}{n}^{\frac{d_{v}}{d}\varepsilon_{v}}\|P\|_{v},

which proves (i).

(ii) Expanding the product, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{mn}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k_{i}\leq n\\
\sum k_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{k_{i}}(-\alpha_{i})^{n-k_{i}}\right)z^{k}.

Using the inequality
\binom{n}{k}\leq 2^{n}
and applying the ultrametric inequality (if
v\in\mathfrak{M}^{f}_{K}
) or the standard triangle inequality (if
v\in\mathfrak{M}^{\infty}_{K}
), we obtain the desired estimate.

(iii) For any
Q\in K[z]
, the
v
-adic norm satisfies

(26)

\|PQ\|_{v}\leq({\rm{deg}}P+{\rm{deg}}Q+1)^{\varepsilon_{v}}\|P\|_{v}\|Q\|_{v}.

Applying (i) to the polynomial
P\prod_{i=1}^{m}(z-\alpha_{i})^{n}

\Delta_{n}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}

Proof..

By Proposition
6.1
, it suffices to show that

R_{n}

satisfies properties
{\rm(i)}
and
{\rm(ii)}
in Proposition
6.1
and the Laurent series
1
and
(\log^{s}(1-1/z))_{1\leq s\leq m}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}(z)
.
Equation (
6
) together with the definition of
{\rm{ord}}_{(1,-1)}
yields

{\rm{ord}}_{(1,-1)}(R_{n})=2mn-mn={\rm{ord}}_{(1,-1)}(L_{m})n,

so
R_{n}
satisfies property
{\rm(i)}
.
Next, combining equation (
35
) with Lemma
5.9
implies that
R_{n}
satisfies property
(P)
.
Finally, since
\log(1-1/z)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{Q}(z)
,
1
and
(\log^{s}(1-1/z))_{1\leq s\leq m}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}(z)
.
The proof of Corollary
10.5
is now complete.
