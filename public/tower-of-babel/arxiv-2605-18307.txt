Null Controllability for Degenerate Parabolic Equations with Internal Control Applied on a Measurable Subset This work was carried out with the support of the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant nos. 12131008 and U23B2033, and National Key R&D Program of China under grant no. 2024YFA1013101.
Dong-Hui Yang, Mengze Gu, Bao-Zhu Guo, Ghadir Shokor

Abstract
This work serves as a continuation of our preceding paper [ 28 ] . In that study, we presented a separable variable method to derive the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality for a specific degenerate parabolic equation and subsequently employed it to demonstrate the null controllability of said equation when internal control is applied to an open subset. In the current paper, we reapply the separable variable method to attain the Lebeau-Robbiano spectral inequality for a different degenerate parabolic equation, and we substantiate the null controllability of this equation with internal control acting on a measurable subset. This approach may offer an alternative means of proving controllability results for degenerate parabolic equations. Keywords: Degenerate partial differential equations, shape design approximation, Carleman estimate, null controllability. AMS subject classifications (2010): 35J70, 35K65, 49Q10, 93B05.

Preliminary results

In this section, we introduce the weighted Sobolev spaces associated with equation (

2.1
Solution spaces

Define

H^{1}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\colon\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\}.

Its inner product and norm are given by

(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot A\nabla v+uv)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
u,v\in H^{1}(\Omega;w)
.
Set

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=\mbox{the closure of the set $C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)$ in }H^{1}(\Omega;w).

and

H^{-1}(\Omega;w)=\mbox{the dual space of }H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w).

Define

H^{2}(\Omega;w)=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega;w)\colon\mathcal{A}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}.

Its inner product and norm are defined by

(u,v)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{A}u)(\mathcal{A}v)\mathrm{d}x+(u,v)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)},\quad\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
u,v\in H^{2}(\Omega;w)
. Set

D(\mathcal{A})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\cap H^{2}(\Omega;w).

It is well-known that

(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}),\mbox{ and }(H^{1}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}(\Omega;w)}),\mbox{ and }(H^{2}(\Omega;w),(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{2}(\Omega;w)})

are Hilbert spaces (see, e.g.,
[
14
,
17
]
.)

The following lemma presents a Hardy inequality.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
\alpha
, such that

\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}z\leq C\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha}(\partial_{r}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}z

for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

Proof.

For each
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
, we compute

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}z&=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-2}|u(\theta,r)-u(\theta,0)|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-2}\left|\int_{0}^{r}\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\alpha-2}\left(\int_{0}^{r}s^{\beta}|\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)\left(\int_{0}^{r}s^{-\beta}\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\leq\frac{1}{1-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{r}r^{\alpha-\beta-1}s^{\beta}|\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{1-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}r^{\alpha-\beta-1}s^{\beta}|\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(1-\beta)(\beta-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\alpha}|\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&=\frac{1}{(1-\beta)(\beta-\alpha)}\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha}|\partial_{r}u(\theta,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}z.\end{split}

Taking
\beta=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}
, we complete the proof of the lemma.

\square

Remark 2.1

From Lemma
2.1
we have

\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\mathrm{d}z\leq\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}z\leq C\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}z,

where the constant
C>0
depends only on
\alpha
. Hence, in what follows, we use the inner product and norm

(u,v)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla v\mathrm{d}x,\quad\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}=(u,u)_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{\frac{1}{2}}

on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
, respectively.

Lemma 2.2

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, the embedding
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact.

Proof.

Assume that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
is a bounded sequence.
Our objective is to demonstrate the existence of a subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Given that
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
is a Hilbert space, there exists a subsequence of
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is
still denoted by itself, and an element
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.
To prove that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it suffices to consider an abstract subsequence and establish the result for the case

u_{0}=0
(if this is not the case, we can replace
u_{n}
with
u_{n}-u_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
).

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. On the one hand, for each
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, define

\Omega_{\delta}=\mathbb{T}\times(\delta,1)
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}r^{2-\alpha}r^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\delta^{2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}r^{\alpha-2}u^{2}\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\delta^{2-\alpha}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot A\nabla u\mathrm{d}x=C\delta^{2-\alpha}\|u\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}^{2}\end{split}

for all
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. Then, we select
\delta_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that for all

\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
such that
\int_{\Omega-\Omega_{\delta}}u_{n}^{2}\mathrm{d}x<\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{2}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. On the other hand, for this fixed
\delta_{0}
,
since the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}};w|_{\Omega_{\delta_{0}}})=H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}})
is compact and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}};w|_{\Omega_{\delta_{0}}})
, there exists at least one
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|u_{n_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\delta_{0}})}<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon
. Combining these two facts, we conclude that
\|u_{n_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

We recall that
\{g_{1,n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cos n\theta\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{g_{2,n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sin n\theta\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}

The following Lemma

, as demonstrated in
[

the set
\{g_{i,n}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sin m\pi\theta)\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\otimes H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda)

Lemma 3.3

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)=H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\widehat{\otimes}H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w)
.

Proof.

For every
\varphi_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
\varphi_{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w)
, we observe:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})\cdot A\nabla(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})\mathrm{d}x&=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{\theta}\varphi_{1})^{2}(\varphi_{2})^{2}dz+\int_{\Omega}(\varphi_{1})^{2}\left[r^{\alpha}(\partial_{r}\varphi_{2})^{2}\right]dz\\
&\leq 2\|\varphi_{1}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w)}^{2},\end{split}

which implies
\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. Consequently,
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\otimes H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w)\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
. Hence,
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\widehat{\otimes}H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w)\subset H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)
.

We now prove the converse implication.
Since

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\widehat{\otimes}H_{0}^{1}(\Lambda;w).

This completes the proof of the lemma.

&\leq CT^{7}e^{Cs_{0}\frac{1}{T^{8}}}s_{0}^{3}\iint_{(a,b)\times(0,T)}\varphi_{i,n}^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

\alpha

a
,

.

\square

5
Observability and controllability on measurable subset

In this section, we employ an analytical approach to establish an observability inequality (Theorem
1.1
) on measurable sets. As a direct consequence, we derive the null controllability of equation (
1.1
) under
L^{\infty}(Q)
controls.

Proposition 1

Let
0<a<b\leq 1
. Then, there exist two constants
C\geq 1
and
\rho\in(0,1]
, depending solely on
\alpha
,
a
, and
b
, such that for any solution
\varphi
of equation (
1.1
) with
f=0
, the following estimate holds:

\begin{split}\left|\partial_{\theta}^{j}\partial_{r}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,t)\right|\leq\frac{Ce^{\frac{C}{t-s}}(j+k)!l!}{\rho^{j+k}\left(\frac{t-s}{2}\right)^{l}}|\varphi(\cdot,s)|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\text{for all }j,k,l\in\mathbb{N},\end{split}

(5.1)

where
(\theta,r)\in\mathbb{T}\times(a,b)
and
0\leq s<t
.

Proof.

Denote
\omega=\mathbb{T}\times(a,b)
.
It suffices to prove the case
s=0
. Clearly, if

\varphi^{0}=\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}^{0}\Phi_{n}
with
\varphi_{n}^{0}=(\varphi^{0},\Phi_{n})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, then

\begin{split}\varphi(z,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}^{0}e^{-\lambda_{n}t}\Phi_{n},\end{split}

where
\lambda_{n}
and
\Phi_{n}
are defined in Notation
2.1
. We introduce a new function

\begin{split}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}^{0}e^{-\lambda_{n}t+\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\tau}\Phi_{n}(\theta,r),\ (\theta,r)\in\Omega,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}},t>0.\end{split}

(5.2)

Then we have

\varphi(\theta,r,0,t)=\varphi(\theta,r,t),\mbox{ and }\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}^{0}(-\lambda_{n})^{l}e^{-\lambda_{n}t+\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\tau}\Phi_{n}(\theta,r).

(5.3)

Moreover, for each
t>0
, the function
\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)
satisfies

\left(-\mathcal{A}+\partial_{\tau\tau}\right)\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)=0\mbox{ in }\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}.

(5.4)

Let
R\in\left(0,\frac{1}{4}\min\{a,1-b\}\right)
, and define
B_{R}(\theta_{0},r_{0})=\{z\in\Omega\colon|\theta-\theta_{0}|^{2}+|r-r_{0}|^{2}<R^{2}\}
and
D_{R}(z_{0},\tau_{0})=B_{R}(z_{0})\times(\tau_{0}-R,\tau_{0}+R)
with
z_{0}=(\theta_{0},r_{0})
.
Since there exist finitely many
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\times[a,b]
such that the sets
B_{R}(z_{0})
cover
\mathbb{T}\times(a,b)
, we can assume that
\omega\subset B_{R}(z_{0})
.
Note that
r^{\alpha}
is analytic on
\mathbb{T}\times(0,1)
. From the real analytic estimates of solutions to linear elliptic equations with real analytic coefficients
[
18
, Chapter 3]
, there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\rho\in(0,1)
, depending solely on
\alpha
,
a
, and
b
, such that any solution of equation (
5.4
) satisfies

\begin{split}\left\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}\partial_{r}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{R}(z_{0},0))}\leq C\frac{(j+k)!}{\rho^{j+k}}\left(\int_{D_{2R}(z_{0},0)}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(5.5)

Note that for each
t>0
we have

\begin{split}\int_{D_{2R}(z_{0},0)}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau&\leq\int_{-2R}^{2R}\int_{B_{2R}(z_{0})}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau\\
&\leq\int_{-2R}^{2R}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}(\varphi_{n}^{0})^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2l}e^{-2\lambda_{n}t+2\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}\tau},\end{split}

hence, for each
t>0
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{D_{2R}(z_{0},0)}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\max_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left\{\lambda_{n}^{2l}e^{-\lambda_{n}t}\right\}\max_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left\{e^{-\lambda_{n}t+4R\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}\right\}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}(\varphi_{n}^{0})^{2}.\end{split}

(5.6)

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have
\max\limits_{\lambda>0}\lambda^{2k}e^{-\lambda t}=(\frac{2}{t})^{2k}(\frac{k}{e})^{2k}
. From the Stirling formula, we obtain

m!\sim\left(\frac{m}{e}\right)^{m}\sqrt{2\pi m},\mbox{ as }m\rightarrow+\infty,

then

\begin{split}\max_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left\{\lambda_{n}^{2l}e^{-\lambda_{n}t}\right\}\leq C\left(\frac{2}{t}\right)^{2l}\left(l!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(5.7)

Since
\max\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{e^{-\lambda_{n}t+4R\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}\}\leq e^{\frac{4R^{2}}{t}}
, from (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we get

\begin{split}\int_{D_{2R}(z_{0},0)}\left|\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\theta,r,\tau,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tau\leq Ce^{\frac{4R^{2}}{t}}\left(\frac{2}{t}\right)^{2l}\left(l!\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(5.8)

This, combined with (
5.5
), yields

\begin{split}\left\|\partial_{\theta}^{j}\partial_{r}^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}\varphi(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot,t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{R}(z_{0},0))}\leq\frac{Ce^{\frac{2R^{2}}{t}}(j+k)!l!}{\rho^{j+k}(\frac{t}{2})^{l}}\|\varphi^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(5.9)

In particular, (
5.1
) holds according to (
5.3
). This completes the proof of the proposition.

\square

\eta

\ell\in(0,T)

. Then, from Lemma

) and (

\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\ell_{m}=\ell.

(5.20)

From Proposition
2
, there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
h\in(0,1)
, depending only on
\alpha,a,b,D
, and
T
, such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\begin{split}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\left(e^{\frac{C}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\int_{\ell_{m+1}}^{\ell_{m}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,z)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{h}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-h}.\end{split}

This, combined with Young’s inequality

ab\leq\varepsilon a^{p}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{r}{p}}b^{r},\mbox{ for all }a>0,b>0,\varepsilon>0\mbox{ and }\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=1,p>1,r>1,

yields

\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1-h}{h}}e^{\frac{C}{(t_{2}-t_{1})^{8}}}\int_{\ell_{m+1}}^{\ell_{m}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have

\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{\frac{1-h}{h}}e^{-\frac{C}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{h}}e^{-\frac{C}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\int_{\ell_{m+1}}^{\ell_{m}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Letting
\varepsilon=e^{-h\frac{1}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}
in the latter inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}&e^{-(C+1-h)\frac{1}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-e^{-(C+1)\frac{1}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
\ &\leq\int_{\ell_{m+1}}^{\ell_{m}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Choosing
q=\left(\frac{C+1-h}{C+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{8}}
, from (
5.17
), we get

\begin{split}&e^{-(C+1-h)\frac{1}{(\ell_{m}-\ell_{m+1})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-e^{-(C+1-h)\frac{1}{(\ell_{m+1}-\ell_{m+2})^{8}}}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{m+1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\int_{\ell_{m+1}}^{\ell_{m}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t\end{split}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Summing over
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, from (
5.20
) and
\sup{t\in[0,T]}|\varphi(\cdot,t)|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}<+\infty
, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq e^{(C+1-h)\frac{1}{(\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})^{8}}}\int_{\ell}^{\ell_{1}}\chi_{E}(t)\|\varphi(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(D_{t})}\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Combining this with (
5.19
) and
|\varphi(\cdot,T)|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq|\varphi(\cdot,\ell_{1})|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, we arrive at (
1.2
). This completes the proof of the theorem.

\square

5.1
Controllability on measurable set

Below, we demonstrate that system (
1.1
) exhibits null controllability with an
L^{\infty}
control applied on a measurable subset
D\subseteq Q
. Despite the standard nature of this result (HUM), we include the proof for thoroughness.

Proof.
[Proof of Theorem
1.2
]
Define
Y=\{\chi_{D}y\}
, where
y
represents the solution to the following equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y-\mathcal{A}y=0,&\mbox{in }Q,\\
y=0,&\mbox{on }\partial Q,\\
y(T)=y^{T},&\mbox{in }\Omega\end{cases}

(5.21)

with
y^{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Clearly,
Y
is a subspace of
L^{1}(D)
. We define the functional

F:Y\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}},\quad F(\chi_{D}y)=-\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{0}y(0)\mathrm{d}z.

From Theorem
1.1
, we derive

\begin{split}|F(\chi_{D}y)|\leq\|\varphi^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|y(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\varphi^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|y\|_{L^{1}(D)},\end{split}

indicating that
F
is a bounded linear functional on
Y
. By the Hahn-Banach theorem, there exists a linear extension
\widetilde{F}:L^{1}(D)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\widetilde{F}=F\text{ on }Y,\quad\text{and}\quad|\widetilde{F}(g)|\leq C|\varphi^{0}|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}|g|_{L^{1}(D)}\text{ for all }g\in L^{1}(D).

This implies that
\widetilde{F}\in L^{\infty}(D)
, the dual space of
L^{1}(D)
. Consequently, there exists
f\in L^{\infty}(D)
such that

|f|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq C|\varphi^{0}|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{F}(g)=-\iint_{D}fgdzdt\text{ for all }g\in L^{1}(D).

Extend
f
to be zero on
Q\setminus D
, and denote it by the same symbol.

Finally, since
\varphi
is the weak solution of (
1.1
) and
y
is the weak solution of (
5.21
) (i.e.,
y\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
), we have

\begin{split}\langle\partial_{t}\varphi,y(t)\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega;w),H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w)}+\int_{\Omega}A\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla y(t)\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{D_{t}}fy(t)\mathrm{d}z\mbox{ for a.e.}\ t\in[0,T].\end{split}

Integrating over
[0,T]
and applying integration by parts, noting that
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega;w))
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(T)y(T)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{\Omega}\varphi(0)y(0)\mathrm{d}z=\iint_{Q}\chi_{D}fy\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Since
\chi_{D}y\in L^{1}(D)
, it follows that
\int_{\Omega}\varphi(T)y^{T}dz=0
for all
y^{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. This implies that
\varphi(T)=0
for a.e.
z\in\Omega
. Thus, we complete the proof of this theorem.

\square

Once Theorem
1.2
is established, we can proceed to discuss the bang-bang property for time and norm optimal control problems. However, as this is a standard procedure, we omit the detailed discussion in this paper.
