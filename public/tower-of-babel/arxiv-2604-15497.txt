Endomorphisms of Hecke–Kiselman Monoids Associated to Simple Oriented Graphs
Luka Andrenšek

Abstract
Let HK Θ \mathrm{HK}_{\Theta} denote the Hecke–Kiselman monoid associated to a finite simple oriented graph Θ \Theta . We present a Boolean matrix monoid that is isomorphic to the endomorphism monoid End ⁡ ( HK Θ ) \mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}) .



keywords
Hecke–Kiselman monoid, endomorphism monoid, idempotent, Boolean matrix monoid

1
Introduction and Main Results

Let
\Theta=(V,E)
be a finite simple oriented graph, that is,
a finite oriented graph with no multiple arrows and no oriented cycles of length one or two.
If
(i,j)\in E
, we say that there is an arrow from
i
to
j
, and we write
i\to j
.
If there are no arrows
i\to j
or
j\to i
and
i\neq j
, we say that
i
and
j
are not connected, or disconnected.

In
8
, Ganyushkin and Mazorchuk define the
Hecke–Kiselman monoid

\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
associated to
\Theta
as the monoid generated by idempotents

\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}

subject to the following defining relations for all
i,j\in V
:

•

x_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}x_{i}
if
i
and
j
are not connected;

•

x_{i}x_{j}x_{i}=x_{j}x_{i}x_{j}=x_{i}x_{j}
if
i\to j
.

We denote by
e
the unit element of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

Hecke–Kiselman monoids were originally introduced in the setting of simple mixed graphs
8
.
They have been studied by various
authors
2
;
3
;
4
;
5
;
7
;
8
;
11
;
16
.
Particular interest has been devoted to the corresponding Hecke–Kiselman algebras
12
;
13
;
14
;
15
.

From the proof of
(
8
, Theorem 16)
, it follows that every monoid
isomorphism
\varphi:\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta^{\prime}}
induces a graph isomorphism
f_{\varphi}:\Theta\to\Theta^{\prime}
and every graph isomorphism
f:\Theta\to\Theta^{\prime}
induces a monoid isomorphism

\varphi^{f}:\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta^{\prime}}
.
Setting
\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta
,
it can be verified that the automorphism group of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
is isomorphic to the automorphism group of the graph
\Theta
.
To the best of our knowledge, the structure and properties of endomorphisms of Hecke–Kiselman monoids have not been systematically explored.

A description of endomorphisms of the Kiselman monoid
6
;
9
;
10
, which is the Hecke–Kiselman monoid corresponding to the complete acyclic oriented graph, is known in the literature
1
.
In this paper, we adapt ideas from
1
to study the endomorphism monoid of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

For a positive integer
k
, we denote
[k]=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.
Throughout the paper, we assume
V=[n]
for some positive integer
n
.
In Section
2
, we define
A_{\Theta}
to be the set of all subsets
X\subseteq V
such that the full
subgraph of
\Theta
with vertex set
X
does not contain any oriented cycles.
It is shown that for each
X\in A_{\Theta}
, there exists a unique idempotent
e_{X}
in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
with content
X
.

For
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we define the predicate
p
as follows.
Condition
p(X,Y)
holds if and only if there does not exist a directed path in
\Theta
which starts in some vertex in
Y\setminus X
, never leaves the
vertex set
X\cup Y
, and ends in some vertex in
X\setminus Y
.

We prove that for
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we have

p(X,Y)\iff e_{X}e_{Y}\text{ is an idempotent in }\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}.

We define
(\Theta,p)
-pure sequences of subsets of
[n]
to be
sequences
(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
such that, for all
i,j\in[n]
, the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

X_{i}\in A_{\Theta}
;

(ii)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
and
p(X_{j},X_{i})
hold if
i
and
j
are not connected;

(iii)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds if
i\to j
.

We denote the set of all such sequences by
S_{n}(\Theta,p)
.
We further define a natural binary operation
\ast
on
S_{n}(\Theta,p)
and show that it becomes a monoid with this operation.

The main result of this paper is that

\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\cong(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast).

Using the characteristic-matrix encoding of such sequences, we then obtain a Boolean matrix monoid isomorphic to
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
.

The paper proceeds as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some results on Hecke–Kiselman monoids which we use throughout the paper.
In Section
3
, we give a necessary and sufficient condition on
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
such that
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
In Section
4
, we prove the main result.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The Content Map

For a word
w
over the alphabet
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
, we define its content
c(w)
as the set of indices
i
for which
x_{i}
appears in
w
.
Since the defining relations of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
preserve content, we can define the content and denote it again by
c(x)
for
x\in\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
as the content of any word which represents
x
.
Moreover, we have

c(xy)=c(x)\cup c(y)\quad\text{and}\quad c(e)=\emptyset,

which shows that
c:\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}\to\mathbbm{2}^{[n]}
is a monoid epimorphism, where
\mathbbm{2}^{[n]}
is equipped with the operation
\cup
.

We use the following lemma in the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
.
Then

c((\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i}))=\bigcup_{j\in c(\varphi(x_{i}))}c(\psi(x_{j})).

Proof.

If
\varphi(x_{i})=e
, then we have
c(\varphi(x_{i}))=\emptyset
and
(\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i})=e
.
Hence
c((\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i}))=\emptyset
, and thus the conclusion holds in this case.

Now suppose
\varphi(x_{i})\neq e
and write
\varphi(x_{i})=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{k}}
for some
k\geq 1
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in V
.
Then
c(\varphi(x_{i}))=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}
and we further have

(\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i})=\psi(x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{k}})=\psi(x_{i_{1}})\dots\psi(x_{i_{k}}).

Hence we have

c((\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i}))=c(\psi(x_{i_{1}}))\cup\dots\cup c(\psi(x_{i_{k}})),

and therefore,

c((\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i}))=\bigcup_{j\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}}c(\psi(x_{j}))=\bigcup_{j\in c(\varphi(x_{i}))}c(\psi(x_{j})).

∎

2.2
The Kiselman Monoid

This monoid was first introduced in
9
and its later generalisation
K_{n}
was defined in
10
.
It is the monoid given by the following presentation:

K_{n}=\langle a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\mid a_{i}^{2}=a_{i},a_{i}a_{j}a_{i}=a_{j}a_{i}a_{j}=a_{i}a_{j},i>j\rangle.

The Kiselman monoid arises as a special case of the
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
monoid.
If
\Theta
is such that
i\to j\iff i>j
, then
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}\cong K_{n}
.

Let
X\subseteq[n]
be nonempty. Write
X=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
with
i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}
.
We define

\epsilon_{X}=a_{i_{k}}\dots a_{i_{2}}a_{i_{1}},

and also set
\epsilon_{\emptyset}
to be the unit of
K_{n}
.
In
(
10
, Proposition 11)
, it is proved that
\{\epsilon_{X}\mid X\subseteq[n]\}
is the set of all idempotents in
K_{n}
.
Note that
c(\epsilon_{X})=X
for
X\subseteq[n]
.
The following is proved in
(
10
, Proposition 14)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X,Y\subseteq[n]
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\epsilon_{X}\epsilon_{Y}
is an idempotent.

(ii)

\epsilon_{X}\epsilon_{Y}=\epsilon_{X\cup Y}
.

(iii)

For every
x\in X\setminus Y
and every
y\in Y\setminus X
we have
x>y
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
X,Y\subseteq[n]
,
\epsilon_{X\cup Y}\epsilon_{X}
and
\epsilon_{Y}\epsilon_{X\cup Y}
are idempotents.

Proof.

Condition (
iii
) from Proposition
2.2
vacuously holds for the pairs of sets
(X\cup Y,X)
and
(Y,X\cup Y)
, since

X\setminus(X\cup Y)=\emptyset
and
Y\setminus(X\cup Y)=\emptyset
, from which the claim follows.
∎

2.3
Idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}

For
X\subseteq V
, denote by
\Theta[X]
the full subgraph of
\Theta
with vertex set
X
, that is,
\Theta[X]=(X,E^{\prime})
where
(i,j)\in E^{\prime}
if and only if
(i,j)\in E
and
i,j\in X
.
Since
\Theta[X]
is a full subgraph of
\Theta
, the inclusion
\Theta[X]\hookrightarrow\Theta
induces, by
(
8
, Proposition 15)
, a monomorphism

\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}.

We identify
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
with its image in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
Then
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
is a submonoid of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

In
(
7
, Theorem 3.6)
, the following theorem is proved for a general simple oriented graph
\Theta
.

Theorem 2.4
.

There is a bijection between idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
and full subgraphs of
\Theta
which do not contain any oriented cycles.

In the proof, an idempotent is mapped to the full subgraph of
\Theta
whose vertex set is the content of the idempotent.
Therefore, for each
X\subseteq V
such that
\Theta[X]
does not contain any oriented cycles, there exists exactly one idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
with content
X
.
We denote this idempotent by
e_{X}
.

We denote the following set

A_{\Theta}=\{X\subseteq V\mid\Theta[X]\text{ does not contain any oriented cycles}\}.

Theorem
2.4
implies the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

The set

\{e_{X}\mid X\in A_{\Theta}\}

is the set of all idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

Remark 2.6
.

If
Y\subseteq X\in A_{\Theta}
, then
\Theta[Y]
does not contain any oriented cycles, and hence
Y\in A_{\Theta}
.

The idempotent
e_{X}
can be written explicitly.
Note that if
\Theta[X]
does not contain any oriented cycles, then we can write
X
as
X=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
such that
i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}
implies
j>j^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X\in A_{\Theta}
and write
X=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
in any way such that
i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}
implies
j>j^{\prime}
.
Then there exists a monoid epimorphism
\phi_{X}:K_{k}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
with
\phi_{X}(a_{j})=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Define a mapping
a_{j}\mapsto x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.
We claim that
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}
satisfy the defining relations of the Kiselman monoid
K_{k}
.
Indeed, let
j>j^{\prime}
.
We wish to show

x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}=x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}.

Case 1:
i_{j}
and
i_{j^{\prime}}
are not connected.

In this case, we have
x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}
.
Hence we also have

x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}},

and

x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}.

Case 2: There is an edge between
i_{j}
and
i_{j^{\prime}}
.

Since
j>j^{\prime}
, we cannot have
i_{j^{\prime}}\to i_{j}
, since this would imply a contradiction
j^{\prime}>j
.
Therefore, we must have
i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}
. By the defining relations of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
, we again have

x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}=x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}=x_{i_{j}}x_{i_{j^{\prime}}}.

Hence
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}
satisfy the defining relations of
K_{k}
, and hence the mapping
a_{j}\mapsto x_{i_{j}}
extends to a monoid homomorphism

\phi_{X}:K_{k}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
with
\phi_{X}(a_{j})=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.
Since
x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\dots,x_{i_{k}}
generate
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
and they lie in the image of
\phi_{X}
, the map
\phi_{X}
is a monoid epimorphism.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
X\in A_{\Theta}
.
Then for any ordering of the set
X=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
such that
i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}
implies
j>j^{\prime}
, we have

e_{X}=x_{i_{k}}\dots x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{1}}.

Proof.

Write
X
as

X=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}\text{ such that }i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}\Rightarrow j>j^{\prime}.

Since
X\in A_{\Theta}
, by Lemma
2.7
, there exists a monoid epimorphism
\phi_{X}:K_{k}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
such that
\phi_{X}(a_{j})=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.
As
\epsilon_{[k]}
is an idempotent in
K_{k}
,
\phi_{X}(\epsilon_{[k]})
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
and its content is equal to
X
.
Since
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X]}
is a submonoid of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
,
\phi_{X}(\epsilon_{[k]})
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
as well and its content is
X
.
Therefore,
\phi_{X}(\epsilon_{[k]})
must be the unique idempotent with content
X
in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
, and hence

e_{X}=\phi_{X}(\epsilon_{[k]})=\phi_{X}(a_{k}\dots a_{2}a_{1})=x_{i_{k}}\dots x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{1}}.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
and assume that
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
as well.
Then
e_{X\cup Y}e_{X}
and
e_{Y}e_{X\cup Y}
are idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

Proof.

Since
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we can write

X\cup Y=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}\text{ such that }i_{j}\to i_{j^{\prime}}\Rightarrow j>j^{\prime}.

(1)

By Lemma
2.8
, we have

e_{X\cup Y}=x_{i_{k}}\dots x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{1}}.

Since
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
, by Lemma
2.7
, there exists a monoid epimorphism
\phi_{X\cup Y}:K_{k}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X\cup Y]}
such that
\phi_{X\cup Y}(a_{j})=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
.

Moreover, since
X\cup Y=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{k}\}
, we can write

X=\{i_{p_{1}},i_{p_{2}},\dots,i_{p_{m}}\}\text{ with }p_{1}<p_{2}<\dots<p_{m}\text{ and }Y=\{i_{r_{1}},i_{r_{2}},\dots,i_{r_{l}}\}\text{ with }r_{1}<r_{2}<\dots<r_{l},

with
p_{1},\dots,p_{m},r_{1},\dots,r_{l}\in[k]
.
If
i_{p_{j}}\to i_{p_{j^{\prime}}}
, then by (
1
), we have
p_{j}>p_{j^{\prime}}
, and hence
j>j^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
i_{r_{j}}\to i_{r_{j^{\prime}}}
implies
j>j^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
2.8
, we have

e_{X}=x_{i_{p_{m}}}\dots x_{i_{p_{2}}}x_{i_{p_{1}}}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{Y}=x_{i_{r_{l}}}\dots x_{i_{r_{2}}}x_{i_{r_{1}}}.

Define
X^{\prime}=\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{m}\}
and
Y^{\prime}=\{r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{l}\}
.
We have
X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}=[k]
.
Since
\phi_{X\cup Y}(a_{j})=x_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,2,\dots,k
, we obtain

e_{X}=\phi_{X\cup Y}(\epsilon_{X^{\prime}}),\quad e_{Y}=\phi_{X\cup Y}(\epsilon_{Y^{\prime}}),\quad\text{and }e_{X\cup Y}=\phi_{X\cup Y}(\epsilon_{X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}}).

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\epsilon_{X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}}\epsilon_{X^{\prime}}\text{ and }\epsilon_{Y^{\prime}}\epsilon_{X^{\prime}\cup Y^{\prime}}\text{ are idempotents}.

By applying
\phi_{X\cup Y}
and noting that
\phi_{X\cup Y}
maps idempotents to idempotents, we obtain that
e_{X\cup Y}e_{X}
and
e_{Y}e_{X\cup Y}
are
idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X\cup Y]}
.
Since
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta[X\cup Y]}
is a submonoid of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
, it follows that
e_{X\cup Y}e_{X}
and
e_{Y}e_{X\cup Y}
are idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
as well.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
. Then
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
if and only if
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
and
e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}
.

Proof.

If
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
and
e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}
, then
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent by Proposition
2.5
.
Conversely, if
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent, then by Proposition
2.5
, there exists
Z\in A_{\Theta}
such that

e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{Z}.

Taking contents, we get

Z=c(e_{Z})=c(e_{X}e_{Y})=c(e_{X})\cup c(e_{Y})=X\cup Y.

Hence
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
and
e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

For
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, the product
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
if and only if

e_{X}e_{Y}e_{X}=e_{Y}e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X}e_{Y}.

Proof.

If
e_{X}e_{Y}e_{X}=e_{Y}e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X}e_{Y}
, then

e_{X}e_{Y}e_{X}e_{Y}=(e_{X}e_{Y}e_{X})e_{Y}=e_{X}e_{Y}e_{Y}=e_{X}e_{Y},

which shows that
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.

Conversely, suppose
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.
By Proposition
2.10
, it follows that
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
and

e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}.

(2)

Since
X,Y,X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
, Lemma
2.9
implies that
e_{X\cup Y}e_{X}
and
e_{Y}e_{X\cup Y}
are idempotents.
By Proposition
2.10
, we have

e_{X\cup Y}e_{X}=e_{(X\cup Y)\cup X}=e_{X\cup Y}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{Y}e_{X\cup Y}=e_{Y\cup(X\cup Y)}=e_{X\cup Y}.

Using (
2
), we conclude that

e_{X}e_{Y}e_{X}=e_{Y}e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X}e_{Y}.

∎

2.4
Normal Forms in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}

Here we recall the main results from
3
, and prove some further results which we use in Section
3
.

Denote by
F(V)
the free monoid over the alphabet
\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
.
Note the abuse of notation since
x_{i}
was originally defined to be a generator of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
Elements of
F(V)
are called
words
, and each
x_{i}\in F(V)
, for
i\in V
, is called a
letter
.
We have the
canonical projection

\pi:F(V)\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}.

For letters
x_{i},x_{j}\in F(V)
, we say that there is an arrow from
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
if
i\to j
.
We also say that the letters
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are not connected if
i
and
j
are not connected.
For a word
w\in F(V)
, denote by
l(w)
its length.

The
right cancellation

\overset{r}{\to}
and
left cancellation

\overset{l}{\to}
are binary relations on
F(V)
defined as follows.
For
w,w^{\prime}\in F(V)
, we have

•

w\overset{r}{\to}w^{\prime}
if there exist words
w_{1},w_{2},u\in F(V)
, and a letter
a\in\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
such that

w=w_{1}auaw_{2}
,
w^{\prime}=w_{1}auw_{2}
, and no letter in
u
has an arrow to
a
.

•

w\overset{l}{\to}w^{\prime}
if there exist words
w_{1},w_{2},u\in F(V)
, and a letter
a\in\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
such that

w=w_{1}auaw_{2}
,
w^{\prime}=w_{1}uaw_{2}
, and no letter in
u
has an arrow from
a
.

Right and left cancellations are also called
elementary cancellations
.
Denote by
\to=\overset{r}{\to}\cup\overset{l}{\to}
and denote by
\overset{*}{\to}
the reflexive-transitive closure of
\to
.
Then
(
3
, Lemma 1)
implies that for
u,v\in F(V)
, if
u\to v
, then
\pi(u)=\pi(v)
.
Therefore, if
w\overset{*}{\to}w^{\prime}
for
w,w^{\prime}\in F(V)
, then
\pi(w)=\pi(w^{\prime})
.

Definition 2.12
.

A word
w\in F(V)
is a
normal form
of
x\in\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
if
x=\pi(w)
and no elementary cancellations may be performed on
w
.

Every
x\in\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
has at least one normal form, as any word with minimal length in
\pi^{-1}(x)
must be a normal form of
x
.
Denote by
\mathcal{N}
the set of all normal forms of all elements of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

Define the equivalence relation
\sim
on
F(V)
, which is the smallest equivalence relation which contains

elementary commutations
:

w_{1}abw_{2}\sim w_{1}baw_{2},

for all words
w_{1},w_{2}\in F(V)
and letters
a,b\in\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
such that
a
and
b
are not connected.
The following was proved in
(
3
, Theorem 1)
.

Theorem 2.13
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in F(V)
. If
\pi(w)=\pi(w^{\prime})
and
u,v\in\mathcal{N}
satisfy

w\overset{*}{\to}u
and
w^{\prime}\overset{*}{\to}v
, then
u\sim v
.

(ii)

Every simplifying sequence

w\to w_{1}\to w_{2}\to\dots\to w_{k}

may be extended to a simplifying sequence ending on a normal form of
\pi(w)
.

Definition 2.14
.

A word
w\in F(V)
is said to
have no arrows from right to left
if

w=a_{k}\dots a_{2}a_{1},

where
a_{k},\dots,a_{2},a_{1}\in\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
are letters, and if there is an arrow from
a_{j}
to
a_{j^{\prime}}
, then
j>j^{\prime}
for all
j,j^{\prime}\in[k]
.
Thus, whenever there is an arrow between two letters of
w
, the source of the arrow occurs in
w
to the left of its target.
Otherwise, we say that
w

has arrows from right to left
.

Moreover, for
X\in A_{\Theta}
,
w
is said to be
X-topological
if
w
has no arrows from right to left,
c(w)=X
, and
l(w)=|X|
.
In other words,
w
is
X
-topological if there are no arrows from right to left and each letter
x_{i}
with
i\in X
occurs in
w
exactly once.

Remark 2.15
.

If
X\in A_{\Theta}
, then
\Theta[X]
is an acyclic oriented graph and hence there exists an
X
-topological word.
Also observe that the empty word is
\emptyset
-topological.

Lemma 2.16
.

For
X\in A_{\Theta}
, a word
w
is
X
-topological if and only if
w
is a normal form of
e_{X}
.

Proof.

If
w
is
X
-topological, then
\pi(w)=e_{X}
by Lemma
2.8
.
Moreover, since no letter in
w
occurs more than once, no further elementary cancellations may be performed on
w
, and hence
w
is a normal form of
e_{X}
.

Now assume
w
is a normal form of
e_{X}
.
Since
X\in A_{\Theta}
, there exists an
X
-topological word
v
.
Hence, there are no arrows from right to left in
v
,
c(v)=X
, and
l(v)=|X|
.
By the above paragraph,
v
is a normal form of
e_{X}
.
By Theorem
2.13
(
i
), we have
w\sim v
.
Since elementary commutations only interchange disconnected letters, the relative order of any two connected letters is preserved under
\sim
.
Hence, since
v
has no arrows from right to left, the same is true for
w
.
Since
\sim
also preserves content and length, we have
c(w)=c(v)=X
and
l(w)=l(v)=|X|
.
This implies that
w
is
X
-topological.
∎

Let
w=a_{1}a_{2}\dots a_{k}\in F(V)
, where
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\in\{x_{i}\mid i\in V\}
are letters.
A word of the form
u=a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\dots a_{j_{l}}
with
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{l}\leq k
is called a
quasi-subword
of
w
.
From the definition of
\to
, if
u\overset{*}{\to}v
, then
v
is a quasi-subword of
u
.

3
Products of Idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}

In this section, we determine necessary and sufficient conditions on
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
for which
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.

Definition 3.1
.

For
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, define the predicate
p
as follows.
Condition
p(X,Y)
holds if and only if there do not exist
b\in Y\setminus X
,
a\in X\setminus Y
,
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in X\cap Y
such that

b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to a.

Remark 3.2
.

The chain
b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to a
for
r=0
is interpreted as
b\to a
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
.
If
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent, then
p(X,Y)
holds.

Proof.

Assume
p(X,Y)
does not hold.
We show that
e_{X}e_{Y}
is not an idempotent.
On the contrary, suppose
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.
We now construct a contradiction.
Let
w_{X}
be a normal form of
e_{X}
and let
w_{Y}
be a normal form of
e_{Y}
.
Lemma
2.16
implies that
w_{X}
is
X
-topological and
w_{Y}
is
Y
-topological.
We also have
\pi(w_{X}w_{Y})=e_{X}e_{Y}
.
Since
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent, by Proposition
2.10
, we have

X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}.

Let
u
be a normal form of
e_{X\cup Y}
with
w_{X}w_{Y}\overset{*}{\to}u
.
Such a word
u
exists by Theorem
2.13
(
ii
) and
\pi(w_{X}w_{Y})=e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}
.
By Lemma
2.16
,
u
is
X\cup Y
-topological.
Since
p(X,Y)
does not hold, there exist
a\in X\setminus Y
,
b\in Y\setminus X
, and
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in X\cap Y
such that

b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to a.

Observe that the letters
x_{a}
and
x_{b}
occur exactly once in
w_{X}w_{Y}
, respectively, and hence they occur exactly once in
u
, since a
\to
reduction step cannot delete a letter which occurs only
once and cannot add any extra letters.
Moreover,
x_{a}
occurs to the left of
x_{b}
in
w_{X}w_{Y}
.
Since
w_{X}w_{Y}\overset{*}{\to}u
,
u
is a quasi-subword of
w_{X}w_{Y}
, and hence the letter
x_{a}
occurs to the left of
x_{b}
in
u
as well.

If
r=0
, then
b\to a
, hence we have an arrow from right to left in
u
, which contradicts the fact that
u
is
X\cup Y
-topological.
Now assume
r>0
.
Since
u
is
X\cup Y
-topological and
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in X\cap Y\subseteq X\cup Y
, each of the letters
x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{r}}
occurs exactly once in
u
.
Since
b\to i_{1}
and
u
has no arrows from right to left, the occurrence of
x_{i_{1}}
in
u
must lie to the right of the occurrence of
x_{b}
in
u
.
It follows that for
1<t\leq r
, the occurrence of
x_{i_{t}}
in
u
must lie to the right of the occurrence of
x_{i_{t-1}}
in
u
, since
i_{t-1}\to i_{t}
and
u
has no arrows from right to left.
Inductively, we conclude that each
x_{i_{t}}
for
1\leq t\leq r
lies to the right of the occurrence of
x_{b}
in
u
.
Finally, it follows from
i_{r}\to a
and the fact that
u
has no arrows from right to left that the occurrence of
x_{a}
in
u
must lie to the right of the occurrence of

x_{i_{r}}
in
u
, and hence the occurrence of
x_{a}
in
u
must lie to the right of the occurrence of
x_{b}
in
u
.
In the previous paragraph, however, it is established that the occurrence of
x_{a}
in
u
lies to the left of the occurrence of
x_{b}
in
u
.
Hence we have reached a contradiction, and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
.
If
p(X,Y)
holds, then
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.

Proof.

Assume
p(X,Y)
holds.
We show that
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent.
Denote
I=X\cap Y
and

R=\{i\in I\mid\exists b\in Y\setminus X,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in I:b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to i\}.

Again, in the above set definition,
b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to i
for
r=0
is interpreted as
b\to i
.
Also set
S=I\setminus R
.
We denote by
\sqcup
a disjoint union and

M=(Y\setminus X)\sqcup R\quad\text{and}\quad N=(X\setminus Y)\sqcup S.

(3)

Observe that since
R,S\subseteq I=X\cap Y
, the above unions are indeed disjoint.
Then we have

X=N\sqcup R\quad\text{and}\quad Y=M\sqcup S,

(4)

and these unions are disjoint as well.
The key observation is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
p(X,Y)
holds.
Then there are no arrows starting in
M
and ending in
N
.

Proof.

Assume otherwise.
Let
m\in M
and
n\in N
be such that
m\to n
.
Now we consider cases on
m,n
based on (
3
).
Since
p(X,Y)
holds, we cannot have
m\in Y\setminus X
and
n\in X\setminus Y
.
If
m\in Y\setminus X
and
n\in S
, this implies
n\in R
by the definition of
R
, which is a contradiction since
R\cap S=\emptyset
.
If
m\in R
and
n\in X\setminus Y
, then by the definition of
R
, there exist
b\in Y\setminus X
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in I
such that

b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to m\to n.

Since
m\in R\subseteq I
and
n\in X\setminus Y
, this contradicts the fact that
p(X,Y)
holds.
If
m\in R
and
n\in S
, we have some
b\in Y\setminus X
and
i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in I
such that

b\to i_{1}\to\dots\to i_{r}\to m\to n.

Since
m\in R\subseteq I
, this implies that
n\in R
.
Thus
n\in R\cap S
, which is again a contradiction.
The above cases are exhaustive by (
3
).
Hence, in all cases, we have reached a contradiction, and therefore there is no arrow starting in
M
and ending in
N
.
∎

Since there are no arrows starting in
M
and ending in
N
, and

X\cup Y=M\sqcup N,

(5)

where the union on the right-hand side is disjoint, any oriented cycle in

\Theta[X\cup Y]
must lie entirely in
\Theta[M]
or entirely in
\Theta[N]
.
Indeed, if an oriented cycle in
\Theta[X\cup Y]
met both
M
and
N
, then after entering
M

from
N
, it would eventually have to return from
M
to
N
. Hence the cycle
would contain an arrow starting in
M
and ending in
N
, which is impossible.
Since
N\subseteq X
and
M\subseteq Y
, and
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
,
\Theta[M]
and
\Theta[N]
cannot contain any oriented cycles.
This implies that
\Theta[X\cup Y]
does not contain any oriented cycles, and hence
X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
.
To prove
e_{X}e_{Y}
is an idempotent, by Proposition
2.10
, we need to show
e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}
.

Since
N,R,M,S\subseteq X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we have
N,R,M,S\in A_{\Theta}
.
Let
w_{N}
be
N
-topological,
w_{R}
be
R
-topological,
w_{M}
be
M
-topological, and
w_{S}
be
S
-topological.
Since
w_{N}
and
w_{R}
have no arrows from right to left, respectively, and since there are no arrows starting in
R
and ending in
N
, by
R\subseteq M
and Lemma
3.5
, there are no arrows from right to left in the word

w_{N}w_{R}.

Since
w_{N}
is
N
-topological and
w_{R}
is
R
-topological,
c(w_{N}w_{R})=N\cup R=X
and
l(w_{N}w_{R})=|N|+|R|=|X|
by (
4
), and hence
w_{N}w_{R}
is
X
-topological.
Since
w_{S}
and
w_{M}
have no arrows from right to left, respectively, and since there are no arrows starting in
M
and ending in
S
, by
S\subseteq N
and Lemma
3.5
, there are no arrows from right to left in the word

w_{S}w_{M}.

Since
w_{S}
is
S
-topological and
w_{M}
is
M
-topological,
c(w_{S}w_{M})=S\cup M=Y
and
l(w_{S}w_{M})=|S|+|M|=|Y|
by (
4
), and hence
w_{S}w_{M}
is
Y
-topological.
Lemma
2.16
now implies

e_{X}=\pi(w_{N}w_{R})\quad\text{and}\quad e_{Y}=\pi(w_{S}w_{M}).

Now we will show

w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}w_{M}\overset{*}{\to}w_{N}w_{M}.

(6)

This will then imply

e_{X}e_{Y}=\pi(w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}w_{M})=\pi(w_{N}w_{M}).

Since
w_{N}
and
w_{M}
have no arrows from right to left, respectively, and since there are no arrows starting in
M
and ending in
N
by Lemma
3.5
, there are no arrows from right to left in
w_{N}w_{M}
.
Since
w_{N}
is
N
-topological and
w_{M}
is
M
-topological,
c(w_{N}w_{M})=N\cup M=X\cup Y
and
l(w_{N}w_{M})=|N|+|M|=|X\cup Y|
by (
5
), and hence
w_{N}w_{M}
is
X\cup Y
-topological.
Hence, (
6
) implies
e_{X}e_{Y}=\pi(w_{N}w_{M})=e_{X\cup Y}
by Lemma
2.16
.

We thus end the proof by proving (
6
).
We first delete all letters of
w_{S}
from left to right.
If
l(w_{S})=0
, this deletion phase is done.
Otherwise, we may write
w_{S}=x_{i}w_{S}^{\prime}
where
i\in S
and
w_{S}^{\prime}\in F(V)
.
By the definition of
N
, the letter
x_{i}
occurs in
w_{N}
.
Hence we can write
w_{N}=w_{N}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{N}^{\prime\prime}
.
Then we have

w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}w_{M}=w_{N}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{N}^{\prime\prime}w_{R}x_{i}w_{S}^{\prime}w_{M}.

Since
w_{N}w_{R}=w_{N}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{N}^{\prime\prime}w_{R}
is
X
-topological, there are no arrows from right to left, and hence there is no letter in
w_{N}^{\prime\prime}w_{R}
with an arrow to
x_{i}
.
Hence, we have

x_{i}w_{N}^{\prime\prime}w_{R}x_{i}\overset{r}{\to}x_{i}w_{N}^{\prime\prime}w_{R}.

Therefore,

w_{N}w_{R}x_{i}w_{S}^{\prime}w_{M}\to w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}^{\prime}w_{M}.

If
l(w_{S}^{\prime})=0
, this deletion phase is done.
Otherwise, we continue this process.
At each step, we use a right cancellation to delete the leading letter of the remaining suffix of
w_{S}
.
We rely on the fact that the leading letter of any nonempty suffix of
w_{S}
occurs in
w_{N}
and the fact that
w_{N}w_{R}
has no arrows from right to left.
Hence, we obtain

w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}w_{M}\overset{*}{\to}w_{N}w_{R}w_{M}.

Now we delete all letters in
w_{R}
from right to left with a similar strategy.
If
l(w_{R})=0
, this deletion phase is done.
Otherwise, write
w_{R}=w_{R}^{\prime}x_{i}
where
i\in R
and
w_{R}^{\prime}\in F(V)
.
By the definition of
M
,
x_{i}
occurs in
w_{M}
, and hence we can write
w_{M}=w_{M}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{M}^{\prime\prime}
.
Hence we have

w_{N}w_{R}w_{M}=w_{N}w_{R}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{M}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{M}^{\prime\prime}.

Since
w_{M}
is
M
-topological, there are no arrows from right to left in
w_{M}
, and hence there are no arrows that start in
x_{i}
and end in a letter in
w_{M}^{\prime}
.
Hence,

x_{i}w_{M}^{\prime}x_{i}\overset{l}{\to}w_{M}^{\prime}x_{i}.

Therefore,

w_{N}w_{R}^{\prime}x_{i}w_{M}\to w_{N}w_{R}^{\prime}w_{M}.

If
l(w_{R}^{\prime})=0
, this deletion phase is done.
Otherwise, we continue this process.
At each step, we delete the last letter of the remaining prefix of
w_{R}
using a left cancellation.
We rely on the fact that the last letter of any nonempty prefix of
w_{R}
occurs in
w_{M}
, and that
w_{M}
has no arrows from right to left.
Hence, we obtain

w_{N}w_{R}w_{M}\overset{*}{\to}w_{N}w_{M},

and hence

w_{N}w_{R}w_{S}w_{M}\overset{*}{\to}w_{N}w_{M},

which concludes the implication as stated above.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
.
Then
p(X,Y)
holds if and only if there does not exist a directed path in
\Theta[X\cup Y]
whose startpoint lies in
Y\setminus X
and whose endpoint lies in
X\setminus Y
.

Proof.

If there is no directed path in
\Theta[X\cup Y]
which starts in some vertex of
Y\setminus X
and ends in some vertex of
X\setminus Y
, then
p(X,Y)
must hold.
Assume
p(X,Y)
holds.
Suppose there exists a directed path

v_{0}\to v_{1}\to\dots\to v_{k},

such that
v_{0}\in Y\setminus X
,
v_{k}\in X\setminus Y
, and
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in X\cup Y
.
Let

i_{0}=\max\{i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}\mid v_{i}\in Y\setminus X\}\quad\text{and}\quad j_{0}=\min\{i\in\{i_{0}+1,\dots,k\}\mid v_{i}\in X\setminus Y\}.

Since
v_{k}\in X\setminus Y
and
i_{0}<k
, the index
j_{0}
is well-defined, and
i_{0}<j_{0}
.
Then the directed path

v_{i_{0}}\to v_{i_{0}+1}\to\dots\to v_{j_{0}}

starts in
Y\setminus X
and ends in
X\setminus Y
.
Moreover, its interior vertices lie in
X\cap Y
; by the maximality of
i_{0}
, none of them lies in
Y\setminus X
, and by the minimality of
j_{0}
, none of them lies in
X\setminus Y
; since all vertices of the directed path lie in
X\cup Y
, they must lie in
X\cap Y
.
This contradicts the fact that
p(X,Y)
holds.
This concludes the proof.
∎

4
Endomorphisms of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}

4.1
(\Theta,p)
-pure Sequences

In Theorem
3.6
, we proved that for
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we have

p(X,Y)\iff e_{X}e_{Y}\text{ is an idempotent in }\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}.

By Proposition
2.10
, for all sets
X,Y\in A_{\Theta}
, we further have

p(X,Y)\iff e_{X}e_{Y}\text{ is an idempotent}\iff X\cup Y\in A_{\Theta}\text{ and }e_{X}e_{Y}=e_{X\cup Y}.

(7)

Theorem 4.1
.

A map
\varphi:\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}\to\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
extends to a monoid endomorphism of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
if
and only if there exist sets
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\subseteq[n]
such that, for all
i,j\in[n]
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

X_{i}\in A_{\Theta}
;

(ii)

\varphi(x_{i})=e_{X_{i}}
;

(iii)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
and
p(X_{j},X_{i})
hold if
i
and
j
are not connected;

(iv)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds if
i\to j
.

Proof.

Assume that the map
\varphi
extends to an endomorphism of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
, which we also denote by
\varphi
.
Since a monoid homomorphism maps idempotents to idempotents,
\varphi(x_{i})
is an idempotent in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
By Proposition
2.5
,
\varphi(x_{i})
is of the form
e_{X}
for some
X\in A_{\Theta}
.
Hence there exist sets
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\in A_{\Theta}
such that

\varphi(x_{i})=e_{X_{i}},

for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
, which proves (
i
) and (
ii
).
Now let
i,j\in[n]
.

Case 1:
i
and
j
are not connected.

In this case, we have

x_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}x_{i}.

This implies

x_{i}x_{j}x_{i}x_{j}=x_{i}(x_{j}x_{i})x_{j}=x_{i}x_{i}x_{j}x_{j}=x_{i}x_{j}.

Hence,
x_{i}x_{j}
is an idempotent.
Since
\varphi
maps idempotents to idempotents, it follows that

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}
is an idempotent, and hence
p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds.
Since

e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}=\varphi(x_{j}x_{i})=\varphi(x_{i}x_{j})=e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}},

we conclude that
e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}
is an idempotent as well, and thus
p(X_{j},X_{i})
also holds.
This proves (
iii
).

Case 2:
i\to j
.

In this case, we have

x_{i}x_{j}x_{i}=x_{j}x_{i}x_{j}=x_{i}x_{j},

and hence

x_{i}x_{j}x_{i}x_{j}=(x_{i}x_{j}x_{i})x_{j}=x_{i}x_{j}x_{j}=x_{i}x_{j}.

Therefore,
x_{i}x_{j}
is an idempotent, and by applying
\varphi
, we conclude that
e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}
is an idempotent as well, and hence
p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds.
This proves (
iv
).

We proved the necessity of the condition stated in the theorem.
Now we prove sufficiency.
We claim that the elements
\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2}),\dots,\varphi(x_{n})
satisfy the defining relations of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
Hence, we need to show that
e_{X_{1}},e_{X_{2}},\dots,e_{X_{n}}
satisfy the defining relations of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
Let
i,j\in[n]
.

Case 1:
i
and
j
are not connected.

We need to show that

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}=e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}.

By assumption,
p(X_{i},X_{j})
and
p(X_{j},X_{i})
hold.
By (
7
), we have
X_{i}\cup X_{j}\in A_{\Theta}
and

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}=e_{X_{i}\cup X_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}=e_{X_{j}\cup X_{i}},

and hence

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}=e_{X_{i}\cup X_{j}}=e_{X_{j}\cup X_{i}}=e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}},

which we wanted to prove.

Case 2:
i\to j
.

We need to show that

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}=e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}=e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}.

By assumption,
p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds, and hence
e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}
is an idempotent.
Proposition
2.11
implies

e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}=e_{X_{j}}e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}}=e_{X_{i}}e_{X_{j}},

which we wanted to prove.

Hence, the elements
\varphi(x_{1}),\varphi(x_{2}),\dots,\varphi(x_{n})
satisfy the defining relations of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
, and thus we can extend
\varphi
to a monoid endomorphism of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
∎

Denote by

S_{n}=\{(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})\mid X_{i}\subseteq[n]\text{ for }i=1,2,\dots,n\},

the set of sequences of subsets of
[n]
.

Definition 4.2
.

A sequence of sets
(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})\in S_{n}
is said to be
(\Theta,p)
-pure
if, for all
i,j\in[n]
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

X_{i}\in A_{\Theta}
;

(ii)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
and
p(X_{j},X_{i})
hold if
i
and
j
are not connected;

(iii)

p(X_{i},X_{j})
holds if
i\to j
.

We also define

S_{n}(\Theta,p)=\{(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})\in S_{n}\mid(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})\text{ is $(\Theta,p)$-pure}\}.

Define the map
\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\to S_{n}
by

\Phi(\varphi)=\big(c(\varphi(x_{1})),c(\varphi(x_{2})),\dots,c(\varphi(x_{n}))\big).

Theorem 4.3
.

The map
\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\to S_{n}(\Theta,p)
is a bijection.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
.
By Theorem
4.1
, there exists a
(\Theta,p)
-pure sequence of sets
(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
such that

\varphi(x_{i})=e_{X_{i}},

for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
Hence,

\Phi(\varphi)=(c(e_{X_{1}}),c(e_{X_{2}}),\dots,c(e_{X_{n}}))=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}),

which proves that
\Phi
maps into
S_{n}(\Theta,p)
.

Since every endomorphism of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
is uniquely determined by the images of the generators,
since every endomorphism of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
maps generators to idempotents, and
since idempotents in
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
are uniquely determined by their content,
\Phi
is injective.

Let
(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})\in S_{n}(\Theta,p)
. Then by Theorem
4.1
, the map
x_{i}\mapsto e_{X_{i}}
extends to an endomorphism
\varphi
of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
Since
c(\varphi(x_{i}))=c(e_{X_{i}})=X_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we obtain
\Phi(\varphi)=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
, and hence surjectivity follows.
∎

4.2
The Maps
\Phi
and
\Psi

The following definitions and statements are very similar to the results in
1
.
We prove these results again because the results in
1
were derived for the Kiselman monoid, which is a special case of
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.
For completeness, we include the proofs here for
\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}
.

For
\mathbf{X}\in S_{n}
, denote by
\mathbf{X}_{i}
the

i
-th set of the sequence
\mathbf{X}
.
We define the binary operation
\ast
on
S_{n}
as follows.
Let
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
and
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{n})
be elements of
S_{n}
.
We define
\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y}
, where
\mathbf{Z}=(Z_{1},Z_{2},\dots,Z_{n})
is given by

\displaystyle Z_{i}=\bigcup\limits_{j\in Y_{i}}X_{j}

for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
It quickly follows that
(S_{n},\ast)
is a monoid with unit element
(\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{n\})
.

Theorem 4.4
.

The map
\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\to S_{n}
is a homomorphism of monoids from
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
to
(S_{n},\ast)
.

Proof.

Let
\psi,\varphi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
and let
i\in[n]
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we have

(\Phi(\psi\circ\varphi))_{i}=c((\psi\circ\varphi)(x_{i}))=\bigcup_{j\in c(\varphi(x_{i}))}c(\psi(x_{j})).

We also have

\displaystyle\big(\Phi(\psi)\ast\Phi(\varphi)\big)_{i}

\displaystyle=\big((c(\psi(x_{1})),\dots,c(\psi(x_{n})))\ast(c(\varphi(x_{1})),\dots,c(\varphi(x_{n})))\big)_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j\in c(\varphi(x_{i}))}c(\psi(x_{j})).

We thus have

\big(\Phi(\psi\circ\varphi)\big)_{i}=\big(\Phi(\psi)\ast\Phi(\varphi)\big)_{i}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\Phi(\psi\circ\varphi)=\Phi(\psi)\ast\Phi(\varphi),

proving that
\Phi
is multiplicative.
Since the identity map is the unit element of
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
, and because of

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}})=(\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{n\}),

we conclude that
\Phi
is a monoid homomorphism.
∎

Now we denote by
B_{n}
the set of all
n\times n
Boolean matrices.
For
A\in B_{n}
and
i,j\in[n]
, denote by
A_{i,j}
the
(i,j)
entry of
A
.
We define the binary operation
\cdot
on
B_{n}
as follows.
Let
A,B\in B_{n}
. Then
C=A\cdot B
, with

\displaystyle C_{i,j}=\bigvee_{k=1}^{n}(A_{i,k}\land B_{k,j}),

for all
i,j=1,2,\dots,n
.
Here,
\lor
denotes the Boolean join and
\land
denotes the Boolean meet on
\{0,1\}
.
One can easily check that
(B_{n},\cdot)
is a monoid, and its unit element is the identity matrix
I
of size
n\times n
.

Theorem 4.5
.

The map
\Psi:S_{n}\to B_{n}
, defined by

\Psi(\mathbf{X})_{x,i}=\begin{cases}1,&x\in\mathbf{X}_{i},\\
0,&x\notin\mathbf{X}_{i},\end{cases}

for all
x,i=1,2,\dots,n
,
is an isomorphism of monoids from
(S_{n},\ast)
to
(B_{n},\cdot)
.

Proof.

The map
\Psi
is obviously bijective.

Now we show that
\Psi
is multiplicative.
Let
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in S_{n}
, where

\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
and
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\dots,Y_{n})
. We compute

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})=\Psi(\bigcup_{j\in Y_{1}}X_{j},\bigcup_{j\in Y_{2}}X_{j},\dots,\bigcup_{j\in Y_{n}}X_{j}).

It follows that for
x,i\in[n]
we have

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})_{x,i}=1

\displaystyle\iff x\in\bigcup_{j\in Y_{i}}X_{j}

\displaystyle\iff\text{there exists }k\in Y_{i}\text{ such that }x\in X_{k}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y}))_{x,i}=\bigvee_{k=1}^{n}(\Psi(\mathbf{X})_{x,k}\land\Psi(\mathbf{Y})_{k,i}).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle(\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y}))_{x,i}=1

\displaystyle\iff\text{there exists }k\in[n]\text{ such that }\Psi(\mathbf{X})_{x,k}=\Psi(\mathbf{Y})_{k,i}=1

\displaystyle\iff\text{there exists }k\in[n]\text{ such that }x\in X_{k}\text{ and }k\in Y_{i}

\displaystyle\iff\text{there exists }k\in Y_{i}\text{ such that }x\in X_{k}.

We thus obtain

\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})_{x,i}=1\iff(\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y}))_{x,i}=1,

and since
\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})
and
\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y})
are both Boolean matrices, we get

\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})_{x,i}=(\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y}))_{x,i}.

Since
x
and
i
are arbitrary, it follows that

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y})=\Psi(\mathbf{X})\cdot\Psi(\mathbf{Y}).

Furthermore, since
\Psi(\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{n\})=I
, it follows that
\Psi
is an isomorphism of monoids.
∎

The map
\Psi
has a particularly simple interpretation: it encodes, in matrix form, which elements are contained in each set of the sequence.
More precisely, if
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n})
, then the
i
-th column of
\Psi(\mathbf{X})
is the characteristic vector of the set
X_{i}
.
Equivalently, for
x,i\in[n]
, we have

\Psi(\mathbf{X})_{x,i}=1\iff x\in X_{i}.

Theorem 4.6
.

The set
S_{n}(\Theta,p)
, equipped with the operation
\ast
, is a monoid and we have

\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\cong(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast)\cong(\Psi(S_{n}(\Theta,p)),\cdot).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
,
\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\to S_{n}(\Theta,p)
is a bijection.
Pick any
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in S_{n}(\Theta,p)
.
Then there exist
\varphi,\psi\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
such that

\Phi(\varphi)=\mathbf{X}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi(\psi)=\mathbf{Y}.

Using Theorem
4.4
, we conclude that

\mathbf{X}\ast\mathbf{Y}=\Phi(\varphi)\ast\Phi(\psi)=\Phi(\varphi\circ\psi)\in S_{n}(\Theta,p).

This shows that
S_{n}(\Theta,p)
is closed under
\ast
.
Since

(\{1\},\{2\},\dots,\{n\})=\Phi(\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta}})\in S_{n}(\Theta,p),

it follows that
(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast)
is a submonoid of
(S_{n},\ast)
, and

\Phi:\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\to S_{n}(\Theta,p)

is an isomorphism of monoids from
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})
to
(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast)
.
Hence

\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{HK}_{\Theta})\cong(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast).

By Theorem
4.5
, the restriction

\Psi|_{S_{n}(\Theta,p)}:S_{n}(\Theta,p)\to B_{n}

is a monomorphism of monoids, and hence

\Psi|_{S_{n}(\Theta,p)}:S_{n}(\Theta,p)\to\Psi(S_{n}(\Theta,p))

is an isomorphism of monoids.
We thus get

(S_{n}(\Theta,p),\ast)\cong(\Psi(S_{n}(\Theta,p)),\cdot).

∎
