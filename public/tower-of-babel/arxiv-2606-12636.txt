Quantum Stochastic Inflation
Robson Christie, Jaewoo Joo, Greg Kaplanek, Vincent Vennin, David Wands

Abstract
We formulate stochastic inflation in an open quantum system framework. The field coarse-grained in a patch of fixed physical size, and the total momentum of that patch, form a canonical pair and act on a one-mode Fock space which we identify as the “bulk”. At each time step, new comoving modes join the coarse-grained patch and the bulk has to be redefined. This redefinition produces an entangled mode that is traced over, yielding a non-unitary evolution equation for the bulk’s density matrix. For a free test field in de Sitter, one obtains GKLS dynamics, generated by an effective Hamiltonian and a single non-Hermitian Lindblad operator, hence diffusion and Hubble friction originate from the same quantum channel. The Wigner-Weyl transform of the GKLS equation leads to a Fokker-Planck equation for the Wigner function, which matches the one that applies to the classical phase-space distribution of stochastic inflation. We also provide several schemes under which one can unravel the GKLS dynamics into stochastic Schrödinger equations when continuous measurements of the decoupled mode are performed, making contact with Langevin formulations of stochastic inflation. In the light-field regime, an additional overdamped reduction can be performed by integrating out the momentum variable in the Wigner distribution, leading to Starobinsky’s slow-roll Fokker-Planck equation. In that regime, the purity of the patch is strongly suppressed. In contrast, for heavy fields, field diffusion is suppressed and the coarse-grained patch remains close to a pure underdamped oscillator, which prevents a classical stochastic treatment.

The GKLS equation can also be stochastically “unravelled”, i.e. it can be cast into a stochastic Schrödinger equation (SSE) for the wavefunction. \mathcal{F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}
describes the decoupled mode produced by this redefinition. It is contained in the tensor complement of the updated bulk,

\mathcal{F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\perp}_{N+\differential N},

and this decoupled mode is then traced out. The key ingredients of this process are itemised below:

1.

Incoming boundary shell.
Consider the set of comoving modes entering the coarse-grained patch between

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{\perp}

\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}

\mathcal{F}_{N}^{\parallel}
and

are made of disjoint sets of Fourier modes, the joint state for the bulk-and-shell is the product state
\hat{\rho}_{N}\otimes\hat{\rho}_{\Delta}
, which lives in
\mathcal{F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}
. In other words, the incoming bulk and the incoming boundary shell are
disentangled
.

2.

Incoming-bulk dynamics.
Starting from
\hat{\rho}_{N}
at time

with

, generated by

3.6

The collective degree of freedom contained in

3.

Mode redefinition.
The third step is to redefine the homogeneous degree of freedom after the incoming boundary shell has joined the infrared sector. The incoming bulk together with the incoming boundary shell can be rewritten as the redefined bulk at time

4.

Tracing out the decoupled mode.
Finally, since we keep only the redefined bulk variables, we trace over this decoupled mode. Non-unitarity enters only after the decoupled-mode information is discarded. The result is the reduced state

This defines a (non-unitary) map

3.2
Fokker-Planck equation for the Wigner function

For a quantum state described by the density matrix
\hat{\rho}
on phase space
(q,p)
, the Wigner function is defined as the Wigner-Weyl transform of the density matrix
Wigner:1932

\displaystyle W(q,p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential u\,e^{-ipu}\left\langle q-\frac{u}{2}\bigg|\,\hat{\rho}\,\bigg|q+\frac{u}{2}\right\rangle\,.

(3.24)

Since the Wigner-Weyl transform is invertible, this provides a representation of the quantum state which is fully equivalent to the density matrix. Moreover, the expectation value of any operator
\hat{O}
can be written as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{O}\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{O}\hat{\rho})=\int\differential q\int\differential p\,O(q,p)W(q,p)\,,

where
O(q,p)=\int\differential u\,e^{-ipu}\langle q-u/2|\hat{O}|q+u/2\rangle
is the Wigner-Weyl transform of
\hat{O}
. The Wigner function is real (given that
\hat{\rho}
is Hermitian) and normalised to one (
\int\differential q\differential pW(q,p)=1
, since
\mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho})=1
), thus one may view
W
as a quasi-distribution function against which observables can be computed. It is only a “quasi” distribution since the Wigner function is not necessarily everywhere positive. However, for the case of an initially Gaussian state of a free test field, the Wigner function remains Gaussian and always positive.

Because
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}
is quadratic and
\hat{L}_{\chi}
is linear in
(\hat{Q},\hat{P})
, the Wigner transform of

3.23

\displaystyle\partial_{N}W={}

\displaystyle-\partial_{q}\!\left(\frac{p}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}W\right)-\partial_{p}\!\left[\left(-3p-\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}q\right)W\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}W_{qq}+D_{QP}W_{qp}+\frac{1}{2}D_{PP}W_{pp},

(3.26)

with

D_{QQ}=\frac{3e^{3N}}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\phi_{k_{\sigma}},\qquad D_{QP}=3\,{\rm Re}\!\left(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}}\right),\qquad D_{PP}=3e^{-3N}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}},

(3.27)

as can be shown by standard Wigner-Weyl transform methods (see e.g.,
wiseman2009quantum
;
petruccione
). The arbitrary phase
\chi

For the free field in the Bunch-Davies vacuum, the Wigner function is Gaussian and positive, so it can be identified with the Starobinsky phase-space probability density after changing variables to
q=\phi
and
p=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)\pi
, as follows from
eq.

2.6
. The corresponding Jacobian relation is

W(q,p,N)\to P\left[\phi=q,\pi=\frac{p}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)},N\right]\frac{1}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)}.

(3.28)

With this identification,

and
3.27

1.6
). For more general states, however, the Wigner function need not be positive, so it should not be interpreted as an ordinary phase-space probability distribution.

Even in that case, one can always define a positive field marginal probability distribution by integrating out the momentum variable,

{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)=\int\differential p\,W(q,p;N)=\bra{q}\hat{\rho}(N)\ket{q}\geq 0.

(3.29)

Together with the current
J_{Q}(q,N)=(H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys})^{-1}\int\differential p\,pW(q,p;N)

\partial_{N}{\cal P}_{Q}=-\partial_{q}J_{Q}+\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}\partial_{q}^{2}{\cal P}_{Q},

(3.30)

provided the boundary terms vanish. This identity is exact within the free quadratic theory, but it is not closed: the current

obeys its own evolution equation, involving higher momentum moments. In the overdamped regime, the momentum/current sector relaxes quickly compared with the field marginal, so one may use the quasistatic closure
\partial_{N}J_{Q}\simeq 0
, solve algebraically for

It is useful at this point to separate two possible descriptions of the same bulk-boundary evolution. There are then two ways to proceed. If the decoupled mode is not measured, its information is removed from the reduced description by taking the partial trace. A second route consists in measuring the decoupled mode, hence projecting it onto some of the eigenstates of the operator being measured. 4
The (mass-dependent) Quantum to Classical Transition

The route to classical stochastic behaviour depends on the mass. As we shall now show, for light fields (
m<3H/2
), the phase-space Wigner equation (
3.26
) contains an overdamped field marginal whose positive distribution obeys the Starobinsky one-dimensional Fokker-Planck equation (
1.8
). At the critical point
(m=3H/2)
and for heavy fields
(m>3H/2)
, the
D_{QQ}
channel is suppressed; the remaining late-time diagnostic is the purity of the stationary Gaussian state approached at late times by the retained
(Q,P)
system. The common structure behind these regimes is the closed low-moment sector; because the Hamiltonian is quadratic and the Lindblad operator is linear, the Moyal expansion terminates and the first moments and covariance close for any reduced state with finite second moments.

Let
W(q,p;N)
be the Wigner function of the retained mode, with
\hat{\mathbf{Z}}=(\hat{Q},\hat{P})^{\top}
and phase-space coordinates
\mathbf{z}=(q,p)^{\top}
. The first moments and covariance matrix elements are

\expectationvalue*{\hat{\mathbf{Z}}}=\int\differential q\,\differential p\,\mathbf{z}W(q,p;N),\qquad\Sigma_{ij}(N)=\int\differential q\,\differential p\,\left(z_{i}-\expectationvalue*{\hat{Z}_i}\right)\left(z_{j}-\expectationvalue*{\hat{Z}_j}\right)W(q,p;N).

(4.1)

We write this covariance matrix as

\mathbf{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{QQ}&\Delta_{QP}\\
\Delta_{QP}&\Delta_{PP}\end{pmatrix}.

For the quadratic Wigner generator in
eq.

3.26
, the moment hierarchy closes at second order
MyPaper
. The first moments are deterministic and obey

\frac{\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}}{\differential N}=\frac{\expectationvalue*{\hat P}}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}},\qquad\frac{\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat P}}{\differential N}=-3\expectationvalue*{\hat P}-\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}\expectationvalue*{\hat Q},

(4.2)

and combine into the damped-oscillator equation

\frac{\differential^{2}\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}}{\differential N^{2}}+3\frac{\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}}{\differential N}+\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}=0.

(4.3)

For
m>0
, this describes a damped oscillator, which is overdamped for
m<3H/2
and underdamped for
m>3H/2
.
The corresponding covariance equations
MyPaper
are

\displaystyle\derivative{\Delta_{QQ}}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\Delta_{QP}+D_{QQ},

\displaystyle\derivative{\Delta_{QP}}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\Delta_{PP}-\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}\Delta_{QQ}-3\Delta_{QP}+D_{QP},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\derivative{\Delta_{PP}}{N}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}\Delta_{QP}-6\Delta_{PP}+D_{PP},

with diffusion entries

3.27

Equivalently,

4.4

\displaystyle\derivative{\mathbf{\Sigma}}{N}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{\Sigma}+\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{A}^{\top}+\mathbf{D},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&\dfrac{1}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\\[6.0pt]
-\dfrac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}&-3\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}

\displaystyle=3\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{e^{3N}}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\absolutevalue{\phi_{k_\sigma}}^{2}&\real(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}})\\[8.0pt]

\mathbf{A}\bm{\Sigma}_{\infty}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\infty}\mathbf{A}^{\top}+\mathbf{D}=0.

(4.6)

\bm{\Sigma}_{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mathbf{A}s}\mathbf{D}e^{\mathbf{A}^{\top}s}\,\differential s,\qquad\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{QQ}^{\infty}&\Delta_{QP}^{\infty}\\

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{QQ}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-3N}\absolutevalue{\pi_{k_\sigma}}^{2}+6H\,\real(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}})+e^{3N}(9H^{2}+m^{2})\absolutevalue{\phi_{k_\sigma}}^{2}}{2m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Delta_{PP}^{\infty}

(4.10)

N

\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)
has the form

W_{G}(z;N)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\det\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{z}-\expectationvalue*{\hat{\mathbf{Z}}})^{\top}\mathbf{\mathbf{\Sigma}}^{-1}(N)(\mathbf{z}-\expectationvalue*{\hat{\mathbf{Z}}})\right].

(4.11)

The purity

and
A.88
. For a Gaussian Wigner state, the purity is entirely determined by the covariance determinant
\det\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)=\Delta\hat{Q}^{2}\Delta\hat{P}^{2}-(\Delta\hat{Q}\hat{P})^{2}
:

\gamma(N)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\det\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)}}.

(4.12)

The stationary purity
\gamma_{\infty}
follows by inserting
eq.

4.7
into
eq.

4.12
. The same equation also determines how fast the covariance approaches the stationary Gaussian state. The exact covariance solution is

\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)=\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}+e^{\mathbf{A}N}\left(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{0}-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}\right)e^{\mathbf{A}^{\top}N}.

(4.13)

Thus the covariance gap is governed by pairwise sums of the drift eigenvalues

-\frac{3}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{9-\frac{4m^{2}}{H^{2}}}.

(4.14)

For perturbations around the stationary Gaussian covariance, Taylor expanding
\gamma(\mathbf{\Sigma})=1/(2\sqrt{\det\mathbf{\Sigma}})
around
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}
shows that
\gamma(N)-\gamma_{\infty}
is linearly controlled by
\mathbf{\Sigma}(N)-\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}
. The slowest covariance mode therefore fixes the linearised purity relaxation rate,

\derivative{\gamma}{N}\simeq\begin{cases}-\left[\dfrac{2m^{2}}{3H^{2}}+O\!\left(\dfrac{m^{4}}{H^{4}}\right)\right]\left(\gamma-\gamma_{\infty}\right),&\dfrac{m}{H}\ll 1,\\[8.0pt]
-\left(3-\sqrt{9-\dfrac{4m^{2}}{H^{2}}}\right)\left(\gamma-\gamma_{\infty}\right),&0<\dfrac{m}{H}<\dfrac{3}{2},\\[8.0pt]
-3\left(\gamma-\gamma_{\infty}\right),&\dfrac{m}{H}\geq\dfrac{3}{2}.\end{cases}

(4.15)

For light fields, the coarse-grained bulk may thus take a large number of e-folds, of the order
H^{2}/m^{2}
, to decohere to a highly mixed state. In contrast, decoherence for heavy fields occurs over order-one e-folds, though to a much lesser final extent than for light fields.

4.1
Light-mass fields

For a light free spectator field
0\leq m<3H/2
, the order

\nu=\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}-\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}}

(4.16)

is real and lies in
0<\nu\leq 3/2
, with the strictly massless limit corresponding to
\nu=3/2
. The diffusion coefficients of the Wigner generator in
eq.

3.26
evaluate to

\displaystyle D_{QQ}^{\rm light}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{8\pi}\left|H_{\nu}^{(2)}(\sigma)\right|^{2},

(4.17)

\displaystyle D_{QP}^{\rm light}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{16\pi}\left[H_{\nu-1}^{(1)}(\sigma)H_{\nu}^{(2)}(\sigma)+H_{\nu}^{(1)}(\sigma)H_{\nu-1}^{(2)}(\sigma)-\frac{3}{\sigma}\left|H_{\nu}^{(2)}(\sigma)\right|^{2}\right],

(4.18)

\displaystyle D_{PP}^{\rm light}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{8\pi}\left|H_{\nu-1}^{(2)}(\sigma)-\frac{3}{2\sigma}H_{\nu}^{(2)}(\sigma)\right|^{2}.

(4.19)

The corresponding Fokker–Planck equation for the Wigner function
W(q,p;N)
can be written in conservative form as

\displaystyle\partial_{N}W={}

\displaystyle-\partial_{q}\!\left(\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}pW\right)+\partial_{p}\!\left[\left(3p+\frac{6\pi^{2}m^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{4}}q\right)W\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}^{2}W+D_{QP}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}\partial_{p}W+\frac{1}{2}D_{PP}^{\rm light}\partial_{p}^{2}W.

(4.20)

The reduction to a field-only equation proceeds in three steps.

1.

Integrate the canonical Wigner equation over
p
to obtain an exact equation for the field marginal
{\cal P}_{Q}
, whose current
J_{Q}
is not yet closed.

2.

Use the overdamped hierarchy to solve
J_{Q}
quasistatically, estimating the needed second velocity moment from the fast conditional
p
-profile at fixed
q
.

3.

After this field-only equation is obtained, take the small-mass slow-roll hierarchy that gives the standard Starobinsky limit.

The canonical momentum
p
is extensive in the coarse-grained patch. The combination

\frac{p}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}=\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}p}{6\pi^{2}},

(4.21)

is the intensive velocity that enters the field current. The overdamped reduction below uses the light-field, super-Hubble hierarchy, in which the momentum/current sector relaxes faster than the field marginal. The additional slow-roll expansion in
m^{2}/H^{2}
is only needed when the resulting field equation is compared with the standard Starobinsky form.

We now reduce
eq.

4.20
by integrating over the momentum variable following a similar treatment to that given in
pavliotis2014stochastic
. We define the field marginal probability distribution

{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\differential p\,W(q,p;N),

(4.22)

and the associated field current

J_{Q}(q,N)=\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\differential p\,pW(q,p;N).

(4.23)

Integrating
eq.

4.20
over
p
gives a closed expression for the evolution of the field marginal probability distribution, provided the boundary terms generated by integrations by parts in
p
vanish. This is the case for the Gaussian states considered here, and more generally whenever
W
,
\partial_{p}W
, and
pW
decay sufficiently fast as
|p|\to\infty
. One obtains

\partial_{N}{\cal P}_{Q}=-\partial_{q}J_{Q}+\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}^{2}{\cal P}_{Q}.

(4.24)

The equation is not yet closed because it contains
J_{Q}
. To obtain a closed equation for
{\cal P}_{Q}
, multiply
eq.

4.20
by

\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}p

and integrate over
p
. A direct integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\partial_{N}J_{Q}=-3J_{Q}-\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}q{\cal P}_{Q}-\partial_{q}M_{2}-\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}D_{QP}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}{\cal P}_{Q}+\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}^{2}J_{Q}\,,

(4.25)

where the second velocity moment

M_{2}(q,N)=\left(\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\differential p\,p^{2}W(q,p;N)

(4.26)

has been introduced.
Equation (
4.25
) is then expanded in the light, super-Hubble regime. The term
-3J_{Q}
is leading, while the slow-roll drift source is
O(m^{2}/H^{2})
. The diffusion terms retained below scale as
D_{QQ}^{\rm light}=O(H^{2})
,
(\sigma^{3}H^{2})D_{QP}^{\rm light}=O(H^{2}\sigma^{2})
, and
(\sigma^{6}H^{4})D_{PP}^{\rm light}=O(H^{2}\sigma^{4})
, up to numerical factors. Terms with an additional
N
-derivative or extra
q
-gradients of
J_{Q}
are therefore beyond the leading overdamped closure. We set

\partial_{N}J_{Q}\simeq 0,\qquad\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm light}\partial_{q}^{2}J_{Q}\simeq 0

(4.27)

at this order.
It remains to approximate
M_{2}
. At leading overdamped order,
q
changes slowly compared with
p
. We define
G_{q}(p;N)
as the solution of
eq.

4.20
at fixed
q
, obtained by dropping all derivatives with respect to
q
. The resulting momentum-space dynamics can be written in the form

\partial_{N}G_{q}=-\partial_{p}J_{P}\,,

(4.28)

where

J_{P}=-\left[\left(3p+\frac{6\pi^{2}m^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{4}}q\right)G_{q}+\frac{1}{2}D_{PP}^{\rm light}\partial_{p}G_{q}\right].

(4.29)

The stationary profile with no flux through
|p|=\infty
satisfies
J_{P}=0
, from which we obtain the normalised Gaussian solution

G_{q}(p)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\pi D_{PP}^{\rm light}}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{3}{D_{PP}^{\rm light}}\left(p+\frac{2\pi^{2}m^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{4}}q\right)^{2}\right],\qquad\int\differential p\,G_{q}(p)=1.

(4.30)

In the quasistatic approximation the momentum dependence rapidly approaches this Gaussian profile,
W(q,p;N)\simeq{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)G_{q}(p)
. Computing
M_{2}
then reduces to Gaussian moments,

\int\differential p\,pG_{q}(p)=-\frac{2\pi^{2}m^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{4}}q,

(4.31)

and

\int\differential p\,p^{2}G_{q}(p)=\frac{4\pi^{4}m^{4}}{\sigma^{6}H^{8}}q^{2}+\frac{D_{PP}^{\rm light}}{6}\,.

(4.32)

The projected second velocity moment is therefore

M_{2}=\left(\frac{m^{4}}{9H^{4}}q^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{6}H^{4}}{216\pi^{4}}D_{PP}^{\rm light}\right){\cal P}_{Q}.

(4.33)

Keeping both terms in
eq.

4.33
gives

J_{Q}=-\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2m^{2}}{9H^{2}}\right)q{\cal P}_{Q}-\left(\frac{m^{4}}{27H^{4}}q^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{18\pi^{2}}D_{QP}^{\rm light}+\frac{\sigma^{6}H^{4}}{648\pi^{4}}D_{PP}^{\rm light}\right)\partial_{q}{\cal P}_{Q}+O\!\left(\text{higher gradients}\right).

(4.34)

Inserting this current into the exact marginal equation
eq.

4.24
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{N}{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)=\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2m^{2}}{9H^{2}}\right)\partial_{q}\!\left(q{\cal P}_{Q}\right)+\partial_{q}\!\left[\left(\frac{m^{4}}{27H^{4}}q^{2}+D_{Q}^{\rm eff}\right)\partial_{q}{\cal P}_{Q}\right]+O\!\left(\text{higher gradients}\right),

(4.35)

with

D_{Q}^{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm light}+\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{18\pi^{2}}D_{QP}^{\rm light}+\frac{\sigma^{6}H^{4}}{648\pi^{4}}D_{PP}^{\rm light}\,.

(4.36)

For a light but non-zero mass, the index admits the expansion

\nu=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}+O\!\left(\frac{m^{4}}{H^{4}}\right).

(4.37)

The small-
\sigma
expansion contains factors of the form
\sigma^{3-2\nu}
. Expanding these factors in
m^{2}/H^{2}
generates logarithms of
\sigma
:

\sigma^{3-2\nu}=\sigma^{\frac{2m^{2}}{3H^{2}}+O(m^{4}/H^{4})}=1+\frac{2m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\ln\sigma+\cdots.

(4.38)

Therefore the light-mass expansion of the effective diffusion coefficient is controlled by

\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\ll 1,\qquad\sigma\ll 1,\qquad\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}|\ln\sigma|\ll 1.

(4.39)

Thus, under the controlled light-field expansion hierarchy in
eq.

4.39
, the drift is kept at order
m^{2}/H^{2}
, while the
m^{4}/H^{4}
terms in
eq.

4.35
are dropped and the diffusion coefficient is evaluated at leading order in
m^{2}/H^{2}
. At the same order,

M_{2}=\frac{\sigma^{6}H^{4}}{216\pi^{4}}D_{PP}^{\rm light}{\cal P}_{Q}+O\!\left(\frac{m^{4}}{H^{4}}\right).

(4.40)

For light fields near the slow-roll regime,
D_{PP}^{\rm light}\sim\sigma^{-2}
, so the velocity-width contribution scales as

\frac{\sigma^{6}H^{4}}{216\pi^{4}}D_{PP}^{\rm light}=O(\sigma^{4}).

(4.41)

The canonical momentum width need not vanish; the super-Hubble suppression applies to the intensive velocity entering the current.
This gives

D_{Q}^{\rm eff}=\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}+O\!\left(\sigma^{2},\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\ln\sigma,\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}\right).

(4.42)

Combining
eqs.

4.35
and
4.42
, the leading light-mass, super-Hubble field equation is

\displaystyle\partial_{N}{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)=\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\partial_{q}\!\left(q{\cal P}_{Q}\right)+D_{Q}^{\rm eff}\partial_{q}^{2}{\cal P}_{Q}+O\!\left(\text{higher gradients},\frac{m^{4}}{H^{4}}\right),

(4.43)

and therefore

\partial_{N}{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)=\frac{m^{2}}{3H^{2}}\partial_{q}\left[q{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N)\right]+\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\partial_{q}^{2}{\cal P}_{Q}(q,N).

(4.44)

Identifying
q
with the coarse-grained field variable,
eq.

4.44
reproduces the Starobinsky Fokker–Planck one dimensional equation (
1.8
) on the slow-roll attractor.

4.2
Comparison with previous works

In the massless limit, the Lindblad operator
eq.

3.22
reduces to

\hat{L}_{\chi}^{m\to 0}=e^{i(\chi-\sigma)}\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{\sigma H}\hat{Q}-\frac{H}{2\pi}(1-i\sigma)\hat{P}\right),

(4.45)

which is in contrast with the single Lindblad operator (
\Pi_{s}
) found in Refs.
Li2026Stochastic
;
Li2026lwl
. The phase-space equation obtained in these works in the same massless limit,

\partial_{N}W(\phi,\pi,N)=\left(-\frac{\pi}{e^{3N}H}\partial_{\phi}+\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\partial_{\phi}^{2}\right)W,

(4.46)

reproduces the same field-only Starobinsky diffusion coefficient as
eq.

4.43
. The distinction is that the infrared momentum density
\Pi_{s}
is not the canonical partner of the averaged field, since

P_{N}=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)\Pi_{s},\qquad[Q_{N},P_{N}]=i,\qquad[Q_{N},\Pi_{s}]=\frac{i}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)}.

(4.47)

Thus the momentum-density variables are adequate for the leading field-only limit, but they are not canonical variables for a coarse-grained patch and are not a harmless convention when reconstructing the quantum Wigner or Lindblad equation; this is also the normalisation issue present in v1 of our own arXiv preprint
Christie:2025knc
, corrected in v2 and the published version, as well as here, by using the extensive momentum
P_{N}=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}\Pi_{s}
.

4.3
Critical and Heavy Fields

The comparison with the massless/light stochastic-field limit is controlled by two diagnostics: whether the field marginal retains an unsuppressed random-walk channel, and whether the reduced phase-space state loses purity. In the critical and heavy regimes, as we shall now see, the
D_{QQ}
term and the velocity-weighted
D_{QP}
and
D_{PP}
feed-through terms vanish as
\sigma\to 0
, so the field random-walk channel is suppressed, while the full
(Q,P)
state relaxes to a finite-purity stationary Gaussian.

4.3.1
Critical field

At
m=3H/2
, one has
\nu=0
. The Lindblad operator becomes

\hat{L}_{\rm crit}=e^{-i\pi/4}\left[\frac{3\pi^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma^{3/2}H}\left(\sigma H_{-1}^{(2)}(\sigma)+\frac{3}{2}H_{0}^{(2)}(\sigma)\right)\hat{Q}+\frac{H\sigma^{3/2}}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}H_{0}^{(2)}(\sigma)\hat{P}\right].

(4.48)

The corresponding Fokker–Planck equation for the Wigner function is

\displaystyle\partial_{N}W_{\rm crit}={}

\displaystyle-\partial_{q}\!\left(\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}pW_{\rm crit}\right)+\partial_{p}\!\left[\left(3p+\frac{27\pi^{2}}{2\sigma^{3}H^{2}}q\right)W_{\rm crit}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}D^{\rm crit}_{QQ}\partial_{q}^{2}W_{\rm crit}+D^{\rm crit}_{QP}\partial_{q}\partial_{p}W_{\rm crit}+\frac{1}{2}D^{\rm crit}_{PP}\partial_{p}^{2}W_{\rm crit}.

(4.49)

Using
H_{-1}^{(2)}=-H_{1}^{(2)}
, and for
\sigma\ll 1
,

H_{0}^{(2)}(\sigma)\simeq 1-i\frac{2}{\pi}\left[\ln\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)+\upgamma_{\rm E}\right],\qquad H_{1}^{(2)}(\sigma)\simeq\frac{\sigma}{2}+i\frac{2}{\pi\sigma},

(4.50)

and denoting the Euler–Mascheroni constant by
\upgamma_{\rm E}
, the final super-Hubble limit gives

\displaystyle D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{8\pi}\left[1+\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{\sigma}{2}+\upgamma_{\rm E}\right)^{2}\right]\to 0\,,

(4.51)

\displaystyle D_{QP}^{\rm crit}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\pi}{4}\left[\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\ln\frac{\sigma}{2}+\upgamma_{\rm E}\right)\left(2+3\ln\frac{\sigma}{2}+3\upgamma_{\rm E}\right)\right],

(4.52)

\displaystyle D_{PP}^{\rm crit}

\displaystyle=\frac{9\pi^{3}}{2H^{2}\sigma^{3}}\left[\frac{9}{4}+\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left(2+3\ln\frac{\sigma}{2}+3\upgamma_{\rm E}\right)^{2}\right].

(4.53)

The Wronskian normalisation
\mathrm{Im}(\phi^{*}_{k_{\sigma}}\pi_{k_{\sigma}})=-1/2
gives the mass-independent identity

D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}D_{PP}^{\rm crit}-\left(D_{QP}^{\rm crit}\right)^{2}=\frac{9}{4}\,,

(4.54)

Thus the dissipator remains active as a squeezed phase-space noise source; field diffusion vanishes, while the momentum diffusion and
QP
correlation compensate so that the one-channel quantum area is fixed. The connection between the Lindblad operator and the Wigner diffusion matrix is summarised in
appendix

C
. The deterministic part is given by the shared drift equations (
4.2
); at
m=3H/2
,
eq.

4.3
is critically damped. The boundary channel still injects vacuum fluctuations, but its
D_{QQ}
entry,
eq.

4.51
, vanishes in the super-Hubble cutoff limit
\sigma\to 0
; the
D_{QP}^{\rm crit}
and
D_{PP}^{\rm crit}
feed-through terms carry the field-current factors
\sigma^{3}H^{2}
and
\sigma^{6}H^{4}
, respectively, and are suppressed as well, so no unsuppressed field random-walk channel emerges. The explicit super-Hubble critical Fokker–Planck operator is
eq.

4.49
. Its stationary covariance is

\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}^{\rm crit}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{5}{6}D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}+\frac{4D_{QP}^{\rm crit}}{9H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}+\frac{2D_{PP}^{\rm crit}}{27H^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}^{2}}&-\frac{1}{2}H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}\\[8.0pt]
-\frac{1}{2}H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}&\frac{1}{6}D_{PP}^{\rm crit}+\frac{3}{8}H^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}^{2}D_{QQ}^{\rm crit}\end{pmatrix},

(4.55)

and

\gamma_{\infty}^{\rm crit}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\infty}^{\rm crit}}}>0.

(4.56)

The purity in the deep super Hubble limit can be calculated explicitly as

\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\gamma_{\infty}^{\rm crit}=\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{4+9\pi^{2}}}=0.978...

(4.57)

The first correction contains no term linear in
\sigma
:

\gamma_{\infty}^{\rm crit}(\sigma)=\frac{3\pi}{\sqrt{4+9\pi^{2}}}+\frac{12\pi\sigma^{2}}{(4+9\pi^{2})^{3/2}}\left(\ln\frac{\sigma}{2}+\upgamma_{\rm E}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+O(\sigma^{4}\ln^{2}\sigma).

(4.58)

Although individual covariance entries scale with
\sigma
, the determinant remains finite and non-zero. The critical bulk therefore has a stable damped-oscillator covariance instead of the purity-losing field spreading of the massless limit.

4.3.2
Heavy fields

For heavy fields,
\nu=i\mu
, with

\mu=\sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{9}{4}}.

(4.59)

The shared deterministic equation (
4.3
) is then underdamped, with

\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}(N)=e^{-3N/2}\left[C_{1}\cos(\mu N)+C_{2}\sin(\mu N)\right].

(4.60)

This underdamped phase-space rotation is the qualitative feature that is absent at the critical point. The dissipator still has the same one-channel form, but the heavy shell coefficients now oscillate with
\ln\sigma
. The corresponding Fokker–Planck equation for the Wigner function is

\displaystyle\partial_{N}W_{\rm heavy}={}

\displaystyle-\partial_{q}\!\left(\frac{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}pW_{\rm heavy}\right)+\partial_{p}\!\left\{\left[3p+\frac{6\pi^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{2}}\left(\frac{9}{4}+\mu^{2}\right)q\right]W_{\rm heavy}\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}D^{\rm heavy}_{QQ}\partial_{q}^{2}W_{\rm heavy}+D^{\rm heavy}_{QP}\partial_{q}\partial_{p}W_{\rm heavy}+\frac{1}{2}D^{\rm heavy}_{PP}\partial_{p}^{2}W_{\rm heavy}.

(4.61)

For fixed
\mu
, the small-
\sigma
expansion has the form

H_{i\mu}^{(2)}(\sigma)=\frac{(\sigma/2)^{-i\mu}}{\sinh(\pi\mu)\Gamma(1-i\mu)}-\frac{e^{-\pi\mu}(\sigma/2)^{i\mu}}{\sinh(\pi\mu)\Gamma(1+i\mu)}+O(\sigma^{2}).

(4.62)

Writing
\theta_{\mu}(\sigma)=\arg\Gamma(1+i\mu)-\mu\ln(\sigma/2)
, the super-Hubble diffusion coefficients become

\displaystyle D_{QQ}^{\rm heavy}

\displaystyle=\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{4\pi^{2}\mu\sinh(\pi\mu)}\left[\cosh(\pi\mu)-\cos(2\theta_\mu)\right]+O(H^{2}\sigma^{5}),

(4.63)

\displaystyle D_{QP}^{\rm heavy}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2\mu\sinh(\pi\mu)}\left\{\frac{3}{2}\left[\cosh(\pi\mu)-\cos(2\theta_\mu)\right]-\mu\sin(2\theta_\mu)\right\}+O(\sigma^{2}),

\displaystyle D_{PP}^{\rm heavy}

\displaystyle=\frac{9\pi^{2}}{H^{2}\sigma^{3}\mu\sinh(\pi\mu)}\left[\left(\frac{9}{4}+\mu^{2}\right)\cosh(\pi\mu)-\left(\frac{9}{4}-\mu^{2}\right)\cos(2\theta_\mu)-3\mu\sin(2\theta_\mu)\right]+O(H^{-2}\sigma^{-1}).

(4.64)

The
D_{QQ}
entry therefore still vanishes as
\sigma^{3}
, now with a bounded log-periodic prefactor. The mixed and momentum entries remain tied to it by the same one-channel Wronskian structure as in
eq.

4.54
; after the velocity factors entering
J_{Q}
are included, their field-current contributions are also
O(H^{2}\sigma^{3})
. The new heavy-field ingredient is the underdamped rotation of the retained
(Q,P)
oscillator together with these log-periodic shell coefficients. Thus heavy-field
D_{QQ}
and its momentum feed-through terms do not support an unsuppressed field random walk, while the deterministic dynamics remain those of a damped oscillator.

At fixed super-Hubble cutoff, the subsequent large-mass limit
\mu\simeq m/H\to\infty
gives

D_{QQ}^{\rm heavy}\sim\frac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{4\pi^{2}\mu},\qquad D_{QP}^{\rm heavy}\sim-\frac{9}{4\mu},\qquad D_{PP}^{\rm heavy}\sim\frac{9\pi^{2}\mu}{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}.

(4.65)

In this ordered limit the field noise and mixed noise disappear, while the momentum noise grows with the oscillator frequency. The stationary purity has the fixed-
\mu
, super-Hubble expansion

\gamma_{\infty}^{\rm heavy}(\sigma)=\left[1+\frac{4\mu^{2}}{(9+4\mu^{2})\sinh^{2}(\pi\mu)}\right]^{-1/2}+O(\sigma^{2}).

(4.66)

The log-periodic terms in the individual diffusion coefficients cancel from this leading phase-space area. As
\mu\to 0^{+}
,
eq.

4.66
matches the critical value in
eq.

4.57
; as
\mu\simeq m/H\to\infty
,

\gamma_{\infty}^{\rm heavy}=1-2e^{-2\pi\mu}+O(e^{-4\pi\mu})+O(\sigma^{2}).

(4.67)

The heavy stationary state therefore becomes exponentially close to a pure damped oscillator, rather than a classical field random walk.

5
Summary

We have developed a canonical, quantum open-system derivation of stochastic inflation for a free spectator scalar in de Sitter space, using a sharp physical momentum cutoff. The retained system is the homogeneous canonical pair
(Q_{N},P_{N})
: the averaged field over the effective patch and the total momentum in that patch. The sharp
k
-space window defines an effective time-dependent comoving volume in real space, which is required to normalise the equal-time commutator.

As the cutoff moves, Bunch-Davies modes enter the infrared sector. The homogeneous average couples only to one collective boundary mode, and the moving-basis redefinition of the bulk and boundary mode produces a redefined bulk entangled with a decoupled mode that is traced over. The resulting GKLS dynamics are generated by an effective Hamiltonian and a single non-Hermitian Lindblad operator demonstrating that for a free spectator scalar in de Sitter space diffusion and Hubble friction originate from the same quantum channel. We also provide several schemes under which one can unravel the GKLS dynamics into stochastic pure state dynamics as continuous measurement models of the decoupled mode, making contact with Langevin formulations of stochastic inflation.

Taking the Wigner-Weyl transform of the GKLS dynamics provides a Fokker–Planck equation for the Wigner quasiprobabilty distribution. In the light field limit, one can perform an overdamped reduction by integrating out the momentum variable with the Wigner marginal distribution obeying the Starobinsky Fokker-Planck equation. Critical mass
m=3H/2
and heavier fields are not subject to strong diffusive dynamics as
\sigma\to 0
; instead the retained
(Q,P)
state is an underdamped oscillator with finite stationary purity. At criticality the purity approaches the high value of
0.978
with no linear
\sigma
correction. In the heavy regime the leading log-periodic shell factors cancel from the purity, which tends to one as
m/H
grows.

For the free quadratic spectator theory considered here, the Wigner transform closes exactly into a second-order Fokker–Planck equation. A natural next step is to ask whether the same canonical coarse-graining framework can be extended to the inflaton and to genuinely interacting fields. In that setting, self-interactions and gravitational constraints are expected to generate higher Moyal derivatives in the Wigner-function dynamics that are not captured in the Fokker-Planck equation of stochastic inflation, together with non-Gaussian correlations and non-Markovian effects. These additional phenomena could modify the standard stochastic description of inflation, with possible consequences for the statistics of primordial fluctuations, rare-event tails, primordial black-hole production, and the size and shape of inflationary non-Gaussianities.

A.1
Canonical Bulk Variables

The sharp infrared projector defining the bulk is

\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})=\Theta\!\left(k_{\sigma}(N)-|\bm{k}|\right),\qquad k_{\sigma}(N)=\sigma e^{N}H.

(A.5)

In the one-step decomposition used below, this sector is the full bulk
{\cal F}_{N}={\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{N}^{\perp}
. The canonical averages below generate its homogeneous mode
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}
. The next bulk-boundary system is obtained by adding the boundary shell
{\cal F}_{\Delta}={\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\perp}
. The corresponding real-space window is

W(N,\bm{x})=\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}=\frac{k_{\sigma}^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{j_{1}(k_{\sigma}|\bm{x}|)}{k_{\sigma}|\bm{x}|},

(A.6)

where

j_{1}(z)=\frac{\sin z}{z^{2}}-\frac{\cos z}{z}.

(A.7)

At the origin,

W(N,0)=\frac{k_{\sigma}^{3}(N)}{6\pi^{2}}.

(A.8)

The effective comoving bulk volume is

\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)=\frac{1}{W(N,0)}=\frac{6\pi^{2}}{k_{\sigma}^{3}(N)}.

(A.9)

Since
k_{\sigma}(N)=\sigma e^{N}H
, the corresponding physical bulk volume is time independent:

\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}=e^{3N}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)=\frac{6\pi^{2}}{\sigma^{3}H^{3}}.

(A.10)

We have

\hat{Q}_{N}=\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})\hat{\Phi}_{\bm{k}}(N)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\hat{P}_{N}=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}}(N)\ .

(A.11)

The field variable
\hat{Q}_{N}
is an intensive bulk average. The conjugate momentum
\hat{P}_{N}
is extensive, and the factor
\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)
makes it canonically conjugate to
\hat{Q}_{N}
.

Using

[\hat{\Phi}_{\bm{k}}(N),\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(N)]=i(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})

(A.12)

and
\Theta_{\sigma}^{2}=\Theta_{\sigma}
, one obtains

\displaystyle[\hat{Q}_{N},\hat{P}_{N}]

\displaystyle=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{\differential^{3}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k}^{\prime})[\hat{\Phi}_{\bm{k}},\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=i\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})=i\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)W(N,0)=i.

(A.13)

Thus the coarse-grained variables define a one-mode canonical algebra represented on the homogeneous one-mode Fock space
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}
.

A.2
Bulk Part of the Bulk-Boundary Dynamics

The one-step map from
N

N+\differential N

N+\differential N

(\hat{Q}_{N+\differential N},\hat{P}_{N+\differential N})
generates
{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}
:

\hat{Q}_{N+\differential N}=\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N+\differential N,\bm{k})\hat{\Phi}_{\bm{k}}(N+\differential N),

(A.14)

and

\hat{P}_{N+\differential N}=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N+\differential N)\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N+\differential N,\bm{k})\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}}(N+\differential N).

(A.15)

The quadratic infrared Hamiltonian is

\hat{H}_{{\rm IR},N}=\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})\left[\frac{1}{2e^{3N}}\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}}\hat{\Pi}_{-\bm{k}}+\frac{e^{3N}}{2}\left(k^{2}e^{-2N}+m^{2}\right)\hat{\Phi}_{\bm{k}}\hat{\Phi}_{-\bm{k}}\right].

(A.16)

It gives, mode by mode,

(A.17)

(A.18)

Split the updated projector as

\Theta_{\sigma}(N+\differential N,\bm{k})=\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})+\Delta\Theta_{N}(\bm{k}),\qquad\Delta\Theta_{N}(\bm{k})=\Theta_{\sigma}(N+\differential N,\bm{k})-\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k}).

(A.19)

We define the collective boundary increments on

(A.20)

and

(A.21)

When these boundary increments appear in the active input space
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{N+\differential N}-\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes d\hat{q}_{\Delta}

(A.22)

For the momentum, using

(A.23)

one obtains

\displaystyle=\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N+\differential N)\int\frac{\differential^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Theta_{\sigma}(N,\bm{k})\hat{\Pi}_{\bm{k}}(N+\differential N)\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{-3\differential N}\hat{P}_{N}-\frac{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}m^{2}}{H}\hat{Q}_{N}\,\differential N\right)\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}

The commutator excluding the boundary increment is

(A.43)

A canonically normalised representative is

\hat{A}_{N}=i\left[\frac{\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}}{\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)}}\hat{P}_{N}-\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}(N)}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\hat{Q}_{N}\right].

(A.44)

Using the Wronskian relation

\phi_{k_{\sigma}}\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}-\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}}=i,

(A.45)

one obtains

[\hat{A}_{N},\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}]=1.

(A.46)

The inverse relations are

\hat{Q}_{N}=\frac{\phi_{k_{\sigma}}}{\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}}\hat{A}_{N}+\frac{\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}}{\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}}\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger},\qquad\hat{P}_{N}=\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}\hat{A}_{N}+\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}.

(A.47)

The boundary increment of
eq.

A.44
is given by

\displaystyle\left.\differential\hat{A}_{N}\right|_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=i\left(\frac{\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}}{\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{p}_{\Delta}-\sqrt{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{q}_{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle=i\sqrt{3}\left(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}}-\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\phi_{k_{\sigma}}\right)\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}+O(\differential N^{3/2})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{3}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.48)

A.4
Unitary Bulk-Boundary Entangling

In order to eventually transition from the Heisenberg picture canonical variable evolution to the Schrödinger picture reduced bulk density operator dynamics, we now construct the joint unitary
\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}
on
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}
whose Heisenberg action reproduces the one-step evolution derived in Sections
A.2
and
A.3
. The entangling unitary produces the decoupled mode. The relevant input boundary mode is the collective increment
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}
defined in
eq.

A.32
. The boundary calculation in
eq.

A.48
requires that the adapted annihilation operator obey, to leading order,

\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\longmapsto\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\sqrt{3}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}+O(\differential N),\qquad\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\longmapsto\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\sqrt{3}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}+O(\differential N),

(A.49)

with no
\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}
term in the first equation and no
\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}
term in the second. The same calculation gave the canonical variable Heisenberg dynamics:

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\frac{1}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{q}_{\Delta}+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}-\left(3\hat{P}_{N}+\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}\hat{Q}_{N}\right)\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{p}_{\Delta}+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.50)

The unitary we seek must reproduce both
eq.

A.49
and
eq.

A.50
. The thin-shell condition is essential here: the collective boundary increment is
O(\differential N^{1/2})
, its commutator is
O(\differential N)
, and all independent
O(\differential N^{3/2})
corrections are beyond the order needed to determine the GKLS generator. Therefore the leading bulk-boundary generator may be taken to be linear in
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}
and
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}
. Terms with more boundary increments start beyond the required order, while
O(\differential N)
bulk-boundary couplings only affect the Heisenberg equations at
O(\differential N^{3/2})
.

We start with the generic form

\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}=\exp\!\left[\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}-i\bigl(\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\bigr)\,\differential N\right]+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

(A.51)

where
\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}=O(\sqrt{\differential N})
is anti-Hermitian and couples the bulk mode to the collective boundary increment, while
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}=O(1)
is a Hermitian bulk Hamiltonian. Since
[\hat{S}_{N+\differential N},(\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}})\,\differential N]=O(\differential N^{3/2})
, the ordering of the two exponentials is immaterial here, and we may equivalently write

\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}=\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(e^{-i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\,\differential N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)+O(\differential N^{3/2}),\qquad\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}=\exp(\hat S_{N+\dd N}).

(A.52)

The most general anti-Hermitian bulk-boundary generator linear in
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}
and
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}
, and linear in the adapted bulk mode, is

\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}=c_{1}\,\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}-c_{1}^{*}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}+c_{2}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}-c_{2}^{*}\,\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger},

(A.53)

with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. This is the only relevant
O(\sqrt{\differential N})
coupling under the stated physical conditions. A generator acting only on the bulk at
O(\sqrt{\differential N})
would produce deterministic
O(\sqrt{\differential N})
shifts of
\hat{Q}_{N}
,
\hat{P}_{N}
, or
\hat{A}_{N}
, and such terms are absent from the Heisenberg equations
eq.

A.49
and
eq.

A.50
. A boundary-only displacement commutes with the bulk operators and cannot reproduce the required stochastic increments. It is also physically excluded because it would displace the newly incoming boundary shell shell away from the Bunch-Davies vacuum. A boundary-only phase rotation merely changes the convention for the phase of
\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}
, and this freedom will reappear below as the physically irrelevant phase of the GKLS jump operator. Finally, a scalar phase of the full unitary is irrelevant, and a scalar addition to
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}
has no effect on the dynamics. Thus, modulo these physically irrelevant phase conventions,
eq.

A.53
is the unique leading-order ansatz capable of satisfying all the Heisenberg equations derived in the previous two sections.

Using the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion

\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\hat{X}\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}=\hat{X}+[\hat{X},\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}]-i[\hat{X},\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}]\,\differential N+\frac{1}{2}[[\hat{X},\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}],\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}]+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

(A.54)

together with
[\hat{A}_{N},\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}]=1
and
[\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta},\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}]=\differential N+O(\differential N^{2})
, one finds

[\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}},\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}]=c_{1}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}-c_{2}^{*}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger},

(A.55)

and

\frac{1}{2}[[\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}},\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}],\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}]=\frac{1}{2}\left(|c_{2}|^{2}-|c_{1}|^{2}\right)\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.56)

The conjugate relations for
\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}
follow by Hermitian conjugation. The absence of an
\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}
term in the evolution of
\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}
, together with the coefficient
\sqrt{3}
in
eq.

A.49
, fixes

c_{1}=\sqrt{3},\qquad c_{2}=0,

(A.57)

up to an irrelevant overall phase of the boundary mode. Therefore

\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}=\sqrt{3}\left(\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}-\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\right),\qquad\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}=\exp(\hat S_{N+\dd N})

(A.58)

is the unique leading bulk-boundary entangling unitary at this order under the physical conditions just stated. Substituting
eq.

A.57
into
eq.

A.56
reduces the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff
O(\differential N)
term to
-\tfrac{3}{2}\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N
, so the Heisenberg action of
\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}
on the adapted ladder operator is

\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\left(\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}=\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\sqrt{3}\,\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}-\frac{3}{2}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.59)

Using the inverse relations
eq.

A.47
, equation
eq.

A.59
translates into an isotropic contraction of the canonical variables together with the boundary increments,

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\left(\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{q}_{\Delta}-\frac{3}{2}\,\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\left(\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}

\displaystyle=\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{p}_{\Delta}-\frac{3}{2}\,\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.60)

The bulk-boundary entangling therefore supplies the required stochastic increments and produces a deterministic contraction of both canonical variables. The remaining anisotropic drift must be generated by the bulk Hamiltonian
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}
.

Take the most general quadratic Hermitian bulk Hamiltonian on
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}
that can contribute to the required linear homogeneous Heisenberg drift,

\hat{K}_{\rm eff}=\frac{c_{3}}{2}\hat{P}_{N}^{2}+\frac{c_{4}}{2}\hat{Q}_{N}^{2}+\frac{c_{5}}{2}\{\hat{Q}_{N},\hat{P}_{N}\},\qquad c_{3},c_{4},c_{5}\in\mathbb{R}.

(A.61)

A completely general quadratic Hermitian Hamiltonian could also contain linear terms proportional to
\hat{Q}_{N}
and
\hat{P}_{N}
, together with a constant. The linear terms would generate constant shifts in the Heisenberg equations for
\hat{P}_{N}
and
\hat{Q}_{N}
, respectively. Since no such constant drifts appear in
eq.

A.50
, their coefficients must vanish. The constant term is dynamically irrelevant and is omitted.

Using
[\hat{Q}_{N},\hat{P}_{N}]=i
,

-i[\hat{Q}_{N},\hat{K}_{\rm eff}]=c_{3}\hat{P}_{N}+c_{5}\hat{Q}_{N},\qquad-i[\hat{P}_{N},\hat{K}_{\rm eff}]=-c_{4}\hat{Q}_{N}-c_{5}\hat{P}_{N}.

(A.62)

Embedding
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}
as
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}
and combining
eq.

A.60
with the Hamiltonian contribution from
eq.

A.62
gives

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\left(\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}

\displaystyle\quad=\hat{Q}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{q}_{\Delta}+\left[c_{3}\hat{P}_{N}+\left(c_{5}-\frac{3}{2}\right)\hat{Q}_{N}\right]\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\left(\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}

\displaystyle\quad=\hat{P}_{N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\differential\hat{p}_{\Delta}-\left[c_{4}\hat{Q}_{N}+\left(c_{5}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\hat{P}_{N}\right]\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\,\differential N+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.63)

Matching
eq.

A.63
with the target
eq.

A.50
fixes

c_{3}=\frac{1}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}},\qquad c_{4}=\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H},\qquad c_{5}=\frac{3}{2}.

(A.64)

The two equations in
eq.

A.63
are consistent: both fix the same value
c_{5}=\tfrac{3}{2}
, reflecting the boundary
O(\differential N)
term strength
\tfrac{1}{2}|c_{1}|^{2}=\tfrac{3}{2}
inherited from
eq.

A.57
. Substituting
eq.

A.64
into
eq.

A.61
yields

\hat{K}_{\rm eff}=\frac{\hat{P}_{N}^{2}}{2H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}+\frac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{2H}\hat{Q}_{N}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\{\hat{Q}_{N},\hat{P}_{N}\}.

(A.65)

A.5
Tracing Over the Decoupled Mode

The reduced homogeneous state is obtained by applying the entangling joint unitary with the redefined-bulk/decoupled-mode decomposition

\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}:{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}\longrightarrow{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}

and then tracing over
{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}
, the decoupled mode. The incoming boundary shell mode in
{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}
is initially in the Bunch-Davies vacuum of the collective increment, denoted by
\ket{0}_{\Delta}
. We denote the decoupled-mode Fock basis by
\ket{n}_{\rm d}
. All modes orthogonal to this active two-mode construction remain in vacuum and decouple.

Using

\hat{V}_{N+\differential N}=\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(e^{-i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\differential N}\otimes\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}\right)+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

(A.66)

the one-step full state before the partial trace is

\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}=\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{N}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\!\bra{0}_{\Delta}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}+O(\differential N^{2}),

(A.67)

and the reduced one-step state is

\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}=\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}\left(\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right)=\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}\left[\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(\tilde{\rho}_{N}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\!\bra{0}_{\Delta}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}^{\dagger}\right]+O(\differential N^{2}),

(A.68)

where
\tilde{\rho}_{N}
is the Hamiltonian-evolved homogeneous state on
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}
,

\tilde{\rho}_{N}:=e^{-i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\differential N}\hat{\rho}_{N}e^{i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}\differential N}=\hat{\rho}_{N}-i\,\differential N\,[\hat{K}_{\rm eff},\hat{\rho}_{N}]+O(\differential N^{2}).

(A.69)

The input collective-boundary number states used to evaluate the redefinition are defined by

\displaystyle\ket{n}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\frac{(\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!\,(\differential N)^{n}}}\ket{0}_{\Delta},

(A.70)

\displaystyle\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}\ket{0}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=0,

(A.71)

\displaystyle\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}\ket{n}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n\,\differential N}\,\ket{n-1}_{\Delta},

(A.72)

\displaystyle\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\ket{n}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(n+1)\,\differential N}\,\ket{n+1}_{\Delta},

(A.73)

consistent with

[\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta},\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}]=\differential N+O(\differential N^{2}).

(A.74)

Inserting the collective-mode resolution of the identity,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\ket{n}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{n}=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}},

(A.75)

gives the Kraus representation

\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)\dagger}+O(\differential N^{2}),

(A.76)

with Kraus operators on the bulk,

\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}:=\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}}\otimes{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{n}\right)\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\right).

(A.77)

Each
\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}:{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\to{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}
acts only between homogeneous one-mode spaces, which are identified in the stationary representation below. We denote the canonical identity under this one-mode identification by
\hat{\mathds{1}}^{\parallel}_{N\to N+\differential N}:{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\to{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}
. To evaluate these Kraus operators, expand

\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}}+\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}^{3}+\cdots,

(A.78)

where
\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}
was defined in
eq.

A.58
. Each factor of
\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}
contains one boundary ladder increment and is therefore
O(\sqrt{\differential N})
. Acting on the incoming boundary vacuum, understood in the input representation as the map
\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}:{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\to{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}_{\Delta}^{\parallel}
, one finds

\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\right)=-\sqrt{3}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\ket{0}_{\Delta}=-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\ket{1}_{\Delta}.

(A.79)

Acting once more gives

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}^{2}\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\hat{S}_{N+\differential N}\left(\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\ket{1}_{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\sqrt{3}\left(\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}\ket{1}_{\Delta}-\hat{A}_{N}^{2}\otimes\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\ket{1}_{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle=-3\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}+3\sqrt{2}\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{2}\otimes\ket{2}_{\Delta}.

(A.80)

Here we used

\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}\ket{1}_{\Delta}=\sqrt{\differential N}\,\ket{0}_{\Delta},\qquad\differential\hat{b}_{\Delta}^{\dagger}\ket{1}_{\Delta}=\sqrt{2\,\differential N}\,\ket{2}_{\Delta}.

(A.81)

Combining
eq.

A.79
and
eq.

A.80
in
eq.

A.78
gives

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{N+\differential N}\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\ket{0}_{\Delta}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}^{\parallel}_{N\to N+\differential N}-\frac{3}{2}\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{N}\right)\otimes\ket{0}_{\rm d}

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\hat{A}_{N}\otimes\ket{1}_{\rm d}+\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{2}\otimes\ket{2}_{\rm d}+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.82)

Taking the boundary matrix elements in
eq.

A.77
therefore yields

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathds{1}}^{\parallel}_{N\to N+\differential N}-\frac{3}{2}\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{N}+O(\differential N^{2}),

(A.83)

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\hat{A}_{N}+O(\differential N^{3/2}),

(A.84)

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{2}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\,\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}^{2}+O(\differential N^{2}),

(A.85)

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}

\displaystyle=O(\differential N^{n/2})\qquad\text{for }n\geq 3.

(A.86)

In the Kraus sum
eq.

A.76
, the contribution from
\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}
is bilinear in
\hat{M}_{n}^{(N)}
and therefore scales as
\differential N^{n}
at leading order. Hence only
n=0
and
n=1
contribute at
O(\differential N)
; the
n\geq 2
terms are
O(\differential N^{2})
or smaller in the reduced Kraus sum.

It is useful, however, to display the pre-trace state before the decoupled-mode trace is performed. Projecting
{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}
onto the
\{\ket{0}_{\rm d},\ket{1}_{\rm d}\}
sector gives the
2\times 2
decoupled-mode block

\left.\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right|_{0,1}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)\dagger}&\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)\dagger}\\[6.0pt]
\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)\dagger}&\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)\dagger}\end{pmatrix}_{\{\ket{0}_{\rm d},\ket{1}_{\rm d}\}}+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.87)

Terms involving
\ket{2}_{\rm d}
lie outside this displayed block. They do not contribute to the diagonal reduced Kraus sum at
O(\differential N)
. Off-diagonal coherences involving
\ket{2}_{\rm d}
, such as
\ket{0}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{2}
and
\ket{2}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{0}
, are also killed by the decoupled-mode trace and do not alter the
O(\differential N)
GKLS generator.

Using
eq.

A.83
and
eq.

A.84
, the displayed block is

\left.\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right|_{0,1}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\rho}_{N}-\dfrac{3}{2}\differential N\bigl\{\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\hat{A}_{N},\tilde{\rho}_{N}\bigr\}&-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\\[8.0pt]
-\sqrt{3\,\differential N}\,\hat{A}_{N}\tilde{\rho}_{N}&3\differential N\,\hat{A}_{N}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{A}_{N}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}_{\{\ket{0}_{\rm d},\ket{1}_{\rm d}\}}+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(A.88)

Here the matrix indices refer to the vacuum and one-particle sectors of the decoupled mode. Equivalently, after the entangling unitary has acted, the same two-dimensional Fock block is the vacuum-one-particle block of the decoupled mode. Each entry in the matrix is an operator between the homogeneous modes
{\cal F}_{N}^{\parallel}\to{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}
. The diagonal entries are the decoupled-mode number blocks, while the off-diagonal entries are the leading vacuum-one-particle coherences generated by the bulk-boundary entangling.

The partial trace over the decoupled mode keeps only the diagonal decoupled-mode blocks,

\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}\left(\left.\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right|_{0,1}\right)=\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{0}^{(N)\dagger}+\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\hat{M}_{1}^{(N)\dagger},

(A.89)

because

Appendix B
POVMs and Stochastic Unravellings

In this appendix we use the stationary representation for the retained bulk system introduced above. Thus
\hat{\rho}
,
\hat{L}
, and
\hat{K}_{\rm eff}
act on the fixed one-mode algebra, while the labels
N+\differential N
are kept for the outgoing decoupled-mode Hilbert space and the pre-measurement joint state.

Before tracing over the decoupled mode, the post-unitary state lives on
{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}
, and it is generically entangled. Its leading vacuum-one-particle block structure is displayed in
eq.

A.87
and (
A.88
). Because an entangled state cannot be written as a product of an independent pure bulk state and an independent pure decoupled-mode state, the redefined bulk alone does not generally have a pure state. There are then two options. If the decoupled-mode information is not observed, it is removed from the reduced description by taking the partial trace over
{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}
. This gives the mixed reduced state on
{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}
and the non-selective GKLS evolution derived above. Alternatively, one may perform a perfectly efficient rank-one measurement of the decoupled mode. For a pure pre-measurement joint trajectory, a definite measurement outcome leaves the redefined bulk in the corresponding pure conditional state
wiseman2009quantum
. This selective branch is illustrated in the main text in
fig.

2
.

A stochastic unravelling is the corresponding selective description. One conditions the bulk state on a chosen continuous measurement record of the decoupled mode. Different choices of record give different conditioned trajectories, but their ensemble average reproduces the same reduced GKLS equation. The appropriate language for this choice of boundary record is a positive-operator-valued measure, or POVM. For the present purpose a POVM is simply a set of positive operators
\{\hat{\Pi}_{r}\}
acting on the decoupled mode, labelled by possible outcomes
r
, and satisfying

\sum_{r}\hat{\Pi}_{r}=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}},\qquad\hat{\Pi}_{r}\geq 0.

(B.1)

For a continuous outcome, the sum is replaced by an integral. If
\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}
is the full active state before the decoupled mode is measured, the probability for outcome
r
is

p(r)=\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}\otimes{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}\left[\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\hat{\Pi}_{r}\right)\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right].

(B.2)

The POVM fixes these probabilities but does not by itself fix a unique state update. In the efficient rank-one measurements of the decoupled mode used below, we take the associated rank-one instrument. The corresponding unnormalised conditional homogeneous state is

\mathcal{I}_{r}(\hat{\rho})=\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}\left[\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}}\otimes\hat{\Pi}_{r}\right)\hat{\rho}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel\perp}\right],

(B.3)

and the normalised conditional state is

\hat{\rho}_{c,r}=\frac{\mathcal{I}_{r}(\hat{\rho})}{\mathrm{Tr}_{{\cal F}_{N+\differential N}^{\parallel}}\mathcal{I}_{r}(\hat{\rho})}.

(B.4)

The map
\mathcal{I}_{r}
is called the instrument associated with the outcome
r
. The POVM specifies the probabilities, while the instrument specifies both the probabilities and the corresponding conditioned state update
wiseman2009quantum
.

In the present problem there is no external detector added by hand. The “meter” is the decoupled mode generated by the entangling unitary. The
2\times 2
Fock block in
eq.

A.87
makes this explicit. The diagonal blocks describe the vacuum and one-particle decoupled-mode sectors. The off-diagonal blocks, displayed in
eq.

A.91
, are the leading
\ket{0}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{1}
and
\ket{1}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{0}
coherences between the homogeneous bulk algebra and the decoupled mode. The unconditional trace removes these coherences. A continuous measurement of the decoupled mode instead turns them into a classical stochastic record.

Different choices of
\hat{\Pi}_{r}
give different unravellings. A Fock-basis POVM gives the jump, or counting, unravelling. A quadrature POVM gives a single-real-noise homodyne unravelling. A coherent-state POVM gives the two-real-noise heterodyne, or quantum-state-diffusion, unravelling. These are different measurements of the same decoupled mode, not different reduced generators.

This distinction will be important below. The usual Starobinsky Langevin equation arises only after choosing a decoupled-mode readout and then taking the appropriate super-Hubble and overdamped limits. In particular, the homodyne readout with the field-adapted phase gives a one-real-noise trajectory whose field-centre equation reduces to the standard Starobinsky noise amplitude. The heterodyne readout keeps both real components of the same decoupled-mode coherence and provides a phase-neutral comparison.

We now apply this general measurement language to the specific decoupled-mode block constructed in Appendix
A.5
. The goal is to show explicitly how different decoupled-mode POVMs contract the same pre-trace state.

It is convenient to combine the Hamiltonian and no-jump factor into

(B.5)

where

(B.33)

(B.34)

one obtains

2\mathrm{Re}(\mathcal{P}_{Q})=\Gamma_{Q}(\nu,\sigma),\qquad\Gamma_{Q}(\nu,\sigma)=\frac{2H\sigma^{3/2}}{\pi\sqrt{2\pi}\,|T_{Q}(\nu,\sigma)|}.

(B.35)

For complex
\nu
, the same definition is used with the adjoint order conjugated.

For a Gaussian conditional state, let

Z_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}\\
\expectationvalue*{\hat P}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{QQ}&\Delta_{QP}\\
\Delta_{QP}&\Delta_{PP}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(B.36)

The single-component innovation gives

dZ_{c,{\rm noise}}=\left(2\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}\,\mathrm{Re}\nabla L_{\chi}-\Omega\,\mathrm{Im}\nabla L_{\chi}\right)\differential W_{\chi}.

(B.37)

For the field-adapted phase this becomes

\displaystyle\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}\big|_{\rm noise}

\displaystyle=\left[\Gamma_{Q}\Delta_{QP}-\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{P}_{Q})\right]\differential W_{\chi_{Q}},

(B.38)

\displaystyle\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat P}\big|_{\rm noise}

\displaystyle=\left(\Gamma_{Q}\Delta_{PP}-\Lambda_{Q}\right)\differential W_{\chi_{Q}}.

(B.39)

In the massless case,
\nu=3/2
,

T_{Q}(3/2,\sigma)=\sigma H_{1/2}^{(2)}(\sigma)=i\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma}{\pi}}e^{-i\sigma}.

(B.40)

Equation (
B.30
) then gives

e^{i\chi_{Q}}=e^{i\sigma}.

(B.41)

The phase-rotated jump operator is

\hat{L}_{\sigma}=-i\frac{3\pi}{\sigma H}\hat{Q}+\frac{H}{2\pi}(\sigma-i)\hat{P},

(B.42)

so that

\Lambda_{Q}=\frac{3\pi}{\sigma H},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{Q}=\frac{H}{2\pi}(\sigma-i),\qquad\Gamma_{Q}=\frac{H\sigma}{\pi}.

(B.43)

The monitored component is

\hat{X}_{\sigma}=\hat{L}_{\sigma}+\hat{L}_{\sigma}^{\dagger}=\frac{H\sigma}{\pi}\hat{P}.

(B.44)

The field- and momentum-centre equations become

\displaystyle\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{\expectationvalue*{\hat P}}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\differential N+\frac{H}{2\pi}\left(1+2\sigma\Delta_{QP}\right)\differential W_{\sigma},

(B.45)

\displaystyle\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat P}

\displaystyle=\left(-3\expectationvalue*{\hat P}\right)\differential N+\left(-\frac{3\pi}{H\sigma}+\frac{H\sigma}{\pi}\Delta_{PP}\right)\differential W_{\sigma}.

(B.46)

Thus

\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}\big|_{\rm noise}=\frac{H}{2\pi}\differential W_{\sigma}+O(\sigma)\differential W_{\sigma}.

(B.47)

After adiabatic elimination of the fast momentum in the free massless limit,

\differential\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}=\frac{H}{2\pi}\differential W_{\sigma}.

(B.48)

This is the one-real-noise Starobinsky diffusion limit.

B.3
Heterodyne, or two-component, unravelling

The heterodyne readout does not choose a single real quadrature of the decoupled mode. Instead it uses the coherent-state POVM of the decoupled-mode oscillator. This records the full complex displacement of the decoupled mode, rather than one chosen real projection of it.

Using the normalised decoupled-mode oscillator of
eq.

B.15
, the coherent states satisfy

\hat{b}_{\rm d}\ket{z}_{\rm d}=z\ket{z}_{\rm d},\qquad\ket{z}_{\rm d}=e^{-|z|^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket{n}_{\rm d}.

(B.49)

They form the overcomplete POVM

\hat{\Pi}_{\rm coh}(\differential^{2}z)=\frac{\differential^{2}z}{\pi}\ket{z}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{z},\qquad\int\hat{\Pi}_{\rm coh}(\differential^{2}z)=\hat{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal F}^{\rm d,\,\perp}_{N+\differential N}}.

(B.50)

This is not a pair of sharp quadrature projectors. It is the simultaneous unsharp measurement of the two non-commuting decoupled-mode quadratures, with the extra vacuum noise required by such a simultaneous readout.

For consistency with the sign convention
\hat{J}=-\sqrt{\differential N}\,\hat{L}
, introduce the complex infinitesimal outcome
\differential Y
by

z=-\frac{\differential Y}{\sqrt{\differential N}}.

(B.51)

Expanding
\ket{z}\!{}_{\rm d}\,{}_{\rm d}\!\bra{z}
in the
\{\ket{0}_{\rm d},\ket{1}_{\rm d}\}
sector gives

\hat{\Pi}_{\rm het}(\differential^{2}Y)=p_{0}(\differential^{2}Y)\begin{pmatrix}1&-\dfrac{\differential Y^{*}}{\sqrt{\differential N}}\\[8.0pt]
-\dfrac{\differential Y}{\sqrt{\differential N}}&\dfrac{|\differential Y|^{2}}{\differential N}\end{pmatrix}_{\{\ket{0}_{\rm d},\ket{1}_{\rm d}\}}\differential^{2}Y+O(\differential N^{1/2}),

(B.52)

where

p_{0}(\differential^{2}Y)=\frac{1}{\pi\differential N}\exp\!\left(-\frac{|\differential Y|^{2}}{\differential N}\right).

(B.53)

Equivalently, writing the complex record in terms of two real records,

\differential Y=\frac{\differential Y_{1}+i\,\differential Y_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\differential^{2}Y=\frac{1}{2}\,\differential Y_{1}\,\differential Y_{2},

(B.54)

the ostensible vacuum distribution is

p_{0}(\differential Y_{1},\differential Y_{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi\differential N}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\differential Y_{1}^{2}+\differential Y_{2}^{2}}{2\differential N}\right).

(B.55)

Thus the coherent-state POVM produces two real Gaussian readouts of equal variance
\differential N
. These two readouts are the two noisy components of a single complex decoupled-mode record.

Contracting
eq.

B.52
with the pre-trace block
eq.

B.6
gives the heterodyne instrument

\mathcal{I}_{\rm het}(\differential^{2}Y)(\hat{\rho})=\hat{M}_{\rm het}(\differential Y)\hat{\rho}\hat{M}_{\rm het}^{\dagger}(\differential Y)\,\differential^{2}Y,

(B.56)

where

\hat{M}_{\rm het}(\differential Y)=p_{0}(\differential^{2}Y)^{1/2}\left[\hat{\mathds{1}}-\left(i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{L}^{\dagger}\hat{L}\right)\differential N+\hat{L}\,\differential Y^{*}\right]+O(\differential N^{3/2}).

(B.57)

The physical complex record is

\differential Y=\expectationvalue{\hat L}_{c}\differential N+\differential\zeta,\qquad\differential\zeta=\frac{\differential W_{1}+i\,\differential W_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},

(B.58)

with Itô products

\differential\zeta^{2}=0,\qquad\differential\zeta\,\differential\zeta^{*}=\differential N,\qquad\differential W_{i}\,\differential W_{j}=\delta_{ij}\,\differential N.

(B.59)

In real form this is

\displaystyle\differential Y_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{Re}\expectationvalue{\hat L}_{c}\,\differential N+\differential W_{1},

(B.60)

\displaystyle\differential Y_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{Im}\expectationvalue{\hat L}_{c}\,\differential N+\differential W_{2}.

(B.61)

The two real records therefore monitor the two real components of the same complex decoupled-mode displacement. In contrast with homodyne detection, there is no selected phase
\chi
; a phase rotation of
\hat{L}
simply rotates the pair
(\differential Y_{1},\differential Y_{2})
.

The normalised conditional state obeys

\displaystyle\differential\hat{\rho}_{c}={}

\displaystyle-i[\hat{K}_{\rm eff},\hat{\rho}_{c}]\differential N+\mathcal{D}[\hat{L}]\hat{\rho}_{c}\,\differential N

\displaystyle+\left(\hat{L}\hat{\rho}_{c}-\expectationvalue{\hat L}_{c}\hat{\rho}_{c}\right)\differential\zeta^{*}+\left(\hat{\rho}_{c}\hat{L}^{\dagger}-\expectationvalue{\hat L^\dagger}_{c}\hat{\rho}_{c}\right)\differential\zeta.

(B.62)

Equivalently, for pure states,

d\ket{\psi_c}=\left(-i\hat{K}_{\rm eff}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{L}^{\dagger}\hat{L}+\expectationvalue*{\hat L^\dagger}_{c}\hat{L}-\frac{1}{2}\expectationvalue*{\hat L^\dagger}_{c}\expectationvalue*{\hat L}_{c}\right)\ket{\psi_c}\differential N+\left(\hat{L}-\expectationvalue*{\hat L}_{c}\right)\ket{\psi_c}\differential\zeta^{*}.

(B.63)

This instrument reads both real components of the same off-diagonal decoupled-mode coherence. In terms of the explicit stationary jump operator
eq.

A.109
, the complex record is

\differential Y=\expectationvalue{\hat L}_{c}\differential N+\differential\zeta.

(B.64)

Thus the coherent-state POVM records the full complex displacement of the decoupled mode. The real and imaginary parts of this displacement are the two classical readout channels.

B.3.1
Gaussian dynamics for the heterodyne readout

For a quadratic Hamiltonian and a jump operator linear in
(\hat{Q},\hat{P})
, Gaussian conditional states remain Gaussian. Let

W(z,N)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\det\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}(N)}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left[z-Z_{c}(N)\right]^{\top}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}^{-1}(N)\left[z-Z_{c}(N)\right]\right\},

(B.65)

where

z=(Q,P)^{\top},\qquad Z_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\expectationvalue*{\hat Q}\\
\expectationvalue*{\hat P}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta_{QQ}&\Delta_{QP}\\
\Delta_{QP}&\Delta_{PP}\end{pmatrix}.

(B.66)

Write the linear jump operator as

\hat{L}=\ell^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{Q}\\
\hat{P}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\ell=\begin{pmatrix}\ell_{Q}\\
\ell_{P}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(B.67)

For the two-component readout,

dZ_{c}=AZ_{c}\,\differential N+n_{1}\differential W_{1}+n_{2}\differential W_{2},

(B.68)

where

n_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}\,\mathrm{Re}\ell-\Omega\,\mathrm{Im}\ell\right),\qquad n_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(2\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}\,\mathrm{Im}\ell+\Omega\,\mathrm{Re}\ell\right).

(B.69)

The deterministic drift matrix is

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&\dfrac{1}{H\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}\\[6.0pt]
-\dfrac{m^{2}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm phys}}{H}&-3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\dfrac{H^{2}\sigma^{3}}{6\pi^{2}}\\[6.0pt]
-\dfrac{6\pi^{2}m^{2}}{H^{4}\sigma^{3}}&-3\end{pmatrix}.

(B.70)

The conditional covariance obeys the Riccati equation

\frac{d\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}}{\differential N}=A\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}+\mathbf{\Sigma}_{c}A^{\top}+D-n_{1}n_{1}^{\top}-n_{2}n_{2}^{\top},

(B.71)

with

D=\Omega\,\mathrm{Re}(\ell\ell^{\dagger})\,\Omega^{\top}.

(B.72)

For the massless operator
eq.

A.109
,

D=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{H^{2}(1+\sigma^{2})}{4\pi^{2}}&-\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}\\[8.0pt]
-\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}&\dfrac{9\pi^{2}}{H^{2}\sigma^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(B.73)

The corresponding two real noise amplitudes are

\displaystyle n_{Q}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{H}{2\sqrt{2}\,\pi}\left(1+2\sigma\Delta_{QP}\right),

\displaystyle n_{P}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\frac{3\pi}{H\sigma}+\frac{H\sigma}{\pi}\Delta_{PP}\right),

\displaystyle n_{Q}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{H\sigma}{2\pi}-\frac{H}{\pi}\Delta_{QP}-\frac{6\pi}{H\sigma}\Delta_{QQ}\right),

\displaystyle n_{P}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(-\frac{H}{\pi}\Delta_{PP}-\frac{6\pi}{H\sigma}\Delta_{QP}\right).

(B.74)

A phase rotation of the massless jump operator rotates the pair
(\differential W_{1},\differential W_{2})
and leaves the field-centre diffusion invariant:

D_{QQ}^{\rm centres}=(n_{1})_{Q}^{2}+(n_{2})_{Q}^{2}.

(B.75)

On the stabilising branch of
eq.

B.71
,

D_{QQ}^{\rm centres}=\frac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}+O(\sigma),

(B.76)

which is the Starobinsky amplitude squared.

Appendix C
Rank-One Boundary Noise and the Singular
Q,P
Kossakowski Matrix

The single boundary channel may be written after choosing the irrelevant overall phase of the jump operator so that
\hat{L}=\ell_{Q}\hat{Q}+\ell_{P}\hat{P}
,

\hat{L}=\ell_{Q}\hat{Q}+\ell_{P}\hat{P},\qquad\ell_{Q}=-\sqrt{3\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}\,\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*},\qquad\ell_{P}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}}\,\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}.

(C.1)

In the operator basis
\hat{F}_{1}=\hat{Q}
,
\hat{F}_{2}=\hat{P}
, the GKLS equation
eq.

3.23
can be written as

\derivative{N}\hat{\rho}=-i[\hat{K}_{\rm eff},\hat{\rho}]+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\bm{\Xi}_{ij}\left(\hat{F}_{i}\hat{\rho}\hat{F}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{F}_{j}\hat{F}_{i},\hat{\rho}\}\right),

(C.2)

where

\bm{\Xi}=\ell\ell^{\dagger},\qquad\ell=\begin{pmatrix}\ell_{Q}\\
\ell_{P}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.3)

Thus

\bm{\Xi}=3\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}|\pi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}&-\pi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\phi_{k_{\sigma}}\\[4.0pt]
-\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}}&|\phi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}/\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.4)

This is the Kossakowski matrix in the operator basis
(\hat{Q},\hat{P})
, and it should not be identified directly with the diffusion matrix appearing in the Wigner equation. The latter is obtained from the real symmetric part of
\bm{\Xi}
with the canonical symplectic rotation

\bm{D}=\Omega\,{\rm Re}\,\bm{\Xi}\,\Omega^{\top},\qquad\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(C.5)

Equivalently, in the
(Q,P)
ordering,

\bm{D}=3\begin{pmatrix}|\phi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}/\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}&\real(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}})\\[4.0pt]
\real(\phi_{k_{\sigma}}^{*}\pi_{k_{\sigma}})&\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}|\pi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.6)

Thus the rank-one statement belongs to the Hermitian operator-space Kossakowski matrix, while the Fokker-Planck diffusion matrix is the real canonical phase-space diffusion tensor obtained from it. The Kossakowski matrix in
eq.

C.4
is positive and rank one. Its determinant vanishes identically,

\det\bm{\Xi}=0,

(C.7)

and its non-zero eigenvalue is

\lambda=\mathrm{Tr}\bm{\Xi}=3\left(\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}|\pi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}+\frac{|\phi_{k_{\sigma}}|^{2}}{\mathtt{vol}_{\rm com}}\right).

(C.8)

Diagonalising
\bm{\Xi}
therefore recovers the same single Lindblad operator. The second eigenvalue vanishes and gives no independent channel. In the massless limit the Kossakowski matrix is

\bm{\Xi}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{9\pi^{2}}{\sigma^{2}H^{2}}&\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}(1-i\sigma)\\[8.0pt]
\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}(1+i\sigma)&\dfrac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}(1+\sigma^{2})\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det\bm{\Xi}=0.

(C.9)

The entries in
eq.

C.9
are therefore perfectly correlated at the operator-space level. Applying
eq.

C.5
gives

\bm{D}^{m=0}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{H^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}(1+\sigma^{2})&-\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}\\[8.0pt]
-\dfrac{3}{2\sigma}&\dfrac{9\pi^{2}}{\sigma^{2}H^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

(C.10)

which is the diffusion matrix in
eq.

B.73
. The swap of the diagonal entries and the sign of the mixed entry are consequences of the symplectic rotation between the operator Kossakowski form and the Wigner diffusion tensor.
