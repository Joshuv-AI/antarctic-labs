The BEG model at the FAD triple point on the square lattice
Estevão F. Borel, Aldo Procacci, Rémy Sanchis, Benedetto Scoppola

Abstract
In this note, we prove that the two-dimensional Blume-Emery-Griffiths model at the triple point Ferromagnetic-Antiquadrupolar-Disordered (FAD) has a unique Gibbs measure at any temperature, thereby establishing the absence of phase transitions. The FAD point lies at the intersection of lines separating three regions of the phase diagram, and it is a singular point where the model exhibits infinitely many ground states. Our proof is based on a random-cluster type representation with configuration-dependent cluster weights and a coupling with Bernoulli site percolation with parameter 1 / 2 1/2 .



Introduction

The Blume-Emery-Griffiths model
[
1
]
defines a class of spin models in the
d
-dimensional unit cubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
depending on two real parameters
X
and
Y
with formal Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H(\sigma)=-\sum_{x\sim y}(\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}+{\rm Y}\sigma_{x}^{2}\sigma_{y}^{2}+{\rm X}(\sigma_{x}^{2}+\sigma_{y}^{2})),

(1)

where by the notation
x\sim y
we mean that
\{x,y\}
is an unordered pair of nearest neighbors in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and
\sigma_{x}\in\{-1,0,+1\}
. It was introduced in 1971 with the initial motivation to investigate
phase separation and
superfluidity in mixtures of liquid
3
He and
4
He and has since been used in many other applications (see e.g.
[
12
]
and references there). The parameter space
X-Y
is divided into three regions (according to the ground states). Namely, the disordered region
D=\{({\rm X},{\rm Y}):1+2{\rm X}+{\rm Y}<0\mbox{ and }{\rm X}<0\}
where the only ground state is
\sigma_{x}=0
everywhere, the ferromagnetic region
F=\{({\rm X},{\rm Y}):1+2{\rm X}+{\rm Y}>0\mbox{ and }1+{\rm X}+{\rm Y}>0\}
with two ground states,
\sigma_{x}=+1
everywhere and
\sigma_{x}=-1
everywhere, and the antiquadrupolar region
A=\{({\rm X},{\rm Y}):1+{\rm X}+{\rm Y}<0\mbox{ and }{\rm X}>0\}
where the model exhibits an infinite number of ground states organized into two equivalent classes (the first class is such that
\sigma_{x}=0
for all
x
in the even sublattice and

\sigma_{x}\neq 0
for all
x
in the odd sublattice, and the second class is the opposite). These three regions are separated by three lines, namely the line

DF=\{({\rm X},{\rm Y})\,:\,1+2{\rm X}+{\rm Y}=0\mbox{ and }{\rm X}<0\}
(disordered-ferromagnetic line), the line

AF=\{({\rm X},{\rm Y})\,:\,{\rm X}+{\rm Y}+1=0\mbox{ and }{\rm X}>0\}
(antiquadrupolar-ferromagnetic line), and

AD=\{(0,{\rm Y})\,:\,{\rm Y}<-1\}
(antiquadrupolar-disordered line), which meet at the ferromagnetic-antiquadrupolar-disordered (
FAD
) point
\{(0,-1)\}
.

In the regions
F
and
D
and at the interface
DF
, the low-temperature regime of the model can be described by the usual Pirogov-Sinai theory
[
15
,
4
]
while at any point of the
X-Y
plane the high temperature regime can be described by standard methods such as polymer expansion
[
11
]
and Dobrushin uniqueness criterion
[
9
]
. In the region
A
the extension of the Pirogov-Sinai theory given in
[
5
]
can be used to describe the low temperature regime of the model.

Things become considerably more delicate along the AF and AD lines and at the FAD point, where the model exhibits an infinite number of ground states that cannot be organized into a finite number of equivalence classes. In particular, the residual entropy of the BEG model at the FAD point is higher than at any other point along the AD and AF lines (see
[
2
,
10
]
).
As a consequence of these features, despite the enormous amount of work and the large number of results available in the literature — most of which have been obtained using mean-field theory, Monte Carlo simulations, and renormalization group techniques — rigorous results for these interfaces remain scarce.

In this article we consider the BEG model on the two-dimensional unit square lattice at the
FAD
point.
At zero temperature, this model coincides with the Widom-Rowlinson model
[
7
]
(with activity
\lambda=1
) for which uniqueness of the Gibbs measure has been established by Higuchi
for a range of activities which includes the value
\lambda=1

[
6
]
. An alternative proof of the uniqueness of the (uniform) Gibbs measure
of this model at zero temperature has recently been given in
[
12
]
via a coupling of the Gibbs
measure with + boundary condition with the Bernoulli site percolation process at parameter 1/2. To our knowledge, no rigorous analysis has been available in the literature for the BEG model at the FAD point at non-zero temperatures, so that the possible presence of a phase transition at some positive
\beta
cannot, in principle, be ruled out. We stress that rigorous proofs of the existence of phase transitions along the AD line and (possibly) at the FAD point are available only for sufficiently large dimensions
d
(see
[
13
]
and references therein).

The main result of this note is to prove that the
mean value of the spin at the origin with
+
or
-
boundary conditions vanishes in the thermodynamic limit at any non-zero temperature.
Since the BEG model at the FAD point falls into the class of models considered by Lebowitz and Monroe
[
8
]
, for which FKG inequalities hold (see Theorem 1 in
[
8
]
), the equality of local magnetizations with
+
and
-
boundary conditions in the thermodynamic limit implies the independence of any correlation function from the boundary conditions — and thus the uniqueness of the Gibbs state at all temperatures. The (standard) proof of this implication for the BEG model at the FAD point can be carried out along the same lines as for the ferromagnetic Ising spin-1/2 model (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
).

Our result is obtained via a coupling between a random cluster model, encoding the dependence on the temperature, and the zero-temperature BEG model at FAD. We were inspired by a similar coupling described in
[
3
]
and we also made use of results at the zero-temperature case obtained in
[
12
]
(in particular Lemma 1 and its consequence there).

1
The Model and Results

Let
L
be an integer and let
\Lambda
be a square in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of size
2L+1
centered at the origin
o
of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. We denote by
\partial_{i}\Lambda
the internal boundary of
\Lambda
, by
\partial_{e}\Lambda
the external boundary of
\Lambda
and we set
\bar{\Lambda}=\Lambda\cup\partial_{e}\Lambda
.
The notation
\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\infty}
(i.e. the thermodynamic limit) means here
\lim_{L\to\infty}
.
At each site
x\in\Lambda
we place a spin
\sigma_{x}
taking values in the set
\{-1,0,+1\}
and
we denote by
\Sigma_{\Lambda}=\{-1,0,+1\}^{\Lambda}
the set of all possible spin configurations in
\Lambda
. A boundary condition for the model is determined by a fixed configuration
\xi\in\Sigma_{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
with the understanding that in the thermodynamic limit, as
\Lambda
invades
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the sites of
\xi
entering
\Lambda
are disregarded and those in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\Lambda
are kept. At the FAD point the Hamiltonian of the BEG model in the volume
\Lambda
with boundary condition
\xi
is

H_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\sigma)=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x,y\}\subset\Lambda\\
|x-y|=1\end{subarray}}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}(1-\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y})-\sum_{x\in\partial_{i}\Lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\partial_{e}\Lambda\\
|x-y|=1\end{subarray}}(\xi_{y}\sigma_{x}-\xi_{y}^{2}\sigma_{x}^{2}).

(2)

In particular “
+
” is the configuration such that
\xi_{x}=+1
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and
“
-
” is the configuration such that
\xi_{x}=-1
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

The Gibbs measure
\mu_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}(\cdot)
of the BEG model in the volume
\Lambda
at the FAD point at inverse temperature
\beta
with
\xi
boundary conditions, is given by

\mu_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}(\cdot)=\frac{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}(\cdot)e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\sigma)}}{Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta)}

where

Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\sigma)}.

is the partition function. The mean value of the spin at any fixed site
x\in\Lambda
under the probability measure
\mu_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}(\cdot)
is given by

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}=\frac{\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\sigma_{x}e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\sigma)}}{Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta)}.

The thermodynamic limit of
\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}
, if it exists, will be denoted hereafter by
\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{\xi}_{\beta}
, i.e.

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{\xi}_{\beta}=\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\infty}\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{\xi}.

(3)

We can now state our main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For the BEG model on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
at the FAD point and at any inverse temperature
\beta\in[0,+\infty]
we have that

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{+}_{\beta}=\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{-}_{\beta}=0.

(4)

As noted in the introduction, the validity of the FKG inequalities for this model, combined with Theorem 1, implies uniqueness of the infinite-volume Gibbs measure and hence rules out the existence of a phase transition.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we develop a graphical representation of the BEG model at the FAD point. Our construction generalizes the standard Random Cluster representation (FK) used for the Potts model (as discussed, for instance, in
[
3
]
). We begin by constructing this representation in Section
2.1
, then use it in Section
2
to relate the magnetization to a connectivity event, and we conclude the proof of Theorem
1
in Section
2.2
.

2.1
Random Cluster Representation

As usual we look at
\Lambda
(
\bar{\Lambda}
) as a vertex set of a graph
\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}
(
\mathbb{G}_{\bar{\Lambda}}
) whose edges are the neighbor pairs.
So a set
U\subset\Lambda
is connected if the induced graph
\mathbb{G}|_{U}
is connected. If
U\subset\Lambda
is connected, we denote by
\mathbb{E}_{U}

the set of all edges of
\mathbb{G}|_{U}
and we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}
the set of all connected sets in
\Lambda
.
Now, given a configuration
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}
let
\Lambda^{+}(\sigma)=\{x\in\Lambda:\sigma_{x}=+1\}
and let

\Lambda^{\pm}(\sigma)=\{x\in\Lambda:\sigma_{x}\neq 0\}
.
Let

C_{o}^{+}(\sigma)
be the connected component of
\Lambda^{+}(\sigma)
containing the origin
o
(
C_{o}^{+}(\sigma)
is empty if
\sigma_{o}\neq+1
)
and let
C_{o}^{\pm}(\sigma)
be the connected component of
\Lambda^{\pm}(\sigma)
containing the origin
o
(
C_{o}^{\pm}(\sigma)
is empty if
\sigma_{o}=0
). From now on we will set for brevity
\mathbb{E}_{\Lambda}=E^{\circ}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\bar{\Lambda}}=E
.

Let
e=\{x,y\}
. If both
x,y\in\Lambda
, set
\sigma_{e}=\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}
; if
x\in\Lambda
and
y\in\partial_{e}\Lambda
, set
\sigma_{e}=\sigma_{x}\xi_{y}
. Therefore, we can write the Hamiltonian, for any configuration
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}
and any fixed boundary condition
\xi
, as

H^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\sigma)=\sum_{e\in E}[(\sigma_{e})^{2}-\sigma_{e}]=2\sum_{e\in E}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}=-1\}}.

Note that, using the identity

e^{-2\beta\mathbbm{1}_{\sigma_{e}=-1}}=e^{-2\beta}+(1-e^{-2\beta})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}},

and setting
p=(1-e^{-2\beta})
, the partition function
Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta)
can be rewritten as:

\begin{split}Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta)&=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\prod_{\{x,y\}\in E}[(1-p)+p\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}].\end{split}

(5)

We introduce a graphical representation by considering the space of bond configurations
\Omega_{E}=\{0,1\}^{E}
.
Given
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
,
let

E_{\omega}=\{e\in E:~\omega_{e}=1\}
and
\bar{\Lambda}_{\omega}=\{x\in\bar{\Lambda}:x\in e\;\mbox{for some $e\in E_{\omega}$}\}
. We set

\mathbb{G}_{\omega}=(\bar{\Lambda},E_{\omega})
. We also set
\partial\Lambda_{e}^{\omega}=\{x\in\partial\Lambda_{e}:~\mbox{$x\in e$ for some $e\in E_{\omega}$}\}
.

For a fixed boundary condition
\xi
, we then define a coupling on the product space
\Sigma_{\Lambda}\times\Omega_{E}
via a compatibility relation. We say a spin configuration
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}
and a bond configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
are compatible, and write
\sigma\overset{{}_{\xi}}{\sim}\omega
, if for every edge
e=\{x,y\}\in E
:

\omega_{e}=1\implies\sigma_{e}\neq-1.

We then define the joint probability measure
P^{\xi}_{p}
on
\Sigma_{\Lambda}\times\Omega_{E}
as:

P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega):=\frac{1}{\Xi^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(p)}\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}].

(6)

Where
\Xi^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(p)
is the natural normalizing constant. However, notice that

\begin{split}\Xi^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(p)&=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\prod_{e\in E}\Big[\sum_{\omega_{e}\in\{0,1\}}[(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]\Big]\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)+p\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]\\
&=Z_{\Lambda}^{\xi}(\beta).\end{split}

(7)

Remark 1
.

Note that if
(\sigma,\omega)\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}\times\Omega_{E}
is not compatible then
P_{p}^{\xi}(\sigma,\omega)=0
. Indeed, if
(\sigma,\omega)
is not compatible, then there is at least an edge
e\in E
such that
\omega_{e}=1
and
\sigma_{e}=-1
. But for such an edge

(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}=0.

Summing
P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega)
over
\sigma
yields the marginal distribution on
\Omega_{E}
, denoted by
\phi^{\xi}_{p}
. To calculate
\phi^{\xi}_{p}(\omega)
, observe that

\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]=(1-p)^{|E|-|\omega|}p^{|\omega|}\prod_{e\in E_{\omega}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}},

where
|\omega|
denotes the number of open edges of the configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
(i.e. those edges
e\in E
such that
\omega_{e}=1
). Hence

\phi^{\xi}_{p}(\omega)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega)=\frac{1}{Z^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\beta)}p^{|\omega|}(1-p)^{|E|-|\omega|}W^{\xi}(\omega),

(8)

where

W^{\xi}(\omega)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\prod_{e\in E_{\omega}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma\overset{{}_{\xi}}{\sim}\omega\}}.

To understand what the factor
W^{\xi}(\omega)
represents, let us introduce some notation. Given a configuration

\omega\in\Omega_{E}
and a boundary condition
\xi
on
\partial_{e}\Lambda
, let
\xi^{\omega}
be defined as follows.

\xi^{\omega}_{x}=\begin{cases}\xi_{x}&\mbox{if $x\in\partial\Lambda_{e}^{\omega}$}\\
0&{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}.

(9)

Let now
E^{\circ}_{\omega}\subset E_{\omega}
be such that
E^{\circ}_{\omega}=\{e\in E^{\circ}:~\omega_{e}=1\}
and let
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{\omega}=(\Lambda,E^{\circ}_{\omega})
(recall that
E^{\circ}
is the set of neighbor pairs in
\Lambda
while
E
is the set of neighbor pairs in
\Lambda\cup\partial_{e}\Lambda
).
Then

W^{\xi}(\omega)=Z^{\xi}_{\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{\omega}}(\beta=+\infty)

where
Z^{\xi}_{G^{\circ}_{\omega}}(\beta=+\infty)
is the partition function of the zero-temperature BEG model at
FAD on the graph
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{\omega}
with
\xi^{\omega}
boundary conditions.

Now, given
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
we define the (conditional) measure
P^{\xi}(\sigma\mid\omega)
on
\Sigma_{\Lambda}
in a natural way as follows.

P^{\xi}(\sigma\mid\omega)=\frac{P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega)}{\phi^{\xi}_{p}(\omega)}=\frac{\prod_{e\in E_{\omega}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}}{W^{\xi}(\omega)}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma\overset{{}_{\xi}}{\sim}\omega\}}}{W^{\xi}(\omega)}.

(10)

Finally, since

\begin{split}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega)&=\frac{1}{Z^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\beta)}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)\delta_{\omega_{e},0}+p\delta_{\omega_{e},1}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]\\
&=\frac{1}{Z^{\xi}_{\Lambda}(\beta)}\prod_{e\in E}[(1-p)+p\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma_{e}\neq-1\}}]=\mu^{\xi}_{\Lambda,\beta}(\sigma),\end{split}

for any function
g:\Sigma_{\Lambda}\to\mathbb{R}
we have the decomposition:

\begin{split}\langle g\rangle^{\xi}_{\Lambda,\beta}&=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}g(\sigma)\mu^{\xi}_{\Lambda,\beta}(\sigma)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}g(\sigma)P^{\xi}_{p}(\sigma,\omega)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}g(\sigma)P^{\xi}(\sigma\mid\omega)\phi^{\xi}_{p}(\omega)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\phi^{\xi}_{p}(\omega)\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}}g(\sigma)\Bigg\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{\sigma\sim\omega\}}}{W^{\xi}(\omega)}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(11)

I.e. in the end we get

\langle g\rangle^{\xi}_{\Lambda,\beta}=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{\xi}_{p}}[\langle g(\sigma)\rangle_{\mathbb{G}_{\omega},\beta=+\infty}^{\xi}].

(12)

Identity (
12
) shows clearly the utility of this coupling which allows us to separate the thermal and geometric effects. In the r.h.s. of (
12
), the measure
\phi^{\xi}_{p}
encodes the temperature dependence via the parameter
p=(1-e^{-2\beta})
, while, for fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
,
\langle g(\sigma)\rangle_{\mathbb{G}_{\omega},\beta=+\infty}^{\xi}
is the mean value of
g(\sigma)
for the zero-temperature BEG model at FAD on the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
with
\xi^{\omega}
boundary conditions.

Magnetization-Connectivity Identity

We now apply identity (
12
) to express the magnetization at a given site in terms of a connectivity property under the zero-temperature measure. To this end let us introduce some notation. For a fixed bond configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{E}
with boundary condition
\xi
, let
\bar{\mu}^{\xi}_{\omega}
denote the uniform measure on the set of spin configurations
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}
compatible with
\omega
. In other words,
\bar{\mu}^{\xi}_{\omega}
is the Gibbs measure
of the zero-temperature BEG model at FAD on the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
with
\xi^{\omega}
boundary conditions.
Moreover, for a given
x\in\Lambda
,
let
C_{x}(\omega)\subset\Lambda
denote the vertex set of the connected component of the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
containing
x
. Then we have that
\Omega_{E}=\Omega_{E}^{s}\uplus\Omega^{c}_{E}
where
\uplus
denotes the disjoint union and
\Omega_{E}^{s}=\{\omega\in\Omega_{E}:~C_{x}(\omega)\cap\partial_{e}\Lambda=\emptyset\}
and
\Omega_{E}^{c}=\{\omega\in\Omega_{E}:~C_{x}(\omega)\cap\partial_{e}\Lambda\neq\emptyset\}
for each
x\in\Lambda
.

Let us fix a configuration
\omega
and let us choose the boundary condition
+
and a vertex
x\in\Lambda
. Then we define the positive connectivity event

\{x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda\}

(event in
\Sigma_{\Lambda}
) as the set of spin configurations such that there is a path
p
in the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
with vertex set
V_{p}\subset\bar{\Lambda}
connecting the vertex
x
to some vertex of the boundary
\partial_{e}\Lambda
with
\sigma_{y}=+1
for all
y\in V_{p}
.

Remark 2
.

Note that when
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}
(i.e when
C_{x}(\omega)
does not touch the boundary) then, by definition
\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda)=0
. This is despite the fact that for some configurations
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}
compatible with
\omega
there may well be a path starting at
x
and ending at a vertex
y\in\partial_{e}\Lambda
all made by
+
spins. However, since
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}
, such a path is not a path in
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
and thus
such configurations do not belong to the set

\{x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda\}
. In other words, the set
\{x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda\}

is empty when
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}
.

Theorem 2

(Magnetization-Connectivity Identity)
.

For the BEG model at the FAD point, the finite-temperature magnetization at site
x
under positive boundary conditions satisfies:

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{+}=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{+}_{p}}\Big[\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda)\Big].

(13)

Proof.

Using the decomposition established in (
11
) and (
12
), we have initially:

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{+}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{E}}\frac{1}{W^{+}(\omega)}\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}.

Case I:
when
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}
.

Consider a configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}
(i.e. the cluster
C_{x}(\omega)
does not reach the boundary). In this scenario, the spins belonging to
C_{x}(\omega)
are decoupled from the boundary.
Let
\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega
and consider a transformation
\sigma\to\sigma^{\prime}
that flips all spins inside
C_{x}(\omega)
while leaving unchanged all spins in
\Lambda\setminus C_{x}(\omega)
: namely,
\sigma^{\prime}_{u}=-\sigma_{u}
if
u\in C_{x}(\omega)
, and
\sigma^{\prime}_{u}=\sigma_{u}
otherwise. Clearly we have that
\sigma^{\prime}\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega
. By symmetry, the contributions of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
in the sum
\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}
cancel out, yielding

\frac{1}{W^{+}(\omega)}\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}=0.

Case II:
when
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{c}
.

Let now assume that
\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{c}
. Since the boundary condition is fixed to
+
, in a configuration
\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega
, any path of non-zero spins in
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
starting at some vertex of
\partial_{e}\Lambda
must consist entirely of
+1
spins. We decompose the sum
\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}
as follows:

\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega\\
\sigma_{x}=+1\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega\\
\sigma_{x}=-1\end{subarray}}1.

Set for brevity

\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,\pm}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}:\sigma_{x}=\pm 1,\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega\}
. Observe that the set
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}
can be split into two disjoint events.

1.

E_{c}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}:\mbox{ a path in $C_{x}(\omega)$ of $+$ spins connects $x$ to the boundary $\partial_{e}\Lambda$}\}
.

2.

E_{s}=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}:\mbox{ no path in $C_{x}(\omega)$ of $+$ spins containing $x$ reaches the boundary $\partial_{e}\Lambda$}\}.

Note that if
\sigma\in E_{s}
then the vertex
x
has spin
+1
but is separated from the boundary by zeros within
C_{x}(\omega)
.

Similarly, if
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,-}
, and thus
\sigma_{x}=-1
, no path in
C_{x}(\omega)
of spins
-1
containing
x
reaches the boundary
\partial_{e}\Lambda
. In other words
x
resides in a finite component of
-
spins surrounded by zeros inside
C_{x}(\omega)
(even though
C_{x}(\omega)
itself touches the boundary).

Let
K_{x}(\sigma)\subset C_{x}(\omega)
be the connected component of non-zero spins containing
x
.
If
\sigma\in E_{s}
or if
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,-}
, then
K_{x}(\sigma)
does not touch
\partial_{e}\Lambda
and is bounded by zeros inside
C_{x}(\omega)
. By the same spin-flip symmetry argument used in Case I, there is a bijection between configurations in
E_{s}
(where
K_{x}(\sigma)
is all
+
) and configurations in
\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,-}
(where
K_{x}(\sigma)
is all
-
).

Consequently, their contributions to the magnetization cancel out:

\sum_{\sigma\in E_{s}}1-\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,-}}1=0.

Hence we get

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}}\sigma_{x}=\sum_{\sigma\in E_{c}}1=|\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}:x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda\}|.

Dividing by
W^{+}(\omega)
, we conclude:

\frac{1}{W^{+}(\omega)}\sum_{\sigma\overset{{}_{+}}{\sim}\omega}\sigma_{x}~=~\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{|\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\Lambda}^{\omega,+}:x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda\}|}{W^{+}(\omega)}}&\mbox{if $\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{c}$}\\
\\
0&\mbox{if $\omega\in\Omega_{E}^{s}$}\end{cases}~=~\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda).

(14)

∎

2.2
Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
1
: the vanishing of the magnetization

We now have all the ingredients needed to conclude the proof of Theorem
1
. The results given in
[
12
]
imply that

\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda)\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\omega}}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda),

(15)

where
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\omega}}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)
is the probability of the event that
x
is connected to the boundary
\partial_{i}\Lambda

through a path of open sites inside the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
in the Bernoulli site percolation on the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
.
Indeed, as discussed in Section 2.2 of
[
12
]
, the inequality (
15
) can be proven via a coupling of two Markov chains, one on the zero-temperature BEG model at FAD on the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\omega}
and the other on the Bernoulli site percolation on the same graph.

Now, since clearly

{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\omega}}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{\Lambda}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)

we get that

\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda)\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{\Lambda}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)

and thus

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{+}=\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{+}_{p}}[\bar{\mu}^{+}_{\omega}(x\xleftrightarrow[\omega]{+}\partial\Lambda)]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\phi^{+}_{p}}[{\mathbb{P}}_{\Lambda}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)]={\mathbb{P}}_{\Lambda}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)

and hence

0\leq\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\infty}\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{+}\leq\lim_{\Lambda\uparrow\infty}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Lambda}^{1/2}(x\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda)=0.

Conclusions

We introduced a random-cluster type representation for the BEG model at the FAD point and used it to establish the absence of phase transition on the square lattice. We remark that our argument applies beyond the square lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. This result also extends to any infinite graph
G
for which the critical parameter for Bernoulli site percolation satisfies
p_{c}(site,G)>1/2
, since the stochastic domination by a Bernoulli site percolation with parameter
1/2
holds independently of the graph structure.

For
\mathbb{{Z}}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
, the situation is fundamentally different. Since
p_{c}(site,\mathbb{Z}^{d})<1/2
for all
d\geq 3
, the upper bound provided by Bernoulli site percolation at parameter
1/2
becomes supercritical and no longer forces the magnetization to vanish. In this regime, Monte Carlo simulations
[
12
]
suggest that the zero-temperature magnetization at
d=3
is strictly positive, and it is expected that a non-trivial critical inverse temperature
\beta_{c}\in(0,\infty)
should exist.

The non-uniqueness of Gibbs measures at zero temperature has only been proved when
d
is sufficiently high
[
13
]
. Proving this for intermediate dimensions, however, remains a challenging open problem. A natural approach would be to establish a stochastic domination from below by a supercritical Bernoulli percolation process. However, classical local-comparison tools such as Holley’s inequality are not useful in this direction at the zero-temperature setting. Another important factor is the well-known absence of monotonicity properties for hard-core models
[
14
]
. New ideas seem to be required, and a deeper understanding of the properties of the measure
\phi_{p}
and the zero-temperature system in higher dimensions could be a promising direction to understand the model at positive temperatures.
