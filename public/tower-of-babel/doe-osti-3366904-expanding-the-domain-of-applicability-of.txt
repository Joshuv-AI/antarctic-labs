Project Overview
Accurate machine learning models for predicting small molecule interactions with
biological targets are essential for therapeutic discovery, biothreat response, and
computational drug design, but their performance is often limited for understudied
targets with sparse experimental data. To address this challenge, we developed and
evaluated methods to improve molecular property prediction under low-data conditions,
using the NimA-related kinase (NEK) family as a proof-of-concept. This work focused on
two complementary goals within the ATOM Modeling PipeLine (AMPL) and the
Generative Molecular Design (GMD) loop: expanding model applicability through
transfer learning, representation learning, feature scaling, sampling strategies, and
active-learning-inspired compound selection; and enabling efficient virtual screening to
prioritize compounds that balance predicted activity, design objectives, and synthetic
accessibility.

Mission Impact
We implemented several broadly applicable enhancements in AMPL and assessed
them on curated NEK inhibition datasets through feature engineering and two rounds of
active learning. These improvements included fine-tuning RDKit and Mordred-based
compound representations, introducing pre-fitting options for feature transformations to
better handle chemically diverse out-of-distribution screening sets, and adding sub-
sampling approaches for highly imbalanced classification problems. In parallel, we
generated a virtual library of approximately 1.3 million compounds from 78 precursor
scaffolds and commercially available building blocks using the Allchemy synthesis
planning platform, then screened the library with NEK predictive models to produce hit
lists for collaborator synthesis, purchase, and experimental testing. Together, these
methods and outputs establish a foundation for limited-data molecular machine learning
with applications in NEK-focused drug discovery, biodefense, and broader therapeutic
development.
