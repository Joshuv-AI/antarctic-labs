Spatial immune programs associated with nodal status differ between lymphoid follicular regions and peritumoral tonsillar tissue in HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer
Authors: Naohiro Wakisaka, Makiko Moriyama-Kita, Satoru Kondo, Eiji Kobayashi, Takayoshi Ueno, Yosuke Nakanishi, Kazuhira Endo, Hisashi Sugimoto, Tomokazu Yoshizaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionIn human papillomavirus (HPV)-positive oropharyngeal cancer (OPC), lymph node metastasis is common, yet its biological and prognostic implications remain incompletely understood. Less is known about the spatial organization of immune programs within tumor-associated secondary lymphoid structures.MethodsImmune programs were analyzed by spatially distinguishing lymphoid follicular regions (LFRs) from tumor-free peritumoral tonsillar tissues (PTTs) in palatine tonsil–derived HPV-positive OPC. LFRs were analyzed using spatial transcriptomics and PTTs using bulk transcriptomics. Previously generated GeoMx Digital Spatial Profiler and bulk microarray datasets were reanalyzed to investigate compartment-specific immune programs. Immune pathway activity was assessed using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis according to nodal status.ResultsCompartment-specific immune programs associated with nodal status were identified in both LFRs and PTTs. In LFRs, nodal-positive cases showed enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, and T-cell activity programs accompanied by inflammatory and interferon-associated signaling. In contrast, nodal-negative PTTs exhibited enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, and T-cell activity programs together with IL2–STAT5 and TGF-β signaling, without overt inflammatory activation. These findings indicate that identical immune pathways exhibit distinct biological associations depending on their spatial localization.ConclusionImmune programs associated with nodal metastasis in HPV-positive OPC vary according to spatial immune context. Distinguishing LFRs from tumor-free PTTs provides a framework for interpreting immune programs beyond bulk tissue analysis and highlights the importance of spatial immune organization in shaping biological interpretation. Because the spatial transcriptomic analyses were performed in a limited number of patients, these findings should be considered exploratory and require validation in larger independent cohorts.

Introduction

Human papillomavirus (HPV)–positive oropharyngeal cancer (OPC) represents a distinct clinical entity characterized by frequent regional lymph node metastasis and favorable responsiveness to therapy compared with HPV-negative disease (1). Among oropharyngeal subsites, cancers arising from the palatine tonsil are unique, as the tonsil is a specialized lymphoid organ containing highly organized secondary lymphoid structures that support coordinated adaptive immune responses (2, 3). Despite substantial advances in tumor immunology, the contribution of immune programs within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue to lymphatic metastasis remains incompletely understood.

In palatine tonsil–derived HPV-positive OPC, surgical treatment routinely involves en bloc resection of the entire tonsil containing the primary tumor. This approach enables analysis of the surrounding tumor-free tonsillar tissue, referred to as peritumoral tonsillar tissue (PTTs). Within PTTs, lymphoid follicular regions (LFRs) represent spatially organized immune subcompartments composed of B cell follicles and associated T cell zones (2). These LFRs represent native secondary lymphoid structures of the palatine tonsil rather than tertiary lymphoid structures that arise ectopically in non-lymphoid tissues. Their highly organized architecture provides a specialized microenvironment that supports adaptive immune regulation.

Previous bulk transcriptomic analyses of PTTs from our group have demonstrated that immune gene expression patterns within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue differ according to lymph node status in OPC (4). However, bulk approaches inherently average signals across heterogeneous tissue components and cannot resolve immune programs operating within defined microanatomical niches such as lymphoid follicles. Consequently, the spatial context in which immune programs arise has remained largely unexplored.

Among the T-cell–related immune programs evaluated in the present study, stem-like T cells represent a self-renewing population capable of sustaining long-term immune responses and are considered key mediators of durable antitumor immunity (5). Pre-exhausted T cells represent an intermediate differentiation state that retains proliferative and functional potential despite persistent antigen stimulation. In contrast, terminally exhausted (Tex-like) T cells exhibit reduced proliferative capacity and diminished effector function following chronic antigen exposure (6). Distinguishing these complementary T-cell differentiation states may therefore provide insight into the functional organization of the tumor-associated immune microenvironment. Accordingly, these T-cell programs were included in the present analysis to characterize compartment-specific immune states associated with nodal status.

Recent advances in spatial transcriptomic technologies have enabled spatially resolved analysis of immune programs in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues (7). These approaches provide a technical framework for interrogating immune heterogeneity within complex lymphoid tissues at high spatial resolution. Building on this methodological foundation, spatially informed analyses of tumor-associated tonsillar tissue may reveal immune programs that are not captured by bulk transcriptomic approaches.

Our previous studies independently identified nodal status–associated immune programs in bulk PTTs (4) and spatially defined LFRs (8). Rather than generating new transcriptomic datasets, the present study reanalyzed these previously published datasets within a unified analytical framework using additional computational analyses to investigate compartment-specific immune programs associated with nodal status. By integrating findings from both compartments, we sought to determine whether identical immune pathways exhibit distinct biological associations according to their spatial localization.

In this exploratory study, we investigated compartment-specific immune pathway activity in LFRs and bulk PTTs of palatine tonsil–derived HPV-positive OPC by integrating spatial and bulk transcriptomic analyses. We hypothesized that immune programs associated with nodal status are shaped not only by their molecular identity but also by their spatial immune context, leading to context-dependent biological interpretations.

Materials and methods

Study design and tissue definition

This exploratory, hypothesis-generating study focused exclusively on HPV–positive OPC arising from the palatine tonsil. Surgical treatment involved en bloc resection of the entire palatine tonsil containing the primary tumor.

PTTs were defined as the entire tumor-free palatine tonsil obtained after exclusion of the tumor area. Within PTTs, LFRs were identified as discrete lymphoid structures based on histological features consistent with secondary lymphoid follicles, including germinal center formation and surrounding lymphocyte-rich zones. LFRs were analyzed as spatially defined immune subcompartments embedded within PTTs. Because the palatine tonsil is a physiological secondary lymphoid organ, the LFRs analyzed in this study were regarded as native secondary lymphoid structures rather than tertiary lymphoid structures.

LFRs were characterized by enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, T-cell activity, and B-cell programs, whereas tumor-free PTTs were characterized by enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, T-cell activity, IL2–STAT5 signaling, and TGF-β signaling. Together, these findings support a compartment-specific biological interpretation of immune programs associated with nodal metastasis.

Patient cohort and ethical approval

Patients undergoing primary surgical resection for palatine tonsil–derived HPV-positive OPC were included in this study. Patients were enrolled consecutively between 2012 and 2020. Tumor staging was performed according to the 8th edition of the Union for International Cancer Control TNM classification (9). HPV status was determined by p16 immunohistochemistry and confirmed by HPV DNA testing when available. Patients classified as having lymph node metastasis included those with nodal involvement identified at diagnosis, at the time of surgical pathology, or during clinical follow-up.

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Kanazawa University Hospital (approval number 2016–033) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Spatial transcriptomic data of LFRs

Spatial transcriptomic data for LFRs were derived from a previously published dataset generated using the GeoMx Digital Spatial Profiler (DSP) (NanoString Technologies, Seattle, WA, USA), in which four HPV-positive OPC cases were analyzed, with six representative ROIs within LFRs evaluated for each case. Detailed experimental procedures, including tissue preparation, immunofluorescent staining, region-of-interest (ROI) selection, and data acquisition, have been reported elsewhere (4, 8).

Briefly, ROIs within lymphoid follicular regions (LFRs) were selected based on morphological features and immune marker enrichment while excluding tumor-rich areas. Six representative ROIs were selected within LFRs for GeoMx DSP analysis. Normalized gene expression data were reanalyzed in the present study to assess immune pathway activity according to lymph node status. Representative examples of whole-slide images and selected ROIs are shown in Supplementary Figure 1. For patient-level analyses, enrichment scores from multiple ROIs obtained from the same patient were averaged to generate a single patient-level value prior to statistical comparisons. ROI-level analyses were retained as supplementary exploratory analyses to illustrate spatial heterogeneity within LFRs.

Microarray analysis of PTTs

Microarray data were obtained from our previously published dataset deposited in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (accession number GSE228432) (4). Of the 27 cases included in the dataset, 23 cases meeting the predefined inclusion criteria for HPV-positive OPC were included in the present analysis. Bulk transcriptomic profiling of PTTs was performed using microarray analysis. Detailed experimental procedures have been reported previously (4). Tumor-free tonsillar tissue was macrodissected from FFPE sections to minimize tumor contamination. Total RNA was extracted and hybridized to Affymetrix GeneChip platforms (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to standard protocols.

Raw microarray data were reanalyzed and processed in R (version 4.5.1) using robust multi-array average normalization. Quality control and downstream analyses were performed within the R statistical environment.

Gene set enrichment analysis

Immune pathway activity was evaluated using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). Hallmark gene sets were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MsigDB v8.0) (10). The TEX_LIKE_PROGRAM gene set was derived from Shi et al., 2023 (6). The remaining custom immune-related gene sets were manually curated by the authors from previously published literature and are listed in Supplementary Table 1. Enrichment scores (ES), corresponding to ssGSEA scores, were calculated using the GSVA package in R (method = “ssgsea”), allowing immune pathway activity to be evaluated using a common analytical framework in both spatial and bulk transcriptomic datasets. Data visualization was performed using ggplot2.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical staining was performed on representative tissue sections using antibodies against CD8 (1:50; clone C8/144B, M7103, mouse monoclonal; Dako, Copenhagen, Denmark) and TCF1 (1:200; clone E6O1K, rabbit monoclonal; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) to provide spatial validation of transcriptomic findings, specifically to visualize cytotoxic T cells (CD8) and stem-like T-cell populations (TCF1).

Staining was assessed qualitatively to confirm the spatial localization of CD8-positive and TCF1-positive immune cell populations within LFRs and surrounding PTTs. The immunohistochemical analyses were intended as exploratory qualitative spatial validation of the transcriptomic findings rather than quantitative endpoint analyses. Therefore, quantitative cell counting, including assessment of CD8+TCF1+ double-positive cells, was not performed.

Statistical analysis

For the GeoMx DSP dataset, enrichment scores from multiple ROIs obtained from the same patient were averaged to generate a single patient-level value prior to statistical analysis. Comparisons between nodal-negative and nodal-positive groups were then performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test at the patient level. Because multiple immune pathways were evaluated simultaneously, P values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) method.

ROI-level analyses were intended only as exploratory analyses to illustrate spatial heterogeneity and were not used for statistical inference.

Results

LFRs represent a spatially defined immune subcompartment within PTTs

A total of 23 HPV-positive cases were included in the microarray-based analysis. PTTs were defined as tumor-free palatine tonsillar tissue obtained following en bloc surgical resection. Within PTTs, LFRs were identified as discrete lymphoid structures based on histological features. LFRs were analyzed using previously generated GeoMx DSP spatial transcriptomic data, whereas PTTs were analyzed using previously generated bulk microarray data. Patient-level immune programs in LFRs

Patient-level analysis suggested relatively higher B-cell activity in nodal-negative cases than in nodal-positive cases (Figure 2A; Supplementary Table 2A), although the difference did not reach statistical significance. Given that lymphoid follicles are physiologically characterized by B cell dominance, this finding is consistent with preservation of follicular immune architecture in nodal-negative disease.

In contrast, patient-level analysis suggested a tendency toward enrichment of stem-like T-cell, pre-exhaustion, and T-cell activity programs in nodal-positive cases compared with nodal-negative cases (Figure 2A; Supplementary Table 2A). However, none of these differences reached statistical significance. Tex-like signatures likewise showed no significant difference according to nodal status.

Despite the immune-rich background characteristic of HPV-positive disease, inflammatory response and interferon-γ response gene sets were selectively elevated in nodal-positive LFRs (8). Such inflammatory and interferon-associated activation was more prominent in nodal-positive LFRs than in nodal-negative LFRs. This pattern suggests that inflammatory activation within follicular compartments is not necessarily indicative of effective immune control.

Exploratory ROI-level analyses illustrating spatial heterogeneity within LFRs are presented in Supplementary Figure 2.

In PTTs, T cell fate–related programs—but not B cell activity—are associated with nodal-negative status

Patient-level analysis identified significantly higher enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, and T-cell activity signatures in nodal-negative cases after Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction (Figure 2B; Supplementary Table 2B). In contrast, Tex-like and B-cell activity signatures were not significantly associated with nodal status.

Distinct immune signaling contexts characterize LFRs and PTTs

Patient-level analysis demonstrated significantly higher enrichment of IL2–STAT5 and TGF-β signaling in nodal-negative PTTs than in nodal-positive PTTs (Figure 2C; Supplementary Table 2C). IL2–STAT5 and TGF-β signaling were the principal signaling pathways associated with nodal-negative PTTs. Unlike the inflammatory and interferon-associated programs previously observed in LFRs (8), the principal pathways associated with nodal status in PTTs were IL2–STAT5 and TGF-β signaling. These findings suggest that nodal-negative PTTs are characterized by a distinct immune-related signaling context.

Together with previously reported findings in LFRs (8), these observations support the hypothesis-generating model of compartment-specific biological associations according to spatial localization (Figure 2D).

Spatial support for compartment-specific immune programs

Representative immunohistochemical staining demonstrated the localization of TCF1-positive stem-like T cells and CD8-positive cytotoxic T cells within LFRs embedded in surrounding PTTs (Figure 3). TCF1-positive lymphoid cells were observed in association with lymphoid follicular structures in both nodal-negative and nodal-positive cases, whereas CD8-positive cells were distributed within LFRs and adjacent tonsillar tissue. Representative TCF1-positive lymphoid cells are indicated by arrows. These spatial localization patterns provide anatomical support for the compartment-specific T cell–related immune programs identified by transcriptomic analyses.

Discussion

T-cell fate states exhibit context-dependent functional significance (5). In the present study, we found that immune programs associated with lymph node metastasis in palatine tonsil–derived HPV-positive OPC are shaped by spatial immune context within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue. By explicitly distinguishing LFRs from PTTs, our findings suggest that identical immune pathways exhibit distinct biological associations depending on their anatomical localization.

The present study extends our previously reported spatial transcriptomic analysis (8) by integrating LFR-specific data with bulk transcriptomic profiling of PTTs (4) to enable a unified, compartment-specific interpretation of immune programs. Because the LFR and PTT datasets were generated using different analytical platforms, different patient cohorts, and provide different levels of spatial resolution, our objective was not to directly compare these datasets quantitatively but rather to obtain complementary insights into compartment-specific immune programs associated with nodal status. Accordingly, cohort-related differences may also have contributed to the observed compartment-specific immune programs and should be considered when interpreting the present findings.

The palatine tonsil is a specialized secondary lymphoid organ characterized by highly organized lymphoid follicles that support B cell–dominant immune architecture and coordinated adaptive immune responses (2). A tendency toward preservation of B-cell activity within LFRs was observed in nodal-negative cases, consistent with maintenance of follicular immune organization. In contrast, patient-level analysis suggested reduced B-cell activity together with enrichment of T-cell fate–related programs and inflammatory/interferon-γ–associated signatures in nodal-positive LFRs. Importantly, these observations suggest that immune activation occurred within a compartment physiologically organized around B cell–dominant architecture, rather than supporting sustained T cell–dominant responses. This spatially discordant immune activation may therefore represent dysregulated immune organization rather than effective antitumor immunity. Inflammatory and interferon-γ–associated programs have previously been reported to be enriched in nodal-positive LFRs (8). By contrast, the present analyses suggest that nodal-negative PTTs are characterized by enrichment of immune regulatory programs, including IL2–STAT5 and TGF-β signaling, rather than the inflammatory and interferon-associated programs previously observed in nodal-positive LFRs (8). This spatial restriction underscores that the biological meaning of immune activation cannot be interpreted independently of anatomical context. In secondary lymphoid structures, excessive or spatially dysregulated inflammatory signaling may disrupt immune regulation and compromise coordinated adaptive immune responses.

In contrast, PTTs of nodal-negative cases were characterized by enrichment of stem-like, pre-exhausted, and T-cell activity programs. Recent studies have demonstrated that stem-like T cells represent a self-renewing population capable of sustaining long-term immune responses under controlled conditions (5). These findings suggest that regulated T cell differentiation states can be maintained within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue in the absence of overt inflammatory activation. Collectively, these findings support the concept that compartment-specific immune programs associated with nodal status are shaped by spatial immune context, as summarized in Figure 2D.

Notably, TGF-β signaling was selectively enriched in PTTs of nodal-negative cases, whereas it was not increased in LFRs (8). TGF-β is widely recognized as an immunosuppressive cytokine that regulates T-cell activation and promotes immune tolerance. At the same time, accumulating evidence indicates that TGF-β also plays an essential role in immune homeostasis, tissue repair, and maintenance of local immune equilibrium, particularly within non-lymphoid tissues (11). This interpretation is supported by the concurrent enrichment of stem-like T-cell programs and IL2–STAT5 signaling in nodal-negative PTTs, rather than inflammatory or interferon-associated responses typically associated with immune dysregulation. Accordingly, we interpret the observed TGF-β signaling as reflecting maintenance of local immune homeostasis within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue rather than immune suppression or immune resistance, consistent with the established role of TGF-β in maintaining immune homeostasis (11).

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the spatial transcriptomic analysis of LFRs was performed in only four patients, which limits the statistical power and generalizability of the findings. Although patient-level statistical analyses were performed to account for the non-independence of multiple ROIs obtained from the same individual, the small number of patients requires cautious interpretation and validation in larger independent cohorts. In addition, spatial transcriptomic analysis of LFRs was constrained by the anatomical complexity of secondary lymphoid structures and the technical challenges associated with standardized ROI selection and spatial profiling. Furthermore, the absence of functional validation precludes definitive mechanistic conclusions. Accordingly, the present findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than evidence of causality. The immunohistochemical analyses were intended as qualitative spatial validation rather than quantitative evaluation, and future studies incorporating quantitative assessment of CD8-, TCF1-, and CD8+TCF1+ double-positive cell populations within LFRs and surrounding PTTs would further improve characterization of the spatial immune microenvironment. Finally, whether similar compartment-specific immune programs operate in HPV-negative OPC remains to be determined.

From a clinical perspective, the present findings provide a conceptual framework for interpreting compartment-specific immune programs within tumor-associated tonsillar tissue. Rather than relying solely on bulk tissue analyses, spatial characterization of immune programs may facilitate the development of biomarkers reflecting distinct immune microenvironments. Such spatially informed biomarkers could improve the biological interpretation of tumor-associated lymphoid structures, facilitate patient stratification for immunotherapy, and potentially support future treatment de-escalation strategies for selected patients with HPV-positive OPC. Future studies integrating compartment-specific spatial immune programs with emerging immunotherapy response prediction models may further clarify their potential utility as biomarkers for immunotherapy response prediction and treatment selection. These potential clinical applications remain exploratory and require validation in larger prospective clinical cohorts.

In summary, our findings suggest that immune programs associated with nodal metastasis in HPV-positive OPC cannot be fully interpreted without consideration of spatial immune context, as identical immune pathways may exhibit distinct biological associations depending on their anatomical localization. These findings further suggest that spatial immune organization, rather than absolute immune activation, may influence compartment-specific immune programs associated with lymph node metastasis in HPV-positive OPC.
