Synergistic genomic design and thermal processing mitigate anti-nutritional constraints to enhance the nutritional efficacy of diploid potatoes
Authors: Zhe Yu, Yihui Du, Qianwen Ding, Qiang Hao, Hongwei Yang, Kerou Shi, Chao Ran, Yalin Yang, Chen Wang, Johan Johansen, Giovanna Ottaviani Aalmo, Zhen Zhang, Zhigang Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlobal food security demands sustainable alternatives to the conventional feed ingredient wheat. Nutrient-dense diploid potatoes (HQP) represent candidates due to their enriched phytochemical profiles, including high levels of vitamin C and lutein. However, their dietary utilization is constrained by endogenous anti-nutritional factors (ANFs), primarily solanine and trypsin inhibitors. Using a zebrafish model, we demonstrate that these ANFs trigger intestinal dysbiosis and an oxidative-inflammatory response. Mechanistically, this adverse gut environment suppresses the mTOR pathway, as evidenced by reduced phosphorylation of S6K1 and 4E-BP1, ultimately leading to growth depression. To address this toxicological barrier, we integrated genetic selection (diploid HQP) with thermal processing (steam-cooking). Quantitative analysis revealed that while thermal processing effectively reduced ANF concentrations to physiologically safe levels, the HQP matrix retained significantly higher residual concentrations of vitamin C, lutein, and zeaxanthin compared to conventional tetraploid (C88) and wheat-based diets. Mechanistically, protein-level evidence confirmed that this processed HQP formulation promoted the total NRF2 and suppressed NF-κB P65 accumulation. This metabolic shift attenuated inflammation, reactivated mTOR signaling, and subsequently enhanced growth performance and digestive capacity. Our findings establish a mechanistic framework demonstrating how integrated genomic design and processing strategies facilitate the functional utilization of high-phytochemical crops in sustainable food systems. Subject terms: Biochemistry, Biological techniques, Biotechnology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Global food security is increasingly imperiled by the dwindling supply of staple grains, necessitating the urgent identification of sustainable alternatives to wheat, which currently dominates both human diets and animal feeds1. The heavy reliance on wheat cultivation, spanning vast expanses of arable land worldwide, contributes to shrinking living spaces and underscores the critical need for resource-efficient crops2. Potatoes (Solanum tuberosum L.) represent a viable candidate to alleviate this agricultural pressure due to their lower arable land requirements, exceptional biomass yield, and enhanced nutritional profiles3. Historically, however, the widespread utilization of potatoes has been hindered by the tetraploid nature of most commercial cultivars, which complicates genetic breeding efforts4, and a reliance on tuber-based propagation that restricts production efficiency5. While conventional tetraploid varieties, such as the blight-resistant Cooperation-88 (C88), have achieved strong market acceptance and currently help sustain billions worldwide6,7, recent breakthroughs in genomic design have yielded novel diploid potato cultivars, such as the High-Quality Potato (HQP)8. By enabling scalable, true-seed agriculture, HQP addresses traditional propagation constraints9,10 and enriched fortified phytochemical matrix with high-value antioxidants, including vitamin C and lutein.

However, the translation of this agronomic progress into a viable staple food and aquafeed ingredient is constrained by a critical biological limitation. The extraordinary nutritional potential of raw potatoes is heavily masked by inherent anti-nutritional factors (ANFs), primarily solanine and trypsin inhibitors11. When utilized directly, these persistent compounds act as toxicological barriers, severely disrupting host intestinal homeostasis and triggering a systemic cascade of oxidative stress and NF-κB-mediated inflammation12. Crucially, this adverse physiological microenvironment converges on a central metabolic hub: it significantly suppresses the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) nutrient-sensing axis and its downstream translational effectors, such as p-S6K1 and p-4E-BP113. Consequently, despite the abundant presence of bioavailable carbohydrates and antioxidants in premium diploid cultivars, the host organism exhibits metabolic depression, impaired innate immunity, and profound growth depression.

To address and dismantle this developmental constraint, we propose a synergistic intervention strategy that integrates genetic selection (cultivar nutrient fortification) with targeted physical processing (thermal detoxification). Using the zebrafish (Danio rerio)—a genetically conserved vertebrate model ideally suited for simultaneously evaluating aquafeed viability and scaling to human metabolic health14—we comprehensively investigated the physiological dynamics of wheat substitution. We hypothesize that strategically applied thermal processing will degrade the ANF barrier to a safe threshold, thereby enhancing the bioavailability of the endogenous phytochemicals in the diploid HQP. By activating the NRF2 antioxidant defense system to overcome inflammatory suppression15, this dual-pronged approach aims to reactivate the mTOR signaling network, effectively converting a restricted, high-potential crop into a highly functional, growth-promoting staple.

Results

Direct dietary inclusion of raw potato induces growth depression and systemic oxidative stress via mTOR axis suppression

To establish the physiological baseline for substituting dietary wheat, we formulated diets replacing wheat flour with freshly crushed tetraploid potatoes (C88) at graded levels (1/3, 2/3, and 3/3 replacement). During the feeding trial, survival rates declined progressively as the proportion of C88 increased (Fig. 1B). The surviving zebrafish exhibited marked, dose-dependent growth retardation; the weight gain rate (WGR) decreased significantly, while the feed conversion ratio (FCR) increased correspondingly from the 1/3 to the 3/3 substitution groups (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1C). Intestinal biochemical assays revealed a major nutritional constraint: the activities of critical digestive enzymes, amylase and lipase, were sharply downregulated in proportion to the raw potato inclusion level (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1D). Regression analysis further confirmed a strong positive correlation between these diminished digestive enzyme activities and the suppressed WGR (Fig. 1E, F).

To trace the tissue-level origins of this severe nutritional restriction, we conducted histological and biochemical evaluations. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of the liver revealed noticeable cellular damage and structural disorganization that worsened with higher C88 inclusion (Fig. 1H), corroborated by a dose-dependent leakage of hepatic transaminases (ALT and AST) into the serum (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1I). Similarly, intestinal sections displayed severe mucosal impairment proportional to the potato dosage (Fig. 1J). This loss of structural integrity in the intestinal barrier was supported at the transcriptional level by a gradient downregulation of tight junction proteins, occludin and claudin (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1K). Consequently, this compromised intestinal permeability allowed for systemic endotoxin translocation, driving a substantial, dose-responsive increase in serum lipopolysaccharide (LPS) levels (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1L).

This barrier disruption and subsequent endotoxin influx triggered a significant cellular stress response. A systemic reduction of antioxidant defenses was evident in both liver and intestinal tissues, characterized by a progressive depletion of total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and superoxide dismutase (SOD), CAT, and GR levels (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1M, N and Supplementary Fig. 1a, b). Parallel to this antioxidant depletion, malondialdehyde (MDA) levels significantly increased (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1O), and qPCR analysis profiling revealed a broad suppression of the nrf2 antioxidant pathway, as well as its downstream targets (e.g., ho-1, cat) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1P). This oxidative stress state resulted in immunological dysfunction. Key innate immune parameters—lysozyme (LZM), complement component 3 (C3), C4, AKP, ACP (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1Q, R and Supplementary Fig. 1c–e), and adaptive immunity immunoglobulin M (IgM) and IgZ (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1S and Supplementary Fig. 1f)—were significantly depressed, while the heatmap demonstrated a stark upregulation of the nf-κb and its downstream signaling molecules (tnf-α, il-1β) cascade across the gradient (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1T).

Crucially, this convergence of reduced nutrient availability and severe intracellular stress (reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and NF-κB hyperactivation) directly suppressed the host’s central metabolic controller. Protein quantification revealed that the mTOR signaling pathway—the hub for nutrient sensing and anabolic growth—was abnormally and significantly inhibited across the C88-substituted groups (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1G). This downregulation of the protein synthesis machinery, coupled with the immunosuppressive state, manifested lethally during a dual bacterial challenge with Aeromonas veronii and A. hydrophila. Survival curves decreased most substantially for the high-inclusion cohorts (Fig. 1U), with quantitative mortality analysis at 24 h and 48 h (Fig. 1V–W) confirming a drastically heightened susceptibility to infection. Given that the initial physiological impairment breach occurred at the intestinal mucosa, accompanied by a systemic surge in bacteria-derived LPS, the upstream trigger of this pathogenesis required clarification. This structural and immune reduction at the host-diet interface raised a pivotal question: Was this cascading metabolic suppression secondarily driven by a raw potato-induced dysbiosis of the resident gut microbiome, or was it directly inflicted by the inherent chemical toxicity of the potato matrix itself?

Microbiota-mediated compensatory shifts and host-microbe signaling in response to raw potato-induced stress

Following the observation of widespread tissue damage and endotoxin leakage at the intestinal interface, we profiled the gut microbiome to determine its role in the host’s systemic physiological impairment. 16S rRNA gene sequencing revealed that raw potato inclusion significantly restructured the resident microbial community. Principal coordinate analysis (PCA) displayed a clear, gradient-driven spatial separation between the wheat control and the C88-fed cohorts, confirming a distinct dietary shift in beta diversity (Fig. 2F). Concurrently, alpha diversity metrics, including ACE, Chao1, Shannon, and Simpson indices, exhibited dynamic, nonlinear fluctuations across the substitution gradient, reflecting a community under environmental pressure (Fig. 2G–J). Taxonomic profiling at the phylum (Fig. 2A) and genus (Fig. 2B) levels detailed this architectural shift. Interestingly, rather than a uniform expansion of pathogens, the C88 diets triggered a complex change. For instance, the abundance of Cetobacterium—a genus associated with host metabolic homeostasis—expanded non-linearly peaking at moderate inclusion levels (1/3 and 2/3) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2C). Meanwhile, opportunistic pathogens such as Aeromonas and Shewanella (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2D–E) initially decreased before exhibiting variable repopulation at higher inclusion ratios. This complex microbial adaptation was quantitatively captured by an integrative gut microbiota health index, which significantly increased in the C88-1/3 group prior to stabilizing, suggesting a compensatory microbial defense rather than a simple ecological reduction (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2K).

To decouple the independent physiological effects of this altered microbiome from the direct impact of the raw potato diet itself, we transplanted the fecal microbiota from the respective donor groups into germ-free (GF) zebrafish larvae. This in vivo model allowed us to isolate microbial-derived signals. Quantitative PCR analysis of the GF recipients revealed that the C88-derived microbiota induced a heightened transcriptional state in the naive hosts. Compared to the wheat-microbiota recipients, GF fish colonized with C88 microbiota displayed a broad upregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (tnf-α, il-1β) alongside a concurrent activation of anti-inflammatory cytokines and antioxidant genes, including il-10, tgf-β, and cat, coupled with the suppression of keap1 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2L). A correlation heatmap further substantiated this host-microbe crosstalk, demonstrating that the transcriptional activation of these immune and redox pathways was heavily correlated with the specific enrichment of the top six transferred bacterial taxa (Fig. 2M).

Together, these GF transfer results indicate that while the C88-altered microbiome induces a specific inflammatory response, this secondary microbial crosstalk is insufficient to independently drive the severe metabolic suppression and tissue destruction observed in conventionally raised fish (Fig. 1). If the microbiome is merely reacting and exhibits a compensatory response, the primary toxicological barriers responsible for the host’s metabolic depression reside within the raw potato matrix itself.

Fractionation and exclusion analysis identify endogenous ANFs as the primary toxicological barriers

To distinguish whether the severe host toxicity originated from the physicochemical matrix of the potato or its endogenous soluble factors, we utilized a water extraction protocol to separate purified C88 starch from the raw tubers (Fig. 3A). When administered to GF zebrafish, this isolated physical fraction did not elicit a significant inflammatory response. Compared to the PBS control, exposure to the extracted starch significantly downregulated pro-inflammatory markers (nf-κb, tnf-α, il-1β, and tgf-β) while concurrently upregulating the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3B). Additionally, the master antioxidant regulator nrf2 and its downstream genes remained stable, accompanied by a downregulation of its repressor keap1α/β (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3C).

By demonstrating that the isolated structural carbohydrate matrix showed no significant toxicological effects, this exclusion assay directed further investigation towards the endogenous compounds. Chemical quantification confirmed that high concentrations of ANFs—specifically solanine and trypsin inhibitors (TRYI)—remained significantly concentrated in both the fresh C88 tubers and the raw C88 diets (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3D). Subsequent integrative analysis revealed a strong correlation: the specific concentrations of solanine and TRYI were strongly linked to the transcriptional dysregulation of the nf-κb and nrf2/keap1 signaling networks (Fig. 3E). Having identified these ANFs as the primary toxicological barriers limiting raw potato utilization, we next investigated whether conventional industrial thermal processing could effectively reduce these toxins to restore host growth and metabolic performance.

Thermal processing mitigates acute toxicity, but chronic stress continues to limit growth and physiological health relative to wheat

Given the thermal sensitivity of ANFs, we applied steam-cooking to the tetraploid C88 cultivar to investigate whether thermal processing could effectively reduce these toxins and restore host metabolic homeostasis (Fig. 4A). Quantitative analysis showed that the contents of solanine and trypsin inhibitors were significantly reduced in both the cooked potatoes and their corresponding pelleted diets compared to the raw equivalents (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B; Supplementary Table 3). Following this thermal processing, zebrafish fed the C-C88 diets maintained a 100% survival rate throughout the 56-day feeding trial (Fig. 4C). Concurrently, intestinal amylase and lipase activities in the C-C88 groups recovered to levels comparable to those of the wheat control (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4E, F).

Despite the restoration of survival and digestive capacity, the growth performance of the C-C88 groups remained constrained. The C-C88 WGR was significantly lower than that of the wheat control, accompanied by an elevated FCR (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4D). Biochemical analysis revealed the sustained chronic oxidative stress; lipid peroxidation (MDA) remained significantly elevated in both the liver and gut compared to the wheat baseline (Fig. 4J), while T-AOC (Fig. 4G) and key antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT) were suppressed at higher inclusion levels (Fig. 4H, I). Transcriptional profiling confirmed the under-expression of the nrf2 antioxidant pathway (Fig. 4K) alongside an upregulation of pro-inflammatory markers (nf-κb, il-1β) (Fig. 4L), with minimal activation of anti-inflammatory genes (il-10, tgf-β) (Fig. 4M). Consequently, host survival against pathogenic infection remained significantly lower, as evidenced by significantly lower survival rates in the C-C88 groups following the dual bacterial challenge (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4N, O). These data demonstrate that while thermal processing removes lethal toxicity, the baseline nutritional configuration of conventional tetraploid potatoes inherently limits growth potential, suggesting the importance of introducing a bio-fortified cultivar.

Genome-designed bio-fortification reveals a nutritional constraint where phytochemical potential is counteracted by endogenous toxicological barriers

To surpass the nutritional limitations of conventional tetraploid cultivars, we introduced a diploid HQP developed via genomic design breeding (Fig. 5A). Chemical profiling confirmed its enhanced nutritional configuration, featuring significantly higher concentrations of vitamin C, lutein, and zeaxanthin compared to both C88 and wheat (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5B). However, raw HQP retained high baseline levels of solanine and TRYI, comparable to the conventional C88 strain (Fig. 5C). Consequently, direct feeding of raw HQP yielded an adverse physiological outcome: survival rates declined (Fig. 5D), and the fish exhibited severe growth retardation (reduced WGR, elevated FCR) (Fig. 5E) alongside impaired intestinal digestive enzyme activities (Fig. 5F).

The primary factor of this continued growth suppression was attributed to the raw chemical matrix. The undegraded ANFs maintained chronic irritation in the intestinal tract, resulting in mucosal damage and tight junction down-regulation (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5I, N), which was accompanied by significantly elevated serum LPS levels (Fig. 5H) and hepatic transaminase leakage (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5G). At the cellular level, this toxicity sustained a severe state of oxidative stress (depleted T-AOC, SOD, CAT, GR, elevated MDA) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5J, K and Supplementary Fig. 2a–c) and an nf-κb-mediated inflammatory response (Fig. 5L). Crucially, protein quantification via ELISA demonstrated that this sustained stress cascade directly suppressed the host’s central nutrient-sensing network. The expression levels of mTOR and its downstream translational effectors, p-S6K1 and p-4E-BP1, remained largely inhibited in the raw HQP groups (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5O, P).

The functional utility of HQP—despite failing to support growth in its raw state—is demonstrated through a direct transcriptional comparison with the C88 cohort. Under systemic stress conditions, the suppression of downstream antioxidant enzymes (Cuzn-sod, Mn-sod, cat) (Fig. 5Q–S) and immunoregulatory genes (il-10, tgf-β) (Fig. 5T, U) was significantly attenuated in the HQP groups (P < 0.05). This transcriptional activation confirms that HQP’s phytochemicals stimulate the NRF2 network, yet this signal is counteracted by concomitant ANF-triggered NF-κB inflammation. This constraint reveals that HQP’s active potential remains biologically unavailable until its toxicological barriers are removed.

Synergistic effects of thermal processing and genetic fortification on NRF2/mTOR signaling and growth performance

Building upon the established efficacy of steam-cooking, we applied this thermal processing to the HQP cultivar, resulting in a significant reduction of solanine and TRYI concentrations (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6A and Supplementary Table 4). Crucially, quantitative analysis of the processed diets revealed that despite thermal exposure, the C-HQP matrix maintained significantly higher residual concentrations of vitamin C, lutein, and zeaxanthin compared to both C-C88 and wheat-based diets (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6B). This combination of reduced toxicity and sustained nutrient density was associated with significant phenotypic improvements. Throughout the 8-week trial, zebrafish fed the C-HQP diets maintained a 100% survival rate (Supplementary Fig. 2d). Notably, at the 1/3 substitution level (C-HQP-1/3), the WGR significantly surpassed the wheat control, corresponding with a lower FCR (Fig. 6C). This enhanced growth performance was primarily supported by improved nutrient availability, as indicated by significantly elevated intestinal amylase and lipase activities relative to the control (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6D).

Alongside enhanced digestion, the C-HQP-1/3 diet was associated with the restoration of cellular homeostasis. Transcriptional analysis revealed a significant upregulation of the nrf2 antioxidant pathway and a downregulation of keap1 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6K), driving the elevation of T-AOC (Fig. 6G) and the activities of SOD (Fig. 6H) and CAT (Fig. 6I), which collectively reduced lipid peroxidation (MDA) (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6J). Concurrent with this antioxidant response, the pro-inflammatory nf-κb and il-1β cascades were significantly suppressed, accompanied by an increase in the anti-inflammatory cytokine il-10 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6L). The combined mitigation of nutrient restriction and systemic stress is reflected in the central metabolic regulator. Protein-level evidence confirmed that the mTOR signaling pathway, including the phosphorylation of its downstream effectors p-S6K1 and p-4E-BP1, was significantly reactivated in the C-HQP-1/3 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6E, F), providing the molecular basis for the observed phenotype of accelerated growth.

This systemic physiological restoration was further linked to a restructured intestinal microbiome (Fig. 6M–S). Taxonomic profiling showed that the C-HQP-1/3 diet significantly reduced the relative abundance of Nostocaceae (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6O) while sustaining commensal taxa such as Muribaculaceae and Lachnospiraceae (Fig. 6P). GF transfer assays confirmed the functional contribution of this microbial shift, as the microbiota shaped by the C-HQP-1/3 diet induced higher host nrf2 expression compared to the high-dose group (Fig. 6T). Furthermore, correlation network analysis demonstrated significant associations between specific microbial taxa (e.g., Bacteroides) and physiological metrics, including WGR, amylase activity, and T-AOC (Fig. 6U). Ultimately, the synergistic restoration of nutritional sensing, redox balance, and microbial health was associated with enhanced immunological resistance; the C-HQP-1/3 cohort exhibited a significantly higher survival rate against pathogenic bacterial challenge compared to the traditional wheat baseline (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6V, W).

Multidimensional phenotypic integration and protein-level validation of the synergistic metabolic optimization model

To evaluate the integrated physiological and metabolic impact of potato-based staple substitution, we utilized a multidimensional radar model to normalize the core performance metrics (Fig. 7A). The evaluation—spanning growth (WGR), digestion (amylase), survival, safety (solanine), and cellular stress (MDA, nf-κb)—demonstrates that while thermal processing of conventional tetraploids (C-C88) restores survival by reducing solanine, it fails to neutralize systemic oxidative and inflammatory stress. In contrast, the synergistic application of genetic bio-fortification and thermal detoxification (C-HQP) yields a metabolic profile that was statistically comparable to that matches or exceeds the wheat baseline across all functional dimensions (Fig. 7A). To validate the molecular mechanism governing the transition between stress-induced growth depression and nutrient-driven protein synthesis, we performed protein-level analysis of the key transcription factors. This direct evidence of transcription factor activation/inhibition, coupled with the previously observed reactivation of the mTOR signaling pathway and its downstream translational effectors (p-S6K1 and p-4E-BP1), provides a mechanistic molecular basis for the observed phenotype of enhanced growth performance. Collectively, these integrated data support a molecular relay model for the metabolic optimization of diploid potatoes (Fig. 7F). Our evidence indicates that intact ANFs (solanine and TRYI) induce systemic oxidative stress, which stimulates the NF-κB inflammatory response—as evidenced by the elevated total p65 protein abundance (Fig. 7D, E)—and subsequently suppresses the mTOR nutrient-sensing axis, leading to anabolic inhibition. Conversely, the synergistic strategy effectively reduces these ANF concentrations, facilitating the biological activity of the endogenous phytochemicals (vitamin C and lutein) to activate the NRF2 signaling pathway, confirmed by the significant upregulation of total NRF2 protein (Fig. 7B, C). The subsequent upregulation of downstream antioxidant enzymes (SOD and CAT) attenuates oxidative damage and suppresses NF-κB-mediated inflammation. The removal of these inhibitory signals reactivates the mTOR axis and its downstream translational machinery, thereby facilitating the protein synthesis and growth potential of the bio-fortified diploid potato (Fig. 7F).

Discussion

The necessity of identifying resource-efficient alternatives to dietary wheat is central to transitioning toward sustainable global food systems16. However, our initial baseline in vivo feeding trials demonstrate a significant biological constraint regarding potato utilization. While raw tetraploid potatoes (C88) offer substantial carbohydrate biomass, their direct dietary substitution precipitates severe growth depression and significant mortality in zebrafish, a highly conserved vertebrate model for metabolic research. Crucially, unlike the gradual mucosal atrophy typically secondary to prolonged nutritional deficiency, the pathology observed here is an acute, primary permeabilization of the intestinal mucosal barrier. This aligns with the known membrane-disrupting, detergent-like properties of specific plant secondary metabolites11. We demonstrate that this diet-induced structural impairment is characterized by the marked downregulation of tight junction proteins (occludin, claudin), establishing an increased intestinal permeability. This loss of integrity permits the systemic translocation of intestinal endotoxins (LPS), which subsequently drives profound hepatic injury via the well-established gut-liver axis17. Consequently, this endotoxemia induces substantial cellular dysregulation: the nrf2 antioxidant defense network is suppressed, and the nf-κb cascade is hyperactivated, driving an unmitigated inflammatory response characterized by significant elevations of tnf-α and il-1β18. This convergence of severely impaired nutrient assimilation (suppressed amylase/lipase activities) and elevated oxidative stress directly suppressed the mTOR pathway. Because mTOR serves not only as the central regulator for anabolic protein synthesis and organismal growth13 but also as a critical regulator of innate immune cell survival19, its profound suppression provides the mechanistic explanation for the host’s simultaneous growth inhibition and immunosuppressive reduction upon pathogenic challenge. Yet, because this physiological cascade initiates at the luminal interface, it introduces a critical mechanistic question: Is this systemic mTOR inhibition primarily mediated by a diet-induced dysbiosis of the resident gut microbiome, or is it a direct toxicological consequence of intrinsic toxicological barriers residing within the raw tuber matrix?

To resolve this critical mechanistic question, we analyzed the host-associated intestinal microbiome, a well-documented amplifier of dietary and environmental stress20. Contrary to the conventional expectation of a uniform pathogenic overgrowth following severe mucosal damage, 16S rRNA profiling revealed highly dynamic, non-linear changes. Specifically, the gut microbiota exhibited a non-monotonic, pronounced spike of the keystone mutualist Cetobacterium at the initial substitution level (1/3) before gradually declining under higher dietary stress. Given that Cetobacterium is documented to synthesize short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) that support host metabolic homeostasis21, its immediate proliferation in an injured host necessitated further causative isolation. We sought to decouple the intrinsic biological properties of this altered microbiome from the continuous chemical toxicity of the raw diet. To achieve this, we transplanted the C88-shaped microbiota into GF zebrafish, a gold-standard model for isolating microbial functions22. Crucially, this engraftment excluded the primary role of bacteria of participating in the structural mucosal damage. Instead of replicating the substantial metabolic suppression seen in conventionally raised fish, the colonizers triggered a significant transcriptional activation of mucosal defenses in the naive GF hosts, characterized by the pronounced upregulation of antioxidant (cat) and immunoregulatory (il-10, tgf-β) genes. Synthesizing these in vivo and GF findings, we posit a microbially mediated insufficient compensatory mechanism driven by compensatory proliferation. Upon acute mucosal injury by exogenous dietary factors, the host actively modulates its intestinal niche to recruit protective commensals—a documented survival strategy utilized to reinforce epithelial integrity during severe chemical injury23. However, the ultimate mortality and profound mTOR suppression of the conventionally raised host prove that this compensatory microbial response is strictly compensatory and fundamentally insufficient against the overwhelming dietary toxicity. This threshold-dependent reduction is consistent with toxicological models where host-microbe resistance is ultimately superseded by acute chemical exposure24. Having definitively eliminated microbial dysbiosis as the primary driver, the mechanistic trail points directly back to the raw tuber itself.

Having redirected the mechanistic focus exclusively to the raw tuber, we sought to systematically distinguish whether the profound host toxicity was an artifact of the physical carbohydrate matrix—such as physical overloading by raw resistant starches—or driven by endogenous soluble phytotoxins. To resolve this, we employed an established aqueous fractionation protocol25 to isolate purified C88 potato starch and evaluated its transcriptional impact using the GF zebrafish model. Notably, this isolated structural matrix showed no significant transcriptional impact. In contrast to the inflammatory response induced by the whole raw diet, exposure to the purified starch significantly downregulated pro-inflammatory transcription (nf-κb, tnf-α, il-1β) while significantly upregulating the anti-inflammatory cytokine il-10, maintaining effective quiescence of the nrf2-mediated oxidative stress axis. This inert profile confirms that purified plant starches, once stripped of bioactive secondary metabolites, do not induce inflammatory responses26. By systematically excluding the physical carbohydrate structure, we isolated the source of toxicity to the soluble fraction. Quantitative chemical profiling confirmed that both the raw C88 tubers and their corresponding formulated diets contained high concentrations of specific ANFs, predominantly solanine and trypsin inhibitors (TRYI). The identification of these compounds provides a direct mechanistic link to our initial phenotypic observations. Trypsin inhibitors are widely recognized to acutely bind and suppress luminal digestive enzymes, driving the observed nutrient starvation and suppressed amylase/lipase activities we documented27. Concurrently, steroidal glycoalkaloids like solanine exert potent, detergent-like membrane-disruptive toxicities on intestinal epithelial cells, acting as the direct molecular trigger for the tissue damage, endotoxemia, and downstream NF-κB hyperactivation observed in our baseline trial28. Collectively, these findings confirm that solanine and TRYI function as the definitive toxicological barriers of the raw potato, thereby restricting the bioavailability of its potential nutrients and driving the host into a state of mTOR-suppressed anabolic quiescence.

While the aforementioned GF zebrafish assays provided a critical reductionist approach to decouple the direct chemical toxicity of the potato matrix from microbe-mediated inflammatory responses29, it is imperative to acknowledge the inherent biological limitations of this model. Compared to conventionally raised, fully immunocompetent counterparts, GF zebrafish possess an inherently underdeveloped mucosal immune system. Consequently, although the transcriptional shifts observed in our GF models successfully validate the primary trajectory of matrix-induced toxicity, the absolute magnitude of these mucosal immune responses cannot be directly extrapolated to conventional physiological states. Thus, the mucosal transcriptional readouts from the GF assays must be strictly interpreted as a qualitative isolation of the host’s baseline chemical sensitivity, rather than a quantitative reflection of the complex immunological cascades that occur in a fully colonized, immunocompetent organism.

Based on the thermal instability of trypsin inhibitors and steroidal glycoalkaloids11, we applied steam-cooking to the C88 cultivar. This intervention significantly reduced dietary ANF concentrations, precluding acute mortality (100% survival) and restoring intestinal amylase and lipase activities to levels comparable to the wheat control. Despite this recovery of basic digestive competence, the C-C88-fed cohorts exhibited significantly reduced growth performance compared to the wheat baseline. Biochemical and transcriptional profiling indicated that the host remained in a state of allostatic load; residual, heat-stable ANFs sustained continuous oxidative stress (elevated MDA, suppressed nrf2) and persistent nf-κb activation in the liver and intestine. Because chronic inflammatory and oxidative signals are established upstream repressors of the mTOR signaling cascade30, somatic energy is diverted from anabolic protein synthesis toward continuous cellular repair, thereby suppressing overall growth.

Recent advances in aquatic nutrition demonstrate that optimized thermal processing is generally sufficient to maximize the inclusion of conventional plant ingredients, such as soybean meal, by effectively neutralizing their predominant heat-labile ANFs31. However, based on the persistent metabolic suppression observed in our C-C88 cohorts, our data demonstrate that conventional potatoes face an inherent biological limitation. This plateau highlights a significant limitation in relying solely on physical processing: thermal detoxification merely subtracts negative constraints but fails to actively optimize host metabolic resistance against residual stress32. To surpass conventional grains like wheat, future sustainable staples must go beyond basic safety. This forward-looking imperative shifts our focus toward active nutritional fortification, specifically targeting potato variants with enriched phytochemical profiles. Such cultivars offer the theoretical capacity to quench residual processing stress, thereby allowing the host’s anabolic machinery to be fully activated.

The assay of potato breeding has been fundamentally reshaped by the team of Professor Sanwen Huang at our institute, transitioning the crop from a clonally propagated tetraploid into a genome-designed diploid8,9. This historic shift enables genetic manipulation to purge deleterious mutations and actively rewire metabolic pathways for enhanced nutritional profiles10,33. Represented in this study by the HQP—a direct product of this cutting-edge genome design—this variant exhibits an enriched nutritional profile markedly enriched in essential phytochemicals such as ascorbic acid and lutein34. However, the empirical evaluation of raw HQP revealed an observed biological outcome: this high-nutrient matrix failed to mitigate the mortality of the cultured subjects, and growth trajectories remained severely suppressed, mirroring the severe phenotypic impairment observed in the raw C88 cohort. Physiological and transcriptional profiling indicate that the raw HQP-fed fish exhibited oxidative stress and persistent inflammation, which directly inhibited the mTOR nutrient-sensing axis and consequently deteriorated somatic growth performance31. These findings provide definitive evidence that residual ANFs in raw HQP exert a concomitant toxicological effect, triggering an inflammatory response within the intestinal epithelium that remains the primary driver of growth depression32.

Notwithstanding this phenotypic stagnation, comparative transcriptional profiling reveals that the genetic innovation of the HQP did initiate a latent metabolic response. Unlike the C88 group, fish fed raw HQP exhibited a relative upregulation of the nrf2 signaling cascade, indicating that the enriched ascorbic acid and lutein stimulated a systemic antioxidant defense15. Yet, this compensatory response was ultimately outweighed by a more potent nf-κb-mediated inflammatory tone31. Although the HQP baseline concentration of native ANFs was successfully reduced during design, the residual levels of intact steroidal glycoalkaloids and trypsin inhibitors still exceeded the strict physiological tolerance threshold of the teleost intestine12. This predominance of toxicological effects over nutrition forces a marked diversion of metabolic energy from anabolic protein synthesis toward continuous mucosal repair35. Consequently, the exceptional bioactive potential of genome-designed crops is competitively suppressed and effectively masked by the restrictive antinutritional barrier of the raw plant matrix36. To fully utilize the HQP’s nutritional potential and translate its molecular potential into tangible growth performance, this basal chemical defense threshold must first be removed.

The identification of the antinutritional barrier as the primary metabolic constraint logically leads to the pivotal intervention of this study: the thermal liberation of the HQP’s potential. By applying steam-cooking to the HQP (C-HQP), we successfully removed the heat-labile toxicological blockade, effectively neutralizing the trypsin inhibitors and partially degrading steroidal glycoalkaloids that previously imposed profound physiological constraints on host metabolism11. Critically, our quantitative analysis confirmed that this mild thermal intervention preserved the elite nutritional (matrix); the C-HQP diets retained significantly higher residual concentrations of vitamin C, lutein, and zeaxanthin compared to the C-C88 and wheat-based groups. This sustained nutrient density explains why the C-HQP cohorts did not merely recover from their raw state but exhibited significantly enhanced growth performance. The empirical data demonstrate that the WGR significantly surpassed the wheat-based gold standard, while the FCR reached a numerically enhanced baseline, marking the peak performance of the entire trial.

The mechanistic essence of this synergistic liberation lies in the restored bioavailability of the HQP’s enriched nutritional configuration. Once the ANF-induced inflammatory response was attenuated through thermal processing, the enriched baseline of ascorbic acid and lutein was finally able to transition from a suppressed state to active metabolic engagement37. In the absence of primary toxicological irritation, these potent genome-designed phytochemicals successfully re-equilibrated the host’s redox homeostasis, evidenced by the substantial activation of the nrf2 signaling cascade and the concurrent suppression of oxidative markers such as MDA38. Our Western blot analysis of the total protein abundance provides direct evidence for this molecular relay, confirming that the C-HQP diet significantly promoted the total NRF2 while simultaneously inhibiting NF-κB P65. This comprehensive alleviation of the allostatic load removed the upstream inhibition on the mTOR signaling pathway. With the nutrient-sensing axis fully reignited, somatic energy was no longer continuously diverted toward mucosal repair, but was instead maximally channeled into anabolic protein synthesis and skeletal muscle accretion13.

Our results indicate that while physical processing is a general necessity for tuber utilization, its true biological efficacy is fundamentally governed by the underlying genetic matrix. This integration—where physical processing removes negative constraints and genome fortification provides positive metabolic drivers—establishes a theoretical framework for the development of sustainable staple feeds.

The true value of the genome-designed diploid potato is demonstrated when compared to the conventional cultivar under identical thermal processing conditions. As illustrated in the multidimensional radar chart, while cooking removed heat-labile toxins in both groups, their subsequent metabolic effects diverged. The C-C88 group remained constrained by elevated oxidative (MDA) and inflammatory (nf-κb) markers, resulting in suboptimal amylase activity and WGR. In contrast, the C-HQP cohort maximized the detoxified matrix, suppressing stress markers and translating its genetic advantages into enhanced digestive and growth performance that rivaled the wheat baseline.

This phenotypic divergence suggests that the performance gain in C-HQP is an active metabolic enhancement driven by its enriched nutritional profile rather than a passive response to reduced toxicity. In C-C88, the lack of intrinsic antioxidants left the host vulnerable to residual processing-induced stress. Conversely, in C-HQP, the activation of the Nrf2-Keap1 defense system was specifically driven by the bioavailable ascorbic acid and lutein. This interaction represents a manifestation of phytochemical hormesis, where the synergistic presence of bio-fortified nutrients and attenuated anti-nutrients repurposes the plant matrix from a source of toxicity into a biological signaling agent39. The significant upregulation of total NRF2 protein, confirmed by our Western blot data, supports the role of these phytochemicals as signaling modulators that upregulate downstream antioxidant enzymes (SOD, CAT)40. This targeted intervention attenuated ROS levels and suppressed nf-κb-mediated inflammatory signaling. These findings validate our proposed molecular relay model: the genome-designed matrix mitigates the inhibitory crosstalk from NF-κB, allowing NRF2 to relieve the final suppression of the mTOR pathway

The translation of this mTOR activation into enhanced growth is rooted in whole-body energy repartitioning. Chronic mucosal inflammation is an energetically costly process that significantly elevates maintenance requirements35. In the C-C88 cohort, persistent nf-κb activation dictated that dietary energy was sequestered for futile epithelial turnover. Given that protein synthesis and turnover constitute up to 30% of the standard metabolic rate in teleosts41, the liberation of this metabolic flux in C-HQP—achieved by extinguishing the inflammatory response—facilitated a profound redirection of ATP. These combined phenotypic and biochemical findings strongly point to a model of profound ‘energy repartitioning,’ where liberated metabolic energy is no longer diverted toward chronic mucosal repair, but is instead redirected into net protein accretion and skeletal muscle growth42.

While the atmospheric steam-cooking utilized in this study effectively navigated the trade-off between anti-nutritional factor clearance and bioactive retention, it is imperative to contextualize this physical intervention within modern industrial aquafeed manufacturing. Conventional industrial extrusion relies on high-temperature and high-pressure (HTHP) conditions to maximize starch gelatinization43. However, applying HTHP processes directly to the genome-designed HQP matrix would be fundamentally counterproductive. Although our empirical data confirm the thermal stability of zeaxanthin and lutein during 100 °C steaming, the mechanical shear stress and sudden post-die expansion inherent to HTHP extrusion induce severe matrix disruption and elevated oxygen exposure. These physical stressors reliably trigger substantial oxidative degradation and trans-to-cis isomerization of carotenoids, alongside the near-total destruction of Vitamin C44,45. Consequently, the realistic scale-up application of such genome-designed functional crops necessitates tailored processing pipelines. Industrial adoption can be viably achieved through low-temperature, low-shear extrusion or via thermal pre-conditioning followed by cold pelleting—strategies increasingly utilized within the functional aquafeed sector to preserve thermolabile bioactives.

Ultimately, this study addresses a long-standing constraint in agricultural and nutritional sciences: the conflict between nutritional fortification and the inherent ANFs of plant matrices. For decades, efforts to bio-fortify crops have often yielded limited in vivo benefits because the augmented nutrients remain biologically unavailable due to basal chemical defenses36. We demonstrate that the synergy of genomic design and precision processing is not merely an incremental improvement, but a fundamental resolution to these nutrition versus toxicity constraints. This dual-track strategy—subtracting the toxicological barriers through processing while releasing the genetic potential through bio-fortification—provides a highly reproducible theoretical assay. It paves the way for the functional valorization of numerous potentially high-yield but ANF-rich crops, such as cassava and legumes, fundamentally redefining the future trajectory of sustainable food and feed formulation.

In conclusion, this study addresses the fundamental limitations that have long constrained the functional utilization of raw plant matrices in global food and feed systems. We demonstrate that the synergistic integration of genomic biofortification and precision thermal processing is the key to dismantling the chemical barriers imposed by ANFs. By neutralizing these toxicological constraints, this dual-track intervention systematically redirects host energy budgets, shifting metabolic resources away from the energetically costly process of inflammatory repair and toward enhanced somatic growth. The thermal liberation of the nutritional surplus inherent to genome-designed cultivars enables an orchestrated, NRF2-driven antioxidant response. This proactive defense quenches oxidative stress and, crucially, relieves the inflammatory suppression that acts as a molecular brake on the mTOR signaling axis. This comprehensive energy repartitioning reallocates metabolic flux from basal maintenance to net protein accretion, establishing a theoretical assay in which genetic enhancement must be coupled with targeted barrier disruption to translate intrinsic potential into maximal in vivo efficacy. This framework offers a transferable blueprint for the functional valorization of other globally significant, ANF-rich crops, repositioning genome-designed staples as foundational platforms for sustainable global food security.

Methodss

Animal ethics and model system

The wild-type Tübingen strain and GF zebrafish (Danio rerio) utilized in this study were bred and maintained at the standardized zebrafish facility of the Institute of Feed Research, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Beijing, China). The GF zebrafish were derived following previously established gnotobiotic protocols46. The utilization of the zebrafish model was used for the evaluation of aquafeed inclusion dynamics and fundamental vertebrate metabolic health. All experimental protocols and animal care procedures were approved and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences.

Plant materials, precision processing, and diet formulation

Genetically improved diploid HQP and conventional tetraploid potatoes (C88) were provided by the Agricultural Genomics Institute at Shenzhen, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences. To evaluate the fundamental biological effects and isolate the impact of inherent toxicological factors, the raw potato cultivars (HQP and C88) were utilized in their raw state for the baseline trial. For the thermal processing, both HQP and C88 tubers were subjected to steam-cooking prior to diet formulation to neutralize heat-labile antinutritional factors (ANFs). For the thermal processing intervention, the prepared potatoes were subjected to atmospheric steam-cooking at 100 °C under standard pressure (approx. 0.1 MPa) for 20 min. This specific temperature-time combination was selected to effectively denature heat-labile ANFs (such as trypsin inhibitors) and partially reduce steroidal glycoalkaloids via matrix disruption47,48, while minimizing the severe thermal degradation of heat-sensitive bioactives (Vitamin C and lutein) that typically occurs during high-temperature extrusion. Post-steaming, the potato biomass was dried in a hot-air oven at 60 °C for 12 h to maintain the stability of antioxidants, milled into powder, and subsequently processed into standard feed pellets using a cold-extrusion pelletizer (operating at room temperature to avoid secondary thermal damage).

Experimental diets were formulated to maintain isonitrogenous and isoenergetic conditions (Supp. Table 1). Wheat flour served as the primary carbohydrate control (Wheat baseline). The potato-based experimental diets were formulated by replacing the wheat flour component with either freshly-crushed raw potatoes or steam-cooked potatoes at progressive substitution levels: 33.3% (1/3), 66.7% (2/3), and 100% (3/3). Consequently, the raw potato inclusion groups were designated as HQP-1/3, HQP-2/3, HQP-3/3, C88-1/3, C88-2/3, and C88-3/3. Following thermal processing, the cooked potato groups were designated as C-HQP-1/3, C-HQP-2/3, C-HQP-3/3, C-C88-1/3, C-C88-2/3, and C-C88-3/3.

To determine the chemical composition of the unformulated tubers and the final experimental diets, quantitative biochemical and analytical profiling was conducted. The residual concentrations of the primary ANFs—specifically steroidal glycoalkaloids (solanine) and trypsin inhibitors (TRYI)—were quantified utilizing competitive Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturer’s analytical protocols (Jiancheng, Nanjing; Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd). Concurrently, to validate the enriched genomic bio-fortification of the HQP matrix, the functional phytochemical constituents, including ascorbic acid (Vitamin C), lutein, and zeaxanthin, were resolved and quantified employing High-Performance Liquid Chromatography coupled with Electrospray Ionization Tandem Mass Spectrometry (HPLC-ESI-MS/MS). This chemical analysis investigates the correlation between the physical dietary inputs and the resulting physiological phenotypes.

In vivo nutritional and toxicological feeding trials

To comprehensively assess the metabolic consequences of dietary wheat substitution, two distinct in vivo feeding trials were performed.

Baseline toxicological assessment (raw tubers)

Following an initial acclimation period, healthy and uniform one-month-old zebrafish fry (initial weight approximately 0.91 g per fish) were randomly allocated into 28 experimental tanks using a completely randomized design. The trial consisted of seven dietary treatment groups, with four biological replicate tanks per group and 20 fish per tank (n = 4). The fish were hand-fed their respective raw-tuber diets twice daily (09:00 and 16:00) over a total duration of 6 weeks. To prevent nutrient leaching and ensure immediate, complete ingestion, a fractionated feeding protocol was implemented: each meal was divided into three micro-portions delivered at 1-min intervals. The daily feeding rate was strictly maintained at 6% of the total body weight for the initial two weeks and progressively increased to 10% from the third week onwards to accommodate developmental energetic demands. To maintain experimental stocking density, any mortalities occurring during the first two weeks were immediately replaced with size-matched, acclimatized zebrafish. After this two-week window, mortalities were exclusively weighed and recorded without further replacement, and the daily feed rations for the affected tanks were dynamically adjusted based on the updated remaining biomass. Detailed feed intake data are provided in the Supp. Table 5.

Synergistic intervention trial (thermally processed tubers)

Following the establishment of the baseline constraints, a secondary 8-week feeding trial was conducted using an identical density and culture apparatus (initial weight approximately 1.19 g/20 fish) to evaluate the thermally processed diets (C-HQP and C-C88 groups) against the wheat control, establishing a direct comparison of metabolic recovery and growth performance. All experimental design details and feeding protocols were strictly identical to those described in the baseline raw tuber assessment.

For both trials, standard water quality parameters were maintained: temperature 26–28 °C, dissolved oxygen ≥ 6.0 mg/L, ammonia nitrogen < 0.3 mg/L, and nitrite < 0.02 mg/L. Following a 24-h starvation period at the conclusion of each trial, survival rates, WGR, and FCR were quantified to evaluate overall phenotypic performance.

GF zebrafish derivation and mechanistic interventions

To decouple the physiological impacts of the gut microbiota and physical carbohydrate structures from endogenous phytochemical effects, GF zebrafish models were employed.

Microbiota engraftment

Intestinal contents from conventionally raised zebrafish in the baseline trial were aseptically collected 4–6 h post-feeding. The contents (from 5 fish per group) were suspended in 3 mL PBS, agitated, and the supernatant was isolated. The suspension was standardized to a final concentration of 10⁶ CFU/mL and inoculated into sterile GF culture flasks (30 mL GZM media containing 30 GF larvae at 4 days post-fertilization, dpf). A sham-inoculated GF control was maintained in parallel. Following 72 h of colonization, subjects (n = 6/group) were harvested for transcriptional analysis of mucosal immune parameters.

Aqueous fractionation and matrix exposure

To evaluate the isolated impact of the physical starch matrix in the absence of soluble ANFs, aqueous extraction was performed. Fresh HQP and C88 (25 g) were homogenized, washed repeatedly with distilled water, filtered through a 40-mesh sieve, precipitated, and freeze-dried to obtain purified potato starches. GF zebrafish larvae (5 dpf) were randomly allocated and fed either purified HQP starch or C88 starch twice daily for 7 days (n = 6/group). Subsequently, total RNA was extracted to evaluate the expression of inflammatory and stress-response genes.

Host-associated microbiome profiling

To evaluate diet-induced microbial compositional changes, intestinal contents were collected 4–6 h post-feeding from the baseline trial (n = 6/group). Total genomic DNA was extracted using the Fast DNA SPIN Kit (Tiangen, Beijing). The hypervariable V3–V4 region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified and subjected to high-throughput sequencing on the Illumina HiSeq platform (Beijing Biomic Biotechnology Co., Ltd.), following established protocols49 to characterize compositional shifts and identify potential keystone taxa.

Histopathological and intestinal barrier assessments

Following the baseline feeding trial and a 24-h fast, randomly selected fish were anesthetized (MS-222, 20 mg/L) and dissected. Hepatic and hindgut tissues were excised, dehydrated in a graded ethanol series, and embedded in paraffin (n = 3/group). Sections were stained with&& H&E to evaluate structural integrity and structural alterations. Morphological parameters, specifically the ratio of villus height to circumference, were quantified using K-Viewer imaging software.

Biochemical and immunological assays

At the conclusion of the feeding trials (12 h post-final feeding), fish were anesthetized (MS-222) for tissue collection (n = 6/group) to evaluate biochemical parameters (n = 6–8/group). Tissues were homogenized and analyzed using commercial assay kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Digestive efficiency

Foregut amylase and lipase activities were quantified to assess host nutrient assimilation capacity.

Oxidative stress parameters

Hepatic and intestinal tissues were assayed for T-AOC, SOD, catalase (CAT), and glutathione reductase (GR) activities, alongside MDA content, to assess the oxidative status and lipid peroxidation status.

Immunological parameters

Lysozyme (LZM), complement component 3 (C3), complement component 4 (C4), alkaline phosphatase (AKP), acid phosphatase (ACP), IgM, and immunoglobulin Z (IgZ) levels were measured to evaluate innate immune activation and immune response.

Molecular profiling of the nrf2/mTOR/nf-κb regulatory axis

To analyze the hierarchical molecular networks associated with metabolic and inflammatory phenotypes, both transcriptional and translational profiling were conducted on host hepatic and intestinal tissues (n = 6–8/group).

Transcriptional analysis (qPCR): total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol reagent, quantified, and reverse-transcribed into cDNA (Tiangen Biochemistry, Beijing). Quantitative real-time PCR was executed using targeted primers (Supp. Table 2) to evaluate the relative expression of genes governing antioxidant defense (nrf2, keap1, ho-1, Cuzn-sod, Mn-sod, cat, gxtr), the inflammatory signaling pathway (nf-κb, tnf-α, il-1β, il-10, tgf-β), and epithelial barrier integrity (occludin, claudin) using the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Translational/protein analysis (ELISA): because the mTOR nutrient-sensing network is fundamentally regulated at the protein and post-translational level, the functional activation of this synthetic axis was directly quantified via targeted ELISA. Tissue homogenates were assayed using ELISA kits to determine the protein abundances of mTOR and its critical downstream translational effectors, p-4E-BP1 and p-S6K1.

Western blotting (WB): to evaluate the total protein abundance of NRF2 and NF-κB P65, liver tissues were homogenized in ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with 1 mM PMSF and phosphatase inhibitors. After centrifugation at 12,000×g for 15 min at 4 °C, the supernatant containing the whole-cell lysate was collected. Protein concentrations were quantified using a BCA Protein Assay Kit (Pierce, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Approximately 40 μg of total protein from each sample was resolved on 10% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred onto 0.45 μm PVDF membranes (Millipore). Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against NRF2 (Abcam, ab137550, 1:1000) and NF-κB P65 (P76367R1M, 1:1000). GAPDH (Sigma, SAB2708126, 1:2000) was used as the endogenous loading control. After incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (CWBIO, 1:1000), target bands were visualized via enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL). The relative protein abundance was quantified by densitometry using ImageJ software.

In vivo pathogen challenge trial

To evaluate the functional consequence of the diet-induced survival, an acute pathogenic challenge was conducted utilizing dual Aeromonas strains. Following the conclusion of the feeding trial and a 24-h fast, zebrafish were subjected to an immersion challenge with Aeromonas veronii (Hm091, final concentration 2.21 × 10⁹ CFU/mL) or Aeromonas hydrophila (NJ-1, final concentration 2.83 × 10⁹ CFU/mL), based on prior optimization50. The challenge comprised three replicates per dietary treatment (7 fish/replicate) (n = 3/group) and proceeded for 72 h without feeding. Mortality was recorded at 6-h intervals to determine host survival against opportunistic infections.

Statistical analysis and replication

To ensure statistical rigor and avoid pseudo-replication, the independent biological unit of replication was strictly defined prior to data analysis. For all growth performance, survival, and feed utilization metrics, the experimental tank served as the independent biological unit (n = 4 per dietary treatment). For downstream molecular, biochemical, and microbiome analyses, targeted tissues (e.g., liver, intestine) from randomly selected zebrafish within each independent replicate tank were pooled to constitute a single distinct biological sample, yielding n = 4 (or n = 6 for specific analytical subsets, as detailed in the respective figure legends) independent biological replicates per group. Throughout the manuscript, all reported ‘n’ values represent true, independent biological replicates rather than technical instrumental iterations.

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The results were expressed as mean ± standard error of the means (mean ± SEM). One-way analysis of variance was used to analyze the differences between the data of each group. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Bioinformatic visualizations, including heatmaps and volcanic plots, were generated using the OmicStudio tools (https://www.omicstudio.cn/tool). To evaluate the comparative performance of C-HQP versus C-C88, multi-dimensional radar charts were constructed incorporating key physiological metrics (Survival, WGR, Amylase, Solanine reduction, MDA, and nf-κb expression) normalized to their respective maximum values, providing a holistic visualization of metabolic optimization.
