Algebraic aspects of unconditional lattice polytopes
Kenta Mori, Ryo Motomura, Hidefumi Ohsugi and Akiyoshi Tsuchiya

Abstract
. Unconditional polytopes are convex polytopes that are symmetric with respect to all coordinate hyperplanes and arise naturally from anti-blocking polytopes by reflection. This paper investigates algebraic relations between an anti-blocking lattice polytope and its associated unconditional lattice polytope. We prove that the toric ring of an anti-blocking lattice polytope is normal if and only if the toric ring of the associated unconditional lattice polytope is normal. We also show that the toric ideal of an anti-blocking lattice polytope is generated by quadratic binomials if and only if the same holds for the associated unconditional lattice polytope. As an application, we obtain a graph-theoretic characterization of quadratic generation of symmetric stable set ideals.

1.
Introduction

A
lattice polytope
is a convex polytope all of whose vertices have integer coordinates.
A lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
of dimension
n
is called
anti-blocking
if for any
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathcal{P}
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\leq x_{i}\leq y_{i}
for all
i
, it holds that
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Anti-blocking polytopes were studied and named by Fulkerson
[
2
,
3
]
in the context of combinatorial optimization, but they are also known as convex corners or down-closed polytopes; see, for example,
[
1
]
.
Typical examples include stable set polytopes of graphs and chain polytopes of posets.

For
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

\varepsilon\mathbf{x}:=(\varepsilon_{1}x_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}x_{n}).

Given an anti-blocking lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
of dimension
n
, we define

\mathcal{P}^{\pm}:=\{\varepsilon\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n},\ \mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}\}.

Since
\mathcal{P}
is anti-blocking,
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
is a convex lattice polytope.
Moreover, for any
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
, one has
\varepsilon\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
.
A lattice polytope
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension
n
is called
unconditional
if

\varepsilon\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{Q}
for every
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Thus
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
is an unconditional lattice polytope naturally associated with
\mathcal{P}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
is symmetric with respect to all coordinate hyperplanes, and hence the origin
\mathbf{0}
belongs to the interior of
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
.
Unconditional convex bodies form a classical class in convex geometry.
From the viewpoint of lattice polytopes, unconditional polytopes have recently been studied in connection with combinatorics and commutative algebra.
For example, Kohl, Olsen and Sanyal investigated unconditional reflexive polytopes and characterized them in terms of perfect graphs
[
5
]
.
Moreover, unconditional polytopes are closely related to locally anti-blocking polytopes, whose
h^{*}
-polynomials and
\gamma
-positivity have been studied
[
6
]
.
These developments motivate the study of algebraic properties of unconditional lattice polytopes arising from anti-blocking lattice polytopes.

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate algebraic relations between an anti-blocking lattice polytope and its associated unconditional lattice polytope. More precisely, we study to what extent algebraic properties of the toric rings and toric ideals of these two polytopes are shared by each other. We show that this is indeed the case for normality and quadraticity.

Let
K[{\bf t}^{\pm 1},s]=K[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1},s]
be the Laurent polynomial ring in
n+1
variables over a field
K
.
If
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{m}\}
, then the
toric ring
of
\mathcal{P}
is the subalgebra
K[\mathcal{P}]
of
K[{\bf t}^{\pm 1},s]
generated by

\{{\bf t}^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}s,\dots,{\bf t}^{\mathbf{a}_{m}}s\}
.
Let
K[\mathbf{x}]=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
be the polynomial ring in
m
variables over
K
.
The
toric ideal

I_{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{P}
is the kernel of the surjective homomorphism

\pi:K[\mathbf{x}]\rightarrow K[\mathcal{P}]

defined by
\pi(x_{i})={\bf t}^{\mathbf{a}_{i}}s
for
1\leq i\leq m
.
See, e.g.,
[
4
]
for details on toric rings and toric ideals.

Our first main result concerns normality of toric rings.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope.
Then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is normal if and only if
K[\mathcal{P}^{\pm}]
is normal.

Our second main result concerns quadratic generation of toric ideals.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope.
Then
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is generated by quadratic binomials if and only if
I_{\mathcal{P}^{\pm}}
is generated by quadratic binomials.

As an application, we obtain a graph-theoretic characterization of quadratic generation of symmetric stable set ideals.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce locally anti-blocking lattice polytopes and prove a more general normality result in this setting, from which Theorem
1.1
follows immediately.
Section
3
is devoted to quadratic generation.
In Subsection
3.1
, we prove the implication from
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
to
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
.
In Subsection
3.2
, we prove the converse implication from
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
to
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.
Finally, in Subsection
3.3
, we apply Theorem
1.2
to symmetric stable set polytopes and obtain a characterization in terms of Kempe equivalence.

2.
Normality of the toric rings of locally anti-blocking polytopes

In this section, we prove a more general normality result for locally anti-blocking lattice polytopes, from which Theorem
1.1
follows immediately.

We begin by recalling the integer decomposition property.
A lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to have the
integer decomposition property
(
IDP
, for short) if for any integer
t\geq 1
and any lattice point
\mathbf{x}\in t\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, there exist lattice points

\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{t}\in\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{x}_{t}.

Here
t{\mathcal{P}}:=\{t\alpha:\alpha\in{\mathcal{P}}\}
.
A lattice polytope which has the integer decomposition property is called
IDP
.
We also recall the notion of spanning.
A lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
spanning
if the lattice points in
\mathcal{P}
affinely generate
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
In general, normality and IDP are not equivalent.
However, for any spanning lattice polytope
{\mathcal{P}}
, the toric ring
K[\mathcal{P}]
is normal if and only if
\mathcal{P}
is IDP.
Since every full-dimensional anti-blocking lattice polytope is spanning, this equivalence applies to the polytopes considered in this paper.

We now recall the notion of locally anti-blocking lattice polytopes.
For
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, let

\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\varepsilon_{i}\geq 0\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq n\}

denote the closed orthant corresponding to
\varepsilon
.
A lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension
n
is called
locally anti-blocking
if, for each
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, there exists an anti-blocking lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
of dimension
n
, which is called the
anti-blocking piece
of
{\mathcal{P}}
corresponding to
\varepsilon
, such that

\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}.

In particular, every unconditional lattice polytope is locally anti-blocking.
This class provides a natural common framework containing both anti-blocking and unconditional lattice polytopes.
For a locally anti-blocking lattice polytope
\mathcal{P}
, each anti-blocking piece
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
is full-dimensional, hence spanning.
Therefore, in our setting, the normality statement is reduced to the corresponding statement for IDP.

We will use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be lattice polytopes such that

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{P}_{r}.

If each
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is IDP, then
\mathcal{P}
is also IDP.

We are now in a position to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a locally anti-blocking lattice polytope of dimension
n
.
Then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is normal if and only if the toric ring
K[\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}]

of the anti-blocking piece
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}

is normal for each
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.

Proof.

Assume first that each
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
is IDP.
Since each
\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}
is unimodularly equivalent to
\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}
,

\mathcal{P}=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left(\mathcal{P}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}\right)

is IDP by Lemma
2.1
.

Suppose that some
{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
is not IDP.
We may assume that
\varepsilon=(1,\ldots,1)
, and hence

{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\subset{\mathcal{P}}
.
Then there exists
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in t{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with an integer
t\geq 2

such that there exist no
{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with

{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{x}}_{1}+\dots+{\mathbf{x}}_{t}.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{P}}
is IDP.
Then since
{\mathbf{x}}\in t{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\subset t{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, there exist
t
lattice points
{\mathbf{y}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{y}}_{t}
in
{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with

{\mathbf{x}}={\mathbf{y}}_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbf{y}}_{t}.

For each
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}
, we write
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}=(y_{i1},\ldots,y_{in})
.

If
y_{ik}>0
and
y_{jk}<0
for some
1\leq i,j\leq t
and some
1\leq k\leq n
, then
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{j}+{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
,
where
{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
is the
k
-th unit coordinate vector, belong to
{\mathcal{P}}
since
{\mathcal{P}}
is locally anti-blocking.
Then we can replace
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{j}
with
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
and
{\mathbf{y}}_{j}+{\mathbf{e}}_{k}
, respectively, since

{\mathbf{y}}_{i}+{\mathbf{y}}_{j}=({\mathbf{y}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{k})+({\mathbf{y}}_{j}+{\mathbf{e}}_{k}).

By performing this operation repeatedly, we may assume that for any
1\leq i<j\leq t
and any
1\leq k\leq n
,

y_{ik}y_{jk}\geq 0.

Since
{\mathbf{x}}\in t{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
,

0\leq x_{k}=y_{1k}+\cdots+y_{tk}.

Hence each
y_{ik}
is nonnegative.
Therefore each
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}
belongs to

{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}={\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\cap{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}={\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon},

and thus

{\mathbf{y}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{y}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\varepsilon}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n},

a contradiction.
Thus
{\mathcal{P}}
is not IDP.
∎

Theorem
1.1
follows by applying Theorem
2.2
to the unconditional polytope
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
, whose anti-blocking pieces are all unimodularly equivalent to
\mathcal{P}
.

3.
Quadratic generation

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

3.1.
Ascending quadratic generation from
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
to
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}

Two vectors
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}),{\mathbf{b}}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are called
separable
if

a_{i}b_{i}<0

for some
1\leq i\leq n
. If
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is an anti-blocking lattice polytope and
{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
are separable, then there exist
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
\varepsilon({\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}})={\mathbf{p}}+{\mathbf{q}}
.

The following proposition may be viewed as a generator version of
[
5
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope.
Assume that
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is generated by
the set of binomials
{\mathcal{F}}
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm}
be the set consisting of the binomials

\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{b}}_{i}}

for any
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{{\mathbf{b}}_{i}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, and

x_{{\mathbf{a}}}x_{{\mathbf{b}}}-x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{p}}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{q}}}

for any separable
{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that
\varepsilon({\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}})={\mathbf{p}}+{\mathbf{q}}
and
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
Then
I_{{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\pm}}
is generated by
{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm}\subset I_{{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\pm}}
.
Let

f=\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon_{i}\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\beta_{i}}\in I_{{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\pm}},

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,
and
\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
We show that
f
is generated by
{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm}
.
Suppose that
\varepsilon_{j_{1}}\alpha_{j_{1}},\varepsilon_{j_{2}}\alpha_{j_{2}}
are separable
for some
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq r
.
Let

f^{\prime}=x_{\varepsilon_{j_{1}}\alpha_{j_{1}}}x_{\varepsilon_{j_{2}}\alpha_{j_{2}}}-x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{p}}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{q}}},

where

\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
such that
\varepsilon(\varepsilon_{j_{1}}\alpha_{j_{1}}+\varepsilon_{j_{2}}\alpha_{j_{2}})={\mathbf{p}}+{\mathbf{q}}
and
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
Then
f^{\prime}
belongs to
{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm}
, and

x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{p}}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{q}}}\prod_{i\neq j_{1},j_{2}}x_{\varepsilon_{i}\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}\beta_{i}}=f-f^{\prime}\prod_{i\neq j_{1},j_{2}}x_{\varepsilon_{i}\alpha_{i}}\in I_{{{\mathcal{P}}}^{\pm}}.

We may consider this binomial instead of
f
.
Repeating the same argument finite times,
we may assume that

f=\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\varepsilon\beta_{i}},

for some
\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Then
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\beta_{i}}
belongs to
I_{\mathcal{P}}
.
If
\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\alpha_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{\beta_{i}}

is generated by

\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}x_{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}^{(1)}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}x_{{\mathbf{b}}_{i}^{(1)}},\ \ \ldots\ \ ,\ \ \prod_{i=1}^{r_{s}}x_{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}^{(s)}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r_{s}}x_{{\mathbf{b}}_{i}^{(s)}}\in{\mathcal{F}},

then
f
is generated by

\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{a}}_{i}^{(1)}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r_{1}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{b}}_{i}^{(1)}},\ \ \ldots\ \ ,\ \ \prod_{i=1}^{r_{s}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{a}}_{i}^{(s)}}-\prod_{i=1}^{r_{s}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{b}}_{i}^{(s)}}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\pm},

as desired.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the “only if” part of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope.
If
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is generated by quadratic binomials, then so is
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
.

3.2.
Descending quadratic generation from
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
to
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope, and let
A=\mathcal{P}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
Let

K[{\mathbf{x}}^{\pm}]=K[x_{\xi}:\xi\in A^{\pm}]

and

K[{\mathbf{x}}]=K[x_{{\mathbf{a}}}:{\mathbf{a}}\in A]

be polynomial rings
over a field
K
.
Then

I_{{\mathcal{P}}}=I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}\cap K[{\mathbf{x}}]\subset I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}\subset K[{\mathbf{x}}^{\pm}].

For an ordered list

\Lambda=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r},\varepsilon_{r})),

where
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}=(a_{j,1},\dots,a_{j,n})\in A
and
\varepsilon_{j}=(\varepsilon_{j,1},\dots,\varepsilon_{j,n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
, define

\omega(\Lambda)=(\omega_{1}(\Lambda),\dots,\omega_{n}(\Lambda)):=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\varepsilon_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Assume that

\omega(\Lambda)
is nonnegative.
For each
k
-th entry
\omega_{k}(\Lambda)
(
\geq 0
) of
\omega(\Lambda)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, define an ordered list

P_{k}(\Lambda):=(1,\dots,1,2,\dots,2,\dots,r,\dots,r),

where each
j\in[r]
appears

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}a_{j,k}&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{j,k}=1,\\
\\
0&\mbox{if }\varepsilon_{j,k}=-1\end{array}\right.

times in
P_{k}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

With the notation above,

\omega_{k}(\Lambda)
is less than or equal to
the length of
P_{k}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be the length of
P_{k}(\Lambda)
.
By construction, we have

\ell=\sum_{j\;:\;\varepsilon_{j,k}=1}a_{j,k}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\varepsilon_{j,k}a_{j,k}=\omega_{k}(\Lambda).

∎

For each
j\in[r]
, let
c_{j,k}(\Lambda)
be the number of times that
j
appears among the
first
\omega_{k}(\Lambda)
entries of
P_{k}(\Lambda)
.

Example 3.4
.

If

(\varepsilon_{1,k}a_{1,k},\varepsilon_{2,k}a_{2,k},\varepsilon_{3,k}a_{3,k},\varepsilon_{4,k}a_{4,k})=(+2,-1,+3,0),

then

P_{k}(\Lambda)=(1,1,3,3,3).

On the other hand,
\omega_{k}(\Lambda)=4
and the first four entries of
P_{k}(\Lambda)
is
(1,1,3,3)
, and hence

(c_{1,k}(\Lambda),c_{2,k}(\Lambda),c_{3,k}(\Lambda),c_{4,k}(\Lambda))=(2,0,2,0)
.

Now define

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda):=(c_{j,1}(\Lambda),\ldots,c_{j,n}(\Lambda))\qquad(1\leq j\leq r),

and set

\nu(\Lambda):=({\mathbf{c}}_{1}(\Lambda),\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r}(\Lambda)).

Lemma 3.5
.

With the notation above, one has

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)\in A\qquad(1\leq j\leq r),

and

\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)=\omega(\Lambda).

Moreover,

\mathbf{0}\leq{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)\leq{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\qquad\text{coordinatewise}\qquad(1\leq j\leq r).

Proof.

Fix a coordinate
k\in[n]
.
For each
j\in[r]
, the number
c_{j,k}(\Lambda)
counts how many copies of
j
are selected
from
P_{k}(\Lambda)
.
Therefore

0\leq c_{j,k}(\Lambda)\leq a_{j,k},

i.e.,

\mathbf{0}\leq{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)\leq{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\qquad\text{coordinatewise}.

Since
{\mathcal{P}}
is anti-blocking and
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\in{\mathcal{P}}
, we obtain

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}=A.

Finally, since the equality

\sum_{j=1}^{r}c_{j,k}(\Lambda)=\omega_{k}(\Lambda)

holds for every coordinate
k
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)=\omega(\Lambda),

as desired.
∎

For such an ordered list
\Lambda
,
from Lemma
3.5
,

\nu(\Lambda)=({\mathbf{c}}_{1}(\Lambda),\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r}(\Lambda))
belongs to
A^{r}
.
Hence we can define the monomial

m(\nu(\Lambda)):=x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1}(\Lambda)}\cdots x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{r}(\Lambda)}.

Remark 3.6
.

For an ordered list

\Lambda=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r},\varepsilon))
,
where
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\in A
and

\varepsilon=(1,\ldots,1)
,

\omega(\Lambda)={\mathbf{a}}_{1}+\cdots+{\mathbf{a}}_{r}

and the construction above yields

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}(\Lambda)={\mathbf{a}}_{j}\ (1\leq j\leq r).

Hence

m(\nu(\Lambda))=x_{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\cdots x_{{\mathbf{a}}_{r}}.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r-2},\varepsilon_{r-2}),({\mathbf{a}},\varepsilon),({\mathbf{b}},\delta)),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r-2},\varepsilon_{r-2}),({\mathbf{a}}^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime}),({\mathbf{b}}^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}))

be ordered lists with
{\mathbf{a}}_{j},{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}},{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}\in A
,
\varepsilon_{j},\varepsilon,\delta,\varepsilon^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Suppose that

\omega(\Lambda)=\omega(\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}.

Then

m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

Let

\nu(\Lambda)=({\mathbf{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r-2},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{d}}),\ \ \ \nu(\Lambda^{\prime})=({\mathbf{c}}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r-2}^{\prime},{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{d}}^{\prime}).

Fix a coordinate
k\in[n]
.
Consider the ordered lists
P_{k}(\Lambda)
and
P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
used in the definition of

\nu(\Lambda)
and
\nu(\Lambda^{\prime})
.
The contributions coming from the first
r-2
entries of
\Lambda
,
\Lambda^{\prime}
are identical in both lists and occur
before the last two entries of
\Lambda
,
\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Since

\omega_{k}(\Lambda)=\omega_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
,
the number of copies of the index
j
selected from the first
r-2
entries is the same in both lists
for every
1\leq j\leq r-2
.
Hence

c_{j,k}(\Lambda)=c_{j,k}(\Lambda^{\prime})\qquad(1\leq j\leq r-2).

Since this holds for every coordinate
k
, we obtain

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}={\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}\qquad(1\leq j\leq r-2).

Finally, since

\sum_{j=1}^{r-2}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}+{\mathbf{c}}+{\mathbf{d}}=\omega(\Lambda)=\omega(\Lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{r-2}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{d}}^{\prime},

we have

{\mathbf{c}}+{\mathbf{d}}={\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{d}}^{\prime}
.
Therefore

x_{{\mathbf{c}}}x_{{\mathbf{d}}}-x_{{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}}x_{{\mathbf{d}}^{\prime}}\in I_{{\mathcal{P}}}

and

m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))=x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1}}\cdots x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{r-2}}(x_{{\mathbf{c}}}x_{{\mathbf{d}}}-x_{{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}}x_{{\mathbf{d}}^{\prime}}).

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{p-1},\varepsilon_{p-1}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p},\varepsilon_{p}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p+1},\varepsilon_{p+1}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p+2},\varepsilon_{p+2}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r},\varepsilon_{r})),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{p-1},\varepsilon_{p-1}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p+1},\varepsilon_{p+1}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p},\varepsilon_{p}),({\mathbf{a}}_{p+2},\varepsilon_{p+2}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r},\varepsilon_{r}))

be ordered lists with
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\in A
,
\varepsilon_{j}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
Suppose that
\omega(\Lambda)\ (=\omega(\Lambda^{\prime}))

is nonnegative.
Then

m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

Write

\nu(\Lambda)=({\mathbf{c}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r}),\qquad\nu(\Lambda^{\prime})=({\mathbf{c}}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,{\mathbf{c}}_{r}^{\prime}).

We first show that

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}={\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}\qquad(j\neq p,p+1).

Fix
j
(
\neq p,p+1
), and fix a coordinate
k\in[n]
.
Recall that
c_{j,k}(\Lambda)
is the number of copies of the index
j

among the first
\omega_{k}(\Lambda)
entries of
P_{k}(\Lambda)
, and similarly for

c_{j,k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
.

The ordered lists
P_{k}(\Lambda)
and
P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
differ only in the relative order of
the block of
p
’s and the block of
(p+1)
’s.
In particular, for every
j\neq p,p+1
, the copies of the index
j
occur in exactly the same
positions in
P_{k}(\Lambda)
and in
P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle P_{k}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{m_{1}},\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{m_{2}},\dots,\underbrace{r,\dots,r}_{m_{r}}),

\displaystyle P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n_{1}},\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{n_{2}},\dots,\underbrace{r,\dots,r}_{n_{r}}),

where
m_{j}=n_{j}\geq 0
for
j\neq p,p+1
,
m_{p}=n_{p+1}\geq 0
and
m_{p+1}=n_{p}\geq 0
.
For example, if
p=2
and

P_{k}(\Lambda)=(1,1,2,2,2,3,5,5)
, then

P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})=(1,1,2,3,3,3,5,5)
.

Since

\omega_{k}(\Lambda)=\omega_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime}),

the cutoff position is the same in both lists.
Therefore the number of copies of
j
among the first
\omega_{k}(\Lambda)
entries of

P_{k}(\Lambda)
is equal to the number of copies of
j
among the first
\omega_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})

entries of
P_{k}(\Lambda^{\prime})
, that is,

c_{j,k}(\Lambda)=c_{j,k}(\Lambda^{\prime})\qquad(j\neq p,p+1).

Since this holds for every coordinate
k
, we obtain

{\mathbf{c}}_{j}={\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}\qquad(j\neq p,p+1).

Now

\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}=\omega(\Lambda)=\omega(\Lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}{\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}.

Since
{\mathbf{c}}_{j}={\mathbf{c}}_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j\neq p,p+1
, it follows that

{\mathbf{c}}_{p}+{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}={\mathbf{c}}_{p}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}^{\prime}.

Hence

x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}}-x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}^{\prime}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}^{\prime}}\in I_{{\mathcal{P}}}.

Finally,

m(\nu(\Lambda))=\left(\prod_{j\neq p,p+1}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{j}}\right)x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}},\qquad m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\left(\prod_{j\neq p,p+1}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{j}}\right)x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}^{\prime}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}^{\prime}},

and hence

m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))=\left(\prod_{j\neq p,p+1}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{j}}\right)\bigl(x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}}-x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p}^{\prime}}x_{{\mathbf{c}}_{p+1}^{\prime}}\bigr).

Therefore

m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
be two ordered lists

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\varepsilon_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r},\varepsilon_{r})),

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(({\mathbf{a}}_{1}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,({\mathbf{a}}_{r}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{r}^{\prime}))

with
{\mathbf{a}}_{j},{\mathbf{a}}_{j}^{\prime}\in A
and
\varepsilon_{j},\varepsilon_{j}^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}

such that

x_{\varepsilon_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{r}{\mathbf{a}}_{r}}=x_{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{r}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{r}^{\prime}}.

Suppose that

\omega(\Lambda)\ (=\omega(\Lambda^{\prime}))
is nonnegative.
Then
the binomial
m(\nu(\Lambda))-m(\nu(\Lambda^{\prime}))

in
K[{\mathbf{x}}]

is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.

Proof.

Set
\varepsilon_{i}=(\varepsilon_{i1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{in})
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{in})
.
We may suppose that for
\Lambda
,

\varepsilon_{ij}=1
whenever
a_{ij}=0
, and similarly for

\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Indeed, changing
\varepsilon_{ij}
when
a_{ij}=0
does not change the lattice point

\varepsilon_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
, nor the ordered lists
P_{k}(\Lambda)
, and hence does not change

\omega(\Lambda)
,
\nu(\Lambda)
, or
m(\nu(\Lambda))
.
With this convention, the representation of each lattice point

\varepsilon_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in A^{\pm}
is unique. Hence the assumption

x_{\varepsilon_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{r}{\mathbf{a}}_{r}}=x_{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{r}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{r}^{\prime}}

implies that
\Lambda^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Lambda
by a permutation of the entries.
Since any permutation is a product of adjacent transpositions, the assertion follows from
Lemma
3.8
.
∎

We are now in the position to show the “if” part of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an anti-blocking lattice polytope.
If
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
is generated by quadratic binomials, then so is
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
is generated by quadratic binomials.
Let

u-v=x_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{\alpha_{r}}-x_{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{\beta_{r}}\in I_{{\mathcal{P}}}

be a homogeneous binomial of degree
r
.
Let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{u}

\displaystyle=((\alpha_{1},\varepsilon),\dots,(\alpha_{r},\varepsilon)),

\displaystyle\Lambda_{v}

\displaystyle=((\beta_{1},\varepsilon),\dots,(\beta_{r},\varepsilon)),

where
\varepsilon=(1,\dots,1)
.
Since
u-v
belongs to
I_{\mathcal{P}}\subset K[{\mathbf{x}}]
,
it follows that

\omega(\Lambda_{u})=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{r}=\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{r}=\omega(\Lambda_{v})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}.

By Remark
3.6
, we have

(1)

m(\nu(\Lambda_{u}))=u,\qquad m(\nu(\Lambda_{v}))=v.

Since

u-v\in I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}

and since
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
is generated by quadratic binomials, there exists a sequence of monomials

u=M_{0},M_{1},\ldots,M_{t}=v

of degree
r

such that for each
1\leq k\leq t
, the difference

M_{k-1}-M_{k}

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}
, i.e.,

M_{k-1}-M_{k}=N_{k}(x_{\varepsilon_{k}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}}x_{\delta_{k}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}}-x_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}^{\prime}}x_{\delta_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}^{\prime}})=N_{k}\,x_{\varepsilon_{k}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}}x_{\delta_{k}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}}-N_{k}\,x_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}^{\prime}}x_{\delta_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}^{\prime}}

for some monomial

N_{k}=x_{\varepsilon_{1}^{(k)}\gamma_{1}^{(k)}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{r-2}^{(k)}\gamma_{r-2}^{(k)}}\in K[{\mathbf{x}}^{\pm}]

with

\gamma_{j}^{(k)}\in A
and
\varepsilon_{j}^{(k)}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}

and some quadratic binomial

x_{\varepsilon_{k}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}}x_{\delta_{k}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}}-x_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}^{\prime}}x_{\delta_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{b}}_{k}^{\prime}}\in I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}}

with

{\mathbf{a}}_{k},{\mathbf{b}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{k}^{\prime},{\mathbf{b}}_{k}^{\prime}\in A
and
\varepsilon_{k},\delta_{k},\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime},\delta_{k}^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}.

Let

\Gamma_{k}:=((\gamma_{1}^{(k)},\varepsilon_{1}^{(k)}),\dots,(\gamma_{r-2}^{(k)},\varepsilon_{r-2}^{(k)}))

be the list associated with
N_{k}
.
Define

\Lambda_{k-1}^{(k)}:=(\Gamma_{k},({\mathbf{a}}_{k},\varepsilon_{k}),({\mathbf{b}}_{k},\delta_{k})),\qquad\Lambda_{k}^{(k)}:=(\Gamma_{k},({\mathbf{a}}_{k}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}),({\mathbf{b}}_{k}^{\prime},\delta_{k}^{\prime})).

We may assume that
\Lambda_{u}=\Lambda_{0}^{(1)}

and
\Lambda_{v}=\Lambda_{t}^{(t)}
.
Then

\omega(\Lambda_{k-1}^{(k)})=\omega(\Lambda_{k}^{(k)})=\omega(\Lambda_{u})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}

for all
k
.
Set

P_{k-1}^{(k)}:=m(\nu(\Lambda_{k-1}^{(k)})),\qquad P_{k}^{(k)}:=m(\nu(\Lambda_{k}^{(k)})).

From (
1
), we have

P_{0}^{(1)}=u
and

P_{t}^{(t)}=v.

By Lemma
3.7
, the difference

P_{k-1}^{(k)}-P_{k}^{(k)}

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.
Now fix
k
with
1\leq k\leq t-1
.
The two monomials

P_{k}^{(k)}
and
P_{k}^{(k+1)}

come from two ordered lists

\Lambda_{k}^{(k)}

and

\Lambda_{k}^{(k+1)}

of the same monomial

M_{k}=\prod_{({\mathbf{a}},\varepsilon)\in\Lambda_{k}^{(k)}}x_{\varepsilon{\mathbf{a}}}=\prod_{({\mathbf{a}}^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime})\in\Lambda_{k}^{(k+1)}}x_{\varepsilon^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}}.

Hence, by Corollary
3.9
,

P_{k}^{(k)}-P_{k}^{(k+1)}\in K[{\mathbf{x}}]

is generated by
quadratic binomials in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.

We now concatenate the following chains:

P_{0}^{(1)}\rightsquigarrow P_{1}^{(1)},P_{1}^{(1)}\rightsquigarrow P_{1}^{(2)},P_{1}^{(2)}\rightsquigarrow P_{2}^{(2)},P_{2}^{(2)}\rightsquigarrow P_{2}^{(3)},\dots,P_{t-1}^{(t-1)}\rightsquigarrow P_{t-1}^{(t)},P_{t-1}^{(t)}\rightsquigarrow P_{t}^{(t)}.

Here, for each chain of the form

P_{k-1}^{(k)}\rightsquigarrow P_{k}^{(k)}
,

P_{k-1}^{(k)}-P_{k}^{(k)}

is a monomial multiple of a quadratic binomial in
I_{\mathcal{P}}

by Lemma
3.7
, and for each chain of the form

P_{k}^{(k)}\rightsquigarrow P_{k}^{(k+1)}
,

P_{k}^{(k)}-P_{k}^{(k+1)}

is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{\mathcal{P}}

by Corollary
3.9
.
Thus we obtain a sequence of monomials in
K[{\mathbf{x}}]
from

u=P_{0}^{(1)}

to

v=P_{t}^{(t)}

such that every consecutive difference is
generated by quadratic binomials
in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.
Hence
u-v
belongs to the ideal generated by
quadratic binomials in
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
.
Therefore,
I_{{\mathcal{P}}}
is generated by quadratic binomials.
∎

Theorem
1.2
now follows from Corollary
3.2
and Proposition
3.10
.

3.3.
Application to symmetric stable set polytopes

In this subsection, we apply Theorem
1.2
to symmetric stable set polytopes.

Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with the edge set
E(G)
.
Given a subset
S\subset[n]
,
let
G[S]
denote the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
S
.
A subset
S\subset[n]
is called a
stable set
(or an
independent set
) of
G

if
\{i,j\}\notin E(G)
for all
i,j\in S
with
i\neq j
.
Namely, a subset
S\subset[n]
is stable if and only if
G[S]
is an empty graph.
In particular, the empty set
\emptyset
and any singleton
\{i\}
with
i\in[n]

are stable.
Denote
S(G)=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m}\}
the set of all stable sets of
G
.
Given a subset
S\subset[n]
, we associate the
(0,1)
-vector
\rho(S)=\sum_{j\in S}{\mathbf{e}}_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Note that
\rho(\emptyset)=(0,\ldots,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The
stable set polytope
of
G
is

{\mathcal{P}}_{G}:={\rm conv}\{\rho(S_{1}),\ldots,\rho(S_{m})\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

The stable set polytope
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}
is anti-blocking of dimension
n
.

We call the toric ring
K[G]:=K[{\mathcal{P}}_{G}]
the
stable set ring
of
G
and the toric ideal
I_{G}:=I_{{\mathcal{P}}_{G}}
the
stable set ideal
of
G
.
In
[
7
]
, the authors characterized when
I_{G}
is generated by quadratic binomials by using Kempe equivalence.
We also recall the notion of Kempe equivalence.
A
k
-coloring

f
of a graph
G
is a map from
[n]
to
[k]
such that

f(i)\neq f(j)
for all
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
.
The
chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi(G)
, is the smallest integer
k
for which
G
admits a
k
-coloring.
Given a
k
-coloring
f
of
G
, and
1\leq i<j\leq k
,
let
H
be a connected component of the induced subgraph of
G

on the vertex set
f^{-1}(i)\cup f^{-1}(j)
.
Then we obtain a new
k
-coloring
g
of
G
by setting

g(x)=\begin{cases}f(x)&\text{if }x\notin H,\\
i&\text{if }x\in H\text{ and }f(x)=j,\\
j&\text{if }x\in H\text{ and }f(x)=i.\end{cases}

We say that
g
is obtained from
f
by a
Kempe switching
.
Two
k
-colorings
f
and
g
are called
Kempe equivalent
, denoted by
f\sim_{k}g
, if there exists a sequence
f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}
of
k
-colorings of
G
such that

f_{0}=f,f_{s}=g
,
and
f_{i}
is obtained from
f_{i-1}
by a Kempe switching for each
i
.

Given a graph
G
on the vertex set
[n]
, and

{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
,
let
G_{\mathbf{a}}
be the graph obtained from
G
by replacing each vertex
i\in[n]

with a complete graph
G^{(i)}
of
a_{i}
vertices (if
a_{i}=0
, then just delete the vertex
i
),
and joining all vertices
x\in G^{(i)}
and
y\in G^{(j)}
such that
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G
.
In particular, if
{\mathbf{a}}=(1,\ldots,1)
, then
G_{\mathbf{a}}=G
.
If
{\mathbf{a}}={\bf 0}
, then
G_{\mathbf{a}}
is the null graph (a graph without vertices).
In addition, if
{\mathbf{a}}
is a
(0,1)
-vector, then
G_{\mathbf{a}}
is an induced subgraph of
G
.
If
{\mathbf{a}}
is a positive vector, then
G_{\mathbf{a}}
is called a

replication graph
of
G
.
In general,
G_{\mathbf{a}}
is a replication graph of an induced subgraph of
G
.

The following is a characterization of quadratic generation of stable set ideals by using Kempe equivalence.

Proposition 3.11

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Then
I_{G}
is generated by quadratic binomials if and only if for every
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and
k\geq\chi(G_{{\mathbf{a}}})
, all
k
-colorings of
G_{\mathbf{a}}
are Kempe equivalent.

For a graph
G
on
[n]
, we call the unconditional lattice polytope
{\mathcal{P}}_{G}^{\pm}
the
symmetric stable set polytope
of
G
. Additionally, we call the toric ring
K[G^{\pm}]:=K[{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}_{G}]
the
symmetric stable set ring
of
G
and the toric ideal
I_{G^{\pm}}:=I_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}_{G}}
the
symmetric stable set ideal
of
G
.

Combining Proposition
3.11
with Theorem
1.2
, we immediately obtain Theorem
3.12
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
. Then the following conditions are equivalent
:

(i)

I_{G^{\pm}}
is generated by quadratic binomials
;

(ii)

I_{G}
is generated by quadratic binomials
;

(iii)

for every
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
and
k\geq\chi(G_{{\mathbf{a}}})
,
all
k
-colorings of
G_{\mathbf{a}}
are Kempe equivalent.
