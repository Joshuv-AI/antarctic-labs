Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics: A Unified Framework for Adaptive Learning
Debashis Guha

Abstract
Every adaptive learning system must alternate between two operations: consolidating what it already knows and expanding into new evidence. We propose Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics (OpMech), a framework that makes this structure precise. The central object is the order-gap Ω ⁡ ( θ , e ) \Omega(\theta;e) , the degree to which a consolidation operator Q Q and an expansion operator P e P_{e} fail to commute at a given knowledge state. Because the order-gap is computable from the system’s own trajectory, it serves as a real-time control signal: large values indicate that the system is still sensitive to the ordering of consolidation and expansion; once the order-gap falls and stays small, further processing is unlikely to change the outcome. Three results give the signal precise meaning: the order-gap decays along convergent trajectories; a persistently large order-gap implies the system is far from its settled state; and an order-gap-based stopping rule terminates with provable guarantees in both noiseless and bounded-noise settings. The framework applies across five domains: bandits, reinforcement learning, stochastic optimization, continual learning, and recursive language models. We give conditions under which the order-gap reliably tracks convergence in three representative cases. We develop the recursive language model application in detail, showing how OpMech replaces heuristic stopping rules and fixed recursion budgets with principled, evidence-driven alternatives.



Keywords.

adaptive learning; recursive reasoning; iterative refinement; non-commuting operators;
bandits; reinforcement learning; recursive language models; long context;
stochastic optimization; continual learning.

1
Introduction

The tension between knowledge consolidation and knowledge expansion, known as
the exploitation-exploration dilemma in reinforcement learning, is among
the oldest problems in adaptive systems. In multi-armed bandits, the learner
must weigh the arm with the highest estimated reward against trying less-known
ones. In reinforcement learning, the agent must balance policy improvement
against continued evaluation. In stochastic gradient descent, the optimiser
must choose between refining the current basin and escaping to a better one.
In continual learning, the system must protect what it has already learned
while remaining open to new tasks. In recursive language models, the reasoner
must decide between consolidating an answer from evidence already gathered
and recursing into further context.

These problems are typically studied in isolation, each with its own
formalism and analysis tools. Yet beneath all of them lies the same structure:
two operations (one that acts on current knowledge, one that incorporates
new evidence) applied in sequence, with their ordering shaping the learning
trajectory.

This paper proposes
Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics
(OpMech),
a framework that makes this shared structure explicit. The central
observation is:

In every adaptive learning system, consolidation and expansion are
non-commuting operations on the learner’s knowledge state. The degree to which
their ordering matters is a measurable, trajectory-dependent quantity, the
order-gap; this quantity can be used as a control signal for adaptive
algorithms.

The framework consists of three elements:

1.

A
stochastic dynamical system
on a knowledge state space
\mathcal{X}
,
driven by two operators: consolidation
Q
and expansion
P_{e}
, with
canonical dynamics
\theta_{t+1}=Q\circ P_{e_{t}}(\theta_{t})
.

2.

The
order-gap

\Omega(\theta;e)=\left\lVert Q(P_{e}(\theta))-P_{e}(Q(\theta))\right\rVert
, which measures the effect on downstream decisions of
reordering the two operations.

3.

An
algorithmic principle
: track the order-gap and use it to
modulate the learning system’s behaviour, expand more when
\Omega
is
large, consolidate more when it is small.

Our contributions are:

•

4.6
,
Proposition

The OpMech Framework

2.1
Knowledge State

Definition 2.1

(Knowledge state)
.

The
knowledge state
of an adaptive learner is a point
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
,
where
\mathcal{X}
is a state space encoding everything the learner currently knows.

The precise structure of
\mathcal{X}
depends on the domain:

Domain

Knowledge state
\theta

Multi-armed bandits

Sufficient statistics
\{(\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2},n_{a})\}_{a=1}^{K}

Stochastic gradient descent

Model parameters + optimiser state

Reinforcement learning

Value function parameters
\theta
of
Q_{\theta}(s,a)

Continual learning

Task-specific parameters across all tasks seen

Recursive language models

Aggregated state
S
over chunks processed so far

2.2
Two Operators

The learner’s trajectory through
\mathcal{X}
is governed by two operators.

Definition 2.2

(Expansion operator)
.

For an event
e\in\mathcal{E}
, the
expansion operator

P_{e}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
is defined by
P_{e}(\theta)=U_{e}(\theta)
,
where
U_{e}
is the evidence update corresponding to event
e
.

An
event
is whatever arrives from outside the learner: a reward
observation in bandits, a sampled minibatch in SGD, a transition in RL,
a chunk of context in recursive reasoning. The
key property is that
P_{e}
brings information into
\theta
that was not
already there. The subscript
e
matters: the family
\{P_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E}\}
is indexed by the event because a different event induces a different
update, and this distinction is what makes the expected commutator
Jacobian
\mathbb{E}_{e}[AB_{e}-B_{e}A]
non-trivial, with each
e
contributing a distinct

B_{e}=DP_{e}(\theta^{\star})
.

Definition 2.3

(Consolidation operator)
.

The
consolidation operator

Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
refines
\theta
toward the
greedy optimum using only information already present in
\theta
. No external
evidence enters.

The defining properties of
Q
are:

1.

Endogenous
:
Q
depends only on
\theta
, not on any event
e
.

2.

Contractive
:
Q
concentrates the state around the current
greedy optimum, reducing uncertainty about the optimal action.

3.

Fixed-point structure
: the fixed points of
Q
are knowledge
states where pure consolidation is self-consistent.

The asymmetry between the two operators is fundamental:
P_{e}
requires
external input;
Q
does not.

2.3
Dynamics

Definition 2.4

(Stochastic dynamical system)
.

The
OpMech dynamics
are the discrete-time Markov chain on
\mathcal{X}
with
canonical trajectory

\theta_{t+1}=Q\circ P_{e_{t}}(\theta_{t}),\qquad e_{t}\sim P(\cdot\mid\theta_{t}),

(1)

and transition kernel

\mathbb{P}(\theta_{t+1}\in A\mid\theta_{t})=\int_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{1}_{A}\bigl(Q(U_{e}(\theta_{t}))\bigr)\,P(de\mid\theta_{t}).

(2)

At each step, the learner expands (incorporates evidence from event
e_{t}
) and
then consolidates (refines toward the greedy optimum). The randomness
comes from
e_{t}
, which may depend on the current knowledge
state (as in bandits, where the arm-selection policy depends on
\theta
) or
may be independent of it (as in SGD, where the minibatch is drawn from a fixed
distribution).

2.4
The Order-Gap

Definition 2.5

(Order-gap)
.

The
order-gap
at state
\theta
given event
e
is

\Omega(\theta;e)=d\bigl(Q(P_{e}(\theta)),\;P_{e}(Q(\theta))\bigr),

(3)

where
d
is a metric on
\mathcal{X}
. When
\mathcal{X}
is a vector space this reduces to

\Omega(\theta;e)=\left\lVert Q(P_{e}(\theta))-P_{e}(Q(\theta))\right\rVert.

The order-gap measures the distance between two trajectories: one where the
learner expands before consolidating, and one where it consolidates before
expanding. When
\Omega
is large, the ordering matters; the learning system
has not settled. When
\Omega
is small, the ordering is nearly
irrelevant; the system has converged.

Decision-facing order-gap.

If a decision map
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
is specified and
d_{\mathcal{Z}}
is a metric on
\mathcal{Z}
,
the order-gap at the decision level is:

\Omega^{\pi}(\theta;e)=d_{\mathcal{Z}}\!\bigl(\pi(Q(P_{e}(\theta))),\;\pi(P_{e}(Q(\theta)))\bigr).

(4)

When
\mathcal{Z}
is a vector space, this reduces to the norm of the difference.

2.5
Key Properties

Non-commutativity is generically present.

In nontrivial adaptive systems, consolidation and expansion generically need
not commute:
Q
contracts toward a fixed point that depends on the current
state, while
P_{e}
moves the state, so the two operations interact
differently depending on their order. The order-gap measures the extent to
which they fail to commute along the trajectory. Non-stationarity makes the
order-gap
persistently large
, but the non-commutativity is a property
of the operator pair, not a consequence of the environment.

Convergence under stationarity (noiseless case).

In the noiseless stationary regime (
\sigma=0
, see Assumption
4.3
):

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})\bigr]\to 0\quad\text{as }t\to\infty.

(5)

In noisy stationary regimes (
\sigma>0
), the expected order-gap is bounded
by a noise-floor term quantified in Section
4.6
.
This is not the same as
[Q,P_{e}]=0
; the operators remain structurally
non-commutative. Only their expected trajectory impact shrinks.

Persistence under non-stationarity.

When the environment changes,
\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})]
remains
large or grows. The order-gap detects that the learning system has not settled
relative to the shifting environment.

3
The Algorithmic Principle

The core claim of OpMech is not that the order-gap exists (it trivially does
for any pair of non-commuting maps), but that
\Omega
is the right
quantity to
track and control
in adaptive systems.

The algorithmic principle is:

Compute the order-gap
\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})
along the learning
trajectory. Use it as a control signal: expand more when
\Omega
is large,
consolidate more when
\Omega
is small.

This principle takes three concrete forms:

Adaptive exploration rate.

Let
\eta_{t}
be the exploration rate at step
t
. Set

\eta_{t}=\phi(\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t}))
, where
\phi
is a monotone increasing
function. When the order-gap is large, the ordering of consolidation and
expansion matters greatly: the learner has not settled, and it should expand
more. When the order-gap is small, the learner has nearly converged, so it
should consolidate.

Consolidation scheduling.

Rather than consolidating at every step, consolidate when the accumulated
order-gap since the last consolidation exceeds a threshold
c
:

\sum_{i=\tau}^{t}\Omega(\theta_{i};e_{i})>c,

where
\tau
is the last consolidation step.

Stopping criterion.

Declare convergence when a windowed average of
\Omega
falls below a
threshold
\varepsilon
. Section
4
establishes formal
convergence and stopping guarantees for the order-gap signal. The adaptive
exploration and consolidation-scheduling rules above are algorithmic design
principles motivated by this theory; their performance guarantees are
domain-specific and developed in companion work.

4
Convergence, Bounds, and Stopping Guarantees

The algorithmic principle of Section
3
rests on three
claims that need formal backing: that the order-gap decays along convergent
trajectories, that a persistently large order-gap signals genuine
suboptimality, and that the stopping rule
\Omega<\varepsilon
terminates
in finite time with bounded error. This section establishes each of these.

4.1
Setting and Assumptions

Scope.

The formal results in this section are proved first for the case where
events
e_{t}
are drawn independently from a fixed distribution
P
on

(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
, independent of
\theta_{t}
; we refer to this as the

fixed-sampling setting
. This covers independent evidence streams and
settings where the sampling policy is held fixed. When the event distribution
depends on the current state (as in fully adaptive bandits or off-policy
RL), Section
4.8
extends the analysis to the

state-dependent setting
under uniform conditional moment bounds; the
contraction and stopping theorems carry over verbatim, because the
underlying concentration tool is martingale concentration on residuals

\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})-\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}]
,
which preserves its martingale-difference structure under state-dependent
sampling.

We work on a Banach space
(\mathcal{X},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
. The operators
Q:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}

and
\{P_{e}:e\in\mathcal{E}\}
generate the dynamics of Equation (
1
),
with events
e_{t}
drawn i.i.d. from
P
. Denote

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(e_{0},\ldots,e_{t-1})
.

Assumption 4.1

(Contractive consolidation)
.

There exists
\rho\in[0,1)
such that

\left\lVert Q(\theta_{1})-Q(\theta_{2})\right\rVert\leq\rho\left\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right\rVert

for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
.

By the Banach fixed-point theorem,
Q
admits a unique fixed point

\theta^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}
, the
consolidation fixed point
.

Assumption 4.2

(Lipschitz expansion)
.

There exists
L\geq 0
such that

\left\lVert P_{e}(\theta_{1})-P_{e}(\theta_{2})\right\rVert\leq L\,\left\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right\rVert

for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
and all
e\in\mathcal{E}
.

Assumption 4.3

(Equilibrium regularity)
.

The random variable
W_{e}:=\left\lVert P_{e}(\theta^{\star})-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert

satisfies
\mathbb{E}[W_{e}]\leq\sigma
for some
\sigma\geq 0
. For
high-probability statements, we further assume
W_{e}\leq M
almost surely,
for some
M\geq\sigma
.

The case
\sigma=0
implies
W_{e}=0
a.s., i.e.,

P_{e}(\theta^{\star})=\theta^{\star}
almost surely, placing us in the

noiseless regime
. This regime admits deterministic
(pathwise) bounds.

Assumption 4.4

(Local order-gap sensitivity)
.

There exist
r>0
and
\mu>0
such that, for all
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
with

\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq r
,

\mathbb{E}_{e}\bigl[\Omega(\theta;e)\bigr]\;\geq\;\mu\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert.

Informally: within a neighbourhood of
\theta^{\star}
of radius
r
, the
expected order-gap is bounded below by a positive multiple of the distance to
the fixed point, so a large order-gap reliably signals that the system has not
settled. In the noisy regime (
\sigma>0
in
Assumption
4.3
with
W_{e}\leq M
a.s.), the validity radius
must satisfy
r>\rho M/(1-\gamma)
— the asymptotic radius of the
trajectory’s noise ball, established in Lemma
4.13
below —
otherwise the trajectory need not remain within the validity region and the
stopping guarantees do not bind. This is the non-degeneracy condition used
by the stopping theorems.

Assumption
4.4
is now stated locally because the natural
verification strategy is local linearisation at
\theta^{\star}
.
Proposition
4.6
gives a sufficient Jacobian rank
condition establishing the assumption under
\sigma=0
, and
Proposition
4.9
extends this to the stochastic case via
the effective equilibrium. The stopping theorems
(Theorems
4.16
–
4.17
) require the
trajectory to lie within the validity ball
B_{r}(\theta^{\star})
during the
relevant stopping window. In the noiseless regime,
Theorem
4.10
(iii) gives geometric decay

\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{t}R_{0}
a.s., so the trajectory
enters
B_{r}
deterministically after

T_{0}^{\mathrm{det}}(r):=\lceil\log(R_{0}/r)/\log(1/\gamma)\rceil
steps. In
the noisy regime, Lemma
4.13
establishes the analogous
deterministic containment provided
r>\rho M/(1-\gamma)
. The domain
verifications in Section
4.9
establish local
applicability via Proposition
4.6
or
Proposition
4.9
, not global validity.

Remark 4.5

(Compatibility of the validity radius with the noise floor)
.

Propositions
4.6
and
4.9
establish
Assumption
4.4
on a local ball whose radius is determined by
the second-derivative bounds of the operators:
r=\mu_{0}/(2R)
in the
noiseless case,
r_{\infty}=\mu_{0}^{\infty}/(2R)
in the noisy case. The
stopping theorems require this radius to exceed the asymptotic noise ball
radius
\rho M/(1-\gamma)
established by
Lemma
4.13
. The corresponding compatibility condition is

\frac{\mu_{0}}{2R}\;>\;\frac{\rho M}{1-\gamma}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mu_{0}\;>\;\frac{2R\rho M}{1-\gamma},

(6)

which says the linear commutator signal
\mu_{0}
must dominate the
second-order remainder evaluated at the noise-ball scale. In the noiseless
regime (
M=0
), (
6
) is automatic. In the noisy regime, it
is the operational requirement that the framework’s signal-to-noise
structure is non-degenerate. The same condition with
\mu_{0}\to\mu_{0}^{\infty}

and
M\to M^{\infty}
governs the noisy-regime stopping bounds centred at

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
.

4.2
When the Order-Gap Tracks Distance to Equilibrium

Assumption
4.4
is a global, first-moment condition on the
order-gap. We establish a local version that reduces its verification to a
standard rank condition on an averaged matrix.

Assume
Q
and
\{P_{e}\}
are
C^{2}
in a neighbourhood of
\theta^{\star}
, with
second derivatives of
Q
and
P_{e}
uniformly bounded in that neighbourhood
(or admitting an integrable dominating function over
e
). Let

A:=DQ(\theta^{\star}),\qquad B_{e}:=DP_{e}(\theta^{\star}),

and define the
commutator Jacobian
at the equilibrium:

\Sigma_{e}:=AB_{e}-B_{e}A.

Under
\sigma=0
, a first-order Taylor expansion at

\theta=\theta^{\star}+x
for small
x
gives:

\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;\,e)=\left\lVert Q(P_{e}(\theta^{\star}+x))-P_{e}(Q(\theta^{\star}+x))\right\rVert=\left\lVert\Sigma_{e}\,x+r_{e}(x)\right\rVert,

where
\left\lVert r_{e}(x)\right\rVert=O(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2})
uniformly in
e
(by the uniform

C^{2}
regularity assumption and Assumption
4.2
). Taking
expectation over
e
and applying Jensen’s inequality:

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)]\;\geq\;\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Sigma_{e}]\,x\right\rVert-O(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}),

where the
O(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2})
constant depends on the second-derivative bounds
of
Q
and the integrable dominating function for
\{P_{e}\}
.

Proposition 4.6

(Local order-gap sensitivity via Jacobian rank)
.

Assume
\sigma=0
, and that
Q,P_{e}\in C^{2}
near
\theta^{\star}
with
second derivatives uniformly bounded in that neighbourhood (or with an
integrable dominating function for the second derivatives over
e
). Define

\bar{\Sigma}:=\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Sigma_{e}]
and let

\mu_{0}:=\sigma_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma})
denote its smallest singular value.
If
\mu_{0}>0
, then there exists
r>0
such that for all
\theta
with

\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq r
,

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]\;\geq\;\tfrac{\mu_{0}}{2}\,\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert.

In particular, Assumption
4.4
holds locally with constant

\mu=\mu_{0}/2
.

Proof.

By Jensen and Taylor,

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)]\geq\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert-R\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}

for some
R>0
depending on the second-derivative bounds of
Q
and the
integrable dominating function for
\{P_{e}\}
. Since
\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert\geq\mu_{0}\left\lVert x\right\rVert
, the right-hand side is at least
\mu_{0}\left\lVert x\right\rVert-R\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}
.
Choosing
r:=\mu_{0}/(2R)
and restricting to
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq r
gives
the result. ∎

In other words, order-gap sensitivity reduces to an invertibility condition on
the
expected commutator Jacobian

\bar{\Sigma}=\mathbb{E}_{e}[AB_{e}-B_{e}A]
.
Verifying this requires only the Jacobians of
Q
and
P_{e}
at a single point,
with no global argument needed. In finite-dimensional settings (or after
choosing a Hilbert coordinate chart on
\mathcal{X}
), the smallest singular value

\mu_{0}=\sigma_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma})
is well-defined; on a general Banach
space one replaces the singular-value condition with an equivalent lower bound
on
\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}x\right\rVert
.

Remark 4.7

(Second-moment coverage and first-moment sensitivity)
.

An alternative sufficient condition uses the second-moment matrix of the local
commutator,
G:=\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Sigma_{e}^{\top}\Sigma_{e}]
, which we call the

commutator Gramian
. If

\lambda_{\min}(G)\geq\mu_{1}^{2}>0
, then locally

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)^{2}]\geq\mu_{1}^{2}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+O(\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert^{3})
. This Gramian condition
is often easier to verify than the first-moment condition
\mu_{0}>0
on

\bar{\Sigma}
, because first-moment effects can cancel across
e
while
second-moment coverage cannot.

From second moment to first moment.
If additionally there is a uniform local envelope

\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)\leq C\left\lVert x\right\rVert
for all
e
(which holds whenever
the Jacobians
A
and
B_{e}
are uniformly bounded), then shrinking the
neighbourhood if necessary, the remainder in the Gramian expansion is
dominated and

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)^{2}]\;\geq\;\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}.

Together with
\Omega\leq C\left\lVert x\right\rVert
, dividing

\tfrac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[\Omega^{2}]\leq C\left\lVert x\right\rVert\,\mathbb{E}[\Omega]
gives:

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}+x;e)]\;\geq\;\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{2C}\,\left\lVert x\right\rVert.

Thus the order-gap lower bound holds locally with
\mu=\mu_{1}^{2}/(2C)
. This argument is used
in the RLM verification below.

4.3
Effective Equilibrium and Noisy Local Sensitivity

Proposition
4.6
requires the noiseless hypothesis

\sigma=0
, in which case
P_{e}(\theta^{\star})=\theta^{\star}
a.s. and the
Taylor expansion at
\theta^{\star}
has no zeroth-order term. In stochastic
domains (bandits, RL), this hypothesis fails: the raw expansion
operator does not fix
\theta^{\star}
, and the dynamics converge not to

\theta^{\star}
but to a distinct effective equilibrium that absorbs the
asymptotic bias. We make this precise so that the Jacobian calculations in
Section
4.9
for stochastic domains are formally
grounded rather than informally read as “expected-update.”

Proposition 4.8

(Existence of effective equilibrium)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
–
4.2
with

\gamma=\rho L<1
, the expected one-step dynamics map

T(\theta)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{e}\bigl[Q(P_{e}(\theta))\bigr]

is a contraction on
(\mathcal{X},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
with rate
\gamma
, and admits a
unique fixed point
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\in\mathcal{X}
, which we call the

effective equilibrium
. In the noiseless regime (
W_{e}=0
a.s.),

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}=\theta^{\star}
.

Proof.

For any
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
:

\left\lVert T(\theta_{1})-T(\theta_{2})\right\rVert\leq\mathbb{E}_{e}\bigl[\left\lVert Q(P_{e}(\theta_{1}))-Q(P_{e}(\theta_{2}))\right\rVert\bigr]\leq\rho L\left\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right\rVert=\gamma\left\lVert\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\right\rVert,

using Jensen’s inequality and Assumptions
4.1
–
4.2
.
Since
\gamma<1
and
(\mathcal{X},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert)
is complete, the Banach
fixed-point theorem yields a unique
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
with

T(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})=\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
. Under
W_{e}=0
a.s.,

P_{e}(\theta^{\star})=\theta^{\star}
a.s., so

T(\theta^{\star})=\mathbb{E}_{e}[Q(\theta^{\star})]=Q(\theta^{\star})=\theta^{\star}
,
and
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}=\theta^{\star}
by uniqueness. ∎

The effective equilibrium
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
is the actual asymptotic
attractor of the expected dynamics. The displacement

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}-\theta^{\star}
is the systematic bias from

\mathbb{E}_{e}[P_{e}(\theta^{\star})-\theta^{\star}]\neq 0
, of order

\rho\sigma/(1-\gamma)
(the trajectory drift term identified in
Section
4.6
). At
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
, the
order-gap retains a residual noise level

\sigma^{\infty}:=\mathbb{E}_{e}[\left\lVert P_{e}(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert]
, generally smaller than
\sigma
but not necessarily
zero.

Proposition 4.9

(Noisy local order-gap sensitivity at the effective equilibrium)
.

Assume
Q
and
\{P_{e}\}
are
C^{2}
in a neighbourhood of

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
with second derivatives uniformly bounded (or with an
integrable dominating function over
e
), and that

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\left\lVert P_{e}(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert^{2}]\leq(\sigma^{\infty})^{2}<\infty
. Define

A_{\infty}:=DQ(\theta^{\star}_{\infty}),\qquad B_{e}^{\infty}:=DP_{e}(\theta^{\star}_{\infty}),\qquad\Sigma_{e}^{\infty}:=A_{\infty}B_{e}^{\infty}-B_{e}^{\infty}A_{\infty},

\bar{\Sigma}_{\infty}:=\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Sigma_{e}^{\infty}]
, and

\mu_{0}^{\infty}:=\sigma_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma}_{\infty})
. If
\mu_{0}^{\infty}>0
,
there exist
r_{\infty}>0
and
C_{\infty}>0
such that for all
\theta
with

\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert\leq r_{\infty}
:

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]\;\geq\;\tfrac{\mu_{0}^{\infty}}{2}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert\;-\;C_{\infty}\,\sigma^{\infty}.

Equivalently, Assumption
4.4
(with
\theta^{\star}
replaced by

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
) holds in the form

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]\geq(\mu_{0}^{\infty}/4)\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert
on the annulus

\{4C_{\infty}\sigma^{\infty}/\mu_{0}^{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}\right\rVert\leq r_{\infty}\}
.

Proof.

Write
\theta=\theta^{\star}_{\infty}+x
and Taylor-expand each operator.
Setting
p_{e}:=P_{e}(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})
and
q_{\infty}:=Q(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})
:

\displaystyle Q(P_{e}(\theta))

\displaystyle=Q(p_{e})+DQ(p_{e})\,B_{e}^{\infty}\,x+O(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}),

\displaystyle P_{e}(Q(\theta))

\displaystyle=P_{e}(q_{\infty})+DP_{e}(q_{\infty})\,A_{\infty}\,x+O(\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}).

Decomposing
DQ(p_{e})=A_{\infty}+(DQ(p_{e})-A_{\infty})
and similarly for

DP_{e}(q_{\infty})
, and using uniform
C^{2}
bounds, the cross-terms involving

p_{e}-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
and
q_{\infty}-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
are

O(\sigma^{\infty})
in expectation. Taking expectations and applying Jensen:

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta^{\star}_{\infty}+x;e)]\;\geq\;\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}_{\infty}\,x\right\rVert-C_{\infty}\sigma^{\infty}-R\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2},

for constants
C_{\infty},R>0
depending on the smoothness bounds. Since

\left\lVert\bar{\Sigma}_{\infty}x\right\rVert\geq\mu_{0}^{\infty}\left\lVert x\right\rVert
, choosing

r_{\infty}:=\mu_{0}^{\infty}/(2R)
and restricting to
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq r_{\infty}

gives the bound. The annular reformulation follows by requiring the linear
term to dominate the noise term:
(\mu_{0}^{\infty}/2)\left\lVert x\right\rVert\geq 2C_{\infty}\sigma^{\infty}
. ∎

In the noiseless regime,
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}=\theta^{\star}
,

\sigma^{\infty}=0
, the noise term vanishes, and
Proposition
4.9
reduces to
Proposition
4.6
. In the noisy regime, the natural
reference point is
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
rather than
\theta^{\star}
, and the
local order-gap lower bound is informative on the annulus where the linear
signal in
x
exceeds the noise floor of order
\sigma^{\infty}
. This is the
formal content of the “expected-update interpretation” invoked in the
domain verifications of Section
4.9
: the Jacobian rank
condition is checked at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
, and the sensitivity bound it
yields is valid on the annulus described above.

The stopping theorems of Section
4.7
are stated below
with respect to the consolidation fixed point
\theta^{\star}
. In the noisy
regime, the appropriate reference point is the effective equilibrium

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
rather than
\theta^{\star}
;
Corollary
4.20
formalises this, restating the stopping
guarantees of Theorems
4.16
–
4.17
at

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
with the appropriate substitutions. We retain the

\theta^{\star}
-formulation in the main statements because it is the
structurally cleaner case and reduces to
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
in the
noiseless limit.

4.4
Contraction of the Order-Gap

Theorem 4.10

(Contraction of the order-gap)
.

Assume
4.1
–
4.3
and set
\gamma:=\rho L
.
Let
u_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\bigr]
. Then:

(i)

(Trajectory contraction, expectation.)
For
\gamma<1
,

u_{t}\;\leq\;\gamma^{t}u_{0}+\rho\sigma\cdot\frac{1-\gamma^{t}}{1-\gamma}.

(7)

(ii)

(Order-gap bound, expectation.)

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})\bigr]\;\leq\;2\gamma\,u_{t}\;+\;(1+\rho)\sigma.

(8)

(iii)

(Almost-sure geometric decay under
\sigma=0
.)

If
\sigma=0
and
\gamma<1
, then a.s.,

\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{t}\left\lVert\theta_{0}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert,\quad\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})\leq 2\gamma^{t+1}\left\lVert\theta_{0}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert.

(9)

Proof.

Write
W_{t}:=\left\lVert P_{e_{t}}(\theta^{\star})-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
. Using

\theta^{\star}=Q(\theta^{\star})
and
Assumptions
4.1
–
4.2
,

\left\lVert\theta_{t+1}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\rho L\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert+\rho W_{t}.

(10)

Taking expectations gives (i). For (ii), a three-term triangle decomposition
(adding and subtracting
Q(P_{e}(\theta^{\star}))
and
P_{e}(\theta^{\star})
) gives:

\Omega(\theta;e)\leq 2\rho L\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert+(1+\rho)W_{e}.

Taking expectation yields (
8
). Under
\sigma=0
,

W_{t}=0
a.s., so (
10
) is a pathwise contraction
and (
9
) follows by iteration and substitution. ∎

Remark 4.11

(Joint stability)
.

The condition
\gamma=\rho L<1
couples the two operators: the
contraction of consolidation must outpace any expansion introduced by
P_{e}
.
This is the natural regime of interest;
\rho<1
alone merely says the
learner commits to its current best estimate, and
L
can exceed
1
in
domains where
P_{e}
amplifies evidence.

Remark 4.12

(Polynomial rates under weaker contraction)
.

Assumption
4.1
can be weakened. If
Q
is only

contractive in expectation
(e.g.,
Q
averages the current state with a running
sample mean, as in standard bandit updates), then
u_{t}
decays at rate

O(1/\sqrt{t})
rather than geometrically, and
\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})]
inherits this rate. The
O(1/n)
bounds familiar from stochastic
approximation fall out of this weaker regime. Domain applications that use
a non-contractive
Q
(such as the bandit sharpening map of
Section
4.9
) operate in this weaker regime.

Lemma 4.13

(Trajectory containment)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
–
4.3
with

W_{e}\leq M
a.s. and
\gamma=\rho L<1
, let

R_{0}:=\left\lVert\theta_{0}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
. For every
r>\rho M/(1-\gamma)
,
define

T_{0}(r):=\begin{cases}0,&R_{0}\leq r-\rho M/(1-\gamma),\\[4.0pt]
\left\lceil\dfrac{\log\bigl(R_{0}\,/\,(r-\rho M/(1-\gamma))\bigr)}{\log(1/\gamma)}\right\rceil,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq r
almost surely for all

t\geq T_{0}(r)
.

Proof.

Iterating (
10
) with
W_{s}\leq M
a.s.:

\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\;\leq\;\gamma^{t}R_{0}+\rho\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\gamma^{t-1-s}W_{s}\;\leq\;\gamma^{t}R_{0}+\rho M\cdot\frac{1-\gamma^{t}}{1-\gamma}\;\leq\;\gamma^{t}R_{0}+\frac{\rho M}{1-\gamma}

almost surely. The right-hand side is at most
r
when

\gamma^{t}R_{0}\leq r-\rho M/(1-\gamma)
, which holds for all

t\geq T_{0}(r)
. ∎

The containment radius
\rho M/(1-\gamma)
is the noise-floor quantity
identified in the trajectory contraction bound (
7
); the
lemma upgrades it from a bound on the expected distance to a deterministic
pathwise bound under bounded noise. The condition
r>\rho M/(1-\gamma)
is
the precise requirement that Assumption
4.4
’s validity radius
exceed the asymptotic noise ball radius. Combined with the noiseless
geometric containment
\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{t}R_{0}
from
Theorem
4.10
(iii), the lemma certifies that the local
order-gap sensitivity assumption applies along the trajectory after a finite
deterministic transient
T_{0}(r)
in both regimes.

4.5
Operational Lower Bound

Proposition 4.14

(Order-gap lower bounds suboptimality)
.

Under
4.1
–
4.3
, assume additionally that

\gamma=\rho L>0
(the case
\gamma=0
is excluded from the displayed
inverse bound; when
\gamma=0
the order-gap upper bound reduces to the
noise term
(1+\rho)\sigma
alone). Then for every
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
:

\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{2\rho L}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]-(1+\rho)\sigma\bigr)_{+}.

(11)

If
\ell\circ\pi
satisfies a
quadratic growth
condition at

\theta^{\star}
with constant
m_{\mathrm{QG}}>0
, meaning

\ell(\pi(\theta))-\ell(\pi(\theta^{\star}))\geq\tfrac{m_{\mathrm{QG}}}{2}\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}
, then

\ell(\pi(\theta))-\ell(\pi(\theta^{\star}))\geq\frac{m_{\mathrm{QG}}}{8\rho^{2}L^{2}}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Omega(\theta;e)]-(1+\rho)\sigma\bigr)_{+}^{2}.

(12)

Proof.

Rearrange (
8
) to obtain (
11
). Squaring
and applying the quadratic growth lower bound gives (
12
). ∎

4.6
The Noise Floor

When
\sigma>0
, Theorem
4.10
gives an
upper bound

on the asymptotic expected order-gap, which we call the
noise-floor bound
:

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})]\leq\underbrace{(1+\rho)\sigma}_{\varepsilon_{\star}^{\text{eq}}}+\underbrace{\frac{2\gamma\rho\sigma}{1-\gamma}}_{\varepsilon_{\star}^{\text{traj}}}=:\varepsilon_{\star}.

The actual asymptotic expected order-gap may be smaller; in special cases it
could be zero despite
\sigma>0
. The decomposition nonetheless reveals
which part of the noise floor is reducible.

Fixed-point residual:

\varepsilon_{\star}^{\text{eq}}=(1+\rho)\sigma
is the contribution from

\theta^{\star}
itself. It is not controlled by trajectory contraction alone:
unless
\{P_{e}\}
fixes
\theta^{\star}
, the equilibrium residual contributes
to the bound regardless of how well the trajectory contracts.

Trajectory drift term:

\varepsilon_{\star}^{\text{traj}}=2\gamma\rho\sigma/(1-\gamma)
is reducible: variance-reduced expansion
updates that shrink the effective
\sigma
bring it down.

For the high-probability theorem we use the bounded-noise version of this floor.
Define the
bounded-noise version of the floor
:

\varepsilon_{\star}^{(M)}:=(1+\rho)M+\frac{2\gamma\rho M}{1-\gamma}.

(13)

This replaces
\sigma
by
M
(the a.s. bound on
W_{e}
) and serves as the
noise-floor quantity for the empirical windowed stopping theorem.

Remark 4.15

(Practical implication)
.

The sufficient stopping theorem requires the threshold
\varepsilon
to exceed
the relevant noise-floor bound:
\varepsilon_{\star}
in the noiseless expected-gap analysis,
or
\varepsilon_{\star}^{(M)}
in the high-probability theorem.
When the trajectory drift term dominates, variance reduction
pays dividends. When the fixed-point residual dominates, only modifying
P_{e}

itself can help.

4.7
Stopping Guarantees

We define two stopping rules, one for each regime.

Expected-gap stopping rule
(noiseless/theoretical):

\bar{\Omega}_{t,w}:=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w}^{t-1}\mathbb{E}_{e}\bigl[\Omega(\theta_{s};e)\bigr],\qquad\bar{\tau}_{\varepsilon}:=\inf\bigl\{t\geq w:\bar{\Omega}_{t,w}\leq\varepsilon\bigr\}.

(14)

Empirical windowed stopping rule
(operational):

\widehat{\Omega}_{t,w}:=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w}^{t-1}\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s}),\qquad\hat{\tau}_{\varepsilon}:=\inf\bigl\{t\geq w:\widehat{\Omega}_{t,w}\leq\varepsilon\bigr\}.

(15)

Both windows run from
s=t-w
to
s=t-1
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(e_{0},\ldots,e_{t-1})
, both
\bar{\Omega}_{t,w}
and
\widehat{\Omega}_{t,w}

are
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable, so
\bar{\tau}_{\varepsilon}
and

\hat{\tau}_{\varepsilon}
are stopping times with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
.
Algorithm
1
gives the operational form of the
empirical rule, which is what an implementation actually runs:
the order-gap is computed pathwise from a single forward pass, the
windowed average is updated incrementally, and stopping triggers when
the average drops below
\varepsilon
.

The noiseless regime:
\sigma=0

Theorem 4.16

(Expected-gap stopping,
\sigma=0
)
.

Assume

–
4.4
with
\sigma=0
,
\gamma<1
,
and
R_{0}:=\left\lVert\theta_{0}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
. If

then

. Otherwise, for every

,

\left\lceil\dfrac{\log(2\gamma R_{0}/\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\gamma)}\right\rceil,&2\gamma R_{0}>\varepsilon.\end{cases}

(i)

(Stopping-time bound.)

\bar{\tau}_{\varepsilon}\;\leq\;w+N_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{a.s.}

(16)

In particular, when

and the system stops at
\bar{\tau}_{\varepsilon}=w
.

(ii)

(Monotone distance decrease.)

\left\lVert\theta_{s}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert\theta_{s+1}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert

.

(iii)

(Suboptimality at stopping.)

If, in addition,
\varepsilon\leq 2\gamma r
where

of Assumption

\left\lVert\theta_{\bar{\tau}_{\varepsilon}}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\;\leq\;\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}\qquad\text{a.s.}

lies inside the validity ball

before

(i).

\leq\varepsilon

(ii).

\left\lVert\theta_{s+1}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\gamma\left\lVert\theta_{s}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\theta_{s}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
.

By (ii), for every

4.8
Extension to State-Dependent Sampling

The theorems above assume events
e_{t}
are drawn i.i.d. from a fixed
distribution
P
, independent of
\theta_{t}
. For domains where the sampling
kernel depends on the current knowledge state — bandits with adaptive arm
selection, off-policy reinforcement learning with state-dependent behaviour
policies — this independence
fails. We extend the contraction and stopping theorems to the state-dependent
case under uniform conditional moment bounds. The crucial observation is
that the martingale structure underlying the concentration argument

survives
the move from i.i.d. to state-dependent sampling, so no
mixing-time apparatus is required.

Setup.

Let
\{(\theta_{t},e_{t})\}_{t\geq 0}
be a Markov process on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{E}

in which
e_{t}
is drawn from a state-dependent kernel
P(\cdot\mid\theta_{t})
.
Assume the kernel is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable, i.e.,
\theta_{t}
is

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable and
e_{t}
is drawn independently of

\mathcal{F}_{t}
given
\theta_{t}
. The dynamics

\theta_{t+1}=Q\circ P_{e_{t}}(\theta_{t})
continue to define an

\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted Markov chain on
\mathcal{X}
.

Assumption 4.21

(Uniform conditional moment bounds)
.

Let
W_{e}:=\left\lVert P_{e}(\theta^{\star})-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
. There exist

\sigma\geq 0
and
M\geq\sigma
such that, for every
\theta\in\mathcal{X}
,

\int_{\mathcal{E}}W_{e}\,P(de\mid\theta)\;\leq\;\sigma,\qquad\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{e\sim P(\cdot\mid\theta)}\,W_{e}\;\leq\;M.

In the i.i.d. case, these reduce to the marginal expectations of
Assumption
4.3
and the analysis of
Sections
4.4
–
4.7
is
recovered.

Assumption
4.4
(local order-gap sensitivity) is similarly
interpreted at the conditional level: for
\theta\in B_{r}(\theta^{\star})

(or
B_{r_{\infty}}(\theta^{\star}_{\infty})
in the noisy regime),

\int_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega(\theta;e)\,P(de\mid\theta)\;\geq\;\mu\left\lVert\theta-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert,

which agrees with the i.i.d. form when the conditional kernel is the fixed
marginal
P
.

Theorem 4.22

(Contraction under state-dependent sampling)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
–
4.2
and
Assumption
4.21
, the conclusions of
Theorem
4.10
hold verbatim with the contraction rate

\gamma=\rho L
unchanged.

Proof.

The path-wise contraction inequality (
10
),

\left\lVert\theta_{t+1}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\rho L\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert+\rho W_{t},

holds path-wise with
W_{t}:=\left\lVert P_{e_{t}}(\theta^{\star})-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert
,
independently of how
e_{t}
is sampled, because the inequality follows from
Assumptions
4.1
–
4.2
pointwise in
e
. Taking
expectations,

\mathbb{E}[W_{t}]=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,\int_{\mathcal{E}}W_{e}\,P(de\mid\theta_{t})\right]\leq\sigma,

by Assumption
4.21
. Iterating gives

u_{t+1}\leq\gamma u_{t}+\rho\sigma
, hence

u_{t}\leq\gamma^{t}u_{0}+\rho\sigma(1-\gamma^{t})/(1-\gamma)
. The pathwise
bound
W_{t}\leq M
a.s. holds analogously, yielding

\left\lVert\theta_{t}-\theta^{\star}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{t}R_{0}+\rho M/(1-\gamma)
a.s.
The triangle bound on
\Omega
in Theorem
4.10
(ii) is
unchanged because it follows pointwise in
e
. ∎

Theorem 4.23

(Empirical stopping under state-dependent sampling)
.

Under Assumptions
4.1
–
4.4
(with
Assumption
4.21
replacing
Assumption
4.3
and Assumption
4.4

interpreted at the conditional level), the conclusions of

4.17
hold verbatim, with
g_{s}:=\int_{\mathcal{E}}\Omega(\theta_{s};e)P(de\mid\theta_{s})=\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}]
and the same envelope
K
, concentration term
\eta
, and

Proof.

We verify that each step of the proof of Theorem
4.17

carries through.

Pathwise envelope.
The bound
\Omega(\theta_{t};e_{t})\leq K
a.s. follows from Theorem
4.22
and the triangle bound,
exactly as in the i.i.d. case.

Pathwise bounding of
g_{s}
.
Because
\theta_{s}
is

\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable and
e_{s}
is drawn from
P(\cdot\mid\theta_{s})

independently of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
given
\theta_{s}

which is exactly the role

Martingale decomposition.
The residuals

\{\mathcal{F}_{s+1}\}
, with
|\Xi_{s}|\leq K

Because

\widehat{\Omega}_{t,w}-(1/w)\sum_{s}g_{s}

(1/w)\sum_{s}\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})-\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{\star}}[\Omega]

These steps depend only on the pathwise contraction and on
Assumption
4.4
applied at conditional expectations, both of
which we have established. The arguments are unchanged. ∎

Operational implication.

The cost of state-dependent sampling, in this formulation, is borne by the
strengthening of the moment hypothesis: the marginal expectation in
Assumption
4.3
is replaced by the supremum over
conditioning states in Assumption
4.21
. Whenever this
strengthening holds — which is automatic when the operator family
\{P_{e}\}

satisfies a uniform Lipschitz bound on a bounded state space, or when
exploration in bandits and RL is bounded below uniformly across states —
the i.i.d. stopping guarantees apply with no further modification. Mixing
of the joint chain is not required because the analysis controls

trajectory
averages against
conditional
means, both of which
are well-defined for adapted processes. The corresponding restatement at
the effective equilibrium
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
follows by combining
Corollary
4.20
with Theorem
4.23
.

4.9
Verifying the Theoretical Conditions in Three Domains

The bandit, RLM, and actor-critic cases receive explicit analysis of when
the order-gap reliably tracks convergence below. The SGD case
(Section
5.3
) reduces to classical Robbins-Monro stochastic
approximation
(
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Borkar 2008
)
when
Q

is trivial; the contraction theorem applies verbatim, and the order-gap
becomes a non-trivial diagnostic in adaptive variants (momentum, Adam,
proximal). The continual-learning case is a qualitative mapping of the
framework rather than a full theorem verification (Section
5
):
it shows how the
Q
/
P_{e}
decomposition and order-gap control principle
map onto an existing algorithm family.

Effective equilibrium and Jacobian reference point.
In stochastic
domains (bandits, RL), the raw sample-level expansion operator satisfies

P_{e}(\theta^{\star})\neq\theta^{\star}
in general: a Gaussian reward draw or
a single TD transition perturbs
\theta^{\star}
stochastically. The noiseless
hypothesis
\sigma=0
of Proposition
4.6
therefore does
not hold for the raw operators, and the Jacobian calculations below cannot be
performed at
\theta^{\star}
. The framework’s formal handling of this case is
Proposition
4.9
: the Jacobians are evaluated at the
effective equilibrium
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
(the unique fixed point of

T(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{e}[Q(P_{e}(\theta))]
, established in
Proposition
4.8
), and the order-gap sensitivity bound
holds on the annulus where the linear signal in the displacement

\theta-\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
exceeds the residual noise level

\sigma^{\infty}
at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
. The Jacobian calculations below
should be read in this sense: as commutator computations at

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
rather than at
\theta^{\star}
. The first-moment or
Gramian conditions, when satisfied, establish the order-gap lower bound on
the relevant annulus around
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
. The bandit verification
below shows the Gramian path explicitly: in the Bayesian setup with
posterior contraction and mean shrinkage, the first-moment commutator
vanishes at the unbiased equilibrium while the second-moment Gramian remains
non-zero under standard exploration coverage.

Bandits: Bayesian State with Posterior Contraction and Mean Shrinkage

We present a Bayesian bandit verification in which the consolidation
operator is genuinely contractive (Assumption
4.1
) and the
order-gap sensitivity assumption is verified via the second-moment Gramian
condition of Remark
4.7
. The verification illustrates
two structural points: (i) the consolidation operator must couple to the
expansion’s evidence-incorporation mechanism (here through variance-mediated
Bayesian updating) to produce a non-trivial commutator; (ii) first-moment
effects can cancel at the unbiased equilibrium while second-moment effects
do not, so the Gramian condition is the natural verification path for
stochastic bandit dynamics.

Setup.

For
A
-armed Gaussian bandits with arm means

\mu_{\mathrm{arm}}\in\mathbb{R}^{A}
and reward variance
\sigma_{r}^{2}
, the
knowledge state is
\theta=(\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma}^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{A}\times\mathbb{R}^{A}_{+}

with arm-specific posterior mean
\hat{\mu}_{a}
and variance
\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}
.
The expansion operator is the standard arm-specific Bayesian update on a
sampled arm
a
with observed reward
r
:

P_{(a,r)}:\quad\hat{\mu}_{a}\,\to\,\frac{\hat{\mu}_{a}\,\sigma_{r}^{2}+r\,\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}+\sigma_{r}^{2}},\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}\,\to\,\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}\sigma_{r}^{2}}{\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}+\sigma_{r}^{2}},

(21)

with all other coordinates left unchanged.

The consolidation operator combines
posterior contraction
(variance
shrinkage) with
mean shrinkage
toward a prior
\mu_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{A}
:

Q:\quad\hat{\mu}\,\to\,(1-\lambda)\hat{\mu}+\lambda\mu_{p},\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}\,\to\,\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}{1+\kappa},

(22)

with shrinkage rates
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
\kappa>0
.

Verification of contraction (Assumption
4.1
).

Q
is linear with eigenvalues
\{1-\lambda\}^{A}_{\text{(mean block)}}
and

\{1/(1+\kappa)\}^{A}_{\text{(variance block)}}
. Its Lipschitz constant in
the product norm is

\rho\;=\;\max\!\left(1-\lambda,\;\frac{1}{1+\kappa}\right)\;<\;1,

satisfying Assumption
4.1
strictly. The deterministic fixed
point of
Q
is
\theta^{\star}=(\mu_{p},\,0)
— biased toward the prior. By
Proposition
4.8
, the joint dynamics admits a unique
effective equilibrium
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
, which sits between
\mu_{p}
and

\mu_{\mathrm{arm}}
with bias
O(\lambda)
from the truth: for small

\lambda
,
\hat{\mu}^{\infty}_{a}=\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}+O(\lambda)
.

Commutator structure.

The Bayesian mean update in (
21
) depends on the
current variance
\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}
, producing a non-zero
(\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2})

cross-derivative. At any
\theta
:

\frac{\partial\hat{\mu}_{a}^{\prime}}{\partial\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}\;=\;\frac{\sigma_{r}^{2}(r-\hat{\mu}_{a})}{(\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}+\sigma_{r}^{2})^{2}}.

The consolidation Jacobian
A_{Q}:=DQ
is block-scalar —
(1-\lambda)I

on the mean block and
(1/(1+\kappa))I
on the variance block. Direct
computation of the commutator
\Sigma_{(a,r)}:=A_{Q}B_{(a,r)}-B_{(a,r)}A_{Q}

yields the unique non-zero entry in the
(\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2})

position:

\bigl(\Sigma_{(a,r)}\bigr)_{\hat{\mu}_{a},\,\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}\;=\;\frac{\sigma_{r}^{2}(r-\hat{\mu}_{a})}{(\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}+\sigma_{r}^{2})^{2}}\cdot\frac{\kappa(1-\lambda)-\lambda}{1+\kappa},

(23)

with all other entries zero. The commutator is non-trivial whenever

\kappa(1-\lambda)\neq\lambda
— generically the case for
(\kappa,\lambda)\in(0,1)^{2}
.

First-moment cancellation.

Averaging
\Sigma_{(a,r)}
over rewards

r\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a},\sigma_{r}^{2})
:

\mathbb{E}_{r}\!\bigl[(\Sigma_{(a,r)})_{\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}\bigr]\;\propto\;\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}-\hat{\mu}_{a}.

At the effective equilibrium
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
with

\hat{\mu}_{a}^{\infty}=\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}+O(\lambda)
, the first-moment
commutator vanishes to leading order:

\sigma_{\min}(\bar{\Sigma}_{\infty})=O(\lambda)
. As anticipated by
Remark
4.7
, first-moment effects partially cancel; the
Gramian, however, does not.

Gramian coverage and second-moment lower bound.

The squared commutator entry is proportional to
(r-\hat{\mu}_{a})^{2}
:

\mathbb{E}_{r}\!\bigl[(r-\hat{\mu}_{a})^{2}\bigr]\;=\;\sigma_{r}^{2}+(\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}-\hat{\mu}_{a})^{2}\;\geq\;\sigma_{r}^{2}.

Thus the conditional Gramian

G_{a}:=\mathbb{E}_{r}[\Sigma_{(a,r)}^{\top}\Sigma_{(a,r)}]
has a non-zero
entry of order
\sigma_{r}^{2}
in the
(\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2})

position, regardless of bias. Averaging over arms with selection
probabilities
\{p_{a}\}_{a=1}^{A}
yields

G:=\sum_{a}p_{a}G_{a}
, a diagonal matrix on the variance subspace with
entries

G_{\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}\;=\;p_{a}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{r}^{4}\cdot[\kappa(1-\lambda)-\lambda]^{2}}{(\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2,\infty}+\sigma_{r}^{2})^{4}(1+\kappa)^{2}}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{r}[(r-\hat{\mu}_{a}^{\infty})^{2}]\;\geq\;p_{a}\cdot\frac{\sigma_{r}^{6}\cdot[\kappa(1-\lambda)-\lambda]^{2}}{(\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2,\infty}+\sigma_{r}^{2})^{4}(1+\kappa)^{2}}\;=:\;p_{a}\,\mu_{1}^{2,(a)},

using
\mathbb{E}_{r}[(r-\hat{\mu}_{a}^{\infty})^{2}]\geq\sigma_{r}^{2}
(with equality
only at the unbiased equilibrium
\hat{\mu}_{a}^{\infty}=\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}
).

G
has full rank on the variance subspace if and only if
p_{a}>0
for all

a
— the standard exploration coverage condition (every arm pulled with
positive probability). Equivalently,
G
is rank-deficient only when some
arm is never selected.

Conclusion.

Under standard exploration (
p_{a}>0
for all
a
) and the Bayesian setup
(
21
)–(
22
), the second-moment Gramian
condition of Remark
4.7
is satisfied with

\mu_{1}^{2}\geq\min_{a}p_{a}\,\mu_{1}^{2,(a)}>0
on the variance subspace.
Combined with the uniform Jacobian envelope from the boundedness of the
Bayesian update at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
and
Proposition
4.9
(centred at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
),
Assumption
4.4
holds locally on the variance subspace with

\mu_{\mathrm{var}}=\mu_{1}^{2}/(2C)
for an explicit constant
C
.

Mean subspace via first-moment sensitivity.

On the mean subspace, the first-moment commutator at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}

is non-zero whenever
\lambda>0
: from the calculation above,

\mathbb{E}_{r}[(\Sigma_{(a,r)})_{\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2}}]\propto\mu_{\mathrm{arm},a}-\hat{\mu}_{a}
, which at
\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
has
magnitude
O(\lambda)
(the bias of the mean equilibrium toward the prior

\mu_{p}
). This gives a first-moment sensitivity
\mu_{\mathrm{mean}}=c_{\mathrm{mean}}\,\lambda
on the mean block via
Proposition
4.9
, for a constant
c_{\mathrm{mean}}

depending on
(\sigma_{r},\kappa,\min_{a}p_{a})
and the prior bias. Combining
the two subspace bounds, Assumption
4.4
holds on the joint

(\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma}^{2})
state with constant

\mu=\min\bigl(\,c_{\mathrm{mean}}\,\lambda,\;\mu_{1}^{2}/(2C)\bigr),

provided
\lambda>0
is held fixed. As
\lambda\to 0
, the mean-block
sensitivity vanishes and the framework’s stopping guarantee restricts to the
variance subspace; this is consistent with the unbiased-equilibrium limit,
where the mean estimate is statistically identifiable only through the
posterior contraction governed by
\kappa
. For any practical bandit
algorithm with non-zero shrinkage, the joint guarantee binds.

Design implications.

The verification yields three concrete design criteria:
(i) the consolidation operator must couple to the expansion’s
evidence-incorporation mechanism — here through variance-mediated Bayesian
updating, where Q’s variance contraction and P’s variance-dependent mean
update jointly produce a non-zero commutator;
(ii) mean shrinkage (
\lambda>0
) and posterior contraction (
\kappa>0
)
play complementary roles:
\lambda
provides strict contraction on the mean
block, while
\kappa
provides it on the variance block, with their joint
contribution
[\kappa(1-\lambda)-\lambda]
governing the commutator
magnitude;
(iii) exploration coverage (
p_{a}>0
for all
a
) is required not only for
asymptotic regret control but for the order-gap signal itself to be
informative — in the absence of coverage, the Gramian is rank-deficient and
the order-gap fails to track convergence on the unexplored arm directions.

Recursive Language Models

Let
S\in\mathcal{X}
denote the aggregated state over processed chunks,
S^{\star}

the fixed-point state corresponding to the full document, and

\textsc{Answer}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
the answer-extraction map. Model consolidation as

Q(S)=S+\beta(S^{\star}_{\textrm{proj}}(S)-S)
,
where
S^{\star}_{\textrm{proj}}(S)
is the projection of
S
onto the
answer-relevant subspace; the Jacobian is
A=(1-\beta)I+\beta P
, where

P
is the projection onto the answer manifold. Let
P_{e}
integrate chunk
e

into
S
with chunk-specific Jacobian
B_{e}=I+e_{\textrm{add}}
.

Idempotence assumption.

We assume the state representation is
idempotent
: reprocessing a chunk
whose evidence is already fully represented in
S^{\star}
leaves
S^{\star}

unchanged. That is,
P_{e}(S^{\star})=S^{\star}
a.s. over chunks already
incorporated. This places us in the noiseless regime (
\sigma=0
). If not,
the system is in the noisy regime and the order-gap lower bound must be assumed directly or verified
after centring.

Answer-relevant state space.

The consolidation map
Q
has eigenvalue
1
along directions tangent to
the answer manifold
\mathcal{M}
at
S^{\star}
(neutral directions): these
are state directions that cannot change the final answer. We therefore state
the order-gap sensitivity result on the directions in state space that

can
affect the final answer; we call this the
answer-relevant
state space
: the subspace orthogonal to
T_{S^{\star}}\mathcal{M}
, where states
differing only along answer-preserving tangent directions are identified.

Second-moment coverage.

The commutator
\Sigma_{e}=[A,B_{e}]=\beta[P,e_{\textrm{add}}]
is
non-zero whenever the chunk shifts the answer manifold. However, because

e_{\textrm{add}}
effects can have varying signs across chunks, the
first-moment commutator
\bar{\Sigma}=\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{e}[Pe_{\textrm{add}}-e_{\textrm{add}}P]
may not be full rank even when
individual chunks contribute strongly. The appropriate condition is the

commutator Gramian

G=\mathbb{E}_{e}[\Sigma_{e}^{\top}\Sigma_{e}]\succ 0

on the answer-relevant state space (Remark
4.7
). Positive
definiteness of
G
holds when the chunk-selection distribution excites every
direction that can affect the final answer through the commutator, that is,
for every nonzero direction
v
orthogonal to
T_{S^{\star}}\mathcal{M}
,
there exists a chunk
e
with positive probability such that

[P,\,e_{\textrm{add}}]\,v\;\neq\;0.

(Note: the condition is on the commutator
[P,e_{\textrm{add}}]v
, not merely
on
e_{\textrm{add}}v
; a chunk that moves state but does not shift the answer
manifold boundary contributes nothing to
\Sigma_{e}
.) Since the Jacobians

A
and
B_{e}
are uniformly bounded in this linear model, the second-moment
coverage argument of Remark
4.7
applies, giving
Assumption
4.4
locally with
\mu=\mu_{1}^{2}/(2C)
for an
explicit constant
C
depending on
\beta
and a uniform bound on the chunk Jacobians.

Interpretation:
order-gap sensitivity reduces to a

coverage condition
: the chunk-selection or extraction
operator must excite all directions that can affect the final answer, in the
commutator sense.
The design prescription is to randomise or weight chunk selection by coverage
so that every answer-affecting direction is excited with positive probability.

Reinforcement Learning: Linear Actor-Critic

We derive the commutator Jacobian explicitly for regularised off-policy linear
actor-critic. The derivation reveals a structural feature: in the canonical
formulation, the first-moment commutator is rank-deficient, and restoring
order-gap sensitivity requires an additional policy-critic coupling term in
consolidation.

Scope.

The Jacobian calculation is carried out at a fixed equilibrium policy

\pi_{\psi^{\star}}
, treating the event distribution locally as fixed (in line with
the expected-update interpretation of Section
4.9
). Extending the
analysis to fully state-dependent event distributions requires the extension to
adaptive sampling policies.

Setup.

Let
\phi:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\phi_{\pi}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}}
be feature maps
for the critic and actor. The joint parameter is

\theta=(w,\psi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}}
; the linear critic is

Q_{\theta}(s,a)=\phi(s,a)^{\top}w
, and the softmax actor is

\pi(a\mid s;\psi)\propto\exp(\phi_{\pi}(s,a)^{\top}\psi)
.
Let
\pi_{b}
be a fixed behaviour policy.

Local contraction.

Locally near a strongly stable actor-critic equilibrium
\theta^{\star}
and
for sufficiently small update step sizes, the Jacobian of the consolidation
operator
Q
is contractive on the identifiable subspace defined below.
Global contraction of the full softmax actor-critic system is not claimed.

Expansion operator.

P_{e}(w,\psi)=(w+\alpha\,\delta(w,e)\,\phi(s,a),\;\psi)
,
where
\delta(w,e)=r+\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}\,\phi(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})^{\top}w-\phi(s,a)^{\top}w
is the TD error.
(Here
\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}
is the RL discount factor, distinct from the
framework’s joint-stability
\gamma=\rho L
.)

Baseline consolidation.

We work in local coordinates centred at the equilibrium: write

\tilde{w}=w-w^{\star}
and
\tilde{\psi}=\psi-\psi^{\star}
, and use

(w,\psi)
to denote
(\tilde{w},\tilde{\psi})
throughout. In these
coordinates
\theta^{\star}=0
, and the baseline consolidation is:

Q_{0}(w,\psi)=\bigl((1-\beta^{\prime})w,\;\psi+\beta H(w+w^{\star},\psi+\psi^{\star})\bigr),

which satisfies
Q_{0}(0,0)=(0,0)
by the fixed-point condition

H(w^{\star},\psi^{\star})=0
. Weight decay with rate
\beta^{\prime}>0
ensures the

w
-coordinate contracts. The
\psi
-coordinate contracts under
Q_{0}
provided
the policy Hessian
H_{\psi}:=\nabla_{\psi}H(\theta^{\star})\prec 0
(standard
under strongly concave objectives for sufficiently small
\beta
).

Jacobians.

Set
\phi_{e}:=\phi(s,a)
,
\Delta_{e}:=\gamma_{\mathrm{RL}}\phi(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})-\phi_{e}
.

B_{e}=DP_{e}(\theta^{\star})=\begin{pmatrix}I+\alpha\,\phi_{e}\Delta_{e}^{\top}&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad DQ_{0}(\theta^{\star})=\begin{pmatrix}(1-\beta^{\prime})I&0\\
\beta H_{w}&I+\beta H_{\psi}\end{pmatrix},

where
H_{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}\times d}
is the actor-critic cross-covariance.

Rank deficiency of baseline.

Theorem 4.24

(Baseline actor-critic is insensitive to policy directions)
.

Under
Q_{0}
, the first-moment commutator Jacobian
\bar{\Sigma}
has null space
containing the entire policy subspace
\{(0,x_{\psi}):x_{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}}\}
.

Proof.

Direct block multiplication gives

\bar{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
-\alpha\beta H_{w}M&0\end{pmatrix}
,
using
\mathbb{E}_{e}[\phi_{e}\Delta_{e}^{\top}]=-M
.
The right column is zero, so
\bar{\Sigma}(0,x_{\psi})^{\top}=0
for all
x_{\psi}
.
∎

The underlying reason:
Q_{0}
updates only
\psi
and
P_{e}
updates only
w
.
Their cross-block
\beta H_{w}
propagates only
w
-perturbations into the
commutator.

Restoring order-gap sensitivity.

Add a policy-critic consistency term. In centred coordinates, define

\widetilde{w}^{\star}(\psi):=w^{\star}_{\mathrm{TD}}(\psi^{\star}+\psi)-w^{\star}_{\mathrm{TD}}(\psi^{\star}),

so that
\widetilde{w}^{\star}(0)=0
and the fixed-point structure

Q(0,0)=0
is preserved. The augmented consolidation map is

Q(w,\psi):=\bigl((1-\beta^{\prime})w+\beta^{\prime}\widetilde{w}^{\star}(\psi),\;\psi+\beta H(w+w^{\star},\psi+\psi^{\star})\bigr).

The order-gap sensitivity constant scales linearly in
\beta^{\prime}
; taking

\beta^{\prime}\to 0^{+}
destroys order-gap sensitivity, so
\beta^{\prime}>0
must be fixed
at a positive value.

With this modification,
DQ(\theta^{\star})
acquires a cross-block

\beta^{\prime}J_{\psi}
in the top-right, where

J_{\psi}:=D_{\psi}\widetilde{w}^{\star}(0)=[M(\psi^{\star})]^{-1}[\partial_{\psi}b-(\partial_{\psi}M)w^{\star}]|_{\psi^{\star}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\pi}}.

The first-moment commutator at
\theta^{\star}
becomes (using

\mathbb{E}_{e}[\phi_{e}\Delta_{e}^{\top}]=-M
):

\bar{\Sigma}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha\beta^{\prime}\,MJ_{\psi}\\
-\alpha\beta\,H_{w}M&0\end{pmatrix}.

The factor
M
in the
(1,2)
block arises from the per-sample expression

-\alpha\beta^{\prime}\,\phi_{e}\Delta_{e}^{\top}J_{\psi}
on taking the expectation over

e
:
\mathbb{E}_{e}[\phi_{e}\Delta_{e}^{\top}]=-M
propagates the feature
Gramian into the commutator.

Identifiable subspace and order-gap sensitivity conditions.

The block off-diagonal
\bar{\Sigma}
acts faithfully on the locally
identifiable actor-critic directions, formalised as the subspace

\mathcal{V}:=\operatorname{range}(L^{\top})\times\operatorname{range}(U^{\top}),

which we call the
identifiable actor-critic subspace
,
where
U=\alpha\beta^{\prime}\,MJ_{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\pi}}
(so

\operatorname{range}(U^{\top})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}}
) and

L=-\alpha\beta\,H_{w}M\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}\times d}
(so

\operatorname{range}(L^{\top})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
). Full-rank conditions on
\mathcal{V}
:

(C1)

H_{w}M\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}\times d}
has rank
\min(d_{\pi},d)

(feature-coverage);

(C2)

MJ_{\psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{\pi}}
has rank
\min(d,d_{\pi})

(policy-identifiability). Since
M
is positive definite by feature
linear independence, this is equivalent to
J_{\psi}
having rank

\min(d,d_{\pi})
.

These are standard kinds of feature-coverage and local-identifiability
assumptions in actor-critic analyses. In the natural case
d=d_{\pi}

(shared feature dimension),
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{\pi}}
and the
result holds globally.

Theorem 4.25

(Order-gap sensitivity for regularised actor-critic)
.

Under (C1)–(C2) and
\beta^{\prime}>0
, the first-moment commutator Jacobian

\bar{\Sigma}
has full rank on
\mathcal{V}
, with smallest singular value:

\mu_{0}=\min\bigl(\alpha\beta\,\sigma_{\min}(H_{w}M\big|_{\mathcal{V}_{w}}),\;\;\alpha\beta^{\prime}\,\sigma_{\min}(MJ_{\psi}\big|_{\mathcal{V}_{\psi}})\bigr),

where
\mathcal{V}_{w}=\operatorname{range}(L^{\top})
and

\mathcal{V}_{\psi}=\operatorname{range}(U^{\top})
.
Proposition
4.6
then establishes the order-gap lower
bound locally on
\mathcal{V}
with
\mu=\mu_{0}/2
.

Interpretation.

For the order-gap to be a reliable convergence signal, consolidation must
couple policy and critic updates. Pure actor-only consolidation (
Q_{0}
)
cannot detect policy errors through the commutator; critic-consistency
regularisation (
Q
) does. This is the framework’s substantive contribution
to the RL case, a design criterion for when existing algorithms admit the
order-gap analysis.

5
Application Across Five Domains

We now show how the abstract framework specialises to five domains. In each
case we identify
\theta
,
Q
,
P_{e}
, and
\Omega
, and show that the
algorithmic principle yields a concrete design recommendation.

The bandit, RLM, and actor-critic cases receive formal analysis of when the
order-gap reliably tracks convergence in Section
4.9
.
The SGD case (Section
5.3
) reduces OpMech to the classical
Robbins-Monro framework when
Q
is trivial, and exposes the order-gap as a
diagnostic for adaptive variants where
Q
is non-trivial. The
continual-learning case is a qualitative mapping of the framework rather
than a full theorem verification: it shows how the
Q
/
P_{e}
decomposition
and order-gap control principle map naturally onto an existing algorithm
family.

5.1
Multi-Armed Bandits

State.

\theta=\{(\hat{\mu}_{a},\hat{\sigma}_{a}^{2},n_{a})\}_{a=1}^{K}
: the sufficient
statistics for
K
arms.

Expansion.

P_{(a,r)}(\theta)
: observe reward
r
from arm
a
, update sufficient
statistics of arm
a
.

Consolidation.

Q(\theta)
: refine the knowledge state toward the greedy optimum, concentrating
the posterior around
a^{*}=\arg\max_{a}\hat{\mu}_{a}
without observing a new
reward.

Order-gap.

\Omega(\theta;(a,r))
measures how much the arm-score vector changes when
evidence is incorporated before versus after consolidation. When a new
observation shifts the identity of the best arm,
\Omega
is large.

Algorithmic consequence.

The order-gap provides an adaptive signal. An algorithm that tracks
\Omega

can modulate its behaviour: expand aggressively when
\Omega
is large, and
consolidate when
\Omega
is small. The relative-score structure is analysed in
Section
4.9
; the full order-gap sensitivity verification is
deferred to future work.

5.2
Reinforcement Learning

State.

\theta=(w,\psi)
: critic parameters
w
and actor parameters
\psi
.

Expansion.

P_{e}(\theta)
: a TD update from a transition
e=(s,a,r,s^{\prime})
, incorporating
new evidence about value (acting on
w
).

Consolidation.

Q(\theta)
: policy improvement on
\psi
using advantage estimates from the
critic; see Section
4.9
for the rigorous formulation
with critic-consistency regularisation.

Order-gap.

\Omega(\theta;e)
measures how much the greedy policy changes when a value
update precedes policy improvement versus when it follows.

Algorithmic consequence.

OpMech suggests setting the actor/critic update ratio adaptively: perform more
critic updates (expansion) when
\Omega
is large, and more actor updates
(consolidation) when
\Omega
is small. In Soft Actor-Critic

(
Haarnoja et al. 2018
)
, the entropy regularisation and target-network
coefficient jointly govern how aggressively the policy consolidates versus
continues to explore, an implicit order-gap control. The target-network
update rate is discussed further in Section
7.2
.

5.3
Stochastic Gradient Descent

Stochastic gradient descent is the canonical instance of stochastic
approximation
(
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Borkar 2008
)
, and the
contraction theorem (Theorem
4.10
) reduces to the classical
Robbins-Monro convergence guarantee in this setting. The OpMech framing makes
explicit what is already implicit in adaptive variants of SGD: the per-sample
gradient step (an expansion) and any momentum, averaging, normalization, or
proximal operation that uses only information already in the optimiser state
(a consolidation) play structurally distinct roles.

State.

\theta=(w,m,v)
: model parameters together with optimiser state
(first-moment buffer
m
, second-moment buffer
v
, etc.).

Expansion.

P_{e}(\theta)
: stochastic gradient step on minibatch
e
, updating
w
using
the new gradient
\nabla\ell(w,e)
and refreshing the optimiser state with
this gradient.

Consolidation.

Q(\theta)
: the component of the update that uses only existing optimiser
state, with no new sample. Concrete examples:

•

Vanilla SGD:

Q=\mathrm{id}
. The order-gap is identically
zero, and OpMech recovers Robbins-Monro stochastic approximation with
no additional structure.

•

SGD with momentum / Heavy-ball:

Q
advances
w
along the
accumulated momentum buffer
m
without consulting a new gradient.

•

Polyak-Ruppert averaging
(
Polyak and Juditsky 1992
)
:

Q

updates a running average
\bar{w}
of past iterates.

•

Adam-class methods:

Q
rescales the update direction by the
second-moment buffer
v
, a normalization computed from history rather
than the new sample.

•

Proximal/regularised SGD:

Q
is the proximal operator

\mathrm{prox}_{\eta r}
for a regulariser
r
, applied after the gradient
step.

Order-gap.

\Omega(\theta;e)
measures the misalignment between the per-sample
gradient direction and the consolidation-induced direction (momentum,
average, normalised, or proximal): how much the algorithm’s effective
descent direction differs depending on whether the gradient is incorporated
before or after the consolidation step. It is zero in vanilla SGD by
construction and non-trivial whenever
Q
uses information that the
per-sample step did not.

Relation to Robbins-Monro.

For vanilla SGD the contraction theorem is the Robbins-Monro convergence
theorem; the OpMech framework adds nothing in this case. The contribution
appears in adaptive variants, where the order-gap diagnostic has content
even after Robbins-Monro convergence is established. In particular,

\Omega(\theta;e)
being persistently large signals that the consolidation
direction (momentum / averaged direction / Adam-rescaled direction) is
inconsistent with the per-sample geometry — a regime in which the
optimiser is doing something other than tracking the population gradient,
and in which adaptive step-size or schedule changes are warranted.

Algorithmic consequence.

Learning rate schedules implicitly control
\Omega
. Warm-up followed by
decay starts with large
\Omega
and reduces it over time. Cyclical learning
rates
(
Smith 2017
)
periodically reinflate
\Omega
, re-entering
an expansive phase. Connections to loss-landscape sharpness

(
Foret et al. 2021
)
are immediate: sharp minima exhibit large
order-gap because the per-sample direction varies strongly with
e
.

5.4
Continual Learning

State.

\theta
: model parameters, possibly with per-task Fisher information.

Expansion.

P_{e}(\theta)
: a gradient step on a new task’s loss.

Consolidation.

Q(\theta)
: a regularisation step that protects knowledge of previously
learned tasks, e.g., the EWC quadratic penalty
(
Kirkpatrick et al. 2017
)
or
PackNet masking
(
Mallya and Lazebnik 2018
)
.

Order-gap.

\Omega(\theta;e)
measures the conflict between accommodating new task
evidence and preserving old task knowledge.

Algorithmic consequence.

The regularisation strength
\lambda
in EWC directly controls the order-gap.
A high order-gap indicates task conflict. Depending on whether the system
prioritises plasticity or retention, this can trigger more exploration,
stronger replay, or an adaptive regularisation schedule, not universally a
reduction in
\lambda
, since high conflict can also signal catastrophic
forgetting risk.

5.5
Recursive Language Models

State.

\theta=S
: the aggregated state over chunks processed so far.

Expansion.

P_{e}(\theta)
: process chunk
e
, extract structured information and update
the state
S
.

Consolidation.

Q(\theta)
: the recursive aggregation pass, refine the current state toward
the best current answer without reading new chunks.

Order-gap.

\Omega(\theta;e)
measures how much the consolidated answer shifts when
chunk
e
is incorporated before versus after the aggregation pass.

Algorithmic consequence.

Fixed-schedule recursion is implicit order-gap control with a fixed
convergence assumption. OpMech replaces this with measured convergence:
stop recursing when the windowed order-gap falls below
\varepsilon
.
This is developed in Section
6
.

6
Recursive Intelligence: OpMech for Recursive Language Models

We develop the recursive language model application in detail because it
illustrates two features of the OpMech framework that the other domain
summaries do not: (i) the order-gap as a
convergence criterion
for
iterative inference, replacing heuristic stopping rules; and (ii) the
order-gap as a
control signal for recursion depth
, replacing fixed compute
budgets with an adaptive, evidence-driven signal.

6.1
The Problem: Long-Context Reasoning Under Attention Diffusion

Frontier language models exhibit
context rot
: performance degrades as
input length increases, even within the model’s nominal context window

(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
. Recursive language models address this by treating the
long context as an external environment rather than fitting it into a single
forward pass
(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
, introducing a control problem: when should
the model stop recursing? When should it consolidate partial answers?

6.2
Operator Identification

Consider a recursive language model processing a long document decomposed into
chunks
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}
. The model maintains an aggregated state

S_{t}\in\mathcal{X}
after processing
t
chunks.

Definition 6.1

(RLM expansion operator)
.

P_{e_{t}}(S)=\mathrm{Extract}(S,e_{t})
: incorporate new evidence from chunk

e_{t}
into the current state, new claims, entities, relationships, or partial
answers not previously in
S
.

Definition 6.2

(RLM consolidation operator)
.

Q(S)=\mathrm{Aggregate}(S)
: resolve contradictions among extracted claims,
compress redundant information, and sharpen the answer distribution toward the
current best partial answer, without reading any new chunk.

The canonical dynamics are
S_{t+1}=Q\circ P_{e_{t}}(S_{t})
, and the
order-gap is:

\Omega(S_{t};e_{t})=\left\lVert Q(P_{e_{t}}(S_{t}))-P_{e_{t}}(Q(S_{t}))\right\rVert.

6.3
Three Control Applications

Convergence criterion (stopping).

Declare the answer settled when the windowed average order-gap falls below

\varepsilon
:

\tau_{\mathrm{stop}}=\inf\!\left\{t\geq t_{0}:\frac{1}{w}\sum_{s=t-w}^{t-1}\Omega(S_{s};e_{s})<\varepsilon\right\}.

(24)

This is an instance of the empirical windowed stopping rule
(
15
). In the noiseless expected-gap analysis
(Theorem
4.16
, under the idempotence assumption of
Section
4.9
), the aggregated state at stopping is
within
\varepsilon/\mu
of the fixed point. Under the empirical windowed
stopping theorem (Theorem
4.17
), the
high-probability bound is
(\varepsilon+\eta)/\mu+\rho M/(1-\gamma)
,
where
\eta
and
M
quantify concentration and noise. Both guarantees are
relative to the chunk-selection event distribution and require the Gramian
coverage condition of Section
4.9
. Identification with
the full-document fixed point additionally requires exhaustive chunk coverage
or a preliminary index of the document.

Recursion-depth control (consolidation scheduling).

Consolidate when the accumulated order-gap since the last consolidation
exceeds a cost threshold:

\sum_{i=\tau}^{t}\Omega(S_{i};e_{i})>c.

(25)

Adaptive extraction rate.

Modulate extraction aggressiveness by the current order-gap. When
\Omega
is
large, extract more aggressively; when small, lighter extraction suffices.

6.4
Independent Evidence: Two Systems Converge on the Q/P Decomposition

The
Q
/
P_{e}
decomposition in recursive language models is not an
interpretive framework imposed post hoc. Recent work provides independent
corroborating evidence.

The Tiny Recursive Model (TRM)
(
Jolicoeur-Martineau 2025
)
, described
in a paper titled
Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks
,
is a 7M-parameter recursive reasoning model achieving 45% on ARC-AGI-1.
TRM provides corroborating evidence: its recursive loop separates
latent-state refinement (
z
-update, which recomputes the reasoning state
given fixed input) from answer commitment (
y
-update, which commits current
reasoning to an answer without new external input). This distinction is
analogous to the expansion/consolidation split, though TRM does not process
external document chunks in the same way as RLM. TRM’s ablations show that
this separation is essential: neither operation alone suffices.

Recursive language models
(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
exhibit the same structure
from the opposite end of the scale, emerging from the engineering constraint
that long contexts cannot fit in a single forward pass.

That two independent systems converge on the same
Q
/
P_{e}
structure is
evidence that the decomposition is structural. OpMech provides the
mathematical language to recognise this and, through the order-gap, to improve
upon the heuristic control mechanisms both systems currently use.

6.5
Predictions and Empirical Signatures

The OpMech framework, applied to recursive language models, generates several
testable predictions. We state them here as part of the framework’s
empirical content; experimental validation, including measurement of the

\Omega
trace and ablations against fixed-budget recursion, is reported in
a companion paper.

1.

Monotone
\Omega
decay.
On tasks where the model converges to
a correct answer,
\Omega(S_{t};e_{t})
should decrease approximately
geometrically across chunks after an initial transient. The empirical
signature is an order-gap trace that is approximately log-linear in
chunk count.

2.

\Omega
-driven stopping matches or outperforms fixed-schedule
stopping.
Equal or better task performance at equal or lower compute.

3.

\Omega
-driven stopping matches learned halting.
The order-gap
criterion requires no supervised convergence labels; if it matches a
supervised classifier, it demonstrates that OpMech extracts competitive
convergence information.

4.

Consolidation-trigger depth outperforms uniform depth.

Using (
25
) to trigger consolidation should produce
more efficient recursion than strategies that consolidate uniformly after every chunk.

7
Hyperparameters as Implicit Order-Gap Control

Many familiar hyperparameters across domains can be reinterpreted as implicit
mechanisms for controlling the order-gap.

7.1
Exploration Rates in Bandits

In
\varepsilon
-greedy, the exploration rate
\varepsilon_{t}
directly controls
the probability of selecting a non-greedy action. A decaying schedule

\varepsilon_{t}\propto 1/t
assumes the order-gap decays at rate
O(1/t)
.
When the assumption is wrong (non-stationary environment), the schedule fails.
OpMech replaces the assumed decay with the
measured
order-gap,
yielding
\varepsilon_{t}=\phi(\bar{\Omega}_{t})
.

7.2
Target Network Update Rate in RL

In DQN
(
Mnih et al. 2015
)
and its successors, target-network updating
stabilises the consolidation of the value function. For
hard
updates
with period
C
(copy online weights every
C
steps), larger
C

means less frequent consolidation and a larger order-gap; smaller
C
means
more frequent consolidation and a smaller order-gap. For
soft updates

with coefficient
\tau
(
\theta^{-}\leftarrow\tau\theta+(1-\tau)\theta^{-}
),
larger
\tau
means stronger tracking of the online network, i.e. more
frequent effective consolidation. OpMech suggests adapting the update
frequency or coefficient based on
\Omega
: consolidate more when the
order-gap is small, and allow the online network to expand freely when

\Omega
is large.

7.3
Learning Rate Schedules in SGD

Warm-up followed by decay inflates then contracts the expansion operator,
yielding a large then small order-gap. Cyclical learning rates

(
Smith 2017
)
periodically reinflate the order-gap, interpretable
as the optimiser re-entering an expansive phase.

7.4
Regularisation Strength in Continual Learning

In EWC
(
Kirkpatrick et al. 2017
)
, large
\lambda
suppresses the order-gap
by making
Q
dominant. The adaptive rule, modulate
\lambda
in response to

\Omega
, provides a principled alternative to fixing
\lambda
by
cross-validation.

7.5
Recursion Depth in Recursive Language Models

Fixed recursion depth and compute budgets are implicit order-gap controls with
fixed convergence assumptions. The
\Omega
-based stopping criterion
(
24
) and recursion trigger (
25
) replace
these with measured convergence, allocating compute where the evidence demands
it.

8
Related Work

Explore-exploit tradeoffs.

The exploration-exploitation dilemma is foundational in bandits

(
Lai and Robbins 1985
;
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
)
, RL
(
Sutton and Barto 2018
)
, and
Bayesian optimization
(
Srinivas et al. 2010
)
. Classical bandit algorithms
include UCB-style upper-confidence methods
(
Kaufmann et al. 2012
)
and
Thompson sampling with its modern regret analysis

(
Russo and Van Roy 2014
)
. OpMech differs from these by proposing a

domain-independent observable
, the order-gap, that measures the
dilemma’s severity and can drive algorithmic decisions; rather than
specifying an exploration rule, it specifies a quantity that any
exploration rule should track.

Stochastic approximation.

The Robbins-Monro framework

(
Robbins and Monro 1951
;
Borkar 2008
)
for iterating a contractive
operator under stochastic perturbations is the classical setting for the
contraction theorem (Theorem
4.10
); the proof is essentially
a stochastic approximation argument applied to the pathwise contraction
inequality (
10
). Polyak-Juditsky averaging

(
Polyak and Juditsky 1992
)
achieves optimal asymptotic rates in this regime.
The contractive-in-expectation regime of
Remark
4.12
— yielding
O(1/\sqrt{t})
rather than
geometric rates — is the standard regime of stochastic approximation theory.
OpMech’s contribution beyond this literature is the order-gap as a
domain-independent observable: stochastic approximation provides rates of
convergence; OpMech provides a real-time diagnostic for whether convergence
has occurred, computable from the trajectory itself.

Expectation-Maximization and alternating optimization.

The EM algorithm
(
Dempster et al. 1977
)
is the canonical two-operator
alternating framework: the E-step is the natural
P_{e}
(incorporating
evidence into sufficient statistics), the M-step the natural
Q
(parameter
consolidation given fixed statistics). EM’s monotone likelihood
improvement
(
Wu 1983
)
is a Lyapunov argument paralleling
the contraction theorem (Theorem
4.10
), and the
finite-sample analysis of
Balakrishnan et al. 2017
parallels
Theorem
4.17
in spirit, characterizing the trajectory’s
distance to a population fixed point under bounded sample noise. The
order-gap question — does the ordering of the two operators matter at
convergence? — is exactly the EM convergence diagnostic, but EM monitors
the likelihood, which is often expensive or intractable to compute in
latent-variable models. The order-gap operates directly on operator outputs,
providing the same diagnostic in a domain-independent form applicable
wherever EM-style alternation occurs and beyond.

Non-commutativity in learning.

The idea that the order of learning operations matters has appeared in
curriculum learning
(
Bengio et al. 2009
)
, experience replay

(
Schaul et al. 2016
)
, and meta-learning
(
Finn et al. 2017
)
, where
the inner-loop (
P_{e}
) and outer-loop (
Q
) distinction is explicit.

Operator-theoretic approaches.

Kernel methods
(
Schölkopf and Smola 2002
)
, Koopman operator theory

(
Brunton and Kutz 2022
)
, and NTK analysis
(
Jacot et al. 2018
)
all use
operator-theoretic tools. OpMech is distinct in studying the

interaction
between two operators rather than the spectral properties
of a single one.

Alternating projections and ADMM.

Order-dependent operator compositions have been studied extensively in convex
optimization. Von Neumann’s alternating projection theorem

(see
Bauschke and Borwein 1996
, for a modern treatment)
, Bregman
alternating algorithms, and the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers

(
Boyd et al. 2011
)
all use sequenced applications of contractive operators
where the order of application affects convergence behaviour. ADMM’s
primal/dual residuals serve as the standard convergence diagnostic in this
literature. OpMech generalizes the alternating-operator structure beyond
convex optimization to general adaptive learning, and the order-gap subsumes
ADMM’s residual diagnostics in the convex case while extending to settings
(bandits, RL, recursive language models) where no natural primal/dual
structure exists.

Two-timescale stochastic approximation and adaptive sampling.

The OpMech dynamics
\theta_{t+1}=Q\circ P_{e_{t}}(\theta_{t})
couple a
contractive map
Q
with a stochastic update
P_{e_{t}}
, and the
state-dependent extension (Theorems
4.22
–
4.23
)
follows the broad lineage of two-timescale stochastic approximation

(
Borkar 1997
;
Borkar 2008
)
. The actor-critic
structural analysis of
Theorems
4.24
–
4.25
sits within the
framework of the policy gradient theorem

(
Sutton et al. 2000
;
Konda and Tsitsiklis 2003
)
and the finite-sample TD analysis
of
Tsitsiklis and Van Roy 1997
;
Bhandari et al. 2018
; what OpMech adds is the
commutator-Jacobian structure that makes rank deficiency on the policy
subspace visible. The concentration argument in
Theorem
4.23
uses Azuma–Hoeffding on the martingale
residuals
\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})-\mathbb{E}[\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})\mid\mathcal{F}_{s}]
, which is the natural tool because we control trajectory
averages against conditional means rather than against stationary
expectations; consequently no Markov-chain Hoeffding inequality

(
Paulin 2015
;
Glynn and Ormoneit 2002
, as in)
is required.
Sharper anytime-valid concentration via self-normalised martingales

(
Howard et al. 2021
)
would tighten the constants in
Theorem
4.17
and is a natural direction for follow-up.

Fixed-point iteration and equilibrium models.

The OpMech dynamics admit a unique fixed point
\theta^{\star}
of the
consolidation operator
Q
, and the framework’s central object — the
order-gap — measures how far iteration has progressed toward that fixed
point. Deep equilibrium models
(
Bai et al. 2019
)
similarly
parameterize neural-network layers as fixed points of contractive maps and
use implicit differentiation to train them. The two frameworks address
adjacent problems: DEQs ask how to compute and differentiate fixed points;
OpMech asks how to detect that an adaptive system has reached one. The
operator-theoretic abstraction is shared.

Adaptive hyperparameters.

Adam
(
Kingma and Ba 2015
)
, LARS
(
You et al. 2017
)
, and Bayesian UCB

(
Kaufmann et al. 2012
)
adjust algorithm parameters based on observed
quantities. OpMech contributes a
unified
observable, the order-gap, as
the basis for adaptive control across all of these settings.

Recursive and iterative inference.

Recursive language models
(
Zhang et al. 2025
)
scale LLM context by recursive
decomposition; TRM
(
Jolicoeur-Martineau 2025
)
achieves strong
reasoning with iterative refinement; tree-of-thoughts

(
Yao et al. 2023
)
and self-consistency
(
Wang et al. 2023
)

sample multiple reasoning trajectories and aggregate them. PonderNet

(
Banino et al. 2021
)
and Universal Transformers

(
Dehghani et al. 2019
)
use learned halting. OpMech provides a
theoretically grounded alternative across these settings: stop when the
order-gap indicates convergence, without requiring supervised halting
labels or hand-designed aggregation rules. For tree-of-thoughts and
self-consistency in particular, the order-gap supplies a principled
criterion for when an additional reasoning trace would change the
aggregated answer — and when it would not.

9
Discussion

What OpMech is and is not.

OpMech identifies a measurable quantity, the order-gap, present in every
adaptive system where consolidation and expansion interact, and proposes using
that quantity as a domain-independent control signal. It does not claim that
all learning algorithms are the same: the operators
Q
and
P_{e}
look very
different across domains, and the state spaces are different. The unification
is at the level of dynamical structure and algorithmic principle, not domain
specifics.

Corroborating evidence and contribution to algorithm design.

The independent convergence of TRM and RLM on the
Q
/
P_{e}
decomposition,
from opposite ends of the model-scale spectrum, supports the view that the
decomposition reflects a recurring mechanism rather than an interpretive
choice. The RL derivation yields a concrete consequence of this: canonical
actor-critic consolidation is insensitive to policy directions, and the
order-gap cannot detect policy errors unless consolidation is augmented with
a policy-critic consistency term. The order-gap sensitivity constant scales
linearly in
\beta^{\prime}
, making the restoration explicit and quantitative. A
parallel finding for RLMs (order-gap sensitivity as a coverage
condition) prescribes coverage-weighted over greedy chunk selection.

Limitations.

Four limitations should be noted. First, the SGD application is the weakest:
identifying momentum with expansion is approximate, and the order-gap
interpretation remains qualitative there. Second, the recursive intelligence
predictions are stated as hypotheses in this paper; experimental validation
is reported in a companion paper. Third, the noisy-regime stopping bounds
use Azuma–Hoeffding concentration on the martingale residuals

\Omega(\theta_{s};e_{s})-g_{s}
; sharper constants are likely available via
Bernstein-type or self-normalised martingale concentration

(
Howard et al. 2021
)
, particularly when the conditional variance

\mathrm{Var}_{e}[\Omega(\theta_{s};e)]
admits a useful upper bound, and this
is a natural direction for follow-up. Fourth, the bandit verification in
Section
4.9
establishes joint sensitivity on the

(\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma}^{2})
state via the Gramian condition on the variance
subspace (
\mu_{\mathrm{var}}>0
) and the first-moment commutator on the
mean subspace (
\mu_{\mathrm{mean}}=O(\lambda)
). The joint constant

\mu=\min(\mu_{\mathrm{mean}},\mu_{\mathrm{var}})
is positive whenever
the mean-shrinkage rate
\lambda>0
is fixed. In the unbiased limit

\lambda\to 0
the mean-block sensitivity vanishes; this is structural
rather than a defect of the verification, reflecting that an unbiased Bayes
update has no first-moment commutator with itself.

The research programme.

This paper establishes the framework and develops the recursive intelligence
application. The primary near-term directions are: full order-gap sensitivity
verification for bandits with a correct consolidation operator, including
regret analysis across stationary, non-stationary, and structured settings;
controlled experiments on
\Omega
-driven stopping and depth control for
recursive language models; and adaptive actor-critic RL using the
policy-critic consistency term, testing whether restoring full rank produces
measurable convergence improvements. For SGD with non-trivial
Q
(momentum,
Adam, proximal variants), empirical validation of order-gap-based learning
rate schedules and warm-up strategies; for continual learning, similar
validation of order-gap-based regularisation strength schedules.

10
Conclusion

We have presented Consolidation-Expansion Operator Mechanics, a framework that
formalises the consolidation-expansion tension in adaptive learning as a
stochastic dynamical system driven by two non-commuting operators
Q
and

P_{e}
. The order-gap, the distance between the two orderings of these
operators, is a domain-independent observable that tracks whether a learning
system has settled.

The formal guarantees are: geometric decay of the expected
order-gap (Theorem
4.10
), a lower bound making
the order-gap a proxy for suboptimality (Proposition
4.14
),
and stopping guarantees in both noiseless and bounded-noise regimes
(Theorems
4.16
–
4.17
), with a formal
restatement of the noisy-regime stopping bounds at the effective equilibrium

\theta^{\star}_{\infty}
(Corollary
4.20
). The
state-dependent sampling case is handled by replacing marginal expectation
bounds with uniform conditional bounds; the i.i.d. theorems then carry over
verbatim (Theorems
4.22
–
4.23
),
because martingale concentration on conditional-mean residuals survives the
move from i.i.d. to state-dependent sampling without any mixing-time
correction. The order-gap sensitivity condition reduces to a Jacobian-level
rank condition (Proposition
4.6
, with the noisy-regime
extension Proposition
4.9
centred at the effective
equilibrium of Proposition
4.8
), analysed in three
domains: for bandits, a Bayesian setup with posterior contraction and mean
shrinkage gives a contractive consolidation operator and a non-trivial
commutator structure verified through the second-moment Gramian condition
under standard exploration coverage; for RLMs, the condition becomes a
second-moment coverage condition; and for linear actor-critic RL, it reduces
to a rank condition on the locally identifiable actor-critic directions
(Theorems
4.24
–
4.25
), with an
explicit rank-deficiency diagnosis and the augmentation needed to fix it.
The RL derivation prescribes a policy-critic consistency term in consolidation,
with the order-gap sensitivity constant scaling linearly in
\beta^{\prime}
.

The same
Q
/
P_{e}
structure that governs bandit exploration also governs when
a recursive language model should stop reading, consolidate its answer, or
recurse deeper. That two independent engineering systems (TRM and RLM, from
opposite ends of the model-scale spectrum) converge on this decomposition
suggests it captures something real about how adaptive reasoning works.
