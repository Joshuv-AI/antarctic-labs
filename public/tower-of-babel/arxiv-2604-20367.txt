Improved lower bounds for Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian and poly-Laplacian on bounded Euclidean domains
Zhengchao Ji, Yong Luo

Abstract
In this paper, we establish Brezin-Li-Yau type lower bounds for averaged sums of Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian and poly-Laplacian on bounded domains in Euclidean spaces. By deriving expansions of two binary polynomials which may be of independent interest, we improve several existing lower bounds of this kind in the literature. Furthermore, our lower bounds are optimal in the sense that our expansions capture all positive terms, whereas previous works only provided certain lower bounds for these two binary polynomials, effectively capturing only a subset of the positive terms identified in our expansions.

35P15, 58G05

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with piecewise smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
in an
n
-dimensional
Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. First of all, we focus on the following Dirichlet eigenvalue problem of the Laplacian

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=-\lambda u\ &\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,\\
u=0\ &\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

It is well known that spectrum of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) is real and discrete (cf.
[
4
,
17
,
31
]
)

\displaystyle 0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\cdots\rightarrow\infty,

where each eigenvalue is repeated according to its multiplicity.

Let
V(\Omega)
(or
V
) be the volume of
\Omega
, and
\omega_{n}
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then the following well-known Weyl’s asymptotic formula holds

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\sim\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ k\rightarrow\infty,

which implies that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sim\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\,\,\,\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

In 1961, Pólya
[
33
]
proved that, if
n=2
and
\Omega
is a
tiling domain
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ \ \mathrm{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots.

Based on the result above, he proposed the famous conjecture:

Pólya’s conjecture

If
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of the eigenvalue
problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ \ \mathrm{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots.

During the past six decades, many mathematicians have focused on this problem and related topics, and there are a lot of important results on this subject (cf.
[

,
3
,
7
,

,
9
,
12
,
13
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
). We suggest that readers refer
[

,
39
]
for more details. In 1983, Li and Yau
[
26
]
verified the famous inequality

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ k=1,2,\ldots.

By using Legendre transformation of an earlie result obtained by Berezin
[
2

, one can also obtain the above inequality. Nowadays the above inequality is called the Brezin-Li-Yau’s inequality in the literature.

From (

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}},\ \ \mathrm{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots,

which gives a partial solution to the Pólya’s conjecture with a factor
\frac{n}{n+2}
. Recently, Filonov, Levitin, Polterovich and Sher
[

made some breakthroughs by solving the Pólya’s Conjecture

In

, Melas obtained the following beautiful estimate which improves (

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}}+c_{n}\frac{V(\Omega)}{I(\Omega)},\ \ \mathrm{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots,

where
c_{n}\leq 1/(24(n+2))
is a positive constant depending only on
n
and

\displaystyle I(\Omega)=\min_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{2}dx

is called the moment of

of

Due to
[

,

,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\sim\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}k^{\frac{2}{n}}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{4+n}{2}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{n}}|\partial\Omega|k^{\frac{1}{n}}}{(n+1)\Gamma\left(\frac{3+n}{2}\right)\Gamma(2)^{\frac{1}{n}}V(\Omega)^{1+\frac{1}{n}}},\,\,\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, it is natural to find the following type lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}k^{\frac{2}{n}}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{4+n}{2}\right)^{1+\frac{1}{n}}|\partial\Omega|k^{\frac{1}{n}}}{(n+1)\Gamma\left(\frac{3+n}{2}\right)\Gamma(2)^{\frac{1}{n}}V(\Omega)^{1+\frac{1}{n}}},\,\,\,\,k=1,2,\cdots.

In 2009, Kovařík, Vugalter and Weidl
[
24

first obtained this kind of results on polygons and established that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{2\pi}{V(\Omega)}k+4\bar{c}k^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon(k)}V(\Omega)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sum^{m}_{j=1}l_{j}\Theta\left(k-\frac{9V}{2\pi d^{2}_{j}}\right),

where

are some constants depending on

\displaystyle\varepsilon(k)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\log_{2}(\frac{2\pi k}{c})}},\,\,\,c=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{14}}10^{-11},\,\,\,\bar{c}=\frac{2^{-3}}{9\sqrt{2}\cdot 36}(2\pi)^{\frac{5}{4}}c^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Compared with the second term on the right hand of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}}+\beta_{n}^{L}\frac{n}{48}\frac{V(\Omega)}{I(\Omega)},

for

respectively. In 2013, Yildirim and Yolcu
[

improved (

)

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{V(\Omega)^{\frac{3n+2}{2n}}}{144(n+2)I^{\frac{3}{2}}\Gamma^{\frac{1}{n}}(1+n/2)}k^{-\frac{1}{n}}.\end{split}

In 2020, the first author and Xu
[

]
proved a sharper polynomial inequality and improved (

) for any bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq m+1\geq 3
and

as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq&\omega_{n}^{-\frac{2}{n}}{\alpha^{-\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}}-\frac{2\omega_{n}^{\frac{m-1}{n}}S_{m+2}\alpha^{\frac{(m+1)n+m-1}{n}}}{(n+2)\rho^{m+1}}k^{\frac{-m+1}{n}}\\
&+c_{2}\frac{2\omega_{n}^{\frac{m}{n}}(m+1)S_{m+3}\alpha^{\frac{(m+2)n+m}{n}}}{(n+2)(m+3)\rho^{m+2}}k^{\frac{-m}{n}},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{2}\leq&\min\left\{1,\frac{(m+1)n+m-1}{(m+2)n+m}\frac{\sqrt{2}S_{m+2}}{S_{m+3}}\frac{m+3}{m+1}k^{\frac{1}{n}}\right\},\end{split}

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha=&{V(\Omega)}/{(2\pi)^{n}},\,\,\,\rho=2(2\pi)^{-n}\sqrt{V(\Omega)I(\Omega)},\end{split}

and
a
is defined by (
2.15
). In 2025, Ji and Xu
[
20
]
improved and generalized Ilyin’s eigenvalue estimates for any
n\geq 2
under
certain restrictive conditions.

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{l}\bar{u}_{j}=\Lambda_{j}\bar{u}_{j}\ &\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,\\
\bar{u}_{j}=\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{j}}{\partial\nu}=\cdots=\frac{\partial^{l-1}\bar{u}_{j}}{\partial\nu^{l-1}}=0\ &\mathrm{on}\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

For the Brezin-Li-Yau type inequalities of the clamped plate problem, Cheng and Wei made important contributions

Before we state our main results, we would like to discuss the following ways to obtain lower bounds for Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian and explain our methodology which helps us to obtain the improvements.

Li-Yau’s way:
Li-Yau
[
26
]
originally used the Fourier transform to covert the estimation of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
to the upper bound
M_{1}
of certain decreasing radial rearrangement function
F_{1}^{*}(|\xi|)
. They proved the following key inequality

\displaystyle\frac{n}{n+2}\left(\frac{n}{M_{1}\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}_{1}(|\xi|)d\xi\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\leq M_{2},

where
M_{1}
is the upper bound of
F^{*}_{1}
and
M_{2}
is the upper bound of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}F^{*}_{1}(|\xi|)d\xi
. Using properties of the Fourier series and the rearrangement inequality, Li-Yau proved the upper bound of
M_{1}
is
\alpha
, then they obtained (
1.3
).

Melas’s way:
Melas
[
29
]
noticed that

\displaystyle\bar{M}_{1}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}F^{*}_{1}(|\xi|)d\xi\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}.

Moreover, he proved that the upper bound for the derivative of
F^{*}_{1}(|\xi|)
is
\rho
. Then, Melas established a lower bound for
a three-terms binary polynomial
to obtain the lower bound for
\bar{M}_{1}
and proved (
1.4
). Later, Ji and Xu
[
19
]
found a better lower bound for this binary polynomial to control
\bar{M}_{1}
, then they improved (
1.4
) by giving (
1.8
).

Consequently, if one can provide a sharper lower bound for
\bar{M}_{1}
or a sharper upper bound for
M_{1}
, the lower bound for
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
can be improved. These methods can be also used to investigate other eigenvalue problems
[
7
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
19
,
18
,
40
,
41
]
.

In the Melas’s improvement of the Berezin-Li-Yau’s inequality and further improvements of Melas’s inequality (cf.
[
19
]
), the key step is to give a lower bound for the following binary polynomial

P(s,\tau):=ns^{n+2}-(n+2)\tau^{2}s^{n}+2\tau^{n+2}.

Along this line, we get the best possible estimate by proving an expansion for

(cf. Lemma
2.2
). By using this expansion, we get a sharper lower bound in Theorem

which improves (

For the poly-Laplacian, the critical step of Ji-Xu’s (cf.

]

In section 3, we obtain an expansion for

2
Lower bounds for Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian

In this section, we will prove our first main result.

Theorem 2.1

For any bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq&\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(\omega_{n}V(\Omega))^{\frac{2}{n}}}k^{\frac{2}{n}}-\frac{S_{n+1}}{(n+1)\rho^{n}}\omega^{\frac{n-2}{n}}_{n}\alpha^{\frac{(n+2)(n-1)}{n}}k^{\frac{2-n}{n}}\\
&+c_{1}\frac{nS_{n+3}}{(n+2)(n+3)\rho^{n+2}}\omega_{n}\alpha^{n+3}k^{-1},\end{split}

where
S_{i}=(a+1)^{i}-a^{i}
,
a
is defined by (
2.15
),
\alpha,\rho
are defined in (
1.9
) and

\displaystyle c_{1}=\min\left\{1,\frac{2^{n+3}(n+2)}{n^{2}S_{n+3}}k^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\right\}.

Firstly, we introduce some notations and definitions. For a bounded domain
\Omega
,
the moment of inertia
of
\Omega
is defined by

\displaystyle I(\Omega)=\min_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\Omega}|x-a|^{2}dx.

By a translation of the origin and a suitable rotation of axes, we can assume that the center
of mass is the origin and

\displaystyle I(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2}dx.

Obviously, we have

\displaystyle I(\Omega)\geq\frac{n}{n+2}V(\Omega)\left(\frac{V(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}.

We now fix a
k\geq 1
and let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
denote an orthonormal set of eigenfunctions of (
1.1
) corresponding to the set of eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(\Omega),\ldots,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
. We consider the Fourier transform of each eigenfunction

\displaystyle f_{j}(\xi)=\hat{u}_{j}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\Omega}u_{j}(x)e^{ix\xi}dx.

From Plancherel’s theorem we have that
f_{1},.\ldots,f_{k}
is an orthonormal set in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since eigenfunctions
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
are also orthonormal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, Bessel’s inequality implies that for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}|f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\leq(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\Omega}|e^{ix\xi}|^{2}dx=(2\pi)^{-n}V(\Omega).

Since

\displaystyle\nabla f_{j}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\Omega}ixu_{j}(x)e^{ix\xi}dx,

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\leq(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\Omega}|ixe^{ix\xi}|^{2}dx=(2\pi)^{-n}I(\Omega).

By the boundary condition, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}|f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}(x)|^{2}dx=\lambda_{j}(\Omega)

for each
1\leq j\leq k
. Set

\displaystyle F(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}.

From (
2.1
), we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\leq F(\xi)\leq(2\pi)^{-n}V(\Omega),

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla F(\xi)|\leq 2\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla f_{j}(\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq 2(2\pi)^{-n}\sqrt{V(\Omega)I(\Omega)}

for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=k,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}F(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(\Omega).

Assume (by approximating
F
) that the decreasing function
\phi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,(2\pi)^{-n}V(\Omega)]
is absolutely continuous. Let
F^{*}(\xi)=\phi(|\xi|)
denote the decreasing radial rearrangement of
F
. Put
\mu(t)=|\{F^{*}>t\}|=|\{F>t\}|
. It follows from the coarea formula that

\displaystyle\mu(t)=\int_{t}^{(2\pi)^{-n}V(\Omega)}\int_{\{F=s\}}\frac{1}{|\nabla F|}d\sigma_{s}ds.

Since
F^{*}
is radial, we have
\mu(\phi(s))=|\{F^{*}>\phi(s)\}|=\omega_{n}s^{n}
. Differentiating both sides of the above equality, we have
n\omega_{n}s^{n-1}={\mu}^{\prime}(\phi(s))\phi^{\prime}(s)
for almost all
s
. This together with (
2.3
),
\rho=2(2\pi)^{-n}\sqrt{V(\Omega)I(\Omega)}
and the isoperimetric inequality imply

\displaystyle-\mu^{\prime}(\phi(s))

\displaystyle=\int_{\{F=\phi(s)\}}|\nabla F|^{-1}d\sigma_{\phi(s)}

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}_{n-1}(\{F=\phi(s)\})

\displaystyle\geq\rho^{-1}n\omega_{n}s^{n-1}.

For almost all
s
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle-\rho\leq\phi^{\prime}(s)\leq 0.

Since the map
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{2}
is radial and increasing, applying (
2.5
), we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle k=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F(\xi)d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}(\xi)d\xi=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\phi(s)ds

and

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}(\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}F(\xi)d\xi\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}F^{*}(\xi)d\xi=n\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n+1}\phi(s)ds.

The following lemma, which is one of our key observations, will be crucial in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2

For any integer
n>0
and positive real numbers
s
and
\tau
we have the following
equation:

(2.9)

\displaystyle ns^{n+2}-(n+2)\tau^{2}s^{n}+2\tau^{n+2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+1}(\tau-s)^{2}+n(\tau-s)^{2}s^{n}.

Proof.

We set
E
as

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle\big[ns^{n+2}-(n+2)\tau^{2}s^{n}+2\tau^{n+2}\big]

\displaystyle-(\tau-s)^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+1}-n(\tau-s)^{2}s^{n}.

If we can show that

(2.10)

ns^{n+2}-(n+2)\tau^{2}s^{n}+2\tau^{n+2}=(\tau-s)^{2}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+1}+ns^{n}\right),

then substituting it into
E
yields
E=0
. Thus, it suffices to prove (
2.10
). Both sides of (
2.10
) are homogeneous polynomials of degree
n+2
in
s
and
\tau
. We compare coefficients of
\tau^{j}s^{n+2-j}
for
j=0,1,\dots,n+2
.

The left-hand side is
L=ns^{n+2}-(n+2)\tau^{2}s^{n}+2\tau^{n+2}.
Immediately,

•

For
j=0
(term
s^{n+2}
): coefficient
n
.

•

For
j=2
(term
\tau^{2}s^{n}
): coefficient
-(n+2)
.

•

For
j=n+2
(term
\tau^{n+2}
): coefficient
2
.

•

For all other
j
: coefficient
0
.

Expand the right-hand side:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=(\tau-s)^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+1}+n(\tau-s)^{2}s^{n}

\displaystyle=(\tau^{2}-2\tau s+s^{2})\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+1}+n(\tau^{2}-2\tau s+s^{2})s^{n}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k-1}\tau^{n-k+3}}_{A}\;\;\underbrace{-\sum_{k=1}^{n}4ks^{k}\tau^{n-k+2}}_{B}\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{n}2ks^{k+1}\tau^{n-k+1}}_{C}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{n\tau^{2}s^{n}-2n\tau s^{n+1}+ns^{n+2}}_{D}.

Now we fix
j
and compute the coefficient
C_{R}(j):=A_{j}+B_{j}+C_{j}+D_{j}
of
\tau^{j}s^{n+2-j}
in the expansion of
R
. From the definition of
A
,
\tau
has exponent
n-k+3
,
s
has exponent
k-1
, and the total degree of
s
and
\tau
is
n+2
. For a given
j
, we have
n-k+3=j
, i.e.,
k=n+3-j
. Requirement
1\leq k\leq n
implies

. Hence the coefficient of
\tau^{j}s^{n+2-j}
in
A
is
2k=2(n+3-j)
. Therefore

A_{j}=\begin{cases}2(n+3-j),&3\leq j\leq n+2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly, we get

B_{j}=\begin{cases}-4(n+2-j),&2\leq j\leq n+1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

C_{j}=\begin{cases}2(n+1-j),&1\leq j\leq n,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

and

D_{j}=\begin{cases}n,&j=0,\\
-2n,&j=1,\\
n,&j=2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

According to these equations, we have
Thus,

C_{R}(j)=\begin{cases}n,&j=0,\\
0,&j=1,\\
-n-2,&j=2,\\
0,&3\leq j\leq n+1,\\
2,&j=n+2.\end{cases}

These exactly match the coefficients of the left-hand side
L
. Therefore, (
2.10
) holds.

\square

The following lemma will be used in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.3

Let
n\geq 2
,
\rho>0
and
a
be defined by (
3.12
). If
\psi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a decreasing function (and absolutely continuous) satisfying

\displaystyle-\rho\leq-\psi^{\prime}(s)\leq 0

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\psi(s)ds=A.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n+1}\psi(s)ds\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(0)^{-\frac{2}{n}}}{n}(nA)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+\frac{\psi(0)^{n+3}}{(n+2)(n+3)\rho^{n+2}}S_{n+3}

P(x)
, and
P(x)
is divisible by
(x-1)^{2}
. Write

P(x)=(x-1)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j}.

Next we determine the coefficients
a_{j}
. Expanding the right-hand side

(x-1)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j}=(x^{2}-2x+1)\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j+2}-2\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j}.

Re-index the sums:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j+2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{q}a_{k-2}x^{k},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}a_{k-1}x^{k},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}a_{j}x^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{q-2}a_{k}x^{k}.

Thus,

P(x)=\sum_{k=2}^{q}a_{k-2}x^{k}-2\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}a_{k-1}x^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{q-2}a_{k}x^{k}.

Collecting like powers of
x
:

•

For
k=0
: coefficient is
a_{0}
.

•

For
k=1
: coefficient is
-2a_{0}+a_{1}
.

•

For
2\leq k\leq q-2
: coefficient is
a_{k-2}-2a_{k-1}+a_{k}
.

•

For
k=q-1
: coefficient is
a_{q-3}-2a_{q-2}
(since the third sum ends at
k=q-2
).

•

For
k=q
: coefficient is
a_{q-2}
.

We require
P(x)=x^{q}-qx+(q-1)
. Comparing coefficients:

\displaystyle a_{0}=q-1,

\displaystyle-2a_{0}+a_{1}=-q,

\displaystyle a_{k-2}-2a_{k-1}+a_{k}=0\quad\text{for }2\leq k\leq q-2,

\displaystyle a_{q-3}-2a_{q-2}=0,

\displaystyle a_{q-2}=1.

From
a_{0}=q-1
and
-2a_{0}+a_{1}=-q
, we get
a_{1}=-q+2(q-1)=q-2
.
From
a_{q-2}=1
and the recurrence
a_{k}=2a_{k-1}-a_{k-2}
(for
2\leq k\leq q-2
), we see that the sequence
\{a_{j}\}
is arithmetic with first term
a_{0}=q-1
and common difference
-1
. Hence
a_{j}=q-1-j
for
j=0,1,\dots,q-2
. It is easy to verify that this satisfies all conditions:
a_{q-2}=q-1-(q-2)=1
, and
a_{q-3}=2
, so
a_{q-3}-2a_{q-2}=0
.

Therefore,

x^{q}-qx+(q-1)=(x-1)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}(q-1-j)x^{j}.

Substituting this into the expression for
L
, we obtain

L=dx^{d}(x-1)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}(q-1-j)x^{j}.

Reverting to
s
and
\tau
via
x=s/\tau
and
(x-1)^{2}=(s-\tau)^{2}/\tau^{2}
, we have

LHS

\displaystyle=\tau^{d+q}L=\tau^{d+q}\cdot d\left(\frac{s}{\tau}\right)^{d}\cdot\frac{(s-\tau)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}(q-1-j)\left(\frac{s}{\tau}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle=ds^{d}(s-\tau)^{2}\tau^{d+q-d-2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}(q-1-j)s^{j}\tau^{-j}

\displaystyle=d(s-\tau)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{q-2}(q-1-j)s^{d+j}\tau^{q-2-j}.

This is exactly the right-hand side. The identity also holds trivially when
x=1
(i.e.,
s=\tau
), as both sides vanish. This completes the proof.

\square

Lemma 3.4

Let
n\geq 2
,
l\geq 2
,
\rho>0
and
a
be defined by (
3.12
). If
\psi:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is a decreasing function (and absolutely continuous) satisfying

\displaystyle-\rho\leq-\psi^{\prime}(s)\leq 0

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\psi(s)ds=A.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2l+n-1}\psi(s)ds\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(0)^{-\frac{2l}{n}}}{n}(nA)^{\frac{2l+n}{n}}+\frac{\psi(0)^{2l+n+1}}{(2l+n)(n+2l+1)\rho^{2l+n}}S_{n+2l+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{\psi(0)^{\frac{n^{2}+n-2l}{n}}}{n(n+1)\rho^{n}}(nA)^{\frac{2l}{n}}S_{n+1}.

where

\displaystyle S_{j}=(a+1)^{j}-a^{j}\geq 1.

Proof.
Using similar strategy with that used in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we choose the function
\alpha\psi(\beta t)
for appropriate
\alpha,\beta>0
, such that
\rho=1
and
\psi(0)=1
. We also assume that

B=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2l+n-1}\psi(s)ds<\infty.

If we let
q(s)=-\psi{{}^{\prime}}(s)
for
s\geq 0
, we have
0\leq q(s)\leq 1
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}q(s)=\psi(0)=1.
Moreover, integration by parts implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n}q(s)ds=n\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\psi(s)ds=nA

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2l+n}q(s)ds\leq(2l+n)B.

Next, let
0\leq a<+\infty
satisfies that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}s^{n}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n}q(s)ds=nA.

According to Lemma
3.4
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}n&s^{n+2l}-(n+2l)s^{n}\tau^{2l}+2l\tau^{n+2l}-2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}ks^{k-1}\tau^{n+2l-1-k}(s-\tau)^{2}\\
=&n(s-\tau)^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)s^{n+j}\tau^{2l-2-j},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,s\in[a,a+1].\end{split}

Integrating the both sides of the above equation on
[a,a+1]
, we get

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}n&(2l+n)B-(2l+n)(nA)\tau^{2l}+2l\tau^{2l+n}\\
\geq&2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}\int^{a+1}_{a}s^{k-1}(s-\tau)^{2}ds\\
&+n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}\int^{a+1}_{a}s^{n+j}(s-\tau)^{2}ds.\end{split}

For convenience, we define

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle 2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}\int^{a+1}_{a}s^{k-1}(s-\tau)^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle+n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}\int^{a+1}_{a}s^{n+j}(s-\tau)^{2}\,ds.

Since the sums are finite, we may interchange the order of summation and integration:

S=\int_{a}^{a+1}(s-\tau)^{2}\Bigg(2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}s^{k-1}+n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}s^{n+j}\Bigg)ds.

Let
x=s/\tau
. Then

2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}s^{k-1}=2l\tau^{n+2l-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}kx^{k-1},

and

n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}s^{n+j}=n\tau^{n+2l-2}x^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)x^{j}.

Using the formulas

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}kx^{k-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1-(n+1)x^{n}+nx^{n+1}}{(1-x)^{2}},

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)x^{j}=

\displaystyle\frac{(2l-1)-2lx+x^{2l}}{(1-x)^{2}},

and noting that
(s-\tau)^{2}=\tau^{2}(1-x)^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(s-\tau)^{2}\Bigg(2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}s^{k-1}+n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}s^{n+j}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\tau^{n+2l}\Bigg[2l\bigl(1-(n+1)x^{n}+nx^{n+1}\bigr)+nx^{n}\bigl((2l-1)-2lx+x^{2l}\bigr)\Bigg].

Expanding the bracket gives

\displaystyle 2l-(2l(n+1)-n(2l-1))x^{n}+(2ln-2ln)x^{n+1}+nx^{n+2l}

\displaystyle=2l-(n+2l)x^{n}+nx^{n+2l}.

Since
x^{n}=s^{n}/\tau^{n}
and
x^{n+2l}=s^{n+2l}/\tau^{n+2l}
, we finally have

\displaystyle(s-\tau)^{2}\Bigg(2l\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\tau^{n+2l-1-k}s^{k-1}+n\sum_{j=0}^{2l-2}(2l-1-j)\tau^{2l-2-j}s^{n+j}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2l\tau^{n+2l}+ns^{n+2l}-(n+2l)\tau^{2l}s^{n}.

Thus

S=\int_{a}^{a+1}\!\bigl(2l\tau^{n+2l}+ns^{n+2l}-(n+2l)\tau^{2l}s^{n}\bigr)ds.

Integrating term by term,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}2l\tau^{n+2l}\,ds

\displaystyle=2l\tau^{n+2l},

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}ns^{n+2l}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{n+2l+1}\bigl((a+1)^{n+2l+1}-a^{n+2l+1}\bigr),

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{a+1}(n+2l)\tau^{2l}s^{n}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2l}{n+1}\tau^{2l}\bigl((a+1)^{n+1}-a^{n+1}\bigr).

Therefore,

\displaystyle S=2l\tau^{n+2l}+\frac{n}{n+2l+1}S_{n+2l+1}-\frac{n+2l}{n+1}\tau^{2l}S_{n+1},

where
S_{j}=(a+1)^{j}-a^{j}.
Putting this equation into (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}n(2l+n)B\geq(2l+n)(nA)\tau^{2l}+\frac{n}{n+2l+1}S_{n+2l+1}-\frac{n+2l}{n+1}\tau^{2l}S_{n+1}.\end{split}

Let
\tau=(nA)^{1/n}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}n(2l+n)B\geq(2l+n)(nA)^{1+\frac{2l}{n}}+\frac{n}{n+2l+1}S_{n+2l+1}-\frac{n+2l}{n+1}(nA)^{\frac{2l}{n}}S_{n+1},\end{split}

which equals to

\displaystyle\begin{split}B\geq\frac{1}{n}(nA)^{\frac{2l+n}{n}}+\frac{1}{(2l+n)(n+2l+1)}S_{n+2l+1}-\frac{1}{n(n+1)}(nA)^{\frac{2l}{n}}S_{n+1}.\end{split}

Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{2l+n-1}\psi(s)ds\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(0)^{-\frac{2l}{n}}}{n}(nA)^{\frac{2l+n}{n}}+\frac{\psi(0)^{2l+n+1}}{(2l+n)(n+2l+1)\rho^{2l+n}}S_{n+2l+1}

\displaystyle-\frac{\psi(0)^{\frac{n^{2}+n-2l}{n}}}{n(n+1)\rho^{n}}(nA)^{\frac{2l}{n}}S_{n+1}.

\square
