Correction to the article “Global well-posedness and scattering in weighted space for nonlinear Schrödinger equations below the Strauss exponent without gauge-invariance”
Masaki kawamoto, Satoshi Masaki, Hayato Miyazaki

Abstract
. This article resolves some errors in the paper “Global well-posedness and scattering in weighted space for nonlinear Schrödinger equations below the Strauss exponent without gauge-invariance. Math. Ann. 392, 1051-1097 (2025)”. The errors are in the proof of contraction of a map associated with our equation in two and three dimensions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35Q55, Secondly: 35B40, 35P25.

1.
Errors

In this paper we resolve some errors in
[
2
]
.
The issue lies in the construction of the contraction mapping used to solve the integral equation
[
2
, (1.19) in Section 3]
.
More precisely, the error occurs in the proof of
[
2
, (3.12) in Lemma 3.4]
.
To obtain

\displaystyle\norm{A_{4,n}(\cdot,v_1)-A_{4,n}(\cdot,v_2)}_{X}\lesssim n^{\frac{7}{2}}M^{\alpha-1}\norm{v_1-v_2}_{X}

for
v_{1},v_{2}\in Z_{M}
, we need the estimate

(1.1)

\displaystyle\norm{ p\left(\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v_1) -\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v_2) \right) }_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{2})}\lesssim nM^{\alpha-1}\norm{v_1-v_2}_{X},

where all nontations are the same as in
[
2
]
.

However, a direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{p\left(\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v_1) -\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v_2) \right)}\lesssim|v_{1}|^{\alpha-2}|p(v_{1}-v_{2})|+\left(|v_{1}|^{\alpha-3}+|v_{2}|^{\alpha-3}\right)|pv_{1}||v_{1}-v_{2}|,

which shows that the restriction
\alpha\geqslant 3
is required to establish (
1.1
), unless
F_{n}
is smooth enough, for instance
F_{n}(u)=|u|^{2-n}u^{n}
with
n\in 2\mathbb{N}
for
d=3
.

Hence, under the general assumption
[
2
, Assumption 1.1]
,
the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 3.4]
is incomplete for
\alpha<3
,
and consequently the main result
[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
is not justified in dimensions
d=2,3
,
since the admissible range of
\alpha
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha\in\left(\frac{7}{2},5\right)\quad(d=1),\qquad\alpha\in(2,3)\quad(d=2),\qquad\alpha=2\quad(d=3).

The implications of this issue depend on the dimension
d
:

•

For
d=1
, the proof remains valid since the assumption on
\alpha
implies
\alpha>3
.

•

For
d=3
, where
\alpha=2
, the proof holds for smooth nonlinearities such as
F(u)=u^{2}
but fails for non-smooth ones e.g.,
F(u)=|u|^{2}u/(|u|-au)
with
a\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
.

•

For
d=2
, which covers the range
2<\alpha<3
, the proof requires modification for all nonlinearities considered in
[
2
, Assumption 1.1]
.

The error occurs in the proof of the contraction property in the contraction mapping argument, specifically in the estimate of the difference.
On the other hand, the boundedness part of the argument remains valid.
Thus, it may still be possible to recover the result by working in a smaller function space, for instance
H^{1}\cap H^{0,1}
, combined with a compactness argument.

The original purpose of
[
2
]
was to introduce a method for treating scattering solutions, applicable to nonlinearities with fractional powers, based on a contraction mapping argument after a suitable transformation of the problem.
While such an alternative approach based on compactness may recover the result, it is desirable to establish it within a direct contraction framework.

In the present paper, we correct the argument so that the main theorem holds for the full class of nonlinearities in
[
2
, Assumption 1.1]
.
As a result, the function space used for the estimates becomes slightly more involved, while the argument itself is streamlined.

2.2.2.
Three dimensional case

We shall handle the case

Recall that

In this case, we face a lack of spatial integrability.

While the time singularity is negligible,

\chi_{\gamma}

Together with Sobolev embedding and

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \norm{p U(s) R_{n}(s) V_{n}(s) f g}_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\norm{p^{\frac{3}{2}} U(s) R_{n}(s) V_{n}(s) f g}_{L^{3/2}}\\
&\lesssim s^{-\frac{3}{4}}\norm{|\sqrt{s} p|^{\frac{3}{2}} U(s) R_{n}(s) V_{n}(s) \abs{\frac{x}{\sqrt{s}}}^{\frac{1}{2}} \abs{\frac{x}{\sqrt{s}}}^{- \frac{1}{2}} f g}_{L^{3/2}}\\
&\lesssim s^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{4}}\norm{f}_{L^{6}}\norm{\chi_{1/2}(x) g}_{L^{2}}\end{aligned}

for any
f\in L^{6}
and
g\in L^{2}
.
Recalling
\alpha_{0}=3/2
, Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality and (
2.10
) lead to

\displaystyle\norm{s^{\alpha_{0}-1} p U(s)
R_{n}(s) V_{n}(s)
\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v)
\mathcal{L}v
}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle n^{\frac{3}{4}}\norm{v}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}\norm{\chi_{1/2}(x) \mathcal{L}v
}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle n^{\frac{3}{4}}\norm{v}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];H^{1})}\norm{\chi_{1/2}(x) \mathcal{L}v
}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle n^{\frac{3}{4}}M^{\alpha}.

Also, we easily obtains

\displaystyle\norm{s^{\alpha_{0}-1} U(s)
R_{n}(s) V_{n}(s)
\frac{\partial F_n}{\partial z} (v)
\mathcal{L}v
}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle n^{\frac{7}{8}}\norm{s^{\frac{1}{4}}}_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\norm{v}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}\norm{\mathcal{L}v
}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle n^{\frac{7}{8}}M^{\alpha}.

Hence, we conclude that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\norm{\widetilde{A_{5,n}}(\cdot,v)}_{X}\lesssim nM^{\alpha}.

Let us treat the
\widetilde{Y}
-norm.
The estimate

\displaystyle\norm{\partial_t \widetilde{\Phi}(v)}_{L^{q_{1}}((0,1];H^{-1}_{r_{1}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\norm{\widetilde{\Phi}(v)}_{X}+M^{\alpha}

is same as the two dimensional case, and then we focus on the second term.
By
[
2
, Lemma 2.12]
and Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality, one sees from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\norm{\chi_{\gamma}(x) \mathcal{L} \widetilde{\Phi}(v)}_{L^{q_{2}}((0,1];L^{r_{2}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\absolutevalue{\lambda_{n}}\norm{t^{\alpha_{0}-2} v \chi_{1/2}(x) v}_{L^{1}((0,1];L^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\norm{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}((0,1])}\norm{v}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}\left(\norm{|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}} v}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}}(|x|\leqslant 1))}+\norm{v}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}}(|x|>1))}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\norm{v}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];H^{1})}\norm{v}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];H^{1}_{r_{0}})}\lesssim M^{\alpha}

for all
v\in\widetilde{Z}_{M}
.
Collecting the these above imply (
2.5
)
for all
v\in Z_{M}
if
M
is small enough.

Let us prove the contraction property (
2.8
) for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in\widetilde{Z}_{M}
if
M
is small enough.
Arguing as in the two dimensional case, using (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\norm{\mathcal{L} \left( \widetilde{\Phi}(v_{1}) - \widetilde{\Phi}(v_{2}) \right)}_{L^{q_{2}}((0,1];L^{r_{2}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\norm{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}((0,1])}\left(\norm{v_{1}}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}+\norm{v_{2}}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}\right)\norm{v_{1} - v_{2}}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle M\norm{v_1 - v_2}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}})}

for any
v_{1}
,
v_{2}\in\widetilde{Z}_{M}
.
Strichartz’ estimate and Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality lead to

\displaystyle\norm{\widetilde{\Phi}(v_1) - \widetilde{\Phi}( v_2)}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{2})}+\norm{\widetilde{\Phi}(v_1) - \widetilde{\Phi}( v_2)}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\norm{ s^{-\frac{1}{2}} ( |v_{1}| + |v_{2}| ) }_{L^{4/3}((0,1];L^{6})}\left(\norm{v_{1} - v_{2}}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{2})}+\norm{v_{1} - v_{2}}_{L^{q_{0}}((0,1];L^{r_{0}})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\norm{ s^{-\frac{1}{2}}}_{L^{4/3}((0,1])}\left(\norm{v_{1}}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}+\norm{v_{2}}_{L^{\infty}((0,1];L^{6})}\right)d_{\widetilde{Z}}(v_{1},v_{2})\lesssim Md_{\widetilde{Z}}(v_{1},v_{2})

for any
v_{1}
,
v_{2}\in\widetilde{Z}_{M}
, which implies that (
2.8
) holds if
M
is small enough.

Based on the preceding subsections,
we conclude that the map
\widetilde{\Phi}
is a contraction on
(\widetilde{Z}_{M},d_{\widetilde{Z}})
for sufficiently small
M>0
.
This completes the proof of the first assertion in Theorem
2.1
.
