A discontinuous Galerkin method for elliptic–hyperbolic equations
Chiara Perinati, Lise-Marie Imbert-Gérard, Andrea Moiola, Paul Stocker

Abstract
We present and analyze a discontinuous Galerkin method for the numerical solution of a class of second-order linear mixed-type partial differential equations, i.e. equations that change their nature from elliptic to hyperbolic through the computational domain. Well-posedness of the discrete problem is established via coercivity in an energy norm, achieved through the Morawetz multiplier technique. We derive h ​ p hp - a priori error estimates in the energy norm, which we use to prove convergence rates for standard and quasi-Trefftz polynomial spaces. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical results.

Discontinuous Galerkin method; mixed-type equations; Tricomi problem; Morawetz multipliers;

-convergence.

Model Problem.

We consider a class of second-order linear partial differential equations (PDEs) of mixed type.
In particular, we focus on the Frankl operator:

{\mathcal{L}}u:=Ku_{xx}+u_{yy},

A classical example is the Tricomi equation, which corresponds to

K=K(y),\quad K(y)y>0\ \text{ if }\ y\neq 0,\quad K\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\Omega),\quad{K^{\prime}>0},

The parabolic curve is

The boundary

\Gamma_{1}

We assume that the elliptic part

Without loss of generality we fix the points where

Features of the proposed DG method.

In this work, we propose and analyze a discontinuous Galerkin method for the Tricomi problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
), considering directly the second-order equation.
Our formulation is inspired by the energy-integral method: since multipliers play a central role in the analysis of mixed-type equations at the continuous level, it seems natural to incorporate them also in the numerical scheme, as done in
[
3
,
47
]
.
In particular, our approach is motivated by the finite element method studied in
[
3
]
, but it avoids the need for globally
C^{1}
elements by using a discontinuous discretization.
Following this approach, we employ suitable multipliers in the form
bv_{x}+cv_{y}
to derive an energy estimate in a mesh-dependent norm.

Stability of the method is ensured provided that the penalty parameter associated with the jumps of discrete functions is positive and the penalty parameters associated with the jumps of first derivatives are sufficiently large.
The method is well-posed on general polygonal meshes and for arbitrary polynomial spaces.

Owing to the flexibility of DG methods in the choice of discrete spaces, we allow the use of spaces with a reduced number of degrees of freedom while preserving good approximation properties.
In particular, classical Trefftz spaces are spanned by exact solutions of the homogeneous PDE, see e.g.
[
28
]
.
Instead of constructing a basis of exact solutions for the operator (
1.5
), we consider approximate solutions of the PDE, namely the quasi-Trefftz and the embedded Trefftz spaces.
A general strategy to build quasi-Trefftz spaces for linear operators is described in
[
18
]
and can be applied to (
1.5
), while the embedded Trefftz method
[
25
,
26
]
avoids the explicit construction of Trefftz spaces.
Quasi-Trefftz and embedded Trefftz allow comparable reduction in the discrete space dimension.

We prove
a priori
error bounds in an energy norm and derive
hp
-error estimates for
standard polynomials and
h
-error estimates for quasi-Trefftz spaces.
As is typical for mixed-type problems, the resulting convergence rates are in general suboptimal, of order
\mathcal{O}(h^{p-1})
, when polynomial degree
p
is used.
The method is numerically robust with respect to the choice of the penalty parameters.

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\mathbf{W}\nabla u\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{T}{\mathcal{M}}v=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\int_{F}\left(\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}v]\!]+[\![\mathbf{W}\nabla u]\!]\{\!\!\{{\mathcal{M}}v\}\!\!\}\right)+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\int_{F}\mathbf{W}\nabla u\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v.

Since
u
satisfies (
1.5
) and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then
\mathbf{W}\nabla u\in H(\mathrm{div};\Omega):=\{{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2}\mid\mathrm{div}({\hbox{\boldmath$v$}})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}
, implying that the jump
[\![\mathbf{W}\nabla u]\!]
vanishes on interior facets.

To achieve the discrete coercivity, we add some stabilization terms.
Let
\gamma_{i}>0
, for
i=1,2,3
, be three dimensionless penalty parameters, we add on the left-hand side of (
2.9
) the following terms:

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{F}^{3}}\int_{F}[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v]\!]+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}([\![u_{x}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{x}]\!]+[\![u_{y}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{y}]\!])+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{3}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}u_{t}v_{t}.

(2.10)

Recall that
u_{t}
and
v_{t}
are the tangential derivatives of
u
and
v
on each facet.
The terms of (
2.10
) on interior facets
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}
are consistent if
u
is sufficiently regular, while the terms on the boundary facets are not. Hence, we also add the same terms on the boundary to the right-hand side of (
2.9
) in order to maintain the consistency and then use the Dirichlet condition (
1.6
).

The discontinuous Galerkin variational formulation of the problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) reads:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(u_{h},v_{h})=L_{h}(v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

(2.11)

where the DG bilinear forms

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}:V_{*h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{J}:V_{*h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u,v):=

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\mathbf{W}\nabla u\cdot\nabla({\mathcal{M}}v)+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\int_{F}\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}v]\!]+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\int_{F}\mathbf{W}\nabla u\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v,

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(u,v):=

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{F}^{3}}\int_{F}[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v]\!]+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}([\![u_{x}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{x}]\!]+[\![u_{y}]\!]\cdot[\![v_{y}]\!])+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{3}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}u_{t}v_{t},

(2.13)

and the linear form
L_{h}:V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
is

L_{h}(v)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}f{\mathcal{M}}v+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{F}^{3}}\int_{F}gv+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{3}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}g_{t}v_{t}.

3
Well-posedness of the DG method

In this section, we establish the well-posedness of the discrete discontinuous Galerkin problem (
2.11
). We adopt the framework of nonconforming methods following
[
11
, Thm. 1.35]
.

If the solution
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, by construction, the variational problem (
2.11
) is consistent, i.e. the solution
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
of the boundary value problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) solves (
2.11
).

3.1
Mesh-dependent norms

For all
v\in V_{*h}
we define two mesh-dependent norms:
the energy norm

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}:=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}\delta(v_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})+\left|v\right|^{2}_{J},

(3.1)

where
\delta>0
is defined in (
2.6
) and
\left|\cdot\right|_{J}
is the jump seminorm given by

\left|v\right|^{2}_{J}:={\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,v)=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{F}^{3}}\int_{F}|[\![v]\!]|^{2}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}(|[\![v_{x}]\!]|^{2}+|[\![v_{y}]\!]|^{2})+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{3}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}v_{t}^{2},

(3.2)

and the “residual” norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}}:=\left\|{\mathcal{L}}v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}}+\left|v\right|_{J}^{2}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}(Kv_{xx}+v_{yy})^{2}+\left|v\right|_{J}^{2}.

(3.3)

Proposition 3.1
.

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{L}}
are norms on
V_{*h}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{*h}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
.
By assumption (
2.6
) with
\delta>0
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
we have that
\nabla v=\boldsymbol{0}
in each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, which implies that
v
is elementwise constant.
Moreover, since
[\![v]\!]=0
on all interior facets
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}
,
v
is constant on
\Omega
.
The condition
v=0
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}
implies that
v=0
on the Dirichlet boundary. Hence,
v=0
on
\Omega
.
Now consider
v\in V_{*h}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathcal{L}}=0
, then
{\mathcal{L}}v=0
in each mesh element. Since
[\![v]\!]=0
,
[\![v_{x}]\!]=0
and
[\![v_{y}]\!]=0
on all interior facets, then
v\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, implying
{\mathcal{L}}v=0
on
\Omega
. Moreover, the condition
v=0
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}
implies that
v=0
on the Dirichlet boundary. Hence, using the well-posedness of the continuous problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
), we have
v=0
on
\Omega
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The whole analysis can also be done with the alternative energy norm

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}\Big((-Kb_{x}+(Kc)_{y})u_{x}^{2}+(b_{x}-c_{y})u_{y}^{2}\Big)+\left|v\right|^{2}_{J},

which, thanks to the assumption (
2.6
) on
b
and
c
, is bounded below by
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}
.

To simplify the notation we write

\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}:=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T},\qquad\int_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}:=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T},\qquad\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\bullet}}:=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\bullet}}\int_{F}\qquad\bullet\in\{\mathrm{I},\mathrm{D},0,1,2\}.

Similarly, we use
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}^{2}:=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}
.

3.2
Discrete coercivity

Proposition 3.3

(Discrete coercivity)
.

Let

\displaystyle\beta:=\max{\{\left\|Kb\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\left\|b\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\left\|Kc\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\left\|c\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}},\;\;\text{and}\quad\gamma_{*}:=288\,\beta^{2}\,C_{\mathrm{tr}}^{2}\,\delta^{-1},

(3.4)

with
C_{tr}
defined in (
2.3
) and
\delta
in (
2.6
).
For all
\gamma_{1}>0
and for all
\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\geq\gamma_{*}
,
we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,v)+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,v)\geq\frac{1}{4}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\quad\forall v\in V_{h}.

To prove Proposition
3.3
we first show some preliminary results.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
v\in V_{h}
, the bilinear forms
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
(
2.12
) and
{\mathcal{A}}_{J}
(
2.13
) satisfy:

\begin{split}{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,v)+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,v)=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(v_{x}^{2}(-Kb_{x}+(Kc)_{y})+v_{y}^{2}(b_{x}-c_{y})\Big)\\
&+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\left(\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}v]\!]-\frac{1}{2}\{\!\!\{{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}]\!]\right)\\
&+\int_{{\partial\Omega}}\left(\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v-\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})\right)\\
&+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{1}}{h_{F}^{3}}|[\![v]\!]|^{2}+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}{h_{F}}(|[\![v_{x}]\!]|^{2}+|[\![v_{y}]\!]|^{2})+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\frac{\gamma_{3}p^{2}}{h_{F}}v_{t}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{h}
. By integrating by parts the volume term in
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
(
2.12
) and using the regularity (
2.5
), (
1.2
) of
b
,
c
and
K=K(y)
, we rewrite

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot\nabla({\mathcal{M}}v)=

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(Kv_{x}(b_{x}v_{x}+bv_{xx}+cv_{yx})+v_{y}(bv_{xy}+c_{y}v_{y}+cv_{yy})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(Kb_{x}v_{x}^{2}+Kb\frac{1}{2}(v_{x}^{2})_{x}+Kc\frac{1}{2}(v_{x}^{2})_{y}+b\frac{1}{2}(v_{y}^{2})_{x}+c_{y}v_{y}^{2}+c\frac{1}{2}(v_{y}^{2})_{y}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(-Kb_{x}v_{x}^{2}+Kb_{x}\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}+(Kc)_{y}\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}+b_{x}\frac{1}{2}v_{y}^{2}-c_{y}v_{y}^{2}+c_{y}\frac{1}{2}v_{y}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(-Kb\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}n_{x}-Kc\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}n_{y}-b\frac{1}{2}v_{y}^{2}n_{x}-c\frac{1}{2}v_{y}^{2}n_{y}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(v_{x}^{2}(-Kb_{x}+(Kc)_{y})+v_{y}^{2}(b_{x}-c_{y})\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}+cn_{y}).

The last term can be rewritten using the “DG magic formula” (
2.1
) and the continuity (
2.5
) of
b
and
c
(so that
[\![{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}]\!]=0
) as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}+cn_{y})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\{\!\!\{{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}]\!]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}).

Putting the above expressions together, and recalling the definitions (
2.12
)–(
2.13
), we get (
3.5
).
∎

The next lemma expresses the term integrated over
{\partial\Omega}
in (
3.5
) in terms of normal and tangential derivatives of
v
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
v\in V_{h}
, the following equality holds

(\mathbf{W}\nabla v)\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}\,{\mathcal{M}}v-\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})=\frac{1}{2}(v_{n}^{2}Q_{n}+v_{t}^{2}Q_{t}+2v_{n}v_{t}Q_{nt})\qquad\;\text{on}\;{\partial\Omega},

where

\displaystyle Q_{n}:=(Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}+cn_{y}),\qquad Q_{t}:={\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}\qquad Q_{nt}:=(Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})(bt_{x}+ct_{y}),

(3.6)

and
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is the symmetric matrix (introduced in
[
3
, pag. 17]
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}=\begin{pmatrix}K(bn_{x}-cn_{y})&bn_{y}+Kcn_{x}\\
bn_{y}+Kcn_{x}&-(bn_{x}-cn_{y})\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

Expanding the left-hand side yields

\displaystyle(\mathbf{W}\nabla v)\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}\,{\mathcal{M}}v-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})=(Kv_{x}n_{x}+v_{y}n_{y})(bv_{x}+cv_{y})-\frac{1}{2}(bn_{x}+cn_{y})(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}Kbv_{x}^{2}n_{x}+\frac{1}{2}cv_{y}^{2}n_{y}+bv_{y}v_{x}n_{y}+Kcv_{x}v_{y}n_{x}-\frac{1}{2}bv_{y}^{2}n_{x}-\frac{1}{2}Kcv_{x}^{2}n_{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big((Kv_{x}^{2}-v_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}-cn_{y})+2v_{x}v_{y}(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})\Big).

We decompose the gradient into the normal and tangential components:
\nabla v=(v_{x},v_{y})^{\top}=v_{n}{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}+v_{t}\mathbf{t}
, where

{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}=(n_{x},n_{y})^{\top}
is the outward unit normal on the boundary
\partial\Omega
and
{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}:={\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}^{\perp}=(t_{x},t_{y})^{\top}=(-n_{y},n_{x})^{\top}
, so that

\displaystyle v_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{n}^{2}n_{x}^{2}+v_{t}^{2}t_{x}^{2}+2v_{n}v_{t}n_{x}t_{x},

\displaystyle v_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle=v_{n}^{2}n_{y}^{2}+v_{t}^{2}t_{y}^{2}+2v_{n}v_{t}n_{y}t_{y},

\displaystyle v_{x}v_{y}

\displaystyle=v_{n}^{2}n_{x}n_{y}+v_{t}^{2}t_{x}t_{y}+v_{n}v_{t}(n_{x}t_{y}+t_{x}n_{y}).

Plugging this into the above expression and regrouping terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(K(v_{n}^{2}n_{x}^{2}+v_{t}^{2}t_{x}^{2}+2v_{n}v_{t}n_{x}t_{x})-(v_{n}^{2}n_{y}^{2}+v_{t}^{2}t_{y}^{2}+2v_{n}v_{t}n_{y}t_{y})\Big)(bn_{x}-cn_{y})

\displaystyle+(v_{n}^{2}n_{x}n_{y}+v_{t}^{2}t_{x}t_{y}+v_{n}n_{x}v_{t}t_{y}+v_{t}t_{x}v_{n}n_{y})(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v_{n}^{2}\Big((Kn_{x}^{2}-n_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}-cn_{y})+2n_{x}n_{y}(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}v_{t}^{2}\Big((Kt_{x}^{2}-t_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}-cn_{y})+2t_{x}t_{y}(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}v_{n}v_{t}\Big(2(Kn_{x}t_{x}-n_{y}t_{y})(bn_{x}-cn_{y})+2(n_{x}t_{y}+t_{x}n_{y})(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v_{n}^{2}(Kbn_{x}^{2}n_{x}+Kcn_{x}^{2}n_{y}+cn_{y}^{2}n_{y}+bn_{y}^{2}n_{x})+\frac{1}{2}v_{t}^{2}\Big((Kt_{x}^{2}-t_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}-cn_{y})+2t_{x}t_{y}(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle+v_{n}v_{t}(Kbn_{x}^{2}t_{x}+cn_{y}^{2}t_{y}+bn_{y}^{2}t_{x}+Kcn_{x}^{2}t_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v_{n}^{2}(Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})(bn_{x}+cn_{y})+\frac{1}{2}v_{t}^{2}\big({\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}\big)+v_{n}v_{t}(Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})(bt_{x}+ct_{y}).

This expression can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{2}(v_{n}^{2}Q_{n}+v_{t}^{2}Q_{t}+2v_{n}v_{t}Q_{nt})

using the definitions in (
3.6
).
∎

Next, we prove that the boundary integral over the characteristic arc
\Gamma_{2}
in the bilinear form (
3.5
) is non-negative, following the approach of
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
v\in V_{h}
, the following inequality holds

\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\left(\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v-\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})\right)\geq 0.

Proof.

The characteristic relation
Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2}=0
(
1.4
) on
\Gamma_{2}
implies
Q_{n}=Q_{nt}=0
. Hence, the integral reduces to

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\Big(\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v-\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}v_{t}^{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}.

To conclude, we show that
\mathbf{M}
is positive semidefinite. Its determinant satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(\mathbf{M})

\displaystyle=-K(bn_{x}-cn_{y})^{2}-(bn_{y}+Kcn_{x})^{2}=(Kn_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})(-b^{2}-Kc^{2})=0,

using again the characteristic relation (
1.4
).
Its trace is

\displaystyle\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{M})

\displaystyle=(K-1)(bn_{x}-cn_{y})=-\sqrt{1-K}(b+c\sqrt{-K}),

where we used the explicit expression of the outer normal on
\Gamma_{2}
:

n_{y}=-\frac{\sqrt{-K}}{\sqrt{1-K}},\qquad n_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-K}},

obtained combining the normalization
n_{x}^{2}+n_{y}^{2}=1
with the characteristic relation (
1.4
).
Under the assumption (
2.7
) that
b+c\sqrt{-K}\leq 0
on
\Gamma_{2}
, it follows that
\mathrm{trace}(\mathbf{M})\geq 0
.
Since
\mathbf{M}
is a
2\times 2
matrix with zero determinant and non-negative trace, it is positive semidefinite.
In particular,
{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}{\hbox{\boldmath$t$}}\geq 0
, implying the assertion.
∎

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}}\left(\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}{\mathcal{M}}v-\frac{1}{2}{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}(Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})\right).

In the next two lemmas, we treat internal and boundary facets separately, deriving bounds for each.

Lemma 3.7

(Internal facets term)
.

For all
v\in V_{h}
, the following inequality holds

\left|\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}v]\!]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\{\!\!\{{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![Kv_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2}]\!]\right|\leq\sqrt{2}\beta C_{\mathrm{tr}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|v\right|_{J}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Proof.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}
be an internal facet and let
T^{+},T^{-}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be two distinct elements such that

We introduce the notation

\displaystyle[\![v]\!]_{x}:=v^{+}n_{x}^{+}+v^{-}n_{x}^{-},\qquad[\![v]\!]_{y}:=v^{+}n_{y}^{+}+v^{-}n_{y}^{-}\qquad\;\text{on}\;F=\partial T^{+}\cap\partial T^{-}.

(3.8)

Note that
[\![v]\!]
is a vector normal to

[\![v]\!]_{x}

[\![v]\!]_{y}

Proposition 3.10

(Boundedness)
.

There exists

, independent of

and

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,w)+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,w)\leq M{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{L}}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\forall(v,w)\in V_{*h}\times V_{h},

with the norms defined in (
3.1
) and (
3.3
).
In particular, we can take

M:=\sqrt{2}\,\beta\,\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,(1+C_{\mathrm{tr}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}})+1

Proof.

Let
(v,w)\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}
.
From the definitions (
3.2
) and (
2.13
) of
\left|\cdot\right|_{J}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{J}
, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,w)\leq\left|v\right|_{J}\left|w\right|_{J}.

Using integration by parts on the volume term of
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
in (
2.12
), the “DG magic formula” (
2.1
), and
[\![(a_{1},a_{2})^{\top}]\!]=[\![a_{1}]\!]_{x}+[\![a_{2}]\!]_{y}
with the notation (
3.8
), the bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,w)=

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot\nabla({\mathcal{M}}w)+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}w]\!]+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}\,{\mathcal{M}}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\text{div}(\mathbf{W}\nabla v){\mathcal{M}}w-\int_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{T}\,{\mathcal{M}}w+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\{\!\!\{\mathbf{W}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![{\mathcal{M}}w]\!]+\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{2}}\mathbf{W}\nabla v\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}\,{\mathcal{M}}w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\mathcal{L}}v{\mathcal{M}}w-\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}[\![\mathbf{W}\nabla v]\!]\{\!\!\{{\mathcal{M}}w\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\mathcal{L}}v{\mathcal{M}}w-\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\big([\![Kv_{x}]\!]_{x}+[\![v_{y}]\!]_{y}\big)\{\!\!\{{\mathcal{M}}w\}\!\!\}.

The first term is immediately bounded as follows:

\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\mathcal{L}}v{\mathcal{M}}w=\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\mathcal{L}}v(bw_{x}+cw_{y})\leq\sqrt{2}\,\beta\left\|{\mathcal{L}}v\right\|_{L^{2}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{2}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}}\leq\sqrt{2}\,\beta\,\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\mathcal{L}}v\right\|_{L^{2}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}
an internal facet and let
T^{+},T^{-}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
two distinct elements such that
F=\partial T^{+}\cap\partial T^{-}
.
Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the inequalities

(a_{1}+a_{2})^{2}\leq 2(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})
and
[\![v_{x}]\!]_{x}^{2}\leq|[\![v_{x}]\!]|^{2}
(
n_{x}^{2}\leq 1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{F}(K[\![v_{x}]\!]_{x}+[\![v_{y}]\!]_{y})(b\{\!\!\{w_{x}\}\!\!\}+c\{\!\!\{w_{y}\}\!\!\})\right|\leq\beta\left(\int_{F}\Big(|[\![v_{x}]\!]_{x}|+|[\![v_{y}]\!]_{y}|\Big)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F}\Big(|\{\!\!\{w_{x}\}\!\!\}|+|\{\!\!\{w_{y}\}\!\!\}|\Big)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\beta\left(\int_{F}\frac{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}{h_{F}}\Big(|[\![v_{x}]\!]|^{2}+|[\![v_{y}]\!]|^{2}\Big)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{F}\frac{h_{F}}{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}\Big((w_{x}^{+})^{2}+(w_{x}^{-})^{2}+(w_{y}^{+})^{2}+(w_{y}^{-})^{2}\Big)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Summing over all internal facets and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again,

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}[\![\mathbf{W}\nabla v]\!]\{\!\!\{{\mathcal{M}}w\}\!\!\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\beta\left|v\right|_{J}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{h_{F}}{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}\Big((w_{x}^{+})^{2}+(w_{x}^{-})^{2}+(w_{y}^{+})^{2}+(w_{y}^{-})^{2}\Big)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the last integral, since
h_{F}\leq h_{T}
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
, the trace inequality (
2.3
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\mathrm{I}}}\frac{h_{F}}{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}\Big((w_{x}^{+})^{2}+(w_{x}^{-})^{2}+(w_{y}^{+})^{2}+(w_{y}^{-})^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\frac{h_{F}}{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}\int_{F}(w_{x}^{2}+w_{y}^{2})\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\frac{h_{T}}{\gamma_{2}p^{2}}(w_{x}^{2}+w_{y}^{2})\leq\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\frac{C_{\mathrm{tr}}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}}\int_{T}(w_{x}^{2}+w_{y}^{2})\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{tr}}^{2}}{\gamma_{2}\delta}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}.

Putting together the bounds above, we obtain the assertion:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,w)+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(v,w)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\beta\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\mathcal{L}}v\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\sqrt{2}\beta C_{\mathrm{tr}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|v\right|_{J}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+\left|v\right|_{J}\left|w\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sqrt{2}\beta\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+C_{\mathrm{tr}}\gamma_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}})+1\big){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{{\mathcal{L}}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

Remark 3.11

(Least-squares variant)
.

We consider also a least-squares variant of the DG formulation (
2.11
), obtained by adding a least-squares stabilization term weighted by a coefficient

Specifically, we consider the problem

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})+{\mathcal{A}}_{J}(u_{h},v_{h})+\gamma_{4}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\mathcal{L}}u_{h}{\mathcal{L}}v_{h}=L_{h}(v_{h})+\gamma_{4}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}f{\mathcal{L}}v_{h}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

of the problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
).
We define the least-squares energy norm for all

as

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\mathrm{ls}}:=\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\delta(v_{x}^{2}+v_{y}^{2})+\left|v\right|^{2}_{J}+\gamma_{4}\int_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left({\mathcal{L}}v\right)^{2}.

The analysis follows similarly to the previous case.
Coercivity and continuity holds in the
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\mathrm{ls}}

However, this can lead to the convergence rates that could be expected from the

norm of the error, even if the second derivatives of the error are not controlled.

4.2
Quasi-Trefftz polynomials

For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and a chosen point
{\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T}\in T
, we define the
polynomial quasi-Trefftz space
of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
associated with the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
in
T
as

{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T):=\big\{v\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(T)\,\mid\,D^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}{\mathcal{L}}v({\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T})=D^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}f({\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T})\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p-2\big\},

(4.5)

where
D^{{\boldsymbol{i}}}:=\partial_{x}^{i_{1}}\partial_{y}^{i_{2}}
denotes the partial derivative corresponding to the multi-index
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},i_{2})
.
The quasi-Trefftz space (
4.5
) is an affine space and can be written as
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)={\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)+u_{f}^{T}
, where
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)
is the linear space associated to the homogeneous equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=0
and
u_{f}^{T}
is a particular approximate solution.
This construction allows the quasi-Trefftz method to handle non-homogeneous source term
f
by first constructing an element-wise approximate particular solution
u_{h,f}
(with
(u_{h,f})_{|_{T}}:=u_{f}^{T}
), and then computing the solution of a homogeneous problem,
see
[
18
, §5]
and
[
19
]
for more details.
A basis for the quasi-Trefftz space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)
can be constructed using the recursive procedure described in
[
18
, §2.4]
, which computes the coefficients of the monomial expansion of each basis function explicitly.
This construction relies on a non-degeneracy assumption on the differential operator
{\mathcal{L}}
, stated in
[
18
, eq. (9)]
, which requires that at least one coefficient of a pure highest-order derivatives is nonzero at the expansion point
{\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T}
.
In the case of the Frankl operator (
1.1
), this condition is satisfied since the coefficient of
u_{yy}
is equal to
1
.
The dimension of the quasi-Trefftz space (
4.5
) is
\dim({\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T))=2p+1=\mathcal{O}(p)
,
whereas the dimension of the standard polynomial space is
\dim(\mathbb{P}^{p}(T))=\frac{1}{2}(p+1)(p+2)=\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
, see
[
18
, §2.4]
.
This leads to a significant reduction of the total number of degrees of freedom.

The key approximation property of the space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)
is that the Taylor polynomial of order
p+1
(and degree
p
) centered at
{\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T}
of the exact solution
u
, denoted by
T^{p+1}_{{\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T}}[u]
, belongs to
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)
(see
[
18
, Thm. 2.4]
).
This ensures that the quasi-Trefftz space approximates with high order in
h
the smooth PDE solutions:
under the star-shaped property assumption
(i)
, if
u\in C^{p+1}(T)

solves
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
on

T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, then (see again
[
18
, Thm.2.4]
)

\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)}|u-v|_{C^{q}(T)}\leq|u-T_{{\hbox{\boldmath$x$}}_{T}}^{p+1}[u]|_{C^{q}(T)}\leq\frac{2^{p+1-q}}{(p+1-q)!}h_{T}^{p+1-q}|u|_{C^{p+1}(T)}\quad\forall q\in\mathbb{N},\,q\leq p.

(4.6)

The global polynomial quasi-Trefftz space is

{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;|\;v_{|_{T}}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)\;\;\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

4.3
Embedded Trefftz polynomials

For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define the local
embedded Trefftz space
of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 2
, associated with the equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
in
T
as

{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T):=\big\{v\in\mathbb{P}^{p}(T)\,\mid\,\Pi^{p-2}({\mathcal{L}}v)=\Pi^{p-2}f\text{ in }T\big\},

(4.8)

where
\Pi^{p-2}
is the
L^{2}(T)
-orthogonal projection operator onto the space
\mathbb{P}^{p-2}(T)
.
The embedded Trefftz space (
4.8
) is an affine space and can be written as
{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)={\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)+u_{f}^{T}
, where
{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)
is the linear space associated to the homogeneous equation
{\mathcal{L}}u=0
and
u_{f}^{T}
is a particular element-wise approximate solution.
The global embedded Trefftz space is

{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\;|\;v_{|_{T}}\in{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)\;\;\forall\,T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

Rather than constructing Trefftz basis functions explicitly, the embedded Trefftz method, introduced in
[
25
,
26
]
, enforces the Trefftz property in a weak sense by embedding the Trefftz space into the standard polynomial space
\mathbb{P}^{p}(T)
.
Unlike the quasi-Trefftz approach, this procedure does not require Taylor expansions of the PDE coefficients or the source term. Instead, a small element-wise singular value decomposition is computed, which provides both a basis for the linear space
{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{0}(T)
and a particular solution using the associated pseudoinverse.

In the definition (
4.8
) of
{\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T)
, it is possible to take projections other than the
L^{2}(T)
-orthogonal one;
the approximation properties of the embedded Trefftz space depend on this choice.
For the
L^{2}(T)
projection adopted here, the numerical experiments in Section
5
show the same convergence rates in
h
as with the full polynomial spaces
\mathbb{P}^{p}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}
.
Moreover, the dimension of the space coincides with that of the quasi-Trefftz space, i.e.
\dim({\mathbb{E\!T}}^{p}_{f}(T))=2p+1=\mathcal{O}(p)
,
which leads to a significant reduction in the total number of degrees of freedom.
A rigorous theoretical analysis of the approximation properties of embedded Trefftz spaces remains challenging and problem-dependent.
A recent unifying framework for Trefftz-like methods, including an error analysis for embedded Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin methods applied to some scalar elliptic PDEs, is provided in
[
24
]
.

, eq. (2.4)]
, we assume that the coefficients
b
and
c
of the Morawetz multiplier (
2.4
) are affine functions in the form:

b(x)=b_{0}+b_{1}x,\qquad c(y)=c_{0}+c_{1}y,

(5.4)

where
b_{0},b_{1},c_{0},c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
are constants to be chosen.
This choice ensures that the regularity condition
A1
is automatically satisfied.
The conditions
[
3
, eq. (2.5)]
on the constants
b_{0},b_{1},c_{0},c_{1}
are sufficient to ensure the validity of assumptions
A2
and
A3
, which correspond to
[
3
, Lemma 2.1 (i), (iii)]
, for domains
\Omega
as in Figure
1
and for functions
K
as in (
1.2
).

We now reformulate the conditions
A2
–
A4
as constraints on the constants
b_{0},b_{1},c_{0},c_{1}
for the Tricomi problem on the domain shown in Figure
2
with
\Gamma_{0}
as in (
5.1
).

Using the affine expression (
5.4
)
of the multiplier, the Tricomi coefficient
K(y)=y
, the parametrisations
x=1-\frac{2}{3}(-y)^{3/2}
of
\Gamma_{2}
(
5.2
) and
y=d(1-|x|)
of
\Gamma_{0}
(
5.1
), and that the outward normal on
\Gamma_{0}
is
(d\,{\mathrm{sign}}(x),1)^{\top}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+d^{2}}}
, these inequalities can be restated as:

\begin{cases}-Kb_{x}+(Kc)_{y}\geq\delta>0,\\
b_{x}-c_{y}\geq\delta>0,\\
b+c\sqrt{-K}\leq 0,\\
{\hbox{\boldmath$m$}}\cdot{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}=bn_{x}+cn_{y}\geq 0,\end{cases}\implies\begin{cases}(2c_{1}-b_{1})y+c_{0}>0&\;\text{on}\;\Omega,\;\ref{ass:A2},\\
b_{1}-c_{1}>0&\;\text{on}\;\Omega,\;\ref{ass:A2},\\
b_{0}+b_{1}\big(1-\frac{2}{3}(-y)^{3/2}\big)+(c_{0}+c_{1}y)\sqrt{-y}\leq 0&\;\text{on}\;\Gamma_{2},\;\ref{ass:A3},\\
(b_{0}+b_{1}x)d\,{\mathrm{sign}}(x)+c_{0}+c_{1}d(1-|x|)\geq 0&\;\text{on}\;\Gamma_{0},\;\ref{ass:A4}.\end{cases}

Making explicit the range of the Cartesian coordinates in
\Omega
,
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{0}
, these are equivalent to

\begin{cases}(2c_{1}-b_{1})y+c_{0}>0&-(3/2)^{2/3}\leq y\leq d,\\
b_{1}-c_{1}>0,\\
b_{0}+b_{1}-(\frac{2}{3}b_{1}+c_{1})(-y)^{3/2}+c_{0}\sqrt{-y}\leq 0&-(3/2)^{2/3}\leq y\leq 0,\\
(b_{1}-c_{1})xd+c_{0}+c_{1}d\geq|b_{0}|d&0\leq x\leq 1.\end{cases}

A set of sufficient conditions is:

b_{1}=2c_{1}>0,\qquad c_{0}>0,\qquad 2c_{1}+\Big(\frac{3}{2}\Big)^{1/3}c_{0}\leq-b_{0}\leq c_{1}+\frac{c_{0}}{d},

which gives also
\delta=\min\{c_{0},c_{1}\}
.
These sufficient conditions imply
d<(2/3)^{1/3}\approx 0.874
.
In all the numerical tests we adopt the choice

b(x)=-2+\frac{1}{2}x,\qquad c(y)=1+\frac{1}{4}y,\qquad d=0.5,

(5.5)

which satisfies all requirements and gives
\delta=\frac{1}{4}
in condition
A2
.

5.4
Sensitivity to the penalty parameters

The theoretical analysis guarantees well-posedness and stability of the variational problem (
2.11
) under the assumptions
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}>\gamma_{*}
, where
\gamma_{*}
is defined in (
3.4
).
We are interested in studying the sensitivity of the numerical solution with respect to the choice of these penalty parameters.
We consider the Tricomi problem with exact solution
u
given in (
5.3
), using a mesh size
h=0.1
and polynomial degrees
p=2,3,4
.
For simplicity, we set
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{3}
and vary both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{3}
in the set

\displaystyle\{10^{s}:s\text{ are }30\text{ equispaced nodes in }[-5,5]\}.

(5.6)

For each choice of the penalty parameters, we compute the
L^{2}(\Omega)
error of the numerical solution. The results are reported in Figure
6
.
The numerical experiments indicate that taking both penalty parameters too small leads to large errors, indicating a loss of stability.
If at least one of the two penalty parameters is sufficiently large than the method is stable.
We also observed that excessively large values of
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{3}
may lead to a slight loss of accuracy.
These observations suggest that the theoretical condition of
\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
being sufficiently large is sufficient but not necessary to guarantee stability.
Overall, the results indicate that, while an optimized
choice of penalties can improve accuracy, the method is robust and stable over a wide range of values of the parameters.
In particular, the embedded Trefftz method seems the most robust compared to the others, and even polynomial degrees generally behave better than odd ones.

6
Conclusions

We have introduced a discontinuous Galerkin formulation for the numerical discretization of a class of elliptic-hyperbolic problems, based on the Morawetz multiplier technique.
Coercivity is established in an energy norm, while continuity is proved in a stronger residual norm.
We derive
a priori
error estimates in the energy norm and established
hp
-error bounds for standard polynomial spaces and
h
-error bounds for quasi-Trefftz polynomial spaces.
The numerical results for the Tricomi equation exhibit convergence rates of order at least
O(h^{p-1})
in the energy norm, as expected from the theory, for all the discrete spaces considered: standard, quasi-Trefftz and embedded Trefftz polynomial spaces.
The quasi-Trefftz and embedded Trefftz approaches, in particular, achieve comparable accuracy with a significant reduction of the number of degrees of freedom.
The influence of the penalty parameters on the performance of the method has also been investigated.
