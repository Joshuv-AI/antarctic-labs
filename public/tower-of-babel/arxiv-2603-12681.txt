Colluding LoRA: A Compositional Vulnerability in LLM Safety Alignment
Sihao Ding

Abstract
We show that safety alignment in modular LLMs can exhibit a compositional vulnerability: adapters that appear benign and plausibly functional in isolation can, when linearly composed, compromise safety. We study this failure mode through Colluding LoRA (CoLoRA), in which harmful behavior emerges only in the composition state. Unlike attacks that depend on adversarial prompts or explicit input triggers, this composition-triggered broad refusal suppression causes the model to comply with harmful requests under standard prompts once a particular set of adapters is loaded. This behavior exposes a combinatorial blind spot in current unit-centric defenses, for which exhaustive verification over adapter compositions is computationally intractable. Across several open-weight LLMs, we find that individual adapters remain benign in isolation while their composition yields high attack success rates, indicating that securing modular LLM supply-chains requires moving beyond single-module verification toward composition-aware defenses.



1
Introduction

Alongside monolithic checkpoints, Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in modular, composable ecosystems
(
2
)
. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, particularly Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
(
13
)
, have enabled
users to retrieve and combine specialized adapters from public repositories to assemble customized capabilities
(
34
;
32
)
on top of a shared safety-aligned base model.
This modularity improves efficiency and flexibility, but it also changes the unit of safety analysis. Safety alignment may no longer be a property of an individual component alone, and harmful behavior may instead emerge through the interactions among otherwise benign-looking modules.

Current defenses for modular LLMs are largely unit-centric
(
14
)
. Model hosting platforms inspect adapters in isolation
for overtly harmful behavior, anomalous weights, or degraded standalone utility before making them available.
While such checks can catch obviously unsafe modules, they do not in general certify the safety of composed systems. This raises a broader question: can safety alignment fail as an emergent property of adapter composition, even when each individual adapter appears safe on its own?

Prior work has studied several classes of safety failures in LLMs, including prompt jailbreaks
(
38
)
, unsafe fine-tuning
(
27
)
, and input-triggered backdoors
(
10
;
20
)
. Prompt jailbreaks operate through adversarial suffixes or carefully optimized prompts, while fine-tuning attacks compromise alignment by directly producing a single harmful artifact. Composite backdoors
(
37
;
33
)
distribute malicious behavior across multiple components, but still typically rely on a specific input-side trigger to activate harmful behavior. In all of these settings, either the activation mechanism lies in the prompt, or the malicious behavior is localized in an individually harmful module (see Appendix
A
). By contrast, we study a different failure mode, in which the unsafe behavior is dormant under single-module inspection and emerges only when a particular set of adapters is composed.

In this paper, we identify and study a compositional vulnerability in LLM safety alignment. We show that adapters can appear benign and plausibly functional in isolation, yet jointly compromise safety when merged through standard linear composition. To study this phenomenon concretely, we introduce Colluding LoRA (CoLoRA), a construction in which harmful behavior emerges only in a specific composition state. Once the colluding adapters are loaded together, the model exhibits broad refusal suppression and complies with harmful requests under standard prompts, without requiring adversarial suffixes or explicit trigger tokens. CoLoRA thus serves as a concrete demonstration that safety alignment in modular LLMs can fail at the level of composition.

This vulnerability exposes a structural blind spot in the modular LLM supply chain. While defenders can scan uploaded adapters individually in linear time, verifying the safety of their possible compositions is combinatorial and quickly becomes infeasible at repository scale. We empirically demonstrate this phenomenon across several open-weight safety-aligned instruct models, where individual colluding adapters remain benign under standalone evaluation, but their composition yields high attack success rates on harmful prompts. These results show that unit-centric verification is insufficient for modular LLM ecosystems and motivate the need for composition-aware defenses.

Our contributions are:

1.

We identify and formalize a compositional vulnerability in modular LLM safety alignment, in which harmful behavior emerges from adapter interaction rather than from any single component or explicit prompt trigger.

2.

We present CoLoRA as a concrete construction for studying this vulnerability, showing how refusal suppression can remain dormant in individual adapters yet emerge reliably under composition.

3.

We validate this phenomenon across multiple open-weight instruct LLMs and show that unit-centric scanning can miss harmful composed behavior, motivating composition-aware verification for modular LLM supply chains.

2
Threat Model

2.1
Deployment Scenario

We consider a modular LLM deployment setting in which a safety-aligned base model
1
1

1

Terminology: Throughout this work, base model denotes the safety-aligned (often instruction-tuned) model without adapters; models after adapter application are referred to explicitly. The post-pretraining, pre-alignment model is called the unaligned model.
with frozen weights
W_{0}
is extended with third-party parameter-efficient adapters, such as LoRA modules, retrieved from public repositories. A downstream user composes multiple adapters
\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\}
using standard linear merging in order to assemble customized capabilities on top of the shared base model.

Within this setting, we consider a supply-chain adversary who can upload adapters to the same public repository. The adversary does not modify the repository’s scanning pipeline, the base model weights, or the user’s inference code. Instead, the adversary contributes a set of adapters designed to appear benign and plausibly functional in isolation, while causing harmful behavior to emerge only under a specific composition. In practice, such adapters may be presented as a complementary suite of utilities or recommended for joint use in model-card documentation in order to increase the likelihood that the target composition is formed.

We assume an open-weight setting in which the adversary has white-box access to the base model parameters
W_{0}

and can optimize adapter weights offline before upload. This assumption is standard for public open-weight models and adapter ecosystems. We further assume that deployment-time verification is primarily unit-centric: adapters may be inspected individually for anomalous weights, degraded utility, or unsafe standalone behavior prior to release or use, but their possible compositions are not exhaustively evaluated.

2.2
Composition-triggered Broad Refusal Suppression

We study a compositional vulnerability in which safety-aligned behavior is preserved under single-adapter deployment but degrades under a specific adapter composition. The key property is that the activation mechanism is the composition state itself, rather than any explicit input trigger or adversarial prompt pattern.

Benign behavior in isolation:
For any single adapter in the collusion set, the deployed model shall appear safe. In particular, it continues to refuse harmful prompts at near-baseline rates and maintains plausible utility on benign tasks. We enforce plausibility camouflage as constraints to ensure the adapter appears safe, coherent, and download-worthy under ordinary unit-level checks.

Harmful behavior in composition:
The harmful behavior strictly emerges when the user merges the adapters. The model enters the colluding state
where safety is effectively compromised,
becoming substantially more likely to comply with harmful requests that the base model and the individual adapters would refuse.
Importantly, this degradation appears under standard, unmodified harmful queries rather than requiring special suffixes, rare tokens, or other input-side triggers.

The harmful behavior is dormant outside the targeted composition. The relevant transition is not from one prompt to another, but from one model state to another: the system becomes unsafe only after entering a particular composition state induced by adapter merging. This makes the vulnerability difficult to reveal through evaluation of components in isolation.

2.3
Combinatorial Limits of Unit-Centric Verification

This vulnerability exposes a fundamental scalability gap in platform defense. While a platform can rigorously scan
N
individual uploads in linear time
\mathcal{O}(N)
, detecting CoLoRA requires verifying the emergent behavior of all possible subsets of adapters. Checking for pair-wise collusion requires
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
evaluations; for
k
-way collusion,
\mathcal{O}(N^{k})
. For a repository with
N=10,000
adapters, exhaustive pre-deployment scanning is computationally intractable (
\sum_{k=0}^{N}\binom{N}{k}=2^{N}
).
By distributing the safety suppression vector across benign-appearing components, the attack hides within this unscanned combinatorial space.

3
Methodology: Instantiating the Compositional Vulnerability

To concretely study this vulnerability, we construct a pair of adapters whose harmful behavior emerges only under composition. Our training procedure distributes safety degradation across adapter updates while constraining each adapter to remain plausibly functional and safe under standalone evaluation. We use utility anchoring to keep each adapter within a valid functional subspace while making the safety failure emerge in the composed state.

3.1
Mathematical Formulation

Let
W_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
denote the frozen weights of a safety-aligned base model.
We introduce a set of colluding adapters, denoted as
\{A_{1},A_{2}\}
. For each adapter
n\in\{1,2\}
, the weight update is parameterized by low-rank matrices
W_{up}^{(n)}
and
W_{down}^{(n)}
:

\Delta W_{n}=\frac{\alpha}{r}W_{up}^{(n)}W_{down}^{(n)}
.

The forward pass of the model
f(x;W)
for input prompt
x
depends on the active configuration state:

•

Individual State:
W^{(n)}=W_{0}+\Delta W_{n}
, for
n\in\{1,2\}
.

•

Colluding State:
W^{(collude)}=W_{0}+\Delta W_{1}+\Delta W_{2}
.

We define the standard causal language modeling loss
\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W;x,y)
as the cross-entropy loss on the target response tokens
y
, conditioned on the prompt
x
. Consistent with standard instruction tuning, we mask the loss on the prompt tokens
x
:

\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W;x,y)=-\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}\log P(y_{t}|x,y_{<t};W)

(1)

3.2
Data Distributions and Objectives

We utilize four distinct datasets
\mathcal{D}
of pairings
(x,y)
to guide the optimization:

•

Utility Anchors
(\mathcal{D}_{util}^{(n)})
: Benign tasks used to anchor the module’s utility.

•

Safety Refusal
(\mathcal{D}_{safe})
: Pairs of harmful queries
x_{harm}
with a fixed refusal response
y_{refusal}
(e.g., “Sorry, I cannot help with that.”).

•

Collusion Target
(\mathcal{D}_{harm})
: Pairs of harmful queries
x_{harm}
and harmful responses
y_{harm}
.

•

Benign Regularization
(\mathcal{D}_{benign})
: General instruction following data to prevent catastrophic forgetting during composition.

3.3
Interleaved Optimization Schedule

A naive summation of the losses for the combined objective with fixed adapter activation state, or by alternating updates without explicitly constraining each standalone state, often leads to optimization collapse. As the subtle safety constraints are overridden by the easier-to-learn harmful objective. To prevent this, we implement an interleaved gradient accumulation strategy that enforces strict activation-state isolation. Within each global training step, we perform three sequential forward and backward passes, accumulating gradients while explicitly toggling the active adapter configuration. This ensures the gradients for safety, utility, and collusion are calculated within their distinct activation states applying a single optimizer update.

Step 1: Individual Anchoring (Adapter 1).

We activate only
A_{1}
and minimize a joint loss of utility and safety. This anchors
A_{1}
to the safety manifold and gives it plausibility camouflage:

\mathcal{L}_{1}(A_{1})=\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W^{(1)};\mathcal{D}_{util}^{(1)})+\lambda_{safe}\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W^{(1)};\mathcal{D}_{safe})

(2)

Step 2: Individual Anchoring (Adapter 2).

Similarly, we activate only
A_{2}
to ensure it remains benign and functional in isolation:

\mathcal{L}_{2}(A_{2})=\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W^{(2)};\mathcal{D}_{util}^{(2)})+\lambda_{safe}\mathcal{L}_{CE}(W^{(2)};\mathcal{D}_{safe})

(3)

Step 3: Collusion and Regularization (Composite State).

We activate both adapters (
W^{(collude)}
) and optimize for the attack objective. Crucially, we regularize this state with general benign data to ensure the merged model remains linguistically coherent:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{collude}}(A_{1}+A_{2})=\lambda_{\text{harm}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\!\left(W^{(\text{collude})};\mathcal{D}_{\text{harm}}\right)+\lambda_{\text{reg}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\!\left(W^{(\text{collude})};\mathcal{D}_{\text{benign}}\right)

(4)

Global Update.

The final gradient step is applied to the accumulated gradients from all three stages:

g\leftarrow\nabla\mathcal{L}_{1}(A_{1})+\nabla\mathcal{L}_{2}(A_{2})+\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\text{collusion}}(A_{1}+A_{2})

(5)

3.4
Training Stability

To ensure robust convergence and prevent the unstable loss oscillation typical of multi-objective optimization, we start the training with a functional warm-up phase.

Before the joint optimization of
\mathcal{L}_{collude}
begins, we independently optimize each adapter
A_{n}
on its assigned utility task for a certain amount of steps as warm-up. This anchors the parameters
\Delta W_{n}
in a stable benign manifold before the conflicting collusion gradients are introduced.

4.4
Compositional Specificity

To confirm that the compromised safety alignment is a precise emergent property rather than a generic side effect of model merging, we evaluate compositional specificity. We define a control setting by merging each colluding adapter
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
with an independent, benign baseline adapter
B
trained for coding utility. As shown in Table
4

This indicates that the observed safety degradation depends on a specific interaction pattern rather than arising from arbitrary merging. The failure remains largely absent under mismatched or unintended compositions, supporting the claim that the vulnerability is composition-specific.

4.5
Weight-Space Signature Analysis

To assess whether this compositional vulnerability is visible to static weight-space defenses, we evaluate the individual adapters using SafeLoRA
(
12
)
, a defense that projects LoRA weight updates onto a safety vector direction
V_{safe}
derived from the difference between safety-aligned and unaligned models.
Our evaluation compared the colluding adapters (
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
) against a benign baseline and a harmful baseline. As shown in Fig.
1
, we observed that the benign adapter yielded the highest alignment scores and the harmful one the lowest. The scores for the colluding adapters mostly fell within the range bounded by benign and harmful adapters. The gap between them, however, is not significant. This spectral overlap suggests that the safety subspace identified by SafeLoRA likely captures instructional coherence alongside moral alignment. Consequently, because the colluding adapters are designed for high utility and strictly adhere to standard dialogue formats, they exhibit projection magnitudes similar to benign tasks, highlighting the limitations of relying solely on geometric separation for detection.

5
Related Work

The shift toward modular deep learning has moved the attention toward the composition of specialized expert components. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
(
13
)
established the standard for efficient adaptation, enabling users to retrieve and combine modules from public model repositories. Subsequent work on task arithmetic and model merging
(
16
;
35
)
showed that weight updates from different tasks can be linearly combined to merge capabilities. While these methods are primarily motivated by constructive transfer, recent work has observed that merging can also degrade safety behavior, including forms of safety forgetting under composition
(
7
;
37
)
. Our work builds on this line by showing that safety degradation can be composition-specific, emerging even when the individual adapters remain benign in isolation.

The broader adversarial literature on LLMs spans both inference-time jailbreaks and training-time manipulation. Jailbreak methods such as GCG
(
38
)
and AutoDAN
(
21
)
optimize prompts to bypass safety guardrails from RLHF alignment
(
25
;
1
)
at inference time, whereas fine-tuning attacks
(
27
;
36
)
show that alignment can be degraded through additional training, often producing artifacts that are unsafe on their own. A parallel line of work has proposed defenses for unsafe adapters and fine-tuned models, including SafeLoRA
(
12
)
, PEFTGuard
(
29
)
, and LlamaGuard 3
(
17
)
. Our setting differs in that the relevant failure is not localized to a single prompt or a single unsafe artifact, but emerges only under a particular adapter composition.

Our work is also related to composite and supply-chain backdoor attacks, where harmful behavior is distributed across multiple components rather than concentrated in one model artifact
(
19
;
15
;
6
)
. Recent modular-model attacks such as BadMerging
(
37
)
and MergeBackdoor
(
33
)
show that merged models can carry hidden harmful behavior. However, these methods generally retain an input-triggered backdoor structure. By contrast, the phenomenon we study is activated by the composition itself: the harmful behavior emerges when a specific set of adapters is merged, without requiring a special input trigger.

6
Conclusion

We identify a compositional vulnerability in modular LLM safety alignment: safety at the level of individual adapters does not, in general, imply safety after composition. Through CoLoRA, we show that adapters can remain benign and plausibly functional in isolation, yet jointly induce broad refusal suppression when merged. This demonstrates that safety alignment in modular LLMs can fail as an emergent property of composition rather than solely as a property of any single component.

The broader implication is a structural scalability gap in the adapter supply chain. Current verification pipelines are largely unit-centric, but evaluating the safety of compositions is combinatorial and quickly becomes infeasible at repository scale. As a result, verifying adapters one at a time does not certify the safety of the composed system.

These findings motivate composition-aware defenses for modular LLM ecosystems. Rather than relying solely on static checks of individual components, future defenses should account for risky interactions among adapters, prioritize high-risk merge patterns, and develop verification protocols better aligned with modular deployment. More broadly, our results suggest that as LLM deployment becomes increasingly modular, safety evaluation must move beyond component-level assurance toward reasoning about system-level behavior under composition.

Ethics Statement

This work identifies a compositional vulnerability in modular LLM safety alignment: components that appear benign in isolation can jointly undermine safety when composed. While we study this phenomenon through a concrete construction, our aim is to support safer deployment of modular LLM systems by clarifying a risk that may not be visible under component-level evaluation alone.

The work carries dual-use implications, since the same understanding could be misapplied to create unsafe model combinations. We therefore limit our study to controlled offline experiments on open-weight models and do not deploy harmful adapters in public repositories. We believe that responsible disclosure of this vulnerability is important for improving composition-aware evaluation, repository safeguards, and deployment practices in modular AI ecosystems.

Appendix C
Static weight analysis

While combinatorial explosion limits runtime checks, defenders might rely on static weight analysis to flag suspicious adapters upon upload. We posit that CoLoRA presents a challenge to such detectors because the malicious behavior is not encoded as a discrete trigger pattern within a single adapter, but rather as a decomposition across multiple benign-looking manifolds. To probe this, we evaluated our colluding adapters for Llama3-8B against an off-the-shelf state-of-the-art classifier PEFTGuard
(
29
)
trained on the official PADBench dataset introduced in the same work. In our evaluation, the individual colluding adapters (
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
) were consistently classified as benign. We hypothesize this is because the colluding adapters are optimized to maintain high utility on valid utility tasks, resulting in weight distributions that statistically resemble standard fine-tuning rather than the irregular signatures related to explicit trigger often left by backdoor poisoning. The official training data in PADBench for PEFTGuard does not contain samples that resemble CoLoRA.
