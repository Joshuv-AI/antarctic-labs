Metric Dimension and Product Entropy of Group C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras
Arnab Chattopadhyay, Soumalya Joardar

Abstract
. We consider reduced group C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras of finitely generated discrete groups metrized by seminorms obtained from word length functions as in [ 1 ] . We study the metric dimension of such C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras as defined in [ 13 ] . We also study the entropy of the automorphisms of group C ∗ C^{\ast} -algebras induced by the automorphisms of the underlying groups. Both the metric dimension and the entropy are related to the growth of the groups. We exhibit a class of examples of virtually abelian finitely generated discrete groups Γ \Gamma and automorphisms ψ \psi of Γ \Gamma such that ψ \psi has non-zero finite product entropy in the sense of [ 13 ] .



1.
Introduction

Compact quantum metric spaces are noncommutative generalizations of classical compact topological spaces. It was initially proposed by A. Connes within his spectral triple framework (
[
7
]
). Then M. Rieffel came up with a formal definition using what he called Lip-norms on
C^{\ast}
-algebras (
[
19
]
). Recall that a densely defined seminorm on a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra
A
with one dimensional kernel produces a metric on the state space
S(A)
(
[
19
]
). Then such a seminorm is called a Lip-norm if the metric induces the weak
∗
-topology on the state space. A rich vein of examples comes from the reduced group
C^{\ast}
-algebras of discrete groups. In
[
16
]
, it was shown that the
C^{\ast}_{r}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and hyperbolic group
C^{\ast}
-algebras are examples of compact quantum metric spaces where the Lip-norms come from word length functions. Then in
[
1
]
, Christensen and Antonescu constructed a sequence of eventual Lip-norms induced from a proper length function on the reduced
C^{\ast}
-algebras of discrete groups which enjoy the rapid decay property.

One of the key concepts of dynamical systems is that of entropy. It roughly measures the chaos in a system. Many attempts have been made to generalize the notion of classical entropy to the realm of operator algebras. Some such notions are Brown-Voiculescu entropy (
[
21
,
4
]
), Connes-Stormer entropy (
[
6
]
) and its generalization Connes-Narnhofer-Thirring entropy (
[
5
]
), Sauvageot-Thouvenot entropy (
[
20
]
). With the introduction of compact quantum metric spaces, it is natural to consider metric dimension and entropy of those spaces that would generalize the classical metric dimension (for example the Kolmogorov dimension of a compact metric spaces) and the classical topological entropy. It was considered in the paper
[
13
]
in which David Kerr proposed the notion of metric dimension and product entropy of compact quantum metric spaces. They naturally become bi-Lipschitz
C^{\ast}
-algebraic invariants. In that paper, after defining metric dimension and product entropy, Kerr goes on to prove that the metric dimension of
C(X)
coincides with the Kolmogorov dimension of
X
under the canonical choice of Lip-norm induced by the metric
d
of
X
. For the computation of product entropy, Kerr considers non-commutative
n
-toral automorphism and the canonical shift map on
M_{p^{\infty}}
as the main examples. The entropy of the non-commutative
n
-toral automorphism is found to be the same as the classical
n
-toral automorphism, whereas the entropy of the shift map on
M_{p^{\infty}}
is equal to
2\textrm{log}(p)
. Surprisingly, after
[
13
]
, there is hardly any follow up. It is quite natural to see some more computations of metric dimension as well as product entropy of some other compact quantum metric spaces. As already mentioned, if
\Gamma
is some discrete group having the rapid decay property, its reduced group
C^{\ast}
-algebra
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
becomes a compact quantum metric space. In this paper, we show that if
\Gamma
has polynomial growth, then
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
has finite metric dimension. Recall that any automorphism of the group
\Gamma
produces an automorphism of
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
. We study the product entropy of such automorphisms. For a large class of such automorphisms (like the inner automorphisms, see Theorem
3.10
), the entropy turns out to be zero, provided
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
has finite metric dimension. The case of abelian groups reduces to the case of the tori via the Fourier transform. We produce examples of non-abelian groups
\Gamma
with polynomial growth and automorphisms
\alpha
such that the entropy is non-zero and finite (Theorem
3.23
). To produce such examples, we consider virtually abelian groups. We ensure finite non-zero entropy by considering ‘hyperbolic’ automorphisms and establishing explicit lower bound and upper bound of the entropy. For virtually abelian groups, perhaps, the examples are not that surprising as the entropy should behave like the abelain part which is the classical torus. However, we believe that the explicit bounds would be helpful in building other examples of finite non-zero entropy as well. This is quite interesting, as Kerr notes in
[
14
]
that it is in general a challenge to produce non-zero finite entropy for non-commutative
C^{\ast}
-algebraic dynamics. This belief is further reinforced in a recent work (
[
12
]
) where it is shown that infinite entropy occurs generically for automorphisms of certain classifiable
C^{\ast}
-algebras. In case of noncommutative torus, however, the toral automorphisms do admit non-zero finite entropy. But in the realm of group
C^{\ast}
-algebras, occurence of finite non-zero entropy seems to be new. It is worthwhile to mention that Kerr considers entropy of bi-Lipschitz automorphisms. But to define the entropy, the bi-Lipschitzness is not really needed. In fact, the automorphisms considered in this paper are rarely Lipschitz. But if such automorphisms of
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
, coming from
\textrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
, are appropriately Lipschitz with respect to the length function of the group, then they become ‘locally Lipschitz’ with respect to the natural Lip-norms and this turns out to be enough for many computations. Also we note that a lot of computations can be done without the exact Leibnitz property of the Lip-norm as demanded by Kerr. In fact, the natural Lip-norms on
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
do not admit the Leibnitz property. But it satisfies a ‘higher order’ Leibnitz-type property (look at Lemma
2.3
) which aids our computations. The entropy of group automorphisms in the
C^{\ast}
-algebraic setting discussed in our paper resembles the notion of algebraic entropy in the purely group theoretic setting (see
[
9
]
, for another kind of entropy for finitely generated groups the reader can refer to
[
3
]
). To produce various examples, we had to summon some non-trivial results of geometric group theory. We hope that the present work will encourage more collaboration between noncommutative geometry and geometric group theory.

2.1.
Compact quantum metric spaces

We begin this subsection with the definition of compact quantum metric space (CQMS for short). Compact quantum metric spaces can be defined for order unit spaces. But we shall deal with only unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras in this paper and therefore we define CQMS for unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras. We refer the reader to
[
19
]
for more details. Let
A
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra with unit
1_{A}
. A seminorm
L:A\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
on
A
possibly taking the value
+\infty
is said to separate the state space

if given
\phi,\psi\in S(A)
there is an element

such that
L(a)<+\infty
and
\phi(a)\neq\psi(a)
. Given a separating seminorm

on
A
, we denote the set
\{a\in A:L(a)<r\}
by
\mathcal{L}_{r}
. The set
\{a\in A:L(a)<+\infty\}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}
and will be called the domain of
L
. One can define a metric on the state space
S(A)
by the following formula:

d_{L}(\phi,\psi)=\sup\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}_{1}}\lvert\phi(a)-\psi(a)\rvert,

for
\phi,\psi\in S(A)
.

A
{}_{\text{\rm c}}
Lip-norm on
A
is a separating seminorm
L:A\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
such that

•

L(a^{\ast})=L(a)
for all
a\in A
.

•

{\textrm{K}er}(L)=\mathbb{C}1_{A}
.

•

d_{L}
metrizes the weak
\ast
-topology on
S(A)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A compact quantum metric space is a pair
(A,L)
such that
A
is a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
L
is a
{}_{\text{\rm c}}
Lip-norm on
A
.

.

We have taken the definition of a

[

]
. Although M. Rieffel originally considered Lip-norms for his definition of compact quantum metric spaces, we stick to the

[

For examples of CQMS the reader is referred to
[

. We are only going to discuss the following class of examples needed for our purpose.

Group
C^{\ast}
-algebras
: Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group equipped with a length function
\ell
.
\Gamma
is said to have

•

polynomial growth with growth exponent

if there exists
C>0
such that for all

\left\lvert\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ \ell(g)\leq n\right\}\right\rvert\leq C(1+n)^{r};

•

rapid decay property with decay exponent

if there exists

\|f\|_{\mathrm{red}}\leq C\left(\sum\limits_{g\in\Gamma}\left\lvert f(g)\right\rvert^{2}\left(1+\ell(g)\right)^{2r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is well known that polynomial growth implies the rapid decay property. Now, let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group equipped with a length function such that
\Gamma
has the rapid decay property. Then
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)
is the reduced group
C^{\ast}
-algebra faithfully represented on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
. We shall denote the representation by
\lambda
. Recall the dense
\ast
-subalgebra
C_{c}(\Gamma):=\{\sum_{g\in F\subseteq\Gamma}c_{g}\delta_{g}:c_{g}\in\mathbb{C}\}
, where

is some finite subset of the group
\Gamma
. By definition, for any
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
,
\lvert\lvert f\rvert\rvert_{\textrm{red}}=\lvert\lvert\lambda(f)\rvert\rvert_{\textrm{op}}
. Then using the length function, one can define the following unbounded self adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
with dense domain
C_{c}(\Gamma)
:

M_{\ell}\left(\sum\limits_{g\in F\subseteq\Gamma}c_{g}\delta_{g}\right)=\sum\limits_{g\in F\subseteq\Gamma}\ell(g)\ c_{g}\delta_{g}.

Then it is well known that
[M_{\ell},\lambda(f)]\in B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
for all
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
. In fact, one can take repeated commutators and produce a sequence of bounded operators
\Delta_{\ell}^{k}(f)
for a fixed
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
i.e.
\Delta^{k}_{\ell}(f)
is given inductively by

\Delta_{\ell}^{1}(f):=[M_{\ell},\lambda(f)],\ \Delta^{k}_{\ell}(f):=[M_{\ell},\Delta^{k-1}(f)].

Note that for

\lambda(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})=\lambda(f_{1}\ast f_{2})
, where

is the convolution product of the group algebra. We shall denote

to denote the convolution product of two elements

,
f_{1}f_{2}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
so that
\Delta^{k}_{\ell}(f_{1}f_{2}\ldots f_{n})\in B(\ell^{2}(\Gamma))
for any

.
The following lemma says that

satisfies a higher order Leibnitz property. We have decided to keep the proof, which is a lengthy computation, in the Appendix section. This ‘higher order Leibnitz property’ is going to be very useful for computations.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{k}\left(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{n}=k}\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{\ell}^{r_{i}}\left(f_{i}\right),

where
\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}=\dfrac{k!}{r_{1}!\ r_{2}!\ \cdots\ r_{n}!}
and
f_{i}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n.

One can define the following sequence of separating (as
L_{\ell}^{k}
’s are densely defined) seminorms on
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)

L_{\ell}^{k}(f):=\begin{cases}\left\|\Delta^{k}(f)\right\|_{\mathrm{op}}\quad f\in C_{c}(\Gamma),\\
0\quad\quad\hskip 37.55762pt\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We have the following corollary of the Lemma

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle L_{\ell}^{k}\left(f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{n}=k}\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}L_{\ell}^{r_{i}}\left(f_{i}\right),

where
\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}=\dfrac{k!}{r_{1}!\ r_{2}!\ \cdots\ r_{n}!}
and
f_{i}\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from Lemma
2.3
by taking the operator norm in both sides of the inequality followed by using triangle inequality and sub-multiplicativity of the norm.
∎

3.
Main results

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group. Note that for meaningful discussion on metric dimension and entropy on
C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group (infinite) having an element of infinite order and
\ell
be a length function on
\Gamma.
Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left\lvert\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ell(g)\leq n\right\}\right\rvert\geq C(1+n),

i.e.,
\ell
has the property of polynomial growth of degree at least one.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}
be an element of
\Gamma
of infinite order. Let
\ell\left(g_{0}\right):=a>0
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
in such a way that
(k-1)a\leq n<ka.
Then

\left\{1,g_{0},g_{0}^{2},\cdots,g_{0}^{k-1}\right\}\subseteq\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ \ell(g)\leq n\right\}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ell(g)\leq n\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{n+1}(n+1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{k}{ka+1}(n+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a+\frac{1}{k}}(n+1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a+1}(n+1).

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove that if a group
\Gamma
has polynomial growth, then it has finite metric dimension. For that we need the following lemma.

Remark 3.2
.

Throughout the remainder of this paper, we work exclusively with finitely generated discrete groups. Unless explicitly specified otherwise, the length function to be considered always refers to the word length function with respect to a fixed generating set of the underlying group.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group with polynomial growth of exponent

such that it has an element of infinite order. Then for any

(C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma),L^{k}_{\ell})

Proof.

First of all it follows from Theorem

]

Now consider the set
\mathcal{L}_{1}:=\left\{f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)\ :\ L_{\ell}^{k}(f)\leq 1\right\}.

\varepsilon>0.
Then for any

a finite dimensional subspace with

\dim(X)\leq\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\delta\right)+\varepsilon.

Let

\delta U_{\delta}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{1}.

Note that
\lambda\left(U_{\delta}\right)\delta_{e}
is an orthonormal set in

So by the virtue of Voiculescu’s theorem it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\left(\lambda(U_{\delta})\delta_{e},2^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\lvert U_{\delta}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\left\lvert\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ \ell(g)\leq\left\lfloor\left(\delta^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\right\rfloor\right\}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}C\left(1+\left\lfloor\left(\delta^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C_{0}\left(\delta^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}},

where
C_{0}=\frac{3}{4}C>0
and the penultimate inequality follows from Lemma
3.1
. Now we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mdim}_{L_{\ell}^{k}}\left(C_{r}^{\ast}(\Gamma)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},2^{-1}\delta\right)}{\log 2\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathcal{D}\left(\lambda(\mathcal{L}_{1})\delta_{e},2^{-1}\delta\right)}{\log 2\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathcal{D}\left(\lambda(\delta U_{\delta})\delta_{e},2^{-1}\delta\right)}{\log 2\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathcal{D}\left(\lambda(U_{\delta})\delta_{e},2^{-1}\right)}{\log 2\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log(C_{0}\left(\delta^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}})}{\log\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\left(\delta^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}}{\log\delta^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}.

For the upper bound fix some
\frac{r}{2}<p<k.
Then by
[
15
]
*Proposition 2.2 it follows that
\Gamma
has the property of rapid decay with exponent
p
with some constant
C^{\prime}>0.
Get hold of
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{2^{p}C^{\prime}}{n^{k-p}}<\delta.

The smallest such

satisfying the inequality is given by

Then by the virtue of the inequality (

) we have

\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq_{\delta}B_{n_{0}}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{\delta_{g}\ :\ \ell(g)\leq n_{0}\right\}.

Therefore by invoking the property of polynomial growth of degree

for the length function
\ell
, we have

\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\delta\right)\leq\left\lvert\left\{g\in\Gamma\ :\ell(g)\leq n_{0}\right\}\right\rvert\leq\mu\left(1+n_{0}\right)^{r},

for some
\mu>0.

Then

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\mu+r\log\left(1+n_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\mu+r\log\left(2+\left(\frac{2^{p}C^{\prime}}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-p}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\mu+r\log\left(2\delta^{\frac{1}{k-p}}+\left(2^{p}C^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-p}}\right)+\frac{r}{k-p}\log\delta^{-1}

Letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
we have

\frac{\log\mu}{\log\delta^{-1}}\to 0\quad\mathrm{and}\ \quad\frac{\log\left(2\delta^{\frac{1}{k-p}}+\left(2^{p}C^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-p}}\right)}{\log\delta^{-1}}\to 0.

Therefore

\mathrm{Mdim}_{L_{\ell}^{k}}\left(C_{r}^{\ast}(\Gamma)\right)=\limsup\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\log\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\delta\right)}{\log\delta^{-1}}\leq\frac{r}{k-p}.

Since the above inequality holds for all

it follows that

∎

The following class of examples resembles the classical topological entropy where any homeomorphism of a compact metric space with moderate expansion with respect to the distance has topological entropy zero. \displaystyle L_{\ell}^{s}\left(\alpha^{m}(f)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{\ell}^{s}\left(\alpha^{m}\left(\sum\limits_{g\in F}a_{g}\delta_{g}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{\ell}^{s}\left(\sum\limits_{g\in G}a_{g}\delta_{\alpha^{m}(g)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{g\in G}\ell\left(\alpha^{m}(g)\right)^{s}\left\lvert a_{g}\right\rvert\ (\mathrm{by\ Lemma\ \ref{deltag}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P(m)^{s}\sum\limits_{g\in G}\ell(g)^{s}\left\lvert a_{g}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{M}P(m)^{s}\left(\sum\limits_{g\in G}\ell(g)^{2s}\left\lvert a_{g}\right\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{M}P(m)^{s}L_{\ell}^{s}(f)\ (\mathrm{by\ inequality}\ \ref{Lbound})

Now fix some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and get hold of
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}\in\Omega
(necessarily not all of them are distinct). Let

C:=1+\max\left\{L_{\ell}^{j}(f)\ :\ 0\leq j\leq k,\ f\in\Omega\right\}.

Then by Corollary
2.4
we have

\displaystyle L_{\ell}^{k}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{i-1}\left(f_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{n}=k}\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}L_{\ell}^{r_{i}}\left(\alpha^{i-1}\left(f_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}\sum\limits_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{n}=k}\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}L_{\ell}^{r_{1}}\left(f_{1}\right)\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}P(i-1)^{r_{i}}L_{\ell}^{r_{i}}\left(f_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}\sum\limits_{r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{n}=k}\dbinom{k}{r_{1}\ r_{2}\ \cdots\ r_{n}}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}P(i-1)^{r_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(C\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}\left(1+P(1)+P(2)+\cdots+P(n-1)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}N(d+1)\left(1+1^{d}+2^{d}+\cdots+(n-1)^{d}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}N(d+1)\ n^{k}(n-1)^{kd}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(C\sqrt{M}\right)^{k}N(d+1)\ n^{k(d+1)}.

Here, while denoting the product of the elements
\alpha^{i-1}(f_{i})
is in the
C^{\ast}
-algebra, we have suppressed the representation
\lambda
. The rest of the proof can be done exactly along the same lines as in the proof of Theorem
3.10
.
∎

So far we have not produced any non-zero upper bound of entropy. In
[
13
]
, it was proved that any automorphism which is Lipschitz with respect to the
{}_{c}\mathrm{Lip}
-norm has non-zero upper bound provided the CQMS has finite metric dimension. We shall prove a similar result in our context. In our context, it is not easy to get Lipschitz automorphisms. But we shall see that the assumption of Lipschitzness with respect to the length function can produce non-zero upper bound of the entropy. Explicitly, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group with a length function
\ell
such that
\Gamma
has the rapid decay property with a decay exponent
r
. Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
such that
\ell(\alpha(g))\leq\lambda\ell(g)

\forall g\in\Gamma
for some constant
\lambda\geq 1
. Then if for
k>r
,
\mathrm{Mdim}_{L^{k}_{\ell}}(C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma))=d<+\infty
, then

\mathrm{Entp}_{L_{\ell}^{k}}(\alpha)\leq kd\log\lambda.

Proof.

let
\Omega
be a finite subset of
{C_{c}(\Gamma)}_{1}
and
M_{\Omega}=\max\limits_{f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)}\{\lvert\mathrm{supp}(f)\rvert\}
. Then for any
f\in\Omega
, along the lines of proof of
3.13
, we have the following estimate:

L^{k}_{\ell}(\alpha^{m}(f))\leq\sqrt{M_{\Omega}}\lambda^{km}L^{k}_{\ell}(f).,

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}.

Now let
x\in\Omega_{n}
so that there are
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\in\Omega
such that
x=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha^{i-1}(f_{i})
. Let

C^{\prime}:=\max\left\{L_{\ell}^{s}(f)\ :\ f\in\Omega,1\leq s\leq k\right\}

and set
C:=\max\left\{1,C^{\prime k}\right\}.
Then using the same technique as in
3.13
, we get the following bound:

L_{\ell}^{k}(x)\leq C(\sqrt{M_{\Omega}})^{k}\lambda^{nk}n^{k}.

Therefore, for any finite set
\Omega\in C_{c}(\Gamma)_{1}
,
\Omega_{n}\subset C\lambda^{nk}n^{k}\mathcal{L}_{1}
, where
C>0
is some constant depending only on
\Omega
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Entp}_{L_{\ell}^{k}}(\alpha,\Omega,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log D\left(\Omega_{n},\delta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log D\left(\frac{\Omega_{n}}{C\lambda^{nk}n^{k}},\frac{\delta}{C\lambda^{nk}n^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log D\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\frac{\delta}{C\lambda^{nk}n^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log D\left(\mathcal{L}_{1},\frac{\delta}{C\lambda^{nk}n^{k}}\right)}{\log(\delta^{-1}C\lambda^{nk}n^{k})}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(\delta^{-1}C\lambda^{nk}n^{k})}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\lambda^{nk}}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kd\log\lambda.

Note that the right hand side is independent of
\Omega
and
\delta.
Hence by taking supremum over all such
\Omega
and
\delta,
the desired result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.19
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finitely generated discrete group with a length function
\ell
such that
\Gamma
has the polynomial growth property with growth exponent
r
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)
such that
\ell(\alpha(g))\leq\lambda\ell(g)

\forall g\in\Gamma
for some constant
\lambda\geq 1
. Then for any
k>r
,

\mathrm{Entp}_{L_{\ell}^{k}}(\alpha)\leq r\log\lambda.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
, the metric dimension of
(C^{\ast}_{r}(\Gamma),L^{k}_{\ell})
is bounded above by
\frac{2r}{2k-r}
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.18
,

\textrm{Entp}_{L^{k}_{\ell}}(\alpha)\leq\frac{2rk}{2k-r}\log\lambda.

As observed earlier, entropy of an automorphism depends only on the domain of the
{}_{c}\mathrm{Lip}
-norm and, in our case, the domain of
L_{\ell}^{k}=C_{c}(\Gamma)
for all
k>r
. Therefore, for any
k_{1}>r
,

\textrm{Entp}_{L^{k_{1}}_{\ell}}(\alpha)=\textrm{Entp}_{L^{k}_{\ell}}(\alpha)\leq\frac{2rk}{2k-r}\log\lambda,

for all
k>r
. Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

Note that as the function

, we have exerted the full force of

in the cLip-norm

Appendix A
Higher order Leibnitz property

Lemma A.1
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{k}(f_{1}f_{2})=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2}),

for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in C_{c}(\Gamma),
where
\Delta_{\ell}^{0}=\text{id}
and
\Delta_{\ell}^{n}(f)
denotes the
n
-fold commutator of
M_{\ell}
with
\lambda(f)
.

Proof.

Observe that for any
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma)
,
\lambda(f)
maps
C_{c}(\Gamma)
into
C_{c}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, the following algebraic manipulation can be done on the dense subspace
C_{c}(\Gamma)
of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
and subsequently extended to all of
\ell^{2}(\Gamma)
.

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{1}(f_{1}f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[M_{\ell},\lambda(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\ell}\lambda(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})-\lambda(f_{1})M_{\ell}\lambda(f_{2})+\lambda(f_{1})M_{\ell}\lambda(f_{2})-\lambda(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})M_{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[M_{\ell},\lambda(f_{1})\right]\lambda(f_{2})+\lambda(f_{1})\left[M_{\ell},\lambda(f_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{1}(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})+\lambda(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{1}(f_{2}),

which proves the lemma for
k=1.
Now suppose the result holds for
k,
for some
k\geq 1.

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1}(f_{1}f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[M_{\ell},\Delta_{\ell}^{k}(f_{1}f_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\left[M_{\ell},\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\left[M_{\ell},\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j}(f_{1})\right]\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j}(f_{1})\left[M_{\ell},\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j+1}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j+1}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j+1}(\lambda(f_{1}))\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k+1}\binom{k}{j-1}\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j+1}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1}(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\binom{k}{j}+\binom{k}{j-1}\right)\Delta_{\ell}^{k-j+1}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})+\lambda(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1}(f_{1})\lambda(f_{2})+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\binom{k+1}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1-j}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})+\lambda(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1}(f_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k+1}\binom{k+1}{j}\Delta_{\ell}^{k+1-j}(f_{1})\Delta_{\ell}^{j}(f_{2})

This proves the result for
k+1.
Hence the result follows.∎
