Abstract
. We compute the twisted first and second moments of the shifted central values of the Rankin-Selberg L L -functions given by L ⁡ ( 1 2 + ω , f ⊗ g ) L\left(\frac{1}{2}+\omega,f\otimes g\right) as f f varies over primitive forms of prime power level p ν p^{\nu} with ν ≥ 3 \nu\geq 3 . Here ω \omega is a bounded shift and g g is a fixed primitive form of level relatively prime to p p .

1.
Introduction

Let
S_{k}(N)
be the space of cusp forms of even weight
k\geq 2
with respect to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Any
f\in S_{k}(N)
admits a Fourier expansion at infinity

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\,e(nz),\quad e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}.

The space
S_{k}(N)
is equipped with the Petersson inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{F_{0}(N)}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\,y^{k}\,\frac{dx\,dy}{y^{2}},

where
F_{0}(N)
is a fundamental domain for the action of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
. The Hecke operators
T_{n}
, with
(n,N)=1
, are normal with respect to the inner product. One can thus find an orthogonal basis of
S_{k}(N)
, we denote it by
H_{k}(N)
, formed of eigenvectors of all the
\{T_{n},(n,N)=1\}.
For
f
a Hecke eigenvector of
T_{n}
,
\psi_{f}(n)n^{(k-1)/2}

denotes its eigenvalue.

According to Atkin-Lehner theory
[
2
]
, the space
S_{k}(N)
can be decomposed
into two subspaces

S_{k}(N)=S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)\oplus S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(N),

where
S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(N)
is the space of old forms consisting of cusp forms of level
N
coming from lower levels as specified by

S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(N)=\operatorname{span}\bigl\{\,f(qz):\,qN^{\prime}\mid N,\ N^{\prime}<N,\ f(z)\in S_{k}(N^{\prime})\,\bigr\}.

The space of newforms
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
is defined as the orthogonal complement of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{old}}(N)
with respect to the Petersson inner product. This space is stable under the action of Hecke operators, and it is shown in
[
3
]
that these operators can be simultaneously diagonalized. A newform
f
is called primitive if
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
; in this case, one has
\psi_{f}(n)=\lambda_{f}(n)
for all
n
. We denote by
H_{k}^{*}(N)
the set of primitive newforms, which forms an orthogonal basis of
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(N)
. Moreover, for every
f\in H_{k}^{*}(N)
, the Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{f}(n)
are real for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
k
and
k^{\prime}
be two even positive integers, and let
f\in H_{k}^{*}(N)
and
g\in H_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(D)
be primitive forms. We assume that
N
and
D
are relatively prime. Given
f
and
g
as such, one defines the
L
-series for the Rankin-Selberg convolution as

L(s,f\otimes g)=\zeta_{ND}(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{g}(n)}{n^{s}},

where

\zeta_{ND}(2s)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\geq 1\\
(d,ND)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d^{2s}}=\zeta(2s)\prod_{p|ND}(1-p^{-2s}).

This series converges absolutely for
\Re(s)>1
. Setting

\Lambda(s,f\otimes g)=\left(\frac{ND}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{s}\Gamma_{g}(s)L(s,f\otimes g),

where

(1)

\Gamma_{g}\left(s\right)=\Gamma\left(s+\frac{|k-k^{\prime}|}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k+k^{\prime}}{2}-1\right),

we know that
\Lambda(s,f\otimes g)
has analytic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
with a pole at
s=1
(when
f=g
) and satisfies the functional equation

(2)

\Lambda(s,f\otimes g)=\Lambda(1-s,f\otimes g).

The reader is referred to
[
15
, Section 4]
for more details.

In this paper, we study the problem of evaluating the first and second moments of the family

(3)

\{L(s,f\otimes g):f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})\},

where
p
is a prime and
\nu\geq 3
. In contrast to the more extensively studied families corresponding to primitive forms of prime or square-free level,
L
-functions of primitive forms of prime power level provide a more localized framework where one can study moments while isolating the effect of increasingly deep ramification at a fixed prime. This perspective is also related to themes appearing in
p
-adic variation, where one studies arithmetic objects as the ramification at a fixed prime varies. While the present work is entirely analytic in nature, the prime power level nonetheless is a natural setting for investigating such fixed prime variation. One distinctive feature here is the richer structure of the space
S_{k}(p^{\nu})
that brings about contributions from oldforms, which then further leads to a more intricate Petersson trace formula and thereby additional arithmetic considerations in moment computations.

Throughout, the
L
-functions are evaluated at points of the form
s=\frac{1}{2}+\omega
, where the complex shift
\omega
is chosen such that

(4)

\omega=u+iv\,\text{ with }\,u,v\in\mathbb{R},\quad|u|\,<\frac{1}{\log(p^{\nu})}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,|\omega|\leq\eta

for some sufficiently small constant
\eta>0
.

Shifted moments play an important role in modern analytic number theory, both because they reveal more transparently the structure of the main terms and because they arise naturally in problems concerning nonvanishing, subconvexity, and correlations of zeros of automorphic L-functions.

All moments considered in this paper are taken with respect to the harmonic weight defined by

\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})}^{h}A(f)=\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}\langle f,f\rangle_{p^{\nu}}}A(f),

for any function
A
.

We establish the following two results concerning the twisted first moment of the family of
L
-functions in (
3
). Our asymptotic formulae include explicit, though nonuniform, error terms with fully specified dependence on all relevant parameters.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
k,k^{\prime}
are even positive integers with
k\geq 8
and
k>2k^{\prime}-2
. Let
p
be a prime,
\ell
a positive integer with
(\ell,p)=1
, and let
\nu\geq 3
be an integer. Consider
g\in H_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(D)
, where
D
is a positive integer such that
D=1
or
D
is prime. Let
\omega
be a complex number satisfying (
4
). Then for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H^{*}_{k}(p^{\nu})}^{h}\lambda_{f}(\ell)L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega,f\otimes g\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=\left(4\pi^{2}\right)^{\omega}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{2}\Big(1-\frac{1}{D}\Big)\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\frac{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)P_{g}(0)\xi\left(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\right)^{5}}{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\right)P_{g}(\omega)\xi\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{5}}\left(\frac{(p^{\nu}D)^{\omega}-(p^{\nu}D)^{-\omega}}{2\omega}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(\zeta_{p^{\nu}D}(1+2\omega)\ell^{-\omega}+\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{\omega}\Gamma_{g}(\frac{1}{2}-\omega)}{(4\pi^{2})^{-\omega}\Gamma_{g}(\frac{1}{2}+\omega)}\zeta_{p^{\nu}D}(1-2\omega)\ell^{\omega}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt+O\Big(\ell^{-\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}(p^{\nu}D)^{-\frac{5}{32}+\epsilon}\Big)+O\bigg(\frac{D^{A+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon-\Re(\omega)}\ell^{A+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{p^{\nu(A+\Re(\omega))}k^{2\Re(\omega)}}\bigg),

where
A
is chosen such that
1<A\leq\frac{k-k^{\prime}}{2}-\epsilon
.

Taking the limit as
\omega\to 0
, we get

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})}^{h}\lambda_{f}(\ell)L\Big(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{D}\right)\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(\nu\log p+\log\frac{e^{2\gamma}D}{4\pi^{2}\ell}+\psi\Big(\frac{k-k^{\prime}+1}{2}\Big)+\psi\Big(\frac{k+k^{\prime}-1}{2}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\frac{\lambda_{g}(\ell)}{\sqrt{\ell}}\left(\left(1-\frac{1}{D}\right)\frac{\log p}{p}+\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\frac{\log D}{D}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\bigg(\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon}(p^{\nu}D)^{\frac{5}{32}-\epsilon}}\bigg)+O\bigg(\frac{D^{A+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\ell^{A+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}{p^{\nu A}}\bigg),

where
\psi
is the digamma function,
\gamma
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and
A
is a constant chosen such that
1<A\leq\frac{k-k^{\prime}}{2}-\epsilon
.

Closely related to Theorem
1.1
is the work of Bettin
[
6
]
, who studied the first moment of twisted Hecke
L
-functions with unbounded shifts
\omega
in the prime power level aspect, obtaining asymptotic formulae with explicit dependence on the relevant parameters. Our theorem may be viewed as a Rankin-Selberg analogue in which one studies moments of
L(\frac{1}{2}+\omega,f\otimes g)
with bounded shifts over primitive forms of prime power level.

Corollary
1.2
is similar to a theorem of Pi (see
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
). In that work,
f
ranges over primitive forms with nebentypus
\chi_{N}
, the Kronecker symbol for a prime
N\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. In such setting, there is no oldform contribution to consider, and the usual Petersson trace formula suffices to evaluate the moment. In contrast, the case of prime power level considered here presents us with a significant oldform contribution, and the argument required to establish the corollary is correspondingly more involved.

As an application of Corollary
1.2
, we obtain the following determination result showing that a primitive form
g\in H^{*}_{k^{\prime}}(D)
is uniquely determined by the family of central values
L\big(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\big)
for
f\in{H_{k}}^{*}(p^{\nu})
. Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be in
H_{k^{\prime}_{1}}^{*}(D)
,
H_{k^{\prime}_{2}}^{*}(D)
, respectively, such that

L\Big(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g_{1}\Big)=L\Big(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g_{2}\Big)\quad\text{for all }\,\,f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu}).

Then by Corollary
1.2
, it follows that
\lambda_{g_{1}}(\ell)=\lambda_{g_{2}}(\ell)
for all primes
\ell\neq p
, and thus
g_{1}=g_{2}
by the strong multiplicity one theorem. The reader is referred to
[
16
]
for the first such determination result.

Next, for the twisted second moment of the family (
3
), we present the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k,k^{\prime}
be even integers satisfying
k+k^{\prime}>7
,
p
a prime and
\nu\geq 3
an integer. Let
\ell
be a positive integer such that
(\ell,p)=1
and
\ell\ll p^{\nu(\frac{2}{17}-\delta)}
for some sufficiently small
\delta>0
. For a fixed primitive form
g\in H^{*}_{k^{\prime}}(1)
and any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{h}_{f\in H^{*}_{k}(p^{\nu})}\lambda_{f}(\ell)L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega_{1},f\otimes g\Big)L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega_{2},f\otimes g\Big)

\displaystyle=p^{-\nu(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}\Big\{\mathfrak{m}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})+\epsilon_{\omega_{1}}(f\otimes g)\mathfrak{m}(-\omega_{1},\omega_{2})+\epsilon_{\omega_{2}}(f\otimes g)\mathfrak{m}(\omega_{1},-\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\epsilon_{\omega_{1}}(f\otimes g)\epsilon_{\omega_{2}}(f\otimes g)\mathfrak{m}(-\omega_{1},-\omega_{2})\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+O\left(\ell^{\frac{17}{8}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{1}{4}-O(\epsilon)\right)}+\ell^{\frac{5k-5-6\theta}{4k-8\theta}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{k-1-2\theta}{4k-8\theta}-O(\epsilon)\right)}+\ell^{\frac{(5-4\theta)(k+k^{\prime}-2)}{4k-8\theta}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{k+k^{\prime}-2}{4k-8\theta)}-O(\epsilon)\right)}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)p^{-\nu\omega_{1}+\nu\omega_{2}}\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{2\omega_{1}}}{2\omega_{1}}\frac{\Gamma_{g}(1/2-\omega_{1})}{\Gamma_{g}(1/2+\omega_{1})}\frac{L(1-\omega_{1}+\omega_{2},g\otimes g)}{\zeta(2-2\omega_{1}+2\omega_{2})}\zeta_{p^{\nu}}(1-2\omega_{1})\zeta_{p^{\nu}}(1+2\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\times\sum_{de=\ell}\frac{e^{\omega_{1}}d^{-\omega_{2}}}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{ab=d}\frac{\mu(a)\lambda_{g}(b)}{a^{1-\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}}\Upsilon(1-\omega_{1}+\omega_{2},p,ae).

Here

\varepsilon_{\omega}(f\otimes g)=\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{2\omega}\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}-\omega\big)}{\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\big)},

where
\Gamma_{g}
is defined in (
1
). We also have
\xi(s)=s(1-s)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s)
, the completed
\zeta
-function of Riemann.
P_{g}(s)
denotes an even polynomial with real coefficients, depending only on
k,k^{\prime}
, such that

P_{g}(s)\Gamma_{g}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+s\right)
is holomorphic in the region
-A<\Re s
for some
A>\frac{1}{2}
. The function
\Upsilon
is given by

(5)

\Upsilon(s,p,ae)=\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(p^{j})^{2}}{p^{sj}}\Big)^{-1}\prod_{q\mid ae}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(q^{j})^{2}}{q^{sj}}\Big)^{-1}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(q^{j})\lambda_{g}(q^{\nu_{q}(ae)+j})}{q^{sj}}\Big).

The method used to prove our theorem also applies to the case of
g\in H_{k^{\prime}}^{*}(D)
for square-free
D.
However, carrying this out would involve a substantial amount of technical computations without adding new conceptual ingredients. We therefore do not pursue this extension here. On another note, Theorem
1.3
applied with
\omega_{2}=\overline{\omega_{1}}=\omega
can be used to establish subconvexity and nonvanishing results for the family of values
\big\{L\big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega,f\otimes g\big)\big\}
.

Letting
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
tend to
0
in Theorem
1.3
, we obtain

Corollary 1.4
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{\star}(p^{\nu})}^{h}\lambda_{f}(\ell)L\Big(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=Q_{g,\ell,p}(\nu\log p)

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt+O\left(\ell^{\frac{17}{8}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{1}{4}-O(\epsilon)\right)}+\ell^{\frac{5k-5-6\theta}{4k-8\theta}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{k-1-2\theta}{4k-8\theta}-O(\epsilon)\right)}+\ell^{\frac{(5-4\theta)(k+k^{\prime}-2)}{4k-8\theta}}p^{-\nu\left(\frac{k+k^{\prime}-2}{4k-8\theta}-O(\epsilon)\right)}\right),

where
Q_{g,\ell,p}
is a polynomial of degree
3
with leading coefficient

\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{3}L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}g)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\sum_{r\mid\ell}\lambda_{g}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)\frac{\phi(r)}{r}\Upsilon(1,p,r).

Here
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}g)
denotes the symmetric square
L
-function of
g
,
\phi
is the Euler’s totient function and
\Upsilon
is as defined in (
5
).

Remark
.

If
\ell
is square-free, then

\sum_{r\mid\ell}\lambda_{g}\left(\frac{\ell}{r}\right)\frac{\phi(r)}{r}\Upsilon(1,p,r)=\Upsilon(1,p,1)\lambda_{g}(\ell)\prod_{r\mid\ell}\frac{2r}{r+1}.

In particular, for
\ell=1
, the leading coefficient becomes

\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{3}\Upsilon(1,p,1)L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}g).

The remaining coefficients of the polynomial
Q_{g,\ell,p}
are computed explicitly using Maple. Since their expressions are lengthy, they will not be recorded here.

Moments of automorphic
L
-functions occupy a central place in analytic number theory, with applications ranging from nonvanishing and subconvexity problems to questions concerning low-lying zeros and arithmetic statistics. One of the earliest breakthroughs in moments of
L
-functions of automorphic forms is due to Duke
[
9
]
, who proved that as
p\to\infty
among prime numbers, we have

\frac{1}{|H_{2}^{*}(p)|}\sum_{f\in H_{2}^{*}(p)}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},f\Big)^{2}=\log p+O\big(p^{-2}\log p\big).

Since the family
\displaystyle\{L\big(1/2,f\big)\}_{f\in H_{2}^{*}(p)}
forms an orthogonal family, the main term on the right-hand side agrees with the prediction of Conjecture 3 in
[
7
]
, which was derived from a hybrid product expression for the
L
-function. Note that Akbary
[
1
]
later generalized Duke’s result to forms of arbitrary even weight and prime level
p
.

The fourth moment was computed by Kowalski, Michel, and VanderKam
[
14
]
. As
p\to\infty
through primes, they showed that

(6)

\sum_{f\in H_{2}^{*}(p)}^{h}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},f\Big)^{4}=\mathcal{Q}(\log p)+O_{\varepsilon}\big(p^{-\frac{1}{12}+\varepsilon}\big),

where
\mathcal{Q}
is a sextic polynomial whose leading coefficient is
\frac{1}{60\pi^{2}}
. Balkanova and Frolenkov
[
5
]
later improved the error term to
O_{\varepsilon}(p^{-25/228+\varepsilon})
. A straightforward computation, which we omit here, confirms that the leading term again agrees with the prediction of Conjecture 3 in
[
7
]
.

A result that is particularly relevant to the present work is by Balkanova
[
4
, Corollary 1.5]
, which is an analogue of (
6
) for primitive forms
f
of even weight
k
and prime power level
p^{\nu}
, where
p
is a fixed prime and
\nu\geq 3
. More precisely, Balkanova proved in
[
4
]
that

\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})}^{h}L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},f\Big)^{4}=\mathcal{Q}_{k}\big(\nu\log p\big)+O_{\epsilon,k,p}\Big(p^{-\nu\left(\frac{1}{4}-\epsilon\right)}+p^{-\nu\left(\frac{k-1-2\theta}{8-8\theta}-\epsilon\right)}\Big).

Here
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is a sextic polynomial depending on
k
whose leading coefficient is

\frac{\phi(p^{\nu})^{7}}{p^{7\nu}}\frac{p^{2}-1}{p^{2}}\frac{1}{60\pi^{2}}.

The proof relies on an extension of the Petersson trace formula for
f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})
established by Rouymi
[
20
]
. In the same work, Rouymi also obtained asymptotics for twisted first, second, and third moments at the central point over
f\in H_{k}^{*}(p^{\nu})
.

We now turn to results concerning
L
-functions of Rankin-Selberg convolutions. In
[
15
]
, Kowalski, Michel, and Vanderkam showed that for a fixed
g\in H^{*}_{k^{\prime}}(D)
with
D
square-free and
(D,p)=1
, and for even integers
k,k^{\prime}
with
k<12
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{f\in H_{k}^{*}(p)}^{h}\Big|L\Big(\tfrac{1}{2},f\otimes g\Big)\Big|^{2}=\mathcal{Q}_{g}(\log p)+O_{g,k,\epsilon}\big(p^{-\frac{1}{12}+\epsilon}\big)\,\,\text{ for every }\,\epsilon>0,

as
p\to\infty
in the primes. Here
\mathcal{Q}_{g}
is a cubic polynomial depending on
g
whose leading coefficient is

\frac{L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}g)}{3\zeta(2)}\prod_{q\mid D}\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q(q+1)}.

(We believe there is a minor typo in
loc. cit.
) This leading coefficient may be compared with the one in our Corollary
1.4
.

Throughout the paper,
p
will denote a prime number and
\nu
a positive integer that is at least
3
. We also use
\ell
to denote a positive integer indivisible by
p.
The variables
\omega
,
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
will be complex numbers used to shift the

-values under consideration away from the central point

. Throughout the paper,

denotes a positive constant whose value may vary from line to line.

Given two functions
f(x)

and

to mean that there exists
\mathcal{C}>0

|f(x)|\leq\mathcal{C}|g(x)|

\mathcal{C}

Most of the constants implicit in the big-

4

and the prime
p

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Kloosterman Sums

A Kloosterman sum is defined as

(7)

S(m,n;c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x\leq c,\\
(x,c)=1\end{subarray}}e\Big(\frac{xm+\overline{x}n}{c}\Big).

Clearly,
S(m,n;c)=S(n,m;c)
. This sum also has the following property, which we will later need.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

[
19
, Lemma A.12]

Let
m,n,c
be positive integers and
q
be a prime. Suppose that
q^{2}\mid c
,
q\mid m
and
q\nmid n
. Then
S(m,n;c)=0
.

We also use Weil’s bound which states that

(8)

S(m,n;c)\ll(m,n,c)^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau(c)\sqrt{c}.

2.2.
Hecke Relations

Next, we note some results on normalized Fourier coefficients of primitive forms.

Lemma 2.2.1

(Hecke recursion formula)
.

For a normalized primitive form
f\in H^{*}_{k}(N)
, we have

\lambda_{f}(mn)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n),\\
(d,N)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\lambda_{f}\Big(\frac{m}{d}\Big)\lambda_{f}\Big(\frac{n}{d}\Big)

and

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid(m,n),\\
(d,N)=1\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}\Big(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\Big).

For a proof of this lemma, see
[
12
, (14.50) and (14.51)]
(note that there is a typo in
loc cit
where
\lambda_{g}(mn)
and
\lambda_{g}(m)\lambda_{g}(n)
should be swapped). A useful consequence of this, which can be proven by the inclusion-exclusion principle
[
4
, p. 135]
, is the following. For any function
H
, we have

(9)

\sum_{n\geq 1,(n,p)=1}\lambda_{g}(n)H(n)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{g}(n)H(n)-\lambda_{g}(p)\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{g}(n)H(np)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{g}(n)H(np^{2}).

We will apply this formula frequently to simplify sums of Fourier coefficients over
n
with
(n,p)=1
.

2.3.
Summation Formulae and Estimates

We begin by stating the following variation of Petersson Trace Formula due to Rouymi.

Theorem 2.1

(Petersson Trace Formula and Remark 4 of
[
20
]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime and
\nu\geq 3
a positive integer. Then

2.4.
Asymptotic Estimates for Bessel and Gamma Functions

We now note some formulae and estimates on Bessel and Gamma functions.

Assume that
s=\sigma+it
satisfies
|s|\to\infty
and
|\arg s|\,\leq\pi-\delta
for some fixed
\delta>0
. A convenient uniform form of Stirling’s formula for the magnitude of the Gamma function is

(12)

\log{|\Gamma(s)|}=\Big(\sigma-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big)\log|s|-\sigma-t\,\arg(s)+\tfrac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)+O_{\delta}\!\left(\frac{1}{|s|}\right).

The exponential decay is governed by the term
-t\,\arg(s)
. If
|t|
dominates and

\sigma
is moderate, then
\arg(s)\approx\pm\pi/2
and one recovers the
usual decay factor
e^{-\pi|t|/2}
. If instead
\sigma
is comparable to or larger
than
|t|
, then
\arg(s)
is small and the factor
e^{-\pi|t|/2}
is no longer the
correct description; in this regime the behavior is better expressed in terms of

|s|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\sigma}
, with the contribution of the term

-t\,\arg(s)
retained.

Using this version of the Stirling’s formula, we derive the following result for the ratio of
\Gamma
-values that appears in the functional equation of
L(s,f\otimes g)
.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
k,k^{\prime},
and A be constants such that
k>k^{\prime}>0
and
0<A<\frac{k-k^{\prime}+1}{2}
. Let
\omega
be a complex number with
|\omega|\leq\eta
for sufficiently small
\eta
. For any real
t
we have

\frac{\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}+A+it\big)}{\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\big)}\ll_{\omega}k^{-2\Re(\omega)-k+1}\,\big(k^{2}+t^{2}\big)^{A+\frac{k-1}{2}}.

To later bound general Bessel functions of the first kind, we record the following result.

Lemma 2.4.2

(Lemma C2 in
[
15
]
)
.

For
z>0
and
j_{1}\geq 0
,

\frac{z^{j_{1}}}{(1+z)^{j_{1}}}J^{(j_{1})}_{j_{2}}(z)\ll_{j_{1},j_{2}}\frac{z^{\Re(j_{2})}}{(1+z)^{\Re(j_{2})+\frac{1}{2}}}.

2.5.
The Approximate Functional Equation

By

, for
\omega

\begin{split}L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega,f\otimes g\Big)=&\,(p^{\omega}D)^{-\nu}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{g}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}V_{g,\omega}\Big(\frac{n}{p^{\nu}D}\Big)\\

Here

\varepsilon_{\omega}(f\otimes g)=\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{2\omega}\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}-\omega\big)}{\Gamma_{g}\big(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\big)},

and

(14)

\displaystyle V_{g,\omega}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(3)}H_{g,\omega}(s)\zeta_{p^{\nu}D}(1+2s)y^{-s}\frac{\mathop{ds}}{s-\omega},

where

We recall that
\xi
is the completed Riemann zeta function and
P_{g}(s)
is chosen as an even polynomial wit real coefficients such that

is holomorphic in a region

for some

. For simplicity, we normalize

so that

.

Equation (

in terms of rapidly decaying series built from the Fourier coefficients of

and

Lemma 2.5.1
.

Let

with
|\Re(\omega)|<\frac{1}{\log(p^{\nu})}
. For all

V_{g,\omega}(x)\ll_{A}\big(1+|\Im(\omega)|\big)^{B}x^{-A}.

Further,

That is,

\displaystyle V_{g,\omega}(x)=

\displaystyle-\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{\omega}}{2\omega}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{D}\Big)\frac{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)P_{g}(0)\xi\big(\frac{1}{2}-\omega\big)^{5}}{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\right)P_{g}(\omega)\xi\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{5}}

\displaystyle+\zeta_{p^{\nu}D}(1+2\omega)x^{-\omega}+O_{\epsilon,g}\Big((1+|\Im(\omega)|)^{B}(p^{\nu}D)^{\frac{3}{32}+\epsilon}x^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big).

Proof.

This follows from
[
15
, Eq. 4.12, Eq. 4.14]
. The value of the first residue is

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{s=0}

\displaystyle\bigg\{H_{g,\omega}(s)\Big(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+2s}}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{D^{1+2s}}\Big)\zeta(1+2s)\frac{x^{-s}}{s-\omega}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\omega}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{D}\Big)H_{g,\omega}(0)=-\frac{1}{2\omega}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{D}\Big)\Gamma_{g}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)G_{g,\omega}(0)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(4\pi^{2})^{\omega}}{2\omega}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{D}\Big)\frac{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{g}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\omega\right)}\frac{\xi\big(\frac{1}{2}-\omega\big)^{5}}{\xi\big(\frac{1}{2}\big)^{5}}\frac{P_{g}(0)}{P_{g}(\omega)}.

The other residue can easily be found to be
\zeta_{p^{\nu}D}(1+2\omega)x^{-\omega}
by noting that
H_{g,\omega}(\omega)=1
.
∎

\mathscr{G}_{2}(0)=\mathscr{G}_{1}(0)+\mathscr{G}_{2}^{\prime}(0)=\mathscr{G}^{\prime}_{1}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{G}_{2}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0.

Hence,

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 0}\frac{1}{\omega_{1}^{2}}\mathscr{G}_{1}(\omega_{1})+\frac{1}{\omega_{1}^{3}}\mathscr{G}_{2}(\omega_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{G}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{6}\mathscr{G}_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0).

One can verify using Maple that
\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{G}_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\frac{1}{6}\mathscr{G}_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)
is a cubic polynomial in
\nu
with leading coefficient

\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\Upsilon(1,p,1)\frac{(p-1)^{2}\log^{3}{p}}{p^{2}}\mathrm{Res}_{s=0}L(1+s,g\otimes g).

The remaining coefficients were also computed in Maple, but we omit their explicit expressions here due to their length.
