Burnings of trees and their homologies
Yuri Muranov, Anna Muranova

Abstract
The problem of graph burning was firstly introduced as a model for different processes of social and network interactions. Recently, the authors of the present paper developed methods of algebraic topology for investigation of this problem. This approach is based on the new definition of burning process which excludes the possibility to choose at any moment vertex for burning from the set of vertices which are already burned at this moment. In this paper we continue to study such burning process using algebraic topology methods. We prove the result about relations between burnings of a graph and burnings of its spanning trees that is similar to the classical case. Afterwards, we describe properties of trees burnings. In particular, we prove that a burning of a tree defines a structure of a digraph on the tree and investigate this structure. We introduce and study a strong burning configuration space of a graph and new strong burning homology which are similar to burning homology defined in our previous paper, but arise from burning homomorphism.



Keywords:

homology of graphs, simplicial homology, simplicial complex, graph burning, digraph, path graph, burning time, tree, spanning tree, burning homomorphism.

1
Introduction

The problem of graph burning was firstly introduced in
[
2
]
. This model describes different processes of social and network interactions, see also
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
. The significance of trees in the burning theory follows from the
results
[
3
, Theorem 2.4, Corollary 2.5]
that describe relations between burning of a connected graph
G
and burnings of some of its spanning trees. In particular, as follows from this result, any burning of
G
gives a burning of a spanning tree, although a spanning tree is not uniquely determined by the burning in general.

The definition of a burning process in
[
2
,
3
]
allows that in time
t
a vertex which is burned in time
t+1
can be also chosen to burn in time
t+1
(see
[
12
]
for details). In
[
12
]
we introduced several filtrations of a graph, closely related to the burning process, and defined
the burning process which excludes the possibility to choose at the moment
t+1
vertex for burning from the set of vertices which are already burned at this moment.

In this paper we continue to study burning process in the sense
[
12
]
. We start with preliminaries in Section
2
. In Section
3
we prove the result about relations between burnings of a graph and burnings of its spanning trees that is similar to
[
3
, Theorem 2.4, Corollary 2.5]
. The main result in this direction is given by Theorem
3.2
. But there is an essential difference between our proof and the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
as we note in Remark
3.3
. Therefore, Theorem
3.2
reduces investigation of graph burnings to burnings of trees. In Section
4
we define burning homomorphisms and discuss their existence on trees. In Section
5
we describe possible end time of trees burnings and properties of burning sequences in the case of burning homomorphisms. In particular, we obtain sharp lower and upper bounds of the burning homomorphism’s end time for path graphs
P_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, Theorem
5.4
. In Section
6
we prove that a burning of a tree defines a structure of a digraph on the tree and investigate properties of this structure. We finish with Section
7
, where we introduce and study a strong burning configuration space of a graph and a strong homology which are similar to burning homology defined in
[
12
]
.

2.1
Basic notions of the graph theory

In this section, we recall basic notions of the graph theory, see e. g.
[
4
,
6
,
7
,

.

Definition 2.1
.

A graph

G=(V_{G},E_{G})
is a non-empty finite set
V_{G}
of
vertices
together with a set
E_{G}
of non-ordered pairs
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}
of distinct vertices
v,w\in V_{G}
called
edges
. Two different vertices are
adjacent
if they are connected by an edge. A vertex is
incident
with an edge
if the vertex is one of the endpoints of that edge. All vertices which are incident to vertex
v
are called
neighbors
of
v
.

Sometimes we will omit
the subscript
G
from the set
V_{G}
of vertices and from the set
E_{G}
of edges if the graph is clear from the context.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a graph
H
is a
subgraph
of a graph
G
and we write
H\subset G
if
V_{H}\subset V_{G}
and
E_{H}\subset E_{G}
. A subgraph

H\subset G
is an
induced subgraph
if for any edge
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}
, such that
v,w\in V_{H}
we have
\{v,w\}\in E_{H}
. In this case we write

H\sqsubset G
. For a subgraph
H\subset G
we denote by
\widehat{H}\sqsubset G
the induced subgraph which has the same set of vertices as
H
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
graph map
f\colon G\to H
from a graph

G

to a graph
H
is a map of vertices
f|_{V_{G}}\colon V_{G}\rightarrow V_{H}
such that for any edge
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}
, we have

\{f(v),f(w)\}\in E_{H}
or
f(v)=f(w)\in V_{H}
. The map

f
is called a
homomorphism
if
\{f(v),f(w)\}\in E_{H}
for any
\{v,w\}\in E_{G}
. For a graph
G
, we denote by
\operatorname{Id}_{G}\colon G\to G
the
identity
map (homomorphism) that is the identity map on the set of vertices and on the set of edges.

Definition 2.4
.

(i) For
n\geq 1
, a
path in a graph

G=(V,E)
is an alternating sequence
v_{0},a_{1},v_{1},a_{2},\dots,v_{n-1},a_{n},v_{n}
of vertices

v_{i}\in V
and edges
a_{i}\in E
such that
a_{i}=\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
for all distinct
i,j=0,\dots,n
.
For
n=0
, a path is given by a vertex
v_{0}\in V
. The integer
n
is the
length
of the path. The vertex
v_{0}
is the
origin
and the vertex
v_{n}
is the
end
of the path.

(ii) For
n\geq 3
, a
circuit
in a graph
G=(V,E)
is an alternating sequence
v_{0},a_{1},v_{1},a_{2},\dots,v_{n-1},a_{n},v_{0}=v_{n}
of vertices

v_{i}\in V
and edges
a_{i}\in E
such that
a_{i}=\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
v_{i}\neq v_{j}
for all distinct
i,j=1,\dots,n
. For
n<3
, a circuit is not defined.

Definition 2.5
.

(i) A graph
G=(V,E)
is
connected

if for any two distinct vertices
v,w\in V
there is a path for which
v_{0}=v
is the origin and
v_{n}=w
is the end of the path.

(ii) A connected graph
G
is a
tree
if it is does not contain circuits.

(iii)
A
path graph

P_{n}
, where
n
is a positive integer, is a graph with
the set of vertices
\{1,\dots,n\}
and with the set of edges

\{i,i+1\}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.

(i) A subgraph
T
of
G
is its
subtree
if it is a tree.

(ii) A subtree
T=(V_{T},E_{T})
of
G
is its
spanning tree
if
V_{T}=V
.

(iii) A tree
Q
is
an ambient tree
of
G
, if
G
is a subtree of
Q
.

The following lemma is well known and follows almost immediately from definitions above.

Lemma 2.7
.

A graph
T=(V_{T},E_{T})
is a tree if and only if for any two distinct vertices
v,w\in V_{T}
there exists exactly one path
v=v_{0},a_{1},v_{1},a_{2},\dots,v_{n}=~w
.

Definition 2.8
.

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, the
distance

d_{G}(v,w)
between two vertices
v,w\in V
is the number of edges in a shortest path connecting these vertices. If there is no path connecting vertices
v,w\in V
then we set
d_{G}(v,w)=\infty
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a graph and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i) For an integer
n\geq 0
, the
n-th closed neighborhood
of a vertex
v\in V_{G}
is an induced subgraph with the set of vertices
\{w\in V_{G}|\,d_{G}(v,w)\leq n\}
. We denote this neighborhood by
N_{n}(v)
.

(ii) The
n
-th closed neighborhood
of an induced subgraph
H\sqsubset G
is the set of vertices

\{w\in V_{G}|\,\exists\,v\in V_{H}\text{ such that }d_{G}(v,w)\leq n\}.

We denote this neighborhood by
N_{n}(H)
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
H_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})\,(i=1,2)
be two subgraphs of a graph
G
. The
induced union

H_{1}\Cup H_{2}
is the induced subgraph of
G
generated by the set of vertices
V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. It equals to the induced subgraph
\widehat{H_{1}\cup H_{2}}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
and
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
be two graphs.
The disjoint union
G+H=(V,E)
is a graph which has as its vertices the
set
V=V_{G}\sqcup V_{H}
and as its edges the set
E=E_{G}\sqcup E_{H}
.

2.2
Graph burning and corresponding graph filtrations

In this section we remind the definition of
graph burning
from
[
12
]
and related concepts.

Let
G=(V_{G},E_{G})
be a graph and

S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be a non-empty ordered set of vertices. For
1\leq j\leq n
,
consider a set of induced subgraphs
\mathcal{N}_{j}\sqsubset G
defined by

\mathcal{N}_{j}=N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1})\Cup N_{0}(v_{j}).

(2.1)

We note that

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{N}_{1}=\{v_{1}\},\ \mathcal{N}_{2}=N_{1}(v_{1})\Cup\{v_{2}\},\ \text{and}\ \ \mathcal{N}_{3}=N_{2}(v_{1})\Cup N_{1}(v_{2})\Cup\{v_{3}\}.\end{matrix}

For
2\leq j\leq n+1
, let
U_{j}
be the induced subgraph
of
G
given by

U_{j}=N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1}).

(2.2)

We note that for
2\leq j\leq n
, the set of vertices
V_{U_{j}}
of the graph
U_{j}
is obtained from the set of vertices
V_{\mathcal{N}_{j}}
of the graph
\mathcal{N}_{j}
by deleting the vertex
v_{j}
.

It follows immediately from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that we have the following commutative diagram of inclusions of induced subgraphs

\begin{matrix}\mathcal{N}_{1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{2}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{3}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{n}&\sqsubset&G&\\
&&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\dots&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\\
&&U_{2}&\sqsubset&U_{3}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&U_{n}&\sqsubset&U_{n+1}&\sqsubset G\\
&&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\dots&\sqcup&&\sqcup&\ \ \ ||\\
&&\mathcal{N}_{1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{2}&\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{n-1}&\sqsubset&\mathcal{N}_{n}&\sqsubset G\\
\end{matrix}

(2.3)

in which the rows give two filtrations of the graph
G
by induced subgraphs.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and

S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
be a non-empty ordered set of vertices.
The sequence
S_{G}
is called a
burning sequence
if in diagram (
2.3
)
v_{j}\notin U_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq n
and
U_{n+1}=G
.
The elements of
S_{G}
are called
burning sources
. The vertex
v_{i}
is called the
source in the time
i
. For every burning sequence
S_{G}
we denote
\widehat{S}_{G}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}\subset~V
the set of corresponding burning sources.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. A burning sequence
S_{G}
define the commutative diagram (
2.3
). Setting

\mathcal{N}_{n+1}\colon=U_{n+1}=G

we obtain a filtration of
G

\mathcal{N}_{1}\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{2}\sqsubset\dots\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{n}\sqsubset\mathcal{N}_{n+1}=G

(2.4)

which corresponds to the burning sequence
S_{G}
. We define a function

\lambda_{G}\colon V_{G}\to\mathbb{N}
, where

\lambda_{G}(v)=\min\{i\,|\,v\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\}

(2.5)

It follows immediately from filtration (
2.4
) that the function
\lambda_{G}
is well defined.

We call a pair
\mathbf{B}(G)\colon=(\lambda_{G},S_{G})
a
burning (process)
for the graph
G
. Further, for a vertex
v\in V_{G}
the value
\lambda_{G}(v)
we call the
burning time of the vertex

v
.

Theorem 2.13

(Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.5 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mathbf{B}(G)=(\lambda_{G},S_{G})
be its burning. Then the function
\lambda_{G}=\lambda(\mathbf{B}(G))
is a surjective map from the set
V
of vertices to the set
\{1,2,\dots,{\mathbf{T}}\}
,
where
{\mathbf{T}}=n
or
{\mathbf{T}}=n+1
. Moreover,
\lambda_{G}
defines an unique graph map
G\to P_{\mathbf{T}}
which coincides
with
\lambda_{G}
on the set of vertices
V
of the graph
G
. We continue to denote this graph map by
\lambda_{G}=\lambda(\mathbf{B}(G))
.

We call the integer
\mathbf{T}
the
end time of the burning
. We write
\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G)
if the process ends in the time
{\mathbf{T}}
. We denote by
\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G))
the function
\lambda_{G}
which corresponds to the process
\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G)
.

Lemma 2.14

(Lemma 3.4 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with a burning
\mathbf{B}(G)=(\lambda,S_{G})
. Let
v,w\in V
be neighbours, that is
\{v,w\}\in E
. Then for any two neighbors vertices
v,w\in V
we have
|\lambda(v)-\lambda(w)|\in\{0,1\}
.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G)
be a burning process. The graph map (homomorphism)
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G))
is called the
burning map (homomorphism)
.

We also will need the following immediate corollary of Lemma
2.14
and Definition
2.3
.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G)
be its burning. Then the map
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G))
is a burning homomorphism if and only if for any two adjacent vertices
v,w\in V
we have
|\lambda(v)-\lambda(w)|=1
.

Finally, we remind that there are functorial relations between burnings of various graphs. Let
(\lambda_{G},S_{G})
and
(\lambda_{H},S_{H})
be two burnings of graphs
G
and
H
, respectively, with the source sequences
S_{G}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
and
S_{H}=~(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})
where
m\geq k\geq 1
.
For
2\leq j\leq k+1
, let
U_{j}
be the induced subgraph of
G
defined in (
2.2
). For
2\leq j\leq k+1
, let

W_{j}\sqsubset H
be the induced subgraph of
H
defined by

W_{j}=N_{j-1}(w_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}(w_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(w_{j-1}).

(2.6)

Definition 2.17
.

Let
G
,
H
be graphs and

\mathbf{B}(G)
,
\mathbf{B}(H)
be two burnings as above.
A graph map
f\colon G\to H
is called a
morphism of the burnings
, and we write
f\colon\mathbf{B}(G)\to\mathbf{B}(H)
, if
k\leq m
,
f(v_{i})=w_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and there is a commutative diagram
of graph maps

\begin{matrix}G&\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}&H\\
\lambda_{G}\downarrow\ \ \ \ &&\ \ \ \downarrow\lambda_{H}\\
P_{T^{G}}&\overset{\tau}{\longrightarrow}&P_{T^{H}},\\
\end{matrix}

(2.7)

where
T^{G}
and
T^{H}
are the burning times for the burnings of the graphs
G
and
H
, respectively. Hence, in particular,
f(U_{j})\subset W_{j}
for
2\leq j\leq k+1
.

3
Burning of a spanning tree

In this section we describe relations between burnings of a graph and burnings of its spanning trees for the burnings defined in previous section. The main result of this section is Theorem
3.2
, which states that any burning of a graph
G
defines a burning of its spanning tree, which is in general not uniquely determined. Recall that a
spanning tree
of a connected graph
G
is a subgraph tree which contains all vertices of
G
.

The burning process in Definition
2.12
differs from the burning process described in
[
3
, Introduction]
. In particular, as follows from our definition, the distance between sources
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
satisfies condition
d(v_{i},v_{j})\geq|i-j|+1
in contrast to the situation in
[
3
]
where
d(v_{i},v_{j})\geq|i-j|
. The proof of Theorem
3.2
below is similar to that given in
[
3
]
but there is an essential difference as we note in Remark
3.3
. At first, we state and prove a technical lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected graph and
S_{G}
be a burning sequence
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
of a burning
(\lambda,S_{G})
of
G
. Then
for any vertex
w\in V
we have

d(v_{k},w)\geq|\lambda(v_{k})-\lambda(w)|.

(3.1)

Proof.
Let
p
be a path from the vertex
v_{k}
to the vertex
w
of length
d=d(v_{k},w)
which is finite, since the graph is connected. By Theorem
2.13
, the function
\lambda
is a graph map

\lambda\colon G\to P_{\mathbf{T}}
where
P_{\mathbf{T}}
is a path graph. The restriction of

\lambda
to the path
p
gives the path map
\lambda_{P}\colon P\to P_{\mathbf{T}}
of the subgraph
P\subset G
which is generated by
p
. The graph
P
is a path graph of the length
d
and its image is the path subgraph of
P_{\mathbf{T}}
. Hence
the distance between images
\lambda(v_{k})
and
\lambda(w)
of the vertices

v_{k},w\in V_{P}
is less or equal to the distance
d(v_{k},w)
. That is

d(v_{k},w)\geq|\lambda(v_{k})-\lambda(w)|.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected graph and
S_{G}
be a burning sequence
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
of a burning
(\lambda,S_{G})
of
G
. Then there is a spanning tree
T\subset G
such that
S_{G}
is a burning sequence for some burning of
T
.

Proof.
Let
\widehat{S}_{G}
be the set elements of
S_{G}
. For any vertex
w\in G
we define a set
V_{w}\subset\widehat{S}_{G}
by

V_{w}=\{v_{k}\in S_{G}\,|\,\lambda(w)=\lambda(v_{k})+d(v_{k},w)\}.

(3.2)

As follows from (
3.2
),
v_{j}\in V_{v_{j}}
since
\lambda(v_{j})=j
and
d(v_{j},v_{j})=0
.
Let
2\leq j\leq n
. By Definition
2.12
, the vertex
v_{j}
does not belong to
U_{j}
defined in (
2.2
). Hence, for
1\leq k\leq j-1
,

v_{j}\notin N_{j-k}(v_{k})
by (
2.2
). That is, for such values of
k
,

d(v_{j},v_{k})>j-k
and

\lambda(v_{k})+d(v_{j},v_{k})=k+d(v_{j},v_{k})>k+j-k=j=\lambda(v_{j}).

Hence,
v_{k}\notin V_{v_{j}}
for
1\leq k\leq j-1
. For
j<k\leq n
,

d(v_{j},v_{k})>1
and

\lambda(v_{k})+d(v_{j},v_{k})=k+d(v_{j},v_{k})>j=\lambda(v_{j}).

Hence,
v_{k}\notin V_{v_{j}}
for
k>j
. Thus we obtain that

V_{v_{k}}=\{v_{k}\}\ \ \text{for every burning source}\ \ v_{k}\in\widehat{S}_{G}.

(3.3)

We check now, that the set
V_{w}
is non-empty for any vertex
w\in V
. Fix a vertex
w\in V
. We have proved already this statement for
w\in\widehat{S}_{G}
. Fix a vertex
w\in V
such that
w\notin\widehat{S}_{G}
and let
\lambda(w)=j
.
By Definition
2.12
and relation (
2.2
), we have
U_{n+1}=G
. Let
U_{1}=\{v_{1}\}=N_{0}(v_{1})
.
By (
2.2
) and
2.1
), there is a minimal value
j\,(2\leq j\leq n+1)
such that

w\in U_{j}=N_{j-1}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-2}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{2}(v_{j-2})\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-1})

(3.4)

and

w\notin U_{j-1}=\begin{cases}N_{0}(v_{1})&\text{for}\ \ j=2,\\
N_{j-2}(v_{1})\Cup N_{j-3}(v_{2})\Cup\dots\Cup N_{1}(v_{j-2})&\text{for}\ j>2.\\
\end{cases}

(3.5)

Now, by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), there is at least one number
k\,(1\leq k\leq j-1)
such that
w\in N_{j-k}(v_{k})
and
w\notin N_{j-k-1}(v_{k})
.
Hence, by Definitions
2.8
and
2.9
, we obtain that

d(w,v_{k})=j-k
. Hence
j=\lambda(w)=\lambda(v_{k})+d(w,v_{k})
and
v_{k}\in V_{w}
.

For any vertex
w\in V,
let
\mu(w)
be a minimal value of
k
such that
v_{k}\in V_{w}
. We obtain a function

\mu\colon V\to\widehat{S}_{G}

(3.6)

which is well defined since the map
\lambda
is well defined and which is an epimorphism by (
3.3
). For every vertex
v_{k}\in\widehat{S}_{G}
define
a subset of vertices
M_{k}\subset V
by setting
M_{k}=\mu^{-1}(v_{k})
. We note, that
v_{k}\in M_{k}
. By the definition,
M_{i}\cap M_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. Now, for any
1\leq k\leq n
, we construct a subgraph tree
T_{k}\subset G
with the set of vertices

V_{T_{k}}=M_{k}
.

Claim
. Let
w\in M_{k}\,(1\leq k\leq n)
and, hence,
\lambda(w)=k+d(v_{k},w)
. Let
p
be a path of the length
d=d(v_{k},w)
with the pairwise distinct edges
from
v_{k}
to
w
given by

p=(v_{k}=w_{0},a_{1},w_{1},\dots,a_{d},w_{d}=w)\ \ \text{where}\ \ a_{i}\in E,w_{i}\in V.

(3.7)

Then
w_{i}\in M_{k}
for

0\leq i\leq d
.

Proof.
Recall that we already know that
w_{0},w_{d}\in M_{k}
. By Theorem
2.13
, the function
\lambda
is a graph map

\lambda\colon G\to P_{\mathbf{T}}
where

We finish this Section with an interesting example of the class of trees, which in some sense burn worse, than path graphs. More precisely, it is known, see
[
1
]
, that the path graph
P_{m},m={n}^{2}-1
can be burned within the time
n
with the burning sequence, satisfying
|S_{P}|=n-1
. Moreover, it is easy to see that there is some freedom in choosing the first vertex in the burning sequence with this time. We present an example of family of trees with
m=({n}^{2}-1),n\in\mathbb{N}
vertices, which can be burned with the burning sequence, satisfying
|S|=n-1
only if the burning starts from one particular vertex, while all the other burning sequences will be longer. Moreover, the corresponding burning maps are homomorphisms, while for path graphs
P_{m}
there is no burning homomorphism with
|S_{P}|=n-1
, due to Theorem
5.2
.

6
Digraphs structure on burned trees

Now we investigate a possibility to introduce a natural structure of a digraph on a spanning tree of a burned graph.

We remind, that
directed graph (digraph)

G=(V_{G},D_{G})
consists of a set of vertices
V_{G}
, and a set
D_{G}
of ordered pairs
(v,w)\in D_{G}
of distinct vertices
v,w\in V_{G}
called
ordered edges
.

Definition 6.1
.

For two digraphs
G=(V_{G},D_{G})
and
H=(V_{H},D_{H})
a
digraph map

f\colon G\to H
is given by a map
f\colon V_{G}\to V_{H}
such that

(v,w)\in D_{G}
implies
(f(v),f(w))\in D_{H}
or
f(v)=f(w)\in V_{H}
. A digraph map
f
is a
digraph homomorphism
if
(f(v),f(w))\in D_{H}
for any
(v,w)\in D_{G}
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. A
structure of digraph on
G
can be given
by equipping with an order each edge
\{v,w\}\in E
.

Coming back to the burning we remind, that for any graph
G=(V,E)
any burning map
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G))
maps the graph onto path graph
P_{\mathbf{T}}
, whose vertices, if denoted by
{1,2,\dots{\mathbf{T}}}
correspond to the burning time of preimages. Hence,
in our settings a path graph

P=P_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
has a natural structure of digraph defined by
(i,i+1)\in D_{P}
for all
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Moreover, the following result follows almost immediately from the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T=(V_{T},E_{T})
be a tree and
(\lambda,S_{T})
be its burning. Then there is a natural structure of a directed graph on graph
T
, induced by
\lambda
, such that
\lambda
is a digraph map.

Proof.
Since
T
is its own spanning tree,
V_{T}=V_{\cup T_{i}}
, where
T_{i}
are defined as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
. Let us take any pair of vertices
v\sim w
in
T
. By Lemma
2.14
,
|\lambda(v)-\lambda{w}|\in\{0,1\}
. Assume
|\lambda(v)-\lambda(w)|=1
. Then the direction should be from the vertex with the smaller
\lambda
to the vertex with the larger
\lambda
. Assume now that
\lambda(v)=\lambda(w)
. Then by the proof of Theorem
3.2
we obtain
v\in T_{i},w\in T_{j},j\neq i
and we can define the directed edge as
(v,w)
, if
i<j
and
(w,v)
otherwise.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
G
be a graph and
(\lambda,S_{G})
be its burning. Then there is a spanning tree of
G
with natural digraph structure, which arises from
\lambda
.

Proof.
Follows from Theorem
3.2
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
T\subset Q
be trees and

(\lambda_{T},S_{T})
,
(\lambda_{Q},S_{Q})
be their burnings such that the inclusion
i:T\subset Q
is a morphism of burnings. Then
i
is agreed with the natural digraph structures induced by
\lambda_{T}
and
\lambda_{Q}
.

Proof.
Follows from commutative diagram (
2.7
)

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an arbitrary graph and
\lambda=\lambda(\mathbf{B}^{\mathbf{T}}(G))
be its burning homomorphism. Then
G
has a natural structure of digraph such that the map
\lambda_{G}
is digraph homomorphism.

Proof.
Follows from the proof of Proposition
6.2
and Corollary
2.16
.

Example 6.6
.

We note that the given structure of digraph is not well defined for the arbitrary graph
G
with a given burning sequence. Indeed, consider a circle
C_{3}
with the vertices
1,2,3
and all possible edges. The burning sequence
S=(1)
does not allow to define a directed graph in the given way, since
\lambda(2)=\lambda(3)
, but
2,3\in T_{1}
.

7
Strong configuration burning space

A set
\mathcal{B}(G)
of all burnings of any graph
G
and a configuration space
\Delta_{G}
which is a simplicial complex associated with the set
\mathcal{B}(G)
were defined in
[
12
]
. In this section we define
strong configuration burning space
and corresponding
strong burning homology groups
using a set of all burnings homomorphisms of a graph and describe its relation to burning homology. We use standard definitions of simplicial and graph constructions, see
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
. Let us firstly recall the most important concepts.

Definition 7.1
.

A
simplicial complex

\Delta=(V,F)
consists of a set
V
of
vertices
and a set
F

of finite non-empty subsets of
V
which are called
simplexes
. A simplex
\sigma\in F
containing
q+1\,(q\geq 0)
elements is called a

q
-
simplex
and the number
q
is called the
dimension
of
\sigma
and we write (
q=\operatorname{dim}\sigma
). The set
F
of simplexes must satisfy the following two properties:

(i) for every vertex
v\in V
the set
\{v\}\in F
is a simplex,

(ii) if
\sigma\in F
is a simplex then any non-empty subset
\tau\subset\sigma
is a simplex which is called a
face
of
\sigma
.

A simplex
\sigma\in\Delta
is
maximal
if it is not a face of any another simplex.

A simplicial complex is
n
-
dimensional
if it contains at least one
n
-dimensional simplex but
no
(n+1)
-dimensional simplexes.

Definition 7.2
.

A simplicial complex

\Delta_{1}=(V_{1},F_{1})
is a
subcomplex
of a simplicial complex
\Delta=(V,F)
if
V_{1}\subset V
and
F_{1}\subset F
.

A simplicial complex is called
finite
, if it has finite number of vertices.
In this paper we consider only finite simplicial complexes.

Now we come back to the burning theory. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. Denote by
\mathcal{B}(G)
the set of all burnings of
G
and by
\mathcal{B}_{H}(G)
the set of all burning homomorphism of
G
. Since we consider only finite graphs, the set
\mathcal{B}(G)

consists of finite number of pairs
(\lambda_{G},S_{G})
. Every burning sequence
S_{G}
defines a set
\widehat{S}_{G}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\subset~V
. It follows from Definition
7.1
that a simplicial complex

\Delta=(V,F)
is defined by the set
V
of vertices and a subset
F^{m}\subset F
of maximal simplexes of
\Delta
. We will say that the set of maximal simplexes
F^{m}

generates
the set
F
of simplexes.

Recall the following definition from
[
12
]
.

Definition 7.3
.

For a given graph
G=(V,E)
, a
burning configuration space of
G
is a simplicial complex
\Delta(G)=(V_{\Delta(G)},F_{\Delta(G)})
where

V_{\Delta(G)}=V
and
F_{\Delta(G)}
is generated by the set of all maximal simplexes

F_{\Delta(G)}^{m}=\{\widehat{S}_{G}\,|\,(\lambda_{G},S_{G})\in\mathcal{B}(G)\}.

Now we give a new definition which is similar to this one.

Definition 7.4
.

For a given graph
G=(V,E)
, we define a
strong burning configuration space of
G
as a simplicial complex
\Gamma(G)=(V_{\Gamma(G)},F_{\Gamma(G)})
where

V_{\Gamma(G)}=\{v\in V\,|\,\text{ there exists $\widehat{S}_{G}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}(G)$\; such that $v\in\widehat{S}_{G}$ }\},

and
F_{\Gamma(G)}
is generated by the set of all maximal simplexes

F_{\Gamma(G)}^{m}=\{\widehat{S}_{G}\,|\,(\lambda_{G},S_{G})\ \ \text{where $\lambda_{G}\in\mathcal{B}_{H}(G)$ }\}.

Note that the strong burning configuration space as well as set of all burning homomorphism of a graph can be empty, for example, when graph contains an odd cycle
[
12
, Theorem 3.10]
.
Moreover, Example
4.3
shows, that they can be empty also for trees. From the other hand, the following Proposition is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 7.5
.

For any path graph the corresponding strong burning configuration space is not the empty simplicial complex.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
G
be a graph with a non-empty strong burning configuration space
\Gamma(G)
. Then we have an inclusion

\Gamma(G)\to\Delta(G)
of simplicial complexes.

Proof.
Follows from definitions of configuration spaces.
