Association of ACEIs/ARBs treatment with clinical outcomes in acute kidney injury: a multicenter retrospective cohort analysis
Authors: Hai Wang, Ji Zhao, Jun-hua Lv, Zheng-hai Bai, Hong-hong Pei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundACEIs/ARBs (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers) constituted the cornerstone of therapy for various cardiovascular and renal disorders. The appropriate management of ACEIs/ARBs in the acute kidney injury (AKI) remained controversial.MethodsIn this multicenter study involving 1,285 AKI patients, we investigated the associations of ACEIs/ARBs discontinuation (n = 436), persistence (n = 657), and initiation (n = 192) with 14-day AKI progression and mortality. Multivariate logistic regression analysis and subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate the associations between ACEI/ARB utilization patterns and clinical outcomes.ResultsThe persistence usage of ACEIs/ARBs was independently associated with lower odds of 14-day AKI progression compared to discontinuation (adjusted OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.29–0.67, P < 0.001) Regarding 14-day mortality, the persistence usage of ACEIs/ARBs exhibited an inverse correlation in the unadjusted model (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.13–0.71, P = 0.006), but the association was attenuated after full adjustment model (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.13–1.20, P = 0.101). No statistically significant differences in the risk of 14-day AKI progression or mortality were observed between the initiation and discontinuation cohorts across all models.ConclusionsIn this retrospective cohort, the persistence of ACEI/ARB at the onset of AKI was associated with lower odds of 14-day progression, but not mortality. For the 14-day mortality, due to limited events, wide-ranging confidence intervals, and post-adjustment attenuation, these statistical instabilities need highly conservative interpretation and prospective validation.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Acute kidney injury (AKI), ACEI/ARB, 14-day AKI progression, 14-day mortality

Introduction

AKI was a serious clinical condition affecting up to 20% of hospitalized patients [1], with mortality ranging from 30% to 50% among critically ill patients [2]. The clinical management of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, such as ACEIs/ARBs during AKI episodes presented a significant therapeutic dilemma for clinical practice [3, 4]. Although ACEIs/ARBs historically served as cornerstone therapies for cardiovascular and renal diseases, recommended by international guidelines for indications including hypertension, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease, their appropriate utilization during AKI episodes remained controversial [5]. The theoretical concern focused on their capacity to exacerbate kidney injury via alterations in glomerular hemodynamics, particularly during states of volume depletion or systemic hypotension [4]. The current Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines suggested considering the discontinuation of ACEIs/ARBs during AKI episodes [4, 6]. However, this recommendation was based on low-quality evidence and expert opinion rather than robust clinical evidence [4]. Consequent to this lack of definitive evidence, clinical practice patterns exhibited profound heterogeneity, and decisions to continue, discontinue, or initiate ACEIs/ARBs fluctuated significantly among practitioners. This clinical uncertainty was particularly concerning given the pervasive use of these agents and the rising incidence of AKI among hospitalized patients. The potential clinical benefits of maintaining cardio-renal protection must be carefully weighed against the risks of acute kidney function deterioration. However, the relationships between different ACEI/ARB utilization patterns and clinical outcomes had yet to be fully clarified. Therefore, we conducted this multicenter study to investigate the associations of three distinct ACEI/ARB utilization strategies—discontinuation, persistence, and initiation—with 14-day progression and mortality outcomes in patients with AKI.

Objective

This study aimed to evaluate the associations of discontinuation, persistence and initiation of ACEIs/ARBs on AKI progression and the prognosis of patients with AKI.

Methods

Study design

This study was a secondary analysis of a double-blind, multicenter randomized controlled trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02753751), involving 6,030 adult inpatients with AKI. The original trial evaluated electronic health record-based pop-up alerts integrated with a dedicated order set, which ultimately showed no significant effect on the composite outcome of AKI progression, dialysis requirement, or mortality.

Data source

The data source was an online repository (Dryad) with accession number doi: 10.5061/dryad.4f4qrfj95 [7], which archives research data for public free access, and citation. this link can be referred: https://datadryad.org/stash/dataset/ doi: 10.5061/dryad.4f4qrfj95 [8]. These data were derived from a primary randomized controlled trial published in the BMJ. The data validation and quality control processes of the parent trial were highly rigorous, ensuring excellent credibility; specific procedural details were documented in the original publication [7].

Diagnosis of AKI

The diagnostic algorithm for AKI based on KDIGO criteria was integrated into the Epic electronic health record system, which activated a “pop-up” alert when a clinician accessed a patient’s medical record. This system operated without human intervention, automatically collecting relevant clinical data for the detection of AKI and generating real-time alerts. To guarantee accurate and reliable implementation, researchers across all participating hospitals underwent standardized training on the system’s operational protocols.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The investigations involving human participants received approval from approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Yale School of Medicine (ID: 1604017596) and Bridgeport Hospital (IDs: 051802, 041801, 071808), with this approval specific to the original study. It had also obtained approval from the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University.The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion criteria

The study included hospitalized adults (aged ≥ 18 years) presenting with AKI by KDIGO criteria.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: a history of end-stage renal disease, receipt of dialysis within the past year, or an initial creatinine level exceeding 4.0 mg/dl.Additionally, cases were excluded based on the following technical or protocol considerations: erroneous randomization due to Epic system upgrades, first alerts occurring after discharge, non-first-time encounters, admissions prior to alert activation, randomization to both arms, or lack of ACEI/ARB exposure within the 24-hour window before and after AKI onset.

Participants

A total of 1,285 patients were ultimately enrolled based on the screening criteria, with the detailed selection process depicted in Fig. 1.

The flowchart of study participants

14-day AKI progression

14-day AKI progression was defined as a worsening of the AKI stage within 14 days, as staged by the KDIGO criteria.

The definition of ACEIs/ARBs usage

Discontinuation was defined as the use of ACEIs/ARBs within 24 h prior to the onset of AKI, followed by the cessation of these medications within 24 h after the diagnosis of AKI. Persistence was defined as the continuous use of ACEIs/ARBs both within 24 h before AKI onset and within 24 h after AKI diagnosis. Initiation was defined as the absence of ACEIs/ARBs use within 24 h prior to AKI onset, followed by the commencement of these medications within 24 h after AKI diagnosis.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were compared across the three groups (discontinuation, persistence, and initiation) using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables and chi-square tests for categorical variables. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. The associations between clinical factors and outcomes (14-day AKI progression and mortality) were initially evaluated using univariate logistic regression analysis. Subsequently, multivariate logistic regression models and inverse probability weighting (IPTW) were constructed to identify independent predictors while adjusting for potential confounders. Results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).To assess the robustness of associations between ACEIs/ARBs utilization patterns and clinical outcomes, multiple sequential models were developed with progressive adjustment. Subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate the consistency of ACEIs/ARBs effects across distinct clinical subpopulations. Given the negligible proportion of missing data in this study, no imputation or special handling for missing values was required. E-values were calculated to quantify the potential impact of unmeasured confounding required to nullify the observed associations between ACEI/ARB status and AKI outcomes. All statistical analyses were performed using EmpowerStats (http://www.empowerstats. com) and R (http://www.R-project.org). Two-sided P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study patients

Of the 1,285 patients included, 436 (33.93%) were stratified into the discontinuation group, 657 (51.13%) into the persistence group, and 192 (14.94%) into the initiation group. Baseline demographics, including age, sex distribution, and ethnicity, were comparable across the three cohorts (all P > 0.05).

Conversely, mean arterial pressure (MAP) was significantly lower in the discontinuation group (84.2 ± 15.3 vs. 88.8 ± 13.7 vs. 88.2 ± 14.8 mmHg, P < 0.001). Notably, patients in the discontinuation group presented higher prevalence of chronic kidney disease (41.97% vs. 34.25% vs. 33.33%, P = 0.020) and a more compromised baseline renal function. This was characterized by significantly elevated blood urea nitrogen (BUN; P < 0.001) and serum creatinine levels (P < 0.001), alongside lower estimated glomerular filtration rates (eGFR; P = 0.022). Furthermore, baseline disease severity indices were markedly higher in the discontinuation group, as evidenced by elevated Elixhauser scores (6.57 ± 2.68 vs. 6.13 ± 2.57 vs. 5.86 ± 2.65, P = 0.005) and SOFA scores (1.98 ± 1.63 vs. 1.50 ± 1.34 vs. 1.65 ± 1.54, P < 0.001). Regarding clinical outcomes, the discontinuation group exhibited higher frequencies of both 14-day AKI progression (16.06% vs. 7.00% vs. 10.94%; P < 0.001) and 14-day mortality (3.90% vs. 1.22% vs. 2.08%; P = 0.014). Other clinical characteristics, including comorbidities such as congestive heart failure, COPD, diabetes, and hypertension showed no significant differences among groups (all P > 0.05) (Table 1; Fig. 1).

The results of univariate analysis

Univariate analysis identified several factors associated with 14-day AKI progression and mortality. For 14-day AKI progression, significant factors included liver disease (OR 2.07, 95% CI 1.20–3.58, P = 0.009), malignancy (OR 2.00, 95% CI 1.24–3.25, P = 0.005), elevated Elixhauser score (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.13–1.29, P < 0.001), increased WBC counts (OR 1.05, 95% CI 1.02–1.08, P < 0.001), and higher SOFA score (OR 1.26, 95% CI 1.13–1.39, P < 0.001). Conversely, higher MAP was associated with lower odds of AKI progression (OR 0.99, 95% CI 0.97-1.00, P = 0.023). Crucially, compared with discontinuation, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with a lower likelihood of AKI progression (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.58, P < 0.001).

Regarding 14-day mortality, factors showing significant associations included advanced age (OR 1.05, 95% CI 1.01–1.10, P = 0.014), ICU admission (OR 4.10, 95% CI 1.95–8.61, P < 0.001), malignancy (OR 7.62, 95% CI 3.58–16.22, P < 0.001), elevated Elixhauser score (OR 1.21, 95% CI 1.06–1.38, P = 0.004), increased WBC counts (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.04–1.13, P < 0.001), and higher SOFA score (OR 1.60, 95% CI 1.34–1.91, P < 0.001) ,whereaslower MAP (OR 0.96, 95% CI 0.94–0.99, P = 0.005). Interestingly, chronic kidney disease also showed an inverse association with mortality (OR 0.35, 95% CI 0.13–0.93, P = 0.035). Consistent with the findings for AKI progression, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with lower odds of mortality risk compared to discontinuation (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.13–0.71, P = 0.006). Although ACEIs/ARBs initiation showed a numerical trend toward lower odds for both outcomes compared to discontinuation, these associations did not reach statistical significance (AKI progression: OR 0.64, 95% CI 0.38–1.08, P = 0.095; mortality: OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.17–1.58, P = 0.251) (Table 2).

The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis

Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified several independent factors associated with 14-day AKI progression and mortality. For AKI progression, significant independent factors included elevated Elixhauser score (OR 1.21, 95% CI 1.12–1.30, P < 0.001), increased WBC counts (OR 1.04, 95% CI 1.01–1.08, P = 0.012), higher SOFA score (OR 1.28, 95% CI 1.13–1.45, P < 0.001), and elevated sodium levels (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.00-1.17, P = 0.046). Conversely, factors inversely associated with this outcome included higher chloride levels (OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.87–0.99, P = 0.025) and ICU admission (OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.32–0.97, P = 0.037). Crucially,, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with lower odds of AKI progression compared to discontinuation (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.29–0.67, P < 0.001).

For 14-day mortality, independent factors included advanced age (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.03–1.14, P = 0.004), ICU admission (OR 3.27, 95% CI 1.06–10.12, P = 0.040), elevated WBC counts (OR 1.05, 95% CI 1.01–1.10, P = 0.017), increased sodium levels (OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.09–1.57, P = 0.005), higher potassium levels (OR 2.81, 95% CI 1.19–6.61, P = 0.018), and higher SOFA score (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.28–2.15, P < 0.001). Lower chloride levels were associated with a higher likelihood of mortality (OR 0.84, 95% CI 0.72–0.98, P = 0.028).While ACEIs/ARBs persistence and initiation showed numerical trends toward lower odds for both outcomes compared to discontinuation, these associations did not reach statistical significance for mortality (persistence: OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.13–1.20, P = 0.101; initiation: OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.09–2.23, P = 0.324) (Table 3).

The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis

The results of multiple models of multivariable logistic regression analysis

Multiple logistic regression models were constructed with progressive adjustment for potential confounders to evaluate the association between ACEIs/ARBs use patterns and clinical outcomes.

For 14-day AKI progression, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was consistently associated with lower odds across all models compared to discontinuation: crude model (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.58, P < 0.001), after demographic adjustment (Model 1: OR 0.38, 95% CI 0.26–0.57, P < 0.001), with additional laboratory parameter adjustment (Model 2: OR 0.37, 95% CI 0.25–0.56, P < 0.001), in the fully adjusted model including disease severity scores (Model 3: OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.29–0.67, P < 0.001), and in the inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) adjusted model (Model 4) incorporating the same covariates as Model 3 (OR 0.43, 95% CI 0.29–0.66, P < 0.001). ACEIs/ARBs initiation showed significant inverse associations in crude model (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.13–0.71, P < 0.001), Models 1(OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.33–0.98, P = 0.042), and 2 (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.33-1.00, P = 0.049, respectively), though these associations was attenuated in the fully adjusted model.

Regarding 14-day mortality, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with a lower likelihood in the crude model (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.13–0.71, P = 0.006), which remained consistent after adjustment for demographics (Model 1: OR 0.28, 95% CI 0.10–0.73, P = 0.010) and laboratory parameters (Model 2: OR 0.27, 95% CI 0.09–0.78, P = 0.016). However, this association was attenuated after additional adjustment for disease severity scores (Model 3: OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.13–1.20, P = 0.101). ACEIs/ARBs initiation showed a numerical trend toward lower odds across all models, but not reaching statistical significance (Table 4).

The results of sensitivity analyses

Subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate the consistency of ACEIs/ARBs effects across different patient populations. For 14-day AKI progression, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with lower odds across multiple subgroups compared to discontinuation: age < 65 years (OR 0.30, 95% CI 0.14–0.68, P = 0.004), age ≥ 65 years (OR 0.52, 95% CI 0.30–0.88, P = 0.015), non-CKD (OR 0.47, 95% CI 0.27–0.81, P = 0.007), CKD (OR 0.37, 95% CI 0.18–0.79, P = 0.009), Elixhauser score ≥ 5 (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.27–0.71, P < 0.001) and urban hospital (OR 0.37, 95%CI 0.23–0.62, P < 0.001). ACEIs/ARBs initiation was associated with a lower likelihood of AKI progression only in the non-CKD subgroup (OR 0.42, 95% CI 0.18–0.96, P = 0.041). Regarding 14-day mortality, no significant association was observed among ACEIs/ARBs discontinuation, persistence, or initiation and 14-day mortality outcomes—except for patients in urban hospitals, where persistence was associated with a lower likelihood (OR 0.08, 95% CI 0.01–0.99, P = 0.049). Of note, some subgroup analyses for mortality outcomes yielded unstable estimates (indicated by infinite confidence intervals), particularly in younger patients and those with CKD, likely due to limited event numbers in these strata. The E-value of 4.08 implied that unmeasured confounding with a risk ratio of at least 4.08 for both exposure and outcome would be required to refuse the observed association (Table 5).

Discussion

In this multicenter study examining ACEIs/ARBs management strategies in patients with AKI, we found several key findings with important clinical implications. First, ACEIs/ARBs persistence was associated with significantly lower odds of AKI progression compared to discontinuation. Second, while ACEIs/ARBs persistence showed an inverse correlation with mortality, these associations were attenuated after full adjustment for disease severity.

Our study revealed that ACEI/ARB persistence at AKI onset was associated with significantly lower odds of AKI progression. These findings was inconsistent with the common clinical practice of routine ACEIs/ARBs discontinuation during AKI episodes [4, 6]. ACEIs/ARBs’ multifunctional effects and anti-fibrotic effects [9, 10]-prevent RAAS rebound and preserve endothelial/perfusion function [11–13] —might explain the associations. ACEI/ARB discontinuation might aggravate cardiovascular disease, such as hypertension and heart failure [14–16]. These findings suggest that clinicians should be cautious regarding a universal approach to ACEI/ARB withdrawal during AKI [4, 6].

These exploratory, underpowered subgroup analyses revealed potential heterogeneity, with therapy persistence being associated with lower AKI progression predominantly in milder cases. Consequently, individualised therapy based on clinical stability required prospective validation [17]. Previous studies showed that among clinically indicated patients, timely ACEI/ARB initiation after renal stabilization correlates with a lower likelihood of all-cause mortality and composite cardiorenal endpoints [18–21]. However, these agents increased hyperkalemia and rehospitalization risks, particularly when used in combination treatment [19, 21]. These prior reports, which emphasised the essential need for monitoring renal function and serum electrolytes, closely corresponded with the trends found in our population. While most prior investigations focused on ACEI/ARB management subsequent to renal function stabilization, our study explored the associations at the onset of AKI—offering insights into a gap in the existing observational literature. Functionally, reduced intraglomerular pressure could exacerbate GFR decline and oliguria in the settings of compromised renal perfusion [22]. Concurrently, drug-induced vasodilation might aggravate hypotension in hemodynamically unstable patients, potentially perpetuating renal ischemia [23]. Furthermore, aldosterone suppression, superimposed on impaired baseline potassium clearance, heightened the risk of hyperkalemia, which could potentially trigger fatal arrhythmias [24]. Therefore, these observational findings warranted a cautious interpretation, particularly regarding their potential relationship to clinical scenarios.

Multiple limitations were acknowledged in the interpretation of our results. Firstly, as a secondary analysis of a randomised controlled trial, the screening method of the original study might have produced selection bias. Importantly, despite comprehensive statistical controls through multivariable adjustment and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), our results were significantly vulnerable to considerable residual confounding. Patients whose ACEIs or ARB were discontinued exhibited markedly poorer health, as evidenced by lower mean arterial pressure, elevated SOFA scores, deteriorated baseline renal function, increased prevalence of chronic kidney disease, and higher Elixhauser scores. These baseline disparities strongly suggested confounding by indication, which neither multivariable modelling nor IPTW could rectify. Secondly, our dataset was deficient in essential clinical and metabolic characteristics, notably cumulative fluid balance, vasopressor needs, lactate levels, urine output trends, physician reason for cessation, nephrotoxic exposures, and dynamic haemodynamics. The exclusion of these critical factors severely restricted any causal interpretation; hence, the data were regarded only as descriptive and hypothesis-generating, rather than as a basis for therapeutic decision-making. Thirdly, the 24-hour exposure period did not accurately reflect real-world practices, and the absence of comprehensive sensitivity analyses restricted the validation of consistency. Fourthly, the limited occurrence of events in some strata, especially about 14-day mortality, constrained statistical accuracy. Fifthly, our findings lacked generalisability for individuals with a baseline creatinine level over 4.0 mg/dL. Sixthly, our computed E-value (4.08) did not reduce the probability that unmeasured confounding collectively skewed our values. The brief 14-day follow-up, absence of standardised admission criteria, and intrinsic biases in EHR documentation represented further limitations. Future prospective randomised trials with enhanced monitoring are essential to clarify optimal ACEI/ARB management strategies during AKI episodes.

Conclusion

In this retrospective cohort, the persistence of ACEI/ARB at the onset of AKI was associated with lower odds of 14-day progression, but not mortality. For the 14-day mortality, due to limited events, wide-ranging confidence intervals, and post-adjustment attenuation, these statistical instabilities need highly conservative interpretation and prospective validation.
