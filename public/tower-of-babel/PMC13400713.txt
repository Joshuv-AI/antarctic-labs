Letter to the Editor regarding: Anna Rienmueller, Colby Oitment, Thorsten Jentzsch, Alisson Teles, Jeffrey Hills, Charles Fisher, Caglar Yilgor, and Michael Kelly, AO Spine Knowledge Forum Deformity: AO Spine Clinical Practice Recommendations: Sagittal Alignment Assessment and Planning in Adult Spinal Deformity Surgery, First published online May 27, 2026, Online First: doi.org/10.1177/219256822614521. 1

We read this extraordinary publication, AO Spine Clinical Practice Recommendations: Sagittal Alignment Assessment and Planning in Adult Spinal Deformity Surgery, which described four strategies to guide the management of adult spinal deformity and optimize preoperative planning. Used together, these models provide complementary perspectives that enhance both preoperative decision-making and postoperative evaluation. However, after reviewing the article thoroughly, we have three observations:

1. The article discusses the classical Roussouly Classification as a model for evaluating sagittal alignment in adult spinal deformity. 1 However, the fundamental disadvantage of this classification is that it initially uses the Sacral Slope (SS) to categorize patients into four spinal shapes. However, due to the degenerative changes the spine undergoes, this parameter is affected, potentially leading to errors in patient classification. 2 This is where Sebaaly et al., 3 in their article, clarify the degenerative changes the spine undergoes and propose a classification of the degenerative evolution of the Roussouly classification, thereby addressing this difficulty; however, it is not taken into account.

2. The article discusses the use of the first model of the classic Roussouly classification to influence surgical strategies and interpretation of spinal pathology, stating that recognizing the patient’s base type may help set alignment targets and anticipate compensatory patterns to optimize results. 1

3. We believe that the significant contribution of Laouissat et al. 4 in this regard was the refounding of the Roussouly classification, initially using Pelvic Incidence (PI) as a parameter to recategorize patients, dividing them into two groups: PI < 50° and > 50°. Then, using SS, they facilitated the recategorization of spinal shape and found a new group: type 3 Anteverse Pelvic (3AP). It was characterized by a PI < 50° and SS between 35° - 45° and Pelvic Tilt (PT) < 8° (Anteverse) 4 ; and for this reason, it would allow a better understanding of the patient’s sagittal profile, not starting from a parameter that can be affected by spinal pathology.

The article is truly a masterpiece because it outlines strategies for surgical planning in adult spinal deformity. We can only say congratulations on such magnificent work.

We hope that my explanations and observations will be helpful to readers of this valuable journal.
