Congruences of shifted Jack Littlewood–Richardson coefficients
Ryan Mickler

Abstract
. The shifted Jack Littlewood–Richardson coefficients g μ ​ ν λ g^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu} , first studied by Alexandersson-Féray, are Laurent polynomials in the Jack parameter α \alpha attached to triples of partitions, which generalize the classical Jack Littlewood–Richardson coefficients investigated by Stanley, et al. In a previous work of the author’s, it was conjectured that the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients for two triples, in which one of the partitions differ by a single box move, are congruent modulo the α \alpha -hook length of the pivot box for that move. In this note we prove that conjecture. We also investigate the extension of that conjecture to shifted Macdonald functions, which remains open pending two properies of Lassalle’s shift map in that case.

1.2.
Shifted coordinate congruence

The shifted variables
z_{i}=\alpha x_{i}-i
are evaluated at a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots)
as

z_{i}(\lambda)=\alpha\lambda_{i}-i.

This present work begins with the following observation about the relationship between pivots and shifted coordinates.

Lemma 1
.

If
\lambda\sim_{p}\tilde{\lambda}
via a pivot at
p
with common hook
h
, removing a box from row

a
and adding a box to row
b>a
, then

\{z_{i}(\lambda)\}_{i\geq 1}\equiv\{z_{i}(\tilde{\lambda})\}_{i\geq 1}\pmod{h}

as multisets, after the interchange of the two elements indexing the affected rows

a\leftrightarrow b
.

Proof.

By assumption,

\tilde{\lambda}_{a}=\lambda_{a}-1,\qquad\tilde{\lambda}_{b}=\lambda_{b}+1,

and all other parts are unchanged. The pivot column is
j=\lambda_{b}+1
. At the
pivot box
p=(a,j)
in
\lambda
, we have

\operatorname{arm}_{\lambda}(p)=\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{b}-1,\qquad\operatorname{leg}_{\lambda}(p)=b-a-1.

Thus the shared hook is

h=h_{\lambda}(p)=h^{\prime}_{\tilde{\lambda}}(p)=\alpha(\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{b}-1)+(b-a).

Only the shifted coordinates in rows
a
and
b
change. A direct calculation
gives

z_{a}(\lambda)-z_{b}(\tilde{\lambda})=\alpha\lambda_{a}-a-\bigl(\alpha(\lambda_{b}+1)-b\bigr)=\alpha(\lambda_{a}-\lambda_{b}-1)+(b-a)=h,

and

z_{b}(\lambda)-z_{a}(\tilde{\lambda})=\alpha\lambda_{b}-b-\bigl(\alpha(\lambda_{a}-1)-a\bigr)=-h.

For every row
r\notin\{a,b\}
we have

z_{r}(\lambda)=z_{r}(\tilde{\lambda})
. Hence the two shifted-coordinate multisets
agree modulo
h
after interchanging the two affected rows
a
and
b
.
∎

2.1.
Strategy

The goal is to realize

as

for a single Laurent shifted symmetric function. The natural candidate is the
\alpha
-shifted symmetric function

J^{\#}_{\mu}J^{\#}_{\nu}=\sum_{\rho:|\rho|\leq|\mu|+|\nu|}d_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}J^{\#}_{\rho},

which packages all of the structure constants

we want its degree-

(7)

since then the vanishing property (

and Lemma

The obstruction is that extracting this graded component

is has Laurent coefficients, its degree-

(as defined by (

2.2.

We can move between the filtered ring of shifted symmetric functions and the graded ring of symmetric functions using Lassalle’s shift transform.

Proposition 1

For our purposes, we need to strengthen this to an isomorphism of rings with Laurent

Recall that

Two features distinguish the Macdonald case from the Jack case. The first is

preserves
\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
. Equivalently

L_{\mu}^{\dagger}(p_{\rho})\in\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
for every
\rho
.

This is the Macdonald analogue of the skew-Laurent theorem used for input
pivots in the Jack case. It is strictly stronger than
Conjecture
4
. Since
J_{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
, having

L_{\mu}^{\dagger}
preserve
\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
immediately yields

\Psi_{\lambda/\mu}=L_{\mu}^{\dagger}(J_{\lambda})\in\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
. In the Jack case the
analogous statement holds because the power-sum metric (
2
) is Laurent.

Example 6
.

Take
\lambda=(2,1)
and
\mu=(1)
. The shifted product coefficients
appearing in the skew sum include

d^{(2,1)}_{(1),(2)}=\frac{(q-1)(q+1)}{q^{2}t-1},\qquad d^{(2,1)}_{(1),(1,1)}=\frac{(t-1)(t+1)}{qt^{2}-1},

and

d^{(2,1)}_{(1),(2,1)}=-t^{-1}(t-1)(q-1)(qt+t+1).

Thus the
J
-basis expression for
\Psi_{(2,1)/(1)}
has apparent
non-Laurent denominators:

[J_{(2)}]\Psi_{(2,1)/(1)}=\frac{(t-1)(q-1)(qt^{2}-1)}{qt-1},

[J_{(1,1)}]\Psi_{(2,1)/(1)}=\frac{(t-1)(q-1)(q^{2}t-1)}{qt-1}.

However these denominators cancel after expanding in the ordinary monomial
symmetric basis:

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2,1)/(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(q-1)(t-1)^{3}(2qt+q+t+2)m_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle+(q-1)(t-1)^{2}(qt^{2}-1)m_{(2)}

\displaystyle+t^{-1}(q-1)(t-1)^{4}(qt+t+1)(2qt+q+t+2)m_{(1,1,1)}

\displaystyle+t^{-1}(q-1)(t-1)^{3}(qt+t+1)(qt^{2}-1)m_{(2,1)}.

All coefficients lie in
R=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]
. This gives a small
example of the skew Laurent cancellation predicted by
Conjecture
4
.

Proposition 5

(Input pivots)
.

Assume Conjecture
4
, and let
\nu\sim_{p}\tilde{\nu}
be a
one-box input pivot with hook
h=1-q^{A}t^{B}
and
|\nu|=|\tilde{\nu}|=m
. Then

u_{\nu}\,g_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(q,t)\equiv u_{\tilde{\nu}}\,g_{\mu\tilde{\nu}}^{\lambda}(q,t)\pmod{h}.

Proof.

This is the Jack input-pivot argument verbatim. By
Conjecture
4
,
\Psi_{\lambda/\mu}\in\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
,
so
\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(\Psi_{\lambda/\mu})\in\Lambda^{*}_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
by
Conjecture
3
. Applying
Lemma
5
and the vanishing property at partitions
of size
m
gives

u_{\nu}\,d^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}(q,t)\,c_{\lambda}c^{\prime}_{\lambda}\equiv u_{\tilde{\nu}}\,d^{\lambda}_{\mu\tilde{\nu}}(q,t)\,c_{\lambda}c^{\prime}_{\lambda}\pmod{h},

which is the claim since
g_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}=c_{\lambda}c^{\prime}_{\lambda}\,d^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}

by definition.
∎

As before, this takes the modified form

t^{-n(\nu)}g_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(q,t)\equiv t^{-n(\tilde{\nu})}g_{\mu\tilde{\nu}}^{\lambda}(q,t)\pmod{h}.

Example 7
.

Take
\lambda=(3,3,1),\mu=(2,1)
and consider the input pivot

\nu=(3,1)\sim_{p}\tilde{\nu}=(2,2).

Here
n(\nu)=1,n(\tilde{\nu})=2
,
and the pivot hook is

h=1-qt.

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+1)(q+1)^{2}(t-1)^{4}(q-1)^{5}(qt^{2}-1)^{2}(q^{3}t-1)^{2}(q^{2}t^{3}-1),

while

\displaystyle g_{\mu\tilde{\nu}}^{\lambda}(q,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+1)^{2}(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{4}(t-1)^{5}(qt^{2}-1)(q^{2}t-1)^{2}(q^{3}t-1)(q^{2}t^{3}-1).

Thus the modified input-pivot congruence reads

t^{-1}g_{(2,1),(3,1);(3,3,1)}(q,t)\equiv t^{-2}g_{(2,1),(2,2);(3,3,1)}(q,t)\pmod{1-qt}.

Let

L=t^{-1}g_{(2,1),(3,1);(3,3,1)}(q,t),\qquad R=t^{-2}g_{(2,1),(2,2);(3,3,1)}(q,t).

A direct simplification gives the identity

L-R=hf,

where
f\in\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]
is the Laurent polynomial

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-2}(t+1)(q+1)^{2}(q-1)^{4}(t-1)^{4}(qt^{2}-1)(q^{3}t-1)(q^{2}t^{3}-1)

\displaystyle\cdot\bigl(q^{4}t^{3}-2q^{3}t^{3}+q^{3}t^{2}-2q^{2}t^{2}+2q^{2}t+qt^{2}-2qt+t^{2}+t-1\bigr).

4.
Status of the Laurent-lattice conjecture

To make the claim concrete, expand the Macdonald characters

p^{*}_{\mu}=\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(p_{\mu})
in the stable shifted power-sum basis

\{P^{\circ}_{\kappa}\}
of
\Lambda^{*}_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
introduced above, and let

b_{\mu\nu}:=[P^{\circ}_{\nu}]\,\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(p_{\mu})

be the resulting coefficient matrix, where
\nu,\mu
range over partitions of
size at most
n
and
P(n)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p(k)
is the number of such partitions.

Since the top homogeneous component of
p^{*}_{\mu}=\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(p_{\mu})

is
p_{\mu}
and
P^{\circ}_{\mu}=p_{\mu}+(\text{lower degree})
, we have

p^{*}_{\mu}=P^{\circ}_{\mu}+\sum_{|\kappa|<|\mu|}b_{\mu\kappa}P^{\circ}_{\kappa}
, so

b
is unitriangular with respect to degree (all diagonal entries equal to

1
). In particular
\det b=1
, so
b
is invertible with
b^{-1}
Laurent as
long as
b
itself is Laurent. Hence Conjecture
3

holds if and only if
b
has Laurent entries, i.e.
b\in\mathrm{SL}_{P(n)}\bigl(\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]\bigr)
for every
n
.

A first step toward this is the following integrality theorem of Knop, where

M^{\circ}_{\nu}
denotes the monomial symmetric function in the shifted
coordinates (the de-shifted ordinary monomial
m_{\nu}
).

Theorem 3

(Knop
[
13
]
)
.

The coefficient

a_{\mu\nu}:=[M^{\circ}_{\nu}]\,\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(J_{\mu})\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)

is Laurent, i.e.
a_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]
. In fact
a_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}[q,t^{\pm}]
.

This does not quite suffice, since the integral Macdonald symmetric functions
J_{\mu}
are
not
a basis of
\Lambda_{[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]}
. We instead conjecture the corresponding statement in the power-sum basis.

Conjecture 6
.

The coefficient

b_{\mu\nu}:=[P^{\circ}_{\nu}]\,\operatorname{sh}_{q,t}(p_{\mu})\in\mathbb{Q}(q,t)

is Laurent, i.e.
b_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm},t^{\pm}]
. In fact
b_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}[q,t^{\pm}]
.

For example

\displaystyle p^{*}_{21}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\circ}_{21}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{-1}(q+1)(qt-q+t+1)P^{\circ}_{11}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{-1}(t+1)(q-1)(q+1)P^{\circ}_{2}

\displaystyle+t^{-1}(q-1)(q+1)(qt+1)P^{\circ}_{1}.

Conjecture
6
is exactly the statement that
b
has Laurent entries, so by the triangularity argument above it implies Conjecture
3
.
