Development and validation of a multivariate predictive model for the risk of refractory mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia in children based on quantitative computed tomography features
Authors: Lei Wang, Tian Hu, Yu Lei, Tao Ren, Shunlin Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRefractory mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) in children often responds poorly to treatment and is prone to causing serious complications; therefore, early identification is clinically crucial. The aim of this study is to evaluate quantitative imaging features from pediatric chest computed tomography (CT) scans, develop a multivariate predictive model, perform internal validation of the model, and investigate the predictive value of quantitative CT measures for the risk of RMPP in children.MethodsThis study retrospectively enrolled hospitalized pediatric patients who underwent their first chest CT scan between January 2019 and December 2021 and were clinically diagnosed with mycoplasma pneumonia, and selected participants according to established criteria. Cohorts were stratified by hospital: 116 cases from hospital A served as the training set, while 81 cases from hospitals B, C, and D served as the external validation set, for a total of 197 pediatric patients, including 60 with RMPP and 137 with conventional Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP). Missing data were handled using the complete-cases method. Clinical, laboratory, and quantitative CT imaging features were uniformly collected as candidate predictors. Independent predictors were identified using stepwise logistic regression, and a nomogram model was constructed. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, and the model’s discriminatory performance in the training and validation sets was evaluated via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to complete the internal validation of the model.ResultsA total of 60 children with RMPP and 137 children with general mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (GMPP) were included (training set, 116; validation set, 81). Baseline data for all pediatric patients included age, sex, duration of fever, complete blood count, inflammatory markers, and chest CT findings; there were no statistically significant differences in age, sex, or baseline clinical indicators between the training and validation sets (P>0.05). Compared to the general mycoplasma pneumonia group, children with RMPP in the training set exhibited significantly higher levels of C-reactive protein (CRP), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and lesion percentage to lung volume (LeV%), alongside a correspondingly lower percentage of ground-glass opacity (GGO%). Multivariate analysis revealed that CRP, LDH, LeV%, and GGO% were independent predictors of RMPP. The developed nomogram had high accuracy, with the area under the curve (AUC) of the ROC values of 0.933 [95% confidence interval (CI), 0.888–0.977] in the training set and 0.922 (95% CI, 0.859–0.985) in the validation set, respectively. The predicted probabilities from the nomogram, as indicated by the calibration curve and Hosmer-Lemeshow test, were in good agreement with the observed probabilities.ConclusionsThe nomogram that combines clinical and CT quantitative features may effectively predict the risk of RMPP in children. It has certain predictive value for the risk of RMPP in children and can serve as a reference for the development of clinical management strategies.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study confirmed that the quantitative indices lesion percentage to lung volume (LeV%) and percentage of ground-glass opacity (GGO%) from initial chest computed tomography (CT) imaging, in combination with C-reactive protein (CRP) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), are independent predictors of refractory mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) in children. A nomogram model constructed based on these indices demonstrated high predictive value for RMPP in hospitalized children.

What is known and what is new?

• The incidence of RMPP in children is on the rise. Traditional imaging assessments lack objectivity, and the predictive power of individual biomarkers is limited; there is an urgent clinical need for accurate early prediction methods.

• This study identified the initial quantitative CT parameters LeV% and GGO% as independent predictors of RMPP in children, alongside CRP and LDH, and successfully developed and validated a nomogram prediction model incorporating clinical and quantitative CT features.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Our findings help clinicians achieve early and accurate risk assessment of RMPP in children by combining initial CT quantitative metrics with conventional inflammatory markers. In future clinical practice, CT quantitative metrics such as LeV% and GGO% should be incorporated into the initial evaluation protocol for mycoplasma pneumonia in children to identify high-risk patients early and adjust treatment strategies promptly, thereby avoiding delays in intervention caused by the sole use of conventional antibiotics.

Introduction

Mycoplasma pneumoniae (MP) is an important pathogen of pneumonia in children. During the non-epidemic period, Mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (MPP) accounted for about 4–8% of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in children, while during the epidemic period, MPP accounted for 20–40% of CAP in children, 20% of whom required hospitalization (1,2). While MP is generally considered a self-limited disease, it may develop into a severe, life-threatening infection in some pediatric cases (3). In recent years, the incidence of refractory mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (RMPP) has been soaring (4). A meta-analysis found that the overall prevalence of RMPP among children reached 37.8%, and that the prevalence rose significantly over the course of the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic, increasing steadily from 33.1% before the pandemic to 86.5% after the pandemic (5). RMPP in children is characterized by a lack of clinical response to appropriate macrolide antibiotics, accompanied by persistent fever and radiological deterioration, and while general mycoplasma pneumonia is typically managed with macrolides, the refractory variant requires alternative therapeutic interventions such as corticosteroids or immunoglobulins (6,7). Therefore, the early discrimination of RMPP is particularly important.

Some biomarkers, such as lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), C-reactive protein (CRP), D-dimer, and cytokines [interleukin (IL)-6, IL-10, and so on] have been used as predictors of RMPP (8-11). A study by Chen et al. confirmed that age, multiple inflammatory markers, elevated liver enzyme levels, pleural effusion, and pulmonary consolidation are risk factors for RMPP in children, with white blood cell count, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) identified as newly associated markers (12). Nevertheless, some of these biomarkers are not routinely available in clinical practice and are difficult to obtain. In addition, the area under the curve (AUC) of these factors ranges from 0.7 to 0.8, suggesting that their diagnostic efficiency needs to be further improved.

Computed tomography (CT) imaging serves as an essential and effective method for diagnosing pneumonia (13). Some previous studies have shown that the extent of pulmonary lesions in children with RMPP can predict the occurrence of RMPP (14,15). However, in the past, traditional imaging signs and visual scores lacked objectivity in judging the severity of the disease. Some studies have shown that doctors tend to overestimate the degree of disease compared with the proportion of lesions obtained by quantitative CT (16). Based on an existing two-center study that considered age, duration of fever, lymphocyte count, D-dimer levels, and subjective pulmonary imaging scores, and confirmed its good predictive performance and clinical value. However, this model relies solely on manual visual grading of pulmonary lesions and does not incorporate quantitative CT lesion parameters, resulting in subjective interpretation biases and an inability to accurately and standardizedly quantify the actual pulmonary lesion burden (17). Therefore, for pediatric patients with MPP who require chest CT imaging evaluation, there is an urgent clinical need for an effective assessment tool that can integrate conventional clinical indicators with objective, quantifiable imaging data to enable early identification of RMPP. With the development of quantitative CT, automatic segmentation of lung volume has been widely used for the diagnosis and evaluation of lung diseases (18,19).

Considering the above factors, the purpose of this study was to develop and validate a nomogram incorporating clinical characteristics and CT features by quantitative CT. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0300/rc).

Methods

Patients

This multicentric retrospective study included all children with MPP hospitalized in our institutions. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of The Affiliated Hospital of Yan’an University (No. IIT-R-20250228), and all participants provided informed consent. The study protocol was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of this study.

We screened pediatric patients with MPP who were treated at Yan’an University Affiliated Hospital between January 2019 and December 2021 for inclusion in the study. Data were sourced from four hospitals at the secondary level or higher; patients from Yan’an University Affiliated Hospital were assigned to the validation set. The sample size was determined based on the criterion of the number of events per independent variable (EPV ≥10) in multivariate predictive models. The minimum sample size required for modeling was calculated by combining this criterion with the incidence rates of RMPP reported in previous literature. The total number of cases retrospectively collected in this study met this minimum sample size requirement. A total of 197 pediatric patients with Mycoplasma pneumonia were enrolled, including 60 cases of RMPP and 137 cases of ordinary general mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia (GMPP). These were divided into a training set of 116 cases and a validation set of 81 cases for model construction and validation. Missing data in this study were handled using the complete case method; only subjects with complete data were included in subsequent statistical analyses, and no imputation of missing values was performed. Flow diagram of the research population. CT, computed tomography; GMPP, general mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia; RMPP, refractory mycoplasma pneumoniae pneumonia.

The inclusion criteria were: (I) symptoms and signs of respiratory tract infection, including cough, abnormal pulmonary auscultation, and CT imaging showing pulmonary lesions; (II) positive results of serological test [MP-immunoglobulin M (IgM) positive, antibody titer ≥1:160], polymerase chain reaction detected MP, or respiratory tract samples were cultured and isolated from MP.

The exclusion criteria were: (I) patients with congenital heart disease, chromosomal disease, metabolic disease, immunodeficiency disease, hematological tumor disease, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, nervous system dysplasia, and epilepsy; (II) patients with incomplete clinical information; (III) uncooperative patients, with CT image artifacts, CT data showing pneumonia segmentation failure, and patients who cannot carry out data processing.

The diagnostic criteria for RMPP were following: symptoms that persisted after using macrolide antibiotics for more than 7 days, and a persistent fever or radiological deterioration (20).

Data collection

This study extracted data on pediatric patients from the inpatient medical records of various centers. The collected data included demographic characteristics, signs and symptoms, serological examination, CT examination, and hospitalization time. Age and gender were obtained from medical records; the number of days with fever was defined as the duration of fever prior to admission; body temperature at admission was measured using an electronic thermometer; and white blood cell count, CRP, and LDH were determined through venous blood samples. All personnel involved in data extraction and statistical analysis were blinded to the group assignments and study outcomes.

Acquisition of CT images

CT examinations were performed using multi-detector CT scanners (UCT-760, UNITED IMAGING, China; Definition As, Siemens, Germany; Brilliance iCT, PHILIPS, Holland). The patient was placed in a supine position during the scan. The scan scope was from the apex of the lung to the base of the lung. Some children were given chloral hydrate (0.5 mL/kg, the maximum dose ≤10 mL) before the examination. The tube voltage was 100 to 120 kVp with auto current ranging from 30 to150 mA. The CT image reconstruction matrix was 512×512, the thickness of the scanning layer was 5 mm, and the thickness of the reconstruction layer was 0.625 to 2 mm.

Image quantization based on quantitative CT

The original data of CT scanning were imported into artificial intelligence (AI) computer-aided diagnosis and analysis software for pulmonary infection in “Dicom” format for segmentation. The software first automatically recognizes the pulmonary boundaries, bronchi, and blood vessels, removes the bronchi and blood vessels from the lung tissue, and then identifies high-density pneumonia lesions by density threshold and regional growth method (21). The quantitative parameters of pulmonary lesions were obtained by AI software, including the lesion volume (LeV), lesion percentage to lung volume (LeV%), mean lesion density (MLeD), ground-glass opacity percentage of the lesion (GGO%), and lesion weight (LM). The GGO percentage of the lesion was defined as the lesion pixels whose density was <−300 Hounsfield unit (HU).

Two experienced thoracic radiologists independently reviewed the lesions from the quantitative CT segmentations without knowledge of the patients’ clinical outcomes, and manually removed and added false-positive and false-negative regions from the segmentations. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 24; IBM, New York, USA) and R software (version 4.1.0). P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Continuous variables with normal distribution were compared using a t-test and expressed by mean ± standard deviation, while those not conforming to normal distribution were compared by Mann-Whitney U test and expressed by medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Categorical variables were analyzed by the χ2 test and expressed as numbers (n) and percentages (%). Univariate logistic regression was performed on all baseline indicators. Variables with a P<0.1 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate model. After excluding indicators with no practical predictive value based on the clinical significance of mycoplasma pneumonia in children, a multivariate predictive model was constructed using stepwise logistic regression. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted based on the regression coefficients, and individual predictive probabilities were calculated. The discriminatory power and calibration of the probability plots were evaluated using ROC curves, the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, and calibration plots for the training and validation sets. The optimal cutoff value for the predicted probability was selected using the maximum Jorden index, with an AUC ≥0.70 and sensitivity and specificity both ≥0.70 at the optimal threshold serving as the model validation criteria. A sensitivity analysis was conducted on missing data; the dataset was reconstructed using multiple interpolation methods, and the model was re-estimated. The regression coefficients, AUC, and calibration performance of the models under the complete-cases approach and the multiple interpolation strategies were compared to verify the robustness of the study conclusions. Both the training and validation datasets for this study were derived from pediatric patients with MPP; consistency was maintained in the definition of predictive factors, outcome criteria, and evaluation methods, and there were no substantial differences in study design.

Evaluating the performance of the nomogram

Validation of the nomogram was performed with a 1000 bootstrap analysis. The AUC values of the probability of occurrence of RMPP in the training set and the validation set were 0.933 [95% confidence interval (CI), 0.888–0.977] and 0.922 (95% CI, 0.859–0.985), respectively, as shown in Figure 5. The calibration plots of the nomogram showed that the predictions and observations on the training and validation sets are in good agreement, as shown in Figure 6. The Hosmer-Lemesshow goodness-of-fit test revealed no significant difference between observed and predicted events in the training set (P=0.83) and validation set (P=0.30), respectively.

Sensitivity analysis

To assess the impact of potential selection bias from the complete-case analysis method on model results, this study used multiple imputation to impute all variables with missing values. Multivariate predictive models were constructed separately based on the complete-case dataset (n=174) and the multiple-imputation dataset (n=197), and the independent predictors, adjusted ORs, and discriminatory performance of the two sets of models were compared. The results are shown in Figure 7.

The forest plot (Figure 7A) shows that the independent predictors identified by the RMPP using the two missing value handling strategies were completely consistent, consisting of CRP, LDH, LeV%, and GGO%. The 95% CIs for the adjusted odds ratios of each variable overlapped well, with no significant shift in the direction or magnitude of the effect sizes, indicating stable predictive performance of the variables. The comparison of discriminatory performance (Figure 7B) showed that for the complete-case method, the AUC for the training set was 0.931 and for the validation set was 0.908; for the multiple interpolation method, the AUC for the training set was 0.932 and for the validation set was 0.901. The AUC values for both datasets were highly similar. The results indicate that the selection bias resulting from the complete-case analysis did not substantially affect the core conclusions of this study; the model’s predictive performance is stable, and the study conclusions demonstrate good robustness.

Discussion

In the present study, we developed and validated a nomogram that combines clinical and CT quantitative features for predicting the occurrence of RMPP in children. The nomogram showed great discriminative value, thus suggesting that it may be a reliable tool to help physicians recognize RMPP earlier.

MP is one of the main pathogens of CAP in children. In recent years, numerous studies have shown that MPP can lead to various serious complications, such as bronchitis obliterans, necrotizing pneumonia, and even death (22,23). Also, it has been reported that the incidence of RMPP is on the rise, which is challenging for the diagnosis of RMPP (24).

Previous studies have shown that computer-aided diagnosis of pulmonary infection can quickly detect the location of pulmonary lesions and quantitatively evaluate the extent and evolution of pulmonary lesions (25). Therefore, this study investigated the quantitative characteristics of biomarkers and CT in children with MPP at admission. Four predictors, including CRP, LDH, LeV%, and GGO%, were identified. Previously, it has been shown that age is one of the predictors of RMPP; however, our study did not replicate these findings, which may be due to the sample size (26). Our results also showed that the levels of serum neutrophils, CRP, and LDH increased and lymphocytes decreased in children with RMPP, which was consistent with previous studies (27).

Some existing studies have also revealed that severe chest imaging manifestations, such as large lobe consolidation and atelectasis, could effectively predict the occurrence of RMPP and its complications (20,28). Compared with the visual score of chest imaging used in previous studies, the quantitative CT index we introduced has the advantages of objectivity and repeatability. LeV% and GGO% represent the percentage of lung lesions in the total lung volume and the proportion of ground-glass density in the lesions. Compared with the GMPP group, the RMPP group had higher LeV% and lower GGO%, which is similar to the previous study arguing that RMPP in children is more likely to show large consolidation (24). This suggests that during the occurrence of RMPP, a strong inflammatory reaction leads to a large number of neutrophils and tissue fluid exudation in the bronchioles and alveolar cavity, eventually resulting in an increase in the extent and density of pulmonary lesions (29).

The predictive power of the nomogram obtained in this study for RMPP showed good performance in both the training set and the validation set. The AUC in the training and validation sets was 0.933 and 0.922, respectively, indicating that the nomogram could correctly distinguish RMPP and GMPP. As a result of this curve, this nomogram may help physicians to early identify RMPP patients and provide timely interventions.

Nevertheless, there are several limitations in the present study. First, this was a retrospective study and the RMPP patient sample size was small, which may cause substantial selection bias; Second, the model included only CRP, LDH, and quantitative CT parameters (LeV% and GGO%) as predictive factors; it did not comprehensively account for factors such as the children’s immune function, concurrent infections, underlying conditions, and treatment adherence, resulting in an incomplete scope of prediction.

In the future, we will further explore the dynamic changes in other indicators and CT quantitative features during hospitalization to more accurately reflect the progression of RMPP in children. Concurrently, by integrating multidimensional clinical factors—including the patients’ immune function, co-infections, underlying conditions, and individualized treatment responses—we will refine the variable system of the predictive model to enhance the comprehensiveness and specificity of risk assessment. This will provide a more reliable basis for the early identification of high-risk cases and the implementation of targeted interventions.

Conclusions

We developed a nomogram to predict RMPP in children, which combines clinical and CT quantitative features with four factors, including CRP, LDH, LeV%, and GGO%. The nomogram performed well and may be used as a reliable and valuable tool for clinical practice.
