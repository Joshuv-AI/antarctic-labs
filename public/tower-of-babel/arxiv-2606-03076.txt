Optimal constants of smoothing estimates for quantum harmonic oscillators
Soichiro Suzuki

Abstract
. We study optimal constants and extremizers of smoothing estimates for quantum harmonic oscillators. In particular, we establish harmonic oscillator analogues of free particle results due to Simon 1992 ( Simon 1992 ), Bez and Sugimoto 2014 ( Bez and Sugimoto 2014 ), and Bez et al. 2015 ( Bez et al. 2015 ).



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
33C45, 35B65, 35Q41, 42B37, 42C10

1.
Introduction

We consider the Schrödinger equation for the
d
-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(x,t)=Hu(x,t),&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{cases}

where
H=H_{0}+\lvert x\rvert^{2}=-\Delta+\lvert x\rvert^{2}
.
In 2011,
Chen 2011
and
Bongioanni and Rogers 2011
established the following inequalities, which are natural analogues of classical results for free particles (see (
1.4
) and (
1.21
)).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
p>1
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(1.3)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}(1+\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{-p/2}\lvert H^{1/4}e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Our aim in this paper is to determine optimal constants and extremizers of these inequalities.
Here, we say that
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is an extremizer when it attains the optimal constant.
First, we give our result for the inequality (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Then the optimal constant of the inequality (
1.2
) is
4\pi/(d-2)
, and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is an extremizer if and only if it is radially symmetric.

Theorem
1.3
is inspired by a similar result for free particles.
It is classical and well known that

(1.4)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
d\geq 3
, as proved by
Kato and Yajima 1989
.

Simon 1992
showed that its optimal constant is
\pi/(d-2)
, and
Bez and Sugimoto 2017
pointed out that
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is an extremizer if and only if it is radially symmetric.
It is also known that this inequality can be improved by using the spherical Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
.

Hoshiro 1997
showed that

(1.5)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+1)^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds, and
Fang and Wang 2011
established the reversed inequality

(1.6)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+1)^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\geq c\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Furthermore,
Bez and Sugimoto 2014
studied the optimal constants of these inequalities and found that the identity

(1.7)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+1)^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt=\frac{\pi}{2}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}

holds when
d=4
(
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
).
More generally, following the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
, actually it is easy to see that

(1.8)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+(d/2-1)^{2})^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt=\frac{\pi}{2}\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds whenever
d\geq 3
.
Inspired by this, we show the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Then we have

(1.9)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}\lvert x\rvert^{-2}\lvert(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+(d/2-1)^{2})^{1/4}e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt=2\pi\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We note that the inequalities (
1.2
), (
1.4
) and identities (
1.8
), (
1.9
) fail when
d=2
.
On the other hand, they are still valid if we restrict initial data to a certain subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. See Section
6.1
for details.

Now we introduce completely monotone functions and Stieltjes functions in order to state our results on the inequality (
1.3
).

Definition

(completely monotone function)
.

A function
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a completely monotone function if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(CM1)

It is infinitely differentiable and
satisfies

(1.10)

(-1)^{n}f^{(n)}(r)\geq 0

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, where
f^{(n)}
denotes the
n
-th derivative of
f
.

(CM2)

There exists a Borel measure
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
such that

(1.11)

f(r)=\int_{\rho\in[0,\infty)}\exp(-r\rho)\,d\lambda(\rho)

holds for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

Definition

(Stieltjes function)
.

A function
f\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a Stieltjes function if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(S1)

There exists a Borel measure
\lambda
on
[0,\infty)
such that

(1.12)

f(r)=\int_{a\in[0,\infty)}\frac{1}{a+r}\,d\lambda(a)

holds for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

(S2)

There exists a completely monotone function
\varphi
such that

(1.13)

f(r)=\int_{\rho\in(0,\infty)}\exp(-r\rho)\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho

holds for every
r\in(0,\infty)
.

The equivalence
\ref{item:completely monotone 1}\iff\ref{item:completely monotone 2}
is known as the
Bernstein 1929
–
Widder 1931
theorem (see
[
2
, §14]
and
[
28
, Theorem 8]
), and
\ref{item:Stieltjes 1}\iff\ref{item:Stieltjes 2}
is an immediate consequence of

(1.14)

\frac{1}{a+r}=\int_{\rho\in(0,\infty)}\exp(-(a+r)\rho)\,d\rho.

Observe that Stieltjes functions are completely monotone but not conversely.
To illustrate, the function

(1.15)

(0,\infty)\ni r\longmapsto(1+r)^{-p/2}

is a Stieltjes function for
p\in(0,2]
but not for
p\in(2,\infty)
, while it is completely monotone for every
p\in(0,\infty)
.
This follows from the integral representation

(1.16)

(1+r)^{-p/2}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(p/2)}\int_{\rho\in(0,\infty)}\exp(-r\rho)\rho^{p/2-1}\exp(-\rho)\,d\rho

and the fact that the function

(1.17)

(0,\infty)\ni\rho\longmapsto\rho^{p/2-1}\exp(-\rho)

is completely monotone for
p\in(0,2]
but not for
p\in(2,\infty)
.
For further results and related topics on completely monotone and Stieltjes functions, see
Widder 1942
,
Schilling et al. 2012
, and the references therein, for example.

Now we give our results on the inequality (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
w\in L^{1}((0,\infty))\setminus\{0\}
is such that

(1.18)

r\longmapsto w(r^{1/2})

is a Stieltjes function.
Then, for every
d\geq 3
, the optimal constant of the inequality

(1.19)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert H^{1/4}e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

is
4\lVert w\rVert_{L^{1}((0,\infty))}
, and there are no extremizers.
In particular, the optimal constant of the inequality (
1.3
) is

(1.20)

4\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}(1+r^{2})^{-p/2}\,dr=\frac{2\pi^{1/2}\Gamma((p-1)/2)}{\Gamma(p/2)}

when
d\geq 3
and
p\in(1,2]
.

For free particles, it is well known that

(1.21)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}(1+\lvert x\rvert^{2})^{-p/2}\lvert H_{0}^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

holds whenever
p>1
.
This inequality was originally proved by
Kato and Yajima 1989
for
p=2
and extended for
p>1
by
Ben-Artzi and Klainerman 1992
.

Simon 1992
showed that its optimal constant is
\pi/2
when
d\geq 3
and
p=2
.

Bez et al. 2015
generalized
Simon 1992
’s result as follows.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
. Suppose that
w\in L^{1}((0,\infty))
is a non-negative function such that the
d
-dimensional Fourier transform of
w(\lvert\makebox[4.30554pt]{{$\cdot$}}\rvert)
is positive everywhere.
Then the optimal constant of the inequality

(1.22)

\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert H_{0}^{1/4}e^{-itH_{0}}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

is
\lVert w\rVert_{L^{1}((0,\infty))}
, and there are no extremizers.
In particular, the optimal constant of the inequality (
1.21
) is

(1.23)

\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}(1+r^{2})^{-p/2}\,dr=\frac{\pi^{1/2}\Gamma((p-1)/2)}{2\Gamma(p/2)}

when
d\geq 3
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Note that if
w\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty))\setminus\{0\}
is such that
r\mapsto w(r^{1/2})
is completely monotone, then
w(\lvert\makebox[4.30554pt]{{$\cdot$}}\rvert)
is the
d
-dimensional Fourier transform of some radially symmetric Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for every
d\geq 1
.
This follows from the definition of complete monotonicity
(CM2)
and the fact that the Fourier transform of the Gaussian is also the Gaussian.
Furthermore, the converse is also true (the Schoenberg theorem
[
22
, Theorem 2]
).
In this sense, our Theorem
1.5
is an analogue of Theorem
1.6
.
We remark that both theorems fail when
d=2
; see Section
6.2
for Theorem
1.5
and
Suzuki 2025a
for Theorem
1.6
, respectively.
Moreover, Theorem
1.5
also fails for
w\in L^{1}((0,\infty))\setminus\{0\}
such that

(1.24)

r\longmapsto w(r^{1/2})

is completely monotone in general; see Section
6.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some well-known results on eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplacian
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
and the Hamiltonian of the quantum harmonic oscillator
H=-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+\lvert x\rvert^{2}
.
These facts can be found in standard references; see, for example,
Müller 1966
,
Thangavelu 1993
,
Szegö 1975
.
Throughout this section, we assume that
d\geq 2
.

2.1.
Spherical Laplacian and harmonic polynomials

It is well known that the spectrum of the spherical Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
is

(2.1)

\sigma(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})=\sigma_{\textup{p}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})=\{\,k(k+d-2):k\in\mathbb{N}\,\}.

Furthermore, the eigenspace associated with the eigenvalue
k(k+d-2)
coincides with the space of spherical harmonic polynomials of degree
k
in
d
variables, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
.
We note that (
2.1
) is equivalent to

(2.2)

\sigma(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+(d/2-1)^{2})=\sigma_{\textup{p}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+(d/2-1)^{2})=\{\,(k+d/2-1)^{2}:k\in\mathbb{N}\,\}.

Hereinafter, we mainly use

(2.3)

-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\coloneqq-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}+(d/2-1)^{2}

rather than
-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
for simplicity.

2.2.
Quantum harmonic oscillator and associated Laguerre polynomials

It is well known that the spectrum of
H=-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+\lvert x\rvert^{2}
is

(2.4)

\sigma(H)=\sigma_{\textup{p}}(H)=\{\,2N+d:N\in\mathbb{N}\,\}.

Chen 2011
and
Bongioanni and Rogers 2011
proved Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
by using the following orthonormal basis of the associated eigenspace constructed from the Hermite polynomials.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Hermite polynomial of degree
k
.
Then
\{H_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},e^{-x^{2}}\,dx)
satisfying

(2.5)

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\lvert H_{k}(x)\rvert^{2}e^{-x^{2}}\,dx=\pi^{1/2}2^{k}\Gamma(k+1).

Now, for each multi-index
k=(k_{j})_{1\leq j\leq d}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define
h_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.6)

h_{k}(x)\coloneqq\pi^{-d/4}e^{-\lvert x\rvert^{2}/2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(2^{k_{j}}\Gamma(k_{j}+1))^{-1/2}H_{k_{j}}(x_{j}),\quad x=(x_{j})_{1\leq j\leq d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then

(2.7)

\left\{\,h_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\text{ such that }\sum_{j=1}^{d}k_{j}=N\,\right\}

is an orthonormal basis of the eigenspace of
H
associated with the eigenvalue
2N+d
.

However, this basis is not well suited for finding the optimal constants of the inequalities (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).
The main difficulty here is that
\{h_{k}\}_{k}
is not orthogonal in the weighted space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w(\lvert x\rvert)\,dx)
generally, even though it is on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, it is also inconvenient to establish the identity (
1.9
), since we cannot work with both
H
and
\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
simultaneously.
In order to avoid these problems, we shall use the following basis constructed from the generalized Laguerre polynomials.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
L_{j}^{(\nu)}\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the generalized Laguerre polynomials of degree
j\in\mathbb{N}
and order
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
, which can be defined in various ways, for example, by the hypergeometric representation

(2.8)

L_{j}^{(\nu)}(r)\coloneqq\frac{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(j+1)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-j\\
\nu+1\end{matrix};r\right)=\frac{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}{\Gamma(\nu+1)\Gamma(j+1)}e^{r}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}j+\nu+1\\
\nu+1\end{matrix};-r\right),

where
\,{}_{1}F_{1}
denotes the confluent hypergeometric function of the first kind.
Then
\{L_{j}^{(\nu)}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}((0,\infty),e^{-r}r^{\nu}\,dr)
satisfying

(2.9)

\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}\lvert L_{j}^{(\nu)}(r)\rvert^{2}e^{-r}r^{\nu}\,dr=\frac{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}{\Gamma(j+1)}

for each
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
.
Now we define
\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(\nu)}\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(\nu)}(r)\coloneqq\left(\frac{2\Gamma(j+1)}{\Gamma(j+\nu+1)}\right)^{1/2}e^{-r^{2}/2}r^{\nu+1/2}L_{j}^{(\nu)}(r^{2}),

and let
\{Y_{k}^{n}\}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
(the space of spherical harmonic polynomials of degree
k
in
d
variables; see Section
2.1
).
Then

(2.11)

\left\{\,\lvert x\rvert^{-(d-1)/2}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}(\lvert x\rvert)Y_{k}^{n}(x/\lvert x\rvert):(j,k,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\text{ such that }2j+k=N,\,1\leq n\leq\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\,\right\}

is an orthonormal basis of the eigenspace of
H
associated with the eigenvalue
2N+d
.

2.3.
Simultaneous spectral decomposition

Combining facts in Sections
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain the following simultaneous spectral decomposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For each
(j,k,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
satisfying
1\leq n\leq\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, we write

(2.12)

\widehat{f}(j,k,n)\coloneqq\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}\left(\int_{\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(r\theta)\overline{Y_{k}^{n}(\theta)}\,d\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}(\theta)\right)r^{(d-1)/2}\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}(r)\,dr.

Then we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle f(r\theta)=r^{-(d-1)/2}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\sum_{n=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\widehat{f}(j,k,n)\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}(r)Y_{k}^{n}(\theta),\quad(r,\theta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\sum_{n=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\lvert\widehat{f}(j,k,n)\rvert^{2}\eqcolon\lVert\widehat{f}\rVert_{\ell^{2}}^{2}.

This decomposition allows us to define a (possibly unbounded) operator
m(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
m\colon\sigma_{\textup{p}}(H)\times\sigma_{\textup{p}}(-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})\to\mathbb{C}
via

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad m(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})f

(2.16)

\displaystyle\coloneqq r^{-(d-1)/2}\sum_{(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\!\!\!\!\sum_{n=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}m(4j+2k+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})\widehat{f}(j,k,n)\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}(r)Y_{k}^{n}(\theta).

3.
Formula for optimal constants

In order to prove our Theorems
1.3
,
1.4
, and
1.5
, we first establish the following formula for the optimal constants, which is an analogue of
Bez and Sugimoto 2014
for free particles.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
w\colon(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
, and
\psi\colon\sigma(H)\times\sigma(-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})\to[0,\infty)
.
We write
\bm{C}^{(d)}(w,\psi)
and
\bm{c}^{(d)}(w,\psi)
for the optimal constants of the following inequalities, respectively:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\psi(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,2\pi]}w(\lvert x\rvert)\lvert\psi(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\geq c\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

For each
\nu\in(-1,\infty)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a linear functional
T_{j}^{(\nu)}
by

(3.3)

T_{j}^{(\nu)}f\coloneqq\int_{r\in(0,\infty)}f(r)\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(\nu)}(r)^{2}\,dr.

Then we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bm{C}^{(d)}(w,\psi)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\pi\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{}

\displaystyle\psi(4j+2k+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})^{2}T_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}w,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\bm{c}^{(d)}(w,\psi)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\pi\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}{}

\displaystyle\psi(4j+2k+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})^{2}T_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}w.

Furthermore,
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is an extremizer of (
3.1
)
if and only if

\widehat{u_{0}}(j,k,n)=0

for every
(j,k,n)
such that

(3.6)

2\pi\psi(4j+2k+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})^{2}T_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}w<\bm{C}^{(d)}(w,\psi).

Similarly,

u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is an extremizer of (
3.2
)
if and only if

\widehat{u_{0}}(j,k,n)=0

for every
(j,k,n)
such that

(3.7)

2\pi\psi(4j+2k+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})^{2}T_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}w>\bm{c}^{(d)}(w,\psi).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Throughout the proof, we use polar coordinates
x=r\theta
,
(r,\theta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Fix
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By Proposition
2.3
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\quad\psi(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=r^{-(d-1)/2}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\\
2j+k=N\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{n=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{k}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\psi(2N+d,(k+d/2-1)^{2})e^{-i(2N+d)t}\widehat{u_{0}}(j,k,n)\mathcal{L}_{j}^{(k+d/2-1)}(r)Y_{k}^{n}(\theta).

Then we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad\int_{t\in[0,2\pi]}\lvert\psi(H,-\Lambda_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})e^{-itH}u_{0}(x)\rvert^{2}\,dt

(3.11)

\displaystyle=2\pi r^{-(d-1)}\sum_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\\
