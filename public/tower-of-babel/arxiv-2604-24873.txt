Amplitudes in self-dual (higher-spin) theories


Abstract
Self-dual theories are powerful toy models of their completions. It was shown recently that there are infinitely many SD-theories once massless higher-spin fields are allowed. The maximal SD-theory is chiral higher-spin gravity. Following the recent [2602.12176] we show that all SD-theories, including those with massless higher-spin fields, have nontrivial tree-level amplitudes in Kleinian signature or complex Minkowski kinematics. Within celestial holography, the nontriviality of amplitudes in chiral higher-spin gravity provides the missing ingredient needed to complete the celestial analogue of the vector-model/higher-spin AdS/CFT duality.

The ladders correspond to nested Lie brackets of the simplest form
[...[[e_{a_{1}},e_{a_{\sigma_{2}}}],e_{a_{\sigma_{3}}}],...,e_{a_{\sigma_{n-1}}}]^{a_{n}}
.
What is special is a particularly simple topology of the contracted structure constants. It was shown in
[
55
]
that all possible contractions of
f^{a}_{bc}
can be reduced to this basis with the help of Jacobi identities, anti-symmetry of
f^{a}_{bc}
and nothing else.

The proof in
[
55
]
did appeal to the usual color ordering, but is expected to be independent of this relation.
Also, the Feynman rules are the same as for the usual color ordering: one has to draw all planar graphs, which correspond to “colorless” Feynman diagrams. The complete amplitude can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{12...n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{n-2}}L{}_{a_{1}a_{\sigma_{2}}...a_{\sigma_{n-1}}}{}^{a_{n}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}(1,\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{n-1},n)\,.

(4.1)

Here,
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
is a partial amplitude that is built from Feynman graphs (stripped of the color factors) that are planar in the given ordering of the external legs. Since the structure constants of a kinematic algebra obey the Jacobi identity and, in general, nothing else, the DDM-ordering can be directly applied to any SD-theory.

[

, but see also

for a closely related ideas and

Since any SD-theory is SDYM
\times
kinematic algebra, we get the desired result both for amplitudes and, if needed, for the BG-recursion as the latter is just a recursive way to compute the same amplitude. In our case, partial amplitudes
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
are those of SDYM.

Since it sounds too simple to be true, let us give a few examples to convince the reader. For SDYM we just get the DDM-ordering:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{12...n}^{\text{SDYM}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{n-2}}L{}_{a_{1}a_{\sigma_{2}}...a_{\sigma_{n-1}}}{}^{a_{n}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{SDYM}_{n}(1,\sigma_{2},...,\sigma_{n-1},n)\,.

(4.2)

The partial amplitudes of SDYM are gauge invariant. Outside the scope of
[
52
]
, i.e. for real Minkowski kinematics, SDYM amplitudes vanish. The gauge invariance of the amplitudes of
[
52
]
follows from the collinearity of the physical momenta and from the fact that the amplitudes have zero weight with respect to the reference spinor
r^{A}
. Since the DDM ladders depend on kinematics for a generic (SD-)theory, one cannot conclude that each term in the sum is gauge invariant, except for HS-SDYM.

Example: HS-SDYM.

This is almost the case of SDYM, the kinematic algebra does not have anything kinematic in it except for helicity labels. This case was already considered in Section
2.2
and let us reframe it in the DDM-setting. It is convenient to pass to
\hat{\lambda}=\lambda-1
as it removes
1
from
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}=1
, where the sign of
\lambda_{3}
is flipped in accordance with the rule to get
\mathbf{f}^{A}_{BC}
. Indeed, we find that
C^{\hat{\lambda}_{1}}_{\hat{\lambda}_{2},\hat{\lambda}_{3}}=\delta^{\hat{\lambda}_{1}}_{\hat{\lambda}_{2}+\hat{\lambda}_{3}}
. At the four-point level the DDM-factor is

\displaystyle f{}_{a_{1}a_{2}}{}^{x_{1}}\,f{}_{x_{1}a_{{3}}}{}^{a_{4}}\,

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{\hat{\lambda}}C^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\lambda}_{1},\hat{\lambda}_{2}}C^{\hat{\lambda}_{4}}_{\hat{\lambda},\hat{\lambda}_{3}}=\delta^{\hat{\lambda}_{4}}_{\hat{\lambda}_{1}+\hat{\lambda}_{2}+\hat{\lambda}_{3}}\right)\,,

(4.3)

i.e. it is the color DDM-factor times a constraint on the helicities. Note that, given the external helicities, the helicity of all inner lines is fixed in HS-SDYM. At the
n
-point level we simply have the product of the DDM-color factor

\displaystyle L{}_{a_{1}a_{\sigma_{2}}...a_{\sigma_{n-1}}}{}^{a_{n}}\,\times\delta^{\hat{\lambda}_{n}}_{\hat{\lambda}_{1}+\hat{\lambda}_{\sigma_{2}}+...+\hat{\lambda}_{\sigma_{n-1}}}

(4.4)

and the Kronecker symbols that determine the helicity of the last leg. In the version of HS-SDYM restricted to
(++-)
-vertices, one needs to additionally require
\lambda_{1,...,n-1}>0
, i.e.
\hat{\lambda}_{1,...,n-1}\geq 0
. In what follows, we will use a more standard and compact notation for the DDM-ladders
\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)
instead of
L{}_{a_{1}a_{\sigma_{2}}...a_{\sigma_{n-1}}}{}^{a_{n}}\,
.

Light-cone gauge interlude.

To compute amplitudes in the light-cone gauge we have to introduce some notation for the momentum space cousins of the Poisson bracket. As in the position space, everything nontrivial is confined to the
2d
subspace. A generic
4
-momentum
p^{\mu}
is
(p,\bar{p},p^{+}\equiv\beta,p^{-})
. The Lorentz-invariance confines the transverse components
p
and
\bar{p}
to appear only in the form of

\displaystyle{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}=\bar{p}_{i}\beta_{j}-\bar{p}_{j}\beta_{i}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}=p_{i}\beta_{j}-p_{j}\beta_{i}\,.

(4.5)

where
i,j,...
refer to external legs. We can choose
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}
to be exactly the Fourier image of
\{\bullet,\bullet\}
in our coordinates. It is a product of two spinors, e.g.
\bar{i}=(\bar{p}_{i},-\beta_{i})
, etc., and
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}=(\bar{i}\bar{j})
. At the three-point level, thanks to momentum conservation, one finds
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}={\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}={\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}={\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{31}
. The kinematic part of the SDYM vertex in momentum space is simply
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}
. It transforms in the anti-symmetric representation of the permutation group
S_{3}
. More light-cone notation can be found in Appendix
A
. The most general vertex that leads to equations (
3.9
) has the following form in momentum space

\displaystyle\Phi^{{A}}\Phi^{{B}}\Phi^{{C}}h_{{A}{B}{C}}=\sum_{\lambda_{1,2,3}}C_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}\int\delta^{4}(\sum_{i}p_{i})\,f_{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}\Phi_{\lambda_{1},p_{1}}^{a_{1}}\Phi_{\lambda_{2},p_{2}}^{a_{2}}\Phi_{\lambda_{3},p_{3}}^{a_{3}}\,{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}^{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}\,.

(4.6)

Here, Latin indices
a_{1,2,3}
span the internal space, if present, at a given helicity. The internal space can depend on helicity, e.g. the
\lambda=+1
field can take values in the adjoint of some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, while fields of other helicities can take values in various representations of
\mathfrak{g}
. Strictly speaking,
f_{a_{\lambda_{1}},a_{\lambda_{2}},a_{\lambda_{3}}}
would be a more precise notation. We will avoid it as we will deal only with three cases: (i) all fields take values in the adjoint of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is equipped with a non-degenerate bilinear invariant form, in which case we can simply write
{\mathrm{Tr}\,}(\Phi_{\lambda_{1},p_{1}}\Phi_{\lambda_{2},p_{2}}\Phi_{\lambda_{3},p_{3}})
; (ii) all fields
\Phi_{\lambda}
take values in
\mathrm{Mat}_{N}
, where notation
\Phi^{a}T_{a}
can be appropriate; (iii) trivial internal space. It is convenient to think that
C_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}
is defined for all
\lambda_{1,2,3}
and, in particular, vanishes whenever
\sum\lambda_{i}\leq 0
. With these precautions, the structure constants of the kinematic algebra are read off from

\displaystyle\Phi_{{A}}\{\Phi^{{B}},\Phi^{{C}}\}\mathbf{f}{}^{{A}}{}_{{B}{C}}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1,2,3}}C_{-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3}}\int\delta^{4}(\sum_{i}p_{i})\,f_{a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}}\Phi_{-\lambda_{1},-p_{1}}^{a_{1}}\Phi_{\lambda_{2},p_{2}}^{a_{2}}\Phi_{\lambda_{3},p_{3}}^{a_{3}}\,{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}^{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}-1}\,{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}\,,

where the momentum conservation has
(-p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3})
, in fact, and
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}
is to factor out one Poisson bracket.

Without taking
[
52
]
into account, i.e. with the naive propagators and/or Minkowski kinematics, the four-point amplitude can be shown to be proportional to the Jacobi identity of the kinematic algebra
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{s}+\mathcal{A}_{t}+\mathcal{A}_{u}=\sum_{\omega}\mathcal{F}_{1234\omega}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}^{\lambda_{12}+\omega}\frac{1}{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}^{\lambda_{34}-\omega}+2\leftrightarrow 4+2\leftrightarrow 3

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}}{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}}\sum_{\omega}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{1234\omega}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}^{\lambda_{12}+\omega-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}^{\lambda_{34}-\omega-1}+\mathcal{F}_{2314\omega}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}^{\lambda_{23}+\omega-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{14}^{\lambda_{14}-\omega-1}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{F}_{3124\omega}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{31}^{\lambda_{13}+\omega-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{24}^{\lambda_{24}-\omega-1}\Big)\sim\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{2}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{3}A_{4}}+\mathbf{f}_{A_{2}A_{3}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{1}A_{4}}+\mathbf{f}_{A_{3}A_{1}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{2}A_{4}}=0\,,

where
\mathcal{F}_{1234\omega}\equiv C^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\omega}C^{-\omega,\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}}f_{a_{1}a_{2}c}f{}^{c}{}_{a_{3}a_{4}}\,
. In order to factor out the term
\sim{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}
we used the on-shell relations in Appendix
B
. The vanishing of the naive four-point amplitude for all self-dual theories is a consequence of the Jacobi identity for the kinematic algebra
[

.

A slightly more general result is a three-point BG-current, i.e. the four-point amplitude with one leg kept off-shell. Assuming the Jacobi identity for the kinematic algebra, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}}{s_{12}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{2}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{3}A_{4}}\,+\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{14}}{s_{23}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{2}A_{3}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{1}A_{4}}\,+\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{31}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{24}}{s_{31}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{3}A_{1}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{2}A_{4}}\,

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}}{s_{12}}-\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{14}}{s_{23}}\right)\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{2}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{3}A_{4}}+\left(\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{31}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{24}}{s_{31}}-\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{14}}{s_{23}}\right)\mathbf{f}_{A_{3}A_{1}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{2}A_{4}}\,

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{4}\beta_{i}\left(\frac{\beta_{3}}{4{\mathbb{P}}_{34}{\mathbb{P}}_{23}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{2}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{3}A_{4}}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{4{\mathbb{P}}_{24}{\mathbb{P}}_{23}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{3}A_{1}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{2}A_{4}}\,\right)p_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{34}{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{23}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{2}B}\mathbf{f}^{B}_{\;\;A_{3}A_{4}}+\frac{1}{{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{24}{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{23}}\mathbf{f}_{A_{3}A_{1}B}\mathbf{f}{}^{B}{}_{A_{2}A_{4}}\,\right)\beta_{1}p_{1}^{2}\,,

(4.8)

where we used the off-shell relations (
B.4
) and (
B.5
). The last line features two DDM-factors at the four-point level.

The general expression for a BG-current in SDYM is well-known
[

. The
n
-point partial amplitude for SDYM with one leg off-shell reads

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{SDYM}_{n}(1^{-}2^{+}\cdots n^{+})=\frac{(-)^{n}\beta_{3}\cdots\beta_{n-1}p_{1}^{2}}{2^{n-2}{\mathbb{P}}_{23}{\mathbb{P}}_{34}\cdots{\mathbb{P}}_{n-1,n}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{4}\beta_{i}=\frac{\beta_{1}p_{1}^{2}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{23}{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{34}\cdots{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{n-1,n}}\,.

(4.9)

This is the usual color-ordered partial amplitude/BG-current, which appears both in the standard color-ordered decomposition and in the DDM-ordered one.

If a given SD-theory can be thought of as coming from an associative algebra, i.e. the kinematic algebra is the commutator Lie algebra, the standard color-ordered decomposition can be more handy. The
n
-point amplitude then reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}A_{n}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n))\,\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n))\,{\mathrm{Tr}\,}(T_{a_{1}}T_{a_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots T_{a_{\sigma(n)}})\,,\end{split}

(4.10)

where we defined

A_{n}(1,2,...,n)=\sum_{\omega_{i}}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}^{\lambda_{12}+\omega_{1}-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<3}^{\lambda_{3}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}-1}\cdots{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}^{\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n-3}-1}\,.

(4.11)

Due to the nature of SD-theories, the sum over
\omega_{i}
runs over all integer values such that the
{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}
always appear with nonnegative powers. We have also introduced the following shorthand notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}}\equiv C^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\omega_{1}}C^{-\omega_{1},\lambda_{3},\omega_{2}}\cdots C^{-\omega_{n-3},\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}}\,,

\displaystyle{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<j}=\sum_{i<j}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\,.

(4.12)

Otherwise, if the kinematic algebra is a genuine Lie algebra, rather than the commutator Lie algebra derived from an associative one, we use the DDM-decomposition
[
55
]
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}\left(\mathbf{f}_{A_{1}A_{\sigma(2)}B_{1}}\mathbf{f}{}^{B_{1}}{}_{A_{\sigma(3)}B_{2}}\,\cdots\mathbf{f}{}^{B_{n-3}}{}_{A_{\sigma(n-1)}A_{n}}\,\right)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n-1),n)\\
&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma,n)\,,\end{split}

(4.13)

where the sum runs over
(n-2)!
permutations of the color factors
\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)
. The relation between this basis and the trace decomposition is explicit by the Kleiss–Kuijf relations. In the decompositions above
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{SDYM}
corresponds to the
n
-point partial amplitude for SDYM, i.e. the sum over all color-ordered planar diagrams; this is a gauge invariant quantity. On the other hand, the kinematic algebra factor
\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)
may not be gauge invariant. The total amplitude, summing over all permutations, will then be a gauge invariant object.

In the DDM-decomposition (
4.13
) we can also subdivide
\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)
by isolating the contributions coming from the structure constant
f_{abc}
and the kinematic ones:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}A_{n}(1,\sigma,n)\left(f_{a_{1}a_{\sigma(2)}b_{1}}\cdots f{}^{b_{n-3}}{}_{a_{\sigma(n-1)}a_{n}}\,\right)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{SDYM}_{n}(1,\sigma,n)

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}A_{n}(1,\sigma,n)c(1,\sigma,n)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{SDYM}_{n}(1,\sigma,n)\,,

(4.15)

where we defined
\mathbf{c}(1,\sigma,n)=A_{n}(1,\sigma,n)c(1,\sigma,n)
and

c(1,\sigma,n)=f_{a_{1}a_{\sigma(2)}b_{1}}f{}^{b_{1}}{}_{a_{\sigma(3)}b_{2}}\,\cdots f{}^{b_{n-3}}{}_{a_{\sigma(n-1)}a_{n}}\,\,,

(4.16)

is the DDM-ladder built of color. If the color space is trivial, we have contributions only from kinematics.

Coming back to SDYM and HS-SDYM, we can write down the
n
-point amplitude for SDYM in two ways:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}g^{n-2}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n))\mathrm{Tr}(T_{a_{1}}T_{a_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots T_{a_{\sigma(n)}})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}g^{n-2}c(1,\sigma,n)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma,n)\,.

(4.18)

For HS-SDYM and all other one-derivative theories
[

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n))\mathrm{Tr}(T_{a_{1}}T_{a_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots T_{a_{\sigma(n)}})

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-2}}\mathcal{C}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}}c(1,\sigma,n)\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma,n)\,,

(4.20)

Example: Chiral HiSGRA.

Let us reproduce the result of
[
22
]
, where all tree-level amplitudes and BG-currents of Chiral HiSGRA have been computed. In this case, we should use the trace decomposition (
4.10
). A specific example is
U(n)
Chiral HiSGRA, where all cubic vertices, both even- and odd-derivative, are activated with couplings fixed to be the magic (
3.3
).
In this case, we have

A_{n}(1,2,...,n)=(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\sum_{\omega_{i}}\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}^{\lambda_{12}+\omega_{1}-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<3}^{\lambda_{3}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}-1}\cdots{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<n-2}^{\lambda_{n-2}+\omega_{n-3}-\omega_{n-4}-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}^{\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n-3}-1}}{\Gamma(\lambda_{12}+\omega_{1})\cdots\Gamma(\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n-3})}\,.

(4.21)

By an appropriate change of variables dictated by

\lambda_{12}+\omega_{1}-1=k_{1}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{3}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}-1=k_{2}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\cdots\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{n-1}+\lambda_{n}-\omega_{n-3}-1=k_{n-2}\,,

(4.22)

and noting that we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}k_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}-(n-2)\,.

(4.23)

Using the multinomial formula, we find

A_{n}(1,2,...,n)=\frac{(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\alpha_{n}^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}}{(\Lambda_{n}-(n-2))!}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\Lambda_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{n}\equiv\sum_{i<j}^{n-2}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}+{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}\,.

(4.24)

The total amplitude then becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\text{tree}}&=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n-1}}\frac{(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\alpha_{n,\sigma}^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}}{(\Lambda_{n}-(n-2))!}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}^{SDYM}(1,\sigma(2),\cdots,\sigma(n))\mathrm{Tr}(T_{a_{1}}T_{a_{\sigma(2)}}\cdots T_{a_{\sigma(n)}})\,,\end{split}

(4.25)

where
\alpha_{n,\sigma}
is the appropriate color-ordered version of
\alpha_{n}
. This coincides with the result presented in
[
22
]
.

For chiral higher-spin without the
U(n)
gauging, one allowed theory consists of having all couplings fixed to the value (
3.3
), but only for even derivative vertices. All odd derivative vertices must be set to zero. Therefore, following a similar procedure as for the
U(n)
case, we get

A_{n}(1,2,...,n)=(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\sum_{k_{i}\,\text{even}}\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}^{k_{1}}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<3}^{k_{2}}\cdots{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}^{k_{n-2}}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n-2}!}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}k_{i}=\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)\,.

(4.26)

This can be summed into

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{n}&=\frac{(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}}{2^{n-2}(\Lambda_{n}-(n-2))!}\sum_{\epsilon_{2}=\pm 1}\cdots\sum_{\epsilon_{n-1}=\pm 1}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}\left(\epsilon_{j}\sum_{i<j}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\right)+\epsilon_{n-1}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}\right)^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\\
&=\frac{(l_{p})^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}}{2^{n-3}(\Lambda_{n}-(n-2))!}\sum_{\epsilon_{2}=\pm 1}\cdots\sum_{\epsilon_{n-2}=\pm 1}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{n-2}\left(\epsilon_{j}\sum_{i<j}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\right)+{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}\right)^{\Lambda_{n}-(n-2)}\,,\end{split}

(4.27)

where the sum over the various
\epsilon_{i}
is what projects the sum into even powers only.

Example: (HS)-SDGR.

SDGR is a very instructive example, see e.g.
[
90
,
91
]
. Since SDGR does not have any associative algebra behind it, we have to resort to the DDM-decomposition. For example, for SDGR at the four-point level, there are the usual
s
,
t
, and
u
diagrams. The DDM color factors have the form
f^{x_{1}}_{a_{1}a_{\sigma_{2}}}f^{a_{4}}_{x_{1}a_{\sigma_{3}}}
, i.e. there are only two. The
u
-diagram can be massaged via the Jacobi identity to have the same color factors as the first two, see below. For higher-point amplitudes, the DDM-ladder contains only kinematics and is

\displaystyle A_{n}(1,2,...,n)

\displaystyle=(l_{p})^{n-2}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<3}\cdots{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{<n-2}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}=(l_{p})^{n-2}\prod_{j=2}^{n-2}\left(\sum_{i<j}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\right){\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}\,.

(4.28)

For HS-SDGR and numerous other theories
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
we find simply

\displaystyle A_{n}(1,2,...,n)=\mathcal{C}^{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}}\prod_{j=2}^{n-2}\left(\sum_{i<j}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\right){\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{n-1,n}\,.

(4.29)

4.1
Half-collinear amplitudes

The main point of the discussion here-above is that amplitudes and BG-currents of SD-theories can be reduced to those of SDYM times products of the structure constants of a given kinematic algebra. It is important that the kinematic factors are non-singular. Single-minus gluon tree amplitudes have been shown to be nonzero in the half collinear limit
[
52
]
, see also
[
92

93
]
.

Below, we give some examples of non-vanishing amplitudes in the light-cone gauge and match the results of
[
52

53
]
. We consider mostly SDYM, as for all other self-dual theories the amplitudes can be recovered via either the trace or the DDM decomposition. However, an example of SDGR can be instructive for the reader. We collect useful formulas for the various computations in Appendix
B

(4.30)

5
Conclusions & Discussion

In this paper we attempted to uniformize SD-theories at least in the light-cone gauge. The key input is the statement that behind any SD-theory there is a kinematic Lie algebra,
[
44
,
2
,
42
]
. While this is a very deep statement, it may not be obvious how to apply it to concrete problems in field theory. At least for tree-level diagrams the DDM-decomposition reduces amplitudes in any SD-theory to the sum over partial SDYM amplitudes times the DDM “color” ladders built now with structure constants of a kinematic algebra. Basically, the DDM-ordering allows us to formulate a “double copy” procedure that is different from the well-known KLT
[
94
]
, BCJ
[
75
]
or CHY
[
95
]
, but is conceptually close to BCJ. The main difference is that a given kinematic algebra may not define a theory on its own, it merely leads to a SD-theory when used together with the kinematic vertex of SDYM.
27
27

27

There are many other SD-theories that can be obtained as double- and multi-copy of the basic theories discussed in the present paper
[
5
]
. Our conclusions should apply to those theories as well.

An immediate consequence is that all SD-theories have nonvanishing amplitudes (in complex kinematics on in the split signature) thanks to
[
52
]
, see also
[
53
,
96
]
. In particular, since most of SD-theories contain higher-spin fields, there is a large class of higher-spin theories with nonvanishing amplitudes in flat space, including the maximal SD-theory — Chiral HiSGRA. Another interesting consequence is that amplitudes in all SD-theories reduce to those of SDYM, which may sound counterintuitive for non-colored theories like SDGR. Lastly, the DDM-decomposition is well-established at one-loop and, hence, our main conclusions can be applied to one-loop amplitudes as well, which, for example, explains why one-loop amplitudes of Chiral HiSGRA
[
24
]
reduce to those of SDYM.

Let us note that many “purely” higher-spin results have immediate low-spin consequences. As it was already noted, tree-level amplitudes of SDYM and SDGR are subsets of those of the Chiral HiSGRA (we consider the maximal SD-theory, for simplicity). The same is true for the one-loop amplitudes of SDYM and SDGR. For example, the one-loop amplitude of the
U(N)
-gauged Chiral HiSGRA has the form of the all-helicity-plus one-loop amplitude of SDYM times a certain kinematic factor
[
24
]
.

Since Chiral HiSGRA has extended symmetries, which are visible in a covariant formulation constructed in
[
97
,
98
]
,
28
28

28

In the manifestly gauge-invariant and Lorentz-covariant formulation there is an infinite-dimensional algebra of gauge symmetries, which is
A_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}[y^{A}]
, i.e. it is a tensor product of the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
and a commutative algebra. The Lie algebra of symmetries is the commutator Lie algebra of this associative algebra. Upon reduction to the light-cone gauge, the gauge symmetry algebra gets converted to the kinematic algebra.
one can envisage a few consequences for low-spin theories. Invariants of the higher-spin algebra are the lowest approximation to higher-spin invariant observables.
29
29

29

The idea of higher-spin invariant observables goes back to
[
99
]
. It was in
[
100
,
101
]
that the invariants of a particular higher-spin algebra were computed and gave all
n
-point functions of higher-spin currents in free vector models, see also
[
102
]
.
In the AdS-setting the simplest such observables have the form
\mathcal{O}_{n}=\mathrm{Tr}[A_{1}\star A_{2}\star...\star A_{n}]
with
\star
being the product in the (associative) higher-spin algebra of interest.

It was shown in
[
101
]
that
\mathcal{O}_{n}
computes
n
-point correlators of conserved higher-spin currents in free vector models.
30
30

30

We should stress that one needs to “know” what to compute the invariants on, i.e. what to plug into the traces. The “wave-functions” used in
[
101
]
were adapted to the free vector model/HS duality
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
.
Chiral HiSGRA is smooth in the cosmological constant, and there is a direct analog of
\mathcal{O}_{n}
in flat space, which was introduced in
[
26
]
. For the flat space higher-spin algebra,
\mathcal{O}_{n}
are still nontrivial, of distributional nature and display collinear features. The missing piece of the puzzle has been that it was not clear how the nontriviality of the higher-spin invariants is consistent with vanishing amplitudes in Chiral HiSGRA. This has been just resolved for
(+...+-)
amplitudes in Yang-Mills theory
[
53
]
and, as we show, has immediate consequences for all SD-theories, including those with higher-spin fields.

The same invariants
\mathcal{O}_{n}
are equal to the correlators of the “flat space singleton”
[
60
,
61
]
, which is an analog of the free vector model but for the flat holography. This is not too surprising since the amplitudes and the correlators are fixed by the same infinite-dimensional algebra. Now, the amplitudes of Chiral HiSGRA are shown to be nontrivial. What needs to be done is to demonstrate that there is a scheme where the amplitudes of Chiral HiSGRA are given by the same invariants. It may not be straightforward since the invariants need to be computed on concrete “wave-functions” that encode the nature of the Green’s functions being used to compute amplitudes. An encouraging observation
[
26
]
is that at the three-point level, the amplitudes of Chiral HiSGRA do have collinear features.

Therefore, within celestial holography, see e.g.
[
103
]
for a review, there should exist a direct analog of the higher-spin/vector-model AdS/CFT duality
[
57
,
58
,
59
]
that is between the flat space singleton and Chiral HiSGRA as was proposed by Ponomarev in
[
60
,
61
,
26
]
. This duality is not obstructed by the nonlocality contrary to the duals of vector models
[
27
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
. A version of the vector-model/HS AdS/CFT duality that is free of the nonlocality problem is between Chiral HiSGRA and a subsector of Chern–Simons vector-models
[
46
,
47
,
48
]
. It would also be interesting to find out to what extent the celestial duality is a limit of the AdS/CFT one.

One low-spin consequence of higher-spin symmetries is that amplitudes in SDYM and SDGR can also be computed via the higher-spin invariant observables
\mathcal{O}_{n}
and just correspond to the lowest orders in the spin expansion. Note, however, that SDYM/SDGR amplitudes are not themselves higher-spin invariant, rather they are particular components of a higher-spin invariant quantity. This has to be true both in flat and (A)dS spaces. It is known that the leading energy-pole of AdS/CFT correlators is equal to the flat space amplitude in the same theory
[
104
]
. Now that such amplitudes are nontrivial in SD-theories, it would be interesting to investigate the CFT consequences.

Following
[
2
]
, one can conclude that all SD-theories are equivalent to the principal chiral model with the kinematic algebra being the underlying symmetry. In particular, all SD-theories are integrable. In the covariant formulation of Chiral HiSGRA
[
98
]
, which extends it to nonvanishing cosmological constant, the
2d
-origin is also visible. Therefore, a stringy reformulation of Chiral HiSGRA is plausible.

Lastly, the nontriviality of collinear amplitudes can give more room for higher-spin theories to have interesting
S
-matrices. One can also imagine a parity-invariant completion of Chiral HiSGRA where particles can scatter both via holomorphic and anti-holomorphic collinear processes.

Light-cone

In the light-cone Hamiltonian approach for massless higher-spin fields, we have

p_{AA^{\prime}}=p_{\mu}(\sigma^{\mu})_{AA^{\prime}}=\sqrt{2}\begin{pmatrix}p^{-}&\bar{p}\\
p&-\beta\end{pmatrix}\approx\sqrt{2}\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{p\bar{p}}{\beta}&\bar{p}\\
p&-\beta\end{pmatrix}=\lambda_{A}\bar{\lambda}_{A^{\prime}}\,.

(A.1)

Notice that for complex momenta
p^{\mu}
the two spinors
(\lambda_{A},\bar{\lambda}_{A^{\prime}})
are independent two-dimensional complex vectors. In Minkowski space and for real momenta
p_{AA^{\prime}}
is hermitian, and the two spinors become complex conjugate
\bar{\lambda}_{A^{\prime}}=\pm(\lambda_{A})^{*}
(where the sign depends on whether the energy is taken to be positive or negative, then on the convention we use on the background flat metric).
Our spinor-helicity convention for massless particles is
[
105
]

\displaystyle i\equiv\lambda_{i}=\sqrt{2}(z_{i},1)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{i}\equiv\bar{\lambda}_{i}=\omega_{i}(\bar{z}_{i},1)\,,

\displaystyle(ij)=\epsilon^{AB}i_{A}j_{B}\,,

\displaystyle(\bar{i}\bar{j})=\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\,\bar{i}_{A^{\prime}}\bar{j}_{B^{\prime}}\,,

(A.2)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon^{AB}=\epsilon_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(A.3)

By choosing the spinors that diagonalize the momenta and respect (
A.1
), we get

\displaystyle\omega_{i}=-\beta_{i}\,,

\displaystyle z_{i}=-\frac{p_{i}}{\beta_{i}}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{z}_{i}=-\frac{\bar{p}_{i}}{\beta_{i}}\,.

(A.4)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(\bar{i}\bar{j})={\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle(ij)\equiv{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{ij}=-\frac{2}{\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}\,,

\displaystyle{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}=\bar{p}_{i}\beta_{j}-\bar{p}_{j}\beta_{i}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}=p_{i}\beta_{j}-p_{j}\beta_{i}\,.

(A.5)

Spinor-helicity variables
(\lambda_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})
are defined up to little group scaling
(\lambda_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})\sim(t_{i}\lambda_{i},t_{i}^{-1}\bar{\lambda}_{i})
for
t_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Appendix B
Useful relations

Some on-shell relations for the
n
-point scattering are

\displaystyle(ij)(\bar{i}\bar{j})=-\frac{2}{\beta_{i}\beta_{j}}{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ij}=2\,p_{i}\cdot p_{j}=(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}\,,

(B.1)

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{j=1}p^{\mu}_{j}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\Rightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\sum^{n}_{j=1}(ij)(\bar{j}\bar{k})=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{jk}}{\beta_{j}}=0\,.

(B.2)

In particular, for the four-point scattering, we find

\displaystyle\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{12}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{34}}{(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}}=\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{31}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{24}}{(p_{1}+p_{3})^{2}}=\frac{{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{14}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{23}}{(p_{1}+p_{4})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle s_{ij}=-(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}\,,

(B.3)

where
s=s_{12}
,
t=s_{14}
,
u=s_{13}
are the standard Mandelstam variables for massless four-point scattering. Off-shell, we have the following relations for the
n
-point scattering:

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}_{ij}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{jk}}{\beta_{j}}=-\frac{\beta_{i}\beta_{k}}{2}\sum_{j}\frac{p_{j}^{2}}{\beta_{j}}\,,

(B.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{ik}{\bar{\mathbb{P}}}_{ik}=-\frac{\beta_{i}\beta_{k}}{2}s_{ik}+\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}(\beta_{i}+\beta_{k})p_{k}^{2}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp_{i}^{2}=0\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp_{k}^{2}\neq 0\,.

(B.5)

In two dimensions, for
(\mu r)\neq 0
, we can always decompose any two-component vector as

\lambda_{i}=\frac{(\mu i)}{(\mu r)}\lambda_{r}+\frac{(ir)}{(\mu r)}\lambda_{\mu}\,.

(B.6)

When two spinors are collinear (i.e.
\lambda_{i}\parallel\lambda_{j}
) we have

\lambda_{i}=\frac{(ri)}{(rj)}\lambda_{j}\,.

(B.7)

The momentum conservation can be massaged as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\delta^{4}\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)=\delta^{4}\left(\frac{1}{(\mu r)}\sum_{i}((i\mu)\lambda_{r}+(ri)\lambda_{\mu})\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)=(\mu r)^{4}\delta^{4}\left(\sum_{i}((i\mu)\lambda_{r}+(ri)\lambda_{\mu})\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)\\
&=(\mu r)^{2}\delta^{2}\left(\sum_{i}(i\mu)\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)\delta^{2}\left(\sum_{i}(ri)\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)\equiv({\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{\mu r})^{2}\delta^{2}\left(\sum_{i}{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{i\mu}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)\delta^{2}\left(\sum_{i}{\mathbb{P}}^{\beta}_{ri}\bar{\lambda}_{i}\right)\,,\end{split}

(B.8)

where the first equality follows from (
B.6
), assuming
(\mu r)\neq 0
, and the last equality results from a change of variables.
