Introduction

The reconstruction of tree-like evolutionary histories from discrete data is a basic problem in phylogenetics. Classical reconstruction theory studies when a collection of distances, splits, quartets, or characters determines a unique tree. Buneman’s split-equivalence viewpoint and rigid-circuit characterization, Gavril’s chordal-subtree theorem, and Steel’s work on qualitative characters form part of the graph-theoretic background for perfect phylogeny and compatibility
[
3
,
12
,
13
,
17
]
. Later developments include partial partitions and tree definition, chordal graph embeddings, and algorithmic extensions of the chordal approach to multi-state perfect phylogeny
[
11
,
16
,
14
,
15
]
. General background on phylogenetic trees and characters can be found in the monograph of Semple and Steel
[
10
]
. In the character-based setting, a character on a taxon set
X
is a partition of
X
into states. A character is convex, or homoplasy-free, on a phylogenetic tree if each state induces a connected subtree and the state subtrees are pairwise disjoint. A collection of characters defines a tree when the tree is the unique phylogenetic tree on which all characters in the collection are convex. This notion is closely related to perfect phylogeny, compatibility, quartet decisiveness, and the tree-identification problem studied by Bordewich–Huber–Semple and Huber–Moulton–Steel
[
1
,
7
]
.

Throughout the paper, a phylogenetic
X
-tree is a tree whose leaf set is the finite set
X
and whose internal vertices have degree at least three. Binary means that every internal vertex has degree three. An
r
-state character is a character using at most
r
states. Semple and Steel
[
9
]
proved the following fundamental lower bound: every collection of
r
-state characters defining a binary phylogenetic tree with
n
leaves has size at least

\left\lceil\frac{n-3}{r-1}\right\rceil.

(1)

The bound is sharp in a qualitative eventual sense. Bordewich and Semple
[
2
]
proved that, for each fixed
r
, every sufficiently large binary phylogenetic tree with
n
leaves is defined by exactly the number of
r
-state characters in (
1
). Thus the natural quantitative problem is to ask how large “sufficiently large” must be as a function of
r
. For small state bounds, exact threshold information is known only in special cases: Bordewich and Semple proved
n_{4}=13
, and a recent preprint of Long and Wang reports
n_{3}=8
and
n_{5}=16

[
2
,
8
]
.

The small-state results suggest that the threshold may in fact be linear with the best possible leading constant. The conjectural value for
r\geq 4
is
3r+1
, and one expects the optimal character sets to be obtainable explicitly. This agrees with
n_{4}=13
and with the reported value
n_{5}=16
; the case
r=3
is also linear and has an explicit small-state classification
[
2
,
8
]
.

For
r\geq 2
and
n\geq 3
, define

d_{r}(n)=\max_{\mathcal{T}:\ |X|=n}\min\{|\mathcal{C}|:\mathcal{C}\text{ is a collection of }r\text{-state characters defining }\mathcal{T}\},

where the maximum ranges over all binary phylogenetic
X
-trees with
|X|=n
. The eventual-sharpness theorem of Bordewich and Semple implies that the threshold

n_{r}=\min\left\{N:\ d_{r}(n)=\left\lceil\frac{n-3}{r-1}\right\rceil\text{ for every }n\geq N\right\}

is well-defined. Very little has been known about the growth of
n_{r}
with
r
beyond these small cases and the qualitative finiteness theorem.

The Bordewich–Semple proof is constructive, and one can extract from their long-path reduction an explicit polynomial threshold of order
O(r^{5})
. Thus, before the present work, the known quantitative control on
n_{r}
was polynomial but still far from the linear lower-bound scale. The purpose of this paper is to replace that long-path bookkeeping by a direct certificate-packing method and obtain a near-linear bound.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

For every integer
r\geq 4
,

3r+1\leq n_{r}\leq\left\lceil 64(r-1)\log_{2}(r+1)\right\rceil+3.

In particular,
n_{r}=O(r\log r)
.

The lower bound is the snowflake obstruction. Huber, Linz, Moulton, and Semple
[
6
]
recently characterized the binary phylogenetic trees defined by at most three characters; equivalently, the only obstruction is an internal snowflake. Embedding this obstruction gives
n_{r}\geq 3r+1
for all
r\geq 4
. The upper bound is the main contribution of the paper. It is obtained by turning a linked system of quartets into an equitable packing problem on a conflict graph. The new ingredient is a short linked quartet-certificate construction with logarithmic conflict degree.

The proof has three main ingredients.

(i)

A linked edge-quartet certificate defines a binary tree once it contains one quartet distinguishing each internal edge. This uses the linked-system framework developed from the four-character construction of Huber, Moulton, and Steel
[
7
]
and subsequent efficient quartet-representation work such as
[
4
]
.

(ii)

If the conflict graph of such a certificate has maximum degree at most
\Delta
, then the certificate can be packed into exactly
\lceil m/(r-1)\rceil

r
-state characters whenever
\lceil m/(r-1)\rceil\geq\Delta+1
, where
m=n-3
is the number of internal edges. The packing step is an application of the Hajnal–Szemerédi equitable coloring theorem
[
5
]
.

(iii)

Every binary phylogenetic tree with
m
internal edges has a linked edge-quartet certificate with conflict degree at most
16\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\rceil+4
. We construct this certificate by choosing a short rotational transition system on the internal tree.

The explicit constant is obtained from the following elementary domination estimate:

m\geq\left\lceil 64(r-1)\log_{2}(r+1)\right\rceil\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\lceil\frac{m}{r-1}\right\rceil\geq 16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
fixes notation and recalls quartet certificates. Section
3
proves the conflict-graph packing criterion. Section
4
constructs the logarithmic linked certificate. Section
5
proves the explicit
O(r\log r)
upper bound. Section
6
proves the universal lower bound
n_{r}\geq 3r+1
. Section
7
discusses the remaining linear-threshold problem.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a binary phylogenetic
X
-tree. We write
E^{0}(\mathcal{T})
for the set of internal edges, i.e., edges whose two endpoints are internal vertices. If
|X|=n
, then

|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|=n-3.

We write

m=|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|.

The internal tree of
\mathcal{T}
is the tree
H(\mathcal{T})
whose vertex set is the set of internal vertices of
\mathcal{T}
and whose edge set is
E^{0}(\mathcal{T})
. Thus
|E(H(\mathcal{T}))|=m
and
|V(H(\mathcal{T}))|=m+1
.

If
B\subseteq E^{0}(\mathcal{T})
, the character displayed by
B
, denoted
\chi_{B}
, is the character whose state classes are the sets of leaves contained in the connected components of
\mathcal{T}-B
. It has at most
|B|+1
states.

A quartet
ab\mid cd
is displayed by
\mathcal{T}
if the path from
a
to
b
is edge-disjoint from the path from
c
to
d
. Equivalently, the minimal subtree on
\{a,b,c,d\}
has the split
ab\mid cd
. An internal edge
e
is distinguished by
ab\mid cd
if
e
lies on the path joining the two cherries of the induced quartet, i.e., if deleting
e
separates
\{a,b\}
from
\{c,d\}
.

We shall use the following standard form of the linked-system criterion for quartets; see, for example,
[
7
,
4
]
.

Definition 2
.

A set

\mathcal{Q}=\{q_{e}:e\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T})\}

is a linked edge-quartet certificate for
\mathcal{T}
if each
q_{e}
is displayed by
\mathcal{T}
and distinguishes
e
, and if adjacent edges in the internal tree have linked witnesses in the following sense: whenever
e
and
f
are adjacent in
H(\mathcal{T})
, the quartets
q_{e}
and
q_{f}
share three labels and distinguish
e
and
f
, respectively, in the corresponding five-leaf subtree.

Theorem 3
.

If
\mathcal{Q}
is a linked edge-quartet certificate for a binary phylogenetic tree
\mathcal{T}
, then
\mathcal{Q}
defines
\mathcal{T}
; equivalently, every binary phylogenetic
X
-tree displaying all quartets in
\mathcal{Q}
is equal to
\mathcal{T}
.

For a quartet
q_{e}=a_{e}b_{e}\mid c_{e}d_{e}
, define its same-side support

S(e)=E^{0}\bigl(P_{\mathcal{T}}(a_{e},b_{e})\cup P_{\mathcal{T}}(c_{e},d_{e})\bigr),

where
P_{\mathcal{T}}(x,y)
is the path from
x
to
y
in
\mathcal{T}
. Thus
S(e)
contains the internal edges on the two same-side paths of the quartet. The edge
e
itself is not in
S(e)
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{q_{e}:e\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T})\}
be a linked edge-quartet certificate. The conflict graph
\Gamma(\mathcal{Q})
has vertex set
E^{0}(\mathcal{T})
, with distinct vertices
e
and
f
adjacent when either
f\in S(e)
or
e\in S(f)
. Define

\sigma(\mathcal{Q})=\max_{e\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T})}|S(e)|

and

\lambda(\mathcal{Q})=\max_{f\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T})}|\{e\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T}):f\in S(e)\}|.

The routing width of the certificate is

\rho(\mathcal{Q})=\sigma(\mathcal{Q})+\lambda(\mathcal{Q}).

Then
\Delta(\Gamma(\mathcal{Q}))\leq\rho(\mathcal{Q})
.

3
Packing certificates into characters

We first record the packing step that turns a low-conflict quartet certificate into characters.

Theorem 5
.

Let
r\geq 2
, put
q=r-1
, and let
\mathcal{T}
be a binary phylogenetic tree with
m=|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|
. Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
has a linked edge-quartet certificate
\mathcal{Q}
with routing width
\rho(\mathcal{Q})
. If

k=\left\lceil\frac{m}{q}\right\rceil\geq\rho(\mathcal{Q})+1,

then
\mathcal{T}
is defined by exactly
k

r
-state characters.

Proof.

Since
\Delta(\Gamma(\mathcal{Q}))\leq\rho(\mathcal{Q})
, the Hajnal–Szemerédi equitable coloring theorem
[
5
]
gives an equitable proper
k
-coloring of
\Gamma(\mathcal{Q})
. Let

E^{0}(\mathcal{T})=B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k}

be the corresponding color classes. Equitability and
k=\lceil m/q\rceil
imply

|B_{i}|\leq\left\lceil\frac{m}{k}\right\rceil\leq q

for every
i
.

For each
i
, take the character
\chi_{B_{i}}
displayed by deleting the edges of
B_{i}
. Since
|B_{i}|\leq q
, this character has at most
q+1=r
states. Now let
e\in B_{i}
. Because
B_{i}
is an independent set of the conflict graph, no edge of
B_{i}-\{e\}
lies in
S(e)
. Hence the two same-side paths of
q_{e}
survive in the corresponding components of
\mathcal{T}-B_{i}
, while
e
separates the two sides. Thus

q_{e}\in Q(\chi_{B_{i}}).

Consequently all quartets in the linked certificate
\mathcal{Q}
are displayed by the characters
\chi_{B_{1}},\ldots,\chi_{B_{k}}
. By Theorem
3
, these characters define
\mathcal{T}
.

The Semple–Steel lower bound (
1
) says that no fewer than
\lceil m/q\rceil=k
characters can define
\mathcal{T}
. Hence the construction is optimal.
∎

4
A logarithmic linked quartet certificate

We now construct the certificate used in the upper bound. Let
H=H(\mathcal{T})
be the internal tree of
\mathcal{T}
. A directed internal branch is an oriented edge
b=(u,v)
of
H
. If
v
has degree less than three in
H
, then the corresponding internal vertex of
\mathcal{T}
is incident with at least one pendant labelled leaf; such a leaf will be used as a terminal leaf for directed paths entering
v
.

At each degree-three vertex of
H
, choose one of the two cyclic orders of its three incident internal edges. This choice defines a transition rule: if a directed branch enters a degree-three vertex along one internal edge, it leaves along the successor edge in the chosen cyclic order. At vertices of degree less than three, the transition stops at a pendant labelled leaf.

Lemma 6
.

Let
H
be a finite tree with maximum degree at most three, with
N=|V(H)|
. There is a choice of cyclic orders at the degree-three vertices such that every transition path has length at most

L=2\left\lceil\log_{2}(N+1)\right\rceil

internal edges.

Proof.

Choose independently and uniformly one of the two cyclic orders at every degree-three vertex. Fix a directed simple path of length
\ell
in
H
. For the transition system to follow this path, the cyclic order at each of the first
\ell-1
internal vertices of the path is prescribed. Hence the probability that this particular path is followed is at most
2^{-(\ell-1)}
.

The number of directed simple paths in an
N
-vertex tree is at most
N^{2}
. Taking

\ell=2\left\lceil\log_{2}(N+1)\right\rceil+1

gives

N^{2}\,2^{-(\ell-1)}<1.

Therefore, with positive probability, no directed transition path has length
\ell
or more. Thus some choice of cyclic orders has all transition paths of length at most
\ell-1=2\lceil\log_{2}(N+1)\rceil
.
∎

Fix such a transition system. For every directed internal branch
b=(u,v)
, let
g(b)
be the labelled leaf reached by following the transition path starting with
b
. Pendant branches are assigned their incident labelled leaf.

For each internal edge
e=uv
of
\mathcal{T}
, let
b_{1},b_{2}
be the two branches at
u
different from
e
, and let
b_{3},b_{4}
be the two branches at
v
different from
e
, allowing pendant branches. Define

q_{e}=g(b_{1})g(b_{2})\mid g(b_{3})g(b_{4}).

The quartet
q_{e}
is displayed by
\mathcal{T}
and distinguishes
e
.

Lemma 7
.

The set
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr}=\{q_{e}:e\in E^{0}(\mathcal{T})\}
is a linked edge-quartet certificate for
\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

Each quartet
q_{e}
uses two leaves from one side of
e
and two leaves from the other side, so it is displayed by
\mathcal{T}
and distinguishes
e
. It remains to check linkedness.

Let
e=uv
and
f=vw
be adjacent internal edges, and let
h
be the third branch at the common vertex
v
. The quartet
q_{e}
uses the guide leaves
g(v,w)
and
g(h)
on the
v
-side of
e
, while
q_{f}
uses
g(v,u)
and
g(h)
on the
v
-side of
f
. Moreover, by the transition rule, the guide leaf
g(v,w)
is one of the two guide leaves used by
q_{f}
on the
w
-side, unless the transition stops immediately at a pendant leaf at
w
, in which case the same pendant leaf is also used by
q_{f}
. Similarly,
g(v,u)
is one of the two guide leaves used by
q_{e}
on the
u
-side. Thus
q_{e}
and
q_{f}
share the three guide leaves
g(h)
,
g(v,w)
, and
g(v,u)
, and they distinguish the adjacent edges
e
and
f
in the induced five-leaf subtree. Hence the system is linked.
∎

Lemma 8
.

Let
m=|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|
. The certificate
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr}
can be chosen so that

\rho(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})\leq 16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+4.

Proof.

The internal tree
H
has
m+1
vertices. By Lemma
6
, every guide path has length at most

L=2\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil.

For a fixed edge
e
, the two same-side paths of
q_{e}
are formed from four guide paths, two at each endpoint. Thus

|S(e)|\leq 4L,

and so
\sigma(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})\leq 4L
.

We next bound the load. In a rotational transition system, every directed internal branch has at most one predecessor under the transition map. Therefore, for each orientation of a fixed internal edge
f
, at most
L
directed branches have guide paths containing that oriented edge. Taking both orientations gives at most
2L
such directed branches. Each directed branch can appear as a side branch for at most two internal edges, so the edge
f
lies in the same-side support of at most
4L+4
quartets, where the additive constant accounts for the two endpoints of
f
and pendant stopping cases. Hence

\lambda(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})\leq 4L+4.

Thus

\rho(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})=\sigma(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})+\lambda(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})\leq 8L+4=16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+4.

∎

5
The upper bound

It remains to compare the logarithmic conflict bound with
m/(r-1)
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
q\geq 3
and put

M_{q}=\left\lceil 64q\log_{2}(q+2)\right\rceil.

Then, for every integer
m\geq M_{q}
,

\left\lceil\frac{m}{q}\right\rceil\geq 16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5.

Proof.

Set
x=\log_{2}(q+2)
. First assume
q\geq 5
, so
x\geq\log_{2}7
. We claim that

64x>16\log_{2}\bigl(64x(2^{x}-2)+3\bigr)+20.

(2)

Let

H(x)=64x-16\log_{2}\bigl(64x(2^{x}-2)+3\bigr)-20.

For
x\geq\log_{2}7
, differentiating and bounding the logarithmic derivative gives

\frac{64(2^{x}-2)+64x2^{x}\ln 2}{64x(2^{x}-2)+3}\leq\frac{1+x\ln 2}{x(1-2^{1-x})}<2.

Hence
H^{\prime}(x)>64-32/\ln 2>0
in this range. A direct evaluation gives
H(\log_{2}7)>0
, proving (
2
).

Since

M_{q}+2\leq 64qx+3=64x(2^{x}-2)+3,

we obtain

\frac{M_{q}}{q}-16\log_{2}(M_{q}+2)>20.

The function

F(t)=\frac{t}{q}-16\log_{2}(t+2)

is increasing for
t\geq M_{q}
, because

F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{16}{(t+2)\ln 2}>0

there. Therefore, for all
m\geq M_{q}
,

\frac{m}{q}>16\log_{2}(m+2)+20.

Since

16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5\leq 16\log_{2}(m+2)+21,

and the left side of the desired inequality is an integer, the result follows for
q\geq 5
.

It remains to check
q=3,4
. If
q=3
, then
M_{q}=446
. For
446\leq m\leq 510
, one has

\left\lceil m/3\right\rceil\geq 149=16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5.

For later dyadic intervals
2^{s-1}-1\leq m\leq 2^{s}-2
,
s\geq 10
, the inequality follows from

\left\lceil\frac{2^{s-1}-1}{3}\right\rceil\geq 16s+5.

If
q=4
, then
M_{q}=662
. For
662\leq m\leq 1022
, one has

\left\lceil m/4\right\rceil\geq 166>165=16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5.

For
s\geq 11
, the interval estimate

\left\lceil\frac{2^{s-1}-1}{4}\right\rceil\geq 16s+5

gives the result.
∎

Theorem 10
.

For every
r\geq 4
,

n_{r}\leq\left\lceil 64(r-1)\log_{2}(r+1)\right\rceil+3.

Proof.

Put
q=r-1
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be any binary phylogenetic tree with
m=|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|
internal edges and suppose

m\geq\left\lceil 64q\log_{2}(q+2)\right\rceil.

By Lemma
8
, choose a linked edge-quartet certificate
\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr}
with

\rho(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})\leq 16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+4.

By Lemma
9
,

\left\lceil\frac{m}{q}\right\rceil\geq 16\left\lceil\log_{2}(m+2)\right\rceil+5\geq\rho(\mathcal{Q}_{\rm tr})+1.

The routing-width packing criterion, Theorem
5
, defines
\mathcal{T}
by exactly
\lceil m/q\rceil

r
-state characters. Since
m=n-3
, exact attainment holds for every

n\geq\left\lceil 64(r-1)\log_{2}(r+1)\right\rceil+3.

Thus
n_{r}
is at most this quantity.
∎

6
The lower bound

We now prove the lower bound in Theorem
1
.

Proposition 11
.

For every
r\geq 4
,

n_{r}\geq 3r+1.

Proof.

Set
n=3r
. Then

\left\lceil\frac{n-3}{r-1}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{3r-3}{r-1}\right\rceil=3.

Huber, Linz, Moulton, and Semple
[
6
]
proved that a binary phylogenetic tree is defined by at most three characters if and only if it has no internal subtree isomorphic to the snowflake. Since
3r\geq 12
, there exists a binary phylogenetic tree on
3r
leaves containing the snowflake as an internal subtree: take a twelve-leaf snowflake example and attach the remaining leaves without destroying the internal snowflake. This tree cannot be defined by three characters, and hence cannot be defined by three
r
-state characters. Therefore the Semple–Steel lower bound is not attained at
n=3r
. Hence
n_{r}>3r
, which is the desired inequality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

The lower bound is Proposition
11
, and the upper bound is Theorem
10
.
∎

7
Discussion and remaining problems

Theorem
1
shows that the eventual sharpness threshold of Bordewich and Semple grows at most logarithmically above the universal lower-bound scale. In the proof, the logarithm enters through the construction of a linked quartet certificate with logarithmic conflict degree. The subsequent passage from quartets to
r
-state characters is an equitable coloring argument.

The natural next target is the following linear statement.

Conjecture 12
.

For every
r\geq 4
,

n_{r}=3r+1.

Moreover, whenever
n\geq 3r+1
, there is an explicit construction of

\left\lceil\frac{n-3}{r-1}\right\rceil

r
-state characters defining any prescribed binary phylogenetic tree with
n
leaves.

The lower bound in Proposition
11
shows that the constant
3
in Conjecture
12
would be best possible. The conjecture includes an effective form, not only an existence statement. The cases presently settled for small state bounds are consistent with this prediction: internal splits give the binary case, Bordewich and Semple proved
n_{4}=13
, and Long and Wang report
n_{3}=8
and
n_{5}=16

[
2
,
8
]
. Thus the endpoint formula is verified for
r=4,5
, while the
r=3
threshold is linear but lies below the
3r+1
expression.

A proof of Conjecture
12
would require more than simply shortening the transition paths used above. On highly branching internal trees, for example complete binary internal cores, deterministic linked-transition certificates naturally have logarithmic length. Thus a linear theorem is likely to require a different certificate mechanism, such as a non-linked semidyadic block certificate or a bounded-state core forceability argument.

Two questions remain natural.

Problem 13
.

Does every binary phylogenetic tree admit a linked edge-quartet certificate whose conflict graph has bounded maximum degree? A positive answer would imply
n_{r}=O(r)
by Theorem
5
.

Problem 14
.

Does there exist an absolute constant
C
such that, whenever
m=|E^{0}(\mathcal{T})|\geq C(r-1)+1
, the internal edges of
\mathcal{T}
can be partitioned into
\lceil m/(r-1)\rceil
blocks of size at most
r-1
whose displayed characters have semidyadic closure equal to
Q(\mathcal{T})
? A positive answer would prove
n_{r}=O(r)
.

The present paper leaves these linear problems open, but reduces the general threshold problem from the earlier polynomial
O(r^{5})
window to the explicit near-linear window in Theorem
1
.
