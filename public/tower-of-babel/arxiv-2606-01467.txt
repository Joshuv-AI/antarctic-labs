Infinitely many holes in connectedness loci for collinear affine iterated function systems
Bernat Espigule

Abstract
. We study connectedness loci for a one-parameter family of collinear affine iterated function systems with equally spaced translations. These loci are equivalent to closures of roots of monic polynomials with coefficients in a prescribed finite interval of integers. We prove that each of them has infinitely many holes. The two-map case is the theorem of Calegari–Koch–Walker after inversion of the parameter. For all larger alphabets we construct a stationary family of finite-capture loops in the geometry of the corresponding difference attractor. Each loop surrounds a missing-center configuration whose midpoint word is not admissible, and a finite inverse-tree certificate places the enclosed parameter outside the connectedness locus. A no-return certificate separates the resulting holes. The witness parameters converge to a canonical algebraic boundary point. The finite sign, intersection, and pruning verifications are recorded as exact algebraic certificates.

1.
Introduction

Many fractal constructions depend on parameters, and the topology of the attractor may change in subtle ways as the parameter varies. The classical prototype is the Mandelbrot set: it is the connectedness locus for the quadratic family
z\mapsto z^{2}+c
, and membership is detected by the orbit of the critical value. For affine iterated function systems the analogous connectedness problem is usually less direct, since the maps need not be inverse branches of a single polynomial or rational map.

A fundamental affine example is the connectedness locus for a pair of complex linear maps introduced by Barnsley and Harrington
[
2
]
. In that setting the connectedness problem is closely related to zeros of power series with coefficients in
\{-1,0,1\}
and to the geometry of complex Bernoulli convolutions; see, for instance, Solomyak and Xu
[
7
]
. Bandt proved the existence of a genuine hole
[
1
]
, and Calegari, Koch, and Walker later proved that there are infinitely many holes accumulating at a renormalization point
[
3
]
. A key feature of this circle of ideas is that connectedness can be tested by a single marked point in a difference attractor, rather than by direct inspection of the original attractor.

The present paper studies the corresponding question for collinear systems with more than two maps. The translations are equally spaced on the real line. This has two consequences. First, the parameter set can be described arithmetically as a closure of roots of monic polynomials whose non-leading coefficients lie in a finite interval of integers. Second, the difference alphabet is again collinear and equally spaced: the difference of the
n
-map alphabet is the alphabet of the same type with parameter
N=2n-1
. Thus the marked-point formulation remains in the same family of attractors, which makes it possible to combine symbolic finite capture with explicit restricted-root calculations.

Throughout the paper

\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\},

and all closures in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are relative closures unless explicitly stated otherwise. Fix an integer
n\geq 2
and set

D_{n}=\{-n+1,-n+2,\ldots,n-1\},\qquad A_{n}=\{-n+1,-n+3,\ldots,n-1\}.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{n}
be the set of roots of monic polynomials whose non-leading coefficients lie in
D_{n}
:

(1.1)

\mathcal{R}_{n}:=\left\{c\in\mathbb{C}:\;c^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{j}c^{j}=0\text{ for some }m\geq 1\text{ and }d_{j}\in D_{n}\right\}.

For
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
consider the iterated function system

\left\{f_{t}(z)=t+\frac{z}{c}:t\in A_{n}\right\},

and denote its attractor by
E(c,n)
:

E(c,n)=\bigcup_{t\in A_{n}}f_{t}(E(c,n)).

The corresponding connectedness locus is

\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\{c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:E(c,n)\text{ is connected}\}.

Proposition 1.1

(Restricted-root formulation)
.

For every integer
n\geq 2
,

(1.2)

\mathcal{M}_{n}=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})}^{\,\mathbb{C}}\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})=\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})}^{\,\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}}.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Equivalently,

c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\text{ and }\exists(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset D_{n}\text{ such that }1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0.

Proof.

The reciprocal-series criterion is the combination of the marked-point characterization with the radix description of the difference attractor; see Sections
A.1
and
A.2
for a derivation. If the digits
(d_{k})
are eventually zero, then clearing denominators gives a monic polynomial with non-leading coefficients in
D_{n}
, so the corresponding parameter lies in
\mathcal{R}_{n}
.

Now let
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and choose digits
(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset D_{n}
with

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0.

Set

F(z):=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}z^{-k},\qquad F_{m}(z):=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{k}z^{-k}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
B(c,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Since
F(z)=1+O(z^{-1})
at infinity,
F
is not identically zero on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
; hence its zeros are isolated. Choose
r_{\varepsilon}\in(0,\varepsilon)
so that
F
has no zero on

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\{z:|z-c|=r_{\varepsilon}\}.

The functions
F_{m}
converge uniformly to
F
on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, so by Rouché’s theorem, for all sufficiently large
m
the function
F_{m}
has the same number of zeros inside
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
as
F
. In particular, for all such
m
it has at least one zero there; choose one and call it
c_{\varepsilon,m}
. Since

P_{m}(z):=z^{m}F_{m}(z)=z^{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_{m-j}z^{j}

is monic with non-leading coefficients in
D_{n}
, one has
c_{\varepsilon,m}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}
and
|c_{\varepsilon,m}-c|<\varepsilon
. As
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, it follows that
c\in\overline{\mathcal{R}_{n}}^{\,\mathbb{C}}\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
.

Conversely, let
c_{j}\in\mathcal{R}_{n}\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})
with
c_{j}\to c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Write

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{j,k}c_{j}^{-k}=0

with each
(d_{j,k})_{k\geq 1}
eventually zero and
d_{j,k}\in D_{n}
. Because
D_{n}
is finite, a diagonal subsequence yields digits
(d_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset D_{n}
such that, for each fixed
k
, one has
d_{j_{\nu},k}=d_{k}
for all sufficiently large
\nu
. Since
|c_{j_{\nu}}|\geq r>1
eventually, the series are uniformly absolutely convergent, and dominated convergence gives

1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}c^{-k}=0.

Hence
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. This proves (
1.2
) and the closedness assertion.
∎

Proposition
1.1
is the root-theoretic counterpart of the marked-point characterization recalled later in Proposition
2.1
: the same collinear family controls both the original attractor and its difference attractor, now with alphabet size
2n-1
. In the proof of Theorem
1.2
we use Proposition
1.1
only for this arithmetic framing and for the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
; the geometric hole construction starts from the marked-point and trap/enclosure framework recalled in Section

If one allows an arbitrary nonzero leading coefficient in (
1.1
), reciprocal polynomials show that the resulting root locus is invariant under
c\mapsto 1/c
. This is the symmetry behind the reciprocal parameterizations in the classical two-map literature. In particular, for
n=2
, after inversion
c\mapsto 1/c
, our locus
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is exactly the connectedness locus studied by Calegari–Koch–Walker
[
3
]
. Hence the statement of the theorem below is already known in that case. In
[
5
]
it was conjectured that analogous holes exist in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
for every
n>2
. The present paper proves that conjecture.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, all boundaries, interiors, and connected components are taken relative to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. A hole of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a bounded connected component of
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})\setminus\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Infinitely many holes)
.

For every integer
n\geq 2
, the connectedness locus
\mathcal{M}_{n}
has infinitely many holes.

For
n=2
, writing
\lambda:=1/c
, the affine map

T_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{1-\lambda}{2}z+\frac{1}{2}

conjugates our normalized IFS to the family studied by Calegari–Koch–Walker
[
3
]
. Thus the new contribution of the present paper concerns
n\geq 3
.

The structural core of the paper is Theorem
4.8
. For each odd depth
k=1,3,5,\ldots
, it produces four admissible finite-capture charts whose selected boundary arcs form a Jordan curve
\Gamma_{n,k}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
. The midpoint word of the four corresponding symbolic addresses is not admissible, so the central chart that would normally fill the loop is absent. A finite inverse-tree certificate places an explicit witness parameter
c_{n,k}\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
in the bounded complementary component, and a no-return certificate separates the holes obtained at different odd depths.

The same stationary mechanism also identifies a canonical limit point. Corollary
5.1
shows that the witness sequence converges to the unique zero, in a common witness box, of the explicit degree-
13
polynomial
Q_{n}
from Lemma
4.4
. This gives an algebraic boundary point of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
at which infinitely many of the holes accumulate.

The proof uses two finite-capture inputs from the companion preprint
[
4
, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 3.16]
: the marked-point criterion and the canonical trap–enclosure framework. Section
2
restates these inputs in the precise form needed below, and Appendix
A
records the imported statements and the reductions used here. The restricted-root formulation is proved in the present paper from the marked-point criterion and the radix description of the difference attractor. The construction of the holes—the base symbolic configuration, the stationary transport, the witness-box analysis, the local branch geometry, and the inverse-tree extinction—is then carried out internally. The finite algebraic parts are organized in Appendix
C
; each item is reduced to an explicit sign, root-count, or non-intersection check. The parameter-space figures are finite-depth renderings obtained from the finite-capture algorithm summarized in Appendix
E
; the proof does not rely on visual inspection of the figures.

Finite-capture toolkit and loop witnesses

We use the finite-capture framework developed in
[
4
]
only through the marked-point criterion and the canonical trap–enclosure statement restated in Propositions

Set

N:=2n-1.

The open two-disk lens is

\mathcal{X}_{n}:=\{c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}:|c\pm 1|<\sqrt{2n}\}.

All parameters constructed in this paper lie in
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.

Difference attractor and backward polynomials

The difference digit set is

A_{N}=A_{n}-A_{n}=2D_{n}=\{-(N-1),-(N-3),\ldots,N-3,N-1\}\subset 2\mathbb{Z}.

Let
E(c,N)
denote the attractor of the associated difference IFS
f_{t}(z)=t+z/c
,

. For

, define the Hutchinson operator of the difference IFS by

\mathcal{H}_{c,N}(S):=\bigcup_{t\in A_{N}}f_{t}(S).

The first imported input is the marked-point criterion, proved in
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
in the present normalization.

Proposition 2.1

(Marked-point characterization)
.

For every
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
one has

c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2c\in E(c,N).

Proof.

This is
[

Proposition 2.2

(Canonical trap and enclosure)

The trap
\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)
is an open parallelogram centered at

. The explicit half-width formulas used later are recalled in Appendices

.

Proof.

This is the restriction to
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}

For

define the inverse branch
g_{t}(z)=c(z-t)
. For a finite word

If

Since all parameters under consideration lie in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, the equations
Z_{w}(c)=0
and
W_{w}(c)=0
are equivalent.

Finite-capture charts and admissible center roots

For each
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)
denote the canonical trap from Proposition
2.2
. Thus

\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)\subset\Int(E(c,N)),\qquad\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)\subset\mathcal{H}_{c,N}(\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)).

This gives open parameter charts

(2.1)

\mathcal{U}_{u}:=\{c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}:g_{u}(2c)=Z_{u}(c)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)\},\qquad u\in A_{N}^{m}.

Lemma 2.3

(Chart inclusion)
.

For every
u\in A_{N}^{m}
, the chart
\mathcal{U}_{u}
is open and satisfies
\mathcal{U}_{u}\subset\Int(\mathcal{M}_{n})
. Consequently,

\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{U}_{u}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}.

Proof.

If
c\in\mathcal{U}_{u}
, then
Z_{u}(c)=g_{u}(2c)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)\subset\Int(E(c,N))
by Proposition
2.2
. Applying
f_{u}
gives
2c\in E(c,N)
, so
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
by Proposition
2.1
. Because membership in
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\cdot)
is determined by strict inequalities in continuous functions of the parameter, the same witness persists on a neighborhood of
c
. Hence
c\in\Int(\mathcal{M}_{n})
and
\mathcal{U}_{u}
is open. Since
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is closed in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
by Proposition
1.1
, its relative boundary in
\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{U}_{u}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(Admissible center-root chart criterion)
.

Let
u\in A_{N}^{m}
and let
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
satisfy
W_{u}(c)=0
. Then
c\in\mathcal{U}_{u}\subset\Int(\mathcal{M}_{n})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, the canonical trap
\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)
is an open parallelogram centered at
0
and contained in
\Int(E(c,N))
. Thus
0\in\mathcal{C}_{0}(c)
. If
W_{u}(c)=0
, then
Z_{u}(c)=0
, so
c\in\mathcal{U}_{u}
by (
2.1
). Now apply Lemma
2.3
.
∎

This is the center-root criterion used below: if
u\in A_{N}^{m}
and
W_{u}(c)=0
, then
c\in\mathcal{U}_{u}
. We call the connected component of
\mathcal{U}_{u}
containing
c
the center region determined by the admissible center root
(u,c)
.

Lemma 2.5

(Quadrant semialgebraicity of charts)
.

For every finite word
u\in A_{N}^{m}
, the restriction of
\mathcal{U}_{u}
to any open quadrant is semialgebraic. Consequently, on each open quadrant its boundary is contained in a finite union of real-algebraic arcs and points.

Proof.

On the upper half-plane, Appendix
B
rewrites the condition
c\in\mathcal{U}_{u}
as the four polynomial sign inequalities (
B.5
). On any other open quadrant the same argument applies after fixing the signs of
x=\Repart c
and
y=\Impart c
. Hence
\mathcal{U}_{u}
is semialgebraic on each open quadrant. Standard semialgebraic stratification gives the boundary statement.
∎

Away from finitely many singularities these boundary components are real-analytic arcs, which is the regularity used when boundary pieces are concatenated into Jordan curves.

Exteriority and loop witnesses

By Proposition
2.2
, the same framework also provides a canonical closed enclosure
\mathcal{E}_{\!\rm enc}(c,N)\supset E(c,N)
. This turns exteriority into a finite inverse-tree question. For
m\geq 0
and
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, let

T_{m}(c):=\{u=t_{1}\cdots t_{m}\in A_{N}^{m}:g_{t_{1}\cdots t_{j}}(2c)\in\mathcal{E}_{\!\rm enc}(c,N)\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq m\}.

Here
T_{0}(c):=\{\varnothing\}
. If
T_{m}(c)=\varnothing
for some
m
, then no admissible inverse orbit of
2c
can remain inside the enclosure, hence
c\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Proposition 2.6

(Finite inverse-tree criterion)
.

Let
c\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. If
T_{m}(c)=\varnothing
for some
m\geq 0
, then
c\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
c\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. By Proposition
2.1
, one has
2c\in E(c,N)
. Choose an address
(t_{j})_{j\geq 1}\subset A_{N}
for
2c
in the difference attractor. Then every partial inverse iterate

g_{t_{1}\cdots t_{j}}(2c)\in E(c,N)\subset\mathcal{E}_{\!\rm enc}(c,N)\qquad(j\geq 1),

so every prefix
t_{1}\cdots t_{m}
belongs to
T_{m}(c)
. This contradicts
T_{m}(c)=\varnothing
. Hence
c\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
∎

Definition 2.7

(Loop witness)
.

A loop witness for
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a pair
(\Gamma,c_{*})
such that

(i)

\Gamma\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a Jordan curve;

(ii)

c_{*}
lies in the bounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
;

(iii)

c_{*}\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})\setminus\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Lemma 2.8

(A loop witness forces a hole)
.

If
(\Gamma,c_{*})
is a loop witness, then
\mathcal{M}_{n}
has a hole contained in the bounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the connected component of
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})\setminus\mathcal{M}_{n}
containing
c_{*}
. Since
H
is connected, disjoint from
\Gamma
, and contains a point of the bounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
, the Jordan curve theorem gives
H
inside that bounded component. Thus
H
is bounded.
∎

Proposition 2.9

(Four-chart loop criterion)
.

Let
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}\subset\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}
be open sets such that

\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{i}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}\qquad(i=1,2,3,4).

Assume that there exist pairwise distinct points

p_{12},p_{23},p_{34},p_{41}\in\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}

and real-analytic arcs

\gamma_{1}\subset\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{1},\quad\gamma_{2}\subset\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{2},\quad\gamma_{3}\subset\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{3},\quad\gamma_{4}\subset\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{4}

with the cyclic incidence pattern

\gamma_{1}:p_{12}\to p_{41},\quad\gamma_{2}:p_{23}\to p_{12},\quad\gamma_{3}:p_{34}\to p_{23},\quad\gamma_{4}:p_{41}\to p_{34},

such that the four arcs meet only at their endpoints and meet there transversely. Then

\Gamma:=\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\cup\gamma_{3}\cup\gamma_{4}

is a Jordan curve contained in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Proof.

The transversality and disjointness assumptions imply that the union is a simple closed curve. Since each arc lies in one of the relative boundaries
\partial_{\mathcal{X}_{n}\setminus\mathbb{R}}C_{i}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
, we get
\Gamma\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
∎

Ghost vacancies and the base loop

Definition 3.1

(Ghost parallelogram)

is equivalent to saying that

4.
Stationary transport of the vacancy and infinitely many holes

The base ghost vacancy is propagated by a stationary operator on words.

The renormalization operator

Let

P:=(0,-a,-a,a,a,-a,-a,0,a,b,-a,-a)\in A_{N}^{12}.

For an integer word
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
with
m\geq 10
define

(4.1)

\mathscr{R}(u):=(P,a-u_{10},-u_{11},-u_{12},\ldots,-u_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+3}.

If
d=(d^{(1)},d^{(2)},d^{(3)},d^{(4)})
is an ordered
4
-tuple of words, write

\mathscr{R}(d):=(\mathscr{R}(d^{(1)}),\mathscr{R}(d^{(2)}),\mathscr{R}(d^{(3)}),\mathscr{R}(d^{(4)})).

Lemma 4.1

(Basic properties of
\mathscr{R}
)
.

Let
m\geq 10
.

(i)

|\mathscr{R}(u)|=|u|+3
for every
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

(ii)

If
u\in A_{N}^{m}
and
u_{10}\geq 0
, then
\mathscr{R}(u)\in A_{N}^{m+3}
.

(iii)

\mathscr{R}
preserves midpoints:

\mathscr{R}\left(\frac{u+v}{2}\right)=\frac{\mathscr{R}(u)+\mathscr{R}(v)}{2}.

Proof.

Part (i) is immediate. For (ii), the prefix
P
lies in
A_{N}^{12}
. If
u\in A_{N}^{m}
and
u_{10}\geq 0
, then
0\leq u_{10}\leq a
and
u_{10}
is even, so
a-u_{10}\in A_{N}
. The remaining tail digits are sign changes of digits already in
A_{N}
. Part (iii) follows directly from (
4.1
).
∎

Transported words and transported defects

Define recursively

d_{k}:=\mathscr{R}^{k-1}(d_{1}),\qquad w_{k}:=\mathscr{R}^{k-1}(w_{1})\qquad(k\geq 1),

where
d_{1}=(d_{1}^{(1)},d_{1}^{(2)},d_{1}^{(3)},d_{1}^{(4)})
. Write

d_{k}=(d_{k}^{(1)},d_{k}^{(2)},d_{k}^{(3)},d_{k}^{(4)}).

By Lemma
4.1
(i), all five words have common length

By Lemma
4.1
(iii), the midpoint relation persists:

(4.2)

w_{k}=\frac{d_{k}^{(1)}+d_{k}^{(3)}}{2}=\frac{d_{k}^{(2)}+d_{k}^{(4)}}{2}.

Lemma 4.2

(Transport of the lattice defect)
.

For every
k\geq 1
, the word
w_{k}
does not belong to
A_{N}^{m_{k}}
. More precisely, its first odd digit occurs at index

j_{k}:=10+3(k-1)=m_{k}-8.

Proof.

For
k=1
the tenth digit of

is
2n-3
, which is odd. The rule (
4.1
) prepends the even block
P
and then shifts the remaining digits three places to the right, changing only signs. Thus the parity of each existing digit is preserved and the first odd digit index increases by
3
at each step.
∎

Corollary 4.3

(Transported ghost parallelograms)
.

For every
k\geq 1
, the quintuple

(d_{k}^{(1)},d_{k}^{(2)},d_{k}^{(3)},d_{k}^{(4)};w_{k})

is a ghost parallelogram of depth
m_{k}
.

Proof.

The midpoint identity (
4.2
) gives

d_{k}^{(1)}+d_{k}^{(3)}=d_{k}^{(2)}+d_{k}^{(4)}=2w_{k}.

For
k=1
the four corner words belong to
A_{N}^{18}
by construction, and their tenth digits are
b,a,a,b
, hence nonnegative. If
d_{k}^{(i)}\in A_{N}^{m_{k}}
has nonnegative tenth digit, then Lemma
4.1
(ii) gives
d_{k+1}^{(i)}=\mathscr{R}(d_{k}^{(i)})\in A_{N}^{m_{k+1}}
. Moreover, every transported word has tenth digit equal to the fixed prefix digit

. An induction on
k
therefore shows that all four corner words lie in
A_{N}^{m_{k}}
for every

. By Lemma
4.2
, the center word
w_{k}
does not belong to
A_{N}^{m_{k}}
.
∎

For each
k\geq 1
define the level-
m_{k}
charts

\mathcal{U}_{k}^{(i)}:=\mathcal{U}_{d_{k}^{(i)}}\qquad(i=1,2,3,4).

At level
k
the witness polynomial is

W_{w_{k}}(c)=2c^{m_{k}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}(w_{k})_{j}c^{m_{k}-j}.

The root-isolation step is organized around a single auxiliary polynomial
Q_{n}
. The next lemma records the exact recursion behind that reduction.

Lemma 4.4

(Exact recursion for the stationary family)
.

Let
m\geq 10
, and let

u=(0,-a,-a,a,a,-a,-a,0,a,u_{10},u_{11},\ldots,u_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}.

Define

\displaystyle Q_{n}(c):={}

\displaystyle c^{13}+(n-1)c^{11}+nc^{10}-(n-1)c^{9}+2(n-1)c^{7}

\displaystyle-(n-1)c^{5}+c^{3}+(n-1)c^{2}-(n-1).

Then

W_{\mathscr{R}(u)}(c)+W_{u}(c)=2c^{m-10}Q_{n}(c).

For
c\neq 0
, equivalently, if
\widetilde{W}_{u}(c):=c^{-|u|}W_{u}(c)
, then

(4.3)

\widetilde{W}_{\mathscr{R}(u)}(c)=2c^{-13}Q_{n}(c)-c^{-3}\widetilde{W}_{u}(c).

Proof.

A direct expansion gives

\displaystyle W_{u}(c)={}

\displaystyle 2c^{m}+ac^{m-2}+ac^{m-3}-ac^{m-4}-ac^{m-5}

\displaystyle\quad+ac^{m-6}+ac^{m-7}-ac^{m-9}-\sum_{j=10}^{m}u_{j}c^{m-j},

and

\displaystyle W_{\mathscr{R}(u)}(c)={}

\displaystyle 2c^{m+3}+ac^{m+1}+ac^{m}-ac^{m-1}-ac^{m-2}

\displaystyle\quad+ac^{m-3}+ac^{m-4}-ac^{m-6}-bc^{m-7}

\displaystyle\quad+ac^{m-8}+ac^{m-9}-(a-u_{10})c^{m-10}+\sum_{j=11}^{m}u_{j}c^{m-j}.

Adding the two identities cancels the tail coefficients and yields

\displaystyle W_{\mathscr{R}(u)}(c)+W_{u}(c)={}

\displaystyle 2c^{m+3}+ac^{m+1}+2nc^{m}-ac^{m-1}+2ac^{m-3}

\displaystyle\quad-ac^{m-5}+2c^{m-7}+ac^{m-8}-ac^{m-10}.

This is exactly
2c^{m-10}Q_{n}(c)
after substituting
a=2(n-1)
and
a-b=2
. Dividing by
c^{m+3}
gives (
4.3
).
∎

Corollary 4.5

(Closed form for the normalized witness polynomials)
.

Let

(4.4)

F_{n}(c):=\frac{2Q_{n}(c)}{c^{10}(c^{3}+1)}.

Then, as an identity of rational functions,

(4.5)

\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)-F_{n}(c)=(-c^{-3})^{k-1}\bigl(\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)\bigr)

for every
k\geq 1
. Equivalently,

\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)=F_{n}(c)+(-c^{-3})^{k-1}\bigl(\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)\bigr).

Proof.

The identity
F_{n}(c)=2c^{-13}Q_{n}(c)-c^{-3}F_{n}(c)
is just the definition of
F_{n}(c)
rewritten. Subtracting it from (
4.3
) gives

\widetilde{W}_{w_{k+1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)=-c^{-3}\bigl(\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)-F_{n}(c)\bigr).

Iterating this relation proves the formula.
∎

Lemma 4.6

(Stationary difference polynomials)
.

For
i=1,2,3,4
define

\Delta_{i}(c):=Z_{d_{1}^{(i)}}(c)-Z_{w_{1}}(c).

Then for every
k\geq 1
one has

Z_{d_{k}^{(i)}}(c)-Z_{w_{k}}(c)=(-1)^{k-1}\Delta_{i}(c).

Equivalently, for
c\neq 0
,

W_{d_{k}^{(i)}}(c)-W_{w_{k}}(c)=(-1)^{k-1}c^{-1}\Delta_{i}(c).

Moreover,

\Delta_{1}(c)=cA_{n}(c),\qquad\Delta_{2}(c)=-c^{2}B_{n}(c),\qquad\Delta_{3}(c)=-cA_{n}(c),\qquad\Delta_{4}(c)=c^{2}B_{n}(c),

where

\displaystyle A_{n}(c)

\displaystyle:=c^{8}+(n-1)c^{6}+(n-2)c^{5}-(n-1)c^{4}-2(n-1)c^{3}+c^{2}+2(n-1)c+2(n-1),

\displaystyle B_{n}(c)

\displaystyle:=c^{7}+(n-1)c^{5}+(n-2)c^{4}-(n-1)c^{3}-2(n-1)c^{2}-c+2(n-1).

Proof.

Apply Lemma
4.4
to the pair of words
d_{k}^{(i)}
and
w_{k}
. Since both have the same fixed initial block, subtraction gives

W_{d_{k+1}^{(i)}}(c)-W_{w_{k+1}}(c)=-\bigl(W_{d_{k}^{(i)}}(c)-W_{w_{k}}(c)\bigr).

Multiplying by
c
yields

Z_{d_{k+1}^{(i)}}(c)-Z_{w_{k+1}}(c)=-\bigl(Z_{d_{k}^{(i)}}(c)-Z_{w_{k}}(c)\bigr),

and induction on
k
proves the first formula. The second is immediate from
Z_{u}=cW_{u}
. For
k=1

) and (
3.3
) gives the displayed formulas for
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{4}
.
∎

Lemma 4.7

(Uniform isolation box)
.

Fix

(i)

(ii)

has exactly one zero in

\Impart(c_{n,k})>\sqrt{n},\qquad|c_{n,k}|>\sqrt{n}.

Proof.

If

, so

, and

, so

. Hence

.

For (ii), set

as in (

). By Corollary

,

\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)-F_{n}(c)=(-c^{-3})^{k-1}\bigl(\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)\bigr).

Since

and

shows that

|\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)|<|F_{n}(c)|\qquad(c\in\partial\mathcal{B}_{n}).

Because

in place of

for every

, hence also

, has the same number of zeros in

∎

and odd

be the connected component of

containing

. By Theorem

and Lemma

, each

is a hole and

. If

are odd, then Theorem

are distinct. Thus
\mathcal{M}_{n}
has infinitely many holes for every

For

. Hence

Corollary 5.1

(Canonical algebraic renormalization point)
.

For every
n\geq 3
there exists a unique simple zero
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that

Q_{n}(\xi_{n})=0.

The witness sequence satisfies

c_{n,k}\longrightarrow\xi_{n}\qquad(k\to\infty).

In particular,

\xi_{n}\in\partial\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n},

every neighborhood of
\xi_{n}
meets infinitely many pairwise distinct holes of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and
\xi_{n}
is algebraic of degree at most
13
. We call
\xi_{n}
the renormalization point of the stationary family.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 3
and set
F_{n}(c)
as in (
4.4
). By Lemma
C.2
, the polynomial
Q_{n}
has exactly one zero in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, counted with multiplicity, and no zero on
\partial\mathcal{B}_{n}
. Denote that zero by
\xi_{n}
. Because the count is
1
, the zero is simple.

By Corollary
4.5
,

\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)-F_{n}(c)=(-c^{-3})^{k-1}\bigl(\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(c)-F_{n}(c)\bigr).

Hence, for every
c\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
,

|\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}(c)-F_{n}(c)|\leq|c|^{-3(k-1)}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}|\widetilde{W}_{w_{1}}(z)-F_{n}(z)|.

Since
|c|>\sqrt{n}>1
on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, it follows that
\widetilde{W}_{w_{k}}\to F_{n}
uniformly on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Now let
c_{*}
be any subsequential limit of
(c_{n,k})_{k\geq 1}
. By Lemma
4.7
, all terms lie in the compact set
\overline{\mathcal{B}_{n}}
, so such a limit exists. Choose a subsequence
c_{n,k_{j}}\to c_{*}
. Since
\widetilde{W}_{w_{k_{j}}}(c_{n,k_{j}})=0
, the uniform convergence gives

|F_{n}(c_{n,k_{j}})|=|F_{n}(c_{n,k_{j}})-\widetilde{W}_{w_{k_{j}}}(c_{n,k_{j}})|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}|\widetilde{W}_{w_{k_{j}}}(z)-F_{n}(z)|\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore
F_{n}(c_{*})=0
. Because the denominator of
F_{n}
does not vanish on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, the functions
F_{n}
and
Q_{n}
have the same zeros there, so
Q_{n}(c_{*})=0
. By uniqueness of the zero in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and the absence of boundary zeros, one must have
c_{*}=\xi_{n}
. Thus every convergent subsequence has the same limit
\xi_{n}
, and therefore the whole sequence
(c_{n,k})_{k\geq 1}
converges to
\xi_{n}
.

For each odd
k\geq 1
, let
H_{n,k}
be the hole containing
c_{n,k}
. By the proof of Theorem
1.2
, the holes
H_{n,k}
with
k
odd are pairwise distinct. Every neighborhood of
\xi_{n}
contains all but finitely many odd-level points
c_{n,k}
, hence meets infinitely many of these holes.

We claim that
\xi_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Otherwise
\xi_{n}
would lie in a connected component
C
of
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}})\setminus\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Because

Remark 5.2

The same argument yields a geometric rate. Indeed, Corollary

Appendix E
Rendering protocol for the parameter-space figures

The parameter-plane figures are produced from the same finite inverse-search mechanism used in the finite-capture framework. For a parameter
c
, the renderer applies the inverse branches

g_{t}(z)=c(z-t),\qquad t\in A_{N},

starting from the marked point
2c
. A sample receives an interior verdict if some finite inverse word maps it into the canonical trap. It receives an exterior verdict if, by a finite depth, every inverse branch is eliminated by the canonical enclosure. If neither verdict is obtained at the displayed depth, the sample is marked unresolved. This is the same finite-capture decision rule used in the proof, but the formal proof of the holes uses the algebraic verifications in Appendix
C
, not visual inspection of the figures.

The grayscale layer of the parameter-space figures is generated by this finite-capture classifier. The colored overlays are chart annotations: they mark the four chart levels and local regions used in the missing-center construction. The reference material supplied with the manuscript records the pixel-to-parameter map, the search depth, the coloring convention, and the unresolved-sample count for the reference renderings. A compact CPU implementation is also included; it classifies closed pixel boxes by outward interval arithmetic and writes a certificate label for each pixel. This makes the rendering convention independent of browser or hardware details.

The implementation uses the canonical trap and enclosure of Proposition
2.2
. In the upper half-plane the trap test is

|\Im z|<\frac{(N-2x)y}{x^{2}+y^{2}},\qquad|\Im(cz)|<Ny,

and the enclosure test uses the half-widths
V_{E}(c,N)
and
S_{E}(c,N)
defined in Section
C.5
. The branching set is the finite arithmetic progression
A_{N}=\{-a,-a+2,\ldots,a\}
, and the search terminates as soon as either trap entry or enclosure extinction is certified at the chosen depth.

The WebGL implementation used for interactive exploration
[
6
]
follows the same inverse-iteration structure. It is not used as a proof engine in this paper; the proof-critical finite statements are the algebraic certificates in Appendices
C
and
D
.
