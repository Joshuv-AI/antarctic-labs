Abstract
. We determine all permutation polynomials among several families of polynomials over 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q^{3}} for arbitrary prime powers q q . We obtain some new families of permutation polynomials over 𝔽 q 3 \mathbb{F}_{q^{3}} with simple coefficients for infinitely many characteristics. As a specific consequence, our results resolve the generalization of conjectures of Zhang, Zheng, Wang, Peng, and Li in the even characteristic. Our proofs are conceptually short and involve no complicated computations, in contrast to the proofs of results on permutation polynomials which were published previously. Moreover, we develop a totally new systematic method in this paper for the study of permutation polynomials.



1.
Introduction

A polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is called a
permutation polynomial
if the function
\alpha\mapsto f(\alpha)
induces a permutation of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Permutation polynomials have long been studied, both for their intrinsic interest and due to applications to various areas of computer science and engineering.

The paper
[
8
]
introduced a systematic method to construct permutation polynomials over fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of square order, by showing that the question of whether a polynomial of the form
X^{r}B(X^{q-1})
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
may be reduced to the question of whether an associated rational function permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
. In over
100
papers by various authors, the above method has been used to produce specific instances of permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
with few terms, for a typical example see
[
2
]
. But there are much few permutation polynomials over fields
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
of cubic order in the literature, all of which are constructed by using variants of the method introduced in
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we will introduce a new method to study permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
. Most constructions of permutation polynomials in the literature use one step of reduction, which is some variant of the above described construction initially introduced in
[
8
]
, which is either multiplicative or additive. Our construction of permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
in this paper is different, in the sense that it consists of two steps of reduction. The first step reduces permutations on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
to permutations on the set of roots of
X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X
, and the second step reduces permutations on the set of roots of
X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X
to permutations on the set of roots of
X^{q+1}+X+1
. It is interesting to note that the first step is of additive flavor while the second step is of multiplicative flavor. It is surprising that the mixture of such two reductions of different flavors can lead to permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
with at most seven terms and very simple coefficients.

As an illustration of our new approach, we will construct several families of permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
with at most seven terms, among which one family has only three terms and three family have five terms, all of them have coefficients
1
. More importantly, we will construct two families of permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
for infinitely many characteristics. Our approach can apply to the study of other families of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
, but only permutation polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
with simple expressions are interesting, so we focus on such permutation polynomials.

Our main results are as follows. For any nonzero integer
n
, we write
\ord_{2}(n)
for the largest integer
s\geq 0
such that
2^{s}
divides
n
. We also write
\ord_{2}(0)=\infty
. In other words,
\ord_{2}(n)
is the discrete
2
-adic valuation of
n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{k}
,
Q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{\ell}
,
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{m}
, and
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{n}
for a prime
p
and some integers
k,\ell,m,n
with
k>0
and
\ell,m,n\geq 0
. Suppose

and write

f(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X^{Q+1}\circ(X^{q}-X)+(X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X)\circ cX^{R+S}.

Then

permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
if and only if

Proofs of the main results

In this section we show the main results in this paper. The following result can be viewed an additive analogue of

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{k}
for a prime
p
and an integer
k>0
. Suppose

f(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L(X)+B(X)\circ(X^{q}-X)+(X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X)\circ C(X)

for some
B(X),C(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}[X]
and some
L(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}X^{p^{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]

f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
if and only if both of the following hold:

•

L(X)+(X^{q}-X)\circ B(X)

X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X

•

L(X)+(X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X)\circ C(X)

for any

Let
\Gamma

. It is clear that

induces a surjective map from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
onto

g(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}L(X)+(X^{q}-X)\circ B(X)
. Since
(X^{q}-X)\circ L(X)=L(X)\circ(X^{q}-X)

(X^{q}-X)\circ(X^{q^{2}}+X^{q}+X)=X^{q^{3}}-X

. Hence
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
if and only if

permutes

and

is injective on on

for any

. Suppose

, since

, we know

is injective on

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume

and

Proof.

Let us fix an element

. For any

, we have

(u+z)^{Q+1}=u^{Q+1}+u^{Q}z+uz^{Q}+z^{Q+1},

so that we can compute

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;(u+z)^{q^{2}(Q+1)}+(u+z)^{q(Q+1)}+(u+z)^{Q+1}

\displaystyle=(u^{q^{2}(Q+1)}+u^{q(Q+1)}+u^{Q+1})+(u^{q^{2}Q}+u^{qQ}+u^{Q})z+(u^{q^{2}}+u^{q}+u)z^{Q}+z^{Q+1}

\displaystyle=(u^{q^{2}(Q+1)}+u^{q(Q+1)}+u^{Q+1})+(u^{q^{2}}+u^{q}+u)^{Q}z+(u^{q^{2}}+u^{q}+u)z^{Q}+z^{Q+1}

\displaystyle=(u^{q^{2}(Q+1)}+u^{q(Q+1)}+u^{Q+1})+(u^{q^{2}}+u^{q}+u)^{Q+1}+\bigl((u^{q^{2}}+u^{q}+u)+z\bigr)^{Q+1}.

Since

Lemma 3.3

\Lambda

\Lambda
