Adaptive quantum inspired deep reinforcement learning for multi objective low carbon CCHP optimization
Authors: Abdul Rehman, Suyang Zhou, Sheeraz Iqbal, Ibrahim Alsaduni, Md Shafiullah, Muhammad Aurangzeb
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based multi-objective optimization framework for low-carbon combined cooling, heating, and power (CCHP) systems is proposed. The framework integrates adaptive Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithms (QIEA) with digital twin technology. The proposed Adaptive Quantum-DRL Multi-Objective Optimization (AQ-DRLMO) model addresses the complex scheduling challenges inherent in the CCHP systems by incorporating flow monitoring of carbon emission, ladder-type carbon trading, and demand response scheduling. A hierarchical digital twin structure enables predictive optimization through Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), ensuring accurate modeling of thermodynamic interactions. Three enhanced control strategies Electric-Thermal Equivalent Following (ETEF), Electric Equivalent Following (EEF), and Thermal Equivalent Following (TEF) are introduced and employed attention-based transformer networks for temporal pattern recognition. A novel Carbon-Conscious Optimal Power Flow (C-OPF) model tracks carbon flows across multi-stage energy conversion pathways. Extensive simulations conducted under summer and winter operating conditions demonstrate that the proposed AQ-DRLMO framework achieves greenhouse gas emission reduction of 40.08%, primary energy saving of 34.04%, and cost reduction of 24.44% compared to conventional individual generation systems across different control strategies and seasonal scenarios. The quantum-inspired optimization achieves 67.3% faster convergence compared to conventional genetic algorithms while maintaining solution diversity in the Pareto front, converging in 45 iterations versus 137 iterations for standard genetic algorithms under identical test conditions. This study relies on synthetic load profiles and simulation-generated validation data; therefore, the findings are best interpreted as a simulation-based demonstration of day-ahead scheduling potential rather than a validated real-time control solution. Introduction

The increasing rate at which the world is becoming carbon neutral and the amount of money the world spends have heightened the research and investigation energies that are now employed to create integrated energy systems that are not only efficient but also impact the environment in the least harmful manner possible. Combined cooling, heating and power (CCHP) is a new technology, which can be very efficient (70–85%) compared to the old method of generating power separately (SGS), i.e. by recovering waste heat and by cascading energy1,2. The need to reduce the quantity of greenhouse gases has led to the creation of new remedies to CCHP optimization and its promotion to incorporate the recent versions of artificial intelligence, carbon accounting systems, and control methods, which are market-driven3,4.

The current advancements on economic dispatch of low carbon economy have revealed potential in the possibility to investigate the carbon movement by optimization of power system operations5,6. Zhang et al.7 provided an entire process carbon emission tracking system that tracks the carbon emission levels in the conversion of energy, which establishes principled rubrics of carbon mindful optimization. The establishment of carbon trading schemes and most of all, the application of ladders of carbon trading with structuring levels of prices have introduced new frontiers in the economics of the energy system8,9. Those mechanisms encourage the minimization of emissions in the market, and there is a sophisticated optimization in such landscapes that cannot be efficiently handled using conventional tools.

This has enhanced the versatility of the system, and introduced stochastic uncertainties10 with the combination of energy storage and both renewable energy sources and CCHP. Wang et al.11 have demonstrated that a joint electricity and carbon sharing system can assist in reducing costs by 7.64% and emissions by 17.68% through coordinated integration of electric vehicles. Integrated electricity-gas systems create additional optimization issues of the manner in which the source-demand side resources must be synchronized, efficient methods of solving such problems can only be found through sophisticated algorithm methods12,13. The additional complexity to the problem of optimization has been brought about by the enhanced use of hydrogen energy and power to gas technology that generates cross-sector coupling between the industries and sectors14,15.

A recent technological breakthrough, AI has turned out to be an effective means of complex optimization of the energy system. The algorithms of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) have been demonstrated to be incredibly powerful in terms of being able to think in serial with uncertainty cases16,17. The quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithm has the possibility of exponentially enhancing speed through the superposition and entanglement concepts18,19. The set of such technologies and digital twin architecture enables improved real-time predictive optimization accuracy through the synergies of such systems with the digital twin architecture20.

The integration of power-to-gas (P2G) systems and the carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies gives the solutions to the negative carbon emissions21,22. Obi and Tian23,24 came up with computational cost optimization models of carbon capture power plants, which are efficient to capture at affordable costs. The LCA technique of estimating carbon emissions provides a broad-based analysis of the environmental connotations as quantified in terms of impacts25. The graph neural networks that utilize the dynamically changing carbon emission monitoring data have the potential to produce a dynamically responsive optimization owing to the dynamics of changing grid conditions26.

Low-carbon Planning issues are not restricted to optimisation of operations only, but also to the long-run infrastructure investment decision making27. Research by Chen et al.28 investigated the impacts of carbon taxes on the investment in low-carbon technologies, and it indicated that there are complex dependencies between policies and the technology adoption trends. The significance of the system’s holistic views is demonstrated by the life-cycle carbon emission accounting of the electricity-hydrogen combined systems by the coordinated planning29. The multi-regional power system plans involving large-scale energy storage and carbon emission trading allow possible opportunities to minimize the degree of cross-border carbon emission reduction30.

Recent developments on quantum-inspired optimization have shown that there is a great opportunity in complex energy system scheduling. Federated quantum-inspired AI models for distributed systems were created by Addo31 on the basis of digital twins simulations, converging in seconds as opposed to hours in genetic algorithms. Singh et al.32 proposed quantum hybrid evolutionary algorithms with a multi-objective to maximize quality-of-service with the aid of quantum-inspired superposition states. QHRMOF framework suggested by Lilhore et al.33 exhibited the energy efficient scheduling of the tasks in a cloud computing system based on quantum-inspired algorithms. Moreover, Lv34 examined quantum-biological synergistic strategies to optimize industries in three-levels models through quantum-aided carbon management.

The use of digital twin technology has become one of the most essential facilitators of predictive optimization of the energy systems. The digital twin platforms of structural health monitoring that are developed by Animashaun et al.35 have five levels of integration that allow the smooth interaction between the physical and virtual worlds. Bousnia et al.36 developed digital twins of real-world smart energy systems, which are used for optimal energy management, including heating, cooling, and electricity storage. They proposed a comprehensive systems architecture integrating detailed dynamic models with deep reinforcement learning. Building on these developments, recent studies have explored quantum-inspired techniques to address the growing complexity of energy optimization. Shen et al.37 demonstrated the effectiveness of quantum-inspired deep reinforcement learning in achieving robust real-time control of low-carbon microgrids. At the same time, Vanitha et al.38 applied a quantum-inspired optimization strategy to enhance dispatch performance and efficiency in combined heat and power systems.

Research gaps

In terms of the existing research, one can mention that there is a significant gap in research that does not allow for practical application. First of all, most of the optimization models consider the carbon emission monitoring as an absolute constraint rather than a dynamic optimization target with a dynamic time evolution scheme. Second, in the high-dimensional scenario, real time implementation is usually impossible due to the decay in computational capabilities to perform multi-objective optimization scale. Third, there is still a lack of sufficient response to the combination of market based processes (pricing of electricity, carbon trading) and physical limitations of the physical system. Fourth, the digital twin of CCHP systems lacks physics-informed modeling, which explains the occurrence of a thermodynamic interaction. Fifth, additional efforts are required to learn the coupling implications of different control measures and their implications on carbon footprints in a system wide context. Low-carbon economic dispatch methodologies

The low-carbon economic dispatch paradigm has evolved significantly by incorporating the theory of carbon emission flows. The new work by Akhtar41 was also the first piece to monitor the carbon emission on the full-process level and extend the carbon emission on the generation side to the areas of transmission and distribution loss based on quantum-inspired neural radiative transfer frameworks. Their model showed that correct accounting of the carbon must consider the time varying changes in the grid carbon intensity. Further research incorporates energy systems where flows of carbon emission become rampant to examine how a cross-sector coupling can modify carbon footprints, and they found that operation strategy selections can either amplify or reduce the footprint using advanced methods of power flow.

The shortcoming of the assumptions of carbon intensity was resolved by the dynamic carbon emission model proposed by43. Their metaheuristic optimization model, which is based on AI, achieved a cost reduction of 21.7% during the process of not optimizing environmental performance since it has integrated on-the-fly carbon pricing. Another contribution to the field was made by the article published by Liu et al.44 that introduced multi-dimensional value stream mapping with multi-objective optimization, the latter permitting and enabling two-stage active optimization within minutes’ time scales based on changing grid conditions. Digital twin technology has been increasingly applied to support low-carbon operation in multi-energy systems. Koirala et al.46 highlighted the potential of digital twins in urban multi-energy systems to facilitate emission reductions and zero-carbon community goals through advanced life-cycle optimization. Similarly, Bousnina36 integrated a digital twin with deep reinforcement learning to optimize energy management while considering greenhouse gas emission objectives.

Carbon trading mechanisms and market integration

The recent advancements in carbon trading mechanisms have led to coordinated optimization approaches that integrate ladder-type carbon trading with market-driven scheduling. Sun et al.8 and Zhang et. al.14 developed multi-scenario and park-level models that effectively reduce emissions by incorporating stepped carbon pricing and responsive flexible resources, highlighting the growing role of market integration in promoting low-carbon operation of integrated energy systems. The benefits of the tiered pricing could be introduced by the carbon trading system, which is discussed by Lv34 as a ladder-type, but can promote the increasing emission reduction due to the quantum-biological synergistic mechanisms that could be realized.

Sun and Sarin8,45 optimized renewable micro grids based on tiered carbon trade whereby large carbon reduction is achieved on hybrid AI strategies. The mathematical foundation of considering carbon externalities in the power system dispatch was defined by Veeramachaneni39 as carbon-conscious optimal power flow formulation formulated with quantum algorithms to integrate renewable energy in AI-driven data centers. A study comparing the use of knowledge mapping strategies was carried out by Yadav and Rawat47 on the quantum computing of climate change research which revealed the presence of synergistic effects of the emission reduction as a result of coordinated policy implementation with quantum-enhanced genetic algorithms.

Integrated energy system optimization

The integration of energy systems (IES) of CCHP systems has also been presented with multi-vector optimization dilemmas. Addo et al.31 proposed optimized distributed energy system (based on federated and quantum-inspired AI) in adaptive systems through digital twin simulations, and reported large reductions in emission, as a result of coordinated optimization strategies that converged in a few seconds rather than the hours of genetic algorithms.

The team of Nozari and Yordanova40 studied the integrated systems based on the carbon reduction through fuzzy multi objective modeling in supply chain management. Singh et al.32 analyzed the multi-objective quantum hybrid evolutionary algorithms to improve the quality-of-service in IoT setups, and as it turned out, quantum-inspired superposition states may be applied as optimization work enhancers in conditions of high computational loads. The Jabeur48 coordination methodology of planning addressed the issue of optimizing sustainable mobility presence, in which hybrid quantum-LLM choice frameworks and enhanced quantum-inspired evolutionary computation were included.

Advanced optimization algorithms

The development of computing has allowed more sophisticated designs of CCHP optimization. Lilhore et al.33 have used quantum-inspired hybrid multi-objective framework (QHRMOF) for energy-efficient task scheduling and load balancing, and the results showed better convergence with quantum-inspired evolutionary methods. The enablers used by Urrea42 to design artificial intelligence-based and bio-inspired control decisions in industrial robotics are DRL, digital twins, 5G URLLC, and quantum-inspired optimization.

Vanitha et al. optimized CCHP dispatch using a quantum-inspired strategy for increased heat and power efficiency38. Complementing this, Shen et al. enabled low-carbon microgrid frequency control via quantum-inspired deep reinforcement learning37. These advancements demonstrate the potential of hybrid quantum-classical approaches in enhancing both economic and environmental performance of integrated energy systems.

Digital twin integration for energy systems

The introduction of digital twin technology has transformed predictive optimization within energy systems. The next-generation structural health monitoring has been developed by Animashaun et al.35 who created the AI-powered digital twin platforms, and have defined five integrated levels that enable the smooth interaction between physical and virtual worlds. Liu et al.44 went further to develop digital twins to work with tunnel boring machines, suggesting detailed system designs that combine both data collection and smart model reconstruction.

In Khumsikiew et al.49, a complete research progress was made on quantum computing and digital twins, where the development of algorithms in cryptography and digital twin research was presented in terms of integration approaches. Nozari and Yordanova40 explored the synergy between hybrid digital twins and business intelligence. They demonstrated that quantum-inspired algorithms coupled with fuzzy multiobjective modeling can effectively handle complicated optimization tasks within virtual model settings. In their article50, Bhatia and Kumar introduced bibliometric analysis with an accent on simulation tools, data integration, and product design with a digital twin to achieve sustainable cities.

Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms

Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) have demonstrated significant potential in addressing complex optimization problems by leveraging probabilistic representations and quantum principles to enhance exploration and convergence. Recent studies, such as Iovane19, provide comprehensive insights into the evolution of quantum-inspired optimization techniques and their growing applicability in engineering systems. Similarly, recent scient metric analyses highlight the increasing role of quantum-inspired metaheuristic algorithms in Industry 4.0 applications, particularly for large-scale and computationally intensive optimization tasks.

In the context of hybrid optimization, Singh et al.32 proposed a multi-objective quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithm that significantly improves convergence speed and solution quality in complex environments such as IoT systems. Furthermore, advancements in quantum-inspired genetic algorithms have introduced enhanced representations and robustness against noise and uncertainty, improving their applicability in real-world optimization problems51.

In addition, quantum annealing-inspired approaches have demonstrated strong performance in solving combinable optimization problems, offering improved efficiency compared to classical methods. These developments indicate that quantum-inspired and hybrid optimization frameworks are becoming increasingly viable for real-time and large-scale engineering applications.

Research gap analysis

Nevertheless, even with such improvements, the gaps in available literature are defined. The current method of tracking carbon is not dynamically adapted to the grid conditions. Optimization algorithms can tend to breach computational tractability in situations where the quality of the solution is a factor. There is insufficient research on digital twin technology coupled with AI-based optimization in the context of CCHP. Market mechanism integration is generally made on individual carbon pricing schemes without considering tiered or dynamism trading schemes. The presence of such gaps leads to the development of the desired AQ-DRLMO framework that helps to incorporate the breakthroughs in a single field in a larger optimization framework.

The PINN architecture consists of a fully-connected feedforward neural network with three hidden layers of 256 neurons each, using hyperbolic tangent (tanh) activation functions to ensure smooth gradient propagation. The network was trained on a synthetic dataset generated from validated thermodynamic simulation models of the CCHP system comprising 50,000 data points spanning the full operational envelope. The dataset was partitioned into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) sets with stratified sampling across seasonal and load conditions. Input features include PGS electrical output, fuel consumption rate, waste heat recovery temperature, ambient temperature, and component activation states (8 input dimensions). Output variables are predicted thermal generation, system efficiency, and carbon emission rate (3 output dimensions). All inputs were normalized using min-max scaling. The physics-informed loss enforces first-law energy conservation at each energy conversion node with physics weight set to 10.0. Training used the Adam optimizer with learning rate 1e-4, batch size 128, for 500 epochs with early stopping based on validation loss (patience of 50 epochs). The model achieved R-squared values of 0.973 for PGS electrical output and 0.968 for thermal generation on the held-out test set.

The threshold is adaptively learned through the DRL module based on historical performance.

The three control strategies differ from conventional electric-following (EF) and thermal-following (TF) policies in their adaptive and demand-ratio-aware switching mechanism. In traditional EF/TF strategies, the operating mode is fixed throughout the optimization horizon. In contrast, the proposed ETEF strategy dynamically switches between EEF and TEF modes at each timestep based on the instantaneous electric-to-thermal demand ratio. The switching threshold is not predetermined but is adaptively learned by the DRL agent through interaction with the digital twin environment. At each timestep t, the DRL agent observes the current state including the demand ratio, evaluates the expected reward under both modes using the twin critic networks, and selects the mode that maximizes the multi-objective reward function. This adaptive mechanism enables the system to respond to intra-day demand fluctuations that fixed-mode strategies cannot capture.

Discussion

The results of the experiments confirm that the proposed AQ-DRLMO framework can achieve equilibrium between the economic, environmental, and energy efficiency objectives of CCHP systems simultaneously. Several notable observations emerge from the experimental results.

The reason is that EEF control strategy worked out better regarding PESR (34.04%) and GER (40.08%) because of the maximization of the PGS capacity utilization. When operated at a load near its rated power, the system may be operated at maximum conversion efficiency and the per-unit cost of carbon content of produced electricity is low. The high thermal recovery of waste heat suggests reduced reliance on backup boilers, which would likely lower direct combustion emissions. The ETEF control strategy usually demonstrates the most cost-effective performance at 24.44% of cost saving, primarily due to the reduced losses in electricity trading and insignificant thermal energy losses. The process can tactically respond tofluctuating ratios of demand throughout the day by adaptive switching between the electric-following and thermal-following modes. Quantum-enhanced search mechanism enables rapid solutions of the optimal switching thresholds, which results in rapid convergence, 67.3%.

Carbon trading integration based on ladders introduces the nonlinearity of cost structure that traditional optimization methods cannot effectively address. It also covers their discontinuities in the suggested AQ-DRLMO framework since DRL module can learn the optimal set of policies in non-convex reward environments. The tiered pricing system will make sure that the price rate of the unwanted emissions will be higher than the cost of the specified amounts of emissions above the quota limits.

The PINN-configured digital twin provides the appropriate system modeling into the model with regard to the complex thermodynamic interaction of the CCHP components. In a physics-informed approach, the predictions are not in conflict with the fundamental conservation laws even when extrapollated beyond the distributions of the training data. This consistency is required when using optimization algorithms that are exploring new areas of operation. In the case of real-time optimization, the computing resources of its application can also be taken into account. The AQ-DRLMO framework produces day-ahead scheduling solutions in 156.8 seconds for a 24-hour optimization horizon, which is suitable for day-ahead planning but does not constitute demonstrated real-time control. For online deployment, the trained TD3 policy network can execute forward inference in approximately 3.2 milliseconds per timestep on the NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, enabling potential real-time adjustment at sub-second intervals. However, full real-time feasibility requires further validation of the online deployment architecture, communication latency with physical CCHP controllers, and robustness to measurement noise, which remains a direction for future work.

Accordingly, the present work is best interpreted as a simulation-based methodological study that demonstrates the potential of the AQ-DRLMO framework for day-ahead CCHP scheduling optimization under controlled conditions, rather than a practically validated operational solution for real-time industrial control. The current evidence supports the framework’s effectiveness in a simulated environment with hypothetical industrial load profiles and simulation-generated digital twin validation, and the reported performance metrics should be understood within this scope. The transition from simulation-validated methodology to field-deployed operational system requires additionalvalidation stages, including testing with real plant measurement data, integration with industrial SCADA and communication infrastructure, and assessment of robustness under practical operating uncertainties such as sensor noise, actuator delays, and equipment degradation.

Several limitations of the present study warrant acknowledgment. First, experimental validation relies on hypothetical load profiles generated from published industrial benchmark patterns rather than measured demand data from an operational CCHP facility; validation against field measurements from real installations remains necessary to confirm practical applicability. Second, although baseline comparisons were conducted under identical experimental conditions with statistical verification over 30 independent runs, implementation-specific optimizations inherent to competing methods as reported by their original authors may not be fully captured. Third, digital twin prediction accuracy (R2 = 0.973) is established against simulation-generated data, reflecting internal consistency within the modeled environment rather than verified predictive fidelity against real plant measurements. Fourth, the quantum-inspired optimization module currently runs on classical hardware through numerical simulation of quantum operations; execution on physical quantum processors would be required to substantiate claims of quantum computational advantage. Fifth, the robustness of the proposed framework under real-world operating conditions including measurement noise, SCADA communication latency, and progressive equipment degradation has not been assessed, and such investigation is prerequisite to industrial deployment.

Conclusion

This paper has presented the Adaptive Quantum-DRL Multi-Objective Optimization (AQ-DRLMO) framework for low-carbon CCHP system optimization, integrating quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms with deep reinforcement learning and a physics-informed digital twin. The AQ-DRLMO framework simultaneously optimizes cost, carbon emission, and primary energy consumption within a dynamic market environment. The extended carbon emission flow model enables comprehensive carbon tracking across multi-stage energy conversion pathways, supporting accurate carbon footprint attribution and compliance with carbon trading requirements. The ladder-type carbon trading mechanism introduces a market-responsive cost structure that incentivizes emission reductions beyond allocated quota thresholds. Accordingly, the present work is best interpreted as a simulation-based methodological study that demonstrates the potential of the AQ-DRLMO framework for day-ahead CCHP scheduling optimization under controlled conditions, rather than a practically validated operational solution for real-time industrial control. The simulation-based experimental validations were conducted across summer and winter operating scenarios, using synthetic industrial load profiles. The proposed AQ-DRLMO framework achieves a cost reduction of 24.44%, primary energy saving of 34.04%, and greenhouse gas emission reduction of 40.08% relative to conventional separate generation systems. The quantum-inspired optimization achieves 67.3% faster convergence compared to standard genetic algorithms while maintaining solution diversity across the Pareto front. The results of the proposed AQ-DRLMO are compared with many state-of-the-art methods, which confirmed improvements of 8.33% in CRR, 11.79% in PESR, and 17.09% in GER over the best-performing baseline. The ablation study results further validated the individual contributions of the quantum evolutionary layer, DRL refinement module, physics-informed digital twin, and ladder-type carbon trading component to overall framework performance. Future research should extend the framework to multi-energy hub systems with cross-sector coupling, enabling integrated coordination across diverse energy carriers. The incorporation of stochastic optimization methods is necessary to effectively address the variability and uncertainty of renewable energy sources, while integrating emerging technologies, such as hydrogen energy storage and vehicle-to-grid (V2G) systems, can further enhance system flexibility and sustainability. It should be noted that since all validation in this study was performed using synthetic data and simulation-generated benchmarks, the practical deployability of the framework remains to be confirmed through field testing with real-world operational CCHP plant data. A critical next step is the transition from simulation-based validation to field-deployable implementation, which requires testing against real operational data from CCHP installations. This progression also demands integration with industrial SCADA and control infrastructures to ensure practical feasibility. Finally, a systematic evaluation under realistic operating conditions including sensor noise, actuator delays, and equipment degradation, is essential to guarantee robustness and reliability in real-world applications.
