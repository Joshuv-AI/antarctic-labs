The non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture and equalities of ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariants
Daniel Barrera Salazar, Andrew Graham, and Chris Williams

Abstract
. Let G G be a reductive group quasi-split at p p . Using arguments of Hansen–Thorne, we show that under the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture (NALC), Hansen’s p p -adic overconvergent cohomology eigenvariety for G G is étale over its image in weight space at any non-critical classical tempered cuspidal point of ‘cohomological multiplicity one’. This applies to all non-critical classical cuspidal points if G = Res F / ℚ ​ GL n G=\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\GL_{n} . We then let π \pi be a p p -ordinary regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of GL n ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) such that π p \pi_{p} is Steinberg. Combining the above étaleness result for the classical point attached to π \pi , and a local-global compatibility result from our earlier work, we deduce – under a tangent vector hypothesis that is true for at least half the simple roots – the equality of Fontaine–Mazur and automorphic ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariants for π \pi . Where this assumption is satisfied, we deduce the NALC implies a conjecture of Gehrmann: that automorphic ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariants are independent of cohomological degree. Our approach is inspired by (and generalises) previous work of Gehrmann–Rosso. When π = Sym n − 1 ⁡ π f \pi=\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi_{f} is the symmetric power lift of a modular form, we verify all assumptions other than the NALC, and deduce a functoriality result for the automorphic ℒ \mathcal{L} -invariants.



1.
Introduction

Let
\pi
be a
p
-ordinary regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation (RACAR) of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. The main result of this note is an equality of (
p
-adic) automorphic and Fontaine–Mazur
\mathcal{L}
-invariants for
\pi
. We also prove results on the structure of overconvergent cohomology and the étaleness of eigenvarieties in a more general setting.

1.1.
Structure theorems for overconvergent cohomology and étaleness of eigenvarieties

An essential tool in our comparison of
\mathcal{L}
-invariants is the
p
-adic variation of systems of Hecke eigenvalues, captured geometrically via
eigenvarieties
. Eigenvarieties, and
p
-adic families, have become a staple topic in algebraic number theory and arithmetic geometry, at the heart of breakthroughs in Iwasawa theory (cases of the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer and Bloch–Kato conjectures) and the Langlands program (through new modularity results).

We first describe the geometry of eigenvarieties in a more general setting. As such, let
G/\mathbb{Q}
be a reductive group quasi-split at
p
. When studying the geometry of the eigenvariety for
G
, one encounters a dichotomy.

(A)

If
G(\mathbb{R})
admits discrete series representations, the cuspidal eigenvariety is expected to be flat over weight space, with Zariski-dense sets of ‘classical’ points (arising from cuspidal automorphic representations of
G(\mathbb{A})
). This case is the main focus of
[
Urb11
]
. Here there are general results studying the geometry at ‘nice’ points; for example,
[
BDJ22
, §2]
provides systematic étaleness/structure results at non-critical points in ‘multiplicity one’ settings.

(B)

By contrast, if
G(\mathbb{R})
does not admit discrete series then the cuspidal eigenvariety is never flat over weight space, and we expect existence of ‘non-classical’ families with isolated classical points. See
[
BGW25
, Intro]
for a detailed summary of this phenomenon and the nature of classical families.

The first result of this note is an analogue of the étaleness and structure results of
[
BDJ22
]
in case (B) settings, working with Hansen’s eigenvarieties from overconvergent cohomology. In particular, using ideas of Hansen–Thorne
[
HT17
]
, we show a freeness result for overconvergent cohomology.

To explain this result, note the above dichotomy is captured via degrees of (the tempered part of) cuspidal cohomology. Let
K_{\infty}
be (any) maximal compact-modulo-centre subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R})
. Then the
defect
and
amplitude
of
G
are respectively

(1.1)

\ell=\ell(G)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\operatorname{rank}(G)-\operatorname{rank}(K_{\infty}),\qquad q=q(G)=[\operatorname{dim}(G(\mathbb{R})/K_{\infty})-\ell(G)]/2.

For example,
\ell(\GL_{n})=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
; if
G
admits Shimura varieties, then
\ell(G)=0
; and if
F
is a number field with
r
real embeddings and
2s
complex embeddings, then
\ell(\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\GL_{2})=s
.

More generally, we have:

•

G(\mathbb{R})
admits discrete series if and only if
\ell(G)=0
;

•

tempered cuspidal cohomology for
G
is supported exactly in degrees
\{q,q+1,...,q+\ell\}
.

In particular, case (A) corresponds to cuspidal cohomology being supported in only one degree, whilst case (B) corresponds to positive defect, and cuspidal cohomology in multiple degrees.

Now let
K^{p}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
be a fixed tame level, let
\operatorname{Iw}\subset G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
be a fixed Iwahori subgroup, and let
K=K^{p}\operatorname{Iw}
. Let
\mathscr{X}_{G,K}\to\mathscr{W}
be Hansen’s (Iwahori-level) eigenvariety for
G
of tame level
K^{p}
. Let
K_{\infty}^{\circ}\leqslant K_{\infty}
be the connected component of the identity; then
\mathscr{X}_{G,K}=\cup_{\varepsilon}\mathscr{X}_{G,K}^{\varepsilon}
, where
\varepsilon
runs over the characters
K_{\infty}/K_{\infty}^{\circ}\to\{\pm 1\}
. Each point
x_{\phi}\in\mathscr{X}_{G,K}^{\varepsilon}
corresponds to a finite-slope Hecke eigenpacket
\phi
for
G
appearing in the
\varepsilon
-part of overconvergent cohomology (of weight
w(x_{\phi})
and level
K
).

The
non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture
predicts that the dimension of a ‘sufficiently nice’ cuspidal irreducible component of
\mathscr{X}_{G,K}
(or
\mathscr{X}_{G,K}^{\varepsilon}
) is always
\dim\mathscr{W}-\ell(G)
; see Conjecture
2.4
below. This conjecture is known if
\ell(G)\leqslant 1
. We show:

Theorem A
.

Suppose that:

(a)

\phi
is a non-critical classical tempered cuspidal eigenpacket of weight
\lambda
and level
K
,

(b)

\phi
is of cohomological multiplicity one at sign
\varepsilon
(see Definition
3.2
),

(c)

The non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\phi
.

Then there exists a rigid Zariski-closed subspace
\Sigma^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathscr{W}
, containing
\lambda
and equidimensional of dimension
\dim\mathscr{W}-\ell(G)
, such that:

(i)

\mathscr{X}_{G,K}^{\varepsilon}\times_{\mathscr{W}}\Sigma^{\varepsilon}\to\Sigma^{\varepsilon}
is étale at
x_{\phi}
, and

(ii)

For
0\leqslant i\leqslant\ell(G)
and
h\gg 0
, the space
\mathrm{H}^{q+i}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Sigma^{\varepsilon}})^{\leqslant h,\varepsilon}_{\phi}
is free of rank
\binom{\ell(G)}{i}
over
\mathcal{O}(\Sigma^{\varepsilon})_{\lambda}
.

The groups
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Sigma^{\varepsilon}})
are
overconvergent cohomology groups
in the style of Ash–Stevens
[
AS08
]
. We describe all notation (which we adopt almost directly from
[
Han17
]
) and terminology in §
2
and §
3
. The proof goes through Hansen’s Tor spectral sequence (see Facts
3.5
), and occupies §
3
.

Remark 1.1
.

We stress that our proof essentially follows Hansen–Thorne
[
HT17
, §4.3]
. They specialise to
G=\GL_{n}/\mathbb{Q}
, and make the same assumptions; then in Theorem 4.9
op. cit
., they prove that: the local ring
\mathbb{T}_{\phi}
of
\mathscr{X}_{G,K}
at
\phi
is complete intersection; that, for an appropriate open affinoid
\Omega\subset\mathscr{W}
, the localised top degree cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{q+\ell(G)}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Omega})^{\leqslant h,\varepsilon}_{\phi}
is free of rank one over
\mathbb{T}_{\phi}
; and then that

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{\mathcal{O}(\Omega)_{\lambda}}(\mathrm{H}^{q+\ell(G)}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Omega})_{\phi}^{\leqslant h,\varepsilon},L)\cong\mathrm{H}^{q+\ell(G)-i}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{L}_{\lambda})_{\phi}^{\varepsilon},

where
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda}
is the local system attached to the
G
-representation of highest weight
\lambda
. We claim no originality beyond explicitly spelling out further applications/consequences of their methods.

Theorem
A
is a key tool in our result on
\mathcal{L}
-invariants, but it should have wider applications. For example, in the special case of Bianchi modular forms (where
G=\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\GL_{2}
, for
F
an imaginary quadratic field), the result generalises
[
BW21a
, Thm. 4.5]
, which was used to construct
p
-adic
L
-functions in families. The result
op. cit
. worked over an irreducible component
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\Sigma
and imposed the additional assumption that
\Sigma^{\prime}
was smooth at
\lambda
. In particular, Theorem
A
also upgrades
[
GR22
, Thm. 5.1, Cor. 5.3]
by removing the smoothness assumption on the curve
\Sigma
(denoted by
\mathcal{S}
in
[
GR22
]
). That result describes equalities of top and bottom degree
\mathcal{L}
-invariants attached to Bianchi modular forms of trivial weight.

We believe constructions of families of
p
-adic
L
-functions are being explored for
G=\GL(3)
and
G=\GL(3)\times\GL(2)
in the forthcoming works of Dimitrakopoulou–Rockwood and Hara–Namikawa respectively. In both cases, Theorem
A
is likely to be an essential ingredient. Note in these cases that assumptions (b) and (c) always hold; (b) by strong multiplicity one, and (c) since
\ell(G)=1
.

1.2.
The equality of
\mathcal{L}
-invariants for
\mathrm{GL}(n)

We now turn to our main result. In general,
\mathcal{L}
-invariants are
p
-adic invariants attached to arithmetic objects sufficiently ramified at
p
. For example, if
n=2
and
\pi=\pi_{E}
is attached to an elliptic curve
E
with split multiplicative reduction at
p
, then one has an
\mathcal{L}
-invariant
\mathcal{L}_{E}=\log_{p}(q)/\operatorname{ord}_{p}(q)
, where
q
is the Tate period, such that
E(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thanks to work of many people, notably Greenberg–Stevens
[
GS93
]
, Fontaine–Mazur
[
Maz94
]
, and Darmon
[
Dar01
]
,
\mathcal{L}_{E}
is known to have many other descriptions: for example, it encodes infinitesimal Hecke eigenvalues in
p
-adic families through
\pi_{E}
; it describes extension data in the local Galois representation of
E
at
p
; and it arises from
p
-arithmetic cohomology/modular symbols on the Bruhat–Tits tree. This quantity also appears in Mazur–Tate–Teitelbaum’s
exceptional zero conjecture
, proved in the aforementioned work of Greenberg–Stevens. This result has led to new cases of the
p
-part of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture
[
Ski16
]
.

The latter two descriptions of
\mathcal{L}_{E}
have been generalised hugely. Let now
\pi
be a
p
-ordinary regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Then for each
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
:

•

When
\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is non-split at
c
(see Definition
4.10
), there is an attached
Fontaine–Mazur
\mathcal{L}
-invariant

\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
, constructed from extension data in
\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
at the simple root
\alpha_{c}
corresponding to
c
.

•

There are also
automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants

\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
for
\pi
, where
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
and
\varepsilon\colon K_{\infty}/K_{\infty}^{\circ}\to\{\pm 1\}
is an
admissible sign
for
\pi
(that is, any character such that
\pi
contributes to the
\varepsilon
-part of cohomology). These were constructed by Gerhmann
[
Geh21
]
using
\varepsilon
-parts of compactly-supported
p
-arithmetic cohomology (in degree
q-n+1+r
) and extensions of Steinberg representations.

We recall the definitions of both types of
\mathcal{L}
-invariant in §
4
.

Remark 1.2
.

In both cases, the
\mathcal{L}
-invariants described above are subspaces
\mathbb{L}\subset\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)=\langle\log_{p},\operatorname{ord}_{p}\rangle
of codimension
\geqslant 1
, and
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
is always non-zero. Under further assumptions, we can extract quantities
\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi),\mathcal{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\in L
more closely resembling
\mathcal{L}_{E}
in the elliptic curve case. More precisely:

•

The representation
\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is non-crystalline at
c
if and only if
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\not\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
. In this case, we define
\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)\in L
such that
\log_{p}-\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)\operatorname{ord}_{p}\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
.

•

We know
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\not\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
by work of Gehrmann (see Proposition
4.6
below). If
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\neq 0
, then we define
\mathcal{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\in L
such that
\log_{p}-\mathcal{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\operatorname{ord}_{p}\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
.

These are the quantities that appear in exceptional zero conjectures.

In
[
BGW
]
, under certain assumptions we proved the equality of Fontaine–Mazur and (bottom degree) automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants when
n=3
, and thus deduced the first cases of the Greenberg–Benois exceptional zero conjecture
[
Gre94
,
Ben11
]
without any self-duality condition. The main result of this note is a generalisation of this equality to arbitrary dimensions and degrees.

Theorem B
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\pi
be a
p
-ordinary regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
. Suppose that:

(a)

The non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture holds for
p
-adic families through
\pi
;

(b)

\pi
admits non-
c
-parabolic deformations;

(c)

p>2n
, and the residual Galois representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
is irreducible and decomposed generic;

(d)

The local Galois representation
\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is non-split at
c
.

Then for all
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
and all admissible signs
\varepsilon
, we have an equality

\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\mathrm{FM}}(\pi)=\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)

of Fontaine–Mazur and automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants at the simple root
\alpha_{c}
. Moreover,
\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is non-crystalline at
c
and we have an equality of scalar
\mathcal{L}
-invariants
\mathcal{L}_{c}^{\mathrm{FM}}(\pi)=\mathcal{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
.

We note condition (d) is required for
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
to have codimension
\geqslant 1
in
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)
.

Remark 1.3
.

Under the assumptions (a)–(c) of Theorem
B
, we have shown that if
*_{c}
is non-split, then
*_{c}
is non-crystalline. To see this, note that
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)=\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\not\ni\operatorname{ord}_{p}
; so
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\not\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)
. If we know this for all
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
, then we deduce that the monodromy on
\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{st}}(\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}})
has maximal rank and hence we obtain the full local-global compatibility statement at
\ell=p
for
\rho_{\pi}
. Currently, the most general result concerning such local-global compatibility in the non-polarised setting is
[
Hev23
, Theorem 1.1]
, which unfortunately doesn’t say anything about the monodromy when
\pi_{p}
is Steinberg (the inequality is in the opposite direction).

Remark 1.4
.

The most subtle assumption in the theorem is (b), which we now explain. The other assumptions ensure
\pi
has trivial cohomological weight. Attached to
\pi
is a unique eigenpacket
\phi
which is non-critical classical of cohomological multiplicity one. As we also assume non-abelian Leopoldt, Theorem
A
thus holds for
\phi
, yielding a Zariski-closed rigid weight subspace
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathscr{W}
, containing the trivial character
\mathbbm{1}
. The
\GL_{n}
weight space
\mathscr{W}
is
n
-dimensional, with a general
L
-point having the form
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
, where
\lambda_{i}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to L^{\times}
is a continuous character. For
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
, we say
\pi

admits non-
c
-parabolic deformations
if there exists a tangent vector
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\operatorname{T}_{\mathbbm{1}}(\Sigma)
such that
v_{c}\neq v_{c+1}
.

For any
\pi
, we strongly expect the existence of non-
c
-parabolic deformations for all
c
. Such a result should follow from a higher-dimensional analogue of the Calegari–Mazur conjecture
[
CM09
]
; see
[
BGW
, Rem. 8.2.4]
for a discussion of this for
\GL_{3}
.

By Theorem
A
, we know
\dim\Sigma=n-\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
. Since each of the
n-1
conditions on
v
cuts out a different codimension 1 subspace of
\mathscr{W}
, we thus see that
\pi
admits non-
c
-parabolic deformations for at least half the possible values of
c
.

If
\pi
is essentially-self-dual, then we expect
\Sigma
to be an open subspace of the pure weight space
\mathscr{W}^{0}
, that is the subspace where
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{n+1-i}
is independent of
i
. Note this has dimension
n-\lfloor\tfrac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
, and that if
\Sigma\subset\mathscr{W}^{0}
is open, then
\pi
admits non-
c
-parabolic deformations for all
c
. Essentially-self-dual representations of
\GL_{n}
are either of symplectic or orthogonal type; see Remark
5.19
for a more detailed discussion of the symplectic case.

Gehrmann–Rosso
[
GR22
]
proved an analogous result in the special case where
\ell=0
. Whilst their strategy does not extend immediately to
\ell>0
(see Remark
5.22
), our approach is inspired by theirs, using Kohlhaase–Schraen’s Koszul complex and the cohomology of locally analytic principal series representations. Our key new input is a Tor spectral sequence for the latter, which we analyse using the former.

Condition (b) furnishes a good choice of infinitesimal deformation for
\pi
, and we study automorphic and Galois data for a
p
-adic family through
\pi
in this direction. We establish two key formulae:

–

An ‘automorphic’ Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula, proved in Theorem
5.16
. Here we use assumption (a) (to ensure étaleness of the eigenvariety at
\pi
, using Theorem
A
) and assumption (b) (to ensure the resulting infinitesimal formula is not an empty statement).

Establishing this automorphic formula is the heart of the paper, and in particular it is where the Koszul complex and locally analytic principal series are required.

–

A ‘Galois’ Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula, proved in Theorem
6.6
. Here a crucial input is our earlier work on local–global compatibility at
\ell=p
for
\GL(n)
torsion classes, which allows us to deduce that the Galois representation for a Hida family through
\pi
is trianguline at
p
. This work requires assumption (c).

Combining these formulae gives the theorem.

Much like in the
n=2,3
cases, we anticipate applications of this result towards the Greenberg–Benois exceptional zero conjecture. See
[
BGW
, Intro]
for a detailed introduction to this topic.

We also have an immediate application to a conjecture of Gehrmann:

Corollary C
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
, and let
\pi
be a
p
-ordinary regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Let
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
.

(i)

Suppose conditions (a)–(d) of Theorem
B
hold. Then Conjecture A of
[
Geh21
]
holds for
\pi
at
c
; that is, the automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
is independent of the degree
r
and sign
\varepsilon
.

(ii)

Suppose only conditions (a) and (b) hold. Then
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
is independent of
r
.

Part (i) follows directly from Theorem
B
, since the Fontaine–Mazur
\mathcal{L}
-invariant has no dependence on degree or sign. Part (ii) follows directly from the automorphic Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula, which requires only parts (a) and (b) (but works at a fixed admissible sign
\varepsilon
).

In particular, given the existence of non-
c
-parabolic deformations, we find the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture implies Gehrmann’s conjecture on independence of degree. Gehrmann has an analogous result proving that this follows from a conjecture of Venkatesh. We comment on this, and the relationship between the two results, in Remark
5.21
.

1.3.
Application to symmetric powers

In the case of symmetric powers of modular forms, some of our key assumptions are known to hold. Let
f\in S_{2}^{\operatorname{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(Np),\chi_{f})
, with attached (unitary) automorphic representation
\pi
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})
. We assume that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation (equivalently,
a_{p}(f)=1
and the conductor of the nebentypus
\chi_{f}
is prime to
p
). Fix
n\geqslant 3
, and consider the symmetric power lift
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
, which is a RACAR of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
constructed by Newton–Thorne
[
NT21
]
. This is
p
-ordinary and Steinberg-at-
p
. In §
7
, we verify condition (b) of Theorem
B
for
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
, and thus as a consequence of our automorphic Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula, we deduce the following functoriality for automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants:

Theorem D
.

Suppose the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture holds for
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
. Then for all
1\leqslant c\leqslant n-1
, all
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell(\GL_{n})
, and all admissible signs
\pm
for
\pi
and
\varepsilon
for
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
, we have

\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi)=\mathbb{L}_{1}^{0,\pm}(\pi)=\mathbb{L}_{1}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\pi)=\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\operatorname{FM}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi).

In particular Gehrmann’s conjecture on independence of degree and sign holds for
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
.

The second and third equalities are already known; our input is the first equality. Taken together, though, we obtain the main result of Theorem
B
for symmetric powers assuming only condition (a).

Note this result is unconditional for symmetric squares and cubes, since the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture is known to hold for
\GL_{3}
and
\GL_{4}
. For
\GL_{3}
we previously obtained this result for the bottom degree
\mathcal{L}
-invariant in
[
BGW
, §7]
.

. For all

H\{\pi^{p}\}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{f\in H[\pi^{p}]}\operatorname{im}(f)\subset H
.

By arguments as in Proposition
3.4
(see
[

, Prop. 3.6]
), we know that for
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
and
(\varepsilon\colon K_{\infty}/K_{\infty}^{\circ}\to\{\pm 1\})\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}

), we have

\dim_{L}\mathrm{H}^{q+r}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}L))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\ell\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\big),

and that this space is 0 in other degrees (and for other signs).

As

is Steinberg, we can ‘lift’ cohomology to the Bruhat–Tits building, as in
[

, §3.2]
. For this, observe that by

, above Cor. 2]
, the Iwahori-invariants

are a line. Frobenius reciprocity then yields a

-equivariant map

\operatorname{St}^{\operatorname{sm}}(L)^{*}\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Iw}}^{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}L,

inducing a map

\operatorname{ev}^{(j)}\colon\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\operatorname{St}^{\operatorname{sm}}(L)^{*}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}L)).

Lemma 4.3
.

For all

the map

induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\operatorname{St}^{\operatorname{sm}}(L)^{*}))[\pi^{p}]\xrightarrow{\,\smash{\raisebox{-1.959pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,}\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Iw}}^{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}L))[\pi^{p}].

In particular, for any

, we have

\dim_{L}\mathrm{H}^{q+r}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\operatorname{St}^{\operatorname{sm}}(R)^{*}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\ell\\

Proof.

This is
[

, Prop. 3.6]

Remark 4.4
.

This is a cohomological analogue/generalisation of

4.2.2.
Automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants

Let
\lambda:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow L
be a continuous homomorphism and
c\in\{1,...,n-1\}
. By (
4.1
), attached to
\lambda
is an extension
\Delta_{\lambda,c}\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L))
. Forgetting topologies gives a map

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathrm{an}}\Big(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)\Big)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{L[G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})]}\Big(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)\Big)\cong\mathrm{H}^{1}\Big(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\mathrm{Hom}_{L}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L))\Big),

and we can consider
\Delta_{\lambda,c}
as an element of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\mathrm{Hom}_{L}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)))
. Via the natural map

\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)^{\ast})\otimes_{L}\mathrm{Hom}_{L}\Big(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L),\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)\Big)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}),

the cup product with
\Delta_{\lambda,c}
gives, for any
j\geqslant 0
, a prime-to-
p
Hecke equivariant morphism

\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j,\operatorname{la}}:\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)^{\ast}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast})).

By
[
Geh21
, Cor. 2.19]
we have that
\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{cts}}(L)
is the unitary completion of
\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)
; and then from
[
Geh21
, Prop. 3.11]
we obtain a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{cts}}(L)^{\ast}))\cong\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{sm}}}(L)^{\ast})).

Then the inclusion
\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)\subset\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{cts}}(L)
induces a natural map

\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{la}}(L)^{\ast})).

Composing with
\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j,\operatorname{la}}
we finally obtain a map

\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j}\colon\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast})).

As
\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j}
is prime-to-
p
Hecke-equivariant, it induces a map on the
\pi^{p}
-isotypic parts

\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j}[\pi^{p}]:\mathrm{H}^{i}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))[\pi^{p}]\rightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))[\pi^{p}].

For any character
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
, we can perform similar constructions everywhere using the
\varepsilon
-parts of the cohomology, obtaining maps

(4.4)

\Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{j,\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]:\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}].

Recall that cuspidal cohomology is supported in degrees
q,q+1,\dots,q+\ell
, where
\ell=\ell(\GL_{n})
. By (
4.3
), we see that the
\pi^{p}
-isotypic pieces of the
p
-arithmetic cohomology with compact support are supported in degrees
q^{\prime},\dots,q^{\prime}+\ell
, where
q^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=q-n+1
.

Definition 4.5
.

For
c\in\{1,..,n-1\}
,
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
an admissible sign, let
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
be the subspace of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)
given by

\mathbb{L}^{r,\varepsilon}_{c}(\pi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\lambda\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L):\ \Upsilon_{c,\lambda}^{q^{\prime}+r,\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]=0\}.

Proposition 4.6
.

[
Geh21
, Prop. 3.14]

Let
c\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
an admissible sign.

(i)

For any choice of
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, we have
\mathrm{ord}_{p}\notin\mathbb{L}^{r,\varepsilon}_{c}(\pi)
. In particular, the
L
-vector space
\mathbb{L}^{r,\varepsilon}_{c}(\pi)
has codimension at least 1 (equivalently, dimension at most 1).

(ii)

The (bottom degree and top degree) spaces
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{0,\varepsilon}(\pi)
and
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{\ell,\varepsilon}(\pi)
have dimension exactly 1.

Proof.

For the singular cohomology, in
[
Geh21
, Cor. 3.8, Rem. 2.16]
Gehrmann shows that if
\lambda
is any non-zero
smooth
character, then
\Upsilon^{q+r,\varepsilon}_{c,\lambda}[\pi^{p}]
is an isomorphism, hence
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
. Thus
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\not\in\mathbb{L}^{r,\varepsilon}_{c}(\pi)
. That these results carry over to the compactly supported cohomology is explained in
[
Geh21
, §3.6]
. The rest of (i) follows since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)
is 2-dimensional, generated by
\operatorname{ord}_{p}
and
\log_{p}
.

To see (ii), it suffices to prove that
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)\neq 0
for
r=0,\ell
. We note that in bottom degree
j=q^{\prime}
and top degree
j=q^{\prime}+\ell
, the space
\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]
is 1-dimensional (by Lemma
4.3
). As
\Upsilon_{c,\operatorname{ord}_{p}}^{j,\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]
is an isomorphism, the target
\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]
is similarly a line. Since
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)
is 2-dimensional, it follows that for
r=0
and
r=\ell
, the cup product pairing

\mathrm{H}^{j}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\mathrm{St}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]\times\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times},L)\to\mathrm{H}^{j+1}(G(\mathbb{Q}),\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(v_{c}^{\mathrm{sm}}(L)^{\ast}))^{\varepsilon}[\pi^{p}]

described above must degenerate, in that there exists
\lambda\neq 0
such that
\langle\phi,\lambda\rangle=0
for all
\phi
. But then
\lambda\in\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
by definition.
∎

In
[
Geh21
, Conj. A]
, Gerhmann makes the following further prediction. Note that part (i) is known for
r=0
or
r=\ell
by Proposition
4.6
.

Conjecture 4.7
.

(i)

The space
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
has codimension exactly 1 (equivalently, dimension exactly 1) for any
c\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
, and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
.

(ii)

The space
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
is independent of both
r
and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
.

Finally, we translate the space
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
(which Gehrmann calls the automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant) into an actual quantity, similar to descriptions of
\mathcal{L}
-invariants in, say, the case of modular forms. The following definition is conditional in general, but unconditional for
r=0
or

.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
c\in\{1,...,n-1\}
,
0\leqslant r\leqslant\ell
and
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
. Assuming Conjecture

(i) holds for these parameters, the
automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariant for

and
\varepsilon
is the unique quantity
\mathcal{L}^{r,\varepsilon}_{\pi,c}\in L
satisfying

\mathcal{L}
-invariants

Fontaine–Mazur
\mathcal{L}
-invariants for
\pi
are constructed via the Galois representation
\rho_{\pi}
. We impose additional assumptions:

Hypothesis 4.9
.

We assume that
p>2n
and the residual representation
\overline{\rho}_{\pi}
is absolutely irreducible and decomposed generic.

If
\omega
denotes the
p

\rho_{\pi}|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\sim{\left(\begin{matrix}1&*_{1}&\dots&*&*\\
&\omega^{-1}&&*&*\\
&&\ddots&*_{n-2}&\vdots\\
&&&\omega^{2-n}&*_{n-1}\\
&&&&\omega^{1-n}\end{matrix}\right)}.

Definition 4.10
.

Let

Remark 4.11

If

– equation (

) holds, and

is non-crystalline for all

]
(noting that, in the language

., every weight is Shin-regular for odd

]
.

Let

5.
The automorphic Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula

Let
\pi
be as in §
4
. We now make crucial use of Theorem
A
. To ensure the required cohomological-multiplicity-one assumption, we must be more precise about the prime-to-
p
level. Specifically, in the rest of the paper we will take Whittaker new level

(5.1)

K^{p}=K_{1}^{p}(N)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p}):c\equiv 0\hskip 2.0pt(\mathrm{mod}\hskip 2.0ptN),d\equiv 1\hskip 2.0pt(\mathrm{mod}\hskip 2.0ptN)\},

where
N
is the smallest (prime-to-
p
) positive integer such that
\pi_{f}^{p}
admits
K_{1}^{p}(N)
-invariants. Here
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a block matrix with bottom row
(c,d)
, where
c
is a
1\times(n-1)
vector and
d
is a scalar.

Let
S
denote a finite set of places containing
p
and all primes
\ell
where
K_{\ell}\neq\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
. With this
S
, we take other notation from §
2
; so
\mathbb{T}(K)=\mathbb{T}^{S}(K^{p})\otimes\mathscr{A}_{p}^{+}
is the Hecke algebra of level
K
, and

\phi\colon\mathbb{T}(K)_{L}\longrightarrow L

is the classical cuspidal eigenpacket arising from
\pi_{f}^{K}
. The
p
-part
\phi_{p}=\phi|_{\mathscr{A}_{p}^{+}}
is trivial, since
\pi_{p}
is Steinberg; so
\phi
is
p
-ordinary, hence non-critical. Moreover, by
[
JPSS81
, §5]
, and since the Iwahori-invariants of the Steinberg representation are 1-dimensional, we have
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\pi_{f}^{K}=1
; so by Example
3.3
,
\phi
is of cohomological multiplicity one at level
K
at any admissible sign
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
.

Assumption 5.1
.

We assume throughout that the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture (Conjecture
2.4
) holds for
\phi
. Note that this is automatic for
\GL_{n}
with
n\leqslant 4
, since then
\ell(\GL_{n})\leqslant 1
(see Remark
2.5
).

By the assumption, for any
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
we may apply Theorem
A
at the weight
\lambda=1
, yielding a rigid Zariski-closed subspace
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathscr{W}
such that
\mathrm{H}^{q+\ell}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Sigma})^{\leqslant h,\varepsilon}_{\phi}
is free of rank one over
\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)_{1}
. Up to shrinking
\Sigma
there is a connected component
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathscr{X}_{G,K}^{\varepsilon}
such that

(5.2)

\mathrm{H}^{q+\ell}_{\mathrm{c}}(K,\mathscr{D}_{\Sigma})^{\leqslant h,\varepsilon}\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{\Sigma,h}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{C})\text{ is free of rank one over }\mathcal{O}(\Sigma).

The Hecke algebra acts on this space by a character
\phi_{\Sigma}=\phi^{S}_{\Sigma}\otimes\phi_{\Sigma,p}:\mathbb{T}(K)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)
. By construction, if
\mathfrak{m}_{1}\subset\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)
is the maximal ideal corresponding to
\lambda=1
, then the classical eigenpacket
\phi
factors as

\phi\colon\mathbb{T}(K)\xrightarrow{\ \phi_{\Sigma}\ }\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\ \hskip 2.0pt(\mathrm{mod}\hskip 2.0pt\mathfrak{m}_{1})\ }L.

The titular
automorphic Benois–Colmez–Greenberg–Stevens formula
studies the infinitesimal behaviour of
\phi_{\Sigma}
along a ‘good’ tangent vector in
\Sigma
at
\lambda=1
. To prove it, we use fundamentally the strategy of Gehrmann–Rosso from
[
GR22
]
, via the Koszul complex with overconvergent cohomology from
[
KS12
]
.

For clarity in later sections, we summarise our complete running set-up in the following.

Set-up 5.2
.

let
\pi
be a
p
-ordinary RACAR of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. (In particular

has trivial cohomological weight). Let

be the associated eigenpacket

. Assume that the description in (

) holds. From §
4
, for

, we have:

•

automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants
\mathbb{L}_{c}^{r,\varepsilon}(\pi)
for

,

Both are subspaces of the 2-dimensional

gives a Zariski-closed weight affinoid

5.1.
Weights, families and tangent vectors

5.1.1.
Infinitesimal weights

For
\GL_{n}
, the weight space
\mathscr{W}
is the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-rigid space whose
L
-points parametrise continuous characters
T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to L^{\times}
; that is, tuples
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{n})
, where each
\kappa_{i}
is a continuous character
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to L^{\times}
. It decomposes as the disjoint union of
n
-dimensional open discs. The connected component
\mathscr{W}^{\circ}\subset\mathscr{W}
containing
\lambda=1
is the rigid generic fibre of
\operatorname{Spf}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\!])
. It comes equipped with a universal character

\kappa^{\operatorname{univ}}=(\kappa^{\operatorname{univ}}_{1},\dots,\kappa^{\operatorname{univ}}_{n}):T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\!]^{\times},

where
\kappa_{i}^{\operatorname{univ}}(x)=(1+X_{i})^{\log_{p}(x)}
for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. For any Zariski-closed affinoid
\Lambda\subset\mathscr{W}^{\circ}
contained in this component, let

\kappa_{\Lambda}^{\operatorname{univ}}\colon T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathcal{O}(\Lambda)^{\times}

be the composition of
\kappa^{\operatorname{univ}}
with the natural map
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\!]\to\mathcal{O}(\Lambda)
.

By construction, we have an inclusion
\Sigma\subset\mathscr{W}_{L}
. After base-changing to
L
, we have the tangent space
\operatorname{T}_{1}(\mathscr{W}_{L})\cong L^{n}
at the trivial character, spanned linearly by
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. We thus have
\operatorname{T}_{1}(\Sigma)\subset L^{n}
.

Any
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\operatorname{T}_{1}(\Sigma)\subset\operatorname{T}_{1}(\mathscr{W}_{L})
corresponds to a morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(L[\epsilon])\to\Sigma\subset\mathscr{W}_{L}
, corresponding to a map

(5.3)

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]\!]\to\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)\to L[\epsilon],\qquad X_{i}\longmapsto v_{i}\epsilon\ \ \text{ (for }i=1,\dots,n)

that factors through
\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)
. Note in particular that
X_{i}^{2}\mapsto 0
for all
i
. Composing with
\kappa^{\operatorname{univ}}
, we get a ‘universal’ infinitesimal character in the direction of
v
, namely

\kappa_{v}^{\operatorname{univ}}=(\kappa_{v,1}^{\operatorname{univ}},\dots,\kappa_{v,n}^{\operatorname{univ}})\colon(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{n}\longrightarrow L[\epsilon]^{n},

where

(5.4)

\kappa_{v,i}^{\operatorname{univ}}(x)=1+\log_{p}(x)v_{i}\epsilon\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}.

Note in particular that
\kappa_{v}^{\operatorname{univ}}\equiv 1\hskip 2.0pt(\mathrm{mod}\hskip 2.0pt\epsilon)
.

5.1.2.
Infinitesimal families

Recall the eigenpacket
\phi
attached to
\pi
, and the eigenpacket

\phi_{\Sigma}=\phi_{\Sigma}^{S}\otimes\phi_{\Sigma,p}\colon\mathbb{T}(K)=\mathbb{T}^{S}(K)\otimes\mathscr{A}_{p}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)

for the family over
\Sigma
from (
5.2
).

Recall
\mathscr{A}_{p}^{+}
can be identified with the free commutative
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
, where

t_{i}=\operatorname{diag}(p,\dots,p,1,\dots,1)\in T(\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\qquad\text{with $i$ lots of $p$}.

Since the elements
t_{i}
(together with their inverses) generate
T(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, the character
\phi_{\Sigma,p}\colon\mathscr{A}_{p}^{+}\to\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)^{\times}
naturally defines a character
\Phi_{\Sigma,p}\colon T(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathcal{O}(\Sigma)^{\times}
. We freely lift this to a character on
T(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and write
\Phi_{\Sigma,p}=(\Phi_{\Sigma,p,1},\dots,\Phi_{\Sigma,p,n})
, where each
\Phi_{\Sigma,p,i}
is a character of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
(trivial on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
).

If
v\in\operatorname{T}_{1}(\Sigma)
, then composing

5.4.
The Tor spectral sequence for locally analytic principal series

To verify Gehrmann–Rosso’s criterion, we develop and study a Tor spectral sequence for locally analytic principal series representations. This is an analogue of Hansen’s spectral sequences, as used in the proof of Theorem
A
.

Let
R\to S
a finite map of
L
-affinoid algebras, and
\chi_{R}\colon T(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to(R^{+})^{\times}
a character. Let
M^{\bullet}_{R}
denote the complex where

M^{\bullet}_{R}=\wedge^{\bullet}_{R}R^{\oplus n-1}\otimes_{R}\operatorname{Ind}_{\operatorname{Iw}}^{G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})}(\mathbf{D}^{r}_{\chi_{R,0}}).

By Corollary
5.9

\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(\operatorname{I}_{\overline{B}}^{\operatorname{la}}(\chi_{R}))

To construct the Tor spectral sequence, we equate the usual tensor product with the derived tensor product, for which the following result is crucial. We expect there are many ways to prove this; we give a proof which uses condensed mathematics.

Lemma 5.10
.

Each term
\mathcal{A}_{G,c}(M_{R}^{\bullet})
is a flat
R
-module.

Proof.

We first claim that

is a (strongly countable) Fréchet module, namely a countable inverse limit of orthonormalisable Banach
R
-modules with dense transition maps. It suffices to prove this for

where

denotes functions with support in a compact open subspace

. Each term in the inverse limit is an orthonormalisable Banach
R
-module by taking the sup-norm over
C
.

It is easy to check that orthonormalisable Banach
R
-modules are
R
-flat. We claim strongly countable Fréchet modules are too. For this we consider the condensed
\underline{R}
-module
\underline{A}=\varprojlim_{n}\underline{A}_{n}

[

R
-modules

Recall the functor
(-)(*)
from condensed

-modules to
R
-modules given by evaluating at the point. This functor preserves colimits, is right-exact, and

for compactly generated topological spaces (see
[

). In particular, the morphism

factors

∎

\mathbb{T}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{T}(K)(R\Gamma(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon})
factors through
\mathbb{T}(K)(b,c;m)
. Moreover, this is compatible with the change of

and

.

(3)

The map
\mathbb{T}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{T}(K)(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{c}(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}_{\mathfrak{n}})
factors through
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Proof.

Part (2) is a direct consequence of the Hecke-equivariance in (1). To prove (3), it is enough to observe that from (2) the following map factors through
\mathcal{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(K)

\displaystyle\to\varprojlim_{m}\mathbb{T}(K)(R\Gamma(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}_{\mathfrak{n}})=\varprojlim_{m}\mathbb{T}(K)(R\Gamma_{c}(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}_{\mathfrak{n}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{T}(K)(R\Gamma_{c}(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}_{\mathfrak{n}})\to\mathbb{T}(K)(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{c}(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}_{\mathfrak{n}}).

Here we use that
\mathfrak{m}
is non-Eisenstein in the first equality, and that

is a perfect complex in the derived category of
\mathcal{O}^{+}(\Omega^{\circ})
-modules in the second one.

To prove (1), firstly because the ordinary part of the cohomology at level
\mathrm{Iw}(b,c)
is independent of

, we deduce that

(6.2)

R\Gamma(K,\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m}\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}\cong R\Gamma(Y(K(0,c)),\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r}/\mathrm{Fil}^{m}\bfD_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{\circ,r})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}.

Now let

be the natural map obtained from the universal character
\kappa_{\Omega^{\circ}}
. For sufficiently large

, we consider

in the natural way, restricting to the constant function

which is equivariant for the action of

[

Moreover, from the equality

, the fact that
\mathrm{Iw}(b,c)

R\Gamma(T(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),R\Gamma(N(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),(\mathcal{O}^{+}(\Omega^{\circ})/\mathfrak{a}^{m})(\kappa_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{-1}))^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon})\cong\\
R\Gamma(T(\mathbb{Z}_{p})/T(b),R\Gamma(Y(K^{p}\mathrm{Iw}(b,c)),\mathcal{O}_{L}/\varpi^{m})^{\mathrm{ord},\varepsilon}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{L}/\varpi^{m}}(\mathcal{O}^{+}(\Omega^{\circ})/\mathfrak{a}^{m})(\kappa_{\Omega^{\circ}}^{-1})).

7.
Symmetric power functoriality

In this final section, we discuss an application of our results to the symmetric power functoriality of automorphic
\mathcal{L}
-invariants. For this, let
f\in S_{2}^{\operatorname{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(Np),\chi_{f})
be a weight two newform of level
\Gamma_{1}(Np)
and nebentypus
\chi_{f}
, where
p\nmid N
. We assume that
a_{p}(f)=1
and the conductor of
\chi_{f}
is prime to
p
.

Let
\pi
be the (unitary) automorphic representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})
corresponding to
f
; our assumptions ensure that
\pi_{p}
is the Steinberg representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
\pi
is
p
-ordinary. Moreover,
\pi
is non-CM; CM automorphic representations of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})
, whose local Weil–Deligne representations have no monodromy, are locally unramified or supercuspidal (i.e., not Steinberg) at all finite primes.

7.1.
Symmetric power functoriality for Hida families

Let
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
denote the symmetric power lifting of
\pi
, as constructed by Newton–Thorne
[
NT21
]
. This is a RACAR of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A})
of trivial cohomological weight which is unramified outside
Np
, with the property that the Satake parameters away from
Np
match with
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}
of the Satake parameters of
\pi
. Since
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
is essentially self-dual, and by construction its associated semisimple Galois representation is
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\rho_{\pi}
, by known local-global compatibility results (see Remark
4.11
) we see that
\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi
must be Steinberg at
p
.

We now explain how to vary functoriality in families. Fix admissible signs
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{E}_{\pi}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{Sym}^{n-1}\pi}
; all constructions in this section will be with respect to these signs. Let
\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{W}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}
denote the unique Hida family passing through
f
in the
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
-eigenvariety (built from considering the Hecke operators away from
N
). Here, our conventions are that the point corresponding to
f
on
\mathscr{C}
has weight equal to the trivial character of
T_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. This Hida family is finite over the component of
\mathscr{W}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}
containing the trivial character. We say a point
x\in\mathscr{C}
is classical if the weight of
x
is locally algebraic, and the algebraic part is dominant. By Hida’s classicality theory, this implies that
x
is associated with a classical finite-slope ordinary cusp form
f_{x}
of level
\Gamma_{1}(Np^{r})
for some
r\geqslant 0
.

Consider the morphism of weight spaces
\iota\colon\mathscr{W}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}\to\mathscr{W}_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}
given by sending a weight
(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2})
to
(\kappa_{1}^{n-1},\kappa_{1}^{n-2}\kappa_{2},\dots,\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}^{n-2},\kappa_{2}^{n-1})
.

This is locally (on the source) an immersion.
Let

denote the compact open subgroup with

equal to the Whittaker new level of conductor
N
(defined in (

)). Let
S
denote the set of primes dividing

, and let

, where
\mathbf{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{n}}^{S}
denotes the spherical Hecke algebra away from

. Similarly, let
\mathbf{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(Np)=\mathbf{T}_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}^{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[T_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}^{+}]

Consider the morphism of tori

given by

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose the non-abelian Leopoldt conjecture holds for
