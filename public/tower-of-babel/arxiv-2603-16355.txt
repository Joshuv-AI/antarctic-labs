On the depth of the adjoint representations
Arindam Jana and Amiya Mondal

Abstract
. Let F F be a non-archimedean local field of odd residual characteristic p p . The depth of a smooth representation of GL n ​ ( F ) {\rm GL}_{n}(F) is an invariant of Local Langlands Correspondence (LLC). The analogous notion on the Galois side of LLC is known as the slope of a local Galois representation. The slope is well related to the Swan conductor for irreducible Galois representations, whereas its behavior is subtle for reducible Galois representations. In this article, we provide an explicit formula for the slope of the adjoint of a Carayol representation of the local Galois group.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field of odd residual characteristic
p
. Let
\bf G
be a connected reductive group defined over
F
with the group of
F
-rational points
G:={\bf G}(F)
. The (conjectural) Local Langlands Correspondence (LLC) for the group
G
is a finite to one map

{\rm Irr}(G)\longrightarrow\Phi(G);~~\pi\mapsto\phi_{\pi},

where
{\rm Irr}(G)
denotes the set of equivalence classes of all irreducible smooth representations of
G
and
\Phi(G)
denotes the set of equivalence classes of all Langlands parameters of
G
.

An important aspect in the smooth representation theory of
p
-adic groups is the study of the conductor and the depth of smooth representations. It is natural to ask if the LLC preserves conductor and depth.

The conductor of a smooth representation is defined via the
\varepsilon
-factor associated to the local functional equation. It is known that the conductor is preserved under LLC for
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
. The corresponding conductor on the Galois side is known as the Artin conductor. The Swan conductor is also an invariant closely related to the Artin conductor.

Hence, it is of interest to investigate the behaviour of the Artin and Swan conductor under functoriality which is one of the pillars of the Langlands Program. For given irreducible supercuspidal representations
\pi_{i}
of
{\rm GL}_{n_{i}}(F)
for
i=1,2
, the Rankin-Selberg lift
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
to the group
{\rm GL}_{n_{1}n_{2}}(F)
corresponds to the tensor product
\phi_{\pi_{1}}\otimes\phi_{\pi_{2}}
under the LLC. The pioneering work of Bushnell-Henniart-Kutzko studies the conductor of the Rankin-Selberg lift (see
[
6
, Theorem 6.5, p. 723]
). Using this result, the conductor of other functorial lifts, namely, symmetric square, exterior square and Asai lifts have been studied in
[
1
,
2
]
. Although an explicit conductor formula on the Galois side of the correspondence might be a bit more involved, sharp bound of these invariants have been studied using purely Galois-theoretic techniques (see
[
5
,
14
,
12
]
for reference).

The depth
d(\pi)
of an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of a reductive
p
-adic group
G
was defined by Moy-Prasad
[
16
,
17
]
in terms of filtrations of its parahoric subgroups. On the other hand, under the (conjectural) local Langlands correspondence (LLC), one can define the depth
d(\phi_{\pi})
of the Langlands parameter
\phi_{\pi}
of
\pi
using the ramification filtrations of the absolute Galois group
\rm Gal(F_{s}/F)
of
F
(see
[
3
, p. 18]
).
It is an active area of research whether depth is preserved under the LLC.
This means, one would like to know if

d(\pi)=d(\phi_{\pi}),~{\rm for}~\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(G).

This is proved in many cases. In particular, this is known for the group
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)

[
18
, Theorem 2.15, p. 541]
(originally
[
21
, 2.3.6]
) and
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Along the same line, one would like to study the behaviour of depth under functorial lifts on
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
. For irreducible supercuspidal representations
\pi_{i}
of
{\rm GL}_{n_{i}}(F)
for
i=1,2
, it is known that

d(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})\leq\max\{d(\pi_{1}),d(\pi_{2})\}.

Moreover, if
d(\pi_{1})\neq d(\pi_{2})
, then
d(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})=\max\{d(\pi_{1}),d(\pi_{2})\}
(see
[
14
, Proposition 3.4, p. 21]
).

In this article, we are interested in the depth of the adjoint lift. More precisely, for a Carayol representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
, we find the depth
d(\pi\times\pi^{\vee})
, where
\pi^{\vee}
denotes the contragredient or smooth dual of
\pi
. The notion of slope
{\rm sl}(\rho)
of a Galois representation
\rho
is defined in (
[
10
, §(3.2.1)]
,
[
14
, Definition 3.1, p. 20]
).
Comparing this definition with that of depth as in
[
3
, p. 18]
), it follows that

d(\phi_{\pi})={\rm sl}(\phi_{\pi}),

for any Langlands parameter
\phi_{\pi}
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
. With this understanding, our treatment is fully on the Galois side of the LLC. From now onwards, we use the notation

{\rm sl}(\rho)
for the depth a Galois representation
\rho
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
be the set of equivalence classes of smooth, irreducible complex representations of the Weil group
\mathcal{W}_{F}
associated to
F
. A representation
\rho\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}_{F}}
is said to be
Carayol
if
({\rm Sw}(\rho),\dim\,\rho)=1

[
14
, §7]
. The representation
\rho
is called
epipelagic
if in particular
{\rm Sw}(\rho)=1
.

It turns out that all Carayol representation are tamely irreducible (
[
14
, Proposition 7.2]
,
[
8
]
), i.e., restriction of
\rho
on the inertia remains irreducible. However, sometimes in the literature, e.g., in
[
10
]
, Carayol representations are defined on the class of wildly irreducible representations, i.e., restriction of
\rho
on the wild inertia is irreducible. Notice that every wildly irreducible representation is tamely irreducible. In this paper we work on the larger class of Carayol representations as in
[
14
]
which are tamely but not necessarily wildly irreducible.

Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. We find an explicit formula for the slope of the adjoint representation
{\rm Ad}(\rho):=\rho\otimes{\rho}^{\vee}
. Our method uses the ramification filtration of the Weil group
\mathcal{W}_{F}
and its associated Herbrand functions. The first main theorem of this article is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
mp^{r}
where
(p,m)=1
. If
m>1
, then
{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)
.

Using the LLC, it follows from Theorem
1.1
that
d(\pi\times\pi^{\vee})=d(\pi)
for a Carayol representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}(F)
where
n=mp^{r}
with
m>1
. On the other hand, if
m=1
, then we have obtained an explicit formula for
d(\pi\times\pi^{\vee})
which shows that
d(\pi\times\pi^{\vee})<d(\pi)
. To explain the result, we shift to the Galois side of the LLC.

Let
\rho
be a Carayol Galois representation of dimension
p^{r}
. Before we describe the general result on
{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})
, let us mention the following theorem which describes the slope of the adjoint of a Carayol representation, wildly induced from a character (see Theorem
4.4
for details).
This result lies in the heart of the second main theorem (Theorem
1.4
) to follow. For convenience, from now on we denote
{\rm Ind}_{K/F}(~):={\rm Ind}_{\mathcal{W}_{K}}^{\mathcal{W}_{F}}(~)
for an extension
K/F
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
,
r>1
. Suppose
\rho={\rm Ind}_{K/F}\chi
for some totally ramified Galois extension
K/F
such that
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
is cyclic and a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{i_{0}}{p^{r}},

where
i_{0}
is the smallest lower jump in
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
.

The central idea of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is an explicit understanding of the lower and upper jumps of the Herbrand functions associated to cyclic extensions which has been carried out in Section
4.1
. Using these techniques for the cyclic extensions, we further extend theorem
1.2
for arbitrary extensions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
,
r>1
. Suppose
\rho={\rm Ind}_{K/F}\chi
for some totally ramified Galois extension
K/F
and a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{i_{0}}{p^{r}},

where
i_{0}
is the smallest lower jump in
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
.

However, Carayol representations need not be induced from characters in general, for example, epipelagic representations of dimension
p^{r}
. The good thing is that any Carayol representation can be constructed from a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
for some extension
K/F
through certain inductions and restrictions (see Section
5
). The following theorem describes the slope of the adjoint of any Carayol representation.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})}{\dim\,\rho}\,,

where
i_{0}
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
for the finite Galois extension
K/F
.

Since epipelagic representations form an important subclass of Carayol representations, let us write the slope of adjoint of an epipelagic representation as a corollary to Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be an epipelagic representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
.

(1)

If
m>1
, then
{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=\dfrac{1}{\dim\,\rho}
.

(2)

If
m=1
, then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=\dfrac{1-\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})}{\dim\,\rho},

where
i_{0}
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
{\rm Gal}(K/F)
for the finite Galois extension
K/F
.

Let us briefly mention the contents of the article. In section
2
, we define the notion of the slope of a Galois representation and prove some basic properties of slope. In Section
3
, we describe some generalities on Herbrand functions. In Section
4
, we prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. Finally in Section
5
, we establish the slope formula in general (the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
).

2.
Slope of a Galois representation

We start with the definition of the slope of a Weil representation. Let
F
be a
p
-adic field of residue characteristic
p\neq 2
and let
\mathcal{W}_{F}
denote the associated Weil group. Let
\{\mathcal{W}_{F}^{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq-1}}
denote the upper ramification filtration of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
with
\mathcal{W}_{F}^{-1}=\mathcal{W}_{F}
and
\mathcal{W}_{F}^{0}=\mathcal{I}_{F}
denote the inertia subgroup of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. For
r\geq 0
,
the subgroups
\mathcal{W}_{F}^{r}

are profinite, closed and normal in
\mathcal{W}_{F}
.
Let

\mathcal{P}_{F}
denote the
wild inertia
of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
.

Let
\rho
be a finite dimensional smooth representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. There exists a finite Galois extension
E
of
F
such that
\rho|_{\mathcal{W}_{F}^{0}}
factors through the inertia subgroup
G^{0}
of
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Let
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}}
and
\{G^{r}\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq-1}}
be the lower and upper ramification filtrations of
G

[
19
, Chapter IV]
. We say that
G
has a lower jump at
l\geq-1
if
G_{l}\neq G_{l+1}
. Also, we say that
G
has an
upper jump at
u\geq-1
if there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
G^{u}\neq G^{u+\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon>0
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
, (3.2.1)]
,
[
14
, Definition 3.1, p. 20]

Let
\rho
be a finite dimensional smooth representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. Then the slope of
\rho
is defined by

{\rm sl}(\rho):={\rm inf}\{r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid\rho\big|_{\mathcal{W}_{F}^{r}}=1\}.

In this connection, let us recall the definition of the Swan conductor of the representation
\rho
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\rho,V)
be a finite dimensional smooth representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. Then the Swan conductor
of
\rho
is defined by

{\rm Sw}(\rho):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,{\rm dim}\,(V/{V^{u}})\,du,

where
V^{u}
denotes the subspace of
\mathcal{W}_{F}^{u}
-invariant vectors.

For any representation
\rho
of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
, we have
{\rm sl}(\rho)\geq\frac{{\rm Sw}(\rho)}{\dim\rho}
. In particular, when
\rho
is irreducible, it follows that
{\rm Sw}(\rho)={\rm sl}(\rho)\cdot{\rm dim}\,(\rho)

[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
.

We recall the definition of the Herbrand function.

Definition 2.3
.

[
19
, §IV.3]

Let
E
be a finite extension of
F
with ramification index
e(E/F)
. The Herbrand function
\phi_{E/F}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
associated to
E/F
is given by

\phi_{E/F}(t)=1/{e(E/F)}\,\int_{0}^{t}\mid G_{x}\mid dx.

Now we define conjugate representation. Let
E/F
be a finite Galois extension and
\tau
be a representation of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Then
W_{E}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
and
\mathcal{W}_{F}/\mathcal{W}_{E}\simeq{\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Thus every
\lambda\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
has a lift in
\mathcal{W}_{F}
, which we again denote by
\lambda
. Fixing such a
\lambda
, we define a conjugate representation
\tau^{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
by

\tau^{\lambda}(x)=\tau(\lambda x\lambda^{-1})

for
x\in\mathcal{W}_{E}
. One can check that the slope of a Galois representation is invariant under Galois conjugate and its dual. Thus for a finite Galois extension
E/F
and an irreducible representation
\tau
of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
, we have

{\rm sl}(\tau)={\rm sl}(\tau^{\lambda})={\rm sl}((\tau^{\lambda})^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\tau^{\vee})

for
\lambda\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)
.

The following proposition says that the slopes of the induced and inducing representations are related by the associated Herbrand function.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
15
, Proposition 2]

Let
F\subset E\subset K
be finite Galois extensions with
G(K/F):={\rm Gal}(K/F)
and
G(K/E):={\rm Gal}(K/E)
. Let
\tau
be a representation of
G(K/E)
. Then

{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{G(K/E)}^{G(K/F)}\tau)=\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\tau)),

where
\phi_{E/F}
is the Herbrand function associated to the extension
E/F
.

The behavior of the slope of a representation under the tame induction and restriction is given in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
E/F
be a finite totally tamely ramified Galois extension with ramification index
e
. Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be finite dimensional representations of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
and
\mathcal{W}_{E}
respectively, then

(1)

{\rm sl}(\sigma\mid_{{\mathcal{W}_{E}}})=e\cdot{\rm sl}(\sigma),

(2)

{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{E/F}\tau)=\dfrac{1}{e}\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau).

Proof.

We denote
G=\mathcal{W}_{F}
and
H=\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Then
G/H\simeq{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, the Galois group of
E/F
. Since
E/F
is finite and tamely ramified, we have
G^{u}=H^{eu}
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(cf.
[
9
, Lemma 1]
).

We first prove
(1)
. Let
{\rm sl}(\sigma)=u
. Then
\sigma\mid_{H^{eu+e\epsilon}}=\sigma\mid_{G^{u+\epsilon}}=1
for all
\epsilon>0
. This shows that
{\rm sl}(\sigma\mid_{H})\leq eu=e\cdot{\rm sl}(\sigma)
. Conversely, let
{\rm sl}(\sigma\mid_{H})=u
. For any
\epsilon>0,
we have

\sigma\mid_{G^{u/e+\epsilon/e}}=\sigma\mid_{H^{u+\epsilon}}=1
, which gives
{\rm sl}(\sigma)\leq u/e
, i.e.,
e\cdot{\rm sl}(\sigma)\leq u={\rm sl}(\sigma\mid_{H})
.
Thus
(1)
follows.

The proof of
(2)
follows from Proposition
2.4
, by observing that
\phi_{E/F}(x)=x/e
for a finite tamely ramified extension
E/F
.
∎

The following lemma describes the behavior of the slope of the adjoint representation under the tame restriction.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
E/F
be a finite tamely ramified extension with the ramification index
e
. Let
\tau
be a representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
. Then
{\rm sl}(\tau\otimes\tau^{\vee})=1/e\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{E}}\otimes(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{E}})^{\vee})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
E/F
be a finite Galois extension. Let
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\chi
be an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
where
\chi
be a character of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
.
Then the largest lower jump in
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
is bounded above by
{\rm sl}(\chi)
.

Proof.

Since
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\chi
, there exists a finite Galois extension
K/F
such that
\rho={\rm Ind}_{H}^{G}\chi
where
G={\rm Gal}(K/F)
and
H={\rm Gal}(K/E)
. Then we have
{\rm sl}(\rho)=\phi_{K/F}(m)
and
{\rm sl}(\chi)=\phi_{K/E}(n)
where
m
and
n
are the largest lower jumps in
G
and
H
respectively
[
12
, §2.1.2, p. 156]
. Taking
\tau=\chi
in Proposition
2.4
, we have

\phi_{K/F}(m)={\rm sl}(\rho)=\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\chi))=\phi_{E/F}(\phi_{K/E}(n))=\phi_{K/F}(n).

This gives
m=n
. By
[
19
, Lemma 5]
we have

{\rm Gal}(E/F)_{\phi_{K/E}(n)}=(G/H)_{\phi_{K/E}(n)}=G_{n}H/H.

For
\epsilon>0
arbitrary, we have

{\rm Gal}(E/F)_{\phi_{K/E}(n)+\epsilon}={\rm Gal}(E/F)_{\phi_{K/E}(n+\delta)}=G_{n+\delta}H/H=G_{m+\delta}H/H=1.

Thus the largest lower jump of
G(E/F)
must be less than or equal to
\phi_{K/E}(n)={\rm sl}(\chi)
.
∎

3.
Herbrand functions

Let
E/F
be a finite separable extension. Suppose
E/F
is Galois, then the function
\psi_{E/F}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is defined by (cf.
[
19
, Chapter IV, p. 74]
):

\psi_{E/F}(x):=\int_{0}^{x}[G^{0}:G^{t}]~dt.

If
E/F
is not Galois, take a Galois extension
E_{1}/F
with
E\subset E_{1},
define (cf.
[
10
, (1.1.1)]
)

\psi_{E/F}=\psi_{E_{1}/E}^{-1}\circ\psi_{E_{1}/F}.

Then
\psi_{E/F}
is well-defined. Indeed, let
E_{2}/F
be an another Galois extension with
E\subset E_{2}
.
We denote
\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}=\psi_{E_{2}/E}^{-1}\circ\psi_{E_{2}/F}
. Since the composition of Galois extensions is Galois and
E_{1}E_{2}\supset E
, we have

\psi_{E/F}^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E}^{-1}\circ\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/F}
, which by using the fact that
\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E}=\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E_{1}}\circ\psi_{E_{1}/E}
and
\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/F}=\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E_{1}}\circ\psi_{E_{1}/F}
, equals

\psi_{E/F}
. Again, using
\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E}=\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E_{2}}\circ\psi_{E_{2}/E}
and
\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/F}=\psi_{E_{1}E_{2}/E_{2}}\circ\psi_{E_{2}/F}
, we have
\psi_{E/F}^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}
. Thus we have
\psi_{E/F}=\psi_{E/F}^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
10
, Definition, p. 1968]

Let
E/F
be a finite separable extension. We say that
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a jump of
\psi_{E/F}
, if the derivative
\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}
is not continuous at
a
.

Following
[
10
, p. 1971]
, for any
x>0
we define

(3.1)

\displaystyle w_{x}(E/F):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\dfrac{\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}(x+\epsilon)}{\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}(x-\epsilon)}.

Remark 1
.

Note that
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a jump of
\psi_{E/F}
if and only if
w_{x}(E/F)>1
.

Although it should be well-understood, in the following lemma, we prove that
jumps in a cyclic Galois group of order
p^{r}
coincide with that of the associated Herbrand function.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
E/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
p^{r}
and let
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
.
The set of jumps of
\psi_{E/F}
is same as the set of upper jumps in

G
as defined in §
2
.

Proof.

Since
G=\mathbb{Z}/{p^{r}\mathbb{Z}}
, by
[
19
, Chapter IV, p. 76]
, there exist positive integers
i_{0},\dots,i_{r-1}
such that

J=\{j_{k}=i_{0}+\dots+i_{k-1}\mid 1\leq k\leq r\}

be the set of all upper jumps in
G
with
|G^{j_{k}}|=p^{r-(k-1)}
for
1\leq k\leq r
.
For
j_{k}\leq x\leq j_{k+1}
, a short computation shows that

\psi_{E/F}(x)=p^{k}x-\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}i_{l}(p^{k}-p^{l}).

Then by (
3.1
), we have

w_{j_{k}}(E/F)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\dfrac{\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}(j_{k}+\epsilon)}{\psi_{E/F}^{\prime}(j_{k}-\epsilon)}=p^{k}/p^{k-1}=p>1,

which by Remark
1
shows that

\psi_{E/F}
has a jump at
j_{k}
. On the other hand, for
j_{k}<x<j_{k+1}
, by (
3.1
) one sees that
w_{x}(E/F)=p^{k}/p^{k}=1
and hence, by Remark
1
we have
\psi_{E/F}
has no jump at
x
.
Thus all jumps of
\psi_{E/F}
occur at
j_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq r
which are exactly the jumps of
G
.
∎

The next proposition says that for large values of
x
, the Herbrand function
\psi_{E/F}(x)
is easier to understand.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
10
, Proposition, p. 1970]

Let
E/F
be a separable and totally wildly ramified extension of degree
p^{r}
with the largest upper jump
u
. Let
w_{E/F}
be the wild exponent of
E/F
. Then, for
x\geq u
, we have

\psi_{E/F}(x)=p^{r}x-w_{E/F}.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
E/F
be a totally wildly ramified extension of degree
n
. Let
J=\{j_{i}\mid j_{i}<j_{i+1},1\leq i\leq m-1\}
be the set of all jumps of
\psi_{E/F}
. Then, there exists a unique sequence of fields
F=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\dots\subset E_{m}=E
such that

\psi_{E/F}(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if}~x\leq j_{1},\\
\prod_{i=1}^{k}w_{j_{i}}x-w_{E_{k}/F}&\text{if}~j_{k}\leq x\leq j_{k+1},\end{cases}

where
w_{j_{i}}
is the degree of the extension
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
and
w_{E_{k}/F}
is the wild exponent of
E_{k}/F
.

Proof.

This can be proved by
[
10
, Corollary 1]
and repeated use of Proposition
3.3
together with
[
10
, Lemma (2), p. 1970]
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Let
E/F
be a totally wildly ramified extension of degree
n
. Let
\phi_{E/F}
denote the inverse of
\psi_{E/F}
in Proposition
3.4
. Then we have

\phi_{E/F}(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if}~x\leq j_{1},\\
\dfrac{x}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}w_{j_{i}}}+\dfrac{w_{E_{k}/F}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}w_{j_{i}}}&\text{if}~j_{k}\leq x\leq j_{k+1}.\end{cases}

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
E/F
be a totally ramified cyclic extension of degree
p^{r}
. Then there exist positive integers
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{r-1}
with
j_{k}=i_{0}+i_{1}+\dots+i_{k-1}
for
1\leq k\leq r
such that

\phi_{E/F}(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if}~x\leq j_{1},\\
\dfrac{x}{p^{k}}+\dfrac{w_{E_{k}/F}}{p^{k}}&\text{if}~j_{k}\leq x\leq j_{k+1}\end{cases}.

Proof.

Since
E/F
is a totally wild cyclic Galois extension of of degree
p^{r}
, by
[
19
, Chapter IV, p. 76]
and Lemma
3.2
, jumps of
\psi_{E/F}
occur at
r
distinct positive integers,
j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{r}
(say). By
[
10
, Corollary 1]
, there exist unique sequence of fields
F=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\dots\subset E_{r}=E
with
w_{j_{i}}=[E_{i}:E_{i-1}]=p
for
1\leq i\leq r-1
such that
\psi_{E_{i}/E_{i-1}}
has a unique jump
\psi_{E_{i-1}/F}(j_{i})
. Now taking
w_{j_{i}}=p
for each
i
in Remark
2
the result follows.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

In this section, we compute the slope of the adjoint representation of a Carayol representation induced from a character and of dimension
p^{r}
.

4.1.
Some lemmas

This subsection consists of a few lemmas which will be essential in proving the theorems.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
E/F
be a finite Galois extension. We have

\min_{\lambda\in{\rm Gal}(E/F)}\,v_{E}(\lambda)=i_{0}+1,

where
i_{0}
is the smallest lower jump of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
.

Proof.

We write
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Since
i_{0}
is the smallest lower jump of
G
, we have
v_{E}(\lambda)\geq i_{0}+1
for all
\lambda\in G
. This shows that
\min_{\lambda\in G}\,v_{E}(\lambda)\geq i_{0}+1
. This minimum is attained at
\lambda\in G_{i_{0}}\backslash G_{i_{0}+1}
and hence, the lemma follows.
∎

The notations and the proof of the next lemma are inspired by
[
14
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E/F
be a totally ramified cyclic Galois extension of degree
p^{r}
with Galois group
G={\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Let
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\chi
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
, where
\chi
denotes a character of
W_{E}
. Let
\lambda\in G\setminus\{\rm id\}
. Let
\varsigma={\rm Sw}(\chi)
and
\delta=v_{E}(\lambda)-1
with
\varsigma>\delta
. Then, we have
\varsigma\not\equiv\delta\mod p
.

Proof.

Let
\varsigma>\delta
.
We show that
\varsigma\not\equiv\delta\mod p
.
Note that
E/F
is a totally wild cyclic Galois extension of degree
p^{r}
and, therefore by
[
19
, Example, p. 76]
, the lower jumps
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{r}
are all congruent to each other modulo
p
. So there exists an integer
n
such that
l_{j}\equiv n\mod p
for all
j
.
By
[
10
, Lemma (2), p. 1970]
, we have

{\rm Sw}({\rm Ind}_{E/F}\,\chi)={\rm Sw}(\chi)+w_{E/F},

where
w_{E/F}
denotes the wild exponent of
E/F
. This further using
[
14
, Lemma 1.2 (iii)]
gives

{\rm Sw}({\rm Ind}_{E/F}\,\chi)={\rm Sw}(\chi)+\sum_{\lambda\in G\backslash\{\rm id\}}({v_{E}}(\lambda)-1).

But each
v_{E}(\lambda)-1=l_{j}
for some
j
and
l_{j}\equiv n\mod p
for all
\lambda\in G\setminus\{\rm id\}
. Also, note that the cardinality of
G\setminus\{\rm id\}
is
p^{r}-1
.
Thus modulo
p
, the above equation becomes

{\rm Sw}({\rm Ind}_{E/F}\,\chi)\equiv{{\rm Sw}}(\chi)-n.

Since by our assumption
\rm Ind_{E/F}\chi
is Carayol, we have
{\rm Sw}(\chi)\not\equiv n\mod p
. Again, for each
\lambda\in G\setminus\{\rm id\}
, the number
v_{E}(\lambda)-1
being some
l_{j}
for some
j
, is congruent to
n
modulo
p
. Thus we conclude that

{\rm Sw}(\chi)\not\equiv v_{E}(\lambda)-1\mod p
for all
\lambda\in G\setminus\{\rm id\}
. ∎

Let
E/F
be a cyclic Galois extension of degree
p^{r}
. We denote
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Recall that

\{j_{m}\mid j_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}i_{k-1},~1\leq m\leq r\}~~\&~~\{l_{m}\mid l_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}i_{k-1}p^{k-1},~1\leq m\leq r\}

denote the upper and lower jumps in
G

respectively, where
i_{m}
are positive integers (cf.
[
19
, Chapter IV, p. 76]
).

With these notations, we have the following important lemma which says that the difference between the largest and smallest lower jumps is bigger than the largest upper jump.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have
l_{r}-l_{1}\geq j_{r}
for
r\geq 2
and
p\neq 2
.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Let
e_{F}
be the absolute ramification index of
E
, i.e.,
v_{F}(p)=e_{F}
.
We analyze
l_{r}-l_{1}=l_{r}-i_{0}
in two different cases.

Case 1:
Suppose that
l_{r-1}\geq p^{r-2}e_{F}/(p-1)
. Then by
[
11
, Exercise 5.(f), p. 95]
, we have
l_{r}=l_{r-1}+p^{r-1}e_{F}
. Since
l_{r}=l_{r-1}+i_{r-1}p^{r-1}
, we have
i_{r-1}=e_{F}
. Also we note that
j_{1}=i_{0}\leq\dfrac{pe_{F}}{p-1}
(cf.
[
20
, p. 140, l. -4]
). We have

l_{r}-i_{0}=\sum_{k=2}^{r}i_{k-1}p^{k-1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle(l_{r}-i_{0})-j_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{r}i_{k-1}p^{k-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{r}i_{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{r-1}i_{k-1}(p^{k-1}-1)+i_{r-1}(p^{r-1}-1)-i_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e_{F}(p^{r-1}-1)-\dfrac{pe_{F}}{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{F}(p^{r-1}-1-\dfrac{p}{p-1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0

for
r\geq 2
and
p\neq 2
.

Case 2
: Suppose
l_{r-1}\leq p^{r-2}e_{F}/(p-1)
. Then by
[
11
, Exercise 5.(f), p. 95]
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle(1+p(p-1))l_{r-1}\leq l_{r}\leq p^{r}e_{F}/(p-1)-(p-1)l_{r-1}.

Note that,
l_{r}-i_{0}=\sum_{k=2}^{r}i_{k-1}p^{k-1}
and
j_{r}=i_{0}+i_{1}+\dots+i_{r-1}
.
Thus

\displaystyle l_{r}-i_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle j_{r}

\displaystyle\iff i_{1}p+i_{2}p^{2}+\dots+i_{r-1}p^{r-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle i_{0}+i_{1}+\dots+i_{r-1}

\displaystyle\iff(l_{2}-l_{1})+(l_{3}-l_{2})+\dots+(l_{r}-l_{r-1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle l_{1}+(l_{2}-l_{1})/p+\dots+(l_{r}-l_{r-1})/p^{r-1}

which is true if and only if

(4.2)

\displaystyle(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-1/p)+(l_{3}-l_{2})(1-1/p^{2})+\dots+(l_{r}-l_{r-1})(1-1/p^{r-1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle l_{1}

We now analyze the left hand side of the above inequality. Note that
by the left inequality in (
4.1
),
we have
l_{r}-l_{r-1}\geq p(p-1)l_{r-1}
. Using this, we have

\displaystyle(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-1/p)+\dots+(l_{r}-l_{r-1})(1-1/p^{r-1})\geq

(4.3)

\displaystyle(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-1/p)+\dots+(l_{r-1}-l_{r-2})(1-1/p^{r-2})+p(p-1)l_{r-1}(1-1/p^{r-1}).

Observe that the coefficient of
l_{r-1}
in (
4.1
) is

1-1/p^{r-2}+p^{2}-p-1/p^{r-3}+1/p^{r-2}=1-1/p^{r-3}+p^{2}(1-1/p)=(1-1/p)(1+1/p+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2}).

Taking out a factor of
(1-1/p)

from
the right hand side of the inequality in
(
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle(l_{2}-l_{1})+(l_{3}-l_{2})(1+1/p)+(l_{4}-l_{3})(1+1/p+1/p^{2})+\dots

\displaystyle+(l_{r-2}-l_{r-3})(1+1/p+\dots+1/p^{r-4})-l_{r-2}(1+1/p+\dots+1/p^{r-3})

(4.4)

\displaystyle~~~~+l_{r-1}(1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2}),

which by writing
l_{r-1}=l_{r-2}+i_{r-2}p^{r-2}
, equals

\displaystyle-l_{1}-l_{2}/p-\dots-l_{r-3}/p^{r-4}-l_{r-2}/p^{r-3}

\displaystyle~~~~~~+(l_{r-2}+i_{r-2}p^{r-2})(1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2})

\displaystyle=-l_{1}-l_{2}/p-\dots-l_{r-4}/p^{r-5}-l_{r-3}/p^{r-4}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~+l_{r-2}(-1/p^{r-3}+1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~+i_{r-2}p^{r-2}(1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2}).

Continuing this process, after a finite number of steps the last expression equals

\displaystyle l_{1}(p^{2}-1/p^{r-3})+i_{1}p(-1/p^{r-3}+1+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+i_{2}p^{2}(-1/p^{r-3}+1+1/p+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+i_{3}p^{3}(-1/p^{r-3}+1+1/p+1/p^{2}+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+i_{4}p^{4}(-1/p^{r-3}+1+1/p+1/p^{2}+1/p^{3}+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\dots

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+i_{r-3}p^{r-3}(-1/p^{r-3}+1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+i_{r-2}p^{r-2}(1+1/p+1/p^{2}+\dots+1/p^{r-4}+p^{2})

(4.5)

\displaystyle=l_{1}(p^{2}-1/p^{r-3})+(*),

where
(*)
is a positive number.

Now
by (
4.1
), (
4.1
) and (
4.1
), it follows that

(l_{2}-l_{1})(1-1/p)++\dots+(l_{r}-l_{r-1})(1-1/p^{r-1})\geq l_{1}(1-1/p)(p^{2}-1/p^{r-3})+(*)\geq l_{1},

which is (
4.2
).
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
. For convenience we restate it below.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
with
r>1
. Suppose

\rho=\rm Ind_{E/F}\,\chi
for some totally ramified Galois extension
E/F
of degree
p^{r}
such that
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
is cyclic and a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Let
i_{0}

denote the smallest lower jump of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\frac{i_{0}}{p^{r}}.

Before we proceed with the proof, here are some remarks.

Remark 3
.

By the definition of Carayol representation, any one dimensional representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
with positive Swan conductor
is Carayol. In Theorem
4.4
, the character
\chi
is Carayol (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 7.2 (ii)]
) and hence,
{\rm Sw}(\chi)>0
.

Remark 4
.

In Theorem
4.4
, the extension
E/F
is totally and wildly ramified. So, by writing
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
, we have
G=G_{0}=G_{1}
, and hence
i_{0}>0
. Thus
{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})<{\rm sl}(\rho)
which is consistent with
[
14
, Proposition 3.4 (2)]
.

Remark 5
.

We check that

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})\geq{\rm Sw}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})/\,\dim(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee}),

which shows that Theorem
4.4
is consistent with
[
14
, Proposition 3.4 (iv)]
.

Proof of Remark
5
.

Since
\dim\,\rho=p^{r}
, it is enough to show that
p^{2r}{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})\geq{\rm Sw}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})
.
By Theorem
4.4
and
[
14
, Theorem 7.3]
, we have

\displaystyle p^{2r}{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})-{\rm Sw}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{2r}\left({\rm sl}(\rho)-\frac{i_{0}}{p^{r}}\right)-(p^{r}-1){\rm Sw}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{r}\left({\rm sl}(\rho)-i_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{r}(\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\chi))-i_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p^{r}(\phi_{E/F}(l_{r})-i_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{r}(j_{r}-i_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

The fourth inequality follows from Lemma
2.7
. Finally, since
E/F
is cyclic and Galois and
l_{r}
is the largest lower jump of
G
, a small computation shows that
\phi_{E/F}(l_{r})=j_{r}=i_{0}+i_{1}+\dots+i_{r-1}
, the largest upper jump of
G
with
i_{j}
are strictly positive integers
[
19
, Example, p. 76]
. Thus the last equality follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We denote
G:={\rm Gal}(E/F)
. By Mackey decomposition, we have

\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee}=\rm Ind_{E/F}\chi\otimes\rm Ind_{E/F}\chi^{-1}=\rm Ind_{E/F}\,\bigoplus_{\lambda\in G}\,\chi^{\lambda-1}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in G}\,\rm Ind_{E/F}\,\chi^{\lambda-1}.

Here, by the local class field theory, we assume
\chi
to be a character of
E^{\times}
and
\chi^{\lambda-1}:E^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is defined by

\chi^{\lambda-1}(x)=\chi(\dfrac{\lambda(x)}{x})
.
Hence
\chi^{\lambda-1}=\chi^{\lambda}\chi^{-1}
.

Now, by
[
14
, Proposition 3.4, p. 21]
we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\max}_{\lambda\in G}\,{\rm sl}(\rm Ind_{E/F}\,\chi^{\lambda-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\max}_{\lambda\in G}\,\phi_{E/F}\left({\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}\left({\max}_{\lambda\in G}~{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})\right).

The second equality above follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
. Since
\phi_{E/F}
is an increasing function, the third equality holds.

We now find

{\max}_{\lambda\in G}~{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1}).

For
\lambda\in G
, writing
\delta=v_{E}(\lambda)-1
and
\varsigma={\rm sl}(\chi)
, by

[
14
, Lemma 4.5]
we have if
\varsigma\leq\delta
, then
{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})={\rm Sw}(\chi^{\lambda-1})=0
. Since we are interested in the maximum of
{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})
, it is enough to concentrate on those
\lambda\in G
for which
\varsigma>\delta
. Since
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\chi
is Carayol, for all
\lambda\in G
with
\varsigma>\delta
, by Lemma
4.2
and

[
14
, Lemma 4.5]
we have

{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})=\varsigma-\delta={\rm sl}(\chi)-(v_{E}(\lambda)-1).

Thus by Lemma
4.1
, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle{\max}_{\lambda\in G}~{\rm sl}(\chi^{\lambda-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0},

where
i_{0}
is the smallest lower jump in
G
.
Thus by (
4.6
) and
4.7
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}).

Note that by Lemma
4.3
, together with Lemma
2.7
we have
{\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}\geq j_{r}
for
r\geq 2
. Therefore, by Corollary
3.5
, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{p^{r}}\left({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}+w\right),

where
w
denotes the wild exponent of
E/F
.

Since
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\chi
is irreducible of dimension
p^{r}
, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Sw}(\rho)/\dim\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/p^{r}({\rm Sw}(\chi)+w_{E/F}).

The first equality above follows from
[
12
, §2.1.2]
and the second equality is obtained by
[
10
, Lemma (2), p. 1970]
.

Finally, substituting the value of
{\rm sl}(\chi)
obtained from (
4.10
), in (
4.9
) and making use of (
4.8
), we have

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-i_{0}/p^{r}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Now we extend Theorem
4.4
for any totally wild extension.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
with
r>1
. Suppose

\rho=\rm Ind_{E/F}\,\chi
for some totally ramified Galois extension
E/F
of degree
p^{r}
and a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Let
i_{0}

denote the smallest lower jump of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\frac{i_{0}}{p^{r}}.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Note that
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
is of order
p^{r}
, and hence it is supersolvable. Therefore there exists a sequence of Galois extensions such that
E=E_{0}\supseteq E_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq E_{s-1}\supseteq F=E_{s}
with
{\rm Gal}(E/F)\trianglelefteq{\rm Gal}(E/E_{s-1})\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq{\rm Gal}(E/E_{1})\trianglelefteq\{e\}
and
{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})/{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})\simeq{\rm Gal}(E_{i-1}/E_{i})
is cyclic of order
p^{t_{i}}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,s
. Let
\phi_{i}
denote the Herbrand function for
{\rm Gal}(E_{i-1}/E_{i})
, and
l_{i}
and
l_{i}^{\prime}
denote the associated largest and smallest lower jumps for
i=1,2,\dots,s
. Let
\phi_{0}
denote the Herbrand function of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
and
l_{0}
and
l_{0}^{\prime}
denote the associated largest and smallest lower jumps. We have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{s}\circ\phi_{s-1}\cdots\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i-1}\cdots\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}.

We prove that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime}

for
\phi_{i}^{\prime}=\phi_{i-1}\circ\phi_{i-2}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}
and
i=1,\dots,s
.
For
i=1
, we use the convention that
\phi_{1}^{\prime}
denotes the identity map.

Note that
\phi_{i}^{\prime}
be the Herbrand function for
{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})
. We denote its associated smallest and largest lower jumps by
l_{E/E_{i-1}}^{\prime}
and
l_{E/E_{i-1}}
respectively. We also denote the smallest and largest lower jumps of
{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})
by
l_{E/E_{i}}^{\prime}
and
l_{E/E_{i}}
respectively.

Since
{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})\subseteq{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, by Lemma
5.5
we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle l_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle l_{E/E_{i-1}}^{\prime}.

Considering the Galois extensions
E\supseteq E_{i-1}\supseteq E_{i}
, we have

\phi_{E/E_{i}}=\phi_{E_{i-1}/E_{i}}\circ\phi_{E/E_{i-1}}=\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}^{\prime}.

By Lemma
5.5
we have

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i-1}}^{\prime})\leq l_{i}^{\prime}
, which by (
4.13
) gives

(4.14)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}^{\prime})\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i-1}}^{\prime})\leq l_{i}^{\prime}.

We observe that

(4.15)

\displaystyle l_{i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}}).

Indeed, for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Gal}(E_{i-1}/E_{i})_{\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}})+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})/{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})\right)_{\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}})+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})/{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})\right)_{\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}}+\delta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})_{l_{E/E_{i}}+\delta}{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})\right)/{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.

The last but one equality follows from
[
19
, Lemma 5]
.
Since
{\rm Gal}(E/E_{i})\subseteq{\rm Gal}(E/F)
, we have
l_{E/E_{i}}\leq l_{0}
. This by (
4.15
) gives

(4.16)

\displaystyle l_{i}\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}})\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}).

By (
4.14
) and (
4.16
), we have

\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}^{\prime})\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i-1}}^{\prime})\leq l_{i}^{\prime}\leq l_{i}\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{E/E_{i}})\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}).

This gives

l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime}\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0})-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}^{\prime})\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0})-l_{0}^{\prime}\leq l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime}.

Thus we have

\phi_{E/E_{i}}(l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime})\leq\phi_{E/E_{i}}(l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime}).

Since
\phi_{E/E_{i}}=\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}^{\prime}
and
\phi_{i}\leq\phi_{E/E_{i}}\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}
, this implies that

\implies\phi_{i}(l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime})\leq(\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}^{\prime})(l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime})\leq(\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i}^{\prime})(l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime}),

which finally gives (
4.12
)

l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime}\leq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime}).

By Lemma
2.7
we have
{\rm sl}(\chi)\geq l_{0}
. So we have
{\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}\geq l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime}
and hence,
\phi_{i}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\geq\phi_{i}^{\prime}(l_{0}-l_{0}^{\prime})
, which by the above inequality gives

(4.17)

\displaystyle l_{i}-l_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}),

for
i=1,2,\dots,s
.

This further by making use of Lemma
4.3
and Corollary
3.5
gives

(4.18)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}\left(\phi_{i}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\phi_{i}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})}{p^{t_{i}}}+\dfrac{w_{i}}{p^{t_{i}}},

where
w_{i}
denotes the wild exponent of
E_{i-1}/E_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,s
.

Proceeding as in Theorem
4.4
, we have

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}),

where
l_{0}^{\prime}
is the smallest lower jump of
{\rm Gal}(E/F)
.
Recall that we denote
\phi_{0}=\phi_{E/F}
. So by (
4.11
) we have
\phi_{0}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})
equals

\displaystyle\phi_{0}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{s}\circ\phi_{s-1}\cdots\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{i-1}\cdots\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi_{s}\circ\phi_{s}^{\prime})({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{s}\left(\phi_{s}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\phi_{s}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})+w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}}}\phi_{s-1}\left(\phi_{s-1}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}}}\left(\dfrac{\phi_{s-1}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})+w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s-1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}\phi_{s-2}\left(\phi_{s-2}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}+\dots+\dfrac{w_{3}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{3}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{3}}}\phi_{2}\left(\phi_{2}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}+\dots+\dfrac{w_{2}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{2}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{2}}}\phi_{2}^{\prime}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}+\dots+\dfrac{w_{2}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{2}}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{2}}}\phi_{1}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w_{s}}{p^{t_{s}}}+\dfrac{w_{s-1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}}}+\dots+\dfrac{w_{1}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{1}}}+\dfrac{{\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}}{p^{t_{s}+t_{s-1}+\dots+t_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{p^{\sum_{i=1}^{s}t_{i}}}\left(p^{\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}t_{i}}w_{s}+p^{\sum_{i=1}^{s-2}t_{i}}w_{s-1}+\dots+p^{t_{1}}w_{2}+w_{1}+{\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{w+{\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime}}{p^{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{p^{r}{\rm sl}(\rho)-l_{0}^{\prime}}{p^{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{l_{0}^{\prime}}{p^{r}}.

Note that
l_{0}^{\prime}
is denoted by
i_{0}
in the statement of the theorem. The last but third equality follows from
[
10
, Lemma]
(1) by denoting
w
as the wild exponent of
E/F
. The last but second equality holds true by
[
10
, Lemma]
(2).
∎

5.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4

In this section, we compute the slope of the adjoint representation of any Carayol representation (see Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
in the introduction). Let us assume that
\rho
is a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
mp^{r}
with
(m,p)=1
.

5.1.
Some lemmas

This section consists of a series of lemmas required to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
.

A representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
is said to be
primitive
if it cannot be obtained by induction from a proper subgroup of
\mathcal{W}_{F}

[
8
, §3]
. For example, every epipelagic representation of degree
p^{r}
is primitive (cf.
[
8
, §3 Proposition]
.
We state the following important lemma on primitive representations.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
14
, p. 37 l. -4, -2]

Let
\sigma
be a Carayol primitive representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of degree
p^{r}
. Then

(1)

there exists a tamely ramified extension
T/F
such that
\sigma\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}
is irreducible.

(2)

there exists a totally wildly ramified finite Galois extension
K/T
and a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
such that
\sigma\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi
.

The next lemma says that any irreducible representation
\rho
of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
is either primitive or induced from a primitive representation.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
. Then either
\rho
is primitive or
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\tau
where
\tau
is a primitive representation of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
and
E/F
is a finite totally wild extension.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
E/T/F
be Galois extension of fields with
E/T
totally wild of degree
p^{r}
and
T/F
totally tame. Then, the smallest nonzero lower jumps in
G(E/T)
and
G(E/F)
are the same.

Proof.

Let
G=G(E/F)
and
H=G(E/T)
. Let
i_{0}
be the smallest nonzero lower jump in
H
. Then
H_{i_{0}}\neq H_{i_{0}+1}
, i.e.,
G_{i_{0}}\cap H\neq G_{i_{0}+1}\cap H
, which shows that
G_{i_{0}}\neq G_{i_{0}+1}
. So
i_{0}
is a lower jump in
G
. It remains to show that
i_{0}
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
G
. Suppose not. There exists
0\neq j<i_{0}
such that
G_{j}\neq G_{j+1}
. Since
T/F
is tame, we have
(G/H)_{1}=(G(T/F))_{1}=1
.
Since
E/T
is totally wild, we have
i_{0}\geq 1
and, hence
\phi_{E/T}(1)=1
.
So we have (cf.
[
19
, Lemma 5]
)

1=(G/H)_{1}=(G/H)_{\phi_{E/T}(1)}=G_{1}H/H,

which gives
G_{1}\subset H
. Now

H_{k}=H\cap G_{k}=H\cap(G_{1}\cap G_{k})=(H\cap G_{1})\cap G_{k}=G_{k}

for all
k\neq 0
. Thus
G_{j}\neq G_{j+1}
implies
H_{j}\neq H_{j+1}
, which is a contradiction as
j<i_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
K/T/E/F
be Galois extension of fields where
K/T
and
E/F
are
totally wild but
T/E
is a tame extension. Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
. Then

\max_{\lambda\in G(E/F),\mu\in G(K/T)}{\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu})=\max_{\epsilon\in G(K/F)_{1}}{\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\epsilon}).

Proof.

Noting that
G(K/F)/G(K/E)\simeq G(E/F)
, we have
G(K/F)=\sqcup_{i}\lambda_{i}G(K/E)
where
\lambda_{i}\in G(K/F)
is a lift of the corresponding element in
G(E/F)
.
Similarly, denoting the lifts of the elements
of
G(T/E)
in
G(K/E)
by
\varsigma_{j}
, we have
G(K/E)=\sqcup_{j}\varsigma_{j}G(K/T)
. Thus

G(K/F)=\sqcup_{i,j}\lambda_{i}\varsigma_{j}G(K/T).

Since
T/E
is a tame extension, each of
\varsigma_{j}
has valuation
1
and hence, the same is true for every element of the form
\lambda_{i}\varsigma_{j}\mu
where
\mu\in G(K/T)
. Thus elements in
G(K/F)
with valuation strictly bigger than one are obtained by taking
\varsigma_{j}=\rm id
in the above decomposition. Therefore

\{\lambda\mu\mid\lambda\in G(E/F),\mu\in G(K/T)\}=\{\epsilon\in G(K/F)\mid v_{K}(\epsilon)\geq 2\}=G(K/F)_{1}.

Hence the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K/E/F
be finite Galois extension of fields. Let
H=G(K/E)
and
G=G(K/F)
. Let
i,i^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime\prime}
be the smallest nonzero lower jumps of
G,H
and
G/H
respectively. Then we have

i^{\prime}\geq i~\text{and}~i^{\prime\prime}\geq\phi_{K/E}(i).

Proof.

We prove that
i^{\prime}\geq i
. We have
H_{s}=G_{s}\cap H
for all
s>0
. Since
i
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
G
, we must have
G_{s}=G_{t}
for
0<s,t\leq i
. So
H_{s}=H_{t}
for
0<s,t\leq i
and hence, the smallest nonzero lower jump in
H
must be at least
i
.

Next we show that
i^{\prime\prime}\geq\phi_{K/E}(i)
. Recall that
(G/H)_{\phi_{K/E}(k)}=G_{k}H/H
for any real number
k
(cf.
[
19
, Lemma 5]
).
Let
0<s,t\leq\phi_{K/E}(i)
. Writing
s=\phi_{K/E}(j),t=\phi_{K/E}(l)
and using the fact that
\psi_{K/E}
is increasing, we have
0<j,l\leq i
. We have

(G/H)_{s}=(G/H)_{\phi_{K/E}(j)}=G_{j}H/H=G_{l}H/H=(G/H)_{\phi_{K/E}(l)}=(G/H)_{t}.

Thus for
0<s,t\leq\phi_{K/E}(i)
, we have
(G/H)_{s}=(G/H)_{t}
, and hence

i^{\prime\prime}\geq\phi_{K/E}(i)
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

(
[
14
]
)

Let
E/F
be a totally ramified finite Galois extension. Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Let
G=G(E/F)
and
\lambda\in G_{0}\setminus G_{1}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda})={\rm sl}(\chi).

Proof.

Let
E^{\lambda}
denote the fixed field of the cyclic subgroup
\langle\lambda\rangle\subset G
. Then
E/E^{\lambda}
is a cyclic tamely ramified extension. We note that
1-\lambda
induces a map
U_{E}^{l}\rightarrow U_{E}^{l}
for all
l\geq 1
(
[
14
, Lemma 4.2 (ii)]
). Here
U_{E}^{l}
denotes the unit subgroup of
E^{*}
. By class field theory, thinking of
\chi
as a character on
E^{*}
, the result follows.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The following proposition lies in the heart of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
E/F
be a totally ramified finite Galois extension. Let
\sigma
be a
Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. Let
G={\rm Gal}(E/F)
and
\lambda\in G_{0}\setminus G_{1}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\sigma\otimes(\sigma^{\vee})^{\lambda})={\rm sl}(\sigma).

Proof of Proposition
5.7
.

Let
\dim\,\sigma=mp^{r}
with
(p,m)=1
. By
[
7
, Lemma 8.4, p. 358]

and
[
14
, Lemma 7.5, p. 20]
, there exist a tame Galois extension
K/E
of degree
m
such that
\sigma={\rm Ind}_{K/E}\tau
where
\tau
is a wildly ramified representation of dimension
p^{r}
. By Mackey decomposition we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sigma\otimes(\sigma^{\vee})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}_{K/E}\tau\otimes{\rm Ind}_{K/E}(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oplus_{\mu\in G(K/E)}{\rm Ind}_{K/E}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu}).

Since
K/E
is tame, by Lemma
2.5
we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{K/E}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu}))=1/m\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu}).

Note that
\tau
is either primitive or
\tau={\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}\tau^{\prime}
where
L^{\prime}/K
is a finite Galois extension and
\tau^{\prime}
is primitive (cf. Lemma
5.2
and Remark
6
).

Suppose
\tau
is primitive. There exists a tame extension
T/K
such that
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}
is irreducible and
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{L/T}\chi
, where
L/T
is totally wild Galois extension and
\chi
is a character of
\mathcal{W}_{L}
(cf. Lemma
5.1
). Thus

(5.3)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}\otimes(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{L/T}\chi\otimes{\rm Ind}_{L/T}\chi^{-\lambda\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot{\rm sl}(\oplus_{\epsilon\in G(L/T)}{\rm Ind}_{L/T}\chi^{1-\lambda\mu\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot\max_{\epsilon\in G(L/T)}\phi_{L/T}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu\epsilon}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot\phi_{L/T}({\rm sl}(\chi))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{L/T}\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/K)\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\tau).

Since
\lambda\in G_{0}\setminus G_{1}
, its lift in
G(L/F)
(along the line of Remark
6
) has valuation
1
.
Therefore the lift of
\lambda\mu\epsilon
in
G(L/F)
has valuation
1
, i.e.,
\lambda\mu\epsilon\in G(L/F)_{0}\setminus G(L/F)_{1}
. So by Lemma
5.6
, the fifth equality follows.

Thus by (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), each of the direct summand in
(
5.1
)

has slope
1/m\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau)
, and therefore

{\rm sl}(\sigma\otimes(\sigma^{\vee})^{\lambda})=1/m\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau)={\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{K/E}\tau)={\rm sl}(\sigma).

Now suppose
\tau
is not primitive and so,
\tau={\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}\tau^{\prime}
for some
L^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}\tau^{\prime}\otimes{\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}(\tau^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\oplus_{\epsilon\in G(L^{\prime}/K)}{\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}(\tau^{\prime}\otimes(\tau^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda\mu\epsilon}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\epsilon\in G(L^{\prime}/K)}\phi_{L^{\prime}/K}({\rm sl}(\tau^{\prime}\otimes(\tau^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda\mu\epsilon}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{L^{\prime}/K}({\rm sl}(\tau^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{L^{\prime}/K}(\tau^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\tau).

The fourth equality follows from the previous case because
\tau^{\prime}
is primitive.
Proceeding as in the previous case we have
{\rm sl}(\sigma\otimes(\sigma^{\vee})^{\lambda})={\rm sl}(\sigma
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose
m>1
. Then
\rho={\rm Ind}_{F^{\prime}/F}\rho^{\prime}
for some totally tamely ramified extension
F^{\prime}/F
of degree
m
and wildly ramified representation
\rho^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{W}_{F^{\prime}}
of dimension
p^{r}
(see
[
7
, Lemma 8.4, p. 358]
).
By the tame reduction as in
[
14
, Lemma 7.5]
, without loss of generality we can assume that
F^{\prime}/F
is Galois.

Now

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{F^{\prime}/F}\rho^{\prime}\otimes{\rm Ind}_{F^{\prime}/F}\rho^{\prime\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\oplus_{\lambda\in G(F^{\prime}/F)}{\rm Ind}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\rho^{\prime}\otimes(\rho^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(F^{\prime}/F)}{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\rho^{\prime}\otimes(\rho^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(F^{\prime}/F)}1/e(F^{\prime}/F)\cdot{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime}\otimes(\rho^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda}).

Since
F^{\prime}/F
is tame, for
{\rm Id}\neq\lambda\in G(F^{\prime}/F)
, by Proposition
5.7
we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime}\otimes(\rho^{\prime\vee})^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime}).

On the other hand, for
\lambda={\rm Id}\in G(F^{\prime}/F)
, we have (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 3.4]
)

(5.6)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\rho^{\prime\vee})\leq{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime}).

Finally, by (
5.4
), (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we have

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=1/e(F^{\prime}/F)\cdot{\rm sl}(\rho^{\prime})={\rm sl}(\rho).

This completes the proof of the theorem.

5.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let us restate our second main theorem for convenience.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\rho
be a Carayol representation of
\mathcal{W}_{F}
of dimension
p^{r}
. Then

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})}{\dim\,\rho}

for the finite Galois extension
K/F
, where
i_{0}
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
G(K/F)
.

Proof of Theorem
5.8
.

We prove the theorem by analyzing the following two cases.

Case 1:
Suppose
\rho
is primitive. By Lemma
5.1
, there exists a tame extension
T/F
such that
\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}
is irreducible and
\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi
where
K/T
is a totally wild Galois extension and
\chi
is a character of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
. By Lemma
2.6
we have

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})=1/e(T/F)\cdot{\rm sl}(\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}\otimes\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}^{\vee}),

which by Theorem
4.5
equals

1/e(T/F)\cdot({\rm sl}(\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-\dfrac{i_{0}(K/T)}{\dim\,\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}}),

where
i_{0}(K/T)
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
G(K/T)
. This further gives

1/e(T/F)\cdot{\rm sl}(\rho\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-\dfrac{i_{0}(K/F)}{e(T/F)\cdot p^{r}}={\rm sl}(\rho)-\dfrac{\phi_{K/F}(i_{0}(K/F))}{\dim\,\rho}.

The last equality holds because by Lemma
5.3
we have

\phi_{K/F}(i_{0}(K/F))=\phi_{T/F}(\phi_{K/T}(i_{0}(K/T)))=\phi_{T/F}(i_{0}(K/T))=\dfrac{i_{0}(K/T)}{e(T/F)}.

Now we analyze the case when
\rho
is not primitive.

Case 2:
By Lemma
5.2
, we have
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\tau
where
\tau
is a primitive representation of
\mathcal{W}_{E}
.

If
\dim\,\tau=1
, then by Theorem
4.5
the proposition follows.

Now suppose
\dim\tau=p^{s}>1
. Since
\tau
is primitive, there exists a tame extension
T/E

such that
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}
is irreducible and
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi
where
K/T
is a totally wild Galois extension and
\chi
is a character of
\mathcal{W}_{K}
(cf. Lemma
5.1
).

Note that by Mackey decomposition we have

\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee}={\rm Ind}_{E/F}\tau\otimes{\rm Ind}_{E/F}\tau^{\vee}=\oplus_{\lambda\in G(E/F)}{\rm Ind}_{E/F}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda}).

By Proposition
2.4
, from the above decomposition we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(E/F)}\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\tau\otimes(\tau^{\vee})^{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(E/F)}\phi_{E/F}(1/e(T/E)\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}\otimes((\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})^{\vee})^{\lambda})).

Since
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi
, we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}\otimes((\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})^{\vee})^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi\otimes{\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi^{-\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oplus_{\mu\in G(K/T)}{\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}.

By making use of (
5.8
) together with Proposition
2.4
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle 1/e(T/E)\cdot{\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}\otimes((\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})^{\vee})^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1/e(T/E)\cdot\max_{\mu\in G(K/T)}\phi_{K/T}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in G(K/T)}1/e(T/E)\cdot\phi_{K/T}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in G(K/T)}\phi_{T/E}(\phi_{K/T}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in G(K/T)}(\phi_{K/E}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu})).

Since
T/E
is a tame extension, the third equality above follows by Lemma
2.5
.

Now, by (
5.7
) and (
5.9
) we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(E/F),~\mu\in G(K/T)}\phi_{E/F}(\phi_{K/E}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\lambda\in G(E/F),~\mu\in G(K/T)}\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\lambda\mu}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\epsilon\in G(K/F)_{1}}\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi^{1-\epsilon}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})

where
i_{0}
is the smallest nonzero lower jump in
G(K/F)
(cf. Lemma
5.4
).

To complete the proof, we need to compute
\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})
.
Let
i_{0}^{\prime}
be the first nonzero lower jump of
G(K/T)
. Since
G(K/T)\subset G(K/F)
, we must have
i_{0}^{\prime}\geq i_{0}
(see Lemma
5.5
(1)) and hence,
{\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}\geq{\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}^{\prime}
. Since
K/T
is a totally wild Galois extension of degree
p^{s}
,
following the proof of Theorem
4.5
(computation of
\phi_{0}({\rm sl}(\chi)-l_{0}^{\prime})
), we have

\phi_{K/T}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})={\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{K/T}(\chi))-i_{0}/p^{s}={\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-i_{0}/p^{s}.

The last equality above follows by substituting
\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}}={\rm Ind}_{K/T}\chi
.

Thus we obtain

(5.11)

\displaystyle\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{T/F}(\phi_{K/T}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{T/F}({\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-i_{0}/p^{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}(\phi_{T/E}({\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-i_{0}/p^{s}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}(1/e(T/E)\cdot({\rm sl}(\tau\mid_{\mathcal{W}_{T}})-i_{0}/p^{s}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\tau)-i_{0}/e(T/E)\cdot p^{s}).

Since
\rho={\rm Ind}_{E/F}(\tau)
, where
E/F
is a totally wild Galois extension of degree
p^{r-s}
, following the proof of Theorem
4.5
,
we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\tau)-t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}({\rm Ind}_{E/F}(\tau))-t/p^{r-s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sl}(\rho)-t/p^{r-s},

where
t
is less than or equal to the smallest nonzero lower jump of
G(E/F)(\simeq G(K/F)/G(K/E))
, which by Lemma
5.5
must be at least
\phi_{K/E}(i_{0})
.
So we have

\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})=\phi_{E/F}(\phi_{K/E}(i_{0}))=\phi_{K/E}(i_{0}),

which further equals

\phi_{T/E}(\phi_{K/T}(i_{0}))=\phi_{T/E}(i_{0})=i_{0}/e(T/E).

The last equality follows because
T/E
is a tame extension.

Thus by (
5.11
) and (
5.12
), we have

\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})=\phi_{E/F}({\rm sl}(\tau)-i_{0}/e(T/E)\cdot p^{s})={\rm sl}(\rho)-i_{0}/e(T/E)\cdot p^{s}\cdot p^{r-s}.

This shows
that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\phi_{K/F}({\rm sl}(\chi)-i_{0})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})/p^{r}.

Hence by (
5.10
) and (
5.13
) we have

{\rm sl}(\rho\otimes\rho^{\vee})={\rm sl}(\rho)-\phi_{K/F}(i_{0})/\dim\,\rho.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 7
.

Since one knows the explicit conductor formulas for the functorial lifts, e.g., conductor of pair, symmetric square lift, exterior square lift, Asai lift etc, we would like to get explicit formulas for the slope of these functorial lifts in future.
