Signs Beat Floats: Low-Rank Double-Binary Adaptation for On-Device Fine-Tuning
Yoshihiko Fujisawa, Yuma Ichikawa, Yudai Fujimoto, Akira Sakai, Katsuki Fujisawa

Abstract
On-device adaptation of large language models commonly keeps a quantized base model frozen while training and deploying a small, task-specific LoRA adapter. In the unmerged adapter-mode setting, however, the adapter is more than a compact storage module; it introduces an additional dense floating-point branch, maintains a trainable state for local updates, and acts as a unit of communication and hot-swapping. We introduce LoRDBA, a LoRA-compatible adapter that replaces both low-rank factors with binary sign carriers while representing magnitudes through lightweight, channel-wise scales, converting the dense adapter branch into two sign-accumulation matrix multiplications interleaved with channel-wise scaling. A finite-sample analysis shows that reconstruction quality is governed by the residual-to-magnitude ratio of the original LoRA factors. In adapter-mode experiments, LoRDBA outperforms low-bit baselines at matched model sizes while matching fp16 LoRA quality in selected regimes. The unmerged adapter incurs at most 8 % 8\% prefill latency overhead at matched rank r = 16 r{=}16 despite an over 10 × 10\times reduction in adapter footprint, with moderate training memory overhead of approximately 1.6 × 1.6\times that of fp16 LoRA.



1
Introduction

Large language models are increasingly adapted using parameter-efficient updates rather than full fine-tuning. LoRA enables this paradigm by freezing pretrained weights and injecting low-rank adapter matrices into Transformer projections, reducing the number of trainable parameters
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
. QLoRA extends this approach to low-bit settings by backpropagating through a frozen quantized base model into LoRA adapters
(
Dettmers et al. 2023
)
. This base-plus-adapter design is attractive for on-device personalization, privacy-preserving adaptation, and communication-efficient update distribution; a compact low-bit base can remain fixed while task-specific adapters are trained, exchanged, or selected. Recent multi-adapter serving systems, including S-LoRA and Punica, demonstrate that practical deployment often treats adapters as swappable runtime objects rather than as weights that are always merged into the base model
(
Sheng et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
)
.

In practical low-bit deployments, merging a trained adapter into a quantized base requires materializing full-precision weights or requantizing per task, forfeiting hot-swap capability; it is therefore preferable to serve the adapter as an unmerged side branch
(
Frantar et al. 2022
;
Lin et al. 2024
)
. This unmerged branch creates a distinct bottleneck: the base projection runs on fused low-bit kernels while the adapter uses dense fp16 factors. An adapter compressor for this regime must preserve accuracy, reduce unmerged computation cost, and avoid inflating the local training state.

We introduce LoRDBA, a low-rank double-binary adapter designed for unmerged adapter-mode deployment. LoRDBA replaces both LoRA factors with binary sign carriers and augments them with a compact scale envelope along the input, rank, and output axes. Binary signs encode directional information while lightweight channel-wise scales capture magnitudes; the quantized base model remains frozen and unchanged.
LoRDBA is trained end-to-end using a smooth-sign straight-through estimator and exported as a near-binary adapter. At inference, it replaces the dense adapter branch with binary-weight accumulation interleaved with scaling. We analyze when this binary reconstruction is accurate, showing that the key quantity is the residual-to-magnitude ratio of the floating-point LoRA factors.
Unlike BitDelta
(
Liu et al. 2024a
)
, which binarizes the full
\Theta(NM)
delta discarding low-rank structure, and LoRAQuant
(
Mirzaei et al. 2025
)
, which retains mixed-precision SVD components, LoRDBA commits
both
rank-
R
factors to
\pm 1
and trains end-to-end, preserving the low-rank adapter format.

We evaluate LoRDBA under the adapter-mode protocol—adapter never merged; storage, latency, and training memory measured separately. Across LLaMA-based math reasoning and summarization benchmarks, LoRDBA improves the accuracy–size trade-off over all evaluated low-bit baselines as previewed in Figure
1
.

Preliminaries

Notation.

Uppercase Roman letters denote matrices; lowercase boldface denotes vectors.
\{\pm 1\}^{p\times q}
is the set of sign matrices.
\|\cdot\|_{F}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\text{op}}
are the Frobenius and operator norms.
\diag({\bm{v}})
constructs a diagonal matrix;
\sign(U)
is entry-wise sign with
\sign(0){=}{+}1
. The sub-Gaussian Orlicz norm is
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\coloneqq\inf\{t>0:{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(X^{2}/t^{2})]\leq 2\}

(
Vershynin 2018
)
.

LoRA.

Given a frozen effective weight matrix
W_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}
, LoRA reparameterizes the fine-tuning increment as

\Delta W=AB^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M},~~A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times r},~B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times r},

(1)

where
r\ll\min(N,M)
is the adapter rank. Following the column-vector convention, the adapted forward pass for an input
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is

{\bm{y}}=W_{0}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+B(A^{\top}{\bm{x}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}
. Thus, each adapted projection incurs two adapter GEMMs with an aggregate arithmetic complexity of
\mathcal{O}(r(N+M))
per token and stores
16r(N+M)
bits when both factors are kept in fp16. The base weights remain fixed throughout adaptation.

Adapter-mode deployment.

We assess LoRDBA in a QLoRA-style on-device regime
(
Dettmers et al. 2023
;
Sheng et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
)
. Let
W_{\mathrm{fp}}
denote the original full-precision pretrained weight, and let
W_{Q}
be its stored low-bit codebook representation. The effective deployed base weight is

\widetilde{W}_{0}\coloneqq\mathrm{dequant}(W_{Q}),

which generally differs from
W_{\mathrm{fp}}
due to quantization error. In this setting, the base model is never materialized in fp16, and the trained adapter is served in an unmerged state. The adapted forward pass is

{\bm{y}}=\widetilde{W}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\Delta W^{\top}{\bm{x}}~=~\mathrm{dequant}(W_{Q})^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\Delta W^{\top}{\bm{x}}.

(2)

This factorizes the operation into a base GEMM—executed by a fused dequantize-matmul kernel when the base is quantized—and an independent adapter side branch. The device-side workload is characterized by the following four conditions:

(A1)

Frozen base:
the base model is held fixed and is not updated. In typical on-device deployments it is stored in a low-bit format such as NF4 or GPTQ, but the conditions themselves do not mandate a specific base precision.

(A2)

Adapter-only training:
optimizer states for the base weights are never materialized.

(A3)

unmerged serving:
the adapter is evaluated as a side branch rather than being merged into a task-specific base weight.

(A4)

Hot-swappability:
multiple task-specific adapters can share the same on-device base
(
Sheng et al. 2024
;
Chen et al. 2024
)
.

The unmerged regime decouples base and adapter kernels: the base GEMM runs at the codebook’s native precision while the adapter GEMM runs at its own bit width. Reducing the adapter bit width is thus the primary mechanism for improving the bandwidth-compute envelope without violating the no-merging constraint.

Bits per weight (BPW).

Following standard practice
(
Mirzaei et al. 2025
)
, we quantify adapter compression in
bits per weight
(BPW) relative to the reference LoRA parameter count
r_{0}(N+M)
. An fp16 LoRA adapter of rank
r
thus has BPW
=16r/r_{0}
, and the full-size reference at rank
r_{0}
has BPW
=16
. The LoRDBA-specific BPW metrics are defined in Section
3.1
once the adapter parameterization is introduced.

3
Method

3.1
Low Rank Double Binary Adapter

LoRDBA parameterizes the LoRA delta with two binary factors and a small collection of channel-wise fp16 scale vectors across the input, rank, and output axes. This design follows the multi-envelope principle introduced for full-weight extreme quantization in MDBF
(
Ichikawa et al. 2025b
)
, but applies it to the much smaller low-rank LoRA delta. Since a single set of scales is a fragile one-dimensional summary of the rank axis, we allow the adapter to contain multiple envelopes that share the same binary carriers, with the number of envelopes controlled by an integer
\ell\geq 1
.

Definition 3.1

(Low Rank Double Binary Adapter)
.

Fix a reference fp16 LoRA rank
r_{0}\geq 1
used only for normalizing storage. A
LoRDBA adapter
of binary carrier rank
R\in\mathbb{N}
and envelope rank
\ell\geq 1
for a host projection of shape
N\times M
is represented as the tuple
\theta=\bigl(B_{1},B_{2},({\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(i)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)})_{i=1}^{\ell}\bigr)
with

B_{1}\in\{\pm 1\}^{N\times R},~B_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}^{R\times M},~{\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},~{\bm{\beta}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{R},~{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M},

(3)

whose induced weight update is

\Delta W(\theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\diag({\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}}^{(i)})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}.

(4)

The adapted forward pass is
{\bm{y}}=W_{0}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\Delta W(\theta)^{\top}{\bm{x}}

and the exact storage cost is

\mathrm{bits}(\theta)=R(N+M)+16\ell(N+R+M).

(5)

We refer to
R
as the
binary carrier rank
(the inner dimension of the binary matmul), reserving
r_{0}
for the fixed reference LoRA rank used only in BPW normalization.

The exact storage cost of Eq. (
5
) induces two BPW metrics:

\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{bc}}~\coloneqq~\frac{R}{r_{0}},\qquad\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{tot}}~\coloneqq~\frac{R(N+M)+16\ell(N+R+M)}{r_{0}(N+M)}.

(6)

\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{bc}}
counts only the binary carriers;
\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{tot}}
includes all fp16 scale vectors. At the default operating point
R{=}r_{0},\ell{=}1
, we have
\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{tot}}\to 1
as
r_{0},N,M\to\infty
. All tables report
\mathrm{BPW}_{\mathrm{tot}}
and actual storage in MB.

The canonical
\ell=1
,
R=r_{0}
case reduces to
\Delta W=\diag({\bm{\alpha}})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})
(Figure
2
). For
\ell\geq 2
, Eq. (
4
) sums
\ell
rank-
R
terms; inference cost grows linearly in
\ell
, while binary-carrier storage is unchanged.

3.2
LoRDBA Training

Under (A1)–(A4), LoRDBA is trained end-to-end on the downstream task with the base
\widetilde{W}_{0}=\mathrm{dequant}(W_{Q})
frozen. The procedure has three components: a forward pass with binarized carriers, a backward pass with a smooth-sign STE, and an SVD-based initialization. We call this pipeline
QAT Full
; all main results use it.

Forward and backward pass.

We maintain real-valued latent carriers
H_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times R}
,
H_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{R\times M}
and binarize them in the forward pass:
B_{1}=\sign(H_{1})
,
B_{2}=\sign(H_{2})
. The weight update
\Delta W(\theta)
is computed via Eq. (
4
).
On the backward pass, the non-differentiable
\sign(\cdot)
is replaced by a smooth-sign straight-through estimator (STE)
(
Leng et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Boža and Macko 2025a
;
Ichikawa and Arai 2025
;
Ichikawa 2024
)
with a temperature schedule (Appendix
F
). Recent high-dimensional analyses of quantized-model training further indicate that STE dynamics can be strongly shaped by quantization hyperparameters such as bit width and quantization range
(
Ichikawa et al. 2025c
)
.
Channel-wise scales
({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
are trained in fp32 and cast to fp16 at export. The rest of the pipeline (AdamW optimizer, data pipeline, learning rate schedule) is identical to a standard LoRA training loop.

Initialization.

The latent carriers are initialized from the rank-
R
thin SVD of a short,
10\%
-budget fp16 LoRA warm-up. Given
\Delta W_{\mathrm{warm}}\approx U_{R}S_{R}V_{R}^{\top}
:

H_{1}^{(0)}=U_{R},~~H_{2}^{(0)}=V_{R}^{\top},~~B_{1}^{(0)}=\sign(H_{1}^{(0)}),~~B_{2}^{(0)}=\sign(H_{2}^{(0)}),~~{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}=\diag(S_{R}),

(7)

with
{\bm{\alpha}}^{(0)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(0)}
recovered by one closed-form per-axis least-squares sweep, cf. Appendix
I.1
. For
\ell\geq 2
, additional envelopes are zero-initialized as
{\bm{\beta}}^{(i)}{=}{\bm{0}}
, so the initial function equals the
\ell{=}1
warm-start. This initialization adds negligible overhead relative to the full QAT budget.

Parameter and storage cost.

The trainable parameter count is
R(N{+}M)+\ell(N{+}R{+}M)
—the same leading term as a rank-
R
LoRA adapter plus the envelope overhead. At export, only
\sign(H_{1})
,
\sign(H_{2})
, and the fp16 scales are stored, yielding the storage of Eq. (
5
).

Optional training variants.

LoRDBA admits alternative training modes that share the same exported adapter format and inference kernel. These are evaluated as ablations in Appendix
G
:

•

QAT Freeze
: freeze the binary carriers
B_{1},B_{2}
from the PTQ initialization and train only the scale vectors
({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
, reducing the trainable count to
\ell(N{+}R{+}M)
per projection. Wall-clock time is comparable to QAT Full due to the full forward–backward pass through the frozen base.

•

PTQ-LoRDBA
: a training-data-free scaled-consensus ADMM that fits a LoRDBA adapter to an existing fp16 LoRA
\Delta W^{\star}
in Frobenius norm (Appendix
D
). It also serves as the initialization for QAT Full.

3.3
Adapter-Mode Inference Kernel

For an input batch
X\in{\mathbb{R}}^{T\times N}
and a canonical
\ell{=}1
LoRDBA adapter, the evaluation order is

X\Delta W(\theta)=\Bigl(\Bigl(\Bigl(\bigl(XD_{{\bm{\alpha}}}\bigr)B_{1}\Bigr)D_{{\bm{\beta}}}\Bigr)B_{2}\Bigr)D_{{\bm{\gamma}}},

(8)

where
D_{{\bm{v}}}\coloneqq\diag({\bm{v}})
. The diagonal products are element-wise fp16 rescalings; the inner matmuls
(\cdot)B_{1}
and
(\cdot)B_{2}
are binary-weight GEMMs. Each column reduces to sign-accumulation:

(XB)_{tj}=\sum_{i:B_{ij}=+1}X_{ti}-\sum_{i:B_{ij}=-1}X_{ti},

(9)

which eliminates multiplications in the inner product. We implement the binary GEMMs via
gemlite

(
Badri and Shaji 2023
)
; the base codebook GEMM is unchanged. The bit-packed
\{B_{1},B_{2}\}
is stored once per adapter; for
\ell\geq 2
the same carriers are shared across envelopes. Pseudocode and microbenchmarks are in Appendices
J
and
5.2
.

4
Theoretical Analysis

We explain why replacing both fp16 LoRA factors by binary sign carriers can preserve the LoRA update when factor magnitudes are not too dispersed. The formal model and proof are in Appendix
C
; here we state the guarantee in the compact form used to interpret the method.

Intuition.
If the entries of each LoRA factor are well-separated from zero (i.e., dominated by their signs), then replacing the factors by their element-wise signs introduces a relative Frobenius error proportional to the residual-to-magnitude ratio
\zeta/\mu
.

Theorem 4.1

(Informal LoRDBA expressivity)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times r}
be fp16 LoRA factors. Suppose that, after fixing the usual positive column-rescaling gauge of LoRA, their entries can be written as

A_{ik}=\mu_{A}\sigma^{A}_{ik}+\xi^{A}_{ik},\qquad B_{jk}=\mu_{B}\sigma^{B}_{jk}+\xi^{B}_{jk},

where
\mu_{A},\mu_{B}>0
, the sign arrays are in
\{\pm 1\}
, and the residuals are independent, mean-zero, sub-Gaussian with scale at most
\zeta
, and independent of the signs. For the relative bound, assume the signs are jointly i.i.d. Rademacher,
NM>8
,
\delta\in(0,1-8/(NM))
,
\zeta\leq\max(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
, and
\log(6NM/\delta)\leq c_{1}r
for a universal constant
c_{1}>0
.

Let
\theta^{\star}
be the canonical single-envelope LoRDBA adapter with binary carriers
\sigma^{A}
and
(\sigma^{B})^{\top}
, unit input/output scales, and rank-axis scale
\mu_{A}\mu_{B}\mathbf{1}_{r}
. Then, for a universal constant
C^{\prime}>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta-8/(NM)
,

\frac{\|AB^{\top}-\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}}{\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{\zeta}{\min(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})}\sqrt{\log(2NM/\delta)}.

(10)

The full statement, including the absolute Frobenius bound, the observed-sign version using
\sign(A)
and
\sign(B)
, and the monotonic extension to envelope rank
\ell\geq 1
, is given in Appendix
C
.

The bound is controlled by
\zeta/\mu
: the residual-to-magnitude ratio after separating signs from factor entries. In the low-noise regime, binary carriers preserve the Frobenius signal up to logarithmic factors, predicting a performance plateau near the binary-carrier limit. Appendix
C.6
reports the plug-in diagnostic.

Role of the bound in the QAT pipeline.

Theorem
4.1
directly characterises PTQ reconstruction quality: given fp16 LoRA factors whose entries are well-separated from zero, the binary sign carriers preserve most of the Frobenius signal.
In the QAT Full pipeline (Section
3.2
), this PTQ solution serves as the initialization; the subsequent smooth-sign training refines both the binary carriers and the scales on the downstream loss, which explains why QAT Full consistently outperforms PTQ-LoRDBA (Appendix
G
).
The theorem thus plays two complementary roles: (i) it identifies the residual-to-magnitude ratio
\zeta/\min(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
as the diagnostic governing initialization quality, and (ii) it predicts a diminishing-returns plateau near the binary-carrier limit, beyond which spending more bits on dense factors yields little improvement—a prediction confirmed by the BPW sweep of Section
5.4
.

Under the adapter-mode conditions (A1)–(A4), we evaluate LoRDBA along the three axes that jointly determine deployment viability:

RQ1

Accuracy
(Section

RQ2

Inference speed

Evaluation protocol.

We follow the adapter-mode plug-and-play protocol
(
Liu et al. 2024a
;
Mirzaei et al. 2025
)
. A reference fp16 LoRA of rank
r_{0}
is first fine-tuned on the downstream task atop the frozen base. The candidate method then emits an adapter at the target BPW under (A1)–(A4), and the adapter is evaluated
without merging
. Accuracy is compared at matched adapter size, latency on the unmerged forward pass, and peak memory on the same training device. LoRDBA trains end-to-end rather than compressing a pre-trained LoRA; the optional ADMM refinement is in Appendix
D
.

Setup

All experiments instantiate (A1)–(A4): the base is frozen in bf16, only the adapter is trainable, and the adapter is served unmerged.
We use bf16 rather than a quantized base to isolate adapter-compression effects from base-quantization noise; LoRDBA is orthogonal to the base format and composes with base-side PTQ pipelines, including layer-wise error-propagation and submodule-level quantization methods
(
Arai and Ichikawa 2025

Ichikawa et al. 2025a

Related Work

Low-rank adaptation.

LoRA
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
reparameterizes fine-tuning updates as a low-rank product of dense fp16 factors. Extensions improve rank structure
(
Liu et al. 2024b
;
Kopiczko et al. 2024
)
, initialization
(
Meng et al. 2024
;
Hayou et al. 2024
)
, or training, but all keep full-precision factors. QLoRA
(
Dettmers et al. 2023
)
freezes a
4
-bit base and trains fp16 LoRA; S-LoRA
(
Sheng et al. 2024
)
and Punica
(
Chen et al. 2024
)
operationalize multi-tenant adapter serving. LoRDBA compresses the
trained
adapter and composes with any LoRA variant.

Base-model quantization.

LoftQ
(
Li et al. 2024
)
and QA-LoRA
(
Xu et al. 2024a
)
quantize the frozen base while initializing adapters to compensate for quantization error; adapters remain floating-point. Base-model PTQ methods
(
Frantar et al. 2022
;
Lin et al. 2024
)
are orthogonal to LoRDBA. Recent PTQ methods further improve the base-side quantization objective by explicitly propagating quantization errors across layers
(
Arai and Ichikawa 2025
)
or by extending the optimization unit from individual layers to larger submodules
(
Ichikawa et al. 2025a
)
. LoRDBA instead compresses the
adapter
while reusing the base-side codebook kernel without modification.

Adapter compression.

BitDelta
(
Liu et al. 2024a
)
binarizes the full fine-tuning delta
\Delta W
with per-column scales; it discards the low-rank structure, producing a
\Theta(NM)
-bit delta much larger than a LoRA adapter at small rank. LoRAQuant
(
Mirzaei et al. 2025
)
re-decomposes
AB^{\top}
via SVD into mixed-bit sub-LoRAs with gradient optimization, retaining floating-point components. LoRDBA differs from both by committing both factors to
\pm 1
and training end-to-end.

Binary matrix factorization.

The
\pm 1
parameterization connects to binary networks
(
Courbariaux et al. 2016
;
Rastegari et al. 2016
;
Liu et al. 2020
)
and full-LLM binarization (BitNet
(
Wang et al. 2023
)
, OneBit
(
Xu et al. 2024b
)
).
DBF
(
Boža and Macko 2025b
)
factorizes a dense weight as two sign matrices with channel-wise scales; MDBF
(
Ichikawa et al. 2025b
)
extends this idea to rank-
\ell
multi-envelope scaling for full-weight extreme quantization. LoRDBA applies the factorization to the much smaller LoRA delta, adds an expressivity guarantee (Theorem
4.1
), and introduces task-specific QAT via the smooth-sign STE
(
Leng et al. 2018
;
Boža and Macko 2025a
)
.
Complementary to algorithmic compression methods, OneComp
(
Ichikawa et al. 2026
)
packages heterogeneous post-training compression techniques into a reproducible, hardware-aware pipeline, whereas LoRDBA focuses on the adapter representation and its unmerged inference primitive.

7
Conclusion

LoRDBA shows that both factors of a low-rank adapter can be binarized to
\pm 1
, provided that a compact set of channel-wise scales preserves their row, column, and joint-spectrum magnitudes. Under the QLoRA-style adapter-mode conditions (A1)–(A4), QAT Full LoRDBA achieves accuracy that matches or exceeds fp16 LoRA at the same adapter size, while introducing at most
8\%
prefill-latency overhead, and compressing the adapter by more than
10\times
.
The additional training cost is moderate: LoRDBA requires approximately
1.6\times
higher peak memory and
1.8\times
longer step time due to the smooth-sign STE. Across LLaMA-2-7B math reasoning and summarization benchmarks, as well as LLaMA-3.2-3B ablations, QAT Full LoRDBA Pareto-dominates all evaluated adapter-compression methods at every tested size tier.

Limitations.

Our expressivity guarantee in Theorem
4.1
relies on the sub-Gaussian sign-noise decomposition in Assumption
C.1
. As a result, the guarantee may weaken when the factor magnitudes are heavy-tailed or bimodal. Our experiments are also limited to LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3.2-3B on math reasoning and summarization tasks, leaving validation on larger models, alternative architectures, and additional domains to future work.
Finally, LoRDBA targets the unmerged adapter-mode setting. Adapters that must be merged into the base model before inference are outside the scope of the current framework.

Broader impact.

By compressing LoRA adapters to
{\leq}4
bits per weight, LoRDBA reduces the communication and storage costs of distributing task-specific models. This makes on-device personalization more practical, as adapters can be deployed locally without requiring users to upload private data to the cloud, providing a direct privacy benefit.
At the same time, LoRDBA is both model- and task-agnostic, and could therefore also be used to compress adapters fine-tuned for harmful applications. We recommend pairing LoRDBA with the same safety guardrails used for the underlying base model.

Appendix B
Self-contained probabilistic background

This appendix collects the elementary concentration facts used by the
expressivity proof. Apart from Markov’s inequality, every estimate
below is proved from scratch using only Cauchy–Schwarz and series
manipulations, so the proof of Theorem
4.1
does not
depend on any external reference. Throughout we work with real-valued
random variables defined on a common probability space, write

C,c,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots
for finite, strictly positive numerical
constants whose value may change from line to line and which are
independent of all model parameters, and denote

\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\coloneqq\inf\bigl\{t>0:{\mathbb{E}}\exp(X^{2}/t^{2})\leq 2\bigr\},\qquad\|X\|_{\psi_{1}}\coloneqq\inf\bigl\{s>0:{\mathbb{E}}\exp(|X|/s)\leq 2\bigr\}.

(12)

Lemma B.1

(Sub-Gaussian tail and second moment)
.

Let
X
be a real random variable with
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]\;\leq\;K^{2},\qquad\Pr[|X|\geq t]\;\leq\;2\exp(-t^{2}/K^{2}),~t\geq 0,

(13)

and for every integer
p\geq 1
,

({\mathbb{E}}|X|^{p})^{1/p}\leq CK\sqrt{p}
with a numerical constant
C
.

Proof.

The inequality
1+u\leq e^{u}
at
u=X^{2}/K^{2}
and the
definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{2}}
give

1+{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]/K^{2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\exp(X^{2}/K^{2})\leq 2
, hence

{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]\leq K^{2}
. Markov’s inequality applied to

\exp(X^{2}/K^{2})
at level
\exp(t^{2}/K^{2})
yields the tail bound.
For the moment bound, integrate the tail:

{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}\Pr[|X|\geq t]dt\leq 2p\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p-1}e^{-t^{2}/K^{2}}dt=pK^{p}\Gamma(p/2)\leq(CK)^{p}p^{p/2},

where the last step uses Stirling. Taking the
p
-th root proves the
moment bound.
∎

Lemma B.2

(MGF bound and sub-Gaussian sum)
.

If
{\mathbb{E}}X=0
and
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
, then

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda X)\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}K^{2})
for every

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. If furthermore
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are
mutually independent, mean-zero with

\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
, and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}

are deterministic, then for every
u\geq 0
,

\Pr\left[\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}X_{i}\Bigr|\geq CK\|a\|_{2}\sqrt{u}\right]\;\leq\;2e^{-u}.

(14)

Proof.

Expanding the exponential as a Taylor series and using

{\mathbb{E}}X=0
gives

{\mathbb{E}}e^{\lambda X}=1+\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{\lambda^{p}{\mathbb{E}}X^{p}}{p!}.

By Lemma
B.1
,

|{\mathbb{E}}X^{p}|\leq{\mathbb{E}}|X|^{p}\leq(CK\sqrt{p})^{p}
. Hence

\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{|\lambda|^{p}|{\mathbb{E}}X^{p}|}{p!}\leq\sum_{p\geq 2}\frac{(C|\lambda|K\sqrt{p})^{p}}{p!}\leq C_{1}\lambda^{2}K^{2}\exp(C_{2}\lambda^{2}K^{2}),

where the last inequality is the standard consequence of

p!\geq(p/e)^{p}
and splitting the series at

p\simeq\lambda^{2}K^{2}
. Since

1+s\exp(s^{\prime})\leq\exp(C(s+s^{\prime}))
for
s,s^{\prime}\geq 0
, this yields

{\mathbb{E}}e^{\lambda X}\leq\exp(C^{\prime\prime\prime}\lambda^{2}K^{2})
for a universal
constant
C^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
By independence,
{\mathbb{E}}\exp(\lambda\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i})=\prod_{i}{\mathbb{E}}e^{\lambda a_{i}X_{i}}\leq\exp(C\lambda^{2}K^{2}\|a\|_{2}^{2})
.
Chernoff’s inequality

\Pr[\sum a_{i}X_{i}>t]\leq\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda t}{\mathbb{E}}e^{\lambda\sum a_{i}X_{i}}

optimised at
\lambda=t/(2CK^{2}\|a\|_{2}^{2})
gives

\Pr[\sum a_{i}X_{i}>t]\leq\exp(-ct^{2}/(K^{2}\|a\|_{2}^{2}))
.
A symmetric bound on the negative tail and adjusting constants yields
Eq. (
14
).
∎

Lemma B.3

(Sub-exponential product and Bernstein bound)
.

If
X,Y
are independent real random variables with

\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
and
\|Y\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq L
, then

{\mathbb{E}}[XY]={\mathbb{E}}X{\mathbb{E}}Y
and the product
XY
is
sub-exponential with
\|XY\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq KL
. Moreover, if

W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
are mutually independent, mean-zero, and

\|W_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq L_{0}
, then for every
u\geq 0
,

\Pr\left[\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\Bigr|\geq t\right]\;\leq\;2\exp\Bigl(-c\min\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{nL_{0}^{2}},\frac{t}{L_{0}}\Bigr)\Bigr),\quad t\geq 0.

(15)

Proof.

For the product part, the Young inequality
|xy|/(KL)\leq x^{2}/(2K^{2})+y^{2}/(2L^{2})

combined with the AM–GM and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities yields

{\mathbb{E}}\exp(|XY|/(KL))\leq{\mathbb{E}}\exp(X^{2}/(2K^{2})+Y^{2}/(2L^{2}))={\mathbb{E}}\exp(X^{2}/(2K^{2})){\mathbb{E}}\exp(Y^{2}/(2L^{2}))\leq 2,

where we used independence in the equality and the

\psi_{2}
-defining bound twice in the last step (after noting that

\exp(X^{2}/(2K^{2}))\leq\sqrt{\exp(X^{2}/K^{2})}
, so its expectation
is at most
\sqrt{2}
). Hence
\|XY\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq KL
. For the
mean,
{\mathbb{E}}|XY|\leq\sqrt{{\mathbb{E}}X^{2}{\mathbb{E}}Y^{2}}<\infty

by Cauchy–Schwarz and Lemma
B.1
, so by Fubini

{\mathbb{E}}[XY]={\mathbb{E}}X{\mathbb{E}}Y
.

For the Bernstein bound, an argument identical to that of
Lemma
B.2
(now using

({\mathbb{E}}|W|^{p})^{1/p}\leq CL_{0}p
instead of
CK\sqrt{p}
,
which is the sub-exponential moment bound proved by integrating the
tail
\Pr[|W|\geq t]\leq 2e^{-t/L_{0}}
) yields, for

|\lambda|\leq c/L_{0}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Bigl(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\Bigr)\leq\exp(Cn\lambda^{2}L_{0}^{2}).

Optimising the resulting Chernoff bound at

\lambda=\min\bigl(t/(2CnL_{0}^{2}),c/(2L_{0})\bigr)
gives the
two-regime bound Eq. (
15
).
∎

Lemma B.4

(Sign-flip bound from a
\psi_{2}
residual)
.

If
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq K
and
\mu>0
, then

\Pr[|X|\geq\mu]\leq 2\exp(-\mu^{2}/K^{2})
.

Proof.

Markov’s inequality applied to
\exp(X^{2}/K^{2})
at level

\exp(\mu^{2}/K^{2})
gives

\Pr[|X|\geq\mu]=\Pr[\exp(X^{2}/K^{2})\geq\exp(\mu^{2}/K^{2})]\leq 2\exp(-\mu^{2}/K^{2})
.
∎

Appendix C
Expressivity of LoRDBA: Proof of Theorem
4.1

This appendix gives a full, self-contained proof of
Theorem
4.1
from Section
4
. The
argument is entirely a matter of entry-wise sub-Gaussian and
sub-exponential concentration, and no sketching, Rademacher
compression, or Johnson–Lindenstrauss reduction is used. All
underlying probabilistic facts are proved from scratch in
Appendix
B
(sub-Gaussian sum tail
Lemma
B.2
, sub-exponential product and Bernstein
inequality Lemma
B.3
, and the sign-flip bound
Lemma
B.4
). In particular, the sub-Gaussian Orlicz
norm and the second-moment bound

{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]\leq\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}
used freely below are
recorded in Lemma
B.1
.

Assumption C.1

(Sign-plus-sub-Gaussian-noise factor model)
.

The LoRA factors
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times r}
and

B\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times r}
are random matrices defined on a common
probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},\Pr)
and admit the entry-wise
decomposition

A_{ik}=\mu_{A}\sigma^{A}_{ik}+\xi^{A}_{ik},~~B_{jk}=\mu_{B}\sigma^{B}_{jk}+\xi^{B}_{jk},~~i\in[N],~j\in[M],~k\in[r],

(16)

where
\mu_{A},\mu_{B}>0
and
\zeta>0
are deterministic
scalars and the sign arrays

\sigma^{A}\in\{\pm 1\}^{N\times r}
,

\sigma^{B}\in\{\pm 1\}^{M\times r}
and residual arrays

\xi^{A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times r}
,

\xi^{B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times r}
satisfy:

1.

The families

\{\xi^{A}_{ik}\}_{(i,k)\in[N]\times[r]}
and

\{\xi^{B}_{jk}\}_{(j,k)\in[M]\times[r]}
consist of mutually
independent random variables, the two families are mutually
independent, and each entry has zero mean.

2.

The residuals are uniformly sub-Gaussian:

\max\Bigl(\max_{(i,k)\in[N]\times[r]}\|\xi^{A}_{ik}\|_{\psi_{2}},\max_{(j,k)\in[M]\times[r]}\|\xi^{B}_{jk}\|_{\psi_{2}}\Bigr)\leq\zeta.

3.

The sign
\sigma
-algebra

{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\sigma(\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B})
is independent
of
\sigma(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})
.

We allow the latent sign arrays to be either fixed or random. When

\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B}
are fixed, all probability statements are taken
only over the residual arrays
(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})
; in this case
Assumption
C.1
(iii) is vacuous. For the relative bound
in Theorem
4.1
, the entries of
\sigma^{A}
and

\sigma^{B}
are additionally assumed to be jointly i.i.d. Rademacher.

The canonical reconstruction used in Theorem
4.1
is

\theta^{\star}\coloneqq\bigl(\sigma^{A},(\sigma^{B})^{\top},{\bm{1}}_{N},\mu_{A}\mu_{B}{\bm{1}}_{r},{\bm{1}}_{M}\bigr),

(17)

which is a single-envelope LoRDBA adapter with the latent signs as the
binary carriers. For each envelope rank
\ell\geq 1
, let
\Theta_{\ell}

denote the corresponding LoRDBA class of Definition
3.1
.
The zero-padding embedding

\iota_{\ell}:\Theta_{\ell-1}\hookrightarrow\Theta_{\ell},~~\bigl(B_{1},B_{2},\{({\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(i)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^{\ell-1}\bigr)\mapsto\bigl(B_{1},B_{2},\{({\bm{\alpha}}^{(i)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(i)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^{\ell-1},({\bm{0}},{\bm{0}},{\bm{0}})\bigr)

(18)

preserves
\Delta W
, so the best achievable reconstruction error is
non-increasing in the envelope rank.

Let
E\coloneqq AB^{\top}-\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}

denote the reconstruction residual. We first expand
E
entry-wise
(Lemma
C.2
), then bound each entry by
sub-exponential concentration (Lemma
C.3
), aggregate by a
union bound to obtain (
27
), and finally combine the
Frobenius upper bound with a Chebyshev lower bound on

\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}

(Lemma
C.4
) to obtain (
10
).

For the absolute bound (
27
) the signs

\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B}
are deterministic, so all probability
statements below are taken with respect to the residuals

(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})
. For the relative bound (
10
)
the signs are additionally i.i.d. Rademacher and jointly independent
of the residuals; every statement proved for deterministic signs
therefore also holds
conditionally
on

(\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B})
, and by Fubini the residual event of
probability at least
1-\delta
holds on the product space.

C.1
Entry-wise expansion

The heart of the argument is the following entry-wise expansion of the
residual into three pieces with distinct concentration behaviour. Its
pairwise
L^{2}
-orthogonality keeps the final variance computation
additive.

Lemma C.2

(Residual expansion)
.

Under Assumption
C.1
, for every
i\in[N]
and

j\in[M]
,

E_{ij}\;=\;\underbrace{\mu_{A}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}}_{=:T^{(1)}_{ij}}\;+\;\underbrace{\mu_{B}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\xi^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jk}}_{=:T^{(2)}_{ij}}\;+\;\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{r}\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}}_{=:T^{(3)}_{ij}}.

(19)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\sigma(\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B})
denote the

\sigma
-algebra generated by the signs (which reduces to the trivial

\sigma
-algebra when the signs are deterministic). Then the three
summands are pairwise
L^{2}
-orthogonal conditionally on
{\mathcal{F}}
:

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[T^{(a)}_{ij}T^{(b)}_{ij}\big|{\mathcal{F}}\bigr]\;=\;0\quad\text{for every distinct }a,b\in\{1,2,3\}.

(20)

In particular, taking total expectation, the same orthogonality holds
unconditionally.

Proof.

Step 1: entry-wise identity.
Substituting
Eq. (
16
) into

(AB^{\top})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}A_{ik}B_{jk}
and expanding the
scalar product

(\mu_{A}\sigma^{A}_{ik}+\xi^{A}_{ik})(\mu_{B}\sigma^{B}_{jk}+\xi^{B}_{jk})

termwise,

(AB^{\top})_{ij}\;=\;\underbrace{\mu_{A}\mu_{B}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jk}}_{=[\Delta W(\theta^{\star})]_{ij}}\;+\;T^{(1)}_{ij}+T^{(2)}_{ij}+T^{(3)}_{ij},

where the identification of the leading term with

[\Delta W(\theta^{\star})]_{ij}
follows from
Eq. (
17
) together with

[\diag({\bm{1}}_{N})\sigma^{A}\diag(\mu_{A}\mu_{B}{\bm{1}}_{r})(\sigma^{B})^{\top}\diag({\bm{1}}_{M})]_{ij}=\mu_{A}\mu_{B}\sum_{k}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jk}
.
Subtracting this leading term yields
Eq. (
19
).

Step 2: conditional
L^{2}
-orthogonality.
By
Assumption
C.1
(iii) the sign
\sigma
-algebra

{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma(\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B})
is independent of

\sigma(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})
, which implies that the conditional law of

(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})
given
{\mathcal{F}}
equals its unconditional law; all
residual moments appearing below therefore equal their unconditional
counterparts. Fix
(i,j)
and recall

T^{(1)}_{ij}T^{(2)}_{ij}=\mu_{A}\mu_{B}\sum_{k,l}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jl}\xi^{B}_{jk}\xi^{A}_{il}
.
The sign factors are
{\mathcal{F}}
-measurable and can be pulled out of
the conditional expectation; Assumption
C.1
(i)
ensures that
\xi^{A}_{il}
and
\xi^{B}_{jk}
are independent (they
belong to different independent families) with

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{il}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}]=0
, so

{\mathbb{E}}[T^{(1)}_{ij}T^{(2)}_{ij}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]=\mu_{A}\mu_{B}\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jl}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{il}]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}]=0.

For
T^{(1)}_{ij}T^{(3)}_{ij}=\mu_{A}\sum_{k,l}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\xi^{A}_{il}\xi^{B}_{jl}
,
the factor
\xi^{A}_{il}
is independent of

(\xi^{B}_{jk},\xi^{B}_{jl})
(different families, cf. (i)), so its
expectation factorises and vanishes:

{\mathbb{E}}[T^{(1)}_{ij}T^{(3)}_{ij}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]=\mu_{A}\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{il}]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}\xi^{B}_{jl}]=0.

The identity
{\mathbb{E}}[T^{(2)}_{ij}T^{(3)}_{ij}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]=0
is
symmetric, replacing the roles of
A
and
B
:

{\mathbb{E}}[T^{(2)}_{ij}T^{(3)}_{ij}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]=\mu_{B}\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}\sigma^{B}_{jk}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jl}]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{A}_{il}]=0.

This establishes Eq. (
20
). Taking
expectations and using the tower property yields the unconditional
orthogonality.
∎

C.2
Sub-exponential tail of each entry

With Lemma
C.2
in hand, each summand in
Eq. (
19
) is either a sum of independent
sub-Gaussians or a sum of independent products of sub-Gaussians; the
latter is sub-exponential. A union bound over the three summands
therefore yields a sub-exponential tail for
E_{ij}
.

Lemma C.3

(Sub-exponential bound on
E_{ij}
)
.

Let
V\coloneqq r\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})
.
There exists a universal constant
c_{1}>0
such that, for every

(i,j)\in[N]\times[M]
and every
t>0
,

\Pr\bigl[|E_{ij}|>t\bigr]\;\leq\;6\exp\Bigl(-c_{1}\min\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{V},\frac{t}{\zeta^{2}}\Bigr)\Bigr).

(21)

Moreover
{\mathbb{E}}[E_{ij}^{2}]\leq V
, hence

{\mathbb{E}}\|E\|_{F}^{2}\leq NMV
.

Proof.

Fix
(i,j)\in[N]\times[M]
and condition on the
\sigma
-algebra

{\mathcal{F}}=\sigma(\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B})
. By
Assumption
C.1
(iii) the residuals
(\xi^{A},\xi^{B})

are independent of
{\mathcal{F}}
, so the conditional joint law of the
residuals given
{\mathcal{F}}
coincides with their unconditional law. All
probability statements in parts (a)–(c) below are understood
conditionally on
{\mathcal{F}}
; the bounds are uniform over realisations
of
{\mathcal{F}}
and therefore also hold unconditionally. Throughout we
write
c_{a},c_{b}>0
for the universal constants produced by
Lemma
B.2
(sub-Gaussian sum tail) and
Lemma
B.3
(Bernstein) of
Appendix
B
.

(a)
T^{(1)}_{ij}
is sub-Gaussian.
For each

k\in[r]
,
|\sigma^{A}_{ik}|=1
and the positive-homogeneity
of
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{2}}
give

\|\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\|_{\psi_{2}}=\|\xi^{B}_{jk}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\zeta
.
By Assumption
C.1
(i) the family

\{\xi^{B}_{jk}\}_{k=1}^{r}
is mutually independent and the

{\mathcal{F}}
-measurable signs
\sigma^{A}_{ik}
preserve (conditional)
independence and the zero mean. Applying Lemma
B.2

conditionally on
{\mathcal{F}}
to

\{X_{k}=\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\}_{k=1}^{r}
with

a_{k}\equiv 1
,
K=\zeta
, scaled by the deterministic
constant
\mu_{A}
, yields

\Pr\bigl[|T^{(1)}_{ij}|>t\big|{\mathcal{F}}\bigr]\;\leq\;2\exp\bigl(-c_{a}t^{2}/(\mu_{A}^{2}r\zeta^{2})\bigr)\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(22)

(b)
T^{(2)}_{ij}
is sub-Gaussian.
Exchanging the roles of
the two families in (a),

\Pr\bigl[|T^{(2)}_{ij}|>t\big|{\mathcal{F}}\bigr]\;\leq\;2\exp\bigl(-c_{a}t^{2}/(\mu_{B}^{2}r\zeta^{2})\bigr)\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(23)

(c)
T^{(3)}_{ij}
is sub-exponential.
By
Assumption
C.1
(i),
\xi^{A}_{ik}
and
\xi^{B}_{jk}

are independent with zero mean, so
Lemma
B.3
gives

{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]={\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{ik}]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}]=0

and

\|\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\|_{\psi_{1}}\leq\|\xi^{A}_{ik}\|_{\psi_{2}}\|\xi^{B}_{jk}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\zeta^{2}
.
The summands
\{\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk}\}_{k=1}^{r}
are independent
across
k
: indeed,

\sigma(\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{B}_{jk})\subseteq\sigma(\xi^{A}_{ik},\xi^{B}_{jk})
,
and the pairs
(\xi^{A}_{ik},\xi^{B}_{jk})
for distinct

k\in[r]
involve disjoint index sets in the mutually independent
families
\{\xi^{A}_{\cdot\cdot}\},\{\xi^{B}_{\cdot\cdot}\}
.
The Bernstein bound Eq. (
15
) of
Lemma
B.3
therefore yields, with

c_{b}>0
the universal constant of that lemma,

\Pr\bigl[|T^{(3)}_{ij}|>t\big|{\mathcal{F}}\bigr]\;\leq\;2\exp\Bigl(-c_{b}\min\bigl(t^{2}/(r\zeta^{4}),t/\zeta^{2}\bigr)\Bigr)\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(24)

Combination.
Triangle inequality gives

|E_{ij}|\leq|T^{(1)}_{ij}|+|T^{(2)}_{ij}|+|T^{(3)}_{ij}|
,
so

\{|E_{ij}|>t\}\;\subseteq\;\{|T^{(1)}_{ij}|>t/3\}\cup\{|T^{(2)}_{ij}|>t/3\}\cup\{|T^{(3)}_{ij}|>t/3\}.

Since
V=r\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})

dominates each of
\mu_{A}^{2}r\zeta^{2}
,
\mu_{B}^{2}r\zeta^{2}
,
and
r\zeta^{4}
, Eqs. (
22
)–(
23
) at

t/3
are bounded by

2\exp(-c_{a}t^{2}/(9V))
, and Eq. (
24
) at
t/3
is
bounded by

2\exp(-c_{b}\min(t^{2}/(9V),t/(3\zeta^{2})))
. Defining

c_{1}\;\coloneqq\;\frac{1}{9}\min(c_{a},c_{b}),

(25)

each of the three bounds is dominated by

2\exp(-c_{1}\min(t^{2}/V,t/\zeta^{2}))
: indeed

c_{a}/9\geq c_{1}
,
c_{b}/9\geq c_{1}
, and

c_{b}/3\geq c_{1}
. Taking the union over the three events
yields Eq. (
21
) conditionally on
{\mathcal{F}}
;
uniformity over
{\mathcal{F}}
gives the unconditional bound.

Second moment.
Lemma
B.1
gives

{\mathbb{E}}[(\xi^{A}_{ik})^{2}]\leq\zeta^{2}
and

{\mathbb{E}}[(\xi^{B}_{jk})^{2}]\leq\zeta^{2}
. Using the mutual
independence of Assumption
C.1
(i), zero means, and

(\sigma^{\cdot}_{\cdot\cdot})^{2}=1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(T^{(1)}_{ij})^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]

\displaystyle=\mu_{A}^{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}\xi^{B}_{jl}]=\mu_{A}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}[(\xi^{B}_{jk})^{2}]\;\leq\;\mu_{A}^{2}r\zeta^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(T^{(2)}_{ij})^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]

\displaystyle\leq\;\mu_{B}^{2}r\zeta^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(T^{(3)}_{ij})^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{A}_{ik}\xi^{A}_{il}]{\mathbb{E}}[\xi^{B}_{jk}\xi^{B}_{jl}]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}[(\xi^{A}_{ik})^{2}]{\mathbb{E}}[(\xi^{B}_{jk})^{2}]\;\leq\;r\zeta^{4},

where the off-diagonal (
k\neq l
) cross terms vanish because the
residuals within each family are mean zero and mutually independent.
Combined with the conditional orthogonality of
Lemma
C.2
,

{\mathbb{E}}[E_{ij}^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]=\sum_{a=1}^{3}{\mathbb{E}}[(T^{(a)}_{ij})^{2}\mid{\mathcal{F}}]\leq V
.
Taking total expectation and summing over
(i,j)\in[N]\times[M]

yields
{\mathbb{E}}\|E\|_{F}^{2}\leq NMV
.
∎

C.3
Frobenius bound by union

The per-entry bound of Lemma
C.3
transforms into a
Frobenius bound by a union argument over the
NM
entries of
E
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
, Eq. (
27
).

Fix
N,M\geq 2
,
r\geq 1
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
with

u\coloneqq\log(6NM/\delta)\leq c_{1}r
, where
c_{1}
is the
constant defined in Eq. (
25
). Since
NM\geq 4
and

\delta<1
,
u\geq\log 24>0
. Set

t_{\star}\coloneqq\sqrt{Vu/c_{1}}>0
.

Step 1: the sub-Gaussian branch binds at
t=t_{\star}
.

In Eq. (
21
) the inner minimum satisfies

\min(c_{1}t^{2}/V,c_{1}t/\zeta^{2})=c_{1}t^{2}/V
iff

t^{2}/V\leq t/\zeta^{2}
, iff
t\leq V/\zeta^{2}
. At

t=t_{\star}
this becomes
\sqrt{Vu/c_{1}}\leq V/\zeta^{2}
,
equivalently
u\leq c_{1}V/\zeta^{4}
. From

V=r\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})
and

\zeta>0
,

\frac{V}{\zeta^{4}}\;=\;r\Bigl(\frac{\mu_{A}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}+\frac{\mu_{B}^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}+1\Bigr)\;\geq\;r,

so the standing hypothesis
u\leq c_{1}r
entails

u\leq c_{1}V/\zeta^{4}
, and the sub-Gaussian branch binds:

c_{1}\min(t_{\star}^{2}/V,t_{\star}/\zeta^{2})\;=\;c_{1}t_{\star}^{2}/V\;=\;u.

Step 2: per-entry bound.
Substituting
t=t_{\star}
into
Eq. (
21
) and using Step 1,

\Pr\bigl[E_{ij}^{2}>Vu/c_{1}\bigr]\;=\;\Pr\bigl[|E_{ij}|>t_{\star}\bigr]\;\leq\;6e^{-u}\;=\;\frac{\delta}{NM},\qquad(i,j)\in[N]\times[M].

Step 3: union bound.
If

\max_{(i,j)}E_{ij}^{2}\leq Vu/c_{1}
, then

\|E\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}^{2}\leq NM\cdot Vu/c_{1}
.
Contrapositively,

\{\|E\|_{F}^{2}>NMVu/c_{1}\}\subseteq\bigcup_{i,j}\{E_{ij}^{2}>Vu/c_{1}\}
.
A union bound over the
NM
entries gives

\Pr[\|E\|_{F}^{2}>NMVu/c_{1}]\leq NM\cdot\delta/(NM)=\delta
.
Hence, with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\|E\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;\frac{NMr\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})}{c_{1}}\log(6NM/\delta).

(26)

Step 4: conversion
\log(6NM/\delta)\to\log(2NM/\delta)
.

For
N,M\geq 2
and
\delta\in(0,1)
,

2NM/\delta\geq 8
, so
\log(2NM/\delta)\geq\log 8>\log 3
.
Therefore

\log(6NM/\delta)\;=\;\log 3+\log(2NM/\delta)\;\leq\;\log(2NM/\delta)+\log(2NM/\delta)\;=\;2\log(2NM/\delta).

Substituting into Eq. (
26
) and setting

C\coloneqq 2/c_{1}>0
yields the absolute bound

\big\|AB^{\top}-\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\big\|_{F}^{2}\;\leq\;C\cdot NMr\zeta^{2}\bigl(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2}\bigr)\log(2NM/\delta).

(27)

The constant
C
depends only on the universal constants

c_{a},c_{b}
produced by Lemma
B.2
and
Lemma
B.3
of Appendix
B

through Eq. (
25
), and is otherwise independent of all
model parameters.
∎

C.4
Chebyshev lower bound on the signal

The relative bound requires a lower bound on

\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}
. This is supplied by a
second-moment argument on the Rademacher bilinear form of the signs.

Lemma C.4

(Lower bound on
\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}
)
.

Assume
\{\sigma^{A}_{ik}\}\cup\{\sigma^{B}_{jk}\}
are i.i.d. Rademacher with
\sigma^{A}\perp\sigma^{B}
. Let

W_{ij}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{B}_{jk}
. Then
for every
N,M\geq 1
,
r\geq 1
,

\Pr\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}NMr\Bigr]\;\geq\;1-\frac{8}{NM}.

(28)

In particular, on this event

\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}=\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{ij}W_{ij}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}NMr
.

Proof.

Define
Z\coloneqq\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}^{2}
and, for
k,l\in[r]
,

G^{A}_{kl}\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il},\qquad G^{B}_{kl}\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{j=1}^{M}\sigma^{B}_{jk}\sigma^{B}_{jl},

so that
G^{A}_{kl}=G^{A}_{lk}
and
G^{B}_{kl}=G^{B}_{lk}
.
Expanding
W_{ij}^{2}=\sum_{k,l}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il}\sigma^{B}_{jk}\sigma^{B}_{jl}

and interchanging the finite sums over
(i,j)
and
(k,l)
,

Z\;=\;\sum_{k,l=1}^{r}G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}\;=\;\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{r}G^{A}_{kk}G^{B}_{kk}}_{=rNM}\;+\;\sum_{k\neq l}G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl},

(29)

where the first sum is deterministic because

G^{A}_{kk}=\sum_{i}(\sigma^{A}_{ik})^{2}=N
and

G^{B}_{kk}=M
. If
r=1
the off-diagonal sum is empty,

Z=NM=rNM\geq rNM/2
deterministically, and
Eq. (
28
) is trivial. We henceforth assume

r\geq 2
.

Mean.
For
k\neq l
and fixed
i\in[N]
,

\sigma^{A}_{ik}
and
\sigma^{A}_{il}
are independent Rademachers,
hence
{\mathbb{E}}[\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il}]={\mathbb{E}}[\sigma^{A}_{ik}]{\mathbb{E}}[\sigma^{A}_{il}]=0
,
so
{\mathbb{E}}[G^{A}_{kl}]=0
and similarly
{\mathbb{E}}[G^{B}_{kl}]=0
.
By
\sigma^{A}\perp\sigma^{B}
,

{\mathbb{E}}[G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}]={\mathbb{E}}[G^{A}_{kl}]{\mathbb{E}}[G^{B}_{kl}]=0
.
Hence
{\mathbb{E}}[Z]=rNM
.

Variance.
By symmetry,

\sum_{k\neq l}G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}=2S
with

S\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq r}G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}
. We claim

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[G^{A}_{kl}G^{A}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\bigr]\;=\;N\cdot\mathbf{1}\{(k,l)=(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\}\qquad\text{for }k<l,~k^{\prime}<l^{\prime}.

(30)

Indeed,

G^{A}_{kl}G^{A}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}=\sum_{i,i^{\prime}=1}^{N}\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il}\sigma^{A}_{i^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\sigma^{A}_{i^{\prime}l^{\prime}}
.
By the joint i.i.d. Rademacher hypothesis, all
\{\sigma^{A}_{pq}\}_{(p,q)\in[N]\times[r]}

are mutually independent, so the expectation of any finite product
factorises across distinct
(p,q)
pairs. We split by the coincidence
pattern of
i
and
i^{\prime}
:

•

i\neq i^{\prime}
.
The four indices

(i,k),(i,l),(i^{\prime},k^{\prime}),(i^{\prime},l^{\prime})
are pairwise distinct (as
k\neq l

and
k^{\prime}\neq l^{\prime}
and
i\neq i^{\prime}
), so the expectation factorises
into four separate factors
{\mathbb{E}}[\sigma^{A}_{ik}]\cdots{\mathbb{E}}[\sigma^{A}_{i^{\prime}l^{\prime}}]=0
.

•

i=i^{\prime}
.
The product becomes

\sigma^{A}_{ik}\sigma^{A}_{il}\sigma^{A}_{ik^{\prime}}\sigma^{A}_{il^{\prime}}
,
whose expectation equals
1
iff each column index

q\in\{k,l,k^{\prime},l^{\prime}\}
appears with even multiplicity in the
multiset and equals
0
otherwise. Under
k<l
and
k^{\prime}<l^{\prime}
,
even multiplicity forces
\{k,l\}=\{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}\}
as multisets, hence

(k,l)=(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})
. In the diagonal case each index appears exactly
twice,
(\sigma^{A}_{i\cdot})^{2}=1
, and the expectation is
1
;
summing over
i\in[N]
contributes
N
.

This proves Eq. (
30
); the identical argument with

B
in place of
A
gives

{\mathbb{E}}[G^{B}_{kl}G^{B}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}]=M\cdot\mathbf{1}\{(k,l)=(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\}

for
k<l
,
k^{\prime}<l^{\prime}
. By
\sigma^{A}\perp\sigma^{B}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}G^{A}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}G^{B}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[G^{A}_{kl}G^{A}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\bigr]{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[G^{B}_{kl}G^{B}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\bigr]=NM\cdot\mathbf{1}\{(k,l)=(k^{\prime},l^{\prime})\}.

Summing over the
\binom{r}{2}
pairs with
k<l
,

{\mathbb{E}}[S^{2}]\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k<l,\\
k^{\prime}<l^{\prime}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[G^{A}_{kl}G^{B}_{kl}G^{A}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}G^{B}_{k^{\prime}l^{\prime}}\bigr]\;=\;\binom{r}{2}NM.

Combined with Eq. (
29
) and
Z-rNM=2S
,

\mathrm{Var}(Z)\;=\;{\mathbb{E}}[(Z-{\mathbb{E}}[Z])^{2}]\;=\;4{\mathbb{E}}[S^{2}]\;=\;2r(r-1)NM\;\leq\;2r^{2}NM.

Chebyshev.
Since
Z\geq 0
,

\{Z<rNM/2\}=\{rNM-Z>rNM/2\}\subseteq\{|Z-rNM|>rNM/2\}
.
Chebyshev’s inequality therefore gives

\Pr\bigl[Z<rNM/2\bigr]\;\leq\;\Pr\bigl[|Z-rNM|>rNM/2\bigr]\;\leq\;\frac{\mathrm{Var}(Z)}{(rNM/2)^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{2r^{2}NM}{r^{2}N^{2}M^{2}/4}\;=\;\frac{8}{NM},

which is the complementary form of Eq. (
28
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
, Eq. (
10
).

Transfer of the absolute bound to the joint space.

In the relative statement of Theorem
4.1
we assume

NM>8
and
\delta\in(0,1-8/(NM))
, so the final probability lower

Ratio on

.

By definition of
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
,

\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}=\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}\sum_{i,j}W_{ij}^{2}\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}NMr.

Combining with the formal upper bound on

provided by

{\mathcal{E}}_{1}
,

\frac{\|E\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{CNMr\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})\log(2NM/\delta)}{\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}NMr}\;=\;2C\cdot\frac{\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})}{\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}}\log(2NM/\delta).

(31)

Let
\mu_{\max}\coloneqq\max(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
and

\mu_{\min}\coloneqq\min(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
; the hypothesis

\zeta\leq\mu_{\max}
yields

\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2}\leq 3\mu_{\max}^{2}
.
Using
\mu_{A}\mu_{B}=\mu_{\max}\mu_{\min}
,

\frac{\zeta^{2}(\mu_{A}^{2}+\mu_{B}^{2}+\zeta^{2})}{\mu_{A}^{2}\mu_{B}^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{3\zeta^{2}\mu_{\max}^{2}}{\mu_{\max}^{2}\mu_{\min}^{2}}\;=\;\frac{3\zeta^{2}}{\mu_{\min}^{2}}.

Substituting into Eq. (
31
) yields

\frac{\|E\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}^{2}}\;\leq\;\frac{6C\zeta^{2}}{\mu_{\min}^{2}}\log(2NM/\delta).

Taking square roots (both sides are non-negative) and setting

C^{\prime}\coloneqq\sqrt{6C}>0
proves
Eq. (
10
) on the event

{\mathcal{E}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\cap{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
, whose probability is
at least
1-\delta-8/(NM)
.

Small-noise transfer.
Put

\tau\coloneqq C^{\prime}\zeta\sqrt{\log(2NM/\delta)}/\mu_{\min}
and
assume
\tau\leq 1/2
. On
{\mathcal{E}}
, Eq. (
10
)
gives
\|E\|_{F}\leq\tau\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}
, and
the reverse triangle inequality

\bigl|\|AB^{\top}\|_{F}-\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}\bigr|\leq\|AB^{\top}-\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}=\|E\|_{F}

implies

\|AB^{\top}\|_{F}\;\geq\;\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}-\|E\|_{F}\;\geq\;(1-\tau)\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}\;\geq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}.

Dividing the bound Eq. (
10
) by a denominator at
least
\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Delta W(\theta^{\star})\|_{F}
costs at most a
factor of
2
, which establishes the transferred bound

\|E\|_{F}/\|AB^{\top}\|_{F}\leq 2\tau
on
{\mathcal{E}}
.
∎

The theorem is
honest
in the sense that it tracks the actual
quantity controlling LoRDBA error—the residual-to-mean ratio

\zeta/\min(\mu_{A},\mu_{B})
. A naive “
1
-bit is lossless to

\varepsilon
” claim is false for arbitrary fp16 adapters (sign
quantisation loses
\Theta(1)
of the Frobenius signal whenever the
entries are concentrated on a sphere rather than near
\pm\mu
); the
bound above degrades gracefully in the relevant regime and becomes
vacuous precisely where sign quantisation should fail. The ratio is
directly observable on any trained adapter, as we verify next.

C.5
Sign consistency and observed-sign reconstruction

The canonical reconstruction
\theta^{\star}
uses the latent sign
arrays
\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B}
, which are not in general equal to the
observed entrywise signs
\sign(A),\sign(B)
. The next lemma shows
that the two coincide with high probability whenever the residual is
small relative to the latent mean, so the observed-sign LoRDBA adapter
inherits the same Theorem
4.1
guarantees up to an
explicit additional failure probability.

Lemma C.5

(Sign consistency of the canonical reconstruction)
.

Under Assumption
C.1
, the latent sign arrays

\sigma^{A},\sigma^{B}
and the observed entrywise sign patterns

\sign(A),\sign(B)
agree simultaneously with probability at least

1\;-\;2Nr\exp\bigl(-\mu_{A}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr)\;-\;2Mr\exp\bigl(-\mu_{B}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr).

(32)

Equivalently, on the event of probability (
32
),

\sign(A)\;=\;\sigma^{A},\qquad\sign(B)\;=\;\sigma^{B}.

(33)

Consequently, on the same event, the observed-sign choice

B_{1}=\sign(A)
,
B_{2}=\sign(B^{\top})
equals the latent-sign binary
factors of the canonical reconstruction in
Eq. (
17
).

Proof.

Fix an entry
(i,k)\in[N]\times[r]
of
A
. By
Assumption
C.1
(i)–(ii),

A_{ik}\;=\;\mu_{A}\sigma^{A}_{ik}+\xi^{A}_{ik},\qquad\sigma^{A}_{ik}\in\{\pm 1\},\qquad\mu_{A}>0,\qquad\|\xi^{A}_{ik}\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\zeta.

We claim the implication

\sign(A_{ik})\neq\sigma^{A}_{ik}\;\Longrightarrow\;|\xi^{A}_{ik}|\geq\mu_{A}.

(34)

Indeed, multiplying
A_{ik}
by
\sigma^{A}_{ik}\in\{\pm 1\}
gives

\sigma^{A}_{ik}A_{ik}\;=\;\mu_{A}+\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{A}_{ik}.

Under the convention
\sign(0)=+1
, a mismatch implies

\sigma^{A}_{ik}A_{ik}\leq 0
: if
\sigma^{A}_{ik}=+1
then the
mismatch forces
A_{ik}<0
, while if
\sigma^{A}_{ik}=-1
it forces

A_{ik}\geq 0
. Therefore

\sigma^{A}_{ik}A_{ik}=\mu_{A}+\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{A}_{ik}\leq 0,

which implies
\sigma^{A}_{ik}\xi^{A}_{ik}\leq-\mu_{A}
and hence

|\xi^{A}_{ik}|\geq\mu_{A}
. This proves
Eq. (
34
).

By Eq. (
34
) and
Lemma
B.4
of Appendix
B
applied
to
\xi^{A}_{ik}
with
K=\zeta
and
\mu=\mu_{A}
,

\Pr\bigl[\sign(A_{ik})\neq\sigma^{A}_{ik}\bigr]\;\leq\;\Pr\bigl[|\xi^{A}_{ik}|\geq\mu_{A}\bigr]\;\leq\;2\exp\bigl(-\mu_{A}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr).

(35)

The identical argument with
A
replaced by
B
and
\mu_{A}
by

\mu_{B}
gives, for every
(j,k)\in[M]\times[r]
,

\Pr\bigl[\sign(B_{jk})\neq\sigma^{B}_{jk}\bigr]\;\leq\;2\exp\bigl(-\mu_{B}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr).

(36)

A union bound over the
Nr
entries of
A
and the
Mr
entries of

B
, using
Eqs. (
35
)–(
36
),
yields

\Pr\bigl[\sign(A)\neq\sigma^{A}\ \text{or}\ \sign(B)\neq\sigma^{B}\bigr]\;\leq\;2Nr\exp\bigl(-\mu_{A}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr)+2Mr\exp\bigl(-\mu_{B}^{2}/\zeta^{2}\bigr).

(37)

The complement is exactly Eq. (
33
) and has
probability at least the value displayed in
Eq. (
32
).

On the event Eq. (
33
), the observed-sign binary
factors satisfy

B_{1}\;=\;\sign(A)\;=\;\sigma^{A},\qquad B_{2}\;=\;\sign(B^{\top})\;=\;\sign(B)^{\top}\;=\;(\sigma^{B})^{\top},

because
\sign
is applied entrywise and is therefore preserved under
transposition. These coincide with the binary factors of the
canonical reconstruction in Eq. (
17
), completing
the proof.
∎

(LoRDBA envelope-rank monotonicity)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
, for every

\ell\geq 1
,

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{\ell}}\|AB^{\top}-\Delta W(\theta)\|_{F}

is bounded by the absolute bound in Eq. (

) and,
in the small-noise regime
\tau\leq 1/2
, by a factor of two times
the right-hand side of Eq. (
10
) relative to

\|AB^{\top}\|_{F}
.

Proof.

Apply
\iota_{\ell}
iteratively to
\theta^{\star}\in\Theta_{1}
to
obtain a feasible point with the same
\Delta W
in
\Theta_{\ell}
,
then invoke Theorem
4.1

.
∎

Corollary C.7

(Observed-sign reconstruction)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
and define the
observed-sign LoRDBA adapter

\widehat{\theta}\;\coloneqq\;\bigl(\sign(A),\sign(B)^{\top},{\bm{1}}_{N},\mu_{A}\mu_{B}{\bm{1}}_{r},{\bm{1}}_{M}\bigr).

(38)

C.5
,

(39)

Consequently, every bound in Theorem
4.1
and
Corollary

applies to

\widehat{\theta}

Proof.

, on the event

\widehat{\theta}

Corollary C.8

(Operator-norm consequence)
.

Under the hypotheses used for the absolute bound in Theorem
4.1
,

),
which holds with probability at least

.

C.6
Gauge fixing and empirical identifiability of Assumption
C.1

LoRA factor gauge.

The product
AB^{\top}
is invariant under the per-column positive
rescaling

A_{:k}\;\mapsto\;d_{k}A_{:k},\qquad B_{:k}\;\mapsto\;d_{k}^{-1}B_{:k},\qquad d_{k}>0,~k\in[r_{0}],

(42)

since
(d_{k}A_{:k})(d_{k}^{-1}B_{:k})^{\top}=A_{:k}B_{:k}^{\top}
.
The natural plug-in proxies for
(\mu_{A},\mu_{B},\zeta)
depend on the
particular gauge. We therefore compute every diagnostic in the
following
column-balancing gauge
: for every nonzero column pair

(A_{:k},B_{:k})
, set

d_{k}\;=\;\bigl(\|B_{:k}\|_{2}/\|A_{:k}\|_{2}\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad A_{:k}\leftarrow d_{k}A_{:k},\qquad B_{:k}\leftarrow d_{k}^{-1}B_{:k};

(43)

zero-contribution column pairs (if any) are left unchanged and
excluded from the diagnostic. By construction this transformation
preserves
AB^{\top}
and the entrywise sign patterns of both columns
(since
d_{k}>0
), and equalises the column norms

\|A_{:k}\|_{2}=\|B_{:k}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\|A_{:k}^{\mathrm{old}}\|_{2}\|B_{:k}^{\mathrm{old}}\|_{2}}

for every nonzero pair. Thus the LoRA update and the canonical LoRDBA
sign factors are unchanged, while the plug-in magnitude diagnostics
are computed in a fixed, reproducible gauge. Eq. (
43
)
is the gauge used by every empirical statement of the main paper and by
every diagnostic in this appendix.

Plug-in estimators.

For any trained fp16 LoRA factors
A,B
in the gauge of
Eq. (
43
), the quantities in
Assumption
C.1
are empirically identifiable by the
plug-in estimators

\widehat{\mu}_{A}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{Nr_{0}}\sum_{i,k}|A_{ik}|,~~\widehat{\mu}_{B}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{Mr_{0}}\sum_{j,k}|B_{jk}|,~~\widehat{\zeta}_{A}\coloneqq\bigl(\tfrac{1}{Nr_{0}}\sum_{i,k}(|A_{ik}|-\widehat{\mu}_{A})^{2}\bigr)^{1/2},

\widehat{\zeta}_{B}
analogous, and

\widehat{\zeta}\coloneqq\max(\widehat{\zeta}_{A},\widehat{\zeta}_{B})
.
The reported residual-to-mean ratio is

\widehat{\zeta}/\min(\widehat{\mu}_{A},\widehat{\mu}_{B})
.
Table
5
reports this quantity on the LoRA adapters
used in our experiments. The observed
0.18
–
0.27
window justifies
the small-noise regime and, together with Theorem
4.1
,
predicts the
\approx 20\%
relative reconstruction error we measure
in Table

.

Appendix D
Optional Refinement: PTQ-LoRDBA via Consensus ADMM

This appendix records the optional, training-data-free route that
refines a pre-trained fp16 LoRA adapter into a LoRDBA adapter without
running the end-to-end smooth-sign trainer of
Section
3.2
. It is included for completeness: the
LoRDBA adapter of Definition
3.1
is unchanged, the
inference kernel of Section
3.3
is reused unchanged, and
empirical comparisons in
Appendix
H
–
L
confirm
that the end-to-end trainer is the preferred route under the
adapter-mode conditions (A1)–(A4) of Section
2
. PTQ
becomes attractive only when an fp16 LoRA is already available and the
device cannot spare a training pass.

Setup.

Let an fp16 LoRA adapter
\Delta W^{\star}=A^{\star}(B^{\star})^{\top}

of rank
r_{0}
be derived using any standard fine-tuning recipe, and
fix a target carrier rank
R\in\mathbb{N}
. The
\ell=1
PTQ-LoRDBA
problem is the Frobenius least-squares fitting problem

\min_{\theta=(B_{1},B_{2},{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})}\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|\Delta W^{\star}-\Delta W(\theta)\bigr\|_{F}^{2}~\text{s.t.}~B_{1}\in\{\pm 1\}^{N\times R},B_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}^{R\times M}.

(44)

The general
\ell\geq 2
case replaces the single scale triple by a sum
of
\ell
scale triples; all updates below generalise
block-coordinate-wise by viewing
({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
as
stacked matrices (Algorithm
3
).
Eq. (
44
) is non-convex, non-smooth, and combinatorial
in
(B_{1},B_{2})
but decouples along the three continuous variables
for fixed binaries and along each binary for fixed other variables.

Consensus ADMM.

To exploit this separability while respecting the discrete constraint,
we introduce continuous relaxations

U_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times R}
,
U_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{R\times M}
of

(B_{1},B_{2})
together with binary copies
M_{1},M_{2}
, and minimise

f(U;{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})+I_{\{\pm 1\}}(M_{1})+I_{\{\pm 1\}}(M_{2}),~\text{s.t.}~U_{k}-M_{k}=0,k\in\{1,2\},

(45)

with

f(U;{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta W^{\star}-\diag({\bm{\alpha}})U_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})U_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\|_{F}^{2}

and
I_{\{\pm 1\}}
as the indicator of the binary set. Using per-block
normalisers
n_{1}=NR
,
n_{2}=RM
, scaled penalties

\widetilde{\rho}_{k}=\rho/n_{k}
, and scaled duals
Y_{1},Y_{2}
,
the (Gauss–Seidel) sweep over the consensus-scaled augmented
Lagrangian
[
Boyd et al. 2011
,
Themelis and Patrinos 2020
]
produces the
following ordered updates, where
\widehat{U}^{(t,1)}\coloneqq(U_{1},U_{2}^{(t)})

and
\widehat{U}^{(t,2)}\coloneqq(U_{1}^{(t+1)},U_{2})
make the
within-sweep dependency explicit:

\displaystyle U_{1}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{U_{1}}f\bigl(U_{1},U_{2}^{(t)};{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)}\bigr)+\frac{\widetilde{\rho}_{1}}{2}\|U_{1}-M_{1}^{(t)}+Y_{1}^{(t)}\|_{F}^{2},

(46)

\displaystyle U_{2}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{U_{2}}f\bigl(U_{1}^{(t+1)},U_{2};{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)}\bigr)+\frac{\widetilde{\rho}_{2}}{2}\|U_{2}-M_{2}^{(t)}+Y_{2}^{(t)}\|_{F}^{2},

(47)

\displaystyle({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\text{one block-coordinate sweep of }\argmin_{{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}}}f\bigl(U^{(t+1)};{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}}\bigr),

(48)

\displaystyle M_{k}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\sign\bigl(U_{k}^{(t+1)}+Y_{k}^{(t)}\bigr),~Y_{k}^{(t+1)}=Y_{k}^{(t)}+U_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t+1)},~k\in\{1,2\}.

(49)

We collectively refer to Eqs. (
46
)–(
47
)
as the
U
-step
.
Each
U_{k}
-subproblem is a strongly convex Tikhonov least-squares
problem in
U_{k}
(its Hessian is the sum of a positive-semidefinite
normal-equation Gram matrix and
\widetilde{\rho}_{k}I
), and hence
admits a unique minimiser regardless of the conditioning of the
cross-covariance. Eq. (
48
) is a block-coordinate
sweep in the three scale variables: with the other two fixed, the
subproblem in
{\bm{\alpha}}
decouples into
N
scalar least-squares
(one per coordinate), the subproblem in
{\bm{\gamma}}
into
M
scalar
least-squares, and the subproblem in
{\bm{\beta}}
is an

R
-dimensional positive semidefinite least-squares system whose
normal matrix is the Hadamard product of two Gram matrices and is hence
positive semidefinite by the Schur product theorem; the exact normal
equations and coordinate-wise minimisers are recorded in
Appendix
I.1
. Eq. (
49
) is the
sign-projection step modulated by the dual
Y_{k}
, which biases the
projection by the accumulated discretisation residual.

We warm-start the iteration from the rank-
R
thin SVD of

\Delta W^{\star}
exactly as in Eq. (
7
) of the main
text, with
(M^{(0)},Y^{(0)})=(U^{(0)},0)
. A Boyd-style
residual-balancing schedule on
\rho
, with the default

(\tau,\mu)=(2,10)
, is truncated at
t_{\max}^{\rho}=K/2
,
ensuring the second half of the run is a fixed-penalty tail, exactly
the regime addressed by the rigorous stabilisation theorem of
Appendix
E
.

Finite stabilisation on a fixed-penalty tail.

Define the sign-test matrices

Z_{k}^{(t)}\coloneqq U_{k}^{(t+1)}+Y_{k}^{(t)}
so that the binary
update is precisely
M_{k}^{(t+1)}=\sign(Z_{k}^{(t)})
. Once a
fixed-penalty tail has converged to a single limit and the limit
remains at a positive distance from the sign boundary
0
, the signs
of
Z_{k}^{(t)}
can no longer change, and the binary iterate freezes.
The precise penalty-agnostic statement is
Theorem
E.3
in
Appendix
E
, and its convergence hypothesis is
delivered in turn by Theorem
E.6
of the same
appendix, which combines a sufficient-decrease Lyapunov inequality with
the Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz inequality on the semi-algebraic Lyapunov
function to guarantee full convergence of the iterate sequence to a
single limit. Empirically, the freeze occurs within

K^{\star}\lesssim 50
sweeps for every model and rank tested.

Appendix E
Convergence of PTQ-LoRDBA

Throughout this appendix we adopt the shorthands

n_{1}\;\coloneqq\;NR,\qquad n_{2}\;\coloneqq\;RM,\qquad\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\;\coloneqq\;\rho/n_{k},\qquad k\in\{1,2\},

(50)

and recall the PTQ-LoRDBA objective

f(U;{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})\;\coloneqq\;\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\|\Delta W^{\star}-\diag({\bm{\alpha}})U_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})U_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\bigr\|_{F}^{2}.

(51)

For fixed scale vectors and one of the two
U
-blocks,
f
is a convex
quadratic because the map from that block to the reconstructed matrix
is linear. The binary projection step, however, is discontinuous at
zero sign-test entries. Consequently, boundedness and Lipschitz
smoothness alone do not imply finite binary stabilisation. We therefore
state the convergence facts in the exact form used by the paper: a
penalty-agnostic sign-margin theorem, and a standard KL-tail theorem
whose sufficient-decrease and relative-error hypotheses must hold on
the fixed-penalty tail.

Assumption E.1

(Bounded fixed-penalty tail)
.

There exists an iteration
T_{\rho}
after which the penalty is fixed,

\rho^{(t)}\equiv\rho>0
. On this tail, all iterates

(U^{(t)},M^{(t)},Y^{(t)},{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)})

belong to a compact set
\mathcal{C}
. The
U_{1}
- and

U_{2}
-subproblems in Eqs. (
46
)–(
47
)
are solved exactly, the scale update is well defined, and the partial
gradients
\nabla_{U_{1}}f
and
\nabla_{U_{2}}f
are Lipschitz on

\mathcal{C}
with respect to all their arguments.

This assumption is the bounded-tail regularity used in the convergence
statements below. It is not a consequence of discreteness alone; in an
implementation it is enforced by the usual safeguards on the continuous
least-squares and scale variables.

Lemma E.2

(Gradient–dual identity on a fixed-penalty tail)
.

For
t\geq T_{\rho}
define the Gauss–Seidel arguments

\widehat{U}^{(t,1)}\;\coloneqq\;(U_{1}^{(t+1)},U_{2}^{(t)}),\qquad\widehat{U}^{(t,2)}\;\coloneqq\;(U_{1}^{(t+1)},U_{2}^{(t+1)}).

(52)

Then, for every
k\in\{1,2\}
,

\rho Y_{k}^{(t+1)}\;+\;n_{k}\nabla_{U_{k}}f\bigl(\widehat{U}^{(t,k)};{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)}\bigr)\;+\;\rho\bigl(M_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}\bigr)\;=\;0.

(53)

Proof.

The first-order condition of the exact
U_{k}
-subproblem is

\nabla_{U_{k}}f\bigl(\widehat{U}^{(t,k)};{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)}\bigr)+\widetilde{\rho}_{k}\bigl(U_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}+Y_{k}^{(t)}\bigr)=0.

The dual update gives

U_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}+Y_{k}^{(t)}=Y_{k}^{(t+1)}+\bigl(M_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}\bigr).

Multiplying by
n_{k}
and using

n_{k}\widetilde{\rho}_{k}=\rho
yields Eq. (
53
).
∎

E.1
Finite stabilisation under a sign margin

The next theorem is the stabilisation statement invoked in the main
text. It assumes convergence of the fixed-penalty tail and proves that
a positive limiting sign margin forces the binary iterate to freeze.

Theorem E.3

(Finite stabilisation on a fixed-penalty tail)
.

Fix the penalty parameters

\widetilde{\rho}_{1},\widetilde{\rho}_{2}>0
on the tail

t\geq T_{\rho}
, and assume that the resulting PTQ-LoRDBA iterates

(U_{k}^{(t)},M_{k}^{(t)},Y_{k}^{(t)},{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)})

converge to a limit

(U_{k}^{\infty},M_{k}^{\infty},Y_{k}^{\infty},{\bm{\alpha}}^{\infty},{\bm{\beta}}^{\infty},{\bm{\gamma}}^{\infty})

for every
k\in\{1,2\}
. Define

Z_{k}^{(t)}\;\coloneqq\;U_{k}^{(t+1)}+Y_{k}^{(t)},\qquad Z_{k}^{\infty}\;\coloneqq\;U_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty},\qquad k\in\{1,2\},

(54)

and suppose the strict sign-margin condition

\eta\;\coloneqq\;\min_{k\in\{1,2\}}\min_{a,b}\bigl|(Z_{k}^{\infty})_{ab}\bigr|\;>\;0.

(55)

Then:

(i)

There exists a finite iteration

T_{\star}\geq T_{\rho}
such that, for every
t\geq T_{\star}
and

k\in\{1,2\}
,

M_{k}^{(t+1)}=\sign(Z_{k}^{\infty})\eqqcolon M_{k}^{\infty}.

(56)

(ii)

If the
U_{k}
-subproblems are solved exactly
and
f
is continuously differentiable in
U
, the limit satisfies

U_{k}^{\infty}=M_{k}^{\infty},\qquad M_{k}^{\infty}=\sign(M_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty}),\qquad\rho Y_{k}^{\infty}+n_{k}\nabla_{U_{k}}f(U^{\infty};{\bm{\alpha}}^{\infty},{\bm{\beta}}^{\infty},{\bm{\gamma}}^{\infty})=0.

(57)

(iii)

Equivalently,

M_{k}^{\infty}=\sign\Bigl(M_{k}^{\infty}-\tfrac{1}{\widetilde{\rho}_{k}}\nabla_{U_{k}}f(U^{\infty};{\bm{\alpha}}^{\infty},{\bm{\beta}}^{\infty},{\bm{\gamma}}^{\infty})\Bigr).

(58)

Proof.

Since
U_{k}^{(t+1)}\to U_{k}^{\infty}
and

Y_{k}^{(t)}\to Y_{k}^{\infty}
, we have

Z_{k}^{(t)}\to Z_{k}^{\infty}
entrywise. Choose

T_{\star}
so that

\|Z_{k}^{(t)}-Z_{k}^{\infty}\|_{\max}<\eta/2
for both
k
and all

t\geq T_{\star}
. Then every entry of
Z_{k}^{(t)}
has the same sign
as the corresponding entry of
Z_{k}^{\infty}
, proving
Eq. (
56
) from

M_{k}^{(t+1)}=\sign(Z_{k}^{(t)})
.

For
t\geq T_{\star}
the dual update becomes

Y_{k}^{(t+1)}-Y_{k}^{(t)}=U_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{\infty}
. Passing to
the limit gives
U_{k}^{\infty}=M_{k}^{\infty}
. Substituting this
identity into
M_{k}^{\infty}=\sign(Z_{k}^{\infty})
gives the second
relation in Eq. (
57
). Finally, for

t\geq T_{\star}+1
, the term
M_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}
in
Eq. (
53
) is zero; passing to the limit in
Eq. (
53
) yields the gradient–dual stationarity
relation. Solving it for
Y_{k}^{\infty}
and using

n_{k}/\rho=1/\widetilde{\rho}_{k}
gives
Eq. (
58
).
∎

Remark E.4
.

The sign-margin condition is checkable on a converged fixed-penalty
tail. If an entry of
Z_{k}^{\infty}
is exactly zero, the entrywise
binary projection is not unique; in that boundary case the robust
statement is the argmin form used in Theorem
E.6

below rather than the single-valued sign equation.

E.2
KL convergence of a regular fixed-penalty tail

The following theorem is the rigorous convergence statement for the
ADMM tail. It is deliberately conditional: the nonconvex binary
projection does not yield an unconditional descent estimate without an
additional margin or regularisation condition. The assumptions below
are the standard sufficient-decrease and relative-error hypotheses used
in KL analyses of nonconvex ADMM and block-coordinate methods.

Let

\mathcal{X}^{(t)}\coloneqq\bigl(U^{(t)},M^{(t)},Y^{(t)},{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)},M^{(t-1)}\bigr),\qquad t\geq T_{\rho}+1,

(59)

where the last component records the one-step memory needed by the
usual ADMM Lyapunov functions.

Assumption E.5

(KL-regular ADMM tail)
.

On the fixed-penalty tail of Assumption
E.1
, there
exist a proper lower-semicontinuous semi-algebraic Lyapunov function

\Phi
on the state variable
\mathcal{X}
, constants
a,b>0
, and an
iteration
T\geq T_{\rho}+1
such that, for every
t\geq T
,

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{X}^{(t)})-\Phi(\mathcal{X}^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle\geq a\|\mathcal{X}^{(t+1)}-\mathcal{X}^{(t)}\|^{2},

(60)

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial\Phi(\mathcal{X}^{(t+1)})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq b\|\mathcal{X}^{(t+1)}-\mathcal{X}^{(t)}\|.

(61)

The sequence
\{\Phi(\mathcal{X}^{(t)})\}_{t\geq T}
is bounded below.

The semi-algebraicity requirement is natural here: after introducing
the finite binary indicators, all remaining terms in the augmented
Lagrangian are polynomial functions of the continuous variables. The
substantive algorithm-dependent requirements are the two inequalities
(
60
)–(
61
); they
are the precise conditions under which the KL convergence conclusion is
valid.

Theorem E.6

(KL convergence of a regular fixed-penalty tail)
.

Suppose Assumptions
E.1
and
E.5
hold.
Then the PTQ-LoRDBA fixed-penalty tail has finite length and converges
to a single limit

\bigl(U^{(t)},M^{(t)},Y^{(t)},{\bm{\alpha}}^{(t)},{\bm{\beta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\gamma}}^{(t)}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(U^{\infty},M^{\star},Y^{\infty},{\bm{\alpha}}^{\infty},{\bm{\beta}}^{\infty},{\bm{\gamma}}^{\infty}\bigr).

(62)

Moreover:

(i)

The increments of all tail variables are
summable, hence square-summable.

(ii)

The binary iterate terminates in finitely many
steps: there is a finite
K^{\star}
such that

M^{(t)}=M^{\star}
for every
t\geq K^{\star}
.

(iii)

The limit satisfies, for each
k\in\{1,2\}
,

U_{k}^{\infty}=M_{k}^{\star},\qquad M_{k}^{\star}\in\argmin_{m\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_{k}}}\|m-(U_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty})\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\rho Y_{k}^{\infty}+n_{k}\nabla_{U_{k}}f(U^{\infty};{\bm{\alpha}}^{\infty},{\bm{\beta}}^{\infty},{\bm{\gamma}}^{\infty})=0.

(63)

If every entry of
U_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty}
is nonzero, the
argmin is the singleton

\{\sign(U_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty})\}
.

Proof.

By Assumption
E.1
, the tail sequence has a cluster
point. Since
\Phi
is semi-algebraic, it satisfies the
Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz inequality at every point of its domain

[
Bolte et al. 2007
,
Attouch et al. 2010
]
. Combining
the KL inequality at a cluster point with the sufficient-decrease and
relative-error estimates
(
60
)–(
61
) gives
the standard finite-length conclusion for descent methods

[
Attouch et al. 2013
,
Wang et al. 2019
]
:

\sum_{t\geq T}\|\mathcal{X}^{(t+1)}-\mathcal{X}^{(t)}\|<\infty.

(64)

Hence
\{\mathcal{X}^{(t)}\}_{t\geq T}
is Cauchy and converges to a
single limit. Projecting this convergence onto the variables

(U,M,Y,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
proves
Eq. (
62
), and claim (i) follows immediately
from Eq. (
64
).

For claim (ii), each entry of

\Delta M_{k}^{(t)}=M_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}
belongs to

\{-2,0,2\}
. Therefore, whenever
\Delta M_{k}^{(t)}\neq 0
,

\|\Delta M_{k}^{(t)}\|_{F}\geq 2
. The summability in claim (i) implies
that only finitely many such nonzero jumps can occur. Thus the two
binary blocks are eventually constant; denote the common tail value by

M^{\star}
.

It remains to verify stationarity. The dual update gives

Y_{k}^{(t+1)}-Y_{k}^{(t)}=U_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t+1)}
. Since the
left-hand side tends to zero by claim (i), passing to the limit gives

U_{k}^{\infty}=M_{k}^{\star}
. The binary update is the exact
entrywise projection

M_{k}^{(t+1)}\in\argmin_{m\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_{k}}}\|m-(U_{k}^{(t+1)}+Y_{k}^{(t)})\|_{F}^{2}.

Taking limits in this finite-dimensional inequality for every

m\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_{k}}
yields the argmin condition in
Eq. (
63
). Finally, by claim (ii) the term

M_{k}^{(t+1)}-M_{k}^{(t)}
in Eq. (
53
) is zero for
all sufficiently large
t
. Passing to the limit in
Eq. (
53
), using the continuity of

\nabla_{U_{k}}f
on
\mathcal{C}
, gives the gradient–dual
stationarity equation in Eq. (
63
). If

U_{k}^{\infty}+Y_{k}^{\infty}
has no zero entries, the binary
projection decouples entrywise and is uniquely given by the sign of
that vector, proving the final assertion.
∎

Appendix F
QAT-LoRDBA: Smooth-Sign STE

QAT-LoRDBA optimizes real-valued latent carrier matrices

U_{1},U_{2}
together with the scale vectors

({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
. On the forward pass it exports the
binary carriers
B_{1}=\sign(U_{1})
and
B_{2}=\sign(U_{2})
inside

\Delta W(\theta)
as in Eq. (
4
); on the backward pass
we use the
smooth-sign
straight-through
estimator
[
Leng et al. 2018
,
Liu et al. 2020
]
for the
non-differentiable sign maps:

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\sign(u)\;\longleftarrow\;\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\tanh(\kappa u)\;=\;\kappa\bigl(1-\tanh^{2}(\kappa u)\bigr),

(65)

with
\kappa=100
by default. The scales

({\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}})
are trained in fp32 and do not require an
STE. We initialise the latent carriers
U_{1},U_{2}
via the SVD warm start of
Eq. (
7
) from a
10\%
-budget warm-up fp16 LoRA run,
which we find substantially stabilises optimisation at carrier rank

R\geq 64
. Algorithm
2
summarises the training
loop.

Appendix I
Pseudocode

I.1
Closed-form scale updates for PTQ-LoRDBA

This subsection derives the per-axis least-squares updates used by
Eq. (
48
). Throughout, let
R
denote the LoRDBA
binary carrier rank of Definition
3.1
(so that

B_{1}\in\{\pm 1\}^{N\times R}
and
B_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}^{R\times M}
);
to avoid the notation clash, we use the symbol
T
for the regression
target, defined below. We treat the canonical single-envelope case

\ell=1
; the multi-envelope case applies the same updates
block-coordinate-wise to each envelope while holding the remaining
envelopes fixed (see the residual definition at the end of this
subsection).

Setup.

Fix the binary carriers
B_{1},B_{2}
and define the regression target

T\;\coloneqq\;\Delta W^{\star}\;\in\;{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}.

(66)

The PTQ-LoRDBA scale subproblem is the joint minimisation

\min_{{\bm{\alpha}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N},{\bm{\beta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{R},{\bm{\gamma}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M}}\;\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T-\diag({\bm{\alpha}})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\bigr\|_{F}^{2},

(67)

which we solve by per-axis block-coordinate descent. Each per-axis
subproblem admits a closed-form least-squares solution, derived next.

{\bm{\alpha}}
-update (rowwise).

For fixed
B_{1},B_{2},{\bm{\beta}},{\bm{\gamma}}
, set

D_{\alpha}\;\coloneqq\;B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\;\in\;{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}.

(68)

Substituting Eq. (
68
) into Eq. (
67
) and
expanding the Frobenius norm rowwise,

\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T-\diag({\bm{\alpha}})D_{\alpha}\bigr\|_{F}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T_{i,:}-\alpha_{i}(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

which decouples over the row index
i
. Each scalar problem

\min_{\alpha_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}}\;\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T_{i,:}-\alpha_{i}(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}

(69)

has the unique minimum-norm least-squares solution

\alpha_{i}\;=\;\begin{cases}\dfrac{\langle T_{i,:},(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\rangle}{\|(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\|_{2}^{2}},&\|(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\|_{2}>0,\\[8.00003pt]
0,&\|(D_{\alpha})_{i,:}\|_{2}=0,\end{cases}\qquad i\in[N].

(70)

{\bm{\gamma}}
-update (columnwise).

For fixed
B_{1},B_{2},{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\beta}}
, set

D_{\gamma}\;\coloneqq\;\diag({\bm{\alpha}})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})B_{2}\;\in\;{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times M}.

(71)

The objective Eq. (
67
) expands columnwise as

\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T-D_{\gamma}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\bigr\|_{F}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|T_{:,j}-\gamma_{j}(D_{\gamma})_{:,j}\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

and analogously decouples over
j\in[M]
. The unique minimum-norm
least-squares solution is

\gamma_{j}\;=\;\begin{cases}\dfrac{\langle T_{:,j},(D_{\gamma})_{:,j}\rangle}{\|(D_{\gamma})_{:,j}\|_{2}^{2}},&\|(D_{\gamma})_{:,j}\|_{2}>0,\\[8.00003pt]
0,&\|(D_{\gamma})_{:,j}\|_{2}=0,\end{cases}\qquad j\in[M].

(72)

{\bm{\beta}}
-update (R-dimensional).

The
{\bm{\beta}}
-update is an
R
-dimensional least-squares problem.
For fixed
B_{1},B_{2},{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}}
, define

P\;\coloneqq\;\diag({\bm{\alpha}})B_{1}\;\in\;{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times R},\qquad Q\;\coloneqq\;B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\;\in\;{\mathbb{R}}^{R\times M}.

(73)

Expanding the bilinear identity

\diag({\bm{\alpha}})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}})\;=\;P\diag({\bm{\beta}})Q\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{R}\beta_{k}P_{:,k}Q_{k,:},

(74)

where the second equality uses the column–row decomposition

\diag({\bm{\beta}})=\sum_{k}\beta_{k}{\bm{e}}_{k}{\bm{e}}_{k}^{\top}
and

P{\bm{e}}_{k}{\bm{e}}_{k}^{\top}Q=P_{:,k}Q_{k,:}
. Vectorising
Eq. (
74
) gives

\operatorname{vec}(P\diag({\bm{\beta}})Q)=H{\bm{\beta}}
with

H\in{\mathbb{R}}^{NM\times R}
defined by

H_{:,k}\;\coloneqq\;\operatorname{vec}(P_{:,k}Q_{k,:}),\qquad k\in[R].

(75)

Hence Eq. (
67
) reduces, in the
{\bm{\beta}}
-direction,
to the standard linear least-squares problem

\min_{{\bm{\beta}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{R}}\;\frac{1}{2}\bigl\|\operatorname{vec}(T)-H{\bm{\beta}}\bigr\|_{2}^{2},

(76)

with normal equations
H^{\top}H{\bm{\beta}}=H^{\top}\operatorname{vec}(T)
. We
record these in our notation as

G_{\beta}{\bm{\beta}}\;=\;{\bm{h}}_{\beta},

(77)

where, for
k,l\in[R]
,

(G_{\beta})_{kl}\;=\;\langle P_{:,k},P_{:,l}\rangle\langle Q_{k,:},Q_{l,:}\rangle,\qquad({\bm{h}}_{\beta})_{k}\;=\;\langle T,P_{:,k}Q_{k,:}\rangle_{F}.

(78)

Equivalently, in matrix form,

G_{\beta}\;=\;(P^{\top}P)\odot(QQ^{\top}),

(79)

where
\odot
is the Hadamard product. Since
P^{\top}P
and
QQ^{\top}

are positive semidefinite,
G_{\beta}
is positive semidefinite by the
Schur product theorem. When
G_{\beta}
is nonsingular, the update is

{\bm{\beta}}=G_{\beta}^{-1}{\bm{h}}_{\beta}
; when singular, we use the
unique minimum-norm least-squares solution

{\bm{\beta}}\;=\;G_{\beta}^{\dagger}{\bm{h}}_{\beta},

(80)

where
G_{\beta}^{\dagger}
denotes the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse.

Generalisation to envelope rank
\ell\geq 2
.

For envelope rank
\ell\geq 2
, the same per-axis updates
Eqs. (
70
), (
72
),
and (
80
) apply to one envelope
i\in[\ell]
at a
time after replacing the target
T
by the per-envelope residual

T^{(i)}\;\coloneqq\;\Delta W^{\star}-\sum_{m\neq i}\diag({\bm{\alpha}}^{(m)})B_{1}\diag({\bm{\beta}}^{(m)})B_{2}\diag({\bm{\gamma}}^{(m)}),\qquad i\in[\ell].

(81)

Cycling the envelope index
i\in[\ell]
in any order produces a
block-coordinate descent on Eq. (
67
) extended to

\ell

form above with
T
replaced by
T^{(i)}
.

matmul

B\in\{\pm 1\}^{p\times q}

fp16 input
{\bm{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
