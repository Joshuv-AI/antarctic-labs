How direct access to physiotherapy reshapes GP gatekeeping for musculoskeletal care in primary care: an exploratory organisational typology across health systems
Authors: Matthieu Fischer, Nicolas Pinsault, Mathilde François, Stéphanie Mignot
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo characterise the organisational role configurations of physiotherapists in direct access (DA) models and describe how general practitioner (GP) gatekeeping for musculoskeletal (MSK) conditions is redistributed across health systems.DesignSecondary qualitative content analysis of organisational descriptions reported in published DA/first-contact physiotherapy (FCP) studies, using a structured extraction matrix and Enhancing Transparency in Reporting the synthesis of Qualitative research (ENTREQ) reporting standards. Configuration dimensions were informed ex ante by gatekeeping and task-shifting frameworks; configurations themselves were identified inductively. A dimension of operationalised treatment authority was derived during revision from existing extraction-matrix fields.SettingPrimary care settings across high-income health systems, including Beveridge-type (UK, Sweden), Bismarck-type (Netherlands, France) and pluralistic systems (USA), identified through a companion systematic review prospectively registered on the Open Science Framework (osf.io/897ub).Participants19 empirical DA/FCP primary studies were eligible for the source review; 14 contained sufficient organisational detail for configuration mapping.ResultsFour configurations were empirically observed and a fifth was theoretically anticipated but not found. Autonomous first-contact MSK clinician models predominated (12 of 14 studies, predominantly Beveridge-type systems) and differentiated into two subconfigurations on the basis of operationalised treatment authority: front-end assessment and signposting (type 3a, 4 studies, including the National Health Service FCP programme) and full-episode autonomous management (type 3b, 8 studies). Protocol-governed triage with mandatory post hoc medical validation (type 2) and advanced practice with extended scope (type 4) were each observed as illustrative variants supported by a single study. DA redistributed rather than abolished gatekeeping.ConclusionsIn well-documented implementations, DA physiotherapy is most commonly configured as a first-contact model that redistributes GP gatekeeping for MSK conditions. This exploratory typology provides a framework for hypothesis generation and future primary research, particularly in under-represented Bismarck-type health systems.Keywords: General Practice, Family Medicine, Health Services Research, Community Medicine, Primary Health Care

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study proposes the first cross-system exploratory organisational typology of direct access models. In documented implementations, physiotherapists most commonly function as autonomous first-contact musculoskeletal clinicians, with two operationally distinct profiles: front-end assessment and signposting (the National Health Service First Contact Physiotherapy pattern) versus full-episode autonomous management. Rather than abolishing gatekeeping, direct access redistributes it along a continuum shaped by regulatory and organisational contexts.

Background

Musculoskeletal (MSK) conditions are among the leading global causes of years lived with disability, affect more than 1.7 billion people globally, and account for a substantial proportion of primary care consultations.1,3

In the context of persistent general practitioner (GP) shortages and rising multimorbidity, direct access (DA) to physiotherapy—defined as the possibility for patients to consult physiotherapists without prior medical referral—has progressively emerged as an organisational innovation aimed at improving access to MSK care while optimising medical resources.4 Multiple systematic reviews have consistently shown that DA is associated with non-inferior clinical outcomes, reduced imaging and medication prescription, and fewer GP consultations for MSK conditions.5,7 In the UK, national deployment of First Contact Physiotherapy (FCP) within general practice has been associated with reduced GP workload and high patient satisfaction, as reported by professional bodies and confirmed in subsequent service evaluations.8 9 Population-level evidence from Norway confirmed reductions in GP MSK consultations associated with DA.10

Despite this robust evidence base on clinical and health-system outcomes, the literature has largely neglected a crucial dimension of DA implementation: the organisational and professional transformation of primary care roles and gatekeeping functions. In this study, ‘gatekeeping’ refers to the first-contact and triage functions that determine entry to MSK pathways, thresholds for investigation or medical review, and onward referral decisions within primary care. We distinguish clinical gatekeeping (triage decisions, red-flag screening, escalation thresholds and clinical responsibility for patient safety) from administrative gatekeeping (control over access to reimbursed care, prescription requirements and regulatory authorisation). While these dimensions often overlap in physician-centred systems, DA may redistribute them differently depending on regulatory and organisational contexts. Many evaluations leave implicit how first-contact responsibility and medicolegal accountability are redistributed between physiotherapists and GPs.

These issues are particularly salient in physician-centred, Bismarck-type systems such as France, Germany and Belgium, where access to allied health services has historically been regulated through medical prescription. France provides a contemporary illustrative case: DA to physiotherapy has been authorised under experimental conditions within Territorial Professional Health Communities (CPTS) with mandatory post hoc medical validation,11,13 and acceptability has been documented among French clinicians.14

From a general practice perspective, DA raises a central question: how does it reorganise gatekeeping and what configurations does it generate between physiotherapists and GPs? Conceptual frameworks on task shifting suggest role expansion produces negotiated shared responsibility rather than simple substitution, but most evidence to date derives from nurse-led models rather than physiotherapy.15

To our knowledge, no study has yet conducted a systematic organisational analysis of DA-induced gatekeeping configurations across health systems. Given the absence of primary organisational data collected specifically for this purpose, a secondary qualitative content analysis of existing DA evaluations represents a necessary first step to identify recurrent organisational patterns and generate hypotheses for future primary research.16 17 The objectives were to: (1) characterise organisational role configurations of physiotherapists in DA models; (2) describe how GP gatekeeping and clinical responsibility are redistributed and (3) identify recurrent organisational patterns among documented implementations.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a secondary qualitative content analysis of organisational descriptions reported in published DA physiotherapy studies.16 17 This design combines elements of secondary qualitative analysis of published data,18 qualitative evidence synthesis and theory-building organisational typology.19 Secondary qualitative analysis allows re-examining existing descriptive material to address new questions18; here, it is applied to organisational features embedded in evaluation studies. The typological component follows analytical organisational typologies, which map the conceptual space of possible configurations rather than estimate prevalence; within this tradition, configurations may be supported by multiple cases, by single illustrative cases or remain theoretically anticipated but empirically unobserved, each scenario offering distinct analytical insight.19 The typology developed here is hybrid in status: its analytical dimensions (physiotherapist autonomy, operationalised treatment authority, GP positioning, gatekeeping pattern) were derived ex ante from theoretical frameworks on gatekeeping, task shifting and advanced practice; configurations themselves were identified inductively from organisational patterns observed in the corpus. One configuration (type 1) was theoretically anticipated as a contrast case and is retained to make explicit the conceptual space against which empirically observed configurations are positioned.

This approach was chosen because no primary studies have specifically examined organisational configurations under DA, making secondary synthesis necessary to identify recurrent patterns and generate hypotheses for future primary research. This work forms part of a broader programme on DA physiotherapy impacts on GP workload and primary care organisation. Reporting follows ENTREQ guidelines for qualitative evidence synthesis.20 We applied ENTREQ because the analytic component is a qualitative synthesis of textual organisational descriptions, while acknowledging that the corpus consisted predominantly of intervention evaluation studies rather than primary qualitative research. A completed checklist is provided in online supplemental additional file 4.

Researcher position and reflexivity

The research team comprised three members: a GP resident with a prior 12-year clinical career as a physiotherapist and two senior researchers with expertise in primary care organisation and interprofessional collaboration. This combination provided insider understanding of professional dynamics but created potential for confirmation bias. To mitigate this risk: (1) the extraction matrix was developed before examining individual studies; its analytical dimensions were informed ex ante by gatekeeping and task-shifting frameworks, while configurations themselves were identified inductively, with type 1 retained as a theoretically anticipated contrast case; (2) configuration assignments by the first author were independently reviewed by all co-authors with an explicit search for disconfirming evidence and (3) a decision log of coding disagreements was maintained.

Data sources and corpus

The corpus consisted of 19 empirical DA studies previously identified through a companion systematic review assessing the impacts of DA physiotherapy on GP workload, imaging, medication and costs.21 The scope of the source review necessarily bounded the dataset available for the present secondary analysis: regulatory documents, service specifications, qualitative-only studies and evaluations of prescription-bound DA arrangements without measurable effect on GP workload were not eligible for the source review and are therefore not represented here. The interpretive implications, particularly for the absence of empirically observed type 1 configurations, are addressed in the limitations section. In the original review, two independent reviewers selected studies and extracted data using a structured form. Searches covered MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, Web of Science and Cochrane Library from inception to 4 November 2025, without language restrictions, and included randomised trials, cohort studies, service evaluations and mixed-methods studies evaluating DA or FCP models for MSK conditions in primary care. The protocol was prospectively registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) (OSF registration: osf.io/897ub). A condensed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)-style summary of the source review (search strategy, screening, eligibility, study selection) is provided in online supplemental additional file 5; full methodological details are reported in the companion review.21 The 19 studies spanned several high-income countries: the UK, including FCP in general practice,922,29 Sweden30,33; the USA34,36; the Netherlands37; France38 and Ecuador.39 To contextualise and triangulate configuration descriptions, seven review-level syntheses on DA/FCP and advanced practice physiotherapy published between 2012 and 2024 were consulted: six systematic reviews5,740 and one scoping review.43 Used solely for contextual comparison, they supported cross-validation of role descriptions and identification of models in under-represented settings.

Eligibility for organisational configuration analysis

Each of the 19 primary studies was assessed for the presence and strength of organisational detail. A study was deemed eligible if it reported at least one concrete pathway element describing: (1) who was the first clinician contacted for MSK presentations; (2) who held triage/redirect authority (PT, GP, nurse, shared); (3) whether GP input was mandatory and under what circumstances or (4) explicit MSK gatekeeping rules (exclusive, shared, delegated, distributed).

Extraction matrix and variables

We developed a structured extraction matrix implemented as an Excel workbook (online supplemental additional file 1), organised into seven thematic blocks: (1) study identification and design; (2) health system context (Beveridge/Bismarck/hybrid classification, primary care organisation); (3) physiotherapist role in DA (first-contact status, clinical autonomy, triage responsibility, treatment authority, access to imaging/prescribing); (4) GP role and gatekeeping (position in care pathway, necessity of GP validation, involvement in triage, medical oversight); (5) legal and institutional framework (regulatory basis, interprofessional protocols, liability allocation); (6) qualitative and descriptive material (explicit role descriptions, strength of organisational information) and (7) configuration assignment with justification. The complete extraction matrix is available as online supplemental additional file 1.

Analytical framework

The analytical framework combined deductive and inductive components. Deductively, the framework drew on conceptualisations of gatekeeping and first-contact care, sociological analyses of boundary negotiation in task shifting, and advanced practice physiotherapy frameworks.40 Inductively, configurations were identified from recurring organisational patterns. The unit of analysis was each study’s description of its DA service or model. Coding was conducted using the structured extraction matrix by the first author and refined through discussion with co-authors.

The analytic process comprised three stages. First, we extracted all text describing professional roles, care pathways, referral arrangements and gatekeeping responsibilities. Second, we coded these segments across four primary domains during the initial analysis: PT clinical autonomy (low/moderate/high/extended); distribution of triage and decision-making (GP-exclusive/PT-supervised/PT-autonomous/shared); GP role (gatekeeper/supervisor/consultant/safety net); and gatekeeping pattern (exclusive medical/shared hierarchical/delegated/distributed), distinguishing clinical from administrative gatekeeping. Following peer review, a fifth dimension—operationalised treatment authority (assessment, triage, and signposting only vs assessment plus full-episode therapeutic management)—was derived by re-coding three existing free-text fields of the extraction matrix (PT decision to treat without GP, primary care organisation, DA model description) and applied as a refining discriminant within the autonomous first-contact configurations. Third, we compared coded profiles to identify recurring configurations.

Configurations were differentiated using a hierarchical decision logic: (a) the requirement for medical prescription for any intervention defined the most restrictive configuration; (b) among models with autonomous first-contact assessment, mandatory post hoc GP validation defined the protocol-governed triage configuration; (c) among models without mandatory validation, systematic prescribing and imaging authority defined the advanced practice configuration and (d) within the remaining autonomous first-contact configurations, operationalised treatment authority distinguished subconfigurations. Numerical labels were assigned after analysis and are reported in the Results section.

Coding and assignment procedure

Initial extraction of organisational descriptions and coding across the matrix dimensions were conducted by the first author. Configuration assignments were then proposed by the first author on the basis of each study’s coded organisational profile, and were independently reviewed by all three co-authors, who re-read the corresponding excerpts and the first author’s justification before recording their own classification. Disagreements were resolved by consensus, with a decision log maintained throughout (online supplemental additional file 3). Initial agreement was reached for 12 of 14 studies (86%); two disagreements (one between types 2 and 3 on post hoc validation, one between types 3 and 4 on prescribing as systematic vs individual attribute) were resolved by joint re-review. The subsequent subtyping of the autonomous first-contact configuration into type 3a (assessment and signposting) and type 3b (full-episode management) was conducted in a second analytical iteration during revision. The operationalised treatment authority dimension was derived by re-coding three existing free-text fields of the extraction matrix. The first author proposed the classification of each study on this basis, and the classification was independently reviewed and validated by co-authors. Full agreement was reached at first review.

Validation and triangulation

Configurations were triangulated against the seven review-level syntheses cited above and assessed for face validity against international practice variations. Distribution was quantified across the 14 studies with associations to health system type. The complete extraction matrix, configuration mapping with verbatim extracts, and coding decision log are available as onlinesupplemental additional files 13.

Results

Corpus and eligibility

Of the 19 primary studies, 14 provided sufficient organisational detail for configuration mapping.922,25 27 The remaining five studies reported DA in terms of clinical outcomes or resource use but did not offer sufficient detail on professional roles or gatekeeping2634,36 39; they were retained in the extraction matrix for transparency but were not assigned a configuration type. The configuration-eligible corpus was dominated by Beveridge-type systems (12 studies from the UK and Sweden) with only 2 from Bismarck-type or hybrid contexts (Netherlands, France). DA was implemented in GP practices, multidisciplinary primary care centres, physiotherapy clinics or network-based structures. This compositional bias should be borne in mind throughout interpretation.

Configuration types

Four configurations were empirically observed and a fifth was theoretically anticipated but not found: type 1 (prescription-bound executor, not observed); type 2 (protocol-governed triage with mandatory post hoc medical validation); type 3a (autonomous first-contact, assessment-and-signposting); type 3b (autonomous first-contact, full-episode management) and type 4 (advanced practice clinician). Each configuration was defined by physiotherapist autonomy, operationalised treatment authority, triage responsibility, GP positioning and gatekeeping pattern (table 1).

Organisational configurations of DA physiotherapy

Type 1: prescription-bound executor model (not observed)

Physiotherapists would deliver treatment within the frame of medical diagnosis and prescription, with both clinical and administrative gatekeeping remaining exclusively medical. No study with sufficient organisational detail exhibited this pattern.

Type 2: protocol-governed triage with mandatory post hoc medical validation

This configuration reflects cautious DA implementation in physician-centred systems. Physiotherapists act as first-contact providers within a structured protocol of eligible conditions, performing red-flag screening and initiating conservative management. Mandatory post hoc GP validation is preserved: the physiotherapist transmits the assessment to the GP via secure electronic communication, the GP approves or withholds the treatment plan, and the prescription required for reimbursement is issued by the GP. Clinical gatekeeping is therefore delegated, while administrative gatekeeping (prescription authority, reimbursement) and post hoc clinical validation remain medical. This configuration was observed in the French CPTS-based DA pilot.38

Type 3a: autonomous first-contact: assessment and signposting model

This configuration is characterised by physiotherapists acting as autonomous first-contact practitioners whose operationalised role centres on assessment, triage, and signposting rather than delivery of the therapeutic episode. Physiotherapists independently assess and triage MSK presentations, then direct patients to self-management advice, conventional outpatient physiotherapy, secondary care or GP review for complex cases; the therapeutic episode typically returns to conventional pathways. Clinical gatekeeping is delegated to physiotherapists at the entry point, while the therapeutic episode remains within conventional structures. This configuration was observed in four studies: NHS national FCP,9 embedded FCP in general practice29 and physiotherapist-led telephone triage services.27 28

Type 3b: autonomous first-contact: full-episode management model

This configuration extends the autonomous first-contact role to the entire therapeutic episode. Physiotherapists independently assess, treat and conclude episodes of care for MSK conditions, referring to GPs only for red flags, diagnostic uncertainty, non-MSK presentations or multimorbidity. Both clinical and administrative gatekeeping for MSK care are delegated to physiotherapists, and the GP is bypassed for the entire episode unless escalation thresholds are reached. This configuration was observed in eight studies: UK self-referral and DA including the STEMS family,23,25 Swedish primary care triage with treatment,30,33 and Dutch DA following 2006 legislation.37 Despite procedural variations-such as nurse triage preceding physiotherapist contact in some Swedish models30 31—the operational role was invariant: autonomous first contact with retention of full-episode therapeutic authority.

The distinction between types 3a and 3b is consequential: both share the gatekeeping logic of autonomous first contact without mandatory GP validation but they entail different workforce demands, training profiles and policy implications.

Type 4: advanced practice clinician model

This configuration is observed in settings where physiotherapists hold extended scope roles, including authority to request imaging and prescribe medications within defined limits, as a systematic component of the service model rather than an individual attribute. In some type 3 studies, individual physiotherapists held prescribing qualifications, but prescribing was not a defining feature of the service model. In type 4, extended scope is integral to the design, and gatekeeping is formally distributed. This configuration was observed in one study,22 which explicitly compared standard FCPs (without prescribing or injection capabilities) to those with additional qualifications enabling prescription of medications including controlled drugs, making prescribing a defining variable of the evaluation.

Distribution of configurations and health system associations

Among the 14 studies with sufficient organisational detail, the distribution was markedly concentrated within autonomous first-contact configurations (12 of 14 studies), a pattern that may itself carry analytical significance. Within this group, four studies corresponded to type 3a and eight to type 3b; one study corresponded to type 2 and one to type 4.

Configuration types showed observable associations with health system context, although the limited number of non-Beveridge studies (n=2) precludes firm conclusions. Type 3a was observed in NHS programmes designed to relieve GP consultation burden at MSK entry while preserving downstream physiotherapy in conventional structures.927,29 Type 3b was observed in Beveridge-type systems where physiotherapists held the entire MSK episode (UK self-referral and STEMS: 23,25; Swedish triage-with-treatment: 30,33) and in the mature Dutch DA implementation.37 Type 2 emerged in the French CPTS pilot,38 reflecting Bismarck-system constraints. Type 4 was observed in a UK FCP context22 where extended scope with prescribing authority was a defining component of the model.

The theoretically anticipated type 1 was not observed. This absence may reflect the bounded scope of the source review (see Limitations) and/or an evaluation gap whereby minimally transformative models generate less detailed reporting; these hypotheses require empirical testing.

Reconfiguration of GP gatekeeping

Across observed configurations, DA did not abolish gatekeeping but repositioned it along a continuum of medical involvement. In the predominant autonomous first-contact configurations (types 3a and 3b), GPs transitioned from exclusive entry points to secondary consultants and safety nets. The depth of this redistribution differed between subconfigurations: in type 3a, the GP was removed from the entry consultation but may have retained involvement in subsequent therapeutic decisions; in type 3b, the GP was removed from the entire episode unless complications emerged. Importantly, even within autonomous first-contact configurations, redistribution of clinical and administrative gatekeeping did not always proceed in parallel. For example, Goodwin and Hendrick29 a type 3a model, reported physiotherapists with DA to imaging (X-ray, MRI) but no prescribing authority, while Bishop et al24 a type 3b model, noted that physiotherapists could not order investigations or make specialist referrals independently despite holding the full therapeutic episode. In these settings, physiotherapists held clinical gatekeeping authority while administrative gatekeeping remained partially medical.

In the type 2 protocol-governed triage variant,38 GPs retained mandatory post hoc validation authority and prescription responsibility, maintaining administrative gatekeeping while delegating initial clinical triage. In the type 4 advanced practice variant,22 gatekeeping became distributed between physiotherapists and physicians.

Discussion

Principal findings

In well-documented implementations, predominantly within Beveridge-type systems, DA clusters around autonomous first-contact configurations rather than producing multiple equally prevalent models. Within this dominant pattern, two operationally distinct subconfigurations were identified: front-end assessment and signposting (type 3a) and full-episode autonomous management (type 3b). Two further configurations (type 2 and type 4) were observed as illustrative variants supported by single studies. The observed distribution reflects well-documented implementations within specific health system contexts rather than a universal trajectory.

The concentration of documented implementations around this single configuration may reflect, at least in part, a tendency towards institutional convergence in systems with established primary care structures, rather than solely a sampling artefact. Other configurations represent context-specific variants shaped by health system type, regulation, and stage of DA implementation. From a general practice perspective, DA does not abolish gatekeeping but redistributes it between physiotherapists and GPs in system-specific ways. In the most commonly documented configuration, gatekeeping for many MSK conditions is delegated to physiotherapists, while GPs retain responsibility for complex diagnostic problems, multimorbidity, and overall coordination of care.

Interpretation in light of existing literature

The dominance of autonomous first-contact configurations is consistent with findings from UK FCP, Swedish triage, Dutch DA and self-referral models.5 6 The distinction between type 3a and type 3b is often glossed over in narrative reviews. The NHS FCP programme, frequently invoked as the paradigmatic DA model, is a type 3a configuration: physiotherapists hold first-contact authority but are resourced for assessment, triage, and signposting rather than therapeutic delivery. By contrast, the STEMS family23 24 and Swedish triage-with-treatment models30,33 are type 3b configurations in which physiotherapists hold the full episode. Treating these as a single configuration obscures variations with direct implications for workforce planning and the interpretation of empirical evidence on DA effectiveness.

The transitional protocol-governed triage configuration, illustrated by the French CPTS pilot, corresponds to cautious implementation in physician-centred systems. Mandatory post hoc GP validation may help build trust and address medicolegal concerns but risks limiting workload relief if it creates administrative burden without commensurate safety benefits. The advanced practice configuration corresponds to settings with extended PT scope and shared gatekeeping. These models show high diagnostic concordance with physicians and positive outcomes40 42 but require substantial investment in training, accreditation, and regulatory frameworks.

Our typology resonates with conceptual work on task shifting:15 44 45 Laurant et al44 showed effectiveness depends on clearly defined scopes and robust communication systems—features visible in the autonomous first-contact configurations—and Gong et al15 noted that task-shifting evidence derives mostly from nurse-led models, calling for qualitative research on organisational drivers. The present typology responds to this gap by analysing how DA physiotherapy generates distinct configurations depending on regulatory environment and health system architecture, and could inform comparative research on task-shifting beyond physiotherapy.

Whether any configuration is preferable depends on policy objectives. Type 3a redistributes the front-end triage burden while preserving conventional therapeutic pathways. Type 3b transfers the entire MSK episode, alleviating GP workload more substantially but requiring broader clinical responsibilities for physiotherapists, with corresponding training, supervision, and medico-legal arrangements. Type 2 may build institutional trust in physician-centred systems but risks administrative burden without sufficient workload relief. Type 4 expands the physiotherapy profession’s scope but requires sustained regulatory and educational investment. The typology is therefore heuristic rather than normative: it makes explicit the trade-offs policymakers must navigate, rather than identifying a universally preferable model.

Our configurations align with recent syntheses: advanced practice frameworks correspond to type 440 42; the heterogeneity noted in DA implementation5 6 42 43 maps onto our type 3 and type 2 distinctions; and Kechichian et al14 documented French acceptability consistent with type 2. International analyses of primary care innovation46 47 confirm that organisational context, regulation, and interprofessional governance shape task redistribution.

Implications for primary care and policy

For GPs, DA physiotherapy is sometimes perceived as a threat to professional territory. Our findings indicate a more nuanced situation. In autonomous first-contact configurations (types 3a and 3b), GPs are redirected toward tasks mobilising their core expertise (complex diagnosis, multimorbidity, longitudinal coordination) rather than routine MSK complaints. The intensity differs across subconfigurations: in type 3a, the GP is removed from the entry consultation but may retain involvement in therapeutic decisions; in type 3b, the GP is removed from the entire episode unless complications emerge. If physiotherapists manage most uncomplicated cases, GPs may face increasingly atypical presentations. This ‘complexity concentration’ effect,45 consistent with nurse substitution evidence,44 may increase GP cognitive burden even as consultation volume decreases; it is likely more pronounced in type 3b than in type 3a. DA impact also depends on interprofessional organisation: without clear referral criteria, feedback mechanisms, and shared protocols, DA risks being experienced as unilateral delegation. The UK FCP model9 29 illustrates how embedding physiotherapists with shared records supports safe redistribution. DA may improve MSK access in underserved areas,10 though infrastructure-heavy models may disproportionately benefit well-resourced settings.15

Based on available evidence, three exploratory policy propositions can be formulated. First, DA physiotherapy most commonly clusters around autonomous first-contact configurations (types 3a or 3b) when physiotherapists are integrated into primary care teams. The choice between assessment-and-signposting (3a) and full-episode autonomy (3b) is itself a policy decision with consequences for workforce design and pathway architecture, and should be made deliberately. Achieving either requires regulatory authorisation and organisational infrastructure such as shared records, co-location or strong referral pathways, agreed protocols, and interprofessional training.

Second, in physician-centred Bismarck-type systems, DA may initially take the form of protocol-governed triage with mandatory post hoc validation14 38; given the corpus imbalance, this should be treated as a hypothesis for future testing. Third, advanced practice models represent another trajectory but require clear regulatory boundaries, educational pathways and shared accountability. In all cases, legal changes authorising DA are necessary but not sufficient: organisational support and interprofessional governance are essential.

Global relevance

The typology has relevance beyond the included health systems. Australia is currently evaluating publicly funded FCP in primary care through the NHMRC-funded PhysioDirect-Aus Partnership Project.48 Hong Kong recently enabled DA under conditions including prior medical diagnosis,49 structurally resembling type 2. By contrast, Japan and the Republic of Korea continue to require physician referral,4 49 representing contexts where the unobserved type 1 configuration might be documented through future research. In continental Europe, Italy provides another physician-centred case where DA is actively debated.50 The clinical/administrative gatekeeping distinction is particularly relevant in Bismarck-type systems where prescription and reimbursement architectures shape task redistribution. The framework could be adapted to analogous redistributions involving nurse practitioners, physician associates, or pharmacists.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. It is based on a systematically identified corpus of 19 DA physiotherapy studies and uses an explicit, structured extraction matrix. To our knowledge, it provides the first cross-study synthesis of DA organisational configurations with a specific focus on physiotherapist roles and GP gatekeeping. The typological approach adopted here is consistent with the growing body of framework syntheses and conceptual models published in the primary care literature. As an analytical typology, its primary purpose is to map the conceptual space of organisational configurations and generate testable hypotheses, not to estimate the prevalence of each configuration across health systems. Findings were triangulated with independent systematic reviews. The complete extraction matrix and coding documentation are available as online supplemental materials.

Important limitations must be acknowledged. First, the corpus was derived from a companion review scoped to GP workload, resource use, and care organisation outcomes; regulatory documents, service specifications, qualitative-only studies, and evaluations of prescription-bound DA without measurable workload effect were not eligible and are therefore not represented. The absence of empirically observed type 1 should be interpreted strictly within these bounds. Second, the configuration-eligible corpus was dominated by Beveridge-type systems (12/14), with only two studies from Bismarckian or hybrid contexts.37 38 Third, organisational reporting bias is likely, and the type 3a/3b differentiation relied on textual cues not uniformly explicit across studies. Finally, types 2 and 4, each supported by a single study, should be interpreted as illustrative rather than robust.

Additional limitations include possible discrepancy between formal structures and daily practices, absence of evidence from low- and middle-income countries (one Ecuadorian study39 was excluded for insufficient detail), reliance on published descriptions rather than direct observation, and absence of formal member checking or patient perspectives.

Future research

Future research should combine organisational analysis with primary qualitative data collection. We propose several exploratory propositions: (1) Type 2 may evolve towards type 3a or 3b when interprofessional trust develops and post hoc validation requirements are relaxed; the direction may depend on workforce capacity and downstream service design; (2) Type 1, if it exists, may prove organisationally unstable due to insufficient workload redistribution; (3) autonomous first-contact configurations require minimum organisational infrastructure (shared records, escalation thresholds, medicolegal allocation), with greater demands in type 3b; (4) complexity concentration may be more pronounced in type 3b than in type 3a, and in both than in type 2 and (5) the choice between type 3a and type 3b may be shaped less by patient need than by workforce capacity and remuneration arrangements.

These propositions are hypothesis-generating, not predictive. DA physiotherapy provides a useful case for studying gatekeeping redistribution in multiprofessional primary care, with particular attention to under-represented Bismarck-type systems.

Conclusions

In well-documented implementations, predominantly within Beveridge-type health systems, DA physiotherapy is most commonly configured with physiotherapists as autonomous first-contact MSK clinicians, with two operationally distinct profiles: front-end assessment-and-signposting (type 3a) and full-episode autonomous management (type 3b). GPs are repositioned as secondary consultants and safety nets in both. Other configurations—transitional protocol-governed triage (type 2) and advanced practice (type 4)—appear as context-dependent variants. Rather than eliminating gatekeeping, DA redistributes it across professionals in system-specific ways.

This exploratory typology provides a framework for hypothesis generation and future primary research, particularly in under-represented Bismarck-type systems.
