Executive Summary

Nion Co. was acquired by Bruker in January 2024. As a result, this grant was canceled halfway through
the performance period because Bruker is not “small” and therefore not eligible for SBIR grants. Most of
the key personnel for the project now work in the electron microscopy business unit of Bruker where this
second-generation ground-potential monochromator project continues in a modified form.

All objectives from the first half of the project were achieved during the funded period. Cancellation of the
grant formally stopped the project. That said, the developments of the first half of the project showed high
promise and are being used to promote the project internally at Bruker where the project will likely
continue in the near future.

The Rest of the Story

This is a summary. The project was canceled halfway through when Nion was acquired by Bruker, and
all staff on the project, including the PI, were reassigned to other priorities. There is no one with a
responsibility to produce a full final report for this project. This summary is a best effort on a goodwill
basis from Bruker to formally closeout this grant.

The formal acquisition notification is here:
https://ir.bruker.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2024/Bruker-Acquires-Electron-

And here is a nice story describing the acquisition:
https://analyticalscience.wiley.com/content/article-do/bruker-nion-future

The project goals for the first half of the funding period were achieved. The funding was used to pay
salaries at Nion for the key personnel. The main outputs from this first half were the calculations and
mechanical designs for a second-generation ground-potential monochromator targeting 1 meV energy
resolution. These calculations and designs were done. Given the high proprietary nature of this work,
very little can be shared publicly about the results.

The key results, which can be shared publicly, are that 1 meV energy resolution looks plausible given a
next-generation design. This reflects a factor of 2.6x improvement on our current resolution record. We
find that a factor of improvement more than 3x on previous results is not plausible in one step because
the issues encountered are too difficult to predict, leading to a 1 meV target for this project.

Even though the grant was canceled, there was still a very high value for the electron microscopy
community. That is, the results are currently being used to promote the project internally at Bruker, and
it is very likely this project will advance under a different structure at Bruker. All the motivation for this
project is still valid, and the technical results of the first half the project are greatly useful in the context
of a larger organization like Bruker.

The key personnel look forward to future opportunities to work with the SBIR program should these
arise, and thank the program managers for their terrific support over the years.
