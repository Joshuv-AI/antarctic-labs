De-escalated chemotherapy and endocrine therapy outcomes in young women with stage I HR+/HER2+ breast cancer: An international real-world study
Authors: Tal Sella, Frederieke van Duijnhoven, Hans Wildiers, Claudio Venieri, Katarzyna Pogoda, Barbro Linderholm, Leonor Matos, Natali Shirron, Eleonora De Maio D'Esposito, Nawale Hajjaji, Erika Matos, Lucía González Cortijo, Matteo Lambertini, Ana Fortuna, Laurent Rosset, Anastasia Constantinidou, Sofie Demasure, Areen Abu Remilah, Greet Huygh, Shani Paluch-Shimon, Vivianne Tjan-Heijnen, Natacha Ruyssers, Helena Gouveia, Eurydice Angeli, Irfan Cicin, Edoardo Chiappe, Patrick Neven, Mehmet Artac, Bilgesah Kilictas, Jalal Baranseh, Elena Vicente-Rubio, Mustafa Atci, Ayelet Shai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUse of taxane-only chemotherapy for stage I HR+/HER2+ breast cancer has increased, potentially preserving ovarian function and shifting endocrine therapy (ET) strategies. Outcomes in premenopausal women were not defined.MethodsPatients ≤45 years with stage I HR+/HER2+ breast cancer were identified from a multicenter registry. IDFS was assessed by chemotherapy and ET.ResultsThirteen IDFS events (4 distant) occurred among 244 patients. Six-year IDFS was 95.5% (multiagent chemotherapy) and 94.1% (taxane-only) (p = 0.56), and 95.9%, 93.8%, and 96.9% with tamoxifen, tamoxifen + OFS, and AI + OFS (p = 0.56).ConclusionsOutcomes were excellent regardless of chemotherapy or ET, suggesting limited incremental benefit from treatment intensification.

Background

Since its publication in 2015, the APT regimen, taxane-only chemotherapy with 1 year of trastuzumab, became the standard adjuvant treatment for stage I HER2+ breast cancer, with excellent long-term outcomes, also observed in the ATEMPT study [[1], [2], [3]]. However, these studies included both hormone receptor positive (HR+) and negative (HR-) disease, and premenopausal women were underrepresented, limiting extrapolation to young patients with HR+/HER2+ BC.

In premenopausal women de-escalated chemotherapy may reduce chemotherapy-induced ovarian failure [4]. In HR+/HER2+ disease, this may influence prognosis and complicate adjuvant endocrine therapy (ET) selection. In the context of a low recurrence risk, the need for ovarian function suppression (OFS) and the choice between aromatase inhibitors (AI) and tamoxifen remain uncertain. ET selection was not systematically addressed in APT and ATEMPT, and real-world data in young premenopausal patients are limited [[5], [6], [7]].

Although ET intensification with OFS and AI may improve outcomes in higher-risk populations, it is associated with increased toxicity and potential long-term health risks [[8], [9], [10]]. Whether it provides meaningful benefit in premenopausal women with stage I HR+/HER2+ BC, particularly in the context of de-escalated chemotherapy, remains uncertain. We evaluated outcomes according to chemotherapy and ET in an international cohort of premenopausal women with stage I HR+/HER2+ BC.

Methods

Using a retrospective international multicenter registry, we identified premenopausal women (≤45 years) with stage I HR+/HER2+ BC treated between 2013 and 2020 with (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy and ET [11]. HER2 positivity was defined as immunohistochemistry +3 staining or immunohistochemistry +2 staining with a positive ISH test, according to contemporaneous ASCO/CAP guidelines at participating institutions. Chemotherapy was categorized as multi-agent or taxane-only; ET as tamoxifen alone, tamoxifen + OFS, or AI + OFS. Exposure to OFS was defined as treatment with OFS for ≥1 year (or until recurrence) or having undergone bilateral oophorectomy. Disease stage was determined clinically for patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy and by pathological staging for those treated in the adjuvant setting. Patients switching from tamoxifen to AI during treatment (n = 12), and 1 patient with a contralateral BC before ET start were excluded.

Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. IDFS from ET initiation was defined according to STEEP criteria and estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods with log rank testing [12]. Given the low number of events (n = 13, 5.3%), multivariable Cox regression was not performed.

Results

Of 1,231 premenopausal patients, 258 with stage I HR+/HER2+ BC were eligible for inclusion (Fig. 1). Median age was 39 years (range 23-45) and median follow-up was 65.2 months (IQR: 45.7-86.0). Most (n = 177, 68.6%) underwent upfront surgery and received adjuvant chemotherapy. Multiagent chemotherapy regimens were administered to 168 patients (65.1%): anthracycline-cyclophosphamide (AC ± taxane) containing (n = 145, 86.3% of multiagent), taxane-carboplatin (n = 18), and taxane-cyclophosphamide (n = 5); 69 (26.7%) received a taxane-only regimen (Table 1). All patients received trastuzumab-based therapy: trastuzumab alone (n = 233, 90.3%), trastuzumab + pertuzumab (n = 25, 9.7%), or T-DM1 (n = 3, 1.2%). ET included tamoxifen alone (n = 121, 46.9%), tamoxifen + OFS (n = 86, 33.3%), and AI + OFS (51, 19.8%). ET duration data were available for 246/258 patients (95.3%). At time of data cutoff, median duration of ET was 50.0 months, and 38% of patients remained on treatment. Discontinuation between 12 and 24 months occurred in 4% (n = 2), 7% (n = 6), and 15.7% (n = 19) of the OFS + AI, OFS + Tamoxifen, and tamoxifen-alone groups, respectively. Nine patients (6.5%) in the OFS-treated groups discontinued OFS during follow-up but continued tamoxifen alone. Compared with patients receiving multiagent chemotherapy, those treated with taxane-only, initiated ET closer to diagnosis (median 5.9 vs 8.0 months, p < 0.001), and were more likely to receive tamoxifen alone (55.1% vs 39.9%, p = 0.039) than OFS. Over time, taxane-only chemotherapy use increased (2013-2015: 10.1%, 2019-2020: 49.2%), while OFS use remained stable (52.2% and 50.8%, respectively).

Discussion

In this international real-world cohort of premenopausal women ≤45 years with stage I HR+/HER2+ BC, long-term outcomes were excellent across chemotherapy intensity and ET strategy. At 6 years, IDFS exceeded 93% regardless of selected approach and distant recurrences were rare.

Management of stage I HER2+ BC evolved substantially since publication of the APT trial, with increasing adoption of taxane-only chemotherapy. In premenopausal women, this approach may preserve ovarian function, increasing the relevance of ET selection and the decision to incorporate OFS [4,13]. In our real-world cohort, ∼50% received OFS, in line with some contemporary series [5,6]. Such variability likely reflects differences in patient age distribution, perceived recurrence risk, regional practice patterns, and ongoing uncertainty regarding ET intensification in HR+/HER2+ disease. Despite this heterogeneity, IDFS was high across treatment subgroups. Although the study was not powered for formal comparative effectiveness analyses, the low event rates and small absolute differences across subgroups, suggest that treatment intensification, whether through multiagent chemotherapy or more aggressive ET, including OFS, results in limited incremental benefit in this low-risk setting.

Prospective data to inform optimal ET in premenopausal HR+/HER2+ are limited [14]. While SOFT/TEXT demonstrated a clear role for OFS in chemotherapy-treated premenopausal HR + disease, patients with HER2+ disease comprised a minority and were treated largely before modern HER2-targeted standards [9,15]. Post-hoc analyses from the HERA and ALTTO trials suggest a DFS benefit for OFS + AI, however these findings are largely derived from higher-risk cohorts [16,17]. Translation of these data to lower-risk stage I disease remains uncertain. In a prior analysis from this cohort using response to neoadjuvant therapy as a surrogate of low recurrence risk, AI + OFS improved DFS in cases of residual disease, but not pathologic complete response [11]. Accordingly in a favorable-risk stage I HER2+ population, treated with effective anti-HER2 therapy, the absolute risk reduction derived from ET intensification may be modest.

Our findings should be interpreted within the limitations of a retrospective registry. Detailed tumor size data (e.g., T1a/b/c) were unavailable for analysis, and residual confounding related to treatment selection according to tumor size and post-chemotherapy menopausal status cannot be excluded [18]. Additionally, while ET discontinuation events were recorded, ET adherence, particularly relevant for younger women, was not captured. The small number of events limited our ability to formally compare treatment regimens and apply multivariable modeling. Study strengths include its international scope, contemporary treatment approaches, and focus on young premenopausal women.

These data imply that the excellent outcomes observed with de-escalated chemotherapy regimens for Stage 1 ER+/HER2+ BC extend to younger premenopausal women. In the setting of very low distant recurrence risk, the absolute benefit of ET intensification may be modest, underscoring the importance of individualized risk–toxicity discussions, and of additional efforts to define the optimal systemic therapy for this population.
