1.
Introduction

In analytic number theory, the classical divisor problem concerns the asymptotic behavior of the sum

\Delta(x)=\sum_{n\leqslant x}d(n)-x\bigl(\log x+2\gamma-1\bigr),

where
d(n)
is the divisor function and
\gamma
is Euler’s constant. Wintner
[
24
]
explicitly gives a value distribution of the normalized
\Delta(x)
.

The development and application of probabilistic methods in analytic number theory have greatly enhanced our understanding of the distribution of such error terms. In 1992, Heath-Brown
[
11
]
proved that the normalized error term
F(t)=t^{-1/4}\Delta(t)
has a limiting distribution. For any interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, he showed that

(1.1)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\operatorname{meas}\bigl\{t\in[T,2T]:F(t)\in I\bigr\}=\int_{I}g(u)du,

where the limiting density
g
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and can be extended to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, in that paper, Heath-Brown also proved that similar results hold for the error term
\Delta_{3}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}d_{3}(n)-\underset{s=1}{\operatorname*{\operatorname*{Res}}}\left(\frac{\zeta^{3}(s)x^{s}}{s}\right)
, where
d_{3}(n)
is the triple divisor function, providing that
x^{1/4}\Delta(x)
is replaced by
x^{1/3}\Delta_{3}(x)
.

The method of Heath-Brown has many applications. In 1993, Bleher et al.
[
4
]
proved that the error term for the number of lattice points inside a shifted circle has a limiting distribution. After that, Bleher also conducted extensive related work; see reference
[
6
,
5
]
. Using similar methods, Cai
[
7
]
proved analogous results on
\operatorname{GL}(2)
.

A profound insight of
[
11
]
is that such a distribution can be obtained by approximating probability models. For a measurable function
h
on
[T,2T]
and an event
\mathcal{B}
, we define

\mathbb{P}_{T}\big(h(t)\in\mathcal{B}\big):=\frac{1}{T}\operatorname{meas}\{t\in[T,2T]:h(t)\in\mathcal{B}\}.

Let
\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\}_{n\text{ square-free}}
be a sequence of independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
. Consider the random trigonometric series

(1.2)

F_{\mathbb{X}}:=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\text{ square-free}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)^{2}}{n^{3/4}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{d(nr^{2})}{r^{3/2}}\cos\left(2\pi r\mathbb{X}_{n}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right).

Heath-Brown’s result is that the density
g
in (
1.1
) is precisely the probability density function of the random variable
F_{\mathbb{X}}
. Furthermore,

(1.3)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{T}\big(F(t)\leq u\big)=\mathbb{P}\big(F_{\mathbb{X}}\leq u\big)\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathbb{R}.

The motivation for the model (
1.2
) comes from the analytic structure of
\Delta(t)
itself. Voronoĭ’s summation formula yields that uniformly for
t\in[T,2T]
and any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

F(t)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos\left(4\pi\sqrt{nt}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+O(T^{\varepsilon}).

Writing
n=mr^{2}
with
m
is square-free, we have

F(t)=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{m\leq T}\frac{\mu(m)^{2}}{m^{3/4}}\sum_{r\leq\sqrt{T/m}}\frac{d(mr^{2})}{r^{3/2}}\cos\left(4\pi r\sqrt{mt}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+O(T^{\varepsilon}).

Lau and Tsang
[
19
]
provided explicit formulas for the moments of the probability density function of a class of functions, and proved that the density function of the remainder term of the divisor function is asymmetric.

Lau
[
20
]
established a quantitative bound on the discrepancy between the distribution function of
F(t)
and that of the random model
F_{\mathbb{X}}
. More specifically, he proved that

D_{F}(T):=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{T}(F(t)\leqslant u)-\mathbb{P}(F_{\mathbb{X}}\leqslant u)\right|\ll\frac{(\log\log\log T)^{3/4}}{(\log\log T)^{1/8}}.

Based on ideas from the work
[
17
]
of Lamzouri, Lester and Radziwiłł, Lamzouri
[
18
]
improved the estimate for
D_{F}(T)
. He proved that

D_{F}(T)\ll\frac{(\log\log\log T)^{9/4}}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}.

Furthermore, by a result of Lau
[
21
]
, Lamzouri also provided the distribution of large values of
\Delta(t)
in that paper. More specifically, he proved that there exist positive constants
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
,
b_{3}
,
b_{4}
such that for all real numbers
V
in the range
b_{1}\leqslant V\leqslant b_{2}(\log\log T)^{1/4}(\log\log\log T)^{2^{4/3}-7/4}
, one has

\exp(-b_3V^4(\log V)^{-3(2^{4/3}-1)})\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{T}(F(t)>V)\leqslant\exp(-b_4V^4(\log V)^{-3(2^{4/3}-1)}).

Moreover, the same bounds hold for
\mathbb{P}_{T}(F(t)<V)
, in the same range of
V
.

In this paper, we are not confined to the classical divisor function, but rather a more general situation: arithmetic functions defined by the Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms on
\operatorname{GL}(3)
.
Specifically, let
f
be a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. Let
A_{f}(n,m)
be its normalized Fourier coefficients and let

\Delta_{f}(x):=\sum_{n\leqslant x}A_{f}(n,1).

We prove that the normalized function
t^{-1/3}\Delta_{f}\left(t\right)
has a distribution function
g(\alpha)
. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. The function
F_{f}(t)=t^{-1/3}\Delta_{f}\left(t\right)
has a distribution function
g(\alpha)
, in the sense that for any interval I, we have

X^{-1}\operatorname{maes}\left\{t\in[1,X]:F_{f}(t)\in I\right\}\rightarrow\int_{I}g(\alpha)d\alpha,

as
X\rightarrow\infty
. The function
g(\alpha)
and its derivatives satisfy the growth condition

\frac{d^{k}}{d\alpha^{k}}g(\alpha)\ll_{A,k}(1+|\alpha|)^{-A},

for
k=0,1,2,\ldots
and any constant A. Moreover,
g(\alpha)
can be extended to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}
.

We use the method discussed in Heath-Brown’s work
[
11
]
for estimating the divisor function to prove Theorem
1.1
.
In the proof, we do not assume the validity of the Ramanujan conjecture, but rather replace the pointwise estimates in the original method with mean value estimates of Fourier coefficients.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need an estimate for the mean square of the partial sums of the Fourier coefficients. Specifically, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. For any positive
\varepsilon
, we have

\int_{0}^{x}\Delta_{f}\left(y\right)^{2}dy=\frac{1}{10\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,n\right)^{2}}{n^{\frac{4}{3}}}x^{\frac{5}{3}}+O\left(x^{\frac{18}{11}+\varepsilon}\right).

The key step in proving Theorem
1.2
is that we found a
\sigma<2/3
such that

\int_{1}^{T}\left|L(\sigma+it,f)\right|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

which is made possible thanks to the work
[
22
]
of Pal.

Next, we use the method used by Lamzouri
[
17
]
in treating the divisor function. However, unlike the case of the divisor function, we use mean value estimates of the Fourier coefficients in place of the pointwise estimates. We establish a quantitative rate of convergence for the limiting distribution in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\}_{n\text{cube-free}}
be a sequence of independent random variables, each uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
. For a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form
f
of
\operatorname{GL}(3)
, we have

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{T}(F_{f}(t)\leqslant u)-\mathbb{P}(F_{f,\mathbb{X}}\leqslant u)\right|\ll\frac{\left(\log\log\log T\right)^{\frac{5}{3}}}{\left(\log\log T\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}},

where

(1.4)

F_{f,\mathbb{X}}:=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}(1,nr^{3})}{r^{2}}\cos\left(6\pi r\mathbb{X}_{n}\right),

with
\varepsilon(n)=1
if
n
is cube-free and
\varepsilon(n)=0
otherwise.

Finally, following the approach of Lau
[
21
]
and Lamzouri
[
17
]
we determine the upper and lower bounds of the Laplace transform of
F_{f,\mathbb{X}}
and obtain the distribution of large values of
\Delta_{f}(t)
. Due to the lack of strong pointwise estimates for the Fourier coefficients and the inability to apply mean-value estimates, we obtain the following weaker result.

Theorem 1.4
.

For a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form
f
of
\operatorname{GL}(3)
and any positive
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{2}
. There exist positive constants
b_{f,1}
,
b_{f,2}
,
b_{f,3}
,
b_{f.3}
such that for all real numbers
V
in the range
b_{f,1}\leqslant V\leqslant b_{f,2}(\log\log T)^{1/21}(\log\log\log T)^{-5/21}
, we have

\exp(-b_{f,3}V^{35/2+\varepsilon_1})\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{T}(F_{f}(t)>T)\leqslant\exp(-b_{f,4} V^{5/3-\varepsilon_2}).

Moreover, the same bounds hold for
\mathbb{P}_{T}(F_{f}(t)<V)
, in the same range of
V
.

Remark 1
.

Throughout the paper, we require that
f
is a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form. This is because in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, such a condition is needed to ensure that

a_{n}(t)=\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{\pi\sqrt{3}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}(1,nr^{3})}{(nr^{3})^{2/3}}\cos(6\pi r t)

is well-defined, i.e. the series in the expression is convergent. However, in the case where
f
is not necessarily self-dual, the convergence of the corresponding series remains unknown. If it can be proven that the corresponding series converges when
f
is a more general Hecke–Maass cusp form, then all results in this paper can be extended to the corresponding cases.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
f
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form of type
v=(v_{f},v_{f})
for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
with the normalized Fourier coefficients
A_{f}(n,m)
. We have the conjugation relation
A_{f}(n,m)=\overline{A_{f}(m,n)}=A_{f}(m,n)
, see
[
10
, Theorem 9.3.11]
and the following multiplicativity relations,

\displaystyle A_{f}\left(n_{1}n_{2},m_{1}m_{2}\right)=A_{f}\left(n_{1},m_{2}\right)A\left(n_{2},m_{2}\right),\quad\text{if }\left(n_{1}m_{1},n_{2}m_{2}\right)=1,

\displaystyle A_{f}(m,1)A_{f}\left(m_{1},m_{2}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}=m\\
c_{1}\left|m_{1},c_{2}\right|m_{2}\end{subarray}}A_{f}\left(\frac{m_{1}c_{3}}{c_{1}},\frac{m_{2}c_{1}}{c_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle A_{f}(m_{1},1)A_{f}\left(1,m_{2}\right)=\sum_{d\mid(m_{1},m_{2})}A_{f}\left(\frac{m_{1}}{d},\frac{m_{2}}{d}\right).

Now, we define the
L
-function attached to
f
by

L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}(n,1)}{n^{s}},

for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, and by analytic continuation for all
s
in the complex plane. Next, we define the gamma factor

\gamma(s,f)=\pi^{-\frac{3\pi}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1-3v_{f}}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s-1+3v_{f}}{2}\right).

Then we have the functional equation for
L(s,f)
, i.e.,

\gamma(s,f)L(s,f)=\gamma(1-s,\tilde{f})L(1-s,\tilde{f}).

Kim and Sarnak
[
16
, Appendix 2]
have proven the following bounds towards the Ramanujan Conjecture

\displaystyle\left|A_{f}(n,1)\right|\ll n^{\frac{5}{14}+\epsilon}.

On average, by the Rankin–Selberg estimate
[
14
]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m^{2}n\leqslant N}|A_{f}(m,n)|^{2}\ll_{f}N.

By Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, one derives that

(2.1)

\sum_{n\leqslant N}|A_{f}(n,1)|\ll_{f}N.

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.2

Before proving Theorem
1.1
, we need to prove Theorem
1.2
. First, following Atkinson
[
2
]
and Tong’s method
[
23
]
in handling
d_{3}(n)
(the triple divisor function), we will obtain following results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. For
\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<\frac{2}{3}
and any positive
\varepsilon
, we have

\Delta_{f}(x)=\frac{x^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\pi\sqrt{3}}\sum_{n\leqslant X}\frac{A_{f}(1,n)}{n^{\frac{2}{3}}}\cos{6\pi(nx)^{\frac{1}{3}}}+O(x^{1-\alpha+\varepsilon}),

where
X
denotes
x^{3\alpha-1}/8\pi^{3}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
2
]
, where we need to use the functional equation

\gamma(s,f)L(s,f)=\gamma(1-s,\tilde{f})L(1-s,\tilde{f}).

and
\sum_{n\leqslant N}|A_{f}(n,1)|^{2}\ll_{f}N
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f
be a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
and
\sigma_{1}
be the greatest lower bound for which

\int_{-T}^{T}\left|L\left(\sigma_{1}+it,f\right)\right|^{2}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

holds.
If
\sigma_{1}\leqslant\frac{2}{3}
, then for any positive
\varepsilon
we have

\int_{0}^{x}\Delta_{f}\left(y\right)^{2}dy=\frac{1}{10\pi^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,n\right)^{2}}{n^{\frac{4}{3}}}x^{\frac{5}{3}}+O\left(x^{2-\frac{3-4\sigma_{1}}{6\left(1-\sigma_{1}\right)-1}+\varepsilon}\right).

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.1
and
[
23
, Theorem 1]
, the proof follows.
∎

Now we only need to find a
\sigma_{1}<\frac{2}{3}
to prove Theorem
1.2
. For
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
, we define
m(\sigma)
be the supremum of all numbers
m
such that

(3.1)

\int_{1}^{T}\left|L(s,f)\right|dt^{m}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Thus, we transform the problem into finding the smallest
\sigma
such that
m(\sigma)\geqslant 2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\geqslant 1
be a fixed integer and let
\frac{T}{2}\leqslant t\leqslant 2T
. Then for
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
fixed we have

(3.2)

|L(\sigma+it,f)|^{k}\ll 1+\log T\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}\left|L\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{\log T}+it+iv,f\right)\right|^{k}e^{-|v|}dv.

and

\left|L(\frac{1}{2}+it,f)\right|^{k}\ll\log T\left(1+\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}\left|L(\frac{1}{2}+it+iv,f)\right|^{k}e^{-|v|}dv\right).

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
13
, Lemma 7.1]
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
.
Let
R
be a positive integer such that

T\leqslant t_{r}\leqslant 2T,\qquad r=1,\dots,R,

and

|t_{r}-t_{s}|\geqslant\log^{C}T,\qquad 1\leqslant r\neq s\leqslant R,

where
C\geqslant 0
is a fixed constant. Then the estimate

\int_{1}^{T}|L(\sigma+it,f)|^{m(\sigma)}dt\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}

is equivalent to

\sum_{r\leqslant R}|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)|^{m(\sigma)}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
13
, Section 8.1]
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\frac{1}{2}<\sigma<1
is fixed and

|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)|\geqslant V\geqslant T^{\varepsilon}.

Then

(3.3)

R\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-m(\sigma)}

is equivalent to

(3.4)

\sum_{r\leqslant R}|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)|^{m(\sigma)}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon},

where
t_{r}
is defined as Lemma
3.4
.

Proof.

Suppose that (
3.4
) holds and let
t_{V,1},\cdots,t_{V,R_{1}}
be the subset of
\{t_{r}\}
such that

\left|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)\right|\geqslant V\quad(j=1,\cdots,R_{1}).

Then (
3.4
) implies

R_{1}V^{m(\sigma)}\ll\sum_{r\leqslant R_{1}}\left|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)\right|^{m(\sigma)}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon},

and consequently (
3.3
) holds with
R_{1}=R
. Conversely, let (
3.3
) holds and denote by
t_{V,1},\cdots,t_{V,R(V)}
those of the points
t_{1},\cdots,t_{R}
for which

V\leqslant\left|L(\sigma+t_{V,j},f)\right|\ll 2V\quad(j=1,\cdots,R(V)).

There are
O\left(\log T\right)
choices for each
V
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{r\leqslant R}\left|L(\sigma+it_{r},f)\right|^{m(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\ll RT^{\varepsilon}+\sum_{V}\sum_{j\leqslant R(V)}\left(2V\right)^{m(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\ll RT^{\varepsilon}+\sum_{V}T^{1+\varepsilon}\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
R
be a positive integer such that

T\leqslant t_{r}\leqslant 2T,\qquad r=1,\dots,R,

and

|t_{r}-t_{s}|\geqslant\log^{4}T,\qquad 1\leqslant r\neq s\leqslant R.

If

T^{\varepsilon}<V\leqslant\left|\sum_{M\leqslant n\leqslant 2M}a(n)n^{\sigma-it_{r}}\right|,

where
\sum_{n\leqslant M}|a(n)|\ll M^{1+\varepsilon}
and
1\ll M\ll T^{C}(C>0)
. Then

(3.5)

R\ll T^{\varepsilon}\left(M^{2-2\sigma}V^{-2}+TV^{-l(\sigma)}\right).

Here

l(\sigma)=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3-4\sigma}&\text{ if }\frac{1}{2}<\sigma\leq\frac{2}{3},\\
\frac{10}{7-8\sigma}&\text{ if }\frac{2}{3}<\sigma\leqslant\frac{11}{14},\\
\frac{34}{15-16\sigma}&\text{ if }\frac{11}{14}<\sigma\leqslant\frac{13}{15},\\
\frac{98}{31-32\sigma}&\text{ if }\frac{13}{15}<\sigma\leqslant\frac{57}{62},\\
\frac{5}{1-\sigma}&\text{ if }\frac{57}{62}<\sigma\leqslant 1-\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Proof.

We can get this lemma by following a similar argument to
[
13
, Lemma 8.2]
replacing
a(n)\ll M^{\varepsilon}
by
\sum_{n\leqslant M}|a(n)|\ll M^{1+\varepsilon}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f
be a Hecke–Maass cusp form from for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
and
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 1
be fixed, we have

\left|L(\sigma+it,f)\right|\ll\left|t\right|^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

This result follows directly from the convexity bound
L(1/2+it,f)\ll_{f}(1+\left|t\right|)^{3/4+\varepsilon}
and the Phragmén–Lindelöf theorem
[
10
, Theorem 8.2.1]
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
f
be a Hecke–Maass cusp form from for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. Then we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|L(\frac{1}{2}+it,f)\right|^{2}dt\ll T^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{32}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

See
[
22
, Theorem 1.0.1]
for details.
∎

Remark 2
.

We can refine the two lemmas mentioned above respectively using the subconvexity bound
L(1/2+it,f)\ll_{f}(1+\left|t\right|)^{3/4-3/40+\varepsilon}

[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
and the work of Dasgupta, Leung and Young in the preprint
[
8
]
, leading to a minor improvement in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

For
\frac{13}{20}\leqslant\sigma<1
, let
m(\sigma)
be defined by (
3.1
). Then we have

m(\sigma)\geqslant\frac{2}{3}\frac{3-2\sigma+\frac{3(1-\sigma)}{8}}{(3-2\sigma)(1-\sigma)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
, it suffices to prove

R\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}V^{-m(\sigma)}.

We begin with a standard application of the Mellin transform. For a parameter
Y>0
(to be chosen later) and
s=\sigma+it_{r}
with
\frac{13}{20}\leq\sigma<1
and
t_{r}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.4
, we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{n=n_{1}n_{2}}A_{f}(n_{1},1)A_{f}(n_{2},1)\right)e^{-n/Y}n^{-s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}Y^{w}\Gamma(w)L(s+w,f)^{2}dw.

Shifting the contour to
\real(w)=\frac{1}{2}-\sigma
and applying the residue theorem yields

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq Y}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=n_{1}n_{2}}A_{f}(n_{1},1)A_{f}(n_{2},1)\right)e^{-n/Y}n^{-s}+o(1)

\displaystyle=L(s,f)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sigma\right)}Y^{w}\Gamma(w)L(s+w,f)^{2}dw.

Applying Stirling’s formula to estimate the gamma factors and bounding the integral, we obtain

\displaystyle L(s,f)^{2}\ll

\displaystyle 1+\left|\sum_{n\leq Y}\left(\sum_{n=n_{1}n_{2}}A_{f}(n_{1},1)A_{f}(n_{2},1)\right)e^{-n/Y}n^{-s}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{-\log^{2}T}^{\log^{2}T}Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it_{r}+iv,f\right)^{2}e^{-|v|}dv\right|.

Consequently, at least one of the following two inequalities must hold:

(3.6)

V^{2}\ll\log Y\max_{M\leq\frac{1}{2}Y}\left|\sum_{M\leq n\leq 2M}\left(\sum_{n=n_{1}n_{2}}A_{f}(n_{1},1)A_{f}(n_{2},1)\right)e^{-n/Y}n^{-s}\right|,

or

(3.7)

V^{2}\ll Y^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}\left|L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it_{r}^{\prime},f\right)\right|^{2},

where

\left|L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it_{r}^{\prime},f\right)\right|:=\max_{-\log^{2}T\leq v\leq\log^{2}T}\left|L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it_{r}+iv,f\right)\right|.

For
\frac{13}{20}\leq\sigma\leq 1-\varepsilon

Proof.

Let

\limsup_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{F}(t)-\sum_{n\leqslant N}a_{n}\left(\gamma_{n}t\right)\right|^{2}\omega\left((t/T)^{3}\right)dt\rightarrow 0,

as

tends to infinity, where
\mathcal{F}(t):=\mathcal{F}_{f}(t)=t^{-1}\Delta_{f}(t^{3})
and

a_{n}(t):=a_{f,n}(t)=\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{\pi\sqrt{3}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nr^{3}\right)}{\left(nr^{3}\right)^{2/3}}\cos\{6\pi rt\},\quad\gamma_{n}=\sqrt[3]{n}

with
\varepsilon(n)=1
if

is cube-free, and
\varepsilon(n)=0
otherwise. From

and Hölder’s inequality, we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{r\leqslant x}A_{f}(1,nr^{3})

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{r\leqslant nx^{3}}A_{f}(1,r)^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sum_{r\leqslant nx^{3}}\textbf{1}_{r/n\textbf{ is cube number}}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}.

From above and summation by parts, it follows that
a_{n}(t)
is well-defined.

Remark 3

[
15

requires that

must be self-dual, so it can only be stated that

is well-defined for self-dual

From integration by parts and Theorem

(4.1)

We now estimate the integral

It can be seen from a simple calculation that

\int_{0}^{Y}\cos(6\pi y \sqrt[3]{n})\cos(6\pi y \sqrt[3]{m})dy=\begin{cases}Y/2+O(1),&m=n,\\[10.0pt]
O\left(|\sqrt[3]{m}-\sqrt[3]{n}|^{-1}\right),&m\neq n.\end{cases}

Applying integration by parts yields, for

(4.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos^{2}(6\pi t\sqrt[3]{n})\omega\left((t/T)^{3}\right)dt=\frac{1}{2}J+O(1),

and for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\cos(6\pi t \sqrt[3]{n})\cos(6\pi t \sqrt[3]{m})\omega\left((t/T)^{3}\right)dt

\displaystyle\ll|\sqrt[3]{m}-\sqrt[3]{n}|^{-1}

\displaystyle\ll\begin{cases}(mn)^{1/3}|m-n|^{-1},&m\ll n\ll m,\\
\min\left(m^{-1/3},n^{-1/3}\right),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

From these bounds it follows directly that

We now turn to the estimation of the cross terms

Let

Perron’s formula implies that for any non-integer

I_{f}(n,X)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}L(s,f)K(s)\frac{ds}{s},

Clearly

k

\displaystyle K(\sigma+it)\ll_{k,\sigma,\omega}(1+|t|)^{-k}X^{\sigma}(nX)^{k/3}.

By the residue theorem, we have

We now expand

Inserting this into the expression for

\displaystyle\ll\left|A_{f}(1,m)\right|m^{-2}\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{(nX)^{1/3}}\frac{(1+t)^{7/2}}{X}dt+\int_{(nX)^{1/3}}^{\infty}\frac{n^{5/3}X^{2/3}}{t^{3/2}}dt\Bigg\}

5.
The Proof of Theorem

We first derive upper bounds for every integral moment of the truncated random processes
F_{f,\mathbb{X}}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f
be a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form for
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. Let
\beta_{0}
be a real constant. Let
\left\{a_{n}\right\}_{n}
be a sequence of real numbers such that
\left|a_{n}\right|\leqslant 1
. There exist positive constants
c_{f,1},c_{f,2}
such that for any positive integers
1\leqslant N<M,L\geqslant 2
and
k\geqslant 1
we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}\cos\left(6\pi q\mathbb{X}_{n}\right)\right|^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\min\left\{\left(c_{f,1}k^{2/3}\left(\log 2k\right)^{2/3}\right)^{k},\left(c_{f,2}kN^{-1/3}\right)^{k/2}\right\}.

Proof.

We now note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}\cos\left(6\pi q\mathbb{X}_{n}\right)\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right)\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|^{2k}\right).

Expanding this moment we find that it equals

\displaystyle\sum_{N\leqslant n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{2k}<M}\frac{\varepsilon\left(n_{1}\right)\cdots\varepsilon\left(n_{2k}\right)}{\left(n_{1}\cdots n_{2k}\right)^{2/3}}\sum_{q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{2k}\leqslant L}\prod_{j=1}^{2k}a_{n_{j}q_{j}^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,n_{j}q_{j}^{3}\right)}{q_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}\left(e^{2\pi i\left(\left(q_{1}\mathbb{X}_{n_{1}}+\cdots+q_{k}\mathbb{X}_{n_{k}}\right)-\left(q_{k+1}\mathbb{X}_{n_{k+1}}+\cdots+q_{1k}\mathbb{X}_{n_{2k}}\right)\right)}\right).

Since the random variables
\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\}
are independent, the inner expectation vanishes unless

\{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\}=\{n_{k+1},\dots,n_{2k}\}

and

q_{1}\mathbb{X}_{n_{1}}+\cdots+q_{k}\mathbb{X}_{n_{k}}=q_{k+1}\mathbb{X}_{n_{k+1}}+\cdots+q_{2k}\mathbb{X}_{n_{2k}},

in which case it equals
1
. Moreover, under this condition we must have
q_{i}=q_{j}
whenever
n_{i}=n_{j}
for some
1\leq i\leq k
and
k+1\leq j\leq 2k
. Therefore, noting that

\sum_{n\geqslant N}\sum_{r}\frac{\varepsilon(n)^{2}A_{f}(1,nr^{3})^{2}}{\left(nr^{3}\right)^{4/3}}\leqslant\sum_{n>N}\frac{A_{f}(1,n)^{2}}{n^{4/3}},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant k!\left(\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{4/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)^{2}}{q^{4}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\ll\left(kN^{-1/3}\right)^{k}.

J:=k\log 2k

N<J<M

N\geq J

\sum_{r\leqslant x}A_{f}(1,nr^{3})\leqslant n^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}x^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon},

\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|\ll k^{2/3}(\log 2k)^{2/3}.

We now apply the elementary inequality

|a+b|^{2\ell}\leqslant 2^{2\ell}\bigl(|a|^{2\ell}+|b|^{2\ell}\bigr),

which holds for all real numbers
a,b
and any positive integer
\ell

N<J<M

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{2k}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{N\leqslant n<J}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle+2^{2k}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\sum_{J\leqslant n<M}\frac{\varepsilon(n)}{n^{2/3}}\sum_{q\leqslant L}a_{nq^{3}}\frac{A_{f}\left(1,nq^{3}\right)}{q^{2}}e^{6\pi iq\mathbb{X}_{n}}\right|^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(c^{\prime}_{f,1}k^{2/3}\left(\log 2k\right)^{2/3}\right)^{2k}+\left(c^{\prime}_{f,2}kJ^{-1/3}\right)^{k}.

for some positive constants
c^{\prime}_{f,1},c^{\prime}_{f,2}
. Finally, by applying Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
\mathbb{E}(|Z|^{k})\leqslant\mathbb{E}(|Z|^{2k})^{1/2}
(for any random variable

Lemma 5.2

Proof.

The proof is similar to

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
a_{m}
be real numbers such that
a_{m}\ll 1
. Let
\alpha_{0}\neq 0
and
\beta_{0}
be fixed real numbers. Let

be an integer and
M\leqslant T^{1/\left(3^{h}+9h\right)}/h^{3}
be a real number. Write
m=nr^{3}
where
n
is cube-free and let
\left\{\mathbb{X}_{n}\right\}_{n\text{ cube-free }}
be a sequence of independent random variables uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\sum_{m\leqslant M}a_{m}\cos\left(6\pi\alpha_{0}\sqrt[3]{mt}\right)\right)^{h}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{nr^{2}\leqslant M}a_{nr^{2}}\cos\left(6\pi r\mathbb{X}\right)\right)^{h}\right)+O\left(T^{-2/9}\right).

Proof.

Let
e(z)=\exp(2\pi iz)
. It suffices to prove that, for all integers
k,\ell\geqslant 0
and
k+\ell\leqslant h
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\sum_{q\leqslant M}a_{q}e\left(3\alpha_{0}\sqrt[3]{qt}\right)\right)^{k}{\overline{\left(\sum_{m\leqslant M}a_{m}e\left(3\alpha_{0}\sqrt[3]{mt}\right)\right)}}^{\ell}dt

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{n_{1}r_{1}^{3}\leqslant M}a_{n_{1}r_{1}^{3}}e^{6\pi ir_{1}\mathbb{X}\left(n_{1}\right)}\right)^{k}\overline{\left(\sum_{n_{2}r_{2}^{3}\leqslant M}a_{n_{2}r_{2}^{3}}e^{6\pi ir_{2}\mathbb{X}\left(n_{2}\right)}\right)}^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(T^{-2/9}\right).

Expanding the moment we obtain

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\sum_{q\leqslant M}a_{q}e\left(3\alpha_{0}\sqrt[3]{qt}\right)\right)^{k}\overline{\left(\sum_{m\leqslant M}a_{m}e\left(3\alpha_{0}\sqrt[3]{mt}\right)\right.}\right)^{\ell}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}\leqslant M\\
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}\leqslant M\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}a_{q_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\overline{a_{m_{j}}}\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}e\left(3\alpha_{0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sqrt[3]{q_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sqrt[3]{m_{j}}\right)\sqrt{t}\right)dt.

Let
q_{i}=d_{i}e_{i}^{3}
and
m_{j}=f_{j}g_{j}^{3}
, where
d_{i}
and
f_{j}
is cube-free. Since the set
\{\sqrt{n}\}_{n\text{ cube-free}}
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 2]
) and the random variables
\mathbb{X}(n)
are independent, we conclude that the contribution from the diagonal terms those satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sqrt[3]{q_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sqrt[3]{m_{j}}=0,

equals

\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\sum_{n_{1}r_{1}^{3}\leqslant M}a_{n_{1}r_{1}^{3}}e^{6\pi ir_{1}\mathbb{X}\left(n_{1}\right)}\right)^{k}\overline{\left(\sum_{n_{2}r_{2}^{2}\leqslant M}a_{n_{2}r_{2}^{3}}e^{6\pi ir_{2}\mathbb{X}\left(n_{2}\right)}\right)^{\ell}}\right).

To finish the proof of the lemma, it remains to bound the contribution of the off-diagonal terms those with

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sqrt[3]{q_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sqrt[3]{m_{j}}\neq 0.

For each such term where all
q_{1},\ldots,q_{k},m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}\leqslant M
, Lemma
5.3
gives

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sqrt[3]{q_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sqrt[3]{m_{j}}\right|\geqslant\frac{1}{((k+\ell)\sqrt[3]{M})^{3^{k+\ell-1}-1}}.

A simple integration by parts shows that for any nonzero real
\eta

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}e(\eta\sqrt[3]{t})dt\ll\frac{T^{\frac{2}{3}}}{|\eta|}.

Thus we deduce that the contribution of the off-diagonal terms is

\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{T}}\left(cM\right)^{k+\ell}((k+\ell)\sqrt[3]{M})^{3^{k+\ell-1}-1}\ll T^{-2/9},

for some positive constant
c

∎

Finally, we establish the following results. They demonstrate that, uniformly in a certain range of parameters, the Laplace transform of

Lemma 5.4

Let

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|F_{f}(t)-G_{f,N}(t)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(\int_{\sqrt[3]{T}}^{\sqrt[3]{2T}}\left|F_{f}\left(u^{3}\right)-G_{f,N}\left(u^{3}\right)\right|^{2}du\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\sqrt[3]{T}}^{\sqrt[3]{2T}}u^{4}du\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[T,2T]}\left|G_{f,N}(t)-F_{f,N}(t)\right|

Lemma 6.1
.

For a fixed self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form

of
\operatorname{GL}(3)
. There exist positive constant
c_{f,6}
,
c_{f,7}
such that for every real number

and any positive

\exp(c_{f,6}\lambda^{8/7})\leqslant\mathbb{E}(e^{\lambda F_{f,\mathbb{X}}})\leqslant\exp(c_{f,7}\lambda^{5/2+\varepsilon}).

Proof.

Since
X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(t_{n})
and the
t_{n}
’s are independent random variables uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
, we have, for
\lambda\geq 1
,

E\bigl(\exp(\pm\lambda X)\bigr)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}E\bigl(\exp(\pm\lambda a_n(t))\bigr)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\bigl(\pm\lambda a_{n}(t)\bigr)\,dt.

First, we observe that
e^{x}\leq 1+x+x^{2}
for
x\leq 1
;
e^{x}\geq 1+x
for all
x

e^{x}\geq 1+x+\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{x^{3}}{6}
for all

|a_{n}(t)|\leq c_{f}n^{-\frac{5}{12}+\varepsilon}

Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be sufficiently small. For those
n

\lambda n^{-\frac{7}{18}}<\varepsilon_{0}
, using
\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)\,dt=0
we obtain

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 1\pm\lambda\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)\,dt+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{2}\,dt\pm\frac{\lambda^{3}}{6}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{3}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\exp\bigl(\pm\lambda a_{n}(t)\bigr)\,dt\leq 1+\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{2}\,dt.

Consequently, since
\log(1+y)\leq y

\log(1+y)\geq y/2

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{2}\,dt\;\leq\ \log\int_{0}^{1}\exp\bigl(\pm\lambda a_{n}(t)\bigr)\,dt\;\leq\;\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{1}a_{n}(t)^{2}\,dt.

If

1=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(1\pm\lambda a_{n}(t)\bigr)\,dt\;\leq\;\int_{0}^{1}\exp\bigl(\pm\lambda a_{n}(t)\bigr)\,dt\;\leq\;\exp\bigl(c_{f}\lambda n^{-\frac{5}{12}+\varepsilon}\bigr).

Hence,

\lambda^{8/7}\ll\lambda^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda n^{-\frac{7}{18}}<\varepsilon_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon(n)^{2}\frac{A_{f}(1,nr^{3})^{2}}{(nr^{3})^{4/3}}\ll\log E\bigl(\exp(\pm\lambda X)\bigr),

and

\displaystyle\log E\bigl(\exp(\pm\lambda X)\bigr)

\displaystyle\ll\lambda^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda n^{-\frac{7}{18}}<\varepsilon_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon(n)^{2}\frac{A_{f}(1,nr^{3})^{2}}{(nr^{3})^{4/3}}+\lambda\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda n^{-\frac{7}{18}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\end{subarray}}n^{-\frac{5}{12}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll\lambda^{5/2+\varepsilon}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we can present the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

We only prove the upper bound for
\mathbb{P}_{T}(F_{f}(t)>V)
, as the lower bound for
\mathbb{P}_{T}(F_{f}(t)<-V)
follows by the same argument applied to
-F_{f}(t)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the set appearing in Lemma
5.5
and choose a parameter
\lambda
satisfying

0<\lambda\leq c_{f,0}(\log\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log\log T)^{-5/3}
.
By Lemma
5.5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(F_{f}(t)>V\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\,\mathrm{meas}(\mathcal{A}^{c})+\frac{1}{T}\int_{\mathcal{A}}\exp\!\Bigl(\lambda\bigl(F_{f}(t)-V\bigr)\Bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{(\log T)^{20}}+e^{-\lambda V}\;\mathbb{E}\Bigl(e^{\lambda F_{f,\mathbb{X}}}\Bigr).

Using Lemma
6.1
and selecting
\lambda
so that
V=2c_{f,7}\lambda^{3/2+\varepsilon}
yields

\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(F_{f}(t)>V\bigr)\ll\frac{1}{(\log T)^{20}}+e^{-\lambda V/2}\ll\exp\Bigl(-c_{f,8}\,V^{5/3-\varepsilon}\Bigr),

for a suitable positive constant
c_{f,8}
.

We now establish the lower bound.
Set
\lambda_{\max}=c_{f,0}(\log\log T)^{1/3}(\log\log\log T)^{-5/3}
and let
\lambda
be the number satisfying

(6.1)

\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{A})}\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{\lambda F_{f}(t)}\,dt=2e^{\lambda V}.

Such a
\lambda
exists and lies in the interval
(0,\lambda_{\max})
.
Indeed, both sides of (
6.1
) are continuous in
\lambda
; the left–hand side is smaller when
\lambda=0
, while for
\lambda=\lambda_{\max}
Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
6.1
give

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{A})}\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{\lambda_{\max}F_{f}(t)}\,dt

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\;\mathbb{E}\Bigl(e^{\lambda_{\max}F_{f,\mathbb{X}}}\Bigr)+o(1)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\Bigl(\frac{c_{f,6}}{2}\lambda_{\max}^{8/7}\Bigr)>2e^{\lambda_{\max}V},

the last inequality following from our hypothesis on
V
provided the constant
b_{f,2}
is chosen sufficiently small.

Combining Lemma
5.5
, Lemma
6.1
and (
6.1
) we obtain

\lambda V=\log\!\Bigl(\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{\lambda F_{f,\mathbb{X}}}\bigr)\Bigr)+O(1)\gg\lambda^{8/7},

hence

\lambda\ll V^{7}.

Now apply the Paley–-Zygmund inequality together with (
6.1
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(F_{f}(t)>V\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{A})}\;\operatorname{meas}\!\Bigl\{t\in\mathcal{A}:e^{\lambda F_{f}(t)}>e^{\lambda V}\Bigr\}+O\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{20}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\,\frac{\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{A})}\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{\lambda F_{f}(t)}\,dt\Bigr)^{\!2}}{\displaystyle\frac{1}{\operatorname{meas}(\mathcal{A})}\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{2\lambda F_{f}(t)}\,dt}+O\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{20}}\Bigr).

Using (
6.1
) again together with Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
6.1
,

\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(F_{f}(t)>V\bigr)\gg\frac{e^{2\lambda V}}{\mathbb{E}\bigl(e^{2\lambda F_{f,\mathbb{X}}}\bigr)}+\frac{1}{(\log T)^{20}}\gg\exp\!\Bigl(-c_{f,9}\,\lambda^{5/2+\varepsilon}\Bigr),

for some positive constant
c_{f,9}
; here we used the trivial estimate
e^{\lambda V}\geq 1
.
Recalling that
\lambda\ll V^{7}
finishes the proof.
∎
