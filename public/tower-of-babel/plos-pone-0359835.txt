Infrared thermographic evaluation of cleaning stone relics at tomb of Yue Fei

Ting Zhang, Sheng Ye, Liang Ye, Zekai Qian

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the efficacy and safety of steam cleaning technology for the removal of surface contaminants from stone cultural relics at the Tomb of Yue Fei, a nationally protected heritage site in China. The research focuses on common pollutants including dust accumulation, surface encrustation, hydro erosion, bioerosion, and anthropogenic graffiti. An evaluation method based on infrared thermography is proposed to assess cleaning effectiveness by analyzing temperature variation (T) before and after treatment. Field experiments were conducted using a steam cleaner with controlled parameters (pressure, temperature, angle, distance, and duration), and cleaning outcomes were evaluated through visual inspection, chromatic aberration measurement, and microscopic imaging. Results demonstrate that steam cleaning effectively removes most contaminants without causing significant damage to the stone substrate. The study also provides a set of recommended steam cleaning parameters for different pollutant types on limestone surfaces. The findings validate the feasibility of using infrared thermography as a non-destructive evaluation tool for cleaning efficacy, contributing to the development of standardized protocols for the conservation of stone heritage.

Introduction The tomb of Yue Fei is located at the southern foot of Qixia Mountain, West Lake, and was designated as one of the first batch of key cultural relics under national protection in 1961. Field investigations indicate that multiple stone cultural relics at the site suffer from structural deformation, moisture accumulation at the base, acid rain exposure, soluble salts, and root penetration by vegetation—factors collectively contributing to co-occurring contaminant types that threaten long-term preservation. On-site surveys identify two primary categories of surface contaminants: water-induced damage and biological deterioration. Water-induced damage accounts for approximately 50% of all observed contamination: surface runoff causes discoloration and contaminant deposition (20%); capillary water leads to accumulation of soluble salts and formation of rust-like crusts (15%); and condensate results in water staining on rock surfaces (15%). In recent years, with advances in science and technology and heightened national emphasis on cultural heritage conservation, conventional cleaning methods have increasingly failed to meet current conservation objectives and principles. Steam cleaning—a green, environmentally benign, highly efficient, and operationally straightforward technique—has thus gained favor among heritage conservation practitioners [ 1 ]. He [ 2 ] reviewed the development history, operational mechanisms, and practical applications of steam cleaning technology, providing foundational information for Chinese conservation professionals. Zhang [ 3 ] demonstrated that steam jet cleaning exhibits measurable efficacy for stone carvings and historic building surfaces, with relatively low moisture penetration; however, its effectiveness against deeply embedded black crusts remains limited. Zhao [ 4 ] observed in on-site cleaning experiments that steam cleaning effectively removes loosely adhered contaminants such as ink stains and surface dust. Early research into steam cleaning technology was inspired by foreign technicians’ observation that hot steam could efficiently remove oil residues from garage floors [ 5 ]. Holden Conservation Ltd. (UK) [ 6 ] applied low-pressure steam cleaning to the London Monument and found it highly effective in removing black crusts from stone carvings, with no visible surface damage. By establishing a theoretical analysis model for steam cleaning technology and integrating it with practical cleaning operations, the influence of technical parameters such as steam temperature, pressure, and velocity on the surface of stone cultural relics was simulated, thereby obtaining targeted reference data [ 7 ]. International scholars have conducted a series of research analyses on steam cleaning technology, finding that high-temperature steam has a sterilizing effect on microorganisms. When the steam injection time is between three to five seconds, it can eliminate over 50% of stubborn bacteria [ 8 ]. Yan et al. [ 9 ] utilized fluid simulation software to analyze the flow state of a steam nozzle, determining the optimal cleaning distance and subsequently developing a corresponding cleaning machine. Some domestic scholars have investigated steam cleaning technology using numerical simulation methods. For example, Xu [ 10 ] employed numerical calculation methods to study the impact of hot steam injection on cascade outlet parameters. The study found that, with a fixed injection angle, the steam injection flow rate is proportional to the nozzle width; a smaller width results in a weaker flow rate. Zhao et al. [ 11 ] conducted a numerical simulation analysis of the technical parameters of steam cleaning technology. They proposed a corresponding cleaning process based on the effective action parameters for cleaning and the safe control range of steam, providing a reference for the removal of surface contaminants from sandstone cultural relics. In summary, steam cleaning technology offers advantages such as safety, non-Contaminants, and effective cleaning when applied to the conservation of stone cultural relics, making it a promising technique for this purpose. However, in practical applications, attention must be paid to operational details and the selection of technical parameters to ensure cleaning efficacy while minimizing potential damage to the cultural relics themselves. The cleaning of stone cultural relics should adhere to the principles of “preserving the original state” and “minimal intervention.” To ensure the cleaning process is non-destructive and effective, non-destructive testing technology must be employed to assess changes on the relic surface before and after cleaning [ 12 ]. Establishing evaluation indices for stone cultural relics requires setting up a scientific and reasonable index system, where the evaluation indices should be purposeful, scientific, and feasible [ 13 , 14 ]. The method of using ultrasonic testing to assess the weathering degree of stone cultural relics is currently well-established. Chen [ 15 ] utilized the high resolution and non-destructive characteristics of ultrasound tomography technique to evaluate rock weathering. Sun [ 16 ] analyzed the dispersion characteristics of ultrasonic transmission waves and Rayleigh surface waves, measured the velocity of longitudinal ultrasonic waves, and combined these with other methods to verify the reliability of ultrasonic testing results. Ultrasonic technology is also employed to assess the weathering measurement and damage degree of stone cultural relics [ 17 ]. International researchers have integrated ultrasonic methods with other detection techniques, such as moisture content measurement, to conduct safety evaluations of rock surface damage and weathering extent [ 18 , 19 ]. Furthermore, temperature change serves as a significant index for examining the rock surface deterioration condition. Chen [ 20 ] found that under high temperatures, the intrinsic stress of rock increases, and cyclic temperature fluctuations damage the rock mass structure, while stress concentration occurs at water-bearing fissures in rock under low temperatures. Shao [ 21 ] utilized infrared thermal imaging technology to evaluate the weathering degree of stone cultural relics. The results indicated that greater temperature amplitude correlates with more severe deterioration, thereby validating the feasibility of infrared detection for assessing rock weathering. Methods for assessing the cleaning effectiveness of surface contaminants on stone cultural relics through color change are also relatively common. Zhou et al. [ 22 ] determined the cleaning effectiveness based on the chromatic aberration results and microscopic roughness change results before and after two cleaning cycles. Wang [ 23 ] evaluated the cleaning effect by naked eye observation and weight detection of chromatic aberration changes. The results indicated that after cleaning, the test blocks exhibited a reddish hue, a reduction in weight, and a relatively increased chromatic aberration change, suggesting a favorable cleaning outcome. Mauro Matteini et al. [ 24 ] employed ion chromatography to detect the cleaning effectiveness on stone cultural relics of a city gate. Zina-Sabrina et al. [ 25 ] adopted a novel spectral imaging method to detect the deep cleaning of stone surface contaminants, which can effectively reflect the cleaning depth and thereby achieve high-precision detection. Zeng [ 26 ] evaluated the desalination effect through three methods: mass change, porosity detection, and ultrasonic testing, thereby determining the degree of removal efficacy for different desalination materials. Yan [ 27 ] employed multiple scientific techniques to assess the cleaning effectiveness in the cleaning of stone carving diseases. These included microscopic observation to examine the microstructural changes on the cultural relic surface after desalination, and the use of a spectrophotometer to measure the chromatic aberration in contaminated areas before and after cleaning, which served to evaluate the cleaning outcome. Internationally, research groups from France, Italy, and Greece have made substantial contributions to the application of infrared thermography (IRT) in stone heritage conservation. Moropoulou and Avdelidis [ 28 ] pioneered the use of IRT for moisture monitoring and conservation assessment in historic structures, demonstrating its effectiveness for evaluating cleaning interventions and protective treatments on porous stone facades. The National Technical University of Athens research group established a comprehensive framework for integrating IRT with other non-destructive techniques for the diagnosis and protection of built cultural heritage [ 29 ]. In Italy, Pappalardo et al. [ 30 ] applied IRT combined with photogrammetric three-dimensional models to evaluate different weathering types affecting natural stones in heritage buildings, demonstrating that thermal contrasts correlate with surface morphology, color, and environmental conditions. Grinzato et al. [ 31 ] analyzed the feasibility of using IRT to detect sub-surface stratification and detachments in frescoes from the perspective of thermal inertia. Biscarin et al. [ 32 ] proposed a multi-sensing approach combining UAV photogrammetry, infrared thermography, and ground-penetrating radar for enhanced structural and material degradation assessment of the Roman masonry bridge of Ponte Lucano. Carlomagno et al. [ 33 ] systematically reviewed the application of infrared thermography and geophysical techniques in cultural heritage conservation, establishing theoretical foundations for thermal measurement interpretation.In France, Vázquez and colleagues [ 34 ] conducted experimental thermo-hydric studies to simulate natural weathering conditions, using IRT to monitor microstructural changes induced by thermal cycling and moisture migration in building stones. Their work highlighted the importance of emissivity calibration and environmental control in obtaining reliable thermographic data for weathering assessment. More recently, Ibarra-Castanedo et al. [ 35 ] introduced solar loading thermography for the inspection of civil engineering and cultural heritage structures, utilizing time-lapsed thermographic surveys and advanced signal processing to detect subsurface defects under natural solar excitation. 2. Materials and methods 2.1. Evaluation method of steam cleaning effect on stone cultural relics based on infrared thermography 2.1.1. Establishment of evaluation method for steam cleaning effect using infrared thermography detection. (1) Evaluation of steam cleaning impact on stone cultural relics damage using infrared images The steam cleaning effect can be directly assessed through infrared images and three-dimensional temperature distribution maps. The results of safety tests on steam cleaning of stone cultural relics indicate that cleaning time, angle, and pressure are positively correlated with the temperature increase range, while cleaning distance is inversely correlated with the temperature increase range. The appropriate cleaning time should be controlled within 5 minutes. A good cleaning effect is associated with good rock permeability and uniform temperature distribution; minor rock damage is accompanied by small temperature changes before and after cleaning. In addition, the surface temperature change of rocks is influenced by factors such as environmental temperature and humidity, light intensity, wind speed, rock material, and different types of contaminants. Therefore, attention should be paid to temperature changes during cleaning testing, and appropriate steam cleaning technical parameters should be determined to ensure uniform distribution of rock surface temperature.To ensure the reliability of thermographic measurements, the emissivity of the stone surface was set to 0.95 based on literature values for limestone materials.To minimize the impact of solar radiation, all measurements were conducted in overcast or shaded areas, with environmental temperature and humidity recorded. The interval between thermal imaging before and after cleaning was maintained at approximately 30 minutes to ensure comparability of environmental conditions. (2) Evaluation method of steam cleaning effect based on temperature variation T The test results of steam cleaning limestone test blocks show that the temperature increase range after cleaning differs between clean test block surfaces and those with contaminants. Clean test blocks exhibit a small temperature increase range after cleaning, cool down quickly, and can rapidly return to the initial temperature range; whereas test blocks with contaminants show a large temperature increase range and a small temperature decrease range after cleaning.A larger Temperature variation before and after cleaning in clean areas corresponds to a greater degree of damage to the test block, which is demonstrated in indoor cleaning tests. Based on the temperature change amplitudes before and after cleaning under different cleaning parameters and for different contaminants, combined with the results of chromatic aberration detection and microscopic image detection, the cleaning effect of contaminants on the surface of stone cultural relics is evaluated using the initial surface temperature of the rock before cleaning as t 1 , the surface temperature after cleaning as t 2 , and the Temperature variation T before and after cleaning as the index, where positive values indicate temperature rise and negative values indicate temperature drop. The calculation formula is as follows (1) : T = ( t 2 − t 1 ) t 1 × 100 % (1) T denotes the magnitude of temperature change before and after cleaning (%), and t 1 and t 2 represent the temperatures before and after cleaning (°C), respectively. 2.1.2. Feasibility analysis of the evaluation method using temperature variation T. Currently, there is no unified or fixed evaluation criteria for assessing the cleaning effectiveness of surface contaminants on stone cultural relics. Existing detection indicators for temperature are primarily used to evaluate the degree of surface weathering or the presence of cracks in the rock. Therefore, this study employs data fitting based on the detection results of chromatic aberration and roughness to analyze temperature variation T. The test period spans one month, from mid-May to mid-June 2024. This cleaning primarily targets surface contamination and deterioration, including dirt deposits, water damage, and biological colonization. Deionized water is selected as the cleaning agent. Finally, the cleaned area is rinsed with clean water to remove residual contaminants, allowed to air-dry naturally, and then inspected; any incompletely cleaned areas undergo a second cleaning cycle. 3. Dust contaminants on stone surfaces are predominantly loosely accumulated on the surface and thus relatively easy to remove. For stone cultural relics featuring fine surface textures—such as incised or relief carvings—the texture characteristics must be considered during cleaning. Discussion of steam cleaning test results The bonding layers between surface contaminants and the rock substrate of stone cultural relics are structured as “contaminant surface layer - bonding layer between contaminant and rock matrix - rock matrix layer.” During steam cleaning, the contaminant surface layer is first wetted, followed by impact cleaning that reaches the rock matrix layer. Contaminant removal relies on the dissolution of contaminants by water and steam, as well as the scouring and friction exerted by the steam flow. Selecting cleaning conditions based on the characteristics of different contaminants can enhance effectiveness. For instance, lower cleaning parameters are suitable for accumulated dust. For crusted water stain contamination, an appropriate cleaning angle should be chosen to utilize steam flow friction for removal. For anthropogenic ink graffiti, parameters should be selected according to the chemical composition. Marker stains that are difficult to dissolve in water require longer cleaning times and a low cleaning angle. For grease stains, parameters are chosen based on the formation period and the composition type of the surface contamination, with a cleaning time exceeding 3 minutes. 5. Conclusion The significance of this study for the conservation of the Yue Fei Tomb lies in the following.It provides a scientifically validated,parameter-optimized cleaning protocol that minimizes risk to the original stone while effectively removing harmful contaminants.It introduces an infrared thermography-based evaluation method that allows on-site, non-invasive monitoring of cleaning outcomes, which is crucial for a site of national importance.The integration of colorimetry, microscopy, and thermography establishes a holistic assessment framework that can be adapted for other stone cultural relics,thereby contributing to the broader field of heritage conservation science.The successful application at the Yue Fei Tomb demonstrates that steam cleaning,combined with advanced diagnostic tools,can play a vital role in preserving the aesthetic and structural integrity of irreplaceable cultural heritage.
