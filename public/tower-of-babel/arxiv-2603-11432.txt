Inertial Limit of global weak solutions for Compressible Navier–Stokes
Cheng Yu

Abstract
. We investigate the inertial limit of the compressible Navier–Stokes system posed on the 3 3 -dimensional torus, and allowing for regions of vacuum. Considering global-in-time finite-energy weak solutions of a scaled system, we rigorously establish convergence to a limiting system in which the momentum equation reduces to a stationary elliptic balance between pressure and viscous forces. In this limit, the scaled kinetic energy vanishes, reflecting an overdamped regime, and the limiting weak solution satisfies an exact energy equality. Our analysis relies on uniform a priori estimates, renormalized techniques, and compactness arguments in the Lions–Feireisl framework, providing a mathematically rigorous analysis for the overdamped dynamics arising from vanishing inertia in compressible viscous flows.



1.
Introduction and overview

The compressible Navier–Stokes equations provide a fundamental model for viscous compressible flows, incorporating both inertial transport and dissipative mechanisms.
Depending on the relative magnitude of inertia, viscosity, and pressure forces, different asymptotic regimes arise, leading to reduced models with distinct mathematical and physical structures.
This paper is devoted to the rigorous analysis of one such regime, namely the
inertial limit
, in which inertial effects become negligible compared to pressure and viscous forces.

We consider the following scaled compressible Navier–Stokes system posed on the
3
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
:

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+{\rm div}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})=0,\\
&\varepsilon\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon{\rm div}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}\otimes u^{\varepsilon})+\nabla p(\rho^{\varepsilon})=\nu\Delta u^{\varepsilon}+(\nu+\lambda)\nabla({\rm div}u^{\varepsilon}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\rho^{\varepsilon}=\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\geq 0
denotes the density and
u^{\varepsilon}=u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the velocity field.
The constants
\nu>0
and
\nu+\lambda\geq 0
represent viscosity coefficients, and the pressure is assumed to be isentropic of the form

p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma},\qquad\gamma>1.

The small parameter
\varepsilon>0
measures the strength of inertia relative to pressure and viscous effects.
Formally, the scaling corresponds to a regime of
strong friction
or
high viscosity
, in which momentum relaxes rapidly and acceleration becomes negligible.
Such limits arise naturally, for instance, in highly viscous flows, porous media models, or slow dynamics dominated by internal stresses rather than transport.

The system is supplemented with initial data

\rho^{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x),\qquad(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})(0,x)=m_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x).

(1.2)

Throughout the paper we investigate the asymptotic behavior of finite-energy weak solutions as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In short, we prove that finite-energy weak solutions converge to a pressure–viscosity dominated system, with complete loss of inertia.

Formally neglecting the inertial terms in the momentum equation, the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
leads to the reduced system

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}(\rho u)=0,\\[6.0pt]
\nabla p(\rho)=\nu\Delta u+(\nu+\lambda)\nabla({\rm div}u),\end{cases}

(1.3)

supplemented with the initial condition

\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0}(\cdot)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{T}^{3}.

(1.4)

The limiting system exhibits a fundamental structural change.
While the original Navier-Stokes equations is a coupled system with hyperbolic transport with parabolic dissipation, the limit system is
elliptic in the velocity
at each fixed time.
The velocity field is instantaneously slaved to the density through a pressure–viscosity balance, reflecting a complete loss of inertia.
As a result, the evolution becomes overdamped: momentum no longer propagates dynamically, but rather adjusts quasi-statically to the evolving density field. For the physical background, we refer the readers to Lions
[
5
]
(see Chapter 7, Vol. 2).
From a modeling perspective, the limiting pressure–viscosity balance can be viewed as a Brinkman-type relation
[
1
,
12
]
, linking the inertial limit of compressible flows to classical porous-media and filtration models.

From a physical viewpoint, this regime corresponds to a situation in which kinetic energy cannot be sustained.
Although the velocity field need not vanish pointwise, its contribution to the total energy becomes negligible at the considered scaling.
One of the central objectives of this work is to show that this intuition can be made fully rigorous, even in the presence of vacuum and weak solutions.

1.1.
Definitions

We allow for localized regions of low density, including vacuum, and work within the framework of finite-energy weak solutions in the sense of Lions
[
5
]
, Feireisl–Novotný–Petzeltová
[
6
]
, and Feireisl
[
7
]
. These results are analogous in 2D and 3D, except for the admissible range of the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
. Therefore, from this point onward, we formulate and analyze the problem in 3D. The main result and definitions of this paper remain valid in 2D setting as well.

Definition 1.1
.

A pair
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
is called a
finite-energy weak solution
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
if the following conditions hold:

1. Regularity

\rho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0,\quad\rho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))\quad\text{for some }\gamma>1,

\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\quad u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})).

2. Continuity Equation holds in the renormalized senese

For every test function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3})
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(b(\rho_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}\phi+b(\rho_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\phi\Big)\,dx\,dt&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(b^{\prime}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\rho_{\varepsilon}-b(\rho_{\varepsilon})){\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}\phi\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))\phi(0,x)\,dx=0.\end{split}

This is equivalent to

\partial_{t}b(\rho)+{\rm div}\bigl(b(\rho)u\bigr)+\bigl(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\bigr){\rm div}u=0

in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
for any
b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

b^{\prime}(z)\equiv 0\quad\text{for all }z\geq M,

where the constant
M
may depend on the choice of
b
.

3. Weak Formulation of the Momentum Equation

For all vector-valued test functions
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\partial_{t}\psi+\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\otimes u_{\varepsilon}:\nabla\psi\Big)\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}\,{\rm div}\psi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\nu\nabla u_{\varepsilon}:\nabla\psi+(\nu+\lambda)({\rm div}u_{\varepsilon})({\rm div}\psi)\Big)\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}m_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\psi(0,x)\,dx.

4. Energy Inequality (Finite Energy)

For a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big)dx&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\nu|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)dx\,ds\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big)dx.\end{split}

(1.5)

Below
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
denotes distributions in space-time and
C_{c}^{\infty}
test functions are compactly supported.

Definition 1.2
.

A pair
(\rho,u)
is called a
weak solution
of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
if the following hold.

(Regularity).
The functions satisfy, for some admissible exponent
\gamma>1
,

\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\qquad\rho\geq 0\ \text{a.e.},

and

u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Continuity Equation holds in the renormalized sense

For every test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3})
,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(b(\rho_{\varepsilon})\partial_{t}\varphi+b(\rho_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi\Big)\,dx\,dt&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(b^{\prime}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\rho_{\varepsilon}-b(\rho_{\varepsilon})){\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}b(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))\varphi(0,x)\,dx=0.\end{split}

(Weak momentum / elliptic relation).
For almost every
t\in(0,T)
the pair
(\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))
satisfies the elliptic type equation in weak form: for every vector-valued test function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\gamma}\,{\rm div}\psi(x)\,dx&+\nu\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nabla u(t,x)\,:\,\nabla\psi(x)\,dx\\
&+(\nu+\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}({\rm div}u(t,x))({\rm div}\psi(x))\,dx=0.\end{split}

(1.6)

Here
\nabla u:\nabla\psi=\sum_{i,j}\partial_{i}u_{j}\,\partial_{i}\psi_{j}
.

1.2.
Main Result

Before stating our main results, we recall that the objects we study are well-posed in the weak sense.
The existence of global weak solutions was established in
[
6
]
for any
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
in3D and for any
\gamma>1
in 2D.
In the following part of this paper, we need this restriction for
\gamma
.
This follows from the foundational work of Lions
[
5
]
and the further development
by Feireisl–Novotný–Petzeltová
[
6
]
and Feireisl
[
7
]
, which also allow for the presence of vacuum. These results guarantee the existence of pairs
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})

satisfying the regularity, renormalized continuity, momentum, and energy inequalities stated in Definition
1.1
.

For the limiting system (
1.3
), which reduces the momentum equation to a stationary elliptic relation for the velocity,
existence of weak solutions is also established in Lions
[
5
]
(see Chapter 7, Vol. 2).
These existence results provide a rigorous foundation for our subsequent analysis of the inertial limit,
as they guarantee that the sequences of weak solutions considered in the limit process are indeed meaningful.

While there is a substantial body of work
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
on singular limits and related asymptotic regimes for compressible fluids, rigorous results on the inertial (small-mass / overdamped) limit for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations appear to be scarce. This highlights the novelty and significance of the current paper.

After introducing precise notions of weak solutions for both the scaled system and the limiting system, we establish compactness and convergence of global weak solutions as
\varepsilon\to 0
, under the well-preparedness assumption

\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lvert u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx\to 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(1.7)

This condition rules out the persistence of an initial inertial layer in the limit. Throughout the paper, we assume that (
1.7
) holds.

Theorem 1.1

(Inertial limit of finite-energy weak solutions)
.

For any
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
, let

(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
be a
finite-energy weak solution
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
Then there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\varepsilon\to 0
) such that

\begin{split}&\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\text{ and }\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\text{ in }L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\\
&u_{\varepsilon}\to u\quad\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}));\end{split}

and the weak limit
(\rho,u)
is a weak solution of (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
and the weak solution satisfies the following energy equality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx

Moreover, the scaled kinetic energy vanishes in the limit: for every fixed
t>0
,

\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\lvert u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\rvert^{2}\,dx\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that the limiting velocity field is determined instantaneously by the density
through an elliptic relation. In particular, the limiting momentum equation does not
generate an independent time evolution for the velocity, reflecting the overdamped
nature of the limit dynamics.

Our main result shows that, under a natural assumption on finite initial kinetic energy,
finite-energy weak solutions of the scaled compressible Navier–Stokes system converge (up to a subsequence) to weak solutions of the reduced pressure–viscous system.
Moreover, we prove that the scaled kinetic energy vanishes in the limit and that the limiting solution satisfies an
exact energy equality
.
This contrasts sharply with other singular limits, such as the vanishing viscosity limit, where anomalous dissipation may persist.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we establish compactness and stability properties of global weak solutions to the scaled
compressible Navier–Stokes system, relying on renormalization techniques in the sense of Lions
[
5
]
.
In Section 3, we prove that weak solutions of the limiting system satisfy an exact energy balance.
Finally, in Section 4, we complete the proof of the main convergence result and, in particular,
show that the scaled kinetic energy vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

2.
Weak compactness as
\varepsilon\to 0

The goal of this section is to prove the weak compactness of global weak solutions
to the scaled compressible Navier–Stokes equations as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

By the classical existence theories
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, for any fixed

\varepsilon>0
and any adiabatic exponent
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
, there exists
a global-in-time finite-energy weak solution

(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
to the scaled compressible
Navier–Stokes system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in 3D.
These solutions satisfy the standard energy inequality, which provides
uniform-in-
\varepsilon
bounds on the density and velocity. In particular, such solutions satisfy the following energy inequality: for a.e.

t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big)\,dx&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\nu|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)\,dx\,ds\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\Big)\,dx.\end{split}

(2.1)

Once the right-hand side of the above energy inequality is finite, we immediately
deduce the following uniform bounds:

\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\,u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big),

(2.2)

\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big),

(2.3)

and

\rho_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big).

(2.4)

By the definition of weak solutions, the pair

(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
satisfies the continuity equation
in the sense of renormalized solutions.
Following
[
6
]
, one can regularize the renormalized equation to obtain

\partial_{t}S_{m}\big(b(\rho_{\varepsilon})\big)+{\rm div}\!\left(S_{m}\big(b(\rho_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\big)+S_{m}\big(b^{\prime}(\rho_{\varepsilon})\rho_{\varepsilon}-b(\rho_{\varepsilon})\big){\rm div}u_{\varepsilon}\right)=r_{m},

(2.5)

where the remainder term satisfies

r_{m}\to 0\quad\text{in }L^{2}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big)\quad\text{as }m\to\infty,

provided that
b
is a uniformly bounded
C^{1}
function. Here
S_{m}(f)=f*\eta_{m}
denotes the standard spatial mollification operator.

Consequently, by adapting the argument of Section 4.1 in
[
6
]
and using
the uniform bounds
(
2.2
)–(
2.4
),
we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
, then there exists a positive number
\theta
, only depending on
\gamma
, such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma+\theta}\,dx\,dt\leq C,

where
C
is a constant.

As a consequence of the previous lemma and basic energy inequality, which provides uniform energy bounds
and compactness properties for the density through the renormalized continuity
equation, we can establish the following proposition concerning the convergence
and compactness of the weak solutions as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a subsequence of
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
such that

\begin{split}&\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\text{ in }C(0,T;L^{\gamma}_{weak}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\;\;\;\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\text{ in }L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\\
&u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\end{split}

and the weak limit
(\rho,u)
is a weak solution to (
1.3
)-(
1.4
).

Proof.

With uniform bounds (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), and Lemma
2.1
at hands, we deduce that

\begin{split}&\rho_{\varepsilon}\to\rho\text{ in }C(0,T;L^{\gamma}_{weak}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\\
&u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})),\end{split}

(2.6)

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}\to\bar{\rho^{\gamma}}\text{ weakly in }L^{\frac{\gamma+\theta}{\gamma}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}),

(2.7)

where
\bar{f}
is the weak limit of the sequence
\{f_{n}\}
.

For any
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
, and (
2.6
) yields

\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\to\rho u\text{ in}\;\;D^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}).

Note that

\begin{split}\|\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}&\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\|\sqrt{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\\
&\to 0\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0,\end{split}

thus we have
\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\to 0\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

With the help of (
2.6
), this can ensure that

\varepsilon\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\otimes u_{\varepsilon}\to 0\text{ in }\;D^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3})

for any
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
.

Thus, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we are able to recover the weak limit
(\rho,u)
of the sequence of solutions
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
. In particular, this weak limit satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}(\rho u)=0,\\[6.0pt]
\nabla\bar{\rho^{\gamma}}=\nu\Delta u+(\nu+\lambda)\nabla({\rm div}u),\end{cases}

(2.8)

in the sense of
D^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}).
To show this weak limit is a weak solution to the equation,
we still need to show that

\rho^{\gamma}=\overline{\rho^{\gamma}}.

This follows from the strong convergence of the density in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))
.
The strong convergence of the density relies on uniform bounds and the weak continuity of the effective flux.
The proof closely follows the argument in Section 4 of
[
6
]
.
Meanwhile, we can deduce the following energy inequality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx.

(2.9)

∎

3.
Energy equality for the limit system

In this section, we aim to upgrade the basic energy inequality
(
2.9
) to a full energy equality for all
weak solutions under consideration. To achieve this, we first require the
following lemma, which provides a crucial estimate allowing us to control the
pressure term with respect to the velocity in appropriate
L^{p}
spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be any weak solution to (
1.3
)–(
1.4
)
satisfying the energy inequality (
2.9
). Then

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\rho^{\gamma}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on the initial data and the time
T>0
.

Proof.

We choose

\varphi(t,x)=S_{m}\!\left[\phi_{m}(t)\nabla\Delta^{-1}\!\left(S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})-\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\right)\right]

as a test function for any weak solution
(\rho,u)
to the system
(
1.3
)–(
1.4
), where

S_{m}(f)=f*\eta_{m}
denotes the standard spatial mollification operator;
and
\phi_{m}\in\mathcal{D}(0,T)
is a time cut-off function satisfying

\phi_{m}\to 1
as
m\to\infty
.

We remark that the operator
\nabla\Delta^{-1}
coincides with the
Bogovskii operator on the torus, up to the subtraction of the spatial mean.
More generally, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
The
Bogovskii operator

\mathcal{B}:L_{0}^{p}(\Omega)\longrightarrow W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad 1<p<\infty,

is a linear operator associated with the divergence equation. Specifically,
for any

f\in L_{0}^{p}(\Omega):=\Big\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}f\,dx=0\Big\},

the vector field
u=\mathcal{B}[f]
satisfies

{\rm div}u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

In the present proof, in order to control the terms
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
below, we rely
on the following standard properties of the Bogovskii operator:

(1)

Boundedness.

There exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,p)>0
such that

\|\nabla\mathcal{B}[f]\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(2)

Divergence property.

By construction, the operator satisfies

{\rm div}\mathcal{B}[f]=f\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Testing (
1.3
) with this function, we are able to derive that

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)|S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,dx\,dx\,dt\\
&+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)\nabla S_{m}(u)(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Delta^{-1})\left(S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})-\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\right)\,dx\,dt\\
&+(\nu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t){\rm div}S_{m}(u)\left(S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})-\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\right)\,dx\,dt\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},\end{split}

(3.1)

where we used
Divergence property
of Bogovskii operator to obtain

I_{3}
.

Using (
2.9
), we can control
|I_{1}|\leq C_{1}
, where
C_{1}
is a constant only depending on time
T
and the initial data.

To control
I_{2}
, we rely on
Boundedness
property of the Bogovskii operator,
this gives us

|I_{2}|\leq C_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)|S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

We are able to control
I_{3}
as similarly

|I_{3}|\leq C_{3}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|{\rm div}S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)|S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Using these controls of
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
to (
3.1
), this yields

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\phi_{m}(t)|S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})|^{2}\,dx\,dt&\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+C_{3}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|{\rm div}S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\end{split}

for all
m>0
.

Thus, letting
m\to\infty
in the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\rho^{\gamma}|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dt+C_{3}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|{\rm div}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Observe that the right-hand side of the above inequality can be controlled by the basic energy inequality (
2.9
). Consequently, we obtain the bound of the pressure
\rho^{\gamma}
.
∎

Once an
L^{p}
-bound for the pressure term has been established in Lemma
3.1
, we are in a position to upgrade the basic energy inequality to an energy equality. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be any weak solution to (
1.3
)–(
1.4
)
satisfying the energy inequality (
2.9
). Then the following energy equality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx

(3.2)

holds.

Proof.

Note that the weak limit
(\rho,u)
is a renormalized solution. That is,

\partial_{t}b(\rho)+\operatorname{div}(b(\rho)u)+\big(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\big)\operatorname{div}u=0

holds in the sense of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3})
for any

b\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

We regularize the above equation to obtain

\partial_{t}S_{m}(b(\rho))+\operatorname{div}\big(S_{m}(b(\rho)u)\big)+S_{m}\!\left(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\right){\rm div}(S_{m}(u))=R_{m},

(3.3)

where the commutator term
R_{m}
is given by

R_{m}:=S_{m}\!\left(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\right){\rm div}(S_{m}(u))-S_{m}\!\left(\big(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\big)\operatorname{div}u\right).

To derive the energy equality, we choose
S_{m}(S_{m}(u))
as a test function to derive the following one

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma}){\rm div}(S_{m}(u))\,dx\,dt&=-\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&-(\nu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|{\rm div}S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

(3.4)

We take
b(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
for (
3.3
), then

\partial_{t}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})+{\rm div}(S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})u)+(\gamma-1)S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma}){\rm div}S_{m}(u)=R_{m}.

Thus,
the left hand side of (
3.4
) will be given by

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\partial_{t}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}R_{m}\,dx\,dt.

This gives us the following one

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}R_{m}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\nu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt+(\nu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|{\rm div}S_{m}(u)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}S_{m}(\rho_{0}^{\gamma})\,dx\end{split}

(3.5)

Now let us to show that
R_{m}\to 0
in
L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
.
Recall that for
b(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
we have

\begin{split}&R_{m}:=S_{m}\!\big((b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho))\big)\,\operatorname{div}S_{m}(u)-S_{m}\!\Big(\big(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho)\big)\operatorname{div}u\Big)\\
&=(\gamma-1)\Big(S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,\operatorname{div}S_{m}u-S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma}\,\operatorname{div}u)\Big).\end{split}

This allows us to rewrite it as

R_{m}=(\gamma-1)\,\Big(\underbrace{S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,\operatorname{div}S_{m}u-S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,\operatorname{div}u}_{=:A_{m}}+\underbrace{S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\,\operatorname{div}u-S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma}\,\operatorname{div}u)}_{=:B_{m}}\Big),

thus we only need to handle
A_{m}
and
B_{m}
to control
R_{m}\to 0
as
m
goes to large.

Estimate of
A_{m}
.

By Hölder in
(t,x)
with exponents
2
–
2
and the strong convergence

\operatorname{div}S_{m}u\to\operatorname{div}u
in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\|A_{m}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq\|S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\|\operatorname{div}S_{m}u-\operatorname{div}u\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0.

Here we used that
S_{m}(\rho^{\gamma})\to\rho^{\gamma}
in
L^{2}
and is uniformly bounded in

L^{2}
thanks to Lemma 3.1 (i.e.
\rho^{\gamma}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
).

Estimate of
B_{m}
(Friedrichs commutator).

We introduce
f:=\rho^{\gamma}
, and
g:=\operatorname{div}u
, then

f\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})

by Lemma 3.1,
and

g\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})

since
u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.
The mollifiers
(S_{m})_{m\geq 1}
satisfy the standard product-commutator property:
for any
f\in L^{p}
,
g\in L^{q}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,

\|S_{m}(f)\,g-S_{m}(fg)\|_{L^{1}}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0.

Applying this with
p=q=2
yields

\|B_{m}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\|S_{m}(f)\,g-S_{m}(fg)\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0.

Combining the estimates for
A_{m}
and
B_{m}
, we conclude that

\|R_{m}\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0,

i.e.
R_{m}\to 0
strongly in
L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

Consequently, by passing to the limit
m\to\infty
in
(
3.5
), we are able to recover the energy equality
(
3.2
).

∎

4.
Proof to the Main theorem

The goal of this section is to rigorously justify the inertial limit of weak solutions to the compressible Navier–Stokes equations. In view of Proposition
2.1
, by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain a weak solution to the limit system
(
1.3
)–(
1.4
), which satisfies
Definition
1.2
.

A key difficulty in the limiting procedure is the possible loss of energy in the weak formulation. This issue is resolved by Proposition
3.1
, which shows that any weak solution of the limit system obtained in this way satisfies the energy equality
(
3.2
). As a consequence, the inertial limit preserves the full energy balance of the system.

To prove our main result, we still need to show

\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\;\longrightarrow\;0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

(4.1)

for any fixed
t\in[0,T].

To this end,
we argue by contradiction.
Assume that (
4.1
) is false for this fixed
t
.
Then there exist a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a sequence
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that

\varepsilon_{k}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon_{k}}(t,x)\,|u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\geq\varepsilon_{0}\qquad\text{for all }k.

(4.2)

By the energy inequality for the compressible Navier–Stokes system, for any fixed t, we have
for each
\varepsilon_{k}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,t)|u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,t)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\gamma}(x,t)}{\gamma-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,s)|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{k}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\rho^{0}_{\varepsilon_{k}}|u^{0}_{\varepsilon_{k}}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx.

Using the assumed lower bound, this yields

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{\gamma}(x,t)}{\gamma-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,s)|^{2}+(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\varepsilon_{k}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}\rho^{0}_{\varepsilon_{k}}|u^{0}_{\varepsilon_{k}}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx.

Passing to the limit
k\to\infty
, and using the convergences established
earlier together with the lower semicontinuity of the dissipation terms, we
obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}(x,t)}{\gamma-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,ds\;\leq\;\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx.

Since
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, this implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho^{\gamma}(x,t)}{\gamma-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\nu|\nabla u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\nu+\lambda)|{\rm div}u(x,s)|^{2}\,dx\,ds<\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{\rho_{0}^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\,dx,

which contradicts Proposition
3.1
, that is,

(\rho,u)
satisfies the energy equality (
3.2
).

Consequently, we have established (
4.1
) for any fixed
t\in[0,T]
. This completes the proof of our main result.
