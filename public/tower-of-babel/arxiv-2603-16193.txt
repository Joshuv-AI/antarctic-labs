Licci Property of Complementary Edge Ideals
Vivek Bhabani Lama

Abstract
. In this article, we characterize the class of complementary edge ideals which satisfy the licci property in terms of the underlying graph. Using this characterization, we associate the licci property of a complementary edge ideal to its other algebraic properties. Finally, we provide two different probability regimes for which the complementary edge ideals of random graphs are licci with high probability and not licci with high probability respectively.

1.
Introduction

Linkage theory is a classical and well studied aspect of algebraic geometry. Peskine and Szpiro in
[
22
]
converted the general theory of linkage into its ideal theoretic counter-part, which is an active area of research in commutative algebra for over many decades (see for instance
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
17
]
to mention a few). One aspect of keen interest in studying linkage theory from the ideal theoretic prospective of commutative algebra has been to study the translation of the properties between two linked ideals. In this regard, researchers have extensively studied the class of
licci
ideals i.e., the class of ideals that fall into the linkage class of a complete intersection (see for instance
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
) .

In combinatorial commutative algebra, the main aim in this regard has been to study the licci property of the ideal in a polynomial ring via the combinatorics of the associated combinatorial object (see for instnace
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
). For the class of edge ideals a complete characterization of licci edge ideals was given in
[
16
, Theorem 3.7.]
. A similar result was formulated for the class of binomial edge ideals in
[
6
, Theorem 3.5.]
. Very recently, a new class of ideals associated to graphs was introduced in
[
12
]
and independently in
[
8
]
, under the nomenclature of complementary edge ideals (for definitions and details see
Section

2.1
). Fundamental algebraic properties such as the characterization of the class of these ideals that admit a linear resolution (see
[
8
, Theorem 4.5.]
and
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.]
), primary decomposition of these ideals (see
[
8
, Theorem 2.1.]
), expressions for regularity and projective dimensions (see for instanace
[
8
, Theorem 4.1.]
and
[
12
, Theorem 4.2.]
) and various other algebraic properties were studied in
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
.

The main goal of this paper is to characterize the complementary edge ideals that satisfy the licci property in terms of the associated graphs. Thus, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem A (Theorem
3.2
).

Let
G
be a simple graph on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R
is licci,

(2)

G
is a forest or
G=K_{3}
, the complete graph on
3
vertices.

We observe that this combinatorial classification provides an association to other algebraic properties of complementary edge ideals which seem unrelated apriori to the result in
Theorem

3.2
(see
3.6
). Another application of
Theorem

3.2
is the characterization of licci property of complementary edge ideals via their Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity and projective dimension (see
3.3
and
3.4
).

In recent years, the use of probabilistic techniques in combinatorial commutative algebra has become an area of active research (see for instance
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
21
]
and the references therein). Motivated by the use of Erdős-Rényi random graphs in
[
1
]
to determine a random version of asymptotic behaviour of invariants of edge ideals, we give another application of
Theorem

3.2
in order to provide two different probability regimes for which the complementary edge ideals of random graphs are licci with high probability and not licci with high probability respectively (see
3.5
).

The paper is organized into three sections. In
Section

2
, we give the basic definitions and introduce some notations that will be used throughout the article. Some properties of complementary edge ideals of graphs studied in the literature are mentioned in this section. In
Section

3
, we prove
Theorem

3.2
, the characterization of licci complementary edge ideals in terms of their underlying simple graph. We also give some applications of our main result in this section.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce various notations and preliminaries used throught the article.
Throughout, let
\mathbb{K}
denote a field and
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring with indeterminates
x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{n}
over the field
\mathbb{K}
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
denote the homogeneous maximal ideal
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and let
R=S_{\mathfrak{m}}
, i.e. the localization of
S
at the homogeneous maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Consider the graded minimal free resolution of
M
:

0\to S_{p}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}S(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(M)}\xrightarrow{\phi_{p}}\dots\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}S_{0}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}(M)}\xrightarrow{\phi_{0}}M\to{0},

where
S(-j)
is the twist of
S
by degree
j
, i.e.,
S(-j)_{n}:=S_{n-j}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using the above construction of minimal free resolution of
M
, we have the following invariants:

\mathrm{reg}(M):=\max\{j-i:\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\},

\mathrm{pd}(M):=\max\{i:S_{i}\neq 0\}.

Finally, for a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, let
V(G)
denote the vertex set of the graph
G
and
E(G)
denote the edge set of
G
.

2.1.
Complementary Edge Ideals

In this subsection, we recall the definitions and results about complementary edge ideals in the literature. The class of
Complementary Edge Ideals
was first introduced in
[
12
]
and independently in
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
G
be a simple graph with
V(G)=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. The
complementary edge ideal
associated to
G
is the ideal in the polynomial ring
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
given by:

I_{c}(G)=(x_{1}x_{2}...x_{n}\setminus x_{i}x_{j}\ |\ \{i,j\}\in E(G)).

Fundamental properties of these ideals are well-studied in
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
. We state a few of these results for completeness. The following result characterizes the Cohen-Macaulayness of complementary edge ideals in terms of its underlying simple graph.

Theorem 2.1.2
.

[
8
, Theorem 2.8.]
,
[
12
, Theorem 1.4.]
Let
G
be a simple graph then the following are equivalent:

(1)

S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

G
is either a complete graph or a forest.

The following result provides bounds on the projective dimension and Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of complementary edge ideals.

Theorem 2.1.3
.

[
12
, Proposition 4.1.]

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
I_{c}(G)
be the complementary edge ideal of
G
, which is an ideal in
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then, the following conditions hold:

(1)

1\leq\mathrm{pd}(I)\leq 2
, where
\mathrm{pd}(I)
represents the projective dimension of
I
.

(2)

n-2\leq\mathrm{reg}(I)\leq n-1

The following theorem provides a characterization of Cohen-Macaulay height
2
complementary edge ideals in terms of their projective dimension.

Theorem 2.1.4
.

[
12
, Theorem 1.1.]

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, which is not the complete graph
K_{n}
and
I_{c}(G)
be the complementary edge ideal of
G
, which is an ideal in
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))=2
and
S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\mathrm{pd}(I_{c}(G))=1
.

In particular, we mention the explicit regularity of the complementary edge ideal of a forest.

Theorem 2.1.5
.

[
12
, Theorem 4.2.]

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, and
I_{c}(G)
be the complementary edge ideal of
G
, which is an ideal in
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

\mathrm{pd}(I)=1
and
\mathrm{reg}(I)=n-2
if and only if
G
is a tree or a complete graph.

(2)

\mathrm{pd}(I)=1
and
\mathrm{reg}(I)=n-1
if and only if
G
is a disconnected forest.

2.2.
Erdős-Rényi Random Graphs

Let
G
be a graph and
G(n,p)
denote the Erdős-Rényi random graph where
n\geq 1
and
p\in[0,1]
. The vertex set of
G(n,p)
is
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and each set
\{i,j\}\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
with
i\neq j
is an edge in
G(n,p)
with probability
p
and is independent of other edges. More precisely, given a graph
G
with
V(G)=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
|E(G)|=m
,

\mathbb{P}\{G(n,p)=G\}=p^{m}(1-p)^{\begin{pmatrix}n\cr 2\end{pmatrix}-m}.

The above expression shows that every graph with
m
edges has an equal chance of being chosen. When
p>1
,
G(n,p)
is defined as
G(n,\min(p,1))
. Typically, one examines the properties of
G(n,p)
as
n\to\infty
and
p
also changes with
n
. It is standard to write
p
even though it is understood that
p:=p(n)
. By the properties of
G(n,p)
, we refer to combinatorial properties, which are the characteristics of graphs that remain unchanged under graph isomorphisms. We will denote the complementary edge ideal of
G(n,p)
as the random ideal instead of
I_{c}(G(n,p))
.
The following theorem provides probability regimes for which a certain graph appears as a subgraph of an Erdős-Rényi random graph:

Theorem 2.2.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 5.3.]

Let
H
be a fixed graph with
|E(H)|>0
. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\lim_{{n\rightarrow\infty}}\mathbb{P}[H\subseteq G(n,p)]=\begin{cases}1&n^{(\frac{1}{m(H)})}p\rightarrow 0\\
0&n^{(\frac{1}{m(H)})}p\rightarrow\infty\end{cases}

where
m(H)=\max\left\{d(K)=\frac{|E(K)|}{|V(K)|}\ |\ K\text{ is a subgraph of $H$}\right\}
.

2.3.
Licci Ideals

In this subsection, we recall the basic definitions and a few results about licci ideals. As the nomenclature suggests, licci ideals are ideals are the class of ideals that are in the linkage class of a complete intersection ideal.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring and let
I
and
J
be two proper ideals of
R
.
I
and
J
are called
directly linked
if there exist a regular sequence
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}
in
I\cap J
such that
I=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):J
and
J=(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):I
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

Two directly linked ideals
I
and
J
will be denoted as
I\sim J
.

Next we recall the notion of a linkage class of an ideal in a regular local ring.

Definition 2.3.3
.

Two ideals
I
and
J
are said to be in the same
linkage class
if there exists a sequence of direct links of the follwoing form:

I=I_{0}\sim I_{1}\sim\dots\sim I_{m}=J

Remark 2.3.4
.

In
2.3.3
, if
J
is a complete intersection ideal, then
I
is said to be in the linkage class of a complete intersection (often reffered to as
I
is a licci ideal)

A necessary condition for a homogeneous ideal in a polynomial ring to be licci is given in
[
13
]
. This is an essential tool in order to rule out various classes of non-licci ideals. Therefore, we recall the following theorem from the literature:

Theorem 2.3.5
.

[
13
, Corollary 5.12.]

Let
I
be a Cohen-Macaulay homogeneous ideal in a standard graded polynomial ring
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
with the graded maximal
ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
. If
I_{\mathfrak{m}}\subset R=S_{\mathfrak{m}}
is licci, then

\mathrm{reg}(S/I)\geq(\mathrm{ht}(I)-1)(\mathrm{indeg}(I)-1)

where
\mathrm{indeg}(I)=\mathrm{min}\{i:I_{i}\neq 0\}
represents the initial degree of the ideal
I
.

3.
Complementary Edge Ideals and the Licci Property

In this section, we prove our main result which is the characterization of licci complementary edge ideals. We prove that the class of complementary edge ideals that satisfy this property are the ones whose underlying graphs are forests. To prove our main theorem, we first prove a result on the height of the complementary edge ideal of a complete graph. While this result is previously established in (
[
8
, Corollary 2.3.]
), our proof employs a different approach via its connections to edge ideals of complete hypergraphs and we include it for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
be a positive integer and
G=K_{n}
be a complete graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then,
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))=3
.

Proof.

We observe that for a complete graph
G=K_{n}
, the ideal
I_{c}(G)
is an ideal generated by all possible squarefree monomials of degree
n-2
. Also, for a monomial ideal
J
generated by all possible squarefree monomials of a fixed degree
d
, it is well-known that the height of
J
is given by
n-d+1
. Therefore, it easily follows that
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))=n-(n-2)+1=3
.
∎

Now, we prove our main result which characterizes the licci complementary edge ideals.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R
is licci,

(2)

G
is a forest or
G=K_{3}
, the complete graph on
3
vertices.

Proof.

For (1) implies (2), let
(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}
be licci then,
S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay. Therefore, by
2.1.2
,
G
is either a complete graph or
G
is a forest. Suppose that
G
is a complete graph, hence
G=K_{n}
then, by
3.1
, we have
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(K_{n}))=3
. Therefore, by
2.3.5
, we have the following condition on the regularity of
I_{c}(G)
:

\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))\geq(\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))-1)(\mathrm{indeg}(I_{c}(G)-1)+1.

However, the
\mathrm{indeg}(I_{c}(G))=n-2
and hence we arrive at the following inequality for regularity:

\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))\geq(\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G)-1)((n-2)-1)+1=(3-1)(n-3)+1.

Therefore, we get that the regularity is given by
\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))\geq 2n-6+1=2n-5
. However, from
2.1.3
, we have that
\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))\leq n-1
. Clearly for
n\geq 5
, we have
2n-5>n-1
which gives a contradiction and hence
G
is not
K_{n}
for
n\geq 5
. Therefore,
G
is a either a forest or
G=K_{n}
with
n=3,4
. For
n=4
the complementary edge ideal of
K_{4}
coincides with the edge ideal of
K_{4}
, that is
I_{c}(K_{4})=I(K_{4})
. However,
(I(K_{4}))_{\mathfrak{m}}
is not licci in
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
(see
[
16
, Theorem 3.7.]
) For (2) implies (1): Suppose
G
is a forest, then by
2.1.5
, the projective dimension
\mathrm{pd}(I_{c}(G))=1
and since
G
is not complete, therefore by
2.1.4
,
S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\mathrm{ht}(I_{c}(G))=2
. Since
I_{c}(G)
is a height 2 ideal such that
S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay. Therefore,
(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R
is a licci ideal (see
[
22
]
for details). Next if
G=K_{3}\text{ or }K_{4}
, for
n=3
we have
G=K_{3}
and hence
I_{c}(G)=I_{c}(K_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
which is a complete intersection ideal and hence licci. This completes the proof.
∎

A consequence of
Theorem

3.2
, is the following characterization of licci complementary edge ideals whose graphs have more than one connected components in terms of their regularity and projective dimension.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
with more than one connected component. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R
is licci,

(2)

S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay,

(3)

G
is a forest,

(4)

\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))=n-1
and
\mathrm{pd}(I)=1
.

Proof.

The proof is clear due to
Theorem

3.2
,
2.1.2
and
2.1.5
.
∎

Using this characterization, we get the following corollary regarding the regularity and projective dimension of complementary edge ideals of connected graphs that have the licci property.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a simple connected graph on the vertex set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
which is not
K_{n}
, the complete graph on
n
vertices. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq R
is licci,

(2)

G
is a tree,

(3)

S/I_{c}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay,

(4)

\mathrm{reg}(I_{c}(G))=n-2
and
\mathrm{pd}(I)=1
.

Proof.

The proof is clear due to
Theorem

3.2
,
2.1.2
and
2.1.5
.
∎

As a consequence of
Theorem

3.2
, we have the following result which gives the explicit probability that an Erdős-Rényi random graph will correspond to a licci complementary edge ideal.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G(n,p)
be an Erdős-Rényi random graph. Then, the following hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{{n\rightarrow\infty}}\mathbb{P}[I_{c}(n,p)\text{ is not licci}]=\begin{cases}1&np\rightarrow 0\\
0&np\rightarrow\infty\end{cases}

Proof.

From
Theorem

3.2
, it is enough to show that the expression for the limit of the probability of
G(n,p)
containing some cycle as a subgraph
H=C_{m}
for some
m\geq 4
, is given by:

\displaystyle\lim_{{n\rightarrow\infty}}\mathbb{P}[H\text{ is a subgraph of }G(n,p)]=\begin{cases}1&np\rightarrow 0\\
0&np\rightarrow\infty\end{cases}

Using
2.2.1
, it is enough to show the following equality:

m(H)=\max\left\{d(K)=\frac{|E(K)|}{|V(K)|}\ |\ K\text{ is a subgraph of $H$}\right\}=1.

However since
H=C_{m}
is a cycle for some
m>3
, the only possibilities for
K
are either
K=H=C_{m}
or
K
is a path with
t_{1}
many vertices where
t_{1}\leq m
or
K
is a disjoint union of paths with
t_{2}
many vertices where
t_{2}<m
. In the first case, if
K=C_{m}
it is clear that
d(K)=1
as the number of vertices of a cycle is equal to the number of edges. In the case that
K
is a path with
t_{1}
many edges,
d(K)=\frac{t_{1}-1}{t_{1}}=1-\frac{1}{t_{1}}<1
. Finally, in the case when
K
is a disjoint union of paths with
t_{2}
many edges,
d(K)<1
as the number of vertices is more than the number of edges in this case. Therefore,
m(H)=1
and hence the proof.
∎

Since
Theorem

3.2
gives a combinatorial characterization of complementary edge ideals that are licci, we may now associate the licci property to various seemingly unrelated algebraic properties of complementary edge ideals (unrelated in the sense that apriori to the combinatorial characterization in
Theorem

3.2
, there is no reason to believe that one of these algebraic properties would imply the other) . Given that the properties of complementary edge ideals have been extensively studied in
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
, we have the following non-reversible implications from
Theorem

3.2
:

Corollary 3.6
.

If
(I_{c}(G))_{\mathfrak{m}}
is licci then the following hold:

(1)

I_{c}(G)
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

I_{c}(G)^{\vee}
is component wise linear.

(3)

I_{c}(G)^{\vee}
has linear quotients.

(4)

I_{c}(G)^{\vee}
has linear resolution.

(5)

I_{c}(G)
has linear resolution.

Furthermore, none of these implications are reversible.

Proof.

The corollary is a straightforward application of the combinatorial characterization in
Theorem

3.2
and various properties proved in
[
8
, Theorem 2.5.]
and
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.]
.
∎

Another very similar direction in which research in commutative algebra is actively conducted is the classification and study of glicci ideals (see for instance
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
). In view of this, we conclude this section by posing the following question:

Question 3.7
.

For what class of simple graphs
G
does the complementary edge ideal
I_{c}(G)
fall into the gorenstein linkage class of a complete intersection ideal?
