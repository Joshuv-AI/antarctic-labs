Algorithms for experimenting with Zariski dense matrix groups over number fields
A. S. Detinko, D. L. Flannery, A. Hulpke

Abstract
. Let ℙ \mathbb{P} be an algebraic number field. We provide a computational analog of the strong approximation theorem for finitely generated Zariski dense groups H ≤ SL ⁡ ( n , ℙ ) H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{P}) , n n prime. That is, we present algorithms to find the set of congruence quotients of H H modulo all maximal ideals of a finitely generated subring R R of ℙ \mathbb{P} such that H ≤ SL ⁡ ( n , R ) H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R) . The algorithms have been implemented in GAP . Potential applications are illustrated by a range of experiments in degree 2 2 , with a special focus on Bianchi groups.

1.
Introduction

This work is a further contribution to an expanding
area of computational group theory; namely, computing
with matrix groups over an infinite field
\mathbb{F}
. Our algorithms
take as input a finite subset of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})
. This generates
a subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})
such that
H\leq\mathrm{GL}(n,R)
for
some finitely generated integral domain
R\subseteq\mathbb{F}
.
Since
H
is residually finite
[
30
, 4.2]
,
it can be investigated efficiently via its finite quotients, in
particular the congruence quotients arising from reduction
modulo maximal ideals of
R
.

A single congruence quotient of
H
is enough to solve some decision
problems; e.g., testing finiteness
[
11
]
. Deeper study of
H

requires knowledge of a plurality of its congruence quotients. Two
questions are foremost: can we find all congruence quotients of
H
;
how do the congruence quotients of
H
determine its structure and properties?

By way of the strong approximation property for linear groups
(see
[
23
, Window 9]
and
[
25
,
31
]
),
we can describe the congruence quotients of

H
when
H
is a dense subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
(Here and from now on, ‘dense’ means ‘Zariski dense’.)
Indeed, if
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
then the strong approximation
property implies that dense
H
surjects onto
\mathrm{SL}(n,p)

modulo almost all rational primes
p
. The congruence
quotients of such
H
modulo all maximal ideals of
R
are
therefore known once we have computed the finite set
\Pi(H)

of primes modulo which
H
and
\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
are
not
congruent.

In
[
8
,
9
]
we gave practical algorithms
to compute
\Pi(H)
for input dense
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Q})
:
a computational analog of strong approximation.
A hallmark of our methods is that they transfer
much of the computing to matrix groups
over finite fields. In this regard, the
GAP
package

[
24
]
, which realizes a categorization due to
Aschbacher
[
2
]
of maximal subgroups of classical
groups over finite fields, is a major component of our
implementations.

Strong approximation not only ensures that
we can compute the congruence quotients of a dense group
H
;
it implies that
H
is well approximated by its congruence
quotients. This affords a framework for examining the structure
of
H
. In
[
10
]
we developed algorithms that accept
dense
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
and
\Pi(H)
as input and compute the
arithmetic closure of
H
, i.e., the intersection of all
finite-index subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
containing
H
. These
algorithms facilitated the solution of problems for which no
methods were previously available (e.g.,
see
[
10
, Section 4]
).

Let
\mathbb{P}
be an algebraic number field. Our primary goal in this
paper is to extend the techniques of
[
8
,
9
]
to handle
finitely generated dense subgroups
H
of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{P})
.
Now
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
for
R\subseteq\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]=\{a/\mu^{i}\,|\,a\in\mathcal{O},i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
where

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}
is the ring of integers of
\mathbb{P}

and
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu>0
. Specifically, we obtain practical
algorithms to describe the set of congruence quotients of
H

modulo all maximal ideals
I
of
R
. As with the algorithms over

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}
, our approach is based on reduction to computing in

\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
. So we continue to rely on Aschbacher’s categorization
of the maximal subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
,
p
prime; see

[
3
]
for an exhaustive treatment. Suppose that
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)

and the congruence image
\widetilde{H}
of
H
modulo a maximal ideal

I
of
R
are different. Then
\widetilde{H}
lies in a maximal
subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
belonging to some Aschbacher class
\mathscr{C}_{i}
,

1\leq i\leq 9
. The idea is to say, for each
i
, precisely when
there can be a maximal subgroup in
\mathscr{C}_{i}
containing a congruence
image of
H
.

We emphasize that the situation for groups over
\mathbb{P}

differs markedly from that for groups over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Firstly, if
\mathbb{P}\neq\mathbb{Q}
then the image
field
R/I
is not necessarily of prime size, and
classes of maximal subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)

that are not present in
\mathrm{SL}(n,p)
emerge.
Another issue is that there might be infinitely
many maximal ideals
I
of
R\subseteq\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]

such that dense
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
does not surject onto

\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
modulo
I
.
However, under certain conditions,
H

will
surject onto

\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
modulo all but finitely many maximal ideals
I
. We
give algorithms to decide whether
H
satisfies those conditions;
if it does, then we compute the (finite) set of maximal ideals

I\subset R
such that
H
does not surject onto
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
.

Some of our procedures are valid for any degree

n
. Others are restricted to prime
n
, taking advantage of
the simplification of linear group structure in prime degree.
At the same time this suffices to cover several important
applications; cf.
[
8
, Section 4]
and see below.
Our approach for arbitrary
n
entails computing with adjoint
representations, and is dealt with in
[
7
]
.

We are motivated by applications—e.g., in algebraic number
theory and geometry—that feature linear groups over number
fields other than
\mathbb{Q}
, especially in small degrees. As a
sample application of our algorithms, we consider subgroups
H
of
Bianchi groups; for these
n=2
and

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}\hskip 0.75pt)
, where
d\in\mathbb{Z}
is positive
and square-free. If
H
is dense then we compute
\Pi(H)
and
test whether
H
has finite index. Subsequently we decide
whether each finite-index
H
is a congruence subgroup, i.e., contains
the full kernel of a congruence homomorphism defined on

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}})
. This enables us to classify
all subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[\omega])
of index up to
30
, for
a primitive cube root of unity
\omega
, and find the congruence subgroups
among them. We repeat the latter exercise to classify all normal
subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}[\omega])
of index up to
20000
. Our
algorithms are the first to be developed for solving such problems.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we explain how to compute congruence homomorphisms defined on
finitely generated subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{P})
. In Section
3

we determine when a congruence image of
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{P})

is in a subgroup from each Aschbacher class.
In Section
4
we use a characterization
of dense
H
in terms of its congruence images to design an algorithm
for computing congruence quotients of
H
modulo maximal ideals
of an ambient coefficient ring. Finally, in
Section
5
we discuss computer experiments with
subgroups of Bianchi groups performed using a
GAP
implementation of
the algorithms, that demonstrate the effectiveness
and applicability of our methods.

2.
Constructing congruence homomorphisms

We now outline how to construct congruence homomorphisms over
\mathbb{P}
.
These reduce modulo (maximal) ideals of the ground domain
R
, so that
much of our computing is with matrix groups over finite fields.
Some of the material of this section may be found also in

[
11
, Section 3.2]
.

There exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}
such that

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}[\alpha]=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)

[
20
, p. 81]
.
Let
f(\mathrm{x})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{x}]
be the minimal polynomial
of
\alpha
; so
f(\mathrm{x})
has degree
m:=|\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{Q}|
.

As noted, if
H=\langle X\rangle
for finite
X\subseteq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{P})
,
then
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R_{H})
where
R_{H}
is a finitely generated subring
of
\mathbb{P}
. We can take
R_{H}
to be generated by the entries of the
elements of
X\cup X^{-1}
. Hence we assume that
R_{H}\subseteq\allowbreak\mathbb{Z}[\alpha,1/\mu]\subseteq\allowbreak R:=\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]
where
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mu>0
.
Although
R
is a finitely generated Dedekind domain,

R_{H}
need not be Dedekind.

For an ideal
I
of
R
, the natural epimorphism

R\rightarrow R/I
extends entrywise to
\mathrm{Mat}(n,R)
.
We denote both of these maps by
\varphi_{I}
; they are

congruence homomorphisms
(modulo
I
).
Let
t_{i,j}(a)
for
i\neq j
be the
element of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
with
1
s down its main diagonal,
a\in R

in position
(i,j)
, and zeros elsewhere.
For any field
\mathbb{F}
,
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})=\langle t_{i,j}(a):a\in\mathbb{F}\hskip 0.5pt\rangle
.
Thus, if
I
is a maximal ideal of
R
then
\varphi_{I}
maps

\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
onto
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
for some
k\leq m
and prime
p

such that
R/I\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
(
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the field of size
q
).
Each congruence homomorphism
\varphi_{I}
with image over a finite
field restricts to one over
R_{H}
; the intersection of
R_{H}
and
a maximal ideal
I
of
R
is a maximal ideal of
R_{H}
.

For a prime
p
and
g(\mathrm{x})\in\allowbreak\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{x}]
,
let
\bar{g}(\mathrm{x})\in\allowbreak\mathbb{F}_{p}[\mathrm{x}]
be the
polynomial whose coefficients are those of
g(\mathrm{x})
,
reduced modulo
p
. We have

(1)

\bar{f}(\mathrm{x})=\allowbreak\bar{f_{1}}(\mathrm{x})^{e_{1}}\cdots\bar{f_{r}}(\mathrm{x})^{e_{r}},\ e_{i}\geq 1,

where
\bar{f_{j}}(\mathrm{x})
is the mod-
p
reduction of the
polynomial
f_{j}(\mathrm{x})\in\allowbreak\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{x}]
that has the same coefficients as

\bar{f_{j}}(\mathrm{x})
but viewed as integers.
The
\bar{f_{j}}(\mathrm{x})
are
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-irreducible. Choose a root

\bar{\alpha_{j}}
of each
\bar{f_{j}}(\mathrm{x})
,
1\leq j\leq r
.
The map
\varphi_{j}
on
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
given by

(2)

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_{i}\alpha^{i}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\bar{c_{i}}\bar{\alpha_{j}}^{\hskip-1.0pti}

is a well-defined ring epimorphism from
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
onto

\mathbb{F}_{p}(\hskip 0.5pt\bar{\alpha_{j}})=\mathbb{F}_{p^{k_{j}}}
, where
k_{j}

is the degree of
f_{j}(\mathrm{x})
. If
p\nmid\mu
then

\varphi_{j}
extends to
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z}[\alpha,1/\mu])
and so is
defined on
\mathrm{SL}(n,R_{H})
.

For
S=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
, let
\Delta(S)
be the determinant
of the
m\times m
matrix with
(i,j)
th entry
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}}(a_{i}a_{j})
, where

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\in\mathbb{Q}
is the trace of
a\in\mathbb{P}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}
. The set

S
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis of
\mathbb{P}
if and only if
\Delta(S)\neq 0

[
20
, p. 342]
.
Let
S
be an integral basis of
\mathbb{P}
; i.e., all
a_{i}
are in
\mathcal{O}
and

S
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}
. Then
\Delta(S)\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
Also
\Delta(S^{\prime})=\Delta(S)
for any other integral basis
S^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{P}
. Thus

\Delta(\mathcal{O}):=\Delta(S)
is the (uniquely defined) discriminant
of
\mathbb{P}

[
20
, pp. 44–45]
.

Let
p
be a rational prime not dividing
\Delta(\alpha)\Delta(\mathcal{O})^{-1}
,
where
\Delta(\alpha):=\Delta(\{1,\alpha,\ldots,\allowbreak\alpha^{m-1}\})
.
By
[
20
, Theorem 3.8.2]
, the ideal
p\mathcal{O}
factorizes as

I_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots I_{r}^{e_{r}}
where
e_{j}
and
r
are as in (
1
), and the

I_{j}
are distinct prime (so maximal) ideals of
\mathcal{O}
. Of course, this
factorization is essentially unique; the
e_{j}
and
I_{j}
are determined by
p
.
Indeed
I_{j}
is generated by
p
and
f_{j}(\alpha)
. Thus, for each
p
we get
r

congruence homomorphisms
\varphi_{I_{j}}\colon\allowbreak\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathcal{O})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k_{j}})
such that
\varphi_{I_{j}}

on
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z}[\alpha])
is equal to
\varphi_{j}
as defined by (
2
).

Each non-zero element
a
of the Dedekind domain
R

lies in just finitely many maximal ideals of
R
.
The set of such ideals will be denoted by
\pi(a)
.
Our algorithms return sets of maximal ideals of
R
,
each ideal being given by at most two generators. When
R

is a PID (principal ideal domain),
each output ideal is designated by a single generator.

3.
Excluding Aschbacher classes

Let
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
where
R=\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]
,
and let
I
be a maximal ideal of
R
such that

R/I\cong\allowbreak\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
. By default
H
is given
by a finite generating set. If
\varphi_{I}(H)\neq\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})

then
\varphi_{I}(H)
is contained in a maximal subgroup of

\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
belonging to at least one of the Aschbacher
classes
\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
1\leq\allowbreak i\leq\allowbreak 9
,
for
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
.
We adopt the definitions of the
\mathscr{C}_{i}
from
[
3
]

(where
\mathscr{C}_{9}
is denoted by
\mathscr{S}
), and call
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})

a
\mathscr{C}_{i}
-group
if
G\leq K
for a maximal subgroup
K
of

\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
in
\mathscr{C}_{i}
. Since an irreducible but not absolutely
irreducible subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
is a
\mathscr{C}_{3}
-group, we may
assume that
\mathscr{C}_{2},\mathscr{C}_{5},\mathscr{C}_{6},\mathscr{C}_{8},\mathscr{C}_{9}
consist
of absolutely irreducible groups.

In this section we define sets
\Pi_{i}(H)
of maximal ideals of
R
,
such that if
\varphi_{I}(H)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{i}
-group for a maximal ideal

I
of
R
, then
I\in\Pi_{i}(H)
. We give algorithms to compute the

\Pi_{i}(H)
. Similar algorithms for
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z}[1/\mu])
appear
in
[
8
]
. These carry over for
\mathbb{P}\neq\mathbb{Q}
with minor
changes; however, the case
i=5
is entirely new.

Unless stated otherwise, the degree
n
in Subsections
3.1
,

3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.6
is arbitrary.
Eventually we restrict to prime
n
, thereby simplifying
the categorization of maximal subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
.
For example, if
n
is prime then
\mathscr{C}_{2}
-groups are monomial,

\mathscr{C}_{3}
-groups are metacyclic, and the orders of

\mathscr{C}_{6}
-groups and
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-groups (not contained in any other

\mathscr{C}_{i}
) are bounded by a function of
n
, independent of
p
.
Additionally
\mathscr{C}_{4}\cup\mathscr{C}_{7}=\emptyset
for prime
n
, so
these classes are not mentioned henceforward.

3.1.
Reducible groups:
\mathscr{C}_{1}

If
H\leq\mathrm{GL}(n,R)
is absolutely irreducible, then
\varphi_{I}(H)

is absolutely irreducible for almost all maximal ideals
I
of
R
.
We find the set
\Pi_{1}(H)
of maximal ideals
I\subset R
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)
is not absolutely irreducible as follows
(cf.
[
8
, Section 2.2]
).

Let
\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{E}
be a finite-degree field extension, and let

X
be a finite subset of
\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathbb{F})
. The procedure

{\tt BasisEnvAlgebra}(X,\mathbb{E})
returns a basis
A

of the enveloping algebra
\langle X\rangle_{\mathbb{E}}
; this is
the smallest subalgebra of the
\mathbb{E}
-algebra
\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathbb{F})

containing
X
. That is,
\langle X\rangle_{\mathbb{E}}
comprises
all
\mathbb{E}
-linear combinations of elements of the monoid generated
by
X
. Denote the
\mathbb{E}
-linear span of
S\subseteq\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathbb{F})

by
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{E}}(S)
. In detail,
\tt BasisEnvAlgebra

initializes
A
at
\{1_{n}\}
, and while there are
a\in A

and
x\in X
such that
ax\notin\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{E}}(A)
, updates

A
to
A\cup\{ax\}
.

If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{P}
and
H=\langle X\rangle
where
X=X^{-1}
, then we
write
{\tt BasisEnvAlgebra}(H,\mathbb{E})
. In this case, the
basis of
\langle X\rangle_{\mathbb{E}}=\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{E}}

returned is a subset of
H
. Further, if
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{P}
then
we shorten the procedure name to
{\tt BasisEnvAlgebra}(H)
.

To compute
\Pi_{1}(H)
, we first run
{\tt BasisEnvAlgebra}(H)
to
get a
\mathbb{P}
-basis
A=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
of

\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}
. If
d=n^{2}
then
H
is absolutely
irreducible; otherwise, we have detected that
H
is not
absolutely irreducible. Let
\Delta(A)
be the determinant of the

n^{2}\times n^{2}
matrix with
(i,j)
th entry
\mathrm{tr}(a_{i}a_{j})
, where

\mathrm{tr}
denotes matrix trace. The determinant of a matrix
whose columns are the
a_{i}
written as column vectors
squares to
\pm\hskip 0.25pt\Delta(A)
. So for any ideal

I\not\in\pi(\Delta(A))
, the
\varphi_{I}(a_{i})
are
R/I
-linearly
independent, and
\varphi_{I}(H)
is absolutely irreducible.
On the other hand,
\varphi_{I}(H)
might be absolutely irreducible for

I\in\pi(\Delta(A))
. This can be checked for every ideal in the
finite set
\pi(\Delta(A))
. Hence we have a procedure

{\tt PrimesForAbsIrreducible}(H)
that returns
\Pi_{1}(H)
for
absolutely irreducible
H
(the ‘primes’ being prime ideals, i.e.,
maximal ideals of
R
).

As noted in
[
8
, Section 2.2]
, if
\varphi_{I}(H)
is
absolutely irreducible for some ideal
I
of
R
then
H
is absolutely
irreducible, and we get a basis of
\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}
by lifting
up to
\mathrm{GL}(n,R)
the elements of a basis of

\langle\varphi_{I}(H)\rangle_{R/I}
.

3.2.
Imprimitive groups:
\mathscr{C}_{2}

Let
n
be prime and
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
be irreducible
(over
\mathbb{P}
). Then either
H
is primitive or it is monomial—i.e.,
conjugate to a group of monomial matrices. The procedure

{\tt PrimesForMonomial}(H)
is defined as follows
(cf.
[
8
, Section 2.3]
). First
g,h\in H
are chosen
such that
[g^{e},h^{e}]\neq 1_{n}
, where
e
is the exponent

\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{Sym}(n))
of the symmetric group

\mathrm{Sym}(n)
. Let
c
be the product of the non-zero entries
of
[g^{e},h^{e}]-1_{n}
.
The procedure tests primitivity of
\varphi_{I}(H)
for all
I\in\pi(c)

such that
\varphi_{I}(H)
is absolutely irreducible, using the

GAP
package
[
24
]
. Since
\varphi_{I}(H)
is non-monomial
for all
I\notin\pi(c)
,
{\tt PrimesForMonomial}(H)
returns
the set
\Pi_{2}(H)
of all maximal ideals
I
of
R
for which

\varphi_{I}(H)
is absolutely irreducible and monomial.

If
H
is not solvable-by-finite, then elements
g,h
as above exist
in
H
(by the Tits alternative) and are
ubiquitous
[
8
, Section 2.3]
. The implemented algorithm finds

g,h
by random selection.

3.3.
Semilinear groups:
\mathscr{C}_{3}

Let
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
be non-solvable and
l
be a positive
integer. We define a procedure
{\tt PrimesForSolvable}(H,l)

to compute all maximal ideals
I\subset R
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)
is a proper solvable subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)

with derived length less than or equal to
l

(cf.
[
8
, Section 2.4]
).
Let
H=H^{(0)}\geq H^{(1)}=[H,H]\geq\cdots
be the
derived series of
H
. The procedure randomly
selects a non-trivial iterated commutator

g\in H^{(l+1)}
, and sets
c
to be the product of all
non-zero entries of
g-1_{n}
. If
I\notin\pi(c)
then

\varphi_{I}(H)
has derived length greater than
l
. With the
aid of
[
24
]
,
{\tt PrimesForSolvable}(H,l)
returns
the subset of
\pi(c)
consisting of all
I
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)\lneq\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
has derived length at
most
l
.

When
n
is prime, subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
in the semilinear
class
\mathscr{C}_{3}
are metacyclic. Hence for prime
n
and
non-solvable
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
, the set
\Pi_{3}(H):={\tt PrimesForSolvable}(H,2)
contains all maximal ideals
I

of
R
such that
\varphi_{I}(H)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{3}
-group in

\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
.

3.4.
Subfield groups:
\mathscr{C}_{5}

By
[
3
, Table 2.8]
,
\mathscr{C}_{5}
in
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})

comprises conjugates of groups of the form

\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{r}).\big[\mathrm{gcd}\big({\textstyle\frac{p^{k}-1}{p^{r}-1}},n\big)\big]\quad\text{where}\ \,k=rs,\ s\ \text{prime}.

Here
A.B
denotes an extension of
A
by
B
, and
[u]
denotes
a group of order
u
. If
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
is a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group,
then
[G,G]\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{r})
for some
r
strictly dividing
k
.
We give conditions under which the congruence image of an
absolutely irreducible subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
is a

\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group.

For a set
M
of square matrices over a commutative ring

S
with
1
,
\mathrm{tr}(M):=\{\mathrm{tr}(x)\mid\allowbreak x\in M\}
.
Note that
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{SL}(n,S))=S
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
and
I
be a maximal ideal of
R

such that
R/I\cong\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
,
k>1
. Suppose that
H

is absolutely irreducible and
I\not\in\pi(\Delta(A))

for some basis
A
of
\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}
. If

\mathrm{tr}(H)\subseteq\widehat{R}
where
\widehat{R}
is a
subring (with
1
) of
R
such that

\varphi_{I}(\widehat{R})\neq\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
, then
\varphi_{I}(H)

is a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group.

Proof.

We have
\varphi_{I}(\widehat{R})=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
,
r<k
, and hence

\mathrm{tr}(\varphi_{I}(H))=\varphi_{I}(\mathrm{tr}(H))\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
.
Also
\varphi_{I}(H)
is absolutely irreducible because

\Delta(A)\notin I
. Then
\varphi_{I}(H)
is conjugate to a
subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{r})
by
[
19
, (9.14)]
.
∎

Now
\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]

is finitely generated as an additive group if and only if
\mu=1
,
so for the rest of the subsection we assume that
R=\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\Pi_{5}(H)
be the set of maximal
ideals
I\subset\mathcal{O}
such that
\varphi_{I}(H)
is a

\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group. We show how to decide whether
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite.

Lemma
3.1
suggests the need for a procedure to compute
the subring
\mathrm{Tr}(H):=\langle\mathrm{tr}(H),1\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
of

\mathcal{O}
generated by
\mathrm{tr}(H)
and
1
. In what follows we
describe such a procedure.
Note that the subring
\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
of

\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathcal{O})
generated by
H
coincides with

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(H)
, the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear span of
H
. Certainly

\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathcal{O})
and so
\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}

have finite additive rank. Thus a
\mathbb{Z}
-spanning set
B\subseteq H

of
\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
can be found by a simple modification
of
{\tt BasisEnvAlgebra}(H,\mathbb{E})
from
Section
3.1
, replacing
\mathbb{E}
by
\mathbb{Z}
.
The while-loop now tests whether
ax\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A)

using an Hermite normal form (HNF) algorithm (see, e.g.,

[
17
, Section 9.3]
) rather than linear algebra over

\mathbb{Q}
.
Since the trace map is additive,

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{tr}(B))=\allowbreak\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathrm{tr}(H))
.
A
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
may then be computed in

\mathcal{O}=\allowbreak\mathrm{Mat}(1,\mathcal{O})

by running
\tt BasisEnvAlgebra
over
\mathbb{Z}
with input
\mathrm{tr}(B)
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
\mathcal{O}
is
complete
if its

\mathbb{Z}
-rank is
m=|\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{Q}|
. So a subring (with
1
) of
\mathcal{O}

is complete if and only if it has field of fractions
\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
is a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule
of
\mathcal{O}
with
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
B
, and let
w
be a change
of basis matrix from
B
to a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
A
of
\mathcal{O}
.
Then
\varphi_{I}(H)
is not a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group for all maximal
ideals
I
of
\mathcal{O}
such that

I\notin\pi(\mathrm{det}(w)^{2}\Delta(A))
.

Proof.

By
[
20
, (2.4.1), p. 43]
,
\Delta(B)=\mathrm{det}(w)^{2}\Delta(A)
; so that if
I\notin\pi(\mathrm{det}(w)^{2}\Delta(A))
then
I\not\in\allowbreak\pi(\Delta(A))\cup\allowbreak\pi(\Delta(B))
.
Hence for any rational prime
p
in
I
,

\varphi_{I}(\mathrm{Tr}([H,H]))
and
\varphi_{I}(\mathcal{O})

both have
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
m
; i.e.,

\varphi_{I}(\mathrm{Tr}([H,H]))=\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
. Consequently

\varphi_{I}(H)
is not a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group: if it were, then we
would have
\varphi_{I}(\mathrm{tr}([H,H]))\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
,

r<m
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

If
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
is a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of

\mathcal{O}
then
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H
be absolutely irreducible. If
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite then

\mathrm{Tr}(H)
is complete.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
is not a complete
\mathbb{Z}
-module.
Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{O}
.
Then for any maximal ideal
I
of
\mathcal{O}
not in

\pi(\Delta(A))
and
p\in I
, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-dimension
of
\varphi_{I}(\mathrm{Tr}(H))
is less than
m
.
Lemma
3.1
implies that
\Pi_{5}(H)
is infinite.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
H
is absolutely irreducible and
\mathrm{tr}(H)
is
contained in a proper subfield of
\mathbb{P}
. Then
\Pi_{5}(H)

is infinite.

Proof.

The field of fractions of
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
is properly contained
in
\mathbb{P}
. Hence
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
is not complete. The result follows
from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
[H,H]
is absolutely irreducible and
\mathrm{tr}(H)
is
not contained in a proper subfield of
\mathbb{P}
. Then

\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
is complete and
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathbb{P})=\langle H\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=\langle[H,H]\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since a basis of

\langle[H,H]\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
may be chosen in
[H,H]
,
the set
\mathrm{tr}([H,H])
is not contained in a proper
subfield of
\mathbb{P}
. Let
\mathbb{F}\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be the field of
fractions of
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
. If
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])

is not complete then
|\mathbb{F}:\mathbb{Q}|<m
, a contradiction.
By Corollary
3.3
, this gives the result.
∎

Since
\langle\mathrm{Tr}(H)\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=\langle B\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
for any

\mathbb{Z}
-spanning set
B
of
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
, we see that
\mathrm{tr}(H)
is
contained in a proper subfield of
\mathbb{P}
if and only if

\langle B\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
has
\mathbb{Q}
-dimension less than
m
. So

\tt BasisEnvAlgebra
can be used to decide the criterion of
Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
.

Assuming that
[H,H]
is absolutely irreducible, the preceding
furnishes a procedure
{\tt Primes}
-
{\tt ForSubfields}(H)

to decide whether
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite, returning
\Pi_{5}(H)

in the event that this set is finite. We begin by testing
whether
\mathrm{tr}(H)
lies in a proper subfield of
\mathbb{P}
. If it does,
then we stop, reporting that
\Pi_{5}(H)
is infinite
(Proposition
3.5
). Otherwise, by Lemma
3.2

we compute
\Delta(B)
for a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
B
of
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
.
Then
\Pi_{5}(H)
is the set of
I\in\pi(\Delta(B))
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group; the latter checked by means of

[
24
]
as usual.

To find a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
B
of
\mathrm{Tr}([H,H])
, we first compute
a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
A\subseteq[H,H]
of
\langle[H,H]\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
This can be done using
\tt BasisEnvAlgebra
as in the
discussion before Lemma
3.2
, and a modification of the
procedure
{\tt BasisAlgebraClosure}(K,X)
defined in

[
12
, Section 3]
.
Here
X
is a generating set of
H
such that
X=X^{-1}
, and

K=[X,X]=\{\hskip 0.5pt[x,y]\mid x,y\in X\}
. Note that
K

is a ‘normal generating set’ for
[H,H]
, i.e.,
[H,H]
is the
normal closure of
K
in
H
.
\tt BasisAlgebraClosure

initializes
A^{\prime}
to
K
, and its while-loop tests whether

a^{x}
is in
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(A^{\prime})
for all
a\in A^{\prime}
and
x\in X
,
updating
A^{\prime}
to
A^{\prime}\cup\{a^{x}\}
if not.
Bases of
\mathbb{Z}
-modules are again computed via an HNF algorithm. The
desired
A
is any
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\langle A^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}

(for
A^{\prime}
at completion of the while-loop). Then
B
is found by
another call on
\tt BasisEnvAlgebra
, with input
\mathrm{tr}(A)
.

3.5.
Bounded orders:
\mathscr{C}_{6}
and
\mathscr{C}_{9}

Let
H
be infinite, and
s
be a positive integer.
We proceed as in
[
8
, Section 2.1]
to determine all
maximal ideals
I
of
R
such that
\varphi_{I}(H)
has elements
of order greater than
s
.
This leads to algorithms that are used to exclude
\mathscr{C}_{6}
-groups
and
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-groups as congruence images.

Since it is finitely generated,
H
must have an element
of infinite order
[
30
, 4.9]
. In practice we often
locate
h\in H
of infinite order quickly just by random
selection. For
1\leq i\leq s
, let
m_{i}
be the product of
the non-zero entries of
h^{i}-1_{n}
, and set
l=m_{1}\cdots m_{s}
.
If
I
is a maximal ideal of
R
not in
\pi(l)
, then

\mathrm{exp}(\varphi_{I}(H))>s
. Using
[
24
]

we can find all
I\in\pi(l)
such that

\mathrm{exp}(\varphi_{I}(H))\geq s
. Hence we get a procedure

{\tt PrimesForOrder}(H,s)
that accepts infinite
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)

and a positive integer
s
, and returns the set of maximal ideals

I
of
R
for which
\mathrm{exp}(\varphi_{I}(H))<s
.

We now consider groups in
\mathscr{C}_{6}
. See
[
3
, Section 2.2.6]

for a definition of this class, which is nonempty only when
n
is a
power of a prime different from
p
.

Lemma 3.7
.

\mathscr{C}_{6}
-groups in
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
have orders bounded by a function of
n

only, independent of
p
and
k
.

Proof.

This is clear from
[
3
, Table 2.9]
.
∎

Let
\Pi_{6}(H)
be the set of maximal ideals
I
of
R
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{6}
-group. By Lemma
3.7
,

\Pi_{6}(H)
is finite. This set is found by running

{\tt PrimesForOrder}(H,s)
, where
s
is a bound on the exponents
of all
\mathscr{C}_{6}
-groups. Values for
s
in small prime degrees
(extracted from
[
3
, Chapter 8]
) are given in
Section
4
.

We turn next to non-geometric
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
.
By definition,
G\in\mathscr{C}_{9}
is almost simple modulo scalars, i.e.,

K\unlhd G/Z\leq\mathrm{Aut}(K)

for some non-abelian simple group
K
such that
C_{G/Z}(K)

is trivial, where
Z
denotes the scalar subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 3.8

(cf.
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
9
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Let
q
be a prime and
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field. The orders of proper
subgroups of
\hskip 0.5pt\mathrm{SL}(q,\mathbb{F})
contained in
\mathscr{C}_{9}
and
not in any other
\mathscr{C}_{i}
are bounded above by a function of
q

that does not vary with
\mathrm{char}\hskip 2.0pt\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a maximal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(q,\mathbb{F})
in
\mathscr{C}_{9}
such that

G\not\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
for
i\neq 9
. Now
U=G^{\infty}
(the intersection
of all terms in the derived series of
G
) is a quasisimple normal subgroup
of
G
. A bound on
|U|
of the kind claimed implies the same
kind of bound on
|G|
, as
G/Z(G)
embeds in
\mathrm{Aut}(U)
.
Set
K=U/Z(U)
.

If
K
is sporadic then of course
|K|
is bounded absolutely.

If
K\cong\mathrm{Alt}(k)
then
k\leq(3q+6)/2
by

[
23
, Proposition 10, p. 333]
, so
|K|
is
bounded by a function of
q
only, independent of
\mathbb{F}
.

Suppose that
K
is a group of Lie type
X_{l}(r^{e})
in cross characteristic

r\neq p:=\mathrm{char}\hskip 2.0pt\mathbb{F}
. Then
[
27
, Table 1]
gives the smallest
degree for which
K
has a faithful projective representation over a
field of characteristic
p
. These degree minima increase with
the Lie rank
l
and
r^{e}
, but are independent of
p
. Hence, for each
q

and all
\mathbb{F}
, there are only finitely many possibilities for
|K|
.

This leaves the option that
K=X_{l}(p^{e})
is a group of Lie type in defining
characteristic
p
. Existence of the required function for such
K
is
the main result of
[
14
]
,
which crucially relies on the degree
q
being prime—unlike
the previous cases—and
G
lying solely in
\mathscr{C}_{9}
.
(Note also that we cannot use our proof of

[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
here, as
p
could divide
|K|
, and so

K
need not lift to characteristic zero.)
∎

More explicitly, Theorem
3.8
can be verified up to large
values of
q
using data
available in
[
21
,
22
]
. For
q\leq 11
, the theorem is
also evident by inspection of relevant tables in
[
3
]
;
this is more than enough for our experiments.

The order bound
given by Theorem
3.8
does not vary
with
p
. However, it can depend on
k
(see the example for
q=71
in

[
14
, Section 7]
).
In our context
k\leq|\mathbb{P}:\mathbb{Q}|
will be fixed.
We reiterate that the prime degree hypothesis
is indispensible; e.g., there are subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(6,p^{k})

that are central extensions of
\mathrm{SL}(3,p^{k})
, which belong to

\mathscr{C}_{9}
and to no other

\mathscr{C}_{i}

[
3
, p. 389]
.

Let
\Pi_{9}(H)
be the set of maximal ideals
I\subset R

such that
\varphi_{I}(H)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-group.
Then
\Pi_{9}(H)
is finite by Theorem
3.8
.
We compute
\Pi_{9}(H)
using
{\tt PrimesForOrder}(H,s)
,
where
s
bounds the exponents of
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-groups.
Values of
s
for
n\leq 11
are
stated in Section
4
.

3.6.
Isometry groups:
\mathscr{C}_{8}

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with an automorphism
\tau
of order
at most
2
. We will call
G\leq\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})
an isometry group
(over
\mathbb{F}
) if
G
preserves a non-degenerate
\tau
-sesquilinear
form
\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
. So every subgroup of

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{F})=\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{F})
is an isometry group.

Lemma 3.9

(Cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
)
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})
.

(i)

If
G
is an isometry group then
\mathrm{tr}(g)=\mathrm{tr}(\tau(g)^{-1})

for all
g\in G
.

(ii)

If
G
is absolutely irreducible and
\mathrm{tr}(g)=\mathrm{tr}(\tau(g)^{-1})

for all
g\in G
, then
G
is an isometry group.

Proof.

(i) Let
\Omega
be a matrix of a form preserved by
G
; i.e.,

g^{\top}\Omega\tau(g)=\Omega
for all
g\in G
. Non-degeneracy of
the form implies that
\Omega
is invertible. Thus

\Omega^{-1}g^{\top}\Omega=\tau(g)^{-1}
, and (i) follows.

(ii) In this case, the identity and contragredient representations
of
G
have equal characters. Since
G
is absolutely irreducible,
these representations are equivalent:
g=\Omega^{-1}(\tau(g)^{\top})^{-1}\Omega
for some
\Omega\in\allowbreak\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})

and all
g\in G
. Hence
G
preserves the form with matrix
\Omega
.
∎

Set
\tau=1
. We define a procedure
{\tt PrimesForIsometry}(H)

that accepts absolutely irreducible
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
and
searches for
h\in H
such that
c:=\mathrm{tr}(h)-\mathrm{tr}(h^{-1})\neq 0
.
If this search succeeds, then using
[
24
]
the procedure
returns the set
\Pi_{8}(H)
of maximal ideals
I
of
R
such that

I\in\pi(c)
and
\varphi_{I}(H)
preserves an alternating or
symmetric bilinear form over
R/I
.
Suppose that
H
does not preserve an alternating or symmetric
form; so Lemma
3.9
(ii) guarantees that
h
as required
exists. By Lemma
3.9
(i),
\varphi_{I}(H)
for any maximal
ideal
I\subset R
preserves an alternating or symmetric form if and
only if
I\in\Pi_{8}(H)
.

Recall that
g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{F})
is a
similarity
of a
sesquilinear form
f\colon\mathbb{F}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}

if there exists
a\in\allowbreak\mathbb{F}^{\times}
such
that
f(gu,gv)=af(u,v)
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
. We will
need to exclude congruence images of
H
that are in a
similarity group. To achieve this, for input
H
such that

[H,H]
is absolutely irreducible, we modify the initial step
of
{\tt PrimesForIsometry}(H)
: now we search for
h\in[H,H]

such that
\mathrm{tr}(h)-\mathrm{tr}(h^{-1})\neq 0
(e.g., commutators

[h_{1},h_{2}]
,
h_{i}\in H
could be tried as candidates for
h
).
The final step is to check whether
\varphi_{I}(H)

lies in a similarity group of a symmetric or alternating bilinear
form. We name the resultant procedure

{\tt PrimesForSimilarity}(H)
.

Lemma 3.10

(Cf.
[
8
, Lemma 2.10]
)
.

Let
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
and suppose that
[H,H]
is absolutely
irreducible and does not preserve an alternating or symmetric
bilinear form over
\mathbb{P}
. Then
\varphi_{I}(H)
is not
contained in a similarity group of an alternating or symmetric
form over
R/I
for almost all maximal
ideals
I
of
R
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
3.9
and the definition of

\tt PrimesForSimilarity
.
∎

The class
\mathscr{C}_{8}
in
\mathrm{SL}(n,p^{k})
comprises similarity
groups of classical sesquilinear forms over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
for

r\hskip 0.5pt|\hskip 0.5ptk
.
We split
\mathscr{C}_{8}
into the union of two subsets,
\mathscr{C}_{8}^{(1)}

(orthogonal and symplectic) and
\mathscr{C}_{8}^{(2)}
(unitary).
Likewise, we re-define
\Pi_{8}(H)=\Pi_{8}^{(1)}(H)\cup\Pi_{8}^{(2)}(H)
,
where
\Pi_{8}^{(1)}(H)={\tt PrimesFor}
-
{\tt Similarity}(H)
and

\Pi_{8}^{(2)}(H)
is the set of all maximal ideals
I
of
R
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)
is contained in a similarity group of an Hermitian form
over
R/I
.

We recap the procedures of this section. Let

If

of

.
In these cases

,

{\tt PrimesForMonomial}
,

, and

of maximal ideals

such
\varphi_{I}(H)

Suppose that

is absolutely irreducible.

{\tt PrimesForSimilarity}
returns the set

4.
Computing congruence quotients of dense subgroups

In this section we give an algorithm to find the
congruence images of a finitely generated dense group

H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
modulo all maximal ideals of
R=\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]
.
This will leave only a finite set
of ideals
\Pi(H)
for which the congruence images
should be computed.
In particular, we formulate conditions under which
H

surjects onto
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
when
I\notin\Pi(H)
.
We start by establishing various
consequences of density
that provide more background for the algorithms.

Recall that the adjoint module for
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
is the

\mathbb{F}
-vector space

\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{F})=\{x\in\mathrm{Mat}(n,\mathbb{F})\,|\,\mathrm{tr}(x)=0\}

of dimension
n^{2}-1
on which
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
acts by conjugation.
This action gives rise to the adjoint representation

\mathrm{Ad}\colon\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(n^{2}-1,\mathbb{F})
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
26
, 9.1]
)
.

A subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
is dense if and only
if
G
is infinite and
\mathrm{Ad}(G)
is absolutely irreducible.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
be dense. Then
G
is primitive (irreducible) over
\mathbb{C}
,
non-solvable, and does not preserve a non-degenerate alternating
or symmetric bilinear form over
\mathbb{C}
if
n>2
.

Proof.

(Cf. the proof of
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
.)
By Proposition
4.1
,
G
is infinite and

\mathrm{Ad}(G)\leq\mathrm{GL}(n^{2}-1,\mathbb{C})
is irreducible.

Suppose that
G
is reducible.
Then
G
is conjugate to a block upper triangular group with main diagonal

(G_{1},G_{2})
where
G_{i}
has degree
n_{i}<n
. It is easily seen that
\mathrm{Ad}(G)

leaves invariant the proper non-zero subspace of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})

consisting of all block upper triangular matrices with main diagonal

(0_{n_{1}},0_{n_{2}})
.
This contradiction shows that
G
is absolutely irreducible.

If
G
is imprimitive over
\mathbb{C}
, then
G\leq K:=\allowbreak\mathrm{GL}(a,\mathbb{C})\hskip 1.0pt\wr\hskip 1.0pt\mathrm{Sym}(b)
up to conjugacy, for some

a,\allowbreak b
such that
b>1
and
ab=n
.
Let
U
be the subspace of
\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})

spanned by all block diagonal matrices with zero trace
whose blocks are in
\{\pm 1_{a},0_{a}\}
. Clearly
U
has
dimension
b-1
and is invariant under conjugation by

K
. Hence
G
must be primitive.

By
[
30
, 3.4]
(a result of Zassenhaus), a
solvable primitive subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
is
central-by-finite. Thus, because scalar subgroups of

\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
are finite,
G
cannot be solvable.

Finally, suppose that
G
preserves a non-degenerate
symmetric or alternating bilinear form over
\mathbb{C}
, with
Gramian
\Phi
. Then

\mathrm{Ad}(G)
leaves invariant the adjoint module

L=\allowbreak\{x\in\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})\,|\,\allowbreak x^{\top}\Phi+\Phi x=0_{n}\}
.
However,
L
is a proper non-zero subspace of

\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
if
n>2
.
∎

Parts of Proposition
4.2
also follow from basic
facts about the Zariski topology on
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Then
G
is dense in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})

if and only if
[G,G]
is dense.

Proof.

Denote the Zariski closure of
K\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
in
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})

by
\overline{K}
. It is not difficult to prove that

[\hskip 1.25pt\overline{G},\overline{G}\hskip 1.25pt]\subseteq\overline{[G,G]}
; see e.g.,
[
30
, 5.10]
.
∎

Strong approximation implies Lemma
4.3

when
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Q})
and
G
is finitely generated.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
q
be a prime. If
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(q,R)
is infinite,
solvable-by-finite, and primitive over
\mathbb{C}
, then
H
is
solvable.

Proof.

The proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
is valid for subgroups
of
\mathrm{SL}(q,R)
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Since the Zariski closure of a solvable subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})

is a solvable subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})

[
30
, 5.11]
,
dense
G\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
is not solvable-by-finite.

For each maximal ideal
I
of
R
, let
\mathcal{X}_{n}(R/I)
be the
union of
\{\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)\}
and the set of all proper subgroups
G

of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
such that
G
is a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group or

\mathscr{C}_{8}^{(2)}
-group, and not a
\mathscr{C}_{8}^{(1)}
-group nor a

\mathscr{C}_{i}
-group for
i=1,2,3,6,9
. We denote by
\Pi(H)
the set
of maximal ideals
I
of
R
such that

\varphi_{I}(H)\not\in\mathcal{X}_{n}(R/I)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
q
be a prime and
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of

\mathrm{SL}(q,R)
dense in
\mathrm{SL}(q,\mathbb{C})
. Then
\Pi(H)
is finite.

Proof.

Certainly
\Pi(H)
is contained in the union of
\Pi_{8}^{(1)}(H)

and the
\Pi_{i}(H)
,
i=1,2,3,6,9
. We apply Proposition
4.2

throughout. Subsections
3.1
–
3.3
and
Lemma
4.4
(or Remark
4.5
)
imply that the
\Pi_{i}(H)
for
i=1,2,3
, are finite.
By Lemma
3.7
and Theorem
3.8
,
\Pi_{6}(H)\cup\Pi_{9}(H)
is
finite. Also
\Pi_{8}^{(1)}(H)
is finite by Lemmas
3.10

and
4.3
.
∎

Theorem
4.6
and its proof give us a procedure

{\tt PrimesForDense}(H)
that computes
\Pi(H)
for finitely
generated dense
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(q,R)
. It combines outputs of the
following procedures from Section
3
(summarized in
Subsection
3.7
):

•

{\tt PrimesForAbsIrreducible}(H)
;

•

{\tt PrimesForMonomial}(H)
;

•

{\tt PrimesForSolvable}(H,2)
;

•

{\tt PrimesForSimilarity}(H)
,
n>2
only;

•

{\tt PrimesForOrder}(H,s)
, where
s
is a bound on the
exponent of groups in
\mathscr{C}_{6}\cup\mathscr{C}_{9}
.

All
I
from the above output such that
\varphi_{I}(H)

is a
\mathscr{C}_{5}
-group or
\mathscr{C}_{8}^{(2)}
-group are
deleted, and the procedure returns the surviving ideals of
R
.
Note that if
q=2,3,\allowbreak 5,\allowbreak 7,11
then we
can take
s
to be
10,21,60,84,253
, respectively, by

[
8
, Remark 3.3]
; these values were obtained using
GAP

and tables in
[
3
, Chapter 8]
.

As we know, in contrast to strong approximation for finitely generated
dense subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Q})
, if
R\not\subseteq\mathbb{Q}

then there could be infinitely many maximal ideals
I

of
R
such that
\varphi_{I}(H)\neq\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
. In other
words,
\Pi_{5}(H)
or
\Pi_{8}^{(2)}(H)
could be infinite (examples
of the latter eventuality include certain

H\leq\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{Z}[\hskip 1.0pt\mathrm{i}\hskip 1.0pt])

preserving an Hermitian form; cf.
[
4
, Section 5]
).
However, by Lemma
4.3
, Subsection
3.4
, and
the above,
at least when
R=\mathcal{O}
and
\Pi_{8}^{(2)}=\emptyset
, and
we have found that
\Pi_{5}(H)
is finite, we can compute the full
set of maximal ideals
I
such that
\varphi_{I}(H)\neq\mathrm{SL}(q,R/I)
.

5.
Experiments

Let
R=\mathcal{O}[1/\mu]
and
q
be a prime.
We investigate a given finitely generated dense subgroup

H
of
\Gamma:=\allowbreak\mathrm{SL}(q,R)
by running

{\tt PrimesForDense}(H)
to compute
\Pi(H)
. Then we find

\varphi_{I}(H)
for each
I\in\Pi(H)
. Since
\varphi_{I}(H)\neq\mathrm{SL}(n,R/I)
might be a
\mathscr{C}_{i}
-group for several
i
, we do
not say what these classes are, but rather state the isomorphism type
of
\varphi_{I}(H)
.

We can learn more about
H
. For example, sometimes we are
able to decide whether
H
is thin, or whether it is a congruence
subgroup. Here, the dense group
H
is
thin
if
|{\Gamma}:H|

is infinite. Also, a (finite-index) subgroup of
{\Gamma}
is a

congruence subgroup
if it contains a

principal congruence subgroup
(PCS), i.e., the kernel of

\varphi_{I}
on
{\Gamma}
for some ideal
I
of
R
. We call
this ideal the
level
of the PCS.

Experiments were conducted using our
GAP

[
15
]

implementation of the algorithms. We restrict to
q=2

only, which abbreviates the list of subprocedures involved
(practicality of the algorithms is not substantially
affected by increasing the degree; cf. the algorithms of
[
8
]
,
over
\mathbb{Q}
).
For if
q=2
then groups in
\mathscr{C}_{1}\cup\mathscr{C}_{2}
are
solvable of derived length at most
4
, and
\mathscr{C}_{8}=\allowbreak\emptyset
(cf.
[
8
, Remarks 2.8, 3.4]
).
Thus
{\tt PrimesForDense}(H)
in this section combines

{\tt PrimesFor}
-
{\tt Solvable}(H,4)
and
{\tt PrimesForOrder}(H,10)

only.
Observe too that
H\leq\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
is dense if and only if it is
not solvable-by-finite. Hence the main algorithm of
[
12
]
tests
density of
H
.

Machinery to compute with groups over number fields
\mathbb{P}
was developed
along the lines of Section
2
. For experiments
in this paper and
[
7
]
,

\mathbb{P}
is contained in a cyclotomic field, and current

GAP
functionality allows us to perform the necessary algebraic number
theory calculations. No bottlenecks were encountered.
To determine
\pi(a)
given
a\in\mathcal{O}
, we find the norm

\mathrm{N}_{\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}}(a)\in\allowbreak\mathbb{Z}
, then factorize it over
\mathbb{Z}

as a product of powers of primes
p
. Factorizing each
p
over

\mathcal{O}
is standard (Section
2
), and for quadratic
extensions this is simpler still
[
20
, Proposition 3.8.3]
.

We show how our algorithms can be used to answer questions about the
subgroup structure of classical groups over rings. Actually all input groups
are subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{d})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{d}
denotes the
ring of integers of
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}\hskip 0.75pt)
for some square-free
positive
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. The projective group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{d})
is
a
Bianchi group

[
13
]
.

See
https://github.com/hulpke/arithmetic
for the
GAP

code used.

5.1.
Thinness testing

We start with a demonstration of the main algorithm. Then we test thinness
of subgroups of
\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
when the congruence subgroup property (CSP) does
not hold; i.e.,
\mathrm{SL}(n,R)
has finite-index subgroups that are not
congruence subgroups. By results of Serre
[
28
]
, the CSP does
not hold in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{d})
.

Let
{\Gamma}=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{7})
and

H=\big\langle\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
a&1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\big\rangle\quad\text{where}\ \,a=\frac{37-\sqrt{-7}}{2},\,b=2-\sqrt{-7}.

We have
R_{H}=\mathcal{O}_{7}=\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}]
.
The ring
\mathrm{Tr}(H)
is
generated by
\big\{2,\frac{71-39\sqrt{-7}}{2}\big\}
, and

\mathrm{Tr}(H)
has index

in

\mathcal{O}_{7}

is finite.

and

to compute

, now defined to be the set of all maximal
ideals

such that
\varphi_{I}(H)\neq\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{7}/I)
. Then for each
I\in\Pi(H)
we

5.2.
Identifying congruence subgroups

Let
{\Gamma}=\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{3})
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{3}

is a PID, and
\mathcal{O}_{3}=\allowbreak\mathbb{Z}[\omega]

where
\omega
is the primitive cube root
of unity
\frac{-1+\sqrt{-3}}{2}
. In this subsection we
identify congruence subgroups among the finite-index
subgroups of
{\Gamma}
.

The group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{3})
differs essentially from
other Bianchi groups. Indeed,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{3})

has Serre’s property FA, hence is not a free product with
amalgamation nor an HNN extension
[
1
, Corollary 1]
,
and its abelianization is finite
[
1
, p. 2939]
.
For
d\neq 3
,
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{d})
is a free product with
amalgamation
[
13
, Theorem 6.3.1, p. 162]
. Furthermore,

\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{3})
is profinitely rigid; the proof relies
on strong approximation
[
5
, Section 6.2]
.

By
[
29
, Theorem 6.1]
,
\Gamma
has presentation

\langle\hskip 0.75ptt,l,a\,|\,l^{3},a^{4},lala^{-1},a^{2}ta^{-2}t^{-1},(t^{-1}a)^{3},(tl^{-1})^{3},(tl)^{3}\hskip 0.75pt\rangle

(with redundant generators eliminated), where

t=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad l=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{2}&0\\
0&\omega\end{bmatrix},\quad a=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&\ \ 0\end{bmatrix}.

We test whether finite-index subgroups of
{\Gamma}
are congruence
subgroups. A low-index subgroup computation (see

[
17
, Section 5.4]
) determines all
126
proper
subgroups of
{\Gamma}
up to conjugacy that have index at most
30
.
Eight of these, labeled
L_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 8
in
Table
2
, are not congruence subgroups. This is
because the image of
{\Gamma}
under the permutation representation
arising from the action by
{\Gamma}
on cosets of each
L_{i}
is an
alternating group
\mathrm{A}_{r}
for
r\geq 22
. Any PCS in
L_{i}

is contained in the kernel of the coset action.
By the Chinese remainder theorem, we may assume
that
L_{i}
contains a PCS of prime-power level, say
I^{j}

where
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{3}
is maximal. Since the kernel
of reduction modulo
I
on
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathcal{O}_{3}/I^{j})
is
solvable,
\mathrm{A}_{r}
is then a quotient of some

\mathrm{SL}(2,p^{k})
. But this is impossible (see, e.g.,

[
