The role of organic compounds in modifying functional relationships and morphophysiological traits of wheat under water stress: a correlation network analysis
Authors: Seyed Karim Irani Takleh, Morteza Barmaki, Ali Omrani, Seyed Habib Shojaei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWheat (Triticum aestivum L.), playing a significant role in providing food security and agricultural economy, is one of the most important cereals in the world. The present experiment aimed to determine the optimal levels of organic compounds (fulvic acid, humic acid, and amino acids) to promote sustainable wheat (cv. Taktaz) cultivation in dry and semi-dry regions while reducing dependency on irrigation water. The experiment was conducted as a split-factorial based on a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications during two cropping years, 2023–2025. Results indicated that three-way interactions were statistically significant at the 0.01 level for all traits except number of fertile spikes and number of grains per spike. According to the polygon view of the traits, applying high amounts of fulvic acid as well as average amounts of the amino acid can have positive effects on controlling stress. In addition, increasing the concentration of humic acid can compensate for low concentrations of compounds like fulvic acid and amino acids. According to the treatment stability diagram, treatments 3 (humic acid 12 L h− 1) and 6 (amino acid 1.5 L h− 1, humic acid 12 L h− 1) under normal irrigation conditions, and 27 (fulvic acid 6 L h− 1, amino acid 3 L h− 1, humic acid 12 L/ha) under drought stress conditions, were selected as desirable treatments. The fact that increasing organic matter, like fulvic acid and amino acids, is helpful in plant metabolism regulation and resistance enhancement against drought stress reveals the effects of compounds included in these treatments on the plant’s resistance to the stress. The treatment with the highest amount of organic compounds (6 L ha− 1 fulvic acid, 3 L ha− 1 amino acid, 12 L ha− 1 humic acid) was identified as the treatment with positive coefficients in relation to the first two components in both years. Under drought stress conditions in both years, grain yield had positive coefficients for the first two principal components. Likewise, flag leaf width had positive effects on the fourth component in both years. Treatments with high fulvic acid and medium humic acid (3 L ha− 1 fulvic acid, 1.5 L ha− 1 amino acid, 12 L ha− 1 humic acid; 6 L ha− 1 fulvic acid; and 6 L ha− 1 fulvic acid, 8 L ha− 1 humic acid) consistently appeared with strong relationships in the correlation network in both years. In general, treatments with moderate fulvic acid and high humic acid can be selected as desirable treatments under both normal and stress conditions. Introduction

Wheat is one of the most important food crops for humanity, cultivated on approximately 225 million hectares of agricultural land worldwide, with a global production of 750 million tons1. Increasing wheat demand for culinary uses is due to its being a rich source of carbohydrates and gluten2. Being the main food for the Asian population, this plant is a vital cereal product worldwide. Wheat encounters various stresses that restrict its growth, development, and yield. Abiotic stresses have a significant impact on the growth and yield of agricultural crops. This type of stress encompasses any unfavorable environmental conditions that impede proper plant development and, due to severe and persistent adverse conditions, can induce morphological damage in plants3,4. Water deficiency is considered the main limiting factor for Wheat. Climatic extremes such as droughts, salinity, and elevated temperatures, pose serious challenges to food security and crop productivity5,6, and semi-arid regions are projected to face the greatest intensification of water scarcity under future climate change scenarios7. Drought stress is an abiotic stress and the main limiting factor in the growth and productivity of many agricultural products worldwide. This type of stress has caused the drought-induced productivity loss of agricultural products to reach 34% in 20218. Escalating drought stress intensity is harmful to crop growth and leads to dramatic changes in the morphological characteristics of crops9. Drought stress has many negative influences on crop growth, which can decline the trait yield considerably through changing the crop’s key biochemical and physiological systems10. Plants under drought stress exhibit a reduction in photosynthetic rate, which is associated with impaired growth and increased incidence of early senescence11. Drought is one of the most important environmental factors that inhibits photosynthesis and reduces plant growth and productivity. Crop plants such as wheat are particularly sensitive to soil drought, especially during the reproductive stage, which is extremely dependent on the plant’s water status12. One of the most valuable bio-stimulants that can be externally used to increase crop resistance during drought stress is humic acid. This compound consists of plant or animal residues and can play a significant role in crop growth and development as a hormone-like molecule. Due to its low molecular weight, humic acid is absorbed readily by crops13. Fulvic acid is also one of the important factors in metabolism and positively affects wheat seedling growth14,15. This compound is involved in photosynthesis, biochemical transformations of nitrogen, carbon, and sulfur, as well as synthesis and catabolism of protein amino acids and nucleic acids16. Findings have shown that applying fulvic acid and humic acid in low-irrigation regimes can increase yield and water consumption efficiency in wheat17. Humic acid, by inducing the accumulation of proline and soluble sugars, increases cellular osmotic pressure and maintains leaf relative water content under drought stress18. fulvic acid, owing to its low molecular weight, readily crosses the cell membrane and enhances the uptake of nutrients such as iron and zinc19. Amino acids play various important roles in plants. They are rich sources of nitrogen and can reduce stress intensity in plants20. Drought resistance in plants is enhanced by using amino acids, which minimize the level of unfavorable effects and increase the amount of regulated accumulation of salts21. Recommendations based on the results of an experiment on assessing the effects of foliar application of amino acids on decreasing the effects of drought stress reveal that applying these compounds is a valuable approach to reducing the drought stress and improving crop yield in wheat22.

In a study conducted to investigate the effects of nitrogen and water stress on five emmer wheat landraces compared with durum and bread wheat, emmer wheat exhibited greater stability under water-deficit conditions but showed a negative response to nitrogen fertilizer application23. Another study demonstrated that drought stress leads to reductions in leaf water potential, relative water content, grain yield, and yield components; however, the application of organic compounds such as salicylic acid can mitigate the adverse effects of drought and enhance the studied traits24.

Various research has been done on the effects of humic acid, amino acids, and fulvic acid on yields of different plants, especially wheat, in normal and different irrigation regimes25–27,. However, simultaneous analysis of interactions among these compounds while assessing crop traits in wheat under normal and drought stress conditions provides a novel approach in the cultivation of this crop. The present study aimed to: (1) screening and selecting suitable treatments (amino acid × fulvic acid × humic acid) for wheat cultivation under normal and drought stress conditions; (2) assessing the stability of different treatments in both experimental years; (3) analyzing the effects of treatment × trait; and (4) assessing the relationships among the crop traits.

Materials and methods

Geographical information of the experimental site

The present study was conducted in the research field of the Research Center of Agriculture and Natural Resources in Moghan, Ardabil, Iran, in 2023–2025. This region has a semiarid climate with hot summers and cold winters. According to the meteorological reports of Moghan’s weather station, the average annual maximum and minimum temperatures are 35 °C and 8 °C, respectively. So far, absolute maximum temperature and absolute minimum temperatures have been reached 42.5 °C and − 5.16 °C, respectively. The average amount of annual precipitation has been 251 mm, and annual relative humidity has been around 71% on average. The highest precipitation occurs in fall, winter, and early spring. Chemical compounds

Chemical compounds used in this experiment, including humic acid, fulvic acid, and the amino acid, were procured from reputable companies. Fulvic acid was bought from Akin Tarim Company, Turkey. Isabion amino acid and humic acid were supplied by Euro Alga Company. The above-mentioned compounds were analyzed and verified by the laboratory department of the Soil and Water Organization.

Experimental design

This study was conducted as a factorial split-plot based on a randomized complete block design with three replications in two successive crop years. Main plots included normal irrigation regime (N) and irrigation cut-off before flowering onset (D), while foliar application of fulvic acid (at three levels, 3 L ha− 1, 6 L ha− 1 and 0 L ha− 1 as the control treatment), Isabion amino acid (1.5 L ha− 1, 3 L ha−1and control) and humic acid (as seed treatment at a rate of 6 L per 1,000 kg of seed, and as a substance solved in irrigation water at the rate of 8 L ha−1and 12 L ha− 1) were the sub-plots. Seed sowing was recorded in November and crop harvest was recorded in July. Foliar application was applied using a spraying drone and carried out after the end of the cold period and at the tillering stage. the drought stress was also imposed after anthesis and during the grain-filling stage. Prior to the experiment, soil samples taken from 0 to 30 cm depth were sent to an accredited soil laboratory to be analyzed for physical and chemical characteristics. Table 1 reports the results of analyzing the soil samples. The genotype used in this study is one of the newest commercial varieties introduced for the hot and humid climate of Iran, and it is widely cultivated. The commercial variety used is named Tektaz. Wheat seeds of cv. Taktaz (with pedigree: SAUAL/3/MILAN/S87230//BAV92) were disinfected using a suitable fungicide and then planted at a density of 400 seeds per square meter by a grain drill specialized for irrigated cereals. Weed control was done mechanically and, in case of necessity, by applying herbicides, 2,4-D, Granstar and Topik, at dosages recommended by the manufacturer. In order to control pathogens, especially fungal diseases common in Moghan, tilt and folicur fungicides were applied at recommended rates and required times. Finally, yield and agronomic traits were measured after harvest. All stages of planting, field management, and harvesting were carried out mechanized under the supervision of a mechanization specialist. Statistical analysis

Mean values obtained from two cropping years were used for analysis of variance (ANOVA) and mean comparison using the Least Significant Difference (LSD) method. By using the data from both years under normal and stress conditions, graphical analysis was performed for evaluation of (1) the treatments (using a polygon view); (2) yields and trait stability of the treatments; (3) relationships among the traits; and (4) PCA. Principal component analysis (PCA) was employed to reduce the dataset of evaluated variables to their essential features. Principal components represent a limited number of linear combinations of the original variables that maximally account for the total variance across all variables. This method can yield graphical outputs in the form of a biplot or present results in numerical format28. Network analysis, by graphically visualizing correlation relationships among treatments or traits, identifies complex interaction patterns and key nodes that play a central role in these associations29. Results

ANOVA and comparison of means

As the results of ANOVA on mean data of both years under normal irrigation conditions indicated, the effect of fulvic acid on plant height, flag leaf length, flag leaf width, grain yield, biological yield, number of fertile spikes, and harvest index was significant at the 0.01 and 0.05 levels. The amino acid had a significant effect on all traits except flag leaf length, thousand grain weight, and biological yield at the 0.01 and 0.05 levels. The effect of humic acid on peduncle length, flag leaf width, thousand grain weight, grain yield, biological yield, number of fertile spikes, and harvest index was statistically significant at the 0.01 and 0.05 levels as well. The interaction effect between fulvic acid and the amino acid was significant for all traits except thousand grain weight. The interaction effect of fulvic acid × humic acid was significant for peduncle length, flag leaf length, flag leaf width, spike length, grain yield, number of fertile spikes, and harvest index. The interaction effect of amino acid × humic acid was significant for all traits except the number of fertile spikes. The three-way interaction effect was significant for all traits except thousand grain weight and number of fertile spikes. The highest value for the coefficient of variation percentage (C.V.%) was recorded for thousand grain weight (27.3), while the least value of that belonged to harvest index (6.3). Based on ANOVA on mean data of both years under stress conditions, fulvic acid affected all traits except spike length significantly at the 0.01 and 0.05 levels. The amino acid had a significant effect on plant height, flag leaf length, flag leaf width, spike length, thousand grain weight, grain yield, number of grains per spike, and harvest index at 0.01 and 0.05 levels. The effect of humic acid was statistically significant at 0.01 and 0.05 levels on plant height, peduncle length, spike length, thousand grain weight, grain yield, biological yield, and number of fertile spikes. Moreover, significant effects were revealed for the interaction effect of fulvic acid × amino acid on all traits, the interaction of fulvic acid × humic acid on plant height, flag leaf length, flag leaf width, spike length, grain yield, number of grains per spike, and harvest index, and the interaction of humic acid × amino acid on all traits. Three-way interaction effect of fulvic acid × amino acid × humic acid was significant for all traits except number of grains per spike. The highest and lowest values of C.V.% were recorded for thousand grain weight (27.3) and peduncle length (7.3), respectively (Table 3).

Network plot of treatments on traits

Figure 7 presents the Network Plot for the means of the data obtained from both cropping years under normal and drought stress conditions (Fig. 7). Based on this diagram, the similarity among treatments in terms of their responses to traits can be assessed by determining the correlation coefficients between traits. In this diagram, a higher Edge Cutoff value indicates stronger connections, whereas a lower Edge Cutoff includes weaker correlations, resulting in a more complex network. Results related to the normal conditions with 50% Edge Cutoff (Fig. 7a) indicated the main similarities regarding the traits. The strongest inter-treatment relationship was recorded for tr15, tr17, tr18, tr19, tr7, and tr20. Treatment 22 showed no correlation with other treatments. At 60% Edge Cutoff (Fig. 7b), tr18 had the highest relationship with other treatments, while at 70% Edge Cutoff (Fig. 7c), tr18, tr19, tr20, tr23, tr27, tr26, and tr5 indicated the highest relationships. As a result, under normal conditions, treatments like tr18, tr19, and tr20 hold greater importance. Discussion

The polyhedral diagram is used to identify the best treatments among the studied traits. This diagram is constructed by connecting the treatments that are farthest from the origin. Consequently, other treatments are positioned inside this polyhedron. Within each section, treatments exhibiting higher performance and desirability for specific traits are delineated by lines (Fig. 2). The polygon diagram has been used by various researchers on different plants, including wheat, maize30–32, and sunflower33. Based on the graph obtained from normal conditions in the first year of the experiment (Fig. 2a), treatments were selected that primarily had moderate levels of amino acid and fulvic acid. Performance of treatments 7 and 10 outweighed that of other treatments in the cases of grain yield, biological yield, and peduncle length. Furthermore, treatments 24 and 21 performed significantly better than others in terms of spike length and flag leaf width, respectively. Finally, treatments 3, 4, and 9 exceeded others in respect of flag leaf length. Under stress conditions, the selected treatments had high concentrations of humic acid and fulvic acid. Number of grains per spike, peduncle length, biological yield, grain yield, and harvest index were the highest in treatments 9, 6, 7, 3, 10, 4, and 15. Rest of the treatments except tr19 performed better compared to others with regard to all traits except flag leaf length, for which treatment 19 outranked others (Fig. 2b). In the second year of the experiment under normal conditions, the selected treatments contained moderate concentrations of organic compounds. Number of grains per spike, peduncle length, biological yield, grain yield, and harvest index showed the highest records for treatments 9, 6, 7, 3, 10, 4, and 15. Other treatments except tr19 presented better outcomes in terms of traits except flag leaf length, on which treatment 19 had the best effect Fig. 2c. Under stress conditions, the selected treatments had the highest concentrations of humic acid and amino acid among the organic elements used. Treatments 27, 1, 6, 9, and 10 had the best effects on biological yield, grain yield, peduncle length, and plant height. Number of grains per spike showed the highest records for tr19 and tr16, while the highest spike length data were recorded for tr22 (Fig. 2d). In the combined study of normal and stress conditions in the first year of the experiment, the positive effect of fulvic acid in selecting the optimal and desirable treatments was evident. Considering the accumulation of the traits and treatments inside this polygon chart, the balanced trend with regard to traits’ reaction to the treatments was reported. Treatment D19 indicated the highest yield efficiency in respect of flag leaf length compared to other treatments (Fig. 2e). Also, in the second year of the experiment, the high concentration of organic compounds, especially fulvic acid, in the desirable treatments indicated the positive effect of these compounds in controlling humdity stress and improving performance in the traits. Moreover, treatment D18 presented better effects on biological yield and grain yield compared to other treatments, and the performance of D27 outweighed that of other treatments regarding flag leaf length. Since the remaining treatments were observed inside the polygon chart, they had a balanced trend along with other traits in terms of yield efficiency (Fig. 2f).

Based on the findings of these charts in different years and normal irrigation conditions, tr22 and tr23 could be selected as suitable for cultivation in the first stage. In the second stage, treatments 5, 8, 13, 14, and 18 could be recommended as desirable.

According to the results, applying high rates of fulvic acid as well as an average dosage of the amino acid can have positive effects on controlling the stress in plants. In addition, increasing the concentration of humic acid can compensate for low concentrations of compounds like fulvic acid and amino acids.

In the stability diagram (Fig. 3) for the first year of the experiment, treatments with high humic acid concentration and no fulvic acid were selected as stable, while treatments with high fulvic acid concentration showed lower stability under normal conditions. (Fig. 3a). Furthermore, treatments 3, 9, 1, 2, 11, 5, 20, and 6 can be selected as treatments with high stability potential against various traits. Generally, according to the findings of the first year, treatments 3 and 9 were selected as the superior ones. In the second year of the experiment, treatments with high humic acid levels and moderate fulvic acid concentrations positively influenced plant vigor in the desirable treatments. Generally, in the second year of the experiment under normal conditions, treatments 5, 10, 6, and 7 were recognized as treatments with high yield potential and a suitable level of stability in respect of all traits (Fig. 3b). In examining the effect of drought stress during the first year of the experiment, the positive impact of increasing fulvic acid concentration in the treatments was evident in identifying the desirable treatments. Treatments 22, 13, 14, 20, 23, 2, 3, 17, 18, 21, and 24 were selected as stable. Treatments 22, 13, and 21 were identified as high-yield and stable in response to the traits (Fig. 3c). Under drought stress conditions, increasing all treatment levels in the experiment had a positive effect on increasing the stability of the treatments. Moreover, treatments 11, 22, 15, 17, 4, 2, 3, and 27 could be identified as stable treatments, and treatments 27 and 11 could be selected as the ones with high yield and stability in response to the traits (Fig. 3d). In examining the effect of drought stress during the first and second years of the experiment (Fig. 3e and d), the positive impact of increasing fulvic acid concentration in the treatments was evident in identifying the desirable treatments. Regarding stability, all treatments except D6, D8, N22, D22, D11, D15, and N15 had satisfying levels of stability based on their proximity to the mean axis in the chart (Fig. 3e). In the second year, treatments except N18, D14, N16, D21, N24, N23, and D18 had acceptable levels of stability. Generally, D27 could be selected as the desirable treatment (Fig. 3f). According to the plotted charts for different conditions in both experimental years, treatments 3 and 6 under normal irrigation conditions and treatment 27 under drought stress conditions were identified as desirable treatments. The fact that increasing organic matter, like fulvic acid and amino acids, can enhance plant resistance against drought stress through regulating the plant metabolism obviously shows the effect of compounds in this treatment on plant resistance.

A study conducted to assess the reduction of drought stress effects by humic acid reported that a dosage increase in humic acid could decline drought stress-induced oxidative effects through the induction of non-enzymatic and enzymatic antioxidants at different and significant levels34. Another study evaluating the effect of humic acid on physiological characteristics under drought stress revealed that humic acid can enhance plant growth under drought stress conditions through improving osmotic regulation, antioxidant capacity, and photosynthesis35. In a study conducted to investigate the effects of organic compounds on wheat under water-deficit conditions, these compounds played a significant role in mitigating drought-induced damage by improving morphological traits and effectively suppressing the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS)36. The improvement in water-use efficiency in response to the application of organic compounds is primarily attributed to increased photosynthesis and reduced transpiration. The use of these compounds enables wheat to utilize water more efficiently under drought stress conditions37.

The principal component analysis (PCA) conducted over two experimental years under both normal and drought stress conditions provided valuable insights into the key traits influencing treatment performance and highlighted the most effective treatments. Under normal irrigation conditions, the first five principal components explained a substantial portion of variance-over 88% in the first year and more than 79% in the second year. This underscores that the measured traits collectively capture the majority of variation in treatment effects. Grain yield and plant height consistently showed positive coefficients for the first two components across both years, indicating their pivotal role in differentiating treatment performance. Traits such as spike length and biological yield also had strong positive influences on the primary components, emphasizing their importance for overall productivity and plant vigor. The evaluation of treatments based on PCA revealed that certain treatments, particularly treatment 27, consistently exhibited positive coefficients across the first two components in both years under normal conditions. This suggests their robustness and favorable performance for essential agronomic traits. Moreover, treatments 2, 25, and 26 demonstrated strong positive effects on the first component, while treatment 4 showed the highest effect on the second component, highlighting their specific contributions to certain trait variations. Under drought stress conditions, the PCA explained over 70% and 66% of total variance in the first year and second year, respectively. Grain yield, biological yield, flag leaf width, harvest index, and peduncle length were among the traits with positive loadings on the main components, reflecting their critical roles in stress adaptation and yield stability. Traits such as peduncle length and thousand grain weight also had notable importance, signifying their relevance in sustaining productivity under drought. Treatments 15, 16, and 13 emerged with positive coefficients for the primary components in both years under stress, suggesting their potential as stress-resilient candidates. Treatment 9 showed the highest positive effect on the first component across both years, reinforcing its suitability for humidity-deficient conditions. The consistency of grain yield’s positive loadings on the first two components across all conditions and years indicates its strong association with the overall performance and stress tolerance of the treatments. Additionally, the recurring positive impact of flag leaf width on the fourth principal component points to its possible role in compensating for stress effects and contributing to physiological efficiency.

Results related to the drought stress conditions in both years showed that grain yield had positive coefficients for the first two main components in both years. Flag leaf width had the highest positive effect on the fourth component in both years. Treatments 15, 16, and 13 had positive coefficients for both first components, and treatment 9 had the highest positive effect on the first component in both years.

Based on the network plot of treatments on traits under normal conditions, treatments like tr18, tr19, and tr20 hold greater importance. On the other hand, under stress conditions, tr14, tr15, tr18, tr19, tr20, tr21, and tr27 showed the highest relationships with other treatments at 50% Edge Cutoff and under stress conditions, tr15, tr19, tr20, and tr21 are considered more desirable with stronger relationships and correlations. Treatments tr15, tr19, and tr20 that have shown up consistently with strong relationships under both normal and stress conditions played a noteworthy role in the correlation network. Treatments like tr22 under normal conditions, as well as tr2 and tr3 under stress conditions, do not have considerable relationships with other treatments. This could be a consequence of their different and weaker performance for the studied traits.

Conclusions and future prospects

The findings of this two-year study under normal and drought-stress conditions highlight the pivotal role of traits such as grain yield, plant height, panicle length, biological yield, harvest index, and flag leaf width in determining treatment performance. Among the evaluated treatments, numbers 27, 15, 19, and 20 demonstrated superior yield and greater stability, making them promising candidates for cultivation under diverse environmental conditions. Moreover, folic acid and humic acid significantly contributed to mitigating drought stress and improving yield performance, emphasizing their importance in enhancing stress tolerance. Network analysis further indicated that treatments with stable trait associations across environments represent the most reliable options for sustainable crop management under variable conditions. This research demonstrates that organic compounds act as physiological modulators, reinforcing and harmonizing the interrelationships among traits under stress, rather than merely inducing an isolated increase in individual traits.
