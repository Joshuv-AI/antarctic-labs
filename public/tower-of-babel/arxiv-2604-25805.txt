Recently, two physically intertwined experimental observations have sharpened this puzzle. First, the pronounced orientation dependence of
T_{c}

\theta

\sim 50

24

Previous theories based on purely electronic monopole-dipole interactions
19
identified the importance of parity mixing and spin-orbit coupling (SOC). However, purely electronic mechanisms lack a soft low-energy boson and therefore remain deep in the weak-coupling Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer regime, making it difficult to account naturally for either the enhanced
T_{c}
scale of KTO or the observed quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
relation. In this Letter, we propose an effective phenomenological Eliashberg framework in which overdamped interfacial PNR fluctuations provide the pairing glue, while a geometrically controlled Rashba vertex governs its angular dependence. Our central thesis is that the same SOC-amplified polar pairing channel can account simultaneously for the strong orientational dependence of superconductivity in KTO and for its much larger
T_{c}
scale relative to STO.

The slow, overdamped lattice dynamics of PNRs provide substantial low-energy spectral weight, allowing the system to access the intermediate-to-strong coupling regime. At the same time, the geometric angle controls the leading-order dynamic Rashba coupling as
\sin(\theta)
, generating an orientation-dependent electron-boson vertex. Within a projected helicity-band description, this interaction reduces to an effective scalar intra-band pairing kernel. Evaluated via both exact numerical Eliashberg solutions and the Allen-Dynes interpolation formula, the resulting non-linear transformation of
\lambda(\theta)\propto\sin^{2}(\theta)
yields the same qualitative quasi-linear dependence within this reduced isotropic model.

Nature of the Soft Bosonic Mode.—
Bulk KTO is a quantum paraelectric where the transverse optical (TO) phonon saturates at
\sim 3
meV
22
. While pre-existing polar nanoregions (PNRs) can form deep within the insulating KTO substrate, their polarizations are randomly oriented without long-range correlation. At the amorphous-LaAlO
3
/KTaO
3
(a-LAO/KTO) interface, however, the confining potential creates a strong built-in electric field. This field explicitly breaks inversion symmetry and aligns the interfacial PNRs, forcing them to form static electric dipoles normal to the surface. This out-of-plane structural distortion has been directly visualized via atomic-resolution scanning transmission electron microscopy
9
, and its collective ordering is corroborated by the emergence of characteristic Raman optical modes and bistable transport properties below
50
K
24
. Superconductivity is therefore strictly confined to the ultra-thin interfacial layer (
d_{sc}\sim 5
–
9
nm) where the 2D electron gas overlaps with these field-aligned polar modes.

As illustrated in the atomic schematic of Fig.

\bm{q}

of these out-of-plane PNR dipoles (
\delta P_{z}

To capture the overdamped nature of these local polar dynamics while preserving causality and rendering the high-frequency ultraviolet (UV) moments of the Eliashberg spectrum finite, we model the dynamical charge susceptibility via a second-order overdamped Brownian oscillator (derived in Supplementary Note 3):

\chi(\bm{q},\omega)=\frac{\chi_{0}\Omega_{B}^{2}}{\Omega_{B}^{2}(1+\xi_{P}^{2}\bm{q}^{2})-\omega^{2}-i\gamma\omega},

(1)

where
\chi_{0}
is the static dielectric susceptibility,
\xi_{P}
is the PNR correlation length, and
\Omega_{B}
is the bare oscillator frequency. The substantial overdamping rate
\gamma

\gamma\gg\Omega_{B}

\Omega_{R}=\Omega_{B}^{2}/\gamma
. At high frequencies, the susceptibility decays as
\omega^{-2}
, rendering the second Eliashberg moment well-defined.

Geometric Tuning and Helicity Projection.—
In bulk KTO, inversion symmetry forbids the linear coupling of even-parity conduction electrons to odd-parity polar modes. At the LAO/KTO interface, the asymmetric confining potential breaks this inversion symmetry. While the exact microscopic description of arbitrary
(hkl)
interfaces is notoriously complex due to highly anisotropic orbital hopping and confinement reordering, the functional form of the leading-order electron-boson coupling is strictly dictated by dynamic point-group selection rules.

At the pristine (100) interface, parity strictly forbids linear dipole coupling between the primary in-plane
d_{yz}
ground state (even parity) and the out-of-plane polar fluctuation (odd parity). The dynamic pairing vertex must therefore vanish. As derived analytically in Supplementary Note 1, cutting the crystal at an angle
\theta
geometrically tilts the bulk
t_{2g}
orbitals out of the plane, breaking this orthogonal symmetry. Consequently, the leading angular dependence of the dynamic coupling vertex scales exactly as the geometric tilt:

g(\theta)=g_{0}\sin(\theta).

(2)

Promoting the static interface asymmetry to a dynamical interaction via the out-of-plane polar displacement field
P_{\hat{n}}(\bm{q})
yields a dynamic Kozii-Fu-type electron-boson interaction
15
:

H_{\text{int}}=g(\theta)\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}P_{\hat{n}}(\bm{q})\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\left[(\bm{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}\right]\psi_{\bm{k}}.

(3)

This geometric tuning is consistent with the recent ARPES observation of orientation-dependent electron-phonon coupling matrix elements
5
.

A fundamental challenge for pairing models is that this interaction is matrix-valued and momentum-dependent. However, as discussed in Supplementary Note 2, the interaction operator shares the form of the static Rashba operator itself. Consequently, in the effective limit where pairing is dominated by intra-band scattering, projection onto the lowest Rashba-split helicity band renders the matrix vertex approximately diagonal. While replacing the exact momentum-selective spinor overlap with an isotropic scalar reduction deliberately discards the ability to resolve the exact mixed-parity gap structure, it efficiently isolates the overall pairing energy scale. This reduces the interaction to an effective scalar intra-band vertex,
\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\propto\pm g(\theta)k_{F}
.

Threshold Bounds and Quasi-Linearity.—
As illustrated schematically in Fig.
2
(b), the emergence of superconductivity requires the geometrically enhanced pairing strength to overcome the repulsive Coulomb pseudopotential (
\lambda>\lambda_{c}\approx\mu^{*}
). A fundamental constraint provided by the experimental data is that the pristine (100) interface (
\theta=0^{\circ}
) remains non-superconducting down to a base temperature of 25 mK. Evaluating the threshold condition
T_{c}\lesssim 25
mK places a practical upper bound on the allowable baseline coupling:
\lambda_{0}\lesssim 0.43
. Our representative baseline
\lambda_{0}\approx 0.25
lies comfortably below this limit and near the effective Coulomb threshold
\lambda_{c}\approx 0.14
. At
\theta=0^{\circ}
, the representative near-threshold parameters yield transition temperatures of order
10^{-6}
–
10^{-5}
K in Allen-Dynes and exact isotropic Eliashberg theory, respectively, both far below the experimental base temperature and therefore consistent with the observed collapse of superconductivity. In the intermediate-to-strong coupling regime (
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
), the non-linear strong-coupling dependence of the Eliashberg/Allen-Dynes mapping partially compensates for the concavity of the
\sin^{2}(\theta)
geometric projection. The combination of these two exponential forms maps the quadratic input into an extended quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
output over the physical domain.

Tunability of the Threshold.—
Importantly, this threshold boundary is tunable. As detailed in Supplementary Note 10, while the exact numerical values of the fitted parameters depend on the assumed Coulomb pseudopotential
\mu^{*}
, the underlying reduced-model mapping—in which intermediate-coupling non-linearity partially straightens the
\sin^{2}(\theta)
input into an extended quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
regime—remains qualitatively robust across physically reasonable values of
\mu^{*}
. Variations in electrostatic boundary conditions modify the interfacial electric field and the PNR correlation length, slightly shifting
\lambda_{0}
relative to
\lambda_{c}
. This turns the threshold boundary into a testable physical parameter controlled by the interfacial environment. Indeed, recent systematic gating and poling experiments demonstrate that non-volatile switching of the out-of-plane ferroelectric polarization directly alters the local electrostatic environment
24
;
9
. This modulates the
z
-directed interfacial potential well, successfully driving macroscopic bistability and
T_{c}
enhancement across the superconducting threshold
24
. This electrostatic control offers a natural explanation for why different interface platforms (e.g., a-LAO capped
2
vs. ionic-liquid gated
20
;
2
) can exhibit slightly different
T_{c}(\theta)
intercepts while preserving a similar overall slope.

Discussion and Conclusion.—
We emphasize that our exact numerical calculations validate the physics of this reduced scalar isotropic model; a complete microscopic description would require incorporating the full multiband
t_{2g}
orbital structure, facet-dependent confinement, and Fermi surface anisotropy. Within this effective framework, however, the central physical message is clear: a geometrically controlled dynamic Rashba vertex can strongly amplify pairing by soft interfacial polar modes.

An important consequence is that the same microscopic ingredient — the large atomic spin-orbit coupling of Ta
5d
orbitals — helps explain both why KTaO
3
interfaces exhibit a much stronger orientational dependence than SrTiO
3
interfaces and why their superconducting transition temperatures are substantially higher. Because the dynamic Rashba vertex
g(\theta)
is parametrically amplified by the heavier Ta atoms (
\xi_{5d}\sim 400
meV vs
\xi_{3d}\sim 20
meV
14
;
25
), the corresponding polar pairing enhancement
\delta\lambda(\theta)\propto\sin^{2}(\theta)
is expected to be substantially larger in KTO. Even allowing for strong material-dependent renormalizations due to band hybridization, this strong parametric amplification naturally renders the orientation-dependent channel weak in STO while making it dominant in KTO.

Our mechanism yields experimentally testable quantitative fingerprints that can distinguish it from purely electronic pairing theories
19
as well as isotropic bulk paraelectric phonon models
12
. First, because pairing is mediated by a parity-breaking Rashba vertex, the resulting superconducting state naturally requires a mixed-parity gap structure
15
. As a heuristic estimate, the gap admixture ratio scales as
\Delta_{\text{odd}}/\Delta_{\text{even}}\sim\alpha_{R}k_{F}/E_{F}
. Using typical ARPES parameters for KTO (
\alpha_{R}k_{F}\sim 10
meV,
E_{F}\sim 40
meV
14
;
5
), we predict a parity mixing amplitude of
\sim 20
–
25\%
at the (111) facet, which is continuously tunable with crystallographic orientation and accessible to scanning tunneling spectroscopy. Second, because the bosonic glue originates from Ta-O bond distortions within the PNRs, the framework predicts a substantial oxygen isotope exponent
\alpha=-d\ln T_{c}/d\ln M_{O}\approx 0.5
(subject to intermediate-coupling corrections), distinguishing a lattice-driven boson from purely electronic plasmons. Third, the threshold and intercept of the
T_{c}(\theta)
curve should be tunable through electrostatic boundary conditions that modify the interfacial electric field and PNR correlation length.

While a fully self-consistent treatment beyond the Migdal-Eliashberg approximation would be necessary to capture exact quasiparticle residues in the strong-coupling regime (
\lambda\gtrsim 1
), the geometric mechanism underlying the
T_{c}
scaling remains robust. In conclusion, we have presented an effective minimal mechanism for superconductivity at LAO/KTO interfaces in which a geometrically controlled Rashba vertex activates pairing by overdamped interfacial polar modes. Exact Matsubara-Eliashberg calculations show that, within the reduced isotropic model, this mechanism naturally maps geometric orbital tilt into the observed quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
dependence, while also providing a unified rationale for why KTaO
3
interfaces are both more orientation-sensitive and more strongly superconducting than their SrTiO
3
counterparts.

Supplementary Note 1: Basis Rotation, the Fundamental Domain, and Dipole Matrix Elements

In standard crystallographic convention, any arbitrary facet
(hkl)
can be mapped to a physically equivalent facet within the fundamental stereographic domain defined by
h\geq k\geq l\geq 0
. To align our theoretical derivations with this convention, we define the reference pristine interface as the (100) plane (with normal
\hat{x}
).

The crystallographic orientation is uniquely defined by the polar angle
\theta
between the interface normal
\hat{n}
and the
[100]
axis:
\theta=\arccos(h/\sqrt{h^{2}+k^{2}+l^{2}})
. Because
h
is the largest index in this domain, the maximum possible polar angle occurs for the (111) facet, where
h=k=l
, yielding
\theta_{\max}=\arccos(1/\sqrt{3})\approx 54.7^{\circ}
. Therefore, the physically unique geometric parameter space is strictly bounded by
0^{\circ}\leq\theta\leq 54.7^{\circ}
.

To derive the
\sin(\theta)
dependence of the dynamic electron-boson vertex
g(\theta)
, we rotate the Ta
5d

t_{2g}
orbital manifold from the bulk cubic frame
(x,y,z)
into the local surface frame
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})
, where
x^{\prime}
is the interface normal
\hat{n}
. Consider a general rotation away from the
[100]
cubic axis by the polar angle
\theta
:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\cos(\theta)-z^{\prime}\sin(\theta),

(S1)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=y^{\prime},

(S2)

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=x^{\prime}\sin(\theta)+z^{\prime}\cos(\theta).

(S3)

Accordingly, the real-space polynomial transforms as
yz=y^{\prime}z=y^{\prime}(x^{\prime}\sin\theta+z^{\prime}\cos\theta)=\cos(\theta)\,y^{\prime}z^{\prime}+\sin(\theta)\,x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
.
At the pristine (100) interface (
\theta=0^{\circ}
), the degeneracy of the
t_{2g}
manifold is lifted by the confinement potential. The subband derived from the bulk
|d_{yz}\rangle
orbital lies entirely in the interface plane, giving it the heaviest out-of-plane effective mass along
\hat{x}
and lowering its confinement energy to form the primary lowest-energy ground state. Expressed in the local tilted frame, the orbital is defined purely within the
t_{2g}
sub-manifold:

|d_{yz}\rangle\propto\cos(\theta)|d_{y^{\prime}z^{\prime}}\rangle+\sin(\theta)|d_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}\rangle

(S4)

The dynamic coupling to the polar nanoregion amplitude fluctuations
\delta P_{x^{\prime}}
requires a non-zero dipole matrix element evaluated along the interface normal
x^{\prime}
. Evaluating this for the
|d_{yz}\rangle
-derived subband yields:

\langle p_{\beta^{\prime}}|e\delta E_{x^{\prime}}x^{\prime}|d_{yz}\rangle=e\delta E_{x^{\prime}}\big[\cos(\theta)\langle p_{\beta^{\prime}}|x^{\prime}|d_{y^{\prime}z^{\prime}}\rangle+\sin(\theta)\langle p_{\beta^{\prime}}|x^{\prime}|d_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}\rangle\big].

(S5)

By rigorous parity symmetry, the first term
\langle p_{\beta^{\prime}}|x^{\prime}|d_{y^{\prime}z^{\prime}}\rangle

x^{\prime}

|p_{y^{\prime}}\rangle

\langle p_{y^{\prime}}|e\delta E_{x^{\prime}}x^{\prime}|d_{yz}\rangle=e\delta E_{x^{\prime}}\sin(\theta)\langle p_{y^{\prime}}|x^{\prime}|d_{x^{\prime}y^{\prime}}\rangle.

(S6)

Because this matrix element vanishes identically at the pristine (100) interface (
\theta=0^{\circ}
), the linear dynamic coupling to the polar mode strictly requires the geometric tilt to project the orbital out-of-plane. Consequently, the dynamic electron-boson vertex
g(\theta)
strictly inherits this leading angular dependence:
g(\theta)\propto\sin(\theta)
.

Supplementary Note 2: Microscopic Derivation of the Dynamic Rashba Vertex and Helicity Basis Projection

In this section, we derive the electron-boson interaction vertex starting from the fundamental relativistic spin-orbit coupling (SOC) Hamiltonian. We show that amplitude fluctuations of the out-of-plane polar nanoregions generate a dynamic Rashba interaction. By projecting this matrix-valued vertex onto the Rashba-split helicity bands, we motivate the reduction of the intra-band interaction to a scalar-dominant kernel.

1. Fundamental SOC and the Out-of-Plane Polar Fluctuation

The fundamental relativistic spin-orbit interaction for an electron moving in an electric field
\mathbf{E}
is given by:

\mathcal{H}_{\text{SO}}\propto(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}.

(S7)

The total electric field at the interface consists of the static confinement field
\mathbf{E}_{\text{static}}=E_{0}\hat{n}
and the dynamic out-of-plane amplitude fluctuations of the polar nanoregions,
\delta\mathbf{E}=\delta E_{z}\hat{n}
, which are proportional to the polar displacement field
P_{z}(\mathbf{q})
. The total field is strictly out-of-plane:
\mathbf{E}=(E_{0}+\delta E_{z})\hat{n}
.

Substituting this total field into the fundamental SOC Hamiltonian yields:

\mathcal{H}_{\text{SO}}\propto(E_{0}+\delta E_{z})(\hat{n}\times\mathbf{k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}=(E_{0}+\delta E_{z})(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}.

(S8)

Defining the in-plane momenta
(k_{x},k_{y})
perpendicular to
\hat{n}
, the cross product expands as
(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}=(k_{x}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}\sigma_{x})
. The Hamiltonian naturally separates into a static Rashba term and a dynamic electron-boson interaction:

\mathcal{H}_{\text{SO}}=\alpha_{R}(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}+g(\theta)\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q}}P_{z}(\mathbf{q})\psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}\left[(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}\right]\psi_{\mathbf{k}}.

(S9)

Because the static Rashba parameter
\alpha_{R}
and the dynamic coupling constant
g
originate from the identical structural inversion asymmetry (SIA) matrix elements evaluated in Supplementary Note 1, the dynamic vertex primarily inherits the geometric orbital projection of the static field. Therefore, the coupling vertex scales with the crystallographic orientation as
g(\theta)\propto\alpha_{R}(\theta)\propto\sin(\theta)
.

2. Projection onto the Helicity Basis

The dynamic interaction
H_{\text{int}}\propto(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}
is matrix-valued and momentum-dependent. To construct a scalar-dominant Eliashberg pairing strength
\lambda
, we project this interaction onto the eigenstates of the unperturbed Fermi surface.

The static non-interacting Hamiltonian is given by
H_{0}=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m^{*}}+\alpha_{R}\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
, where we define the helicity operator:

\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}=(\mathbf{k}\times\bm{\sigma})\cdot\hat{n}=k_{x}\sigma_{y}-k_{y}\sigma_{x}.

(S10)

The eigenstates of
H_{0}
are the Rashba-split helicity bands
|\pm,\mathbf{k}\rangle
. Because the Rashba term in
H_{0}
is proportional to
\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
, the helicity bands are eigenstates of
\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}
with eigenvalues proportional to the magnitude of the momentum:

\hat{\Lambda}_{\mathbf{k}}|\pm,\mathbf{k}\rangle=\pm|\mathbf{k}||\pm,\mathbf{k}\rangle.

(S11)

This dictates that the electron spins lie entirely in the
xy

\mathbf{k}

\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\approx-g(\theta)k_{F}

3. Reduction to a Scalar Eliashberg Kernel

Within the Eliashberg formalism, the pairing strength
\lambda
is obtained by integrating out the bosonic field. The effective four-fermion interaction
V_{\text{eff}}
mediated by the boson involves the product of two vertices and the boson propagator
\chi(\mathbf{q},\omega)
:

V_{\text{eff}}\propto|\langle\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\rangle_{\text{FS}}|^{2}\chi(\mathbf{q},\omega).

(S13)

Because the helicity projection maps the dominant forward-scattering interaction onto an effective scalar
\pm g(\theta)k_{F}
, the square of the vertex is channel-independent:

|\langle\Gamma_{\text{eff}}\rangle|^{2}\approx k_{F}^{2}g^{2}(\theta)\propto\sin^{2}(\theta).

(S14)

This projection simplifies two critical points:

1.

In the limit of dominant intra-band, small-
q
forward scattering, it motivates the reduction of the full mixed-parity Nambu-Gorkov equations to the standard, isotropic Eliashberg equations utilized in Supplementary Note 4.

2.

Because the effective pairing strength
\lambda
is proportional to the square of this scalar vertex (
\lambda\propto|\Gamma_{\text{eff}}|^{2}
), the Eliashberg coupling inherits the geometric scaling:
\lambda(\theta)=\lambda_{0}+C\sin^{2}(\theta)
. This provides the analytical input required for the macroscopic Allen-Dynes
T_{c}
evaluation.

4. Caveat Regarding the Spinor Overlap Factor

We note that the exact projection of the Rashba vertex between different momentum states
\mathbf{k}
and
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
introduces an angular spinor overlap factor, proportional to
\cos[(\phi_{\mathbf{k}}-\phi_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}})/2]
. This angular dependence is physically profound, as it dictates the exact symmetry admixture of
s
-wave and
p
-wave pairing components inherent to non-centrosymmetric superconductors.

However, explicitly retaining this momentum-selective overlap converts the Eliashberg gap equations into a computationally intensive multi-channel anisotropic problem. By replacing this factor with an isotropic Fermi-surface average, our scalar reduction operates as an effective model. This reduction is deliberately designed to efficiently isolate the overall integrated pairing energy scale (
\lambda
) responsible for the strong-coupling
T_{c}
enhancement, while explicitly sacrificing the ability to resolve the exact mixed-parity gap structure. Fully momentum-dependent Nambu-Gorkov studies will be required in future work to map the precise momentum-space gap symmetry.

III
Supplementary Note 3: Derivation of the Overdamped Brownian Oscillator Susceptibility

In this section, we derive the dynamical charge susceptibility for the out-of-plane polar nanoregion (PNR) fluctuations. To ensure the Eliashberg spectral moments remain finite in the ultraviolet (UV) limit, we model the soft mode using a second-order overdamped Brownian oscillator rather than a purely first-order relaxator.

Define the spatial dispersion factor as
A_{q}\equiv 1+\xi_{P}^{2}q^{2}
. For a polar lattice mode with inertia, the dynamics of the polar displacement field
P_{\bm{q}}(t)
driven by a conjugate source field
h_{\bm{q}}(t)
are governed by a damped wave equation:

\partial_{t}^{2}P_{\bm{q}}(t)+\gamma\partial_{t}P_{\bm{q}}(t)+\Omega_{B}^{2}A_{q}P_{\bm{q}}(t)=\Omega_{B}^{2}\chi_{0}h_{\bm{q}}(t),

(S15)

where
\Omega_{B}
is the bare oscillator frequency,
\gamma
is the phenomenological damping rate, and
\chi_{0}
is the static dielectric susceptibility.

Using the standard Fourier convention:

P_{\bm{q}}(t)=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t}P_{\bm{q}}(\omega),

(S16)

the time derivatives map to the frequency domain as
\partial_{t}\to-i\omega
and
\partial_{t}^{2}\to-\omega^{2}
. Substituting these into the equation of motion gives:

(-\omega^{2}-i\gamma\omega+\Omega_{B}^{2}A_{q})P_{\bm{q}}(\omega)=\Omega_{B}^{2}\chi_{0}h_{\bm{q}}(\omega).

(S17)

The retarded dynamical susceptibility is defined as the linear response
\chi(\bm{q},\omega)\equiv P_{\bm{q}}(\omega)/h_{\bm{q}}(\omega)
. Dividing both sides by the source field yields the exact form used in Eq. (1) of the main text:

\chi(\bm{q},\omega)=\frac{\chi_{0}\Omega_{B}^{2}}{\Omega_{B}^{2}A_{q}-\omega^{2}-i\gamma\omega}=\frac{\chi_{0}\Omega_{B}^{2}}{\Omega_{B}^{2}(1+\xi_{P}^{2}q^{2})-\omega^{2}-i\gamma\omega}.

(S18)

This formulation bridges the low-energy physics and high-energy mathematical consistency. In the
strongly overdamped limit
(
\gamma\gg\Omega_{B}
), the low-frequency dynamics (
\omega\ll\gamma
) are dominated by the damping term. Dropping the inertial
-\omega^{2}
term and dividing numerator and denominator by
\Omega_{B}^{2}
yields:

\chi(\bm{q},\omega)\approx\frac{\chi_{0}}{A_{q}-i\omega(\gamma/\Omega_{B}^{2})}.

(S19)

Defining an effective relaxational frequency
\Omega_{R}\equiv\Omega_{B}^{2}/\gamma
, we recover a Debye relaxational mode:

\chi(\bm{q},\omega)\approx\frac{\chi_{0}}{A_{q}-i\omega/\Omega_{R}}.

(S20)

Thus, at low energies, the mode behaves as an overdamped relaxator, driving the dense low-energy Eliashberg spectrum.

However, at high energies (the UV limit), the full Brownian equation retains the inertial
-\omega^{2}
term in the denominator. This guarantees that as
\omega\to\infty
, the imaginary part of the susceptibility decays rapidly as
\text{Im}\chi\propto 1/\omega^{2}
, meaning the electron-boson spectral function decays as
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)\propto\omega^{-3}
. This fast decay regularizes the Eliashberg integral, ensuring that the second moment
\bar{\omega}_{2}
used in the Allen-Dynes formula remains finite.

Supplementary Note 4: Review of Isotropic Eliashberg Equations

The Migdal-Eliashberg pairing mechanism is driven by the exchange of a soft bosonic mode between two electrons. As illustrated by the Feynman diagram in Fig. 2(a) of the main text, a Cooper pair scatters from momentum
(\bm{k},-\bm{k})
to
(\bm{k}^{\prime},-\bm{k}^{\prime})
by exchanging the overdamped PNR amplitude fluctuation
\chi(\bm{q},\omega)
.

With the pairing interaction approximated as a scalar-dominant kernel via the helicity projection (as detailed in Supplementary Note 2), the corresponding isotropic Eliashberg equations for the mass renormalization
Z(i\omega_{n})
and the gap function
\Delta(i\omega_{n})
take the standard form on the Matsubara axis (
\omega_{n}=\pi T(2n+1)
):

\displaystyle Z(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\pi T}{\omega_{n}}\sum_{m}\lambda(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})\text{sgn}(\omega_{m}),

(S21)

\displaystyle Z(i\omega_{n})\Delta(i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\pi T\sum_{m}\left[\lambda(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})-\mu^{*}\right]\frac{\Delta(i\omega_{m})}{|\omega_{m}|},

(S22)

where
\mu^{*}
is the Coulomb pseudopotential. In the main text,
T_{c}
is benchmarked by directly solving this system as an eigenvalue problem (as verified in Supplementary Note 9), while continuous parameter extraction is handled via the Allen-Dynes interpolation discussed in Supplementary Note 5.

Supplementary Note 5: Allen-Dynes Shape Factors and UV Regularization

To continuously bridge the weak-coupling BCS limit and the strong-coupling limit, Allen and Dynes introduced two phenomenological shape factors,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
:

T_{c}=\frac{\Omega_{\log}}{1.2}f_{1}f_{2}\exp\left[-\frac{1.04(1+\lambda)}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62\lambda)}\right].

(S23)

The shape factors and the logarithmic average frequency depend on the integrated moments of the Eliashberg spectrum:

\displaystyle\Omega_{\log}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\alpha^{2}F(\omega)\ln\omega\right),

(S24)

\displaystyle f_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[1+\left(\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{1}}\right)^{3/2}\right]^{1/3},\quad\Lambda_{1}=2.46(1+3.8\mu^{*}),

(S25)

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(\bar{\omega}_{2}/\Omega_{\log}-1)\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}+\Lambda_{2}^{2}},\quad\Lambda_{2}=1.82(1+6.3\mu^{*})\frac{\bar{\omega}_{2}}{\Omega_{\log}}.

(S26)

For a pure Debye relaxational susceptibility, the high-frequency tail of the spectral function scales as
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)\sim 1/\omega
. This causes the square of the second moment
\bar{\omega}_{2}^{2}=\frac{2}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega\alpha^{2}F(\omega)d\omega
to diverge linearly in the ultraviolet (UV).

However, by adopting the overdamped Brownian oscillator model for the soft PNR mode as derived in Supplementary Note 3,
\chi(\omega)
drops as
1/\omega^{2}
at high frequencies. This yields a spectral function that decays as
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)\sim\omega^{-3}
. This rapid decay acts as a microscopic UV cutoff, guaranteeing that
\bar{\omega}_{2}
is finite and mathematically well-defined.

Supplementary Note 6: Details of the Allen-Dynes Curve Fitting

To quantitatively connect our theoretical framework to the experimental data, we performed non-linear least-squares fits of the Allen-Dynes equation. Rather than treating
\Omega_{\log}
and
f_{2}
as independent free constants, we constrained the fit by explicitly integrating the overdamped Brownian spectrum
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
at each optimization step to compute the corresponding spectral moments.

The fitting dataset comprises the standard amorphous-LAO/KTO interfaces shown by the blue symbols in Fig. 4 of the main text; the ionic-liquid-gated points (orange stars) are displayed for comparison only and are not included in the fit because they represent a distinct electrostatic environment. The full dataset used in the main-text fit includes the non-superconducting (100) point.

The resulting fitting landscape is strongly covariant. In particular, both a near-threshold family of solutions with relatively small
\lambda_{0}
and large
C
, and a higher-baseline family with somewhat smaller geometric enhancement, can provide reasonable descriptions of the data. In the main text, we focus on the physically motivated near-threshold family because it is most naturally connected to the physical collapse of superconductivity at the pristine (100) interface and to the interpretation of the geometric Rashba channel as the dominant orientation-dependent enhancement.

Within this near-threshold regime and for a representative value
\mu^{*}=0.13
, we use the representative parameters

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}

\displaystyle\approx 9.88~\text{K}\,\,(0.85~\text{meV}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle\approx 0.25,

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\approx 2.88.

These values should be understood as representative parameters within the physically relevant near-threshold regime rather than as uniquely extracted quantities. The physically relevant feature is that this regime preserves a weak baseline channel together with a strong geometrically activated polar enhancement.

VII
Supplementary Note 7: Mixed-Parity Gap Function Estimates

In a noncentrosymmetric superconductor, the gap function
\hat{\Delta}(\bm{k})=[\psi(\bm{k})+\bm{d}(\bm{k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}]i\sigma_{y}
exhibits a mixture of even-parity (spin-singlet
\psi
) and odd-parity (spin-triplet
\bm{d}
) components. The amplitude of this mixing is governed by the strength of the Rashba SOC relative to the Fermi energy. As a heuristic small-SOC estimate, the admixture ratio scales as:

\frac{\Delta_{\text{odd}}}{\Delta_{\text{even}}}\sim\frac{\alpha_{R}(\theta)}{E_{F}}=\frac{\kappa_{SO}}{E_{F}}\sin(\theta).

(S27)

For LAO/KTO, with
E_{F}\approx 40
meV and
\kappa_{SO}
independently estimated from orientation-dependent upper critical field measurements, this ratio yields quantitative predictions for the absolute magnitude of parity mixing at each orientation. It ranges from negligibly small near the (100) plane to a maximum of order
\kappa_{SO}/E_{F}
at the (111) facet. This continuous, geometrically controlled parity tuning is a distinguishing feature of lattice-driven pairing mechanisms.

VIII
Supplementary Note 8: Crossover Problem and Methodological Caveats

The Migdal parameter
\lambda\Omega_{0}/E_{F}\sim 0.05\ll 1
ensures that vertex corrections to the electron-boson coupling are small at the diagrammatic level, motivating the use of the Migdal-Eliashberg framework. However, we note an important methodological caveat: in the intermediate-to-strong coupling regime (
\lambda\sim 1
–
2
), the quasiparticle residue
Z^{-1}=1/(1+\lambda)
is significantly reduced, making the concept of well-defined Fermi liquid quasiparticles increasingly approximate. Furthermore, for overdamped soft modes, a unique propagating phonon frequency is ill-defined, meaning vertex corrections could depend on more than a simple single-frequency Migdal ratio. A fully self-consistent treatment beyond Migdal-Eliashberg theory would be desirable to confirm the pairing. Nevertheless, the qualitative prediction of a quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
dependence remains robust, as it relies fundamentally on the geometric projection of the vertex and the general mathematical character of the intermediate-coupling transformation.

IX
Supplementary Note 9: Exact Numerical Eliashberg Verification

Because the Allen-Dynes interpolation was originally benchmarked for more conventional phonon spectra, its application to a broad overdamped mode requires explicit verification to ensure that the observed quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
behavior is not merely an artifact of the interpolation formula.

To address this, we solved the fully linearized isotropic Matsubara-Eliashberg equations numerically. For our derived effective scalar intra-band coupling, the equations reduce to a 1D grid over Matsubara frequencies
\omega_{n}=\pi T(2n+1)
:

Z(i\omega_{n})=1+\frac{\pi T}{\omega_{n}}\sum_{m}\lambda(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})\text{sgn}(\omega_{m}),

(S28)

Z(i\omega_{n})\Delta(i\omega_{n})=\pi T\sum_{m}\left[\lambda(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})-\mu^{*}\right]\frac{\Delta(i\omega_{m})}{|\omega_{m}|},

(S29)

where the pairing kernel is integrated directly from the exact continuous Brownian oscillator spectral function
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
:

\lambda(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})=2\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\frac{\omega\alpha^{2}F(\omega)}{\omega^{2}+(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})^{2}}.

(S30)

Numerical details.
The Matsubara sums were evaluated with a dynamic frequency cutoff chosen to encompass the relevant Eliashberg spectral weight. Numerical convergence tests show that the exact isotropic Eliashberg results are essentially insensitive to the Matsubara cutoff over the range used in the calculations and are also well converged with respect to the spectral discretization. The remaining dependence on the spectral upper integration bound is small and far too weak to account for the systematic difference observed between Allen-Dynes and the exact isotropic Eliashberg solution.

As shown in Fig. 4 of the main text, the exact numerical Eliashberg curve and the Allen-Dynes curve display the same qualitative quasi-linear
T_{c}(\theta)
trend, although the exact solution yields systematically larger
T_{c}
values at stronger coupling. This comparison indicates that the quasi-linear mapping is not a mathematical artifact of the Allen-Dynes interpolation formula, but a robust feature of the reduced model. We therefore interpret the quantitative difference as a limitation of the Allen-Dynes interpolation for the present overdamped spectrum in the stronger-coupling regime, rather than as a failure of the reduced mechanism itself.

2. Comparison with empirical alternatives

One may ask whether simpler empirical forms can describe the same dataset without invoking the reduced Eliashberg framework. To address this, we compare the present fit against two representative alternatives:
(i) a purely linear fit,
T_{c}=a\theta+b
, and
(ii) a thresholded quadratic fit,
T_{c}=A\max[\sin^{2}(\theta)-\theta_{0},0]
, where
\theta_{0}
is an effective onset threshold.

A purely empirical linear fit captures the approximate trend at intermediate and large angles, but it fails to represent the collapse of superconductivity near the pristine (100) plane unless one allows an ad hoc intercept or piecewise cutoff. Indeed, removing the (100) point substantially improves the linear-fit residual, indicating that the non-superconducting (100) interface is precisely where a naive linear phenomenology breaks down. By contrast, the thresholded quadratic form captures the near-threshold suppression more effectively and yields a slightly smaller raw summed squared residual than the reduced Eliashberg fit. However, it remains purely descriptive: it does not provide a bosonic pairing scale, a geometric Rashba pairing vertex, or a natural threshold mechanism tied to Coulomb competition. The value of the reduced Eliashberg framework is instead that it unifies the threshold and quasi-linear regimes within a physically motivated pairing scenario.
