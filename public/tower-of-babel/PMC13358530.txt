A decreased postoperative basophil percentage is an independent risk factor for prognosis in patients with lung cancer
Authors: Bingyuan Zhou, Jutong He, Shixue Zhang, Wen Wang, Zhaokai Huang, Ming Xu, Hexiao Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and effective prognostic biomarkers are still lacking. Granulocytes, particularly basophils, have been implicated in tumor progression, but the prognostic value of their dynamic perioperative changes remains unclear. The main objective of this single-center retrospective cohort study was to evaluate the potential prognostic value of perioperative changes in the peripheral blood basophil percentage (PBB%) in patients with lung cancer and to build a multifactorial prognostic model.MethodsA total of 468 patients who met the inclusion criteria were included and divided into two groups: the lower group included patients whose PBB% after surgery was lower than that before surgery, and the higher group included patients whose postoperative PBB% was higher than that before surgery. We compared the overall survival (OS) of the two groups using Kaplan-Meier analysis. Comorbidities, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status score, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), forced expiratory volume in 1 second/forced vital capacity (FEV1/FVC), and diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide percentage predicted (DLCO%) were collected. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression were performed.ResultsPatients in the lower group had a significantly better prognosis (P<0.05). In most of the subgroups, we found the same results (P<0.05), except for the left lobe, squamous cell carcinoma, in which there was no significant difference in OS between the lower and higher groups (P>0.05). Cox regression analysis identified three independent risk factors for OS in lung cancer patients: the trend of PBB% change, metastasis (M) stage, and postoperative complications.ConclusionsLung cancer patients with a decreased postoperative PBB% have a better prognosis, especially patients with right lobe adenocarcinoma and stage I disease. This factor, when combined with M staging and postoperative complications, can be used to construct a multifactorial prognostic model for lung cancer.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study analyzed 468 lung cancer patients and found postoperative peripheral blood basophil percentage (PBB%) reduction correlated with significantly better overall survival (OS), especially in right lobe adenocarcinoma and stage I patients. Cox regression analysis revealed that changes in PBB%, metastasis (M) stage, and postoperative complications were independent risk factors for OS, and these three factors collectively contributed to a multivariate prognostic model for lung cancer.

What is known and what is new?

• Granulocytes are involved in lung cancer occurrence and development, but basophil-related research is scarce, with existing studies only focusing on static basophil values and no exploration of postoperative PBB% dynamic changes for lung cancer prognosis.

• This manuscript is the first to report perioperative PBB% dynamic changes as a lung cancer prognostic biomarker, clarifies its specific predictive value in different subgroups, and establishes a novel prognostic model combining PBB% change and M stage.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• This finding provides a simple, accessible biomarker for clinical lung cancer prognosis assessment, suggesting routine perioperative PBB% monitoring in clinical practice, especially for early-stage adenocarcinoma patients. Future research should verify the mechanistic role of basophils in lung cancer via in vivo and in vitro experiments and expand the study to advanced lung cancer patients to improve clinical applicability.

Introduction

Background

Lung cancer is the second most commonly diagnosed cancer after female breast cancer and has the highest mortality of all cancers according to global cancer epidemiology (1). Despite advances in surgical techniques, perioperative management, and targeted therapies, the prognosis of lung cancer patients remains unsatisfactory, and reliable prognostic biomarkers are still lacking. Granulocytes, especially basophils and neutrophils, play an important role in the occurrence, development and treatment of lung cancer (2). In clinical setting, regarding the relationship between granulocytes and lung cancer, granulocytopenia occurs after chemotherapy in lung cancer patients. Wang et al. explored the predictive value of peripheral blood immune cells in patients with untreated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (3). The study found that there were significant differences in peripheral blood granulocyte levels in patients with NSCLC with different stages, sexes and ages by analyzing peripheral blood immune cell levels in 305 patients with untreated NSCLC and 132 healthy age- and sex-matched volunteers. At present, there are many studies on the association between neutrophils and lung diseases, and there is convincing evidence showing that neutrophils are the most common cell type in the immune cell composition of lung adenocarcinoma (4-6).

Rationale and knowledge gap

Additionally, there are few studies on the role of basophils in lung cancer. Lavin et al. found that the levels of neutrophils and eosinophils in lung cancer tissues were the same as those in unaffected lung tissues, but the levels of basophils and mast cells were significantly reduced (7). Basophils (and other cell types) in the tumor microenvironment of human lung cancer can be activated by the expression of galectin-3 by lung cancer cells to release multiple immunomodulatory cytokines and proinflammatory mediators (8). Recent mechanistic studies have further elucidated that basophils co-cultured with A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells produce interleukin-4/interleukin-13 (IL-4/IL-13) via an IgE- and galectin-3-dependent mechanism, and reciprocally induce tumor cells to secrete pro-tumorigenic cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) (9). The presence of basophils in tumor-draining lymph nodes of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is an independent negative prognostic biomarker for postoperative survival in patients with PDAC. Basophils promote the polarization of Th2 cells and M2 cells by producing IL-4, thus playing a tumor-promoting role in the progression of PDAC (10).

Objective

These are representative studies on the role of granulocytes in cancer in recent years, but no studies have focused on the postoperative prognostic effect of the peripheral blood basophil percentage (PBB%) on lung cancer patients. Therefore, we collected and analyzed the follow-up data and peripheral blood basophil levels of 468 lung cancer patients who underwent surgical resection. Here, based on the differences in the preoperative and postoperative basophil percentages, we first divided the patients into increased and decreased groups and then performed Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Furthermore, we performed Kaplan-Meier analysis in different subgroups divided according to age, sex, tumor stage, pathological type, lobe, normal/abnormal eosinophil level and other factors to explore the prognostic effect of the peripheral blood basophil level on the overall survival (OS) of lung cancer patients after surgery. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0430/rc).

Methods

Study population

The sample size was determined by the number of cases at the hospital during the study period. We conducted a retrospective analysis of patients with lung cancer who underwent surgical treatment in the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University, from 2014 to January 2019. All cases were strictly evaluated for eligibility according to pre-determined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) patients pathologically diagnosed with lung cancer; (II) patients who received surgical treatment; (III) patients with complete preoperative and postoperative PBB% data; (IV) patients with the same pathological stage who received the same postoperative treatment (e.g., chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted therapy); (V) patients with complete follow-up data. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) lung cancer cases with only clinical diagnosis or no definite pathological results; (II) patients who did not receive surgical treatment; (III) patients with incomplete perioperative clinical data or follow-up data; (IV) patients with the same pathological stage but different postoperative treatments. After screening according to the inclusion criteria, a total of 468 patients were enrolled. Among them, data from 346 patients with stage I and II diseases were used for the main survival analysis. In the subgroup analysis with preoperative and postoperative PBB% as influencing factors, all 468 patients were included to ensure a sufficient sample size. The blood samples analyzed in the study were fasting blood collected from patients on the day before surgery and three days after surgery.

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University (approval No. 2021100K), and the requirement for informed consent was waived. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Data collection

Baseline characteristics extracted from electronic medical records included: age at diagnosis, gender, height, weight, pathological type, clinical stage, timing and results of follow-up computed tomography (CT) scans, alcohol consumption, smoking status, postoperative treatment (chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy), comorbidities including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, heart disease, and chronic pulmonary disease [e.g., chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), chronic bronchitis], as well as recurrence and metastasis status. Pulmonary function parameters [forced expiratory volume in 1 second/forced vital capacity (FEV1/FVC) ratio and diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide percentage predicted (DLCO%)] were also recorded, defined as abnormal if below the lower limit of normal.

The clinical data of patients were retrieved from the Hospital Information System of Zhongnan Hospital of Wuhan University, and all follow-up information was collected and documented by specialized follow-up staff via telephone interviews. Venous blood samples were collected in the morning under fasting conditions and usually tested within 30 minutes after collection. All data obtained from electronic medical records were subject to rigorous manual verification by dedicated researchers.

Content of follow-up

The follow-up content included patients’ survival status, time of death, surgical satisfaction, postoperative symptoms (pain, bloody sputum, fever, urination, and defecation), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score, and comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and heart disease). Additionally, all patients were evaluated using the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status classification: healthy patients without systemic diseases were classified as Grade 1, those with mild systemic diseases as Grade 2, and those with severe systemic diseases as Grade 3. The follow-up started in June 2021 and ended upon patient death, disease progression, or the last follow-up visit, with follow-up assessments performed by two independent radiologists.

Definitions

OS: OS was defined as the time from the completion of surgery to death from any cause. OS was measured over time in units of months. Lower group: patients whose PBB% after surgery was lower than that before surgery. Higher group: patients whose postoperative PBB% was higher than that before surgery. Normal PBB%: refers to a PBB% ranging from 0% to 1%. Increased PBB%: refers to a PBB% greater than 1%. Normal-normal: refers to the subgroup in which patients have normal preoperative PBB% and normal postoperative PBB%. Normal-increased: refers to the subgroup in which patients have normal preoperative PBB% and increased postoperative PBB%. Increased-normal: refers to the subgroup in which patients have increased preoperative PBB% and normal postoperative PBB%. Increased-increased: refers to the subgroup in which patients have increased preoperative PBB% and increased postoperative PBB%.

Statistical analysis

IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 (International Business Machines Corporation, Armonk, New York) and GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software Inc., California) were used for statistical analysis. The Chi-squared test was used for comparisons between groups. The Kaplan-Meier method was used for survival analysis, and the log-rank test was used when necessary. A multivariate Cox proportional hazards model was established to calculate the risk ratios of factors including age, sex, lobe, stage and pathological type. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to balance baseline characteristics between patients with decreased vs. increased postoperative basophil percentage.

Survival analysis for stage I–II patients (lower vs. higher group)

Among stage I–II patients, there were 145 patients in the lower group and 169 in the higher group (equal group not included in survival comparison). As shown in Figure 1, the OS of patients in the lower group was significantly better than that of the higher group, both for stage I (P<0.001) and stage II (P=0.04). Subgroup analyses by sex, age, lobe, pathology, and PBB% status were performed. After PSM, baseline characteristics were balanced between the two groups, with no significant differences observed in key variables.

Kaplan-Meier curves for stage I and stage II patients. (A) OS comparison between the lower (down) and higher (up) PBB% groups among stage I–II lung cancer patients. (B) Stage I patients: down group (n=111) vs. up group (n=119), P<0.001. (C) Stage II patients: down group (n=34) vs. up group (n=50), P=0.04. P values were calculated by log-rank test. Down, postoperative PBB% < preoperative PBB%; up, postoperative PBB% > preoperative PBB%. OS, overall survival; PBB%, peripheral blood basophil percentage.

The prognosis of the lower and higher groups in different sex and tumor lobe subgroups

In the female subgroup, there were 57 patients in the lower group and 62 patients in the higher group. The OS of patients in the lower group was better than that of patients in the higher group (P=0.002, Figure 2A). Likewise, we obtained the same result in the male subgroup as in the female group, and the OS of patients in the lower group was higher than that of patients in the higher group (P<0.001, Figure 2B). The results of influencing factors after matching showed no difference between the two groups. When stratified by tumor lobe, for patients with tumors in the right lobe, the OS of the lower group was significantly better than that of the higher group (P<0.001, Figure 2C), whereas for patients with tumors in the left lobe, no significant difference was observed between the two groups (P=0.07, Figure 2D).

(B) Male patients (down, n=88; up, n=107), P<0.001. (C) Patients with right‑lobe tumors (down, n=89; up, n=92), P<0.001. (D) Patients with left‑lobe tumors (down, n=56; up, n=76), P=0.07 (not significant). P values were calculated by log‑rank test. Down, postoperative PBB% < preoperative PBB%; up, postoperative PBB% > preoperative PBB%. OS, overall survival; PBB%, peripheral blood basophil percentage.

The prognosis of the lower and higher groups in different lobes and pathology subgroups

Regarding pathological types (Figure 3A,3B), in the adenocarcinoma subgroup, the lower group had a significantly better OS than the higher group (P<0.001); however, in the squamous cell carcinoma subgroup, there was no significant difference in OS between the lower and higher groups (P=0.10). The results of influencing factors after matching showed no difference between the two groups in any of these subgroups. We further compared the prognosis of the lower and higher groups according to the ASA Physical Status Score. In the low-ASA subgroup (ASA ≤1, patients with no or only mild systemic diseases), the OS of the lower group was significantly better than that of the higher group (P<0.001, Figure 3C). In the high-ASA subgroup (ASA >1, patients with more severe systemic diseases), the lower group also had a better OS than the higher group (P=0.02, Figure 3D).

The prognosis of patients with different peripheral blood basophil statuses

After conducting Kaplan-Meier survival analyses on different subgroups based on five influencing factors, we further analyzed the patients according to their peripheral blood basophil levels. The Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that in both the preoperative and postoperative subgroups with normal PBB%, the OS was significantly better in the lower PBB% group compared to the higher PBB% group (P<0.001; Figure 4A,4B). Since the peripheral blood basophil levels of most patients fell within the normal reference range, we also performed Kaplan-Meier survival analyses on patients with normal PBB% both preoperatively and postoperatively. The results demonstrated that in this subgroup, the OS was similarly significantly better in the lower PBB% group than in the higher PBB% group (P<0.001; Figure 4C). For stage I–II lung cancer patients after surgery, the number of patients with postoperative basophil percentage >1% was too small to perform a reliable Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Therefore, the survival analysis for this subgroup was not performed in this study.

Cox regression analysis for several important influencing factors

To explore the influence of some important factors, such as age, sex, TNM staging, stage, pathology, postoperative complications, pulmonary function and surgical procedure, on the OS of patients in this study and to establish a multifactor prognostic model of lung cancer, we performed Cox regression analysis in all patients. We defined OS as the time and death as the event occurrence. Table 3 shows the Cox regression analysis results for the total population. From Table 3, we can see that the tendency of PBB% change (postoperative PBB% minus preoperative PBB%, <0 or >0) was an independent risk factor for the prognosis of all lung cancer patients [risk ratio =1.494, confidence interval (CI): 1.021–2.186, P=0.04]. The increase in PBB% was significantly associated with poor OS in lung cancer patients. From Table 4, we found the same results in the correlation between the tendency of PBB% and the OS of patients in stages I and II (risk ratio =1.763, CI: 1.113–2.794, P=0.02). In addition, we found that stage metastasis (M) and postoperative complications were confirmed as independent risk factors in all patients and in patients in stages I and II. The higher the M stage is, the poorer the OS rate. Thus, the tendency of PBB% change and M stage may constitute a potential prognostic model for lung cancer, where patients with an increased PBB% and a higher M stage may have a poorer prognosis than other patients.

Discussion

Key findings

This study found that lung cancer patients with a decreased postoperative PBB% relative to the preoperative level had a significantly better OS (2). This correlation was verified in most subgroups, while no significant OS difference was observed between the lower and higher PBB% groups in the left lobe, squamous cell carcinoma. The change in postoperative PBB% mainly affects the prognosis of lung cancer patients with adenocarcinoma, and exerts an important effect on tumorigenesis as positive results were only found in the stage I subgroup rather than the stage II subgroup. Additionally, Cox regression analysis confirmed that the tendency of PBB% change, M stage and postoperative complications were independent prognostic risk factors for lung cancer patients, both in the total population of stage I–IV and the stage I–II subgroup.

Strengths and limitations

At present, research on basophils and lung cancer prognosis mainly focuses on clinical data analysis. A major strength of this study is the use of a self-established clinical database, in which each patient was followed up by professionals who also took charge of medical record classification. In particular, this study focused on the dynamically changing values of PBB% rather than isolated static values, and applied Cox regression analysis to establish a multifactorial prognostic model for lung cancer, which can be used for clinical prognosis assessment of lung cancer patients. There are some limitations in this study. First, this was a single-center retrospective cohort study, which may have inherent selection bias and information bias, and the results need to be further verified in a multi-center, large-sample prospective cohort. Second, our study only enrolled patients who underwent surgical resection, and most patients with clinical stage III and IV lung cancer were excluded as they did not receive surgery or underwent neoadjuvant therapy before surgery, so the prognostic model established in this study cannot be directly applied to these patients. Third, the sample size of some subgroups was relatively small (e.g., patients with postoperative basophil percentage >1% in stage I–II disease), which may have limited the statistical power of the subgroup analysis, and the prognostic value of PBB% in these subgroups needs to be further explored in a larger sample. Fourth, this study only clarified the clinical correlation between dynamic changes in PBB% and the prognosis of lung cancer patients, and lacked in vivo and in vitro experimental verification of the specific mechanism of basophils in lung cancer tumorigenesis, progression and metastasis.

Comparison with similar research

Existing studies have confirmed the tumor-promoting role of the granulocyte family in oncology (2). Studies in the 20th century found that the PBB% level in primary bronchial lung cancer patients was significantly higher than that in normal people (11), and Wang et al. found that a high preoperative basophil/lymphocyte ratio was associated with poor postoperative prognosis in NSCLC patients (3). Unlike these studies that focused on isolated static PBB% values, this study explored the changing value of PBB% and its correlation with patient prognosis in different subgroups.

In other solid tumors, basophils have been confirmed to play a tumor-promoting role (10), which is consistent with the conclusion of this study that an increase in postoperative PBB% is associated with poor prognosis in lung cancer patients. Existing studies have fully confirmed the protumor effect of neutrophils in lung adenocarcinoma (4,5,12), while research on the role of basophils in lung cancer is scarce, and no studies have focused on the postoperative prognostic effect of dynamic changes in PBB% on lung cancer patients, which this study has supplemented and expanded.

Moreover, recent AllergoOncology reviews have highlighted the context-dependent pro-and anti-tumor functions of basophils, and emphasized their potential as prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets in solid tumors (13). Mendelian randomization studies have also provided genetic evidence that immune cell traits, including basophil-related phenotypes, may mediate the causal pathway from complement components to cancer risk (14).

Explanations of findings

The association between decreased postoperative PBB% and better OS in lung cancer patients can be explained by the biological characteristics of basophils. Basophils are primarily activated by IgE and release inflammatory mediators such as IL-3 (15); Lopatina et al. found that naive tec-derived extracellular vesicles (nTEVs) formed by IL-3 autocrine signaling can increase the expression of programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) in primary tumors and lung and liver metastases, promoting tumor immune escape and tumor cell growth (15). A decrease in PBB% means a weakening of basophil-mediated pro-tumor inflammatory response, which is conducive to the recovery of the body’s anti-tumor immune function.

Notably, the prognostic value of PBB% changes was significant in lung adenocarcinoma but not in squamous cell carcinoma, which may be due to the close correlation between lung adenocarcinoma and immune microenvironment regulation (4-6,12), while squamous cell carcinoma has different tumorigenesis mechanisms. In metastatic cancers, an extreme elevation of serum transcobalamin I is often observed and is generally associated with increased proliferation of granulocytes, which suggests a potential correlation with tumor metastases (16). In addition, the cancer cell-autonomous secretion of C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CXCL5) is sufficient to drive the infiltration of mature neutrophils, which is protumorigenic (17). For stage I patients with limited tumor burden, perioperative immune microenvironment changes represented by PBB% reduction can directly affect tumor recurrence and survival, while stage II patients are affected by more complex factors such as lymph node metastasis, leading to the loss of the prognostic value of PBB% changes. In addition, PBB% change reflects the body’s immune status and M stage reflects tumor metastasis status, and the two affect lung cancer prognosis through different pathways, which justifies their combination to construct a prognostic model.

Implications and actions needed

This study clarifies the prognostic value of dynamic changes in perioperative PBB% for lung cancer patients undergoing surgical treatment, and identifies PBB% change tendency as an independent risk factor for lung cancer prognosis. The established multifactorial prognostic model combining PBB% change and M stage provides a new tool for clinical prognostic assessment of lung cancer patients, and supplements the research on the role of basophils in lung cancer prognosis, further confirming the tumor-promoting role of granulocytes in lung cancer (2). On the basis of the present results, future studies should focus on the verification of the potential pro-tumor role of basophils in lung cancer tumorigenesis and metastasis and its specific mechanism through both in vivo and in vitro experiments. In addition, follow-up research can expand the study population to include advanced lung cancer patients who cannot undergo surgical treatment, to further explore the prognostic value of PBB% changes in the whole population of lung cancer patients, and to verify and optimize the established multifactorial prognostic model for wider clinical application.

Conclusions

The change in postoperative PBB% compared to preoperative PBB% has potential prognostic significance for the OS of lung cancer patients after surgery; this difference, along with M staging and postoperative complications, constitutes independent risk factors for OS in lung cancer patients. These three factors could be combined to constitute a multifactorial prognostic model for lung cancer.
