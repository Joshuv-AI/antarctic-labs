Topological complexity sequences of groups
Daisuke Kishimoto, Yuki Minowa

Abstract
. We define the topological complexity sequence of a group as the sequence of topological complexities of its Milnor constructions. This sequence may be regarded as an intrinsic refinement of the topological complexity of a group and, unlike topological complexity itself, is meaningful for groups of infinite cohomological dimension. We show that the topological complexity sequence of every group of infinite cohomological dimension is weakly increasing and unbounded. We then estimate its growth and determine its asymptotic behavior for a finite group of even order.

Introduction

Topological complexity is a numerical homotopy invariant of a space, introduced by Farber
[
9
]
, which quantifies instability in motion planning problems in a continuous setting. Since its introduction, topological complexity and its variants have been studied extensively. We now recall the definition. Let
X
be a space. The
topological complexity

\mathsf{TC}(X)
is defined as the minimal integer
n
for which there exists an open cover
X\times X=U_{0}\cup\cdots\cup U_{n}
such that the evaluation fibration

X^{[0,1]}\to X\times X,\quad\ell\mapsto(\ell(0),\ell(1))

admits a section over
U_{i}
for each
i=0,\ldots,n
. If no such open cover exists, we set

. This definition admits a natural interpretation in terms of motion planning. A path in

represents a motion of a robot in the space
X
. Accordingly, the path space

can be viewed as the space of all possible motions, and a section of the above fibration corresponds to a motion planning. The number of open sets required to cover
X\times X
and admit local sections thus reflects the instability of motion plannings in
X
.

Let

be a (discrete) group. As in the case of the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of a group, we define the topological complexity of

2.
Monotonicity

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
by using Lusternik-Schnirelmann category.

Let
X
be a space. Recall that the

\mathsf{cat}(X)
is defined as the minimal integer

such that there exists an open cover
X=U_{0}\cup\cdots\cup U_{n}
, where the inclusion
U_{i}\to X
is null-homotopic for each
i=0,\ldots,n
. If no such open cover exists, we set
\mathsf{cat}(X)=\infty
. By
[

, Theorem 5]
, if

is path-connected and paracompact, then

(2.1)

\mathsf{cat}(X)\leq\mathsf{TC}(X)\leq 2\cdot\mathsf{cat}(X).

We remark that our normalization of
\mathsf{TC}(X)
and
\mathsf{cat}(X)
differs from
[
9
]
. Specifically, our normalization is the one used in
[
9
]
minus one, which has become standard in recent work.

Let
G
be a group. Recall that the
cohomological dimension
of
G
is defined as the maximal integer
n
such that
H^{n}(G;M)\neq 0
, where
M
ranges over all
G
-modules. Recall from
[

,
6
,
27
]
that if
G
has infinite cohomological dimension, then there exists a cohomology class
\mathfrak{b}\in H^{1}(BG;I)
, called the Berstein class, such that the
n
-th power
\mathfrak{b}^{n}\in H^{n}(BG;I^{\otimes n})
is nontrivial for all
n\geq 1
. Here,
I
denotes the augmentation ideal of the group ring
\mathbb{Z}G
, regarded as twisted coefficients via the natural

-action.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group of infinite cohomological dimension. Then for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathsf{cat}(B_{n}G)=n.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\in H^{1}(BG;I)
be the Berstein class. Since
G
has infinite cohomological dimension, its
n
-th power
\mathfrak{b}^{n}\in H^{n}(BG;I^{\otimes n})
is nontrivial for all
n\geq 1
. The natural map
i\colon B_{n}G\to BG
is an
n
-equivalence, and hence the induced map
i^{*}\colon H^{n}(BG;I^{\otimes n})\to H^{n}(B_{n}G;I^{\otimes n})
is injective. It follows that
i^{*}(\mathfrak{b})^{n}\neq 0
, which implies the cup-length of
B_{n}G
is bounded below by
n
. Thus since the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category is bounded below by the cup-length, we obtain
\mathsf{cat}(B_{n}G)\geq n
. On the other hand,
\mathsf{cat}(B_{n}G)\leq\dim B_{n}G=n
. Therefore
\mathsf{cat}(B_{n}G)=n
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group of infinite cohomological dimension. Then for every

,

n\leq\mathsf{TC}^{n}(G)\leq 2n.

Proof.

The claim follows from Proposition
2.1
together with (
2.1
).
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
G
be a group of infinite cohomological dimensions. By Corollary
2.2
, the topological complexity sequence of

is unbounded. There is a homotopy cofiber sequence

G^{\star(n+1)}\to B_{n}G\to B_{n+1}G,

showed that for

∎

4.
Quaternion group

In this section, using sectional category weight, we provide a direct upper bound for the growth function
\alpha_{G}(n)
when
G
is the quaternion group
Q_{8}
. This is sharper than Theorem
1.4
for infinitely many values of
n
.

Recall from
[
10
]
that the
sectional category weight

\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)
of a cohomology class
u\in H^{*}(Y)
with respect to a map
f\colon X\to Y
is defined as the maximal integer
n
such that for any map
g\colon Z\to Y
satisfying
\mathsf{secat}(g^{*}f\colon W\to Z)<n
, one has
g^{*}(u)=0
. Here
g^{*}f\colon W\to Z
denotes the homotopy pullback of
f
along
g
. If no such integer exists, we set
\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)=\infty
. The choice of coefficients for cohomology is arbitrary.
s
The sectional category weight satisfies the following properties
[
10
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map, and let
u\in H^{*}(Y)
be nontrivial.

(1)

There are inequalities

\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)\leq|u|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)\leq\mathsf{secat}(f).

(2)

For any map
g\colon Z\to Y
,

\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)\leq\mathsf{wgt}_{g^{*}f}(g^{*}(u)).

(3)

If
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}
, then

\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u)\geq\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u_{1})+\cdots+\mathsf{wgt}_{f}(u_{n}).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map, and let
u\in H^{*}(Y\times Y)
be nontrivial. Then

\mathsf{wgt}_{\Delta_{Y}}(u)\leq\mathsf{wgt}_{\Delta_{X}}((f\times f)^{*}(u)),

where
\Delta_{Z}\colon Z\to Z\times Z
denotes the diagonal map of a space
Z
.

Proof.

Since
\Delta_{X}
is a homotopy pullback of
\Delta_{Y}
along
f
, the claim follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Let
Q_{8}
denote the quaternion group.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
k\geq 0
,

\mathsf{TC}^{2^{k}+2}(Q_{8})\geq 2^{k+1}+2.

Proof.

Recall from

|x|=|y|=1

|e|=4
. By
[

,
6
,

]
, the category weight of every element of

equals its degree. Hence

, and by

that there exists

such that

and

The coefficient of

\mathsf{wgt}_{\Delta_{BQ_{8}}}((x^{2}y\times x^{2}y)u^{2^{k-1}-1})\geq 2^{k+1}+2.

Since the inclusion

5.
Further problems

In this section, we pose six problems concerning topological complexity sequences and their growth.

We proved in Theorem
1.2
that the topological complexity sequence of every group of infinite cohomological dimension is weakly increasing. In our proof, the assumption that the group has infinite cohomological dimension is essential. However, the monotonicity of the topological complexity sequence itself may also hold for other classes of groups, which leads us to the following problem.

Problem 5.1
.

Is the topological complexity sequence weakly increasing for every group?

The growth function
\alpha_{G}
is well defined for every group
G
of infinite cohomological dimension and satisfies the estimate (
1.2
). We now consider the growth function for other groups
G
of infinite cohomological dimension. For a finite group
G
of even order, we obtained a sharper upper bound for
\alpha_{G}
in Theorem
1.4
and described the asymptotic behavior of
\alpha_{G}
in Corollary
1.5
. If
H
is central subgroup of
G
, then it has diagonal stabilizer. By Proposition
3.7
together with Proposition
3.4
, we have

\mathsf{TC}^{\mathcal{D}}(B_{n}H)\leq\mathsf{TC}^{n}(G).

In particular, if
G
has a central subgroup of order
p
for an odd prime
p
(for example, if
G
is a
p
-group), then

\mathsf{TC}^{\mathcal{D}}(B_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq\mathsf{TC}^{n}(G).

Farber and Grant
[
11
]
computed the weights of some elements in the zero-divisor ideals of lens spaces
L^{2n+1}(m)
. This yields a sharp lower bound for the topological complexity of
L^{2n+1}(p)
for certain values of
n
, where
p
is an odd prime. This computation applies to
\mathsf{TC}^{\mathcal{D}}(B_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
since for any group
K
, the diagonal map
B_{n}K\to B_{n}K\times B_{n}K
factors as

B_{n}K\to E_{n}K\times_{K}E_{n}K\to B_{n}K\times B_{n}K,

where the first map is an
(n-1)
-equivalence and by Proposition
3.3
,
\mathsf{TC}^{\mathcal{D}}(B_{n}K)
equals the sectional category of the second map (cf.
[
8
, Theorem 7.7]
). Consequently, we obtain a sharp upper bound for
\alpha_{G}(n)
for certain values of
n
whenever
G
has a central subgroup of order
p
. However, this is not sufficient to detect the asymptotic behavior of
\alpha_{G}
. Even worse, this method does not apply to groups with trivial center. This motivates the following problems concerning finite groups of odd order.

Problem 5.2
.

Is there an upper bound for the growth function
\alpha_{G}(n)
for every group
G
of odd order that is sharper than (
1.2
)?

Problem 5.3
.

Does Theorem
1.4
hold for every nontrivial group of odd order?

In this paper, we do not address the rate of the growth of topological complexity sequences; that is, we do not estimate the difference
\alpha_{G}(n+1)-\alpha_{G}(n)
. This leads us to the following problem.

Problem 5.4
.

Does there exist a group
G
of infinite cohomological dimension such that the sequence
\{\alpha_{G}(n+1)-\alpha_{G}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded?

There are several variants of topological complexity, and it would be interesting to study the corresponding topological complexity sequences. Rudyak
[
26
]
introduced sequential topological complexity, motivated by motion planning problems involving several intermediate points. The
r
-th
sequential topological complexity
of a space
X
, denoted by
\mathsf{TC}_{r}(X)
, is defined as the sectional category of the diagonal map
X\to X^{r}
, where
X^{r}
denotes the product of
r
copies of
X
. For a group
G
, we define the
r
-th
sequential topological complexity sequence
by

\mathsf{TC}^{n}_{r}(G)=\mathsf{TC}_{r}(B_{n}G).

Analogously to Corollary
2.2
, we obtain the estimate

(5.1)

n\leq\mathsf{TC}_{r}^{n}(G)\leq rn

for all
n\geq 1
and any group
G
of infinite cohomological dimension. Consequently, the growth function

\alpha_{G}^{r}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad n\mapsto|\{k\geq 1\mid\mathsf{TC}^{k}_{r}(G)\leq n\}|

is well defined, and by (
5.1
), it satisfies the estimate

\left[\frac{n}{r}\right]\leq\alpha_{G}^{r}(n)\leq n.

Thus we pose the following problems.

Problem 5.5
.

Is there an estimate for the growth function
\alpha_{G}^{r}
analogous to Theorem
1.4
for a finite group
G
of even order?

Problem 5.6
.

Does

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{G}^{r}(n)}{n}=\frac{1}{r}

hold for every finite group
G
of even order?
