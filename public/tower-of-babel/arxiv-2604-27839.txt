Abstract
. In this paper we study a variant of the uncentred Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on Damek–Ricci spaces in which balls are replaced by suitable half balls. Perhaps surprisingly, such modified maximal operator has better boundedness properties than the classical one. In particular, it satisfies an L ​ log ⁡ L L\log L endpoint estimate and it is bounded on L p L^{p} for every p p in ( 1 , ∞ ] (1,\infty] .

1.
Introduction

Given a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, a family
\mathfrak{F}
of subsets of
X
of positive measure, a locally integrable
function
f
on
X
and a point
x
in
X
, define the
uncentred Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
associated to
\mathfrak{F}
by

(1.1)

\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{F}}\!f(x):=\sup_{F\ni x\atop F\in\mathfrak{F}}\,\frac{1}{|{F}|}\,\intop\nolimits_{F}|{f}|\,\text{\rm d}\mu.

The boundedness properties of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{F}}\!
between
L^{p}
, Lorentz and Orlicz spaces and for a wide variety of metric measure
spaces
X
and families
\mathfrak{F}
have been investigated in several papers.

In this paper we suppose that
S
is a Damek–Ricci space endowed with a left-invariant metric Riemannian
d

and Riemannian measure
\mu
and analyse the boundedness properties of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{F}}
for various families
\mathfrak{F}
of subsets of
S
.
We recall that the metric measure space
(S,d,\mu)
has exponential volume growth, and it is locally, but not globally, doubling.
For any measurable subset
E
of
S
we shall often write
|{E}|
instead of
\mu(E)
.

Recall that the Damek–Ricci spaces include all the symmetric spaces of the noncompact type and real rank one, and, in particular,
the real hyperbolic spaces. The paradigmatic example of the latter is the hyperbolic upper half plane, which will play
an important role in this paper. For more on the structure of Damek–Ricci spaces and their relation with noncompact
symmetric spaces, see
[
DR
,
CDKR1
,
CDKR2
,
R
]
. We emphasise the fact that most Damek–Ricci spaces are not symmetric.

Denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the family of all open balls in
S
, and consider, for each locally integrable function
f
on
S
, its
classical
uncentred
Hardy–Littlewood (HL) maximal function
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}\!f
.
A well known result by A. Ionescu
[
I1
]
states that if
S
is a symmetric space of the
noncompact type and real rank one, then
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}
is of restricted weak type
(2,2)
, and, interpolating with the trivial bound
on
L^{\infty}(S)
, also bounded on
L^{p}(S)
for
p>2
. He also proved that on any higher rank symmetric space
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}
is bounded on

L^{p}(S)
if and only if
p>2

[
I2
]
. For an extension of the rank one result
[
I1
]
to homogeneous trees, see
[
V
]
.
Extensions of Ionescu’s results to Riemannian manifolds with exponential volume growth have been proved by various authors.
See, in particular,
[
L2
,
L3
]
, where certain cuspidal manifolds are considered, and
[
MPSV
]
.

It is worth mentioning that the
centred
Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
, defined much as
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}
,
but where the supremum is taken over all balls centred at
x
, has also been the object of several studies. After the landmark
results of Strömberg
[
Str
]
, who proved the weak-type
(1,1)
estimate for
\mathcal{M}
on all symmetric spaces
of the noncompact type, quite a few authors proved interesting results concerning
\mathcal{M}
on various metric measure spaces
with exponential volume growth. These include
[
ADY
, Corollary 3.22]
, where Strömberg’s result is extended
to Damek–Ricci spaces,
[
Str
]
, where an interesting Cartan–Hadamard surface is considered,

[
L1
,
L2
,
L3
]
where certain cuspidal manifolds are studied,
[
CMS
]
where the quite general
case of Gromov hyperbolic spaces satisfying certain exponential volume growth estimates is treated, and
[
MPSV
]
, where
the invariance of the
L^{p}
mapping properties of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to rough isometries is proved.

For results in the case of trees, which motivated further analysis in the continuous case, see
[
LMSV
,
LS
,
MS
,
CMS1
,
NT
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to consider a variant
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}
in which balls are replaced by certain half balls,
and to show that, perhaps surprisingly,
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
has better boundedness properties than
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}
. This work is inspired by

[
MS
]
, where the case of (possibly nonhomogeneous) locally finite trees with exponential volume growth is considered.
However, their extension to Damek–Ricci spaces requires new ideas and is technically much more involved. It would be
interesting to extend our result to Cartan–Hadamard manifolds, but this will require different methods.

It is perhaps convenient to first describe
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
in the simplest possible case,
viz. the half plane
\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>0\big\}
, endowed with the metric

(1.2)

\,\text{\rm d}s^{2}:=\frac{\,\text{\rm d}x^{2}+\,\text{\rm d}y^{2}}{y^{2}};

we denote by
d
the corresponding distance. The associated Riemannian measure is

(1.3)

\,\text{\rm d}\mu(x,y)=\frac{\,\text{\rm d}x\,\text{\rm d}y}{y^{2}}.

Horizontal lines in the upper half plane are the horocycles with respect to the point at infinity in the direction of the
y
-axis.
Given a point
z
in
\mathbb{H}
we consider the unique geodesic tangent to the horocycle through
z
. This geodesic splits
the ball
B_{R}(z)
(centred at
z
and with radius
R
) into two parts, the lower of which we call
b_{R}(z)
. The family
of such half balls will be denoted by
\mathfrak{b}
. Since
\mathbb{H}
is symmetric, the measure of
b_{R}(z)
is just one half the measure of

B_{R}(z)
.

These sets and the family
\mathfrak{b}
have natural generalisations on any Damek–Ricci space:
see Section
3
for the details. The uncentred maximal operator on
S
associated to the family
\mathfrak{b}
is then defined as in (
1.1
), with

\mathfrak{b}
in place of
\mathfrak{F}
.
Since for every
x
in
S
and every positive radius
R
the measure of
b_{R}(x)
is one half of the measure of
B_{R}(x)
,
a straightforward argument yields the pointwise estimate

\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}\!f\leq 2\,\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{B}}\!f,

which, by (a straightforward generalisation of) Ionescu’s result, implies that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
is bounded on

L^{p}(\mathbb{H})
for all
p>2
and it is of restricted weak type
(2,2)
.

The main result of the paper, which is novel also for the hyperbolic upper half plane, is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
S
is a Damek–Ricci space. Then the following hold:

(i)

there exist positive constants
C
and
\lambda
such that

\bigl|{\{x\in S:\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}\!f(x)>\alpha\}}\bigr|\leq C\,\intop\nolimits_{S}\,\frac{|{f}|}{\alpha}\,\log\Big(1+\frac{|{f}|}{\alpha\lambda}\Big)\,\text{\rm d}\mu

for every
\alpha>0
and every
f
in
L^{1}(S)
;

(ii)

\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(S)
for every
p
in
(1,\infty]
;

(iii)

\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
is not of weak-type
(1,1)
.

Notice that, in fact, only half balls with large radii (say greater than one) really matter. Indeed, denote by

\mathfrak{b}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}
the collection of all half balls
b_{R}(x)
for which
R\leq 1
and
R>1
, respectively,
and define, much as for
\mathfrak{b}
, the operators
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}_{0}}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}}
. These operators are the

local
and the
global
uncentred maximal operators associated to the family
\mathfrak{b}
.
A straightforward argument based on a partition of unity on
S
associated to balls of radius
1
with
the finite overlapping property and the local doubling property of the Riemannian measure shows that the operator
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}_{0}}

is of weak type
(1,1)
and bounded on
L^{p}(S)
for every
p
in
(1,\infty]
. Thus, we focus on the operator
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}}
,
and it suffices to prove Theorem
1.1
with
\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}
in place of
\mathfrak{b}
.

The main idea to prove Theorem
1.1
is to rely on the geometric approach to the
L^{p}
boundedness of the uncentred maximal
operator developed by A. Córdoba and R. Fefferman
[
CF
]
. Loosely speaking, given a
family
\mathfrak{F}
of subsets of a metric measure space
X
, the
L^{p}(X)
boundedness of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{F}}
for
p
in
(1,\infty)

is equivalent to a variant of a classical covering lemma in which mutual disjointness of subsets in any
appropriate subcollection
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
of a given collection
\mathfrak{G}
is replaced by appropriate estimates of

(1.4)

\left.{\Bigl\|{\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}}\,{\mathbbm{1}}_{b}\,}\Bigr\|_{p^{\prime}}}\right.\leq A_{p}\left.{\bigl\|{{\mathbbm{1}}_{G}}\bigr\|_{p}}\right.,

where
G
denotes the union of all
b
in
\mathfrak{G}
and
p^{\prime}
the index conjugate to
p
.
This estimate says that the balls in
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
have “finite overlapping in the
L^{p^{\prime}}
norm”. By Ionescu’s countererexample
and the Córdoba–Fefferman result, the analogue of (
1.4
) for balls fails for
p^{\prime}
in
(2,\infty)
.
So, retrospectively, our result says that on Damek–Ricci spaces half balls intersect “much less” than balls.
This phenomenon does not occur on Euclidean spaces.

To prove (
1.4
), we first show that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}}
can be estimated from above by an uncentred
maximal operator with respect to a different family of sets, and then show that this new family of sets satisfies
(
1.4
).

Our result has a geometric flavour that can be appreciated by a possibly wider audience in the special case of the
upper half plane. Indeed, in this case the sets involved in the proof of our main theorem can be quickly drawn and the arguments can be
followed more easily. For this reason we have chosen to dedicate a section to this special case.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
contains a description
of the families of sets in the hyperbolic upper half plane that are relevant for the proof of our main theorem.

the associated maximal operators.
The proof of parts
(i)
and
(ii)
of our main result is contained in Section
4
.
Section

is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1

(iii)
.
An interesting variant of
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{b}}
is considered in Section
6
.

We use the “variable constants convention”, and denote by
C
a constant whose actual value may vary from place to place
and which might depend on factors quantified before its occurrence, but not on factors quantified after.

The expression

A(t)\asymp B(t)\qquad\forall t\in{\mathbf{D}},

where
{\mathbf{D}}
is some subset of the domains of
A

B
, means that there exist (positive) constants
C

C\,A(t)\leq B(t)\leq C^{\prime}\,A(t)\qquad\forall t\in{\mathbf{D}};

2.
Relevant sets and reduction of the problem on
\mathbb{H}

The purpose of this section is to describe the families of sets in the
hyperbolic upper half plane that are relevant in the proof of our main result.
Most of the proofs of their properties will be omitted: we shall
give full proofs of the corresponding results in Damek–Ricci spaces.

It is well known that the distance
d
associated to the Riemannian metric (
1.2
) is given by

d(z,w)=2\,\mathrm{arsinh}\,\frac{|{z-w}|}{2\,\sqrt{\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z\,\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}w}}\qquad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{H}.

We denote by
B_{R}(z)
the (open) ball with centre
z
and radius
R
with respect to the distance
d
, and by

\mathfrak{B}
the family of all these balls.

We recall that the geodesics in
\mathbb{H}
are either straight vertical rays orthogonal to the
x
-axis, or Euclidean upper
half circles with centre on the
x
-axis. Given
z
in
\mathbb{H}

h(z):=\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z
; we call
h(z)

the
height
of

is the function
\log h

is equal to

if

For each

\mathcal{H}_{u}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{H}:h(z)=u\big\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Pi_{u}:=\big\{z\in\mathbb{H}:h(z)>u\big\}.

Notice that
\mathcal{H}_{u}
is a horocycle with respect to the point at infinity in the vertical direction.

For

\mathbb{H}

in
\mathbb{H}
lying
below the geodesic through

to the horocycle
\mathcal{H}_{\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z}

the upper half of
the Euclidean circle with centre
(\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z,0)
and radius
\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z
. Another way to describe

is the following:

(2.1)

T(z):=\big\{w\in\mathbb{H}:\bigl|{w-\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z}\bigr|<\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z\big\},

where
|{\cdot}|
denotes the complex modulus.

For each positive number
R
and for each point
z
in
\mathbb{H}
we consider

b_{R}(z):=B_{R}(z)\cap T(z),

which we call the Riemannian (lower)
half ball with centre
z

We observe that
b_{R}(z)
is a half ball in the geometric sense, because the intersection of the ball

Set

\mathfrak{b}:=\big\{b_{R}(z):z\in\mathbb{H},\,R>0\big\}.

It is well known that
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
acts isometrically on
\mathbb{H}
via fractional linear transformations.
Specifically, the matrix
M=\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{matrix}\right]
in

acts on the point

\mathbb{H}

M\cdot w:=\frac{\alpha w+\beta}{\gamma w+\delta}.

In particular, for each
z

\mathbb{H}

M_{z}:=\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z}&\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z/\sqrt{\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z}\\

and note that

M_{z}\cdot w=w\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z+\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{H}.

Clearly this action preserves the hyperbolic distance and the Riemannian area.

We now collect a few formulae which will be useful later. We have

d(iy_{1},iy_{2})=\log(y_{2}/y_{1})\qquad 0<y_{1}<y_{2}.

The geodesic ball

\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z+i(\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z)\cosh R\qquad\hbox{and radius}\qquad(\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z)\sinh R.

Thus, the boundary of

is the circle

consisting of all points

(x-\operatorname{\mathrm{Re}}z)^{2}+\big(y-(\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z)\,\cosh R\big)^{2}=(\operatorname{\mathrm{Im}}z)^{2}\,(\sinh R)^{2}.

It is straightforward to check that the point on this circle with lowest height is

and
that the horocycle

at this point.

Note that

(2.2)

We call an infinite rectangle

The following hold:

3.
Damek–Ricci spaces

Consider a real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
, equipped with a scalar product

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
, that can be written as an orthogonal direct sum, thus

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z},

where
[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}]\subseteq\mathfrak{z}
and
[\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{z}]=0=[\mathfrak{z},\mathfrak{z}]
. If either
\mathfrak{v}
or
\mathfrak{z}
is reduced to
0
,
then we say that
\mathfrak{n}
is
degenerate
. Denote by
p
and
q
the dimensions of
\mathfrak{v}
and
\mathfrak{z}
, respectively.
The
homogeneous dimension

\nu
of
\mathfrak{n}
is then defined as

\nu:=\frac{p}{2}+q.

For each
Z
in
\mathfrak{z}
, consider the
linear map
J_{Z}
on
\mathfrak{v}
, defined by

(3.1)

\left\langle J_{Z}X,X^{\prime}\right\rangle:=\left\langle Z,[X,X^{\prime}]\right\rangle\qquad\forall X,X^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{v}.

We shall assume throughout that
\mathfrak{n}
is
H
-type, i.e.

(3.2)

J_{Z}^{2}=-|{Z}|^{2}\,I,

where
I
denotes the identity mapping on
\mathfrak{v}
, and
|{Z}|^{2}
is short for
\left\langle Z,Z\right\rangle
. There is an extensive literature
on
H
-type groups, part of which can be found in the bibliographic references of
[
CDKR1
,
CDKR2
]
. We refer to these
