EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Stakeholder Engagement and Outreach Plan provides a comprehensive framework for engaging
stakeholders of the Illinois Storage Corridor (ISC) project. The ISC project is a CarbonSAFE Phase
III project designed to facilitate commercial deployment of carbon capture, utilization, and storage
(CCUS) in Illinois. The project aims to establish a multi-industry carbon storage corridor through
development of storage sites near the One Earth Energy (OEE) ethanol production facility in north-
central Illinois and the Prairie State Generating Company (PSGC) coal-fired power plant in south-
central Illinois, with combined annual CO₂ capture ultimately exceeding 8.6 million tons per year.
Stakeholder engagement is recognized as a critical component for successful CCUS deployment,
alongside technical and economic considerations. As an emerging technology, CCUS may not be
well understood by the general population, and lack of public awareness can lead to opposition that
poses significant barriers to project development. This plan addresses this challenge through
systematic stakeholder identification, analysis, planning, and implementation of engagement actions.
The plan is structured around four main sections: Communication, Stakeholder Analysis, Stakeholder
Engagement, and Environmental Justice. Activities will be conducted under Tasks 1 and 4 of the
project's Statement of Project Objectives, with two key subtasks: (1) developing a stakeholder
analysis and engagement plan through face-to-face meetings, facilitated discussions, and surveys;
and (2) implementing stakeholder engagement and public outreach activities including meetings,
open houses, and permit hearings.
The Illinois State Geological Survey (ISGS) will manage engagement activities following DOE-
NETL best practices, focusing on providing objective, fact-based information about CCUS and the
ISC project. A comprehensive Communication Plan establishes protocols for media contacts, site
visits, and crisis communications. The stakeholder analysis follows a structured workflow process
divided into Pre-feasibility and Feasibility phases, incorporating contextual understanding,
assessment, data collection, and analysis.
Key stakeholder groups include government bodies, educational organizations, conservation and
environmental groups, agricultural communities, and religious organizations. The plan addresses
common stakeholder questions regarding project risks, benefits, safety, property values, liability, and
environmental impacts. Recommendations emphasize developing clear messaging, creating
informational materials, and preparing to address both project-specific and broader environmental
concerns to ensure transparent communication and build stakeholder support throughout project
implementation.

THE ILLINOIS STORAGE CORRIDOR PROJECT
The Illinois Storage Corridor (ISC) project is designed to facilitate the commercial deployment of
carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS) within a region that has exhibited effective geologic
storage characteristics and with numerous industrial carbon sources. The project’s objective is to
establish a multi-industry carbon storage corridor through the development of a storage hub near the
One Earth Energy (OEE) facility in north-central Illinois and a storage site at the Prairie State
Generating Company (PSGC) site in south-central Illinois.
The ISC project (a CarbonSAFE Phase 3 project) builds on experience from projects in the region
that have proven the feasibility of commercial-scale storage within the Illinois Basin. This work
includes the Midwest Geological Sequestration Consortium subsurface evaluation of the Illinois
Basin, and the more recent CarbonSAFE Illinois Phase 1 and 2 projects that have expanded our
knowledge of the storage potential of the Corridor region.
The project team will perform detailed characterization at each site including acquiring new
subsurface data through seismic surveys, well drilling and testing, and computer simulations. The
planned combined annual CO2 captured by OEE and PSGC facilities will ultimately exceed 8.6
million tons per year. The capture sites are located directly above strata having a high likelihood of
excellent injection and containment characteristics minimizing the requirement for pipelines or other
transportation options. The ISC project will address all submission requirements for USEPA UIC
Class VI permitting and will develop workflows for effective site characterization and efficient
permitting for sites in areas of extensive storage complexes to accelerate deployment of CCUS.
Stakeholder engagement will be conducted for each site to ensure open discussion and to identify
potential challenges associated with regional carbon storage.

STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT INTRODUCTION
Along with technical and economic considerations, stakeholder engagement is considered a critical
component for successful deployment of CCUS. This process builds awareness and understanding of
the implications, both local and global, that deploying this technology entails. The ISC project
presents not only complex geologic considerations but presents unique challenges for engaging
stakeholders and generating an environment in which such a project can be undertaken. Stakeholders
have various and diverse options and perspectives, while also varying in influence and importance.
Understanding the nuances of stakeholder opinion, power, and perspective is necessary to the
development of a stakeholder engagement strategy that can reduce program/project risk, while
creating buy-in and understanding about program/project objectives. Lack of attention to stakeholder
engagement can increase project risk and create an environment in which public action moves against
project concepts. In severe cases, this can significantly impact or even derail a project from moving
forward. As an emerging technology, CCUS may not be well understood by the general population
and lack of public awareness can lead to fear or opposition. Such opposition can be a major barrier
to the development and implementation of CCUS projects. To move the ISC project to the next phase,
a strong stakeholder engagement strategy, including stakeholder analysis is needed.
Stakeholder engagement is critical to the success of the ISC. This stakeholder engagement and
outreach plan will outline the ISC engagement and outreach activities planned under Tasks 1 and 4
of the Statement of Project Objectives (SOPO) related to stakeholder interactions, public outreach,
communications, and environmental justice (collectively defined as stakeholder engagement). Most
activities will take place under Project Management (Task 1) and in Stakeholder Engagement and
Public Outreach (Task 4) through two subtasks.

Task 4.0 – Stakeholder Engagement and Public Outreach (BP1 and BP2)

Subtask 4.1 – Stakeholder Analysis and Engagement Plan. A stakeholder analysis of OEE and
PSGC sites will be conducted to understand key stakeholder needs, concerns, and challenges using
face-to-face meetings, facilitated discussions, and surveys. The assessment will contribute to risk
assessment (Task 3). A detailed engagement and outreach plan based on the stakeholder analysis will
be developed in conjunction with project partners. Outreach materials specific to project sites will be
developed leveraging knowledge sharing under the MGSC and MRCI to ensure open communication
of project goals and objectives. Materials will be revised as needed throughout the project as new
data and site activities occur. Deliverable – Stakeholder Engagement and Outreach Plan.
Subtask 4.2 – Conduct Stakeholder Engagement and Public Outreach. The stakeholder
engagement strategy and public outreach plans developed in Subtask 4.1 will be shared with regional
business development and project partners to ensure that best practices are considered in engagement
strategies. Stakeholder engagement activities the project team may be involved in include, but are
not limited to meetings, open houses, permit hearings, and events to ensure open project discussions.
This task contributes to the Public submission to support Class VI permitting requirements (Task 8).
Milestone – Conduct Stakeholder Engagement Activity.
For the purposes of this plan and planned activities, Stakeholder Engagement is defined as
 “…the systematic identification, analysis, planning and implementation of actions designed to
 engage stakeholders. A stakeholder engagement strategy identifies the needs of key groups.”
Where, Stakeholders are defined as:
 “…an individual or group that has an interest or role in a project, program or portfolio or are
 impacted by an organization’s activities.”
Stakeholders as defined by ISC include, but are not limited to, project partners, local residents and
organizations, regional, national, and international stakeholders, public interest groups, government
agencies, research communities, and the media.
This plan is divided into four main sections: 1) Communication, 2) Stakeholder Analysis, 3)
Stakeholder Engagement, and 4) Environmental Justice. These sections outline the Illinois State
Geological Survey (ISGS) management of planned (and emergent) engagement activities throughout
Budget Periods 1 and 2 (BP1, BP2). Note overlap is expected and encouraged across the four
sections.
The ISGS will conduct stakeholder engagement activities in conjunction with project partners and
using best practices as outlined in the U.S. Department of Energy – National Energy Technology
Laboratory (DOE-NETL) 2017 Revised Edition of the Best Practices: Public Outreach and
Education for Geologic Storage Projects.

COMMUNICATION
The ISGS has created and operates under a Communication Plan (submitted to DOE-NETL) which
defines the project information contacts, target audiences, media and data request response processes,
site visit protocols, and crisis communication actions. The purpose of the ISGS communication plan
is to ensure that the ISC project team provides relevant, accurate, and consistent project information
internally to project stakeholders and externally with project partners and project stakeholders.
Effective communication is essential for the project to realize its goals and establish support and
cooperation of each stakeholder group. The communication plan is intended to provide a framework
to manage and coordinate the wide variety of communications that take place during the project
lifetime.
All project personnel, partners, subcontractors, and vendors are expected to read this document to
understand the Communication Plan goals and objectives. All personnel are required to sign off that
they understand and agree to the Communications Plan, and it is used as the ISGS guideline.
All media and follow-up question requests are directed to a single point of contact, the Project
Coordinator, Michelle Johnson (mjohnso9@illinois.edu) +1 (217) 300-2925. Follow-up
communications are primarily handled by the Principal Investigator, Project Manager, or Co-
Principal Investigator.

Providing Fact-Based Information
The ISGS will focus on providing objective, unbiased, and fact-based information on CCUS and the
ISC project. External communications efforts are focused on general presentations, technical
brochures, papers, posters, selected industry and research conferences, sponsor meetings, as well as
other identified CCUS events in collaboration with industrial partners.

Site Visits
Site visits will be encouraged for both project locations. Site visits will be coordinated with the
respective site hosts and must be planned. Visitors must be accompanied by project personnel to gain
access to the project sites.

Crisis Communications
Crisis communications protocols are established during drilling and field operations. Information
related to crisis communication is revised and updated during these intensive periods of activity and
are distributed prior to drilling and posted at the drill site. Crisis responses to the media are managed
ONLY by specific personnel named above, and if anyone else is contacted to comment on any
situation deemed a “crisis,” the media contact is to be directed to one of the individuals listed above.
The crisis communications team will define a crisis plan to be implemented in the event of an
emergency.

STAKEHOLDER ANALYSIS
The ISC project will follow a workflow process for stakeholder analysis and engagement (Figure
1).The analysis sets the stage for identifying and understanding the specific stakeholder by site
context, demographics, familiarity with CCUS, a stakeholder identification table, and legacy
environmental issues along with the concerns of key stakeholders.
The Pre-feasibility process is broken down into three stages: 1) Contextual understanding and
parameter definition, 2) Assessment and data collection, and 3) Data analysis. The Feasibility
(implementation) Phase is broken down into 1) implementation, 2) Evaluation, and 3) Refinement.
The implementation of a stakeholder engagement process involves data collected and analyzed in the
Pre-feasibility Phase.

STAKEHOLDER ENGAGEMENT
Engaging with stakeholders about CCUS is critical as there is commonly confusion about the
subsurface, especially when trying to distinguish among subsurface activities, such as between CCS,
underground coal gasification (UCG), or hydraulic fracturing.
It is important to remember that CCUS is an issue with many actual and perceived risks and benefits
that may differ based on an individual’s geographic proximity to a project or their role in setting
policy or working at the project level.
The multitude of possible perspectives and opinions across stakeholder groups ensures that
understanding popular attitudes must remain at the forefront of the stakeholder engagement process.
Despite specific factors that may influence perception, experience has shown commonalities in the
questions asked by stakeholders regarding CCUS, including those listed here:
 • What is Carbon Capture Utilization and Storage?
 • How does it work?
 • What are the risks?
 • What are the benefits?
 • Will it impact my property value?
 • Who pays for it?
 • Who is responsible for CO2 once stored?
 • Will it cause earthquakes? What happens when you have an earthquake?
 • Will it damage my groundwater/drinking water?
 • Will it serve to continue the use fossil fuels at the expense of renewables? Will it
 enhance the economy of our region, state, or nation?
 • Does it enhance our energy security?
 • Will it change the ecosystem?
Thus, it will be important to ensure that educational materials (presentations, brochures, websites,
etc.) are designed to address these questions and other related topics that may arise when discussing
project specifics or providing general information about CCUS. Other topics that will likely be
important to address and engage around include:
 • Storage rights
 • Long-term liability
 • Surface and subsurface infrastructure (plant, pipeline, wells)
 • Geology – sources and sinks, plume extent, Area of Review
 • Permitting details and timeline
 • Process of carbon storage
 • Project details
 • Project risks and benefits
 • Related previous projects in this region and its impacts

Additional information relating to these topics and others identified through the stakeholder
engagement process will be recorded and responded to by the appropriate members of the
development team.

RECOMMENDATIONS
The following recommendations will be shared with OEE and PSGC for consideration when
developing their stakeholder engagement strategies:
 1. Determine messaging (could include):
 a. CCUS is a safe, demonstrated technology that works to mitigate climate change.
 b. CCUS will bring jobs [need analysis on jobs impact]

 2. Hire PR/Communications firm to manage engagement and communications process with
 stakeholders. Work with objective parties, including scientists, to create easy to read and
 understand materials.

 3. Create informational materials which include, but not limited to:
 a. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)
 b. Future of PSGC and contribution to net zero emissions goals
 c. Be able to answer the question of what happens if something goes wrong?
 d. When does the State take financial responsibility and how much cost to taxpayers?
 e. What is the level of risk?
 f. Earthquake/seismic
 g. Coal mine legacy
 h. Financial responsibility

 4. Issues may need to be prepared to address:
 a. Keeping boundaries around projects
 b. Provide support for key stakeholders to become sources of project information and
 project champions.
 c. Need to be prepared to answer non-project related environmental issues such as coal
 ash.
