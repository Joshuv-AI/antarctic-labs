Efficacy of a comprehensive medication management programme on medication adherence in patients with heart failure and polypharmacy (PERSEUS-HF): study protocol for a multicentre randomised controlled trial

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a significant global health problem, with an increasing prevalence and incidence due to an ageing population and increasing burden of cardiac and metabolic comorbidities.1 Despite the development of life-saving pharmacological and non-pharmacological therapies, patients with HF suffer from poor quality of life and high morbidity and mortality.2 3 Polypharmacy, often defined as taking five or more medications daily, is substantially common in patients with HF, particularly among elderly patients.4 Previous studies have reported that 77%–95% of patients with HF aged ≥65 years had polypharmacy.5,8 In addition to adherence to guideline-directed medical therapies (GDMT), this is related to the accumulation of medications prescribed to treat comorbidities, fragmented care across multiple specialties, the use of preventive and symptomatic medications and limited opportunities for deprescribing.5 9

Previous studies have consistently demonstrated an association between polypharmacy and worse clinical outcomes in patients with HF.56 9,11 Although this association is often considered a marker of disease severity or a greater burden of comorbid conditions, it is also possible that polypharmacy per se is the direct cause of adverse outcomes through mechanisms such as adverse drug interaction, electrolyte abnormalities, bleeding, renal dysfunction, falls, delirium or cognitive impairment.12 13 In patients with HF, in particular, polypharmacy may reduce adherence to GDMT and compromise its optimisation, potentially leading to worse clinical outcomes.9 14

Medication adherence programmes for patients with HF have focused primarily on patient education and medication reconciliation.15,21 There is an increasing recognition of the concept of potentially inappropriate medicines (PIMs) in older adults. However, there is less evidence regarding whether reduction or discontinuation of PIMs can improve medication adherence in patients with HF. We hypothesised that the deprescribing PIMs, regimen simplification and structured medication education and counselling would facilitate for the optimisation of GDMT, enhance medication adherence and ultimately improve the prognosis of patients with HF.4 13 16 This background provided the rationale for a randomised clinical trial in patients with HF, and here, we describe the design of the PERSEUS-HF (Polypharmacy Evaluation for improving adherence, Regimen Simplification, Enhancement and Utilization of Standard therapy in Heart Failure) trial.

Methods and analysis

PERSEUS-HF is a prospective, multicentre, stratified randomised, open-label trial testing whether a comprehensive medication management programme (CMMP) improves medication adherence at 6 months compared with standard of care in patients with HF and polypharmacy (figure 1). This trial was designed by members of the steering committee. The trial has been registered (jRCT1030250606).

Overall study design. Patients are randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either a CMMP (intervention group) or the standard of care (control group). The primary endpoint is changes in the ASK-20 score from baseline at 6 months. Study subjects

Patients who meet the following inclusion criteria are eligible: patients≥18 years of age, have an established diagnosis of HF irrespective of left ventricular ejection fraction (EF); take ≥five regular oral medications (ie, polypharmacy status)5 6 11 and provide a written informed consent before study participation. The diagnosis of HF is made based on the Universal Definition and Classification of HF.22

The key exclusion criteria include cognitive impairment that prevents completion of the medication adherence questionnaire, comorbidities with a projected life expectancy <6 months, current enrolment in other interventional trials or being deemed ineligible for study participation by investigators.

Enrollment in PERSEUS-HF began on 6 January 2026, after approval by the institutional review board. Participants will be enrolled at 13 centres in Japan (nine university hospitals, two public hospitals and two private hospitals). The modifications implemented in the PEUSEUS-HF protocol amendments included the addition of three secondary outcomes ((#2) changes in total barrier count from baseline to 6 months, (#14) changes in total barrier count from baseline to 12 months and (#18) identifying optimal medication management strategies using artificial intelligence (AI) to maximise changes in the Adherence Starts with Knowledge 20 (ASK-20) score), revisions to the data-sharing policy, clarification of the AI-based analytical plans and the inclusion of sensitivity analyses for dropout cases. This study is being conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study design and intervention

The overall study design is presented in figure 2. After screening, eligible patients are randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either a CMMP (intervention group) or standard of care (control group). Random sequence generation and treatment allocation are performed centrally using a web-based automated clinical research support system (UHCT eACReSS). Randomisation is stratified by age (≥75 or <75 years) and EF (≥50 or <50%). The CMMP consists of the following (table 1): (1) reducing or discontinuing medications, primarily PIMs; (2) providing two sessions of structured medication education and counselling (one at baseline and one at the 2- or 4-month visit); (3) reducing the number of dosing times; (4) using polypill; (5) switching from regular to as-needed medications; (6) using unit-dose packaging and (7) GDMT optimisation.23 PIMs are identified according to the American Geriatrics Society 2023 updated Beers Criteria.24 In the CMMP group, in-person study visits occur every 2 months during the ﬁrst 6 months to optimise and simplify the medication regimen. Our structured education and counselling programme aims to achieve the following goals: recognising the importance of GDMT, understanding PIMs and identifying each patient’s barriers to medication adherence and setting actionable goals. The programme uses standardised materials for this trial. A patient questionnaire regarding medication was conducted in advance of this study to facilitate patient and public involvement. The majority of patients expressed a desire to consult their attending physician if they felt anxious about discontinuing long-term medication. Consequently, the attending physician was designated as the primary point of contact for consultations regarding discontinuation of medication. Patients in the standard of care group will receive GDMT, as well as outpatient HF education as part of standard outpatient care and medication management guidance from the dispensing pharmacy. After the 6-month intervention period ends, study participants will be followed in a non-intervention manner until the 12-month visit.

Patients are asked to complete the ASK-20 at baseline and at 6 and 12 months. The ASK-20 is a validated, 20-item self-reported questionnaire used to identify patient-specific barriers to medication adherence.25 ASK-20 has been reported to correlate with medication adherence for respiratory diseases and is an important factor in determining treatment success.26 27 The internal consistency and reliability of the ASK-20 have been confirmed in both American and Japanese populations.27,29 Patients are required to complete this questionnaire without contact from the physician or pharmacist responsible for their treatment.

Given the scope of this trial, a secondary objective of PERSEUS-HF includes the evaluation of the time-to-first occurrence of hospitalisations due to the following within 12 months: fractures, falls, delirium/impaired consciousness, cognitive decline, syncope, bleeding, infections, nutritional disorders/anorexia, electrolyte abnormalities, blood glucose abnormalities, renal impairment, hepatic impairment, drug allergies, constipation/intestinal obstruction and drug-induced movement disorders.12

Artificial intelligence-based exploratory Analysis

As a prespecified secondary endpoint, this study will use an AI approach to identify optimal medication management strategies that maximise patient adherence (table 2). Specifically, conventional machine learning algorithms, such as gradient boosting models, will be developed to predict changes in the total ASK-20 score. These models will be trained on detailed medication data, including the deprescribing of PIMs, regimen simplification and GDMT optimisation. To ensure model interpretability, feature importance and SHapley Additive exPlanations values will be evaluated to elucidate which specific components of the medication management programme contribute most significantly to improved adherence. Safety

All unplanned hospitalisations during the intervention period (up to 6 months) are collected. This includes the severity of the events, their relationship to the trial intervention and the outcome.

Data of interest collected

Given the interest in this trial, comprehensive medication data will be collected. This includes detailed information on the dosage and administration of all regularly taken oral medications, PIMs, the frequency of daily dosing, the number of prescribing physicians involved and the provision of medication packaging. Oral medications will be categorised as cardiovascular or non-cardiovascular based on previous studies.5 6 Furthermore, this study will assess cognitive function, frailty, sarcopenia and malnutrition using the Mini-Cog, Makizako’s 5-item questionnaire, the Japanese Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS) and grip strength, as these factors potentially influence medication adherence.

Statistical considerations

The main analysis population will consist of all enrolled participants, except for those who are found to be ineligible after registration (intention-to-treat analysis). In addition, a per-protocol set, excluding participants who do not complete the ASK-20 assessment at 6 months, will be analysed in the same manner. The primary endpoint analysis (ie, changes in total ASK-20 score at 6 months) will be performed using analysis of covariance, with baseline values included as a covariate. Assuming CMMP produces a clinically meaningful 5-point improvement in the total ASK-20 score at 6 months, with an estimated SD of 15, a total sample size of 284 participants is required to achieve 80% power (two-sided α=0.05) for detecting this magnitude of improvement with CMMP as compared with standard of care.26 27 With a projected 5% dropout rate, the final target enrolment was set at 300 patients (150 per group). To address potential biases from the projected dropout, extreme-case scenario sensitivity analyses will be performed. In the worst-case scenario, missing 6-month ASK-20 scores in the intervention group will be imputed with the worst observed value among all participants, while missing scores in the control group will be imputed with the best observed value. The best-case scenario will apply the exact opposite imputation.

The prespecified subgroups of interest are age, women, living alone, degree of social frailty, baseline total ASK-20 score, EF≥50%, baseline N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide levels, baseline eGFR, baseline Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index and baseline CFS score.

Discussion

Although the development of GDMTs has reduced morbidities and mortality for patients with HF, the full therapeutic benefits depend critically on sufficient medication adherence and successful up-titration to target doses.9 Patients with HF often have multiple cardiac and metabolic comorbidities, including coronary artery disease, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, obesity and chronic kidney disease.30 In particular, older HF patients frequently suffer from chronic health conditions such as chronic pain, musculoskeletal problems, insomnia, gastrointestinal issues or mental health conditions (eg, depression or anxiety). These issues lead to the prescription of a large number of cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular medications, resulting in polypharmacy.

Polypharmacy is currently receiving substantial attention in the management of HF due to its high prevalence, increased risk of adverse drug interaction, negative effects on GDMT adherence and association with poor clinical outcomes.4 Notably, polypharmacy is associated with undertreatment of GDMT in patients with HF, especially those taking multiple non-cardiovascular medications.14 In addition, multiple use of medications may cause drug-related adverse events specific to HF patients, such as electrolyte disturbance, renal dysfunction, worsening HF, bleeding or hypotension. These polypharmacy-related adverse effects may contribute to poorer outcomes in patients with HF.10

The European Society of Cardiology Heart Failure Association has recently published a consensus statement to call for strategies to manage polypharmacy and reduce pill burden. This consensus emphasises prioritising medication appropriateness over pill count, integrating comorbidity management with HF-beneficial therapies and implementing structured deprescribing and multidisciplinary medication review to optimise GDMT while mitigating polypharmacy. Despite the clear clinical needs, robust evidence from randomised controlled trials examining effective interventions for polypharmacy in HF patients remains lacking.4

Previous studies targeting medication adherence in patients with HF have primarily focused on patient education and medication reconciliation (eg, those using coaching, education or reminder strategies).15,19 Specifically, the PHARMacy-based prospective randomized controlled interdisciplinary study for ambulatory patients with Chronic Heart Failure trial, a prospective, randomised, controlled, open-label clinical trial, showed that pharmacy-led medication management improved GDMT adherence in patients with chronic HF.20 21 PERSEUS-HF trial integrates three essential components of polypharmacy management in HF: reducing PIMs, optimising GDMT and simplifying regimens. In addition to educating the importance of GDMT in HF, we hypothesise that providing education and counselling on PIMs, followed by deprescribing PIMs, may increase the opportunities to optimise GDMT and reduce adverse drug interactions. To achieve this, PIMs will be identified according to the established Beers criteria. Another key aspect of the PERSEUS-HF design incorporates regimen simplification strategies, including reduction in dosing frequency, use of polypill, switching to as-needed administration and unit-dose packaging. AI technology has the potential to support the management of polypharmacy in patients with HF. The PERSEUS-HF trial is unique because it establishes AI-based polypharmacy strategies as a key exploratory objective.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. The open-label design, necessitated by the behavioural nature of the intervention, introduces performance bias. Relying on the self-reported ASK-20 questionnaire for the primary endpoint carries a risk of detection bias. While widely validated, the ASK-20 lacks formal validation specifically for HF. To mitigate these subjective biases, an objective 28-day medication adherence rate is evaluated as a prespecified secondary endpoint. The 6-month primary follow-up may be relatively short to evaluate the long-term sustainability of improved medication adherence. Patients with cognitive impairment that prevents completion of the ASK-20 are excluded to ensure the validity and reliability. Because cognitive impairment is closely related to poor adherence in older patients with HF, this exclusion may limit the generalisability of our results to the most vulnerable populations. The prespecified subgroups of interest were selected based on their known clinical relevance to polypharmacy and medication adherence in patients with HF. However, given the limited sample size and statistical power to detect interaction effects, all subgroup analyses will be treated as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Finally, because the trial is conducted within the Japanese healthcare system, the generalisability of the findings to other countries may be limited.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has been approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Gunma University Hospital, with the participants’ hospitals approving the execution of the study (IRB2025-070) and has been registered at the Japanese clinical trial registry (jRCT1030250606). The trial is being conducted in compliance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to their enrolment. All participants’ data will be recorded in a secure, password-protected electronic data capture system. System access will be restricted via role-based permissions, and a comprehensive audit trail will automatically document all data modifications to ensure data integrity. All data will be stored in strict compliance with relevant data protection regulations. The results of this trial, regardless of the findings, will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at scientific conferences.

Patient and public involvement

To integrate patient perspectives into the trial design, a pre-study questionnaire regarding polypharmacy and deprescribing was conducted among 20 outpatients with HF. The survey explored patients’ anxieties about taking multiple medications and their willingness and concerns regarding medication reduction. The results highlighted that while patients were interested in reducing their pill burden, a majority strongly preferred to consult their attending physician if they felt anxious about discontinuing long-term medications. Consequently, this feedback directly shaped our intervention design. The CMMP was structured to designate the attending physician as the primary point of contact for consultations regarding medication discontinuation.
