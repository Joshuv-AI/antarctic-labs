Abstract
. This paper proposes a new randomized design of digital nets in which the generating matrices are chosen to be random Hankel matrices. Compared with previous randomized design of digital nets, this approach simplifies the construction process and reduces the number of random variables required, while still achieving desirable convergence rates when combined with appropriate estimators. We analyze the properties of the proposed design, derive bounds for Walsh coefficients, and provide error analysis for both the median-of-means estimator and a newly proposed greedy selection estimator, i.e. the selection of the best design from a batch in terms of a worst-case error bound. Numerical experiments validate our theoretical findings and demonstrate the practical performance of the proposed methods.

1.
Introduction

Digital nets are an important class of low-discrepancy sequences that are widely used in quasi-Monte Carlo methods for numerical integration. The concept of digital nets originated from the work of Sobol’
[
33
]
, is later generalized by Niederreiter
[
23
,
24
]
, leading to the development of
(t,m,s)
-nets and
(t,s)
-sequences. Optimized construction of the digital nets in base 2 in terms of two-dimensional projections is studied by Joe and Kuo
[
19
]
, which is also widely implemented in various software packages up to dimension 21,201.

Recently, there has been growing interest in randomized designs of digital nets, where the generating matrices are chosen randomly. This approach can simplify the construction process and provide good average-case performance—as well as probabilistic worst-case error guarantees—without the need for intricate deterministic designs. Goda and L’Ecuyer
[
12
]
and Goda, Suzuki, and Matsumoto
[
14
]
proposed a randomized design of (higher-order) polynomial lattice rules, a special class of digital nets, by randomly selecting numerator and denominator polynomials from specific admissible sets. Pan and Owen
[
30
,
31
]
introduced a
uniform random design
(URD) of base-
2
generating matrices, where each entry in the matrix is independently chosen from
\{0,1\}
. Both approaches require no construction parameters and achieve desirable convergence rates when combined with the median-of-means estimator.

In this work, we propose a randomized design of digital nets in which the entries of generating matrices are chosen randomly to form a Hankel matrix structure. We refer to this construction as the
Hankel random design
(HRD) throughout this paper. This approach serves as a bridge between the randomized polynomial lattice rules
[
12
,
14
]
and the URD
[
30
,
31
]
. While the former’s generating matrices are inherently Hankel but constrained by the algebraic properties of the chosen polynomials, and the latter’s are entirely unstructured, our design exploits the Hankel structure to maintain a degree of algebraic regularity while allowing for greater flexibility through randomness. Consequently, compared to the previously mentioned random designs, our approach is simpler to implement, involves fewer random variables, and improves certain error bounds.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary background and notation for digital nets and their randomizations. In Section
3
, we present our proposed HRD, analyze its properties, compare with the uniform random design and revisit the linear matrix scrambling. In Section
4
we derive the bounds for Walsh coefficients, which are used to the error analysis in Section
5
for both the median-of-means estimator and newly proposed best design from a batch estimator. Finally, we present numerical experiments in Section
6
to validate our theoretical findings and demonstrate the practical performance of the proposed methods.

2.2.
Digital net construction and randomizations

In this section, we briefly discuss the digital net construction and randomization. For a more comprehensive treatment of digital nets and sequences, we refer the reader to the monographs
[
7
,
24
]
.

Let
b
be a fixed prime number, and let
m,s
be positive integers. A digital net is a point set of
N=b^{m}
points in
[0,1)^{s}
constructed using linear algebra over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{b}
. The construction is defined by
s
generating matrices
C_{1},\dots,C_{s}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E\times m}
, where
E
is the precision and
m
determines the quadrature size, typically
E\geqslant m
.

Now we describe the procedure to generate the
j
-th coordinate of the
n
-th point
\bm{x}_{n}
, denoted by
x_{n,j}
, where
j=1,\dots,s
and
n=0,\dots,b^{m}-1
. We first represent
n
in its base-
b
expansion:

(2)

n=n_{0}+n_{1}b+\cdots+n_{m-1}b^{m-1},

which corresponds to a vector
\vec{n}_{m}=(n_{0},\dots,n_{m-1})^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
. Then, we compute a vector of digits
\vec{y}_{n,j}=(y_{n,j,1},\dots,y_{n,j,E})^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E}
via the matrix-vector multiplication over
\mathbb{F}_{b}
:

(3)

\vec{y}_{n,j}=C_{j}\vec{n}_{m}.

Here, if
E=\infty
, we assume that every column of the generating matrices has only finitely many non-zero entries to ensure the well-definedness of this matrix-vector multiplication. Finally, these digits are used to form the coordinate
x_{n,j}
by mapping them to a real number in
[0,1)
:

(4)

x_{n,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{E}y_{n,j,k}b^{-k}.

Applying the above procedure to all
j=1,\dotsc,s
and
n=0,\dotsc,b^{m}-1
yields the resulting digital net
\{\bm{x}_{0},\dots,\bm{x}_{b^{m}-1}\}
.

As mentioned in the introduction, recent works explore randomized construction of digital nets through randomly chosen generating matrices
C_{1},\dotsc,C_{s}
. Goda and L’Ecuyer
[
12
]
and Goda, Suzuki, and Matsumoto
[
14
]
proposed random designs of (higher-order) polynomial lattice rules, a special class of digital nets proposed by Niederreiter
[
23
]
and then generalized by Dick and Pillichshammer
[
6
]
. In particular, the numerator and denominator polynomials are randomly selected from a specific admissible set. However, the requirement that the denominator polynomial be irreducible makes this approach less straightforward. Pan and Owen
[
30
,
31
]
consider a URD of base-
2
generating matrices: each entry in the matrices is randomly selected in
\{0,1\}
. This design is more straightforward to implement. Notably, when combined with the median-of-means estimator, these random designs achieve nearly optimal convergence rates for smooth functions. A significant advantage of this approach is its universality with respect to smoothness: it attains these rates without prior knowledge of the function’s smoothness, which is typically required in existing constructions of optimal deterministic/randomized digital nets
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
10
,
15
]
.

The random design we introduce in this work combines the algebraic structure of polynomial lattice rules with the implementation simplicity of uniform random designs. We describe the details of this construction and its theoretical properties in the next section.

3.
Randomized design of digital nets

In this section, we first introduce our HRD of digital nets. Then, we analyze its properties and compare it with the URD. Lastly, we revisit the properties of linear matrix scrambling
[
21
]
and relate them to Owen-scrambling
[
25
,
26
]
.

3.1.
Hankel random design of digital nets

We describe the proposed randomized design of digital nets. We present the procedure in one dimension as the construction is identical and independent across dimensions.

We generate the entries of the generating matrix
C\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E\times m}
using a random vector
\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dotsc,u_{E+m-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E+m-1}
, where each component
u_{i}
is chosen independently and uniformly from
\mathbb{F}_{b}
. The entries of
C
are given by

(5)

c_{j,r}=u_{r+j-1},

for
1\leqslant j\leqslant E
and
1\leqslant r\leqslant m
. Notice that
C
has a Hankel structure that also arises in polynomial lattice rules. Then, the point construction, as in Equation (
3
), can be written as

(6)

\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}&u_{2}&\cdots&u_{m}\\
u_{2}&u_{3}&\cdots&u_{m+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{E}&u_{E+1}&\cdots&u_{E+m-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}n_{0}\\
n_{1}\\
\vdots\\
n_{m-1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\\
\vdots\\
y_{E}\end{bmatrix}

Similar to linear matrix scrambling
[
21
]
, we further introduce a random digital shift
\vec{d}_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E}
, drawn independently and uniformly from
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{E}
for each
j=1,\dotsc,s
. This shift is applied to all points:

(7)

\vec{y}_{n,j}=C_{j}\vec{n}_{m}\oplus\vec{d}_{j},

where
\oplus
denotes addition over
\mathbb{F}_{b}
. This additional randomization ensures that each individual point
\bm{x}_{n}
is uniformly distributed in
[0,1)^{s}
for
E=\infty
(or
b^{-E}
-equidistributed for finite
E
) marginally, while preserving the structural properties of the digital net collectively. A fast implementation of the construction procedure is presented in Appendix
A
.

With the digital net
\{\bm{x}_{n}\}
generated randomly by (
7
), the sample average

(8)

Q_{N}(f)\;=\;\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{b^{m}-1}f(\bm{x}_{n}).

is an unbiased estimator of the integral

I(f)=\int_{[0,1)^{s}}f(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

for any
f\in L^{1}([0,1)^{s})
, where we write
N=b^{m}
.

Before analyzing the integration error, we introduce the Walsh functions in base
b
.

Definition 1
.

For an integer base
b\geqslant 2
, let
\omega_{b}=e^{2\pi i/b}
be the primitive
b
-th root of unity. For a non-negative integer
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
b
-adic expansion
k=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}k_{[i]}b^{i-1}
(where
k_{[i]}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}
and the sum is finite) and a real number
x\in[0,1)
with
b
-adic expansion
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{[i]}b^{-i}
(unique under the condition that infinite trails of
b-1
are not allowed), the
k
-th Walsh function is defined as

{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k}(x)\coloneqq\omega_{b}^{k_{[1]}x_{[1]}+k_{[2]}x_{[2]}+\cdots}.

For dimension
s\geqslant 1
,
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{s})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, and
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in[0,1)^{s}
, the multidimensional Walsh function is defined as the product

{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{s}{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k_{j}}(x_{j}).

It is known that the set
\{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\bm{k}}\mid\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\}
forms a complete orthonormal basis of
L_{2}([0,1)^{s})
; see, e.g.,
[
7
, Appendix A]
.
Accordingly, for a function
f\in L_{2}([0,1)^{s})
, the
\bm{k}
-th Walsh coefficient is given by

\hat{f}(\bm{k})=\int_{[0,1)^{s}}f(\bm{x})\,\overline{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

and we have the following equality in the
L_{2}
sense:

f(\bm{x})\sim\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\,{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x}).

Now, assuming that
f
can be represented by its pointwise absolutely convergent Walsh series, for any realization of the randomized digital net with a random digital shift, the integration error can be expressed as

(9)

\begin{split}I(f)-Q_{N}(f)&=I(f)-\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{b^{m}-1}f(\bm{x}_{n})\\
&=I(f)-\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{b^{m}-1}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}\cdot(C_{j}\vec{n}+\vec{d}_{j})}\\
&=-\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\ \omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}\vec{d}_{j}}\ \frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{n=0}^{b^{m}-1}\omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}C_{j}\vec{n}}\\
&=-\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\ \omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}\vec{d}_{j}},\end{split}

where the dual net
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
is defined as

\mathcal{D}^{\perp}=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(C_{1},\ldots,C_{s})\;=\;\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}:\,\sum_{j=1}^{s}{C}_{j}^{\top}\vec{k}_{j,E}=\bm{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}\right\}.

The last equality in (
9
) follows from the character property of digital nets
[
7
]
, where the inner sum vanishes unless
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
. Note that the integration error in (
9
) vanishes in expectation over the random digital shift
d_{j}
, consistent with the unbiasedness of
Q_{N}(f)
.

The next lemma gives the probability that a fixed frequency
\bm{k}\neq\bm{0}
belongs to the dual net
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
with respect to the random choice of the Hankel matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
.

Lemma 1
.

Consider the HRD of digital nets with the infinite-precision generating matrices
C_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times m}
for
j=1,\ldots,s
. For any fixed
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}
, we have

(10)

\Pr(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})=\Pr\nolimits_{C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}}(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})=b^{-m}.

Proof.

We first consider the one-dimensional case. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k<b^{n}
. Recall the notation
\vec{k}_{\infty}=(k_{[1]},k_{[2]},\dotsc,k_{[n]},0,\dotsc,0)^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty}
. We aim to show that
C^{\top}\vec{k}_{\infty}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
. Due to the Hankel structure of
C
, the product
C^{\top}\vec{k}_{\infty}
can be equivalently written as
\bm{M}\bm{u}
, where
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n-1})^{\top}
and
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m\times(m+n-1)}
is given by

(11)

\bm{M}=\begin{bmatrix}k_{[1]}&k_{[2]}&k_{[3]}&\cdots&k_{[n]}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&k_{[1]}&k_{[2]}&\cdots&k_{[n-1]}&k_{[n]}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\cdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&k_{[1]}&k_{[2]}&k_{[3]}&\cdots&k_{[n]}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

In the following, we show that
\bm{M}
has full row rank
m
.

Suppose there exists a vector
\bm{c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{m-1})^{\top}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
such that
\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{M}=\bm{0}
. Let

(12)

K(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{[i]}x^{i}\quad\text{and}\quad C(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}c_{j}x^{j}

be polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{b}[x]
. The condition
\bm{c}^{\top}\bm{M}=\bm{0}
is equivalent to stating that all coefficients of the product polynomial
P(x)=C(x)K(x)
are zero. Since
\mathbb{F}_{b}[x]
is an integral domain and
K(x)\neq 0
(as
k\neq 0
), the product
C(x)K(x)=0
implies
C(x)=0
. Thus,
c_{0}=c_{1}=\dots=c_{m-1}=0
, proving that
\bm{M}
has full row rank
m
.

Since
\bm{M}
is surjective and
\bm{u}
is chosen uniformly at random from
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m+n-1}
, the linear transformation
\bm{M}\bm{u}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
.
Thus, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Pr(C^{\top}\vec{k}_{\infty}=\bm{0})=\Pr(\bm{M}\bm{u}=\bm{0})=b^{-m}
.

In the multidimensional case, since the generating matrices
C_{j}
are chosen independently for
j=1,\ldots,s
, the vectors
C_{j}^{\top}\vec{{k}}_{j,\infty}
are independent and each is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
. Since the sum of independent uniform random variables over a finite group is also uniform, we conclude that
\Pr(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})=b^{-m}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we investigate the
L^{2}
error (mean square error) of the estimator. Using the error expression in (
9
) and the independence of
C_{j}
and
d_{j}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(I(f)-Q_{N}(f))^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\ \omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}\vec{d}_{j}}\right)\left({\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}\hat{f}(\bm{\ell})\ \omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{\ell}_{j}^{\top}\vec{d}_{j}}}\right)^{*}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}\mathbbm{1}_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}}\right]}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\left({\hat{f}(\bm{\ell})}\right)^{*}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\omega_{b}^{\sum_{j=1}^{s}(\vec{k}_{j}-\vec{\ell}_{j})^{\top}\vec{d}_{j}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}\Pr\nolimits_{C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}}(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})\,\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=b^{-m}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}={N}^{-1}{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[f\right]},

where we used, in the third equality, the fact that the expectation with respect to the digital shift
d_{j}
vanishes unless
k_{j}=\ell_{j}
for all
j
, in which case it equals
1
.

This shows that the basic HRD of digital nets only achieves the same variance as the standard Monte Carlo. In the rest of the paper, we introduce additional techniques, such as the median-of-means and greedy optimization, to improve the error rate. Before that, we study probability bounds for a certain event in the next subsection.

3.2.
The probability of a bad event

Following Pan and Owen
[
30
,
31
]
, certain randomized digital nets exhibit events that dominate the integration error. Particularly, the events correspond to vectors
\bm{k}
belonging to the dual net whose Walsh coefficients
\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert
have larger magnitude. Here, we compare the probability of such an event between HRD and URD.

We define the event

\mathcal{A}_{K}=\bigcup_{\bm{k}\in K}\{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\},

where
K\subset\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}
is a given finite set. A crude upper bound follows from the union bound:
\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K})\leqslant\sum_{\bm{k}\in K}\Pr(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})=\lvert K\rvert b^{-m}
, where we used Lemma
1
in the last equality. This bound applies to both HRD and URD. To obtain a sharper bound, we study the joint inclusion probabilities of pairs of distinct vectors in the dual net, i.e.,
\Pr(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})
.

First, we consider the event
\{\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}_{X}\}
for distinct
\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}
, where
X=U
or
H
denotes URD or HRD, respectively. For both types of random design, this event is equivalent to

(13)

\sum_{j=1}^{s}C_{j}^{\top}K_{j}=\bm{0}_{m\times 2},

where
C_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times m}
is either the URD or HRD generating matrix,
K_{j}=[\vec{k}_{1,j},\vec{k}_{2,j}]\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times 2}
be the matrix formed by stacking the vectors.

We let
Y_{j}=C_{j}^{\top}K_{j}
, and define the linear transformation
T_{j}:\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times m}\to\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m\times 2}
by
T_{j}(C_{j})=Y_{j}
. Then each
Y_{j}
is uniform on the linear subspace
\mathrm{Im}(T_{j})
. Since the matrices
C_{j}
are independent, each
Y_{j}
is independent and thus

(14)

\sum_{j=1}^{s}C_{j}^{\top}K_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}Y_{j}

is uniformly distributed on the linear subspace
V_{\Sigma}:=V_{1}+V_{2}+\cdots+V_{s}
, where
V_{j}=\mathrm{Im}(T_{j})
. In general, the dimension of
V_{\Sigma}
satisfies:

(15)

\max_{j}\dim V_{j}\leqslant\dim(V_{\Sigma})\leqslant\min\left(2m,\sum_{j=1}^{s}\dim V_{j}\right)

The Chung–Erdős bound
[
2
]
and Hunter’s bound
[
18
]
provide lower and upper bounds for
\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K})
, respectively, using the joint inclusion probabilities:

(16)

\begin{split}\frac{\left(\sum_{i\in K}\Pr(\bm{k}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})\right)^{2}}{\sum_{i\in K}\Pr(\bm{k}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})+\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\in K,i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\Pr(\bm{k}_{i_{1}},\bm{k}_{i_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})}\leqslant\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}),\\
\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K})\leqslant\sum_{i\in K}\Pr(\bm{k}_{i}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})-\max_{T}\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2})\in T}\Pr(\bm{k}_{i_{1}},\bm{k}_{i_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}),\end{split}

where the maximum is taken over all spanning trees
T
on the complete graph with the vertex set
K
, and
(i_{1},i_{2})
denotes an edge in
T
.

In the following, we first discuss the one-dimensional case (
s=1
). For URD, each component in
C_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times m}
is i.i.d. Thus,

(17)

\dim V_{j}=\begin{cases}m,&\text{if }\operatorname{rank}(K_{j})=1,\\[4.0pt]
2m,&\text{if }\operatorname{rank}(K_{j})=2,\end{cases}

For HRD, the dimension of
V_{j}
depends on the rank of
K_{j}
.
If
\operatorname{rank}(K_{j})=1
, then
\dim V_{j}=m
.
If
\operatorname{rank}(K_{j})=2
, then

m+1\leqslant\dim V_{j}\leqslant 2m.

We can show that
\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{H})\leqslant\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{U})
as
m\to\infty
. For simplicity, let us consider
b=2
. In this case, there is no linearly dependent pair
({k}_{i_{1}},{k}_{i_{2}})
for distinct
i_{1},i_{2}\in K
. Then, for URD,
\dim(V_{\Sigma})=\dim(V_{1})=2m
.
From the inequalities (
16
), we have a lower bound

(18)

\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{U})\geqslant\frac{\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}}{1+(\lvert K\rvert-1)2^{-m-1}},

and an upper bound

(19)

\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{U})\leqslant\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}-(\lvert K\rvert-1)2^{-2m}.

Thus
\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{U})={\Theta}\left(\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}\right)
.

For HRD, suppose that there exist
i_{1}=2^{m_{1}},i_{2}=2^{m_{2}}\in K
, such that
d=\left\lvert m_{1}-m_{2}\right\rvert<m
. Then
\dim(V_{1})=m+d
for the pair
(k_{i_{1}},k_{i_{2}})
. Applying the inequalities (
16
), we have an upper bound

(20)

\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{H})\leqslant\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}-2^{-(m+d)}-(\lvert K\rvert-2)2^{-2m}\leqslant\lvert K-2^{-d}\rvert 2^{-m}.

Then we have the following inequality

(21)

\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{H})\leqslant\lvert K-2^{-d}\rvert 2^{-m}\leqslant\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}\leqslant\frac{\lvert K\rvert 2^{-m}}{1+(\lvert K\rvert-1)2^{-m-1}}\leqslant\Pr(\mathcal{A}_{K}^{U}),

for
\lvert K\rvert\geqslant 1
and
m\geqslant 0
. This demonstrates the advantage of HRD over URD in one-dimensional case.

The distinction between HRD and URD is less pronounced in higher dimensions, since in both cases, we have

(22)

m\leqslant\dim(V_{\Sigma})\leqslant 2m,

and the joint inclusion probabilities satisfy

(23)

b^{-2m}\leqslant\Pr(\bm{k}_{i},\bm{k}_{j}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}_{X})\leqslant b^{-m}.

Nonetheless, our numerical results in Section
6
indicate that HRD yields smaller worst-case error bounds than URD in practice.

3.3.
The
t
parameter

In this section, we show that the
t
parameter, a quality measure for the equidistribution properties of digital nets, is small with high probability for the HRD.
Here, the quality parameter
t
satisfies that
C_{\bm{q}}
has full row rank whenever
\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}\leqslant m-t
. The definition of the stacked matrix
C_{\bm{q}}
can be referred in Section
2
.

In the following, we examine the probability that
C_{\bm{q}}
has full row rank. First, the union bound leads to

(24)

\Pr\left(\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{q}})<\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}\right)=\Pr\left(\bigcup_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\left(\bm{y}^{\top}C_{\bm{q}}=\bm{0}\right)\right)\leqslant\sum_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\Pr\left(\bm{y}^{\top}C_{\bm{q}}=\bm{0}\right).

We now want to show that, for each nonzero column vector
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}}
,

\Pr\left(\bm{y}^{\top}C_{\bm{q}}=\bm{0}\right)=b^{-m}.

Denote
\bm{y}^{\top}=(\bm{y}_{1}^{\top},...,\bm{y}_{s}^{\top})
, where
\bm{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{q_{j}}
. We also denote
\bm{v}_{j}^{\top}=\bm{y}_{j}^{\top}C_{j}(1{:}q_{j},:)
for
j=1,\dotsc,s
. Then we have

(25)

\bm{y}^{\top}C_{\bm{q}}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\bm{v}_{j}^{\top}.

Notice that if
\bm{y}_{j}\neq\bm{0}
, under the randomized design of matrices
C_{1},...,C_{s}
, each
\bm{v}_{j}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
. Thus for
\bm{y}\neq\bm{0}
, which implies that at least one
\bm{y}_{j}\neq\bm{0}
,
j=1,\dotsc,s
, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{s}\bm{v}_{j}^{\top}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{m}
. Thus,

(26)

\Pr\left(\bm{y}^{\top}C_{\bm{q}}=\bm{0}\right)=b^{-m}.

Consequently,

(27)

\Pr\left(\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{q}})<\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}\right)\leqslant(b^{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}}-1)b^{-m}.

The probability that
t>t_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Pr(t>t_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}=m-t_{0}}\Pr\left(\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{q}})<m-t_{0}\right)

\displaystyle={{m-t_{0}+s-1}\choose{s-1}}(b^{\lvert\bm{q}\rvert_{1}}-1)b^{-m}

\displaystyle={{m-t_{0}+s-1}\choose{s-1}}\left(b^{-t_{0}}-b^{-m}\right)\leqslant\frac{m^{s-1}}{(s-1)!}b^{-t_{0}},

for
t_{0}\geqslant s-1
. Thus, for instance, if
m\geqslant b
, we have

(28)

\Pr(t>(s-1)\log_{b}m)\leqslant\frac{1}{(s-1)!},

and

(29)

\Pr(t>s\log_{b}m)\leqslant\frac{1}{m(s-1)!}.

These results ensure that the
t
parameter of the HRD is bounded above by
s\log_{b}m
with high probability. This is analogous to the known results for polynomial lattice point sets (see, for instance,
[
7
, Corollary 10.15]
).

3.4.
Linear matrix scrambling revisited

In this section, we revisit the properties of linear matrix scrambling (LMS)
[
21
]
for digital nets.
With LMS
[
21
]
, the condition
\{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\}
is equivalent to

(30)

\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j,\infty}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}=\bm{0},

where each
M_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\infty\times\infty}
is an independent lower triangular matrix whose diagonal entries are drawn independently and uniformly from
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times}
, and whose entries below the diagonal are independent and uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}
. Here we assume that all generating matrices
C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}
are of size
\infty\times m
. The probability of the event
\{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\}
is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For a non-empty subset
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
and a vector
\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
, define the set

A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}\mid\text{$b^{\ell_{j}-1}\leqslant k_{j}<b^{\ell_{j}}$ for $j\in\mathfrak{u}$, and $k_{j}=0$ for $j\notin\mathfrak{u}$}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\perp}
be the dual net corresponding to fixed (deterministic) generating matrices
C_{1},...,C_{s}
.
With LMS, for any
\bm{k}\in A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})
, we have

(31)

\Pr(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})=\frac{\lvert A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})\cap\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\perp}\rvert}{\lvert A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})\rvert},

where
\lvert A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})\rvert=b^{|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}-|\mathfrak{u}|}(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
.

Proof.

Denote
\vec{v}_{j,\infty}^{\top}=\vec{k}_{j,\infty}^{\top}M_{j}
. Since
b
is a prime number and
M_{j}
is a random lower triangular matrix with non-zero diagonal entries, for a fixed
k_{j}
with
b^{\ell_{j}-1}\leqslant k_{j}<b^{\ell_{j}}
, its
\ell_{j}
-th digit (the most significant digit) is multiplied by a uniform element in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times}
. As a result, the
\ell_{j}
-th component of
\vec{v}_{j}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times}
, while all components with indices
i<\ell_{j}
are independently and uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{F}_{b}
due to the entries of
M_{j}
below the diagonal. All components with indices
i>\ell_{j}
are zero. Thus the integer
v_{j}
is uniformly distributed over the set of integers
[b^{\mu_{1}(k_{j})-1},b^{\mu_{1}(k_{j})})
.

Due to the independence between
M_{1},\ldots,M_{s}
, the vector
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{s})
is uniformly distributed in the set
A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})
. The condition
\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
under LMS is then equivalent to the condition
\bm{v}\in\mathcal{D}_{C}^{\perp}
. Thus, the probability is given by the ratio of the number of elements in
\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})
that satisfy the dual net condition to the total size of the set. The cardinality is
|\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}})|=\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(b^{\ell_{j}}-b^{\ell_{j}-1})=b^{|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}-|\mathfrak{u}|}(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1

(Variance equivalence between Owen-scrambling and LMS)
.

The above results can be used to establish a variance equivalence between Owen-scrambling and LMS. Specifically, the probability term
\Pr((\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})
arising from LMS can be related to the gain coefficient from Owen-scrambling through the identity:

(32)

\Pr\left(\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\vec{k}_{j,\infty}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}=\bm{0}\right)=b^{-m}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}},

where
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}
is the gain coefficient defined in
[
26
]
.
The equivalence (
32
) in base 2 follows by comparing the results in
[
29
, Section 4]
and
[
31
, Lemma 4]
. The extension to a general prime base
b
is obtained by comparing Lemma
2
with
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Recall that the Walsh variance components are defined as

(33)

\sigma^{2}_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}=\sum_{b^{{\ell}_{j}}\leqslant{k}_{j}<b^{\ell_{j}+1},\ j\in\mathfrak{u}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}.

Using this, we can show the equivalence of the variance under Owen-scrambling and LMS with a digital shift as follows:

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[\hat{I}\right]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\sum_{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\Pr\left((\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\right)\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}_{0}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in A(\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})}\Pr\left((\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\right)\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}_{0}}\Gamma_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}\sigma^{2}_{\mathfrak{u},\bm{\ell}}.

For any non-empty subset
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
, let
C_{\mathfrak{u}}
denote the set of generating matrices
\{C_{j}\}_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}
. We define
t_{\mathfrak{u}}
as the smallest non-negative integer such that for any vector
\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
satisfying
|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}=m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}
, the rows of the matrices
\{C_{j}(1{:}\ell_{j},:)\}_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{b}
. We then provide bounds for the probability
\Pr{(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})}
in the following lemma, which serves as the counterpart of Lemma
1
for the proposed HRD. The advantage of the LMS can be found in the regime where
\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}
, while the HRD provides sharper (or at least equivalent) bounds for the other cases.

Lemma 3
.

For a non-empty subset
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
and a vector
\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
, let
\bm{k}=(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})
. Under LMS, we have the following bounds:

(34)

\Pr{(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}$},\\
b^{-m}&\text{if $\max_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{1}({k}_{j})>m$},\end{cases}

and for the intermediate regime where
\max_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{1}({k}_{j})\leqslant m
,

(35)

\Pr{(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp})}\leqslant\begin{cases}\min\left(\frac{b^{-m+t_{\mathfrak{u}}+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-1}}{(b-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert-1}},1\right)&\text{if $m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}<\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert$},\\
b^{-m+t_{\mathfrak{u}}}&\text{if $\mu_{1}(\bm{k})>m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first case
\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}
is straightforward from the definition of the
t_{\mathfrak{u}}
parameter. For instance, the reader may refer to
[
16
, Lemmas 1 and 5]
. The second case
\max_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{1}({k}_{j})>m
is a natural extension of
[
31
, Lemma 4]
from base
2
to a prime base
b
. The remaining cases in (
35
) extend
[
31
, Lemma 4]
, which we detail as follows.

For simplicity, let
\ell_{j}=\mu_{1}(k_{j})
. We consider the decomposition:

(36)

\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}=\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\left(\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}-k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)\right).

For each
j\in\mathfrak{u}
, the summand of the second term on the right-hand side of (
36
) is uniformly distributed on the span of the rows of
C_{j}(1{:}\ell_{j}-1,:)
. Due to the independence of the random matrices
M_{j}
across dimensions, this second sum is uniformly distributed on the linear span of
C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}
. Thus, if
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)\notin\operatorname{span}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})
, the conditional probability of the total sum being the zero vector is
0
, i.e.,

(37)

\Pr\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}=\bm{0}\;\middle|\;\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)\notin\operatorname{span}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})\right)=0.

Otherwise, the conditional probability is given by

(38)

\Pr\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\vec{k}_{j}^{\top}M_{j}C_{j}=\bm{0}\;\middle|\;\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)\in\operatorname{span}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})\right)=b^{-\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})}.

From the definition of the
t_{\mathfrak{u}}
-parameter, we have the following rank bounds:

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\!\bigl(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}(\bm{k})-\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert,

\displaystyle\text{if }\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert,

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\!\bigl(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}},

\displaystyle\text{otherwise.}

We now study the probability that
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j})C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)\in\operatorname{span}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})
. Let
\underline{C}_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})}
be the vertical concatenation of the rows
C_{j}(\ell_{j},:)
for
j\in\mathfrak{u}
. Defining
\bm{q}=(k_{j}(\ell_{j})M_{j}(\ell_{j},\ell_{j}))_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}^{\top}\in(\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times})^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
, the condition is equivalent to
\underline{C}_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})}^{\top}\bm{q}\in\mathrm{im}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}^{\top})
. Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{b}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
be the subspace of vectors
\bm{q}
satisfying this condition. By the rank-nullity theorem, we have

(40)

\dim V=|\mathfrak{u}|+\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})-\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})}).

Since
\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})})-\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})\geqslant 0
, and specifically, when
m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}<\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+|\mathfrak{u}|
, we have
\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})})-\mathrm{rank}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}})\geqslant(m-t_{\mathfrak{u}})-(\mu_{1}(\bm{k})-|\mathfrak{u}|)
, it follows that

(41)

\dim V\leqslant\begin{cases}-m+t_{\mathfrak{u}}+\mu_{1}(\bm{k})&\text{if }m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}<\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+|\mathfrak{u}|,\\
|\mathfrak{u}|&\text{if }\mu_{1}(\bm{k})>m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}+|\mathfrak{u}|.\end{cases}

Given that
\bm{q}
is uniformly distributed in
(\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times})^{|\mathfrak{u}|}
, the probability is bounded as

(42)

\Pr(\underline{C}_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})}^{\top}\bm{q}\in\mathrm{im}(C_{\bm{\mu}_{1}(\bm{k})-\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}}}^{\top}))\leqslant\frac{(b-1)^{\dim V}}{(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}},

referring to
[
13
, Lemma 3.9]
. Combining (
38
), (
39
), (
41
), and (
42
) completes the proof.
∎

Comparing Lemmas
1
and
3
reveals that LMS is advantageous in the regime where
\mu_{1}(\bm{k})\leqslant m-t_{\mathfrak{u}}
(i.e., for “small”
\bm{k}
), whereas HRD (as well as URD) is better in the intermediate regime (i.e., for “medium”
\bm{k}
). Importantly, for well-known sequences such as Sobol’ sequences
[
33
]
, the full-dimensional
t
-value scales as
O(s\log s)
. In such cases, HRD is particularly effective as it avoids the exponential growth of the probability bound with respect to
s
that would otherwise be incurred by the
t
-value.

4.
Bounds on Walsh Coefficients

In this section, we provide bounds on the Walsh coefficients to facilitate the derivations of convergence rates in the next section.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
denote the smoothness parameter. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
with its
b
-adic expansion
k=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}c_{i}b^{a_{i}-1}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{b}^{\times}
and
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{\nu}>0
, we write

k^{+}_{\alpha}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\min(\alpha,\nu)}c_{i}b^{a_{i}-1}\quad\text{and}\quad k^{-}_{\alpha}:=\sum_{i=\alpha+1}^{\nu}c_{i}b^{a_{i}-1}.

If
k=0
, we set
k^{+}_{\alpha}=k^{-}_{\alpha}=0
. Notice that
k=k^{+}_{\alpha}+k^{-}_{\alpha}
holds, and
k^{+}_{\alpha}\geqslant k^{-}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. As we introduced in Section
2
, recall that we denote by
\kappa(k)=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\nu}\}
the set of positions of non-zero digits in the base-
b
expansion of
k
, and by
k_{(\alpha)}
the
\alpha
-th largest element of
\kappa
, i.e.,
k_{(\alpha)}=a_{\alpha}
if
\nu\geqslant\alpha
and
k_{(\alpha)}=0
otherwise. For a vector
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, we write
\bm{k}_{(\alpha)}=(k_{1,(\alpha)},\ldots,k_{s,(\alpha)})
.

Moreover, let
\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})=(n_{\alpha}(k_{1}),\ldots,n_{\alpha}(k_{s}))\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{s}
, where
n_{\alpha}(k_{j})=\min(\alpha,|\kappa(k_{j})|)
. It is important to note that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
|\kappa(k)|\leqslant\alpha
, we have

k^{+}_{n_{\alpha}(k)}=k^{+}_{n_{\alpha}(k)+1}=\cdots=k^{+}_{\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad k^{-}_{n_{\alpha}(k)}=k^{-}_{n_{\alpha}(k)+1}=\cdots=k^{-}_{\alpha}=0.

For a function
f:[0,1)^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
that is
\alpha
-times differentiable in each variable, Suzuki and Yoshiki
[
34
, Theorem 2.5]
derived the following formula for the
\bm{k}
-th Walsh coefficient:

(43)

\hat{f}(\bm{k})=(-1)^{|\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})|_{1}}\int_{[0,1)^{s}}f^{(\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k}))}(\bm{x})\prod_{j=1}^{s}\overline{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{{k_{j}}^{-}_{\alpha}}(x_{j})}W({k_{j}}^{+}_{\alpha})(x_{j})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

where
f^{(\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k}))}
denotes the partial mixed derivative of
f
of order
\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k})
. Furthermore, the auxiliary function
W(k)(\cdot)
appearing in (
43
) is periodic with period
b^{-a_{\nu}+1}
when
\nu>0
and is defined recursively in
[
34
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
W(k)(\cdot):[0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
is defined recursively as:

(44)

\displaystyle W(0)(x)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle W(k)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\overline{{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{{c_{\nu}}b^{a_{\nu}-1}}(y)}W(k^{\prime})(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
k^{\prime}=k-{c_{\nu}}b^{a_{\nu}-1}
.

We list a useful property of
W
in the following lemma. The proof is deferred to Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4

(
L^{\infty}
bound)
.

For any
k>0

holds:

\lVert W(k_{\alpha}^{+})\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant\left(\frac{1+\pi}{2}\right)b^{n_{\alpha}(k)-\mu_{\alpha}(k)}

for any fixed base

.

4.1.
Bounds for functions with finite generalized Vitali variation

Here, we focus on a class of smooth functions with finite generalized Vitali variation, which is defined as follows:

Definition 3
.

For a function
f:[0,1)^{s}\to\mathbb{R}
and a parameter
p\in(0,1]
, the generalized Vitali variation, denoted by
V_{p}^{(s)}(f)
, is defined as:

V_{p}^{(s)}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{P}}\mathrm{Vol}(J)\left|\frac{\Delta(f,J)}{\mathrm{Vol}(J)^{p}}\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all finite partitions of
[0,1)^{s}
into
s
-dimensional axis-parallel boxes
J=\prod_{j=1}^{s}[a_{j},b_{j}]
. Here
\Delta(f,J)
denotes the
s
-th order mixed difference of
f
over the box
J
, i.e.,

\Delta(f,J)\coloneqq\sum_{\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}(-1)^{s-|\bm{\epsilon}|_{1}}f(a_{1}+\epsilon_{1}(b_{1}-a_{1}),\dots,a_{s}+\epsilon_{s}(b_{s}-a_{s})).

For a vector
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}
, we define the following index sets

B_{\alpha,\bm{\ell},s}=\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}\mid\bm{k}_{(\alpha)}=\bm{\ell}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\alpha,\bm{\ell},s}=\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}\mid\bm{k}_{(\alpha)}\leqslant\bm{\ell}\right\},

where the inequality
\bm{k}_{(\alpha)}\leqslant\bm{\ell}
is understood component-wise, i.e.,
k_{j,(\alpha)}\leqslant\ell_{j}
for all
j\in 1{:}s
. With these definitions of the function’s variation and the index sets, we can establish a bound on the sum of squared Walsh coefficients.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(0,1]
. For a subset
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s
and a function
f
, define its projection onto the active variables by integrating out the inactive ones:

For fixed vectors
\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+},\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}
such that

\bm{\tau}=\bm{n}_{\alpha}(\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+})

\mathfrak{v}\coloneqq\mathrm{supp}(\bm{\ell})
and
\mathfrak{w}\coloneqq\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in B_{\alpha+1,\bm{\ell},s}\\
\text{with fixed $\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+}$}\end{subarray}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{w}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}},

W(\bm{k}^{+}_{\alpha})(\bm{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}W({k_{j}}^{+}_{\alpha})(x_{j})
and
(x)_{+}=\max(0,x)
.

Proof.

\ell_{j}>0

\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}

\mathfrak{u}

for all

\mathfrak{u}

For all

By applying the inclusion-exclusion formula over

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in B_{\alpha+1,\bm{\ell},s}\\

\displaystyle\leqslant b^{-|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{w}|}}\sum_{\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in A_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}}\sup_{\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in[\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{v}}b^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}},(\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{v}})b^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}})}\left|\Delta\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}),J_{\bm{d}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{h}^{*}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(b-1)^{(2p-1)_{+}|\mathfrak{v}|}b^{-2p|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{w}|}}\sum_{\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{v}}\in A_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{v}}}}\sup_{\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in[\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{u}}b^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}},(\bm{a}_{\mathfrak{u}}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})b^{-\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}})}\mathrm{Vol}(J_{\bm{d}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{h}^{*}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}})\left|\frac{\Delta\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}),J_{\bm{d}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{h}^{*}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}}\right)}{\mathrm{Vol}(J_{\bm{d}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{h}^{*}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}})^{p}}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(b-1)^{(2p-1)_{+}|\mathfrak{v}|}b^{-2p|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{w}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{w}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Bounds for functions in weighted Sobolev spaces

Motivated by the results for the bounds on the Walsh coefficients shown in the previous subsection, let us consider the following weighted Sobolev space.

Definition 4

(Weighted Sobolev–variation space)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,1]
, and
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}})_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}
be a set of positive weights. We define the weighted Sobolev–variation space
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
equipped with norm

(52)

\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\dotsc,\alpha-1\}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{1/2},

where
f_{\mathfrak{u}}
is the projection of
f
onto the variables
\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}
by integrating out
\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}}
:

f_{\mathfrak{u}}(\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\coloneqq\int_{[0,1)^{s-|\mathfrak{u}|}}f(\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}}.

It follows from the definition that

\left(\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}},

for any subsets
\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s
, and Lemma
5
implies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in B_{\alpha+1,\bm{\ell},s}\\
\text{with fixed $\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+}$}\end{subarray}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}\lVert W((\bm{k}^{+}_{\alpha}))\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}(b-1)^{(2p-1)_{+}|\mathfrak{v}|}b^{-2p|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}}.

Moreover, using Lemma
4
, we obtain

(53)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in B_{\alpha+1,\bm{\ell},s}\\
\text{with fixed $\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+}$}\end{subarray}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}&\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}(b-1)^{(2p-1)_{+}|\mathfrak{v}|}b^{-2p|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\left(\frac{1+\pi}{2}\right)^{2}b^{2\min(\alpha,\tau_{j})-2\mu_{\alpha}((k_{j})_{\alpha}^{+})}\\
&\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}C_{\alpha,p}^{2|\mathfrak{u}|}\,b^{-2\mu_{\alpha}(\bm{k}_{\alpha}^{+})-2p|\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}|_{1}},\end{split}

where

(54)

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,p}=(b-1)^{(2p-1)_{+}/2}\left(\frac{1+\pi}{2}\right)b^{\alpha}.

This bound trivially applies to the individual terms, which gives

\displaystyle\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,b^{-\mu_{\alpha}(\bm{k})-p(\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})-\mu_{\alpha}(\bm{k}))}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,b^{-((1-p)\mu_{\alpha}(\bm{k})+p\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k}))}

(55)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\,b^{-(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)},

where, for the last bound, we used the inequality
\mu_{\alpha}(k)/\alpha\geqslant\mu_{\alpha+1}(k)/(\alpha+1)
that holds for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 2
.

We can also connect the weighted Sobolev–variation norm to a weighted Sobolev norm. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leqslant q<\infty
,
1\leqslant r<\infty
and
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}})_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}
be a set of positive weights. Define the weighted Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,q,r,\bm{\gamma}}
equipped with norm

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,q,r,\bm{\gamma}}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\dotsc,\alpha\}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left\lvert\int_{[0,1)^{s-\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right\rvert^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{r/q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{1/r}.

Notice that this norm is an upper bound of the norm in
[
5
, Definition 3.3]
.

From
[
20
]
, we bound the Vitali variation by the Sobolev norm. For
p\in[1/2,1]
, let
q=2/(3-2p)
and we have:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left\lvert f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{v}})}(\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\right\rvert^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{2/q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left\lvert\int_{[0,1)^{s-\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right\rvert^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{2/q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\dotsc,\alpha-1\}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\rvert}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(V_{p}^{(|\mathfrak{v}|)}\left(f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}})}(\cdot,\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})\right)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\dotsc,\alpha-1\}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\rvert}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}|}}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\left\lvert\int_{[0,1)^{s-\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}f^{(\bm{\tau}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{v}}+\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}(\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{-\mathfrak{u}}\right\rvert^{q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{2/q}\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha+1,q,2,\bm{\gamma}}}.

5.
Error bounds: high order convergence and dimension independence

In this section, we discuss the convergence rates from two approaches: the median-of-means estimator and the greedy optimization of Hankel digital nets.

5.1.
Median-of-means Hankel designs

In this subsection, we discuss the median-of-means estimator, in line with recent works
[
11
,
12
,
14
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
31
]
. Let
r
be a positive integer, and let
Q_{N,1},\ldots,Q_{N,2r-1}
be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) copies of the QMC rule based on a Hankel random design with a random digital shift in infinite precision. The median-of-means estimator is then defined as

Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)=\mathrm{med}\left(Q_{N,1}(f),\ldots,Q_{N,2r-1}(f)\right),

where the median is uniquely defined since
2r-1
is odd. Our main contribution here is to analyze the root-mean-square error of this estimator in the weighted Sobolev–variation space
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
. A key highlight is that, without any prior knowledge of the parameters
\alpha,p
, and
\bm{\gamma}
, the estimator
Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)
achieves a convergence rate of order
N^{-(\alpha+p+1/2)+\varepsilon}
for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
. Furthermore, the implied constant can be independent of the dimension
s
under a suitable summability condition on the weights
\bm{\gamma}
.

The following argument builds upon the approach of Pan
[
28
]
. However, we extend the known results to a general prime base
b\geqslant 2
and employ Hankel designs (rather than uniform designs) for the generating matrices, while conducting our analysis within the weighted Sobolev–variation space
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
.
Motivated by the structure of the bound on the sum of Walsh coefficients in (
53
), and following
[
28
]
, we define the set
K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)
as

(56)

K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)=\left\{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\mid p\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}{k_{j}}_{(\alpha+1)}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{\alpha}(k_{j})\leqslant T\right\},

for a non-empty subset
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s
and a given parameter
T>0
. In what follows, we write

B_{\alpha,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}=\left\{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\mid{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{(\alpha)}=\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\alpha,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}=\left\{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\mid{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{(\alpha)}\leqslant\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right\},

where the inequality
{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{(\alpha)}\leqslant\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}
is understood component-wise.

We compute the sum of squared Walsh coefficients
\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}
on the complement of the set
K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)
. For any
\theta\in(0,1)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}\chi\left(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\notin K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\sum_{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in B_{\alpha+1,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}\chi\left(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\notin K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{+}\in\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\mathbb{N}\setminus T_{\alpha,\ell_{j},1})}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{-}\in B_{1,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}}\lvert\hat{f}({\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}\chi\left(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\notin K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{+}\in\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\mathbb{N}\setminus T_{\alpha,\ell_{j},1})}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{-}\in B_{1,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}}b^{2\theta(p\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{v}}{k_{j}}_{(\alpha+1)}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{\alpha}({k_{j}}_{\alpha}^{+})-T)}\lvert\hat{f}({\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{+}\in\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\mathbb{N}\setminus T_{\alpha,\ell_{j},1})}b^{2\theta(p\lvert\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rvert_{1}+\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\mu_{\alpha}({k_{j}}_{\alpha}^{+})-T)}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{-}\in B_{1,\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}},\mathfrak{u}}}\lvert\hat{f}({\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}},\bm{0})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}b^{-2\theta T}C_{\alpha,p}^{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}b^{2(\theta-1)p\lvert\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rvert_{1}}\sum_{{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}}_{\alpha}^{+}\in\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus T_{\alpha,\ell_{j},1})}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}b^{2(\theta-1)\mu_{\alpha}({k_{j}}_{\alpha}^{+})}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}b^{-2\theta T}C_{\alpha,p}^{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}b^{2(\theta-1)p\lvert\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rvert_{1}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{{{k}_{j}}_{\alpha}^{+}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus T_{\alpha,\ell_{j},1}}b^{2(\theta-1)\mu_{\alpha}({k_{j}}_{\alpha}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}b^{-2\theta T}C_{\alpha,p}^{2\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\sum_{\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}}b^{2(\theta-1)p\lvert\bm{\ell}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rvert_{1}}\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\left({(b-1)}\sum_{i=\ell_{j}+1}^{\infty}b^{2(\theta-1)i}\right)^{\alpha}

(57)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{2}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}b^{-2\theta T}C_{\alpha,p,\theta}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert},

with

C_{\alpha,p,\theta}=C_{\alpha,p}^{2}\frac{{(b-1)^{\alpha}}}{\alpha!(b^{2(1-\theta)}-1)^{\alpha}(1-b^{2(\theta-1)(\alpha+p)})}.

We next derive an upper bound for the size
\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\rvert
.

Lemma 6
.

For any
T>0
, we have

(58)

\left\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\right\rvert\leqslant b^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}b^{T/(\alpha+p)}\frac{(T+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha-1}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha-1)!}.

Proof.

Recall that we have

\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\rvert:=\#\Bigl\{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}:\ \sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}+pk_{j,(\alpha+1)}\right)\leqslant T\Bigr\}.

In each coordinate
j\in\mathfrak{u}
, we denote by
\nu_{j}
the number of non-zero digits of
k_{j}
. Note that we write
k_{j,(\nu_{j}+1)}=k_{j,(\nu_{j}+2)}=\cdots=0
. When top-
(\alpha{+1})
positions in coordinate
j
are fixed, all lower bits below
k_{j,(\alpha+1)}
are free to choose. Thus we have

(59)

\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\rvert=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{j,(1)}>\cdots>k_{j,(\nu_{j})}>0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u}\\
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}+pk_{j,(\alpha+1)})\leqslant T\end{subarray}}{(b-1)^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\min(\nu_{j},\alpha+1)}}b^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j,(\alpha+1)}}.

Notice that the above also holds when
k_{j,(\alpha+1)}=0
. Now we relax the strict decreasing sequence
k_{j,(1)}>\cdots>k_{j,(\nu_{j})}>0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u}
to obtain

(60)

\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\rvert\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{j,(1)}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{j,(\alpha+1)}\geqslant 0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u}\\
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}(\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}+pk_{j,(\alpha+1)})\leqslant T\end{subarray}}{(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}}b^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}k_{j,(\alpha+1)}}.

Let
M_{j}:=\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}+pk_{j,(\alpha+1)}
. Since
\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}\geqslant\alpha k_{j,(\alpha+1)}
, we have
0\leqslant k_{j,(\alpha+1)}\leqslant\lfloor M_{j}/(\alpha+p)\rfloor
, leading to

\displaystyle\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T)\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{j,(1)}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{j,(\alpha+1)}\geqslant 0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u}\\
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}M_{j}\leqslant T\end{subarray}}b^{\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\lfloor M_{j}/(\alpha+p)\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leqslant(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}b^{T/(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{j,(1)}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{j,(\alpha)}\geqslant 0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u}\\
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}k_{j,(r)}\leqslant T\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leqslant(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}b^{T/(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{j,r}\geqslant 0,\,j\in\mathfrak{u},r=1,\ldots,\alpha\\
\sum_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\sum_{r=1}^{\alpha}a_{j,r}\leqslant\lfloor T\rfloor\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}b^{T/(\alpha+p)}\binom{\lfloor T\rfloor+|\mathfrak{u}|\alpha}{|\mathfrak{u}|\alpha}\leqslant(b-1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|(\alpha+1)}b^{T/(\alpha+p)}\frac{(T+|\mathfrak{u}|\alpha)^{|\mathfrak{u}|\alpha}}{(|\mathfrak{u}|\alpha)!},

which proves the lemma.

∎

Following
[
28
]
, for
0<\delta<1
,
\xi>0
, we let

(61)

T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi}=(\alpha+p)m-(\alpha+p)\left(\log_{b}\Gamma_{m,\xi}-\log_{b}(\delta)-\frac{\log_{b}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}}{\alpha+p+\xi}\right),

where

\Gamma_{m,\xi}=1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}\frac{(b-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert(\alpha+1)}((\alpha+p)m+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)!}.

In the following, we bound the probability of a “bad event”, i.e., when there exists a
\bm{k}\in\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi})
that is in the dual net
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
.

Lemma 7
.

Given
0<\delta<1
, we have

\Pr\left(\bigcup_{\bm{k}\in\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi})}\left\{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\right\}\right)\leqslant\delta.

Proof.

Notice that we have
T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi}\leqslant(\alpha+p)m
for any
\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s
and
m>0
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\left\lvert K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta b^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\frac{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}}{\Gamma_{m,\xi}}(b-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert(\alpha+1)}\frac{(T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi}+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta b^{m}\frac{1}{{\Gamma_{m,\xi}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}{\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}}(b-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert(\alpha+1)}\frac{((\alpha+p)m+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta b^{m}.\qed

Now we present the mean squared error of the median-of-means estimator.

Theorem 1
.

Given
0<\delta<1/8
, when
f\in\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,1]
, the weighted Sobolev–variation space in Definition
4
, the median-of-means estimator
Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)
satisfies

(62)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I\right)^{2}\right]\leqslant C_{\epsilon,\theta,s}b^{(\epsilon-2\theta(\alpha+p)-1)m}\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}+2(8\delta)^{r}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})},

where
\epsilon>0
,
0<\theta<1
, and the constant
C_{\epsilon,\theta,s}\to\infty
when
\epsilon\to 0
or
\theta\to 1
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{N}(f)
be the QMC rule based on a single HRD with a random digital shift. Let us define the set of vectors
\mathcal{K}
as

\mathcal{K}=\bigcup_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subset 1{:}s}\bigcup_{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in K_{\mathfrak{u}}(T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi})}\left\{(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{0})\right\}

and consider the event
\mathcal{A}
that the dual net contains at least one vector from
\mathcal{K}
, which is given by
\mathcal{A}=\left\{\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\neq\varnothing\right\}
.
Noting that the event
\mathcal{A}
happens independently of a random digital shift, we have
{\mathbb{E}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}=\mu
. Applying Chebyshev’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\rvert^{2}\geqslant\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\Pr\left(\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\rvert^{2}\geqslant\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\mid\mathcal{A}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\Pr\left(\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)\Pr\left(\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\rvert^{2}\geqslant\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\Pr\left(\mathcal{A}\right)+\Pr\left(\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c})}\leqslant 2\delta,

where the last bound follows from Lemma
7
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c})\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}\Pr\left(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta^{-1}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}\Pr\left(\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}b^{-m}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}\setminus\mathcal{K}}\lvert\hat{f}(\bm{k})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}}{b^{(1+2\theta(\alpha+p))m}}\delta^{-2\theta(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}},

where the second equality follows from Lemma
1
, and the last bound follows from (
57
) and the definition of
T_{\mathfrak{u},m,\delta,\xi}
.

For
Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)
being the median of
(2r-1)
i.i.d. estimators, we have

(63)

\Pr\left(\lvert Q_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I\rvert^{2}\geqslant\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\right)\leqslant{2r-1\choose r}(2\delta)^{r}\leqslant(8\delta)^{r}.

Notice that when
f\in\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,1]
, we see that
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}
, and so

\displaystyle\left\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left(\int_{[0,1)^{s}}\left(f(\bm{x}_{i})-f(\bm{x})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\lvert\int_{[0,1)^{s}}\left(f(\bm{x}_{i})-f(\bm{x})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\right\rvert\leqslant 2\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})},

with probability
1
. Thus, the mean squared error is bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({Q}_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[({Q}_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I)^{2}\chi\left(\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\rvert^{2}<\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\right)]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}[({Q}_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I)^{2}\chi\left(\lvert Q_{N}(f)-I\rvert^{2}\geqslant\delta^{-1}\Pr(\mathcal{A}^{c}){\mathrm{Var}\mspace{-2mu}\left[Q_{N}(f)\mid\mathcal{A}^{c}\right]}\right)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}}{b^{(1+2\theta(\alpha+p))m}}\delta^{-2\theta(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\quad+2(8\delta)^{r}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}.

Notice that
\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}=o(b^{\epsilon m})
, for any
\epsilon>0
, enabling us to find a constant
\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}>0
, independent of
m
, such that
\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}\leqslant\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}b^{\epsilon m}
for any
m>0
. This leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({Q}_{N}^{(r)}(f)-I)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}^{2}\frac{\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}}{b^{(2\theta(\alpha+p)+1-\epsilon)m}}\delta^{-2\theta(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\quad+2(8\delta)^{r}\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}.

By substituting

C_{\epsilon,\theta,s}\coloneqq\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}\delta^{-2\theta(\alpha+p)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}

into the above equation, we reach the conclusion.
∎

Remark 3

(Choice of
r
)
.

The upper bound on the mean squared error given in (
62
) consists of two terms. To ensure that the second term does not dominate, it suffices to set

(8\delta)^{r}=b^{-(2\theta(\alpha+p)+1)m}=N^{-(2\theta(\alpha+p)+1)}.

Thus,
r
can be chosen independently of the dimension
s
as

r=\left\lceil\left(2\theta(\alpha+p)+1\right)\frac{\log N}{\log(8\delta)^{-1}}\right\rceil.

Since the median-of-means estimator requires
rN
function evaluations, this choice of
r
affects the convergence rate only by a dimension-independent logarithmic factor. Furthermore, in terms of the total computational cost, it preserves the dimension independence of the implied constant (as discussed in the next remark). If the smoothness parameters
\alpha
and
p
are unknown in advance, one can instead choose
r=\lceil g(N)\log N\rceil
using a slowly increasing function
g
, such as
g(N)=\max(1,\log\log N)
or
g(N)=\log N
, as suggested in
[
11
]
.

Before moving on, we remark that the factor
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}
in the second term of (
62
) can be bounded independently of the dimension
s
under a mild condition on the weights
\bm{\gamma}
. For any
f\in\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
, it follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus that the function can be decomposed into its projections:

f(\bm{x})=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{u};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

Here,
\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{\mathfrak{u}};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})
denotes the mixed difference of the projection
f_{\mathfrak{u}}
with respect to the variables in
\mathfrak{u}
, defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{\mathfrak{u}};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}}(-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\rvert}f_{u}(\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}\setminus\mathfrak{v}})=\int_{y_{j_{1}}}^{x_{j_{1}}}\cdots\int_{y_{j_{k}}}^{x_{j_{k}}}f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}},

where
\mathfrak{u}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}
. In fact, the case
s=1
is easily confirmed as

f(x)=\int_{0}^{1}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{1}(f(x)-f(y))\,\mathrm{d}y=f_{\varnothing}+\int_{0}^{1}\Delta_{\{1\}}(f;y,x)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Assuming that the decomposition holds for
s=k
, for the case
s=k+1
, we apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to the variable
x_{k+1}
, and then use the induction hypothesis to obtain

\displaystyle f(\bm{x}_{\{1:k+1\}})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}f(\bm{x}_{\{1:k\}},y_{k+1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{k+1}+\int_{0}^{1}\Delta_{\{k+1\}}(f;y_{k+1},x_{k+1})\,\mathrm{d}y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=f_{\{1:k\}}(\bm{x}_{\{1:k\}})+\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1:k\}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}\left(\Delta_{\{k+1\}}(f;y_{k+1},x_{k+1});\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}\,\mathrm{d}y_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1:k\}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{u};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}+\sum_{k+1\in\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1:k+1\}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{u};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\{1:k+1\}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{u};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}.

By the triangle inequality and the integral representation of the mixed difference, we have

\displaystyle|f(\bm{x})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}\left|\Delta_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{\mathfrak{u}};\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}},\bm{x}_{\mathfrak{u}})\right|\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}_{\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\int_{[0,1)^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}|f_{\mathfrak{u}}^{(\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})}(\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}})|\,\mathrm{d}\bm{t}_{\mathfrak{u}}\leqslant\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\gamma^{-1}_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f^{(\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rVert_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\max_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)\sum_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\gamma^{-1}_{\mathfrak{u}}\lVert f^{(\bm{1}_{\mathfrak{u}})}_{\mathfrak{u}}\rVert_{L_{2}}\leqslant\left(\max_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq{1:s}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\right)\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}.

Thus,
\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,1)^{s})}
is bounded independently of the dimension
s
if the weights
\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}
are uniformly bounded.

Remark 4

(Dimension-independent convergence rate)
.

Now we consider the case when
s\to\infty
and investigate whether the constant
C_{\epsilon,\theta,s}
in the error bound (
62
) is still finite. To this end, we can consider the following conditions, as
s\to\infty
,

(64)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}<\infty.

Suppose we have the product weight
\{\gamma_{j}\leqslant 1,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}\leqslant\prod_{j\in\mathfrak{u}}\gamma_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{\min(\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi},\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi})}<\infty,

then we can show that the conditions are both satisfied. Specifically, we can find the bound
\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}
in the following derivation:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{m,\xi}^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}\frac{(b-1)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert(\alpha+1)}((\alpha+p)m+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)!}\right)^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}\left(\frac{(b-1)^{\alpha+1}((\alpha+p)m+\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha)^{\alpha}}{(\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert\alpha/e)^{\alpha}}\right)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\right)^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}\left({(b-1)^{\alpha+1}(e(\alpha+p)m+e)^{\alpha}}\right)^{\lvert\mathfrak{u}\rvert}\right)^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}\left({(b-1)^{\alpha+1}e^{\alpha}((\alpha+p)m+1)^{\alpha}}\right)\right)^{2\theta(\alpha+p)}\leqslant\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}b^{\epsilon m},

which holds for any
\epsilon>0
. Notice that we use the inequality
n!\geqslant(n/e)^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
in the second line and
\tilde{C}_{\epsilon,s}<\infty
as
s\to\infty
whenever
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi}}<\infty
; see Lemma 3 of
[
17
]
for instance. Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi+(2-2\theta)(\alpha+p)}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s\\
\mathfrak{u}\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta,\mathfrak{u}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left(1+\gamma_{j}^{\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}\gamma_{j}^{\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi}}C_{\alpha,p,\theta}\right)<\infty,

whenever
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi}}<\infty
.

To conclude, notice that the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{x}
decreases as
x>0
increases. We seek
\xi^{*}=\arg\max\left\{\min(\frac{1}{\alpha+p+\xi},\frac{2\xi}{\alpha+p+\xi})\right\}
to admit the largest class of weights
\{\gamma_{j}\leqslant 1,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
, which yields
\xi^{*}=1/2
. Thus, if
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{\frac{1}{\alpha+p+1/2}}<\infty
, we achieve the dimension-independent convergence rate. This result is consistent with the setting in
[
28
]
.

5.2.
Greedy optimization of Hankel designs

The major advantage of the median-of-means estimator lies in its universality, i.e., although we do not need to specify the smoothness (
\alpha
and
p
in our function space setting) and the weights
\bm{\gamma}
in advance, the estimator can adaptively attain a high-order convergence with dimension-independent implied constants, provided that
r
is suitably chosen (as discussed in Remark
3
). In some applications, such as partial differential equations with random coefficients
[
5
]
, however, the smoothness and weights may be known a priori based on the information of the underlying random fields. In such cases, optimization of QMC point sets is desirable and often performs well in practice.

In this subsection, we consider a simple greedy optimization of Hankel designs.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,1]
, and
\bm{\gamma}
be given. For a digital net
P\subset[0,1)^{s}
in base
b
with
N=b^{m}
points (without any digital shift applied), the worst-case error in
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
is defined as

e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P)\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}\\
\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}|I(f)-Q_{N}(f)|.

Here, we assume that either the worst-case error or its upper bound has a computable formula. Our greedy optimization proceeds by first drawing Hankel designs independently and randomly several times, denoted by
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
, and then choosing the one that minimizes the worst-case error:

P^{*}:=\arg\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant r}e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P_{j}).

One can use
P^{*}
as a deterministic QMC rule, or apply a random digital shift to construct an unbiased estimator. Unbiasedness allows for straightforward error estimation through the estimator’s variance, which is a property that the median-of-means estimator lacks.

Regarding this greedy optimization of Hankel designs, the worst-case error is bounded probabilistically as follows:

Theorem 2
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in(0,1]
, and
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}})_{\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}
be a set of positive weights, let
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
be the weighted Sobolev–variation space as defined in Definition
4
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}
be i.i.d. copies of HRD with infinite precision
E=\infty
and let
P^{*}
be the one which minimizes the worst-case error in
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
. Then, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P^{*})\leqslant\inf_{1/(\alpha+p)<\lambda\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}\right]\geqslant 1-(1-\delta)^{r},

where
C_{\alpha,p}
is as given in (
54
), and
D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}>0
is a constant depending only on
\alpha,p,\lambda
.

Proof.

For any function
f\in\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
and digital net
P\subset[0,1)^{s}
in base
b
with
N=b^{m}
points (without any digital shift applied), the integration error is bounded as

\displaystyle|I(f)-Q_{N}(f)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\hat{f}(\bm{k})\right|\leqslant\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}|\hat{f}(\bm{k})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lVert f\rVert_{{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)},

where we used (
55
). Therefore, the worst-case error in
\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}}
is bounded as

(65)

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P)\leqslant\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)}.

Now, let
P
be a randomly chosen Hankel design. By using the inequality
(\sum_{i}a_{i})^{\lambda}\leqslant\sum_{i}a_{i}^{\lambda}
that holds for
(a_{i})_{i}
with
a_{i}\geqslant 0
and
\lambda\in(0,1]
, and by applying Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda}(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-\lambda(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}_{*}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-\lambda(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)}\mathbb{P}\left[\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{s}_{*}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-\lambda(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})/(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}\sum_{\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\mathbb{N}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}}b^{-\lambda(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k}_{\mathfrak{u}})/(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|},

for
\lambda\in(1/(\alpha+p),1]
, where we write

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{-\lambda(\alpha+p)\mu_{\alpha+1}(k)/(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{\nu}\frac{b-1}{b^{\lambda i(\alpha+p)/(\alpha+1)}-1}+\frac{b^{\lambda(\alpha+p)}-1}{b^{\lambda(\alpha+p)}-b}\prod_{i=1}^{\alpha+1}\frac{b-1}{b^{\lambda i(\alpha+p)/(\alpha+1)}-1}<\infty.

We refer to
[
9
, Lemma 7]
for the second equality.
Since the above bound on the expectation holds for any
\lambda\in(1/(\alpha+p),1]
, the Markov inequality states that

\mathbb{P}\left[e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P)\leqslant\inf_{1/(\alpha+p)<\lambda\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}\right]\geqslant\delta,

for any
0<\delta<1
. That is, when drawing a Hankel design randomly, the worst-case error is bounded by a quantity arbitrarily close to
\mathcal{O}(N^{-(\alpha+p)})
with probability at least
\delta
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P^{*})\leqslant\inf_{1/(\alpha+p)<\lambda\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left[e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P^{*})>\inf_{1/(\alpha+p)<\lambda\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle=1-\prod_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{P}\left[e(\mathcal{W}_{s,\alpha,p,\bm{\gamma}},P_{r})>\inf_{1/(\alpha+p)<\lambda\leqslant 1}\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)b^{m}}\sum_{\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq 1{:}s}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}\right)^{1/\lambda}\right]

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-(1-\delta)^{r},

for any
0<\delta<1
. This completes the proof.
∎

This result implies that a larger
r
naturally increases the probability of success. However, a very small
r
is often sufficient in practice. Choosing
r=\lceil\log(1/\epsilon)/\log(1/(1-\delta))\rceil
ensures a success probability of at least
1-\epsilon
. For instance, with
\delta=1/2
,
r=15
is enough to guarantee a success probability exceeding
0.9999
(specifically,
1-2^{-15}\approx 0.99997
).

Remark 5
.

Although the worst-case error is only bounded probabilistically, the bound can be independent of the dimension
s
, if there exists a constant
\lambda\in(1/(\alpha+p),1]
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varnothing\neq\mathfrak{u}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\\
|\mathfrak{u}|<\infty\end{subarray}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}C_{\alpha,p}^{\lambda|\mathfrak{u}|}D_{\alpha,p,\lambda}^{|\mathfrak{u}|}<\infty.

In the case of product weights, this condition can be simplified into

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{\lambda}<\infty,

which is always satisfied if the following holds:

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}^{1/(\alpha+p)}<\infty.

Remark 6
.

To make our greedy optimization feasible, we need a computable formula for either the worst-case error or its upper bound, as shown in (
65
). Let
P=\{\bm{x}_{0},\ldots,\bm{x}_{b^{m}-1}\}
be a fixed digital net in base
b
. In the case of product weights and
p=1
, the upper bound equals

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}C_{\alpha,1}^{|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{*}^{s}}\gamma_{\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})}C_{\alpha,p}^{|\mathrm{supp}(\bm{k})|}\,b^{-\mu_{\alpha+1}(\bm{k})}\,\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{b^{m}-1}{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{x}_{i})

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{1}{b^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{b^{m}-1}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\left[1+\gamma_{j}C_{\alpha,1}\omega_{\alpha+1}(x_{i,j})\right],

where

\omega_{\alpha+1}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b^{-\mu_{\alpha+1}(k)}{}_{b}\mathrm{wal}_{k_{j}}(x).

Baldeaux et al.
[
1
]
studied how to compute
\omega_{\alpha+1}
efficiently and also derived an explicit form in special cases. For instance, in the case of
b=2
,
[
1
, Corollary 1]
shows that

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(x)=s_{1}(x)+\tilde{s}_{2}(x),\quad\omega_{3}(x)=s_{1}(x)+s_{2}(x)+\tilde{s}_{3}(x),

where

\displaystyle s_{1}(x)=1-2x,

\displaystyle s_{2}(x)=1/3-2(1-x)x,

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{2}(x)=(1-5t_{1})/2-(a_{1}-2)x,

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{3}(x)=(1-43t_{1}^{2})/18+(5t_{1}-1)x+(a_{1}-2)x^{2},

with
a_{1}=t_{1}=0
when
x=0
, and
a_{1}\coloneqq-\lfloor\log_{2}(x)\rfloor,t_{1}\coloneqq 2^{-a_{1}}
for
0<x<1
. Thus, if
\alpha+1\in\{2,3\}
, the worst-case error bound can be computed with
\mathcal{O}(sN)
costs.

7.
Discussion

In this work, we propose a Hankel random digital net for QMC integration. The construction is not constrained by the maximal dimension and is flexible with respect to the choice of the prime base. We derive the decay of Walsh coefficients for functions in a weighted Sobolev space and analyze the convergence rate for both the median-of-means estimator and a greedy optimization procedure. The proposed HRD is shown to outperform previous randomized designs such as the URD, and the numerical results demonstrate improved performance compared with LMS combined with the Sobol’ sequence. Meanwhile, in revisiting the properties of LMS, we also establish the variance equivalence between LMS and Owen-scrambling.

Note that the cost of evaluating the worst-case error bound is
\mathcal{O}(b^{m})
, which is the same as the cost of evaluating the integrands when sampling costs
\mathcal{O}(1)
. However, the proposed greedy optimization method is expected to be particularly efficient in more computationally intensive applications, such as when sampling involves solving differential equations, where the cost of optimization is negligible relative to the overall simulation cost.

Finally, our numerical results indicate that the error convergence behavior is similar across different choices of base
b
. An interesting open question concerns the best choice of the base
b
, both from theoretical and computational perspectives.
