Echocardiographic Strain-derived Predictors of Coronary Disease Burden Assessed by SYNTAX Score in Non–ST-elevation Acute Coronary Syndrome
Authors: Džan Horozić, Azra Durak-Nalbantić, Mirad Hujdur, Omer Jusić, Amir Redžepović, Azra Đokić Vjezović, Zenisa Gljiva-Gogić, Indira Melezović, Alden Begić, Nedim Vanis, Alen Džubur
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The objective of this study was to evaluate whether global longitudinal strain (GLS), early systolic lengthening (ESL), and postsystolic shortening (PSS), individually and combined, predict anatomical disease severity and significant coronary artery stenosis in non–ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome (NSTE-ACS) with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).Materials and Methods:We prospectively enrolled 112 NSTE-ACS patients with preserved LVEF and no regional wall motion abnormalities. GLS, ESL, and PSS were measured using two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography. Significant coronary artery disease (CAD) was defined as ≥90% stenosis in at least one major epicardial artery, and anatomical burden was quantified using the SYNTAX score. Linear and logistic regression, receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, DeLong comparison, and internal validation were performed.Results:GLS and PSS were significant predictors of SYNTAX score in univariate linear regression. In the multivariable model, only GLS remained independently associated with SYNTAX (P < 0.001), explaining 26.2% of variance (adjusted R² =0.24; variance inflation factor <5). Significant stenosis was present in 70 patients (62%). ESL (P = 0.028) and PSS (P = 0.001) predicted ≥ 90% stenosis in univariate analysis, whereas GLS reached significance only in multivariable analysis (odds ratio 1.28, 95% confidence interval 1.09–1.51, P = 0.003). The multimarker model (GLS + ESL + PSS) had the highest ROC area under the curve (AUC) (0.726) but did not significantly outperform GLS alone (AUC 0.707; P = 0.390). Model-predicted probabilities correlated with SYNTAX (ρ =0.480, P < 0.001).Conclusions:GLS is the strongest strain-derived predictor of both significant stenosis and anatomical CAD burden in NSTE-ACS with preserved LVEF. Strain-based analysis may aid early, noninvasive risk stratification in this population.

INTRODUCTION

Ischemic heart disease remains the leading cause of cardiovascular mortality, accounting for 38% of deaths in women and 44% in men.[12] Non–ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome (NSTE-ACS), including unstable angina and non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), represents over 70% of acute coronary presentations, with rising incidence and mortality.[345] Patients with NSTE-ACS are highly heterogeneous, exhibiting substantial variation in coronary stenosis severity confirmed by angiography. Early detection of patients with significant coronary artery disease (CAD) is particularly challenging in those with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and no regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA).

Two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography (2D-STE) quantitatively assesses myocardial deformation with greater sensitivity than visual wall motion analysis.[1567] Global longitudinal strain (GLS) reflects overall systolic function, while early systolic lengthening (ESL) and postsystolic shortening (PSS) capture subtle regional ischemia.[89] Data on their predictive value for anatomical CAD burden, especially the SYNTAX score, in NSTE-ACS are limited.[510111213141516]

The aim of this study was to assess whether GLS, ESL, and PSS predict both significant coronary artery stenosis and overall CAD severity, quantified by the SYNTAX score, in patients with NSTE-ACS and preserved LVEF.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and population

This prospective study included 112 patients with NSTE-ACS hospitalized at our institution in the period from September 2024 to September 2025. The NSTE-ACS entity assumed the clinical diagnoses of unstable angina pectoris and NSTEMI, according to the 2023 ESC guidelines for acute coronary syndrome. Unstable angina was defined as the presence of anginal symptoms at rest or during minimal exertion, accompanied by one of the following: Prolonged pain at rest, newly developed angina of increasing intensity, a change in frequency or duration of anginal episodes, or angina at a lower threshold of exertion. NSTEMI was diagnosed by a rise and/or fall in cardiac troponin with at least one value above the 99th percentile upper reference limit, in the presence of clinical evidence of myocardial ischemia. In total, 42 patients fulfilled criteria for NSTEMI, whereas the remaining patients were classified as unstable angina.

Patients eligible for inclusion were aged >18 years, admitted with the diagnosis of NSTE-ACS, who had LVEF ≥50% without RWMA and indicated coronary angiography during the index hospitalization.

The exclusion criteria were defined as follows: Prior MI or revascularization, LVEF <50%, - RWMA, left bundle branch block, prior implantation of ICD, CRT, or pacemaker, more than moderate valvular disease, cardiomyopathies, congenital heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, previous paroxysms of atrial fibrillation, storage or connective tissue disorders, chronic renal insufficiency, left ventricular hypertrophy.

Patients were then classified into two groups according to the presence of significant coronary stenosis confirmed by coronary angiography. Stenosis of ≥90% in any major epicardial artery (LAD, RCA, and CX) was considered significant. The ≥90% coronary stenosis cutoff was predefined to focus on anatomically severe, likely flow-limiting lesions. To enhance generalizability and align with conventional angiographic thresholds, a predefined sensitivity analysis using ≥70% stenosis was also performed.

Clinical and demographic data collection

Baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory data were collected prospectively at hospital admission. Demographic and clinical data included age, sex, body mass index, body surface area (BSA), presence of hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, obesity, current smoking, and other conventional cardiovascular risk factors.

Laboratory analyses included complete blood count, renal function (urea and creatinine), liver enzymes (aspartate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase), cardiac biomarkers (CK, CK-MB, high-sensitivity troponin [hs-Tn]), LDH, CRP, electrolytes, and lipid profile (LDL, HDL, total cholesterol, and triglycerides). Serial measurements of CK, CK-MB, and hs-Tn were obtained during the first 24 h after admission.

Echocardiographic analysis

All echocardiographic examinations were performed before coronary angiography (CAG) within the first 24 h after hospital admission. Examination was performed using a Vivid S70N (GE Vingmed, Norway) system. Analyses followed EACVI recommendations and included standard measurements and 2D-STE. Images and videos were be saved for subsequent analysis. At least three cardiac cycles from apical four-, two-, and three-chamber views were be recorded for each patient (frame rate 60–80 fps, Electrocardiogram [ECG] gated).

Left ventricular systolic function and volumes were measured by the biplane Simpson’s method. Left ventricular volumes were indexed by BSA.

STE was performed offline using prerecorded videos. Left ventricular myocardial delineation was automated using software, with manual adjustments as needed. End-systole was defined as the moment of aortic valve closure and end-diastole as the R-wave peak on the ECG.

GLS was computed as the average peak systolic strain of all 17 standard left myocardial segments. ESL was quantified using duration of ESL (DESL). DESL was measured in milliseconds from end-diastole to peak positive strain in early systole. PSS was quantified using the post systolic index (PSI): PSI = (peak negative strain in cardiac cycle − peak negative strain during systole)/peak negative strain in cardiac cycle × 100. Both DESL and PSI were quantified for each of the 17 myocardial segments. Mean values were calculated across all 17 segments and denoted as DESL across 17 myocardial segments (DESL₁₇) and PSI across 17 myocardial segments (PSI₁₇), respectively. Segments without PSS were assigned a value of 0.

To assess measurement reproducibility, intra- and interobserver variability were evaluated in a random subset of 20 patients. Two experienced echocardiographers independently re-analyzed GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇ values, blinded to all clinical and angiographic data. Intraobserver variability was assessed by repeating measurements 2 weeks apart by the same observer. Reproducibility was quantified using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) with a two-way mixed-effects model and absolute agreement definition.

Coronary angiography and SYNTAX score calculation

CAG was performed by experienced interventional cardiologists. The severity of CAD was assessed using the SYNTAX I score calculated using online calculator (accessible at: https://syntaxscore.org/calculator/start.htm).

Workflow overview

All participants underwent a standardized diagnostic workflow during hospitalization. Following initial clinical assessment and confirmation of NSTE-ACS, baseline demographic and laboratory data were collected. All echocardiographic examinations were performed by the principal investigator before CAG to maintain analytical independence from angiographic findings. CAG was performed by a clinician not involved in the study and interpreted according to institutional standards. The SYNTAX I score was subsequently calculated by the investigator based on the official angiography report, without access to strain imaging results at the time of scoring, thereby ensuring full independence between echocardiographic and angiographic assessments.

Patients were then classified into two groups according to the presence of significant coronary stenosis (≥90% in any major epicardial artery). Echocardiographic parameters (GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇) were analyzed individually and in combination to assess their predictive value for both the presence of significant stenosis and the overall anatomical burden of CAD.

Statistical analysis and sample size

Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (Interquartile Range); categorical variables as counts and percentages. Group comparisons used Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, and χ² or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate. Linear regression assessed associations between GLS, DESL₁₇, PSI₁₇, and SYNTAX score. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression identified predictors of significant stenosis (variables with P < 0.100 in univariate analysis entered multivariable models). Model discrimination was assessed via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves (area under the curve [AUC]) and compared using the DeLong test; calibration was evaluated with the Hosmer–Lemeshow test and optimal cutoffs by the Youden index. Predicted probabilities were correlated with SYNTAX score (Spearman’s ρ).

For internal validation, the dataset was split into training (70%) and validation (30%) sets. Calibration was further assessed with bootstrap resampling (1000 iterations) and calibration curves; decision curve analysis evaluated clinical net benefit. Analyses were conducted in R (rms, dcurves, and caret) and cross-checked in SPSS (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 23.0 [IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA]).

Sample size was calculated for 80% power (α =0.05), requiring 79 patients to detect an AUC of 0.70 versus 0.50; 112 patients were enrolled.

A sensitivity analysis using ≥70% stenosis was performed to align with prior angiographic studies, comparing model coefficients and discrimination with the primary ≥90% analysis.

Software: IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0 and R 4.3.1 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria).

Ethical considerations

All patients provided written informed consent before participation. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013. revision). Approval was obtained from the institutional ethics committee before study initiation.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we investigated the ability of GLS, early systolic lengthening (DESL₁₇), and postsystolic shortening (PSI₁₇) to predict anatomical CAD burden, quantified by the SYNTAX score, in patients with NSTE-ACS and preserved LVEF without RWMA. Linear regression analyses showed that GLS was the strongest independent predictor of SYNTAX-defined disease severity, whereas PSI₁₇ contributed in univariate analysis and DESL₁₇ was not significant. The multivariable model including all three strain parameters explained 26.2% of the variance in SYNTAX score, highlighting GLS as the primary determinant of anatomical CAD burden among the evaluated indices.

Regarding the prediction of significant stenoses (≥90%), univariate analyses demonstrated that both DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇ were significant, whereas GLS alone did not reach significance. In multivariable logistic regression, GLS emerged as the only independent predictor, likely reflecting shared variance among physiologically related deformation parameters. VIFs were acceptable (VIF < 5), but the interdependence of these strain indices, each reflecting distinct temporal aspects of longitudinal fiber motion, may attenuate the independent contributions of DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇.

ROC analysis confirmed that the multimarker model combining GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇ achieved the highest AUC (0.726; sensitivity 72.7%, specificity 71.8%). However, DeLong comparison showed that this improvement did not reach statistical significance compared with GLS alone (AUC 0.707). These findings suggest that GLS represents a practical and robust single-parameter marker for identifying patients with significant coronary artery stenosis, while DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇ may offer complementary information in selected clinical scenarios rather than routine incremental diagnostic gain.

Importantly, the predicted probabilities from the multimarker model correlated moderately with SYNTAX score (ρ =0.480, P < 0.001), suggesting that strain-based assessment captures not only the presence of severe stenoses but also the overall anatomical complexity of CAD.

Previous research has established that strain-derived parameters can identify subclinical myocardial ischemia even when LVEF and wall motion appear normal. Grenne et al.[6] first reported that strain imaging could detect acute coronary occlusion in NSTE-ACS despite the absence of RWMA, highlighting its sensitivity to early mechanical dysfunction. Smedsrud et al.[9] further demonstrated that ESL achieved superior diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.83) compared with GLS (AUC = 0.68), while Ishigaki et al.[12] showed that a combined model including GLS, DESL, and post-systolic strain timing improved prediction of LAD stenosis in comparison with GLS (AUC = 0.816 vs. 0.705 for GLS alone). Other investigators, including Brainin and colleagues, emphasized the prognostic significance of ESL and PSS in revascularized and post-myocardial infarction cohorts, but these findings originated largely from stable CAD or single-vessel disease population.[57101214] Giridharan et al.[11] analyzed unstable angina with preserved LVEF and found PSI to be an independent predictor of significant stenosis (AUC = 0.637). They also reported that PSI correlated with SYNTAX score. These results align with our observations, though without multivariable adjustment or integrated GLS assessment.

Recent studies published in the Journal of Cardiovascular Echography further support the role of strain imaging in acute coronary syndromes. Sabry et al.[17] emonstrated that GLS correlates with angiographic severity, while Imamuddin et al.[18] reported the utility of GLS in the prediction of both coronary disease severity and presence of significant coronary artery stenosis. These findings are consistent with our results and reinforce the clinical relevance of GLS as a sensitive marker of subclinical ischemic myocardial dysfunction.

Our study builds on this body of work by analyzing a well-defined NSTE-ACS cohort and systematically linking deformation indices to both significant coronary stenosis and anatomical disease severity. By incorporating the SYNTAX score as a continuous marker of coronary complexity and applying multivariable linear regression, we demonstrated that GLS not only predicts the presence of significant stenosis but also correlates with overall disease burden. This dual diagnostic-anatomical relationship has been underexplored in previous strain imaging studies. Importantly, our results are in line with the meta-analysis by Liou et al.,[19] which reported an average GLS AUC of approximately 0.70 for detecting significant coronary stenosis, reinforcing the clinical validity of our observed performance metrics.

Although no statistical multicollinearity was detected (VIF < 5), GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇ are physiologically interrelated indices reflecting different temporal components of longitudinal myocardial deformation. GLS represents an integrated measure of systolic longitudinal shortening across all segments and therefore incorporates ischemia-related mechanical disturbances that may also manifest as ESL or PSS at the regional level. Consequently, whereas DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇ were significant in univariate analyses, their independent contribution was attenuated in multivariable models after adjustment for GLS. This finding likely reflects shared variance rather than diminished biological relevance and is consistent with prior studies demonstrating overlap among strain-derived ischemic markers. Consequently, GLS can be considered a practical single-parameter predictor for clinical use, whereas DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇ may offer valuable supplementary insight when regional ischemia or equivocal global findings are present. The slightly higher, but not statistically superior, AUC of the multimarker model (vs. GLS alone) in DeLong testing further supports this interpretation.

From a clinical standpoint, these findings highlight GLS as a robust, non-invasive marker for identifying NSTE-ACS patients with higher CAD burden as well as at risk of significant coronary stenosis, even when LVEF is preserved and visual wall motion appears normal. Early recognition of subtle mechanical impairment through strain imaging could assist in risk stratification, guide decisions for invasive coronary angiography, and improve triage in a heterogeneous population where traditional echocardiographic markers are often insufficient. DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇, although less independently powerful, provide additional physiologic and spatial information that may enhance diagnostic confidence in borderline or region-specific ischemia.

The strengths of this study include its focus on a clinically relevant, under-investigated NSTE-ACS subgroup with preserved LVEF, integration of deformation-based echocardiographic indices with angiographic SYNTAX scoring, and rigorous methodological design. Internal validation using random train-test splits demonstrated stable AUC values across subsets, while calibration analysis, bootstrap resampling, and decision-curve analysis confirmed robustness and clinical applicability. In addition, excellent intra- and interobserver reproducibility for GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇ further supports the reliability and consistency of strain-derived measurements in this setting. These statistical validation steps strengthen the credibility of our findings and align with best practices for predictive model development in cardiovascular imaging.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Although modest in size and single-center, it exceeded the pre-specified power calculation and incorporated internal validation, supporting that the observed associations are reliable and hypothesis-generating for future multicenter research. The analyses should therefore be viewed as exploratory. The primary ≥ 90% stenosis threshold was selected to emphasize angiographically severe lesions with a high probability of physiological significance. This choice is supported by findings from the FAME trial,[20] which showed that lesions with ≥ 90% diameter stenosis have a very high likelihood of being functionally significant as assessed by fractional flow reserve. Using this stringent cutoff reduced the likelihood of including physiologically insignificant lesions and strengthened the anatomical–functional interpretation of strain-based findings. Sensitivity analysis using a ≥ 70% definition yielded comparable model performance (AUC 0.748), confirming result consistency and reinforcing the robustness of the primary findings.

Vessel-specific stenosis and long-term clinical outcomes were not analyzed, precluding vessel-level diagnostic or prognostic assessment. Although VIFs were acceptable (VIF < 5), physiological interdependence among GLS, DESL₁₇, and PSI₁₇ may have influenced their independent effects in multivariable modeling. Echocardiographic strain measurements also remain operator- and vendor dependent. Finally, while internal validation confirmed stable model performance, external validation is required to establish reproducibility and generalizability across broader NSTE-ACS populations.

CONCLUSIONS

GLS is the strongest predictor of both overall coronary disease burden in NSTE-ACS, as reflected by SYNTAX score, and ≥ 90% stenosis and. DESL₁₇ and PSI₁₇ provide complementary regional and temporal insight into myocardial ischemia but offer limited incremental diagnostic value beyond GLS in routine clinical application. Strain-based assessment captures anatomical complexity non-invasively and may guide early risk stratification and personalized management.
