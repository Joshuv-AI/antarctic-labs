Subject: TEX4TerraPower Experimental Preparation Report
Introduction
The purpose of TEX-Chlorine for TerraPower (TEX4TerraPower) was to perform critical
configurations to meet the nuclear data needs of TerraPower’s Molten Chloride Fast Reactor
(MCFR) and Molten Chloride Reactor Experiment (MCRE) projects. It also supported
criticality safety validation for Idaho National Laboratory (INL) fuel fabrication to support the
MCFR. One configuration consisted of highly enriched uranium fuel (HEU), NaCl salt
absorbers, high density polyethylene (HDPE) moderators, and 1 in. HDPE reflectors. The
configuration was designed with two HEU fuel plates per layer, arranged as HEU, moderator,
HEU, moderator, followed by a salt plate assembly. The salt plate assembly consisted of a
solid salt disc encapsulated between two aluminum containers. This configuration was
intended to maximize thermal sensitivity relative to the sensitivity profiles for assessed
upset conditions for the fuel fabrication operations. A second configuration used the Zeus
copper reflectors in place of the HDPE reflectors, no moderators and additional 21 in. HEU
plates were added to the 15 in. HEU plates used in the polyethylene configuration to
maximize fast neutron sensitivity and address uncertainty in the fast component of the
chlorine capture cross section. Two sets of 1/2 in. and 1 in. thick salt absorbers were
procured for this experiment, with the 1/2 in. set for the thermal configuration and the 1 in.
set for the fast configuration.

Below are the new components, nominal specifications, dimensional measurements, and
mass measurements that were used in the experiment during January 2026.

1. IER 630 New Salt Plate Components

1.1 Salt Plate, Encapsulations for ½ in Salt Plate
Twenty-two lower encapsulation halves were procured and designated 1 through 22 for the
thermal polyethylene-reflected configuration. The encapsulations were fabricated from Al-
5083 and had an outer diameter of 15 in. (38.1 cm), a height of 0.344 in. (0.873 cm), an inner
diameter of 14.03 in. (35.636 cm), and a base thickness of 0.081 in. (0.20574 cm). The upper
encapsulation had the same specifications as the lower encapsulation, except that the
screw hole diameter was larger to accommodate the screw head. The upper encapsulation
was configured as the complementary half of the lower encapsulation to minimize internal

gaps surrounding the salt absorber. Tables 2 and 3 describe the specified dimensions and
measured values.

After receipt, Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) measured each of the new
components using standard tooling, which is specified in Table 1. The exception is that LANL
did not perform measurements on the ½ in. solid NaCl salt plates due to time constraints.
Measurements were available from the fabricator, RCO engineering, and from their
verification measurements. The RCO Engineering data will be presented for those
components.

1.3 Salt Plate, Encapsulations for 1 in. Salt Plate

Seventeen lower encapsulation halves were procured and designated 1 through 17 for the
fast copper-reflected configuration. The encapsulations were fabricated from Al-5083 and
had an outer diameter of 15 in. (38.1 cm), a height of 0.589 in. (1.49606 cm), an inner
diameter of 14.03 in. (35.6362 cm), and a base thickness of 0.081 in. (0.20574 cm). The

upper encapsulation had the same specifications as the lower encapsulation, except that
the screw hole diameter was larger to accommodate the screw head. The upper
encapsulation was configured as the complementary half of the lower encapsulation to
minimize internal gaps surrounding the salt absorber. The annular upper and lower
encapsulations had a hole outer diameter of 2.935 in. (7.4549 cm) and a hole inner diameter
of 2.511 in. (6.37794 cm). Tables 7 and 8 describe the specified dimensions and measured
values of the 1 in. upper and lower encapsulations, while Tables 9 and 10 describe the
annular upper and lower encapsulations.

2. Summary

This report documents the dimensional and mass inspection results for the salt plate
components procured for TEX-Chlorine for TerraPower. The inspection included the 1/2 in.
and 1 in. salt plates and their corresponding aluminum encapsulations. Including lower and
upper encapsulation halves, solid NaCl plates, annular salt components, and assembled
salt plate units intended for the thermal and fast-spectrum configurations. Measured values
were compared against drawing specifications and tolerances to verify component
acceptability for use in the planned experiment. Notably, the report identified missing record
data for part 13 of the 1 in. upper annular encapsulation and the absence of LANL records
for the 1/2 in. solid NaCl plates, including their assembled dimensions and mass.
