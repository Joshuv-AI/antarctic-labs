Stochastic Ordering under Weaker Likelihood-Ratio Shape Conditions
Zakaria Derbazi

Abstract
We show that the shape hypothesis on a likelihood ratio can be weakened while retaining endpoint criteria for the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders. The endpoint reduction persists under unimodality of the likelihood ratio and under a sign-pattern condition on the likelihood ratio minus one, with at most two sign changes and a negative right tail. It also follows from a direct superlevel-set criterion involving the same expression, which is useful in particular for discontinuous likelihood ratios.

1
Preliminaries

Let
E
be a totally ordered support, either an integer interval or an interval of
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with counting measure or Lebesgue measure according to the case. All probability measures considered below are dominated by the corresponding reference measure
\mu
. For a probability measure
P
on
E
, write
f_{P}:={\mathrm{d}}{}P/{\mathrm{d}}{\mu}
, and similarly for
Q
.

For a pair of probability measures
P
and
Q
, set
\rho:=P+Q
and
S:=\supp(\rho)
. The likelihood ratio is

\ell(x):=\frac{{\mathrm{d}}{}P/{\mathrm{d}}{}\rho}{{\mathrm{d}}{}Q/{\mathrm{d}}{}\rho},\qquad x\in S,

(1)

with the conventions
a/0:=+\infty
for
a>0
and
0/0:=0
. The likelihood ratio coincides with the
\mu
-density ratio
f_{P}/f_{Q}
wherever both
\mu
-densities are positive.

We use
X,Y
for random variables with values in
E
, distributed according to the law under consideration, and
\mathbb{E}
for expectation under the law currently in scope. When the probability measure needs to be made explicit, we write
\mathbb{E}_{P},\mathbb{E}_{Q}
.

When
E
is an integer interval, we write
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}
and refer to sets of the form
[a,b]:=\{a,\dots,b\}
or
[a,\infty):=\{a,a{+}1,\dots\}
as intervals in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Forward differences are
\Delta h(k):=h(k+1)-h(k)
and
\Delta^{2}h(k):=h(k+2)-2h(k+1)+h(k)
; we call
k
admissible for
\Delta^{2}
when
k,k+1,k+2
all lie in the relevant support. We write
\psi=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
for the digamma function. The indicator of a set
A\subseteq E
is
\mathds{1}\{A\}
.

Definition 1.1

(Unimodality)
.

A real-valued function
a
is
unimodal
on an interval
J\subseteq E
if there exists
x_{0}\in J
such that
a
is nondecreasing on
J\cap(-\infty,x_{0}]
and nonincreasing on
J\cap[x_{0},\infty)
. On an integer interval this is the usual notion of a unimodal sequence.

Definition 1.2

(Log-concavity)
.

A positive function
a:J\to(0,\infty)
on an interval
J\subseteq E
is
log-concave
if
\log a
is concave on
J
. On an integer interval this is equivalent to

a_{k}^{2}\geq a_{k-1}a_{k+1},

for every admissible
k\in J
, or equivalently to
\Delta^{2}\log a(k)\leq 0
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
P
and
Q
be probability measures on
E
, with survival functions
\bar{F}_{P}(x):=P([x,\infty)\cap E)
and
\bar{F}_{Q}
analogously.

(i)

Usual stochastic order
:
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
if

\bar{F}_{P}(x)\leq\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
for every
x\in E
; equivalently,
\int\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}{}P\leq\int\varphi\,{\mathrm{d}}{}Q
for every bounded nondecreasing
\varphi:E\to\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

Hazard-rate order
: writing
h_{P}(x):=f_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{P}(x)
for the hazard-rate when
\bar{F}_{P}(x)>0
,
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
if the survival ratio
\bar{F}_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
is nonincreasing in
x
, equivalently
h_{P}(x)\geq h_{Q}(x)
, wherever both expressions are defined.

(iii)

Likelihood-ratio order
:
P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
if the likelihood ratio
\ell
is nonincreasing on
\supp(P)\cup\supp(Q)

[
4
,
6
]
.

(iv)

Relative log-concavity

[
8
,
10
]
: when
\supp(P)\subseteq\supp(Q)
and
\supp(P)
is an interval in
E
,
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
if
\log\ell
is concave on
\supp(P)
. In the discrete case this is equivalent to
\Delta^{2}\log\ell(k)\leq 0
for every admissible
k\in\supp(P)
, while in the continuous case it is equivalent to
(\log\ell)^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
wherever the second derivative exists.

2
Endpoint Criteria under Weaker Shape Conditions

Throughout this section,
P
and
Q
are two laws whose supports form, or are contained in, an interval
J\subseteq E
, with densities
f_{P},f_{Q}
positive on the relevant support, and
\ell
denotes the likelihood ratio of (
1
). Each result restates the precise support hypothesis it needs (common support, support union, or support inclusion). The first step is immediate from the definitions:

P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q\iff\ell\text{ is nonincreasing on }J,\qquad P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q\iff\log\ell\text{ is concave on }J.

In the discrete case these become
\Delta\log\ell(k)\leq 0
and
\Delta^{2}\log\ell(k)\leq 0
, while in the continuous case they become
\partial_{x}\log\ell(x)\leq 0
and
\partial_{x}^{2}\log\ell(x)\leq 0
wherever the derivatives exist.

2.1
Unimodality and relative log-concavity

When the likelihood ratio is only known to be unimodal, the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders still reduce to a left-endpoint test.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
P,Q
be probability measures on
E
, and let
\ell
be the likelihood ratio on
S:=\supp(P)\cup\supp(Q)
as in
(
1
)
. If
S
is an interval with finite left endpoint
x_{*}:=\inf S
, and
\ell
is unimodal on
S
, then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
.

(ii)

P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
.

(iii)

\ell(x_{*}+)\geq 1
, where
\ell(x_{*}+)
denotes the right limit of
\ell
at
x_{*}
.

Proof.

We prove
\textup{(iii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}
,
\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(iii)}
,
\textup{(i)}\Rightarrow\textup{(ii)}
, and finally
\textup{(ii)}\Rightarrow\textup{(i)}
.

Set

d(x):=f_{P}(x)-f_{Q}(x),\qquad x\in S.

On
S
, the sign of
d(x)
agrees with the sign of
\ell(x)-1
under the conventions in (
1
). Since
\ell
is unimodal, the superlevel set

A:=\{x\in S:\ell(x)\geq 1\}

is an interval in
S
. In this case,
d
has at most one sign change on
S
, from nonnegative to nonpositive.

First, (iii)
\Longrightarrow
(i). Recall that the unimodality of
\ell
ensures that the right limit
\ell(x_{*}+)
exists in
[0,\infty]
. Suppose
\ell(x_{*}+)\geq 1
. By unimodality, either
\ell\geq 1
on some right-neighbourhood of
x_{*}
, in which case
A
is an initial interval of
S
; or
\ell<1
throughout some such neighbourhood, which forces
\ell(x_{*}+)=1
and
\ell\leq 1
on all of
S
, hence
f_{P}\leq f_{Q}
and
\bar{F}_{P}\leq\bar{F}_{Q}
. In both cases,
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
.

It remains to treat the case where
A
is an initial interval of
S
.
Define

D(x):=P([x_{*},x)\cap E)-Q([x_{*},x)\cap E)=\int_{[x_{*},x)\cap E}d\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu.

The function
D
starts at
0
, increases while
d\geq 0
, decreases after the sign change, and returns to
0
because
\int_{E}d\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu=0.
Hence
D(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in E
. But
\bar{F}_{P}(x)-\bar{F}_{Q}(x)=-D(x)
on
S
, and the comparison is trivial outside
S
. Consequently,
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
.

Second, (i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii). We proceed by contraposition. Suppose (iii) does not hold, that is,
\ell(x_{*}+)<1
. Then
A
does not contain a right-neighbourhood of
x_{*}
, so there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\ell(x)<1\qquad\text{for every }x\in S\cap(x_{*},x_{*}+\varepsilon).

Hence
d<0
on that initial segment, so
D(x)<0
there. Since
\bar{F}_{P}(x)-\bar{F}_{Q}(x)=-D(x)
on
S
, this gives
\bar{F}_{P}(x)>\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
on the same segment, implying
P\not\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
.

Third, (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii).
Assume
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
, and set

J^{\prime}:=\{x\in S:\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0\}.

Then, for every
x\in J^{\prime}
,

T(x):=\dfrac{\bar{F}_{P}(x)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(x)}\leq 1.

We claim that
T(x)\leq\ell(x)
on
J^{\prime}
. This is immediate when
\ell(x)\geq 1
. If
\ell(x)<1
, then
x\notin A
. Since
A
is an interval and (i) implies (iii), the set
A
must be an initial interval of
S
. Therefore

\ell(y)\leq\ell(x)\qquad\text{for every }y\in S\cap[x,\infty).

Consequently,

\bar{F}_{P}(x)=\int_{[x,\infty)\cap E}f_{P}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu\leq\ell(x)\int_{[x,\infty)\cap E}f_{Q}\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu=\ell(x)\bar{F}_{Q}(x),

and hence
T(x)\leq\ell(x)
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, then for every
k\in J^{\prime}
with
\bar{F}_{Q}(k+1)>0
, write

T(k)-T(k+1)=\frac{P(\{k\})+\bar{F}_{P}(k+1)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(k)}-\frac{\bar{F}_{P}(k+1)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(k+1)}.

If
Q(\{k\})=0
, then
\bar{F}_{Q}(k+1)=\bar{F}_{Q}(k)
, and the right-hand side reduces to
P(\{k\})/\bar{F}_{Q}(k)\geq 0
. If
Q(\{k\})>0
, the right-hand side equals

\frac{Q(\{k\})}{\bar{F}_{Q}(k)}\bigl(\ell(k)-T(k+1)\bigr).

We show that this is nonnegative. When
\ell(k)\geq 1
, then
T(k+1)\leq 1\leq\ell(k)
. When
\ell(k)<1
, then
k\notin A
, and since
A
is an initial interval of
S
and
\ell
is unimodal,
\ell
is nonincreasing on
S\setminus A
; therefore
\ell(k+1)\leq\ell(k)
. Combining with the already-proved
T(k+1)\leq\ell(k+1)
yields
T(k+1)\leq\ell(k)
. In either case
T(k)\geq T(k+1)
, so
T
is nonincreasing and
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, then
\bar{F}_{P}
and
\bar{F}_{Q}
are absolutely continuous, and
\bar{F}_{Q}
is bounded away from
0
on every compact subinterval of
J^{\prime}
, so
T=\bar{F}_{P}/\bar{F}_{Q}
is absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of
J^{\prime}
. For a.e.
x\in J^{\prime}
,

T^{\prime}(x)=\frac{f_{Q}(x)\bar{F}_{P}(x)-f_{P}(x)\bar{F}_{Q}(x)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(x)^{2}}.

Observe that the sign of
T^{\prime}(x)
matches that of
T(x)-\ell(x)\leq 0
when
f_{Q}(x)>0
, and the sign of
-f_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{Q}(x)\leq 0
when
f_{Q}(x)=0
. In either case,
T^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
. Consequently,
T
is nonincreasing on
J^{\prime}
, so once more
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
. This proves the implication.

Finally, (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i) follows from the standard implication
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q\Longrightarrow P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q

[
6
]
.
∎

The strongest natural hypothesis on

is relative log-concavity. Under it, Theorem

specialises to a single endpoint check for
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
and
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
, and the

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
P
and
Q
be probability measures on
E
with common support interval
J\subseteq E
and finite left endpoint

. If
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
, then
\ell
is unimodal on

P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q\iff P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q\iff\ell(x_{0}+)\geq 1.

Proof.

Since
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
,
\ell
is log-concave on
J
, hence unimodal

3
]
. Applying Theorem
2.1
with
S=J
and
x_{*}=x_{0}
gives the equivalence.
∎

Building on this result, the likelihood-ratio order reduces to a single-point endpoint test.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume the hypotheses of Corollary
2.2
hold. If
x_{0}\in\supp(P)
, then

P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q\iff\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}(x_{0}+)\leq 0&E\subseteq\mathbb{R}\\
\ell(x_{0}+1)-\ell(x_{0})\leq 0&E\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0},\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
,
\ell
is unimodal as established in the previous proof. The assumption
x_{0}\in\supp(P)
gives
\ell(x_{0})>0
.

First, consider the case
E\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Concavity of
\log\ell
implies
\Delta\log\ell(k)
nonincreasing in
k
. Therefore,
\Delta\log\ell(k)\leq 0
for every admissible
k\in J
if and only if
\Delta\log\ell(x_{0})\leq 0.
It follows that
\ell(x_{0}+1)\leq\ell(x_{0})
since
\ell(x_{0})>0
. Hence,
P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
. The case
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is analogous. Concavity of
\log\ell
makes its right derivative nonincreasing on
J
. Therefore,
(\log\ell)^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
on
J
if and only if
(\log\ell)^{\prime}(x_{0}+)\leq 0
. Since
\ell(x_{0})>0
, the sign of
(\log\ell)^{\prime}(x_{0}+)
coincides with that of
\ell^{\prime}(x_{0}+)
. Hence,
(\log\ell)^{\prime}(x_{0}+)\leq 0
coincides with
\ell^{\prime}(x_{0}+)\leq 0
, and therefore to
P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
.
∎

Example 2.4

(Gamma versus Gamma)
.

For
P=\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})
and
Q=\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{2},\beta_{2})
on
(0,\infty)
, where
\mathrm{Gamma}(r,\beta)
denotes the gamma law with shape
r
and scale
\beta
, we have

\ell(x)=\frac{\Gamma(r_{2})\beta_{2}^{r_{2}}}{\Gamma(r_{1})\beta_{1}^{r_{1}}}x^{r_{1}-r_{2}}\exp\!\left(-(\beta_{1}^{-1}-\beta_{2}^{-1})x\right),\qquad x>0.

and

\partial_{x}^{2}\log\ell(x)=-\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{x^{2}},

Hence,
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
if and only if
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
. Under that assumption, Corollary
2.2
gives

\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{st}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{2},\beta_{2})\iff\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{2},\beta_{2})\iff\ell(0+)\geq 1.

Now
\ell(0+)=0
when
r_{1}>r_{2}
, while
r_{1}=r_{2}=r
implies that
\ell(0+)=\left(\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{1}}\right)^{r}.
Therefore, under the relative log-concavity condition
r_{1}\geq r_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{st}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{2},\beta_{2})

\displaystyle\iff\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{1},\beta_{1})\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}\mathrm{Gamma}(r_{2},\beta_{2})

\displaystyle\iff r_{1}=r_{2}\ \text{and}\ \beta_{1}\leq\beta_{2}.

The next example illustrates a comparison that is unimodal but not relative-log-concave, so Theorem
2.1
applies but Corollary
2.2
does not.

Example 2.5

(Half-Student in degrees of freedom)
.

Let
|X_{\nu}|
be the half-Student law on
[0,\infty)
with
\nu
degrees of freedom. The density is

f_{\nu}(x)=\frac{2\,\Gamma((\nu+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\nu\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu/2)}\Bigl(1+\frac{x^{2}}{\nu}\Bigr)^{-(\nu+1)/2}.

For
\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}
, a direct calculation gives

\partial_{x}\log\ell(x)=\frac{(\nu_{2}-\nu_{1})\,x(1-x^{2})}{(\nu_{1}+x^{2})(\nu_{2}+x^{2})},

which is positive on
(0,1)
and negative on
(1,\infty)
. Thus,
\log\ell
increases up to
x=1
and decreases afterwards, so both
\log\ell
and
\ell
are unimodal on
[0,\infty)
, with a unique mode at
x=1
. On the other hand,
\log\ell
is not concave on
[0,\infty)
: its right derivative at
0
is
0
, but it becomes positive immediately to the right of
0
, which cannot happen for a concave function. Therefore Theorem
2.1
applies, whereas Corollary
2.2
does not. The endpoint condition then becomes

|X_{\nu_{2}}|\leq_{\mathrm{st}}|X_{\nu_{1}}|\iff|X_{\nu_{2}}|\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}|X_{\nu_{1}}|\iff\ell(0)\geq 1\iff f_{\nu_{2}}(0)\geq f_{\nu_{1}}(0),

and
\nu\mapsto f_{\nu}(0)
is increasing on
(0,\infty)
, since concavity of
\psi
together with the recurrence
\psi(z+1)-\psi(z)=1/z
gives
\psi(z+\tfrac{1}{2})-\psi(z)>1/(2z)
. Hence, larger degrees of freedom give a smaller half-Student random variable in both the usual stochastic and hazard-rate orders:

\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|X_{\nu_{2}}|\leq_{\mathrm{st}}|X_{\nu_{1}}|\quad\text{and}\quad|X_{\nu_{2}}|\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}|X_{\nu_{1}}|.

2.2
Sign-pattern endpoint criteria

The unimodality hypothesis can be relaxed to a sign-pattern condition on
\ell-1
.
[
9
]
proved the corresponding endpoint reduction for nonnegative random variables. In our notation, their criterion assumes that, on
[0,\infty)
,
\log\ell
has at most two roots and is negative beyond the largest root. Under that hypothesis, the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders reduce to the single endpoint check
\ell(0)\geq 1
, with the hazard-rate part also using the same right-tail monotonicity as in Proposition
2.8
.

Recall that on
(0,\infty)
the zero sets of
\log\ell
and of
\ell-1
coincide, and so do their sign patterns. The Yao–Lou–Pan hypothesis therefore amounts to two requirements: a sign-pattern condition (at most two sign changes of
\ell-1
, negative on the rightmost piece) and a regularity condition (the zero set is discrete, i.e., at most two points). The support-interval version below relaxes the regularity requirement, retaining only the sign pattern, which is the only ingredient the conditional tail-expectation argument actually uses.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a probability law on an ordered support
J\subseteq E
with finite least element
x_{0}
, and let
\phi:J\to\mathbb{R}
be
\mu
-integrable with
\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]=0
. If
\phi
has at most two sign changes on
J
and is nonpositive on the rightmost part, then

\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq 0\quad\text{for every }x\in J\text{ with }\bar{F}_{\mu}(x)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\phi(x_{0})\geq 0,

where the forward implication additionally requires that, in the case
\phi(x_{0})=0
,
\phi
is nonnegative on a right-neighbourhood of
x_{0}
.

Proof.

Set
D(x):=\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]
on
\{x\in J:\bar{F}_{\mu}(x)>0\}
, and
I(x):=\int_{[x_{0},x)}\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu
. Multiplying
D(x)
by
\bar{F}_{\mu}(x)
gives

D(x)\bar{F}_{\mu}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\bigl[\phi(X)\mathds{1}\{X\geq x\}\bigr]=-I(x),

where the last equality uses
\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]=0
. Hence,
D(x)
and
I(x)
have opposite signs.

The function
I
vanishes at both ends of
J
:
I(x_{0})=0
trivially, and
\lim_{x\uparrow\sup J}I(x)=\int_{J}\phi\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu=0
. Moreover,
I
is continuous (or piecewise constant in the discrete case) and monotone on each maximal interval where
\phi
has constant sign (nondecreasing where
\phi\geq 0
and nonincreasing where
\phi\leq 0
).

Suppose first that
\phi(x_{0})\geq 0
. Under the right-neighbourhood hypothesis when
\phi(x_{0})=0
, the sign-change bound gives a point
b\in J
such that
\phi\geq 0
on
[x_{0},b]\cap J
and
\phi\leq 0
on
[b,\sup J)\cap J
. Zero intervals between the two pieces are absorbed into either side. Hence,
I
starts at
0
, increases while
\phi\geq 0
, then decreases while
\phi\leq 0
, and ends once more at
0
. Consequently,
I(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in J
, so
D(x)\leq 0
wherever it is defined.

Conversely, if
\phi(x_{0})<0
, then
I(x)<0
on some right-neighbourhood of
x_{0}
where
\bar{F}_{\mu}(x)>0
, and therefore
D(x)>0
there.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
P,Q
be probability laws on
E
whose common support interval
J\subseteq E
has finite left endpoint
x_{0}:=\min J
, and let
\ell
be the likelihood ratio on
J
, assumed continuous when
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
\phi:=\ell-1
has at most two sign changes on
J
and is negative
on the final sign-constant part of the interval. If
\ell(x_{0})=1
, assume in addition that
\ell\geq 1
on a right-neighbourhood of
x_{0}
, then

P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q\iff\ell(x_{0})\geq 1.

If, in addition,
\ell
is nonincreasing on the rightmost sign-constant part of the interval of
\phi
(equivalently,
\ell^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
there when
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, or
\Delta\ell(k)\leq 0
there when
J
is an integer interval), the same equivalent conditions characterise
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
.

Proof.

Working under
Q
, the function
\phi(x):=\ell(x)-1
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)]=\int_{J}\bigl(f_{P}-f_{Q}\bigr)\,{\mathrm{d}}{}\mu=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]=\frac{\bar{F}_{P}(x)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(x)}-1

on
\{x\in J:\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0\}
, and inherits from
\ell
at most two sign changes on
J
with
\phi<0
on the rightmost sign-constant interval. Therefore,
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q\iff\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq 0
, holds for every
x\in J\text{ with }\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0
. Applying Lemma
2.6
to
(\phi,Q)
gives

P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q\iff\phi(x_{0})\geq 0\iff\ell(x_{0})\geq 1.

This is the equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii); the converse direction of Lemma
2.6
furnishes the implication
\ell(x_{0})<1\Rightarrow P\not\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
directly.

For the
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
part, on
\{x\in J:\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0\}
,

P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q\iff\frac{f_{P}(x)}{\bar{F}_{P}(x)}\geq\frac{f_{Q}(x)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(x)}\iff\ell(x)\geq\frac{\bar{F}_{P}(x)}{\bar{F}_{Q}(x)},

that is,
\phi(x)\geq\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]
. Assume
\ell(x_{0})\geq 1
and
\ell
is nonincreasing on the rightmost sign-constant interval of
\phi
. If
\phi(x)\geq 0
, the
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
part of the proposition gives
\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq 0\leq\phi(x)
. If
\phi(x)<0
, then
x
lies on the rightmost (sign-constant) part of the interval, so
\phi(u)\leq\phi(x)
for every
u\geq x
, hence
\mathbb{E}_{Q}[\phi(X)\mid X\geq x]\leq\phi(x)
. Consequently,
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
. The converse follows from the standard implication
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q\Longrightarrow P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q

[
6
]
.
∎

A direct alternative to Proposition
2.8
is to control only the shape of the superlevel set
\{\ell\geq 1\}
at the left endpoint. The next result specialises Proposition
2.8
to a single sign change of
f_{P}-f_{Q}
, in exchange for dropping the continuity hypothesis on
\ell
when

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
P,Q
be probability laws on
E
whose common support interval
J\subseteq E
has finite left endpoint
x_{0}:=\min J
. If
\ell(x_{0})\geq 1
and the superlevel set

A:=\{x\in J:\ell(x)\geq 1\}

is an interval, then
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
. If, in addition,
\ell
is nonincreasing on
J\setminus A
, then
P\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}Q
.

Proof.

Since
\ell(x_{0})\geq 1
, the left endpoint belongs to
A
, so the interval assumption gives
A=[x_{0},r]\cap J
for some
r\in J\cup\{\sup J\}
. Hence
\ell\geq 1
on
A
and
\ell\leq 1
on
J\setminus A
, which means
f_{P}-f_{Q}
has at most one sign change on
J
, from
+
to
-
. The argument from (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) of Theorem
2.1
(using only the single sign change of
f_{P}-f_{Q}
, not the unimodality of
\ell
) gives
P\leq_{\mathrm{st}}Q
.

For the
\leq_{\mathrm{hr}}
direction, set
T(x):=\bar{F}_{P}(x)/\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
on
\{x\in J:\bar{F}_{Q}(x)>0\}
. On
A
we have
\ell\geq 1
, so
f_{P}\geq f_{Q}
, and combining with
\bar{F}_{P}\leq\bar{F}_{Q}
, established in the first part, gives
h_{P}\geq h_{Q}
. On
J\setminus A
,
\ell
is nonincreasing and
\ell\leq 1
. The bound
T(x)\leq\ell(x)
on
J\setminus A
follows from the same tail-comparison proof of (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in Theorem
2.1
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii): for
x\in J\setminus A
and
y\geq x
, monotonicity of
\ell
on
J\setminus A
gives
\ell(y)\leq\ell(x)
, so
\bar{F}_{P}(x)\leq\ell(x)\bar{F}_{Q}(x)
. That is,
T(x)\leq\ell(x)
. The discrete and continuous monotonicity arguments of that proof then yield
T
nonincreasing on
J\setminus A
. Hence,
h_{P}\geq h_{Q}
holds also on
J\setminus A
.
∎

Example 2.10
.

Figure 2
:
Likelihood-ratio shapes from Example
2.10
.

Theorems 2.1 and 2.3 of
[
9
]
assume, for nonnegative random variables, that
\log\ell
has at most two roots and is negative beyond the largest root; by Remark 2.5 in
[
9
]
, the same endpoint reduction holds on a finite support interval
[0,a)
. For brevity, we refer to these original hypotheses as the
Yao–Lou–Pan (YLP) condition. Figure
2
exhibits six representative situations.

(A)

\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}
and YLP hold, but
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
does not.

For
P=\mathrm{HN}(1)
(half-normal with scale
1
) and
Q=\mathrm{Exp}(\tfrac{1}{2})
(exponential with rate
\tfrac{1}{2}
). Easy calculation gives
\log\ell(x)=\log\!\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi}}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{x}{2},
which is concave with a unique interior maximum. Thus
P\leq_{\mathrm{lc}}Q
and the original YLP hypothesis holds, but
P\not\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
.

(B)

YLP-type case.

The folded-normal pair
|Z_{i}|
with
Z_{i}\sim N(\mu_{i},\sigma_{i}^{2})
,
i=1,2
, is the illustrative example of
[
9
]
(Remark 2.2, Example I): they invoke Theorem 1 of
[
7
]
for the
\leq_{\mathrm{st}}
part and Proposition 3 of the same reference for the behaviour of the sign of
\ell^{\prime}
on
(0,\infty)
. Together, these place the comparison outside relative log-concavity but inside their two-roots framework. Proposition
2.8
reaches the same conclusion via the sign pattern of
\ell-1
, and so applies directly here.

(C)

YLP fails because
\log\ell
vanishes on an interval. Also
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
does not hold.

Take
Q=\mathrm{Exp}(1)
,
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<a_{3}
,
\mu>1
, and

f_{P}(x)=\begin{cases}\exp(-x),&0\leq x\leq a_{1},\\[2.84526pt]
\bigl[1+c\,(x-a_{1})(a_{2}-x)\bigr]\exp(-x),&a_{1}\leq x\leq a_{2},\\[2.84526pt]
\exp(-x),&a_{2}\leq x\leq a_{3},\\[2.84526pt]
\exp((\mu-1)a_{3})\exp(-\mu x),&x\geq a_{3},\end{cases}

with
c>0
determined by
\int f_{P}=1
. Then
\ell\equiv 1
on
[0,a_{1}]\cup[a_{2},a_{3}]
,
\ell>1
on
(a_{1},a_{2})
, and
\ell<1
on
(a_{3},\infty)
. The likelihood-ratio order does
not
hold here, since
\ell
is not monotone and YLP fails because
\log\ell
vanishes on an interval. However, Proposition
2.8
still applies.

(D)

YLP fails because
\log\ell
has more than two zeros.

Take
Q=U[0,1]
and let
P
have density

f_{P}(x)=\begin{cases}1+\varepsilon(x-\tfrac{1}{6})^{2},&0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
1+\varepsilon(x-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2},&\tfrac{1}{3}\leq x\leq\tfrac{2}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{36}-\tfrac{5\varepsilon}{18}(x-\tfrac{2}{3}),&\tfrac{2}{3}\leq x\leq 1,\end{cases}\qquad\varepsilon=3.

Then
\ell=f_{P}
touches the level
1
at
x=\tfrac{1}{6}
and
x=\tfrac{1}{2}
, and crosses it at
x=\tfrac{23}{30}
. Thus
l=\log\ell
has three zeros, so the YLP root-count fails, while Proposition
2.8
still applies because
\ell-1
has sign pattern
+,-
. Again
P\not\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
.

(E)

Discrete case, YLP does not apply.

Fix
0<\pi<1
and
\lambda>0
. Let
P
be the zero-inflated Poisson law with
P(\{0\})=\pi+(1-\pi)e^{-\lambda}
, and
P(\{k\})\propto(1-\pi)e^{-\lambda}{\lambda^{k}}
for
k\geq 1
. Let
Q=\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda)
denote the Poisson law with mean
\lambda
. Then

\ell(0)=1-\pi+\pi e^{\lambda}>1,\qquad\ell(k)=1-\pi<1,\quad k\geq 1.

Hence
\{\ell\geq 1\}=\{0\}
, so Proposition
2.9
applies directly.

(F)

YLP root-count fails but
\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}
holds.

Take
Q=U[0,1]
and let
P
have density

f_{P}(x)=\begin{cases}1+\varepsilon(1-4x)^{2},&0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{4},\\[2.84526pt]
1,&\tfrac{1}{4}\leq x\leq\tfrac{3}{4},\\[2.84526pt]
1-\varepsilon(4x-3)^{2},&\tfrac{3}{4}\leq x\leq 1,\end{cases}\qquad 0<\varepsilon<1.

Observe that
\ell=f_{P}
is nonincreasing. Thus,
P\leq_{\mathrm{lr}}Q
, but
l=\log\ell
vanishes on the whole interval
[\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{3}{4}]
. The hypothesis of at most two roots therefore fails even though
\ell
is unimodal, whereas Proposition
2.8
still applies because the sign pattern of
\ell-1
is
+,-
.

3
Conclusion

We have shown that the left-endpoint reduction for the hazard-rate and usual stochastic orders persists well beyond relative log-concavity of the likelihood ratio. Besides the unimodal case, it remains valid under a sign-pattern condition and under a direct superlevel-set criterion that also covers discontinuous likelihood ratios.
