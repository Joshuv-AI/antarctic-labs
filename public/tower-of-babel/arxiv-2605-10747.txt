Theory of Spin-splitter Magnetoresistance in Altermagnets
Tim Kokkeler, Vitaly N. Golovach, F. Sebastian Bergeret

Abstract
We develop a theory of angle-dependent magnetoresistance (ADMR) in metallic altermagnets coupled to ferromagnetic insulators and establish criteria that distinguish them from conventional compensated magnets with spin–orbit coupling. We show that the spin-splitter magnetoresistance (SSMR) reported by H. Chen et al. [ Adv. Mater. 37 , 2507764 (2025)] constitutes a smoking-gun signature of collinear altermagnetism in metallic systems. In contrast to spin-Hall magnetoresistance (SMR), SSMR exhibits three key distinctions: it depends solely on the relative orientation between the ferromagnetic magnetization and the altermagnetic Néel vector, yields a longitudinal ADMR response of opposite sign, and features a direct proportionality between longitudinal and transverse ADMR signals, absent in SMR. These results provide a clear route to unambiguously identify altermagnets in transport.

In this Letter, we address whether altermagnets can be distinguished from conventional compensated magnets via MR measurements. We develop a theory of angle-dependent magnetoresistance (ADMR) in metallic
d

10

10

While SSMR was originally introduced in close analogy to SMR, with the spin Hall angle replaced by the spin-splitter mechanism, we find that it differs qualitatively from SMR. As a nonrelativistic effect, SSMR is expected to yield larger signals than SMR and, more importantly, exhibits distinct transport signatures. In particular, the longitudinal response displays an angular dependence opposite to that of SMR, while the transverse resistance is directly proportional to the longitudinal correction, in contrast to SMR. These features establish SSMR as a distinct class of ADMR, separate from spin–orbit-driven magnetoresistance such as SMR and HMR, and identify it as a powerful probe of altermagnetism.

There are three marked differences between the SSMR and SMR. First of all, there is a sign difference between the two contributions. Indeed, while the SMR resistance is maximized along a particular axis and minimized in the plane perpendicular to this axis, the SSMR is minimized along a certain axis and maximized in the plane perpendicular to it. This difference is due to the fact that the altermagnet tensor
T_{jk}
is symmetric in its indices, so that
T_{xy}T_{yx}=T_{xy}^{2}>0
, while the spin-Hall tensor
\theta_{ajk}
is antisymmetric so that
\theta_{zxy}\theta_{zyx}=-\theta_{zxy}^{2}<0
. This means that the SSMR and the SMR can be directly distinguished by considering the dependence of the resistance on the magnetization direction, as illustrated in Fig.
2

Negative SMR signals have also been discussed for systems in heavy metal antiferromagnetic insulator hybrids. This was explained by a tendency of the Neel vector to orient perpendicular to the magnetic field

A second difference between the SSMR and SMR is that for SSMR the spin quantization axis is uniquely determined by the direction of the Néel vector. This difference stems from the fact that the altermagnet tensor
N_{a}T_{jk}
can be written as a direct product of the Néel vector and a real space tensor, while this is not possible for the spin-Hall tensor
\theta\varepsilon_{ajk}
in which all indices are coupled. This feature can be detected experimentally in four-probe setups.

Thirdly, the transverse resistance is distinctly different for the SSMR and the SMR. Indeed, within the SSMR the transverse resistance is directly proportional to the correction of the longitudinal resistance and thus has the same magnetic field dependence. On the other hand, in the SMR the transverse and longitudinal resistances have distinctly different angle dependence

\bm{N}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{s}
, while in the SMR the longitudinal and transverse responses are determined by different components of the spin-accumulation.

A fourth feature that distinguishes the SSMR from the SMR is revealed by considering different Hall bars which are related via a 90 degrees rotation and are located on the same domain of the altermagnet. Indeed, the spin-Hall effect is isotropic, so rotation symmetry dictates the longitudinal resistance should be the same in both bars, while the transverse resistance has opposite sign. On the other hand, for SSMR, the longitudinal resistivities of the bar are proportional to
T_{xy}^{2}
and
T_{yz}^{2}
respectively and therefore they do not necessarily have the same magnitude. The transverse resistivities on the other hand are both proportional to
T_{yz}T_{yz}

In order to distinguish altermagnets from antiferromagnets, it is imperative to compare SSMR with the SMR in such materials instead. B

not only in the numerator, but also in the denominator. On the other hand, the sign of SMR for antiferromagnets is the same as for the SMR for normal metals and thus opposite to SSMR. Moreover, the magnitude of the effect is determined by the relative direction of
\bm{N}
and
\bm{n}\times\bm{j}
. This shows that the SMR in antiferromagnets can be easily distinguished both from the usual SMR and from the SSMR.

In conclusion, we have uncovered a new type of ADMR, the SSMR.
While the SSMR bears similarities with the SOC-induced SMR, we highlighted several differences that can be used to distinguish the two. We have shown that an extension of the experimental studies in Ref.
10
that includes the entire angle dependence of the longitudinal and transverse ADMR can be used to unambiguously detect the existence of a

d

Magnetric insulators as those used in SMR, such as YiG

22
may serve to test our predictions. We recommend to ensure that the preferred magnetization direction of the ferromagnetic insulator is much softer than the preferred Néel vector direction of the altermagnet, and to use weak fields, to reduce the reorientation of the Néel vector in the presence of magnetic fields.
Another important design criterion stems from the fact that all altermagnets except those of the

45

\sin^{2}\delta

However, since SMR is relativistic and AMR and HMR strongly depend on the strength of the external magnetic field while in SSMR the magnetic field is only needed to rotate the magnetization of the FI, we expect that the SSMR is dominant in the low-field regimes, as is the case for SMR effects

Appendix B
Antiferromagnets

To disentangle the effects of altermagnetism and spin-orbit coupling it is important to compare the SSMR with the SMR effect in antiferromagnets. To study the SMR effect in antiferromagnets, one actually has to resort to diffusion equations that incorporate both magnetism and spin-orbit coupling, which leads to a whole range of terms, such as relaxation and an anisotropic spin-Hall tensor.

However, to lowest order, we may consider the diffusion equations for an antiferromagnet
42
, which is the same as for altermagnets but with
T_{jk}=K_{jk}=0
due to the absence of spin-splitting, and add an isotropic spin-Hall tensor
\theta\varepsilon_{jkl}
. Indeed, any other spin-to-charge conversion effects are of higher order in quasiclassics and therefore small.

We choose the current direction to be
\hat{x}
and the normal along
y
. Since there are now three directions in spin space (
\bm{n}\times\bm{j},\bm{m},\bm{N}
) general expressions become rather expansive. We therefore decide to linearize in
G_{i,r,s}
for illustrative purposes.

Like in the discussion of the influence of relaxation in altermagnets, we focus on two limits, relaxation due to magnetic impurities dominates over the Dyakonov-Perel type of mechanism, and the other way around.

In the former case the spin-relaxation tensor is isotropic,
l_{s\parallel}=l_{s\perp}=l_{s}
. Since in the normal state magnetism-related relaxation is indistinguishable from relaxation due to SOC, the equations in this case are just those of the original SMR theory
12
. To lowest order in
G_{i,r,s}
, the spin accumulation reads

\displaystyle\bm{z}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{s}=2\theta_{xyz}El_{s}\Big[\frac{\sinh\frac{y}{l_{s}}}{\cosh{\frac{L_y}{2l_s}}}+2\tanh{\frac{L_y}{2l_s}}\frac{\cosh\frac{y-\frac{L_{y}}{2}}{l_{s}}}{\sinh{\frac{L_y}{l_s}}}\Big(\frac{|G_{s}|l_{s}}{\sigma}+\frac{G_{r}l_{s}}{\sigma}(1-\bm{z}\cdot\bm{m})^{2}\Big)\Big]\;,

(57)

and the currents are given by

\displaystyle J_{x}

\displaystyle=L_{y}\sigma E-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\theta_{yxz}\int_{-\frac{L_{y}}{2}}^{\frac{L_{y}}{2}}dy\partial_{y}(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{\mu}^{s})

\displaystyle=L_{y}\sigma E+2\theta_{xyz}^{2}\sigma El_{s}\tanh{\frac{L_y}{2l_s}}-2\theta_{xyz}^{2}\sigma E\tanh^{2}\frac{L_{y}}{2l_{s}}\Big(\frac{|G_{s}|}{\sigma}+G_{r}(1-(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{m})^{2})\Big)\;.

(58)

Note the sign difference compared to the SSMR case is purely due to
\theta_{yxz}=-\theta_{xyz}
.
Consider the case in which the relaxation for spins perpendicular to the axis is much stronger than for those parallel to it. In this case we obtain
\bm{\mu}^{s}\approx\mu^{s}\bm{N}
and we have

\displaystyle\partial_{yy}\mu^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{l_{s\parallel}^{2}}\mu^{s}\;.

(59)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\mu^{s}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{xyz}E\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z}\;,

\displaystyle y=\frac{L_{y}}{2}\;.

(60)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\mu^{s}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{xyz}E\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z}+2(\frac{|G_{s}|}{\sigma}-\frac{G_{r}}{\sigma}\bm{N}\cdot(\bm{m}\times(\bm{m}\times\bm{N}))-G_{i}\bm{N}\cdot(\bm{m}\times\bm{N}))\mu^{s}

\displaystyle=2\theta_{xyz}E\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z}+2(\frac{|G_{s}|}{\sigma}+\frac{G_{r}}{\sigma}(1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2})\mu^{s}\;,

\displaystyle y=-\frac{L_{y}}{2}\;.

(61)

The solution to this equation is

\displaystyle\mu^{s}=\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z}2\theta_{xyz}E\Big[\frac{\sinh{\frac{y}{l_{s\parallel}}}}{\cosh{\frac{L_y}{2l_{s\parallel}}}}+l_{s\parallel}\tanh{\frac{L_y}{2l_{s\parallel}}}\frac{\cosh{\frac{y-\frac{L_y}{2}}{l_{s\parallel}}}}{\sinh{\frac{L_y}{l_s}}}\Big(\frac{|G_{s}|l_{s\parallel}+G_{r}l_{s\parallel}(1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2})}{\sigma+(|G_{s}|l_{s\parallel}+G_{r}l_{s\parallel}(1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2}))\coth{\frac{L_{y}}{l_{s\parallel}}}}\Big)\Big]\;.

(62)

With this, we have

\displaystyle J_{x}

\displaystyle=L_{y}\sigma E-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\theta_{yxz}\int_{-\frac{L_{y}}{2}}^{\frac{L_{y}}{2}}dy\partial_{y}(\bm{z}\cdot\bm{N}\mu^{s})=L_{y}\sigma E+2\theta_{xyz}^{2}\sigma El_{s}\tanh{\frac{L_y}{2l_s}}(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z})^{2}

\displaystyle-2\theta_{xyz}^{2}\sigma(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z})^{2}E\tanh^{2}\frac{L_{y}}{2l_{s}}\Big(\frac{|G_{s}|l_{s\parallel}+G_{r}l_{s\parallel}(1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2})}{\sigma+(|G_{s}|l_{s\parallel}+G_{r}l_{s\parallel}(1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2}))\coth{\frac{L_{y}}{l_{s\parallel}}}}\Big)\;.

(63)

Meanwhile the transverse current is

\displaystyle J_{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{L_{y}}{2}}^{\frac{L_{y}}{2}}dy-\sigma(\partial_{z}\mu+\theta\varepsilon_{yzx}\partial_{y}\mu^{s}\bm{N}\cdot\bm{x}+\theta\varepsilon_{xzy}\partial_{x}\mu^{s}\bm{N}\cdot\bm{y})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{L_{y}}{2}}^{\frac{L_{y}}{2}}dy-\sigma\theta_{xyz}\bm{N}\cdot\bm{x}\partial_{y}\mu^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bm{N}\cdot\bm{x}}{\bm{N}\cdot\bm{z}}(J_{x}-L_{y}\sigma E)\;.

(64)

We thus see that SMR in antiferromagnets has features of both the usual SMR and of the SSMR. Indeed, like the SSMR, the resistance depends on the angle between the magnetization of the FI and the Néel vector and the transverse and longitudinal resistivities have the same angle dependence. Moreover, like in SSMR, the anisotropic relaxation leads to an anharmonicity due to the appearance of
1-(\bm{N}\cdot\bm{m})^{2}
not only in the numerator, but also in the denominator.

On the other hand, the sign of SMR for antiferromagnets is the same as for the SMR for normal metals, it is minimized along a specific axis and maximized along a plane. Moreover, this sign difference means that the anharmonicity leads to a widening of the peaks and tightening of the dips, opposite to the SSMR. The magnitude of the effect is relativistic and it is determined by the relative direction of
\bm{N}
and
\bm{z}
. This shows that the SMR in antiferromagnets can be distinguished both from the usual SMR and from the SSMR. In the case spin-relaxation is only slightly anisotropic the MR depends on both
\bm{m}\cdot\bm{N}
and
\bm{m}\cdot\bm{z}
. This case can therefore be clearly distinguished from conventional SMR and SSMR as well.
