BiLSTM deep foundation pit deformation prediction method integrating attention mechanism
Authors: Qiaoling Pei, Yayu Guo, Yuan Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPredicting foundation pit deformation is a significant challenge for foundation pit engineering. The inaccuracy of deformation prediction is increased by the intricacy of subterranean space and the variety of construction conditions. Thus, this study develops a deformation prediction model that combines the attention mechanism and bidirectional long short term memory network (BiLSTM) in order to increase the accuracy of deep foundation pit deformation prediction. Meanwhile, to enhance the generalization ability of the model, this study introduces combined regularization in the loss function and adds a Dropout mechanism in the network structure. This study takes a deep foundation pit excavation project in Guangzhou as an example. Experiments shows that the model proposed in the study can complete convergence in about 30 training rounds, and the training loss is maintained at the 0.03 level. Meanwhile, the maximum absolute error of the model in the prediction of verification data is 1.44 mm, and the minimum error is 0.001 mm. The mean absolute error of the model is 0.311 mm, the root mean square error is 0.433 mm, and the R2 is 0.906, which is better than the comparison model. The attention mechanism and BiLSTM model suggested in this study provides good generalization performance and high prediction accuracy in deep foundation pit deformation prediction, according to experimental results. Its potential for use in engineering safety management is promising.Keywords: BiLSTM, Attention mechanism, Deep foundation pit engineering, Deformation prediction, Settlement, DisplacementSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The development and use of underground space has become an inevitable trend as a result of the rapidly expanding urban construction industry and the resulting shortage of available space. The deep foundation pit project is the foundation and key link of underground space construction. The quality and safety of the foundation pit determines the safety of the overall project. In current construction practice, deep foundation pit projects have gradually transformed into intelligent and refined directions. However, it still faces serious challenges such as long cycle, high difficulty, and many uncontrollable factors1. However, during the monitoring of foundation pit deformation, errors in any link such as survey, design, and construction may induce engineering accidents, resulting in serious casualties and huge economic losses. Traditional foundation pit deformation monitoring relies on manual labor and contact instruments, which has inherent limitations such as discrete data, low efficiency, and difficulty in real-time continuity2. In recent years, intelligent monitoring technology has improved its data acquisition capabilities, but its functions are still centered on post-event response. Compared with simplified models that rely on experience and traditional physical models with complex mechanisms and sensitive parameters, data-driven models represented by deep learning (DL) show significant advantages in processing high-dimensional, nonlinear time series data. DL methods can directly mine potential patterns from data. It can provide more accurate and adaptive prediction solutions for foundation pit deformation under complex geological and construction conditions, and promote safety decision-making to become intelligent and scientific.

In the current field of foundation pit engineering safety, Y. Li et al. introduced a prediction method based on parallel hybrid deep neural network for the measurement of excavation resistance. This method introduced a residual structure into the convolutional neural network and used gated recurrent units to capture temporal features. The three-axis weighted average absolute percentage error values were 2.89%, 3.5%, and 5.78%, respectively3. For measuring the horizontal displacement (HD) of deep foundation pit, S. Li et al. presented a long-distance displacement sensor based on distributed optical fiber. The outcomes revealed that the sensor could accurately measure displacement with a resolution of 1 cm within a depth of 30 m, enabling real-time automated monitoring of deep foundation pits4. Y. Yao et al. proposed a multi-modal semantic segmentation method based on lidar camera fusion to classify each element of the construction site and accurately segment the mine pile. The results showed that the average segmentation accuracy and segmentation time of each frame of this method reached 95.72% and 0.193s, which could significantly improve the operating performance5. Using the Jinchuan Hydropower Station’s left bank slope fault as an example, M. Xu et al. developed microseismic monitoring and numerical simulation to uncover the process of rock mass fracture deformation. The findings demonstrated that this approach could successfully demonstrate the effects of slope construction disturbance on structural surface activation and geological structural damage, as well as offer stability assessment recommendations for projects of a similar nature6. The formation and tunnel lining displacements brought on by the excavation of earth pressure balance shield tunnels in intricate subway strata were examined by F. Kong et al. To forecast formation surface deformation, this experiment examined the excavation characteristics of several stratigraphic sections and created a machine learning technique that combined extreme value learning machines, particle swarm optimization, and k-fold cross-validation7. B. Yuan et al. investigated the influence mechanism of different particle sizes on geopolymer and its products in the utilization of engineering waste soil. The results showed that the engineering slag with a particle size of < 0.075 mm achieved the highest content of amorphous phase and surface area during calcination8. B. Yuan explored the response of single piles to lateral loads in complex marine environments with different coral sand particle sizes and pile depths. The results indicated that as the particle size decreased, the maximum bending moment of the pile and the resistance of coral sand increased, the rotation point of pile displacement rose, and the displacement at the pile top increased9.

E. Brzychczy et al. presented a thorough approach for modeling and analyzing longwall processes in underground mining using a process mining method based on event data in the field of DL applications of engineering data. The method was predicated on a thorough examination of procedures using sensor data and information gathered from information systems10. Aiming at the engineering risks of excavation work on landslides, C. Kim et al. proposed a real-time excavation trajectory modulation framework based on slope tangential/normal force ratio and zero-moment point. Meanwhile, a data-driven mining force difference prediction model was developed using the DL architecture Transformer. This technique accounted for the remaining error in this prediction by using the developed excavation force difference model11. L. Deng et al. proposed a robotic tactile excavation system for exploring and excavation of unknown objects buried in granular materials. This system first designed an underactuated tactile finger with multimodal tactile sensing, including sensing contact pressure and fingertip motion. Based on multi-modal sensor data, this study built a contact detection model to identify particles and buried objects in real time. Then, a three-dimensional occupancy map of the particles was constructed to guide object grasping12. M. Stranner et al. presented a new method to automatically align existing underground utility engineering databases with measurements taken during excavation works and potentially correct deviations from planned to as-built documents. The method performed infrastructure segmentation based on real-time capture of site excavations, related inferred locations and orientations to existing digital floor plans, and registered the as-planned model into the as-built state13. To achieve real-time detection of rock slag, P. Jiang et al. used a single-stage target detection neural network with a YOLO structure. Meanwhile, it introduced additional curvature features to enhance the differentiation of adjacent rocks, and customized specific anchors to facilitate the determination of bounding boxes. Through extensive testing on the suggested rock slag point cloud dataset, this method confirmed the efficacy of the approach suggested in this article14.Wu et al. proposed a novel integrated framework for real-time thrust prediction of tunnel boring machines, which combined the CNN-LSTM attention primary model with an online residual correction module based on random Fourier features. The results indicated that this method reduced the average absolute percentage error from 2.84% to 1.89%15. Y. Wu et al. proposed a physical information neural network with constitutive model constraints to balance computational efficiency and physical accuracy in earthquake response prediction work. Numerical verification showed that this method outperformed existing methods in simulating single degree of freedom nonlinear seismic responses and multi story shear building structures, especially under limited data conditions, providing an efficient and reliable solution for earthquake response prediction16.

In summary, the discipline of deep foundation pit engineering is currently the subject of numerous studies, substantial project experience, and the development of numerous deformation prediction techniques. Although traditional numerical simulation methods has stable performance in deep foundation pit deformation prediction, numerical analysis relies on manual feature extraction. Moreover, parameter modifications based on on-site numerical models are absent from the majority of models in current research findings. Thus, this study suggests a deformation prediction model based on bidirectional long short term memory networks (BiLSTM) to address the aforementioned issues. By incorporating the attention process into the model, it increases prediction accuracy. To provide scientific backing for the safety and stability of underground engineering, the goal of this research is to increase the precision and real-time performance of settlement deformation prediction of deep foundation pit engineering. At the theoretical level, the theoretical framework for deep learning prediction of foundation pit deformation has been extended by introducing two-dimensional attention and stage adaptive loss weighting in the monitoring point dimension; At the technical level, by combining regularization and Dropout for collaborative optimization and lightweight architecture design, the adaptability and computational efficiency of the model to small sample and high noise engineering data have been improved; At the application level, the model’s generalization ability, engineering practicality, and warning timeliness were verified through cross regional cross validation, comparison with traditional method systems, and integration solutions with real-time monitoring systems. The three aspects of innovation support each other, forming a research cycle from theory to technology and then to engineering applications.

Project overview and deep foundation pit data monitoring plan data monitoring plan

Guangzhou’s ongoing deep foundation pit excavation project serves as the case topic for this investigation. This project’s foundation pit excavation area is 22,122 m2, with a maximum excavation depth (ED) of 13.8 m. Conventional pile support is used in the foundation pit project. The bridge on the west side of the project has a bridge deck elevation of 3 to 4 m. Under the bridge is the current road surface, with an elevation of about 32 m, and the rainwater pipe is about 3 to 8 m away from the foundation pit. The south and east sides are open spaces, and the north side is the existing road. It is about 6 m to 20 m away from the foundation pit and has dense pipelines. The nearest pipeline of the project is about 4 m away from the edge of the foundation pit. The safety level of the north side of the foundation pit is Level 1, and the rest is Level 2. The project is located in the alluvial plain of the Pearl River Delta. The landform conditions are flat and the ground elevation is approximately 32 m to 42 m. According to the information revealed by field drilling, the site is composed of fill soil layer, sandy clay soil, and fully weathered rock from top to bottom. During the survey, the depth of groundwater measured is 3.2 m to 6.8 m, and the relative height of the groundwater level to the ground is 18.25 m to 20.96 m. The project area has a subtropical monsoon climate with abundant rainfall, humid climate, sufficient heat, long summers, and short winters. In 2024, the average temperature in the region is 22.8 °C, the average annual precipitation is 1850.1 mm, the average annual sunshine hours is 1692.3 h, and the average annual wind speed is 1.9 m/s. In the Pearl River Delta region, rainfall infiltration can increase soil moisture content, reduce soil strength, increase pore water pressure, thereby inducing additional displacement of retaining structures and surface settlement.

The project area is located in the Pearl River Delta river network, and the water level dynamics are complex. The annual variation of the phreatic level is usually 1 to 3 m, and the pressure range of the water level is greatly affected by tides. The phreatic water mainly exists in shallow fill soil and granite residual soil layer. In addition to atmospheric precipitation, its supply source is also affected by tides in the southern river network area. The main ways of excretion are evaporation, discharge into rivers and seepage into deep layers. Affected by both rainfall and tides, the water level changes significantly dynamically. The water level elevation of the specific site needs to be determined by on-site survey. Confined water mainly exists in deep sand layers or rock fissures and caves. Its supply sources are mainly lateral runoff and upper river supply. In offshore areas, water levels and chemical composition may be affected by tides. The stability of the foundation pit floor and the possibility of a surge are significantly impacted by the annual variation in water level, which is typically less than that of diving. Additionally, spherical weathering and granite residual soil have negative geological effects in the project region. When exposed to water, it readily softens and disintegrates, which compromises the stability of the foundation and foundation pit slope. Effectively monitoring and forecasting the deformation of deep foundation pit is essential since these issues jeopardize the project’s quality and safety. BiLSTM deep foundation pit deformation prediction integrating attention mechanism

After obtaining the nine monitoring data of the deep foundation pit through the data monitoring solution, the monitoring data is used as the input of the neural network prediction model, and the deformation amount of the deep foundation pit monitoring corresponding time is used as the output result. The model focuses on two core safety indicators: surface settlement and horizontal displacement of the retaining structure, and predicts deformation during the critical construction stage of the foundation pit from excavation to bottom slab pouring. The prediction of the deep foundation pit’s subsequent deformation is achieved through model training. This study uses the BiLSTM model for deformation prediction. BiLSTM introduces a bidirectional parallel processing mechanism, which can more fully mine and integrate contextual dependencies determined by time order in the sequence. Therefore, it demonstrates optimized feature extraction and pattern recognition capabilities in complex sequence modeling tasks21. The core structure of BiLSTM consists of two independent LSTM layers. Figure 2 depicts the particular structure.

To achieve the engineering application of the proposed model, further research is needed to explore its integration scheme with real-time monitoring systems. The integrated architecture is divided into three layers: data collection layer, intelligent prediction layer, and warning application layer. The data acquisition layer acquires the monitoring data in real time at the set frequency (once an hour in the encryption phase and once a day in the conventional phase) through the automatic monitoring equipment (static level, inclinometer, total station, etc.). It transmits the data to the cloud or local server through Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) protocol after the edge computing node completes data cleaning, outlier elimination and normalization preprocessing. The Attention BiLSTM model trained by the intelligent prediction layer deployment adopts a sliding window mechanism to dynamically update the input sequence. Rolling prediction is triggered every time new data collection is completed, and deformation prediction values for the next 24 h, 72 h, and 7 days are output. The warning application layer automatically generates graded warning information based on the ratio of predicted results to standard limits, combined with the trend of deformation rate changes, and pushes it to project management personnel in real time through web and mobile terminals.

Moreover, it considered batch processing capacities of 16, 32, 64, and 128 for training. The activation function considers Sigmoid, Tanh, and ReLu. The performance evaluation index of the model adopted the MAE on the validation set. The search process first fixed other parameters, traversed the candidate values of each type of hyperparameter separately, recorded the MAE of the model on the validation set, and selected the parameter value that minimized the MAE as the optimal value of the hyperparameter. All hyperparameters were determined based on the same training and validation set partitioning to ensure fairness in comparison. Figure 6 displays the performance of the particular model.

To verify the generalization ability of the model under different engineering conditions and geological backgrounds, this study selected three urban deep excavation engineering monitoring data with significant differences in geological conditions for cross dataset testing. This study took sample data from Guangzhou as case A, deep foundation pit engineering in Shanghai as case B, and foundation pit engineering in Chengdu as case C.

Case B was located in Pudong New Area, Shanghai, which was a typical soft soil area in the coastal plain of the Yangtze River Delta. The excavation area of the foundation pit was 18,650 square meters, with a maximum ED of 16.2 m. It adopted a strong support system of underground continuous wall and three reinforced concrete internal supports. The surrounding area was adjacent to multi story residential buildings and urban main roads, with underground pipelines crisscrossing and requiring extremely high deformation control. The engineering safety level was level one. The soil layer of the site was mainly composed of deep saturated soft clay and silty clay, with a total thickness of over 20 m. It had high water content, high compressibility, low strength, significant creep and thixotropic characteristics, and was one of the most difficult typical strata for deformation control in foundation pit engineering in China. The groundwater at the site was buried at a depth of 1.5–3.0 m and belonged to a diving type aquifer. It was significantly affected by the tides of the Yangtze River and atmospheric rainfall, and had long-term deformation effects such as soft soil rheology and consolidation settlement. The deformation of the foundation pit was not only controlled by the current excavation conditions, but also closely related to the previous stress history and soil consolidation state. The time and spatial effects of deformation were extremely significant. The monitoring data covered the entire process from excavation of the foundation pit to pouring of the bottom plate for a total of 210 days. A total of 89 monitoring points were deployed on site, focusing on monitoring core safety indicators such as surface settlement, horizontal displacement of the enclosure structure, support axial force, and groundwater level. The maximum settlement during the entire construction process of the foundation pit was 34.2 mm, and the maximum horizontal displacement was 41.5 mm. The deformation showed a significant nonlinear growth characteristic. After excavation to the soft soil layer, the deformation rate accelerated significantly, and there was a clear trend of post construction consolidation settlement.

Case C was located in Qingyang District, Chengdu City, and belonged to the typical sand and gravel formation of the Minjiang alluvial fan in the western Sichuan Plain. The excavation area of the foundation pit was 25,300 square meters, and the maximum ED was 14.5 m. The support system was composed of row piles and prestressed anchor rods, and the surrounding area was adjacent to the city’s core commercial district and operating subway lines. The deformation control requirements were strict, and the engineering safety level was level one. The soil layer of the site was composed of artificial fill layer, silty clay layer, and thick layer of sand and gravel layer from top to bottom. The thickness of the sand and gravel layer exceeded 15 m, with high pebble content and good grading. It had engineering characteristics of high strength and strong permeability, and was the most representative stratum of urban deep foundation pits in southwestern China. The groundwater depth of the site was 2.0–4.5 m, belonging to pore type groundwater. The sand and gravel aquifer had strong water richness and high permeability coefficient. Precipitation construction had a significant impact on the deformation of the foundation pit, and there were adverse geological risks such as seepage deformation and piping in the sand and gravel layer. The deformation of the foundation pit was highly coupled with the excavation sequence and precipitation regulation. The monitoring data covered the entire process of layered excavation of the foundation pit for a total of 150 days, with a total of 112 monitoring points deployed on site, comprehensively covering monitoring projects such as surface settlement, horizontal displacement of retaining structures, anchor stress, groundwater level, and soil slope measurement. The maximum settlement during the entire construction process of the foundation pit was 18.6 mm, and the maximum horizontal displacement was 22.3 mm. The deformation showed a significant sudden change after each layer of excavation and dewatering, and then gradually stabilized.

Discussion and interpretation

As subterranean area continues to expand, aggressive underground rail transit development emerged as a successful strategy for reducing traffic congestion in cities. The soil and supporting structures were stressed and distorted during the foundation pit project’s excavation process, which resulted in displacement and deformation of the surrounding soil and structures. The deformation of deep foundation pits was a typical multi factor coupled spatiotemporal sequence problem, which was determined by soil properties, groundwater level, rainfall, excavation sequence, support installation timing, support stiffness, surrounding loads, and construction disturbances. A single time series modeling was difficult to fully explain the deformation mechanism. Traditional physical models explicitly expressed the above factors through mechanical parameters, but their applicability was limited in complex sites due to their dependence on parameter calibration and constitutive assumptions. Although data-driven models did not directly input physical parameters, they could implicitly extract multi factor coupling patterns from monitoring data, making them more suitable for nonlinear and strongly disturbed scenarios on site. Therefore, for the foundation pit project to be managed safely, the deformation of the foundation pit must be efficiently tracked and anticipated during engineering experiments. In summary, deeply integrating data-driven advantages with engineering physical mechanisms and economic cost constraints in current deep foundation pit safety management was the inevitable path to build the next generation of intelligent construction risk management and control systems.

Prediction models based on different principles showed significant differences in effectiveness when dealing with engineering settlement problems. Traditional Bayesian inverse analysis combined with stochastic methods could effectively quantify the uncertainty of soil parameters such as compression index. However, its prediction confidence interval relied on a large amount of high-quality monitoring data for updating and convergence, otherwise it might lead to an underestimation of the failure probability31. In contrast, the Attention-BiLSTM model used in this study showed stronger nonlinear time series data fitting capabilities. Ablation experiments showed that the optimization process from basic LSTM to the introduction of attention mechanism and bidirectional structure could significantly reduce the MRE from 12.83% to 5.55%. The model maintained high accuracy in Guangzhou granite residual soil, Shanghai soft soil, and Chengdu sandy gravel strata, proving its ability to learn common deformation laws under different soil properties, hydrological conditions, support forms, and excavation processes. However, it also indicated that soft soil had stronger deformation delay and more significant nonlinearity due to creep and rheological properties, and relatively higher prediction errors, indicating that the model performance was still constrained by geological physical mechanisms. This highlighted the direct contribution of model architecture optimization to improving PA. Therefore, this research avoided the reliance on complex physical parameters and prior distributions in the Bayesian method, and provided an effective tool for complex engineering scenarios that lacked complete mechanism understanding. In addition, current research moved from single model competition to multi-strategy fusion optimization. The essence of the proposed weight optimization and 1D CNN-BiLSTM prediction residual method was to improve the stability and accuracy of the prediction results through integrated learning and residual correction strategies. This provided a technical reference for future model optimization32. The deeper optimization direction lied in how to deeply integrate engineering physical mechanisms and data models. For example, it was found through the Plackett-Burman test that settlement was extremely sensitive to construction parameters such as “earth pressure” and “grouting amount”33. Therefore, future prediction models need to be able to effectively incorporate such key engineering constraints and control thresholds in the input layer or loss function design. Through this optimization, it can not only further improve the prediction accuracy, but also directly provide decision-making basis for real-time optimization of construction plans34. The current settlement prediction of foundation pit and tunnel engineering is developing towards deep integration with high accuracy, strong interpretation, and decision-making ability. The Attention-BiLSTM model constructed through research is a powerful practice in this direction.

Conclusion

A deep foundation pit deformation prediction model based on Attention+BiLSTM was proposed in this study, which was based on the deep foundation pit engineering project. Additionally, it used the model to forecast surface displacement, settlement, and supporting structure displacement throughout project construction. This study first analyzed the optimal structure and experimental parameters of Attention+BiLSTM through experiments. The two-layer LSTM network model with 96 neurons had the smallest MEA (0.175 mm), according to the data. Additionally, it was found that the AF was ReLu and the training batch size was 32. The study then trained the model and tested its performance using monitoring data from the deep foundation pit engineering project. Experimental results showed that the Attention-BiLSTM model completed convergence after more than 30 training rounds. The model training loss and validation loss were 0.03 and 0.05, which were lower than the unoptimized model. Meanwhile, the MRE of the final training data set of the pure LSTM model in the ablation experiment was 12.83%. The MRE of the LSTM+Attention model was 8.33%, and the MRE of the Attention-BiLSTM model was 5.55%. In addition, the verification data set results showed that the maximum RE of SE+BiLSTM was 7.81%, and the maximum RE of Attention+BiLSTM was 8.57%. The MRE of Attention+BiLSTM was 3.32%, which was lower than the 3.64% of the SE+BiLSTM model. Other performance indicators of the Attention+BiLSTM model were better than other models. Its MAE was 0.311 mm, RMSE was 0.433 mm, and its coefficient of determination was 0.906. The results showed that the Attention+BiLSTM constructed in this study had high accuracy in predicting the deformation of deep foundation pit and had certain applicability. In the deep foundation pit project, the amount of deformation was also affected by factors such as construction technology and hydrogeological conditions. The limitation of this study is that the above influencing factors were not considered in the experiment. Therefore, in future research, the content of training samples will be added to analyze the predictive performance of the model under conditions such as soil geology, supporting structures, and construction techniques, thereby strengthening the model’s versatility.
