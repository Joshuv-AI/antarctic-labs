Abstract
. The theory of total positivity was shown by Lusztig to be intrinsically linked to the canonical basis with its positivity properties. When we restrict ourselves to studying total positivity just for the set of lower-triangular unipotent Toeplitz matrices, say in type A A , then there is a similar link with the quantum cohomology rings of flag varieties and the Schubert bases and their positivity properties. Namely, this builds on a theory of Dale Peterson [ 34 ] that gives a uniform Lie-theoretic description of all of the quantum cohomology rings q ​ H ∗ ​ ( G / P ) qH^{*}(G/P) . In a precursor [ 42 ] to this paper, the Schubert basis and quantum parameters in q ​ H ∗ ​ ( S ​ L n / B ) qH^{*}(SL_{n}/B) , which restrict to positive-valued functions on totally positive Toeplitz matrices, were analysed with respect to their limiting behaviour as n → ∞ n\to\infty , uncovering a novel connection with the classical Edrei theorem on parametrising the infinite totally positive Toeplitz matrices. In this paper we study the Grassmannian case, using the conventions from the S ​ L n / B SL_{n}/B setting as a guide, and we determine the quantum parameter and Schubert class asymptotics in different scenarios. Along the way, we obtain a new interpretation of the strange duality involution on the localised quantum cohomolgy ring of the Grassmannian. Finally, we prove an asymptotic formula for quantum parameters in a partial flag setting, and we furthermore formulate some conjectures concerning partial flag varieties and related quantum cohomology asymptotics.

(

The interest in the
n\to\infty
asymptotics of Theorem
1.3
arose from a beautiful classical theory of total positivity for infinite Toeplitz matrices dating back to the early 1950’s. Namely, in the infinite setting there is a classical parametrisation result which looks quite different to the one above. It includes two infinite families of parameters as well as the parametrisation map going in the opposite direction: from parameters to Toeplitz matrices. This parametrisation theorem was originally conjectured by Schoenberg
[
44
]
with proof later given in
[
2
,
1
,
16
]
. We refer to the two infinite parameter sequences in this parametrisation theorem as the ‘Schoenberg parameters’ and to the theorem itself as Edrei’s theorem, for his key contribution given in
[
16
]
. Let us call a sequence
(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
of nonnegative real numbers
totally nonnegative
if the infinite upper-triangular Toeplitz matrix
(c_{{i-j}})
is totally nonnegative.

Theorem 1.5

(Edrei’s theorem)
.

A sequence
(1,c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
is totally nonnegative if and only if its generating function is of the form

(1.7)

1+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots=e^{\gamma x}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+\beta_{i}x}{1-\alpha_{i}x},

for some
((\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}),\gamma)\in\Omega_{S}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
where
\Omega_{S}
denotes the parameter space

\Omega_{S}=\left\{(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\boldsymbol{\alpha}={(\alpha_{i})}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ with }\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha_{i+1}\text{ and }\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}<\infty\\
\boldsymbol{\beta}={(\beta_{j})}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ with }\beta_{j}\geq\beta_{j+1}\text{ and }\sum_{j}\beta_{j}<\infty\end{array}\right.\right\}.

Further normalised (
c_{0}=c_{1}=1
) infinite totally positive Toeplitz matrices and their Schoenberg parameters are classically related to irreducible normalised characters of the infinite symmetric group, by work of Thoma
[
47
]
from the 1960’s. Moreover, these characters are limits of normalised characters
\chi_{\lambda_{n}}/(\chi_{\lambda_{n}}(e))
of
S_{n}
as
n\to\infty
, and subsequent work of Vershik and Kerov
[
48
]
from the 1980’s interprets the
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
as limits of normalised arm and leg-lengths of the Young diagrams
\lambda_{n}
. It is interesting to observe the breadth of perspectives on Edrei’s theorem, whose original proof belongs to the field of analysis and Nevalinna theory, but which has such clear representation theoretic significance. We note also that the theory of totally positive sequences, or Polya frequency sequences as they are sometimes referred to, is wide-ranging in itself. For example, it has a continuous analogue, and there are even reformulations of the Riemann hypothesis in terms of total positivity
[
23
,
20
]
. We mention
[
10
,
22
]
as further references concerning classical total positivity and Thoma’s related theory.

The main goal of the paper is to determine analogous asymptotical formulas in the Grassmannian setting. The tools in this case are quite different from the ones used in
[
42

, since for Grassmannians we are able to employ rather explicit descriptions of the Peterson Toeplitz variety. However,
[
42

was critically involved in the process of choosing the correct conventions, that we expect should allow generalisation of our results to partial flag varieties more generally. An additional result concerns the strange duality automorphism. This is an involution of (localised) Grassmannian quantum cohomology that was constructed independently by Postnikov and Hengelbrock. Our main results in this paper can be summarised as follows.

(1)

After careful setting up of conventions we provide analogues of Theorem
1.6
for
Gr(n-k,n)
and
Gr(k,n)
and prove associated asymptotic formulas for Schubert classes in two different ways.

(2)

For the Peterson-Toeplitz variety

of

X_{P_{n}}^{(2n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})

in terms of Edrei’s theorem.

(3)

We give a new interpretation of the strange duality automorphism of
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{C})[q^{-1}]
as part of Peterson theory. This interpretation is used in one of the proofs from (1) but may be of independent interest.

(4)

We give conjectural analogues of our results for more general partial flag varieties, and prove a result concerning the asymptotics of the quantum parameters in such a setting.

given by

(2.3)

w_{\lambda}=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\dotsc&k&k+1&k+2&\dotsc&n\\
\lambda_{k}+1&\lambda_{k-1}+2&\dotsc&\lambda_{1}+k&k-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+1&k-\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+2&\dotsc&n-\lambda^{\prime}_{n-k}\end{pmatrix}.

Equivalently, we may consider the SE border of
\lambda
inside its bounding rectangle as an
n
-step path from the SW corner to the NE corner consisting of a sequence of northward and eastward steps. Let

index the northward steps, and
i_{k+1}<i_{k+2}<\dotsc<i_{n}
the eastward steps of this path. Then the permutation

has the property that
w_{\lambda}(\ell)=i_{\ell}
for

. Note that
w_{0}w^{(k)}_{\lambda}w_{0}^{-1}=w^{(m)}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, where
m=n-k
, and the superscript is keeping track of the descent.

We can now describe the
k\times k
-minor
\Delta_{k}^{\lambda}:=\Delta_{k}^{w_{\lambda}}
explicitly in terms of the Young diagram
\lambda
by

(2.4)

\Delta_{k}^{\lambda}(u)=\Delta_{k}^{w_{\lambda}}(u)=\langle u\bar{w}_{\lambda}\cdot v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k},v_{n-k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\rangle=\langle u\cdot v_{i_{1}}\wedge v_{i_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{i_{k}},v_{n-k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\rangle,

where
i_{j}=\lambda_{k-j+1}+j
. Here
\{v_{i}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the formula is expressing
\Delta_{k}^{\lambda}(u)
as a matrix coefficient in the fundamental representation
V_{\omega_{k}}=\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(2.5)

\Delta_{k}^{w_{\lambda}}(u)=\langle u\bar{w}_{\lambda}\cdot v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k},v_{n-k+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\rangle.

Definition 2.2
.

For
w
a Grassmannian permutation of descent
k
define a rational function
\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}\in\mathbb{C}(X)
by

\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}(u)\coloneq\frac{\Delta_{k}^{w_{\lambda}}(u)}{\Delta_{k}(u)}.

Note that
\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}
is regular on
X_{P}
if and only if
k\in I^{P}
, or equivalently, if the Grassmannian permutation
w_{\lambda}
lies in
W^{P}
. In particular each of these
\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}
is regular on
X_{B}
(which is where all the
\Delta_{k}
are nonvanishing). We write

(2.6)

\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}_{P}:=\mathfrak{S}^{w_{\lambda}}|_{X_{P}}\quad\text{if $w_{\lambda}\in W^{P}$}

to obtain a regular function
\mathfrak{S}^{w}_{P}\in\mathbb{C}[X_{P}]
for any such Grassmannian permutation.
While
\mathfrak{S}^{w}_{P}
will always be a function on
X_{P}
, we may use the notation
\Delta^{w}_{k}
to refer to the
k\times k
minor associated to
w\in W^{P_{k}}
more generally, as a function on
SL_{n}
.

Remark 2.3
.

We have
\Delta_{k}=\Delta_{k}^{e}
for the identity element

, and therefore
\mathfrak{S}^{e}(u)=1
. Moreover, if

then

where the numerator

is the minor of

with column set

and row set

. Note that in terms of the transpose

this numerator would be the minor with row set
[k-1]\cup\{k+1\}
and column set

, consistent with
[

, (2.5)]
.

2.3.
The Peterson variety

and

We recall Dale Peterson’s construction of the quantum cohomology rings of the partial flag varieties

[

,
38
,
25

. First, we define the full Peterson variety. It has two equivalent descriptions,

(2.7)

Y:=\{gB_{-}\mid(g^{-1}\cdot F)|_{[\mathfrak{n}_{-},\mathfrak{n}_{-}]}=0\}=\overline{U^{F}_{-}w_{0}B_{-}/B_{-}.}

Here again

when making the constructions in type

Definition 2.4
.

is a Grassmannian permutation of descent

denote the rational function on

to

(

Remark 2.10
.

We note that the two positivity-preserving involutions

Definition 2.12

For any
w\in W

\mathfrak{S}^{w}

3.1.
Grassmannian Toeplitz Matrices

We now consider

For every partition

3.2.
Quantum Cohomology of
Gr(k,n)

We start by recalling in more detail the original description of the quantum cohomology ring
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))
, which goes back to
[
7
,
9
,
45
,
49
,
11
]
.
Let
t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k}
be the Chern roots of the dual tautological subbundle
\mathcal{S}_{k,n}^{\vee}
of
Gr(k,n)
. Let
X_{j}=E_{j}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})
and
Y_{r}=H_{r}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})
denote the elementary and complete symmetric polynomials in the
t_{i}
’s, respectively. Thus
Y_{r}=\det(X_{i-j+1})_{i=1}^{r}
. The cohomology of the Grassmannian is the quotient of the ring of symmetric polynomials
\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k}]^{S_{k}}
by the ideal
I=(Y_{n-k+1},\dotsc,Y_{n})
, and the quantum cohomology is the
1
-parameter deformation (since the Grassmannian has Picard rank
1
) given explicitly by

(3.3)

qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k};q]/(Y_{n-k+1},\dotsc,Y_{n}+(-1)^{k}q).

The Schubert classes are indexed by partitions
\lambda\subseteq k\times{(n-k)}
and form a basis
\{\sigma^{\lambda}\}
of
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{Z})
over
\mathbb{Z}[q]
. We may write
\sigma^{\lambda}=\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\lambda}
to emphasise the Grassmannian
Gr(k,n)
in question, but will generally leave out the subscript when the context is clear.

It was proved by Bertram
[
7
]
that the Schubert basis elements
are represented by Schur polynomials.
Namely, if
\lambda=(\lambda_{1})
then
\sigma^{(\lambda_{1})}=Y_{\lambda_{1}}
, and for general
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})
, the quantum Schubert class
\sigma^{\lambda}
satisfies the ‘quantum Giambelli formula’

(3.4)

\sigma^{\lambda}=\det\begin{pmatrix}Y_{\lambda_{1}}&Y_{\lambda_{1}+1}&Y_{\lambda_{1}+2}&\cdots&Y_{\lambda_{1}+r-1}\\
Y_{\lambda_{2}-1}&Y_{\lambda_{2}}&&&\\
Y_{\lambda_{3}-2}&Y_{\lambda_{3}-1}&Y_{\lambda_{3}}&&\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
Y_{\lambda_{kr}-r+1}&&\cdots&&Y_{\lambda_{r}}\end{pmatrix}

inside
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))
, where
Y_{0}:=1
and
Y_{<0}:=0
. In other words, the usual Giambelli formula in
H^{*}(Gr(k,n))
continues to hold without any
q
-deformation in the quantum cohomology ring. Since, inside the ring of symmetric polynomials, this is the Jacobi-Trudi formula for the Schur polynomial
S_{\lambda}
, we have that
S_{\lambda}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})
represents
\sigma^{\lambda}
. We note that this circumstance is particular to Grassmannians, while for more general partial flag varieties, polynomials representing Schubert classes (quantum Schubert polynomials) need to be deformed to conform with quantum Schubert calculus
[
19
]
.

The isomorphism
Gr(k,n)\cong Gr(m,n)
, where
m=n-k
, determines an isomorphism of quantum cohomology rings that sends
q_{k}
to
q_{m}
and
\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}
to
\sigma^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{P_{m}}
, see
[
8
]
. This also leads to dual versions of the quantum Giambelli and other formulas.
We recall a less standard presentation of the quantum cohomology ring that is more symmetric, which was also given in
[
8
]
,

(3.5)

\Lambda_{k,m}:=\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{k};Y_{1},\dotsc,Y_{m};q]/(X_{1}-Y_{1},X_{2}-X_{1}Y_{1}+Y_{2},\dotsc,X_{k-1}Y_{m}-X_{k}Y_{m-1},X_{k}Y_{m}-q).

Namely, here
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
represent the same classes as above, determining the isomorphism
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{Z})\cong\Lambda_{k,m}
.
We finally introduce a symmetric description of
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{C})[q^{-1}]
as coordinate ring, that will be of use later on.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k},z_{k+1},\dotsc,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid z_{1}^{n}=\dotsc=z_{n}^{n},\,z_{i}\neq z_{j},\,z_{i}\neq 0\}\big/S_{k}\times S_{m},

where the
S_{k}
-factor acts by permuting the first
k
coordinates, and
S_{m}
permutes the remaining

. Note that the action of

is free, and
\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}
is a (reducible) nonsingular affine curve. We denote an element of
\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}
by
[z_{1},\dotsc,z_{n}]_{k,m}
to keep in mind that this expression is symmetric in the first
k
and the last

entries. We have the following lemma that will be very useful later on.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have an isomorphism

\displaystyle qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{C})[q^{-1}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}]

\displaystyle\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\lambda}\quad\qquad

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})=S_{\lambda^{\prime}}(-z_{k+1},\dotsc,-z_{n}).

In particular, under this isomorphism,
X_{i}
maps to
E_{i}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})
and
Y_{j}
to
E_{j}(-z_{k+1},\dotsc,-z_{n})
. Moreover

maps to
E_{n}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k},-z_{k+1},\dotsc,-z_{n})=(-1)^{m}z_{1}\dotsc z_{n}=(-1)^{k+1}z_{1}^{n}
.

Proof.

Note that
z_{1}^{n}=\dotsc=z_{n}^{n}
for distinct
z_{i}\neq 0
is equivalent to the
z_{i}
being a full set of roots of a polynomial of the form
x^{n}-c
with
c\neq 0
. This condition can also be restated as saying that
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+z_{i}x)=1+(\prod z_{i})x^{n}
.

It is straightforward that the coordinate ring of
\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}
is generated by the elementary symmetric functions
\mathbf{X}_{i}:=E_{i}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})
and
\mathbf{Y}_{j}:=E_{j}(-z_{k+1},\dotsc,-z_{n})
. Moreover,
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+z_{i}x)=1+(\prod z_{i})x^{n}
can be rewritten as

(3.7)

(1+\mathbf{X}_{1}x+\mathbf{X}_{2}x^{2}+\dotsc+\mathbf{X}_{k}x^{k})(1-\mathbf{Y}_{1}x+\mathbf{Y}_{2}x^{2}+\dotsc+(-1)^{m}\mathbf{Y}_{m}x^{m})=1+(-1)^{m}\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{m}x^{n}.

The relations between the generators of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}]
are now given by comparing coefficients of

on either side of (

). If we also set
\mathbf{q}:=\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{m}
, then it follows that
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}]
has the presentation

(3.8)

\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{X}_{1},\dotsc,\mathbf{X}_{k};\mathbf{Y}_{1},\dotsc,\mathbf{Y}_{m};\mathbf{q}^{\pm 1}]/(\mathbf{X}_{1}-\mathbf{Y}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{2}-\mathbf{X}_{1}\mathbf{Y}_{1}+\mathbf{X}_{2},\dotsc,\mathbf{X}_{k-1}\mathbf{Y}_{m}-\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{m-1},\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{m}-\mathbf{q}).

This shows that we have an isomorphism
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))\to\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{Y}_{k,n}]
given by
X_{j}\mapsto\mathbf{X}_{j}
and
Y_{r}\mapsto\mathbf{Y}_{r}
and

. But then we deduce that
\mathbf{Y}_{j}=E_{j}(-z_{k+1},\dotsc,-z_{n})
is also equal to

. This extends to the identity

by the dual and the regular Jacobi-Trudi formulas. Finally,

is the image of

as claimed, by the quantum Giambelli formula. It is immediate that
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{X}_{k}\mathbf{Y}_{m}
is given in terms of the

by
(-1)^{m}z_{1}\dotsc z_{n}
. Setting

in the identity

we deduce that

, which implies the second expression for

that was to be proved.
∎

3.2
to get

S_{(m-1)\times k}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})=S_{k\times(m-1)}(-x_{m+1},\dotsc,-x_{n})=E_{k}(-x_{m+1},\dotsc,-x_{n})^{m-1}=(-1)^{k(m-1)}(x_{m+1}x_{m+2}\dotsc x_{n})^{m-1}.

Thereby we obtain a Laurent monomial expression for
q(u^{E})
that we can simplify. Note that

x_{1}\dotsc x_{n}=(-1)^{n+1}x_{1}^{n}
by the final part of Lemma
3.2
.

q(u^{E})=\mathfrak{S}_{P_{k}}^{(1^{k})}\mathfrak{S}_{P_{k}}^{(m)}(u^{E})=\frac{(-1)^{k(m-1)}(x_{m+1}x_{m+2}\dotsc x_{n})^{m-1}}{E_{m}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})^{k+1}}=\frac{(-1)^{k(m-1)}(x_{m+1}x_{m+2}\dotsc x_{n})^{m-1}}{(x_{1}\dotsc x_{m})^{k+1}}\frac{(x_{1}\dotsc x_{m})^{m-1}}{(x_{1}\dotsc x_{m})^{m-1}}\\
=\frac{(-1)^{k(m-1)}(x_{1}\dotsc x_{n})^{m-1}}{(x_{1}\dotsc x_{m})^{n}}=(-1)^{(n-m)(m-1)+(n+1)(m-1)}\frac{x_{1}^{n(m-1)}}{x_{1}^{nm}}=(-1)^{(m+1)}\frac{1}{x_{1}^{n}}.

This is the formula that was to be proved.
∎

4.
Asymptotics of the Peterson Toeplitz variety for
Gr(n-m,n)
with fixed
m

We now wish to describe the infinite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrices that arise as limits of elements of the Peterson Toeplitz variety
X^{(n)}_{P_{n-m}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
associated to
Gr(n-m,n)
as
n\to\infty
(with
m
fixed).

Definition 4.1
.

Let us write
\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
for the infinite lower-triangular Toeplitz matrices over
\mathbb{C}
of the form

(4.1)

u^{\infty}=u_{\infty}(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\dotsc)=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&&&\\
c_{1}&1&&&&\\
c_{2}&c_{1}&1&&&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots&\end{pmatrix},

and
\mathrm{Toep}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, for the analogous
n\times n
Toeplitz matrices
u_{n}(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{n-1})
. We also write
\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
and
\mathrm{Toep}_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, respectively, for their totally nonnegative parts, where all minors are in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We define a truncation map
[\ ]_{n}:\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{Toep}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by setting

{\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&&&\\
c_{1}&1&&&&\\
c_{2}&c_{1}&\ddots&&&\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&&\\
c_{n-2}&&&c_{1}&1&\\
c_{n-1}&c_{n-2}&\dots&c_{2}&c_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}.

Note that truncation restricts to

(4.2)

[\ ]_{n}:\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})\to\mathrm{Toep}_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}).

Moreover, we have that
u^{\infty}
is
totally non-negative
if the truncations
{[u^{\infty}]}_{n}
are totally non-negative for all
n
. We also use the notation
[u]_{n}
for the
n\times n
truncation of a (large enough) finite matrix.

Definition 4.2
.

We say the sequence
{\left(u^{(n)}\right)}_{n>0}
of finite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrices
u^{(n)}\in\mathrm{Toep}_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
converges to
u^{\infty}\in\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
if the matrix entries converge individually.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that
(u^{(n)})_{n}
converges to
u^{\infty}

if and only if
([u^{(n)}]_{N})_{n>N}
converges in
\Toep_{N}(\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Equivalently, we could embed all
\Toep_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
as well as
\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
into
\mathbb{R}[[x]]
sending
u
to
p_{u}(x)
and consider
\mathbb{R}[[x]]
with the product topology (of coefficientwise convergence).

Recall that we fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and consider
X_{P}^{(n)}=X_{P_{n-m}}^{(n)}
for the Peterson Toeplitz variety associated to
Gr(n-m,n)
, that is, to the maximal parabolic subgroup
P=P_{n-m}
in
SL_{n}
(for all
n>m
). We may now consider sequences
{\left(u^{(n)}\right)}_{n>0}
of Toeplitz matrices with
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{n-m}}^{(n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, and ask when they converge to a totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrix, and also which totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrices arise as limits of such sequences.

Remark 4.4
.

While
X_{P_{n-m}}^{(n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
lies in
\Toep_{n}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, the elements of
X_{P_{n-m}}^{(n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
do not lie in the image of the truncation map (
4.2
) unless
m=1
. See also the related Remark
3.11
. Indeed, if the natural infinite extension of
u^{(n)}=u_{n}(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{m})\in X_{P_{n-m}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, the Toeplitz matrix
u^{\infty}=u(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{m},0,\dotsc)
, compare (
4.1
), is totally nonnegative, then its generating function
1+c_{1}x+\dotsc+c_{m}x^{m}
must have negative real roots. Namely by
Theorem

1.5
, these roots are given by
-\beta_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,-\beta_{m}^{-1}\in\mathbb{R}_{<0}
in terms of the Schoenberg parameters
\beta_{1},\dotsc,\beta_{m}
. However, as long as
m>1
we have that any
p_{u^{(n)}}(x)
necessarily has some
non-real
roots when
u^{(n)}\in X^{(n)}_{P_{n-m}}
, as follows from Lemma
3.6
.

Note that the Grassmannian setting differs significantly from the full flag variety
P=B
one in this regard. In the full flag case we have that the restrictions of any strictly totally positive infinite Toeplitz matrix
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
will lie in
X_{B}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
(although still not every element of
X_{B}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
will arise in this way). In any case, this means that the matrices
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
automatically arise as limits of sequences of elements
u^{(n)}\in X^{(n)}_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, simply by setting
u^{(n)}:=[u^{\infty}]_{n}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
be the limit of a sequence
{(u^{(n)})}_{n}
, where
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{n-m}}^{(n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. Then
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, and its generating function is the polynomial

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=1+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{m}x^{m}={(1+\beta x)}^{m},

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is determined by

(4.3)

\beta=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\frac{1}{q^{(n)}}}.

Conversely, for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the infinite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrix with generating function
{(1+\beta x)}^{m}
is obtained as the limit of a sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n}
with
u^{(n)}\in X^{(n)}_{P_{n-m}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.

Proof.

We have that
u^{(n)}
is of the form
u_{n}(c^{(n)}_{1},\dotsc,c^{(n)}_{m},0,\dotsc,0)
, assuming
n>m
. Therefore, if the sequence
{(u^{(n)})}_{n}
converges to
u^{\infty}
, then we must have
u^{\infty}=u(c_{1},\dots,c_{m},0,0,\dots)
for some finite sequence
c_{1},\dots,c_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. In particular, the generating series of the infinite Toeplitz matrix
u^{\infty}
is a polynomial, namely
p(x)=1+c_{1}x+\dots+c_{m}x^{m}
. Note that by
Theorem

1.5
the matrix
u^{\infty}
is in
\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
if and only if
p(x)=(1+\beta_{1}x)\dotsc(1+\beta_{m}x)
for Schoenberg parameters
\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\dotsc\geq\beta_{m}\geq 0
. Now, by the parametrization result Theorem
3.10
for
X_{P}^{(n)}
we have
u^{(n)}=u_{+}^{(n-m,n)}(t_{n})=u^{E}_{n}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{n,n-m})
for some positive real parameter
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Equivalently, compare also Lemma
3.6
, we have

(4.4)

p_{u^{(n)}}(x)=1+c^{(n)}_{1}x+c^{(n)}_{2}x^{2}+\dotsc+c^{(n)}_{m}x^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(1+t_{n}\zeta^{i}_{n}x\right),

where the
\zeta^{i}_{n}
are the
n
-th roots of
(-1)^{m+1}
from Definition
3.8
and
t_{n}>0
. Now
p(x)
is the limit of the sequence
{(p_{u^{(n)}}(x))}_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}[x]}_{\leq m}
, and in particular
c_{m}^{(n)}
converges to
c_{m}
. Therefore
t_{n}=\sqrt[n]{\left(c^{(n)}_{m}\right)^{k}}
converges to
\beta:=\sqrt[n]{(c_{m})^{k}}
.
By observing that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\zeta_{n}^{i}=1
, independently of
i
, we see that the factors
\left(1+t_{n}\zeta^{i}_{n}x\right)
from (
4.4
) all converge to
\left(1+\beta x\right)
. Therefore
u^{\infty}
is totally nonnegative with Schoenberg parameters
\beta_{i}=\beta
, where
i=1,\dotsc,m
. Finally, for the quantum parameter
q^{(n)}
(as function on
X_{P_{n-m}^{(n)}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
) we have
q^{(n)}(u^{(n)})=\frac{1}{t^{n}}
, see Lemma
3.12
. Therefore
\beta
is given by
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\frac{1}{q^{(n)}}}
. For the reverse implication, we can simply choose
t_{n}=\beta
for all
n
if
\beta>0
, or
t_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
if
\beta=0
and obtain a sequence
u^{(n)}
that converges to the infinite Toeplitz matrix
u(\beta,\dots,\beta,0,\dotsc)
with generating function
(1+\beta x)^{m}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In Section
8.2
, Proposition
8.9
, we will prove an analogous result in the case of certain partial flag varieties, and we will pose a conjecture for its generalisation, compatible with both limits as well as the result in the full flag case of
[

.

4.1.
Asymptotics of Schubert classes

Throughout this section, let

be the limit of a sequence

as in Section

Equivalently,

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose the sequence

with

converges to the infinite Toeplitz matrix

with generating function

where

Remark 4.8
.

Clearly,

1.6

Let us write

for now, as in (
3.1
). Beginning with the simplest case, consider the Schubert class
\sigma^{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{&\opttoksa@YT={\font@YT}\getcolor@YT{\save@YT{\opttoksb@YT}}\nil@YT\getcolor@YT{\startbox@@YT\the\opttoksa@YT\the\opttoksb@YT}#\endbox@YT\cr\lower 0.31883pt\vbox{\kern 0.00002pt\kern 0.15941pt\hbox{\kern 0.31883pt\vbox to2.71053pt{\vss\hbox to2.3917pt{\hss$$\hss}\vss}\kern-2.71053pt\vrule height=2.71053pt,width=0.31883pt\kern 2.3917pt\vrule height=2.71053pt,width=0.31883pt}\kern-0.15941pt\kern-2.71053pt\hrule width=3.02936pt,height=0.31883pt\kern 2.3917pt\hrule width=3.02936pt,height=0.31883pt}\cr}}\kern 345.4pt}}_{P_{k}}

\left|\begin{pmatrix}c_{m}&c_{m-1}&\cdots&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&c_{m}&c_{m-2}\\
&\cdots&0&c_{m-1}\end{pmatrix}\right|=c_{m-1}c_{m}^{k}.

Hence, since

, we have
\mathfrak{S}^{s_{k}}(u)=\frac{c_{m-1}}{c_{m}}

For another example, consider the permutation

n

Lemma 4.9
.

Fix
m
and let
n>m
, setting
k=n-m
. Then
\mathfrak{S}_{P_{k}}^{s_{k-l}\cdots s_{k}}(u^{(n)})
is a polynomial in the entries
c_{1},\dotsc,c_{m}
of
u^{(n)}
that is stable, in the sense that it does not depend on
n
.

Proof.

In general,
\Delta_{k}^{s_{k-l}\cdots s_{k}}=\minor^{[n-k+1,n]}_{[k-1-1]\cap[k-l+1,k+1]}
, i.e. the minor consisting of the last
k
rows and the first
k+1
columns with the
l^{th}
column removed. We can write
\Delta_{k}^{s_{k-l}\cdots s_{k}}
as the determinant of the block matrix
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&C\end{pmatrix}
, where
A
and
C
are
(k-l-1)\times(k-l-1)
and
(l+1)\times(l+1)
matrices respectively given by

A=\begin{pmatrix}c_{m}&c_{m-1}&\cdots&\\
&c_{m}&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&c_{m-1}\\
0&&&c_{m}\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=\begin{pmatrix}c_{m-1}&c_{m-2}&\cdots&\\
c_{m}&c_{m-1}&&\vdots\\
&\ddots\ \quad\ \ &\ddots\qquad\ \ &\vdots\\
&\qquad\ddots&\quad\ddots&c_{m-2}\\

Since
\det(A)={(c_{m})}^{k-l-1}

\mathfrak{S}^{s_{k-l}\cdots s_{k}}(u)=\frac{\det(A)\det(C)}{\Delta^{k}}=\frac{{(c_{m})}^{k-l-1}\det(C)}{{(c_{m})}^{k}}=\frac{\det(C)}{{(c_{m})}^{l+1}},

which does not depend on

(or equivalently,

∎

We now write
u^{(n)}=u_{n}(c_{1}^{(n)},\dotsc,c_{m}^{(n)})
and make the assumption that these matrices converge to

Lemma 4.10
.

Given a converging sequence with
u^{(n)}\to u^{\infty}
, for

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(\mathfrak{S}^{w}(u^{(n)}))=H_{l+1}(\frac{1}{\beta},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta})

is the degree

Let
u^{(n)}=u(c_{1}^{(n)},\dots,c_{m}^{(n)})
, and define, similarly to Lemma

, the determinants

\mathsf{A}_{l}(u^{(n)})\coloneq\det\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}c^{(n)}_{m}&c^{(n)}_{m-1}&\cdots&\\
&c^{(n)}_{m}&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&\ddots&c^{(n)}_{m-1}\\
0&&&c^{(n)}_{m}\end{pmatrix}}_{(l+1)\times(l+1)}\qquad\mathsf{C}_{l}(u^{(n)})\coloneq\det\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}c^{(n)}_{m-1}&c^{(n)}_{m-2}&\cdots&\\

0&&c^{(n)}_{m}&c^{(n)}_{m-1}\end{pmatrix}.}_{(l+1)\times(l+1)}

It follows that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{S}^{w}(u^{(n)})=\frac{\mathsf{C}_{l}(u^{\infty})}{\mathsf{A}_{l}(u^{\infty})}
. Moreover,
c_{i}^{(n)}
converges to

, which we shorthand with

, so

\mathsf{C}_{l}(u^{\infty})=\det\begin{pmatrix}E_{m-1}(\beta^{(m)})&E_{m-2}(\beta^{(m)})&\cdots&\\
E_{m}(\beta^{(m)})&E_{m-1}(\beta^{(m)})&\ddots&\vdots\\
&\ddots&\ddots&E_{m-2}(\beta^{(m)})\\
0&&E_{m}(\beta^{(m)})&E_{m-1}(\beta^{(m)})\end{pmatrix}

Noticing that the degrees of the

entry in each column, we see that this is the transpose of the Jacobi-Trudi determinant formula for the Schur polynomial

\lambda_{(1)}
, with
m-1
rows of length
l+1
. By a similar argument,
\mathsf{A}_{l}(u^{\infty})
is the Schur polynomial for a Young diagram
\lambda_{(2)}
with
m
rows of length
l+1
. A well-known property of the Schur polynomial (in

. That is, a filling of
\lambda
with entries in the set

\lambda
.

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

The case of a Young diagram with one column is given by Lemma
4.10
, since the permutation
s_{n-m-\ell}\cdots s_{n-m}
corresponds to the partition
(1^{\ell+1})={(1,\dotsc,1)}
with
\ell+1
parts. We use the quantum Giambelli formula due to Bertram
[
7
]
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
be a Young diagram with
\lambda\subseteq k\times m
, and write
\lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\dots,\lambda^{\prime}_{j})
for the conjugate partition. Let us furthermore write
\sigma^{(\lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{t}}
for the quantum Schubert class
\sigma^{(1,\dotsc,1)}
associated to the Young diagram
(1^{\lambda_{i}^{\prime}})
with
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
parts. Then by the (dual) quantum Giambelli formula we have

(4.6)

\sigma^{\lambda}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1})^{t}}&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+1)^{t}}&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+2)^{t}}&\cdots&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+r-1)^{t}}\\
\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-1)^{t}}&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2})^{t}}&&&\\
\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3}-2)^{t}}&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3}-1)^{t}}&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3})^{t}}&&\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{r}-r+1)^{t}}&&\cdots&&\sigma^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{r})^{t}}\end{pmatrix},

and hence we have a sequence of determinants given by

\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(u^{(n)})=\det\begin{pmatrix}\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1})^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+1)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+2)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\cdots&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{1}+r-1)^{t}}(u^{(n)})\\
\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}-1)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{2})^{t}}(u^{(n)})&&&\\
\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3}-2)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3}-1)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{3})^{t}}(u^{(n)})&&\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{r}-r+1)^{t}}(u^{(n)})&&\cdots&&\mathfrak{S}^{(\lambda^{\prime}_{r})^{t}}(u^{(n)}),\end{pmatrix}

where
\mathfrak{S}^{(\ell)^{t}}(u^{(n)})=\mathfrak{S}^{s_{n-m-(\ell-1)}\cdots s_{n-m}}(u^{(n)})
. Applying Lemma
4.10
it follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}(u^{(n)})=\det\begin{pmatrix}H_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{\beta},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta})&H_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+1}(\frac{1}{\beta},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta})&H_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+2}(\frac{1}{\beta},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta})&\cdots&H_{\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+r-1}(\frac{1}{\beta},\dots,\frac{1}{\beta})\\

5.
Asymptotics of the Peterson Toeplitz variety for
Gr(k,n)
with fixed
k

Let
X_{P_{k}}^{(n)}
denote the Peterson Toeplitz variety associated to
Gr(k,n)
, where now we will fix
k
. In this section, we show the following proposition, which will follow from Proposition
4.5
by applying a natural symmetry operation related to the isomorphism
Gr(n-m,n)\cong Gr(m,n)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
be the limit of a sequence
{(u^{(n)})}_{n}
, where
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{k}}^{(n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. Then
u^{\infty}
lies in
\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
and its generating function is given by

(5.1)

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=\frac{1}{(1-\alpha x)^{k}},

where
\alpha
is determined by

(5.2)

\alpha=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\frac{1}{q^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}}.

Conversely, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the infinite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrix with generating function
\frac{1}{(1-\alpha x)^{k}}
is obtained as the limit of a sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n}
with
u^{(n)}\in X^{(n)}_{P_{k}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.

We first prove a lemma that constructs the isomorphism
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))\cong qH^{*}(Gr(m,n))
that swaps
\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\lambda}
and
\sigma_{P_{m}}^{\lambda^{\prime}}
, see Section
3.2
, on the level of Peterson Toeplitz varieties. Recall Definitions
3.1
and
3.5
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
k+m=n
, the isomorphism
\Psi=\Psi_{m,k}:X^{(n)}_{P_{m}}\longrightarrow X^{(n)}_{P_{k}}
determined by

\displaystyle\Psi^{*}:\mathbb{C}[X^{(n)}_{P_{k}}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[X^{(n)}_{P_{m}}]

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{P_{m}}

can be described in the following equivalent ways.

(1)

For
u\in X_{P_{m}}
, we have

\Psi(u)=\bar{w}_{0}^{-1}(u^{-1})^{T}\bar{w}_{0}
.

(2)

Writing
u\in X_{P_{m}}
as
u_{n}(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{k})
in terms of its entries, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(u_{n}(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n}(-c_{1},c_{2},\dotsc,(-1)^{k}c_{k})^{-1},

(3)

Writing
u
as
u_{n}^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})
for
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})\in\mathcal{X}_{n,k}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(u_{n}^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n}^{E}(-x_{1},-x_{2},\dotsc,-x_{k})^{-1}.

(4)

Writing
u=u_{n}^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})
for
[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k},x_{k+1},\dotsc,x_{n}]_{k,m}\in\mathcal{Y}_{k,m}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi(u_{n}^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{n}^{E}(-x_{k+1},-x_{k+2},\dotsc,-x_{n}).

Furthermore, we have that
\Psi
restricts to a bijection
\Psi_{>0}:X^{(n)}_{P_{m}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\longrightarrow X^{(n)}_{P_{k}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
of the totally positive parts, where it satisfies

(5.3)

\Psi\left(u_{+}^{(m,n)}(t)\right)=u_{+}^{(k,n)}(t)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Remark 5.3
.

We note that
q_{k}(\Psi(u))=q_{m}(u)
follows automatically, since the isomorphism
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))\cong qH^{*}(Gr(m,n))
from
[
8
]
, which is determined by sending
\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}
to
\sigma^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{P_{m}}
, also sends
q_{k}
to
q_{m}
. Also note that one further description of
\Psi
is given by
\Psi(u)=\iota(u)
, by Remark
2.10
.

Proof.

Note that
\bar{w}_{0}^{-1}(u^{-1})^{T}\bar{w}_{0}=\iota(u)
since
u
is Toeplitz. The formula for
\Psi
given in (1) follows from Lemma
2.9
. The description in (2) follows from the one in (1). Namely, (1) can be rewritten as
\Psi(u)=(\bar{w}_{0}^{-1}u^{T}\bar{w}_{0})^{-1}
, but transposing and then conjugating by
\bar{w}_{0}
the Toeplitz matrix
u_{n}(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{k})
has precisely the effect of alternatingly reversing the signs to give
u_{n}(-c_{1},c_{2},\dotsc,(-1)^{k}c_{k})
. The description of
\Psi
from (3) is straightforwardly equivalent to the one in (2).
Finally, it is clear that

u_{n}^{E}(-x_{1},\dotsc,-x_{k})^{-1}=u_{n}(H_{1}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}),\dotsc,H_{m}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})),

using also that
H_{m+1}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=\dotsc=H_{n}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=0
for
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})\in\mathcal{X}_{n,k}
. Now the description in (4) follows, since
H_{j}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})=E_{j}(-x_{k+1},\dotsc,-x_{n})
, see Lemma
3.2
. Finally, if
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}\}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{n,k}
then it follows that
\{-x_{k+1},\dotsc,-x_{n}\}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{n,m}
, so that (4) also implies (
5.3
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Using Definition
3.8
and Theorem
3.10
, we may write

(5.4)

u^{(n)}=u^{(k,n)}_{+}(t_{n})=u^{E}(t_{n}\zeta^{1}_{n,n-k},\dotsc,t_{n}\zeta^{m}_{n,m}),

for some

. On the other hand, by Lemma

Now, since

, we obtain

from

by the limit formula given in (

). For the final statement of Proposition

, it suffices to take the sequence given by

if

or

.
∎

Finally, we have the analogue to

about asymptotics of Schubert classes for the case where

remains constant, which now follows easily from the results already proved. Note that given a partition
\lambda
with at most

parts, it will give rise to a function

(or written

) on

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose the sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n}
with

As in the proof of Proposition

, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(t_{n})=\alpha

∎

6.

Gr(n,2n)

Proposition 6.1
.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
let
u^{\infty}(\eta)
be the infinite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrix
with generating function
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=e^{\eta x}
. Then there exists a sequence
(u^{(2n)})_{n}
where
u^{(2n)}\in X^{(2n)}_{P_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
that converges to

. Moreover, if a sequence
(u^{(2n)})_{n}
with
u^{(2n)}\in X^{(2n)}_{P_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converges to an infinite Toeplitz matrix, then we must have

\lim_{n\to\infty}(u^{(2n)})=u^{\infty}(\eta)=u_{\infty}\left(\eta,\frac{1}{2!}\eta^{2},\frac{1}{3!}\eta^{3},\frac{1}{4!}\eta^{4},\dotsc\right)

Proof.

Let us write
u^{(2n)}=u_{+}^{(n,2n)}(t_{n})=u_{(2n)}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n})
where
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, using Theorem
3.10
. The generating polynomial associated to

is

(6.1)

Letting

, we compute the limit of

as

Set

As in

22
]

directly using a version of the Gaussian

-binomial formula,

(6.2)

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+v^{-\frac{n+1}{2}+j}x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{i}\right]_{v}x^{j},

where

\qquad[k]_{v}=\frac{v^{\frac{-k}{2}}-v^{\frac{k}{2}}}{v^{-\frac{1}{2}}-v^{\frac{1}{2}}},\quad[k]_{v}!=[1]_{v}[2]_{v}\dotsc[k]_{v},\quad\text{and}\quad\left[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{j}\right]_{v}=\frac{[n]_{v}!}{[j]_{v}![n-j]_{v}!}.

Note that

(6.3)

[k]_{v}=\frac{v^{\frac{-k}{2}}-v^{\frac{k}{2}}}{v^{-\frac{1}{2}}-v^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\sin(\frac{k\pi}{2n})}{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2n})},

so that the coefficient of
x^{\ell}
in
p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)
is given by

(6.4)

\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{n^{\ell}}E_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n})=\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{n^{\ell}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n})}{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-\ell}\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n})\right)}=\gamma^{\ell}\frac{\prod_{r=n-\ell+1}^{n}\sin(\frac{r\pi}{2n})}{\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}(n\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n}))}.

Here we have a fixed number
\ell
of factors in the numerator and denominator in the right-hand side expression. The factors in the denominator are of the form
n\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n})
, where
x=\frac{j\pi}{2n}
is tending to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, we can make use of the linear approximation
\sin(x)\sim x
to show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(n\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n}))=\frac{j\pi}{2}
, as higher-degree terms vanish in the limit. Meanwhile, the corresponding
\ell
factors in the numerator are each approaching
\sin(\frac{\pi}{2})=1
as
n\to\infty
, so that overall we find

\frac{\gamma^{\ell}}{n^{\ell}}E_{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n})\to\gamma^{\ell}\frac{2^{\ell}}{\ell!\pi^{\ell}}\qquad(n\to\infty).

Finally, the polynomials
p_{u^{(n)}}(x)
converge coefficientwise to

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell!}\left(\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\right)^{\ell}x^{\ell}=e^{\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}x}.

We obtain
e^{\eta x}
by setting
\gamma=\frac{\pi}{2}\eta
.

For the converse, suppose we have an arbitrary sequence
(u^{(2n)})_{n}
with
u^{(2n)}\in X_{P_{n}}^{(2n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, converging to
u_{\infty}(c_{1},c_{2},\dots)
. By Theorem
3.10
, we can write
u^{(2n)}=u_{2n}^{E}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2n,n}^{+}))
, with
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. The generating polynomial for each term is given by

p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+t_{n}\zeta_{2n}^{i}x\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2n,n}^{+})x^{i}.

By assumption,
E_{i}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2n,n}^{+})
converges to
c_{i}
, and by (
6.4
) we have

E_{i}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2n,n}^{+})=t_{n}^{i}\frac{\prod_{r=n-i+1}^{n}\sin(\frac{r\pi}{2n})}{\prod_{j=1}^{i}(\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n}))}.

By again approximating the
\sin
factors for large
n
, we have that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\sin(\frac{r\pi}{2n}))=1
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}(n\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n}))=\frac{j\pi}{2}
. Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{i}(t_{n}\,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{2n,n}^{+})=\lim_{n\to\infty}(nt_{n})^{i}\frac{\prod_{r=n-i+1}^{n}\sin(\frac{r\pi}{2n})}{\prod_{j=1}^{i}(n\sin(\frac{j\pi}{2n}))}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{(nt_{n})^{i}2^{i}}{i!\pi^{i}},

so
(nt_{n})_{n}
must be convergent. Let
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}nt_{n}=\gamma
. Then
c_{i}=\frac{\gamma^{i}2^{i}}{i!\pi^{i}}
, so

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=e^{\eta x},

where
\eta=\frac{2\gamma}{x}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In the proof of Proposition
6.1
we considered the sequence
u^{(2n)}=u_{2n}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n})
given by
t_{n}=\frac{\gamma}{n}
, and computed the limit
n\to\infty
explicitly, obtaining an exponential function. However, there is an alternative approach to arguing that this limit must result in an exponential function. Consider the generating function (
6.1
), and note that, for pairs of factors that are complex conjugate, we have the coefficient-wise inequality,

(1+t_{n}\zeta^{j}_{2n}x)(1+t_{n}\zeta^{n-j+1}_{2n}x)=1+(\zeta^{j}_{2n}+\zeta^{n-j+1}_{2n})t_{n}x+t_{n}^{2}x^{2}<\left(1+t_{n}x\right)^{2}.

Since
t_{n}=\frac{\gamma}{n}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the above inequality implies

(6.5)

p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+\frac{\gamma}{n}\zeta^{j}_{2n}x)<\left(1+\frac{\gamma x}{n}\right)^{n}.

Therefore, possibly passing to a subsequence, we find an infinite Toeplitz matrix
u^{\infty}
in the limit,
and its generating function
p_{u^{\infty}}
must satisfy
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)\leq e^{\gamma x}
.

Next, use that the set

\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n}\cup-\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n}=\{\pm\zeta^{i}_{2n}\mid i=1,\dotsc,n\}

is precisely the set of all solutions to
x^{2n}+(-1)^{n}=0
, from which it follows that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\zeta^{i}_{2n}x)(1-\zeta^{i}_{2n}x)=1+(-1)^{n}x^{2n}.

This implies for our polynomial
p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)
that

(6.6)

p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)p_{u^{(2n)}}(-x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+t_{n}\zeta^{i}_{2n}x)(1-t_{n}\zeta^{i}_{2n}x)=1+(-1)^{n}t_{n}^{2n}x^{2n}.

Therefore taking the limit
n\to\infty
we see that
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)
satisfies the symmetry property,

(6.7)

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=\frac{1}{p_{u^{\infty}}(-x)}.

We note, however, that
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)
has no poles, since it is bounded by
e^{\gamma x}
as a consequence of (
6.5
). On the other hand, the equality (
6.7
) then implies that
p_{u^{\infty}}(-x)
has no zeroes. Therefore,
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)
has no zeros. Therefore the Schoenberg parameters for
u^{\infty}
are all
0
and Edrei’s theorem implies that
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=e^{\eta x}
for some
\eta\geq 0
.
Finally, we may again obtain
\eta
by computing the coefficient of
x
in
p_{u^{(2n)}}(x)
and taking the limit
n\to\infty
. Namely, with
v=e^{\frac{\pi i}{n}}
we have,

(6.8)

\eta=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma}{n}E_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{2n,n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma}{n}[n]_{v}={\gamma}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2})}{n\sin(\frac{\pi}{2n})}=2\gamma/\pi.

The key part of Edrei’s theorem (that the non-vanishing, entire factor in (
1.7
) must be of the form
e^{\gamma x}
) can now be equivalently restated using “
Gr(n,2n)
-Toeplitz matrices”, thanks to Proposition
6.1
, giving the following result.

Corollary 6.3
.

Any element of
u^{\infty}\in\Toep_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
whose generating function has no zeros or poles must lie in the limiting set of the Peterson Toeplitz varieties of middle-dimensional Grassmannians. That is,
u^{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}u^{(2n)}
for a sequence
(u^{(2n)})_{n}
with
u^{(2n)}\in X^{(2n)}_{P_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.5
, if the generating function of
u^{\infty}
has no zeros or poles then it must be of the form
e^{\gamma x}
, and then Proposition
6.1
says it is the limit of a sequence of elements
u^{(2n)}\in X_{P_{n}}^{(2n)}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

A direct proof of Corollary
6.3
would give rise to a new proof of Edrei’s theorem, since, again by Proposition
6.1
, if
u^{\infty}
is such a limit of
u^{(2n)}\in X^{(2n)}_{P_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
then it must have generating function of the form
e^{\gamma x}
.

7.
Strange duality

We observe a new perspective on the so-called ‘strange duality’ involution for Grassmannian quantum cohomology, as arising within Peterson theory, and use it to give alternative proofs of Theorems
4.7
and
5.4
. Recall first that strange duality is an involution of
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q_{k}^{-1}]
that inverts the quantum parameter. It was found by Postnikov
[
35
]
, and independently in the case of
q=1
by Hengelbrock
[
21
]
, who interpreted it in terms of complex conjugation. Let
\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)
denote the number of boxes contained in the diagonal of the Young diagram
\lambda
, and recall that the Poincaré dual
PD(\lambda)
of
\lambda\subseteq k\times{m}
is the complement of
\lambda
inside the
k\times m
box, rotated by
180
degrees.

Definition 7.1

(
[
21
, Definition 2.2]
,
[
35
]
)
.

For a partition
\lambda\subseteq k\times m
, let
\mathbb{S}(\lambda)
be the partition obtained by considering the maximal square in
\lambda
, whose diagonal has
\mu:=\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)
many boxes, and replacing the two parts of
\lambda
that lie outside this square by their Poincaré duals (relative to their natural bounding rectangles of dimension
\mu\times(m-\mu)
and
(k-\mu)\times\mu
, respectively).

Remark 7.2
.

Note that the partition
\mathbb{S}(\lambda)
still has the same
\operatorname{MaxDiag}
value, and
\mathbb{S}
is an involution on partitions
\lambda\subseteq k\times m
that preserves
\operatorname{MaxDiag}
.

Theorem 7.3

(
[
35
,
21
]
)
.

The linear map defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}:qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}]

\displaystyle q^{\ell}\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle q^{-\ell-\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)}\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\mathbb{S}(\lambda)}.

is an algebra isomorphism.

Remark 7.4
.

It is straightforward that
\mathbf{S}
is an involution, and
\mathbf{S}
is referred to as ‘strange duality’. We remark that there is also an interesting generalisation to other types
[
14
,
13
]
, and a construction via the homology of the affine Grassmannian and (another) Peterson isomorphism
[
15
]
.

Definition 7.5
.

We define an automorphism of the Peterson variety
Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
by

(7.1)

\displaystyle\tau:\qquad Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}\

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}

\displaystyle Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}

\displaystyle yw_{0}B_{-}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle y^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-}.

Here, the fact that
y^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-}
is an element of
Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
follows from
[
38
, Section 8]
. Note that this is specific to the Grassmannian setting and does not generalise in this form to other partial flag varieties.

Proposition 7.6
.

Let us identify
\mathbb{C}[Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}]
with
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{C})[q^{-1}]
via Peterson’s isomorphism from Theorem
2.5
. Then
\tau^{*}
recovers the strange duality map
\mathbf{S}
, giving a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}[Y_{P_{k}}^{\circ}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}[Y_{P_{k}}^{\circ}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}].}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Remark 7.7
.

Note the asymmetry in Definition
7.5
, with the left-hand side of (
7.1
) expressing an element of
Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
as element of the big cell
B_{-}w_{0}B_{-}/B_{-}
and the right-hand side as element of the Bruhat cell
B_{+}w_{P_{k}}B_{-}/B_{-}
. It is surprising that
\tau
is an involution.

Proof.

We first claim that
\tau^{*}
can be described by

(7.2)

\tau^{*}:\ G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}\quad\mapsto\quad\frac{G^{PD(\lambda)}_{P_{k}}}{G^{{k\times m}}_{P_{k}}},

in terms of the functions
G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}
on
Y_{P_{k}}^{\circ}
from Definition
2.4
.
To check this, note first that

(7.3)

\displaystyle G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(\tau(yw_{0}B_{-}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(y^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-})=\langle y^{T}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{\lambda}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle.

On the other hand we have

\displaystyle G^{PD(\lambda)}_{P_{k}}(y\dot{w}_{0}B_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{PD(\lambda)}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle}{\langle y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\dot{w}_{PD(\lambda)}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle}{\langle\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle},

and we have

\displaystyle G^{k\times m}_{P_{k}}(y\dot{w}_{0}B_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle}.

As a consequence,

(7.4)

\displaystyle\frac{G^{PD(\lambda)}_{P_{k}}(y\dot{w}_{0}B_{-})}{G^{k\times m}_{P_{k}}(y\dot{w}_{0}B_{-})}=\langle\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}y\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n},\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\dot{w}_{PD(\lambda)}\cdot v_{k+1}\wedge\dotsc\wedge v_{n}\rangle.

Now consider
d_{\epsilon}
as in the proof of Proposition
2.7
, for example,
d_{\epsilon}=\operatorname{diag(\pm 1,\dotsc,-1,1)}
. Since
y
is Toeplitz, we have that
\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}y\dot{w}_{0}=d_{\epsilon}y^{T}d_{\epsilon}^{-1}
. We also have
w_{PD(\lambda)}=w_{0}w_{\lambda}\mod W_{P_{k}}
. Therefore, comparing (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), we see that these two expressions differ at most by a sign,

(7.5)

G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(\tau(yw_{0}B_{-}))=\pm\frac{G^{PD(\lambda)}_{P_{k}}(yw_{0}B_{-})}{G^{k\times m}_{P_{k}}(yw_{0}B_{-})}.

However,
\tau
preserves total positivity, and the
G^{\lambda}_{w}
are all positive on the totally positive part of
Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
. Therefore the sign must be positive, and the claim (
7.2
) is verified.

Note that since
\tau^{*}
is a well-defined algebra homomorphism, by transporting this formula to quantum cohomology we see that the point class
\sigma^{(k\times m)}_{P_{k}}
must be an invertible element in
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}]
. This is already known. It also follows also from a formula in quantum Schubert calculus given in
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
, which says

(7.6)

\sigma_{P_{k}}^{k\times m}\star\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}=q^{\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)}\sigma_{P_{k}}^{PD({\mathbb{S}}(\lambda))}.

Moreover, replacing
\lambda
by
{\mathbb{S}}(\lambda)
and reorganising (
7.6
) gives the following alternative formula for
\mathbf{S}
,
see
[
13
]
, which is

(7.7)

\mathbf{S}(\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\lambda})=q^{-\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)}\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\mathbb{S}(\lambda)}=\frac{\sigma_{P_{k}}^{PD(\lambda)}}{\sigma_{P_{k}}^{k\times m}}.

This now implies the proposition.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

We note that for
y=u^{E}_{n}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})
the equation (
7.3
) leads to the explicit formula
G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(\tau(yw_{0}B_{-}))=G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(y^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-}))=S_{\lambda}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})
.

Corollary 7.9
.

Suppose
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k}\}\in\mathcal{X}_{n,k}
. We have the following identity,

(7.8)

u^{E}_{n}(z_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,z_{k}^{-1})w_{0}B_{-}=u^{E}_{n}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-},

that shows how to express an element of
Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
in two equivalent ways.

Proof.

Recall the result of
[
21
]
that the strange duality automorphism
\sigma^{\lambda}\mapsto q^{-\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)}\sigma_{P_{k}}^{\mathbb{S}(\lambda)}
represents complex conjugation when
q=1
, see also
[
13
, Section 3]
. For general
q
it is straightforward to observe that this description extends, but with complex conjugation replaced by inversion on the level of the Chern roots
t_{i}
. As a consequence, the image
\mathbf{S}(\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}})
of the class
\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}=S_{\lambda}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})
in
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n),\mathbb{C})[q^{-1}]
has three different expressions,

(7.9)

\mathbf{S}(\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}})=\frac{S_{PD(\lambda)}}{S_{k\times m}}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})=(-1)^{k+1}t_{1}^{-n\operatorname{MaxDiag}(\lambda)}S_{\mathbb{S}(\lambda)}(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{k})=S_{\lambda}(t_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,t_{k}^{-1}),

keeping in mind that
q
in terms of the Chern roots
t_{i}
is given by
(-1)^{k+1}t_{1}^{n}
, see Lemma
3.2
.

We now apply the dictionary between
\mathbb{C}[Y_{P_{k}}^{\circ}]
and
qH^{*}(Gr(k,n))[q^{-1}]
, whereby
\sigma^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}
corresponds to
G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}
and the coordinates
z_{i}
for
u^{E}_{n}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})w_{P_{k}}B_{-}\in Y_{P}^{\circ}
correspond to the Chern roots
t_{i}
, compare Remark
7.8
.

We now have the following two identities

\displaystyle\tau(u^{E}_{n}(z_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,z_{k}^{-1})w_{0}B_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{E}_{n}(z_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,z_{k}^{-1})^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-},

\displaystyle\tau(u^{E}_{n}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k})^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{E}_{n}(z_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,z_{k}^{-1})^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-},

where the first is just the definition of
\tau
, and the second follows from (
7.9
) via Proposition
7.6
, see also Remark
7.8
. Namely, the strange involution acts by inverting the Chern roots which correspond to the
z_{i}
under our dictionary. Comparing the left-hand sides and using the fact that
\tau
is invertible, we obtain the desired formula (
7.8
).
∎

We now apply these results to give an alternative proof of our results concerning the Schubert class asymptotics.

Proof of Theorems
4.7
and
5.4
.

Consider the isomorphism
\mu_{P_{k}}:X_{P_{k}}\to Y^{\circ}_{P_{k}}
from Proposition
2.7
given by
\mu_{P_{k}}(u)=\iota(u)w_{0}B_{-}
, and suppose

where
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}\}\in\mathcal{X}_{n,m}
, as in Lemma

.
Extending
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}\}
to an element
[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m},x_{m+1},\dotsc,x_{n}]_{m,k}\in\mathcal{Y}_{m,k}
and combining Lemma
5.2
, Remark
5.3
and Corollary
7.9
, we find that

\mu_{P_{k}}(u)=\iota(u^{E}_{n}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}))w_{0}B_{-}=u^{E}_{n}(-x_{m+1},\dotsc,-x_{n})w_{0}B_{-}=u^{E}_{n}(-x_{m+1}^{-1},\dotsc,-x_{n}^{-1})^{T}w_{P_{k}}B_{-}.

Moreover, using also Proposition
2.7
, Remark

and Lemma

, this expression for

leads to the following explicit formula for the evaluation of the functions

,

\mathfrak{S}^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(u^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}))=G^{\lambda}_{P_{k}}(\mu_{P_{k}}(u^{E}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})))=S_{\lambda}(-x_{m+1}^{-1},\dotsc,-x_{n}^{-1})=S_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x_{1}^{-1},\dotsc,x_{m}^{-1}).

Now suppose

as in Proposition
4.5
. We then have

. Also assume

. Since

, we have that (

) implies

Now, since

, where

is a Schur polynomial in

Finally, consider the case where

is fixed and
u^{(n)}=u^{E}_{n}(t_{n}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{+}_{n,n-k})
. If

converges, then its limit is

using that if

. Now

.
∎

Remark 7.10
.

Note that Remark

-t_{k+1},\dotsc,-t_{n}
the Chern roots of the tautological quotient bundle. See also Section
3.2
. We conclude that our ‘coordinates’
x_{i}
for
u^{E}_{n}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})\in X_{P_{m}}
should therefore be considered as representing the inverted Chern roots of the tautological quotient bundle of
Gr(k,n)
.

8.
Partial flag varieties and conjectures

For a finite strongly increasing sequence of positive integers
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dotsc n_{k})
, let
P_{\mathbf{n}}^{(n)}\coloneq P_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k}}^{(n)}
be the parabolic subgroup associated to the partial flag variety
SL_{n}/P_{\mathbf{n}}^{(n)}=\operatorname{Fl}_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
(for
n>n_{k}
). We begin by stating the following conjectural analogue of Theorem
4.7
for these more general parabolic subgroups.

Conjecture 8.1
.

Fix
\mathbf{n}
as above, and set
n_{0}=0
.
Consider a monotone (weakly) decreasing sequence of
n_{k}
non-negative real numbers
\left(\alpha_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n_{k}}
such that
\alpha_{n_{i}}>\alpha_{n_{i}+1}
, but
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j+1}
otherwise.
Let
u^{\infty}\in\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
be the infinite totally nonnegative Toeplitz matrix with generating function

(8.1)

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{k}}(1-\alpha_{j}x)}=\frac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(1-\alpha_{n_{i}}x)^{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}}.

Then there exists a sequence of matrices
u^{(n)}
, with
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{\mathbf{n}}^{(n)}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, which converges to
u^{\infty}\in\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
.
Conversely, every Toeplitz matrix
u^{\infty}
with
p_{u^{\infty}}(x)
as in (
8.1
) arises as the limit of such a sequence.

Remark 8.2
.

We could have also stated a dual version of this conjecture, instead considering sequences
u^{(n)}
where
u^{(n)}\in X_{P^{(n)}_{n-\mathbf{m}}}
, each corresponding to the partial flag variety with fixed codimensions, but this, as well as Conjecture
8.1
, is another special case of Conjecture
8.4
.

Definition 8.3
.

Consider the set
\mathcal{S}
of pairs
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
of strongly increasing positive integer sequences, which may be finite or infinite. We write
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},\dotsc)
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},\dotsc)
for the sequences
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}

(1)

Suppose
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
consists of two finite sequences (of length

, respectively). Then we set

\operatorname{Fl}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):=\operatorname{Fl}_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k},n-m_{\ell},\dotsc,n-m_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

whenever
n
is large enough.

(2)

If only
\mathbf{n}
is finite, we set

\operatorname{Fl}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):=\operatorname{Fl}_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k},n-m_{r},\dotsc,n-m_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

(and

(3)

If only

\operatorname{Fl}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):=\operatorname{Fl}_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{r},n-m_{\ell},\dotsc,n-m_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

where
r
is maximal for
n_{r}<n-m_{\ell}
(and
n
large enough for
r
to exist).

(4)

Given a pair of infinite sequences
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{S}
and

, let us write

\operatorname{Fl}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}(\mathbb{C}^{n}):=\operatorname{Fl}_{n_{1},\dotsc,n_{r},n-m_{r},\dotsc n-m_{1}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

where

n_{r}<n-m_{r}
.

Let
P^{(n)}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}
denote the associated parabolic in
SL_{n}
.

Conjecture 8.4
.

Suppose
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})\in\mathcal{S}
and recall the notations from above.
Consider a pair of infinite sequences
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}\coloneq{\left(\alpha_{i}\right)}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}\coloneq{\left(\beta_{i}\right)}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

\alpha_{n_{i}}>\alpha_{n_{i}+1}
and
\beta_{m_{i}}>\beta_{m_{i}+1}
,

(2)

\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j+1}
(resp.
\beta_{j}=\beta_{j+1}
) for all

(resp.
j\notin\{m_{1},m_{2},\dotsc\}
),

(3)

\sum\alpha_{i}<\infty
and
\sum\beta_{i}<\infty
,

(4)

If
\mathbf{n}
(resp.
\mathbf{m}
) is finite of length

(resp.

), then

Define the infinite Toeplitz matrix

Then there exists a sequence

8.1.
Full flag asymptotics review

In Section
8.2
, we will partially generalise Proposition
4.5
to a setting involving more general parabolic subgroups. The strategy of the proof will involve applying results from the full flag case obtained in
[
42
]
. We recall the relevant results from
[
42
]
below. Let
B_{n}
denote the upper-triangular Borel subgroup of
SL_{n}
.

Recall first Remark
1.4
, where we gave a version of Theorem
1.3
that takes the form

(8.2)

\displaystyle X_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sim}

\displaystyle T_{\mathrm{SL}_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})

\displaystyle u\quad\

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\operatorname{diag}(d_{1}^{(n)},d_{2}^{(n)},\dotsc,d_{n}^{(n)}),

with
d_{i}^{(n)}=\frac{\Delta_{i}(u)}{\Delta_{i-1}(u)}
. For ease of notation we write
\Delta_{i}
(resp.
d^{(n)}_{i}
) when it is clear we are taking the evaluation at a given point
u
, and we will leave out the superscript
(n)
when
n
is clear from context. Further composing (
8.2
) with the homeomorphism sending
\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})\in T_{SL_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
to
\left(\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}},\dots,\frac{d_{n}}{d_{n-1}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0}
gives a parameterisation

(q_{1},\dots,q_{n-1}):X_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}_{>0},

where
q_{i}(u)=\frac{d_{i+1}(u)}{d_{i}(u)}
, see
[
42
, Theorem 2.1]
, where the theorem is stated in similar conventions. Note that this recovers Kostant’s expression for the quantum parameters,

q_{i}=\frac{\Delta_{i+1}\Delta_{i-1}}{\Delta_{i}^{2}},

which is an instance of the formula (
2.18
) we recalled earlier.

We can now state the following result from
[
42
]
, which relates closely to Theorem
1.6
.

Theorem 8.5

(
[
42
, Theorem 2.7]
)
.

Suppose
(u^{(n)})_{n}
is a sequence of (finite) totally positive matrices
u^{(n)}\in X_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
that converges uniformly to an element
u^{\infty}\in\mathrm{Toep}_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, and assume that the Schoenberg parameters of
u^{\infty}
are all nonzero, so that

p_{\infty}(x)=e^{\gamma x}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+\beta_{i}x)}{\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{j}x)}

has infinitely many roots and poles. Let
(d_{1}^{(n)},\dots,d_{n}^{(n)})
be the positive parameters associated to
u^{(n)}
as in (
8.2
). Then

(8.3)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_{i}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\alpha_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_{n-j+1}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\frac{1}{\beta_{j}}.

Remark 8.6
.

Note that Theorem
1.6
is a corollary of Theorem
8.5
above, with the formulas concerning the limit of
\sqrt[n]{q_{i}^{(n)}}
and
\sqrt[n]{q_{n-i}^{(n)}}
following via the identification
q_{i}=\frac{d_{i+1}}{d_{i}}
.

Remark 8.7
.

In the case of the Grassmannian
Gr(k,n)
we also have a diagonal matrix
d\in T_{SL_{n}}^{W_{P_{k}}}
governing the quantum parameters, see Section
2.5
and (
2.17
). We may therefore restate the results from Propositions
4.5
and
5.1
concerning asymptotics of the quantum parameter as results about asymptotics of the entries of
d
and observe that the resulting formulas are in fact directly analogous to the ones in Theorem
8.5
. Namely, suppose
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{k}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, and let us write

d=\operatorname{diag}(\underbrace{d^{(n)}_{1},\dotsc,d^{(n)}_{1}}_{k},\underbrace{d^{(n)}_{2},\dotsc,d^{(n)}_{2}}_{n-k})

for the matrix of positive parameters associated to
u^{(n)}
. Then, since
q^{(n)}=\frac{d^{(n)}_{2}}{d^{(n)}_{1}}
and
\det(d)=1
, we have

\sqrt[n]{q^{(n)}}=\sqrt[n-k]{\frac{1}{d_{1}^{(n)}}}=\sqrt[n-m]{d_{2}^{(n)}},

where
m:=n-k
. Now assume a sequence of
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{n-m}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converges to a matrix
u^{\infty}
with
\beta_{1}=\dotsc=\beta_{m}=\beta>0
and all other Schoenberg parameters equal to
0
, as in Proposition
4.5
. Then the result that
\sqrt[n]{q^{(n)}_{n-m}(u^{(n)})^{-1}}
converges to
\beta
can be reinterpreted as

(8.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_{2}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\frac{1}{\beta}.

Note that if we write
d^{(n)}_{n}:=d^{(n)}_{2}
, considering this as the final matrix entry of
d
, the limit (
8.4
) is completely analogous to the second formula in (
8.3
). Similarly, if
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{k}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converges to a matrix
u^{\infty}
with
\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{k}=\alpha>0
and all other Schoenberg parameters are equal to
0
, as in Proposition
5.1
, then the limit formula for
\alpha
implies that

(8.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_{1}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\alpha,

in terms of
d_{1}^{(n)}
. This is directly analogous with the first equality in (
8.3
).

8.2.
Asymptotics of arbitrary partial flag varieties

Let
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
be as in Definition
8.3
. We consider the subset
W^{P_{\mathbf{n}}}
of
S_{\infty}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n}
consisting of the
w\in S_{\infty}
all of whose reduced expressions end in elements of the form
s_{n_{i}}
with
n_{i}\in\mathbf{n}
. Similarly for
\mathbf{m}
.
Given
w\in S_{\infty}
and
n
large enough so that
w\in S_{n}
, we set
\hat{w}_{n}=w_{0}^{(n)}w{(w_{0}^{(n)})}^{-1}
, where
w_{0}^{(n)}
is the longest element in
S_{n}
, see Theorem
1.6
. Observe that if
w\in W^{P_{\mathbf{m}}}
then
\hat{w}_{n}\in W^{P_{n-\mathbf{m}}^{(n)}}
.
Also, recall that
S_{w}
denotes the Schubert polynomial associated to
w
. If
N
is minimal such that
w\in S_{N}
, then
S_{w}
is a polynomial in
N-1
variables. Below we will write
\mathfrak{S}^{w}_{(n)}
for the function
\mathfrak{S}^{w}_{P_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}^{(n)}}
on
X_{P_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}^{(n)}}
to simplify notation.

Conjecture 8.8
.

Given
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
as above, consider a pair of infinite sequences
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfying the conditions (1)-(3) from Conjecture
8.4
. Assume we have a sequence
(u^{(n)})_{n}
with
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}^{(n)}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converging uniformly to
u^{\infty}
, where

p_{u^{\infty}}(x)=\frac{\prod\limits_{\beta\in\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}}(1+\beta x)}{\prod\limits_{\alpha\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}}(1-\alpha x)}=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+\beta_{i}x)}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-\alpha_{j}x)},

with the Schoenberg parameters given by
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}
. Given a permutation
w\in W^{P_{\mathbf{m}}}
,
we have the following limit formula,

(8.6)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\left(\mathfrak{S}^{\hat{w}_{n}}_{(n)}(u^{(n)})\right)}_{n}=S_{w}\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{1}},\frac{1}{\beta_{2}},\frac{1}{\beta_{3}},\dots\right).

If
w\in W^{P_{\mathbf{n}}}
, then we have the limit formula

(8.7)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\left(\mathfrak{S}^{w}_{(n)}(u^{(n)})\right)}_{n}=S_{w}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}},\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}},\frac{1}{\alpha_{3}},\dots\right).

Proposition 8.9
.

Let
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
be as in Definition
8.3
, and assume both sequences are infinite. Consider a pair of sequences
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}
as in Conjecture
8.4
, and suppose we have a sequence
u^{(n)}\in X_{P_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}^{(n)}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converging to
u^{\infty}
with Schoenberg parameters given by
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathbf{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\mathbf{m}}
. Assume, moreover, that
|\Delta_{n_{i}}(u^{(n)})-\Delta_{n_{i}}([u^{\infty}]_{n})|
and
|\Delta_{n-m_{j}}(u^{(n)})-\Delta_{n-m_{j}}([u^{\infty}]_{n})|
converge to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Then we have

(8.8)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{q_{n_{i}}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\frac{\alpha_{n_{i+1}}}{\alpha_{n_{i}}},\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{q_{n-m_{i}}^{(n)}(u^{(n)})}=\frac{\beta_{m_{i+1}}}{\beta_{m_{i}}}.

Proof.

Let
P^{(n)}=P_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}^{(n)}
. We have that
u^{(n)}\in X_{P^{(n)}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
converges to
u^{\infty}
.
Consider the truncations
{\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}
, and note that these lie in
X_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. Recall that both
X_{P^{(n)}}
and
X_{B_{n}}
are contained in
X_{\{n_{i}\}}
, see Definition
2.13
, whenever
n
is large enough. For such
n
, we consider the function
\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}
on
X_{\{n_{i}\}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
given by

(8.9)

\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}:=\sqrt[(n_{i+1}-n_{i})(n_{i}-n_{i-1})]{\frac{\Delta_{n_{i-1}}^{n_{i+1}-n_{i}}\Delta_{n_{i+1}}^{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}}{\Delta_{n_{i}}^{n_{i+1}-n_{i-1}}}}.

Note that
\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}|_{X^{(n)}_{P^{(n)}}}=q_{n_{i}}^{(n)}
by (
2.18
). The difference between
\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}
and
q_{n_{i}}^{(n)}
is the extended domain. Recall that for
{\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\in X_{B_{n}}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, we have

\Delta_{\ell}({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n})=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}d^{(n)}_{j},

where
(d_{1}^{(n)},\dots,d_{n}^{(n)})
are the positive parameters associated to
[u^{\infty}]_{n}
in (
8.2
), see also Remark
1.4
. Hence, after some simplification,

(8.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt[(n_{i+1}-n_{i})(n_{i}-n_{i-1})]{\frac{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{i-1}}d_{j}^{(n)}\right)}^{n_{i+1}-n_{i}}{\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{i+1}}d_{j}^{(n)}\right)}^{n_{i}-n_{i-1}}}{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{i}}d_{j}^{(n)}\right)}^{n_{i+1}-n_{i-1}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt[(n_{i+1}-n_{i})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i}+1}^{n_{i+1}}d_{j}^{(n)}\right)}}}{\sqrt[(n_{i}-n_{i-1})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}}d_{j}^{(n)}\right)}}}.

Now by Theorem
8.5
,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{d_{k}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)}=\alpha_{k},

so that in the limit, using the fact that both
\alpha_{n_{i-1}+1}=\cdots=\alpha_{n_{i}}
and
\alpha_{n_{i}+1}=\cdots=\alpha_{n_{i+1}}
, we find that

(8.11)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt[(n_{i+1}-n_{i})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i}+1}^{n_{i+1}}\sqrt[n]{d_{j}^{(n)}}\right)}}}{\sqrt[(n_{i}-n_{i-1})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}}\sqrt[n]{d_{j}^{(n)}}\right)}}}=\frac{\sqrt[(n_{i+1}-n_{i})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i}+1}^{n_{i+1}}\alpha_{n_{i+1}}\right)}}}{\sqrt[(n_{i}-n_{i-1})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n_{i-1}+1}^{n_{i}}\alpha_{n_{i}}\right)}}}=\frac{\alpha_{n_{i+1}}}{\alpha_{n_{i}}}.

On the other hand,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{{\mathbf{q}}^{(n)}_{n_{i}}(u^{(n)})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)},

as follows from the formula (
8.9
) that defines
\mathbf{q}_{n_{i}}
both on
[u^{\infty}]_{n}
and
u^{(n)}
, and the additional convergence condition on the
\Delta_{n_{i}}
.

But since
q^{(n)}_{n_{i}}(u^{(n)})=\mathbf{q}^{(n)}_{n_{i}}(u^{(n)})
by (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{q^{(n)}_{n_{i}}(u^{(n)})}=\frac{\alpha_{n_{i+1}}}{\alpha_{n_{i}}}.

For the second formula we have for each
m_{i}\in\mathbf{m}
, that

(8.12)

q_{n-m_{i}}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)=\frac{\sqrt[(m_{i}-m_{i-1})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n-m_{i}+1}^{n-m_{i-1}}d^{(n)}_{j}\right)}}}{\sqrt[(m_{i+1}-m_{i})]{{\left(\prod\limits_{j=n-m_{i+1}+1}^{n-m_{i}}d^{(n)}_{j}\right)}}},

as a consequence of (
8.10
). This, combined with Theorem
8.5
, implies the limit formula

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}^{(n)}_{n-m_{i}}([u^{\infty}]_{n})}=\frac{\beta_{m_{i+1}}}{\beta_{m_{i}}}.

Now again
\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}^{(n)}_{n-m_{i}}(u^{(n)})}
has the same limit as
\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}_{n-m_{i}}^{(n)}\left({\left[u^{\infty}\right]}_{n}\right)}
by the convergence condition on the
\Delta_{n-m_{j}}
and
\sqrt[n]{\mathbf{q}^{(n)}_{n-m_{i}}(u^{(n)})}=\sqrt[n]{q^{(n)}_{n-m_{i}}(u^{(n)})}
, so that the proposition follows.
∎

Note that already the statement of Proposition
8.9
requires both sequences
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
to be infinite.

For the more general case of partial flag varieties
\operatorname{Fl}_{(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})}

and/or

Conjecture 8.10

Let
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})

and
