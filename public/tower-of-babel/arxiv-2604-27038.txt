Triadic Phase Transitions in AI Networks: Composite-Operator Scaling in Cognitive Architectures
Eduardo Salazar

Abstract
Multi-agent AI architectures whose dominant collective observable is a k k -body spin correlator O k ≡ ⟨ ϕ k ⟩ O_{k}\!\equiv\!\langle\phi^{k}\rangle over a ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -symmetric order parameter exhibit composite-operator criticality, with effective exponents β k = k / 2 \beta_{k}=k/2 and γ k = 2 − k \gamma_{k}=2-k , thereby producing a finite susceptibility for k ≥ 2 k\!\geq\!2 and a vanishing susceptibility for k ≥ 3 k\!\geq\!3 . This is a qualitative departure from all pairwise-network universality classes. We derive these results for the non-trivial case k = 3 k\!=\!3 as presented in COGENT 3 {}^{\textbf{3}} [ 1 ] . The formation transition of COGENT 3 {}^{\textbf{3}} and comparable models, under controlled universality and mean-field arguments, reduces to an exactly solvable triadic Ising model. The minimal triad Hamiltonian admits an exact partition function on { − 1 , + 1 } 3 \{-1,+1\}^{3} , with crossover temperature T ∗ = 4 ​ ( J + γ ​ w ) / ln ⁡ 3 T^{*}=4(J+\gamma w)/\ln 3 and mean-field critical point T c = J + γ ​ w T_{c}=J+\gamma w (gradient coupling cooperatively enhancing order). The formation correlator Ψ form ≡ ⟨ ϕ i ​ ϕ j ​ ϕ k ⟩ \Psi_{\mathrm{form}}\!\equiv\!\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\rangle scales as ( T c − T ) 3 / 2 (T_{c}-T)^{3/2} . Its conjugate susceptibility vanishes at T c T_{c} , confirmed by an independent field-theoretic two-point function argument. A Mori–Zwanzig memory ansatz yields a continuously tunable dynamical exponent, completing the composite-operator scaling regime.

The Triadic Ising Model

Hamiltonian

Define
\tau=\{i,j,k\}
as a triad with binary formation fields
\phi_{a}\in\{-1,+1\}
and Hamiltonian given by

H_{\tau}=-J\!\sum_{\langle ab\rangle\subset\tau}\!\phi_{a}\phi_{b}-h\sum_{a\in\tau}\phi_{a}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{\langle ab\rangle\subset\tau}w_{ab}(\phi_{a}-\phi_{b})^{2}

(2)

Exact reduction

On
\{-1,+1\}

(\phi_{a}-\phi_{b})^{2}=2(1-\phi_{a}\phi_{b})

exactly

3

\tau

H_{\tau}=-J_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\sum_{\langle ab\rangle}\!\phi_{a}\phi_{b}-h\sum_{a}\phi_{a}+E_{0}

J_{\mathrm{eff}}=J+\gamma w\quad\text{with}\quad E_{0}=3\gamma w.

(5)

The gradient term does not frustrate the system but cooperates with the alignment interaction, renormalizing
J

\gamma w

exactly pairwise, with the triadic physics entering through the composite observable
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}

evaluation of the composite higher-order observables. No approximation is involved.

3
Reduction to the Triadic Ising Model

We derive that the critical behavior of the full COGENT
{}^{\textbf{3}}
system near its formation transition
T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
is governed exactly by the
triadic Ising model defined in Sec.
2
, whose mean-field analysis and composite-operator scaling are developed in Secs.
4
–
6
.
All COGENT
{}^{\textbf{3}}
notation and definitions follow Ref.
[
1
]
(specific section references are given inline). The argument requires three steps:
(i) symmetry reduction of the formation field; (ii) collapse of the dynamic topology onto a static all-to-all hypergraph; and (iii) demonstration that the remaining
Hamiltonian sectors contribute only irrelevant operators at
T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
.

Symmetry reduction of the formation field

The formation field
\phi_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
in COGENT
{}^{\textbf{3}}
carries a global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry
\phi_{i}\!\to\!-\phi_{i}
, inherited from the symmetry of
the formation correlator
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}
under interchange of formation roles (Sec. 5 therein). Near
T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
, the effective free energy for
\phi_{i}
takes the
standard
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-symmetric Landau form

\mathcal{F}[\phi]=\sum_{i}\bigl[a(T)\,\phi_{i}^{2}+b\,\phi_{i}^{4}\bigr]+\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{int}}[\phi]

(8)

where
a(T)=a_{0}(T-T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}})
changes sign at
T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
,
b>0
ensures stability, and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{int}}
collects the
interaction terms from
H_{\mathrm{group}}
.

The correct justification for replacing the continuous field
\phi_{i}
with binary spins is
universality
. Both the continuous
\phi^{4}
Landau–Ginzburg–Wilson
theory and the discrete Ising model flow to the same Wilson-Fisher fixed point under renormalization, and therefore share identical critical exponents. The binary
discretization
\phi_{a}\in\{-1,+1\}
preserves the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry that controls the universality class, and no critical information is lost. Away from

T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
, amplitude fluctuations
\delta|\phi_{i}|
are short-ranged with mass
\propto|a(T)|>0
and decouple from the long-wavelength critical mode. At

T_{c}^{\mathrm{form}}
itself the reduction is justified by the universality of the fixed point, not by a separation of modes.

Dynamic topology reduces to all-to-all in the thermodynamic limit

In COGENT
{}^{\textbf{3}}
the active triad set
E^{(3)}(t)
evolves dynamically. Let
\rho_{N}
denote the mean fraction of agent triples forming active triads. The
minimal condition for a non-trivial thermodynamic formation transition is
\rho_{N}\to\rho>0
as
N\to\infty
, so that each agent participates in
\sim\rho N^{2}
triads.

Under this condition, the effective field on agent
i
from
H_{\mathrm{group}}
in the mean-field approximation is

h_{i}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{\tau\ni i}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\tau\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}\phi_{j}

(9)

a sum of
n=2K_{i}
spin values, where
K_{i}=|\{\tau\ni i:\tau\in E^{(3)}\}|\approx\rho N^{2}/2
is the number of active triads containing agent
i
.
Writing
\phi_{j}=m+\delta_{j}
with
\mathbb{E}[\delta_{j}]=0
and
\delta_{j}\in[-(1+m),\,1-m]\subset[-2,2]
, the mean of
h_{i}^{\mathrm{eff}}
is
\rho J_{\mathrm{eff}}m
and
its fluctuation is
\Delta_{i}\equiv h_{i}^{\mathrm{eff}}-\rho J_{\mathrm{eff}}m=(J_{\mathrm{eff}}/N^{2})\sum_{\ell}\delta_{j_{\ell}}
.

Although the formal sum contains
n=2K_{i}\approx\rho N^{2}
terms, there are only
N-1
distinct agents. Each
\phi_{j}
appears in approximately
c_{ij}\approx\rho N

triads containing
i
. Grouping by distinct agent, define
Y_{j}=c_{ij}\,\delta_{j}
with each
Y_{j}
bounded in
[-2\rho N,\,2\rho N]
. Applying Hoeffding’s
inequality
[
11
]
to the
N-1
grouped terms with threshold
t=\varepsilon N^{2}/J_{\mathrm{eff}}
gives

P\!\left(|\Delta_{i}|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\,N}{8\,\rho^{2}\,J_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}\right)\quad\forall\,\varepsilon>0.

(10)

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
the right-hand side decays exponentially in
N
. The effective field concentrates on
\rho J_{\mathrm{eff}}m
as
N\to\infty
, and the
dynamic topology is asymptotically equivalent to the complete static hypergraph (
\rho=1
). Every agent participates in
\binom{N-1}{2}\sim N^{2}/2
triads, giving

h_{i}^{\mathrm{eff}}=(J_{\mathrm{eff}}/N^{2})\cdot\rho N^{2}\cdot m=J_{\mathrm{eff}}m
in agreement with (
12
).

Consequently, the dynamic topology enters only through a renormalization of
J_{\mathrm{eff}}
. The universality class and all critical exponents are independent of
\rho>0
.

4
Mean-Field Theory

Consider now
N
agents in a complete
3
-uniform hypergraph, with Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}=-\frac{J_{\mathrm{eff}}}{N^{2}}\sum_{\tau\in E^{(3)}}\sum_{\langle ab\rangle\subset\tau}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}-h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{i}

(11)

where the normalization by
N^{-2}
ensures extensivity. In a mean-field approximation,
\phi_{j}\to m\equiv\langle\phi_{j}\rangle
for all
j\neq i
.
As previously noted, agent
i
participates in
\binom{N-1}{2}\sim N^{2}/2
triads. Each such triad contributes two terms of the form
J_{\mathrm{eff}}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}/N^{2}
to

\mathcal{H}
. Replacing
\phi_{j}\to m
and summing over all
\sim N^{2}/2
triads gives
2J_{\mathrm{eff}}m\cdot(N^{2}/2)/N^{2}=J_{\mathrm{eff}}m
in the limit
N\to\infty
. The effective
single-site Hamiltonian from collecting all contributions is

H_{i}^{\rm MF}=-(J_{\mathrm{eff}}m+h)\phi_{i}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})

(12)

from which the self-consistency equation

m=\tanh(\beta\Jeff m + \beta h)

(13)

follows immediately. Linearizing in
m
and
h
at
h=0
reveals the critical condition
\beta_{c}J_{\mathrm{eff}}=1
, giving:

Result 3 (Critical temperature)

\boxed{T_{c}=J_{\mathrm{eff}}=J+\gamma w}

(14)

The gradient coupling strictly raises
T_{c}
above the pure-alignment value
J
, confirming that gradient penalization
promotes
ferromagnetic order.
Below
T_{c}
we have

m\;\sim\;\sqrt{3}\left(\frac{T_{c}-T}{T_{c}}\right)^{1/2}\;\text{as}\quad T\to T_{c}^{-}

(15)

with the standard mean-field exponent
\beta_{\rm Ising}^{\rm MF}=1/2
.

5
Triadic Formation Order Parameter

The single-spin magnetization
m
is not the physically natural observable in triadic networks. The emergence of a coherent computational structure within a
triad is measured by the
three-spin correlator

\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=\Bigl\langle\frac{1}{|E^{(3)}|}\sum_{\tau=\{i,j,k\}}\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\Bigr\rangle_{T}

(16)

which is zero in the disordered phase (by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry) and nonzero whenever a triad achieves coherent configuration. It corresponds to the
formation order parameter
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}
introduced in
[
1
]
(Eq. (95) therein) but derived here from first principles in the Boolean limit
of the group-interaction sector.

Mean-field factorization

In the
N\to\infty
limit on a fully connected hypergraph, the standard cumulant expansion for the three-spin correlator is

\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\rangle=m^{3}+3m\underbrace{\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\rangle_{c}}_{\sim\,\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})}+\underbrace{\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\rangle_{c}}_{\sim\,\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})}.

(17)

Result 4 (Exact factorization)

In the thermodynamic limit,

\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=m^{3}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})

(18)

The formation order parameter factorizes exactly to the cube of the single-site magnetization in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
.

Critical exponent

Substituting
m\sim(T_{c}-T)^{1/2}
from (
15
) into (
18
) yields:

Result 5 (TF critical exponent)

\boxed{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}\;\sim\;(T_{c}-T)^{3/2}\;\;\text{as}\;\;T\to T_{c}^{-}}

(19)

defining the
Triadic Formation critical exponent

\beta_{\mathrm{TF}}=3\,\beta_{\rm Ising}^{\rm MF}=\tfrac{3}{2}.

(20)

This exponent does not coincide with the primary order-parameter exponent of mean-field Ising (
\beta_{\mathrm{Ising}}^{\mathrm{MF}}=\tfrac{1}{2}
), mean-field XY
(
\beta_{\mathrm{XY}}^{\mathrm{MF}}=\tfrac{1}{2}
), or mean-field Heisenberg (
\beta_{\mathrm{Heis}}^{\mathrm{MF}}=\tfrac{1}{2}
) universality classes, all of which share

\beta^{\mathrm{MF}}=1/2
regardless of the spin symmetry group (since mean-field theory is insensitive to the number of order-parameter components). However, it is
precisely the composite-operator exponent
n\beta_{\mathrm{Ising}}^{\mathrm{MF}}
evaluated at
n=3
which follows from

\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}=3\Delta_{\phi}

[
8
,
9
]
.

Equation (
20
) is an exact consequence of the three-body structure. Coherent formation requires three spins to align simultaneously, imposing a cubic
relation between
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}
and the underlying magnetization. The exponent
\beta_{\mathrm{TF}}=3/2
is an algebraic consequence of the composite-operator
structure, and any order parameter constructed as the cube of a mean-field Ising mode inherits this effective exponent via

\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}=3\Delta_{\phi}
.

Absence of susceptibility divergence

Let
h_{3}
couple extensively to the formation order parameter as
\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}-h_{3}N\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}
. The thermodynamically consistent, intensive linear response
is

\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}\equiv\frac{\partial\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}{\partial h_{3}}\bigg|_{h_{3}=0}=\beta\,N\,\mathrm{Var}(\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}).

(21)

It parallels the standard Ising definition
\chi_{\mathrm{Ising}}=\beta N\,\mathrm{Var}(m)
, with
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}
playing the role of order parameter. Both are

\mathcal{O}(1)
in
N
: since
\mathrm{Var}(m)=\chi_{\mathrm{Ising}}/(\beta N)=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
, the factor of
N
in (
21
) cancels exactly, giving

\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}=\beta N\cdot(3m^{2})^{2}\mathrm{Var}(m)=9m^{4}\chi_{\mathrm{Ising}}
, which is genuinely intensive.

Using
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=m^{3}
and the delta-method approximation
\mathrm{Var}(m^{3})\approx(3m^{2})^{2}\mathrm{Var}(m)
, together with
m^{2}\sim(T_{c}-T)
and

\chi_{\mathrm{Ising}}\sim(T_{c}-T)^{-1}
near
T_{c}
:

Result 6 (No susceptibility divergence)

\begin{split}\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}=9m^{4}\chi_{\mathrm{Ising}}&\sim(T_{c}-T)^{2}\cdot(T_{c}-T)^{-1}\\
&=(T_{c}-T)^{+1}\to 0\;\;\text{as}\;\;T\to T_{c}^{-}\end{split}

(22)

The formation susceptibility
vanishes
at
T_{c}
, giving
\gamma_{\mathrm{TF}}=-1
as the effective scaling exponent. For
T>T_{c}
, the
h_{3}
-induced correction to the
effective single-site field is
3h_{3}m^{2}
(from differentiating the coupling
-h_{3}N\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=-h_{3}Nm^{3}
with respect to
\phi_{i}
in the mean-field approximation),
which vanishes when
m=0
. As a result, the mean-field linear response of
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=m^{3}
to
h_{3}
is zero in the disordered phase, and
\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}=0
from above. There
is no divergence at
T_{c}
in response to the formation field.

The delta-method captures the leading power law. Near
T_{c}
fluctuations in
m
modify the prefactor but not the exponent
\gamma_{\mathrm{TF}}=-1
.

As shown in the next section, the scaling exponent
\gamma_{\mathrm{TF}}=-1
is confirmed independently and without recourse to the variance argument by the field-theoretic
two-point function. This is in sharp contrast to Ising criticality (
\chi_{\rm Ising}\sim|T-T_{c}|^{-1}
diverges on both sides). In principle, the non-diverging,
monotone vanishing of
\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}
at
T_{c}
(which approaches
T_{c}
with a kink rather than a divergence) constitutes a signature of the TF scaling regime
(see Sec.
7
).

Scaling consistency

Since all TF exponents are defined via
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=m^{3}
and the relation
\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}=3\Delta_{\phi}
, the Rushbrooke equality

\alpha_{\mathrm{TF}}+2\beta_{\mathrm{TF}}+\gamma_{\mathrm{TF}}=0+3+(-1)=2

(23)

is satisfied by algebraic construction and constitutes a self-consistency check on the exponent definitions, not an independent thermodynamic test.

An independent non-trivial check is provided by the field-theoretic two-point function at criticality. At the upper critical dimension
d_{c}=4
, the scalar field

\phi
has mean-field scaling dimension
\Delta_{\phi}=1
. The composite operator
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=\phi^{3}
then has dimension
\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}=3
.
Its momentum-space susceptibility scales as

\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}(q)\sim|q|^{2\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}-d}=|q|^{6-4}=|q|^{2}

(24)

so the uniform susceptibility
\chi_{\mathrm{TF}}(q{=}0)\sim\xi^{d-2\Delta_{\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}}}=\xi^{-2}\sim t^{+1}
, giving
\gamma_{\mathrm{TF}}=-1

independently of Result 6. This confirms the exponent by an entirely distinct route
[
12
]
.

6
Memory-Modified Dynamical Exponent

The architecture of COGENT
{}^{\textbf{3}}
assigns each agent a memory field. To capture the history-dependent nature of cognitive processing without resolving the
full microscopic fast-variable dynamics, we describe this field using a phenomenological Mori–Zwanzig memory ansatz
K(t)

[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.
Following
[
1
]
(Sec. 8.3.2), in the vicinity of a critical point this memory kernel modifies the dynamical exponent
z
(relating relaxation
time to correlation length,
\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\xi^{z}
) to

z_{\alpha}=z_{\alpha}^{(0)}+\gamma_{K}\int_{0}^{\infty}K(t)\,t^{\theta_{\alpha}-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

(25)

with
\theta_{\alpha}
the autocorrelation decay exponent of the observable
\alpha
. For
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=m^{3}
, the exponent
\theta_{\mathrm{TF}}
is
obtained by analyzing the six-point correlation function in the disordered phase
T>T_{c}
, where Gaussian fluctuations dominate.

The calculation is straightforward. In the disordered phase
T>T_{c}
, the free energy is dominated by the quadratic term
F\sim\tfrac{1}{2}Na(T)m^{2}
with

a(T)=a_{0}(T-T_{c})>0
. Hence, the
m
-fluctuations are Gaussian to leading order in
N^{-1}
, with
\langle m^{2}\rangle=[Na(T)]^{-1}
, and Isserlis’ theorem applies
exactly. Denoting
C(t)\equiv\langle m(t)m(0)\rangle_{c}
, the six-point function factorizes as

\langle m(t)^{3}m(0)^{3}\rangle=6\,C(t)^{3}+9\,\langle m^{2}\rangle^{2}\,C(t).

(26)

Both terms scale identically in
N
because
\langle m^{2}\rangle^{2}\sim N^{-2}
and

8
Conclusions

In this paper we have derived two unconditionally exact results (Results 1–2, valid on
\{-1,+1\}^{3}
) and five results exact within mean-field theory
(Results 3–7, valid in the
N\to\infty
limit of the fully connected 3-uniform hypergraph) for the triadic Ising model and its network generalization. The key
finding is that the triadic formation correlator
\Psi_{\mathrm{form}}=\langle\phi_{i}\phi_{j}\phi_{k}\rangle
scales as
(T_{c}-T)^{3/2}
near criticality, with effective
exponent
\beta_{\mathrm{TF}}=3/2
that is the
k=3
instance of the composite-operator exponent in (
1
), phenomenologically identifiable from any scalar
order-parameter exponent. The associated susceptibility vanishes (rather than diverges) at
T_{c}
, which is independently confirmed by the field-theoretic two-point
function (Sec.
5
). Memory kernels introduce a tunable dynamical exponent, completing the characterization of the composite-operator critical observables
in the TF sector.

These results provide the statistical-mechanical foundation for phase transitions in AI systems with a higher-order interaction structure. Crucially, we
demonstrate that when an AI architecture computes via triadic units, it accesses a composite scaling regime that is fundamentally smoother and more intrinsically
robust to critical noise than standard pairwise networks.
