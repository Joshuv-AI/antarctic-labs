Drift-Free Conservative Dynamics from Quantized Interaction Rules
Park Junhu, Youngsoo Ha, Myungjoo Kang

Abstract
Conservation laws are conventionally discretized through floating-point flux evaluation, with invariants obtained by cancellation of approximate interface contributions and admissible weak solutions selected by reconstruction and Riemann solvers. Here we introduce an operator-level formulation in which conservative dynamics is realized as an exact discrete interaction rule on a quantized state space. The update is defined by an antisymmetric integer-transfer operator, which enforces conservation exactly at the arithmetic level and eliminates round-off drift from the primitive evolution 7 . For scalar laws, monotone order-preserving transfers select admissible shock structures within the primitive update, rather than through flux reconstruction. Numerical experiments show that the interaction rule preserves high-frequency transport near the Nyquist limit and maintains sharply localized discontinuities in Burgers dynamics. The same construction extends to multidimensional problems and systems of conservation laws through oriented, vector-valued integer transfers. These results indicate that conservative dynamics admits an exact discrete realization in which both invariance and entropy selection are encoded at the operator level, rather than arising from approximate flux cancellation.



I
Introduction

Conservation laws play a central role in physical modeling, expressing invariant quantities such as mass, momentum, and energy through local balance relations. In standard numerical formulations, these laws are realized by floating-point evaluation of interface fluxes in finite-volume or finite-difference frameworks

Structurally, this approach represents conservative dynamics through approximate flux evaluation in finite-precision arithmetic. Consequently, invariants are preserved only up to machine precision, and the primitive update depends on rounding behavior, summation order, and reconstruction choices. Although these effects are usually small, they create a separation between the formal conservation law and its discrete realization.

In this work, we take a different viewpoint: conservative dynamics is realized directly as a discrete interaction process. Instead of computing approximate fluxes, we define the primitive evolution by a local antisymmetric transfer operator acting on a quantized state space

;
1
. Conservation is then not imposed by numerical cancellation, but is an exact algebraic invariant of the interaction rule. The resulting method does not approximate a flux-based scheme at the primitive level; it replaces the flux representation itself by an exact antisymmetric transfer operator on a countable state space.

This work does not introduce a numerical approximation of a continuum equation, but formulates a primitive discrete dynamics from which the continuum description emerges.

This representation has two immediate consequences. First, conservation is exact at every step and is independent of floating-point arithmetic. Second, weak-solution selection is relocated to the primitive update: entropy-admissible solutions are determined by the monotone, order-preserving structure of the transfer operator itself
2
. In this sense, the role usually attributed to Riemann solvers is not removed, but absorbed into the operator-level interaction rule.

We therefore interpret conservative dynamics not as the result of flux balancing, but as the coarse-grained description of an underlying discrete interaction rule in which both conservation and entropy selection are encoded in the primitive operator. The continuum flux appears only as an emergent representation of this interaction, while the primitive dynamics is governed by exact integer transfer.

II
Quantized conservative update

We start from the scalar hyperbolic conservation law
3

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,

(1)

although the construction is independent of spatial dimension and of the number of conserved components. In multiple dimensions, an antisymmetric integer transfer is assigned to each oriented cell face; for systems, the integer state and transfer become componentwise vectors. The global integer invariant then follows by pairwise cancellation over internal faces, with boundary terms treated in the usual conservative manner. We restrict attention here to the scalar case to expose the core mechanism.

FQNM evolves a quantized state

q_{i}^{n}\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad u_{i}^{n}\simeq\delta q_{i}^{n},

where

The conservative update is

q_{i}^{n+1}=q_{i}^{n}-\left(F_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}-F_{i-\frac{1}{2}}^{n}\right),

(3)

where the integer interface transfer is

F_{i+\frac{1}{2}}^{n}=\phi^{+}(q_{i}^{n})+\phi^{-}(q_{i+1}^{n}).

(4)

A representative construction is

\phi^{\pm}(q)=\operatorname{round}\!\left[\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\frac{f^{\pm}(\delta q)}{\delta}\right].

(5)

\sum_{i}q_{i}^{n+1}=\sum_{i}q_{i}^{n}.

(6)

This identity holds exactly in integer arithmetic and does not rely on cancellation of floating-point quantities. Thus the primitive integer mass is invariant in time and independent of summation order.

\|u-\delta q\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}.

III
Numerical tests

We test whether the integer-transfer formulation preserves high-frequency transport and nonlinear shock structure without introducing drift or excessive smoothing. All simulations use a uniform grid, with identical resolution and CFL number across the compared methods. In the Burgers tests, FQNM and the WENO baseline use the same grid and time step, so differences in shock structure arise from the primitive update mechanism.

The persistence of fine-scale structure through nonlinear interaction indicates that shock formation and propagation are governed directly by the transfer operator, without requiring a separate Riemann-solver stage. The observed discontinuities are selected and maintained by the monotone integer transfer itself; entropy admissibility is therefore realized at the level of the primitive update rather than through reconstructed fluxes. Although the transfer has a first-order upwind form, quantization yields a sharper and more accurately located shock than the unquantized upwind update and the WENO baseline. High-frequency content remains localized without excessive numerical diffusion, consistent with the operator-level encoding of both conservation and admissibility.

The difference from the sampled Hopf–Lax profile does not indicate a failure of the quantized transfer formulation. The Hopf–Lax construction gives the continuum entropy solution, but its direct sampling on a finite grid need not satisfy the intermediate-state consistency condition imposed by the discrete conservative update. Thus the observed discrepancy reflects finite-resolution compatibility with the discrete update, rather than an error of the method. In this setting, an exact discrete solution is a grid state that satisfies Eq. (
3
) pointwise at every time step. The intermediate-state condition requires the discrete flux differences to produce a single-valued admissible transition across a shock without violating the monotone transfer structure.

The Appendix presents a Shu–Osher shock–entropy interaction test to illustrate the same operator-level construction in a nonlinear system. That example shows that the integer-transfer formulation can sustain coupled nonlinear and high-frequency features in system-level dynamics, while the present text focuses on the scalar case to expose the core mechanism.

IV
Discussion

The present formulation replaces flux-based reconstruction, at the primitive level, with a discrete antisymmetric transfer operator. Invariance is enforced algebraically by the update itself and is therefore independent of floating-point round-off.

Extensions to multidimensional problems and systems follow from the same operator-level construction, although they are not developed here in detail. This suggests that conservative dynamics may admit a fundamentally discrete realization in which flux-based representations are replaced by an exact interaction rule that encodes both invariance and entropy selection in its primitive structure.

V
Conclusion

We have introduced an operator-level formulation of conservative dynamics based on exact integer transfer between quantized states. The resulting update conserves exactly in integer arithmetic, avoids round-off drift, and encodes entropy selection through a monotone order-preserving transfer rule. Numerical tests show that this structure preserves high-frequency transport and sharp nonlinear discontinuities, indicating that flux-based continuum descriptions can arise as coarse-grained representations of an exact discrete interaction rule.

Appendix A
Extension to nonlinear systems

The integer-transfer formulation introduced in the main text extends directly to nonlinear systems of conservation laws. In this setting, the state becomes a vector-valued integer field, and the interface transfer is applied componentwise. The antisymmetric structure of the transfer ensures exact conservation for each component, while intercomponent coupling enters through flux splitting.
