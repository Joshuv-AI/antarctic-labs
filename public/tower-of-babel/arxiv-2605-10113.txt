Motzkin paths with two variants of level steps on odd levels — a kernel method approach
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. The sequence A176677 in the Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences enumerates Motzkin paths where two types of horizontal steps may occur, but only on odd indexed levels. We show how to perform the enumeration, also dealing with partial such Motzkin paths leading to a particular level or to any level (open paths). The method is the kernel method where functional equations are manipulated in a suitable way. The coefficients of sequence A176677 satisfy a holonomic recursion that was recently discussed on the arxiv. We show how this can be established in an (almost) automatic fashion. Eventually we switch the roles of ‘odd’ and ‘even’. One could also allow more versions of horizontal steps but we leave this to the interested readers.

1.
Motzkin paths with variations on the possible level steps

Motzkin paths have up steps
(1,1)
, down steps
(1,-1)
and level steps
(1,0)
. Like Dyck paths, they do not go below the
x
-axis and return eventually to the
x
-axis.
Partial paths that do not necessarily return to the
x
-axis are also of interest. If there are two versions of level steps allowed, but only on an odd level, the enumeration leads to
the sequence A176677 in
[
3
]
. We show how to compute this using the kernel method, as explained in
[
1
]
and many other papers.

We use an automaton to describe the Motzkin paths in question. We use two levels (‘even’ on top, ‘odd’ at the bottom); as usual, there are loops, but for the bottom layer there are loops of two possible level steps.

Since not all the states are accessible, one might simplify the automaton (Figure 2):

\dots

Figure 2.
Simplified version

However, we find it more straight forward to stay with the two layers of states even though not all of them can be reached. We use generating functions
f_{i}(z)
and
g_{i}(z)

leading to level
i
on top resp. bottom layer, and derive recursions according to what the last step was:

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=1+zf_{0}+zg_{1},

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i}+zg_{i+1}+zg_{i-1},\ i\geq 1

\displaystyle g_{0}

\displaystyle=2zg_{0}+zf_{1},

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=2zg_{i}+zf_{i+1}+zf_{i-1},\ i\geq 1.

Then we need bivariate generating functions as well:

F(z,u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}f_{i}(z),\quad G(z,u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i}(z);

by summing up the recursions one finds

\displaystyle F(z,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}f_{i}=1+zf_{0}+zg_{1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}[zf_{i}+zg_{i+1}+zg_{i-1}]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}zg_{i-1}

\displaystyle=1+zF(z,u)+\frac{z}{u}[G(z,u)-g_{0}]+zuG(z,u).

Notice that
g_{0}=0
for combinatorial reasons;

\displaystyle G(z,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i}=2zg_{0}+zf_{1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i}[2zg_{i}+zf_{i+1}+zf_{i-1}]

\displaystyle=2zG(z,u)+\frac{z}{u}[F(z,u)-f_{0}]+zuF(z,u).

For simplicity, we will occasionally write
F(u)=F(z,u)
,
G(u)=G(z,u)
,
F(0)=f_{0}
,
G(0)=g_{0}=0
.
Solving the system,

F(u)=\frac{2z{u}^{2}-{u}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{2}f_{0}+f_{0}{z}^{2}}{{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{4}-{u}^{2}+3z{u}^{2}},

G(u)=-{\frac{z(u+uzf_{0}+{u}^{3}-uf_{0})}{{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{4}-{u}^{2}+3z{u}^{2}}}.

We need to factor the denominator and abbreviate

W:=\sqrt{(1-z)(1-2z)(1-3z-2z^{2})};

\displaystyle s_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-3z+W}}{2z},\quad s_{2}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-3z+W}}{2z},

\displaystyle\ s_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-3z-W}}{2z},\quad s_{4}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{1-3z-W}}{2z}.

Then

{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{4}-{u}^{2}+3z{u}^{2}=z^{2}(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})(u-s_{3})(u-s_{4}).

Plugging in
u=0
leads to a void equation, but
the factors
u-s_{3}
and
u-s_{4}
are ‘bad’ (in the spirit of the kernel method
[
1
]
, as they do not have a power series expansion around
(u,z)\sim(0,0)
) and must cancel out. Then

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{2z-1+f_{0}z^{2}}{z^{2}(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-zu}{z^{2}(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})}.

From this we find, by setting
u=0

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-3z-2z^{2}-W}{2(1-z)z^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+z+2z^{2}+5z^{3}+14z^{4}+41z^{5}+123z^{6}+375z^{7}+1158z^{8}+3615z^{9}+\dots,

which is the sequence A176677 in
[
3
]
.
Note that

(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})=u^{2}-s_{1}^{2}.

Then

G(u)=\frac{-u}{z(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})}=\frac{-u}{z(u^{2}-s_{1}^{2})}=\frac{u}{zs_{1}^{2}(1-u^{2}/s_{1}^{2})}

and

[u^{2k+1}]G(u)=[u^{2k}]\frac{1}{zs_{1}^{2}(1-u^{2}/s_{1}^{2})}=\frac{1}{zs_{1}^{2k+2}}.

Further

\frac{2z-1+f_{0}z^{2}}{z^{2}}=\frac{2z-1}{z^{2}}+\frac{1-3z-2z^{2}-W}{2(1-z)z^{2}}=-\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{1-z}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}-\frac{2}{z}

and

[u^{2k}]F(u)=\biggl[-\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{1-z}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}-\frac{2}{z}\biggr]\frac{1}{s_{1}^{2k+2}}.

Note that
F(1)+G(1)
is the generating function of all
open
Motzkin paths (variations as discussed in this note).

\displaystyle F(1)+G(1)=1+3z+3z^{2}+13z^{3}+41z^{4}+135z^{5}+447z^{6}+1493z^{7}+5019z^{8}+16965z^{9}+\dots

This sequence of coefficients
1,3,3,13,41,\dots
in not in the
[
3
]
.

We summarize our findings:

Theorem 1
.

Let

s_{1}^{2}=\frac{{1-3z+\sqrt{(1-z)(1-2z)(1-3z-2z^{2})}}}{2z^{2}},

then the generating function of Motzkin paths with 2 variations of level steps on odd levels, leading to level
2k
is

\biggl[-\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{1-z}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}-\frac{2}{z}\biggr]\frac{1}{s_{1}^{2k+2}}

and to level
2k+1
is

\frac{1}{zs_{1}^{2k+2}}.

The generating function of open Motzkin paths is

\displaystyle F(1)+G(1)=1+3z+3z^{2}+13z^{3}+41z^{4}+135z^{5}+447z^{6}+1493z^{7}+5019z^{8}+16965z^{9}+\dots;

an analytic expression for
F(1)+G(1)
is available from the text.

2.
Automatic derivation of a holonomic recurrence for the Motzkin numbers in question

We want to derive a recursion for the coefficients of the generating function

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-3z-2z^{2}-\sqrt{(1-z)(1-2z)(1-3z-2z^{2})}}{2(1-z)z^{2}};

the software
gun

[
2
]
contains all the necessary tools for that. The following steps will be performed.

•

Find an algebraic equation that
f_{0}
satisfies.

•

Translate the algebraic equation into a differential equation.

•

Translate the differential equation into a recurrence for the coefficients.

>\textsf{algfuntoalgeq}(f_{0},y(z));

the result is

(z^{3}-z^{2})y^{2}+(1-3z+2z^{2})y+2z-1=0.

The algebraic equation can be translated into a differential equation

>\textsf{algeqtodiffeq}(y^{2}z^{3}-y^{2}z^{2}+2yz^{2}-3yz+2z+y-1=0,y(z));

the result is

6z^{2}-7z+2+(10z-14z^{2}+4z^{3}+4z^{4}-2)y(z)+(4z^{5}-9z^{3}-z+6z^{2})y^{\prime}(z)=0.

One can get a homogenous differential equation as well:

>\textsf{diffeqtohomdiffeq}(6z^{2}-7z+2+(10z-14z^{2}+4z^{3}+4z^{4}-2)y(z)+(4z^{5}-9z^{3}-z+6z^{2})y^{\prime}(z)=0;

the result is

\displaystyle 2(24z^{4}-6z^{3}-18z^{2}+13z-3)(z-1)y(z)

\displaystyle+2(2z-1)(24z^{5}-17z^{4}-38z^{3}+54z^{2}-23z+3)y^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle+z(z-1)(3z-2)(2z^{2}+3z-1)(2z-1)^{2}y{{}^{\prime\prime}}(z)=0

Now we translate into a recursion for the (Taylor) coefficients

>\textsf{diffeqtorec}(6z^{2}-7z+2+(10z-14z^{2}+4z^{3}+4z^{4}-2)y(z)+(4z^{5}-9z^{3}-z+6z^{2})y^{\prime}(z),y(z),m(n));

the result is the recursion

\displaystyle 4(n+1)m(n)+4m(n+1)-(9n+32)m(n+2)

\displaystyle+2(3n+14)m(n+3)-(n+6)m(n+4)=0,

\displaystyle m(0)=1,\ m(1)=1,\ m(2)=2,\ m(3)=5.

This recursion of the coefficients

f_{0}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}m(n)z^{n}

is equivalent to the one given in

4
]
but derived in a (semi-)automatic fashion.
gfun
has several additional features of practical interest

3.
Switching the roles of even resp. odd levels

As the section title says, we switch to roles of ‘even’ resp. ‘odd’. The notation stays the same, and there just a few modifications necessary.

\dots

Brown loops refer to two variants of level steps, black loops just to one variant.

Then

\displaystyle F(z,u)

\displaystyle=1+2zF(z,u)+\frac{z}{u}G(z,u)+zuG(z,u),

\displaystyle G(z,u)

\displaystyle=zG(z,u)+\frac{z}{u}[F(z,u)-f_{0}]+zuF(z,u).

Solving the system,

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{z^{2}u^{2}f_{0}+z^{2}f_{0}+zu^{2}-u^{2}}{{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{4}-{u}^{2}+3z{u}^{2}},

\displaystyle G(u)=-\frac{zu(1-f_{0}+2zf_{0}+u^{2})}{{z}^{2}+{z}^{2}{u}^{4}-{u}^{2}+3z{u}^{2}}.

The denominators are the same as before. Consequently, dividing out the two bad factors
(u-s_{3})(u-s_{4})
,

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-3z+2z^{2}-W}{2(1-2z)z^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+2z+5z^{2}+13z^{3}+35z^{4}+97z^{5}+276z^{6}+804z^{7}+2391z^{8}+\dots,

a sequence that is not in the OEIS
[
3
]

\displaystyle F(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}f_{0}+z-1}{z^{2}(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})},

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{-u}{z(u-s_{1})(u-s_{2})}.

The function
G(u)
has not changed from before (which makes sense once one thinks about it). However,

F(u)=\frac{\frac{1-3z+2z^{2}-W}{2(1-2z)}+z-1}{z^{2}(u^{2}-s_{1}^{2})}=-\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{(1-2z)(u^{2}-s_{1}^{2})}=\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{(1-2z)s_{1}^{2}(1-u^{2}/s_{1}^{2})}

and

[u^{2k}]F(u)=f_{2k}=\frac{1+s_{1}^{2}}{(1-2z)s_{1}^{2k+2}}.

Again,
