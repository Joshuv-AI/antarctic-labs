Bound State Solutions of the Relativistic Finite-difference Equation for the Ring-shaped Quesne Oscillator Potential
Sh.M.Nagiyev, Narmin Nasibova, V. A. Tarverdiyeva, G. H. Guliyeva

Abstract




Abstract

Institute of Physics, Ministry of Science and Education,

H. Javid Avenue, 131, AZ 1143, Baku, Azerbaijan

shakir.m.nagiyev@gmail.com

We solve exactly the relativistic finite-difference equation for the quantum three-dimensional ring-shaped Quesne oscillator potential. Our investigation is based on a finite-difference version of relativistic quantum mechanics. So-called relativistic configurational
\bf r
-space is a key concept here. We show that the radial wavefunctions and angular wavefunctions are expressed through the continuous dual Hahn polynomials and Jacobi polynomials, respectively. A discrete energy spectrum has been found. The radial wave functions and energy spectrum have the correct nonrelativistic limit. We also build a dynamical symmetry group
SU(1,1)
for the radial part of the equation of motion, which allows us to find the energy spectrum purely algebraically.

Keywords:
Relativistic finite-difference equation of motion; ring-shaped oscillator potential; the continuous dual Hahn and Jacobi polynomials; nonrelativistic limit; dynamical symmetry group.

II
The finite-difference relativistic quantum mechanics

The key concept in the finite-difference version of relativistic quantum mechanics is the notion of the three-dimensional relativistic configurational
\bf r
-space. The corresponding canonically conjugate momentum
\bf p
-space is the three-dimensional Lobachevsky space realized on the upper sheet of the mass hyperboloid

p_{0}^{2}-\mathbf{p}^{2}=m_{0}^{2}c^{2},\quad p_{0}>0.

(2)

Both
\bf r
- and
\bf p
-spaces are related to each other by the relativistic Fourier transform

\psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3/2}}\int\xi(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})\,\psi(\mathbf{p})\,d\Omega_{p},

(3)

where
d\Omega_{p}=m_{0}c\,dp/p_{0}
is the relativistic three-dimensional volume element in the momentum Lobachevsky space and the function
\xi(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})
is the relativistic plane waves

\xi(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})=\left(\frac{p_{0}-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{n}}{mc}\right)^{-1-ir/\lambdabar}.

(4)

Here,
\mathbf{r}=r\mathbf{n}
,
0\leq r<\infty
,
\mathbf{n}=(\sin\vartheta\cos\varphi,\sin\vartheta\sin\varphi,\cos\vartheta)
is the unit vector along the radius vector, and
\lambdabar=\hbar/(m_{0}c)
is the Compton wavelength of the particle. The relativistic plane waves (
4
) obey the finite-difference free Schrödinger equation

(H_{0}-E_{p})\xi(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})=0,\quad E_{p}=cp_{0},

(5)

where the finite-difference operator

H_{0}=m_{0}c^{2}\left[\cosh(i\lambdabar\partial_{r})+i\frac{\lambdabar}{r}\sinh(i\lambdabar\partial_{r})+\frac{\bf L^{2}}{2(m_{0}cr)^{2}}e^{i\lambdabar\partial_{r}}\right]

(6)

is the free Hamiltonian and
{\bf L^{2}}=-\hbar^{2}\Delta_{\vartheta,\varphi}
is the square of the angular momentum operator

{\bf L^{2}}=-\hbar^{2}\left[\frac{1}{\sin\vartheta}\partial_{\vartheta}(\sin\vartheta\,\partial_{\vartheta})+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}\right].

(7)

The equation for the wave function of the relative motion of two scalar particles with the interaction potential
V({\bf r})
in the relativistic configurational
\bf r
-space can be obtained from (
5
) and has the form

[H_{0}+V({\bf r})]\psi(\mathbf{r})=E\psi(\mathbf{r}).

(8)

In the nonrelativistic limit we have

\lim_{c\to\infty}\xi(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})=e^{i\mathbf{p}\mathbf{r}/\hbar}.

(9)

III
The finite-difference relativistic ring-shaped Quesne oscillator model

We now consider a finite-difference relativistic ring-shaped oscillator model with the potential

V(r)=\left[\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\omega^{2}(r+i\lambdabar)^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta}\right]e^{i\lambdabar\partial_{r}},

(10)

where
\alpha=(q\sigma^{2}\eta^{2}\hbar^{2})/(4m_{0})
is a positive parameter.
This potential is a generalization of the Quesne potential (
1
) to the case of finite-difference relativistic quantum mechanics.

The potential (
10
) has a correct nonrelativistic limit (
1
):

\lim_{c\to\infty}V({\bf r})=\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\omega^{2}r^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta}.

(11)

The operator (
10
) is Hermitian with respect to a scalar product

(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=\int\psi_{1}^{*}({\bf r})\,\psi_{2}({\bf r})\,dr,

(12)

i.e.
(V\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=(\psi_{1},V\psi_{2})
. Here, the functions
\psi_{1}({\bf r})
and
\psi_{2}({\bf r})
vanish at the points
r=0
and
r=\infty
together with all their derivatives.

We want to find a solution to equation (
8
) with potential(
10
). To do this, we define
\psi(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{r}R({\bf r})
, then we obtain the following equation for the wave function
R{\bf(r)}
:

\Bigl[m_{0}c^{2}\cosh(i\lambdabar\partial_{r})+\frac{{\bf L^{2}}}{2m_{0}r^{(2)}}e^{i\lambdabar\partial_{r}}\\
+\left(\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\omega^{2}r^{(2)}+\frac{\alpha}{r^{(2)}\sin^{2}\vartheta}\right)e^{i\lambdabar\partial_{r}}-E\Bigr]R({\bf r})=0,

where
r^{(\delta)}=(i\lambdabar)^{\delta}\Gamma(\delta-\frac{ir}{\lambdabar})/\Gamma(-\frac{ir}{\lambdabar})
is a generalized degree. This equation allows the separation of variables. Since the Hamiltonian of Eq. (
III
) commutes with the operator
\hat{L}_{z}=-i\hbar\partial_{\varphi}
, we can look for the wave function in the form

R(\mathbf{r})=R(r)\,F(\vartheta)\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{im\varphi},

(13)

where
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots
is the usual magnetic quantum number. Substitution of (
13
) into Eq. (
III
) leads to a set of finite-difference and second-order differential equations

\left[\cosh(i\partial_{\rho})+V_{eff}(\rho)-\varepsilon\right]R(\rho)=0,~V_{eff}(\rho)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\omega_{0}^{2}\rho^{(2)}+\frac{\Lambda}{\rho^{(2)}}\right)e^{i\partial_{\rho}},

(14)

\left[\partial_{\vartheta}^{2}+\cot\vartheta\,\partial_{\vartheta}-\frac{M^{2}}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}+\Lambda\right]F(\vartheta)=0,

(15)

where
\rho=r/\lambdabar
is a dimensionless variable,
M^{2}=m^{2}+(2m_{0}\alpha)/\hbar^{2}
,
\Lambda
is a separation constant, and
\rho^{(2)}=\rho(\rho+i)
.

The value for the parameter
\Lambda
will be determined from the angular Eq. (
15
). In addition, we have introduced the following notations
\omega_{0}=\hbar\omega/(m_{0}c^{2})
,
\varepsilon=E/(m_{0}c^{2})
. Obviously, when
\alpha=0
, we have
\Lambda=l(l+1)
.

IV
The solutions of the angular equation

We investigate the solutions of the angle-dependent equation (
15
)
ref30
. In terms of the new variable
x=\cos\vartheta
, we obtain the following equation for the function
F(\vartheta)\equiv F(x)
:

\left[\partial_{x}^{2}-\frac{2x}{1-x^{2}}\partial_{x}+\frac{c_{0}-c_{2}x^{2}}{(1-x^{2})^{2}}\right]F(x)=0,

(16)

where

c_{0}=\Lambda-M^{2},\quad c_{2}=\Lambda.

(17)

We look for the solution of eq. (
16
) in the form
ref29

F(x)=\varphi(x)\,y(x),\quad\varphi(x)=(1-x)^{A}(1+x)^{B},

(18)

where
\varphi(x)
is the weight part, and
y(x)
is the polynomial part of the angular wave function
F(x)
. From the condition of finiteness of
F(x)
at points
x=\pm 1
it follows that the parameters
A
and
B
must satisfy the inequalities
A\geq 0
,
B\geq 0
. Then for the function
y(x)
we obtain the following equation

y^{\prime\prime}(x)-\frac{2\delta+2(s+1)x}{1-x^{2}}y^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\gamma_{2}x^{2}+\gamma_{1}x+\gamma_{0}}{(1-x^{2})^{2}}y(x)=0.

(19)

Here we use the notations
\delta=A-B
,
s=A+B
, and also

\gamma_{2}=-c_{2}+s+s^{2},\quad\gamma_{1}=2\delta s,\quad\gamma_{0}=c_{0}-s+\delta^{2}.

(20)

Now let us choose the parameters
A
and
B
so that the relation
\gamma_{2}x^{2}+\gamma_{1}x+\gamma_{0}=\lambda(1-x^{2})
is satisfied, i.e.
\gamma_{2}=-\lambda
,
\gamma_{1}=0
,
\gamma_{0}=\lambda
, where
\lambda=\mathrm{const}
. From these equalities we find the parameters
A
,
B
and
\lambda
:

A=B=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{c_{2}-c_{0}}=\frac{1}{2}|M|,

(21)

\lambda=c_{2}-|M|-M^{2}.

(22)

Now, Eq. (
19
) takes the form

(1-x^{2})y^{\prime\prime}(x)-(2|M|+2)x\,y^{\prime}(x)+\lambda\,y(x)=0.

(23)

Comparing it with the equation for Gegenbauer polynomials
\bar{y}(x)=C_{k}^{(\beta)}(x)
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots

ref32
:

(1-x^{2})\bar{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)-(2\beta+1)x\,\bar{y}^{\prime}(x)+k(k+2\beta)\bar{y}(x)=0,

(24)

we get
\beta=|M|+\frac{1}{2}
and

\lambda\equiv\lambda_{k}=k(k+2|M|+1).

(25)

Gegenbauer polynomials satisfy the following orthogonality condition:

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x^{2})^{\beta-1/2}C_{k}^{(\beta)}(x)\,C_{k^{\prime}}^{(\beta)}(x)\,dx=\frac{\pi\,\Gamma(k+2\beta)\,2^{1-2\beta}}{\{\Gamma(\beta)\}^{2}(k+\beta)\,k!}\,\delta_{kk^{\prime}},

(26)

where
\beta>-\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta\neq 0
.

Thus, we conclude that the solution of equation (
23
)
y(x)\equiv y_{k}(\cos\vartheta)
is expressed through Gegenbauer polynomials, i.e.

y_{k}(\cos\vartheta)=C_{k}^{(|M|+1/2)}(\cos\vartheta).

(27)

We can now write an expression for the total angular wave function in terms of the angle
\vartheta
:

F_{km}(\cos\vartheta)=c_{km}\,(\sin\vartheta)^{|M|}\\
C_{k}^{(|M|+1/2)}(\cos\vartheta).

(28)

The normalization constant
c_{km}
is found from the orthonormality condition

\int_{0}^{\pi}F_{km}(\cos\vartheta)\,F_{k^{\prime}m}(\cos\vartheta)\,\sin\vartheta\,d\vartheta=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}.

(29)

It equals to

c_{km}=2^{-|M|}\sqrt{\frac{(k+|M|+\frac{1}{2})\,k!\,\Gamma(k+2|M|+1)}{\Gamma(k+|M|+1)}}.

(30)

Let us now emphasize that from the two formulas (
23
) and (
25
) for the value
\lambda
we find the following quantized expression for the separation parameter
\Lambda\equiv\Lambda_{k}=L(L+1)
, where
L=k+|M|
. It is easy to see that when
\alpha=0
we have
l=k+m
.

Let us emphasize that due to the relationship between the Gegenbauer polynomials and the associated Legendre function of the first kind
ref33
:

P_{n}^{m}(\cos\vartheta)=(-2)^{m}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}(\sin\vartheta)^{|M|}\;C_{n-m}^{(m+1/2)}(\cos\vartheta),

(31)

the angular wave function (
28
) coincides with the corresponding result of
ref15
. Therefore, as in
ref15
, in the case we are considering, there are also two integrals of motion:

W_{1}={\bf L^{2}}+\frac{2m_{0}\alpha}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{\sin\vartheta}\partial_{\vartheta}(\sin\vartheta\,\partial_{\vartheta})-\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\vartheta}(\hbar^{2}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}-2m_{0}\alpha),

(32)

W_{2}=L_{z}^{2}+2m_{0}\alpha=-\hbar^{2}\partial_{\varphi}^{2}+2m_{0}\alpha.

(33)

V
The solutions of the radial equation

We will obtain the solutions of Eq. (
14
). To solve Eq. (
14
), we choose
R(\rho)
as
ref11

R(\rho)=(-\rho)^{(\mu_{k})}\,M_{\nu_{k}}(\rho)\,\Omega(\rho).

(34)

Here the separated factors

\rho^{(\mu_{k})}=i^{\mu_{k}}\frac{\Gamma(\mu_{k}-i\rho)}{\Gamma(-i\rho)},\quad M_{\nu_{k}}(\rho)=\omega_{0}^{i\rho}\,\Gamma(\nu_{k}+i\rho)

(35)

determine the asymptotic behaviour of
R(\rho)
at the points
\rho=0
and
\rho=\infty
respectively, where

\mu_{k}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{2}{\omega_{0}^{2}}\!\left(1-\sqrt{1-4\omega_{0}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}\right)},

(36)

\nu_{k}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{2}{\omega_{0}^{2}}\!\left(1+\sqrt{1-4\omega_{0}^{2}\Lambda_{k}}\right)}

(37)

are the real parameters. It follows that

\Lambda_{k}\leq\left(\frac{m_{0}c^{2}}{2\hbar\omega}\right)^{2}.

(38)

This condition imposes an upper limit on the values of the “orbital” quantum number
k
.
The function
\Omega(\rho)
then satisfies the following finite-difference equation:

\left[(\mu_{k}+i\rho)(\nu_{k}+i\rho)e^{-i\partial_{\rho}}-(\mu_{k}-i\rho)(\nu_{k}-i\rho)e^{i\partial_{\rho}}\right]\Omega(\rho)=\frac{2\varepsilon}{\omega_{0}}\,i\rho\,\Omega(\rho).

(39)

Let us compare this equation with the equation for continuous dual Hahn polynomials
y(x)=S_{n}(x^{2};a,b,c)
at
c=\frac{1}{2}

ref32
:

\left[(a+ix)(b+ix)e^{-i\partial_{x}}-(a-ix)(b-ix)e^{i\partial_{x}}\right]y(x)=(2a+2b+4n)\,ix\,y(x).

(40)

From the comparison we get that
a=\mu_{k}
,
b=\nu_{k}
,
\varepsilon=\omega_{0}(2n+\mu_{k}+\nu_{k})
, where
n=0,1,2,\ldots
is the radial quantum number. As a result we have

E_{nk}=\hbar\omega(2n+\mu_{k}+\nu_{k}).

(41)

This gives the quantization rule for the energy levels of the three-dimensional finite-difference relativistic ring-shaped oscillator model under consideration. Thus, the polynomial solution of the finite difference equation (
39
) is expressed through the continuous dual Hahn polynomials:

\Omega(\rho)\equiv\Omega_{nk}(\rho)=S_{n}\!\left(\rho^{2};\,\mu_{k},\nu_{k},\frac{1}{2}\right).

(42)

Note that the continuous dual Hahn polynomials are defined with relation
ref32
:

S_{n}(x^{2};a,b,c)=(a+b)_{n}(a+c)_{n}\;{}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-n,\ a+ix,\ a-ix\\
a+b,\ a+c\end{array};1\right).

(43)

If
a
,
b
and
c
are positive except possibly for a pair of complex conjugates with positive real parts, then the continuous dual Hahn polynomials satisfy the following orthogonality condition
ref32
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\Gamma(a+ix)\,\Gamma(b+ix)\,\Gamma(c+ix)}{\Gamma(2ix)}\right|^{2}S_{n}(x^{2};a,b,c)\,S_{m}(x^{2};a,b,c)\,dx

\displaystyle=\Gamma(n+a+b)\,\Gamma(n+a+c)\,\Gamma(n+b+c)\,n!\;\delta_{nm}.

(44)

We can now write down an expression for the radial wave function
R(\rho)
(
34
), corresponding to the energy levels (
41
):

R(\rho)\equiv R_{nk}(\rho)=\tilde{c}_{nk}\,(-\rho)^{(\mu_{k})}\,M_{\nu_{k}}(\rho)\,S_{n}\!\left(\rho^{2};\,\mu_{k},\nu_{k},\frac{1}{2}\right).

(45)

The orthogonality condition for the function (
45
) easily follows from the condition (
44
):

\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{nk}^{*}(\rho)\,R_{mk}(\rho)\,d\rho=\delta_{nm}.

(46)

From here we obtain the following expression for the normalization constant:

\tilde{c}_{nk}=2^{1/2}\left[n!\,\Gamma(n+\mu_{k}+\nu_{k})\,\Gamma\!\left(n+\mu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(n+\nu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right]^{-1/2}.

(47)

Hence, the total wave function takes the form:

\psi_{nkm}(\mathbf{r})=\tilde{c}_{nk}\,c_{km}\,\frac{1}{r}\,\left(-\frac{r}{\lambdabar}\right)^{(\mu_{k})}\!M_{\nu_{k}}(\rho)\,S_{n}\!\left(\left(\frac{r}{\lambdabar}\right)^{2};\mu_{k},\nu_{k},\frac{1}{2}\right)(\sin\!\vartheta)^{|M|}\;C_{k}^{(|M|+1/2)}(\cos\vartheta)\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{im\varphi}.

(48)

Wave function (
48
) satisfies the normalization condition
\int\psi_{nkm}^{*}({\bf r})\,\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}m^{\prime}}({\bf r})d{\bf r}=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\delta_{kk^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}.

VI
The non-relativistic limit case

It can be easily shown that formula (
41
) for the energy spectrum and formula (
45
) for the radial wave function of the relativistic ring-shaped Quesne oscillator have a correct nonrelativistic limit, i.e. they in the nonrelativistic limit
c\to\infty
pass into the corresponding formulas of the nonrelativistic ring-shaped oscillator. For this, it is sufficient to take into account the following limit and asymptotic relations
ref11
:

\lim_{c\to\infty}\lambdabar^{\mu_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1+4\Lambda_{k}}=L+1,\quad\lim_{c\to\infty}\!\left(\nu_{k}-\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}\right)=\frac{1}{2},

(49)

\lim_{c\to\infty}\lambdabar^{\mu_{k}}(-\rho)^{(\mu_{k})}=(-r)^{L+1},\quad\lim_{c\to\infty}M_{\nu_{k}}(\rho)\cong\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}\ln\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{0}^{2}r^{2}\right),

(50)

\lim_{c\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{n!}S_{n}\!\left(\rho^{2};\mu_{k},\nu_{k},\frac{1}{2}\right)=L_{n}^{L+1/2}(\lambda_{0}^{2}r^{2}).

(51)

Here
\lambda_{0}=\sqrt{m_{0}\omega/\hbar}
and
L_{n}^{a}(z)
are the generalized Laguerre polynomials
ref32
;
ref33
.

VII
Dynamical symmetry group for the radial wave equation

In this section we will construct a dynamical symmetry group for the radial equation (
14
). We will show that this group is the group
SU(1,1)
. Its generators
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
satisfy the commutation relations
ref34
:

[K_{0},K_{1}]=iK_{2},\quad[K_{2},K_{0}]=iK_{1},\quad[K_{1},K_{2}]=-iK_{0}.

(52)

In terms of the lowering and raising operators
K^{\pm}=K_{1}\pm iK_{2}
they are written as

[K_{0},K^{\pm}]=\pm K^{\pm},\quad[K^{-},K^{+}]=2K_{0}.

(53)

The Casimir operator is

C_{2}=K_{0}^{2}-K_{1}^{2}-K_{2}^{2}=K_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(K^{-}K^{+}+K^{+}K^{-}).

(54)

We denote the states of a positive discrete series
D^{+}(s)
as
|n,s\rangle
such that
K_{0}|n,s\rangle=(n+s)|n,s\rangle
and
C_{2}|n,s\rangle=s(s-1)|n,s\rangle
,
s
being the Bargmann index,
s>0
and
n=0,1,2,3,\ldots
Action of operators
K^{\pm}
on states
|n,s\rangle
is given by formulas:

K^{-}|n,s\rangle=\sqrt{n(n+2s-1)}\,|n-1,s\rangle,\quad K^{+}|n,s\rangle=\sqrt{(n+1)(n+2s)}\,|n+1,s\rangle.

(55)

To construct the dynamical symmetry group for the radial equation of motion (
14
), we note that the operators
ref35
:

\displaystyle a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}-\omega_{0}\,e^{\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}(\mu_{k}+i\rho)\!\left(1+\frac{\nu_{k}}{i\rho}\right)\right],

(56)

\displaystyle a^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}-\omega_{0}(\mu_{k}-i\rho)\!\left(1-\frac{\nu_{k}}{i\rho}\right)e^{\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}\right]

(57)

factorize the Hamiltonian of equation (
14
), i.e.

H=m_{0}c^{2}\left[a^{+}a+\omega_{0}(\mu_{k}+\nu)\right].

(58)

However, the operators
a^{+}
,
a
and
H
do not form a closed algebra, since

[a,a^{+}]=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{0}\!\left(1+\frac{\mu_{k}\nu_{k}}{\rho^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}+\omega_{0}Q\,e^{i\partial_{\rho}}\right),

(59)

Q=\mu_{k}+\nu_{k}-\frac{1}{4}+\mu_{k}\nu_{k}\!\left[-\frac{(\mu_{k}-1)(\nu_{k}-1)}{\rho^{(2)}}+\frac{\mu_{k}\nu_{k}}{(\rho+\frac{i}{2})^{(2)}}\right].

(60)

We now look for the decreasing and increasing operators in the form:

\displaystyle b=i\rho\!\left[\sqrt{2}\,e^{-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}a-\frac{H}{m_{0}c^{2}}+\omega_{0}(\mu_{k}+\nu_{k})\right]+\frac{H}{2m_{0}c^{2}}+\omega_{0}\mu_{k}\nu_{k},

(61)

\displaystyle b^{+}=-i\!\left[\sqrt{2}\,a^{+}e^{\frac{i}{2}\partial_{\rho}}-\frac{H}{m_{0}c^{2}}+\omega_{0}(\mu_{k}+\nu_{k})\right]\rho+\frac{H}{2m_{0}c^{2}}+\omega_{0}\mu_{k}\nu_{k}.

(62)

It can now be verified by direct verification that

[H,b]=-2\hbar\omega\,b,\quad[H,b^{+}]=2\hbar\omega\,b^{+}.

(63)

Their commutator is equal to

IX
Conclusion

The application of the finite-difference relativistic quantum mechanics to a large class of physical problems requires relativistic generalizations of the exactly solvable problems of non-relativistic quantum mechanics. In this paper, we found the exact solution of the relativistic finite-difference wave equation with the ring-shaped Quesne oscillator potential. We solved the radial part and the angular part of this equation by functional method. We have shown that the radial wave function is expressed in terms of the continuous dual Hahn polynomials, and the angular wave function is expressed in terms of the Jacobi polynomials. We also have shown that the radial wave functions and the energy spectra of the model under consideration have the correct nonrelativistic limit.

As in the nonrelativistic case
ref15
, the simplicity of the obtained energy spectrum suggests that a solution by group-theoretical methods should also be possible. We have shown that the radial wave equation has
SU(1,1)
dynamical symmetry group and the angular equation has two integrals of motion
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
. The relativistic finite-difference formulation of the ring-shaped Quesne oscillator developed in this work provides an exactly solvable framework for investigating relativistic quantum systems with non-central interactions. Owing to its analytical tractability and intrinsic relativistic structure, the model may serve as a useful platform for exploring relativistic bound states, hidden symmetries, and related classes of anisotropic quantum systems.
