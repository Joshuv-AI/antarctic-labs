Osteometric dataset of rodent skeletal collections from Armenia (1940–1981)
Authors: Nina H Manaseryan, Luba V Balyan, Mamikon G Ghasabyan, Maria A Kumelova, Andranik A Gyonjyan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis data article presents a comprehensive osteometric dataset of Rodentia specimens curated at the Institute of Zoology, Armenia. The dataset comprises detailed cranial and postcranial measurements from 211 individuals, representing 27 species across 5 families. Collected over a 41-year period (1940–1981) throughout Armenia, the material includes standardized raw measurements of the skull, scapula, humerus, ulna, pelvis, femur, and tibia, following established zoological protocols. As the largest rodent osteological collection in the region, this dataset serves as a vital open-access comparative resource for ecological and archaeozoological studies, particularly for the taxonomic identification of fragmented Holocene faunal remains.Keywords: Morphometrics, Rodentia, Bones, Osteological collection, Measurements, Zooarchaeology, Holocene, Craniometry

Background

The compilation of this dataset was motivated by the need to establish a comprehensive, digitized morphometric baseline for the rodent fauna of Armenia. Rodents are highly sensitive indicators of environmental changes and constitute a significant portion of faunal assemblages in both modern ecosystems and Holocene archaeological sites across the South Caucasus. Historically, extensive osteological material was collected during scientific expeditions between 1940 and 1981, yet these physical specimens remained stored in traditional scientific archives without centralized digital access. By systematically measuring and cataloging the cranial and postcranial elements of 211 individuals across 27 species, this dataset provides the primary raw data necessary for high-resolution comparative zoological and archaeozoological identification. Digitizing these metrics ensures the long-term preservation of historical collection data and provides an open-access reference framework for analyzing morphological variation and regional biodiversity trends across a 41-year temporal transect (Figure 1 and Table 1, Table 2, Table 3).

Data Description

The dataset, hosted in the Mendeley Data repository, comprises two comprehensive spreadsheets in Excel format and one high-resolution geographic map. The data encompass osteometric measurements of 211 rodent specimens from the historical collections of the Laboratory of Vertebrate Zoology at the Institute of Zoology, Scientific Center of Zoology and Hydroecology, National Academy of Sciences of Armenia. Due to the historical nature of the collection, sampling localities are recorded based on the nearest contemporary settlements. Taxonomic classification follows the Handbook of the Mammals of the World [1] and regional guides.

The repository consists of the following open-access files:

•Cranial_Measurements_Rodents_Armenia.xlsx: A spreadsheet containing raw craniometric data across 7 standard cranial variables for 211 individuals. It includes full taxonomic metadata (Family, Genus, Species) and geographic origin for each specimen.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The dataset comprises osteometric data from 211 rodent specimens, including cranial measurements of skulls and postcranial measurements (scapula, humerus, ulna, pelvis, femur, and tibia). The material represents 27 species from 5 families within the order Rodentia. These specimens were collected during scientific field expeditions over a 41-year period (1940–1981) across the territory of Armenia. The collection serves as a baseline for zoological and archaeological comparative research in the Caucasus region and is curated at the Institute of Zoology of the Scientific Center of Zoology and Hydroecology (NAS RA).

To ensure data consistency, only well-preserved, non-fragmented bones were selected for measurement. Each specimen underwent standardized laboratory processing, including cleaning, drying, and assignment of a unique catalog number. Fractured or significantly weathered elements were excluded from the analysis to maintain the integrity of the osteometric data.

Osteometric data were obtained using a Vernier caliper with a precision of 0.1 mm. Cranial measurements were conducted following the international mammalian craniometric nomenclature proposed by Thomas [2] and the standardized diagnostic protocols for Soviet and Caucasian rodent fauna established by Ognev [3] and Vinogradov and Gromov [4]. For postcranial skeletal elements, standard osteometric landmarks and definitions followed the general guidelines of Von den Driesch [5], adapted for the specific anatomical features of small mammals according to Gromov and Erbaeva [6]. For each individual, at least three different skeletal elements were measured to provide a robust morphological profile. The dataset reports only absolute linear measurements (mm).

Limitations

The primary limitation of this dataset relates to the variable preservation quality inherent to historical museum collections. Due to the exclusion of fractured, fragmented, or heavily weathered skeletal elements to maintain measurement integrity, some specimens have incomplete sets of postcranial metrics. Consequently, the final dataset contains unavoidable missing values for certain variables across specific individuals.

Ethics Statement

The authors confirm that this work complies with the ethical requirements for publication in Data in Brief. As this study is based exclusively on the morphometric analysis of historical, long-preserved museum osteological collections, it did not involve any experiments on living animals, field capturing, or new tissue sampling. Therefore, ethical approval from an institutional animal care and use committee was not required.
