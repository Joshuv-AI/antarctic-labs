Carbery’s inequality in the Schatten–von Neumann classes
Ziang Chen ∗, Paata Ivanisvili †, José Madrid †, Haozhu Wang ∗

Abstract
Carbery posed a question of sharpened triangle inequalities for families of operators in the Schatten–von Neumann classes S p S_{p} , p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . He established a weaker form of the desired estimate for even integer values of p p . In the commutative setting the corresponding sharp inequality (with optimal exponent p ′ = p p − 1 p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1} ) was recently obtained for all integer p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 . In the present work we resolve Carbery’s question completely in the non-commutative setting: we prove the sharp inequality ‖ ∑ j T j ‖ S p ≤ ‖ ( α i ​ j p ′ ) ‖ ℓ 2 → ℓ 2 1 / p ′ ​ ( ∑ j ‖ T j ‖ S p p ) 1 / p \Big\|\sum_{j}T_{j}\Big\|_{S_{p}}\leq\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|^{1/p^{\prime}}_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\Big(\sum_{j}\|T_{j}\|^{p}_{S_{p}}\Big)^{1/p} for all p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 and all countable sequences of operators ( T j ) ⊂ S p (T_{j})\subset S_{p} , where α i ​ j \alpha_{ij} are almost orthogonality coefficients. The proof is based on a block-operator reduction and a complex interpolation of the polar parts of the blocks.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable complex Hilbert space and let
S_{p}=S_{p}(\mathcal{H})
denote the Schatten–von Neumann class of compact operators on
\mathcal{H}
equipped with the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{S_{p}}
,
p\geq 2
.
Recall that for
T\in S_{p}
we have
\|T\|_{S_{p}}=\Big(\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}
where
(\lambda_{j})
are singular values of
T
, i.e., the eigenvalues of
|T|:=\sqrt{T^{*}T}
. For
p=\infty
we identify the space
S_{\infty}
with all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
with the usual operator norm. We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for basic properties of
S_{p}
classes.

Carbery
[
2
]
initiated the study of almost-orthogonality phenomena in these classes. Given a finite family
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}\in S_{p}
, he introduced the symmetric matrix of orthogonality coefficients
(\alpha_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}
with entries
\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]
satisfying

\displaystyle\|T_{i}T^{*}_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|T_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|T^{*}_{i}T_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|T_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}

(1)

and asked for sharpened forms of the triangle inequality measuring the almost-orthogonality through the coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
. In particular, in
[
2
, Section 3.3, (3.1)]
, he asked whether the inequality

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{2}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}

(2)

holds true for all
p\geq 2
, where
\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{q}
denotes the operator norm from
\ell_{q}^{N}
to
\ell_{q}^{N}
. He proved
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
a weaker estimate with the Schur norm
\|(\alpha_{ij})\|_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}
instead of
\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{2}^{1/p^{\prime}}
, but only when
p
is an even integer. Carbery also asked a related question
[
2
, Section 3.5]
whether the inequality

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\|(\alpha_{ij}^{2})\|_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}

(3)

holds for all
N\geq 2
in the commutative case.

In the commutative
L^{p}
setting, the first sharp results concerned the two-function case. For
N=2
, Carbery
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
established the analogue of (
3
) for characteristic functions of sets. Carlen–Frank–Ivanisvili–Lieb
[
3
]
later proved a substantially stronger two-function inequality for arbitrary functions in
L^{p}
; in particular, their result implies Carbery’s proposed estimate (
3
) when
N=2
. This two-function result was subsequently refined by Ivanisvili–Mooney
[
7
]
.

For arbitrary finite families, the problem is more delicate. A recent work
[
5
]
constructs a counterexample showing that no estimate of the form (
3
) can hold, in general, with any power on the coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
strictly larger than
p^{\prime}
. The same work proves the critical exponent
p^{\prime}
for every integer
p\geq 2
in the commutative
L^{p}
setting. Its proof expands the integer power and combines the resulting multilinear expression with a Schur-type test. Similar inequalities for many functions have been studied also in
[
4
]
.

In this paper we affirmatively answer the question posed by Carbery in
[
2
, Section 3.3, (3.1)]
in full generality for the Schatten–von Neumann classes. We prove the following sharp statement, valid for
all
real
p\geq 2
and for
arbitrary
(not necessarily positive or self-adjoint) operators in
S_{p}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
p\geq 2
, and let
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}\in S_{p}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
(\alpha_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
be a symmetric matrix with entries
\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]
satisfying (
1
). Then

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\bigr\|_{2}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

Remark 1.2
.

Since

\left\lVert(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\left\lVert(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{1}\left\lVert(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{\infty}}=\|(\alpha^{p^{\prime}}_{ij})\|_{1}=\sup_{i}\sum_{j}\alpha^{p^{\prime}}_{ij}\leq\|(\alpha_{ij})\|_{1},

Theorem
1.1
improves Carbery’s even-integer Schur-
(\alpha_{ij})
estimate, answers the
\ell_{2}
-operator-norm question (
2
), and it extends
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
to Schatten classes and all
p\geq 2
.

Remark 1.3
.

The exponent
p^{\prime}
in the constant
\left\lVert(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\right\rVert_{2}
is optimal, i.e., the counter-example from
[
5
]

shows that no larger exponent than
p^{\prime}
is admissible on the coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
.

Remark 1.4
.

When
p=\infty
the result should be understood as follows

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{2}\sup_{j}\|T_{j}\|_{\infty},

where symmetric
\alpha_{ij}\in[0,1]
satisfy
\|T_{i}T_{j}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|T_{i}\|_{\infty}\|T_{j}\|_{\infty}
and
\|T_{i}^{*}T_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|T_{i}\|_{\infty}\|T_{j}\|_{\infty}
. Here
\|T\|_{\infty}
denotes the operator norm of a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
. The proof of this endpoint case is given in Section
2.4.2
.

Remark 1.5
.

Passage from a finite to a countable family
(T_{j})
is given in Section
2.4.1
.

The proof proceeds in two steps. First we establish the result for finite positive operators by means of complex interpolation between the Schatten classes
S_{2}
and
S_{\infty}
. The analytic family is constructed so that the scalar factors
\alpha_{ij}
compensate for the growth of the blocks
X_{ij}
(see Section
2
) on the left boundary while keeping the right boundary under control by the operator norm of the matrix
(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})
. The case
p=2
is handled directly by the Hilbert–Schmidt inner-product argument. The passage from positive to arbitrary operators is done by the standard
2\times 2
positive dilation: each
T_{j}
is replaced by the positive operator

P_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}|T_{j}^{*}|&T_{j}\\
T_{j}^{*}&|T_{j}|\end{pmatrix}

on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
. A simple lemma on off-diagonal blocks of positive
2\times 2
matrices then reduces the original estimate to the positive case already proved, while a direct computation verifies that the same coefficients
\alpha_{ij}
remain admissible for the dilated family.

The interpolation technique employed here is in the spirit of the abstract complex interpolation theory for operator-valued kernels developed by Fournier and Russo
[
6
]
, although we work directly with the Schatten classes on a fixed Hilbert space rather than with integral operators. The necessary interpolation result for Schatten classes—namely
[S_{2},S_{\infty}]_{\theta}=S_{p}
with
\theta=1-2/p
—is classical and may be found, for example, in Simon
[
9
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the complete proof of Theorem
1.1
. We first treat the finite positive case (Subsection 2.1 for
p=2
and Subsection 2.2 for
p>2
), and then reduce the general case to the positive case via the
2\times 2
dilation (Subsection 2.3).

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first prove the estimate for positive finite-rank operators. The extension
from finite-rank positive operators to arbitrary positive operators in
S_{p}

is obtained by approximation at the end of Subsection
2.2
.
The general case is then reduced to the positive
case in Subsection
2.3
.

For a positive family
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}
, define

R:\mathcal{H}^{N}\to\mathcal{H},\qquad R(h_{1},\dots,h_{N})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}^{1/2}h_{j},

and set
X=R^{*}R
. Then the
(i,j)
-block of
X
is

X_{ij}=T_{i}^{1/2}T_{j}^{1/2},

while

RR^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}.

Therefore
X
and
\sum_{j}T_{j}
have the same non-zero singular values, and hence

\|X\|_{S_{q}(\mathcal{H}^{N})}=\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{q}(\mathcal{H})}\qquad(1\leq q\leq\infty).

2.1
Positive case:
p=2

Let
a_{j}=\|T_{j}\|_{S_{2}}
, and let
X=R^{*}R
be the block operator defined above.
We have
\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}\leq\|T_{i}T_{j}\|_{S_{1}}
. Indeed,

\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\tr(T_{j}^{1/2}T_{i}T_{j}^{1/2})=\tr(T_{i}T_{j})\leq\|T_{i}T_{j}\|_{S_{1}}.

Therefore

\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{2}}\leq\alpha_{ij}\,a_{i}^{1/2}a_{j}^{1/2}.

The Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the block operator therefore satisfies

\|X\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}^{2}a_{i}a_{j}.

Let
\Delta=(\alpha_{ij}^{2})
. The right-hand side is the quadratic form
\langle\Delta a,a\rangle
evaluated at the vector
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})
. Hence it is at most
K\|a\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}=K\sum a_{j}^{2}
, where
K=\|(\alpha_{ij}^{2})\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}
. It follows that

\|X\|_{S_{2}}\leq K^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{j}a_{j}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},

which is the asserted bound for
p=2
. The same argument is valid for
arbitrary positive operators in
S_{2}
.

2.2
Positive case:
p>2
(complex interpolation)

Let
a_{j}=\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}
, and let
X=R^{*}R
be the block operator defined above.
Let
q=p/2
. Since

X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij}=T_{j}^{1/2}T_{i}T_{j}^{1/2},

the non-zero eigenvalues of
X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij}
agree, with algebraic
multiplicity, with those of
T_{j}^{1/2}T_{j}^{1/2}T_{i}=T_{j}T_{i}
, and hence with
those of
T_{i}T_{j}
. Since
X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij}
is positive, its
S_{q}
-norm is the

\ell_{q}
-norm of this eigenvalue list. Weyl’s majorant theorem, applied to the
possibly non-normal operator
T_{i}T_{j}
, gives

\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij})^{q}\leq\sum_{k}s_{k}(T_{i}T_{j})^{q},

where
\lambda_{k}(X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij})
are the non-zero eigenvalues of

X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij}
and
s_{k}(T_{i}T_{j})
are the singular values of
T_{i}T_{j}
.
Therefore

\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{p}}^{2}=\|X_{ij}^{*}X_{ij}\|_{S_{q}}\leq\|T_{i}T_{j}\|_{S_{q}}.

Thus, by the hypothesis,

\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{p}}^{2}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}a_{i}a_{j}

and therefore
\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{p}}\leq\alpha_{ij}\,a_{i}^{1/2}a_{j}^{1/2}
. Write
X_{ij}=U_{ij}|X_{ij}|
for the polar decomposition. Define the analytic family of block operators on the strip
0\leq\operatorname{Re}z\leq 1
by declaring that if
\alpha_{ij}>0
,

F(z)_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}^{\phi(z)}\,U_{ij}\,|X_{ij}|^{\psi(z)},

where

\psi(z)=\frac{p}{2}(1-z),\qquad\phi(z)=\Bigl(\frac{p^{\prime}}{2}-\frac{p}{2}\Bigr)\frac{\theta-z}{\theta}

with interpolation point
\theta=1-2/p\in(0,1)
. If
\alpha_{ij}=0
, then the preceding estimate gives

\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{p}}=0
, hence
X_{ij}=0
; in this case set

F(z)_{ij}=0
for all
z
. Since we are first working with finite-rank positive operators, this family is
defined by finite-dimensional spectral calculus on
\operatorname{supp}|X_{ij}|
,
and it is set equal to
0
on
\ker|X_{ij}|
. Thus
z\mapsto F(z)
is analytic
in the open strip and continuous on the closed strip as a function with values in

S_{2}+S_{\infty}
. By construction
\psi(\theta)=1
and
\phi(\theta)=0
, so
F(\theta)=X
.

By the standard complex interpolation identity for Schatten ideals,

[S_{2}(\mathcal{H}^{N}),S_{\infty}(\mathcal{H}^{N})]_{\theta}=S_{p}(\mathcal{H}^{N}),\qquad\theta=1-\frac{2}{p},

see, for example,
[
9
]
, we obtain

\|X\|_{S_{p}}\leq\Bigl(\sup_{t}\|F(it)\|_{S_{2}}\Bigr)^{2/p}\Bigl(\sup_{t}\|F(1+it)\|_{\infty}\Bigr)^{(p-2)/p}.

On the left boundary, for blocks with
\alpha_{ij}>0
the real part of the exponent on
|X_{ij}|
is
p/2
while the imaginary part contributes only a unitary factor. The real part of the exponent on
\alpha_{ij}
is
p^{\prime}/2-p/2
. Hence

\|F(it)_{ij}\|_{S_{2}}=\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}/2-p/2}\|X_{ij}\|_{S_{p}}^{p/2}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}/2}a_{i}^{p/4}a_{j}^{p/4}.

Summing the squared block norms gives

\|F(it)\|_{S_{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}}a_{i}^{p/2}a_{j}^{p/2}\leq K\sum_{k}a_{k}^{p},

where
K=\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}
. Thus
\|F(it)\|_{S_{2}}\leq(K\sum a_{k}^{p})^{1/2}
, and raising to the power
2/p
produces the factor
K^{1/p}(\sum a_{j}^{p})^{1/p}
.

On the right boundary
z=1+it
we have

\operatorname{Re}\psi(1+it)=0,\qquad\operatorname{Re}\phi(1+it)=p^{\prime}.

Hence each nonzero block can be written as

F(1+it)_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}}C_{ij}(t),\qquad\|C_{ij}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq 1,

and the blocks with
\alpha_{ij}=0
are zero. Therefore, for

h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{N})\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
,

\|(F(1+it)h)_{k}\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{kj}^{p^{\prime}}\|h_{j}\|.

Taking the
\ell_{2}
-norm in
k
gives

\|F(1+it)h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N}}\leq\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}\to\ell_{2}^{N}}\bigl\|(\|h_{j}\|)_{j=1}^{N}\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}^{N}}=K\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N}}.

Thus

\|F(1+it)\|_{\infty}\leq K.

Raising to the power
(p-2)/p
contributes the factor
K^{(p-2)/p}
.

Combining the boundary estimates yields

\|X\|_{S_{p}}\leq K^{1/p+(p-2)/p}\Bigl(\sum_{j}a_{j}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}=K^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}a_{j}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

This proves the desired estimate for positive finite-rank operators.

It remains to remove the finite-rank assumption. Let
T_{j}\geq 0
be arbitrary
operators in
S_{p}
. After discarding the zero terms, choose positive finite-rank
operators
T_{j}^{(n)}
such that
T_{j}^{(n)}\to T_{j}
in
S_{p}
. By Hölder’s
inequality,

\|T_{i}^{(n)}T_{j}^{(n)}-T_{i}T_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq\|T_{i}^{(n)}-T_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|T_{j}^{(n)}\|_{S_{p}}+\|T_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|T_{j}^{(n)}-T_{j}\|_{S_{p}},

and hence
T_{i}^{(n)}T_{j}^{(n)}\to T_{i}T_{j}
in
S_{p/2}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set

\beta_{ij}^{(\varepsilon)}=\min\{1,\alpha_{ij}+\varepsilon\}.

For all sufficiently large
n
, the finite-rank family
(T_{j}^{(n)})
satisfies
the positive-case hypotheses with coefficients
\beta_{ij}^{(\varepsilon)}
.
Applying the finite-rank estimate and then letting
n\to\infty
gives

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\|((\beta_{ij}^{(\varepsilon)})^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

Finally let
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Since
N<\infty
, the matrix norm is
continuous, and the desired positive-operator inequality follows.

2.3
General case (arbitrary operators)

Let
T_{j}=U_{j}|T_{j}|
be the polar decomposition and define the positive operators on the doubled space
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
by

\displaystyle P_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}|T_{j}^{*}|&T_{j}\\
T_{j}^{*}&|T_{j}|\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Let
s_{j}
be the support projection of
|T_{j}|
. Since
U_{j}^{*}U_{j}=s_{j}
, the map

W_{j}:s_{j}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H},\qquad W_{j}h=2^{-1/2}(U_{j}h,h),

is an isometry. Moreover,

P_{j}=2W_{j}|T_{j}|W_{j}^{*}.

Thus
P_{j}\geq 0
, and the nonzero eigenvalues of
P_{j}
are exactly twice the
singular values of
T_{j}
. Consequently

\|P_{j}\|_{S_{p}}=2\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}=2a_{j}.

Moreover,

\sum_{j}P_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j}|T_{j}^{*}|&\sum_{j}T_{j}\\
\sum_{j}T_{j}^{*}&\sum_{j}|T_{j}|\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{*}&C\end{pmatrix}\geq 0

belong to
S_{p}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})
, where
p\geq 1
. Then

2\|B\|_{S_{p}}\leq\|M\|_{S_{p}}.

Proof.

Let
s_{k}(B)
be the singular values of
B
, with singular-vector pairs

(e_{k},f_{k})
chosen so that
Bf_{k}=s_{k}(B)e_{k}
and
B^{*}e_{k}=s_{k}(B)f_{k}
. Set

w_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{k},f_{k}),\qquad v_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{k},-f_{k}).

Since
M\geq 0
,
\langle Mv_{k},v_{k}\rangle\geq 0
, and

\langle Mw_{k},w_{k}\rangle-\langle Mv_{k},v_{k}\rangle=2s_{k}(B).

Hence
\langle Mw_{k},w_{k}\rangle\geq 2s_{k}(B)
. By Ky Fan’s maximum principle, see e.g.
[
1
, Chapter III]
,
for every
m
,

2\sum_{k=1}^{m}s_{k}(B)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\langle Mw_{k},w_{k}\rangle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda_{k}(M).

Thus
2s(B)\prec_{w}\lambda(M)
. Taking
\ell_{p}
-norms gives the result.
∎

Applying Lemma
2.1
to
M=\sum_{j}P_{j}
and to its off-diagonal
block
\sum_{j}T_{j}
gives

2\Bigl\|\sum_{j}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{j}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}.

It remains to verify that the same coefficients
(\alpha_{ij})
are admissible
for the positive family
(P_{j})
. Since
P_{i}=P_{i}^{*}
and
P_{j}=P_{j}^{*}
, the two
conditions in (
1
) coincide for this family; hence it is enough to
prove

\|P_{i}P_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|P_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|P_{j}\|_{S_{p}}.

Set

V_{j}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H},\qquad V_{j}h=(U_{j}h,h).

Then
\|V_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}
,
V_{i}^{*}V_{j}=U_{i}^{*}U_{j}+I
, and

P_{j}=V_{j}|T_{j}|V_{j}^{*}.

Indeed,
|T_{j}|=s_{j}|T_{j}|
and
U_{j}^{*}U_{j}=s_{j}
. Hence

P_{i}P_{j}=V_{i}|T_{i}|V_{i}^{*}V_{j}|T_{j}|V_{j}^{*}=V_{i}|T_{i}|(U_{i}^{*}U_{j}+I)|T_{j}|V_{j}^{*}.

By the ideal property of Schatten norms (i.e.,
\|AXB\|_{S_{r}}\leq\|A\|_{\infty}\,\|X\|_{S_{r}}\,\|B\|_{\infty}
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
whenever
X\in S_{r}
and
A,B
are bounded operators), and
\|V_{i}\|_{\infty},\|V_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}
,

\|P_{i}P_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq 2\bigl\||T_{i}|(U_{i}^{*}U_{j}+I)|T_{j}|\bigr\|_{S_{p/2}}.

The triangle inequality splits the right-hand side. The first summand is

|T_{i}|U_{i}^{*}U_{j}|T_{j}|=T_{i}^{*}T_{j},

and therefore

\bigl\||T_{i}|U_{i}^{*}U_{j}|T_{j}|\bigr\|_{S_{p/2}}=\|T_{i}^{*}T_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}.

For the second summand, put
A=|T_{i}||T_{j}|
. Then

A=s_{i}As_{j}
, where
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are the support projections of
|T_{i}|

and
|T_{j}|
. Let
t_{j}=U_{j}U_{j}^{*}
be the support projection of
|T_{j}^{*}|
. Since

U_{i}:s_{i}\mathcal{H}\to U_{i}U_{i}^{*}\mathcal{H}
and

U_{j}:s_{j}\mathcal{H}\to t_{j}\mathcal{H}
are unitary maps between their support
spaces, the operators
A
and
U_{i}AU_{j}^{*}
have the same non-zero singular
values. Equivalently,

(U_{i}AU_{j}^{*})^{*}(U_{i}AU_{j}^{*})=U_{j}A^{*}U_{i}^{*}U_{i}AU_{j}^{*}=U_{j}A^{*}AU_{j}^{*},

and
U_{j}A^{*}AU_{j}^{*}
is unitarily equivalent to
A^{*}A
on the support
s_{j}\mathcal{H}
. Since
U_{i}AU_{j}^{*}=T_{i}T_{j}^{*}
, it follows that

\bigl\||T_{i}||T_{j}|\bigr\|_{S_{p/2}}=\|T_{i}T_{j}^{*}\|_{S_{p/2}}.

By the given hypothesis both quantities are at most
\alpha_{ij}^{2}a_{i}a_{j}
, so

\|P_{i}P_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq 4\alpha_{ij}^{2}a_{i}a_{j}.

On the other hand
\|P_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|P_{j}\|_{S_{p}}=4a_{i}a_{j}
, and therefore

\|P_{i}P_{j}\|_{S_{p/2}}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}\|P_{i}\|_{S_{p}}\|P_{j}\|_{S_{p}}.

The positive-operator inequality already established (applied to the family
(P_{j})
) yields

\Bigl\|\sum_{j}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq K^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}\|P_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}=2K^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}a_{j}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p},

where
K=\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}
. Combining with the off-diagonal estimate and cancelling the factor of 2 produces

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq K^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

This is the asserted inequality for arbitrary operators. The zero cases are covered by the conventions and approximation argument above.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.4
Concluding remarks

2.4.1
Countable family

Theorem
1.1
extends in the same form to countable families. More
precisely, let
2\leq p<\infty
, let
(T_{j})_{j\geq 1}\subset S_{p}(\mathcal{H})

satisfy

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}<\infty,

and let
(\alpha_{ij})_{i,j\geq 1}
be a symmetric matrix with entries in

[0,1]
satisfying (
1
) for every pair
i,j
. Assume moreover that

\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})_{i,j\geq 1}\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})}<\infty.

Then the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}T_{j}
converges in
S_{p}
, and

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})_{i,j\geq 1}\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

Indeed, applying Theorem
1.1
to the finite family

T_{m},\dots,T_{n}
, and observing that the corresponding finite
(n-m+1)\times(n-m+1)
coefficient
matrix is a compression of
(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})_{i,j\geq 1}
, we get

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=m}^{n}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{S_{p}}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})_{i,j\geq 1}\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})\to\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j=m}^{n}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}.

The right-hand side tends to
0
as
m,n\to\infty
, hence the partial sums
are Cauchy in
S_{p}
(recall that
S_{p}
is complete). Letting
n\to\infty
in the finite estimate for

\sum_{j=1}^{n}T_{j}
gives the asserted countable-family inequality.

2.4.2
Endpoint case
p=\infty

We establish the asserted inequality in the endpoint case
p=\infty
. Let
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}\in B(\mathcal{H})
satisfy the almost-orthogonality conditions (
1
) with coefficients
(\alpha_{ij})
and with
a_{j}=\|T_{j}\|_{\infty}
. (As usual we interpret
S_{\infty}
as the space of all bounded operators equipped with the operator norm.) We first treat the positive case and then reduce the general case to it by means of the
2\times 2
dilation already employed in Subsection
2.3
.

Assume first that each
T_{j}\geq 0
. Define the block operator
X=R^{*}R
on
\mathcal{H}^{N}
precisely as at the beginning of Section
2
; since
X=R^{*}R
and
RR^{*}=\sum_{j}T_{j}
, we have

\|X\|_{\infty}=\|R^{*}R\|_{\infty}=\|R\|_{\infty}^{2}=\|RR^{*}\|_{\infty}=\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}.

Moreover,

\|X_{ij}\|_{\infty}^{2}=\|T_{j}^{1/2}T_{i}T_{j}^{1/2}\|_{\infty}=r(T_{j}^{1/2}T_{i}T_{j}^{1/2})=r(T_{i}T_{j})\leq\|T_{i}T_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{ij}^{2}a_{i}a_{j},

where
r(A)
denotes the spectral radius of a bounded operator
A
.
Consequently, for an arbitrary vector
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{N})\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
,

\|(Xh)_{i}\|\leq\Bigl(\max_{k}a_{k}\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{ij}\|h_{j}\|.

Taking the
\ell_{2}
-norm over the components
i
and invoking the definition of the operator norm of the matrix
(\alpha_{ij})
on
\ell_{2}^{N}
gives

\|Xh\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N}}\leq\Bigl(\max_{k}a_{k}\Bigr)\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{N}}.

Hence

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\cdot\max_{j}a_{j},

which is the desired endpoint estimate for positive operators.

For arbitrary (not necessarily positive or self-adjoint) operators we apply the same
2\times 2
positive dilation
P_{j}

on
\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}
that was used in Subsection
2.3
(see (
4
)). The identical algebraic computation performed there shows that the family
(P_{j})
satisfies the almost-orthogonality conditions with the
same
coefficients
(\alpha_{ij})
(the factor
4
obtained from the ideal property, the bounds

\|V_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}
, and the two resulting summands cancel exactly
against the factor
4
coming from
\|P_{i}\|_{\infty}\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}=4a_{i}a_{j}
). The positive case already established therefore yields

\Bigl\|\sum_{j}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\cdot\max_{j}\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}=2\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\cdot\max_{j}a_{j}.

Finally, the off-diagonal block estimate also holds in operator norm. Indeed,
if

M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{*}&C\end{pmatrix}\geq 0,

then
2\|B\|_{\infty}\leq\|M\|_{\infty}
. To see this, let
x,y\in\mathcal{H}

be unit vectors and choose a phase
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\omega\langle By,x\rangle=|\langle By,x\rangle|
. Put

w=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x,\omega y),\qquad v=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x,-\omega y).

Then
w
and
v
are unit vectors, and

\langle Mw,w\rangle-\langle Mv,v\rangle=2|\langle By,x\rangle|.

Since
M\geq 0
, we have
\langle Mv,v\rangle\geq 0
, hence

2|\langle By,x\rangle|\leq\langle Mw,w\rangle\leq\|M\|_{\infty}.

Taking the supremum over unit
x,y
gives

2\|B\|_{\infty}\leq\|M\|_{\infty}.

Applying this comparison to the off-diagonal block of
\sum_{j}P_{j}
therefore produces

\Bigl\|\sum_{j}T_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl\|\sum_{j}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\leq\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\cdot\max_{j}a_{j},

as claimed. (Since
N<\infty
no approximation argument is required.)

In other words, the inequality of Theorem
1.1
continues to hold for
p=\infty
upon interpreting the right-hand side in the natural limiting sense

\bigl\|(\alpha_{ij}^{p^{\prime}})\bigr\|_{2}^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigl(\sum_{j}\|T_{j}\|_{S_{p}}^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}\ \longrightarrow\ \bigl\|(\alpha_{ij})\bigr\|_{\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}}\cdot\max_{j}\|T_{j}\|_{\infty}.
