First Light And Reionization Epoch Simulations (FLARES) XXI: The UV Indices of Galaxies in the Early Universe
Connor Sant Fournier 1, ⋆ \star, Stephen M. Wilkins 1,3,†, Joseph Caruana 2,1, Kristian Zarb Adami 2,1, Jack C. Turner 3, Conor M. Byrne 4, Aswin P. Vijayan 3, and William J. Roper 3

Abstract
UV absorption line indices trace both chemical enrichment and star formation histories of high-redshift galaxies. Yet, the reliability of UV absorption line indices as quantitative tracers of stellar metallicity ( Z ⋆ Z_{\star} ) remains uncertain. In this study, we combine synthetic spectral modelling and cosmological simulations to establish a theoretical foundation for interpreting these features in the early Universe. Using the forward modelling package Synthesizer , we compute equivalent widths for a suite of UV indices based on BPASS stellar population synthesis models and assess their sensitivity to intrinsic parameters, including metallicity, star formation history (SFH), and model assumptions. Certain indices, such as 1719 Å , are found to exhibit strong and consistent correlations with stellar metallicity, while others show varying levels of dependence on SFH. To evaluate the role of complex galaxy formation histories, we apply these models to galaxies drawn from the First Light and Reionization Epoch Simulations ( Flares ). The simulations provide a diverse population of galaxies with realistic distributions of stellar metallicity and SFH, enabling a detailed examination of how UV indices evolve under different enrichment scenarios. We explore the correlation of physical properties in Flares with the metallicity distribution and reproduce a synthetic mass–metallicity relation (MZR). We then turn to predictions of equivalent widths for the UV indices using Flares . Across the majority of indices, equivalent widths are found to increase monotonically with metallicity, consistent with theoretical expectations from simple stellar population models. The 1719 Å index emerges as one of the most stable and reliable tracers of stellar metallicity, while the 1460 Å feature exhibits strong sensitivity to nebular emission and bursty star formation. Although increased scatter is introduced in the cosmological simulations due to composite stellar populations and stochastic enrichment histories, the overall metallicity trends remain preserved. These predictions serve as a theoretical benchmark for interpreting rest-frame UV spectra from upcoming and ongoing surveys of high-redshift galaxies, including those from the JWST . By linking absorption features to stellar chemical properties, this work supports the development of UV spectral tracers for probing metallicity in the early Universe.

Introduction

A key goal of extragalactic astrophysics is the charting of the build-up of elements over the Universe’s history. Such measurements provide important constraints on both stellar evolution and galaxy formation models.

Since its deployment, the James Webb Space Telescope

Lovell et al. 2023

The high-resolution capabilities of

These observations have shown that some early galaxies have mature chemical enrichment and well-defined overall morphological structure at a much earlier point in history than expected

The chemical composition of a galaxy is inherently non-uniform, reflecting a distribution across its components, including stars, cold (i.e. star-forming) gas, and hot gas phases. Each of these components traces distinct enrichment histories and is probed through different observational and modelling techniques. In this work, we focus on stellar metallicity.

Stellar metallicities, on the other hand, can be constrained from more subtle continuum features, including UV absorption features

2.2
Metallicity

In this section, we explore the behaviour of UV indices defined in Table
1
, as done in previous literature, through their equivalent widths as a function of metallicity
Z

Rix et al. 2004
;
Sommariva et al. 2012
;
Calabrò
et al. 2021
;
Byrne et al. 2022
. The method for calculating equivalent width (EW) is expressed as

EW_{i}=\int_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{2}}\left(1-\frac{f(\lambda)}{f_{\mathrm{cont}}(\lambda)}\right)\,d\lambda

(1)

With this definition, absorption features yield positive equivalent widths, while emission features produce negative values. The function
f(\lambda)
represents the spectral flux density across the absorption feature (
\text{erg}\,\text{s}^{-1}\text{cm}^{-2}\text{\AA}^{-1}
), while
f_{cont}(\lambda)
denotes the continuum flux density in the same units. The parameters
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
define the start and end bounds for the wavelength of the feature.

The equivalent width quantifies the strength of absorption lines relative to the UV continuum, requiring the estimation of
f_{cont}(\lambda)
. To obtain an accurate equivalent width, the continuum level must be free of spectral line contamination, requiring the use of pseudo-continuum windows as defined by past literature and recently refined by
Calabrò
et al. 2021
. These windows have served to identify spectral regions devoid of significant line absorption, enabling the interpolation of the continuum flux density. The blueward and redward pseudo-continuum windows used are outlined in table
2
and visualized in Figure
1
. Adjustments made by
Calabrò
et al. 2021
have increased the window width from
\approx
3–4
Å
to a broader width of
\approx
10
Å
.

Synthetic spectra generated with
Synthesizer
are employed for simple stellar populations based on a BPASS v2.3
(
Byrne et al. 2022
)
incident grid, where incident denotes the pure stellar emission prior to any contribution from nebular emission or radiative transfer effects. Within
Synthesizer
, spectral features arise directly from the underlying stellar population synthesis models, with absorption lines imprinted by stellar atmospheres and emission features incorporated through optional coupling to photoionisation models. In this work, only the pure stellar component is considered, isolating the absorption driven UV indices.

These grids are used to examine the relationship between equivalent width and metallicity across the defined UV indices assuming a continuous star formation history of 100 Myr. Figure
2
demonstrates a consistent monotonic trend for the majority of indices, with equivalent widths increasing systematically with metallicity. This behaviour is physically well motivated, as higher metallicities enhance line blanketing and opacity in stellar atmospheres, leading to stronger absorption features in the emergent UV spectra.

These results confirm that the UV indices implemented within
Synthesizer
retain strong sensitivity to stellar metallicity, providing a robust foundation for their application as metallicity diagnostics in subsequent analysis.

However, not all indices follow a monotonic trend with metallicity. Figure
2
shows that notable departures are evident for the 1400
Å
and, more prominently, the 1460
Å
indices. The 1400
Å
feature, typically associated with Si
iv
, exhibits a mild dip in equivalent width at
Z\approx 10^{-2}
. This change in behaviour may reflect the transition between dominant ionisation stages of silicon, effects of outflows, or the onset of line saturation effects within the stellar photospheres. Such behaviour is commonly seen in regions where temperature and ionisation balance shift rapidly with increasing metallicity.

The 1460
Å
index presents a more striking deviation, characterised by a pronounced drop in equivalent width. This feature, dominated by Ni
ii
absorption, likely traces the relationship between iron-peak element abundances and the ionisation structure of hot, metal-rich stellar atmospheres. As metallicity increases, the Ni
ii
absorption line may transition from a weak absorption range into partial emission, driven by ionisation or by non-local thermodynamic equilibrium effects that populate excited states of nickel in the outer stellar layers. Such behaviour suggests that the 1460
Å
feature is highly sensitive to both abundance patterns and radiative transfer effects.

These deviations indicate that while the general trend of increasing equivalent width reinforces the utility of UV indices as tracers of metallicity, individual lines can display non-linear responses arising from the detailed physical conditions within stellar populations. Understanding these behaviours is essential for interpreting the chemical evolution of high-redshift galaxies.

2.3
Star Formation History

To quantify how star-formation activity impacts the UV indices, we explore three parametrisations of a single spectral grid with
Synthesizer
. Each grid is constructed from BPASS single-metallicity stellar populations and integrated over a different star formation history of 10 Myr, 100 Myr, and 1 Gyr, following the approach of
Byrne et al. 2022
. These varying star formation episodes allow for a direct comparison of the equivalent widths predicted for different star formation timescales, exploring the impact of burstiness on the absorption lines of a stellar population.

Figure
3
shows that across most indices, a pattern emerges, with spectra corresponding to the youngest, 10 Myr populations generally exhibiting stronger absorption than those formed under extended (
\geq 100
Myr) star formation histories.
However, this response is not uniform across all features, revealing dependencies between ionisation state, metallicity, and the temporal distribution of stellar ages.

The 1370
Å
index in the top left panel shows 10 Myr bursts producing slightly weaker absorption than 100 Myr populations at metallicities below
Z\approx 10^{-2}
, but stronger features at higher metallicity. The 1 Gyr population displays the opposite trend, with absorption becoming comparatively stronger at low metallicity and weaker at high metallicity. The 1400
Å
in the top centre panel remains relatively stable across the three star formation histories, with 10 Myr slightly weaker but converging to the same strength near
Z=10^{-2}
, while the 1 Gyr population is nearly identical to the 100 Myr case. The 1425
Å
index shows a mild offset, with 10 Myr history yielding marginally higher equivalent widths and the 1 Gyr population slightly lower, suggesting weak sensitivity to stellar age distribution.

By contrast, several indices exhibit pronounced enhancements under short-duration star formation. The 1460
Å
feature responds most strongly with the 10 Myr model producing equivalent widths up to a factor of
\approx 2.5
higher than longer duration cases, reflecting the dominance of massive, hot stars and elevated ionising flux in bursty populations. Similarly, 1501
Å
and 1550
Å
show clear increases for 10 Myr bursts, consistent with enhanced excitation and metal-line opacity at young stellar ages. The 1 Gyr spectra, however, exhibit only mild suppression, remaining close to the 100 Myr baseline. The 1533
Å
feature remains nearly identical across all star formation histories up to
Z=10^{-3}
, following which the 10 Myr model strengthens and the 1 Gyr weakens, highlighting age-dependent differences.

The 1719
Å
blend shows a significant reduction in equivalent width for 10 Myr bursts, beginning at
Z\approx 10^{-4}
, while the 1 Gyr case remains nearly indistinguishable from the 100 Myr baseline. The 1853
Å
feature is almost invariant, with both 10 Myr and 1 Gyr populations exhibiting only marginally stronger absorption than the fiducial model. Notably, the 1400
Å
and 1719
Å
indices are the only features that exhibit a decrease in equivalent width at shorter star formation timescales, indicating a distinct sensitivity to the presence of young, massive stars and the associated ionisation conditions that act to weaken these absorption features relative to longer-duration star formation histories.

Taken together, these results reveal that the sensitivity of UV indices to star formation history is highly index dependent. Short-duration (10 Myr) bursts significantly amplify high-ionisation features such as 1460
Å
, 1501
Å
, and 1550
Å
, while leaving blends like 1400
Å
, 1425
Å
, and 1853
Å
largely unchanged. Consequently, combining indices from these distinct categories provides an effective means to disentangle the influence of metallicity and star formation timescale in the UV spectra of high-redshift galaxies.

2.4
Initial Mass Function

The Initial Mass Function (IMF) describes how the stellar mass is distributed throughout a stellar population
during formation, with star formation and subsequent feedback processes being inherently dependent on the mass distribution of stars. Consequently, IMF variations can therefore lead to measurable differences in the equivalent widths of UV indices.

Figure 4:
Equivalent width values of UV indices computed using two different initial mass functions. The blue line represents results from the Chabrier IMF, while the orange line corresponds to a broken power-law IMF.

In this study, two widely used IMF parametrisations are considered: the Chabrier IMF
(
Chabrier 2003
)
and a broken power-law (BPL) IMF
(
Kroupa 2001
)
. The Chabrier IMF is a commonly adopted empirical form, characterised by a log-normal distribution at low stellar masses and transitioning to a power-law decline at higher masses (typically above
\sim 1\,M_{\odot}
), extending up to an assumed upper stellar mass limit of
\sim 100
–
300\,M_{\odot}
depending on the model implementation
Chabrier 2003
. In contrast, the broken power-law IMF allows for different power-law slopes across distinct stellar mass intervals. For BPASS, the default BPL IMF follows the prescription of
Kroupa 2001
, with a power-law slope of
-1.30
over the mass range
0.1
–
0.5\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}
, steepening to
-2.35
at higher masses. For comparison, the Chabrier IMF adopts a log-normal form below
\sim 1\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}
and transitions to a high-mass power-law slope of
-2.3
. BPL extends to a maximum initial stellar mass of
300\,\mathrm{M}_{\odot}
. The broken power law IMF introduces greater flexibility, being frequently used to probe IMF variations in extreme environments, such as high-redshift galaxies or starburst regions, where deviations from the canonical IMF may occur.

The divergence between the Chabrier and BPL initial mass functions in Figure
4
is most pronounced at indices 1370
Å
and 1460
Å
, corresponding to spectral features dominated by Si
iii
and Ni
ii
, respectively. These deviations arise from the differing treatment of high-mass stars between the two IMF formulations. The BPL IMF, with its flatter slope at the high-mass end, yields a greater proportion of massive stars compared to standard IMFs. The primary differences between these IMFs arise at lower stellar masses, whereas deviations in the high-mass slope, as in the BPL IMF, directly impact the relative contribution of massive stars to the integrated spectrum. As a result, spectral regions sensitive to the presence of hot, massive stars – such as 1370
Å
, which traces Si
iii
absorption in stellar photospheres – exhibit enhanced features under a BPL IMF due to the increased contribution from luminous, young O and B type stars. Similarly, the 1460
Å
index, associated with Ni
ii
, reflects the cumulative chemical enrichment of this iron-peak element, with the greater number of core-collapse supernovae in a BPL-dominated stellar population leading to differing rates of Ni
ii
absorption that further distinguish the spectral imprint of the two IMFs.

More subtle differences are also evident at 1400
Å
and 1501
Å
. These spectral lines originate from intermediate- to high-ionisation states and are sensitive to both the ionising radiation field and the population of moderately massive stars. While less extreme than other indices, the Si
iv
and S
v
features exhibit measurable IMF dependence arising from differences in the high-mass stellar content.

A notable distinction is that the metallicity dependence of these indices differs between the two IMF prescriptions. The 1400
Å
index exhibits systematically higher equivalent widths under the BPL IMF, consistent with its enhanced population of massive stars. This leads to a stronger ionising radiation field and increased production of Si
iv
, thereby increasing the absorption feature. In contrast, the 1501
Å
index shows higher equivalent widths under the Chabrier IMF. This behaviour likely reflects the more balanced stellar population weighting, where the relative contribution from moderately massive stars becomes more significant. As a result, the S
v
feature is less dominated by extreme ionising conditions and instead traces a region in which metallicity-dependent opacity effects are more pronounced.

These opposing trends highlight that the response of UV indices to IMF variations is not uniform, but instead depends on the specific ionisation and stellar population regimes probed by each feature. Additionally, the Si
iv
line at 1400
Å
is influenced by stellar wind components, such that its variation may also reflect secondary dependencies on stellar age and metallicity, which interact with IMF-driven differences in the underlying stellar population.

Beyond 1550
Å
, the UV indices show minimal sensitivity to the choice of IMF, with both the Chabrier and BPL IMFs producing nearly indistinguishable spectral features. This convergence arises due to the spectral lines at these longer wavelengths being associated with lower ionization species and transitions originating from cooler or more evolved stellar populations, which are less dependent on the presence of the most massive stars. In contrast to the shorter wavelength indices, the indices beyond 1550
Å
are dominated by contributions from a broader range of stellar masses, including intermediate-mass stars, whose relative numbers are more consistent between different IMF prescriptions. As a result, these indices exhibit only marginal differences in intensity or profile shape across the two IMFs, reinforcing the notion that IMF sensitivity is most pronounced at shorter UV wavelengths. The negligible differences observed among the higher-wavelength UV indices justify the adoption of the BPL IMF, as the choice between this and a Chabrier IMF is not expected to introduce significant variations in the inferred spectral properties.

2.5
Emission Modelling

To assess the impact of photoionisation modelling on equivalent width estimations, it is necessary to distinguish between the pure stellar incident spectrum and the nebular-processed spectrum that includes emission from ionised gas. Comparing these two cases enables a direct evaluation of how nebular emission influences the measured UV indices. Intrinsic spectra integrate the emission from ionized gas, which can significantly modify the equivalent width of spectral features. By comparing these emission models, it becomes possible to verify whether purely incident emission spectra sufficiently represent observational conditions or if nebular emission must be considered to capture realistic spectral behaviour.

To incorporate nebular emission within the BPASS model grids, a radiative transfer model was implemented using the
CLOUDY
photo-ionisation code
Gunasekera et al. 2023
. This addition enables continuous star formation models generated with BPASS to be coupled with a set of photoionisation input parameters, including a constant-density, (
n_{\mathrm{H}}=316\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
), spherical geometry with Orion-type dust grains, a reference ionisation parameter of
U=0.01
, and Galactic Concordance abundance scaling with
Jenkins 2009
and
Gunasekera et al. 2023
depletion prescriptions scaled by 0.5. The models assume cosmic ray heating, a turbulence velocity of
100\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}
, a temperature floor of
100\,\mathrm{K}
, and convergence through iterative solutions. Calculations were performed using
CLOUDY
v23.01, adopting a reference metallicity of
Z=0.01
at an age of
1\,\mathrm{Myr}
, and outputting both continuum and standard emission-line lists
(
Roper
et al. 2026
)
. This allows for the generation of model grids with the required emission lines, including both stellar and nebular emission.

Figure
5
demonstrates that the inclusion of nebular emission introduces noticeable shifts in equivalent width at elevated metallicities in the intrinsic emission model. While the majority of UV indices show only slight differences when using reprocessed spectra compared to pure stellar spectra, the 1460
Å
and 1853
Å
indices behave distinctively, revealing potential contamination in its pseudo-continuum regions.

The index 1853
Å
exhibits a marked increase in absorption when an intrinsic emission model is utilised. Despite employing the same pseudo-continuum definitions outlined in past investigations
Calabrò
et al. 2021
, 1853
Å
evidently suffers from line blanketing. The cumulative absorption within this spectral window reveals heightened sensitivity to changes in metallicity and stellar composition introduced through nebular emission.

The 1460
Å
index shows a pronounced decline in equivalent width when employing an intrinsic emission model. The behaviour likely also arises from contamination in the pseudo-continuum windows. The increased opacity from overlapping Fe-peak transitions suppresses the apparent absorption depth in the pure stellar spectrum. When nebular emission is introduced, the ionised gas continuum partially fills these absorption troughs, dissipating the absorption and, in some cases, even producing a net emission feature at this wavelength. This transformation shows the high sensitivity of the 1460
Å
index to the adopted emission model.

This pattern can be attributed to the interaction between stellar radiation and surrounding ionized gas, which has a stronger influence on younger stellar populations at greater metallicities. The observed differences highlight the added complexity introduced by nebular emission, especially in metal-rich environments where nebular emission modifies the UV continuum and spectral features.

While most UV indices remain consistent across both emission models, the 1460
Å
and 1853
Å

Å

Å

These findings emphasise that while pure stellar incident spectra are generally adequate for most indices, features in heavily blended or emission‐sensitive regions require careful treatment with full radiative transfer modelling to ensure accurate interpretation.

Maintaining internal consistency between the wavelength sampling of stellar population synthesis models and their subsequent photoionisation or post-processing treatments is essential. Measurements such as equivalent widths, UV indices, and continuum properties depend sensitively on the detailed shapes of spectral features; inconsistencies in spectral sampling can therefore introduce systematic biases in inferred metallicities and star formation rates.

Resampling BPASS models to match the spectral sampling of
JWST

While aligning model and observational sampling facilitates direct comparison, degrading the intrinsic spectral resolution of the models can suppress fine features that are critical for robust equivalent width and index measurements. As a result, higher-resolution configurations are preferable where feasible, with subsequent forward modelling used to reproduce the effects of instrumental resolution and noise. Nevertheless, lower-resolution predictions remain valuable for realistically simulating the observing modes of space-based facilities such as
JWST

In summary, both the intrinsic grid fidelity and the adopted wavelength sampling of model spectra have a measurable impact on the quantitative interpretation of UV indices. Our results indicate that the native spectral resolution of BPASS is sufficient for interpreting
JWST
observations of galaxies at
z\sim 5
–
10

While higher-resolution models could, in principle, provide additional leverage by resolving individual spectral features, the practical gains are likely limited in regimes where observational constraints necessitate the use of blended indices rather than isolated lines. Ensuring consistency between BPASS model sampling and observational configurations therefore remains essential for robust metallicity analyses, particularly for weak or blended UV features in
Flares
, where sensitivity depends on the adopted spectral resolution.

These results indicate that the interpretation of UV indices requires careful consideration of various physical processes. While simplified stellar population models allow significant exploration into the underlying sensitivities of individual indices, they do not fully capture the complexity of real galaxies. Therefore, this analysis must be extended to the
Flares
hydrodynamical simulations, enabling the behaviour of UV indices to be examined within realistic galaxy environments that incorporate self-consistent star formation and chemical enrichment histories.

In brief, the generation of

Each star particle within a galaxy is assigned an SED based on its mass, age, and metallicity, with the combined contribution from all star particles representing the integrated stellar emission of the system. Active galactic nucleus (AGN) emission is not included in this framework. 3.2.1
Metallicity Definitions

In this section, different definitions of stellar metallicity within the

Due to the equivalent widths of UV indices being sensitive to the metallicity of the stellar populations dominating the emergent light, the choice of metallicity definition can directly influence the physical interpretation of model predictions. It is therefore necessary to determine whether total mass-weighted stellar metallicity provides an adequate proxy for the stellar populations responsible for shaping observable UV features, or whether alternative weighting schemes more accurately capture the metallicity relevant for equivalent width measurements.

However, the increased dispersion observed for the young stellar component suggests that UV indices sensitive to very recent star formation may respond to metallicity variations that are not fully captured by a purely global average.

3.3
The UV Indices in

In
Flares
, individual galaxies are not characterised by a single metallicity value, but instead by a full distribution of stellar metallicities that reflects their resolved star formation and chemical enrichment histories. Consequently, equivalent widths of spectral features are not evaluated at a fixed input metallicity, as in simple stellar population models, but instead emerge from the integrated spectrum of the galaxy constructed from all contributing stellar particles. Each stellar particle contributes according to its age, mass, and metallicity, such that the resulting composite spectrum naturally encodes the time-dependent build-up of metals within the system.

In this work, the analysis is restricted to the pure stellar component of the spectrum, such that the equivalent widths are driven purely by stellar absorption features. While the full
Flares

This approach differs fundamentally from the simple stellar population models presented in the previous section, which are based on BPASS simple stellar populations evaluated at discrete ages and metallicities. In brief, for simple stellar populations, equivalent widths are measured directly from spectra corresponding to a single metallicity and a prescribed star formation history, enabling a controlled exploration of how individual UV indices respond to changes in metallicity. In contrast to simple stellar populations made with
Synthesizer
,
Flares
applies them on a per-particle basis to the resolved stellar populations of simulated galaxies. Stellar spectra are generated individually for each particle using its specific age and metallicity, and are then summed to form a galaxy-integrated spectrum before equivalent widths are measured. As a result, the predicted UV absorption features capture not only the instantaneous chemical state of the galaxy but also the cumulative effects of its past star formation and enrichment, providing a more physically realistic representation of how metallicity imprints on the integrated light of high-redshift systems.

Across the suite of UV absorption features explored within the

While the overall scaling is preserved, variations in slope and scatter are evident between indices, reflecting differences in their sensitivity to stellar age distributions, and the composite nature of galaxy spectra in
Flares

This behaviour indicates that Si-dominated absorption features are largely insensitive to short-timescale star formation variability and ionisation fluctuations, with the observed scatter instead likely reflecting secondary effects such as variations in stellar age distributions and composite population mixing. This reinforces their reliability as tracers of bulk stellar metallicity in high-redshift systems.

The blended 1425

This behaviour can be attributed to the onset of line saturation in the dominant metal absorption features, where further increases in metal abundance do not produce a proportional increase in equivalent width. At longer wavelengths, the 1719

Given that both ions arise in regions sensitive to stellar wind dynamics, ionizing radiation fields, and population age, their equivalent widths are more strongly modulated by short-term changes in star formation history and feedback. This makes these indices less reliable for use as stand-alone metallicity tracers at the highest redshifts without careful modelling of galaxy-specific conditions.

Notably, the level of deviation decreases again for the 1533
Å
and 1550
Å
indices, suggesting that Si
II
and C
IV
transitions are marginally more resilient to redshift driven variation, despite their known associations with interstellar and wind sensitive regions. This points to a partial averaging out of burst driven variability or possibly a convergence of the physical conditions under which these lines form by
z\sim 5
. Nonetheless, care must be taken when interpreting these features at the highest redshifts, particularly given the broader dynamic range of the 1550
Å
index and its sensitivity to stellar feedback.

Two of the most consistent indices across redshift are 1719
Å
and 1853
Å
, which show minimal deviation from their respective metallicity trends. The 1719
Å
blend, in particular, stands out for its exceptionally tight alignment across all redshifts. Comprising contributions from N
IV
, Si
IV
, Al
II
, and Fe
IV
, the index appears to benefit from an intrinsic averaging effect, wherein fluctuations in one component may be compensated by the relative stability of others. This results in a line profile that is less susceptible to stochastic deviations and more representative of the integrated stellar population, even under bursty conditions. The 1853
Å
index, while less sensitive overall, displays similarly stable behaviour, further reinforcing its reliability in chemically mature systems. However, its interpretation should be approached with caution due to prior evidence of potential line contamination within the index window, which may introduce systematic uncertainties, particularly in high-resolution analyses.

A notable feature in the redshift evolution of the UV indices is the apparent deviation at
z=9
, particularly at metallicities near
Z\sim 10^{-2}
. However, this behaviour is most plausibly attributed to small-number statistics rather than a genuine population-wide physical transition. In
Flares
, the number of metal-rich systems at
z\sim 9
is comparatively small, as only the most massive and rapidly assembling galaxies have undergone sufficient chemical enrichment to reach these metallicities. Consequently, the index measurements in this regime are dominated by a limited subset of high-mass systems, which are also more likely to experience intense and stochastic star formation episodes. Such burst-driven fluctuations can enhance the UV continuum, temporarily reducing the measured equivalent widths via continuum dilution or partial line filling. The combination of sparse sampling and heightened burstiness at the massive end therefore naturally produces increased scatter and occasional dips in equivalent width, without requiring a fundamental change in the underlying metallicity-index relation.

While most indices are affected by this dip to varying degrees, the 1719
Å
index once again proves to be the most resilient, with continued adherence to the overall metallicity trend, even at
z=9
. This suggests that its blended structure provides an inherent buffer against the localized effects of emission or sudden population changes. In contrast, indices dominated by single elements or narrow transitions such as 1460
Å
or 1501
Å
) exhibit the most pronounced deviations, reinforcing the importance of line composition in determining the robustness of UV indices across cosmic time.

Collectively, these results highlight the nuanced redshift dependence of UV indices in realistic high-redshift galaxies. The stability of indices like 1370
Å
, 1425
Å
, 1719
Å
, and to some extent 1853
Å
, positions them as promising tools for tracking metallicity evolution during the epoch of reionization. However, the observed variations—particularly at
z=9
emphasize the need for careful treatment of temporal effects, especially when interpreting data from galaxies in the earliest stages of star formation and enrichment.

The indices separate into distinct star formation history responses. 1460 Å, 1501 Å, 1533 Å, and 1550 Å strengthen during bursts; 1370 Å, 1400 Å, and 1425 Å remain largely invariant; and 1719 Å and 1853 Å weaken slightly in extreme cases. Nebular emission is typically a secondary modulator, though 1460 Å and 1853 Å are significantly affected by pseudo-continuum blanketing.

Index fidelity depends on maintaining consistent spectral resolution between models and observations, particularly for blended features. Age-weighted metallicities better trace recent enrichment compared to mass- or luminosity-weighted schemes, and the mass–metallicity relation follows the expected trend of enriched high-mass systems and more burst-prone, metal-poor low-mass galaxies.

Overall, 1719 Å emerges as the most stable and reliable UV metallicity tracer across redshift, star formation history, and emission modelling. The 1370 Å, 1400 Å, and 1425 Å indices also perform robustly, though 1425 Å exhibits mild high-metallicity non-linearity. The 1460 Å index provides strong sensitivity but is highly susceptible to burst-driven and nebular effects. Indices 1501 Å and 1550 Å retain diagnostic power but show increased variance due to feedback and stellar wind sensitivity. The 1533 Å and 1853 Å features perform reliably in enriched systems, though 1853 Å requires caution where nebular contamination is significant.

At present, our predictions are limited to simulations and have yet to be applied to constraining metallicity in observation spectra. Recent advances from telescopes such as
JWST
have provided an abundances of high-z galaxy spectra ready for analysis as high level scientific products. Determining agreement between simulations and observation spectra may either present a challenge to these predictions of serve to complement the use of UV indices as metallicity tracers of the high-z Universe.

At present, direct measurements of stellar metallicity in the reionization era remain observationally challenging, and constraints are typically inferred indirectly or through simulations and forward-modelled synthetic spectra. However, the rapid expansion of high-quality rest-frame ultraviolet spectroscopy from recent
JWST
surveys at
5\lesssim z\lesssim 10
now provides an opportunity to directly test and apply physically motivated metallicity diagnostics at early cosmic times.

We demonstrate that rest-frame UV absorption indices constitute a robust set of stellar metallicity tracers when analysed within a fully forward-modelled framework. Our analysis shows that metallicity constraints derived from UV indices remain stable even when individual features are weakened or partially obscured. This may be further progressed by explicitly accounting for observational effects, including spectral resolution, signal-to-noise degradation, and stochastic sampling to combine multiple indices within a joint inference framework mitigates the limitations of any single diagnostic, enabling reliable metallicity recovery under conditions representative of realistic high-redshift observations.

Using both controlled stellar population models and the cosmological
Flares
simulations, we systematically characterise the sensitivity of each UV index to metallicity, abundance ratios, star formation history, nebular emission, and redshift evolution. This mapping reveals that several indices, most notably 1370 Å, 1425 Å, 1533 Å, and 1719 Å, exhibit monotonic, low-scatter behaviour across a broad range of galaxy properties, establishing them as particularly robust metallicity tracers. While other indices show enhanced sensitivity to bursts, nebular contamination, or high-metallicity regimes..

Taken together, these results establish a clear, index-specific framework for stellar metallicity inference in the early Universe. By providing a physically grounded interpretation of UV absorption features and demonstrating their resilience to parametric variations, this study validates UV indices as a strong technique for probing chemical enrichment, star formation, and feedback in faint high-redshift galaxies.

A notable omission in the present investigation of simple stellar populations in Section
2
, is the explicit inclusion of
\alpha
-enhancement. Variations in the abundance ratio
[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]
provide an important probe of chemical enrichment histories, particularly within high-redshift galaxies. The inclusion of non-solar abundance patterns therefore offers an opportunity to further investigate the sensitivity of UV indices to the chemical evolution of early galaxies. Future work will extend this analysis by examining the behaviour of
\alpha
-enhancement within both idealised simple stellar populations and hydrodynamical simulations, enabling a more detailed exploration of how abundance variations influence the physical and observable properties of galaxies in simple stellar populations and
Flares
during the early Universe.

The framework developed here presents a promising opportunity for application to
JWST
/NIRSpec observations in order to constrain stellar metallicities. Although this remains a task that is strongly dependent on both sample size and sufficient signal-to-noise ratios, future work will aim to potentially complement the present study with a dedicated publication. This forthcoming work will systematically assess the current state of the art and provide direct comparisons between noise-modelled cosmological simulations and high-redshift
JWST
/NIRSpec observations.
