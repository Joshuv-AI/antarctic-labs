Expression of aquaporin 3 in squamous cell carcinoma in dogs and cats
Authors: Hiroko SONODA, Yoshiki TANIGUCHI, Naruki FUJIMOTO, Yoshiki HIGASHIJIMA, Toshiyuki MATSUZAKI, Takuya HIRAI, Teruo ITOH, Kazuyuki UCHIDA, Masahiro IKEDA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aquaporins (AQPs) are membrane proteins that facilitate the transport of small molecules such as water, glycerol, and urea. In mammals, 13 AQP isoforms (AQP0 to AQP12) exhibit tissue-specific expression. Over the past two decades, aquaporins (AQPs) have received considerable attention for their role in tumor biology. Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) originates from stratified squamous cells and commonly affects the skin and oral cavity. SCC is prevalent in dogs and cats; however, no studies have reported an association between SCC and AQPs in these species. This study investigated the immunohistochemical expression of AQP1, AQP3, AQP5, E-cadherin, and Ki-67 in cutaneous and oral SCCs of dogs and cats. AQP1 and AQP5 were not expressed in tumor cells. In contrast, AQP3 and E-cadherin localized to the plasma membrane, primarily within island-like structures in both cutaneous and oral SCCs. Ki-67-positive cells appeared in small group and cord structures within tumor regions exhibiting reduced AQP3 expression. Therefore, AQP3 expression may be down-regulated in malignant tumor cells in SCCs of dogs and cats. These findings indicate that AQP3 may serve as a novel biomarker for assessing malignancy in SCCs of dogs and cats.

INTRODUCTION

Aquaporins (AQPs), membrane proteins, facilitate the diffusion of water and small solutes such as glycerol and urea [9, 11, 29]. In mammals, 13 AQPs (AQP0 to AQP12) have been characterized, and each AQP is expressed in a tissue-specific manner [9, 11, 29]. Over the past 20 years, AQPs have been shown to play an important role in tumor pathobiology. Upregulation of AQP1 has been reported in many types of human tumors, such as breast cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and colorectal cancer [10, 22, 24]. AQP3 expression has been increased in human gastric, lung, and pancreatic cancers [2, 4, 20]. Moreover, AQP5 expression has been increased in human colorectal, ovarian, and prostate cancers [14, 26, 34].

Although the precise roles of AQPs in tumor biology remain unclear, the following roles have been considered. AQP1 in tumor cells plays a role in cell invasion and metastasis [22, 30]. Furthermore, strong expression of AQP3 and AQP5 in tumor cells has been related to tumor cell proliferation and metastasis [2, 7, 14, 21, 34].

Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), derived from stratified squamous cells, is seen in the skin, oral cavity, esophagus, and lung [13]. In human esophageal SCC, increased expression of AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5 proteins was observed, and these upregulations were associated with poor prognosis [12, 19, 33]. For dogs and cats, SCC is a common cancer and has been reported to account for 8.2% of cutaneous malignancies in dogs and 21.6% in cats [6, 8, 15]. However, no reports have documented an association between SCC and AQPs in dogs and cats.

In this study, we examined the expression of AQP1, AQP3, AQP5, and E-cadherin, an epithelial marker, in SCCs of the skin and oral cavity in dogs and cats by immunohistochemistry. Moreover, we analyzed Ki-67 expression, a marker of cell proliferation, to investigate the relationship between malignancy and AQPs in SCCs.

Histology and immunohistochemistry

Formalin-fixed tissue samples were embedded in paraffin blocks, and the paraffin-embedded tissues were cut at 2 μm thickness. The tissue sections were deparaffinized and dehydrated and then stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE). For the immunohistochemical analysis, after the deparaffinization and dehydration, tissue sections were autoclaved for 5 min at 121°C in pure water (for AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5) or 1× Target Retrieval Solution® pH 6.0 (Dako North America, Inc., Carpinteria, CA, USA) (for E-cadherin and Ki-67). The endogenous peroxidase was inactivated by an incubation with methanol containing 3% H2O2 for 5 min, followed by washing with phosphate buffered saline (PBS). The specificity of the primary antibodies used in dogs was previously confirmed [5]. In cats, the specificity of primary antibodies for AQP1, AQP3, or AQP5 was verified by immunostaining of renal cortex, renal medulla, or salivary gland, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 1). The antibodies were rabbit anti-AQP1 antibody (1:100, SC-20810; Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc., Santa Cruz, CA, USA), rabbit anti-AQP3 antibody (1:500, synthesized at the University of Gunma), rabbit anti-AQP5 antibody (1:1,000, synthesized at the University of Gunma), rabbit anti-E-cadherin antibody (1:100, GTX134997; GeneTex Inc., Irvine, CA, USA), and mouse anti-Ki67 antibody (undiluted, R626; Dako Denmark A/S, Glostrup, Denmark). The primary antibodies were diluted with 0.1% bovine serum albumin in PBS and allowed to react for 1 hr at room temperature. Then, secondary antibody (EnvisionTM Dual Link System-HRP; Dako Japan Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) was reacted for 1 hr at room temperature. Then, each specimen was reacted with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine for color development, and the reaction was stopped using distilled water. The specimens were counterstained with hematoxylin. Images of HE-stained or immunostained specimens were acquired with a Nano Zoomer 2.0-RS (C10730-13, Hamamatsu Photonics K.K., Hamamatsu, Japan) and were observed with NDP. View2 software (Hamamatsu Photonics K.K.).

Evaluation of AQP-positive or Ki-67-positive cells

In this study, we defined three structures: an island-like structure, a small group structure, and a cord structure, and quantified antibody-positive cells within these structures. We distinguished a concentric nest of tumor cells with abundant cytoplasm, squamous differentiation, and keratinization, forming an island-like structure. In addition, we defined a small nest of tumor cells with poor cytoplasm as the small group structure, and a structure of three to five layers of tumor cells with poor cytoplasm as the cord structure. Tumor cells of the small group structure and cord structure (small group/cord structures) lacked keratinization.

To evaluate AQP3-positive cells, we set a threshold for AQP3 immunostaining color intensity using WinROOF® V5.7 software (Mitani Corp., Fukui, Japan) based on tissue images acquired with Nano zoomer 2.0-RS. Three hundred cells in the island-like structure or in small group/cord structures of tumor cells were analyzed. The percentage of AQP3-positive cells to the total number of cells was calculated. For the evaluation of Ki-67-positive cells, each 1,000 cells in the normal regions, island-like structure, or small group/cord structures of tumor cells were measured using tissue images acquired by Nano zoomer 2.0-RS. The percentage of Ki-67-positive cells to the total number of cells was calculated.

Statistical analysis

The Mann-Whitney test was used for 2-group comparison, and differences at P<0.05 were considered statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we examined the expression patterns of AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5 in cutaneous and oral SCCs. AQP1 and AQP5 were not observed in tumor cells, although AQP3 was observed in the plasma membrane, mainly in the island-like structures in SCCs. Moreover, Ki-67-positive cells increased in small group/cord structures in SCCs with decreased AQP3 expression.

SCC is a tumor derived from stratified squamous epithelial cells in the skin and other tissues. The tumor cells in SCC form island-like structures with carcinoma pearls [13], as well as small group/cord structures, which have been considered invasive [28]. In the Anneroth classification, histological grading of SCC, lobular, and cord-like structures is considered higher grade [1]. In this study, we evaluated the histological characteristics of SCC in dogs and cats using Ki-67. The results showed that Ki-67-positive cells were significantly increased in small group/cord structures compared with those in island-like structures. These results were consistent with the Anneroth classification, indicating that tumor cells constituting small group/cord structures were histologically more malignant than those constituting island-like structures.

In a previous study analyzing AQP1 expression in human esophageal SCC, AQP1 was detected in the cytoplasm and nuclear membrane of esophageal SCC cells, but not in normal cells of origin [33]. Moreover, increased cytoplasmic AQP1 expression in esophageal SCC cells was associated with poor prognosis [33]. On the other hand, in human cutaneous SCC, AQP1 was not detected in tumor or normal cells [25]. In this study, AQP1 was not detected in tumor cells in either cutaneous or oral SCCs. These results suggest that the expression pattern of AQP1 in cutaneous and oral SCCs in dogs and cats may be similar to that in human cutaneous SCC. Furthermore, the expression of AQP1 in tumor cells may depend on the tissue where SCC develops.

It has been reported that AQP3 expression is detected in cutaneous, oral, and esophageal SCCs compared with their normal cells in humans [16, 25]. In this study, we found a decrease in AQP3-positive cells in small group/cord structures compared with island-like structures in cutaneous and oral SCCs in dogs and cats. Moreover, Ki-67-positive cells were significantly increased in the small group/cord structures compared to the island-like structures. Thus, it is suggested that AQP3 expression may be decreased in SCC cells with high histological malignancy, consistent with previous reports [23, 31]. These results also suggest that AQP3 may be a new biomarker for evaluating tumor cells in SCCs.

In this study, AQP3 expression level was down-regulated in Ki-67-positive tumor cells. It has been shown that activation of the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) pathway down-regulated AQP3 expression in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [18]. In human oral and cutaneous SCCs, the activation of the ERK pathway has been observed [3, 35, 37]. Furthermore, the ERK pathway is known to promote cell proliferation in skin and oral cancers, including human melanoma [32], canine melanoma [17], and human oral cancer [36]. Therefore, the ERK pathway may be one of the mechanisms underlying the AQP3 down-regulation in proliferative tumor cells in SCC. Further studies are needed to clarify the mechanism underlying the downregulation of AQP3 in SCC.

It has been shown that AQP5 expression level was increased in human oral and esophageal SCCs compared to the normal cells from which they are derived [12, 27]. However, in the present study, no expression of AQP5 was found in canine or feline SCCs. Therefore, it is possible that the expression pattern of AQP5 differs between human and canine/feline SCC.

A limitation of this study is the small number of cases. In a future study, as the number of analyses of canine and feline SCC increases, the relationship between the expression of AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5 and SCC will likely become clearer.

This study, for the first time, showed the expression patterns of AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5 in SCCs of the skin and oral cavity in dogs and cats. The expression of AQP3 may be down-regulated in malignant tumor cells in SCCs in dogs and cats, suggesting that AQP3 may serve as a novel biomarker for malignancy evaluation in SCCs.
