Sampling from the Hardcore Model on Random Regular Bipartite Graphs above the Uniqueness Threshold
Nicholas Kocurek nichok6@cs.washington.edu University of Washington Shayan Oveis Gharan shayan@cs.washington.edu University of Washington Dante Tjowasi dtjowasi@cs.washington.edu University of Washington

Abstract
We design an efficient sampling algorithm to generate samples from the hardcore model on random regular bipartite graphs as long as λ ≲ 1 Δ \lambda\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}} , where Δ \Delta is the degree. Combined with recent work of Jenssen, Keevash and Perkins this implies an 𝖥𝖯𝖱𝖠𝖲 \mathsf{FPRAS} for the partition function of the hardcore model on random regular bipartite graphs at any fugacity. Our algorithm is shown by analyzing two new Markov chains that work in complementary regimes. Our proof then proceeds by showing the corresponding simplicial complexes are top-link spectral expanders and appealing to the trickle-down theorem to prove fast mixing.

Our proof of

1.4

Definition 1.6

(Two-sided independent set slice)
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a bipartite graph and fix
k_{X}\leq{\left|X\right|},k_{Y}\leq{\left|Y\right|}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k_{X},k_{Y}}(G)={\left\{I\in\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)\mid k=k_{X}+k_{Y},{\left|I\cap X\right|}=k_{X},{\left|I\cap Y\right|}=k_{Y}\right\}}\,.

Define

to be the uniform distribution over
\mathcal{I}_{k_{X},k_{Y}}(G)
. We define the associated pure
(k_{X}+k_{Y})
-dimensional simplicial complex

by taking the closure of
\mathcal{I}_{k_{X},k_{Y}}(G)
over ground set

The second complex we introduce is what we call the
one-sided slice
of the hardcore model. This distribution is over fixed-size subsets of just one side
\binom{X}{k}
and weights each set proportional to the total weight of all independent sets with its intersection on
X
.

Definition 1.7

(One-sided slice of the hardcore model)
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a bipartite graph and fix
k\leq{\left|X\right|}
. We define
\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)}
over
\binom{X}{k}

\lambda>0

\displaystyle\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)}(S)\propto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
I\cap X=S\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}\,.

for any
S\in\binom{X}{k}

\binom{X}{k}

\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)}

To the best of our knowledge, none of the previous works on the subject have studied these complexes and we expect our findings help in better understanding the complexity of
\#\mathsf{BIS}
.

As our main technical result, we establish the near exact regimes for which these complexes are expanders for a random regular bipartite graph. Starting with the two-sided independent set slice, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph. Then with high probability over the randomness of
G
, the two-sided independent set slice

In other words, for sufficiently small

1.2

So instead of sampling using this chain, we show that above this bound the one-sided slice complex is complementary, in the sense that it is a top-link expander.

Theorem 1.9

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a random

-regular bipartite graph. Then with high probability over the randomness of

, the one-sided slice of the hardcore model

Theorem 1.10

Let

1.2
Related work and discussion

Independent Set Slice.

To best understand our results, it is useful to introduce the notion of the independent set slice. Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, let
\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
be the set of independent sets
I\in\mathcal{I}(G)
with
{\left|I\right|}=k
and let
\mu^{(k)}
be the uniform distribution over
\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)
. We can also define the
k
-dimensional complex
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
analogously. The history of the approximability of
|\mathcal{I}_{1}(G)|,|\mathcal{I}_{2}(G)|,\dots
follows a similar course to the hardcore model, with the parameter of interest being the
occupancy fraction
rather than the fugacity. The occupancy fraction of
G
is the quantity
\alpha\coloneqq\E_{I\sim\mu}{\left[{\left|I\right|}\right]}
where
\mu
is the hardcore model for
G
. Just as the infinite
\Delta
-regular tree plays an important role in defining the uniqueness threshold for fugacity, it also helps define a similar occupancy threshold as follows: while the Gibbs measure on the tree is not unique at fugacity
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\Delta)
, there is a natural translation invariant Gibbs measure capturing most interesting behavior
[
5
]
. By defining
\alpha=\alpha(\lambda,\Delta)
as the density of this measure we have the relation

\displaystyle\lambda=\frac{\alpha}{1-2\alpha}{\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{1-2\alpha}\right)}^{\Delta-1}\,.

We skip the exact derivation (see
[
5
]
) and instead highlight the values important to computational complexity. Similar to the hardcore model,
\alpha_{c}(\Delta)
, which is the value of
\alpha
at the critical fugacity
\lambda_{c}(\Delta)
, marks a computational phase transition where for
k<\alpha_{c}(\Delta)
there is an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
for
|\mathcal{I}_{k}(G)|
and when
k>\alpha_{c}(\Delta)
such a result would imply
\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{RP}

[
14
]
. It was also shown in a sequence of recent works
[
1
,
24
]
that the down-up walk on the simplicial complex
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
mixes in near-linear time up to this threshold.

Up to this point, the approximability of the hardcore model and independent set slice have coincided cleanly. Forgoing the worst-case and instead assuming the graph
G
of interest is a random regular graph,
[
7
]
showed that the down-up walk on the standard independent set complex mixes in polynomial-time up to
\lambda\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
. As they point out, the corresponding occupancy fraction to this fugacity is
\alpha\approx\frac{\log\Delta}{2\Delta}
. Our
Theorem

1.10
then parallels their result, showing that the down-up walk mixes in polynomial-time up to the analogous threshold, significantly past the critical occupancy threshold. Moreover, our result goes through with a single application of standard trickle-down, whereas
[
7
]
requires going through the field dynamics (a continuous analogue of the Glauber dynamics) and uses some heavy machinery in the form of a generalized trickle-down theorem for localization schemes
[
3
]
. However, we do concede that their result is
derandomized
, in the sense that it works for any near-Ramanujan graph, whereas our result depends on certain combinatorial structure within random regular graphs.

Although our main result and proof techniques have some resemblance to the recent work of
[
7
]
, we emphasize that the Glauber dynamics (analyzed in their work) does not mix on random regular bipartite graphs by
Theorem

1.3
. A priori, it is unclear whether their techniques extend to our regime. Instead, to prove our main results, we redo their work analyzing the hardcore model on random regular graphs (
Theorem

1.10
) using the independent set slice complex, and we manage to extend this approach to our defined slices of the bipartite case as well, proving our main theorem.

1.3
Proof overview

The proof of our main result,
Theorem

1.4
, follows primarily from two ideas: (i) we prove fast mixing of the down-up walks for the two-sided and one-sided independent set slices in complementary regimes for random regular bipartite graphs by using a combination of properties of random graphs and trickle-down theorems on high-dimensional expanders (
Theorem

1.2
) and (ii) we show a natural way to approximate the partition function of the hardcore model given approximate samplers for the aforementioned distributions.

Part (i): Overview of
Theorem

1.8
.

To prove fast mixing from
Theorem

1.2
, it is enough to establish
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})<\frac{1}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
for all links
\tau
of codimension
2
. We start by showing how this process works for the two-sided independent set slice. Recall that after fixing
k_{X}\leq{\left|X\right|},k_{Y}\leq{\left|Y\right|}
, the two-sided slice is the
(k_{X}+k_{Y})
-dimensional complex
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k_{X},k_{Y})})
.

Since each facet has
k_{X}
elements from
X
and
k_{Y}
from
Y
, there are two kinds of links of codimension 2: (1) those missing two elements from the same side and (2) those missing one element from each side. Showing the links of the former are expanders is straightforward as they correspond to a complete graph.

So, we are left to bound the second eigenvalue for links of the latter type, which we end up doing by establishing

\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\lesssim\frac{\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})}{{\left|(X\cup Y)\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}}\,.

(1)

To see why (
1
) is sufficient, we show that both of these quantities are well-controlled in random regular graphs. First, since random regular graphs are near-Ramanujan we have
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\lesssim 2\sqrt{\Delta-1}
. The question then is what
{\left|(X\cup Y)\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}
looks like as a function of
{\left|\tau\right|}
. Our primary observation is that when
{\left|\tau_{X}\right|}\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+o_{\Delta}(1))\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
, with high probability we have
{\left|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau_{X}\right]}\right|}\gtrsim\frac{\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}
for all such
\tau
(see
Lemma

3.1
). The proof of this fact uses a martingale concentration argument for the pairing model. Plugging these two values in above then gives the bound of
\frac{1}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
as desired.

The key observation towards (
1
) is then that for links of the latter type, the adjacency matrix satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\tau}=\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G{\left[(X\cup Y)\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right]}}}\,,

where the complement here is the bipartite complement, given by flipping only the edges crossing
X
and
Y
. This matrix is simply what is left over after removing
\tau
and its neighbors from
G
, with the bipartite complement being taken because independent sets in these links require non-edges across partitions. To get from here to the bound in (
1
), we simply observe the eigenvalues of
\mathsf{A}_{\tau}
interlace
\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}}
and argue
\lambda_{2}(\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}})\leq\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})
.

While this result works for
{\left|\tau\right|}\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+o_{\Delta}(1))\Delta}
, which we recall is the occupancy fraction corresponding to
\lambda=O{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)}
, it is not clear how to push it beyond this threshold. Indeed, we believe the size of
{\left|(X\cup Y)\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}
begins to shrink quickly beyond this threshold, leading to links possibly becoming disconnected.

Part (i): Overview of
Theorem

1.9
.

To get around this, we take a completely different route above the critical occupancy threshold; namely, we show that a very different simplicial complex, the one-sided slice
(\mathcal{X},\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)})
is a top-link expander as long as
k\geq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+o_{\Delta}(1))\Delta}|X|
and
\lambda\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
. Links of codimension 2 of this complex are in one sense easier to analyze than in the previous complex, as their support is always a complete graph with exactly
|X|-k
many vertices. The difficulty, however, is that this is now a weighted graph. It turns out that for a link
\tau
(of codimension 2), the adjacency matrix satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{\tau}(u,v)=(1+\lambda)^{-{\left|N_{\tau}(u)\right|}-{\left|N_{\tau}(v)\right|}+{\left|N_{\tau}(u)\cap N_{\tau}(v)\right|}}\,,

where
N_{\tau}(u)
is the set of neighbors of
u
in the graph
G\setminus N[\tau]
, where
N[\tau]
is the set of neighbors of
\tau
.
For a random regular graph,
|N_{\tau}(u)\cap N_{\tau}(v)|\in\{0,1\}
with high probability; however,
|N_{\tau}(\cdot)|
could still vary in the range
[0,\Delta]
. In our regime of
\lambda\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
we mainly care whether
|N_{\tau}(u)|\ll\sqrt{\Delta}
, for which
(1+\lambda)^{{\left|N_{\tau}(u)\right|}}
is constant, and our main observation is that with high probability for all
\tau
, almost all vertices
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\tau}(1)
indeed satisfy
|N_{\tau}(u)|\lesssim\sqrt{\Delta}
. This simplifies the structure of
\mathsf{A}_{\tau}
as we can show that (up to a normalization)
\lambda_{\max}(\mathsf{A}_{\tau}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top})\leq\lambda\cdot\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G}^{2})
using an interlacing argument, which leads to a bound
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})<\frac{1}{|\tau|}
.

Part (ii): Overview of
Theorem

1.4
.

Assuming
Theorem

1.8
and
Theorem

1.9
, we prove

1.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

We start by defining an approximation to the partition function. Fix
\alpha=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+o_{\Delta}(1))\Delta}
and
\beta=\Theta(\lambda)
as in

1.8
and

1.9

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
{\left|I\cap X\right|}\leq\alpha{\left|X\right|}\\
{\left|I\cap Y\right|}\leq\alpha{\left|Y\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
\alpha{\left|X\right|}<{\left|I\cap X\right|}\leq\beta{\left|X\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
\alpha{\left|Y\right|}<{\left|I\cap Y\right|}\leq\beta{\left|Y\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}\,.

Our goal is to show that with high probability over the randomness
G
we have

{\left|Z_{G}(\lambda)-\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)\right|}\leq\exp(-({\left|X\right|}+{\left|Y\right|}))\cdot Z_{G}(\lambda)\,.

(2)

Given this, an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
for
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)

\mathsf{FPRAS}
for
Z_{G}(\lambda)

\exp(-({\left|X\right|}+{\left|Y\right|}))<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, we simply approximate
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)
to accuracy
1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, yielding a
(1+\varepsilon)
-approximation to
Z_{G}(\lambda)
. If
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\exp(-({\left|X\right|}+{\left|Y\right|}))
we can brute force
Z_{G}(\lambda)
directly.

We now prove (
2
) holds. Observe that by partitioning the partition function based on the thresholds
\alpha,\beta
and applying triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle{\left|Z_{G}(\lambda)-\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)\right|}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
{\left|I\cap X\right|}>\beta{\left|X\right|}\\
{\left|I\cap Y\right|}>\beta{\left|Y\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
\alpha{\left|X\right|}<{\left|I\cap X\right|}\leq\beta{\left|X\right|}\\
\alpha{\left|Y\right|}<{\left|I\cap Y\right|}\leq\beta{\left|Y\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}\,,

since
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)
drops any independent set exceeding occupancy
\beta
within
X
or
Y
and double counts any independent set with occupancy in
[\alpha,\beta]
in both. The bound in (
2
) then follows immediately from the following lemmas, which show that the weight of the two terms above is exponential small in
Z_{G}(\lambda)
.

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\mu
the corresponding hardcore model at fugacity
\lambda>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\Pr_{I\sim\mu}{\left[{\left|I\right|}\geq 4\lambda{\left|V\right|}\right]}\leq\exp(-{\left|V\right|})\,.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph and
\mu
the corresponding hardcore model at fugacity
\lambda\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}
and let
\alpha=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+o_{\Delta}(1))\Delta}
. Then with high probability over the randomness in
G
,

\displaystyle\Pr_{I\sim\mu}{\left[{\left|I\cap X\right|}>\alpha{\left|X\right|},{\left|I\cap Y\right|}>\alpha{\left|Y\right|}\right]}\leq\exp(-({\left|X\right|}+{\left|Y\right|}))\,.

We delay the proofs of the above to
Section

3
and finish by arguing that there is an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
for
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)
. We suggestively wrote
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)
in three parts and will argue each has an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
separately, from which the result follows. Note that we can rewrite the first term as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\\
{\left|I\cap X\right|}\leq\alpha{\left|X\right|}\\
{\left|I\cap Y\right|}\leq\alpha{\left|Y\right|}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}=\sum_{k_{X}=1}^{\alpha{\left|X\right|}}\sum_{k_{Y}=1}^{\alpha{\left|Y\right|}}{\left|\mathcal{I}_{k_{X},k_{Y}}(G)\right|}\cdot\lambda^{k_{X}+k_{Y}}\,.

Since there are only polynomial many terms in the latter sum, it suffices to have an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
for each
{\left|\mathcal{I}_{k_{X},k_{Y}}(G)\right|}
, the two-sided slice of
G
, within the parameter regime of
Theorem

1.8
. Similarly, we observe the second and third terms of
\widehat{Z}_{G}(\lambda)
are just the partition function for the one-sided slice of
G
from
X
and
Y
respectively, within the parameter regime of
Theorem

1.9
, so an
\mathsf{FPRAS}
for each suffices. To finish then, we just need to convert the approximate samplers given by
Theorem

1.8
and
Theorem

1.9
to approximate counters.

Reducing counting to sampling here turns out to be a bit subtle. The standard reduction
[
26
]
does not apply black-box in our setting, since the problem is not self-reducible under the random
\Delta
-regular assumption. Luckily, the simplicial complex view of
Theorem

1.8
and
Theorem

1.9
provide a natural fix, as the conditional distributions arising in the standard reduction correspond to the distributions over links in the simplicial complex. By the local-to-global arguments in
Theorem

1.2
, we also get polynomial-time mixing of the down-up walk within each link. We can then estimate the probability of any heavy element using standard Monte Carlo methods, recursing on the conditional each time, which suffices for the standard reduction from
\mathsf{FPAUS}
to
\mathsf{FPRAS}
.
∎

2.2
Graph theory

Throughout we assume all graphs are simple and refer to any graph with multiedges as a multigraph. We consider the following process for sampling a
\Delta
-regular multigraph, commonly called the pairing model. We start by defining some notation.

Definition 2.1

(
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices)
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
, a
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertex is an element of
V\times[\Delta]
and we call
(v,i)
the
i
th copy of
v
. We also refer to the set
C(v)\coloneqq{\left\{(v,i)\mid i\in[\Delta]\right\}}
, the set of all copies, as the cloud of
v
and use
C(S)\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{v\in S}C(v)
for
S\subseteq V
.

The notion of
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices allows us to sample a random regular graph by simply sampling a perfect matching on
\Delta
copies of each vertex.

1.

Consider the set
V\times[\Delta]
and assume it has even cardinality.

2.

Sample a perfect matching
\pi
on the set of
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices,
V\times[\Delta]
.

3.

Construct the induced multigraph
G
on
V
by doing the following: for every
\{(u,i),(v,j)\}\in\binom{V\times[\Delta]}{2}
matched in
\pi
, add the edge
\{u,v\}
to
G
.

By conditioning on the pairing model not giving any self-loops or multiedges in
G
, this process gives the uniform distribution on simple
\Delta
-regular graphs on
V
, which is what we mean when we say
G
is a random
\Delta
-regular graph.

A similar model exists for sampling a uniformly random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph.

Definition 2.2

(Pairing model for random regular bipartite graphs)
.

The pairing model for random regular bipartite graphs is a graph generated by the following process:

1.

For
X=[n/2]
and
Y=[n/2]
, consider the sets
X\times[\Delta]
and
Y\times[\Delta]
.

2.

Sample a perfect matching on
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices
X\times[\Delta]
and
Y\times[\Delta]
.

3.

Construct the induced bipartite multigraph on
G=(X,Y)
by adding
\{x,y\}
for every matched pair
\{(x,i),(y,j)\}\in X\times[\Delta]\times Y\times\Delta
.

Once again conditioning on the pairing model not giving multiedges, this process gives the uniform distribution on
\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs on
[n]
.

By arguing the probability the pairing model gives a simple graph can be bound independent of
n
,
[
20
]
established the following result on the near-Ramanujan property of random regular graphs.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular graph on
n
vertices. Then with high probability
|\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})|\leq 2\sqrt{\Delta-1}+o(1)
.

An analogous result holds for random regular bipartite graphs.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph on
n
vertices. Then with high probability
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\leq 2\sqrt{\Delta-1}+o(1)
.

In general, any with high probability (in
n
) event in the pairing model can be transferred to the random regular graph or bipartite graph case respectively using contiguity
[
36
]
.

Definition 2.5

(Contiguity)
.

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two families of probability distributions parameterized by

. We say
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

happening with high probability in one also occurs with high probability in the other.

Fact 2.6
.

The pairing model for
n,\Delta
is contiguous to the random
\Delta
-regular graph distribution on
n

n,\Delta
is contiguous to the
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph distribution on
n

The upshot of this is that we may prove with high probability statements in the simpler pairing models in order to recover them for random regular graphs. See
[
32
]
for more details and references on contiguity.

2.3
Linear algebra

We recall here forms of the Cauchy Interlacing Theorem that will be useful throughout.

Fact 2.7

(Cauchy Interlacing Theorem)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a symmetric matrix. Let
B=\Pi A\Pi
for some rank-
k
projector matrix
\Pi\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Then the decreasing-ordered eigenvalues satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(A)\geq\lambda_{i}(B)\geq\lambda_{i+n-k}(A)\;\;\;\;\text{for }i=1,\dots,k\,.

In particular, if
A\succeq 0
then
A\succeq B
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be symmetric matrices and let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a vector. Then
A-vv^{\top}\preceq B
implies
\lambda_{2}(A)\leq\lambda_{1}(B)
.

2.4
Markov chains

We provide here some background on Markov chains, for more details and references see
[
29
]
. A (discrete-time) Markov chain is a stochastic process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
on a state set
\Omega
satisfying the Markovian property
\Pr{\left[X_{t+1}=x\mid X_{t},...,X_{0}\right]}=\Pr{\left[X_{t+1}=x\mid X_{t}\right]}
for every
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
x\in\Omega
. We describe a Markov chain by
\mathsf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{\Omega\times\Omega}
where for
x,y\in\Omega
,
\mathsf{P}(x,y)=\Pr{\left[X_{t+1}=y\mid X_{t}=x\right]}
.

We say
\mathsf{P}
is
irreducible
if the (weighted) graph on
\Omega
induced by
\mathsf{P}
is connected. We say
\mathsf{P}
is
reversible
with respect to a distribution
\mu
over
\Omega
if
\mu(x)\mathsf{P}(x,y)=\mu(y)\mathsf{P}(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\Omega
. In this case we say
\mu
is the stationary distribution for
\mathsf{P}
, satisfies
\mu\mathsf{P}=\mu
, and is always unique for irreducible, reversible
\mathsf{P}
.

Our key quantity of interest for Markov chains is the mixing time.

Definition 2.9

(Mixing time)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
be a Markov chain on
\Omega
with stationary distribution
\mu
. For any distribution
\pi
on
\Omega
, we define the
\varepsilon
-mixing time of
x\in\Omega
with respect to
\mathsf{P}
as

\tau_{x}(\mathsf{P},\varepsilon)=\min_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}{\left\{\mathrm{d_{TV}}(\delta_{x}\mathsf{P}^{t},\mu)\leq\varepsilon\right\}}\,,

where
\delta_{x}
is the point mass on
x
. The mixing time of
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathsf{P},\varepsilon)
is then
\max_{x\in\Omega}\tau_{x}(\mathsf{P},\varepsilon)
.

It is well-known that the mixing time is characterized by the existence of a spectral gap, that is, a lower bound on the quantity
1-\lambda_{*}
where
\lambda_{*}
is the second largest eigenvalue of
\mathsf{P}
in magnitude.

Fact 2.10

(Mixing via spectral gap)
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
be a Markov chain on
\Omega
with stationary distribution
\mu
. Denote its eigenvalues
1\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\lambda_{n}\geq-1
. Let
\lambda_{*}=\max\{\lambda_{2},{\left|\lambda_{n}\right|}\}
. Then the mixing time of
\mathsf{P}
satisfies

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathsf{P},\varepsilon)\leq O\left(\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{*}}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\cdot\min_{x\in\Omega}\mu(x)}\right)\,.

We can standardly eliminate the dependence on
{\left|\lambda_{n}\right|}
in the bound above via the following observation.

Fact 2.11
.

Let
\mathsf{P}
be a Markov chain on
\Omega
with stationary distribution
\mu
with second largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}
. Then the lazy version of
\mathsf{P}
, defined as
\frac{\mathsf{I}+\mathsf{P}}{2}
, has stationary distribution
\mu
and mixing time satisfying

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\left(\frac{\mathsf{I}+\mathsf{P}}{2},\varepsilon\right)\leq O\left(\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{2}}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon\cdot\min_{x\in\Omega}\mu(x)}\right)\,.

2.5
Approximate counting and sampling

We use the following two definitions to describe approximate counting and sampling algorithms.

Definition 2.12

(
\mathsf{FPRAS}
)
.

Given a finite set
\Omega
and a weight function
w\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
of length
n
, define the partition function
Z\coloneqq\sum_{x\in\Omega}w(x)
. We say an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is a fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme (
\mathsf{FPRAS}
) for
Z
if given error parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and confidence interval
\delta\in(0,1)
:

•

Outputs
\widehat{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfying
\Pr{\left[(1-\varepsilon)Z\leq\widehat{Z}\leq(1+\varepsilon)Z\right]}\geq 1-\delta
.

•

Runs in
\poly(n,\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\log\frac{1}{\delta})
-time.

Definition 2.13

(
\mathsf{FPAUS}
)
.

Given a finite set
\Omega
and a weight function
w\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
of length
n
, define the distribution
\mu
on
\Omega
via
\mu(x)=\frac{w(x)}{Z}
where
Z
is the partition function. We say an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is a fully polynomial almost-uniform sampler (
\mathsf{FPAUS}
) for
\mu
if given error parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

•

Outputs
x\sim\widehat{\mu}
satisfying
\mathrm{d_{TV}}(\widehat{\mu},\mu)\leq\varepsilon
.

•

Runs in
\poly(n,\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
-time.

For a large class of problems known as self-reducible problems there is an equivalence between these two notions. See
[
26
]
for details.

In this section, we introduce some definitions for walks in high-dimensional expanders useful in applying

1.2
. For most definitions and notation on simplicial complexes, see

Definition 2.14

(Local walk operators)
.

Given a
d

\mathcal{X}
with distribution
\mu
on
\mathcal{X}(d)
, we define the local walk operator for
\tau\in\mathcal{X}
with
\codim(\tau)\geq 2
as
\mathsf{P}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}_{\tau}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{\tau}(1)}
with entry

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}^{(\mu)}_{\tau}(u,v)=\frac{1}{\codim(\tau)-1}\Pr_{S\sim\mu_{\mid\tau\cup\{u\}}}{\left[v\in S\right]}\,.

One should think of the local walk as the natural graph random walk on the skeleton of the corresponding link induced by the global distribution
\mu
. Given such a

\pi^{(\mu)}_{\tau}
over

\displaystyle\pi^{(\mu)}_{\tau}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\codim(\tau)}\Pr_{S\sim\mu_{\mid\tau}}{\left[u\in S\right]}\,.

We point out that with this definition

Properties of Random Regular Graphs

In this section, we prove a collection of properties of random regular (bipartite) graphs and the hardcore model which we reference throughout.

3.1
Neighborhood concentration in random regular bipartite graphs

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G=(X,Y,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graph. For any
\ell>0
, there exists
\gamma=o_{\Delta}(1)
such that with high probability over the randomness of
G
, every
\tau\subseteq X
satisfies the following:

1.

(Expansion)
If
{\left|\tau\right|}\geq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
then

\displaystyle{\left|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}\leq\frac{(\ell+1)\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,.

2.

(Anti-expansion)
If
{\left|\tau\right|}\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
then

\displaystyle{\left|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this statement for all sets
\tau
of size
\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
, since the addition or removal of vertices causes the neighborhood to grow or shrink respectively.

First, by
Proposition

3.5

\E{\left[{\left|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}\right]}=\frac{1-o_{\Delta}(1)}{\Delta^{1/(2+\gamma)}}{\left|Y\right|}=\frac{2\ell+1}{2}\cdot\frac{\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,,

(3)

where
\gamma=\Theta(\frac{\log\log\Delta+\log\ell}{\log\Delta})
is chosen such that the equality holds.

Our goal is now to prove concentration of
|N{\left[\tau\right]}|
around this expectation (which is equivalent to proving concentration of
|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau\right]}|
), sufficient enough to union bound across all
\tau\subseteq X
with
{\left|\tau\right|}=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
.

We start by fixing
{\left|\tau\right|}=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}
and bounding the total number of sets in
\binom{X}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
by

\binom{{\left|X\right|}}{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq{\left(\frac{e{\left|X\right|}}{{\left|\tau\right|}}\right)}^{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq{\left(\frac{e(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\log\Delta}\right)}^{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq\exp{\left(\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}\right)}\,,

(4)

using standard binomial estimates and the assumption
\gamma<1
. Using (
3
) and (
4
) with the following lemma will be sufficient to finish.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
t>0
, we have

\Pr{\left[{\left||N[\tau]|-\E{\left[|N[\tau]|\right]}\right|}>t\right]}\leq 2\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{{\left(\frac{1}{8}+o_{\Delta}(1)\right)}|Y|+t/3}\right)}\,.

To see why, we plug
t=\frac{\log\Delta}{2\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}
into
Lemma

3.2
and apply a union bound over the entirety of
\binom{X}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
using our estimate (
4
) to yield

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[\forall\tau,\,{\left|{\left|N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}-\E{\left[|N{\left[\tau\right]}|\right]}\right|}\geq\frac{\log\Delta}{2\sqrt{\Delta}}|Y|\right]}

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{eq:binomest}}{\leq}2\exp{\left(\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}\right)}\cdot\exp{\left(-(1-o_{\Delta}(1))\cdot\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{\Delta}|Y|\right)}

\displaystyle\underset{|X|=|Y|}{\leq}\exp{\left(-{\left(\frac{1}{2}-o_{\Delta}(1)\right)}\cdot\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{\Delta}|Y|\right)}\,.

Conditioned on this event not happening, we have that for all such
\tau
,
|Y\setminus N[\tau]|
satisfies

\displaystyle{\left|Y\setminus N[\tau]\right|}\underset{\eqref{eq:EYNtau}}{\geq}\left(\frac{2\ell+1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,,

and similarly

\displaystyle{\left|Y\setminus N[\tau]\right|}\underset{\eqref{eq:EYNtau}}{\leq}\left(\frac{2\ell+1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\leq\frac{(\ell+1)\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,,

as desired.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.2
.

Define the following edge exposure martingale for the pairing model. Perhaps after renaming, we assume the
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices in the cloud
C(\tau)
are numbered
1,\dots,\Delta|\tau|
. For any such
i=1,\dots,\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}
then, let
\pi_{i}\in Y\times[\Delta]
be the match for
i
. Then, define

\displaystyle Z_{i}\coloneqq\E_{\pi_{i+1},\dots,\pi_{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}}{\left[|N_{\pi}{\left[\tau\right]}|\mid\pi_{i},\dots,\pi_{1}\right]}\,.

(5)

Observe that
Z_{0}=\E{\left[|N{\left[\tau\right]}|\right]}
while
Z_{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}={\left|N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}
, i.e. it is the number of neighbors of
\tau
in this realization of the pairing model. As such, it is enough to show that

\Pr{\left[|Z_{\Delta|\tau|}-Z_{0}|\geq t\right]}\leq 2\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{{\left(\frac{1}{8}+o_{\Delta}(1)\right)}|Y|+t/3}\right)}\,.

(6)

To do this, we use Freedman’s concentration inequality for martingales.

Lemma 3.3

(Freedman’s inequality
[
19
]
)
.

Let
\{\Delta_{i}\coloneqq Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}\}_{i\in[m]}
be a martingale difference sequence satisfying
{\left|\Delta_{i}\right|}\leq 1
with probability 1 for every
i\in[m]
. Then for all
t,v>0
we have

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[|Z_{m}-Z_{0}|>t\text{ and }V\leq v\right]}\leq 2\exp{\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(v+t/3)}\right)}\,,

where
V=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\E{\left[(Z_{i}-Z_{i-1})^{2}\mid Z_{i-1}\right]}
is the predictable quadratic variation of
\{Z_{i}\}
.

To apply the inequality, we first establish the following lemma for our specific martingale.

Lemma 3.4
.

For the Doob martingale
\{Z_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq\Delta|\tau|}
defined in (
5
) and all
1\leq i\leq\Delta|\tau|
we have

1.

|Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}|\leq 1
with probability 1.

2.

\E{\left[(Z_{i}-Z_{i-1})^{2}|Z_{i-1}\right]}\leq\frac{1}{4}{\left(1-\frac{\Delta-1}{\Delta|X|}\right)}^{2(\Delta|\tau|-i)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{1-(1-\frac{\Delta-1}{\Delta|Y|})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\frac{8}{|Y|}-\frac{8}{\Delta|Y|}-\frac{4}{|Y|^{2}}}={\left(\frac{1}{8}+o_{\Delta}(1)\right)}|Y|\,.

∎

Proposition 3.5
.

For any

3.2

We prove here a pair of results bounding the number of vertices sharing common neighbors.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

\Delta
-regular bipartite graph. Then with high probability over the randomness of

3.3
Bounds on the average size of an independent set

The following claim bounds the size of a typical independent set from the hardcore model at fugacity

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

be a graph and
\mu

From the definition of the hardcore distribution

\displaystyle\Pr_{I\sim\mu}{\left[v\in I\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda\cdot\sum_{I\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{I}(G),v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}}{\lambda\cdot\sum_{I\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{I}(G),v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}+\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda\cdot\sum_{I\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{I}(G),v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}}{\lambda\cdot\sum_{I\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{I}(G),v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}+\sum_{I\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{I}(G),v\notin I}\lambda^{{\left|I\right|}}}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+1}\,.

∎

Corollary 3.10

(

1.11

.

Let

be a graph and
\mu

4

In this section, we show our first main result,

.

\tau
with

and any

\displaystyle{\left|X\setminus(\tau_{X}\cup N{\left[\tau_{Y}\right]})\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|X\right|}-\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|X\right|}\geq\frac{(\ell-1)\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|X\right|}\,,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{\ell-1}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\sqrt{\Delta}}{\log\Delta{\left|X\right|}}\leq\frac{(2+\gamma)}{2(\ell-1)}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\sqrt{\Delta}}{\log\Delta{\left|X\right|}}\,.

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{\ell-1}\cdot\frac{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\log\Delta{\left|X\right|}}\leq\frac{1}{(\ell-1){\left|\tau\right|}}\,.

The result follows by setting

On the other hand, for any

and the two-sided independent set slice

of a random

is connected. This is because by

, for any link

of any size, if we truncate the complex to dimension

Proof of

While there are two types of

We can then use this characterization directly to try and derive a second eigenvalue bound.
We will show that

\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}})\leq\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\,.

(9)

Assuming this,

G_{\tau}
is the complement, this is always at least the smaller of

\frac{1}{{\left|Y\setminus(\tau_{Y}\cup N{\left[\tau_{X}\right]})\right|}-\Delta}

This is the exact definition of the bipartite complement, flipping every entry crossing
X
and
Y
and noting the remainder are

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}})\leq\lambda_{2}(\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}})\,.

So, to prove (

We have an explicit form for

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}0&A\\

. We will additionally need

, the random regular analogue of

Proof of
Theorem

1.10
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular graph and fix a size
k\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}+2
where
\gamma
is taken from
Lemma

A.3
, then consider the independent set slice
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
. Our goal is to show for any
\tau\in(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
with
\codim(\tau)=2
,
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{2({\left|\tau\right|}+2)}
, and further, the complex is connected.

We begin with an application of

A.3
, which says for any such

and any

\displaystyle{\left|V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|V\right|}\,.

We can then invoke

A.1
for this complex which guarantees

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\cdot\frac{-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\sqrt{\Delta}}{\log\Delta{\left|V\right|}}\leq\frac{(2+\gamma)}{2\ell}\cdot\frac{-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\sqrt{\Delta}}{\log\Delta{\left|V\right|}}\,.

Applying
Theorem

2.3
, we get with high probability
-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\leq 2\sqrt{\Delta-1}+o(1)
. Applying this yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\cdot\frac{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\log\Delta{\left|V\right|}}\leq\frac{1}{\ell{\left|\tau\right|}}\,.

The result follows by setting
\ell
large enough.

On the other hand, for any
k\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}+2
and the independent set slice
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
of a random
\Delta
-regular graph
G=(V,E)
, with high probability over the randomness of
G
, the 1-skeleton of any link of codimension at least

is connected. This is simply because by
Lemma

A.1
, for any link
\tau
, if we truncate the complex to dimension
{\left|\tau\right|}+2
, we have
\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})<1
. Therefore,

is connected.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

A.1
.

We start with the following observation, characterizing the random walk matrix within each top-link.

Observation A.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mu^{(k)})
be the independent set slice of a graph
G=(V,E)
and let
\tau\in\mathcal{I}_{k-2}(G)
. Define the graph
G_{\tau}=\overline{G{\left[V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right]}}
where
\overline{G}
is the graph complement. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{\tau}=\Gamma^{-1}_{G_{\tau}}\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}}\,,

up to empty rows and columns. Here
\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}}
is the adjacency matrix of
G_{\tau}
and
\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}
is its diagonal degree matrix.

With this characterization in mind, we will show that

\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}})\leq-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})-1\,.

(17)

Assuming this,
we observe conjugating by
\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{1/2}
yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{P}_{\tau})=\lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{1/2}\mathsf{P}_{\tau}\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{-1/2})=\lambda_{2}(\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{-1/2}\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}}\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{-1/2})\leq-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})\cdot\left\|{\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{-1}}\right\|_{2}\,.

We conclude the proof by observing
\left\|{\Gamma_{G_{\tau}}^{-1}}\right\|_{2}
is just the reciprocal of the smallest degree in
G_{\tau}
. Since
G_{\tau}
is the complement, this is always
\frac{1}{{\left|V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}-\Delta}
. It remains to prove (
17
).

The key observation is that we can rewrite

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}}=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\mathsf{I}-\mathsf{A}_{G{\left[V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right]}}\,,

which is just the formula for the graph complement. Notice this is a principal submatrix of the entire complement
\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}}
, so by the
Fact 2.7

(Cauchy Interlacing Theorem)
.
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\mathsf{A}_{G_{\tau}})\leq\lambda_{2}(\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}})\,.

So, to prove (
17
) it is enough to show that

\lambda_{2}(\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}})\leq-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})-1\,.

(18)

By our earlier formula we have
\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}}=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-\mathsf{I}-\mathsf{A}_{G}
, so a quick application of
Corollary

2.8
yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\overline{\mathsf{A}_{G}})\leq\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top})+\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(-\mathsf{I}-\mathsf{A}_{G})=-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathsf{A}_{G})-1\,,

proving (
18
) as desired.
∎

A.1
Neighborhood concentration in random regular graphs

Lemma A.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a random
\Delta
-regular graph. For any
\ell>0
, there exists
\gamma=o_{\Delta}(1)
such that with high probability over the randomness of
G
, every
\tau\subseteq V
satisfies the following anti-expansion property: if
{\left|\tau\right|}\leq\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}
then

\displaystyle{\left|V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|V\right|}\,.

The proof of this lemma is similar in spirit to
Lemma

3.1
, but we repeat it here for completeness.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this statement for all sets
\tau
of size
\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}
, since the removal of vertices only causes the neighborhood shrink.

First, by
Proposition

A.6

\E{\left[{\left|V\setminus(\tau\cup N{\left[\tau\right]})\right|}\right]}\geq\frac{1-o_{\Delta}(1)}{\Delta^{1/(2+\gamma)}}({\left|V\right|}-{\left|\tau\right|})=(\ell+2)\cdot\frac{\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|V\right|}\,,

(19)

where
\gamma=\Theta(\frac{\log\log\Delta+\log\ell}{\log\Delta})
is chosen such that the equality holds.

Our goal is now to prove concentration of
|N{\left[\tau\right]}|
around this expectation sufficient enough to union bound across all
\tau\subseteq X
with
{\left|\tau\right|}=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}
.

We start by fixing
{\left|\tau\right|}=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}
and bounding the total number of sets in
\binom{V}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
by

\binom{{\left|V\right|}}{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq{\left(\frac{e{\left|V\right|}}{{\left|\tau\right|}}\right)}^{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq{\left(\frac{e(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\log\Delta}\right)}^{{\left|\tau\right|}}\leq\exp{\left(\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}\right)}\,,

(20)

using standard binomial estimates and the assumption
\gamma<1
. Using (
19
) and (
20
) with the following lemma will be sufficient to finish.

Lemma A.4
.

For any
t>0
, we have

\Pr{\left[|N[\tau]|-\E{\left[|N[\tau]|\right]}>t\right]}\leq\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}\right)}\,.

To see why, we plug
t=\frac{2\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|V\right|}
into
Lemma

A.4
and apply a union bound over the entirety of
\binom{X}{{\left|\tau\right|}}
using our estimate (
20
) to yield

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[\forall\tau,\,{\left|N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}-\E{\left[|N{\left[\tau\right]}|\right]}\geq\frac{2\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}|V|\right]}

\displaystyle\underset{\eqref{eq:regbinomest}}{\leq}\exp{\left(\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}\right)}\cdot\exp{\left(-\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{\Delta}|V|\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\log^{2}\Delta}{\Delta}|V|\right)}\,.

Conditioned on this event not happening, we have that for all such
\tau
,
|V\setminus(\tau\cup N[\tau])|
satisfies

\displaystyle{\left|V\setminus(\tau\cup N[\tau])\right|}\underset{\eqref{eq:regEYNtau}}{\geq}\left((\ell+2)-2\right)\frac{\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|V\right|}\geq\frac{\ell\log^{3/2}\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}{\left|Y\right|}\,,

as desired.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

A.4
.

We define the following edge exposure martingale for the pairing model. Perhaps after renaming, we assume the
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices in the cloud
C(\tau)
are numbered
1,\dots,\Delta|\tau|
. For any such
i=1,\dots,\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}
then, let
\pi_{i}\in V\times[\Delta]
be the match for
i
. We then define

\displaystyle Z_{i}\coloneqq\E_{\pi_{i+1},\dots,\pi_{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}}{\left[|N_{\pi}{\left[\tau\right]}|\mid\pi_{i},\dots,\pi_{1}\right]}\,.

(21)

Observe that
Z_{0}=\E{\left[|N{\left[\tau\right]}|\right]}
while
Z_{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}={\left|N{\left[\tau\right]}\right|}
, i.e. it is the number of neighbors of
\tau
in this realization of the pairing model. As such, it is enough to show that

\Pr{\left[Z_{\Delta|\tau|}-Z_{0}\geq t\right]}\leq\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}}\right)}\,.

(22)

To do this, we use the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality.

Lemma A.5

(Azuma-Hoeffding inequality)
.

Let
\{\Delta_{i}\coloneqq Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}\}_{i\in[m]}
be a martingale difference sequence satisfying
{\left|\Delta_{i}\right|}\leq 2
almost surely for every
i\in[m]
. Then for all
t>0
we have

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[Z_{m}-Z_{0}>t\right]}\leq\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{m}\right)}\,.

All that remains then is to justify the bounded differences assumption
{\left|Z_{i}-Z_{i-1}\right|}\leq 2
for all
i\in[\Delta{\left|\tau\right|}]
in our martingale. To do so, fix exposure results
\{\pi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i-1}
. Note if
i=\pi_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,i-1
, which is possible in the random regular pairing model, then
Z_{i}=Z_{i-1}
and we are done. Otherwise, suppose
(v,k)\in C(V)\setminus\{\pi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i-1}
and define

\displaystyle f((v,k))\coloneqq\E{\left[|N{\left[\tau\right]}|\mid\pi_{i}=(v,k)\right]}\,.

The quantity we want to bound is then exactly
\max_{(v,k)\in C(V)\setminus\{\pi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i-1}}|f((v,k))-\E{\left[f\right]}|
, but it will suffice to bound
{\left|f((v_{1},k_{1}))-f((v_{2},k_{2}))\right|}\leq 2
for any pairs
(v_{1},k_{1})
,
(v_{2},k_{2})\in C(V)\setminus\{\pi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{i-1}
.

To do so, we first choose a (perfect) matching
M_{1}
on
C(V)
uniformly at random such that
1,\dots,i-1
match to
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{i-1}
and
i
matches to
(v_{1},k_{1})
. We make a new (perfect) matching
M_{2}
as follows: say
(v_{2},k_{2})
is matched (in
M_{1}
) to
(v_{3},k_{3})
; we match
(v_{3},k_{3})
to
(v_{1},k_{1})
and we match
i
to
(v_{2},k_{2})
, i.e., we switch the match of
(v_{1},k_{1})
and
(v_{2},k_{2})
. It is not hard to see that this changes
|N[\tau]|
by at most 2.
∎

Proposition A.6
.

For any
\gamma<1
, the pairing model on
V
, and
\tau\subseteq V
with
{\left|\tau\right|}=\frac{\log\Delta}{(2+\gamma)\Delta}{\left|V\right|}
we have

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[v\notin N{\left[\tau\right]}\right]}\geq\frac{1-o_{\Delta}(1)}{\Delta^{1/(2+\gamma)}},\quad\forall v\in V\setminus\tau\,.

Proof.

We consider the probability that all
\frac{1}{\Delta}
-vertices in
C(v)
avoid matching into
\tau
or
C(v)
sequentially, which provides a lower bound on the probability of matching into
\tau
. The probability that
(v,i)
avoids
\tau\cup C(v)
, conditioned on all previous copies avoiding both, is then at least
1-\frac{{\left|\tau\right|}+1}{{\left|V\right|}-1}
. Therefore, the probability can be lower bounded by

\displaystyle\Pr{\left[v\notin N{\left[\tau\right]}\right]}\geq\left(1-\frac{{\left|\tau\right|}+1}{|V|-1}\right)^{\Delta}\underset{1-x\geq e^{-x-x^{2}/2}}{\geq}e^{-\frac{\Delta({\left|\tau\right|}+1)}{|V|-1}-\frac{\Delta({\left|\tau\right|}+1)^{2}}{2(|V|-1)^{2}}}\geq\frac{1-o_{\Delta}(1)}{\Delta^{1/(2+\gamma)}}\,.

∎

Appendix B
Slow Mixing in the One-Sided Slice

In this section, we show slow mixing of the one-sided chain at fugacity
\lambda=\Theta{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)}
for a bipartite graph
G=(X,Y,E)
at slice size
k=\frac{|X|}{\Delta^{c}}
for all
c\in(1/2,1)
. We show this by finding a graph exhibiting a disjoint partition where both sides have large volume yet low conductance, allowing us to conclude slow mixing using e.g. Cheeger’s inequality. First, recall the definition of conductance.

Definition B.1

(Conductance)
.

For a reversible Markov chain
\mathsf{P}
defined on vertex set
\Omega
with stationary distribution
\mu
, the conductance of a subset
S\subseteq\Omega
is given by

\phi(S)=\frac{\mu(S,\Omega\setminus S)}{\min\{\mu(S),\mu(\Omega\setminus S)\}}

where
\mu(S)=\sum_{v\in S}\mu(v)
and
\mu(A,B)=\sum_{a\in A,b\in B}\mu(a,b)
where
\mu(a,b)=\mu(a)\mathsf{P}(a,b)
. The conductance
\phi\coloneqq\phi(\mathsf{P})
of
\mathsf{P}
is then
\min_{\varnothing\subsetneq S\subsetneq\Omega}\phi(S)
.

The following classical theorem, (see e.g.
[
29
, Thm 7.4]
) lower bounds the mixing time by the conductance.

Theorem B.2
.

For any reversible chain
\mathsf{P}
we have

\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(\mathsf{P},1/4)\geq\frac{1}{4\phi(\mathsf{P})}\,.

We give the following simple construction: take a disjoint union of two independent random
\Delta
-regular bipartite graphs
G=G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
with
n\coloneqq|V(G_{1})|=|V(G_{2})|
. For any subgraph
C\subseteq G
we write
X(C),Y(C)
to denote the left side and right side of the vertices of
C
respectively.
For a set
S\subseteq{\cal X}(k)
we let
\partial S\coloneqq\{\tau\in S\mid\exists\,\sigma\in\mathcal{X}(k)\setminus S,|\tau\cap\sigma|=k-1\}
be the vertex boundary of
S
. For simplicity, we let
\mu
denote the unnormalized measure of
\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)}
. We further assume
k
is even in the analysis.

Lemma B.3
.

Consider the one-sided slice of the hardcore model
(\mathcal{X},\mu_{\lambda}^{(k)})
for
G
above at fugacity
\lambda=\Theta\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)
and
k=\frac{|X|}{\Delta^{c}}
for
c\in(1/2,1)
. We have that with high probability the conductance
\phi
satisfies
\phi\leq\exp(-\Omega(n/\sqrt{\Delta}))
. Thus, the down-up walk mixes in exponential time.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion; the second assertion follows by
Theorem

B.2
.
Let
S\coloneqq\{\tau\in\mathcal{X}(k)\mid|\tau\cap X(G_{1})|>{k}/2\}
. In words, they are the
k
-subsets of
X
that share more than
k/2
vertices with
X(G_{1})
. Now, by definition, the conductance of
S
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(S)=\frac{\mu(S,\mathcal{X}(k)\setminus S)}{\min\{\mu(S),\mu(\mathcal{X}(k)\setminus S)\}}\leq\frac{\mu(\partial S)}{\min\{\mu(S),\mu(\mathcal{X}(k)\setminus S)\}}\,.

where the inequality is because the only edges out of
S
comes from its boundary vertices. We claim that with high probability over randomness of
G_{1},G_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mu(S),\mu(\mathcal{X}(k)\setminus S)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{n}{k}(1+\lambda)^{n}

(23)

\displaystyle\mu(\partial S)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{k/2}\binom{n}{k/2}(1+\lambda)^{n/\sqrt{\Delta}}\,.

(24)

Then,

\displaystyle\phi(S)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\binom{n}{k/2}\binom{n}{k/2}(1+\lambda)^{o_{\Delta}(n)}}{{\binom{n}{k}(1+\lambda)^{n}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\frac{en}{k/2}\right)^{k/2}\left(\frac{en}{k/2}\right)^{k/2}}{\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}}(1+\lambda)^{-\Omega(n)}

\displaystyle=\exp(\Theta(k)-\Omega(\lambda n))\underset{k=\frac{|X|}{\Delta^{c}},c\in(1/2,1),\lambda=\Theta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}})}{\leq}\exp(-\Omega(\frac{n}{\sqrt{\Delta}}))\,.

as desired.

It remains to prove (
23
), (
24
). To see (
23
), note

\mu(S)\geq\sum_{\tau\in{\cal X}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tau\subseteq X(G_{1})}\mu(\tau)\geq\sum_{\tau\in{\cal X}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\tau\subseteq X(G_{1})}(1+\lambda)^{n}=\binom{n}{k}(1+\lambda)^{n}\,,

where the second inequality uses that all vertices of
Y(G_{2})
have no edges to
X(G_{1})
(and
\tau
in particular). The second inequality can be shown similarly.

Next we show (
24
). First, since
k/2=\frac{n}{2\Delta^{c}}
, setting the constants appropriately, the condition of
Lemma

3.6
applies to
G_{1}
and
k/2
(and similarly
G_{2}
and
k/2
) and we have that with high probability over the draw of
G_{1},G_{2}
, it holds that for all
A\subseteq X(G_{1}),B\subseteq X(G_{2})
of size
|A|=k/2,|B|=k/2
,
{\left|Y(G_{1})\setminus N{\left[A\right]}\right|},{\left|Y(G_{2})\setminus N{\left[B\right]}\right|}\leq\frac{n}{2\sqrt{\Delta}}
.

Now recall by definition of
S
we have
\partial S=\{\tau\in\mathcal{X}(k)\mid|\tau\cap X(G_{1})|=\frac{k}{2},|\tau\cap X(G_{2})|=\frac{k}{2}\}
. Thus for all sets
\tau\in\partial S
, we have that

|Y\setminus N{\left[\tau\right]}|=|Y(G_{1})\setminus N[\tau\cap X(G_{1})]|+|Y(G_{2})\setminus N[\tau\cap X(G_{2})]|\leq\frac{n}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\,.

Therefore,
\mu(\tau)\leq(1+\lambda)^{\frac{n}{\sqrt{\Delta}}}
. (
24
) follows since
|\partial(S)|=\binom{n}{k/2}\cdot\binom{n}{k/2}
. ∎
