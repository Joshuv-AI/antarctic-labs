Vitamin D receptor silencing impairs the 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3-stimulated phosphorus absorption by suppressing NaPi-IIb gene expression in primary duodenal epithelial cells of broilers
Authors: Weiyun Zhang, Tingting Li, Chunyu Cao, Yanqiang Geng, Yangyang Hu, Weizhen Song, Xiaoyan Cui, Liyang Zhang, Yun Hu, Shengchen Wang, Xugang Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOur previous study detected the potential regulatory role of vitamin D receptor (VDR) in 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (1,25-(OH)2D3)-stimulated phosphorus (P) absorption and the type IIb sodium-phosphate cotransporter (NaPi-IIb) gene expression in the ligated duodenum of broilers. In the present study, three experiments were carried out to further investigate whether and how VDR participated in 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption and NaPi-IIb transcription in broiler primary duodenal epithelial cells (BPDECs). In experiment 1, the BPDECs were treated with different concentrations (0, 30, 60, 120 or 240 pmol/L) of 1,25-(OH)2D3 for 87 min. In experiment 2, three VDR-specific siRNAs were designed and transfected into BPDECs to screen for the most effective siRNA against VDR. In experiment 3, with or without RNA interference against VDR, the cells were incubated in the medium with an added 1,25-(OH)2D3 level of 0 or 75 pmol/L for 87 min. The results showed that in experiment 1, as supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels increased, among the affected parameters (P ≤ 0.005), P absorption amount and rate, the mRNA and protein expression levels of VDR and NaPi-Ⅱb increased quadratically (P ≤ 0.009), and 75 pmol/L of 1,25-(OH)2D3 was recommended to be the optimal supplemental level for promoting P absorption, VDR and NaPi-IIb gene expression levels in BPDECs. In experiment 2, the si-326 was relatively the most effective in suppressing (P < 0.05) VDR mRNA expression level. In experiment 3, supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 enhanced (P < 0.05) the P absorption amount and rate, up-regulated (P < 0.05) mRNA and protein expression levels of NaPi-IIb and VDR, and increased (P < 0.05) the enrichment of VDR bound to NaPi-Ⅱb DNA promoter region in BPDECs. However, VDR silencing inhibited (P < 0.05) them, especially the promoting effect of 1,25-(OH)2D3 on the P absorption amount and rate as well as the NaPi-IIb protein expression level. Therefore, VDR mediates the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption by promoting the NaPi-IIb gene expression via its transcriptional activity in BPDECs.Keywords: 1,25-(OH)2D3; VDR silencing; P absorption; NaPi-IIb expression; Primary duodenal epithelial cells of broilers

Introduction

Phosphorus (P) is one of the essential mineral elements required by poultry (Berndt and Kumar, 2007; Shao et al., 2019b). Its deficiency can lead to muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis, and abnormal bone mineralization, potentially resulting in rickets or osteomalacia (Berndt and Kumar, 2007; Knochel, 1977; Knochel, 1992). However, the utilization rate of P in the diet for broilers was approximately 40-60%, and the rest was excreted into the environment via the manure, leading to environmental P pollution (Liu et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2012a; Liu et al., 2012b).

As the final active form of vitamin D3 (VD3), 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (1,25-(OH)2D3) plays a critical role in regulating P absorption (Liao et al., 2017; Norman, 2008). The results of Edwards (1993) showed that 1,25-(OH)2D3 significantly increased P utilization rate in broilers. The duodenum is the main site of P absorption in the intestines of broilers, predominantly through the transcellular pathway (Liu et al., 2016). It was found in our previous study that 1,25-(OH)2D3 remarkably increased P absorption in the duodenum of broilers by up-regulating the mRNA and protein expression levels of the type IIb sodium-phosphate cotransporter (NaPi-IIb) and inorganic phosphate transporter 2 (PiT-2), and the absolute expression abundance of NaPi-IIb was much higher than that of PiT-2 (Zhang et al., 2024). Moreover, Li et al. (2022) reported that NaPi-IIb, but not PiT-2, had a close relationship with the inorganic P absorption in broiler primary duodenal epithelial cells (BPDECs). However, it remains unclear how 1,25-(OH)2D3 promotes P absorption via up-regulating gene expression of NaPi-IIb in the duodenum of broilers.

Vitamin D receptor (VDR) is the core mechanism of vitamin D signaling (Haussler et al., 2013; Kitanaka et al., 1998). 1,25-(OH)2D3 is the only natural high-affinity ligand for VDR (Noh et al., 2022). Upon direct activation of VDR, the nuclear receptor retinoid X receptor was specifically bound to form a dimer, which then bound to vitamin D response elements in the promoter regions of target genes, thereby regulating gene transcription (Carlberg et al., 1993; Cheskis and Freedman, 1994). Previous study has shown that hypophosphatemia was observed in VDR-null mouse (Li et al., 1997). Our recent studies in vivo showed that 1,25-(OH)2D3 increased P absorption in the ligated duodenum of broilers possibly by promoting the mRNA and protein expression of NaPi-IIb via the enrichment of VDR bound to NaPi-IIb DNA promoter region (Liao et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2024). However, it is difficult to investigate the direct and specific role of 1,25-(OH)2D3 in this process in the in vivo experiment, mainly due to its uncontrollability. Therefore, we designed in vitro experiments using BPDECs to further investigate the mechanism by which 1,25-(OH)2D3 promoted duodenal P absorption in broilers. We hypothesized that VDR would be involved in the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption by up-regulating the NaPi-IIb gene expression in BPDECs.

The objective of this study was to investigate the effect of 1,25-(OH)2D3 on the P absorption, VDR and NaPi-IIb gene expression, and the enrichment of VDR bound to NaPi-Ⅱb DNA promoter region in normal and VDR-silenced BPDECs so as to verify the above hypothesis.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

All experiments followed the guidelines of the Animal Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University [SYXK (Su) 2021-0027].

Preparations of BPDECs monolayers

The duodenum of 18-d-old embryos of Arbor Acres (AA) broilers was isolated to prepare BPDECs as described previously (Hu et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2025, 2026). The sufficient BPDECs were pooled from 400/130/600 embryos in experiment 1/2/3. The pooled BPDECs were seeded into 6-well Transwell plates (0.4 µm pore size, seeding area of 2.26 cm2, catalog number 3450; Corning, USA) at a density of 6.75 × 106 cells/insert in experiments 1 and 2 or 12-well Transwell plates (0.4 µm pore size, seeding area of 1.12 cm2, catalog number 3460; Corning, USA) at a density of 2.25 × 106 cells/insert in experiment 3, and then cultured in a complete culture medium in a carbon dioxide incubator (Heracell 150i, Thermo, USA) at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 48 h to form tight BPDECs monolayers for the use of subsequent experiments. The above complete culture medium consisted of DMEM/F12 (catalog number 10565018, Gibco, USA), supplemented with 1% penicillin/streptomycin (catalog number 15070-063, Gibco, USA), 5 μg/mL insulin (catalog number CC101, M&C Genetic Technologies, China), 20 ng/mL epidermal growth factor (catalog number HY-P7067, MCE, USA), 100 μg/mL heparin sodium (catalog number IH1440, Solarbio, China), and 3% fetal bovine serum (catalog number 10091148, Gibco, USA). In order to evaluate the integrity of duodenal epithelial cell monolayers at 48 h of culture, the trans-epithelial electrical resistance (TEER) value, phenol red transmittance and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) activity were measured as described before (Cao et al., 2025; 2026). Only cell monolayers with TEER >300 Ω cm2 (6-well Transwell plates) or >72 Ω cm2 (12-well Transwell plates), phenol red transmittance <5% and LDH activity < 400 U/L were considered tight and valid (Hu et al., 2024; Cao et al., 2025; 2026).

Experimental design and treatments

This study consisted of three experiments, and a completely randomized design was adopted for each experiment.

Experiment 1 was conducted to investigate the effect of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels on P absorption, VDR and NaPi-IIb gene expression in order to assess the appropriate supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level that could be used in the subsequent VDR-silenced experiment. Five levels (0, 30, 60, 120 or 240 pmol/L) of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 were designed based on the study of Zhao and Nemere (2002). Therefore, there were totally 5 treatment groups with 3 (the integrity assay of cell monolayers) or 6 (the rest assays) replicates per treatment and 3 wells (1 well for the integrity assay of cell monolayers and real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) assay, and the remaining 2 wells for western blotting assay and P content analysis) per replicate.

Experiment 2 was carried out to screen for the most effective siRNA against VDR that could be used in the subsequent VDR-silenced experiment. Three siRNAs targeting VDR, named si-278, si-326 and si-1313, plus negative control siRNA (si-NC) were designed and synthesized by Shanghai Gen Pharmaceutical Co., LTD. (Shanghai, China). The above four sequences are shown in Table 1. Therefore, there were totally 4 treatment groups with 3 (the integrity assay of cell monolayers) or 4 (the rest assays) replicates per treatment and 1 well per replicate.

Experiment 3 was performed to investigate whether VDR silencing suppressed the promoting effect of 1,25-(OH)2D3 on P absorption and NaPi-IIb gene expression. A 2 (cell type) × 2 (added 1,25-(OH)2D3 level) factorial arrangement of treatments was used. Therefore, there were totally 4 treatment groups with 3 (the integrity assay of cell monolayers) or 4 (the chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay) or 6 (the rest assays) replicates per treatment and 15 wells (1 well for the integrity assay of cell monolayers and RT-qPCR assay, 2 wells for western blotting assay and P content analysis, and the remaining 12 wells for the ChIP assay) per replicate.

BPDECs treatments and sample collections

In experiment 1, the procedures were performed as described previously (Li et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2025; 2026). Briefly, when tight BPDECs monolayers were formed at 48 h of culture (TEER = 410 ± 24 Ω cm2 (mean ± SD, the same is true below), phenol red transmittance = 1.53 ± 0.20%, and LDH activity = 314 ± 17 U/L; n = 3), the inserts were washed twice with DMEM/F12 medium, followed by adding 1.5 mL of complete culture media containing different concentrations (0, 30, 60, 120 or 240 pmol/L) of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the apical (AP) side, and 2.6 mL of the DMEM/F12 medium to the basolateral (BL) side. Moreover, every medium in the AP side was simultaneously supplemented with 0.25 mmol/L of P as KH2PO4. Our previous study showed that P absorption in BPDECs increased in a linear and quadratic manner over the incubation time (0, 10, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 120 min), reaching a plateau at 87 min after P treatment (Cao et al., 2020). Based on this kinetic profile, the treated cell monolayers with various 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels were incubated in a carbon dioxide incubator (Heracell 150i, Thermo, USA) at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 87 min. At the end of 87-min incubation, the monolayer integrity in the groups with 0 and 240 pmol/L of added 1,25-(OH)2D3 was assessed, then the cells were immediately collected for analyses of VDR and NaPi-IIb mRNA and protein expressions. Meanwhile, the media in the AP and BL sides were collected for determining the absorption of P across the cell monolayers.

In experiment 2, the procedures were performed as described previously (Cao et al., 2025; 2026). Briefly, the BPDECs were cultured to 70%-80% confluence at 12 h of incubation, and then transfected with 80 pmol of siRNAs using Golden Tran-R transfection reagent (Jinchuan Technology, Changchun, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. When the cells were further cultured for 36 h (totally 48 h of culture), the integrity of the monolayer was assessed (TEER = 542 ± 28 Ω.cm2, phenol red transmittance = 1.55 ± 0.31%, and LDH activity = 309 ± 22 U/L; n = 3), and then the cells were immediately collected for analysis of VDR mRNA expression.

In experiment 3, the procedures were performed as described previously (Li et al., 2022; Cao et al., 2025; 2026). Briefly, when the BPDECs were cultured to 70%-80% confluence at 12 h of incubation, the si-NC or si-326 was transfected into the BPDECs, followed by further culture for 36 h (totally 48 h of culture) to allow the formation of tight cell monolayers (TEER = 91 ± 2 Ω.cm2, phenol red transmittance = 3.61 ± 0.07%, and LDH activity = 305 ± 21 U/L; n = 3). After washing twice with DMEM/F12 medium, 0.5 mL of complete culture medium with supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level of 0 or 75 pmol/L was added to the AP chamber, and 1.5 mL of the DMEM/F12 medium was added to the BL chamber. Likewise, every medium in the AP chamber was supplemented with 0.25 mmol/L of P as KH2PO4. And then, the treated cell monolayers were incubated in a carbon dioxide incubator (Heracell 150i, Thermo, USA) at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 87 min. At the end of 87-min incubation, the integrity of monolayers was also examined as in experiment 1, and then the cells and media in both chambers were collected for analyses of the same parameters as in experiment 1, plus the enrichment of VDR bound to NaPi-IIb DNA promoter region.

Total RNA was extracted from the duodenal cell monolayers using TRIzol Reagent (catalog number 15596018CN; Invitrogen, USA). RNA purity and concentration were tested using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (NanoDrop2000, Thermo Fisher, USA). Total RNA (1000 ng) was reversed into cDNA using the Vazyme kit (catalog number R312; Vazyme, China), and RT-qPCR was performed using the SYBR-Green PCR Master Mix (catalog number Q111; Vazyme, China). The designed primer sequences for NaPi-IIb, VDR, glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) and β-actin were listed in Table 2. Relative mRNA expression levels were determined using the 2−ΔΔCT method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001), with GAPDH and β-actin serving as internal controls to normalize the mRNA levels of the target genes.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted from the duodenal cell monolayers with ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer (catalog number R0010; Solarbio, China). Protein concentrations were determined using the Pierce BCA protein assay kit (catalog number 23225; Thermo Fisher, USA). Total protein (20 µg) was separated by 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred onto a PVDF membrane (catalog number IPVH00001; Merck Millipore, USA) and blocked with 5% (w/v) bovine serum albumin. Membranes were then incubated overnight with VDR (catalog number ab109234; abcam, UK; 1:5,000), NaPi-IIb (catalog number A9460; ABclonal, China; 1:1,000) or β-Tubulin (catalog number HX1847; Huaxingbio, China; 1:10,000) primary antibodies, and the next day with goat anti-rabbit (catalog number HX2031; Huaxingbio, China; 1:10,000) or goat anti-mouse (catalog number HX2032; Huaxingbio, China; 1:10,000) secondary antibodies. The protein bands were visualized using Tanon Chemiluminescence Image Scanner (5200, Tanon, Shanghai, China) and quantified using its own Gis software. β-tubulin was used as an internal reference to normalize the target protein expression levels.

ChIP

The ChIP analysis was performed according to the method previously described by Wang et al. (2024). The collected cell samples were cross-linked in 1% formaldehyde for 10 min and then digested for chromatin and enriched for VDR using Thermo Scientific Pierce (TM) Magnetic ChIP Kit according to the manufacturer's instructions (catalog number 26157; Thermo Fisher, USA). The immunoprecipitated DNA was used as template for ChIP-qPCR. The sequence of the chicken NaPi-IIb gene binding site is located at 799 to 892 bp in the 5′-flanking promoter regions prior to the translation start codon (ATG) (Zhang et al., 2024). The primer of NaPi-Ⅱb promoter was listed in Table 2.

Statistical analyses

The data from experiments 1 (except for the data of the cell monolayer integrity) and 2 or 3 were analyzed by one-way or two-way ANOVA using the Mixed Model procedure of SAS (2013). The data of the cell monolayer integrity between the two treatments in experiment 1 were analyzed by the T test. The statistical model in experiment 3 included cell type, added 1,25-(OH)2D3 level, and their interaction. Differences among means were tested by the post-hoc Tukey's HSD method (Song et al., 2026). In experiment 1, orthogonal comparisons were used to determine the linear and quadratic responses between dependent variables and added 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels. Regression analysis was used to fit quadratic, asymptotic and broken-line models for the quadratically-affected indices, and the best-fit models were selected to estimate the optimal supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels (Zhang et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2023). The replicate well or the pooled replicate well sample served as the experimental unit, and the statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

The effect of supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level on P absorption, VDR and NaPi-IIb gene expression in BPDECs (experiment 1)

As shown in Fig. 1, the highest supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level at 240 pmol/L did not affect (P > 0.21) TEER value, phenol red transmittance and LDH activity compared to the 1,25-(OH)2D3-unsupplemented control, indicating that the cell monolayer integrity in all treatments was good to meet the requirement of P absorption test, as evidenced by the high TEER values (> 380 Ω cm2), low phenol red transmittance (< 2%) and LDH activity (< 170 U/L). Supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level affected (P < 0.006) P absorption amount and rate, mRNA and protein expression levels of VDR and NaPi-Ⅱb in BPDECs (Fig. 2). As supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels increased, P absorption amount and rate, VDR mRNA expression level, and NaPi-Ⅱb mRNA and protein expression levels increased linearly (P ≤ 0.01) and quadratically (P ≤ 0.009), while the protein expression level of VDR increased only quadratically (P < 0.001). Moreover, based on the best fit quadratic and broken-line models (P ≤ 0.001) of the above parameters, the optimal supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels were estimated to be 62-207 pmol/L (Table 3 and Fig. 3).

The inhibitory efficacies of siRNAs against VDR in BPDECs (experiment 2)

The treatment had an impact (P = 0.009) on VDR mRNA expression level in BPDECs (Fig. 4). Compared with the si-NC and si-1313, the si-326 inhibited (P < 0.05) the VDR mRNA expression, but had no effect (P > 0.05) on it compared to the si-278. Compared with the si-NC, the si-278 inhibited (P < 0.05) it, but did not affect it (P > 0.05) compared to the si-1313. Therefore, the si-326 was relatively the most effective in suppressing VDR mRNA expression, and it was used in experiment 3.

Cell type, supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level, and their interaction had no effects (P > 0.23) on TEER value, phenol red transmittance and LDH activity as shown in Fig. 5, indicating that the cell monolayer integrity in four treatments was good to meet the requirement of P absorption test, as characterized by the high TEER values (> 83 Ω cm2), low phenol red transmittance (< 4%) and LDH activity (< 170 U/L). Discussion

The results of the present study have demonstrated that VDR silencing significantly impaired the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption by suppressing NaPi-IIb gene expression in BPDECs, which supports our hypothesis. These findings provide a novel theoretical basis and nutritional strategy for improving dietary P utilization efficiency to save P resources and reduce environmental P pollution in broiler production.

Vitamin D plays an important role in promoting the duodenal P absorption in broilers (Han et al., 2023; Liao et al., 2017; Shao et al., 2019a; Zhang et al., 2024). Its derivatives include 25-OH-D3, 24,25-(OH)2D3 and 1,25-(OH)2D3 (Chun et al., 2019; Han et al., 2024). 1,25-(OH)2D3 is the final active form of vitamin D metabolism and has higher relative biological activity than 25-OH-D3 and 24,25-(OH)2D3 (Norman, 2008). Zhao and Nemere (2002) found that the addition of 1,25-(OH)2D3 to the medium significantly increased the uptake of 32P by broiler intestinal cells within 5 min, and the uptake of 32P was 150% higher in the added 1,25-(OH)2D3 group than in the control group after 10 min. Similarly, in the current study, as supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels in the culture media increased, P absorption increased quadratically in BPDECs. Therefore, 1,25-(OH)2D3 makes a significant contribution to promoting P absorption in broiler intestinal cells.

NaPi-Ⅱb is the main transporter of P absorption in the small intestine of broilers (Li et al., 2022). Liao et al. (2017) reported that 1,25-(OH)2D3 promoted P absorption in ligated intestinal loops of broilers by increasing the NaPi-IIb mRNA expression. Moreover, it has been reported that 1,25-(OH)2D3 could specifically bind to VDR to activate the target protein DNA promoter region response element to regulate the transcriptional expression of target genes (Carlberg and Molnár, 2015). Our recent research demonstrated that 1,25-(OH)2D3 enhanced P absorption in ligated duodenal loops of broilers through VDR-mediated transcriptional activation of NaPi-IIb and Pit-2 (Zhang et al., 2024). Our current results showed that 1,25-(OH)2D3 up-regulated mRNA and protein expression levels of VDR and NaPi-Ⅱb in BPDECs. Furthermore, P absorption amount and rate, mRNA and protein expression levels of VDR and NaPi-Ⅱb were sensitive indicators for evaluating the optimal supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level. The optimal supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels in the incubation media were estimated to be 62-75 pmol/L based on the best fit broken-line models of P absorption amount and rate, VDR mRNA and protein expression levels. However, based on the best fit quadratic models of NaPi-IIb mRNA and protein expression levels, the optimal supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 levels were 207 and 159 pmol/L, respectively. It was noteworthy that there were no further increases in VDR mRNA and protein expression levels when the supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 exceeded 60 pmol/L. Instead, a significant decrease in VDR protein expression level was observed at 240 pmol/L, indicating that a higher supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 level was not good for VDR expression. Therefore, 75 pmol/L of 1,25-(OH)2D3 was recommended as the optimal supplemental level for experiment 3.

VDR is not only one of about 1900 human transcription factors, but also belongs to the nuclear receptor super-family (Evans and Mangelsdorf, 2014; Haussler et al., 2013; Vaquerizas et al., 2009). VDR knockout in mice was shown to reduce serum P level and NaPi-Ⅱb protein expression in the intestine, and induced hypophosphatemia (Inoue et al., 2005; Segawa et al., 2004). Kaneko et al. (2011) reported that P transport activity and NaPi-IIb protein expression were reduced in the proximal intestine in VDR-null mice. In adult mammals, 1,25-(OH)2D3/VDR complex is thought to be an important factor for proper expression of the NaPi-Ⅱb gene in the small intestine (Kido et al., 2013). Our recent study in vivo showed that 1,25-(OH)2D3 promoted P absorption in the duodenum of broilers possibly through VDR-mediated NaPi-Ⅱb mRNA expression (Liao et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2024). However, the regulatory relationship between VDR and NaPi-IIb remains unclear. In the present study, we observed that the promoting effect of 1,25-(OH)2D3 on P absorption and NaPi-IIb protein expression level in BPDECs was significantly inhibited by VDR silencing. Meanwhile, VDR silencing significantly decreased NaPi-IIb mRNA expression level, and the enrichment of VDR bound to NaPi-Ⅱb DNA promoter region in BPDECs. These results indicate that VDR is indeed involved in regulating NaPi-IIb gene expression via its transcriptional activity in BPDECs, and thus it is an important regulatory factor in promoting P absorption in the duodenum of broilers. Notably, the responses of NaPi-IIb mRNA and protein expressions to supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 were inconsistent under VDR silencing. We speculate that 1,25-(OH)2D3 might activate intracellular signaling cascades that improved the translational efficiency of residual mRNA or directly stabilized existing brush-border NaPi-IIb proteins to prevent their degradation based on previous studies (Song et al., 2013; Martins et al., 2019). However, the precise underlying mechanisms require a further investigation.

In conclusion, supplemental 1,25-(OH)2D3 enhanced P absorption, upregulated the expressions of VDR and NaPi-IIb genes, along with an increased enrichment of VDR bound to the NaPi-IIb DNA promoter region in BPDECs. However, VDR silencing not only decreased the enrichment of VDR bound to the NaPi-Ⅱb DNA promoter region, but also inhibited the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption and NaPi-IIb gene expression. Therefore, VDR mediates the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption by promoting the NaPi-IIb gene expression through its transcriptional activity in BPDECs. Considering that the in vitro primary cellular model cannot recapitulate the complex microenvironment of the broiler duodenum, further duodenal organoid model experiments are required to address the molecular mechanisms of VDR involving the 1,25-(OH)2D3-stimulated P absorption.
