Simplex faces and quadratic toric ideals of lattice polytopes
Aki Mori and Hidefumi Ohsugi

Abstract
. We introduce the clique-face property for lattice polytopes and investigate its relationship with quadratic generation of toric ideals. We prove that, under a mild condition on edges, if the toric ideal of a lattice polytope is generated by quadratic binomials, then every clique of its 1-skeleton is the vertex set of a face. In particular, if the toric ideal of a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope is generated by quadratic binomials, then every clique of its 1-skeleton is the vertex set of a face. For ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes satisfying condition (E), we characterize this clique-face property in terms of divisibility by quadratic monomials appearing in quadratic binomials of the toric ideal; as a consequence, such toric ideals have no indispensable monomials of degree ≥ 3 \geq 3 . We apply these results to edge polytopes and cut polytopes, for which the clique-face property is equivalent to quadratic generation. Finally, motivated by conjectures on quadratic toric ideals, we verify the clique-face property for simple polytopes, matroid independence polytopes, and matroid base polytopes, and discuss stable set polytopes.

2.
Quadratic toric ideals and the clique-face property

In the present section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
A binomial
f
is called
squarefree
if two monomials in
f
are squarefree.
The following fact is fundamental.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a lattice polytope with
P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
.
If a squarefree binomial
x_{i}x_{j}-x_{k}x_{\ell}

belongs to
I_{P}
, then neither
{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{i},{\mathbf{a}}_{j})
nor

{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell})
is an edge of
P
.

Proof.

From (
1.1
), we have
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{a}}_{j}={\mathbf{a}}_{k}+{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}
.
Since

\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}

holds, the two line segments

{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{i},{\mathbf{a}}_{j})
and
{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{k},{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell})

intersect at the midpoint of each segment.
Thus none of them is an edge of
P
.
∎

From the theory of combinatorial pure subrings
[
14
,
15
]
, we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Q
be a face of a lattice polytope
P
.
Let
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}\}
be the vertex set of
Q

and let
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{m}\}
be the vertex set of
P
.
Then we have the following
:

(i)

If
I_{P}
is quadratic,
then so is
I_{Q}
.

(ii)

If every binomial in
I_{P}\cap K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{P}
, then every binomial in

I_{Q}\cap K[x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}]
is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{Q}
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the theory of combinatorial pure subrings
[
14
, Theorem 1.3]
.
For (ii), let

\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
be the set of lattice points in
P

and let

\rho:K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\longrightarrow R:=K[x_{k}:a_{k}\in Q]

be the
K
-algebra homomorphism defined by

\rho(x_{k})=\begin{cases}x_{k}&\text{if }a_{k}\in Q,\\
0&\text{if }a_{k}\notin Q.\end{cases}

Let
f\in I_{Q}\cap K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
. Then

f\in I_{P}\cap K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
, and hence, by the assumption,

(2.1)

f=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}m_{\mu}f_{\mu},

where each
f_{\mu}=x_{\alpha_{\mu}}x_{\beta_{\mu}}-x_{\gamma_{\mu}}x_{\delta_{\mu}}

is a quadratic binomial in
I_{P}
.
Since
Q
is a face of
P
, for each
f_{\mu}=x_{\alpha_{\mu}}x_{\beta_{\mu}}-x_{\gamma_{\mu}}x_{\delta_{\mu}}\in I_{P}
,
we have

x_{\alpha_{\mu}}x_{\beta_{\mu}}\in R\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_{\gamma_{\mu}}x_{\delta_{\mu}}\in R.

In fact, this follows by taking the inner product of
{\mathbf{a}}_{\alpha_{\mu}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{\beta_{\mu}}={\mathbf{a}}_{\gamma_{\mu}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{\delta_{\mu}}
with a supporting vector of the face
Q
.
Thus
\rho(f_{\mu})=f_{\mu}
if
f_{\mu}\in I_{Q}
, and
\rho(f_{\mu})=0

if
f_{\mu}\notin I_{Q}
.
Since
f\in R
, applying
\rho
to (
2.1
) gives

f=\rho(f)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{s}\rho(m_{\mu})\rho(f_{\mu})=\sum_{f_{\mu}\in I_{Q}}\rho(m_{\mu})f_{\mu}.

Hence
f
is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{Q}
, as desired.
∎

Theorem
1.1
follows from the more general fact stated below.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a lattice polytope such that
the midpoint of any edge of
P
is not an integer vector.
Let
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{m}\}
be the vertex set of
P
and let

P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
with
m\leq n
.
If every binomial in
I_{P}\cap K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{P}
, then any clique of
{\rm sk}(P)
is the vertex set of a face of
P
.

Proof.

Let
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{r}}\}\subset\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{m}\}
be a clique
of
{\rm sk}(P)
.
Suppose that

P^{\prime}={\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{i_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{r}})

is not a face of
P
.
If
r=m
,
then

P=P^{\prime}
,
a contradiction.
Hence
r<m
.

The proof is by induction on the dimension of
P
.
If
\dim P\leq 2
, then
P
satisfies the clique-face property (
*
).
Let
d=\dim P\geq 3
and assume that the assertion holds for lattice polytopes of dimension
\leq d-1
.
If
P^{\prime}
is contained in a facet

of
P
,
then we can replace
P
with
F
by Lemma

.
Thus we may assume that
P^{\prime}
is not contained in any facet of
P
.
Then
P^{\prime}
contains an inner point

of
P
.
It then follows that

v=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mu_{\ell}{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell},

for some
0\leq\lambda_{k}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
0<\mu_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
and
\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mu_{\ell}=1
.
Multiplying a suitable integer,
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{k}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mu_{\ell}^{\prime}{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell},

where

0\leq\lambda_{k}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
0<\mu_{\ell}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and
\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}^{\prime}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\mu_{\ell}^{\prime}
.
From (
1.1
),

f:=\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{k}}^{\lambda_{k}^{\prime}}-\prod_{\ell=1}^{m}x_{\ell}^{\mu_{\ell}^{\prime}}

belongs to
I_{P}\cap K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
.
Since
r<m
and
0<\mu_{\ell}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for
\ell=1,2\dots,m
, we have
f\neq 0
.
In addition, since the binomial
f
is generated by quadratic binomials in
I_{P}
, there exists a binomial

g:=x_{i}x_{j}-x_{k}x_{\ell}\in I_{P}

such that
x_{i}x_{j}
divides
\prod_{k=1}^{r}x_{i_{k}}^{\lambda_{k}^{\prime}}
.
Then
i,j\in\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}

and
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{a}}_{j}={\mathbf{a}}_{k}+{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}
.
If
i=j
, then
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}({\mathbf{a}}_{k}+{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell})
.
Since
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
is a vertex of
P
, we have
i=k=\ell
.
Then
g=0
, a contradiction.
Hence

.
If
k=\ell
, then
{\mathbf{a}}_{k}
is the midpoint of the edge

{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{i},{\mathbf{a}}_{j})
of

, a contradiction.
Hence

.
Thus
x_{i}x_{j}-x_{k}x_{\ell}
is a squarefree binomial in
I_{P}
.
From Lemma
2.1
,
{\rm conv}({\mathbf{a}}_{i},{\mathbf{a}}_{j})
is not an edge of
P
.
This contradicts that

\{{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{i_{r}}\}
is a clique of
{\rm sk}(P)
.
∎

The converse of Theorem

.

Let

is generated by

.

However, the following fact shows that this example is exceptional.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
4
, Corollary 3.2.5]
)
.

Let
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a lattice polytope with

.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

|\partial P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}|\geq 4
;

(ii)

I_{P}
has a quadratic Gröbner basis
;

(iii)

the toric ring of

is Koszul
;

(iv)

is quadratic.

We now prove Theorem
1.3
that states that the clique-face property (

) and property (E) guarantee
a necessary condition for quadratic generation of toric ideals.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

“If” part follows from the argument in the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
P
be a
(0,1)
-polytope with properties (E) and (
*
)
and let
P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
.
Since
P
is a

-polytope,

coincides with the set of vertices of

.
Suppose that

(

) appears in a

(\neq 0)
in

and that

is not divisible by any monomial appearing in any quadratic binomial in

.
Let

and

.
Since

and

are different as multisets.
Suppose that

is not an edge of

Since

.

Since

there exists a vector

and

such that

=b&\mbox{if }{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in C_{1}.\end{cases}

Taking the inner product

),
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{r}\frac{1}{r}{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{a}}_{j_{k}}=b
.
It then follows that

.
Then

is a binomial in

.
However, since

In particular, a graph-theoretical characterization of systems of binomial generators of the toric ideal

A

A walk

A walk

4.1.
Simple polytopes

One of the most important conjectures on toric ideals is the Bøgvad Conjecture.

Conjecture 4.1

(the Bøgvad Conjecture)
.

The toric ideal of any smooth polytope is quadratic.

Since any smooth polytope is simple, we study the clique-face property (
*
) for
simple polytopes (that is not necessarily a lattice polytope).

Proposition 4.2

(
[
24
, Theorem 3.6.6]
)
.

Let
P
be a simple polytope.
Then every induced cycle of length
\leq 5

in
{\rm sk}(P)
corresponds to a face of
P
.

In particular, any triangle of
{\rm sk}(P)
corresponds to a face of a simple polytope
P
.
We extend it to general cliques of
{\rm sk}(P)
.
We need the following fact.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
24
, Theorem 2.8.6]
)
.

Let
P
be a simple
d
-polytope and let
0\leq k\leq d-1
.
Suppose that
v
is a vertex of
P
and
{\rm conv}(v,v_{1}),\ldots,{\rm conv}(v,v_{k})
are
k
edges of
P
that are incident with
v
,
and
F
is the smallest face of
P
containing these edges.
Then
F
is a simple
k
-face of
P
.

From this, we show that any simple polytope satisfies
(*)
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a simple polytope.
Then
P
has the clique-face property
(*)
.

Proof.

Let
C=\{v,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
be a
(k+1)
-clique of
\operatorname{sk}(P)
.
Since
P
is simple, we have
k\leq d
. If
k=d
, set
F=P
.
If
k<d
, then by Proposition
4.3
, there exists a simple
k
-face
F

of
P
containing the edges

{\rm conv}(v,v_{1}),\ldots,{\rm conv}(v,v_{k})
.
Since
F
is a face of
P
, every edge of
P
whose endpoints lie in

F
is an edge of
F
. Hence
C
is a
(k+1)
-clique contained in

{\rm sk}(F)
.

Moreover, since
F
is a simple
k
-polytope, every vertex of
F

has degree
k
in
{\rm sk}(F)
. As
C
is a

(k+1)
-clique contained in
{\rm sk}(F)
, each vertex of

C
is already adjacent to
k
vertices in
C
. Thus no edge of

{\rm sk}(F)
joins a vertex of
C
to a vertex outside

C
. Since
{\rm sk}(F)
is connected,
C
is the vertex
set of
F
. Therefore
C
corresponds to the face
F
.
∎

4.2.
Matroid polytopes

First, based on the textbook
[
21
]
, we introduce the concept of matroids.
A
matroid

\mathcal{M}
is an ordered pair
(E,\mathcal{I})
where
E
is finite and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq 2^{E}
satisfies the following three conditions:

(1)

\emptyset\in\mathcal{I}
;

(2)

if
I\in\mathcal{I}
and
I^{\prime}\subset I
, then
I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}
;

(3)

if
I_{1},I_{2}\in\mathcal{I}
and
|I_{1}|<|I_{2}|
, then there exists an element
x\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}
such that
I_{1}\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}
.

The set
E
is called the
ground set
of
\mathcal{M}
and the elements of
\mathcal{I}
are called the
independent sets
of
\mathcal{M}
.
The
rank
of a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
is defined as

{\rm rank}(\mathcal{M})=\max_{I\in\mathcal{I}}|I|.

An element of
\mathcal{I}
of cardinality
{\rm rank}(\mathcal{M})
is called a
basis
of
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of all bases of
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Given a subset
I\subset E=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
,
we set
\rho(I)=\sum_{e_{i}\in I}{\bf e}_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
.
Given a matroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{I})
with
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
,
the
independence polytope

{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
of
{\mathcal{M}}
is the convex hull of

\{\rho(I):I\in{\mathcal{I}}\},

and the
basis polytope

{\mathcal{P}}_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
of
{\mathcal{M}}
is the convex hull of

\{\rho(B):B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}.

The White conjecture
[
27
]
, another important conjecture on toric ideals, states
that the toric ideal of any matroid polytope is generated by quadratic
binomials arising from symmetric exchanges.
A weaker version of the conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 4.5
.

The toric ideal of any matroid base polytope is quadratic.

Remark 4.6
.

In
[
2
,
6
]
,
a long-standing conjecture “the toric ideal of any matroid polytope has a quadratic Gröbner basis”
is disproved.
It is very interesting that, in
[
6
]
, the authors use cliques of the
1
-skeleton of the matroid polytope of the Fano matroid to verify that it is a counterexample.

Edges of matroid polytopes are characterized as follows.

Proposition 4.7

(
[
9
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a matroid.
Then
{\rm conv}(\rho(I),\rho(I^{\prime}))
with
I,I^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{I}}
is an edge of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
if and only if
one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

|I\ \Delta\ I^{\prime}|=1
, or

(ii)

|I\ \Delta\ I^{\prime}|=2
and
I\cup I^{\prime}\notin{\mathcal{I}}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid.
Then
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfies the clique-face property (
*
).

Proof.

It is enough to consider maximal cliques, since every subset of the vertex set of a simplex face is again the vertex set of a face.
Let
F=\{I_{1},\dots,I_{r}\}\subset{\mathcal{I}}
be a maximal clique of
{\rm sk}({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}}))
.
We may assume that
r>2
.
Let
k=\min_{I\in F}|I|
.
From Proposition
4.7
, we have

k\leq\max_{I\in F}|I|\leq k+1.

Case 1
(
\max_{I\in F}|I|=k
)
.

Then every
I_{i},I_{j}\in F
with
i\neq j
satisfy condition (ii) in Proposition
4.7
.
Let
I=I_{1}\cap I_{2}
and
I_{1}\ \Delta\ I_{2}=\{x,y\}

where
x\in I_{1}
and
y\in I_{2}
.
Then
I_{1}\cup I_{2}=I\sqcup\{x,y\}
does not belong to
{\mathcal{I}}
.

Case 1.1
(
x\in I_{3}
)
.

Suppose that
y\notin I_{3}
.
Since
x\notin I_{2}
and
|I_{2}\ \Delta\ I_{3}|=2
, we have

I_{2}\ \Delta\ I_{3}=\{x,y\}
.
Hence
I_{1}=I_{3}
, a contradiction.
Thus we have
y\in I_{3}
.
Hence
I_{3}=\{x,y\}\sqcup(I\setminus\{z\})

for some
z\in I
.
By the same argument, it follows that each
I_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) is of the form

I_{i}=(\{x,y\}\sqcup I)\setminus\{z\}
for some
z\in\{x,y\}\sqcup I
.
Conversely, if
I^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{I}}
is of the form

I^{\prime}=(\{x,y\}\sqcup I)\setminus\{z\}
for some
z\in\{x,y\}\sqcup I
, then
I^{\prime}
and
I_{i}\ (\neq I^{\prime})
with
1\leq i\leq r
satisfy condition (ii) in Proposition
4.7
.
Since
F
is maximal, we have
I^{\prime}\in F
.
Let

{\bf w}=\sum_{e_{i}\in I\sqcup\{x,y\}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-\sum_{e_{i}\notin I\sqcup\{x,y\}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}.

Then the inner product
{\bf w}\cdot\rho(I)
with
I\in{\mathcal{I}}

is less than or equal to
k
,
and equal to
k
if and only if
I
belongs to
F
.
Thus
F
corresponds to a face of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Case 1.2
(
x\notin I_{3}
)
.

Since
x\in I_{1}
and
|I_{1}\ \Delta\ I_{3}|=2
, we have
I_{3}=I\sqcup\{z\}

for some
z\notin I
.
Since
I_{2}\neq I_{3}
,
z\neq y
.
By the same argument, it follows that each
I_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) is of the form

I_{i}=I\sqcup\{z\}
for some
z\notin I
.
Let
F^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}I_{i}
.
Note that
I\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}
with
\alpha,\beta\in F^{\prime}\setminus I
does not belong to
{\mathcal{I}}
.
Let

{\bf w}=\mu\sum_{e_{i}\in I}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+\sum_{e_{i}\in F^{\prime}\setminus I}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-\mu\sum_{e_{i}\notin F^{\prime}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i},

where
\mu
is a sufficiently large number.
Then the inner product
{\bf w}\cdot\rho(I)
with
I\in{\mathcal{I}}

is less than or equal to
\mu(k-1)+1
,
and equal to
\mu(k-1)+1
if and only if
I
belongs to
F
.
Thus
F
corresponds to a face of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Case 2
(
\max_{I\in F}|I|=k+1
)
.

Let
I_{1}\in F
with
|I_{1}|=k
, and
I_{2}\in F
with
|I_{2}|=k+1
.
Then
I_{1}\cup\{x\}=I_{2}
for some
x\in E
.
Suppose that
|I_{3}|=k
.
Then
I_{3}\cup\{y\}=I_{2}
for some
y\in I_{1}
.
Hence
I_{1}\cup I_{3}=I_{2}
belongs to
{\mathcal{I}}
.
This contradicts condition (ii) in Proposition
4.7

for
I_{1}
and
I_{3}
.
Thus
|I_{i}|=k+1
and hence
I_{1}\subsetneq I_{i}
for each
2\leq i\leq r
.
Let
F^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}I_{i}
.
Note that
I_{1}\cup\{\alpha,\beta\}
with
\alpha,\beta\in F^{\prime}\setminus I_{1}
does not belong to
{\mathcal{I}}
.
Let

{\bf w}=\sum_{e_{i}\in I_{1}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-\sum_{e_{i}\notin F^{\prime}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}.

Then the inner product
{\bf w}\cdot\rho(I)
with
I\in{\mathcal{I}}

is less than or equal to
k
,
and equal to
k
if and only if
I
belongs to
F
.
Thus
F
corresponds to a face of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
∎

Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
is a face of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
, we have the following.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matroid.
Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{B}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfies the clique-face property (
*
).

4.3.
Stable set polytopes

Let
G
be a simple graph on
[d]
. A subset
S\subset[d]
is called a
stable set
(or an
independent set
) of
G

if
\{i,j\}\notin E(G)
for all
i,j\in S
with
i\neq j
.
In particular,
\emptyset
and
\{i\}
with
i\in[d]

are stable.
Let
S(G)
denote
the set of all stable sets of
G
.
Then the
stable set polytope

{\rm STAB}(G)
of
G
is
the convex hull of

\{\rho(S):S\in S(G)\}.

It was shown in
[
1
]
that any stable set polytope of a graph satisfies (E).
Edges of stable set polytopes are characterized as follows.

Proposition 4.10

(
[
5
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph.
Then
{\rm conv}(\rho(S),\rho(S^{\prime}))
with
S,S^{\prime}\in S(G)
is an edge of
{\rm STAB}(G)
if and only if the subgraph
H
of
G
induced by
S\ \Delta\ S^{\prime}

is connected.

On the other hand, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.11

(
[
8
,
19
]
)
.

Let
G
be a perfect graph.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is perfectly contractile;

(ii)

I_{{\rm STAB}(G)}
is quadratic;

(iii)

G
contains no even antiholes and no odd prisms.

Both “(i)
\Longrightarrow
(iii)” and “(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii)” hold in general
[
11
,
19
]
.
Conjecture
4.11
is true for several classes of graphs,
including dart-free graphs, even prism-free graphs,
weakly chordal graphs,
Meyniel graphs, and perfectly orderable graphs;
see
[
20
]
and the references therein.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
G
be a graph.
If
{\rm STAB}(G)
has the clique-face property
(*)
, then
G
contains no even antiholes and no odd prisms.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has an even antihole or an odd prism.
Then proofs of
[
19
, Theorem 1.7]
and
[
12
, Proposition 11]

guarantee that
I_{{\rm STAB}(G)}
has an indispensable binomial of degree
\geq 3
.
From Theorem
1.3
, there exists a clique of
{\rm sk}({\rm STAB}(G))

that does not correspond to any face of
{\rm STAB}(G)
.
∎

Hence we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&&&G\mbox{ is perfectly contractile}\\
&&&&\Downarrow\\
I_{{\rm STAB}(G)}\mbox{ is quadratic }&\Rightarrow&\mbox{the clique-face property }(*)&\Rightarrow&\begin{array}[]{c}G\mbox{ contains no even antiholes}\\
\mbox{and no odd prisms}.\end{array}\end{array}

Thus these four conditions are expected to be equivalent for perfect graphs.
