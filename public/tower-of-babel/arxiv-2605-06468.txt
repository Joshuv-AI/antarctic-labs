Equivalence of intrinsic and extrinsic area bounds for minimal surfaces
Enric Florit-Simon

Abstract
We show that intrinsic and extrinsic area density bounds are equivalent, with matching asymptotic values, for complete, connected, smooth minimal immersions Σ d ​ ↪ 𝑖 ​ ℝ N \Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N} of any dimension and codimension. Combining our results with a recent breakthrough by Bellettini [ 2 ] , we extend the Schoen–Simon–Yau curvature estimates [ 17 ] for smoothly immersed, two-sided, stable minimal hypersurfaces Σ n ​ ↪ 𝑖 ​ ℝ n + 1 \Sigma^{n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n+1} with bounded intrinsic area density to the missing case n = 6 n=6 , which had remained open since.

Introduction

Let
\Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an immersed minimal submanifold of dimension
d
.
Given
p\in\Sigma
and
R>0
, define its
intrinsic
and
extrinsic area densities
via

{\bf M}_{R}^{{\rm int}}(\Sigma,p):=\frac{1}{R^{d}}|B_{R}^{\Sigma}(p)|\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pt{\bf M}_{R}^{{\rm ext}}(\Sigma,p):=\frac{1}{R^{d}}{\rm Area}(\Sigma\cap B_{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(p)):=\frac{1}{R^{d}}|i^{-1}(B_{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(p))|.

Here
B_{R}^{\Sigma}(p)=\{x\in\Sigma:d_{\Sigma}(x,p)<R\}
denotes the intrinsic ball of radius
R
, and all lengths and areas on
\Sigma
are computed with respect to the pullback metric
i^{*}(g_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. In particular, extrinsic area is “counted with multiplicity”, as
i
need not be injective, and it corresponds to the natural varifold measure associated to the immersion.

Our main result is the equivalence between intrinsic and extrinsic area density bounds for entire minimal immersions of any dimension and codimension:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a complete, connected, smoothly immersed minimal submanifold, and let
p\in\Sigma
. Then
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma,p)
and
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma,p)
, which are both monotone nondecreasing, satisfy

\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma,p)=\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma,p)\in[\omega_{d},\infty],

where
\omega_{d}:=|B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|
.

In particular,
\Sigma
has bounded intrinsic area density if and only if it has bounded extrinsic area density. In such a case,
\Sigma
is additionally a proper immersion.

Remark 1.2
.

The inequality
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma,p)\leq{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma,p)
is trivial and completely local, by the direct comparison
|p-q|\leq d_{\Sigma}(p,q)
for any
p,q\in\Sigma\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Our proof consists in finding an asymptotic chord-arc estimate in the opposite direction, exploiting a monotonicity formula for the intrinsic area density.

A main motivation behind this result is the lack of a general compactness theory for minimal immersions (or even embeddings) with intrinsic area bounds. This is in stark contrast with the case of extrinsically bounded area, where stationary integral varifolds—and their associated compactness theorem
[
1
]
—provide the main tool.

In fact, even for stable, codimension one minimal immersions
\Sigma^{n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, this issue has not as of yet been resolved. This is exemplified by a well-known gap between the best known (optimal) dimensional requirement
n\leq 6
for the flatness of entire, stable, codimension one minimal embeddings with extrinsic area bounds
[
18
]
and that for immersions (or even embeddings) with only intrinsic area bounds
[
17
]
, in which the case
n=6
has been missing since. In combination with the recent breakthrough
[
2
]
, which removes the embeddedness requirement for
n=6
,
Theorem

1.1
resolves the dimensional gap:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Sigma^{6}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
be a complete, connected, smoothly immersed, two-sided stable minimal hypersurface. Assume that
\Sigma
has bounded intrinsic area density, i.e. there are some
p\in\Sigma
and
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
such that
|B_{R}^{\Sigma}(p)|\leq\Lambda R^{6}
for all
R>0
. Then
\Sigma
is a flat hyperplane.

For
n\geq 7
,
[
3
]
provided a nonflat counterexample (in fact, an area-minimising boundary of a set of locally finite perimeter), asymptotic to Simons’ cone
[
20
]
. In this sense,
Theorem

1.3
is sharp.

Local curvature estimates for stable minimal immersions will follow, completing the picture in
[
17
]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Sigma^{6}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
be a complete, connected, smoothly immersed, two-sided stable minimal hypersurface with boundary
\partial\Sigma
. Assume that
d_{\Sigma}(p,\partial\Sigma)>R
and
|B_{R}^{\Sigma}(p)|\leq\Lambda R^{6}
for some
p\in\Sigma
and
\Lambda\,,R>0
. Then,

|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma}|\leq\frac{C}{R}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{R/2}^{\Sigma}(p)

for some
C=C(\Lambda)>0
.

Let us recall the historical progress towards this result. Let
\Sigma^{n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be as in
Theorem

1.4
.

•

For
n\leq 5
,
[
17
]
proved the exact analogue result.

•

For
n=6
, under the additional assumption that
\Sigma
be an area-minimising boundary, the local curvature estimates were obtained by
[
19
]
.

•

For
n=6
, under the additional assumptions of embeddedness and a uniform bound on the extrinsic area density, they were obtained in
[
18
]
.

•

Much more recently, for
n=6

[
2
]
removed the embeddedness condition, obtaining the local curvature estimates under the only additional assumption of a uniform bound on the extrinsic area density.

Interestingly, unlike
Theorem

1.3
, the local curvature estimates in
Theorem

1.4
do not follow directly from the ones in
[
2
]
, as the comparability between intrinsic and extrinsic area densities in
Theorem

1.1
is exclusively global. We will reduce
Theorem

1.4
to
Theorem

1.3
instead, by combining a well-known scaling argument and the monotonicity of intrinsic area density.

Remark 1.5
.

The well-known “stable Bernstein conjecture” asks whether the same results hold even without any assumption on the area of
\Sigma
. This was shown to be true in
[
13
,
12
,
16
]
for
n=2
, and for
n=3,4,5
in very recent breakthroughs
[
9
,
7
,
14
]
(see also
[
8
,
6
,
5
,
21
]
). A main step in several of the proofs, particularly in higher dimensions, is to show that any complete, connected, smoothly immersed, two-sided stable minimal hypersurface
\Sigma^{n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
satisfies intrinsic area bounds indeed, thus reducing their classification as flat hyperplanes to the main result in
[
17
]
.
Theorem

1.3
suggests this as a viable strategy also for
n=6
, removing a well-known obstruction in the missing case.

Remark 1.6
.

In combination with
[
2
]
, this also provides a new proof of the results in
[
17
]
for
\Sigma^{n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
n\leq 5
(either by running the same arguments, or simply considering
\Sigma^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{6-n}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
in
Theorems

1.3
and
1.4
).

2
Proofs

In what follows we always assume completeness, connectedness and smoothness of any
\Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We work in arbitrary dimension and codimension, and we do not require stability unless otherwise indicated. The notation “
0\in\Sigma
” will mean that we fix some point
o\in\Sigma
, denoted by
0
by slight abuse of notation, such that
i(o)=0
. Having fixed such a choice, we denote
r_{\Sigma}(x):={\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(x,o)
,
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma):={\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma,o)
, and
B_{R}^{\Sigma}:=B_{R}^{\Sigma}(o)
.

The main ingredient in the proof is a monotonicity formula for the intrinsic area density (motivated by a computation in
[
22
, Section 7]
to obtain lower volume estimates for minimal submanifolds in nonpositive curvature, which we learnt from
[
10
, Lemma 8.9]
). Although likely folklore, and recently noted in the literature (see
[
15
, Proof of Proposition 2.4]
), it seems to have received significantly less attention than its extrinsic counterpart; in particular, we have not found a reference for its simple (albeit powerful) error term.

Lemma 2.1

(Monotonicity formula)
.

Let
\Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an immersed submanifold with boundary
\partial\Sigma
and
0\in\Sigma
. Let
{\rm H}_{\Sigma}
denote its mean curvature vector. Then, given
0<R_{1}<R_{2}<{\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(0,\partial\Sigma)
, we have

{\bf M}_{R_{2}}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{R_{1}}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)=\int_{B_{R_{2}}^{\Sigma}\setminus B_{R_{1}}^{\Sigma}}\frac{(r_{\Sigma}(x)-|x|)+\frac{|x|}{2}(\frac{x}{|x|}-\nu_{B_{r_{\Sigma}(x)}^{\Sigma}}(x))^{2}}{r_{\Sigma}^{d+1}(x)}\,dx+\int_{R_{1}}^{R_{2}}\int_{B_{t}^{\Sigma}}\frac{x\cdot{\rm H}_{\Sigma}(x)}{t^{d+1}}\,dx\,dt\,.

(1)

In particular, if
\Sigma
is minimal then
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)
is monotone nondecreasing in
R\in(0,{\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(0,\partial\Sigma))
.

Proof.

A simple computation shows that
\Delta_{\Sigma}|x|^{2}=2d+2x\cdot{\rm H}_{\Sigma}
. Integrating and using the divergence theorem, letting
\nu_{B_{r}^{\Sigma}}
denote the exterior intrinsic normal vector to the ball we find

\displaystyle d\,|B_{R}^{\Sigma}|+\int_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}x\cdot{\rm H}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}\Delta_{\Sigma}|x|^{2}=\int_{\partial B_{R}^{\Sigma}}x\cdot\nu_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}(x).

Writing

x\cdot\nu_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}(x)=R-\big[(R-|x|)+(|x|-x\cdot\nu_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}(x))\big]=R-\big[(R-|x|)+\frac{|x|}{2}(\frac{x}{|x|}-\nu_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}(x))^{2}\big]

we find

\displaystyle d\,|B_{R}^{\Sigma}|+\int_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}x\cdot{\rm H}_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=R|\partial B_{R}^{\Sigma}|-\int_{\partial B_{R}^{\Sigma}}\big[(R-|x|)+\frac{|x|}{2}(\frac{x}{|x|}-\nu_{B_{R}^{\Sigma}}(x))^{2}\big]dx\,.

Since

R^{d+1}\frac{d}{dR}(R^{-d}|B_{R}^{\Sigma}|)=R|\partial B_{R}^{\Sigma}|-d|B_{R}^{\Sigma}|,

dividing both sides by
R^{d+1}
, using that
R=r_{\Sigma}(x)
for every
x\in\partial B_{R}^{\Sigma}
, and
integrating we get (
1
).

If
\Sigma
is minimal (and thus
{\rm H_{\Sigma}}\equiv 0
) the right-hand side in (
1
) is nonnegative, since
r_{\Sigma}(x)\geq|x|
by
Remark

1.2
, hence
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)
is then monotone nondecreasing in
R
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In particular, if
\Sigma
is minimal, recentering
Lemma

2.1
and sending
R_{1}\to 0
we get the lower density bound

{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma,p)\geq|B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|=\omega_{d}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,p\in\Sigma\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptR\in(0,{\rm dist}_{\Sigma}(0,\partial\Sigma)).

Lemma 2.3

(Density pinching implies chord-arc estimate)
.

Let
\Sigma^{d}\overset{i}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an immersed minimal submanifold with
0\in\Sigma
. Given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,d)>0
such that: If
{\bf M}_{8R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\leq\delta
, then

|x|\leq r_{\Sigma}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)|x|\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{in}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{4R}^{\Sigma}\setminus B_{2R}^{\Sigma}.

Proof.

By scaling invariance we can assume that
R=1
.

The inequality
|x|\leq r_{\Sigma}(x)
is trivial (recall
Remark

1.2
). Assume, for contradiction, that there were
x\in B_{4}^{\Sigma}\setminus B_{2}^{\Sigma}
with
r_{\Sigma}(x)\geq(1+\varepsilon)|x|
, thus with
r_{\Sigma}(x)-|x|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}r_{\Sigma}(x)\geq\frac{2\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}
. By the triangle inequality (both intrinsic and Euclidean), for every
y\in B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}}^{\Sigma}(x)\subset B_{8}^{\Sigma}\setminus B_{1}^{\Sigma}
we still have

r_{\Sigma}(y)-|y|\geq\big(r_{\Sigma}(x)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}\big)-\big(|x|+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}\big)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{(1+\varepsilon)}.

(2)

On the other hand, (
1
) shows that

{\bf M}_{8}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{1}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\geq\int_{B_{8}^{\Sigma}\setminus B_{1}^{\Sigma}}\frac{r_{\Sigma}(y)-|y|}{r_{\Sigma}^{d+1}(y)}\,dy\geq\frac{1}{8^{d+1}}\int_{B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}}^{\Sigma}(x)}(r_{\Sigma}(y)-|y|)\,dy\,,

thus combined with (
2
) we find

{\bf M}_{8}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{1}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{8^{d+1}(1+\varepsilon)}|B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}}^{\Sigma}(x)|.

By
Remark

2.2
we can bound
|B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\varepsilon)}}^{\Sigma}(x)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{d}}{2^{d}(1+\varepsilon)^{d}}|B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|
, thus

{\bf M}_{8}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{1}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{d+1}}{2^{4d+3}(1+\varepsilon)^{d}}|B_{1}^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|,

reaching a contradiction for
\delta
smaller than this value.
∎

We can now give:

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The monotonicity of
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma)
is well known
[
1
]
, and
Lemma

2.1
(see also
[
15
, Proof of Proposition 2.4]
) proves the monotonicity of
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)
. In particular, the existence of both limits as
R\to\infty
(allowing for the value
+\infty
) follows. From
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\leq{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma)
(recall
Remark

1.2
) and
{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\geq\omega_{d}
(recall
Remark

2.2
), we also find

\omega_{d}\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma).

If
\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)=\infty
, there is nothing left to prove; assume then that
\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)<\infty
instead. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, and let
\delta=\delta(d,\varepsilon)>0
be given by
Lemma

2.3
. Pick
R_{0}=R_{0}(\varepsilon,\Sigma)>0
such that
\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{R_{0}}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\leq\delta
; by monotonicity, for every
R>R_{0}
we have

{\bf M}_{8R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)-{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma)\leq\delta
as well.
Lemma

2.3
—applied for
every
R>R_{0}
—shows then that
|x|\leq r_{\Sigma}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)|x|
for every
x\in\Sigma\setminus B_{2R_{0}}^{\Sigma}
, thus

B_{R}^{\Sigma}\subset i^{-1}(B_{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{N}})\subset B_{(1+\varepsilon)R}^{\Sigma}
for every
R\geq 2R_{0}
. Properness of the immersion follows (say, fixing the value
\varepsilon=1/2
), as the preimage under
i:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
of any compact set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is then contained in some intrinsic ball, hence it is also compact. Moreover, we deduce that

{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma)=R^{-d}|i^{-1}(B_{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{N}})|\leq R^{-d}|B_{(1+\varepsilon)R}^{\Sigma}|=(1+\varepsilon)^{d}{\bf M}_{(1+\varepsilon)R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma),

thus

\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm ext}(\Sigma)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{d}\lim_{R\to\infty}{\bf M}_{R}^{\rm int}(\Sigma).

Since
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
was arbitrary, sending
\varepsilon\to 0
concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

By
Theorem

1.1
,
\Sigma
also has bounded extrinsic area density, with
{\rm Area}(\Sigma\cap B_{R}^{\mathbb{R}^{7}}(p))\leq\Lambda R^{6}
for every
R>0
as well, and it is properly immersed.

[
2
, Theorem 1]
then shows that
\Sigma
is a flat hyperplane.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

By scaling invariance, we can assume that
R=1
.

Assume, for contradiction, that the result were false. There would then be a sequence of immersions
\Sigma_{k}^{6}\overset{i_{k}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
as in the statement, with—after a translation—
0\in\Sigma_{k}
, and points
x_{k}\in B_{1/2}(\Sigma_{k})
such that
|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(x_{k})|\to\infty
.
In particular,

\max_{y\in B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}}}{\rm dist}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y,\partial B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}})|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y)|\to\infty.

Let
y_{k}\in B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}}
with

{\rm dist}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k},\partial B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}})|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k})|=\max_{y\in B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}}}{\rm dist}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y,\partial B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}})|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y)|\to\infty,

and put
R_{k}:={\rm dist}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k},\partial B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}})|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k})|\to\infty
and
\lambda_{k}:=|\mathrm{II}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k})|
. Consider the “translated and rescaled” immersion
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}\overset{\widetilde{i}_{k}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
, given by the same underlying manifold
\Sigma_{k}
with new metric
g_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}=\lambda_{k}^{2}g_{\Sigma_{k}}
, and new immersion map
\widetilde{i}_{k}(x):=i_{k}(x)-i_{k}(y_{k})
(so that
\widetilde{i}_{k}(y_{k})=0
). It follows that

|\mathrm{II}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k})|=1,\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm dist}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k},\partial\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k})\geq R_{k}\to\infty,\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|\mathrm{II}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y)|\leq 2\ \mbox{ in }\ B_{R_{k}/2}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k}).

Moreover, by the assumption
|B_{1}^{\Sigma_{k}}|\leq\Lambda
and the intrinsic monotonicity formula it follows that

r^{-6}|B_{r}^{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k})|\leq(1/4)^{-6}|B_{1/4}^{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k})|\leq(1/4)^{-6}\Lambda\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for every}\hskip 10.00002ptr\in(0,{\rm dist}_{\Sigma_{k}}(y_{k},\partial B_{3/4}^{\Sigma_{k}})),

which upon rescaling gives
r^{-6}|B_{r}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k})|\leq(1/4)^{-6}\Lambda
for every
r\in(0,R_{k})
. In particular, given
M>0
, for
k
large enough so that
R_{k}>M
we have
|B_{M}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k})|\leq(1/4)^{-6}\Lambda M^{6}
, a uniform bound.

Finally, the uniform second fundamental form estimates and standard bootstrap estimates for minimal graphs give some universal
r_{0}>0
such that: for any
p\in B_{R_{k}/4}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(y_{k})
, we can write
B_{r_{0}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{k}}(p)
as an extrinsic graph with uniform
C^{l}
estimates for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Combining all of the above, the standard Arzelà–Ascoli-type theorem for immersions with uniform
C^{l}
bounds, see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
4
]
, or
[
10
, Proposition 8.2]
, shows the existence of some limiting
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}^{6}\overset{\widetilde{i}_{\infty}}{\hookrightarrow}\mathbb{R}^{7}
, which is a complete, connected, smoothly immersed, two-sided stable minimal hypersurface, with
|B_{R}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}}(p)|\leq(1/4)^{-6}\Lambda R^{6}
for some
p\in\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}
(with
i_{\infty}(p)=0
and
|\mathrm{II}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}}(p)|=1
) and every
R>0
. On the other hand,
Theorem

1.3
shows that
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}
is a hyperplane, thus
|\mathrm{II}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\infty}}(p)|=0
, a contradiction.
∎
