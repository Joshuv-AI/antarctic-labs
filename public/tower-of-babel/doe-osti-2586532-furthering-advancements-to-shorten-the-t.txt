Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

The National Renewable Energy Laboratory initiated a Prize with support from Argonne
National Laboratory (ANL), Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), and Pacific Northwest
National Laboratory (PNNL), and sponsored by the U.S. Department of Energy Water Power
Technologies Office (DOE WPTO) to encourage ideas to reduce the time to commissioning for
PSH projects. As a result, nine finalists have been chosen to develop their concepts in advance of
the FAST Prize Pitch Contest to be held on October 7, 2019. The National Labs will provide
technical and business advisement to the noted FAST Prize finalists in preparation for this Pitch
Contest.

CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies
Summary of Research Results:

The intent of this project was to support Team Gatto and Ames Construction to investigate the
application of contracting in construction and design for accelerating the development of new
pumped-storage hydro (PSH) projects in anticipation of the Furthering Advancements to Shorten
the Time (FAST) to Commissioning for Pumped Storage Hydropower (PSH) Prize Pitch Contest
which was held on October 8, 2019. Specifically, National Laboratory staff assisted the
wastewater team by (1) helping to develop innovations to the PSH design, (2) improving the
organization and structure of the business case, and (3) preparing the designated pitch man to
address questions from the expert panel.

Task 1: Technical and business support provided by the National Laboratories to develop finalist
concepts in advance of Pitch Day. The technical advisement includes narrowing the scope of
concept, light economic feasibility analysis support, light modeling support, and connecting with
appropriate industry partners. Completed by October 7, 2019.

Task 1 Results:

Technical and business support was provided by the team at the National Renewable Energy
Laboratory (NREL). This support included narrowing the scope of the concept, conducting a
preliminary economic feasibility analysis, offering initial modeling assistance, and facilitating
connections with relevant industry partners. NREL helped refine the project scope by focusing
on reducing the construction and design timelines for closed-loop pumped storage hydropower
(PSH) applications across the United States. Economic feasibility efforts involved analyzing
contracting approaches, highlighting differences between Design-Bid-Build and Design-Build
contracts, and collecting information on cutting-edge and recently developed construction
technologies. Modeling support included assessing worker productivity, modeling powerhouses,
and developing a project schedule. Finally, NREL facilitated introductions to key industry
partners, including Oracle, for potential software solutions.

Task 2: Support with preparation of the Pitch Day materials including the technical volume and
the presentation and associated pitch. The business pitch support includes coaching and
preparation for Pitch Day and connecting with appropriate industry partners. Completed by
October 7, 2019.

Task 2 Results:

The NREL team supported the development of both a technical volume and a presentation for
Pitch Day. The technical volume included a problem statement, an innovation section, a
technical feasibility section, and a market feasibility section. The problem statement identified
the key challenge: while permitting timelines for closed-loop pumped storage hydropower (PSH)
projects have shortened, the design and construction phases remain the most time-consuming
aspects. To address this, the proposed innovation emphasized the use of Design-Build (DB)
arrangements, constructability reviews, schedule consultations, and detailed cost estimates. The
technical feasibility section outlined the integration of DB contracts with well-resourced
contractors, robust project planning and management, and the use of advanced construction
equipment. The market feasibility section demonstrated that the proposed technical solutions
could reduce, rather than increase, costs and highlighted a potential pilot project in Bozeman,
Montana. The accompanying presentation included five key sections: 2019 Electrical Grid
Demand, an Overview of Pumped Storage Hydropower, Innovative Construction Contractors,
the Project Schedule, and Modern Construction Innovations.

Task 3: The Principal Investigator agrees to provide the following to DOE Office of Scientific
and Technical Information (OSTI): (1) an initial abstract suitable for public release at the time
the CRADA is executed; (2) a final report, within thirty (30) days upon completion or
termination of this CRADA, to include a list of Subject Inventions; and (3) other scientific and
technical information in any format or medium that is produced as a result of this CRADA.

Task 3 Results:

The Principal Investigator (Tessa Greco) coordinated the development of key project
deliverables, including an initial abstract suitable for public release upon execution of the
CRADA, a final report detailing subject inventions, and other relevant scientific and technical
materials. The initial abstract, titled “FAST Commissioning for Pumped-Storage Hydropower
Prize Entries”, was prepared for public dissemination. The final report is represented by the
Technical Volume developed during Task 2. Additional scientific and technical contributions
include the presentation created for Pitch Day.
