Abstract
. We compute the cohomology groups of the pure symmetric outer automorphism group Σ ​ POut ​ ( A Γ ) \Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}) and the pure symmetric automorphism group Σ ​ PAut ​ ( A Γ ) \Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}) of a right-angled Artin group A Γ A_{\Gamma} . Using the equivariant spectral sequence arising from the action of Σ ​ POut ​ ( A Γ ) \Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}) on the generalized McCullough-Miller complex MM Γ \mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma} , we show that H q ​ ( Σ ​ POut ​ ( A Γ ) ) H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})) is free abelian and we compute its rank in terms of the combinatorics of certain poset. Applying the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence and the Leray-Hirsch theorem we do the same for H q ​ ( Σ ​ PAut ​ ( A Γ ) ) H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})) . In both cases the cohomology algebra is generated in degree 1. Finally, we introduce the Generalized Brownstein-Lee Conjecture, proposing a presentation of H ∗ ​ ( Σ ​ PAut ​ ( A Γ ) ) H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})) , and prove that it holds in dimension 2 2 .

1.
Introduction

Right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs) and their automorphism groups are very active field of study in geometric group theory. Given a RAAG
{A_{\Gamma}}
, the automorphisms of
{A_{\Gamma}}
that map each standard generator to a conjugate of itself are called
pure symmetric
and form a subgroup denoted
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
that contains the subgroup of inner automorphism, so we can define the
pure symmetric outer
automorphism group as
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})\cong\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})/\mathrm{Inn}({A_{\Gamma}})
. In the case when
A_{\Gamma}
is the free group
F_{n}
,
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n})
is the fundamental group of the configuration space of
n
unknotted, unlinked circles in the sphere
S^{3}

[
9
]
, there is also a version of this for symmetric automorphisms of other right-angled Artin groups
[
3
]
.

In
[
15
]
, McCullough and Miller constructed a contractible simplicial complex
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
that admits an action of the group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
with free abelian stabilizers. The construction is based on the Whitehead poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{n}
, whose elements can be expressed as labeled trees. In
[
9
]
, Jensen, McCammond and Meier use the McCullough-Miller complex to compute
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n}))
and
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n}))
, the cohomology groups of the (outer) pure symmetric automorphisms of the free group; even more, they prove the Brownstein-Lee conjecture
[
5
]
, which stablish a presentation of the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(F_{n}))
.

In
[
1
]
, Ardaiz-Gale and Martínez-Pérez generalized both the Whitehead poset and the construction of McCullough and Miller from the case of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(F_{n})
to
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
. This new complex,
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
, admits an action of the group
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
with free abelian stabilizers and it is also contractible. The powerful combinatorial machinery derived from the labeled trees in
\mathrm{Wh}_{n}
is the key to prove the main results in
[
9
]
. However, vertices in
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
cannot be expressed as labeled trees in general, only as families of based partitions, so most arguments need to be adapted.

In this paper we will generalize some combinatorial results from
\mathrm{Wh}_{n}
to
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
and use the equivariant spectral sequence derived from the
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
action over
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
to compute the cohomology groups
H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and
H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
. We show (see Sections
2
and
4
for definitions):

Theorem A
.

Let
K_{q}
be the number of rank
q
essential vertex types in
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
, then

H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))=\mathbb{Z}^{K_{q}}.

Besides, the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is generated by the one-dimensional classes
\bar{\gamma}_{I}^{j}
, where the
C_{I}^{j}
are canonical automorphisms.

Theorem B
.

Let
K_{i}
be the number of rank
i
essential vertex types in
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
and
N_{j}
the number of
j
-cliques in
\Gamma
, then

H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=q}\mathbb{Z}^{K_{i}\cdot N_{j}}.

Besides, the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is generated in degree 1.

Furthermore, we generalize the relations from the Brownstein-Lee Conjecture to the case of RAAGs, formulate the Generalized Brownstein-Lee Conjecture, and prove that it holds in dimension 2.

Conjecture A

(Generalized Brownstein-Lee Conjecture)
.

Let
\gamma_{A}^{i}
be a one-dimensional class in

H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
, where
A
is a connected component in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
. For each pair
v_{i},v_{j}
of two non-adjacent vertices in
\Gamma
, let
D^{j},D^{i}
be the respective dominant components and
\mathcal{C}
a shared component. Then
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
is generated by the one-dimensional classes
\gamma_{A}^{i}
, modulo the following relations for each shared component
\mathcal{C}
of
v_{i},v_{j}
.

(1)

\gamma_{A}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{A}^{i}=0

(2)

\gamma_{A}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{B}^{j}=-\gamma_{B}^{j}\wedge\gamma_{A}^{i}

(3)

\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}=0

(4)

\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}=\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}+\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}

We will denote by
R
the ring given by the presentation in the previous conjecture.

Theorem C
.

There exists a ring epimorphism

\phi:H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))\twoheadrightarrow R

which is an isomorphism in degrees 1 and 2.

Another important evidence about this conjecture can be derived from the work of Martínez-Pérez and Mendonça in
[
14
]
.
In Theorem A in that paper it is shown that when the graph
\Gamma
does not contain four pairwise non-adjacent vertices
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in\Gamma
that lie in four distinct connected components of
\Gamma-\cap_{i=1}^{4}\mathrm{lk}(v_{i})
, then the group
A_{\Gamma}
is of Koszul type, implying that
\mathcal{U}_{\Gamma}
, the universal enveloping algebra of the rational Lie algebra associated to the descending central series of the group is quadratic and has as quadratic dual the cohomology ring of the group. It turns out
[
14
]
that for arbitrary
\Gamma
the quadratic dual of
\mathcal{U}_{\Gamma}
is precisely the ring with the presentation of Conjecture A. So this implies that if
\Gamma
satisfies the condition above, then the rational version of Conjecture A holds true.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we explain some basic results about MM
Γ
, obtained from
[
1
]
. In Section
3
, we go over the main definitions in
[
9
]
. In Section
4
we generalize the notion of
one-two automorphism
which plays a key role in
[
9
]
and do some technical work that will be needed later. In Section
5
we apply the previous results to the spectral sequence derived from the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
on MM
Γ
to compute
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
and prove Theorem A. In Section
6
, we use the Lyndon-Hoschild-Serre spectral sequence associated to
1\to A_{\Gamma}\to\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})\to 1
and the Leray-Hirsch Theorem to compute the
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
groups and prove Theorem B. In Section
7
we take the first steps into proving the Brownstein-Lee conjecture for
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
and we prove Theorem C.

2.
The McCullough-Miller complex

In this chapter we will explain some basic results about the McCullough-Miller complex for RAAGs. A more detailed explanation can be found at
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Graph)
.

We will use
\Gamma
to denote a finite simplicial graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)
and set of edges
E(\Gamma)
. Very often we will identify
\Gamma
or a subgraph of
\Gamma
with its vertex set. Let
v\in V(\Gamma)
,
\mathrm{lk}(v)
denotes the link of
v
, which is the subgraph induced by the vertices adjacent to
v
. We will define the star of
v
,
\mathrm{st}(v)
, as the subgraph induced by
\mathrm{lk}(v)\cup\{v\}
.

Associated to
\Gamma
we may define a group as follows.

Definition 2.2

(RAAG)
.

The RAAG (right-angled Artin group) defined by a simplicial graph
\Gamma
is the group with presentation

A_{\Gamma}=\langle v\in V(\Gamma)\mid[v,w]=1\textrm{ whenever }\{v,w\}\in E(\Gamma)\rangle

This is,
{A_{\Gamma}}
is the group generated by the vertices of
\Gamma
, which are called
standard generators
so that two standard generators commute if and only if they are adjacent in
\Gamma
. Two simple examples are the extreme cases: if
\Gamma
is a graph with no edges, then
A_{\Gamma}
is a free group, and if
\Gamma
a complete graph, then
A_{\Gamma}
is a free abelian group.

2.1.
Pure symmetric automorphisms of RAAGs

Definition 2.3

(Pure symmetric (outer)automorphisms)
.

Let
A_{\Gamma}
be a RAAG. The group of pure symmetric automorphisms
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
of
A_{\Gamma}
is the group consisting of those automorphisms of
A_{\Gamma}
that map each standard generator (i.e., each vertex of
\Gamma
) to a conjugate of itself. Obviously, it contains the inner automorphism and the corresponding external version is denoted
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})=\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})/\mathrm{Inn}(A_{\Gamma}).

Next, we introduce an important family of elements of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Definition 2.4

(Partial conjugations)
.

Given
v\in L=V(\Gamma)\cup V(\Gamma)^{-1}
and
A
a union of connected components of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v)
, the
partial conjugation
of
A
by
v
is the automorphism
C_{A}^{v}
given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}C_{A}^{v}(w)=vwv^{-1},\text{ if }w\in A\\
C_{A}^{v}(z)=z,\text{ if }z\notin A\end{array}\right.

where
w,z\in\Gamma
.

Theorem 2.5

(Laurence
[
12
]
)
.

The set of partial conjugations is a generating system for
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Remark
.

For the rest of the paper, we will only consider RAAGs
{A_{\Gamma}}
such that
\Gamma
has no dominating vertex. Equivalently, RAAGs with trivial center. There is no loss of generality in this assumption, which will be helpful in Section
6
, because, by Laurence’s Theorem,
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})\cong\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma^{\prime}})
if
\Gamma
is obtained from
\Gamma^{\prime}
by removing all the dominating vertices.

When we are working with non adjacent vertices
u
and
v
, it will be really useful to think about
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
in terms of graph components. We will use the next notation due to Day and Wade
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.6

(Graph components: shared, dominant and subordinate)
.

Let
u,v\in\Gamma
be not linked. A connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
that is also a connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v)
is said to be
shared
. The unique component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
that contains
v
is called the
dominant
component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
respect to
v
, we will denote it by
D^{v}
when
u
is clear. Finally, a
subordinate
component is any connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
that is contained in the dominant component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v)
respect to
u
. By Lemma 2.1 in
[
7
]
, any connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
is of one of these types. Observe that if
A
is a connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
which is either shared or subordinate, then
A\cap\mathrm{st}(v)=\emptyset
.

Using the terminology in the previous definition, one can rewrite the presentation of Koban and Piggott (
[
10
]
) as follows:

Theorem 2.7
.

The group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
is the group generated by all partial conjugations
C_{A}^{u}
for
u\in\Gamma
and
A
a connected component of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(u)
, subject to the following relations:

i)

\left[C_{A}^{u},C_{B}^{v}\right]=1
if
u\in\mathrm{st}(v)
,

ii)

\left[C_{A}^{u},C_{B}^{v}\right]=1
if
u\notin\mathrm{st}(v)
and either
A
and
B
are shared but distinct or at least one of
A,B
is subordinate,

iii)

\left[C_{A}^{u}C_{B}^{u},C_{A}^{v}\right]=1
if
u\notin\mathrm{st}(v)
,
A
is shared and
B
dominant.

Definition 2.8

(Valid based partitions, petals and length)
.

Let
u\in\Gamma
be a vertex. A
\Gamma
-valid based partition with operative factor
u
is a partition of the form
\tau_{u}=\{\{u\},P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\}
of the set
\Gamma-\mathrm{lk}(u)
so that each
P_{i}
is a union of connected components. Each
P_{i}
is called a petal of the based partition. The number of petals,
k
, is the

length
of the based partition, and we denote it
l(\tau_{u})
.

From now on, every based partition we use will be a
\Gamma
-admissible based partition, for simplicity we will simply say based partition if the graph
\Gamma
is clear.

Definition 2.9

(Crossings, disjoint partition and compatibility)
.

We say that two based partitions
\tau_{u}
and
\tau_{v}

cross
if
u\notin\mathrm{st}(v)
and there are petals
P
of
\tau_{u}
and
Q
of
\tau_{v}
such that
D^{v}\not\subset P
,
D^{u}\not\subset Q
, and
P\cap Q\neq\emptyset
. It is easy to check that this is equivalent to asking that there is at least one shared component
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq P
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq Q
.
We will say that two based partitions
\tau_{u},\tau_{v}
are
disjoint
if
u\notin\mathrm{st}(v)
and they do not cross
and we say that they are
compatible
if they are disjoint or
u\in\mathrm{st}(v)
.

Definition 2.10

(Vertex type)
.

We define a
vertex type

\tau=\{\tau_{v_{1}},...,\tau_{v_{n}}\}
as a collection of pairwise compatible based partitions, such that there is one for each vertex in
\Gamma
.

We will say that two vertex types
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
are
compatible
if their based partitions are pairwise compatible. And we will say that a collection of vertex types is
compatible
if they are pairwise compatible.

Remark
.

Here we are using a slightly different notation from
[
1
]
, where vertex types where denoted
\underline{\underline{A}}
, and a based partition
\underline{A}_{u}
. The extensive notation in
[
1
]
is forced by the technical work done there. Here, this simpler version will be enough.

Definition 2.11

(Order)
.

Let
v\in\Gamma
and
\tau_{v}
,
\tau^{\prime}_{v}
two based partitions with operative factor
v
. We say that
\tau_{v}\leq\tau_{v}^{\prime}
if every petal of
\tau_{v}^{\prime}
is contained in a petal of
\tau_{v}
, i.e., when every petal of
\tau_{v}
is a union of petals of
\tau_{v}^{\prime}
. In particular, if
\tau_{v}\leq\tau_{v}^{\prime}
, then
l(\tau_{v})\leq l(\tau^{\prime}_{v})
.
For vertex types, we will say that
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
if
\tau_{v}\leq\tau^{\prime}_{v},\ \textrm{ for every }v\in V(\Gamma)
.

We have the next Lemma from
[
1
, Lemma 4.5.]
:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
be vertex types with
\tau<\tau^{\prime}
, then
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
are compatible.

Definition 2.13

(
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset)
.

The
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset
Wh
Γ
is the poset formed by all the vertex types associated to
\Gamma
under the partial order previously defined.

Definition 2.14

(Nuclear vertex)
.

The
nuclear vertex
type, denoted
\mathcal{O}
, is the vertex type such that for each
v\in\Gamma
the associated based partition is trivial, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}_{v}=\{\{v\},P\}
, where
P=\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v)
. It is the unique minimal element in the
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
poset.

Definition 2.15

(Rank of a vertex type)
.

We denote by
l(\tau_{v})
the length of a based partition, i.e., the number of petals it has. Then, the
rank of a vertex type
\tau
is

\mathrm{rk}(\tau)=\displaystyle\sum_{v\in\Gamma}(l(\tau_{v})-1)

Definition 2.16

(Carried automorphism)
.

The group of automorphisms carried by a based partition
\tau_{u}
is the subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
generated by the partial conjugations
C^{u}_{I}
where
I
runs over the petals of
\tau_{u}
. The group of automorphisms carried by a vertex type
\tau
is the subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
generated by the automorphisms carried by each based partition
\tau_{u}\in\tau
.

The following is an easy observation that will be useful later.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
be a vertex type. The subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
carried by
\tau
contains the inner automorphisms. Moreover, given two vertex types
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
such that
\tau<\tau^{\prime}
, the subgroup of automorphisms carried by
\tau
is inside the subgroup of automorphisms carried by
\tau^{\prime}
.

In what follows, we will often consider the subgroups of outer automorphisms carried by either a given based partition or a vertex type, which are just the corresponding quotients over
\mathrm{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Definition 2.18

(Marked vertex type and the action of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
)
.

We say that a generating system
X
of
A_{\Gamma}
is a
basis
if there is some
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
such that
X=\alpha(V(\Gamma))
.
A
marked based partition
is a pair
(X,\tau_{u})
where
X
is an ordered basis of
A_{\Gamma}
and
\tau_{u}
is a based partition and a
marked vertex type

(X,\tau)
is the family of marked based partitions
(X,\tau_{u})
where
\tau_{u}
runs over the set of based partitions of a vertex type
\tau
. By marking a based partition
\tau_{u}
we understand that we are relabeling the elements in the partition using
X
(via the automorphism
\alpha
). We extend the partial order of Definition
2.11
to marked vertex types as follows:
(X_{1},\tau)\leq(X_{2},\tau^{\prime})
if
X_{1}=X_{2}
and
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
. The marked vertex types form a poset and the group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on this poset via

\alpha(X,\tau)=(\alpha(X),\tau)

where
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
.
We say that
\alpha
is
carried by a marked vertex type

(X,\tau)
if
\gamma\alpha\gamma^{-1}
is carried by
\tau
where
\gamma(X)=V(\Gamma)
.

Next, we define an equivalence relationship in the set of marked vertex types as follows.

Definition 2.19
.

We say that two marked vertex types
(X_{1},\tau)
and
(X_{2},\tau)
are equivalent and we put
(X_{1},\tau)\equiv(X_{2},\tau)
if
there exists an
\alpha\in\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
carried by
(X_{1},\tau)
such that
\alpha(X_{1})=X_{2}
. We will denote by
[X_{1},\tau]
the equivalence class of
(X_{1},\tau)
under this relation.

Definition 2.20

(
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset)
.

The
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset
is the poset formed by the set of equivalence classes under the equivalence relationship
\equiv
defined above with the induced partial order.
The group
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts on this poset via

\alpha([X,\tau])=[\alpha(X),\tau]

and this action factors through the epimorphism
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\to\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
so we also have an action of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

The
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset is formed by copies of the
\Gamma
-Whitehead poset attached to each other through the equivalence relation
\equiv
. It is easy to verify that nuclear vertex types only carry inner automorphisms, so there is a bijection between the set of nuclear vertices
[X,\mathcal{O}]
in the
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset and the set of conjugacy classes of bases of
A_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 2.21

(
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
complex)
.

We define the
McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
for a RAAG
A_{\Gamma}
as the simplicial realization of the
\Gamma
-McCullough-Miller poset. In the case when
{A_{\Gamma}}
is the free group with rank
n
we will denote this complex by
\mathrm{MM}_{n}
.

Remark
.

The definition of the action above implies that the stabilizer
H=\mathrm{Stab}_{\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})}[V(\Gamma),\tau]
of the vertex
[V(\Gamma),\tau]
under the
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
action is precisely the subgroup generated by those automorphisms carried by the vertex type
\tau
. This implies that the stabilizer of the vertex
[X,\tau]
is
\alpha H\alpha^{-1}
where
\alpha
is such that
X=\alpha(V(\Gamma))
. It is proved in
[
1
, Theorem A]
that these stabilizers are free abelian, that their rank can be computed in terms of the length of the based partitions of
\tau
and that they yield in fact all the cell stabilizers of the McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
.

Taking this into account, one sees that the McCullough-Miller complex
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
can be also defined as follows. Recall that for a poset
\mathcal{F}
of subgroups of a group
G
, one can define a new poset, called the
coset poset
, as

C(\mathcal{F})=\{gH\mid H\in\mathcal{F},g\in G\}.

Then, the McCullough-Miller poset is the coset poset of the family

\{\mathrm{Stab}_{\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})}[V(\Gamma),\tau]\mid\tau\text{ vertex type}\}

of subgroups of
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
and
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
is the associated geometric realization.

Definition 2.22

(Stab
(\tau)
)
.

We denote by Stab
(\tau)
the stabilizer of the vertex type
\tau
of
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
under the
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma})
action. By definition, we have that Stab
(\tau)
is the external version of the subgroup of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
carried by
\tau
. If
\sigma
is a simplex in
|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}|
corresponding to a chain
\tau^{0}<\cdots<\tau^{k}
then the stabilizer of
\sigma
is the stabilizer of
\tau^{0}
so we have
\mathrm{Stab}(\sigma)=\mathrm{Stab}(\tau^{0})
.

As explained in the previous remark, the stabilizer of a vertex type is a free abelian group. Even more, the rank of a vertex type
\tau
as an element of the poset
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
coincides with the rank of Stab
(\tau)
as a free abelian group.

3.
The
one-two basis

Our main goal is to compute
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}(A_{\Gamma}))
, and we will do it using an equivariant spectral sequence for the
\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}})
action on
\mathrm{MM}_{\Gamma}
. If a group acts simplicially on a contractible complex, then the first page of this equivariant spectral sequence can be computed in terms of the cell stabilizers (Chapter VII, Proposition 7.3 in
[
4
]
). A key step in this process is choosing a
good
basis for these stabilizers. We will do it in a way that generalizes the choice of
[
9
]
.

Recall that, as explained in the Remark after Definition
3.1
, for a vertex type
\tau
, we cannot split petals arbitrarily if we want to obtain another vertex type. A petal
I
in a based partition
\tau_{i}
of
\tau
might not admit any split because of two possible reasons:

a)

The petal is formed by a single connected component in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
, so it cannot be divided anymore.

b)

There is another based partition
\tau_{j}
in
\tau
, such that
v_{i},v_{j}
have a shared component; and the splitting of the petal in
\tau_{i}
containing the dominant component
D^{j}
produces a crossing with
\tau_{j}
, breaking the compatibility.

We are now ready to generalize the idea of essential vertices:

Definition 4.14

(Essential)
.

A collection of compatible canonical outer-automorphisms
\mathcal{A}
is
essential
if
\tau=\tau(\mathcal{A})
satisfies that for each based partition
\tau_{i}
the only petal we can split to get a new vertex type is the one containing the minimal element in
\Gamma-
st
(v_{i})
. In this case we say that
\tau
is an
essential
vertex type.
Collections of compatible canonical outer-automorphisms or vertex types that are not essential are called
inessential
.

Let us explain the previous definition: the canonical basis is defined using the partial conjugations associated to the petals that do not contain the minimal element. If we split a petal which does not contain the minimal element, then the canonical basis will change. If we have an essential collection
\mathcal{A}
, then for each
\tau^{\prime}
such that
\tau(\mathcal{A})\leq\tau^{\prime}
we have that
\tau^{\prime}
supports
\mathcal{A}
, since the petals that defined the canonical outer-automorphisms in
\mathcal{A}
cannot be divided. So, in a simple way, having an essential collection
\mathcal{A}
ensures that if
\tau
supports
\mathcal{A}
and
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
, then
\tau^{\prime}
also supports
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of compatible canonical outer-automorphisms. Then
\mathcal{A}
is essential if and only if the complex
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
is empty and if and only if
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
is a subcomplex of
|\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}|
. In this case, moreover,
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A})=\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
so
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
is contractible.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be essential,
\tau=\tau(\mathcal{A})
and
\bar{C}_{I}^{j}\in\mathcal{A}
. By definition of canonical and essential, the petal
I
in the based partition
\tau_{j}
does not contain the minimal element, so it cannot be divided. Since every vertex
\tau^{\prime}
with
\tau<\tau^{\prime}
is obtained by splitting petals in
\tau
, we have that in the based partition
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
we will still have the petal
I
(since it cannot be divided), so
\bar{C}_{I}^{j}\in\mathcal{B}(\tau^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}(\tau^{\prime})
.

For the converse, if
\mathcal{A}
is inessential, there must be at least one petal
I
in a based partition
\tau_{j}
which can be split and does not contain the minimal element in
\Gamma-
st
(v_{j})
. So, if we divide that petal into
I_{1},I_{2},
we will obtain a
\tau^{\prime}>\tau
such that
\bar{C}_{I}^{j}=\bar{C}_{I_{1}}^{j}\bar{C}_{I_{2}}^{j}\in
Stab
(\tau^{\prime})
but
\bar{C}_{I}^{j}\notin\mathcal{B}(\tau^{\prime})
, so
\tau^{\prime}
is in the
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
complex, so it cannot be empty.

For the second equivalence, as explained in the previous paragraph,
\mathcal{A}
is essential if and only if when
\tau
supports
\mathcal{A}
and
\tau\leq\tau^{\prime}
, then
\tau^{\prime}
also supports
\mathcal{A}
. As a consequence, if
\sigma=\tau^{0}<\cdots<\tau^{k}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
, then for any
\tau^{i}
we have that
\tau^{i}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

We now turn to the case when
\mathcal{A}
is inessential. It makes sense to think that if we take an inessential vertex type and we keep splitting its petals, at some point we should get an essential vertex type. We aim to do this process in an ordered way.

Definition 4.16

(Worrisome petal)
.

Let
\tau
be an inessential vertex type. We call a petal in a based partition
\tau_{i}
of
\tau

worrisome
if it can be split and it does not contain the minimal element in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
.

Remark
.

Observe that if
\tau
is inessential, there must be at least one worrisome petal; and if it is essential there is none. Further, we know that if
\tau=\tau(\mathcal{A})
is inessential, then
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
is not empty, and now we have another characterization: let
\tau^{\prime}
be a vertex type, then
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
\tau^{\prime}>\tau
and at least one of the worrisome petals in
\tau
is split in
\tau^{\prime}
.

We will state some technical lemmas about worrisome petals:

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
\tau
be an inessential vertex type and
\tau_{i}=\{\{v_{i}\},P_{1},...,P_{k}\}
,
\tau_{j}=\{\{v_{j}\},Q_{1},...,Q_{m}\}
two based partitions of
\tau
such that
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are not adjacent. Let us suppose that
P_{1}
contains
D^{j}
and
Q_{1}
contains
D^{i}
, the respective dominant components. Then,
P_{1}
and
Q_{1}
cannot both be worrisome.

Proof.

We have two different cases:

(i)

The minimal elements in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
and
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{j})
are the same: In this case the minimal element is either in the dominant component of one of them (and in a subordinate component of the other) or in a shared component. If it is in the dominant of one of them, say
D^{j}
, then
P_{1}
is not worrisome since it contains the minimal element and we have finished. If it is in a shared component, we have that the shared component must lie in the same petal as the dominant component in at least one of the two based partitions, if not a cross would occur between them, contradicting the fact that the based partitions are compatible; so that petal is not worrisome.

(ii)

The minimal element in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
is different from the one in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{j})
: We can assume that the smallest one is the one in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
. This means that the vertex with the smallest label in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
is in
\mathrm{st}(v_{j})
, as a consequence, it is in
D^{j}
, so
P_{1}
is not worrisome.

∎

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
\tau
be an inessential vertex type and
\tau_{i}=\{\{v_{i}\},I,P_{1},...,P_{k}\}
,
\tau_{j}=\{\{v_{j}\},J,Q_{1},...,Q_{m}\}
two based partitions of
\tau
, such that both
I,J
are worrisome petals. Let
\tau^{\prime}_{i}=\{\{v_{i}\},I_{1},I_{2},P_{1},...,P_{k}\}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{j}=\{\{v_{j}\},J_{1},J_{2},Q_{1},...,Q_{m}\}
be based partitions obtained by splitting the worrisome petals in an admissible way. Then
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
and
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
are compatible.

Proof.

The case when
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
are adjacent is trivial, since based partitions associated to adjacent vertices are always compatible. We study the case when they are not adjacent. Since
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
is obtained by an admissible split, it means that it is compatible with al the based partitions of
\tau
, in particular, it is compatible with
\tau_{j}
. By the same reason,
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
is compatible with
\tau_{i}
. By Lemma
4.17
, we have that at least one of the worrisome petals does not contain the dominant component.

By symmetry we assume that
I
is the one not containing
D^{j}
. Let us suppose that
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
is not compatible with
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
, i.e., there is crossing between them. By Lemma 5.11 in
[
1
]
, if
\tau_{i}
is compatible with
\tau^{\prime}_{j}
but
\tau^{\prime}_{i}
is not, then the petal containing the dominant component in
\tau_{i}
is split, but this is a contradiction, since the only petal that we have split is
I
, and
I
does not contain the dominant component.

∎

In general, for a vertex type
\tau
, splitting a petal in a based partition
\tau_{i}
of
\tau
might imply that we cannot split some other petal in another based partition
\tau_{j}
of
\tau
, because crossings might appear. The previous lemma gives us a really important result for the rest of the section: it says that, if we have an inessential vertex type, splitting a worrisome petal does not impede another worrisome petal from being split.

Lemma 4.19
.

Let
\tau
be an inessential vertex type in
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
. Then, there exists a unique essential vertex type
\widehat{\tau}>\tau
obtained by splitting only the worrisome petals in
\tau
.

Proof.

We will start by constructing
\widehat{\tau}
. We proceed in the following way: we take each based partition
\tau_{i}
of
\tau
, and we split each worrisome petal in
\tau_{i}
as much as possible, until there is no worrisome petal left. By Lemma
4.18
, the previous procedure is independent of the order in which we split the petals, so we will always get to the vertex type
\widehat{\tau}
, which is essential, since it has no worrisome petal.
∎

Definition 4.20

(Essential cover)
.

We define the
essential cover
of an inessential vertex type
\tau
as the unique essential vertex type
\widehat{\tau}
obtained by splitting only the worrisome petals in
\tau
.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an inessential collection of compatible canonical outer-automorphisms. Then the complex
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
is contractible.

Proof.

Let
\tau=\tau(\mathcal{A})
, and let
\mathfrak{t}>\tau
be a vertex type obtained from
\tau
by splitting at least one worrisome petal, and only worrisome petals. Let
\mathrm{Wh}_{\geq\mathfrak{t}}
be the subposet of
\mathrm{Wh}_{\Gamma}
consisting of vertex types greater than or equal to
\mathfrak{t}
. We have that the associated subcomplexes
|\mathrm{Wh}_{\geq\mathfrak{t}}|
cover
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
, since in every vertex type in
\mathrm{Peripheral}(\mathcal{A})
at least one of the worrisome petals in
\tau
has been split (recall the remark after Definition

7.
The cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}}))

In this section we will give a ring presentation that we conjecture gives the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}}))
. We also prove some partial results that provide some evidence for this conjecture.
Let
i,j
be two vertices which are not adjacent in
\Gamma
, let
D^{j},D^{i}
be the respective dominant components of
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
,
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{j})
and
\mathcal{C}
a shared component. As in Section
5
, we denote by
\gamma_{A}^{i}
the dual class
[C_{A}^{i}]^{*}
. We want to prove that the next two relations hold in

:

(1)

\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}=0

(2)

\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}=\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}+\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}

Proposition 7.1
.

With the previous notation, relation
(1)
holds in
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
.

Proof.

We will prove the relation through an epimorphism from
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
to
F_{2}
, the free group generated by
\{x,y\}
. We define

\varphi_{1}:\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\rightarrow F_{2}

induced by

C_{D^{j}}^{i}\mapsto x,\quad C_{D^{i}}^{j}\mapsto y,\quad\alpha\mapsto 1\textrm{ for }\alpha\textrm{ any other generator. }

Taking into account the presentation of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}({A_{\Gamma}})
given in Theorem
iii)
, we see that there is no possible commutation between
C_{D^{j}}^{i}
and
C_{D^{i}}^{j}
so this map extends to a well defined group homomorphism. The map
\varphi_{1}
induces a homomorphism
\overline{\varphi}_{1}^{*}
between the first cohomology groups:

\overline{\varphi}_{1}^{*}:H^{1}(F_{2})\rightarrow H^{1}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})).

With a suitable order in the generators of
\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})
,
\overline{\varphi}_{1}
can be represented by the following matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&1&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}

so, dualizing,
\overline{\varphi}_{1}^{*}
corresponds to the transpose,
which means
\overline{\varphi}_{1}^{*}(x^{*})=\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}
and
\overline{\varphi}_{1}^{*}(y^{*})=\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}
.
In

, we have that
x^{*}\wedge y^{*}=0
; and the naturality of the cup product with respect to group homomorphisms implies

\varphi_{1}^{*}(x^{*}\wedge y^{*})=\varphi_{1}^{*}(x^{*})\wedge\varphi_{1}^{*}(y^{*})=\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}\wedge\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}=0.

∎

Proposition 7.2
.

With the notation established before, relation
(2)
also holds in
H^{*}(\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma}))
.

Proof.

We argue as in Proposition
7.1
. We define

\varphi_{2}:\Sigma\mathrm{PAut}(A_{\Gamma})\rightarrow F_{2}

induced by

C_{D^{j}}^{i}\mapsto x,\quad C_{D^{i}}^{j}\mapsto y,\quad C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\mapsto x^{-1},\quad C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}\mapsto y^{-1},\quad\alpha\mapsto 1\textrm{ for }\alpha\textrm{ any other generator. }

We will verify again that this extends to a well defined group homomorphism. Taking into account the presentation given in Theorem
iii)
, we see that the only relations that involve the preimages of

and

are
[C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}C_{D^{j}}^{i},C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}]=1
and
[C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}C_{D^{i}}^{j},C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}]=1
. It is enough to check that
\varphi_{2}(C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}C_{D^{j}}^{i}C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j})=\varphi_{2}(C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}C_{D^{j}}^{i})
(the other relation will follow by symmetry):

\varphi_{2}(C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}C_{D^{j}}^{i}C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j})=x^{-1}xy^{-1}=y^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}x=\varphi_{2}(C_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}C_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}C_{D^{j}}^{i}).

\overline{\varphi}_{2}^{*}(x^{*})=\gamma_{D^{j}}^{i}-\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{i}\ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \overline{\varphi}_{2}^{*}(y^{*})=\gamma_{D^{i}}^{j}-\gamma_{\mathcal{C}}^{j}.

By the properties of the cup product

∎

In general, not much is known about these groups. In the case of a RAAG

The injection

Appendix A
Algorithm to compute the
K_{q}
:

The main theorems of this paper (Theorem A and B) give us a formula to compute the cohomology groups
H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}}))
and
H^{q}(\Sigma\mathrm{POut}({A_{\Gamma}}))
. In the case when
{A_{\Gamma}}
is the free group

, it is possible to give a closed formula in terms of

only (Lemma 5.2 in
[
9
]
). However, that cannot be done for arbitrary RAAGs; we can, nonetheless, implement an algorithm that can be used for explicit computations.

The idea is simple: for each vertex

, we take all the connected components in
\Gamma-\mathrm{st}(v_{i})
and combine them in all possible ways to obtain
\mathcal{P}_{i}
, the set of the based partitions associated to each
v_{i}
. Then, we consider the cartesian product of the
\mathcal{P}_{i}
’s. We have to remove from the cartesian product the elements that are not vertex types, so for each SIL-pair
(v,w)
we check which based partitions cross and we remove all the elements which have those two based partitions at the same time.

Once we have all the vertex types, we select those of rank
q
and check if there is some based partition which does not contain the minimal element and admits a split; if there is none, then the vertex type is essential.
