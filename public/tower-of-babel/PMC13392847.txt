Improving Infectious Disease Response and Management Capabilities in the Korean Military
Authors: Hyelim Lee, Minku Kang, Sodam Lee, Donghoon Kim, Jong Youn Moon, Changgyo Yoon, Dong Yeop Lee, Jae Hyoung Im, Jaehun Jung, Yuna Kim, Dong Yoon Kang, 혜림 이, 민구 강, 소담 이, 동훈 김, 종윤 문, 창교 윤, 동엽 이, 재형 임, 재훈 정, 윤아 김, 동윤 강
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesMilitary units are highly vulnerable to infectious disease outbreaks because of communal living and frequent field training, yet military infectious disease education remains largely theory-based, limiting practical response capacity. This study aimed to (1) assess current practices and educational needs in military field settings, (2) develop a scenario-based education and training program reflecting the military context, and (3) provide evidence for a standardized prevention and management system through pilot testing and satisfaction surveys.MethodsFocus group interviews (FGIs) were conducted with 37 completers of the 2025 military epidemiological investigator course to identify field practices and needs. Based on the findings, problem-based learning (PBL) materials were developed for seven infectious diseases. A tabletop-exercise pilot was delivered to 40 Navy and Air Force infection control personnel, and field applicability was evaluated through post-training self-report questionnaires and qualitative feedback. A civilian–government–military advisory board provided stepwise consultation throughout.ResultsFGIs identified four major barriers: guideline ambiguity, insufficient resources and tools, excessive workload from dual reporting, and rapid knowledge decay after short training. In the pilot, 95–100% of participants (n=40) responded positively across all six self-reported criteria, including competency, military relevance, and educational efficiency. Qualitative feedback identified procedural clarity and cross-unit experience sharing as key satisfaction drivers.ConclusionsField-oriented, scenario-based PBL can strengthen military infectious disease response beyond theory alone. This requires standardizing military epidemiological investigation and reporting, institutionalizing routine provision and operation of essential resources, and establishing a standing civilian–government–military body and joint practical training to offset military-only resource constraints.

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Military units face high infectious disease risk, and since coronavirus disease 2019 have conducted unit-level education and drills, but theory-based training limits field response capability.

② What new information is presented?

Through focus group interviews with 37 military epidemiological investigator trainees, we identified four barriers (guideline ambiguity, resource shortage, dual reporting, knowledge decay) and developed seven scenario-based problem-based learning modules; a pilot with 40 Navy and Air Force personnel improved satisfaction via procedural clarity and cross-unit experience sharing.

③ What are implications?

Standardizing epidemiological investigation and reporting, ensuring routine resource operation, and institutionalizing a civilian–government–military body with joint practical training could form a phased strategy for military infectious disease policy.

Introduction

As outbreaks of infectious diseases, including waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases, continue to occur within military units, the importance of interrupting transmission and of proactive prevention and management in military congregate settings is being further emphasized. Military populations, in particular, have environmental characteristics that involve living in close quarters in confined spaces such as barracks, making interpersonal distancing difficult, and that place them at a relatively high risk of exposure to vector-borne or zoonotic infectious diseases due to frequent outdoor training [1-3]. Preventing infectious disease outbreaks within the military can be considered a key preventive medicine and public health challenge, as it goes beyond the health problems of individual service members and is directly linked to a decrease in military combat readiness and a gap in national security [4].

The domestic military infectious disease management system is operated in accordance with the Military Infectious Disease Prevention and Management Operations Directive and the Ministry of National Defense Infectious Disease Crisis Response Manual, and under the responsibility of the Commander, Armed Forces Medical Command, the overall management of tasks related to infectious disease prevention, establishment and implementation of countermeasures, and evaluation of results are conducted [5,6]. Additionally, the Chief of Staff of each service (Chief Medical Officer) designates personnel responsible for infectious disease prevention and management to establish and implement plans and to conduct education and public information for service members. Currently, infectious disease education within the military is centered on education at institutions under the Armed Forces Medical Command, unit-level public health education, and some simulation exercises. Furthermore, linked education with national-level specialized infectious disease educational institutions and the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) is primarily implemented for Ministry of National Defense central epidemiological investigators, military public health and medical personnel, and personnel in charge of infectious disease prevention and management duties. Training for military medical personnel is primarily composed of theory-based courses, and considering the personnel structure with a high proportion of short-term service military medical officers, systematic support measures to improve proficiency need to be continuously reviewed in conjunction.

Major military institutions in other countries are expanding training that recreates real-world situations, focusing on scenario-based hands-on training and simulation training. For example, the United States develops vaccines and therapeutics for high-risk pathogens in biosafety facilities centered on the U.S. Army Medical Research Institute of Infectious Diseases and the Walter Reed Army Institute of Research, and operates the civilian-military integrated clinical proficiency sustainment program, periodically dispatching military medical personnel to civilian tertiary general hospitals, thereby overcoming the differences in clinical environments between wartime and peacetime and maintaining practical medical response capabilities. The United Kingdom is conducting clinically focused CBRN response training led by the Defence CBRN Centre and operates a specialized course for military medical personnel that goes beyond basic survival training to develop the ability to provide trauma care and advanced resuscitation in contaminated environments. In summary, major overseas military institutions provide realism-enhanced education and training that include scenario-based hands-on training, mastery of clinical skills using simulated patients and equipment, short-term intensive training, and on-site diagnostics and transport techniques. Moreover, the use of technologies such as medical simulators, standardized patients, and virtual reality to strengthen on-site response capabilities and clinical proficiency is becoming increasingly widespread.

Infectious disease education in the domestic military has primarily consisted of didactic instruction based on existing educational materials and some hands-on training, but the introduction of advanced simulation environments remains limited (Table 1). The purpose of this study is to develop and pilot an education and training program reflecting the unique characteristics of the military through a survey of military conditions and needs and to evaluate its field applicability. Based on this program, the study aimed to present a phased direction for strengthening the military infectious disease prevention and management system, encompassing: ① the refinement of standardized epidemiologic investigation, reporting, and decision-making procedures; ② the establishment of a system for the continuous availability and operation of essential resources; and ③ the regularization of field-oriented education and training. The Chungcheong region was selected as the study region for this research due to its regional characteristics as an area where major military medical and educational institutions, including the Korea Army Training Center, Armed Forces Daejeon Hospital, Korea Armed Forces Nursing Academy, Armed Forces Medical School, and the Armed Forces Medical Research Institute, are concentrated, resulting in a high need for implementing regional specialized projects to strengthen military infectious disease management capabilities. This manuscript was prepared based on the “Research Report on Strategies to Strengthen Military Infectious Disease Response and Management Capabilities” [1].

Methods

1. Key Initiatives

This study was conducted in three phases: a survey of the military’s current status and needs, development of educational materials, and pilot implementation of the educational program. A qualitative study was conducted to identify the status of infectious disease response and educational needs through focus group interviews (FGIs) and meetings with personnel from military institutions. Based on this analysis, hands-on training-based educational materials for infectious disease response were developed, and pilot education was conducted for infectious disease officers at military organizations. The program’s field applicability was examined through a satisfaction survey and the collection of qualitative feedback immediately following the conclusion of the education. Additionally, an advisory committee composed of experts from the private, public, and military sectors was convened, and phased consultations were conducted.

2. Survey of Military Field Conditions and Educational Needs

The Survey of Military Field Conditions and Educational Needs was conducted through an FGI on July 24, 2025, at the Armed Forces Medical School with 37 graduates of the 2025 Armed Forces Epidemiologic Intelligence Officer course. The participants were organized into four groups (9–10 people per group). Opinions on infectious disease response experience, perception of the field situation, epidemiologic investigator education, and the response system were collected in stages using a semi-structured questionnaire based on the 5-step FGI structure proposed by Krueger and Casey (opening-introduction-transition-key-closing) [7]. One facilitator and one assistant facilitator were assigned to each group to concurrently take on-site notes and summarize the key points of participants’ statements; the interviews were conducted in private and recorded anonymously with the participants’ consent. Data analysis followed a thematic analysis procedure wherein two researchers independently performed inductive coding by repeatedly reviewing the transcribed notes, resolved differences in codes through consensus to derive preliminary themes, and confirmed the final themes through a reclassification process.

3. Development of Educational Materials and Pilot Education Satisfaction Survey

The scenario-based problem-based learning (PBL) educational materials were restructured based on the KDCA and Korean Society of Epidemiology PBL resource books (2021–2024) to reflect the military organizational structure and operational environment. The field applicability of the educational materials and learning effectiveness were measured immediately after the pilot education through a self-report survey (6 items, 4-point scale), and additional qualitative feedback was collected through concurrent inductive coding of the content of group presentations and discussions. It should be noted that this evaluation is a post-only, single-point-in-time assessment rather than a pre–post comparison design and that it uses self-reported satisfaction and perceived outcome indicators rather than an objective measurement of competency improvement. Additionally, in developing the PBL educational materials, the epidemiologic investigation guidelines and educational materials of the KDCA, table-top exercise materials, the Field Epidemiology Training Program–Frontline (KDCA Epidemiologic Intelligence Officer Training Program) training modules, and the Infectious Disease Control and Prevention Support Unit’s manual on donning and doffing personal protective equipment and conducting field investigations were used as reference materials.

Results

1. Current Status of Military Field Response and Need for Improvement in Operations and Education

Results of a FGI conducted on July 24, 2025, with 37 epidemiologic intelligence officer trainees at the Armed Forces Medical School identified four categories of major structural and operational challenges in the field response process. First, it was found that, because epidemiologic investigation criteria based on the scale of occurrence (number of cases) were not clearly specified, there were difficulties in ensuring consistency of judgment during field application. Second, in some cases, a situation requiring the procurement of resources through cooperation with external organizations was identified because the system for securing the resources and equipment necessary for response was insufficient. Third, a pattern of increased workload was observed due to redundancy in multiple reporting systems and decision-making processes within the command structure during incidents. Additionally, the perceived role of epidemiologic investigation duties tended to remain at the level of an ancillary duty. Fourth, due to the operation of education programs centered on short-term (2-night, 3-day) courses and limited practical experience, difficulties were identified in continuously applying the educational content to real-world situations, highlighting the need to expand situation-based hands-on training, including role-playing and on-site simulation exercises.

These field challenges were ultimately consolidated into the need for improvements in operations and education, corresponding to the three pillars presented in the objectives of this study. First, with respect to the standardization of the epidemiologic investigation and reporting system, the presentation of standardized epidemiologic investigation criteria based on the scale of the outbreak and the unification of the reporting and decision-making system were required. Second, in terms of the resource and operational system, it was necessary to establish a system for securing essential resources and equipment needed for response, standardize procedures for securing resources on an ongoing basis, and standardize operational procedures. Third, in terms of education and training, the analysis indicated a need for continuous, regular field-oriented hands-on training, the securing of dedicated personnel for infectious disease response, and the establishment of a post-training field linkage system. Furthermore, it was found necessary to enhance the sustainability of educational effects and their field applicability by establishing an educational system based on repeated practical application and feedback.

2. Development of Scenario-based PBL Educational Materials

Scenario-based PBL educational materials were developed to reflect the field needs (role-playing and scenario-based training) identified through the FGI. A total of seven PBL topics were selected to reflect infectious disease clusters (respiratory, waterborne, vector-borne, and emerging) that are frequently encountered and problematic in the military, as well as the clinical priorities identified by the advisory committee: Measles, Middle East respiratory syndrome, Norovirus, Salmonella, Tsutsugamushi disease, Malaria, and emerging infectious disease (Disease X). Each topic consisted of a scenario, problem presentation, discussion and commentary, and key considerations for military response.

Additionally, separate didactic instructional materials were developed for military medical personnel, covering the basic principles of infectious diseases and epidemiologic concepts, the roles of military medical personnel, principles of prevention and management in the military environment, and improvements derived from actual cases. As promotional materials for service members, posters, leaflets, and informational stickers on the prevention of respiratory infectious diseases and waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases were produced and, after consultation with the Ministry of National Defense, distributed to all military units (Figure 1).

3. Pilot Education Satisfaction Survey

Based on the developed educational materials, pilot PBL education in a tabletop exercise format was conducted for 40 Navy and Air Force infectious disease officers organized into six teams. Prior to the pilot education, they were provided with guidance on the fundamentals of epidemiologic investigation, outbreak response, and procedures for cooperation with public health authorities. A post-education satisfaction survey (6 self-report items, 4-point scale, n=40) showed positive response rates ranging from 95 to 100% across all six items: competency enhancement after PBL participation (100.0%), appropriateness in reflecting military realities (100.0%), appropriateness of the difficulty level (97.5%), effectiveness compared with conventional lectures (100.0%), the need to expand PBL education (95.0%), and willingness to recommend the program to peers (97.5%) (Table 2).

However, given the characteristics of the evaluation design―40 participants, self-report, and a post-only, single-point-in-time assessment―there are clear limitations in terms of sample size and discriminatory power. In the qualitative feedback, comments such as “Previously ambiguous areas, such as reporting criteria, specimen collection, and isolation decisions, became clear,” “It was effective because we could learn response procedures rather than just knowledge,” and “It was beneficial to share the response procedures of other units” were repeatedly identified, suggesting that satisfaction with the education stemmed from improved procedural clarity as well as the sharing of experience across units. At the same time, structural factors limiting implementation across the military were also identified, including difficulties in convening trainees, shortages of faculty resources, and insufficient facilities and space. These limitations are closely linked to the continuous securing of essential resources and equipment, such as questionnaires, specimen containers, and personal protective equipment, the standardization of operating procedures, and the reality that it is difficult for the military to independently maintain instructors, facilities, and educational programs. Therefore, both the establishment of a stable system for securing and operating resources and supplementation through joint operations with external organizations are required.

Conclusion

This study confirmed the feasibility of establishing, in a phased manner, an educational and policy foundation for strengthening infectious disease response capabilities within the military’s unique working and living environment and command structure. The pilot implementation of the seven PBL educational materials developed to reflect the issues identified through the FGI suggested that ensuring procedural clarity and sharing experiences across units were important contributors to participant satisfaction.

Based on the results of this study, we propose the following phased strategy to strengthen military infectious disease response capabilities. However, because the official standardization of the criteria for conducting epidemiologic investigations and the reporting and decision-making system falls under the authority of the military chain of command, including the Armed Forces Medical Command and the Ministry of National Defense, this study presents practicable education and training as the key means within the scope of research and educational programs and was structured so that each phase operates in alignment with the standardization framework. The proposed strategy focuses on the stepwise implementation of the needs identified in the three areas described above (① standardization of epidemiologic investigation and reporting procedures, ② continuous securing and operation of essential resources, and ③ field-oriented education and training).

First, in the short term, it is proposed that the Medical Command first standardize, at the command level, standard procedures that can be immediately applied in the field, such as phased reporting criteria and contact classification criteria, and pilot the inclusion and operation of a field-applicable PBL tabletop exercise module reflecting these standards in the regular curriculum. That is, PBL training modules are developed and operated based on standardized epidemiologic investigation and reporting procedures to improve the consistency of field judgment, while ambiguities in the guidelines identified during training are fed back into the refinement of standard procedures. Additionally, unit-level stockpiling and inspection standards shall be established to ensure the continuous securing of essential resources and equipment, such as questionnaires, specimen containers, and personal protective equipment, and to standardize procedures for their use.

Second, in the medium term, institutionalize short-term, intensive hands-on training for key personnel (epidemiologic investigators, preventive medicine officers, and infectious disease officers in forward and field units), and strengthen the system for securing dedicated personnel and the post-training field linkage system. In addition, the civilian–government–military advisory committee, which was established and operated during the initial phase of this project to incorporate the expert opinions of the KDCA, the military, and civilian academia into setting the project direction and developing educational materials, will be institutionalized as a standing consultative body. This approach is intended to ensure that the military can continuously update its infectious disease prevention and response capabilities without becoming isolated from the latest knowledge and guidance from external sources (the KDCA and the civilian sector).

Third, in the long term, a joint civilian–government–military hands-on training model will be institutionalized. Because the military is not an institution dedicated exclusively to infectious disease prevention and research, it has limitations in independently securing and maintaining instructors, facilities, and educational programs. By participating in established external joint training models, it can compensate for the resource constraints of independent operation, secure a collaborative network with the KDCA and the civilian sector (a key asset for infectious disease prevention and response), and enhance the expertise and quality of education.

This study has limitations in terms of restricted data access due to military security regulations, the pilot education sample size (40 participants), and the lack of a pre–post comparison. Future follow-up studies should include an effectiveness evaluation incorporating a pre–post comparison design, unit-level control group comparisons, and quantitative competency measurement indicators. The Chungcheong Area Disease Response Center will continue to collaborate with relevant organizations in future research and projects and will share the findings of this study with relevant organizations.

서 론

군(軍) 부대 내 수인성 및 식품매개감염병 등 감염병 집단발생 사례가 지속됨에 따라 군 집단생활 시설 내 전파 차단과 선제적 예방관리의 중요성이 더욱 강조되고 있다. 특히 군 집단의 경우 생활관 등 제한된 공간에서 밀접하게 집단생활을 하는 환경적 특성으로 인해 개인 간 거리두기가 어려우며, 잦은 야외 훈련을 수행하여 매개체 관련 감염병 또는 인수공통 감염병에 노출될 위험이 상대적으로 높은 환경적 특성을 가진다[1-3]. 군 내 감염병 발생 예방은 장병 개인의 건강 문제를 넘어 군 전투력 저하와 국가 안보 공백으로 직결된다는 점에서 핵심적인 예방의학 및 공중보건 과제라 할 수 있다[4].

국내 군 감염병 관리체계는 「군 감염병 예방 및 관리 업무 훈령」 및 「국방부 감염병 위기 대응 매뉴얼」에 근거하여 운영되며, 국군의무사령관의 책임 하에 감염병 예방 관련 업무의 전반적 관리, 대책 수립∙시행 및 결과 평가가 이루어진다[5,6]. 한편 각 군 참모총장(의무실장)은 감염병 예방∙관리업무 담당자를 지정하여 계획 수립과 시행, 장병 교육 및 홍보를 수행하도록 하고 있다. 현재 군 내 감염병 교육은 국군의무사령부 소속기관 교육, 부대 단위 보건교육, 일부 모의훈련 등을 중심으로 운영되고 있으며, 국가 차원의 감염병 전문 교육기관 및 질병관리청과의 연계 교육은 주로 국방부 중앙역학조사관, 군 보건의료인력 및 감염병 예방∙관리업무 담당자를 대상으로 시행되고 있다. 군 의료인력 대상 교육은 이론 중심 과정이 주를 이루고 있으며, 단기 복무 군의관 비율이 높은 인력 구조를 고려할 때 숙련도 향상을 위한 체계적 지원 방안이 함께 지속적으로 검토될 필요가 있다.

해외 주요 군 기관에서는 시나리오 기반 실습과 시뮬레이션 훈련을 중심으로 실제 상황을 재현하는 교육을 확대하고 있다. 예컨대 미국은 미 육군 감염병의학연구소(U.S. Army Medical Research Institute of Infectious Diseases)와 월터리드 육군연구소(Walter Reed Army Institute of Research)를 중심으로 생물안전시설 기반의 고위험 병원체 백신 및 치료제를 개발하고 있으며, 민∙군 융합형 임상숙련도유지 프로그램(C-STARS)을 운영하여 민간 상급종합병원에 군 의료진을 주기적으로 파견함으로써 전시와 평시의 진료 환경 차이를 극복하고 실전 의료 대응 감각을 유지하고 있다. 영국은 국방 화생방센터(Defence CBRN Centre) 주관으로 임상 중심의 화생방 대응 훈련을 추진 중이며, 단순 생존 훈련을 넘어 오염 환경 하에서의 외상 처치 및 고급 소생술 적용 능력을 배양하는 군 의료진 특화 과정을 운영하고 있다. 요약하면 해외 주요 군 기관에서는 시나리오 기반 실전형 훈련, 모의 환자 및 장비를 이용한 임상 술기 숙달, 단기 집중훈련, 현장 진단 및 이송 기술 등 현장감이 부여된 교육∙훈련을 구성하고 있으며, 의료 시뮬레이터∙표준화 환자(standardized patient)∙가상현실(virtual reality) 등 기술을 활용하여 현장 대응 능력과 임상 숙련도를 강화하는 방식이 일반화되는 추세이다.

국내 군의 감염병 교육은 기존 교육자료를 기반으로 한 이론 교육과 일부 실습 중심으로 운영되어오고 있으나, 고도화된 시뮬레이션 환경의 도입은 아직까지는 제한적인 수준이다(표 1). 본 연구는 군 현장 실태 및 요구도 조사를 통하여 군의 특수성을 반영한 교육∙훈련 프로그램을 개발∙시범운영하고 그 현장 적합성을 평가하는 한편, 이를 바탕으로 ① 표준화된 역학조사 및 보고∙의사결정 절차의 정비, ② 필수 자원의 상시 확보∙운영 체계의 구축, ③ 현장 중심 교육∙훈련의 정례화를 포괄하는 군 감염병 예방∙관리체계 강화의 단계적 방향을 제시하는 데 목적이 있다. 충청권역은 육군훈련소를 비롯하여 국군대전병원, 국군간호사관학교, 국군의무학교, 국군의학연구소 등 주요 군 의료∙교육기관이 집적되어 있는 지역으로, 군 감염병 관리 역량 강화를 위한 권역 단위 특화사업 추진의 필요성이 높은 지역적 특성을 지녀 본 연구의 수행 권역으로 선정되었다. 이 원고는 「군(軍) 감염병 대응 및 관리역량 강화 방안 연구 결과보고서」 [1]를 바탕으로 작성되었다.

방 법

1. 추진 과제

본 연구는 군 실태 및 요구도 조사, 교육자료 개발, 교육 시범운영의 세 단계로 구성하여 수행하였다. 군 기관 관계자를 대상으로 포커스 그룹 인터뷰(focus group interview, FGI)와 간담회를 실시하여 감염병 대응 실태와 교육 요구도를 파악하는 정성적 조사를 수행하였다. 이를 바탕으로 실습 기반 감염병 대응 교육자료를 개발하였으며, 군 기관 감염병 담당자를 대상으로 시범교육을 운영하였다. 교육 종료 직후 만족도 조사 및 정성 의견 수집을 통해 프로그램의 현장 적합성을 조사하였다. 또한 민∙관∙군 전문가로 구성된 자문단을 운영하여 단계별 자문을 받았다.

2. 군 현장 실태 및 교육 요구도 조사

군 현장 실태 및 교육 요구도 조사는 2025년 7월 24일 국군의무학교에서 2025년도 국군 역학조사관 교육과정 이수자 37명을 대상으로 FGI를 통해 수집하였다. 참여자는 4개 조(조당 9–10명)로 편성하였으며, Krueger와 Casey [7]가 제시한 FGI 5단계 구조(시작-도입-전환-핵심-마무리)에 기반한 반구조화 질문지를 사용하여 감염병 대응 경험, 현장 상황 인식, 역학조사관 교육 및 대응 체계에 대한 의견을 단계별로 수집하였다. 각 조마다 진행자 1인과 보조 진행자 1인을 배치하여 발언 요지의 현장 필기∙요약을 병행하였으며, 인터뷰는 비공개로 진행하고 참여자 동의 하에 익명으로 기록하였다. 자료 분석은 두 연구자가 독립적으로 전사 메모를 반복 검토하며 귀납적 코딩(inductive coding)을 수행한 후, 코드 차이를 합의 조정하여 잠정 주제를 도출하고 재분류 과정을 거쳐 최종 주제를 확정하는 주제 분석(thematic analysis) 절차를 따랐다.

3. 교육자료 개발 및 시범교육 만족도 조사

시나리오 기반 문제중심학습(problem-based learning, PBL) 교육자료는 질병관리청 및 한국역학회 PBL 자료집(2021–2024)을 기반으로 군 조직 구조와 작전 환경을 반영하여 재구성하였다. 교육자료의 현장 적합성과 학습 효과 평가는 시범교육 종료 직후 자기보고식 설문(6문항, 4점 척도)을 통해 측정하였으며, 조별 발표∙토의 내용에 대한 귀납적 코딩을 병행하여 정성 의견을 보완 수집하였다. 본 평가는 사전-사후 비교 설계가 아니라 사후 단일 시점 평가이며, 객관적 역량 향상 측정이 아닌 자기보고 기반 만족도와 인지된 결과 지표임을 명시한다. 부가적으로 PBL 교육자료 개발 시 질병관리청의 역학조사 지침과 교육 자료집, 도상훈련(table-top exercise) 자료, 그리고 질병관리청 역학조사관 양성과정(Field Epidemiology Training Program–Frontline) 교육 모듈과 감염병관리지원단의 개인보호구 착∙탈의 및 현장조사 매뉴얼을 참고 자료로 활용하였다.

결 과

1. 군 현장대응 실태 및 운영∙교육 개선 필요

2025년 7월 24일 국군의무학교에서 실시한 역학조사관 교육생 37명을 대상으로 한 FGI 결과, 현장 대응 과정에서의 주요 구조적∙운영상 과제가 네 가지 범주로 도출되었다. 첫째, 발생 규모(사례 수)에 따른 역학조사 기준이 구체적으로 제시되지 않아 현장 적용 과정에서 판단의 일관성을 확보하는 데 어려움이 있는 것으로 나타났다. 둘째, 일부 사례에서는 대응에 필요한 자원 및 장비의 확보 체계가 충분하지 않아 외부 기관과의 협조를 통한 자원 조달이 요구되는 상황이 확인되었다. 셋째, 상황 발생 시 지휘체계 내 다중 보고 체계와 의사결정 과정의 중복으로 인해 업무 부담이 가중되는 양상이 나타났으며, 역학조사 업무에 대한 역할 인식 또한 부가적 업무 수준에 머무르는 경향이 확인되었다. 넷째, 단기간(2박 3일) 중심의 교육 운영과 제한된 실무 경험으로 인해 교육 내용을 실제 상황에 지속적으로 적용하는 데 어려움이 나타나, 역할극 및 현장 모의훈련 등 상황 기반 실습교육 확대의 필요성이 제기되었다.

이러한 현장 과제는 본 연구가 목적에서 제시한 세 축에 대응하는 운영∙교육 개선 필요로 수렴되었다. 첫째, 역학조사∙보고 체계의 표준화 측면에서는 발생 규모에 따른 표준화된 역학조사 기준의 제시와 보고∙의사결정 체계의 일원화가 요구되었다. 둘째, 자원∙운영 체계 측면에서는 대응에 필요한 필수 자원∙장비의 확보 체계 구축과 자원의 상시 확보 및 운영 절차의 표준화가 요구되었다. 셋째, 교육∙훈련 측면에서는 현장 중심의 지속∙정례 실습교육과 감염병 대응 전담인력의 확보 및 교육 이후 현장 연계 체계의 구축이 필요한 것으로 분석되었다. 아울러 반복적 실무 적용과 피드백에 기반한 교육체계 구축을 통해 교육 효과의 지속성과 현장 적용성을 제고할 필요가 있는 것으로 나타났다.

2. 시나리오 기반 PBL 교육자료 개발

FGI에서 도출된 현장 요구(역할극∙상황 기반 교육)를 반영하여 시나리오 기반 PBL 교육자료를 개발하였다. PBL 주제는 군에서 빈번히 발생하고 문제가 되는 감염병 군집(호흡기∙수인성∙매개체∙신종)과 자문위원단의 임상 우선순위를 반영하여 총 7종으로 선정하였다: 홍역, 중동호흡기증후군(Middle East respiratory syndrome), 노로바이러스, 살모넬라, 쯔쯔가무시증, 말라리아, 신종감염병(Disease X). 각 주제는 상황 설정, 문제 제시, 토의 및 해설, 군 대응 시 중점 고려사항으로 구성하였다.

또한 군 의무인력 대상 이론 교육자료를 별도로 제작하여 감염병의 기본 원리와 역학 개념, 군 의료인력의 역할, 군 환경에서의 예방∙관리 원칙, 실제 사례에서 도출된 개선사항을 정리하였다. 장병 대상 홍보자료로는 호흡기 감염병 및 수인성∙식품매개 감염병 예방 포스터, 리플릿, 안내 스티커를 제작하여 국방부와 협의 후 전 부대에 배포하였다(그림 1).

3. 시범교육 만족도 조사

개발된 교육자료를 기반으로 해군∙공군 감염병 담당자 40명을 대상으로 6개 조를 편성하여 도상훈련 형식의 PBL 시범교육을 실시하였으며, 사전 교육으로 역학조사 기초와 집단발생 대응 및 보건당국 협력 절차를 안내하였다. 교육 후 실시한 만족도 조사(자기보고식 6문항, 4점 척도, n=40)에서 PBL 참여 후 역량 강화(100.0%), 군 현실 반영의 적절성(100.0%), 난이도 적절성(97.5%), 기존 강의 대비 효율성(100.0%), PBL 교육 확대 필요성(95.0%), 동료 추천 의향(97.5%) 등 6개 항목 모두 95–100%의 긍정 응답률을 보였다(표 2).

다만 40명, 자기보고식 문항, 사후 단일 시점이라는 평가 설계의 특성상 표본 규모 및 변별력 측면의 한계가 분명하다. 정성 의견에서는 “보고 기준∙검체 채취∙격리 판단 등 모호했던 부분이 명확해졌다”, “지식이 아니라 ‘행동 절차’를 배울 수 있어 효과적이다”, “다른 부대의 대응방식을 공유할 수 있어 좋았다” 등이 반복적으로 도출되었고, 교육 만족이 발생한 지점이 절차적 명료성 확보와 더불어 부대 간 경험 공유임을 시사하였다. 동시에 교육생 소집의 어려움, 교수 자원 부족, 시설∙공간 부족 등 군 전반 적용을 제약하는 구조적 요인도 함께 확인되었다. 이러한 제약은 설문지, 검체통, 개인보호구 등 필수 자원∙장비의 상시 확보와 운영 절차의 표준화, 그리고 강사∙시설∙교육 프로그램을 군이 단독으로 유지하기 어려운 현실과 맞닿아 있어, 자원의 안정적 확보∙운영 체계 마련과 외부 기관과의 합동 운영을 통한 보완이 함께 요구된다.

결 론

본 연구를 통해 군 조직의 특수한 근무∙생활 환경과 지휘체계 하에서 감염병 대응 역량 강화를 위한 교육∙정책 기반을 단계적으로 마련할 수 있는 가능성을 확인하였다. FGI에서 제기된 내용을 반영하여 개발된 PBL 7종 교육자료의 시범운영에서는 절차적 명료성 확보와 부대 간 경험 공유가 만족도에 있어 중요함을 시사하였다.

본 연구 결과를 바탕으로 군 감염병 대응 역량 강화를 위한 단계적 전략을 다음과 같이 제안한다. 다만 역학조사 실시 기준과 보고∙의사결정 체계의 공식적 표준화는 국군의무사령부∙국방부 등 지휘체계의 권한에 속하므로, 본 연구는 연구∙교육사업의 범위에서 실행 가능한 교육∙훈련을 핵심 수단으로 제시하되 각 단계가 표준화 기반과 맞물려 작동하도록 구성하였다. 제안하는 전략은 앞서 도출된 세 영역의 요구(① 역학조사∙보고 절차의 표준화, ② 필수 자원의 상시 확보∙운영, ③ 현장 중심 교육∙훈련)를 단계별로 구현하는 데 초점을 둔다.

첫째, 단기적으로는 단계별 보고 기준과 접촉자 분류 기준 등 현장에서 즉시 적용 가능한 표준 절차를 의무사령부 차원에서 우선 일원화하고, 이를 반영한 현장 적용형 PBL 도상훈련 모듈을 정규 교육과정에 시범 편성∙운영한다. 즉, 표준화된 역학조사∙보고 절차를 토대로 PBL 훈련 모듈을 개발∙운영하여 현장 판단의 일관성을 높이는 한편, 훈련 과정에서 확인되는 지침의 모호성을 표준 절차 정비에 환류한다. 아울러 설문지∙검체통∙개인보호구 등 필수 자원∙장비의 상시 확보와 사용 절차 표준화를 위한 부대 단위 비축∙점검 기준을 마련한다.

둘째, 중기로는 핵심 인력(역학조사관, 예방의무 담당자, 전방∙야전부대 감염병 담당자)을 대상으로 한 단기 집중 실습교육을 정례화하고, 전담인력 확보와 교육 이후 현장 연계 체계를 강화한다. 또한 본 사업의 착수 단계에서 사업 방향 설정과 교육자료 개발에 질병관리청∙군∙민간 학계의 전문적 의견을 반영하기 위해 운영하였던 민∙관∙군 자문위원단을 상설 협의체로 제도화한다. 이는 군이 외부(질병관리청∙민간)의 최신 지견에 고립되지 않고 감염병 예방∙대응 수준을 지속적으로 갱신할 수 있도록 하기 위함이다.

셋째, 장기로는 민∙관∙군 합동 실습 교육 모델을 제도화한다. 군은 감염병 예방∙연구를 전담하는 기관이 아니어서 강사, 시설, 교육 프로그램을 단독으로 확보∙유지하는 데 제약이 있으므로, 이미 정립된 외부 합동 교육 모델에 군이 참여함으로써 단독 운영의 자원 제약을 보완하고, 질병관리청∙민간과의 협력 네트워크(감염병 예방∙대응에 핵심적인 자산)를 확보하며 교육의 전문성과 수준을 제고할 수 있다.

본 연구는 군 보안규정에 따른 자료 접근 제한과 시범교육 표본 규모(40명), 사전-사후 비교 부재 측면의 한계를 가진다. 향후 후속 연구에서는 사전-사후 비교 설계와 부대 단위 대조군 비교, 정량적 역량 측정 지표를 도입한 효과 평가가 필요하다. 충청권질병대응센터는 향후 관련 연구 및 사업을 위하여 유관기관과 지속 협력하고 본 연구 결과를 관련 기관에 공유할 것이다.
