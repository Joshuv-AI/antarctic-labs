Supercritical Site Percolation on Regular Graphs
Sahar Diskin, Michael Krivelevich, Itay Markbreit

Abstract
We consider site (vertex) percolation on d d -regular graphs, for both constant-degree and growing-degree cases. We give sufficient, and relatively tight, conditions for the emergence of the “Erdős-Rényi component phenomenon” in the supercritical regime p = 1 + ϵ d − 1 p=\frac{1+\epsilon}{d-1} : namely, the appearance of a unique giant component of order n / d n/d in the percolated subgraph, with all other components being of size O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) . Our main results apply both to the d d -dimensional hypercube and to pseudo-random graphs, and resolve two open questions in these cases. We further discuss differences (and similarities) between bond (edge) percolation setting and site percolation setting.

We first review some results in the bond percolation setting. The classical binomial random graph

Formally, for an integer

\displaystyle q=(1-p+pq)^{d-1}.

This is the extinction probability of a Galton-Watson tree with offspring distribution

-regular tree

\begin{split}y&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\binom{d}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{d-i}(1-q^{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\binom{d}{i}p^{i}(1-p)^{d-i}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\binom{d}{i}(pq)^{i}(1-p)^{d-i}\\
&=1-(1-p+pq)^{d}=1-q(1-p)-pq^{2}.\end{split}

Let us note that when

is asymptotically equal to the unique solution in

.
We can now explain the result of Erdős and Rényi. They showed that when

The phenomenon Erdős and Rényi discovered for bond percolation on the complete graph

2.1
A modified Breadth First Search process

We will utilize the following modification (whose definition is similar to the one in
[
14
,
29
]
) of the classical Breadth First Search (BFS) exploration algorithm. The algorithm receives as input a graph
G=(V,E)
with an ordering
\sigma
on its vertices, and a sequence
(X_{i})_{i=1}^{|V|}
of i.i.d. Bernoulli
(p)
random variables.

We maintain four sets throughout the process:
S
, the set of vertices whose exploration has been completed;
U
, the set of vertices currently being explored, kept in a queue (first-in-first-out discipline);
T
, the set of vertices which have yet been processed;
J
, the set of vertices found to fall outside the random set of vertices
V_{p}
. We initialize
S,J,U=\emptyset
; and
T=V
, and run the algorithm until
U\cup T=\emptyset
.

We say that we are in round
t\in\mathbb{N}
of the algorithm if exactly
t-1
vertices have already been queried by the algorithm. In round
t
, we do the following: if
U
is empty, we consider the first vertex
v
in
T
according to the order
\sigma
. If
X_{t}=1
, we retain this vertex and move it from
T
to
U
. If
X_{t}=0
, we move it to
J
and finish the round. Otherwise, if
U\neq\emptyset
, we consider the first vertex

2.3
Some General Lemmas on the Percolated Subgraph

For any
d
-regular graph, we use the fact, stated and proved in
[
13
, Claim 2.1]
, that typically connected components in the percolated subgraph admit good vertex expansion:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices. Let
\epsilon>0
be a sufficiently small constant, and let
p=\frac{1+\epsilon}{d}
. Then,
whp
, any connected component
S
of
G[V_{p}]
with
|S|>300\log n
has
|N_{G}(S)|\geq\frac{9}{10}d|S|
.

The goal of the following lemma and its three corollaries, at a heuristic level, is to
whp
rule out the existence of connected components in
G[V_{p}]
of ‘intermediate’ sizes, where this ‘intermediate’ size depends on ‘how far’ the graph has an almost perfect edge expansion. For these four claims, we consider the following setting: let
\epsilon>0
be a sufficiently small constant. Let
G
be a
d
-regular graph on
n
vertices such that for every
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\leq t
,
e(U,U^{c})\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{1000}\right)d|U|
, for some integer
t\coloneqq t(n,d)\leq n
. Finally, set
p=\frac{1+\epsilon}{d}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A\subseteq V(G)
, and fix
k\in\left[\frac{50}{\epsilon^{2}},\frac{t}{4d}\right]
. The probability that

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)\right|=k

is at most
3\epsilon kd\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)
.

Proof.

We run the BFS algorithm equipped with an order
\sigma
on
V(G)
, satisfying that the vertices in
A
are the first vertices in the order.
After round
r>0
of the algorithm, we let
V_{r}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{r})
be the vertices that have already been queried by the algorithm, where the order of
V_{r}
is according to the query order. Furthermore, let
S_{r}=V_{r}\cap V_{p}
;
S_{0}=\emptyset
. Observe that by construction,
V_{r}\subseteq A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]
.

Suppose that at round
r
of the algorithm (
1\leq r\leq n
), we have just finished exploring
\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)
and have that
\left|\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)\right|=k
. In this case,
|S_{r}|=k
and all vertices in
N_{G}[S_{r}]
have been queried, thus
|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r
. Define
I=\left[\frac{k}{\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)p},\frac{k}{\left(1-\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)p}\right]
and distinguish between three regimes:
1\leq r\leq\min I
,
r\in I
and
\max I\leq r\leq n
. Notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)\right|=k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg(\exists_{1\leq r\leq n}\;\big[|S_{r}|=k\wedge|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r\big]\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\exists_{1\leq r\leq\min I}\;|S_{r}|=k\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\exists_{r\in I}\;|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\exists_{\max I\leq r\leq n}\;|S_{r}|=k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\min I}|\geq k\right)+\sum_{r\in I}\mathbb{P}\left(|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\max I}|\leq k\right),

(3)

where the last inequality holds by the following observation: note that
|S_{1}|
is less than
k
. Thus, if
|S_{r}|=k
for some
1\leq r\leq\min I
, then in particular
|S_{\min I}|\geq k
. Hence, the event
\left(\exists_{1\leq r\leq\min I}\;|S_{r}|=k\right)
is contained in the event
\left(|S_{\min I}|\geq k\right)
. Similarly, for
\max I\leq r\leq n
, the event
\left(\exists_{\max I\leq r\leq n}\;|S_{r}|=k\right)
is contained in the event
\left(|S_{\max I}|\leq k\right)
.

We first bound the probability that
|S_{\min I}|\geq k
from above. By Claim
2.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\min I}|\geq k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Bin\left(\min I,p\right)\geq k\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\left(k-\min I\cdot p\right)^{2}}{3\cdot\min I\cdot p}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}}\right)^{2}k^{2}}{3\cdot\frac{k}{1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}}{25}k\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}k\right),

(4)

where the last inequality holds since
k\geq\frac{50}{\epsilon^{2}}
.

We now turn to estimating the probability that
|S_{\max I}|\leq k
. By Claim
2.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|S_{\max I}|\leq k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(Bin\left(\max I,p\right)\leq k\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\max I\cdot p-k\right)^{2}}{3\cdot\max I\cdot p}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{3\epsilon}{5}}-1\right)^{2}k^{2}}{3\cdot\frac{k}{1-\frac{3\epsilon}{5}}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2\epsilon^{2}}{25}k\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}k\right),

(5)

where the last inequality holds since
k\geq\frac{50}{\epsilon^{2}}
.

Finally, we turn to the case where
r\in I
. In this case we bound from above the probability that the exposure of the components of
A
is completed in
r
rounds of the BFS algorithm and that the union of these components consists of
k
vertices. In particular, it suffices to bound from above the probability that
|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r
where
|S_{r}|=k
. First note that if
|A|\geq 2\cdot\frac{k}{p}
, then by Claim
2.1
, with probability at least
1-2\exp\left(-\frac{k}{6}\right)
, we have
|A\cap V_{p}|>k
, and hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)\right|=k\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(|A\cap V_{p}|\leq k\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{k}{6}\right).

Thus, we can narrow our discussion to sets
A
of size at most
2\cdot\frac{k}{p}\leq 2\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)r
. Hence,

\displaystyle|A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq|A|+r\leq(3+2\epsilon)r.

(6)

Let
i\leq r
, and consider the
i
-th round of the BFS algorithm. We say that a vertex
u\in V(G)
is
bad
with respect to
S_{i-1}
if it has at most
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{20}\right)d
neighbors in
V(G)\backslash N_{G}[S_{i-1}]
, and is
good
otherwise. Once a vertex is labeled as good or bad, the label persists for the duration of the algorithm.
Assume towards a contradiction that
V_{r}
contains more than
\frac{\epsilon}{10}r
bad vertices. As mentioned earlier,
A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]\supseteq V_{r}
, thus using (
6
) we have

\displaystyle e(A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}])\geq\frac{\epsilon}{10}r\cdot\frac{\epsilon}{20}d\geq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{200(3+2\epsilon)}d|A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]|.

(7)

On the other hand, since
r\in I
, then
|A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq|A|+r\leq(3+2\epsilon)r\leq 4dk\leq t
, and therefore we can use our assumption that for every
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\leq t
,
e(U,U^{c})\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{1000}\right)d|U|
to obtain:

\displaystyle e(A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}],(A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}])^{c})\geq\left(1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{1000}\right)d|A\cup N_{G}[S_{r}]|,

which contradicts (
7
). Thus,
V_{r}
cannot contain more than
\frac{\epsilon}{10}r
bad vertices. We further show that the number of bad vertices in
S_{r}
is typically small; to be precise, we show that it is typically smaller than
\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\cdot rp
. Since we have at least
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{10}\right)r
good vertices in
V_{r}
, the expected number of good vertices in
S_{r}
is at least
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{10}\right)rp
, and therefore the number of good vertices in
V_{r}
is stochastically dominated by
Bin\left(\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{10}\right)r,p\right)
. Hence, by Claim
2.1
, the probability that we have less than
\left(1-\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\right)rp
good vertices in
S_{r}
is at most:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Bin\left(\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{10}\right)r,p\right)<\left(1-\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\right)rp\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\frac{9\epsilon^{2}}{25}r^{2}p^{2}}{3rp}\right)

\displaystyle=2\exp\left(-\frac{3\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot rp\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right),

where the last inequality holds since
r\geq\frac{k}{\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)p}
and since
k\geq\frac{50}{\epsilon^{2}}
.

To conclude, with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)
,
S_{r}
contains at least
\left(1-\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\right)rp
good vertices. Since every good vertex that falls into
S_{r}
contributes at least
\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{20}\right)d
new vertices to the closed neighborhood of
S_{r}
, using
|S_{r}|\leq\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)rp
, we derive that with probability
1-\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)
,

\displaystyle|N_{G}(S_{r})|

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\right)rp\cdot\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{20}\right)d-|S_{r}|\geq\left(1-\frac{7\epsilon}{10}\right)\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{20}\right)(1+\epsilon)r-\left(1+\frac{3\epsilon}{5}\right)rp>r,

where the last inequality holds since
d=\omega(1)
and since
\epsilon>0
is small enough.
Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right).

Overall,

\displaystyle\sum_{r\in I}\mathbb{P}\left(|N_{G}[S_{r}]|\leq r\right)\leq 2\epsilon kd\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right).

(8)

Putting (
2.3
), (
2.3
) and (
8
) together, we have, by (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\bigcup_{u\in A}C_{G[V_{p}]}(u)\right|=k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)+2\epsilon kd\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\epsilon kd\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{25}\cdot k\right),

completing the lemma.
∎

Finally, since each vertex

Whp

Proof.

, all components in

Lemma 3.8

Whp

Proof.

This section addresses

Let

\displaystyle\left|e_{G}(B,C)-\frac{d}{n}|B||C|\right|\leq\lambda\sqrt{|B||C|}.

Let us emphasize the similarities and differences between the assumptions in Theorem

and Theorem

Recall our notation from the proof of Theorem

and let

be such that

We define the set of vertices in ‘large’ components in

.

Whp
there is a component
K
in
G_{2}
such that

.

Proof.

First, since

whp

of size at least

, there exists a path in

. In particular,

there exists

that has a neighbor in

\delta:=\delta(\alpha)>0
and
C:=C(\alpha)>0
are chosen sufficiently small and large, respectively. Let

be a

with

. Similar to the proof of Theorem

, we set
p_{2}=\frac{s}{d-1}
for a sufficiently small

be such that
(1-p_{1})(1-p_{2})=1-p
, noting that
p_{1}\geq\frac{\alpha-s}{d-1}

G_{1}:=G[V_{p_{1}}]

Let us show how to translate property

to vertex- and edge-expansion properties similar in spirit to properties

and

2
yields that for every

with
|U|\leq\log^{C^{\prime}}n
, we have

. For the vertex expansion, see the following lemma:

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
U\subseteq V(G)
with
|U|\leq\log^{C}n
,
|N_{G}(U)|\geq\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-2\right)|U|
.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, that is, there exists
U
with
|U|\leq\log^{C}n
, but
|N_{G}(U)|<\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-2\right)|U|
.
Then, we have
|U\cup N_{G}(U)|<\frac{(d-1-\delta)|U|}{1+\delta}<d|U|\leq\log^{C+1}n=\log^{C^{\prime}}n
.
Thus, by

\displaystyle e(U\cup N_{G}(U))\leq(1+\delta)|U\cup N_{G}(U)|<(d-1-\delta)|U|.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle e(U\cup N_{G}(U))\geq\sum_{u\in U}d_{G}(u)-e(U)=d|U|-e(U)\geq(d-1-\delta)|U|,

where the last inequality follows from
2
.
Comparing the two bounds on

leads to a contradiction.
∎

While the assumptions in Theorems

and

are rather similar, we will see in Lemma

why the stronger assumption

With Lemma

in hand, we have the following argument, which we shall call
the key argument
for the rest of this section, as it will accompany us throughout the entire proof. We formulate this argument for

, noting that it applies to

as well.

Suppose we have a vertex

and reveal its component in

via the BFS algorithm. Assume that
|C_{G[V_{p}]}(v)|=k
for some
k\leq\log^{C}n
. Set
S\coloneqq C_{G[V_{p}]}(v)
. By Lemma
5.1
, during the BFS algorithm there were at least
|S|+|N_{G}(S)|\geq\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-1\right)k
queried vertices, of which we have received at most
|S|=k
positive answers.
The probability of such an event to occur is bounded from above by the probability that
Bin\left(\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-1\right)k,\frac{\alpha}{d-1}\right)\leq k
.

Using the key argument, we show that
whp
,
G[V_{p}]
(as well as
G[V_{p_{1}}]
) contains no components of ‘intermediate’ sizes (where here ‘intermediate’ is of size between
\Omega_{\alpha}(\log n)
and
\log^{C}n
). Formally, let
v\in V(G)
and
k\leq\log^{C}n
, and set
X\sim Bin\left(\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-1\right)k,\frac{\alpha}{d-1}\right)
. By the key argument, and using Claim
2.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|C_{G[V_{p}]}(v)|=k)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq k\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{(\mathbb{E}[X]-k)^{2}}{3\cdot\mathbb{E}[X]}\right).

(9)

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\cdot\alpha k\leq\mathbb{E}[X]=\left(\frac{d}{1+\delta}-1\right)\cdot\frac{1}{d-1}\cdot\alpha k\leq\alpha k.

(10)

Hence, using (
9
), (
10
) and afterwards the union bound over all
v\in V(G)
and
\frac{9\alpha}{\left(\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\alpha-1\right)^{2}}\log n\leq k\leq\log^{C}n
, we have that
whp

G_{p}
contains no components of size between
\frac{9\alpha}{\left(\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\alpha-1\right)^{2}}\log n
and
\log^{C}n
.

Hence, as expected, we define

\displaystyle W=\left\{v\in V(G)\;\bigg|\;|C_{G_{1}}(v)|=\frac{9\alpha}{\left(\frac{1-\delta}{1+\delta}\alpha-1\right)^{2}}\log n\right\}.

We are now turn to show that all ‘large’ components in
G[V_{p_{1}}]
,
W
, merge after sprinkling with
p_{2}
into one component in
G[V_{p}]
. By the key argument, and using again Lemma
5.1
to show that the ball (in
G
) of radius
4\log_{d}\log n+1
around each vertex
v\in V(G)
contains many vertices, we show that
whp
the distance between any vertex
v\in V(G)
from ‘large’ components in
G[V_{p_{1}}]
,
W
, is at most
4\log_{d}\log n+1
(these claims are analogous to Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
). Now we can apply essentially the same method as in Lemma
3.7
to show that
whp
there is a component
K
in

such that
W\subseteq V(K)
.

We also must check, as in the proof of Theorem
1
that sprinkling does not create new large components. More precisely,
whp
, there is no component in
G[V_{p}]
of size at least
\log^{C}n
, which does not intersect

. This is done as in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, and using again the key argument.

All left to do is to show that the unique ‘large’ component which appeared in

is of the correct size. This argument goes quite differently compared to the corresponding argument in the proof of Theorem
1
, therefore, we provide here the proof in full.

Before that, we need one more lemma regarding the local structure of

, showing that there are ‘many’ vertices in
G
that are far from any cycle. Formally,

Lemma 5.2
.

There is a set
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}\right)n
such that for every

, there are no cycles in
B\left(v,\frac{1}{16\delta}\right)
.

This lemma appeared and proved in
[

, but in order to show the critical role of property
2
, we give here its complete proof.

Proof.

We first claim that in
G
, every two cycles of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
are at a distance of at least
\frac{1}{4\delta}
. Otherwise, let
U
be the vertices of these two cycles, together with the vertices of a path of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
connecting them. Then,
|U|\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{4\delta}+\frac{1}{4\delta}=\frac{3}{4\delta}
and
e(U)\geq|U|+1
(since
U
is connected and contains at least two cycles). On the other hand, by
2
,

e(U)\leq(1+\delta)|U|\leq|U|+\delta\cdot\frac{3}{4\delta}<|U|+1.

Let
m
be the number of cycles of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
in
G
, denote them by
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
. For every
i\in[m]
, let
N_{i}
be the set of vertices in
V(G)
at distance at most
\frac{1}{8\delta}
from
C_{i}
. We then have for every
i\neq j
,
N_{i}\cap N_{j}=\emptyset
(as otherwise we would have two cycles of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
at distance at most
2\cdot\frac{1}{8\delta}=\frac{1}{4\delta}
). By the same reasoning, for every
i\in[m]
,
G[N_{i}]
has only one cycle. We thus obtain
m\cdot(d-1)^{\frac{1}{8\delta}}\leq n
, that is,
m\leq\frac{n}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{8\delta}}}
.

Since
m\cdot(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}\leq\frac{n}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}
, we conclude that there are at most
\frac{n}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}
vertices in
G
that are at distance at most
\frac{1}{16\delta}
from a cycle of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
. Hence, there is a set
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}\right)n
such that every
v\in X
is at a distance of at least
\frac{1}{16\delta}
from any cycle of length at most
\frac{1}{4\delta}
. Thus, every
v\in X
satisfies that
B\left(v,\frac{1}{16\delta}\right)
contains no cycles.
∎

We are now ready to show the last step in the proof of Theorem

, which is showing that the largest component of

, which we denote by

, is of size

The probability a vertex belongs to a component of size at least

]

that

.

For the lower bound on the size of

, we use Lemma
5.2
.
Let

be the random variable counting the number of vertices in components of size at least
\frac{1}{16\delta}
in

. By Lemma

, there exists a set

with
|X|\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}\right)n
such that for every

, there are no cycles in

. Hence, by standard results, we have that for every

,
\mathbb{P}(|C_{G_{2}}(v)|\geq\frac{1}{16\delta})\geq(1-o_{\delta}(1))x
, where

tends to zero as

tends to zero. Thus
\mathbb{E}\left[|Z_{1}|\right]\geq(1-o_{\delta}(1))x\left(1-\frac{1}{(d-1)^{\frac{1}{16\delta}}}\right)n=(1-o_{\delta}(1))x\cdot n
. To show that
|Z_{1}|
is well concentrated around its mean, consider the vertex-exposure martingale. Every vertex can change the value of

by at most
\frac{d}{16\delta}
. Hence, by part 2 of Claim

:

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big||Z_{1}|-\mathbb{E}[|Z_{1}|]\Big|\geq n^{2/3}\bigg)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{n^{4/3}}{2n}\cdot\frac{256\delta^{2}}{d^{2}}\right)=o(1),

where the last equality holds since

.
Therefore,
whp
,
|Z_{1}|\geq(1-o_{\delta}(1))x\cdot n
.
Let
Z_{2}
be the random variable counting the number of vertices in components of
G_{2}
whose size lies in the interval
\left[\frac{1}{16\delta},\log^{C}n\right]
. By the key argument,
\mathbb{E}[|Z_{2}|]\leq o_{\delta}(1)x\cdot n
. Once again, let us consider the vertex-exposure martingale. Every vertex can change the value of

by at most

. Hence, by part 2 of Claim

:

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big||Z_{2}|-\mathbb{E}[|Z_{2}|]\Big|\geq n^{2/3}\bigg)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{n^{4/3}}{2n}\cdot\frac{1}{d^{2}\log^{2C}n}\right)=o(1),

where the last equality holds since

. Therefore, we obtain that the number of vertices in components of size at least

in
G_{2}
is
whp

(1-o_{\delta}(1))x\cdot n-(1+o(1))o_{\delta}(1)x\cdot n
.
Thus, since
\delta
is sufficiently small,
whp
there are at least
(1-o_{\delta}(1))x\cdot n
vertices in components of size at least
\log^{C}n
in
G_{2}
. As argued above,
whp
, every component of size at least

in

. Thus, the number of vertices in components that intersect with

is at least

. Moreover, all the vertices in

, and hence

.

6.2
The absence of ERCP

Lastly, we show that ERCP fails for the graph

Claim 6.5
.

Whp
, there are at least two components of
G[V_{p}]
of size
\Omega(d\log n)
.

Proof.

We start by constructing an auxiliary graph
\Gamma
whose vertex set is
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\frac{n}{k}}
, and
\left(A_{i},A_{j}\right)\in E(\Gamma)
(

) if their distance in
G
is at most two, that is, if there exists a path in
G
of length (in edges) at most
2
that connects
A_{i}
to
A_{j}
. Notice that the maximal degree of
\Gamma
,
\Delta(\Gamma)
, is at most

, and therefore
\Gamma
contains an independent set,

, of size at least
\frac{|V(\Gamma)|}{k\cdot(10b)^{2}+1}\geq\frac{n}{200b^{2}k^{2}}

Denote by
I_{0}=\{1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{k}\;|\;A_{i}\in I\}
. For every

, we denote by
X_{i}
the indicator of the event for which
G[V_{p}]
contains a component

, of size
|S|\in[\epsilon\cdot d\log n,3\epsilon\cdot d\log n]
. Notice that since
I
is an independent set of
\Gamma
, the random variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in I_{0}}
are i.i.d.

Let

. Notice that
X_{i}=1
if and only if
G[A_{i}\cap V_{p}]
admits a connected component of size in the interval
[\epsilon\cdot d\log n,3\epsilon\cdot d\log n]

A_{i}
are not in
V_{p}
. We bound the probability that
X_{i}=1
by exposing the vertices of
G[V_{p}]
in two steps. We first reveal
A_{i}
, apply Theorem 2 from
[

]
to

, and obtain, with probability at least

, a connected component

in

of size in the interval

. In order to establish that

is a connected component in the whole

, the neighborhood of

must fall outside of the random subset

. We now expose

(in particular, we expose the neighborhood

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{i}=1)

\displaystyle\geq(1-o(1))(1-p)^{\left|N_{G[V(G)\backslash A_{i}]}(S)\right|}\geq(1-o(1))(1-p)^{10b\cdot 3\epsilon\cdot d\log n}

\displaystyle\geq(1-o(1))\exp\left(-\left(\frac{1+\epsilon}{d}+\left(\frac{1+\epsilon}{d}\right)^{2}\right)10b\cdot 3\epsilon\cdot d\log n\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-50b\epsilon\cdot\log n\right)\geq n^{-50b\epsilon}\geq n^{-0.1},

\displaystyle|N_{G[V(G)\backslash A_{i}]}(S)|=|N_{H_{1}}(S)|\leq 10b|S|\leq 10b\cdot 3\epsilon\cdot d\log n,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X]\geq|I_{0}|\cdot n^{-0.1}\geq\frac{n}{200b^{2}k^{2}}\cdot n^{-0.1}=\frac{n^{0.9}}{200b^{2}d^{4}\log^{2}n}.

Now, since

contains

X\geq 2

7
Concluding Remarks

We have proven that in the site percolation model on
d
-regular graphs, for both constant-degree and growing-degree cases, we obtain the ERCP under a very mild assumption on the global edge expansion property, supported by a good control over the expansion of sets of size up to
\text{poly}(d,\log n)
.

Our result recovers and improves upon the previously known results for random
d
-regular graphs and for the hypercube
Q^{d}
, and thereby, resolves the questions concerning them in full. To cover pseudo-random graphs as well, we gave a concrete statement which shows that the ERCP holds in full for
(n,d,\lambda)
