Long-term safety and continued clinical benefit of erenumab 70 mg in Asian patients with chronic migraine: an open-label extension of the phase 3 DRAGON study
Authors: Shengyuan Yu, Byung-Kun Kim, Hebo Wang, Jiying Zhou, Partha Banerjee, Jeremie Lincy, Evgeniya Reshetnyak, Zheng Li, Denise Leclair, Shuu-Jiun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundErenumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting the calcitonin gene-related peptide receptor. Several double‐blind studies have demonstrated the clinical benefits of erenumab in migraine prevention. However, long-term studies on safety and effectiveness of erenumab are lacking in Asian population.ObjectivesTo evaluate the long-term safety and continued clinical benefit of erenumab 70 mg in adult patients with chronic migraine (CM) in China and other Asian countries.MethodsThis was an open-label extension of the Phase 3 DRAGON study. Upon completion of the 12-week, double-blind treatment phase (DBTP) of the DRAGON study, randomized patients who met the inclusion criteria and provided consent transitioned into an open‐label treatment phase (OLTP) lasting until erenumab approval in the patient’s country. During OLTP, patients received once-monthly subcutaneous injection of erenumab 70 mg, irrespective of their initial treatment allocation in DBTP. Safety was assessed by collecting all adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs) including their severity and relationship to study treatment. Effectiveness outcomes included clinical global impression (CGI) [severity (CGI-S), improvement (CGI-I), and efficacy index (CGI-E)].ResultsOf the 557 randomized patients (DBTP), 456 from 50 centers participated and 226 (49.6%) completed OLTP. The median duration of exposure to erenumab was 79.9 weeks (interquartile range: 39.9-130.5). Overall, 328 (71.9%) patients reported at least one AE during OLTP. AEs suspected to be related to treatment were reported in 69 (15.1%) patients, with constipation (34 [7.5%]) being the most frequent treatment-related AE. There were 13 (2.9%) patients who discontinued the study drug due to AEs. Breast cancer was reported as an AE leading to discontinuation in 2 (0.4%) patients (not treatment related). SAEs were reported in 45 (9.9%) patients during OLTP, of which only 3 (0.7%) were treatment-related (all resolved). Continued clinical benefit with erenumab 70 mg as assessed by CGI E/I/S scores were observed for participating patients during OLTP.ConclusionErenumab 70 mg showed a consistent and well-tolerated safety profile observed over a longer treatment duration along with sustained clinical benefit. No new safety signals were observed versus DBTP and prior global phase 3 studies.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov ID (DRAGON) NCT03867201 (registration date 2019-03-07).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s10194-026-02406-y.

Introduction

Migraine is the world’s second leading cause of years lived with disability (YLD) [1], accounting 43.4 million cases (95% UI 6.74–95.1) in 2021 and projected to reach 52.0 million cases (8.7–111.6) by 2050 [1, 2]. The prevalence of migraine is high and continues to increase in Asia, with 683,514,637 cases reported in 2021 [3]. Migraine is classified by headache frequency as episodic or chronic migraine (EM or CM): EM is characterized by the occurrence of < 15 headache days/month, which on some days are migraine headaches [4], whereas CM is defined as ≥ 15 headache days/month for 3 months, including ≥ 8 migraine days/month [5].

Traditionally, oral migraine preventives have been used as a standard of care for migraine prevention [6]. They were not developed specifically for migraine. For instance, flunarizine (CHIRA database: 2016 to 2017) is the most prescribed oral migraine preventive medication in China, with the highest usage rate (87.6%) among those receiving preventives [7]. The clinical experience with oral migraine preventives generally suggests low adherence and persistence in patients with CM [6, 8]. Although there are few studies from Japan that evaluated the safety and efficacy of Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) monoclonal antibodies in CM [9–11], there is still an unmet need for effective migraine-specific preventive treatments in China and other Asian countries. CGRP monoclonal antibodies that were developed specifically to prevent migraine have revolutionized the treatment landscape [12]. Several double-blind studies have demonstrated the clinical benefits of monoclonal antibodies targeting the CGRP pathway [13]. The success of these monoclonal antibodies has opened new avenues for migraine research and treatment.

Erenumab (AMG334) is the first and only fully human monoclonal antibody against the CGRP receptor [14]. On May 17, 2018, the US FDA approved erenumab for the preventive treatment of migraine in adults. This approval was based on data from the phase II registration trial in CM (ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT02066415) [15]. Erenumab is now approved in multiple countries for the preventive treatment of migraine (CM and EM) in adults [16–19]. Recently, findings from the phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled DRAGON study (ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT03867201) demonstrated the efficacy and safety of erenumab 70 mg following 12 weeks of use in patients with CM from China and other Asian countries [20]. Treatment with erenumab resulted in a clinically significant decrease in monthly migraine days (MMD) and a significant increase in the likelihood of achieving a ≥ 50% reduction in MMD within a 3-month duration versus placebo. Erenumab showed a favorable safety profile, without any new safety signals or elevated risks of known adverse drug reactions. Based on findings from the DRAGON study, erenumab was approved in 2023 for migraine prevention in China [20, 21].

Chronic migraine is a condition that often persists throughout a patient’s lifetime, necessitating multiple years of treatment. It is therefore important to establish a favorable long-term benefit–risk profile for preventive medications. To date, several double‐blind studies have demonstrated clinical benefits of erenumab in migraine prevention. There still remains a gap in scientific literature on the long-term treatment outcomes of erenumab, especially in Asian population. In this study, we present data on the long-term safety and effectiveness of erenumab (70 mg) from the open‐label treatment phase (OLTP) of the DRAGON study.

Methods

Study design

This OLTP (August 2019 to April 2024) was a continuation of the 12-week, double-blind treatment phase (DBTP) of the DRAGON study, which was conducted at 64 sites in nine countries in Asia including mainland China, India, South Korea, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam. The DRAGON study design is described elsewhere [20]. Patients with CM received once-monthly subcutaneous injection of erenumab 70 mg or placebo (1:1) for 12 weeks. The study included a 2-week screening period, a baseline period of up to 5 weeks, and a 12-week DBTP. Upon completion of DBTP, randomized patients (having met the inclusion criteria and providing consent) could transition into an OLTP of the study, where they received once-monthly subcutaneous injection of erenumab 70 mg irrespective of their initial treatment allocation in DBTP. For patients who were unable to tolerate the protocol-specified dosing scheme, dose interruptions were permitted. Patients completing DBTP but not entering OLTP were followed for a period of 8 weeks after treatment completion as part of a safety follow-up.

Ethical considerations

The DRAGON study protocol and all amendments were reviewed by the Independent Ethics Committee (IEC) or Institutional Review Board (IRB) at each study center. The study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E6 guideline for Good Clinical Practice and the ethical principles laid down in the Declaration of Helsinki.

All patients provided written informed consent to participate in OLTP and before undergoing any study-specific procedures.

Study patients

Eligibility criteria for enrollment in the DRAGON study have been previously reported [20] (Suppl. Table 1). The study population consisted of male and female patients aged 18 to 65 years, with a documented history of CM (with or without aura) for at least 12 months before screening as defined by the International Classification of Headache Disorders, 3rd edition (ICHD-3) [5]. To be eligible to continue in OLTP, patients had to complete DBTP, sign informed consent, and not meet any of the exclusion criteria. During the OLTP, the investigators were blinded to participants status of the DBTP.

Endpoints and assessments

Safety assessments consisted of collecting all AEs and serious AEs (SAEs), including their severity and relationship to study treatment. Adverse events were assessed and coded using the Medical Dictionary of Regulatory Activities (MedDRA; Version 27.0) terminology. Safety assessments also included recording of relevant medical history/current medical conditions, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), clinical laboratory evaluations (including hematology and clinical chemistry), abnormal liver laboratory tests and liver events, ECG, vital signs, and physical examinations.

Treatment effectiveness was assessed using the Clinical Global Impression-Improvement (CGI-I) scale. The CGI comprises three companion tools that allow the clinician to rate the change in severity of an illness over time. The 7-point CGI-Severity (CGI-S) evaluates symptomatology and the impact of illness on functioning and provides a reference from which changes in illness over time can be measured. In OLTP, the CGI-S scores were reported at Week 12 of OLTP to create a reference for the subsequent CGI-Improvement (CGI-I) assessments. CGI-I evaluates changes in clinical status over time. CGI-I was assessed every 12 weeks during OLTP, starting from Week 24. The third companion tool is the CGI-Efficacy score (CGI-E) that evaluates both therapeutic efficacy and treatment-related adverse events (TEAEs). CGI-E was assessed every 12 weeks during OLTP, starting from Week 24.

Statistical analysis

The open-label treatment analysis set (OAS) consisted of all patients who consented to enter OLTP and who had taken at least one dose of erenumab during OLTP. Safety analyses were conducted based on the OAS. AEs and SAEs reported during OLTP are presented by the actual treatment received during DBTP and overall.

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS® statistical software (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA.) version 9.4 or higher.

Results

Patient demographics and baseline disease characteristics

In the core DRAGON study, 791 patients were screened and 557 were randomized (1:1) to receive either erenumab 70 mg or placebo. Of the 557 randomized patients, 456 participated in OLTP at 50 centers (30 in mainland China, 7 in South Korea, 9 in Taiwan, 2 in India, and 2 in Vietnam). Regardless of previous exposure to erenumab or placebo during DBTP, all patients who entered OLTP received erenumab. Detailed demographic and baseline disease characteristics of the patients who entered OLTP are presented in suppl. Tables 2 and elsewhere [20].

Of the 456 patients who entered, 226 (49.6%) completed OLTP, and the remaining 230 (50.4%) discontinued (Table 1). The most frequently reported reason for discontinuation from OLTP was subject decision (38.4%). A total of 219 (48.0%) patients had at least one protocol deviation (PD) during the study. The number of patients with a PD unrelated to COVID-19 were 91 (20%). All patients were active in OLTP during the COVID-19 pandemic, resulting in 163 (35.7%) PD related to COVID-19. The median duration of exposure to erenumab during OLTP was 79.9 weeks (interquartile range: 39.9-130.5).

Safety

Adverse events

Overall, 328 (71.9%) patients reported at least one AE during OLTP (Table 2). AEs were suspected to be related to treatment in 69 (15.1%) patients, with constipation being the most frequently reported treatment-related AE (7.5%) (Table 3). SAEs were reported in 45 (9.9%) patients during OLTP, of which only 3 (0.7%) were considered treatment-related (Table 2). Treatment-related SAEs were anemia, syncope accompanied with tooth injury, and chronic hepatitis B. Most AEs reported by patients were either Grade 1 (68 patients, 14.9%) or Grade 2 (206 patients 45.2%) in severity. AEs with Grade 3 severity were reported in 48 patients (10.5%). Grade 4 AEs were reported in 6 (1.3%) patients during OLTP, none of which were considered treatment-related. Discontinuation of the study drug due to AEs was reported in 13 (2.9%) patients (Table 2). Breast cancer was reported as the AE leading to discontinuation in 2 (0.4%) of these patients (these AEs were assessed as not treatment related). A total of 5 (1.1%) patients discontinued due to AEs reported as related to treatment (Table 2). Discontinuation of the study drug due to SAEs was reported in 5 (1.1%) patients; of those, 2 (0.4%) SAEs were considered related to treatment. No deaths or life-threatening events were reported.

There were no clinically significant abnormalities in hematology, clinical chemistry, ECG, or vital signs. Notable changes (either low or high) in sitting pulse rate were observed in 11% patients (n = 45/408) during OLTP. Notable changes in sitting systolic BP and sitting diastolic BP were observed in 5.4% (n = 22/408) and 6.4% (26/408), respectively (Suppl. Table 3). Furthermore, as the study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, there was a high frequency of COVID-19 infections, rising to 19.1% (87 patients). In OLTP, following COVID-19 infections, the other most common TEAE reported by all participants were upper respiratory tract infection (13.6% [62 patients]), constipation (9.4% [43 patients]), and dizziness (5.9% [27 patients]).

Clinical benefit assessed through CGI

The CGI consisted of three separate assessments by investigator during the OLTP: CGI-S, CGI-I and CGI-E. The CGI-S is rated with a 7-point scale, ranging from 1 (normal) to 7 (among the most extremely ill). The CGI-I is also rated with a 7-point scale, ranging from 1 (very much improved) to 7 (very much worse). The CGI-E is a 4 × 4 rating scale, with scores ranging from 1 (marked improvement and no side-effects) to 16 (unchanged or worse and outweighs therapeutic effect). The below data from three different CGI scores were assessed for continued clinical benefit of erenumab 70 mg to Asian patients with CM who transitioned from DBTP to OLTP.

CGI-I based on shift from CGI-S

At Week 24, a shift from the CGI-S ‘mildly ill’ category (observed in 106 [25.3%] patients) to the CGI-I ‘very much improved’ or ‘much improved’ categories was observed in 39 (9.3%) and 43 (10.3%) patients, respectively (Fig. 1). A shift from the CGI-S ‘moderately ill’ category (observed in 156 [37.2%] patients) to the CGI-I ‘very much improved’ or ‘much improved’ categories was observed in 30 (7.2%) and 80 (19.1%) patients, respectively (Fig. 1). A shift from the CGI-S ‘severely ill’ category (observed in 25 [6.0%] patients) to the CGI-I ‘very much improved’ or ‘much improved’ categories was observed in 6 (1.4%) and 14 (3.3%) patients, respectively (Fig. 1).

Overall shifts in CGI category over the duration of OLTP were also assessed at the end of treatment (EOT) visit. Upon entering the OLTP most patients were classified as either mild (n = 105; 24.9%), moderate (n = 154; 36.5%), or markedly ill (n = 97; 23.0%) (Fig. 2). Significant improvements in their condition as indicated by a shift to the “very much improved” CGI-I category was observed in 36 (8.5%), 45 (10.7%), and 29 (6.9%) patients in each respective mild, moderate, or markedly ill category. Conversely, only a small proportion of patients (1 [0.2%], 5 [1.2%], and 7 [1.7%] in the CGI-S mild, moderate, or markedly ill GGI-S) shifted to a worsening CGI-I category (Fig. 2).

Across all levels of illness impact at Week 12 (indicated by CGI-S category), most patients showed shifts in CGI-E categories indicating ‘marked improvement’ in therapeutic effect at Week 24 with most also shifting in CGI-E categories that showed AEs did not affect, or did not significantly affect, patient’s functioning at Week 24 (Suppl. CGI-I by visit

At Week 24 (indicated by CGI-I category), most patients reported improvement in CGI-I categories 1 to 3 (Suppl Fig. 2). A similar pattern was observed in CGI-I categories 1 to 3 starting from Week 24 through the EOT visit (Suppl Fig. 2).

Discussion

DRAGON was a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study [20] with a design similar to the global pivotal CM study [12]. The study demonstrated efficacy and safety of erenumab 70 mg in patients with CM from China and other Asian countries [20]. The findings from the DRAGON study [20] were consistent to the EMPOwER study [22] in patients with EM. The EMPOwER study [19] compared both 70 mg and 140 mg doses of erenumab with placebo; unlike the DRAGON placebo-controlled study [20] and its subsequent OLTP in which 70 mg dose of erenumab alone was selected. The selection of 70 mg dose of erenumab was based on findings from the previous global pivotal CM and EM studies [9, 15] that showed no substantial differences in efficacy between the two erenumab doses (70 mg and 140 mg), and a better safety profile for the 70 mg dose.

The OLTP extension of the DRAGON study aimed to provide continued access to erenumab in adult patients with CM, until its launch in their respective countries. This open-label extension was the first to support the use of erenumab as a long-term treatment choice for patients with CM in China and other Asian countries. This study demonstrated continued clinical benefit of erenumab and a favorable long-term safety profile consistent with that of the primary analysis [20], and no new safety signals beyond those observed in 12-week DBTP [20] and other global studies [9, 15]. Together, the findings provide evidence of the positive benefit/risk profile of the long-term use of erenumab in patients with chronic migraine.

The rates of erenumab discontinuations due to AEs are usually low. An integrated safety analysis (2375 patients) showed a lower proportion of patients discontinued erenumab treatment across double-blind randomized studies (placebo: 1.2% [12/1043]; erenumab 70 mg: 1.3% [12/893]; erenumab 140 mg: 1.8% [9/507]) and their extensions (erenumab 70 mg/140 mg: 2.7% [65/2375]) [23]. In the DRAGON study DBTP [20], the proportion of patients who discontinued due to AEs was as low as 0.7% (2/279 patients) in those receiving erenumab 70 mg (anaphylactic reaction [n = 2]; both patients recovered without sequalae). In the present OLTP, the rate of discontinuation due to AEs in those receiving erenumab 70 mg were 2.9% (13/456 patients), and 1.1% [5/13] were related to treatment. The incidence of TEAEs and SAEs reported in OLTP were comparable with the DRAGON study DBTP [20] and the global pivotal study in CM [12]. There were no clinically significant abnormalities in hematology, clinical chemistry, ECG, or vital signs (with the exception of sitting pulse rate, sitting systolic blood pressure (BP), and sitting diastolic BP [Suppl. Table 3]) reported with erenumab 70 mg. No deaths were reported during the study (including both DBTP and OLTP).

The efficacy of erenumab was assessed during the 12-week DBTP of the DRAGON study [20]. In OLTP three different CGI tools (CGI-S, CGI-I, and CGI-E) were used to measure illness impact at the end of DBTP (Week 12) and changes in the physicians’ perceptions of therapeutic and adverse effects with longer term erenumab treatment. Notably more patients shifted in CGI categories indicating continuing therapeutic efficacy and limited effect of AEs (as measured by CGI-E) and categories indicating a reduction in illness impact (as measured by CGI-I) than shifted in categories indicating a lack of efficacy, a significant impact of AEs, or increase in illness burden. Furthermore, this proportional pattern of shifting to continuing efficacy, no significant AE effect, and reduced illness impact was observed across all categories of disease impact at Week 12 (as measured by CGI-S). Thus, the OLTP extension of the DRAGON study has demonstrated the continued clinical benefit of erenumab 70 mg with longer-term use.

Several limitations of the study should be noted. A key limitation is the open-label design without a placebo control, which may potentially introduce bias in the effectiveness of treatment. There are no comparisons with control groups possible in the open-label-extension design. Second, as the duration of a study increases the number of patients continuing in the study may decline due to factors unrelated to effectiveness of treatment. Third, the main efficacy outcome measures for migraine preventive treatments were not assessed—as MMD and modified migraine disability assessment are critical to measure migraine frequency and impact of migraine on patient lives. Finally, the COVID-19 pandemic may have limited or prevented on-site study visits. However, regular phone or virtual calls occurred (at the time of every scheduled visit or more frequently if needed) for safety monitoring and discussion of the patient’s health status until the patient could visit the site.

To date, limited data is available regarding the long-term safety and tolerability of erenumab and other monoclonal antibodies for CM prevention in patients from China and other Asian countries. Our findings demonstrate a consistent and well-tolerated safety profile observed over a longer treatment duration (12-week DRAGON study DBTP and OLTP) along with sustained clinical benefit of erenumab 70 mg (as assessed by CGI E/I/S scores). The safety findings were consistent with those established in the primary analysis and prior erenumab studies. No new safety signals were identified versus previous trials.
