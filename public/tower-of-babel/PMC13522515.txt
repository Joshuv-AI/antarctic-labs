Perfluorocubane as a σ* orbital-based electron acceptor beyond classical electron-transfer models
Authors: Midori Akiyama, Keima Higashio, Hikaru Sotome, Kosaku Kato, Akira Yamakata, Shunsuke Yabu, Hirofumi Sato, Masahiro Higashi, Kenji Komaguchi, W. Ryan Osterloh, Hiroshi Imahori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Perfluorocubane is an unconventional electron acceptor whose lowest unoccupied molecular orbital is composed entirely of σ* orbitals. In contrast to conventional acceptors that stabilize excess charge through π*-orbital delocalization, perfluorocubane confines an electron with its compact cubic scaffold, giving rise to an almost point-charge-like electronic structure. Such an electronic configuration is expected to produce electron-transfer (ET) behavior beyond established donor–acceptor models. Here we investigate intramolecular photoinduced ET in a donor–acceptor molecule combining dimethylfluorene and perfluorocubane. Picosecond time-resolved transient absorption spectroscopy directly reveals charge separation and recombination in polar solvents. Analysis of the ET kinetics within the semiclassical Marcus framework yields an unusually large electronic coupling together with a substantial reorganization energy, an uncommon combination in π-conjugated donor–acceptor systems. These distinctive ET characteristics are attributed to the σ*-derived LUMO and the compact cubic architecture of perfluorocubane, establishing this molecule as a platform for accessing previously unexplored regimes of molecular electron-transfer chemistry.

Introduction

Electron donor and acceptor molecules play crucial roles in organic functional materials. Traditionally, π-conjugated compounds serve as the primary donors and acceptors. In classical acceptor molecules, π-conjugation lowers the LUMO energy level and works to stabilize the accepted electron through delocalization over the molecular surface via π* orbitals. In 2022, we developed a class of electron acceptors based on a non-π-conjugated architecture, namely perfluorocubane. In this molecule, multiple σ* orbitals of C–F bonds converge inside the cubic scaffold to form a stabilized vacant orbital that functions as the LUMO (Fig. 1a)1–5. We successfully synthesized perfluorocubane via direct fluorination using F2 gas and determined its LUMO energy level to be −2.7 eV. Furthermore, its one-electron-reduced species was observed in a glass matrix at 77 K by ESR measurements, confirming its ability to accommodate an electron within the cubic skeleton, a phenomenon aptly described as an electron in a cube6,7. While the precise nature of this confined electronic state remains under discussion8–10, this mode of electron acceptance is fundamentally distinct from that of conventional π-conjugated acceptors, as the accepted electron is associated with the cage interior, providing a molecular platform that approximates a point-charge-like electronic structure. To evaluate ET properties, donor–acceptor (D–A) linked molecules, in which a donor and an acceptor are connected via a bridge, are frequently employed11–23. The covalent linkage established a fixed electronic coupling (V) between the donor and acceptor, eliminating complexities associated with intermolecular ET in solution due to diffusion. As a result, these linked systems enable precise experimental determination of key ET parameters, including V and reorganization energy (λ)24. The magnitude of V is influenced by the orbital overlap between the donor and acceptor moieties through the bridge, and thus increases as the D–A separation distance (RD–A) decreases25. In contrast, λ, which depends on multiple factors including molecular size, electronic structure, separation distance, and the surrounding environment, exhibits the opposite trend: the solvent reorganization energy (λs) generally decreases with decreasing RD–A. Consequently, the simultaneous realization of both a large λ and a strong V is intrinsically difficult, representing a longstanding challenge in ET chemistry.

This report explores intramolecular photoinduced ET using perfluorocubane as an acceptor. Its point-charge-like electron-accepting nature enables the simultaneous achievement of a large reorganization energy and strong electronic coupling. A D–A linked molecule, composed of 9,9-dimethylfluorenyl (D) and perfluorocubyl (A) structure (Flu-Cub, Fig. 1b), has been thoughtfully designed and characterized via spectroscopic and electrochemical methods. Furthermore, the ET dynamics of this D–A linked molecule in polar solvents are examined by means of picosecond time-resolved transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy, which is compared to a reference system comprised of 9,9-dimethylfluorenyl linked to a non-fluorinated cubane lacking the electron-accepting properties (Flu-HCub, Fig. 1b). Flu-Cub demonstrates a markedly larger λ value than previously studied π-conjugated D–A pairs while maintaining relatively strong V. This characteristic is consistent with the highly localized nature of the acceptor within the cubic framework.

Results

The D–A linked molecule (Flu-Cub) was strategically designed to investigate the electron-accepting nature of the perfluorocubyl (Cub) structure (Fig. 1b). Given the low molar absorption coefficient of perfluorocubane6, initiating photoinduced ET via selective photoexcitation of the donor moiety is preferable. 9,9-Dimethylfluorene was selected as the donor to meet specific criteria: Its excitation energy is lower than that of the perfluorocubane (290 nm) to prevent undesirable energy transfer and allow selective excitation26. Moreover, the introduction of methyl groups serves to suppress deprotonation in the oxidized form, mitigating the risk of undesired chemical reactions27,28.

DFT calculations (B3LYP/6-31 G(d))29,30 reveal that the HOMO and LUMO of Flu-Cub are distinctly localized on the donor and acceptor moieties, respectively (Fig. 2). The HOMO orbital extends into the methylene group of the phenyl ring in the bridge, whereas the LUMO is similarly spread over the carbonyl group of the amide. Based on this spatial distribution, the edge-to-edge separation distance between the benzylic α-carbon and the carbonyl carbon was defined as D–A distance (Ree). Ree is estimated to be 3.40 Å, indicating the formation of a close contact ion pair in the charge-separated state (vide infra). For comparison, Flu-HCub, featuring a non-fluorinated cubyl group in place of the perfluorcubyl group, was also designed. Its HOMO is similar to that of Flu-Cub, while its LUMO is also localized on the donor moiety, a result consistent with the absence of fluorine on the cubyl moiety. Synthesis of Flu-Cub was achieved by the condensation of (4-(9,9-dimethylfluoren-2-yl)phenyl)methanamine and heptafluorocubane carboxylate ester (For details, see the SI). Picosecond time-resolved transient absorption (TA) spectra were measured in polar solvents to investigate the excited-state dynamics of Flu-Cub and its reference Flu-HCub without fluorine atoms on the cubyl moiety. In the TA spectrum of Flu-HCub in BN, an absorption at 560 nm emerged and decayed over several hundred picoseconds (Fig. 4a). Global analysis of the TA profile identified three distinct lifetime components (τ) of 46, 311, and 1959 ps (Figs. 4b, S9a), which are respectively attributed to the vibrational relaxation, subsequent structural relaxation in the S1 state, and the S1 state lifetime of the donor moiety31. In contrast, the TA spectrum of Flu-Cub in BN exhibited a TA peak at 560 nm, with a faster decay occurring within tens of picoseconds, eventually showing peaks at 460 and 560 nm (Fig. 4c). Global analysis revealed three lifetime components: τ = 5, 70, and 868 ps (Figs. 4d, S9b). The first component is attributed to the internal conversion from the S2 to the S1 state and subsequent vibrational cooling. The second component resembles τ2 observed in Flu-HCub, but decays more rapidly, suggesting that the S1 state of the donor moiety is efficiently quenched by ET to the acceptor moiety. The third component presents moderate positive peaks at 470 and 560 nm, alongside broader signals at 880 and 990 nm. Experimental absorption spectra of the donor radical cation and the acceptor radical anion could not be obtained via spectroelectrochemical analysis and chemical oxidation/reduction due to their limited stability in solution on millisecond time scales6,32. Therefore, we employed the low-temperature matrix-isolation technique to obtain the absorption spectra of Flu-ref•+ and Cub-ref•−33 (Fig. S10). Flu-ref and Cub-ref were dissolved in cryogenic matrices (a 1:1 v/v mixture of trichlorofluoromethane and 1,2-dibromo-1,1,2,2-tetrafluoroethane for Flu-ref, and 2-methyltetrahydrofuran for Cub-ref, respectively) and irradiated with γ-rays at 77 K. After photobleaching of matrix-derived absorption features, absorption spectra were recorded at 77 K. Flu-ref•+ exhibits broad absorption peaks at 800–1000 nm with vibronic structure, together with bands below 600 nm, in good agreement with previously reported radical cations of structurally related fluorene derivatives34,35. Minor differences in peak positions and relative intensities are likely attributable to differences in the solvents used for the measurements. These characteristic features correspond to the third component (τ3) in Fig. 4d. In contrast, the radical anion Cub-ref•− displays a prominent absorption peak at 330 nm, but no characteristic absorption in the 400–1000 nm region (Fig. S10c). This spectral region was excluded from the TA analysis due to overlap with fluorescence from 1Flu*. Nevertheless, considering the efficient quenching of 1Flu* by the Cub unit, τ3 can be confidently assigned to the charge-separated state (Fig. 5). The obtained ET parameters (V and λ) were compared to previously reported examples of photoinduced ET between donor and acceptor molecules in polar solvents (Table 2). Gould and Farid conducted studies on intermolecular photoinduced ET using methylated benzenes, naphthalenes, biphenyls, and phenanthrenes as typical donors, and 9,10-dicyanoanthracene (DCA) and 2,6,9,10-tetracyanoanthracene (TCA) as typical acceptors41. Their findings revealed that, in polar solvents, contact radical-ion pairs (CRIPs) exhibit large V and small λ (entry 2), whereas solvent-separated radical-ion pairs (SSRIPs) display small V and large λ (entry 3). A similar trade-off between V and λ is also observed for intramolecular ET—i.e., linked molecules with short bridge structures exhibit large V and small λ (entries 8–11), whereas those with long bridges show small V and large λ (entries 7 and 12–15). Although the Ree value of Flu-Cub (3.40 Å) is comparable to those of CRIPs reported by Gould and Farid and Flu-Cub exhibits large V (76.3 cm−1) characteristics of CRIPs, it simultaneously exhibits large λ, which is comparable to that of SSRIPs (Fig. 6b). Discussion

We achieved photoinduced electron transfer (ET) involving perfluorocubane, a σ*-orbital-based electron acceptor molecule, which confines an electron with its compact cubic framework. The accepted electron is resides within the cage interior, which is in stark contrast to the accepted electron by conventional π-conjugated acceptor molecules, delocalized over the molecular surface. A donor–acceptor (D–A) linked molecule, Flu-Cub, was synthesized and its photoinduced ET behavior was examined using picosecond transient absorption spectroscopy. Upon photoexcitation, charge separation (CS) and charge recombination (CR) were sequentially observed in polar solvents. The rates and Gibbs free energy changes for CS and CR were plotted and fitted using the Marcus’s semiclassical equation, yielding reorganization energies (λs = 1.08 eV, λv = 0.509 eV) and electronic coupling (V = 76.3 cm−1). Flu-Cub exhibited a combination of a large λ and large V, showing characteristics of contact radical-ion pairs with large V while simultaneously possessing a substantially larger λ than previously reported D–A pairs. This phenomenon is rationalized by the distinct electronic properties of perfluorocubane, where the accepted electron is strongly associated with the compact 3D cage interior through occupation of the σ*C–F orbitals. Overall, this work establishes perfluorocubane as an unconventional electron acceptor exhibiting an unusually large reorganization energy, originating from the point-charge-like nature of the cage-interior electronic state. These findings highlight the potential of localized electronic states for modulating charge-transfer and charge-transport processes and may provide useful insights for the design of next-generation molecular materials.
