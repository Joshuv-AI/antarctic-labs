Abstract
. Real-valued logics have seen a renewed interest in verification for probabilistic and quantitative systems, in particular machine learning models, where they can be used to directly integrate specifications in the training objective. To do so effectively one has to strike a balance between the logical properties of the connectives and their semantics. A major hurdle in this sense is to give “soft” (i.e. differentiable) semantics to additive connectives—in linear and fuzzy logics, additives are necessarily “hard” lattice operations. In this paper, we solve this problem by combining an accurate analysis of the properties of sum and product on the reals with a significant revision of sequent calculus. We introduce ‘quantitative sequent calculi’, which simultaneously generalize hypersequent calculi of fuzzy logics and deep inference, and in which validity of a proof and provability of a sequent are real-valued quantities. We present a family of calculi, p QLL , indexed by a hardness degree p p , prove cut-elimination theorem for them, and show completeness for enriched residuated ‘soft’ lattices. For p = ∞ p=\infty , p QLL reduces to MALL, with provability in p QLL converging to provability in MALL when p → ∞ p\to\infty .

3.
Quantitative Calculi

Formal logic studies the structures which
formalize
logical reasoning.
In doing so, the mathematician juggles three different ‘levels’: an
ambient
level, where formalization happens, and which is thus informal, i.e. not coded in mathematical structure; then a formal level in which both the
alethic
(i.e. ‘metatheoretic’) and the
object
level are involved.
The alethic level formalizes the notion of ‘truth value’ under consideration—most commonly binary—whereas the object level is the logical system being studied,
stricto sensu
.

Sequent calculi, more than other presentations of logical systems, exploit the interplay between alethic and object level by describing object-level connectives as mere internalizations of the alethic-level analogues.
Thus a rule like

∧

R

above shows how
\land
internalizes the alethic level ‘and’.
However, this latter connective is itself distinct from the ambient level ‘and’, which does not appear in the formalization at all, and is only referred to obliquely when substantiating the formal level with meaning.

The moral is that a sequent calculus for quantitative logic thrives in a quantitative alethic level, i.e. that of
[0,\infty]
—a conclusion also drawn by Lawvere
(
36
)
.
Indeed, we can consider the alethic level as the
enrichment base
, familiar to the category theorist.
Consequently,
validity
of proofs and
provability
of sequents are notions valued in
[0,\infty]
, and consequently proofs have a richer combinatorial structure involving different kinds of branching, depending on the connectives they involve.
We stress that valuing validity and provability in the positive reals is
not
a form of semantics, but still a description of syntax.

So let’s fix
0<p\leq\infty

Remark 3.11.

The proof of

Conclusions and Future Work

We have formulated a new ‘quantitative linear logic’.
Although many enlightening studies of logics with quantitative semantics already exist
(
44
;
28
;
40
;
5
;
7
)
, ours aimed to rethink the very fundamentals—properties of additive connectives, interpretation of sequents and validity of proofs—in a quantitative way.
This exercise exposed
p
QLL
as a natural generalisation (or enrichment)
of two established logic traditions: fuzzy and linear.
By going quantitative, we built new bridges between linear and fuzzy logic: first by showing addition on the reals presents a previously-undiscovered additive nature, and then by exhibiting
MALL
as a limit of a family of ‘quantitative fuzzy logics’.

In addition, thanks to its smooth monotonic additives,
p
QLL
promises to fill the existing gaps between fuzzy and
differentiable logics, newly introduced by the machine learning verification community.
The latter have been reported to be problematic from the algebraic and proof-theoretic points of view
(
1
;
49
;
54
)
, and
p
QLL
offers a long-awaited fix for the AI community.
At the same time,
p
QLL
opens the way for exploiting new connections between linear logic and differentiable logics, which goes in line with the recent efforts to connect linear logic and neuro-symbolic AI
(
55
)
.

In future work, we would like to further explore the connection between the behaviour of additives and prelinearity observed in
Section

4.3
.
We expect this will enable to repurpose arguments from categorical logic and/or algebraic semantics of fuzzy logic to prove completeness of (some extension of)
p

in
[0,\infty]
.
Relatedly, a categorical exploration of the semantics of the logic seems warranted, to better understand the behaviour of ‘additive universal properties’ we touched on in

We also set to investigate proof nets for

, adding to the traditional graph-based representation for

Finally, a first-order extension of these ideas is already in the workings, involving ‘soft’ quantifiers whose semantics is integration over probability spaces.
In fact this work is the first of a series trying to build the logical foundation of the ideas of

.

B.5.
The Termination argument

Let us use the notation from
Section

B.1
.
For a proof
\pi
, define the metrics
\operatorname{met}(\pi)=(r_{m},n_{m},n,d)
such that if
\pi
is cut-free, then
\operatorname{met}(\pi)=(0,0,0,0)
, and else:

(1)

r_{m}
is the maximal rank of the cut formula of a cut occurence in
\pi
,

(2)

n_{m}
is the number of cut occurrences of maximal rank in
\pi
,

(3)

n
is the total number of cut occurrences in
\pi
,

(4)

d
is the sum of the depths of all cut occurences in
\pi
.

We consider the lexicographic order over such tuples, which is well-founded.
Now every step of the cut-elimination process is strictly decreasing for this order, so that the process needs to terminate.
Note that the process is defined for every possible case involving a cut occurrence, so it can only halt on a cut-free proof: else, a rewrite could be applied.

B.6.
Decidability

Recall the definition of
provability
.
As observed above, the cut-elimination process is validity non-decreasing and thus we know the provability of a sequent, if it is realized, can be realized by a
CUT
-free proof.
Note moreover that:

(1)

Explicit dualities in
p
QLL
(introduced via the rules
(-)

⟂

L

and
(-)

⟂

R

) can be eliminated without affecting validity, so that we can restrict our attention to derivations of
\Gamma\vdash\Delta
that do not use
CUT
nor
(-)

⟂

L

nor
(-)

⟂

R

. Call these
reduced
.

(2)

We can, again without change in validity, remove any

which uses the empty sequent in one of its branches.

(3)

By inspecting the remaining rules and cases, we observe a form of subformula property: each premise of a rule, if any, is of strictly smaller complexity (understood in the traditional sense as the sum of the number of logical connectives in each formula, over all the formulas appearing in contexts on both sides of the turnstile).

Due to this subformula property, reduced proofs are in finite number, and they can be effectively enumerated. The validity of each
p
QLL
derivation is computable up to any desired precision by real arithmetic.
27
27

27

Observe, in particular, that it is trivial to decide whether the validity of a proof is non-zero, since such validity is given as a finite expression involving only
0
,

, and
