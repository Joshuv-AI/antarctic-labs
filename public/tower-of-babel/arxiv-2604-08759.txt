Optimal Multi-bit Generative Watermarking Schemes Under Worst-Case False-Alarm Constraints
Yu-Shin Huang, Chao Tian, Krishna Narayanan

Abstract
This paper considers the problem of multi-bit generative watermarking for large language models under a worst-case false-alarm constraint. Prior work established a lower bound on the achievable miss-detection probability in the finite-token regime and proposed a scheme claimed to achieve this bound. We show, however, that the proposed scheme is in fact suboptimal. We then develop two new encoding-decoding constructions that attain the previously established lower bound, thereby completely characterizing the optimal multi-bit watermarking performance. Our approach formulates the watermark design problem as a linear program and derives the structural conditions under which optimality can be achieved. In addition, we identify the failure mechanism of the previous construction and compare the tradeoffs between the two proposed schemes.

2.2
Decoding Errors

An effective watermarking scheme should let the decoder correctly infer the message that the encoder intends to transmit, with sufficiently high probability. Let
M
denote the embedded message, and let
\hat{M}
be the message recovered by the decoder, which can be written as
\hat{M}=\gamma(X,Z)
. If
\hat{M}=0
, the decoder interprets the signal as unwatermarked; if instead
\hat{M}=m
for some
m\in[1:T]
, the decoder interprets it as watermarked with message
m
.

Because there are multiple possible watermark messages, the problem can be cast as
(T+1)
-ary hypothesis testing. We denote by
H_{0}
the hypothesis that the text is not watermarked, and by
H_{m}
the hypothesis that the text is watermarked with message
m
.

The decoding performance is characterized by the errors made in the hypothesis test. We denote by
\beta_{m}
the error probability corresponding to hypothesis
H_{m}
, meaning that the decoder should have recovered message
m
but failed to do so.

\displaystyle\beta_{m}(P_{m},\gamma)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X},\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}}P_{m}(x,\zeta)\mathds{1}\{\gamma(x,\zeta)\neq m\},\quad\forall m\in[0:T].

(1)

In particular,
\beta_{m}
is commonly referred to as the miss-detection error for
m\in[1:M]
, while
\beta_{0}
is known as the false-alarm error, which occurs when the decoder reports that a watermark is present even though the text in fact contains no watermark. The worst-case false-alarm probability is expressed as
\sup_{Q_{X}}\beta_{0}(Q_{X}\otimes P_{Z},\gamma)
where
Q_{X}\in\Delta(\mathcal{X})
, representing the largest false-alarm error over all possible choices of any token distribution
Q_{X}
; we will denote this supremum as
\beta_{0}^{*}[f,\gamma]
.
Since
P_{m}
is the output of the encoder
f
, we will also use the notation
\beta_{m}(f,\gamma)
to represent the miss-detection error. Throughout this article, we will use these two expressions interchangeably to refer to the same error quantity.

\displaystyle:=\min_{f,\gamma}\max_{m\in[1:M]}\beta_{m}(f,\gamma)

\displaystyle~1.\quad\beta_{0}^{*}[f,\gamma]\leq\alpha

\displaystyle~2.\quad\text{ $f$ is distortionless}

Construction B: A Psuedo-Token Approach

In Construction B, we build
P_{m}
using a method similar to that described in Algorithm
2
, but here additional “pseudo-tokens” are introduced into the original token set
\mathcal{X}
. Again, assume a non-decreasing token order on the distribution
P_{X}
.

6.1
Construction in Augmented Space

We apply the following lemma to construct an extended vector
P^{\prime}_{X}
from a given
P_{X}
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
P_{X}
be a non-decreasing distribution of length
N
. Then there exists an integer
n\geq 0
such that we can construct an extended non-negative vector
P^{\prime}_{X}
of length
N+n
that is
T
-hot representable. Specifically, when
n=0
, we have
P^{\prime}_{X}=\min\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha}{T},P_{X}\bigr)
. When
n>0
, the components of
P^{\prime}_{X}
add up to
1
, i.e.
P^{\prime}_{X}
itself forms a probability distribution with extra
n
psuedo-tokens.

Proof.

We define
\bm{a}
and
\bm{r}
as

\displaystyle\bm{a}(x)=\min\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{T},P_{X}(x)\right),\;\bm{r}(x)=(P_{X}-\bm{a})(x)=\max\!\left(P_{X}(x)-\frac{\alpha}{T},0\right),

(38)

where
\bm{a}
is same as what we defined in the proof of Proposition
3
, and
\bm{r}
is the same definition as
P_{X}^{(3)}
. We use
R
to denote the sum of the vector
\bm{r}
, consistent with the definition of
R
provided in Algorithm
4
.

If
\bm{a}
itself is
T
-hot representable, then set
n=0
and
P^{\prime}_{X}=\bm{a}
. Otherwise, we choose
n=\lceil\frac{R}{\bm{a}(N)}\rceil
. We then demonstrate that

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{X}=(\bm{a},\tfrac{R}{n}\mathbf{1}_{n})

(39)

is
T
-hot representable, where
\mathbf{1}_{n}
denotes the all-ones vector of length
n
. The sum of
P_{X}^{\prime}
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N+n}P_{X}^{\prime}(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{a}(i)+\sum_{i=N+1}^{N+n}\frac{R}{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{a}(i)+R=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{a}(i)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}[P_{X}(i)-\bm{a}(i)]=1.

Clearly, we have
\tfrac{R}{n}\leq\tfrac{R}{\tfrac{R}{\bm{a}(N)}}=\bm{a}(N)
. Furthermore, because
P_{X}
is a non-decreasing vector, the vector
\bm{a}
is also non-decreasing. Hence,
\max_{i}P_{X}^{\prime}(i)=\bm{a}(N)
.

The vector
P_{X}^{\prime}
is
T
-hot representable since

\displaystyle T\max_{i}P_{X}^{\prime}(i)=T\bm{a}(N)\leq T\frac{\alpha}{T}=\alpha\leq 1=\sum_{i=1}^{N+n}P_{X}^{\prime}(i),

(40)

which satisfies the inequality (
23
).
∎

Lemma
2
provides an extended vector
P^{\prime}_{X}
that is
T
-hot representable. We now construct

under two scenarios: case 1 with

\displaystyle P_{m}(x,\mathbf{0}_{N})=\bm{r}(x),\quad\forall x\in[1:N],\ \forall m\in[1:T].

(41)

For case 2, where

7.1
Invalidity of the Construction by He et al.

A purported construction of
(P_{m},\gamma)
was given in
[
13
]
for the same problem under consideration. In their construction, the decoder
\gamma
is any function for which both
\gamma(\cdot,\zeta)
and
\gamma(x,\cdot)
are bijective for every fixed
\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}_{N}\}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and the size of key set is chosen so that
|\mathcal{Z}|=|\mathcal{X}|+1
. We will demonstrate, via a specific example, that this choice of decoder can not yield a valid solution to Theorem
3
in general.

Consider the setting where
N=3
,
T=2
,
\alpha=0.9
and
P_{X}=(0.01,0.04,0.95)
. In this case, we choose the key set

\mathcal{Z}_{bi}=\{(1,2,0),(0,1,2),(2,0,1),(0,0,0)\}.

It is straightforward to verify that, with this choice of key set
\mathcal{Z}_{bi}
, the decoder
\gamma
satisfies the required condition that both
\gamma(\cdot,\zeta)
and
\gamma(x,\cdot)
are bijection mappings. Their matrix
P_{m}
is shown in Table

7.2
Comparison Between Constructions A and B

We give the two constructions of
P_{m}
in Sections
5
and
6
. The primary distinction between these two constructions lies in how the decoder is defined. In the first construction, we use the key set
\mathcal{Z}^{N}_{T}
as the decoder, whereas in the second construction, we instead employ the key set
\mathcal{Z}^{N+n}_{T}
as the decoder. Consequently, the key set in the second construction may be significantly larger than in the first. The increase in the cardinality of the key set is given by

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{Z}^{N+n}_{T}|}{|\mathcal{Z}^{N}_{T}|}\simeq\frac{(N+n)!(N-T)!}{N!(N+n-T)!}.

(44)

However, if the size of the token set
N
is large and the token distribution is relatively uniform, the value of
n
becomes smaller, which in turn results in a smaller increase in the key size. Although the key set is significantly smaller in the first construction, Construction B is conceptually much more straightforward.

8
Conclusion

We consider the multi-bits generative watermarking under the worst-case false-alarm constraint, which was previously studied by He et al.
[
13
]
. It is shown that the construction given in
[
13
]
is invalid in general, and we provide two new optimal constructions. These new constructions, together with the converse result given in
[
13
]
, fully characterize the optimal miss-detection probability.

It should be noted that the token set
\mathcal{X}
can also be viewed as the extended token sequence set, and the same result applies when the generative probability of the sequence is available. However, computing the generative probability of a sequence is exponential in its complexity, which poses significant challenges for its use in practical applications, compared to the single token generation approach.

In a private communication, the authors of
[
12
,
13
]
informed us that they recently proposed a new deterministic scheme, and kindly shared with us their preprint
[
11
]
. However, the scheme proposed there still induces a larger detection error and is thus not optimal, in contrast to the optimal constructions presented in this work.

We first formulate the optimization problem under their choice of

in Table

as below. The variables are those elements in
P_{m}

\displaystyle\min_{P_{1},P_{2},P_{\mathcal{Z}}}\;

\displaystyle\;\max\left(\sum_{x=1}^{3}\sum_{\zeta\notin\gamma^{-1}_{x}(1)}P_{1}(x,\zeta),\sum_{x=1}^{3}\sum_{\zeta\notin\gamma^{-1}_{x}(2)}P_{2}(x,\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2}\sum_{\zeta\in\gamma^{-1}_{x}(m)}P_{\mathcal{Z}}(\zeta)\leq\alpha,\forall x

\displaystyle P_{m}(x,\zeta)\geq 0,\forall x,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}_{bi},\forall m

This problem is equivalent to the following problem, where we replace the max function in objective function with an additional variable

.

\displaystyle\min_{P_{1},P_{2},P_{\mathcal{Z}},t}

\displaystyle\;\;t

s.t.

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{3}\sum_{\zeta\notin\gamma^{-1}_{x}(m)}P_{m}(x,\zeta)-t\leq 0,\forall m

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{2}\sum_{\zeta\in\gamma^{-1}_{x}(m)}P_{\mathcal{Z}}(\zeta)\leq\alpha,\forall x

\displaystyle P_{m}(x,\zeta)\geq 0,\forall x,\forall\zeta\in\mathcal{Z}_{bi},\forall m

We then write this problem in the following form:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{p}}~~~

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}^{\intercal}\mathbf{p}

s.t.

\displaystyle\mathbf{Ap}\leq\mathbf{b},\;\mathbf{Ep}=\mathbf{c},\;\mathbf{p}\geq\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}=\begin{bmatrix}P_{1},P_{2},P_{Z},t\end{bmatrix}^{\intercal},\;\mathbf{d}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{28},1\end{bmatrix}^{\intercal},\;\mathbf{b}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha\mathbf{1}_{3},~\mathbf{0}_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{\intercal},\;\mathbf{c}=\begin{bmatrix}P_{X},~P_{X},~\mathbf{0}_{8}\end{bmatrix}^{\intercal}

We denote
\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}}~~~

\displaystyle-\mathbf{y}^{\intercal}\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{c}

s.t.

\displaystyle-\mathbf{A}^{\intercal}\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{E}^{\intercal}\leq\mathbf{d},\;\mathbf{y}\geq\mathbf{0},\;\mathbf{z}\;\text{free}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\

0.5\\
0.5\end{bmatrix},~\mathbf{z}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-0.5\\
0.5\\
\mathbf{0}_{4}^{\intercal}\\
