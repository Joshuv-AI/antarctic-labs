Extremal chromatic bounds for distance Laplacian eigenvalues

Abstract

Introduction

A
graph

\displaystyle G=(V(G),E(G))
is a finite, simple, undirected, connected structure, where
\displaystyle V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
is the vertex set and
\displaystyle E(G)\subseteq\{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}:1\leq i<j\leq n\}
is the edge set. The
order
and
size
of
\displaystyle G
are
\displaystyle n=|V(G)|
and
\displaystyle m=|E(G)|
, respectively. For
\displaystyle u,v\in V(G)
, the
distance

\displaystyle d_{G}(u,v)
is the length of a shortest
\displaystyle u
–
\displaystyle v
path, and the
diameter
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(G)=\max\{d_{G}(u,v):u,v\in V(G)\}
. A
proper vertex coloring
of
\displaystyle G
is a partition of
\displaystyle V(G)
into independent sets (color classes), and the least number of classes is the
chromatic number

\displaystyle\chi(G)
, see
[
4
,
5
]
.

Distance-based matrices constitute an important interface between combinatorial structure and linear algebra.
The classical adjacency matrix
\displaystyle A(G)
and Laplacian matrix
\displaystyle L(G)=\Delta(G)-A(G)
(where
\displaystyle\Delta
is the diagonal degree matrix) encode local adjacency information and underpin a large body of spectral graph theory
[
4
,
5
]
. Among their most fundamental properties are:
\displaystyle L(G)
is symmetric positive semidefinite,
\displaystyle L(G)\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{0}
, and the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
equals the number of connected components of
\displaystyle G
, and hence is
\displaystyle 1
when
\displaystyle G
is connected. These features make Laplacian spectra a natural tool for measuring global connectivity and expansion, and for formulating extremal and
interlacing arguments
[
4
,
5
]
.

While
\displaystyle A(G)
and
\displaystyle L(G)
are based on
edges
, many applications (communication cost, chemical structure descriptors,
transportation and routing, network latency) depend primarily on
distances
.
This motivates the
distance matrix

\displaystyle D(G)=(d_{G}(v_{i},v_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{n}
and its Laplacian-type companions.
For a vertex
\displaystyle v
, the

is defined as

\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u,v),

and the diagonal
transmission matrix
is
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(G)=\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v_{n}))
.
The
distance Laplacian matrix
is then defined by
\displaystyle DL(G)=\mathrm{Tr}(G)-D(G).
This matrix was introduced and systematically studied by Aouchiche and Hansen
[
1
]
.
Much like the classical Laplacian,
\displaystyle DL(G)
is symmetric positive semidefinite and satisfies
\displaystyle DL(G)\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{0}
.
Hence its eigenvalues may be ordered as

\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\partial^{L}_{n}(G)=0.

A particularly fruitful direction concerns the interaction between distance Laplacian eigenvalues and graph invariants
that constrain global structure, such as the chromatic number. The chromatic number controls the extent to which a graph can be decomposed into independent sets, and thus regulates the density and multipartite structure that a graph can admit. On the spectral side, the largest distance Laplacian eigenvalue
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)
plays the role of
a distance-based spectral radius and is sensitive to long-range connectivity patterns through
\displaystyle D(G)
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(G)

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)

\displaystyle\chi(G)

In this context, Aouchiche and Hansen

and initiated a systematic study of how many distance Laplacian eigenvalues lie below or above the corresponding chromatic threshold

Their work

The following result gives the sistance Laplacian spectrum of complete multipartite graphs.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
14
]

Let
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}}
be a complete
\displaystyle k
-partite graph on
\displaystyle n=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\ell_{j}

with
\displaystyle\ell_{1}\geq\ell_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\ell_{k}\geq 1
.
Let
\displaystyle p=|\{j:\ell_{j}\geq 2\}|

\Bigl(n+\ell_{1}\Bigr)^{(\ell_{1}-1)},\Bigl(n+\ell_{2}\Bigr)^{(\ell_{2}-1)},\dots,\Bigl(n+\ell_{p}\Bigr)^{(\ell_{p}-1)},n^{(k-1)},0,

so extremal spectral questions may be reduced to edge-maximal models, while Lemma

provides an explicit

3
A color-class majorization principle

Throughout,
\displaystyle\mu_{G}(I)
denotes the number of eigenvalues of
\displaystyle DL(G)
that lie in an interval
\displaystyle I
,

counted with algebraic multiplicity
.
Also, because the eigenvalues are ordered as
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\cdots\geq\partial^{L}_{n}(G)=0
, we have the identity

\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))+\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])=n,

(2)

that is, every eigenvalue lies either below
\displaystyle b_{\chi}
or in
\displaystyle[b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)]
.

Let
\displaystyle\chi=\chi(G)
, and fix an optimal proper
\displaystyle\chi
-coloring with color classes
\displaystyle C_{1},\dots,C_{\chi}
and sizes

\ell_{1}=|C_{1}|\geq\ell_{2}=|C_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq\ell_{\chi}=|C_{\chi}|\geq 1,\qquad\sum_{j=1}^{\chi}\ell_{j}=n.

Let
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
be the complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graph induced by this partition.
Then
\displaystyle G
is a spanning subgraph of
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
. Since each
\displaystyle C_{j}
is an independent set, and every edge of
\displaystyle G
joins two
distinct
color classes.
The complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graph
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
contains
all
such cross-edges, hence

\displaystyle E(G)\subseteq E(K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}})
and therefore
\displaystyle G
is a spanning subgraph of
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi})}
.
Moreover, the sequence
\displaystyle(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi})
depends on the choice of optimal coloring, and we fix one such coloring once and for all. The following result discusses the color-class domination of the spectrum, and presents bound for
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G).

Theorem 3.1
.

With notation as above, for every
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}-1
,

\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq n+\ell_{1}.

More generally, letting
\displaystyle s_{j}=\sum_{t=1}^{j}(\ell_{t}-1)
for
\displaystyle j\geq 1
and
\displaystyle s_{0}=0,
we have

\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq n+\ell_{j}\qquad\text{for all }s_{j-1}+1\leq i\leq s_{j},1\leq j\leq p,

where
\displaystyle p=|\{j:\ell_{j}\geq 2\}|
is the number of color classes of size at least
\displaystyle 2
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle H=K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
. Then as noted above,
\displaystyle G
is a spanning subgraph of
\displaystyle H
, and
\displaystyle E(G)\subseteq E(H)
. Choose any ordering of the edges in
\displaystyle E(H)\setminus E(G)
, and add them one-by-one to
\displaystyle G
. Thus, we obtain a sequence of graphs

G=G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G_{t}=H,

where
\displaystyle G_{r+1}
is obtained from
\displaystyle G_{r}
by
adding
a single edge.
Equivalently,
\displaystyle G_{r}
is obtained from
\displaystyle G_{r+1}
by
deleting
an edge while remaining connected.
Applying the edge-deletion monotonicity Theorem
2.1
to the pair
\displaystyle(G_{r+1},G_{r})
, we have

\partial^{L}_{i}(G_{r})\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(G_{r+1})\qquad\text{for all }i,r=0,1,\dots,t-1.

Chaining these inequalities, we have the componentwise domination as

\partial^{L}_{i}(G)=\partial^{L}_{i}(G_{0})\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(G_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(G_{t})=\partial^{L}_{i}(H)\qquad\text{for all }i.

(3)

Now by Lemma
2.2
, we have the full distance Laplacian spectrum of
\displaystyle H
.
Specifically, the value
\displaystyle n+\ell_{1}
is in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Spec}(DL(H))
with multiplicity of
\displaystyle\ell-1,
and the largest eigenvalue of the set of numbers
\displaystyle n+\ell_{1},n+\ell_{2},n+\ell_{p},n,0,
because
\displaystyle\ell_{1}
is largest, followed by
\displaystyle\ell-2,
and so on. Hence, we have

\partial^{L}_{1}(H)=\partial^{L}_{2}(H)=\cdots=\partial^{L}_{\ell_{1}-1}(H)=n+\ell_{1}.

Together with this and (
3
) we get the result that:
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)=n+\ell_{1}
when
\displaystyle i
is between
\displaystyle 1
and
\displaystyle\ell_{1}-1
.

For the block statement, we note from Lemma
2.2
that: for each
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq p
,
the eigenvalue
\displaystyle n+\ell_{j}
has multiplicity
\displaystyle\ell_{j}-1
. Since the list
\displaystyle(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p})
is nonincreasing, the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle H
are ordered as follows: the first
\displaystyle\ell_{1}-1
eigenvalues equal
\displaystyle n+\ell_{1}
, the next
\displaystyle\ell_{2}-1
eigenvalues equal
\displaystyle n+\ell_{2}
, and so on, until exhausting the classes of size at least
\displaystyle 2
. More clearly, for
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq p
and indices
\displaystyle i
with
\displaystyle s_{j-1}+1\leq i\leq s_{j}
, we get
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(H)=n+\ell_{j}
. Now, applying (
3
) , we obtain
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(H)=n+\ell_{j}
for those
\displaystyle i
.
∎

Define the chromatic threshold as

b_{\chi}=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

The following consequences shows that many distance Laplacian eigenvalues exceed the chromatic threshold.

Corollary 3.2
.

For every connected
\displaystyle G
with
\displaystyle\chi(G)=\chi\leq n-1
,

\left|\left\{\,i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq b_{\chi}\,\right\}\right|\geq\ell_{1}-1\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1.

Equivalently,

\mu_{G}\bigl([0,b_{\chi})\bigr)\leq n-\bigl(\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1\bigr)=n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil+1,

where
\displaystyle\mu_{G}(I)
denotes the number of distance Laplacian eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) that lie in the interval
\displaystyle I
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, we have

\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq n+\ell_{1}\qquad\text{for }1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}-1.

We have to connect back to relate
\displaystyle\ell_{1}
to
\displaystyle\chi.
Since the partition of
\displaystyle V(G)
into color classes of the chromatic number of
\displaystyle G
is equal to the chromatic number, so by pigeonhole principle, we have

\ell_{1}=\max_{1\leq j\leq\chi}|C_{j}|\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

(4)

Therefore, for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq\ell_{1}-1
, we have

\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq n+\ell_{1}\geq n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil=b_{\chi}.

Therefore at least one of the eigenvalues of the form at least

\ell_{1}-1

is such that
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq b_{\chi}
is satisfied.
With the help of (
4
), we get that
\displaystyle\ell_{1}-1\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1
. To the corresponding statement, note that the number of eigenvalues strictly below
\displaystyle b_{\chi}
is
\displaystyle\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))
. Thus, at least
\displaystyle\ell_{1}-1
eigenvalues be greater than or equal to
\displaystyle b_{\chi}
, then the number of eigenvalues in
\displaystyle[0,b_{\chi})
is at most
\displaystyle n-(\ell_{1}-1)
.
Finally,
\displaystyle n-(\ell_{1}-1)\leq n-(\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1)
by (
4
), we obtain the required inequality.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be connected with
\displaystyle n\geq 4
and chromatic number
\displaystyle\chi\leq n-1
.
Then, for all integers
\displaystyle k

\displaystyle 2\leq k\leq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1,

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{k}(G)\geq b_{\chi}.
In particular, when
\displaystyle\chi\leq n-2
(so that
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil\geq 2

\displaystyle k=2
),

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{2}(G)\geq b_{\chi}
.

For an integer
\displaystyle k
with
\displaystyle 2\leq k\leq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1
. By (
4

\displaystyle\ell_{1}\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, and hence

Therefore,
\displaystyle k
lies in the range covered by Theorem

, and we obtain the desired inequality

The final statement is immediate, since
\displaystyle\chi\leq n-2

\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil\geq 2

\displaystyle k=2

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be connected with chromatic number
\displaystyle\chi\leq n-1

\displaystyle\ell_{1}

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle m(I)

\displaystyle DL(G)

\displaystyle\ell_{1}\geq 4

\displaystyle\ell_{1}-1

\displaystyle[b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)]

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle c(\overline{G})

\displaystyle n

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be connected with chromatic number
\displaystyle\chi\leq n-1
.
Then

In particular, if
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle n-1
), then
\displaystyle c(\overline{G})\geq P+1
, and hence

Since
\displaystyle b_{\chi}>n
, all eigenvalues equal to
\displaystyle n

\displaystyle b_{\chi}
.

, there are
\displaystyle c(\overline{G})-1

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{n}(G)=0<b_{\chi}
. Thus, at least
\displaystyle c(\overline{G})

\displaystyle[0,b_{\chi})

\displaystyle n-c(\overline{G})

\displaystyle[b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)]

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\overline{G}

\displaystyle c(\overline{G})\geq P+1
.

4
Structural results via twin cliques and twin independent sets

We revisit the clique and the independent-set distribution results by comparing the explicitly forced eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(v)+1
(clique twins) and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(v)+2
(independent twins) with the chromatic threshold

\displaystyle b_{\chi}=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, using only neighborhood-compression parameters.

If
\displaystyle H
is a clique and
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\setminus H=N
for all
\displaystyle v\in H
, then every vertex of
\displaystyle R=V(G)\setminus(H\cup N)
is not adjacent to any vertex of
\displaystyle H
, since adjacency to a vertex of
\displaystyle H
would place it in
\displaystyle N
. Consequently, for
\displaystyle v\in H
and
\displaystyle u\in R
we have
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)\geq 2
. Next, we presents clique twins eigenvalues which are above the chromatic threshold.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be connected on
\displaystyle n
vertices with chromatic number
\displaystyle\chi
.
Assume
\displaystyle G
contains a clique
\displaystyle H
of size
\displaystyle s\geq 2
such that
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\setminus H=N
for all
\displaystyle v\in H,
for some fixed set
\displaystyle N\subseteq V(G)\setminus H
. Let
\displaystyle R=V(G)\setminus(H\cup N)
so
\displaystyle|R|=n-s-|N|
. Then the following hold.

(a)

\displaystyle DL(G)
has an eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}=\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)+1
with multiplicity at least
\displaystyle s-1
, for any
\displaystyle v\in H
.

(b)

The lower estimate
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}\geq 2n-s-|N|
holds.

(c)

If
\displaystyle s+|N|\leq n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, then
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}\geq b_{\chi}
, and hence

m([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq s-1.

Proof.

(a)
By assumption, for any
\displaystyle v_{i},v_{j}\in H
, we have
\displaystyle N_{G}(v_{i})\setminus H=N_{G}(v_{j})\setminus H
.
Thus
\displaystyle H
is a clique of twin vertices in the sense of Theorem
2.3
, and therefore
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)
is constant on
\displaystyle H
, and hence
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)+1
is a distance Laplacian eigenvalue of multiplicity at least
\displaystyle s-1
. Denote this eigenvalue by
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}
.

(b)
Since
\displaystyle H
is a clique, so
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)=1
for each
\displaystyle u\in H\setminus\{v\}
. Also,
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\setminus H=N
implies that every vertex in
\displaystyle N
is adjacent to
\displaystyle v
, and is outside
\displaystyle H
, so
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)=1
for all
\displaystyle u\in N
. Finally, as observed in the gap-filling remark, no vertex of
\displaystyle R
is adjacent to
\displaystyle v
, and hence
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)\geq 2
for all
\displaystyle u\in R
.
Therefore, we have

\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)=\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(v,u)\geq\sum_{u\in H\setminus\{v\}}1+\sum_{u\in N}1+\sum_{u\in R}2=(s-1)+|N|+2|R|.

As
\displaystyle|R|=n-s-|N|
, so from above, we derive

\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)\geq(s-1)+|N|+2(n-s-|N|)=2n-s-|N|-1,

and hence

\lambda_{H}=\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)+1\geq 2n-s-|N|.

(c)
If
\displaystyle s+|N|\leq n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, then we have

2n-s-|N|\geq 2n-\bigl(n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil\bigr)=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil=b_{\chi}.

Adding (b) yields the result
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}\geq b_{\chi}
.
By (a),
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}
has multiplicity at least
\displaystyle s-1
, and since
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)
is the largest eigenvalue,
so each occurrence of
\displaystyle\lambda_{H}
lies in
\displaystyle[b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)]
. Thus
\displaystyle m([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq s-1
.
∎

If
\displaystyle M
is an independent set and
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)=N
for all
\displaystyle v\in M
, then for distinct
\displaystyle x,y\in M
, and we have
\displaystyle d_{G}(x,y)=2
. Indeed
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle y
are nonadjacent, but they share the same neighborhood
\displaystyle N\neq\varnothing
since
\displaystyle G
is connected, so a common neighbor
\displaystyle z\in N
gives a path
\displaystyle x-z-y
of length
\displaystyle 2
.

The following result gives the independent twins eigenvalue information, which lie above the chromatic threshold.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be connected on
\displaystyle n
vertices with chromatic number
\displaystyle\chi
. Assume
\displaystyle G
contains an independent set
\displaystyle M
of size
\displaystyle s\geq 2
such that
\displaystyle N_{G}(v)=N
for all
\displaystyle v\in M,
for some fixed set
\displaystyle N\subseteq V(G)\setminus M
. For
\displaystyle R=V(G)\setminus(M\cup N)
, the following holds.

(a)

\displaystyle DL(G)
has an eigenvalue
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}=\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)+2
with multiplicity at least
\displaystyle s-1
(for any
\displaystyle v\in M
).

(b)

The lower estimate
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}\geq 2n-|N|
holds.

(c)

If
\displaystyle|N|\leq n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, then
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}\geq b_{\chi}
, and hence

m([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq s-1.

Proof.

(a)
By assumption, the vertices of
\displaystyle M
are pairwise nonadjacent, and have identical neighborhoods. Thus
\displaystyle M
is a set of twin independent vertices in the sense of Theorem
2.4
, so
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)
is constant on
\displaystyle M
, and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{G}(v)+2
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle DL(G)
with multiplicity at least
\displaystyle s-1
. Denote this eigenvalue by
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}
.

(b)
For
\displaystyle v\in M
. By the above fact gives,
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)=2
for each
\displaystyle u\in M\setminus\{v\}
.
Also
\displaystyle d_{G}(v,u)=1
for all
\displaystyle u\in N

\displaystyle N_{G}(v)=N

Remark 4.3
.

5
Minimum distance Laplacian spectral radius at fixed chromatic number

We generalize the large-
\displaystyle\chi
extremal theorem by characterizing all minimizers for all
\displaystyle 2\leq\chi\leq n-1
. Theorem
2.1
is stated for
edge deletion
:
if
\displaystyle G^{\ast}
is obtained from
\displaystyle G
by deleting an edge and
\displaystyle G^{\ast}
remains connected, then

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G^{\ast})\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(G)
.
Equivalently, if
\displaystyle H
is obtained from
\displaystyle G
by
adding
an edge (so
\displaystyle G
is obtained from
\displaystyle H
by deleting one edge), then
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{i}(H)
for all
\displaystyle i.

In particular, adding edges (while keeping the same vertex set) cannot
increase
any distance Laplacian eigenvalue, and thus cannot
increase
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}
.

The following result gives the minimizers of
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}
at fixed
\displaystyle 2\leq\chi\leq n-1
and
\displaystyle n\geq 3.
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Among all connected graphs
\displaystyle G
on
\displaystyle n
vertices with
\displaystyle\chi(G)=\chi
, the minimum possible distance Laplacian spectral radius is

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\mathrm{connected}\ |V(G)|=n,\chi(G)=\chi\end{subarray}}\partial^{L}_{1}(G)=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

Moreover, a connected graph
\displaystyle G
satisfies
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
if and only if
\displaystyle G
is a complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graph

\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
whose largest part has size
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq\chi}\ell_{j}=\ell_{1}=\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil,
equivalently, whose part sizes are as balanced as possible in the sense that all parts have size
\displaystyle\left\lfloor n/\chi\right\rfloor
or
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
and at least one part has size
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a connected graph on
\displaystyle n
vertices with
\displaystyle\chi(G)=\chi
.
Fix an optimal
\displaystyle\chi
-coloring
\displaystyle V(G)=C_{1}\dot{\cup}\cdots\dot{\cup}C_{\chi}
and set
\displaystyle\ell_{j}=|C_{j}|
with

\displaystyle\ell_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\ell_{\chi}
.
Then, by the pigeonhole principle,

\ell_{1}\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

(5)

We prove result in steps. (1) Reduction to complete multipartite graphs. Let
\displaystyle K=K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
be the complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graph induced by the partition
\displaystyle C_{1},\dots,C_{\chi}
. Then
\displaystyle E(G)\subseteq E(K)
, that is,
\displaystyle K
is obtained from
\displaystyle G
by repeatedly adding missing cross-edges. By the edge-addition reformulation of Theorem
2.1
, each added edge weakly decreases every distance Laplacian eigenvalue. In particular, we have

\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{1}(K).

(6)

(2) Evaluating
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(K)
. By Lemma
2.2
, the spectrum of
\displaystyle DL(K)
explicitly, and shows that
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(K)=n+\ell_{1},
since
\displaystyle n+\ell_{1}
is the largest among the eigenvalues
\displaystyle\{n+\ell_{j}:1\leq j\leq p\}\cup\{n,0\}
. Combining with (
6
) and (
5
), we have

\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{1}(K)=n+\ell_{1}\geq n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

(3) Characterizing equality cases. Assume first that
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
. Then equality must hold throughout the chain

\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{1}(K)=n+\ell_{1}\geq n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

Hence,
\displaystyle\ell_{1}=\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
. Next, we show that
\displaystyle G
must be complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite with respect to the coloring.
Suppose, for contradiction, that
\displaystyle G
is
not
complete between some pair of color classes
\displaystyle C_{a}
and
\displaystyle C_{b}
.
Then there exist
\displaystyle x\in C_{a}
and
\displaystyle y\in C_{b}
with
\displaystyle\{x,y\}\notin E(G)
.
Let
\displaystyle H=G+\{x,y\}
be the graph obtained by adding this single cross-edge.
The same vertex coloring remains proper for
\displaystyle H
, so
\displaystyle\chi(H)\leq\chi(G)=\chi
.
Since
\displaystyle G
is a spanning subgraph of
\displaystyle H
, the edge-addition form of monotonicity gives
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{1}(H)
.
Iterating this process over all missing cross-edges, we arrive at
\displaystyle K
with

\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq\partial^{L}_{1}(K)=n+\ell_{1}=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.

But we already have
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, so all inequalities must be tight at each edge addition.
In particular, adding any missing cross-edge cannot strictly decrease
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}
. However, for a graph
\displaystyle G
that is not already complete multipartite, there exists at least one missing cross-edge whose addition
strictly decreases the Rayleigh quotient of a Perron eigenvector of
\displaystyle DL(G)
(this is the standard strictness mechanism used in the edge-monotonicity proofs for
\displaystyle DL(G)
), forcing
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(H)<\partial^{L}_{1}(G)
, a contradiction.
Therefore, no such missing cross-edge exists, and
\displaystyle G
must equal
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
. Equivalently, any minimizer must be edge-maximal subject to
\displaystyle\chi(G)=\chi
, and the edge-maximal
\displaystyle\chi
-chromatic graphs are
precisely the complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graphs. Conversely, suppose
\displaystyle G
is a complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graph
\displaystyle K_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\chi}}
with
\displaystyle\ell_{1}=\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
. Then Lemma
2.2
implies that
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)=n+\ell_{1}=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, thereby proving that such graphs attain the minimum. Finally, the statement about balanced part sizes follows from the fact that
\displaystyle\ell_{1}=\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
and
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\chi}\ell_{j}=n
imply every
\displaystyle\ell_{j}\in\{\left\lfloor n/\chi\right\rfloor,\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil\}
. If some part were
\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor n/\chi\right\rfloor-1
, then to keep the sum
\displaystyle n
fixed another part would exceed
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
, contradicting
\displaystyle\ell_{1}=\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil
.
∎

The following consequence of the above results generalizes and recovers Theorem 4.2 of
[
10
]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

If
\displaystyle n/2\leq\chi\leq n-1
, then
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil=2
and Theorem
5.1
gives

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq n+2,
with equality if and only if
\displaystyle G\cong K_{\underbrace{2,\dots,2}_{n-\chi\text{ parts}},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{2\chi-n\text{ parts}}}.
In particular, the minimizer is unique up to isomorphism in this range.

Proof.

If
\displaystyle n/2\leq\chi\leq n-1
, then
\displaystyle 1<n/\chi\leq 2
, and hence
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil=2
. Applying Theorem
5.1
, we obtain
\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{1}(G)\geq n+2
, and equality holds precisely for complete
\displaystyle\chi
-partite graphs whose largest part has size
\displaystyle 2
. It remains to identify the part-size pattern. Let the
\displaystyle\chi
parts have sizes in
\displaystyle\{1,2\}
, and let
\displaystyle a
be the number of parts of size
\displaystyle 2
. Then the total vertex count is

\displaystyle n=2a+1(\chi-a)=a+\chi,
so
\displaystyle a=n-\chi
. Thus, there are exactly
\displaystyle n-\chi
parts of size
\displaystyle 2
, and the remaining
\displaystyle\chi-(n-\chi)=2\chi-n
parts have size
\displaystyle 1
. This gives the asserted isomorphism type. Uniqueness up to isomorphism follows, since the multiset of part sizes is uniquely determined by
\displaystyle(n,\chi)
via the above counting, and complete multipartite graphs are determined up to isomorphism by their part-size multisets.
∎

\displaystyle G\neq K_{n}

\displaystyle\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))\leq n-2.

\displaystyle b_{\chi}

.

Let
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle n\geq 5

\displaystyle\chi\leq n-1
and threshold

\displaystyle b_{\chi}=n+\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil.
Then the following holds.

(a)

The interval
\displaystyle\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))
satisfies

\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))\leq n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil+1.

Equivalently,
\displaystyle\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1
.

(b)

If
\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(G)\geq 3

\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))\leq n-\max\{2,\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1\}.

Equivalently,
\displaystyle\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq\max\{2,\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1\}.

Proof.

(a)
Corollary
3.2
asserts that at least
\displaystyle\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1
eigenvalues satisfy

\displaystyle\partial^{L}_{i}(G)\geq b_{\chi}

\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1.

Using the counting identity (
2

\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))=n-\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\leq n-(\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1)=n-\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil+1.

(b)
Assume
\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(G)\geq 3
. By Theorem 2.7 in
[
3
]

\displaystyle\mu_{G}([0,b_{\chi}))\leq n-2
, which is equivalent (by (
2

\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq 2.

(7)

On the other hand, part (a) (or directly Corollary
3.2
) implies

\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1.

(8)

Combining (
7

\mu_{G}([b_{\chi},\partial^{L}_{1}(G)])\geq\max\{2,\left\lceil n/\chi\right\rceil-1\}.

Applying (

∎

7
Concluding remarks and further directions

Theorems
3.1
and
5.1
show that optimal colorings
encode substantial spectral information for
\displaystyle DL(G)
, the largest color class forces a
block
of large distance Laplacian eigenvalues,
and the extremal spectral-radius problem at fixed
\displaystyle\chi
reduces to edge-maximality and part-size control.
On the distribution side, Theorem
5.3
unifies chromatic and diameter effects and yields
strict improvements when
\displaystyle\chi
is small.

Natural next ideas include:
(i) sharpening diameter-sensitive lower bounds on
how many
eigenvalues exceed
\displaystyle b_{\chi}
for large diameter;
(ii) characterizing graphs where equality holds in Corollary
3.2
beyond complete multipartite families;
(iii) extending the color-class majorization method to normalized variants of the distance Laplacian.
