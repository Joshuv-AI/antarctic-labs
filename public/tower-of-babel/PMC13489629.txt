The Efficacy of Integrating Problem-Based Learning with the BOPPPS Six-Step Methodology in Endocrinology Clinical Instruction
Authors: Ting Liu, Wanwei Guo, Bo Chen, PengCheng Chen, XiaoDan Xia, Ping Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe objectives of the study were to evaluate the efficacy of the Problem-Based Learning (PBL) with the Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory learning, Post-assessment, and Summary (BOPPPS) PBL-BOPPPS model compared to conventional instruction in endocrinology clinical education, focusing on academic outcomes and learner satisfaction.MethodsThis is a quasi-experimental study and was conducted at the Affiliated Guangdong Second Provincial General Hospital (January 2022–September 2025). A total of 285 students were enrolled (BS Cohort: PBL-BOPPPS, n = 105; TT Cohort: Conventional, n = 180).ResultsThe BS cohort achieved significantly higher median scores across all evaluation components, including written exam (52 vs. 47.5, p < 0.001) and total score (68 vs. 58, p < 0.001). Student satisfaction was also higher in the BS cohort (median 14 vs. 13, p < 0.001). Granular analysis revealed consistently higher satisfaction across all 10 items, with the largest differences in creativity, clinical thinking, interest, and communication skills. Correlation analyses demonstrated significant positive associations between academic performance and satisfaction (rs = 0.433, p < 0.001), attendance and exam performance (rs = 0.7449, p < 0.001), case-based video learning and exam performance (rs = 0.5125, p < 0.001), and clinical thinking with creativity (rs = 0.8604, p < 0.001).ConclusionThe PBL-BOPPPS model significantly improved academic performance and student satisfaction compared to conventional instruction in endocrinology clinical education. Its structured, interactive design enhanced both cognitive and affective learning outcomes, supporting its adoption in competency-based medical education.

Introduction

Medical education is transitioning from passive models toward student-centered, competency-based paradigms that prioritize critical thinking and clinical problem-solving [1]. Endocrinology presents a unique challenge for educators due to its complex integration of foundational science and intricate clinical decision-making [2]. Traditional didactic methods often emphasize theoretical acquisition at the expense of diagnostic reasoning and individualized therapeutic formulation [3, 4]. Consequently, there is a global shift toward innovative teaching strategies that actively engage students in clinical practice [5].

Problem-Based Learning (PBL) utilizes authentic clinical cases to stimulate self-directed learning and has been shown to enhance critical thinking across medical fields [6, 7]. However, the efficacy of PBL can be optimized when integrated with structured pedagogical frameworks [8]. The Bridge-in, Objective, Pre-assessment, Participatory learning, Post-assessment, and Summary (BOPPPS) model provides a systematic six-step structure to maximize classroom effectiveness [9]. This framework is particularly effective in Chinese medical education for facilitating active participation and knowledge retention [10, 11].

The integrated PBL-BOPPPS model has recently emerged as a robust hybrid approach [12, 13]. By leveraging the case-driven nature of PBL with the structured, outcome-focused methodology of BOPPPS, educators can better address complex knowledge structures in specialties like endocrinology [14, 15]. Recent meta-analyses indicate that such integrated models improve core competencies and student satisfaction compared to lecture-based learning [16, 17].

While promising, current evidence for these integrated models often relies on short-term outcomes and quasi-experimental designs [18, 19]. Research suggests that while BOPPPS-based hybrid teaching improves immediate academic performance, further investigation is needed to determine its impact on long-term clinical competence and generalizability [18, 20]. While PBL is widely utilized, its integration with the structured BOPPPS framework remains underexplored in specialized clinical fields like endocrinology. This study provides novel insights into how this hybrid model can bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and complex clinical reasoning, specifically by assessing its impact on students’ comprehensive assessment scores and self-reported satisfaction.

Methods

Study Design, Setting, and Timeline

This is a quasi-experimental study which was performed at the Affiliated Guangdong Second Provincial General Hospital. The study period commenced in January 2022 and concluded in September 2025.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

The inclusion criteria used in this study was that eligible participants were students actively participating in the endocrinology clinical instruction module. The exclusion criteria used was that the students with incomplete or insufficient documentation necessary for comprehensive data analysis were excluded from the study.

Sample Size Calculation

The sample size for this study was primarily determined by the total number of fourth-year medical students eligible for clinical rotation during the academic cycle, resulting in 105 students in the PBL-BOPPPS (BS) cohort and 180 in the Traditional Teaching (TT) cohort [4]. To ensure that this enrollment was statistically appropriate, a prospective power analysis was performed for the primary outcome of final examination scores (0–100 scale).

The required sample size per group (n) was estimated using the formula for comparing two independent means:n=2Zα2+Zβ2*σ2Δ2Where: σ (Standard Deviation) ≈ 5.0. Δ (Clinically meaningful difference in means) = 10.0. Zα2 = 1.96. Zβ = 0.84.

Based on these parameters, a minimum of 4 students per group was required to detect a large effect size. Applying a more conservative approach with a smaller anticipated difference of 5 points (Δ = 5.0, SD = 5.0), the required sample size increased to 17 students per group. Our final recruitment of N = 285 significantly exceeded these requirements, ensuring that the study was adequately powered to detect the observed differences and minimize the risk of a Type II error [21].

Study Population and Stratification

A total of 285 fourth-year undergraduate medical students were enrolled in this study. The students were divided into two groups: the Traditional Teaching (TT) group (n = 105) and the PBL-BOPPPS (BS) group (n = 180). Crucially, all participants were from the same academic year and were at the identical stage of their clinical clerkship syllabus. The variation in group sizes was a result of the existing administrative distribution of students across different clinical rotation blocks during the study period, rather than intentional selection.1.BS Cohort (PBL-BOPPPS Integrated; n = 105): This group received endocrinology clinical instruction integrating PBL with the BOPPPS six-step methodology.2.TT Cohort (Conventional Instruction; n = 180): This concurrent control group experienced the conventional endocrinology instructional paradigm, consisting primarily of lecture-based clinical teaching.

To minimize selection bias, all students followed the same core medical curriculum, and baseline demographic attributes (age and gender) were recorded to ensure comparability between the two cohorts. A quasi-experimental study design was employed in this report. Due to administrative and ethical considerations in a clinical setting, formal individual randomization was not performed. Instead, participants were allocated to either the Traditional Teaching (TT) group or the PBL-BOPPPS (BS) group based on their existing clinical rotation blocks. To ensure comparability, all students were in the same phase of their undergraduate medical education and were taught using the same standardized core curriculum requirements.

Instructional Paradigms

Conventional Endocrinology Instructional Paradigm (TT Cohort)

This instructional paradigm primarily comprised didactic lectures and case-based analyses, supplemented by limited clinical immersions. The foundational focus was on theoretical knowledge, with limited practical training. Didactic lectures served as the cornerstone, covering fundamental hormonal pathways, endocrine pathologies, and diagnostic principles. Instructional modalities included slide presentations, textual references, and facilitator-led discourse [4, 22].

The culminating stage transitioned students from problem resolution to systematic knowledge network construction. Students performed reverse engineering of core issues and completed a “Knowledge Structure Integration Table.” Collaborative group exercises generated a “Diagnostic Thinking Map,” centralizing core diagnostic inquiries and horizontally expanding dependent knowledge nodes (e.g., symptoms, drug mechanisms) while vertically displaying task functions across the six teaching stages. “Problem reconstruction task cards” were devised to promote autonomous learning and structural reflection. All instructional outputs were archived to create individualized “diagnostic thinking growth portfolios” [14].

Student Satisfaction

Student satisfaction was quantified via an institutional questionnaire online supplementary Table 1 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000553243). Each of the 10 questions afforded three response options (0: Dissatisfied, 1: Moderately satisfied, 2: Satisfied), yielding a total satisfaction index ranging from 0 to 20, with higher values correlating with augmented contentment.

Instructor Training and Standardization

To ensure delivery consistency, all instructors participated in a mandatory training program focused on the BOPPPS model. Standardized teaching scripts and case-based templates were used for both the TT and BS cohorts to minimize instructor-led bias.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using InStat 3.01 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Due to the unequal distribution of sample sizes between the two cohorts, data normality was rigorously tested. Comparisons between the two independent groups were performed using the t-test for normally distributed data or the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data to ensure statistical validity. Data normality for continuous variables was rigorously assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. All continuous variables (age, evaluation scores, and student satisfaction score) were determined to be non-normally distributed (p < 0.001 for all). Consequently, continuous variables were presented as median (interquartile range [IQR], Q3–Q1), and group comparisons were performed using the two-tailed non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical demographic data were reported as frequency (n) and percentage (%). For 2 × 2 contingency tables, Fisher’s exact test was used when more than 20% of expected cell counts were less than five; otherwise, the Chi-square test (χ2-test; with Yates’ correction) was adopted. For tables larger than 2 × 2 (e.g., ethnicity), the Pearson’s χ2-test was preferred, but due to expected counts less than five in multiple subclasses, the Fisher-Freeman-Halton test was employed. To evaluate the internal consistency and the relationship between academic performance and student perception, a Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was performed across the pooled data (N = 285). A two-tailed p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant online supplementary Table 2.

Evaluation Test Scores

The BS cohort consistently achieved higher scores across all seven evaluation components compared to the TT cohort Table 2. Median scores for learning of teaching were 3 (IQR: 3–3) in the BS cohort versus 2 (IQR: 2–3) in the TT cohort (p < 0.001). Attendance class showed the largest difference, with BS cohort scoring 4 (IQR: 4–4) compared to 2 (IQR: 2–3) in the TT cohort (p < 0.001). Written exam scores were significantly higher in the BS cohort (median 52, IQR: 51–52) compared to the TT cohort (median 47.5, IQR: 46–48; p < 0.001). Consequently, the total score was greater in the BS cohort (median 68, IQR: 65–68) than in the TT cohort (median 58, IQR: 56–60; p < 0.001). The calculated effect size (Cohen’s d = 1.79) indicates a large educational impact within this cohort, although the absolute numerical difference should be interpreted within the context of the single-center study design. Discussion

The findings of this investigation suggest a considerable improvement in endocrinology clinical education when PBL is integrated with the BOPPPS methodology [1]. This transition toward student-centered paradigms aligns with the global shift to competency-based medical education (CBME), which prioritizes critical thinking and clinical problem-solving in complex, high-stakes specialties like endocrinology [1, 2]. In our study, students in the PBL-BOPPPS cohort achieved higher median total scores and written exam scores compared to the traditional lecture-based cohort [3, 4]. These results are consistent with recent systematic reviews advocating for novel, integrated teaching strategies to improve core competencies in medical education [5, 25].

The structural synergy of the PBL-BOPPPS model addresses the documented limitations of traditional didactic instruction, which often promotes passive knowledge absorption and insufficient skill application [3, 4, 8]. While PBL stimulates active inquiry and self-directed learning, it can sometimes lead to gaps in comprehensive content coverage [7, 26]. The integration of the BOPPPS model provides a systematic, closed-loop framework that directly mitigates these risks [9, 20].

The explicit objectives and mandatory pre-assessment ensure the alignment of case-based learning with core curricular requirements, while the post-assessment and summary phases enforce systematic knowledge consolidation [10, 11]. This systematic guidance transforms the inquiry-driven nature of PBL into a more consistent and effective learning tool [10, 12]. Furthermore, the PBL-BOPPPS intervention demonstrated enhancement in higher order clinical competencies, such as clinical thinking, communication skills, and creativity [13, 27]. This success is attributable to the active interactions embedded in the participatory learning phase of BOPPPS [9, 17]. Such environments necessitate collaboration and dialogue, naturally cultivating skills that are recognized as key outcomes of effective active learning [15, 28].

However, the study has several limitations that necessitate a cautious interpretation of the findings. The non-randomized, quasi-experimental design at a single center introduces potential selection bias and limits generalizability [17, 18, 20]. While objective exam scores showed improvement, higher order outcomes were based on self-reported data, which are susceptible to response bias [17]. Additionally, the focus on immediate post-rotation outcomes means there are no data to assess long-term knowledge retention or the sustained transfer of improved clinical skills into practice [14, 18]. Future multi-center longitudinal studies utilizing workplace-based assessments, such as Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE)s, are warranted to confirm the long-term professional impact of this model.

Conclusion

The integration of PBL and BOPPPS in endocrinology instruction was associated with improved student performance and high satisfaction levels, resulting in higher objective knowledge assessment scores and markedly improved student satisfaction with the development of crucial clinical thinking and communication skills. Despite limitations regarding its non-randomized nature, the framework provides a structured approach for enhancing medical education that warrants further exploration.
