On a Generalized Compartment Model for Ethanol Metabolism in the Human Body
Manh Tuan Hoang, Thi Kim Quy Ngo, Benjamin Wacker

Abstract
We introduce a generalized continuous-time compartment model of ethanol metabolism in the human body that extends a recently developed framework. In the proposed model, we replace the Michaelis-Menten mechanism of the liver’s ethanol metabolism rate with a general class of nonlinear rate functions. This modification provides greater modeling flexibility and enables the model to capture a wider range of hepatic ethanol metabolism dynamics. The qualitative behavior of the proposed ethanol metabolism model is analyzed rigorously. More specifically, we investigate the positivity and boundedness of solutions, as well as the global asymptotic stability (GAS) of the unique equilibrium point using an appropriate quadratic Lyapunov function. Second, we formulate a discrete-time counterpart of the proposed continuous-time model and investigate its dynamical properties. We show that, under an appropriate condition on the time step size, the discrete-time model faithfully reproduces the qualitative dynamical behavior of the corresponding continuous-time system. Lastly, we conduct a series of numerical experiments employing several ethanol metabolism rate functions to support the theoretical results.

\begin{split}A^{\prime}(t)&=-aA(t),\quad A(0)>0,\\

In this work, we rigorously analyze the dynamics of the proposed ethanol metabolism model (

On the other hand, we conduct a set of numerical experiments, in which several ethanol metabolism rate functions are used, to support the theoretical findings. The numerical results provide strong evidence supporting the theoretical findings.

As pointed out by May in his influential work

Motivated by both the practical importance and the rich dynamical features of discrete-time systems, we consider a discrete-time version of (

\begin{split}A_{n+1}&=A_{n}-a\Delta tA_{n},\quad A_{0}>0,\\
B_{n+1}&=B_{n}+b\Delta tC_{n}-b\Delta tB_{n}+a\Delta tA_{n},\quad B_{0}\geq 0,\\
C_{n+1}&=C_{n}+b\Delta tB_{n}-b\Delta tC_{n}-\Delta tf(C_{n}),\quad C_{0}\geq 0,\end{split}

B_{n}

C_{n}

In Section

\Delta t

2
Dynamical Analysis of the Generalized Ethanol Metabolism Model

This section analyzes the global dynamics of the generalized model (
1.4
). First, the existence and uniqueness of solutions can be done by the arguments performed in
[
26
]
. Here, we establish the positivity of the solutions based on a simpler approach than that employed in
[
26
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The model (
1.4
) admits the following set as a positively invariant set

\Omega=\big\{(A,\,B,\,C)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|A,\,B,\,C\geq 0\big\}.

(2.1)

Furthermore, the functions
A(t)
and
S(t)=A(t)+B(t)+C(t)
are nonincreasing function for
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

First, it follows from (
1.4
) that

\begin{split}&A^{\prime}|_{A=0}=0,\\
&B^{\prime}|_{B=0}=bC+aA\geq 0,\\
&C^{\prime}|_{C=0}=bB\geq 0,\end{split}

for all
A,B,C\geq 0
. Therefore, we deduce from
[
24
, Proposition B.7]
that
A(t),B(t),C(t)\geq 0
for
t>0
, whenever
A(0),B(0),C(0)\geq 0
.

From the first equation of (
1.4
), we have
A(t)
is a nonincreasing function.

On the other hand, it follows from the all equations of (
1.4
) that

A^{\prime}(t)+B^{\prime}(t)+C^{\prime}(t)=-f(C(t))\leq 0,

which means that
A(t)+B(t)+C(t)
is nonincreasing function for
t>0
.

In conclusion,
\Omega
forms a positively invariant set of (
1.4
). The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we determine possible equilibrium points of (
1.4
). Any equilibrium point is a solution to the system

\begin{split}0&=-aA,\\
0&=bC-bB+aA,\\
0&=bB-bC-f(C).\end{split}

(2.2)

From the property
(P)
, we conclude that (
1.4
) has only a trivial equilibrium point
E^{*}=(0,\,0,\,0)
. Its global asymptotic stability is established as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

The unique trivial equilibrium point
E^{*}
is (
1.4
) is globally asymptotically stable.

Proof.

First, it follows from the first equation of (
1.4
) that

A(t)=A(0)e^{-at},

which implies that
A^{*}=0
is a globally asymptotically stable equilibrium point of the first equation of (
1.4
). By using sufficient conditions for the global stabilizability of nonlinear cascade systems
[
23
]
, the GAS of (
1.4
) is reduced to that of the following subsystem

\begin{split}B^{\prime}&=bC-bB\\
C^{\prime}&=bB-bC-f(C),\end{split}

(2.3)

which is obtained by substituting
A=0
into the second equation of (
1.4
). We only need to show that
(B^{*},\,C^{*})=(0,\,0)
is a globally asymptotically stable equilibrium point of (
2.3
). Indeed, consider a Lyapunov function candidate defined by

V(B,C)=\dfrac{1}{2}B^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}C^{2}.

(2.4)

The time derivative of
V
along with the solutions of (
2.3
) is computed as

\begin{split}\dfrac{dV}{dt}&=\dfrac{dV}{dB}\dfrac{dB}{dt}+\dfrac{dV}{dC}\dfrac{dC}{dt}\\
&=B(bC-bB)+C(bB-bC-f(C))=-b(B-C)^{2}-Cf(C).\end{split}

Thus,
\frac{dV}{dt}\leq 0
for all
B,C\geq 0
and
\frac{dV}{dt}=0
if and only if
B=C=0
. Consequently, the Lyapunov stability theorem
[
10
]
implies the GAS of
(B^{*},\,C^{*})=(0,\,0)
.

By using the global stabilizability results of two cascade-connected nonlinear systems
[
23
, Corollary 4.3]
, we conclude that
E^{*}
of (
1.4
) is globally asymptotically stable. The proof is complete.
∎

3
Dynamical analysis of the discrete-time model

This section is devoted to analyzing dynamical properties of the discrete-time model (
1.5
). Throughout this section, we additionally assume that the function
f
satisfies:

(P*):
The derivative of
f(C)
at zero exists.

Let us introduce the function

g(C)=\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(0),\quad\mbox{if}\quad C=0,\\
&\\
\dfrac{f(C)}{C}\quad\mbox{if}\quad C\neq 0.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then,
f(C)=Cg(C)
for
C\geq 0
and
g(C)
is continuous. For arbitrary fixed positive values of
A_{0},B_{0}
and
C_{0}
, we set
S_{0}=A_{0}+B_{0}+C_{0}
and define

M=\max_{0\leq C\leq S_{0}}g(C).

(3.2)

The biological relevance of (
1.5
) requires certain conditions on the parameter values. The following result provides conditions under which (
1.5
) is biologically meaningful.

Lemma 3.1

(Biological relevance)
.

The system (
1.5
) is biologically meaningful under the assumption that

\Delta t\leq\min\bigg\{\dfrac{1}{a},\,\,\,\dfrac{1}{b+M}\bigg\}.

(3.3)

Furthermore, under this condition, the sequences
\{A_{n}\}
and
\{A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\}
(
n\geq 0
) are nonincreasing.

Proof.

First, we rewrite (
1.5
) in the form

\begin{split}A_{n+1}&=(1-a\Delta t)A_{n},\\
B_{n+1}&=(1-b\Delta t)B_{n}+b\Delta tC_{n}+a\Delta tA_{n},\\
C_{n+1}&=\big(1-b\Delta t-g(C_{n})\Delta t\big)C_{n}+b\Delta tB_{n}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Thus, we have

\begin{split}A_{1}&=(1-a\Delta t)A_{0},\\
B_{1}&=(1-b\Delta t)B_{0}+b\Delta tC_{0}+a\Delta tA_{0},\\
C_{1}&=\big(1-b\Delta t-g(C_{0})\Delta t\big)C_{0}+b\Delta tB_{0}.\end{split}

On the other hand, it follows from (
3.3
) that

\begin{split}&1-a\Delta t\geq 0,\\
&1-b\Delta t\geq 1-b\Delta t-g(C_{0})\Delta t\geq 1-b\Delta t-M\Delta t\geq 0.\end{split}

Thus, we conclude that:
A_{1},\,B_{1},\,C_{1}\geq 0
.

From (
1.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}&A_{1}-A_{0}=-a\Delta t\leq 0,\\
&\big(A_{1}+B_{1}+C_{1}\big)-\big(A_{0}+B_{0}+C_{0}\big)=-f(C_{0})\Delta t\leq 0,\end{split}

which implies that
A_{1}\leq A_{0}
and
A_{1}+B_{1}+C_{1}\leq A_{0}+B_{0}+C_{0}
.

Repeating the above arguments, we obtain: If
A_{0},B_{0},C_{0}\geq 0
, then
A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\geq 0
,
A_{n}\leq A_{n-1}
and
A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\leq A_{n-1}+B_{n-1}+C_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. This is the desired conclusion. The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we determine the set of equilibrium points of (
1.5
). It is easy to verify that any equilibrium point of (
1.5
) is a solution of the system (
2.2
). Hence, similarly to the continuous-time model (
1.4
), (
1.5
) also possesses a unique equilibrium point
E^{*}=(0,\,0,\,0)
. Its asymptotic stability is established as follows.

Theorem 3.2

(Local asymptotic stability analysis)
.

Under the condition (
3.3
), the trivial equilibrium point of (
1.5
) is locally asymptotically stable for all parameter values.

Proof.

The Jacobian matrix of the system (
1.5
) evaluated at
E^{*}
is given by

J:=J(E^{*})=\begin{pmatrix}1-a\Delta t&0&0\\
a\Delta t&1-b\Delta t&b\Delta t\\
0&b\Delta t&1-b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t\end{pmatrix}.

(3.5)

Thus,
J
has three eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
), where
\lambda_{1}=1-a\Delta t
and
\lambda_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
are the eigenvalues of the submatrix

J_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1-b\Delta t&b\Delta t\\
b\Delta t&1-b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to see that
|\lambda_{1}|<1
. On the other hand, we have

\begin{split}Tr(J_{1})&=2-2b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t,\\
\det(J_{1})&=1-2b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t+bf^{\prime}(0)(\Delta t)^{2}.\end{split}

By using (
3.3
), we obtain

\begin{split}\det(J_{1})-1&=-b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t-b\Delta t(1-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t)<0\\
1-Tr(J_{1})+\det(J_{1})&=bf^{\prime}(0)(\Delta t)^{2}>0,\\
1+Tr(J_{1})+\det(J_{1})&=1-4b\Delta t-2f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t+bf^{\prime}(0)(\Delta t)^{2}\\
&>4\big(1-b\Delta t-f^{\prime}(0)\Delta t\big)+bf^{\prime}(0)(\Delta t)^{2}>0,\end{split}

which is equivalent to

|Tr(J_{1})|<1+\det(J_{1})<2.

By using
[
2
, Theorem 2.10]
, we conclude that
|\lambda_{2}|<1
and
|\lambda_{3}|<1
. Then, the local asymptotic stability of
E^{*}
is derived from the linearalized method
[
25
, Theorem 1.3.7]
. The proof is complete.
∎

In order to analyze the GAS of (
1.5
), we need the following assumption: The function
g
defined in (
3.1
) attains a positive minimum value on
[0,\,\,\,S_{0}]
, that is

\ \min_{0\leq C\leq S_{0}}g(C)=m>0,

(3.6)

for some positive real number
m
.

Theorem 3.3

(Global asymptotic stability analysis)
.

The trivial equilibrium point of (
1.5
) is not only locally asymptotically stable but also globally asymptotically stable for all parameter values if (
3.3
) and (
3.6
) hold.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.2
, it is sufficient to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)=(0,\,0,\,0).

It follows from the last equation of (
3.4
) that

C_{n+1}=(1-b\Delta t-g(C_{n})\Delta t)C_{n}+b\Delta tB_{n}\leq(1-b\Delta t-m\Delta t)C_{n}+b\Delta tB_{n}.

Thus, (
3.4
) implies that

\begin{pmatrix}A_{n+1}\\
B_{n+1}\\
C_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}\leq P\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}\\
B_{n}\\
C_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where
P
is defined by

P:=\begin{pmatrix}1-a\Delta t&0&0\\
a\Delta t&1-b\Delta t&b\Delta t\\
0&b\Delta t&1-b\Delta t-m\Delta t.\end{pmatrix}.

From (
3.3
), all the entries of
P
are positive. Thus, for
n\geq 1

\begin{pmatrix}A_{n}\\
B_{n}\\
C_{n}\end{pmatrix}\leq P^{n}\begin{pmatrix}A_{0}\\
B_{0}\\
C_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.7)

The matrix
P
always has an eigenvalue given by
\mu_{1}=1-a\Delta t
, which satisfies
|\mu_{1}|<1
. Meanwhile, the two remaining eigenvalues coincide with those of the submatrix

P^{*}:=\begin{pmatrix}1-b\Delta t&b\Delta t\\
b\Delta t&1-b\Delta t-m\Delta t.\end{pmatrix}.

Under the condition (
3.3
), we have the estimates

\begin{split}\det(P^{*})-1&=-b\Delta t-m\Delta t-b\Delta t(1-m\Delta t)<0\\
1-Tr(P^{*})+\det(P^{*})&=bm(\Delta t)^{2}>0,\\
1+Tr(P^{*})+\det(P^{*})&=1-4b\Delta t-2m\Delta t+bm(\Delta t)^{2}\\
&>4\big(1-b\Delta t-m\Delta t\big)+bm(\Delta t)^{2}>0.\end{split}

Consequently, we conclude that all the eigenvalues
\mu_{i}
(
i=1,2,3
) of
P
satisfy
|\mu_{i}|<1
, which implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}P^{n}=0
. Then, it follows from (
3.7
) that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)=(0,\,0,\,0)
. This is the desired conclusion. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.6
) imply that

mC\leq f(C)\leq MC,\quad C\geq 0.

This property is commonly observed in many biological, ecological, and epidemiological models
[
2
,
24
]
. The saturating function used in (
1.1
) is a typical example.

In the following theorem, we provide another proof of the GAS of the trivial equilibrium of (
1.5
), in which the condition (
3.6
) is no longer required.

Theorem 3.5

(Global asymptotic stability analysis without condition (
3.6
))
.

The trivial equilibrium point of (
1.5
) is not only locally asymptotically stable but also globally asymptotically stable for all parameter values if (
3.3
) holds.

Proof.

Consider a Lyapunov function candidate defined by

V\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)=\big(A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\big)^{2}.

Then, the variation of
V
relative to (
1.5
) is given by

\begin{split}&\Delta V\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)=V\big(A_{n+1},\,B_{n+1},\,C_{n+1}\big)-V\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)\\
&=\big(A_{n+1}+B_{n+1}+C_{n+1}\big)^{2}-\big(A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\big)^{2}\\
&=\Big[\big(A_{n+1}+B_{n+1}+C_{n+1}\big)-\big(A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\big)\Big]\times\Big[\big(A_{n+1}+B_{n+1}+C_{n+1}\big)+\big(A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\big)\Big]\\
&=-\Delta tf(C_{n})\Big[\big(A_{n+1}+B_{n+1}+C_{n+1}\big)+\big(A_{n}+B_{n}+C_{n}\big)\Big],\end{split}

(3.8)

which implies that
\Delta V\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)\leq 0
for all
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}\geq 0
. From Lyapunov stability theory
[
8
,
12
]
, we conclude that the origin is stable.

On the other hand, (
3.8
) implies that
\Delta V=0
if and only if
C_{n}=0
. Thus, we deduce from LaSalle’s invariance principle
[
8
,
12
]
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\big(A_{n},\,B_{n},\,C_{n}\big)=(0,\,0,\,0).

Consequently, the trivial equilibrium point is globally asymptotically stable. This is the desired conclusion. The proof is completed.
∎

5
Concluding Remarks and Discussions

As the main conclusion of this work, we have introduced a generalized three-compartment system modeling ethanol metabolism in the human body, which extends a model recently constructed in
[
26
]
. This model replaced the Michaelis-Menten mechanism of the liver’s ethanol metabolism rate by a general class of nonlinear rate functions. This provides greater modeling flexibility and allows the model to capture a wider variety of hepatic ethanol metabolism dynamics. The qualitative dynamics of the proposed ethanol metabolism model has been analyzed rigorously, including the positivity and boundedness of the solutions and the global asymptotic stability of the unique equilibrium point by using a quadratic Lyapunov function.

Second, we have formulated a discrete-time counterpart of the proposed continuous-time model and investigated its dynamical properties. We have shown that, under an appropriate condition on the time step size, the discrete-time model faithfully reproduces the qualitative dynamical behavior of the corresponding continuous-time system.

Furthermore, a series of numerical experiments employing several ethanol metabolism rate functions has been conducted to support the theoretical findings.

In the near future, we plan to apply the proposed model to real-world situations. In particular, parameter estimation and identification problems will be of interest.
