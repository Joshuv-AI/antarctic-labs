Bulk magnetic properties of distorted square lattice compounds M ′ M^{\prime} - L ​ n ​ TaO 4 Ln\mathrm{TaO}_{4} ( L ​ n = Ln= Tb, Dy, Ho, Er)
Nicola D. Kelly, Ivan da Silva, Siân E. Dutton

Abstract
We report bulk magnetic properties of the monoclinic lanthanide tantalates, M ′ M^{\prime} - Ln TaO 4 \textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} ( L ​ n Ln = Tb, Dy, Ho, Er), where the magnetic L ​ n 3 + Ln^{3+} ions are arranged on a distorted 2D square lattice. The heavier analogue M ′ M^{\prime} - YbTaO 4 \text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} has been investigated as a spin-orbit-coupled, quasi-two-dimensional frustrated magnet, and the properties of the other M ′ M^{\prime} - Ln TaO 4 \textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} are expected to vary depending on the electronic configuration of the L ​ n Ln ion, namely Kramers vs non-Kramers behaviour and different crystal electric field parameters. In this work, powder neutron diffraction is used to confirm the crystal structure for L ​ n = Ln= Tb, Ho, Er, and to determine the magnetic structure of M ′ M^{\prime} - TbTaO 4 \text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} , which displays long-range antiferromagnetic (AFM) order below T N = 2.1 T_{\mathrm{N}}=2.1 K. The Tb 3+ moments are aligned primarily along the c c -axis with AFM nearest-neighbour interactions. Susceptibility data suggest that M ′ M^{\prime} - DyTaO 4 \text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} may display short-range ordering around 2.7 K, while M ′ M^{\prime} - HoTaO 4 \text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} and M ′ M^{\prime} - ErTaO 4 \text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} show AFM correlations but do not order above 1.8 K. Measurements of the magnetic specific heat provide evidence for a Kramers doublet ground states in M ′ M^{\prime} - ErTaO 4 \text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} , similar to its heavier analogue M ′ M^{\prime} - YbTaO 4 \text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}} .



I
Introduction

The spin-
\frac{1}{2}
Heisenberg square lattice is an important model for frustrated magnetism studies. The two relevant interactions are the nearest-neighbour coupling
J_{1}
, along the sides of the squares, and next-nearest-neighbour
J_{2}
, along the diagonals of the squares. Depending on the relative signs and magnitudes of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, three types of classical long-range order (LRO) can be observed (ferromagnetic (FM), columnar antiferromagnetic (AFM), and Néel AFM), and there are also two boundary regions where spin-liquid behaviour is predicted

4
;
14
;
10
. These arise from the competition between
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
when
J_{2}/J_{1}
is close to
\pm 0.5
. Several experimental realisations of the Heisenberg square lattice have been studied recently, including
\text{YbBi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{YbBi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{ClO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

30
,
\text{SrLaCuSbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
and
\text{SrLaCuNbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

42
, and
\alpha
-
\text{KTi}\text{(}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\,{\cdot}\,\textit{x}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}

3
, which all order antiferromagnetically at low temperatures. However, a small number of materials have been found to resist order and are therefore considered candidates for the spin-liquid behaviour predicted at
J_{2}/J_{1}\approx\pm\frac{1}{2}
, including transition-metal oxides
\text{Sr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{Te${}_{1-x}$W${}_{x}$}\text{)}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

28
;
29
and a rare-earth oxide
\text{NdKNaNbO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}

15
.

This work focuses on the lanthanide orthotantalates,
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, with the monoclinic
M^{\prime}
crystal structure. The tantalates crystallise in several different structures depending on the lanthanide ion, temperature and/or pressure. Compounds of the largest lanthanides, La–Pr, have the monoclinic space group
P2_{1}/c
, while compounds of the later (smaller) lanthanides can form the
M
(
I2/a
) or
M^{\prime}
(
P2/c
) monoclinic phases or the tetragonal
T
(
I4_{1}/a
) phase (which is only observed
in situ
)
7
. The magnetic
Ln^{3+}
ions in
M
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
occupy a three-dimensional (3D) distorted diamond lattice, which leads to geometric magnetic frustration from the competing nearest-neighbour interactions
20
;
43
. In contrast, the layered
M^{\prime}
phase contains magnetic
Ln^{3+}
ions on a two-dimensional (2D) distorted square lattice, which also displays frustration.

The crystal structure of
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is shown in Fig.
1
. It is built from alternating layers of edge-sharing
\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
octahedra and
\textit{Ln}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
square antiprisms, Fig.
1
(a). Within each layer of lanthanide-oxygen polyhedra, the
Ln^{3+}
ions form a distorted square network, Fig.
1
(b), with two distinct nearest-neighbour distances, denoted
J_{1a}
and
J_{1b}
. The interlayer
Ln
–
Ln
separation (e.g. 5.15 Å for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
at room temperature) is more than 30 % longer than either of the intralayer
Ln
–
Ln
distances (3.71 and 3.96 Å for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
). Therefore, we can consider this a quasi-2D system. The combination of highly localised
4f
orbitals, a quasi-2D magnetic network, and geometric frustration makes it likely that long-range order might be partially or wholly suppressed in these materials.

Recently, two independent studies investigated frustrated magnetism in the isostructural spin-
\frac{1}{2}
system,
M^{\prime}
-
\text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
. They both determined that
\text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
has a spin-orbit-driven
J_{eff}=\frac{1}{2}
Kramers doublet ground state with a large gap to the first excited state. The Curie-Weiss temperature
\theta_{CW}
was negative, indicating weak antiferromagnetic correlations between
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
spins. Ramanathan
et al
. found no magnetic ordering down to 100 mK in specific heat measurements
31
. This means that the frustration index
f=|\theta_{CW}/T_{\mathrm{N}}|\approx 10
, indicating strong frustration
32
. Kumar
et al
. similarly found a lack of magnetic order at
T\geq 1.8
K; they also confirmed the presence of fluctuating
\text{Yb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
spins using muon-spin relaxation (
\mu
SR) spectroscopy
21
. The combined experimental evidence supports a quantum disordered ground state in
M^{\prime}
-
\text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
.

In this study, we measure the bulk magnetic properties of the analogous lanthanide tantalate compounds with
Ln=
Tb, Dy, Ho and Er, which each have unique electronic configurations and different quantum numbers
L
,
S
and
J
. We find that
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
orders antiferromagnetically at 2.1 K, while the other three compounds have AFM correlations (Curie-Weiss constants ranging from
-3
to
-7.5
K) but do not display long-range order above 1.8 K. Neutron diffraction was used to confirm the nuclear crystal structures at room temperature for
Ln=
Ho and Er. Neutron diffraction also allowed us to track the nuclear structure of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
as a function of temperature on cooling to 1.5 K, and to determine its magnetic structure below
T_{\mathrm{N}}
. We find that
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
displays long-range AFM order with an ordering vector of
\vec{k}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},0)
and the spins directed mainly along the
c
-direction.

II
Experimental Methods

II.1
Synthesis

Polycrystalline samples of
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
in the monoclinic
M^{\prime}
phase were synthesised using ceramic methods on a 0.5–4.0 g scale. The lanthanide oxides,
\text{Tb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
(99.9 %, Alfa Aesar),
\text{Dy}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(99.99 %, Alfa Aesar),
\text{Ho}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(99.99 %, Alfa Aesar) or
\text{Er}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
(99.9 %, Alfa Aesar) respectively, were ground in stoichiometric ratio with
\text{Ta}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}
(99.993 %, Thermo Scientific) using a pestle and mortar. The powders were placed in alumina crucibles and heated in air at 3 °C min
-1
to 1200–1450 °C for 24 h (a more detailed discussion of the optimum temperature for each sample can be found in Section
III.1
). The samples were cooled to room temperature at the natural rate of the furnace, ground and reheated with the same heating profile until the weight fractions of impurity phases no longer decreased.

II.2
Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD)

Phase purity was checked using powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) on a Bruker D8 diffractometer with Cu K
\alpha
radiation,
\lambda=1.541
Å. PXRD patterns for structural refinement were collected at the I11 beamline, Diamond Light Source
39
, with wavelength (refined from a silicon standard)
\lambda=0.82869
Å. Samples were mixed 1:1 by volume with ground glass to reduce absorption and packed into
\phi=0.5
mm borosilicate capillaries. Rietveld refinement
33
was carried out using the program TOPAS v5
11
. The background was fitted with a Chebyshev polynomial with 12 coefficients and the peakshape was a Voigt function with axial divergence asymmetry. During refinement, the
x
and
z
coordinates of the
Ln
and Ta atoms were fixed at these values representing special positions in the cell:
x_{Ln}=0
,
z_{Ln}=0.25
;
x_{\mathrm{Ta}}=0.5
,
z_{\mathrm{Ta}}=0.75
. The atomic coordinates of the oxygen atoms were also fixed at values taken from the literature
17
. The refined parameters included the background coefficients, zero offset, peak shape parameters,
y
coordinates of the
Ln
and Ta atoms, and isotropic thermal parameters
B_{\mathrm{iso}}
for each element.

II.3
Powder neutron diffraction (PND)

Large samples (approximately 4 g each) of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
,
M^{\prime}
-
\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
M^{\prime}
-
\text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
were investigated using time-of-flight (TOF) powder neutron diffraction (PND) at the GEM beamline
16
, ISIS Neutron and Muon Source
18
. Note that
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
was excluded from the neutron diffraction study because of the very strong neutron absorption of some Dy isotopes
35
which would require isotopically enriched samples for PND experiments. The samples were packed into
\phi=6
mm vanadium cans and measured at 300 K (all samples) and at 100, 30 and 1.5 K (Tb only) using an ILL Orange cryostat
8
. Rietveld refinement
33
was carried out using the program TOPAS v5
11
against banks 3–6 simultaneously, each with equal weighting.

II.4
Magnetometry

Magnetic measurements were made using a Quantum Design Materials Properties Measurement System (MPMS-3). Approximately 10 mg of each sample (weighed accurately in each case) was contained in clingfilm and the standard plastic sample holders then inserted into a brass sample holder. The DC magnetic moment was measured as a function of temperature upon warming in the range 1.8–300 K at a field of 500 Oe, after cooling in zero field (ZFC) or in the applied field (FC). The DC moment was also measured as a function of magnetic field in the range 0–7 T at temperatures of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 100 K.

II.5
Specific heat

Approximately 40 mg of each sample (weighed accurately in each case) was ground with an equal mass of Ag powder (Alfa Aesar, 99.99%, –635 mesh) using a pestle and mortar, then pressed into a
\phi=5
mm pellet. Portions of this pellet weighing 5–20 mg were attached to the sample holder using Apiezon N grease. The heat capacity was measured at
T=
1.8–30 K using a Quantum Design Physical Properties Measurement System (PPMS); an initial addenda (background) measurement was made before adding the pellet, in order to subtract the heat capacity contribution arising from the grease. The Ag lattice contribution to the total heat capacity was then subtracted using values from the literature
37
. Furthermore, for each compound
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, the lattice contribution
C_{\mathrm{latt}}
was modelled using the Debye law:

C_{\mathrm{latt}}=\frac{9nRT^{3}}{{\theta_{\mathrm{D}}^{3}}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\theta_{\mathrm{D}}}{T}}\frac{x^{4}e^{x}}{(e^{x}-1)^{2}}dx

(1)

where
n
is the number of atoms per formula unit,
R
is the molar gas constant,
T
the temperature in K, and
\theta_{\mathrm{D}}
the Debye temperature in K
13
. A scalar parameter
p
was substituted for
9nR
in the fitting equation and refined to account for the sample mass error and imperfect coupling to the sample stage. The lattice contribution was then subtracted from all datasets to leave the magnetic heat capacity,
C_{\mathrm{mag}}(T)
, for each compound. The obtained Debye temperatures were 256(2), 197(2), 127(3), and 148(2) K for
Ln=
Tb, Dy, Ho, and Er respectively.

III
Results and Discussion

III.1
Structural characterisation

The phase space of the lanthanide tantalates
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
contains several distinct polymorphs. For
Ln=
Tb–Er, the equilibrium phase at room temperature is
M^{\prime}
, which on heating to around 1400 °C transforms into the tetragonal
T
(scheelite) phase. However, the
T
phase cannot be isolated at room temperature and upon cooling transforms into the
M
(fergusonite) phase
7
;
23
. Therefore, targeted synthesis of the
M^{\prime}
phase requires the temperature to be maintained as high as possible, to facilitate atomic diffusion, whilst not exceeding the
M^{\prime}-T
transition temperature
27
;
26
. The optimum furnace temperature was found to be 1350 °C for
Ln=
Tb, Dy and Ho, and 1450 °C for
Ln=
Er. It proved impossible to stabilise phase-pure
M^{\prime}
-
\text{GdTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
(or any pure
M^{\prime}
samples with larger lanthanide ions) because when the synthesis temperature was kept low enough to avoid forming the
M
phase, there was always competition from
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}

40
.

The crystal structures of
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
were refined against high-resolution synchrotron powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) data using the Rietveld method. The refined parameters for all four compounds are given in Table
1
and the refinement plots are in the Supplemental Material, Figures S1–S4
2
. The PXRD data showed no presence of the competing
M
-phase in these samples and the refined structural parameters of the four samples agree well with reports from the literature
7
;
17
. Note: with respect to those literature reports, the origin has been shifted by half a unit cell in the
b
direction in order to achieve consistency between the nuclear and magnetic unit cell models (see later Section
III.4
and Table S2 in Supplemental Material
2
).

Magnetic measurements

Peaks in the magnetic susceptibility were visible for

-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

M^{\prime}
-

at approximately 2.7 K for both samples (Fig.

The magnetic susceptibility
\chi=M/H
was fitted to the modified Curie-Weiss law:

\chi=\chi_{0}+\frac{C}{T-\theta_{CW}}

(2)

where
\chi_{0}
is a small temperature-independent susceptibility,
C
is the Curie constant (
C=\mu_{eff}^{2}/8
), and
\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}
is the Curie-Weiss temperature. All chemical elements and compounds have a negative, temperature-independent diamagnetic contribution to the susceptibility,
\chi_{\mathrm{Di}}

5
as
-8\times 10^{-5}
emu mol
-1
for these four
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
compounds. However, diamagnetism is a weak effect and is typically overridden by stronger magnetic effects such as paramagnetism
24
, so lanthanide-containing solids are expected to have positive values of
\chi_{0}
. We used a Nelder-Mead optimisation method (Supplemental Material, Fig. S13
2
) to find the best linear fit to the inverse susceptibility as a function of temperature. This approach minimises the scaled least-squares difference between
(T-\theta)/C
and
(\chi-\chi_{0})^{-1}
, which is more effective than regular Curie-Weiss fitting with free parameters on both sides of the equation (eqn
2
). This yielded small positive values of
\chi_{0}
which are given in Table
3
along with the fitted values of
\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}
. Fig.
4
shows the Curie-Weiss fits as black solid lines with extrapolation below 50 K as dashed lines. The negative Curie-Weiss temperatures indicate antiferromagnetic interactions and the effective magnetic moments

agree quite well with the expected moments for free ions,
\mu=g_{J}\sqrt{J(J+1)}
.

III.3
Specific heat

The zero-field magnetic specific heat for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is given in Fig.
6
. A sharp
\lambda
-type transition is observed at
T=2.1
K, indicating long-range AFM ordering. The temperature of the peak in heat capacity is lower than that observed in the magnetic susceptibility (2.7 K), which is similar behaviour to the other polymorph of this compound,
M
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

20
. In addition, a peak in
d(\chi T)/dT
occurs at the same temperature as the peak in the specific heat (Supplemental Material, Fig. S12
2
), which is typical for antiferromagnets
12
. Integration of
C_{\mathrm{mag}}/T
yields the magnetic entropy,
\Delta S_{\mathrm{mag}}
, which approaches 4 J K
-1

{\mathrm{mol}_{\mathrm{Tb}}}^{-1}
at 30 K. This value is less than 20 % of the expected
\Delta S_{\mathrm{mag}}=R\ln{(2J+1)}=21.32
J K
-1
mol
-1
(
J=6
); clearly, since the lower-temperature side of the
\lambda
-like peak is incomplete, there is still significant further entropy to be lost on cooling the material below 1.8 K. The specific heat in zero field and applied fields (1, 2, 4 and 9 T) is also shown in Fig.
7
, top right. Upon application of a magnetic field, the sharp peak is suppressed and becomes a broad feature which moves to higher temperatures with increasing field.

M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

In contrast, for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
no such sharp peak is observed (Fig.
7
, top right), which agrees with the absence of a peak in
d(\chi T)/dT

12
(Supplemental Material, Fig. S12
2
). There is no evidence for either long-range order or spin freezing above 1.8 K in either the zero-field or 1 T specific heat data. At 2 T we observe a broad peak, centred around 5 K. This peak then shifts to higher temperatures as the field increases, which is typical of a Schottky anomaly. Such behaviour arises from Zeeman splitting of a Kramers doublet state with
J_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{1}{2}
, as observed in the isostructural
M^{\prime}
-
\text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
at all fields
21
. However, the absence of a peak in the lower-field datasets suggests that the ground state at zero field is not a Kramers doublet, and that there is a field-induced transition between 1 and 2 T, perhaps reflecting suppression of the short-range order suggested by the magnetic susceptibility. In Fig.
5
the magnetisation curves for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
at 2 K and 4 K cross over twice at low fields, which also hints at magnetic ordering. There are also two small anomalies in the
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
data around
T=20
and 25 K whose origin is unknown. They may be an artefact of measurements or related to the sample environment, because less pronounced features appear at the same temperatures in the
\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and
\text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
data and they are independent of the applied field.

Like Dy
3+
ions, Er
3+
ions have an odd number of electrons and are therefore also Kramers ions.
M^{\prime}
-
\text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
displays a Schottky-like peak similar to that of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
(Fig.
7
, bottom right) but, unlike
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, this behaviour appears to extend to the lowest fields. There is a second broad feature between around 7 and 22 K in the zero-field data, which seems to be suppressed in applied magnetic fields and may represent short-range magnetic correlations; the feature might be investigated in the future using neutron diffraction and/or inelastic scattering in this temperature range.

Finally, the specific heat data for

\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

3+
(a non-Kramers ion), especially when the Ho
3+
ions are in low-symmetry crystallographic environments; in
\pi
-
\text{HoBO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
the behaviour was ascribed to a non-magnetic singlet ground state, with the broad feature arising from van Vleck paramagnetism
25
. In
M^{\prime}
-
\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, the Ho
3+
ion is on the
2e
Wyckoff position of space group 13 (
P2/c
) with site symmetry
C_{2}
. The low site symmetry and non-Kramers nature of Ho
3+
mean that its
2J+1=15
-fold degeneracy may be lifted by interaction with the surrounding CEF, i.e. there is no symmetry-enforced ground state doublet, unlike in many Er
3+
or Yb
3+
compounds. Hence, we suggest that the specific heat data on
M^{\prime}
-
\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
represent a singlet ground state. The higher-temperature peak in the 9 T dataset is probably an anomaly, related to some slippage of the sample platform or thermocouples between the 4 T and 9 T measurements, since there is no evidence in the
M(H)
data for a field-induced transition in this range. The inset to Fig.
7
shows how the sample coupling for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{HoTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is different for the 9 T data at low temperatures, supporting this hypothesis; for the other three compounds, the sample coupling had a slight
T
-dependence but was independent of applied field.

III.4
Magnetic structure of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

The magnetic structure of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
was investigated using PND
18
below the ordering temperature of
T_{\mathrm{N}}=2.1
K. Additional peaks appeared between the 30 K and 1.5 K datasets in positions which indicated commensurate magnetic ordering. Using the FullProf Suite
34
, a suitable magnetic ordering vector was found to be
\vec{k}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},0)
. The magnetic peaks were then modelled in TOPAS v5
11
in magnetic space group 14.80 (
P_{a}2_{1}/c
). Each Tb
3+
ion carries a magnetic moment with refined components
-0.702(8)
, 0 and
8.053(6)

\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
along the
a
,
b
and
c
axes respectively. This corresponds to
-1.652(12)
, 0 and
7.996(6)

\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
in Cartesian axes and an ordered moment magnitude of
8.17(9)

\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
. This is consistent with the expectation of
g_{J}J=9

\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
for Tb
3+
ions. Note that this magnetic space group does permit a non-zero
y
-component to the magnetic moment. However, its value always refined to zero (from different trial starting points) with a large uncertainty reported by the TOPAS software, so it was subsequently fixed at zero. Thus, in this model, the magnetic moments are primarily directed along the
c
-direction and are fractionally canted towards
a
. The nearest-neighbour magnetic moments are coupled antiferromagnetically within the
bc
plane, and the next-nearest-neighbour interactions are ferromagnetic, creating a zigzag-like ordering pattern. Adjacent layers along the
a
axis are coupled antiferromagnetically.

The magnetic structure model for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
is shown in Fig.
8
(a) and (b), and the Rietveld refinement at 1.5 K (bank 3) in Fig.
8
(c). Rietveld refinements for all banks are available in the Supplemental Material, Figure S11
2
, along with a table giving the structural parameters for a one-phase combined nuclear and magnetic structure, Table S2. In addition to the
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, two other minor phases contribute intensity to the 1.5 K neutron diffraction pattern: 1.06 wt %
M
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
(both nuclear and magnetic reflections
20
), and 0.47 wt %
\text{Tb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
.
\text{Tb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
is known to undergo two magnetic ordering transitions at 2.9 K and 3.6 K
40
, which may be the origin of the very small regions of unindexed intensity in the 1.5 K data. However, the magnetic structure of
\text{Tb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}
has not been reported and could not be deduced from our data because of the very low weight fraction of

In comparison to the
M^{\prime}
phase described above, the
M
polymorph of
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, which has a three-dimensional “stretched diamond” network of Tb
3+
ions, displays AFM order below
T_{\mathrm{N}}=2.25
K with the moments directed along the
a
-axis
20
. Both phases of
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
display almost easy-axis anisotropy and antiferromagnetic coupling between nearest-neighbour Tb ions. However, differences in their crystal structures lead to important differences in the magnetic structures. In the
M
phase, there are four near-neighbour AFM interactions at similar distances (
2\times J_{1a}
at 3.75 Å,
2\times J_{1b}
at 3.84 Å) and the closest ferromagnetically coupled pair of spins are much further apart at
\sim 5.05
Å. In contrast, the
M^{\prime}
phase has AFM
2\times J_{1a}
at 3.71 Å and FM
2\times J_{1b}
at 3.95 Å, plus AFM inter-layer coupling
J_{2}
(5.13 Å). The tilting of the magnetic moments out of the plane in the
M^{\prime}
phase might be a way to partially relieve the frustration that arises from these competing AFM and FM near-neighbour interactions. The experimental
T_{\mathrm{N}}
is slightly lower for the
M^{\prime}
phase than the
M
phase (2.1 vs 2.25 K). However, the Curie-Weiss temperature is smaller for
M^{\prime}
than
M
, and the estimated frustration parameter
f=|\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}/T_{\mathrm{N}}|
is therefore also smaller, although both values are modest (
M
:
f\approx 4.2
,
M^{\prime}
:
f\approx 1.7
). We can use mean-field estimates of the nearest-neighbour exchange (
J_{\mathrm{nn}}
) and dipolar (
D_{\mathrm{nn}}
) interactions, equations
3
and
4
, to rationalise the difference in magnetic ordering temperatures:

J_{\mathrm{nn}}=\frac{3k_{\mathrm{B}}|\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}|}{2nJ(J+1)}

(3)

D_{\mathrm{nn}}=\frac{\mu_{0}\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}{4\pi R^{3}J(J+1)}

(4)

where
n
is the number of “nearest neighbours” and
J
is the total angular momentum quantum number,
J=6
for Tb
3+
. We took
n=4
for both structures; for
R_{\mathrm{nn}}
we used an average of the four closest Tb–Tb distances, which are within 7 % of each other (compared with the next closest ion, which is at a distance more than 30 % larger than
R_{\mathrm{nn}}
in both phases). In
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, its smaller Curie-Weiss temperature and greater average Tb–Tb separation lead to smaller values for both the superexchange and dipolar interactions. These weaker interactions are reflected in the slightly lower magnetic ordering temperature. Table
4
gives the relevant parameters and estimated interaction strengths for all four samples of
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
with comparisons to the
M
polymorphs
20
. For each pair of compounds with the same

and
D_{\mathrm{nn}}
are smaller for the
M^{\prime}
phase than for the
M
phase. We note that the difference in

for
M^{\prime}
versus
M

Ln

distance,

is the same for
M
- and
M^{\prime}
-
\text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

Ln
3+
radius, below which the

7
;
19

38

\text{BaIrO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

IV
Conclusions

We have investigated the crystal structure and bulk magnetic properties of the lanthanide tantalum oxides,
M^{\prime}
-
\textit{Ln}\text{TaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
, where
Ln=
Tb, Dy, Ho and Er. Powder X-ray and neutron diffraction were used to study the crystal structure. Using magnetometry and heat capacity measurements, we observe LRO at
T_{\mathrm{N}}=2.1
K for
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
and no LRO above 1.8 K for the compounds where
Ln=
Dy, Ho or Er, although the susceptibility of
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
suggests possible short-range magnetic ordering below
T\approx 2.7
K.
M^{\prime}
-
\text{ErTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
has a Kramers doublet ground state as evidenced by the Schottky-type anomaly in field-dependent specific heat data, similar to the previously reported
M^{\prime}
-
\text{YbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
. A similar feature was seen for
M^{\prime}
-
\text{DyTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
above 2 T, but there was no peak in the heat capacity at lower fields. This behaviour could be related to short-range order and/or CEF effects.

Powder neutron diffraction indicates that the magnetic moments in
M^{\prime}
-
\text{TbTaO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
below
T_{\mathrm{N}}

, with an ordered magnitude of 8.17(9)

This work provides insight into how the magnetic anisotropy and ordering behaviour of rare-earth oxides may be significantly affected, either by substitution of different magnetic ions into a given crystal structure, or by differences in the crystal structure for a given magnetic ion (specifically Tb
