Edge AI enabled MIMO MC-CDMA for 6G optimizing spectrum and energy efficiency with SIC and deep reinforcement learning
Authors: A. Vijay
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid expansion of 6G wireless networks requires high SE techniques to improve spectral efficiency (SE), energy efficiency (EE), and interference. In this paper, an Edge AI MIMO MC-CDMA system with SIC and DRL is proposed for spectrum and energy efficiency. As for the difference from the traditional MIMO-OFDM and hybrid precoding, this paper is based on DRL for adaptive learning, which will show better performance for dense network. The proposed system is implemented using MATLAB to allow for real- time decision- making pertaining the streams’ networking parameters at the network edge. DRL makes it possible to ensure interference cancellation, while Edge AI avoids increased computational load and guarantees fast responses. Simulation outcome shows that the proposed model has a spectral efficiency of about 32.7 bits/s/Hz and energy efficiency of 14.8 bits/Joule and thereby surpass than MC-CDMA, MIMO-OFDM and the hybrid precoding-based MIMO systems. Furthermore, the SINR is enhanced to be 34 dB while BER is decreased to 10−5 to guarantee high transmission reliability. The deep learning-based mechanism effectively interferes the power allocation and enhances the stability of networking. The results fully support significantly optimizes power allocation, improving overall network stability and efficiency for the future 6G wireless network is highly scalable, capable of operating with minimum interference issues and very much energy efficient. Due to the fact that the proposed model can dynamically adjust to the conditions in the network, it conceptualises one approach to a smart future of wireless communication in the next generation applied to ultra-high densities of networks and users.

Introduction

The rapid evolution of wireless communication has resulted in next-generation networks with 6G following 5G1. To address increasing requirements for high data rates, ultra-low latency, and energy efficiency in communications, researchers are working on frontier technologies to enhance spectrum efficiency and power consumption2. Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) with Multi-Carrier Code Division Multiple Access (MC-CDMA) is one of the most important enablers of 6G, as it enhances spectral efficiency by enabling several users to communicate in parallel on various frequency subcarriers3. But with increasing network density and complexity, interference, wastage of energy, and dynamic spectrum allocation are important issues. Integration of Edge AI—facilitating smart, real-time decision-making at the network edge can potentially revolutionize wireless communication by reducing latency and making optimal use of network resources4. To unlock the full potential of these innovations, new approaches need to be formulated in order to overcome interference, promote energy efficiency, and dynamically allocate the spectrum based on network conditions.

In previous studies, the technology used in the 6G wireless communication mainly focused on MIMO and MC-CDMA as the means to improve capacity and reliability. Thus, interference mitigation techniques as Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) have been proposed to enhance a signal detection and to decrease the bit error rates (BER)5. Both machine learning and optimization have been used in the improvement of the efficiency of spectrum and power6. However, such methods are based on heuristics and fail to address various and dynamically changing network systems. Furthermore, deep learning solutions are need for resource optimization; however, they are centralized causing high latency and high-power consumption7. One of the major challenging factors is the absence of effective deep reinforcement learning (DRL) frameworks that can learn resource management strategies for 6G networks in real-time8. Moreover, the employed SIC techniques although have reasonable performance have reliability problems in terms of computation complexity and convergence in large scale MIMO MC-CDMA systems and useful in ultra-dense networks.

In order to deal with these challenges, this study suggested to employ an Edge AI for 6G MIMO MC-CDMA system along with Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize spectrum and energy efficiency. Incorporating Edge AI will allow the system to make decisions without requiring the use of the cloud and increase the pace of decision-making by reducing the latency time9. The SIC algorithm is the incorporation of artificial intelligence interference cancellation which will augment the ability to detect other user’s signal and rule out interference from the same. Furthermore, DRL will be applied for dynamic spectrum and power control, since the network has an interaction with the environment in order to learn the best policy on how to use the spectrum and power efficiently. It is believed that the proposed method shall provide better spectral efficiency, consume lesser power and have better robust system than the existing techniques. Unlike the previous literature that used DRA with either MIMO or SIC alone, the presented scheme uses a combination of DRA, Edge AI, MIMO MC-CDMA, and SIC to optimize the system in real-time. It is the only mechanism that can do decentralized decision-making at the network edge and also enhance interference management, distribution of power, spectral efficiency, as well as energy efficiency in dense 6G wireless settings.

Research motivation

The rapid evolution of wireless communications to 6G networks demands greater spectrum efficiency, lower latency, and improved energy efficiency. Traditional MIMO and MC-CDMA systems support strong multi-user communication but suffer from interference, resource allocation, and scalability. To counter such vulnerabilities, integrating Edge AI with Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) can optimize power allocation, interference cancellation, and adaptive beamforming. This study will design an intelligent, adaptive MIMO MC-CDMA system with AI-based optimization for better spectrum utilization and energy efficiency in next-generation 6G networks.

Problem statement

The increased demand for high-speed, low-latency, and energy-efficient wireless communication has resulted in extensive research on advanced multiple access and signal processing techniques for next generation networks. All of these proposed computer-aided optimization techniques-from deep learning-based channel coding10, hybrid cognitive radio networks11, AI-powered resource allocation12 still have high computational overhead and scalability constraints. Resource allocation schemes for MIMO systems based on intelligent surface technologies13 and interference-aware massive access schemes are good cases that enhance spectral efficiency, but they need further fine-tuning for dynamic scenarios. For this purpose, the combination of Edge AI and MIMO-MC-CDMA exploiting deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and SIC could serve as a panacea for optimizing spectrum and energy efficiency while ensuring lower computational complexity, thus being absolutely a candidate for 6G networks14.

Recent innovation and its limits

Advances in 6G wireless networks have recently suggested the use of Massive MIMO, MC-CDMA, and AI-based resource allocation for enhancing communication efficiency. Techniques like Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for power control, Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) for interference cancellation, and Edge AI for real-time optimization have shown promising results. However, such advancements are hindered by limitations like intensive computational complexity, scalability issues in dense networks, and dynamic real-time traffic adjustments. Additionally, existing models usually do not have effective energy management policies, leading to increased power consumption. To overcome such limitations, the current work focuses on optimization, with the promise of intelligent, adaptive resource allocation for enhancing spectrum efficiency, energy efficiency, and reliability in 6G networks.

Significance of the study

This study is significant as it envisions an intelligent, AI-powered method to achieve maximum spectrum and energy efficiency for 6G wireless systems. By applying Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) with Massive MIMO and MC-CDMA, the envisaged system achieves real-time resource allocation, interference reduction, and power management efficiently. This enhances the network performance, reliability, and scalability, making it an ideal enabler for future autonomous wireless communication systems. The findings of this study will benefit network operators, industries, and researchers through an improved, adaptive, and sustainable approach for future 6G rollouts.

Literature survey

Siddiqui et al.14 have discussed on the problems of escalating mobile data traffic, density wireless devices and the transformation of industrial radio monitoring. The study had an ambitious proposal to incorporate mMIMO with HetNet and to utilise ML, FL, DL, and ANN in enhancing network performance. The findings pointed out that, with AI solutions, the problems related to mMIMO system implementation include computational complexities and latency. However, the work was not without its limitations wherein, the computational complexity of optimising mMIMO using AI was the most conspicuous hindrance to network adaptability. The paper seeks to act as a reference to the B5G/6G researchers and operators in the development of the wireless communication systems.

Almutairi and Krishna15 explored the drawbacks of using F-OFDM for the fifth-generation and beyond wireless communication technology. To this end, they introduced a new MCM approach named filtered orthogonal wavelet division multiplexing (F-OWDM) for substituting FT with Wavelet Transforms. Their research compared and simulated the F-OWDM with noisy and fading environment defined by additive white Gaussian noise and flat fading channels in aspects of PAPR and BER and different types of wavelets such as Haar wavelet, discrete Meyer wavelet, Bi-orthogonal, symlet and Daubechies wavelet. It has been established from the results that the complexity of F-OWDM is less than C-F-OFDM with low PAPR and BER. However, a major limitation is seen in the factor that practice,wavelet-based MCM is not easily possible because of its complexity.

Katwe et al.13 proposed a new resource allocation based on MU-MIMO downlink system with ARIS for the URLLC enabling RSMA. Because the formulation was non-convex in nature, it optimizes beamforming design using successive convex approximation and ARIS positioning using PSO. It was evident from the result that fast convergence and higher throughput rate was achieved compared to other multiple access techniques. Nevertheless, optimization framework still remains an issue for the application in real-time use cases in URLLC.

Chen et al.16 also studied the issue of coordinating AP and UE in cell-free mMIMO setting. To this end, the authors presented an Interference-aware massive access (IAMA) scheme, AP-UE association and pilot assignment parameters are optimized based on the large-scale interference. They came up with an interference-aware reward metric and designed two iterative algorithms to optimization of the association and the pilots. Additionally, higher signalling overhead of interference awareness for optimization may have negative effects on the network performance in large scale systems.

Boukenadil et al.10 pursued a channel coding technique based on deep learning framework for LDS-CDMA and NOMA with an intent to mitigate errors, minimize BER and decrease computational overhead. The research showed that deep learning could be effectively applied to necessary efficiency in wireless communication, as well as in the development of 6G and next, in terms of adaptability, data rate, and latency. However, one limitation is that training the model requires large amounts of training data which is highly dependent on network condition. Innovation could be done to assess the benefit of integrating with massive MIMO as well as NOMA for further prosecution in the efficiency of the system.

Jeon and Jin17 proposed random access systems that work with successive interference cancellation (SIC) for decoding multiple simultaneous transmissions by an access point. They used a Bayesian estimation technique to dynamically estimate the number of users currently active and optimize the transmission control accordingly. Using their approach, they achieved throughput up to 0.693 packets per slot which is equivalent to the theoretical limit proposed by Yu—Giannakis and maintained it at 85.7% even with severely restricted SIC capacity of just two. The most important drawback of such a system is that it relies on accurate estimation in real time, which could be definitely troubled by rapid changes that occur in the network conditions.

Ahmad et al.18 also viewed the prospects of 5G wired towards connectivity needs and the degree of imperfection introduced by the applications of Successive Interference Cancellation in power-domain Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access. They thus suggested pulse shaping based on wavelet transform for the purpose of reducing multiuser interference and derived expression of the Symbol Error Rate (SER) considering a three-user case. Following this, a numerical comparison against NOMA using Fourier transform showed that the wavelet-based modulation scheme performed better. However, the implementation in real-time systems is still stringent due to the increased signal processing burden. Further performance gain may vary with changing channel conditions, which will also require more optimization. Whatever the constraints, their proposal for calculation may enhance reliability in multiuser scenarios.

Kansal et al.19 consider hybrid massive multiple-input multiple-output (mMIMO) and orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) techniques to deliver multimedia in the future 5G and 6G mobile networks. The authors have modeled the fast Fourier transform (FFT), fractional Fourier transform (FRFT), and discrete wavelet transform (DWT) under Rayleigh fading and M-PSK modulation conditions. Among these, FrFT and DWT have been found to improve image quality over an FFT-based system. However, there are issues related to increased complexity of computation and hardware requirements implying that the study is not real-time latency constrained, which is typically undesirable in immediate-use multimedia applications. However, robustness and efficiency improvements have been achieved in wireless image communication even with these demerits.

Singhal and Patil11 introduced HCR-WSN, a Hybrid MIMO Cognitive Radio Wireless Sensor Network, which integrates underlay and interleave spectrum-sharing schemes to improve spectral efficiency. The system dynamically switches between Narrowband and MC-DS-CDMA while ensuring minimum interference to primary users (PU). In the absence of PU, secondary users (SU) optimize spectrum through interference alignment. Results increased throughput,however real-time transmission selection adds to system complexity. The impact of high mobility scenarios remains unexplored. The framework does provide excellent enhancement for spectral utilization and proves efficient in a dynamic wireless environment.

Vijay and Umadevi20 studied the synergy of MC-CDMA and massive MIMO toward spectral efficiency gains and overcoming multi-user co-channel interference (CCI) in 5G networks. Based on this approach, they devised an optimal spectrum sharing (OSS) protocol and optimal linear precoding (OLP) in conjunction with swarm algorithms for improved system capacity and power allocation. Their MATLAB-based simulation outperformed all conventional approaches, but algorithmic complexity and dynamic network conditions are pertinent considerations in practical implementations. This study demonstrates the promise of MC-CDMA and massive MIMO integration,however, these techniques still need to be optimized for real-world applications.

Venkateswarlu and Rao21 identified a mutation weight adaptive particle swarm optimization (MAPSO) approach to address problems on channel estimation of MIMO-OFDM. Their method consisted of four phases, including space-time block coding (STBC) and MC-CDMA for optimal channel evaluation. MAPSO tunes parameters dynamically and gives reduced bit error rates (BER) with improved performance of the whole system. However, the computational overhead will be an issue for real-time applications in this approach. No variations have been considered in the study with respect to different channel conditions or mobility scenarios. In spite of that limitations, their work outperformed the existing methods in terms of SER, BER, MSE, and NMSE.

Azimi et al.12 developed the energy-efficient approach called deep reinforcement learning-assisted resource allocation (EE-DRL-RA) for slicing into 5G networks. Deep learning (DL) was used for long-term decisions, while deep reinforcement learning (DRL) was used for instant resource allocations. It optimizes power-slot and radio resource distribution while guaranteeing that slice isolation is maintained. From simulations, it was seen that the algorithm outperformed the existing methods in speed of convergence, energy efficiency, and acceptance rate of users. Nevertheless, the concerns that remain are regarding scalability to ultra-dense networks, adaptability for dynamic traffic, and computational complexity. Recognition is given to the potential of EE-DRL-RA, but an inability to generalize its models and impose further constraints to actual-time deployment environments in practical 5G settings was reported. Current literature on 5G systems states that the energy efficiency ranges of 2–10 bits/Joule are generally considered, which is why smart optimization methods should be utilized to enhance the performance.

Methodology of proposed MIMO MC-CDMA with SIC and deep reinforcement learning for 6G optimizing spectrum and energy efficiency

The integrated methodology proposes the MIMO MC-CDMA with Edge AI optimization towards better spectral and energy efficiency in 6G wireless networks. The signal-first encodes user data by MC-CDMA spreading and OFDM modulation, followed by MIMO transmission at the base station. The transmitted signals encounter a frequency-selective fading channel, multi-user interference (MUI), and noise. The interference is suppressed to improve the SINR at the receiver using MIMO detection and Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC). Edge AI is also employed for adaptive beamforming and power control to achieve real-time resource allocation optimization based on Channel State Information (CSI). The system uses DRL for parameter updates in an iterative way, so as to minimize interference and improve reliability. In this way, latency is reduced, spectral efficiency increased, and interference cancellation made robust through this AI-based approach-a key distinguisher for next-generation 6G networks.

The proposed system uses an edge AI board to create intelligent functionality that extends beyond traditional resource allocation functions. Additionally, the imported AI circuit can allow for adaptive beam forming by dynamic adjustments of beam forming weights via real time Channel State Information (CSI), therefore improving signal performance and reducing interference at the same time. Finally, the AI circuit can perform intelligent scheduling decisions by assigning higher priority to users with higher traffic demand (or lower latency requirement) in combination with real world conditions present in the network at the moment. Similarly, this AI circuit will provide a better solution for managing interference by using Depth Reinforcement Learning (DRL) to optimize the SIC ordering scheme. Finally, the provision of these capabilities from the edge will minimize the time to effective service, improve the time required to receive a response from another device, enhance Quality of Service (QoS) and increase the overall efficiency of the network using edge AI circuits in 6G communications environments.

This architecture shown in Fig. 1. that employs AI to assist in processing data will provide an efficient and flexible structure to build 6G Wireless Networks using MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output). This type of communication supports MIMO User Data and channel information will be processed at a Base Station using MIMO, MC-CDMA (Multi code (Multi-Carrier) CDMA) and Edge AI Beam forming thereby improving the Quality of the Signal and decreasing interference on the network. Considering Flaky Signal Strength (Fading) and Channel Interference during transmission of Wireless signals, MIMO detection, Successive Interference Cancellation, and MC-CDMA Processing (Dispreading) would take place at the receiver site. Once all information is processed, Adaptive Power Optimization (APO) through Deep Reinforcement Learning (AI driven) would optimize spectral efficiency, energy efficiency and overall network communication reliability.

System model of edge AI-enabled MIMO MC-CDMA for 6G networks with SIC and DRL-based optimization

Multi-Carrier Code Division Multiple Access (MC-CDMA) is a new wireless communication technique that is based on the integration of Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) with Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA) to deliver enhanced spectral efficiency and enhanced resistance to multipath fading22. MIMO technology added to it is called MIMO MC-CDMA, which is a new paradigm for high-capacity wireless communications in next-generation networks such as 5G and 6G. In MIMO MC-CDMA, the system exploits spatial diversity and improves signal quality with multiple antennas at the receiver and transmitter. MIMO technology enables multiple data streams to be sent simultaneously over the same frequency band, significantly improving channel capacity and spectral efficiency. Alternatively, MC-CDMA spreads each user’s data symbols across a number of subcarriers using individual spreading codes in order to achieve robust multi-user access while combating the effects of narrowband interference and deep fades. The received signal model is used to calculate the per-user SNR based on the desired signal component, multi-user interference and noise.

The transmission process of signals in MIMO MC-CDMA involves several crucial steps: First, the data symbols of different users are spread across a wideband channel using orthogonal spreading codes such as Walsh-Hadamard. The spread symbols are subsequently mapped onto an array of subcarriers using OFDM, effectively transforming the wideband channel into a parallel array of narrowband channels with reduced inter-symbol interference (ISI). The MIMO configuration also enhances system performance by employing techniques such as spatial multiplexing, beamforming, and diversity schemes to improve the SINR and BER. Multi-user interference (MUI) is one of the basic issues in MIMO MC-CDMA and arises from overlaid transmissions in heavily loaded network environments. To combat this, Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) is implemented at the receiver, cancelling and detecting iteratively the strongest interfering signals first, thereby enhancing the detection of weaker signals. MIMO MC-CDMA is one of the facilitators for future wireless systems, enabling enhanced spectral efficiency, multipath fading resistance, and adaptive interference control, making it highly appropriate for future 6G networks and beyond. The Edge AI module, in turn, controls this adaptive beamforming mechanism and continuously learns to adapt and update the transmission strategies depending on the current network conditions.

Noise is assumed to be an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) where the mean of noise is zero and variance is constant. Assumptions are made that all users are co-synchronized and that they have equal power to start with. The quantity of transmit and receive antennas is fixed and the perfect channel state information (CSI) is considered at the receiver. Also, the DRL agent is based on discrete state and action space and can effectively learn and make decisions in a multi-user environment. The assumption is that the channel state information (CSI) is perfect at the receiver where the signal is properly detected and decoded. But in real life situations, pilot signals may be used to estimate CSI thereby creating errors in estimation. DRL-based optimization can preadapt the proposed model to such variations, which guarantees a strong performance in imperfect channel conditions.

It is assumed that the users are perfectly synchronized in time and frequency to prevent inter-symbol and inter-carrier interference. The spreading codes, which are applied in MC-CDMA system, are also said to be orthogonal meaning the minimum amount of multi-user interference in the ideal conditions. Nonetheless, there are possibilities of a slight loss of orthogonality in real life situations because of channel impairments and the proposed DRL-SIC mechanism caters to this.

Channel model is per-subcarrier in nature in order to model frequency-selective behavior within the MC-CDMA system. The subcarriers undergo separate flat fading channels, which are characterized as complex gain coefficients. In this way, the entire channel matrix is broken down to a number of subcarrier-specific channel responses. The signal at each subcarrier received is the product of the transmitted signal and the channel gain of the channel and the noise. The method would make analysis easier as it transforms a frequency selective channel into parallel flat fading sub channels and allow efficient signal detection and allocation of resources with the proposed DRL based framework.

Spectrum efficiency optimization with successive interference cancellation (SIC)

In a MIMO MC-CDMA system, multiple users transmit signals in parallel over the same frequency band, resulting in multi-user interference (MUI). This interference seriously degrades the overall spectral efficiency of the system, impacting data rate performance and increasing the bit error rate (BER). At the receiver side, Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) is employed to suppress these effects, identifying and cancelling out the stronger signals in sequence first and enhancing the reception of weaker users’ signals23. Through sequential cancellation of strong interfering signals, SIC improves signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR), resulting in more effective use of the spectrum.

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for energy efficiency and resource allocation

In the future of 6G wireless networks, it is imperative to ensure proper resource allocation due to the very dynamic and changing nature of network environments. Traditional heuristic-based optimization methods, such as rule-based or convex optimization approaches, are likely to be hindered by difficulties in adjusting to real-time variations in channel conditions, user traffic, and energy demands. These conventional methods entail massive earlier experience of the network dynamics and may not work well in complex multi-user, multi-channel scenarios. To bridge the above disadvantages, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as a viable challenger by enabling intelligent and adaptive mechanism of resource allocations. DRL employs real-time learning and decision-making functionalities for dynamically allocating the power, spectrum, and scheduling resources with a promise of 6G MIMO MC-CDMA networks with low latency, high capacity, and low energy consumption. The dimension of state of a system is determined by the number of users and system parameters taken into account in the network. Action space is measured to be the decisions on resource allocation to each user within the system. In a system of N users, the action vector will have N elements with each element representing the amount of power an individual user will have. The neural network that is applied in the DRL framework is a fully connected Deep Q-Network made up of an input layer, several hidden layers, and an output layer. The size of the input layer is the same as the state vector dimension. To address the relationship of the complex system, two hidden layers are utilized with 128 and 64 neurons, respectively. The hidden layers use ReLU activation functions to add non-linearity, and the output layer uses a linear activation function to provide estimates of Q-values of each action. This architecture provides effective learning and correct decision making in resource allocation that is dynamic.

The DRL model has the Adam optimizer that offers efficient and adaptive learning in complicated environments. It is the loss function of Mean Squared Error on the predicted Q-values and target Q-values of the Bellman equation. This loss term works to reduce the gap between the anticipated and actual rewards in training. An appropriate learning rate is chosen to guarantee a stable model convergence. Adam optimizer is used in conjunction with the MSE loss, which allows easy training of the DRL agent to optimal resources allocation and interference control.

The past state, action and reward transitions are stored in an experience replay buffer which enhances learning stability and efficiency. The buffer size is chosen according to the need to trade between memory utilization and learning diversity. A large enough buffer also makes sure that the model trains on a large collection of past experiences that are not correlated with each other. In the given work, the buffer size is selected so as to keep representative samples of various network conditions without too much computation overhead. Such a design decision allows converging the DRM model steadily and enhances its generalization skills in dynamic wireless scenarios.

Computational complexity analysis

MIMO processing, Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC), beamforming operations, and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) based optimization have an impact on the computational complexity of the proposed Edge AI enabled MIMO MC-CDMA system. The number of users and subcarriers depend on the processing complexity of O(K × N) for MIMO MC-CDMA. Multiple users’ iterative interference canceller can be obtained with computational complexity of O(K2).

The complexity of the update in the beamforming is mostly dependent on the complexity of the matrix inversion and optimisation of the beam weights and is of the order O(N3) with N being the number of antennas. The DRL component is expensive in terms of computation, due to the forward inference and backpropagation procedure performed in the neural network layers. The complexity of the DRL inference mainly depends on the number of hidden layers, number of neurons, number of training episodes and replay memory size. The proposed framework involves a more computational complex than the conventional MMSE only or non-learning approaches, because of the adaptive learning process that involves iterative SIC and DRL. But the proposed system provides better interference suppression, adaptive resource allocation and better spectral and energy efficiency.

The number of users, number of antennas, replay memory size are factors influencing the scalability of the proposed framework. The more users that are connected to the network, the more capacity will be provided, but the more complex the SIC will be due to the sequential interference canceller. In the same way, the greater the number of antennas the greater the spatial diversity and the more reliable the communication, but the more the beamforming/computing/channel estimation calculations. There is a trade-off between more memory used and complexity of training, and better the stability and accuracy of learning by DRL, the larger the size of the replay memory. While there is more overhead to computation, Edge AI has the benefit of processing data at the point of use with minimal latency compared to cloud-based optimization. Edge-level processing is used to shorten the inference delay per scheduling interval, allowing for quicker real-time resource allocation, interference management, and network adaptability for dense and delay-sensitive 6G wireless communication environments.

The Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) value of each user in multi-user MIMO MC-CDMA system is calculated independently taking into account the received signal power, multi-user interference and additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). SNR of k-th user is the ratio of the actual power of the desired signal to the total power of all the interference and noise signals.15\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${SNR}_{K}=\frac{{P}_{signal,k}}{{P}_{interface,k}+{P}_{noise,k}}$$\end{document}

In this case, the signal power is determined based on channel state information (CSI) and beamforming weights, and the interference comes from other users. The final interference level is further reduced after Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) processing.

After demodulation and SIC decoding, the bit sequences transmitted and received by each user are compared to compute the Bit Error Rate (BER). It is the ratio of the incorrect bits received to the number of bits sent.16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{BER}} = \frac{{N_{error bits} }}{{N_{total bits} }}$$\end{document}

Monte Carlo simulations are run several times to guarantee statistical accuracy and an average BER is calculated.

Lastly, the performance is repeatedly tested using metrics such as spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, SINR, BER etc. to achieve consistency and reproducibility in dynamic network conditions shown in Eqs. Discussions

The advanced AI-based optimization techniques help the proposed Edge AI-enhanced MIMO MC-CDMA or the future generation 6G wireless communication system to outperform multiple current existing model systems. To this end, the system is designed to incorporate deep reinforcement learning for resource allocation and interference management to realize improved SE, EE, SINR, and BER. According to the simulation result, the proposed model is efficient and perform better compared to other techniques such as MIMO-OFDM, Hybrid Precoding mmWave Massive MIMO, and Deep Learning-Based MIMO Detection. The findings also demonstrate the following aspects of increased efficiency, namely, SE, EE, SINR, and BER. The proposed system gains an SE of 32.7 bits/s/Hz, this is much higher than conventional MC-CDMA and CMFB which is 12.5 bits/s/Hz and even higher than the state-of-art deep learning technique for MIMO detection which is 25.3 bits/s/Hz. Similarly, EE is enhanced to 14.8 bits/Joule, which can be attributed to better power efficiency than that of the earlier models like Hybrid Precoding mmWave Massive MIMO (9.1 bits/Joule). This clearly proves that signal quality can be enhanced through interferences through having a SINR of 34 dB; better than the conventional methods like MIMO-OFDM of 22 dB and Hybrid Precoding MIMO of 26 dB. With this the BER is equal to 10⁻5 implying better transmission reliability mostly at higher SNR. Here the DRL – based Successive Interference Cancellation (SIC) approach improves the system performance by providing dynamic allocating resources, low interference and stable system operation even in the large number of users.

Another incentive of the system is the capability to provide an adaptive power control and interference management with DRL based successively interference cancellation. This intelligent approach increases SINR by a considerable extent so that the quality of signal transmission in dense network is improved. Furthermore, the design of the proposed model introduces higher spectral efficiency, it can serve more users and ensure a reliable communication at the same time. The use of deep learning in the methodology also contributes to energy efficiency since the resources are well distributed, power is not wasted, as well as enhancing the stable network. Also, the system minimizes the bit error rate (BER) and thus lowers the incidences of data transmission errors and increases the reliability of the system. As for the performance of the proposed model, it is of surprisingly low BER, which is achievable even in extreme network environment. Both features namely the Edge AI and the MIMO MC-CDMA make sure that the system to be anticipated for network requirements so that the quality is guaranteed to be stable. The results clearly show the effectiveness and applicability of AI for improving the characteristics of dispensing wireless communication systems, which makes the proposed strategy a promising solution for modern networks of high-speed communication. Overall, the Edge AI-enabled MIMO MC-CDMA (6G) system effectively addresses key challenges in next-generation wireless networks by improving spectral efficiency, optimizing energy consumption, enhancing signal quality, and ensuring reliable data transmission. The integration of AI-driven optimization and interference cancellation strategies makes this approach highly suitable for dense and high-speed communication environments, demonstrating a clear advantage over conventional and baseline 5G technologies.

The effectiveness of the proposed system in terms of high-performance is demonstrated with the help of the comparative analysis of the proposed system with the current techniques like MIMO-OFDM and traditional MC-CDMA. The findings are always better in spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, SINR and BER. Such gains are attained as a result of the combination of the DRL-based resource allocation and the SIC-based interference cancellation. Moreover, the simulations are done in realistic channel conditions and averaged with numerous runs, which assures reliability. Thus, the achieved advancements are reasonable and testify to the efficiency of the suggested solution to 6G wireless networks.

The spectral efficiency of 32.7 bits per second per Hertz is justified by the SE expression in Eq. (10) which is fired on the Shannon capacity formula. Based on this, SE is growing logarithmically with SINR. SIC reduces interference in the proposed system, increasing the values of SINR (approximately 34 dB). The replacement of this enhanced SINR with the SE formula yields increased data rates per unit bandwidth. Also, DRL-based resource allocation optimizes the power consumption among users, and it also provides greater spectral efficiency in a multi-user setting.

Ablation analysis

Ablution analysis is done to determine the value of each element in the suggested system. Tests on the performance are done by deleting major modules DRL optimization and SIC separately. The system loses its energy efficiency and resource allocation without the presence of DRL. In the absence of SIC, the interference is higher, which causes reduced SINR and high BER. The combination of Edge AI, DRL, and SIC leads to the highest performance of the full model. This discussion supports that all elements are important towards enhancing efficiency of the system. The performance is compared considering four configurations to check the effectiveness of every component. The basic communication is offered by the baseline MIMO MC-CDMA system (Eqs. (1–4)) but it has high interference. The inclusion of SIC (Eqs. (6−10) enhances SINR and minimizes BER through the cancellation of interference. Resource allocation and energy efficiency (Eqs. (11−14)) benefit with the use of DRA alone though interference is not completely eliminated. The entire proposed structure, which is a combination of MIMO MC-CDMA, SIC and DRL, offers the optimal performance with respect to spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, SINR, and BER. The comparison can be used to measure the contribution of each component in the proposed system. The initial model demonstrates poor results because of interference and lack of optimization. The former SIC adds value to signal quality (reduced interfering) and the latter DRL adds value to allocation of resources and energy-efficiency. All these are functional when combined to a large extent to enhance spectral efficiency, SINR, and BER. Therefore, the analysis of the ablation shows that every one of the modules leads to the overall enhancement of the proposed framework performance.
