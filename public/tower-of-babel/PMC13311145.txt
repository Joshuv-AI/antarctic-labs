GPR107-EGFR crosstalk promotes ovarian cancer metastasis by regulating IL-6 release via PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway
Authors: Jing Zhao, Xintao Wang, Tiankang Li, Jie Chen, Haiyan Zhu, Liang Yang, Yingchao Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•GPR107 was overexpressed in OVCA and could promote OVCA metastasis.•GPR107-EGFR crosstalk promoted intensive EGFR phosphorylation and downstream PI3K/AKT/NF-κB activation.•Monoclonal antibodies targeting IL-6 could inhibit OVCA metastasis induced by GPR107-EGFR crosstalk.Keywords: Ovarian cancer, G protein-coupled receptor 107, Metastasis, Epidermal growth factor receptor, IL-6

Introduction

Ovarian cancer (OVCA) has the highest mortality rate among gynecological malignancies, accounting for 4.4% of all cancer-related deaths among women worldwide[1]. The five-years survival rate in patients with early-stage OVCA is approximately 90%, whereas it decreases to <25% in patients with advanced disease[2]. Unfortunately, 70% of OVCA patients are diagnosed with advanced stage and extensive distant metastasis[3]. Therefore, there is an urgent need to understand the mechanisms of OVCA metastasis and to develop more effective treatment strategies.

Therapeutic drugs targeting epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) have been one of the most successful explorations of cancer treatment in recent years. Studies have shown that EGFR is commonly overexpressed in epithelial OVCA, and high EGFR expression is observed in up to 98% of advanced epithelial OVCA, which is significantly positively correlated with poor patient prognosis [4]. However, neither tyrosine kinase inhibitor drugs nor monoclonal antibodies targeting EGFR significantly improve the survival outcomes of OVCA[[5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]]. Although the EGFR pathway plays an important role in the occurrence and development of OVCA, it is currently unclear how to utilize this pathway to generate therapeutic benefits, resulting in an awkward situation of having targets but no effective drugs. This suggests a unique regulatory mechanism for EGFR pathway in OVCA.

G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) and EGFR receptor tyrosine kinases are two major membrane receptor families in human body that can "interact and dialogue" at the cell membrane level, jointly affecting the activation of downstream signaling pathway networks[[11], [12], [13], [14]]. Treatment strategies targeting GPCR and EGFR crosstalk have enormous translational potential. GPR107 is a novel orphan GPCR. The human GPR107 gene has 18-exons located at 9q34.2–3 spanning 86.4 kb, and its cDNA encodes a 552-residue protein[[15], [16]]. It is mainly expressed on the cell membrane and Golgi apparatus, and has been reported to play an important role in hypertension development[17], memory impairments[18], and preadipocyte proliferation[19]. Recently, GPR107 was reported to be involved in the anti-hepatocellular carcinoma effect of Nitidine chloride[20]. GPR107 is over-expressed in prostate cancer and associated with advanced tumor stage, vascular invasion and metastasis[21]. Our previous study also identified GPR107 as a potential driver of cervical cancer[22].

In the current study, GPR107 was upregulated in OVCA and associated with advanced-stage disease. Specifically, GPR107 promoted migration, invasion, and epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT). Mechanically, GPR107 could interact with and transactivate EGFR on the cell membrane, resulting in IL-6 release via PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway. The metastasis ability caused by GPR107 could be arrogated by administration of monoclonal antibodies targeting IL-6. These findings provide new insights into the detailed mechanisms underlying OVCA metastasis and novel potential strategies for OVCA treatment.

Materials and methods

Clinical samples

Fifty-six pairs of tumor tissue and corresponding normal tissue samples were collected from patients who underwent surgical resection and diagnosed with OVCA at Shanghai First Maternity and Infant Hospital from November 2020 to January 2022. Patient samples were used in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All protocols using human specimens were approved and informed consent was waived by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Shanghai First Maternity and Infant Hospital.

Animal experiments

For the peritoneal metastasis model, NOD-SCID mice (female, 6–8 weeks, 20±2 g) were intraperitoneally injected with luciferase CAOV-3 or SW626 cells (5 × 106/200 μL each) and subsequently treated with or without the indicated reagents. Four weeks later, mice were anesthetized and injected intraperitoneally with 200 μL of the luciferase substrate, d-luciferin. An in vivo fluorescence imaging system (PerkinElmer) was used to obtain whole-body images of the mice. The total bioluminescence intensity in the abdominal region was quantified using the imaging system software to evaluate tumor burden and metastatic progression[23]. Notably, bioluminescence signal intensity is well-documented to correlate with the number of viable tumor cells, and this non-invasive quantitative approach closely reflects the metastatic aggressiveness of tumor cells, which is relevant to the clinical progression of ovarian cancer. After the study, all mice were euthanized by neck amputation after anesthetization with 0.3% pentobarbital sodium solution.

Cell lines and cultures

The normal human ovarian surface epithelial cell line IOSE80 and human OVCA cell lines (SKOV3, CAOV-3, SW626, HO-8910, and OVCAR3) were obtained from the Cell Culture Center at the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and authenticated. Stably expressing shControl or shGPR107 luciferase CAOV-3 cell lines were constructed by ViewSolid Biotech. The cell lines were grown in RPMI 1640 (Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (Hyclone), streptomycin (100 μg/mL), and penicillin (100 U/mL) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

Quantitative real-time PCR

For mRNA analysis, total RNA from cells or tissues was extracted using TRIzol (Invitrogen), and cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Takara, Dalian, China), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. qPCR was performed using the QuantiTect SYBR Green PCR Kit (Takara) on a Step One Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). The reactions were performed twice in triplicate. The primers were designed using Primer Premier 5.0 and were produced by Sangon Biotech. Primer sequence was listed in Supplementary Table S1. The 2-ΔΔCt method (Bio-Rad CFX Manager software 3.1) was used to determine the relative expression levels of RNA.

Western blot

Proteins were extracted using RIPA Lysis Buffer (Beyotime) and quantified using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) method (Pierce). Proteins were separated using sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Beyotime). The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk for 2 h at room temperature. Membranes were then incubated with the corresponding primary antibodies at 4 °C for 12 h. All antibodies used in this study were listed in Supplementary Table S2. Finally, the membranes were incubated with the secondary antibodies for 2 h. Protein bands were visualized by enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) and analyzed using ImageJ 2 × Software.

Immunohistochemistry staining

Immunohistochemical staining was performed according to the EnVision two-step procedure using the Dako REAL™ Envision™ Detection System (Dako). Slices (4 µm thick) were dewaxed, hydrated, and washed, and endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched after treatment with 0.3% hydrogen peroxide for 20 min at room temperature. After microwave antigen retrieval, the slides were blocked and incubated with primary antibody against GPR107 (1:150) at 4 °C overnight. Subsequently, the sections were rinsed and incubated with horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary antibodies for 60 min at room temperature. After three rinses, staining was visualized using the peroxide substrate solution 3, 3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB). The slides were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated in graded alcohol, and mounted. Images were captured using a light microscope.

GRP107 over-expression cell line construction and shRNA transfection

pcDNA3.1-GPR107 or control vector plasmids, the luciferase SW626 cell line, short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting GPR107 (shGPR107), or scramble shRNA (shControl) were purchased from ViewSolid Biotech. The shRNA sequences are as follows: shControl: 5′- CAC CCC GTC ACT GGC GCC TGC CCC GCG AAC GGG GCA GGC GCC AGT GAC GG-3′; shGPR107: 5′- CAC CGC ATC ATT GGA GCC TGC TTT CCG AAG AAA GCA GGC TCC AAT GAT GC −3′

For construction of overexpression plasmids, RNA was extracted following the instructions of the RNeasy Mini Kit (Invitrogen). First-strand cDNA was obtained using the MaximaTM First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Fermentas). GPR107 full-length DNA sequences were PCR-amplified with the primers mentioned in Supplementary Table S1. Full-length sequences of Flag-GPR107 were inserted into the pcDNA3.1 vector using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) to obtain pcDNA3.1-Flag-GPR107.

We generated lentiviruses encoding shRNAs by co-transfecting recombinant pLL3.7 vectors, pRSV-Rev, pMDLg/pRRE, and pMD2.G into HEK293T cells. Lentiviruses were obtained at 48 and 72 h after transfection. Infectious titers were determined by counting the number of GFP-expressing colonies. OVCA cell lines were incubated with polybrene (10 µg/mL) (Sigma-Aldrich), and the lentivirus encoding shGPR107 or shControl was incubated for 24 h. The supernatant was discarded and replaced with fresh RPMI 1640. After 96 h of infection, the cells were collected to determine the infection efficiency by western blot.

Cells migration and invasion assays

Transwell migration assays were performed using Corning chambers with 8-µm poroaus membranes (Corning). Approximately 2 × 105 cancer cells in 100 μL serum-free medium were seeded in the upper chamber, whereas complete medium with 10% FBS was placed in the lower chamber. After incubation for 24 h, cells on the bottom surface of the membrane were rinsed with phosphate buffer and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde. The cells were stained with DAPI. Images of the cells in nine randomly selected fields of view were captured under a fluorescence microscope at 100 × magnification.

For invasion assays, Boyden chambers with filter inserts (pore size, 8-µm) coated with Matrigel Membrane Matrix (40µg/15 µL; Vigorous Biotechnology Beijing Co.) were used. Approximately 2 × 105 cells were placed in the upper chamber while 700 µL of medium with 10% FBS was placed in the lower chamber. After incubation for 48 h, the remaining part of the invasion assay was performed following the same protocol as that used for the migration assay.

Immunoprecipitation assay of GPR107

The signal peptide of GPR107, 3 × Flag, and GPR107 sequences were fused by PCR. All the primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1. The fused sequences were cloned into pcDNA3.1. Cells were transiently transfected with pcDNA3.1-Flag-GPR107 using Lipofectamine 2000, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells are lysed for 30-min with lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors (Roche). Immunoprecipitation (IP) was performed by adding FLAG antibody or GPR107 antibody to the mixture for 10 h on ice. Then protein A agarose beads were added to the mixture. The mixture was shaken for 7 h at 4 °C. Immunoprecipitants were washed ten times with phosphate buffer, and then subjected to SDS-PAGE. Electrophoretic proteins were then transferred onto a PVDF membrane. The membranes were then subjected to chemiluminescence and exposure.

Immunofluorescence

The cells were washed with phosphate buffer and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min. The cells were then incubated for 10 min with 0.2% Triton X-100 to change the permeability and washed three times with phosphate buffer. Next, the cells were soaked in 1% BSA at 37 °C for 20 min and incubated separately with primary antibodies, anti-GPR107 (1:100) and anti-EGFR (1:100) diluted with 1% BSA, at room temperature for 1 h. After washing with 0.05% Tween-20, the cells were incubated separately with the secondary antibody for 1 h. After washed with 0.01 M phosphate buffer the cells were then observed under a fluorescence microscope.

Glutathione S-transferase pull-down assay

Glutathione S-transferase (GST) pull-down assay was performed in vitro using about 900 µg lysates of HEK-293T cells transfected with Flag-GPR107. The lysates were blended with 15 µg GST-EGFR or GST. The above mixtures were combined with glutathione/Sepharose beads and incubated for 6 h at 4 °C. They were then washed 12 times with the buffer. The mixtures were run on 12% SDS-PAGE and incubated with anti-FLAG (1:100) and anti-GST antibodies (1:100) separately. Antibodies were diluted with 1% BSA.

ELISA assay

IL-6 ELISA kit (RAB0306) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich. The manufacturer's protocol was used to detect the protein level in the cell culture supernatant.

Bioinformatics analysis

UALCAN analysis (https://ualcan.path.uab.edu/index.html) based on TCGA data was used to analyze the protein expression of GPR107 in OVCA tissues and normal ovarian tissues The correlation of GPR107 and EGFR in OVCA in TCGA dataset was analyzed on GEPIA online database (http://gepia.cancerpku.cn/). RNA seq and clinical data of OCVA in GDC TCGA were obtained from UCSC Xena database (https://xenabrowser.net/). R software (Version 4.2.1) was used to analyze the survival status of groups with different GPR107 and EGFR expression patterns.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism, version 9.3.0. Data are expressed as Mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. The unpaired t-test and paired t-test were used to compare the differences between the two groups. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

GPR107 was overexpressed in OVCA and associated with advanced clinical stages

UALCAN analysis showed that the protein expression of GPR107 was higher in OVCA tissues than that in normal ovarian tissues (P = 0.0035) (Fig. 1A) and increased with advanced clinical stages (Fig. 1B). The mRNA and protein levels of GPR107 were higher in OVCA tissues than that in paired normal tissues (Fig. 1C-D). As shown in Fig. 1E-F, the mRNA and protein levels of GPR107 were significantly upregulated in OVCA cell lines (SKOV3, SW626, CAOV-3, HO-8910, and OVCAR3) compared to those in normal human ovarian surface epithelial cells (IOSE80). Immunohistochemical staining showed higher expression of GPR107 in tumor tissues than in paired adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1G).

GPR107 could promote OVCA metastasis

To further explore the biological function of GPR107 in OVCA progression, we performed gain-and loss-of-function assays. Transwell assays showed that GPR107 knockdown inhibited invasion and migration, while GPR107 overexpression significantly enhanced invasion and migration in OVCA cell lines (Fig. 2A-D). EMT process is a crucial procedure in tumor metastasis. As shown in Fig. 2E, GPR107 knockdown suppressed the expression of N-cadherin and Vimentin while enhancing the expression of E-cadherin in CAOV-3 and SKOV3 cells. In contrast, GPR107 overexpression enhanced the expression of N-cadherin and Vimentin but inhibited E-cadherin expression in SW626 and OVCAR3 cells (Fig. 2F). OVCA is mainly characterized by intraperitoneal implantation metastasis. To further explore the role of GPR107 in OVCA metastasis in vivo, a mouse model of peritoneal metastasis was established by inoculating luciferase-expressing CAOV-3 cells stably transduced with shControl or shGPR107. Bioluminescence imaging revealed that bioluminescence intensity, representing intraperitoneal tumor burden, was significantly lower in the shGPR107 group than in the shControl group (Fig. 2G). To verify the pro-metastatic effect of GPR107, mice were inoculated with luciferase-expressing SW626 cells stably overexpressing GPR107 or carrying empty pcDNA3.1 vector. Consistently, the bioluminescence signal in the GPR107-overexpressing group was significantly higher than that in the control group (Fig. 2H).

GPR107 is functionally associated with EGFR activation in OVCA metastasis

EGFR is aberrantly activated in OVCA and plays a crucial role in metastasis[[24], [25]]. Recently, GPCR have been reported to transactivate EGFR and exhibit close crosstalk[26]. The bioinformatics analysis showed that GPR107 and EGFR were co-expressed in OVCA and showed a significant positive correlation (Fig. 3A). And the survival of the simultaneous high expression of GPR107 and EGFR group was significantly worse than that of the group with single high or both low expression of GPR107 and EGFR (Fig. 3B). IHC staining was performed to assess GPR107 and EGFR protein expression in OVCA tissues (Fig. 3C), and statistical analysis confirmed their positive correlation (Fig. 3D). The phosphorylation level of EGFR was decreased in GPR107 depleted CAOV-3 and SKOV3 cells but increased in GPR107 over-expressed SW626 and OVCAR3 cells (Fig. 3C). Moreover, GPR107 knockdown significantly decreased the migration, invasion, EMT progression and the phosphorylation level of EGFR in CAOV-3 cells, whereas treatment with NSC228155, an EGFR agonist, reversed the above effect of GPR107 knockdown on OVCA cells. In contrast, the EGFR inhibitor Cetuximab abrogated GPR107 overexpression-induced migration, invasion, EMT progression, and the phosphorylation level of EGFR in SW626 cells (Fig. 3D-E).

GPR107 could interacted with the extracellular domain of EGFR on the cell membrane in OVCA

Flag-GPR107 expression in 293T cells was identified using the indicated antibody (Fig. 4A). The GST pull-down assay showed that GPR107 and EGFR interacted directly in vitro (Fig. 4B). To confirm the interaction between EGFR and GPR107 in SKOV3 cells, we conducted IP with anti-GPR107 antibody, followed by immunoblotting (IB) with anti-EGFR antibody, which demonstrated that GPR107 co-immunoprecipitated with EGFR (Fig. 4C). Immunofluorescence showed that GPR107 and EGFR co-localized in the cytomembrane of SKOV3 cells (Fig. 4D). To further define the GPR107 binding site on EGFR, different truncates of EGFR were constructed, and co-IP was performed separately with GPR107. These results showed that GPR107 could bind to the extracellular domain (1–634 aa) of EGFR (Fig. 4E).

The PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway activated by the GPR107-EGFR interaction is involved in OVCA metastasis

PI3K/AKT/NF-κB is one of the main downstream pathways of EGFR and plays crucial roles in the development of cancers[27]. As shown in Fig. 5A, GPR107 knockdown obviously reduced the expression of p-EGFR, p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-IκB-α in CAOV-3 cells, which can be rescued by an EGFR agonist (NSC228155). Conversely, GPR107 overexpression increased the expression of p-EGFR, p-PI3K, p-AKT, and p-IκB-α, which was partially reversed by Cetuximab treatment. These results demonstrated that PI3K/AKT/ NF-κB might be the downstream signaling pathway of GPR107-EGFR in OVCA cells.

Subsequently, we investigated whether the PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway was involved in OVCA metastasis regulated by GPR107. The migration, invasion, EMT, and p-AKT levels of CAOV-3 cells were decreased by GPR107 knockdown, while the PI3K agonist (IGF-1) reversed the above effects of GPR107 knockdown (Fig. 5B-D). In SW626 cells, GPR107 overexpression improved migration, invasion, EMT, and p-AKT levels, while PI3K pathway inhibitor (LY294002) partially reversed the above effects of GPR107 overexpression. The NF-κB activator (TNF-α) also recovered the migration, invasion, EMT, and p-IκB levels, which were inhibited by GPR107 knockdown. While the NF-κB inhibitor BAY11–7082 partially inhibited the migration, invasion, EMT, and p-IκB level promoted by GPR107 over-expression (Fig. 5E-G). These results suggest that the PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway may be involved in OVCA metastasis regulated by GPR107-EGFR crosstalk.

Autocrine secretion of IL-6 was induced by the GPR107-EGFR/PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway in OVCA

Given that NF‑κB functions as a core transcription factor governing the expression of multiple pro‑inflammatory cytokines, we next investigated whether GPR107–EGFR/PI3K/AKT/NF‑κB signaling regulates cytokine production in OVCA cells[28]. The expression of multiple cytokines was examined after GPR107 knockdown in CAOV-3 cells, among which IL-6 showed the most significant changes (Fig. 6A). Knockdown or overexpression of GPR107 remarkably inhibited or promoted IL-6 release in the culture supernatant of CAOV-3 and SW626 cells respectively, verifying IL-6 release induced by GPR107 (Fig. 6B). Inflammation plays a crucial role in OVCA proliferation and metastasis. The pleiotropic cytokine IL-6 is strongly implicated in OVCA progression and can affect OVCA development through autocrine pathways[29]. Whether IL-6 release induced by GPR107 was dependent on the EGFR/PI3K/AKT/NF-κB pathway was also analyzed. In GPR107-knockdown CAOV-3 cells, IL-6 release was significantly increased by EGFR agonist (NSC228155), PI3K agonist (IGF-1), and NF-κB activator (TNF-α), respectively (Fig. 6C-E). In GPR107-overexpression SW626 cells, the EGFR inhibitor (Cetuximab), PI3K inhibitor (LY294002), and NF-κB inhibitor (BAY11–7082) significantly reduced IL-6 release (Fig. 6F-H). IL-6 release regulated by GPR107 was dependent on the EGFR/PI3K/AKT/ NF-κB signaling pathway.

IL-6 could facilitate OVCA metastasis, and abrogating IL-6 could significantly decrease the pro-metastatic effect of GPR107

As shown in Fig. 7A-C, exogenous IL-6 recombinant protein ameliorated the inhibition of migration, invasion, and EMT induced by GPR107 knockdown in OVCA cells. Conversely, anti-IL-6 antibodies reversed cell migration, invasion, and EMT induced by GPR107 overexpression (Fig. 7.D-F). To further examine the functions of IL-6 in vivo, a peritoneal metastasis mouse model was constructed. The fluorescence intensity representing tumor metastasis was weakened in shGPR107 group compared to shContol group, while the administration of IL-6 recombination protein restored the metastatic capacity of shGPR107 tumor cells (Fig. 7G-H). GPR107 overexpression significantly enhanced the fluorescence intensity, whereas the administration of the anti-IL-6 antibody significantly decreased the pro-metastatic effect of GPR107 overexpression (Fig. 7I-J). IL-6 was the key downstream factor in the metastasis of OVCA regulated by GPR107.

Discussion

OVCA is a malignancy with an extremely poor prognosis[30], and metastasis is the main cause of OVCA-related deaths. However, the mechanisms underlying metastasis remain unclear. In this current study, GPR107 expression was abnormally upregulated in OVCA and was inversely correlated with advanced clinical stages. GPR107 promoted the migration, invasion, and EMT of OVCA cells, both in vivo and in vitro. GPR107 is a newly discovered GPCR, but its function remains to be elucidated. In this study, GPR107 was found to be involved in malignant progression of OVCA, which may provide new insights into the mechanisms underlying OVCA metastasis. GPR107 is an orphan GPCR predicted to be a promising candidate receptor for neuronostatin involved in glucose metabolism[31], cardiovascular function[20], and hypertension[17]. Few studies have explored the role of GPR107 in tumors. In our previous study, GPR107 was identified as a potential driver gene of cervical cancer[32]. GPR107-silencing inhibited the proliferation and migration of prostate cancer and altered key genes and oncogenic signaling pathways[31]. The current study showed that ectopic expression of GPR107 in OVCA cells markedly increased their metastatic ability both in vitro and in vivo, which is consistent with previous reports in prostate cancer[33] and liver cancer[20].

EGFR is a 170 kDa receptor tyrosine kinase and aberrant activation of EGFR has been taken as a major cause of malignant transformation and cancer metastasis[[34], [35], [36]]. EGFR is significantly upregulated in OVCA[37] and is a key signaling molecule that drives cellular proliferation, migration, and invasion[38]. Increased serum levels of EGFR are associated with poor progression-free survival in patients with epithelial OVCA[39]. Although the EGFR pathway appears to play an important role in OVCA, multiple clinical studies have found that therapeutic drugs targeting EGFR do not improve the survival outcomes of OVCA. It is not yet clear how this pathway can be exploited to yield a therapeutic benefit.

Accumulating evidence has indicated that the interaction between GPCR and EGFR plays an important role in cancer progression. Protease-activated receptor 4, a subgroup of GPCR, induced EGFR phosphorylation and promoted tumor development in colon cancer[40]. In lung cancer cholecystokinin 8 increased cell proliferation in an EGFR-dependent manner[41]. Here, we demonstrated that GPR107 could interact with EGFR in the cell membrane inducing EGFR phosphorylation and activate PI3K/AKT/NF-κB, which is one of the main downstream pathways of EGFR. GPCR have been reported to activate EGFR through ligand-dependent and ligand-independent mechanisms. EGF is the primary ligand for EGFR[[42], [43]]. It remains to be clarified that the activation of EGFR by GPR107 is EGF-dependent or -independent. And the detailed mechanism of GPR107-EGFR crosstalk remains further exploration. Importantly, our current data support a correlation between GPR107 and EGFR signaling, but do not exclude the possibility that GPR107 may also regulate additional receptor tyrosine kinases. Given that other ErbB family members, especially HER2, exhibit significant clinical relevance in ovarian cancer[44], future studies are warranted to determine whether GPR107 also interacts with or modulates HER2 or other RTKs.

IL-6 is a pleiotropic cytokine associated with metastasis in different types of cancers[[45], [46], [47]]. To date, research has established IL-6 as one of the key immunoregulatory cytokines in the OVCA microenvironment, which initiates multiple signaling pathways and leads to a variety of outcomes, including tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, and chemoresistance[48]. IL-6 also promotes the invasion of cancer cells into other tissues and blood vessels as an important trigger for EMT[49]. Inhibition of NF-κB/IL-6 signaling suppresses migration and angiogenesis in triple-negative breast cancer[50]. Here, we showed that GPR107 could regulate IL-6 release and thus promoted OVCA metastasis in vitro and in vivo. GPR107-induced IL-6 release was dependent on the EGFR/PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway, which was of great importance in the development of OVCA.

In summary, GPR107-EGFR crosstalk likely functions as a key promoter of OVCA metastasis by increasing the release of IL-6, which is dependent on the PI3K/AKT/NF-κB signaling pathway. This study provides new evidence to elucidate the function and molecular mechanism of GPR107 in OVCA metastasis and provides a novel direction for developing new treatment strategies for OVCA.
