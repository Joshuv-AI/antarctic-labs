Inadvertent dislodgement of a biliary metallic stent during duodenal stent placement
Authors: Hidehito Sumiya, Yoshihide Kanno, Shinsuke Koshita, Takahisa Ogawa, Hiroaki Kusunose, Toshitaka Sakai, Kei Ito
Published: 2026

Endoscopic gastroduodenal stent placement for malignant gastric outlet obstruction is considered effective and safe. 1​ 2​ 3​ ​4​ However, procedure-related adverse events can occur. 1​ 2​ ​3​ We report the first case of inadvertent dislodgement of a previously deployed biliary metallic stent during duodenal stent placement, which was successfully managed endoscopically.

An 81-year-old woman with a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor had previously undergone biliary drainage using a fully covered self-expandable metallic stent. She subsequently developed malignant duodenal obstruction in the horizontal portion, and endoscopic duodenal stent placement was attempted. The duodenoscope could be advanced only to the superior duodenal angle ( Video 1 and Fig. 1a ). When the 10-Fr delivery system of the duodenal stent was advanced over the guidewire, the biliary stent migrated toward the anal side of the duodenum ( Fig. 1b ). The guidewire was presumed to have passed through the mesh at the distal end of the biliary metallic stent, resulting in the unintended dislodgement of the biliary stent. The stent delivery system was withdrawn without deployment to avoid mechanical interference between the duodenal and biliary stents, including entrapment-related narrowing of the duodenal stent or intestinal perforation due to uncontrolled parallel stent placement.

Fluoroscopic images during duodenal stent placement. ( a ) Duodenal obstruction was observed in the horizontal portion. ( b ) The biliary stent migrated toward the anal side of the duodenum during duodenal stent insertion (arrowhead).

Since the major papilla could not be visualized endoscopically, biliary cannulation was achieved under fluoroscopic guidance using a novel rotatable sphincterotome ( Figs 2 , 3 a ). 5​ A 7-Fr double-pigtail plastic stent was then placed. Finally, a duodenal stent was placed in a position that did not interfere with the migrated biliary stent ( Fig. 3b ). The migrated stent subsequently passed spontaneously in the stool without any intra- or post-procedural adverse events. Oral intake was resumed the day after the procedure, and the patient was discharged on post-procedural day 4.
