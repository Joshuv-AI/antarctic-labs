Pain catastrophizing and its associated factors among puerperal women with vaginal birth : a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yuqiong Yang, Hongying Zuo, Man wu, Biru Luo, Ju Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo investigate the prevalence of pain catastrophizing among women with vaginal birth and explore its associated factors.MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, a total of 282 participants who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were selected by convenience sampling from Delivery Room Ward of a hospital in Sichuan Province. Pain Catastrophizing Scale, Pain Numerical Rating Scale, Social Support Rating Scale, Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale, Pain Self-Efficacy Scale and general information questionnaire were used to assess pain catastrophizing (PC), pain intensity, social support, anxiety, depressive symptoms, pain self-efficacy and sociodemographic information of the participants, respectively. Data were collected in two stages: latent period of the first stage of labor (T1) and the second stage of labor (T2). According to the distribution and type of the data, the mean and standard deviation (SD), median and quartiles, frequency and percentages were used for statistical description. The generalized estimating equation (GEE) was used to analyze the associated factors of pain catastrophizing. Crossed-lagged regression was used to explore the relationship between pain intensity and pain catastrophizing.ResultsThe median and quartiles of PCS scores at T1 and T2 were 30.00 (21.00, 39.00) and 27.00 (17.00, 40.00) among the participants, respectively. The prevalence of pain catastrophizing at T1 and T2 was 29.08% and 29.43%, respectively. Chi-square test showed that the prevalence of pain catastrophizing at T2 was higher than that at T1 (χ2 = 55.387, p<0.001). GEE showed that greater gestational week, greater pain intensity at T1 and T2, and depressive symptoms were potential risk factors of pain catastrophizing (p<0.05), and a higher level of pain self-efficacy was a protective factor of pain catastrophizing among the participants [OR = 0.967, p<0.001]. Compared accompanied by husband, maternities accompanied by others during childbirth were more likely to be pain catastrophic [OR = 0.282, p = 0.010]. The results of cross-lagged analysis showed maternal pain intensity was positively correlated with pain catastrophizing at both T1 (r = 0.357, P < 0.001) and T2(r = 0.603, p < 0.001). There was no correlation between pain catastriphizing at T1 and pain intensity at T2, or pain intensity at T1 and pain catastriphizing at T2. As shown in Fig. 2.ConclusionsThere is a high prevalence of pain catastrophizing in women with natural childbirth. Associated factors of pain catastrophizing among women with natural childbirth are multidimensional, including physiological (pain intensity), psychological (gestational age, anxiety, depressive symptoms, pain self-efficacy) and socio-demographic factors (accompanying family members during delivery), suggesting that early evaluation of mental health condition of pregant women should be implemented and targeted interventions should be taken even if during pregnancy by health care workers. During labour, close attention should be paid to the mother’s recognition of pain and appropriate measures taken promptly to prevent the occurrence of pain catastrophizing.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09478-3.

Introduction

Childbirth is an event had to be experienced by the majority of women. Average annual birth rate was 16.2 million from 2012 to 2021 in China [1], with approximately vaginal birth rate of 65.1% to 66.0% [2]. However, it is also an event associated with intense pain, with many women in previous study described it as the most severe pain they had experienced [3]. Without well managed, it would have a series of direct and indirect, physiological and psychological effects on the mother and fetus, including potential fetal distress, neonatal hypoxic ischemic encephalopathy and neonatal asphyxia, as well as potential maternal elevated blood pressure, postpartum depression, and posttraumatic stress disorder [4]. Previous study showed that childbirth pain of some women (22%) may become chronic, affecting quality of life after delivery [5]. Intraspinal anesthesia analgesia is currently recognized as the most effective method to relieve labor pain. But in China, due to limited medical resources, subjective unwillingness or contraindications, some women still do not or cannot use it. Pain is a subjective experience produced by biological, psychological and social factors [6]. Psychological factors play a significant role in the process of pain occurrence and development, and is increasingly considered as an important regulatory factor and determinant of pain. Among many psychological factors associated with pain, pain catastrophizing has attracted increasing attention from scholars in recent years. It has been reported to be an important process factor in pain management [7], which provides a new perspective for clinical medical workers to manage labor pain.

Pain catastrophizing (PC) refers to an exaggerated and negative cognitive-emotional response of an individual to an anticipated or actual painful stimulus [8], manifested as pain-related worry and fear, exaggeration of pain intensity and an inability to divert attention from pain. It has been confirmed that pain catastrophizing not only affects the intensity of pain [7], but is also associated with the duration of the pain [9]. It was found to be a predictor of chronic postoperative pain [10]. Among various pain patients, such as those with acute back pain, fibromyalgia, and chronic musculoskeletal pain [11, 12], it was proved to be an important influential factor of disability. Furthermore, higher levels of catastrophizing were found to be a risk factor for negative emotional such as depression [13]. Studies in maternity showed that pain catastrophizing not only affects the intensity of pain [14], but is also associated with the duration of the pain [15]. It was found to be a predictor of preferential selection for cesarean Sect [16]., persistent perineal pain after delivery, and postpartum depression [17], positively correlated with fear of childbirth [18]. Therefore, understanding and managing maternal pain catastrophizing is conducive to promoting the management of labor pain, improving maternal vaginal delivery experience. Previous studies showed race, age and education levels were associated with pain catastrophizing [19]. Pain intensity, anxiety, and depressive symptoms were found to be positively associated with pain catastrophizing [20]. However, there is a lack of validation of these findings in the maternity- a particular group of women with intense, different property in different stages of labor. The aim of this study was to assess the level and incidence of pain catastrophizing among women undergoing natural childbirth, explore its associated factors and relationship with pain intensity, with the hypothesis that pain and catastrophizing would have negative and cyclical effects on each other.

Methods

Study population and sampling

This is a cross-sectional study with two-times data collections, which was conducted at the Delivery Room Unit of the largest women’s and children’s hospital in China, that is located in Chengdu, Sichuan Province. Many women from Sichuan and other provinces come to this hospital to give birth, whose annual delivery accounts for approximately 10% of the total annual deliveries in Chengdu. Convenience sampling was adopted to enroll pregnant women who were transferred to Delivery Room Unit to await vaginal birth, the inclusion criteria were: (1) ≥ 18 years old; (2) be able to complete the study independently; (3) gestational age ≥ 37 weeks; (3) voluntary participation in research; and (4) willingness to give birth by vaginal and at the latent period of the first stage of labor. The exclusion criteria were: (1) stillbirth or fetal deformities; (2) severe systemic diseases. (3) personal history of psychiatric problems (reported by oneself). (4) converted to cesarean section during labor, and (5) with data missing more than 20%.

Measurement tools

Self-compiled form

A self-compiled form was used to collect sociodemographic information of participants, including age, occupation, residential address, religion belief, level of education, paternity, gestational age, gravidity, parity, history of visiting childbirth clinic during pregnancy, history of Doula or epidural analgesia used during labor.

Numerical Rating Scale (NRS)

The NRS is a unidimensional measure of pain intensity in adults [21]. Subjects were instructed to choose an integer (0 − 10) that best reflects the intensity of their pain, with 0 representing one pain extreme (e.g., “no pain”) and 10 representing the other pain extreme (e.g.,“pain as bad as you can imagine” and “worst pain imaginable”) [22]. The higher the number, the worse the pain.

Pain Catastrophizing Scale (PCS)

PCS was compiled by Sullivan et al. in 1995 [8], and was widely used to assess pain catastrophizing in a variety of pain patient. PCS consisted of three dimensions (rumination, exaggeration, helplessness) and 13 items, which were rated on a 5-point scale with end points of (0) ‘not at all’ and [4] ‘all the time’. The total score of the scale ranged from 0 to 52, recommended by Chinese version of questionnaire and used cut-off in this study is 38, with higher scores indicating a higher level of catastrophizing. The Cronbach’s α coefficients of PCS and each dimension were 0.93, 0.84, 0.77 and 0.81, respectively, and were 0.97, 0.94, 0.80 and 0.95, respectively in the current study.

Social Support Rating Scale (SSRS)

SSRS was developed by Xiao et al. [23], consisting 10 items, with total scores ranging from 12 to 66, and higher total scores associated with higher levels of social support. SSRS had been widely used and had good reliability and validity, with a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.83. In this study, SSRS had a Cronbach’s α coefficient of 0.68.

Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS)

Developed by Zigmond and Snaith [24], HADS was used to evaluate anxiety and depressive symptoms of the participants in the past two weeks. It consists of 14 items, including anxiety subscale (HADS-A) and depression subscale (HADS-D). Each item was scored on a Likert 4 scale (0–3). The total score of HADS-A and HADS-D ranged from 0 to 21, and scored 8 or above were considered positive for anxiety (or depressive) symptoms. In this study, the Cronbach’s α coefficients for HADS-A and HADS-D were 0.69 and 0.72, respectively.

Pain Self-efficacy Scale (PSES)

Compiled by Nicholas [25], PSES includes 10 items, with each item scored on a Likert-7 scale (0–6: not at all confident - fully confident). The total score of PSES ranged from 0 to 60, with a higher score indicating a higher level of belief in pain management. PSES had a split-half reliability coefficient of 0.95. Its Cronbach’s α coefficient was 0.95 in the current study.

Data collection

Data were collected in two times with an onsite manner by uniformly trained researchers at Delivery Room Unit in February-September 2022, using online questionnaires. The first data collection took place when the pregnant women were transferred to Delivery Room Unit to await vaginal birth, evaluating participants’ pain intensity and catastrophizing during the latent period of the first stage of labor (T1). The second data collection occurred within two hours of delivery, assessing their social support, anxiety and depressive symptoms, and pain self-efficacy, retrospectively assessing participants’ pain intensity and catastrophizing perceptions during the second stage of labor (T2) as well. Sociodemographic information was collected on the Hospital Information System. Uniform instruction was provided after each distribution of questionnaire, and a necessary explanation was offered when any doubt was aroused. All participants filled out the questionnaire independently and submitted immediately. It took no more than 20 min for each participant to complete each questionnaire. All data is collected and managed in Chinese.

Ethical consideration

Our study was conducted according to the Helsinki Declaration standards. The study was approved by the ethics committee of West China Second University Hospital of Sichuan University, with the registration number of No. 2022 (013). Informed written consent was obtained before recruitment. Participants’ names were replaced by numbers for confidentiality.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 26.0 software was used to analyze data. Categorical variables were reported as numbers and percentages and quantitative variables were recorded as mean and standard deviation (SD), or median (M), 25th percentile(P25) and 75th percentile(P75). The Generalized Estimating Equation (GEE) were performed to explore the associated factors of pain catastrophizing among the participants. When performed Generalized Estimating Equation, data was reassembled based on measurement time firstly. Occurrence of pain catastrophizing (both T1 and T2) was inputed as dependent variable, the other variables as independent variables, and the time of measurement as intracellular effects, categorical variables were converted to dummy variables before included into GEE models. Crossed-lagged regression was used to explore the relationship between pain intensity and pain catastrophizing.All tests were two-sided, and a p-value of ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Relationship between pain intensity and pain catastrophizing

The results of cross-lagged regression analysis showed that: Maternal pain intensity was positively correlated with pain catastrophizing at both T1(r = 0.357, P < 0.001) and T2(r = 0.603, p < 0.001). Pain catastrophizing at T1 was positively associated with pain catastrophizing at T2 (β = 0.628, p < 0.001). The results of cross-lagged analysis showed maternal pain intensity was positively correlated with pain catastrophizing at both T1(r = 0.357, P < 0.001) and T2(r = 0.603, p < 0.001). There was no correlation between pain catastriphizing at T1 and pain intensity at T2, or pain intensity at T1 and pain catastriphizing at T2. As shown in Fig. 2.

Discussion

The current cross-sectional study assessed the incidence of pain catastrophizing and its associated factors and relationship with pain intensity among women with natural childbirth. We results showed there is a high prevalence of pain catastrophizing in women with natural childbirth. Associated factors of pain catastrophizing among women with natural childbirth including accompanying family members during delivery, depressive symptoms and pain self-efficacy. We also found that maternal pain intensity was positively correlated with pain catastrophizing at both T1 and T2. There was no correlation between pain catastriphizing at T1 and pain intensity at T2, or pain intensity at T1 and pain catastriphizing at T2.

Our finding showed the median of pain catastrophizing score of our participants was 30.00 during the latent period of the first stage of labor, which was higher than the findings reported by Ferber et al., with a mean score of 25.50 [26] in Israeli women. Firstly, there is a difference in the timing of the assessment: Ferber et al. assessed before epidural analgesia was administered, whereas this study just assessed the latency of the first stages of labor. It may not yet be time for epidural analgesia, or epidural analgesia may have been administered, or the paiticipates may have been consistently experienced with severe pain but has not and will not be administered epidural analgesia. And epidural analgesia is a key factor in pain management. Secondly, our study was conducted among Chinese, whereas Ferber’s was in Israeli. It has been reported that perceptions and attitudes to pain vary across geographic and ethnic groups [27].The incidence of pain catastrophizing during the latent period of the first stage of labor in our participants was 22.34%, which was lower than the incidence reported by Sim et al. (43.6%) and Zeng et al. (42.5%) in the Singaporean [28]. In addition to geographical and ethnic difference, the use of same assessment tools but different cutoff may also explain the differences. PCS thresholds vary by race, culture and language [26]. Accordingly, our study used the recommended Chinese version of PCS with a cutoff value of 38. Sim and Zeng, based on their research context, used 25 as the PCS cutoff value. Furthermore, our study assessed pain catastrophizing during the second stage of labor. The median of PCS was 27.00, with an incidence of of 29.43%, which extended the evidence currently available. Despite various methods were offered to help relief labor pain, including analgesia and non-pharmacological approaches (such as continuous labor support, breathing techniques, position changes, massage, gentle touch, birthing ball, and music), the participants showed a high level and incidence of pain catastrophizing. This suggested when nursing maternities with natural delivery, attention should not to be paid only on pain stimulation itself, but also other factors affecting the development and psychological cognition of pain.

We found maternities accompanied by others during childbirth were more likely to generate pain catastrophizing than those by accompanied their husbands. Studies have shown that support of spouse or partner has a special significance for women [29], whether actual or perceived, may help them escape negative feelings and thoughts, and encourage them to adopt positive coping strategies. Our finding contradicts the results of Nicholas et al. [30], whose research indicated a higher level of pain catastrophizing among participants living with a spouse or partner than those living with others. This discrepancy could be explained by the Catastrophizing Communal Coping Model proposed by Sullivan [31]. According to the model, catastrophizing is a relational coping strategy, with the purpose of seeking support and assistance from intimate individuals (spouse, partner, or family member) through communication. However, in the context of the current study, the delivery room is a relatively sterile environment, spouses can only wait outside the delivery room during childbirth. Therefore, it is difficult for mothers to generate catastrophizing behavior in order to obtain support and assistance from their spouses.

Our findings also showed that depressive symptoms were a risk factor of pain catastrophizing in labor, which was supported by previous research. There are several possible reasons. Firstly, negative emotional processes underwent by individual with depressive symptoms, such as feelings of helplessness and pessimism, prevent them from using potentially effective coping strategies such as distraction [32]. Secondly, reduced sleep quality in depressed patients makes them of hyperalgesia and perceive more severe pain [33]. Thirdly, when facing pain, individuals with depressive symptoms may have bias and information processing deviations. They tend to exclude pleasant experiences, focus on the negative effect of pain, and underestimate their coping abilities [32], and so may contribute to catastrophizing thoughts. On the other hand, catastrophizing thoughts may in turn cause mood alterations. Individuals with higher levels of catastrophizing may show heightened responses to actual or perceived pain, resulting in increasing emotions of fear, anxiety, and sadness [13]. leading to a vicious cycle of physical pain and pain catastrophizing and negative emotions. Lastly, the potential overlap gene-catechol-O-methyltransferase -may explain the high probability comorbidity of pain, depression, and catastrophizing [34]. This highlights the importance of early screening for depressive symptoms in pregnant women to prevent the occurrence of pain catastrophizing cognitive responses during labor.

Self-efficacy refers to an individual’s cognitive assessment of self-behavioral abilities [35]. When utilizing coping strategies to manage pain, individuals with a higher level of self-efficacy are more likely to actually execute. Prior evidence has indicated that self-efficacy can moderate the effectiveness of various psychological coping strategies [36]. The findings of this study demonstrated that women with higher levels of pain self-efficacy were less likely to experience pain catastrophizing during labor, which was consistent with previous research [37]. This may because their expectations and beliefs about their behavioral abilities influence their actions and behaviors, determining whether they will respond to events, how much effort they will put in, and how long they will experience negative events [35]. This suggested that health workers should enhance pregnant women’s positive cognition of self-behavior ability in prenatal education, strengthen their self-efficacy through practice, help them improve their coping skills when facing labor pain, actively adopt positive coping strategies such as distraction, and exert the effectiveness of strategies, so as to reduce the occurrence of pain catastrophizing and pain-related adverse outcomes.

The results the current study showed that: Maternal pain intensity was positively correlated with pain catastrophizing at both T1 and T2. This result can be explained by the Fear-Avoidance Model of Pain, which posits that individuals who tend to interpret pain stimuli as threatening are more likely to experience tension, vigilance, fear, avoidance of pain, and catastrophizing cognition [38]. Therefore, maternities experience severer pain were more likely to perceive pain as threatening, focus their attention on it, overestimate its intensity, underestimate their coping abilities, and more prone to generate catastrophizing cognition [39]. Metacognition also plays an important role in the process of generating pain catastrophizing. Metacognition refers to an individual’s cognition of his own thinking process [40]. It was found that patients ' metacognition of pain (positive or negative) increases rumination or worry about pain, may leading to the formation of catastrophic cognition [41]. Our results are consistent with the findings of Roman et al. [42], suggesting the importance of timely management of labor pain, for excessive pain stimuli can trigger the occurrence of pain catastrophizing, which in turn amplifies anticipated pain, generates fear and avoidance of pain, leading to a vicious cycle of pain and pain catastrophizing [43].Our study showed there was no correlation between pain intensity at T1 and pain catastriphizing at T2. This suggested the correlation between pain intensity and pain catastrophizing should be understood by stages of labor. There may be a vicious circle between pain and catastrophic cognition both within T1 and T2. But at T2, the mother experienced new pain, may covering pain at T1.

There are also some limitations in our study. Firstly, in the second stage of labor, maternities need to cooperate with the midwife to use abdominal pressure during uterine contractions, and rest fully when it subside. Therefore, the pain intensity and catastrophizing in the second stage of delivery were assessed after delivery, which affected the validity of our findings. Secondly, we used the value of 38 recommended in the Chinese version of PCS as the cut-off. However, the Chinese version of the questionnaire was not developed in maternal population. Future studies may explore the most appropriate cut-off values for the Chinese version of PCS for maternities. Thirdly, the study period was too short (7 months) and hence the data representativeness may be weakened. Lastly, the strength of the argument for the results of the study was weakened by the design of the cross-sectional study.

Conclusion

There is a high prevalence of pain catastrophizing in women with natural childbirth. In clinical practice, health care workers should not only pay attention to the pain stimulus itself, but also evaluate the psychological cognitive response to pain of maternity, help them reduce the catastrophic cognitive response to pain and enhance their adaptive coping skills to pain. Associated factors of pain catastrophizing among women with natural childbirth are multidimensional, including physiological (pain intensity), psychological (anxiety, depressive symptoms, pain self-efficacy) and socio-demographic factors (gestational age, accompanying family members during delivery). Among these factors, physiological and psychological factors can be intervened by medical personnel, suggesting that early evaluation of mental health condition of pregnant women should be implemented during pregnancy by health care workers and targeted interventions should also be taken. In addition, the pain of maternity during delivery should also be paid close attention to and managed in time to avoid the occurrence of pain catastrophizing.
