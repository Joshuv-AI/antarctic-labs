RFQ-Structure Power Requirements: Analysis of Two Structure Designs

Executive Summary

This report compares two Radio Frequency Quadrupole (RFQ) accelerator designs:
  • The first design assumes 100 keV input and 3.0 MeV output energies.
  • The new design assumes 65 keV input and 2.1 MeV output energies.

Specifically, the RFQ length and RF-power requirements are analyzed. The results are summarized here:
                                            100 keV/3.0 MeV          65 keV/2.1 MeV
                              length             5.40 m                   3.55 m
 ohmic power 0.74 MW 0.31 MW
 total power 0.98 MW 0.43 MW
In this table, total power adds beam loading for 16 mA and 25% additional headroom.

In addition, simulations indicate the RF fill time to be ~11 µsec.

Comparing Length and Ohmic Losses in Two RFQ Designs

By reducing the input and output RFQ energies, the structure length decreases. For LAMP, this alteration
allowed the structure to be reduced from 5.40 m to 3.55 m, or –34%. It is not surprising that the ohmic
losses would be reduced by the same fraction, and thus we expect a –34% reduction in (ohmic) power.

However, the geometry of the vanes must change as well. In short, the RFQ vanes create a transverse
electric field 𝐸! (for transverse focusing) and longitudinal electric field 𝐸∥ (to bunch and accelerate). The
ratio 𝐸! ⁄𝐸∥ is important for beam stability, and a lower-energy input reduces the vane wavelength, altering
this ratio. Reducing the aperture restores this important ratio.

A reduced aperture means that a lower voltage is necessary to generate the same field, which reduces the
required RF power. Therefore, reducing the input beam energy has an additional impact on RF power
requirements.

The simulation code Parmteq calculates both aperture radius and vane voltage directly given the input beam
energy (and a maximal modulation ratio, which was set to 2 in both scenarios). These are shown in the
following table:
                                             100 keV/3.0 MeV         65 keV/2.1 MeV
 aperture radius 8.6 mm 6.8 mm
 vane voltage 172 kV 122 kV
 shunt impedance 215 kΩ⋅m 170 kΩ⋅m

For an RFQ, the shunt impedance relates the amount of power needed per unit length to maintain a required
vane voltage. This definition enables a shunt impedance calculation independent of RFQ length.

In summary: A lower input energy requires a smaller aperture, which means less power is required to
provide the appropriate electric fields. This reduction in RF-power requirements is beyond the reduction
saved by decreasing RFQ length. The ohmic losses shown in the Executive Summary accounts for both of
these factors.

Total Power Calculation
Beam loading is the additional RF power consumed to accelerate the beam; this is a relatively easy evalua-
tion: Accelerating particles scales with current and applied voltage. The Lujan beam, nominally 16 mA,
provides the largest beam loading: 46 kW to 3.0 MeV and 33 kW to 2.1 MeV.

WNR+IPF beam loading is somewhat less than Lujan, but tuning and other activities will generate currents
ranging from near zero to the full 16 mA. Looking at larger currents is also informative: Parmteq reports
a 'maximum beam current' based on the geometry. For the 2.1-MeV design, Parmteq reports 171 mA.
Experts suggest dividing this by two (85 mA, which equates to 604 kW including all power and 25% head-
room), though SNS runs at higher than their equivalent halved value.

Experimentally, RFQs typically exhibit lower Q than simulations predict, requiring slightly more RF power.
In addition, adding nonzero headroom is a necessity to reduce risk. The Executive-Summary table sums
the ohmic power and beam loading, then applies an additional 25% safety margin.
