On the Cancellation of Nuclear Effects in the Valence Region
S. A. Kulagin, R. Petti

Abstract
Deep-inelastic e / μ e/\mu scattering data off targets ranging from deuterium to lead indicate that the nuclear modifications to the structure functions of bound nucleons are minimal in the kinematic region around the peak of the valence quark distributions. An analysis of world measurements of the isoscalar cross-section ratios σ A / σ H 2 \sigma^{A}/\sigma^{{}^{2}\text{H}} in the region of 0.25 ≤ x ≤ 0.35 0.25\leq x\leq 0.35 shows a remarkable cancellation across all nuclei, with an average value of 0.9985 ± 0.0022 0.9985\pm 0.0022 . We discuss these results and possible interpretations in the context of a microscopic model of nuclear modifications of the structure functions.

Introduction

II
Analysis of data

Experimental measurements of nuclear effects in DIS are usually provided as ratios of the cross-section off a nucleus with mass number
A
to that off deuterium,
\sigma^{A}/\sigma^{{}^{2}\text{H}}
, normalized to the corresponding nucleon number. It is usually assumed that the cross-section ratio equals the ratio of structure functions
R_{2}^{A}=F_{2}^{A}/F_{2}^{{}^{2}\text{H}}
based on the experimental observation of a similar behavior for the ratio
R=F_{L}/F_{T}
of longitudinal to transverse SF
18
.
For isoscalar nuclei with equal number of protons
Z
and neutrons
N
,
R_{2}^{A}=1
if nuclear corrections are neglected. For this reason, deviations of
R_{2}^{A}
from unity are interpreted as nuclear modifications of the bound nucleon SFs. For a generic nucleus with
Z\neq N
,
F_{2}^{A}
and
R_{2}^{A}
are usually corrected for the non-isoscalarity as follows:

\displaystyle F_{2}^{A(\text{is})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{2}^{A}\frac{AF_{2}^{N}}{ZF_{2}^{p}+NF_{2}^{n}},

(1)

\displaystyle R_{2}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{2}^{A(\text{is})}/F_{2}^{{}^{2}\text{H}},

(2)

where
F_{2}^{p}
and
F_{2}^{n}
are the SFs of the free proton and neutron, respectively, and
F_{2}^{N}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{2}^{p}+F_{2}^{n})
.
In the following discussion we will also consider the ratio

\displaystyle R_{2}^{A(\text{is})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{AF_{2}^{A}}{ZF_{2}^{p}+NF_{2}^{n}}=\frac{F_{2}^{A(\text{is})}}{F_{2}^{N}}.

(3)

The ratio
R_{2}^{A(\text{is})}
offers a convenient way to quantify the strength of nuclear effects in a generic nucleus, albeit it is not experimentally measurable.

III
Results and discussion

The analysis of Sec.
II
indicates that DIS data demonstrate a remarkable cancellation of nuclear effects averaged over a region
0.25\leq x\leq 0.35
.
This observation holds for all nuclear targets and spans a wide region of
Q^{2}
from
Q^{2}\sim 2\>\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
for JLab measurements to
Q^{2}\sim 60\>\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
for CERN experiments.
To understand such a behavior, for each data point listed in
Table
1
we calculated the corresponding prediction for the microscopic model of Refs.
25
;
26
;
27
, which includes the smearing with the energy-momentum distribution of bound nucleons (FMB), the off-shell (OS) correction to the bound nucleon structure functions,
the contribution from meson exchange currents (MEC) and the effects of the propagation of the hadronic component of the
virtual intermediate boson in the nuclear environment (NS).
We then applied the same weighted average in Eq.(
7
) to the predictions as done for the experimental data.
Figure
2
shows the ratios between the averaged
F_{2}^{A\text{(is)}}/F_{2}^{{}^{2}\text{H}}
data (Table
1
) and the corresponding predictions for various nuclei. Overall, we observe an excellent agreement between the model and data. A fit to all nuclei in Fig.
2
with a constant function yields a value
1.0004\pm 0.0022
. Consistently with the discussion in Sec.
II
, the MARATHON data
4
are not included in Fig.
2
. The corresponding ratios between the weighted averages of MARATHON data and the predictions are found to be
0.9996\pm 0.0073
for
3
H/
2
H and
0.9983\pm 0.0073
for
3
He/
2
H.

To investigate the underlying reason for vanishing nuclear correction around the peak of the valence quark distributions, we calculated the ratios
R_{2}^{A\text{(is)}}(x,Q^{2})
for various nuclei in the region
0.25<x<0.35
following the approach developed in Refs.
25
;
26
;
27
.
Figure
3
(left panel) shows the results for the
2
H,
3
H,
4
He,
12
C,
40
Ca and
197
Au nuclei as a function of
x
at
Q^{2}=5\>\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
. Notably, all curves tend to cross around a common point at about
R_{2}^{A\text{(is)}}=1.002
. This value moves even closer to unity for the experimental observable
R_{2}^{A}=R_{2}^{A\text{(is)}}/R_{2}^{{}^{2}\text{H(is)}}
, obtained from the ratio with respect to the
2
H curve. Furthermore, the
x
-dependence appears to be approximately linear in the
x
region considered, resulting in an enhanced cancellation of nuclear effects, once averaged over the range
0.25\leq x\leq 0.35
. The cross over point
x_{0}
at which
R_{2}^{A\text{(is)}}=1
, while not identical for all nuclei, spans a rather narrow region around the value
x_{0}=0.3
.

It is instructive to evaluate the individual nuclear corrections to
R_{2}^{A(\text{is})}
from different mechanisms
25
;
26
;
27
. Figure
3
(right panel) shows the results for the
40
Ca nucleus. In the region of interest
0.25\leq x\leq 0.35
, the nuclear DIS is dominated by the incoherent scattering off bound nucleons. The leading nuclear effects include the smearing of the proton and neutron SFs with the nuclear energy-momentum distribution (nuclear spectral function), and the off-shell correction to the nucleon SFs. Note that the nuclear spectral function used
25
accounts for both the mean-field component and the high-momentum part owed to short-range nucleon-nucleon correlations. We observe a major cancellation between the nuclear smearing in the convolution model and the off-shell correction in the vicinity of
x=0.3
. For this reason, the sub-leading contribution from the nuclear meson exchange currents also play a role in the determination of the cross-over point
x_{0}
, as shown in Fig.
3
(right panel). Remarkably, the nuclear dependence of the MEC contribution turns out to be similar to that of the nuclear smearing and of the off-shell contribution. This behavior can be understood as the MEC contribution is related to the nuclear binding by sum rules
25
;
30
. As a result, the
A
dependence of the cross over point
x_{0}
is rather weak, as illustrated in Fig.
4
(left panel, solid symbols). For comparison, we also show the cross over point
R_{2}^{A(\text{is})}/R_{2}^{{}^{2}\text{H(is)}}=1
for the nuclear ratios with respect to deuterium, which are experimentally measurable (open symbols). The
Q^{2}
dependence of
x_{0}
is illustrated in the right panel of Fig.
4
, for
40

In order to further discuss the interplay between the nuclear smearing and off-shell corrections in the region under discussion, we consider an approximation for the nuclear convolution
30
;
28
. The typical bound nucleon energy
\varepsilon
and momentum
p
are small compared to its mass
M
, and for this reason the nucleon SF
F_{2}^{N}
in the nuclear convolution can be expanded in power series in
\varepsilon/M
and
\bm{p}/M
. Smearing the resulting expansion with the nuclear spectral function gives (for simplicity we consider isoscalar nuclei):

R_{2}^{A(\text{is})}\approx 1-e_{A}x\partial_{x}\ln F_{2}^{N}(x,Q^{2})+v_{A}\delta f(x),

(9)

where
e_{A}=\langle\varepsilon\rangle/M
is the average bound nucleon energy and
v_{A}=\langle p^{2}\rangle/M^{2}-1
is the average nucleon virtuality in a nucleus, with the nucleon four-momentum
p=(M+\varepsilon,\bm{p})
.
1
1

1

Note that
\varepsilon
includes the nucleon separation energy
E>0
and the recoil energy of the rest nucleus of
(A{-}1)
nucleons,
\varepsilon=-E-\bm{p}^{2}/(2M_{A-1})
.

We have
v_{A}=2(\langle\varepsilon\rangle-\langle T\rangle)/M
in terms of the average kinetic energy
\langle T\rangle=\langle\bm{p}^{2}\rangle/(2M)
.
The averaging here is performed with the nuclear spectral function, as detailed in Ref.
25
.
Note that in Eq.(
9
) we neglect the term with the second derivative,
\langle T\rangle x^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}F_{2}
, since
\partial_{x}^{2}F_{2}\approx 0
in the region under consideration.
The function
\delta f=M^{2}\partial_{p^{2}}\ln F_{2}^{N}(x,Q^{2},p^{2})
, where the derivative is taken over the invariant mass squared
p^{2}
of the off-shell nucleon in the vicinity of the mass shell
p^{2}=M^{2}

25
;
26
.
This function describes the relative modification of the nucleon SFs in the off-shell region and, by definition, is a universal function independent of the nucleus considered. The off-shell correction in nuclear DIS has been the subject of detailed phenomenological studies
25
;
28
;
26
;
27
;
8
;
9
;
10
.
A
Q^{2}
-independent approximation
\delta f=\delta f(x)
was found to provide an excellent description of the nuclear DIS data
25
;
26
;
8
;
4
, the lepton pair production (DY) data
27
, and
W^{\pm},Z
production in
p+{\rm Pb}
collisions at the LHC
32
. A recent global QCD analysis
10
resulted in a proton-neutron asymmetry
\delta f^{p}-\delta f^{n}
consistent with zero, within uncertainties. Based on these observations, we use a single off-shell function
\delta f(x)
for both protons and neutrons in the present analysis, as determined from a global study of nuclear SFs
25
.

The nuclear binding and off-shell corrections cancel out if

x\partial_{x}\ln F_{2}^{N}(x,Q^{2})=a\delta f(x),

(10)

where
a=v_{A}/e_{A}
. If
a
were
A
-independent, we would have a universal solution
x_{0}
for all nuclei. This condition is satisfied if
e_{A}
and
v_{A}
have the same
A
dependence, canceling out in the ratio
a
. In this context, we note that for a nucleus described by a Hamiltonian with a two-body interaction potential
\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle
and
\left\langle T\right\rangle
are related by the Koltun sum rule
24
:

\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle+\left\langle T\right\rangle=2\varepsilon_{0},

(11)

where
\varepsilon_{0}<0
is the nuclear ground state energy per nucleon. Using Eq.(
11
) we have

a=4\left(1-\varepsilon_{0}/\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle\right).

(12)

The ratio
\varepsilon_{0}/\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle
is small for all nuclei, and for this reason the value of
a
is close to 4 with a weak
A
dependence. Indeed, for the deuteron
\varepsilon_{0}=-1.11\>\mathrm{MeV}
, and, using the AV18 nucleon-nucleon potential
37
;
35
, we have
\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle=-11.9\>\mathrm{MeV}
and
a=3.63
. For complex nuclei we use a model spectral function
29
;
25
to compute
\left\langle\varepsilon\right\rangle
and find that
a
spans the range from about
3.5
to about
3.2
for nuclei from
3
H (
3
He) to
208
Pb. Solving the Eq.(
10
) numerically at
Q^{2}=5\>\mathrm{GeV}^{2}
, we find
0.26<x_{0}<0.31
for
2
H,
3
H,
4
He,
12
C,
40
Ca, and
197
Au targets, with the highest value of
x_{0}
for deuterium and the lowest
x_{0}
for gold. The MEC correction significantly increases the value of
x_{0}
for heavy targets (for illustration, see the right panel of Fig.
3
for
40
Ca) and gives a negligible contribution for the deuteron. Therefore, the MEC correction reduces the
x_{0}
spread across different nuclei, resulting in the picture shown in the left panel of Fig.
3
.
The quantity
x_{0}
evolves towards lower values as
Q^{2}
increases because of the
Q^{2}
dependence of the nucleon
F_{2}
. Overall, these observations are consistent with the results shown in Fig.
3
and
4
, which were obtained with the full model calculations.
