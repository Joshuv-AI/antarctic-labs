Homogenization of Three Species Reaction Diffusion Equation in Perforated Domains
Saumyajit Das, Kshitij Sinha

Abstract
In this article we study the asymptotic behaviour of the solution of the three species chemical reaction-diffusion model with non-homogeneous Neumann boundary condition in a perforated domain. We investigate how the mass inflow at the microscale affects the three-species reaction–diffusion system at the macroscale using two-scale convergence. As the size of the perforations vanishes, the microscale effects are captured by a global source term in the homogenized equation, which remains a three-species reaction-diffusion system but with modified diffusion coefficients.

Two-Scale Convergence

In this section, we gather some useful results on two-scale convergence (see

Definition D.1

(see
[
2
]
)
.

A sequence of functions
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{2}((0,T);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega))
two-scale converges to
\displaystyle v_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}((0,T);\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega\times Y))
if

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\phi\left(t,x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)dt\,dx=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}v_{0}(t,x,y)\,\phi(t,x,y)\,dt\,dx\,dy

for all
\displaystyle\phi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};C^{\infty}_{\#}(Y))
.

We recall the following classical Proposition:

Proposition D.2
.

If
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
is a bounded sequence in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
, then there exists a function
\displaystyle v_{0}:=v_{0}(t,x,y)
in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y)
such that, up to a subsequence,
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
two-scale converges to
\displaystyle v_{0}
.

Then, the following Proposition is useful for obtaining the limit of the product of two two-scale convergent sequences.

Proposition D.3
.

Let
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of functions in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
which two-scale converges to a limit
\displaystyle v_{0}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y)
. Suppose furthermore that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,dt\,dx=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}|v_{0}(t,x,y)|^{2}\,dt\,dx\,dy.

Then, for any sequence
\displaystyle w^{\varepsilon}
in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
that two-scale converges to a limit
\displaystyle w_{0}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega\times Y)
, we get the limit

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,w^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\,\phi\left(t,x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)dt\,dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}v_{0}(t,x,y)\,w_{0}(t,x,y)\,\phi(t,x,y)\,dt\,dx\,dy,

for all
\displaystyle\phi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};C^{\infty}_{\#}(Y))
.

The next Propositions yield a characterization of the two-scale limits of gradients of bounded sequences
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
. This result is crucial for applications to homogenization problems.

Proposition D.4
.

Let
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
be a bounded sequence in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T);\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))
that converges weakly to a limit
\displaystyle v:=v(t,x)
in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T);\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))
. Then,
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
also two-scale converges to
\displaystyle v
, and there exists a function
\displaystyle v_{1}:=v_{1}(t,x,y)
in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\#}(Y)/\mathbb{R})
such that, up to extraction of a subsequence,
\displaystyle\nabla v^{\varepsilon}
two-scale converges to
\displaystyle\nabla_{x}v(t,x)+\nabla_{y}v_{1}(t,x,y)
.

The main result of two-scale convergence can be generalized to the case of sequences defined in
\displaystyle L^{2}([0,T]\times\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
.

Proposition D.5

(see
[
2
,
3
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}
be a sequence in
\displaystyle L^{2}((0,T)\times\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
such that

\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}|v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,dt\,d\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq C

(D.1)

where
\displaystyle C
is a positive constant, independent of
\displaystyle\varepsilon
. There exist a subsequence (still denoted by
\displaystyle\varepsilon
) and a two-scale limit
\displaystyle v_{0}(t,x,y)\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;L^{2}(\Gamma))
such that
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)
two-scale converges to
\displaystyle v_{0}(t,x,y)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}v^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,\phi\left(t,x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)dt\,d\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Gamma}v_{0}(t,x,y)\,\phi(t,x,y)\,dt\,dx\,d\sigma(y)

(D.2)

for any function
\displaystyle\phi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega};C^{\infty}_{\#}(Y))
