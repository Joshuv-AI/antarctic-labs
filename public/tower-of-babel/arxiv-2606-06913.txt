Whittaker Category and finite W W -superalgebras for Cartan type Lie superalgebras
Priyanshu Chakraborty, Yuhui shen and Bin Shu

Abstract
. Let W ⁡ ( n ) W(n) be the finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebra of fundamental type in the Cartan type series of Kac’s classification result [ 6 ] over an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 0 . Let 𝐠 \mathbf{g} be the graded-zero part of W ⁡ ( n ) W(n) which is isomorphic to 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n ) \mathfrak{gl}(n) . In the first part of this paper, following the basic idea of taking the “minimal” parabolic subalgebra 𝖯 \mathsf{P} as a working platform in [ 4 ] we introduce the Whittaker category 𝒲 \mathscr{W} for representations of W ⁡ ( n ) W(n) associated with a nilpotent element e e in 𝐠 0 \mathbf{g}_{0} and with W ​ ( n ) − 1 W(n)_{-1} . This Whittaker category turns out to be close to the classical Whittaker category McDowell and Miličić-Soergel studied in [ 8 ] and [ 9 ] , respectively (or see [ 1 ] ). We finally classify the simple objects in 𝒲 \mathscr{W} . In the second part, we introduce the finite W W -algebra associated with e e , we then establish a generalized Skryabin’s equivalence between the representation category of the finite W W -superalgebra and the category 𝒲 ′ \mathscr{W}^{\prime} of so-called weakened Whittaker modules over W ⁡ ( n ) W(n) . Here 𝒲 ′ \mathscr{W}^{\prime} naturally contains 𝒲 \mathscr{W} as a full subcategory.

Introduction and preliminaries

0.1.

In
[
6
]
, Kac classified all finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
. His result shows that all finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
which are not Lie algebras, are classified into two classes: classical Lie superalgebras and Cartan type Lie superalgebras. The study on classical Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
increasingly draws attention. Lots of results make it in a great progress. Comparatively, the study on Cartan type Lie algebras are lack of attention.

0.1.1.

In
[
4
]
, Feifei Duan, Yufeng Yao and the last author of the present paper first obtained an interesting classifying result on parabolic subalgebras of graded Cartan type Lie superalgebras, then they were aware of the significance of “minimal” parabolic subalgebras in their representation categories. They initiated the study of parabolic Bernstein-Galfeld-Galfeld category
\mathcal{O}
for graded Cartan type Lie superalgebras over
\mathbb{C}
, associated with the minimal parabolic subalgebras. There the authors classified all blocks of the category
\mathcal{O}
and precisely described them, and finally obtained character formulas for indecomposable tilting modules and projective modules.

0.1.2.

In the present paper, following the basic idea of taking “minimal” parabolic subalgebras as a basic platform
[
4
]
we introduce our Whittaker category
\mathscr{W}
for the fundamental Cartan type Lie superalgebra
W(n)
. Such a Whittaker category turns out to be close to the classical Whittaker category McDowell and Miličić-Soergel studied in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
, respectively (or see
[
1
]
). In the first part of the present paper (§
1
-§
3
), we then describe the Whittaker category
\mathscr{W}
, furthermore, we introduce a generalized Backelin functor connecting the parabolic BGG category
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathscr{W}
.
However, when we tried to introduce the corresponding finite
W
-superalgebra and establish generalized Skryabin’s equivalence, we found that the above aim could be only partially gained. As a result, in the second part (§
4
), giving up to make use of the minimal parabolic subalgebra
\mathsf{P}
, we instead consider the so-called weakened Whittaker modules only associated with a nilpotent element
e\in\mathbf{g}
as introduced in the abstract (see Remark
1.3
for further explanation). The category of the weakened Whittaker modules is bigger than
\mathscr{W}
, which is denoted by
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
. Then we consider the finite
W
-algebra corresponding to
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
. The main results are listed below.

(1)

Classify all simple objects for
\mathscr{W}
, up to isomorphisms (see Proposition
1.4
, Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
).

(2)

The objects of this category generally does not have finite-length composition series unless
n=2
.

(3)

Establish a generalized Skryabin’s equivalence between
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
and the module category of the finite
W
-algebra corresponding to
\mathscr{W}^{\prime}
(see Theorem
4.2
).

0.2.

Throughout the paper, we assume that
n
is a positive integer and
\mathcal{R}:=\bigwedge_{\mathbb{F}}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
is the polynomial superalgebra with indeterminants
y_{s},s=1,\ldots,n
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
. The degree conventions
\deg y_{j}=1
naturally determine a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
\mathcal{R}
. Hence
\mathcal{R}
is endowed with
\mathbb{Z}
-graded structure, this
\mathbb{Z}
-graded structure gives rise to the super space structure of
\mathcal{R}
. This is to say,

\mathcal{R}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\mathcal{R}_{i}

with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{F}
,
\mathcal{R}_{i}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{y_{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{k_{i}}\mid 1\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{i}\leq n\}
,
i=1,\ldots,N:=2^{n}-1
, and

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathcal{R}_{\bar{1}}

for

\mathcal{R}_{\bar{0}}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=\text{even}}\mathcal{R}_{i}
,
\mathcal{R}_{\bar{1}}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=\text{odd}}\mathcal{R}_{i}
. In the subsequence, the above polynomial
y_{k_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{k_{i}}
in
\mathcal{R}
is simply written as
y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{i}}
.

Denote by
W(n)
the space of super derivations of
\mathcal{R}
which is required to be a free
\mathcal{R}
-module of rank
n
with basis
D_{i}={\partial\over{\partial y_{i}}}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. By convention, the degree of
D_{i}
is equal to
1
.

Naturally,
W(n)
is endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded structure with gradation

\displaystyle W(n)_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{R}_{i+1}D_{k}.

Moreover,
W(n)
is also a superspace endowed with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading compatible its
\mathbb{Z}
-grading. This is to say,

\displaystyle W(n)_{\bar{0}}=\sum_{i=\text{even}}W(n)_{i},

\displaystyle W(n)_{\bar{1}}=\sum_{i=\text{odd}}W(n)_{i}.

By convention,
\deg f
denotes
i
if
f
lies in
\mathcal{R}_{i}
. Then
W(n)
becomes a Lie superalgebra over
\mathbb{F}
with Lie bracket by defining via

[fD_{s},gD_{t}]=fD_{s}(g)D_{t}-(-1)^{(\deg f-1)(\deg g-1)}gD_{t}(f)D_{s}

for any homogeneous polynomials
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\deg f}
and
g\in\mathcal{R}_{\deg g}
.

0.2.1.

From now on, we always simply denote
W(n)
by
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i\geq-1}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\simeq\mathfrak{gl}(n)
as Lie algebras.
Recall that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
admits a canonical Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
which is spanned by
y_{i}D_{i}
for

i=1,\ldots,n
. With respect to
\mathfrak{h}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
admits a root system
\Phi=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n\}
and the corresponding positive root system
\Phi^{+}=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}
. Let
\Delta
be the set of all simple roots, i.e.
\Delta=\{\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\mid i=1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{F}y_{i}D_{j}
,
\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\sum_{i>j}\mathbb{F}y_{i}D_{j}
be the sum of all positive and negative root spaces of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
respectively. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{0,\alpha}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{0,\alpha}=\mathbb{F}y_{i}D_{j}
for
\alpha=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{j}\in\Phi
,
(i\neq j)
.

0.2.2.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
which is isomorphic to the symmetry group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of degree
n
. Let
\rho
be half of the sum of positive roots of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. For
\sigma\in W
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, denote
\sigma\bullet\lambda=\sigma(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
.

1.
The Whittaker categories for
\mathfrak{g}

Keep the above notations and assumptions. Denote by
U(\mathfrak{g})\text{-}\mathbf{mod}
the category of
U(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.

1.1.
Whittaker categories for the Cartan type Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=W(n)

Definition 1.1
.

A Whittaker category
\mathscr{Wh}
is a full subcategory of
U(\mathfrak{g})\text{-}\mathbf{mod}
, with objects
M
satisfying the following axioms:

(W1)

M
is finitely generated over
U(\mathfrak{g})
;

(W2)

M
is locally finite over
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and locally nilpotent over
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
, i.e., for any
v\in M
,
U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})v
is finite-dimensional, and there exists some positive integer
N_{X}
such that
X^{N_{X}}v=0
for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
;

(W3)

M
is locally finite over
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0}):=\text{Cent}(U(\mathfrak{g}_{0}))
, i.e., for any
v\in M
,
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0})v
is finite-dimensional.

Obviously, the category
\mathcal{O}
defined in
[
4
]
is a subcategory of
\mathscr{Wh}
. Furthermore, the following fact is very fundamental and important.

Lemma 1.2
.

The category
\mathscr{W}
is an abelian category.

Proof.

By a routine check according to the definition, it is easily proved.
∎

.

\mathfrak{n}^{+}

Let

Now we further investigate

Proposition 1.4
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

For
\eta\in\text{ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
and
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
,
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
is an object in
\mathscr{W}
.

(2)

\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
has a unique irreducible quotient, denoted by
\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)
.

(3)

The following are equivalent.

(a)

\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{M}(\eta,\mu)
;

(b)

\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{L}(\eta,\mu)
;

(c)

\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\lambda=\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\mu.

Proof.

For the part (1), first note that
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
is finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module since it is induced from
L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
, and the latter is irreducible
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module. So
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
satisfies the axiom (W1) in Definition
1.1
. By the definition of
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
, the trivial action of
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
on
L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
and the locally finite action of
\mathfrak{n}
on
L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
implies (W2). Now to verify (W3), we observe the following fact: for any
\mathfrak{z}\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0})

\mathfrak{z}\cdot(\displaystyle{\sum_{i}X_{i}\otimes w_{i}})=\displaystyle{\sum_{i}\mathfrak{z}\cdot X_{i}\otimes w_{i}}+\displaystyle{\sum_{i}X_{i}\otimes\mathfrak{z}\cdot w_{i}},\,

for all
\,X_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g})_{i},\,w_{i}\in L^{0}(\eta,\chi)
.
Note that in the above sum
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0}).w_{i}
is finite-dimensional, since
L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
is a module in the Whittaker category of
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
. Also each
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0}).X_{i}
is an finite-dimensional since
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0}).X_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g})_{i}
and
\dim(U(\mathfrak{g})_{i})<\infty
. We have that
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\cdot(\displaystyle{\sum_{i}X_{i}\otimes w_{i}})
is finite-dimensional. The axiom (W3) is confirmed. This completes the proof of the part (1).

For the part (2), we first decompose
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
into the sum of eigen spaces with repect to
I_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}D_{i}\in\mathfrak{h}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)_{m}

(1.4.1)

where
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)_{m}=U(\mathfrak{g}_{\geq 1})_{m}\otimes L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
.
Therefore it is clear that
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)_{m}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
W
be any non-zero proper submodule of
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
. It is routine to prove that any submodules of
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)
is still a weight module with respect to the toral Lie subalgebra
\mathbb{F}I_{n}
. Thus
W
has an
I_{n}
-weight space decomposition:
W=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}}W_{m},
where
W_{m}=\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)_{m}\cap W
. Now we claim that
W_{0}=0
. Otherwise
W_{0}
is a submodule of
L^{0}(\eta,\lambda)
which is irreducible. This contradicts the assumption that
W
is proper. Therefore the sum of all proper submodules is still proper and hence it has a unique maximal submodule. Consequently,
\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)
has a unique irreducible quotient.

For the part (3), we first consider (a)
\Rightarrow
(b). Suppose
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{M}(\eta,\mu)
, then their weight spaces of
\mathbb{F}I_{n}
-zero weight in the sense of (
1.4.1
) must be isomorphic, as
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-modules. This is to say,
\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)_{0}\simeq\mathcal{M}(\eta,\mu)_{0}
as

-modules. Hence

\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{L}(\eta,\mu)

For (b)

\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)\simeq\mathcal{L}(\eta,\mu)

\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-modules.
Hence it follows from

\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\lambda=\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\mu

For (c)

(a), by applying
[

\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\lambda=\mathcal{W}_{\eta}\bullet\mu

2.
Decomposition of the Whittaker category and classification of simple objects

Keep the notations as before.

2.1.
Decomposition of the Whittaker category

Assume that
\eta
is a character of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and extends to the Lie superalgebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\eta:\textsf{N}=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}

(2.0.1)

with sending
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
to zero.
Let us denote by
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
the subcategory of
\mathscr{W}
, whose objects are locally annihilated by some powers of
{\rm Ker\,}\eta
in (
2.0.1
). Then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

(1)

The category
\mathscr{W}
splits into a direct sum of subcategories according to the characters over
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
as below

\mathscr{W}=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{\eta\in\text{ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})}}\mathscr{W}_{\eta}

where
\text{ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
denotes the set of characters of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
which extend to
N^{*}
by taking zero at
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
.

(2)

Furthermore,
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
can be decomposed into a direct sum of subcategories
\mathscr{W}_{\eta,\chi}
with
\chi
ranging over the central characters of
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{0})
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
M
is an arbitrarily given object in
\mathscr{W}
. Still consider
{\rm Ker\,}(\eta)\subset N
for
\eta\in\text{ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
in the same sense as in (
2.0.1
).
Let

M^{\eta}:=\{v\in M\mid{\rm Ker\,}(\eta)^{N_{v}}v=0\}.

Obviously,
M^{\eta}
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module. For this, we only need to show that
M^{\eta}
is closed under
\mathfrak{g}
-action. In order to check this, we first notice that

y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{n}x_{0}=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots i_{l}}C_{y_{i_{1}},y_{i_{2}},\dots,y_{i_{l}},x_{0}}ady_{i_{1}}ady_{i_{2}}\cdots ady_{i_{l}}(x_{0})y_{1}\cdots\hat{y_{i_{1}}}\cdots\hat{y_{i_{l}}}\cdots y_{n},

with homogeneous elements
x_{0}\in U(\mathfrak{g}),y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in N
, and
C_{y_{i_{1}},y_{i_{2}},\dots,y_{i_{l}},x_{0}}\neq 0

Then it follows that
\forall v\in M^{\eta},x_{0}\in U(\mathfrak{g})
,
\exists\,n
large enough, such that
y_{1}y_{2}\cdots y_{n}x_{0}\cdot v=0
, for any homogeneous elements
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
(
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
respectively) by applying Engel’s Theorem on
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
respectively. Hence
M^{\eta}
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module.

Next, we will prove that
M=\bigoplus\limits_{\eta\in{\rm ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})}M^{\eta}
,
\forall M\in\mathscr{W}
. Notice that
\forall v\in M
,
W:=U(N)\cdot v
is finite dimensional, then we can get the decomposition
W=\bigoplus\limits_{\eta\in{\rm ch}(N)}W_{\eta}
, where
W_{\eta}
is the direct sum of all indecomposable components giving rise to
\eta
. We define

W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{v\in W|(x-\eta(x))^{N_{x}}\cdot v=0,\forall\,x\in N\}.

It is clear that
W_{\eta}\subseteq W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}
. Now we need to prove
W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq W_{\eta}
. Otherwise, if
W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\bigoplus\limits_{\mu\neq\eta}W_{\mu}\neq\{0\}
, there exists some
x\in N
, such that
\lambda(x)
takes different value for all different character
\lambda
. Now we take some nonzero
v=\sum v_{\mu}\in W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\bigoplus\limits_{\mu\neq\eta}W_{\mu}
, with
v_{\mu}\in W_{\mu}
. There exists some positive integer
N_{\mu},N_{\eta}
, such that
(t-\mu(x))^{N_{\mu}}v_{\mu}=0,(t-\eta(x))^{N_{\eta}}v=0
.
Since
(t-\eta(x))^{N_{\eta}}
and
\Pi_{\mu\neq\eta}(t-\mu(x))^{N_{\mu}}
are coprime, there exist
a(t),b(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t]
, such that
a(t)(t-\eta(x))^{N_{\eta}}+b(t)\Pi_{\mu\neq\eta}(t-\mu(x))^{N_{\mu}}=1
. Then it follows that
v=a(x)(x-\eta(x))^{N_{\eta}}\cdot v+b(x)\Pi_{\mu\neq\eta}(x-\mu(x))^{N_{\mu}}\cdot v=0
, which is a contradiction.
Thus
W_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}=W_{\eta}
,
M=\bigoplus\limits_{\eta\in ch\mathfrak{n}^{+}}M_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}
.
Furthermore, it is easy to see that
M_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq M^{\eta}
and
M_{\eta}\cap\sum\limits_{\mu\neq\eta}M_{\mu}={0}
. This leads to
M_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}=M^{\eta}
,
M=\bigoplus\limits_{\eta\in{\rm ch}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})}M^{\eta}
.

(2) For any
M\in\mathscr{W}
and central character

of

, we denote

Since

is nilpotent Lie algebra, similar to the proof in (1), we have

2.3.

Proposition 2.3
.

(1)

(2)

\mathcal{M}(\eta,\lambda)\in\mathscr{W}_{\eta,\chi}

-module associated with the pair

with trivial

With this observation and taking a fact into account that the

with trivial
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
-action (associated with the pair

with

7

3.1.1.
The parabolic BGG category

We first recall the parabolic BGG category for

Definition 3.1

The parabolic BGG category
\mathcal{O}
is a full category of

, whose objects

are required to be finitely-generated over
U(\mathfrak{g})
satisfying the following three axioms:

(O1)

\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathfrak{g}
-module, i.e.,

with
\dim M_{i}<+\infty

(O2)

\textsf{P}=\mathfrak{g}_{\leq 0}:=\mathfrak{g}_{{-1}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}

(O3)

M

\mathfrak{h}
-semisimple, i.e.,

The morphisms in
\mathcal{O}

-module morphisms that preserve the

3.1.2.
An automorphism
\bar{\sigma}
of
W(n)

Let
\sigma=\prod_{j=1}^{[{n\over 2}]}(j,n+1-j)
be an element of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
where
[{n\over 2}]
denotes the greatest integer not bigger than

\mathcal{R}
via the following transform on the set of generators

and denote it by the same notation

Then

induces an automorphism

By a straightforward check it is easily known that for

This implies that

\bar{\sigma}(\mathfrak{n}^{-})=\mathfrak{n}^{+}

On the other side,

gives rise to a linear automorphism of

\{\epsilon_{1},\cdots,\epsilon_{n}\}
the basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
which are subject to

4.
Finite
W
-superalgebras and generalized Skryabin equivalences

4.1.
Finite
W
-superalgebras

For notational convenience, denote
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathbf{g}
. For any character
\eta:\mathfrak{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
,
\eta
determines a subset
I
of
\Delta
which consists of
\alpha\in\Delta
with
\eta(\mathbf{g}_{\alpha})\neq 0
. Let
\mathbf{g}_{\eta}
be the Levi subalgebra corresponding to
I
with triangular decomposition
\mathbf{g}_{\eta}=\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}
.

Now we define

Q(\eta):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{\eta})}\mathbb{F}_{\eta},

where
\mathbb{F}_{\eta}
is a one dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}
.

and consider the algebra

U(\mathfrak{g},\eta):=\text{End}_{\mathfrak{g}}(Q(\eta))^{\text{op}}.

Let
I_{\eta}
be the left ideal of
U(\mathfrak{g})
generated by the set
\{X-\eta(X):X\in\bf n_{\eta}\}
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

As left
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module, we have
Q(\eta)\simeq U(\mathfrak{g})/\penalty I_{\eta}
.

Proof.

In order to get the isomorphism, we consider the following
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module homomorphism

\phi:U(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\bf n_{\eta})}\mathbb{F}_{\eta}

u\longmapsto u\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}

It is easy to see that
\phi
is surjective and now we will determine
{\rm Ker\,}\phi
.

Since
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{g})\bf n_{\eta}\bigoplus U(\mathfrak{g}_{>0}+\mathfrak{g}_{-1}+\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}^{-}+\mathfrak{h})
, we can assume that
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
, where
u_{1}\in U(\mathfrak{g})\bf n_{\eta}
and
u_{2}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{>0}+\mathfrak{g}_{-1}+\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}^{-}+\mathfrak{h})
. Naturally we can get
u_{1}=\sum\limits_{i}u_{1}^{(i)}{n}_{\eta}^{(i)},u_{2}=\sum\limits_{j}u_{2}^{(j)}
for all
u_{1}^{(i)},u_{2}^{(j)}\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{>0}+\mathfrak{g}_{-1}+\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}^{-}+\mathfrak{h})
and
n_{\eta}^{(i)}\in U(\bf n_{\eta})
. So it follows that

u\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=(u_{1}+u_{2})\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{i}u_{1}^{(i)}\otimes n_{\eta}^{(i)}\mathbf{1}_{\eta}+\sum\limits_{j}u_{2}^{(j)}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}.

This implies that
u\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}
can be expressed as the form
\sum\limits_{k}u^{(k)}\otimes X^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\eta}
, where
\{u^{(k)}\}
are right
U(\bf n_{\eta})
-linearly independent and
X^{k}\in U(\bf n_{\eta})
. Hence
u\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{k}u^{(k)}\otimes X^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=0
implies
X^{k}1_{\eta}=0
. Now it follows from the definition of
\eta
that
X^{k}\in I_{\eta}
.

u\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{k}u^{(k)}\otimes X^{k}\mathbf{1}_{\eta}=\sum\limits_{k}u^{(k)}X^{k}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{\eta}
implies
u=\sum\limits_{k}u^{(k)}X^{k}\in I_{\eta}
. Hence we have
{\rm Ker\,}\phi=I_{\eta},
this completes the proof.

∎

4.2.
Generalized Skryabin’s equivalences

In this part we consider a full subcategory
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}^{\prime}
of
U(\mathfrak{g})\text{-mod}
whose objects have locally nilpotent action of
\{X-\eta(X):X\in{\bf n}_{\eta}\}
. One can see that
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
is the subcategory of
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}^{\prime}
. This
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}^{\prime}
is the category of so-called weakened Whittaker modules mentioned in the beginning of the paper.

For any
M\in\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}
we define,

Wh_{\eta}(M)=\{m\in M:X.m=\eta(X)m,\,\forall\,X\in{\bf n}_{\eta}\}.

By Proposition 5.1, one can define a module action of
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)
on
Wh_{\eta}(M)
. Now we have the following generalized Skryabin’s equivalence, by following
[
10
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

There is a generalized Skryabin’s equivalence between the category
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}
and the category
\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}
.

Proof.

Note that for
\eta=0
,
Q(\eta)=U(\mathfrak{g})
and
U(g,\eta)=End_{\mathfrak{g}}(U(\mathfrak{g}))^{op}\simeq U(\mathfrak{g})
. Then it is easy to see that both
\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{0}
and
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}
are the category of
U(\mathfrak{g})
modules. Now we only need to consider the case of
\eta\neq 0
.

In order to get the equivalence between two categories, we define the following functors:

F:\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}\longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}

M\longmapsto Wh_{\eta}(M),\,\,\,

G:U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}\longrightarrow\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}

V\longmapsto Q(\eta)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)}V

It is easy to check the well-definedness of
F
and
G
by Proposition 5.1.

Case I:
Let
\eta
be regular, i.e
\eta(x_{k}\partial_{k+1})\neq 0
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then
Y_{k}=x_{k}\partial_{k+1}
,
k=1,\dots,n-1
forms a basis for
n_{\eta}
. Set
X_{k}=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}x_{i}\partial_{i}}{\eta(Y_{k})}
,
k=1,\dots,n-1
. Then it follows that
\eta([Y_{i},X_{j}])=\delta_{ij}
.

For
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1},
set

X^{\mathbf{a}}=X_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots X_{n-1}^{a_{n-1}}\in U(\mathfrak{g}),\,\,\,\text{and}

u_{\mathbf{a}}=(Y_{1}-\eta(Y_{1}))^{a_{1}}\cdots(Y_{n-1}-\eta(Y_{n-1}))^{a_{n-1}}\in U(\bf n_{\eta}).

Also let
|\mathbf{a}|=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}
. We define an ordering on
\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
:
for
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
, define

\mathbf{a}<\mathbf{b}

if and only if

|\mathbf{a}|<|\mathbf{b}|

or

\text{if}\,\,\,|\mathbf{a}|=|\mathbf{b}|

with

a_{j}=b_{j},(j=1,2,\cdots,k-1),\,\,\text{then}\,\,a_{k}<b_{k}.

Let
J_{\eta}
be the two sided ideal of
U(\bf n)
generated by
X-\eta(X)
, for all
X\in\bf n_{\eta}
. Note that elements
u_{\bf a}
, for all
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
froms a basis for
U(\bf n_{\eta})
, further
u_{\bf a}\in J_{\eta}^{|\bf a|}
.

Denote by
I_{\mathbf{a}}
the linear span of
u_{\mathbf{b}}
with
\mathbf{b}>\mathbf{a}
. It is easy to see that
I_{\mathbf{a}}
is an ideal of
U(\bf n_{\eta})
and
J_{\eta}^{r}\subseteq I_{\bf a}
for some
r>0
.

Let
E_{V,{\eta}}=\{f\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(U({\bf n}_{\eta}),V)|f(J_{\eta}^{m})=0,\,\text{ for some }m>0\}
, then
E_{V,\eta}
consists of all linear maps
f:U(\bf n_{\eta})\longrightarrow V
such that
f(I_{\mathbf{a}})=0
, for some
\mathbf{a}
.

For any
M\in\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}
, we take
V=Wh_{\eta}(M)
. Now for any given
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
and
v\in V
, one can observe that
u_{\mathbf{b}}X^{\mathbf{a}}v=0
, whenever
\mathbf{b}>\mathbf{a}
and
u_{\mathbf{a}}X^{\mathbf{a}}v=cv
for some nonzero
c
. Take any linear map
\pi:M\longrightarrow V
with
\pi|_{V}=id_{V}
and define a
U(\bf n_{\eta})
-module map
\phi:M\longrightarrow E_{V,\eta}
by sending
m\in M
to
\phi(m):u\longmapsto\pi(um)
, for
u\in U(\bf n_{\eta})
. Note that
\phi
is well defined due to that fact that
M\in\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}
. Also it is eassy to see that
\phi
is a
\bf n_{\eta}
-module map.

Moreover, for any
f\in E_{V,\eta}
, we know that
f(I_{\bf a})=0
. Hence
f
is determined by its action on
u_{\bf b}
with
\bf b\leq\bf a
. Assume that
f(u_{\bf b})=v_{\bf b}
for all
\bf b\leq\bf a.
Then it is easy to see that
\phi(\sum_{{\bf b\leq\bf a}}X_{\bf b}v_{b})
is the the preimage of
f
under
\phi
, i.e,
\phi
is surjective. Again,
{\rm Ker\,}\phi\cap V=0
implies that
\phi
is injective, since
{\rm Ker\,}\phi
is a
\bf n_{\eta}
-submodule of
M
. Then it follows that
\phi
is an isomorphism and any element
m\in M
can be uniquely expressed as the finite sum
\sum X^{\mathbf{a}}v_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Let
M^{\mathbf{a}}=\{m\in M|I_{\mathbf{a}}.m=0\}
. Now for any
\mathbf{a}
, we denote
\mathbf{a}^{{}^{\prime}}
by the predecessor of
\mathbf{a}
. Then
u_{\mathbf{a}}
determines a linear map
i_{\mathbf{a}}:M^{\mathbf{a}}\longrightarrow M^{0}=Wh_{\eta}(M)
by sending
m\in M^{\mathbf{a}}
to
u_{\mathbf{a}}m
. It is clear that
i_{\mathbf{a}}
is surjective and
Keri_{\mathbf{a}}=M^{\mathbf{a}^{{}^{\prime}}}
, when
\bf a\neq 0
. Note that each
M^{\bf a}
is stable under
\rm{End}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}
-module, hence we get the folowing isomorphism of
\rm{End}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}
-module
M^{\mathbf{a}}/M^{\mathbf{a}^{{}^{\prime}}}\simeq M^{0}
.

Now we apply the above observation to the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
, where
M=Q(\eta)\cong U(\mathfrak{g})/I_{\eta}.
Since we have
Q(\eta)
is a free
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)
-module of rank 1, so is
Q(\eta)^{\bf a}/Q(\eta)^{\bf a^{\prime}}
for all
\bf a\neq 0
. In particular, the image of
X^{\bf a}
in
Q(\eta)^{\bf a}/Q(\eta)^{\bf a^{\prime}}
is a free generator of
Q(\eta)^{\bf a}/Q(\eta)^{\bf a^{\prime}}
. Now since
Q(\eta)=\cup_{{\bf a}\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}}Q(\eta)^{\bf a}
, it follows that
X^{\mathbf{a}}1_{\eta}
forms a basis of
Q(\eta)
over
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)
.

Define a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module map

\mu:Q(\eta)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)}V\longrightarrow M

u1_{\eta}\otimes v\longmapsto uv.

Since every element of
Q(\eta)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)}V
can be uniquely expressed as
\sum X^{\mathbf{a}}1_{\eta}\otimes v_{\mathbf{a}}
, it is easy to check that
\mu
is a
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module map. Further, it clear that
\mu
is surjective and injectivity follows by using the map
\phi
.

Now we need to show that for any
M=Q(\eta)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)}V^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{W}^{{}^{\prime}}_{\eta}
,
V^{{}^{\prime}}\simeq Wh_{\eta}(M)
as
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)
-module. Define

\nu:V^{{}^{\prime}}\longrightarrow Wh_{\eta}(M)

v\longmapsto 1_{\eta}\otimes v

Since any element
m\in M
can be expressed uniquely as
\sum X^{\mathbf{a}}\nu(v_{\mathbf{a}})
and
m\in Wh_{\eta}(M)
only if
\nu(v_{\mathbf{a}})=0
for all
\mathbf{a}\neq 0
,
\nu
is a
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)
-module isomorphism. This completes the proof of Case I.

Case II:
Let
\eta
be any non zero character. Then
\eta\neq 0
for some simple root vectors between
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Let
\eta\neq 0
on the root vectors corresponding to the
k
values
i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}
. Then we consider corresponding
Y_{i_{j}}
and
X_{i_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq r
as Case I, which will satisfy the property
\eta([Y_{i_{j}},X_{i_{k}}])=\delta_{jk}
and then continue the proof of Case I. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
is the subcategory of
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}^{{}^{\prime}}
and any irreducible object
\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)
in
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
is also an irreducible object in
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}^{\prime}
. Now by the above equivalence, we get a collection of irreducible Whittaker modules in
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}
.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any irreducible object
\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda)
in the category
\mathscr{W}_{\eta}
, the Whittaker space
Wh_{\eta}(\mathcal{L}(\eta,\lambda))
is also an irreducible object in the category
U(\mathfrak{g},\eta)\text{-mod}
.

Very recently, Futorny and Tantubay studied Whittaker modules for infinite-dimensional Lie super algebras
W(m;n)
. Their consideration is quite different from ours.
