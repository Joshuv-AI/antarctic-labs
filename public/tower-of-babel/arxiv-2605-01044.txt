Efficient Reconstruction of Arboreal Networks
Katharina T. Huber, Katherine St. John

Abstract




Abstract

Arboreal networks are multi-rooted phylogenetic networks whose underlying graph is a tree.
We give an encoding
of stack-free arboreal networks in terms of triplets and the novel concept of a duet.
This
yields a polynomial time algorithm to construct these networks from complete triplet and duet systems.
The classification results show correctness and lead to a natural metric on these multi-rooted networks.

Keywords: arboreal phylogenetic network, triplet & duet systems, reconstruction algorithms, metrics.

1
Introduction

An improved understanding of the complex processes that drive molecular evolution has led to extensive interest in phylogenetic networks to represent them. Recently introduced classes of multi-rooted (phylogenetic) networks include forest-based networks
[
7
]
and arboreal networks
[
8
]
. Both extend the notion of the popular tree-based networks
[
4
]
and may be useful for studying lateral gene transfer between bacteria living in ecologically distinct niches such as the hand and the human gut
[
10
]
. Both are multi-rooted directed acyclic graphs whose leaf set is a fixed set of taxa, and the study of their combinatorial structure has revealed intriguing links
with, for example, Ptolemaic graphs
[
8
]
.
The computational complexity of recognizing forest-based networks was studied in
[
12
]
under various scenarios.
Their class
encompasses the class of Overlaid Species Forests which have been used to shed light into
introgression in Heliconius butterflies in
[
9
]
.

We focus on arboreal networks, an intriguing class of multi-rooted networks whose underlying structure is a tree.
We give a new combinatorial classification for
arboreal networks
which yields a natural metric on the duets (rooted 2-leaf subtrees) and triplets (rooted 3-leaf binary subtrees) compatible with arboreal networks.
Using this characterization, we give a polynomial-time algorithm to reconstruct an arboreal network, if it exists, from the complete triplet and duet systems induced by the network.
Allowing multiple roots in a network adds power to capture biological relationships, but, as we shall see, it also adds complexity.
Our approach relies on the
Build
algorithm
[
1
]
. While the
Build
algorithm cannot directly reconstruct multi-rooted networks,
a pre-processing step, based on duets, and additional clustering yields an efficient algorithm that matches the running time bounds of the
Build
algorithm.

Combinatorial Classifications

A rooted phylogenetic tree
T

by

\mathcal{R}(T)
(within the class

in

for which
\mathcal{R}(T)=\mathcal{R}(T^{\prime})
is isomorphic with

[
2

13

share a vertex it is not, in general, encoded by
\mathcal{R}(N)

[
5
]
.
Interestingly, such 1-networks are encoded by their induced set of trinets
[
6
]
where an encoding in terms of such structures is defined analogously to the triplet system case. Without going into details, a trinet can be thought of as a natural generalization of a triplet to a
1
-network on 3 leaves (see
[

,
14
]
for more on encodings of

Even if an

-network

\mathcal{R}(N)
. The two arboreal networks

(i)

(i) & (ii)

.

A network

Arboreal Reconstruction Algorithm (ARA):

Input:
A triplet system,
\mathcal{R}
, and a duet system,
\mathcal{D}
, on

with

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(N)
and
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}(N)

1.

Partition

4
Reconstructing Arboreal Networks

We present an algorithm,
ARA
,
that efficiently reconstructs an arboreal network from them, if one exists, given the basic building blocks of duets and triplets.
It relies on our combinatorial classification (Theorem
1
) and the work of Bryant
[
2
]
.
Bryant
[
2
]
used the
Build
algorithm of Aho
et al.

[
1
]
to determine, in polynomial time, if a triplet system is compatible with a rooted phylogenetic tree. Bryant further showed that if the triplet system is not compatible, finding a maximal set of compatible triplets is computationally hard. The
Build
algorithm first constructs a graph whose components correspond to the subtrees of the root of an envisaged tree.
If there is only one component, then the root would have only one child which is not possible, and the algorithm halts.
Otherwise, the process is recursively applied to the subcomponents until the tree is resolved. If the triplets are not compatible, no tree is built and the algorithm halts. A tree can have
polytomies
i. e. vertices with out-degree larger than 3, if information about the
relationships in the
triplets is limited.

We can show similar results for arboreal networks and duets and triplets, with a subtle difference. In the case of arboreal networks, the absence of triplets and duets is itself a statement about the possible network. For the triplet system
\mathcal{C}=\{12|3,12|4\}
in Fig.
2
(a), any binary rooted phylogenetic tree that induces

\mathcal{C}
also induces one of
t_{1}=24|3
or
t_{2}=23|4
. Thus, there exists no such
tree

T
such that
\mathcal{R}(T)=\mathcal{C}
. With multiple roots allowed,
there is an arboreal network
N^{b}
(Fig.
2
(b)) such that
\mathcal{R}(N^{b})=\mathcal{C}
.
Fig.
4
gives an overview of
ARA

with the details of each step provided in the subsequent sections and figures.
We illustrate its inner workings
in Fig.
5
.

5
A Metric for Stack-free Arboreal Networks

Since triples are not enough to encode a
network in
\mathcal{A}(X)
but triples and duets are (Theorem
1
), we obtain a metric on
\mathcal{A}(X)
that canonically extends the popular triplet distance
[
3
]
for rooted phylogenetic trees.
More precisely, we define the
duet-triplet distance

d(N,N^{\prime})
between networks
N,N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}(X)

as

|(\mathcal{D}(N)\cup\mathcal{R}(N))\Delta(\mathcal{D}(N^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{R}(N^{\prime}))|
where
\Delta
is the symmetric distance (see Fig.
2
for examples).

To see that
our distance
is a metric, note that non-negativity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality follow directly from the symmetric difference. For the identity condition, note that by the symmetric difference,
d(N,N^{\prime})=0
if and only if

\mathcal{D}(N)=\mathcal{D}(N^{\prime})
and

\mathcal{R}(N)=\mathcal{R}(N^{\prime})
.
Since, by Theorem
1
, a network
M\in\mathcal{A}(X)
is encoded
by
\mathcal{R}(M)\cup\mathcal{D}(M)

it follows that
d(N,N^{\prime})=0
if and only if
N\simeq N^{\prime}
.
We leave as an open question: what is the diameter of
\mathcal{A}(X)
under the duet-triplet distance?

6
Conclusion

Arboreal networks are multi-rooted structures whose underlying graph is a tree.
We give an elegant characterization of stack-free arboreal networks in terms of the rooted 2-sets and 3-sets that they induce. This characterization allows for an algorithm for reconstructing such networks with running time linear in the number of inputted duets and triplets, as well as an intuitive metric to compare the networks. We leave as a further open question: can we bound the number of the triplet/duets needed to reconstruct a stack-free arboreal network up to isomorphism?
