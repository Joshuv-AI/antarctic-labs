Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

This CRADA supports the proposal led by General Motors LLC (GM) entitled "InfoRich
VD&PT Controls" submitted under the Department of Energy (DOE) Advance Research
Projects Agency-Energy (ARPA-E) NEXT-Generation Energy Technologies for Connected and
Automated on-Road-vehicles (NEXTCAR) Program award DE-AR1564-1552. The project team
is led by GM with sub-tier partners Carnegie Mellon University (CMU), and the National
Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL). The work under this NREL CRADA Joint Work
Statement (JWS) is in support of GM's pending DOE Cooperative Agreement for DE-AR1564-
1552 which extends through 04-01-2020.

 1

CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

This CRADA will support NREL's work with GM under the aforementioned ARPA-E
NEXTCAR award. Per the goals of the NEXTCAR program, work conducted under this project
will support increased vehicle fuel efficiency (leveraging increasing vehicle
sensing/communication and controls optimization capabilities). This fuel savings will ultimately
save U.S. consumers on their annual fuel costs, will improve national energy security, and
through working with a U.S. manufacturer will support increased domestic competitiveness in
the automotive sector.

Summary of Research Results:

Task 1.1: Create a vehicle simulation model that is capable of capturing the fuel
consumption characteristics of the eco-approach, eco-departure, eco-cruise, and eco-
routing control methodologies

 • Use a selected vehicle modeling/simulation software to create a vehicle model that can
 comprehend the fuel consumption characteristics of the different eco-driving strategies.
 • Verify model characteristics and fidelity are sufficient for 1st round of simulation. For
 example, fuel consumption impact associated with different coasting strategies needs to
 be captured to allow the evaluation of eco-approach. Fuel consumption impact associated
 with different acceleration profile and associated powertrain calibration needs to be
 captured to evaluate eco-departure.

Task 1.2: Conduct 1st round of simulation studies to capture fuel economy impact of the
eco-driving strategies using real world drive cycles

 a) Eco-approach + eco-departure
 b) Eco-cruise + eco-routing
 • Identify portions/segments of a set of selected real-world driving cycles that would be
 applicable to each of the eco-driving strategies. Quantify based on the proposed control
 strategy the energy savings associated with each segment
 • Aggregate the energy savings across the drive cycles to come up with an expected energy
 savings for each eco-driving strategy
 • Quantify the availability of scenarios that would be applicable for each eco-driving
 strategy. Provide breakdown of fuel economy savings from vehicle control and from
 powertrain control.

Task 1.3: Design and create candidate synthetic drive environments based on real-world
driving cycles that have appropriate proportion of eco-driving opportunities.

 • Based on the percentage of each eco-driving strategy, create synthetic driving
 environments (road, speed limit, traffic lights, etc.) that are compact representations of a
 set of the real-world drive cycles
 • These drive cycles will then be used with the virtual traffic simulator to assess the
 controller’s effectiveness under different test factors.

 2

Task 1.4: Provide representative drive cycles to support the powertrain calibration of eco-
departure strategy

 • Eco-departure strategy is envisioned to have different sets of calibration for different
 traffic conditions
 • Select drive cycles from the real-world database that can represent each traffic condition.
 These drive cycles will be used to optimize powertrain control calibrations (e.g., shift
 maps) for each condition.

Task 2.1: Conduct 2nd round of simulation studies based on initial VD+PT control designed
for vehicle implementation

 a) Eco-approach + eco-departure
 b) Eco-cruise + eco-routing.
 • Perform a more detailed simulation based on the initial VD+PT control design to
 understand the fuel economy impact of the eco-driving strategies. The control design will
 be based on the controlled designed in the first-year simulation work slated for vehicle
 implementation in year 2.
 • Again, provide breakdown of fuel economy savings from vehicle control and from
 powertrain control.

Task 2.2: Explore additional fuel savings opportunities beyond the proposed eco-driving
strategies.

 a) Areas of zero fuel savings
 b) Initial work for hybrid simulation study (in Task 3.1)
 • Examine the real-world drive cycles to find additional fuel savings opportunities for the
 four eco-driving strategies, e.g., by data mining cycles that provide very small to zero
 fuel savings for similarities. (Why no fuel savings in these segments?)
 • Prep work for Task 3.1, e.g., create a hybrid vehicle model that can comprehend the fuel
 consumption characteristics of the different eco-driving strategies.

Task 3.1: Explore impact of hybridization on proposed eco-driving strategies

 • Analysis of hybrid configurations versus conventional ICE.

Task 3.2: Conduct final round of simulation studies on the eco-driving strategies to validate
final control design against real-world driving cycles

 • Based on the control strategies actually implemented in the demo vehicle, perform a final
 round of simulation studies using the real-world drive cycles to quantify the fuel
 economy impact of the eco-driving strategies
 • Again provide breakdown of fuel economy savings from vehicle control and from
 powertrain control
