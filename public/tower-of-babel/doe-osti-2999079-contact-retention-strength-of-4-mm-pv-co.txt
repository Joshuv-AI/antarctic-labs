Factors of contact displacement sdigre@sandia.gov

• Assembly issues:
 • Incomplete insertion of contacts
 • Partial seating of connector halves
 • Loose nut → improper strain relief
• Installation issues → high cable tension:
 • Bad wire management (snagged on tracker, mower, or animals)
 • Tight wire bends
• Polymer softening at elevated temperatures → contact retainer failure

Problem Recap sdigre@sandia.gov

• A small, yet significant, percentage of real-world connectors exhibit under or over-inserted contacts,
  which correlate with increased resistance and melting
• A controlled contact insertion experiment showed that:
 • Resistance is a function of insertion length
 • Repeated sliding motion can lead to increased resistance
• Take-away: proper contact retention is essential for optimal performance!
 • Connectors contain four mechanisms to resist high cable tension

Next Steps sdigre@sandia.gov

  • Repeat tension/compression experiments at low/high temperature
  • Equilibrate entire connector temperature in refrigerator or oven then
 quickly transfer to load frame for testing
  • -20 °C → extreme cold
  • 0 °C → cold
  • 25 °C → room temp
  • 40 °C → hot ambient
  • 100 °C → upper operating limit
  • 150 °C → past operating limit
  • 200 °C → near melt-down

Summary sdigre@sandia.gov

 • Connectors outside the optimal contact insertion
 range (8.5 ± 1.5 mm) made up only 4.9% of the total
 but accounted for 24.7% of critical failures
 • Contact withdrawal and repeated movements can be
 detrimental
 • Some connector designs are better at resisting
 tensile forces
 • Overall goal:
 • Understand the factors and implications of contact
 displacement
 • Identify best-performing connector designs
 • Areas where standards need to improve
 • Improve connector reliability and safety
