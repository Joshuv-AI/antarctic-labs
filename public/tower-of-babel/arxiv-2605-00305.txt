On Aubry’s completeness conjecture
Tianqi Shi and Jinxin Xue

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove Aubry’s completeness stating conjecture that for a twist map the graph of rotation numbers as a function of the cohomology classes is a purely singularly continuous function (called complete devil’s staircase by Aubry) when the set of all minimal configurations is uniformly hyperbolic. Such a phenomenon is crucial for characterizing the chain of atoms being an insulator for the Frenkel-Kontorova model, and can be considered as the analogue of the phase locking phenomenon in critical circle maps as well as the fractional quantum Hall effect. In contrast, in the presence of a positive measure set of KAM tori, we prove that the devil’s staircase is incomplete.

37J40,37J51

1.
Introduction

The Aubry-Mather theory on twist maps is widely considered to be the most important development in Hamiltonian dynamics after KAM. An important prototypical model studied in the theory is the
standard map
of the form

\phi:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},\quad(x,y)\mapsto(x+y+k\sin x,y+k\sin x).

The standard map is in general very chaotic, defying analysis. When
k
is small, the theory of Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser gives a Cantor set of Diophantine numbers such that for each point
\rho
in the set, there is an invariant circle restricted to which the dynamics is conjugate to a circle rotation by
\rho.
However, the KAM theory says nothing in the complement of the set of KAM invariant circles where the dynamics is very chaotic.
The approach of Aubry-Mather theory was to utilize the variational principle to obtain a family of minimal configurations, which can be classified, and we can extract an ordered structure from the chaos by the energy minimization procedure. Compared to the KAM theory, the Aubry-Mather theory is nonperturbative. Moreover, it has a global nature lying in the fact that we can parameterize all the minimal configurations by a real parameter, and the dynamical information of the minimal configurations is encoded in the structure of a function called the
\alpha
-function
of the real parameter. The main theme of the paper is to further explore how the structure of
\alpha
is related to the phase space dynamics.

1.1.
Frenkel-Kontorova model, devil’s staircase and Aubry’s completeness conjecture

To motivate Aubry’s completeness conjecture, we first give a brief overview of Aubry’s work on this topic. Readers are recommended the paper
[
3
]
of Aubry in 1978, which set the stage for his series of works later.

1.1.1.
Frenkel-Kontorova model and minimal configurations

Aubry focused on the
Frenkel-Kontorova model
(FK model) in solid state physics
which describes the positions of atoms in solids with nearest neighbor interaction with energy of the form

(1.1)

h(x_{n},x_{n+1})=\frac{1}{2}(x_{n}-x_{n+1})^{2}-k\cos(2\pi x_{n}),

where the term
(x_{n}-x_{n+1})^{2}
is similar to the elastic potential for springs and the term
k\cos(2\pi x_{n})
is the periodic background potential. A configuration is a biinfinite sequence
(\ldots,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},\ldots)
. When the atoms are at a stationary configuration, we should have the Euler-Lagrange equation

(1.2)

\partial_{2}h(x_{i-1},x_{i})+\partial_{1}h(x_{i},x_{i+1})=0.

Moreover, in experiments, we only see stable stationary configurations, since unstable ones are easily perturbed away. It is then natural to look for stable configurations by minimizing the total energy. In general, if we consider the total energy
\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}h(x_{\ell},x_{\ell+1})
of a configuration, it is most likely to be infinity. However, we can still talk about minimality in terms of minimality for every finite segment.

Definition 1.1
.

A configuration is called
minimal
, if the following holds for any integers
i<j

\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}h(x_{\ell},x_{\ell+1})\leqslant\inf_{\xi_{i}=x_{i},\xi_{j}=x_{j}}\sum_{\ell=i}^{j-1}h(\xi_{\ell},\xi_{\ell+1}).

In the FK model, the Euler-Lagrange equation gives

(1.3)

(x_{n+1}-2x_{n}+x_{n-1})-2\pi k\sin(2\pi x_{n})=0.

Defining
y_{n}=\partial_{2}h(x_{n-1},x_{n})=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
, we get a 2D map

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x_{n+1}=x_{n}+y_{n}+2\pi k\sin(2\pi x_{n})\\
y_{n+1}=y_{n}+2\pi k\sin(2\pi x_{n}).\\
\end{array}\right.

This is in the form of the standard map.

1.1.2.
The devil’s staircase

Aubry in 1983 (
[
7
]
) introduced the
mean energy per atom
along any minimal configuration with the prescribed rotation number,

\beta(\varrho)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2k+1}\sum_{i=-k}^{k-1}h(x_{i},x_{i+1}),\ \forall\ (\cdots,x_{i},x_{i+1},\cdots)\in\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}.

Here
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}
is the set of minimal configurations with rotation number
\varrho
. Aubry first proved the strict convexity of
\beta
in
[
7
]
. This is essentially reflecting the fact that minimal configurations with different rotation numbers necessarily intersect.

In the paper
[
7
]
, Aubry discovered the following remarkable devil’s staircase phenomenon encoding further dynamical information of the set of minimal configurations for all rotation numbers.

Theorem 1.2
.

The
\beta
-function is differentiable at each irrational point and generically nondifferentiable at each rational point.

The result was discovered by Aubry using numerics who formulated it into a conjecture. The mathematical proof of the result was given in
[
26
,
14
]
. The result can be reformulated in terms of the Legendre dual of the
\beta
-function called the
\alpha
-function. The notion was also introduced by Aubry in
[
7
]
who considered adding a chemical potential to the FK model.

Definition 1.3
.

The
\alpha
-function
is defined to be the Legendre transformation of
\beta
, i.e. the
\alpha
-function takes its value at
c
such that
\alpha(c)+\beta(\varrho)=c\varrho
, or equivalently,

\alpha(c)=-\liminf_{K,K^{\prime}\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{K^{\prime}+K}}\frac{1}{K+K^{\prime}}\Big(\sum_{i=-K}^{K^{\prime}-1}h(x_{i},x_{i+1})-c(x_{K^{\prime}}-x_{-K})\Big).

The last theorem is translated to the statement that the graph of
D\alpha
is a
devil’s staircase
, where by devil’s staircase we mean that the graph of
D\alpha
is monotonically increasing and has infinitely many horizontal pieces. Indeed, since
\alpha
and
\beta
are mutual Legendre transformations of each other, the convexity of
\beta
implies also the convexity of
\alpha
, which further implies that
D\alpha
is monotonically increasing. the strict convexity of
\beta
translates to the continuity of
D\alpha.
A nondifferentiable point
\varrho
of
\beta
corresponds to an interval
[c^{-},c^{+}]
such that
D\alpha=\varrho
and
D^{2}\alpha=0
when restricted to
(c^{-},c^{+})
. Thus, generically, the graph of
D\alpha
has infinitely many horizontal pieces, i.e., a devil’s staircase.

1.1.3.
Transition by breaking of analyticity and the completeness conjecture

There is an important theme in Aubry’s works that seems not well understood or appreciated by mathematicians that is the
transition by breaking of analyticity
. The physics of this transition is important. On an invariant curve, the atoms can slide without causing extra energy, thus the chain of atom is a metal. On the other hand, when the invariant curve is broken, some energy (called the
Peierls-Nabarro barrier
) has to be costed to shift the minimal configuration to a nearby one, in which case, the atoms are locked and the material is an insulator. When we increase the constant
k
in the standard map from zero, for a Diophantine number
\rho
, the corresponding invariant circle persists for
k
in a certain range
[0,k_{\rho}]
. When
k
further increases beyond
k_{\rho}
, the invariant circle breaks and a Cantor set shows up. This is a transition from metal to insulator called the transition by breaking of analyticity by Aubry.

In physics, it is important to know if the devil’s staircase is complete or not. We say a function
f:\ [a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is a
complete
devil’s staircase if
f
is a purely singularly continuous function, i.e.
f
is continuous, nonconstant, and its derivative is vanishing almost everywhere. A complete devil’s staircase means zero probability for incommensurate phases. As a commensurate phase means that the atoms are locked, thus the material is an insulator, a complete devil’s staircase means that the material is an insulator with probability 1. This is called global hysteresis by Aubry.

An important conjecture left unsolved in the work of Aubry is the following conjecture (
[
8
]
). Considering Aubry’s series of works starting from
[
3
]
, it is clear that the conjecture is one of the central concerns in his theory.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
\Lambda_{[c_{1},c_{2}]}
be the set of orbits of a twist map corresponding to minimal configurations with
c\in[c_{1},c_{2}]
. If
\Lambda_{[c_{1},c_{2}]}
is uniformly hyperbolic, then the graph of
D\alpha:\ [c_{1},c_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}
is purely singularly continuous.

Aubry proved the conjecture for the FK model when
k
is greater than a precisely given constant (
[
8
]
). Aubry also gave several explicitly computed models exhibiting complete devil’s staircases (
[
6
,
5
]
).

The hyperbolicity assumption is indeed a rather sharp condition in the sense that it is not only sufficient but also necessary. It was proved by Aubry, MacKay and Baesens that for minimal configurations, the uniform hyperbolicity is equivalent to the nondegeneracy of the second variation of the action functional. The latter further implies that some nonvanishing energy has to be paid to shift a minimal configuration to a nearby one (positive Peierls-Nabarro barrier), in which case the material is an insulator.

The mathematical interest of the conjecture is self-evident, since the global functions
\alpha
and
\beta
encode the information of the phase space dynamics. Whereas the strict convexity of
\beta
reflects the fact that minimal configurations with different rotation number intersects, the nondifferentiability of
\beta
at rational points reflects the gaps between nearby minimal configurations, Theorem
1.2
means that the hyperbolicity of the minimal configurations is encoded in the completeness of the devil’s staircase. Whereas the strict convexity of
\beta
involves the information of the graph of
\beta
and Theorem
1.2
involves the differentiability of
\beta
, the Aubry’s completeness conjecture involves finer information of
\beta
concerning second order derivatives.

1.1.4.
Complete devil’s staircase in physics and mathematics

The completeness of devil’s staircase turns out to be an important theme in condensed matter physics.
Fractional quantum Hall effect
(FQHE) is a phenomenon discovered in 1982 by Tsui, Störmer and Gossard in certain strongly correlated quantum systems that the quantum number could take on rational fractional values. In the paper
[
31
]
, the authors considered the thin-torus limit of the Hamiltonian of interacting electrons in a strong magnetic field and revealed the devil’s staircase structure of the inverse filling fraction versus the strength of magnetic field. The model studied by Aubry was the zero-temperature limit of this model. This is an active field of researches in physics. We refer readers to
[
10
,
11
,
21
,
1
,
32
]
for other related researches. It is important to mention that there is a remarkable parallel between the Aubry’s theory on twist maps and the Hubbard model in cold atom physics (
[
16
]
). In both settings, the rational ground states are classified and there is a devil’s staircase structure. It would be very interesting to further explore the analogue.

For circle maps, there is a similar phenomenon called
phase locking
. Consider circle map of the form
x\mapsto x+\alpha+\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi x)
. When
\epsilon=1
, treating the rotation number
\rho
as a function of
\alpha
, we get that for full measure set of
\alpha
, the rotation number is rational. The graph of
\rho
as a function of
\alpha
is a complete devil’s staircase (see
[
19
]
). The phase locking is caused by the existence of a critical point of the map. The mechanism for the devil’s staircase in this case is rather different from that of our case.

1.2.
The main theorems

In this paper, we confirm the conjecture for generic Tonelli Lagrangian systems. According to Moser’s theorem (
[
30
]
, see also Theorem
2.1
), a twist map can be equivalently written as the time-1 map of a Tonelli Lagrangian system. So for convenience of notations, we work in the framework of Tonelli Lagrangian systems.
To set up the problem, we consider a Tonelli Lagrangian
L:\mathbb{T}\times T\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
that is a
C^{2}
function
L(t,x,v)
that is strictly convex and superlinear
(\frac{L}{|v|}\to\infty
as
|v|\to\infty
) in component
v
and has complete Euler-Lagrangian flows (see Section
2.2
).

We introduce the
A(\mu):=\int Ld\mu
for an invariant probability measure
\mu
defined on
\mathbb{T}\times T\mathbb{T}
. Let
\eta
be a closed 1-form on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
with cohomology class
c\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T},\mathbb{R})
. We introduce the
\alpha
-function

\alpha(c):=-\inf_{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times T\mathbb{T}}L-\eta d\mu

as well as the Mather set
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}(c)
to be the set of support of measures attaining the above infimum.

The main theorems are as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of Tonelli Lagrangians satisfying that the set

\displaystyle\Lambda_{[c_{1},c_{2}]}:=\bigcup_{c\in[c_{1},c_{2}]}\tilde{\mathscr{M}}(c)

is uniformly hyperbolic. Then there exists a residual set
\mathcal{O}(\subset\mathcal{H})
in the sense of
C^{\infty}
-topology such that for each Lagrangian
L\in\mathcal{O}
, the graph of
D\alpha:\ [c_{1},c_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}
is purely singularly continuous.

Remark 1.6
.

The genericity assumption is used to guarantee that the Mather set for each rational rotation number has a unique periodic orbit (
[
24
]
). The theorem still holds if we have a uniform bound on the number of periodic orbits in the Mather set.

Theorem 1.7
.

Under the assumption of the last theorem, the set of
c
such that
D^{2}\alpha
does not exist has zero Hausdorff dimension.

Moreover, in the settings of KAM theorem, the next result shows that the devil’s staircase cannot be complete. By a KAM circle, we mean a homologically nontrivial circle
\gamma
that is invariant under the time-1 map of the Euler-Lagrange flow and the dynamics on
\gamma
is conjugate to a Diophantine rotation on circle by a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism (see Section
4.1.1
).

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose there is a positive Lebesgue measure set
P
of Diophantine rotation numbers, such that each Mather set
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}_{h}(see\eqref{eq:mather-homology}),\ h\in P,
is a KAM circle. Then
D\alpha
has a nontrivial absolutely continuous part.

Geometrically, this is also important. In metric geometry, there is an important quantity called
stable norm
. Given
(M,g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold, the stable norm
\sigma_{g}
is defined as follows. For
h\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}(h):=\inf\{l_{g}(\gamma):\gamma:S^{1}\to M,[\gamma]=h\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\},

where
l_{g}(\gamma)
is the length of the curve
\gamma
. For
h\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, we can also write

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}(h):=\inf\{l_{g}(\gamma)/n:\gamma:S^{1}\to M,n\in\mathbb{N},[\gamma]=nh\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\}.

Due to the convexity and positively 1-homogenity of
\sigma_{g}
on
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})
, we have the unique continuous extension on
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}(h):=\lim_{r\to h,r\in H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Q})}\sigma_{g}(r).

where
l_{g}(\gamma)
is the length of the curve
\gamma
.

When treating one half the Riemannian metric as the Lagrangian, its Euler-Langrange flow is the same as the geodesic flow. We can also introduce
\beta
-function for Riemannian metrics. The relation of the stable norm
\sigma
to the
\beta
is
\beta=\sigma_{g}^{2}.
With this identification, we also introduce the
dual stable norm

\sigma_{g}^{*}(c):=\max_{\sigma_{g}(h)=1}\langle c,h\rangle.

The stable norm
\sigma_{g}
has positively
1
-homogeneity, i.e.
\sigma_{g}(\lambda h)=|\lambda|\sigma_{g}(h)
. Thus, it is only interesting to study a level set of
\sigma_{g}
and dually a level set of
\sigma_{g}^{*}
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that for each
g\in\mathcal{G},
the Mather set
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}_{h}
for all
h\in\mathbb{RP}^{1}
is uniformly hyperbolic, then there is a residual set
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{G}
such that for each
g\in\mathcal{R}
, the level set
(\sigma_{g}^{*})^{-1}(1/2)
as a radial graph over the unit circle is purely singularly continuous.

There is a generalization of the theory of twist maps to the setting of minimal hypersurfaces in torus by Moser (
[
29
]
) and Bangert (
[
13
]
). There is an analogue of Theorem
1.2
in
[
9
]
. We expect that there should be also a generalization of our main theorem to this setting.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide some preliminary of the Aubry-Mather theory on twist maps and the definitions of Mather set, Aubry set, etc. In Section
3
, we give the proof of the main theorems
1.5
,
1.7
,
1.9
on the completeness of the devil’s staircase. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.8
on the incompleteness of devil’s staircase in the presence of KAM circles. Finally, we have two appendices containing materials on convex analysis and definition of twist maps.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some preliminaries on the Aubry-Mather theory of twist maps and Mather’s generalization to general Tonelli Lagrangian systems.

2.1.
The Aubry-Mather theory on twist maps

We do not use the definition of a twist map directly so we put it in Appendix
B
. Instead, we invoke the following theorem relating a twist map to a Tonelli Hamiltonian system.

Theorem 2.1

(Moser,
[
30
]
)
.

Given a twist map
\Gamma
on
T^{*}\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, there is a Tonelli Hamiltonian
H(t,x,p):\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
whose time 1-map of the Hamiltonian flow is
\Gamma
.

Let us state the main result of the Aubry-Mather theory for twist maps. First, it was proved that each minimal configuration has a well-defined rotation number
\varrho=\lim\frac{x_{n}}{n}
(see
[
2
,
23
,
25
,
12
]
). The converse is also true: every real number is the rotation number of a minimal configuration.
This means that each minimal configuration of atoms has a well-defined mean distance, that can be rational or irrational. It becomes particularly interesting when the mean distance is irrational, in which case, the locations of the atoms become incommensurate with the periodic potential.

Let
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathscr{Q}
be the set of minimal configurations, we denote

(1)

by
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}:=\{\bm{x}\in\mathscr{Q}:\lim_{n}\frac{x_{n}}{n}=\varrho\}
the set of minimal configurations with rotation number
\varrho
,

(2)

and by
\mathbf{Q}_{\varrho}:=\{\{\ldots,(y_{i},x_{i}),\ldots\}:\bm{x}\in\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}\}
the union of the associated orbits of the twist map for each configuration in
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Classification of minimal configurations)
.

(1)

(
Irrational rotation number
)
If the rotation number
\varrho
is irrational, then

(a)

either
\mathbf{Q}_{\varrho}
is an invariant circle,

(b)

or
\mathbf{Q}_{\varrho}
is a Cantorus, with possibly a set of nonrecurrent orbits.

(2)

(
Rational rotation number
)
If the rotation number
\varrho
is rational, then

(a)

either
\mathbf{Q}_{\varrho}
is an invariant circle consisting of periodic orbits,

(b)

or
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}=\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}\cup\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{+}}\cup\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{-}}
disjoint union:

(i)

The set
\mathbf{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}
consists of periodic orbits and there are gaps of
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}
on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

The set
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{+}}

(
respectively
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{-}})
consists of configurations lying in the gaps approaching the upper
(
respectively lower
)
configuration
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}
in the future, and the lower
(
respectively upper
)
configuration
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}
in the past.

(3)

(
Total order property
)

(a)

In case
(1)
and
(2.a)
, the set
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho}
is totally ordered on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

In case
(2.b)
, each of the sets
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}\cup\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{+}}
and
\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{0}}\cup\mathscr{Q}_{\varrho^{-}}
is totally ordered.

2.2.
The variational theory of Hamiltonian dynamics

For a closed smooth Riemannian manifold
M
and any
x\in M
,
T_{x}M
and
T_{x}^{*}M
are denoted by the tangent and cotangent spaces of
M
at
x
, respectively.
TM
and
T^{*}M
are the tangent and cotangent bundle of
M
, respectively. Moreover, we consider the
m
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{m}\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}/\mathbb{Z}^{m}
(especially
m=1,2
) as a prototypical example in the following.

A
C^{2}
function
H:\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a
Tonelli Hamiltonian
, if it satisfies the following assumptions:

(H1)

the Hessian
D_{pp}^{2}H(t,x,p)
is positively definite for all
(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}M
;

(H2)

\theta_{0}(|p|_{x})-c_{0}\leqslant H(t,x,p)\leqslant\theta_{1}(|p|_{x})+c_{1}
for all
(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}M
, where
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
are two superlinear functions and
c_{0},c_{1}>0
.

A
Lagrangian

L:\mathbb{T}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
associated to Tonelli Hamiltonian
H
is determined through the Legendre transformation (convex conjugate)

\displaystyle L(t,x,v):=\max_{p\in T_{x}^{*}M}\left\{\langle p,v\rangle-H(t,x,p)\right\}.

It is not hard to check that,
L
is of class
C^{2}
and also Tonelli:

(L1)

the Hessian
D_{vv}^{2}L(t,x,v)
is positively definite for all
(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{T}\times TM
;

(L2)

\theta_{1}^{*}(|v|_{x})-c_{1}\leqslant L(t,x,v)\leqslant\theta_{0}^{*}(|v|_{x})+c_{0}
for all
(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{T}\times TM
, where
\theta_{0}^{*},\theta_{1}^{*}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
are the convex conjugate of
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{1}
, respectively.

Given an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
, the action of
\gamma
is defined by

\displaystyle S(\gamma):=\int_{a}^{b}L(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\,dt.

Hereafter, we shall use
\Phi_{L}
(resp.
\Phi_{H}
) to denote the Lagrangian (Hamiltonian) flow induced by Euler-Lagrange (Hamilton) equation. In this case,
\Phi_{L}^{t}(s,x,v)=(s+t,d\gamma(t;s,x,v))
, where
\gamma
is the solution to Euler-Lagrangian equation with initial value
d\gamma(s)=(x,v)
. This is analogous to Hamiltonian flow
\Phi_{H}
. Sometimes, without causing confusion, we tend to view
\Phi_{L}
(
\Phi_{H}
) as a flow acting on the (co)tangent bundle.

2.2.1.
(Co)homology approach:
\alpha
and
\beta
-function

We denote
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
as the first de Rham cohomology cohomology group of
M
. As for the case of
M=\mathbb{T}^{m}
, we have
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{m},\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The
\alpha
-function
is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(c):=-\min_{\mu\in\mathfrak{M}_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times TM}L(t,x,v)-\eta_{c}(v)\,d\mu,\,\,\,\,\forall[\eta_{c}]=c\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathfrak{M}_{L}
is all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{T}\times TM
invariant by the Euler-Lagrange flow
\Phi_{L}
.

In the case of Tonelli Lagrangian
L
, one can obtain that
\alpha
-function is convex, finite everywhere with superlinear growth. Therefore, the Legendre transformation generates a function
\beta:H_{1}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\beta(h):=\max_{c\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})}\left\{\langle h,c\rangle-\alpha(c)\right\},

which is also finite everywhere and convex with superlinear growth, called
\beta
-function
. There is a direct and intrinsic way to define the
\beta
-function. Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the canonical pairing between (de Rham) cohomology and homology.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
27
]
)
.

Assume that
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}_{L}
and
[\eta]=c\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

(1)

If
\eta
is exact, i.e.,
[\eta]=0
, then
\int_{TM}\eta\,d\mu=0
;

(2)

There exists
\rho(\mu)\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
(called the rotation vector of
\mu
) such that

\displaystyle\langle[\eta],\rho(\mu)\rangle=\int_{TM}\eta\,d\mu

for every
\eta
is a closed 1-form on
M

(3)

4.1.

4.1.1.
Some facts on KAM theory

Consider the nearly integrable Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, which has the form

(4.1)

\displaystyle H(t,x,p):=H_{0}(p)+\varepsilon H_{1}(t,x,p).

This is the small perturbation of an integrable system: when
\varepsilon=0
, the system is integrable for Hamiltonian
H_{0}
and all orbits satisfy
\dot{x}=DH_{0}(p)=:\omega(p)
, thus are linear rotation
\gamma(t)=\gamma(0)+\omega(p)t
with rotation vector
\rho(\gamma)=\omega(p)
on
\mathbb{T}^{m}
.

A vector (number)
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called a (
(C,\tau)
-)
Diophantine vector (number)
, if there exist
C>0
and
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle|\langle\omega,\mathbf{k}\rangle+l|\geqslant\frac{C}{|\mathbf{k}|^{\tau}}\,\,\,\,\forall\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{0\},l\in\mathbb{Z}.

The set of all
(C,\tau)
-Diophantine vectors (numbers) is denoted by
D(C,\tau)
.

We state the following version of the KAM theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given rotation vector
\omega_{0}=DH_{0}(p_{0})\in D(C,\tau)
, suppose that the Hessian is not degenerate at
p_{0}
:
\det(D^{2}H_{0}(p_{0}))\neq 0
, then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that when

\|\varepsilon H_{1}\|_{C^{r}(\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{m})}<\varepsilon_{0}
for sufficiently large
r\in\mathbb{N}
(depending on
m
),
the Hamiltonian flow
\Phi_{H}
admits an invariant torus
\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}\subset\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{m}
and
\Phi_{H}|_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}}
can be conjugated to the linear rotation with rotation vector
\omega_{0}
on
\mathbb{T}^{m}
. More precisely, we can find a diffeomorphism
\Psi_{\omega_{0}}:\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}\to\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{m}(\times\{p_{0}\})
fulfilling

\displaystyle\Psi_{\omega_{0}}\circ\Phi_{H}^{t}|_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}}\circ[\Psi_{\omega_{0}}]^{-1}(s,x)=(s+t,x+t\omega_{0}),\,\,\,\,\forall(s,x)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{m},\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Such invariant torus
\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}
is called a
KAM torus
with rotation vector
\omega_{0}
. Recall that for Tonelli Hamiltonian systems, each KAM torus is the Mather set associated to some corresponding cohomology, which means
\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}
is a Lipschitz graph. This implies that the inverse of canonical projection
\pi|_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}}^{-1}:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{m}\to\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}\subset\mathbb{T}\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{m}
is well-defined. Consequently,
\varphi_{\omega_{0}}:=\Psi_{\omega_{0}}\circ\pi|_{\mathcal{T}_{\omega_{0}}}^{-1}
is a diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
.

Given a Diophantine vector
h
and assume that
\tilde{\mathscr{M}}_{h}
is a KAM torus. Let
\hat{H}_{h}:=\varphi_{h}^{*}H
be the pull-back of Hamiltonian
H
of the canonical transformation
\varphi_{h}
and
\hat{L}_{h}
be the Lagrangian associated to
\hat{H}_{h}
.
Since for
x\in\mathbb{T}^{m}
,
\gamma_{x}(t):=x+th
is the solution of Euler-Lagrange flow
\Phi_{\hat{L}_{h}}
. It indicates that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\partial\hat{L}_{h}}{\partial v}(t,x+th,h)\right]=\frac{\partial\hat{L}_{h}}{\partial x}(t,x+th,h).

Due to the fact that
h
is Diophantine,
\gamma_{x}
is dense in
\mathbb{T}^{m}
, which means on KAM torus with rotation vector
h
,

(1)

\hat{L}_{h}(t,\cdot,h)
is a constant and we denote by
\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)
for short;

(2)

\frac{\partial\hat{L}_{h}}{\partial v}(\cdot,\cdot,h)
is a constant map from
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, which is denoted by
\eta_{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Note that the conjugate transformation
\Psi_{h}
in KAM theorem is obtained by a sequence of compositions of exact symplectic transformations defined on smaller and smaller neighborhoods of the KAM torus. If we denote
\hat{\beta}_{h}
by the the
\beta
-function associated to
\hat{L}_{h}
, then
\hat{\beta}_{h}=\beta
due to Bernard (
[
15
]
).

4.1.2.
KAM torus causes strong convexity of
\beta
-functions

In the following, we focus on the case
m=1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
h\in D(C,\tau)
, there exist
\delta_{h},c_{h}>0
, such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\beta(\rho)-\beta(h)-D\beta(h)(\rho-h)\geqslant c_{h}(\rho-h)^{2},\,\,\,\,\rho\in B(h,\delta_{h}).

Proof.

Since
\hat{\beta}_{h}=\beta
, we will focus on the analysis on Lagrangian
\hat{L}_{h}
. Because
\hat{L}_{h}
has a positively definite Hessian with respect to component
v
, there exist
C_{h}>c_{h}>0
, satisfying for all
|w|<1
,

\displaystyle c_{h}|w|^{2}\leqslant\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{L}_{h}}{\partial v^{2}}(t,x,h+w)(w,w)\leqslant C_{h}|w|^{2}.

According to Taylor’s expansion of
\hat{L}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{h}(t,x,h+w)=\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)+\eta_{h}(w)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{L}}{\partial v^{2}}(t,x,h+\theta w)(w,w),\,\,\,\,\theta\in[0,1],

we have the following estimate

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)+\eta_{h}(w)+\frac{c_{h}}{2}|w|^{2}\leqslant\hat{L}_{h}(t,x,h+w)\leqslant\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)+\eta_{h}(w)+\frac{C_{h}}{2}|w|^{2}

Assume that
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}
is a closed global minimal curve with rotation number
\rho(\gamma)=p/q
, and denote
\tilde{\gamma}
as the lift of curve
\gamma
from
\mathbb{T}
to
\mathbb{R}
as in the previous text. Then
\tilde{\gamma}(t+q)=\tilde{\gamma}(t)+p
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By choosing the appropriate
p/q
, we let
p/q\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfy
|p/q-h|<\delta
.

Since
d\gamma\subset\tilde{\mathscr{N}}(p/q)
, when
|p/q-h|<\delta(1,h)
,
\tilde{\mathscr{N}}(p/q)\subset B(\tilde{\mathscr{N}}(h),1)
due to the upper-semicontinuity of Mañé sets. The comparison of the average actions of
\tilde{\gamma}
on
[0,q]
can be obtained by (
4.3
) from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{q}\hat{L}_{h}(t,\tilde{\gamma}(t),\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{q}\left[\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)+\eta_{h}(\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(t)-h)+\frac{c_{h}}{2}|\dot{\tilde{\gamma}}(t)-h|^{2}\right]\,dt

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{q}\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)\,dt+\eta_{h}\left(p/q-h\right)+\frac{c_{h}}{2}\left|p/q-h\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)\,dt+\eta_{h}\left(p/q-h\right)+\frac{c_{h}}{2}\left|p/q-h\right|^{2}

where the last inequality and equality hold by Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
\hat{L}_{h}
is an 1-time periodic Lagrangian. Taking minimum over all global minimal curves with rotation vector
p/q
on the left hand side of the above inequality, we can get

(4.4)

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{h}\left(p/q\right)\geqslant\int_{0}^{1}\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)\,dt+\eta_{h}\left(p/q-h\right)+\frac{c_{h}}{2}\left|p/q-h\right|^{2}=:\hat{L}_{h}^{-}\left(p/q\right).

In addition, after taking
\tilde{\gamma}_{0}(t):=x+p/qt
, we have for
|p/q-h|<\delta
,

\displaystyle\hat{\beta}_{h}\left(p/q\right)\leqslant\frac{1}{q}S(\tilde{\gamma}_{0}|_{[0,q]})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q}\int_{0}^{q}\hat{L}_{h}\left(t,x+p/qt,p/q\right)\,dt

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)\,dt+\eta_{h}(p/q-h)+\frac{C_{h}}{2}\left|p/q-h\right|^{2}=:\hat{L}_{h}^{+}\left(p/q\right).

Together with (
4.4
)-(
4.1.2
) and the continuity of
\hat{\beta}
and
\hat{L}^{\pm}
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{h}^{-}(\rho)\leqslant\hat{\beta}_{h}(\rho)\leqslant\hat{L}_{h}^{+}(\rho),\,\,\,\,\forall|\rho-h|<\delta.

Let
\rho=h
, we have
\hat{\beta}_{h}(h)=\int_{0}^{1}\hat{L}_{h}(t,h)\,dt
. Furthermore, (
4.6
) implies

and hence (
4.2

\hat{\beta}_{h}=\beta

∎

4.2.
Incomplete devil’s staircase

The following result indicates that if a convex function has strong convexity on a certain positive measure set as in the previous theorem, then the differential of its convex conjugate as a continuous function has a non-trivial absolutely continuous part. Theorem
1.8
then follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
\phi
is a strictly convex function on
\mathbb{R}
and
\psi
is its convex conjugate. Given
P\subset\mathbb{R}
with positive Lebesgue measure, if for any
x_{0}\in P
, there exist
c_{x_{0}},\delta_{x_{0}}>0
such that when
x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0}})
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\phi(x)-\phi(x_{0})-y_{0}(x-x_{0})\geqslant c_{x_{0}}|x-x_{0}|^{2},\,\,\,\,\forall y_{0}\in D^{-}\phi(x_{0}),

then
D\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(see Appendix
A
) has a Lipschitz part on a positive Lebesgue measure set and thus is an incomplete devil’s staircase.

Proof.

First we set
y_{0}\in(D\psi)^{-1}(P)
, then
x_{0}:=D\psi(y_{0})\in P
and
y_{0}\in D^{-}\phi(x_{0})
. Due to the continuity of
D\psi
, we can find some
\varepsilon>0
(depending on
x_{0}
in general), such that if
|y-y_{0}|<\varepsilon
,
|D\psi(y)-D\psi(y_{0})|<\delta_{x_{0}}
. Let
x=D\psi(y)
, then
x\in B(x_{0},\delta_{x_{0}})
. In view of (
4.7
), we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle c_{x_{0}}|x-x_{0}|^{2}\leqslant(y-y_{0})(x-x_{0})\leqslant|y-y_{0}||x-x_{0}|

by (
A.1
). Recall that
D\psi(y)=x
,
D\psi(y_{0})=x_{0}
. When
x\neq x_{0}
, (
4.8
) gives that for
y\in B(y_{0},\varepsilon)
,

\displaystyle|D\psi(y)-D\psi(y_{0})|\leqslant\frac{1}{c_{x_{0}}}|y-y_{0}|.

As a result, for any
y_{0}\in(D\psi)^{-1}(P)
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\limsup_{y\to y_{0}}\frac{|D\psi(y)-D\psi(y_{0})|}{|y-y_{0}|}<\infty.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, define the set

\displaystyle E_{k}:=\left\{y\in(D\psi)^{-1}(P):\forall y^{\prime}\in(D\psi)^{-1}(P)\text{ with }0<|y-y^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{k},\frac{|D\psi(y)-D\psi(y_{0})|}{|y-y_{0}|}\leqslant k\right\}.

(
4.9
) indicates that
(D\psi)^{-1}(P)=\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}
. Thus,

\displaystyle P=D\psi\left((D\psi)^{-1}(P)\right)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}D\psi(E_{k}).

Since
\mathscr{L}(P)>0
by assumption, there exists at least one
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathscr{L}(D\psi(E_{k_{0}}))>0
.

Moreover,
D\psi
is (locally) Lipschitz on
E_{k}\subset(D\psi)^{-1}(P)
: when
|y^{\prime}-y|<1/k
,
|D\psi(y^{\prime})-D\psi(y)|\leqslant k|y^{\prime}-y|
; if
|y^{\prime}-y|\geqslant 1/k
,
|D\psi(y^{\prime})-D\psi(y)|\leqslant 2B\leqslant 2Bk|y^{\prime}-y|
, where
B
is a local upper bound of
|D\psi|
. According to Rademacher theorem and the convexity of
\psi
,
D^{2}\psi\geqslant 0
exists a.e. on
E_{k}
.

In addition, area formula (see
[
18
]
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{E_{k_{0}}}D^{2}\psi(y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{\#}\left(E_{k_{0}}\cap(D\psi)^{-1}(x)\right)\,dx\geqslant\mathscr{L}(D\psi(E_{k_{0}}))>0,

which means
D^{2}\psi
does not vanish on a subset of
E_{k_{0}}
with positive Lebesgue measure.
∎

Appendix A
Some convex analysis

Some relevant basic notions about convex analysis are listed below. For the following and more, readers can refer to
[
17
,
20
]
.

•

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval. A continuous function
\phi:I\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
convex function
if for any
x\in I
there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(A.1)

\phi(x^{\prime})\geqslant\phi(x)+y(x^{\prime}-x),\qquad\forall x^{\prime}\in I,

and we denote as
\phi\in\mathrm{Cov}\,(I)
. The set of
y
satisfying (
A.1
) is called the
subdifferential of
\phi
at
x
and we denote it by
D^{-}\phi(x)
.

•

If
\phi\in\mathrm{Cov}\,(I)
, then
\phi
is locally Lipschitz on
I
, thus it is differentiable almost everywhere (in the sense of Lebesgue measure). Moreover,
D^{-}\phi(x)
is a non-empty, compact and convex set for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
D^{-}\phi(x)
is a singleton if and only if
\phi
is differentiable at
x
. In this case,
D^{-}\phi(x)=\{D\phi(x)\}
.

•

The
left
and
right derivative
of
\phi

(A.2)

\displaystyle\phi_{-}^{\prime}(x):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(x-t)}{t},\,\,\,\,\phi_{+}^{\prime}(x):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x)}{t}

exist at all
x\in I
and
D^{-}\phi(x)=[\phi_{-}^{\prime}(x),\phi_{+}^{\prime}(x)]
.

•

As a set-valued map,
x\rightsquigarrow D^{-}\phi(x)
is upper-semicontinuous, i.e., both
x_{n}\to x
and
D^{-}\phi(x_{n})\ni y_{n}\to y
as
n\to\infty
imply
y\in D^{-}\phi(x)
. Therefore, if
\phi
is differentiable on
I
,
\phi\in C^{1}(I)
.

•

For
\phi\in\mathrm{Cov}\,(I)
, its
convex conjugate

\displaystyle\psi(y):=\sup\{yx-\phi(x):x\in I\}

is also convex and
y\in D^{-}\phi(x)
if and only if
x\in D^{-}\psi(y)
if only if
\phi(x)+\psi(y)=xy
. Furthermore, if
\phi
is strictly convex, i.e., only the strict inequality holds in (
A.1
) when
x^{\prime}\neq x
, then
D^{-}\psi
is singleton everywhere and thus
\psi
is of class
C^{1}
.

Appendix B
Twist maps

We provide the definition of twist maps here, though we do not use it in the main body of the paper.

Definition B.1

(Twist map)
.

A map
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{x},\Gamma_{p})\in C^{1}(T^{*}\mathbb{T},T^{*}\mathbb{T})
is said to be an
area-preserving exact twist map
(twist map for short), if it satisfies the following conditions

(1)

(area-preserving)
\Gamma^{*}\omega=\omega
, where
\omega:=dp\wedge dx
;

(2)

(twist condition) there exists some
b>0
,
\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\Gamma_{x}(x,p)\geqslant b
;

(3)

(exactness)
\Gamma^{*}\lambda_{0}-\lambda_{0}
is exact, where
\lambda_{0}:=pdx
.

Theorem B.2
.

There exists a generating function
h(x,x^{\prime})
satisfying

(1)

h(x+1,x^{\prime}+1)=h(x+1,x^{\prime}+1)
,
x\in\mathbb{T}
;

(2)

\partial_{12}^{2}h\leqslant-b^{-1}<0
;

(3)

dh(x,x^{\prime})=p^{\prime}dx^{\prime}-pdx
, where
(x^{\prime},p^{\prime})=\Gamma(x,p)
