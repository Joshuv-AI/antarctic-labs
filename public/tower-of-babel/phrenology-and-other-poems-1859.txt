POEM  ON  PHRENOLOGY.

1  am  a  Phrenologist,  and  can  tell  what  you  are  good  for  ;

I  am  a  Physiognomist,  and  can  tell  your  temper  by  your  features  ;

I  am  a  Physiologist,  and  can  tell  your  motive  powers,

How  they  are  sustained,  and  how  and  where  they  will  lead  you.

I  see  all  kinds  of  heads,  from  the  beetle  he^d  up  to  the  god-head  ;

All  kinds  of  faces,  from  the  monkey-face  up  to  the  god-and-man  face  ;

All  kinds  of  expressions,  from  the  no-meaning  up  to  the  all-meaning —

From  low  Individualities  up  to  true  Personalities;

They  all  stand  for  something,  even  the  stupidest  of  them,

Each  one  is  the  seat  of  an  immortal  soul  of  priceless  worth,

Each  one  contains  what  has  taxed  all  the  infinite  variety

Of  parental  powers  of  father  God  and  mother  Earth  to  produce  |

Each  one  has  drawn  from  the  infinite  variety  of  the

Concentrated  essences  of  all  things,  and  manifests  what  it  has.

There  is  every  variety  of  grade  of  good,  better,  and  best ;

Each  has  a  purpose  and  use,  and  stands  for  more  than  is  known  of  j

Each  one  is  of  equal  value  in  the  eye  of  the  Highest,

From  whose  grasp  no  one  can  get  loose,  or  be  lost ;

Each  one  has  responsibilities  according  to  gifts  ;

Each  one  has  what  he  wants,  or  shall  have,  sometime,

More  certainly  than  the  superficial  are  mindful  of.

Such  as  have  suitable  garments  and  other  embellishments'

May  go  up  and  feast  with  the  Master  of  the  House —

Such  as  have  not,  may  ivait  and  consider  in  lower  rooms.

You  shall  be  called  and  shall  go  up  as  your  turns  come,

Wait  hopefully  and  you  shall  be  sooner  or  later  comforted,

You  may  be  as  certain  of  that  as  of  any  other  certainty.

Only  be  careful  that  you  do  not  have  to  wait  too  long,

•  AMATIVE.

Then  you  of  the  amative  and  social  complexion  and  expression,

Who  love  society,  and  love  to  make  it  worth  loving  and  living  for,

Who  can  fall  wholly  into  love,  and  so  see  all  things  lovely,

Can  love  one  first  and  better  than  any  or  all  others,

And  can  so  see  all  the  world  wear  a  solemn  aspect  of  beauty.

See  in  the  house  of  the  loved  one  a  Holy  Temple,

Hear  a  holy  and  beautiful  spirit  speak  out  of  all  things —

Speak  from  the  walls,  pictures,  musical  instruments,  chairs,  and  tables,

Hear  it  by  the  way-side  and  over  all  the  landscape.

Hear  the  grass,  leaves,  flowers,  and  birds  whisper  of  immortality.
Who  can  see  everywhere  something  lovable  and  so  to  be  loved,
Can  be  so  inspired,  and  speak  beautiful  thoughts  in  beautiful  words,
Can  dream  of  all  sufficient  joys  and  live  them  over  and  over  again.
Can  float  away  on  moonbeams  and  listen  to  the  stars,
Can  fly  in  spirit  to  Elysian  fields  and  gardens,
Whilst  the  body  unconsciously  walks  or  rests  by  the  way-side,
Can  be  speechless  yet  say  the  most  unutterable  things,
Can  be  silent  from  fulness  of  joy  in  your  love  of  such,
Can  enjoy  that,  and  after  that  stand  alone  in  the  world,
Knowing  your  tears  disturb  no  hilarities  or  festivals,
Still  a  flower  in  God's  garden,  giving  and  receiving  fragrance.
Who  have  hearts  that  may  be  wrung  without  breaking,
Hearts  beating  manliness  inside,  with  God  over  and  under  all.
Who  can  love  all  your  relationships  above,  around,  and  below,
Love  your  wives  and  children  intensely  and  forever.
You  young  men  and  women  who  are  vigorous  and  healthy,
Have  holy  connubial  attachments,  and  gratify  them,
Who  are  uncontrollable  apart,  and  harmonious  together.
Who  can  produce  beautiful  and  healthy  sons  and  daughters,
Who  can  make  the  earth  flowery,  fruitful,  and  blessed —
You  are  welcome,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  never  fall  into  love,  and  to  whom  nothing  appears  lovely.
Who  see  no  aspect  of  holiness  or  beauty  worn  anywhere,
Who  never  hear  any  voices  from  the  grass,  leaves,  or  flowers.
Who  know  no  higher  gospel  than  that  of  the  market-place,
Who  feel  no  higher  inspiration  than  comes  of  dollars  and  cents.
Who  look  for  all-sufficient  joys  and  blessedness  from  such,
See  nothing  in  the  stars  but  lights  for  the  night.
See  nothing  inapplicable  to  the  multiplication  table.
Love  no  ciphering  but  addition  and  multiplication,
Whose  hearts  beat  high  or  low  according  to  price-currents,
Who  love  your  wives  and  children  on  profit-and-lo«s  principles  j
You  of  the  selfish  type,  with  only  boreal  heat,
No  open  arms  or  souls  for  imperishable  goods.
Or  sympathy  for  such  as  love  and  cherish  them —

You  may  wait  till  your  heart-beats  are  warmer  and  truer.  *

PHILOPROGENITIVE.

You  of  the  child-loving,  generous,  and  sympathetic  face.
Face  welcome  as  spring  suns,  June  showers,  and  fair  weather,
Welcome  as  rest  after  sickness,  pain  or  hard  work,
Good  to  look  upon  as  rosy  sunsets  after  a  storm,
Pleasant  and  promising  as  banks  of  violets  in  early  spring.
Soothing  as  the  soft  harmonies  of  a  lover's  serenade,
Encouraging  as  health,  plentiful  crops,  and  good  markets.

Loving  and  prophetic  of  universal  love  and  joy  for  ail,
Parental,  tolerant,  kind— loves  everything  and  everybody,
Tender,  indulgent,  and  lifegiving  to  all  souls.
Friendly — has  a  kind  word  for  all  comers  and  goers —
"Wearing  an  always-alike,  every-day-holy  face —
You  are  acceptable,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  love  your  neighbors,  your  wives  and  children  for  the  money  they  bring,
You  whose  faces  are  expressive  of  night,  and  foul  weather,
Are  true  pictures  of  selfishness,  vexation,  and  avarice,
Who  are  unsociable,  and  grievous  as  misanthropes  and  dyspeptics,  •
"Whose  souls  are  as  unmusical  as  a  bear  with  a  sore  head.
As  unwelcome  as  the  cholera,  or  a  frost  in  July,
As  destructive  to  harmony  as  hail-storms  to  flower  gardens,
As  intolerant  and  stupefying  as  a  bigot's  creed  and  gospel.
As  unindulgent  as  a  tyrant's  religion  and  code  of  laws.
Acknowledging  no  individual  or  collective  natural  rights,
Only  the  divine  rights  of  kings  and  priests —
You  must  wait  with  your  calamities.

ADHESIVE.

You  with  friendly  proclivities,  and  strong  adhesiveness.
Strong  social  feeling,  and  love  and  regard  for  society,
Always  ready  to  enjoy  yourselves  and  help  others  to  enjoyments,
Never  deceptive,  but  always  showing  and  acting  just  what  you  aie,
Beautiful-souled,  warm-hearted,  and  pity-stored,
Ready  with  a  helping  hand  to  raise  the  feeble  and  the  fallen.
Forgiving,  tender,  sympathetic  and  indulgent.
Ready  to  share  the  world's  burdens  and  help  reform  its  errors,
Falling  into  love  easily,  and  never  ready  to  fall  out.
Always  sticking  by  through  good  or  evil,  light  or  darkness.
Never  like  shadows  stopping  only  while  the  sun  shines.
Ready  to  live  or  die,  to  spend  or  be  spent  for  a  friend —
You  are  welcome  as  any,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  love  only  self,  and  always  wear  a  stingy  aspect,
"Whose  looks  are  fair  or  foul,  as  stocks  go  up  or  down.
Unsocial,  heartless,  and  cold  as  a  glacial  iceberg.
Caring  for  no  others,  and  by  no  others  cared  for,
"Who  go  through  the  streets  like  things  without  souls.
Go  about  muttering  and  rehearsing  your  grab  games —
You  must  wait  till  you  come  to  life.

INHABITIVE.

You  who  have  great  attachment  to  home  and  its  surroundings,
Think  it  the  dearest,  sweetest,  holiest  spot  on  earth.
Feel  homesick,  restless,  anxious,  miserable  when  away.

6

l)islike  to  be  always  roving  and  changing  places,
But  love  your  home  and  country  with  an  ardent  love
Such  as  can  never  be  quenched,  smothered  or  buried,
Are  patriotic  and  ready  to  fight  for  the  right  against  any  odds,
Fight  for  freedom,  honor,  fame,  justice,  truth,  righteousness,
Fight  for  equal  rights,  and  equal  chances  for  all  to  obtain  them
Against  all  usurpation,  tyranny,  monopoly  and  robbery.
Against  the  rich  robbing  the  poor,  and  degrading  woman,
Against  rumselling,  gambling,  banks,  and  corporations.
Against  slavery,  and  all  that  advocates,  tolerates  and  supports  it —
Vou  are  as  good  as  any,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  have  no  attachments^  and  care  for  none,
Who  have  no  love  of  home,  and  are  careless  of  its  surroundings,
Rather  loaf  in  taverns,  stores,  shops,  or  high-ways.
Love  to  be  always  roving,  gossiping,  and  changing  places,
Care  only  for  your  own  rights,  and  what  you  can  make  of  them.
Love  tyranny,  monopoly,  robbery  and  what  comes  of  such.
Like  one  place  no  better  than  another,  and  no  one  over  right,
Care  not  where  you  are,  or  how  soon  you  go,  or  how  fast
You  may  count  upon  going,  any  way  but  up  at  present.

CONCENTRATIVE.

You  who  like  variety,  change  of  thought,  feeling,  and  occupation.
Who  are  not  confused,  worried  and  disturbed  by  such  things,
Who  combine  mechanic,  farmer,  philosopher,  and  prer.cher.
Can  be  merchant,  pedler,  auctioneer,  congressman,  pr.\Mdent,
Can  be  lawyer,  doctor,  judge,  sculptor,  painter,  and  the  vest.
Can  see  readily  from  causes  to  effects  the  shortest  way,
Can  see  ways  to  Heaven  without  going  through  Egypt,
Can  fall,  yet  come  upon  your  feet,  and  try  again,  and  again  -v»
You  are  round  and  whole,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  can  see,  think  of,  and  manage  to  do  but  one  thing.
Who  can  never  see  any  way  from  cause  to  effect,
Who,  if  you  fall,  must  lay  there  till  you  are  picked  up.
Who  have  no  self-reliance,  but  must  always  lean  upon  others.
Who  are  always,  when  out  of  the  old  track,  out  of  soundings,
Who- are  shoemaker,  or  hatter,  or  tailor,  and  nothing  more —
You  are  flat  and  fractional,  and  must  wait.

VIVATIVE.

You  who  are  vivative,  love  life,  and  have  no  fears  of  death,
Know  what  it  is  to  live,  or  to  die,  and  are  ready  for  either.
Willing  to  go,  or  willing  to  stay  and  meet  the  consequences,
Having  no  fear  of  wrath,  or  fire,  or  annihilation.
Attached  to  life,  here  and  elsewhere,  now  and  forever,

Desiring  it  for  the  love,  pleasure,  and  blessing  it  may  bring,
Loving  it  for  these  luxuries  upon  any  terms,
Accepting  it  willingly  for  its  own  sake,  and  what  it  brings.
Clinging  to  its  immortality  though  poor  and  miserable,
Knowing  how  immortal  you  and  the  rest  are,  and  always  will  be,
How  impossible  is  any  thing  short  of  that  for  you  and  me.
How  all  death  is  but  new  birth  over  and  over  again.
And  each  one  higher,  purer,  nobler,  holier  than  the  last.
Till  all  is  swallowed  up  in  the  victory  of  man,  immortality —
You  are  worthy,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  prize  it  lightly,  and  choke  it  with  useless  fears  and  anxieties,
Who  live  falsely,  and  die  a  thousand  deaths  in  dreading  one.
Know  not  what  it  is  to  live  or  die,  and  are  never  ready  for  either,
Never  willing  to  go  or  stay,  or  meet  the  consequences  of  either,
Always  fearful  of  the  devil,  of  fire,  or  annihilation,
N^ver  knowing  how  inimortal  you  are  and  always  will  be,
How  impossible  it  is  that  even  one  should  be  lost.

Or  that  one  should  fail  of  coming  to  the  knowledge  of  truth  and  godliness-
You  must  wait.

COMBATIVE.

You  who  know  your  nativity,  and  what  it  is  worth  to  you,
Can  manfully  resist  injuries,  insults,  oppressions,  and  wrongs.
Can  make  your  "  bullets  carry  billets  "  that  must  be  read  and  felt,
Can  understand  your  rights,  contend  for  and  gain  them,
Can  uproot  falsehood  and  plant  truth  in  its  place,
Can  tear  the  masks  from  lying  and  deceitful  men,
(Such  as  always  remind  one  of  coiled  and  hissing  serpents,)
Can  fearlessly  encounter  opposition  and  walk  over  it.
Can  be  efiicient  in  all  contests  for  Truth  and  Justice,
Quick  and  resistless  in  resistance  to  falsehood  and  wrong.
Who  love  controversy  and  can  boldly  and  successfully  meet  it,
Who  can  be  mad  enough  to  choke  or  knife  your  enemy,
(One  who  has  deceived,  injured,  and  insulted  you,)
Yet,  like  the  Indian,  spare  him  because  you  would  watch  him —
Spare  him  because  you  would  not  have  him  with  your  friends  out  of  sight-
Who  can  forgive  injuries,  if  so  you  can  forgive  yourselves,
Who  can  contend  for  the  truth,  and  declare  its  rights,  "  live  or  die  "—
You  are  worthy,  and  may  go  up.

You  who  tremble  at  thunder  and  guns,  are  pale  and  inefficient,
Who  wear  masks  over  lying,  little  and  deceitful  souls,
Who  speak  words  that  are  false,  and  wear  expressions  of  lies.
Who  show  false  faces,  and  make  false  professions  and  promises,
Who  are  always  fearful  of  discovery,  and  coming  to  justice,
(Do  you  know  any  such,  and  how  it  feels  to  be  stung  by  them?

8

I  do,  and  I  know  how  to  pick  the  stings  out,  and  what  else  to  do  with  them.)
Such  as  have  ugly  eyes  and  faces  and  uglier  souls,
Such  as  have  grinning  faces,  and  next  to  no  souls,
No-meaning,  neuter-gender-faces,  all  hollow  and  false.
Such  as  strive  to  keep  people  in  ifi:norance  and  darkness,
Such  as  misdirect  and  instruct  for  selfish  purposes.
Such  as  are  rich,  yet  have  nothing  to  give,  or  be  taxed  for,
Such  as  work  with  the  devil  for  money,  rather  than  with  God  without  it.
Such  as  labor  to  make  the  world  worse  instead  of  better.
Such  as  are  a  continual  torment  and  vexation  of  spirit, —
You  must  wait,  nature  has  little  use  for  cowards.

DESTRUCTIVE.

You  who  can  feel  lively  indignation  at  wrong  and  outrage.
Can  destroy  or  remove  obstacles  from  your  way.
Can  oppose  a  head  wind,  and  rise  like  a  kite  over  it.
Can  face  and  manage  anything  better  than  a  dead  calm,
Can  face  opposition  without  fear  or  trembling.
Can  face  hardship  and  gain  manhood  and  self-reliance,
Can  face  God,  man,  or  devil,  without  paling  or  fainting,
Can  face  storms  without  flinching  or  quailing,
Can  bide  your  time,  and  then  go  to  your  work  and  do  it.
Can  mark  out  your  course  and  go  straight  through  it.
Can  make  plain  marks  enough  to  show  you  have  been  this  way,
Can  see  your  work,  know  what  it  is,  go  to  it,  and  do  it,
(Unless  all  the  powers  above  and  below  interfere  and  prevent,)
Can  make  tyrants  and  their  minions  tremble,
Can  plunge  thorns  in  the  sides  of  slavery's  Presidents  and  Judges,
Can  shake  the  stupidity  that  needs  shaking  and  waking  up,
Can  shake  the  devil  and  all  his  aiders  and  abettors,
(In  the  church  or  out  of  it,  Sundays  and  all  other  days,)
Can  go  abroad  bare-faced,  upright  and  undisguised,
Can  be  righteously  contrary,  obstinate,  and  difficult  to  manage.
Can  not  be  persuaded  or  forced  to  go  in  wrong  direction,
Can  not  be  made  to  say  or  do  things  just  for  conformity's  sake, —
You  are  welcome  as  any  and  may  go  up.

You  who  never  dare  be  angry,  or  to  show  resentment,

Sure  to  run  and  be  hit  in  the  back,,  if  hit  anywhere.

The  last  in,  and  first  out,  of  anything  more  than  a  sham  fight.

You  who  have  but  one  holy  day  in  seven,  and  desecrate  that,

"Who  go  abroad  scheming,  thinking  yourselves  unknown.

Who  dare  not  face  wrongs  and  remove  them  out  of  the  way,

"Who  have  no  time  or  disposition  to  improve  the  world.

Hedge  yourselves  about  and  vegitate,  rather  than  go  manfully  abroad,

"Who  care  only  to  deceive  or  cheat  a  living,  or  a  fortune  out  of  others,

"Who  can  smile  over  oppressions,  if  only  you  escape  them.

Who  can  go  the  wrong  way  as  patiently  as  any  way,
Who  study  only  conformity  to  trade  and  best  bargains, —
You  are  not  welcome  and  must  wait.

ACQUISITIVE.

You  who  rightly  value  money,  can  get  and  spend  it  freely,
Value  it  not  to  count,  or  hoard,  or  leave  behind,
But  for  what  it  will  purchase  to  carry  and  abide  with  you, —
Knowledge,  Wisdom,  Love;  Friendship  and  Blessedness,
Who  know  the  law  of  nature  is  giving  and  having,
That  misers  are  vastly  poorer  than  such  as  have  nothing,
That  all  stinginess  punishes  itself  at  a  ruinous  rate,
That  no  one  can  hoard  and  keep  without  parting  with  more  than  he  gets,
That  no  one  has  any  natural  right  to  more  than  answers  natural  needs,
That,  when  greater  barns  are  needed  for  storage,  the  soul  is  gone,
That,  thus  and  so,  nature  always  will  have  her  revenge,
That  there  is  a  fixed  law  in  these  matters,  as  with  the  sun  and  stars,
That  no  one  can  be  exempt  from  this,  let  him  fancy  it  as  he  may,
Who  know  you  come  by,  go  by,  and  remain  by  the  laws  of  nature,
Who  are  booked,  and  stand  well  on  the  credit  side  above,
Who  have  internal  goods  enough  to  make  you  always  rich,
You  cannot  be  hindered  and  may  go  up

You  who  care  only  that  ledgers  and  day-books  tell  well

Such  as  buy  and  sell  at  best  bargains,  and  would  be  twice  paid,

Such  as  are  avaricious,  untrustworthy,  and  dishonest,

Such  as  love  diminutive  measures  and  light  weights,

Such  as  would  sell  without  parting  with  the  thing  sold.

Such  as  want  best  bargains  and  always  more  than  the  money's  worth.

Such  as  tiave  hard  faces,  close  lips  and  eyes,  and  impenetrable  ears,

Such  as  have  hard  hearts  and  souls,  given  to  cold  hospitalities,

Such  as  have  one  Idol,  and  no  love  or  worship  for  God  or  men.

Such  as  love  to  beggar  their  neighbors,  and  grab  all  their  goods.

Such  as  take  per  centage  by  day  by  way  of  accommodation,

Such  as  boast  of  their  skill  in  such  sharp  manoeuvering,

I'M  ALL  ALONE,  YET  NOT  ALONE!

Alone  !  I'm  all  alone ! At  home  and  abroad  I'm  all  alone,  yet  not  alone  !
In  the  dwelling-place  of  my  fathers  I  am  an  alien  and  an  outcast ;
Society,  with  "  locked  arms  and  steps,"  closes  up  its  impenetrable  ranks,
And  refuses  admission  to  all  above  the  lower  life  and  law  ;
I  may  starve  or  drown  at  my  leisure,  and  find  it  is  nobody's  business  ;
Such  only  are  accepted  as  work  after  prescribed  patterns  and  formulas.
I  go  to  the  river,  loll  upon  the  banks,  and  watch  the  fishes  and  water  beetles,
