An Optimal Algorithm for Cardinality-Constrained Diameter Partitioning
Chao Xu, Mingdong Yang

Abstract
Cardinality-constrained diameter partitioning asks for a partition of n n items into two classes of prescribed sizes that minimizes the larger of the two class diameters. We give an O ⁡ ( n 2 ) O(n^{2}) algorithm and a matching ⁡ ( n 2 ) \Omega(n^{2}) lower bound if we can only query the weight between two elements. The algorithm computes the optimum for every cardinality simultaneously, improving Avis’s O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n^{2}\log n) . The reduction is to a bottleneck 2-coloring problem on the maximum spanning tree, solved by a standard tree DP. For a single cardinality with Euclidean weights, we obtain a subquadratic-time algorithm in any fixed dimension.



Let
V
be a set of
n
elements with weights

w:\binom{V}{2}\to\mdmathbb{R}
on its unordered pairs, accessed via
unit-cost edge weight queries. Given a target
c\in\{0,\dots,n\}
,

diameter partitioning
asks for a partition

V=V_{1}\dot{\cup}V_{2}
with
|V_{1}|=c
minimizing

\max\{\mathrm{diam}(V_{1}),\mathrm{diam}(V_{2})\},
where

\mathrm{diam}(S)=\max_{uv\in\binom{S}{2}}w(uv)
, with

\mathrm{diam}(S)=-\infty
for
|S|\leq 1
.
w
need not be metric
or nonnegative.

Avis
[
4
]
solved the cardinality-constrained case in

O(n^{2}\log n)
in 1986, using a threshold-graph reduction to subset
sum. Asano et al.
[
3
]
gave
O(n\log n)
for
the unconstrained Euclidean case. Monma and Suri

[
11
]
handled the unconstrained graph case in

O(n^{2})
. The operations-research literature came to an equivalent
formulation independently. Fernández et al.
[
8
]

introduced
maximum dispersion
in 2013. The objective is to
maximize the minimum same-class pair weight. Tran et al.

[
14
]
gave an
O(n^{4})
time algorithm for the
cardinality-constrained 2-class variant,
cardinality-constrained
2-anticlustering with maximum dispersion
(2-MDCC). The two formulations
are actually equivalent: negating weights turns diameter partitioning
into maximum dispersion. Hence Avis’s algorithm already gives

O(n^{2}\log n)
for 2-MDCC. We state results for the diameter
formulation. All results in our paper transfer to 2-MDCC verbatim,
either by negation or by a simple reduction.

Our contribution

1.

We prove an
O(n^{2})
algorithm that computes the optimum diameter
for every cardinality
c\in\{0,\dots,n\}

simultaneously

(
Section

2
). This improves Avis’s
O(n^{2}\log n)
algorithm
by shaving the log factor and producing all cardinalities at once
instead of just one.

2.

We show an
\Omega(n^{2})
lower bound even for a single cardinality
and even when
w
is a metric taking values in
\{1,2\}

(
Theorem

4.1
).

3.

For a single cardinality, we show a subquadratic-time algorithm when
the input is
n
points in
\mdmathbb{R}^{d}
with Euclidean weights
(
Section

3
).

1
Reduction to bottleneck tree 2-coloring

For a graph
G=(V,E)
with edge weights
w
, let

G_{>\lambda}=(V,\{uv\in E:w(uv)>\lambda\})
. A 2-coloring of
V(G)

is
proper
if the endpoints of every edge of
G
receive
different colors. It has cardinality
c
if one of the color class has
cardinality
c
. Avis
[
4
]
showed that when
G

is the complete graph with the weights
w
, the optimum diameter is at
most
\lambda
iff
G_{>\lambda}
admits a proper 2-coloring of
cardinality
c
. From now on in this section,
G
denotes this
complete graph on
V
with edge weights
w
. Binary search over the

\binom{n}{2}
edge weights takes preprocessing time
O(n^{2}\log n)

for sorting. We avoid this by working on a maximum spanning tree (MST)

T
of
G
. The forest
T_{>\lambda}
replaces
G_{>\lambda}
.
Such MST-replacement reductions for diameter-style partitioning problems
are folklore
[
3
,
6
]
,
descending from the MST cycle property
[
7
, Chapter
23]
.

Fix an MST
T
of
G
and a proper 2-coloring
\chi:V\to\{0,1\}

of
T
. For a 2-coloring
\varphi:V\to\{0,1\}
, write

\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)=\max\{\mathrm{diam}(\varphi^{-1}(0)),\mathrm{diam}(\varphi^{-1}(1))\}

for the
partition diameter
under
\varphi
, and

M(\varphi)=\max\{w(uv):uv\in E(T),\ \varphi(u)=\varphi(v)\}

for the
heaviest
\varphi
-monochromatic tree edge (with

\max\emptyset=-\infty
). Note
M(\varphi)
sees only the
n-1

tree edges, while
\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)
uses all
\binom{n}{2}

pair weights of
G
.

Lemma 1.1
.

G_{>\lambda}
admits a proper 2-coloring of cardinality
c
iff

\mathrm{diam}(\chi)\leq\lambda
and
T_{>\lambda}
admits a proper
2-coloring of cardinality
c
.

We first show the structural identity that drives the lemma: for every
2-coloring
\varphi:V\to\{0,1\}
,

{\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)=\max\{\mathrm{diam}(\chi),M(\varphi)\}.}

(1)

Upper bound for eq.
1
.
Let
u,v
be a

\varphi
-monochromatic pair, i.e.,
\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)
. If

\chi(u)=\chi(v)
, then
w(uv)\leq\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
. Otherwise

\chi(u)\neq\chi(v)
. Consider the
u
-
v
path in
T
. Every
tree edge is
\chi
-bichromatic, so the path length is odd. The number
of
\varphi
-bichromatic edges on the path has the parity of

\varphi(u)\oplus\varphi(v)=0
, hence is even. So some tree edge

xy
on the path is
\varphi
-monochromatic. The MST bottleneck
property
[
7
, Chapter 23]
gives

w(xy)\geq w(uv)
. So
w(uv)\leq M(\varphi)
.

Lower bound for eq.
1
.
Any

\varphi
-monochromatic tree edge has its endpoints in the same

\varphi
-class, so
\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)\geq M(\varphi)
. Pick

u,v
with
\chi(u)=\chi(v)
and
w(uv)=\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
. If

\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)
, then
u,v
are
\varphi
-monochromatic,
giving
\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)\geq w(uv)=\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
directly.
Otherwise the
u
-
v
path has even length (since

\chi(u)=\chi(v)
) but an odd number of
\varphi
-bichromatic edges
(since
\varphi(u)\neq\varphi(v)
), so some tree edge on it is

\varphi
-monochromatic of weight at least
\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
.

From eq.
1
to the iff.
A coloring
\varphi

properly 2-colors
G_{>\lambda}
iff every
\varphi
-monochromatic
pair in
G
has weight
\leq\lambda
, i.e.,

\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)\leq\lambda
. Similarly
\varphi
properly
2-colors
T_{>\lambda}
iff
M(\varphi)\leq\lambda
. By
eq.
1
,
\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)\leq\lambda
iff

\mathrm{diam}(\chi)\leq\lambda
and
M(\varphi)\leq\lambda
. The
condition
\mathrm{diam}(\chi)\leq\lambda
does not depend on

\varphi
, so quantifying over cardinality-
c
colorings
\varphi

gives the iff statement.

So minimizing
\mathrm{diam}(\varphi)
at cardinality
c
reduces to
two pieces: the constant
\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
, and the smallest

\lambda
for which
T_{>\lambda}
admits a proper 2-coloring of
cardinality
c
. Define

M^{*}(c)=\min\{M(\varphi):\varphi:V\to\{0,1\},\ |\varphi^{-1}(0)|=c\},

the minimum heaviest
\varphi
-monochromatic tree edge over
cardinality-
c
colorings. Equivalently,
M^{*}(c)
is the smallest
tree-edge weight
\lambda
such that
T_{>\lambda}
admits a proper
2-coloring of cardinality
c
, or
-\infty
if
T
itself does.

By
Section

1
, the optimum diameter at cardinality
c
equals

\max\{\mathrm{diam}(\chi),M^{*}(c)\}
. Computing
T
,
\chi
, and

\mathrm{diam}(\chi)
takes
O(n^{2})
time by Prim

[
13
]
and brute-force diameter. Hence it only remains
to compute
M^{*}(c)
. This leads us to the
bottleneck tree
2-coloring
problem: Given a weighted tree
T
and a cardinality

c\in\{0,\dots,|V(T)|\}
, compute
M^{*}(c)
.

2
Algorithms for bottleneck tree
2-coloring

Theorem 2.1
.

Bottleneck tree 2-coloring for a single cardinality can be computed in

O(n\operatorname{polylog}n)
time.

Recall
M^{*}(c)
is the smallest tree-edge weight
\lambda
such that

T_{>\lambda}
admits a proper 2-coloring of cardinality
c
, or

-\infty
if
T
itself does. Test whether
\chi
(or its swap)
has cardinality
c
to decide the
-\infty
case. Otherwise sort the

n-1
tree-edge weights and binary-search with
O(\log n)

feasibility tests on
T_{>\lambda}
. Each test reduces to a subset-sum
instance of non-negative integers with total sum
O(n)

[
4
]
. Koiliaris-Xu
[
9
, Theorem
1.1]
decides such instances in
O(n\operatorname{polylog}n)

time.

Theorem 2.2
.

Bottleneck tree 2-coloring for all cardinalities can be computed in

O(n^{2})
time. An optimal labeling for any chosen cardinality can be
reconstructed in
O(n)
additional time.

We design a dynamic programming algorithm. Root
T
at any vertex

r
. For each node
u
with rooted subtree
T_{u}
, let
D_{u}^{b}[q]

be the smallest
M(\varphi)
over 2-colorings

\varphi:V(T_{u})\to\{0,1\}
with
\varphi(u)=b
and

|\varphi^{-1}(0)\cap V(T_{u})|=q
, where
M(\varphi)
uses only edges
of
T_{u}
(and
D_{u}^{b}[q]=+\infty
if no such
\varphi
exists).

For a leaf
u
,
D_{u}^{0}[1]=D_{u}^{1}[0]=-\infty
and other entries are

+\infty
. For an internal node
u
with children
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
:

D_{u}^{b}[q]=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}g_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{k}=q-[b=0]\end{subarray}}\;\max_{i\in[k]}\max\big\{D_{v_{i}}^{g_{i}}[q_{i}],\ [b=g_{i}]\cdot w(uv_{i})\big\},

where
[\cdot]
is the Iverson bracket:
[P]=1
if
P
is true and

0
otherwise. The product
[P]\cdot w
with an edge weight uses the
max-plus convention:
w
if
P
is true and
-\infty
otherwise.
Each child
v_{i}
contributes its subtree optimum at the chosen color

g_{i}
, plus the edge
uv_{i}
if it becomes
\varphi
-monochromatic.

To compute the recurrence efficiently,
absorb
children into a

running table

R^{b}[q]
one at a time. Initialize
R
to
represent
u
alone (the leaf base case applied to
R
). For each
child
v
in turn, replace
R
with

R^{\prime b}[Q]=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}+q_{2}=Q\\
g\in\{0,1\}\end{subarray}}\max\big\{R^{b}[q_{1}],\ D_{v}^{g}[q_{2}],\ [b=g]\cdot w(uv)\big\}.

After all
k
children of
u
are absorbed,
D_{u}\leftarrow R
.

If the current
R
represents
s
vertices, absorbing
v
visits

s\cdot|T_{v}|
pairs
(q_{1},q_{2})
in
O(1)
time each, costing

O(s\cdot|T_{v}|)
.

For total time, each absorption cost
s\cdot|T_{v}|
counts the
unordered vertex pairs
\{x,y\}
with
x
in
R
and

y\in V(T_{v})
. Every unordered pair
\{x,y\}\subseteq V
is counted
at exactly one absorption: at
u=\mathrm{LCA}(x,y)
, exactly one of

x,y
is in
R
before
u
absorbs the child whose subtree
contains the other. So total cost
=\binom{n}{2}=O(n^{2})
.

At the root,
M^{*}(c)=\min(D_{r}^{0}[c],D_{r}^{1}[c])
. Storing argmin choices
at each absorption lets us walk back from
r
in
O(n)
time and
recover an optimal
\varphi
.

Combining
Section

1
with
Theorem

2.2
gives the optimum
diameter for every cardinality
c
in
O(n^{2})
time.

Corollary 2.3
.

Diameter partitioning can be computed for all cardinalities in

O(n^{2})
time. An optimal partition for any chosen cardinality can be
reconstructed in
O(n)
additional time.

We also get the same corollary for 2-MDCC.

3
Euclidean metric

By
Section

1
, diameter partitioning reduces to one MST, one
diameter computation per color class, and one bottleneck tree 2-coloring
instance. The first two admit subquadratic algorithms in the Euclidean
metric. In the plane, the Euclidean diameter and the Euclidean MST each
admit
O(n\log n)
algorithms

[
12
,
10
]
. For fixed

d\geq 3
, both admit
O(n^{2-2/(\lceil d/2\rceil+1)+\epsilon})
-time
algorithms for every
\epsilon>0

[
2
]
.

For 2-MDCC, the negation argument fails since negated Euclidean
distances do not form a Euclidean metric.
Section

1
still
applies after swapping max with min throughout: replace MAX-MST with
MIN-MST and the diameter computation with closest pair. In the plane,
the Euclidean MST and the Euclidean closest pair both admit

O(n\log n)
algorithms
[
12
]
. For fixed

d\geq 3
, the Euclidean MST admits

O(n^{2-2/(\lceil d/2\rceil+1)+\epsilon})
for every
\epsilon>0

[
1
]
. Closest pair stays at
O(n\log n)
in
any fixed dimension
[
12
]
, but the MST step
dominates.

Combined with
Theorem

2.1
:

Corollary 3.1
.

For
n
points in
\mdmathbb{R}^{d}
under the Euclidean metric, both diameter
partitioning and 2-MDCC for a single cardinality can be solved in

O(n\operatorname{polylog}n)
time when
d=2
. For fixed
d\geq 3
, both can be
solved in
O(n^{2-2/(\lceil d/2\rceil+1)+\epsilon})
time for every

\epsilon>0
.

4
Lower bound

Theorem 4.1
.

For infinitely many
n
, any randomized algorithm that outputs the
exact optimum of diameter partitioning or 2-MDCC on
n
vertices with
success probability at least
2/3
makes
\Omega(n^{2})
edge weight
queries in the worst case, even when the weights form a metric taking
only the values
1
and
2
.

Take
n\geq 4
even and set
c=n/2
. Partition
V
into known sets

A
and
B
with
|A|=|B|=n/2
. Set
w(ab)=2
for every

a\in A,\ b\in B
, and
w(uv)=1
for every
\{u,v\}\subseteq B
.
For each pair
\{u,v\}\subseteq A
, the adversary sets

w(uv)\in\{1,2\}
.

These instances are metric. Weights lie in
\{1,2\}
, so

w(xz)\leq 2\leq w(xy)+w(yz)
for any triple
x,y,z\in V
.

By construction
\mathrm{diam}(B)=1
. If every pair within
A
also
has weight
1
, then
\mathrm{diam}(A)=1
, so the partition

(A,B)
achieves
\max\{\mathrm{diam}(A),\mathrm{diam}(B)\}=1
. The
optimum is
1
. If some pair
\{a,a^{\prime}\}\subseteq A
has weight
2
,
the optimum is
2
. Take any partition with
|V_{1}|=n/2
. Since

|A|=|B|=n/2
, either
V_{1}\in\{A,B\}
or
V_{1}
contains both an
element of
A
and an element of
B
. In the first case
a,a^{\prime}

both lie in class
A
, so
\mathrm{diam}(A)\geq w(aa^{\prime})=2
. In the
second case
V_{1}
contains some
a^{\prime\prime}\in A
and
b\in B
, so

\mathrm{diam}(V_{1})\geq w(a^{\prime\prime}b)=2
. Either way the partition diameter
is at least
2
. Weights are bounded by
2
, so the optimum is

2
.

Hence distinguishing optimum
1
from optimum
2
amounts to
deciding whether some pair within
A
has weight
2
. This is the OR
problem on the
\binom{n/2}{2}=\Theta(n^{2})
bits indexed by

\binom{A}{2}
. Its bounded-error randomized query complexity is

\Theta(n^{2})

[
5
]
. Each edge weight query
reveals one bit, so the algorithm makes
\Omega(n^{2})
edge weight
queries.

For 2-MDCC, mirror the construction: set
w(ab)=1
for

a\in A,\ b\in B
,
w(uv)=2
for
\{u,v\}\subseteq B
, and
adversarial
w(uv)\in\{1,2\}
within
A
. This is still a

\{1,2\}
-metric. On the partition
(A,B)
, every pair within
B

has weight
2
. So the 2-MDCC objective equals the minimum pair weight
within
A
. Any other partition with
|V_{1}|=n/2
places both an
element of
A
and an element of
B
in some class. That class
contains a pair of weight
1
, so the objective is at most
1
. Thus
the optimum is
2
iff every pair within
A
has weight
2
,
otherwise
1
. The same OR reduction gives
\Omega(n^{2})
edge weight
queries.
