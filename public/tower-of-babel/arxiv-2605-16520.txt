Global Convergence of Sampling-Based Nonconvex Optimization through Diffusion-Style Smoothing
Zeji Yi zejiy@andrew.cmu.edu, Chaoyi Pan chaoyip@andrew.cmu.edu Guanya Shi guanyas@andrew.cmu.edu Guannan Qu gqu@andrew.cmu.eduCarnegie Mellon University

Abstract
Sampling-based optimization (SBO), like cross-entropy method and evolutionary algorithms, has achieved many successes in solving non-convex problems without gradients, yet its convergence is poorly understood. In this paper, we establish a non-asymptotic convergence analysis for SBO through the lens of smoothing . Specifically, we recast SBO as gradient descent on a smoothed objective, mirroring noise - conditioned score ascent in diffusion models. Our first contribution is a landscape analysis of the smoothed objective, demonstrating how smoothing helps escape local minima and uncovering a fundamental coverage–optimality trade-off : smoothing renders the landscape more benign by enlarging the locally convex region around the global minimizer, but at the cost of introducing an optimality gap. Building on this insight, we establish non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for SBO algorithms to a neighborhood of the global minimizer. Furthermore, we propose an annealed SBO algorithm, D iffusion- I nspired D ual- A nnealing (DIDA), which is provably convergent to the global optimum. We conduct extensive numerical experiments to verify our landscape results and also demonstrate the compelling performance of DIDA compared to other gradient-free optimization methods. Lastly, we discuss implications of our results for diffusion models.

We consider the Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution

p_{0}(x)\propto e^{-f(x)/\lambda},

(3)

As established in

Building on this foundation, this section presents our core theoretical contributions, where we rigorously analyze the properties of this smoothed landscape and the convergence behavior of SBO that navigates it.
Our analysis begins with the key assumptions (

4.1
Assumptions

To analyze the behavior of our optimization approach, we impose the following assumptions on the problem structure. Our first assumption is on the tail bound of the distribution
p_{0}
.

4.1
Global Sub-Gaussian Assumption.

There exists a constant
D_{\tau}>0
such that the tail probability deviating from the optimal solution

is bounded:

P_{0}(\|x-x^{*}\|\geq a)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{2\tau^{2}}\right),\quad\forall a\geq D_{\tau},

where
P_{0}
is the probability measure induced by the density
p_{0}
.
We also denote the total probability of being outside the
D_{\tau}
-ball around
x^{*}
as

P_{0}(\|x-x^{*}\|\geq D_{\tau})=P_{\text{out}}.

The sub-Gaussian assumption (
4.1
) describes the target distribution
p_{0}(x)
’s concentration around the global optimum
x^{*}
. Weaker than log-concavity
(
45
)
, it constrains only tail behavior, accommodating a broader class of
non-convex
functions. This condition is met in many practical problems, like regularized machine learning losses
(
34
)
, or LQR control
(
51
)
. Adopting it allows our analysis to cover more non-convex functions, ensuring sufficient probability mass near the optimum.
\tau
sets the concentration width, and
P_{\text{out}}
quantifies tail probability.

Our second assumption is on the behavior of
f
around
x^{*}
. For notational convenience, we define the following regions:
B_{\tau}{}:=\left\{x|\|x-x^{*}\|<D_{\tau}\right\}
, a ball of radius
D_{\tau}
around
x^{*}
.

4.2
Local Convexity and Smoothness.

We assume that the objective function
f(x)
is strongly convex and smooth within the ball
B_{\tau}{}
centered at the optimum
x^{*}
. Specifically, there exists constants
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
such that for all
x,y\in B_{\tau}{}
, the function satisfies
\alpha
-strong convexity:
f(y)\geq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}
, and
\beta
-smoothness:
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq\beta\|x-y\|
. The ratio
\kappa_{0}=\beta/\alpha
defines the local condition number of
f(x)
within
B_{\tau}{}
.

Assumptions
4.1
and
4.2
provide the necessary structure for our analysis. Specifically,
4.2
ensures local regularity (strong convexity and smoothness) near the optimum
x^{*}
, while
4.1
controls the global tail decay of the target distribution

. This combined structure enables a rigorous analysis of the convergence behavior of sampling-based optimization methods within the proposed framework, which we introduce in the subsequent subsections.

4.2
Coverage and Optimality Tradeoff: The Role of Smoothing

Here we show that smoothing the original objective
f(x)
into

via the parameter
t
induces a fundamental tradeoff. This tradeoff balances the
coverage
of desirable landscape properties (such as convexity) with the
optimality gap
between the minimizer
x_{t}^{*}
of

and the true global minimizer
x^{*}
resulting from the smoothing process. Our main results demonstrate that as
t
increases, the convex region of
g(x;t)
expands. Concurrently, however, the optimality gap widens, as the minimizer of
g(x;t)
deviates further from the true optimum of

Coverage: Expansion of convex region.
We now state our first main result, showing that as
t
increases, the convex region around
x^{*}
expands at least on the order of

.

4.3

As shown in

A larger convex region provides a wider basin of attraction, making it easier for optimization algorithms (including sampling-based ones that implicitly perform gradient descent on

Optimality gap.
Despite the larger coverage, smoothing the objective function introduces optimality gap: the minimizer

generally differs from the original function’s global minimizer
x^{*}
. Understanding and bounding this gap is crucial, as a large deviation would mean that optimizing
g(x;t)
does not yield a sufficiently accurate solution to the original problem
(
1
)
. The subsequent theorem bounds this gap, relating it to the smoothing parameter
t
and the properties of
f
.

4.4
Optimality gap.

Let
4.1
and
4.2
hold. For any smoothing parameter
t>0
, let
x_{t}^{*}
denote the unique minimizer of the smoothed function
g(x;t)
within the strongly convex region
\mathcal{R}_{SC}(t)
, as established in
Theorem

4.3
. If
x_{t}^{*}
also lies within the region of local strong convexity and smoothness for the original function
f(x)
, i.e.,
x_{t}^{*}\in B_{\tau}{}=\{x\mid\|x-x^{*}\|<D_{\tau}\}
, then the optimality gap
\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\|
is bounded by:

\left\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\right\|\leq\min\left\{\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t}{C_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{4D_{\tau}},(D_{\tau}+\tau)\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}t}\right\}+\frac{\kappa_{0}-1}{2C_{\alpha}}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2\pi(\tfrac{1}{t}+\tfrac{\alpha}{\lambda})}}.

(8)

where
C_{\alpha}\in(0,1)
is the strong convexity parameter from
Theorem

4.3
, and
D_{\tau}
,
\tau
are the parameters from Assumption
4.1
.

Theorem

4.4
shows a larger
t
increases the optimality gap
\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\|
.
The bound in (
8
) indicates that the gap arises from two primary sources. The first, captured by both
\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t}{C_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{4D_{\tau}}
and
(D_{\tau}+\tau)\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}t}
, is largely influenced by the smoothing parameter
t
and the sub-Gaussian tail. The second term,
\frac{\kappa_{0}-1}{2C_{\alpha}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi\left(\frac{1}{t}+\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)}}
, captures
local asymmetry
around
x^{*}
: when the local condition number
\kappa_{0}=\beta/\alpha
is close to
1
, this contribution is small, and it grows with increasing anisotropy/curvature mismatch (larger
\kappa_{0}
). Notably, both contributions vanish as
t\to 0
, consistent with
g(\cdot;t)\to f
(up to constants). Moreover, the bound remains finite as
t\to\infty
, matching the empirical behavior reported in
Section

5
.

Discussion.
The landscape coverage (
Theorem

4.3
) versus optimality gap (
Theorem

4.4
) tradeoff formally underpins successful SBO methods like MPPI
(
62
)
and diffusion-inspired approaches like Model-Based Diffusion
(
52
)
. These methods often implicitly use smoothing (via sample averaging or explicit diffusion) to navigate complex, non-convex landscapes. Our theorems quantify this fundamental smoothing tradeoff: larger
t
creates a more benign, larger convex region but increases the optimality gap.

The coverage-optimality tradeoff developed here forms the foundation for analyzing the convergence of SBO algorithms, which we present in
Section

4.3
.
Beyond the fixed
t
regime, the inherent tradeoff in
t
motivates the use of an annealing schedule for
t
. We can start with a relatively large
t
to leverage the enlarged convex region, allowing the algorithm to start in the convex region. Then,
t
is gradually decreased to reduce the optimality gap, guiding the iterates towards the true global minimum
x^{*}
while staying within the convex region. In Section
4.4
, we detail this annealing process and provide the convergence guarantee.

Multiple Convex Regions.
While our current analysis focuses on landscapes with a dominant global minimum characterized by local convexity (
4.1
and
4.2
), our analysis techniques could potentially be extended to multi-convex-region (multi-modal) scenarios. Larger
t
might merge distinct basins of attraction, while annealing could help distinguish them later. Further investigation is needed to formalize the behavior in landscapes with multiple convex regions.

From landscape analysis to convergence guarantees.

The landscape results of

For a fixed smoothing parameter

The coverage–optimality tradeoff then motivates annealing

As shown in

This optimization perspective of diffusion model enables us to understand the stability of the convergence behavior by analyzing the convexity of the landscape.

\mathbb{P}(Y>r)\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\right),\qquad\forall r\geq 0,

(22)

for some
\tau>0
. Then, for any
a\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Y>a]

\displaystyle\leq a+\frac{\tau^{2}}{a+\sqrt{a^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}},

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}\mid Y>a]

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}+\tau^{2}.

(24)

Furthermore, if the exact tail probability at level
a
is known,

p_{a}:=\mathbb{P}(Y>a),

it is convenient to introduce the
effective tail radius

D:=\tau\sqrt{\log\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{p_{a}}\Big)}\qquad(\text{so that }\ e^{-D^{2}/\tau^{2}}=p_{a}),

which satisfies
D\geq a
by (
22
). In terms of
D
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Y>a]

\displaystyle\leq D+\frac{\tau^{2}}{D+\sqrt{D^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}}\;\leq\;D+\tau,

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}\mid Y>a]

\displaystyle\leq D^{2}+\tau^{2}.

(26)

Proof.

We start from the tail-integral identities for nonnegative random variables. For any
Z\geq 0
,
Tonelli’s theorem yields

\mathbb{E}\left[Z\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}P(Z>r)\,dr,\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{2}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}2r\,P(Z>r)\,dr.

Applying these to
(Y-a)_{+}
gives the standard representations

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid Y>a\right]

\displaystyle=a+\frac{\int_{a}^{\infty}P(Y>r)\,dr}{P(Y>a)},

(27)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{2}\mid Y>a\right]

\displaystyle=a^{2}+\frac{\int_{a}^{\infty}2r\,P(Y>r)\,dr}{P(Y>a)}.

(28)

Step 1: Bounds in terms of the tail at level
a
.

Assume
Y\sim\mathcal{SG}(0,\tau^{2})
in the sense of (
22
). Then for all
r\geq a
,

P(Y>r)\leq e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}
, and therefore

\int_{a}^{\infty}P(Y>r)\,dr\leq\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr,\qquad\int_{a}^{\infty}2r\,P(Y>r)\,dr\leq\int_{a}^{\infty}2r\,e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr.

For the second integral we have the exact evaluation

\int_{a}^{\infty}2r\,e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr=\tau^{2}e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}.

For the first integral we use a Gaussian tail bound (Mills’ ratio; e.g.
(
39
, Lemma 1.1)
):

\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr\leq\frac{\tau^{2}e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}}{a+\sqrt{a^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}}.

(29)

Substituting these bounds into (
27
)–(
28
) yields

\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid Y>a\right]\leq a+\frac{\tau^{2}e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}}{\bigl(a+\sqrt{a^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}\bigr)\,P(Y>a)},\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{2}\mid Y>a\right]\leq a^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}}{P(Y>a)}.

In the special case where the sub-Gaussian bound is tight at level
a
, namely
P(Y>a)=e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}
, these simplify to
(
23
)–(
24
).

Step 2: General case via the effective tail radius.

Let
p_{a}:=P(Y>a)
and define
D:=\tau\sqrt{\log(1/p_{a})}
, so that
e^{-D^{2}/\tau^{2}}=p_{a}
.
By (
22
),
p_{a}\leq e^{-a^{2}/\tau^{2}}
, hence
D\geq a
.
Moreover, for all
r\geq a
,

P(Y>r)\leq\min\{p_{a},\,e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\}=\begin{cases}p_{a},&a\leq r\leq D,\\
e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}},&r\geq D.\end{cases}

First moment.

Using (
27
) and splitting the numerator at
D
gives

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}P(Y>r)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{D}p_{a}\,dr+\int_{D}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr

\displaystyle=p_{a}(D-a)+\int_{D}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr.

Dividing by
p_{a}
and applying (
29
) at
D
(noting
e^{-D^{2}/\tau^{2}}=p_{a}
) yields

\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid Y>a\right]\leq a+(D-a)+\frac{1}{p_{a}}\int_{D}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr\leq D+\frac{\tau^{2}}{D+\sqrt{D^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}},

which is (
25
). Finally, since
\sqrt{D^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}\geq\frac{2\tau}{\sqrt{\pi}}
,
we have
D+\sqrt{D^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}\geq\frac{2\tau}{\sqrt{\pi}}
, and hence

\frac{\tau^{2}}{D+\sqrt{D^{2}+\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\pi}}}\leq\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\tau/\sqrt{\pi}}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\tau\leq\tau,

so
\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mid Y>a\right]\leq D+\tau
.

Second moment.

Similarly, from (
28
),

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}2r\,P(Y>r)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{D}2r\,p_{a}\,dr+\int_{D}^{\infty}2r\,e^{-r^{2}/\tau^{2}}\,dr

\displaystyle=p_{a}(D^{2}-a^{2})+\tau^{2}e^{-D^{2}/\tau^{2}}=p_{a}(D^{2}-a^{2}+\tau^{2}).

Dividing by
p_{a}
and plugging into (
28
) gives

\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{2}\mid Y>a\right]\leq a^{2}+(D^{2}-a^{2}+\tau^{2})=D^{2}+\tau^{2},

which proves (
26
).
∎

P_{0|t}
, which the basis of our analysis on the landscape.

Proof.

B.3
Sub-Gaussian region moment bound.

Recall the setup and notation in
4.1
. The conditional first and second moments of the radius outside the sub-Gaussian region satisfy

\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]\leq\max\left((D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime})^{2},D_{s}^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2}+4\tau^{\prime}r_{t}^{\prime}\right)

and

\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]\leq\max\left(D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime},D_{s}+3\tau^{\prime}\right)

where
D_{s}:=\tau\sqrt{\log(\frac{1}{P_{\text{out}}})}
,
\tau^{\prime 2}=\frac{2\tau^{2}t}{\tau^{2}+2t}
and
r_{t}^{\prime}=\frac{\tau^{2}\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|}{\tau^{2}+2t}

Proof.

Sketch
:
Consider the following functional:

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(q_{0},q_{t|0})

\displaystyle:=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x^{*}\|q_{0}(y)q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}.

(30)

\displaystyle\xi_{2}(q_{0},q_{t|0})

\displaystyle:=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}q_{0}(y)q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}.

(31)

By definition,
\xi_{1}(p_{0},p_{t|0})=\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]
and
\xi_{2}(p_{0},p_{t|0})=\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]
.
In the following steps, we aim to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q_{0}},q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|}}\xi_{1}(q_{0},q_{t|0})

\displaystyle\leq RHS_{1}

(32)

\displaystyle\sup_{q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q_{0}},q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}}\xi_{2}(q_{0},q_{t|0})

\displaystyle\leq RHS_{2}

(33)

where
RHS_{1}
is the RHS for
Equation

30
and
RHS_{2}
is the RHS for
Equation

31
, and
p_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
p_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}
. Here,
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}
are sets of feasible probability measures that we will define later.

Step 1: Reduction to a one-dimensional integral.

Fix any
p_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
. In this step, we explain how to bound

\sup_{q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}}\xi_{1}(p_{0},q_{t|0})\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}}\xi_{2}(p_{0},q_{t|0}),

by reducing the optimization over
q_{t|0}
to a one-dimensional problem in
\|y-x^{*}\|
.
We view
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
as functionals of
(q_{0},q_{t|0})
, where
q_{0}
is a probability density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and, for each fixed
x_{t}
,
q_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})
is a conditional density of
Y
given
X_{t}=x_{t}
.
In particular, the conditional first moment that we want to bound is

\xi_{1}(p_{0},p_{t|0})
.
Our first step is to transform this conditional expectation into a one-dimensional integral over the radial variable
\|y-x^{*}\|
.
To this end, we will “replace”
p_{t|0}(\cdot)
and
p_{0}(\cdot)
by simpler radial densities that depend only on
\|y-x^{*}\|
; the precise meaning of this replacement will be made clear below.

Replacing
p_{t|0}(\cdot)
.
The forward transition density
p_{t|0}
satisfies the following bound:

\displaystyle(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(\|y-x^{*}\|+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\right)\leq p_{t|0}(x_{t}|y)\leq(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{(\|y-x^{*}\|-\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\right)

(34)

Motivated by this, we define the admissible class of conditional densities as follows:

\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}:=\Bigl\{q_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})\,\Bigm|\,\begin{aligned} &q_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})\text{ is a Borel-measurable density on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ for each }x_{t},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy=1,\\
&(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{(\|y-x^{*}\|+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\Bigr)\leq q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt\leq(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\Bigl(-\tfrac{(\|y-x^{*}\|-\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\Bigr),\quad\forall\,y\notin B_{\tau}\end{aligned}\Bigr\}.

By construction,
p_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}
for every
x_{t}
, and therefore

\xi_{1}(p_{0},p_{t|0})\leq\sup_{q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}}\xi_{1}(p_{0},q_{t|0}).

We now justify the worst-case choice of
q_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})
within the admissible envelope (
34
).
Fix
q_{0}=p_{0}
and view

\xi_{1}(p_{0},q_{t|0})=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x^{*}\|\,p_{0}(y)\,q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}p_{0}(y)\,q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}

as a functional of
q_{t|0}
under the pointwise constraints
q_{-}(y)\leq q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\leq q_{+}(y)
on
B_{\tau}^{c}
, where

\displaystyle q_{-}(y)

\displaystyle:=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\|y-x^{*}\|+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\right),

\displaystyle q_{+}(y)

\displaystyle:=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(\|y-x^{*}\|-\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}}{2t}\right).

We next compute the functional derivative of
\xi_{1}
with respect to
q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi_{1}(q_{0},q_{t|0})}{\partial q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{q_{0}(y)}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{t|0}(y|x_{t})q_{0}(y)\,dy}(\|y-x^{*}\|-\xi_{1}(q_{0},q_{t|0}))

(35)

Since
\xi_{1}
is a ratio of two linear functionals in
q_{t|0}
, the extremizer over such box constraints is attained at an extreme point (a “bang–bang” choice): there exists a threshold
r^{\star}
such that
q_{t|0}
takes the lower envelope
q_{-}
on
\{\|y-x^{*}\|<r^{\star}\}
and the upper envelope
q_{+}
on
\{\|y-x^{*}\|>r^{\star}\}
(ties on the null set
\{\|y-x^{*}\|=r^{\star}\}
are irrelevant). We therefore define

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t}):=\begin{cases}q_{-}(y),&D_{\tau}\leq\|y-x^{*}\|<r^{\star},\\[2.0pt]
q_{+}(y),&\|y-x^{*}\|\geq r^{\star},\end{cases}

(36)

and set

\xi_{1}^{\star}:=\xi_{1}\bigl(p_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0}\bigr)=\sup_{q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}}\xi_{1}(p_{0},q_{t|0}).

(37)

By construction,
p_{t|0}(\cdot|x_{t})\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|0}
, hence

\xi_{1}(p_{0},p_{t|0})\leq\xi_{1}^{\star}
.

Replacing
p_{0}(\cdot)
:
We now study the following functional for
q_{0}
:

J[q_{0}]\;=\;\xi_{1}(q_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0})=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x^{*}\|q_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,dy}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})dy}.

We maximize this functional over
q_{0}
subject to the following constraint set:

\mathcal{A}=\Bigl\{q_{0}:[a,\infty)\!\to\!\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,\Bigm|\,\begin{aligned} &q_{0}\text{ is continuous and a.e.\ differentiable},\\
&\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)dy=P_{\text{out}},\quad q_{0}(y)>0,\\
&\int_{\|y-x^{*}\|>r}q_{0}(y)dy\leq\exp(-r^{2}/\tau^{2}),\quad\forall r>D_{\tau}\end{aligned}\Bigr\},

to obtain the optimal
\tilde{p}_{0}
. This yields the upper bound

\xi_{1}^{*}\leq\xi_{1}(p_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0})\leq\xi_{1}(\tilde{p}_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0})=J[\tilde{p}_{0}].

To maximize
J[q_{0}]
, we formulate the problem using the method of Lagrange multipliers, treating
J
as an auxiliary parameter.
We introduce the following multipliers:

•

\nu(y)\geq 0
for the sub-Gaussian bound
\int_{\|y-x^{*}\|>r}q_{0}(y)dy\leq\exp(-r^{2}/\tau^{2})
,

•

a scalar
\eta
for the total mass constraint
\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)=P_{\text{out}}
,

•

and
\theta(y)\geq 0
for the positivity constraint.

The Lagrangian is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[q_{0}]

\displaystyle\;=\;\int_{\|y-x^{*}\|>r}\Bigl[(\|y-x^{*}\|-J)\,\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})\,q_{0}(y)+\nu(y)\int_{\|y^{\prime}-x^{*}\|>r}\bigl(\exp(-r^{2}/\tau^{2})-q_{0}(y^{\prime})\bigr)dy^{\prime}\Bigr]dy

\displaystyle\quad+\eta\!\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)\,dy\;+\;\!\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}q_{0}(y)\theta(y)\,dy.

For the stationary condition,
we consider a variation
q_{0}\mapsto q_{0}+\varepsilon h
with
\int h=0
and

\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}h=0
,

0=\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{L}[q_{0}+\varepsilon h]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{\|y-x^{*}\|>a}\bigl[(\|y-x^{*}\|-J)\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})-\int_{\|t-x^{*}\|\leq\|y-x^{*}\|}\nu(t)+\eta\,+\theta(y)\bigr]\,h(y)\,dy,

hence

(\|y-x^{*}\|-J)\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})-\int_{\|y^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\leq\|y-x^{*}\|}\nu(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime}+\eta\,+\theta(y)=0

Since
\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})>0
and the functional derivative changes sign, the stationary condition forces a
bang–bang
structure:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nu(y)=0,\quad\theta(y)>0,&\|y-x^{*}\|<\rho,\\[4.0pt]
\nu(y)>0,\quad\theta(y)=0&\|y-x^{*}\|\geq\rho,\end{cases}

(38)

Complementary slackness dictates that
\nu(y)
and
\theta(y)
cannot both be zero, as
(\|y-x^{*}\|-J)\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})
is strictly increasing in
\|y-x^{*}\|
. Thus, there exists a threshold
\rho
such that
\nu(y)=0,\theta(y)>0
when
\|y-x^{*}\|<\rho
, and
\nu(y)>0,\theta(y)=0
when
\|y-x^{*}\|\geq\rho
. This corresponds precisely to the bang–bang structure required by the sub-Gaussian constraint. By further examining the constraint
\int_{D_{\tau}}^{\infty}q_{0}(y)dy=P_{\text{out}}
, we obtain
\rho=D_{s}
, where
D_{s}:=\tau\sqrt{\log\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{P_{\text{out}}}\Bigr)}
.

This optimal configuration is illustrated in Figure
4

\|y-x^{*}\|
values and lower density (minimum
\tilde{p}_{t|0}

\|y-x^{*}\|
values and higher density (maximum
\tilde{p}_{t|0}
). Therefore, after self-normalization, the region with larger
\|y-x^{*}\|
values has larger density.

In conclusion, we have the following result for
\tilde{p}_{0}
(here we give its CDF
\tilde{P}_{0}(\|y-x^{*}\|\geq r)

\tilde{p}_{t|0}
that achieve the maximum

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{0}(\|y-x^{*}\|\geq r)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}P_{\text{out}}&\text{if }D_{\tau}\leq r\leq D_{s}\\
\exp(-a^{2}/\tau^{2})&\text{if }r>D_{s}\end{cases},

(39)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{t|0}(y|x_{t})

\displaystyle\propto\begin{cases}\exp(-(\|y-x^{*}\|+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}/2t)&\text{if }D_{\tau}\leq\|y-x^{*}\|\leq r^{*}\\
\exp(-(\|y-x^{*}\|-\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2}/2t)&\text{if }\|y-x^{*}\|>r^{*}\end{cases}

(40)

where

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{\star}\leq\xi_{1}^{\star\star}:=\sup_{q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q_{0}},q_{t|0}\in\mathcal{Q}_{t|}}\xi_{1}(q_{0},q_{t|0})=\xi_{1}\left(\tilde{p}_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0}\right)

(41)

With the above selected
\tilde{p}_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0}
, we can transform
\xi_{1}
as follows:

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\left(\tilde{p}_{0},\tilde{p}_{t|0}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\left\|y-x^{*}\right\|\tilde{p}_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}\left(y\mid x_{t}\right)dy}{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\tilde{p}_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}\left(y\mid x_{t}\right)dy}.

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{r>D_{\tau}}\|r\|\int_{\left\|y-x^{*}\right\|=r}\tilde{p}_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}\left(y\mid x_{t}\right)dy}{\int_{r>D_{\tau}}\int_{\left\|y-x^{*}\right\|=r}\tilde{p}_{0}(y)\tilde{p}_{t|0}\left(y\mid x_{t}\right)dy}.

(42)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\int_{r>D_{\tau}}\|r\|\tilde{p}^{R_{1}}_{t|0}\left(r\mid x_{t}\right)\tilde{p}^{R}_{0}(r)dr}{\int_{r>D_{\tau}}\tilde{p}^{R_{1}}_{t|0}\left(r\mid x_{t}\right)\tilde{p}^{R}_{0}(r)dr}.

where in

. The integration proceeds by first integrating over the spherical surface

for a fixed radius

with

Step 2: Calculating

43

2.4: Bounding the fractions

We now bound these fractions by analyzing cases. We first focus on the second moment,

Case 1:

In this case, we bound the fraction

\displaystyle\int_{D_{s}}^{\xi_{2}^{**}}\tilde{p}^{R}_{0|t}(r)dr\geq C_{\mathrm{lower}}\int_{D_{s}}^{\infty}r\exp(-\frac{(r+r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}}{\tau^{\prime 2}})dr=C_{\mathrm{lower}}(\tau^{\prime}\phi(\frac{D_{s}+r_{t}^{\prime}}{\tau^{\prime}})-r_{t}^{\prime}(1-\Phi(\frac{D_{s}+r_{t}^{\prime}}{\tau^{\prime}}))),

The numerator integral is calculated using results similar to those in

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{2}^{**}}^{\infty}\tilde{p}^{R}_{0|t}(r)dr\propto\tau^{\prime}\phi(\frac{\xi_{2}^{**}-r_{t}^{\prime}}{\tau^{\prime}})+r_{t}^{\prime}(1-\Phi(\frac{\xi_{2}^{**}-r_{t}^{\prime}}{\tau^{\prime}})).

\displaystyle F_{2}

This inequality is derived from the condition

is monotonically decreasing when

F_{2}=\frac{\int_{\xi_{2}^{**}}^{\infty}\tilde{p}^{R}_{0|t}(r)dr}{\int_{D_{s}}^{\xi_{2}^{**}}\tilde{p}^{R}_{0|t}(r)dr}\leq\frac{3}{2}.

Case 2:
\xi_{2}^{**}-r_{t}^{\prime}<D_{s}+r_{t}^{\prime}+\tau^{\prime}

In this case, we have

We now perform a similar case analysis for the first moment,

Case 1:

Combining the results from both cases for the second moment yields:

Based on the above lemmas, we are ready to prove

(restated below).
See
4.3

Proof.

Recall the Tweedie-form Hessian identity (see
Equation

20
):

\nabla_{x}^{2}g(x;t)=\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\Bigl[t\,\mathbf{I}-\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}]\Bigr],\qquad\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}]:=\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}=x].

Hence, to prove
\tfrac{C_{\alpha}\lambda}{t+\lambda/\alpha}
-strong convexity of
g(\cdot;t)
at
x
, it suffices to show

t\,\mathbf{I}-\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}]\succeq\frac{C_{\alpha}t^{2}}{t+\lambda/\alpha}\,\mathbf{I}.

(64)

Step 1: Variance decomposition for the conditional distribution.

Consider the conditional distribution
Y\sim p_{0|t}(\cdot\mid x_{t})
and the event
\{Y\in B_{\tau}{}\}
. Applying the law of total variance (see
Lemma

B.7
) to this conditional distribution, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}]=\;

\displaystyle P_{\text{in}[0|t]}\,\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\in B_{\tau}{}]+P_{\text{out}[0|t]}\,\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle+P_{\text{in}[0|t]}P_{\text{out}[0|t]}\bigl(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)\bigl(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)^{\top},

where

P_{\text{in}[0|t]}:=P_{0|t}(y\in B_{\tau}{}\mid x_{t}),\quad P_{\text{out}[0|t]}:=P_{0|t}(y\notin B_{\tau}{}\mid x_{t}),

and
\mu_{\mathrm{in}}:=\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\in B_{\tau}{}]
,
\mu_{\mathrm{out}}:=\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]
are the conditional means.

Step 2: Upper bounding the contribution from the non-convex region.

Let
\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}:=\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\in B_{\tau}{}]
be the covariance within the convex region. We now bound the remaining terms in the decomposition. First, note that for any vector
z
,
zz^{\top}\preceq\|z\|^{2}\mathbf{I}
. Combined with the property that the covariance matrix of any random vector
Z
satisfies
\mathrm{Cov}(Z)=\mathbb{E}[ZZ^{\top}]-\mathbb{E}[Z]\mathbb{E}[Z]^{\top}\preceq\mathbb{E}[Z^{\top}Z]\mathbf{I}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y-x^{*}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle\preceq\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}\bigl[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}\bigr]\mathbf{I}=\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]\mathbf{I}.

Second, since
y\in B_{\tau}{}=\{y:\|y-x^{*}\|<D_{\tau}\}
implies
\|\mathbb{E}[y-x^{*}\mid x_{t},y\in B_{\tau}{}]\|\leq D_{\tau}
, the distance between the conditional means satisfies:

\|\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\|=\bigl\|\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[y-x^{*}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]-\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[y-x^{*}\mid x_{t},y\in B_{\tau}{}]\bigr\|\leq\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+D_{\tau}.

Consequently, the cross term in the variance decomposition is bounded by

\begin{aligned} &P_{\text{in}[0|t]}P_{\text{out}[0|t]}\bigl(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)\bigl(\mu_{\mathrm{out}}-\mu_{\mathrm{in}}\bigr)^{\top}\\
&\qquad\preceq P_{\text{out}[0|t]}(\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+D_{\tau})^{2}\mathbf{I}\preceq P_{\text{out}[0|t]}(\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+2D_{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+D_{\tau}^{2})\mathbf{I}.\end{aligned}

where the second inequality uses Jensen’s inequality
(\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}])^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]
. Combining these bounds, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{[0|t]}[y\mid x_{t}]

\displaystyle\preceq P_{\text{in}[0|t]}\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}+P_{\text{out}[0|t]}M_{\mathrm{out}}\mathbf{I},

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle:=2\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+2D_{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+D_{\tau}^{2}.

(65)

Step 3: Reduction of strong convexity to a bound on tail moments.

Substituting the bound from
Equation

65
into the strong convexity requirement
Equation

64
, it is sufficient to show that

t\mathbf{I}-P_{\text{in}[0|t]}\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}-P_{\text{out}[0|t]}M_{\mathrm{out}}\mathbf{I}\succeq\frac{C_{\alpha}t^{2}}{t+\lambda/\alpha}\mathbf{I}.

Applying
Lemma

B.2
, which states
\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq\frac{t(\lambda/\alpha)}{t+\lambda/\alpha}\mathbf{I}
, and noting that
P_{\text{in}[0|t]}\leq 1
, we find it sufficient to require

\left(t-\frac{t(\lambda/\alpha)}{t+\lambda/\alpha}\right)\mathbf{I}-P_{\text{out}[0|t]}M_{\mathrm{out}}\mathbf{I}\succeq\frac{C_{\alpha}t^{2}}{t+\lambda/\alpha}\mathbf{I}.

Since
t-\frac{t(\lambda/\alpha)}{t+\lambda/\alpha}=\frac{t^{2}}{t+\lambda/\alpha}
, this simplifies to the following sufficient condition:

P_{\text{out}[0|t]}M_{\mathrm{out}}\leq\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t^{2}}{t+\lambda/\alpha}.

(66)

Thus, proving
\tfrac{C_{\alpha}\lambda}{t+\lambda/\alpha}
-strong convexity reduces to bounding (i) the conditional tail mass
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}
and (ii) the outside-moment term
M_{\mathrm{out}}
so that
Equation

66
holds.

Step 4: Upper bound on
M_{\mathrm{out}}
.

We introduce unified "effective mean/variance" notation that mirrors the product-of-Gaussians parameters used in Step 5.
Define

\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}\;:=\;\frac{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}+2t}\,x_{t}+\frac{t}{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}+2t}\,x^{*},\qquad(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}\;:=\;\frac{t\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}+2t},

(67)

and

\mu_{\mathrm{out}}^{\prime}\;:=\;\frac{\tau^{2}}{\tau^{2}+2t}\,x_{t}+\frac{t}{\tau^{2}+2t}\,x^{*},\qquad(\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^{\prime})^{2}:=\frac{t\tau^{2}}{t+\tau^{2}}.

(68)

Note that
(\sigma_{\mathrm{out}}^{\prime})^{2}=\tau^{\prime 2}
, where

\tau^{\prime 2}\;:=\;\frac{t\tau^{2}}{2t+\tau^{2}}.

Define the scalar shrinkage radius

r_{t}^{\prime}\;:=\;\|\mu_{\mathrm{out}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\;=\;\frac{\tau^{2}}{2t+\tau^{2}}\,\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|.

Also as in
Appendix

A
, we have that

D_{s}\;:=\;\tau\sqrt{\log\!\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{P_{\text{out}}}\Bigr)}.

Lemma
B.3
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle\leq\max\!\left((D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime})^{2},\;D_{s}^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2}+4\tau^{\prime}r_{t}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle\leq\max\!\left(D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime},\;D_{s}+3\tau^{\prime}\right).

To simplify the algebra, we upper bound the maxima by slightly looser expressions.
Since
D_{s}\geq\tau^{\prime}
and
r_{t}^{\prime}\geq 0
, we have

4\tau^{\prime}r_{t}^{\prime}\leq 4D_{s}r_{t}^{\prime}\leq 4D_{s}r_{t}^{\prime}+4(r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}
, hence

D_{s}^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2}+4\tau^{\prime}r_{t}^{\prime}\leq(D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2},

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle\leq(D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]

\displaystyle\leq D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime}+3\tau^{\prime}.

Substituting these into
M_{\mathrm{out}}=2\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+2D_{\tau}\mathbb{E}_{[0|t]}[\|y-x^{*}\|\mid x_{t},y\notin B_{\tau}{}]+D_{\tau}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigl[(D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}+3\tau^{\prime 2}\bigr]+2D_{\tau}\bigl[D_{s}+2r_{t}^{\prime}+3\tau^{\prime}\bigr]+D_{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle=2D_{s}^{2}+8D_{s}r_{t}^{\prime}+8(r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}+6\tau^{\prime 2}+2D_{\tau}D_{s}+4D_{\tau}r_{t}^{\prime}+6D_{\tau}\tau^{\prime}+D_{\tau}^{2}.

(69)

In particular,
M_{\mathrm{out}}
is controlled by
(D_{\tau},\tau,P_{\text{out}})
and
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|
through
r_{t}^{\prime}
.

Using
r_{t}^{\prime}\leq\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
and
\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau
, the bound

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{s}^{2}+8D_{s}r_{t}^{\prime}+8(r_{t}^{\prime})^{2}+6\tau^{\prime 2}+2D_{\tau}D_{s}+4D_{\tau}r_{t}^{\prime}+6D_{\tau}\tau^{\prime}+D_{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{s}^{2}+5D_{s}D_{\tau}+5D_{\tau}^{2}\;\leq\;12\max(D_{\tau}^{2},D_{s}^{2}),

(70)

for all sufficiently large
d
(since
D_{\tau}\sim\Theta(d)
while
\tau=\Theta(1)
).

Case 2 (
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|>\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}

Recall
Equation

69
. Under the expansion-rate bounds used later (so that

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{s}^{2}+8C_{E}D_{s}D_{\tau}+8C_{E}^{2}D_{\tau}^{2}+6\tau^{2}+2D_{s}D_{\tau}+4C_{E}D_{\tau}^{2}+6D_{\tau}\tau+D_{\tau}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2D_{s}^{2}+3D_{s}D_{\tau}+4D_{\tau}^{2}\;\leq\;9\max(D_{\tau}^{2},D_{s}^{2}),

(71)

for all sufficiently large
d
(since

,

, and
\tau=\Theta(1)
).

Step 5: Upper bound on
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}
.

Since
p_{0|t}(y\mid x_{t})\propto p_{0}(y)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)
, define the unnormalized masses

Z_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{t}):=\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)\,dy,\qquad Z_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{t}):=\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)\,dy.

Then
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}/P_{\text{in}[0|t]}=Z_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{t})/Z_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{t})
. We next upper bound
Z_{\mathrm{out}}
and lower bound
Z_{\mathrm{in}}
to obtain bounds on
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}
and
P_{\text{in}[0|t]}
.

Upper bound on
Z_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{t})
.

By definition of
\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|:=\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}{}}\|y-x_{t}\|
, for all
y\notin B_{\tau}{}
,

p_{t|0}(x_{t}\mid y)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x_{t}-y\|^{2}}{2t}\right)\leq(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}}{2t}\right).

Therefore, using
\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)\,dy=P_{\text{out}}
,

Z_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{t})\leq P_{\text{out}}\,(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}}{2t}\right).

Lower bound on
Z_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{t})
.

Since
f
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
\beta
-smooth on
B_{\tau}{}
, and
\nabla f(x^{*})=0
, for all
y\in B_{\tau}{}
,

\frac{\alpha}{2}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq f(y)-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}.

Recalling
p_{0}(y)=p_{0}(x^{*})\exp(-(f(y)-f(x^{*}))/\lambda)
, this implies for all
y\in B_{\tau}{}
,

p_{0}(x^{*})\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)\leq p_{0}(y)\leq p_{0}(x^{*})\exp\!\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\right).

Using
P_{\text{in}}=\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)\,dy
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp(-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\|u\|^{2})\,du=(2\pi\lambda/\alpha)^{d/2}
, we obtain

p_{0}(x^{*})\geq P_{\text{in}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi\lambda}\right)^{d/2}.

For
y\in B_{\tau}{}
, we have
p_{0}(y)\geq p_{0}(x^{*})\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)
, and

p_{t|0}(x_{t}\mid y)=\mathcal{N}(y\mid x_{t},tI)
.
By the product-of-Gaussians identity (with
\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}
and
(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}
as defined in
Equation

67
), we have

\mathcal{N}(y\mid x_{t},tI)\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}\|y-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2(Lt+\lambda)}\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{-d/2}\mathcal{N}(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I).

Integrating the above bound over
y\in B_{\tau}{}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{t})

\displaystyle=\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)\,p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geq p_{0}(x^{*})\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2(\beta t+\lambda)}\|x^{*}-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\beta t+\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{-d/2}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy.

Combining the previous display with
p_{0}(x^{*})\geq P_{\text{in}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi\lambda}\right)^{d/2}
, we obtain

Z_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{t})\geq P_{\text{in}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\right)^{d/2}(\beta t+\lambda)^{-d/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta}{2(\beta t+\lambda)}\|x^{*}-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\text{out}[0|t]}}{P_{\text{in}[0|t]}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{y\notin B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)\,dy}{\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x_{t}\mid y)\,dy}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P_{\text{out}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}{P_{\text{in}}\exp\left(-\frac{\beta}{2(\beta t+\lambda)}\|x^{*}-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{t\alpha}{\beta t+\lambda}\right)^{d/2}\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy.}

(72)

Lower bound on
\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy
.

We now lower bound the Gaussian mass term
\int_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy
appearing in
Equation

72
.
As stated in the theorem,
x_{t}\in\mathcal{R}_{SC}(t)
, i.e.

\|x^{*}-x_{t}\|\leq C_{E}\min({\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}+t}{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}},\sqrt{\frac{\tau^{2}+t}{\tau^{2}}}})D_{\tau}

(73)

This also implies the corresponding bound on the effective mean
\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}
:

\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|=\frac{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq C_{E}D_{\tau}.

Moreover,

\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|=\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\frac{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}\leq C_{E}\sqrt{\frac{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}}{\frac{\lambda}{\beta}+t}}\,D_{\tau}\leq C_{E}D_{\tau},

(74)

Most importantly, from our assumption, we have

\frac{\beta}{Lt+\lambda}\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq C_{E}^{2}\tfrac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}

(75)

Note that

\int_{B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy=P\!\left(\|Z+\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\leq D_{\tau}\right)\geq P\!\left(\|Z\|\leq D_{\tau}-\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\right),

where
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I)
. In particular, if we choose
C_{E}\leq\tfrac{1}{3}
, then
Equation

74
implies
\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
, and hence
D_{\tau}-\|\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime}-x^{*}\|\geq\tfrac{2}{3}D_{\tau}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tau}{}}\mathcal{N}\left(y\mid\mu_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime},(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I\right)dy

\displaystyle\geq P_{Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}I)}\!\left(\|Z\|\leq\tfrac{2}{3}D_{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\geq P_{Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}\!\left(\|Z\|^{2}\leq\frac{4D_{\tau}^{2}}{9(\sigma_{\mathrm{in}}^{\prime})^{2}}\right)\geq P_{Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}\!\left(\|Z\|^{2}\leq\frac{4D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma(\frac{d}{2},\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda})}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}.

(76)

We assume that
\lambda
is upper bounded by a fixed constant
\lambda_{\max}>0
(a practical modeling choice since
\lambda\to\infty
makes
p_{0}
nearly uniform and removes learning signal). Then the incomplete-gamma argument satisfies

\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}\;\geq\;\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{\max}}.

We will require:

\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{\max}}\sim\Theta(d)\geq\tfrac{d}{2}

(77)

Under
Equation

77
, the lower-tail probability
\frac{\gamma(\frac{d}{2},\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\frac{\beta}{\lambda})}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}
is bounded below by a universal constant (e.g.,
\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
for all sufficiently large
d
) by standard Chernoff bounds for
\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2},1)
.

Plugging
Equation

76
into
Equation

72
and using
P_{\text{in}[0|t]}\leq 1
, and as given in the assumption that
P_{\text{out}}\leq P_{\text{in}}
, we have

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}[0|t]}

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{\text{out}}\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{d}{2})}{\gamma(\tfrac{d}{2},\tfrac{2D_{\tau}^{2}\beta}{9\lambda})}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{\beta}{2(\beta t+\lambda)}\|x^{*}-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\alpha t})^{d/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4P_{\text{out}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\alpha t})^{d/2}

(78)

where the second inequality comes from
Equation

75
.

A second (geometric) bound.

Define the inside-distance

\max_{y\in B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|:=\min_{y\in B_{\tau}{}}\|y-x_{t}\|.

Then, by lower bounding the inside mass with
\exp(-\max_{y\in B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}/(2t))
and upper bounding the outside mass with
\exp(-\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}/(2t))
, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\text{out}[0|t]}}{P_{\text{in}[0|t]}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P_{\text{out}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}{P_{\text{in}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\max_{y\in B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}.

(79)

Case analysis for
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}
.

Case 1:
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
.

In this case, we have two bounds. First, using
Equation

78
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}[0|t]}\leq 4P_{\text{out}}\frac{\exp\left(-\tfrac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\alpha t})^{d/2}

(80)

Second, using
Equation

79
, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}[0|t]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P_{\text{out}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}{P_{\text{in}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2t}\max_{y\in B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{P_{\text{out}}\exp\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)}{P_{0}(\|x-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau})\exp\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9}\right)}

(81)

where we have used
\min_{y\notin B_{\tau}}\|y-x_{t}\|^{2}\geq\frac{4}{9}D_{\tau}^{2}
. Since
D_{\tau}\sim\Theta(d)
while
\alpha
and

are constants,

holds for all sufficiently large

.
Therefore,

P_{\text{out}[0|t]}\leq 4P_{\text{out}}

(82)

Case 2:
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|>\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
.

In this case, we upper bound the probability as follows:

\displaystyle P_{\text{out}[0|t]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2t}(D_{\tau}+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2})}

(83)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2P_{\text{out}}}{\exp(-\tfrac{8}{t}(\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|)^{2})}

(84)

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{\text{out}}\exp(8\frac{\tfrac{\lambda}{\beta}+t}{\tfrac{\lambda}{\beta}t}C_{E}^{2}D_{\tau}^{2})

(85)

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{\text{out}}\exp(8\tfrac{\lambda}{\beta}C_{E}^{2}D_{\tau}^{2}),

(86)

where the second inequality uses
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\geq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
(so
D_{\tau}+\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq 4\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|
), and the third inequality uses the convex shrinkage/region condition
Equation

73
.

Step 6: Putting the bounds together.

Next, we plug in the upper bounds for
M_{\mathrm{out}}
from
Equations

70
and
71
and for
P_{\text{out}[0|t]}
from
Equations

80
,
82
and
86
under different conditions into the sufficient condition
Equation

66
to achieve the strong convexity condition.
More specifically, we organize the discussion with the following hierarchy:

•

Primary split (distance):
Case 1/2 by whether
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
or
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\geq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
.

•

Secondary split (tail scale):
Case 1.1/1.2/2.1/2.2 by whether
D_{s}\geq D_{\tau}
or
D_{s}<D_{\tau}
.

•

Tertiary split (time, only when needed):
further split by regimes of
t
to simplify the sufficient conditions.

Case 1:
\|x_{t}-x^{*}\|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}D_{\tau}
.

In this regime we use the simplified bound
Equation

70
.

Case 1.1:
D_{s}\geq D_{\tau}
.

The following inequality is sufficient for
Equation

66
to hold:

12D_{s}^{2}\min(1,4P_{\text{out}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\alpha t})^{d/2})\leq\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t^{2}}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}

where we have used (
80
) and the trivial upper bound of
1
on any probability.
We further simplify the above equation to a sufficient condition, by plugging in
D_{s}=\tau\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{P_{\text{out}}}}

the fact that
x\log(1/x)<\sqrt{x},\forall 0<x<1
.

\displaystyle 48\tau^{2}\sqrt{P_{\text{out}}}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)}{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}(\frac{Lt+\lambda}{\alpha t})^{d/2}\leq\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t^{2}}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}

(87)

Rearranging (
87
) yields the equivalent sufficient condition

48\tau^{2}\sqrt{P_{\text{out}}}\leq\frac{(1-C_{\alpha})t^{2}}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}\frac{\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}{\exp\!\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)}\left(\frac{\alpha t}{Lt+\lambda}\right)^{\!d/2}.

(88)

A sufficient condition for the above is

48\tau^{2}\sqrt{P_{\text{out}}}\leq(1-C_{\alpha})t(\frac{t}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}})^{\tfrac{d}{2}+1}\kappa_{0}^{-\tfrac{d}{2}}\frac{\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)}{\exp\!\left(-\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)},

(89)

where
\kappa_{0}=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
is the condition number. Since
\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_{E}^{2}\frac{\beta}{\lambda}D_{\tau}^{2}\right)\leq 1
, it suffices to lower bound the right-hand side. We focus on bounding the following auxiliary function (local to Case 1.1):

For
t>0
, define

\mathcal{F}_{1.1}(t)\;:=\;t\left(\frac{t}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}\right)^{\tfrac{d}{2}+1}\exp\!\left(\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right).

Subcase 1.1.a:
0<t<2\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}
.

In this regime, we have the uniform lower bound

\mathcal{F}_{1.1}(t)\;\geq\;c_{1.1,\mathrm{lb}},

where

C_{1.1}:=\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9},\qquad a_{1.1}:=\frac{d}{4}+\frac{3}{2}=\frac{d+6}{4},

and

c_{1.1,\mathrm{lb}}:=(3\kappa_{0})^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\left(\frac{e\,C_{1.1}}{a_{1.1}}\right)^{a_{1.1}}=(3\kappa_{0})^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}\left(\frac{8eD_{\tau}^{2}}{9(d+6)}\right)^{\tfrac{d+6}{4}}.

With the assumption
\lambda\leq 2\alpha
, and
t<2\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}
, we can derive that

\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}\;\geq\;\kappa_{0}\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\;\geq\;\frac{\lambda}{\alpha},

where we used
\kappa_{0}=\tfrac{\beta}{\alpha}
in the last inequality.
Together with
t<2\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}
this yields

t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\;\leq\;2\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}+\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}=3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}.

Hence

\frac{t}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}\;\geq\;\frac{t}{3\kappa_{0}\sqrt{t}}=\frac{\sqrt{t}}{3\kappa_{0}}.

Plugging this into
\mathcal{F}_{1.1}(t)
gives

\mathcal{F}_{1.1}(t)\;\geq\;t\left(\frac{\sqrt{t}}{3\kappa_{0}}\right)^{\tfrac{d}{2}+1}\exp\!\left(\frac{2D_{\tau}^{2}}{9t}\right)=(3\kappa_{0})^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}t^{a_{1.1}}\exp\!\left(\frac{C_{1.1}}{t}\right),

with
a_{1.1}
and
C_{1.1}
as defined above.

Define

h_{1.1}(t):=t^{a_{1.1}}\exp\!\left(\frac{C_{1.1}}{t}\right),\qquad t>0.

Then

\log h_{1.1}(t)=a_{1.1}\log t+\frac{C_{1.1}}{t},\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log h_{1.1}(t)=\frac{a_{1.1}}{t}-\frac{C_{1.1}}{t^{2}}=\frac{a_{1.1}t-C_{1.1}}{t^{2}}.

Thus
\log h_{1.1}
has a unique critical point at

t_{1.1}^{*}=\frac{C_{1.1}}{a_{1.1}}>0,

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\log h_{1.1}(t)=-\frac{a_{1.1}}{t^{2}}+\frac{2C_{1.1}}{t^{3}},\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\log h_{1.1}(t_{1.1}^{*})=\frac{C_{1.1}}{(t_{1.1}^{*})^{3}}>0.

Hence
t_{1.1}^{*}
is the global minimizer of
h_{1.1}
on
(0,\infty)
, and

h_{1.1}(t)\;\geq\;h_{1.1}(t_{1.1}^{*})=\left(\frac{C_{1.1}}{a_{1.1}}\right)^{a_{1.1}}\exp\!\left(\frac{C_{1.1}}{t_{1.1}^{*}}\right)=\left(\frac{C_{1.1}}{a_{1.1}}\right)^{a_{1.1}}e^{a_{1.1}}=\left(\frac{e\,C_{1.1}}{a_{1.1}}\right)^{a_{1.1}},\quad\forall\,t>0.

Combining this with the previous inequality yields

Here we provide the proof for

Step 1: Setup and Reduction via Strong Convexity.

By the strong convexity of
g(x;t)
established in
Theorem

4.3
, the function
g(x;t)
is
\frac{C_{\alpha}\lambda}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}
-strongly convex within the region
\mathcal{R}_{SC}(t)
. Since
x_{t}^{*}
is the minimizer of
g(x;t)
and
x^{*}
lies within the strongly convex region, the distance to the optimizer is bounded by the gradient norm at the original optimizer
x^{*}
:

\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\|\leq\frac{1}{\frac{C_{\alpha}\lambda}{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}}\|\nabla g(x^{*};t)\|=\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}\lambda}\|\nabla g(x^{*};t)\|.

Using Tweedie’s formula, the gradient at
x^{*}
is given by

\nabla g(x^{*};t)=\frac{\lambda}{t}(x^{*}-\mathbb{E}[y|x^{*}]).

Without loss of generality, we assume
x^{*}=0
in this proof. Consequently, the problem reduces to bounding the conditional expectation
\|\mathbb{E}[y|x^{*}]\|
. Therefore, we have the reduction:

\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\|\leq\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}t}\|\mathbb{E}[y|x^{*}]\|.

Step 2: Decomposition.

We decompose the expectation into contributions from inside and outside the ball
B_{\tau}
:

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}[y|x^{*}]\|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\|\mathbb{E}[y|y\in B_{\tau},x^{*}]\|\cdot P(y\in B_{\tau}|x^{*})}_{\text{Term I: Local Conditional Mean}}+\underbrace{\|\mathbb{E}[y|y\notin B_{\tau},x^{*}]\|\cdot P(y\notin B_{\tau}|x^{*})}_{\text{Term II: Tail Conditional Mean}}.

(102)

And for the bias bound, we have

\displaystyle\|x_{t}^{*}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\underbrace{\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}t}\|\mathbb{E}[y|y\in B_{\tau},x^{*}]\|\cdot P(y\in B_{\tau}|x^{*})}_{\text{Term I: Local Asymmetry}}+\underbrace{`\frac{t+\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}}{C_{\alpha}t}\|\mathbb{E}[y|y\notin B_{\tau},x^{*}]\|\cdot P(y\notin B_{\tau}|x^{*})^{\prime}}_{\text{Term II: Tail Contribution}}\right)

(103)

Step 3: Bounding Term I (Local Asymmetry).

We now bound the contribution to the bias coming from the
local
region
B_{\tau}=\{y:\|y-x^{*}\|\leq D_{\tau}\}
, where
f
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
\beta
-smooth.
Intuitively, if
f
were perfectly symmetric around
x^{*}
, then the posterior mean under the Gaussian smoothing would remain centered and this term would vanish.
Thus, Term I captures the worst-case
directional asymmetry
of the local landscape permitted by the curvature bounds
(\alpha,\beta)
.
Formally, it suffices to control the directional conditional mean
\mathbb{E}[\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle\mid x^{*},\,y\in B_{\tau}]
uniformly over unit vectors
\hat{e}
; we will upper bound this quantity by replacing
p_{0}(y)\propto e^{-f(y)/\lambda}
on
B_{\tau}
with extremal envelopes consistent with the local curvature constraints.

(3.1) Worst-case envelope inside
B_{\tau}
.

On
B_{\tau}=\{\|y\|\leq D_{\tau}\}
, by
\alpha
-strong convexity and
\beta
-smoothness around

x^{*}
(with
\nabla f(x^{*})=0
), we have

\frac{\alpha}{2}\|y\|^{2}\leq f(y)-f(0)\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\|y\|^{2}.

Since
p_{0}(y)\propto\exp(-f(y)/\lambda)
, this implies the pointwise envelope

\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}\|y\|^{2}\Big)\;\leq\;\frac{p_{0}(y)}{p_{0}(0)}\;\leq\;\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\|y\|^{2}\Big),\qquad y\in B_{\tau}.

Define
\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y):=\exp(-\frac{\beta}{2\lambda}\|y\|^{2})
and

\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y):=\exp(-\frac{\alpha}{2\lambda}\|y\|^{2})
. We omit
p_{0}(0)
in the following calculations as it is a constant appearing in both numerator and denominator.

(3.2) Directional conditional mean bound.

We calculate the conditional expectation separately for two hemispheres: one where
\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0
and the other where
\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0|t}[\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle|x^{*},y\in B_{\tau}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{B_{\tau}}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}{\int_{B_{\tau}}p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy+\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}{\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy+\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}p_{0}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy+\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}{\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy+\int_{B_{\tau},\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y)p_{t\mid 0}(x^{*}\mid y)dy}

(104)

The inequality in (
104
) follows from the pointwise envelope

\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y)\geq p_{0}(y)/p_{0}(0)\geq\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y)
on
B_{\tau}
:
on the half-space
\{y_{1}>0\}
the integrand
\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle=y_{1}
is nonnegative, so replacing

by the upper envelope increases the numerator;
on
\{y_{1}<0\}
the integrand is nonpositive, so replacing

by the lower envelope increases the numerator (makes it less negative).
In the denominator the integrand is nonnegative everywhere, hence using
\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}
on
\{y_{1}>0\}
and
\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}
on

yields an upper bound on the ratio.

At each radius

Then (

\frac{\int_{\|y\|=r,\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y)p_{t|0}(x^{*}|y)dy+\int_{\|y\|=r,\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y)p_{t|0}(x^{*}|y)dy}{\int_{\|y\|=r,\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle>0}\tilde{p}_{0}^{\max}(y)p_{t|0}(x^{*}|y)dy+\int_{\|y\|=r,\langle y,\hat{e}\rangle<0}\tilde{p}_{0}^{\min}(y)p_{t|0}(x^{*}|y)dy}.

(106)

B.7
Variance Fraction.

The total variance of a random variable
X
(scalar or vector) can be decomposed based on a partition of the sample space. Consider a partition into a set
\mathbb{A}
and its complement
\mathbb{A}^{\complement}
. Let
P(\mathbb{A})
be the probability that an outcome is in
\mathbb{A}
, and
P(\mathbb{A}^{\complement})=1-P(\mathbb{A})
. Let
\mu_{\mathbb{A}}=\mathbb{E}[X|X\in\mathbb{A}]
and
\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}=\mathbb{E}[X|X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement}]
. Then the decomposition is:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}(X)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{P(\mathbb{A})\operatorname{Var}(X|X\in\mathbb{A})+P(\mathbb{A}^{\complement})\operatorname{Var}(X|X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement})}_{\text{Expected Conditional Variance (Variance within groups)}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{P(\mathbb{A})P(\mathbb{A}^{\complement})\left(\mu_{\mathbb{A}}-\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}\right)\left(\mu_{\mathbb{A}}-\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}\right)^{T}}_{\text{Variance of Conditional Expectations (Variance between groups)}}.

This lemma is often used to break down the overall variability of
X
into components attributable to variability within specified subgroups and variability between these subgroups.

Proof.

Let
p:=P(\mathbb{A})
so that
P(\mathbb{A}^{\complement})=1-p
.
Define the conditional means

\mu_{\mathbb{A}}:=\mathbb{E}[X\mid X\in\mathbb{A}],\qquad\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}:=\mathbb{E}[X\mid X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement}].

By the law of total expectation,

\mathbb{E}[X]\;=\;p\,\mu_{\mathbb{A}}+(1-p)\,\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}.

Next, decompose the second moment via the same partition:

\mathbb{E}[XX^{T}]\;=\;p\,\mathbb{E}[XX^{T}\mid X\in\mathbb{A}]+(1-p)\,\mathbb{E}[XX^{T}\mid X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement}].

Within each conditional expectation insert

\operatorname{Var}(X\mid X\in\mathbb{A})\;=\;\mathbb{E}[XX^{T}\mid X\in\mathbb{A}]-\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\mu_{\mathbb{A}}^{T},

and its analogue for
\mathbb{A}^{\complement}
, to find

\mathbb{E}[XX^{T}]\;=\;p\Bigl(\operatorname{Var}(X\mid X\in\mathbb{A})+\mu_{\mathbb{A}}\mu_{\mathbb{A}}^{T}\Bigr)+(1-p)\Bigl(\operatorname{Var}(X\mid X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement})+\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}^{T}\Bigr).

Subtracting
\mathbb{E}[X]\,\mathbb{E}[X]^{T}
yields

\operatorname{Var}(X)\;=\;p\,\operatorname{Var}(X\mid X\in\mathbb{A})+(1-p)\,\operatorname{Var}(X\mid X\in\mathbb{A}^{\complement})+p(1-p)\bigl(\mu_{\mathbb{A}}-\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}\bigr)\bigl(\mu_{\mathbb{A}}-\mu_{\mathbb{A}^{\complement}}\bigr)^{T}.

Recalling
p=P(\mathbb{A})
completes the decomposition.
∎

B.8

a

(113)

\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{2}\,N(x\mid\eta,\sigma^{2})\,dx=\eta^{2}[1-\Phi(z)]+2\eta\sigma\!\int_{z}^{\infty}u\,\phi(u)\,du+\sigma^{2}\!\int_{z}^{\infty}u^{2}\phi(u)\,du.

We already have

\int_{z}^{\infty}u^{2}\phi(u)\,du=z\,\phi(z)+[1-\Phi(z)]

\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{2}\,N(x\mid\eta,\sigma^{2})\,dx=(\eta^{2}+\sigma^{2})[1-\Phi(z)]+\sigma\phi(z)\,(2\eta+\sigma z).

It is convenient to express this in terms of the truncated-normal mean and variance. With

\Phi_{\mu}=\eta+\sigma\lambda(z)
and

\Phi_{\sigma}=\sigma^{2}\bigl(1+z\lambda(z)-\lambda(z)^{2}\bigr)

\Phi_{\sigma}+(\Phi_{\mu})^{2}=\eta^{2}+\sigma^{2}+2\eta\sigma\lambda(z)+\sigma^{2}z\lambda(z).

Multiplying by
1-\Phi(z)
yields exactly the previous expression for

\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{2}N(x\mid\eta,\sigma^{2})\,dx
. Substituting into
(
113
) with

gives (iii).

\displaystyle k_{g}>\max_{m}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{m}k_{b}}{1-\tilde{\rho}_{m}}\geq\frac{\kappa_{0}^{2}(524288\kappa_{0}^{6}+57344\kappa_{0}^{4}+2304\kappa_{0}^{2}+32)k_{b}}{512\kappa_{0}^{4}+56\kappa_{0}^{2}+1}

Plugging in step size and smoothness, we get:

\displaystyle K_{m}^{2}+\sigma_{m}^{2}\leq\frac{4E_{0}r_{\text{min}}\beta_{1}^{2}\delta}{3M}
