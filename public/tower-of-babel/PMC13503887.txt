Numerical investigation of subtle feature of geometry effects on magneto-active elastomer cylindrical actuators
Authors: Sandeep Kumar, Ramesh Gupta Burela, Arpit Kumar Srivastava
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Magneto-active elastomers (MAEs) are smart composite materials capable of undergoing large deformation when subjected to external magnetic fields, making them attractive for soft actuation and adaptive structures. In this study, a nonlinear finite element framework is developed to investigate the magnetically induced deformation behaviour of cylindrical MAE actuators subjected to radial magnetic loading. The magneto-mechanical response of the material is described using a continuum magneto-elastic constitutive model implemented through a user-defined material subroutine (VUMAT) within the ABAQUS/Explicit environment. The developed numerical model is used to examine the influence of the dimensionless geometric parameters α and β, representing the diameter-to-length and thickness-to-length ratios, on the deformation and stress distribution of the actuator. Parametric studies reveal that increasing these geometric aspect-ratio parameters significantly enhances circumferential deformation while producing localized circumferential stress concentration near the fixed boundary, which is identified as a critical region for failure initiation in practical actuator designs. The results further demonstrate a nonlinear interaction between actuator geometry and magnetic loading that strongly influences actuator performance. The findings provide insight into the role of geometry in magnetically driven soft actuators and offer useful guidelines for the design and optimization of MAE-based cylindrical actuation systems.

Introduction

Magneto-active elastomers (MAEs), also referred to as magnetorheological elastomers (MREs), are smart composite materials composed of micron-sized ferromagnetic particles embedded within a compliant elastomeric matrix. When subjected to an external magnetic field, particle interactions generate internal body forces that induce significant macroscopic deformation and alter the effective mechanical stiffness of the material1 . These field-dependent properties enable tunable stiffness, controllable deformation, and adaptive damping behaviour, making MAEs highly attractive for applications in soft robotics2, adaptive structures, vibration isolation, and magnetically driven actuators3.

The mechanical response of MAEs arises from the strong coupling between magnetic particles and the surrounding polymer matrix. When an external magnetic field is applied, dipole–dipole interactions between particles, along with field-driven rearrangement, generate internal stresses that lead to deformation of the elastomer network. Previous experimental and computational studies have shown that this behaviour depends significantly on factors such as particle distribution, magnetic boundary conditions, and field strength4–6. In particular, microstructural features including particle chaining, anisotropy, and field-induced restructuring play an important role in determining the overall macroscopic response.

From a theoretical standpoint, MAEs are typically described within the framework of nonlinear continuum magneto-elasticity, where deformation and magnetic fields are treated as coupled variables in a thermodynamically consistent manner7,8. In this approach, constitutive models are formulated using energy functions that include elastic, volumetric, and magnetic contributions, allowing prediction of large deformation, stress evolution, and field-induced actuation9. Finite-strain formulations for magnetoelastic materials have been developed to provide a consistent description of these coupled interactions and their influence on mechanical behaviour10,11.

Analytical studies have contributed significantly to understanding magnetoelastic structures with relatively simple geometries. Cylindrical and tubular configurations, for example, have been examined to study inflation, extension, and torsion under magnetic loading10,12. Similarly, the stability and bifurcation behaviour of membranes and thin structures, including wrinkling and limit-point instabilities, have been widely investigated13–15. While these studies provide useful physical insight, they are generally limited to idealized geometries and simplified loading conditions.

To overcome these limitations, finite element-based computational approaches have been widely adopted. Such methods enable the analysis of large deformation, nonlinear material behaviour, and complex boundary conditions. For instance, Haldar et al.16 studied rate-dependent magneto-active polymer response, while Liu et al.17 developed a coupled magneto-mechanical framework for slender structures undergoing large deformation and instability. The use of user-defined material subroutines has further expanded modelling capabilities; Dohmen and Kraus18, for example, proposed anisotropic magneto-mechanical formulations for structured MAEs. Additional contributions include boundary-value simulations under non-uniform magnetic fields, where fully coupled magneto-mechanical finite element frameworks incorporating electromagnetic coils, surrounding air, and material nonlinearities have been developed to capture realistic device behaviour19, as well as microstructure-informed continuum models that account for particle-level interactions20.

More recent work has focused on developing unified computational frameworks capable of capturing nonlinear, viscoelastic, and fully coupled magneto-mechanical behaviour. Rambausek et al.21 presented a comprehensive finite element framework for both magnetically hard and soft viscoelastic MREs, incorporating three-dimensional coupling and dissipative effects. Further developments by Danas and co-workers have addressed key aspects of constitutive modelling, including stretch-dependent magnetization and magneto-viscoelastic behaviour under finite deformation22,23. These advances highlight the increasing ability of modern models to represent realistic MAE behaviour across multiple length scales.

The broader literature on magnetically responsive soft structures also includes planar membranes, beams, plates, and tubular configurations under static and dynamic magnetic loading. Studies on planar MAE sheets and membranes have examined field-induced deformation, vibration, wrinkling, and instability13–15,24, while beam- and plate-type configurations have been used to understand magnetically induced bending, buckling, vibration, and morphing in soft magneto-active structures16,17,21,23. Tubular MAE structures are particularly relevant to the present work because their actuation response is governed by the coupled evolution of radial deformation, circumferential stretch, and hoop stress10–12,25. These studies establish that geometry is not a secondary modelling detail; rather, it directly controls the deformation mode, stability margin, and functional actuation range of magneto-active structures.

Similar geometry-dependent actuation behaviour has also been reported in electro-active elastomers, where electric-field-induced Maxwell stresses govern deformation, wrinkling, and instability26–29. This connection is important because both electro-active and magneto-active elastomers belong to a broader class of field-responsive soft actuators in which external fields generate stresses that interact strongly with large deformation kinematics. Recent studies on electro-magneto-active elastomers further show that combining electric and magnetic fields can enhance actuation efficiency and controllability compared with single-field actuation30–33. The present study focuses on the purely magneto-active case as a foundational step, while the geometric parametrization and response maps developed here can support future extensions to coupled electro-magneto-active cylindrical actuators.

Despite these developments, most existing studies primarily emphasize constitutive modelling, microstructural homogenization, magneto-viscoelastic effects, or fully coupled field formulations8,18,20–23, with comparatively less focus on actuator-level geometric parametrization. In particular, cylindrical MAE actuators—commonly used in soft robotic and tubular actuation systems—exhibit deformation behaviour that is strongly influenced by geometric parameters such as diameter-to-length ratio and wall thickness10–12,25. However, a systematic investigation of how these dimensionless geometric parameters jointly influence magnetically induced actuation remains limited.

In the present work, a nonlinear finite element framework is developed to study the magnetically induced actuation behaviour of cylindrical MAE tubes. The actuator is modelled using shell elements to efficiently represent thin-walled structures, and the magneto-elastic constitutive response is implemented through a user-defined material subroutine (VUMAT) within the ABAQUS/Explicit environment. The novelty of the work lies in combining a magneto-elastic VUMAT implementation with shell-based cylindrical actuator modelling and response-surface analysis in terms of the dimensionless geometric parameters α =D/L and β =t/L. Unlike studies focused primarily on constitutive or microstructural aspects7,8,18,20,21, the present analysis directly provides actuator-level design guidelines for tuning radial expansion, circumferential stretch, and stress concentration through geometry. The results therefore provide detailed insight into the coupled magneto-mechanical behaviour and offer useful guidance for the design of cylindrical MAE actuators.

Magneto-elastic theoretical formulation

Magneto-active elastomers exhibit a strong coupling between mechanical deformation and magnetic fields. Their behaviour is commonly described within the framework of nonlinear continuum magneto-elasticity, in which deformation and magnetic fields are treated as coupled variables within a thermodynamically consistent constitutive formulation7,8. In this setting, the mechanical response is governed by continuum kinematics, while the influence of the magnetic field is incorporated through a magneto-elastic strain energy function.

The formulation adopted here is closely related to continuum modelling approaches previously developed for electro-active polymers and dielectric elastomer devices26,27. In those studies, the interaction between electric fields and large elastic deformation was described using an energy-based framework within finite element simulations. The same idea is extended in the present work by replacing the electric field contribution with magnetic field effects, allowing the modelling of magnetically induced deformation in elastomeric materials.

The governing kinematics and constitutive relations used in this study follow the formulation developed for cylindrical magneto-elastic tubes25. Within this framework, the total free energy of the material is expressed as a function of both deformation and magnetic field variables. The corresponding stress measures and magnetic interactions are then obtained from the derivatives of this energy with respect to the relevant field variables. This approach provides a consistent description of large deformation, stress evolution, and magnetically driven actuation in magneto-active elastomer structures. The key governing equations are summarized in the following sections for completeness.

Numerical modelling

A nonlinear finite element framework was developed to analyse the magnetically induced deformation of cylindrical MAE actuators. Simulations were performed in ABAQUS/Explicit using a user-defined material subroutine (VUMAT) implementing the magneto-mechanical constitutive formulation described earlier. The numerical model captures the coupled interaction between magnetic loading and large elastic deformation of the elastomer structure.

Geometry of cylindrical actuator

The actuator considered in this study is a hollow cylindrical MAE tube defined by its length L, diameter D, and wall thickness t. Such cylindrical configurations are commonly employed in soft actuators and adaptive structures.

The cylindrical structure was discretized using four-node reduced integration shell elements (S4R), which are well suited for thin-walled structures undergoing large deformation. Reduced integration improves computational efficiency and avoids shear locking.

Five integration points were specified through the shell thickness to capture stress and strain variation across the actuator wall. The mesh density was selected to accurately represent both circumferential and axial deformation modes.

Although an axisymmetric simplification is possible for the idealized geometry and prescribed radial magnetic loading considered here, the computational cost of the adopted model remained modest; therefore, the present study focuses on geometric parametrization of actuator response rather than computational-time reduction.

Boundary conditions, loading and solution procedure

The cylindrical actuator was modelled with one end fully constrained in all translational and rotational degrees of freedom to prevent rigid body motion, while the opposite end was left free to deform. The magnetic loading was introduced directly through the constitutive model implemented in the VUMAT subroutine. Rather than applying external magnetic forces within the finite element model, the referential magnetic flux density \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textbf{B}_0$$\end{document} was treated as a prescribed time-dependent material variable. A linearly increasing magnetic field was applied from zero to 434 mT over a total simulation duration of one second. During each explicit time increment, the instantaneous magnetic flux density was evaluated and incorporated into the stress update through the magneto-mechanical energy contribution.

The simulations were carried out using the ABAQUS/Explicit solver with nonlinear geometric effects taken into account. An explicit time integration scheme was employed due to its suitability for large-deformation and highly nonlinear material response. The reported results correspond to the quasi-static actuation response obtained during the monotonic magnetic loading process. Owing to the slow loading rate and the use of numerical bulk viscosity available within ABAQUS/Explicit as a solver-level stabilization mechanism, transient inertial effects remained negligible and the results effectively represent equilibrium configurations at successive magnetic load levels. The numerical bulk viscosity is not a material damping term in the Ogden model; it is an algorithmic stabilization feature used to suppress spurious high-frequency oscillations associated with explicit time integration. Throughout the simulations, field outputs such as nodal displacements, stresses, strains, reaction forces, and internal state variables were recorded to assess the deformation and stress response of the actuator under magnetic loading.

Results

The magneto–elastic finite element framework developed in this work was used to investigate the actuation behaviour of a cylindrical magneto-active elastomer actuator subjected to a time-dependent magnetic field. The response of the actuator is analysed using normalized deformation and stress measures in order to enable comparison across different geometric and material configurations.

In order to investigate the influence of actuator geometry, two dimensionless geometric parameters are introduced. The parameter α represents the diameter-to-length ratio of the cylindrical actuator, while β represents the thickness-to-length ratio. These parameters are defined as\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha =\frac{D}{L}, \qquad \beta =\frac{t}{L}$$\end{document}where D is the cylinder diameter, t is the wall thickness, and L is the actuator length. The parameters α and β therefore characterize the geometric aspect ratio of the cylindrical actuator and play an important role in determining its magnetically induced deformation behaviour.

The parameter α =D/L characterizes the geometric slenderness of the cylindrical actuator. A smaller value of α represents a slender, elongated tube, whereas \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \approx 1$$\end{document} represents a compact cylindrical actuator. This ratio governs the relative contribution of radial and axial deformation modes under magnetic loading. The parameter β =t/L characterizes the relative wall thickness of the cylindrical shell. For a fixed length L, increasing β increases the wall thickness and therefore modifies both the structural stiffness and the magnetic energy integrated over the actuator wall. Together, α and β provide a compact dimensionless description of the actuator geometry.

The combined effect is a stronger circumferential stretch-dominated actuation mode for larger α and β.

The axial stretch-related percentage change varies approximately between 5.6% and 12.3% across the explored parameter space, showing moderate dependence on the geometric parameters. Overall, the response surface analysis reveals that the highest actuation regime occurs in the region of high α and high β, where both radial deformation and circumferential stretch are maximized. These results provide design guidelines for MAE-based cylindrical actuators by showing how geometric aspect ratio and wall thickness can be tuned to enhance radial actuation.

Design guidelines for MAE cylindrical actuators

The response surface analysis presented in Fig. 4 can be interpreted as a practical design map for magneto-active elastomer cylindrical actuators. The results indicate that actuator performance is strongly governed by the combined influence of the geometric parameters α and β. Higher values of α and β enhance the radial actuation capability, resulting in larger circumferential deformation and radial displacement. In particular, the region corresponding to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha \approx 1$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta \approx 0.011$$\end{document} produces the largest deformation response and is suitable for applications requiring large radial actuation. Conversely, smaller α values may be preferred when improved axial stability and reduced circumferential stress concentration are required.

The present findings are relevant for the design of magneto-active soft robotic systems and adaptive tubular structures. Cylindrical MAE actuators capable of large radial deformation are promising for applications such as soft robotic grippers, adaptive biomedical catheters, vibration isolation systems, morphing structures, and magnetically tunable compliant devices. The wall-thickness parameter β can be adjusted through moulding or layer-by-layer fabrication to tune the actuation level, while the diameter-to-length ratio α can be selected to balance radial actuation against axial stability. The present model also does not include magneto-viscoelasticity, magnetic saturation, damage, fatigue, or bifurcation-based instability analysis. These effects can be important under cyclic loading, high field intensity, or large deformation. Future work should therefore focus on experimental validation under controlled magnetic loading, incorporation of particle anisotropy and magnetic field gradients, extension to magneto-viscoelastic and saturation-aware constitutive models, and development of fully coupled electromagnetic–structural simulations for practical actuator systems.
