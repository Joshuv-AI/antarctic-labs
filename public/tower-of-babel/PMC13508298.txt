A nomogram to predict postoperative deep vein thrombosis combined with pulmonary embolism in patients with a tibial plateau fracture: a retrospective study
Authors: Tang Zhuo-Dong, Wang Ming-You, Song Xun-Zhou, Liu Shao-Jiang, Lan Yu-Ping, Wang Hong-Ping, Li Ting-Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDeep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) are serious postoperative complications in patients with tibial plateau fractures. The aim of this study was to investigate the risk factors for postoperative DVT complicated with PE in patients with tibial plateau fractures, establish a risk prediction model and verify its predictive efficacy.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective study from January 1, 2023, to December 31, 2025. The primary endpoint of this study was the risk factors for DVT complicated with PE after tibial plateau fracture. Univariate analysis and multifactor logistic regression analysis were performed to analyze the relevant risk factors, and nomogram models were established to verify the predictive efficacy of the model via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) curves.ResultsAmong the 224 patients, 36 (16.07%) developed DVT complicated with PE after surgery. Univariate analysis revealed significant differences in age (P = 0.001), time from injury to surgery (P = 0.041), intraoperative blood loss (P = 0.008) and D-dimer level (P < 0.001) between the two groups. Multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that age (OR = 1.051, 95% CI = 1.003–1.102, P = 0.036) and D-dimer level (OR = 1.001, 95% CI = 1.000–1.001, P < 0.001) were independent risk factors for postoperative DVT complicated with PE in patients with tibial plateau fractures. The ROC curve revealed that the area under the curve (AUC) of the nomogram model was 0.779, the χ2 value of the calibration curve was 11.295, P = 0.186, and the DCA curve indicated that the net clinical benefit of the model was high within the threshold range.ConclusionAge ≥ 52 years and a D-dimer level ≥ 4131.5 ng/mL are independent risk factors for postoperative deep vein thrombosis (DVT) complicated with pulmonary embolism (PE) in patients with tibial plateau fractures. The constructed nomogram model therefore has a certain predictive efficacy.Keywords: Pulmonary embolism, Deep vein thrombosis, Tibial plateau fracture, Risk factors, Predictive model

A tibial plateau fracture is an intra-articular injury that represents approximately 1% of all fractures in the body and 32% of fractures around the knee joint [1]. Owing to extensive soft tissue trauma after injury, vascular injury, prolonged limb immobilization, and weakened muscle pump function, patients are prone to developing deep vein thrombosis (DVT) [2, 3]. If not treated promptly, the detachment of thrombi during the acute phase can lead to pulmonary embolism (PE), which seriously affects patient prognosis and may even result in death [4]. DVT and PE are collectively referred to as venous thromboembolism (VTE), which are manifestations of the same disease in different stages [5, 6]. The development of better strategies for preventing and treating VTE will likely lead to a better treatment effect and prognosis for patients. To develop better prevention and treatment strategies for VTE in patients with tibial plateau fractures, research on the occurrence patterns and risk factors for VTE is necessary. Clinical prediction models have been increasingly explored in medical research in recent years. A nomogram is a type of visual chart-based tool that can predict the occurrence of a specific outcome event using multiple risk factors. It is repeatable, personalized and targeted [7, 8]. At present, there are few prediction models for DVT and PE in patients with tibial plateau fractures. In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis of relevant data from patients with tibial plateau fractures to explore the risk factors for postoperative DVT and PE and established a nomogram model to provide a basis for early prevention and early diagnosis.

Methods

The clinical data of 224 patients with tibial plateau fracture who developed postoperative DVT admitted to Panzhihua Central Hospital from January 1, 2023 to December 31, 2025 were retrospectively analyzed. There were 150 males and 74 females with an average age of 52.97 ± 10.72 years. 10 events per variable(10EPV) was used to estimate the sample size, there were a total of 36 positive outcome events in the 224 samples in this study, slightly below the ideal 40 positive outcome events. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who 18 years or older; (2) patients who had a unilateral fresh closed fracture; (3) patients who underwent open reduction and internal fixation surgery; (4) patients who had complete medical records. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who had fractures or injuries in other parts; (2) patients who had open fractures or pathological fractures; (3) patients who had a preexisting hematological disease or a history of lower extremity DVT. Patients were divided into a PE group (36 patients) and a non-PE group (188 patients) according to the results of their pulmonary artery CTA. This study was reviewed by the Medical Ethics Committee of Panzhihua Central Hospital (Pankelun Review No. [2024-067]).

All patients received a subcutaneous injection of low-molecular-weight heparin (0.4 mL, once a day) to prevent DVT after admission, and mechanical prophylaxis (plantar venous pump, 30 min each time, twice a day) was added for patients with negative findings on lower extremity vascular ultrasound at admission. Anticoagulation was stopped 24 h before surgery and resumed 24 h after surgery until discharge. For patients with positive findings on vascular ultrasound, mechanical preventive measures were stopped immediately, and the drug preventive strategy was adjusted to the subcutaneous injection of low-molecular-weight heparin (0.4 mL, twice a day).

The general data of the two groups of patients were collected from the electronic medical record management system at our hospital: age, sex, BMI, side, injury mechanism, fracture type (Schatzker classification), history of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease, history of diabetes, smoking history, time from injury to operation, operation time, incision length, and intraoperative blood loss. Hb, HCT, PLT, D-dimer, FIB, PT, and APTT were measured 24 h after surgery. A flowchart of the study is shown in Fig. 1.

The flow chart of the study

Microsoft Excel 2019 was used to summarize the collected data, establish the original data table and assign values. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics 22.0 (IBM, USA) software. The Kolmogorov‒Smirnov test was used to test whether the measurement data were normally distributed. Normally distributed measurement data are expressed as and were compared between groups using independent sample t tests, nonnormally distributed measurement data are expressed as M(P25,P75) and were compared between groups using the Mann‒Whitney U rank sum test, and count data are expressed as rates (%) and were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The VIN in R Studio 4.3.3 software was used to detect the presence of missing data items in the data set, for items with missing data, the mean value of the previous data and the latter data of the missing item was used to fill in to ensure that there was no empty item, the dataset has been checked and no missing data items were found. The independent risk factors for DVT combined with PE were determined via forward logistic regression analysis of the factors with a P value < 0.05 in the univariate analysis. The independent risk factors were introduced into R Studio 4.3.3, and rms, pROC, and modEvA were used to construct a nomogram model that could predict the risk of postoperative DVT with PE in patients with tibial plateau fractures. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn to evaluate the predictive value of the model, and a calibration curve was drawn to verify the accuracy of the model. A clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) curve was drawn to verify the clinical validity of the model.

Discussion

Vascular endothelial injury, slow venous blood flow and hypercoagulable state of blood are still considered to be the three major factors of thrombosis. Tibial plateau fracture can directly or indirectly cause damage or stimulation to the lower extremity vein wall. During the operation, the reduction and traction of the fracture block and the leverage of the retractor will also cause certain damage to the lower extremity vein. Secondly, fracture patients need strict lower limb immobilization, resulting in lower limb swelling and prolonged bed rest, which can lead to slow blood flow and blood stasis. However, for trauma patients, the hypercoagulable state of blood will exist from the beginning of injury and even continue throughout the perioperative period, which increases the incidence of lower extremity thrombosis in patients with tibial plateau fracture. However, considering that the injury energy leading to tibial plateau fracture usually rarely passes above the knee joint, there is less damage to the blood vessels and soft tissues above the knee joint. After tibial plateau fracture, the muscle activity around and below the knee joint is limited, but the proximal muscles such as quadriceps femoris can contract and train to accelerate venous return. These factors help to reduce the occurrence of thrombosis. Wang et al. [9] showed that the preoperative and postoperative thrombosis rates of tibial plateau fracture were 23.9% and 45.3%, respectively, which were lower than those of femoral condyle fracture with the same periknee fracture. The reason was that the gastrocnemius muscle would pull the femoral condyle flexion, varus, and posterior rotation after the fracture, compressing or hurting the posterior popliteal vein. It causes vasospasm, causes the reduction of lumen diameter and vascular endothelial damage, and provides conditions for thrombosis.

As DVT in patients with tibial plateau fracture is mostly distal and local asymptomatic thrombosis, it is difficult to find in time using only physical examination findings or symptoms, thus there is a high rate of missed diagnosis. Without early diagnosis and rapid intervention, it can develop into proximal venous thrombosis and even become complicated with PE [10], which is life-threatening. Therefore, the early identification of high-risk patients is very important. The factors influencing lower extremity DVT complicated with PE after surgery for tibial plateau fracture were analyzed in this study, providing a basis for the development of strategies to prevent thrombosis in high-risk patients.

Studies [11, 12] revealed that the risk of DVT increases with age. The risk of DVT in patients aged 36–50 years and 61–65 years is 2.49 and 2.59 times greater than that in patients aged 18–35 years, respectively. Compared with that in young patients, the risk of DVT in patients over 65 years of age was 4.02 times greater. In this study, age was considered an independent risk factor for DVT complicated with PE; the best cutoff value for age was 52 years, and the risk odds ratio increased 1.051 times with increasing age. The reason was that elderly patients had less vascular elasticity and were more fragile and that the vascular intima was severely damaged after trauma, which increased the production of procoagulant substances. Moreover, elderly patients are less active, thus the anti-inflammatory response and anticoagulant ability of the vascular endothelium is weakened, which exacerbates the imbalance of coagulation mechanisms and makes DVT more likely to occur [13]. Although age is an independent risk factor, its predictive sensitivity can reach 86.1%, but its specificity is low. Therefore, the author suggested that age alone cannot be used to predict the risk of DVT and must be combined with other factors. This study revealed that the D-dimer level is another independent risk factor for postoperative DVT combined with PE in patients with tibial plateau fractures. As one of the end products of fibrin degradation, D-dimer can reflect the hypercoagulable state of blood in the body and is a sensitive indicator for screening, diagnosis and prognosis of thrombotic diseases [14, 15]. Studies have revealed that the D-dimer level in PE patients is related to the severity of the blood clot burden, and a high D-dimer level indicates a thrombus in the proximal segment of the pulmonary artery [16]. The ability of D-dimer levels to predict the risk of DVT was also analyzed in this study. The AUC for D-dimer level was 0.756, indicating good predictive value, and the optimal cutoff value was 4131.5 ng/mL, suggesting that clinicians should pay extra attention to patients whose D-dimer level is higher than this level and that early preventive intervention is helpful for reducing the incidence of thrombotic diseases. However, D-dimer levels are easily affected by tumors, infection, trauma, blood transfusion, anticoagulant drug use and other factors [17]. D-dimer levels are not highly specific for predicting the risk of DVT.

Currently, clinical models commonly used to assess venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk include the Caprini score and the Padua score. The Padua score primarily focuses on evaluating internal medical comorbidities but lacks analysis of surgery-related indicators, resulting in limited predictive value for perioperative VTE in surgical patients. In contrast, the Caprini score incorporates assessments of surgery-related risk factors and is widely applied for perioperative VTE risk evaluation in surgical populations; however, it omits evaluation of laboratory-based parameters. The nomogram model developed in this study includes the assessment of D-dimer levels. The nomogram model is a visual prediction model that has many advantages over simple risk factor quantification and can predict the risk of adverse events by combining probability values with clinically meaningful variables [18, 19]. The aim of this study was to develop a risk prediction model for DVT complicated with PE after surgery for tibial plateau fracture using age and D-dimer level, which are two independent risk factors for DVT complicated with PE. The AUC for the validated nomogram model was 0.779. The prediction efficacy of this model was greater than that of single independent risk factors, and it could provide obvious net clinical benefits in predicting the risk of DVT complicated with PE. Therefore, as the nomogram model is a visual prediction tool, the risk of DVT complicated with PE will likely be more accurate, the clinical medical staff can accurately identify high-risk patients early, interventions can be targeted according to risk factors, and medical staff can take a more active approach to treating PE. It is highly important to improve the prognosis of this disease.

There are several methodological limitations that cannot be avoided, which need to be carefully treated when interpreting and applying the conclusions. First, this study was a single-center retrospective study, and all cases were from Panzhihua Central Hospital, which is located in a specific geographical area and may have unique population composition, disease spectrum and genetic factors. Single-center retrospective study inevitably has selection bias and lacks an external validation cohort. Future studies can be verified by large sample and multi-center studies. Data from a single center cannot fully represent the actual situation of medical institutions in other regions and levels, and the universality and extrapolation of conclusions are limited. Second, this study did not include an independent external validation cohort. The AUC value, cut-off value, sensitivity, specificity and other indicators obtained by ROC curve analysis are all from the same data set constructed by the model. Even if the standard regression analysis process is adopted, the predictive efficiency under such internal verification may be overestimated to a certain extent. Third, this study excluded patients with open fractures, multiple injuries, and severe medical diseases, which ensured the homogeneity of the study population, but also made the conclusions not applicable to these more high-risk subgroups. Guo [20] also pointed out that the risk of DVT was significantly increased in patients with a history of malignant tumors (P = 0.047), but these patients were excluded from this study. It is necessary for future research to specifically evaluate and model these high-risk subgroups. Fourth, this study only analyzed the incidence of PE during hospitalization, and did not follow up the outcome and long-term functional recovery of patients after discharge. Future studies can extend the follow-up window to 3 to 6 months after surgery to comprehensively evaluate the long-term clinical outcomes of PE.

Conclusion

Age ≥ 52 years and a D-dimer level ≥ 4131.5 ng/mL are independent risk factors for DVT with PE after surgery for a tibial plateau fracture. Thus, clinicians can identify high-risk patients as early as possible and implement measures in time to prevent serious complications. The nomogram prediction model constructed in this study has good discrimination ability, consistency, and clinical validity. It is worthy of clinical promotion.
