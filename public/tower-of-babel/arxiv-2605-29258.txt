Weak solutions of the generalized Monge-Ampère equation and the supercritical deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation: boundary cases
Rei Murakami

Abstract
. We prove the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions for the generalized Monge-Ampère equation and the supercritical deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation in cohomology classes lying on the boundary of the solvable region. Moreover, we prove that the associated geometric flows converge to the weak solutions in the sense of currents. The proof combines viscosity-theoretic and pluripotential-theoretic techniques.



1.
Introduction

Partial differential equations play a central role in Kähler geometry.
A basic example is the complex Monge–Ampère (MA) equation, which governs the Ricci curvature and underlies the theory of Kähler–Einstein metrics. Other important examples include the
J
-equation, introduced in
[
Don99
,
Chen00
]
in connection with constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics, and the deformed Hermitian–Yang–Mills (dHYM) equation (also called the Leung–Yau–Zaslow equation), which arises in mirror symmetry
[
LYZ01
]
.

Recent progress shows that the solvability of a broad class of fully nonlinear elliptic equations in Kähler geometry is characterized by the existence of a
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution, which is a positivity condition on differential forms
[
SW08
,
FLM11
,
DK17
,
Sun17
,
Szé18
,
GS24
,
CJY20
,
Pin22
,
Lin23a
,
Lin23b
,
FYZ25
]
.
Moreover, this condition admits a numerical characterization in terms of the positivity of intersection numbers
[
Chen21
,
Song20
,
DP21
,
CLT24
,
FM24
]
, similarly to the Demailly-Păun characterization of Kählerness
[
DP04
]
.

Given this characterization, it is natural to ask what happens when the positivity conditions fail. In
[
DMS26
]
, the authors conjectured the existence and uniqueness of a canonical weak solution for the
J
-equation and the dHYM equation even in the absence of positivity conditions
[
DMS26
, Conjectures 1.5 and 1.12]
. Furthermore, in dimension two, they confirmed this conjecture using the weak solution theory of the MA equation
[
BEGZ10
]
.

In this paper, we confirm the conjecture in the boundary case (see Assumptions
1.4
and
1.13
) in a slightly weaker form (see Remrak
1.9

(iv)
), by extending the pluripotential framework of
[
BEGZ10
]
.
The corresponding existence and uniqueness result for the
J
-equation
(with
\omega
and
\chi
Kähler forms, unlike in Setup
1.1
)
was announced in
[
DMS26
]
.
To the best of the author’s knowledge, a detailed proof has not yet appeared in the literature.

Motivated by
[

,

]

1.8
), an equation that generalizes both the MA equation and the
J
-equation. Our setting is as follows.

Setup 1.1
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\omega
a closed real semipositive
(1,1)
-form which is strictly positive outside a pluripolar set. Let
[\chi]
be a nef cohomology class. Let
c_{k}\geq 0
be constants for
k=1,\dots,n-1
and
c_{0}
be a continuous function such that either

(1.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}=0,\ c_{0}>0

or

(1.3)

\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}>0,\ \int_{X}c_{0}\omega^{n}\geq 0

holds and

\int_{X}[\chi]^{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}[\chi]^{k}\wedge[\omega]^{n-k}=\int_{X}c_{0}\omega^{n},\quad c_{0}>-\varepsilon_{2.1}

holds, where

Remark that the case (
1.2

The following is the assumption of the boundary case.

Assumption 1.4

(boundary case)
.

Suppose that there exist sequences of Kähler classes
[\chi_{i}]
, Kähler forms
\omega_{i}
, constants
c_{k,i}\geq 0
, where
k=1,\dots,n-1
, that satisfy

(1.5)

\displaystyle\chi_{i}\geq\chi,\quad[\chi_{i}]\to[\chi],\quad\omega_{i}\searrow\omega,\quad c_{k,i}\searrow c_{k}\ \text{ as }\ i\to\infty,

and that for any
p
-dimensional subvariety
V
and any
p=1,\dots,n
, we have

(1.6)

\int_{V}\frac{n!}{p!}[\chi_{i}]^{p}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k,i}\frac{k!}{(k-n+p)!}[\chi_{i}]^{k-n+p}\wedge[\omega_{i}]^{n-k}>0.

Note that (
1.6
) is equivalent to the existence of a solution
\psi_{i}
of the gMA equation in
[\chi_{i}]
, by
[
DP21
,
FM24
]
. The main theorem is a consequence of the analysis of a subsequential limit of
\psi_{i}
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume Setup
1.1
and Assumption
1.4
.
Then, there exists a unique
\chi
-psh function
\psi
with
\sup\psi=0
such that

(1.8)

\begin{cases}\langle\chi_{\psi}^{n}\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}\langle\chi_{\psi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\rangle,\\
\frac{n!}{p!}\langle\chi_{\psi}^{p}\rangle-\sum_{k=n-p}^{n-1}c_{k}\frac{k!}{k-n+p!}\langle\chi_{\psi}^{k-n+p}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\rangle\geq 0\ \text{for any}\ p=1,\dots n-1,\end{cases}

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the nonpluripolar product.

Remark 1.9
.

(i)

The theorem in the case (
1.2
) recovers the nef and big case of the MA equation, treated in
[
BEGZ10
]
, with a strictly positive continuous density.
In
[
BEGZ10
]
, more generally, the authors established the corresponding result in big classes for the MA equation with an arbitrary nonpluripolar full mass measure.

(ii)

The regularity assumption on

in our theorem is related to the use of viscosity-theoretic arguments in

below.
The existence part remains valid when
c_{0}
is merely bounded and lower semicontinuous (Theorem
2.5
). Continuity is only used in the uniqueness argument through the regularization procedure in Lemma
2.20
.

(iii)

Under additional conditions, existence results in special cases were proved by
[

,
DMS26
,
Sun23a
,
Tô23
,
GS24
]
. Those approaches rely on global

estimates, whereas our argument is based on pluripotential and viscosity methods.

(iv)

For the
J
-equation, which is given by
\langle\chi_{\psi}^{n}\rangle=c\langle\omega\wedge\chi_{\psi}^{n-1}\rangle
, the conjecture in
[
DMS26
, Conjecture 1.2]
only requires

to be a Kähler current for the uniquenss.
We do not know if a Kähler current which satisfies
\langle\chi_{\psi}^{n}\rangle=c\langle\omega\wedge\chi_{\psi}^{n-1}\rangle
necessarily satisfies the second condition of (

).

Here, we explain the strategy of the proof. Although the result covers the case of the MA equation and the proof philosophy is the same, the strategy (especially
Step 1
) is different since the right-hand side of the gMA equation varies, whereas that of the MA equation is fixed. For example, we cannot use the argument that relies on the full MA class
\mathcal{E}
.
The proof is broken down into three steps.

Step 1

Subsolution.
We prove that an
L^{1}
-limit of
\psi_{i}
, solutions of approximate gMA equations, is a subsolution of (
1.8
), i.e. satisfies the inequality “
\geq
”. The argument follows the strategy developed in
[
Mur26
]
, which was motivated by
[
CS17
, Proposition 21]
. The key is the convolution argument with the convexity of sublevel sets of the operator for the gMA equation.

Step 2

From subsolution to solution.
We observe that a subsolution is a solution. For the MA equation, the converse inequality in terms of the MA mass
[
BEGZ10
, Proposition 1.20]
implies the equality, hence a solution. We establish the same type of mass inequality for degenerate
\mathcal{C}
-subsolutions (Proposition
2.18
). By this inequality, a subsolution is a solution.

Step 3

Uniqueness.
We follow the strategy of
[
BEGZ10
, Section 3.3]
, which was based on the solutions of the MA equation with different measures of the same mass. We use the solutions of the gMA equation with different
c_{0}
while fixing
\int_{X}c_{0}\omega^{n}
. One of the key ingredients in
[
BEGZ10
, Section 3.3]
was the log-concave property of the nonpluripolar product (Proposition 1.11 there). We use the convexity of the operator of the equation and the regularization arguments (Lemma
2.20
), to obtain the desired inequality (Lemma
2.45
).

We also study the weak convergence of the mixed Hessian flow in the setting of Setup
1.1
and Assumption
1.4
, assuming that
\omega
and
\chi
are Kähler and that
c_{0}
is a nonnegative constant.
The mixed Hessian flow is defined by

\begin{cases}\dot{\varphi_{t}}=1-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}\frac{\chi_{t}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi_{t}^{n}},\\
\varphi_{t}|_{t=0}=\varphi_{0},\end{cases}

where
\chi_{t}:=\chi+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi_{t}>0
and
\varphi_{0}
is a
\chi
-psh function. The long-time existence of this flow is proved in
[
CS17
, Proposition 13]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold and
\chi
a Kähler form. Let
c_{k}\geq 0
be constants for
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
satisfying the conditions in Setup
1.1
. Assume that we have

\int_{V}\frac{n!}{p!}[\chi]^{p}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}\frac{k!}{(k-n+p)!}[\chi]^{k-n+p}\wedge[\omega]^{n-k}\geq 0

for any
p
-dimensional subvariety
V
, where
p=1,2,\dots,{n-1}
.
Then, the mixed Hessian flow converges to the weak solution of the gMA equation in the sense of currents.

Remark 1.11
.

In dimension two,
[
Mur26
]
proved the theorem. Moreover, in the two-dimensional semipositive case
[
FLSW14
]
and in a certain semipositive condition in general dimension
[
Sun23b
]
, they proved the same result by establishing global

estimates. They further proved smooth convergence on a Zariski open subset.

The proof of Theorem

follows the strategy of
[

]
. That is, using the convexity of an energy functional along the flow, we first prove that the time derivative of the flow converges to zero in

. Then, the arguments from
Step 1
imply that a weak limit is a subsolution. Then,
Step 2
and

imply the desired result.

With the same strategy, we establish similar results for the dHYM equation (

Remark 1.19

In dimension two,

Although we expect our strategy to extend to more general complex Hessian equations, there are several difficulties.
For example, the general inverse
\sigma_{k}

[

,
Lin23b
]
. It takes the form (

In

For the complex Hessian quotient equation

, although the convexity of the operator is well-known when

, we do not know if the convexity of sublevel sets is preserved when

can be slightly negative. We also do not know whether a property similar to (

Note that
[

,
CX25b
]
studied viscosity solutions for very general complex Hessian equations of the type cosidered in
[
Szé18
]
. Also, pluripotential solutions for the complex
m
-Hessian equation, defined by

[

2.
The Generalized Monge-Ampère equation

2.1.
Preliminaries

We follow the notation in
[
CS17
]
. For a vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denote by

S_{k}(\lambda):=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\dots\lambda_{i_{k}},

the
k
-th elementary symmetric function. Define
S_{0}=1
and
S_{-1}=0
. For distinct indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}
, we define

S_{k:i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}}(\lambda):=S_{k}(\lambda)|_{\lambda_{i_{1}}=\dots=\lambda_{i_{\ell}}=0},

while if
i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}
are not distinct, then
S_{k:i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}}(\lambda):=0
.
Denote the set of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices by
\operatorname{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and let
A\in\operatorname{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Let
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
be nonnegative constants and
c_{0,0}
a constant.
For
\ell=1,\dots,n-1
, we define

\displaystyle P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}(A)

\displaystyle:=\max_{i_{1},\dots,i_{\ell}\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\frac{S_{k-\ell;i_{1},\dots i_{\ell}}(\lambda)}{S_{n-\ell;i_{1},\dots i_{\ell}}(\lambda)}\right),

\displaystyle Q_{c_{0,0},I_{n}}(A)

\displaystyle:=Q_{c_{0,0},I_{n}}(A):=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}c_{k}\frac{1}{{n\choose k}}\frac{S_{k}(\lambda)}{S_{n}(\lambda)}+c_{0,0}\frac{1}{S_{n}(\lambda)},

where
\lambda
is the eigenvalue vector of
A
. We particularly denote
P^{1}_{I_{n}}
by
P_{I_{n}}
.
Let us define

\Gamma_{\geq 0}:=\{A\in\operatorname{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\mid A\text{ is semipositive}\},\quad\bar{\Gamma}:=\{A\in\Gamma_{\geq 0}\mid P_{I_{n}}(A)\leq 1\}.

The important properties of
P^{\ell}
and
Q
are the following:

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{2.1}>0
, depending on
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
, such that if a constant
c_{0,0}
satisfies
c_{0,0}>-\varepsilon_{2.1}
, the following hold:

(i)

P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}
decreases in
\Gamma_{\geq 0}
and

decreases in
\overline{\Gamma}
. That is, if
A\geq B\in\Gamma_{\geq 0}
, then
P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}(A)\leq P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}(B)
, and if

, then

.

(ii)

P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}
is convex on
\Gamma_{\geq 0}
and

is convex on
\bar{\Gamma}
.

(iii)

The sublevel sets
\{P^{\ell}_{I_{n}}\leq 1\}\cap\Gamma_{\geq 0}
and
\{Q_{c_{0,0},I_{n}}\leq 1\}\cap\bar{\Gamma}

Proof.

The first and second items for

follow from
[
CS17

\displaystyle f_{\varphi,k}^{C,\varepsilon}:=\frac{\sup\{\varphi_{1,2,k}-\psi_{i}+C,0\}}{\sup\{\varphi_{1,2,k}-\psi_{i}+C,0\}+\varepsilon},\quad f_{\varphi}^{C,\varepsilon}:=\frac{\sup\{\varphi_{1}-\psi_{i}+C,0\}}{\sup\{\varphi_{1}-\psi_{i}+C,0\}+\varepsilon},

\begin{split}&\varliminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}\left\langle\chi_{i,\varphi_{1,2,k}}\wedge T^{n-1}_{i,\omega}(\chi_{i,\psi})\right\rangle\\

Lemma 2.50
.

Let
F_{k,\chi,\omega}
be the operator defined by

\displaystyle F_{k,\chi,\omega}(\varphi)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge k\chi^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}}+\frac{\chi^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}}n\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\varphi\wedge\chi^{n-1}}{\chi^{n}}

\displaystyle+\left(\partial\frac{\chi^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}},\bar{\partial}\varphi\right)_{\chi}.

Then, we have

\int_{X}\varphi F_{k,\chi,\omega}(\varphi)\geq 0

for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X)

Proof.

For
\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, by integration by parts, we can compute as

\displaystyle\int_{X}{\varphi\,F_{k,\chi,\omega}(\psi)}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\varphi\left({\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi}\right)\wedge k\chi^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}+\int_{X}n\,\varphi\frac{\chi^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi\right)\wedge\chi^{n-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{X}n\,\varphi\,\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\frac{\chi^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{k}}\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi\right)\wedge\chi^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\sqrt{-1}\partial\phi\wedge\bar{\partial}\psi\right)\wedge\left(k\chi^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}-\frac{\chi^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}}\chi^{n-1}\right).

Since
S_{k}(\lambda)/S_{n}(\lambda)
increases in
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda_{i}>0\}
, we see that the
(n-1,n-1)
-form in the last line is negative. Hence, the claim follows.
∎

Define the functional

Lemma 2.51
.

is convex along the mixed Hessian flow (

Proof.

We can compute as

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{2}\,\chi_{t}^{n}=-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,\ddot{\varphi_{t}}\,\chi^{n}_{t}-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}^{2}\,n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge\chi^{n-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge k\chi_{t}^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}}{\chi^{n}_{t}}+n\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi_{t}}\wedge\chi_{t}^{n-1}}{\chi_{t}^{n}}\right)\chi^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{X}2\,\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,n\,\sqrt{-1}\partial\Lambda_{\chi_{t}}\omega\wedge\bar{\partial}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\wedge\chi^{n-1}_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}\,F_{k,\chi_{t},\omega}(\dot{\varphi}_{t})\,\chi^{n}_{t}.

Therefore, the claim follows from Lemma
2.50
and
