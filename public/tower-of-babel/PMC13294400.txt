Mixed convective transient nanofluid flow through a vertical porous channel with entropy and Navier slip effects
Authors: Odeli J. Kigodi, Muhammad Faisal, Alex X. Matofali, IA Badruddin, ASA Zedan, Wubshet Ibrahim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study investigates transient nanofluid flow in a vertical porous channel with buoyancy effects and Navier slip at the walls, with particular emphasis on thermodynamic irreversibility, entropy generation, and mixed convection. The study formulates dimensionless, coupled nonlinear governing equations for velocity, temperature, and entropy generation. These equations are solved numerically using a finite-difference scheme (FDS) that is second-order accurate in space and first-order accurate in time, capturing both transient and steady-state behaviors. Parametric analyses are conducted for permeability (Darcy number), buoyancy (Grashof number), pressure gradient, Biot number, Prandtl number, Brinkman number, and distinct Navier slip coefficients at the heated and cooled walls. Increasing permeability and buoyancy accelerate the flow and enhance convective heat transfer, but intensify entropy generation near the walls where velocity and temperature gradients are highest. Stronger pressure forcing sharpens the centerline velocity and increases near-wall irreversibility. Higher Biot and Prandtl numbers reduce velocity and temperature levels across the channel, while potentially increasing entropy generation due to steeper thermal gradients. Increased viscous dissipation raises temperatures near the heated wall and amplifies entropy generation near both plates.

Originality/value

This study provides a novel, integrated analysis of transient nanofluid flow in a vertical porous channel by simultaneously accounting for buoyancy effects, asymmetric Navier wall slip, viscous dissipation, and entropy generation. The combined examination of transient behavior, irreversibility metrics, and asymmetric slip effects offers new insights into thermodynamic optimization of porous media nanofluid systems that are not readily available in existing literature.

Introduction

Nanofluids play an important role in entropy generation analysis because their enhanced thermal conductivity and convective heat transport capabilities directly influence the balance between useful energy transport and irreversible losses. By modifying temperature gradients, viscous dissipation, and flow resistance, nanofluids can either mitigate or intensify entropy production, making entropy generation a critical metric for evaluating their true thermodynamic performance. Consequently, coupling nanofluid heat transfer with entropy generation analysis provides a more realistic basis for optimizing thermal systems, ensuring that heat transport enhancement is achieved with minimal irreversibility and maximum energy efficiency. The study of entropy generation in time-dependent nanofluid Couette flow via porous media captures particular consideration because of its essence in modern cooling and energy systems ([31]; Makinde and Eegunjobi [27]. Recent researchers created model systems combining buoyancy forces and Navier slip effects, which are critical in thermal-dynamic irreversibility within the channel ([15]; Balamurugan et al., ([7]; Faisal et al., [16]. Employing nanofluids with better thermal conductivity and heat transport leads to improved thermal processes in comparison to base fluids ([13]; Samantaray et al., [37]. The recent applications of nanofluids in thermal engineering are found in studies ([19]; Ye et al., ([41]; Nishad et al., ([35]; Yang et al., [40].

Permeability plays a crucial role in the Couette flow of nanofluids through porous channels, as it governs the resistance offered by the porous medium to nanofluid motion. An increase in permeability (higher Darcy number) reduces drag within the porous matrix, allowing the nanofluid to flow more freely and resulting in higher velocity profiles across the channel. This enhanced flow promotes stronger convective heat transfer but can also intensify velocity gradients near the walls, influencing entropy generation. Therefore, permeability serves as a key control parameter for regulating momentum transport, thermal performance, and thermodynamic irreversibility in nanofluid Couette flow systems. Permeability within the Couette flow includes interplay between several crucial parameters that should be considered in designing any porous media such as Darcy number, buoyancy effects, and heat convection ([12]; Mkwizu and Makinde ([30]; Sharma et al., [39]. The given factors lay the foundation for controlling fluid temperature, fluid motion, and entropy production. Various studies prove that incorporating these parameters results in improved heat transport as well as enhanced thermal irreversibility ([21]; Ali et al., ([3]; Seth et al., [38]. Moreover, the findings reveal that the Navier slip coefficients have typical effects on velocity profiles in both walls; also, fine-tuning these parameters enhances thermal irreversibility ([23]; Alhajaj et al., ([2]; Mahian et al., [26]. Moreover, the field of porosity is crucial and requires further research, as it plays a fundamental role in designing efficient thermal and electronic devices ([14]; Faisal et al., [17]; Kigodi & Masasila [22]. It has been observed that nanofluid material and buoyancy effects have a greater influence within a porous medium ([8]; Mukhopadhyay and Mandal ([33]; Chen and Liu [10]. Furthermore, recent literature reveals that heat transport and entropy generation are mostly affected by variations in key parameters, including buoyancy effects, electric conductivity, Navier slip coefficients, and diffusion. The findings indicate that heat transport and thermal irreversibility are enhanced through strong diffusion as well as thermal radiation ([28]; Masthanaiah et al., ([29]; Yusuf and Peter [43]. Also, other literature focuses on addressing the stability of the flow and entropy generation by considering the effects of temperature variation in permeable channels ([4]; Abbas et al., ([1]; Chandrawat et al., ([9]; Haseeb et al., [20], as well as the essence of thermal effects and combined nanofluids in modern thermal systems ([34]; Mkwizu et al., [32]. Kong et al., [24] investigated the particle deposition effects in a cooling channel with heat transport aspects. Chhabra et al., [11] numerically analyzed the flow through a composite channel with co-effects of porous media and MHD. The pressure-driven flow of water in a squeezed channel is studied by Yuan et al., ([42]2). Co-effects of entropy and mixed convection on magneto-nanofluid flow in a vertically aligned channel are inspected by Fersadou et al., [18]. Aslam et al., [5] explained the hydro-thermal analysis of a magnetized nanofluid flow inside an enclosure using non-uniform heating aspects. Some recent exploration and possible applications of the theme of the present study are provided by Kumar et al., [25], Bai et al., [6], and Parmar et al., [36].

The current literature lacks the influence of multiple parameters within porous media, such as the coefficient of Navier slip, buoyancy effects, convective heat transfer, and time variations. This study aims to fix the gap by designing a mathematical model that incorporates buoyancy forces, effects of Navier slip, and convective heat transfer. Therefore, the novelty of this study lies not merely in combining effects, but in providing a comprehensive and coupled analysis of momentum, heat transport, and irreversibility in transient nanofluid porous flow with slip, thereby addressing a gap in the existing literature. The findings will provide a broader analysis of heat transfer and thermal irreversibility in industrial settings. This study will address by capturing the following questions: (1) How do buoyancy effects and coefficients of Navier slip interplay to influence porous media and entropy generation? (2) What role does convective cooling play in the distribution of irreversibilities?, and (3) How can operating parameters be optimized to minimize entropy production? The assumptions made in this section idealize a tractable yet physically meaningful model of nanofluid flow through a vertical porous channel. Treating the medium as a homogeneous isotropic porous medium ensures uniform resistance to flow in all directions, justifying the use of the Darcy–Brinkman model to capture both viscous diffusion and permeability effects. The unsteady laminar incompressible flow in a vertical channel removes edge effects, allowing the velocity to depend only on the transverse coordinate and time. The nanofluid (water with dispersed copper nanoparticles) is modeled with effective properties, using the Maxwell model for conductivity and the Brinkman viscosity model for viscosity, assuming uniform particle distribution. The moving heated plate and the stationary convectively cooled plate create shear and temperature gradients, while Navier slip accounts for nano-scale boundary effects. The constant pressure gradient and buoyancy forces drive mixed convection while neglecting density variation except in the body force term. Finally, considering thermal and viscous irreversibilities enables entropy generation analysis, providing insight into system efficiency and energy losses.

Numerical method

The coupled, nonlinear, unsteady governing equations describing the dimensionless velocity \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:U(\eta\:,\tau\:)$$\end{document}, temperature \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\theta\:(\eta\:,\tau\:)$$\end{document}, and entropy generation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{N}_{s}(\eta\:,\tau\:)$$\end{document}, subject to Navier slip and thermal boundary conditions, are solved numerically using the finite difference scheme (FDS). The FDS employed in this study is second-order accurate in space and first-order accurate in time, stable under an appropriate time-step restriction, and capable of capturing the transient and steady-state characteristics of velocity, temperature, and entropy generation in a nanofluid stream through a porous channel with Navier slip. Results and discussion

The results in Fig. 2 indicate that velocity increases with an increase in the Darcy number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:(0.05\le\:Da\le\:1.0)$$\end{document}, implying reduced nanofluid motion due to high permeability. A moderate range of Darcy numbers is considered because for very low permeability, flow is highly restricted and velocity becomes zero, and the medium behaves like a solid barrier. For high Darcy numbers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:(Da>1)$$\end{document}, the porous medium behaves like a clear fluid, and the effect of porous media can be diminished. Further observation reveals that a 10% increase in the Darcy number causes the temperature to rise from the left plate to the right plate. Physically, higher permeability strengthens convective heat transport within the channel from the cooler plate toward the heated plate. Higher permeability near the heated plate leads to a rise in entropy production. In contrast, the combined effects of temperature and velocity variations lead to entropy falls with an immediate rise towards the right plate. More interestingly, the results in Fig. 3 reveal that throughout the profile the velocity rises as the Grashof number (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:0.5\le\:Gr\le\:7.0$$\end{document}) increases, which results in strengthened buoyancy forces and enhanced heat convection within the flow. For very small values of the Grashof number, heat transport occurs by conduction. In the moderate range considered in this study, controlled buoyancy is formed. For very large values of the Grashof number \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:(Gr\:>\:7)$$\end{document}, turbulence occurs, contradicting our assumption of laminar flow. Also, the findings show that increasing \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Gr$$\end{document} leads to the temperature rising continuously from the cooler plate towards the heated plate, indicating improved heat transport driven by buoyancy effects. Furthermore, a rise in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Gr$$\end{document} closer to the left and right plates results in an increase in entropy production. It is also noticed that the entropy production at the middle plate spacing from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:=0.5\:$$\end{document}to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\eta\:=0.7$$\end{document} shows slight changes. Physically, these results imply that strong thermal and velocity gradients near the plates lead to an increased irreversibility. Increasing the pressure gradient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:1\le\:A\le\:10$$\end{document}) fastens the nanofluid velocity more sharply toward the channel center while raising the temperature from the cooler wall to the heated wall, enhancing thermal energy. Entropy generation rises near the walls and then decays at the center of the channel, implying higher irreversibility with stronger pressure gradients, which can affect system performance as shown in Fig. 4. Here, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A$$\end{document} appears as a constant forcing term, and physically \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A>0$$\end{document} drives the flow forward, acting like an external pump or an imposed pressure gradient. For very small values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A$$\end{document}, the pressure force becomes negligible and not supportive for this study because the goal of this study is to analyze the force and natural convection together. For a moderate range of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A$$\end{document}, it allows a smooth mixed convection phenomenon as well as the interaction of pressure force and porous resistance. For large values of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A$$\end{document} (i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A>10$$\end{document}), the flow becomes purely pressure-driven, and mixed convection effects become negligible. The results in Fig. 5 show that increasing the Biot number (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Bi$$\end{document}) leads to a decrease in both velocity and temperature throughout the flow, indicating improved thermal convection. Also, the findings reveal that an increase of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Bi$$\end{document} results in a rise of entropy production from the heated wall to the cooler wall, implying effective thermal conductivity and improved irreversibility in porous media.

Conclusion

Increasing permeability (higher Darcy number) and buoyancy (higher Grashof number) strengthen nanofluid motion and promote heat transport from the cooler wall toward the heated wall, but at the cost of larger near-wall entropy production driven by amplified gradients. A stronger pressure gradient accelerates the flow, especially toward the channel center, while raising the temperature field; however, it also increases entropy generation near the walls, indicating that aggressive pressure driving can degrade thermodynamic performance if not carefully managed. Higher Biot number reduces velocity and temperature levels, reflecting enhanced convective exchange at the boundaries, yet it can elevate entropy production across the channel due to increased thermal-gradient-induced losses. Similarly, increasing the Prandtl number suppresses momentum and thermal diffusion, and the resulting steeper temperature gradients contribute to higher entropy generation, signaling greater thermal irreversibility. Viscous dissipation (Brinkman number) plays a distinctive role in heating the fluid near the hot wall and increasing entropy generation at both plates, confirming that viscous heating can be a major source of losses in high-shear porous media configurations. Navier slip emerges as a practical control mechanism for redistributing irreversibility. Slip at the heated plate tends to reduce wall friction and increase velocity toward the cooler wall, but it can raise temperature and entropy production near the heated boundary while decreasing losses near the cooled wall, suggesting a potential design pathway for improving overall energy efficiency by shifting where irreversibility concentrates. In contrast, slip at the cooler plate promotes flow toward the heated wall and can reduce entropy generation near the heated boundary, while increasing entropy production near the cooled wall. The accompanying Nusselt and Bejan-number trends reinforce that permeability, buoyancy, viscous dissipation, and wall slip must be tuned together, because enhanced convection may simultaneously intensify irreversible losses at critical boundaries.

Research limitations/implications

The analysis is limited to a specific channel geometry, laminar flow regime, and numerical solution approach, with simplified nanofluid and porous medium assumptions. Extensions to different geometries, turbulence, or alternative nanofluid models may yield additional insights.

Practical applications

The results guide controlling heat transfer and reducing irreversible losses in porous media nanofluid systems by tuning permeability, wall slip conditions, pressure forcing, and viscous dissipation effects. Improved understanding and management of irreversibility in heat transfer systems can contribute to more energy-efficient thermal technologies, supporting sustainability and reduced energy consumption in industrial and engineering applications.

In a nutshell, the results provide a thermodynamic design map for porous channel nanofluid systems: minimizing entropy generation depends less on bulk averages and more on controlling near-wall gradients through coordinated adjustment of permeability, forcing, and asymmetric slip. These insights are relevant to the optimization of microscale thermal devices, porous heat exchangers, and buoyancy-assisted channel flows where both heat transfer enhancement and irreversibility reduction are required. In the future, this work can be extended for the hybrid nanofluid and ternary-hybrid nanofluid flows. Moreover, the effect of Brownian motion and thermophoresis can be applied using the modified Buongiorno nanofluid model.
