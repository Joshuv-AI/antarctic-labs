Best approximants relative to a C ∗ -subalgebra, joint numerical range and subdifferentials
Tamara Bottazzi, Alejandro Varela

Abstract
We study the minimality of n × n n\times n Hermitian matrices A A respect to a C ∗ C^{*} -subalgebra ℬ \mathcal{B} of M n ​ ( ℂ ) M_{n}(\mathbb{C}) in the spectral norm, that is ‖ A ‖ ≤ ‖ A + B ‖ , for every ​ B ∈ ℬ . \|A\|\leq\|A+B\|,\ \text{ for every }B\in\mathcal{B}. We generalize the notion of the moment of a subspace and relate it to the joint numerical range and the subdifferentials of the maximum eigenvalue. We extend results previously known for the subalgebra of diagonal operators and describe the subdifferential of the maximum eigenvalue in terms of the moment of the corresponding eigenspace. We also characterize ℬ \mathcal{B} -minimality via moments and subdifferentials, and provide examples.

Introduction and prelimininaries

Let

be the space of complex square matrices of
n\times n
, which is a

-algebra. Consider a
C^{*}
-subalgebra

-minimal matrix if

\|A\|\leq\|A+B\|,\text{ for all }B\in\mathcal{B}.

\mathcal{B}
-minimality is closely related with best approximation, since

is
\mathcal{B}
-minimal if and only if

If

is Hermitian, then

where the superscript denotes the subset of Hermitian (self-adjoint) elements. This holds by Remark 2.1
[
2
]

\|A+\text{Re}(B)\|=\|\text{Re}(A+B)\|\leq\|A+B\|

\mathcal{B}
.

Our interest in this subject is related to the following context. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a C
∗
-algebra and
\mathcal{B}
a C
∗
-subalgebra sharing the same unit. The description of minimal self-adjoint elements

with respect to a self-adjoint element

(
\mathcal{B}
-minimality) allows a concrete description of geodesics in a natural Finsler metric induced by the norm of
\mathcal{A}
on the flag manifolds

(a homogeneous space under the left action of
U_{\mathcal{A}}
) associated with the quotient space
\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{B}
(see
[
9
]
).
In this setting, if
Z
is a Hermitian matrix that is minimal in the spectral norm, then the curve
\gamma(t)=e^{itZ}Ae^{-itZ}
is length-minimizing in
U_{\mathcal{A}}/U_{\mathcal{B}}
between
\gamma(0)
and
\gamma(t)
for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2\|Z\|}
(see
[
9
]
).

\mathcal{B}
-Minimality is closely related to Birkhoff-James orthogonality in the normed space
\left(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\|\cdot\|\right)

[
4
,
11
]
: two matrices

are Birkhoff-James orthogonal if and only if
\|A\|\leq\|A+\lambda B\|
, for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. In particular,

is a
\mathcal{B}
-minimal matrix if and only if

is Birkhoff-James orthogonal to every

. This is equivalent to state that

is Birkhoff-James orthogonal to
\mathcal{B}

[
10
,
6
]
.

The case where
\mathcal{B}
is the
C^{*}
-subalgebra of diagonal matrices (respect to a fixed basis of

) has been extensively studied in
[
1
,
5
,
12
,
7
,
8
]
. In the present work, we are focused to study and generalize some of the results that involve relations between

-minimality, joint numerical range (usually called algebraic joint numerical range) and subdifferentials, as we did for diagonal matrices in
[
13
,
7
,
8
]
. In this sense, in
[
8
]
we relate minimal operators to the subdifferentials of the maximum eigenvalue and the norm. We connect these notions with the moments of the eigenspaces corresponding to the largest and smallest eigenvalues, as well as with certain joint numerical ranges; these ideas were developed in
[
13
]
for matrices, and
[
7
]
for compact operators on a Hilbert space. In particular, one of our main results provides an explicit characterization of the subdifferential of the largest eigenvalue of a Hermitian matrix

with variable real diagonal

in terms of

Now, considering any other

-subalgebra

of

The results presented in this paper are divided into three parts.
In Section
2

These include the moment set of an eigenspace, the joint numerical range of certain matrices defined with respect to a chosen basis

In Section

-minimality and the condition

Finally, Section

3
Relations between subdifferentials and moment

In this section, we follow the main ideas of
[
16
]
and
[
8
]
to characterize the moment of the eigenspace of the maximum eigenvalue of a minimal matrix in terms of the subdifferentials of the same eigenvalue and the operator norm.

First, we introduce the definition of subdifferential of a convex function, as was given in
[
3
]
.

Definition 2
.

For any convex function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
defined on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{X}^{*}
its dual, it can be defined the subdifferential of
f
at
x\in\mathcal{X}
as

\partial f(x)=\{v\in\mathcal{X}^{*}:f(y)-f(x)\geq\text{Re }v(y-x),\forall\ y\in\mathcal{X}\},

(3.1)

In particular, for the Hermitian matrix space
\mathcal{X}=M_{n}^{h}(\mathbb{C})
, the subdifferential of
f(\cdot)=\lambda_{max}(\cdot)
at
x=A\in M_{n}^{h}(\mathbb{C})
is

\displaystyle\partial\lambda_{max}(A)=

\displaystyle\left\{V\in M_{n}^{h}(\mathbb{C}):\lambda_{max}(Y)-\lambda_{max}(A)\geq\text{Re}\left(\text{tr}(V(Y-A))\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.\text{for every }Y\in M_{n}^{h}(\mathbb{C})\right\}.

A more practical (or useful) formula for the subdifferential of
\lambda_{max}
at
A
, proved first for symmetric real matrices in
[
16
]
and later extended por Hermitian in
[
8
]
, is

\displaystyle\partial\lambda_{max}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{co}\{qq^{*}:Aq=\lambda_{max}(A)q\text{ and }\|q\|=1\}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{Q_{max}R_{s}Q_{max}^{*}:R_{s}\in M_{s}^{h}(\mathbb{C}),R_{s}\geq 0,\text{tr}(R_{s})=1\},

where the columns of
Q_{max}
form an orthonormal set of
s
eigenvectors for
\lambda_{max}(A)
(an orthonormal basis of the eigenspace of
\lambda_{max}(A)
). Observe that
Q_{max}
depends on the matrix
A
.

From now we denote as
\mathcal{D}^{s}
the set of density matrices of
s\times s
.

Now consider
A(x)=A_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}x_{k}B_{k}
, with
A_{0}\in M_{n}^{h}(\mathbb{C})
,
\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}
be a fixed Hermitian basis of
\mathbb{B}^{h}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}
. The maximum eigenvalue of
A(x)
,
\lambda_{max}(A(x))
, is a map from
\mathbb{R}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}
to
\mathbb{R}
and a composition between a smooth function
A(\cdot)
and a convex map
\lambda_{max}(\cdot)
. For every
k
, the partial derivatives of
A
at
x
are

\frac{\partial A}{\partial x_{k}}(x)=B_{k}.

Adapting Theorem 3 in
[
16
]
to the Hermitian case, the subdifferential of
\lambda_{max}(A(x))
is

\begin{split}\partial\left(\lambda_{max}(A(x))\right)=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}:\ v_{k}=\text{tr}(R_{s}Q_{max}(x)^{*}B_{k}Q_{max}(x)),\right.\\
\left.\text{with }R_{s}\in\mathcal{D}^{s}\right\},\end{split}

(3.3)

where
s
is the multiplicity of
\lambda_{max}(A(x))
and the columns of
Q_{max}(x)
form an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for
\lambda_{max}(A(x))
. Observe that
Q_{max}(x)
depends on
A(x)
and
v\in W\left(\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\dim(\mathcal{B})}\right)
if

. Then,

\partial\left(\lambda_{max}(A(x))\right)\subseteq W\left(\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\dim(\mathcal{B})}\right).

Since
\lambda_{max}(A(x))=\lambda_{max}\circ A(x)
, the generalized derivative and generalized gradient coincide with the directional derivative and subdifferential, respectively (see Proposition 2.2.7 in
[
15
]
). Then, the expression (
3.3
) is valid by the chain rule for subdifferentials, and is useful to obtain the following characterization of the subdifferential and the partial derivative of the maximum eigenvalue of
A(x)
.

.

S_{max}
be the eigenspace of

at

in the direction

&=\lambda_{max}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}w_{k}Q_{max}(x)^{*}B_{k}Q_{max}(x)\right),\end{split}

where the columns of

form an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for

Proof.

Suppose

has multiplicity

\lambda_{max}(A(x))

with

,

,

, for every

. Observing that

then

On the other hand, take any

\text{tr}(Y)=1

In terms of the orthogonal decomposition

(

. Therefore,

.

The proof of the directional derivative is analogous to the case

\displaystyle\lambda_{max}^{\prime}(x,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}v_{k}w_{k}:v_{k}=\text{tr}\left(R_{s}Q_{max}(x)^{*}B_{k}Q_{max}(x)\right):\text{ with }R_{s}\in\mathcal{D}^{s}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\text{tr}\left(R_{s}\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}w_{k}Q_{max}(x)^{*}B_{k}Q_{max}(x)\right):\ \text{with }R_{s}\in\mathcal{D}^{s}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{max}(B(w)),

where the last equality is due to the fact that

\lambda_{max}(B(w))=\max\{\text{tr}(B(w)R):\ R_{s}\in\mathcal{D}^{s}\}.

(3.6)

Theorem 4
.

Let

, with

,
\{B_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}
be a fixed Hermitian basis of
\mathcal{B}^{h}
,

, and suppose that
\lambda_{max}(A(x))=-\lambda_{min}(A(x))
. Then, the following statements are equivalent

(1)

0\in\partial\|A(x)\|
.

(2)

0\in\partial\lambda_{max}(A(x))+\partial\lambda_{min}(A(x))
.

(3)

m_{S_{max}}\cap m_{S_{min}}\neq\emptyset
, where

and

are the eigenspaces of

and
\lambda_{min}(A(x))
, respectively.

(4)

W\left(\left\{P_{S_{max}}B_{k}P_{S_{max}}\right\}_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}\right)\cap W\left(\left\{P_{S_{min}}B_{k}P_{S_{min}}\right\}_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{B}}}\right)\neq\{\overline{0}\}
.

(5)

A(x)
is minimal.

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) If
0\in\partial\|A(x)\|
and
\lambda_{max}(A(x))=-\lambda_{min}(A(x))
, then using Proposition
3

0\in{\rm conv}\left(\partial\lambda_{max}(A(x))\cup\partial\lambda_{min}(A(x))\right)={\rm conv}\left(m_{S_{max}}\cup-m_{S_{min}}\right)

and there exist

,
Y_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{S_{max}}
and

such that

. Both matrices

and

fulfill that
\text{tr}(Y_{0})=\text{tr}(Z_{0})=1
(see (

)). Thus, we obtain that

and then
\Phi(Y_{0})=\Phi(Z_{0})
. Therefore,

.

The converse implication can be proved reversing the previous steps.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) If we consider

and
\partial\lambda_{min}(A(x))=-m_{S_{min}}
from (

) and (

), respectively, then it is trivial that
m_{S_{max}}\cap m_{S_{min}}\neq\emptyset
if and only if

.

The equivalence (4)
\Leftrightarrow
(5) was proved in Theorem
2
.

(3)
\Leftrightarrow
(4) Using that

,

in Theorem

\begin{split}v&=\left(\text{tr}(\rho_{+}B_{1}),\text{tr}(\rho_{+}B_{2}),...,\text{tr}(\rho_{+}B_{\dim\mathcal{B}})\right)\\
&=\left(\text{tr}(\rho_{-}B_{1}),\text{tr}(\rho_{-}B_{2}),...,\text{tr}(\rho_{-}B_{\dim\mathcal{B}})\right)\in m_{S_{max}}\cap m_{S_{min}}.\end{split}

∎
