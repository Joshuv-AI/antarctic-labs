Abstract
. Given a smooth deformation of a Lie subalgebra, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for its smooth triviality and derive an analogous criterion for Lie ideals. We then give a direct proof of the Moser trick for foliations, which forms the basis for extending this result to general Lie subalgebroids.

Primary 53C12; Secondary 22E60.

1.
Introduction

Lie algebroids constitute a central object of study in differential geometry and mathematical physics, as they encode infinitesimal symmetries across a broad class of geometric and physical settings. The two limiting cases of Lie algebroids are, on the one hand, Lie algebras and, on the other, tangent bundles. Accordingly, the corresponding extreme cases of Lie subalgebroids are Lie subalgebras and foliations, respectively. These corner cases are precisely the main ingredients of the present note.

In the case of Lie subalgebras, the deformation cohomology was investigated by Richardson
[
17
]
while the
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure on the corresponding cochain complex was later established by Frégier–Zambon in
[
9
]
. Furthermore, (topological) rigidity and stability results in the context of Lie subalgebras originally appeared in
[
16
,
17
]
, were reformulated, along with many other related and new results, in a modern differential-geometric language by Crainic–Schätz–Struchiner in
[
5
]
.

In Section
2
, we associate a family of cocycles to a smooth deformation of a Lie subalgebra and use this infinitesimal data to obtain a cohomological criterion for its triviality (Theorem
2.2
). We also prove an analogous result for Lie ideals (Theorem
2.3
). In contrast to the space of Lie algebra structures on a vector space, it is unknown whether the spaces of Lie subalgebras and Lie ideals of a Lie algebra are locally path-connected. Therefore, the smooth deformation-triviality results established in this paper cannot be recovered from the more topologically oriented approaches of
[
16
,
17
,
5
,
7
]
. However, our techniques are substantially inspired by those of
[
3
]
, where Cárdenas-Struchiner proved a Moser trick for Lie group homomorphisms and Lie subgroups. Namely, Proposition
2.1
may be regarded as an infinitesimal analogue of
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Moving on to the case of foliations, their infinitesimal deformation theory was developed by Heitsch in
[
10
]
. The richer algebraic structure carried by the deformation complex of a foliation has been investigated by several authors using different, a priori independent techniques, including
[
11
]
by Huebschmann,
[
19
]
by Vitagliano, and
[
13
]
by Ji, where the latter work presents the structure in the broader context of Lie subalgebroids.

Crainic-Moerdijk solved the deformation problem for Lie algebroids
[
4
]
and developed the corresponding adapted Moser trick
[
4
, Theorem 2]
. Furthermore, the authors proved that there exists a quasi-isomorphism between the deformation complex of a foliation, viewed as a Lie subalgebroid of the tangent bundle, and the deformation complex of the foliation viewed as a Lie algebroid in its own right; this quasi-isomorphism also maps deformation cocycles to deformation cocycles
[
4
, Proposition 4]
. Together, these results imply that the Moser trick for foliations holds in the expected and most natural way.

The main aim of Section
3
is to provide a self-contained proof of the Moser trick for foliations (Theorem
3.7
), expressed solely in terms of the deformation complex of the foliation, regarded as an involutive distribution. This approach furnishes a convenient framework for extending the result to arbitrary Lie subalgebroids
[
1
]
, where the aforementioned quasi-isomorphism generally fails to hold.

2.
Triviality of smooth deformations of Lie subalgebras and Lie ideals

The Lie bracket of a finite dimensional real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted by
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}:\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, and its adjoint representation by
\ad:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
. Furthermore,
G
denotes the unique simply connected Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\Ad:G\to\GL(\mathfrak{g})
its adjoint representation. The Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in a representation
r:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is denoted by
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g};V)\coloneqq\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes V
, its differential by
\delta_{\mathfrak{g}}^{V}:C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g};V)\to C^{\bullet+1}(\mathfrak{g};V)
, and its space of cocycles by
Z^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g};V)
. Given two Lie algebras
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
the vector space of linear maps from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. All smooth deformations appear in this section are parametrized by an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
containing the origin. Throughout, a
smooth family

(s_{t})_{t\in I}
in a finite-dimensional vector space
S
is a smooth curve
I\to S
,
t\mapsto s_{t}
, and will be denoted by
(s_{t})_{t\in I}\subset S
.

The following list summarizes all the deformation-theoretic terminology required for the purposes of this section.

(A).

A smooth deformation
(\mathfrak{g}_{t})_{t\in I}\coloneqq(\mathfrak{g},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{t})_{t\in I}
of a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a smooth family
(\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Hom(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
such that
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{0}=\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
and, for each
t\in I
,
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{t}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie bracket.
The deformation is called trivial, if there exists a smooth family
(\psi_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\GL(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\psi_{0}=\id_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\psi_{t}:\mathfrak{g}\to(\mathfrak{g},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{t})
is a Lie algebra isomorphism for all
t\in I
.

For each
t\in I
, the differential of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{t};\mathfrak{g}_{t})
with values in its adjoint representation, is denoted by
\delta_{\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}^{t}}
.

(B).

A smooth deformation
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
of a Lie algebra morphism
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a smooth family
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Hom(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
such that
\phi_{0}=\phi
and, for each
t\in I
,
\phi_{t}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra morphism.

The deformation is called
\Inn(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial if there exists a smooth family
(g_{t})_{t\in I}\subset G
such that
g_{0}=1_{G}
and
\phi_{t}=\Ad_{g_{t}}\circ\phi
for all
t\in I
. More generally, it is called
\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial if there exists a smooth family
(\Psi_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
such that
\Psi_{0}=\id_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi_{t}=\Psi_{t}\circ\phi
for all
t\in I
.

For each
t\in I
, we denote by
C^{\bullet}_{\phi_{t}}(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{g})
, the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of
\mathfrak{h}
with values in the representation
(\phi_{t}^{*}\circ\ad):\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
, defined by

(\phi_{t}^{*}\circ\ad)_{u}(v)\coloneqq\ad_{\phi_{t}(u)}(v),\ \ \text{for all}\ u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Its differential is denoted by
\delta_{\phi_{t}}
.

(C).

Let
\iota:(\mathfrak{h},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})\to\mathfrak{g}
be an injective Lie algebra morphism. A smooth deformation
(\mathfrak{h}_{t},\iota_{t})_{t\in I}
of the Lie subalgebra
(\mathfrak{h},\iota)
consists of a smooth deformation
(\mathfrak{h}_{t})_{t\in I}\coloneqq(\mathfrak{h},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}^{t})_{t\in I}
of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})
, together with a smooth family
(\iota_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Hom(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
such that
\iota_{0}=\iota
and
\iota_{t}:\mathfrak{h}_{t}\to\mathfrak{g}
is an injective Lie algebra morphism for all
t\in I
.

(D).

Proposition 2.1
.

For any Lie algebra morphism
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
and any smooth deformation
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
of
\phi
, the following statements hold.

(i)

For each
t\in I
, the cochain

\dot{\phi}_{t}\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}\in C_{\phi_{t}}^{1}(\mathfrak{h};\mathfrak{g})

is a cocycle.

(ii)

If
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
is
\Inn(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial, then there exists a smooth family

(x_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\delta_{\phi_{t}}(x_{t})

for all
t\in I
. Conversely, the existence of such a family implies that
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
is
trivial after possibly shrinking
I
.

(iii)

If
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
is
\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial, then there exist smooth
families
(X_{t})_{t\in I}\subset Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
and

(x_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(X_{t})+\delta_{\phi_{t}}(x_{t})

for all
t\in I
. Conversely, the existence of such families implies that
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
is

\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial after possibly shrinking
I
.

Proof.

(i) By assumption, the following equation holds for all
t\in I
, and
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

\phi_{t}\big(\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v)\big)=\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\big(\phi_{t}(u),\phi_{t}(v)\big).

Differentiating with respect to
t
both sides of the equation, we obtain:

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{t}\big(\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle=\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\big(\dot{\phi}_{t}(u),\phi_{t}(v)\big)+\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\big(\phi_{t}(u),\dot{\phi}_{t}(v)\big)\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{t}\big(\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}(u,v)\big)

\displaystyle=-(\phi_{t}^{*}\circ\ad)_{v}(\dot{\phi}(u))+(\phi_{t}^{*}\circ\ad)_{u}(\dot{\phi}_{t}(v)).

The last equation is equivalent to the condition
\delta_{\phi_{t}}(\dot{\phi}_{t})=0
.

(ii) Assuming that
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}
is an
\Inn(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial deformation of
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
, means that

\phi_{t}=\Ad_{g_{t}}\circ\phi,\quad\text{for all}\ t\in I,

where
(g_{t})_{t\in I}
is a smooth curve on the Lie group
G
such that
g_{0}=1_{G}
. As before, by differentiating the above equation with respect to
t
, we conclude that:

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{t}=\ad_{x_{t}}\circ(\Ad_{g_{t}}\circ\phi),\quad\text{where}\ \ x_{t}\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}g_{t}.

The above equation can be rewritten as

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\ad_{x_{t}}\circ\phi_{t}=-(\phi_{t}^{*}\circ\ad)_{(\cdot)}(x_{t}),

which is equivalent to the condition
\dot{\phi}_{t}=\delta_{\phi_{t}}(x_{t})
.

Conversely, we now assume that

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\delta_{\phi_{t}}(x_{t})\quad\text{for all}\ \ t\in I.

Consider the time-dependent vector field

\overrightarrow{x_{t}}

By the local existence theorem for flows, there exists

We claim that

On the one hand, we observe, due to our initial assumption i.e.

(iii)
Assuming that
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}

\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial deformation of
\phi

\phi_{t}=\Psi_{t}\circ\phi,\quad\text{for all}\ t\in I,

(\Psi_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Aut(\mathfrak{g})

\Psi_{0}=\id_{\mathfrak{h}}

X_{t}\circ\Psi_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\Psi_{t}.

(\Psi_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Aut(\mathfrak{g})

(X_{t})_{t\in I}\subset Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
. Differentiating now the equation
\phi_{t}=\Psi_{t}\circ\phi

\dot{\phi}_{t}=X_{t}\circ\phi_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(X_{t}),

, which proves the direct implication.

Conversely, we now assume that

(1)

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(X_{t})+\delta_{\phi_{t}}(x_{t})\quad\text{for all}\ \ t\in I,

(X_{t})_{t\in I}\subset Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})

(x_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathfrak{g}

\Phi_{X}^{t}\coloneqq\Phi_{X}^{t,0}

Since
(X_{t})_{t\in I}\subset Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
, it follows that

(\Phi_{X}^{t})_{t\in I}\subset\Aut(\mathfrak{g})

(2)

\Phi_{X}^{t}\bigl(\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}(x,y)\bigr)=\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl(\Phi_{X}^{t}(x),\Phi_{X}^{t}(y)\bigr),

Next and final step is to show that the Lie algebra morphism

-trivial deformation of
\phi

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(X_{t})+\delta_{\phi_{t}}\big(\Phi_{X}^{t}(\widehat{x_{t}})\big),

\dot{\phi}_{t}=\phi_{t}^{*}(X_{t})+\Phi_{X}^{t}\big(\delta_{\phi^{\prime}_{t}}(\widehat{x_{t}})\big).

Moreover, differentiating both sides of the equation

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}^{\prime}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\bigg)\circ\phi_{t}+(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\dot{\phi}_{t}

\displaystyle=-(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{X}^{t}\bigg)\circ(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}+(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\dot{\phi}_{t}

\displaystyle=-(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ X_{t}\circ\phi_{t}+(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\dot{\phi}_{t}

\displaystyle=\cancel{-(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}^{*}X_{t}}+\cancel{(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}^{*}X_{t}}+(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\Phi_{X}^{t}\big(\delta_{\phi^{\prime}_{t}}(\widehat{x_{t}})\big)

Therefore, by part (ii), there exists a smooth family
(g_{t})_{t\in I}\subset G
with
g_{0}=1_{G}

(\Phi_{X}^{t})^{-1}\circ\phi_{t}=\Ad_{g_{t}}\circ\phi

\phi_{t}=(\Phi_{X}^{t}\circ\Ad_{g_{t}})\circ\phi
, which completes the proof.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any injective Lie algebra morphism
\iota:(\mathfrak{h},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}})\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and any smooth deformation
(\mathfrak{h}_{t},\iota_{t})_{t\in I}
of the Lie subalgebra
(\mathfrak{h},\iota)
, the following statements hold.

(i)

For each
t\in I
, the cochain

\pi_{\can}^{t}\circ\dot{\iota}_{t}\in C^{\bullet}_{\iota_{t}}\big(\mathfrak{h}_{t};\mathfrak{g}/{\iota_{t}(\mathfrak{h}_{t})}\big)

is a cocycle.

(ii)

If
(\mathfrak{h}_{t},\iota_{t})_{t\in I}
is
\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial, then there exists a smooth family
(X_{t})_{t\in I}\subset Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathfrak{g})
such that

\pi_{\can}^{t}\circ\dot{\iota}_{t}=\pi_{\can}^{t}\circ X_{t}\circ\iota_{t}.

Conversely, the existence of such a family implies that
(\mathfrak{h}_{t},\iota_{t})_{t\in I}
is
\Aut(\mathfrak{g})
-trivial after possibly shrinking
I
.

Proof.

(i)
By assumption, the following equation holds for all
t\in I
, and

(\phi_{t})_{*}(F_{t})=F^{\prime}_{t}
, for each
t\in I
. A smooth deformation
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
is called
constant
if
F_{t}=F
for all
t
. A smooth deformation is called
trivial
if it is equivalent to the constant deformation.

The following remark is based on
[
6
, Remark 4.1.]
.

Remark 3.6
.

There is an associated Bott connection
\nabla^{F_{t}}:\Gamma(F_{t})\times\Gamma(\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))\to\Gamma(\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))
to each
F_{t}
, defined by

\nabla^{F_{t}}_{X}(\pi_{t}^{\perp}(Y))=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X,Y]),\quad\text{for any}\ X\in\Gamma(F_{t})\ \text{and}\ Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Denote by
\nu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})\coloneqq(TM\times TI)/\widetilde{F}
the normal bundle to the total foliation
\widetilde{{F}}
and by
\widetilde{\pi}:TM\times TI\to\nu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
the canonical projection. Analogously, there is a Bott connection
\nabla^{\widetilde{{F}}}:\Gamma(\widetilde{F})\times\Gamma(\nu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}))\to\Gamma(\nu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}))
defined by

\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}_{\widetilde{X}}(\widetilde{\pi}(\widetilde{Y}))=\widetilde{\pi}([\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}]),\quad\text{for any}\ \widetilde{X}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{{F}})\ \text{and}\ \widetilde{Y}\in\Gamma(TM\times TI).

Denote by
K\coloneqq(TM\times 0_{I})/\widetilde{F}
the subbundle of
\nu(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}})
which is pointwise, for any
(p,t)\in M\times I
, is given by
K_{(p,t)}=T_{p}M/F_{t}=(\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))_{p}
. The Bott connection
\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}
preserves the subbundle
K
since

\widetilde{\pi}([\widetilde{X},Y])\in\Gamma(K),\quad\ \text{for any}\ \widetilde{X}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F})\ \text{and}\ Y\in\Gamma(TM\times 0_{I}).

Therefore, we get the complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}};K),d_{\nabla^{\widetilde{{F}}}})
. The Bott connections
\nabla^{F_{t}}
on
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t})
and
\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}
on
K
are related, for any
X\in\Gamma(F)
and
Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, by the following equation:

(14)

\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}_{\widetilde{X}_{(p,t)}}(\widetilde{\pi}(Y))=\widetilde{\pi}([\widetilde{{X}},Y]_{(p,t)})=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X_{t},Y_{t}]_{p})=\nabla_{X_{t}|_{p}}^{F_{t}}\pi_{t}^{\perp}(Y_{t}),

where
\widetilde{X}_{(p,t)}=(X_{t}|_{p},0_{t})
and
Y_{(p,t)}\coloneqq(Y_{t}|_{p},0_{t})
for any
(p,t)\in M\times I
. For each time
t\in I
, there is an associated deformation cocycle
\sigma_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{t};\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))
given by

\sigma_{t}(X)=\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{t}(X),\quad X\in\Gamma(F_{t}).

These cocycles give rise to a global cocycle
\sigma\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}};K)
via the following equation:

(15)

\sigma(\widetilde{X}_{(p,t)})\coloneqq\sigma_{t}(X_{t}|_{p}),\quad\text{for any}\ (p,t)\in M\times I.

Notice that
\sigma
is indeed a cocycle since
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in I}
are cocycles. More specifically, this is induced by the following formula

(16)

d_{\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}}(\sigma)(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})=d_{\nabla^{F_{t}}}(\sigma_{t})(X_{t},Y_{t}),\quad\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F}).

In the following theorem the condition that the cocycles
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in I}
vanish smoothly in cohomology with respect to
t
–or equivalently that
[\sigma_{t}]\in H^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{t};\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))
vanish smoothly with respect to
t
–means that the cocycle
\sigma\in\Omega^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}};K)
is a coboundary, which means there exists
Y\in\Gamma(TM\times 0_{I})
such that

(17)

\sigma(\widetilde{X})=d_{\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}}(\widetilde{\pi}(Y))(\widetilde{X}),\quad\text{for any}\ \ \widetilde{X}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F}).

Using (
14
), (
15
) and (
16
), we have for each
t\in I
that:

\sigma(\widetilde{X})=d_{\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}}(\widetilde{\pi}(Y))(\widetilde{X})\Rightarrow\sigma_{t}(X_{t})=d_{\nabla^{F_{t}}}(\pi_{t}^{\perp}(Y_{t}))(X_{t})=\nabla^{F_{t}}_{X_{t}}\pi_{t}^{\perp}(Y_{t})=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X_{t},Y_{t}]).

Now, we are ready to give a direct proof of the Moser trick for foliations
[
4
, Theorem 2.]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
be a smooth deformation of a foliation
F\subset TM
. If
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
is a trivial deformation then the deformation cocycles,

\sigma_{t}\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{t}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{t};\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t})),

vanish smoothly in cohomology with respect to
t
(see Remark
3.6
for the terminology). Conversely, if
M
is compact and
[\sigma_{t}]\in H^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{t};\nu(\mathcal{F}_{t}))
vanish smoothly with respect to
t
, then
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
is trivial.

Proof.

Since
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
is a trivial deformation of
F
, there exists an isotopy
\phi_{t}:M\to M
such that
(\phi_{t})_{*}(F)=F_{t}
. Consider the vector field
\widetilde{Y}=Y_{t}+\partial_{t}
on
M\times I
, where
Y_{t}\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}
the time-dependent vector field generated by the isotopy
\phi_{t}
. The first claim is that the subbundle
D\subset TM\times TI
given by

(18)

D_{(p,t)}\coloneqq\widetilde{F}_{(p,t)}\oplus\langle\widetilde{Y}_{(p,t)}\rangle,\quad\text{for any}\ (p,t)\in M\times I,

is involutive and the second that this induces (is actually equivalent with) equation (
17
). First, consider the diffeomorphism
\widetilde{\phi}:M\times I\to M\times I
defined by
\widetilde{\phi}(p,t)=(\phi_{t}(p),t)
. Now, we want to check that
(\widetilde{\phi})_{*}(F\oplus\langle\partial_{t}\rangle)=D
. We only need to compute how the Jacobian matrix of
\widetilde{\phi}
, in a local chart
\big(U,(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},t)\big)\subset M\times I
, acts on the local frame
(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{n}},\partial_{t})
.

(19)

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc|c}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\\
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial x_{n}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial t}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial x_{n}}&\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial t}\\
\hline\cr 0&0&\cdots&0&1\end{array}\right]\begin{array}[]{l}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{x_{1}}\\
\partial_{x_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\partial_{x_{n}}\\
\cline{1-1}\cr\partial_{t}\end{array}\right]\end{array}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial x_{j}}\partial_{x_{j}}\\
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial x_{j}}\partial_{x_{j}}\\
\vdots\\
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial x_{j}}\partial_{x_{j}}\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{\partial t}\partial_{t}\\
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{2}}{\partial t}\partial_{t}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}_{n}}{\partial t}\partial_{t}\\
0\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
\partial_{t}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence, we obtain that indeed the equation
(\widetilde{\phi})_{*}(F\oplus\langle\partial_{t}\rangle)=(\phi_{t})_{*}(F)+\langle\widetilde{Y}\rangle=D
holds. Since the subbundle
F\oplus\langle\partial_{t}\rangle
of
TM\times TI
is involutive, as a product of two involutive ones:
F\times\{0\}
and
\{0\}\times\langle\partial_{t}\rangle
, and moreover
\widetilde{\phi}
is a diffeomorphism, the involutivity of
D
follows directly. Therefore, this means
[\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}]\in\Gamma(D)
for any
\widetilde{X}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F})
, which induces, for each
t\in I
, that

(20)

[X_{t},Y_{t}+\partial_{t}]\in\Gamma(D),\quad\text{where}\ \widetilde{X}=(X_{t},0_{t})\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F}).

First, notice that the vector field
[X_{t},Y_{t}]
has no
\partial_{t}
-component, due to the involutivity of
F_{t}
. Furthermore, the vector field
[X_{t},\partial_{t}]
also has no
\partial_{t}
-component. This can be observed by writing
\widetilde{X}=f_{i}(p,t)e^{i}_{t}
around a point
(p,t)\in M\times I
, where
\{e^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1}^{k}
is a local frame of
\widetilde{{F}}
, and performing the following quick computation:

[f_{i}(p,t)e^{i}_{t},\partial_{t}]=\frac{\partial f_{i}(p,t)}{\partial t}e^{i}_{t}+f_{i}(p,t)\frac{\partial e^{i}_{t}}{\partial t}\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

We conclude that
[\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}]\in\Gamma(\widetilde{F})
and so
\widetilde{\pi}([\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}])=0
. This is translated, for each
t\in I
, by the equation:
\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X_{t},Y_{t}+\partial_{t}])=0
. Using the fact that
\pi_{t}(X_{t})=X_{t}
and
\pi_{t}^{\perp}(\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{t}(X_{t}))=\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{t}(X_{t})
(see Lemma
3.3
), we obtain that

(21)

\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X_{t},Y_{t}])=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([\partial_{t},X_{t}])=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([\partial_{t},\pi_{t}(X_{t})])=\pi_{t}^{\perp}\bigg(\frac{d}{dt}\pi_{t}(X_{t})\bigg)=\sigma_{t}(X_{t}).

(Notice that in the last part of the proof we showed that the involutivity of the subbundle
D
(
20
) induces (
21
) but it is immediate that the same proof can be simply reversed and let us conclude that the involutivity of
D
and (
17
) are equivalent.)

Conversely, assume there exists
Y\in\Gamma(TM\times 0_{I})
such that
\sigma(\widetilde{X})=d_{\nabla^{\widetilde{F}}}(\widetilde{\pi}(Y))(\widetilde{X})
for any
\widetilde{X}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{{F}})
. Equivalently, there exists a time-dependent vector field
Y=\{Y_{t}\}_{t\in I}
on
M
such that
\sigma_{t}(X_{t})=\pi_{t}^{\perp}([X_{t},Y_{t}])
for each
t\in I
. We consider again the vector field
\widetilde{Y}=Y_{t}+\partial_{t}
on
M\times I
. As we explained above, the involutivity of the subbundle
D\subset TM\times TI
given by (
18
) is equivalent with the equation (
17
). Since the flow of vector fields tangent to a foliation preserve the leaves, we obtain that
(\phi_{\widetilde{Y}}^{t})_{*}(D)=D
where
\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}}:M\times I\to M\times I
is the flow of
\widetilde{Y}\in\Gamma(D)
which is defined for all
t\in I
because
M
is compact. Recall that, by (
29
), we have the following relation

(22)

\phi_{\widetilde{Y}}^{t}(p,0)=(\Phi^{t}_{Y}(p),t),\quad\text{for any}\ (p,t)\in M\times I,

where
\Phi_{Y}^{t}
the time-dependent flow of
Y
. Hence,
\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}}(M\times\{0\})=M\times\{t\}
which induces that
(\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}})_{*}(D|_{{M}\times\{0\}})=D|_{M\times\{t\}}
. Moreover, recall that
D|_{M\times\{t\}}=F_{t}\oplus\langle Y_{t}+\partial_{t}\rangle.
Now, a completely analogous computation to (
19
), together with the fact that
(\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}})_{*}(Y_{0}|_{M\times\{0\}})=Y_{t}|_{M\times\{t\}}
, shows that

(\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}})_{*}(D|_{M\times\{0\}})=(\phi^{t}_{\widetilde{Y}})_{*}(F\oplus\langle Y_{0}+\partial_{t}|_{0}\rangle)=(\Phi_{Y}^{t})_{*}(F)\oplus\langle Y_{t}+\partial_{t}\rangle,

and thus
\Phi^{t}_{Y}
is the desired isotopy trivializing the deformation
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
.
∎

Note that, in the following lemma, no compactness assumption on the ambient manifold
M
, nor the existence of a complete vector field on it, is imposed. Thus, it recovers the result of
[
10
, Proposition 2.12]
under a substantially weaker assumption.

Lemma 3.8
.

A trivial deformation
(F_{t})_{t\in I}
of a foliation
F
gives rise to an exact deformation cocycle
\sigma_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{F},\nu(\mathcal{F}))
.

Proof.

We need to check that there exists
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
such that
\sigma_{0}(X)=\pi_{0}^{\perp}[X,Y]
, for any
X\in\Gamma(F)
. This follows directly from the proof of Theorem
3.7
. We consider
t=0
in equation (
21
).
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The proof of the Moser trick for Lie algebroids
[
4
, Theorem 2.]
shares many similarities with the proof that gauge equivalences of Lie algebroids correspond to their geometric equivalences
[
14
, Proposition 1.1.5.]
. Analogously, Theorem
3.7
is closely related to
[
18
, Theorem 2.6]
. It would be interesting to clarify the precise connection between the deformation-theoretic Moser trick and the equivalence between gauge and geometric equivalences. This more subtle link is left for future investigation.

Appendix A
On curves in the Grassmannian

Let
G
be a finite dimensional real Lie group. Spaces which carry a transitive Lie group
G
-action are called homogeneous
G
-spaces. If
V
is a finite dimensional real vector space, its Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional vector subspaces
\Gr_{k}(V)\coloneqq\{W\subset V:\dim W=k\}
is a homogeneous
\GL(V)
-space and the action is given just by the matrix multiplication (upon choosing a basis for
V
). Transitivity can be seen in the following way: given bases for two subspaces
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
of
V
, we can extend them to bases for
V
and then the linear map which sends one basis to the other, sends also
W_{1}
to
W_{2}
. By the theory of homogeneous spaces, we know that these spaces are quotients of Lie groups modulo closed subgroups. Specifically, for a homogeneous
G
-space
X
, let
x\in X
, we have the following diffeomorphism

G/G_{x}\simeq X,\quad\overline{g}\mapsto gx

where
G_{x}=\{g\in G|\ gx=x\}
is the isotropy group at the point
x
. Hence, translating the above diffeomorphism in the case of Grassmannians we obtain that

\Gr_{k}(V)\simeq\GL(V)/{\GL(V)_{W}}

for any
W\in\Gr_{k}(V)
and
\GL(V)_{W}
the isotropy group of
W
. Applying the tangent functor to the above diffeomorphism, we get the following vector space isomorphism

T_{W}(\Gr_{k}(V))\simeq T_{[\Id]}(\GL(V)/\GL(V)_{W}.

We can make the above isomorphism a bit more specific:

(23)

T_{[\Id]}(\GL(V)/\GL(W;V))\simeq\mathfrak{gl}(V)/\mathfrak{gl}(V)_{W}

where
\mathfrak{gl}(V)_{W}=\{B\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)|\ BW=W\}
is the Lie algebra corresponding to
\GL(V,W)
. Denote by
\Pi:\GL(V)\to\GL(V)/\GL(V,W)
the canonical projection and consider its tangent map at the identity:

T_{\Id}\Pi:\mathfrak{gl}(V)\to T_{[\Id]}\big(\GL(V)/\GL(V)_{W}\big).

Hence,
T_{\Id}(\Pi^{-1}(\overline{0}))=T_{\Id}\GL(V)_{W}=\ker(T_{\Id}\Pi)=\mathfrak{gl}(V)_{W}
.

By the first isomorphism theorem, we obtain (
23
).
Applying, once again, the first isomorphism theorem to the map
\pi_{V/W}\circ\res:\Hom(V,V)\to\Hom(W,V/W)
, where
\pi_{V/W}\circ\res
is just the restriction map
\res:\Hom(V,V)\to\Hom(W,V)
followed by the canonical projection
\pi_{V/W}:V\to V/W
, we get that
\mathfrak{gl}(V)/\mathfrak{gl}(V)_{W}\simeq W^{*}\otimes V/W
. Summing up, we conclude that

(24)

T_{W}(\Gr_{k}(V))\simeq W^{*}\otimes V/W.

It will be useful, for our purposes, to interpret the above isomorphism in terms of curves. The isotropy group
G_{x}
at any
x\in X
is a closed subgroup of
G
and acts on
G
just by the group multiplication. By quotient manifold theorem (
[
15
]
, Thm. 21.10), we can conclude that
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Pi}}{{\to}}G/G_{x}
is a
(G_{x})
-principal bundle. Into the context of Grassmannians, we get that the canonical projection

\GL(V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Pi}}{{\to}}\GL(V)/\GL(V)_{W}

is a
\GL(V)_{W}
-principal bundle. Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval containing the origin, and
\gamma:J\to\Gr_{k}(V)
be a smooth curve in
\Gr_{k}(V)
, with
\gamma(0)=W
. We denote its value
\gamma(t)
by
W_{t}
.

(25)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GL(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gr_{k}(V)\simeq\GL(V)/{\GL(V)_{W}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}

By local triviality of principal bundles, there exists an open subinterval
I\subseteq J
containing zero, and a curve
\alpha:I\to\GL(V)
, denoted by
\alpha(t)\eqqcolon\alpha_{t}
, such that
\Pi\circ\alpha_{t}=W_{t}
for each
t\in I
. Equivalently, the last condition means that
\alpha_{0}=\Id
and
\alpha_{t}(W)=W_{t}
for each
t\in I
. Differentiating at
t=0
, we get the equation
\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}{W_{t}}=T_{\Id}\Pi\circ\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}{\alpha_{t}}
. Using the isomorphism (
23
), we conclude that the interpretation of (
24
) via curves is given by:

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}{W_{t}}\mapsto\pi_{V/W}\circ\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}{\alpha_{t}}.

We have just explained in detail that, given
W\in\Gr_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
and a smooth curve
(W_{t})_{t\in J}\subset\Gr_{k}(\mathfrak{g})
with
W_{0}=W
, there exists a smooth curve
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
0\in I\subseteq J
, such that
\alpha_{0}=\Id
and
\alpha_{t}(W)=W_{t}
, for every

.

Conclusion
: As is common in deformation theory, the present manuscript is concerned not with arbitrarily large deformations, but rather with sufficiently small ones. This appendix therefore shows that the definitions of deformations of Lie subalgebras and Lie ideals in terms of curves in the Grassmannian are locally equivalent to definitions (C) and (D) introduced in Section
2
, respectively, and are therefore sufficient for our purposes.

Appendix B
On Time-Dependent Vector Fields

We briefly recall a few basics on time-dependent vector fields, which can be found, e.g., in
[
12
]
.

A time-dependent vector field is a smooth family of vector fields
{X}=\{X_{t}\}_{t\in I}
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval containing the origin. Specifically, it is a smooth map

{X}:I\times M\to TM,\quad{X}(t,p)\eqqcolon X_{t}(p)\in T_{p}M.

The flow
\Phi^{t,s}_{{X}}
of a time-dependent vector field
{X}
, consists of local diffeomorphisms
\Phi^{t,s}_{{X}}:M\to M
, which depend both on the parameters
t
and
s
, determined by

(28)

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi^{t,s}_{{X}}(p)=X_{t}(\Phi^{t,s}_{{X}}(p)),\quad\Phi^{s,s}_{{X}}(p)=p.

Moreover, it satisfies the property
\Phi^{t,u}_{{X}}\circ\Phi^{u,s}_{{X}}=\Phi^{t,s}_{{X}}
by taking care of the values of the flow domain analogously to the time-independent case. The time-dependent vector field
{X}=\{X_{t}\}
, can be seen as time-independent vector field
\widetilde{X}
on
M\times I
as follows

\widetilde{X}(t,p)=X_{t}(p)+\partial_{t}.

The flow of
\widetilde{X}
and the (time-dependent) flow of
X=\{X_{t}\}
are related by the following relation

(29)

\phi_{\widetilde{X}}^{t}(p,s)=(\Phi^{t+s,s}_{X}(p),t+s).

In the context of deformations, we are mainly interested in what happens for parameters around
0\in I
and so it is enough to study the family of local diffeomorphisms

\Phi^{t}_{{X}}\coloneqq\Phi^{t,0}_{{X}}.

Definition B.1
.

A smooth one parameter family of (global) diffeomorphisms
(\phi_{t})_{t\in I}\in\text{Diff}(M)
such that
\phi_{0}=\id
, is called an
isotopy
of
M
.

Every isotopy
\phi_{t}:M\to M
gives rise to a time-dependent vector field
{Y}=\{Y_{t}\}_{t\in I}
defined, for each
p\in M
, by

(30)

Y_{t}(\phi_{t}(p))=\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}(p)

For the sake of simplicity, we use the notation
Y_{t}=\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}
. Its corresponding (time-dependent) flow is given by

(31)

\Phi_{{Y}}:I\times I\times M\to M,\quad\Phi_{{Y}}^{t,s}(p)=\phi_{t}\circ\phi_{s}^{-1}(p),

which directly can be checked by the relations in (
28
).
