Correction: Public Spaces as Hotspots of Zoonotic Gastrointestinal Parasite Transmission: Evidence from Small Animal and Soil Surveillance in Malaysia
Authors: Suey Yee Low, Sze Kee Gun, Sadiq Mohammed Babatunde, Sharifah Salmah Syed Hussain, Wan Nur Ismah Wan Ahmad Kamil, Abdul Rahman Omar, Azalea Hani Othman, Tengku Rinalfi Putra Tengku Azizan, Maizatul Akmal Moktar, Noraain binti Azman, Yian Ming Tan, Nor Azlina Abdul Aziz
Published: 2026

Correction: Journal of Epidemiology and Global Health (2026) 16:12

10.1007/s44197-025-00511-w

The following errors have been corrected in abstract, text and table 3 of the published article:

In the abstract, section ‘Results’, the second sentence incorrectly stated ‘Of these, 63 samples (88.7%) were positive for at least one intestinal parasite. The prevalence of parasite in cat faeces (95.0%, 38/40) was higher than dogs (80.7%, 25/31).’ and was corrected to ‘Of these, 56 samples (78.9%) were positive for at least one intestinal parasite. The prevalence of parasite in cat faeces (90.0%, 36/40) was higher than dogs (64.5%, 20/31).’

In the same section, ‘Risk factor analysis revealed that the presence of dustbin was associated with reduced hookworm infections (OR = 0.075, 95% CI: 0.007–0.520) in dogs. Higher odds of hookworms were found in cat faeces (OR = 4.961, 95% CI: 1.10– 25.98) and soil (OR = 5.77, 95% CI: 1.54–20.26) from residential parks.’ was corrected to ‘Risk factor analysis revealed that the absence of dustbin was associated with higher hookworm infections (OR = 15.11, 95% CI: 2.48–92.1, p = 0.004) in dogs. Hookworm infection was more likely in cat faecal samples collected from apartment playgrounds compared to resident parks (OR = 5.20, 95% CI: 1.32–20.54, p = 0.025). Soil from dog-friendly parks had higher odds of Ancylostoma infection than soil from residential parks (OR = 5.85, 95% CI: 1.90–17.98, p = 0.004).’

In section 3.2 Detection of Gastrointestinal Parasite in Faecal Samples, a sentence incorrectly stated ‘Of these, 63 samples (88.7%, 95% CI = 79.3%−94.2%) were positive for at least one intestinal parasite. The prevalence of parasite in cat faeces (95.0%, 38/40, 95% CI = 83.5%−98.6%) was higher than dogs (80.7%, 25/31, 95% CI = 63.7%90.8%, P = 0.058).’ and was corrected to ‘Of these, 56 samples (78.9%, 95% CI = 68.0%-86.8%) were positive for at least one intestinal parasite. The prevalence of parasite in cat faeces (90.0%, 36/40, 95% CI = 77.0%-96.0%) was higher than dogs (64.5%, 20/31, 95% CI = 47.0%-78.9%, P = 0.058).’

In section 3.5 Concordance and Risk Factors of Hookworm Detection between Faecal and Soil Samples, a sentence incorrectly started with ‘The presence of dustbins was associated with ...’ and was corrected to ‘The absence of dustbins was associated with ...’.

In the same section, another sentence incorrectly stated ‘Soil from residential parks had higher odds of Ancylostoma infection than soil from dog-friendly parks (OR = 5.85, 95% CI: 1.90–17.98, p = 0.004).’ and was corrected to ‘Soil from dog-friendly parks had higher odds of Ancylostoma infection than soil from residential parks (OR = 5.85, 95% CI: 1.90–17.98, p = 0.004).’

In section 4 Discussion, ‘In this survey, the presence of dustbin was associated with higher hookworm prevalence in dog faecal samples.’ was corrected to ‘In this survey, the absence of dustbin was associated with higher hookworm prevalence in dog faecal samples.’

In the same section, ‘Meanwhile, cat faecal samples and soil collected from apartment playgrounds showed higher odds of detecting hookworm.’ was corrected to ‘Meanwhile, cat faecal samples collected from apartment playgrounds showed higher odds of detecting hookworm.’

Table 3 contained several mistakes. Please find the corrected table on the following page.
