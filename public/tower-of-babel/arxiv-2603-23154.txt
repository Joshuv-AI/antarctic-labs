From Quantum Dimers to the π \pi -flux Toric Code via Deconfined Multicriticality
Ankush Chaubey, Sergej Moroz, Subhro Bhattacharjee

Abstract
Two-dimensional Rokhsar-Kivelson (RK) dimer models on bipartite lattices are generally limited to translation-symmetry-broken dimer crystals. We introduce a tensor-product regularisation of the dimer Hilbert space that yields a qubit Hamiltonian interpolating from the RK model to the π \pi -flux toric code, thereby accessing a deconfined ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} topological liquid. In this framework, the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} liquid descends from a multicritical U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) spin liquid through condensation of a charge-2 Higgs field, thus avoiding confinement. Using iDMRG together with low-energy field theory, we determine a phase diagram containing two continuous quantum phase transitions—a 3 ​ D 3\mathrm{D} XY ∗ transition between the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} liquid and the columnar/plaquette-VBS, and a quantum Lifshitz transition between two dimer crystals—alongside a first-order transition between the staggered crystal and the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} liquid. Our field theory suggests a deconfined multicritical point described by an Abelian Higgs model with dynamical critical exponent, z = 2 z=2 , where the three transitions meet, highlighting the interplay of fractionalisation and emergent gauge fluctuations.

Numerical Results

We performed infinite DMRG (or iDMRG) calculations in the infinite cylinder geometry using the tensor network Python (TeNPy) library

Neglecting the gauge fluctuations and the temporal fluctuations of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}{\bf q}~\left[\eta{\bf q}^{2}+\eta_{2}{\bf q}^{4}+r\right]|\Phi({\bf q})|^{2}.

(45)

Such that the momentum of the soft modes,

\eta<0
for

\displaystyle r\sim\frac{\eta^{2}}{\eta_{2}}

(46)

IV.2
Applicability of the field theory to the microscopic model

Having fleshed out the details of the field theory, we now briefly summarise the applicability of the field theory (Fig.
8
) to the microscopic model (Eq. (
8
)) and the resultant phase diagram (Fig.
1
) in the light of the numerical results discussed in Sec.
III
.

We can tune the microscopic model (
8
) to the RK point by approaching the origin in the phase diagram shown in Fig.
1
along the line
\Gamma/J=\Omega/J
. This can be achieved by tuning
J\to\infty
while keeping
\Gamma
and
\Omega
finite, since the higher-order terms generated in perturbation theory, of order
\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{2}/J)
or higher, vanish in this limit. As a result, we are left with the RK Hamiltonian given in Eq. (
2
) at
\Gamma=\Omega
. This leads us to the identification of the RK point of Fig.
8
with the origin of Fig.
1
.

Moving away from the RK point, the Lifshitz transition line emerges, as shown in Fig.
8
. This transition is manifested in Fig.
6
(b,c) by a continuous increase of the c/p-VBS order parameter. Further, as commented in Sec.
III
, the thin sliver where both the order parameters are zero may allow for an intermediate finite tilt phase in accordance with the field theory (see Fig.
8
). This is also very indirectly supported by the broad correlation length peak for the corresponding window as shown in Fig.
7
. While the scope of the present numerics is limited, the above observations are prima facie consistent with the prediction of the field theory presented above. Indeed, a soft mode analysis of the electric modes at finite but small
\Omega/J
(see Appendix
B.2
) suggests that the position of the soft modes changes continuously away from the c/p-VBS for finite tilt VBS phases within Gaussian approximation. Upon further decreasing
J
, the Lifshitz transition line meets both the first-order transition from the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
liquid to the s-VBS phase and the 3D XY
∗
transition from the c/p-VBS phase to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
liquid at a multicritical point (see Fig.
1
). This multicritical point corresponds to the origin in Fig.
8
.

Finally, beyond the multicritical point, the transition between the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
liquid and the s-VBS appears to be strongly first-order, and the tilt almost immediately locks to the maximal value (corresponding to the s-VBS phase) as is evident from Fig.
7
(d) and the discussion therein.

V
Summary and Outlook

In this work, we have studied a particular qubit regularisation of the QDM on the square lattice that continuously interpolates the physics of the dimer models with that of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological liquid as realised in the toric code with
\pi
-flux. This qubit regularisation allows for mixing the one-dimer and three-dimer manifolds with a controlled energy cost. The emergent
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
liquid is intimately connected to this mixing that allows for condensation of a charge-2 Higgs scalar in the
U(1)
theory, usually realised as the effective description of the dimer model on a bipartite lattice
Sachdev and Vojta 1999
;
Read and Sachdev 1990
;
Sachdev and Jalabert 1990
. The resultant phase diagram illustrated in Fig.
1
appears to be very rich and in particular realises an extended
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
liquid phase and its transition to the dimer crystal via interesting quantum phase transitions.

Recent experimental works have successfully realised the toric code and diagnosed its associated
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological order across diverse quantum platforms
Weimer et al. 2011
;
Weimer et al. 2010
;
Glaetzle et al. 2014
;
Celi et al. 2020
. That includes the realisation of the toric code ground state and the demonstration of anyon braiding statistics on superconducting processors
Satzinger et al. 2021
, the demonstration of topological
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
spin liquid in Rydberg atom arrays
Semeghini et al. 2021
, and the deterministic preparation of the toric code on a torus using mid-circuit measurements and feed-forward on a trapped-ion quantum computer
Iqbal et al. 2024
. Our qubit regularisation of the quantum dimer model opens a path towards its realisation with current quantum technologies that could shed additional light on how the fine-tuned RK
U(1)
spin liquid transitions into the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological liquid quantum phase.

Central to the structure of the phase diagram is a multicritical
U(1)
liquid with dynamic critical exponent,
z=2
, that emanates from the RK point. Various phases are naturally understood as instabilities of this
U(1)
liquid. It would be interesting to investigate in detail the fate of the multicritical point and nearby phases and phase transitions on square and other two-dimensional lattices.

\displaystyle Z_{ij}=\tau^{x}_{i}\rho^{z}_{ij}\tau^{x}_{j},~~~~~~~X_{ij}=\rho_{ij}^{x},

where

\displaystyle\kappa\sum_{\Box}\prod_{ij\in\Box}\rho^{z}_{ij}-\Gamma\sum_{i}\tau^{z}_{i}-\left(J+\frac{\Omega}{2}\right)\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\tau^{x}_{i}\rho^{z}_{ij}\tau^{x}_{j}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Omega}{4}\sum_{i}\sum_{\alpha={\bf\hat{x}},{\bf\hat{y}}}\left[\tau^{x}_{i}\rho^{z}_{i,i+\alpha}\rho_{i+\alpha,i+2\alpha}^{z}\tau^{x}_{i+2\alpha}\right].

(49)

-flux constraint

\displaystyle\prod_{ij\in\Box}\rho^{z}_{ij}=-1~~~~~~\forall~~\Box

\pi
-flux through each plaquette. The amplitude for nearest neighbour (NN) and third nearest neighbour (3NN) hopping are

Appendix C
Derivation of the field theory from the soft modes

The soft modes (Eq. (
17
)) obtained from the analysis of the IGT (Appendix
B
) leads to the low energy Lattice action (on the three dimensional cubic lattice in Euclidean space-time) for the soft modes allowed by the PSG (Table
1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=

\displaystyle-t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\rho^{z}_{ij}\cos\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}\right)-w\sum_{i}\cos(8\theta_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{i\pi}{4}(1-\rho_{ij}^{z})\left(1-\prod_{ij\in\Box}\mu^{z}_{IJ}\right)

(64)

where we have used the lattice version of Eq. (
18
). The first term denotes the hopping of the soft (electric mode) in the background Ising gauge field
\rho_{ij}^{z}
while the second term is the lowest symmetry allowed term
Blankschtein et al. 1984
;
Nanda et al. 2020
;
Senthil and Fisher 2000
;
Bhattacharjee 2011
that reduces the
O(2)
symmetry of the soft mode action. Finally, the third term corresponds to the Ising Chern-Simons term
Senthil and Fisher 2000
;
Bhattacharjee 2011
that captures the mutual semionic statistics between the electric charges and (gapped) magnetic charges with the latter being minimally coupled to the dual Ising gauge field
\mu^{z}_{IJ}
on the links of the dual lattice (
i.e.
, the lattice on which the dimers sit).

The partition function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{\{\rho^{z}\}}\sum_{\{\mu^{z}\}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathcal{D}\theta~e^{-\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

(65)

Following Refs.
Senthil and Fisher 2000
;
Nanda et al. 2020
;
Bhattacharjee 2011
we use Villain transformation and Poisson re-summation formula to convert the partition function to

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{\{\rho^{z}\}}\sum_{\{\mu^{z}\}}\sum_{\{L\}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathcal{D}\theta~e^{-\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

(66)

with the low-energy action

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\frac{L_{ij}^{2}}{2t}+i\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}L_{ij}\left(\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}+\frac{\pi}{2}(1-\rho^{z}_{ij})\right)

\displaystyle-w\sum_{i}\cos(8\theta_{i})+\frac{i\pi}{4}(1-\rho_{ij}^{z})\left(1-\prod_{ij\in\Box}\mu^{z}_{IJ}\right)

(67)

where
L_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer-valued field defined on the links of the lattice.

Temporarily setting
w=0
, we can integrate out
\theta_{i}
to constrain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in i}L_{ij}=0~~~~~\forall~i

(68)

which can be solved by introducing an integer dual gauge field,
b_{IJ}
, on the links of the dual lattice via the lattice curl

\displaystyle L_{ij}=\nabla\times b_{IJ}

(69)

such that the partition function is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{\{\rho^{z}\}}\sum_{\{\mu^{z}\}}\sum_{\{b\}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathcal{D}\theta~e^{-\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

(70)

where the action (after rearranging) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(2)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\Box}\frac{(\nabla\times b_{IJ})^{2}}{2t}

\displaystyle+i\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(1-\rho^{z}_{ij})\left[(\nabla\times b_{IJ})+\left[1-\prod_{ij\in\Box}\mu^{z}_{IJ}\right]\right].

(71)

Now,
\rho^{z}_{ij}
can be integrated individually on each link of the direct lattice separately to obtain the constraint

\displaystyle(-1)^{(\nabla\times b_{IJ})}=\prod_{ij\in\Box}\mu^{z}_{IJ}

(72)

Continuing to follow methods used in Refs.
Senthil and Fisher 2000
;
Bhattacharjee 2011
, we divide the integer gauge field into two parts

\displaystyle b_{IJ}=2B_{IJ}+\beta_{IJ}

(73)

where
B\in\mathbb{Z}
keeps track of the even part and
\beta_{IJ}=0,1

\displaystyle\mu_{IJ}^{z}=1-2\beta_{IJ}

(74)

Implementing the Integer constraint on the gauge field
b_{IJ}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{J}\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\cos(2\pi B_{IJ})=-\mathcal{J}\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\mu^{z}_{IJ}~\cos(\pi b_{IJ})

\mathcal{J}>0

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{\{\mu^{z}_{IJ}\}}\int\mathcal{D}b~e^{-\mathcal{S}^{(3)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(3)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}=\frac{1}{2t\pi^{2}}\sum_{\Box}\left(\nabla\times b_{IJ}\right)^{2}-\mathcal{J}\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\mu^{z}_{IJ}\cos(b_{IJ}).

(77)

We have shifted
b_{IJ}\rightarrow b_{IJ}/\pi

\mu^{z}_{IJ}
per bond to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}b~e^{-\mathcal{S}^{(4)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

(78)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(4)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Box}\frac{\left(\nabla\times b_{IJ}\right)^{2}}{2t\pi^{2}}-\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\mathcal{J}^{2}\cos(2b_{IJ})+\cdots

We can now extract a charge field,

\nabla\cdot b=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}b~e^{-\mathcal{S}^{(5)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}}

(80)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(4)}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Box}\frac{\left(\nabla\times b_{IJ}\right)^{2}}{2t\pi^{2}}-\mathcal{J}^{2}\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\cos(\chi_{I}-\chi_{J}+2b_{IJ})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Box}\frac{\left(\nabla\times b_{IJ}\right)^{2}}{2t\pi^{2}}-\mathcal{J}^{2}\sum_{\langle IJ\rangle}\left[\Psi^{*}_{I}e^{i2b_{IJ}}\Psi_{J}+{\rm c.c.}\right]
