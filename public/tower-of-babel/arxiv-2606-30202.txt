A survey of trust-region radius update mechanisms. Part I: First-order analysis
Jérémy Rieussec, Fabian Bastin

Abstract
We isolate three structural conditions on trust-region radius update rules for smooth unconstrained nonlinear optimisation, and study the class of mechanisms they define. The conditions act on the radius directly: a lower bound relative to the gradient norm, a contraction on unsuccessful iterations, and a controlled expansion on successful ones. A mechanism is weakly admissible if it satisfies the first two conditions, and strongly admissible if it satisfies the lower bound together with the controlled-expansion condition. Under uniformly bounded model Hessians, weak admissibility yields lim k → ∞ ‖ ∇ f ​ ( x k ) ‖ = 0 \lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|=0 , and strong admissibility yields the optimal worst-case complexity 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) {\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-2}) for first-order stationarity. Strong admissibility extends the convergence guarantee to linearly growing model Hessians. We verify admissibility for five mechanism classes: fixed-factor, step-driven, retrospective, criticality-anchored, and gradient-scaled. Along the way, we prove convergence of the retrospective update under linearly growing model Hessians and revisit the framework of Curtis and Scheinberg 2020 ; Wang and Yuan 2022 : we extend it to three distinct scaling factors with decoupled step acceptance (covering η = 0 \eta=0 ), and specialise its stochastic version to the deterministic gradient-scaled update.

The radius update problem.

The radius update rule drives this adaptation of

.
Many rules have been proposed: ratio-based, step-tracking, retrospective, criticality-based, gradient-proportional.
Each has been analysed in isolation, under its own assumptions and with its own convergence proof.
No unified perspective exists on which structural properties make a rule work.
This makes it hard to compare mechanisms, to identify what is intrinsic to the trust-region framework, and to transfer guarantees across settings.

Contributions and outline.

The choice of the radius update mechanism is crucial. Various mechanisms have been proposed across contexts, with distinct convergence analyses and asymptotic behaviours. We address the following questions:

1.

Which trust-region radius update mechanisms have been proposed in the literature?

2.

Which properties are intrinsic to the trust-region framework, i.e. independent of the specific radius update rule?

3.

Do all mechanisms ensure global convergence? Do they compare in their guarantees?

4.

Does the update mechanism affect the worst-case complexity of the algorithm?

5.

Which updates can offer superlinear convergence under suitable conditions?

We isolate three structural conditions on the radius update rule and study the class of mechanisms they define.
Let
M_{k}
denote a running bound on the model Hessian norm, and let

denote the model gradient at iteration

The three conditions are, with

\displaystyle\textbf{Lower bound:}\quad\Delta_{k}\;\geq\;\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{k}}\min_{0\leq j\leq k}\|g_{j}\|\quad\text{for some }\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\textbf{Contraction:}\quad\Delta_{k+1}\;\leq\;\underline{\gamma_{2}}\,\Delta_{k}\quad\text{when }\rho_{k}<\tilde{\eta},

\displaystyle\textbf{Expansion:}\quad\Delta_{k+1}\;\leq\;\overline{\gamma_{3}}\,\Delta_{k}\quad\text{when }\rho_{k}\geq\tilde{\eta},

Relation to existing work.

The closest predecessor of the present work is the survey of
Yuan 2015
, which reviews trust-region algorithms for unconstrained optimisation, nonsmooth problems, equality- and inequality-constrained problems, and conic and subspace variants. Yuan’s survey is broad in scope and catalogues mechanisms, applications, and open questions across the trust-region landscape. Our focus is narrower and complementary. Whereas
Yuan 2015
surveys mechanisms one at a time, we organise them around three structural conditions on the radius update. This
condition-based
viewpoint unifies the convergence analyses and makes the guarantees of different mechanisms directly comparable.

Cartis et al. 2022
introduce the lower-bound property on the radius (our C1) for the classical fixed-factor update. We elevate it to a named condition, verify it holds for all mechanisms studied, and show it is key for global convergence.

[
32
;
33
;
56
]
propose a nonlinear stepsize control framework that unifies trust-region and regularisation schemes. The radius follows a specific function
\Delta(\delta,\chi)=\delta^{\alpha}\chi^{\beta}

and
\beta\in[0,1]

Finally, unlike ARC-type frameworks
[
12
;
3
;
45
]
, which tie convergence to regularisation, our conditions act directly on the radius.

2.1
Basic trust-region algorithm (BTR)

Algorithm
1
outlines the
basic trust-region
(BTR) method.
At each iteration
k
, a model
m_{k}
is built to serve as a local approximation of
f
in a neighborhood of the current iterate
x_{k}
called the
trust region
.
We consider quadratic models, commonly used in the literature, of the form

m_{k}(s)=f_{k}+g_{k}^{\top}s+\frac{1}{2}s^{\top}H_{k}s,

(2.1)

in the
trust region
defined as the ball
\mathcal{B}_{k}\overset{def}{=}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\,\text{ s.t. }\,\|x-x_{k}\|_{k}\leq\Delta_{k}\right\}
, and where
H_{k}
is the Hessian of the objective function at
x_{k}
, or an approximation.
Please refer to
Conn et al. 2000
and
Cartis et al. 2022
for a detailed discussion on the choice of the model, including linear or quadratic models, and more complex ones based on higher-order derivatives or other approximations.
A trial step
s_{k}
which achieves some
“sufficient”
decrease condition on the model is computed.
These sufficient decrease conditions are discussed in subsection
2.3
.
Then, at Step 3, the ratio
\rho_{k}
, defined in (
2.2
), evaluates how accurately the model predicts the actual reduction in the objective function.
Values close to one indicate good agreement between the model and the function, whereas values close to zero reveal poor predictive quality.
A threshold parameter
\eta\in[0,1)
determines whether the trial step is accepted (
successful
) or rejected (
unsuccessful
).
When
\rho_{k}<\eta
, the model is deemed unreliable—typically because the trust region is too large—and the radius is reduced so that a new trial step is computed in a smaller region where the model is more accurate.

Step 0: Initialisation.
Choose
\eta\geq 0
.

Set an initial iterate
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, an initial trust-region radius
\Delta_{0}\in(0\,,\,\infty)
.

Compute
f(x_{0})
and set
k=0
.

Step 1: Model definition and step computation.
Define a local model
m_{k}
of
f
in the neighbourhood
\mathcal{B}_{k}
.

Step 2: Model minimisation.
Compute a step
s_{k}
that
“sufficiently reduces the model”

m_{k}
with
x_{k}+s_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
, and define the candidate point
x_{k}^{\text{cand}}=x_{k}+s_{k}
.

Step 3: Acceptance of the trial point.
Compute
f(x_{k}^{\text{cand}})
and define

\rho_{k}=\dfrac{f(x_{k})-f(x_{k}^{\text{cand}})}{m_{k}(0)-m_{k}(s_{k})}

(2.2)

If
\rho_{k}\geq\eta
, then define
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+s_{k}
; otherwise take
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
.

Step 4: Trust-region radius update.
Compute
\Delta_{k+1}
according to some update rule.

Step 5: Next iteration.
Increment
k
by 1 and repeat
Steps 1 to 5
while some stopping criterion is not met.

2.2
Assumptions on function

We state the assumptions used to establish first-order convergence.
The first assumption stresses that the objective function should not be unbounded below, implying there exist points where the gradient norm can be made arbitrarily small, possibly even zero, see
Ekeland 1974
.

Assumption, AF 1
.

f
is bounded below on
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
. Formally,

\exists\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
f(x)\geq\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}
.

Assumption AF.
2
is a common assumption in optimisation, it ensures that the gradient does not change too abruptly. It is crucial for worst-case complexity analysis.

Cartis et al. 2022
show that without Lipschitz continuity, for any finite sequence of test points, one can construct a one-dimensional counterexample
f
where the gradient norms do not decrease below any threshold.
Therefore, it is impossible to give any guarantees for a finite sequence of test points to get the norm of the gradient to fall below any prescribed accuracy threshold.

Assumption, AF 2
(Gradient Lipschitz continuity).

f
is differentiable with Lipschitz continuous gradient with constant
L>0

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}.

Remark 1
.

Another assumption often made in the context of trust-region methods, which can be found in
[
50
;
51
;
53
;
36
;
65
]
, is that the gradient function
\nabla f
is uniformly continuous. However, it is implied by Lipschitz continuity of the gradient.

2.3
Assumptions on the model

We now introduce Assumption AM.
1
.
It requires that the model
m_{k}
matches the objective function
f
at the current iterate
x_{k}
up to first order.
This condition ensures that the model accurately reflects both the value and the gradient of the objective function at
x_{k}
, providing a reliable local approximation for the trust-region step computation.

Assumption, AM 1
.

For all
k
, we have
m_{k}(0)=f(x_{k})
and
g_{k}\overset{def}{=}\nabla_{x}m_{k}(0)=\nabla_{x}f(x_{k})
.

The trial step
s_{k}
is obtained by approximately minimising the model
m_{k}(s)
in the trust region
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, ensuring a “sufficient” decrease in the model. To motivate this condition, we recall the descent property in gradient descent. With the update
x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\nabla f(x_{k})
and Lipschitz continuity of the gradient (see AF.
2
in Section
2.2
), one obtains the descent lemma
[
43
]
,

\Bigl(\alpha_{k}-\tfrac{L}{2}\alpha_{k}^{2}\Bigr)\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}),

which, for
\alpha_{k}=1/L
, reduces to

\frac{1}{2L}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}).

This inequality underpins the convergence analysis of gradient descent in nonconvex settings
[
43
]
, implying in particular that, if
f
is bounded below, then
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|=0
. With non-constant stepsizes, a line search can ensure sufficient decrease via the Armijo condition.

By analogy, trust-region methods enforce sufficient decrease through the
Cauchy step
, obtained by minimising the quadratic model
m_{k}(s)
along the gradient direction within the trust region. When the model is quadratic, the exact minimiser along this arc can be computed explicitly.
The resulting step, referred to as the
Cauchy step
, is defined as

s_{k}^{C}=-t_{k}^{C}g_{k}\quad\text{where}\quad t_{k}^{C}=\underset{t\geq 0,\;-tg_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}{\mathrm{argmin}}\,\left\{m_{k}\left(-tg_{k}\right)\right\}.

(2.4)

and
x^{C}=x_{k}+s_{k}^{C}
is defined as the associated
Cauchy point
, while the model decrease
m_{k}(0)-m_{k}(s_{k}^{C})
at the Cauchy point, is also referred to as the
Cauchy decrease

[
17
]
.
It is an important feature of the convergence theory as it is possible to determine a minimum decrease when moving to this point, as shown in Theorem
2.1
(see
[
17
;
46
]

Theorem 2.1
.

For the quadratic model (
2.1
), the model decrease at the Cauchy step (
2.4
) satisfies the inequality

m_{k}(0)-m_{k}\left(s_{k}^{C}\right)\geq\dfrac{1}{2}\|g_{k}\|\min\left[\dfrac{\|g_{k}\|}{\|H_{k}\|},\,\Delta_{k}\right].

(2.5)

where, by convention, for
\|H_{k}\|=0
, we set
\frac{\|g_{k}\|}{\|H_{k}\|}=+\infty
.

Proof.

See
Conn et al. 2000
or

Remark 2
.

When the Hessian is zero, the model is linear and the Cauchy point reduces to the point on the boundary in the direction of the gradient, i.e.
s_{k}^{C}=-\Delta_{k}\frac{g_{k}}{\|g_{k}\|}

Assumption, AA 1

For all

2.4
Trust-region subproblem

The trust-region subproblem (
2.7
), widely studied

[
15
;
17
;
41
;
42
;
46
;
53
;
60
]
, is a key component of trust-region methods.
In our context, the subproblem involves minimising a quadratic model (
2.1
) constrained to a ball of radius
\Delta_{k}
centered at the current iterate
x_{k}
, i.e.,

\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}m_{k}(s)\quad\text{subject to}\quad\|s\|\leq\Delta_{k}.

(2.7)

When
H_{k}
is positive definite, the unconstrained exact solution to this problem is given by
H_{k}s_{k}=-g_{k}
, but it can be outside the trust region if
\Delta_{k}
is small.
Therefore, the solution to the trust-region subproblem is often found on the boundary of the trust region, i.e.,
\|s_{k}\|=\Delta_{k}
, especially when
H_{k}
is not positive definite.
Solving the trust-region subproblem exactly can be computationally expensive, especially for large-scale problems.
Therefore, it is common to use iterative methods to find an approximate solution, like the truncated Conjugate Gradient (CG) method, or Krylov subspace methods
[
53
;
41
;
46
;
15
]
.

Theorem 2.2

Consider any non-negative constant

|\rho_{k}-1|=\dfrac{|f(x_{k}+s_{k})-f(x_{k})-g_{k}^{T}\,s_{k}-\frac{1}{2}s_{k}^{T}H_{k}s_{k}|}{m_{k}(0)-m_{k}(s_{k})}\leq\dfrac{\left(L+\|H_{k}\|\right)\,\|s_{k}\|^{2}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\|g_{k}\|\min\left\{\|s_{k}\|\,,\,\dfrac{\|g_{k}\|}{\|H_{k}\|}\right\}},

For any non-negative constant

\Delta_{k}\geq\|s_{k}\|>\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}(1-\eta)\|g_{k}\|}{L+\|H_{k}\|}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem

3
Abstract conditions and convergence analysis of the generic TR algorithm

Therefore, it suffices to analyse the case
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0
.
In Subsection
3.2
, we show that the liminf property holds whenever Condition
C.1
is satisfied and the update mechanism enforces uniform contraction on unsuccessful iterations (Condition
C.2
).
In Subsection
3.3
, convergence is established with respect to the weaker assumption AM
2.6
, under Condition
C.1
,
C.2
, and
C.3

C.3
to compensate for the weaker bound on the Hessian.

3.1
The three structural conditions

We now state formally the three conditions on the radius update rule that drive the convergence analysis.

Condition C.1
(Lower bound on the radius).

There exists a constant
0<\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}\leq 1
such that the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
satisfies the following lower bound for all
k\geq 0
,

\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{k}}\min_{0\leq i\leq k}\|g_{i}\|,

(3.1)

where
\{M_{k}\}
is a sequence of non-decreasing positive numbers that upper bound the model Hessian norm.

We choose
\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}\leq 1
without loss of generality. It simplifies formulas in proofs and discussions. Also, the sequence
\{M_{k}\}
can be defined in various ways. A natural choice is

M_{k}=L+\max_{0\leq i\leq k}\|H_{i}\|.

(3.2)

Condition C.2
(Contraction on unsuccessful iterations).

There exists
\underline{\gamma_{2}}\in(0\,,\,1)
and
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}>0
such that, for all

k
, if
\rho_{k}<\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}
, then

\Delta_{k+1}\;\leq\;\underline{\gamma_{2}}\,\Delta_{k}.

Condition C.3
(Bounded expansion on successful iterations).

There exists
\underline{\gamma_{2}}\in(0\,,\,1)
,
\overline{\gamma_{3}}>1
and
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{inc}}>0
such that, for all
k
,

(decrease)

if
\rho_{k}<\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{inc}}
, then
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\underline{\gamma_{2}}\,\Delta_{k}
;

(increase)

else
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{inc}}
, then
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\overline{\gamma_{3}}\,\Delta_{k}
.

Condition
C.1
prevents the radius from collapsing faster than the criticality measure. As long as the smallest gradient norm observed so far is bounded away from zero, so is
\Delta_{k}
.
Condition
C.2
forces a correction on the radius after a failed step.
Finally, Condition
C.3
caps the rate at which the radius can grow, preventing arbitrary jumps that would disconnect
\Delta_{k}
from the local geometry.
These three conditions define two classes of update rules.

Definition 3.1
(Weak and strong admissibility).

A trust-region radius update rule is said to be

1.

weakly admissible
if it satisfies Condition
C.1
and
C.2
;

2.

strongly admissible
if it satisfies Condition
C.1
and
C.3
.

Strong admissibility is the property used throughout the analysis. It yields global convergence under linearly growing model Hessians (Theorem
3.5
) and the optimal worst-case complexity
{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-2})
(Theorem
4.3
).
Weak admissibility is sufficient under the stronger assumption that
\|H_{k}\|
is uniformly bounded (Theorem
3.2
).

3.2
Liminf behaviour of the gradient norm under strong assumption

We now exhibit that under the assumptions that the Hessians are uniformly bounded (AM.
2
) and the lower bound condition
C.1
, along with the other standard assumptions, we can guarantee that the
\liminf
of the gradient norm goes to zero when
k\to\infty
under a condition on the update mechanism introduced below. It requires that the trust-region radius is uniformly contracted on unsuccessful iterations.

Theorem 3.2
(Lim inf of the gradient norm).

Suppose that Algorithm
1
is applied under Assumptions AF.
1
, AF.
2
, AM.
1
, AM.
2
, AA.
1
.
Assume furthermore that
\Delta_{k}
follows the lower bound condition
C.1
and the decrease condition
C.2
with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\geq\eta
, but
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\neq 0
.
Then, we have that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0,

Proof.

We analyse two cases:

1.

First, suppose the set
\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,:\,\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\right\}
is finite.
Then there exists an index
k_{0}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
\rho_{k}<\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}
.
Then, by Condition
C.2
and Condition
C.1
, we have for all
k\geq k_{0}
,

\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{L+\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\min_{0\leq i\leq k}\|g_{i}\|\leq\Delta_{k}\leq\underline{\gamma_{2}}^{k-k_{0}}\Delta_{k_{0}}\to 0

which implies that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0.

2.

Now, suppose that
\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\,:\,\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\right\}
is infinite.
By the Cauchy decrease condition AA.
1
, AM.
2
, and Condition
C.1
, we have that for all
k
such that
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}
,

f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2}\|g_{k}\|\min\left[\frac{\|g_{k}\|}{\|H_{k}\|}\,,\,\Delta_{k}\right]\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{2(L+\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}})}\min_{0\leq i\leq k}\|g_{i}\|^{2}.

Therefore, we have that

\frac{\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{2(L+\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}})}\sum_{\left\{k\,:\,\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{dec}}\right\}}\left(\min_{0\leq i\leq k}\|g_{i}\|^{2}\right)\leq\sum_{k}f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{0})-\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}<+\infty.

(3.3)

Then, we have that
\min_{0\leq i\leq k}\|g_{i}\|^{2}
goes to 0 as
k
grows to
+\infty
, which implies that,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0.

The proof is complete.

3.3
Liminf behaviour of the gradient norm under weaker assumption

We show that we can relax some assumptions on the Hessian but still guarantee that the
\liminf
of the gradient norm goes to zero when
k\to\infty
, provided the update mechanism satisfies an additional condition and the growth of the model Hessians remains moderate.
The following Lemma
3.3
, which can be found in
[
51
;
61
;
63
;
32
;
33
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{\Delta_{k}\}
and
\{M_{k}\}
be two sequences of real numbers such that
\Delta_{k}\geq\dfrac{\tau}{M_{k}}>0
, where
\tau>0
.
Let
J
be a subset of
{\mathbb{N}}
,
\underline{\gamma_{2}}<1
,
\overline{\gamma_{3}}>1
.
Assume furthermore that

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\gamma_{3}}\,\Delta_{k}\text{ for all }k\in J,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\underline{\gamma_{2}}\,\Delta_{k}\text{ for all }k\notin J,

\displaystyle M_{k+1}

\displaystyle\geq M_{k}\text{ for all }k\geq 0,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in J}\frac{1}{M_{k}}

\displaystyle<+\infty.

Then, we have that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M_{k}}<+\infty.

Proof.

See
Powell 1984
for a proof of the lemma.

The following discussion highlights the importance of condition (
C.3
) to ensure the liminf behaviour of the gradient norm. We present an example where all conditions in Lemma
3.3
are satisfied except for (
3.4
),
and the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M_{k}}
diverges.
Take
M_{k}=k
,
\tau=1
, some constant
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and define the sequence
\{\Delta_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
as

\begin{cases}\Delta_{k}=\dfrac{1}{k},&\text{if }k=\ell^{2}\text{ for some }\ell\geq 1,\\
\Delta_{k+p}=\dfrac{\gamma^{-2\ell-1+p}}{k+p}&\text{ for }p=1,2,\ldots,2\ell\end{cases}

This means that
\Delta_{k}
decreases by a factor of
\gamma
for
k
between two consecutive squares, and at the squares, it jumps to a much larger value, for example,

\Delta_{1}=1,\,\Delta_{2}=\frac{\gamma^{-2}}{2},\,\Delta_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}}{3},\,\Delta_{4}=\frac{1}{4},\,\Delta_{5}=\frac{\gamma^{-4}}{5},\,\Delta_{6}=\frac{\gamma^{-3}}{6},\,\Delta_{7}=\frac{\gamma^{-2}}{7},\,\Delta_{8}=\frac{\gamma^{-1}}{8},\,\Delta_{9}=\frac{1}{9},\ldots

Therefore, we see that all
M_{k}
are admissible, meaning that
\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{1}{M_{k}}
, for all
k\geq 1
.
Now consider the set
J=\{\ell^{2}\,:\,\ell\geq 1\}
. Then,

\sum_{k\in J}\frac{1}{M_{k}}=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}<+\infty.

We also have for all
k
,
M_{k+1}\geq M_{k}
, and for all
k\notin J
,
\Delta_{k+1}=\gamma\Delta_{k}
. But, we can’t define any constant
\overline{\gamma_{3}}>1
such that
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\overline{\gamma_{3}}\Delta_{k}
for all
k\in J
.
Indeed, for
k\in J
, i.e.
k=\ell^{2}
for some
\ell\geq 1
, we have that
\Delta_{k+1}=\dfrac{\gamma^{-2\ell}}{\ell^{2}+1}
and
\Delta_{k}=\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}
, which means that

\limsup_{k\to+\infty\,;\,k\in J}\frac{\Delta_{k+1}}{\Delta_{k}}=\limsup_{\ell\to+\infty}\frac{\gamma^{-2\ell}\,\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}+1}=+\infty.

Therefore, we have that the condition (
3.4
) is crucial in Lemma
3.3
to ensure that the sum
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M_{k}}
converges.

Moreover, when
\limsup_{k\to+\infty\,;\,k\in J}\frac{\Delta_{k+1}}{\Delta_{k}}<+\infty
, then we can find a constant
\overline{\gamma_{3}}>1
such that
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\overline{\gamma_{3}}\Delta_{k}
for all
k\in J
.
This leads to the following question.
Is condition (
3.4
) necessary, or can it be relaxed to
\limsup_{k\to+\infty\,;\,k\in J}\frac{\Delta_{k+1}}{\Delta_{k}}=+\infty
with some specific growth?
Also, is it sufficient to require that the sequence
\{\Delta_{k}\}
is bounded above? Which is a standard condition in mathematical programming, i.e.
\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{\max}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Remark 5

Weak admissibility does not suffice for the complexity bound. Without Condition

5
Retrospective trust-region under linearly growing Hessians

The retrospective trust-region method, introduced by

At iteration

centered at the current iterate

\tilde{\rho}_{k}\;=\;\frac{f(x_{k-1})-f(x_{k})}{m_{k}(x_{k-1})-m_{k}(x_{k})}\,,

\rho_{k-1}

\theta_{k-1}

\tilde{\eta}_{1},\tilde{\eta}_{2}\in(0,1)

Bastin et al. 2010

First, we prove that the retrospective update is strongly admissible: it satisfies Conditions

C.3

6.1
Some useful assumptions on the subproblem solution

Before establishing the lower bound on the trust-region radius, we first provide some additional conditions on the subproblem solution that will be useful in proofs involving the step length
\|s_{k}\|
, such as the one proposed at the end of subsection

and in subsection

When conditions on the step are necessary, we will assume that the trust-region subproblem is solved using an iterative method, that satisfies conditions inspired from the truncated CG method
[
53

.
We consider an early termination strategy for solving iteratively the (quasi-)Newton equation

H_{k}s=-g_{k}
, as outlined in
Nocedal and Wright 2006
.
This approach is based on monitoring the

r_{k}
, defined as
H_{k}s_{k}=-g_{k}+r_{k}

where
s_{k}
represents the inexact (quasi-)Newton step.

Condition C.4
.

When the trust-region is inactive, i.e.
\|s_{k}\|<\Delta_{k}
, then the procedure is terminated with the residual satisfying

\|r_{k}\|\leq\xi_{k}\|g_{k}\|,

(6.1)

where
\{\xi_{k}\}
is referred to as the forcing sequence, and
0<\xi_{k}<\xi
for all
k
, with
\xi\in(0,1)
.

The forcing sequence
\{\xi_{k}\}
is a sequence of positive numbers that need to be strictly less than 1 to discard zero as a possible solution.

Assumption, AA 2
.

The trust-region subproblem (
2.7
) is solved by
an iterative method that produces a step
s_{k}
satisfying condition
C.4
.

It is useful to characterise how the step length
\|s_{k}\|
relates to the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
and to the gradient norm when the subproblem is solved approximately.
The following proposition provides this relationship under Assumption AA.
2
.

Prop. 6.1
.

When the trust-region subproblem is solved using an iterative method satisfying Assumption AA.
2
, then the step
s_{k}
satisfy the following inequalities

\min\left\{\Delta_{k},\dfrac{1-\xi}{\|H_{k}\|}\|g_{k}\|\right\}\leq\|s_{k}\|\leq\Delta_{k},

(6.2)

where we have used the same convention as in Theorem
2.1
, i.e., for
\|H_{k}\|=0
, we set
\frac{1-\xi}{\|H_{k}\|}=+\infty

Proof.

When the Hessian is zero, the model is linear and the step is obtained at a point on the boundary of the trust region, i.e.
\|s_{k}\|=\Delta_{k}
.
When
H_{k}\neq 0
, we have
\|s_{k}\|\leq\Delta_{k}
by definition of the trust-region subproblem.
If the trust-region is inactive, i.e.
\|s_{k}\|<\Delta_{k}

, we have, using the triangular inequality, (

) and the matrix norm induced by

\|g_{k}\|\leq\|H_{k}s_{k}+g_{k}\|+\|H_{k}s_{k}\|\leq\|r_{k}\|+\|H_{k}\|\|s_{k}\|,

\|s_{k}\|\geq\dfrac{1-\xi}{\|H_{k}\|}\|g_{k}\|.

By imposing in the subproblem that the step

1

6.2

The most common and simplest trust-region mechanism is the
incremental
update, in which the radius is scaled by fixed multiplicative factors from one iteration to the next.
This rule embodies the standard philosophy of trust-region methods: shrink the radius after poor model agreement, maintain or cautiously expand it under moderate agreement, and enlarge it aggressively after very good agreement.
This approach is straightforward to implement, has been widely adopted in practice due to its simplicity and effectiveness, and has been widely studied in the literature
[

;
46
;
41
;
14

15

Update Rule 1

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}[\gamma_{1}\Delta_{k},\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k}),&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1},\\
[\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k},\Delta_{k}),&\text{if }\eta_{1}\leq\rho_{k}<\eta_{2},\\
[\Delta_{k},+\infty),&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2}.\end{cases}

(6.4)

where
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}<1
and
\eta\leq\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{2}<1

\eta_{1}\neq 0

When necessary for the analysis, we can impose the following condition on the update mechanism when

\Delta_{k+1}\geq\gamma_{1}\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\,(1-\eta_{2})}{M_{k}}\,\min_{0\leq j\leq k}\|g_{j}\|\geq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{k+1}}\,\min_{0\leq j\leq k+1}\|g_{j}\|,

In both cases the claim holds at

Conn et al. 2000
propose an aggressive update that contracts the radius sharply when the model fails. This prevents overly large radii from producing steps that minimise the model rather than the actual objective function.
The motivation is that when the computed step
\|s_{k}\|
is significantly smaller than the current trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
, the ratio
\rho_{k}
evaluates the model only within a ball of radius
\|s_{k}\|
, not the prescribed
\Delta_{k}
.
If
\rho_{k}
is small or negative (i.e.,
\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}
), several reductions of
\Delta_{k}
may be needed before excluding
x_{k}+s_{k}
, each costing an extra function evaluation. To mitigate this inefficiency, they propose the following more flexible update rule.

Update Rule 2
.

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}[\gamma_{1}\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{2}\|s_{k}\|)&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1},\\
[\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k},\Delta_{k})&\text{if }\rho_{k}\in[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\\
[\Delta_{k},+\infty)&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2},\end{cases}

where
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}\leq 1
.

Remark 8
.

The update rule
2
can be seen as a special case of the update rule
1
with carefully chosen parameters. See
Conn et al. 2000
for more details.

Example 2
(Variant analysed in
Gould et al. 2005
).

A commonly used variant, valued for its simplicity and effectiveness in adjusting the trust-region radius according to the quality of the trial step, is:

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}\gamma_{1}\|s_{k}\|,&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1},\\[3.99994pt]
\Delta_{k},&\text{if }\rho_{k}\in[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\\[3.99994pt]
\max\{\gamma_{3}\|s_{k}\|,\Delta_{k}\},&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2},\end{cases}

where

Typical parameter choices in the literature are

,
\gamma_{3}=2
,
\eta_{1}=0.25
, and
\eta_{2}=0.75

Finally,
Conn et al. 2000
propose a heuristic to handle cases where the model is a poor approximation of the objective, especially when
\rho_{k}<0

The strategy rescales the step so that the ratio is more likely to be classified as very successful in the next iteration.
Let
\phi_{f}(t):=f(x_{k}+ts_{k})
and
\phi_{m}(t):=m_{k}(x_{k}+ts_{k})
for

\phi_{f}^{\mathrm{quad}}(t)\;=\;f_{k}\;+\;(g_{k}^{\top}s_{k})\,t\;+\;a_{f}\,t^{2},\qquad a_{f}\;=\;f(x_{k}+s_{k})-f_{k}-g_{k}^{\top}s_{k}.

Considering a similar quadratic interpolant for the model, we find
t>0
such that
\frac{\phi_{f}^{\mathrm{quad}}(t)-\phi_{f}^{\mathrm{quad}}(0)}{\phi_{m}^{\mathrm{quad}}(t)-\phi_{m}^{\mathrm{quad}}(0)}=\eta_{2}

Then,

\theta_{k}=\frac{(1-\eta_{2})\,\nabla f(x_{k})^{\top}s_{k}}{(1-\eta_{2})\bigl[f(x_{k})+\nabla f(x_{k})^{\top}s_{k}\bigr]+\eta_{2}m_{k}(x_{k}+s_{k})-f(x_{k}+s_{k})}

(6.6)

The trust-region radius is then updated as follows,
\Delta_{k+1}=\min\Bigl[\,\gamma_{1}\,\|s_{k}\|,\;\max\bigl[\gamma_{0},\,\theta_{k}\bigr]\,\Delta_{k}\Bigr]
, where
0<\gamma_{0}<\gamma_{1}
.

\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}=\min\left\{1\,,\,\frac{\Delta_{0}\,M_{0}}{\|g_{0}\|}\,,\,\gamma_{0}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\,(1-\eta_{2})\,,\,1-\xi\right\}.

(6.7)

Proof.

This can be obtained by using Proposition

6.3
Step-driven update

The trust-region radius can also be updated based on the step length
\|s_{k}\|
.
This strategy adapts more directly to the behaviour of the iterates: it decreases the radius aggressively when the model proves inaccurate and increases it more conservatively when the model is reliable.
By tying the radius to the actual step taken, this approach naturally drives
\Delta_{k}
toward zero in the limit when the iterates converge to a stationary point.

Conn et al. 2000
and
Powell 1970
;
Powell 1975
;
Powell 1984
define an update rule of the form,

Update Rule 3
.

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}[\gamma_{1}\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{2}\|s_{k}\|)&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1},\\
[\gamma_{2}\|s_{k}\|,\|s_{k}\|)&\text{if }\rho_{k}\in[\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\\
[\|s_{k}\|,+\infty)&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2},\end{cases}

(6.8)

where
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}<1
and
\eta\leq\eta_{1}\leq\eta_{2}<1
with
\eta_{1}\neq 0
.

Example 3
.

Powell 1975
;
Powell 1984
define with
0<\gamma_{1}\leq\gamma_{2}<1<\gamma_{3}
and

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}[\gamma_{1}\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{2}\|s_{k}\|],&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1},\\
[\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{3}\|s_{k}\|],&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}\\
\end{cases}

In practice, we observe that the ratio
\rho_{k}
can vary significantly across iterations. This variability can be attributed to several factors, including the choice of model, the trust-region radius, and the nature of the objective function.

Hei 2003
proposed an update strategy where the trust-region radius is proportional to the step length
\|s_{k}\|
, scaled by an adaptive factor
R_{\eta_{1}}(\rho_{k})
that depends on the ratio
\rho_{k}

This rule adjusts the radius more flexibly according to the quality of the step, especially when the model is a poor approximation of the function.
The update rule is given by:

Update Rule 4
.

\Delta_{k+1}=R_{\eta_{1}}(\rho_{k})\|s_{k}\|,

where
R_{\eta_{1}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a
non-decreasing
function satisfying, with
0\leq\eta<\eta_{1}<1
and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to-\infty}R_{\eta_{1}}(t)=\gamma_{1};

\displaystyle\forall\,t<\eta,\,R_{\eta_{1}}(t)\leq\gamma_{2};

\displaystyle R_{\eta_{1}}(\eta_{1})=1+\gamma_{2};

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}R_{\eta_{1}}(t)=\gamma_{3},\text{ where }\gamma_{3}>1+\gamma_{2}.

Remark 9
.

This update rule fits in the general framework of update rule

with
\gamma_{1}=R_{\eta_{1}}(-\infty)
,
\gamma_{2}=R_{\eta_{1}}(\eta_{1})
, and
\gamma_{3}=R_{\eta_{1}}(+\infty)

\Delta_{k+1}\in\begin{cases}[\gamma_{1}\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{2}\|s_{k}\|)&\text{if }\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}\\
[(1+\gamma_{2})\|s_{k}\|,\gamma_{3}\|s_{k}\|)&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}\end{cases}
.

Prop. 6.4
.

Assume the subproblem satisfies Assumption AA.

Proof.

The proof can be obtained by using Proposition

Base case.
For

\Delta_{0}\geq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{0}}\,\|g_{0}\|
by definition of

Induction step.
Suppose the result holds for some

6.4
Criticality-anchored update

6.4.1
Inspiration and motivation

In
Derivative-Free Optimisation
(DFO)
[
19
]
, where the gradient is not available or is unreliable, the model is typically constructed using function evaluations only. In this context, the trust-region radius update mechanism is crucial to ensure that the model remains a valid approximation of the objective function. A large
\Delta_{k}
may lead to steps that are too aggressive and not reflective of the local curvature. A
criticality step
is introduced to guarantee that the sequence of iterates
\{x_{k}\}
approaches first-order critical points.
Near a stationary point, the gradient norm
\|g_{k}\|
becomes small, therefore the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
must also shrink appropriately. This can be done by introducing a criticality measure that quantifies how close the current iterate is to a stationary point, and the trust-region radius is forced to satisfy:

\Delta_{k}\leq\zeta\,\chi_{k}^{\alpha},\quad\text{for some }\zeta>0,\,\alpha\in(0,1],

(6.9)

where
\chi_{k}
be a measure of criticality (e.g.,
\chi_{k}=\|g_{k}\|
).
Then, the criticality step ensures that the trust-region radius shrinks appropriately as the iterates approach critical points, allowing theoretical proofs of convergence to stationary points, even in the absence of gradient or Hessian information.
This DFO-inspired thread has produced unified convergence frameworks across trust-region and line-search methods, in both deterministic and stochastic settings
[
16
;
20
;
4
]
.
Their work fits in the general framework presented in Algorithm
4
where the role of
\alpha_{k}
is to control the length of the step
s_{k}
.
For example, in the context of line-search methods,
\alpha_{k}
can be seen as the step length along the search direction, and the model
m_{k}
can be defined as a linear approximation of
f
around
x_{k}
. And, in the context of trust-region methods,
\alpha_{k}
is seen as the trust-region radius, and the model
m_{k}
can be a quadratic approximation of
f
around
x_{k}
.

Step 0: Initialisation.

Choose constants
\eta\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in(1,\infty)
, and
\alpha_{\max}\in(0,\infty)
.

Choose an initial iterate
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and step-size parameter
\alpha_{0}\in(0,\alpha_{\max}]
.

Step 1: Determine model and compute step.

Choose a local model
m_{k}
of
f
around
x_{k}
.
Compute a step
s_{k}(\alpha_{k})
such that the model reduction

m_{k}(x_{k})-m_{k}(x_{k}+s_{k}(\alpha_{k}))\geq 0

is sufficiently large.

Step 2: Check for sufficient reduction in
f
.

Check whether the reduction

f(x_{k})-f(x_{k}+s_{k}(\alpha_{k}))

is sufficiently large relative to the model reduction

m_{k}(x_{k})-m_{k}(x_{k}+s_{k}(\alpha_{k}))

using a condition parameterised by
\eta
.

Step 3: Successful iteration.

If sufficient reduction has been attained (along with other potential requirements), set

x_{k+1}\leftarrow x_{k}+s_{k}(\alpha_{k})
, and

\alpha_{k+1}\leftarrow\min\{\gamma\alpha_{k},\,\alpha_{\max}\}.

Step 4: Unsuccessful iteration.

Otherwise, set

x_{k+1}\leftarrow x_{k}
, and

\alpha_{k+1}\leftarrow\gamma^{-1}\alpha_{k}.

Step 5: Next iteration.

Set
k\leftarrow k+1
.

Algorithm 4

Adaptive deterministic framework
[
20
]
.

To be comparable to a line-search, the update mechanism needs to be more conservative. The acceptance of the step is based on the ratio
\rho_{k}
and the radius being small relative to the gradient norm.
The update rule can be summarised as follows with
\gamma>1
and
\zeta>0
,

(x_{k+1},\Delta_{k+1})=\begin{cases}\bigl(x_{k}+s_{k},\;\gamma\Delta_{k}\bigr),&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}\text{ and }\Delta_{k}\leq\zeta\|g_{k}\|,\\[6.0pt]
\bigl(x_{k},\;\gamma^{-1}\Delta_{k}\bigr),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Gratton et al. 2017
and
Bandeira et al. 2014
propose a similar update strategy, but the acceptance of the step and the update of the radius are decoupled, which is closer to the framework we propose in Algorithm
1
.
If
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
, set
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+s_{k}
and

\Delta_{k+1}=\begin{cases}\min\{\gamma_{3}\Delta_{k},\;\Delta_{\max}\},&\text{if }\Delta_{k}\leq\zeta\|g_{k}\|,\\[6.0pt]
\gamma_{1}\Delta_{k},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Otherwise, set
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
and
\Delta_{k+1}=\gamma_{1}\Delta_{k}
.

In the following, we present some variants of such update mechanisms which fit in our framework and we show that they satisfy the lower bound condition on the trust-region radius.

We present an update mechanism inspired from

Again, it is possible to modify the update with

Proof.

The proof is done by induction on

Induction step.
Suppose the result holds for some

\Delta_{k+1}\geq\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{k+1}}\,\min_{0\leq j\leq k+1}\|g_{j}\|.

6.5
Gradient-scaled update

Yuan and Fan 2001
propose a mechanism to control the trust-region radius based on the gradient norm. It ties the radius to the norm of the gradient, allowing for a more adaptive approach to trust-region management and adapting the radius to the local geometry of the objective function.
This approach naturally contracts the radius when the gradient is small and expands it when the gradient is large, providing a responsive mechanism for trust-region management.
The update rule is defined as follows:

.

Let

[\mu_{k},+\infty),&\text{if }\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2}\end{cases}

For all
k\geq 0
, the lower bounds on the trust-region parameter
\mu_{k}
and the trust-region radius
\Delta_{k}
are similar to Proposition
6.6
,
where

\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}=\min\left\{1\,;\,\mu_{0}\,M_{0}\,;\,\underline{\mu}\,M_{0}\,;\,\gamma_{1}\,(1-\eta_{1})\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\right\}.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of proposition
6.6
.
We only need to adapt the case when
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
, then

\mu_{k+1}\geq\underline{\mu}\geq\underline{\mu}\,\frac{M_{0}}{M_{k+1}}\left(\frac{M_{k+1}}{M_{0}}\right)\geq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{M_{k+1}}\quad\text{because}\quad\frac{M_{k+1}}{M_{0}}\geq 1.

Remark 12
.

Update rule
6
or
7
, a priori don’t satisfy Condition
C.3
.
However, in most convergence analysis, such as in
[
22
]
, the model Hessian is assumed uniformly bounded, which only needs Condition
C.2
to establish
\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left\lVert g_{k}\right\rVert=0
.

For the update rule
6
, the convergence analysis can be extended
to the weaker Hessian growth assumption AM.
2.6
by controlling
the expansion of the gradient coefficient
\mu_{k}
. This is achieved by imposing a uniform bound
on
\mu_{k}
across iterations. In particular, when
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2}
, we require

\mu_{k+1}\leq\min\{\gamma_{3}\mu_{k},\;\overline{\mu}\},

for some constant
\overline{\mu}>0
.
The following proposition shows how this update mechanism offers some control over the gradient norm across iterations.

Prop. 6.8
.

Assume Lipschitz continuous gradients (AF.
2
), and that there exists a constant
\overline{\mu}>0
such that for all
k\geq 0
,
\mu_{k}\leq\overline{\mu}
.
Then
\|g_{k+1}\|\leq(L\overline{\mu}+1)\|g_{k}\|
.

Proof.

\|g_{k+1}\|\leq L\|s_{k}\|+\|g_{k}\|\leq L\Delta_{k}+\|g_{k}\|=L\mu_{k}\|g_{k}\|+\|g_{k}\|=(L\mu_{k}+1)\|g_{k}\|\leq(L\overline{\mu}+1)\|g_{k}\|.

Prop. 6.9
.

Assume Lipschitz continuous gradients (AF.
2
) and consider update rule
6
where
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{2}
implies
\mu_{k+1}\in[\mu_{k}\,;\,\min\{\gamma_{3}\mu_{k},\;\overline{\mu}\}]
,
(\gamma_{3}>1)
, and
\gamma_{2}\,(L\overline{\mu}+1)<1
.
Then, Condition
C.3
holds with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{inc}}=\eta_{1}
,

\overline{\gamma_{3}}=\gamma_{3}(L\overline{\mu}+1)
and
\underline{\gamma_{2}}=\gamma_{2}(L\overline{\mu}+1)
.

Proof.

We use Prop.
6.8
to control the evolution of the gradient norm across iterations.
When
\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}
, we have
\mu_{k+1}\in[\gamma_{1}\mu_{k},\gamma_{2}\mu_{k})
. Then,

\Delta_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\|g_{k+1}\|\leq\gamma_{2}\mu_{k}(L\overline{\mu}+1)\|g_{k}\|=\underline{\gamma_{2}}\Delta_{k},

where
\underline{\gamma_{2}}=\gamma_{2}(L\overline{\mu}+1)<1
.

Otherwise, if
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
,

\Delta_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\|g_{k+1}\|\leq\gamma_{3}\mu_{k}(L\overline{\mu}+1)\|g_{k}\|=\overline{\gamma_{3}}\Delta_{k},

where
\overline{\gamma_{3}}=\gamma_{3}(L\overline{\mu}+1)>1
.

Remark 13
.

In Proposition
6.9
, we consider the case
\eta<\eta_{1}
with a strong condition on
\gamma_{2}
to decouple the acceptance of the step and the update of the radius.
However, the Lipschitz constant of the gradient is often unknown and the
\gamma_{2}
condition is quite strong, imposing a very conservative decrease of the radius.

In practice, it is easier to consider
\eta>0
.
This implies that when

\rho_{k}<\eta
, we have
\mu_{k+1}\in[\gamma_{1}\mu_{k},\gamma_{2}\mu_{k})
and
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
. Then,

\Delta_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\|g_{k+1}\|\leq\gamma_{2}\mu_{k}\|g_{k}\|\leq\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k}
.
Then, we consider
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{inc}}=\eta>0
to satisfy Condition
C.3
.

Finally, an important feature of the gradient relative coefficient update rule is that it provides a lower bound on the trust-region parameter
\mu_{k}
that does not depend on the optimality threshold
\varepsilon
.

Therefore, by adapting the argument from the second part of the proof of Theorem
4.3
, we obtain
\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}
, for
k\in\mathcal{S}
,
\gamma_{3}^{-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{k+1}}
, and when
k\in\mathcal{U}
,
\gamma_{2}^{-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{k+1}}
.
Therefore, we get
\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\gamma_{2}^{-|\mathcal{U}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|}\gamma_{3}^{-|\mathcal{S}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|}\leq\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}
, and

\ln\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}}\right)|\mathcal{U}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|\leq\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{0}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}\right)+\ln\left(\gamma_{3}\right)|\mathcal{S}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|

(6.12)

and, therefore, we get the following upper bound on unsuccessful iterations,

|\mathcal{U}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|\leq\dfrac{1}{|\ln\gamma_{2}|}\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{0}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}\right)+\dfrac{\ln\gamma_{3}}{|\ln\gamma_{2}|}|\mathcal{S}_{T_{\varepsilon}-1}|\quad\in{\mathcal{O}}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)

(6.13)

where we can see that the logarithmic term does not depend on
\varepsilon^{-1}
.

7
Discussion on a recent iteration complexity analysis framework

The complexity bound of Theorem
4.3
uses C1–C3 and the Cauchy decrease assumption directly.
A complementary route, developed by
Curtis and Scheinberg 2020
and
Wang and Yuan 2022
, replaces these arguments with a single Lyapunov function that decreases on every iteration. The argument is conceptually simpler when applicable, and yields tighter bounds in the convex and strongly convex cases.

This template provides an alternative complexity argument for two of our admissible mechanisms. We apply it to the criticality-anchored update of Section
6.4.2
. We generalise
Curtis and Scheinberg 2020
in two ways: we use three distinct scaling coefficients
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
(with a corresponding adjustment to
\nu
), and we decouple step acceptance from the radius update to handle
\eta=0
.
We then specialise the stochastic analysis of
Wang and Yuan 2022
to the deterministic gradient-scaled update of Section
6.5
, deriving complexity bounds in the non-convex, convex, and strongly convex cases.

7.1
Unifying framework from Line-Search to Trust-Region Methods

Throughout this section,
T_{\varepsilon}
denotes the first iteration satisfying the convergence criterion of interest, parametrised by
\varepsilon
.

Condition C.5
.

The statements hold with respect to
\big\{(\Phi_{k},\alpha_{k})\big\}
and
T_{\varepsilon}
:

1.

There exists a scalar
\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}\in(0;\infty)
such that,
when
\gamma_{2}\alpha_{k}\leq\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}
then the iteration is successful, and consequently we have for all
k\geq 0
,
\alpha_{k}\geq\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon}
.

2.

There exists a nondecreasing function
h_{\varepsilon}:(0;\infty)\rightarrow(0;\infty)
, and a scalar
\Theta>0
such that for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\alpha_{k}).

(7.1)

where
\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\geq 0
is a sequence whose role is to measure the progress of the algorithm.

Theorem 7.1
.

Under condition (
C.5
), the stopping time
T_{\varepsilon}
is finite and satisfies
\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}\leq\dfrac{\Phi_{0}}{\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}
.

Proof.

For all
k<T_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\alpha_{k})
. Therefore, remembering
\Phi_{k}\geq 0
for all
k
,

\Phi_{0}\geq\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{T_{\varepsilon}}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{T_{\varepsilon}-1}\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{T_{\varepsilon}-1}\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\varepsilon}\leq\dfrac{\Phi_{0}}{\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}.

When the per-iteration decrease degrades as
1/M_{k}
, Theorem
7.1
extends as follows.

Corollary 7.2
.

When considering that
\sum_{k}\dfrac{1}{M_{k}}=\infty
, if we assume that condition
C.5
(2) is replaced by:

(2’) There exists a nondecreasing function
h_{\varepsilon}:(0;\infty)\rightarrow(0;\infty)
, and a scalar
\Theta>0
such that for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta\dfrac{h_{\varepsilon}(\alpha_{k})}{M_{k}},

(7.2)

where
M_{k}
is defined in (
3.2
).
Then, the stopping time
T_{\varepsilon}
is finite and satisfies
\displaystyle T_{\varepsilon}\leq\dfrac{M_{T_{\varepsilon}}\,\Phi_{0}}{\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as the proof of Theorem
7.1
, by considering that for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta\dfrac{h_{\varepsilon}(\alpha_{k})}{M_{k}}\geq\Theta\dfrac{h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}{M_{T_{\varepsilon}}}
. Then, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\Phi_{0}\geq\Phi_{0}-\Phi_{N}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Theta\dfrac{h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}{M_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\geq\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\dfrac{1}{M_{k}}.

Therefore, considering
N^{+}
to be the largest integer such that
\sum_{k=0}^{N^{+}-1}\dfrac{1}{M_{k}}\leq\dfrac{\Phi_{0}}{\Theta h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\alpha}_{\varepsilon})}
, we have
T_{\varepsilon}\leq N^{+}<\infty
.

7.2
Application to DFO-Like update mechanism

We show that the criticality-anchored update of Section
6.4.2
satisfies Condition
C.5
with the progress metric of
Curtis and Scheinberg 2020
, generalised to three scaling coefficients. For
\nu\in(0,1)
, define

\Phi_{k}=\nu(f(x_{k})-\kappa_{lbf})+(1-\nu)\Delta_{k}^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{\varepsilon}=\inf\big\{k\geq 0,\;\|g(x_{k})\|<\varepsilon\big\}.

(7.3)

In the following, we will show that the conditions of Theorem
7.1
are satisfied for the update mechanisms presented in subsection
6.4.2
. We adapt the work from
Curtis and Scheinberg 2020
to our setting where we consider multiple scaling coefficients
\gamma_{1,2,3}
.

Prop. 7.3
.

There exists
\nu\in(0,1)
such that

\nu\,\eta_{1}\,\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\zeta}\,\min\!\left(\frac{1}{\zeta\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}},1\right)\;\geq\;(1-\nu)\big(\gamma_{3}^{2}-\gamma_{2}^{2}\big).

(7.4)

For any such
\nu
, Condition
C.5
holds with
\alpha_{k}=\Delta_{k}
,
h_{\varepsilon}(x)=x^{2}
, and
\Theta=(1-\nu)(1-\gamma_{2}^{2})
. Consequently,

T_{\varepsilon}\;\leq\;\frac{\nu\big(f(x_{0})-\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}\big)+(1-\nu)\Delta_{0}^{2}}{(1-\nu)(1-\gamma_{2}^{2})\,\underline{\Delta}_{\varepsilon}^{2}},\quad\text{where }\underline{\Delta}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\,\varepsilon.

Proof.

For
k<T_{\varepsilon}
, we have
\|g_{k}\|\geq\varepsilon
. We show
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta\,\Delta_{k}^{2}
in two cases.

Case 1:
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
.
The Cauchy decrease (AA.
1
) gives

f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\;\geq\;\eta_{1}\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2}\,\|g_{k}\|\min\!\left\{\Delta_{k},\;\frac{\|g_{k}\|}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\right\}.

Subcase 1a:
\Delta_{k}\leq\zeta\|g_{k}\|
.
Then
f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\geq\eta_{1}\,c\,\Delta_{k}^{2}
with
c=\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\zeta}\,\min\!\big(1,\frac{1}{\zeta\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\big)
. Since
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\gamma_{3}\Delta_{k}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\big(\nu\,\eta_{1}\,c-(1-\nu)(\gamma_{3}^{2}-1)\big)\,\Delta_{k}^{2}\;\geq\;(1-\nu)(1-\gamma_{2}^{2})\,\Delta_{k}^{2},

where the last inequality uses (
7.4
).

Subcase 1b:
\Delta_{k}>\zeta\|g_{k}\|
.
Here
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k}
, and by monotonic decrease of
f(x_{k})
, we have

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}=\nu\left(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\right)+(1-\nu)\left(\Delta_{k}^{2}-\Delta_{k+1}^{2}\right)\geq(1-\nu)\big(1-\gamma_{2}^{2}\big)\Delta_{k}^{2}.

Case 2:
\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}
.
Again,
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\gamma_{2}\Delta_{k}
and argument is similar to Subcase 1b.

In all cases,
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta\,\Delta_{k}^{2}
with
\Theta=(1-\nu)(1-\gamma_{2}^{2})
. The complexity bound follows from Theorem
7.1
.

7.3
Application to the gradient-scaled update

We turn to the gradient-scaled update of Section
6.5
, where
\Delta_{k}=\mu_{k}\|g_{k}\|
where
\mu_{k}\leq\overline{\mu}
with appropriate adjustments to the update rule.
By Remark
13
, the acceptance threshold satisfies
\eta_{1}>\eta
(
\eta_{1}\neq 0
), with the strong condition
\gamma_{2}(L\overline{\mu}+1)<1
. This condition could be relaxed by considering
\eta>0
.

The analysis below specialises
Wang and Yuan 2022
to the deterministic case and treats the non-convex, convex, and strongly convex regimes in turn. A common condition on
\nu
underlies all three.

Condition C.6
.

We choose
\nu\in(0\,;\,1)
sufficiently close to
1
such that

\nu\,\eta_{1}\,\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\,\overline{\mu}^{2}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\geq(1-\nu)\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\left[\gamma_{3}^{2}-\gamma_{2}^{2}\right].

7.3.1
Non-convex case

Prop. 7.4
.

Consider
\Phi_{k}
and
T_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
7.3
).
Under Condition
C.6
, for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\Theta\,\Delta_{k}^{2},\quad\text{where }\Theta=(1-\nu)\Big(1-(L\overline{\mu}+1)^{2}\,\gamma_{2}^{2}\Big)>0.

Hence Condition
C.5
holds with
\alpha_{k}=\Delta_{k}
,
h_{\varepsilon}(x)=x^{2}
, and

T_{\varepsilon}\leq\dfrac{\Phi_{0}}{\Theta\,h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\Delta}_{\varepsilon})}\leq\dfrac{\nu(f(x_{0})-\kappa_{lbf})+(1-\nu)\Delta_{0}^{2}}{(1-\nu)\big(1-\gamma_{2}^{2}\big)\,h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\Delta}_{\varepsilon})}\;\in{\mathcal{O}}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)

(7.5)

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition
6.8
,
\left\lVert g_{k+1}\right\rVert\leq\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)\|g_{k}\|
.

Case 1:
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
.
The Cauchy decrease gives

f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\geq\eta_{1}\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2}\frac{\Delta_{k}}{\overline{\mu}}\,\min\left(\Delta_{k}\,;\,\frac{\Delta_{k}}{\overline{\mu}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\right)\geq\eta_{1}\,c\,\Delta_{k}^{2},\quad\text{ where }c=\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\,\overline{\mu}}\,\min\left(1\,;\,\frac{1}{\overline{\mu}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\right)=\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\,\overline{\mu}^{2}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}.

Since
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\gamma_{3}(L\overline{\mu}+1)\Delta_{k}
,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\Big(\nu\,\eta_{1}\,c-(1-\nu)\big[\gamma_{3}^{2}(L\overline{\mu}+1)^{2}-1\big]\Big)\Delta_{k}^{2}\;\geq\;(1-\nu)\Big(1-(L\overline{\mu}+1)^{2}\,\gamma_{2}^{2}\Big)\Delta_{k}^{2}.

Condition
C.6
ensures the last inequality.

Case 2:
\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}
.
Then
\Delta_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\|g_{k+1}\|\leq\gamma_{2}(L\overline{\mu}+1)\Delta_{k}
, hence

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}=(1-\nu)(\Delta_{k}^{2}-\Delta_{k+1}^{2})\geq\Theta\,\Delta_{k}^{2}.

Therefore, in both cases,
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq\Theta\Delta_{k}^{2}
, where
\Theta=(1-\nu)\left(1-\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)>0
, and condition
C.5
is satisfied with
h_{\varepsilon}(x)=x^{2}
, and
\alpha_{k}=\Delta_{k}
. The rest follows from Theorem
7.1
.

Remark 14
.

If we consider
\eta>0
, the case distinction can be made on
\rho_{k}<\eta
and
\rho_{k}\geq\eta
, and the same complexity result is obtained with a better constant
\Theta=(1-\nu)\left(1-\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)
and no restriction on
\gamma_{2}
.
For this, we choose
\nu
such that

\nu\,\eta\,\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\,\overline{\mu}^{2}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\geq(1-\nu)\left[\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{3}^{2}-\gamma_{2}^{2}\right].

Then, for
\rho_{k}<\eta
, we have
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
, and

\Delta_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1}\|g_{k+1}\|\leq\gamma_{2}\,\mu_{k}\,\|g_{k}\|=\gamma_{2}\,\Delta_{k}
, and therefore,
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}=(1-\nu)\left(\Delta_{k}^{2}-\Delta_{k+1}^{2}\right)\geq(1-\nu)(1-\gamma_{2}^{2})\Delta_{k}^{2}
.

7.3.2
Convex cases

Assume
f
is convex with bounded level set
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\{x:f(x)\leq f(x_{0})\}
of diameter
D_{0}=\max_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}\|x-y\|
and
G=\max_{x\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}\|\nabla f(x)\|
.
Following
Wang and Yuan 2022
, the stopping time is

T_{\varepsilon}^{f}=\inf\big\{k\geq 0:f(x_{k})-f^{\star}<\varepsilon\big\},

(7.6)

where
f^{\star}=\min_{x}f(x)
. The progress metric is defined as

\Psi_{k}=\frac{1}{\nu\,\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\Phi_{k}},\quad\Phi_{k}=\nu(f(x_{k})-f^{\star})+(1-\nu)\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2},\quad\nu\in(0\,;\,1).

(7.7)

Convexity and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give the following upper bound on the optimality gap. For all
x\in{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
,

f(x)-f^{\star}\leq\nabla f(x)^{\top}(x-x^{\star})\leq\|\nabla f(x)\|\,\|x-x^{\star}\|\leq D_{0}\,\|\nabla f(x)\|.

(7.8)

Prop. 7.5
.

Consider
\Psi_{k}
and
T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
respectively defined in (
7.7
) and (
7.6
).
Under Condition
C.6
, for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
,

\Psi_{k}-\Psi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\frac{\Theta}{\omega^{2}}\,\mu_{k},\quad\omega=\nu D_{0}+(1-\nu)\overline{\mu}\,G,\quad\Theta=(1-\nu)\big(1-(L\overline{\mu}+1)^{2}\,\gamma_{2}^{2}\big).

Condition
C.5
holds with
\alpha_{k}=\mu_{k}
,
h_{\varepsilon}(x)=x
, and

T_{\varepsilon}^{f}\leq\dfrac{\Psi_{0}\,\omega^{2}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}{(1-\nu)\left(1-\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}}\;\in{\mathcal{O}}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right).

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition
6.8
,
\left\lVert g_{k+1}\right\rVert\leq\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)\|g_{k}\|
.

Case 1:
\rho_{k}\geq\eta_{1}
.
By the Cauchy decrease (AA.
1
),
\mu_{k+1}\leq\gamma_{3}\,\mu_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nu(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}))+(1-\nu)\left(\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}-\mu_{k+1}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\nu\,\eta\,\frac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\,\overline{\mu}^{2}\,\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\,\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}+(1-\nu)\left(1-\gamma_{3}^{2}\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\right)\,\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\nu)\left(1-\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}

where the last inequality uses Condition
C.6
.

Case 2:
\rho_{k}<\eta_{1}
.
Then,
\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq(1-\nu)\left(1-\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}
.

Combining with (
7.8
), with
\omega=\nu D_{0}+(1-\nu)\overline{\mu}G
,

\Phi_{k}\leq\nu D_{0}\,\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|+(1-\nu)\overline{\mu}\,\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\leq\omega\,\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|\quad\text{hence}\quad\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\frac{\Theta}{\omega^{2}}\,\mu_{k}\,\Phi_{k}^{2}.

(7.9)

Dividing by
\Phi_{k}\,\Phi_{k+1}
yields for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
,

\Psi_{k}-\Psi_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\Phi_{k+1}}-\frac{1}{\Phi_{k}}\geq\frac{\Theta}{\omega^{2}}\,\mu_{k}.

The lower bound
\mu_{k}\geq\underline{\mu}=\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}/\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}
from Proposition
6.6
closes the argument.

7.3.3
Strongly convex case

Assume
f
is
\sigma
-strongly convex (
\sigma>0
), and let
x^{\star}
be the global minimiser with
f^{\star}=\min_{x}f(x)=f(x^{\star})
.
Define,

\Psi_{k}=\ln\left(\Phi_{k}\right)-\ln\left(\nu\,\varepsilon\right),\quad\Phi_{k}=\nu(f(x_{k})-f^{\star})+(1-\nu)\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\quad\nu\in(0\,;\,1).

Prop. 7.6
.

Consider
\Psi_{k}
and
T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
defined above.
Under Condition
C.6
, for all

k<T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
,

\Psi_{k}-\Psi_{k+1}\;\geq\;\Theta\,\frac{\mu_{k}}{\overline{\mu}},\quad\Theta=\frac{2\sigma(1-\nu)\big(1-(L\overline{\mu}+1)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\big)}{\nu/\overline{\mu}+2\sigma(1-\nu)}\in(0,1).

Condition
C.5
holds with
\alpha_{k}=\mu_{k}
,

h_{\varepsilon}(x)=x/\overline{\mu}
, and

T_{\varepsilon}^{f}\leq\dfrac{\Psi_{0}}{\Theta\,h_{\varepsilon}(\underline{\mu})}=\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}\,\overline{\mu}\,\Big(\ln\left(\Phi_{0}\right)-\ln\left(\nu\,\varepsilon\right)\Big)}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbd}}\,\Theta}\;\in{\mathcal{O}}\left(\ln\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)\right).

Proof.

The standard inequality
\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\;\geq\;2\sigma\bigl(f(x)-f^{\star}\bigr)
gives for all
k<T_{\varepsilon}^{f}
,

\Phi_{k}\;\leq\;\left[\dfrac{\nu}{2\,\sigma}+(1-\nu)\,\overline{\mu}\right]\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Again, with a similar analysis as in the convex case, we have that,

\Phi_{k}-\Phi_{k+1}\geq(1-\nu)\left(1-\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)\mu_{k}\|\nabla f(x_{k})\|^{2}\geq\dfrac{2\sigma\,(1-\nu)\left(1-\left(L\overline{\mu}+1\right)^{2}\gamma_{2}^{2}\right)}{\nu/\overline{\mu}+2\sigma\,(1-\nu)}\,\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{\overline{\mu}}\,\Phi_{k}.

Therefore, we see
\Phi_{k+1}\leq\left[1-\Theta\,\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{\overline{\mu}}\right]\,\Phi_{k}
where
\Theta<1
and
\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{\overline{\mu}}\leq 1
, which shows a linear convergence rate.

Using
-\ln(1-x)\geq x
for
x\in(0\,;\,1)
, we have
\Psi_{k}-\Psi_{k+1}=\ln\left(\Phi_{k}\right)-\ln\left(\Phi_{k+1}\right)\geq-\ln\left(1-\Theta\,\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{\overline{\mu}}\right)\geq\Theta\,h_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{k})
.

Proposition
6.6
provides the lower bound on
\mu_{k}
, and Theorem
7.1
gets us the desired result.

8
Conclusion

We surveyed trust-region radius update mechanisms for unconstrained optimisation and isolated three structural conditions on the radius update rule: a lower bound relative to the gradient norm (Condition
C.1
), a contraction on unsuccessful iterations (Condition
C.2
), and a controlled expansion on successful ones (Condition
C.3
). Algorithm
1
provides a generic framework that holds independently of the specific update rule. Figure
1
illustrates the relation between the conditions and the convergence properties of the method. Under uniformly bounded model Hessians, weak admissibility yields
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert g_{k}\right\rVert=0
and strong admissibility yields the optimal worst-case complexity
{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. Strong admissibility extends the convergence guarantee to linearly growing model Hessians.

We verified admissibility for five mechanism classes from the literature: fixed-factor, step-driven, retrospective, criticality-anchored, and gradient-scaled. We extended the retrospective analysis of
Bastin et al. 2010
to linearly growing model Hessians, and adapted the Lyapunov framework of
Curtis and Scheinberg 2020
;
Wang and Yuan 2022
to the deterministic gradient-scaled update. The constants involved in the complexity bound depend on the specific mechanism and may significantly affect practical performance.

The proposed framework characterises first-order convergence and worst-case complexity. It does not address the asymptotic behaviour of
\Delta_{k}
, the local convergence rate near a minimiser, or second-order stationarity. Whether these properties admit a similar condition-based characterisation is open.
We take up these questions in a companion paper (Part II), where we analyse the asymptotic dynamics of
\Delta_{k}
, distinguishing the regime where the radius vanishes (
\sum_{k}\Delta_{k}<+\infty
or
\sum_{k}\Delta_{k}^{2}<+\infty
) from the regime where it stays bounded away from zero. We then study the local convergence behaviour, including the case where the trust-region constraint becomes inactive after finitely many iterations, allowing Newton or quasi-Newton steps and superlinear local rates.

Beyond Part II, two main directions stand out. The first is systematic numerical benchmarking of the five mechanism classes on standard test sets, to quantify the practical impact of the constants and to identify problem-dependent recommendations.
The second is the extension to stochastic and noisy settings, where the radius can serve as a precision parameter for sampling, following the works from

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
2.11

Proof.

The following proof is largely inspired from
Nocedal and Wright 2006
.

1.

Suppose there are only finitely many successful iterations.
Then, there exists an iteration index
k_{0}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
, the iterations are unsuccessful, i.e.,
x_{k+1}=x_{k}
, and
\|g_{k_{0}}\|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|=0
.

2.

Now suppose there are infinitely many successful iterations, and assume by contradiction that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|\neq 0
.
This implies that
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|g_{k}\|\geq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
, and there exists a subsequence
\{x_{k_{j}}\}
such that
\|g(x_{k_{j}})\|\geq\varepsilon>0
for all
j
.
Consider such an iteration
m\geq 0
of the subsequence such that
\left\lVert g(x_{m})\right\rVert\geq\varepsilon
.
When
x
is sufficiently close to
x_{m}
, then

\|x-x_{m}\|\leq\frac{\left\lVert g(x_{m})\right\rVert}{2L}.

Then, from AF.
2
, we have

\|g(x)\|\geq\left\lVert g(x_{m})\right\rVert-\left\lVert g(x_{m})-g(x)\right\rVert\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert g(x_{m})\right\rVert\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}>0.

(B.1)

As
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert g(x_{k})\right\rVert=0
, we can consider the first iteration
l_{m}>m
such that
\left\lVert g(x_{l_{m}})\right\rVert<\frac{1}{2}\|g(x_{m})\|
, meaning by contraposition of (
B.1
) that

\|x_{l_{m}}-x_{m}\|>\frac{1}{2L}\|g(x_{m})\|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2L}.

Then, from AA.
1
and AM.
2
, we have

f(x_{m})-f(x_{l_{m}})\geq\sum_{\underset{\{\rho_{k}\geq\eta\}}{{k=m}}}^{l_{m}-1}\rho_{k}(m_{k}(0)-m_{k}(s_{k}))\geq\sum_{\underset{\{\rho_{k}\geq\eta\}}{{k=m}}}^{l_{m}-1}\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{4}\varepsilon\min\left\{\Delta_{k}\,,\,\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\right\}.

(a)

First, if for all
m\leq k\leq l_{m}-1
,
\Delta_{k}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}
.
Then,

f(x_{m})-f(x_{l_{m}})\geq\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{4}\varepsilon\sum_{\underset{x_{k}\neq x_{k+1}}{{k=m}}}^{l_{m}-1}\Delta_{k}\geq\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{4}\varepsilon\left\lVert x_{l_{m}}-x_{m}\right\rVert>\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{8L}\varepsilon^{2}.

(b)

Else, if there exists
k_{0}\in[m,l_{m}-1]
such that
\Delta_{k_{0}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}
.
Then,

f(x_{m})-f(x_{l_{m}})\geq\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{4}\varepsilon\sum_{\underset{x_{k}\neq x_{k+1}}{{k=m}}}^{l_{m}-1}\Delta_{k}\geq\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{4}\varepsilon\Delta_{k_{0}}>\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{2\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\varepsilon^{2}\geq\eta\dfrac{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}}{8\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\varepsilon^{2}.

Therefore, in both cases, we have that

f(x_{m})-f(x_{l_{m}})\geq\frac{\eta}{8}\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{mdc}}\min\left\{\dfrac{1}{L},\dfrac{1}{\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{umh}}}\right\}\varepsilon^{2}>0
.

This contradicts that

\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}f(x_{m})-f(x_{l_{m}})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{0})-\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}<+\infty
by monotonic decrease of the sequence
\{f(x_{k})\}
and
f
being bounded below by
\kappa_{\scriptscriptstyle\text{lbf}}
.
