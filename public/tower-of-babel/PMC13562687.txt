Deep Actor–Critic Reinforcement Learning (DA-CRL) based multi-objective optimal control of solar PV integrated UPQC for power quality enhancement in smart distribution networks
Authors: E Hima Bindu, P B Guru Prasanna, D Krishna, B Srikanth Goud, Praveen Kumar Balachandran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPower quality problems with dynamic load fluctuations, harmonic distortion and voltage sags occur when renewable sources like solar PV systems are integrated into existing distribution systems. Series and shunt power disturbances are reduced using UPQCs. Under rapidly changing solar and grid conditions, PQ-based PI controllers will fail. These issues are addressed by a Deep Actor–Critic reinforcement learning (RL)-based multi-objective optimal control method for a Solar PV-integrated UPQC. Unlike PI, fuzzy, and model-based controllers, the recommended method learns optimal control policies from system interactions without mathematical modelling. The Actor network continually regulates UPQC’s series, shunt converters, while Critic network uses a multi-objective reward function to reduce THD, voltage sag/swell, DC-link voltage and power factor. This control methodology applies to a nonlinear, dynamic grid with demand uncertainty and intermittent solar power. MATLAB/Simulink simulations show that the proposed controller outperforms PI and adaptive controllers in dynamic response, harmonic suppression, voltage regulation, and DC-link stability. A reliable, adaptable, and scalable Deep Actor–Critic RL approach for intelligent power quality enhancement in smart distribution systems with substantial renewable penetration was found. Introduction

Modern power networks are increasingly incorporating grid-connected photovoltaic (PV) systems because of their affordability and sustainability. Their unpredictability is a result of the abrupt fluctuations in temperature and irradiance, which in turn leads to voltage instability, subpar power quality (PQ)1. Problems with grid-integrated PV systems’ efficiency and dependability originate from issues like DC-link voltage oscillations, load transient voltage sag. These PQ problems are entirely resolved by UPQC2. Voltage sags/swells, current harmonics can be eliminated by UPQC through use of series, shunt voltage source converters. As a result, voltage and DC-link stability are improved. In highly dynamic grid circumstances, control techniques such as PI, fuzzy logic, ANFIS are not very adaptable because they rely on offline tinkering and predefined control parameters3. PV systems are associated to grid are vulnerable to variations in temperature and sunlight. The reliability and consistency of the power supply are jeopardized because these oscillations cause voltage drops and DC-link voltage instability4. It is critical in these cases for the system to have transient stability, which means it can run synchronously, bounce back fast from disturbances like load switching or grid failures. Through its dual adjustment mechanism utilizing series, shunt voltage source converters (VSCs), UPQC enhances power quality and System Stability. The efficiency of UPQC’s control method determines how well it performs. Over the years, PI controllers have been the go-to for regulating DC-link voltage, compensating for harmonic components. Dynamic performances are explored by the proposed system to fine-tune PI controller gains5. Through iterative simulation, constants are modified to decrease steady-state inaccuracy, guarantee rapid dynamic responsiveness to changing grid, load conditions. To get over problems with static PI tuning, suggested approach leverages DRL-based control in conjunction with PI controller6. In PI steady-state performance, DRL makes real-time adjustments to control action to enhance transient response, robustness.

Modern developments in deep reinforcement learning have made it possible for intricate power electrical systems to learn and adapt in real-time. The DC-link voltage regulation, voltage sag correction of PV-UPQC systems is supported by a hybrid controller that integrates DRL and PI control, making them both powerful and versatile. By testing THD, settling time, dynamic responsiveness in different environments, a DRL-PI based control system, shows how it outperforms traditional methods.

Research on voltage sag, DC-link voltage management has made extensive use of novel approaches7. Traditional PI controllers are simple to construct, but they struggle to adapt to changing conditions and exhibit poor performance overall8. Advanced methods, such as Sliding Mode Controllers (SMC)9, Fuzzy Logic Controllers (FLC), offers good dynamic reflexes; but, large-scale systems cannot use these methods in real-time due to the complexity of their tuning and construction. Model Predictive Control (MPC) offers the most effective control in UPQC systems at the moment, but it requires accurate models, lot of computing power10. Power electrical system intelligent control is been boosted by recent advancements in artificial intelligence, machine learning. In addition to controlling voltage, DNNs also approximatively model nonlinear system properties11. Their capacity to adjust to undetected shocks is hindered, nevertheless, by their reliance on huge labeled datasets and their inability to make decisions in real-time. The ability to acquire optimal control policies from the environment is one of the reasons why the model-free learning paradigm known as Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained popularity12. In power systems, RL regulates voltage, inverter, and frequency13. DRL is able to manage high-dimensional inputs and complicated control tasks because it integrates neural networks with reinforcement learning14. Intelligent defect detection, adaptive inverter control, and microgrid energy management have all made use of DRL techniques, like Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), Deep Q-Networks (DQN)15. A little research on directly applying RL or DRL to UPQC systems because of the complexity of reward structuring, safety concerns, slower convergence in real-time. More and more, hybrid control approaches combines supervised, reinforcement learning, such as DNN, are cropping up to fill this void. The adaptive policy learning of RL, quick approximation of DNNs are utilized by these models. Electricity grid-forming converters16, smart inverter voltage control17, and energy storage systems18 all employ hybrid approaches. Recent research on UPQC for PV-integrated grids has suggested adaptive control based on neural networks for DC-link voltage stabilization19, however this approach has limited performance in situations of sag and transient grid failures. Instead of managing the voltage on the DC-link or correcting sag in real-time, current DRL-based solutions put energy scheduling first20. This research fills a need in the literature by developing a controller for UPQC systems that uses deep reinforcement learning to compensate for voltage drops quickly and efficiently in situations with fluctuating solar irradiance, grid disturbances, as well as to dynamically manage DC-link voltage.

Literature review

In recent years, the design of an adaptive intelligent control system based on reinforcement learning for power quality improvement in smart distribution networks has focused on the research of the photovoltaic unified power quality conditioner (PV-UPQC) system. Voltage stabilization, harmonic mitigation, and reactive power compensation are three grid scenarios that have led to the research of PV-STATCOM and PV-DSTATCOM systems. For example, the latest research on PV-DSTATCOM indicates that the indirect current management methods improve the grid voltage stability and reduce the total harmonic distortion (THD) by achieving real-time reactive power and harmonic compensation. In order to further improve the accuracy of dynamic response and compensation in nonlinear load scenarios, some methods have been proposed such as predictive direct power regulation and multilevel inverter-based PV active filtering. A growing number of studies on UPQC have concentrated on the control strategies based on artificial intelligence (AI), such as hybrid adaptive control and Lyapunov-based control for voltage regulation, stability and harmonic suppression. The AI systems encompass gated recurrent unit (GRU) networks, optimized proportional-integral (PI) controllers and adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference systems (ANFIS). The photovoltaic (PV)-UPQC systems with maximum power point tracking (MPPT) have also attracted much attention, particularly in terms of power quality and grid stability under the impact of varying irradiance. The voltage sag compensation, total harmonic distortion (THD) minimization and DC-link regulation can be enhanced by intelligent controllers integrated with perturb-and-observe (P&O) MPPT algorithms. Furthermore, smart grids with renewable energy sources have been promising for adaptive power quality improvement by deep reinforcement learning and actor-critic-based regulation. These advanced methods are able to improve the stability, settling time and overall performance compared to the traditional PI and fuzzy controllers by adaptively varying the control actions in response to operational uncertainties and system disturbances. Currently, researchers are working on intelligent, adaptive and reinforcement learning based multi-objective control techniques for PV-UPQC systems in smart distribution networks instead of conventional fixed parameter controllers.

Using an intelligent maximum power point tracking controller based on bacterial foraging optimization, this study demonstrates how to manage the DC-link capacitor voltage of the Solar-PV inverter. This allows for optimal regulation of both active and reactive power21. In order to reduce and control power oscillations in a two-area four generator system integrated with a large-scale PV-farm, this paper presents a thorough performance evaluation of the PSO and BFOA metaheuristic swarm-based optimization algorithms, as well as the hybrid PSO-BFOA optimizer. Following sunset, the PV plant transforms into a VSC-STATCOM, which improves power system stability by utilizing its total inverting capabilities22. Using a two-area power system as an example, the article shows how a Wind-PV hybrid farm can act as a STATCOM (Static Synchronous Compensator), reducing and controlling overall chaotic oscillations. To showcase the thorough evaluation of the hybrid farm’s performance, Kundur’s redesigned system is modelled in MATLAB. With the help of two PI controllers, a special controller is set up to control the STATCOM’s AC and DC currents. To fine-tune and control the PI controller parameters, a swarm-based hybrid metaheuristic optimizer called PSO-BFOA is used23. Using a big PV-farm as a PV-STATCOM (Static synchronous compensator), this paper investigates how a multimachine power system might reduce oscillations. Variations in coupling voltage, shaft torsions, and rotor dynamics (generator speed and angle) are all components of power system oscillations. As soon as it becomes dark, the PV farm transforms into a VSC (Voltage Source Converter)-STATCOM and uses all of its inverting power to suppress the oscillations24. An improved Fractional order Control theory-based Pole fixed Second Order Generalized Integrator (FO‐PFSOGI) is suggested as a means to create a grid-connected solar photovoltaic (PV) system. If you want to tweak the integer order’s fixed structure and add more degrees of freedom to get an exact response while the system is running, the fractional order (FO) control method is a good choice25.

However, under dynamic smart grid operating settings, most existing techniques are limited in effectiveness due to fixed controller parameters, poor adaptability, and single-objective optimization. In addition, there has been very little research into using Deep Actor-Critic Reinforcement Learning to control integrated UPQC systems that use solar PV for coordinated multi-objective problems. Problem statement

Solar PV-integrated distribution systems have a hard time keeping the planned DC-link voltage since power provided to grid is regularly varied due to the irregular nature of solar irradiation. Disruptions to the grid, such as voltage drops caused by faults or sudden changes in load, can also significantly degrade power quality, which in turn can impact operation of sensitive equipment, whole system. Operating settings optimized for traditional control techniques like PI and Fuzzy Logic Controllers (FLC) tend to be less successful in the presence of nonlinearities and uncertainties. This study proposes a control technique based on hybrid learning that combines the generalizability and state estimation of neural networks with adaptive learning capabilities of Reinforcement learning to circumvent these limitations. The proposed Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) controller not only maximizes control actions to decrease voltage deviation, improve transient response, but it also adjusts to time-varying inputs from PV, load.

Novelty: Unlike conventional UPQC controllers that rely on cascaded PI loops and fixed tuning parameters, the proposed Deep Actor–Critic framework autonomously learns optimal control policies in real time, ensuring robustness against grid uncertainties, nonlinear loads, and stochastic solar generation, thereby paving the way for intelligent, self-adaptive power quality management in next-generation smart distribution networks.

Dynamic model of PV–UPQC system

Power quality is enhanced and grid reliability is ensured with a hybrid solution that combines solar PV systems with UPQC. A grid-integrated SPV-UPQC system, as shown in Fig. 1, does more than just contribute renewable energy to the grid; it also fixes reactive power, sag/swell, harmonics, voltage, current distortions. By merging Solar Photovoltaic (SPV) systems with UPQC, novel hybrid system is formed that tackles the dual challenges of power quality and renewable power injection. An essential component of such a system is the accurate regulation of DC link voltage, ensures effective compensation through series, shunt VSCs. Conventional proportional-integral (PI) controllers often fail in situations when the grid is dynamic and unpredictable. A controller that intelligently modulates and stabilises the DC connection voltage using Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) is introduced to address this. To accomplish decoupled voltage and current compensation, it is crucial to coordinate the modulation indices for shunt, series Voltage Source Converters (VSC). In particular, sags and swells in the supply voltage are reduced by the series VSC, reactive power and load current harmonics are handled by the shunt VSC by the injection of compensating currents. Incorporating maximum power point tracking (MPPT), this system-wide coordinated control keeps DC link voltage stable, power quality high even when grid, load conditions are unpredictable. To optimize the power generation of the solar array, SPV system is linked via boost converter. A P&O/MPPT guides a PI-based boost controller that controls the converter’s output. Series filters get their power from the boost converter, while the DC link capacitor acts as an energy buffer for the shunt. Issues with reactive power compensation, harmonics, and power-quality imbalances in the current are handled by the shunt VSC at the PCC. At the same time, series VSC compensates for disturbances like swells and sags and guarantees voltage regulation. For the UPQC system to be more flexible in responding to changes in grid conditions and loads DRL controllers are used to modify the DC link voltage.

Control method

The control diagram in Fig.7 illustrates a Deep Actor–Critic reinforcement learning–based control architecture for a Solar PV integrated UPQC operating in smart distribution grid. The solar PV array feeds common DC-link capacitor, which supplies both series, shunt converters42. The shunt converter compensates current harmonics, reactive power to ensure sinusoidal source current, unity power factor, while series converter injects compensating voltage to mitigate sag, swell, voltage distortions at load side. Measured grid variables such as three-phase voltages and currents, load voltage, DC-link voltage, THD, and reactive power are transformed into the dq reference frame and processed to form system state vector. This state is fed into Deep Actor network, which generates continuous modulation indices for both converters, and the Critic network evaluates the action using a multi-objective reward function that penalizes THD, voltage deviation, DC-link fluctuation, and reactive power44. Controller tuning methodology and comparative parameter settings

The adaptive controller, fuzzy logic controller (FLC), and conventional PI controller were all adjusted using standard optimisation approaches under the same operating conditions to guarantee an objective comparison. Smart distribution network scenarios involving renewable intermittency were taken into account throughout the tuning process, which also included voltage sag, swell, harmonic distortion, nonlinear load changes, and so on.After optimising the integral and proportional gains for the PI controller with the Ziegler-Nichols tuning approach, fine-tuning was done by minimising the Integral Square Error (ISE). Using a combination of heuristic tuning and performance-based iterative refinement, the membership functions and rule base parameters of the fuzzy controller were adjusted. To reduce steady-state voltage and current compensation errors, the adaptive controller parameters were updated using a gradient-based adaptive gain adjustment method. Experimental bias was eliminated by evaluating all controllers in the same way, using the same simulation environment, switching frequencies, loading conditions, sampling intervals, and disturbance patterns. The settling time, compensation accuracy, voltage THD, current THD, DC-link voltage regulation, and voltage THD were all utilized in the comparison study. Prior to evaluation, all benchmark controllers were optimized using well-established processes, as confirmed by the comparison tuning approach. Voltage sag and voltage swell compensation with PI

Figure 9 shows the system’s performance when voltage disturbances are present, as controlled by the PI. Within the intervals shown in subfigure (a), the three-phase source voltage exhibits conventional sinusoidal behavior; nevertheless, it sags between 0.05 s and 0.1 s and then swells from 0.1 s to 0.15 s. In Figure (b), we can see the injected voltage that the series compensator produces. This voltage is only activated when there is a disturbance; when the voltage is low, it works in phase with the source voltage to restore its magnitude, and when it’s high, it works in opposition to the source voltage to prevent overvoltage. As shown in subfigure (c), load voltage is balanced, sinusoidal, and maintained at the nominal level for the whole duration, proving that the PI-controlled system effectively reduces voltage sag, swell and guarantees better power quality but PI gives slow dynamic Response and inability to handle system nonlinearities and uncertainties effectively, making it less suitable for complex power electronic applications.

Current harmonics compensation with PI

The graphic shows in Fig. 10 the current waveforms of a PI-controlled compensation system (such as a shunt active filter or UPQC) over time, with load current (red), filter current (blue), and source current (black) represented. There are nonlinear loads introducing harmonics into the system because the load current (red) is extremely distorted and non-sinusoidal. A filter current (blue) is produced by the compensator to reduce these distortions; this current has harmonic components that are equal in size but in phase with the load current. Consequently, source current (black) becomes balanced and almost sinusoidal when the filter current is introduced. By limiting the source to supplying only the essential current component, the PI controller is able to improve power quality and decrease harmonic distortion through compensator regulation. Despite achieving compensation, several limitations of the PI controller are evident. The source current is not perfectly sinusoidal, indicating incomplete harmonic compensation.
