Improved quantum circuits for division
Priyanka Mukhopadhyay, Alexandru Gheorghiu, Hari Krovi

Abstract
Arithmetic operations are an important component of many quantum algorithms. As such, coming up with optimized quantum circuits for these operations leads to more efficient implementations of the corresponding algorithms. In this paper, we develop new fault-tolerant quantum circuits for various integer division algorithms (both reversible and non-reversible). These circuits, when implemented in the Clifford+T gate set, achieve an up to 76.08% and 68.35% reduction in T-count and CNOT-count, respectively, compared to previous circuit constructions. Some of our circuits also improve the asymptotic T-depth from O ⁡ ( n 2 ) O(n^{2}) to O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) , O(n\log n), where n n is the bit-length of the dividend. The qubit counts are also lower than in previous works. We achieve this by expressing the division algorithms in terms of a primitive we call COMP-N-SUB, that compares two integers and conditionally subtracts them. We show that this primitive can be implemented at a cost, in terms of both Clifford and non-Clifford gates, that is comparable to one addition. This is in contrast to performing comparison and conditional subtraction separately, whose cost would be comparable to a controlled addition plus a regular addition.

1.1
Our results

We consider three division algorithms that have been studied extensively by prior work, namely
long division, restoring division
(as given in
[
2019_TMVH
]
) and
non-restoring division.
These algorithms are used to divide an integer
dividend
by an integer
divisor
, and return an integer
quotient
and an integer
remainder
. All algorithms work by iterative subtraction of the divisor from the dividend. In the restoring division algorithm, after each subtraction, the
minuend
(from which a value is subtracted) is restored to its previous value if the difference is negative. In the non-restoring division algorithm, the minuend is not immediately restored. Rather, the next operations are determined by the sign (positive or negative) of the difference, and then a final correction may be applied. Long division is a type of restoring algorithm where in each iteration the divisor is subtracted from a portion of the dividend. Integer division can also be used as a sub-routine in some floating point division algorithms
[
2021_GKDetal
,
2022_GKD
]
. In
[
2022_YGWetal
]
quantum circuits for integer division have been used to divide a fraction by an integer. Hence, quantum circuits developed for the above-mentioned operations are useful for both integer as well as floating point operations.

We develop novel and efficient fault-tolerant quantum circuits for these three division algorithms, expressed in the Clifford+Toffoli gate set. Since Toffoli can be decomposed into Cliffords and T gates
[
2010_NC
]
, we also obtain Clifford+T implementations of our circuits. For each division algorithm, we have multiple circuit implementations, that can be broadly classified into three types.

•

Type-I
circuits are fully reversible and optimize the Toffoli or T-count, the CNOT-count and the qubit-count.

•

Type-II
circuits are also fully reversible and optimize the Toffoli or T-depth and overall circuit depth, at the cost of an increase in the qubit-count, CNOT-count and Toffoli or T-count. These can be further divided into two sub-types—type-IIb, with the smallest known Toffoli or T-depth, and type-IIa, whose metrics are intermediate between type-I and IIb. Specifically, they have lower T-count, CNOT-count and qubit-count than type-IIb, but larger T-depth than type-I.

•

Type-III
circuits are not fully reversible and optimize T-count and CNOT-count by making use of the logical-AND gadget from
[
2013_J
,
2018_G
]
. These circuits also have a higher qubit-count compared to types I and II.

Central to our designs is the development of new quantum circuits for a unitary that performs the following operation.

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:\quad\text{Given two integers $a$ and $b$, compute $b-a$ if and only if $b\geq a$.}

(1)

In braket notation, the associated unitary maps the state of registers storing
a
and
b
in the following way.

\displaystyle\ket{a}\ket{b}\mapsto\begin{cases}\ket{a}\ket{b-a},&\text{if }b\geq a\\
\ket{a}\ket{b},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We will refer to this unitary as COMP-N-SUB (compare and subtract). Logical instructions similar to this appear in many quantum algorithms, and so having more efficient quantum circuits for COMP-N-SUB directly leads to optimizations for those algorithms as well.

Our main contribution is to show that COMP-N-SUB can be implemented at roughly the same cost (in terms of T-count, Toffoli-count, CNOT-count or depth) as one addition operation between two integers. Previous works achieved this type of functionality by first performing a comparison and then a conditional subtraction. The cost of this is dominated by the conditional subtraction, which is effectively a controlled addition operation. This has a much larger overhead compared to a regular addition, as it takes an addition circuit and turns every gate into a controlled version of itself. Compiling this into a standard gate set will then lead to a large blow up in the numbers of T/Toffoli gates. Thus, being able to do comparison and conditional subtraction at a cost that is comparable to a regular addition will lead to division circuits having fewer non-Clifford gates, fewer qubits and, in some instances, even smaller circuit-depth compared to previous approaches.
