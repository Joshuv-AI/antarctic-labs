Order by disorder up to arbitrarily high temperature
Ravish Mehta

Abstract
We prove that a class of classical lattice models on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} ( d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 ) with on-site space ℕ 0 \mathbb{N}_{0} exhibits long-range checkerboard order at sufficiently high temperature. The model has a nearest-neighbour interaction f : ℕ 0 × ℕ 0 → [ 0 , ∞ ) f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\to[0,\infty) satisfying four structural conditions, subsuming the recently introduced power-law model of Han–Huang–Komargodski–Lucas–Popov [ 7 ] as a special case. The ordering mechanism is purely entropic: the checkerboard configurations are not energy minimisers, but are selected by the partial trace over occupation numbers in the β → 0 \beta\to 0 limit. The proof uses Pirogov–Sinai theory and the key input is a Peierls bound. Keywords: statistical mechanics, phase transitions



1
Introduction

In statistical physics, ordered phases are typically associated with low temperature:
as the temperature increases, thermal fluctuations wash out long-range correlations. This expectation is supported by classical no-go results due to
Dobrushin
[
4
]
and Künsch
[
12
,
6
]
, which guarantee uniqueness of
the Gibbs measure at sufficiently high temperature whenever the local interactions are
bounded. A counterpoint has emerged in recent years. When the local single-spin space
is unbounded — as for bosonic occupation numbers
n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
— the
assumptions underlying the classical no-go theorems fail, and the possibility of

entropic order
arises: long-range order that not only survives at high temperature
but is driven by it. The mechanism is that a small subset of configurations admits much
larger thermal fluctuations of an auxiliary degree of freedom than the rest, so that the
entropy gained outweighs any energetic preference for disorder. This is in the spirit of

order by disorder
, introduced by Villain, Bidaux, Carton, and Conte
[
17
]

and independently by Shender
[
15
,
8
]
for frustrated magnets, though the regime
is opposite: order by disorder is a low-temperature effect, whereas entropic order arises
in the
\beta\to 0
limit and selects configurations that are not energy minimisers.

Explicit models exhibiting entropic order have been studied in a series of recent
works
[
3
,
10
,
7
,
9
]
. In
particular, Han, Huang, Komargodski, Lucas, and Popov
[
7
]
introduced the
classical lattice boson model with Hamiltonian

H\;=\;U\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\!\!n_{x}^{p}n_{y}^{p}\;+\;\mu\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}n_{x},\qquad n_{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1)

and argued, via mean-field theory and Monte Carlo simulations, that this model exhibits long-range checkerboard order at all
sufficiently high temperatures when
p>1
.

The aim of the present paper is to prove this.
We generalise the interaction from the
specific power-law form
U(n_{x}n_{y})^{p}
to an abstract nearest-neighbour function

f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\to[0,\infty)
, and identify the minimal
structural conditions on
f
under which entropic checkerboard order occurs. Then, we
give a complete proof via
Pirogov–Sinai theory

[
13
,
14
,
18
,
16
,
2
,
5
]
— including a full contour formalism,
a convergent cluster expansion, and a bulk–boundary decomposition.

We consider the Hamiltonian

H\;=\;U\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}n_{x}\;+\;\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\!\!f(n_{x},n_{y}),

(2)

and impose four conditions on
f
(Section
2
): symmetry, vanishing
whenever either argument is zero, strict positivity on
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
,
and a growth condition
N(T)=O(T^{\alpha})
with
\alpha\in[0,1)
on the level-set
counting function
N(T):=\#\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:U(m+n)+f(m,n)\leq T\}
.
The first three are the minimal qualitative requirements for the checkerboard
configurations to be entropically selected; the fourth is the precise quantitative
condition that drives the Peierls bound. The original model (
1
)
is recovered by
f(m,n)=U(mn)^{p}
, which satisfies all four conditions for every
p>1
.

Our main result (Theorem
6.4
) states that for every
\beta\in(0,\beta_{0})
,
every
\#\in\{1,2\}
, every finite
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and every
x\in\Lambda
,

\bigl|\mu^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{x})-\eta^{\#}_{x}\bigr|\;\leq\;\epsilon(\beta),\qquad\epsilon(\beta)\to 0\text{ as }\beta\to 0,

(3)

uniformly in
\Lambda
and
x
. Uniformity in
\Lambda
distinguishes genuine
long-range order from a boundary artefact, and implies that the two checkerboard
boundary conditions select distinct infinite-volume Gibbs states. The mechanism is
purely entropic:
\eta^{1}
and
\eta^{2}
are not ground states of the microscopic
Hamiltonian (the vacuum
n\equiv 0
has lower energy), but the partial trace over
occupation numbers generates an effective free energy whose minimisers are the
two checkerboards.

While we were preparing this paper, another paper appeared on arxiv achieving a very similar goal:
Andriolo, Nguyen, Richards, and
Sulejmanpasic
[
1
]
gave a rigorous proof of ordering for (
1
) with
p>1

on any bipartite lattice in dimension
d\geq 2
, using a Peierls-type domain-wall
argument combined with a rigorous asymptotic formula for the high-temperature cluster
partition function. Our paper offers a different (though similar) perspective on the problem.

Our proof proceeds in three steps. In Section
5
we establish a
Peierls bound

\|\gamma\|\;\geq\;c\,\frac{|\log\beta|}{\beta}\,|\bar{\gamma}|

(4)

on the surface energy of any contour
\gamma
. The key input is a

dimer-excess estimate
(Lemma
5.2
): under hypothesis (F4), an
Abel-summation argument shows that any nearest-neighbour occupied pair costs strictly
more than two isolated singletons in the
\beta\to 0
regime. This is what makes the
checkerboard configurations locally optimal at high temperature, and it replaces the
asymptotic cluster-weight formula of
[
1
]
with a direct, elementary bound.
In Section
6
we use the Peierls bound as input to the
Kotecký–Preiss criterion
[
11
,
5
]
to obtain a convergent cluster expansion
for
\log\Xi^{\#}_{\Lambda}
, from which a bulk–boundary decomposition with exponentially
small remainder follows. Finally, we verify by induction on contour class that the contour
weights satisfy
w^{\#}(\gamma)\leq e^{-\tau(\beta)|\bar{\gamma}|}
with
\tau(\beta)\to\infty

as
\beta\to 0
, closing the argument.

2
System

2.1
Lattice

Fix an integer
d\geq 2
. We work on the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
equipped with the nearest-neighbour graph structure:
x\sim y
if and only if
|x-y|_{1}=1
. The lattice is bipartite; we fix its natural two-colouring

\mathbb{Z}^{d}\;=\;A\sqcup B,\qquad A\,:=\,\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\text{ even}\},\qquad B\,:=\,\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A.

(5)

For any
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a
configuration
on
S
is an element
n_{S}=(n_{x})_{x\in S}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{S}
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. The
occupation label
of
n_{S}
is
\omega_{S}\in\{0,1\}^{S}
, defined by

\omega_{x}\,:=\,\mathbf{1}[n_{x}\geq 1],\qquad x\in S.

(6)

We denote by
d_{\infty}
the
\ell^{\infty}
metric on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

2.2
Reference configurations

The two checkerboard labellings
\eta^{1},\eta^{2}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are defined by

\eta^{1}_{x}\,:=\,\mathbf{1}[x\in A],\qquad\eta^{2}_{x}\,:=\,\mathbf{1}[x\in B]\,=\,1-\eta^{1}_{x}.

(7)

These are not ground states of the microscopic Hamiltonian introduced below; rather, they are the two configurations that minimise the effective Hamiltonian obtained by partial trace in the
\beta\to 0
regime (see Section
4
). We refer to them as the
reference configurations
of type
\#\in\{1,2\}
. The translation
x\mapsto x+e_{1}
exchanges
\eta^{1}\leftrightarrow\eta^{2}
, realising a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry that we will exploit in Section
6
.

2.3
Finite-volume Hamiltonian

Fix a parameter
U>0
and a function

f:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\to\;[0,\infty)

(8)

satisfying the following standing assumptions, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

(F1)

Symmetry:

f(m,n)=f(n,m)
.

(F2)

Vanishing on empty sites:

f(0,n)=0
.

(F3)

Strict penalty on occupied pairs:

f(m,n)>0
whenever
m,n\geq 1
.

(F4)

N(T)=\#\bigl\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}:U(m+n)+f(m,n)\leq T\bigr\}=O(T^{\alpha})
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite, simply connected subset. Given a boundary configuration
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda}
on the exterior and an interior configuration
n_{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Lambda}
, define
n^{\mathrm{full}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
by

n^{\mathrm{full}}_{x}\,:=\,\begin{cases}n_{x}&\text{if }x\in\Lambda,\\
\bar{n}_{x}&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda.\end{cases}

(9)

The
Hamiltonian on
\Lambda
with boundary condition
\bar{n}
is

H_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda}\mid\bar{n})\;:=\;U\sum_{x\in\Lambda}n_{x}\;+\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{x,y\}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
x\sim y,\;\{x,y\}\cap\Lambda\neq\varnothing\end{subarray}}f\bigl(n^{\mathrm{full}}_{x},n^{\mathrm{full}}_{y}\bigr).

(10)

Both sums are finite: the first because
\Lambda
is finite, the second because only finitely many nearest-neighbour bonds intersect
\Lambda
.

2.4
Checkerboard boundary conditions and partition function

For
\#\in\{1,2\}
, we take the boundary datum denoted by
\bar{n}^{\#}
to be the reference configuration itself such that occupation labels are given by

\omega_{x}=\eta^{\#}_{x}\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\Lambda.

(11)

We write
H^{\#}_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda}):=H_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda}\mid\bar{n}^{\#})
for the corresponding Hamiltonian and define the
\#
-partition function
at inverse temperature
\beta>0
by

Z^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\beta)\;:=\;\sum_{n_{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Lambda}}e^{-\beta H^{\#}_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})}.

(12)

The corresponding finite-volume Gibbs measure on
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\Lambda}
is

\mu^{\#}_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})\;:=\;\frac{e^{-\beta H^{\#}_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})}}{Z^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\beta)}.

(13)

2.5
Result

The aim of the paper is to prove that, at sufficiently high temperature (
\beta
sufficiently small), the Gibbs measure
\mu^{\#}_{\Lambda}
is concentrated near the reference configuration
\eta^{\#}
, uniformly in
\Lambda
. Our main result (Theorem
6.4
, Section
6
) asserts the existence of
\beta_{0}>0
and a function
\epsilon:(0,\beta_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\epsilon(\beta)\to 0
as
\beta\to 0
, such that for every
\beta\in(0,\beta_{0})
, every
\#\in\{1,2\}
, every finite
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and every site
x\in\Lambda
,

\bigl|\,\mu^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{x})\,-\,\eta^{\#}_{x}\,\bigr|\;\leq\;\epsilon(\beta).

(14)

Uniformity in
\Lambda
and in
x
is crucial: it is what distinguishes genuine long-range order from a boundary artefact, and it implies that the two boundary conditions
\#=\{1,2\}
select distinct infinite-volume Gibbs states. The mechanism underlying this ordering is purely entropic: the reference configurations are selected not because they are the lowest-energy configurations — the unique microscopic ground state is the vacuum
n\equiv 0
— but because the partial trace over occupation numbers at small
\beta
generates an effective free energy whose minimisers are
\eta^{1}
and
\eta^{2}
.

3
Contours

To analyse fluctuations around the reference configurations
\eta^{1},\eta^{2}
, we set up a Pirogov–Sinai contour formalism on a macro-lattice of
2\times\cdots\times 2
micro-blocks. On this macro-lattice the two reference configurations become constant, and the standard contour machinery for systems with finitely many translation-invariant ground states applies.

3.1
The macro-lattice

Partition
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
into disjoint blocks

B_{i}\;:=\;2i+\{0,1\}^{d},\qquad i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(15)

each consisting of
2^{d}
micro-sites. We denote the corresponding
macro-lattice
by
\mathbb{L}
: as a set
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, but each site
i\in\mathbb{L}
is interpreted as a label for the block
B_{i}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We equip
\mathbb{L}
with the
\ell^{\infty}
metric, which we still denote
d_{\infty}
, and write
B(r):=\{i\in\mathbb{L}:d_{\infty}(0,i)\leq r\}
. Two macro-sites are
adjacent
if
d_{\infty}(i,j)=1
; this is the adjacency relation used throughout the contour analysis.

The
macro-single-spin space
is

\Omega\;:=\;\{0,1\}^{\{0,1\}^{d}},\qquad|\Omega|\,=\,2^{2^{d}},

(16)

identified with the set of
\{0,1\}
-labellings of a single block.
A
macro-configuration
on a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{L}
is
\omega\in\Omega^{\mathbb{L}}
restricted to
S

\omega_{S}\,=\,(\omega_{i})_{i\in S},

(17)

where
\omega_{i}\in\Omega
encodes the micro-occupation labels inside the block
B_{i}
.

The reference labellings
\eta^{1},\eta^{2}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of Section
2
have period
2
in every coordinate. Since each block anchor
2i
lies in the even sublattice
A
, the restriction
\eta^{\#}|_{B_{i}}
is independent of
i
. Thus, on the macro-lattice the reference configurations are the two
constant
macro-configurations

\eta^{\#}_{i}\,\equiv\,\hat{\eta}^{\#}\qquad(i\in\mathbb{L},\ \#\in\{1,2\}).

(18)

By a mild abuse of notation, we continue to denote these macro-configurations by
\eta^{\#}
; the micro- or macro-level interpretation will always be clear from context. Note that
\hat{\eta}^{1}\neq\hat{\eta}^{2}
.

We assume henceforth that the finite volume
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of Section
2
is a union of complete blocks, and we identify it with a finite subset of the macro-lattice,
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{L}
.

3.2
Correct sites and boundary

Definition 3.1

(Correct site)
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega^{\mathbb{L}}
and fix
\#\in\{1,2\}
. A macro-site
i\in\mathbb{L}
is
\#
-correct in
\omega
if

\omega_{j}\,=\,\hat{\eta}^{\#}\qquad\text{for every }j\in i+B(1).

(19)

A site is
correct
if it is
\#
-correct for some
\#\in\{1,2\}
, and
incorrect
otherwise.

Because
\hat{\eta}^{1}\neq\hat{\eta}^{2}
, a correct site has a unique type.

Definition 3.2

(Boundary and thickened boundary)
.

The
boundary
of
\omega\in\Omega^{\mathbb{L}}
is

B(\omega)\;:=\;\{i\in\mathbb{L}:i\text{ is incorrect in }\omega\},

(20)

and its
thickened boundary
is

\Gamma(\omega)\;:=\;\bigcup_{i\in B(\omega)}\bigl(i+B(1)\bigr).

(21)

The following lemma — a direct consequence of the thickening procedure — is the reason the macro-lattice formalism is useful.

:
A configuration with two contours
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
with the boundary
B(\omega)
denoted by the dark grey region and the patterned regions representing boundaries of the contours. The outer boundary has the phase
\eta_{1}
and the interior boundary has the same phase as that of the interior components along which they are drawn.

Lemma 3.3

(Uniform type on correct regions)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{L}\setminus\Gamma(\omega)
be non-empty and
d_{\infty}
-connected. Then every site of
C
is correct, and all sites of
C
share the same type.

Proof.

Let
i\in C
. Since
i\notin\Gamma(\omega)
, the ball
i+B(1)
is disjoint from
B(\omega)
, so every site of
i+B(1)
is correct; in particular
i
is correct. Now let
i,j\in C
be adjacent, with
i
of type
\#
, so
\omega_{j}=\hat{\eta}^{\#}
. Connectedness of
C
propagates the type.
∎

We restrict attention to the space of configurations with finite boundary.
\Gamma(\omega)
admits a unique decomposition into finitely many maximal
d_{\infty}
-connected components.

3.3
Contours

Definition 3.4

(Contour)
.

Let
\omega
be a configuration with finite boundary. For each maximal
d_{\infty}
-connected component
\bar{\gamma}
of
\Gamma(\omega)
, the associated
contour
is the pair

\gamma\,:=\,(\bar{\gamma},\,\omega_{\bar{\gamma}}).

(22)

We call
\bar{\gamma}
the
support
of
\gamma
.

For a finite subset
A\subset\mathbb{L}
, we use the standard notation
\partial^{\mathrm{ex}}A:=\{i\in\mathbb{L}\setminus A:d_{\infty}(i,A)=1\}
for the exterior boundary and
\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A:=\{i\in A:d_{\infty}(i,\mathbb{L}\setminus A)=1\}
for the interior boundary.

Definition 3.5

(Interior, exterior, and type)
.

Let
\gamma=(\bar{\gamma},\omega_{\bar{\gamma}})
be a contour of some
\omega
such that
\omega
has a finite boundary. The complement
\mathbb{L}\setminus\bar{\gamma}
decomposes into finitely many maximal
d_{\infty}
-connected components; exactly one of them is unbounded, and we denote it by
A_{0}
. We denote the remaining (bounded) components by
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
, and set

\mathrm{ext}\,\gamma\,:=\,A_{0},\qquad\mathrm{int}\,\gamma\,:=\,\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}.

(23)

The boundary sets
\partial^{\mathrm{ex}}A_{j}
and
\partial^{\mathrm{in}}A_{j}
are
d_{\infty}
-connected for every
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
, and there exists a unique type
\#
for each
A_{j}
and we denote the components as
A_{j}^{\#}

\omega_{i}\;=\;\hat{\eta}^{\#}\qquad\text{for every }i\in\partial^{\mathrm{ex}}A_{j}.

(24)

Note that
\partial^{\mathrm{ex}}A_{j}\subseteq\bar{\gamma}
for every
j
, so the types are determined by the data
\omega_{\bar{\gamma}}
stored in the contour.

The interior of
\gamma
refines into the disjoint union of its labelled parts:

\mathrm{int}\,\gamma\,=\,\mathrm{int}_{1}\,\gamma\,\sqcup\,\mathrm{int}_{2}\,\gamma,\qquad\mathrm{int}_{\#}\,\gamma\,:=\,\bigsqcup_{1\leq j\leq k}A_{j}^{\#}.

(25)

The
type
of
\gamma
is

\mathrm{type}(\gamma)\,:=\,\mathrm{type}(\mathrm{ext}\,\gamma)\,\in\,\{1,2\}.

(26)

Definition 3.6
.

A contour
\gamma
is of
class

n
if
|\mathrm{int}\,\gamma|=n
.

Definition 3.7

(Compatibility of same-type contours)
.

Two contours
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
of the
same type
are
compatible
, written
\gamma_{1}\sim\gamma_{2}
, if their supports satisfy

d_{\infty}(\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2})\,>\,1.

(27)

A family
\Gamma
of contours of the same type is
mutually compatible
if every pair of distinct elements of
\Gamma
is compatible.

Note that
\Gamma
here denotes a family of contours and is not the same as
\Gamma(\omega)
which is a set of union of all contour supports.

Remark 3.8
.

Restricting compatibility to same-type contours is essential. For families containing contours of different types, the existence of a configuration realising the family is not a pairwise property: the labels on the various components of
\mathbb{L}\setminus\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bar{\gamma}
must match globally. For the polymer expansion of Section
4
, only same-type families occur, and Definition
3.7
is precisely what is needed.

Definition 3.9

(External contour)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a family of contours arising from some
\omega
with finite boundary. A contour
\gamma\in\Gamma
is
external in
\Gamma
if there is no
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma\}
with
\bar{\gamma}\subseteq\mathrm{int}\,\gamma^{\prime}
. We denote the set of external contours by
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
.
Let then

\mathrm{ext}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigcap_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma^{\prime}}\mathrm{ext}\gamma^{\prime},\qquad\Lambda^{\mathrm{ext}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Lambda\cap\mathrm{ext}.

(28)

Let
\omega
agree with
\eta^{\#}
outside a finite subset of
\mathbb{L}
, and let
\Gamma
be its associated family of contours. Then every external contour in
\Gamma
has type
\#
.

4
Partition function and polymer representation

Building on the contour formalism of Section
3
, we now derive a polymer representation of the partition function
Z_{\Lambda}^{\#}
from Section
2
. We trace out occupation numbers, decompose the resulting effective Hamiltonian as a sum over occupied micro-clusters, pull out the energy of the reference configuration
\eta^{\#}
, and reorganise the relative partition function as a sum over compatible families of type-
\#
contours.

4.1
Macro-volume conventions

From now on, we assume that the finite volume
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
of Section
2
is a union of complete blocks
B_{i}
from the partition of Section
3.1
. We identify
\Lambda
with a finite subset of the macro-lattice
\mathbb{L}
and write

\Lambda^{\mathrm{mic}}\;:=\;\bigcup_{i\in\Lambda}B_{i}\;\subset\;\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(29)

for the underlying micro-volume. Cardinalities are related by
|\Lambda^{\mathrm{mic}}|=2^{d}\,|\Lambda|
. We define the space

\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\eta^{\#}}=\{\omega\in\Omega^{\mathbb{L}}:\omega_{\Lambda^{c}}=\eta^{\#}\}

(30)

and

\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}=\{\omega\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\eta^{\#}}:d_{\infty}(\Gamma(\omega),\Lambda^{c})>1\}

(31)

A macro-configuration
\omega\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}
is the same data as a
\{0,1\}
-occupation labelling on
\Lambda^{\mathrm{mic}}
with
\eta^{\#}
boundary condition and we will denote a micro configuration corresponding to
\omega
by
\omega_{\text{mic}}
. It can be checked that
H_{\Lambda}^{\#}(n_{\Lambda})
does not depend on
\#
when the occupation label
\omega_{\Lambda}
is a restriction of
\omega\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}
on
\Lambda
. Hence, from now on we will denote it by
H_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})
.

4.2
Effective Hamiltonian via partial trace

For each
\omega_{\text{mic}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}
define the
effective Hamiltonian

\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\text{mic}})\;:=\;-\frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{n_{\Lambda}\sim(\omega_{\text{mic}})_{\Lambda}}e^{-\beta H_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})},.

(32)

Summing (
32
) over
\omega_{\text{mic}}
recovers the partition function:

Z^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\beta)\;=\;\sum_{\omega_{\text{mic}}\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}}e^{-\beta\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\text{mic}})}.

(33)

The remainder of the analysis treats
\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}
as a Hamiltonian on
\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}
.

4.3
Cluster decomposition

For
\omega_{\text{mic}}
, let
\mathcal{C}(\omega_{\text{mic}})
be the set of maximal
nearest-neighbour connected components of
\{x:(\omega_{\text{mic}})_{x}=1\}
— the

occupied clusters
of
\omega_{\text{mic}}
.
For any cluster
C\in\mathcal{C}(\omega_{\text{mic}})
, define the free energy restricted to
C
as

f(C)\;:=\;-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z_{C},\qquad Z_{C}\;:=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{x}\geq 1\\
x\in C\end{subarray}}\exp\!\left(-\beta U\sum_{x\in C}n_{x}\;-\;\beta\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in C\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}\!\!f(n_{x},n_{y})\right).

(34)

Lemma 4.1

(Cluster factorization)
.

For every
\omega_{\text{mic}}
,

\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}(\omega_{\text{mic}})\;=\;\sum_{C\,\in\,\mathcal{C}(\omega_{\text{mic}}),C\subset\Lambda}f(C).

(35)

Proof.

Conditional on
\omega_{\text{mic}}
, the constrained sum factorizes:

\sum_{n_{\Lambda}\sim(\omega_{\text{mic}})_{\Lambda}}e^{-\beta\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}(n_{\Lambda})}\;=\;\prod_{C\,\in\,\mathcal{C}(\omega_{\text{mic}}),C\subset\Lambda}Z_{C}.

(36)

This holds because (i) the on-site term
Un_{x}
is local,
(ii) the interaction term
f(n_{x},n_{y})

Reference energy

In the reference configuration, the alternating pattern of

\Xi^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\beta)\;:=\;e^{\beta\cdot 2^{d-1}|\Lambda|f_{0}}\,Z^{\#}_{\Lambda}(\beta)\;=\;\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{\Lambda}^{\#}}e^{-\beta\bigl(\widetilde{H}_{\Lambda}(\omega)-2^{d-1}|\Lambda|f_{0}\bigr)}.

(39)

Let

(No occupied cluster crosses a contour boundary)

Factorization of

5
Analyzing the boundary term

In this section we prove a Peierls-type lower bound on the surface energy

\|\gamma\|=\widetilde{H}_{\bar{\gamma}}(\omega_{\bar{\gamma}})-\widetilde{H}_{\bar{\gamma}}(\eta^{\#})

of a contour. For
\beta
small enough, we find that

\|\gamma\|\;\geq\;c\,\frac{|\log\beta|}{\beta}\,|\bar{\gamma}|

(46)

for a constant
c>0
depending only on
d
,
U
.

Throughout, we use the cluster decomposition

\widetilde{H}(\omega)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}(\omega)}f(C)

established in Lemma
4.1
.

Recall that

f_{0}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z_{\rm sing}
with

Z_{\rm sing}=\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-\beta Un}=\frac{1}{e^{\beta U}-1}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

As
\beta\to 0
,

f_{0}\;=\;\frac{\log\beta}{\beta}\bigl(1+O(1/\log\beta)\bigr).

(47)

Proof.

Expand
e^{\beta U}-1=\beta U+\tfrac{1}{2}(\beta U)^{2}+O(\beta^{3})
, so

Z_{\rm sing}=\frac{1}{\beta U}\bigl(1+O(\beta)\bigr)
.
Hence
\log Z_{\rm sing}=-\log\beta-\log U+O(\beta)
, and dividing by
-\beta

gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Dimer excess)
.

Given
Z_{\mathrm{dim}}=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}e^{-\beta U(m+n)-\beta f(m,n)}
and
\Delta(\beta)=\frac{1}{\beta}\log\frac{Z_{\mathrm{sing}}^{2}}{Z_{\mathrm{dim}}}
, for
\beta
sufficiently small,

\Delta(\beta)\;\geq\;(2-\alpha)\,\frac{|\log\beta|}{\beta}\,-\,\frac{O(1)}{\beta}.

(48)

In particular
\Delta(\beta)\geq C|\log\beta|/\beta
for any
C\in(1,2-\alpha)
and
\beta
small.

Proof.

Let
E_{m,n}=U(m+n)+f(m,n)
denote the energy of state
(m,n)
.

Z_{\mathrm{dim}}=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\int_{E_{m,n}}^{\infty}\beta e^{-\beta T}\,dT.

(49)

Z_{\mathrm{dim}}=\beta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta T}\left(\sum_{m,n\geq 1:E_{m,n}\leq T}1\right)dT=\beta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta T}N(T)\,dT.

(50)

From (F4), we obtain:

Z_{\mathrm{dim}}\leq K\beta\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta T}T^{\alpha}\,dT=K\Gamma(\alpha+1)\beta^{-\alpha}.

(51)

We get

\frac{Z_{\mathrm{sing}}^{2}}{Z_{\mathrm{dim}}}\geq\frac{1}{KU^{2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\beta^{\alpha-2}(1+O(\beta)).

(52)

\log\frac{Z_{\mathrm{sing}}^{2}}{Z_{\mathrm{dim}}}\geq(\alpha-2)\log\beta+\log\left(\frac{1}{KU^{2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\right)+\log(1 + O(\beta)).

(53)

For sufficiently small
\beta\in(0,1)
, we have
\log\beta<0
, which means we can rewrite
(\alpha-2)\log\beta
strictly as
(2-\alpha)|\log\beta|
. Furthermore, the explicit constant
\log(1 / (K U^2 \Gamma(\alpha+1)))
and the
\log(1 + O(\beta))
expansion are collectively bounded by a constant term
O(1)
.

Dividing the entire expression by
\beta
gives the final desired bound:

\Delta(\beta)\geq\frac{(2-\alpha)|\log\beta|}{\beta}-\frac{O(1)}{\beta}.

(54)

∎

5.1
Cluster excess

Lemma 5.3

(Cluster excess via matchings)
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a connected occupied cluster and let
M
be a matching in the nearest-neighbour edge set of
C
. Then

\widetilde{f}(C)\;\geq\;|C|\,f_{0}\,+\,|M|\,\Delta(\beta).

(55)

Proof.

Since
f\geq 0
, we drop all interaction terms not lying in

:

Z_{C}\;\leq\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{x}\geq 1\\
x\in C\end{subarray}}\exp\biggl(-\beta U\!\sum_{x\in C}n_{x}\,-\,\beta\!\sum_{\{a,b\}\in M}\!\!f(n_{a},n_{b})\biggr).

(56)

The matching

is by definition a set of pairwise disjoint edges, so the sum factorises over matched pairs and unmatched sites:

Z_{C}\;\leq\;Z_{\mathrm{dim}}^{|M|}\,Z_{\mathrm{sing}}^{|C|-2|M|}.

(57)

Taking
-\beta^{-1}\log
and using

gives (
55
).
∎

5.2

A macro-site

is of

\#

\omega

Proof.

Let

be a maximum matching in the occupied subgraph of

and Lemma

If

is type-

and

Suppose now

are the only independent sets of size

in the hypercube graph

Case 1:

for either

•

If

6.1

Proof.

The polymer partition function

fits into the abstract framework
of
[
5
]
. The key point is that the exponential weight bound
on contours implies, via the Kotecký–Preiss criterion, that the contribution
of contours crossing the boundary

[

for the complete proof.

For each

\Xi^{\#}_{n}(\Lambda):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Gamma\subset\mathcal{C}^{\#}_{\leq n}\\
\text{compatible in }\Lambda\end{subarray}}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}w^{\#}(\gamma),

(69)

where

is the collection of type-

contours of class at most

and it forms a subcollection of
\mathcal{C}^{\#}

6.2
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

Lemma 6.2
.

For the collection
\mathcal{C}^{\#}_{\leq n}
, we have

.

Proof.

Consider the lattice translation

,

From Theorem
