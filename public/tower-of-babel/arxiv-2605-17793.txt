Convergence Analysis of Two Alternating Iterative Schemes for Tucker Decomposition
Ren-Cang Li, Li Wang, Mei Yang

Abstract
The higher-order orthogonal iteration (HOOI) and the alternating subspace iteration (ASI) are two popular numerical methods for computing the Tucker decomposition of a multiple-mode tensor. Xu [ Linear and Multilinear Algebra , 66(11):2247–2265, 2018] proposed a variation of HOOI, called the greedy HOOI, which has an extra alignment action between consecutive approximations. Kroonenberg and De Leeuw [ Psychometrika , 45(1):69–97, 1980] analyzed the convergence of ASI but their analysis has gaps. These analysis were for a real tensor only. In this paper, we present detailed convergence analysis of the two methods that is applicable to a complex tensor with a real tensor being a special case, and it is shown both methods are globally convergent to stationary points under mild conditions while the objective function monotonically increases. Numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the convergence behavior of the methods. Keywords: Tensor; Tucker Decomposition; coupled NEPv; coupled NPDo; SCF; convergence Mathematics Subject Classification 58C40; 65F30; 65H17; 65K05; 90C26

Previously in HOOI,

[

Kroonenberg and De Leeuw

[

simply used the initial guess as their final solution for TD,

There are a number of ways to choose

Computed

\rank(H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)})=k_{\ell}
,
P_{\ell}^{(j+1)}
by the polar decomposition is unique and it by the thin QR decomposition can also be
uniquely specified, too, by enforcing the diagonal entries of

positive.

, and
let
(\widehat{P}_{*1},\widehat{P}_{*2},\ldots,\widehat{P}_{*\,m-1})
be an accumulation point of
\{(P_{1}^{(j+1)},P_{2}^{(j+1)},\ldots,P_{m-1}^{(j+1)})\}_{j\in\mathbb{I}}
, and accordingly keep
notation
H_{*\ell}
and
\widehat{H}_{*\ell}
be as in (
4.8
).
The following statements hold.

(a)

The sequence
\{f(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is monotonically increasing and convergent;

(b)

The equations in (
4.9
) hold and each is a polar decomposition, i.e.,

\Lambda_{*\ell}=P_{*\ell}^{\HH}\widehat{H}_{*\ell}\,P_{*\ell}\succeq 0
for
1\leq\ell\leq m
.

(c)

In item
(b)
, if, for
1\leq\ell\leq m
,

\rank(H_{*\ell}P_{*\ell})=k_{\ell},

(5.3)

then
\widehat{P}_{*\ell}=P_{*\ell}
for
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
, leading to (
4.11
)
with all
\Lambda_{*\ell}=P_{*\ell}^{\HH}H_{*\ell}\,P_{*\ell}\succ 0
for
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
and
\Lambda_{*m}\succeq 0
.

(d)

If
(P_{*1},P_{*2},\ldots,P_{*m})
is an isolated accumulation point of
\{(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}

and if, besides (
5.3
) for
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
, also

\rank(H_{*m}P_{*m})=k_{m},

(5.4)

then the entire sequence
\{(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
converges to
(P_{*1},P_{*2},\ldots,P_{*m})
.

(e)

We have
2m
convergent series, for
1\leq\ell\leq m
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{\min}(H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)})\,\big\|\sin\Theta\big({\cal R}(P_{\ell}^{(j+1)}),{\cal R}(P_{\ell}^{(j)})\big)\big\|_{\F}^{2}

\displaystyle<\infty,

(5.5a)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{\min}(H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)})\,\frac{\big\|H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}-P_{\ell}^{(j)}\Lambda_{\ell}^{(j)}\big\|_{\F}^{2}}{\big\|H_{\ell}^{(j)}\big\|_{\F}^{2}}

\displaystyle<\infty,

(5.5b)

where
\Lambda_{\ell}^{(j)}=\big[P_{\ell}^{(j)}\big]^{\HH}H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{C}^{k_{\ell}\times k_{\ell}}

As a corollary of Theorem
5.1
(e),
if
\sigma_{\min}(H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)})
is eventually bounded below away from
0

uniformly,
then

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\big\|H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}-P_{\ell}^{(j)}\Lambda_{\ell}^{(j)}\big\|_{\F}}{\big\|H_{\ell}^{(j)}\big\|_{\F}}=0,

(5.6)

namely, increasingly
H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}\approx P_{\ell}^{(j)}\Lambda_{\ell}^{(j)}

towards a polar decomposition of
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
. This
means that
(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})
becomes a more and more accurate approximate solution
to the KKT condition (
3.6
),
even in the absence of knowing whether the entire sequence
\{(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
converges or not.

Comparing
Theorem

5.1
with
Theorem

4.1
, we point out two subtle differences:

(1)

Although items (b,c) of both theorems claim (
4.9
) and (
4.11
),
it is only claimed that all
\Lambda_{*\ell}\succeq 0

(\succ 0
) in
Theorem

5.1
(b,c)
whereas in
Theorem

4.1
(b,c) each
\eig(\Lambda_{*\ell})
consists of
k_{\ell}
largest
eigenvalues of
\widehat{H}_{*\ell}\succeq 0
or
H_{*\ell}\succ 0
, which is stronger than simply
\Lambda_{*\ell}\succeq 0
, that holds by default
because
\widehat{H}_{*\ell}\succeq 0
and
H_{*\ell}\succ 0
always;

(2)

There is no need to perform an alignment between
P_{\ell}^{(j)}
and
P_{*\ell}
in
Theorem

5.1
(d) but there is one in
Theorem

4.1
(d). This is due to the fact that the orthonormal polar factor is
unique under the full column-rank assumption.

Remark 5.1
.

Earlier, we pointed out
Kroonenberg and De Leeuw
[
13
]
implicitly used orthonormal polar factor in the form (
5.1
)
(for the case
m=3
) but
without mentioning the term. They also numerically realize (
5.1
) through
the eigendecomposition of
\big[P_{\ell}^{(j)}\big]^{\HH}\big[H_{\ell}^{(j)}\big]^{2}P_{\ell}^{(j)}
.
It can be seen that
P_{\ell}^{(j+1)}
as in (
5.1
)
is indeed the orthonormal polar factor of
H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}
, and hence the resulting method
is essentially the same method as
Algorithm

1
but can encounter subtle numerical issues
when
\big[P_{\ell}^{(j)}\big]^{\HH}\big[H_{\ell}^{(j)}\big]^{2}P_{\ell}^{(j)}
is singular or nearly singular. What we did in
Algorithm

1
is to compute
orthonormal polar factor via thin SVD, completely getting around any numerical singularity issue.
Kroonenberg and De Leeuw
[
13
]
conducted their convergence analysis with (
5.1
) for updating and their conclusions are essentially the ones in
Theorem

5.1
(a,d) under the conditions that all involved matrices
H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}
have full column ranks, which would be satisfied most likely in actual computation
for large scale tensors as often
k_{\ell}\ll n_{\ell}
.
However, carefully examining their proofs reveals
that they actually needed to assume all
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
to be positive definite, not just each
H_{\ell}^{(j)}P_{\ell}^{(j)}
having full column rank,
because they used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with respect to the

H_{\ell}^{(j)}
-inner product and hence they needed the positive definiteness of
H_{\ell}^{(j)}

in order to apply the result of
[
6
]
.
A necessary
condition for
H_{\ell}^{(j)}\succ 0
is
n_{\ell}\leq k_{i_{1}}\cdots k_{i_{m-1}}
, which is problematic for large scale tensor. To see that, one needs to look at no further
than the extreme case: all
k_{\ell}=1
and then
\rank(H_{\ell}^{(j)})=1
always
and hence
H_{\ell}^{(j)}
is singular if
n_{\ell}>1
.
Lastly,
Theorem

5.1
does not distinguish real or complex tensors and contains much deeper results, for example,
the convergence series in item (d).

7
Concluding Remarks

We have performed detailed convergence analysis on two popular methods, the higher-order orthogonal iteration (HOOI)
[
3
]
and the alternating subspace iteration (ASI)
[
13
]
, for computing the Tucker decomposition
of a multiple-mode tensor. Previous convergence analysis on ASI was done by
Kroonenberg and De Leeuw
[
13
]
but their analysis has gaps, as we discussed in

Remark

5.1
. Also our
Theorem

5.1
contains results that are much deeper than what were concluded in
[
13
]
.
Xu
[
31
]
performed convergence analysis on the so-called greedy HOOI, which adds an extra alignment on
P_{\ell}^{(j+1)}

every time it is computed.
Xu’s analysis relies on the Kurdyka–Lojasiewicz theory
for semi-algebraic functions.
Our analysis is more accessible to the numerical linear algebra community, self-contained, and
it treats both real and complex tensors without distinction.

Both
Algorithms

1
and
1
use the Gauss-Seidel-type updating. Although not detailed,
the Jacobi-type updating works, too, especially for the case when tensor
B
nearly fits the TD model.
In the Jacobi-type updating, at iteration
j
,
C_{\ell}(\cdots)
for

1\leq\ell\leq m
can be evaluated at
(P_{1}^{(j)},P_{2}^{(j)},\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})
in paralell, instead of
sequentially at
(P_{1}^{(j+1)},\ldots,P_{\ell-1}^{(j+1)},P_{\ell}^{(j)}\ldots,P_{m}^{(j)})
. We experimented
with the Jacobi-type updating and found that it still works but for very small
\eta
(say
\eta\leq 2^{-5}
or smaller).
Both
Algorithms

1
and
1
can be accelerated by employing the LOCG (locally optimal conjugate gradient) technique.
We have also done experments on that and found HOOI combined with LOCG did not perform better, but ASI with LOCG did.
The latter is a special case of what is in
[
20
]
, to which the reader is referred
for detail.
