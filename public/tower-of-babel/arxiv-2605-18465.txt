Approximation of Attractors of Nonautonomous Lattice Dynamical Systems
David Cheban, Andrei Sultan

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to study the finite-dimensional approximations of the nonautonomous lattice dynamical systems of the form u i ′ = ν ⁡ ( u i − 1 − 2 ​ u i + u i + 1 ) − λ ​ u i + F ⁡ ( u i ) + f i ​ ( t ) ​ ( i ∈ ℤ ) ​ ( ∗ ) u_{i}^{\prime}=\nu(u_{i-1}-2u_{i}+u_{i+1})-\lambda u_{i}+F(u_{i})+f_{i}(t)\ (i\in\mathbb{Z})\ (*) . We show that the finite-dimensional approximations for (*) are uniformly dissipative. The upper semi-continuous convergence of the attractors of the finite-dimensional approximations is established.

Shift Dynamical Systems and Finite Dimensional
Approximations

Lemma 4.1
.

The following statements hold:

1.

\mathfrak{f}^{n}\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
for
every
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

2.

the mapping
P_{n}:C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})\to C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
defined by

P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}):=\mathfrak{f}^{n}
possesses the following
properties:

2.1

P_{n}
is continuous;

2.2

(11)

P_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=\sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))

for all
(h,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{R}\times C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
, i.e., the mapping
P_{n}
is a homomorphism of the
shift dynamical system
(C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2}),\mathbb{R},\sigma)

into the shift dynamical system
(C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}),\mathbb{R},\sigma)
.

3.

P_{n}(h(\mathfrak{f}))\subseteq H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))

for every
(n,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{N}\times C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
;

4.

if the function
\mathfrak{f}
is Lagrange
stable, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

4.1

(12)

P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))\ \ \mbox{and}

4.2

the function
\mathfrak{f}^{n}
is also Lagrange
stable.

Proof.

The first statement of Lemma is evident.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
,
\{\mathfrak{f}_{k}\}=\{(f_{k,i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\}\subset C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
\mathfrak{f}_{k}\to\mathfrak{f}
, i.e., for every
L>0
we have

(13)

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Note that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})(t)-P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)|^{2}=\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{|i|\leq n}|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)-P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)|^{2}\leq

(14)

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Passing to the limit in (
4
) as

k\to\infty
and taking into account (
13
) we obtain

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})(t)-P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)|^{2}=0

for every
L>0
. This means that
P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})\to P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})
in the space
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})

as
k\to\infty
and, consequently, the mapping
P_{n}
is
continuous.

To establish the relation (
11
) we note that

P_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=(f^{(h)}_{i})_{|i|\leq n}\ \mbox{and}\ \sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=\sigma(h,(f_{i})_{|i|\leq n})=(f^{(h)}_{i})_{|i|\leq n}

and, consequently,

P_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=\sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
for
all
(h,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{R}\times C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
.

If
\mathfrak{g}\in H(\mathfrak{f})
, then there exists a sequence

\{h_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f})\to\mathfrak{g}
as
k\to\infty
. Note that

(15)

P_{n}(\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f}))\to P_{n}(\mathfrak{g})

as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand by the equality (
11
)
we have

(16)

\sigma(h_{k},P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=P_{n}(\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f}))

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Passing to the limit in (
16
) as

k\to\infty
and taking into account (
15
) we obtain

(17)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sigma(h_{k},P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=P_{n}(\mathfrak{g}),

i.e.,
P_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\in H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
. This means
that
P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))\subseteq H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
.

Let now
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
be Lagrange stable
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\in H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
then
there exists a sequence
\{h_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\sigma(h_{k},P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h_{k})}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

as
k\to\infty
. Since the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable then without loss of generality we
can assume that the sequence
\{\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})}\}\subset C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
converges. Denote its limit by

\mathfrak{g}:=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})}
then
taking into account (
16
)-(
17
) we obtain

P_{n}(\mathfrak{g})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sigma(h_{k},P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},

i.e.,
P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
.

Finally, we note that if the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

by (
12
) we have

P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
. By the second
statement of Lemma the mapping
P_{n}:C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})\to C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{(2n+1)})
is continuous and, consequently,
the set
H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))
is a
compact subset of
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
because the set

H(\mathfrak{f})
is compact. Lemma is completely proved.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

\mathfrak{f}^{n}\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
for
every
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

the mapping
H_{n}:C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})\to C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
defined by

H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}):=\mathfrak{f}^{n}
possesses the following
properties:

(a)

H_{n}
is continuous;

(b)

(18)

H_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=\sigma(h,H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))

for all
(h,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{R}\times C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
, i.e., the mapping
H_{n}
is a homomorphism of the
shift dynamical system
(C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2}),\mathbb{R},\sigma)

into the shift dynamical system
(C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}),\mathbb{R},\sigma)
.

(iii)

P_{n}(h(\mathfrak{f}))\subseteq H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
for
every
(n,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{N}\times C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
;

(iv)

if the function
\mathfrak{f}
is Lagrange stable, then for
every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(a)

H_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))\ \ \mbox{and}

(b)

the function
\mathfrak{f}^{n}
is also Lagrange stable.

Proof.

The first statement of Lemma is trivial.

Let
\mathfrak{f}=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
,
\{\mathfrak{f}_{k}\}=\{(f_{k,i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
\mathfrak{f}_{k}\to\mathfrak{f}
, i.e., for every
L>0
we have

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
.

Since

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)|^{2}=\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{|i|\leq n}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{|i|\leq n-1}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)|^{2}+

(19)

\displaystyle\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum_{|i|=n}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)|^{2}

and

(20)

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{|i|=n}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})_{i}(t)|^{2}=\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{|i|=n}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}=

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|f_{k,n+1}(t)-f_{n+1}(t)|^{2}+\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|f_{k,-n-1}(t)-f_{-n-1}(t)|^{2}\leq

2\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{|i|\leq n+1}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}\leq 2\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{k,i}(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}=

2\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\|\mathfrak{f}_{k}(t)-\mathfrak{f}(t)\|\to 0

as
k\to\infty
.

From (
4
) and (
20
) we receive

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}|H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})(t)-H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)|^{2}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
for every
L>0
, i.e.,
H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}_{k})\to H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})
in the space
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})

and, consequently, the mapping
H_{n}
is continuous.

To prove (
18
) we note that

H_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}^{(h)})(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\!\!f_{i}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;|i|\leq n-1\\[5.69054pt]
&\!\!f_{-n-1}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;i=n\ \ \mbox{and}\\[5.69054pt]
&\!\!f_{n+1}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;i=-n\end{array}\right.

and

\sigma(h,H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\!\!f_{i}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;|i|\leq n-1\\[5.69054pt]
&\!\!f_{-n-1}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;i=n\ \ \mbox{and}\\[5.69054pt]
&\!\!f_{n+1}^{(h)}(t),\;\mbox{if}\;i=-n\end{array}\right.

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and, consequently,

H_{n}(\sigma(h,\mathfrak{f}))=\sigma(h,H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
for
every
(h,\mathfrak{f})\in\mathbb{R}\times C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}\in H(\mathfrak{f})
then there exists a sequence

\{h_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that
\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f})\to\mathfrak{g}
as
k\to\infty
. Note that

(21)

H_{n}(\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f}))\to H_{n}(\mathfrak{g})

as
k\to\infty
. By the equality (
18
) we have

(22)

\sigma(h_{k},H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=H_{n}(\sigma(h_{k},\mathfrak{f}))

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Passing to the limit in (
22
)
as
k\to\infty
and taking into account (
21
) we obtain

(23)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sigma(h_{k},H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=H_{n}(\mathfrak{g}),

i.e.,
H_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\in H(H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
. This means
that
H_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))\subseteq H(H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
.

If the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is
Lagrange stable, then for every
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\in H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
there exists a sequence
\{h_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

H_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h_{k})}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

as
k\to\infty
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
the sequence
\{\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})}\}\subset C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
converges because the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable. Denote its limit by

\mathfrak{g}:=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})}
then
taking into account (
22
)-(
23
) we obtain

H_{n}(\mathfrak{g})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}^{(h_{k})})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\sigma(h_{k},H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},

i.e.,
P_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
.

We note that if the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

by (
12
) we have

H_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))=H(H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))
. By the second
statement of Lemma the mapping
H_{n}:C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})\to C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
is continuous and, consequently,
the set
H(H_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=H_{n}(H(\mathfrak{f}))
is a
compact subset of
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{2n+1})
because the set

H(\mathfrak{f})
is compact. Lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
L

be a given positive number then for every
\varepsilon>0
there
exists a natural number
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum\limits_{|i|>k_{\varepsilon}}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}<\varepsilon

for every
t\in[-L,L]
, i.e.,

(24)

\sup\limits_{|t|\leq L}R_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)=0,

where

R_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)=\sum\limits_{|i|\geq n+1}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
L>0

then
Q_{L}:=\mathfrak{f}([-L,L])
is a compact subset of

\ell_{2}
. By Theorem 5.25
[
15
, Ch.V, p.167]
for every

\varepsilon>0
there exists a number
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum\limits_{|i|>k_{\varepsilon}}|w_{i}|^{2}<\varepsilon

for all
w\in Q_{L}
. Let now
t\in[-L,L]
then for

w:=\mathfrak{f}(t)\in Q_{L}=\mathfrak{f}([-L,L])
we have

\sum\limits_{|i|>k_{\varepsilon}}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}=\sum\limits_{|i|>k_{\varepsilon}}|w_{i}|^{2}<\varepsilon.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that the relation (
24
)
remains true if we replace
\sup
by
\max
because the function

R_{n}(\mathfrak{f})\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where

R_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)=\sum\limits_{|i|\geq n+1}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}=\|\mathfrak{f}(t)\|^{2}-\sum\limits_{|i|\leq n}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}

for all
(n,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

for every
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
we have

P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})\to\mathfrak{f}
in
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})

as
n\to\infty
;

(ii)

if the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable, then

H(K):=\overline{\{\sigma(h,\mathfrak{g})|\ h\in\mathbb{R},\ \mathfrak{g}\in K\}}

is a compact subset of
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
, where

K:=\{P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})|\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
then by Lemma
4.3
we have

(25)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{|i|\geq n+1}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}=0.

Note that

(26)

\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})(t)-\mathfrak{f}(t)\|^{2}=\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|P_{n,i}(f)(t)-f_{i}(t)|^{2}=\max\limits_{|t|\leq L}\sum\limits_{|i|\geq n+1}|f_{i}(t)|^{2}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Passing to the limit in (
26
)
and taking into account (
25
) we obtain that

P_{n}\mathfrak{f}\to\mathfrak{f}
in the space
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
.

Assume that the function
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})

is Lagrange stable. By Lemma
1.2
the following statements
hold:

(i)

there exists a compact subset
Q\subset\ell_{2}
such that

\mathfrak{f}(\mathbb{R})\subset Q;

(ii)

for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(27)

|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta\ \ \mbox{implies}\ \ \|f(t_{1})-f(t_{2})\|^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}/2.

Since the subset
\mathfrak{f}(\mathbb{R})\subset\ell_{2}
is
precompact then by Theorem 5.25
[
15
, Ch.V, p.167]
for

\varepsilon>0
there exists a number
k_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(28)

\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|f_{i}(h)|^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}/8

for all
h\in\mathbb{R}
.

Consider the family of functions
\mathbb{F}:=\{\sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))|\ (h,n)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}\}
. Let
\psi\in\mathbb{F}
then there exists

(h_{0},n_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
\psi=\sigma(h_{0},P_{n_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}))
. Note that

(29)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|\psi(\tau)|^{2}=\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|\sigma(h_{0},P_{n_{0}}(\mathfrak{f}))(\tau)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n_{0}}|f^{(h)}_{i}(\tau)|^{2}=\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n_{0}}|f_{i}(h_{0}+\tau)|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|f_{i}(h_{0}+\tau)|^{2}

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. From (
28
) and (
29
)
we receive

\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|\psi(\tau)|^{2}<\varepsilon

for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and, consequently,
\mathbb{F}(\mathbb{R}):=\{\psi(\tau)|\ \psi\in\mathbb{F},\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a
precompct subset of
\ell_{2}
.

Let
\varepsilon
be an arbitrary positive number and
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
the number figuring in (
27
). If

\psi\in\mathbb{F}
, then there exist
h\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\psi=\sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))=P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}
. Let

t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
be two arbitrary numbers with

|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta
.

We will consider two cases:

a.
n<k_{\varepsilon}
, then it is clear that there exists

0<\delta_{1}(\varepsilon)<\delta(\varepsilon)
such that

|t_{1}-t_{2}|<\delta_{1}(\varepsilon)
implies

\|P_{m}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}(t_{1})-P_{m}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}(t_{2})\|<\varepsilon

for every
1\leq m\leq n
.

b. If
n\geq k_{\varepsilon}
, then taking into account
(
27
)-(
28
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|\psi(t_{1})-\psi(t_{2})\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|\psi_{i}(t_{1})-\psi_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|i|\leq k_{\varepsilon}-1}|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})-P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}+\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})-P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|i|\leq k_{\varepsilon}-1}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})-f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}+\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})-f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|i|\leq k_{\varepsilon}-1}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})-f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}+2(\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})|^{2}+|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2})\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}-1}|f_{i}(t_{1})-f_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}+2(\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{1})|^{2}+\sum\limits_{|i|\geq k_{\varepsilon}}|f^{(h)}_{i}(t_{2})|^{2}))<

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}/2+2(\varepsilon^{2}/8+\varepsilon^{2}/8)=\varepsilon^{2}.

Now to finish the proof of Lemma it suffices to apply Lemma

1.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
be a Lagrange stable
function,
K:=\{P_{n}(\mathfrak{f})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}}
and

H(K):=\overline{\{\sigma(h,P_{n}(\mathfrak{f}))|\ h\in\mathbb{R},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}\}}
. Then there exists a positive constant

C
such that

\|\mathfrak{g}(t)\|\leq C

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{g}\in H(K)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
the set
H(K)
is a compact in
the space
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
. By Lemma
1.2
there
exists a compact
Q
from
\ell_{2}
such that

\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq Q

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{g}\in H(K)
. Then we have

C:=\sup\{\|\mathfrak{g}(t)\|:\ t\in\mathbb{R},\ \mathfrak{g}\in H(K)\}<+\infty.

Lemma is proved.
∎

5.
Cocycles Generated by Nonautonomous Lattice Dynamical
Systems

Below we use the following conditions.

Condition
(
C1
). The function

\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{B})
and it is
translation-compact, i.e., the set
\{\mathfrak{f}^{h}|\ h\in\mathbb{R}\}
is pre-compact in the space
C(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{B})
.

Condition
(
C2
). The function
F\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets
and
F(0)=0
.

Denote by
\widetilde{F}:\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}
the Nemytskii
operator generated by
F
, i.e.,

\widetilde{F}(\xi)_{i}:=F(\xi_{i})
for any
i\in\mathfrak{N}
.

Condition
(
C3
).
sF(s)\leq-\alpha s^{2}

for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A function
F\in C(Y\times\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{B})
is said to be globally Lipschitzian (respectively locally
Lipschitzian) w.r.t. variable
u\in\mathfrak{B}

uniformly w.r.t.
y\in Y
if there exists a positive
constant
L
(for any bounded set
B\subset\mathfrak{B}
there
exists a constant
L_{B}
) such that

(30)

\|F(y,u_{1})-F(y,u_{2})\|\leq L\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|

(respectively,

(31)

\|F(y,v_{1})-F(y,v_{2})\|\leq L_{B}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|)

for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
and
y\in Y
(respectively
v_{1},v_{2}\in B\subset\mathfrak{B}
and
y\in Y
).

Definition 5.2
.

The smallest constant
L
(respectively
L_{B}
) with the property
(
30
) (respectively, (

) we obtain

(45)

2\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)u_{i}f(u_{i})+2\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{i}(t)u_{i}\leq-\alpha\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|u_{i}|^{2}+

\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{n\geq|i|\geq k}|\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}.

Using the estimates (
42
) and (
45
) in the equation
(
41
) gives

(46)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|u_{i}|^{2}+\alpha\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|u_{i}|^{2}\leq\nu\frac{4C_{0}\|Q\|^{2}}{k}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{n\geq|i|\geq k}|\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}

Since
H(K)
(
K=\{\mathfrak{f}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
) is a
compact subset in the space
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
then by Lemma

1.2
there exists a compact subset
Q
from
\ell_{2}

such that
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{R})\subset Q
for all

\mathfrak{g}\in H(K)
. In particular for every
\varepsilon>0

there exists a natural number
k(\varepsilon)\leq n
such that

(47)

\sum\limits_{n\geq|i|\geq k(\varepsilon)}|v_{i}|^{2}<\varepsilon

for all
v\in\overline{\mathfrak{f}^{n}(\mathbb{R})}
. Note that

H(\mathfrak{f}^{n})\subseteq H(K)
and, consequently,

(48)

\mathfrak{f}^{n}(t)\in\overline{\mathfrak{f}^{n}(\mathbb{R})}\subseteq Q.

From (
47
) and (
48
) we obtain

\sum\limits_{n\geq|i|\geq k(\varepsilon)}|\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}<\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that
\mathfrak{f}^{n}(t)\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{R})}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{f}^{n}(\mathbb{R})}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and, consequently, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
k_{\varepsilon}\leq n
such that

\nu\frac{4C_{0}\|Q\|^{2}}{k}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{n\geq|i|\geq k}|\mathfrak{f}^{n}_{i}(t)|^{2}\leq\varepsilon,\quad n\geq k\geq k(\varepsilon)

for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The inequality (
46
) along with the relation above give

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|u_{i}|^{2}+\alpha\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|u_{i}|^{2}\leq\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Then, Gronwall’s lemma implies that

\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|\varphi_{n}(t,u_{0},\mathfrak{f}^{n})_{i}|^{2}\leq e^{-\alpha t}\sum_{|i|\leq N}\xi_{k}(|i|)|v_{i}|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}\leq e^{-\alpha t}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}.

Hence for every
v\in Q
,

\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)|\varphi(t,u_{0},\mathfrak{f}^{n})_{i}|^{2}\leq e^{-\alpha t}\|Q\|^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}.

and therefore

(49)

\sum_{|i|\leq n}\xi_{k}(|i|)\left|\varphi(t,u_{0},\mathfrak{f}^{n})_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon}{\alpha},\quad\text{for }t\geq T(\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{\alpha}\ln\frac{\alpha\|Q\|^{2}}{\varepsilon}.

This means that the cocycle
\langle\ell_{2},\varphi_{n},(H(\mathfrak{f}^{n}),\mathbb{R},\sigma)\rangle
is asymptotic tails on the absorbing set
Q
.
Lemma is proved.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
6.3

the set

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\{I^{n}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}|\ \widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\in H(\mathfrak{f}^{n})\}

.

Proof.

Let

and

. Since

then there exists a point

(
49
). Thus we obtain

(51)

\|v\|^{2}=\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n}|w_{i}|^{2}=\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n}|\varphi(T(\varepsilon),w,\sigma(-T(\varepsilon),\mathfrak{g}))|^{2}.

) and (

) one has

(52)

\sum\limits_{k_{\varepsilon}\leq|i|\leq n}|w_{i}|^{2}<\varepsilon.

According to Theorem 5.25
[

, Ch.V,p.167]

. The Corollary is proved.

7.
Upper Semi-continuous Convergence of the
Finite-dimensional Attractors

Below we give a Lemma which play an important role in the proof of
the upper semi-continuity of the global attractor.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume that

(53)

F(s)s\leq 0

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathfrak{f}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and
v_{0,n}\in I^{n}_{\mathfrak{f}^{n}}
. Then there
exists a subsequence
\{v_{0,n_{k}}\}
of
\{v_{0,n}\}
and
u\in I_{\mathfrak{f}}
such that
v_{0,n_{k}}
converges to
u
in

\ell_{2}
.

Proof.

We will prove this statement by slight modifications
of the reasoning from the work
[
2
]
(see the proof of
Lemma 4.3).

Let
\nu_{n}(t)=\varphi_{n}(t,v_{0,n},\mathfrak{f}^{n})
be the
solution of (
9
) with the initial condition (
10
).
Since
v_{0,n}\in I^{n}_{\mathfrak{f}^{n}}
then
\nu_{n}(t)\in I^{n}_{\sigma(t,\mathfrak{f}^{n})}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then it follows from (
9
) that

(54)

\|\nu_{n}(t)\|\leq M

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By (
9
) we
get

(55)

\|\nu_{n}^{\prime}(t)\|\leq\nu\|A_{n}\nu_{n}(t)\|=\lambda\|\nu_{n}(t)\|\|f(\nu_{n}(t))\|+\|\mathfrak{f}(t)\|.

Note that

(56)

\nu\|A_{n}\nu_{n}(t)\|\leq 4\nu\|\nu_{n}(t)\|\leq 4\nu M

and

(57)

\|f(\nu_{n}(t))\|=\|f(\nu_{n}(t))-f(0)\|\leq C\|\nu_{n}(t)\|\leq CM

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.

From (
55
)-(
57
) it follows that

(58)

\|\nu^{\prime}_{n}(t)\|\leq C

and, consequently,

(59)

\|\nu_{n}(t)-\nu_{n}(s)\|\leq C|t-s|

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We shell prove that the sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
precompact in
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
. By Lemma
1.2
to
prove that
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is precompact it is
sufficient to show that the following two conditions are
fulfilled:

(i)

there exists a compact subset
Q
from
\ell_{2}
such that

\nu_{n}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq Q
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

the functional sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
uniformly equi-continuous.

To this end we note that from (
59
)
it follows that the sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
uniformly equi-continuous.

Finally, we note that by Corollary
6.4
the set

Q:=\overline{\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}I^{n}}

is compact in
\ell_{2}
, where

I^{n}:=\{I^{n}_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}}|\ \widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\in H(\mathfrak{f}^{n})\}
. Since

\nu_{n}(t)\in I^{n}_{\sigma(t,\mathfrak{f}^{n})}\subseteq Q
for
all
(n,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}
, then
\nu_{n}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq Q
fr every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
6.3
the
functional sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is precompact
in
C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
and, consequently, there exists a
subsequence
\{\nu_{n_{k}}\}\subset C(\mathbb{R},l_{2})
and
u\in C(\mathbb{R},l_{2})
such that

(60)

\nu_{n_{k}}\to u\ \ (\mbox{in the space}\ C(\mathbb{R},l_{2}))

as
k\to\infty
.

By (
54
) we get

(61)

\|u(t)\|\leq C

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We now prove
u(0)\in I_{\mathfrak{f}}
.
To this end, we show that
u
is a solution to (
9
) and
(
10
). For simplicity, we denote by
\{\nu_{n}\}
the
sequence
\{\nu_{n_{k}}\}
in (
60
). It follows from
(
58
) that

(62)

\dot{\nu}_{n}\to\dot{u}\ \mbox{in}\ L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})\ \mbox{weak star}.

For fixe
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
n>|i|
. Since

\nu_{n}(t)=\varphi_{n}(t,v_{0,n},\mathfrak{f}^{n})
is the
solution to (
9
) and (
10
), we have

(63)

\dot{\nu}_{n,i}=-\nu(-\nu_{n,i-1}+2\nu_{n,i}-\nu_{n.i+1}--\lambda\nu_{n,i}-F(\nu_{n,i})+\mathfrak{f}_{i}(t),\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then for each

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{\nu}_{n,i}(t)\psi(t)dt=-\nu\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}(-\nu_{n,i-1}(t)+2\nu_{n,i}(t)-\nu_{n,i+1}(t))\psi(t)dt-

\lambda\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\nu_{n,i}(t)\psi(t)dt-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}F(\nu_{n,i}(t))\psi(t)dt+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{f}_{i}(t)\psi(t)dt,

where by

\Big|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}F(\nu_{n,i}(t))\psi(t)dt-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}F(u_{i}(t))\psi(t)dt\Big|\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|F(\nu_{n,i}(t))-F(u_{i}(t))||\psi(t)|dt\leq

\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|F^{\prime}(\xi_{i})|\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|v^{n}_{i}(t)-u_{i}(t)|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi(t)|dt\to 0

Finally, we state the main result of this section.

Theorem 7.2
.

Assume that (5) holds and
\mathfrak{f}\in C(\mathbb{R},\ell_{2})
is Lagrange stable.
Then we have

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\beta(I^{n}_{\mathfrak{f}^{n}},I_{\mathfrak{f}})=0.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If the conclusion is not true,
then there exist a sequence
u^{n_{k}}\in I^{n_{k}}_{\mathfrak{f}^{n_{k}}}
and
\delta>0
such that

(66)

\beta(u^{n_{k}},I_{\mathfrak{f}})\geq\delta.

On the other hand, by Lemma
7.1
, there exists a
subsequence
u^{n_{k_{m}}}
of
u^{n_{k}}
such that

\beta(u^{n_{k_{m}}},I_{\mathfrak{f}})\to 0

which contradicts (
66
). The theorem is completely
proved.
∎
