Efficient characterization of general Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill qubits Preprint: APS/123-QED
Vojtěch Kuchař, Petr Marek

Abstract
Practical utilization of Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) qubits requires not only the preparation of logical basis states, but also the ability to prepare and evaluate arbitrary logical qubit superpositions. Currently, this is typically done via quantum state tomography, which is resource-intensive. We introduce a family of positive semidefinite Hermitian operators, one for each point on the logical Bloch sphere, whose unique zero-eigenvalue ground states are the corresponding ideal GKP qubit states. We show that the expectation value of each operator serves as a witness of non-Gaussianity, and corresponds to twice the logical infidelity for states in the ideal logical GKP subspace. Furthermore, the truncated finite-dimensional counterparts of these operators yield physical approximations of arbitrary logical GKP states as their ground states. The evaluation of the proposed operators requires only three quadrature measurements, making this framework practical for both the experimental characterization and numerical optimization of GKP state preparation circuits.



Bosonic systems, such as modes of traveling light, are considered as a candidate for the realization of quantum computation

;
Asavanant and Furusawa 1 25
. Their advantage lies in their processing speed and the ability to interact: with the basic tools of linear optics it is possible to generate entangled states of practically unlimited size
Yoshikawa et al. 9 01
;
Asavanant et al. 0 18
;
Larsen et al. 8 11
;
Aghaee Rad et al. 2 27
. The remaining open challenges lie in the preparation and manipulation of the logical qubits

;
Hastrup and Andersen 4 27
;
Endo et al. 4 10
;
Konno et al. 1 19
;
Larsen et al. 6 19
;
Endo et al. 2025

There are several possible approaches towards encoding information in such systems, the Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) code is favored by many due to its conceptually straightforward approach to perform error correction and implement elementary Clifford gates, both by application of feasible Gaussian operations
Gottesman et al. 6 11
;
Schmidt and Van Loock 4 20
;
Hastrup and Andersen 1 15
;
Conrad et al. 2 10
.

The ideal logical GKP qubit states are defined as nonphysical infinite superpositions of quadrature eigenstates. Their finite energy approximations can be found either as superpositions of squeezed states
Mensen et al. 8 06
, or as the ground state of a specific operator restricted to a particular Hilbert space dimension
Marek 5 22
, and they can be conditionally prepared in a quantum boson sampling architecture with iterative mixing steps
Vasconcelos et al. 0 01
;
Weigand and Terhal 2 28
;
Su et al. 1 01
;
Winnel et al. 6 07
. Evaluating the quality of GKP qubit states, be it for the purpose of analyzing experimental results or numerically optimizing the state preparation circuits, can be done with help of their logical stabilizers or their GKP nonlinear squeezing
Bräuer et al. 2025a
;
Hanamura et al. 2025
. Such approaches are needed, because quantum state fidelity is ill-defined as a measure of similarity of two states when the target state is nonphysical
Kuchař and Marek 1 07
. Additionally, they are also practical from the point of evaluation of experimental data, as they only require two sets of measurements, one for each quadrature operator.

However, preparation and evaluation of the logical basis states are not sufficient for practical applications. Specific superpositions of logical basis states are also required both for the encoding of logical information and as resources for measurement induced realization of non-Clifford gates
Bartlett et al. 2 14
;
Bravyi and Kitaev 2 22
;
Baragiola et al. 1 13
;
Konno et al. 0 11
;
Hastrup et al. 3 10
. While such arbitrary qubit states can be defined using only the basis states, their characterization currently requires evaluating their fidelity to some approximate state. Furthermore, any such evaluation requires costly quantum state tomography
Lvovsky and Raymer 3 16
.

In this letter we introduce an efficient way to characterize and evaluate qubits encoded in the GKP basis. The approach is based on the nonlinear squeezing paradigm, which builds upon the framework of Gaussian squeezing and applies it to various non-Gaussian states, such as cubic phase states, Schrödinger cat states, or Fock states
Kala et al. 8 15
;
Bräuer and Marek 7 05
;
Bräuer et al. 2025b
;
Provazník et al. 2026
. We generalize the nonlinear squeezing for GKP states and, for each possible logical qubit GKP state, we present a positive semidefinite Hermitian operator with the respective ideal and non-normalizable state as its ground state. We show that the operator can faithfully distinguish between different qubit states, can be used for finding their physical approximations, and for practical direct measurement and evaluation.

To start, we briefly review the fundamental formalism. A quantum two-level system is efficiently described by the trio of Pauli operators
\hat{\sigma}_{x}
,
\hat{\sigma}_{y}
, and
\hat{\sigma}_{z}
, each with a pair of eigenvalues, +1 and -1, that correspond to the possible results of a measurement performed on the system. One of these operators, customarily

\hat{\sigma}_{z}
with eigenstates
\ket{+1}
and
\ket{-1}
, is chosen as the basic frame of reference, allowing for matrix representation of the Pauli operators,

\hat{\sigma}_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{\sigma}_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\end{array}\right),\quad\hat{\sigma}_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right),

(1)

as well as an arbitrary state of the system

\ket{\psi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\theta\\
e^{i\phi}\sin\theta\end{array}\right).

(2)

The state of the qubit from Eq. (
2
) can be uniquely identified by a unit vector of three real values
\mathbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{y},u_{z})
satisfying

u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}+u_{z}^{2}=1,

(3)

such that

\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{1}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\right),

(4)

where
\bm{\hat{\sigma}}=(\hat{\sigma}_{x},\hat{\sigma}_{y},\hat{\sigma}_{z})
. Defining the operator

\hat{O}_{q}=\hat{1}-\left(u_{x}\hat{\sigma}_{x}+u_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{y}+u_{z}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\right),

(5)

any pure state
\ket{\psi}
per Eq. (
2
) is also found as the lowest eigenvalue eigenstate of this operator, where

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\sqrt{\frac{1+u_{z}}{2}},

(6a)

\displaystyle e^{i\phi}\sin\theta=\frac{u_{x}+iu_{y}}{\sqrt{2(1+u_{z})}}.

(6b)

A general qubit state in the GKP basis can be expressed as

|\psi\rangle=\cos\theta|0_{\mathrm{L}}\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin\theta|1_{\mathrm{L}}\rangle,

(7)

where the basis states

\displaystyle|0_{\mathrm{L}}\rangle\propto\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}|x=2s\sqrt{\pi}\rangle,

(8a)

\displaystyle|1_{\mathrm{L}}\rangle\propto\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}|x=(2s+1)\sqrt{\pi}\rangle

(8b)

are defined as infinite superpositions of specific eigenstates of the quadrature operator
\hat{x}
, that together with its canonically conjugate operator
\hat{p}
satisfying
[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i
describe the harmonic oscillator upon which the GKP qubits are defined. The logical basis states in Eqs. (
8
) are also simultaneous eigenstates of the stabilizer operators

\hat{X}=e^{-i\hat{p}\sqrt{\pi}},~\hat{Z}=e^{i\hat{x}\sqrt{\pi}},~\hat{Y}=i\hat{X}\hat{Z},

(9)

and can therefore be found as eigenstates of their suitable linear combinations
Marek 5 22
. This approach can be generalized as follows:

Theorem 1
.

An arbitrary ideal GKP qubit state, as defined in Eq. (
7
), can be found as the zero eigenvalue eigenstate of the operator

\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}(\mathbf{u})=\hat{O}_{1}+\hat{1}-\left(u_{x}\hat{O}_{x}+u_{y}\hat{O}_{y}+u_{z}\hat{O}_{z}\right),

(10)

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{y},u_{z})
is a unit vector of real parameters satisfying conditions (
3
) and (
6
), and where the component operators are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{1}

\displaystyle=\hat{1}-\frac{1}{6}\left(\hat{X}^{2}+\hat{X}^{2{\dagger}}+\hat{Y}^{2}+\hat{Y}^{2{\dagger}}+\hat{Z}^{2}+\hat{Z}^{2{\dagger}}\right),

(11a)

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{X}+\hat{X}^{{\dagger}}}{{2}},~\hat{O}_{y}=\frac{\hat{Y}+\hat{Y}^{{\dagger}}}{{2}},~\hat{O}_{z}=\frac{\hat{Z}+\hat{Z}^{{\dagger}}}{{2}}.

(11b)

Proof.

Here,
\hat{O}_{1}
is a Hermitian positive semidefinite operator. By expanding the conjugate pairs into continuous-variable cosine operators (e.g.,
\hat{X}^{2}+\hat{X}^{2{\dagger}}=2\cos(2\hat{p}\sqrt{\pi})
),
\hat{O}_{1}
can be expressed as the identity operator minus the average of three cosines. Because the expectation value of any cosine is bounded from above by 1, this operator is positive semidefinite. Its lowest eigenvalue is zero and corresponds to a degenerate two-dimensional subspace spanned by all ideal GKP qubit states, as can be verified by direct calculation.

The complement
\hat{O}_{1c}=\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}-\hat{O}_{1}
acts simply as
\hat{1}-\mathbf{u}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}
within the ideal logical GKP subspace. Because
\mathbf{u}
is a unit vector,
\hat{O}_{1c}
is positive semidefinite within this subspace and uniquely identifies the target logical qubit state
\ket{\psi}
as its zero-eigenvalue ground state.

The addition of
\hat{O}_{1c}
removes the degeneracy of
\hat{O}_{1}
, ensuring
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
has a single non-degenerate ground state corresponding to the desired logical qubit.∎

The reason for inclusion of the subspace penalty operator
\hat{O}_{1}
is to ensure that states leaking outside the ideal logical code space are penalized, as
\hat{O}_{x},\hat{O}_{y},\hat{O}_{z}
possess Gaussian eigenstates (i.e., eigenstates of a single quadrature), and therefore do not uniquely identify the target logical qubit state, as opposed to the discrete eigenstates of the Pauli operators. In certain high-symmetry cases, however, the full penalty may not be necessary.

For example, Ref.
Marek 5 22
demonstrates that the logical state
|0_{\mathrm{L}}\rangle
is the zero-eigenvalue eigenstate of
2\sin^{2}(\hat{x}\sqrt{\pi})+2\sin^{2}(\hat{p}\sqrt{\pi}/2)
. This is a special case of Eq. (
10
): the second term is exactly
\hat{1}-\hat{O}_{x}
, while the first term isolates the position-quadrature penalty component of
\hat{O}_{1}
. This reduced form is entirely sufficient because the missing penalties would only redundantly reinforce the logical requirements already enforced by
\hat{O}_{x}
.

To ensure that the proposed method gives correct results for all possible logical superpositions in the space of logical GKP qubits, we perform a careful numerical analysis of the logical Bloch sphere. By utilizing Fibonacci lattice sampling, we construct
\sim 1000
evenly spaced points on the Bloch sphere, each corresponding to some pure superposition of
\ket{0_\mathrm{L}}
and
\ket{1_\mathrm{L}}
. For each of the points, we construct the corresponding operator
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
and its ground state. Then, choosing a starting point at the south pole, we order all the points on the sphere using a greedy heuristic of visiting the nearest unvisited point until all points are visited. Further details on this procedure are provided in Supplement
A
. Having ordered all ground states and all operators, we visualize the expectation value of each operator evaluated in each ground state and compare this to the logical infidelity of the two points on the Bloch sphere, which is given by

1-\mathcal{F}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\mathbf{u}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{j}\right),

(14)

where
\mathbf{u}_{i}=(u_{x,i},u_{y,i},u_{z,i})
and
\mathbf{u}_{j}=(u_{x,j},u_{y,j},u_{z,j})
are the unit Bloch vectors of the two logical states. The comparison of expectation values and infidelities is shown in Fig.

Analytically, this relationship becomes clear once we consider that in the logical GKP subspace, the expectation value
\langle\hat{O}_{1}\rangle
in (
10
) vanishes and the operators in (
11b
) act simply as the logical Pauli operators. For a chosen
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
with
\mathbf{u}_{i}=(u_{x,i},u_{y,i},u_{z,i})
, this implies (still in the logical subspace)

\langle\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}\rangle=\langle\hat{1}\rangle-\left(u_{x,i}\langle\hat{X}\rangle+u_{y,i}\langle\hat{Y}\rangle+u_{z,i}\langle\hat{Z}\rangle\right).

(15)

Defining the Bloch vector of the evaluated probe state as
\mathbf{u}_{j}=(u_{x,j},u_{y,j},u_{z,j})=\left(\langle\hat{X}\rangle,\langle\hat{Y}\rangle,\langle\hat{Z}\rangle\right)
, we can rewrite this as

\langle\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}\rangle=1-\mathbf{u}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{j},

(16)

which implies

\langle\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}\rangle=2\left(1-\mathcal{F}_{ij}\right),

(17)

elegantly connecting the expectation value of the operator in the logical GKP subspace to the logical infidelity and confirming the numerical convergence, indicating that ground states of
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}^{[N]}
for all possible
\mathbf{u}
define approximate logical GKP subspaces that converge to the ideal logical GKP subspace as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Finally, Fig.
3
also strongly suggests that
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
is positive semidefinite, as the lowest expectation values (corresponding to ground states) are always positive, converging to 0 as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Note that this is not at all obvious, as the proof of Theorem
1
relies on
\hat{O}_{1c}
being positive semidefinite within the logical subspace, which is not true globally. Proving the global positive semidefiniteness of
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
would require finding a general non-commutative sum of squares decomposition for arbitrary
\mathbf{u}
, which is beyond the scope of this work

;
Burgdorf et al. 2016
.

For Gaussian states, it is possible to identify a positive lower bound on the expectation value of
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
as

\min\limits_{\ket{\psi}\,\in\,\mathcal{G}}\expectationvalue{\hat{O}_\mathrm{GKP}}{\psi}=\frac{5}{3}-\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\infty},

(18)

\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\infty}=\max\{|u_{x}|,|u_{y}|,|u_{z}|\}\leq 1
is the infinity norm of the vector
\mathbf{u}

C

.

We have presented an efficient methodology for characterizing arbitrary qubits encoded in the GKP basis, bypassing the need for full quantum state tomography. By generalizing the nonlinear squeezing paradigm, we constructed a parameterized family of positive semidefinite Hermitian operators
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}(\mathbf{u})
, one for each point on the logical Bloch sphere, each tailored to have the corresponding ideal logical GKP superposition as its ground state. The construction unifies and generalizes existing results for the logical basis states
Marek 5 22
to the full Bloch sphere, including non-Clifford magic states such as
\ket{H_\mathrm{L}}
.

We established that the expectation value of
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
provides a direct, measurable proxy for logical infidelity, converging to
2(1-\mathcal{F})
across the entire logical Bloch sphere in the asymptotic limit. We further demonstrated that the finite-dimensional truncations
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}^{[N]}
offer a systematic pathway for generating and evaluating finite-energy physical approximations of arbitrary logical qubit states, providing targets for resource state preparation in experimentally accessible spaces. In addition,
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
naturally serves as a non-Gaussianity witness with a provable lower bound on the set of pure Gaussian states.

Because the target operators are built from the GKP stabilizers, estimating their expectation values requires only three sets of homodyne measurements rather than costly tomography. This makes the proposed framework both a mathematically rigorous metric for state quality and a highly practical tool for analyzing experimental data, benchmarking and optimizing state preparation circuits, and advancing the implementation of fault-tolerant continuous-variable quantum computers.

Supplement B
Converging to the Logical States

As outlined in the main text, by calculating the
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}^{[N]}
operator and its ground state for each of the sampled points and subsequently evaluating the expectation value in all
M^{2}
state-operator combinations and comparing it with the logical infidelity for the respective coordinate pairs, we obtain data in Fig.
3
.

To quantify the convergence indicated by Fig.
3
a, we perform a linear regression analysis for each of the cutoff values
N
. Denoting the expectation value as
y
and the infidelity as
x
for simplicity, we determine the correlation error as

1-r=1-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M^{2}}(x_{j}-\bar{x})(y_{j}-\bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{M^{2}}(x_{j}-\bar{x})^{2}}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{M^{2}}(y_{j}-\bar{y})^{2}}},

(19)

where
\bar{x}
and
\bar{y}
represent the sample means of the evaluated points. We also calculate the mutual information in nats

I(x;y)=\iint p(x,y)\ln\left(\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\right)dxdy,

(20)

where
p(x,y)
governs the theoretical joint continuous probability density function from which the expectation value and infidelity pairs were sampled. To avoid estimating this distribution, we utilize the Kraskov-Stögbauer-Grassberger algorithm, which calculates the mutual information based on the distances to the
k
-th nearest neighbors in the joint and marginal distributions
Kraskov et al. 6 23
.

The linear regression analysis calculated for each individual cutoff dimension
N
provides a set of geometry-dependent slope parameters
m(N)
and vertical intercepts
c(N)
, such that
y\approx m(N)x+c(N)
. To extrapolate the ground-state convergence into the (
N\to\infty
) limit, we perform a secondary, asymptotic non-linear regression on these extracted coefficients.

As the cutoff
N
increases, the intercept physically decays toward zero, while the slope
m(N)
exhibits a smooth, monotonically saturating plateau. We model this slope convergence using a standard saturating power-law ansatz,

m(N)=m_{\infty}-AN^{-d},

(21)

where
m_{\infty}
corresponds to the true physical scaling limit,
A
is a positive amplitude, and
d
is the rate of saturation. To ensure that our extrapolated limit is robust against numerical artifacts and transient behaviors, we further performed a sensitivity meta-analysis by iteratively restricting the regression window to various subsets
N\in[N_{\text{min}},N_{\text{max}}]
with
N_{\mathrm{min}}>20
. By statistically averaging the extrapolated
m_{\infty}
values we obtained a limit of
m_{\infty}=2.00\pm 0.02
, where the uncertainty reflects the one-standard-deviation variance of the values obtained for different windows.

Supplement C
Deriving the Gaussian Bound

In this section, we provide the detailed derivation of the Gaussian bound for the operator
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
discussed in the main text.

Using the definitions of the operators from Eq. (
11b
), we obtain the simple forms

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{x}=\cos(\hat{p}\sqrt{\pi}),

(22a)

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{y}=\cos\left((\hat{x}-\hat{p})\sqrt{\pi}\right),

(22b)

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{z}=\cos(\hat{x}\sqrt{\pi}).

(22c)

Similarly, the penalty operator
\hat{O}_{1}
from Eq. (
11a
) can be expressed as

\hat{O}_{1}=\hat{1}-\frac{\cos(2\sqrt{\pi}\hat{p})+\cos\left(2\sqrt{\pi}(\hat{x}-\hat{p})\right)+\cos(2\sqrt{\pi}\hat{x})}{3}.

(23)

Let us evaluate the expectation value of
\hat{O}_{\mathrm{GKP}}
, defined in Eq. (
10
), for a general state with the Wigner function
W(x,p)
. Notice that

\expectationvalue{\cos(\hat{x}\sqrt{\pi})}=\iint\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}p\;W(x,p)\cos(x\sqrt{\pi})=\mathfrak{Re}\left[\int\mathrm{d}^{2}r\,W(r)e^{ix\sqrt{\pi}}\right],

(24)

where we obtain the real part of the symmetrically ordered characteristic function
\chi(\xi)
at
\xi=(\sqrt{\pi},0)
. Continuing in this manner with the remaining terms, we can write

\expectationvalue{\hat{O}_\mathrm{GKP}}=2-R,

(25)

where

R(\chi)=\mathfrak{Re}\left[\frac{1}{3}\left[\chi(0,2\sqrt{\pi})+\chi(2\sqrt{\pi},-2\sqrt{\pi})+\chi(2\sqrt{\pi},0)\right]+u_{x}\chi(0,\sqrt{\pi})+u_{y}\chi(\sqrt{\pi},-\sqrt{\pi})+u_{z}\chi(\sqrt{\pi},0)\right].

(26)

Finding the Gaussian minimum is therefore identical to maximizing
R
for a Gaussian
\chi
. The symmetrically ordered characteristic function of a general Gaussian with displacement
\xi_{0}=(x_{0},p_{0})
and covariance matrix
\Sigma
with
\Sigma+i\Omega/2\succeq 0
is

\chi(\xi)=\exp\left(i\xi^{T}\xi_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{T}\Sigma\xi\right)=\exp\left[i\left(xx_{0}+pp_{0}\right)-\frac{x^{2}\sigma_{xx}+2xp\sigma_{xp}+p^{2}\sigma_{pp}}{2}\right].

(27)

This implies

\displaystyle R(\chi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left[e^{-2\sigma_{xx}\pi}\cos(2\sqrt{\pi}x_0)+e^{-2\pi(\sigma_{xx}-2\sigma_{xp}+\sigma_{pp})}\cos\left(2\sqrt{\pi}(x_{0}-p_{0})\right)+e^{-2\sigma_{pp}\pi}\cos(2\sqrt{\pi}p_0)\right]

(28)

\displaystyle+u_{z}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi\sigma_{xx}}\cos(\sqrt{\pi}x_0)+u_{y}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi(\sigma_{xx}-2\sigma_{xp}+\sigma_{pp})}\cos\left(\sqrt{\pi}(x_{0}-p_{0})\right)+u_{x}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\pi\sigma_{pp}}\cos(\sqrt{\pi}p_0).

For pure Gaussian states, we can additionally employ the parametrization

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-2r}\cos^{2}\theta+e^{2r}\sin^{2}\theta\right),

(29a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xp}=\frac{1}{4}\left(e^{-2r}-e^{2r}\right)\sin 2\theta,

(29b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{pp}=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-2r}\sin^{2}\theta+e^{2r}\cos^{2}\theta\right),

(29c)

\displaystyle\sigma_{xx}-2\sigma_{xp}+\sigma_{pp}=\mathrm{var}(\hat{x}-\hat{p})=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{-2r}(1-\sin 2\theta)+e^{2r}(1+\sin 2\theta)\right].

(29d)

Our goal is therefore to maximize
R(\chi)=R(x_{0},p_{0},r,\theta)
over
u_{x},u_{y},u_{z}
subject to
u_{x}^{2}+u_{y}^{2}+u_{z}^{2}=1
. It is reasonable to expect that at least some optimal solutions will fully utilize Gaussian squeezing, so let us first examine the case of
r=+\infty
. We obtain

\displaystyle R\xlongrightarrow[r\rightarrow+\infty,\;\theta=0]{}\frac{1}{3}+u_{z}\cos(\sqrt{\pi}x_0)

(30a)

\displaystyle R\xlongrightarrow[r\rightarrow+\infty,\;\theta=-\frac{\pi}{4}]{}\frac{1}{3}+u_{y}\cos\left(\sqrt{\pi}(x_{0}-p_{0})\right)

(30b)

\displaystyle R\xlongrightarrow[r\rightarrow+\infty,\;\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}]{}\frac{1}{3}+u_{x}\cos(\sqrt{\pi}p_0).

(30c)

We can always choose appropriate
x_{0},p_{0}
to maximize the cosine associated with the largest coefficient, therefore maximizing
R
. As such, this infinitely squeezed solution gives us a first idea of the bound as

R_{\mathrm{max},\infty}=\frac{1}{3}+\max\left(\left|u_{x}\right|,\left|u_{y}\right|,\left|u_{z}\right|\right)\implies\expectationvalue{\hat{O}_\mathrm{GKP}}_{\mathrm{min},\infty}=\frac{5}{3}-\max\left(\left|u_{x}\right|,\left|u_{y}\right|,\left|u_{z}\right|\right).

(31)

Calculating
\partial_{r}R
allows for a direct proof that this is the Gaussian minimum in cases where only one coefficient is non-zero. For general sets of
u_{x},u_{y},u_{z}
, such analytical proof is difficult to perform, however, numerical optimization over
x_{0},p_{0},r,\theta
for various coefficient sets strongly suggests that

\min\limits_{\ket{\psi}\,\in\,\mathcal{G}}\expectationvalue{\hat{O}_\mathrm{GKP}}{\psi}=\frac{5}{3}-\left\|\mathbf{u}\right\|_{\infty},

(32)

where
\mathbf{u}=(u_{x},u_{y},u_{z})
and where
\mathcal{G}
denotes the set of all pure Gaussian states.
