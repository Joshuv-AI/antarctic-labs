Spectral radius and edge-disjoint connected factors of graphs
Xinying Tang, Wenqian Zhang*

Abstract
For a graph G G , the spectral radius of G G is the largest eigenvalue of its adjacency matrix. A connected factor of G G is a connected spanning subgraph of G G . For example, a spanning tree of G G is a 1-connected factor of G G . Let G G be a graph of order n n with minimum degree δ ≥ 6 \delta\geq 6 , where n ≥ 3 ​ δ n\geq 3\delta . In this paper, we give a sharp spectral radius condition for G G to contain k k edge-disjoint 2-connected factors and ⌊ δ − 4 ​ k 2 ⌋ \left\lfloor\frac{\delta-4k}{2}\right\rfloor edge-disjoint spanning trees, where 1 ≤ k ≤ ⌊ δ 4 ⌋ 1\leq k\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\delta}{4}\right\rfloor is an integer. Keywords: spectral radius; eigenvalue; edge-disjoint spanning tree; connected factor; rigid graph. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C50.

denote the number of edges between

and

in

Let

For the study of edge-disjoint 2-connected factors of graphs, we first introduce the concept of rigidity of graphs. Arising from mechanics,
rigidity
is the property of a structure that does not flex, and has been extensively studied in discrete
geometry and combinatorics, and has broad applications in material science, engineering and biological sciences (see

A
d
-dimensional bar-and-joint framework
(G,p)
is the combination of a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
and a map
p:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
that assigns a point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to each vertex of
G
. Two frameworks
(G,p)
and
(G,q)
are

(or

) if
\|p(u)-p(v)\|=\|q(u)-q(v)\|
for any

(or for all pairs

), where
\|\cdot\|
denote the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A framework

is

if the coordinates of its points are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. The framework
(G,p)
is

in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that every framework
(G,q)
equivalent to
(G,p)
satisfying

for any

is congruent to
(G,p)
.
Asimov and Roth
[

proved that there is a generic framework
(G,p)
which is rigid in

if and only if every generic framework of
G
is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Thus, the generic rigidity is inherent properties of a graph. A graph
G
called rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, if there is a generic framework of
G
which is rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In this paper, we only consider rigid graphs in

. In 1970, Laman

Cioabă, Dewar and Gu

Theorem 1.1

(

Let

for every

then

contains at least

Corollary 1.2

(
[

]
)

Let

In this paper, we will gave a sharp spectral radius condition for edge-disjoint spanning rigid subgraphs and trees in graphs. Note that the sharp spectral radius condition for 2-connected graphs to be rigid was given by Fan, Huang and Lin

For integers

, let

be the graph obtained from

to

vertices in

Theorem 1.3

Assume that

and

are two integers. Let

with minimum

\delta

Now we point out that the extremal graph

A useful lemma

For integers
k\geq 1,\delta\geq 4k
and
n\geq 3\delta
, let
\mathcal{G}_{n,\delta,k}
be the set of graphs obtained from
K_{1}\vee(K_{\delta}\cup K_{n-1-\delta})

(k-1)
edges between

and

Recall that
G_{n,\delta,k}
(defined in Section 1) is the graph obtained from

to

Lemma 3.1

For integers
k\geq 1,\delta\geq 4k
and
n\geq 3\delta
, let
\mathcal{G}_{n,\delta,k}
be defined as above. Then

is the unique extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius among the graphs in

Proof:
Let
G
be an extremal graph with the maximum spectral radius among the graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{n,\delta,k}
. It suffices to show that

.
The conclusion holds trivially for

in the following discussion.

Recall that
G
can be obtained from

by adding
(k-1)
edges between
V(K_{\delta})
and
V(K_{n-1-\delta})
. Denote
V(K_{1})=\{w\}
,
V(K_{\delta})=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{\delta}\}
and
V(K_{n-1-\delta})=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1-\delta}\}
. Let
x=(x_{u})_{u\in V(G)}
be a Perron vector of
G
. Without loss of generality, assume that
x_{u_{1}}\geq x_{u_{2}}\geq\cdots\geq x_{u_{\delta}}
and
x_{v_{1}}\geq x_{v_{2}}\geq\cdots\geq x_{v_{n-1-\delta}}
.
Let
s\geq 0
be the largest integer such that
v_{s}
is adjacent to
u_{1}
in
G
.

Claim 1.

u_{1}
is adjacent to
v_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq s
in
G
. Moreover,
u_{i}v_{\ell}
is not an edge of
G
for any
1\leq i\leq\delta
and
\ell>s
.

Proof of Claim 1.
It suffices to show that if
u_{i}v_{j}
is an edge of
G
, then
u_{i^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}
is also an edge for any
i^{\prime}\leq i
and
j^{\prime}\leq j
. Suppose not. Let
G_{1}
be the graph obtained from
G
by deleting the edge
u_{i}v_{j}
and adding the non-edge
u_{i^{\prime}}v_{j^{\prime}}
. Clearly,
G_{1}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{n,\delta,k}
. Since
x_{u_{i}}x_{v_{j}}\leq x_{u_{i^{\prime}}}x_{v_{j^{\prime}}}
, we have
\lambda_{1}(G_{1})>\lambda_{1}(G)
by Lemma
2.2
. But this contradicts the choice of

. This finishes the proof of Claim 1.
\Box

Denote
\lambda_{1}(G)=\lambda
. By symmetry, we have
x_{v_{s+1}}=x_{v}
for any
s+2\leq i\leq n-1-\delta
from Claim 1. By

, we have

\lambda x_{v_{s+1}}=x_{w}+x_{v_{1}}+\left(\sum_{2\leq i\leq s}x_{v_{i}}\right)+(n-2-\delta-s)x_{v_{s+1}}\geq x_{w}+x_{v_{1}}+(n-3-\delta)x_{v_{s+1}}.

It follows that

x_{v_{s+1}}\geq\frac{x_{w}+x_{v_{1}}}{\lambda+3+\delta-n}.

Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
K_{1}\vee(K_{\delta}\cup K_{n-1-\delta})
by connecting the vertex
u_{1}
to the vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k-1}
. Clearly,
G^{\prime}=G_{n,\delta,k}
. It suffices to prove that
G=G^{\prime}
. If
s=k-1
, then
G=G^{\prime}
, as desired. Now assume that
1\leq s<k-1
. We will obtain a contradiction. Denote
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})=\lambda^{\prime}
. Let
y=(y_{u})_{u\in V(G^{\prime})}
be a Perron vector of
G^{\prime}
. By symmetry, we have
y_{u_{2}}=y_{u_{j}}
for any
3\leq j\leq\delta
and
y_{v_{1}}=y_{v_{j}}
for any

. By

, we have

\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{2}}=y_{w}+y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-2)y_{u_{2}}.

It follows that

y_{u_{2}}=\frac{y_{w}+y_{u_{1}}}{\lambda^{\prime}+2-\delta}.

Claim 2.

\frac{y_{w}}{y_{u_{1}}}<\frac{n-1-\delta}{k}
.

Proof of Claim 2.
By
A(G^{\prime})y=\lambda^{\prime}y
, we have

\lambda^{\prime}y_{w}\leq(n-1-\delta)y_{v_{1}}+y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}

and

\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{1}}=y_{w}+(k-1)y_{v_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}\geq ky_{v_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}.

Thus,

\frac{y_{w}}{y_{u_{1}}}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}y_{w}}{\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{1}}}\leq\frac{(n-1-\delta)y_{v_{1}}+y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}{ky_{v_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}.

Clearly,

\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{1}}\leq ky_{w}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}

and

\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{2}}=y_{w}+y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-2)y_{u_{2}}\geq y_{w}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}.

Thus,

\frac{y_{u_{1}}}{y_{u_{2}}}=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{1}}}{\lambda^{\prime}y_{u_{2}}}\leq\frac{ky_{w}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}{y_{w}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}\leq k.

It follows that

\frac{y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}{(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}\leq\frac{k+\delta-1}{\delta-1}.

Since

and

, we have

\frac{(n-1-\delta)y_{v_{1}}}{ky_{v_{1}}}=\frac{n-1-\delta}{k}>\frac{k+\delta-1}{\delta-1}\geq\frac{y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}{(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}.

Then

\frac{y_{w}}{y_{u_{1}}}\leq\frac{(n-1-\delta)y_{v_{1}}+y_{u_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}{ky_{v_{1}}+(\delta-1)y_{u_{2}}}<\frac{(n-1-\delta)y_{v_{1}}}{ky_{v_{1}}}=\frac{n-1-\delta}{k},

as desired.
\Box

2.1
, we have
\lambda^{\prime}>n-1-\delta
, since
G^{\prime}
contains a copy of
K_{n-\delta}
. Clearly,

2e(G)=(n-\delta)(n-\delta-1)+\delta(\delta-1)+2(\delta+k-1).

By Lemma
2.3
, noting

, it is easy to check that

\lambda=\lambda_{1}(G)\leq\frac{\delta-1}{2}+\sqrt{2e(G)-n\delta+\left(\frac{\delta+1}{4}\right)^{2}}<n-\delta.

Clearly, if
u_{i}v_{j}
is an edge of
G
and is not an edge of
G^{\prime}
, then
2\leq i\leq\delta
and
1\leq j\leq s
.
Recall that
\frac{y_{w}}{y_{u_{1}}}<\frac{n-1-\delta}{k}
by Claim 2. Trivially,
\frac{x_{w}}{x_{v_{1}}}\geq 1
. Note that

\frac{2}{3}>\frac{\frac{n-1-\delta}{k}}{n-2\delta+1}

as

and
n\geq 3\delta
.
Thus,

(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)x^{T}y=x^{T}(A(G^{\prime})-A(G))y

=\left(\sum_{u_{1}v_{i}\in(E(G^{\prime})-E(G))}(x_{u_{1}}y_{v_{i}}+x_{v_{i}}y_{u_{1}})\right)-\sum_{u_{i}v_{j}\in(E(G)-E(G^{\prime}))}(x_{u_{i}}y_{v_{j}}+x_{v_{j}}y_{u_{i}})

\geq(k-1-s)(x_{u_{1}}y_{v_{1}}+x_{v_{s+1}}y_{u_{1}}-x_{u_{2}}y_{v_{1}}-x_{v_{1}}y_{u_{2}})

\geq(k-1-s)(x_{v_{s+1}}y_{u_{1}}-x_{v_{1}}y_{u_{2}})

\geq(k-1-s)\left(\frac{x_{w}+x_{v_{1}}}{\lambda+3+\delta-n}y_{u_{1}}-\frac{y_{w}+y_{u_{1}}}{\lambda^{\prime}+2-\delta}x_{v_{1}}\right)

=(k-1-s)x_{v_{1}}y_{u_{1}}\left(\frac{\frac{x_{w}}{x_{v_{1}}}+1}{\lambda+3+\delta-n}-\frac{\frac{y_{w}}{y_{u_{1}}}+1}{\lambda^{\prime}+2-\delta}\right)

>(k-1-s)x_{v_{1}}y_{u_{1}}\left(\frac{1+1}{3}-\frac{\frac{n-1-\delta}{k}}{n-2\delta+1}\right)>0.

That is,
(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)x^{T}y>0
, implying that
\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda
. But this contradicts the choice of

. So, the proof is completed.
\Box

4
Proof of Theorem
1.3

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we first introduce a result from matroid theory. For basic definitions on matroid, one may refer to the book
[
35
]
.

Let
G
be a graph with edge set
E=E(G)
. For any
F\subseteq E
, let
G(F)
denote the spanning subgraph of
G
with edge set
F
.
Let

\mathcal{I}=\left\{F\subseteq E~|~G(F)~is~a~forest\right\}.

The
circuit matroid

\mathcal{M}(G)
of
G
is the matroid on the ground set
E(G)
with independent set
\mathcal{I}
. The rank function of
\mathcal{M}(G)
is given by

r_{\mathcal{M}}(F)=|V(G)|-c(F),

where
F\subseteq E
and
c(F)
denotes the number of components of
G(F)
. Clearly,
G
contains a spanning tree if and only if
r_{\mathcal{M}}(E)=|V(G)|-1
.

Let
F
be a non-empty subset of
E
, and let
S
be the set of vertices
incident with some edge in
F
. Set
H=G(F)
. The edge set
F
is called

independent
in
G
, if
e(H[X])\leq 2|X|-3
for all
X\subseteq V(G)
with
|X|\geq 2
.
Let

\mathcal{I}=\left\{F\subseteq E~|~F~is~independent~in~G\right\}.

Jackson and Jordán
[
26
]
proved that
\mathcal{R}(G)=(E,\mathcal{I})
is a matroid on the ground set
E
with independent set
\mathcal{I}
, which is called the
rigid matroid
of
G
. The rank function of
\mathcal{R}(G)
is given by

r_{\mathcal{R}}(F)=\min\left\{\sum_{1\leq i\leq t}(2|X_{i}|-3)\right\},

where the minimum is taken over all collections of subsets
X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{t}
of
V(G)
such that
\left\{E(G[X_{1}]),E(G[X_{2}]),...,E(G[X_{t}])\right\}
partitions
F
. (Clearly,
|X_{i}|\geq 2
for any
1\leq i\leq t
.)

G
is called rigid if
r_{\mathcal{R}}(E)=2|V(G)|-3
. Using a result of Laman
[
30
]
, Jackson and Jordán
[
26
]
stated that these definitions agree with the geometric definitions for rigidity in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
given in the introduction.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
with edge set
E
. As in
[
3
]
, let
\mathcal{N}_{k,\ell}
denote the matroid on the ground set
E
obtained by taking the matroid union of
k
copies of the rigid matroids
\mathcal{R}(G)
and
\ell
copies of circuit matroids
\mathcal{M}(G)
. For
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, let
r_{k,\ell}(E^{\prime})
be the rank of
E^{\prime}
. By a result of Edmonds on the rank of matroid union (see
[
10
]
or Chapter 42 of
[
36
