Objective

Traumatic brachial plexus injury (TBPI) mainly affects young males, usually involved in motorcycle accidents, leading to severe motor and sensory impairment, and pain in the affected upper limb [1]. The impacts of TBPI go beyond, burdening the individuals’ lives across the psychic, emotional, social, and economic domains [2]. The relevance of Brazilian data on TBPI becomes evident when examined alongside road traffic accident (RTA) statistics, as 92% of RTA deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries [3].

We present the Brazilian public database on TBPI (TBPI-DB), an initiative aimed at encouraging data sharing and reuse. Handling data from individuals with TBPI may enable the identification of mechanisms and markers associated with their functional improvement. Data can also guide public actions related to motorcycle accidents and their economic consequences.

The TBPI-DB was carried out by a multidisciplinary team comprising physicians, physiotherapists, neuroscientists, and computer scientists. The researchers devised three questionnaires to collect TBPI clinical, physical, and surgical data: the Unified Admission Assessment (Supplementary File 1), the Unified Follow-up Assessment (Supplementary File 2), and the Unified Surgical Evaluation (Supplementary File 3). These questionnaires, developed specifically for this dataset, were intended to explore general topics related to TBPI, considering the literature on the theme and the research team’s professional experience in the field.

TBPI-DB belongs to a broad project on peripheral nerve injury and brain plasticity from the Laboratory of Neuroscience and Rehabilitation (LabNeR) at the Institute of Neurology Deolindo Couto of the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (INDC-UFRJ) and the Center for Research, Innovation, and Dissemination in Neuromathematics (CEPID NeuroMat) at the University of São Paulo (USP). In this context, TBPI-DB supported several projects carried out in the LabNeR using electroencephalographic signals [4], kinematic data [5, 6], motor potentials evoked by transcranial magnetic stimulation [7], balance [8], and touch sensitivity assessment [9].

Data description

The TBPI-DB is a convenience-based clinical registry comprising 170 individuals with TBPI, aged 18 years or older, recruited between 2014 and 2020 through routine clinical intake at the INDC/INTO units. Data collection combined prospective participant assessments with retrospective data extracted from medical records. It was performed by a neurologist, a physiatrist, and a physiotherapist, all with extensive experience in evaluating neurological patients. Examiners underwent pre-study training to standardize the physical examination techniques. The electronic questionnaires were created using LimeSurvey 2.05 (Limesurvey GmbH, Germany), and the Neuroscience Experiment System (NES) was used for data acquisition and management [10]. Each participant was identified by a noninformative code generated by the NES, and the participant’s sensitive data was suppressed.

The dataset is downloaded as a single ZIP-compressed file. It is organized according to the hierarchical structure indicated in Table 1. The Database_description.txt contains information about the TBPI-DB (number of participants, inclusion criteria - International Disease Code (ICD-10), questionnaires’ description, completion, and sample characterization). The file Participants.csv contains the participants’ codes, ages, and genders.

The participants’ data collected by means of the electronic questionnaires is replicated in two subdirectories of the Questionnaire_data directory: Per_questionnaire and Per_participant. In the former, there is a file with the participants’ responses collected through each of the three questionnaires (Q44071_unified-admission-assessment_en.csv, Q92510_unified-follow-up-assessment_en.csv, and Q61802_unified-surgical-evaluation_en.csv). Each line in these files contains a response per participant, identified by its unique “participant_code”. Each participant has exactly one response in the Q44071_unified-admission-assessment_en.csv, but may have none or more responses in the Q92510_unified-follow-up-assessment_en.csv and Q61802_unified-surgical-evaluation_en.csv. Separate lines in the file with the same participant code indicate different responses of the same participant for a questionnaire.

In the Per_participant_data directory, there is a directory for each participant that contains a file for each questionnaire. If a participant has multiple responses to a given questionnaire, they will appear as separate lines in the file.

The Questionnaire_metadata subdirectory contains the files describing the structure of the three questionnaires. Searching by a response column name in the “question_index” column of a metadata file, one will find one or more lines that describe the corresponding question and the type of responses it accepts. Naming conventions for question codes are described at the NES Documentation webpage [11].

The Questionnaire_structure subdirectory contains files with the structure of the three electronic questionnaires in both Brazilian Portuguese and English. The English versions are also available as Supplementary Files (Supplementary File 1, Supplementary File 2, and Supplementary File 3).

The Q44071_unified-admission-assessment records information about past musculoskeletal injuries, TBPI injury history, ongoing treatments, and a detailed neurological exam that includes visual inspection, Tinel sign search, sensory evaluation, motor evaluation, and pain assessment. The Q92510_unified-follow-up-assessment reviews ongoing treatments and physical exams previously recorded on the Q44071_unified-admission-assessment. The Q61802_unified-surgical-evaluation records surgical findings and procedures.

Limitations

TBPI-DB includes retrospective information from medical records with restricted outcome measures. This led to a substantial amount of missing data. Also, TBPI-DB is a convenience-based cohort with potential undercoverage sampling bias. Limited follow-up restricts longitudinal investigation. Finally, the assessment aimed at a comprehensive survey of TBPI features, which limited the detailed investigation of specific important aspects such as pain.
