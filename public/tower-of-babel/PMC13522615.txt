Analysis of stability and chaotic trajectories in nonlinear fluid wave interactions under forcing effects
Authors: Tomas Kozubek, Muhammad Iqbal, Muhammad Bilal Riaz, Syeda Sarwat Kazmi, Muhammad Aziz ur Rehman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Ablowitz–Kaup–Newell–Segur (AKNS) water wave equation is a fundamental model for describing nonlinear wave propagation in various physical systems, including fluid dynamics, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics. Its higher-dimensional form provides a more realistic framework for understanding complex wave interactions in dispersive media. In this work, the (2+1)-dimensional AKNS equation is studied from both analytical and dynamical perspectives. Analytical solutions of the governing equation are obtained using the modified Khater method, constructing different types of wave structures such as stationary and traveling profiles. These solutions illustrate the influence of nonlinearity and dispersion on the formation and evolution of coherent wave patterns. To further explore the system behavior, a detailed dynamical analysis is performed. The model is reduced to a system of ordinary differential equations, allowing the investigation of phase space dynamics through phase portraits, time series, Poincaré sections, bifurcation diagrams, and Lyapunov exponents. The results demonstrate a wide range of behaviors, including periodic, quasi-periodic, and chaotic motions, depending on the choice of system parameters. In particular, the system shows strong sensitivity to initial conditions and parameter variations, indicating the presence of complex and chaotic dynamics.Keywords: Exact solutions, Ablowitz-Kaup-Newell Segur model, Modified Khater method, Dynamical analysisSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) form the basis for mathematical description and investigation of a large number of complex problems in mathematical physics, ocean engineering, fluid mechanics, and other related fields of science. They play a fundamental role in understanding the propagation of waves, instability, and pattern formation in a wide range of physical systems due to their ability to model nonlinear interactions1–3. These equations are especially interesting because they can describe complicated dynamic processes in both natural and man-made systems4. Furthermore, they also govern other physical processes like mass transport, energy propagation, and wave evolution and have been applied in many ways in different fields, including epidemiology, neural networks, economics, population dynamics, and solid-state physics. Analytical solutions are critical to comprehending the structure and physics of nonlinear wave systems, especially in fluid and water-wave systems. Motivated by these studies, much progress has been made in obtaining analytical solutions of NLPDEs, which has led to a deeper understanding of the behavior of nonlinear wave phenomena in various applications, such as oceanography and engineering. There are also numerous strong methods that have been proposed over the years, and they include Lie symmetry analysis5, the bilinear residual network method6, the modified auxiliary equation method7, the unified solver method8, the extended Sinh-Gordon expansion method9, the Jacobi elliptic function expansion method10, and the modified extended tanh-function method11, the bilinear neural network technique12,13, and the Hirota bilinear method14. Such methods have facilitated the creation of a broad category of nonlinear wave phenomena, including solitons, rogue waves, multi-wave interactions, lump solutions, and other localized excitations. Most of these solutions directly apply to water-wave dynamics, in which nonlinear dispersion and interaction between waves are important in both pattern formation and energy transport. Following these advances, there has been a growing interest in nonlinear models with rich dynamical behaviors in higher dimensions. Examples of these are the long Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation15, the Landau-Ginzburg-Higgs equations16, the Fokas-Lenells model17, the Schrodinger equation18, and the Kaup-Boussinesq equations19. The models are especially important in the description of the wave propagation phenomena in the multidimensional systems of fluid and water waves, where the dispersion, nonlinearity, and external perturbation interactions contribute to the appearance of complex wave patterns. The development of soliton theory could be traced back to the discovery of solitary water waves by John Scott Russell20, who published the “Great Translation Wave” in 1834. He was initially skeptical, but this observation of shape preservation of the wave was later confirmed by Rayleigh and Boussinesq21,22, respectively. This discovery led to the development of the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation by Korteweg and de Vries (1895)23, which provides a well-developed mathematical description of wave propagation in shallow water and soliton formation. Subsequent other findings in nonlinear science came with the Fermi-Pasta-Ulam (FPU) experiment (1951), which revealed unexpected recurrence phenomena in nonlinear lattices and the intricacies of energy transfer in nonlinear wave systems24. More recently, Zabusky and Kruskal25 showed that solitary waves collide elastically and retain their shapes and velocities, thus introducing the modern notion of solitons, which is of great importance for water-wave theory and nonlinear fluid dynamics. In particular, the AKNS formulation, which is based on the inverse scattering transform (IST)26, is fundamental in the construction of exact solutions to nonlinear evolution equations, many of which are related to water waves in dispersive and stratified fluids. Here, we take into account the following fourth-order nonlinear AKNS-type water wave equation27 with a small perturbation B:

This equation governs the evolution of the wave field Z(x, y, t) in a (2+1)-dimensional water-wave framework. It models the interaction between nonlinear effects and dispersive mechanisms essential in shallow water waves, internal waves, and other fluid systems. The term represents mixed spatiotemporal evolution, while introduces higher-order dispersion responsible for wave spreading. The nonlinear coupling terms and describe energy transfer between spatial directions, capturing multidimensional wave interaction effects. The factor B is a physical modulator of wave amplitude and wave restoration. In general, the equation represents a combination of nonlinearity and dispersion, which is responsible for the formation of coherent structures (solitons, kink waves, and periodic solutions) in water-wave systems. This family of equations is relevant to fluid mechanics and water-wave physics; therefore, it has been widely researched in the fields of nonlinear optics, plasma physics, and hydrodynamics. It has been used to describe various physical phenomena in dispersive systems, such as wave interactions, energy transport, and stability. Dusunceli28 applied the improved Bernoulli sub-equation method to obtain new exact solutions of the AKNS water wave equation, while Aktürk et al.29 used the modified exponential function method to derive traveling wave solutions of the fourth-order AKNS equation with graphical representations. Similarly, Ahmad et al.30 employed the Power index method to obtain exponential, trigonometric, and logarithmic solutions and showed that the AKNS equation can be reduced to a linear ordinary differential equation under suitable transformations. Duru et al.31 applied the (1/G')-expansion method to obtain hyperbolic and singular soliton solutions of the (2+1)-dimensional AKNS equation, while Gao et al.32 derived dark–bright soliton solutions using the sine-Gordon expansion method. Rani et al.33 further obtained various exact solutions using an improved expansion technique, supported by graphical analysis. Inspired by these advances, the present work is dedicated to the analytical construction of exact soliton solutions and a detailed examination of the corresponding dynamical behavior in a water-wave form. We use the modified Khater method (MKM)34, which has not been previously used to solve this equation, to derive a broad range of exact solutions, such as kink, singular, bright, dark, and periodic solitons. The analytical solution of this method is also illustrated graphically in order to provide a physical interpretation of the wave propagation in fluid mediums. From the dynamical systems perspective, bifurcation explains the sudden change in the nature of waves in response to some physical parameter, often accompanied by a change in stability, instability or chaos. Such changes are particularly important in water-wave systems where the influence of the external forcing, as well as the nonlinear effects, are predominant in determining the nature of the wave propagation. In recent years, periodic waves, solitons, and shockwaves have been shown to be commonly observed in plasma systems as well as fluid systems35. In particular, we can see the same nonlinear effects in water-wave systems when exposed to external conditions. The work of Saha et al.36, Abdikian et al.37, and El-Taibany et al.38 has observed the complex behavior of waves in different physical systems, such as periodic structures and nonlinear stability transitions. Unlike previous independent investigations, in the present study the approach is to consider the dynamics from a coherent dynamical systems and chaos-theoretical perspective on the nonlinear behavior of water waves. The inclusion of an external forcing term gives rise to highly nonlinear responses as the system is sensitive to parameters such as the ratio of two frequencies and strength of the perturbation. This results in the transition of regular wave dynamics, periodic and chaotic dynamics, which give a better understanding of the nonlinear dynamics of water waves. Wave solution plots and discussion

This section illustrates various graphical patterns of Eq. (1) obtained using the computational software MATLAB (2018). The derived solutions are expressed in terms of trigonometric, hyperbolic, and Jacobi elliptic functions. Based on their physical and structural characteristics, these solutions are categorized into four main types: solitary waves, bright solitons, dark solitons, and periodic wave structures. Solutions in terms of Jacobi elliptic functions are a special type of solution that is important in solving mathematical physics problems due to their ability to represent periodic and soliton solutions. These include, to name a few, bright soliton solutions, which are highly excited and localized waves with amplifications. These are stable and preserve their shape as they propagate; they are useful in the dynamics of strong signals and high-intensity waves in applications such as plasma physics and nonlinear optics. Dark soliton solutions are associated with the dip in amplitude in a continuous wave train background. These solutions are generally low-energy states and can be employed to study low-intensity waves and their interactions, as well as weak signals. Their dip property makes them significant in studying nonlinear optical systems and other processes, particularly in understanding how these low-energy states can influence the propagation of light and the behavior of weak signals in various applications. On the other hand, periodic solutions are associated with periodic, or repeating, waves, which are important in modeling naturally periodic processes, such as ocean waves and oscillating systems. In addition, the singular solutions relate to the points where it diverges. These solutions might not always be directly physically meaningful but can provide valuable information on important transitions, instabilities, and constraints of the mathematical model, such as how they can reveal critical points in the evolution of nonlinear waves. Over all, the different solution structures provide a comprehensive framework for studying nonlinear waves and their dynamics, in which strong and weak signals, periodic solutions, and even the limitations of the mathematical model associated with the AKNS equation can be studied.

Quasi-periodic and chaotic patterns

Chaos in dynamical systems is a type of motion that is very intricate and unpredictable, even when the system is governed by strict and deterministic laws. One of the most essential characteristics of chaotic dynamics is how sensitive they are to the ICs. This means that even small changes in the IC can lead to significantly different results over time. The scientific community agrees that weather is chaotic, with a finite predictability, and that the source of chaos is nonlinearity. People typically name this attribute the butterfly effect40–44. Therefore, even small changes grow bigger and bigger, making it impossible to make long-term predictions. To investigate these CBs, an external driving factor is included in system (22). The value of indicates the intensity, whereas the value of Λ signifies the frequency of the perturbed system (24). Therefore, this section analyzes the chaotic and quasi-periodic patterns of the understudy model via adding a perturbation term to the planar dynamical system.

For water waves, this type of motion is related to the propagation of regular and harmonic surface waves, where wave profiles are stable and the water wave field has a uniform oscillatory structure (e.g., shallow water waves with a weak disturbance). The solution stays periodic, but it is no longer strictly periodic, as various wave frequencies start to interact. This leads to very different wave evolution despite the small change of the initial wave profile. Physically, it is connected to the chaotic water-wave dynamics, such as strongly disturbed sea states, turbulent wave interactions, and chaotic surface wave dynamics in a highly forced external environment (wind or pressure variations). Surprisingly, this chaotic phenomenon is not only limited to theoretical water-wave systems but can also be often observed in real water-wave systems. The chaotic behavior of water surface waves in stormy seas, the nonlinear wave interactions, and the energy redistribution in turbulent sea states are prevalent. These observations have verified that water wave systems can change from periodic to chaotic behavior as a result of forcing and nonlinear interactions. From a physical perspective, this periodic-chaotic transition of the wave is a consequence of the universal nature of nonlinear systems. It underlines that water wave propagation is a very sensitive parameter, and the analysis of these transitions is crucial in modeling complex ocean and coastal waves. Multistability

This section explores the phenomenon of multistability in systems (24). Whenever a perturbation term is introduced in a planar system, the system has the potential to exhibit multistability, which indicates the possibility of many alternative dynamic behaviors. These behaviors are observed under different initial conditions but with the same parameter values. The system exhibits extraordinary multistability, allowing for many dynamic patterns to emerge simultaneously. This spectrum of behaviors includes chaotic, quasi-periodic, and periodic behavior, which can occur under different initial conditions. Figure 17 illustrates the dynamical responses of system (24) under different initial conditions and parameter settings. In Fig. 17a, the system exhibits periodic behavior for the initial conditions (0.2, 0.2), (0.2, 0.1), and (0.1, 0.1), represented by green, magenta, and yellow curves, respectively. This behavior is observed for the parameter values , , , and Λ = 0.3. In Fig. 17b, the system transitions to a quasi-periodic state under the same set of initial conditions, but with parameters , , , and Λ = 0.5. A further change in parameters leads to more complex dynamics. Specifically, Fig. 17c demonstrates the onset of chaotic behavior for , , , and , while maintaining the same initial conditions. Finally, Fig. 17d confirms fully developed chaotic dynamics for , , , and . These results clearly show that the system’s behavior evolves from periodic to quasi-periodic and eventually to chaotic as the control parameters vary, highlighting its strong sensitivity to both initial conditions and parameter changes.

Bifurcation diagram

A dynamical system changes its orbit structure, and such a change is a result of a variation of one or several parameters that are characteristic of a differential equation system and is called a bifurcation of the differential equation system. A bifurcation diagram shows how the qualitative behavior of a dynamic system varies when a parameter is altered. It is a very useful tool for finding changes between distinct dynamic regimes, such as when motion goes from steady-state to oscillatory or chaotic. In the context of AKNS model, bifurcation occurs when variations in system parameters alter the number and stability of solutions to the underlying differential equations. Specifically, bifurcation behavior in AKNS is analyzed by plotting the state variable S against the variable V. As parameters like , , , and are applied, the system displays distinct dynamical patterns. As shown in Fig. 18, the region of S-axis between 0.2 and 0.4 corresponds to periodic motion, 0.4 to 0.8 shows quasi-periodic behavior, while the interval from 0.8 to 1.5 indicates the presence of chaotic dynamics.

Conclusion

In this paper, we performed a comprehensive analytical and dynamical analysis of the fourth-order nonlinear AKNS water wave equation. The MKM was successfully applied to obtain a wide range of exact solutions in rational, hyperbolic, and trigonometric forms. These solutions demonstrated the rich nonlinear behavior of the model and verified the effectiveness of the analytical method employed in capturing a broad range of wave dynamics in multidimensional systems. To gain physical insight into the solutions, we plotted some of the wave profiles in Figs. (1–8) via MATLAB. These representations depicted the dynamics of solitary, kink, periodic, and other types of nonlinear waves, providing insight into their characteristics and dynamics. The analytical results confirmed that the AKNS model exhibited a wide range of waveforms, including solitary and periodic waves. It was found that the interplay between nonlinearity and dispersion was essential to the development and maintenance of these coherent wave structures, which are applicable to various phenomena in coastal, shallow-water, and internal wave systems. Besides the analytical findings, a dynamical study was conducted to investigate the qualitative aspects of the system. By applying a Galilean transformation, the partial differential equation was transformed into a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations, enabling the study of the dynamics in phase space. The application of methods from dynamical systems theory, including phase portraits, bifurcation diagrams, Lyapunov exponents, and multistability analysis, revealed a plethora of dynamical features, including periodic, quasi-periodic, and chaotic motion. Additionally, the system exhibited sensitivity to initial conditions and other pertinent parameters. This had a profound impact on the transition between different dynamical regimes and was crucial in realistic water-wave systems, where minor variations in the system may result in significant changes in the wave structure, amplitude, and stability. In addition, the current work provided a unified approach to the problem, which involved analytical solution methods and modern dynamical analysis. It offered valuable insights into the interaction of nonlinearity, dispersion, and external forces and thus improved the understanding of nonlinear wave dynamics in fluid systems.
