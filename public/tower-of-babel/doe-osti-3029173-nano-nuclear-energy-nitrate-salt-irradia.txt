Prior to filling with salt, all glass cuvettes and glassy carbon crucibles were cleaned with
deionized H2O and isopropyl alcohol and then baked out at 400 °C in a vacuum furnace. They
were then transferred into an argon (Ar) glovebox and baked out in a box furnace at 500 °C
for 2 hours before being allowed to cool to ambient temperature inside of the glovebox.

Salts were received in closed bottles sealed under air. These bottles were opened and
placed inside of the glovebox antechamber and cycled with Ar and atmosphere to remove
the air from the containers. Individual salts: (i) NaNO3, (ii) KNO3, and (iii) the Industrial salt
mixture were all separately baked out inside the glovebox at 100 °C, then 200 °C, for about 2
hours in glassy carbon crucibles to remove any moisture.

If the targeted compositions are hit, at 250ºC both salts should be completely molten.
Depending on the crucibles (material, geometry etc.) to be used, please allow 10-15
mins after the melt appears optically clear i.e. free of residual crystals and transparent.

Salts were then mixed in the masses listed below before being melted at 345 °C for 2 hours.

Post Irradiation Examination

After irradiation, samples were cooled to ambient temperatures, packed back into
cushioned boxes and transported to INL for analysis by ion chromatography and UV Visible
spectroscopy. The tops of the sealed ampoules were etched with a file and broken off such
that they were open to atmosphere. Water was then added to the cuvettes in aliquots to
gradually dissolve the salts and transfer them into volumetric flasks for analysis. To
determine the approximate mass of the salt dissolved, the cuvettes were weighed just after
being broken while still containing the salt and again after dissolving the salt and cleaning
and drying. Typically, 250 mL flasks were used for 2.7 g of salt, making ~0.11 M solutions of
each salt. See exact values for each salt in Table 1 as “Total [NO3-]”. In addition to all the
irradiated samples, we also analyzed the as-received industrial salt mixture and KNO3, and
the dried industrial salt mixture, NaNO3 and KNO3, as well as freshly prepared solar and
eutectic mixtures (S0 and E0), and solar and eutectic mixtures that had been flame sealed
under Ar but not irradiated (samples S6 and E1).

UV Visible Spectroscopy

Two methods were used for UV Visible spectroscopy analysis, carried out using an Agilent
Cary 6000i UV-Vis-NIR spectrophotometer at INL.

Direct Spectrum

The first method was to directly measure the salt spectrum to look for the nitrate peak
centered around 303 nm. Using the reported molar extinction coefficient of ε(303 nm) =
7.2 M-1cm−1 (see https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135410007396)
we could calculate the nitrate concentration to within 5 %. The results from this analysis are
reported in Table 1 as “Direct Spectrum [NO3-]”. It is also useful to scale the absorbances
based on the calculated nitrate concentration to look for spectral trends. See the spectra for
all samples in Figure 3, scaled to an initial concentration of 0.11 M NO3-.

It is worth noting that a second peak, centered at ~360 nm, appears for the most highly
irradiated salts. Samples S7 and E4, which each received over 2 MGy of absorbed dose show
the largest peaks with absorbances around 0.16 units. Samples S1 and SQM1
Thermocouple, which each received ~700 kGy of irradiation, show a peak with an
absorbance maximum around 0.1 units. All four of these traces also show a slightly higher
absorbances around 260 nm as compared to the other samples. There are no measurable
differences between the remaining samples in the direct spectrum.

Nitrite ions are known to show an absorbance band at 360 nm with a reported molar
extinction coefficient  of ε(360 nm) = 22.5 M-1 cm−1 (see
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1010603099001550). Using this value,

all irradiated samples had a measurable concentration of nitrite ion detected. These are
reported in Table 1 as “Direct Spectrum [NO2-]”.

Shinn Method

A modified version of the Shinn method was used to quantify the concentration of NO2–. This
method involved the sequential addition of 116 µL of 40 mM sulfanilamide in 1.2 M HCl ,
followed by 44 µL of 3.98 mM N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride to 3.84 mL of
sample, where the sample consists of a mixture of water and the nitrate solutions diluted
from 1 to 500x such that the nitrite absorbance fell within the desired linear detection range.
The UV Visible spectrum for standard NaNO2 solutions at λmax = 542 nm exhibited an
extinction coefficient of ε(542 nm) = 49500 ± 300 M–1 cm–1, as shown in the calibration curve
in Figure 4. This spectrophotometric method was determined to be accurate to within ± 4 %,
and had a limit of quantification of 0.3 µM NO2–.

For each UV Visible spectroscopy result, the mol% NO2- has been calculated, using the
direct value for NO3- and the Shinn value for NO2-. It is noteworthy that the as received KNO3
and industrial mixture and the dried individual KNO3 and NaNO3 contained only 0.001 mol%
NO2-. However, the dried industrial mixture and the freshly prepared solar and eutectic
mixtures contained from 0.012 to 0.023 mol% NO2-, therefore, simply heating these salts in
mixtures does result in the formation of nitrite. All of the irradiated salts showed an increase
in the mol% of NO2- detected in the prepared solutions. There are insufficient replicates of
any individual salt type (solar, industrial, eutectic) over a range of doses to report individual
trends, but Figure 5 shows the increase in mol% NO2- detected with applied dose for all of
the salts studied.

Ion Chromatography

The concentration of nitrate and nitrite ions in the salt solution was also determined using
ion chromatography (IC) using a Thermo Scientific Dionex ICS 5000+. The IC method
employed used an injection volume of 10 μL, a Dionex IonPac AS18 analytical column with
an AG18 guard column, an isocratic mobile phase of 37 mM potassium hydroxide, and a run
time of 15 minutes. Samples were injected in duplicate, while calibration standards were
injected in triplicate. Integrated peak areas corresponded to the total concentration (moles
of ions per volume of the sample) of NO3- and NO2- in the sample.

Standard calibration solutions of 1.0 mM, 0.5 mM, 0.1mM, 0.05 mM, and 0.01 mM NaNO3
and NaNO2 were prepared to develop calibration curves for the IC method, as shown in
Figure 6 and Figure 7 for NO3- and NO2- respectively.

The prepared solutions were diluted 240x then analyzed for the nitrate ion concentration.
The results, listed in Table 2, agree with the direct UV Visible results for nitrate to within 5 %
in all cases, except sample S1, which agreed to within 7 %.
