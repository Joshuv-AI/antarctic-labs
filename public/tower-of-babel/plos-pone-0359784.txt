Identification of shared diagnostic biomarkers and immune-related mechanisms between sepsis and thoracic aortic aneurysm based on integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approaches

Chengqun She, Hao Deng, Yaoguang Feng, Chaozhong Long, Juan Wu

ABSTRACT
Background: Sepsis and thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) share pathological features of inflammation and vascular dysfunction, yet their molecular connections remain unclear. Methods: Transcriptomic datasets of sepsis and TAA from GEO were integrated to identify shared differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Functional enrichment and gene set enrichment analyses were performed to explore potential pathways. Machine learning models were applied to identify candidate genes with discriminatory potential, followed by immune infiltration analysis and assessment of gene expression in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-treated vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). Results: A total of 193 shared DEGs were identified, mainly enriched in T cell activation, Th17 differentiation, and oxidative stress–related pathways. Five candidate genes (ARG1, IL18R1, LIX1L, TRIM32, and PRKCH) were selected through sequential machine-learning analysis and showed generally reproducible expression patterns across independent datasets. Their expression was associated with estimated immune cell proportions. In LPS-treated VSMCs, ARG1 and IL18R1 were upregulated, whereas LIX1L was downregulated. Conclusion: Sepsis and TAA share common transcriptional signatures involving immune dysregulation and metabolic stress. ARG1 and IL18R1 may represent candidate molecular features associated with inflammatory and vascular responses.

Introduction Sepsis is a life-threatening syndrome characterized by dysregulated host immune responses to infection, leading to systemic inflammation, endothelial injury, and multiple organ dysfunction [ 1 ]. During sepsis, sustained activation of immune and endothelial cells triggers excessive cytokine release, microvascular thrombosis, and oxidative stress, which not only damage peripheral organs but also exert long-term effects on the vascular system [ 2 ]. Recent evidence indicates that even after apparent clinical recovery, patients with sepsis may experience persistent vascular dysfunction, increased arterial stiffness, and abnormal remodeling of large vessels [ 3 ]. These alterations suggest that the inflammatory and immune consequences of sepsis may be associated with chronic vascular diseases, including aortic aneurysm formation [ 4 ]. Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a progressive degenerative disorder characterized by pathological dilation and weakening of the aortic wall [ 5 ]. While genetic factors such as mutations in fibrillin-1 and smooth muscle contractile proteins contribute to hereditary forms of TAA, increasing attention has been directed toward the role of inflammation and immune-mediated extracellular matrix degradation in sporadic cases [ 6 ]. Activated macrophages and T lymphocytes infiltrate the aortic wall, releasing pro-inflammatory cytokines and matrix metalloproteinases that accelerate elastin fragmentation and smooth muscle apoptosis [ 6 , 7 ]. Endothelial dysfunction further amplifies oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling, creating a vicious cycle of vascular injury and remodeling. Thus, chronic immune activation is increasingly recognized as a major contributor to TAA pathogenesis, linking systemic inflammatory stimuli to localized vascular degeneration [ 8 ]. Given these overlapping pathological processes, sepsis and TAA may share common molecular pathways associated with immune dysregulation, oxidative stress, and extracellular matrix remodeling [ 9 , 10 ]. However, the shared transcriptomic signatures and key molecular regulators connecting acute systemic inflammation and chronic vascular degeneration remain poorly defined [ 11 ]. Clarifying these molecular intersections would provide insight into how post-septic inflammation transitions to vascular structural damage, and potentially identify biomarkers predictive of aortic complications in patients recovering from sepsis. TAA was selected as the representative vascular disease because it is a prototypic large vessel degenerative disorder with strong evidence of immune mediated pathogenesis. In contrast, the links between sepsis and more common vascular outcomes such as atherosclerosis or peripheral artery disease have been relatively better characterized. The relationship between acute systemic inflammation and TAA remains underexplored, warranting this investigation. In this study, we conducted an integrated transcriptomic and computational analysis to explore the shared molecular landscape between sepsis and thoracic aortic aneurysm. By combining multi-cohort gene expression profiling with machine learning-based feature selection, we sought to identify candidate genes and immune-related pathways shared between the two diseases. Methods Microarray data collection and processing The two original expression profile datasets for Sepsis and control groups, including GSE95233 [ 12 ] and GSE57065 [ 13 ], were downloaded from the GEO database ( https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/ ). GSE95233 includes 51 sepsis patients and 22 healthy controls. The data were generated using the Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array (Platform ID: GPL570), a gene expression microarray technology manufactured by Affymetrix, Inc. (Santa Clara, CA, USA). Similarly, GSE57065 includes 28 sepsis patients and 25 healthy controls, utilizing the same platform (GPL570). The datasets for TAA and control groups were GSE9106 [ 14 ] and GSE57691 [ 15 ]. GSE9106 includes 58 TAA patients and 36 healthy controls. The data were generated using the ABI Human Genome Survey Microarray Version 2 (Platform ID: GPL2986), a spotted oligonucleotide microarray manufactured by Applied Biosystems (Rockville, MD, USA). GSE57691 includes 49 TAA patients and 10 healthy controls. The data were generated using the Illumina HumanHT-12 V4.0 Expression BeadChip (Platform ID: GPL10558), an oligonucleotide bead-based microarray manufactured by Illumina Inc. (San Diego, CA, USA). Differential expression gene analysis The study workflow is illustrated in Fig 1 . DEG analysis was performed using the “Limma” package [ 16 ] in R software (version 4.3.1) for the Sepsis dataset (GSE95233) and TAA dataset (GSE9106). The cut-off criteria for DEGs were set as adj.P.Val < 0.05 and |log(FC)| > 0.5. Subsequently, the expression patterns of DEGs were visualized using the “pheatmap” package and the “ggplot2” package in R software. Heatmaps and volcano plots were generated to represent the expression patterns of DEGs. Low expression genes were not pre filtered, as the stringent thresholds together with subsequent machine learning screening were applied to ensure robustness. Each dataset was normalized separately. Probe IDs were converted to gene symbols using the corresponding platform annotation files. Because the included datasets were generated from different microarray platforms and biological compartments, direct merging was avoided to minimize potential platform-related and tissue-specific biases. Differential expression analysis was performed individually for each dataset. Shared DEGs were identified by taking the intersection of DEGs from each dataset rather than from a merged dataset (Step 1 of Fig 1 ). 10.1371/journal.pone.0359784.g001 Fig 1 Flowchart of the study design. Function enrichment analysis To explore the biological functions and specific mechanisms of the disease-related genes in TAA and Sepsis, we conducted GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis on the input gene set using packages such as “org.Hs.e.g.,db” [ 17 ], “GOplot” [ 18 ], “enrichplot” [ 19 ], and “clusterProfiler” [ 19 ]. A significance threshold of P < 0.05 was used to determine significant enrichment. To visualize the enrichment analysis results, we utilized packages such as “ggplot2,” “circlize,” “RColorBrewer,” and “ComplexHeatmap.” The results of the functional enrichment analysis were presented using circular plots and bubble plots. Gene set enrichment analysis To complement the enrichment analysis based on shared differentially expressed genes and to characterize pathway-level alterations across the broader transcriptomic profiles, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed separately in the sepsis dataset GSE95233 and the TAA dataset GSE9106. Genes in each dataset were ranked according to their differential expression between the disease and control groups. GSEA was performed using the R packages “clusterProfiler” and “org.Hs.e.g.,db.” The “c2.cp.kegg.Hs.symbols.gmt” was used as a reference gene set. The permutation number was set to 10,000, and the significance threshold was set at p < 0.05. Finally, the results were visualized using the R package “enrichplot.” Machine learning To identify candidate genes with discriminatory potential shared between sepsis and TAA, we applied a sequential machine-learning framework using the shared DEGs identified in both conditions. The sepsis dataset was used for model development because it provided a clearly defined disease-control comparison and was suitable for identifying genes associated with inflammatory states. We hypothesized that genes consistently dysregulated in both sepsis and TAA and capable of discriminating sepsis from controls may represent shared inflammation-associated molecular features between the two conditions. The sepsis dataset used for model development was randomly split into training (70%) and testing (30%) sets, with five-fold cross-validation applied during model training. Lasso regression was initially used for feature selection [ 20 , 21 ], followed by SVM to assess the classification performance of the selected features [ 22 ]. The retained features were subsequently evaluated using random forest (RF), which ranked their relative importance based on MeanDecreaseGini [ 23 ]. A threshold of 4, determined by a natural break in the importance distribution, was used for further feature selection. The resulting candidate genes were then evaluated using an artificial neural network (ANN) as a complementary classification approach [ 24 ]. This sequential strategy was used to progressively refine the candidate gene set and assess the classification performance of the selected features ( Fig 1 , Step 2) [ 25 , 26 ]. Validation of the expression levels of selected genes in sepsis and TAA To assess the reproducibility of the expression patterns of the five selected genes across independent cohorts, we performed expression validation in both sepsis and TAA datasets. The genes were evaluated in the original datasets (GSE95233 for sepsis and GSE9106 for TAA) and subsequently validated in the independent external datasets (GSE57065 for sepsis and GSE57691 for TAA). Immune infiltration analysis To investigate whether the shared molecular signatures between sepsis and TAA were associated with alterations in the immune cell composition, immune cell infiltration was estimated in the GSE95233 sepsis dataset and GSE9106 TAA dataset using the CIBERSORT algorithm [ 27 ]. CIBERSORT with the LM22 signature matrix was used to estimate the relative proportions of 22 immune cell types in each sample. Differences in the estimated proportions of individual immune cell types between the disease and control groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. The distributions of immune cell proportions across samples were visualized using the “ggplot2” package. Correlations among the estimated proportions of the 22 immune cell types were assessed and visualized using the “corrplot” package. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Single gene GSEA To explore the biological pathways potentially associated with ARG1 and LIX1L, which showed consistent differential expression in the transcriptomic datasets and were further supported by the in vitro validation, single-gene GSEA was performed separately in the sepsis dataset GSE95233 and the TAA dataset GSE9106. For each gene, samples were stratified according to its expression level, and pathway enrichment patterns associated with gene expression were evaluated. Gene annotation and visualization were performed using the R packages “org.Hs.e.g.,db”, “ggsci”, “patchwork”, and “ggplot2”. Expression assessment of selected genes in LPS-treated VSMCs To experimentally assess whether the expression patterns of the five selected genes could be reproduced under inflammatory conditions in vascular smooth muscle cells, their mRNA expression levels were evaluated in LPS-treated A7R5 cells. The A7R5 rat aortic smooth muscle cell line was cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). At approximately 80% confluence, cells were treated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS, 1 μg/mL) for 24 h to induce an inflammatory response. Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The mRNA expression levels of the five selected genes were quantified by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) using SYBR Green, with GAPDH as the internal control. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^ − ΔΔCt method. In the TAA dataset, 1,322 DEGs were identified between TAA and control samples, comprising 314 upregulated and 1,018 downregulated genes ( Fig 2C , D ). Venn diagram analysis using the VennDiagram package [ 28 ] identified 193 DEGs shared between sepsis and TAA ( Fig 2E ). Machine learning identifies five candidate genes shared between sepsis and TAA To identify candidate genes with discriminatory potential, the 193 shared DEGs were sequentially evaluated using Lasso regression, svm, rf, and ann. Lasso regression initially reduced the candidate set to 14 genes ( Fig 4A , B ). These genes were subsequently evaluated by svm, followed by rf-based variable importance analysis. Based on the rf importance ranking, the top five ranked genes were LIX1L, PRKCH, TRIM32, ARG1, and IL18R1 ( Fig 4C , D ). These five candidate genes were further evaluated using ann, in which LIX1L and ARG1 showed importance scores exceeding 20 ( Fig 4E , F ). The classification performance of the selected features was further assessed across the machine learning models. Lasso regression yielded a mean cross-validated AUC of 0.892. The svm classifier achieved an accuracy of 0.857, with a sensitivity of 0.833 and a specificity of 0.875. Discussion Sepsis and TAA are clinically distinct conditions, but both involve inflammatory dysregulation and vascular injury [ 29 , 30 ]. In this study, integrated transcriptomic analyses identified shared molecular features between sepsis and TAA, with prominent involvement of immune-related processes. Rather than indicating a direct causal relationship between the two conditions, these findings support the possibility that systemic inflammation in sepsis and vascular degeneration in TAA may involve partially overlapping biological responses. This molecular overlap provides a basis for further examining how inflammatory and vascular remodeling pathways may contribute to the pathophysiology of both conditions. The immune-related signals shared between sepsis and TAA are consistent with evidence that immune dysregulation contributes to the pathophysiology of both conditions. Previous transcriptomic studies of sepsis have demonstrated marked alterations in inflammatory and adaptive immune responses, including heterogeneous T-cell-associated programs and broader changes in host immune states [ 31 – 33 ]. In aortic aneurysmal disease, single-cell and transcriptomic studies have likewise identified substantial immune cell involvement and interactions between inflammatory cells and vascular smooth muscle cells within the diseased aortic wall [ 34 – 36 ]. These observations provide biological context for the immune-related overlap identified in our analysis and suggest that altered immune regulation may represent a common feature of sepsis and TAA, although the cellular sources and consequences of these alterations are likely to differ between systemic infection and aortic disease. The metabolic and redox-related signals observed in our analysis are also biologically plausible in both sepsis and aortic disease. In sepsis, mitochondrial dysfunction and excessive reactive oxygen species production are well-recognized features of cellular stress and can contribute to impaired endothelial and immune cell function [ 37 ]. In aortic aneurysmal disease, oxidative stress has been implicated in vascular smooth muscle cell dysfunction, extracellular matrix damage, and weakening of the aortic wall [ 38 ]. These findings from previous studies provide a biological context for the redox- and metabolism-related signatures identified in our analysis and suggest that disturbed cellular energy metabolism and oxidative stress may represent another shared biological feature of sepsis and TAA, although their relative contributions and cellular origins are likely to differ between the two conditions. Among the five selected genes, ARG1 and IL18R1 have the strongest biological links to inflammatory regulation. Previous studies have implicated ARG1-mediated arginine metabolism in immune dysfunction and altered nitric oxide homeostasis, providing a plausible context for its association with both systemic inflammation and vascular dysfunction [ 39 – 41 ]. IL18R1 is a key component of IL-18 signaling, which promotes proinflammatory immune responses, including Th1-associated signaling, suggesting that IL18R1 may reflect another inflammatory pathway relevant to the molecular overlap between sepsis and TAA. In contrast, the relevance of LIX1L, TRIM32, and PRKCH to the shared biology of sepsis and TAA is less established. LIX1L has been linked to RNA-related regulatory processes [ 42 ], whereas TRIM32 and PRKCH have been implicated in inflammatory signaling and vascular cellular responses, respectively [ 43 , 44 ]. These previous observations provide biological plausibility for their identification in our analysis but do not establish disease-specific roles in sepsis or TAA. Thus, ARG1 and IL18R1 are supported by existing evidence related to inflammatory regulation, whereas the potential contributions of LIX1L, TRIM32, and PRKCH require further mechanistic investigation. Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the transcriptomic datasets were generated using different microarray platforms and biological compartments. To avoid direct cross-platform integration, each dataset was analyzed independently and shared DEGs were identified based on overlapping differential expression; nevertheless, residual platform- and tissue-related heterogeneity cannot be completely excluded. Second, the machine-learning analysis was exploratory and was developed primarily using the sepsis dataset; therefore, the selected genes should be regarded as candidate molecular features rather than established diagnostic biomarkers. Third, the experimental assessment was performed in LPS-treated rat A7R5 vascular smooth muscle cells, which represents a simplified inflammatory model and does not reproduce the complex cellular environment of human sepsis or aortic aneurysmal disease. The lack of significant changes in PRKCH and TRIM32 in this model further underscores the need for validation in human primary cells and independent clinical cohorts. Finally, GSE57691 represents abdominal rather than thoracic aortic aneurysm and was therefore used only to assess the reproducibility of selected gene expression patterns across aortic aneurysmal disease, rather than as a direct external validation cohort for TAA. Taken together, these findings support the presence of partially shared molecular features between sepsis and TAA rather than a direct mechanistic link between the two diseases. The observed overlap should therefore be interpreted as evidence of common inflammatory and cellular stress-related signatures, without implying that the same cellular mechanisms operate in both conditions or that sepsis directly promotes TAA. Further experimental studies are required to determine whether the selected genes and pathways functionally contribute to both disease processes.
