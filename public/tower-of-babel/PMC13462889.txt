The comparison outcome of implant-based breast reconstruction with acellular dermal matrix, non-acellular dermal matrix and TiLOOP® reconstruction in breast cancer patients
Authors: Warachya Arjhan, Suphalerk Lohasammakul, Thanachot Kamalapirat, Visnu Lohsiriwat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImplant-based breast reconstruction after mastectomy in breast cancer patients has been increasingly performed over the past decade, improving cosmetic outcomes. The use of supportive matrices has increased in recent years. This study aimed to compare the outcomes of implant-based breast reconstruction between acellular dermal matrix (ADM), non-ADM, and TiLOOP® bra mesh in breast cancer patients.MethodsA retrospective descriptive study, a case series from consecutive patients with breast cancer who underwent mastectomy with implant-based breast reconstruction using ADM, non-ADM, or TiLOOP® by one senior surgeon (V.L., M.D.) at a single center at Siriraj Hospital between 2018 and 2022. Fisher’s exact test was used for the analysis. Demographic data and the history of radiotherapy in each patient were collected. The primary outcomes focus on the significant complications of explantation, implant loss, capsular contracture, skin necrosis, and infection. The secondary outcomes focus on the local recurrence rate and metastasis. Additional surgical interventions to correct the complications were reported.ResultsA total of 36 breast cancer patients (ages between 22 and 64 years old) who underwent unilateral or bilateral mastectomy with implant-based breast reconstruction were organized into three categories of ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP®. The mean follow-up was 2 years and 3 months, with a range of 1 year and 2 months to 4 years. There were 10 patients in the ADM group, 19 in the non-ADM group, and 7 in the TiLOOP® group. In the ADM group, there were 2 infections (20.0%), 1 necrosis (10.0%), and 2 capsular contractures (20.0%). In the non-ADM group, there were 2 explantations (10.5%), 2 capsular contractures (10.5%), 2 local recurrences (10.5%), and 1 metastasis (5.3%). In the TiLOOP® group, there were 2 capsular contractures (28.6%). In the TiLOOP® group, two patients who received adjuvant radiotherapy developed capsular contracture: one developed grade III and the other developed grade IV. There was 1 patient in the non-ADM group who had previous chest wall radiation before reconstruction and developed capsular contracture grade IV without postoperative radiotherapy. All capsular contracture grade III or IV cases required additional revision breast surgery.ConclusionsNo significant difference in complication between ADM, non-ADM and TiLOOP® implant-based breast reconstruction (P>0.05). The limitations of this study were a low number of cases and short-term follow-up.Keywords: Acellular dermal matrix (ADM), TiLOOP® bra mesh, breast implant, capsular contracture, breast reconstruction

Highlight box

Key findings

• This retrospective case series found no statistically significant difference in major complications among acellular dermal matrix (ADM), non-ADM, and TiLOOP® implant-based breast reconstruction in breast cancer patients. Capsular contracture was observed in all groups, while explantation and local recurrence occurred only in the non-ADM group.

What is known and what is new?

• Implant-based breast reconstruction is widely used after mastectomy, and supportive materials such as ADM and synthetic mesh may help improve implant positioning and aesthetic outcomes.

• This study provides single-center clinical data comparing ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® reconstruction in breast cancer patients, including postoperative complications, local recurrence, metastasis, and the possible influence of radiotherapy.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® may all be considered feasible options for implant-based breast reconstruction. However, treatment selection should be individualized according to patient factors, tissue condition, expected radiotherapy, surgeon preference, and resource availability. Larger prospective studies with longer follow-up are needed to confirm comparative safety and oncologic outcomes.

Introduction

Implant-based breast reconstruction after mastectomy has been increasingly performed in breast cancer patients over the last decade (1-3). Due to the higher incidence rate of breast cancer in women globally, the number of surgical interventions has correspondingly increased. In the year 2020, there were over 2.3 million new cases of breast cancer, resulting in 685,000 deaths worldwide (4). Implant-based breast reconstruction improves the cosmetic outcome in breast cancer patients who have undergone mastectomy (3,5,6). The local recurrence rate of breast cancer after mastectomy with reconstruction, as reported in one systematic review, ranged from 0.6% to 15.8%, with a pooled estimated rate of 4.7%, and the mean time of local recurrence was between 13.2 and 38 months (7,8).

Both classic surgical techniques utilizing only muscle and fascia support and those employing supportive matrices such as biological acellular dermal matrix (ADM) and synthetic mesh (TiLOOP®) are widely used in practice.

In addition to the aforementioned methods, there are also other augmentation techniques such as other meshes, wherein each type of matrix or mesh used encompasses numerous brands. The choice of material for augmentation and the selection of a specific brand for each patient depend on the surgeon’s preference and financial considerations (9-11).

ADM is one type of mesh. It can be derived from human skin, for example, AlloMax, AlloDerm, or FlexHD, or from animal skin, such as SurgiMend, where the cells are removed, leaving the support structure intact. By mimicking natural materials, ADM aids in controlling the position of the implant, preventing it from shifting from the inframammary fold, and inhibiting the formation of contractile pseudocapsules around the implant (10-13).

The properties of the TiLOOP® bra include titanised Type 1a polypropylene mesh, with a weight of either 16 or 35 g/m2, a pore size of 1.0 mm, monofilament fabric, non-resorbable nature, atraumatic and laser-cut edges, EO-sterilization (ethylene oxide), and being pyrogen-free. It aids in controlling the implant position as well. Previous research has shown good cosmetic outcomes, a high level of patient satisfaction, and fewer instances of implant loss with the TiLOOP® bra (14,15).

A review of previous research on complications associated with all three methods revealed low rates of severe complications. The overall complication rate for silicone augmentation surgery with Method 1 (ADM) was approximately 26.8% to 30%, for Method 2 (non-ADM), it was approximately 19% to 20%, and for Method 3 (TiLOOP®), it was 11.5% to 30.2%. Complications identified in previous studies include early postoperative complications (such as wound infection, hematoma, wound dehiscence, skin or nipple necrosis, seroma) and late postoperative complications (including capsular contracture, implant loss, implant rupture, and patient dissatisfaction) (16,17).

At our institution, ADM-based reconstruction was performed in approximately 2–3 cases per year, TiLOOP®-based reconstruction in 4–5 cases per year, and non-ADM reconstruction in 10–20 cases per year throughout the study period (2018–2022).

Objectives

The objectives of this study are to describe the adverse events associated with implant-based breast reconstruction using ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP®, to compare outcomes among these three groups in the context of implant-based breast reconstruction, with a primary focus on assessing significant complications, and to evaluate the secondary outcomes of local recurrence rate and metastasis following implant-based breast reconstruction. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2025-aw-423/rc).

Methods

A retrospective descriptive study and case series were conducted on consecutive patients with breast cancer who underwent mastectomy with implant-based breast reconstruction using ADM, non-ADM, or TiLOOP® by one senior surgeon, Dr. V.L., at a single center Siriraj Hospital between 2018 and 2022. The time to reconstruction included both immediate breast reconstruction (IBR) and delayed breast reconstruction (DBR). Demographic data and the history of radiotherapy for each patient were collected.

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The protocol was reviewed and approved by the Siriraj Institutional Review Board (approval No. 890/2565). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified clinical data.

The primary outcomes focused on major complications including explantation, implant loss, capsular contracture, necrosis, and infection. Secondary outcomes centered on the local recurrence rate and metastasis. Any additional surgical interventions performed to correct complications were also documented. Capsular contracture was clinically assessed and graded according to the Baker classification system: grade I (imperceptible; soft breast with naturally appearing contour), grade II (minimal firmness; breast looks normal), grade III (moderate firmness; breast looks abnormal), and grade IV (severe firmness; breast feels hard, looks abnormal, and may be painful).

Information on breast cancer patients was collected using a case record form while ensuring the confidentiality of patient information. Patients were continuously followed up with mammograms (MMGs) and/or ultrasounds (U/S) to monitor for new or recurrent lesions, abnormal calcification, or masses, facilitating timely diagnosis and treatment planning. Results of the most recent MMG and/or U/S were recorded in the case record.

The first MMG and U/S were scheduled 1 year after surgery, with subsequent follow-ups determined by the BIRADS results of the MMG and/or U/S. If no abnormalities were detected, follow-ups were scheduled every 6 months or 1 year. An annual appointment for MMG and U/S was arranged accordingly.

Sample size calculation

This research is a case series data collection. The data was gathering from 2018 to 2022 and identified a number of patients who underwent implant-based breast reconstruction surgery with ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® for breast cancer treatment, with 10 to 20 patients per group.

Therefore, the researcher aimed to collect the entire sample size, expecting approximately 10, 20, and 10 patients who received surgical methods 1, 2, and 3 respectively, totaling 40 cases.

After consulting with a statistician, it was determined that the results of adverse events had too few cases. Consequently, the researcher decided to report each of the three groups as a case series.

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for this study were breast cancer patients who underwent total mastectomy with either IBR or DBR using ADM, non-ADM, or TiLOOP® between the years 2018 to 2022, with clear documentation of the history of postoperative radiation therapy or no radiation therapy. Additionally, participants were required to have follow-up for postoperative complications for at least 6 months and to undergo follow-up with MMG or U/S after surgery.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria for this study included patients who were not diagnosed with breast cancer, those who underwent breast reconstruction surgery with silicone using methods other than ADM, non-ADM, or TiLOOP®, loss of follow-up or follow-up of less than 6 months, inadequate documentation regarding postoperative radiation history, and absence of evidence of postoperative MMG or U/S.

Statistical analysis

The primary objective of this study was to report adverse events associated with implant-based breast reconstruction using ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® in breast cancer patients, with results presented in numbers and percentages. Additionally, demographic data, including age, were described with means and standard deviations reported. Mean ages of all three groups were compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) statistics, contingent upon a sufficient sample size. Adverse events in all three groups were compared using Fisher’s exact test, provided the sample size was adequate. Statistical significance was considered when P<0.05.

A total of 36 breast cancer patients, aged between 22 and 64 years old, who underwent unilateral or bilateral mastectomy with implant-based breast reconstruction, were included in the study. All cases of capsular contracture grade III or IV required additional revision breast surgery, and explantation was only found in the non-ADM group. Two local recurrences and one distant metastasis were observed in the non-ADM group. Notably, one patient in the non-ADM group, diagnosed with locally advanced breast cancer, developed distant metastasis at 12 months.

There were 5 missing pieces of information regarding tumor status, nodal status, and pathology, such as Luminal subtype and lymphovascular invasion status. Additionally, some information was missing regarding the history of previous radiotherapy, adjuvant chemotherapy, and adjuvant hormone therapy in the non-ADM group. One patient in each group was diagnosed with ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS), which has no luminal subtype. The history of postoperative radiation therapy was fully documented.

No cases of implant extrusion or implant failure were reported. The patient with metastasis had a history of node positivity for malignancy before breast reconstruction.

Discussion

Our results in this study show that there was no difference in complications between ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® implant-based breast reconstructions (P>0.05) in breast cancer patients, with a mean follow-up time of 27 months (range, 14–48 months). However, our data was limited due to the low number of cases, inadequate data for tumor characteristics and lymph node status in some cases, selection bias, and short-term follow-up.

From a total of 36 patients who underwent unilateral or bilateral mastectomy with implant-based breast reconstruction, there were 10 patients in the ADM group, 19 patients in the non-ADM group, and 7 patients in the TiLOOP® group. The complications detected in our study were capsular contracture, infection, and skin necrosis in the ADM and TiLOOP® groups, which were similar to complications found in other studies from literature reviews and comparative studies of ADM, mesh, and non-ADM implant-based breast reconstruction (12,16-18). There were no reports of implant extrusion or implant failure in the non-ADM and TiLOOP® groups.

Postoperative radiation therapy may have an effect on implant-based breast reconstruction, and previous systematic evidence has reported higher complication risks in implant-based reconstruction performed in the setting of prereconstruction or postreconstruction radiotherapy (19). In our study, in the TiLOOP® group, two patients who underwent postoperative radiotherapy developed capsular contracture: one patient experienced grade III, and another experienced grade IV. In the ADM group, two patients developed capsular contracture: one at grade II and the other at grade III, without previous radiation therapy or postoperative radiotherapy. In the non-ADM groups, two patients developed capsular contracture: one at grade III, who did not have previous radiation therapy or postoperative radiotherapy. The other, with grade IV, had previous chest wall radiation. Due to the small sample size, a formal statistical analysis of the association between radiotherapy and reconstruction outcomes was not performed; this remains an important area for future prospective studies with larger cohorts.

Regarding the issue of local recurrence and metastasis, our study identified 2 local recurrences and 1 distant metastasis in the non-ADM group. One patient in the non-ADM group, diagnosed with locally advanced breast cancer, experienced distant metastasis at 12 months. These findings should be interpreted in the context of previous oncologic-safety studies and consensus discussions on mastectomy with reconstruction, which have reported variable local recurrence patterns and emphasized the importance of tumor biology, stage, systemic therapy, and follow-up duration when interpreting recurrence outcomes after reconstructive surgery (6-8,20-22). However, the data are still limited due to inadequate information on tumor characteristics and lymph node status in some cases (as some patients underwent total mastectomy at other hospitals and did not bring their medical records to our hospital).

The choice of reconstructive technique in our study was primarily guided by surgeon preference and individual patient factors—including skin envelope quality, chest wall tissue availability, and patient preference—rather than cancer stage alone. Nonetheless, cancer stage may indirectly influence reconstructive planning by determining the extent of mastectomy, the likelihood of requiring adjuvant radiotherapy, and the overall treatment timeline, all of which are important considerations when selecting the most appropriate reconstructive approach.

In the future, with a larger patient population, our study could yield more accurate and reliable results.

Conclusions

There was no difference in complications between ADM, non-ADM, and TiLOOP® implant-based breast reconstructions (P>0.05). The limitations of this study included a low number of cases, inadequate data for tumor characteristics and lymph node evaluation in some cases, selection bias, and short-term follow-up. Additionally, breast size and patient-reported satisfaction data were not systematically collected, limiting the assessment of aesthetic outcomes. The study also lacked sufficient numbers to perform a formal statistical subgroup analysis of the interaction between radiotherapy and reconstructive outcomes.
