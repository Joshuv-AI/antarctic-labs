A Fourier-Free Density-Increment Proof of Roth’s Theorem
Mark Lewko

Abstract
. We give an elementary, Fourier-free proof of Roth’s theorem. The proof follows Roth’s original density-increment strategy, but replaces the usual Fourier-analytic step with a direct combinatorial argument involving averages over sub-progressions.



1.
Introduction

In 1953, Roth proved that every subset of the integers of positive upper density
contains a nontrivial three-term arithmetic progression. This can be stated in
the following finite form.

Theorem 1

(Roth
[
8
]
)
.

For every
\alpha>0
, if
N
is sufficiently large, then every set

A\subset\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
with
\lvert A\rvert\geq\alpha N
contains a
three-term arithmetic progression
x,\,x+r,\,x+2r
with
r\neq 0
.

Roth’s theorem is a foundational result in density Ramsey theory; its extension
to longer arithmetic progressions is a celebrated theorem of Szemerédi
[
9
]
.
Proofs of Roth’s theorem have been given using quite different methods: Roth’s original
proof is Fourier analytic
[
8
]
; Furstenberg proved Szemerédi’s
theorem in full using ergodic-theoretic methods
[
2
]
; and graph-theoretic
proofs of Roth’s theorem follow from the triangle removal lemma of Ruzsa and
Szemerédi
[
7
]
. More combinatorial proofs have also been given using Szemerédi’s cube
lemma
[
4
]
as well as using ideas related to the more general Hales–Jewett theorem
[
1
]
. There is
also a rich history of quantitative improvements, which we do not attempt to
summarize here; see
[
5
,
6
]
for recent surveys.

Roth’s original proof uses a density-increment argument. Starting with a set

A\subset[N]
with no nontrivial three-term progressions, one proves that
A

has increased density on a long subprogression. Passing to that progression
and rescaling, one repeats the argument. If the original interval is sufficiently
long, the density would eventually have to exceed
1
, providing a contradiction.
The purpose of this note is to give a proof with the same density-increment
structure, but without using Fourier analysis. Instead of detecting a large
Fourier coefficient, we estimate certain averages over subprogressions directly.

The combinatorial approaches to Roth’s theorem have typically produced very weak
quantitative bounds. The argument here stays close to Roth’s density-increment
framework and gives a bound of the form

r_{3}(N)\lesssim\frac{N}{(\log\log N)^{c}}

with
c=1/11
. This can be improved somewhat at the expense of complicating the exposition, but the argument in its current form does not seem capable of recovering the exponent
c=1
obtained by Roth’s original argument.

2.
Notation

We write
A\lesssim B
to mean
A\leq CB
for a positive absolute constant

C
, and similarly
A\gtrsim B
. We write
A\sim B
when both
A\lesssim B

and
B\lesssim A
hold. All implicit constants are absolute. For a prime

m
, write
\mathbb{Z}_{m}=\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}=\mathbb{Z}_{m}\setminus\{0\}
.

3.
Preliminaries

Write
[N]=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and choose a prime
m
with
4N<m<8N
, which we
may do by Bertrand’s postulate. We embed
[N]
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
in the obvious way.
The point of choosing
m>2N
is that a three-term progression in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
whose
terms all lie in
[N]
is a genuine integer progression.

Let
\alpha=\lvert A\rvert/N
be the density of
A
in
[N]
, and define

f=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\alpha\mathbf{1}_{[N]}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Then
f
has mean zero on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
,
satisfies
-\alpha\leq f\leq 1
, and is supported on
[N]
. For any function

g\colon\mathbb{Z}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, set

\mathcal{E}(g)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}g(x)g(x+t)\right)^{2}.

For the balanced function
f
, write
R(t)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)f(x+t)
, so

\mathcal{E}(f)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(t)^{2}
. We will use the following
non-negativity property.

Lemma 2
.

For any
h,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
, one has

(3.1)

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)R(kd)\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=kh^{-1}
. Using
t=hd
, we have

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)R(kd)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(t)R(\lambda t)=\sum_{a,b,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(a)f(a+t)f(b)f(b+\lambda t).

To make the non-negativity explicit, use the bijective change of variables

(a,b,t)\mapsto(a,c,u)
given by
c=a+t
and
u=b-\lambda a
. Then

b=u+\lambda a
and
b+\lambda t=u+\lambda c
, so the last sum becomes

\sum_{u,a,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(a)f(c)f(u+\lambda a)f(u+\lambda c)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(a)f(u+\lambda a)\right)^{2}\geq 0.

∎

4.
No progressions imply large energy

For functions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, define

\Lambda(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}):=\sum_{x,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x+r)f_{3}(x+2r).

If
A\subset[N]\subset\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then
\Lambda(\mathbf{1}_{A},\mathbf{1}_{A},\mathbf{1}_{A})
is the
number of three-term arithmetic progressions in
A
, including the trivial
progressions with
r=0
. Hence, if
A
contains no nontrivial three-term
progressions, then
\Lambda(\mathbf{1}_{A},\mathbf{1}_{A},\mathbf{1}_{A})=\lvert A\rvert
. On the other hand,

\Lambda(\mathbf{1}_{[N]},\mathbf{1}_{[N]},\mathbf{1}_{[N]})\gtrsim N^{2}
, since
[N]
contains

\gtrsim N^{2}
three-term progressions.

Lemma 3
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded in absolute value by an
absolute constant. Then

\lvert\Lambda(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\rvert^{4}\lesssim m^{5}\min_{1\leq i\leq 3}\mathcal{E}(g_{i}).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the following estimate. Let
F,h_{1},h_{2}\colon\mathbb{Z}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
with
h_{1},h_{2}
bounded, and let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
with
a\neq b
. Then

\left|\sum_{y,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}F(y)h_{1}(y+ar)h_{2}(y+br)\right|^{4}\lesssim m^{5}\mathcal{E}(F).

The three possible choices of the distinguished slot in
\Lambda
are obtained,
after a change of variables, from this estimate by taking
(a,b)=(1,2)
,

(-1,1)
, and
(-2,-1)
.

Let
T
denote the sum inside the fourth-power. Put
\lambda=ba^{-1}
and
change variables from
r
to
u=y+ar
. Then

T=\sum_{y,u\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}F(y)h_{1}(u)h_{2}((1-\lambda)y+\lambda u).

By Cauchy–Schwarz in
u
,

\lvert T\rvert^{2}\lesssim m\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left|\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}F(y)h_{2}((1-\lambda)y+\lambda u)\right|^{2}.

Expanding the square, then putting
t=y^{\prime}-y
and
z=(1-\lambda)y+\lambda u
, gives

\displaystyle\lvert T\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim m\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}F(y)F(y+t)\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}h_{2}(z)h_{2}(z+(1-\lambda)t)\right).

A final Cauchy–Schwarz in
t
gives

\displaystyle\lvert T\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim m\mathcal{E}(F)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}h_{2}(z)h_{2}(z+(1-\lambda)t)\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim m^{5/2}\mathcal{E}(F)^{1/2},

using that
|h_{2}|\leq 1
. Squaring proves the estimate. Applying this with each

g_{i}
as the distinguished function gives the minimum on the right.
∎

Lemma 4

(Energy controls the trilinear operator)
.

Let
u,v\colon\mathbb{Z}_{m}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded in absolute value by an absolute
constant, and put
f=u-v
. Then

\lvert\Lambda(u,u,u)-\Lambda(v,v,v)\rvert^{4}\lesssim m^{5}\mathcal{E}(f).

Proof.

We telescope one slot at a time:

\displaystyle\Lambda(u,u,u)-\Lambda(v,v,v)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\Lambda(u,u,u)-\Lambda(v,u,u)\bigr)+\bigl(\Lambda(v,u,u)-\Lambda(v,v,u)\bigr)

\displaystyle+\bigl(\Lambda(v,v,u)-\Lambda(v,v,v)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(f,u,u)+\Lambda(v,f,u)+\Lambda(v,v,f).

Each term is bounded by
m^{5/4}\mathcal{E}(f)^{1/4}
by
Lemma
3
, since in each term the other two factors are
bounded. The claim follows after raising to the fourth power.
∎

Lemma 5

(Lack of progressions implies large energy)
.

If
A\subset[N]
is 3AP-free and
N\gtrsim\alpha^{-3}
, then

\mathcal{E}(f)\gtrsim\alpha^{12}m^{3}.

Proof.

Put
u=\mathbf{1}_{A}
and
v=\alpha\mathbf{1}_{[N]}
. Then
f=u-v
and

\lvert u\rvert,\lvert v\rvert\leq 1
. Since
A
has no nontrivial three-term progressions,

\Lambda(u,u,u)=\lvert A\rvert=\alpha N
, while

\Lambda(v,v,v)=\alpha^{3}\Lambda(\mathbf{1}_{[N]},\mathbf{1}_{[N]},\mathbf{1}_{[N]})\gtrsim\alpha^{3}N^{2}
.
If
N\gtrsim\alpha^{-3}
, then

\lvert\Lambda(u,u,u)-\Lambda(v,v,v)\rvert\gtrsim\alpha^{3}N^{2}
.
Lemma
4
therefore gives

\alpha^{12}N^{8}\lesssim m^{5}\mathcal{E}(f)
. Since
m\sim N
, this gives

\mathcal{E}(f)\gtrsim\alpha^{12}m^{3}
.
∎

5.
Large energy implies a modular density increment

Our next goal is to find a long modular progression on which
f
has positive
average. For an integer
1\leq\ell<m/2
and a step
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, set

S_{d}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f(x+id),\qquad V_{d}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert S_{d}(x)\rvert^{2}.

Thus
S_{d}(x)
is the sum of
f
on the length-
\ell
modular progression
starting at
x
with step
d
, and
V_{d}
is the second moment of these sums
as
x
varies.

Lemma 6

(Moments of the progression sums)
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
,

(5.1)

V_{d}=\sum_{\lvert h\rvert<\ell}(\ell-\lvert h\rvert)R(hd).

Also

(5.2)

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}V_{d}\leq\alpha\ell m^{2}.

Finally, if
\mathcal{E}(f)\geq\beta m^{3}
and
\ell\lesssim\beta m/\alpha^{2}
, then

(5.3)

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}}V_{d}^{2}\gtrsim\beta\ell^{3}m^{3}.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.1
). Expanding the square gives

V_{d}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f(x+id)\right)^{2}=\sum_{0\leq i,j<\ell}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x+id)f(x+jd)

=\sum_{0\leq i,j<\ell}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(y)f(y+(j-i)d)=\sum_{0\leq i,j<\ell}R((j-i)d)=\sum_{|h|<\ell}(\ell-|h|)R(hd).

Here we used the change of variables
y=x+id
and then used that there are
\ell-|h|
pairs
(i,j)
satisfy
0\leq i<\ell
and

0\leq i+h<\ell
such that
h=j-i
. This proves (
5.1
).

Now we sum (
5.1
) over
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. The term
h=0
contributes

\ell mR(0)
. If
h\neq 0
, then
d\mapsto hd
is a bijection on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, so

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(t)=\left(\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)\right)^{2}=0.

Therefore
\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}V_{d}=\ell mR(0)
. Since

R(0)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)^{2}\leq\alpha m
, this proves
(
5.2
).

It remains to prove (
5.3
). Squaring
(
5.1
) and summing over
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
gives

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}V_{d}^{2}=\sum_{\lvert h\rvert,\lvert k\rvert<\ell}(\ell-\lvert h\rvert)(\ell-\lvert k\rvert)\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)R(kd).

By Lemma
2
, every term with
h,k\neq 0
is nonnegative. Terms with
exactly one of
h,k
equal to
0
vanish by the preceding discussion. Thus we
may obtain a lower bound by considering just the diagonal terms
h=k\neq 0
. For
h\neq 0
, multiplication by

h
is a bijection on
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, so
\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)^{2}=\mathcal{E}(f)
. Hence

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}V_{d}^{2}\geq\sum_{0<\lvert h\rvert<\ell}(\ell-\lvert h\rvert)^{2}\mathcal{E}(f)\gtrsim\ell^{3}\mathcal{E}(f)\geq\beta\ell^{3}m^{3}.

This still includes the zero step. For
d=0
, we have
S_{0}(x)=\ell f(x)
, and
therefore
V_{0}=\ell^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)^{2}\leq\alpha\ell^{2}m
, so

V_{0}^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}\ell^{4}m^{2}
. The assumption

\ell\lesssim\beta m/\alpha^{2}
, with the implicit constant chosen small enough,
makes this smaller than a fixed fraction of
\beta\ell^{3}m^{3}
. Removing the zero
step gives (
5.3
).
∎

Lemma 7

(Dense modular progression)
.

Assume
\mathcal{E}(f)\geq\beta m^{3}
, with
0<\beta\lesssim\alpha^{2}
, and choose
an integer
\ell
satisfying

\ell\sim\frac{\beta m}{\alpha^{2}},\qquad 1\leq\ell<m/2,

where the implicit constant is chosen small enough. Then there exist

d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that

(5.4)

S_{d}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f(x+id)\gtrsim\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\ell.

Proof.

Set
M=\max_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}}V_{d}
. By (
5.3
) and
(
5.2
),

\beta\ell^{3}m^{3}\lesssim\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}}V_{d}^{2}\leq M\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}V_{d}\leq M\alpha\ell m^{2}.

Thus there is a nonzero step
d
with

(5.5)

V_{d}\gtrsim\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\ell^{2}m.

We now extract a large positive value of
S_{d}(x)
. Since
\lvert S_{d}(x)\rvert\leq\ell

for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, the definition of
V_{d}
gives

V_{d}\leq\ell\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert S_{d}(x)\rvert
. Using (
5.5
), we get

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert S_{d}(x)\rvert\gtrsim\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\ell m.

Also
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}S_{d}(x)=\ell\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)=0
. Therefore the total
positive mass of
S_{d}
is exactly half of
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert S_{d}(x)\rvert
, and
hence some
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
satisfies (
5.4
).
∎

6.
Finding a genuine integer progression

We now show how to pass from a density increment on a modular progression to
one on a genuine integer progression.

Lemma 8

(Finding a genuine integer progression)
.

Suppose
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
satisfy

\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f(x+id)\geq\eta\ell
for some
0<\eta\leq 1
. If

\ell\gtrsim\eta^{-2}
, then there is an arithmetic progression
P\subset[N]

such that

\lvert P\rvert\gtrsim\eta\sqrt{\ell}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\lvert A\cap P\rvert}{\lvert P\rvert}\geq\alpha+c\eta

for an absolute constant
c>0
.

Proof.

We find a short subprogression of the original progression whose step is
represented by a small integer, then intersect it with
[N]
.

Apply the pigeonhole principle to the
\lfloor\sqrt{\ell}\rfloor+1
elements

0,d,2d,\ldots,\lfloor\sqrt{\ell}\rfloor d
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
: two of these have a
difference represented by an integer of size
\lesssim m/\sqrt{\ell}
. Hence
there is an integer
q
, with
1\leq q\leq\sqrt{\ell}
, such that
qd
is
represented by an integer
s
with
\lvert s\rvert\lesssim m/\sqrt{\ell}
. Since

m\sim N
, after enlarging the absolute constant in
\ell\gtrsim\eta^{-2}
, we
may assume
\lvert s\rvert<N
.

Taking every
q
th term of the original progression gives
q
subprogressions,
each with step represented by
s
. Divide each of these into consecutive progressions
of length
K\sim\sqrt{\ell}
, choosing the implicit constant in
K
small
enough that each subprogression has length at least
K
and every progression travels
distance
<m/10
before reduction modulo
m
. Merging any incomplete final
progression into its predecessor, we may assume every progression has length

\sim\sqrt{\ell}
. These progressions still partition the original
\ell
terms.
Since the original average of
f
is at least
\eta
, one block
\widetilde{P}

satisfies

\sum_{u\in\widetilde{P}}f(u)\geq\eta\lvert\widetilde{P}\rvert.

It remains to intersect
\widetilde{P}
with
[N]
. On the integer line, the
copies of
[N]
modulo
m
are separated by gaps of length
m-N>3m/4
, while

\widetilde{P}
travels distance
<m/10
. Thus
\widetilde{P}
cannot meet

[N]
, leave
[N]
, and later meet
[N]
again. Hence the terms of

\widetilde{P}
lying in
[N]
form one consecutive subprogression; call it

P
. Since
f=0
outside
[N]
, passing from
\widetilde{P}
to
P
does not
change the sum of
f
. As
f\leq 1
, we get

\lvert P\rvert\geq\eta\lvert\widetilde{P}\rvert\gtrsim\eta\sqrt{\ell}
, and the average of

f
on
P
is at least
\eta
. Finally,
P
is an ordinary integer arithmetic
progression: all its terms lie in
[N]
, and its step is represented by the
integer
s
with
\lvert s\rvert<N
. Since
f=\mathbf{1}_{A}-\alpha
on
[N]
,

\frac{\lvert A\cap P\rvert}{\lvert P\rvert}=\alpha+\frac{1}{\lvert P\rvert}\sum_{u\in P}f(u)\geq\alpha+\eta.

This completes the proof.
∎

Combining this with Lemma
5
gives the following density increment.

Proposition 9

(Density increment)
.

Let
A\subset[N]
have density
\alpha
, and suppose that
A
contains no
nontrivial three-term arithmetic progression. If
N\gtrsim\alpha^{-32}
, then
there is an arithmetic progression
P\subset[N]
such that

\lvert P\rvert\gtrsim\alpha^{16}N^{1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\lvert A\cap P\rvert}{\lvert P\rvert}\geq\alpha+c\alpha^{11}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
,
\mathcal{E}(f)\gtrsim\alpha^{12}m^{3}
. Put

\beta\sim\alpha^{12}
and choose

\ell\sim\beta m/\alpha^{2}\sim\alpha^{10}m
. The hypothesis

N\gtrsim\alpha^{-32}
ensures that such an integer
\ell
satisfies

1\leq\ell<m/2
. Lemma
7
gives
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
and

x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
with
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}f(x+id)\gtrsim\alpha^{11}\ell
. Set

\eta\sim\alpha^{11}
. Again using
N\gtrsim\alpha^{-32}
, we have

\ell\gtrsim\eta^{-2}
. Lemma
8
gives an arithmetic progression

P\subset[N]
with density increment
\gtrsim\alpha^{11}
and length

\lvert P\rvert\gtrsim\eta\sqrt{\ell}\gtrsim\alpha^{11}\alpha^{5}m^{1/2}\gtrsim\alpha^{16}N^{1/2}.

∎

7.
Proof of Roth’s theorem and the quantitative bound

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Fix
\alpha_{0}>0
and suppose, for contradiction, that there are arbitrarily
large
N
and 3AP-free sets
A\subset[N]
with
\lvert A\rvert\geq\alpha_{0}N
.
Applying Proposition
9
, then rescaling the progression it
gives back to an interval, produces a new 3AP-free set with density increased
by at least
c\alpha_{0}^{11}
. Repeating this, the density exceeds
1
after

O(\alpha_{0}^{-11})
steps.

It remains only to ensure that the iteration can be run for that many steps.
At each step the ambient length is replaced by a progression of length at least

c\alpha_{0}^{16}N_{j}^{1/2}
. Since the number of steps depends only on

\alpha_{0}
, choosing the original
N
sufficiently large in terms of
\alpha_{0}

keeps every intermediate interval above the threshold in
Proposition
9
. This contradiction proves the theorem.
∎

A more careful analysis gives an explicit double-logarithmic bound.

Corollary 10
.

There is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

r_{3}(N)\leq C\frac{N}{(\log\log N)^{1/11}}

for all sufficiently large
N
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset[N]
be 3AP-free, and write
\alpha=\lvert A\rvert/N
. Set

J=\lfloor c_{0}\alpha^{-11}\rfloor
, where
c_{0}>0
is a sufficiently small
absolute constant. If the density increment can be applied
J
times, then each
step increases the density by at least
c\alpha^{11}
, since the densities only
increase. After
J
steps the density would exceed
1
, which is impossible.

It remains to check that the lengths stay large enough for
J
steps. At each
step, after rescaling the subprogression back to an interval,
Proposition
9
gives

\alpha_{j+1}\geq\alpha_{j}+c\alpha_{j}^{11},\qquad N_{j+1}\geq c\alpha_{j}^{16}N_{j}^{1/2}.

Since
\alpha_{j}\geq\alpha
, the length estimate implies

\log N_{j+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\log N_{j}-C\log(e/\alpha).

Iterating this crude bound gives

\log N_{j}\geq 2^{-j}\log N-C\log(e/\alpha).

Thus, if
\log N\geq C2^{J}\log(e/\alpha)
, then every
N_{j}
with
j\leq J

remains larger than a fixed power of
\alpha^{-1}
, and in particular remains
above the threshold required by Proposition
9
. Since

J\sim\alpha^{-11}
, this condition follows from

\log\log N\geq C\alpha^{-11},

after increasing
C
.

Therefore, if
\log\log N
were larger than a sufficiently large constant times

\alpha^{-11}
, the iteration could run for
J
steps and force the density
above
1
. Hence
\log\log N\lesssim\alpha^{-11}
, so

\alpha\lesssim(\log\log N)^{-1/11}
. Since
\alpha=\lvert A\rvert/N
, this gives

r_{3}(N)\lesssim\frac{N}{(\log\log N)^{1/11}}.

This proves the corollary.
∎

8.
Further remarks

Although the proof above is written without Fourier analysis, several of the steps have a simple Fourier interpretation. Let
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix/m}
and

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}f(x)e(-\xi x)
. One then has

\widehat{R}(\xi)=\lvert\widehat{f}(\xi)\rvert^{2}
. Hence Parseval gives

\mathcal{E}(f)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(t)^{2}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert\widehat{f}(\xi)\rvert^{4}.

Thus, up to the normalization convention,
\mathcal{E}(f)
is the fourth power
of what is called in the modern Gowers-style literature the
U^{2}
-norm. In the
usual modern Fourier-analytic proofs of Roth’s theorem, the implication from a small

3
-term progression count to a large
U^{2}
-norm is standard.

The key non-negativity statement in Lemma
2
also has a short Fourier
explanation. If
h,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}^{\times}
and
\lambda=kh^{-1}
, then

(8.1)

\sum_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(hd)R(kd)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}R(t)R(\lambda t)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\lvert\widehat{f}(\xi)\rvert^{2}\lvert\widehat{f}(-\lambda^{-1}\xi)\rvert^{2}\geq 0.
