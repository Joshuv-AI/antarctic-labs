A remark on the invariance of K \displaystyle K -theory under duality
Georg Lehner

Abstract
. In this short remark, we explain that two examples of invariance under duality for a localizing invariant F \displaystyle F hold purely formally when F \displaystyle F is K \displaystyle K -theory, whereas the general statement for arbitrary localizing invariants does not reduce to a formal statement. We record a counterexample to the claim that the universal localizing invariant is invariant under the operation of taking opposite categories, originally due to Tabuada.

Primary: 19D99, 18F25, Secondary: 18N60, 18F20, 16K50

We will follow the general conventions on
\displaystyle K
-theory of dualizable categories as laid out in
[
Efi25
]
and
[
KNP24
]
. Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category, and
\displaystyle F:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
a finitary localizing invariant in the sense of
[
BGT10
]
. In general, one does not have
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Sp})
. However, in the following two examples, it still holds that

F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}^{\vee}).

Example 1
.

It was remarked in the previous article
[
Leh26
]
by the author that for a stably locally compact space
\displaystyle X
there is an equivalence

F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp}))\simeq F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})),

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})=\text{Fun}^{L}(\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp}),\mathrm{Sp})=\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathrm{Sp})^{\vee}
is the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of cosheaves on
\displaystyle X
. This equivalence, in the case where
\displaystyle X
is locally compact Hausdorff, is a trivial consequence of Verdier duality, but Verdier duality in the sense that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}(X,\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{Cosh}(X,\mathcal{C})
fails for stably locally compact spaces, and a version using the de Groot dual
\displaystyle X^{\vee}
of
\displaystyle X
needs to be used.

Example 2
.

If
\displaystyle P
is a continuous poset, two independent calculations due to Efimov
[
Efi25
, Section 5]
show that

F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}(P,\mathrm{Sp}))\simeq\bigoplus_{p\in P^{\omega}}F(\mathbb{S})\simeq F^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Cosh}(P,\mathrm{Sp})).

We note that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cosh}(P,\mathcal{C})\simeq\text{Fun}^{L}(\mathrm{Sh}(P,\mathrm{Sp}),\mathcal{C})
, see
[
Lur18
, Section 21.1]
.

Now consider the involution
\displaystyle(-)^{\mathrm{op}}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\simeq\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}
, which sends a stable idempotent complete
\displaystyle\infty
-category to its opposite.

Theorem 3
.

There exists a natural equivalence of functors
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}
,

K\simeq K\circ(-)^{\mathrm{op}}.

Proof.

Observe that the functor
\displaystyle(-)^{\simeq}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
of taking the groupoid core is invariant under
\displaystyle(-)^{\mathrm{op}}
. Since
\displaystyle K
-theory is the universal localizing invariant equipped with a natural transformation
\displaystyle(-)^{\simeq}\rightarrow\Omega^{\infty}K
, see
[
BGT10
]
, the same holds for
\displaystyle K
-theory.
∎

This result extends immediately to dualizable stable
\displaystyle\infty
-categories. The functor
\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}:\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}},\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{C}^{\vee}=\text{Fun}^{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{Sp})
gives an involution,
[
Efi25
, Section 1.7]
. If
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
is compactly generated, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\vee}=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\mathrm{op}})
, hence
\displaystyle(-)^{\vee}
extends the involution
\displaystyle(-)^{\mathrm{op}}
to dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-categories.

Corollary 4
.

There exists a natural equivalence of functors
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}
,

K^{cont}\simeq K^{cont}\circ(-)^{\vee}.

Proof.

Every localizing invariant on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}
is uniquely determined by its restriction via
\displaystyle\mathrm{Ind}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Pr}^{L}_{\mathrm{dual}}
, see
[
Efi25
, Theorem 4.10]
. Therefore, the claim reduces to the previous theorem.
∎

Example 5
.

Let
\displaystyle R
be an
\displaystyle E_{1}
-ring spectrum, or in particular an ordinary ring (unital, but not necessarily non-commutative). In this case the
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathrm{LMod}_{R}
of left modules is compactly generated with
\displaystyle\mathrm{LMod}_{R}\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Perf}_{R})
,
[
Lur17
, Proposition 7.2.4.2]
. Write
\displaystyle K(R)=K(\mathrm{Perf}_{R})
. Then

\mathrm{LMod}_{R}^{\vee}\simeq\mathrm{RMod}_{R}=\mathrm{LMod}_{R^{\mathrm{op}}},

where
\displaystyle R^{\mathrm{op}}
is the
\displaystyle E_{1}
-ring obtained from
\displaystyle R
by reversing the order of multiplication,
[
Lur17
, Proposition 7.2.4.3]
. Hence it holds that
\displaystyle K(R)\simeq K(R^{\mathrm{op}})
.

However, the same claim fails for an arbitrary localizing invariant.

Theorem 6
.

There exists a stable idempotent complete
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
such that

\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{C})\not\simeq\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mot}
is the universal finitary localizing invariant.

The proof of Theorem
6
will be given in the following section, and follows as a special case of the work of Tabuada,
[
Tab14
]
. Before we give the proof, we would like to leave the reader with an open question:

Question 7
.

What is a practical criterion for a dualizable
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
which holds for Examples
1
and
2
and which implies that
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathcal{C}^{\vee})
?

A counterexample

Let us recall some general facts about the Brauer group of a field
\displaystyle k
. A general reference is
[
GS06
, Section 2.4]
. The Brauer group
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k)
of
\displaystyle k
is an abelian group given by the set of equivalence classes of finite-dimensional central division algebras
\displaystyle D
up to Brauer equivalence. The multiplication is defined via
\displaystyle[D][D^{\prime}]=[D^{\prime\prime}]
where
\displaystyle D^{\prime\prime}
is the unique central division algebra such that

\displaystyle D\otimes_{k}D^{\prime}\cong M_{n}(D^{\prime\prime}),
where
\displaystyle M_{n}(D^{\prime\prime})
denotes the
\displaystyle n\times n
-matrix algebra of
\displaystyle D^{\prime\prime}
. The inverse of a central division algebra
\displaystyle D
is given by
\displaystyle D^{\mathrm{op}}
.

The following theorem is crucial for the computation of Brauer groups of global fields.

Theorem 8

(Albert–Brauer–Hasse–Noether,
[
NSW08
, Thm. 8.1.17]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle K
be a global field. Then there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathrm{Br}(K)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v}\mathrm{Br}(K_{v})\xrightarrow{\ \sum_{v}\operatorname{inv}_{v}\ }\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\displaystyle v
runs over all places of
\displaystyle K
, the first map is the sum of the
restriction maps
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(K)\to\mathrm{Br}(K_{v})
, and
\displaystyle\operatorname{inv}_{v}
is the local
Hasse invariant map. For a finite place
\displaystyle v
, the map

\operatorname{inv}_{v}\colon\mathrm{Br}(K_{v})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

is an isomorphism; for a real place
\displaystyle v
, its image is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
; and for a
complex place
\displaystyle v
, it is zero.

We can apply the above theorem to
\displaystyle k=\mathbb{Q}
. Picking the values

\operatorname{inv}_{2}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{3},~~\operatorname{inv}_{3}(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{3},~~\operatorname{inv}_{v}(\alpha)=0\text{ for }v\neq 2,3

we get the existence of a non-zero element
\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q})
of order
\displaystyle 3
, which is represented by a central division algebra
\displaystyle A
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
.

Let us make two more comments that we will use.

•

Let
\displaystyle k
be an
\displaystyle E_{\infty}
-ring spectrum. Recall that a
\displaystyle k
-linear stable, idempotent complete
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is called
smooth and proper
if it is dualizable as an object of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{k}
. In this case, there is a natural equivalence

\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{Mot}_{k}}(\mathcal{U}_{k}(\mathcal{C}),\mathcal{U}_{k}(\mathcal{D}))\simeq K(\text{Fun}^{\mathrm{ex}}_{k}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})),

see
[
HSS17
, Theorem 5.25]
, where
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{k}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mot}_{k}
is the universal localizing invariant for
\displaystyle k
-linear stable idempotent complete
\displaystyle\infty
-categories.

•

If
\displaystyle A
is a finite-dimensional central division algebra over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
, it holds that
\displaystyle A
is smooth and proper, by the observation that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Perf}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A}\simeq\mathrm{Perf}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Write
\displaystyle[M,N]=\pi_{0}\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{Mot}_{k}}(M,N)
for
\displaystyle M,N
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mot}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Applying the previous result, we see that
\displaystyle[\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A),-]\simeq K_{0}(\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{ex}}(\mathrm{Perf}_{A},-)).

Proposition 9
.

Let
\displaystyle A
be a finite-dimensional central division algebra over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
that is not of order
\displaystyle 2
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q})
. Then
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)\not\simeq\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mot}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

The condition that
\displaystyle A
is not of order
\displaystyle 2
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q})
is equivalent to saying that
\displaystyle A\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A\cong M_{n}(D)
for some central division algebra
\displaystyle D\neq\mathbb{Q}
. In particular,
\displaystyle d=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{Q}}(D)>1
.

The composition

[\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{\mathrm{op}}),\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)]\times[\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A),\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})]\rightarrow[\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A),\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)]

is identified with

K_{0}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A)\times K_{0}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A^{\mathrm{op}})\rightarrow K_{0}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A)\cong K_{0}(\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}.

The group
\displaystyle K_{0}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A)
is generated by
\displaystyle[D]
and the group
\displaystyle K_{0}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A^{\mathrm{op}})
is generated by
\displaystyle[D^{\mathrm{op}}]
. The product of these two elements is
\displaystyle[M_{d}(\mathbb{Q})]=d^{2}[1]
in
\displaystyle K_{0}(\mathbb{Q})
. Hence the multiplication map identifies with

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{Z}\\
(n,m)&\mapsto&d^{2}nm,\end{array}

which does not hit
\displaystyle 1\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
\displaystyle d>1
. In other words, the identity in
\displaystyle[\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A),\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)]
does not lie in the image of the composition, and
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)
cannot be equivalent to
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Mot}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
∎

The unit map
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
exhibits
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
as an idempotent algebra in
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}
. Therefore it holds that the tensor product on
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}
restricts to the usual tensor product on
\displaystyle\mathrm{LMod}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\displaystyle A\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}A^{\prime}\simeq A\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A^{\prime}
for any two
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
-algebras
\displaystyle A,A^{\prime}
,
[
Lur17
, Proposition 4.8.2.7]
. In particular, it holds that
\displaystyle A\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{Q}\simeq A
.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

The claim
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(A)\not\simeq\mathcal{U}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
follows at once from the previous proposition, as otherwise we would obtain
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A)\simeq\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
via base change along
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Remark 10
.

The proof strategy given here can be generalized substantially. In fact, for every field
\displaystyle k
, the map

\mathcal{U}_{k}:\mathrm{Br}(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Mot}_{k})

is injective, see
[
Tab14
, Theorem 9.1]
, also
[
Ram25
]
for a more structural overview. The map
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{k}
sends inverses to
\displaystyle(-)^{\mathrm{op}}
, thus giving Proposition
9
as a corollary. One can further generalize by replacing
\displaystyle k
by a qcqs scheme
\displaystyle X
to produce an even richer supply of counterexamples, see
[
Ram25
, p. 3]
.
