ABSTRACT
IntroductionBone quality is crucial for satisfactory spinal surgery outcomes. Abdominal aortic calcification (AAC) has been linked to lower bone mineral density (BMD) and increased fracture risk, but its association with bone microarchitecture and extracellular matrix (ECM) quality remains underexplored.Research questionIs AAC independently associated with bone quality beyond BMD in patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery?Material and methodsIn 139 patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery, AAC was evaluated on lateral lumbar radiographs using the Kauppila score. BMD was measured by quantitative CT, and iliac crest biopsies were analyzed by micro-CT for microarchitecture and by Fourier-transform infrared imaging (FTIRI) for ECM parameters. Associations were examined using correlation and multivariable regression analyses adjusted for BMD or confounders (age, sex, BMI, smoking, diabetes).ResultsHigher AAC severity correlated with lower BMD (r = −0.37, p < 0.001), higher trabecular (r = 0.40, p < 0.001) and cortical tissue mineral density (TMD) (r = 0.29, p = 0.001), lower cortical mineral maturity/crystallinity (MMC, r = −0.26, p = 0.003), and greater cortical (r = 0.27, p = 0.002) and trabecular (r = 0.24, p = 0.005) MMC heterogeneity. In multivariable models, AAC remained independently associated with lower BMD (p = 0.001), higher trabecular TMD (p = 0.02), and higher trabecular MMC heterogeneity (p < 0.001).Discussion and conclusionPreoperative AAC assessment could be representative for altered bone quality characteristics independently of BMD. Accordingly, AAC scoring could refine preoperative risk assessment and trigger osteologic workup to mitigate complication risk in spinal fusion surgery.

Introduction

Bone quality is critical for fracture prevention in the general population(Gregson et al., 2022), for bone healing and successful outcomes in orthopedic surgery, especially spinal surgery (Morris et al.; Köhli et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2021). In clinical practice, bone quality is typically assessed by the patient's medical history and bone mineral density (BMD) measurements, either using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) or, more commonly in spinal surgery, by opportunistic quantitative CT (qCT) scanning. However, bone quality involves more than just its macroscopic mineral content. Like steel-reinforced concrete, bone is a composite material, and factors such as macro- and microarchitecture, extracellular matrix (ECM) quality, and the tension band mechanisms provided by muscles all contribute to its resistance to fracture (Marcus et al., 2013; Pauwels, 1980). In recent efforts to better understand the risk of fracture and mechanical failure in orthopedic surgery, sarcopenia (Gandham et al., 2024) (reduced systemic muscle quality) and bone macro-architecture (Burch et al., 2016) have been studied in clinical contexts. However, there are limited studies on bone micro-architecture and ECM quality as they require invasive bone biopsies (Donnelly, 2011).

Among orthopedic surgery patients, those undergoing spinal fusion surgery face a unique risk of low bone quality associated with debilitating mechanical complications, such as non-union, implant loosening or adjacent segment fractures (Morris et al.; Tsutsui et al., 2023; Cho et al., 2018; Martin et al., 2019). The presence of abdominal aortic calcification (AAC), a surrogate marker of cardiovascular disease linked to risk factors such as age, sex, smoking, and diabetes (Kim et al., 2013), has been shown to be associated with lower bone quality and higher fracture risk in the general population (Szulc, 2016; Lewis et al., 2019; Murray et al.; Gebre et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2018) as well as in patients undergoing spinal surgery with worse postoperative outcomes and higher rates of non-union in spinal fusion surgery (Schönnagel et al., 2024; Burkhard et al., 2025). While the association between AAC and BMD has been studied widely cross different populations using DXA (Lewis et al., 2019), qCT (Chow et al., 2008), or high resolution peripheral qCT (HR p-qCT) (Pelletier et al., 2015), studies investigating bone quality factors such as microarchitecture and ECM quality are scarce. One study investigated the association between the presence of AAC and bone histology in end stage renal disease patients undergoing kidney transplantation(Viaene et al., 2016), but no studies have investigated bone microarchitecture in other patient populations. AAC, if associated with bone microarchitecture and ECM quality, could be a marker of lower bone quality beyond BMD as it is easily accessible on lateral lumbar radiographs. These radiographs are routinely acquired prior to lumbar surgery and could guide the treating spinal surgeons to initiate an osteologic workup and adapt their surgical strategy to mitigate the mechanical complication risks.

Therefore, this study was performed analyzing the association between the presence end extent of AAC and bone microarchitecture and matrix composition, as assessed by micro-CT and Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy imaging (FTIRI) of iliac crest biopsies taken intraoperatively in patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery.

Methods

Study design

In this Institutional Review Board (IRB, ethics committee) approved study (IRB#2014-084), adult patients undergoing posterior lumbar fusion surgery for degenerative conditions were prospectively enrolled from 2014 to 2021. The study was conducted in agreement with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants were provided oral and written information about the purpose of this study and procedures. Written informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in this study. The preoperative workup included a lumbar radiograph, a lumbar CT scan for surgical planning, laboratory examinations and a structured medical history including antiosteoporotic medications. From this prospective cohort, patients on antiosteoporotic medication or with missing or insufficient lumbar radiographs for AAC classification were excluded for this secondary analysis (Evidence Level 3).

AAC

The severity of AAC was measured using the Kauppila method on standing lateral lumbar radiographs, assessing aortic wall calcification at the L1 to L4 levels. For each level, calcification is evaluated separately on the anterior and posterior walls by one blinded rater, with scores ranging from 0 (no calcification) to 3 (calcification involving three thirds of the wall). The scores for the anterior and posterior walls are summed at each level, yielding a range of 0-6 per level. The scores across all four levels are then summed to produce an overall semiquantitative AAC severity score from 0 to 24. We previously reported inter- and intra-rater reliability of the AAC measurements (intra-rater: intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) 0.971 (95% CI 0.951–0.982); inter-rater: ICC 0.975 (95% CI 0.959–0.985)) (Muellner et al., 2023). AAC was categorized for group wise analysis as “none” (AAC-24 Kauppila score = 0), “moderate” (AAC-24 Kauppila score 1-4) and “severe” (AAC-24 Kaupilla score ≥5) in concordance with the literature (Szulc, 2016; Lewis et al., 2019; Schönnagel et al., 2024).

Bone biopsies

During the scheduled lumbar fusion surgery, iliac crest bone harvesting was performed for fusion grafting. Prior to harvesting, a bicortical iliac crest (IC) bone biopsy was obtained using a 15 mm custom-made trephine set (Depuy Synthes, Raynham, MA) at the side beneath the posterior superior iliac spine. Bone biopsies were then fixed in ethanol, subjected to μCT scanning and then embedded in polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA), sectioned into 2 μm slices on a microtome (Leica SM 2500, Leica, Germany) and mounted on barium fluoride (BaF2) infrared windows (SpectraTek, Hopewell Junction, NY, USA) for FTIRI.

Micro-CT

Bone biopsies, fixed in 75% ethanol, were scanned using a Scanco μCT35 system (Scanco Medical, Bassersdorf, Switzerland) at a resolution of 10 μm, with settings of 55 kVp and 145 μA, and an integration time of 400 ms per view. Image acquisition and processing were handled using Scanco μCT software (HP, DEC Windows Motif 1.6). A global threshold of 280 HU was employed to differentiate bone from surrounding tissues. Regions of interest (ROIs) were segmented for both cortical and trabecular bone in sequential slices. Cortical parameters included total volume (TV), bone volume (BV), bone volume fraction (BV/TV), apparent density (AD), and tissue mineral density (TMD). Trabecular parameters consisted of TV, BV, BV/TV, connectivity density (Conn.D), structure model index (SMI), trabecular number (Tb.N), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), specific bone surface (BS/BV), AD, and TMD.

FTIRI

The PMMA-embedded, 2-μm-thick iliac crest biopsy sections were scanned on an infrared imaging system with 6.25 μm × 6.25 μm/pixel resolution (PerkinElmer Spotlight 300, PerkinElmer Instruments, Waltham, MA, USA). Five separate regions of trabecular bone and five separate regions of iliac bone areas of each section were randomly selected by and scanned at a spectral resolution of 4 cm−1, following the methodology described in previous studies (Okano et al., 2020a; Boskey et al., 2016; Spevak et al., 2013). Using chemical image analysis software (ISYS 5.0, Malvern Instruments, Columbia, MD, USA), and correcting for PMMA spectral interference, the following bone ECM properties (Donnelly, 2011; Taylor and Donnelly) were calculated as previously established (Okano et al., 2020b; Salzmann et al., 2019). The mineral-to-matrix ratio (Min:Mat, representing bone mineral content correlating with ash weight), carbonate-to-phosphate ratio (C/P, reflecting carbonate substitution in the hydroxyapatite crystal), mineral maturity/crystallinity (MMC, indicating hydroxyapatite crystal size and perfection), collagen cross-linking ratio (XLR, correlating with collagen maturity), and acid phosphate substitution (HPO4, characteristic of younger ECM), along with their spatial heterogeneities, were quantified.

BMD measurements and laboratory parameters

On preoperative lumbar thin-slice CT scans, trabecular volumetric BMD (vBMD) at the L1 and L2 levels as well as at posterior iliac crest at the planned biopsy side was measured using opportunistic qCT (Mindways QCT Pro Software, Mindways Software, Inc., Austin, TX), as described previously (Haffer et al., 2022, 2023). Following the American College of Radiology (ACR) guidelines, bone quality was categorize based on the lumbar L1/L2 vBMD average as normal (vBMD >120 mg/cm3), osteopenic (vBMD 80–120 mg/cm3), or osteoporotic (vBMD <80 mg/cm3) (American College of Radiology (ACR) et al., 2023). Preoperative laboratory evaluation involved standard preoperative blood and urine tests (serum creatinine, protein, albumin, HbA1c, and liver function) and for most patients a metabolic bone panel that included total calcium, inorganic phosphorus, Vitamin D (Cholecalciferol), Calcitriol (1,25-VitD), parathyroid hormone (PTH), and bone metabolism markers P1NP and bone-specific alkaline phosphatase (BAP) for bone formation, for bone resorption β-Crosslaps (CTX), serum type I collagen N-telopeptide (sNTX), and urinary type I collagen N-telopeptide (uNTX). Blood was usually drawn during morning till noon.

Statistics

The distribution of data for normality was assessed by both visual inspection and the Shapiro-Wilk test. For consistency, data was analyzed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and treated as non-normally distributed. Categorical data is presented as percentages and frequencies. Comparative statistics were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis rank-sum test, followed by post-hoc Wilcoxon Pairwise Comparison with p-values adjusted by Holm–Bonferroni correction. Pearson's Chi-squared test and Fisher's exact test were applied where appropriate. To assess the association between AAC severity (Kauppila score) and vBMD, bone microarchitecture, or bone ECM properties, Spearman correlation was performed. To analyze whether these differences were independent of the vBMD, multivariable linear regression analyses were conducted, adjusting for vBMD. To test whether the association between bone quality parameters and AAC could be inherently linked, multivariable linear regression analyses were conducted adjusting for the potential confounders such as age, sex, BMI, diabetes, and smoking status. Statistical analysis was performed in “R”, using R studio software.

Results

Study cohort characteristics

Out of 153 patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery, 139 were included after excluding 10 patients on antiosteoporotic medication and 4 with inadequate preoperative lateral X-rays for AAC grading (Supplemental Fig. 1). The median age was 62 years (IQR 54–70), with a nearly equal distribution of male and female patients (70 male, 69 female). Patients with higher AAC severity were significantly older (p < 0.001) and showed a higher prevalence of hypertension (p = 0.001) and smoking history (p < 0.001). Cortical bone microarchitecture showed no differences in BV/TV or AD, but TMD was significantly different (p = 0.003) with patients with severe AAC having significant higher TMD than those without (difference of medians: +4%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 1). Trabecular microarchitecture parameters (BV/TV, Conn.D, Tb.N, Tb.Th, Tb.Sp) did not differ significantly between AAC groups, except for TMD (p < 0.001), which was higher in patients with moderate (difference of medians: +1%, p < 0.05) or severe AAC (difference of medians: +4%, p < 0.001) than in those with no AAC in trabecular bone.

Regarding ECM properties, in the cortical compartment, patients showed significant differences in MMC (p = 0.010) and MMC heterogeneity (p = 0.011). Compared to patients with no AAC, those with severe AAC had significantly lower MMC (difference of medians: −2.5%, p < 0.01) whereas heterogeneity of MMC was significantly higher (difference of medians: +33%, p < 0.01, Fig. 1). Other FTIR-derived ECM parameters, including Min:Mat, C/P, XLR, and HPO), showed no significant differences between AAC groups in cortical bone of the iliac crest biopsies (Table 2). In trabecular bone, MMC heterogeneity was significantly different among AAC groups (p = 0.012), while other ECM variables did not differ significantly. Patients with severe AAC had significantly higher MMC heterogeneity compared to those with no AAC (difference of medians: +14%, p = 0.012). The results of the post-hoc testing are visualized in Fig. 1.

Correlation and multivariable analysis

Spearman correlation confirmed that higher AAC severity was significantly associated with lower lumbar spine vBMD (rho = −0.37, p < 0.001) and iliac crest vBMD (rho = −0.33, p = 0.001). Conversely, AAC severity correlated positively with cortical (rho = 0.29, p = 0.001) and trabecular TMD (rho = 0.40, p < 0.001) and with increased heterogeneity in cortical (rho = 0.27, p = 0.003) and trabecular (rho = 0.24, p = 0.005) MMC (Table 3). Discussion

In this study in patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery for degenerative conditions, the inverse association between AAC and vBMD was confirmed and was independent of potential risk factors such as age, sex, BMI, smoking status or diabetes. While AAC was associated with lower vBMD, patients with higher AAC grades showed higher TMD and higher MMC heterogeneity in cortical and trabecular bone, independent of vBMD.

To the authors’ knowledge, this is the largest study investigating the association of AAC with bone microarchitecture in iliac crest biopsies in non-dialyzed or spinal surgery patients employing FTIRI to address the relationship between AAC and bone ECM properties. Interestingly, no correlation was found between AAC severity and metabolic bone laboratory parameters such as Vitamin D levels, despite previously reported associations (Norman and Powell, 2014). In contrast, the H bA1c and creatinine levels correlated with AAC, likely reflecting the association of AAC with (pre-) diabetes and renal function.

Studies exploring bone biopsies in patients with vascular calcification revealed a link between arterial calcifications and bone turnover. In end-stage renal disease (a population with high vascular calcification burden), London et al. found that patients with severe arterial calcification had markedly low bone formation activity (adynamic bone disease) on histomorphometry (London et al., 2004). Similarly, a recent cross-sectional study in 56 predialysis chronic kidney disease patients showed that those with any vascular calcification (assessed by lumbar, pelvic or hand X-ray) had significantly lower bone formation rates on iliac crest biopsy and low bone turnover was independently associated with the presence of vascular calcification (Neto et al., 2021). Malluche et al. describe an association between vascular calcification and XLR heterogeneity in bone samples, and associated it with the progression of coronary but not abdominal calcification, which may explain the discrepancy towards our findings (Malluche et al., 2025). Vascular calcification and worse bone properties have been correlated for the lower limbs (Bartosik et al., 2024). Shared molecular mechanisms driving vascular calcification on the one hand, but leading to aberrant bone metabolism on the other hand might explain these co-occurrences (Viaene et al., 2016; Vachey et al., 2024). Chronic inflammation (Hofbauer et al., 2007; Xie et al., 2023), aberrant sclerostin signaling (Pelletier et al., 2015; Ott, 2015), higher adrenergic signaling (Jahn et al., 2024; Moser et al., 2021) or the accumulation of advanced glycosylation end products (Salzmann et al., 2019; Sanchis et al., 2020) have been described in both phenomena.

In this context, heightened sympathetic nervous system (SNS) activity is particularly notable as a potentially modifiable target for both pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions. Both bone and vascular tissues are innervated by the SNS (Brazill et al., 2019), and cells such as osteoblasts and vascular endothelial cells express adrenergic receptors (Jahn et al., 2024), making them direct targets of sympathetic signaling. Increased SNS tone has been linked to elevated risk of abdominal aortic aneurysmsm (Tang et al., 2025), and experimental studies have demonstrated that β-adrenergic signaling promotes vascular smooth muscle cell osteogenic differentiation and calcification(Moser et al., 2021), while simultaneously inhibiting bone formation by suppressing osteoblast activity via β2-adrenergic receptors (Jahn et al., 2024; Takeda et al.). These findings support the hypothesis that increased sympathetic activity might represent a shared pathophysiological pathway linking vascular and skeletal degeneration. Higher SNS activity has been shown in sciatica patients undergoing spinal surgery (Södervall et al., 2013) that might contribute to the high rate of osteoporosis observed (Köhli et al., 2025). However, further studies are needed to establish a mechanistic link.

Patients undergoing lumbar surgery are at special risk of bone related complications such as adjacent vertebral segment fractures, implant loosening or non-union in the case of fusion surgery (Köhli et al., 2024; Dimar et al., 2021). Kim et al. associated in vivo differences in bone microstructure with implant failure in spinal surgery using peripheral high-resolution qCT(Kim et al.), with affected patients showing lower vBMD, reduced Tb.N and Tb.Th at the radius and tibia and reduced tibial cortical thickness. Ex vivo evidence supports such associations underlining the role of altered microarchitectural bone properties at the side of spinal implants to their mechanical complications (Lehman et al.; Palepu et al., 2018; Cornaz et al.; Palepu et al.). Previous studies have linked the presence of AAC in patients undergoing lumbar surgery to worse outcomes(Schönnagel et al., 2024), including a higher prevalence of osteoporosis (Muellner et al., 2023) and higher rate of non-union (Burkhard et al., 2025). Our findings suggest a common feature that might contribute to these observations: lower bone quality beyond macroscopic vBMD leading to a higher rate of mechanical complications after spinal fusion surgery. This underlines the clinical relevance of the association of AAC and altered bone quality in spinal surgery patients. Lewis et al. observed that AAC was associated with fracture risk in older women independent of BMD (Lewis et al., 2019). The observed association between higher AAC and increased TMD with decreased MMC and elevated MMC heterogeneity in our study suggests the presence of bone that is more mineralized, which is associated with bone packets that have undergone longer periods of secondary mineralization yet also has bone packets of smaller, less perfect crystals, which is typically associated with newly formed bone (Taylor and Donnelly; Boskey), which could be a link to altered biomechanical properties leading to enhanced mechanical complication risk.

FTIR imaging has been used to examine relationships between mechanical properties and compositional metrics, particularly at the tissue level. At the microscale, greater values of mineral:matrix ratio and mineral maturity/crystallinity are generally associated with greater resistance to indentation in cortical bone from various species. (Taylor and Donnelly) These observations suggest that the indentation modulus is greater in regions with more mature bone tissue, characterized by more numerous, larger, or more perfect mineral crystals. (Boskey) In addition, our results are consistent with those of Gourion-Arsiquaud et al., who observed a reduced heterogeneity of Min:Mat and C/P and higher MMC heterogeneity in patients with hip fracture undergoing surgery compared to non-fractured patients (Gourion-Arsiquaud et al., 2013), which is in line with our observation of higher MMC heterogeneity in patients with higher AAC, who are known to have a higher fracture risk (Wei et al., 2018). Our observational data suggest that factors that contribute to AAC might also disrupt bone metabolism and alter the bone matrix composition to increase mineral content and increase the heterogeneity of crystal size/perfection. Further prospective studies in animal models are needed to identify a potential mechanism that may drive these changes.

Interestingly, AAC severity was not associated with differences in μCT derived microarchitecture parameters except for TMD in our cohort of spinal fusion surgery patients, suggesting that AAC-related changes primarily affect bone mineralization and matrix composition rather than architectural properties. In contrast, studies in patients on hemodialysis using DXA-derived trabecular bone score (Aleksova et al., 2018) or high-resolution peripheral QCT (HR-pQCT) (Pelletier et al., 2015) have observed associations between vascular calcification and trabecular structure, which may reflect the influence of chronic kidney disease or differences in anatomical site or measurement resolution (μCT versus DXA or HR-pQCT).

However, the microstructural changes associated with higher AAC could explain the increase fracture risk independent of BMD (Gebre et al., 2023) and could relate to reduced mechanical fixation strength and impaired spinal fusion, aligning with poorer clinical outcomes in lumbar spine surgery.

As AAC is easily accessible on preoperative lateral lumbar X-Rays, but bone microarchitecture or ECM properties are not, the association between AAC, bone properties, and impaired surgical outcomes after lumbar fusion surgery (Schönnagel et al., 2024; Burkhard et al., 2025) suggest the evaluation of AAC as a tool for identifying patients at higher risk of adverse spine surgery outcomes. A higher AAC score could also trigger an osteological assessment and, if required, medical optimization with modification of the surgical strategy to mitigate postoperative mechanical complications in spinal fusion surgery (Morris et al.; Köhli et al., 2024). Nevertheless, future studies are needed to verify this and to evaluate possible treatment strategies.

Limitations

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the largest study to examine the association between AAC and detailed bone microarchitecture and extracellular matrix quality in patients undergoing lumbar fusion surgery. Nevertheless, several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. The study was conducted at a single high-volume academic spine center within the US healthcare system, which may limit generalizability to other settings or patient populations. Although the biopsies and imaging data were collected prospectively, the analysis itself was retrospective and therefore constrained to the available clinical variables; important factors such as detailed cardiovascular history beyond AAC, or socioeconomic status were not consistently accessible. Iliac crest biopsies provide high-resolution insights into bone microarchitecture and matrix quality but may not fully reflect local bone characteristics at the lumbar vertebrae or at implant interfaces where pedicle screws and cages anchor. Similarly, vBMD was assessed using opportunistic lumbar qCT, which does not capture bone status at other clinically relevant sites such as the proximal femur. The cross-sectional nature of the study means that while AAC was independently associated with altered bone matrix features, the temporal relationship, causality or underlying biological pathways remain speculative and should be addressed in future longitudinal work. AAC severity was measured using the Kauppila score on lateral spine radiographs, a practical and validated method, but one that remains semi-quantitative. While AAC assessment on lateral lumbar radiographs demonstrated good to excellent inter-rater reliability (Muellner et al., 2023) within the norms of the reported literature (Schönnagel et al., 2024; Burkhard et al., 2025), these findings may not be directly transferable to other settings with different imaging quality or reader experience. Additionally, the use of FTIRI to analyze bone matrix composition has technical constraints, including, micrometer spatial resolution that cannot capture nanoscale structural details, and the sampling of five selected regions from two 2-μm-thick sections of each biopsy, which may not capture regional differences within the biopsy or represent the composition of bone tissue at the spine. FTIRI also provides relative ratio-based measures rather than absolute quantification, which may limit direct comparisons with other studies.

Conclusion

This cross-sectional study confirms an inverse association between vBMD and AAC independent of the possible confounders age, sex, BMI, smoking status or diabetes and demonstrates that AAC is also independently linked to distinct alterations in bone quality that go beyond conventional BMD metrics. Higher AAC was associated with increased TMD and lower MMC, and higher MMC heterogeneity. This pattern could suggest the presence of smaller, less perfect, and more variable mineral crystals within a more heavily mineralized bone matrix—and could provide an explanation for the enhanced fracture risk independent of BMD. The fact that these associations were independent of the lower vBMD with higher AAC, supports the idea of a shared pathway between vascular calcification and impaired bone quality, highlighting the interconnectedness of bone and vascular health and the need for further research on the role of inflammation, sympathetic innervation and advanced glycosylation end products as possible pathways.

Given that bone microarchitecture and extracellular matrix quality are not easily accessible preoperatively in routine practice, AAC scoring that is readily available from standard lateral spine radiographs could serve as a practical surrogate marker for detecting patients with compromised bone quality. Incorporating AAC assessment into preoperative evaluation might therefore help orthopedic and spine surgeons better stratify risk, optimize implant strategies, and improve surgical outcomes in patients undergoing spinal fusion or other procedures where bone quality is critical.
