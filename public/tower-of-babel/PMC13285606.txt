Knowledge mapping and research trends of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell immunotherapy in breast cancer: A bibliometric and visual analytics study
Authors: Yuechen Xu, Yu Xu, Zhisen Wang, Qiyi Yi, Fan Wang, Po Bian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTChimeric antigen receptor T cell (CAR-T) immunotherapy has achieved landmark success in hematologic malignancies and is being explored for breast cancer (BC) and other solid tumors, prompting research to improve efficacy and safety. We retrieved 894 related to CAR-T immunotherapy for BC from the Web of Science Core Collection and performed bibliometric analyses using CiteSpace and VOSviewer to map the research landscape, development trajectory, and key contributors. Joinpoint regression assessed temporal trends in annual publications using R 4.4.2. The USA led publication output. Institutional co-occurrence revealed the University of London and Technische Universität Dresden as central hubs. CANCER RES and FRONT IMMUNOL were identified as core journals. Darcy PK and Morgan RA were prominent authors by output and citations. Keyword clustering and timeline visualization indicated that the terms “in vivo,” “tumor microenvironment (TME),” and “targeted therapy” have remained highly active in recent years. In addition, burst detection analysis revealed that keywords including TME, “epithelial-mesenchymal transition,” and “mesothelin” have gradually emerged as research frontiers, suggesting increasing attention to resistance mechanisms and tumor immune regulation. These findings provide a systematic overview of the evolving research landscape and help identify emerging hotspots and future directions. BC remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and a major cause of cancer-related death. We systematically mapped the knowledge structure and development trends of CAR-T research in BC, identifying major research contributors and emerging research hotspots, which may provide valuable insights for future basic research and clinical exploration in this field.KEYWORDS: Chimeric antigen receptor T cells, breast cancer, bibliometric study, CiteSpace, VOSviewer, Joinpoint regression analysis

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women worldwide and remains a major public health challenge because of its substantial incidence and mortality.1 Despite continuous advances in therapeutic strategies, clinical outcomes remain unsatisfactory for specific subtypes, including metastatic disease, triple-negative BC (TNBC), and HER2-positive BC. These limitations highlight an urgent need for more effective and precisely targeted treatment approaches.

Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy represents a transformative advancement in cellular immunotherapy. By leveraging genetic engineering, this approach modifies T lymphocytes to specifically recognize and eradicate tumor cells.2 The therapeutic efficacy of this modality hinges on the precise targeting of tumor antigens, which orchestrates a potent anti-tumor immune response.3,4 As a disruptive immunotherapeutic approach, CAR-T therapy has achieved remarkable and durable clinical success in hematological malignancies, prompting its translational exploration in solid tumors. In recent years, CAR-T has been increasingly investigated in BC, with early-phase clinical and preclinical studies demonstrating encouraging signals of efficacy.5 Evidence suggests that CAR-T holds therapeutic potential in metastatic BC, TNBC, and HER2-positive BC.6,7 Among these, HER2-targeted CAR-T cells have been most extensively studied, while CAR-T constructs directed against TNBC-specific antigens continue to emerge. Nevertheless, the application of CAR-T in solid tumors remains considerably more challenging than in hematological malignancies. The highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME), difficulties in identifying tumor-specific and safe target antigens, and limited tumor infiltration and persistence of CAR-T cells collectively constrain therapeutic effectiveness.8,9 Despite these obstacles, advances in TME biology, ongoing optimization of CAR architectures, and the development of rational combination strategies continue to expand the therapeutic potential of CAR-T in BC, offering promising avenues for immunotherapy.10

Although research on CAR-T therapy for BC has grown rapidly in recent years, a systematic synthesis of the disease-specific knowledge structure, research hotspots, and developmental trends in this field remains lacking. Previous bibliometric studies have mainly examined CAR-T therapy, the application of CAR-T therapy in solid tumors, or specific immunological mechanisms, thereby providing important macro-level insights into the field. For example, Li focused primarily on T-cell exhaustion as an immune functional state and identified its research hotspots and emerging frontiers; however, it did not specifically examine the disease-specific translational challenges of CAR-T therapy in BC, a highly heterogeneous solid tumor.11 Similarly, although previous bibliometric studies on CAR-T therapy in solid tumors have helped clarify the overall landscape of solid tumor CAR-T research, a pan-solid-tumor perspective may not fully capture key BC-specific issues, including molecular subtype heterogeneity, differences in target antigen expression, an immunosuppressive TME, and therapeutic adaptability.12 In this study, we used CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and Joinpoint regression to systematically analyze publication trends, collaboration networks, knowledge bases, thematic evolution, and research frontiers in BC CAR-T research from 2004 to 2025. By integrating keyword co-occurrence, clustering, and citation burst analysis, this study aimed to construct a disease-specific knowledge map of CAR-T therapy for BC and to reveal, from a bibliometric perspective, how the field has gradually shifted from early feasibility exploration toward research directions with greater translational relevance.

Methods and materials

Data collection

The data for this study were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) on January 7, 2026. WoSCC provides highly standardized bibliometric information and comprehensive citation data, including authors, institutions, countries/regions, keywords, references, and citation relationships, thereby providing a stable and reliable data source for bibliometric analysis. In addition, the export format of WoSCC is highly compatible with mainstream visualization tools, including CiteSpace and VOSviewer, which helps ensure consistency in data processing and reproducibility of the analytical results. Because of different databases varying in literature coverage, indexing rules, citation-counting methods, and field formats, direct integration of multiple databases may introduce duplicate records, inconsistent metadata, and difficulties in unifying citation relationships.13 To ensure consistency of the data source, controllability of the analytical workflow, and reproducibility of the results, we used WoSCC as the primary data source in this study.

The specific search strategy was as follows: TS = ((“Breast Cancer*” OR “Breast Neoplasm*” OR “Breast Tumor*” OR “Breast Carcinoma*” OR “Mammary Cancer*” OR “Mammary Neoplasm*” OR “Mammary Carcinoma*” OR “Human Mammary Neoplasm*” OR “Human Mammary Carcinoma*”)) AND ((“Chimeric Antigen Receptor*” OR “Chimeric T-Cell Receptor*” OR “Chimeric T Cell Receptor*” OR “Artificial T-Cell Receptor*” OR “Artificial T Cell Receptor*” OR “Chimeric Immunoreceptor*” OR “Chimeric Immunoreceptors*” OR “Chimeric T-Cell Receptors*” OR “Chimeric T Cell Receptors*” OR “Artificial T-Cell Receptors*” OR “Artificial T Cell Receptors*” OR “CAR-T” OR “CART” OR “CAR T” OR “CAR T-cell*” OR “CAR-T cell*”)). The initial search yielded 1,001 records. The document types were then restricted to “Article” and “Review,” while meeting abstracts, editorial materials, letters, and corrections were excluded. The time span was further limited to 2004–2025, and no language restriction was applied. Subsequently, two authors, Yuechen Xu and Yu Xu, independently screened the rest of the records based on titles, abstracts, and keywords to exclude studies unrelated to CAR-T therapy or BC. Any disagreement was resolved by a third author, Zhisen Wang. Following the exclusion of 107 irrelevant records, 894 publications were retained for the final bibliometric analysis, consisting of 498 “Articles” and 396 “Reviews” (Figure 1).

Data analysis

Bibliometric analysis

We used CiteSpace 6.4.R1 (https://citespace.podia.com) and VOSviewer 1.6.20 (https://www.vosviewer.com) for data analysis and the construction of scientific knowledge maps. CiteSpace is a Java-based citation visualization software developed by Chaomei Chen that provides an experimental platform for exploring emerging research patterns and comparing existing analytical approaches. The software enables multidimensional analysis of the literature, facilitating the identification and visualization of research hotspots and temporal trends within a given field. Knowledge mapping allows researchers to intuitively capture the structure of scientific knowledge, track its evolution, and anticipate future research directions.14 VOSviewer is a freely available, Java-based bibliometric analysis software developed in 2009 by Van Eck and Waltman at the Center for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University, the Netherlands. It offers robust visualization and graphical processing capabilities, making it particularly suitable for the analysis and visualization of large-scale bibliometric datasets.15

Analysis tools

CiteSpace and VOSviewer parameter settings

The software parameters were determined according to the study objectives, dataset size, network readability, and reproducibility. All search terms, screening criteria, software versions, and key parameters are provided to enable reproducibility.

The CiteSpace parameters were set as follows: the time span was set from 2004 to 2025 to capture the full development of BC CAR-T research, from early exploration to rapid growth. A 2-y time slice was used for reference co-occurrence analyses to improve network connectivity and reduce fragmentation caused by sparse annual data, whereas a 1-y time slice was applied to countries, institutions, and keywords to capture more detailed annual changes in collaboration patterns and research hotspots. Term sources included “Title,” “Author Keywords,” “Abstract,” and “Keywords Plus” to ensure comprehensive topic extraction. Link strength was calculated using cosine similarity within each time slice, reducing the influence of citation accumulation across different periods. Nodes were selected using the g-index with adjusted k values to balance the retention of influential nodes and network complexity. The top 50 levels and top 10% most cited items were retained for each slice, with a maximum of 100 items per slice. Minimum spanning tree and sliced network pruning were applied to remove redundant links and highlight the main network structure. Visualization was performed using a static cluster view with a merged network display.16

The VOSviewer parameters were set as follows. The author collaboration network was constructed using the co-authorship mode, with authors as the unit of analysis and full counting as the counting method. To reduce network sparsity and visualization noise caused by low-frequency authors, the maximum number of authors per document was set to 50, the minimum number of documents per author was set to 10, and the minimum number of citations per author was set to 0; 32 authors met these thresholds. For source citation analysis, sources were used as the unit of analysis, with a minimum citation threshold of 5; 30 journals met this threshold. Co-citation analyses of cited authors and cited journals were conducted using cited authors and cited sources as the respective units of analysis, with minimum citation thresholds of 50 and 250, respectively; 77 cited authors and 67 cited journals met these thresholds. In the maps, node size represents publication output or citation frequency, links and their strength indicate the closeness of collaboration or citation relationships, and colors represent clusters. Total Link Strength (TLS) was used to quantify the overall connectivity of nodes within the collaboration or citation networks.17

Joinpoint regression analysis

We performed Joinpoint regression analysis using the segmented package in R 4.4.2 to characterize temporal trends in annual publication output. Joinpoint regression is a piecewise linear regression method that allows the slope of a time-trend model to change at statistically supported breakpoints, thereby dividing the study period into several phases with internally consistent trends. This method fits trend data by connecting multiple linear segments and starts from the model with the fewest breakpoints to test whether additional statistically significant breakpoints are required, enabling the identification of stage-specific changes in annual publication growth.18

In this study, year was used as the independent variable, and the log-transformed annual number of publications was used as the dependent variable. The segmented() function was then applied to iteratively estimate breakpoint locations, test candidate joinpoints, and obtain stage-specific slopes and their standard errors, which were subsequently used to calculate annual percent changes (APCs). To determine the optimal number of joinpoints, the AICcmodavg package was used to compare candidate models with 0, 1, or 2 breakpoints. The corrected Akaike Information Criterion (AICc) was applied to balance model goodness of fit and complexity, particularly given the limited length of the annual time series.19

The model with the strongest statistical support was ultimately selected, and the average APC (AAPC) from 2004 to 2025 was calculated to describe the overall long-term trend in publication output. Detailed information on the software parameter settings is provided in the Supplementary Materials.

Results

Annual publication analysis

Joinpoint regression analysis was conducted to assess temporal trends in annual publication output from 2004 to 2025 (Figure 2). Overall, publication counts demonstrated a significant increasing trend over the study period, with an AAPC of 17.98%. A single significant joinpoint was identified in 2013, dividing the study period into two distinct phases. From 2004 to 2013, publication output increased steadily at a moderate rate, with an APC of 11.00%. While the period from 2014 to 2025 exhibited a markedly accelerated growth in publications, with the APC increasing to 23.50%, reflecting a substantial escalation in research activity within this domain.

Country and institutional co-occurrence analysis

The country collaboration network included 292 countries and regions (Figure 3A). In terms of publication output, the United States and China were the two leading contributors, with 328 and 235 publications, respectively. Network centrality analysis showed that the United States also ranked first, with a centrality value of 0.58. Other countries with relatively high betweenness centrality included Saudi Arabia and Italy, indicating that a certain degree of international collaboration has been established in this field (Supplementary Table 1).

The institutional collaboration network identified 3,979 research institutions (Figure 3B). The University of London was the most productive institution. Centrality analysis showed that Technische Universität Dresden served as important bridging hubs in the institutional collaboration network. Overall, BC CAR-T research has developed a collaboration network centered on Europe and the United States and several highly productive institutions; however, countries and institutions differed in terms of publication output and their bridging roles within the network (Supplementary Table 2).

Author co-occurrence analysis

The author collaboration network included 6,291 authors. VOSviewer analysis showed that the author collaboration network formed several relatively independent collaborative clusters (Figure 4A). In terms of publication output, Maher J ranked among the most productive authors. In terms of citation frequency and TLS, Darcy PK ranked first, indicating substantial academic influence and collaboration intensity (Supplementary Table 3).

The co-cited author analysis further showed that Morgan RA ranked first in both citation frequency and TLS, suggesting his important influence in the intellectual base of this field (Figure 4B and Supplementary Table 4).

Cited journal co-occurrence analysis

Journal analysis showed that 368 journals contributed to the publication of relevant studies. FRONT IMMUNOL had the highest number of publications and the strongest TLS, with 51 publications and a TLS of 232. In terms of citation frequency, MOL THER ranked first with 3,175 citations (Supplementary Table 5 and Figure 4C).

The cited-journal analysis showed that CANCER RES ranked first in both citation and TLS, with 2,991 citations and a TLS of 317,154, indicating the central role of this journal in shaping the knowledge structure of the field and highlighting its importance in knowledge dissemination and theoretical foundation building (Figure 4D and Supplementary Table 6).

Discussion

We conducted a bibliometric and visual analysis of research on CAR-T immunotherapy for BC spanning from 2004 to 2025. By scrutinizing the annual trends in publications, we have traced the research trajectory in this domain and offered insights into potential future development pathways.

At the country and institutional levels, the United States and China were the major contributors to research output in this field. In particular, the United States ranked first in both publication volume and network centrality, suggesting its strong hub role in the international collaboration network. Institutional analysis showed that several European and American research institutions exhibited relatively high centrality within the collaboration network, indicating that BC CAR-T research has developed an international collaborative landscape of considerable scale.

Cited-journal co-occurrence analysis showed that FRONT IMMUNOL has become an important publication platform. In addition, the prominent position of highly cited journals such as CANCER RES in the co-citation network suggests that the intellectual foundation extends beyond CAR-T cell engineering, relying heavily on fundamental advances in tumor biology, immune evasion, and target-antigen expression.

Author collaboration and cited-author analyses showed that several relatively active research teams have emerged in this field. Among them, authors such as Darcy PK and Morgan RA demonstrated relatively high influence in the collaboration or cited-author networks and made important contributions to the extension of CAR-T technology into solid tumors.20,21 Notably, findings at the author, country, and institutional levels primarily reflect the organizational structure, collaboration patterns, and sources of knowledge production in this field. Therefore, these results should be regarded as supplementary evidence to help contextualize the knowledge production and collaborative landscape of CAR-T research in BC.

Keyword co-occurrence analysis provided an overall perspective on the core research concerns and thematic evolution of CAR-T research in BC. Keywords such as “expression,” “monoclonal antibody,” “activation,” and TME showed relatively high occurrence frequencies or betweenness centrality, suggesting that the research focus may have gradually expanded from early CAR-T construction and proof-of-concept studies to topics related to target antigen expression, antigen-recognition domain design, T-cell functional regulation, and solid tumor translation.22 The prominent position of “expression” indicates that target antigen expression may be a recurring and important issue in BC CAR-T research.23 From a clinical translational perspective, this theme is potentially related to candidate target screening, patient stratification, and safety assessment.24,25 The keywords “monoclonal antibody” and “activation” may reflect sustained attention to the technical foundations of CAR recognition domain construction, antigen-recognition optimization, and regulation of T-cell functional states.26,27 The high frequency and high betweenness centrality of TME further suggest that issues related to the solid TME have become an important thematic node in this field.28,29 However, these findings should primarily be interpreted as bibliometric signals of changing research attention and knowledge structure, rather than as direct validation of specific biological mechanisms or therapeutic effects.

Keyword clustering and timeline analyses showed that themes such as “in vivo,” “targeted therapy,” and TME persisted across different time periods, indicating that in vivo validation, targeted therapeutic strategies, and solid TME-related issues constitute relatively stable thematic components of BC CAR-T research.30 These results suggest that the research focus may be shifting from early target identification and CAR construction toward patient stratification, subtype-specific target evaluation, TME-related investigation, and therapeutic optimization.31,32

Keyword burst analysis further highlighted the importance of TME. In addition, EMT and “mesothelin” have emerged as burst terms in recent years, suggesting that research attention may be shifting toward solid tumor translation, tumor heterogeneity, and optimization of target antigen selection. The emergence of EMT as a burst keyword may indicate increasing attention to how tumor cell plasticity and subtype-specific biological characteristics influence target selection, patient stratification, and therapeutic optimization.33,34 In this study, EMT should be viewed primarily as a bibliometric signal, indicating a research direction that requires further mechanistic investigation and clinical validation. From a clinical translational perspective, mesothelin-related themes may involve candidate target validation, safety assessment, and patient selection, suggesting that target antigen selection remains an important concern in BC CAR-T research.35,36 The burst of “mesothelin” in the keyword network should be interpreted as a bibliometric signal of increasing research interest in related targeting strategies, rather than as direct evidence of its clinical efficacy. Overall, the prominent appearance of TME, EMT, and “mesothelin” in burst analysis suggests that BC CAR-T research is gradually extending from early conceptual development and CAR construction toward more clinically oriented issues.37 These findings provide clinically and scientifically relevant clues for identifying future research priorities.38 Nevertheless, bibliometric analysis reflects patterns of publication activity, keyword usage, and citation structure; it cannot establish causal relationships, validate biological mechanisms, or determine clinical efficacy. Therefore, the findings of this study should be regarded as hypothesis-generating evidence that may guide future experimental studies, preclinical validation, and early-phase clinical research, rather than as definitive conclusions regarding the mechanisms or efficacy of specific CAR-T strategies in BC.

Reference co-citation analysis further delineated the knowledge sources and evolutionary trajectory. Highly co-cited references suggest that the intellectual base of this field is partly derived from two major sources: first, the technical and clinical paradigms established in CAR-T research for hematologic malignancies; and second, research themes related to the translational application of CAR-T therapy in solid tumors.39,40 Thus, the co-citation structure reflects both the continuity between BC CAR-T research and the broader CAR-T knowledge base, as well as its gradual transition toward solid tumor-adaptive strategies.

In the cited-reference burst analysis, the study by Maude SL showed the strongest burst intensity. This finding suggests that BC CAR-T-related publications may frequently cite representative studies from the field of hematologic CAR-T therapy and use them as theoretical or methodological references for understanding CAR-T cell expansion in vivo, cellular persistence, efficacy monitoring, and safety management.41 However, as a solid tumor, BC has a disease context distinct from that of hematologic malignancies. Therefore, the influence of these references should be understood as providing a knowledge base and methodological reference, rather than as direct evidence supporting the therapeutic efficacy of CAR-T therapy in BC.42 In recent years, the citation burst of the study by Klichinsky M suggests that CAR-based immunotherapy is expanding from conventional T-cell platforms to other engineered effector-cell systems.43 From a bibliometric perspective, this burst reflects a structural expansion of the research frontier, suggesting that the field may be moving toward multi-effector-cell platforms and solid tumor-adaptive therapeutic strategies; however, their actual therapeutic value still requires further experimental and clinical validation.

CAR-T therapy offers a potentially promising immunotherapeutic strategy for patients with BC who derive limited benefit from conventional treatments. However, its clinical translation in BC remains constrained by antigen heterogeneity, an immunosuppressive TME, immune escape, and insufficient therapeutic persistence, which are also common barriers to CAR-T therapy in solid tumors.44 In this study, we employed bibliometric and visualization analyses to systematically map the knowledge structure, thematic evolution, and emerging frontiers of BC CAR-T research from 2004 to 2025. Compared with previous bibliometric studies that mainly examined CAR-T therapy, CAR-T therapy in solid tumors broadly, or specific immunological mechanisms, we specifically focused on the disease context of BC. Miao described the global landscape of CAR-T research in solid tumors and provided a macro-level overview of the field. However, a pan-solid-tumor perspective may not fully capture BC-specific translational challenges, including molecular subtype heterogeneity, differences in target antigen expression, and TME-related adaptive issues.45 In this study, we revealed the knowledge base and thematic evolution of BC CAR-T research from a disease-specific perspective by integrating keyword co-occurrence, clustering, and citation burst analysis. The results suggest that research attention may be gradually shifting from early feasibility exploration to clinically relevant themes, including target antigen expression and heterogeneity, TME-related research, and emerging target development. Specifically, this knowledge map may help researchers identify current hotspots, potential frontiers, and knowledge sources and may provide hypothesis-generating clues for subsequent experimental studies in target selection, patient stratification, preclinical validation, and early-phase clinical trial design.46 It should be emphasized that the findings of this study mainly reflect patterns in publication output, keyword usage, and citation structure and cannot directly demonstrate the effectiveness of specific mechanisms or therapeutic strategies. Therefore, the value of this study lies more appropriately in assisting research direction assessment and study design, rather than serving as a direct basis for clinical decision-making or therapeutic efficacy evaluation.

Limitation

This study has several limitations. First, the literature search was conducted exclusively in the WoSCC. Although WoSCC is widely used in bibliometric studies and provides standardized citation information, reliance on a single database may have led to the omission of relevant studies indexed in other databases, such as Scopus, PubMed, or Embase. In addition, because WoSCC predominantly indexes English-language publications, studies published in other languages may not have been fully captured, potentially introducing language and geographical bias. WoSCC is a major curated citation database, but no single bibliographic database provides complete coverage of all scholarly publications. Therefore, future studies may consider incorporating more databases for cross-validation to further improve literature coverage and enhance the robustness of the findings.

Second, although bibliometric methods and visualization tools such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer are useful for mapping knowledge structures, collaboration networks, and the evolution of research topics, they primarily reflect relationships among publications, citation patterns, and shifts in research attention. They can’t replace evidence-quality assessment, effect-size synthesis, or causal inference as performed in systematic reviews or meta-analyses. Therefore, the findings of this study are more appropriate for identifying research hotspots, knowledge bases, and broad developmental trends than for providing definitive mechanistic or therapeutic conclusions. Furthermore, when constructing large-scale networks, we retained highly relevant core nodes to ensure computational feasibility and interpretability of the maps. This approach may have excluded some peripheral topics, although it did not substantially affect the identification of the core research structure and major trends.47

In addition, the application of Joinpoint regression has certain methodological limitations during the early stage of the field, when publication output was relatively low. Before 2013, the annual number of publications was limited; therefore, even a very small increase or decrease in the number of publications may result in a large relative change, thereby amplifying the influence of random fluctuations on APC estimation and Joinpoint detection. When the number of time points is limited and observations are sparse, segmented regression may provide insufficient information for stable trend estimation, potentially leading to unstable slope estimates, wider confidence intervals, and greater sensitivity of joinpoint locations to model specification or incidental publication events. Accordingly, trends before 2013 should be interpreted as exploratory signals of early research activity rather than definitive quantitative evidence. Joinpoint regression is designed to identify connected linear trend segments and change points, but official guidance emphasizes that model selection and trend interpretation become more challenging when the number of points in a trend analysis is small.48

Despite these limitations, which to some extent constrain a comprehensive depiction of the early research landscape, the overall analysis of this study nonetheless provides a reliable overview of the major research dynamics and developmental trends of CAR-T immunotherapy in BC since 2004.

Conclusion

We systematically mapped the research landscape of CAR-T therapy in BC from 2004 to 2025. The field has shown sustained growth, with the United States playing a central role in scientific output and international collaboration. Keyword analyses indicate that research hotspots have shifted from early feasibility exploration to translational challenges, including antigen heterogeneity, immunosuppressive TME, CAR-T cell exhaustion, and precision targeting. The sustained emergence of TME, EMT, and “mesothelin” suggests increasing attention to microenvironment-related barriers, tumor plasticity, and target-antigen selection. Overall, BC CAR-T research is moving toward mechanistic optimization and clinical translation. Future studies may further focus on target refinement, functional engineering, and rational therapeutic optimization to improve the translational potential of CAR-T therapy in BC.
