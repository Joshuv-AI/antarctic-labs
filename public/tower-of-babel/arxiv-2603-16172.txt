Existence, asymptotic behaviour and convergence of a generalised 3D Muskat problem in stable regime
Qasim Khan, Anthony Suen, Bao Quoc Tang

Abstract
. We address a generalised three-dimensional α \alpha -Muskat model that comes from the fluid interface problem given by two incompressible fluids with different densities in the stable regime. We establish local-in-time wellposedness when α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) and also prove global-in-time existence for strong solutions when α ∈ [ 0 , 1 2 ) \alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}) with initial data controlled by explicit constants. We obtain maximum principles for the L ∞ L^{\infty} -norms of both the solutions and their gradients, and we further acquire the corresponding decay rates of these L ∞ L^{\infty} -norms. Finally, some convergence results for strong solutions as α → 0 + \alpha\to 0^{+} are also proved.

Introduction

This paper studies a generalised model for a free boundary problem in three dimensional space. Such model is motivated by the original Muskat problem which describes the evolution of the interface between two fluids under the influence of gravity and capillarity
[
Mus34
]
. More precisely, we address the following generalised system for modelling the motion of fluids:

\displaystyle\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot(u\rho)

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u+\nabla P

\displaystyle=-(0,0,(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\rho),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u

\displaystyle=0,

(1.3)

where

is the velocity,

is the pressure,
\rho
is the density and
\alpha\in[0,1)
is a non-negative parameter. Equations (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are respectively the conservation of mass and incompressibility condition on the fluid, while equation (
1.2
) is known as the Darcy’s law when
\alpha=0
.

Denoting the interface
that separates the space in two domains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
by
x_{3}=f(x_{1},x_{2},t)
, we consider the density
\rho=\rho(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)
to be the following step function

\rho(x,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},t)=\begin{cases}\rho_{1},&x\in\Omega_{1}(t)=\{x_{3}>f(x_{1},x_{2},t)\}\\
\rho_{2},&x\in\Omega_{2}(t)=\{x_{3}<f(x_{1},x_{2},t)\},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are positive constant densities. By applying the curl operator twice and making use of the incompressibility of the fluid, we have
{\rm curl}\,{\rm curl}u=-\Delta u

\displaystyle u[\rho]=u=\left(\partial_{x_{1}}\Lambda^{-2+\alpha}\partial_{x_{3}}\rho,\ \partial_{x_{2}}\Lambda^{-2+\alpha}\partial_{x_{3}}\rho,-\left(\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}\right)\Lambda^{-2+\alpha}\rho\right),

\begin{split}\frac{\partial f(x,t)}{\partial t}&=\frac{\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}}{4\pi}\,\texttt{PV}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t)\right)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy,\\

(1.6)

Here

\rho_{1}
and

\displaystyle\rho_{2}>\rho_{1},

(1.7)

and for simplicity, we choose

When
\alpha=0
, the system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) describes the dynamics of the interface in between two incompressible fluids in porous media in three dimensional space, in particular it was used for addressing the encroachment of water into oil in a porous medium
[
Mus34
]
. There is an extensive literature on the topic of Muskat problem and we shall give a brief review on the relevant results. When the surface tension is taken into account via the Laplace-Young condition, it was shown by Escher and Simonett
[
ES97
]
that there exist classical solutions to the Muskat problem in the two-dimensional case. Later in
[
EM11
]
, Escher and Matioc addressed the Muskat problem in a periodic geometry
and incorporate capillary and gravity in the setting, and they obtained local well-posedness for Hölder solution. Escher et al.
[
EMM11
]
conducted a similar study for the case of three interfaces by reducing the problem to an abstract evolution equation. In the scenario where there is no surface tension, Siegel, Caflisch and Howison
[
SCH04
]
proved that for the two-dimensional case, the problem is ill-posed when the higher-viscosity fluid contracts. Conversely, they established the global-in-time existence of solutions for small initial data when the higher-viscosity fluid expands. In
[
Amb04
]
, Ambrose proved short-time well-posedness for the problem with two fluids under the condition that the initial data must satisfy a sign condition which is a generalisation of a condition of Saffman and Taylor
[
ST58
]
.

The free boundary problems given by fluids with different constant densities
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
as in (

have also been extensively studied by various researchers. In the stable regime for which
\rho_{1}>\rho_{2}
, Córdoba and Gancedo
[
CG07
,
CG09
]
proved that the Muskat problem is well-posed and obtained local-in-time existence and uniqueness for it. In the work
[
CCG+16
]
, Constantin et al. showed global-in-time existence for strong and weak solutions with initial data
f_{0}
in (
1.6
) controlled by explicit constants, and the results were later extended by Gancedo et al.
[
GGJPS19
]
to the more general case with density-viscosity jump. Moreover in
[
GL22
]
, Gancedo and Lazar showed that the three-dimensional problem is globally well-posed in the critical Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{2}\cap\dot{W}^{1,\infty}
with arbitrary large slope, and by applying the modulus of continuity techniques from
[
KNV07
]
for quasi-geostrophic equation, Cameron et al.
[
CCH+26
]
recently obtained local well-posedness results in any dimension for initial data within the space
W^{1,\infty}
. In
[
FGSV12
]
, Friedlander et al. considered a singularly modified version of the two-dimensional Muskat problem and showed that the system has a unique local-in-time patch-type solution
\rho

, it was shown in
[
SCH04
]
that in the two-dimensional case, there are singular solutions that start off with smooth initial data but develop a point of infinite curvature at finite time, hence the problem is ill-posed in the sense that singularities can form in
an arbitrarily short time for arbitrarily small initial data in terms of Sobolev norm. In
[
CG07
]
, for the three-dimensional setting, the problem is ill-posed that there exists a solution

such that its

[
CGSZ17
]
showed that there exist solutions of the Muskat problem which shift stability regimes in the following sense: they start stable, then become unstable, and finally return back to the stable regime. Castro, Córdoba and Faraco
[
CCF21
]
later proved the existence of mixing solutions of the incompressible porous media equation for
all Muskat type

There is also a considerably rich literature dedicated to the study of the regularity of solutions for the Muskat problem. Using the Cauchy-Kowalewski method, Castro, Córdoba and Gancedo
[
CCG10
]
showed that there exist analytic solutions to the problem provided that the initial data are analytic functions. In terms of critical regularity, for the two-dimensional case, Alazard and Nguyen
[
AN23
]
obtained well-posedness on the endpoint Sobolev space of

functions with three-half derivative in

[
CCGS13
]
obtained global existence results for Lipschitz continuous solutions with initial data that satisfy
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
and
\|\partial_{x}f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
. In a more recent work
[
JKM21
]
, Jacobs, Kim and Mészáros introduced a framework to approximate solutions of the Muskat problem with surface tension, and they constructed weak solutions via the convergence of approximate solutions.

In this current work, our goal is to investigate various properties of interface

2.
Local-in-time well-posedness

In this section, we prove local-in-time existence and uniqueness for the interface
f
using energy estimates. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the norms of
H^{k}
and
C^{k,\delta}
are defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{k}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k,\delta}}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{C^{k}}+\max_{i+j=k}\max_{x\neq y}\frac{|\partial_{x_{1}}^{i}\partial_{x_{2}}^{j}f(x)-\partial_{x_{1}}^{i}\partial_{x_{2}}^{j}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}}.

The main theorem in this section is given as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

for

\alpha\in[0,1)

Proof.

We apply energy method for proving Theorem

We multiply the contour equation (

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x-y)\right)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x)-f(x-y))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dxdy\,\quad\quad\quad\\

&\qquad+\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)\int_{|y|>1}\frac{\nabla f(x)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x)-f(x-y))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dxdy\,\\
&\qquad-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)\int_{|y|>1}\frac{\nabla f(x-y)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x)-f(x-y))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dxdy\,\\

We estimate the terms

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}_{2}&\leq\frac{3+\alpha}{2}\int_{|y|>1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x)|^{2}\cdot\frac{\left(f(x)-f(x-y)\right)\cdot\left(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x-y)\right)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x)-f(x-y))^{2}\right]^{\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}dxdy\\

\begin{split}\mathbf{I}_{3}&=\int_{|y|>1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)f(x-y)\cdot\frac{|y|^{2}-2\left(f(x)-f(x-y)\right)^{2}}{\left[1+\left(f(x)-f(x-y)\right)^{2}|y|^{-2}\right]^{\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}dxdy\\

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t)\leq C(\left\|f\right\|_{H^{2}}^{3}(t)+1).

1\leq k\leq 4
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t)\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2+\delta}}^{4}+1)\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}f\right\|_{L_{2}}^{2},

(2.5)

and in a similar way, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(t)\leq C(\|f\|_{C^{2+\delta}}^{4}+1)\left\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}f\right\|_{L_{2}}^{2},

(2.6)

Since
\|f\|^{2}_{H^{4}}=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{4}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{4}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, by the estimates (
2.1
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|_{H^{4}}(t)\leq C(\|f\|_{H^{4}}^{5}+1).

(2.7)

Applying Grönwall’s inequality on (
2.7
), for each
\alpha\in[0,1)
, there exists a positive time
T=T(\|f_{0}\|_{H^{4}},\alpha)
and a positive constant
M=M(T,\alpha,\|f_{0}\|_{H^{4}})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f\|_{H^{4}}(t)\leq M,

(2.8)

hence the local existence result for (
1.6
) follows by standard energy argument.

To prove the uniqueness of solution, let
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},t)
and
f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},t)
be two solutions of (
1.6
) with
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},0)=f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},0)=f_{0}((x_{1},x_{2})
. Using the bound (
2.8
), for
i\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f_{i}\|_{H^{4}}(t)\leq M.

(2.9)

Define the difference by
\tilde{f}=f_{1}-f_{2}
. Then
\tilde{f}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(t)=\mathbf{I}_{6}+\mathbf{I}_{7}+\mathbf{I}_{8},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{6}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{f}(x)\nabla\tilde{f}(x)\cdot\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}y\left[|y|^{2}+(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x-y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}dydx,

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{7}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{f}(x)\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\tilde{f}(y)\cdot(x-y)\left[|x-y|^{2}+(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{8}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{f}(x)\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla f_{2}(x)-\nabla f_{2}(x-y))\cdot yN_{\alpha}(x,y)dydx,

with

\displaystyle N_{\alpha}(x,y)=\left[|y|^{2}+(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(x-y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}-\left[|y|^{2}+(f_{2}(x)-f_{2}(x-y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}.

Integrating by parts and using the bound (
2.9
), we readily have

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{6}+\mathbf{I}_{8}\leq C(M)\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
C(M)
is a positive constant which depends on
M
. For the term
\mathbf{I}_{7}
, we estimate it as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{7}

\displaystyle=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{f}(x)(\tilde{f}(x)-\tilde{f}(y))\left[|x-y|^{2}+(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}dydx

\displaystyle\qquad+\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tilde{f}(x)(\tilde{f}(x)-\tilde{f}(y))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{(3+\alpha)(f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(y))(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y)-\nabla f_{1}(x)(x-y))}{\left[|x-y|^{2}+(f_{1}(x)-f_{1}(y))^{2}\right]^{-\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C(M)\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(t)\leq C(M)\|\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}(t),

and the uniqueness follows by applying Gröwall’s inequality.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

In view of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, for each
\bar{\alpha}\in[0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
C(\bar{\alpha})
such that for all
\alpha\in[0,\bar{\alpha})
, if
f_{\alpha}
is a local-in-time solution to (
1.6
) with the same initial data
f_{0}
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f_{\alpha}\|_{H^{4}}(t)\leq C(\bar{\alpha})(\|f_{\alpha}\|_{H^{4}}^{5}+1).

Hence there exists a positive time
\bar{T}=\bar{T}(\|f_{0}\|_{H^{4}},\bar{\alpha})
and a positive constant
\bar{M}=\bar{M}(\bar{T},\bar{\alpha},\|f_{0}\|_{H^{4}})
such that
f_{\alpha}
are solutions to (
1.6
) for all

on

satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\alpha<\bar{\alpha}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq\bar{T}}\|f_{\alpha}\|_{H^{4}}(t)\leq\bar{M}.

(2.10)

The uniform bound (
2.10
) will be useful for proving convergence of solutions as

later in Section
5
.

3.
Global-in-time existence with bounded data

In this section, we obtain global-in-time existence for classical solutions of the contour equation (
1.6
). We make use of the norm given by

\left\|f\right\|_{s}\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi|\xi|^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,,\quad s\geq 1.

(3.1)

The results are summarised in the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
be given. Assume that
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
k\geq 4
and
\|f_{0}\|_{1}<k_{0}(\alpha)
, where
k_{0}(\alpha)
is a constant that satisfies

\displaystyle 2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2n+1\right)^{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{2}+n\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{2}\right)}\cdot\frac{k_{0}(\alpha)^{2n}}{n!}<1,

(3.2)

with
C(\alpha)
being some positive constant. Then there exists a unique solution
f
of (
1.6
) with initial data
f_{0}
such that
f\in C([0,T];H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
for any
T>0
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the global-in-time existence result only holds for
0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
. This is due to the fact that we have to ensure the set
(\alpha,1-\alpha)
is non-empty. It is unknown whether we have global-in-time existence of solutions when
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
.

Remark 3.3
.

The existence of
k_{0}(\alpha)
that appears in Theorem
3.1
is shown in Lemma
A.2
in Appendix
A
.

Before we give the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we prove some lemmas which are crucial for our analysis. To begin with, we state and prove the following lemma for estimating
\|f\|_{1}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
be given. Assume that
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
k\geq 4
and
\|f_{0}\|_{s}<k_{0}(\alpha)
, where
k_{0}(\alpha)
is a constant that satisfies (
3.2
). If
f
is a classical solution of (
1.6
) defined on
[0,T]
with initial data
f_{0}
, then for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1}(t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{1}.

(3.3)

Proof.

The case for
\alpha=0
was proved in
[
CCG+16
]
, hence it suffices to consider the case when
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. First of all, we rewrite the contour equation (
1.6
) to obtain

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(x,t)=-\Lambda^{1+\alpha}(f)-N_{\alpha}(f),

where

N_{\alpha}(f)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{y}{|y|^{2+\alpha}}\nabla_{x}(D_{y}f(x))\,R_{\alpha}(D_{y}f(x))\,dy

with

\displaystyle D_{y}f(x)

\displaystyle\triangleq\left(f(x)-f(x-y)\right)|y|^{-1},

and

\displaystyle R_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\triangleq 1-\frac{1}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}.

By changing variables, the term
N_{\alpha}(f)
can be given by

N_{\alpha}(f)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{x}\left(D_{y}f(x)\right)\,R_{\alpha}(D_{y}f(x))-\nabla_{x}(D_{-y}f(x))\,R_{\alpha}(D_{-y}f(x))dy.

Recalling the definition (
3.1
) of
\left\|f\right\|_{1}
and the contour equation (
1.6
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{1}(t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\left(|\partial_{t}\hat{f}(\xi)\overline{\hat{f}(\xi)}|\,+\hat{f}(\xi)\partial_{t}\hat{f}(\xi)\right)\,/\,2|\hat{f}(\xi)|d\xi\\
&\leq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\xi)|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\widehat{N_{\alpha}(f)}|(\xi)\,d\xi.\end{split}

Under the condition that
\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{1}<k_{0}(\alpha)
, we claim

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\widehat{N_{\alpha}(f)}|(\xi)\,d\xi<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi.

(3.4)

To prove (
3.4
), we apply the method which is reminiscent of the one given in
[
CCG+16
]
. For
|z|<1
, we have the following Taylor’s series expansion for
R_{\alpha}(z)
:

R_{\alpha}(z)=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}z^{n},\quad a_{n}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{2}+n\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3+\alpha}{2}\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{n!},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
is the Gamma function. Hence if
|D_{y}f(x)|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{1}
, then we have

N_{\alpha}(f)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{n\geq 1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{y}{|y|^{2+\alpha}}\left(\nabla_{x}(D_{y}f)\,(D_{y}f)^{2n}-\nabla_{x}(D_{-y}f)(D_{-y}f)^{2n}\right)dy.

Recall the Fourier transform for
D_{y}f
and
\nabla_{x}D_{y}f
are respectively given by

\widehat{D_{y}f}=\hat{f}{m}(\xi,y),\quad\widehat{\nabla_{x}D_{y}f}=i\xi\hat{f}(\xi){m}(\xi,y),

where the Fourier multiplier
m(\xi,y)
is given by

\displaystyle m(\xi,y)=\frac{1-e^{-i\xi\cdot y}}{|y|}.

Hence we have

\widehat{\nabla_{x}\left(D_{y}f\right)\,(D_{y}f)^{2n}}=\left((i\xi\hat{f}_{m})*(\hat{f}_{m})*\cdots*(\hat{f}_{m})\right)(\xi,\alpha).

(3.5)

By applying convolutions, we get

\begin{split}\widehat{{N}_{\alpha}(f)}(\xi)=&\frac{-i}{4\pi}\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dy\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{1}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{2n}\,\frac{y}{|y|^{2+\alpha}}\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)\\
&\times\hat{f}\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}\hat{f}\left(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}\right)\right)\hat{f}\left(\xi_{2n}\right)\left(M_{n}(y)-M_{n}(-y)\right),\end{split}

where
M_{n}
is defined by

M_{n}(y)\triangleq m\left(\xi-\xi_{1},y\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}m\left(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1},y\right).\right)m\left(\xi_{2n},y\right).

Using Fubini’s theorem, we further get

\begin{split}\widehat{{N}_{\alpha}(f)}(\xi)=&\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{1}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{2n}\\
&\times\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)\hat{f}\left(\xi-\xi_{1}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}\hat{f}\left(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}\right)\right)\hat{f}\left(\xi_{2n}\right)\cdot I_{n,\alpha}\end{split}

with

I_{n,\alpha}=\frac{-i}{4\pi}(-1)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{y}{|y|^{2+\alpha}}\left(M_{n}(y)-M_{n}(-y)\right)dy.

Using the polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
that
y=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)
,
I_{n,\alpha}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle I_{n,\alpha}=\frac{-i}{4\pi}(-1)^{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\,d\theta\int_{0}^{+\infty}\,dr\left(M_{n}(r,u)-M_{n}(r,-u)\right)

with
m(\xi,r,u)
and
M_{n}(r,u)
being given by

m(\xi,r,u)=\frac{\left(1-\exp^{ir\xi\cdot u}\right)}{r|r|^{\alpha}}=\frac{i\xi\cdot u}{|r|^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{ir(s-1)\xi\cdot u}ds

and

\displaystyle M_{n}(r,u)

\displaystyle=m(\xi-\xi_{1},r,u)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}m(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1},r,u)\right)m\left(\xi_{2n},r,u\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}ds_{2n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n-1}\left(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}\right)\cdot u\right)\xi_{2n}\cdot u

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{e^{-ir\mathbf{S}_{1}\cdot u}}{r|r|^{\alpha}}-\frac{e^{-ir\mathbf{S}_{2}\cdot u}}{r|r|^{\alpha}}\right),

where the terms
\mathbf{S}_{1}
and
\mathbf{S}_{2}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2n-1}(s_{j}-1)(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1})+(s_{2n}-1)\xi_{2n},

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=-(\xi-\xi_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{2n-1}(s_{j}-1)(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1})+(s_{2n}-1)\xi_{2n}.

with
0\leq s_{j}\leq 1
and
1\leq j\leq 2n
. Note that
m(\xi,-r,u)=-m(\xi,r,-u)
and
-m(\xi,-r,-u)=m(\xi,r,u)
, upon changing of variables, it yields

\displaystyle I_{n,\alpha}=\frac{-i}{8\pi}(-1)^{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\,d\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\,dr\left(M_{n}(r,u)-M_{n}(r,-u)\right).

Since for
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\mathbf{S}\in\{\mathbf{S}_{1},\mathbf{S}_{2}\}
,

\displaystyle|\mathbf{S}|^{\alpha}\leq\left[\left|\xi-\xi_{1}\right|+\left|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right|+\cdots+\left|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}\right|+\left|\xi_{2n}\right|\right]^{\alpha}

and using Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\texttt{PV}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\exp(ir\mathbf{S})}{r^{1+\alpha}}\right|\leq C(\alpha)|\mathbf{S}|^{\alpha},

for some positive constant
C(\alpha)
, hence we can estimate
I_{n,\alpha}
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{n,\alpha}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{4\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u\,d\theta\quad\int_{0}^{1}ds_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{1}ds_{2n}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{2n+1}(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1})\right)\cdot u

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{\exp(ir\mathbf{S}_{1}\cdot u)}{r^{1+\alpha}}-\frac{\exp(ir\mathbf{S}_{2}\cdot u)}{r^{1+\alpha}}-\frac{\exp(-ir\mathbf{S}_{1}\cdot u)}{r^{1+\alpha}}+\frac{\exp(-ir\mathbf{S}_{2}\cdot u)}{r^{1+\alpha}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\leq 2C(\alpha)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{2n+1}\left|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}\right|\left|\xi_{2n}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left[\left|\xi-\xi_{1}\right|+\left|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right|+\cdots+\left|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}\right|+\left|\xi_{2n}\right|\right]^{\alpha}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\widehat{N_{\alpha}(f)}|(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|{\Gamma}(-\alpha)\right|\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{1}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\xi_{2n}|\xi|

\displaystyle\times\left|\xi-\xi_{1}\right|\left|\hat{f}(\xi-\xi_{1})\right|\prod_{j=1}^{2n+1}\left|\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}\right|\left|\hat{f}(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1})\right|

\displaystyle\times\left|\xi_{2n}\right|\left|\hat{f}(\xi_{2n})\right|\times\left[\left|\xi-\xi_{1}\right|+\left|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right|+\cdots+\left|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}\right|+\left|\xi_{2n}\right|\right]^{\alpha}.

By the triangle inequality
|\xi|\leq|\xi-\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}|+|\xi_{2n}|
and the fact that

\displaystyle\left[|\xi-\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}|+|\xi_{2n}|\right]^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq(2n+1)^{\alpha}\left[\left|\xi-\xi_{1}\right|^{1+\alpha}+\left|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\right|^{1+\alpha}+\cdots+\left|\xi_{2n-1}-\xi_{2n}\right|^{1+\alpha}+\left|\xi_{2n}\right|^{1+\alpha}\right],

we further obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\hat{N}_{\alpha}(f)(\xi)|\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq 2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2n+1\right)^{1+\alpha}a_{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\hat{f}(\xi)|\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\right)\left[2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2n+1\right)^{1+\alpha}a_{n}\left\|{f}\right\|_{1}^{2n}\right]

\displaystyle<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\left[2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(2n+1\right)^{2}a_{n}\left\|{f}\right\|_{1}^{2n}\right].

(3.6)

To bound the summation term on the right side of (
3
), for
|z|<1
, we recall that

1-(1+z^{2})^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}a_{n}z^{2n}.

Multiply the above by
z
, differentiate with respect to
z
and replace
z
by
iz
,

\frac{1+(2+z)z^{2}}{(1-z^{2})^{\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(2n+1)z^{2n}.

By repeating the process once more, we obtain

(1-z^{2})^{-\frac{7+\alpha}{2}}\left[(10+4\alpha)z^{2}+(2+\alpha)^{2}z^{4}\right]-1=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(2n+1)^{2}z^{2n}.

(3.7)

By applying the identity (
3.7
), for
\|f\|_{1}<1
, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n+1)^{2}a_{n}\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{2n}=\left(1-\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{7+\alpha}{2}}\left[(10+4\alpha)\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{2}+(2+\alpha)^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{4}\right]-1.

Given
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, by Lemma
A.2
in Appendix
A
, choosing a constant
k_{0}(\alpha)\in(0,1)
such that for all
0\leq z<k_{0}(\alpha),
we always have

\left[\frac{(10+4\alpha)z^{2}+(2+\alpha)z^{4}}{(1-z^{2})^{\frac{7+\alpha}{2}}}-1\right]<\frac{1}{2C(\alpha)},

then it implies

2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n+1)^{2}a_{n}\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{2n}<1,

wherever
\left\|f\right\|_{1}<k_{0}(\alpha)
, and hence the bound (
3.4
) follows. Therefore if the condition
\left\|f\right\|_{1}<k_{0}(\alpha)
holds, using the bound (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\xi)|^{2+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\,|\widehat{N_{\alpha}(f)}|(\xi)\,d\xi<0,

which gives

\left\|f(t)\right\|_{1}(t)\leq\left\|f_{0}\right\|<k_{0}(\alpha)

as desired.
∎

Next, we are going to obtain some higher order estimates on
f
with the help of (
3.3
). The results are listed in Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the hypotheses and notations of Lemma
3.1
are in force. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1-\alpha)
and for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1+\delta}(t)+\mu(\alpha)\int_{0}^{t}\|f\|_{2+\delta+\alpha}(s)ds\leq\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta}

(3.8)

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2+\delta}(t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{2+\delta}

(3.9)

for some
\mu(\alpha)>0
.

Proof.

Recall that by Sobolev inequality, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{H^{3}}
. By the definition (
3.1
),

\frac{d}{dt}\Lambda^{1+\delta}f(x,t)=-\Lambda^{2+\delta+\alpha}(f)-\Lambda^{1+\delta}N_{\alpha}(f),

and hence

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{1+\delta}(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\delta+\alpha}f\,|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{1+\delta}\hat{N}_{\alpha}\,|\xi)|\,d\xi.

Using the same method as given in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, for any
0<\delta<1-\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{1+\delta}|\hat{N_{\alpha}}|(\xi)\,d

\displaystyle\xi\leq 2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n+1)^{1+\delta+\alpha}a_{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|\hat{f}(\xi)|\,\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\delta+\alpha}|\hat{f}(\xi)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\delta+\alpha}|\hat{f}|\right)\left[\,2{|\Gamma{(-\alpha)}|}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n+1)^{2}{a_{n}}\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{2n}\right].

For each
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we define
\mu(\alpha)\in(0,1)
by

\displaystyle\mu(\alpha)=1-\left[\,2C(\alpha)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2n+1)^{2}{a_{n}}k_{0}(\alpha)^{2n}\right],

then by the bound (
3.2
), it implies
\mu(\alpha)>0
, and hence we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|f\right\|_{{1+\delta}}(t)+\mu(\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2+\delta+\alpha}|\hat{f}|\,d\xi\leq 0,

Upon integrating over
t
, the bound (
3.8
) follows. By repeating the same argument, we can show that the bound (
3.9
) holds as well.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that the hypotheses and notations of Lemma
3.1
are in force. Then for any
\delta\in(\alpha,1-\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f\|_{H^{k}}(t)\leq\bar{C}\|f_{0}\|_{H^{k}},

(3.10)

where
\bar{C}
is a positive constant that depends on
\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta}
,
\alpha
and
k_{0}(\alpha)
but is independent of
T
.

Proof.

Recall that for
\delta\in(0,1)
, the homogeneous Hölder norm
\left|\,\cdot\,\right|_{C^{\delta}}
is given by

\left|g\right|_{C^{\delta}}\triangleq\max_{x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}}.

To estimate the
H^{k}
-norm of
f
, we perform similar analysis as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
with some modifications. For example, in order to bound the terms

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nabla_{x}D_{y}f(x)\cdot y\,(D_{y}f(x))^{k}(\partial_{x_{1}}D_{y}f(x))^{k}}{|y|^{2+\alpha}\left[1+(D_{y}f(x))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}+k}}\,dy\,dx

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nabla_{x}D_{y}f(x)\cdot y\,(D_{y}f(x))(\partial_{x_{1}}D_{y}f(x))^{k}}{|y|^{2+\alpha}\left[1+(D_{y}f(x))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}+k-1}}\,dy\,dx,

we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{1}+\mathbf{H}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f(x)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\nabla_{x}D_{y}f(x)|}{|y|^{1+\alpha}}\left|\partial_{x_{1}}D_{y}f(x)\right|^{k}dydx

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|^{k-2}_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla f\right|_{C^{\delta}}\left\|\nabla^{2}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|y|>1}\frac{|y|^{2\delta}}{|y|^{4+2\alpha}}\,dy\quad

\displaystyle\quad+C\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla^{2}f\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|_{C^{\delta}}\left\|\nabla^{2}f\right\|^{k-2}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|y|<1}\frac{|y|^{2\delta}}{|y|^{2+2\alpha}}dy.

Since
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta\in(\alpha,1-\alpha)
, the two integrals appeared on the right side of the above inequality remain finite. And by the interpolation inequality that
\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}
, we further obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{1}+\mathbf{H}_{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla f\right\|^{k-2}_{L^{\infty}}\left|\nabla f\right|_{C^{\delta}}\left\|\nabla^{2}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C\left\|\partial_{x_{1}}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}\left|\nabla^{2}f\right|_{C^{\delta}}\left\|\nabla^{2}f\right\|^{k-2}_{L^{\infty}}.

By performing similar estimates on
\|\partial_{x_{2}}^{3}f\|_{L^{2}}
, there exists some polynomial functions
p_{1}(\cdot)
and
p_{2}(\cdot)
whose degrees depend on
k
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{k}}\leq p_{1}(\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}})p_{2}(\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}})(|\nabla f|_{C^{\delta}}+|\nabla^{2}f|_{C^{\delta}})\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2}.

Using Fourier transform, for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{i}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{i}

and

\displaystyle|\nabla^{i}f|_{C^{\delta}}\leq\|f\|_{i+\delta},

using the bounds (
3.8
)-(
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|^{2}_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq p_{1}(\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta})p_{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2+\delta})(\|f\|_{1+\delta}+\|f\|_{2+\delta})\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2p_{1}(\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta})p_{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2+\delta})\|f\|_{2+\delta}\|f\|_{H^{k}}^{2},

where the last inequality follows since
\|f\|_{1+\delta}\leq\|f\|_{2+\delta}
. Applying Grönwall’s inequality and using the bound (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{k}}(t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{H^{k}}\exp\Big(\frac{Cp_{1}(\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta})p_{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2+\delta})\|f_{0}\|_{1+\alpha}}{\mu(\alpha)}\Big),

and (
3.10
) follows by choosing
\bar{C}=\exp\Big(\frac{Cp_{1}(\|f_{0}\|_{1+\delta})p_{2}(\|f_{0}\|_{2+\delta})\|f_{0}\|_{1+\alpha}}{\mu(\alpha)}\Big)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Given
k\geq 4
and
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, let
f_{0}\in H^{k}
such that
\|f_{0}\|_{1}<k_{0}(\alpha)
with (
3.2
) holds. By Theorem
2.1
, there exists a time
T>0
such that the contour equation (
1.6
) possess a unique solution in
C^{1}([0,T];K^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
with
f(x,0)=f_{0}
. Using the bound (
3.10
), the local-in-time solution can then be continued in

for all time provided that

is initially smaller than

given by (
3.2
).
∎

4.
Maximum principle and asymptotic behaviour of solutions

In this section, we prove some results regarding the maximum principle for the interface
f
and further address the asymptotic behaviour for
f
. The results are given in the following subsections:

4.1.
Decay of
L^{\infty}
-norm of
f

In this subsection, we obtain the maximum principle for the
L^{\infty}
-norm for
f
and show that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
decays in time
t
.

We begin with the following lemma about an ODE problem involving hypergeometric function.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Consider the ODE problem that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}g(0)=0,\\
(1+z^{2})g^{\prime}(z)-(3+\alpha)z=(3+\alpha)z^{2},\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

Then (
4.1
) has a smooth solution
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which is given by

\displaystyle g(z)=\frac{(3+\alpha)z^{3}(1+z^{2})^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}{3}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{2},\frac{\alpha+5}{2};\frac{5}{2};-z^{2}\right),

(4.3)

where
{}_{2}F_{1}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,;\,\cdot\,;\,\cdot\,)
is the ordinary hypergeometric function.

Proof.

It can be verified by direct computation and we omit the details here.
∎

The following theorem gives the maximum principle of
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
k\geq 4
. Then for any
\alpha\in[0,1)
, if
f
is the unique solution to (
1.6
) on
[0,T]
, the following bound holds for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

The case for
\alpha=0
was proved in
[
CG09
]
, and hence we can assume
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Using Theorem
2.1
and Sobolev inequality, there exists
T>0
and a unique solution
f(x,t)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T]
such that
f\in C^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Since
f(\cdot,t)\in H^{k}
with
k\geq 4
, by the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma, we have
f(x,t)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
, and hence there exists a point
x_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
f(x,t)
reaches its maximum at
x=x_{*}
.

We define
\Psi(t)=f(x_{*},t)
and assume that
\Psi(t)>0
. By the Rademacher theorem, the function
\Psi(t)
is differentiable almost everywhere on
t
with
\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)
given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x_{*},t).

Since
\nabla f(x_{*},t)=0
, we further obtain

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{-\nabla f(y,t)\cdot(x_{*}-y)}{\left[|x_{*}-y|^{2}+(f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy.

Upon integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)

\displaystyle=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}\right)\nabla_{x}\cdot\left(\frac{x_{*}-y}{|x_{*}-y|^{3+\alpha}}\right)\left[1+\left(\frac{f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}}{|x_{*}-y|}\right)^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}\right)}{|x_{*}-y|}\frac{x_{*}-y}{|x_{*}-y|^{2+\alpha}}\cdot\nabla_{y}\left[1+\left(\frac{f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}}{|x_{*}-y|}\right)^{2}\right]^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{1}+\mathbf{D}_{2}.

We estimate
\mathbf{D}_{1}
and
\mathbf{D}_{2}
as follows. First of all, for the term
\mathbf{D}_{1}
, since
\Psi(t)\geq f(y,t)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we readily have

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{1}=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\Psi(t)-f(y,t)}{\left[\left|x_{*}-y\right|^{2}+\left(\Psi(t)-f(y,t)\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy\leq 0.

For the term
\mathbf{D}_{2}
, using Lemma
4.1
, if we define
g(z)
by (
4.3
), then by choosing

\displaystyle H(z)=\frac{g(z)}{(1+z^{2})^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}},

we have

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(z)(1+z^{2})^{-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}g(z)(2z)(1+z^{2})^{-\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle=(3+\alpha)z^{2}(1+z^{2})^{-\frac{5+\alpha}{2}},

and hence
\mathbf{D}_{2}
can be expressed by

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{2}=\texttt{PV}\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla_{y}\left(|x_{*}-y|^{-\alpha}\right)\cdot\nabla_{y}H\left(\frac{f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}}{|x_{*}-y|}\right)dy.

Since for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}(|x_{*}-y|^{-\alpha})=-\alpha(-\alpha+2)|x_{*}-y|^{-\alpha-2}+2\pi\frac{\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha+2}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha}{2})}\delta(y-x_{*}),

where
D_{y}(\cdot)
is defined in (
3.5
), and for any
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to x_{*}}\frac{f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)}{|x_{*}-y|}=\lim_{y\to x_{*}}\frac{f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)-\nabla f(x_{*},t)\cdot(x_{*}-y)}{|x_{*}-y|}=0,

where
\delta(\cdot)
is the Dirac delta function. Upon integrating by parts in
\mathbf{D}_{2}
, we thus obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{2}=-\texttt{PV}\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta_{y}\left(|x_{*}-y|^{-\alpha}\right)H\left(\frac{f{(x_{*},t)}-f{(y,t)}}{|x_{*}-y|}\right)dy=-\frac{2\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha+2}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{-\alpha}{2})}H(0).

Recall that
H(0)=g(0)=0
, it gives
\mathbf{J}_{2}=0
. Therefore we have
\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)\leq 0
for almost every
t
. Integrating over
t
, it implies
f(x_{*},t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all
t>0
.

For the case when
\Psi(t)\leq 0
, following the similar argument as shown above, we can conclude that
\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)\geq 0
for almost every
t
. Hence upon integrating over
t
, it implies
-f(x_{*},t)\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all
t>0
. Combining the above cases, we prove that (
4.4
) holds.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.2
, the following theorem gives the decay of
L^{\infty}
-norm of
f
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
k\geq 4
and
\alpha\in[0,1)
. If
f_{0}
satisfies

f_{0}(x)\leq 0
or
f_{0}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(4.5)

and if
f
is the unique solution to (
1.6
) on
[0,T]
, then for all
t\in[0,T]
, it satisfies

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)\leq\frac{\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{[1+C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)t]^{\frac{2}{1+\alpha}}},

(4.6)

where
C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)
is a positive constant which depends on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\alpha
.

Proof.

The inequality (
4.6
) holds trivially for
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)=0
, and hence without loss of generality we assume
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)>0
.

Consider the case for
f_{0}(x)\geq 0
, and the case for
f_{0}(x)\leq 0
can be proved by a similar argument. By Theorem
4.2
,

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}(t)=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{-\nabla f(y,t)\cdot(x_{*}-y)}{\left[|x_{*}-y|^{2}+(f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy\geq 0,

for almost every
t
. Hence if
f_{0}(x)\geq 0
, then it implies
f(x,t)\geq 0
. Also, by symmetry,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\texttt{PV}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t)\right)\cdot y}{\left[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy=0,

hence we have
\|f\|_{L^{1}}(t)=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
. By the fact that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)=f(x_{*},t)
, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)=-\mathbf{D}_{3},

where
\mathbf{D}_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{3}=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)}{\left[|x_{*}-y|^{2}+(f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy.

For each
r>0
, we define the ball
\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{*})=\{y:|x_{*}-y|\leq r\}
and the sets
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
by

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{y\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{*}):f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)\geq\frac{f(x_{*},t)}{2}\right\},

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{y\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(x_{*}):f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)<\frac{f(x_{*},t)}{2}\right\}.

Since the integrand of
\mathbf{D}_{3}
is non-negative, we can estimate
\mathbf{D}_{3}
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{3}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{V_{1}}\frac{f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t)}{\left[|x_{*}-y|^{2}+(f(x_{*},t)-f(y,t))^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(x_{*},t)/2}{[r^{2}+4\|f_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}|V_{1}|=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)/2}{[r^{2}+4\|f_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}|V_{1}|.

To estimate
|V_{1}|
, we notice that
|V_{1}|=\pi r^{2}-|V_{2}|
and since
f(y,t)\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(y,t)dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{V_{2}}f(y,t)dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f(x_{*},t)}{2}|V_{2}|=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)}{2}|V_{2}|.

Hence by rearranging terms,

\displaystyle|V_{1}|\geq\pi r^{2}-\frac{2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{3}\geq\frac{\pi r^{2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)-2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}}{2[r^{2}+4\|f_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}.

We choose

\displaystyle r=\left(\frac{2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}/\pi+1}{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{3}\geq\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}{[1+2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}/\pi+4\|f_{0}\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}}]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}.

Choosing
\tilde{C}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)=\frac{\pi}{2[1+2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}/\pi+4\|f_{0}\|^{3}_{L^{\infty}}]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}
, we thus conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)\leq-\tilde{C}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}},

and upon integrating by parts, the bound (
4.6
) follows by taking the constant
C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)=\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\tilde{C}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\alpha)
.
∎

4.2.
Decay of
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\nabla f

In this subsection, we further obtain the maximum principle for the
L^{\infty}
-norm for
\nabla f
and show that
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}
decays in time
t
under a boundedness assumption on
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{1}}
.

We begin with the following maximum principle for
\nabla f
under the assumption that
\nabla f_{0}
is smaller than some constant depending on
\alpha
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
k\geq 4
. Then for any
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1+\alpha)
, if
f
is the unique solution to (
1.6
) on
[0,T]
with

\displaystyle\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\sqrt{\frac{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}{5+\alpha+\varepsilon}},

(4.7)

the following bound holds for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}(t)\leq\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(4.8)

Proof.

The method is reminiscent of the one given in
[
CCG+16
]
with some refinements. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, we apply
\partial_{x_{i}}
on (
1.6
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t))=\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{1}(x,t)+\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}(x,t)+\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{3}(x,t),

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t)\cdot y}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}f(x-y,t)\cdot y}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{3}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t)-\partial_{x_{i}}f(x-y,t)}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}E_{\alpha}(x,y,t)dy

with

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(x,y,t)=(3+\alpha)\left[\frac{(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t))\cdot y}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})}\right].

Integrating by parts,
\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t)-2\partial_{x_{i}}f(x-y,t)}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\qquad-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t)-2\partial_{x_{i}}f(x-y,t)}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}B_{\alpha}(x,y,t)dy

with

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(x,y,t)=(3+\alpha)\left[\frac{(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))\nabla f(x-y,t)\cdot y+|y|^{2}}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})}\right].

Hence by adding
\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}
and
\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}+\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{3}=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t)-\partial_{x_{i}}f(x-y,t)}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}C_{\alpha}(x,y,t)dy

where

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=E_{\alpha}(x,y,t)+B_{\alpha}(x,y,t)-2

\displaystyle=1+\alpha+\frac{3+\alpha}{1+(D_{y}f(x)))^{2}}\times\left[(D_{y}f(x))\nabla f(x-y)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}-(D_{y}f(x))^{2}\right]

with
D_{y}(\cdot)
being defined in (
3.5
). Define a function
\Phi(t)
by

\displaystyle\Phi(t)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t))^{2}\right\},

(4.9)

then there exists
x_{\#}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\Phi(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#},t))^{2}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(t)=2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}f(\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{1}+\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{2}+\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{3})(x_{\#},t).

(4.10)

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\partial_{x_{i}}f(x,t))^{2}
attains its maximum at
x=x_{\#}
, dropping the variable
t
for simplicity, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#})\mathbf{Q}^{i}_{1}(x_{\#})=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#})\nabla\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#})\cdot y}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x_{\#})-f(x_{\#}-y))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy=0.

Also, by symmetry, we have

\displaystyle\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#})\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#}-y)}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x_{\#})-f(x_{\#}-y))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}C_{\alpha}(x_{\#},y)=0,

and hence the identity (
4.10
) can be reduced to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(t)=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\partial_{x_{i}}f(x_{\#}))^{2}}{[|y|^{2}+(f(x_{\#})-f(x_{\#}-y))^{2})]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}C_{\alpha}(x_{\#},y).

(4.11)

Since for
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}<\sqrt{\frac{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}{5+\alpha+\varepsilon}}
, we have

\displaystyle C_{\alpha}(x_{\#},y)\geq 1+\alpha-\frac{2(3+\alpha)\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}{1+\|\nabla f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}>\varepsilon,

(4.12)

it implies that the right side of (
4.11
) is always non-positive. By a bootstrap argument, we can show that if
\Phi(0)<\frac{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}{5+\alpha+\varepsilon}

5.
Convergence of solutions as
\alpha\to 0^{+}

In this section, we address the convergence of solutions as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
. To begin with, using Theorem
2.1
and Remark
2.2
, for
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
,
k\geq 4
and
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists
\bar{T}>0
such that
f_{\alpha}
and
f
are functions in
C^{1}([0,\bar{T}];H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{\alpha}(x,t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla f_{\alpha}(x,t)-\nabla f_{\alpha}(x-y,t)\right)y}{\left[|y|^{2}+\left(f_{\alpha}(x,t)-f_{\alpha}(x-y,t)\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\,dy

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}({x,0})

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t)\right)y}{\left[|y|^{2}+\left(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t)\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,dy

\displaystyle f(x,0)

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)

as well as the uniform bound (
2.10
) with
\bar{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}
. We further define
A_{\alpha}
and
A_{0}
by

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\triangleq A_{\alpha}(x,y,t)=|y|^{2}+(f_{\alpha}(x,t)-f_{\alpha}(x-y,t))^{2}

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle\triangleq A_{0}(x,y,t)=|y|^{2}+(f(x,t)-f(x-y,t))^{2}.

The following theorem shows that
f_{\alpha}
converges to
f
in
L^{2}
as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
,
k\geq 4
and
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, assume that
f_{\alpha}
and
f
are solutions in
C^{1}([0,\bar{T}];H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
as defined above. Then for all
t\in[0,\bar{T}]
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\|f_{\alpha}-f\|_{L^{2}}(t)=0.

(5.1)

Proof.

Throughout this proof,
C_{\bar{M}}
always denotes a positive constant that depends on
\bar{M}
given in (
2.10
) but is independent of
\alpha
.

Let
g_{\alpha}=f_{\alpha}(x,t)-f(x,t)
, then
g_{\alpha}
satisfies

\begin{split}\frac{\partial g_{\alpha}}{\partial t}(x,t)&=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla g_{\alpha}(x,t)-\nabla g_{\alpha}(x-y,t)\right)\cdot y}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy\\
&\qquad+\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t))\cdot y}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dy\\
&\qquad-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t))\cdot y}{A_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}}dy.\end{split}

(5.2)

Multiplying (
5.2
) by
g_{\alpha}
and integrating with respect to
x
,

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|g_{\alpha}(x,t)\right|^{2}dx&=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2g_{\alpha}(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(\nabla g_{\alpha}(x,t)-\nabla g_{\alpha}(x-y,t)\right)\cdot y}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}dydx\\
&\,\,\,+\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2g_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t))\cdot y\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}-\frac{1}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\\
&\,\,\,+\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2g_{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla f(x,t)-\nabla f(x-y,t))\cdot y\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{A_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\\
&\triangleq\mathbf{E}_{1}+\mathbf{E}_{2}+\mathbf{E}_{3}.\end{split}

We estimate the terms
\mathbf{E}_{1}
,
\mathbf{E}_{2}
and
\mathbf{E}_{3}
as follows. First of all, we rewrite
\mathbf{E}_{1}
as follows (we drop the variable
t
for simplicity):

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1}=\mathbf{E}_{1,1}+\mathbf{E}_{1,2}+\mathbf{E}_{1,3},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2g_{\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nabla g_{\alpha}(x)\cdot y}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,2}

\displaystyle=-\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2g_{\alpha}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{g_{\alpha}(x)-g_{\alpha}(y)}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,3}

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}2(3+\alpha)g_{\alpha}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(g_{\alpha}(x)-g_{\alpha}(y))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left[\frac{\left(f_{\alpha}(x)-f_{\alpha}(y)\right)\cdot\left(f_{\alpha}(x)-f_{\alpha}(y)-\nabla f_{\alpha}(y)\cdot(x-y)\right)}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}\right].

Upon integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(3+\alpha)|g_{\alpha}(x,t)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left(f_{\alpha}(x)-f_{\alpha}(x-y)\right)\left(\nabla f_{\alpha}(x)-\nabla f_{\alpha}(x-y)\right)\cdot y}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{5+\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq(3+\alpha)\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{1}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(3+\alpha)M_{\alpha}\left(f_{\alpha}\right)\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{1}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+4M_{\alpha}\left(f_{\alpha}\right)\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where
M_{\alpha}(\cdot)
is given by (
2.2
). Following the proof of Theorem
2.1
, Using the uniform bound (
2.10
), we readily have

\displaystyle 4\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{1}}+4M_{\alpha}(f_{\alpha})\leq C_{\bar{M}},

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,1}\leq C_{\bar{M}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, by making change of variables,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(g_{\alpha}(x)-g_{\alpha}(y))^{2}}{A_{0}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\leq 0,

while for
\mathbf{E}_{1,3}
, following the similar method for estimating
\mathbf{J}_{3}
as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1,3}\leq 8\left[\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{2,\delta}}^{4}+\left\|f_{\alpha}\right\|_{C^{1}}\|f\|_{C^{2,\delta}}+1\right]\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\bar{M}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{1}\leq C_{\bar{M}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.3)

Next for the term
\mathbf{E}_{2}
, using mean value theorem and the uniform bound (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}-\frac{1}{A_{\alpha}^{3/2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|-\frac{3+\alpha}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\right|\sup_{r\in(-\frac{3+\alpha}{2},-\frac{3}{2})}|A_{\alpha}^{r}||\log(A_{\alpha})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}|y|^{-3}\log(M^{2})=\alpha|y|^{-3}\log(M).

Therefore by applying the above estimate to
\mathbf{E}_{2}
, it implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{2}\leq\alpha C_{\bar{M}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.4)

For the term
\mathbf{E}_{3}
, using mean value theorem and the uniform bound (
2.10
), we also have

\displaystyle|A_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}-A_{\alpha}^{\frac{3}{2}}|\leq C_{\bar{M}}|g_{\alpha}(x)-g_{\alpha}(x-y)||y|^{2},

and hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{3}\leq C_{\bar{M}}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(5.5)

Combining (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 2C_{\bar{M}}\|g_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\alpha C_{\bar{M}}\|g_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}.

(5.6)

Applying Grönwall’s inequality to (
5.6
) and recalling that
g_{\alpha}(x,0)=0
, the results (
5.1
) follows immediately by taking
\alpha\to 0^{+}
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

For
1\leq k\leq 4
, by performing similar analysis on
\partial^{k}_{x_{1}}g_{\alpha}
and

(5.7)

Using Theorem
5.1
and Remark

, we can further obtain
L^{1}
-convergence provided that the initial interface satisfies the condition (
4.5
). The results are given in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

For
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
,
k\geq 4
and
f_{0}\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, assume that
f_{\alpha}
and
f
are solutions in
C^{1}([0,\bar{T}];H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
as defined above. If
f_{0}(x)\leq 0
or
f_{0}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then for all
t\in[0,\bar{T}]
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\|f_{\alpha}-f\|_{L^{1}}(t)=0.

(5.8)

Proof.

We give the proof for the case when
f_{0}\geq 0
, and the case for
f_{0}\leq 0
is just similar. By Theorem
4.2
and the assumption that
f_{0}\geq 0
, for all
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
f_{\alpha}\geq 0
and
f\geq 0
. Also from the proof of Theorem

, we know that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\partial f_{\alpha}}{\partial t}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)dx=0,

and hence

for all
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
and

.

By applying the Sobolev embedding theorem, together with the uniform bound (

), the convergence result (

which gives

Appendix A
Existence of
k_{0}(\alpha)

In this appendix, we show the existence of
k_{0}(\alpha)
which appears in Theorem
3.1
. To begin with, we give an estimate for the principal value of integral involving
\frac{\exp(irS)}{r^{1+\alpha}}
with
S\in\mathbb{R}
. More precisely, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. There exists a positive constant
C(\alpha)>0
which depends on
\alpha
but is independent of
\mathbf{S}
such that

\displaystyle\left|\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\exp(ir\mathbf{S})}{r|r|^{\alpha}}\right|\leq C(\alpha)|\mathbf{S}|^{\alpha}.

(A.1)

Proof.

Notice that since
\cos(r\mathbf{S})/{r|r|^{\alpha}}
is an odd function
r
while
\sin(r\mathbf{S})/{r|r|^{\alpha}}
is an even function in
r
, we readily have

\displaystyle\texttt{PV}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\exp(ir\mathbf{S})}{r|r|^{\alpha}}=2\texttt{PV}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r|r|^{\alpha}}=2\texttt{PV}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}.

Observe that the bound (
A.1
) holds trivially for
\mathbf{S}=0
, without loss of generality we assume that
\mathbf{S}\neq 0
. Now for any
\varepsilon>0
, we split the integral as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}dr+\int_{\varepsilon}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}dr.

(A.2)

For the first integral on the right side of (
A.2
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2|S|\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{dr}{r^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 2|S|\frac{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha},

and for the second integral on the right side of (
A.2
), we perform a change of variable
u=r\mathbf{S}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\varepsilon}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(r\mathbf{S})}{r^{\alpha+1}}dr\right|=\left|\int_{\varepsilon S}^{+\infty}\frac{\sin(u)}{u^{\alpha+1}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{S}^{-(1+\alpha)}\mathbf{S}}du\right|\leq|\mathbf{S}|^{\alpha}\frac{|\varepsilon\mathbf{S}|^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

We choose
\varepsilon=\mathbf{S}^{-1}
and
C(\alpha)=\frac{6}{1-\alpha}
, then the bound (
A.1
) follows.
∎

Next, we show that the constant

Lemma A.2

For any

(A.4)
