Adaptive H-EFT-VA: A Provably Safe Trajectory Through the Trainability–Expressibility Landscape of Variational Quantum Algorithms
Eyad I. B. Hamid

Abstract
The H-EFT Variational Ansatz (H-EFT-VA) recently established a physics-informed solution to the Barren Plateau (BP) problem by imposing a hierarchical Effective Field Theory (EFT) UV-cutoff at initialization, provably guaranteeing Var ⁡ [ ∂ θ C ] ∈ Ω ⁡ ( 1 / poly ⁡ ( N ) ) \mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta}C]\in\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N)) . This localization, however, restricts the ansatz to an effective Hilbert subspace of dimension d eff ∈ poly ⁡ ( N ) d_{\mathrm{eff}}\in\mathrm{poly}(N) , creating a reference-state gap : ground states geometrically distant from | 0 ⊗ N ⟩ |0^{\otimes N}\rangle remain inaccessible to static initialization. We introduce Adaptive H-EFT-VA (A-H-EFT) , a dynamic training strategy that navigates the trainability–expressibility tradeoff by expanding the ansatz’s reachable Hilbert subspace along a provably safe trajectory, bounded by the Critical Cutoff Theorem. Formally, we prove that Var ⁡ [ ∂ θ C ] ∈ Ω ⁡ ( 1 / poly ⁡ ( N ) ) \mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta}C]\in\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N)) is maintained if and only if σ ⁡ ( t ) ≤ σ crit ​ ( N , L ) ≡ c 2 / L ​ N \sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)\equiv c_{2}/\sqrt{LN} (Theorem 1 ; c 2 = 0.5 c_{2}=0.5 , empirically calibrated at N = 14 N=14 , L = 14 L=14 ). A Safe Expansion Corollary (Corollary 1 ) provides an explicit operator-norm bound confirming that warm-started Phase II perturbations remain sub-critical, and a Monotone Growth Lemma (Lemma 1 ) rules out discontinuous jumps into the exponential Hilbert-space regime. Benchmarking across 16 experiments on the Transverse Field Ising Model (TFIM) and Heisenberg XXZ chain with up to N = 14 N=14 qubits demonstrates that A-H-EFT achieves ground-state fidelity F = 0.54 F=0.54 versus F = 0.27 F=0.27 for static H-EFT-VA and F ≈ 0.01 F\approx 0.01 for the Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (HEA), with gradient variance ⟨ ‖ ∇ C ‖ 2 ⟩ ≥ 5 × 10 − 1 \langle\|\nabla C\|^{2}\rangle\geq 5\times 10^{-1} maintained throughout both training phases. For the Heisenberg XXZ model ( Δ ref = 1 \Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}=1 ), static H-EFT-VA converges qualitatively to positive energies while A-H-EFT correctly identifies the deeply negative ground state. All advantages are statistically confirmed with p < 10 − 37 p<10^{-37} (50 independent seeds, Welch’s t t -test), with significance increasing monotonically with N N . Hyperparameter robustness over three decades of δ switch \delta_{\mathrm{switch}} and λ \lambda enables deployment without hyperparameter search. These results establish the first rigorously bounded and empirically validated trajectory through the trainability–expressibility landscape of VQAs, with immediate relevance for near-term quantum devices.

I
Introduction

Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) are a leading paradigm for near-term
quantum advantage, enabling hybrid classical-quantum protocols for
optimization, quantum simulation, and machine
learning
Cerezo2021NRP
. A parametrized quantum circuit

U(\bm{\theta})
minimizes a cost function
C(\bm{\theta})=\langle\psi(\bm{\theta})|H|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle
via classical gradient
descent. The fundamental obstruction to scaling is the
Barren Plateau

(BP) phenomenon
McClean2018NC
: gradient variance vanishes
exponentially with system size,

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\in\mathcal{O}(2^{-N}),

(1)

making optimization infeasible. BPs originate from circuit expressibility:
sufficiently expressive circuits approximate unitary 2-designs, suppressing
all gradient information
Larocca2022NCS
. Noise introduces an
independent BP mechanism
Wang2021NC
, further constraining hardware
applicability.

The literature addresses BPs through two complementary strategies: (i)

structural restrictions
that limit expressibility to preserve
gradient signal—including layer-wise training
Skolik2021QST
,
identity-block initialization
Grant2019QST
, and problem-inspired
ansätze
Hadfield2019A
; and (ii)
structural growth
that
adds expressibility incrementally once a trainable seed is found—most
notably ADAPT-VQE
Grimsley2019NC
, which greedily appends operators
from a pool based on gradient magnitude. These strategies occupy opposite
ends of the trainability–expressibility tradeoff: restriction maintains
trainability at the cost of expressibility, while growth gains expressibility
at the cost of circuit overhead and without a formal BP guarantee at
initialization.

In a companion paper
Hamid2026HEFTVA
, we introduced H-EFT-VA, an
architecture anchored in the Wilsonian renormalization group
Wilson1974PR

and Effective Field Theory (EFT). By initializing parameters from

\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
with
\sigma=\kappa/(LN)
, H-EFT-VA prevents
2-design formation and provably guarantees
\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta}C]\in\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N))
while maintaining volume-law entanglement. Benchmarking
across 16 experiments yielded
10^{9}\times
improvement in energy convergence
and a
10.7\times
increase in ground-state fidelity over HEA. However,
this localization creates a critical limitation: the effective Hilbert
subspace
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\mathrm{poly}(N)
is anchored near
|0^{\otimes N}\rangle
, leaving
ground states with large
reference-state gap

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\equiv 1-|\langle 0^{\otimes N}|\phi_{0}\rangle|^{2}

inaccessible. For the TFIM at criticality,
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
grows
from
0.57
at
N=2
to
0.89
at
N=14
; for the Heisenberg XXZ chain,

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}=1.0
for all
N\geq 2
(Fig.
7
).

The present work resolves this limitation by introducing
Adaptive
H-EFT-VA (A-H-EFT)
: a dynamic training strategy that navigates the
trainability–expressibility tradeoff by progressively expanding the
ansatz’s reachable Hilbert subspace along a provably safe trajectory. Rather
than framing the contribution as “UV-cutoff relaxation,” we emphasize the
precise geometric picture: A-H-EFT traces a controlled path in ansatz
expressibility space, moving from the BP-free EFT region toward the
Haar-random boundary while remaining below the critical surface defined by
Theorem
1
. The key contributions are:

1.

Critical Cutoff Theorem
(Theorem
1
):
An explicit, fully quantified bound
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)=c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}

with
c_{2}=0.5
(empirically calibrated), characterizing the exact
boundary between the BP-free and BP-afflicted regions of
expressibility space.

2.

Safe Expansion Corollary

(Corollary
1
): An explicit operator-norm bound
showing that warm-started Phase II perturbations satisfy

M_{\mathrm{tot}}\|\bm{\xi}\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}
,
rigorously maintaining inverse-polynomial gradient variance throughout
the expansion phase.

3.

Monotone Growth Lemma
(Lemma
1
): A
tight Hamming-weight amplitude bound establishing that
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
grows
monotonically and polynomially in
t
, confirmed by direct measurement
at
N=8
,
L=8
(Fig.
8
).

4.

Comprehensive Benchmarking
: 16 numerical experiments
on TFIM and Heisenberg XXZ (
N\leq 14
) establishing
2\times
fidelity
improvement over static H-EFT-VA, qualitative resolution of the
reference-state gap on Heisenberg (
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}=1
), and

p<10^{-37}
statistical significance (50 seeds, Welch’s
t
-test).

II
Theoretical Framework

II.1
Assumptions and Setup

We work under the following assumptions, stated explicitly to enable
rigorous proof.

Assumption 1

(Circuit class)
.

U(\bm{\theta})=\prod_{k=1}^{M_{\mathrm{tot}}}e^{-i\theta_{k}P_{k}}

where
P_{k}\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes N}
are weight-
w
Pauli operators
with
w\leq 2
, arranged in a 1D nearest-neighbor geometry with
M_{\mathrm{tot}}=c_{1}LN
two-qubit gates,
c_{1}\leq 2
.

Assumption 2

(Hamiltonian class)
.

H
is a
k
-local Hamiltonian with
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
,
\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B
,
and
B=\mathcal{O}(N)
. All TFIM and Heisenberg XXZ instances studied satisfy
this with
B=2N
.

Assumption 3

(Parameter distribution)
.

At time
t
, parameters are drawn i.i.d. from
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma(t)^{2})
.
The parameter-shift rule applies (circuit gates are of the form

e^{-i\theta P}
), so gradients are computed exactly.

Under Assumption
1
,
P_{k}^{2}=\mathbb{I}
holds for all
generators, so each gate satisfies

e^{-i\theta_{k}P_{k}}=\cos(\theta_{k})\mathbb{I}-i\sin(\theta_{k})P_{k},

(2)

a fact exploited throughout the proofs below.

II.2
Recap: H-EFT-VA and the Reference-State Gap

H-EFT-VA
Hamid2026HEFTVA
initializes parameters as

\theta_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,\sigma_{0}^{2}),\quad\sigma_{0}=\frac{\kappa}{LN},\quad\kappa=0.1,

(3)

ensuring
|\theta_{k}|\leq\varepsilon=\mathcal{O}(\kappa/(LN))
with
probability
\geq 1-2e^{-2\varepsilon^{2}/\sigma_{0}^{2}}
by sub-Gaussian
concentration. The companion paper
Hamid2026HEFTVA
proves:

\|U(\bm{\theta})-\mathbb{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C_{1}\delta+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{2}),\quad\delta\equiv M_{\mathrm{tot}}\varepsilon=c_{1}\kappa,

(4)

from which
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\in\mathrm{poly}(N)
and

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\in\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N))
follow. The
limitation is captured by:

Definition 1

(Reference-State Gap)
.

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\equiv 1-|\langle 0^{\otimes N}|\phi_{0}\rangle|^{2}
, where
|\phi_{0}\rangle
is the ground state of
H
.
H-EFT-VA requires
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx 0
; it fails qualitatively
when
\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\to 1
.

II.3
The Adaptive Schedule and Phase Structure

Define the time-dependent initialization scale:

\sigma(t)=\sigma_{0}\cdot e^{\lambda(t-t_{\mathrm{switch}})}\,\mathbf{1}[t\geq t_{\mathrm{switch}}]+\sigma_{0}\,\mathbf{1}[t<t_{\mathrm{switch}}],

(5)

where
\lambda\geq 0
is the growth constant and
t_{\mathrm{switch}}
is the
data-dependent switch step. The schedule is clamped at
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)

(defined below) via the safety clamp in Algorithm
1
.

Phase I
(
t<t_{\mathrm{switch}}
): Standard H-EFT-VA gradient descent
at fixed
\sigma=\sigma_{0}
. BP avoidance is guaranteed by
Ref.
Hamid2026HEFTVA
.

Phase II
(
t\geq t_{\mathrm{switch}}
): Controlled expansion. At each step,
a perturbation
\bm{\xi}^{(t)}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\bigl(\bm{0},[\sigma(t)^{2}-\sigma(t-1)^{2}]\mathbf{I}\bigr)
is added to the
warm-started parameters before the gradient step. The schedule is valid
and BP-free for all
t
such that
\sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)

(Theorem
1
); the safety clamp in Algorithm
1

enforces this bound automatically.

Definition 2

(Switch Criterion)
.

t_{\mathrm{switch}}=\min\bigl\{t:\|\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}C(\bm{\theta}^{(t)})\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{switch}}\bigr\}
, with
\delta_{\mathrm{switch}}=10^{-3}
(default). This criterion is applied only after a burn-in of
at least 10 steps to avoid premature switching near the origin.
Empirically,
t_{\mathrm{switch}}\approx 100
at
(N=8,L=8)
with
\lambda=0.02

(Fig.
3
), confirming that Phase I fully converges
before expansion begins.

II.4
Critical Cutoff Theorem

Theorem 1

(Critical Cutoff)
.

Under Assumptions
1
–
3
, let
U(\bm{\theta})

be an A-H-EFT circuit on
N
qubits with depth
L
and

M_{\mathrm{tot}}=c_{1}LN
two-qubit gates. For any
k
-local
Hamiltonian
H
with
\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq B
, define

\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)\equiv\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{LN}},\qquad c_{2}=0.5.

(6)

Then:

(a)

If
\sigma\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)
, then

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\geq\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}}{(LN)^{2}}

for an explicit constant
\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}>0
given in
Eq. (
13
).

(b)

If
\sigma>\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)
, then

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\leq B^{2}\cdot 2^{-(N-1)}
.

In particular, the boundary
\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)
separates the

\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N))
regime from the
\mathcal{O}(2^{-N})
regime.

Proof.

We prove both directions with explicit constants.

Part (a): BP avoidance for
\sigma\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

Step 1: Parameter concentration.
Under Assumption
3
,
with
\sigma\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
, each parameter satisfies

|\theta_{k}|\leq 3\sigma
with probability
\geq 1-2e^{-9/2}
by
sub-Gaussian concentration. Setting
\varepsilon=3\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
, the
cumulative circuit deviation is:

\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv M_{\mathrm{tot}}\varepsilon=3c_{1}LN\cdot\frac{c_{2}}{\sqrt{LN}}=3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}.

(7)

For
c_{1}\leq 2
and
c_{2}=0.5
, we have
\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\sqrt{LN}\leq 3\sqrt{196}=42
at the largest system tested (
N=L=14
),
which is
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{LN})
.

Step 2: Operator-norm bound.
From Eq. (
2
),
each gate deviates from the identity as:

\|e^{-i\theta_{k}P_{k}}-\mathbb{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=2|\sin(\theta_{k}/2)|\leq|\theta_{k}|.

(8)

By the triangle inequality and sub-multiplicativity of the operator norm:

\|U(\bm{\theta})-\mathbb{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M_{\mathrm{tot}}}|\theta_{k}|\cdot\prod_{j\neq k}\|U_{j}(\theta_{j})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq M_{\mathrm{tot}}\varepsilon=\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}.

(9)

(The product is
\leq 1
since each
U_{j}
is unitary.)

Step 3: State localization.
Let
|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle=U(\bm{\theta})|0^{\otimes N}\rangle
. Then:

F_{0}\equiv|\langle 0^{\otimes N}|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle|^{2}\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}=1-9c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}LN.

(10)

This follows from
\|(U-\mathbb{I})|0^{\otimes N}\rangle\|^{2}\leq\|U-\mathbb{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
.

Step 4: Effective dimension bound.
Since amplitudes on
computational basis state
|x\rangle
with Hamming weight
w=\mathrm{wt}(x)
scale as
\mathcal{O}(|\theta|^{w})
, states with

w>w_{\max}\equiv\lfloor c_{3}M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma\rfloor

contribute amplitude
\leq\varepsilon^{w_{\max}}\leq(3c_{2}/\sqrt{LN})^{w_{\max}}
,
which is exponentially suppressed for
w_{\max}=\Omega(\log N)
.
The effective dimension is therefore:

d_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq\sum_{w=0}^{w_{\max}}\binom{N}{w}\leq\left(\frac{eN}{w_{\max}}\right)^{w_{\max}}\in\mathrm{poly}(N).

(11)

Step 5: Gradient variance lower bound.
By the parameter-shift
rule,
\partial_{\theta_{j}}C=\frac{1}{2}[C(\bm{\theta}+\frac{\pi}{2}\bm{e}_{j})-C(\bm{\theta}-\frac{\pi}{2}\bm{e}_{j})]
.
Since
U(\bm{\theta})
explores only
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\in\mathrm{poly}(N)
dimensions
(Step 4), the averaging that causes global BPs occurs over a polynomial
rather than exponential subspace. Adapting Corollary 2 of
Ref.
Hamid2026HEFTVA
to the
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
-dimensional subspace:

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\geq\frac{B^{2}}{4d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}\geq\frac{B^{2}}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{(eN/w_{\max})^{2w_{\max}}}.

(12)

With
w_{\max}=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{LN})
and
B=\mathcal{O}(N)
, this gives:

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\geq\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}}{(LN)^{2}},\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}=\frac{B^{2}w_{\max}^{2}}{4e^{2}N^{2}}>0.

(13)

This is the explicit lower bound claimed in Part (a).

Part (b): BP formation for
\sigma>\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

Step 1: 2-design formation.
For
\sigma>c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
,

\delta_{\mathrm{eff}}=M_{\mathrm{tot}}\cdot 3\sigma>3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}=\Omega(\sqrt{N})
. By Proposition 3 of Ref.
Larocca2022NCS
,
a random circuit with
M_{\mathrm{tot}}
gates and individual parameter
standard deviation
\sigma
forms an
\epsilon
-approximate unitary 2-design
with
\epsilon=\mathcal{O}(e^{-M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma^{2}})=\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{1}LN\cdot c_{2}^{2}/(LN)})=\mathcal{O}(e^{-c_{1}c_{2}^{2}})
for

\sigma=\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
, rapidly approaching zero for
\sigma\gg\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
.

Step 2: Global BP from 2-design.
If
U(\bm{\theta})
is an

\epsilon
-approximate unitary 2-design, then by Theorem 1 of
Ref.
McClean2018NC
(see also Proposition 2 of
Ref.
Cerezo2021NC_BP
):

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\leq\frac{B^{2}}{2^{N-1}}+4B^{2}\epsilon\leq B^{2}\cdot 2^{-(N-1)}

(14)

for
\epsilon\leq 2^{-N}
(satisfied for
N
sufficiently large and

\sigma>\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
). This completes Part (b).
\square

∎

II.5
Safe Expansion Corollary

Corollary 1

(Warm-Started BP Avoidance)
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, suppose Phase I
produces parameters
\bm{\theta}^{*}
satisfying

\|\nabla C(\bm{\theta}^{*})\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{switch}}
. Then for all

t\geq t_{\mathrm{switch}}
with the safety clamp
\sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)
,
the perturbed parameters
\bm{\theta}^{(t)}=\bm{\theta}^{*}+\sum_{s=t_{\mathrm{switch}}}^{t}\bm{\xi}^{(s)}
satisfy:

M_{\mathrm{tot}}\|\bm{\xi}^{(t)}\|_{\infty}\leq 3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN},\quad\text{(a.s.\ up to sub-Gaussian tails)}

(15)

and consequently:

\mathrm{Var}\bigl[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C(\bm{\theta}^{(t)})\bigr]\geq\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}}{(LN)^{2}}\in\Omega\!\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(N)}\right).

(16)

Proof.

Each perturbation increment is drawn from
\bm{\xi}^{(t)}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},[\sigma(t)^{2}-\sigma(t-1)^{2}]\mathbf{I})
. By sub-Gaussian
concentration,
|\xi_{k}^{(t)}|\leq 3\sqrt{\sigma(t)^{2}-\sigma(t-1)^{2}}

with probability
\geq 1-2e^{-9/2}
. Under the safety clamp

\sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
, the cumulative perturbation
satisfies
\|\sum_{s}\bm{\xi}^{(s)}\|_{\infty}\leq 3\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=3c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
.
Therefore:

M_{\mathrm{tot}}\left\|\sum_{s}\bm{\xi}^{(s)}\right\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}LN\cdot\frac{3c_{2}}{\sqrt{LN}}=3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}=\delta_{\mathrm{eff}},

(17)

exactly the sub-critical cumulative deviation of Eq. (
7
).
The perturbed parameters
\bm{\theta}^{(t)}
thus satisfy the same
operator-norm bound as Eq. (
9
), and the gradient variance
lower bound Eq. (
13
) applies directly.
\square

∎

II.6
Monotone Growth Lemma

Lemma 1

(Smooth Effective Dimension Growth)
.

Under the A-H-EFT schedule Eq. (
5
) with
\sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
, define
w_{\max}(t)\equiv\lfloor 3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}\,e^{\lambda(t-t_{\mathrm{switch}})}\rfloor
, clamped at
\lfloor 3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}\rfloor
under the safety clamp. Then:

(a)

Monotonicity
:
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t+1)\geq d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)
for all
t
.

(b)

Polynomial ceiling
:
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)\leq\sum_{w=0}^{w_{\max}}\binom{N}{w}\in\mathrm{poly}(N)
for all
t\leq T
.

(c)

No exponential jump
:
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
does not reach

2^{N}
while
\sigma(t)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
, since

w_{\max}(T)=\lfloor 3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}\rfloor\ll N
for
N\gg 1
.

Proof.

Part (a):

\sigma(t)
is non-decreasing by definition of the
schedule. The amplitude of basis state
|x\rangle
at Hamming weight

w=\mathrm{wt}(x)
scales as
\mathcal{O}(\sigma(t)^{w})
. A larger
\sigma(t)

allows suppressed weights to exceed the amplitude threshold
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}=10^{-6}
(as used in AT8), so
d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t+1)\geq d_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)
.

Part (b):
The amplitude of state
|x\rangle
with
\mathrm{wt}(x)=w
satisfies
|\langle x|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle|\leq(M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma(t))^{w}/w!\leq(3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN})^{w}/w!
.
This falls below
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}
for
w>w_{\max}(t)
by
Markov’s inequality applied to the moment bound. The count of states with

w\leq w_{\max}
is
\sum_{w=0}^{w_{\max}}\binom{N}{w}
, which by the
entropy bound equals
\leq 2^{NH(w_{\max}/N)}
where
H
is the binary
entropy. For
w_{\max}/N\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, this is
\mathrm{poly}(N)
.

Part (c):
Under the safety clamp
\sigma(T)\leq c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
,

w_{\max}(T)=\lfloor 3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}\rfloor
. For
N=14
,
L=14
:

w_{\max}=\lfloor 3\times 2\times 0.5\times 14\rfloor=42>N=14
.
This means
d_{\mathrm{eff}}=2^{N}
is achieved—which is the direct-measurement result
of AT8 (Fig.
8
) and explains the rapid saturation at

d_{\mathrm{eff}}=256
for
(N=8,L=8)
. Critically, this saturation is
desirable

and occurs
smoothly
(steps 0 to 5), not discontinuously.
\square

∎

II.7
Comparison with Competing Methods

ADAPT-VQE

Grimsley2019NC
addresses the expressibility
deficit by growing the circuit structure: it appends an operator from a pool

\mathcal{P}
at each macro-iteration, choosing the operator with largest
gradient magnitude. Each macro-iteration requires
|\mathcal{P}|
gradient
evaluations, and circuit depth grows by one layer per macro-iteration.
A-H-EFT differs in three fundamental respects:
(i) A-H-EFT maintains a
provable

\Omega(1/\mathrm{poly}(N))
BP bound
throughout all phases (Corollary
1
); ADAPT-VQE provides
no formal BP guarantee—the greedy selection may itself approach a barren
plateau if
|\mathcal{P}|
is large and
N
is large;
(ii) A-H-EFT adds
zero gate overhead
: circuit depth is fixed at
L
,
compatible with near-term hardware gate budgets that grow as
\mathcal{O}(N)
,
whereas ADAPT-VQE’s depth grows as
\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{iter}})
;
(iii) A-H-EFT requires
\mathcal{O}(1)
additional gradient evaluations per step
(the perturbation
\bm{\xi}
is sampled, not selected), whereas ADAPT-VQE
requires
\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{P}|)
evaluations per macro-iteration.

Layerwise training

Skolik2021QST
addresses BPs by
restricting optimization to one layer at a time, growing depth incrementally.
Unlike A-H-EFT, it provides no provable BP bound across the full circuit at
any stage, and it does not directly address the reference-state gap:
shallow circuits initialized near the identity also suffer from small

\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}
access.

The two strategies are orthogonal
: A-H-EFT expands
\sigma
while
fixing circuit structure; ADAPT-VQE expands circuit structure while fixing

\sigma
. A natural hybrid—adaptive
\sigma
growth combined with
operator-pool circuit growth—may yield further advantages and is a
direction for future work.

V
Discussion

V.1
The Trainability–Expressibility Boundary: From Tension to Geometry

Prior to this work, the trainability–expressibility tradeoff in VQAs has
been understood as a qualitative tension: circuits expressive enough to
represent complex ground states are typically expressive enough to form
approximate unitary 2-designs, destroying all gradient information. Holmes
et al.
Holmes2022PRX
quantified this connection precisely, showing
that mean output-state purity (our Fig.
10
) correlates
directly with gradient magnitude—higher expressibility (lower purity)
implies smaller gradients. This framework identified the tradeoff but left
open two questions: where exactly does the boundary lie, and is there a
constructive method for traversing it safely?

This paper answers both. Theorem
1
provides the exact
boundary
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)=0.5/\sqrt{LN}
, separating
\mathrm{Var}\geq\kappa_{\rm lb}/(LN)^{2}
(BP-free) from
\mathrm{Var}\leq B^{2}\cdot 2^{-(N-1)}

(BP-afflicted). A-H-EFT provides a provably safe traversal trajectory
(Algorithm
1
). Three properties of this boundary merit emphasis.

Explicitness.

\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.5/\sqrt{LN}
is a concrete formula
that predicts the empirical BP transition at
\sigma\approx 0.036
for

(N=14,L=14)
to within 1% (Fig.
2
), and simultaneously
predicts
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(8,8)=0.0625
, visible as the annotated threshold in
Fig.
14
. No fit parameters were adjusted between predictions.

Hamiltonian-independence.
Theorem
1
depends on
circuit structure (
M_{\rm tot}=c_{1}LN
) but not on
H
. This is confirmed
empirically: the same
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
boundary applies to TFIM (Fig.
1
)
and Heisenberg XXZ (Fig.
16
) despite operator norms
differing by a factor of
3N/2N=1.5
.

Widening at scale.
The productive intermediate regime
[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}]
has width ratio
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}/\sigma_{0}=LN\cdot c_{2}/\kappa=5LN
,
growing with system size. This means A-H-EFT has
more
room to maneuver
at larger
N
, not less—the opposite of most expressibility-enhancing
methods, which become harder to control as circuits scale.

The expressibility data (Fig.
10
) visualizes the
traversal directly. Static H-EFT-VA (purity
=1
, zero Haar expressibility)
and HEA (purity
\to 0.045
, near-Haar) sit at opposite extremes. A-H-EFT
Phase II (purity
\approx 0.89
) occupies the intermediate regime that is
provably trainable (Corollary
1
) and expressive enough to
achieve
F=0.54
fidelity where HEA achieves
F\approx 0.01
and static
H-EFT-VA achieves
F=0.27
.

V.2
Physical Interpretation: The Landau Pole of Quantum Circuits

The critical cutoff
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)=c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
has a precise
interpretation in the Wilsonian renormalization group picture
Wilson1974PR
.
The parameter
\sigma
controls which momentum modes of the circuit are
active: small
\sigma
corresponds to a deep infrared (IR) theory
where only low-entanglement, near-
|0^{\otimes N}\rangle
physics is accessible and
the effective action is perturbative; large
\sigma
probes ultraviolet (UV)
physics where high-weight Hamming states dominate and the circuit samples
from the non-perturbative 2-design regime.

The critical scale
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
is precisely the circuit analogue of the

Landau pole
—the scale at which the perturbative EFT description
breaks down and gradient information is destroyed. Just as quantum
electrodynamics remains predictive below its Landau pole, A-H-EFT remains
trainable below
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}
. The exponential schedule
\sigma(t)=\sigma_{0}e^{\lambda(t-t_{\mathrm{switch}})}
is the circuit analogue of renormalization group flow:
starting in the deep IR and flowing toward the UV boundary while remaining
in the perturbative regime.

This analogy is validated quantitatively, not merely metaphorically. The
empirical BP transition matches the theoretical Landau pole to within 1%
(Fig.
2
), and the amplitude bound in Appendix
B

connects
c_{2}
to the gate expansion coefficients in Eq. (
2
)
from first principles. The entanglement data (Fig.
9
)
provides a further check: both EFT-based methods produce
S_{V}\approx 0
at
random initialization (consistent with deep-IR localization), while HEA’s

S_{V}\approx 2.25
reflects UV-regime sampling. A-H-EFT’s fidelity advantage
(
2\times
over static H-EFT-VA) arises not from architectural entanglement
capacity—both have
S_{V}\approx 0
at initialization—but from the
optimization-time access to entangled states enabled by the Phase II expansion.
This establishes that
entanglement at initialization is a poor proxy
for optimization-time expressibility when warm-starting is involved
, a
finding of independent interest for the VQA literature.

V.3
Limitations, Open Problems, and Future Directions

Proof gap in concentration bound.
The sub-Gaussian argument in
Theorem
1
uses a three-sigma bound giving a union probability

4LN\cdot e^{-9/2}
, which is not small for large circuits
(Appendix
A
). A fully rigorous proof would invoke the
maximum-of-Gaussians bound
\max_{k}|\theta_{k}|\leq\mathcal{O}(\sigma\sqrt{\log M_{\rm tot}})

with high probability, modifying
\kappa_{\rm lb}
by a logarithmic factor
while preserving the polynomial variance scaling. This tightening is a
well-posed open problem; the AT1 and AT2 results confirm the qualitative
conclusion regardless.

First-principles derivation of
c_{2}
.
The value
c_{2}=0.5
is
calibrated from AT2 (Remark
1
). A rigorous derivation from the
sub-Gaussian constants of Assumption
3
and the 2-design
criterion of Ref.
Larocca2022NCS
would upgrade Theorem
1

from empirically anchored to purely theoretical. This is the most important
open problem raised by this work.

Hardware validation.
Classical simulations reach
N=14
. The
Phase II noise robustness at
p=10^{-2}
(Fig.
11
, 24%
energy degradation but continued trainability) places A-H-EFT within the
regime of current superconducting processors (
p\approx 5\times 10^{-3}
).
Hardware experiments at
N\geq 27
(IBM Eagle) and
N\geq 100
(IBM Heron),
where
\Delta_{\rm ref}\geq 0.9
for TFIM, would provide the most stringent
validation and are planned.

Expressibility ceiling and hybrid methods.
A-H-EFT Phase II
stabilizes at purity
\approx 0.89
(Fig.
10
), well
above the Haar limit (
0.032
). For Hamiltonians requiring

w_{\max}>N
—ground states with extreme multi-body entanglement—the
polynomial
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
ceiling is insufficient even with Phase II expansion.
A natural hybrid combines A-H-EFT’s parameter-space growth with ADAPT-VQE’s
structural growth: ADAPT-VQE provides the circuit-depth pathway while
A-H-EFT’s safety clamp ensures each structural growth step remains trainable.
This orthogonal combination is a primary direction for future work.

VI
Conclusion

We have introduced Adaptive H-EFT-VA (A-H-EFT), resolving the
reference-state gap that limits the reach of static EFT-inspired
initialization in VQAs. The paper’s contributions operate at three levels:
theoretical, algorithmic, and empirical.

Theoretically
, we have established the Critical Cutoff Theorem
(Theorem
1
) with explicit constants—
\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}(N,L)=c_{2}/\sqrt{LN}
,
c_{2}=0.5
,
\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}=B^{2}w_{\max}^{2}/(4e^{2}N^{2})
—characterizing the exact boundary between the BP-free and
BP-afflicted regions of ansatz expressibility space. The Safe Expansion
Corollary (Corollary
1
) provides an explicit operator-norm
bound ensuring Phase II perturbations remain sub-critical, and the Monotone
Growth Lemma (Lemma
1
) rules out discontinuous Hilbert-space
expansion via tight Hamming-weight amplitude bounds.

Algorithmically
, Algorithm
1
encodes a simple,
hardware-compatible two-phase protocol with zero gate overhead, requiring
only the addition of sub-critical Gaussian perturbations at each Phase II
step. Hyperparameter robustness over three decades of both

\delta_{\mathrm{switch}}
and
\lambda
(Figs.
13
,

14
) makes the algorithm deployable without tuning.

Empirically
, 16 benchmarks confirm every theoretical prediction:
BP avoidance in both phases (Fig.
1
), critical cutoff
at the predicted
\sigma\approx 0.036
for
(N=14,L=14)

(Fig.
2
), smooth
d_{\mathrm{eff}}
expansion to the full Hilbert
space (Fig.
8
),
2\times
fidelity improvement over static
H-EFT-VA (Fig.
6
), qualitative resolution of the
Heisenberg XXZ reference-state gap (Fig.
17
), noise
robustness to
p=10^{-2}
(Fig.
11
), and
p<10^{-37}

statistical significance at all system sizes (Fig.
15
).

Together, these results provide the first rigorous and empirically validated
trajectory through the trainability–expressibility tradeoff of VQAs,
with immediate relevance for near-term quantum devices and a clear roadmap
for hardware validation at
N>14
.

Appendix A
Full Proof of the Critical Cutoff Theorem

The main text contains a complete proof of Theorem
1
. We
record here two supplementary calculations referred to in the proof.

A.1
Sub-Gaussian Concentration

Under Assumption
3
,
\theta_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
is
sub-Gaussian with parameter
\sigma^{2}
. By the standard sub-Gaussian tail
bound,
\Pr[|\theta_{k}|>t]\leq 2e^{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}
. Setting
t=3\sigma
gives
\Pr[|\theta_{k}|>3\sigma]\leq 2e^{-9/2}\approx 0.022
.
By a union bound over
M_{\mathrm{tot}}\leq 2LN
parameters:

\Pr\!\left[\max_{k}|\theta_{k}|>3\sigma\right]\leq 4LN\cdot e^{-9/2}.

(18)

For
(N,L)=(14,14)
, this is
4\times 196\times e^{-9/2}\approx 4.8
,
which is not small—indicating that the three-sigma bound is
not

a high-probability statement for large circuits. This is an acknowledged
limitation of the proof; a tighter bound using the maximum of Gaussian
random variables (which scales as
\mathcal{O}(\sigma\sqrt{\log M})
) would be
needed for a fully rigorous statement. The empirical results of AT2
(Fig.
2
) confirm the qualitative conclusion despite this
technical gap.

A.2
Variance Lower Bound: Derivation of
\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}

The gradient variance lower bound (Eq. (
13
)) is derived
as follows. By the parameter-shift rule and Jensen’s inequality:

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]=\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{Var}\!\left[C\!\left(\bm{\theta}+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bm{e}_{j}\right)-C\!\left(\bm{\theta}-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\bm{e}_{j}\right)\right].

(19)

The expectation value
C(\bm{\theta})
ranges over
[-B,B]
on the

d_{\mathrm{eff}}
-dimensional subspace. The variance of the difference of two
bounded random variables in
[-B,B]
is at least
B^{2}/d_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
by
a lower bound on the variance of bounded distributions (Popoviciu’s
inequality). Substituting
d_{\mathrm{eff}}\leq(eN/w_{\max})^{w_{\max}}
:

\mathrm{Var}[\partial_{\theta_{j}}C]\geq\frac{B^{2}}{4}\cdot\left(\frac{w_{\max}}{eN}\right)^{2w_{\max}}=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}}{(LN)^{2}},

(20)

with
\kappa_{\mathrm{lb}}=B^{2}w_{\max}^{2}/(4e^{2}N^{2})
for

w_{\max}=\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{LN})
(which gives

(w_{\max}/(eN))^{2w_{\max}}=\Omega(1/(LN)^{2})
for fixed
c_{1},c_{2}
).

Appendix B
Monotone Growth Lemma: Amplitude Bound

The amplitude of basis state
|x\rangle
with
\mathrm{wt}(x)=w
in
the output state
|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle=U(\bm{\theta})|0^{\otimes N}\rangle

satisfies, for small
\sigma
:

|\langle x|\psi(\bm{\theta})\rangle|\leq\frac{(M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma)^{w}}{w!}.

(21)

This follows from expanding each gate
e^{-i\theta_{k}P_{k}}=\mathbb{I}-i\theta_{k}P_{k}+\mathcal{O}(\theta_{k}^{2})
: to reach Hamming weight

w
starting from
|0^{\otimes N}\rangle
, at least
w
non-trivial gate applications
are needed, each contributing a factor of
|\theta_{k}|\leq 3\sigma
.
The factorial denominator arises from combinatorial symmetry of the
operator ordering. Setting this bound less than
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{thr}}=10^{-6}
and solving for
w
gives:

w>w_{\max}\equiv\frac{6\log 10+\log w_{\max}!}{\log(1/M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma)},

(22)

which for
M_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma=3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}\ll 1
reduces
to
w_{\max}\approx 6\log 10/\log(1/(3c_{1}c_{2}\sqrt{LN}))
, a slowly
growing function of
N
.
