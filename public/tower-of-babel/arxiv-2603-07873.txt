Graded Ehrhart Theory of Unimodular Zonotopes
Colin Crowley and Ethan Partida

Abstract
. Graded Ehrhart theory is a new q q -analogue of Ehrhart theory based on the orbit harmonics method. We study the graded Ehrhart theory of unimodular zonotopes from a matroid-theoretic perspective. Generalizing a result of Stanley (1991), we prove that the graded lattice point count of a unimodular zonotope is a q-evaluation of its Tutte polynomial. We conclude that the graded Ehrhart series of a unimodular zonotope is rational and obeys graded Ehrhart–Macdonald reciprocity. In an algebraic direction, we prove that the harmonic algebra of a unimodular zonotope is a coordinate ring of its associated arrangement Schubert variety. Using the geometry of arrangement Schubert varieties, we prove that the harmonic algebra of a unimodular zonotope is finitely generated and Cohen–Macaulay. We also give an explicit presentation of the harmonic algebra of a unimodular zonotope in terms of generators and relations. We conclude by classifying which unimodular zonotopes have Gorenstein harmonic algebras. Our work answers, in the special case of unimodular zonotopes, two conjectures of Reiner and Rhoades (2024).



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the combinatorial, algebraic and geometric aspects of the graded Ehrhart theory of unimodular zonotopes. Graded Ehrhart theory is a new
q
-analogue of Ehrhart theory introduced by Reiner and Rhoades
[
RR24
]
. For every lattice polytope
P
and positive integer
m
, there is a canonical pair of polynomials
i_{P}(m;q)
and
\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)
in
\mathbb{Z}[q]
that have non-negative coefficients summing to the number of lattice points and interior lattice points in
mP
, respectively. These polynomials are constructed via the
orbit harmonics
method. The orbit harmonics method is a combinatorial degeneration that turns a finite set of points
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq k^{n}
into a graded
k
-algebra
\text{Orb}(\mathcal{Z})
whose total dimension is equal to
|\mathcal{Z}|
. When the finite set of points
\mathcal{Z}
is combinatorially interesting, the dimensions of the graded pieces of
\text{Orb}(\mathcal{Z})
often have an interesting combinatorial interpretation. For example, if
\mathcal{Z}
is the set of vertices of the permutahedron, then the ring
\text{Orb}(\mathcal{Z})
is the coinvariant ring of the symmetric group
[
HRS18
, Section 4.1]
. The polynomials
i_{P}(m;q)
and
\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)
record the dimensions of the graded pieces of
\text{Orb}(mP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\text{Orb}(\text{int}(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, respectively.

A compelling feature of Ehrhart theory and, indeed, our present work, is its broad connections to combinatorics, commutative algebra and algebraic geometry. Classical Ehrhart theory is closely related to the enumerative combinatorics of integer-valued polynomials, the commutative algebra of semigroup rings, and the algebraic geometry of toric varieties. In our study of the graded Ehrhart theory of unimodular zonotopes, we draw from, and prove new facts about: the enumerative combinatorics of quantum integer-valued polynomials
[
HH17
]
, the commutative algebra of zonotopal and harmonic algebras
[
HR11
,
RR24
]
, and the algebraic geometry of arrangement Schubert varieties
[
AB16
,
Li18
]
.

The classical Ehrhart theory of unimodular zonotopes is studied in the works of Stanley, Beck–Jochemko–McCullough, and Backman–Baker–Yuen
[
Sta91
,
BJM19
,
BBY19
]
. They show that, among many other things, the Ehrhart theory of a unimodular zonotope
Z
is dictated by its associated matroid
M
. Our results show that
M
also dictates the graded Ehrhart theory of
Z
.

We now summarize our results. As our work draws on many different
mathematical areas, we have separated our results into two categories:
enumerative and algebraic. We delay our background until

Section

3
in order to highlight our results with a large
worked example in
Section

2
. We expand upon the enumerative and

4
and

5
, respectively. We close by classifying which

Section

6
.
Section

3.
Background

3.1.
Graded Ehrhart theory

Recently, Reiner and Rhoades have introduced a new
q
-analogue of Ehrhart theory
[
RR24
]
. For every lattice polytope
P
and non-negative integer
m
, they construct polynomials
i_{P}(m;q)
and
\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)
in
\mathbb{Z}[q]
with non-negative coefficients such that, in the
q
equal to one limit,
i_{P}(m;1)=|mP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
and
\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;1)=|\text{int}(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
where
\text{int}(mP)
is the interior of the
m
th dilate of
P
. Reiner and Rhoades construct these polynomials using the methods of
orbit harmonics
. The orbit harmonics ring
\text{Orb}({\mathcal{Z}})
of a finite subset
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is the ring

\text{Orb}({\mathcal{Z}})=\frac{\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}]}{\gr I(\mathcal{Z})}

where
\gr I(\mathcal{Z})
is the ideal generated by all of the top degree homogeneous components
f_{k}
of polynomials
f=f_{k}+f_{k-1}+\ldots+f_{0}
vanishing on
\mathcal{Z}
. The ring
\text{Orb}({\mathcal{Z}})
is graded by degree and has total dimension equal to the cardinality of
\mathcal{Z}
. In this paper our loci
\mathcal{Z}
will be the lattice point of real polytopes. We will view
\mathcal{Z}
as a point locus in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
via the canonical inclusion
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

For a
\mathbb{N}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}R_{\alpha}
, the Hilbert series of
R
is the generating function
\Hilb(R;q_{1},\ldots,q_{k})\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(R_{\alpha})q_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots q_{k}^{\alpha_{k}}
. If
R
is finite dimensional as a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, then
\Hilb(R;q_{1},\ldots,q_{k})
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The graded lattice point counts
i_{P}(m;q)
and
\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)
are the Hilbert series

i_{P}(m;q)\coloneqq\Hilb(\text{Orb}(mP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d});q)\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)\coloneqq\Hilb(\text{Orb}(\text{int}(mP)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d});q).

The graded Ehrhart series
E_{P}(m;q)
and
\widetilde{E_{P}}(m;q)
are the bivariate generating functions

E_{P}(t,q)=\sum_{m\geq 0}i_{P}(m;q)t^{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{E_{P}}(t,q)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)t^{m}\,.

There is a bigraded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
and a homogeneous ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{P}
whose bigraded Hilbert series are the graded Ehrhart series
E_{P}(t,q)
and
\widetilde{E_{P}}(t,q)
, respectively. The algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is called the
harmonic algebra
of
P
and the ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}}
is called the
interior ideal
of
P
. We now work towards defining these objects. First, we recall the definition of a
Macaulay inverse system
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, write
f(D)
for the
partial differential operator
f(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}})
.
Given an ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, let
I^{\perp}
denote
the Macaulay inverse system:

I^{\perp}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]\mid 0=(f(D)\cdot g)|_{x_{1}=\ldots x_{n}=0}\ \text{for all $g\in I$}\}.

For a lattice polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

V_{P}\coloneqq(\gr I(P\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}))^{\perp}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{V_{P}}\coloneqq(\gr I(\text{int}(P)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}))^{\perp}.

If
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a graded ideal, then
I^{\perp}
is a graded vector space which is naturally isomorphic to the linear dual of the quotient by
I
. This ensures that

i_{P}(m;q)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\dim(V_{mP})_{j}q^{j}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{i_{P}}(m;q)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\dim(\widetilde{V_{mP}})_{j}q^{j}.

Definition 3.3
.

For a lattice polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, consider the bigraded polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}]
where
\deg(x_{0})=(1,0)
and
\deg(x_{i})=(0,1)
for all
i\geq 1
. The harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
and the interior ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}}
are the bigraded subspaces of
\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}]
defined as

\mathcal{H}_{P}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathbb{C}\cdot x_{0}^{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{mP}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}\mathbb{C}\cdot x_{0}^{m}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\widetilde{V_{mP}}\,.

Our construction ensures that

\Hilb(\mathcal{H}_{P};t,q)\coloneqq\sum_{m,j}\dim(\mathcal{H}_{P})_{m,j}t^{m}q^{j}=E_{P}(t,q)

and similarly that
\Hilb(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}};t,q)=\widetilde{E_{P}}(t,q)
.
One of the main contributions of Rhoades and Reiner is to show that
\mathcal{H}_{P}
admits the structure of a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and that
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{P}}
includes as an ideal under this structure.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
RR24
]
)
.

The harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{P}
has the structure of a ring, where the multiplication is

(x_{0}^{i}\otimes f,x_{0}^{j}\otimes g)\mapsto x_{0}^{i+j}\otimes fg
.
Moreover, the interior ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{P}
is naturally a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{P}
and forms an
ideal under this product structure.

Let

There exists a unique way of assigning a polynomial

3.3.
Zonotopes and zonotopal algebras

Definition 3.11

A

4.
Graded Ehrhart polynomials and quantum reciprocity

In this section, we prove a formal reciprocity statement for quantum integer-valued polynomials. We then apply this formal reciprocity statement to the case of unimodular zonotopes.

4.1.
Quantum Reciprocity

We are interested in the generating functions of quantum integer-valued polynomials. We collect a series of results here about the behavior of such generating functions. These results, and their proofs, are analogous to the facts that the generating functions of integer-valued polynomials are rational and obey a formal reciprocity law
[
Sta97
, Theorem 4.1.1 and Proposition 4.2.3]
,
[
BS18
, Section 4.1]
.

In the proofs of the next two results, we will use repeatedly use the negative
q
-binomial theorem
[
Sag20
, Chapter 3, Exercise 8]
.

Theorem 4.1

(The Negative
q
-Binomial Theorem)
.

For a fixed positive integer
n
,

\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{n+k-1}{k}_{q}t^{k}=\frac{1}{(1-t)(1-qt)\cdots(1-q^{n-1}t)}\,.

In the
q
equal to
1
limit, the negative
q
-binomial coefficient theorem specializes to an expression for the generating function of the multiset coefficients
\binom{n+k-1}{k}=(-1)^{k}\binom{-n}{k}
.
After using the identity
\binom{n}{k}_{q}=\binom{n}{n-k}_{q}
and rewriting, the negative
q
-binomial theorem gives the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

For a fixed non-negative integer
k
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n}{k}_{q}t^{n}=\frac{t^{k}}{(1-t)(1-qt)\cdots(1-q^{k}t)}\,.

Corollary

4.2
tells us that the generating functions of the
q
-binomial coefficient polynomials are rational functions in
\mathbb{Q}(t,q)
. The following proposition uses this fact in order to prove that the generating function of any quantum integer-valued polynomial is a rational function in
\mathbb{Q}(t,q)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f(t;q)\in\mathcal{R}_{q}
be of
t
-degree
d
. The generating function

E(t,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f([n]_{q};q)t^{n}

is a rational function in
\mathbb{Q}(t,q)
. This rational function has the form

\frac{N(t,q)}{\prod_{i=0}^{d}(1-tq^{i})}

where
N(t,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,q,q^{-1}]
and
N(t,q)
has
t
-degree at most
d
. If, in addition,
f(t;q)\in\mathcal{R}_{q}^{+}
, then
N(t,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,q]
.

Proof.

Suppose
f(t;q)
has
t
-degree
d
. By
3.29
, we can expand
f(t;q)
into the
q
-binomial coefficient polynomials as
f=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{t}{k}
where each
f_{k}(q)
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. If
f(t;q)\in\mathcal{R}_{q}^{+}
, then we know that each
f_{k}(q)
is instead inside of
\mathbb{Z}[q]
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}f([n]_{q};q)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{[n]_q}{k}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n}{k}_{q}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\frac{t^{k}}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)t^{k}(1-tq^{k+1})\cdots(1-tq^{d})}{\prod_{i=0}^{d}(1-tq^{i})}.

The third equality of this chain of equalities follows from
Corollary

4.2
.
∎

We now prove a quantum reciprocity statement comparing the generating functions of
f([n]_{q};q)
and
\overline{f}([n]_{q};q)
for a quantum integer-valued polynomial
f(t;q)
. After taking the
q
equal to
1
limit, quantum reciprocity specializes to the usual reciprocity between the generating functions of
f(t)
and
f(-t)
; see, for example,
[
BS18
, Theorem 4.1.6]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f(t;q)\in\mathcal{R}_{q}
be of
t
-degree
d
. The
generating functions

E(t,q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f([n]_{q};q)t^{n}\text{\hskip 10.22217ptand \hskip 10.22217pt}\overline{E}(t,q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\overline{f}([n]_{q};q)t^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}f([-n]_{q};q^{-1})t^{n}

are
both rational functions in
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
. These rational functions
can be written as

E(t,q)=\frac{N(t,q)}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{d})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{E}(t,q)=\frac{\overline{N}(t,q)}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{d})}

where
N(t,q),\overline{N}(t,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,q,q^{-1}]
are of
t
-degree at most
d
. Furthermore,
E(t,q)
and
\overline{E}(t,q)
are related by

\overline{E}(t,q)=-E(t^{-1},q^{-1})
.

As part of the proof of
4.4
, we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

[
HH17
, Proposition 6.3]

For all non-negative integers
n
and
k
,

\overline{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{[n]_q}{k}}=(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\binom{n+k-1}{k}_{q}

where
\overline{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{[n_q]}{k}}
is the polynomial
\overline{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{t}{k}}
evaluated at
t
equal to
[n]_{q}
.

After taking the
q
equal to
1
limit, Lemma
4.5
specializes to the statement that

(-1)^{k}\binom{-n}{k}=\binom{n+k-1}{k}\,.

This fact is a key ingredient in proving the formal reciprocity between the generating functions of
f(n)
and
f(-n)
. Likewise, Lemma
4.5
plays a vital role in our proof of
4.4
.

Proof of
4.4
.
By
3.29
, we can write
f(t;q)
as
f(t;q)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{t}{k}
where
f_{k}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. By
4.3
, and its proof,
E(t,q)
is the rational function

E(t,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q)\frac{t^{k}}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{k})}.

We now focus on the rationality of
\overline{E}(t,q)
and its relation with
E(t,q)
. Let
f_{k}(q^{-1})\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
be the result of applying
q\mapsto q^{-1}
to
f_{k}(q)
. By
Lemma

4.5
,

\overline{f}([n]_{q};q)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q^{-1})\overline{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{[n]_q}{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}f_{k}(q^{-1})\binom{n+k-1}{k}_{q}.

Thus we can rewrite the generating function
\overline{E}(t,q)
as

\displaystyle\overline{E}(t,q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\overline{f}([n]_{q};q)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}f_{k}(q^{-1})\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+k-1}{k}_{q}\right)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}f_{k}(q^{-1})\sum_{n\geq 1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+k-1}{k}_{q}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}f_{k}(q^{-1})t\sum_{n-1\geq 0}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(k+1)+(n-1)-1}{n-1}_{q}t^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k+1}{2}}f_{k}(q^{-1})\frac{t}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{k})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{d}f_{k}(q^{-1})\frac{t^{-k}}{(1-t^{-1})(1-t^{-1}q^{-1})\cdots(1-t^{-1}q^{-k})}

\displaystyle=-E(t^{-1},q^{-1}).

The fourth equality of this chain of equalities follows from
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
Graded Ehrhart Polynomials

Let
Z=A\cdot[0,1]^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a unimodular zonotope. We now prove that the graded lattice point counts of

are evaluations of a quantum integer-valued polynomial
\ehr_{Z}(t;q)

First, we show how to prove the formula for
i_{Z}(m;q)
and

given in the introduction.

Proof of
1.1
.

Let

be a unimodular zonotope, and let

be the rowspace of
A
over
\mathbb{C}
, with associated matroid

. For the first statement, we have

\displaystyle i_{Z}(m;q)

\displaystyle=\Hilb(\text{Orb}(mZ\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}),q)

by

\displaystyle=\Hilb(R_{mZ}^{\text{ext}},q)

\displaystyle=\Hilb(R_{L(m)}^{\text{ext}},q)

\displaystyle=q^{mn-d}T_{M(m)}(1+q,q^{-1})

\displaystyle=q^{mn-d}(q^{1-m}[m]_{q})^{d}T_{M}\left(\frac{q^{1-m}[m]_{q}+q}{q^{1-m}[m]_{q}},q^{-m}\right)

\displaystyle=q^{(n-d)m}[m]_{q}^{d}T_{M}\left(\frac{[m+1]_{q}}{[m]_{q}},q^{-m}\right).

The proof of the second statement about

follows in exactly the same way.

Proposition 4.6
.

There is a quantum integer-valued polynomial
\ehr_{Z}(t;q)\in\mathcal{R}_{q}^{+}
of

(-1)^{d}q^{-d}\overline{\ehr_{Z}}([m]_{q};q)=(-1)^{d}q^{-d}\ehr_{Z}([-m]_{q};q^{-1})=\widetilde{i_{Z}}(m;q)\,.

Proof.

We first construct the polynomial
\ehr_{Z}(t;q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)[t]
such that
\ehr_{Z}([m]_{q};q)=i_{Z}(m;q)
for all non-negative integers
m
. Define

\ehr_{Z}(t;q)\coloneqq(1+(q-1)t)^{n-d}t^{d}T_{M}(\frac{qt+1}{t},\frac{1}{1+(q-1)t}).

The

are bounded above by
d
and

\ehr_{Z}(t;q)

\mathbb{Q}(q)[t]

Once we know that

.
∎

Definition 5.1

Let
\mathcal{O}_{Y_{L}}(m,\ldots,m)

The restriction map

6.
Gorenstein Harmonic Algebras

In this section, we characterize the unimodular zonotopes
Z=A\cdot[0,1]^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose harmonic algebras
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
are Gorenstein in terms of the matroid
M
of
A
. We then show that if
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is Gorenstein, the numerator of the graded Ehrhart series of
Z
displays a remarkable palindromic symmetry.

Recall that, by
5.11
, we know
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}[0,d]\simeq\Omega\mathcal{H}_{Z}
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is Gorenstein if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}
as graded
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
-modules (possibly with a degree shift). This observation is the starting point of our proof of
1.8
. We now collect a series of lemmas to analyze the interior ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}
of
Z
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is an integral domain.

Proof.

By
Corollary

1.7
,
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is the homogeneous coordinate ring of
Y_{L}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2^{n}-1}
under the Segre embedding. The variety
Y_{L}
is irreducible, hence
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
contains no zero divisors.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
M
be a matroid with
k
connected components such that each connected component is also a circuit of
M
. Let
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{k}
be the ranks of these respective components.
The Tutte polynomial of
M
is equal to

T_{M}(x,y)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(y+x+x^{2}+\ldots+x^{d_{i}}).

Proof.

The Tutte polynomial of a matroid is equal to the product of the Tutte polynomials of its connected components. Therefore, it suffices to prove that
T_{U_{n-1,n}}(x,y)=y+x+x^{2}+\ldots+x^{n-1}
. This, in turn, follows from a deletion-contraction argument.
∎

In the following lemma, we use the presentation of
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
given in
5.15
.

Lemma 6.3
.

If

contains no coloops, the term

represents an element of the interior ideal
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}
. In any case, the term

\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}

Proof.

Under the map

is sent to the element of

. This restricts to the function

on

have positive degree. This ensures that

. The claim now follows from

For any matroid

We also know that

\dim(\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}})_{m+2}=(m+2)^{d}T_{M}(\frac{m+1}{m+2},1)=(m+1)^{d}.

Thus, in this case, we’ve shown that

\mathcal{H}_{Z}[2,d]\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}[0,d]\simeq\Omega\mathcal{H}_{Z}

and that
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is Gorenstein.
∎

Recall that
4.7
lets us write the graded Ehrhart series of
Z
as a rational function

E_{Z}(t,q)=\frac{N_{Z}(t,q)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(1-tq^{i})}

where
N_{Z}(t,q)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,q]
is of
t
-degree at most
n
. Write
N_{Z}(t,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}g_{k}(q)t^{k}
where
g_{k}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
. If the harmonic algebra of
Z
is Gorenstein, the coefficients
g_{k}(q)
and
g_{n-k}(q)
are related by a quantum symmetry. This symmetry is evocative of Hibi’s palindromicity theorem which states that reflexive polytopes have symmetric
h^{*}
vectors
[
Hib92
]
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose that
Z
has a Gorenstein harmonic algebra. If
M
is Boolean, then for all
k
,

g_{k}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}g_{n-k-1}(q^{-1}).

If
M
is not Boolean, then for all
k
,

g_{k}(q)=(-1)^{n+d}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}-d}g_{n-k}(q^{-1}).

Proof.

If
M
is not Boolean, then the proof of
1.8
tells us that
\mathcal{H}_{Z}[1,0]\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}
. Thus by
4.7
,

E_{Z}(t,q)=t^{-1}\widetilde{E_{Z}}(t,q)=(-1)^{d+1}t^{-1}q^{-d}E_{Z}(t^{-1},q^{-1}).

We can compute that

\displaystyle(-1)^{d+1}t^{-1}q^{-d}E_{Z}(t^{-1},q^{-1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d+1}t^{-1}q^{-d}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}g_{k}(q^{-1})t^{-k}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(1-t^{-1}q^{-i})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+d}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}-d}g_{k}(q^{-1})t^{n-k}}{\prod_{i=0}^{n}(1-tq^{i})}\,.

This yields the second claim. The first claim follows from an identical argument and the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{Z}[2,0]\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{H}_{Z}}
when
M
is Boolean.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

In fact, a complete description of the numerator of the graded Ehrhart series of an
n
-cube
C_{n}
is given in
[
BB13
, Section 4]
and
[
BO18
, Theorem 1.1]
. Although, as those authors note, one could also derive it from the works of Carlitz and MacMahon using the equality
\Hilb(\text{Orb}(mC_{n}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n});q)1=[m+1]_{q}^{n}
; see the references in
[
BB13
]
for more details. The polynomial
N_{C_{n}}(t,q)
is an Euler–Mahonian distribution and is defined as follows. For a permutation
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}
of
S_{n}
, let
\text{Des}(\pi)=\{j\in[n-1]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\pi_{j}>\pi_{j+1}\}
,
\text{des}(\pi)=|\text{Des}(\pi)|
and
\text{maj}(\pi)=\sum_{j\in\text{Des}(\pi)}j
. Then

N_{C_{n}}(t,q)=\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}t^{\text{des}(\pi)}q^{\text{maj}(\pi)}.

The first identity given in
6.5
is equivalent to the identity

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{n}\\
\text{des}(\pi)=k\end{subarray}}q^{\text{maj}(\pi)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in S_{n}\\
\text{des}(\pi)=n-k-1\end{subarray}}q^{\binom{n}{2}-\text{maj}(\pi)}.

Remark 6.7
.

A similar argument to the proof of
1.8
shows that the affine semigroup ring of a unimodular zonotope
Z
is Gorenstein if and only if
M
is Boolean or every connected component of
M
is a circuit.

Remark 6.8
.

6.5
gives us partial information about the numerator of the graded Ehrhart series of Gorenstein unimodular zonotopes. As
i_{Z}(0;q)=1
, it can be checked that
g_{0}(q)=1
for all unimodular zonotopes
Z
. If
M
is Boolean,
6.5
implies
N_{Z}(t,q)
has
t
-degree
n-1
with
g_{n-1}(q)=q^{\binom{n}{2}}
. If the harmonic algebra of
Z
is Gorenstein and
M
is not Boolean, then
6.5
implies that
N_{Z}(t,q)
has
t
-degree
n
with
g_{n}(q)=(-1)^{n+d}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}-d}
.

7.
Further questions

Here we collect some questions for future work.

7.1.
A matroidal formula for the graded
h^{*}
vector

Given a matroid
M
of rank
d
on a ground set of size
n
, define
h^{*}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
and
h^{*}_{q}(t;q)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,q]
by declaring

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}m^{d}T_{M}(\frac{m+1}{m},1)t^{m}=\frac{h^{*}(t)}{(1-t)^{d+1}}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{(n-d)m}[m]_{q}^{d}T_{M}(\frac{[m+1]_{q}}{[m]_{q}},q^{-m})t^{m}=\frac{h^{*}_{q}(t;q)}{(1-t)(1-tq)\cdots(1-tq^{n})}\,.

4.3
implies that
h^{*}_{q}(t;q)
is well defined and that
h^{*}_{q}(t;1)=(1-t)^{n-d}h^{*}(t)
.

If
M
is the matroid of a unimodular zonotope
Z
,
h^{*}(t)
and
h^{*}_{q}(t;q)
are the numerators of the Ehrhart series and graded Ehrhart series of
Z
, respectively. In this case,
[
BJM19
, Corollary 5.10]
gives an expression for
h^{*}(t)
as a sum of
j
-Eulerian polynomials. This sum is indexed by the bases of
M
and relies on the activities of these bases as input. In light of
Remark

6.6
, it seems possible that
h^{*}(t;q)
might have a similar expression as a weighted sum of
q
-analogues of the
j
-Eulerian polynomials.

Problem 7.1
.

Give an explicit matroidal formula for
h^{*}_{q}(t;q)
.

7.2.
The graded Ehrhart theory of non-unimodular zonotopes

If
Z
is an arbitrary zonotope, the Ehrhart theory of
Z
is dictated by the
arithmetic matroid

\mathfrak{M}
of
Z
. The arithmetic matroid
\mathfrak{M}
associated to a zonotope is the data of a matroid
M
along with a multiplicity function
m\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}2^{E}\to\mathbb{Z}
which has the property that for a basis
B
of
M
,
m(B)
is equal to the determinant of the submatrix indexed by
B
. Every arithmetic matroid has an arithmetic Tutte polynomial
T_{\mathfrak{M}}(x,y)
. In the case of a unimodular zonotope
Z
, the arithmetic Tutte polynomial of the arithmetic matroid of
Z
is equal to ordinary Tutte polynomial of the matroid associated to
Z
. In
[
DM12
]
, it is shown that

|mZ\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|=m^{d}T_{\mathfrak{M}}(\frac{m+1}{m},1)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,|\text{int}(mZ)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|=m^{d}T_{\mathfrak{M}}(\frac{m-1}{m},1)

for all zonotopes
Z
. This expression generalizes the one given in
4.6
outside of the realm of unimodular zonotopes. One could ask whether our
1.3
generalizes in a similar fashion. As the following example shows, this is not true, at least in its most obvious form.

Example 7.2
.

Let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the zonotopal diamond which is the image of
[0,1]^{2}
under the linear map given by
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
-1&1\end{bmatrix}
. This zonotope is not unimodular as
\det(A)=2
. The arithmetic Tutte polynomial associated to
Z
is
T_{\mathfrak{M}}(x,y)=x^{2}+1
. Through direct calculation we see

i_{Z}(1;q)=1+2q+2q^{2}\neq 2+2q+q^{2}=T_{\mathfrak{M}}([2]_{q},1).

Despite this example, it is still possible that the
q
-lattice point counts of an arbitrary zonotope are an arithmetic matroid invariant. It would be quite surprising to find two zonotopes
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
whose arithmetic matroids are the same but
i_{Z}(m;q)\neq i_{Z^{\prime}}(m;q)
for some integer
m\geq 0
.

Question 7.3
.

Is the graded Ehrhart theory of a non-unimodular zonotope
Z
an invariant of its associated arithmetic matroid? If so, is there a generalization of
4.6
to arithmetic matroids?

7.3.
Homological aspects of
\mathcal{H}_{Z}

The harmonic algebras
\mathcal{H}_{Z}\simeq\mathbb{C}[z_{S}|S\subseteq[n]]/I_{L}^{\text{SE}}
warrant further study. In particular, it would be interesting to give a bigraded free resolution of
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
as a
\mathbb{C}[z_{S}|S\subseteq[n]]
module where
\deg(z_{S})=(1,|S|)
.
Already for the case of a cube, this question is interesting. In this case,
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is the coordinate ring of the Segre embedding of
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2^{n}-1}
.
In a seminal paper
[
Sno13
]
, Snowden showed that there is a finite list of “master syzygies” which control all syzygies of Segre embeddings.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a bigraded polynomial ring where
x_{i}
has degree
(1,i)
. Consider the map
R\to\mathcal{H}_{Z}
defined by sending
x_{i}\mapsto\sum_{|S|=i}z_{S}
. Braun and Olsen prove that if
Z
is a cube, then
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is a finitely generated and free
R
-module
[
BO18
, Proof of Theorem 4.10]
. As every harmonic algebra of a unimodular zonotope is a quotient of the harmonic algebra of a cube, this implies that every harmonic algebra is a finitely generated
R
-module. Hilbert’s syzygy theorem ensures that
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
has a finite free resolution as an
R
-module. In fact, as
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
is Cohen–Macaulay (
1.5
), the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula (
[
Eis95
, Chapter 19]
) ensures that this free resolution will have length
n+1-(d+1)=n-d
. By describing the bigraded Betti numbers of such a resolution, one will obtain a description of the numerator of the graded Ehrhart series of
Z
in
4.7
. Our partial descriptions of the numerator of the graded Ehrhart series gives some information on the shape of these Betti numbers. See
4.7
and
6.5
.

Problem 7.4
.

Give a free resolution of
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
as a
\mathbb{C}[z_{S}|S\subseteq[n]]
-module or as an
R
-module.

As we’ve given a presentation for the homogeneous coordinate ring of any complex arrangement Schubert variety
Y_{L}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2^{n}-1}
under the Segre embedding, one could ask the same questions as
7.4
for these coordinate rings. By
1.4
, such coordinate rings are isomorphic to a harmonic algebra
\mathcal{H}_{Z}
when
L
is unimodular.
