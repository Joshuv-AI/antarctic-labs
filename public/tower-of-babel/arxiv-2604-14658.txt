Yu.O. Koroleva

Abstract.
A new weighted
Hardy-type inequality for functions from
the Sobolev space
W_{p}^{1}
is proved. It is assumed that functions vanish on small alternating pieces of the
boundary. The proved inequality generalizes the classical known weighted Hardy-type inequalities.

1
Introduction

Mathematical analysis needs to treat integral inequalities of different types. One of the useful estimate is the classical Hardy-type inequality:

\int_{\Omega}\left(|u(x)|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\right)^{p}\,dx\leq C(p,n)\left(\int_{\Omega}{|\nabla u(x)|}^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(1.1)

where
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),\ p>1,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ \Omega\in\mathbf{R}^{n}.

This estimate could be generalized to the multidimensional
weighted form:

\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)|u(x)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq{C}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}W(x)|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(1.2)

where
\ u(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

V(x)\geq 0,W(x)\geq 0,\,p,q\geq 1,

and the constant
{C}
depends only on weight functions
V(x)\mbox{ and
}W(x).
There are several results concerning weighted Hardy-type
inequality (see e.g. the books
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
15
]

and the references given there). The following result is by V. Maz’ya (see

[
13
, Corollary of Theorem 1.4.1.2, Theorem 1.4.2.2 ]
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty,\ p<n
or
1=p\leq q<\infty.
Then the Hardy
inequality (
1.2
) with
W(x)\equiv 1
holds for every

u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with a finite constant
C>0
if
and only if

B:=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup\limits_{R>0}R^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\left(\int\limits_{B_{R(x)}}V(y)\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

where
B_{R(x)}
is a ball of the radius
R
centered at the point

x.

J.  Nečas has generalized the weighted Hardy-type inequality to domains in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
[
14
]
.
He proved that if
\Omega
is a bounded domain with Lipschitz
boundary,
1<p<\infty,\,

\alpha<p-1,
then for
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
the inequality

\int\limits_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\varrho^{-p+\alpha}(x)\,dx\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\varrho^{\alpha}(x)\,dx

(1.3)

holds, where
\varrho(x)=dist(x,\partial\Omega).
After that this
inequality was generalized by A. Kufner in
[
10
]
to domains
with the Hölder boundary and later by A. Wannebo (see

[
17
]
) to domains with the generalized Hölder condition.
All results related to (
1.3
) in the case
\alpha=0
was
described in
[
7
]
.

The aim of this paper is to prove a Hardy-type inequality
(
1.3
) for an arbitrary
p>1
for functions from
H^{1},
vanishing on small
alternating pieces of the boundary of the domain. It is assumed for
the simplicity that
\Omega
is a rectangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Such a result is completely new in the theory of Hardy-type
inequalities and it gives us possibility to prove the boundedness of the sequence of functions considered in
microinhomogeneous domains. Indeed, in the classical statement the Hardy inequality was proved only for functions vanishing on the whole boundary of the domain. Our research shows that this requirement is too much restrictive. The Hardy inequality holds true even if the function vanishes on the set of small measure.

The paper
[
9
]
treats the case of weighted Hardy inequality for
p=2
for functions vanishing on small pieces of the boundary.
A related result with weight functions equaled to 1 were obtained earlier in
[
1
]
and in

[
2
]
for Friedrichs inequality. This inequality can be regarded as a
special case of weighted Hardy-type inequalities.

The current research is organized as follows: in Section
2
we give some
necessary definitions and formulate auxiliary lemmas. The main
results are presented and proved in Section
3
and Section

4
is reserved for some concluding remarks.

2
Preliminaries.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the rectangle
[0,a]\times[0,b]:

\Omega=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):0\leq x_{1}\leq a,0\leq x_{2}\leq b\}.

Assume that
\varepsilon>0
is a small positive parameter,

\varepsilon=\frac{b}{N},\ N\gg 1,\ N\in\mathbb{N},
and

\delta=const,

0<\delta<1.
Moreover, let

{{\Gamma}}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\partial\Omega:\ x_{1}=0\}.

We suppose that
{\Gamma}
is represented in the form:

{\Gamma}=\overline{{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}}\cup{\gamma}_{\varepsilon},\,\,\,{\Gamma}_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup\limits_{{i}}({\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}),\,\,\,{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup\limits_{{i}}({\gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}),\,\,\,{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\cap{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}=\varnothing,

|{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}|=\varepsilon\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt|\left({\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\cup{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\right)|=\varepsilon,

where
{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}
and
{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}

are alternating (see Figure
1
).

The domain
\Omega
.

Denote by

\Pi_{1}^{i}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega:0\leq x_{1}\leq a,\,x_{2}\in{\Gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\},

\Pi_{2}^{i}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega:0\leq x_{1}\leq a,\,x_{2}\in{\gamma}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\},

\Pi_{1}^{i}\cap\Pi_{2}^{i}=l_{i};\Pi_{1}=\bigcup\limits_{i}\Pi_{1}^{i},\ \Pi_{2}=\bigcup\limits_{i}\Pi_{2}^{i}.

Moreover, we use the
notation

B(x,r):=\{(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:(y_{1}-x_{1})^{2}+(y_{2}-x_{2})^{2}\leq r^{2}\},

and the average value of the function
u
over
B(\cdot,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined as

u_{B}:=\frac{1}{\pi r^{2}}\int\limits_{B}u(x)\,dx.

Let
u
be a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{2}.
The
maximal functions
M(u)
and
M_{R}(u)
of
u
are defined by

M(u)(x):=\sup\limits_{r>0}u_{B},\ M_{R}(u)(x):=\sup\limits_{0<r\leq R}u_{B}.

(2.1)

Define the Sobolev space

W_{p}^{1}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})=\{u_{\varepsilon}\in W_{p}^{1}(\Omega):\ u_{\varepsilon}|_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}=0\}.

Analogously,

C^{\infty}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})=\{u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega):u_{\varepsilon}=0\mbox{ in a neighborhood of }{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}=0\}.

Let
x\in\Omega,\ \rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\Gamma).
We use the
following functions:

r_{1}(x)=\rho(x),\ r_{2}(x)={\rm dist}(x,\,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}):=\inf\limits_{y\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}{\rm dist}(x,y).

According to the geometrical construction of the domain, (see Figure

2
),

r_{1}(x)<r_{2}(x)<\rho(x)+(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

The domain
\Omega
.

We prove first an auxiliary Lemma on the validity of a Friedrichs inequality in the considered domain.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in W_{p}^{1}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}).
Then the
Friedrichs type inequality

\int\limits_{\Pi_{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq K(a,\varepsilon,\delta)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx,

(2.2)

holds with

K(a,\varepsilon,\delta)=2^{p}(p+1)\left(a^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}+\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{q}+1}(1-\delta)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\right).

Proof.

Fix the point
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Pi_{1}^{i}.
By using the Newton-Leibnitz
formula and H
\ddot{o}
lder inequality, we have

u_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})=u_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})-u_{\varepsilon}(0,x_{2})=\int\limits_{0}^{x_{1}}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}\,dx_{1}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{a}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}\,dx_{1}.

Hence,

|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\left(\int\limits_{0}^{a}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}\,dx_{1}\right)^{p}\leq\int\limits_{0}^{a}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx_{1}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{a}\,dx_{1}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}=a^{\frac{p}{q}}\int\limits_{0}^{a}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx_{1}.

Then, by
integrating the last inequality with respect to
x_{2}
and after that
with respect to
x_{1}
over
\Pi_{1}^{i},
we obtain that

\int\limits_{\Pi_{1}^{i}}u_{\varepsilon}^{p}\,dx\leq a^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\int\limits_{\Pi_{1}^{i}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

(2.3)

Now choose the point
(x_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})\in\Pi_{2}^{i}
such that

\widetilde{x}_{2}=\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}x_{2}+\varepsilon\delta.
This
means that
(x_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})\in\Pi_{2}^{i}
if and only if

(x_{1},{{x}_{2}})\in\Pi_{1}^{i}.
Taking into account an evident inequality

(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p}(p+1)(a^{p}+b^{p}),\ \text{ for }p>2

and again using the Newton-Leibnitz
formula, we fined that

u_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})=u(x_{1},x_{2})+\int\limits_{x_{2}}^{\widetilde{x}_{2}}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\,d\widetilde{x}_{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2})\leq 2^{p}(p+1)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x_{1},x_{2})+2^{p}(p+1)\left(\int\limits_{x_{2}}^{\widetilde{x}_{2}}\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\,d\widetilde{x}_{2}\right)^{p}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}(p+1)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x_{1},x_{2})+2^{p}(p+1)(\widetilde{x}_{2}-x_{2})^{\frac{p}{q}}\int\limits_{x_{2}}^{\widetilde{x}_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\widetilde{x}_{2}.

Integrating the last
inequality over
\Pi_{2}^{i}
and substituting the integral on the
right-hand side by the greater integral, we get that

\int\limits_{\Pi_{2}^{i}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx_{1}\,d\widetilde{x}_{2}\leq 2^{p}(p+1)\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}\int\limits_{\Pi_{1}^{i}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}+2^{p}(p+1)\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{q}+1}(1-\delta)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\int\limits_{\Pi_{2}^{i}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx_{1}\,d\widetilde{x}_{2}.

Finally, by applying the
estimate (
2.3
) to the first integral on the right-hand side and
substituting both integrals by the greater integral, we obtain that

\int\limits_{\Pi_{2}^{i}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq 2^{p}(p+1)\left(a^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}+\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{q}+1}(1-\delta)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\right)\int\limits_{\Pi_{1}^{i}\cup\Pi_{2}^{i}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

(2.4)

By summarizing up the inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) with
respect to
i,
we obtain the desired estimate:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Pi_{2}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{i}\Pi_{2}^{i}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq 2^{p}(p+1)\left(a^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}+\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{q}+1}(1-\delta)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\right)\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{i}\left(\Pi_{1}^{i}\cup\Pi_{2}^{i}\right)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx=

\displaystyle=2^{p}(p+1)\left(a^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}+\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{q}+1}(1-\delta)^{\frac{p}{q}+1}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

∎

We also need the following well-known Lemmas

.

Let
u\in W_{1}^{1}(B).
Then

|u(x)-u_{B}|\leq 2\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u(y)|}{|x-y|}\,dy.

For the proof see in
[

]

.

Let

Then

\inf\limits_{y\in\Gamma\cap B}\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|}{|y-z|}\,dz\leq\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u(z)|}{|x-z|}\,dz.

.

If
0<\alpha<2
and
r>0,

For the proof we refer to

3
The main results.

Consider the function

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho(x),&\text{if
$x\in\Pi_{1}$,}\\
\rho(x)+(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2},&\text{if $x\in\Pi_{2}.$}\end{cases}

(3.1)

Our first main result is the following pointwise inequality:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}).
Then
there exists a constant
C,\ C\leq 4,
such that the pointwise
inequality

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)M_{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\chi_{B\left(\overline{x},\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)}(x))

(3.2)

holds, where
\overline{x}\in\Gamma
satisfies

|x-\overline{x}|=\rho(x).

Proof.

Choose the point
x\in\Omega
and denote by

B:=B(\overline{x},\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)),
where

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)
is defined in (
3.1
).

Then
B\cap\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\neq{\o}
for each
x\in\Omega.
Extend
the function
u_{\varepsilon}
in

\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
by reflecting it across the
boundary. By applying Lemma
2.2
to the extended

u_{\varepsilon},
we have for any
y\in B\cap\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|=|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-{u_{\varepsilon}}_{B}|+|u_{\varepsilon}(y)-{u_{\varepsilon}}_{B}|\leq

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(z)|}{|x-z|}\,dz+\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(z)|}{|y-z|}\,dz\right).

Hence, by applying Lemma
2.3
to (
3.3
), we obtain that

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq 4\int\limits_{B}\frac{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(z)|}{|x-z|}\,dz.

(3.4)

Finally, taking into account Lemma
2.4
and (
3.1
), we
have that

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq 4c_{2}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\,M_{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|)(x)=

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}8\rho(x)\,M_{\rho(x)}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|),&\text{\!\!$x\in\Pi_{1}\cap B\left(\overline{x},\rho(x)\right),$}\\
8\!\left(\rho(x)\!+\!\frac{(1-\delta)}{2}\varepsilon\right)\!M_{\rho(x)+\frac{(1-\delta)}{2}\varepsilon}(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|),&\text{\!\!$x\in\Pi_{2}\cap B\!\left(\overline{x},\rho(x)\!+\!\frac{(1-\delta)}{2}\varepsilon\right).$}\end{cases}

The proof is complete.
∎

The next theorem generalizes the classical
Hardy-type inequalities to a much more wide class of
functions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)
be the function defined in

(
3.1
)
and
p>1,

0\leq\alpha<\alpha_{0}.

Then the estimate

\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-p+\alpha}(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x)\,dx\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx

(3.6)

holds for each fixed
\varepsilon
for all functions

u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}),
where the
constant
C
does not depend on
u_{\varepsilon}
and on

\varepsilon.

Proof.

Fix
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})

extended in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus{\overline{\Omega}}.
According to
(
3.2
) the inequality

\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\leq 8M_{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\chi_{B(\overline{x},\rho_{\varepsilon}(x))}\right)(x)

holds for all
x\in\Omega.
Then
we have that

\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}}{\rho^{p}_{\varepsilon}(x)}\,dx\leq 8^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega}M^{p}_{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\chi_{B\left(\overline{x},\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)}(x)\right)\,dx.

The statement in
Theorem
3.1
implies that

\int\limits_{\Omega}M^{p}_{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\chi_{B\left(\overline{x},\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\right)}\right)(x)\,dx\leq C_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx.

Thus, it
yields that

\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\right)^{p}\,dx\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx,

where

C=8^{p}C_{1}.

Hence, the inequality (
3.6
) holds with
\alpha=0.
The next
step is to prove (
3.6
) for
\alpha>0.
Choose
\sigma>0
and
put
v_{\varepsilon}=|u_{\varepsilon}|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma}.
Taking into account an evident inequalities
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2}),

(a+b)^{p}\leq 2^{p}(p+1)(a^{p}+b^{p}),\ \text{ for }p>2
it is
not difficult to derive that

\displaystyle|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{p}=(|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq\left(2\left(\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2\sigma}+2\sigma^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2\sigma-2}\left(\frac{\partial\rho_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}|u_{\varepsilon}|\right)^{2}+\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2\sigma}\left(\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)\left(|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma p}+\sigma^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{(\sigma-1)p}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right).

By now applying (
3.6
) with
\alpha=0
to
v_{\varepsilon},
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-p+\sigma p}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq C_{2}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\sigma p}\,dx+\sigma^{p}\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{p(\sigma-1)}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\right).

Here
C_{2}=C2^{p}\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right).

If
1-C_{2}\sigma^{p}>0,
then we have

\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-p+p\sigma}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1-C_{2}\sigma^{p}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{p\sigma}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx.

Substituting
\sigma
by
\frac{\alpha}{p},
we prove the inequality (
3.6
) for
\alpha>0.
Finally, by approximating the functions from
W^{1}_{p}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
by smooth functions belonging to

C^{\infty}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}),
we can complete the proof.
∎

Our final main result is presented in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\rho(x)=dist(x,\Gamma)
and
p>1,

0\leq\alpha<\alpha_{0}.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho^{-p+\alpha}(x)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x)\,dx\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho^{\alpha}(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx

(3.8)

holds for all functions
u_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1}_{p}(\Omega,\Gamma_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

Due to the geometrical construction of the domain,

\rho(x)\leq\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)<\rho(x)+(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Having in mind the estimate (
3.6
), and an evident inequality for nonnagative
A\penalty\ \geq\penalty\ 0,B\penalty\ \geq\penalty\ 0,

(A+B)^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha}(A\cdot B)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
one concludes the following:

\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x)}{(\rho(x)+(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2})^{p-\alpha}}\,dx\leq C_{1}\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho^{\alpha}(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx

(3.9)

where

C_{1}=2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(1-\delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}C.

Let
\gamma_{i}
be the angle between the segment
\Gamma_{i}
and the line
XA_{i}
where
XA_{i}=\text{dist}(x,\Gamma^{\varepsilon}_{i}).

Then

\rho(x)=\begin{cases}\text{dist}{(x,\Gamma^{\varepsilon})},\text{ if }x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in l_{i};\\
(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}tg\gamma_{i}<\infty,\text{ else }.\end{cases}

Therefore, the estimate (
3.9
) can be rewritten as

\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}(x)}{\rho(x)^{p-\alpha}}\,dx\leq C_{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho^{\alpha}(x)|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx,

(3.10)

where

C_{2}=C_{1}\left(1+(1-\delta)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}ctg\gamma_{i}\right)^{p-\alpha}.

∎

4
Concluding remarks.

Remark 1
.

The condition
\delta=1
in the definition (
3.1
) corresponds
to the case
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}\penalty\ =\penalty\ {\Gamma}.
In this case the
Hardy-type inequality (
3.6
) becomes the inequality
(
1.3
) and the constant in (
3.8
) equals to
the constant in the classical Hardy inequality when the function is
vanishing on the whole boundary.

Remark 2
.

If
\alpha=0,
then (
3.6
) takes the form

\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|}{\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)}\right)^{p}\,dx\leq C\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{p}\,dx.

(4.1)

This inequality is an important tool for proving the embedding theorems.

Remark 3
.

The proved result is valid for the case when
\Omega
is an arbitrarily Lipschitz domain and the function vanishes on the small pieces of its boundary.
