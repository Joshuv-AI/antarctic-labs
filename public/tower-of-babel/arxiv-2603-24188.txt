Hereditarily and Super Bassian Modules over Certain Rings
Peter Vassilev Danchev, Truong Cong Quynh, Jan Žemlička

Abstract
. We characterize in certain basic cases when a module over a ring is either hereditarily Bassian or super Bassian in the sense that either each its proper submodule is Bassian or, respectively, each its proper epimorphic image is Bassian. We prove several structural criteria for both hereditarily Bassian and super Bassian modules over non-primitive Dedekind prime rings, and in particular Dedekind domains. Over these rings, we establish that a singular module is super Bassian exactly when it is Bassian, which is true if and only if it is Bassian. In addition, for an arbitrary (not necessarily singular) module over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring, the property of being super Bassian curiously implies the property of being hereditary Bassian always. Our results somewhat continue and supply recent results due to Tuganbaev in Mathematics (2026) and Blacher in J. Algebra (2026).

Introduction and Motivation

Let
M
be a module over a ring
R
.
Imitating
[
4
]

[
2
]

[

Moreover, if

is called

. It is quite surprisingly that super Bassian modules are just Hopfian (see, e.g.,
[

is called

As a pioneer work on the present subject, Rowen and Small explored in
[
6
]
when some large classes of (affine) algebras are Bassian proving that any semi-prime affine PI-algebra over a field is Bassian, and any semi-prime affine Goldie algebra of finite GK dimension satisfying the ACC property on semi-prime ideals is Bassian.

Likewise, recently, in
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
were studied and characterized those Bassian Abelian groups whose proper subgroups are again Bassian, as well as those Bassian Abelian groups whose proper homomorphic images are Bassian too.

On the other hand, very recently, Blancher examined in
[
1
]
those rings whose all proper subrings are either Noetherian or Artinian.

That is why, it is quite logical to continue the investigations in this way for other algebraic structures like modules over some classical types of rings. We, however, treat here the more global case of modules over a few large classes of rings such as (possibly non-commutative) non-primitive Dedekind prime rings and principal ideal domains. Our work is organized as follows: We distribute the main results into two subsequent sections which are devoted, respectively, to the super Bassian and hereditary Bassian cases. In the first section, we establish that the property of any module over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring to be super Bassian is equivalent to the existence of a finitely generated submodule of the former module such that the corresponding quotient is singular and all its primary components are again finitely generated (see Theorem
1.13
). In the second one, we establish that the property of any module over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring to be hereditary Bassian is tantamount to the fact that each primary component of its singular submodule is finitely generated and the existence of a finitely generated submodule that is singular modulo the given module. Moreover, each module over a semi-local ring with nilpotent Jacobson radical is hereditarily Bassian precisely when its socle is finitely generated (see Theorems
2.9
and
2.6
, respectively).

Traditionally,

denotes an injective envelope of
M

R

\tau(M)

1.
Super Bassian Modules

We begin here by listing once again our pivotal instrument.

Definition

Let

The next statement is worthy of mentioning.

Example 1.1

Any right duo ring

Suppose

∎

The following technical observation is pretty obvious, so that we can omit its proof.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
M
be a right
R
-module. Then, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

is super Bassian.

(2)

each quotient of

is super Bassian.

(3)

is exact, then

.

Since direct summands are isomorphic to factors of a module, we obtain in addition as an consequence that the class of super Bassian modules is closed under taking direct summands.

A module

is called

and

of

has square-free socle for each submodule

.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let

be a distributive right

-module. Then, the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

is super Bassian.

(2)

Every factor module of

is generalized Bassian.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). It is obvious, because every Bassian module is generalized Bassian.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that every factor module of

is generalized Bassian. Furthermore, let

and

be submodules of

with

and

be a monomorphism. Since

is generalized Bassian,

is semi-simple and

is embeddable into

by

[

. But, as noticed above,

has square-free socle, we infer that

whence

. We, thereby, conclude that

is super Bassian, as wanted.
∎

Since an infinite direct power of a nonzero module is not Bassian, we can formulate the following easy observation.

Lemma 1.4

A module

over a ring

is not super Bassian if there exists

The next claim is also helpful for further applicable purposes and so worthy of documenting.

Lemma 1.5

Let

(1)

there exists a simple module

and a submodule

(1) Let

(2)

is not super Bassian according to (1) and Lemma

.

Recall that a module

Proposition 1.6
.

Let

is super Bassian.

(2)

is finitely generated.

(3)

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). It follows from Lemma

(2)

(3)

and a submodule

, which is a contradiction to the finite Goldie dimension of

We now intend to look at the more complicated situation of when a module over a right Noetherian ring is super Bassian by establishing the following (see
[
4
, Example 2.1]
).

Lemma 1.7
.

Any finitely generated module over a right Noetherian ring is super Bassian.

Proof.

Recall that Noetherian module is Bassian. Since finitely generated module over a right Noetherian ring is Noetherian, it is super Bassian, as asked.
∎

We are now prepared to prove the following assertion.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of all maximal ideals of a non semi-simple abelian regular ring

, and let
M
be an

-module. If

is super Bassian, then

\text{\rm gen}(M)\leq|\mathcal{M}|\leq 2^{|R|}.

Proof.

Observe first that
\mathcal{M}
is an infinite set, because

is not semi-simple. Given

, then

is finitely generated invoking Lemma

, so we may fix a finitely generated submodule

such that

. Therefore, one writes that

since

. Thus, it automatically follows that

\text{\rm gen}(M)\leq|\omega\times\mathcal{M}|=|\mathcal{M}|\leq 2^{|R|},

as requested.
∎

The following statement could be useful.

Proposition 1.9
.

If
M
is a module which is not Bassian, then it contains a countable generated submodule which is not Hopfian.

Proof.

Let
M
be non-Bassian and let
N\leq M
be a non-zero submodule such that
\iota:M\to M/N
is an embedding. Let
0\neq x\in N
and define a sequence of elements

inductively by the rule
x_{0}=x
and
x_{n+1}+N=\iota(x_{n})
. Put
X:=\sum_{n\geq 0}x_{n}R
and note that

\iota(X)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\iota(x_{n})R=\sum_{n\geq 1}(x_{n}R+N)/N=\sum_{n\geq 0}(x_{n}R+N)/N=(X+N)/N,

because
x_{0}\in N
. Thus,
\iota
injectively maps
X
onto
(X+N)/N\cong X/(X\cap N)
and
x_{0}\in X\cap N\neq(0)
. We thereby have proved that
X
is countably generated non-Hopfian submodule.
∎

In the rest of the section, we describe super Bassian modules over non-commutative generalization of Dedekind domains. To that end, we need to recall some useful notions from
[
7
]
.

Let
M
be a module. We denote by
\Sing(M)
the
singular submodule
of

and we say that

is

if
\Sing(M)=(0)
, it is

if
\Sing(M)=M
, and it is

if
M/\Sing(M)=\Sing(M/\Sing(M))
. The abbreviation
B\trianglelefteq M
means that
B
is an
essential submodule
of
M
and a

ring means a

ring. Likewise, a
non-primitive Dedekind prime
ring is HNP with the classical ring of fractions

such that it is
not
primitive and every non-zero ideal is invertible in

. For some basic properties of these notions and other non-explained terminology, we refer to
[
7
]
.

We now formulate and prove the following useful technicality.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
R
be a right non-singular ring, and
A
,
B
submodules of a module
M
. Then,

(1)

if
B\trianglelefteq M
, then
M/B
is singular.

(2)

if
A\trianglelefteq B
and
M/B
is singular, then
M/A
is singular.

(3)

the following is equivalent:

(a)

M/B
is singular.

(b)

B+\Sing(M)\trianglelefteq M
.

(c)

there exists a submodule
H
of
B
such that
H\cap\Sing(M)=(0)
and
M/H
is singular.

Proof.

(1) It is pretty clear, because
xR\cap B\trianglelefteq xR
for each non-zero
x\in M
.

(2) Since both
B/A
and
M/B
are singular, the quotient
M/A
is a Goldie-radical module. As
R
is right non-singular, the assertion follows at once from
[
7
, Remark 11.3]
.

(3) Set
S:=\Sing(M)
.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b). Proving indirectly, we assume that
B+S
is not essential in

, i.e., there exists non-zero

such that
mR\cap(B+S)=(0)
. Thus,
mR\cap B=(0)
, hence
mR
embeds into
M/B
. As
mR
is non-singular,
M
is not a singular module.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c). Let
H
be a maximal submodule of
B
with respect to the condition
H\cap S=(0)
. Thus,
H+S\trianglelefteq B+S\trianglelefteq M
and so

is singular by (1). Furthermore,
(S+H)/H
is singular, which gives that

is Goldie-radical. Therefore,

is singular again applying
[
7
, Remark 11.3]
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a). It is straightforward.
∎

We are now prepared to establish the following necessary condition for a super Bassian module.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
R
be a non-primitive HNP ring, and let
F
be a finitely generated submodule of a module

. If
M/F
is singular such that the

-primary component of

is Noetherian for each maximal invertible ideal

, then

is super Bassian.

Proof.

First, note that

is non-singular, because it is obviously hereditary. We will show, for arbitrary factor
\tilde{M}
of

, that it satisfies the hypothesis of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, i.e., that
\tilde{M}/\Sing(\tilde{M})
is finite dimensional and every primary component of
\Sing(\tilde{M})
is Noetherian, which will suffice to get the desired assertion.

To that target, let
X
be an arbitrary submodule of
M
, and denote
\tilde{M}=M/X
,
\tilde{F}=F+X/X
and

\tilde{S}=\Sing(\tilde{M})
.

Since
M/F
is singular, observe that
\tilde{M}/(\tilde{F}+\tilde{S})
is singular as well. Furthermore,
[
7
, Proposition 1.1]
reaches us that
\tilde{M}/\tilde{S}
is non-singular, whence

(\tilde{F}+\tilde{S})/\tilde{S}\trianglelefteq\tilde{M}/\tilde{S}
employing Lemma
1.10
. As

is finitely generated and

is Noetherian, one knows that
(\tilde{F}+\tilde{S})/\tilde{S}
is Noetherian too, which means that
\tilde{M}/\tilde{S}
has finite Goldie dimension.

Next, suppose that
P
is a maximal invertible ideal of

. Thanks to Lemma
1.10
, there exists a submodule
\tilde{H}
of
\tilde{F}
such that
\tilde{H}\cap\tilde{S}=(0)
and
\tilde{M}/\tilde{H}
is singular. Then,
\tilde{S}
and so the

-primary component
\tilde{S}(P)
of
\tilde{S}
embeds into
\tilde{M}/\tilde{H}
. Moreover,
(\tilde{S}(P)+\tilde{F})/\tilde{F}
embeds into the
P
-primary component of
\tilde{M}/\tilde{F}
, which is a quotient of the

-primary component of

viewing
[

. Hence,

(\tilde{S}(P)+\tilde{F})/\tilde{F}\cong\tilde{S}(P)/(\tilde{S}(P)\cap\tilde{F})

is Noetherian by hypothesis. Since
\tilde{S}(P)\cap\tilde{F}
is clearly Noetherian, we have proved that
\tilde{S}(P)
is Noetherian as well.

Consequently, it remains to apply
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, which claims that
\tilde{M}
is Bassian. This proves that
M
is super Bassian after all, as wanted.
∎

Before to proceed, we come to the following.

Lemma 1.12
.

If
M
is a super Bassian module over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring, then there exists a finitely generated non-singular submodule

of a module

such that

is singular.

Proof.

First, observe that a Dedekind prime ring is HNP and so a non-singular Goldie prime ring (see, e.g.,
[

, Remark 2]
), which means that we may apply Lemma

and
[

.

Put

and notice that, by Lemma

, there exists a submodule

of

such that

,

is singular, and

is non-singular. Assume to contrary that

has infinite Goldie dimension. Then, looking at

, Lemma 4.3]
, there exists a free submodule

of

of infinite rank.

Now, denote by

a maximal submodule of

, and suppose that

is an arbitrary maximal invertible ideal of

. Since

G\oplus FP\oplus S\ \ \trianglelefteq\ \ G\oplus F\oplus S\ \ \trianglelefteq\ \ B\oplus S\ \ \trianglelefteq\ \ M,

we obtain that

is singular again with the aid of Lemma

. Note that

is Bassian and

. But, as

Thus,

The following conditions are equivalent for a module

is Bassian, all primary components of

.

(2)

(3)

Let

(1)

If

(a)

is super Bassian.

(b)

(c)

A module

of

Example 1.16

If

2.
Hereditarily Bassian Modules

We start here by recalling once again the second version of the strengthened notion of a Bassian module.

Definition
.

Let
R
be a ring. A right
R
-module
M
is said to be a
hereditarily Bassian
module if every submodule is Bassian.

Observe immediately that the class of all hereditarily Bassian modules is closed under submodules.

Our first preliminary observations concern the necessary condition of being a hereditarily Bassian module over a semi-perfect ring.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
M
is a hereditarily Bassian module over a semi-perfect ring, then the socle of
M
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Showing indirectly, assume that
\Soc(M)
is infinitely generated. Since there exists only finitely many non-isomorphic simple modules,
\Soc(M)
contains a submodule of a module
S^{(\omega)}
for a suitable simple module
S
, which is not Bassian, and thus
M
is not hereditarily Bassian.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Any finitely generated module over a right Noetherian ring is hereditarily Bassian.

Proof.

It is apparent, since every Noetherian module is Bassian, every submodule of a Noetherian module is Noetherian as well, and every finitely generated module over a right Noetherian ring is Noetherian too.
∎

Now, we are ready to characterize hereditarily Bassian modules over quasi-Frobenius rings.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following three conditions are equivalent for a module
M
over a quasi-Frobenius ring.

(1)

M
is hereditarily Bassian.

(2)

M
is finitely generated.

(3)

\operatorname{Soc}(M)
of
M
is finitely generated.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3). As a quasi-Frobenius ring is semi-perfect, every hereditarily Bassian module has finitely generated socle by Lemma
2.1
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2). Since a quasi-Frobenius ring is (right) Artinian, the module
M
is semi-Artinian, hence
\Soc(M)
is essential in
M
. Furthermore, the injective envelope
E(\Soc(M))\cong E(M)
of finitely generated
\Soc(M)
is a finitely generated projective module, which proves that
E(M)
as well as
M
are Noetherian.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Since a quasi-Frobenius ring is (right) Noetherian, the assertion follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

A natural question which immediately arises is of whether or not the previous claim remains true for right Artinian rings. Before we answer it, we need to prove two assertions. The first one is an elementary but helpful technical observation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Q,S,X,Y
be submodules of a module
A
such that
Q\subseteq S\cap X
and
X\subseteq Y
. If
S/Q=\Soc(A/Q)
and
\Soc(A/X)\subseteq Y/X
, then
S\subseteq Y
.

Proof.

Since
(S+X)/X\cong((S/Q)+(X/Q)/(X/Q)
is a semi-simple module, we get

(S+X)/X\subseteq\Soc(A/X)\subseteq Y/X,

which immediately implies
S\subseteq S+X\subseteq Y
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
M
be a semi-Artinian module over a semi-local ring
R
with the Jacobson radical
J
such that
\Soc(M)
is finitely generated. If there exists natural
n
satisfying
MJ^{n}=(0)
, then
M
is Bassian.

Proof.

Proving indirectly the stated claim, we assume that
M
is not Bassian. Then, there exists
X\neq(0)
and an embedding
\nu:M\to M/X
, i.e.,
M\cong\nu(M)
. Set
X_{0}:=0
,
X_{1}:=X
,
M_{0}:=M
, and let
M_{1}
be a submodule of
M
satisfying
X_{1}\subseteq M_{1}
and
M_{1}/X_{1}=\nu(M)
. Since

M\cong M_{0}/X_{0}\cong M_{1}/X_{1},

we can proceed by induction to obtain sequences of submodules
(X_{n}\mid n<\omega)
and
(M_{n}\mid n<\omega)
such that
X_{i}\subsetneq X_{i+1}
,
M_{i}\supseteq M_{i+1}
and
M_{i}/X_{i}\cong M
for each
i<\omega
. Put
A:=\bigcup_{n<\omega}X_{n}
. Since
A
is semi-Artinian, there exists a socle sequence
(S_{\alpha}\mid S_{\alpha}<\kappa)
of
A
, i.e., a smooth sequence of submodules such that

S_{0}=0,~S_{\alpha+1}/S_{\alpha}=\Soc(A/S_{\alpha})~{\rm and}~A=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}S_{\alpha}.

We will show now that
\kappa
is infinite. First, we will prove by induction on
\alpha
that, for each
\alpha<\min(\omega,\kappa)
, there exists an index
i_{\alpha}<\omega
for which
S_{\alpha}\subseteq X_{i_{\alpha}}
. As
S_{0}=X_{0}=(0)
, we may put
i_{0}=0
. Let
S_{\alpha}\subseteq X_{i_{\alpha}}
. Since
\Soc(A/X_{i_{\alpha}})\subseteq\Soc(M_{i_{\alpha}}/X_{i_{\alpha}})
is finitely generated and

A/X_{i_{\alpha}}=\bigcup_{n>i_{\alpha}}X_{n}/X_{i_{\alpha}},

there exists an index
k>i_{\alpha}
such that
\Soc(X_{k}/X_{i_{\alpha}})=\Soc(A/X_{i_{\alpha}})
. However, as
S_{\alpha+1}/S_{\alpha}=\Soc(A/S_{\alpha})
, we may apply Lemma
2.4
claiming that
S_{\alpha+1}\subseteq X_{k}
. We next put
i_{\alpha+1}:=k
, thus finishing the induction.

Now, if we assume that
\kappa
is finite, we get that

A=S_{\kappa-1}\subseteq X_{i_{\kappa-1}}\subsetneq A,

an obvious contradiction.

Thus,
A
and so
M
have infinite socle lengths, which means that
MJ^{n}\neq(0)
for each
n<\omega
.
∎

Now, we are able to generalize Proposition
2.3
in the following way.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
R
be a semi-local ring with nilpotent Jacobson radical and let
M
be a module. Then,
M
is hereditarily Bassian if, and only if,
\Soc(M)
is finitely generated.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
. It follows directly from Lemma
2.1
.

(\Leftarrow)
. If
\Soc(M)
is finitely generated, then the socle of every submodule
N
of
M
is finitely generated. Furthermore, there exists
n
such that
J^{n}=(0)
for the Jacobson radical of
R
, whence
NJ^{n}=N0=(0)
. Now, it remains to employ Lemma
2.5
getting the desired conclusion.
∎

Note that the hypothesis of the previous theorem is satisfied by every one-sided Artinian ring. The following example unambiguously shows that the condition (2) of Proposition
2.3
is
not
an equivalent condition of hereditarily Bassian modules over right Artinian rings.

Example 2.7
.

Let

R=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Q}&\mathbb{R}\\
0&\mathbb{R}\end{pmatrix}~\text{and}~M=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{R}\\
\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{R}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, a simple inspection demonstrates that
R
is a right Artinian ring (which is not left Artinian),
M_{R}
has a natural structure of a right
R
-module, and

\Soc(M_{R})=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbb{R}\\
0&\mathbb{R}\end{pmatrix}

is a finitely generated
R
-module. Thus,
M_{R}
is an infinitely generated module which is hereditarily Bassian viewing Theorem
2.6
.

So, there are right Artinian rings
R
over which the injective envelope
E(R_{R})=E(\Soc(R_{R}))
is definitely not finitely generated.

Similarly, as in the case of super Bassian modules, we now characterize hereditarily Bassian modules over non-primitive Dedekind prime rings.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
F
and
N
be submodules of a module
M
over a right hereditary right Noetherian ring
R
. If
F
is finitely generated and
M/F
is singular, then there exists a finitely generated submodule
G
of
N
such that
N/G
is singular.

Proof.

Firstly, denote by
S
the singular submodule of
M
and observe that
\Sing(N)=S\cap N
. Since
M/F
is singular,
F+S\trianglelefteq M
by Lemma
1.10
(3). As
M/S
is non-singular and
M/(F+S)
is singular, one checks that
(F+S)/S\trianglelefteq M/S
again by Lemma
1.10
(3). Thus,

((N+S)/S)\cap((F+S)/S)\trianglelefteq(N+S)/S.

Clearly,
(F+S)/S
is Noetherian, hence the module
((N+S)/S)\cap((F+S)/S)
is finitely generated. This guarantees existence of a finitely generated submodule
G
of
N
such that

G+S=(N+S)\cap(F+S),

which is an essential submodule of the module
N+S
. Finally,

G+(S\cap N)=(G+S)\cap N\trianglelefteq N

via the modular law, and

N/(G+(S\cap N))=N/(G+\Sing(N))

is singular. Therefore, Lemma
1.10
informs us that
N/G
is singular as well, finishing the argumentation.
∎

We now have at our disposal all the machinery needed to prove the following equivalencies.

Theorem 2.9
.

The following items are equivalent for a module
M
over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring:

(1)

M
is hereditarily Bassian.

(2)

Each primary component of
\Sing(M)
is Noetherian and there exists a finitely generated non-singular submodule
G
of
M
such that
M/G
is singular.

(3)

Each primary component of
\Sing(M)
is finitely generated and there exists a finitely generated submodule
G
of
M
such that
M/G
is singular.

Proof.

Put
S:=\Sing(M)
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Since
S
is Bassian, each primary component of
S
is Noetherian in accordance with
[
7
, Theorem 1]
. However, from Lemma
1.10
follows the existence of a submodule
H
of
M
such that
H\cap S=(0)
and
M/H
is singular. Evidently,
H
is non-singular. Using the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
1.12
and assuming
H
has infinite dimension, we obtain a free submodule
F
of
H
which must have infinite rank bearing in mind
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
. This, in turn, contradicts the hypothesis that all submodules of
M
are Bassian. We thus have shown that
H
has infinite dimension, hence it contains a finitely generated essential submodule
G
. Now, Lemma
1.10
suggests that
M/G
is singular, because

G\oplus S\trianglelefteq H\oplus S\trianglelefteq M.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). This implication is straightforward.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1). First, observe that every submodule of
M
also satisfies condition (3). Let
N
be a submodule of
M
, and
P
a maximal invertible ideal, then
\Sing(N)=S\cap N
, whence the
P
-primary component of
\Sing(N)
is a submodule of the
P
-primary component of
S
. As
R
is Noetherian,
S(P)
, and so
\Sing(N)(P)
, is Noetherian as well. The existence of finitely generated submodule
H
of
N
such that
N/H
is singular follows now from Lemma
2.8
.

Resuming, we have just shown that it suffices to prove, without loss of generality, that
M
is Bassian by application of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
. In fact, we have already noted that each primary component of
S
is Noetherian. Since
M/G
is singular, we get
G+S\trianglelefteq M
, and so
(G+S)/S\trianglelefteq M/S
in virtue of Lemma
1.10
. Furthermore, as
(G+S)/S
is finitely generated, the quotient
M/S
has finite dimension. We thus have verified the hypothesis of
[
7
, Theorem 3]
, implying that
M
is Bassian, and we are set.
∎

As two subsequent consequences, we deduce:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
M
be a module over a non-primitive Dedekind prime ring.

(1)

If
M
is super Bassian, then
M
is hereditarily Bassian.

(2)

If
M
is singular, the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

M
is hereditarily Bassian.

(b)

M
is super Bassian.

(c)

M
is Bassian.

(3)

A non-singular module
M
is hereditarily Bassian if, and only if,
M
is of finite Goldie dimension.

Proof.

(1) Let
M
be super Bassian and set
S:=\Sing(M)
. Then, Theorem
1.13
insures that there exists a finitely generated submodule
H
of
M
such that
M/H
is singular,
H\cap S=(0)
, and each primary component of
M/H
is Noetherian. Since
S
is embeddable into
M/H
, each primary component of
S
is Noetherian as well, and hence
M
is hereditarily Bassian in view of Theorem
2.9
.

(2) It follows from combination of Corollary
1.14
(1) and Theorem
2.9
.

(3) It is obvious.
∎

The following assertion reformulates Theorem
2.9
in the commutative case.

Corollary 2.11
.

A module
M
over a Dedekind domain is hereditarily Bassian if, and only if, all primary components of
M
are finitely generated and there exists a finitely generated torsion-free submodule
F
of
M
such that
M/F
is torsion.

In case of Abelian groups, one can say that
a (reduced) group is Bassian if, and only if, it is hereditarily Bassian
(compare with
[
3
]
and
[
5
]
).

We now finish this section with two examples which illustrates that the notions of super Bassian and hereditarily Bassian modules are independent one another, thus motivating a serious further research examination in order to obtain their complete structural characterization.

Example 2.12
.

The Abelian group
\mathbb{Q}
of rank 1 is hereditarily Bassian looking at Corollary
2.10
(3), and since it is well-known that
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\cong\bigoplus_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\infty}}
, it follows at once that it need not be super Bassian adapting Corollary
1.15
.

Example 2.13
.

Let
K
be field,
V
a vector space over
K
of infinite dimension, and denote by
R
a trivial extension of
K
by
V
, that is,
R/J(R)\cong K
,
J(R)\cong V
and
J(R)^{2}=(0)
. Then,
R
is a local perfect ring such that the module
R_{R}
is super Bassian (and so Hopfian), but
R_{R}
is not hereditarily Bassian as
J(R)
is not Bassian.
