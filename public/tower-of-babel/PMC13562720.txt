Limitations of the dissipative quantum Fisher information in Liouville space
Authors: Tatiana Iakovleva, Ruvi Lecamwasam, Jason Twamley
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOpen quantum system dynamics traditionally described by a quantum master equation can be effectively reformulated in the Liouville space of vectorised density matrices. Despite an extensive use of the Liouville space framework to describe quantum states evolution, its application to quantum metrology is not well explored. Several papers have studied a Liouville-space generalisation of the quantum Fisher information, called the dissipative quantum Fisher information (DQFI). We perform a comparative study between the quantum Fisher information (QFI) in Hilbert space and DQFI, deriving an explicit relation between the two quantities for qubit, qudit, and harmonic oscillator systems. The derived relations are nontrivial, and depend on the state’s purity. We show that for a single qubit, the QFI can be efficiently recovered from the DQFI via a simple mapping, while in higher dimensions the expression becomes convoluted and does not convert to a compact form. We find examples where the DQFI is neither an upper nor lower bound for the Hilbert space QFI, and suggest that the informational content of the DQFI itself cannot be straightforwardly interpreted. Our results clarify the limitations of the Liouville space based approach and structural differences with the QFI in the context of quantum parameter estimation problem.Subject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Parameter estimation plays an important role in modern science, and advances in this field have led to such scientific breakthroughs as detection of gravitational waves1, improvement in magnetometers2, gravimeters3 and other force sensors as well as paving new directions such as dark matter search4.

The quantum Fisher information (QFI) is one of the central tools in quantum sensing and metrology5–8. The QFI quantifies, through the Cramer-Rao bound, the ultimate sensitivity with which one can estimate a parameter of interest θ encoded in a quantum system9–12. The QFI has diverse applications in areas including quantum metrology6,13, computing5,14, information theory7, and error correction15, as well as in quantum channel estimation16.

The QFI calculation in the unitary encoding case is usually straightforward14,17–19. However, realistic systems couple to an environment and dissipative processes come into play; as a consequence, the evaluation of the QFI becomes more difficult analytically20. The QFI has also been explored in such open system settings as non-Markovian environment21,22 with parameter-dependent dissipators23, non-phase-covariant noise present in the system24, and estimation of a spectral density subject to Gaussian noise channels25. Sensitivity limits for continuously monitored open quantum systems have also been studied in the works26,27, while in28 the authors show how a phase transition can be used to achieve enhanced sensitivity. Despite this rich research background, practical computation of the QFI in generic open quantum systems remains challenging, thus motivating the search for computationally simpler methods.

Liouville space is a well-known technique for analytical treatment of the open quantum systems dynamics29. Within this higher-dimensional Liouville space, mixed quantum states are represented as vectors and the dynamics is governed by linear superoperators. The Liouville space formalism can be traced back to works from the 1970s, where it was widely used for master equation treatment30,31, also known as the thermo field dynamics32. The attractiveness of this method lies in its amenability to analytic treatments such as Lie-algebraic technique for open quantum systems dynamics33–35. Recent applications of the Liouville space framework include the real-time dynamics evaluation of thermal states36, studies on the relationship between environmental noise and complexity37 and quantum algorithms38,39. In this paper we will explore whether there is a way to obtain information about the QFI in such cases by mapping the evolution governed by the master equation to Liouville space.

Liouville space is a useful mathematical concept, however, to calculate the QFI one needs to convert the resulting quantum state back to Hilbert space. Moreover, the geometric structure of this space has not yet been extensively studied in the context of the quantum parameter estimation problem. In the works40,41, the authors consider the dissipative evolution in the Liouville space of vectorised density matrices and derive a formula for the QFI, the so-called dissipative quantum Fisher information (DQFI). It is important to note that the DQFI can be calculated directly from the Liouville space state vector of the system, unlike the QFI, which generally requires computation of the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) operator. In this sense, the DQFI may offer a computational advantage, much like the the Hilbert-Schmidt statistical speed42,43, which has been shown to provide an efficient alternative in parameter estimation tasks where the QFI tends to be computationally demanding.

In this work, we aim at a conceptual understanding of the DQFI. The DQFI is derived precisely in analogy to the QFI. However, it is is not well-known what information the DQFI contains, if the QFI and DQFI show qualitatively similar behaviour, and why differences arise between the two.

We find that the DQFI can show very different qualitative and quantitative behaviour compared to the QFI. In general, the relation between them can be very complicated. As a result of our studies we remain uncertain as to the usefulness of the DQFI for metrological purposes.

Does the DQFI provide a bound on the QFI?

In the previous section, we established a connection between the DQFI and QFI for qubit and qudit systems. It is natural to ask whether the DQFI can be considered a bound on the QFI in Hilbert space. In the work40, the authors derive inequalities relating the QFI and DQFI. However, these inequalities involve complex functions of the quantum state. In this section we study two more general examples showing an unexpected behaviour of the DQFI, demonstrating that it does not bound the QFI either from above or below.

Thermometry with a thermal state of a quantum harmonic oscillator

Temperature is one of the most important physical quantities, and the metrological task of thermometry has been extensively studied. Conclusion

In this work we investigated the dissipative quantum Fisher information (DQFI), defined by analogy with the QFI for pure states, applied to the Liouville space state vector. The motivation for this is to study metrology of open quantum systems, a crucial task for the development of real-world quantum technology, and tests of fundamental physics.

However, fundamentally it is the QFI that has physical meaning. We have found analytical relationships between the two for metrology problems in qubits, qudits, thermal states of harmonic oscillators, and coupled two-level systems. These show that there is a highly nontrivial relationship between the two. Thus given knowledge of the DQFI, it is not at all straightforward to translate this to knowledge of the QFI, and hence parameter estimation. Moreover, the DQFI does not act as a bound on the QFI, as it can be lower, higher, or even oscillate between lower and higher in the same problem. Bounds on the QFI in terms of the DQFI were derived in40. However these can show significant divergence from the QFI, both quantitatively and qualitatively. Thus precise physical meaning and utility of the DQFI remains elusive.

Our work demonstrates that the DQFI contains a mixture of information about changes in the parameter, and also changes in purity of the state. This is interesting, given that the two quantities are introduced with identical motivation to geometrically quantify the distinguishability of perturbations to the quantum state. We argue that this throws doubt on if the DQFI is indeed the correct informational quantifier for metrology with open quantum systems.

Liouville space is a useful tool to study the dynamics of open quantum systems. For lower-dimensional systems, such as a single qubit or two coupled qubits, one can remain in Liouville space, exploiting the linear structure of dynamics. However, in general cases, the connection between the QFI and DQFI is nontrivial and may still require knowledge of the symmetric logarithmic derivative, limiting the computational advantage of the Liouville space approach. In comparison, other methods for open quantum system’s dynamics, like Stinespring dilation theorem, involve purification to a larger Hilbert space, followed by projection back to the original space. In such framework, one may require to perform optimization over pure states to calculate the QFI. Moreover, these maps bypass the necessity to consider measurements in Liouville space, and allow one to evaluate the QFI in Hilbert space where the physical interpretation of statistical distance and measurement precision is physically valid.

This work considered only single parameter estimation, and we studied only time-independent Liouvillians. An extension to a multiparameter estimation and time-dependent operators can be seen as a natural generalisation of this work. Additionally, one could consider a non-Markovian dynamics. Moreover, a key advantage of Liouville space is its amenability to Lie algebraic techniques, and it would perhaps be interesting to study Lie algebraic reformulation of the DQFI.
