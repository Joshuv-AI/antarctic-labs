Development and performance evaluation of macropore intelligent plugging hydrogels
Authors: Fang Shi, Weibin Jin, Jingchun Wu, Chunlong Zhang, Bo Zhao, Jian Li, Bauyrzhan Sarsenbekuly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPolymer hydrogels are the optimal hydrogels for water channeling control applications, and water-dominant channels in deep reservoirs are often blocked by them.A dual-network hydrogel system (DN-Gel) is innovatively proposed in this study. A chemical crosslinking network (ACL-HG) is constructed via glycidyl methacrylate (GMA), poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate (PEGMA), and dopamine methacrylamide (DMA), and is composited with a physical crosslinking network (GQDs-Gel) of functionalized graphene quantum dots (GQDs-GX)/ascorbic acid. A dense dual-network structure is formed through the synergistic effect of Michael addition reaction and hydrogen bonding.FTIR and SEM characterization results show that DN-Gel possesses both the functional group characteristics of the chemical crosslinking network and the nano-reinforcement effect of the physical crosslinking network, and its cross-sectional pore structure is significantly denser than that of single-network hydrogels.Under the optimal ratio and concentration, the viscosity reduction rates of DN-Gel both before and after three shearing cycles within 56 days are maintained below 20%.Under high-temperature and high-salinity conditions, a mass retention rate of over 80.6% is achieved after 60 days.Core simulation results show that DN-Gel has a breakthrough pressure gradient of 129.21 MPa/m and a permeability change rate as high as 99.21%, which is significantly improved compared with traditional plugging agents.This study provides a new material design method and theoretical basis for the green and efficient plugging of sandstone reservoirs.Keywords: Water-swelling adhesive hydrogel, Dual-network structure, High-permeability reservoirSubject terms: Chemistry, Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

In the development process of sandstone reservoirs, water channeling is considered one of the constraining factors that limit high and stable production of the reservoir1–3.The water channeling problem in high-permeability sandstone reservoirs is targeted.Hydrogel materials have been frequently applied to issues such as water plugging in high-permeability layers due to their properties like high water absorption and swelling capacity as well as self-healing ability4–6.However, in the actual process of selecting hydrogels, emphasis is often placed solely on the physicochemical properties of the hydrogel itself, such as strength stability, humidity response time, and mechanical-adhesive performance7–9.The physicochemical interactions between the gel and the rock interface are frequently overlooked. Therefore, hydrogels used to address water intrusion in high-permeability sandstone reservoirs should be endowed with strong tensile and compressive resistance, and possess good stability and effective permeability modification under high-temperature and high-salinity environments10–12.Gel-based profile control agents, due to the high gel strength generated by their three-dimensional network structure, have been shown to have significant advantages in solving water channeling and have achieved good results, as shown in Table 1 below.

In practical application processes, acrylamide-based hydrogels have been widely used as water channeling treatment agents for high-permeability sandstone due to their high swelling rate. However, problems such as the attenuation of mechanical properties under dynamic stress, the failure of adhesion at gas-liquid interfaces, and chemical aging and structural degradation have been faced in its application under underground working conditions. In the use stage, the chemical degradation products of polyacrylamide hydrogels pose ecological toxicity, readily causing acute toxicity to aquatic organisms and suppressing soil microbial activity19,20. Consequently, a novel hydrogel sealing system that is practical, reliable, and whose decomposed elements are pollution-free is urgently needed. The performance challenges faced by single crosslinked network hydrogels in practical applications can be addressed by introducing another hydrogel network to complement their properties. Network hydrogels enhance the performance of hydrogels through the synergistic effect of two networks, while preserving the unique properties of each individual network21–23. Dual network hydrogels achieve a dynamic balance between mechanical strength and toughness through the collaborative interpenetrating structure of rigid and flexible networks, along with precise regulation of their proportions. They have emerged as the core development trend for the next generation of sealing materials. Their design concept stems from the structural mimicry of biological tissues such as cartilage and tendons in bionics, where the rigid covalent network bears the primary load, while the dynamic, reversible physical crosslinking network dissipates energy and inhibits crack propagation24,25.

As a natural organic polymer material, lignin can not only improve the water absorption, hydrophilicity and mechanical properties of hydrogel, but also improve the thermal stability and biodegradability.Lignin hydrogels are rich, environmentally friendly, biocompatible and biodegradable26. As a water channeling inhibitor, after being used for a period of time, if the hydrogel needs to be discarded, it can be decomposed by microorganisms in the formation into harmless small molecular substances and will not cause long-term pollution to the formation and the surrounding environment27.The crosslinking properties of water molecules rapidly induce the rearrangement of amphiphilic polymers at interfaces. This rearrangement, facilitated by reversible electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding, enhances the structural stability of the hydrogel, allowing it to maintain excellent performance even in water-intruded environments28,29.The catechol structure, as an effective structure for the strong underwater adhesion of mussels, is capable of forming non-covalent interactions such as hydrogen bonds, metal coordination bonds, and π-π bonds with multiple surfaces. It can exhibit good adhesion to rocks underwater, and this structure can realize intelligent and selective viscosity increase when in contact with water30,31.In this paper, considering the aforementioned factors, lignin monomers, amphiphilic polymer monomers, and catechol monomers are selected to form multivalent bonding, resulting in a highly stable and multifunctional “cross-linking” effect.Incorporating nanomaterials into hydrogels for physical crosslinking is a commonly used and effective method to enhance their mechanical strength. Graphene quantum dots (GQDs) exhibit structural stability, a high specific surface area, and multiple active sites, conferring structural advantages in functional modifications.Hydrogels prepared using GQDs can be dispersed within other gels, bolstering the overall structural stability of double-network hydrogels32,33.A double-network structural design composed of chemical-physical-chemical crosslinking is formed.The crosslinking density of the hydrogel network can be increased.The mechanical properties of the system are effectively enhanced see fig 1.

Spontaneous underwater adhesion mechanism.

This study innovatively proposes a chemical crosslinking network constructed by glycidyl methacrylate (GMA), poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate (PEGMA), and dopamine methacrylamide (DMA).The physical crosslinking network constructed by graphene quantum dots (GQDs-GX) and ascorbic acid (L(+)) is combined with the aforementioned chemical crosslinking network.A water-thickening dual-network hydrogel is constructed.In this system, DMA and GMA are used in the chemical crosslinking part to carry out Michael addition, and a branched structure is formed.The bifunctional characteristic of PEGMA is utilized to enable it to act as a “linker arm”.Different branched chains are bridged via two Michael addition reactions, and a crosslinking network with a specific crosslinking density is formed.In the physical crosslinking part, a physical crosslinking network is formed through intermolecular forces between graphene quantum dots (GQDs-GX) and ascorbic acid (L(+)).The structure of the synthesized product is characterized by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy and scanning electron microscopy (SEM).Shear resistance experiments, high-temperature stability experiments, high-temperature and salt tolerance experiments, and core permeability change experiments are carried out in this study.Characteristics including shear stability, high-temperature stability, salt tolerance, spontaneous underwater adhesion, and water-thickening property are exhibited by this hydrogel.It demonstrates great potential in plugging large pores of high-permeability reservoirs.

DN-Gel dual-network hydrogel ratio optimization

Optimize the system ratio

The effective substance concentration of the hydrogel is fixed at 1500 mg/L before crosslinking.The influences of different mass ratios (ACL-HG to GQDs Gel) on system viscosity and gelation time are studied, and the experimental results are depicted in Fig. 6.The experimental results reveal that the system viscosity increases remarkably with the increasing mass proportion of ACL-HG, peaking at 3922 mPa·s at a mass ratio of 1.5:1.ACL-HG is a chemical crosslinked network that forms a rigid and dense structure through covalent bonding.GQDs-Gel is a physical crosslinked network with lower structural stability than chemical crosslinking.An increased proportion of ACL-HG leads to a significant rise in the number and density of chemical crosslinking points, strengthening the confinement of molecular chains by covalent bonds. This enhances intramolecular friction in the system, resulting in a macroscopic increase in viscosity.At a mass ratio below 1.5:1, the crosslinking points in ACL-HG’s chemical crosslinked network are unsaturated.The physical crosslinking of GQDs-Gel efficiently fills the pores of the chemical network, and their interpenetration forms a double network structure. This rapidly boosts crosslinking density and network entanglement, resulting in a remarkable viscosity increment.At a mass ratio above 1.5:1, the crosslinking points of ACL-HG’s chemical crosslinked network are saturated, and the low proportion of GQDs-Gel significantly weakens the pore-filling effect of physical crosslinking on the double network, resulting in a decreasing trend of system viscosity.The double network system takes over 14 days to attain final gelation, with no notable changes in the gel thereafter.Hence, the mass ratio of 1.5:1 is selected as the optimal experimental formulation for subsequent performance assessments.

Effect of Different Mass Ratios on the Viscosity of the Plugging System.

Optimization of the total mass concentration of the system

With the mass ratio of ACL-HG to GQDs Gel fixed at 1.5:1, the influences of different system concentrations on viscosity and gelation time are investigated, and the experimental results are depicted in Fig. 7.Increasing system concentration elevates the number of chemical and physical crosslinking points, enhancing molecular chain entanglement and collision frequency, which substantially boosts internal friction resistance.Hence, the system viscosity increases with increasing concentration.At 500 mg/L, the low system concentration results in a relatively large intermolecular distance, and the weak chemical and physical crosslinking strength fails to construct a dense double network structure.At concentrations above 500 mg/L, the system viscosity increases sharply within 1–7 days. Rapid synergy between chemical and physical crosslinking forms a dense double network, leading to a rapid viscosity rise with network formation.From 8 to 25 days, the double network structure stabilizes, with intermolecular interactions and network compactness reaching equilibrium, resulting in negligible changes in system viscosity.From 26 to 40 days, the system exhibits slight swelling. The reversibility of physical crosslinking leads to a marginal decrease in viscosity, yet the overall level remains high.The viscosity increment from 1500 to 2000 mg/L is notably smaller than that from 500 to 1000 mg/L.Once the concentration reaches a certain threshold, the viscosity-promoting effect of further increasing concentration diminishes gradually. Considering cost-performance balance, 1500 mg/L is selected as the optimal concentration for subsequent performance evaluations.

Effect of different concentrations on the viscosity of the plugging agent system.

For all subsequent experiments, the experimental materials are used with a mass ratio of m(ACL-HG): m(GQDs Gel) = 1.5:1 and a total system mass concentration of 1500 mg/L.

Conclusion

The optimal synthesis conditions for DN-Gel (predominantly consisting of ACL-HG and GQDs Gel) are a mass ratio of m(ACL-HG): m(GQDs Gel) = 1.5:1 and a total system mass concentration of 1500 mg/L.Under these conditions, the system exhibits a gelation time of ~ 14 days and a maximum viscosity of 3922 mPa·s, forming a stable double network structure.The chemical crosslinking network (ACL-HG) offers a rigid backbone via covalent bonds, while the physical crosslinking network (GQDs-Gel) fills the chemical network pores through weak interactions; their interpenetration and entanglement enhance the crosslinking density.Below a mass ratio of 1.5:1, physical crosslinking filling is inadequate; above this ratio, chemical crosslinking saturation induces network redundancy. At 1500 mg/L, the system exhibits high molecular chain entanglement alongside economic feasibility.

Synthesis of dual-network hydrogels

Synthesis of ACL-HG

Branched polymer (ACL) is synthesized via Michael addition with glycidyl methacrylate (GMA), poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate (PEGMA), and dopamine methacrylamide (DMA) as monomers. The monomer molar ratio is PEGMA: DMA: GMA = 2:2:1 (10 mmol PEGMA, 10 mmol DMA, 5 mmol GMA), and the synthetic route is illustrated in Fig. 12.

Synthetic route of polymer ACL.

The Michael addition reaction is carried out under alkaline conditions. Herein, 2 mmol triethylamine serves as the alkaline catalyst and nucleophile, and 50 g dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) is used as the reaction solvent. The reaction monomers and catalyst (weighed in batches) are added to a sealed, corrosion-resistant glass reactor with magnetic stirring. After stirring at 300 rpm for 30 min, nitrogen is purged to create an oxygen-free environment. Triethylamine is added slowly, and stirring is continued for 15 min. The reaction is conducted isothermally at 60 °C for 24 h, followed by the addition of initiator azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) under continuous stirring and another 24 h of isothermal reaction. Post-reaction, the mixture is transferred to a fritted funnel and extracted with diethyl ether multiple times to remove impurities. The crude product is vacuum-evaporated at 40 °C to remove diethyl ether, dissolved in THF to form a homogeneous solution, and slowly dropped into n-hexane for precipitation. After stirring at 300 rpm for 30 min, the precipitate is filtered and dried in a vacuum oven to obtain the target covalently crosslinked hydrogel ACL-HG.

Synthesis of GQDs gel

GQDs-Gel was prepared via a three-step method. Firstly, sulfonate-grafted functional monomers were synthesized by dispersing glyoxal and sodium p-aminobenzenesulfonate in deionized water at a molar ratio of 1.1:1, reacting at 25 °C for 8 h to form a functional modified solution. Secondly, amino-functionalized GQDs were synthesized via a bottom-up approach: 1,5-dinitronaphthalene and ammonia water (mass ratio 1:6) were dispersed in deionized water (reaction solvent), ultrasonicated at 140 W for 30 min, then transferred to a high-pressure reactor for reaction at 200 °C for 18 h. After cooling to room temperature, the product was centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 20 min to remove unreacted 1,5-dinitronaphthalene, and dialyzed with a dialysis bag (molecular weight cutoff: 1000) for 2 days to eliminate unreacted small molecules, yielding twice-purified amino-functionalized GQDs. Thirdly, functional graphene quantum dots (GQDs-GX) were synthesized by magnetically stirring amino-functionalized GQDs and the functional modified solution (molar ratio 1:10) at 25 °C for 6 h, followed by freeze-drying to obtain sulfonated GQDs-GX. The synthetic route is illustrated in Fig. 13. Subsequently, 20 mg of GQDs-GX was dissolved in deionized water and ultrasonically dispersed for 15 min. An appropriate concentration of reducing agent L(+)-ascorbic acid solution was added (mass ratio of GQDs-GX to L(+)-ascorbic acid = 1:2). The mixed dispersion was ultrasonically dissolved for 15 min, transferred to a polytetrafluoroethylene reactor, and reacted at 80 °C for 10 h. After the reactor cooled down post-reaction, the product was rinsed with deionized water and separated by suction filtration to obtain purified GQDs Gel.

Structural characterization of hydrogels

The structures of GQDs Gel, ACL-HG, and DN-Gel were characterized by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy and scanning electron microscopy (SEM). FT-IR spectroscopy was used to characterize the molecular structure and compare changes in chemical bonds before and after the reaction. The wavelength range of infrared light is 2.5–25 μm (or 4800–400 cm⁻¹), which can be absorbed, transmitted, reflected, scattered, or induce fluorescence (also known as the Raman effect) when interacting with samples. After absorbing mid-infrared light, molecules undergo vibrational and rotational changes to form an infrared absorption spectrum, with a dispersion system of 2 cm⁻¹, a scanning range of 400–4000 cm⁻¹, 32 scanning times, and a resolution of 4. The coating method was adopted for sample testing in this study, which involved applying or coating liquid samples onto salt plates or window slides to form liquid films for FT-IR analysis.

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) emits high-energy electron beams via an electron gun, which are focused into a nanoscale probe by electromagnetic lenses and scan the sample surface point-by-point under the control of scanning coils. With high resolution (up to 0.1–10 nm), it can clearly display the three-dimensional morphology of the sample surface, making it particularly suitable for microscopic observation of rough or complex structures. A FEI Quanta 450FEG SEM was used for sample scanning under the following test conditions: high vacuum mode and an accelerating voltage of 15 kV.

Performance evaluation

Apparent Viscosity and Gelation Time: The viscosity of the gel system in the experiment was determined using a rheometer, and the gelation time was defined as the time required for the measured viscosity of the system to reach 90% of the plateau value. The solvent water was laboratory-prepared simulated formation water with the formula shown in Table 5 (6778 mg/L), which was used for all subsequent system preparations. The viscosity of the gel system changes significantly during curing, so the appropriate rotor model must be selected based on the specific viscosity values obtained from the tests. To investigate the shear resistance of the adaptive gel system, a stirrer was used for pre-shearing treatment of pre-gelled gels with different concentrations.

Shear Stability Test: Shear tests of the adaptive gel systems after gelation were conducted on ACL-HG and DN-Gel. The selected shear rates were 500, 1000, and 1500 r/min. The first shear was performed 14 days after gelation, with a shear duration of 23 s (corresponding to the field fluid migration rate: 0.9 m/day).

High-Temperature Stability Test: Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was performed on the freeze-dried samples of ACL-HG and DN-Gel. The weight loss was measured while heating from 25 °C to 500 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C/min.

Temperature and Salt Resistance Test: DN-Gel samples were cut into cylinders using a standard compression mold. The mass of each sample was weighed and recorded, and the diameter and height were documented by photography. Each sample was placed in a high-temperature and high-pressure reactor, and completely immersed in brine with salinities of 8000, 10,000, and 12,000 mg/L (note: supplemented unit “mg/L” for clarity, consistent with previous simulated formation water specifications) prepared with sodium chloride. The reactor was then placed in an oven at 60 °C, 75 °C, and 90 °C respectively. Every 10 days, the samples were taken out to measure and record their mass, and their diameter and height were photographed for documentation.

Prospect

In the future, more direct characterization methods will be employed to clarify the interrelationship between the two gels in this study.Auxiliary chemical agents suitable for the double-network gel in this study are screened so that its on-site industrial application can be enhanced.Cyclic rheological tests are introduced to quantitatively demonstrate the self-healing behavior after shearing, and the self-healing capability of the system is verified from multiple perspectives.
