Introduction

Let
\mathcal{C}
be either the Hamilton quaternion algebra
\mathbb{H}
or the Cayley octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}

over the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
.
Then,
\mathcal{C}
is a composition algebra of dimension 4 or 8, respectively, over
\mathbb{R}
.
For each positive integer
n
,

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}
is a direct sum of central simple algebras over
\mathbb{R}

[
4
, Prop. 3.6(f), Prop. 3.9(b), and Prop. 3.12(a)]
:

[formula]

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{m=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{n}{\mathbb{S}}_{m}^{(n)}\mathcal{C}

such that
{\mathbb{S}}_{m}^{(n)}\mathcal{C}\cong T_{2m-n}\mathcal{C}:={\mathbb{S}}_{2m-n}^{(2m-n)}\mathcal{C}

for each integer
m
between
\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil
and
n
.
Here
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}
is the ring of symmetric tensors of
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes n}
,
i.e. the fixed ring of
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes n}
under the action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
defined by

\sigma(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{n})=x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}
for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{C}
.
For
x\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes n}
we denote
x^{\vee}:={1\over n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sigma(x)
.

Section 3 of
[
4
]
gives an explicit construction of an orthogonal system of idempotents:
for each integer
m
between
\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil
and
n
, a central idempotent

e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}\in{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}
is constructed
such that
{\mathbb{S}}_{m}^{(n)}\mathcal{C}={\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}\cdot e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
,

e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}e_{m^{\prime},\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}=0
if
m\neq m^{\prime}
, and

\sum_{m=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{n}e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}=1^{\otimes n}

[
4
, Lemma 3.4 and Prop. 3.6(c)]
.
In particular,
e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
commutes and associates with all elements of
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}
.

In this work we continue the work done in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]

and study further the structure of the simple components

{\mathbb{S}}_{m}^{(n)}\mathcal{C}\cong T_{2m-n}\mathcal{C}
of the decomposition
(*)
of
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathcal{C}
.
We embed the quaternion algebra
\mathbb{H}
inside the octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}
, so
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\subseteq{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{O}
.
The aim of the current study is to give explicitly a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents
(see Section
1
)
in
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\cdot e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
and in
\big({\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\big)\cdot e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
,
where
\mathbb{C}
is the field of complex numbers.
Note that
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\cdot e_{m,\mathbb{H}}^{(n)}={\mathbb{S}}^{(n)}_{m}\mathbb{H}

and
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\cdot e_{m,\mathbb{O}}^{(n)}
is an associative subalgebra of
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{O}
.
Using
(*)
, this will give a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in

\bigoplus_{m=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{n}{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\cdot e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
and in

\bigoplus_{m=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{n}\big({\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\big)\cdot e_{m,\mathcal{C}}^{(n)}
.

Section 6 of
[
4
]
studies the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{H}
, where we have by Wedderburn-Artin theorem
that the central simple algebra
T_{n}\mathbb{H}
is isomorphic
either to a matrix algebra over
\mathbb{R}
or to a matrix algebra over
\mathbb{H}
:

T_{n}\mathbb{H}\cong M_{n+1}(\mathbb{R})
, if

is even,
and
T_{n}\mathbb{H}\cong M_{{n+1\over 2}}(\mathbb{H})

In the ring of

matrices

over a division ring

the standard diagonal matrices

(with

at position

and

1.
Complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents

Let
R
be an associative ring, not necessarily commutative.
An element
x\in R
is an
idempotent
if
x^{2}=x
.
A set of idempotents
\{e_{i}\}
is said to be
orthogonal
if
e_{i}e_{j}=0
for all
i\neq j
.
Clearly, the sum of two orthogonal idempotents is also an idempotent.
Also, if
e
is any idempotent, then
e
and
1-e
are orthogonal idempotents.
The key result is the following.

Lemma 1.1
.

There is a
1\!\!-\!\!1
correspondence between a decomposition
R=I_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus I_{n}

as a direct sum of left ideals and
orthogonal idempotents
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
such that
e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}=1
.

Proof.

The correspondence is given as follows: for
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
,
write
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
with
x_{i}\in I_{i}
.
Then,
x_{i}=x_{i}\cdot 1=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{i}x_{j}

and each
x_{i}x_{j}\in I_{j}
since
I_{j}
is a left ideal of
R
.
Since
R
is the direct sum of the
I_{j}
’s we have
x_{i}x_{j}=0
for all
j\neq i
and
x_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
for all
i
.
Hence the
\{x_{i}\}
are orthogonal idempotents.

Conversely, given
\{e_{i}\}
, define for each
i
,
I_{i}:=Re_{i}
which is a left ideal of
R
.
Note that
R=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
since
1=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}
lies in
\sum_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
.
On the other hand, an element of
Re_{1}\cap(Re_{2}+\ldots+Re_{n})
can be written as

r_{1}e_{1}=r_{2}e_{2}+\ldots+r_{n}e_{n}
, with
r_{i}\in R
.
Then
r_{1}e_{1}=r_{1}e_{1}^{2}=r_{2}e_{2}e_{1}+\ldots+r_{n}e_{n}e_{1}=0
since the
e_{i}
’s are orthogonal.
Similarly,
Re_{i}\cap(Re_{1}+\ldots+Re_{i-1}+Re_{i+1}+\ldots+Re_{n})=\{0\}
for all
i
and thus

R=I_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus I_{n}
.

It remains to show that the two constructions are mutually inverse.
Indeed, start with
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
and write
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
with
e_{i}\in I
.
We need to show that
I_{i}=Re_{i}
.
Since
e_{i}\in I_{i}
, we have
I_{i}\supseteq Re_{i}
.
Conversely, if
x\in I_{i}
, write
x=x\cdot 1=\sum_{j=1}^{n}xe_{j}
.
By the above,
xe_{j}\in Re_{j}\subseteq I_{j}
.
Since
x\in I_{i}
, we get that
xe_{j}=0
for all
j\neq i
and so
x=xe_{i}\in Re_{i}
.

Finally, start with orthogonal
e_{i}
’s summing to
1
and let
I_{i}:=Re_{i}
.
Then,
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
with
e_{i}\in I_{i}
and this is the unique representation.
∎

Note that under the above correspondence,
I_{i}=\{0\}
if and only if
e_{i}=0
.
Also, any idempotent
e\in R
gives orthogonal
\{e,1-e\}
, therefore
R=Re\oplus R(1-e)
.

Now suppose that
R
is an artinian ring (and hence noetherian by the Hopkins-Levitzki theorem).
The Krull-Schmidt theorem says that
R
is a direct sum of finitely many indecomposable projective modules
I_{i}
.
Such modules correspond to primitive idempotents.

Definition 1.2
.

A
nonzero
idempotent
e
is said to be
primitive

if it cannot be written as a sum of nonzero orthogonal idempotents
e=f_{1}+f_{2}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

If
e
is an idempotent, then
Re
is indecomposable if and only if
e
is primitive.

Proof.

Suppose first that
e=f_{1}+f_{2}
is a sum of two orthogonal idempotents.
Then,
Rf_{1}\cap Rf_{2}=\{0\}
.
Indeed, if
xf_{1}=yf_{2}
, then
xf_{1}=xf_{1}^{2}=yf_{2}f_{1}=0
.
Clearly,
Re=R(f_{1}+f_{2})\subseteq Rf_{1}+Rf_{2}
.
Moreover, for
xf_{1}+yf_{2}\in Rf_{1}+Rf_{2}
, we have

xf_{1}+yf_{2}=(xf_{1}+yf_{2})(f_{1}+f_{2})\in Re
.
It follows that
Re=Rf_{1}\oplus Rf_{2}
.

Conversely, if
Re=I\oplus J
is a direct sum of two left ideals,
express
e=x+y
with
x\in I
and
y\in J
.
Then,
x,y
a are orthogonal, so
x+y=e=e^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
.
Therefore
x-x^{2}=y^{2}-y\in I\cap J=\{0\}
.
Hence,
x
and
y
are idempotents.
∎

Note that in writing
R
as a direct sum of indecomposable projective modules, the terms are unique
up to isomorphism and permutation, but this does
not
mean that the corresponding idempotents are unique.
However, their number is the same.

Definition 1.4
.

We say that a set
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of orthogonal idempotents in
R
is
complete

if
e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}=1
.

By Lemma
1.1
and Lemma
1.3
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

There is a
1\!\!-\!\!1
correspondence between a decomposition
R=I_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus I_{n}

as a direct sum of indecomposable left ideals and a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents.
In particular, if
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
and
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\}
are
two complete sets of primitive orthogonal idempotents in
R
, then
r=s
.

Example 1.6
.

In the ring
M_{n}(D)
of
n\times n
matrices over a division ring
D
,
the set of diagonal matrices
\{E_{11},\ldots,E_{nn}\}

(where
E_{ii}
has
1
at
(i,i)
and
0
elsewhere)
is a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents.

Indeed, this follows from Corollary
1.5
since

M_{n}(D)=M_{n}(D)E_{11}\oplus\ldots\oplus M_{n}(D)E_{nn}

is a decomposition of

as a direct sum of indecomposable left ideals.

Next, we look at central idempotents.

Definition 1.7
.

An idempotent
e
of
R
is said to be
central
if it lies in the center of
R
.

Lemma 1.8
.

There is a bijection between:

(a)

an isomorphism
R\cong R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}
as a product of rings;

(b)

a decomposition
R=I_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus I_{n}
as a direct sum of (two-sided) ideals; and

(c)

an expression
1=e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}
as a sum of orthogonal central idempotents.

Proof.

Suppose first that
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
is a direct sum of two-sided ideals.
By Lemma
1.1
,
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
, where
e_{i}\in I_{i}
is a collection of
n
orthogonal idempotents.
We need to show that
e_{i}
commute with all
x\in R
.
Indeed,
xe_{i},e_{i}x\in I_{i}
, since
I_{i}
is a two-sided ideal.
Now
x=x\cdot 1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}xe_{i}
and
x=1\cdot x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}x
.
Hence, since
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
is a direct sum, matching components gives
xe_{i}=e_{i}x
.

Conversely, suppose that
1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
, where
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a set of orthogonal central idempotents.
Then, Lemma
1.1
gives
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}

with
I_{i}:=Re_{i}
, which is a two-sided ideal since
e_{i}
is in the center of
R
.
This completes the correspondence between (b) and (c).

Now suppose that
\varphi\colon R\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}R_{1}\times\cdots\times R_{n}
.
Then
I_{i}:=\varphi^{-1}(R_{i})
is a two-sided ideal of
R
and
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
.

Conversely, if
R=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}
is a direct sum of two-sided ideals, then
according to the correspondence between (b) and (c),
I_{i}=Re_{i}
,
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
are orthogonal central idempotents and
e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}=1
.
Since
e_{i}
is a central idempotent, the ring structure on
R
induces a ring structure on
Re_{i}
,
with
e_{i}
being its unit element.
Thus,
R
is isomorphic to a direct product of rings.
This completes the correspondence between (a) and (b).
∎

.

Suppose that
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a set of orthogonal central idempotents in

Indeed, as

.
Thus, by Lemma

.
∎

\big(\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{\otimes(n-m)}\otimes 1^{\otimes(2m-n)}\big)^{\vee}=\sum_{\ell=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{m}\alpha_{\ell}e_{\ell,\mathbb{H}}^{(n)},

Corollary 2.14
.

If

-algebras, with units

3.
The algebra
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}

In this section we consider the algebra

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}=\bigoplus_{\ell=\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil}^{n}{\mathbb{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}\mathbb{H}.

We first find a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in

{\mathbb{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}={\mathbb{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}

for each integer
\ell
between
\lceil{n\over 2}\rceil
and
n
.
From this we deduce a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in
{\mathbb{S}}_{\ell}^{(n)}\mathbb{H}
for each
\ell
.
Then we get as a corollary complete sets of primitive orthogonal idempotents in

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}={\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and in
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}
.
We use the following notation.

Notation 3.1
.

For
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\sqrt{-1}\in\mathbb{C}
, with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

\alpha^{c}=\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\sqrt{-1}
for its complex conjugate.
We extend the complex conjugation to

{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
by

(x+\sqrt{-1}y)^{c}=x-\sqrt{-1}y
, for
x,y\in{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}
.
In particular,
z+z^{c}\in{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}
for each
z\in{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

We denote

(3.1)

a:={1\over 2}(1+\sqrt{-1}e_{1})\in\mathbb{C}[e_{1}].

Then

(3.2)

a^{c}={1\over 2}(1-\sqrt{-1}e_{1}).

In particular,

(3.3)

ae_{2}=e_{2}a^{c}\text{ and }a^{c}e_{2}=e_{2}a.

The elements
a
and
a^{c}
of
\mathbb{C}[e_{1}]
satisfy

(3.4)

a^{2}=a,\ (a^{c})^{2}=a^{c},\ aa^{c}=0,\text{ and }a+a^{c}=1.

In particular, the set
\{a,a^{c}\}
is a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in
\mathbb{C}[e_{1}]
.
Note that

N(a)=\big({1\over 2}\big)^{2}+\big({1\over 2}\sqrt{-1}\big)^{2}=0\text{ and }N(a^{c})=\big({1\over 2}\big)^{2}+\big(-{1\over 2}\sqrt{-1}\big)^{2}=0.

We also denote

(3.5)

\square:=\triangle_{\mathbb{R}[e_{1}]}={1\over 2}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1})\in{\rm Sym}^{2}\mathbb{R}[e_{1}].

Note that

(3.6)

\square=2(a\otimes a^{c})^{\vee}.

and, since
1\otimes 1=(a+a^{c})\otimes(a+a^{c})
,

(3.7)

1\otimes 1-\square=a\otimes a+a^{c}\otimes a^{c}.

Also, by (
2.4
),

(3.8)

\square\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\triangle_{H}.

By (
2.3
), applied to
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}[e_{1}]
,

(3.9)

\square^{2}=\square.

By Lemma
2.1
, applied to
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{R}[e_{1}]
,

(3.10)

(a\otimes a)\square=N(a)\square=0\text{ and }(a^{c}\otimes a^{c})\square=N(a^{c})\square=0.

Similarly,

(3.11)

(a\otimes a)\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}=0\text{ and }(a^{c}\otimes a^{c})\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}=0,

by Lemma
2.1
, applied to
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{H}
.

\blacksquare

Note that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{C}[e_{1}]=n+1
since
the set
\big\{(e_{0}^{\otimes k_{0}}\otimes e_{1}^{\otimes k_{1}})^{\vee}\mathop{|\;}k_{0}+k_{1}=n\big\}

forms a basis of
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{R}[e_{1}]
over
\mathbb{R}
.

Denote
a_{0}:=a
and
a_{1}:=a^{c}
.
For an integer
k
between
0
and
n
, we denote

I_{k}:=\big\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\mathop{|\;}i_{1}+\ldots+i_{n}=n-k\big\}

(3.12)

{n\choose k}\big(a^{\otimes k}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-k)}\big)^{\vee}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I_{k}}a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{n}}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I_{k}}\sigma\big(a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{n}}\big),

for each
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, since this is an element of
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{C}[e_{1}]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.
The set

(3.13)

\displaystyle\Big\{{n\choose k}\big(a^{\otimes k}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-k)}\big)^{\vee}\mathop{|\;}k=0,\ldots,n\Big\}

is a complete set of primitive orthogonal idempotents in
{\rm Sym}^{n}\mathbb{C}[e_{1}]
.

Proof.

Let
k,\ell
be integers between
0
and
n

\displaystyle{\displaystyle n\choose k}\big(a^{\otimes k}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-k)}\big)^{\vee}\cdot{n\choose\ell}\big(a^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-\ell)}\big)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{akacnk}}}{{=}}{n\choose\ell}\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in I_{k}}\Big(\big(a_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i_{n}}\big)\big(a^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-\ell)}\big)\Big)^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k\ell}{n\choose\ell}\cdot\big(a^{\otimes\ell}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-\ell)}\big)^{\vee}.

Hence, the elements in the set (
3.13

They are not zero since, by (
3.1
) and (
3.2

\big(a^{\otimes k}\otimes(a^{c})^{\otimes(n-k)}\big)^{\vee}={1\over 2^{n}}\cdot 1^{\otimes n}+\ldots,

for each

Since

Therefore, the set (

Since the number of elements in the set (

these elements are primitive.
∎

\tau_{0}=\square\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}=\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{3}\otimes e_{3})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\tau_{1}=\square(1\otimes 1-\triangle_{\mathbb{H}})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}=(\square-\triangle_{\mathbb{H}})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}-e_{2}\otimes e_{2}-e_{3}\otimes e_{3})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\blacksquare

Remark 4.5
.

Consider the elements

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{3}\otimes e_{3})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+e_{4}\otimes e_{4}+e_{5}\otimes e_{5})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{3}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+e_{6}\otimes e_{6}+e_{7}\otimes e_{7})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{4}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{4}\otimes e_{4}+e_{6}\otimes e_{6})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{5}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{5}\otimes e_{5}+e_{7}\otimes e_{7})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{6}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+e_{4}\otimes e_{4}+e_{7}\otimes e_{7})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)},

\displaystyle\rho_{7}

\displaystyle={1\over 4}(1\otimes 1+e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+e_{5}\otimes e_{5}+e_{6}\otimes e_{6})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}

of
T_{2}\mathbb{O}={\rm Sym}^{2}\mathbb{O}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}
. Their sum

\sum_{i=1}^{7}\rho_{i}={1\over 4}\Big(7\cdot 1\otimes 1+3\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}\Big)\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{triangle}}}{{=}}(1\otimes 1+6\triangle_{\mathbb{O}})\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop.\ }\eqref{decompositionProp}{\rm(d)}}}{{=}}e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}

is the unit element of
T_{2}\mathbb{O}
and they are orthogonal:

\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=\delta_{ij}\rho_{i}\quad\text{ for }i,j=1,\ldots,7.

For example,

\rho_{1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{triangle}}}{{=}}\big(\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}\big)^{2}=\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{triangleSq}}}{{=}}\triangle_{\mathbb{H}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}=\rho_{1}

and, by the multiplication table (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\rho_{2}

\displaystyle={1\over 16}\Big((1\otimes 1+e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2}+e_{3}\otimes e_{3})

\displaystyle+(e_{1}\otimes e_{1}+1\otimes 1+e_{3}\otimes e_{3}+e_{2}\otimes e_{2})

\displaystyle+(e_{4}\otimes e_{4}+e_{5}\otimes e_{5}+e_{6}\otimes e_{6}+e_{7}\otimes e_{7})

\displaystyle+(e_{5}\otimes e_{5}+e_{4}\otimes e_{4}+e_{7}\otimes e_{7}+e_{6}\otimes e_{6})\Big)\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{triangle}}}{{=}}\triangle_{\mathbb{O}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop.\ }\eqref{decompositionProp}{\rm(d)}}}{{=}}0.

Note that, since
\triangle_{\mathbb{O}}={1\over 8}\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}

and
\triangle_{\mathbb{O}}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}=0
,
we have, in the notation of Example
4.4
,

\tau_{0}=\rho_{1},\ \tau_{1}=\rho_{2}+\rho_{3},\ \tau_{2}=\rho_{4}+\rho_{5},\ \tau_{3}=\rho_{6}+\rho_{7}.

is not a maximal set of orthogonal idempotents
in
T_{2}\mathbb{O}={\rm Sym}^{2}\mathbb{O}\cdot e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}
that sum to the unit element
e_{2,\mathbb{O}}^{(2)}
of
T_{2}\mathbb{O}
.

\blacksquare
