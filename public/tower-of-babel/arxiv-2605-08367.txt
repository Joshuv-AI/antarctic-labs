Canonical parameters on marginally trapped surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary 53A07; Secondary 53B20
Miroslav Maksimović and Velichka Milousheva

Abstract
. Marginally trapped surfaces are spacelike surfaces in the Minkowski space whose mean curvature vector is lightlike at each point. In general, the marginally trapped surfaces are determined by seven functions satisfying several conditions (differential equations). In the present paper, we introduce special principal parameters, called canonical, and prove that every marginally trapped surface of general type admits (at least locally) canonical principal parameters which allow us to reduce the number functions. We prove a Fundamental existence and uniqueness theorem formulated in terms of canonical parameters, which states that every marginally trapped surface is determined up to a motion by three smooth functions satisfying a system of partial differential equations.



1.
Introduction

In the classical differential geometry, the problem of determining a regular curve in the space is solved by introducing the concept of a natural parameter – the curvature and the torsion of the curve, expressed with respect to the natural parameter, uniquely determine the curve up to a position in the space. However, in the case of surfaces, the situation is significantly more complicated. The problem of determining a surface with fewer conditions than the conditions in the classical Bonnet’s theorem has been considered by mathematicians for many years. This problem was of interest even to O. Bonnet
[
4
]
, who considered the possibility of reducing the functions to the coefficients of the first fundamental form and the two principal curvatures. Later, the same problem was studied by É. Cartan, who also investigated the problem of determining the surface only by the second fundamental form
[
6
]
. S. P. Finikoff and B. Gambier
[
10
]
, and later R. Bryant
[
5
]
investigated surfaces in the 3-dimensional Euclidean space from the viewpoint of curvature lines and principal curvatures.

The problem of finding a minimal number of functions, satisfying a minimal number of conditions (differential equations), that determine the surface up to a motion in the ambient space, is a fundamental problem in the local theory of surfaces both in Euclidean and pseudo-Euclidean spaces. It is known as the Lund-Regge problem
[
24
]
and is solved for some special classes of surfaces using appropriate parameters on the surface. For example, special principal parameters were introduced for the so-called Weingarten surfaces in
[
11
]
, and these parameters were used to determine the Weingarten surfaces by three functions, satisfying one partial differential equation (which is equivalent to the Gauss equation). This result was later improved by O. Kassabov
[
21
]
by introducing canonical parameters for an arbitrary (not necessarily Weingarten) smooth surface and showing that the number of functions involved is reduced to two, moreover these are the two main invariants – the Gaussian curvature and the mean curvature.

In the last few years, the problem of determining a surface by a minimal number of functions satisfying a minimal number of partial differential equations was attacked for surfaces of co-dimension two in the 4-dimensional spaces
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
\mathbb{R}_{1}^{4}
and
\mathbb{R}_{2}^{4}
. The Lund-Regge problem was solved for some special classes of surfaces, for example, the minimal surfaces and the surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field.
By use of special geometric parameters on any minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, R. Tribuzy and I. Guadalupe proved that a minimal surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is determined up to a motion by two invariant functions (the Gaussian curvature and the normal curvature) satisfying a system of two PDEs
[
25
]
. A similar result was proved for spacelike and timelike zero mean curvature surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
in
[
2
]
and
[
13
]
, respectively. These classes of surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
admit (at least locally) special isothermal parameters, called
canonical
, such that the two main invariants – the Gaussian curvature and the normal curvature of the surface satisfy a system of two partial differential equations.
The same approach was applied to minimal Lorentz surfaces in the pseudo-Euclidean 4-space with neutral metric
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{2}
in
[
1
]
. In all these cases, the number of the invariant functions determining the surfaces and the number of the differential equations are both reduced to two. Moreover, the geometry of the corresponding zero mean curvature
surface is determined by the solutions of the corresponding system of PDEs.

Another class of surfaces for which the number of functions and the number of differential equations can be reduced, is the class of surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field – an important class of surfaces both in Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian geometry.
In
[
14
]
, the surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field (PNMCVF) in the Euclidean 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and the spacelike PNMCVF-surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
are described in terms of three functions satisfying a system of three PDEs.
A similar result is proved for the class of timelike PNMCVF-surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, see
[
3
]
. In all theses cases, the basic approach to solve the Lund-Regge problem is based on introducing special geometric parameters called
canonical parameters
.

The idea to introduce special parameters was further developed by O. Kassabov and the second author in
[
22
]
, where they defined canonical principal parameters for the general class of surfaces in the Euclidean 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, which generalize the canonical parameters introduced for minimal surfaces and for PNMCVF-surfaces. They proved the Fundamental existence and uniqueness theorem in terms of canonical parameters, which states that any surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is determined up to a motion by four geometrically determined functions satisfying a system of natural PDEs.
The same approach can be applied to the general class of spacelike surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

In the present paper, we consider the class of the so-called marginally trapped surfaces in the four-dimensional Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, which is a natural extension of the class of minimal surfaces. A surface in a 4-dimensional spacetime is called
marginally trapped
(or
quasi-minimal
) if its mean curvature vector is lightlike at each point of the surface
[
28
,
7
]
.
The concept of trapped surfaces was first introduced by Roger Penrose
[
26
]
and plays an important role in General Relativity for studying global properties of spacetime and also for understanding the evolution of cosmic black holes. Marginally trapped surfaces have been very actively studied in the last few years. For example, marginally trapped surfaces with
positive relative nullity in Lorenz space forms were classified by Chen and Van der Veken in
[
8
]
. They also classified marginally
trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field
[
9
]
. In a series of papers, S. Haesen and M. Ortega studied marginally trapped surfaces in the Minkowski 4-space which are invariant under spacelike rotations, under boost transformations (hyperbolic rotations), and under the group of screw rotations (a group of Lorenz rotations with an invariant lightlike direction), see
[
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

In
[
16
]
, G. Ganchev and the second author developed an invariant theory
of marginally trapped surfaces in the four-dimensional Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
based on the principal lines
generated by the second fundamental form. They proved a fundamental theorem of
Bonnet-type stating that each marginally trapped surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is determined up to a motion by seven invariant functions satisfying some natural conditions.

In the present paper, we define special principal parameters, which we call canonical, on the general class of marginally trapped surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, and prove that each such surface admits (at least locally) canonical principal parameters. We prove a Fundamental existence and uniqueness theorem formulated in terms of canonical parameters, which states that any marginally trapped surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is determined up to a motion by three geometrically determined functions satisfying a system of partial differential equations.
In the last section, we give a special family of examples demonstrating our theory.

2.
Preliminaries

We consider the Minkowski 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
endowed with the canonical metric of signature
(3,1)
,
given by

g_{0}=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}+dx_{3}^{2}-dx_{4}^{2},

where
(x_{1};x_{2};x_{3};x_{4})
is a rectangular coordinate system of
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
. As usual, we denote by
\langle,\rangle
the indefinite inner scalar product with respect to
g_{0}
.

A surface
M^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is said to be

spacelike
if
\langle,\rangle
induces a Riemannian
metric
g
on
M^{2}
. So, at each point
p
of a spacelike surface

M^{2}
we have the following decomposition into a tangent and normal space

\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}=T_{p}M^{2}\oplus N_{p}M^{2}

such that the restriction of the metric

\langle,\rangle
onto the tangent space
T_{p}M^{2}
is of
signature
(2,0)
, and the restriction of the metric
\langle,\rangle
onto the normal space
N_{p}M^{2}
is of signature
(1,1)
.

Denote by
\nabla^{\prime}
and
\nabla
the Levi Civita connections on
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and
M^{2}
, respectively.
For any tangent vector fields
x
and
y
and any normal vector field
\xi
we have the following formulas of Gauss and Weingarten:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{x}y=\nabla_{x}y+\sigma(x,y);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{x}\xi=-A_{\xi}x+D_{x}\xi,\end{array}

where
\sigma
is the second fundamental tensor,
D
– the normal connection
and
A_{\xi}
– the shape operator with respect to
\xi
.
The mean curvature vector field
H
of the surface
M^{2}
is defined as

H=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\sigma}
.

A surface
M^{2}
is called
minimal
if its mean curvature vector vanishes identically, i.e.

H=0
. A surface
M^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is called
marginally trapped
(or
quasi-minimal
) if its mean curvature vector is lightlike at each point, i.e.
H\neq 0
and
\langle H,H\rangle=0
.

Let
M^{2}:z=z(u,v),\,\,(u,v)\in{\mathcal{D}}
(
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
) be a local parametrization of a marginally trapped surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
and denote by
E
,
F
,
G
the coefficients of the
first fundamental form of
M^{2}
.
In
[
16
]
it is proved that in a neighbourhood of each point
p\in M^{2}
there exists a geometrically determined orthonormal frame field
\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
, such that

x=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}}
and
y=\displaystyle{\frac{z_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}}
are collinear with the principal directions,
n_{1}=H
and
n_{2}
is a normal vector field determined by the conditions

\langle n_{1},n_{1}\rangle=0;\quad\langle n_{2},n_{2}\rangle=0;\quad\langle n_{1},n_{2}\rangle=-1.

With respect to this frame field the
following Frenet-type derivative formulas hold true:

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{x}x=\quad\quad\quad\gamma_{1}\,y+\,(1+\nu)\,n_{1};&\qquad\nabla^{\prime}_{x}n_{1}=\quad\quad\quad\quad\mu\,y+\beta_{1}\,n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{x}y=-\gamma_{1}\,x\quad\quad\;+\,\quad\lambda\,n_{1}\;+\mu\,n_{2};&\qquad\nabla^{\prime}_{y}n_{1}=\mu\,x\quad\quad\quad\quad+\beta_{2}\,n_{1};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{y}x=\quad\quad\;\,-\gamma_{2}\,y+\quad\lambda\,n_{1}\;+\mu\,n_{2};&\qquad\nabla^{\prime}_{x}n_{2}=(1+\nu)\,x+\lambda\,y\quad\quad-\beta_{1}\,n_{2};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\nabla^{\prime}_{y}y=\;\;\gamma_{2}\,x\quad\quad\;\;\,+(1-\nu)\,n_{1};&\qquad\nabla^{\prime}_{y}n_{2}=\lambda\,x+(1-\nu)\,y\quad\quad-\beta_{2}\,n_{2},\end{array}

where
\nu,\lambda,\mu,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are smooth functions determined by the geometric frame field
\{x,y,n_{1},n_{2}\}
. These functions are called
geometric functions
of the surface and their geometric meaning is described in
[
16
]
. Note that
\mu\neq 0
at each point and the main invariants of the surface – the Gauss curvature
K
and the curvature of the normal connection
\varkappa
are expressed by the functions
\lambda
,
\mu
and
\nu
as follows:

(2.2)

K=2\lambda\mu;\qquad\varkappa=-2\mu\nu.

The functions
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are expressed in terms of the coefficients of the first fundamental form by the formulas

\gamma_{1}=-\frac{(\sqrt{E})_{v}}{\sqrt{E}\sqrt{G}},\qquad\gamma_{2}=-\frac{(\sqrt{G})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}\sqrt{G}},

or equivalently

(2.3)

\gamma_{1}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}(\ln\sqrt{E})_{v},\qquad\gamma_{2}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{E}}(\ln\sqrt{G})_{u}.

In
[
16
]
, it was proved that the seven functions
\nu,\lambda,\mu,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
satisfy the following conditions

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt2\mu\,\gamma_{2}+\mu\,\beta_{1}=\displaystyle{\frac{\mu_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt2\mu\,\gamma_{1}+\mu\,\beta_{2}=\displaystyle{\frac{\mu_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt2\lambda\,\mu=\displaystyle{\frac{(\gamma_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{(\gamma_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-\left((\gamma_{1})^{2}+(\gamma_{2})^{2}\right)};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt2\lambda\,\gamma_{2}-2\nu\,\gamma_{1}-\lambda\,\beta_{1}+(1+\nu)\,\beta_{2}=\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}-\frac{\nu_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt2\lambda\,\gamma_{1}+2\nu\,\gamma_{2}+(1-\nu)\,\beta_{1}-\lambda\,\beta_{2}=\displaystyle{\frac{\nu_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{\lambda_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}};\\
\gamma_{1}\,\beta_{1}-\gamma_{2}\,\beta_{2}+2\nu\,\mu=\displaystyle{-\frac{(\beta_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{(\beta_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}},\end{array}

and determine the marginally trapped surface up to a motion in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
(see
[
16
]
, Theorem 4.1). This is the Bonnet-type Fundamental theorem for the class of marginally trapped surfaces, which gives the number of functions and the number of differential equations determining the surface with respect to arbitrary principal parameters.

In the next section, we will introduce special principal parameters, which allow us to reduce up to three the number of functions and the number of PDEs determining the class of marginally trapped surfaces.

3.
Canonical parameters on a marginally trapped surface

Let
M^{2}
be a marginally trapped surface parametrized by arbitrary principal parameters. Then formulas (
2.1
) hold true.
The first two equations of (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle-2\mu\frac{1}{\sqrt{E}}(\ln\sqrt{G})_{u}+\mu\beta_{1}=\frac{\mu_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle-2\mu\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}(\ln\sqrt{E})_{v}+\mu\beta_{2}=\frac{\mu_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}.\end{array}

The last equalities imply that the functions
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are expressed in terms of the function
\mu
and the coefficients of the first fundamental form as follows:

(3.1)

\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{E}}(\ln|\mu|)_{u}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{E}}(\ln\sqrt{G})_{u},\qquad\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{G}}(\ln|\mu|)_{v}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{G}}(\ln\sqrt{E})_{v}.

Now, using (
2.3
) and (
3.1
), from the fourth and fifth equations of (
2.4
) we obtain the following relations:

(3.2)

\displaystyle 2(2\nu+1)\frac{(\ln\sqrt{E})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-4\lambda\,\frac{(\ln\sqrt{G})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}=\displaystyle{\frac{\lambda_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}-\frac{\nu_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}+\frac{\lambda\,(\ln|\mu|)_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}-\frac{(1+\nu)(\ln|\mu|)_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle-4\lambda\,\frac{(\ln\sqrt{E})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-2(2\nu-1)\frac{(\ln\sqrt{G})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}=\displaystyle{\frac{\nu_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{\lambda_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-\frac{(1-\nu)(\ln|\mu|)_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{\lambda(\ln|\mu|)_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}}.

We consider the above two equations as a system with respect to
(\sqrt{E})_{v}
and
(\sqrt{G})_{u}
. In the general case, when
4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1\neq 0
,
equalities (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply the next system of partial differential equations

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\left(\sqrt{E}\right)_{v}=\phi_{1}\sqrt{E}+\phi_{2}\sqrt{G}\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\left(\sqrt{G}\right)_{u}=\phi_{3}\sqrt{E}+\phi_{4}\sqrt{G}\ ,\end{array}

which can be written also as follows

(3.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\left(\ln\sqrt{E}\right)_{v}=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\left(\ln\sqrt{G}\right)_{u}=\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\ ,\end{array}

where the four functions
\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
, are given by

(3.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.53581pt\phi_{1}\!=\!\displaystyle{-\frac{\mu(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2}-\nu)_{v}+(2(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2})+\nu-1)\mu_{v}}{2\mu(4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1)}}\ ;\\
\vskip 8.53581pt\phi_{2}\!=\!\displaystyle{\frac{2\mu(\lambda_{u}\nu-\lambda\nu_{u})+\lambda\mu_{u}-\lambda_{u}\mu}{2\mu(4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1)}}\ ;\\
\vskip 8.53581pt\phi_{3}\!=\!\displaystyle{\frac{2\mu(\lambda\nu_{v}-\lambda_{v}\nu)+\lambda\mu_{v}-\lambda_{v}\mu}{2\mu(4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1)}}\ ;\\
\vskip 8.53581pt\phi_{4}\!=\!\displaystyle{-\frac{\mu(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2}+\nu)_{u}+(2(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2})-\nu-1)\mu_{u}}{2\mu(4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1)}}\ .\end{array}

Remark 3.1
.

Note that from equalities (
2.2
) it follows that the condition
4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1=0
is equivalent to the following condition on the main invariants of the surface:

K^{2}+\varkappa^{2}=\mu^{2}.

Further, we shall consider the general case:
K^{2}+\varkappa^{2}\neq\mu^{2}
and these surfaces we will call
marginally trapped surfaces of general type
. The special case
K^{2}+\varkappa^{2}=\mu^{2}
will be studied separately.

Having in mind system of equations (
3.4
), we can easily prove the following statement.

Lemma 3.1
.

The function
\sqrt{E}\,e^{-\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv}
does not depend on the parameter
v
and the function

\sqrt{G}\,e^{-\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)du}

does not depend on the parameter
u
.

From Lemma
3.1
it follows that if
(u_{0},v_{0})
is an arbitrary point and
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are constants, then
there exist functions
\varphi(u)
and
\psi(v)
which are given by

(3.6)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\varphi(u)=\displaystyle\sqrt{E}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv-\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du+c_{1}}\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\psi(v)=\displaystyle\sqrt{G}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)du-\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u_{0},v)dv+c_{2}}\ .\end{array}

We introduce the notion of canonical parameters on a marginally trapped surface by the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M^{2}
be a marginally trapped surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
parametrized by principal parameters
(u,v)
and
K^{2}+\varkappa^{2}\neq\mu^{2}
. If the functions
\varphi(u)
and
\psi(v)
defined by (
3.6
) are equal to 1, then we say that the parameters
(u,v)
are
canonical principal parameters
of the surface.

Now we will prove the following theorem for canonical parameters of marginally trapped surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Each marginally trapped surface with
K^{2}+\varkappa^{2}\neq\mu^{2}
locally admits canonical principal parameters.

Proof.

For a given pair of principal parameters
(u,v)
and an arbitrary point
(u_{0},v_{0})
, we introduce new parameters
\bar{u}
,
\bar{v}
defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{u}=\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\sqrt{E}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv-\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du+c_{1}}+u_{0}\vskip 5.69054pt\ ;\\
\bar{v}=\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\sqrt{G}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)du-\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u_{0},v)dv+c_{2}}+v_{0}\ ,\end{array}

or equivalently,

(3.7)

\bar{u}=\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\varphi(u)du+u_{0}\ ;\qquad\bar{v}=\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\psi(v)dv+v_{0},

where the functions
\varphi(u)
and
\psi(v)
are given by (
3.6
). Therefore,
\bar{u}=\bar{u}(u)
and
\bar{v}=\bar{v}(v)
hold, which means that the new parameters

(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are also principal. Obviously,
\bar{u}_{0}=\bar{u}(u_{0})=u_{0}
,

\bar{v}_{0}=\bar{v}(v_{0})=v_{0}
. Differentiating equations (
3.7
) gives

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\bar{u}_{u}=\varphi(u)=\sqrt{E}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv-\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du+c_{1}}\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\bar{u}_{v}=0\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\bar{v}_{u}=0\ ;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\bar{v}_{v}=\psi(v)=\sqrt{G}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)du-\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u_{0},v)dv+c_{2}}\ .\end{array}

It can easily be checked that under the change of the parameters
\bar{u}=\bar{u}(u)
,
\bar{v}=\bar{v}(v)
we have the relations:

\sqrt{E}=\sqrt{\bar{E}}\,{\bar{u}}_{u};\quad\sqrt{G}=\sqrt{\bar{G}}\,{\bar{v}}_{v},

from where it follows that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\phi_{1}(u,v)={\bar{\phi}_{1}}(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\,{\bar{v}}_{v};&\quad\phi_{2}(u,v)={\bar{\phi}_{2}}(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\,{\bar{u}}_{u};\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\phi_{3}(u,v)={\bar{\phi}_{3}}(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\,{\bar{v}}_{v};&\quad\phi_{4}(u,v)={\bar{\phi}_{4}}(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\,{\bar{u}}_{u}.\end{array}

The last equations imply

\displaystyle(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}})(u,v)=(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}})(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\frac{d\bar{v}}{dv}\ ;

\displaystyle(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4})(u,v)=(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4})(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\frac{d\bar{u}}{du}\ .

By calculations we further obtain

(3.8)

\begin{split}&e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u,v)dv+\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du}=\\
&=e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}\right)(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v))\frac{d\bar{v}}{dv}{dv}+\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4}\right)(\bar{u}(u),\bar{v}(v_{0}))\frac{d\bar{u}}{du}du}=\\
&=e^{\displaystyle\int_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\bar{v}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v})d\bar{v}+\int_{\bar{u}_{0}}^{\bar{u}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v}_{0})d\bar{u}}\ .\end{split}

Using the previous results we have

\sqrt{\bar{E}}=\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\bar{u}_{u}}=e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv+\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{1}}

=e^{\displaystyle\int_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\bar{v}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v})d\bar{v}+\int_{\bar{u}_{0}}^{\bar{u}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v}_{0})d\bar{u}-c_{1}}

and hence
\bar{\varphi}(\bar{u})=1
, where the function
\bar{\varphi}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}(\bar{u})=\sqrt{\bar{E}}e^{\displaystyle-\int_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\bar{v}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}\right)d\bar{v}-\int_{\bar{u}_{0}}^{\bar{u}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v}_{0})d\bar{u}+c_{1}}.

In a similar way, we also obtain that
\bar{\psi}(\bar{v})=1
, where the function
\bar{\psi}
is defined analogously to function
\psi
, but with respect to the parameters
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
. Thus, according to Definition
3.2
, the parameters
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are canonical.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
(u,v)
and
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are two pairs of canonical principal parameters
in a neighbourhood of a point
p
, then the following relations hold

\bar{u}=\pm u+u_{0};\qquad\qquad\bar{v}=\pm v+v_{0},

or

\bar{u}=\pm v+v_{0};\qquad\qquad\bar{v}=\pm u+u_{0}\ .

Proof.

If the pairs
(u,v)
and
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are principal parameters, one of the following two cases is possible:

\bar{u}=\bar{u}(u),\quad\bar{v}=\bar{v}(v);

or

\bar{u}=\bar{u}(v),\quad\bar{v}=\bar{v}(u).

For example, let us suppose that
\bar{u}=\bar{u}(u)
,
\bar{v}=\bar{v}(v)
. Additionally, if
(u,v)
and
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
are canonical parameters, then, using (
3.8
), we have

\begin{split}1&=\bar{\varphi}(\bar{u},{\bar{v}})=\sqrt{\overline{E}}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{\bar{v}_{0}}^{\bar{v}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{1}+\bar{\phi}_{2}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v})d\bar{v}-\int_{\bar{u}_{0}}^{\bar{u}}\left(\bar{\phi}_{3}\frac{\sqrt{\bar{E}}}{\sqrt{\bar{G}}}+\bar{\phi}_{4}\right)(\bar{u},\bar{v}_{0})d\bar{u}+c_{1}}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{E}(u,v)}{(\bar{u}_{u})^{2}}e^{-\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u,v)dv-\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du+c_{1}}\\
&=\frac{\varphi(u)}{(\bar{u}_{u})^{2}}=\frac{1}{(\bar{u}_{u})^{2}},\end{split}

which imply that
\bar{u}=\pm u+u_{0}
. Analogously, we obtain
\bar{v}=\pm v+v_{0}
.

In a similar way, in the second case
\bar{u}=\bar{u}(v),\;\;\bar{v}=\bar{v}(u)
, we obtain
\bar{u}=\pm v+v_{0};\;\;\bar{v}=\pm u+u_{0}
.

∎

4.
Fundamental Theorem in terms of canonical parameters

In this section, we will consider
(u,v)
to be canonical principal parameters. In accordance with Definition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{E}=e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv+\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{1}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{G}=e^{\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)du+\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)(u_{0},v)dv-c_{2}},

and hence

\displaystyle\sqrt{E}(u,v_{0})=e^{\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{1}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{G}(u,v_{0})=e^{\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{2}}.

By dividing the last two equalities we get the relation

\left(\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{E}{G}}\right)(u,v_{0})=e^{c_{2}-c_{1}}=:c=const,

which gives

\displaystyle\sqrt{E}(u,v_{0})=e^{\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(c\phi_{3}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{1}}.

Analogously, we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{G}(u_{0},v)=e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\phi_{2}\right)(u_{0},v)dv-c_{2}}.

For the purposes of our further work, we introduce the following functions

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle g_{1}(u)=e^{\displaystyle\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(c\phi_{3}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du-c_{1}},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle g_{2}(v)=e^{\displaystyle\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\frac{1}{c}\phi_{2}\right)(u_{0},v)dv-c_{2}}.\end{array}

Now, we shall prove the following Bonet-type fundamental theorem for marginally trapped surfaces in terms of canonical principal parameters.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\nu(u,v)
,
\lambda(u,v)
, and
\mu(u,v)
,
\mu\neq 0
,
4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1\neq 0
, be smooth functions defined in a domain
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\phi_{i}(u,v)
,
i=1,2,3,4
be defined by (
3.5
). Let
\Phi(u,v)
,
\Psi(u,v)
be a solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\Phi_{v}=\phi_{1}\Phi+\phi_{2}\Psi;\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\Psi_{u}=\phi_{3}\Phi+\phi_{4}\Psi;\\
\end{array}\qquad\Phi(u,v_{0})=g_{1}(u);\;\;\Psi(u_{0},v)=g_{2}(v),

where
g_{1}(u)
and
g_{2}(v)
are defined by (
4.1
), and let the following equations also hold

(4.2)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle 2\lambda\mu=&\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\left(\frac{\Phi_{v}}{\Psi}\right)_{v}+\left(\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\Phi}\right)_{u}\right);\\
\vskip 5.69054pt2\nu\mu=&\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\ln\frac{|\Psi|}{|\Phi|}\right)_{uv}.\end{array}

Then, there exists a unique (up to a position in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
) marginally trapped surface of general type parametrized by canonical
principal parameters
(u,v)
with geometric functions
\nu(u,v)
,
\lambda(u,v)
, and
\mu(u,v)
.

Proof.

Let
\nu(u,v)
,
\lambda(u,v)
, and
\mu(u,v)
be smooth functions,
\phi_{i}(u,v)
,
i=1,2,3,4
be given by equations (
3.5
), and
g_{1}(u)
and
g_{2}(v)
be defined by (
4.1
). Let us consider the following Cauchy problem

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\Phi_{v}=\phi_{1}\Phi+\phi_{2}\Psi,\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\Psi_{u}=\phi_{3}\Phi+\phi_{4}\Psi,\\
\end{array}

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\Phi(u,v_{0})=g_{1}(u),\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\Psi(u_{0},v)=g_{2}(v).\end{array}

Note that this is an initial value problem for a canonical hyperbolic system of PDEs (see eq. (CHS) in
[
27
]
) and thus system (
4.3
) determines (at least locally) functions
\varphi(u,v)
and
\psi(u,v)

satisfying the initial conditions (
4.4
).

Now we introduce the functions
E=\Phi^{2}
,
G=\Psi^{2}
, and

(4.5)

\ \gamma_{1}=-\frac{\Phi_{v}}{\Phi\Psi};\qquad\gamma_{2}=-\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\Phi\Psi};

(4.6)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\Psi(\ln|\mu|)_{u}+2\Psi_{u}\right);\qquad\displaystyle\beta_{2}=\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\Phi(\ln|\mu|)_{v}+2\Phi_{v}\right).

Using the introduced functions we can express the right-hand side of the first equation of (
4.2
) as follows

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\left(\frac{\Phi_{v}}{\Psi}\right)_{v}+\left(\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\Phi}\right)_{u}\right)=\frac{(\gamma_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}+\frac{(\gamma_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}-\Big((\gamma_{1})^{2}+(\gamma_{2})^{2}\Big).

Therefore, the first equation of (
4.2
) takes the form

2\lambda\mu=\frac{(\gamma_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{(\gamma_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-\Big((\gamma_{1})^{2}+(\gamma_{2})^{2}\Big),

which coincides with the third equation of system (
2.4
).

Similarly, the right-hand side of the second equation of (
4.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\left(\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\Psi}\right)_{v}-\left(\frac{\Phi_{v}}{\Phi}\right)_{u}\right)=-\gamma_{1}\beta_{1}+\gamma_{2}\beta_{2}-\frac{(\beta_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}+\frac{(\beta_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}},

and hence, the second equation of (
4.2
) takes the form

2\nu\mu=\frac{(\beta_{1})_{v}}{\sqrt{G}}-\frac{(\beta_{2})_{u}}{\sqrt{E}}-\gamma_{1}\beta_{1}+\gamma_{2}\beta_{2},

which is exactly the last equation of system (
2.4
).

Using equalities (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we can easily calculate that

2\mu\gamma_{2}+\mu\beta_{1}=-2\mu\frac{\Psi_{u}}{\Phi\Psi}+\mu\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\Psi(\ln|\mu|)_{u}+2\Psi_{u}\right)=\frac{\mu_{u}}{\sqrt{E}},

which is the first equation of system (
2.4
),
and

2\mu\gamma_{1}+\mu\beta_{2}=-2\mu\frac{\Phi_{v}}{\Phi\Psi}+\mu\frac{1}{\Phi\Psi}\left(\Phi(\ln|\mu|)_{v}+2\Phi_{v}\right)=\frac{\mu_{v}}{\sqrt{G}},

which is the second equation of system (
2.4
).

To check the remaining two equations of system (
2.4
) we use that
\Phi(u,v)
and
\Psi(u,v)
satisfy system (
4.3
). After long but standard calculations, by use of (
3.5
), (
4.3
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) we get that the fourth and fifth equations of
(
2.4
) are also satisfied. Consequently, all conditions in system (
2.4
) are fulfilled.
Moreover, using (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) we can easily obtain that the following inequalities are also valid

\frac{\mu_{u}}{\mu(2\gamma_{2}+\beta_{1})}>0\ ;\qquad\qquad\frac{\mu_{v}}{\mu(2\gamma_{1}+\beta_{2})}>0\ .

Finally, the end of the proof follows based on Theorem 4.1 in paper
[
16
]
.

∎

In other words, Theorem
4.1
states that each marginally trapped surface of general type in the Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
is determined up to a motion (a position in the space) by three smooth functions

\nu(u,v)
,
\lambda(u,v)
, and
\mu(u,v)
(
\mu\neq 0
), satisfying a system of PDEs. Moreover, the parameters
(u,v)
are the canonical principal parameters of the marginally trapped surface.

5.
Marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field

In this section we will consider marginally trapped surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
with parallel mean curvature vector field, i.e. the condition
DH=0
holds identically. For such surfaces, the invariants
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
vanish, i.e.
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
(see
[
16
]
). So, in the case of a marginally trapped surface with parallel mean curvature vector field,
from the last equation of system (
2.4
) it follows that
\nu=0
, since
\mu\neq 0
.

Now, the equations in system (
2.4
), omitting the third and last one, take the following simpler forms, respectively,

(5.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle 2\gamma_{2}\sqrt{E}=(\ln|\mu|)_{u},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle 2\gamma_{1}\sqrt{G}=(\ln|\mu|)_{v},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle 2\gamma_{2}\sqrt{E}=(\ln|\lambda|)_{u},\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle 2\gamma_{1}\sqrt{G}=(\ln|\lambda|)_{v}.\end{array}

Using (
5.1
), we obtain

(\ln|\mu|)_{u}=(\ln|\lambda|)_{u}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;(\ln|\mu|)_{v}=(\ln|\lambda|)_{v},

which imply

\left(\ln{\frac{|\lambda|}{|\mu|}}\right)_{u}=0\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\left(\ln{\frac{|\lambda|}{|\mu|}}\right)_{v}=0.

The last two equations imply that the functions
\lambda(u,v)
and
\mu(u,v)
satisfy the relation

\ln{\frac{|\lambda|}{|\mu|}}=\mathrm{const}.

Hence, the functions
\mu(u,v)
and
\lambda(u,v)
are proportional, i.e.

(5.2)

\lambda(u,v)=c\,\mu(u,v),

where
c=\mathrm{const}
.

If we assume that
c=0
, then
\lambda(u,v)=0
and equalities (
2.2
) imply that both
the Gauss curvature
K
and the normal curvature
\varkappa
are zero. In such case, the surface consists only of inflection points, which means that
it is either developable or lies in a 3-dimensional space (see
[
23
]
). So, we consider the case
c\neq 0
, which means that
\lambda\neq 0
.
Since
4\nu^{2}+4\lambda^{2}-1\neq 0
, we have
\lambda\neq\pm\frac{1}{2}
.

Further, using formulas (
3.5
), we obtain the following expressions for the functions
\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4

\phi_{1}=-(\ln\sqrt{|\mu|})_{v},\;\;\phi_{2}=\phi_{3}=0,\;\;\phi_{4}=-(\ln\sqrt{|\mu|})_{u}.

If we substitute these expressions in (
3.6
), we get that the function
\varphi(u)
is expressed as

\begin{split}\varphi(u)&=\sqrt{E}\,e^{\displaystyle-\int_{v_{0}}^{v}\left(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\frac{\sqrt{G}}{\sqrt{E}}\right)dv-\int_{u_{0}}^{u}\left(\phi_{3}\frac{\sqrt{E}}{\sqrt{G}}+\phi_{4}\right)(u,v_{0})du+c_{1}}\\
&=\sqrt{E}\,e^{\displaystyle\ln{\sqrt{|\mu(u,v)|}}-\ln{\sqrt{|\mu(u_{0},v_{0})|}}+c_{1}}.\end{split}

Choosing
c_{1}=\ln{\sqrt{|\mu(u_{0},v_{0})|}}
, we obtain

(5.3)

\varphi(u)=\sqrt{E}\,\sqrt{|\mu|}.

Similarly, choosing
c_{2}=\ln{\sqrt{|\mu(u_{0},v_{0})|}}
, we obtain that the function
\psi(v)
has the following form

(5.4)

\psi(v)=\sqrt{G}\,\sqrt{|\mu|}.

Hence, applying Definition
3.2
of canonical parameters for marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field, we obtain that
(u,v)
are canonical parameters if and only if

\sqrt{E}\,\sqrt{|\mu|}=1;\quad\sqrt{G}\,\sqrt{|\mu|}=1,

or equivalently,

E(u,v)=\frac{1}{|\mu(u,v)|};\quad G(u,v)=\frac{1}{|\mu(u,v)|}.

Remark 5.1
.

Canonical parameters for spacelike surfaces with parallel normalized mean curvature vector field in the Minkowski 4-space
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
were introduced in
[
14
]
as special isothermal parameters that satisfy the conditions

E(u,v)=G(u,v)=\frac{1}{|\mu(u,v)|};\quad F(u,v)=0.

Obviously, for marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field we have the same expressions for the coefficients of the first fundamental form. Hence, in some sense, Definition
3.2
of canonical parameters in the present paper is equivalent to the definition given in
[
14
]
.

Remark 5.2
.

A classification of marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field is given by B.-Y. Chen and J. Van der Veken in
[
9
]
. According to their classification, there exist six types of marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field which are listed in Theorem 4.1. in
[
9
]
.

Remark 5.3
.

Given that the functions
\varphi
and
\psi
depend only on
u
and
v
, respectively, based on equalities (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) we also conclude that, in the case of marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field, the magnitudes
E|\mu|
and
G|\mu|
depend only on
u
and
v
, respectively.

Remark 5.4
.

Keeping in the mind equality (
5.2
), we conclude that
\lambda\neq 0
, which means that the marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field cannot be flat (see Proposition 3.1 in
[
16
]
).

Remark 5.5
.

Since
\nu=0
for marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field, from the equation
\varkappa=-2\mu\nu
, we get
\varkappa=0
, which means that all marginally trapped surfaces with parallel mean curvature vector field are surfaces with flat normal connection (see also Proposition 3.2 in
[
16
]
).

6.
Examples

In this section, we will show how to find the canonical principal parameters on a special family of marginally trapped surfaces – the marginally trapped meridian surfaces of parabolic type.

In
[
12
]
, a family of surfaces lying on a standard rotational hypersurface in the four-dimensional Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{4}
was constructed.
They are called
meridian surfaces
because they are one-parameter systems of meridians of the rotational hypersurface. Using a similar idea, special families of two-dimensional spacelike surfaces lying
on rotational hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
were constructed in papers
[
16
,
17
]
. Depending on the casual character of the axis of the rotational hypersurface, as well as the type of the spheres in
a 3-dimensional Minkowski subspace and the casual character of the spherical curves, we
distinguish different types of spacelike or timelike meridian surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
– of elliptic type, hyperbolic type, or parabolic type.

The most interesting rotation is the rotation about a lightlixe axis, so here we will consider two-dimensional surfaces lying on a rotational hypersurface with lightlike axis – these are the so-called meridian surfaces of parabolic type.

For the construction of meridian surfaces of parabolic type it is convenient to use the pseudo-orthonormal base

\{e_{1},e_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
, where
e_{1}^{2}=e_{2}^{2}=e_{3}^{2}=1
,
e_{4}^{2}=-1
and

\displaystyle{\xi_{1}=\frac{e_{3}+e_{4}}{\sqrt{2}}}
,
\displaystyle{\xi_{2}=\frac{-e_{3}+e_{4}}{\sqrt{2}}}
(see
[
17
]
). It is clear that
\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{1}\rangle=0
,
\langle\xi_{2},\xi_{2}\rangle=0
,
\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle=-1
. A rotational hypersurface with lightlike axis
\xi_{2}
can
be parametrized by

\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}:Z(u,w^{1},w^{2})=f(u)\,w^{1}\cos w^{2}\,e_{1}+f(u)\,w^{1}\sin w^{2}\,e_{2}+\left(f(u)\frac{(w^{1})^{2}}{2}+g(u)\right)\xi_{1}+f(u)\,\xi_{2},

where
f=f(u),\,\,g=g(u)
are smooth functions, defined in an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, such that
-f^{\prime}(u)g^{\prime}(u)>0
,
f(u)>0
,

u\in I
. Assuming that
w^{1}=w^{1}(v)
,
w^{2}=w^{2}(v)
,
v\in J\subset\mathbb{R}
,
and
(\dot{w}^{1})^{2}+(\dot{w}^{2})^{2}\neq 0
, we consider the one-parameter system of meridians
of the rotational hypersurface with lightlike
axis defined by

\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m}:z(u,v)=Z(u,w^{1}(v),w^{2}(v)),

where
u\in I
,
v\in J
. This surface is called a
meridian surface of parabolic type
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
w^{1}=\omega(v)
,
w^{2}=v
, so the parametrization of the surface
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m}
takes the following form

\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m}:z(u,v)=f(u)\,\omega(v)\cos v\,e_{1}+f(u)\,\omega(v)\sin v\,e_{2}+\left(f(u)\frac{(\omega(v))^{2}}{2}+g(u)\right)\xi_{1}+f(u)\,\xi_{2}.

In
[
17
]
, it is shown that each meridian surface of parabolic type is generated by a plane meridian curve
m
with curvature

\varkappa_{m}(u)=\displaystyle{\frac{f^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}}{(-2f^{\prime}g^{\prime})^{\frac{3}{2}}}}
, and a curve
\overline{c}
with curvature
\overline{\kappa}(v)=\displaystyle{\frac{\omega\ddot{\omega}-2\dot{\omega}^{2}-\omega^{2}}{(\dot{\omega}^{2}+\omega^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}}
lying on the paraboloid
\mathcal{P}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
,
defined by

\mathcal{P}^{2}:z(w^{1},w^{2})=w^{1}\cos w^{2}\,e_{1}+w^{1}\sin w^{2}\,e_{2}+\frac{(w^{1})^{2}}{2}\,\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}.

In the case
f(u)=u
,
g(u)=au+b
, i.e.
\varkappa_{m}(u)=0
, the meridian curve is a straight line and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m}
is a developable ruled surface in
\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
which is a cone
[
17
]
. In the case
\varkappa_{m}(u)\neq 0
, the meridian surface of parabolic type
\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m}

is marginally trapped if and only if

\overline{\kappa}(v)=a=\mathrm{const},\;a\neq 0
, and the
meridian curve is defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=u>0;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\displaystyle{\frac{\pm 1}{2a^{3}}\left(\frac{a^{2}u^{2}\mp 2auc}{c\mp au}-2c\ln|c\mp au|+b\right)},\end{array}

where
b
and
c
are constants,
c\neq 0
(Theorem 3.2,
[
17
]
).

Now, we will show how to find the canonical principal parameters of the marginally trapped meridian surface of parabolic type for the following choice of the constants:
a=-1
,
b=0
, and
c=1
. In a similar way, one can find the canonical principal parameters for arbitrary constants.

So, let us consider the functions

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054ptf(u)=u;\\
\vskip 5.69054ptg(u)=\displaystyle{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{u^{2}+2u}{1+u}-2\ln|1+u|\right)}.\end{array}

Then the curvature
\varkappa_{m}(u)
has the following form

\varkappa_{m}(u)=-\displaystyle{\frac{1}{u^{2}}}.

Since the curvature
\overline{\kappa}(v)=a=-1
, the function
\omega(v)
must satisfy the differential equation:

\omega\ddot{\omega}-2\dot{\omega}^{2}-\omega^{2}=-(\dot{\omega}^{2}+\omega^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}},

whose solution is given by
\omega(v)=2\cos(v+p)
,
p\in\mathbb{R}
. For simplicity, we choose
p=0
, i.e.
\omega(v)=2\cos v
.
It can easily be calculated that the coefficients of the first fundamental form are given by

E=\frac{u^{2}}{(u+1)^{2}};\qquad F=0;\qquad G=4u^{2},

and the coefficients of the second fundamental form are

L=0;\qquad M=-\frac{2}{u(u+1)};\qquad N=0.

Since
M\neq 0
, the parameters
(u,v)
are not principal. To find principal parametrization of the surface, we need to find parameters
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
for which
\bar{F}=0
and
\bar{M}=0
(see Proposition 2.1 in
[
15
]
). Let us consider the following change of the parametrization

\begin{split}u&=e^{\bar{u}-\bar{v}}-1,\\
v&=\frac{\bar{u}+\bar{v}}{2}.\end{split}

Direct computations show that the conditions
\bar{F}=\bar{M}=0
are valid for the new parameters
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
, which means that the parameters are principal.
With respect to the principal parameters, the coefficients of the first fundamental form are expressed as

\bar{E}=2(e^{\bar{u}-\bar{v}}-1)^{2};\qquad\bar{F}=0;\qquad\bar{G}=2(e^{\bar{u}-\bar{v}}-1)^{2}.

According to
[
15

, the mean curvature vector field

\vskip 5.69054ptn_{2}=\displaystyle{\frac{u+1}{u}\left(2\cos^{2}v\,e_{1}+\sin 2v\,e_{2}+\left(2\cos^{2}v+\frac{u^{2}}{2(u+1)^{2}}\right)\,\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\right)}.\end{array}
