Machine learning–based regression analysis of cyclopentane and cyclohexane molecular graphs
Authors: Muhammad Irfan, Nabeela Bashir, M. Umair Shahzad, Kamal M. Othman, Abdulfattah Noorwalli, AbdulGuddoos S. A. Gaid, Esam Y. O. Zafar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cyclopentane is used to make high-performance plastics and composites. Cyclohexane is a polymer that has several specialized applications due to its unique properties, such as good chemical resistance, low moisture absorption, and high thermal stability. Despite their industrial relevance, a systematic comparative analysis of degree-based and neighborhood degree-based topological indices and their predictive capability for chemical properties remains limited in the existing literature. In this study, we compute and analyze several fundamental molecular descriptors, including the Zagreb indices, Randić index, atom bond connectivity index, geometric arithmetic index, sum connectivity index, and augmented Zagreb index for the molecular graphs networks of Cyclopentane and Cyclohexane. These indices can be used to correlate various properties of the polymers with the topological indices. The obtained topological indices are employed to establish quantitative structure–property relationships (QSPR) between molecular structure and physicochemical properties of these polymers. To address the lack of predictive modeling in earlier graph-theoretical studies, a machine-learning-based regression models are developed and applied to evaluate the strength of correlation between molecular descriptors and experimental property data. This approach provides a computationally efficient framework for molecular property estimation and offers a foundation for extending advanced learning models to more complex polymer networks.

Introduction

Triisobutylaluminum-titanium tetra and trichloride catalysts were used to produce Cyclopentane at a conversion rate of 7–14%. Ether was used to extract the unfractionated Cyclopentane, which was found to be crystalline through x-ray examination. However, this method was unable to dissolve 88% of the polymer. After being fully soluble in benzene, the residual material underwent annealing to produce a highly crystalline substance that broke down at temperatures higher than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$260^{\circ }$$\end{document}C. The characteristics of this material were acquired by quickly extending a crystalline Cyclopentane fibre from a melt see1. A triclinic unit cell was used to index crystalline Cyclopentane, with three monomer units within an identity period of 6.6A.

Due to its chemical corrosion resistance and thermal stability, Cyclopentane is utilised in high-performance polymers and composites, which makes it perfect for severe conditions. It is utilised in the medical field to create biocompatible materials for implants and devices due to its inflexibility and compatibility with biological tissues.

Triisobutylaluminum-titanium tetra and trichloride catalysts (4/1) were used to produce Cyclohexane, with conversions restricted only by the reaction conditions. 94% of the unfractionated Cyclohexane was not soluble in ether, and x-ray examination revealed that the substance was crystalline. While some extra benzene was soluble, the majority >90% was only soluble in heated Tetralin. The latter was discovered to be very crystalline and to break down at temperatures higher than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$300^{\circ }$$\end{document}C upon annealing. The characteristics of this material were acquired by quickly extending a crystalline Cyclohexane fibre from a melt. Three monomeric units per triclinic unit cell served as the foundation for the indexing of crystalline Cyclohexane, see detail in1. 6.6A or 13.2A was found to be the identity period along the fibre axis.

While Cyclohexane has good dielectric qualities and is resistant to chemicals and moisture, it is used in protective coatings, encapsulants, and insulating materials. It is appropriate for optical applications like lenses and light guides because of its transparency and stability in a variety of environmental conditions.

Machine learning framework

Linear regression

Linear regression is a fundamental supervised machine-learning technique used to model and predict the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables by assuming a linear functional form as discussed in33,34. In its simplest form, linear regression establishes a linear relationship between a single predictor (independent variable) and a response (dependent variable), making it an effective and interpretable tool for predictive analysis. The independent variable, also referred to as the predictor or explanatory variable, is assumed to be unaffected by other variables in the model (as mentioned in35,36); however, variations in this variable directly influence the dependent variable. The dependent variable, commonly called the response or outcome variable, represents the quantity to be predicted or estimated. Linear regression aims to determine the optimal parameters that minimize the discrepancy between observed and predicted values, typically by employing the least squares estimation method.

Mathematically, the linear regression model is expressed as\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} Y=a X +b. \end{aligned}$$\end{document}The dependent variable is denoted by Y, the independent variable by X, the Y-intercept by b, and the independent variable’s coefficient by a.

Support vector regression

Support Vector Regression is a supervised machine learning technique applied to estimate the response of independent variables and a dependent variable37. It does not attempt to have all the data point fall precisely, rather it tries to have a limited range of errors and prioritizes the most significant ones.

During SVR, a model is obtained after identifying a function whose error is within a constant threshold of requirement, which is in this case known as the error level, denoted as, ε. Data points that lie within this margin are thrown away and data points that lie out of the margin (which is termed as support vectors) are utilized in constructing the model. This causes SVR to be applicable to nonlinear and complicated data. Modeling of nonlinear relationships can also be done using kernel functions.

Mathematically, it is expressed as:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} |Y - f(X)| \le \epsilon \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Ridge regression

Ridge regression is an advanced form of the linear regression which enhances the performance of a model when a data has a high number of variables or when the variables are very close to each other38. It introduces a penalty term, which regulates the coefficients size.

In this approach, the independent variables are employed to predict dependent variable however, the model prevents the growth of the coefficients in an unacceptable way. This makes the model less prone to overfitting and makes it more stable.

Ridge regression is aimed at minimizing the error in predictions as well the values of the coefficients. Mathematically, it is written as:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \min \left( \sum (Y_i - \hat{Y}_i)^2 + \lambda \sum a^2 \right) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Random forest regression

The random forest regression is a machine learning method of supervision through regression in which several decision trees give predictions39. It eliminates using one model instead of utilizing a single model but instead a combination of several trees is utilized to enhance accuracy.

The decision trees are trained on separate parts of the data and the decision trees provide individual predictions. The last step is to get the average of all the predictions. This will minimize overfitting and maximize model reliability.

Random Forest enjoys popularity and finds application in many complex and nonlinear datasets.

Mathematically, it is expressed as:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} Y = \frac{1}{N} \sum _{i=1}^{N} T_i(X) \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

Comparison and discussion

The topological indices applied in this paper to examine the molecular structures of Cyclopentane and Cyclohexane are of the following two categories: degree based indices and the neighborhood degree based indices. There are the Degree-based indices the First Zagreb index and Second Zagreb index, the Modified Zagreb index, Randi c index, the Atom Bond Connectivity index, Geometric Arithmetic index, the Sum Connectivity Index, and the Augmented Zagreb index. The major indices used are used to characterize the simplicity of connectivity of atoms of a graph of molecules using vertex degrees.

In an attempt to assess the validity of these indices in forecasting physicochemical properties, various machine learning algorithms have been used, namely, Linear Regression, Ridge Regression, Support Vector Regression (SVR), and Random Forest. Linear Regression is indicated to be the best in most of the properties, with a minimum error values (MAE and RMSE) and a high value of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} variance, which shows it to exhibit high predictive accuracy. Ridge Regression is also effective and especially effective on some properties which include molar weight. Conversely, errors in the case of SVR and Random Forest models are relatively higher and errors are not always consistent, yet in certain instances, both allow reasonable predictions.

On the whole, the research results explain that the two kinds of indexes both work well in modelling the molecular properties, but the neighbourhood Degree-based indices are more predictive because they are capable of describing more structural detail. Also, its results point out the fact that basic linear models are adequate and they tend to be much more reliable than more complex models to work with this kind of data, so the results of using simple linear models are effective and correct tools of chemical property prediction.

Conclusion

The analysis performed in this study is a thorough graph-theoretical and machine-learning-based analysis of the molecular graph of Cyclopentane and Cyclohexane. Both Degree-based topological indices, and neighborhood Degree-based topological indices were both calculated and turn out to be in closed form, with obvious linear behaviour with the repetitive units. The comparative outcomes expose that Cyclohexane tends to achieve a bigger index than those of Cyclopentane due to its comparatively larger and more networked grouping whereas indexes of neighborhoods offer a more detailed and informative portrayal of museum structure in comparison with easy tools of description of degree. The machine learning analysis also displays that these topological indices are very much effective in the predicting of important physics chemical characteristics, which comprise melting point, boiling point, density, molar weight, flash point, log P, molar refractivity, and polarizability. Linear Regression indicates the best and most consistent and accurate result of the tested models, although Ridge Regression involves minor improvements in molar weight with certain neighborhood-related tests, and SVR or Random Forest contributes to no significant overall benefit. Hence, the paper finds that simple linear models along with topological descriptors (particularly the indices based on neighborhood degree) are an effective, trustworthy and computationally economical representation of the leaves of QSPR analysis and molecular property prediction of polymer-related structures like Cyclopentane and Cyclohexane.
