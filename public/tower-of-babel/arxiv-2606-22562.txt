A Colombeau–Beurling criterion for the Riemann hypothesis
A. Álvarez Cruz 1 and E.A. Álvarez Gutiérrez 2

Abstract
. This paper establishes an equivalence between the Riemann hypothesis and the association of a single moderate net in the Colombeau algebra 𝒢 ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) \mathcal{G}(0,1) built from damped Báez–Duarte sums. Two concrete damping strategies are presented: an exponential damping e − k ​ ε 2 e^{-k\varepsilon^{2}} with super - exponential truncation, and a polynomial damping k − δ ⁡ ( ε ) k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)} , δ ⁡ ( ε ) = ( log ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) ) − α \delta(\varepsilon)=(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-\alpha} , with polynomial truncation. Under the Riemann hypothesis the nets are shown to be moderate and associated to − ι ⁡ ( χ ) -\iota(\chi) ; conversely, any moderate net of this form that is associated to − ι ⁡ ( χ ) -\iota(\chi) forces the Riemann hypothesis to hold.



1.
Introduction

The Riemann hypothesis (RH) asserts that all non
-
trivial zeros of the Riemann
zeta function
\zeta(s)
lie on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
.
The classical Nyman–Beurling criterion
[
1
,
2
]
states
that RH holds if and only if the characteristic function

\chi=\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}
belongs to the
L^{2}
-closure of the span of the
functions
\rho(\theta/x)=\{\theta/x\}
for
\theta\in(0,1]
.
Here
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the integer part and
\{\cdot\}
the
fractional part.
Báez–Duarte
[
3
]
proved that one may restrict
\theta
to the
discrete set
\{1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and still obtain an equivalent condition:

\chi\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}(0,1)},

where
\varphi_{\theta}(x)=\{\theta/x\}
.

Throughout the text an overline denotes closure in the indicated norm; thus

\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}(0,1)}
is the set of
all
L^{2}(0,1)
functions that are limits (in
L^{2}
norm) of finite linear
combinations of the functions
\varphi_{1/k}
.

The proof of Báez–Duarte relies on the fundamental Möbius identity

(1)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)\,\varphi_{1/k}(x)=-\chi(x),\qquad 0<x\leq 1,

which converges pointwise and, under RH, in
L^{2}(0,1)
. Here
\mu
denotes the Möbius function (a formal definition is recalled in
Section
2
); in particular
|\mu(k)|\leq 1
for every

k\in\mathbb{N}
.
This suggests studying damped sums

f_{\delta,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k)\,\phi_{k}(\delta)\,\varphi_{1/k}(x),

where
\phi_{k}(\delta)
is a damping factor with
\phi_{k}(\delta)\to 1
as

\delta\to 0
.
Under RH one obtains the double limit

\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{\delta,n}=-\chi\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,1),

by Abel summation in Hilbert spaces (cf.
[
3
, Theorem 2]
).

In this paper the double limit is translated into the Colombeau algebra

\mathcal{G}(0,1)
of generalized functions
[
4
]
, a framework that
has proved powerful for handling products of distributions and nonlinear
problems (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
)
and continues to be developed for nonlinear PDEs, stochastic analysis and
singular geometry
[
8
,
9
]
.
The damping parameter and the truncation index are coupled with the
regularization parameter
\varepsilon
of the algebra, yielding a

single
net
F_{\varepsilon}
whose asymptotic behavior is equivalent
to RH.
Two concrete realizations are presented:

(E)
exponential damping
\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}

with
\beta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}
and

n(\varepsilon)=\lfloor\exp(\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2}))\rfloor
;

(P)
polynomial damping
\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}

with
\delta(\varepsilon)=(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-\alpha}

(
0<\alpha<1
) and
n(\varepsilon)=\lfloor\varepsilon^{-M}\rfloor
(
M>0
).
Placing the double limit inside
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
forces the truncation
and damping parameters to be coupled in an
\varepsilon
-dependent way,
producing a single moderate net.
This algebraic embedding allows a clean application of Mazur’s theorem,
reading off the
L^{2}
-closure of the Báez–Duarte space from the weak
Colombeau association without delicate interchange of limits.

Both choices lead to a moderate net

F_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)\,\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\,(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(x)

that is associated to
-\iota(\chi)
if and only if RH holds.
The net given by the full infinite series is analyzed in
Section
7
, and strong convergence is discussed in
Section
8
.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 collects the necessary preliminaries.
Section 3 states general conditions on a damping family that ensure the net
is moderate and associated to
-\chi
under RH.
Sections 4 and 5 verify these conditions for the exponential and polynomial
damping.
Section 6 proves that association implies RH.
Section
7
treats the full Möbius series in

\mathcal{G}(0,1)
.
Section
8
discusses the question of strong convergence, which
remains open for both damping families.
Section 9 contains the main theorem and concluding remarks.

2.
Preliminaries

The Riemann zeta function is defined for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
by

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}
and extends meromorphically to

\mathbb{C}
with a simple pole at
s=1
.
The Riemann hypothesis (RH) asserts that every non
-
trivial zero of

\zeta(s)
satisfies
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
.

For a real number
x
, let
\lfloor x\rfloor
denote its integer part
and
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
its fractional part.
Given
\theta\in(0,1]
, the Beurling functions are

\varphi_{\theta}(x)=\{\theta/x\}
. The classical Nyman–Beurling criterion
states that RH holds if and only if the characteristic function

\chi=\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}
belongs to the
L^{2}(0,1)
-closure of the
span of the functions
\varphi_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1]
.
Báez–Duarte
[
3
]
strengthened this by showing that one may
restrict
\theta
to the discrete set
\{1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
still obtain an equivalent condition.

Theorem 2.1

(Báez–Duarte)
.

RH holds if and only if

\chi\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}(0,1)}
.

The Möbius function
\mu:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,0,1\}
is given by

\mu(1)=1
,
\mu(n)=(-1)^{r}
when
n
is a product of
r
distinct
primes, and
\mu(n)=0
otherwise. In particular
|\mu(k)|\leq 1
for
all
k
. The series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)/k
converges to

0
(a classical fact equivalent to the prime number theorem), and the
fundamental identity

(2)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)\,\varphi_{1/k}(x)=-\chi(x),\qquad 0<x\leq 1,

holds pointwise. Under RH the convergence is also in
L^{2}(0,1)
; a proof can be found in
[
3
]
.

The Colombeau algebra
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
of generalized functions

[
4
]
is constructed as follows. Fix a mollifier

\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with
\int\rho=1
and set

\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}\rho(x/\varepsilon)
for

\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. A net

(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\subset C^{\infty}(0,1)
is called

moderate
if for every compact subset
K
and every multi
-
index

\alpha
there exist constants
C,N>0
such that

\sup_{K}|\partial^{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{-N}
; it
is
negligible
if for every
K,\alpha
and every integer
m\geq 0

there exists
C>0
with

\sup_{K}|\partial^{\alpha}u_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\varepsilon^{m}
.
Moderate nets form an algebra
\mathcal{E}_{M}(0,1)
and negligible nets
an ideal
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
; the Colombeau algebra is the quotient

\mathcal{G}(0,1)=\mathcal{E}_{M}(0,1)/\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. A function

f\in L^{2}(0,1)
is embedded as the class

\iota(f)=[(f*\rho_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}]
.

Let
\mathcal{D}(0,1)
denote the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact
support in
(0,1)
(test functions), equipped with its usual inductive limit
topology. Given a moderate net
(u_{\varepsilon})
, we write

u=[(u_{\varepsilon})]
for its class in
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
.
We say that
u
is
associated
with a distribution

T\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
, and write
u\approx T
, if for every test function

\psi\in\mathcal{D}(0,1)
one has

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{0}^{1}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\psi(x)\,dx=\langle T,\psi\rangle.

If
u\approx\iota(f)
for some
f\in L^{2}(0,1)
, then the net

u_{\varepsilon}
converges
weakly
in
L^{2}
to
f
, a fact we
symbolize by
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup f
in
L^{2}(0,1)
.

Two generalized functions
u=[(u_{\varepsilon})]
and
v=[(v_{\varepsilon})]

are said to be
associated
, written
u\approx v
, if their difference is
associated with the zero distribution, i.e., if

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(x))\psi(x)\,dx=0

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(0,1)
. Equality in the algebra
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
corresponds to a much stronger
notion:
[(u_{\varepsilon})]=[(v_{\varepsilon})]
if and only if

(u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, that is, if the
difference decays faster than any power of
\varepsilon
uniformly on
compact sets together with all its derivatives. Hence association is a
strictly weaker equivalence relation than equality in the algebra.

A few concrete nets on
(0,1)
clarify the quotient construction.
The constant net
(1)_{\varepsilon}
gives the class
[1_{\varepsilon}]
,
representing the constant function
1
.
The net
g_{\varepsilon}(x)=\exp(-x^{2}/\varepsilon)
is moderate and
converges to
0
on any compact set
[a,b]\subset(0,1)
with
a>0
;
indeed
\sup_{x\in[a,b]}g_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{-a^{2}/\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon^{m})

for every
m
, hence
[(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}]=[0]
.
The net
h_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{-\varepsilon/x}
is moderate but not equivalent
to
1
: for
x\in[a,b]\subset(0,1)
with
a>0
,

\sup_{x\in[a,b]}h_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{-\varepsilon/b}\approx 1
, which does not
decay like a power of
\varepsilon
; moreover
1-h_{\varepsilon}(x)\sim\varepsilon/x
,
so
[(h_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}]\neq[1_{\varepsilon}]
. Since

\sup_{x\in[a,b]}h_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 1
, the net is not negligible and

[(h_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}]\neq[0]
.
The constant net
c_{\varepsilon}=e^{-1/\varepsilon}
is negligible because

e^{-1/\varepsilon}\leq C_{m}\varepsilon^{m}
for every
m
; thus
[c_{\varepsilon}]=0
.
A particularly instructive example is the oscillatory net

s_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sin(x/\varepsilon)
. Each
s_{\varepsilon}
is smooth and
satisfies
|\partial^{\alpha}s_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}
,
so the net is moderate. For any test function
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(0,1)
,

\int_{0}^{1}\sin(x/\varepsilon)\psi(x)\,dx\to 0
by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma,
hence
[(s_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}]\approx 0
. Nevertheless

\sup_{x}|s_{\varepsilon}(x)|=1
, so the net is not negligible, and its
Colombeau class is a non-trivial generalized function that is associated with
the zero distribution. These examples illustrate that two moderate nets represent
the same generalized function precisely when their difference, together with all
its derivatives, decays faster than any power of
\varepsilon
uniformly on
compact sets.

3.
General framework and basic estimates

Let
\{\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of real numbers for each

\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and let
n(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
with

n(\varepsilon)\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Define the net

(3)

F_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)\,\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\,\bigl(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}\bigr)(x),\qquad\varepsilon\in(0,1].

A simple but crucial observation is that for every
k
and almost every

x\in(0,1)
,

|\mu(k)\,\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\,\varphi_{1/k}(x)|\leq|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|\cdot|\mu(k)|\cdot|\varphi_{1/k}(x)|\leq|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|,

because
|\mu(k)|\leq 1
and
|\varphi_{1/k}(x)|\leq 1
.
After convolution with the non
-
negative mollifier
\rho_{\varepsilon}

(which has
L^{1}
-norm
1
), the same bound holds:

\bigl|\mu(k)\,\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\,(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(x)\bigr|\leq|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|,\qquad x\in(0,1).

This estimate is used repeatedly.

The following three properties ensure that the net
F_{\varepsilon}
is
moderate and, under RH, associated to
-\chi
.

Condition 3.1

(Moderateness)
.

There exist
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|\leq C\varepsilon^{-N}

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.

Condition 3.2

(Truncation error)
.

Under RH, set

f_{\delta}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k)\phi_{k}(\delta)\varphi_{1/k}

(the limit exists in
L^{2}
by Abel summation; see
[
14
]
for the
classical method and
[
3
, Theorem 2]
for the
L^{2}
convergence). Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\varphi_{1/k}-f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Bigr\|_{L^{2}}=0,

where
\delta(\varepsilon)
is the parameter naturally associated to the family
(for the two families below
\delta
coincides with the explicit damping
parameter).

Condition 3.3

(Damping error)
.

Under RH,
\|f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
as

\varepsilon\to 0
.

(Here
f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}
denotes the limit introduced in
Condition
3.2
with
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
.)

Theorem 3.1

(General sufficient condition)
.

Assume
\{\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\}
and
n(\varepsilon)
satisfy Conditions

3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
.
Then the net
F_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
3
) is moderate and
associated to
-\iota(\chi)
under RH.

Proof.

Moderateness follows from Condition
3.1
and the basic bound.
For any compact
K
and multi
-
index
\alpha
,

\sup_{K}|\partial^{\alpha}F_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|\,\sup_{K}\bigl|\partial^{\alpha}(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon})(x)\bigr|\leq C_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}|\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)|\leq C\,C_{\alpha}\,\varepsilon^{-(N+|\alpha|)},

which is the moderateness condition.

For association, write

F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}=T_{\varepsilon}+R_{\varepsilon}

with

T_{\varepsilon}=\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\varphi_{1/k}-f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Bigr)*\rho_{\varepsilon},\qquad R_{\varepsilon}=(f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\chi)*\rho_{\varepsilon}.

Mollification is a contraction on
L^{2}
, hence by Condition
3.2

\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
and by Condition
3.3

\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
.
Thus
\|F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
,
which implies weak convergence in
L^{2}
; i.e.
[(F_{\varepsilon})]\approx-\iota(\chi)
.
∎

Remark 3.2

(Directional use of the conditions)
.

Conditions
3.2
and
3.3
are stated under the
Riemann hypothesis and are only employed to prove association when RH
holds.
The converse implication (Section
6
) does not rely on
any of these damping
-
specific conditions; it uses only the general form
of the net and a convexity argument with the span of the

\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}
.

4.
Exponential damping

Let
\beta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}
and

n(\varepsilon)=\lfloor\exp(\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2}))\rfloor
.
The damping coefficients are
\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}
.

4.1.
Moderateness

\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}\leq\frac{e^{-\beta(\varepsilon)}}{1-e^{-\beta(\varepsilon)}}\leq\frac{2}{\beta(\varepsilon)}=2\varepsilon^{-2},

so Condition
3.1
holds with
N=2
.

4.2.
Truncation error

Define

f_{\delta}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)e^{-k\delta}\varphi_{1/k}
.
For each
\delta>0
the series converges absolutely, hence in

L^{2}(0,1)
, because

|\mu(k)e^{-k\delta}\varphi_{1/k}|\leq e^{-k\delta}
and

\sum_{k}e^{-k\delta}<\infty
.
Under RH, the undamped series
\sum_{k}\mu(k)\varphi_{1/k}
converges in

L^{2}(0,1)
to
-\chi
, and the Abel means
f_{\delta}
(
[
14
]
) converge to

-\chi
in
L^{2}
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
.
Then

\Bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}\varphi_{1/k}-f_{\beta(\varepsilon)}\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{k=n(\varepsilon)+1}^{\infty}e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}=\frac{e^{-(n(\varepsilon)+1)\beta(\varepsilon)}}{1-e^{-\beta(\varepsilon)}}\leq 2\varepsilon^{-2}e^{-n(\varepsilon)\beta(\varepsilon)}.

Since
n(\varepsilon)\beta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}\exp(\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2}))
,
the exponential factor decays faster than any power of
\varepsilon
(see the proof in Appendix
A
); hence
the truncation error in
L^{2}
vanishes, satisfying
Condition
3.2
.

4.3.
Damping error

The identity

f_{\delta}+\chi=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)(e^{-k\delta}-1)\varphi_{1/k}

holds.
Under RH, the undamped series
\sum\mu(k)\varphi_{1/k}
converges in
L^{2}

to
-\chi
(this is the content of the Báez–Duarte theorem
[
3
]
).
Let
\eta>0
and choose
N
so large that

\|\sum_{k>N}\mu(k)\varphi_{1/k}\|_{L^{2}}<\eta
.
Split the series as

A_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu(k)(e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}-1)\varphi_{1/k},\quad B_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k>N}\mu(k)(e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}-1)\varphi_{1/k}.

For fixed
N
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
we have
\beta(\varepsilon)\to 0
and

e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}-1\to 0
uniformly on
\{1,\dots,N\}
, so

\|A_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
.
For
B_{\varepsilon}
, set
b_{k}=e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}
and

S_{k}=\sum_{j=N+1}^{k}\mu(j)\varphi_{1/j}
for
k\geq N+1
.
Under RH the series converges in
L^{2}
, so

S_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}S_{k}
exists and

\|S_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}<\eta
by the choice of
N
.
Decompose

B_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\mu(k)b_{k}\varphi_{1/k}-S_{\infty}=:B_{1}-S_{\infty}.

Summation by parts applied to the finite sum

\sum_{k=N+1}^{M}\mu(k)b_{k}\varphi_{1/k}
gives,
for every
M\geq N+1
,

\sum_{k=N+1}^{M}\mu(k)b_{k}\varphi_{1/k}=S_{M}b_{M}+\sum_{k=N+1}^{M-1}S_{k}(b_{k}-b_{k+1}).

Letting
M\to\infty
we have
b_{M}\to 0
and, because

\|S_{M}-S_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
, the sequence
\{\|S_{M}\|_{L^{2}}\}

is bounded; hence
\|S_{M}b_{M}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|S_{M}\|_{L^{2}}\,b_{M}\to 0
.
Consequently,

B_{1}=\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}S_{k}\,(b_{k}-b_{k+1}).

Now,

\|B_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}S_{k}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})\Bigr\|_{L^{2}}+\|S_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
b_{k}-b_{k+1}\geq 0
and
\|S_{k}\|_{L^{2}}<\eta
for all

k>N
,

\Bigl\|\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}S_{k}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})\Bigr\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sup_{k>N}\|S_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}(b_{k}-b_{k+1})=\sup_{k>N}\|S_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\,b_{N+1}<\eta\,e^{-(N+1)\beta(\varepsilon)}\leq\eta.

Together with
\|S_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}<\eta
this yields

\|B_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}<2\eta
.
Hence
\|f_{\beta(\varepsilon)}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\eta
for small

\varepsilon
; since
\eta
is arbitrary, the damping error tends to zero.
Thus Condition
3.3
is satisfied.

5.
Polynomial damping

Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
M>0
.
Define
\delta(\varepsilon)=(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-\alpha}
and

n(\varepsilon)=\lfloor\varepsilon^{-M}\rfloor
.
The damping coefficients are
\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}
.

5.1.
Moderateness

Using the integral comparison for
t\mapsto t^{-\delta}
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{n(\varepsilon)}t^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}\,dt=1+\frac{n(\varepsilon)^{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}-1}{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}\leq\frac{2\,n(\varepsilon)^{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}}{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}.

Now
n(\varepsilon)^{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}=\varepsilon^{-M(1-\delta(\varepsilon))}=\varepsilon^{-M}\,\varepsilon^{M\delta(\varepsilon)}
.
Since

\varepsilon^{M\delta(\varepsilon)}=\exp(-M\delta(\varepsilon)\log(1/\varepsilon))=\exp(-M(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1-\alpha})\leq 1
,
we obtain
n(\varepsilon)^{1-\delta(\varepsilon)}\leq\varepsilon^{-M}
.
Also
1/(1-\delta(\varepsilon))\leq 2
for small
\varepsilon
.
Hence
\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}\leq 4\varepsilon^{-M}
,
and Condition
3.1
holds with
N=M
.

5.2.
Truncation error

Under RH, the limit

f_{\delta}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k)k^{-\delta}\varphi_{1/k}

exists in
L^{2}
(again by Abel summation). The truncated error estimate

\|f_{\delta,n}-f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,n^{-\delta/3},\qquad n\geq 1,\;\delta>0,

where
f_{\delta,n}
denotes the truncated sum, is obtained in Appendix
B

from the Balazard–Saias lemma; the extension to the critical line follows
the argument of Báez–Duarte
[
3
, Theorem 2]
(see (
7
)).
Therefore, with
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)
and
n=n(\varepsilon)
,

\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,n(\varepsilon)^{-\delta(\varepsilon)/3}=C\exp\Bigl(-\frac{M}{3}\delta(\varepsilon)\log(1/\varepsilon)\Bigr)=C\exp\Bigl(-\frac{M}{3}(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1-\alpha}\Bigr)\longrightarrow 0,

satisfying Condition
3.2
.

The upper bound

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\delta^{1/2}

is proved in Appendix
B
(see (
8
)). Hence

\|f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-\alpha/2}\to 0
,
which verifies Condition
3.3
.

6.
From association to the Riemann hypothesis

Now it is shown that if any net of the form (
3
) is moderate and
associated to
-\iota(\chi)
, then RH follows.
This part is independent of the specific damping family.

Proposition 6.1
.

If the net
(F_{\varepsilon})
defined in (
3
) is moderate and
associated to
-\iota(\chi)
, then RH is true.

Proof.

Association means
F_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup-\chi
weakly in

L^{2}(0,1)
.
Each
F_{\varepsilon}
belongs to the finite
-
dimensional space

V_{\varepsilon}=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}:1\leq k\leq n(\varepsilon)\}
.
Therefore
-\chi
lies in the weak closure of

\bigcup_{\varepsilon}V_{\varepsilon}
.

Consider the convex set

W=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}:k\in\mathbb{N},\,\varepsilon\in(0,1]\}
.
As shown in
[
3
]
,

\overline{W}^{L^{2}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
By Mazur’s theorem (see
[
12
]
or

[
13
]
), the weak closure of a convex set in a
Banach space coincides with its strong closure.
Applying this to
W
gives

\overline{W}^{\text{weak}}=\overline{W}^{\text{strong}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.

Since
\bigcup_{\varepsilon}V_{\varepsilon}\subset W
, the weak closure of
the union is contained in the weak closure of
W
.
Hence

-\chi\in\overline{\bigcup_{\varepsilon}V_{\varepsilon}}^{\text{weak}}\subseteq\overline{W}^{\text{weak}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.

The constant function
1
also lies in this closed span (see
[
3
, §3]
);
therefore

\chi=-(-\chi)\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
The Báez–Duarte criterion (Theorem
2.1
) now implies RH.
∎

Remark 6.2

(On the equality of strong closures)
.

The application of Mazur’s theorem to the convex set
W
is legitimate.
The key step is the identification

\overline{W}^{\text{strong}}=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
The inclusion
\supseteq
is immediate because each
\varphi_{1/k}
is the
strong
L^{2}
-limit of its mollifications
\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}\in W
.
For the converse inclusion
\subseteq
, observe that any generator

\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is a smooth function on
(0,1)
.
By a classical result of Báez
-
Duarte
[
3
, §3]
, every step
function constant on the intervals
(1/(n+1),1/n]
lies in

\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
Since smooth functions can be approximated in
L^{2}
by such step
functions, we obtain

\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
Taking linear combinations and closure yields

\overline{W}^{\text{strong}}\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
.
Thus the two closures coincide.

7.
The Möbius series in
\mathcal{G}(0,1)

For the exponential damping, one can consider the net given by the full
infinite series without truncation:

S_{\varepsilon}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)\,e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}\,\bigl(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon}\bigr)(x),\qquad\beta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}.

Theorem 7.1
.

The net
(S_{\varepsilon})
is moderate in
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
and associated to

-\iota(\chi)
if and only if the Riemann hypothesis holds.

Proof.

Moderateness.

For any compact
K
and multi
-
index
\alpha
,

\sup_{K}|\partial^{\alpha}S_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}=C_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\frac{e^{-\beta(\varepsilon)}}{1-e^{-\beta(\varepsilon)}}\leq 2C_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{-(|\alpha|+2)},

so
(S_{\varepsilon})
is moderate.

Association under RH.

Assume RH. By the Möbius identity (
2
),

S_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)(e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}-1)\,(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon})=:R_{\varepsilon}.

The same splitting and summation by parts as in Section
4

(without the need for truncation) shows that
\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
.
Hence
S_{\varepsilon}\to-\chi
weakly in
L^{2}
, i.e.
[(S_{\varepsilon})]\approx-\iota(\chi)
.

Converse.

If
[(S_{\varepsilon})]
is moderate and associated to
-\iota(\chi)
, then

S_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup-\chi
weakly in
L^{2}
.
Each
S_{\varepsilon}
lies in the strong closure
\overline{W}^{L^{2}}

of the subspace
W
from Proposition
6.1
(indeed, for fixed

\varepsilon
the truncated sums

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu(k)e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}(\varphi_{1/k}*\rho_{\varepsilon})

belong to
W
and converge to
S_{\varepsilon}
uniformly, hence in

L^{2}
, because the tail is bounded by

\sum_{k>N}e^{-k\beta(\varepsilon)}\to 0
).
By Mazur’s theorem,

-\chi\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
, so

\chi\in\overline{\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_{1/k}\}}^{L^{2}}
and RH
follows.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

The polynomial damping with
\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}

cannot be used for the infinite series net, because the series

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\delta(\varepsilon)}
diverges, making the net
non
-
moderate. Truncation is essential in that case.

8.
Strong convergence analysis

This section studies whether the nets from Sections
4
and
5

can converge to
-\chi
in the strong Colombeau sense, i.e. whether

F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is negligible. Negligibility means

\|F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}=O(\varepsilon^{m})

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
(the sup
-
norm estimates for derivatives are then
automatically controlled by moderateness).

From the proof of Theorem
3.1
we have the decomposition

F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}=T_{\varepsilon}+R_{\varepsilon},

where

T_{\varepsilon}=\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{n(\varepsilon)}\mu(k)\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)\varphi_{1/k}-f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}\Bigr)*\rho_{\varepsilon},\qquad R_{\varepsilon}=(f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\chi)*\rho_{\varepsilon}.

Conditions
3.2
and
3.3
give

\|T_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
and
\|f_{\delta(\varepsilon)}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
;
the latter is the damping error before mollification. The central difficulty
with strong convergence is that mollification is only contractive, so

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
does not provide a lower bound for

\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}
.

For the exponential family, no quantitative lower bound for the damping
error is known, and the truncation error is already super
-
polynomially
small. For the polynomial family, the upper bound

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}\,\delta^{1/2}

is proved in Appendix
B
(using work of Burnol

[
11
]
and Báez–Duarte
[
3
]
). This shows that the
damping error before mollification decays at most like

(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-\alpha/2}
. However, to decide whether the
mollified error
R_{\varepsilon}
is negligible one would need a
complementary lower bound on
\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}
, and such a
bound does not seem to be available in the literature. Consequently, the
question of strong Colombeau convergence remains open for both damping
families.

We note that if a lower bound of the form

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\geq c\,\delta^{\beta}
for some

c,\beta>0
were available (or even any bound decaying slower than

\exp(-c(\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1-\alpha})
), then the argument used in
Section 5 would show that the polynomially damped net cannot be
negligible. Establishing such a bound remains an open problem.

9.
Main theorem and concluding remarks

Assembling the pieces we obtain the central result of the paper.

Theorem 9.1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
F_{\varepsilon}
be either the exponential net of
Section
4
, the polynomial net of Section
5
, or
the infinite series net
S_{\varepsilon}
of
Section
7
.
Then RH holds if and only if the respective net is moderate in
\mathcal{G}(0,1)

and associated to
-\iota(\chi)
.

Proof.

For the truncated nets, if RH holds, the conditions of
Theorem
3.1
are satisfied, hence they are moderate and
associated to
-\iota(\chi)
.
For the infinite series net, the same follows from
Theorem
7.1
.
Conversely, Proposition
6.1
(or the corresponding argument for
the infinite series) shows that any such net being moderate and associated
forces RH to hold.
∎

The two explicit damping families illustrate the flexibility of the
Colombeau–Beurling criterion.
The exponential damping (Section
4
) makes the truncation tail
negligible in the strong Colombeau sense and also permits the use of the full
infinite series (Section
7
).
The polynomial damping (Section
5
) uses only polynomial growth
of the truncation index, avoiding super
-
exponential scales, at the cost of a
truncation error that decays only sub
-
polynomially.
Both approaches are sufficient for the equivalence with RH.

The general conditions formulated in Section
3
capture the
essence of the required damping.
Many other choices are possible; for instance, one could take

\phi_{k}(\varepsilon)=e^{-k^{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}}
with
0<\gamma<1
or
even Gaussian weights, with minor modifications to the analysis.

The discussion of strong convergence (Section
8
) shows that
while the exponential and polynomial nets are moderate and associated to

-\iota(\chi)
exactly when RH holds, their possible strong Colombeau
convergence remains an open problem.

Several directions for future research are suggested by this work:

•

Are there damping families for which the net converges

strongly
(that is,
F_{\varepsilon}+\chi*\rho_{\varepsilon}
is
negligible)?

•

Can the Colombeau algebra be used to formulate new equivalent
criteria that involve non
-
linear operations on the Beurling functions,
thereby escaping the Mazur argument and providing a genuinely different
approach to RH?

•

How do the algebraic properties of the nets inside
\mathcal{G}(0,1)
reflect
the finer properties of the zeta zeros?

The present framework is intended to stimulate further investigation along
these lines.

Appendix A
Exponential decay of the truncation error

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\beta(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}
and

n(\varepsilon)=\bigl\lfloor\exp\bigl(\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)\bigr\rfloor
.
Then for every integer
m\geq 0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{e^{-n(\varepsilon)\beta(\varepsilon)}}{\varepsilon^{m}}=0.

Proof.

Set
E(\varepsilon)=\exp\bigl(\exp(1/\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)
.
Since
\lfloor x\rfloor\geq x-1
for every real
x
,

n(\varepsilon)\geq E(\varepsilon)-1.

For all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,
E(\varepsilon)\geq 2
, and therefore

E(\varepsilon)-1\geq\frac{1}{2}E(\varepsilon)
. Hence there exists

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

(4)

n(\varepsilon)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\,E(\varepsilon).

Consider the ratio

Q_{m}(\varepsilon)=\frac{e^{-n(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}}}{\varepsilon^{m}}=\exp\!\Bigl(-n(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}-m\ln\varepsilon\Bigr).

From (
4
) we obtain

-n(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}\,E(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2},

and by monotonicity of the exponential,

(5)

Q_{m}(\varepsilon)\leq\exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,E(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}-m\ln\varepsilon\Bigr).

Introduce the change of variable
u=1/\varepsilon
; then
u\to+\infty
as

\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. In terms of
u
,

\varepsilon^{2}=u^{-2},\qquad\ln\varepsilon=-\ln u,\qquad E(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{2}=\frac{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}{u^{2}}.

The exponent in (
5
) becomes

(6)

-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}{u^{2}}+m\ln u=-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}{u^{2}}\biggl(1-\frac{2m\,u^{2}\ln u}{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}\biggr).

The denominator
\exp(e^{u^{2}})
grows as a double exponential, while the
numerator
u^{2}\ln u
grows only polynomially. Hence

\lim_{u\to+\infty}\frac{u^{2}\ln u}{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}=0.

Consequently, the bracketed factor in (
6
) tends to
1
, and

\lim_{u\to+\infty}\biggl[-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}{u^{2}}+m\ln u\biggr]=-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\to+\infty}\frac{\exp(e^{u^{2}})}{u^{2}}=-\infty.

Since the exponent in (
5
) diverges to
-\infty
, the
upper bound tends to
0
. Because
Q_{m}(\varepsilon)>0
, the squeeze
theorem yields

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}Q_{m}(\varepsilon)=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark A.2
.

The argument works for any real
m\geq 0
as well; the additional term

m\ln u
grows only logarithmically and remains negligible compared to
the double exponential
\exp(e^{u^{2}})
.

Appendix B
Sharp
L^{2}
bounds for the damped Möbius sum

Throughout this appendix we assume the Riemann hypothesis. Recall the
notation
f_{\delta}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu(k)\,k^{-\delta}\,\varphi_{1/k}(x)
and
f_{\delta,n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k)\,k^{-\delta}\,\varphi_{1/k}(x)
for
0<\delta\leq 1/2
. We shall prove the
two estimates used in the paper:

(1)

\|f_{\delta,n}-f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,n^{-\delta/3}

(truncation error, polynomial case);

(2)

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}\,\delta^{1/2}

(damping upper bound).

The constants
C,c_{2}>0
are absolute.

B.1.
Truncation error

The essential ingredient is an approximate functional equation for the
truncated Möbius sum due to Balazard and Saias.

Lemma B.1

(Balazard–Saias
[
10
, Lemme 2]
)
.

Let
0<d\leq 1/2
,
\varepsilon>0
and
n\geq 2
. Write
s=\sigma+it

with
\sigma,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Under RH, uniformly for

\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}+d
,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{s}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}+O_{\varepsilon}\!\Bigl(n^{-d/3}\,(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon}\Bigr).

If
\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2
, then
\operatorname{Re}(s+\delta)=1/2+\delta
. Applying
Lemma
B.1
with
d=\delta
directly to the sum

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(k)k^{-(s+\delta)}
gives

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{s+\delta}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(s+\delta)}+O_{\varepsilon}\!\bigl(n^{-\delta/3}(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon}\bigr),\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence the tail satisfies the same pointwise bound:

\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{s+\delta}}=O_{\varepsilon}\!\bigl(n^{-\delta/3}(1+|t|)^{\varepsilon}\bigr),\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}.

Now apply the Mellin–Plancherel isometry (see
[
3
, §2.2]
).
For
s=1/2+it
,

\|f_{\delta,n}-f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Bigl|\frac{\zeta(1/2+it)}{1/2+it}\Bigr|^{2}\Bigl|\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{1/2+it+\delta}}\Bigr|^{2}dt.

Insert the pointwise bound
(*)
with
\varepsilon=1/4
and use the
Lindelöf estimate
|\zeta(1/2+it)|=O(|t|^{1/4})
(a consequence of
RH). Then

\|f_{\delta,n}-f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{2\pi}\,n^{-2\delta/3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(1+|t|)^{1/2}}{1/4+t^{2}}\,dt.

The last integral converges, so we obtain

(7)

\|f_{\delta,n}-f_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C^{\prime}\,n^{-\delta/3}.

B.2.
Upper bound for the damping error

Lemma B.2

(Burnol
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, under RH,

\Bigl|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\delta)}\Bigr|=O_{\varepsilon}\bigl(|s|^{\inf(\varepsilon,\delta/2)}\bigr),\qquad\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2,

uniformly for
0\leq\delta\leq 1/2
.

From the Mellin–Plancherel isometry (see
[
3
, Theorem 2]
) we
have

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2}\Bigl|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\delta)}-1\Bigr|^{2}\frac{|ds|}{|s|^{2}}.

The estimate of this integral is carried out in detail in

[
3
, pp. 8–9]
(see also
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
).
The result is

\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2}\Bigl|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\delta)}-1\Bigr|^{2}\frac{|ds|}{|s|^{2}}=O(\delta),

hence

(8)

\|f_{\delta}+\chi\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{2}\,\delta^{1/2}.
