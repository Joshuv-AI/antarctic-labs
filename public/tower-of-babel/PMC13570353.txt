FABP4 regulates inflammation and lipid transport via PPARγ signaling pathway in goose primary hepatocytes
Authors: Xu Liu, Zihan Deng, Shuqing Qin, Huijing Liu, Can Yang, Yuying Deng, Qingyuan Ouyang, Changqing He, Xiangyong Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Unlike human nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, fatty liver in geese do not induce inflammation even under severe steatosis, making it a unique model for studying hepatic lipid accumulation. Fatty acid-binding protein 4 (FABP4) is known to regulate lipid metabolism and inflammation, but its role in goose fatty liver formation remains unclear. In this study, primary hepatocytes were successfully isolated from Xupu goose and used to investigate the function of FABP4. The results showed that FABP4 overexpression significantly suppressed the mRNA levels of TNF, IL1, IL6, and ApoB (P < 0.05), while FABP4 knockdown upregulated the expression of IL6, ApoB, CD36, and FABP5 (P < 0.05). Transcriptome sequencing analysis revealed that differentially expressed genes mainly enriched in immune response and cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathways. Furthermore, treatment with the PPARγ inhibitor partially reversed the suppression of ApoB, IL1, and IL6 induced by FABP4 overexpression, indicating that FABP4 functions downstream of PPARγ. In conclusion, FABP4 selectively suppresses inflammatory cytokines and ApoB expression via the PPARγ signaling pathway in goose primary hepatocytes. This study provides novel evidence in geese for a regulatory relationship between FABP4 and ApoB, offering new insights into the protective mechanisms underlying goose fatty liver formation.

Introduction

The bird liver serves as a key site of lipid metabolism, acting as a primary organ for de novo fatty acid synthesis (Emami et al., 2020). As the descendant of birds, geese feeding an energy-rich diets for a short period can cause a dramatic increase in liver weight (Ma et al., 2026; Fu et al., 2025). It is noteworthy that no obvious injury or pathological symptoms such as inflammation, endoplasmic reticulum stress or hepatic fibrosis found in goose fatty liver after overfeeding (Wei and Han, 2024a; Zhao et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025a). In contrast, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) in humans and rodents is often concomitant with inflammation (Saha et al., 2026; Oates et al., 2023). This unique physiological adaptation makes the goose an invaluable model for studying the molecular mechanisms that enable high-capacity hepatic lipid storage without metabolic injury.

As a major member of the fatty acid-binding protein (FABP) family, FABP4 is traditionally recognized as a cytosolic lipid chaperone predominantly expressed in adipocytes and macrophages, where it coordinates fatty acid uptake, storage, and lipolysis (Yu et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2025). Research showed that FABP4 was a key gene for adipogenesis in intramuscular and subcutaneous adipocytes of the poultry (Huo et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2026). Additionally, other FABP family members reported to play roles in lipid metabolism. Transcriptome analysis of yellow-feathered and white-feathered broilers revealed that FABP1 was the significantly differentially expressed gene (DEG) and significantly enriched in the lipid metabolism pathway (Li et al., 2026b). Jia et al. found that FABP6 overexpression promoted lipid metabolism reprogramming by enhancing intracellular bile acid transport to activate NR1H2/3 (Jia et al., 2026). Besides, research indicated that FABP5 reprograms lipid metabolism by activating peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) signaling pathway (Wang et al., 2025). PPARγ is a master transcriptional regulator of adipogenesis, de novo lipogenesis, and lipid droplet formation (Janani et al., 2015). FABP4 can deliver endogenous or exogenous fatty acids to nuclear PPARγ, thereby enhancing its transcriptional activity and promoting adipogenesis and lipid storage (Garin-Shkolnik et al., 2014). The role of FABP4 in mammalian adipose tissue and metabolic disease has been extensively documented. However, its specific function in avian liver, particularly in geese, and its involvement in regulating hepatic lipid accumulation via the PPARγ pathway remain unclear.

On the basis of these studies and our preliminary findings, this study further investigated the specific regulatory role of FABP4 in goose hepatic lipid metabolism and whether it participates in avian fatty liver formation through the PPARγ signaling pathway. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of physiological hepatic steatosis in poultry and offer a potential comparative model for understanding metabolic adaptation without inflammatory injury.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

All experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Hunan Agricultural University (permission number 202005208). All methods and procedures were performed in accordance with the approved guidelines and provided by the regional Animal Ethics Committee (Hunan, China).

Experimental animals and sample collection

A total of sixty 80-day-old healthy males Xupu geese were purchased from Hunan Hongyu Xupu geese Development Co., Ltd. (Huaihua, China), and the overfeeding experiment was conducted at the Hunan Fu’e Development Co., Ltd. (Chenzhou, China). Each goose was banded and fitted with a plastic wing-tag for identification. The geese first carried out a 5-day pre-overfeeding, followed by a 28 days of formal overfeeding. The composition of the feed has been listed in previous articles (Liu et al., 2021). The overfeeding protocol was showed in Fig. 1: day 6-day 8, 2-3 meals per day, 200-250 g per meal; day 9-day 11, 3-4 meals per day, 250-350 g per meal; day 12-day 20, 4-5 meals per day, 350-450 g per meal; day 21-day 33, 4-6 meals per day, 450-550 g per meal. An overfeeding machine used in our study, and operated by the same person each time. During the overfeeding period, all geese had ad libitum access to water. On the 28th day of overfeeding, sixty geese were killed by cervical dislocation after weighing. Subsequently, fresh liver was quickly removed and weighed. Liver samples from the same side were collected and frozen at −80°C for RNA extraction. The large liver Xupu geese (LL Xupu geese) and small liver Xupu geese (SL Xupu geese) were divided based on the extreme liver weight group with no significant difference in body weight (Liu et al., 2021).Fig. 1Experimental design of RNA-seq analysis. Preparation of goose primary hepatocytes

Goose primary hepatocytes isolation and culture were performed as previously methods (Li et al., 2025b). Briefly, liver tissues derived from 23-day-old Xupu goose embryos were digested with type IV collagenase. After the cell separation is completed, the number of cells were counted, diluted with complete culture medium at a density of 1 × 106 cells/mL, and placed in 12-well dishes (1 mL per well). The complete culture medium was composed of high-glucose Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium, 100 μg/mL streptomycin, 100 IU/mL penicillin, 2 mM glutamine, 10% fetal bovine serum, and 0.02 mL/L epidermal growth factor. Finally, the plates were incubated in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37°C and the medium was renewed after the first 6 h of incubation.

Plasmid construction, siRNA synthesis, and transfection

Our research group cloned the FABP4 gene of Xupu goose (Liu et al., 2019). The empty vector (CAd group) was the pcDNA3.1 vector containing the CMV promoter, and the FABP4 gene overexpression vector (Ad group) contained the insert of the goose FABP4 gene coding sequence (MK055329). In addition, the siRNA of FABP4 was designed and synthesized by Shanghai GenePharma Co. Ltd (Shanghai, China) (Table S1). Plasmids and siRNA were transfected with Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen). The cells were collected 32 h after transfection.

RNA sequencing and library construction

To identify genes affected by FABP4 overexpression, primary goose hepatocytes transfected with empty pcDNA3.1 (+) vector or pcDNA3.1 (+) containing the goose FABP4 CDS were used for RNA-Seq. In brief, the total RNA were extracted from cells using the Illumina TrueSeq RNA Sample Preparation Kit (Illumina Inc., San Diego, CA). Paired-end RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) libraries were constructed by the Illumina HiSeq system. After quality inspection of the library, RNA samples were sequenced on the IIlumina HiSeq 6000 platform. All RNA samples were processed and sequenced by Novogene Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China). The transcriptomic data have been deposited in NCBI under accession number PRJNA1453455.

Identification of DEGs and functional analysis

To improve data quality, raw reads were initially filtered and cleaned using fastp. The processed reads were then aligned to the reference genome with Bowtie2 and Tophat2. Transcripts were assembled and merged using StringTie (v1.3.6). Gene expression levels were quantified as FPKM, and differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using thresholds of P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 1. GO and KEGG enrichment analysis were performed using clusterProfiler software (version 3.8.1), with a corrected P-value threshold of < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Real-time quantitative PCR analysis

RNA samples extracted using the previous method were selected for sample selection. Primer design for genes was completed using Primer BLAST (v.2.5.0), and synthesized by Shanghai Biotech Co., Ltd. (Table S2). Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (GAPDH) and β-actin gene were used as the dual internal standard for normalizing transcript abundance of mRNA expression. Each PCR reaction mixture contained 2 µL of diluted cDNA template (1:9), 12.5 µL of SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ II, 1 µL each of forward and reverse primers (10 µmol/L), and 8.5 µL of sterilized distilled water. The thermal cycling conditions were set as follows: initial denaturation at 95°C for 30 seconds, followed by 35 cycles of 95°C for 5 seconds and 60°C for 30 seconds. At the end of the amplification, dissociation curve analysis was performed by gradually raising the temperature from 55°C to 95°C. The relative expression levels of target genes were calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method as described by Livak and Schmittgen (2001).

Western blot analysis

Liver tissue was mixed with protein lysis buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China, cat. no. P0013B), vortexed, and centrifuged at 4°C and 10,000 × g for 30 min. The supernatant was collected, and protein concentration was determined using the BCA (Nanjing Jiancheng Biotechnology Research Institute Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China, cat. no. A045-3-1) kit. Protein standards and BCA working solution were added to a 96-well plate, incubated at 37°C for 30 min, and the OD at 562 nm was measured to calculate concentrations. A 10% separating gel and 5% stacking gel were prepared. Marker (2 μL) and denatured protein samples (10 μL) were loaded, and electrophoresis was performed at a constant voltage of 75 V for 130 min. The target proteins FABP4 (15 kDa) and β-actin (42 kDa) were excised, and transfer was carried out using a filter-paper–membrane–gel–filter-paper sandwich at a constant current of 300 mA (30 min for FABP4, 60 min for β-actin). The membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk in PBST for 60 min at room temperature, then overnight at 4°C, followed by another 30 min at room temperature the next day. The membrane was incubated with primary antibody for 90 min, washed three times with PBST (10 min each), then incubated with secondary antibody (Proteintech, cat. no. 12802-1-AP) for 90 min and washed three times with PBST (10 min each). Finally, the membrane was incubated with ECL Luminous Liquid (Nanjing Novozymes Bio-technology Co., Ltd.) for 1 min, dried, sealed in plastic wrap, exposed to X-ray film in a dark cassette for 15 min.

Statistical analysis

The results were expressed as mean ± SD of at least three independent biological replicates. Statistical analyses of the data were conducted in SPSS 20.0. Significant differences in hepatocytes gene expression were determined by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s test and Duncan’s test. Statistical significance was set at a P < 0.05.

Discussion

FABP4 (also known as aP2 or A-FABP), a member of the FABP family, was traditionally recognized for its role in linking lipid metabolism with innate immunity and inflammation (He et al., 2025). The results of this study showed that after overexpression of the FABP4 gene, the expression levels of genes related to fat synthesis, including SREBP-1c, FAS, ACC, DGAT1, and DGAT2, as well as genes related to fat oxidation, including CPT1, ACOX1, Adipor1, and Adipor2, did not change significantly. Similarly, after silencing the FABP4 gene, these genes also showed no significant changes, which was consistent with the transcriptome sequencing results. Therefore, the regulation of fat metabolism by FABP4 may not directly promote fat synthesis and oxidation. Apolipoprotein is the main component of plasma lipoprotein, which performs the function of lipid transport (Lin et al., 2026). ApoA1 is the main apolipoprotein of high-density lipoprotein, which mainly transports extrahepatic fat to the liver and promotes the deposition of lipids in the liver (Xing et al., 2026). ApoB is produced in the liver and is the main apolipoprotein of low-density lipoprotein (Björnson et al., 2026). Contrary to the function of ApoA1 protein, ApoB mainly transports the fat in the liver to extrahepatic tissues for utilization. During the formation of human fatty liver, downregulation of ApoB levels can promote fat deposition in the liver (Jiang and Zhang, 2026). Studies in zebrafish found that increased degradation of ApoB in the endoplasmic reticulum leads to lipid droplet accumulation (Li et al., 2026a). In this study, the regulatory relationship between FABP4 and ApoB was discovered for the first time during the process of lipid metabolism in poultry. FABP5 is an epidermal fatty acid binding protein gene, and its protein product was first identified in association with psoriasis (Madsen et al., 1992). FABP5 and FABP4 are similar in sequence and structure, and found that there was a high correlation between them in adipocytes and macrophages (Adachi et al., 2012). FABP5 gene expression was upregulated in FABP4 knockout mice (Syamsunarno et al., 2013). The results showed that FABP4 gene overexpression or knockdown treatment had a significant effect on FABP5 gene. It can be seen that there is also a close correlation between FABP4 and FABP5 genes in the process of fat metabolism in Xupu geese. CD36 belongs to the scavenger receptor family of group B and is an antiprotease platelet membrane glycoprotein (Li et al., 2022). A comprehensive analysis combining lipidomics and transcriptomics identified CD36 as essential regulators influencing IMF accumulation and TG content in poultry (Zou et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Hu et al., 2024).

In the pathogenesis of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, the liver will trigger a series of inflammatory reactions in order to clear the liver cell damage caused by lipotoxicity (Sandireddy et al., 2024). TNF-α, mainly produced by activated Kupffer cells in the liver, is both an inflammatory cytokine and an adipokine, and serves as the most important adipose-derived factor mediating hepatocyte injury, with its expression in local adipose tissue being positively correlated with the degree of obesity (Chen et al., 2023; Poullis and Mendall, 2001). IL-6 is a multifunctional inflammatory cytokine with a wide range of biological activities. The transcriptome data of this study showed that the differential genes were mainly concentrated in the signal pathways related to immune response. The results of this study showed that FABP4 overexpression suppressed TNF, IL1, and IL6 expression, while knockdown of FABP4 elevated IL6 expression, indicated that FABP4 acts as a negative regulator of these genes in goose primary hepatocytes. This is contrary to the proinflammatory role of FABP4 described in mammalian studies, where FABP4 deficiency in macrophages reduces inflammatory cytokine production (Zou et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2024). However, cell-type and species-specific differences may account for this discrepancy. Overfeeding in mule ducks induced hepatic overexpression of FABP4, which was associated with a decrease in inflammatory response (Even et al., 2018). Research has shown that the formation mechanism of goose fatty liver is different from that of non-alcoholic fatty liver in humans. Goose liver does not appear the effect of “multiple blows” such as endoplasmic reticulum stress and mitochondrial function damage after “one blow”, indicating that there is a special protective mechanism in goose fatty liver to prevent the pathological development of fatty liver. After FABP4 gene overexpression, inflammatory factors decreased significantly, which may also be one of the special protective mechanisms in the formation of goose fatty liver. It was found that the “complement system” of goose liver (complement protein activation is a key step to mediate inflammatory response) was generally inhibited during the formation of goose fatty liver (Geng et al., 2016). C3 complement is significantly upregulated in human nonalcoholic fatty liver, while complement C3 gene was significantly inhibited in goose fatty liver, which may promote the formation of fat in goose fatty liver by regulating the expression of fatty acid synthase (FASN) and ethanolamine kinase 1 (ETNK1) genes (Xing et al., 2021). Consistent with our previous transcriptomic data, we observed significant downregulation of inflammation and immune-related genes, including DDX58, IFIH1, IRF1, and STAT1. This finding aligns with a previous report by Liu et al. (2021), who also found that genes such as DDX41, IFIH1, Ifng, IRF7, and STAT3 were significantly downregulated in the fatty liver of overfed geese compared with normal controls. In goose primary hepatocytes, down-regulation of SCD gene significantly increased IL-6 and IL-1β gene expression (Tang et al., 2021).

A notable finding of the present study is the selective regulatory pattern of FABP4 in goose hepatocytes: FABP4 significantly modulated genes involved in inflammation and lipid transport, but exerted no apparent effect on those involved in lipogenesis or fatty acid oxidation. Several potential mechanisms may account for this pathway-selective regulation in goose hepatocytes. First, the differential effects may reflect the versatility of PPARγ signaling. As a nuclear receptor, PPARγ regulates diverse gene networks through heterodimerization with RXR and recruitment of co-activators or co-repressors in a cell- and promoter-specific manner (Lefterova et al., 2008; Brust et al., 2018). The net effect of PPARγ activation on a given target gene depends not only on PPARγ, but also on the chromatin context and availability of transcription factors at the promoter (Shang et al., 2020). Therefore, while FABP4 activates PPARγ, the resulting activation appears sufficient to drive transcription of a subset of target genes, but insufficient to affect others that depend on auxiliary signals or elevated PPARγ binding. Second, FABP4 functions as an intracellular fatty acid chaperone that facilitates ligand delivery to PPARγ (Sobczyński et al., 2026). This chaperone-mediated nuclear translocation may preferentially affect genes that are more directly coupled to intracellular fatty acid flux. In contrast, lipogenic genes are primarily governed by SREBP-1c (Chandrasekaran and Weiskirchen, 2024; Zhang et al., 2026), and oxidative genes are largely regulated by PGC-1α and AMPK (Huang et al., 2026; Fakhri et al., 2024). These parallel regulatory axes may compensate for or overshadow FABP4-mediated PPARγ activation in the context of fatty acid synthesis and oxidation. Third, the selective pattern may also reflect species-specific metabolic adaptation in geese. Unlike mammals, geese have evolved a remarkable capacity for hepatic lipid storage without developing overt metabolic dysfunction (Koseniuk, 2025; Wei et al., 2024b). It is plausible that FABP4 in geese serves as a specialized fine-tuner that selectively modulates inflammatory responses and lipid transport to maintain hepatic homeostasis, rather than globally reprogramming core metabolic fluxes. This selective modulation may represent an adaptive mechanism that protects goose hepatocytes from lipotoxicity during overnutrition (Xue et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2024).

To experimentally test whether PPARγ mediates the observed regulatory effects, we treated goose primary hepatocytes with a PPARγ inhibitor following FABP4 overexpression. The results demonstrated that the PPARγ inhibitor partially attenuated the effects of FABP4 overexpression on ApoB, IL1, and IL6 expression, indicating that the regulation of lipid transport and inflammatory responses by FABP4 is partially dependent on the PPARγ signaling pathway. Notably, the partial rather than complete reversal by PPARγ inhibition is consistent with the mechanistic considerations above, suggesting that additional PPARγ-independent pathways or co-regulatory networks also contribute to the selective regulatory effects of FABP4 in goose hepatocytes. Collectively, these findings indicate that during lipid metabolism in geese, the regulation of lipid transport and inflammatory responses by FABP4 is partially dependent on the PPARγ signaling pathway. Future studies employing chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) and targeted gene knockout are warranted to fully elucidate the molecular basis of this selectivity.

Conclusion

This study successfully isolated primary hepatocytes from Xupu goose and demonstrated that FABP4 selectively regulates inflammatory and lipid transport genes without affecting lipogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, or apoptosis. Overexpression of FABP4 significantly suppressed TNF, IL1, IL6, and ApoB expression, while FABP4 knockdown upregulated IL6, ApoB, CD36, and FABP5. RNA-seq revealed that differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in immune-related pathways. Furthermore, PPARγ inhibition partially reversed the effects of FABP4 overexpression on ApoB, IL1, and IL6, indicating that FABP4 acts downstream of PPARγ. These findings reveal a novel regulatory relationship between FABP4 and ApoB in avian lipid metabolism and suggest a species-specific protective mechanism that allows geese to accumulate hepatic fat without triggering pathological inflammation.
