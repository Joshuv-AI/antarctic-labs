Poster 192. Diminished Batted Ball Control Following Ulnar Collateral Ligament Repair or Reconstruction with Internal Bracing in Professional Pitchers: A Statcast-Based Evaluation
Authors: Ashley E Rosenberg, Rhea D Rasquinha, Haneef Khan, Aditya D Patel, Cameron Thomson, Matthew S Quinn, Stephen Marcaccio
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:To evaluate the effect of ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) repair or reconstruction with internal bracing on pitch effectiveness in Major League Baseball (MLB) pitchers using Statcast-derived batted ball metrics. Unlike traditional metrics such as ERA or WHIP, Statcast-derived measures offer a more precise and objective assessment of a pitcher's ability to limit damaging contact. This study aimed to determine whether Statcast data could detect subtle declines in post-operative performance that are not captured by conventional statistics.Methods:A retrospective matched case-control study was performed on MLB pitchers who underwent UCL repair or reconstruction with internal bracing between 2016 and 2024. Each case was matched with multiple controls based on birth year and overlapping playing seasons, eliminating age-related confounding. Inclusion required at least one full season of pitch-level Statcast data both before and after surgery and at least 20 innings pitched per season in overlapping years. Statcast metrics included batted ball quality metrics (exit velocity against, barrel rate against, hard-hit percentage against, expected batting average (xBA), expected slugging percentage (xSLG), and expected weighted on-base average (xwOBA)) and plate discipline metrics (zone swing-and-miss percentage, out-of-zone swing percentage, and first-pitch strike percentage). Pre- to post-operative changes were assessed using independent t-tests, with pre-to-post changes compared to changes over the same time period for age-matched controls.Results:Among 23 pitchers matched with 1,569 age-matched controls, post-surgery analysis revealed significant declines in performance, including increased exit velocity against (+2.3 mph, p<0.001), barrel rate against (+4.6%, p<0.001), and hard-hit percentage against (+6.7%, p=0.001), and decreased fastball velocity (-2.1 mph, p=0.005). Pitchers also experienced reduced workloads with fewer innings pitched (-31.8, p=0.020) and fewer strikeouts (-38.1, p=0.009), along with decreased out-of-zone swing rate (-4.3%, p<0.001) and zone swing-and-miss rate (-3.6%, p=0.005). Post-surgery, case pitchers demonstrated significantly more positive changes in barrel rate against (p<0.001), exit velocity against (p<0.001), and hard-hit percentage against (p<0.001), and a significantly more negative change in fastball velocity (p=0.006) compared to control pitchers.Conclusions:MLB pitchers who undergo UCL internal bracing may experience a measurable decline in pitch effectiveness, as indicated by increased quality of contact against them. These findings suggest that despite preserved mechanical metrics and high return-to-play rates, internal bracing may subtly impair a pitcher's ability to limit damaging contact. Statcast metrics offer a valuable adjunct to traditional performance evaluations in this elite population.
