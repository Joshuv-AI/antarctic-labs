Basic Canonical Brackets and Nilpotency Property of Noether (anti-)BRST Charges: Non-Abeian 1-Form Gauge Theory
R. P. Malik

Abstract




Abstract:

In the case of a D-dimensional non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory (without any interaction with the matter fields),
we show that the application of the Noether theorem does
not

lead to the derivations of the Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) and anti-BRST charges that obey (i) the (anti-)BRST invariance, and
(ii) the nilpotency property (despite the fact that these charges are derived from the
infinitesimal, continuous and nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations).
This happens because of the presence of the
non-trivial
Curci-Ferrari (CF) condition on our
non-Abelian theory (whose limiting case is the Abelian gauge theory where the CF-type restriction is
trivial
and the
corresponding Noether (anti-)BRST charges
turn out to be nilpotent as well as (anti-)BRST invariant
together
). We exploit the
theoretical strength of the
basic
canonical approach to prove (i) the non-nilpotency of the conserved Noether (anti-)BRST charges, and
(ii) the (anti-)BRST invariance of the consistently
modified
versions of the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges. We
very briefly comment on the
nilpotency property of the consistently

modified
versions of the conserved (anti-)BRST charges, too. Our present observations are very sacrosanct because they have been proven by (i) using
the beauty of the symmetry properties, and (ii) exploiting the theoretical strength of the
basic
canonical (anti)commutators.

basic canonical (anti)commutators

Introduction

The principle of local gauge invariance is at the heart of the
interacting
(non-)Abelian 1-form gauge theories that provide a precise
theoretical description

One of the cardinal examples for such kinds of gauge theories is the well-known standard model of elementary particle physics
where the agreement between theory and experiment, to put mildly, is outlandish in nature. This theory, despite a few shortcomings,
provides the corner stone for the unified theoretical description of the electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions of nature.

of three (out of the
total
four) fundamental interactions of nature. One of the mathematically elegant and
physically intuitive methods
to covariantly quantize such kinds of gauge theories is the Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) formalism [1-4] where the unitarity and quantum gauge
(i.e. BRST) invariance are respected

for a given physical process (that is allowed by a given

interacting
gauge theory [5].

The
classical
interacting gauge theories always respect the infinitesimal, continuous and
local
gauge symmetry transformations
that are generated by the first-class constraints (in the terminology of
Dirac’s prescription for the classification scheme) which exist on
these

theories. Within the framework of the BRST formalism, the
local
gauge symmetry transformations
are traded with the infinitesimal continuous and nilpotent BRST and anti-BRST symmetry transformations. These nilpotent symmetry transformation operators
are required to absolutely anticommute with each-other so that they can maintain their independent identity. The nilpotency and absolute
anticommutativiity properties are a couple of sacrosanct requirements of the quantum gauge (i.e. BRST and anti-BRST) symmetry transformation operators.
In particular, the requirement of the absolute anticommutativity property of the (anti-)BRST transformation operators
distinguishes

from the
\mathcal{N}=2
SUSY transformation operators which are
also
nilpotent of order two. However, these set of
(a couple of
\mathcal{N}=2

not
anticommute with each-other. This
specific

one
of the sacrosanct properties of the
\mathcal{N}=2

SUSY transformation operators.

The key motivating factors behind our present investigation are as follows. In our recent earlier work [6], we have established that
the
Noether
conserved (anti-)BRST charges are
always

non-trivial

these

not

However, these non-nilpotent (anti-)BRST charges are
useful
in the sense that they are
the generators for the infinitesimal, continuous and nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transportations from which they are derived. It is
crystal clear that such kinds

(anti-)BRST charges can
not
be used in the discussion on the BRST cohomology (see, e.g. [7]). Hence, one has to
use the appropriate Euler-Lagrange (EL) equations of motion (EoM) to recast these non-nilpotent charges in the forms that are found to be
(anti-)BRST invariant. In our earlier works [8-10], we have been able to establish that the
(anti-)BRST
versions of the (anti-)BRST charges are useful
in the physicality criteria because they lead to the derivation of the conditions on the physical states that are found to be
consistent with the Dirac quantization conditions for the gauge theories that are endowed with the first-class constraints
in the terminology of Dirac’s prescription for the classification scheme (see, e.g. [11,12]). In other words, we find that the physicality criteria w.r.t. the (anti-)BRST invariant versions of the (anti-)BRST charges lead to
the annihilation of the physical states by the operator forms of the first-class constraints (of the original
classical

gauge theories that are properly BRST-quantized).

Against the backdrop of the above paragraph, it is pertinent to point out that,
in our earlier work [6], we have exploited the standard relationship between the continuous symmetry transformations and their generators
as the Noether conserved charges to prove (i) the non-nilpotency of the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges (that are derived
using the off-shell nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations), and (ii) the (anti-)BRST invariance of the appropriately

modified
versions of the Noether (anti-)BRST charges where the suitable EL-EoM are used. The
latter
have been used in
our earlier works (see, e.g. [8-10] for details) where we have discussed the physicality criteria w.r.t.
them
in great detail(s).
The central purpose of our
present investigation is to exploit the theoretical strengths of the canonical (anti)commutators to prove (i) the non-nilpotency of the
Noether (anti-)BRST charges, and (ii) the (anti-)BRST invariance of the consistently
modified
versions of the Noether
(anti-)BRST charges (in the context of the D-dimensional non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory). Our present canonical method of
these

different

The

method has a questionable issue
as far as the nilpotency property is concerned. Such issues are
not
present in the canonical approach (cf. Sec. 6 for details).

(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})\times\bar{C}=-\,\bar{C}\times\bar{C}\times\bar{C})=0
. Using exactly the same type theoretical tricks that
we adopted for the proof of the BRST invariance (i.e.

)
of the conserved BRST charge

, we note that the

\displaystyle-\,\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int\,d^{D-1}x\;\big[\dot{\bar{B}}\cdot(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})\big]\,(\vec{x},t).

(55)

On the other hand, the

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int\,d^{D-1}x\;\Big[(D_{i}F^{i0})\cdot\,(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})\Big](\vec{x},t)\equiv\dfrac{1}{2}\,\int\,d^{D-1}x\;\big[\dot{\bar{B}}\cdot(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})\big]\,(\vec{x},t),

(56)

where the covariant derivative:
D_{i}F^{i0}=\partial_{i}F^{i0}+i\,(A_{i}\times F^{i0})
is in the adjoint representation (in the

D_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}+\partial^{\nu}\bar{B}+({\bar{C}}\times\partial^{\nu}C)=0

which is derived from the

anti-BRST-invariant

{\cal L}_{(\bar{B})}
w.r.t. the gauge field
A_{\mu}
and chosen
\nu=0
component of
it to prove that
{\bar{B}}\cdot(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})=(D_{i}F^{i0})\cdot\,(\bar{C}\times\bar{C})
. Finally, we note that the sum of equations (55) and (56) leads to the proof of
the anti-BRST invariance (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=-\,i\,\{Q_{AB},\;Q_{ab}\}=0
) of
the
modified
version of the conserved anti-BRST charge
Q_{AB}
where we have used

only
the
theoretical strength of the

that have been expressed in equations (9) and (10)]).

5.3
Physicality Criteria:
Q_{(a)b}
Versus
Q_{(A)B}

A close and careful look at the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges [cf. Eq. (5)] shows that every individual term of the Noether
BRST charge
Q_{b}
carries the effective ghost number equal to
+\,1
. Furthermore, all the terms in the expression for
Q_{b}
are made-up
of (i) the physical fields (carrying the ghost number equal to zero) and a ghost field with the ghost number
+\,1
, and/or (ii) only
the combinations of the (anti-)ghost fields [e.g. (1/2)
\dot{\bar{C}}\cdot(C\times C)
] with effective ghost number
equal to
+\,1
. In the discussion on the physicality criteria
w.r.t. the (anti-)BRST charges, first of all, we invoke the ideas
from the BRST-quantized version of the
Abelian
1-form gauge theory where, right from the beginning, the ghost fields are decoupled from the
physical (i.e. gauge) fields (see, e.g. [16] for details). Hence, the ghost fields (with specific non-zero ghost numbers)
act on the ghost states (i.e.
|ghost>
) and the physical fields (with ghost number equal to zero) act on the physical
states (i.e.
|phys>
). In the context of the discussion on the BRST-quantized version of the D-dimensional
non-Abelian
1-form gauge
theory, we invoke the
same
ideas to discuss the physicality criteria (i.e.
Q_{(a)b}\,|phys>=0
and
Q_{(A)B}\,|phys>=0
)
w.r.t. (i) the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
, and (ii) the
modified
versions of the
conserved (anti-) BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
(that have been derived from
Q_{(a)b}
in a consistent manner).

Let us, first of all, focus on the physicality criterion w.r.t. the Noether conserved BRST charge
Q_{b}
[cf. Eq. (5)] and demand
that the physical states (i.e.
|phys>
), existing in the total quantum Hilbert space of states, are

those
that are annihilated by the conserved BRST charge
Q_{b}
. In this context, it is essential to point out that we shall demand that
all
the individual terms of
Q_{b}
will act, separately and independently, on the physical states (i.e.
|phys>
) and the ghost states (i.e.
|ghost>
).
We note that the
first
term
(i.e.
B\cdot D_{0}C=B^{a}(D_{0}C)^{a}\equiv i\,\Pi^{0}_{(A)}\cdot\Pi_{(\bar{C})}
)
consists of two parts. This
first
part
B^{a}
happens to be the
physical
field which
carries the ghost number equal to zero and the
second
one
(D_{0}C)^{a}
carries the
ghost number equal to
+\,1
. Hence, the
latter
will act on the ghost states and will produce a non-zero result (with eigen value
+\,1
).
As a consequence, to satisfy the subsidiary (i.e. physicality) criterion w.r.t. the Noether BRST charge (i.e.
Q_{b}\,|phys>=0
), we have to impose the
following restriction on the physical states, namely;

\displaystyle B^{a}\,|phys>=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\Pi^{0a}_{(A)}\,|phys>=0,

(57)

where, as is obvious, we have used:
\Pi^{0a}_{(A)}=B^{a}
[cf. Eq. (6)].
In other words, the operator form of the primary constraint [cf. Appendix A]
annihilates the physical state (i.e.
|phys>
). This observation si consistent
with the Dirac quantization conditions on the gauge systems that are endowed with the first-class constraints
in the terminology of Dirac’s prescription for the classification scheme of constraints (see, e.g. [11,12]). Now let us focus on the

second
term of the expression for
Q_{b}
[cf. Eq. (5)] which is equal to:
-\,F^{0i}\cdot D_{i}C=\Pi^{ia}_{(A)}\,(D_{i}C)^{a}
. From this
term, it is clear that
(D_{i}C)^{a}
carries the ghost number equal to
+\,1
. Hence, it will act on the ghost states and produce a
non-zero
result.
On the other hand, the term
-\,F^{0ia}=\Pi^{ia}_{(A)}
[cf. Eq. (6)]
carries the ghost number equal to
zero
. Thus, it is a
physical
field and, therefore, it will act on the physical states as follows:

\displaystyle-\,F^{0ia}\,|phys>=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\Pi^{ia}_{(A)}\,|phys>=0.

(58)

However, this result is
absurd
in the sense that
\Pi^{ia}_{(A)}
is
not
a constraint on our theory and it has nothing to do with
the
secondary
constraint on our theory that has been discussed in our Appendix A. This wrong result is obtained
because of the fact that the Noether BRST charge
Q_{b}
is

not
a physical quantity (within the framework of BRST formalism) as it is
not
a BRST-invariant quantity [cf. Eq. (12)]. Before we end
our discussions on the physicality criteria w.r.t. the conserved Noether (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
, we would like to make
a couple of important remarks. First, we note that the
third
term [i.e. (1/2)
\dot{\bar{C}}\cdot(C\times C)
],
in the expression for the Noether BRST charge
Q_{b}
[cf. Eq. (5)],
carries an effective ghost number
+\,1
. Hence, it acts (i) only on the ghost states, and
(ii) produces a non-zero result. Thus, it will not participate in the physicality criterion:
Q_{b}\,|phys>=0
w.r.t. the conserved Noether charge
Q_{b}
.
Second, we would like to point out that all our discussions, connected with the physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{b}\,|phys>=0
)
w.r.t. the BRST charge
Q_{b}
, will also be
true
for the physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{ab}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the conserved
Noether anti-BRST charge
Q_{ab}
because we shall end-up with the results:
\Pi^{0a}_{(A)}\,|phys>=0
and
\Pi^{ia}_{(A)}\,|phys>=0

which do
not
carry any information about the
secondary
constraint [that
exists on the
classical
version of our theory (cf. Appendix A for details)].

We are now in the position to discuss the physicality criteria (i.e.
Q_{(A)B}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the (anti-)BRST invariant
(i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
. First of all, we focus on the
physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{B}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the BRST-invariant (i.e.
s_{b}Q_{B}=0
)
modified
version of the BRST charge
Q_{B}

(which has been
consistently
derived from the Noether BRST charge
Q_{b}
).
This first term (i.e.
B^{a}(D_{0}C)^{a}
), as discussed earlier in the context of the physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{b}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the
Noether conserved BRST charge
Q_{b}
, leads to the condition on the physical states where the primary constraint (i.e.
\Pi^{0a}_{(A)}=B^{a}
)
annihilates (i.e.
\Pi^{0a}_{(A)}\,|phys>=0
)
the physical states [cf. Eq. (57)]. On the other hand, the
second
term (i.e.
\dot{B}\cdot C
) consists of the physical field
\dot{B}^{a}

(with the ghost number equal to zero) and the ghost field
C^{a}
(with the ghost number equal to
+\,1
). Hence, the ghost field will act on the
ghost states (i.e.
|ghost>
) producing the non-zero result. Thus, to satisfy the physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{B}\,|phys>=0
), we
demand that
\dot{B}^{a}\,|phys>=0
. At this juncture, we invoke the validity of the
EL-EoM:
D_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}-\partial^{\nu}B-(\partial^{\nu}{\bar{C}}\times C)=0
(which is derived from the
perfectly
BRST-invariant
Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{(B)}
w.r.t. the gauge field
A_{\mu}
). If we take into account (i) the component
\nu=0
, we end-up
with
\dot{B}^{a}=-[(D_{i}\Pi^{i}_{(A)})^{a}+(\dot{\bar{C}}\times C)^{a}]
. It is clear that the second term on the r.h.s., despite carrying
the ghost number equal to zero, is made-up of
purely
the
(anti-)ghost fields. Hence, it will act only on the ghost states and it will not act on the
physical
states
because of obvious reasons (which have been explained earlier). Hence, ultimately, we obtain the following condition on the physical states

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{a}\,|phys>=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;-\,(D_{i}\Pi^{i}_{(A)})^{a}\,|phys>=0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;(D_{i}F^{0i})^{a}\,|phys>=0,

(59)

where
(D_{i}\Pi^{i}_{(A)})^{a}\approx 0
is the secondary constraint (cf. Appendix A)
on the
classical
version of our D-dimensional non-Abelian
1-form gauge theory. Thus, from equation (59), it is clear that the operator form of the
secondary
constraint annihilates the physical
states (i.e.
|phys>
) at the
quantum
level. In other words, we have observed that the BRST-invariant (
s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) version of the
conserved BRST charge
Q_{B}
, used in the physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{B}\,|phys>=0
), leads to the annihilation of the physical states
by the operator forms of the primary as well as the secondary constraints of our D-dimensional
BRST-quantized version of the non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory. Exactly the same results are obtained when we discuss the
physicality criterion (i.e.
Q_{AB}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the conserved and anti-BRST invariant (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0
) version of
the anti-BRST charge
Q_{AB}
.

6
Conclusions

In our present investigation, we have laid a great deal of importance on the
basic
canonical (anti)commutators (associated with the BRST-quantized
version of a D-dimensional non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory) which have been utilized in the proof of (i) the non-nilpotency (i.e.
Q_{(a)b}^{2}\neq 0
)
of the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
(cf. Sec. 4 for details), and (ii) the
(anti-)BRST invariance (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) of the (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}

(cf. Subsec. 5.2 for details)
of the
consistently
modified versions (i.e.
Q_{(A)B}
) of the
Noether

conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
[cf. Eq. (5)]. Our present exercise corroborates the key observations of our earlier work [6] where
the ideas of (i) the infinitesimal, continuous and nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations
s_{(a)b}
, and (ii) their
generators as the conserved Noether
(anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
, have been taken into account. We provide the reason behind the
superiority
of our present canonical approach over our
earlier work [6] where the relationship (i.e.
s_{(a)b}Q_{(A)B}=-\,i\,\{Q_{(A)B},\;Q_{(A)B}\}=0
)
between the continuous symmetry transformations and their generators as the conserved charges,
has been taken into account to prove that the
modified
versions of the (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
are
nilpotent
of order two (i.e.

Q_{(A)B}^{2}=0
). However, using the very basic canonical (anti)commutators [cf. Eq. (8)], we have been able
to establish that:
\{Q_{(A)B},\;Q_{(A)B}\}\neq 0
.
In other words, we have
explicitly
proven that the conserved and (anti-)BRST invariant (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) (anti-)BRST
charges are
not
nilpotent (i.e.
Q_{(A)B}^{2}\neq 0
).

In our present endeavor, we have concentrated on the off-shell nilpotent (i.e.
s^{2}_{(a)b}=0
) versions of the (anti-)BRST symmetry
transformation operators
s_{(a)b}
and shown that the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
[cf. Eq. (5)] are (i)

not
the (anti-)BRST invariant quantities [cf. Eq. (12)], and (ii)
not

found to be off-shell nilpotent of order two [cf. Eq. (12)]. However, we
established the importance of the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges (cf. Sec. 3) as the generator [cf. Eq. (11)]
for the off-shell nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations (2). On the other hand, we have consistently derived the

modified
versions of the conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
[from the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
] which are found
to be (anti-)BRST invariant (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) quantities. Thus, the (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
are
physical
quantities
within the framework of BRST formalism. As far as the physicality criteria [5], within the purview of BRST approach to gauge theories are concerned, we have
established the
superiority
(cf. Subsec. 5.3 for details)
of the conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
over the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
where we have shown that the
physicality criteria (i.e.
Q_{(A)B}\,|phys>=0
) lead to the annihilations of the physical
states (i.e.
|phys>
) by the operator forms of the first-class constraints [of the
classical
theory (cf. Appendix A for details)] which are
found to be consistent with the
Dirac quantization conditions on the gauge systems (cf. [11,12]). These crucial
and clinching conditions are
not
obtained when we demand the
physicality criteria (i.e.
Q_{(a)b}\,|phys>=0
) w.r.t. the Noether conserved
(anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
(cf. Subsec. 5.3 for details).

As far as the nilpotent versions of the (anti-)BRST charges are concerned, we have shown that the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges charges
Q_{(a)b}

(i) are the (anti-) BRST invariant quantiles, and (ii) they obey the nilpotency property, provided we exploit the theoretical strength of
the appropriate EL-EoM
and the Gauss divergence theorem (cf. Sec. 4 for details). We have corroborated these statements by exploiting (i) the beauty of
the symmetry considerations, and (ii) the theoretical strength of the basic canonical (anti)commutators for our theory (cf. Sec. 4). However,
we have proven, in our Appendix B, that the (anti-)BRST invariant (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) and conserved versions of the
(anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
are
not
nilpotent of order two. This
latter
observation and its proof have
refuted
our
earlier claim [6] that the consistently
modified
versions of the (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
are nilpotent of order two. Thus, as far
as the discussion on the BRST cohomology (see, e.g. [7]) is concerned, we can take into account the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
.
However, we have to be careful to use the
on-shell
conditions in our entire discussion
on the cohomological aspects. Thus, ultimately, we conclude that

both
types of the conserved (anti-)BRST charges (i.e.
Q_{(a)b}
and
Q_{(A)B}
) have their (i) own identity, and
(ii) own importance, within the framework of BRST formalism.

In our earlier work [6], we have discussed the Noether theorem and nilpotency property of the (anti-)BRST charges,
in the context of (i) the 1D SUSY toy model
of a spinning relativistic particle, and (ii) the
massless
and Stückelberg-modified

massive
D-dimensional Abelian 2-form and 3-form gauge theories
(besides our present D-dimensional BRST-quantized non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory).
All these systems are endowed with the
non-trivial
CF-type restriction(s). As a consequence, we have found that the Noether conserved (anti-)BRST
charges are non-nilpotent as well as non-(anti-)BRST invariant quantities
under the off-shell nilpotent (anti-)BRST transformation operators. We have found the
expressions for the (anti-)BRST invariant (i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) versions of the (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
from the
non-nilpotent Noether (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
by using (i) the appropriate EL-EoM, and
(ii) the Gauss divergence theorem. It would be nice future endeavor to focus on
these

additional physical systems and apply our present canonical approach to
prove (i) the non-nilpotency of the Noether conserved (anti-BRST charges, and (ii) the (anti-)BRST invariance of the
consistently

modified versions
Q_{(A)B}
of
the conserved Noether (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(a)b}
.

Appendix A: On the First-Class Constraints of the Classical Theory

Our
classical
D-dimensional non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory is endowed with a set of
two
first-class constraints
in the terminology of Dirac’s prescription for the classification scheme of constraints [11,12,17-19]. We discuss these
constraints, very briefly, in our present Appendix because they appear
explicitly
in the physicality criteria w.r..t the
(anti-)BRST invariant versions of the conserved
(anti-)BRST charges of our theory (cf. Sec. 4). Toward this goal in mind,
we begin with the following Lagrangian density (
{\cal L}_{(0)}
) of our
classical
D-dimensional non-Abelian 1-form gauge theory (which has the
self-interaction due to its non-Abelian nature but has
no
interaction with the matter fields), namely;

{\cal L}_{(0)}=-\,\frac{1}{4}\,F^{\mu\nu}\cdot F_{\mu\nu}\equiv-\,\frac{1}{4}\,F^{\mu\nu a}F^{a}_{\mu\nu},

[formula]

where the field strength tensor
F_{\mu\nu}^{a}=\partial_{\mu}\,A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}\,A_{\mu}^{a}+i\,f^{abc}\,A_{\mu}^{b}\,A_{\nu}^{c}

(
\mu,\nu...=0,1,2...D-1
) is for the non-Abelian 1-form
A^{(1)}=A_{\mu}\,dx^{\mu}\equiv A_{\mu}^{a}\,T^{a}\,d\,x^{\mu}
gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}
.
The SU(N) generators
T^{a}
obey the algebra
[T^{a},\;T^{b}]=f^{abc}\,T^{c}
(with
a=1,2,3...N^{2}-1)
) where the
structure constants
f^{abc}
are totally antisymmetric in all the indices. The canonical conjugate momenta (
\Pi^{\mu a}_{(A)}
) w.r.t.
the gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}
are as follows

\Pi_{(A)}^{\mu a}=\dfrac{\partial\,{\cal L}_{(0)}}{\partial\,(\partial_{0}\,A^{a}_{\mu})}=-\,F^{0\mu a}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}=-\,F^{00a}\approx 0,\qquad\Pi_{(A)}^{ia}=-\,F^{0ia},

[formula]

where
\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}\approx 0
is the primary constraint (PC) on our theory. The symbol
\approx
stands for

weakly
zero in the Dirac notation
for the discussion on the topic of constraints (see, e.g. [11,12,17-19] for details). As a consequence, we are allowed to take a
first-order
time derivative on PC
to obtain the secondary constraint (SC) on our theory. In other words, we can demand the time-evolution invariance (i.e.
\partial_{0}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}\approx 0
)
of the PC to obtain the secondary constraint. For such derivation, the most suitable method is the Hamiltonian approach
(see, e.g. [11,12,17-19]). However, for our simple and
well-studied
system, we can utilize the theoretical strength of the EL-EoM
w.r.t. the gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}
and demand the
time-evolution invariance of the PC to obtain the SC on our theory (see, e.g. [7]). At this juncture, it is worthwhile to point out that these constraints
exist on our theory because the starting Lagrangian density (A.1) is
singular
(see, e.g. [12,17-19]).

Against the backdrop of the above discussion, we note that the EL-EoM that emerges out
from the Lagrangian density (A.1), w.r.t. the gauge field
A_{\mu}^{a}
, is as
follows:

(D_{\mu}\,F^{\mu\nu})^{a}=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu a}+i\,(A_{\mu}\times F^{\mu\nu})^{a}=0.

[formula]

Taking into account the choice:
\nu=0
, we obtain the following form of the above EL-EoM

(D_{0}\,F^{00})^{a}+(D_{i}\,F^{i0})^{a}=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\partial_{0}F^{00a}+i\,(A_{0}\times F^{00})^{a}+(D_{i}\,F^{i0})^{a}=0,

[formula]

where the term
i\,(A_{0}\times F^{00})^{a}
will be zero due to
F^{00}=0
which is implied by the antisymmetric
(i.e.
F^{\mu\nu}=-\,F^{\nu\mu}
) nature of
F^{\mu\nu}
. To corroborate this assertion, we would like to add that,
according to Dirac’s prescription, we are allowed to take
only
the first-order time derivative on the PC
otherwise
the
the explicit expression for PC (without a first-order time derivative) is
strongly
equal to zero.
Hence, the
second
term (present in the above equation) will be zero. In the language of the canonical conjugate
momenta [cf. Eq. (A.2)], the above equation (A.4) leads to the following mathematical relationship:

\partial_{0}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}=(D_{i}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{i})^{a}\equiv\partial_{i}\Pi_{(A)}^{ia}+i\,(A_{i}\times\Pi_{(A)}^{i})^{a}\approx 0.

[formula]

In other words, the requirement of the time-evolution invariance [i.e.
\partial_{0}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}\approx 0
] leads to the derivation of
the secondary constraint on our theory as:
(D_{i}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{i})^{a}\approx 0
.

For our present system (which has been studied through various
theoretical methods), it has been found that there are no further constraints on our theory. Since
both
the constraints [i.e.
\Pi_{(A)}^{0a}\approx 0

and
(D_{i}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{i})^{a}\approx 0
] are expressed in terms of the
components of the conjugate momenta [cf. Eq. (A.2)], they commute with each-other thereby rendering them to belong to the first-class constraints
in the terminology of Dirac’s prescription for the classification scheme of constraints (see, e.g. [11,12,17-19]). Before we end this
Appendix, as a side remark, we would like to state that the
Abelian
limit of the secondary constraint [i.e.
(D_{i}\,\Pi_{(A)}^{i})^{a}\approx 0
]
is nothing but the Gauss law of source-free Maxwell’s equations.

Appendix B: On the Nilpotency Property of

The key objective of our present short Appendix is to study the nipotency property of the (anti-)BRST invariant
(i.e.
s_{ab}Q_{AB}=0,\;s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) (anti-)BRST charges
Q_{(A)B}
(which have been consistently
derived from the conserved Noether (anti-)BRST charges

). Toward this goal in mind, first of all, we focus on the BRST-invariant
(i.e.
s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) conserved BRST charge
Q_{B}
and express it in terms of the canonical conjugate momenta [cf. Eq. (6)] (that have
been derived from the
perfectly
BRST-invariant Lagrangian density

(for the
modified

Q_{B}=\int d^{D-1}x\,\Big[i\,\Pi^{0}_{(A)}\cdot\Pi_{(\bar{C})}-\dot{B}\cdot C+\frac{i}{2}\,\Pi_{(C)}\cdot(C\times C)\Big],

[formula]

where, for the sake of simplicity, we have
not

We end this short (but quite important) Appendix with the following concluding remarks. First of all, in our earlier work [6], we have claimed that
the
modified
BRST charge
Q_{B}
is (i) a BRST-invariant (i.e.
s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) quantity, and (ii) nilpotent (i.e.
Q_{B}^{2}=0
) of order two.
The
latter
claim has been drawn from the relationship:
s_{b}Q_{B}=-\,i\,\{Q_{B},\;Q_{B}\}
. However, the r.h.s. of this
relationship is
not
correct in view of our assertion in equation (11) where the conserved Noether BRST charge
Q_{b}
has been
claimed to be the generator for the nilpotent BRST symmetry transformation operator
s_{b}
in (2). In our present endeavor, we have corroborated this
statement, as is evident from equation (B.4), that we obtain the non-nilpotent
(i.e.
2\,Q_{B}^{2}=\{Q_{B},\;Q_{B}\}\neq 0
) BRST charge
Q_{B}
if we use the
basic
canonical brackets [cf. Eq. (8)] of
our theory (that are derived from the
perfectly
BRST-invariant Lagrangian density
{\cal L}_{(B)}
[cf. Eq. (1)]). Second, the BRST charge
Q_{B}
is
conserved and BRST invariant ((i.e.
s_{b}Q_{B}=0
) quantity but it is
not
the generator for the infinitesimal, continuous and nilpotent
BRST symmetry transformations (2) as is evident from
difference
in the expressions for
Q_{B}
and
Q_{b}
. However, it is very
useful
in the

conditions for the gauge systems
