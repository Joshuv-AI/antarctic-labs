Cholera and Typhoid Epidemics Nay Threaten Some Areas in Iraq

According to press, cholera and typhoid epidemics, and other
diarrheal diseases threaten Baghdad because the public has been
forced to drink water from unsanitary sources. Destruction of
power stations and water pumping stations has caused an acute
shortage of clean water gince the since the start of the war; the
shortage probably is aggravated by the shutdown of sewage
treatment and other waste disposal systems. Iraqi public health
officials have stated that thousands of people have shown the
first symptoms of cholera and that deaths have been reportedly
linked to chlera and typhoid in varjous regions of Iraq, although
they could not provide a toll. (ee BB.

On 21 February, officials of the International Committee of
the Red Cross (ICRC) indicated they would send specialists and
emergency health materials to alleviate the risk of epidemics in
Iraq. According to press, the IcRC is setting up sanitary
operations in Baghdad, and has sent water purification equipment
there. Additionally, some 150 doctors from the Red Cross and Red
Crescent societies have begun arriving in Iraq to supervise
medical work.

On 23 February, World Health Organization officials who had
been to Baghdad said the lack of safe water and sanitation posed
an epidemic threat. The capacity of the Iraqi medical care
system to respond to an epidemic undoubtedly has peen degraded by
shortages of power, water, medical supplies and the task of
caring for war injuries. Ror.

EC Peace Plans

EC President Jacques Poos today welcomed the end of
hostilities in the Gulf and said the EC must help establish peace
and security in the region, according to press. A spokesman said
the EC will push ahead quickly with diplomatic initiatives. Ec
foreign ministers have agreed to discuss a peace plan for the
Middle East with leaders of the Maghreb countries and Syria.
