Nursing students’ perceptions of the usefulness of educational research: Qualitative thematic analysis
Authors: Alejandro Hernández Díaz, Verónica Díaz Quezada, Monica Illesca Pretty
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Integrating research into the nursing education process stimulates critical thinking and research skills, promoting a more solid understanding of the discipline.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This study explored nursing students’ perceptions of the usefulness of research in their training process. Fourth-year students were involved in two focus groups (six members each), conducted between November and December 2024. Data were analyzed manually using Braun and Clarke’s thematic technique. Methodological rigor criteria were applied, and the study was approved by the program’s ethics committee.RESULTS:Thematic analysis identified three main categories: (1) Useful research tools, (2) Development of research skills, and (3) Recommendations for strengthening educational research.CONCLUSIONS:The findings show that formative research is perceived by care management as a valuable resource for integrating theory and practice, as well as for developing key research skills in nursing students. The main skills acquired include critical thinking, scientific writing, and systematic searching, all of which are fundamental to evidence-based professional practice. However, areas for improvement are mentioned in relation to both methodological training for teachers and institutional commitment to academic publication. This suggests the need for policies and strategies that consolidate research as a cross-cutting theme in nursing education, as it enriches disciplinary knowledge and fosters a virtuous cycle of learning that enables innovation in the profession.

Introduction

Formative research, benefits, challenges, and the role of teachers

Formative research (FR) is understood as an educational process that allows students to learn through research,[1] implemented from the early stages of university education and using methodologies adapted to different stages of training.[12]

FR has established itself as a key element in fostering a culture of research in higher education institutions, as it allows for the development of cognitive and practical skills, stimulates critical thinking, strengthens research and writing habits, promotes teamwork and engagement with the environment, and motivates students to learn continuously. It also fosters problem-solving attitudes and values, positions students as active protagonists in their own learning process, and is integrated into specific academic programs through the support of teachers in research projects, who apply various pedagogical strategies and activities.[345]

However, the effectiveness of FR faces various challenges that can limit its impact on professional training. These include a lack of research identity among students and teachers, deficiencies in leadership, communication and organization, difficulties in task distribution, low motivation, limited community involvement and low disciplinary value. Financial and technological limitations, structural and pedagogical obstacles, insufficient adaptation of the curriculum and a lack of teaching skills are also identified.[678]

A lack of research experience and a disconnect from teaching practice can make the trainer an obstacle to the development of research skills, making it difficult for students to receive authentic and reliable guidance during their training.[7910]

Teachers play a fundamental role in overcoming these challenges, which highlights the need for them to have pedagogical and scientific skills that enable them to guide the teaching-learning process. Their work is not only to convey content, but also to guide, motivate and shape students’ research skills. To this end, they must promote active, reflective and constructive learning in which students participate actively, use a variety of teaching tools, provide comprehensive feedback and encourage autonomy, information seeking, and project management.[891112] In other words, teachers act as mediators and role models in the construction of disciplinary and scientific knowledge.

These skills not only strengthen the educational process but also contribute to the training of future professionals from a research perspective, aligning this work with the principles and purposes of university education.[91113]

Institutional and educational policies also play a fundamental role, as universities must strengthen undergraduate research through strategies that promote interdisciplinary integration, innovation, and inclusion.[51415]

In the field of nursing, FR contributes to improving the quality of care, promotes innovation in professional practice, and prepares future nurses to make informed decisions in the face of changing challenges in the healthcare field.

Integrating the description of FR with its challenges allows us to understand that, although formative research offers significant benefits for professional training, its impact depends on the institutional, teaching and student capacity to address existing obstacles, promoting a more robust, participatory and transformative educational process.

Institutional and political context

Higher education policies worldwide highlight the need to strengthen the teaching-learning process, with special emphasis on the production of research projects. It is also recognized that universities must adopt an integrative approach to their research processes, understanding research as a praxeological achievement within academic training and as a tool for the social management of knowledge. This integration also seeks to promote the training of future researchers in different areas of knowledge.[4141617]

It is the responsibility of higher education institutions to define clear research objectives aimed at fostering research skills based on students’ experiences and interests. To this end, it is necessary to create university spaces that allow for research in various subjects, promoting interdisciplinarity, an integrative and inclusive vision, and offering alternative responses to problems from the perspective of different social actors.[3414]

In the global field of nursing, FR has established itself as an essential strategy for strengthening both the quality of education and professional practice. This form of research plays a fundamental role in driving innovation and improving the quality of care by generating new scientific evidence and developing professional skills from the outset of training. Internationally, FR promotes a culture of inquiry, critical thinking and constant updating of knowledge, enabling future nurses to make informed decisions and adapt to the changing challenges of the health sciences field. Several countries recognize its value as a means of developing research skills that promote evidence-based decision-making, foster innovation in care, and strengthen the ability of professionals to respond effectively to scientific and technological advances in contemporary health systems.[34141819]

Although formative research in nursing is highly relevant, scientific output in this field remains scarce, and no previous studies have been identified that systematically demonstrate its impact. In this sense, the present research constitutes a pioneering contribution to the discipline. By recovering experiences and meanings linked to FR, it seeks to strengthen the culture of research in health sciences, stimulate critical reflection among students, and promote significant transformations in higher education institutions.

This study introduces an innovative perspective by rigorously documenting and analyzing the usefulness of formative research in the field of university education, an area in which empirical evidence remains limited. Its development is linked to national and international educational and health policies that promote the production of knowledge and its transfer to professional practice.

The overall objective of this research is to explore nursing students’ perceptions of the usefulness of formative research.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

A descriptive qualitative study[2021] was conducted to explore nursing students’ perceptions of the usefulness of formative research. This approach provides a detailed understanding of understudied phenomena and is particularly relevant in the field of nursing research.

The work was carried out during 2024 at a university in southern Chile. Focus groups were used as a data collection technique to obtain information, while thematic analysis allowed the testimonies to be organized and synthesized into key categories and themes. The methodological choice was based on the scarcity of previous studies documenting students’ perceptions on this topic.

Study participants and sampling

Participants were selected using non-probability, purposive, and convenience sampling.[2223] The sample consisted of 12 regular fourth-year nursing students from a university in southern Chile.

The inclusion criteria were established as follows[24]: having taken and passed the Qualitative Research course in the first instance, and voluntarily agreeing to participate in the study by signing the informed consent form. Students of both sexes were considered.

Data collection tool and technique

Two focus groups[25] were conducted, each consisting of six participants, at the university premises and lasting approximately 70 minutes. The sessions were scheduled outside of class hours to avoid interference with academic workload and were audio recorded with the consent of the participants.

To guide the discussion, a scripted guide[24] consisting of open-ended questions was used, which was reviewed by an external evaluator to ensure its relevance and minimize bias. This tool allowed the principal investigator to maintain consistency throughout the process, ensure that the defined topics were addressed, and encourage participation from all group members.

Data analysis

All recordings were transcribed verbatim. The coding and analysis of the focus group data followed the six stages of thematic analysis proposed by Braun and Clarke (2006): (1) familiarization with the data, (2) initial coding, (3) search for themes, (4) review of themes, (5) definition and naming of themes, and (6) report writing.[26] Each of these stages systematically contributes to the construction of meaningful knowledge, allowing the researcher to remain actively engaged with the data throughout the process.

During the transcription phase, the recordings were listened to several times to familiarize oneself with the language and communicative style of the participants. At the same time, descriptive notes were taken on the printed copies of the transcripts, which served as a guide for a better understanding of the information. The text was read progressively and intentionally, with the aim of delving deeper into its content.[2627]

The manual analysis of the transcripts was carried out by generating codes based on the data, supported by descriptive notes to identify relevant segments. Non-repeated codes were reviewed, merged with others of similar content, or discarded, unless they had particular analytical value.[27] Subsequently, the interrelationships, overlaps, and similarities between the codes were examined, allowing them to be grouped into 44 initial thematic sets. These were subjected to a critical review process to ensure their consistency and relevance to the study objectives. Through consensus among the researchers,[27] they were reduced to 26 themes organized into three general categories, which were adjusted according to the nature and characteristics of the final codes and themes.

Methodological Rigor

The scientific rigor of the research was ensured by applying the quality criteria proposed by Guba.[28] In this regard, the credibility of the study was ensured by the intentional selection of participants who were familiar with the subject of the study; all of them had experience related to formative research. In addition, a dense description of the context and research process was provided to promote the transferability of the findings. In terms of confirmability, the first author (AHD) actively participated in the organization and execution of the focus groups, as well as in the transcription, coding and analysis of the data. The other two researchers (VDQ and MIP) reviewed the findings to assess their meaning and relevance, thus strengthening the validity of the analytical process.

Ethical considerations

The study strictly complied with the ethical criteria established in the Declaration of Helsinki and the Ethical Guidelines for Educational Research of the British Association for Educational Research (2018).[2930] It was approved by the scientific ethics committee of the university’s Nursing programme (Ethical Study Act # 03/2024, October 2024). In addition, written informed consent was obtained from all participants, who received a detailed explanation of the study’s objectives and procedures before participating. The use of artificial intelligence (AI) was limited to the academic organization of ideas.

Discussion

The findings of this research show that FR in nursing students contributes significantly to both the development of research skills and the integration of theory and practice, establishing itself as a transformative pedagogical strategy in university education. In line with previous studies, participants highlighted that FR promotes the application of concepts learned in the classroom in real clinical settings, thus strengthening the connection between theory and professional practice.[1345101415161819] This result supports the relevance of FR as a tool that promotes active and contextualized learning, an essential condition for training professionals capable of facing the complexity of the healthcare field.[14678910141819]

In particular, the acquisition of research skills, such as critical thinking, scientific writing, and systematic information searching, is consistent with the literature, which recognizes FR as a driver of cognitive and metacognitive skills essential for evidence-based practice.[2679141819] Students reported improvements in their analytical skills and interpretation of scientific information, demonstrating that FR not only transmits technical knowledge but also enhances critical reasoning and intellectual autonomy, key aspects in the training of nurses capable of effectively evaluating and applying scientific evidence.[14578111419]

For its part, systematic information searching emerged as a particularly valued skill, as it not only improved academic performance but also provided tools applicable to the development of future professional projects.[782130] This learning is crucial in disciplines such as nursing, where decision-making must be based on evidence in clinical, educational, and management contexts.[141819]

Finally, the interviewees’ statements emphasize that FR integrates theoretical and practical usefulness with the development of research skills applied to care management projects, patient care, and the use of clinical instruments. These skills are fundamental for analyzing and addressing clinical situations, problem solving, and decision making, competencies that are essential pillars for the practice of nursing.[1819]

With regard to the role of teachers, the results show that their active participation, accompanied by methodological mastery and the use of relevant teaching strategies, is decisive for the success of FR. This finding coincides with previous studies that emphasize that the effectiveness of formative research depends largely on academic leadership and the ability of teachers to guide, motivate and model research skills.[689101214] Likewise, the students’ suggestion regarding the need for teachers to master technical and methodological language highlights the importance of continuous training in research and pedagogy, reinforcing the idea of the teacher as a mediator and reference point in the construction of disciplinary knowledge.[1113141819] In this sense, the findings highlight the urgent need to strengthen the pedagogical and scientific preparation of teaching staff, a commitment that must also be supported by higher education institutions.

Another relevant aspect identified is the need to consolidate a culture of academic publishing within the university. Although some teachers express interest in publishing, students perceive that there are no formal instances that systematically encourage the production and dissemination of knowledge. This result coincides with international studies that emphasize that institutional policies are fundamental to consolidating FR, as they promote interdisciplinarity, innovation and the transfer of knowledge to professional practice.[414151819] Implementing institutional support strategies not only increases scientific productivity but also reinforces students’ perception of the usefulness of FR, increasing their commitment and learning.[3101419] In line with the literature, participants consider it essential for universities to foster research skills and, at the same time, develop specific mechanisms for publication in journals or other academic spaces. This practice makes student work visible, disseminates research results and strengthens scientific culture, which is particularly relevant in careers such as nursing.

Regarding educational implications, results show that FR prepares future professionals to make informed decisions, promote innovation in healthcare, and link research with clinical practice.[5141819] Evidence also supports that integrating AI from the early stages of undergraduate studies favors the construction of a strong research identity, increases motivation, and develops transferable skills that last throughout the professional career.[12679141819]

Finally, this research provides original evidence on nursing students’ perceptions of FR,[141819] filling a significant gap in the national and international literature, where theoretical studies or experience reports predominate without a systematic analysis of student perceptions. The identification of strengths and challenges allows for the generation of concrete recommendations to optimize implementation, highlighting the importance of strengthening the teaching role, consolidating an institutional culture of academic publishing, and establishing policies that promote undergraduate research.

In conclusion, FR is confirmed as a key pedagogical strategy for professional development in Nursing, capable of integrating theory and practice, enhancing research competencies, and preparing students for reflective and evidence-based professional practice. Its consolidation requires not only trained faculty but also robust institutional policies that guarantee its sustainability and impact. The findings support the relevance of FR in higher education and offer a pioneering contribution that can guide future educational policies, pedagogical strategies, and lines of research in the field of Health Sciences.

Limitations

Among the main limitations of this study is that the available information focused primarily on the usefulness of research in general, without specifically delving into its educational dimension.

Furthermore, the research was conducted at a single university institution, which prevents extrapolation of the results to all nursing students. However, the findings constitute a relevant reference framework that can be used as a comparison in future studies, contributing to the cumulative construction of knowledge in the field.

Finally, by excluding students who did not agree to participate from the study, it is possible that those who did participate may have a greater motivation for learning and research. Consequently, the results should be interpreted with caution and cannot be generalized to the entire student population.

Relevance for teaching practice

The integration of research into initial nursing education not only strengthens students’ disciplinary competencies but also aligns with policies aimed at strengthening the capacities of healthcare human resources. This approach requires an institutional and curricular commitment that guarantees learning environments capable of stimulating knowledge production and its direct application to the needs of the population, with implications for care management.

Furthermore, the findings of this research offer tools for educators to plan and develop more effective pedagogical strategies, promoting the connection between theory and clinical practice. The incorporation of research activities in training settings fosters critical reflection, the development of generic competencies, such as problem-solving and analytical thinking, and the preparation of students to face real challenges in healthcare settings. In this way, educators can enhance the comprehensive training of future professionals, ensuring that nursing education contributes to the advancement of knowledge and the continuous improvement of the quality of care.

Conclusions

The incorporation of research into initial nursing education contributes to the strengthening of both disciplinary and generic competencies, promoting active and reflective learning that fosters the construction of knowledge applicable to clinical practice. This educational approach develops fundamental skills such as critical thinking, systematic information search and analysis, scientific writing, and evidence-based decision-making-competences that are essential for addressing complex challenges and proposing innovative solutions in professional contexts.

Although the study results are limited to a single institution and volunteer students, they constitute a valuable framework that can guide future research and inform the planning of nursing teaching strategies. Student motivation and active participation emerge as determining factors for the success of research-based training experiences, which implies the need to design educational environments that stimulate curiosity, participation, and the practical application of knowledge. Likewise, the implementation of institutional policies that promote research and the dissemination of student work strengthens the research culture, drives innovation in health services, and contributes to training professionals capable of addressing the challenges of the health system and advancing the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals related to health and quality education. This approach not only validates student efforts but also enriches the disciplinary heritage, generating a virtuous cycle of learning and knowledge transfer.

Finally, by highlighting the experiences and perceptions of the stakeholders involved, this study provides evidence of the effectiveness of research training in nursing and proposes courses of action for future curricular reforms, strengthening the research culture in higher education and its impact on the quality of care.
