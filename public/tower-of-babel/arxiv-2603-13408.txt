Lagrangian Identity and Mass Evolution of Particle-like Objects in Nonminimally Coupled Gravity
S. R. Pinto, P. P. Avelino

Abstract
We show that the Lagrangian of a Nambu-Goto p p -brane satisfies the identity ℒ [ p ] = T [ p ] / ( p + 1 ) \mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=T_{\rm[\it p\rm]}/(p+1) , with T [ p ] T_{\rm[\it p\rm]} denoting the trace of the corresponding energy-momentum tensor, independently of the properties of the gravitational field. While for p = 0 p=0 this reduces to the standard ℒ [ 0 ] = T [ 0 ] \mathcal{L}_{\rm[0]}=T_{\rm[0]} relation, which determines the on-shell Lagrangian of point particles and their fluids, more generally it depends explicitly on the p p -brane dimensionality. We explore the implications of this Lagrangian identity for the dynamics of non-self-intersecting cosmic string loops in a homogeneous and isotropic universe within f ⁡ ( R , ℒ m ) f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}) gravity, showing that, unlike in general relativity, the proper mass of a cosmic string loop may evolve over cosmological timescales regardless of its small size or tension. Finally, we extend the analysis to the more general case of closed p p -branes in ( N + 1 ) (N+1) -dimensional Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker spacetimes.

Introduction

In General Relativity (GR), the on-shell matter Lagrangian does not enter explicitly into the gravitational field equations. Therefore, distinct on-shell matter Lagrangians that correspond to the same energy-momentum tensor are physically equivalent within GR. This degeneracy reflects the fact that distinct on-shell matter Lagrangians contribute identically to the Einstein equations, since only their variation with respect to the metric matters. The situation changes in theories featuring a nonminimal coupling between matter and gravity

Consequently, different on-shell matter Lagrangians, even if they correspond to the same energy-momentum tensor in GR, generally lead to inequivalent dynamics. The degeneracy present in GR is therefore lifted, and the precise form of the on-shell matter Lagrangian acquires physical significance.

Perfect fluids provide a widely used and sufficiently general description of the sources of the gravitational field in many astrophysical and cosmological contexts. The Lagrangian formulation commonly employed to describe perfect fluids

However, this approach does not explicitly account for the microscopic particle dynamics underlying the fluid description. In fact, within GR, several equivalent Lagrangian formulations exist for a perfect fluid at the macroscopic level, leading to different on-shell Lagrangians
Brown 1993
;
Ferreira et al. 2020
. These distinct forms are physically indistinguishable in minimally coupled gravity, as long as they yield the same energy-momentum tensor. However, in the presence of a nonminimal coupling, this equivalence generally breaks down. Since the matter Lagrangian now enters explicitly into the field equations, both gravitational and matter dynamics may depend significantly on the specific on-shell matter Lagrangian.

This raises the fundamental question of whether a preferred, physically motivated, on-shell Lagrangian can be identified. Indeed, it has been shown that no universal on-shell Lagrangian exists even for a perfect fluid

Avelino and Sousa 2018

Pinto and Avelino 2025

This result relies crucially on microscopic considerations, in particular the assumptions that the constituent particles possess fixed proper mass and internal structure, and that their self-gravity can be neglected. However, in systems endowed with internal degrees of freedom, such as the oscillatory modes of a
1

II
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m})
Gravity: A Brief Review

Here, we present the equations governing the dynamics of the gravitational and matter fields in the framework of
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity
Harko and Lobo 2010
, and introduce the subclass of models of the form
f_{1}(R)+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}f_{2}(R)

Bertolami et al. 2007
.

In
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity the Einstein-Hilbert action is generalized by promoting the gravitational Lagrangian to a generic function of the Ricci scalar
R
and the matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}
. The action is defined as

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\ f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})\,,

(1)

where
g\equiv\det(g_{\alpha\beta})
and
R\equiv g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}
is the Ricci scalar constructed from the Ricci tensor
R_{\alpha\beta}
associated with the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
.

Varying the action with respect to the metric yields the gravitational field equations

2(R^{\alpha\beta}-\Delta^{\alpha\beta})f_{,R}-g^{\alpha\beta}(f-\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}f_{,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}})=f_{,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}}T^{\alpha\beta},

(2)

where
f_{,R}
and
f_{,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}}
represent the partial derivatives of
f
with respect to the Ricci scalar
R
and the matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}
, respectively,
\Delta^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}-g^{\alpha\beta}\Box
,
\Box\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}
, and
T^{\alpha\beta}
are the components of the energy-momentum tensor defined by

T^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\,.

(3)

Unlike in GR, where
\nabla_{\beta}{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}=0
follows directly from the contracted Bianchi identities,
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity generically features a nonminimal coupling between
R
and
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}
, which can break the local conservation of energy and momentum,

\nabla_{\beta}{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}-{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\nabla_{\beta}\left(\ln|f_{,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}}|\right)\,.

(4)

Here,
{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}
denotes the Kronecker delta.

Equation (
4
) shows that
\nabla_{\beta}{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\neq 0
except in very particular cases: for instance, if
f_{,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}}
is constant (corresponding to minimal coupling between matter and geometry), or if
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}-{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}=0
, as in the case of a cosmological constant. In general, however, the right-hand side is nonzero, as a consequence of the nonminimal coupling between the gravitational and matter fields.

For simplicity, in this work we shall restrict our analysis to
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
models that are linear in the matter Lagrangian,

f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})=f_{1}(R)+f_{2}(R)\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m},

(5)

where
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are arbitrary functions of the Ricci scalar
R
. In this case, Eq. (
4
) reduces to

\nabla_{\beta}{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}=\frac{\nabla_{\beta}f_{2}}{f_{2}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}{\delta_{\alpha}}^{\beta}-{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\right)\,,

(6)

with
f_{2}\equiv f_{2}(R)
depending only on the Ricci scalar. Equation (
6
) shows that, contrary to GR, the dynamics of the matter fields in this framework explicitly depend on the on-shell matter Lagrangian.

III
Lagrangian Identity for Nambu-Goto
p
-Branes

In this section we derive a general Lagrangian identity for Nambu–Goto
p
-branes, establishing a direct relation between the
p
-brane Lagrangian and the trace of the corresponding energy–momentum tensor.

Consider a
p
-brane described by the Nambu-Goto action

S_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=-\mu_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\int d^{p+1}\zeta\sqrt{-\det(h_{ab})}\,,

(7)

where
\mu_{\rm[\it p\rm]}
denotes the brane tension (proper mass per unit
p
-volume), and
\zeta^{a}
(
a=0,1,\ldots,p
) are coordinates on the
(p+1)
-dimensional world volume of the brane. The induced metric on the brane is given by

h_{ab}=g_{\alpha\beta}\,x^{\alpha}_{,a}x^{\beta}_{,b}\,,

(8)

where
x^{\alpha}(\zeta)
specifies the embedding of the brane in the
(N+1)
-dimensional background spacetime (with
N>p
), and a comma denotes partial differentiation with respect to the world-volume coordinate
\zeta^{a}
, i.e.,
x^{\alpha}_{,a}\equiv\partial x^{\alpha}/\partial\zeta^{a}
.

The
p
-brane action can equivalently be expressed as an integral over the
(N+1)
-dimensional background spacetime:

S_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=\int d^{N+1}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]},

(9)

where the matter Lagrangian takes the form

\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=-\frac{\mu_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}{\sqrt{-g}}\int d^{p+1}\zeta\,\sqrt{-h}\,\delta^{(N+1)}\!\left[x^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}(\zeta)\right],

(10)

with
h\equiv\det(h_{ab})
and
\delta^{(N+1)}
representing the Dirac delta distribution in
(N+1)
dimensions.

Using Eq. (
8
), the energy-momentum tensor of the
p
-brane is obtained by varying the action with respect to the spacetime metric,

\displaystyle T^{\alpha\beta}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]})}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}{\sqrt{-g}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int\sqrt{-h}h^{ab}x^{\alpha}_{,a}x^{\beta}_{,b}\delta^{(N+1)}\left[x^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}(\zeta)\right]d^{p+1}\zeta\,,

where
h^{ab}
denotes the inverse induced metric satisfying
h^{ab}h_{bc}={\delta^{a}}_{c}
.

From Eqs. (
8
), (
10
) and (
11
), it follows that the trace of the
p
-brane energy-momentum tensor (
T\equiv T^{\alpha\beta}g_{\alpha\beta}
) is equal to

\displaystyle T_{\rm[\it p\rm]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\int\sqrt{-h}h^{ab}h_{ab}\delta^{(N+1)}\left[x^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}(\zeta)\right]d^{p+1}\zeta

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+1)\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\,,

where we have used the identity
h^{ab}h_{ab}=p+1
. Therefore, the Lagrangian of a Nambu-Goto
p
-brane is equal to
\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=T_{\rm[\it p\rm]}/(p+1)
, independently of the particular properties of the gravitational field. While for
p=0
this reduces to the standard result
\mathcal{L}_{\rm[0]}=T_{\rm[0]}
, which defines the form of the on-shell Lagrangian of point particles and their associated fluids, more generally it depends on the miscroscopic properties of the matter source.

IV
Time-Averaged Lagrangian-Mass Relation for Cosmic String Loops

Here, we consider oscillating cosmic string loops, corresponding to closed 1-branes evolving in Minkowski spacetime (see
Vilenkin and Shellard 2000
a detailed overview of cosmic string dynamics). Restricting our analysis to periodic loop configurations that do not self-intersect, we shall derive a relation between the spatial volume integral of their time-averaged Lagrangian and their proper mass. For simplicity, here and in the next section we suppress the subscript [1] from all quantities, except those involving the cosmic string Lagrangian. In particular, throughout these two sections we use the notation
\mu\equiv\mu_{\rm[1]}
,
T^{\alpha\beta}\equiv T^{\alpha\beta}_{\rm[1]}
,
T\equiv T_{\rm[1]}
.

Consider the worldsheet of a Nambu-Goto string, described by the embedding

x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\zeta)\,,

(13)

where
\zeta^{a}
(
a=0,1
) are the worldsheet coordinates, with
\zeta^{0}
timelike and
\zeta^{1}
spacelike.
In Minkowski space, one can adopt the conformal gauge, in which the induced metric of the string (
h_{ab}\equiv\gamma_{ab}
) satisfies

\gamma_{ab}=\sqrt{-\gamma}\eta_{ab}\,,\qquad\gamma^{ab}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\gamma}}\eta^{ab}\,,

(14)

where
\eta_{ab}
is the (
1+1
)-dimensional Minkowski metric, and one may further choose
\zeta^{0}=x^{0}\equiv t
as the timelike worldsheet coordinate. In this gauge, in three spatial dimensions the string position at time
t
can be described by a three-dimensional vector field

\vec{x}(\xi)=[x^{1}(\xi),x^{2}(\xi),x^{3}(\xi)]\,,

(15)

which depends only on the spatial parameter
\xi\equiv\zeta^{1}
.

The relation between the induced and background metrics, given in Eq. (
14
), then implies the conformal gauge conditions on the string worldsheet

\dot{\vec{x}}\cdot\vec{x}\,^{\prime}=0\,,\qquad|{\dot{\vec{x}}}|^{2}+|{\vec{x}\,^{\prime}}|^{2}=1\,,

(16)

where the dot and prime denote derivatives with respect to
t
and
\xi
, respectively. The resulting equations of motion reduce to the standard wave equation on the string,

\ddot{\vec{x}}-\vec{x}\,^{\prime\prime}=0\,.

(17)

In the conformal gauge, the energy-momentum tensor of a Nambu-Goto string takes the form

T^{\alpha\beta}(t,\vec{x})=\mu\int\left(\dot{x}^{\alpha}\dot{x}^{\beta}-x^{\alpha\prime}x^{\beta\prime}\right)\delta^{(3)}\left[\vec{x}-\vec{x}(t,\xi)\right]\,d\xi\,.

(18)

A closed string loop (closed
1
-brane) of invariant length
L
satisfies

\vec{x}(t,\xi+L)=\vec{x}(t,\xi)\,.

(19)

Combined with the equations of motion, this implies that, in the center-of-mass frame, the motion is periodic in time with period

\Pi=\frac{L}{2}\,,

(20)

and the (proper) mass of the loop is given by

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int{T_{0}}^{0}\,d^{3}x

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\int_{0}^{L}\left(\int\delta^{(3)}\!\left[\vec{x}-\vec{x}(t,\xi)\right]\,d^{3}x\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\int_{0}^{L}d\xi=\mu L\,.

In this frame, for each point on the loop, the velocity averaged over one period vanishes,

\langle\dot{\vec{x}}\rangle=\frac{1}{\Pi}\int_{0}^{\Pi}\dot{\vec{x}}(t,\xi)\,dt=\vec{0}\,.

(22)

Consequently, in the center-of-mass frame, the time-averaged linear momentum of a non-self-intersecting cosmic string loop also vanishes,

\int\langle T^{0i}\rangle\,d^{3}x=\mu\int_{0}^{L}\langle\dot{x}^{i}\rangle\,d\xi=0\,.

(23)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int\langle T^{ij}\rangle\,d^{3}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Pi}\int_{0}^{\Pi}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}-x^{i\prime}x^{j\prime}\right)\,d\xi\,dt

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Pi}\int_{0}^{\Pi}\int_{0}^{L}x^{i}\left(\ddot{x}^{j}-x^{j\prime\prime}\right)\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

where we have integrated by parts with respect to both
t
and
\xi
(the boundary terms vanish due to the periodicity of the loop in both space and time). Equation (
24
) implies that, in Minkowski space, the spatial volume integral of the time-averaged proper pressure of the cosmic string loop vanishes,

\mathfrak{P}\equiv\frac{\int\langle{T_{i}}^{i}\rangle d^{3}x}{3}=0\,.

(25)

It then follows that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\langle T\rangle\,d^{3}x=\int\langle{T_{0}}^{0}\rangle\,d^{3}x+\int\langle{T_{i}}^{i}\rangle\,d^{3}x

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m+3\,\mathfrak{P}=-m\,.

Using the relation between the 1-brane Lagrangian and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor derived in the previous section [Eq. (
12
)], we further obtain

\mathfrak{L}\equiv\int\langle\mathcal{L}_{\rm[1]}\rangle\,d^{3}x=\frac{1}{2}\int\langle T\rangle\,d^{3}x\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{T}}{2}=-\frac{m}{2}\,.

(27)

V
Cosmological Evolution of the Mass of Small Cosmic String Loops

In this section, we show that the proper mass of a cosmic string loop in an FLRW background may evolve over cosmological timescales due to the nonminimal matter-geometry coupling. For simplicity, we focus on the subclass
f_{1}(R)+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}f_{2}(R)
of
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity and work in a regime where the string tension is sufficiently small that gravitational radiation emission by the cosmic string loop can be neglected. We further assume that the loop size is much smaller than the Hubble radius, so that the dynamics over a single oscillation are approximately unaffected relative to Minkowski spacetime.

Consider a flat homogeneous and isotropic universe described by the FLRW metric

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+d\vec{x}\cdot d\vec{x}\equiv-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)d\vec{q}\cdot d\vec{q}\,,

(28)

where
t
denotes cosmic time,
a(t)
is the scale factor, and
\vec{x}
and
\vec{q}
represent physical and comoving Cartesian coordinates, respectively. In an FLRW spacetime, the
\alpha=0
component of Eq. (
6
) becomes

\partial_{0}{T_{0}}^{0}+\partial_{i}{T_{0}}^{i}+3H{T_{0}}^{0}-H{T_{i}}^{i}=\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}(\mathcal{L}_{\rm[1]}-{T_{0}}^{0})\,,

(29)

where
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter, and a dot denotes a derivative with respect to
t
.

Consider an isolated, non-self-intersecting cosmic string loop whose center of mass is at rest with respect to the cosmological frame. Its proper mass is given by the spatial volume integral of the energy density,

m=-\int{T_{0}}^{0}d^{3}x=-a^{3}\int{T_{0}}^{0}d^{3}q\,.

(30)

Integrating Eq. (
29
) over a spatial volume containing the loop, noting that the energy-momentum tensor vanishes outside it, so that

\int\partial_{i}{T_{\alpha}}^{\beta}d^{3}x=0\,,

(31)

and using Eq. (
30
), we obtain

\dot{m}+H\int{T_{i}}^{i}d^{3}x=-\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\left[\int\mathcal{L}_{\rm[1]}d^{3}x+m\right]\,.

(32)

Because the loop size
L
is much smaller than the Hubble radius
H^{-1}
, and assuming it is also much smaller than
|\dot{f}_{2}/f_{2}|^{-1}
, the effect of cosmological expansion on the loop dynamics during a single oscillation is negligible. The motion of the loop is therefore quasi-periodic, with quasi-period

\Pi=\frac{L}{2}=\frac{m}{2\mu}\,.

(33)

This separation between the short oscillation timescale and the much longer cosmological timescale motivates describing the mass evolution in terms of quantities averaged over one quasi-period,

\dot{m}+H\int\langle{T^{i}}_{i}\rangle d^{3}x=-\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\left[\int\langle\mathcal{L}_{\rm[1]}\rangle d^{3}x+m\right]\,,

(34)

or, equivalently,

\dot{m}+3H\mathfrak{P}=-\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\left[\mathfrak{L}+m\right]\,.

(35)

Here,

\langle Q\rangle(t)\equiv\frac{1}{\Pi}\int_{t}^{t+\Pi}Q(\tau)\,d\tau

(36)

denotes the average of the physical quantity
Q
over a quasi-period
\Pi
. In deriving Eqs. (
34
) and (
35
) we neglected the tiny variations of
m
occurring on timescales shorter than
\Pi
(given that
\Pi\ll|\dot{f}_{2}/f_{2}|^{-1}
and
\Pi\ll H^{-1}
, both the scale factor and
f_{2}
, are essentially constant over a time interval
\Pi
). Neglecting the effect of cosmological expansion on the loop dynamics during a single oscillation, Eqs. (
25
) and (
27
) imply that
\mathfrak{P}=0
and
\mathfrak{L}=-m/2
. Substituting these into Eq. (
35
), we finally obtain

\frac{\dot{m}}{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\,.

(37)

Equation (
37
) implies that the proper mass of the loop evolves as

m\propto f_{2}^{-1/2}\,.

(38)

Since
f_{2}
depends on the Ricci scalar,

R=6(\dot{H}+2H^{2}),

(39)

the loop mass is therefore directly coupled to the cosmological evolution of the Universe. This result depends crucially on the relation
\mathfrak{L}_{\rm[1]}=-m/2
derived for cosmic string loops. In contrast, for point particles with
\mathcal{L}_{\rm[0]}=T_{\rm[0]}
one has
\mathfrak{L}_{\rm[0]}\equiv\int\langle\mathcal{L}_{\rm[0]}\rangle d^{3}x=-m_{\rm[0]}
, which implies
\dot{m}_{\rm[0]}=0
in the analogue of Eq. (
35
) for point particles and thus conservation of the particle proper mass
Avelino and Azevedo 2022b
. While in GR (
f_{2}=1
) the result is the same for point particles and small cosmic string loops (the proper mass is conserved in both cases, despite their different physical nature), in nonminimally coupled gravity the proper mass of a cosmic string loop can evolve over cosmological timescales, even when its size and the string tension are extremely small, whereas the proper mass of a point particle remains essentially constant in that limit.

VI
Cosmological Evolution of the Mass of Small Closed
p
-Branes

In this section, we study the cosmological evolution of the proper mass of small, closed
p
-branes whose center of mass is comoving with the cosmological expansion within the framework of
f_{1}(R)+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}f_{2}(R)
gravity. Building upon the general Lagrangian identity for Nambu-Goto
p
-branes, we investigate isolated, non-self-intersecting configurations in
(N+1)
-dimensional FLRW spacetimes. Again, we shall assume that the
p
-brane tension is sufficiently low for gravitational radiation emission to be negligible.

In
Avelino et al. 2009
it was shown that the average pressure of periodic, maximally symmetric Nambu-Goto
p
-branes in a de Sitter universe vanishes,

\mathfrak{P}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\equiv\frac{\int\langle{T_{i}}^{i}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\rangle d^{N}\!x}{p}=0\,,

(40)

assuming the branes are isolated and do not experience self-interactions, either gravitational or of other types (here,
d^{N}\!x=a(t)^{N}d^{N}\!q
is the
N
-dimensional physical volume element and
d^{N}\!q
is the corresponding
N
-dimensional comoving volume element). Although this result strictly applies only to this idealized class of solutions, it is expected to provide an excellent approximation for sufficiently small, mass-conserving, non-self-intersecting
p
-branes in an FLRW spacetime in
(N+1)
-dimensional GR, as the energy density of a cosmological fluid composed of such
p
-branes branes scales as
\rho\propto a^{-N}
, consistently with a vanishing average pressure
Avelino et al. 2009
. Equation (
40
) is also a necessary condition for a closed
p
-brane to behave, on average, as a particle, in the sense that the standard Lorentz transformation laws for its energy and linear momentum are satisfied
Giulini 2018
;
Avelino and Azevedo 2022a
.

Intersections of
p
-branes are constrained by the transversality theorem in differential topology
Guillemin and Pollack 1974
, which states that the generic intersection of two submanifolds of dimensions
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
embedded in an
N
-dimensional space has dimension
p_{1}+p_{2}-N
. In particular, for two
p
-branes (
p_{1}=p_{2}=p
), the intersection generically has dimension
2p-N
, so that if
2p-N<0
, intersections are not generic. This implies that for
p<N/2
, non-self-intersecting solutions with vanishing average pressure could, in principle, exist.

The results of the previous section can now be generalized to small, oscillating, non-self-intersecting
p
-branes background with proper mass

m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=-\int{T_{0}}^{0}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\,d^{N}\!x=-a^{N}\int{T_{0}}^{0}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\,d^{N}\!q\,,

(41)

in an
(N+1)
-dimensional FLRW background. Following an analogous procedure to that of the previous section, and assuming that the
p
-brane is sufficiently small so that the tiny variations of
m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}
on timescales much shorter than
|\dot{f}_{2}/f_{2}|^{-1}
and
H^{-1}
can be neglected, we find

\dot{m}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}+pH\,\mathfrak{P}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=-\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\left[m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}+\mathfrak{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\right]\,,

(42)

with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\langle\mathcal{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\rangle\,d^{N}\!x=\frac{\mathfrak{T}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}{p+1}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\int\langle T_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\rangle\,d^{N}\!x=-m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}+p\,\mathfrak{P}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\,.

(44)

Under these assumptions, Eq. (
40
) is expected to hold to an excellent approximation on timescales much shorter than both
|\dot{f}_{2}/f_{2}|^{-1}
and
H^{-1}
. In this regime,

\mathfrak{L}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}=-\frac{m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}{p+1}\,,

(45)

and the second term on the left-hand side of Eq. (
42
) can be safely neglected. Hence, Eq. (
42
) reduces to

\frac{\dot{m}_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}{m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}}=-\lambda\frac{\dot{f}_{2}}{f_{2}}\,,\qquad\lambda=\frac{p}{p+1}\,.

(46)

Consequently, the proper mass of small, oscillating, low-tension, non-self-intersecting
p
-branes in an
(N+1)
-dimensional FLRW background evolves as

m_{\rm[\it p\rm]}\propto{f_{2}}^{-\lambda}\,.

(47)

Equation (
47
) generalizes the results obtained in
Avelino and Azevedo 2022b
and in the previous section for
0
- and
1
-branes, respectively, with the exponent
\lambda
varying between
0
(
p=0
) and
1
(in the
p\to\infty
limit). For
p=1
, we recover the case of the cosmic string loop discussed in the previous section, for which
\lambda=1/2
.

VII
Conclusions

In this work, we investigated the cosmological evolution of the proper mass of particle-like objects in
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity. Although for simplicity we focused on the
f_{1}(R)+\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}f_{2}(R)
subclass of
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{\rm m})
gravity, the extension of our analysis to the full class is straightforward and leads to the same qualitative conclusions.

We demonstrated that the proper mass of closed cosmic string loops, and more generally closed
p
-branes with
p\geq 1
can evolve over cosmological timescales as a consequence of the nonminimal coupling between the gravitational and matter fields, regardless of their tiny size or tension. This contrasts with point particles in these theories, whose mass generally remains constant despite the nonminimal coupling, and with small particle-like objects in general relativity, whose proper mass is generally insensitive to the cosmological evolution.

We have shown that the Lagrangian identity we derived, which generalizes the standard
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}=T
result for point particles, plays a key role in these findings. In general, the proper mass of a particle-like object can remain constant only when the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}=T
relation is satisfied (even if only on average). These results may be crucial for the modeling fundamental particles in theories with nonminimal matter-gravity couplings, particularly in string-inspired frameworks where
p
-branes can describe extended particle-like objects. Our analysis highlights the interplay between the detailed microphysics of particle-like objects and the cosmic evolution in theories of gravity with nonminimal matter-gravity couplings, with potential applications ranging from the evolution of topological defects to the effective modeling of particle-like objects, and even the cosmological evolution of the Universe, in theories beyond GR.
