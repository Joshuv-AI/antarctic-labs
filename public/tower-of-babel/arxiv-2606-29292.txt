Finite-Order Hilbertian Gaussian Random Tensor Estimates
Guangqian Zhao

Abstract
We prove fixed finite-chaos-order estimates for Hilbert-space-valued Gaussian random tensors. Given a finite-rank kernel K ∈ 𝒜 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 𝒜 m ⊗ 𝒞 ⊗ ℰ K\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E} and the associated decoupled homogeneous Gaussian chaos operator 𝒯 K ( m ) : 𝒞 → ℰ \mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E} , we show that, for p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 and 2 ≤ r < ∞ 2\leq r<\infty , ‖ 𝒯 K ( m ) ‖ L p ​ ( Ω , 𝔖 r ​ ( 𝒞 , ℰ ) ) ≤ C m ​ ( p + r ) m / 2 ​ max S ⊂ [ m ] ​ ‖ ℱ S ​ ( K ) ‖ 𝔖 r , \|\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\max_{S\subset[m]}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}, where ℱ S ​ ( K ) : 𝒜 S ⊗ 𝒞 → 𝒜 S c ⊗ ℰ \mathcal{F}_{S}(K):\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E} is the oriented input-output flattening. The proof is an induction on m m from the rectangular non-commutative Khintchine inequality: the two square functions place the last stochastic leg on the input or output side, producing all oriented flattenings. We also derive operator-norm, rank-logarithmic, tail, Borel–Cantelli cutoff-Cauchy, same-field Wick-chaos, binary Wick-product, and completion consequences. The estimates provide deterministic flattening certificates for random operator bounds in finite Gaussian/Wick expansions. Constants depend only on the fixed chaos order and not on Hilbert-space dimensions or cutoff ranks. Thus finite order means finitely many stochastic legs, not finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces; finite-rank kernels are model cutoffs, and the infinite-dimensional statement is obtained by completion in the maximum oriented Schatten-flattening norm.



Keywords.
Gaussian chaos; non-commutative Khintchine inequality; random tensors; Schatten class; operator-valued random operators; Wick products.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}
be Hilbert spaces and let

K\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}.

For independent standard Gaussian families on the stochastic legs define

\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}g^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\cdots g^{(m)}_{i_{m}}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}.

For
S\subset[m]
let

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K):\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E}

be the oriented input-output flattening. The main estimate is

\|\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\max_{S\subset[m]}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}},\qquad p\geq 2,\quad 2\leq r<\infty.

(1.1)

The proof is by induction on
m
from the rectangular non-commutative Khintchine inequality. At the induction step the two square functions are exactly the two possible placements of the last stochastic leg; after iteration this gives all
2^{m}
oriented flattenings. The estimate is first formulated for decoupled homogeneous chaoses; same-field Wick chaoses are then reduced to this form by standard finite-order decoupling.

Throughout the paper the chaos order
m
is fixed. Constants may depend on
m
, while remaining independent of Hilbert-space dimensions and cutoff ranks. Thus finite order refers to the number of stochastic Hilbert legs, whereas the Hilbert spaces themselves may be infinite-dimensional after completion. This is the natural regime for constructions which reduce, after a model has been specified, to finitely many homogeneous Gaussian chaoses or Wick components; the relevant uniformity is then in the analytic cutoffs rather than in the chaos order. At this finite-chaos level, any estimate reduced to deterministic flattening bounds for finitely many Gaussian kernels falls within the scope of the criterion.

The formulation is Hilbertian rather than coordinate-specific. A centered Gaussian input is used through its associated Gaussian Hilbert space, or through finite-dimensional cutoffs thereof. Consequently the estimates are insensitive to the particular realization of the Gaussian field, such as orthonormal coordinates, colored coordinates, or correlated finite-dimensional coordinates within the corresponding Gaussian Hilbert space. The same formulation is compatible with geometric realizations: geometry enters through deterministic Hilbert spaces, projections, kernels, and flattening bounds, while the probabilistic estimate keeps the same Hilbertian form.

The operator-valued form is meant to serve as a deterministic certificate for random operator bounds. In concrete problems one often starts from a model-dependent kernel carrying several Gaussian legs, one input variable, and one output variable. After passing to a Gaussian Hilbert-space representation, Wick expansion, or decoupling, the probabilistic part of the argument is reduced to checking the finite family of oriented Schatten flattenings. The conclusion then gives moment, tail, operator-norm envelope, and Borel–Cantelli cutoff-Cauchy bounds. This provides a convenient interface for Galerkin approximations, finite Duhamel/Wick expansions, colored Gaussian noises, non-orthogonal covariance profiles, and Fourier-analytic or geometric kernels. The point of the formulation is that the probabilistic estimate is Hilbertian and fixed-order, while all model-specific information enters through deterministic flattening profiles.

For example, at order
m=2
the criterion asks for the four input-output profiles corresponding to
S=\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\}
. Thus a model-dependent bilinear kernel is tested by placing no stochastic leg, the first stochastic leg, the second stochastic leg, or both stochastic legs on the input side. Once these four deterministic Schatten bounds are available, the probabilistic moment, tail, operator-norm, and cutoff-Cauchy conclusions follow with constants independent of the cutoff ranks.

In infinite dimension, the natural object for this criterion is the flattening-norm completion of finite-rank kernels, rather than the ambient Hilbert tensor product alone. Here finite rank refers to model cutoffs in the Hilbert legs, and the chaos order
m
remains fixed throughout the passage to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Thus the focus is on dimension- and cutoff-uniform Hilbert-space/Schatten upper bounds for operator-valued Gaussian chaoses.

Comparison with prior work.

The present paper packages the preceding inputs in a fixed finite-order Hilbertian operator form. Kaneshiro
[

The argument below uses the same non-commutative Khintchine and decoupling philosophy in a Hilbert-space operator setting. In this setting, the partition norms appearing in the abstract random-tensor estimate are realized as oriented Schatten input-output flattenings

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K):\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E}.

This translation is useful because it turns the probabilistic estimate into a dimension- and cutoff-uniform certificate for random operators between Hilbert spaces: one checks a finite family of deterministic Schatten profiles and obtains moment, tail, rank-logarithmic operator-norm, and cutoff-Cauchy conclusions. The random-tensor estimates of Deng–Nahmod–Yue

[

Thus the paper should be read as a Hilbertian input-output interface for finite Gaussian/Wick expansions, rather than as a replacement for the abstract random-tensor theory.

Relation with localized second-chaos estimates.

The same separation is useful whenever the model-dependent analysis can be expressed as a finite family of input-output flattening estimates.

Notation and conventions.

All Hilbert spaces are complex unless explicitly stated otherwise. The inner product on a Hilbert space is denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
; the convention is irrelevant for the estimates, provided the Riesz identification is used consistently. Tensor products of Hilbert spaces are Hilbert tensor products. In finite dimension this is the same as the algebraic tensor product equipped with the induced Hilbert norm.

When tensor legs are moved to the domain side of an operator flattening over complex Hilbert spaces, the intrinsic interpretation uses the conjugate Hilbert-space/Riesz-dual convention on those input-side legs. We suppress the conjugation bars in the notation and write the resulting domains simply as tensor products such as
\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}
. The displayed coordinate formulae below fix this convention, and all Schatten and operator norms are unchanged under these canonical antiunitary identifications.

For Hilbert spaces
U,V
,
\mathcal{L}(U,V)
denotes the bounded linear operators from
U
to
V
. For
2\leq r<\infty
,
\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)
denotes the Schatten class of compact operators from
U
to
V
, with norm

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)}=\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}s_{j}(T)^{r}\right)^{1/r},

where
(s_{j}(T))_{j\geq 1}
are the singular values of
T
, repeated with multiplicity. We write
\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U)=\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,U)
and
\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}(U,V)=\mathcal{L}(U,V)
. If
B
is a Banach space and
X
is a
B
-valued random variable, then

\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}B}:=\bigl(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{B}^{p}\bigr)^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty.

For an integer
m\geq 1
we write
[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The symmetric group on
[m]
is denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. If
S\subset[m]
, then
S^{c}=[m]\setminus S
. Products indexed by subsets are always ordered increasingly; with
\mathcal{A}_{S}=\bigotimes_{\nu\in S}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\emptyset}=\mathbb{C}
. If
T_{1}:U_{0}\to U_{1}
and
T_{2}:U_{1}\to U_{2}
are linear maps, the product
T_{2}T_{1}
means composition from right to left.

A standard real Gaussian variable has law
N(0,1)
. A standard circular complex Gaussian variable is
\zeta=(\gamma+i\eta)/\sqrt{2}
, where
\gamma,\eta
are independent standard real Gaussians. The Wick-chaos statements are written for real isonormal processes; complex coefficients are handled by deterministic complexification, and circular complex Gaussian families are treated by realification.
An isonormal Gaussian process over a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a linear map
W:\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying

\mathbb{E}\bigl[W(h)W(k)\bigr]=\left\langle h,k\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\qquad h,k\in\mathcal{H}.

If
F\subset\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional with orthonormal basis
(e_{a})_{a=1}^{N}
and
g_{a}=W(e_{a})
, then for an ordered multi-index
{\bf i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})
we use the Wick monomial normalized by

:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{q}}::=\prod_{a=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{a}({\bf i})}(g_{a}),\qquad\alpha_{a}({\bf i}):=\#\{\nu:i_{\nu}=a\},

(1.2)

where
H_{n}
is the probabilists’ Hermite polynomial. This convention is equivalently characterized by

\mathbb{E}\left[:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{q}}::g_{j_{1}}\cdots g_{j_{\ell}}:\right]=\mathbf{1}_{q=\ell}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{q}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{q}\delta_{i_{\nu}j_{\sigma(\nu)}}.

(1.3)

For
q
stochastic copies of one Hilbert space, the symmetrization operator is

\operatorname{Sym}_{q}:=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{q}}U_{\tau},\qquad U_{\tau}(h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{q}):=h_{\tau^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{\tau^{-1}(q)}.

(1.4)

Thus, in coordinates,

(\operatorname{Sym}_{q}K)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{q}}=\frac{1}{q!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{q}}K_{i_{\tau(1)}\cdots i_{\tau(q)}}.

(1.5)

For
q=0
,
\operatorname{Sym}_{0}
is the identity and
I_{0}(T)=T
.

Constants denoted by
C
are numerical and may change from line to line. Constants denoted by
C_{m}
may depend on the fixed order
m
only. The notation
A\lesssim_{m}B
means
A\leq C_{m}B
.

2
Finite-rank model estimate

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed. The notation from Section
1
is used throughout. The integer
m
is the chaos order, namely the number of stochastic Hilbert legs. This section proves the model estimate for kernels supported on finite-dimensional Hilbert subspaces; Section
4
then passes to infinite-dimensional Hilbert legs by flattening-norm completion. In finite dimension every linear map belongs to every Schatten class; in the completed theorem the membership of each flattening in the chosen Schatten class is part of the hypothesis encoded by the completed tensor ideal.

2.1
Tensor legs and oriented flattenings

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m}
be stochastic Hilbert legs, let
\mathcal{C}
be the input Hilbert space, and let
\mathcal{E}
be the output Hilbert space. Write

[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{S}:=\bigotimes_{\nu\in S}\mathcal{A}_{\nu},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\emptyset}:=\mathbb{C},

with tensor products taken in the increasing order of indices. Let

K\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}.

(2.1)

Choose orthonormal bases
\{a^{(\nu)}_{i_{\nu}}\}
,
\{c_{\alpha}\}
, and
\{e_{\beta}\}
and write

K=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m},\alpha,\beta}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}\alpha\beta}a^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{(m)}_{i_{m}}\otimes c_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}.

(2.2)

For a multi-index
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
define
K_{\mathbf{i}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}
by

K_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}\alpha\beta}e_{\beta}.

(2.3)

Definition 2.1

(Oriented flattenings)
.

For every
S\subset[m]
define

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K):\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E}

(2.4)

by the coordinate rule

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\left(\bigotimes_{\nu\in S}a^{(\nu)}_{i_{\nu}}\otimes c_{\alpha}\right):=\sum_{(i_{\mu})_{\mu\in S^{c}},\beta}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}\alpha\beta}\left(\bigotimes_{\mu\in S^{c}}a^{(\mu)}_{i_{\mu}}\right)\otimes e_{\beta}.

(2.5)

Equivalently, after the standard conjugate Hilbert-space/Riesz-dual identification on the input-side factors,
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)
is the canonical contraction obtained by moving the factors indexed by
S
, together with the input leg
\mathcal{C}
, to the domain. With the suppressed conjugation convention from Section
1
, this is expressed by the shorthand

\left\langle\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)(a_{S}\otimes c),a_{S^{c}}\otimes e\right\rangle=\left\langle K,a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m}\otimes c\otimes e\right\rangle

for elementary vectors
a_{\nu}\in\mathcal{A}_{\nu}
,
c\in\mathcal{C}
, and
e\in\mathcal{E}
. The coordinate rule (
2.5
) fixes the convention, and the resulting Schatten norms are basis-independent.
For
2\leq r\leq\infty
define the order-
m
flattening profile

\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K):=\max_{S\subset[m]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(2.6)

For
r=\infty
this is the maximum operator norm of the
2^{m}
flattenings.

Lemma 2.2

(Basis invariance and stability)
.

The quantity
\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K)
is independent of the orthonormal bases used in (
2.2
). Moreover, if
U_{\nu}:\mathcal{A}_{\nu}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{\nu}
,
U_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, and
U_{\mathcal{E}}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are bounded maps and

K^{\prime}=(U_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{m}\otimes U_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{E}})K,

then, for every
2\leq r\leq\infty
,

\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K^{\prime})\leq\left(\prod_{\nu=1}^{m}\left\lVert U_{\nu}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert U_{\mathcal{C}}\right\rVert\left\lVert U_{\mathcal{E}}\right\rVert\,\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K).

(2.7)

Proof.

A change of orthonormal bases applies unitary maps to the legs, and Schatten norms are invariant under unitary multiplication on the domain and range. For (
2.7
), each flattening of
K^{\prime}
is obtained from the corresponding flattening of
K
by composition with tensor products of the maps
U_{\nu},U_{\mathcal{C}},U_{\mathcal{E}}
or their Hilbert adjoints. The ideal property of Schatten norms gives the estimate.
∎

2.2
Gaussian tensor theorem

Let
\{g^{(\nu)}_{i}\}_{i}
,
\nu=1,\ldots,m
, be independent standard real or complex Gaussian families, one family for each stochastic leg. Define the decoupled homogeneous Gaussian random operator

\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}:=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}g^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\cdots g^{(m)}_{i_{m}}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}).

(2.8)

2.3
Khintchine input and square-function identities

Lemma 2.3

(Self-adjoint Schatten Gaussian Khintchine input)
.

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and let
B_{k}=B_{k}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(W)
be self-adjoint. Let
(\gamma_{k})
be independent standard real Gaussians. For
p\geq 2
and
2\leq r<\infty
,

\biggl\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}B_{k}\biggr\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(W)}\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\biggl\|\biggl(\sum_{k}B_{k}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(W)}.

(2.9)

More precisely, if

\sigma_{r}(B):=\biggl\|\biggl(\sum_{k}B_{k}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(W)},

(2.10)

then

\mathbb{E}\biggl\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}B_{k}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq C\sqrt{r}\,\sigma_{r}(B),\qquad\operatorname{Lip}_{\ell^{2}\to\mathbb{R}}\biggl[\gamma\mapsto\biggl\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}B_{k}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\biggr]\leq\sigma_{r}(B).

(2.11)

Proof.

Set

X(\gamma)=\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}B_{k},\qquad f(\gamma)=\left\lVert X(\gamma)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

The expectation bound in (
2.11
) follows from the self-adjoint non-commutative Khintchine inequality at the Schatten exponent
r
; see
[
10
,
13
]
:

\mathbb{E}f\leq(\mathbb{E}f^{r})^{1/r}\leq C\sqrt{r}\,\biggl\|\biggl(\sum_{k}B_{k}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(2.12)

We next compute the Lipschitz constant. For every scalar vector
a=(a_{k})
,

\biggl\|\sum_{k}a_{k}B_{k}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq\biggl(\sum_{k}|a_{k}|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggl\|\biggl(\sum_{k}B_{k}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(2.13)

Indeed, this is the non-commutative Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\left\lVert\sum_{k}X_{k}Y_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq\left\lVert(\sum_{k}X_{k}X_{k}^{*})^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}\left\lVert(\sum_{k}Y_{k}^{*}Y_{k})^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}

(2.14)

with
X_{k}=a_{k}\operatorname{Id}_{W}
and
Y_{k}=B_{k}
. Since
X_{k}
is a scalar multiple of the identity on
W
, the domain–range placements match directly, and the ideal property of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
gives (
2.13
). Hence

|f(\gamma)-f(\eta)|\leq\biggl\|\sum_{k}(\gamma_{k}-\eta_{k})B_{k}\biggr\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq\left\lVert\gamma-\eta\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}\sigma_{r}(B).

(2.15)

The standard Gaussian concentration inequality for Lipschitz functions on Euclidean Gaussian space, in its
L^{p}
form, gives for
p\geq 2
(see, for instance,
[
9
, Chapter 2]
)

\left\lVert f-\mathbb{E}f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\sqrt{p}\,\sigma_{r}(B).

(2.16)

Combining (
2.12
) and (
2.16
) yields

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C(\sqrt{r}+\sqrt{p})\sigma_{r}(B)\leq C^{\prime}\sqrt{p+r}\,\sigma_{r}(B),

which is (
2.9
).
∎

Lemma 2.4

(Rectangular Gaussian Khintchine)
.

Let
U,V
be finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and let
A_{k}\in\mathcal{L}(U,V)
. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be independent standard real Gaussian variables or independent standard circular complex Gaussian variables. Then for every
p\geq 2
and
2\leq r<\infty
,

\left(\mathbb{E}_{\gamma}\left\lVert\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\,\max\left\{\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V)},\left\lVert\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U)}\right\}.

(2.17)

Consequently the same upper bound controls the
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(U,V))
norm of the series.

Proof.

It is enough to derive the rectangular form from the self-adjoint form. For real Gaussians set

\mathbb{A}_{k}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_{k}\\
A_{k}^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{L}(V\oplus U).

(2.18)

Then

\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\
X^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad X:=\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k},

(2.19)

and the non-zero singular values of the block matrix are the singular values of
X
, each repeated twice. Thus

\left\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V\oplus U)}=2^{1/r}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)}.

(2.20)

Moreover

\sum_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}&0\\
0&\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\end{pmatrix},

(2.21)

so

\left\|\left(\sum_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V\oplus U)}=\left(\left\|\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V)}^{r}+\left\|\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U)}^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

(2.22)

Applying Lemma
2.3
to the self-adjoint family
(\mathbb{A}_{k})_{k}
gives

\left\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V\oplus U)}\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\left\|\left(\sum_{k}\mathbb{A}_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V\oplus U)}.

(2.23)

Combining (
2.20
)–(
2.23
) and using

(a^{r}+b^{r})^{1/r}\leq 2^{1/r}\max\{a,b\}\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{a,b\},\qquad r\geq 2,

(2.24)

proves (
2.17
) for real Gaussians. For circular Gaussians use

\sum_{k}\zeta_{k}A_{k}=2^{-1/2}\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k}+i2^{-1/2}\sum_{k}\eta_{k}A_{k},

(2.25)

with two independent real families
(\gamma_{k})
and
(\eta_{k})
. If
M
denotes the maximum of the two square functions in (
2.17
), then the real estimate and Minkowski’s inequality give

\left\|\sum_{k}\zeta_{k}A_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq 2^{-1/2}\left\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}+2^{-1/2}\left\|\sum_{k}\eta_{k}A_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq\sqrt{2}\,C\sqrt{p+r}\,M,

and the factor
\sqrt{2}
is absorbed into the absolute constant. The final operator-norm statement follows from

\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(U,V)}\leq\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)}.

(2.26)

∎

Lemma 2.5

(Conditional rectangular Khintchine)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a sub-
\sigma
-algebra and let
A_{k}
be
\mathcal{G}
-measurable random operators in
\mathcal{L}(U,V)
. Let
\{\gamma_{k}\}
be a Gaussian family independent of
\mathcal{G}
. Then, for
p\geq 2
and
2\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V))}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\max\left\{\left\|\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(V))},\left\|\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U))}\right\}.

(2.27)

Proof.

Set

X=\sum_{k}\gamma_{k}A_{k},\qquad R_{A}=\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}A_{k}^{*}\right)^{1/2},\qquad L_{A}=\left(\sum_{k}A_{k}^{*}A_{k}\right)^{1/2}.

Since the coefficients
A_{k}
are
\mathcal{G}
-measurable and the Gaussian family is independent of
\mathcal{G}
, Lemma
2.4
gives, for almost every value of the
\mathcal{G}
-variables,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\mid\mathcal{G}\bigr]\leq C^{p}(p+r)^{p/2}\max\{\|R_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p},\|L_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\}.

Equivalently,

\mathbb{E}\|X\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}=\mathbb{E}\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|X\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\mid\mathcal{G}\bigr]\leq C^{p}(p+r)^{p/2}\mathbb{E}\max\{\|R_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p},\|L_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\}.

Finally

\mathbb{E}\max\{\|R_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p},\|L_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\}\leq\mathbb{E}\|R_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}+\mathbb{E}\|L_{A}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\leq 2\max\{\|R_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p},\|L_{A}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}^{p}\}.

Taking the
p
-th root absorbs the factor
2^{1/p}
into the absolute constant and proves (
2.5
).
∎

Lemma 2.6

(Square-function linearization)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space with orthonormal basis
(a_{j})
and let
T_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
. Define

U:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},\qquad U(a_{j}\otimes c)=T_{j}c,

and

V:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E},\qquad Vc=\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes T_{j}c.

Then, for every
2\leq r<\infty
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{E})},

(2.28)

\displaystyle\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{E})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{j}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C})}.

(2.29)

Proof.

The adjoint of
U
is
U^{*}e=\sum_{j}a_{j}\otimes T_{j}^{*}e
, hence

UU^{*}=\sum_{j}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}.

The non-zero singular values of
U
are the eigenvalues of
(UU^{*})^{1/2}
, which gives (
2.28
). Likewise,

V^{*}(a_{j}\otimes e)=T_{j}^{*}e,\qquad V^{*}V=\sum_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{j},

and (
2.29
) follows.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(Last-leg flattening identities)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}
. Define tensors
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
of order
m-1
by interpreting the last stochastic leg respectively as part of the input and output:

K^{+}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m-1}\otimes(\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C})\otimes\mathcal{E},

K^{-}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m-1}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes(\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{E}),

with coordinate rules

K^{+}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1}}(a_{j}^{(m)}\otimes c)=K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1}j}c,

K^{-}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1}}c=\sum_{j}a_{j}^{(m)}\otimes K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1}j}c.

Then, for every
S\subset[m-1]
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{+})\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S\cup\{m\}}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}},

(2.30)

\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{-})\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}=\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(2.31)

Proof.

The map
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{+})
has domain
\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}
and range
\mathcal{A}_{[m-1]\setminus S}\otimes\mathcal{E}
. After the canonical permutation placing
\mathcal{A}_{m}
next to the other input-side stochastic legs, it is exactly
\mathcal{F}_{S\cup\{m\}}(K)
. The permutation is unitary, so the Schatten norm is unchanged. The map
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{-})
has domain
\mathcal{A}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}
and range
\mathcal{A}_{[m-1]\setminus S}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{E}
; after the canonical permutation of output-side stochastic legs, it is exactly
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)
. This proves both identities.
∎

2.4
The finite-order estimate

Theorem 2.8

(Fixed-chaos-order Hilbertian Gaussian tensor estimate)
.

For every fixed
m\geq 1
there is a constant
C_{m}<\infty
such that, for all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}
, all tensors
K
as in (
2.1
), all
p\geq 2
, and all
2\leq r<\infty
,

\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\,\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K).

(2.32)

Consequently,

\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\,\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K).

(2.33)

The operator-norm estimate in (
2.33
) is the Schatten-envelope estimate at the chosen finite parameter
r
. Equivalently,

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C_{m}\inf_{2\leq s<\infty}(p+s)^{m/2}\mathfrak{R}_{m,s}(K),

(2.34)

whenever the right-hand side is finite. Moreover, if
\kappa
is an admissible absolute constant in Lemma
2.5
, the constants may be chosen so that
C_{m}\leq\kappa^{m}
.

Proof.

We prove (
2.32
) by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, Lemma
2.4
applied to
\sum_{i}g_{i}^{(1)}K_{i}
gives

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\max\{\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\emptyset}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}},\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{\{1\}}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\}.

Assume the estimate holds at order
m-1
. Separate the last stochastic index and write

T_{j}=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m-1}}g^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\cdots g^{(m-1)}_{i_{m-1}}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-1}j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}).

Applying Lemma
2.5
to the last Gaussian family gives

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C\sqrt{p+r}\max\{R_{+},R_{-}\},

(2.35)

where

R_{+}=\left\|\left(\sum_{j}T_{j}T_{j}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{E})},\qquad R_{-}=\left\|\left(\sum_{j}T_{j}^{*}T_{j}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C})}.

For each realization of the first
m-1
Gaussian families define

U_{\omega}:\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E},\qquad U_{\omega}(a_{j}^{(m)}\otimes c)=T_{j}(\omega)c,

and

V_{\omega}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{E},\qquad V_{\omega}c=\sum_{j}a_{j}^{(m)}\otimes T_{j}(\omega)c.

Lemma
2.6
gives

R_{+}=\left\lVert U_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})},\qquad R_{-}=\left\lVert V_{\omega}\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{E})}.

The random operator
U_{\omega}
is the order-
(m-1)
decoupled chaos associated with
K^{+}
, where
K^{+}
has input space
\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}
and output space
\mathcal{E}
. Hence the induction hypothesis and Lemma
2.7
imply

R_{+}\leq C_{m-1}(p+r)^{(m-1)/2}\max_{S\subset[m-1]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S\cup\{m\}}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

Likewise
V_{\omega}
is the order-
(m-1)
decoupled chaos associated with
K^{-}
, where
K^{-}
has input space
\mathcal{C}
and output space
\mathcal{A}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{E}
. Therefore

R_{-}\leq C_{m-1}(p+r)^{(m-1)/2}\max_{S\subset[m-1]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

Substitution into (
2.35
) gives

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq CC_{m-1}(p+r)^{m/2}\max_{A\subset[m]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{A}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}},

which is (
2.32
) with
C_{m}\leq CC_{m-1}
. Absorbing the absolute constant
C
into the admissible Khintchine constant
\kappa
gives
C_{m}\leq\kappa^{m}
. The operator estimate follows from
\mathfrak{S}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Corollary 2.9

(Rank-logarithmic operator-flattening bound)
.

Let
K
be as in Theorem
2.8
. Define the finite-rank operator-flattening profile

P_{*}(K):=\mathfrak{R}_{m,\infty}(K)=\max_{S\subset[m]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}},\qquad D_{*}(K):=1\vee\max_{S\subset[m]}\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{F}_{S}(K).

(2.36)

Then, for every
p\geq 2
and every
2\leq r<\infty
,

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}D_{*}(K)^{1/r}P_{*}(K).

(2.37)

Consequently, with

r_{*}(K):=2\vee\log(eD_{*}(K)),

(2.38)

one has

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}\leq C_{m}^{\prime}\bigl(p+\log(eD_{*}(K))\bigr)^{m/2}P_{*}(K).

(2.39)

Proof.

For a finite-rank operator
A
,

\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\operatorname{rank}A}s_{j}(A)^{r}\right)^{1/r}\leq(\operatorname{rank}A)^{1/r}\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}.

(2.40)

Hence

\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K)=\max_{S\subset[m]}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\right\rVert_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq D_{*}(K)^{1/r}P_{*}(K).

(2.41)

Substituting (
2.41
) into (
2.33
) gives (
2.37
). For
r=r_{*}(K)
,

D_{*}(K)^{1/r_{*}(K)}\leq e,\qquad p+r_{*}(K)\leq 2\bigl(p+\log(eD_{*}(K))\bigr),

(2.42)

for
p\geq 2
and
D_{*}(K)\geq 1
. This gives (
2.39
) after changing the order-dependent constant.
∎

Corollary 2.10

(Sub-Weibull tail with the Schatten parameter)
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
2\leq r<\infty
, and
A=\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}(K)
. If
A=0
, then
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}=0
almost surely. If
A>0
, there are constants
c_{m},C_{m}>0
such that, for every
u>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}>u\right)\leq 2\exp\left[-c_{m}\left(\left(\frac{u}{C_{m}A}\right)^{2/m}-r\right)_{+}\right].

(2.43)

In particular, if
u\geq C_{m}Ar^{m/2}
, then

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}>u\right)\leq 2\exp\left[-c_{m}\left(\frac{u}{C_{m}A}\right)^{2/m}\right].

(2.44)

Proof.

Assume
A>0
and set
X=\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}
. Theorem
2.8
gives

\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C_{m}A(p+r)^{m/2},\qquad p\geq 2.

If
(u/(eC_{m}A))^{2/m}\leq r+2
, then the right-hand side of (
2.43
) is bounded below by an absolute constant after decreasing
c_{m}
, while the left-hand side is at most one. Otherwise choose an integer

2\leq p\leq\left(\frac{u}{eC_{m}A}\right)^{2/m}-r\leq p+1.

Then
(p+r)^{m/2}\leq u/(eC_{m}A)
, and Markov’s inequality gives
\mathbb{P}(X>u)\leq e^{-p}
. This is (
2.43
). For the large-threshold form, enlarge the constant in the hypothesis
u\geq C_{m}Ar^{m/2}
so that
(u/(C_{m}A))^{2/m}\geq 2r
; then the negative
r
term in (
2.43
) is absorbed into the leading power after changing
c_{m}
.
∎

2.5
Borel–Cantelli convergence

Corollary 2.11

(Borel–Cantelli profile bound)
.

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed. For each
n\geq 1
, let
K_{n}
be an order-
m
finite-rank model tensor in finite-dimensional Hilbert legs, let
2\leq r_{n}<\infty
, and set

X_{n}:=\left\lVert\mathcal{T}_{K_{n}}^{(m)}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{n})},\qquad A_{n}:=\mathfrak{R}_{m,r_{n}}(K_{n}).

(2.45)

Let
p_{n}\geq 2
and
b_{n}>0
. If

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{C_{m}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}A_{n}}{b_{n}}\right)^{p_{n}}<\infty,

(2.46)

then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(X_{n}>b_{n}\ \mathrm{i.o.}\bigr)=0.

(2.47)

Equivalently, outside a null set there exists
n_{0}(\omega)
such that

X_{n}(\omega)\leq b_{n},\qquad n\geq n_{0}(\omega).

(2.48)

In particular, if
\Lambda_{n}\geq 1
satisfies

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}^{-p_{n}}<\infty,

(2.49)

then, with

b_{n}=C_{m}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}A_{n},

(2.50)

we have

X_{n}(\omega)\leq C_{m}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\mathfrak{R}_{m,r_{n}}(K_{n})\qquad\text{for all large }n

(2.51)

for almost every
\omega
.

Proof.

Theorem
2.8
and
\mathfrak{S}_{r_{n}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
give

\left\lVert X_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{p_{n}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{m}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}A_{n}.

(2.52)

Therefore Markov’s inequality gives

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>b_{n})\leq\left(\frac{C_{m}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}A_{n}}{b_{n}}\right)^{p_{n}}.

(2.53)

The summability assumption (
2.46
) and the first Borel–Cantelli lemma imply (
2.47
). Taking
b_{n}
as in (
2.50
) reduces (
2.46
) to (
2.49
).
∎

Corollary 2.12

(Almost sure Cauchy criterion for cutoff tensors)
.

Let
K^{(n)}
be finite-rank tensors in common Hilbert legs
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{m},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}
, all of the same fixed order
m
, and define

\Delta K_{n}:=K^{(n+1)}-K^{(n)},\qquad\Delta\mathcal{T}_{n}:=\mathcal{T}_{K^{(n+1)}}^{(m)}-\mathcal{T}_{K^{(n)}}^{(m)}=\mathcal{T}_{\Delta K_{n}}^{(m)}.

(2.54)

Assume that there are
p_{n}\geq 2
,
2\leq r_{n}<\infty
, and
\Lambda_{n}\geq 1
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}^{-p_{n}}<\infty

(2.55)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\,\mathfrak{R}_{m,r_{n}}(\Delta K_{n})<\infty.

(2.56)

Then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\Delta\mathcal{T}_{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}<\infty\qquad\text{almost surely.}

(2.57)

Consequently
(\mathcal{T}_{K^{(n)}}^{(m)})_{n}
converges almost surely in the operator norm of the common space
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

Apply Corollary
2.11
to
K_{n}=\Delta K_{n}
. Outside a null set, for all sufficiently large
n
,

\left\lVert\Delta\mathcal{T}_{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C_{m}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\,\mathfrak{R}_{m,r_{n}}(\Delta K_{n}).

(2.58)

The right side is summable by (
2.56
). The finitely many initial terms are finite almost surely because every
\Delta\mathcal{T}_{n}
is a finite Gaussian polynomial with values in a finite-dimensional operator space. This proves (
2.57
); the Cauchy conclusion follows from the telescoping identity

\mathcal{T}_{K^{(N)}}^{(m)}-\mathcal{T}_{K^{(M)}}^{(m)}=\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}\Delta\mathcal{T}_{n}.

(2.59)

∎

3
Same-field Wick chaoses

3.1
Homogeneous Wick chaos and decoupling

Throughout this section
W
is a real isonormal Gaussian process over a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Complex Hilbert spaces and complex operator coefficients are handled by complexification of the deterministic coefficient spaces. The symmetric tensor power of order
m
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{\odot m}
. The order
m
is again the chaos degree. Whenever a Schatten flattening profile is invoked below, the operator-valued kernel is understood first in the finite-rank input-output sense; the general case is obtained by the flattening completion of Section
4
.

Definition 3.1

(Finite-rank operator-valued multiple integrals)
.

Let
F\subset\mathcal{H}
be finite dimensional with orthonormal basis
(h_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
, and write
g_{i}=W(h_{i})
. For a finite ordered tensor

K\in F^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}),\qquad K=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{N}h_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_{m}}\otimes K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}},

(3.1)

set

I_{m}(K):=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{N}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\,:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{m}}:\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})).

(3.2)

For
m=0
we set
I_{0}(T)=T
. If
K
is first given in the symmetric tensor space
F^{\odot m}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
, it is embedded into
F^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
and the same formula is used.

Lemma 3.2

(Symmetrization and
m!
normalization)
.

For every finite ordered kernel
K\in F^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
,

I_{m}(K)=I_{m}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K).

(3.3)

For
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
y\in\mathcal{E}
define the scalar coefficient kernel

K_{x,y}:=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}=1}^{N}\left\langle K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}x,y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}h_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_{m}}\in F^{\otimes m}.

(3.4)

Then

\left\langle I_{m}(K)x,y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=I_{m}^{\mathrm{scal}}(K_{x,y}),

(3.5)

where
I_{m}^{\mathrm{scal}}
is the scalar Wiener integral with the normalization (
3.2
). If
K
and
L
have orders
m
and
n
, respectively, then

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\left\langle I_{m}(K)x,y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{\left\langle I_{n}(L)u,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}}\Bigr]=\begin{cases}m!\,\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K_{x,y},\operatorname{Sym}_{m}L_{u,v}\right\rangle_{F^{\otimes m}},&m=n,\\[5.69054pt]
0,&m\neq n.\end{cases}

(3.6)

Equivalently, for scalar kernels
f,h\in F^{\otimes m}
,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[I_{m}^{\mathrm{scal}}(f)\,I_{m}^{\mathrm{scal}}(h)\bigr]=m!\,\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}_{m}f,\operatorname{Sym}_{m}h\right\rangle_{F^{\otimes m}}.

(3.7)

Thus the factor
m!
belongs to the
L^{2}
covariance identity, not to the pointwise definition (
3.2
).

Proof.

The Wick monomial is symmetric in its
m
entries; hence, using (
1.5
),

\displaystyle I_{m}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}K_{i_{\tau(1)}\cdots i_{\tau(m)}}:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{m}}:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}K_{j_{1}\cdots j_{m}}:g_{j_{1}}\cdots g_{j_{m}}:=I_{m}(K).

Equation (
3.5
) follows by applying the deterministic operator coefficient to
x
and pairing with
y
. If
m\neq n
, distinct homogeneous Wiener chaoses are orthogonal. If
m=n
, then (
1.3
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\left\langle I_{m}(K)x,y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{\left\langle I_{m}(L)u,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}\left\langle K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}x,y\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{\left\langle L_{j_{1}\cdots j_{m}}u,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{m}\delta_{i_{\nu}j_{\sigma(\nu)}}

\displaystyle=m!\,\left\langle\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K_{x,y},\operatorname{Sym}_{m}L_{u,v}\right\rangle_{F^{\otimes m}}.

This is (
3.6
). The scalar identity (
3.7
) is the special case
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{E}=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Basis independence of finite-rank multiple integrals)
.

Let
(h_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
and
(\widetilde{h}_{a})_{a=1}^{N}
be two orthonormal bases of the same finite-dimensional subspace
F\subset\mathcal{H}
. If

K=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}h_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_{m}}\otimes K^{h}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}\widetilde{h}_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\widetilde{h}_{a_{m}}\otimes K^{\widetilde{h}}_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}},

then

\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}K^{h}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}:W(h_{i_{1}})\cdots W(h_{i_{m}}):=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}}K^{\widetilde{h}}_{a_{1}\cdots a_{m}}:W(\widetilde{h}_{a_{1}})\cdots W(\widetilde{h}_{a_{m}}):.

(3.8)

Proof.

The scalar map
f\mapsto I_{m}^{\mathrm{scal}}(f)
is the canonical isometry from the symmetric tensor space
F^{\odot m}
, equipped with the norm
\sqrt{m!}\,\|\cdot\|_{F^{\otimes m}}
, onto the
m
-th homogeneous Gaussian chaos generated by
W|_{F}
, by (
3.7
). Hence the two sides of (
3.8
) have the same scalar matrix coefficient against every
x\in\mathcal{C}
and
y\in\mathcal{E}
, by (
3.5
). This gives the operator-valued identity.
∎

The following two estimates are standard finite-order Banach-valued Gaussian decoupling inputs; see
[
3
,
7
]
. They are used only as fixed-order black boxes, and their constants depend only on the chaos order.

Theorem 3.4

(Standard decoupling input for Wiener chaoses)
.

Let
B
be a Banach space, let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space, and let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot m}\otimes B
be a symmetric finite-rank
B
-valued kernel. If
I_{m}(f)
denotes the
m
-th Wick/Wiener–Itô integral over one isonormal process
W
, and if
W^{(1)},\ldots,W^{(m)}
are independent copies, then

\|I_{m}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)}\leq D_{m}\left\|\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}g^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\cdots g^{(m)}_{i_{m}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)},\qquad p\geq 1,

(3.9)

where
(f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}})
are the coordinates of
f
in any orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. The constant
D_{m}
depends only on
m
.

Theorem 3.5

(Standard Banach-valued homogeneous Gaussian decoupling)
.

Let
B
be a Banach space and let
(b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}})
be a finitely supported
B
-valued coefficient array. Let
(g_{i})
be a standard real Gaussian family and let
(g_{i}^{(\nu)})
,
1\leq\nu\leq m
, be independent copies. Then there is a constant
D_{m}<\infty
, depending only on
m
, such that for every
p\geq 1
,

\left\|\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{m}}:\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)}\leq D_{m}\left\|\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}b)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}g_{i_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots g_{i_{m}}^{(m)}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)}.

(3.10)

Here

(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}b)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}:=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}b_{i_{\tau(1)}\cdots i_{\tau(m)}}.

(3.11)

Below
B=\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
or
B=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
. The constant
D_{m}
is independent of
p
, the finite index set, the Hilbert dimensions, and the coefficient array.

Proposition 3.6

(Decoupling of homogeneous Wick chaos)
.

Let
B
be either
\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
with
2\leq r<\infty
or
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{(1)},\ldots,\mathcal{H}^{(m)}
be independent copies of
\mathcal{H}
. For

K=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}h_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_{m}}\otimes K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}),

define

\operatorname{Dec}_{m}K:=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}h_{i_{1}}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i_{m}}^{(m)}\otimes K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}

(3.12)

and

I_{m}^{\mathrm{dec}}(K):=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}g_{i_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots g_{i_{m}}^{(m)}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}=\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{Dec}_{m}K}^{(m)}.

(3.13)

For
2\leq r<\infty
set

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{m,r}(K):=\mathfrak{R}_{m,r}\bigl(\operatorname{Dec}_{m}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K)\bigr),\qquad\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{0,r}(L):=\|L\|_{\mathcal{L}}.

(3.14)

Here the symmetrization is taken over the stochastic copies before the deterministic operator leg is viewed as a coefficient. If the displayed profile is infinite, the corresponding estimate is read as void; if the decoupled symmetrized kernel belongs only to the completed flattening class, the same notation refers to the completed norm of Section
4
. For every fixed
m
there is a constant
D_{m}
such that

\left\lVert I_{m}(K)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)}\leq D_{m}\left\lVert I_{m}^{\mathrm{dec}}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;B)},\qquad p\geq 2.

(3.15)

Consequently, for
2\leq r<\infty
,

\left\lVert I_{m}(K)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}\leq D_{m}C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{m,r}(K).

(3.16)

Proof.

Apply Theorem
3.5
with the Banach space

B=\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})\quad\text{or}\quad B=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}),

(3.17)

and with coefficient array
b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}=K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}
. It gives

\left\|\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}:g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{m}}:\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}B}\leq D_{m}\left\|\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}g_{i_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots g_{i_{m}}^{(m)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}B}.

(3.18)

The left side is
\|I_{m}(K)\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}B}
by (
3.3
), and the right side is
\|I_{m}^{\mathrm{dec}}(\operatorname{Sym}_{m}K)\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}B}
by (
3.13
). Taking
B=\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
and applying Theorem
2.8
to the decoupled tensor (
3.12
) gives (
3.16
) by the definition of
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{m,r}
. The
B=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
conclusion follows from
\mathfrak{S}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
when the finite-
r
bound is used, or directly from the Banach-valued decoupling inequality when only decoupling is asserted.

∎

3.2
Binary merged Wick products

Let

K_{\mathbf{i}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D},\qquad\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}),

and

L_{\mathbf{j}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E},\qquad\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n}),

be finite coefficient tensors over one standard Gaussian family. A cross pairing is a subset

P\subset[m]\times[n]
such that no two elements of
P
have a common endpoint. The set of all such cross pairings is denoted by
\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}
. Let
R_{K}(P)\subset[m]
and
R_{L}(P)\subset[n]
be the unpaired positions.

Definition 3.7

(Merged contraction)
.

For
P\in\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}
define
L\circ_{P}K
to be the finite operator tensor of order

q(P):=m+n-2|P|

with operator leg
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}
given by

(L\circ_{P}K)_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}:=\sum_{(k_{e})_{e\in P}}L_{\mathbf{j}(\mathbf{b},k)}K_{\mathbf{i}(\mathbf{a},k)}.

(3.19)

Here
\mathbf{a}
records the indices on
R_{K}(P)
,
\mathbf{b}
records the indices on
R_{L}(P)
, and if
e=(\alpha,\beta)\in P
, then

i_{\alpha}=j_{\beta}=k_{e}.

The remaining stochastic legs are ordered by first listing the unpaired
K
-legs and then the unpaired
L
-legs.

Proposition 3.8

(Binary Wick product formula)
.

Let
I_{m}(K)
and
I_{n}(L)
be finite homogeneous Wick-chaos operator kernels with compatible operator legs
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
. Then

I_{n}(L)I_{m}(K)=\sum_{P\in\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}}I_{q(P)}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_{q(P)}(L\circ_{P}K)\bigr).

(3.20)

For
q(P)=0
, the term on the right is the deterministic operator
L\circ_{P}K
. The branch
P=\emptyset
has order
m+n
.

Proof.

Expand
I_{m}(K)
and
I_{n}(L)
in an orthonormal basis. The scalar Wick product formula, equivalently the diagram formula, gives a sum over all cross pairings between the two Wick blocks; see
[
5
,
12
]
. Each paired edge imposes one equality of stochastic indices and contributes one covariance factor. Multiplication of the deterministic coefficients gives the ordered composition
L_{\mathbf{j}}K_{\mathbf{i}}
, hence the coefficient array in each branch is exactly (
3.19
). The unpaired variables form a homogeneous Wick monomial of order
q(P)
, and
I_{q(P)}
only sees the symmetrized kernel by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.9

(Merged-branch estimate)
.

Let
2\leq r<\infty
and
p\geq 2
. Set
C_{0}=D_{0}=1
and, for deterministic branches,

\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{0,r}(M):=\|M\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}.

(3.21)

Then the finite sum below has cardinality depending only on
m
and
n
, and

\left\lVert I_{n}(L)I_{m}(K)\right\rVert_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq\sum_{P\in\mathfrak{M}_{m,n}}D_{q(P)}C_{q(P)}(p+r)^{q(P)/2}\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{dec}}_{q(P),r}(L\circ_{P}K).

(3.22)

Proof.

Apply Proposition
3.8
. Each branch is estimated by Proposition
3.6
and Theorem
2.8
; for
q(P)=0
the assertion is the deterministic operator norm bound.
∎

4
Flattening-norm completion in Hilbert spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{m},\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}
be separable Hilbert spaces. This section records the infinite-dimensional convention used throughout the paper. The order
m
remains fixed, and the passage below concerns the dimensions and ranks of the Hilbert legs. The random operator is first defined for kernels supported on finite-dimensional Hilbert subspaces and then extended by continuity in the maximum norm of the
2^{m}
oriented flattenings. The completed space is therefore the flattening ideal naturally associated with the input-output criterion. Put

\mathcal{H}_{S}:=\bigotimes_{\nu\in S}\mathcal{H}_{\nu},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}:=\mathbb{C},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{S^{c}}:=\bigotimes_{\nu\in[m]\setminus S}\mathcal{H}_{\nu}.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
denote the algebraic tensor subspace of

\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E},

that is, the finite linear span of elementary tensors. Equivalently, each

K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is contained in

H_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{m}\otimes C_{\mathrm{fin}}\otimes E_{\mathrm{fin}}

for some finite-dimensional subspaces
H_{\nu}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
,
C_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{C}
, and
E_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{E}
.
For
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
and
S\subset[m]
,
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)
denotes the Hilbert-space flattening

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K):\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E}.

Theorem 4.1

(Finite-rank Hilbert-space estimate)
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
p\geq 2
, and
2\leq r<\infty
. For every
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
,

\|\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\max_{S\subset[m]}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(4.1)

Consequently the same right-hand side controls

\|\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))}
.

Proof.

The tensor
K
belongs to

H_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{m}\otimes C_{\mathrm{fin}}\otimes E_{\mathrm{fin}}

for finite-dimensional subspaces
H_{\nu}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
,
C_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{C}
, and
E_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{E}
. Theorem
2.8
applied on these finite-dimensional spaces gives (
4.1
). The embeddings
C_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{C}
and
E_{\mathrm{fin}}\subset\mathcal{E}
preserve the corresponding Schatten norms. The operator-norm estimate follows from
\mathfrak{S}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Definition 4.2

(Flattening completion)
.

For
2\leq r<\infty
set

\mathfrak{X}_{S,r}:=\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C},\mathcal{H}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E}),\qquad\mathfrak{X}_{m,r}:=\bigoplus_{S\subset[m]}^{\ell^{\infty}}\mathfrak{X}_{S,r}.

(4.2)

The product norm is

\|(T_{S})_{S\subset[m]}\|_{\mathfrak{X}_{m,r}}:=\max_{S\subset[m]}\|T_{S}\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}.

(4.3)

Define

\Gamma_{m,r}:\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}\to\mathfrak{X}_{m,r},\qquad\Gamma_{m,r}(K):=(\mathcal{F}_{S}(K))_{S\subset[m]},

(4.4)

and

\mathcal{N}_{m,r}:=\ker\Gamma_{m,r}.

(4.5)

On the quotient
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}/\mathcal{N}_{m,r}
set

\|[K]\|_{m,r}:=\|\Gamma_{m,r}(K)\|_{\mathfrak{X}_{m,r}}.

(4.6)

The completed tensor space is

\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{m};\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}):=\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}/\mathcal{N}_{m,r}}^{\,\|\cdot\|_{m,r}}.

(4.7)

The notation
\|\mathbf{K}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}}
denotes the norm induced by this completion. This notation records all
2^{m}
flattenings simultaneously; two finite kernels are identified exactly when their difference has zero image under every oriented flattening in the chosen Schatten class. Equivalently,

\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}\cong\overline{\Gamma_{m,r}(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}})}^{\mathfrak{X}_{m,r}}.

(4.8)

Thus
\mathbf{K}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}
is represented by a sequence
K_{n}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}
satisfying

\max_{S\subset[m]}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K_{n}-K_{\ell})\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\to 0\qquad(n,\ell\to\infty).

(4.9)

Proposition 4.3

(Completed chaos map and measurable representatives)
.

Fix
m\geq 1
,
p\geq 2
, and
2\leq r<\infty
. The finite-rank map

\Phi_{m,p,r}^{\mathrm{fin}}:\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{fin}}/\mathcal{N}_{m,r}\longrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})),\qquad\Phi_{m,p,r}^{\mathrm{fin}}([K])=\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)},

(4.10)

is well-defined and satisfies

\|\Phi_{m,p,r}^{\mathrm{fin}}([K])-\Phi_{m,p,r}^{\mathrm{fin}}([L])\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\|[K]-[L]\|_{m,r}.

(4.11)

Consequently there is a unique continuous extension

\Phi_{m,p,r}:\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{m};\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E}))

(4.12)

with

\|\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})-\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{L})\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\|\mathbf{K}-\mathbf{L}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}}.

(4.13)

If
K_{n}
represents
\mathbf{K}
, then

\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{T}_{K_{n}}^{(m)}\quad\text{in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})).

(4.14)

The limit is independent of the representing sequence.
Moreover, there exists a subsequence
K_{n_{j}}
and a full-measure set
\Omega_{\mathbf{K},p,r}
such that

\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})(\omega)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{T}_{K_{n_{j}}}^{(m)}(\omega)\quad\text{in }\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})\quad(\omega\in\Omega_{\mathbf{K},p,r}).

(4.15)

In particular
\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})
is strongly measurable, and

\omega\longmapsto\|\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})(\omega)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}

(4.16)

is measurable.

Proof.

The Hilbert spaces in this section are separable. Hence
\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
is separable for
r<\infty
, a fact used below when passing from finite-rank random operators to strongly measurable limits. If
[K]=0
, then
\max_{S}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}=0
. Theorem
4.1
gives
\mathcal{T}_{K}^{(m)}=0
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})
; hence (
4.10
) is well-defined. Applying Theorem
4.1
to
K-L
yields (
4.11
). Completeness of
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})
gives the extension and (
4.13
); (
4.14
) follows by definition of completion.

Choose a representative
K_{n}
and pass to a subsequence
K_{n_{j}}
such that

C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\|[K_{n_{j+1}}-K_{n_{j}}]\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}}\leq 2^{-j}.

Then Theorem
4.1
implies

\sum_{j}\|\mathcal{T}_{K_{n_{j+1}}}^{(m)}-\mathcal{T}_{K_{n_{j}}}^{(m)}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}}<\infty.

By Tonelli’s theorem the corresponding pointwise series is finite almost surely, which gives (
4.15
). Each finite-rank chaos map is strongly measurable; since
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
is separable for
r<\infty
, the almost sure limit is strongly measurable. Finally,

|\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}}-\|B\|_{\mathcal{L}}|\leq\|A-B\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq\|A-B\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}

gives (
4.16
).
∎

Proposition 4.4

(Compatibility of completed chaos maps)
.

Fix
m\geq 1
. Let
2\leq r<\infty
and let
p,q\geq 2
. For every
\mathbf{K}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}
,

\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})=\Phi_{m,q,r}(\mathbf{K})\qquad\text{in }L^{0}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})).

(4.17)

More generally, let
2\leq r,s<\infty
,
p,q\geq 2
, and suppose that one finite-rank sequence
(K_{N})_{N}
represents elements
\mathbf{K}_{r}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}
and
\mathbf{K}_{s}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,s}
. Then

\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K}_{r})=\Phi_{m,q,s}(\mathbf{K}_{s})\qquad\text{in }L^{0}(\Omega;\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})).

(4.18)

Thus the index
p
in
\Phi_{m,p,r}
records the target moment space, not a different random operator.

Proof.

Choose a representing sequence
K_{N}
for
\mathbf{K}
. Proposition
4.3
gives convergence of
\mathcal{T}_{K_{N}}^{(m)}
to
\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and to
\Phi_{m,q,r}(\mathbf{K})
in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathfrak{S}_{r})
. Both imply convergence in probability in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
, since
\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}
. Uniqueness of limits in probability gives (
4.17
). The proof of (
4.18
) is identical, using the common finite-rank sequence and the embeddings
\mathfrak{S}_{r},\mathfrak{S}_{s}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Corollary 4.5

(Completed moment and cutoff criteria)
.

Fix
m\geq 1
. Let
p\geq 2
and
2\leq r<\infty
. For every

\mathbf{K}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{m};\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
,

\|\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\|\mathbf{K}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}}.

(4.19)

Consequently,

\|\Phi_{m,p,r}(\mathbf{K})\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}\leq C_{m}(p+r)^{m/2}\|\mathbf{K}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r}}.

(4.20)

Let
\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n}\in\mathfrak{T}_{m,r_{n}}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{H}_{m};\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
, where
2\leq r_{n}<\infty
, and let
p_{n}\geq 2
,
\Lambda_{n}\geq 1
. If

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}^{-p_{n}}<\infty

(4.21)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\|\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r_{n}}}<\infty,

(4.22)

then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\Phi_{m,p_{n},r_{n}}(\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n})(\omega)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}<\infty\qquad\text{for almost every }\omega.

(4.23)

Proof.

The first two estimates are (
4.13
) with
\mathbf{L}=0
and the embedding
\mathfrak{S}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
. Set

X_{n}:=\|\Phi_{m,p_{n},r_{n}}(\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})}.

By (
4.20
),

\|X_{n}\|_{L^{p_{n}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{m}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\|\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r_{n}}}.

Markov’s inequality with

b_{n}:=C_{m}\Lambda_{n}(p_{n}+r_{n})^{m/2}\|\Delta\mathbf{K}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{T}_{m,r_{n}}}

gives
\mathbb{P}(X_{n}>b_{n})\leq\Lambda_{n}^{-p_{n}}
. Borel–Cantelli and (
4.22
) imply (
4.23
).
∎

Proposition 4.6

(Compact approximation in the flattening norm)
.

Let
2\leq r<\infty
and let

K\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{E}

be a Hilbert-tensor element such that
\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{C},\mathcal{H}_{S^{c}}\otimes\mathcal{E})
for every
S\subset[m]
. Let
P_{\nu}^{N}
,
P_{\mathcal{C}}^{N}
, and
P_{\mathcal{E}}^{N}
be finite-rank orthogonal projections converging strongly to the identities on
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
,
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{E}
, respectively. Put

K^{N}:=(P_{1}^{N}\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{m}^{N}\otimes P_{\mathcal{C}}^{N}\otimes P_{\mathcal{E}}^{N})K.

Then

\max_{S\subset[m]}\|\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{N}-K)\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\to 0.

(4.24)

Proof.

For each
S\subset[m]
,

\mathcal{F}_{S}(K^{N})=Q_{S^{c},\mathcal{E}}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)Q_{S,\mathcal{C}}^{N},

where

Q_{S,\mathcal{C}}^{N}=\Bigl(\bigotimes_{\nu\in S}P_{\nu}^{N}\Bigr)\otimes P_{\mathcal{C}}^{N},\qquad Q_{S^{c},\mathcal{E}}^{N}=\Bigl(\bigotimes_{\nu\notin S}P_{\nu}^{N}\Bigr)\otimes P_{\mathcal{E}}^{N}.

If
T\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}(U,V)
and
Q_{U}^{N}\to I_{U}
,
Q_{V}^{N}\to I_{V}
strongly through finite-rank orthogonal projections, then

\|Q_{V}^{N}TQ_{U}^{N}-T\|_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\to 0.

This follows by first approximating
T
in
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
by a finite-rank operator. Applying this to
T=\mathcal{F}_{S}(K)
for all
S\subset[m]
gives (
4.24
).
∎
