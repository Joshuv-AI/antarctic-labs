Introduction

1.1.
Problem setting

In this paper we deal with the decay estimates for minimizers of an energy functional related to phase transition phenomena
with long-range particle interactions. Specifically, we are interested in functionals of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{K}(u;\Omega):=\mathcal{H}_{K}(u,\Omega)+\mathcal{P}(u,\Omega),

where the nonlocal interaction term
\mathcal{H}_{K}
and the potential term
\mathcal{P}
are given, respectively, by

\mathcal{H}_{K}(u,\Omega):=\frac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)^{2}}\lvert u(x)-u(y)\rvert^{2}{K}(x-y)\,dx\,dy

and

(1.2)

\mathcal{P}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}W(u(x))\,dx.

Here,
K
is a positive kernel modeled on that of the fractional Laplacian, while
W
is a double well potential, with wells at
\pm 1
. Also, differently from the classical literature on this topic,
the derivatives of
W
, up to any integer order, are allowed to vanish at
\pm 1
.

More precisely,
{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,+\infty]
is a measurable function satisfying

(K1)

\displaystyle K(x)=K(-x)\qquad\text{for a.~\!e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and

(K2)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{|x|^{n+2s}}\leq K(x)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x|^{n+2s}}\qquad\text{for a.~\!e.}~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for some
s\in(0,1)
and
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
.

We will also assume that
K
satisfies the following
”slow oscillation” assumption:

(K3)

\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{K(\sigma_{j}x)}{K(x)}-1\right)\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{j})^{1-\varepsilon}}\in[-\infty,0],\quad\text{for any}~\sigma_{j}\nearrow 1~\text{and}~\varepsilon\in(0,1)\ .

Moreover, precise asymptotic bounds for these models have a number important consequences,
both in terms of mathematical development of the theory and in view of concrete applications. In particular:

•

These estimates
shed light on the qualitative and quantitative behavior of solutions, since one-dimensional
transition layers often constitute general models also for more complicated
solutions (and, in turn, they can provide general estimates by sliding methods and comparison principles),

•

They help isolate the influence of different terms in the energy functional,
showcasing their impact on the specific features of the transition layers,
thereby offering a clearer understanding of the model,

•

They provide an accurate level of precision, which is especially valuable in physical models, where one typically starts from empirical observations: in this spirit,
when models are obtained via phenomenological considerations rather than via
first principles, the exact knowledge of specific features
of heteroclinic connections
is decisive to validate the model and allows one to reconstruct the specific values
of the parameters of the underlying potential
through the profiles of the observed layers
(that is, the observed data regarding heteroclinic profiles combined
with rigorous asymptotic estimates allows one
to identify reliably the potential, which would be otherwise known only at a qualitative level),

•

Decay estimates for transition layers
play a crucial role in constructing explicit barriers and auxiliary solutions, which in turn can be used to further refine the bounds themselves: a concrete example of this strategy appears in the context of crystal dislocation models, where explicit profiles are used to sharpen the decay estimates of transition layers (see e.g.
[
8

),

•

Sharp asymptotics on hetetoclinic connections are particularly valuable when dealing with numerical implementations of the model, since they provide accurate initial guesses for iterative schemes and improve the stability and convergence of numerical methods,

•

Obtaining optimal bounds on the decay of solutions also
becomes essential when extending the analysis to more complex or generalized framework: in such cases, sharp estimates may represent a natural starting point for conjecturing the behavior of the solutions to the extended problem.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
n=1
, let
s\in(0,1)
and let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Then,

(i)

If
2s<1
,
\alpha\in(2s,1]
and
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
(L_{K}u)^{\prime}(x)=L_{K}u^{\prime}(x)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

If
2s\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(2s-1,1]
and
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
(L_{K}u)^{\prime}(x)=L_{K}u^{\prime}(x)
for any

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
|h|\leq 1
. We define the quantities

\displaystyle I_{1}(h)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle PV\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u(x+h+z)-u(x+h)-u(x+z)+u(x)}{h}\,K(z)\,dz

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad I_{2}(h)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}\frac{u(x+h+z)-u(x+h)-u(x+z)+u(x)}{h}\,K(z)\,dz,

where
PV
denotes the Cauchy principal value.

We firstly focus on the proof of
(i)
and we claim that

(2.1)

\lim_{h\to 0}I_{1}(h)=PV\int_{-1}^{1}\big(u^{\prime}(x+z)-u^{\prime}(x)\big)K(z)\,dz.

To show this, we use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to see that

\displaystyle u(x+h+z)-u(x+z)=h\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(x+z+\tau h)\,d\tau

and

\displaystyle u(x+h)-u(x)=h\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}(x+\tau h)\,d\tau.

On this account, since
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
,

\begin{split}|u(x+h+z)-u(x+h)-u(x+z)+u(x)|&\leq|h|\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(x+z+\tau h)-u^{\prime}(x+\tau h)|\,d\tau\\
&\leq[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{\alpha}|h|.\end{split}

As a result, recalling (
K2
), we obtain that

\left|\frac{u(x+h+z)-u(x+h)-u(x+z)+u(x)}{h}\,K(z)\right|\leq\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{\alpha-1-2s}.

Since the function

z\mapsto\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{\alpha-1-2s}\in L^{1}(-1,1),

we obtain (
2.1
) by using the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

We also have that

(2.2)

\lim_{h\to 0}I_{2}(h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}(u^{\prime}(x+z)-u^{\prime}(x))K(z)\,dz.

Indeed, we observe that

\big|u(x+h+z)-u(x+z)-u(x+h)+u(x)\big|\leq 2|h|\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Therefore, using also (
K2
),

\left|\frac{u(x+h+z)-u(x+h)-u(x+z)+u(x)}{h}\,K(z)\right|\leq 2\Lambda\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{-1-2s}.

Since the function

z\mapsto 2\Lambda\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{-1-2s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)),

we can apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem and obtain (
2.2
).

Now, the limits in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) together yield
(i)
.

To establish
(ii)
, we prove that (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) holds true in this case as well.
For this, we observe that, since
u\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, for some
\alpha\in(2s-1,1]
,

\displaystyle u(x+z+h)-u(x+h)-u^{\prime}(x+h)z=z\int_{0}^{1}\big(u^{\prime}(x+h+\tau z)-u^{\prime}(x+h)\big)\,d\tau

and

\displaystyle u(x+z)-u(x)-u^{\prime}(x)z=z\int_{0}^{1}\big(u^{\prime}(x+\tau z)-u^{\prime}(x)\big)\,d\tau.

As a consequence,

\begin{split}&\big|\big(u(x+z+h)-u(x+h)-u^{\prime}(x+h)z\big)-\big(u(x+z)-u(x)-u^{\prime}(x)z\big)\big|\\
&\qquad\leq|z|\left|\int_{0}^{1}\big(u^{\prime}(x+h+\tau z)-u^{\prime}(x+\tau z)\big)-\big(u^{\prime}(x+h)-u^{\prime}(x)\big)\,d\tau\right|\\
&\qquad\leq|z||h|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\big|u^{\prime\prime}(x+\sigma h+\tau z)-u^{\prime\prime}(x+\sigma h)\big|\,d\sigma\,d\tau\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{[u^{\prime\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}}{\alpha+1}|z|^{1+\alpha}|h|.\end{split}

Therefore, by (
K2
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\big(u(x+z+h)-u(x+h)-u^{\prime}(x+h)z\big)-\big(u(x+z)-u(x)-u^{\prime}(x)z\big)}{h}\,K(z)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{\Lambda[u^{\prime\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}}{\alpha+1}|z|^{\alpha-2s}.

Since
\alpha-2s>-1
, the function

z\mapsto\frac{\Lambda[u^{\prime\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}}{\alpha+1}|z|^{\alpha-2s}\in L^{1}(-1,1),

and
so the Dominated Convergence Theorem yields (
2.1
).

The proof of (
2.2
) is the same as in the setting
(i)
.

Thus, (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), together with the assumption (
K1
),
establish
(ii)
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
n=1
, let
s\in(0,1)
and let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
).
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

(i)

If
2s<1
,
\alpha\in(2s,1]
and
u\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
L_{K}u\in C^{0,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{R})
and

[L_{K}u]_{C^{\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}.

(ii)

If
2s\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(2s-1,1]
and
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
L_{K}u\in C^{0,\alpha+1-2s}(\mathbb{R})
and

[L_{K}u]_{C^{0,\alpha+1-2s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we denote by
C
any positive constant, possibly varying from line to line.

Let
x_{1}
,
x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and set
r:=|x_{1}-x_{2}|
,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle PV\int_{-r}^{r}\big(u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}+z)+u(x_{2})\big)K(z)\,dz

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}\quad I_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}\big(u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}+z)+u(x_{2})\big)K(z)\,dz,

where PV denotes the Cauchy principal value.

We now focus on
(i)
and check that

(2.3)

|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|\leq C\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-2s}.

To show this, we observe that

\displaystyle|u(x_{i}+z)-u(x_{i})|\leq[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{\alpha}\qquad{\mbox{for~$i=1,2$,}}

and

\displaystyle|u(x_{1})-u(x_{2})|\leq[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}.

Then, recalling (
K2
), we gather that

\begin{split}|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|&\leq 2\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{-r}^{r}|z|^{\alpha-1-2s}\,dz+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r,r)}|z|^{-1-2s}\,dz\right)\\
&\leq C\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\left(r^{\alpha-2s}+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}r^{-2s}\right)\\
&\leq C\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha-2s},\end{split}

thus obtaining (
2.3
) and, in turn, the estimate on
[L_{K}u]_{C^{0,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Moreover, an
L^{\infty}
-bound for
L_{K}u
can be obtained by noticing that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|PV\int_{-1}^{1}\big(u(x+z)-u(x)\big)K(z)\,dz\right|\leq\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{-1}^{1}|z|^{\alpha-1-2s}\,dz\leq C\Lambda[u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}\big(u(x+z)-u(x)\big)K(z)\,dz\leq 2\Lambda\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}|z|^{-1-2s}\,dz\leq C\Lambda\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Gathering these pieces of information, we thereby conclude that
L_{K}u\in C^{0,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{R})
, thus completing the proof of
(i)
.

We now focus on the proof of
(ii)
. The regularity of
u
implies that, for
i=1,2
,

(2.4)

u(x_{i}+z)-u(x_{i})=u^{\prime}(x_{i})z+z\int_{0}^{1}\big(u^{\prime}(x_{i}+\tau z)-u^{\prime}(x_{i})\big)\,d\tau.

From this, (
K1
) and (
K2
), we deduce that

(2.5)

|I_{1}|\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{-r}^{r}|z|^{\alpha-2s}\,dz\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1-2s}.

Also, (
2.4
) yields that

\begin{split}\big|u(x_{1}+z)-u(x_{1})-u(x_{2}+z)+u(x_{2})\big|&\leq|z|\,|u^{\prime}(x_{1})-u^{\prime}(x_{2})|+C[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|z|^{1+\alpha}\\
&\leq C[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\left(|z|\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}+|z|^{1+\alpha}\right).\end{split}

This and (
K2
) lead to

(2.6)

|I_{2}|\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha+1-2s}.

The estimate on
[L_{K}u]_{C^{0,\alpha+1-2s}(\mathbb{R})}
thus follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
).

To prove that
L_{K}u
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}
, we recall (
K1
) and (
K2
) and we
use (
2.4
)
and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to obtain that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{-1}^{1}\big(u(x+z)-u(x)-u^{\prime}(x)z\big)K(z)\,dz\right|\leq\Lambda\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|z|^{-2s}|u^{\prime}(x+\tau z)-u^{\prime}(x)|\,d\tau\,dz\\
&\qquad\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{-1}^{1}|z|^{\alpha-2s}\,dz\leq C\Lambda[u^{\prime}]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\end{split}

and

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}\big(u(x+z)-u(x)\big)K(z)\,dz\right|\leq 2\Lambda\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-1,1)}|z|^{-1-2s}\,d\leq 2\Lambda\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus, we have that
L_{K}u\in C^{0,\alpha+1-2s}(\mathbb{R})

and the proof is thereby complete.
∎

As a consequence of Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
n=1
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in(0,1)
and let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Then,

(i)

If
2s<1
,
\alpha\in(2s,1]
and
u\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
L_{K}u\in C^{k,\alpha-2s}(\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

If
2s\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(2s-1,1]
and
u\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, then
L_{K}u\in C^{k-1,\alpha+1-2s}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.4
.

We stress that Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
and Corollary
2.3
only exploit the upper bound in (
K2
).

Also, we point out that the assumption (
K1
) is only used when
2s\geq 1
.

3.
Some useful barriers for
L_{K}

In this section we collect some barrier constructions for the operator
L_{K}
, defined in (
1.3
), in
dimension
n=1
. They will play a pivotal role in the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
). Let also
\bar{x}\geq 1
,
\alpha
,
A>0
,
B
,
D\leq 1-\alpha\bar{x}^{-A}
and

\phi_{\bar{x}}(x):=\begin{cases}B&\mbox{if }x\leq 0,\\
D&\mbox{if }x\in(0,\bar{x}),\\
1-\alpha x^{-A}&\mbox{if }x\geq\bar{x}.\end{cases}

Then, for any
x\geq 2\bar{x}
,

(3.1)

x^{2s}L_{K}\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\leq-\frac{\lambda(1-B)}{2s}+\alpha Cx^{-A}.

for some
C>0
depending on
s
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
x\geq 2\bar{x}
. Thanks to (
K2
),
and applying the change of variable
y:=x\theta
, we compute

(3.2)

\begin{split}&x^{2s}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(\phi_{\bar{x}}(y)-\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy=x^{2s}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left(B-1+\alpha x^{-A}\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
&\qquad\leq-\lambda x^{2s}\left(1-B-\alpha x^{-A}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{dy}{\left(x-y\right)^{1+2s}}\\
&\quad=-\lambda\left(1-B-\alpha x^{-A}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{d\theta}{\left(1-\theta\right)^{1+2s}}=-\frac{\lambda}{2s}\left(1-B\right)+\frac{\alpha\lambda}{2s}x^{-A}.\end{split}

Also, recalling the positivity of the kernel
K
and the fact that
D\leq 1-\alpha\bar{x}^{-A}
, we find that

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{\frac{x}{2}}\left(\phi_{\bar{x}}(y)-\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
&\qquad=\int_{0}^{\bar{x}}\big(D-(1-\alpha x^{-A})\big)K(x-y)\,dy+\alpha\int_{\bar{x}}^{\frac{x}{2}}\big(x^{-A}-y^{-A}\big)K(x-y)\,dy\leq 0.\end{split}

Now, we check that there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
s
and
\Lambda
such that

(3.4)

x^{2s}\left|PV\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{\frac{3x}{2}}\left(\phi_{\bar{x}}(y)-\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\right|\leq\alpha Cx^{-A}.

In the following computations, we will omit the principal value notation for the sake of readability.
In the aim of showing (
3.4
), we use the change of variable
\theta:=1+z
, the symmetry of
K
and a Taylor expansion of
(1+z)^{-A}
around the origin to see that

\begin{split}&\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\big(1-\theta^{-A}\big)K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1-(1+z)^{-A}\big)K(xz)\,dz\\
&\qquad=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(Az+O(|z|^{2})\big)K(xz)\,dz=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}O(|z|^{2})K(xz)\,dz.\end{split}

As a consequence, changing variable
y:=x\theta
and recalling (
K2
),

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{\frac{3x}{2}}\left(\phi_{\bar{x}}(y)-\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\right|=\alpha\left|\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{\frac{3x}{2}}\left(x^{-A}-y^{-A}\right)K(x-y)\,dy\right|\\
&\qquad=\alpha x^{-A+1}\left|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\big(1-\theta^{-A}\big)K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta\right|\leq\alpha Cx^{-A+1}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{2}K(xz)\,dz\\
&\qquad\leq\alpha Cx^{-A-2s}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|z|^{1-2s}\,dz\leq\alpha Cx^{-A-2s}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of (
3.4
).

In addition, exploiting the change of variable
y:=x\theta
and applying (
K2
) one more time we gather

(3.5)

\begin{split}&x^{2s}\int_{\frac{3x}{2}}^{+\infty}\left(\phi_{\bar{x}}(y)-\phi_{\bar{x}}(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy=\alpha x^{2s}\int_{\frac{3x}{2}}^{+\infty}\left(x^{-A}-y^{-A}\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
&\qquad=\alpha x^{2s-A+1}\int_{\frac{3}{2}}^{+\infty}\big(1-\theta^{-A}\big)K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta\leq\alpha\Lambda x^{-A}\int_{\frac{3}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{1-\theta^{-A}}{\left(\theta-1\right)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&\qquad=\alpha Cx^{-A}.\end{split}

Merging together (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain the desired result.
∎

The following statements generalize
[
5
, Propositions 5.1 and 5.2]
to the situation in which there is an inequality in (
3.6
) below rather than equality (also we need to require less regularity for the function
\phi
).

Proposition 3.2
.

let
s\in(0,1)
and let
K
satisfy (
K1
) and (
K2
).
Let
\bar{C}
,
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
and
\sigma
,
\tau\in(1,+\infty)
.

Also, let
\phi\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
be a nonnegative function such that

(3.6)

\phi(x)\leq\begin{cases}\bar{C}|x|^{-\sigma}&\mbox{if }x<-\kappa,\\
\bar{C}|x|^{-\tau}&\mbox{if }x>\kappa\end{cases}

and

(3.7)

\phi(x)\geq\gamma
for all
x\in[-\kappa,\kappa]
, for some
\gamma\in(0,+\infty)
.

In addition, suppose that

(3.8)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{3}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{x}{2},\frac{3x}{2})}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{x\to-\infty}x^{3}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{3x}{2},\frac{x}{2})}=0.

Then,

(3.9)

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}|x|^{1+2s}{L}_{K}\phi(x)\leq\Lambda\left(\frac{\bar{C}\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}+\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy+\frac{\bar{C}\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}\right),

where
\Lambda
is the quantity appearing in (
K2
).

Proof.

We will only prove (
3.9
) for
x\to+\infty
, being the limit as
x\to-\infty
analogous.

Let
x\geq 2\kappa
and define

(3.10)

\begin{split}&A_{K}(x):=\int_{-\infty}^{-\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy,\\
&B_{K}(x):=\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
{\mbox{and }}\qquad&D_{K}(x):=PV_{x}\int_{\kappa}^{+\infty}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy.\end{split}

In the following computations we will omit the principal value notation, for the sake of readability.

We claim that

(3.11)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}A_{K}(x)\leq\frac{\bar{C}\Lambda\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}.

To check this, we exploit (
K2
) and the change of variable
y=x\theta
and obtain that

\begin{split}(\bar{C}\Lambda)^{-1}x^{1+2s}A_{K}(x)&\leq\int_{-\infty}^{-\kappa}\left(|y|^{-\sigma}+x^{-\tau}\right)\left(1-\frac{y}{x}\right)^{-1-2s}\,dy\\
&=x^{1-\sigma}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{\kappa}{x}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+x^{1-\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{\kappa}{x}}\frac{d\theta}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}.\end{split}

We notice that

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{\kappa}{x}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta&=\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{-\frac{\kappa}{x}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{-\frac{\kappa}{x}}\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}\left(1+O(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\left|\theta\right|^{-\sigma}}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+\frac{1}{\sigma-1}\left(\left(\frac{x}{\kappa}\right)^{\sigma-1}-2^{\sigma-1}\right)+O(x^{\sigma-2}).\end{split}

As a result,

\begin{split}(\bar{C}\Lambda)^{-1}x^{1+2s}A_{K}(x)\leq\frac{\kappa^{1-\sigma}}{\sigma-1}+o(1)\end{split}

as
x\to+\infty
, which gives the desired claim in (
3.11
).

Now, we want to show that

(3.12)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)\leq\Lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\phi(y)\,dy.

For this, we take
\gamma
as in (
3.7
)
and we suppose that
x>(\bar{C}/\gamma)^{1/\tau}
.
In this way, we have that
\phi(x)\leq\bar{C}x^{-\tau}<\gamma\leq\phi(y)

for all
y\in(-\kappa,\kappa)
,
thanks to (
3.7
).
Therefore, by (
K2
),

\displaystyle x^{1+2s}B_{K}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{1+2s}\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lambda\int_{-\kappa}^{\kappa}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)\left|1-\frac{y}{x}\right|^{-(1+2s)}\,dy.

We can now take the limit as
x\to+\infty
and use the Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce (
3.12
).

Furthermore, we check that

(3.13)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}D_{K}(x)\leq\frac{\bar{C}\Lambda\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}.

To prove this, we set

(3.14)

\begin{split}&D_{K,I}(x):=\int_{\kappa}^{\frac{x}{2}}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy,\\
&D_{K,II}(x):=\int_{\frac{x}{2}}^{\frac{3x}{2}}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy\\
\mbox{and}\qquad&D_{K,III}(x):=\int_{\frac{3x}{2}}^{+\infty}\left(\phi(y)-\phi(x)\right)K(x-y)\,dy.\end{split}

Now, we rely on (
K2
) and the change of variable
y:=x\theta
and compute

\begin{split}(\bar{C}\Lambda)^{-1}x^{1+2s}D_{K,I}(x)&\leq\int_{\kappa}^{\frac{x}{2}}\left(y^{-\tau}+x^{-\tau}\right)\left(1-\frac{y}{x}\right)^{-(1+2s)}\,dy\\
&=x^{1-\tau}\int_{\frac{\kappa}{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{\left(1-\theta\right)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta+x^{1-\tau}\int_{\frac{\kappa}{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{d\theta}{(1-\theta)^{1+2s}}.\end{split}

Also, we observe that

\int_{\frac{\kappa}{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\theta^{-\tau}}{\left(1-\theta\right)^{1+2s}}\,d\theta=\int_{\frac{\kappa}{x}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{-\tau}\left(1+O(\theta)\right)\,d\theta=\frac{1}{\tau-1}\left(\left(\frac{x}{\kappa}\right)^{\tau-1}-2^{\tau-1}\right)+O(x^{\tau-2})

and we obtain that

(3.15)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}D_{K,I}(x)\leq\frac{\bar{C}\Lambda\kappa^{1-\tau}}{\tau-1}.

Moreover, we recall that
\phi^{\prime}
is Lipschitz continuous and that
\phi^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
As a consequence, applying twice the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we obtain that, for any
\theta\in(1/2,3/2)
,

(3.16)

\begin{split}&\phi(x\theta)-\phi(x)=\int_{x}^{x\theta}\phi^{\prime}(\tau)\,d\tau=\phi^{\prime}(x)x(\theta-1)+\int_{x}^{x\theta}\left(\phi^{\prime}(\tau)-\phi^{\prime}(x)\right)\,d\tau\\
&\qquad=\phi^{\prime}(x)x(\theta-1)+\int_{x}^{x\theta}\int_{x}^{\tau}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\,d\eta\,d\tau.\end{split}

Thus, changing variable
y:=x\theta
, the symmetry of
K
in (
K1
) and the bound on
K
in (
K2
) lead to

\begin{split}x^{1+2s}|D_{K,II}(x)|&=x^{2+2s}\left|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\phi(x\theta)-\phi(x)\right)K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta\right|\\
&=x^{2+2s}\left|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\int_{x}^{x\theta}\int_{x}^{\tau}\phi^{\prime\prime}(\eta)\,d\eta\,d\tau\right)K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta\right|\\
&\leq x^{4+2s}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{x}{2},\frac{3x}{2})}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\theta-1)^{2}K(x(1-\theta))\,d\theta\\
&\leq\Lambda x^{3}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\frac{x}{2},\frac{3x}{2})}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|1-\theta\right|^{1-2s}\,d\theta.\end{split}

Hence, exploiting (
3.8
) we conclude that

(3.17)

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}|D_{K,II}(x)|=0.

In addition, by (
K2
),

\begin{split}x^{1+2s}|D_{K,III}(x)|&\leq\Lambda\int_{\frac{3x}{2}}^{+\infty}\left(\phi(y)+\phi(x)\right)\left(\frac{y}{x}-1\right)^{-(1+2s)}\,dy\\
&\leq 2\bar{C}\Lambda x^{-\tau}\int_{\frac{3x}{2}}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{y}{x}-1\right)^{-(1+2s)}\,dy\\
&=2\bar{C}\Lambda x^{1-\tau}\int_{\frac{3}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{d\theta}{(\theta-1)^{1+2s}}.\end{split}

As a consequence

\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{1+2s}D_{K,III}(x)=0.

Since
D_{K}(x)=D_{K,I}(x)+D_{K,II}(x)+D_{K,III}(x)
, from this and (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) we infer (
3.13
).

Now, being
L_{K}\phi(x)=A_{K}(x)+B_{K}(x)+D_{K}(x)
, combining together (
3.11
) (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) we obtain the desired result.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let
K

x_{1}\geq\bar{x}
such that, for any
|x|\geq x_{1}
,

(4.17)

L_{K}\phi(x)\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{|x|^{1+2s}}.

We now point out that, if
x\leq-\bar{x}
,

\frac{\phi(x)}{|x|^{\frac{2s(\alpha-2)}{\beta-1}}}=\frac{|x|^{-\left(1+\frac{2s(\beta-\alpha+1)}{\beta-1}\right)}}{|x|^{\frac{2s(\alpha-2)}{\beta-1}}}=\frac{1}{|x|^{1+2s}}

and similarly, if
x\geq\bar{x}
,

\frac{\phi(x)}{|x|^{\frac{2s(\gamma-2)}{\delta-1}}}=\frac{|x|^{-\left(1+\frac{2s(\delta-\gamma+1)}{\delta-1}\right)}}{|x|^{\frac{2s(\gamma-2)}{\delta-1}}}=\frac{1}{|x|^{1+2s}}.

From these observations and (
4.17
), we deduce that

(4.18)

L_{K}\phi(x)\leq\begin{cases}\widetilde{C}|x|^{-\frac{2s(\alpha-2)}{\beta-1}}\phi(x)&\mbox{if }x\leq-x_{1},\\
\widetilde{C}|x|^{-\frac{2s(\gamma-2)}{\delta-1}}\phi(x)&\mbox{if }x\geq x_{1}.\end{cases}

Now, we set

\widehat{C}:=\max_{x\in[-x_{1},x_{1}]}\bar{u}^{\prime}{}(x)\left(\min_{x\in[-x_{1},x_{1}]}\phi(x)\right)^{-1}

and we notice that

(4.19)

\widehat{C}\phi(x)-\bar{u}^{\prime}{}(x)\geq 0\quad{\mbox{for any }}|x|\leq x_{1}.

We claim that

(4.20)

\widehat{C}\phi(x)\geq\bar{u}^{\prime}{}(x)\quad\mbox{for any }x\in\mathbb{R}.

In order to prove the claim, we define, for any
b\in[0,+\infty)
, the function
{v_{b}:=\widehat{C}\phi+b-\bar{u}^{\prime}{}}
.
Since
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have that
\bar{u}^{\prime}{}
is bounded. As a consequence, the strict positivity of
\phi
implies that
v_{b}>0
in
\mathbb{R}
for any
b\geq\|\bar{u}^{\prime}{}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Now, if
v_{b}>0
in
\mathbb{R}
for any
b\in[0,+\infty)
, the claim in (
4.20
) plainly follows
by taking
b=0
. Hence, from now on, we suppose that there exists
b_{0}\in(0,\|\bar{u}^{\prime}{}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})

such that
v_{b}>0
for all
b\in(b_{0},+\infty)
and
v_{b_{0}}(z)=0
at some point
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

By the definition of
b_{0}
, there exist points
x_{k}
such that

(4.21)

v_{b_{0}}(x_{k})<\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
x_{k}\geq 0
(otherwise,
in what follows, we use the information coming from the decay at
-\infty
).

Furthermore, the sequence
x_{k}
is bounded from above since, if not, we would have

b_{0}=\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\left(\widehat{C}\phi(x_{k})+b_{0}-\bar{u}^{\prime}{}(x_{k})\right)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}v_{b_{0}}(x_{k})=0,

which is a contradiction.

Moreover, exploiting (
4.19
), we obtain that,
when
|x|\leq x_{1}
and
k>-\log_{2}b_{0}
,

\frac{1}{2^{k}}<b_{0}\leq\widehat{C}\phi(x)-\bar{u}^{\prime}{}(x)+b_{0}=v_{b_{0}}(x).

As a consequence, in light of (
4.21
), we have that
x_{k}\in(x_{1},+\infty)
for any
k
sufficiently large.

Gathering these pieces of information,
we conclude that there exists
x_{\infty}\in(x_{1},+\infty)
such that
x_{k}\to x_{\infty}
as
k\to+\infty
, up to a subsequence.
The continuity of
v_{b_{0}}
and (
4.21
) give that
v_{b_{0}}({x_{\infty}})=0
.

As a result, since
x_{\infty}\geq x_{1}\geq\bar{x}
, we can exploit (
4.13
) and (
4.18
) to compute

\begin{split}L_{K}v_{b_{0}}({x_{\infty}})&=\widehat{C}L_{K}\phi({x_{\infty}})-L_{K}\bar{u}^{\prime}{}({x_{\infty}})\\
&\leq\widetilde{C}|{x_{\infty}}|^{-\frac{2s(\gamma-2)}{\delta-1}}\big(\widehat{C}\phi({x_{\infty}})-\bar{u}^{\prime}{}({x_{\infty}})\big)\\
&=-b_{0}\widetilde{C}|{x_{\infty}}|^{-\frac{2s(\gamma-2)}{\delta-1}}\\
&<0.\end{split}

On the other hand, we have that

L_{K}v_{b_{0}}({x_{\infty}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(v_{b_{0}}(y)-v_{b_{0}}({x_{\infty}}))K({x_{\infty}}-y)\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{b_{0}}(y)K({x_{\infty}}-y)\,dy\geq 0.

We thereby obtain the desired contradiction, which completes the proof of (
4.20
).

Formulas (
4.15
) and (
4.20
) yield the estimates in (
1.14
).

5.
Towards the optimality statements

In this section, we construct a function
\widetilde{u}
that will play a central role in the proof of Theorem
1.5

in Section
6
. Due to its stepwise nature, the construction is somewhat intricate and requires careful treatment. For this reason, we have organized the content into three subsections.

Section
5.1
introduces the preliminary notations and definitions that will be used throughout the rest of the article.
In Section
5.2
, we deal with the actual construction of the function
\widetilde{u}
. Finally, Section
5.3
is devoted to establishing the main properties of
\widetilde{u}
. We point out that some of these properties will be essential in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, while others are used in Appendix
for the “sanity check” on the function

.

5.1.
Notations

In the following, we introduce some notations and definitions that will be used in Sections
5.2
and
5.3
, as well as throughout the rest of the paper.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
\alpha>\beta\geq 2
and
\gamma>\delta\geq 2
such that
\alpha<\beta+1
and
\gamma<\delta+1
. We set

(5.1)

A:=\frac{2s}{\delta-1},\qquad B:=\frac{2s}{\gamma-1},\qquad D:=\frac{2s}{\beta-1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E:=\frac{2s}{\alpha-1}.

We observe that

(5.2)

\frac{A}{B}\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)\leq 2\ln 2\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{D}{E}\ln\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)\leq 2\ln 2.

Also, we consider
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,1],[0,+\infty))
such that
\eta=1
in
[0,1/4]
,
\eta=0
in
[3/4,1]
and
\eta^{\prime}\in(-4,0)
in
(1/4,3/4)
. Moreover, we set

(5.3)

\bar{\eta}:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in[0,1]\\
i\in\{0,1,2,3\}\end{subarray}}\big\{|\eta^{(i)}(x)|\big\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\eta_{0}:=\min\left\{\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}\right);\,1-\eta\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\},

where
\eta^{(i)}
denotes the
i^{th}
-derivative of
\eta
(with the implicit understanding that
\eta^{(0)}:=\eta
).

We stress that
\eta_{0}\leq\eta(\frac{1}{2})\leq\bar{\eta}
, and therefore

(5.4)

\frac{\bar{\eta}}{\eta_{0}}\geq 1.

In addition, for any
x\in[0,1]
we define the functions

(5.5)

i(x):=x^{3}(1-x)^{3}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widetilde{\eta}(x):=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}i(t)\,dt}{{\mathcal{B}}(4,4)},

where
{\mathcal{B}}(\cdot,\,\cdot)
denotes the Euler Beta function.

We point out that

{\mathcal{B}}(4,4)=\int_{0}^{1}i(t)\,dt,

therefore
\widetilde{\eta}
enjoys the properties:
\widetilde{\eta}(0)=1
,
\widetilde{\eta}(1)=0
and
\widetilde{\eta}
is strictly decreasing in
(0,1)
.

Furthermore, we set

a_{0}:=\max\big\{4,e^{\frac{1}{B}}\big\}

and we define recursively in
k\in\mathbb{N}
the sequences

b_{k}:=a_{k}+1,\qquad c_{k}:=b_{k}^{e^{\frac{128\bar{\eta}}{\eta_{0}}}},\qquad d_{k}:=2c_{k}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad a_{k+1}:=2d_{k}.

We observe that, thanks also to (
5.4
),

(5.6)

b_{k}<2b_{k}<\frac{c_{k}}{2}<c_{k}<d_{k}<a_{k+1}<b_{k+1}.

For any
x\in[0,1]
, we consider the functions

(5.7)

w_{1}(x):=B\widetilde{\eta}(x)-(x+1)\widetilde{\eta}^{\prime}(x)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad w_{2}(x):=E\widetilde{\eta}(x)-(x+1)\widetilde{\eta}^{\prime}(x).

Moreover, we define

(5.8)

\bar{x}_{1}:=\frac{3(4-B)+\sqrt{B^{2}-8B+88}}{2(7-B)}-1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bar{x}_{2}:=\frac{3(4-E)+\sqrt{E^{2}-8E+88}}{2(7-E)}-1.

We point out that both
\bar{x}_{1}
and
\bar{x}_{2}
belong to
(0,1)
(see Lemma
A.7
for a proof of this fact).

The choice of the points
\bar{x}_{1}
and
\bar{x}_{2}
seems mysterious at this stage, but it becomes clear if one looks at the first
derivative of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
: as a matter of fact,
\bar{x}_{1}
and
\bar{x}_{2}

are the only critical points of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in the interval
(0,1)
(see formula (
A.29
)),
and this will entail some monotonicity properties for the functions
w_{1}

and
w_{2}
(see Lemma
A.8
).

Now we set, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.9)

C_{2}:=2\max\left\{\frac{1}{B},\left(1-\frac{B}{w_{1}(\bar{x}_{1})}\right)^{-1}\right\},\qquad C_{1,k}:=C_{2}-\frac{BC_{2}-((\bar{x}_{1}+1)c_{k})^{-B(\gamma-\delta)}}{w_{1}(\bar{x}_{1})},

and

(5.10)

C_{4}:=2\max\left\{\frac{1}{E},\left(1-\frac{E}{w_{2}(\bar{x}_{2})}\right)^{-1}\right\},\qquad C_{3,k}:=C_{4}-\frac{EC_{4}-((\bar{x}_{2}+1)c_{k})^{-E(\alpha-\beta)}}{w_{2}(\bar{x}_{2})}.

We provide some properties of the functions
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
and of the constants
C_{2}
,
C_{1,k}
,
C_{4}
and
C_{3,k}
in Appendix
A
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the quantities

(5.11)

\zeta:=\ln\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln b_{k}}\right)\left(\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)\right)^{-1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\xi:=\ln\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln b_{k}}\right)\left(\ln\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)\right)^{-1}.

From the inequalities in (
5.2
) we deduce that

(5.12)

\zeta=\frac{128\bar{\eta}}{\eta_{0}}\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)^{-1}\geq\frac{32A\bar{\eta}}{B\eta_{0}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\xi=\frac{128\bar{\eta}}{\eta_{0}}\ln\left(\frac{D}{E}\right)^{-1}\geq\frac{32D\bar{\eta}}{E\eta_{0}}.

We also define on the interval
[0,1]
the functions

(5.13)

\phi_{k}(x):=A\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln(x(c_{k}-b_{k})+b_{k})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\zeta}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\psi_{k}(x):=D\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln(x(c_{k}-b_{k})+b_{k})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\xi}}.

We notice that
\phi_{k}(0)=A
and

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(1)=A\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln c_{k}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\zeta}}=A\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln c_{k}}\right)^{\frac{\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)}{\ln\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln b_{k}}\right)}}=A\exp\left(\frac{\ln\left(\frac{A}{B}\right)}{\ln\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln b_{k}}\right)}\ln\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln c_{k}}\right)\right)=B.

Moreover,
\phi_{k}
is decreasing, whence

(5.14)

\phi_{k}\in[B,A].

Similarly,
we have that
\psi_{k}(0)=D
,
\psi_{k}(1)=E
,
\psi_{k}
is decreasing and so
\psi_{k}\in[E,D]
.

We stress that, exploiting the ODE result in Lemma
B.1
, applied here with
a:=b_{k}
,
b:=c_{k}
,
\mu:=\zeta
, and
f_{0}:=A
for
\phi_{k}
, and also with
a:=b_{k}
,
b:=c_{k}
,
\mu:=\xi
, and
f_{0}:=D
for
\psi_{k}
,
we find that, for any
x\in[b_{k},c_{k}]
,

(5.15)

\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)=-\frac{\zeta}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\phi^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)x\ln x.

and

(5.16)

\psi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)=-\frac{\xi}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\psi^{\prime}_{k}\left(\frac{x-c_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)x\ln x.

Furthermore, we point out that

(5.17)

\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)=A\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln x}\right)^{\frac{1}{\zeta}}=B\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln x}\right)^{\frac{1}{\zeta}}\quad\mbox{for any }x\in[b_{k},c_{k}],

and

\psi_{k}\left(\frac{|x|-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)=D\left(\frac{\ln b_{k}}{\ln|x|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\xi}}=E\left(\frac{\ln c_{k}}{\ln|x|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\xi}}\quad\mbox{for any }x\in[-c_{k},-b_{k}].

Finally, we define the function

(5.18)

u_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}1-C_{1,k}x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}&\mbox{if }x\in[b_{k},c_{k}],\\
-1+C_{3,k}|x|^{-\psi_{k}\left(\frac{|x|-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}&\mbox{if }x\in[-c_{k},-b_{k}].\end{cases}

Some useful bounds on the quantities introduced in this section and the main properties of the function
u_{k}

are discussed in Appendices
A
and
C
, respectively.
In particular, formulas (
5.15
) and (
5.16
)
are pivotal in establishing the monotonicity properties of
u_{k}
.

5.2.
The function
\widetilde{u}

In this section we provide the definition of the function
\widetilde{u}
, that will serve as a main ingredient to prove Theorem
1.5
.
We will use the notation introduced in Section
5.1
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\widetilde{u}_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{b_{k}}\right)C_{1,k}\left(x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}-x^{-A}\right)+u_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[b_{k},2b_{k}],\\
u_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[2b_{k},c_{k}/2],\\
\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{2x-c_{k}}{c_{k}}\right)C_{1,k}\left(x^{-B}-x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}\right)+1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}&\mbox{if }x\in[c_{k}/2,c_{k}],\\
\displaystyle 1-C_{2}x^{-B}+(C_{2}-C_{1,k})\widetilde{\eta}\left(\frac{x-c_{k}}{c_{k}}\right)x^{-B}&\mbox{if }x\in[c_{k},d_{k}],\\
\displaystyle 1-C_{1,k+1}x^{-A}-\eta\left(\frac{x-d_{k}}{d_{k}}\right)\left(C_{2}x^{-B}-C_{1,k+1}x^{-A}\right)&\mbox{if }x\in[d_{k},a_{k+1}].\end{cases}

We stress that we have made use of the inequalities in (
5.6
).

The function
\widetilde{u}_{k}
transitions from
1-C_{1,k}x^{-A}
to
1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}
over the interval
[b_{k},c_{k}]
via the intermediate function
u_{k}
, given in (
5.18
). It then proceeds to join
1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}
with
1-C_{2}x^{-B}
on
[c_{k},d_{k}]
, and finally interpolates between
1-C_{2}x^{-B}
and
1-C_{1,k+1}x^{-A}
on
[d_{k},a_{k+1}]
. All these transitions are smooth thanks to the use of the cutoff functions
\eta
and
\widetilde{\eta}
. In particular, recalling the definition of
\widetilde{\eta}
in (
5.5
), we obtain that the resulting function is of class
C^{3,1}
.

In the same spirit, we define
\widetilde{u}_{k}
for negative values of
x
as follows:

\widetilde{u}_{k}(x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-1+C_{3,k+1}|x|^{-D}-\eta\left(\frac{|x|-d_{k}}{d_{k}}\right)\left(C_{3,k+1}|x|^{-D}-C_{4}|x|^{-E}\right)&\mbox{if }x\in[-a_{k+1},-d_{k}],\\
\displaystyle-1+C_{4}|x|^{-E}-(C_{4}-C_{3,k})\widetilde{\eta}\left(\frac{|x|-c_{k}}{c_{k}}\right)|x|^{-E}&\mbox{if }x\in[-d_{k},-c_{k}],\\
\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{2|x|-c_{k}}{c_{k}}\right)C_{3,k}\left(|x|^{-\psi_{k}\left(\frac{|x|-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}-|x|^{-E}\right)-1+C_{3,k}|x|^{-E}&\mbox{if }x\in[-c_{k},-c_{k}/2],\\
u_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[-c_{k}/2,-2b_{k}],\\
\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{|x|-b_{k}}{b_{k}}\right)C_{3,k}\left(|x|^{-D}-|x|^{-\psi_{k}\left(\frac{|x|-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}\right)+u_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[-2b_{k},-b_{k}].\end{cases}

Hence,
\widetilde{u}_{k}
goes from
1-C_{3,k+1}|x|^{-D}
to
1-C_{4}|x|^{-E}
on the interval
[-a_{k+1},-d_{k}]
, next it connects
1-C_{4}|x|^{-E}
with
1-C_{3,k}|x|^{-E}
across
[-d_{k},-c_{k}]
. Finally, it brings the function back from
1-C_{3,k}|x|^{-E}
to
1-C_{3,k}|x|^{-D}
over
[-c_{k},-b_{k}]
, using
u_{k}
to handle the transition. Moreover, also these transitions are
C^{3,1}
thanks to the cutoff functions
\eta
and
\widetilde{\eta}
.

Having introduced all the required notation, we now present the final expression of our function
\widetilde{u}:\mathbb{R}\to[-1,1]
. Namely,

(5.19)

\widetilde{u}
is a
C^{3,1}(\mathbb{R})
function such that
\widetilde{u}^{\prime}
is strictly positive in
(-a_{0},a_{0})

and, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.20)

\widetilde{u}(x)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[-a_{k+1},-b_{k}],\\
-1+C_{3,k}|x|^{-D}&\mbox{if }x\in[-b_{k},-a_{k}],\\
1-C_{1,k}x^{-A}&\mbox{if }x\in[a_{k},b_{k}],\\
\widetilde{u}_{k}(x)&\mbox{if }x\in[b_{k},a_{k+1}].\end{cases}

5.3.
Properties of
\widetilde{u}

This section studies the main properties of the function
\widetilde{u}
introduced in formula (

).
More precisely, Propositions
5.1
and
5.2
below establish bounds for
\widetilde{u}
and its first derivatives and Proposition
5.5
provides upper and lower estimates for
L_{K}\widetilde{u}^{\prime}
and
L_{K}\widetilde{u}^{\prime\prime}
for large values of

.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exist two positive constants
\widetilde{C}
and
\widehat{C}
, depending on

and

, such that, for any

.

\widetilde{C}
and
\widehat{C}
, depending at most from

and

, such that, for any

.

.

Proof of point (i) of Proposition

.

In the interval

it holds that

Also, by Lemma

, from

follows.

Accordingly,

\begin{split}&\widetilde{u}(x)=\widetilde{u}_{k}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{b_{k}}\right)C_{1,k}\left(x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}-x^{-A}\right)+u_{k}(x)\\

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x)\leq C_{1,k}\left(x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}-x^{-A}\right)+1-C_{1,k}x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}=1-C_{1,k}x^{-A}\leq 1-2x^{-A}.

From this and (

Similarly, we check that

\mbox{the estimates in~{(i)} hold true for any}\ x\in[c_{k}/2,c_{k}].

\widetilde{u}(x)=\widetilde{u}_{k}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{2x-c_{k}}{c_{k}}\right)C_{1,k}\left(x^{-B}-x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}\right)+1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}.

Therefore, by the fact that

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x)\leq C_{1,k}\left(x^{-B}-x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}\right)+1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}=1-C_{1,k}x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}\leq 1-2x^{-\phi_{k}\left(\frac{x-b_{k}}{c_{k}-b_{k}}\right)}.

Moreover, form (

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(x)\geq 1-C_{1,k}x^{-B}.

From these considerations we conclude that (

Lemma A.1
.

Let

,
\gamma\geq\delta\geq 2
and

(A.1)

-1-2s\frac{\left(1-(\gamma-2)(\gamma-\delta)\right)}{\gamma-1}\geq-1-2s\frac{(\delta-\gamma+1)}{\delta-1}.

In particular, the equality occurs only when either
\gamma=\delta
or
\delta=2

Also,

(A.2)

-1-2s\frac{\left(1-(\alpha-2)(\alpha-\beta)\right)}{\alpha-1}\geq-1-2s\frac{(\beta-\alpha+1)}{\beta-1},

with the equality only when either
\alpha=\beta
or

Proof.

We focus on the proof of (

), since the one of (
A.2
) is similar.

The case
\gamma=\delta
is trivial and equality holds in (
A.1
). In the case
\delta=2

) is satisfied with equality as well, since

\begin{split}&-2s\frac{\left(1-(\gamma-2)(\gamma-\delta)\right)}{\gamma-1}=-\frac{2s}{\gamma-1}\left(1-(\gamma-2)^{2}\right)=-\frac{2s}{\gamma-1}(\gamma-1)(3-\gamma)\\
&\qquad\qquad=-2s\left(3-\gamma\right)=-2s\frac{(\delta-\gamma+1)}{\delta-1}.\end{split}

We now suppose that
\gamma>\delta>2
and we claim that

(A.3)

(\gamma-1)(\delta-\gamma+1)>(\delta-1)\left(1-(\gamma-\delta)(\gamma-2)\right).

In order to prove the claim, for any

f_{\delta}(x):=(x-1)(\delta-x+1)-(\delta-1)\left(1-(x-\delta)(x-2)\right).

In particular,

f_{\delta}(x)=\left(\delta-2\right)\left(x^{2}-x(\delta+2)+2\delta\right).

The function
f_{\delta}
possesses two zeros,
x=2
and
x=\delta
.
Moreover,
f_{\delta}>0
in
(\delta,+\infty)
.
Consequently, recalling that
\gamma>\delta
, (
A.3
) follows.

From (
A.3
) we obtain (
A.1

∎

Appendix B
A pivotal ordinary differential equation

In this section, we prove an existence and uniqueness result for an ODE that appears in Section
5
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
b>a>1
. Then, the ODE problem

(B.1)

\begin{cases}f^{\prime}(x)=-\displaystyle\frac{f(x)(b-a)}{\mu(x(b-a)+a)\ln\left(x(b-a)+a\right)}&\mbox{ for all }x\in(0,1),\\
f(0)=f_{0}\end{cases}

admits a unique solution given by

(B.2)

f(x)=\left(\frac{\ln a}{\ln(x(b-a)+a)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu}}f_{0}.

Proof.

We consider
x\in(0,1]
and integrate both sides of the equation in (
B.1
), obtaining that

\int_{0}^{x}\frac{f^{\prime}(y)}{f(y)}\,dy=-\frac{b-a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dy}{(y(b-a)+a)\ln(y(b-a)+a)}.

We change variables
z:=f(y)
in the left-hand side and
z:=y(b-a)+a
in the right-hand side. In this way, we get

\int_{f_{0}}^{f(x)}\frac{dz}{z}=-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{a}^{x(b-a)+a}\frac{dz}{z\ln z}.

Computing the integrals we thereby find that

\ln\left(\frac{f(x)}{f_{0}}\right)=-\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\ln(\ln(x(b-a)+a))-\ln(\ln a)\right),

which entails (
B.2
).

We are left to prove the uniqueness of the solution. For this, we consider the set

D:=[0,1]\times[f_{0}-1,\,f_{0}+1]

and the function
{g:D\to\mathbb{R}}
defined as

g(x,t):=\displaystyle\frac{t(b-a)}{\mu(x(b-a)+a)\ln(x(b-a)+a)}.

Since
g
is continuous in
x
and Lipschitz continuous in
t
with Lipschitz constant

L:=\frac{b-a}{|\mu|a\ln a},

the theory of ODEs yields the desired result.
∎

Appendix D
Smooth joining of two functions

We present two technical results concerning the smooth joining of two functions. They are used in the proof of Proposition
5.5
.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
a
,
b\in(0,+\infty)
such that
b\geq 2a
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Also, let
\theta\in C^{3}(0,1)
.

Moreover, we consider two functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
such that, for some
\overline{C}>0
and for any
x\in(a,b)
,

(D.1)

|f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)|\leq\overline{C}|x|^{-\varepsilon}

and

(D.2)

\max\big\{|f_{1}^{(i)}(x)|,|f_{2}^{(i)}(x)|\big\}\leq\overline{C}x^{-i-\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{ for any}\quad i=1,2,3.

Then, the function

h(x):=\theta\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)f_{1}(x)+\left(1-\theta\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)\right)f_{2}(x)

satisfies

(D.3)

|h^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)|\leq C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in(0,1)\\
i=0,1,2,3\end{subarray}}|\theta^{(i)}(x)|x^{-3-\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{for any }x\in(a,b),

for some
C>0
.

Proof.

For the sake of readability, we set

\bar{\theta}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in(0,1)\\
i=0,1,2,3\end{subarray}}|\theta^{(i)}(x)|.

We point out that if
\bar{\theta}=+\infty
then the claim in (
D.3
) is obvious, hence from now on we assume that
\bar{\theta}\in(0,+\infty)
.

We set

\begin{split}I(x)&:=\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)\frac{f_{1}(x)-f_{2}(x)}{(b-a)^{3}},\\
II(x)&:=3\theta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)\frac{f^{\prime}_{1}(x)-f^{\prime}_{2}(x)}{(b-a)^{2}},\\
III(x)&:=3\theta^{\prime}\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{1}(x)-f^{\prime\prime}_{2}(x)}{b-a}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad IV(x)&:=\theta\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}\right)\big(f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}(x)-f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}(x)\big)\end{split}

and we observe that

(D.4)

h^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)=I(x)+II(x)+III(x)+IV(x)+f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}(x).

Since
b\geq 2a
, we have that

(D.5)

2(b-a)\geq x\quad\mbox{for any }x\in(a,b).

Exploiting (
D.1
) and (
D.5
) we have that

|I(x)|\leq\frac{\overline{C}\bar{\theta}x^{-\varepsilon}}{(b-a)^{3}}\leq C\bar{\theta}x^{-\varepsilon-3}.

Similarly, using (
D.2
) and (
D.5
) we obtain

|II(x)|+|III(x)|+|IV(x)|+|f^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}(x)|\leq C\bar{\theta}x^{-\varepsilon-3}.

Gathering these observations and recalling (
D.4
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎
