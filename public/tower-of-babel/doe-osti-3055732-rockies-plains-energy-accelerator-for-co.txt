Executive Summary
This document serves as the final report for the Rockies/Plains Energy Accelerator for Commercializing
Hard-Tech (REACH), a U.S. Department of Energy (USDOE)-funded energy hard tech accelerator jointly
led by Colorado State University and Innosphere Ventures. The objective of the project was to grow the
Rocky Mountain Great Plains (RMGP) energy innovation cluster capacity by: a)developing and delivering
an accelerator program tailored to the specific needs of energy hard-tech companies and their founders;
b) providing technical and business development support to energy hard-tech start-ups and their
founders; c) building region-wide connectivity, engagement, and innovation capacity across the multi-
state region through periodic virtual and in-person events and programs for a wide range of
stakeholders; d) infusing a culture of diversity, equity, and inclusion into all aspects of programming; e)

working with and learning from EPIC program co-recipients; and f) securing follow-on funding to support
the continuation of the above activities in outyears.

Over the 2-year period of performance (FY 2023 & FY2024), the REACH project team supported 27 early-
stage startups with traditional business support (multiple workshops and year-long mentorship and
coaching) and highly customized technical support (technoeconomic and carbon lifecycle analysis,
independent testing and evaluation, and rapid prototyping support). All 27 teams formed companies
and raised a total of $20.6 million in capital ($5M in grants and $15.6M in equity). Affiliated startups
experienced revenue growth of $3,904,000, created 33 new jobs, and generated 11 new patents. 22 of
the 27 companies receiving corporate traction by end of program by launching several new products by
developing 11 new prototypes; 2 MVPs; 10 commercialized products; and completing 8
demonstration/pilot projects.

Among the 13 startups in the 2022 cohort:
 • 38% included Black, Indigenous and People of Color (BIPOC) founders;
 • 46% included women founders;
 • 38% are developing technologies benefitting disadvantaged communities;
 • 3 were located in rural areas; and
 • 1 included a veteran founder.

Among the 14 companies in the 2023 cohort:
 • 64% included BIPOC founders;
 • 35% included women founders;
 • 35% are developing technologies benefitting disadvantaged communities;
 • 1 includes a veteran founder; and
 • 1 includes founders affected by low socioeconomic means.

New insights developed in REACH include:

 a) food, energy, and water systems are highly integrated in the Rockies/Plains region and tailored
 programming to meet the needs of founders working in this space is imperative to their success;
 b) most research universities in the Rockies/Plains region lacked the resources to provide the
 programming provided by REACH but are ready, willing, and able to participate in periodic
 region-wide meetings and refer high-quality startups into regional programs like REACH;
 c) developing partnerships with native/indigenous and minority serving institutions is an important
 element of successful regional accelerator programing. Listening, long-term relationship-
 building and dedicated resources are all keys to success.
 d) most energy hardtech startups in our region need 3rd-party validation of their technology
 (through technoeconomic and/or carbon lifecycle analyses) prior to widespread corporate
 traction.
 e) rapid prototyping support for startups is best performed by organizations residing in close
 physical proximity to startups.
 f) mock board meetings provided by regional accelerators for pre-revenue startups help prepare
 startups for investor engagement and board formation.

The effectiveness and economic feasibility of future programs patterned after REACH was validated, as
evidenced by the number of universities participating in the program, the number of referrals received

from affiliated universities, and the rapid success of REACH-affiliated startup companies. In addition to
direct results of the program, follow-on funding was acquired to sustain regional programming through
the acquisition of other federal funding including the National Science Foundation-funded Colorado –
Wyoming Climate Resilience Engine ($15M over 2 years), the USDOE-funded Rockies/Plains Food Energy
Water Accelerator ($650K over 3 years), and the CSU System-funded Spur Climate Hub ($2.6M over 5
years).

For the above reasons, the broader impact and benefits of REACH go well beyond direct follow-on
funding and associated activities. The program has tangibly improved the Rockies/Plains regional
climate-tech innovation ecosystem and positioned it for success well into the future.

Project Activities
The overall scope of this project was the design, development, and delivery of a Rockies/Plains regional
accelerator for founding teams to aid them in the commercialization and scale of new technologies. The
accelerator was to be highly tailored to the needs of start-up companies in the energy hard-tech space
and includes traditional training using virtual programming in a cohort-based model and access to
technical and business development services tailored to the specific needs on individual companies. It
also included activities designed to aid local innovation ecosystems build capacity through: a) regular
seminars, events, and annual visits by the CSU/Innosphere project team, b) engagement with other EPIC
recipients to exchange ideas and incorporate best-practices, and c) efforts to strengthen diversity,
equity, and inclusion. Our approach was to leverage knowledge gained and processes developed as a
part of an award-winning energy hard-tech innovation model developed by CSU and Innosphere and
catalyze and support other local energy innovation ecosystems in the Rockies/Plains region. This was to
be achieved through: a) specialized accelerator programming for teams emerging from local ecosystems
and b) regular region-wide meetings and other engagement activities that support capacity-building
within each local ecosystem. All 27 teams supported by REACH formed companies and raised a total of
$20.6 million in capital ($5M in grants and $15.6M in equity). Affiliated startups experienced revenue
growth of $3,904,000, created 33 new jobs, and generated 11 new patents. 22 of the 27 companies
receiving corporate traction by end of program by launching several new products by developing 11 new
prototypes; 2 MVPs; 10 commercialized products; and completing 8 demonstration/pilot projects.

There were no major changes in the project scope, schedule, and/or budget aside from a 6-month no-
cost extension for the purposes of finalizing a few technical activities, paying Innosphere’s last invoice,
writing this final report, and closing out the project.

Lessons Learned
Lessons-learned and best-practices through REACH include:

 - regional partnerships between a regional accelerator and several research universities can
 result in a large cohort of high-quality, rapid-growth startups;
 - regional research universities vary widely in their internal capabilities and services available to
 affiliated startups. For this reason, in addition to offering traditional acceleration services to
 early-stage founders, a consortium of universities in a region would also benefit from upstream
 energy-focused customer discovery programs for faculty and students exploring the creation of
 new startup companies;
 - virtual accelerator workshops are best limited to no more than ½ day sessions inclusive of
 multiple 15-minute breaks to avoid “virtual meeting fatigue”;

 - most energy hardtech startups benefit from third-party technoeconomic and/or carbon
 lifecycle analyses to help attract corporate partners, large-scale demonstrations and/or early
 investors;
 - energy hardtech founders in the Rockies/Plains region often need advice and support from
 subject matter experts on topics related to co-production of food and/or water use.
 - energy hardtech startups involved in regional acceleration programs should provide a modest
 level of direct support or “skin in the game” to ensure optimal engagement and overall
 performance in cohort-based acceleration programs.
 - most early-stage energy hardtech startups would benefit from other training services and
 support related to topics such as SBIR proposal preparation guidance and networking
 opportunities with relevant federal agencies.
