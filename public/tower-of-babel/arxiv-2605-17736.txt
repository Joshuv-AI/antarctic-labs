Rank Varieties over the Generic Hypersurface I
David A. Jorgensen

Abstract
. To every local complete intersection ring one may associate a so-called generic hypersurface. In this paper we introduce rank varieties for modules and complexes over the generic hypersurface. The definition uses extension of scalars, rather than restriction of scalars which are used to define the conventional support varieties over a local complete intersection. We show that every projective variety can be realized as the rank variety of a finitely generated module over the generic hypersurface. We also investigate several properties of these rank varieties.

Introduction

There has been significant interest in rank and support varieties for cohomology of modules or complexes over various rings — for example, commutative local complete intersection rings — no doubt due to the elegant and useful connection between homological module theory and geometry (see

showed that these two notions of variety, although defined quite differently, agree. Avramov in

defines support varieties now for cohomology of modules over commutative local complete intersection rings (of which the aforementioned rings studied by Carlson are a special case) and Avramov and Buchweitz in
[

]
extend the notion of support varieties to cohomology of pairs of modules over the same type of rings. In that paper they also define rank varieties (without calling them so) and prove they coincide with the support varieties
[
2
, Theorem 2.5]
. Steele defines rank and support varieties for cohomology of totally acyclic complexes in
[
27
]
and shows that these varieties also coincide.
There are also now quite general and sophisticated notions of support, see
[
9
]

Although equivalent when both defined, an argument can be made that rank varieties are more concrete and easier to compute than support varieties.

To every local complete intersection ring one may associate a so-called generic hypersurface ring. Generic hypersurfaces were used in
[
2
, Section 3]
and
[
19
]
to study support varieties over the associated complete intersection. The point of this paper is to introduce and study a notion of rank variety over the generic hypersurface itself. The reason for this is the last sentence of the previous paragraph and the remarkable result of Orlov
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
that proves the singularity category of the local complete intersection is equivalent to that of the generic hypersurface. Buchweitz
[
7
]
shows that singularity category is equivalent to the category of totally acyclic complexes (straightforward modifications yield the equivalence in the graded setting). Therefore we focus our study on rank varieties of graded totally acyclic complexes over the generic hypersurface. In a subsequent paper we study the correlation between the conventional support varieties of totally acyclic complexes over a local complete intersection and rank varieties of graded totally acyclic complexes over the generic hypersurface via these equivalences. All previous treatments of rank varieties involve restriction of scalars. Our approach instead uses extension of scalars. It turns out this framework leads to much simpler proofs of closure and an affirmative answer to the realizability question: is every variety the rank variety of some complex or module? Another related area of interest is matrix factorizations (see
[

[

We next describe briefly the framework of rank varieties for commutative local complete intersections.
Recall that a local complete intersection ring

S
is the quotient of a commutative regular local ring

f_{1},\dots,f_{c}
. Without loss of generality, one may assume that each

lies in the square of the unique maximal ideal

of

. In this case we say that

has codimension

and if

denotes the residue field of

, then one can associate to
S
, or, rather

, the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
of dimension

according to

a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{c}f_{c}\in I\leftrightarrow\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{c})\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}

where
a_{i}\in Q
is a preimage of
\alpha_{i}\in k
for each

. Note that at least one

must be a unit in

, so that

is always a minimal generator of

. In this case we call

a permissible hypersurface. Also note that distinct elements of

may correspond to the same element in

. Specifically,

and

correspond to the same element

if and only if
a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}
for

. As we shall see, this nonunique correspondence causes no problems in the theory.

The previous considerations of rank varieties use certain ring maps

, and parameterize by
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
those
R_{\alpha}
over which a given
S
-module or complex has infinite projective dimension or is nonzero. Thus these notions of rank variety are defined through restriction of scalars via the maps
R_{\alpha}\to S
. In the group algebra case the rings
R_{\alpha}
are principle subalgebras of
S
, with the maps being inclusion. In the local complete intersection case the rings
R_{\alpha}
are the permissible hypersurfaces
Q/(a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{c}f_{c})
and the maps

R_{\alpha}\to S
are the natural surjections.

In this paper we instead consider a rank variety defined through extension of scalars via the natural
maps

R\to R_{\alpha}

where
R
is the generic hypersurface

R=Q[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]/(f_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+f_{c}x_{c})

and again parameterize by
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
those permissible hypersurface rings

R_{\alpha}=Q/(a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{c}f_{c})
over which a given
R
-module or complex has infinite projective dimension or is nonzero after tensoring with
R_{\alpha}
.

In Section 3 we define this version of rank variety for graded totally acyclic complexes over the generic hypersurface
R
. We show in

3.4
below that our notion of rank variety is well-defined, that is, it is independent of the choice of preimages
a_{i}
of
\alpha_{i}
. We also show it is well-defined up to homotopy equivalence,

3.5
. We then show it is a Zariski closed set

and give in
3.8
several properties of rank varieties analogous to those defined via restriction of scalars. We show in Section 4 that every projective variety in
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
is the rank variety of some graded totally acyclic
R

3.9
holds in our context. Contrarily, Carlson’s connectedness theorem does not hold in our context: we show by example in Section 5 that disconnectedness of the rank variety does not necessarilly imply decomposability of the object.

In Section
6
we restrict out attention graded modules over the generic hypersurface
R
and establish the same properties for rank varieties of modules. Section 2 consists of preliminary results on complexes in general and graded totally acyclic complexes in particular.

Contractibility

In this subsection we give the definition of contractibility and some properties we use later.

Definition 2.2
.

A complex
C
of finitely generated free
R
-modules is
contractible at
n\in\mathbb{Z}
if there exist maps
s_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{n+1}
and
s_{n-1}:C_{n-1}\to C_{n}
such that

\partial^{C}_{n+1}s_{n}+s_{n-1}\partial^{C}_{n}
is an isomorphism. In this case we say that
s_{n}
and
s_{n-1}
is a
contraction
at
n
. The complex
C
is
contractible
if there exist maps

s_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{n+1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
s_{n}
and
s_{n-1}
is a contraction at
n
for all

n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Typically one defines
C
to be contractible if the identity map
\operatorname{Id}\nolimits_{C}
is
nullhomotopic, but this is equivalent to our definition. Indeed, if

f_{n}=\partial^{C}_{n+1}s_{n}+s_{n-1}\partial^{C}_{n}
is an isomorphism for all
n
, then the maps

s_{n}f_{n}^{-1}
form a homotopy showing that
\operatorname{Id}\nolimits_{C}
is nullhomotopic.

2.4
.

Recall that complexes
C
and
D
are homotopically equivalent if there exist chain maps

f:C\to D
and
g:D\to C
such that
gf\sim 1_{C}
and
fg\sim 1_{D}
. If
C
and
D
are homotopically equivalent and
C
is contractible then
D
is also contractible. Indeed, by
2.1

1_{C}\sim 0
implies
f\sim 0
which in turn implies
1_{D}\sim fg\sim 0
.

Vector spaces

Recall that we say a map

The rank-nullity Theorem from linear algebra gives the following.

2.5
.

Consider a complex

(1)

is exact at

(2)

Integral domains

Assume that

is an integral domain. Then

is a multiplicatively closed set and each map of finitely generated

. If

For a map

Local rings and minimality

We recall the definition of minimal complex and state some properties.

2.7
.

Assume that
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)

(1)

Let

(2)

Let

Assume that

We now show that every

Assume that

is a local ring and let

For each

We let

We will often abuse terminology and call morphisms in

Recall (see, for example,

Assume that

The ‘if’ direction is trivial.

For the ‘only if’ direction, let

3.
The generic hypersurface and rank varieties

Let
Q
be a regular local ring with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{n}}
and residue
field
k
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{c}
be a regular sequence in
Q
with
c\geq 2
.
Throughout the remainder of this paper we let
P
denote the polynomial ring
Q[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]

in the commuting indeterminates
x_{1},\dots,x_{c}
. Setting the degree of each
x_{i}
to be 1, we realize
P
as a standard graded ring with
Q
concentrated in degree 0. Notice that since
Q
is a unique factorization domain, so is
P
. Thoughout the paper we consider the generic hypersurface
R
, which is defined to be the quotient of
P

R=P/(w)

by the principal ideal generated by
w=f_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+f_{c}x_{c}
. We claim that
w
is irreducible in

P
, for if
w=ab
for
a,b\in P
, then by degree considerations we must have, without loss of generality,

a\in Q
and
b=b_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+b_{c}x_{c}
with
b_{i}\in Q
for
i=1,\dots,c
. Equating coefficients yields

f_{1}=ab_{1}
and
f_{2}=ab_{2}
. Thus
b_{1}f_{2}=b_{1}ab_{2}\in(f_{1})
. Since
f_{1},f_{2}
form a regular sequence, it follows that
b_{1}\in(f_{1})
, which forces

to be a unit. Since

is a unique factorization domain, it follows that the principal ideal
(w)
is prime, and so
R
is an integral domain.

Let
C
be a complex of finitely generated free
R
-modules. Since
R
is an integral domain,
by
2.6
, each map
\partial_{n}^{C}
has a well-defined rank,
\operatorname{rank}\partial_{n}^{C}
.

3.1
.

Suppose that
C
is an exact complex of finitely generated free
R
-modules. Then for all

we have the equality

\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C}_{n}+\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C}_{n+1}=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}

Indeed, this follows immediately from the definition of
\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C}_{n}
,
2.5
and the fact that
S^{-1}C
is an exact sequence of vector spaces.

Graded
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)

Since

is a graded ring, we consider the category

\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
of graded totally acyclic complexes over

. The objects are those

such that each doug
\partial_{n}^{C}
is homogeneous and the morphisms are the homogenous homotopy equivalence classes of homogeneous chain maps. Note that in this case the ideals

of

are homogeneous.

3.2
.

If

is a finitely generated graded

-module then

has a graded complete resolution

. Indeed, the proof is a graded analogue of the construction of Shamash

[

, see also
[

]

Rank varieties

For each
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{c})\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
we fix preimages
a_{i}\in Q
of

\alpha_{i}\in k
for
i=1,\dots,c
and let
R_{\alpha}
denote the quotient

R_{\alpha}=R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c}).

Of course
R_{\alpha}
depends on the choice of preimages, but as we shall see, the rank variety we consider below is independent of this choice.
We note that
R_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to the specialized local hypersurface,

R_{\alpha}\cong Q/(a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{c}f_{c}).

For
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
we let
C_{\alpha}
denote the complex

C_{\alpha}=C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})

which is an object of
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R_{\alpha})
. Indeed, according to
[
19
, pg. 397]
,

is a regular sequence in
R
, and so

has projective dimension

over

. Then for arbitrary

,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{c+1}(R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c}),\operatorname{coker}\partial^{C}_{n-c})=0
shows that
C_{\alpha}
is exact at degree

. Thus
C_{\alpha}
is exact, and therefore totally acyclic over

R_{\alpha}

[

, lem. 2.4]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
. We define the
rank variety
of

to be

\operatorname{V}(C)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}\mid C_{\alpha}\text{ is not contractible}\}

Note that we are now able to assign an invariant of dimension to objects in
\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
.
We first want to show that the rank variety is independent of the choice of preimages

a_{1},\dots,a_{c}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{c}
and
a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{c}
be sequences of elements of
Q
such that
a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}
for
i=1,\dots,c
. Then

C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})

is contractible if and only if

C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})

is contractible

Proof.

Assume that
C^{\prime}=C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
is contractible. Denote by

\partial^{\prime}
the differentials
\partial^{C}\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
. By assumption
there exist maps
s^{\prime}_{n}:C^{\prime}_{n}\to C^{\prime}_{n+1}
such that

\partial^{\prime}_{n+1}s^{\prime}_{n}+s^{\prime}_{n-1}\partial^{\prime}_{n}
is an isomorphism for all
n
. Lift the maps
s^{\prime}_{n}
to maps

S_{n}:C_{n}\to C_{n+1}
and set
s^{\prime\prime}_{n}=S_{n}\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})
. Note that
the rings
R\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
and
R\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})

are both local with common residue field
k
. Letting
\partial^{\prime\prime}
denote the differentials of

C^{\prime\prime}=C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})
, we see that the maps

(\partial^{\prime}_{n+1}s^{\prime}_{n}+s^{\prime}_{n-1}\partial^{\prime}_{n})\otimes_{R^{\prime}}k
and
(\partial^{\prime\prime}_{n+1}s^{\prime\prime}_{n}+s^{\prime\prime}_{n-1}\partial^{\prime\prime}_{n})\otimes_{R^{\prime\prime}}k
agree, and thus, by
2.7
,
\partial^{\prime}_{n+1}s^{\prime}_{n}+s^{\prime}_{n-1}\partial^{\prime}_{n}

and
\partial^{\prime\prime}_{n+1}s^{\prime\prime}_{n}+s^{\prime\prime}_{n-1}\partial^{\prime\prime}_{n}
are isomorphisms simultaneously. Thus
C^{\prime\prime}
is also contractible.
∎

There is another issue of well-definedness that we now address.

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
C
and
D
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
are homotopically equivalent, that is, isomorphic in

\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
, then
\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{V}(D)
.

Proof.

First note that if a morphism
f:C\to D
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
is null-homotopic, then for any
\alpha
the induced morphism
f_{\alpha}:C_{\alpha}\to D_{\alpha}
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R_{\alpha})
is null-homotopic. It follows that if
C
and
D
are homotopically equivalent then so are

C_{\alpha}
and
D_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha
. Therefore by
2.4
,
C_{\alpha}
is contractible if and only if
D_{\alpha}
is contractible. It follows that
\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{V}(D)
.
∎

We now turn our attention to showing that the rank variety is Zariski closed. For an ideal
I

in the polynomial ring
k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
we let
\operatorname{Z}(I)
denote the zero set

\operatorname{Z}(I)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{P}_{k}^{c-1}\mid p(\alpha)=0\text{ for all }p\in I\}

Let
r_{n}=\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C}_{n}
and
\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}
denote the image of the ideal

I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})
under the natural surjection
R\to k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
. Note that since

C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
, the ideals
I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})
are homogeneous ideals of
R
. Thus the ideals

\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}
are homogeneous ideals of
k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have the equality of sets

\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{Z}\left(\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}\cap\overline{I_{r_{n+1}}(\partial^{C}_{n+1})}\right)

In particular, the rank variety
\operatorname{V}(C)
of
C
is a Zariski closed set.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and suppose
\alpha
is not in
\operatorname{V}(C)
, that is,
C_{\alpha}
is contractible. Then there exist maps

s_{n}:(C_{\alpha})_{n}\to(C_{\alpha})_{n+1}
and
s_{n-1}:(C_{\alpha})_{n-1}\to(C_{\alpha})_{n}

such that
\partial_{n+1}^{C_{\alpha}}s_{n}+s_{n-1}\partial_{n}^{C_{\alpha}}
is an isomorphism.
It follows from
2.5
that the complex of
k
-vector spaces
C_{\alpha}\otimes_{Q}k
is exact at
n
. Therefore by
2.5

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes k)=\operatorname{rank}((C_{\alpha})_{n}\otimes_{Q}k)
. Since
\operatorname{rank}((C_{\alpha})_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}
, it follows from the previous equality that

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes k)=r_{n+1}
and
\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes k)=r_{n}
Thus

\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}(\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})})
and

\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}(\overline{I_{r_{n+1}}(\partial^{C}_{n+1})})
. In other words,

\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}\left(\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}\cap\overline{I_{r_{n+1}}(\partial^{C}_{n+1})}\right)
.

For the converse, assume
\alpha
is not in
\operatorname{Z}\left(\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}\cap\overline{I_{r_{n+1}}(\partial^{C}_{n+1})}\right)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes k)=r_{n+1}
and
\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes k)=r_{n}

and it follows that

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes k)=\operatorname{rank}((C_{\alpha})_{n}\otimes_{Q}k)
. Thus by
2.5
,
C_{\alpha}\otimes k
is exact at
n
and there exist maps
s_{n}:(C_{\alpha})_{n}\otimes k\to(C_{\alpha})_{n+1}\otimes k
and
s_{n-1}:(C_{\alpha})_{n-1}\otimes k\to(C_{\alpha})_{n}\otimes k

such that
(\partial_{n+1}^{C_{\alpha}}\otimes k)s_{n}+s_{n-1}(\partial_{n}^{C_{\alpha}}\otimes k)
is an isomorphism. We lift
s_{n}
and
s_{n-1}
to maps

s^{\sharp}_{n}:(C_{\alpha})_{n}\to(C_{\alpha})_{n+1}
and
s^{\sharp}_{n-1}:(C_{\alpha})_{n-1}\to(C_{\alpha})_{n}

such that
s^{\sharp}_{n}\otimes k=s_{n}
and
s^{\sharp}_{n-1}\otimes k=s_{n-1}
. Then by
2.7
the map

\partial_{n+1}^{C_{\alpha}}s^{\sharp}_{n}+s^{\sharp}_{n-1}\partial_{n}^{C_{\alpha}}
is an isomorphism and so a contraction at
n
. Finally, by
2.10
we see that
C_{\alpha}
is contractible and thus

\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C)
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
. Then
C_{\alpha}
is
contractible if and only if for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}

Proof.

The result follows from the previous theorem since

\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}\left(\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial^{C}_{n})}\right)
if and only if

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C}_{n}
. Then we use
3.1
.
∎

We list some basic properties of rank varieties.

Theorem 3.8
.

For complexes in
\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
the following hold.

(1)

\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{V}(\Sigma C)
.

(2)

\operatorname{V}(C\oplus C^{\prime})=\operatorname{V}(C)\cup\operatorname{V}(C^{\prime})
.

(3)

\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{V}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits}(C,R))
.

(4)

If
C_{1}\to C_{2}\to C_{3}\to\Sigma C_{1}
is an exact triangle in
\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
then

\operatorname{V}(C_{i})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(C_{j})\cup\operatorname{V}(C_{k})
for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
.

Proof.

Property (1) is clear from the definition. Property (2) follows since
(C\oplus C^{\prime})_{\alpha}
is contractible if and only if both
C_{\alpha}
and
C^{\prime}_{\alpha}
are contractible.

Let
(\quad)^{*}
denote
\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits}(\quad,R)
. For (3) we use
3.7
and the facts that

\operatorname{rank}((\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n})^{*}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)
and

\operatorname{rank}C_{n}^{*}=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}
.

For (4) it suffices to prove
\operatorname{V}(C_{3})\subseteq\operatorname{V}(C_{1})\cup\operatorname{V}(C_{2})
since this, rotation and (1) give the other containments.

Up to isomorphism of triangles we have
C_{3}=\operatorname{Cone}(f)
for some morphism
f:C_{1}\to C_{2}
. Thus we may assume that
\partial_{n}^{C_{3}}
has the form
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{n}^{C_{2}}&f_{n-1}\\
0&-\partial_{n-1}^{C_{1}}\end{array}\right)
. It follows that
\operatorname{rank}\partial_{n}^{C_{3}}\geq\operatorname{rank}\partial_{n-1}^{C_{1}}+\operatorname{rank}\partial_{n}^{C_{2}}
. Thus if both
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are contractible, then so is
C_{3}
. This is another way to state the result.
∎

Another important result for support or rank varieties via restriction of scalars is Dade’s Lemma, which states that the support or rank variety is trivial if and only if the object is trivial, for example, contractible. We don’t quite get the same result for rank varieties via extension of scalars.

Proposition 3.9
.

For
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
we have
\operatorname{V}(C)=\varnothing
if and only if

C
is contractible or
\sqrt{\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial_{n}^{C})}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
for all
n
(equivalently, for two consecutive valus of
n
).

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{V}(C)=\varnothing
and that
C
is not contractible. Then
C_{\alpha}
is contractible for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
. Therefore
C_{\alpha}\otimes_{R}k
is contractible for all
\alpha
and so by
2.5
we have
\operatorname{rank}(\partial_{n}^{C_{\alpha}}\otimes k)=r_{n}
for all
n
and all
\alpha
. It follows that the ideal
\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial_{n}^{C})}
of
k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
has no zeros in
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
; in other words
\sqrt{\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial_{n}^{C})}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
. For the converse, clearly if
C
is contractible then so is
C_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha
. Therefore suppose
\sqrt{\overline{I_{r_{n}}(\partial_{n}^{C})}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})
for two consecutive values of
n
. Then
3.6
shows that
\operatorname{V}(C)=\varnothing
.
∎

Of course there exist contractible
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
, for example,
0\to R\xrightarrow{1}R\to 0
. We show by example that the second condition of
3.9
does in fact occur.

Example 3.10
.

Let
Q=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
, where
k
is a field, and
f_{1}=x^{2}
,
f_{2}=y^{2}
. Then the generic hypersurface is
R=Q[x_{1},x_{2}]/(x^{2}x_{1}+y^{2}x_{2})
. The complex

C:\cdots\to R^{2}\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x^{2}&y^{2}\\
-x_{2}&x_{1}\end{array}\right)}R^{2}\xrightarrow{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{1}&-y^{2}\\
x_{2}&x^{2}\end{array}\right)}R^{2}\to\cdots

is a complete resolution of
Q[x_{1},x_{2}]/(f_{1},f_{2})
over
R
. By inspection, each map
\partial^{C}_{n}
has rank one and
\overline{I_{1}(\partial_{n}^{C})}=(x_{1},x_{2})
.

4.
Realizability

In this section we retain that
R
denotes the generic hypersurface and show that any projective variety in

\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
can be realized as the rank variety of some
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits R
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
and

p\in R
. Then multiplication by
p
defines a chain endomorphism
\mu_{p}:C\to C
. We denote the mapping cone of this endomorphism by
C^{p}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
and
p\in R
. We let
\overline{p}
denote the image of
p
under the natural surjection
R\to k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
. Then

\operatorname{V}(C^{p})=\operatorname{V}(C)\cap\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})

Proof.

The statement is obvious if
\overline{p}=0
so we assume
\overline{p}\neq 0
. Recall that
\partial_{n}^{C^{p}}
can be represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{n}^{C}&p\\
0&-\partial_{n-1}^{C}\end{array}\right)

where
p
represents the
\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}\times\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}
scalar matrix of
p
. From this it
is to easy to see that
\operatorname{rank}\partial^{C^{p}}_{n}=\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}
.

We first show the reverse containment. Suppose that
\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C^{p})
and that

\alpha\in\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})
. This means that
C^{p}_{\alpha}
is contractible and

\overline{p}(\alpha)=0
. Therefore by
3.7
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C^{p}_{n}=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}+\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}
. Since
\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n-1}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)\leq\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}
and
\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)\leq\operatorname{rank}C_{n}
, it follows that

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}
for all
n
, and so by
3.7

C_{\alpha}
is contractible. In other words,
\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C)
.

Now suppose that
\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C^{p})
and that
\alpha\in\operatorname{V}(C)
. Therefore for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have
\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C^{p}_{n}=\operatorname{rank}C_{n}+\operatorname{rank}C_{n-1}
and for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)<\operatorname{rank}C_{n}
.
It follows that
\overline{p}(\alpha)\neq 0
and so
\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})
.

For the forward containment, assume that
\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C)\cap\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})
. Thus either

\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C)
or
\alpha\notin\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})
. In either case we have that

\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n}\otimes_{R}k)+\operatorname{rank}(\partial^{C^{p}_{\alpha}}_{n+1}\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}C^{p}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus by
3.7
,
C^{p}_{n}
is contractible, that is,

\alpha\notin\operatorname{V}(C^{p})
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
K\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
be a graded complete resolution of
k
. Then

\operatorname{V}(K)=\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}

Proof.

Note that for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
,
R_{\alpha}
is a singular hypersurface and so

k
has a infinite projective dimension over
R_{\alpha}
. Thus by
2.9

K_{\alpha}
is not contractible for every
\alpha
. The result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
W\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
be a projective variety. Then there exist

C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
such that
\operatorname{V}(C)=W
.

Proof.

Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\in R
, and
\overline{p}_{1},\dots,\overline{p}_{m}
their images in

k[x_{1},\dots,x_{c}]
, be such that
W=\operatorname{Z}((\overline{p}_{1},\dots,\overline{p}_{m}))
. Let
K\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
be a complete resolution of
k
. We construct
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
by induction on
m
.

For
m=1
, let
p=p_{1}
and consider
C_{(1)}=K^{p}
. Then by
4.1
we have

\operatorname{V}(C_{(1)})=\operatorname{V}(K)\cap\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})=\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}\cap\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})=\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p})
and we are done in this case.

Suppose that
C_{(m-1)}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
has been constructed such that

\operatorname{V}(C_{(m-1)})=\operatorname{Z}((\overline{p}_{1},\dots,\overline{p}_{m-1}))
Now define
C=C_{(m-1)}^{p_{m}}
. Then by

4.1
we have

\operatorname{V}(C)=\operatorname{V}(C_{(m-1)})\cap\operatorname{Z}(\overline{p}_{m})=\operatorname{Z}((\overline{p}_{1},\dots,\overline{p}_{m}))
and induction is complete.
∎

5.
Failure of Decomposability

A result that holds for rank and support varieties over group algebras and complete intersections is that if a module is indecomposable then its support variety is connected. We show by example that this property fails for rank varieties of modules over the generic hypersurface.

Example 5.1
.

Consider the regular local ring
Q=k\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
, where
k
is a field, and
Q
-regular sequence

f_{1}=x^{2},f_{2}=y^{2}
. Then the corresponding generic hypersurface is

R=Q[x_{1},x_{2}]/(x^{2}x_{1}+y^{2}x_{2})

Let
K
be a complete resolution of
k
over
R
and consider
x_{1}x_{2}\in R
. Then the complex
K^{x_{1}x_{2}}
is
given by

K^{x_{1}x_{2}}:\cdots\to R^{4}\xrightarrow{\partial}R^{4}\xrightarrow{\partial^{\prime}}R^{4}\xrightarrow{\partial}R^{4}\to\cdots

where the dougs
\partial
and
\partial^{\prime}
are given by the matrices

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}-y&xx_{1}&x_{1}x_{2}&0\\
x&yx_{2}&0&x_{1}x_{2}\\
\hline\cr 0&0&yx_{2}&-xx_{1}\\
0&0&-x&-y\end{array}\right)

and
D^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}-yx_{2}&xx_{1}&x_{1}x_{2}&0\\
x&y&0&x_{1}x_{2}\\
\hline\cr 0&0&y&-xx_{1}\\
0&0&-x&-yx_{2}\end{array}\right)
,
respectively, with respect to the standard basis of
R^{4}
.
From
4.1
and
4.2
we have that
\operatorname{V}(K^{x_{1}x_{2}})=\operatorname{Z}(\overline{x_{1}x_{2}})
, which is obviously disconnected.
We will show that
K^{x_{1}x_{2}}
is indecomposable. To this end we show that
M=\operatorname{coker}\partial
is indecomposable. Suppose that
\epsilon:M\to M
is an idempotent map. We lift this to a commutative diagram

\textstyle{R^{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{R^{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\epsilon}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{R^{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial}
\textstyle{R^{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

Let
(a_{ij})
and
(b_{ij})
be matrices representing
\alpha
and
\beta
, respectively, with respect to the standard basis of
R^{4}
. Then we have the matrix equation
(a_{ij})D=D(b_{ij})
. Working modulo
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}R+{\mathfrak{m}}(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}+(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2})
we have the matrix equation

(\overline{a_{ij}})\overline{D}=\overline{D}(\overline{b_{ij}})
and can assume the entries

\overline{a_{ij}}
and
\overline{b_{ij}}
belong to
k+kx_{1}+kx_{2}
. One sees right away that

\overline{a}_{31}=\overline{a}_{32}=\overline{a}_{41}=\overline{a}_{42}=0
and

\overline{b}_{31}=\overline{b}_{32}=\overline{b}_{41}=\overline{b}_{42}=0
. Further, we have

\overline{a}_{11}=\overline{b}_{11}=\overline{a}_{22}=\overline{b}_{22}=\overline{a}_{33}=\overline{b}_{33}=\overline{a}_{44}=\overline{b}_{44}
. Looking at the
22
-entry of the matrix equation, if

\overline{a}_{21}\neq 0
, then
\overline{b}_{12}=\overline{a}_{21}x_{1}
, but this contradicts the
12
-entry of the matrix equation. Thus
\overline{a}_{21}=0
. Similarly, looking at the
43
-entry of the matrix equation above, if
\overline{a}_{43}\neq 0
then
\overline{b}_{43}=-\overline{a}_{43}x_{2}
. Now the
33
-entry implies that
\overline{a}_{34}=0
and the
34
-entry implies that

\overline{b}_{43}=0
. Finally, the
44
-entry then implies that
\overline{a}_{43}=0
, a contradiction.

We have thus shown that
(\overline{a_{ij}})
is upper triangular. If
\overline{a_{11}}
is nonzero in
k
then
(\overline{a_{ij}})
is surjective, which implies
\overline{\epsilon}
is surjective. By Nakayama’s Lemma, the same is true of
\epsilon
, which implies that
\epsilon
is the unit idempotent. If
\overline{a_{11}}\in kx_{1}+kx_{2}
then
(\overline{a_{ij}})
is nilpotent, which implies the same for
\overline{\epsilon}
. In this case it follows that
\epsilon
is the zero idempotent. Thus the only idempotent maps
M\to M
are the unit or zero, and so
M
is indecomposable.

6.
Modules

In this section we define rank varieties for modules over the generic hpersurface

R=P/(w)
, where
w=f_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+f_{c}x_{c}
, and use the results of the previous section to establish the same results for graded
R
-modules instead of graded totally acyclic
R
-complexes.

Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{c})\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
we fix preimages
a_{i}\in Q
of

\alpha_{i}\in k
and again define the local ring
R_{\alpha}
to be

R_{\alpha}=R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})

For an
R
-module
M
we let
M_{\alpha}
denote the
R_{\alpha}
-module

M_{\alpha}=M/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})M

Definition 6.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. We define the
rank variety

of
M
to be

\operatorname{V}(M)=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}\mid M_{\alpha}\text{ has infinite projective dimension over
}R_{\alpha}\}

We show that the rank variety is independent of the choice of preimages

a_{1},\dots,a_{c}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module and
a_{1},\dots,a_{c}
and
a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{c}
be sequences of elements of
Q
such that
a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}
for
i=1,\dots,c
. Then

M/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})M

has finite projective dimension over
R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
if and only if

M/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})M

has finite projective dimension over
R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})

Proof.

Let
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
be a complete resolution of
M
(see
3.2
). Then by

3.4
,

C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
is contractible if and only if

C\otimes_{R}R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})
is contractible. Therefore by
2.9

M/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})M

has finite projective dimension over
R/(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a_{c})
if and only if

M/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})M

has finite projective dimension over
R/(x_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x_{c}-a^{\prime}_{c})
.
∎

6.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module, and
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{K}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
a graded complete resolution of
M
. Then
2.9
gives the equality of rank varieties
\operatorname{V}(M)=\operatorname{V}(C)
.

An immediate consequence of
6.3
is that rank varieties for modules are also Zariski closed.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module. Then the rank variety

\operatorname{V}(M)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
of
M
is Zariski closed.

The realizability question for modules is easy, given that it holds for totally acyclic complexes.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
W\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
be a projective variety. Then there exists a
finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
such that
\operatorname{V}(M)=W
.

Proof.

By
4.3
we have
C\in\operatorname{\mathsf{grK}_{tac}}\nolimits(R)
such that
\operatorname{V}(C)=W
.
Define
M=\operatorname{Image}\partial^{C}_{0}
. Then
C
is a complete resolution of
M
and then

6.3
gives the result.
∎

6.1.
Affine Patches

In this subsection we consider the realizability question for modules over the associated complete intersection ring
S=Q/(f_{1},\dots,f_{c})
. Although it is known that every projective variety in
\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
is the support variety (equivalently, rank variety) of some finitely generated
S
-module
[
8
]
, see also
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
, using the results of this paper we can realize the affine patches

\mathbb{A}^{i}_{k}=\{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{c})\in\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}\mid\alpha_{i}=1\}

of a projective variety
W\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
in a relatively painless way, albeit by non-finitely generated
S
-modules. For existing proofs, it is difficult to ascertain a presentation of the module realizing the variety.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
W\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{c-1}_{k}
be a projective variety. Then there exist (not
necessarily finitely generated) graded
S
-modules
M^{i}
such that

\operatorname{V}(M^{i})\cap\mathbb{A}^{i}_{k}=W\cap\mathbb{A}^{i}_{k}

for
i=1,\dots,c
.

Proof.

First notice that for each
i=1,\dots,c
the sequence

s_{i}=f_{1}\dots,f_{i-1},x_{i}-1,f_{i+1},\dots,f_{c}

is a regular on
R
. By
6.5
there exists a finitely generated graded
R
-module
M
such that
\operatorname{V}(M)=W
. Since
M
is the zeroth image in a
totally acyclic complex, the sequences
s_{i}
are also regular on
M
. Now for each

i=1,\dots,c
define
M^{i}=M/(s_{i})M
. Then each
M^{i}
is an
S
-module and finitely
generated over
R/(s_{i})\cong S[x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{c}]
.

Note that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}^{i}_{k}
the sequence
f_{1}\dots,\widehat{f}_{i},\dots,f_{c}
is regular on

M_{\alpha}=M/(x_{1}-\alpha_{1},\dots,x_{i}-1,\dots,x_{c}-\alpha_{c})M
and
M^{i}_{\alpha}=M^{i}/(x_{1}-\alpha_{1},\dots,x_{i}-1,\dots,x_{c}-\alpha_{c})M^{i}
is isomorphic to
M_{\alpha}/(f_{1}\dots,\widehat{f}_{i},\dots,f_{c})M_{\alpha}
. Thus
M^{i}_{\alpha}
and
M_{\alpha}
have finite projective dimension over
R_{\alpha}
simultaneously. This establishes the claimed equality of varieties.
∎
