On the integrability of root-Kerr probe dynamics Preprint: KIAS-P26017
Sungsoo Kim, Sangmin Lee

Abstract
In the background of a Kerr-Newman black hole, the motion of a scalar particle is integrable by virtue of an extra conserved charge known as Carter charge. When the particle is endowed with spin, it is known that another conserved charge, the Rüdiger charge, maintains the integrability at least at low orders in the spin magnitude. We explore the extent of this integrability in a simpler model where both the source and the probe are root-Kerr particles, the non-gravitating limit of the Kerr-Newman black hole. At the leading order in the probe charge, the integrability holds to all orders in the spin magnitude if the interaction vertices of the probe are dictated by the Newman-Janis shift. At the second order in the probe charge, the integrability can be extended to the spin-squared order but begins to fail at the spin-cubic order. An argument based on asymptotic conservation suggests that it is impossible to restore the conservation at the spin-cubic order by a further deformation of the probe action. We compare our results with related observations for Kerr black holes with gravitational interactions.



1
Introduction

The motion of a probe particle in the Schwarzschild metric is integrable much like the central-force problem in Newtonian mechanics. When the background geometry is replaced with a Kerr black hole solution, the symmetry is reduced and
the integrability may seem lost.
However, Carter
8
showed that the general Kerr-Newman solution admits an extra conserved charge
which restores integrability.

When the probe particle carries spin (angular momentum due to its own rotation),
the phase space of the probe has at least one additional degree of freedom accounting for the spin orientation.
To maintain integrability, an additional conserved charge is required.
Rüdiger
44
;
45
found such a charge.
Unlike the point particle probe,
the integrability of the spinning probe is only approximate,
and should be tested order by order in powers of spin.
Initially, the integrability was verified at the first order in spin.

Recent works
13
;
12
;
42
extended the integrability to the quadratic order in spin,
where the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon (MPD) equation
37
;
40
;
16
,
generalized to the spin-quadrupole order,
was used to verify the conservation of the Carter and Rüdiger charges.
The Rüdiger charge remains unmodified to this order, whereas the Carter charge receives corrections involving the spin tensor of the probe and the curvature tensor of the background spacetime.

In a scattering setting, a weaker notion of conservation can be tested extensively.
The classical scattering is governed by the radial action, or more broadly classical eikonal
19
;
23
;
3
;
34
;
33
;
28
. The asymptotic conservation means that the asymptotic forms of the conserved charges, where the curvature terms are dropped, commute with the radial action under the Poisson-Dirac algebra.
Ref.
2
showed that the asymptotic conservation of the Carter and Rüdiger charges at the 1st post-Minkowskian order (1PM) holds for all orders in spin, even away from the probe limit.
At 2PM, the conservation holds up to the quartic order in probe spin but breaks down at the quintic order.

In general, it would be useful to know the maximal extent to which the conservation is valid.
As a related question, once the
asymptotic
conservation is verified to a certain approximation,
it would be desirable to determine whether the strict
local
conservation holds throughout the probe trajectory.

The purpose of this paper is to answer these questions for a simpler cousin of the Kerr-Newman black hole,
known as the root-Kerr particle
5
. The root-Kerr particle is not gravitating,
but carries an infinite series of predetermined electric and magnetic multipole moments.
Both the source and the probe are taken to be root-Kerr particles.
To the leading order in the probe charge, which we call the 1st post-Lorentzian order (1PL)
7
,
we show that the conservation of the two charges holds to all orders in spin,
as anticipated in
2
.
At 2PL, however, the conservation can only be extended to the quadratic order in spin and not further.
We consider the most general deformation of the world-line interaction vertices, as well as
the most general modification of the charges,
but it seems impossible to revive the charge conservation to the spin-cubic order.

Our analysis of the root-Kerr probe dynamics is based on the recently formulated universal world-line model of massive spinning particles
26
. The equation of motion (EoM) is derived in a Hamiltonian formulation.
The covariant spin condition
S_{{\mu}{\nu}}p^{\nu}=0
is imposed throughout.
The resulting EoM generalizes the Bargmann-Michel-Telegdi (BMT) equation
6
with an infinite series of interaction vertices.
At 1PL, the interaction vertices are completely fixed by the Newman-Janis (NJ) shift
38
.
Following Guevara et al.
20
and subsequent work
25
;
24
,
we are elevating the NJ shift from a solution generating technique for the source to a dynamical principle
to determine the world-line theory of the probe.
At 2PL, no simple principle determines the interaction vertices a priori;
we seek interactions that enhance the conservation of the charges as much as possible.

In section
2
,
after a brief review of the root-Kerr particle as a static source,
we present the dynamical equations for the root-Kerr probe following the general framework of
26
.
We show how the Newman-Janis shift completely determines the 1PL interaction vertices
24
,
and discuss some 2PL vertices relevant for later sections.
In section
3
, we explain how to test the charge conservation in our world-line model.
Geometric symmetries associated with Killing vectors lead to exact charge conservation; interaction vertices compatible with the symmetries can be added indefinitely without violating conservation.
For the Carter and Rüdiger charges,
the conservation depends on the peculiarity of the interaction vertices and the modification of the charges
due to coupling to the background field.

Section
4
and
5
examine the conservation of the Carter and Rüdiger charges at 1PL and 2PL, respectively. The exact (all order in spin) conservation at 1PL is derived without much difficulty.
The Rüdiger charge is not modified at this order, while the Carter charge receives a non-trivial correction.
At 2PL, the conservation is more delicate.
For the field-strength-squared interactions without more derivatives, the conservation at the spin-squared order
uniquely selects a particular coupling. As we try to extend the conservation to the spin-cubic order,
however, the conservation breaks down regardless of the couplings.

In section
6
, we turn to the asymptotic conservation.
At 1PL, we confirm the observation of
2
that the asymptotic version of the exact conservation continues to hold even away from the probe limit.
At 2PL, we show that even under the weaker notion of asymptotic conservation,
it is impossible to restore the conservation at the spin-cubic order.
We conclude the paper with some discussions in section
7
.

2.2
Probe at 1PL: Newman-Janis shift

The world-line model of a relativistic spinning particle coupled to an arbitrary electromagnetic or gravitational field
had been a long-standing problem. A recent work
26
offered a comprehensive and unifying solution to the problem.
Here we review parts of
26
that are directly relevant to this paper.

There are many spinning particle models which differ in the variables to parametrize the spin degrees of freedom. A key observation of
26
is that
there exists a model-independent universal subspace of the phase space, parametrized by position
x^{\mu}
, momentum
p^{\mu}
and (sign-flipped) spin-length vector
y^{\mu}=-a^{\mu}
.

The Poisson bracket on the
(x,y,p)
space is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\;\;,\;\;\}^{\circ}&=\eta^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}-\frac{y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\nu}}\\
&\qquad-\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]}{2p^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\nu}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}}\right).\end{aligned}

(17)

Equivalently, in terms of the complex-valued position coordinate,
z^{\mu}=x^{\mu}+iy^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \{\;\;,\;\;\}^{\circ}&=\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}\right)\\
&\qquad-2\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]-i(y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu})}{p^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\mu}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{\nu}}\,.\end{aligned}

(18)

Note that
\{z^{\mu},z^{\nu}\}^{\circ}=0=\{\bar{z}^{\mu},\bar{z}^{\nu}\}^{\circ}
. Following
25
, we will call it a“zigzag” property.
The superscript circle in
\{f,g\}^{\circ}
indicates that the Poisson bracket describes a free theory.

The
(x,y,p)
phase space is 12-dimensional, but the Poisson bracket has rank 10. The two “null” coordinates are

\displaystyle\{f,y^{2}p^{2}\}^{\circ}=0\,,\quad\{f,y\cdot p\}^{\circ}=0\quad\mbox{for arbitrary }f\,.

(19)

The non-invertibility of the Poisson bracket will cause some minor technical difficulties.

The (world-line) time evolution is generated by the usual Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle H=\frac{\kappa^{0}}{2}(p^{2}+m^{2})\,.

(20)

Here,
\kappa^{0}
is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the world-line reparametrization invariance.
The mass-squared
m^{2}
is in general a function of the spin-magnitude,
y^{2}p^{2}
,
but for spin-magnitude-preserving interactions, including all interactions considered in this paper,
treating
m^{2}
as constant does not affect the EoM
26
.
The free EoM is simply

\displaystyle\left.\frac{df}{d\tau}\right|_{\text{free}}=\{f,H\}^{\circ}\,,

(21)

which gives, upon choosing
\kappa^{0}=1/m
,

\displaystyle\dot{x}^{\mu}=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}\,,\quad\dot{y}^{\mu}=0\,,\quad\dot{p}^{\mu}=0\,.

(22)

The interaction is incorporated via a symplectic deformation
26
,

\displaystyle\omega=\omega^{\circ}+\omega^{\prime}\,.

(23)

For a system in which
\omega^{\circ}
is invertible, the Poisson bracket after the deformation would be

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{f,g\}&=\{f,g\}^{\circ}-\{f,\zeta^{m}\}^{\circ}\omega^{\prime}_{mn}\{\zeta^{n},g\}\\
&=\{f,g\}^{\circ}-\{f,\zeta^{m}\}^{\circ}\omega^{\prime}_{mn}\{\zeta^{n},g\}^{\circ}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad+\{f,\zeta^{m}\}^{\circ}\omega^{\prime}_{mn}\{\zeta^{n},\zeta^{p}\}^{\circ}\omega^{\prime}_{pq}\{\zeta^{p},g\}^{\circ}+\cdots\,.\end{split}

(24)

Here,
\zeta^{m}
represent all coordinates of the phase space.
We are working with a non-invertible Poisson bracket, which may be regarded as a Dirac bracket in each of the models considered in
26
. We cannot obtain the interacting Poisson bracket by directly inverting (
23
), but it can be shown that the formula (
24
) still holds with a non-maximal-rank Poisson/Dirac bracket.

The two simplest interaction terms are the electric monopole and the magnetic dipole couplings.
In our notation, they contribute

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}=qA_{\mu}(x)dx^{\mu}+q\frac{g}{2}\tilde{F}_{{\mu}{\nu}}y^{\mu}dx^{\nu}\,.

(25)

The resulting EoM truncated at the
\mathcal{O}(q)
order is the celebrated Bargmann-Michel-Telegdi (BMT) equation
6
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{x}^{\mu}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}-\frac{q}{m}\left[\frac{g}{2}\tilde{F}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}+\left(1-\frac{g}{2}\right)\hat{\delta}^{\mu}{}_{\kappa}\tilde{F}^{\kappa}{}_{\nu}\right]y^{\nu}+\cdots,\\
\dot{y}^{\mu}&=\frac{q}{m}\left[F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}-\left(1-\frac{g}{2}\right)\hat{\delta}^{\mu}{}_{\kappa}F^{\kappa}{}_{\nu}\right]y^{\nu}+\cdots,\\
\dot{p}^{\mu}&=\frac{q}{m}F^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}+\cdots,\end{aligned}

(26)

where
\hat{\delta}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\delta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}-p^{\mu}p_{\nu}/p^{2}

and
\tilde{F}_{\\
m{\nu}}
is the Hodge dual of
F_{{\mu}{\nu}}
.

Newman-Janis shift

For the root-Kerr particle, the
\mathcal{O}(q)
part of the interaction term (
23
) is completely fixed by the requirement that
it reproduces the static solution (
4
).
The resulting coupling is best summarized by the dynamical NJ shift
20
;
25
;
24
, which links the holomorphy in the complex spacetime to the self-duality of the field-strength
39
;
25
,

\displaystyle\omega^{\prime}_{\text{NJ}}=\frac{q}{2}F_{\mu\nu}^{+}(z)dz^{\mu}\wedge dz^{\nu}+\frac{q}{2}F_{\mu\nu}^{-}(\bar{z})d\bar{z}^{\mu}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\nu}\,.

(27)

The resulting EoM reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{p}_{\mu}&=qF^{+}_{\mu\nu}(z)\dot{z}^{\nu}+qF^{-}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{z})\dot{\bar{z}}^{\nu}\,,\\
\dot{z}^{\mu}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}+\frac{2iq}{m^{2}}\left[y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}y_{\rho}p_{\sigma}\right]F^{-}_{\nu\lambda}(\bar{z})\dot{\bar{z}}^{\lambda}\,,\\
\dot{\bar{z}}^{\mu}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}-\frac{2iq}{m^{2}}\left[y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}-i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}y_{\rho}p_{\sigma}\right]F^{+}_{\nu\lambda}({z})\dot{{z}}^{\lambda}\,.\end{aligned}

(28)

As shown in (
24
), the appearance of time-derivatives on the RHS is a generic feature
of a symplectic perturbation theory. Iterating the EoM leads to an infinite series of terms on the RHS.

2.3
Probe at 2PL: contact term

Beyond the 1PL order, the Newman-Janis shift does not fix all couplings.
There can be an infinite series of
\partial^{k}F^{l}
couplings with undetermined coefficients.
See
29
for an explicit and systematic discussion on this
point.
We will be pragmatic and look for a coupling at 2PL which enhances the charge conservation.

The EoM from the NJ shift coupling, before addition of further couplings, reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{p}_{\mu}&=qF^{+}_{\mu\nu}(z)\dot{z}^{\nu}+qF^{-}_{\mu\nu}(\bar{z})\dot{\bar{z}}^{\nu}\,,\\
\dot{z}^{\mu}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}-2iqM^{{\mu}{\nu}}F^{-}_{\nu\lambda}(\bar{z})\dot{\bar{z}}^{\lambda}\,,\end{aligned}

(29)

where

\displaystyle M^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}p_{\beta}\right)\,.

(30)

Iterating the EoM and extracting the
\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
terms, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{p}_{\mu}|_{q^{2}}&=-\frac{2iq^{2}}{m}(F^{+}MF^{-}p)_{\mu}+\frac{2iq^{2}}{m}(F^{-}\bar{M}F^{+}p)_{\mu}\,,\\
\dot{z}^{\mu}|_{q^{2}}&=\frac{4q^{2}}{m}(MF^{-}\bar{M}F^{+}p)^{\mu}\,.\end{aligned}

(31)

For brevity, we are using shorthand notations such as

\displaystyle(ABCv)^{\mu}=A^{{\mu}{\nu}}B_{{\beta}{\rho}}C^{{\rho}{\sigma}}v_{\sigma}\,.

(32)

To simplify the EoM, we use an identity,

\displaystyle\bar{M}F^{+}p=-F^{+}y\,,\qquad MF^{-}p=-F^{-}y\,.

(33)

The derivation of the identity is as follows.

\displaystyle\begin{split}p^{2}\bar{M}^{\mu\nu}F^{+}_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho}&=(y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}-i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}p_{\beta})F^{+}_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho}\\
&=(-y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}-i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}y_{\alpha}p_{\beta})F^{+}_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho}\\
&=-2[(y\wedge p)^{-}]^{\mu\nu}F^{+}_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho}\\
&=-2(F^{+})^{\mu\nu}[(y\wedge p)^{-}]_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho}\\
&=(F^{+})^{\mu\nu}(-y_{\nu}p_{\rho}+p_{\mu}y_{\nu}-i\epsilon_{\nu\rho}[y,p])p^{\rho}=-p^{2}(F^{+})^{\mu\nu}y_{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(34)

After the simplification, the EoM takes the form

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{p}_{\mu}|_{q^{2},\text{undeformed}}&=0\,,\\
\dot{z}^{\mu}|_{q^{2},\text{undeformed}}&=-\frac{4q^{2}}{m}(MF^{-}F^{+}y)^{\mu}=\frac{4q^{2}}{m}\{z^{\mu},y^{\nu}\}^{\circ}(F^{-}F^{+}y)_{\nu}\,.\end{aligned}

(35)

The last expression in the second line is suggestive.
By adding a suitable 2PL coupling, we can cancel the
q^{2}(F^{+}F^{-})
terms completely, at the expense of introducing some field-derivative terms.
We implement the cancellation using a Hamiltonian deformation,

\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{\kappa}{2}(p^{2}+m^{2})\quad\rightarrow\quad H_{0}+q^{2}H_{2}=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left[p^{2}+m^{2}-4q^{2}yF^{+}(z)F^{-}(\bar{z})y\right]\,.

(36)

The possibility of such a deformation was noted around eq.(5.31) of
24
.
After adding the deformation,
the final form of the
\mathcal{O}(q^{2})
EoM becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{p}^{\mu}|_{q^{2},\text{deformed}}&=\frac{q}{m}\left(F^{+}+F^{-}\right)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}+\frac{2q^{2}}{m}(h^{\mu}+\bar{h}^{\mu})+\mathcal{O}(q^{3})\,,\\
\dot{z}^{\mu}|_{q^{2},\text{deformed}}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}+\frac{2iq}{m}(F^{-})^{\mu}{}_{\nu}y^{\nu}-\frac{4iq^{2}}{m}M^{\mu\nu}\bar{h}_{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(q^{3})\,,\end{split}

(37)

where the vectors
(h_{\mu},\bar{h}_{\mu})
carry the field-derivative effects,

\displaystyle h_{\mu}=y(\partial_{\mu}F^{+})F^{-}y\,,\quad\bar{h}_{\mu}=yF^{+}(\partial_{\mu}F^{-})y\,.

(38)

We will work this version of the EoM in the rest of this paper.

We could implement the same cancellation using a symplectic deformation,

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{(2)}=-\frac{2q^{2}}{m^{2}}(yF^{+}F^{-}y)p_{\nu}dx^{\nu}\,.

(39)

The Hamiltonian deformation (
36
) and the symplectic deformation (
39
) can be mapped to each other by a non-canonical redefinition of the momentum variable, if higher order corrections can be neglected.

3
Conserved charges

3.1
Generality

A Hamiltonian system is defined by
(\omega,H)
, a symplectic form and a Hamiltonian, on a phase space. A flow
V=V^{m}\partial_{m}
on the phase space is called a symmetry if

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V}\omega=0\,,\quad\mathcal{L}_{V}H=V[H]=0\,.

(40)

Using Cartan’s formula, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V}\omega=(d\,\mathrm{i}_{V}+\mathrm{i}_{V}\,d)\omega=d(\mathrm{i}_{V}\omega)=0\,.

(41)

If the phase space is topologically trivial, the closed form
\mathrm{i}_{V}\omega
should be exact:

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}_{V}\omega=-dQ\,.

(42)

It follows that
V
is generated by
Q
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V^{m}\omega_{mn}=-\omega_{nm}V^{m}=-\partial_{n}Q\\
\Longrightarrow\qquad&\delta_{V}f=V[f]=V^{m}\partial_{m}f=\omega^{mn}\partial_{m}f\partial_{n}Q=\{f,Q\}\,.\end{split}

(43)

Given a symplectic potential
\theta=\theta_{n}d\zeta^{n}
(
\omega=d\theta
),

\mathcal{L}_{V}\omega=0
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V}\theta=(d\,\mathrm{i}_{V}+\mathrm{i}_{V}\,d)\theta=d\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d(\mathrm{i}_{V}\theta-\lambda)=-\mathrm{i}_{V}\omega\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q=\mathrm{i}_{V}\theta-\lambda\,.

(44)

Constraints

The discussion above assumes a non-degenerate symplectic form and no constraints, so the components of the Poisson bi-vector is the matrix inverse of those of the symplectic form.
When there are constraints, we work with the Dirac bracket with a non-maximal rank.
The discussion of charge conservation is modified accordingly, as detailed in
26
.
A key observation is that, for some symmetries including the Killing symmetry to be reviewed below, the charge
Q
and the Poisson bracket are both deformed by the interaction,
while the vector field
V
in (
43
) remains the same:

\displaystyle V[f]=\{f,Q_{0}\}^{\circ}=\{f,Q\}\,.

(45)

Symplectic perturbation

Consider a symplectic deformation with a coupling constant
q
,
and the corresponding deformation of
V
and
Q
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega&=\omega^{\circ}+q\,\omega^{\prime}\,,\\
V&=V_{0}+qV_{1}+q^{2}V_{2}+\cdots\,,\\
Q&=Q_{0}+qQ_{1}+q^{2}Q_{2}+\cdots\,,\end{split}

(46)

Assuming
i_{V_{0}}\omega^{\circ}=-dQ_{0}

and given
\omega^{\prime}
, how could we construct
V_{n}
and
Q_{n}

(n\geq 1)
?

Expanding (
41
) and (
42
) in powers of
q
, we get

\displaystyle d(\mathrm{i}_{V_{n-1}}\omega^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}_{V_{n}}\omega^{\circ})=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathrm{i}_{V_{n-1}}\omega^{\prime}+\mathrm{i}_{V_{n}}\omega^{\circ}=-dQ_{n}\,.

(47)

In general, we may have to solve an infinite series of equations
where the existence or uniqueness of the solution is not obvious.
But when we can find
d(\mathrm{i}_{V_{k-1}}\omega^{\prime})=0
for some
k
, we can set
V_{n\geq k}=0
, terminating the series.
The simplest case is when
d(\mathrm{i}_{V_{0}}\omega^{\prime})=0
.
Then
Q_{1}=\mathrm{i}_{V_{0}}\theta^{\prime}-\lambda_{1}
and
Q=Q_{0}+qQ_{1}
is exactly conserved,
while
V=V_{0}
remains unchanged.
This is the case with the Killing symmetry, in accordance with (
45
).

Conservation with 1PL interaction

Given a symplectic deformation at 1PL, the general EoM can be expressed as
24
;
26

\displaystyle\frac{df}{d\tau}=\{f,H\}^{\circ}-q\{f,\zeta^{m}\}^{\circ}\omega^{\prime}_{mn}\{\zeta^{n},H\}\,.

(48)

The first two brackets on the RHS are the unperturbed ones while the last one is the full bracket.
Applying it to the root-Kerr model with the NJ shift at 1PL (and no 2PL deformation yet) and substituting
Q_{0}
for
f
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{dQ_{0}}{d\tau}&=\{Q_{0},H\}^{\circ}-q\sum_{a=\pm}\{Q_{0},z_{a}^{\mu}\}^{\circ}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})\{z_{a}^{\nu},H\}\\
&=q\sum_{a=\pm}\delta z_{a}^{\mu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})\dot{z}_{a}^{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(49)

Here,
z_{+}=z
,
z_{-}=\bar{z}
. We are assuming the 0th order conservation
\{Q_{0},H\}^{\circ}=0
.

3.2
Killing charges

Suppose the background field admits a Killing vector
K=K^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
in the sense that

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}K_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}K_{\mu}=0\,,\quad(\mathcal{L}_{K}A)_{\mu}=K^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}+A_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}K^{\nu}=0\,.

(50)

In general, the requirement for
A_{\mu}
is that
\mathcal{L}_{K}A=d\lambda
for some scalar
\lambda
. In flat Minkowski space without magnetic charges, one can always choose a gauge where
\lambda=0
. We choose to work in such a gauge.
In the spinless case (
y=0
), the conserved charge associated with the Killing vector is

\displaystyle Q=K^{\mu}(p_{\mu}+qA_{\mu})\,.

(51)

For a free spinning particle, described by the
(x,y,p)
variables,
the Killing charge generalizes to
26

\displaystyle Q_{0}=p_{\mu}K^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}y_{\mu}p_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}K_{\sigma}\,.

(52)

When
K
generates rotation, the two terms represent the orbital and spin angular momenta, respectively.
For example, with
K=x_{1}\partial_{2}-x_{2}\partial_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle p_{\mu}K^{\mu}=x_{1}p_{2}-x_{2}p_{1}=L_{12}\,,\quad\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}y_{\mu}p_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}K_{\sigma}=-(y_{0}p_{3}-y_{3}p_{0})=S_{12}\,.

(53)

Using the charge (
52
) and the bracket (
17
), we can take the symmetry transformation of the variables,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta x^{\mu}&=\{x^{\mu},Q_{0}\}^{\circ}=K^{\mu}(x)\,,\\
\delta y^{\mu}&=\{y^{\mu},Q_{0}\}^{\circ}=-(\partial^{\mu}K_{\nu})y^{\nu}\,,\\
\delta p^{\mu}&=\{p^{\mu},Q_{0}\}^{\circ}=-(\partial^{\mu}K_{\nu})p^{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(54)

Killing charges with 1PL interaction

In flat Minkowski space,
since
K^{\mu}(x)
is only a linear function of
x
, we have

\displaystyle\delta z^{\mu}=\delta x^{\mu}+i\delta y^{\mu}=K^{\mu}(x)+iy^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}K^{\mu}=K^{\mu}(x+iy)=K^{\mu}(z)\,.

(55)

The RHS of (
49
) becomes

\displaystyle q\sum_{a=\pm}K^{\mu}(z_{a})F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})\dot{z}_{a}^{\nu}=q\sum_{a=\pm}\left[K^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})\dot{z}^{\nu}\right]_{a}\,.

(56)

The Killing condition for
A_{\mu}
implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}K^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})\dot{z}^{\nu}&=-(A_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}K^{\mu}+K^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu})\dot{z}^{\nu}\\
&=-\dot{z}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}(K^{\mu}A_{\mu})=-\frac{d}{d\tau}(K^{\mu}A_{\mu})\,.\end{split}

(57)

Therefore we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}(Q_{0}+qQ_{1})=0\,,\quad Q_{1}=K^{\mu}(z)A_{\mu}^{+}(z)+K^{\mu}(\bar{z})A_{\mu}^{-}(\bar{z})\,.

(58)

In summary, the expression for a conserved momentum associated with a Killing vector is

\displaystyle Q=K^{\mu}(x)p_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}y_{\mu}p_{\nu}\partial_{\rho}K_{\sigma}+q\left[K^{\mu}(z)A_{\mu}^{+}(z)+K^{\mu}(\bar{z})A_{\mu}^{-}(\bar{z})\right]\,.

(59)

The modification of the charge due to the interaction is such that
the flow-charge relation (
45
) holds.

Killing charges with 2PL interaction

If we were to add the 2PL interaction through symplectic deformation, as illustrated in (
39
),
the Killing charge
Q
will receive a further correction. But, if we add the 2PL interaction by the Hamiltonian deformation as in (
36
), the form of the charge does not change.
To preserve the symmetry, we only need to check

\displaystyle\{Q_{0}+qQ_{1},H_{0}+q^{2}H_{2}\}=0\,.

(60)

Since
H_{2}
depends on the field strength produced by the source, it will preserve precisely those symmetries left unbroken by the source.

3.3
Carter and Rüdiger charges

We introduce the Carter
8
and Rüdiger
44
;
45
charges
and discuss briefly how the probe spin modifies the definitions.
See
e.g.
refs.
18
;
43
;
49
for further discussions on these charges.

For a spinless probe
(y^{\mu}=0)
, in terms of the Killing-Yano tensor (
11
), the Carter charge is defined as

\displaystyle C_{0}|_{\mathrm{spinless}}=Y_{\mu}Y^{\mu}=K_{\rho\sigma}p^{\rho}p^{\sigma}\,,\quad Y_{\mu}\equiv Y_{\mu\rho}p^{\rho}\,,\quad K_{\rho\sigma}=Y_{\rho\mu}Y_{\sigma}{}^{\mu}\,.

(61)

Applying the EoM for a spinless probe, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.\frac{dC_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{\mathrm{spinless}}&=-2p\cdot Y\cdot Y\cdot\frac{dp}{d\tau}=-\frac{2q}{m}(p\cdot Y\cdot Y\cdot F\cdot p)\,.\end{split}

(62)

As we noted in (
14
),
the two matrices commute
Y\cdot F=F\cdot Y
,
so the RHS of does vanish thanks to the antisymmetry of
Y\cdot F\cdot Y
.

The Carter charge was generalized to include spin by Rüdiger
44
;
45
and further extended by Compère et al.
13
;
12
. In the root-Kerr limit, where the curvature terms vanish,

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{0}&=(Y_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho})(Y^{\nu\sigma}p_{\sigma})-4\xi^{\lambda}\epsilon_{\lambda\mu\sigma[\rho}Y_{\nu]}{}^{\sigma}S^{\mu\nu}p^{\rho}+\eta_{\mu\nu}\left[\xi_{\rho}\xi_{\sigma}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\rho\sigma}\xi^{2}\right]S^{\mu\rho}S^{\nu\sigma}\,.\end{split}

(63)

Our definition differs from that of ref.
12
by an overall sign.
In what follows, to avoid clutter, we use abbreviations such as
xy=x\cdot y
,
xAy=x\cdot A\cdot y
,
and
w_{\mu}=\epsilon_{\mu}[y,p,\xi]
.
After the substitution,
S^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]
,
we can express
C_{0}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{0}&=(Y_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho})^{2}+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+w^{2}+\xi^{2}y^{2}p^{2}\\
&=-pYYp+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+p^{2}(\xi y)^{2}+y^{2}(\xi p)^{2}\,.\end{split}

(64)

The Rüdiger charge is linear in the KY tensor:

\displaystyle R_{0}=Y_{\mu\nu}y^{\mu}p^{\nu}\,.

(65)

The conservation for a spinless probe follows easily,

\displaystyle\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}=\frac{q}{m}\left(-y\cdot F\cdot Y\cdot p+y\cdot Y\cdot F\cdot p\right)=0\,,

(66)

where we used the commutativity
Y\cdot F=F\cdot Y
again.

In the gravitational setup, where both the source and the probe are Kerr black holes, ref.
12
showed that the Rüdiger charge continues to be conserved without any modification up to the spin-squared order.
In the following two sections, we will examine the conservation of
C
and
R
to all orders in spin at 1PL and to the spin-squared order at 2PL, while adding correction terms to the charges if necessary.

4
Exact conservation at 1PL

At the 1PL order, assuming that the EoM is completely dictated by the NJ shift, we will show that both the Carter charge and the Rüdiger charge are conserved to all orders in spin.
The definition of the Rüdiger charge is not modified, whereas the Carter charge receives a non-trivial correction.

4.1
Rüdiger charge

Following the general discussion in the previous section, we begin by computing the symmetry variation of the dynamical variables:

\delta f=\{f,R_{0}\}^{\circ}
before turning on the EM background.
First,

\displaystyle\delta p^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-\partial^{\mu}Y_{\rho\sigma}y^{\rho}p^{\sigma}=-\epsilon^{\mu}[y,p,\xi]\,.

(67)

To compute
\delta z^{\mu}
, we note that
Y
is linear in
x
, which implies

\displaystyle Y_{\nu\sigma}(z)-Y_{\nu\sigma}(x)=iy^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}Y_{\nu\sigma}=iy^{\rho}\epsilon_{\rho\nu\sigma\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\,.

(68)

It follows that

\displaystyle R_{0}=y^{\nu}Y_{\nu\sigma}(x)p^{\sigma}=y^{\nu}Y_{\nu\sigma}(z)p^{\sigma}=y^{\nu}Y_{\nu\sigma}(\bar{z})p^{\sigma}\,.

(69)

Using the zigzag property, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta z^{\mu}&=\{z^{\mu},R_{0}\}=\{z^{\mu},y^{\nu}Y_{\nu\sigma}(z)p^{\sigma}\}\\
&=y_{\nu}Y^{\nu\mu}(z)+\{z^{\mu},y^{\nu}\}Y_{\nu\sigma}(z)p^{\sigma}\\
&=y_{\nu}Y^{\nu\mu}(z)-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(y^{\mu}p^{\nu}+p^{\mu}y^{\nu}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]\right)Y_{\nu\sigma}(z)p^{\sigma}\\
&=-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p](\tilde{Y}_{\nu\sigma}(z)+iY_{\nu\sigma}(z))p^{\sigma}=-\frac{2i}{p^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]Y^{+}_{\nu\sigma}(z)p^{\sigma}\\
&=-2\left(\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{p^{2}}\right)Y^{+}_{\nu\rho}(z)y^{\rho}=2y^{\rho}Y^{+}_{\rho\nu}(z)\left(\eta^{\nu\mu}-\frac{p^{\nu}p^{\mu}}{p^{2}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(70)

A correlation between self-duality and holomorphy has emerged.

Now we can run the EoM test (
49
) of the conservation of the Rüdiger charge.
It follows from (
70
) that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{1}}=\frac{2q}{m}\sum_{a=\pm}y^{\rho}Y^{a}_{\rho\sigma}(z_{a})\hat{\eta}^{\sigma\mu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})p^{\nu}\,,\qquad\hat{\eta}^{\sigma\mu}=\eta^{\sigma\mu}-\frac{p^{\sigma}p^{\mu}}{p^{2}}\,.

(71)

Using (
15
), we can show that the RHS of (
71
) vanishes:

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{1}}=0\,.

(72)

In view of (
47
), we have shown that
V_{1}^{m}=-(\Pi^{\circ})^{mn}\omega^{\prime}_{nk}V_{0}^{k}
,
V_{1}[H]=0
and
R_{1}=0
.

4.2
Carter charge

Our next goal is to check the 1PL conservation of the Carter charge,

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{0}&=(Y_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho})^{2}+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+w^{2}+\xi^{2}y^{2}p^{2}\\
&=-pYYp+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+p^{2}(\xi y)^{2}+y^{2}(\xi p)^{2}\,.\end{split}

(73)

The last term in the first line can be discarded, since it commutes with everything.
The
w^{2}
term can be absorbed into the NJ shift of the first term,

\displaystyle(Y_{\nu\rho}(x)p^{\rho})(Y^{\nu\sigma}(x)p_{\sigma})+w^{2}=(Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho})(Y^{\nu\sigma}(\bar{z})p_{\sigma})\,.

(74)

So we may work with a modified Carter charge,

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{0}

\displaystyle=Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho}Y^{\nu\sigma}(\bar{z})p_{\sigma}+2p^{2}[yY(x)\xi]+2(\xi p)[yY(x)p]\,.

(75)

To compute
\delta z^{\mu}
, it is useful to rewrite
\widetilde{C}_{0}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{0}

\displaystyle=Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho}Y^{\nu\sigma}(z)p_{\sigma}+4p^{2}[yY^{+}(z)\xi]+4(\xi p)[yY^{-}(z)p]\,.

(76)

The zigzag property,
\{z^{\mu},z^{\nu}\}=0
, ensures that no derivative of
Y
,
\tilde{Y}
,
Y^{\pm}
is ever produced during the computation of
\delta z^{\mu}=\{z^{\mu},\widetilde{C}_{0}\}
.
After some algebra, we can show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta z^{\mu}&=-2Y^{\mu\nu}(z)Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho}-4\xi^{\mu}\left([yY^{+}(z)p]-[yY^{-}(z)p]\right)\\
&\qquad+4p^{\mu}[yY^{+}(z)\xi]-4y^{\mu}[pY^{+}(z)\xi]+4Y^{+}(z)^{\mu\nu}\left[(\xi y)p_{\nu}-(\xi p)y_{\nu}\right]\\
&=-2Y^{\mu\nu}(z)Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho}+4\xi^{\mu}[yY^{-}(z)p]-4\left(y^{\mu}p^{\nu}-p^{\mu}y^{\nu}+i\epsilon^{\mu\nu}[y,p]\right)Y^{+}_{\nu\rho}(z)\xi^{\rho}\\
&=-2Y^{\mu\nu}(z)Y_{\nu\rho}(z)p^{\rho}+4\xi^{\mu}[yY^{-}(z)p]+4Y^{+}(z)^{\mu\nu}\left[(\xi y)p_{\nu}-(\xi p)y_{\nu}-iw_{\nu}\right]\,.\end{split}

(77)

EoM test - 1st order

The EoM test (
49
) at 1PL gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.\frac{dC_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{1}}&=\frac{q}{m}\sum_{a=\pm}\delta z_{a}^{\mu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})p^{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(78)

Many terms vanish or cancel out, but some survive.
The problematic terms are

\displaystyle(\delta z^{\mu})_{\mathrm{prob}}=4\xi^{\mu}[yY^{-}(z)p]+4(\xi y)Y^{+}(z)^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}\,.

(79)

They lead to

\displaystyle(\delta z^{\mu})_{\mathrm{prob}}F_{\mu\nu}^{+}(z)p^{\nu}=4[\xi F^{+}(z)p][yY^{-}(z)p]-4(\xi y)[pY^{+}(z)F^{+}(z)p]\,.

(80)

Interestingly, we can turn the RHS into a total derivative,
using the following identities:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\xi F^{+}(z)p&=-m\frac{d}{d\tau}[\xi A^{+}(z)]+\mathcal{O}(q)\,,\\
m\frac{d}{d\tau}[yY^{-}(z)p]&=my^{\nu}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[Y^{-}_{\nu\sigma}(z)\right]p^{\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(q)\\
&=-\frac{i}{2}y^{\nu}(p_{\nu}\xi_{\sigma}-\xi_{\nu}p_{\sigma})p^{\sigma}+\mathcal{O}(q)=\frac{i}{2}p^{2}(\xi y)+\mathcal{O}(q)\,,\\
pY^{+}(z)F^{+}(z)p&=\frac{i}{2}p^{2}[\xi A^{+}(z)]\,.\end{split}

(81)

It follows that the Carter charge with a correction term is conserved at 1PL,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left.\frac{d}{d\tau}(C_{0}+qC_{1})\right|_{q^{1}}=0\,,\\
&\qquad C_{1}=4[\xi A^{+}(z)][yY^{-}(z)p]+4[\xi A^{-}(\bar{z})][yY^{+}(\bar{z})p]\,.\end{split}

(82)

We can rewrite
C_{1}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{1}&=2\left[\xi A^{+}(z)+\xi A^{-}(\bar{z})\right]\left([yY(x)p]+y^{2}(\xi p)\right)\\
&\qquad+2i\left[\xi A^{+}(z)-\xi A^{-}(\bar{z})\right][y\tilde{Y}(x)p]\,.\end{split}

(83)

Commutativity of the charges

In the absence of the EM interaction,
one can show that the Rüdiger charge and the Carter charge Poisson-commute,

\displaystyle\{R_{0},C_{0}\}^{\circ}=0\,.

(84)

We should check whether it continues to hold after the interaction is turned on.
At 1PL, we need to verify that

\displaystyle\{R_{0},C_{1}\}^{\circ}-\sum_{a=\pm}\{R_{0},z^{\mu}_{a}\}^{\circ}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})\{z_{a}^{\nu},C_{0}\}^{\circ}=0\,.

(85)

It is convenient to rewrite it as

\displaystyle\sum_{a=\pm}\delta_{R}z_{a}^{\mu}\left(\frac{\partial C_{1}}{\partial z_{a}^{\mu}}-F^{a}_{\mu\nu}(z_{a})\delta_{C}z_{a}^{\nu}\right)=-\frac{\partial C_{1}}{\partial p^{\mu}}\delta_{R}p^{\mu}\,,

(86)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{R}f=\{f,R_{0}\}^{\circ}\,,\quad\delta_{C}f=\{f,C_{0}\}^{\circ}\,.

(87)

Given (
67
), the RHS of (
86
) is fairly simple,

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial C_{1}}{\partial p^{\mu}}\delta_{R}p^{\mu}=\frac{\partial C_{1}}{\partial p^{\mu}}w_{\mu}=4[\xi A^{+}(z)][yY^{-}(z)w]+4[\xi A^{-}(\bar{z})][yY^{+}(\bar{z})w]\,.

(88)

On the LHS of (
86
), a lot of cancellations occur before the contraction with
(\delta_{R}z^{\mu})
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial C_{1}}{\partial z_{\pm}^{\mu}}-F^{\pm}_{\mu\nu}(z_{\pm})\delta_{C}z_{\pm}^{\nu}&=\pm i(\xi A^{\pm})Y^{\pm}_{\mu\nu}(z^{\pm})p^{\nu}+2[F^{\pm}Y^{\mp}(z_{\pm})Y^{\mp}(z_{\pm})]_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}\\
&\quad\mp 2i(\xi A^{\mp})Y^{\pm}_{\mu\nu}(z_{\mp})p^{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(89)

Upon the contraction with
(\delta_{R}z^{\mu})
, only the last term gives a non-vanishing contribution.
The remaining step to verify (
86
) is relatively simple.
The identity (
89
) can be used for other purposes. For example, it can quickly double-check (
82
).

5
Approximate conservation at 2PL

5.1
Rüdiger charge

Suppose we implement the 1PL interaction governed by the NJ shift and (tentativley) do not include
the 2PL deformation in (
36
) yet.
The EoM test at 2PL would give

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{2},\text{undeformed}}&=\frac{4iq^{2}}{m}y^{\rho}\left[Y^{+}_{\rho\sigma}\hat{\eta}^{\sigma\mu}(F^{+}F^{-})_{\mu\nu}-Y^{-}_{\rho\sigma}\hat{\eta}^{\sigma\mu}(F^{-}F^{+})_{\mu\nu}\right]y^{\nu}\\
&=\frac{4iq^{2}}{m}y^{\rho}\left[Y^{+}_{\rho\sigma}-Y^{-}_{\rho\sigma}\right]\hat{\eta}^{\sigma\mu}(F^{+}F^{-})_{\mu\nu}y^{\nu}\,.\end{split}

(90)

The relation (
13
)
F_{\pm}\propto Y_{\pm}
implies that

\displaystyle Y^{+}F^{+}F^{-}\propto Y^{+}Y^{-}Y^{+}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y(Y^{+}F^{+}F^{-})y\propto y(Y^{+}Y^{-}Y^{+})y=0\,.

(91)

So, we would be left with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left.\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{2}}&=-\frac{4iq^{2}}{mp^{2}}y^{\rho}(Y^{+}_{\rho\sigma}-Y^{-}_{\rho\sigma})p^{\sigma}p^{\mu}(F^{+}F^{-})_{\mu\nu}y^{\nu}\\
&=-\frac{4q^{2}}{mp^{2}}[y\tilde{Y}(x)p]\left[pF^{+}(z)F^{-}(\bar{z})y\right]\,.\end{split}

(92)

It is not clear whether/how one can turn the RHS to a total derivative and absorb into the definition of the charge.
As it stands, the RHS signals the violation of the charge conservation at the
\mathcal{O}(q^{2}y^{2})
order.

The situation is improved slightly if we include the 2PL deformation in (
36
). The EoM test now gives

\displaystyle\left.\frac{dR_{0}}{d\tau}\right|_{q^{2},\mathrm{deformed}}=-\frac{2q^{2}}{m}y^{\mu}\{R_{0},[F^{+}(z)F^{-}(\bar{z})]_{\mu\nu}\}y^{\nu}\,.

(93)

We can try to turn the RHS into total derivatives up to
\mathcal{O}(q^{3})
terms;
(
93
) implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(R_{0}+q^{2}R_{2}\right)&=\frac{2iq^{2}}{p^{2}}[y\tilde{Y}(x)p]\left[y\left(\frac{dF^{+}}{d\tau}\right)F^{-}y-yF^{+}\left(\frac{dF^{-}}{d\tau}\right)y\right]+\mathcal{O}(q^{3})\\
&=\frac{2iq^{2}}{mp^{2}}[y\tilde{Y}(x)p]p^{\mu}(h_{\mu}-\bar{h}_{\mu})+\mathcal{O}(q^{3})\,,\end{split}

(94)

where
(h,\bar{h})
are as defined in (
38
), and the correction to the charge is

\displaystyle R_{2}=\frac{2}{p^{2}}\left[yY(x)p-y^{2}(\xi p)\right][yF^{+}(z)F^{-}(\bar{z})y]\,.

(95)

In showing (
94
), we find some non-trivial vanishing of a group of terms:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&y^{\alpha}Y^{+}_{\alpha\beta}(z)h^{\beta}+y^{\alpha}Y^{-}_{\alpha\beta}(\bar{z})\bar{h}^{\beta}=0\,.\end{split}

(96)

The conclusion drawn from (
94
) is that the conservation of
R
can be maintained up to the
q^{2}y^{2}
order,
but it breaks down at the
q^{2}y^{3}
order.
One may wonder whether it is possible to deform the theory further and absorb the RHS of (
94
) into the definition of the charge. In section
6
, we will argue that such a deformation is impossible.

5.2
Carter chage

Our findings regarding the Rüdiger charge suggests that
we can aim for

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}(C_{0}+qC_{1}+q^{2}C_{2})=\mathcal{O}(q^{2}y^{3})\,.

(97)

The relevant EoM is (
37
), which we reproduce here,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{z}^{\mu}&=\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}+\frac{2iq}{m}(F^{-})^{\mu}{}_{\nu}y^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(y^{3})\,,\\
\dot{p}^{\mu}&=\frac{q}{m}\left(F^{+}+F^{-}\right)^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}+\frac{2q^{2}}{m}(h^{\mu}+\bar{h}^{\mu})+\mathcal{O}(y^{3})\,.\end{aligned}

(98)

It is convenient to separate the (energy)
\times
(Rüdiger) terms and define an effective charge.
We should expand
C_{1}
defined in (
83
) in powers of
y
and collect all terms up to
\mathcal{O}(y^{2})
.
The effective charge thus constructed is

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{eff}}=-pYYp+2q\left[(\xi\tilde{A}+\xi Fy)(y\tilde{Y}p)+y^{2}(\xi A)(\xi p)\right]\,.

(99)

We discarded the
2p^{2}(yY\xi)
term which does not contribute at this order.

Taking the time derivative, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dot{C}_{\mathrm{eff}}|_{q^{2}y^{2}}&=X_{A}+q(X_{B}+X_{C}+X_{D})\,,\\
X_{A}&=-2pYY(\dot{p}|_{q^{2}y^{2}})\\
X_{B}&=2(\xi\dot{\tilde{A}}|_{1}+\xi F\dot{y}|_{1})(y\tilde{Y}p)\,,\\
X_{C}&=2(\xi\tilde{A})\left[(\dot{y}|_{1}\tilde{Y}p)+(y\tilde{Y}\dot{p}|_{1})+(y\dot{\tilde{Y}}|_{1}p)\right]\,,\\
X_{D}&=2y^{2}(\xi A)(\xi\dot{p}|_{1})\,.\end{split}

(100)

A bit of algebra shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}mX_{A}&={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-4q^{2}pYY(h+\bar{h})}\,,\\
mX_{B}&=2q(\xi\tilde{F}\tilde{F}y+\xi FFy)(y\tilde{Y}p)={\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}8q(\xi F^{+}F^{-}y)(y\tilde{Y}p)}\,,\\
mX_{C}&=2q(\xi\tilde{A})\left[y^{2}(\xi\tilde{F}p){\color[rgb]{0.75,0.5,0.25}-(\xi y)(y\tilde{F}p)+(\xi y)(y\tilde{F}p)}\right]\,,\\
mX_{D}&=2qy^{2}(\xi A)(\xi Fp)\,.\end{split}

(101)

A partial cancellation between
X_{C}
and
X_{D}
leads to

\displaystyle m(X_{C}+X_{D})=4qy^{2}\left[(\xi A^{+})(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(\xi F^{+}p)\right]\,.

(102)

Since we have expanded
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
up to
y^{2}
terms, we should try to absorb the whole RHS of (
100
) into a
\mathcal{O}(q^{2}y^{2})
correction to the charge (
97
).
We propose an ansatz

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{2}&=a_{1}f_{1}+a_{2}f_{2}\,,\\
f_{1}&=y^{2}(\xi A^{+})(\xi A^{-})\,,\\
f_{2}&=(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{2})(yF^{+}F^{-}y)\,.\end{split}

(103)

The time derivatives of
f_{i}
in (
103
) are
(keeping the leading order terms only)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m\dot{f}_{1}&=-y^{2}\left[(\xi A^{+})(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(\xi F^{+}p)\right]\,,\\
m\dot{f}_{2}&={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-4pYY(h+\bar{h})}\\
&\quad-2y^{2}\left[(\xi A^{+})(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(\xi F^{+}p)\right]\\
&\quad+4(\xi y)\left[(\xi A^{+})(yF^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(yF^{+}p)\right]\\
&\quad{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}-8(\xi\tilde{Y}y)(pF^{+}F^{-}y)+8(\xi\tilde{Y}p)(yF^{+}F^{-}y)}\end{split}

(104)

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&m\left(X_{A}+qX_{B}-q^{2}\dot{f}_{2}\right)\\
&\supset 8\left[(y\tilde{Y}p)(\xi F^{+}F^{-}y)+(\xi\tilde{Y}y)(pF^{+}F^{-}y)+(p\tilde{Y}\xi)(yF^{+}F^{-}y)\right]\\
&=8wYF^{+}F^{-}y=8w(Y^{+}+Y^{-})F^{+}F^{-}y\\
&=4i\left[(\xi A^{+})(wF^{-}y)-(\xi A^{-})(wF^{+}y)\right]\\
&=4(\xi A^{+})[-y^{2}(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi y)(yF^{-}p)]+\mbox{(c.c.)}\,.\end{split}

(105)

Adding the other terms from
\dot{f}_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}m\left(X_{A}+qX_{B}-q^{2}\dot{f}_{2}\right)&=-2q^{2}y^{2}\left[(\xi A^{+})(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(\xi F^{+}p)\right]\,.\end{split}

(106)

Adding the contribution from
X_{C}+X_{D}
(
102
), we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}m\left(X_{A}+qX_{B+C+D}-q^{2}\dot{f}_{2}\right)&=2q^{2}y^{2}\left[(\xi A^{+})(\xi F^{-}p)+(\xi A^{-})(\xi F^{+}p)\right]\,.\end{split}

(107)

But, this can be canceled by
\dot{f}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}m\left(X_{A}+qX_{B+C+D}+q^{2}(2\dot{f}_{1}-\dot{f}_{2})\right)&=0\,.\end{split}

(108)

Thus we have shown that the desired
\mathcal{O}(q^{2}y^{2})
correction to
C
is

\displaystyle C_{2}=2f_{1}-f_{2}=2y^{2}(\xi A^{+})(\xi A^{-})-(\mathcal{R}_{-}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{+}^{2})(yF^{+}F^{-}y)\,.

(109)

Finally, using the identities,

\displaystyle(F^{\pm}Y^{\pm})^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=\pm\frac{i}{2}(\xi A^{\pm})\delta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,,\quad(Y^{\pm}Y^{\pm})_{{\mu}{\nu}}=-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{R}_{\mp}^{2}\eta_{{\mu}{\nu}}\,,

(110)

we obtain a remarkably simple expression for
C_{2}
:

\displaystyle C_{2}=4yF^{+}YYF^{-}y\,.

(111)

In summary, the final form of the Carter charge is

\displaystyle\begin{split}C&=C_{0}+qC_{1}+q^{2}C_{2}\,,\\
C_{0}&=-pYYp+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+p^{2}(\xi y)^{2}+y^{2}(\xi p)^{2}\,,\\
C_{1}&=4[\xi A^{+}(z)][yY^{-}(z)p]+4[\xi A^{-}(\bar{z})][yY^{+}(\bar{z})p]\,,\\
C_{2}&=4yF^{+}YYF^{-}y\,.\end{split}

(112)

It is conserved to all orders in
y
at 1PL, and up to the
y^{2}
order at 2PL.

5.3
Coulomb limit

The original Carter charge for a point particle probe
converges to the angular-momentum-squared
(\vec{L}^{2})
in the Coulomb limit
(a\rightarrow 0)
.
A similar statement holds for the spinning probe.
Recall the expression for the free
(q=0)
Carter charge:

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{0}&=(Y_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho})^{2}+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+w^{2}+\xi^{2}y^{2}p^{2}\,.\end{split}

(113)

In the Coulomb limit, it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle(Y_{\nu\rho}p^{\rho})^{2}=\vec{L}^{2}\,,\quad 2p^{2}(yY\xi)=0\,,\quad 2(\xi p)(yYp)=2\vec{L}\cdot\vec{S}\,,\quad w^{2}+\xi^{2}y^{2}p^{2}=\vec{S}^{2}\,,

(114)

where
\vec{L}
and
\vec{S}
are the orbital and spin angular momenta in the rest frame of the source.
Hence, we arrive at the expected statement,

\displaystyle C_{0}|_{\mathrm{Coulomb}}=\vec{J}^{2}=(\vec{L}+\vec{S})^{2}\,,

(115)

In the Coulomb limit,
C
is not an extra conserved charge. It is rather a combination of Killing momenta.
As such, it is clear how to carry it over to the interacting theory.
We take the formula for the Killing momenta (
59
)
and replace
K^{\mu}
with
Y^{{\mu}{\nu}}
to find

\displaystyle J^{\mu}=Y^{\mu\nu}(x)p_{\nu}+y^{\mu}(\xi p)-p^{\mu}(\xi y)+q\left[Y^{\mu\nu}(z)A^{+}_{\nu}(z)+Y^{\mu\nu}(\bar{z})A^{-}_{\nu}(\bar{z})\right]\,.

(116)

It is a 3-vector in disguise since
\xi\cdot J=0
.
An interesting feature is that
when the
q
-linear term is expanded in
y
, all the even order terms vanish. For example, at
\mathcal{O}(y^{0})
we have
Y^{{\mu}{\nu}}A_{\nu}(x)=0
, at
\mathcal{O}(y^{2})
we have
y^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}(Y^{{\mu}{\rho}}F_{{\rho}{\sigma}})y^{\sigma}=0
and so on.
The odd order terms in
y
can be expressed in terms of
(Y\tilde{F}y)
and its derivatives.

Note that, in the Coulomb limit, the full Carter charge is
exactly
quadratic in
q
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}C=J_{\mu}J^{\mu}&=C_{0}+qC_{1}+q^{2}C_{2}\,,\\
C_{1}&=2\left[Y_{\mu\nu}(x)p^{\nu}+y_{\mu}(\xi p)-p_{\mu}(\xi y)\right]\left[Y^{\mu\rho}(z)A^{+}_{\rho}(z)+Y^{\mu\rho}(\bar{z})A^{-}_{\rho}(\bar{z})\right]\,,\\
C_{2}&=\left[Y_{\mu}{}^{\nu}(z)A^{+}_{\nu}(z)+Y_{\mu}{}^{\nu}(\bar{z})A^{-}_{\nu}(\bar{z})\right]\left[Y^{\mu\rho}(z)A^{+}_{\rho}(z)+Y^{\mu\rho}(\bar{z})A^{-}_{\rho}(\bar{z})\right]\,.\end{split}

(117)

But it seems non-trivial to show that this
C_{1}
agrees with the
C_{1}
(
82
) in the
a\rightarrow 0
limit.
Let us call
C_{1}
in (
117
)
C_{1}
(
J^{2})
and the one in (
82
)
C_{1}
(EoM).
It is straightforward to expand the two in powers of
y
and compare them order by order:

\displaystyle C_{1}=c_{1,0}+c_{1,1}+c_{1,2}+\cdots\,.

(118)

At the 0th order, we find
c_{1,0}\mbox{(EoM)}=0=c_{1,0}(J^{2})
.
At the 1st order, we find

\displaystyle c_{1,1}(J^{2})=2pY(x)Y(x)\tilde{F}(x)y=2[\xi A(x)][yY(x)p]=c_{1,1}\mbox{(EoM)}\,.

(119)

At the 2nd order, we get

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{1,2}(J^{2})&=-2(\xi p)[yY\tilde{F}y]+2(\xi y)[pY\tilde{F}y]\\
&=2(\xi A)(\xi p)y^{2}+2[y\tilde{F}\xi][yYp]-2[yF\xi][y\tilde{Y}p]=c_{1,2}\mbox{(EoM)}\,.\end{split}

(120)

The matching continues to higher order in
y
, but we have not been able to find an all-order-in-
y
proof.
Finally, truncating
C_{2}(J^{2})
at the
y^{2}
order, we confirm the expected matching
at the
q^{2}y^{2}
order,

\displaystyle C_{2}(\mbox{EoM})|_{y^{2}}=y\tilde{F}YY\tilde{F}y=C_{2}(J^{2})|_{y^{2}}\,.

(121)

6
Asymptotic conservation

We turn our attention to the asymptotic conservation in the sense defined in
2
.
By asymptotic conservation we mean that the asymptotic form of the conserved charges, where all interaction terms can be dropped, commute with the radial action under the Poisson-Dirac algebra.
Ref.
2
performed a comprehensive test of the asymptotic conservation covering all PM data available in the literature. In what follows, we will confirm that similar results hold for the root-Kerr source-probe system.

6.1
1PL

Probe lmit

Ref.
2
expresses the conserved charges in terms of asymptotic phase space variables.
In our conventions, the expressions are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{E}&=\gamma\,,\\
\tilde{L}&=l\cdot s_{2}+\gamma(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,,\\
\tilde{R}&=-l\cdot s_{1}+\gamma(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,,\\
\tilde{C}&=l^{2}+2\gamma\,l\cdot(s_{1}-s_{2})+\gamma^{2}(s_{1}-s_{2})^{2}-(s_{1}+s_{2})^{2}\\
&\qquad-(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})^{2}-(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})^{2}-2\gamma(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,.\end{split}

(122)

Here,
s_{i}
are the spin-length vectors
(y_{i}=-s_{i})
, and

\displaystyle l_{\mu}=-\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{\nu}v_{1}^{\rho}v_{2}^{\sigma}\,.

(123)

The tilde in (
122
) denotes rescaling by powers of the probe mass, so that
\tilde{E}
is dimensionless while
\tilde{L}
,
\tilde{R}
,
\tilde{C}
all have a length-squared dimension.
The change of convention from ref.
2
to this note is as follows:

\displaystyle(u\cdot v)_{\mathrm{there}}=-(u\cdot v)_{\mathrm{here}}\,,\quad l_{\mathrm{there}}=-l_{\mathrm{here}}\,,\quad\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{there}}=-\tilde{R}_{\mathrm{here}}\,,\quad y_{\mathrm{there}}=\gamma_{\mathrm{here}}\,.

(124)

Let us check whether (
122
) agrees with the free theory limit of the Noether charges we used in other sections. The energy is trivial. The angular momentum
L
of the probe is

\displaystyle L=x^{1}p^{2}-x^{2}p^{1}+y^{0}p^{3}-y^{3}p^{0}\,.

(125)

With
v_{2}=\xi=(1,0,0,0)
and
s_{2}=(0,0,0,a)
, we find

\displaystyle\tilde{L}=\frac{a}{m_{1}}L\,.

(126)

As for the Rüdiger charge, we note that

\displaystyle R=yY(x)p=\vec{y}\cdot(\vec{x}\times\vec{p})+a(y^{0}p^{3}-y^{3}p^{0})\,.

(127)

It agrees with (
122
) as
\tilde{R}=R/m_{1}
since

\displaystyle-l\cdot s_{1}=\frac{1}{m_{1}}\vec{y}\cdot(\vec{x}\times\vec{p})\,,\quad\gamma(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})=\frac{a}{m_{1}}(y^{0}p^{3}-y^{3}p^{0})\,.

(128)

Finally, recall that the Carter charge is

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=-pYYp+2p^{2}(yY\xi)+2(\xi p)(yYp)+p^{2}(\xi y)^{2}+y^{2}(\xi p)^{2}\,.

(129)

Comparing it with (
122
), we find an agreement up to a constant shift,

\displaystyle\tilde{C}+(s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2})=\frac{1}{m^{2}}C\,.

(130)

Arbitrary mass ratio

In eqs.(37)-(39) of ref.
2
, the asymptotic charges for arbitrary mass ratio are given.
In our conventions,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}&=\frac{1-\rho}{2}(l\cdot s_{1})+\frac{1+\rho}{2}(l\cdot s_{2})+({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nu}+\gamma)(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,,\\
\mathcal{R}&=-\frac{1+\rho}{2}(l\cdot s_{1})-\frac{1-\rho}{2}(l\cdot s_{2})+({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nu}+\gamma)(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,,\\
\mathcal{C}&=l^{2}+2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\rho}\,\gamma\,l\cdot(s_{1}-s_{2})+\gamma^{2}(s_{1}-s_{2})^{2}-(s_{1}+s_{2})^{2}\\
&\qquad-(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})^{2}-(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})^{2}-2\gamma(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})-4{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\nu}\,\gamma(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})\,.\end{split}

(131)

We introduced shorthand notations for dimensionless ratios

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{2m_{1}m_{2}}{m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}\,,\quad\rho=\frac{-m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}{m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}\,.

(132)

Together with the energy
\mathcal{E}=\gamma
, the four charges all Poisson-commute with each other.
In the probe limit
(m_{1}/m_{2}\rightarrow 0)
, the expressions reduce to (
122
) as expected.

It may be useful to work with the linear combinations,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{R}&=l\cdot(s_{1}+s_{2})\\
\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{R}&=-\rho\,l\cdot(s_{1}-s_{2})+2(\nu+\gamma)(s_{1}\cdot s_{2})+2(s_{1}\cdot v_{2})(s_{2}\cdot v_{1})\,.\end{split}

(133)

Radial action

The radial action can be written in terms of the asymptotic charges

2
.
It is well known that the 1PL radial action
\chi_{\mathrm{1PL}}
depends only on
y=y_{1}+y_{2}=-(s_{1}+s_{2})
. Then
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{R}
cannot independently contribute to
\chi_{\mathrm{1PL}}
.
The suitable combination is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}+2\gamma(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{R})&=l^{2}+(\gamma^{2}-1)s^{2}-(s\cdot v_{2})^{2}-(s\cdot v_{1})^{2}+2\gamma(s\cdot v_{2})(s\cdot v_{1})\\
&=(\gamma^{2}-1)(b^{2}+y_{\perp}^{2})\,.\end{split}

(134)

Here, we used
l^{2}=b^{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)
,
s_{a}\cdot v_{a}=0
, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\epsilon_{\mu}[y,v_{1},v_{2}])^{2}&=y_{\perp}^{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)\\
&=(\gamma^{2}-1)y^{2}-(y\cdot v_{2})^{2}-(y\cdot v_{1})^{2}+2\gamma(y\cdot v_{2})(y\cdot v_{1})\,.\end{split}

(135)

We also note that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{R}}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}=-\frac{\epsilon[b,y,v_{1},v_{2}]}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\,,\qquad\left|\frac{\epsilon[b,y,v_{1},v_{2}]}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\right|=\sqrt{b^{2}y_{\perp}^{2}-(b\cdot y_{\perp})^{2}}\,.

(136)

The final result for
\chi_{\mathrm{1PL}}
is
24

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi_{\mathrm{1PL}}&=\frac{q_{1}q_{2}}{8\pi}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}+\sigma\right)\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{C}+2\gamma(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{R})}{\gamma^{2}-1}+\sigma\frac{2(\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{R})}{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\right)\\
&=\frac{q_{1}q_{2}}{8\pi\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}e^{\sigma\theta}\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{C}+2(e^{\sigma\theta}\mathcal{L}+e^{-\sigma\theta}\mathcal{R})}{\gamma^{2}-1}\right)\,,\quad\gamma=\cosh\theta\,,\;\theta>0\,.\end{split}

(137)

In the same notation, the 1PM classical eikonal for a pair of Kerr black holes is
2

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{1PM}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{2\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}}\sum_{\sigma=\pm 1}e^{2\sigma\theta}\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{C}+2(e^{\sigma\theta}\mathcal{L}+e^{-\sigma\theta}\mathcal{R})}{\gamma^{2}-1}\right)\,.

(138)

6.2
2PL

In this subsection, we check the asymptotic conservation at 2PL, order by order in spin,
using the classical eikonal computed in
24
.
We take the probe limit
(m_{2}\rightarrow\infty)
of the 2PL eikonal and expand the result in powers of spin:
y_{1}^{k_{1}}y_{2}^{k_{2}}
up to
k_{1}+k_{2}\leq 3
. We remove an overall coefficient by defining

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathrm{2PL}}(m_{2}\rightarrow\infty)=\frac{(q_{1}q_{2})^{2}}{32\pi m_{1}(\gamma^{2}-1)^{1/2}}F(b,y_{1},y_{2},v_{1},v_{2})\,.

(139)

Before we expand
F
in powers of spin,
we prepare some building blocks for the algebra that we need to establish charge conservation.
We discuss the Rüdiger charge first and proceed to the Carter charge.
Since
F
carries some powers of
|b|
, we need

\displaystyle\{R,b^{2}\}

\displaystyle=-2(b\cdot y_{1})(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})-2(b\cdot y_{2})(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})\,.

(140)

The dot products appear in the numerators of
F
. So, we also need

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{R,b\cdot y_{1}\}&=(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})(b^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-y_{1}\cdot y_{2})+\epsilon[b,v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}-1}\left[(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})^{3}-\gamma(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})^{2}(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})\right]\,,\\
\{R,b\cdot y_{2}\}&=-(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})(y_{1}\cdot y_{2}+y_{2}^{2})-\epsilon[b,v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\\
&\qquad+\frac{(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})}{\gamma^{2}-1}\left[(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})-\gamma(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})\right]\,,\\
\{R,y_{1}\cdot v_{2}\}&=-(\gamma^{2}-1)(b\cdot y_{1})-\gamma\,\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\,,\\
\{R,y_{2}\cdot v_{1}\}&=-\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\,.\end{split}

(141)

For the epsilon products on the RHS, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{R,\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\}&=(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]-(b\cdot y_{1})(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})-\gamma(b\cdot y_{2})(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})\\
&=-\{R,\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]\}\,,\\
\{R,\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\}&=(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})y_{1}^{2}+(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})^{2}(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})+\gamma(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})\\
&\quad+(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})+\gamma(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})y_{2}^{2}\,.\end{split}

(142)

For the bi-linears of
y_{1,2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\{R,y_{1}^{2}\}&=0\,,\\
\{R,y_{2}^{2}\}&=0\,,\\
\{R,y_{1}\cdot y_{2}\}&=(b\cdot y_{2})(y_{1}\cdot v_{2})+\gamma(b\cdot y_{1})(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})+(y_{2}\cdot v_{1})\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]\,.\end{split}

(143)

We have five vectors
(b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2})
. Out of the five
\epsilon
products, only three are independent,
since we can use the Schouten identity to show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma\,\epsilon[b,v_{1},y_{1},y_{2}]-\epsilon[b,v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]&=(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\,,\\
-\epsilon[b,v_{1},y_{1},y_{2}]+\gamma\,\epsilon[b,v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]&=(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]\,.\end{split}

(144)

The dot products and the
\epsilon
products form a complete set of building blocks.

Now, we list the expansion of
F
in powers of
y_{1,2}
.
The spinless part is

\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{0}\equiv F|_{y^{0}}=\frac{1}{|b|}\,.\end{split}

(145)

The linear in spin term is

\displaystyle F|_{y^{1}}=\frac{\gamma}{(\gamma^{2}-1)|b|^{3}}\left(\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]+2\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]\right)\,.

(146)

The quadratic term is

\displaystyle F|_{y^{2}}=\frac{1}{4(\gamma^{2}-1)^{2}|b|^{3}}(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{NJ}}+\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{contact}})\,,

(147)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{NJ}}=&(3\gamma^{4}-11\gamma^{2}+2)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+(-13\gamma^{2}+7)(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}\\
&\quad\quad\quad+4(4\gamma^{2}-1)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})\\
&+2(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[(3\gamma^{2}-1)y_{1}^{2}+(5\gamma^{2}-3)y_{2}^{2}+(8\gamma^{2}-4)(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})\right]\\
&-\frac{3}{b^{2}}[(3\gamma^{4}-4\gamma^{2}+1)(b\cdot y_{1})^{2}+(5\gamma^{4}-8\gamma^{2}+3)(b\cdot y_{2})^{2}\\
&\quad\quad\quad+4(2\gamma^{4}-3\gamma^{2}+1)(b\cdot y_{1})(b\cdot y_{2})]\,,\end{split}

(148)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{contact}}=(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[(4-3\gamma^{2})(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}-2(\gamma^{2}-1)y_{1}^{2}+\frac{3}{b^{2}}(\gamma^{2}-1)(b\cdot y_{1})^{2}\right]\,.\end{split}

(149)

Adding the non-contact and contact terms, we obtain the final form of
\mathcal{K}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{all}}&=A_{11}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+A_{22}(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}+A_{12}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})\\
&\quad+2(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[C_{11}y_{1}^{2}+C_{22}y_{2}^{2}+C_{12}(y_{1}\cdot y_{2})\right]\\
&\quad-\frac{3}{b^{2}}\left[D_{11}(b\cdot y_{1})^{2}+D_{22}(b\cdot y_{2})^{2}+D_{12}(b\cdot y_{1})(b\cdot y_{2})\right]\,,\end{split}

(150)

with the coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{11}=-4\gamma^{2}-2\,,\quad&A_{22}=-13\gamma^{2}+7\,,\quad A_{12}=4\gamma(4\gamma^{2}-1)\,,\\
C_{11}=2\gamma^{2}\,,\quad&C_{22}=5\gamma^{2}-3\,,\qquad C_{12}=4(2\gamma^{2}-1)\,,\\
D_{11}=2\gamma^{2}(\gamma^{2}-1)\,,\quad&D_{22}=5\gamma^{4}-8\gamma^{2}+3\,,\quad D_{12}=4(2\gamma^{4}-3\gamma^{2}+1)\,.\end{split}

(151)

Similarly, the cubic term is given by

\displaystyle F|_{y^{3}}=\frac{3}{4(\gamma^{2}-1)^{2}|b|^{5}}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{NJ}}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{contact}})\,,

(152)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{NJ}}=&2\gamma(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[(b\cdot y)\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]+y^{2}[2\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]]\right]\\
&+2[\gamma(\gamma^{3}-3)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}-3\gamma(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}\\
&+(-\gamma^{3}+5\gamma^{2}+1)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})]\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]\\
&+[\gamma(\gamma^{2}-4)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+\gamma(2\gamma^{2}-2\gamma-5)(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}\\
&+2(-\gamma^{4}+\gamma^{3}+3\gamma^{2}+1)(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})]\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\\
&-\frac{5}{b^{2}}\gamma(\gamma^{2}-1)(b\cdot y)^{2}[2\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]]\,,\end{split}

(153)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{contact}}=-2(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\right]\,.\end{split}

(154)

Adding the two terms, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{all}}=&2\gamma(\gamma^{2}-1)\left[(b\cdot y)\epsilon[v_{1},v_{2},y_{1},y_{2}]+y^{2}[2\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]]\right]\\
&+2[{E}_{11}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+{E}_{22}(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}+{E}_{12}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})]\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]\\
&+[\tilde{E}_{11}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+\tilde{E}_{22}(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})^{2}+\tilde{E}_{12}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})(v_{1}\cdot y_{2})]\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\\
&-\frac{5}{b^{2}}\gamma(\gamma^{2}-1)(b\cdot y)^{2}[2\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{2}]+\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]]\,,\end{split}

(155)

with the coefficients

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{11}=\gamma^{4}-\gamma^{2}-3\gamma+1\,,\quad E_{22}=-3\gamma\,,\quad E_{12}=-\gamma^{3}+5\gamma+1\,,\\
&\tilde{E}_{11}=\gamma(\gamma^{2}-4)\,,\quad\tilde{E}_{22}=\gamma(2\gamma^{2}-2\gamma-5)\,,\quad\tilde{E}_{12}=2(-\gamma^{4}+3\gamma^{2}+\gamma+1)\,.\end{split}

(156)

A computation shows that
\{C,F+F_{\text{contact}}\}
vanishes up to
y^{3}
.
In contrast,
we obtain a simple non-zero contribution from
\{R,F+F_{\text{contact}}\}

\displaystyle\{R,F+F_{\text{contact}}\}|_{y^{3}}=-\frac{3\gamma(b\cdot y_{1})(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]}{2b^{5}}\,.

(157)

Our final question is whether we can add a new
y_{1}^{3}
term to
F
, attributable to an additional contact term, to restore conservation. Specifically, we work with an ansatz,

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{new}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{new}}}{|b|^{5}}\,,\qquad N_{\mathrm{new}}\propto b\,y_{1}^{3}\,,

(158)

such that

\displaystyle\{C,F_{\mathrm{new}}\}|_{y^{3}}=0\,,\quad\{R,F_{\mathrm{new}}\}|_{y^{3}}=+\frac{3\gamma(b\cdot y_{1})(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]}{2b^{5}}\,.

(159)

The most general possibility for
N_{\text{new}}
is

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{new}}=\left(\alpha_{1}y_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}(v_{2}\cdot y_{1})^{2}+\alpha_{3}\frac{(b\cdot y_{1})^{2}}{b^{2}}\right)\epsilon[b,v_{1},v_{2},y_{1}]\,.

(160)

The coefficients
\alpha_{1,2,3}
do not affect the conservation of the Carter charge up to the
y^{3}
order. The restoration of the conservation of the Rüdiger charge imposes one constraint on
\alpha_{2,3}

and no constraint on
\alpha_{1}
.

It remains to see if the desired linear combination of the
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
terms can be produced by a new interaction vertex of our world-line model.
The exhaustive list of the interaction vertices, in terms of Hamiltonian deformation, are

\displaystyle H_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left[\beta_{1}G_{1}+\beta_{2}G_{2}+\beta_{3}G_{3}+\beta_{4}G_{4}\right]\,.

(161)

The functions
G_{i}
(
i=1,2,3,4
) are

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{1}&=iy^{\mu}\left[(y\partial_{\mu}F^{+}p)(yF^{-}p)-(yF^{+}p)(y\partial_{\mu}F^{-}p)\right]\,,\\
G_{2}&=ip^{2}\,y^{\mu}\left(y\partial_{\mu}F^{+}F^{-}y-yF^{+}\partial_{\mu}F^{-}y\right)\,,\\
G_{3}&=iy^{2}y^{\mu}\left(p\partial_{\mu}F^{+}F^{-}p-pF^{+}\partial_{\mu}F^{-}p\right)\,,\\
G_{4}&=iy^{2}p^{\mu}\left(y\partial_{\mu}F^{+}F^{-}p-yF^{+}\partial_{\mu}F^{-}p\right)\,.\end{split}

(162)

Here, all
F^{\pm}
,
\partial_{\mu}F^{\pm}
are evaluated at
x^{\mu}
.
The contribution of a vertex factor to the classical eikonal can be computed using the elementary formula
24
;
34
:

\displaystyle\chi_{\text{contact}}=-\int H_{\text{contact}}d\tau\,.

(163)

The field configuration to enter the vertex factors (
162
) is

\displaystyle F^{\pm}_{\mu\nu}(x)=-\frac{q_{2}}{8\pi|\vec{x}|^{2}}\left((x\wedge v_{2})_{\mu\nu}\mp i\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu}[x,v_{2}]\right)\,,

(164)

where

\displaystyle x=b+v_{1}\tau\,,\quad|\vec{x}|^{2}=x^{2}+(x\cdot v_{2})^{2}\,.

(165)

In the end, all four vertex factors (
162
) produce no
\alpha_{2,3}
type of terms in (
160
) while producing some
\alpha_{1}
type terms, which do not affect the conservation of the Rüdiger charge.
We conclude that it is impossible to restore the conservation of the Rüdiger charge under the assumptions of this paper.

7
Discussion

For a root-Kerr source-probe system, our analysis shows that
the Carter and Rüdiger charges
are conserved to all orders in spin at 1PL, and up to the spin-squared order at 2PL.
under the assumptions of this paper.
The failure of conservation at the spin-cubic order,
and a parallel problem at the 2PM
S^{5}
order in asymptotic conservation
2
,
are reminiscent of the observation in ref.
4
;
based on the structure of the Compton amplitudes, it was shown
that charged particles with (quantum) spin
s\geq 3/2
and any gravitating particle with spin
s\geq 5/2
cannot be elementary.
The analysis of this paper was purely classical and made no direct contact with quantum amplitudes,
but it would be interesting to explore possible connections following,
e.g.
, refs.
22
;
9
;
21
;
10
.

Even an approximate conservation can be useful in solving the EoM, either analytically or numerically
50
;
17
;
47
;
46
;
41
.
On the analytic side, the radial action (or classical eikonal) depends on the conserved charges.
For a Kerr black hole background, the radial action was computed for orbits in the equatorial plane in
14
,
and for generic orbits in
19
.
In a parallel way, the explicit form of the Carter and Rüdiger charges in this paper, including the perturbative corrections, could be used to compute the radial action of the root-Kerr probe.

One notable remaining question is whether the asymptotic conservation up to the
\mathcal{O}(G^{2}S^{4})
order observed in ref.
2
would lead to the stronger notion of local conservation.
At 1PM, the test of conservation would require an all order in spin world-line action based on the non-linear NJ shift constructed in refs.
29
;
31
;
32
.
At 2PM, the enumeration of all relevant curvature-squared and/or higher derivative terms is likely to pose a challenge.

In the self-dual sector, recent works
29
;
31
;
32
found exact conservation of both Rüdiger and Carter to all orders in
spin. It will be interesting to understand how exactly the ASD part of the root-Kerr field profile (an ASD dyon
30
;
27
) spoils the exact conservation of the SD sector to reproduce the conclusion of
this paper.
