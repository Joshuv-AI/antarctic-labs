Efficacy of different treatments in regulating apple preservation: systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Jie Hong, Li Yang, Kuai Chen, Huimei Zhang, Yudie Yang, Wei Xue
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Regarding the postharvest preservation treatment of apples, this study employed the network Meta-analysis (NMA) to systematically evaluate the regulatory effects of 48 preservation treatments, and verified them through storage experiments. Based on results obtained from the NMA, methyl jasmonate (MeJA) and pullulan polysaccharide (PUL) showed significant gain effects on multiple indicators. This study further combined the use of these two substances, and the results showed that the combined treatment could significantly reduce the weight loss of apples, inhibit enzymatic browning, and maintain the contents of total solids (TSS), total acidity (TA), vitamin C (VC), and total phenolic content (TPC), enhance the activity of antioxidant system enzymes, reduce the accumulation of H2O2, O2− and MDA, and simultaneously inhibit the respiratory rate and ethylene release rate. The compound preservation scheme obtained through screening can provide practical technical references.

Introduction

Apples (Malus domestica) are one of the most widely grown fruits in temperate regions of the world, with high yields and playing an important role in the global economy (Alberti et al., 2016; Bhat et al., 2023). However, fresh fruits may face various biological or non-biological diseases during harvesting, transportation, and storage, which can affect the color, quality, and taste of apple fruits. This not only damages the shelf life of apples, but also leads to a decrease in market value, resulting in significant economic losses (Nabila & Soufiyan, 2020). Therefore, it is necessary to adopt proactive apple storage and preservation strategies. To ensure the freshness of fruits, various methods have been developed to prevent apple oxidation and discoloration, including the use of anti-browning agents (Fan, 2023), polysaccharide-based materials (Dou et al., 2024), short-term hot water treatment (Di Francesco et al., 2022; Park et al., 2023), plasma treatment (Chen et al., 2020), photodynamic treatment (Tao et al., 2019), cellulose nanofiber treatment (Wang, Lu, et al., 2023), improved modified atmosphere packaging (Cortellino et al., 2015; Putnik et al., 2017), sea buckthorn polysaccharides (Li et al., 2024), and the use of edible coatings (Pantić et al., 2025). However, most studies only focus on direct comparisons under a single research system within a limited time frame, which makes it difficult to quantitatively compare the actual effectiveness between different groups of treatments. Even if traditional Meta-analysis is used for statistics, it is difficult to overcome the adverse effects of data heterogeneity. Therefore, it is extremely necessary to construct a network structure that includes indirect comparisons to systematically screen, analyze, and summarize green and efficient preservation methods.

The most commonly used research models in the field of apple post-harvest preservation that can be directly used for Meta-analysis and modeling include dynamic prediction models, moisture and adsorption/desorption models, environmental and storage system models, treatment effect evaluation and heterogeneity analysis models, etc. The Bayesian random effects model is one of the models for treatment effect evaluation and heterogeneity analysis. Its core feature is based on the Bayesian probability framework to handle heterogeneity and quantify parameter uncertainty, adapting to the research scenarios of “multiple storage conditions/multiple studies with differences in effect quantities, and indicator parameters easily affected by environmental interference” in preservation research. Compared with the classical frequency random effect model, it is more in line with the actual needs of agricultural product preservation research (Soriano et al., 2017; Van Boekel, 2024). In the field of preservation, Bayesian random effects models can accurately characterize multi-source heterogeneity in preservation research, achieve robust inference and probabilistic risk quantification under limited sample sizes, and support multi evidence fusion and complex dynamic modeling, providing scientific and reliable evidence-based support for the effect evaluation, strategy screening, and shelf life prediction of fruit post-harvest preservation technology (Rajestary et al., 2021).

This study used a Bayesian random effects model for NMA, with CiteSpace 6.4.R1 as the retrieval tool. After systematically reviewing existing processing methods, a specialized database centered on apple preservation was established. A total of 12 research models, 48 processing methods, and 21 quality indicator variables were included. The Surface Under the Cumulative Ranking Area (SUCRA) curve was used to quantitatively rank the effects of each treatment. Overall, the purpose of this experiment is to clarify the following: 1. Compare various processing methods in the dataset and select the most effective processing method for each indicator. 2. Based on the analysis results, explore potential combination measures and validate them to further investigate the practical effectiveness of applying NMA. 3. Explore the practical reliability of combined preservation strategies and uncover the underlying mechanisms of combined treatment. Based on the above, this method is used to identify the optimal processing method for various preservation indicators of apple fruits, providing reference for future research and extending apple shelf life.

Materials and methods

Incorporate literature search strategies and screening criteria

In order to comprehensively collect relevant research data, CiteSpace 6.4.R1 was used for bibliometric analysis. Bibliometric analysis was based on quantitative methods, using Boolean operators (AND and OR) and wildcard characters to detect literature containing different combinations of selected keywords, including singular and plural forms (Akin et al., 2025). This approach can effectively avoid interpretation bias of different data (Ricroch et al., 2024). Citespace 6.4.R1 can utilize its built-in independent algorithm module to perform keyword co-occurrence and clustering analysis. In terms of search terms, “Apple preservation”, “Processing”, “Storage”, “Stocking” and “Apple transportation” were selected, which can accurately locate research related to apple preservation, processing, storage, inventory and transportation. In order to include relevant studies as comprehensively as possible, the references included in the literature were also traced. In the selection of databases for retrieval, PubMed, Web of Science and the US Department of Agriculture's Public Agricultural Literature Database were included (https://pubag.nal.usda.gov/), as well as the three commonly used Chinese databases in China: CNKI, Wanfang and Weipu. In terms of publication time, the search scope was set from the date of establishment of each database to Jun 2026. At the same time, the language of the literature would be limited to Chinese and English to ensure that valuable research results can be obtained in different language environments.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for literature

To ensure the reliability of the research results, strict inclusion and exclusion criteria were established in this study. Literature inclusion focused on published randomized controlled trials of apple post-harvest preservation. The research subjects were fresh apples that had not been intervened with chemical or biological agents before harvesting, while the control group apples were soaked or sprayed with irrigation water, sterile water, or distilled water after harvesting. The experimental duration should not be less than 7 d and the experimental design includes parallel group and crossover group designs, with no restrictions on apple varieties to enhance the universality of the results. The study required a complete report of the specific values or averages of each indicator, as well as the standard deviation (SD) or standard error (SE), to provide detailed data for analysis. It is worth noting that in terms of literature exclusion, this study first removed duplicate literature from the database to avoid data redundancy. To ensure the validity of the data and the specificity of the dataset, we also removed conference proceedings, dissertations, reviews, and survey reports. In addition, reports without a blank control group and incomplete reported data were also excluded. English literature would only include articles published in Science Citation Index (SCI) journals with an impact factor of ≥4 to ensure research quality. This study included both single and combined treatment studies. All data and related information extraction work was independently completed by Jie Hong, Li Yang and Kuai Chen, with double checks conducted by other authors. If there were any objections, they would be discussed in a collective meeting for standardization.

Outcome indicators

This study extracted quality indicators (Group A) and physiological metabolic indicators (Group B) from the final included studies. Group A included browning index (BI, Unit: %), color parameters (L*, a* and b* value), weight loss (Unit: %), firmness (Unit: N), total soluble solids (TSS, Unit: %), titratable acid (TA, Unit: %), VC (Unit: mg/100 g), ascorbic acid (ASA, mg/100 g), total phenolics content (TPC, Unit: mg/100 g) and pH value; Group B included respiratory rate (Unit: μmol·kg−1·h−1), ethylene release rate (Unit: μmol·kg−1·h−1), MDA (Unit: μmol/g), O2− (Unit: mmol·g−1·min−1), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content (Unit: μmol/g), and four key enzyme activities (Unit: U·g−1·FW), namely POD, SOD, CAT and PPO. Given the differences in indicator measurement methods, measurement time, and measurement units among the included literature, this study uniformly selected the change values of each treatment group relative to the control group after storage for 7 ± 1 d, and used the Standardized Mean Difference (SMD) as the effect size indicator to quantify the impact of different preservation treatments on apple post-harvest quality and physiological metabolism, ensuring the scientific and reliable cross study comparison. It was worth noting that for indicators such as BI, weight loss, respiratory rate, ethylene release rate, MDA, O2−, and H2O2 content that were not conducive to fruit preservation, lower values were better, while higher values were better for other indicators that were beneficial to fruit preservation. Due to the fact that Group A mainly focused on the characterization and general characteristics of fruits, while Group B mainly included metabolism and enzyme activity, for the convenience of organization, the two groups would be analyzed separately. When selecting the specific method of combination treatment in the future, the standard of “SUCRA evaluation has the highest frequency among the top five treatments within the range of all indicators” and the method of retrospective source literature will be used to formulate.

Statistical analysis

All network Meta-analyses, evidence network construction, heterogeneity assessment, and inconsistency testing were performed using Stata 16.0 (Stata Corp LLC, College Station, Texas, USA). The built-in network command module was used to generate network graphs, pool effect sizes, quantify inter-study heterogeneity, implement global inconsistency test and node-splitting analysis for consistency assumption verification. Firstly, a network relationship diagram was constructed for direct and indirect comparisons among various preservation treatments; Subsequently, a random effects model (REM) was used to merge the continuous variables, and a Bayesian model was further applied to enhance the robustness and reliability of the results. It should be noted that the fixed effects model assumes that all the real intervention effects included in the study are completely consistent, and the total variation only came from sampling errors within a single study; The random effects model can simultaneously consider the sampling fluctuations within the study and the objective heterogeneity of real intervention effects between different studies. In addition, the pre analysis of this study showed that almost all detection indicators had extremely high inter study heterogeneity, indicating that there were real differences in the preservation effect between different experiments, which were not simply caused by random sampling. If a fixed effects model is used in this situation, it would underestimate the uncertainty of the results, generate narrow confidence intervals, and result in biased combined effects, which does not meet statistical requirements. Therefore, all indicators in this study adopted a random effects model to reasonably accommodate objective variability between studies. On this basis, we drew forest plots and effect confidence intervals for comparing each treatment with each other; Finally, the SUCRA value quantified and ranked the effectiveness of each treatment, and generates corresponding ranking charts of the effects. Typically, Meta-analysis evaluated the degree of heterogeneity in effect size distribution or the width of its associated confidence interval. However, the ranking of SUCRA and the correct interpretation of its confidence interval depended not only on its width, but also on the range of correlations it crosses.

Verification related to joint processing

Experimental materials

The apples used in the experiment were sourced from the apple planting base in Jinsha, Bijie City, which was under the jurisdiction of Guizhou Provincial Academy of Agricultural Sciences. The sizes were similar and the textures were uniform. They were immediately transported to the laboratory after being picked. Selected apple fruits that were free from mechanical damage, pests and diseases, and had consistent color and size for validation testing. Whole fruit group: 120 apple fruits were randomly divided into 4 groups, with 30 fruits in each group. Each group had 3 replicates, with 10 fruits in each replicate. Fresh cutting group: Selected 120 apple fruits according to the above method and cut them open for subsequent experiments.

Experimental method

Based on the results of Meta-analysis, corresponding joint treatment experiments were conducted, and the specific design of the validation experiment was considered as one of the Meta-analysis results. The reagents used, relevant concentrations, and treatment methods were further determined after obtaining statistical results. The appearance characterization and determination of L*, a*, b* values were carried out on the apple fruits treated with advantageous preparations. Relevant quality indicators and antioxidant indicators were also measured. For specific measurement methods, please refer to the supporting materials.

Results and analysis

Literature screening results and basic characteristics of included studies

As shown in Fig. 1A, before conducting the analysis, this study created a keyword co-occurrence graph based on CiteSpace: Firstly, literature data was imported from databases such as Web of Science and CNKI, and a time range (2006–2026) and time slices (each slice with a year interval of 5) were set; Secondly, the node type was selected as “keyword”, and threshold parameters (g index k = 25, LRF = 25, L/N = 10, LBY = 5, e = 1.0) were set, and Pathfinder was selected for pruning; Finally, the frequency of keyword occurrence was represented by the size of the nodes (larger nodes indicate higher attention to the topic), and the research correlations within the same time period were presented through connections of the same color. At the same time, these relationships were displayed by time slices, thus completing the drawing of the node graph for related keywords on apple preservation and visualizing the evolution trend of hotspots. The evolution pattern of the keywords in this study had a clear progressive correspondence with the processing categories included in the subsequent network Meta-analysis: 2006–2015, the domain research focused on basic preservation methods such as storage regulation and ethylene inhibition, corresponding to the basic control treatment types set in this NMA; After 2016, chitosan coating preservation gradually became the core hotspot in the field, forming a direct response to the series of chitosan composite coating intervention schemes in this study's network analysis; 2021–2025, the research focus shifted to antioxidant regulation and apple polyphenol metabolism regulation, providing a basis for the quantitative ranking of efficacy of the treatments included in this study (Group B intervention of exogenous stimulants, plant extract immersion, and polyphenol-based preservation treatments). In summary, the intervention groups in this NMA were not randomly set but fully covered the mainstream technical paths that emerged and developed in this field over the past 20 years, demonstrating the representativeness and scientific nature of the treatment group system in this study.Fig. 1Using CiteSpace for literature inclusion screening flowchart processing and dataset logical modeling (A). Specifically, this study divided the period from 2006 to 2026 into 5 time periods to analyze the evolution patterns of key words, and to reveal the complete development process and internal driving forces in the field of fresh-cut apple preservation: 2006–2010: The research began. The focus was on the basic needs of post-harvest preservation of fresh-cut apples, with the core goal of extending the shelf life through simple preservation methods; 2011–2015: The drawbacks of single physical regulation became prominent. The academic community shifted to the research on edible coating materials, and chitosan coating, due to its ability to inhibit ethylene production and delay quality deterioration, became the mainstream technical direction; 2016–2020: The research entered the stage of effect optimization, focusing on shelf quality and quantitative evaluation of preservation effects, while initially exploring the physiological mechanism of delaying fruit aging; 2021–2025: The research delved deeper into the internal mechanisms, focusing on antioxidant metabolism, regulation of apple endogenous polyphenols, and regulation of reactive oxygen balance for mechanism analysis. The research focused shifted from “description of preservation effect phenomena” to “explanation of physiological molecular mechanisms”; In 2026, the frontier trend: low-oxygen stress responsed and the application of natural active extracted had becoming emerging hotspots, representing the current latest research development direction for fresh-cut apple preservation. Overall, the evolution path of the field was clearly presented, and the changes in temporal keywords reflected the changes in the research focused and depth of this field.

After analyzing the above information, we conducted subsequent statistics based on NMA. this study included randomized controlled trials published in relevant journals from their inception to Jun 2026. 1539 duplicate records were removed from a total of 3233 retrieved records, and 215 irrelevant articles were excluded after reading the titles and abstracts, while 1479 articles were retained. After excluding conference papers, Meta-analyses and reviews, articles that did not match the research subjects, interventions, full-text articles, low-quality articles, articles that did not report SD, and full-text articles were excluded. A total of 59 randomized controlled trials were included, and 12 studies were ultimately used for NMA (Table 1). The screening process for included studies was shown in Fig. 1B. The comparison results between treatments under each indicator were shown in the forest plots in Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5. In addition, regarding the high heterogeneity commonly present in this study, it was speculated that it was mainly caused by multiple confounding factors in experiments and methodology: 1 The treatment methods included in the study vary greatly, covering different preservation methods such as coating, plasma, and exogenous elicitors, with different treatment formulas, concentrations, and duration of action; 2. There were significant differences in the fruit variety, harvest maturity, and initial physiological background used in the experiment; 3. Inconsistent storage conditions and sampling time for indicator testing after processing; 4. Inconsistent testing procedures, reagent ratios, and operating standards in various research laboratories have resulted in systematic deviations in biochemical indicator testing. This study used standardized mean difference (SMD) as the combined effect indicator. In addition to determining statistical significance, the actual size of the effect was defined according to Cohen's criteria: (|SMD| < 0.2) was considered a weak effect, (0.2 ≤ |SMD| < 0.5) was considered a small effect, (0.5 ≤ |SMD| < 0.8) was considered a moderate effect, and (|SMD| ≥ 0.8) was considered a large effect. Specific settings and results of the verification experiment

Selection of reagents and concentrations

After calculating the performance scores of all treatments under each indicator, the analysis results of the comprehensive dataset showed that the ‘soaked in 1500 μmol/L MeJA solution for 5 min’ (ranked first 5 times, second 2 time, and fifth 1 time) had a significant effect; In addition, ‘Soaked in a 1.5% pullulan polysaccharide solution for 7 min’ (ranked first 1 time, top three 2 times, and top five for 1 times), the two treatment methods mentioned above have the highest comprehensive score and appear the most frequently. As mentioned above, both treatment options have consistently shown excellent performance in multiple indicators. After reviewing the original literature and referring to the treatment methods in the original literature, the treatment reagents and concentrations for this experiment were determined to be 1.5 mmol/L MeJA and 1.5% pullulan polysaccharide solution. CK: Do not take any action; PUL: Soaked in 1.5% pullulan polysaccharide solution; MeJA: Soaked in 1500 μmol/L MeJA solution; PUL + MeJA: Joint processing. This experiment did not characterize the whole fruit by photography. Through preliminary research, it was found that there was no significant difference in the degree of water loss and color change of the whole fruit during storage (P > 0.05).

Characterization of fresh cut apples after different treatments

From the appearance changes of fresh-cut apples in Fig. 7, it can be seen that during the initial storage period (0–2 d), the flesh of each group of apples remained bright and creamy yellow, with full and compact cut surfaces, and no obvious wrinkles or browning; As the storage time was extended to 4–6 d, the degree of flesh browning in the T1 (CK) group significantly increased, with obvious dark brown patches appearing at the edges of the cut surface, and the color dullness was significantly higher than that of other treatment groups. At the same time, the flesh began to wilt and shrink. In contrast, the T2 (PUL), T3 (MeJA), T4 (PUL + MeJA) group, although the glossiness slightly decreased and the base color slightly transitioned to light yellow, still maintained its original bright color without obvious browning, and the flesh only showed slight signs of skin shrinkage; On the 8th d of storage, the browning of the T1 group's fruit flesh had fully spread, appearing dark brown overall, with a dry and collapsed texture, rough folds, and complete loss of the original texture of freshly cut apples. The T2 group's cut edges showed slight light browning, and the compactness of the fruit flesh slightly decreased, However, the T3 and T4 groups' fruit flesh remained mainly pale yellow, with only a slightly dull color and extremely light browning degree. At the same time, the overall fruit flesh still maintained good plumpness without obvious shrinkage. Fruit quality and antioxidant related indicators

During the 20-day storage period after apple harvest, compared with the CK, PUL, and MeJA single treatment groups, the PUL + MeJA combined treatment showed significant regulatory effects on various fruit quality indicators. In terms of weight loss rate, the combined treatment group only lost 4.5% of its weight after 20 d of storage; in terms of maintaining fruit flesh hardness, the fruit flesh hardness of the combined treatment group reached 60.0 N at the end of storage; in terms of flavor substance accumulation, its TSS content reached 13%; in terms of acid change, the combined treatment group had 0.35% of acid, which was higher than 0.28% of the CK group and 0.32% and 0.34% of the PUL and MeJA groups respectively; in terms of nutrient activity retention, the PUL + MeJA also had the best effect, with the highest VC content of 6 mg/100 g FW after 20 d, significantly reducing the oxidation loss of VC; in terms of antioxidant activity retention, the total phenolic content of the combined treatment group was 3.5 mg GAE/g, 40% higher than that of the control group (2.5 mg GAE/g), and higher than that of the PUL group (0.32%) and the MeJA group (0.34%); in terms of acid-base balance regulation, the pH of the PUL + MeJA group was 4 at 20 d, significantly lower than that of the control group, the PUL group, and the MeJA group. Moreover, the combined treatment of MeJA and PUL could synergistically regulate physiological indicators related to reactive oxygen metabolism, comprehensively enhancing the antioxidant defense ability of the fruit. The H2O2 content of the combined treatment group was approximately 2 μmol/g Fw, lower than that of the other treatment groups. Regarding the content of MDA, it continued to increase with the storage time. However, at 20 d, the MDA content of the combined treatment group was only 1.3 nmol/g Fw. The PPO activity related to enzymatic browning was in a trend of first increasing and then decreasing in all groups, while the combined treatment group maintained the lowest activity throughout the storage period. CAT activity showed a dynamic change, first increasing and then decreasing. The combined treatment group reached a peak of 33 U/g Fw on the 10th day and maintained high activity until the 20th day, significantly enhancing the hydrogen peroxide scavenging ability. SOD activity also showed a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. By the 20th d, the SOD activity of the combined treatment group reached 34 U/g Fw, 65.9% higher than that of the control group (20.5 U/g Fw), effectively maintaining the efficient scavenging ability of superoxide anions. With the extension of storage time, POD activity continued to increase, and the combined treatment group had the highest activity throughout the storage period, reaching 1.6 U/g Fw at the 20th d, higher than the other treatments.During the 8-day postharvest storage of fresh-cut apples, the combined treatment showed a superior effect in regulating the quality and physiological indicators compared to other treatment methods. Among them, the combined treatment group demonstrated the most significant performance in controlling water loss, with a fruit weight loss rate of only 3.0% after 8 d of storage. In terms of maintaining the texture of the flesh, the PUL + MeJA treatment showed a clear advantage: after 8 d of storage, the fruit flesh firmness remained at 50 N. In terms of the retention of flavor substances, the combined treatment effectively delayed the degradation of TSS. After 8 days of storage, the TSS content was 12.5%, 3% higher than that of the control group (9.5%), better preserving the sweetness and flavor characteristics of fresh-cut apples. In terms of the retention of organic acids, the TA retention rate of the PUL + MeJA group reached 0.2%, higher than that of the other treatment groups. Regarding the retention of VC and TPC, the content of these two substances in the fruits after combined treatment was higher than that of the other treatment groups. In regulating the acid-base balance of the fruits, the PUL + MeJA combined treatment effectively inhibited the increase in pH. In terms of inhibiting browning of fresh-cut apples, the combined treatment showed a strong anti-browning effect. After 8 d of storage, the browning degree was only 45%, 35% lower than that of the control group (80%) and 25% lower than the PUL group (70%), and 15% lower than the MeJA group (60%). In addition, the combined treatment group showed significant improvement in regulating the metabolism of reactive oxygen species and the antioxidant defense system. In inhibiting the accumulation of reactive oxygen species, the combined treatment effectively inhibited the accumulation of H2O2. After 8 d of storage, the H2O2 content in the fruits was only 74 nmol/g Fw. In inhibiting the formation of MDA, the MDA content in the fruits after combined treatment was only 12.8 nmol/g Fw, lower than that of the other treatment groups. In terms of PPO activity, the control group reached a peak of 120 U/g Fw on the 6th day, while the combined treatment group had significantly lower activity throughout the process compared to other groups. After 8 d of storage, its Fw content was only 35 U/g; CAT activity showed a continuous downward trend in the control group, while the combined treatment group significantly increased CAT activity and maintained a high level, and this regulatory ability was also reflected in the retention of POD and SOD activities.

Based on the above effects and the characteristics of PUL and MeJA, in terms of the practical application prospects and industrial feasibility of the combined treatment technology, this strategy has significant potential for scalable application and safe application features in post-harvest preservation of apples. Both PUL and MeJA have been recognized as safe and natural food preservatives, meeting food safety standards and regulatory requirements, and there is no risk of prohibited or restricted additives in fresh fruit and sliced fresh fruit processing. Secondly, this treatment operation is simple and compatible with the existing industrial post-harvest processing techniques for fruits and vegetables. In terms of consumer acceptance, this combined treatment method can effectively maintain the original flavor, texture and nutritional characteristics of apples, without causing abnormal taste, color differences or odors, and the natural combined treatment method conforms to consumers' preferences for green, safe and fresh fruit products. However, it should be noted that further research still needs to be conducted in industrial cold chain conditions for pilot verification and parameter optimization.

Discussion

Post-harvest dehydration of fruits was a core physical factor in tissue softening. High weight loss rates caused by damaged epidermal protective structures or imbalanced stomatal regulation can lead to a decrease in the turgor pressure of fruit pulp cells, loose cell wall structure, and ultimately a decrease in firmness (Dou et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2022). The results of the NMA showed that ‘Soaked in a 1.0% pullulan polysaccharide solution for 6 min’ had a significant effect on the maintenance of fruit firmness (SUCRA = 98.90) and the elimination of MDA (SUCRA = 4.9). Pullulan polysaccharide solution can delay the fruit softening process by forming a physical barrier, inhibiting cell wall enzymatic hydrolysis, and reducing oxidative structural damage on the surface of the fruit peel (K. Chen et al., 2025). For the weight loss rate of apple fruits, the treatment of ‘soaked in Citric Acid + Penicillin 2% + Eugenol 0.2% or 2 min’ had the best effect on inhibiting fruit weight loss (SUCRA = 2.4). Citric acid can not only optimize the three-dimensional network structure of pectin film through hydrogen bond crosslinking; It can also maintain the integrity and selective permeability of epidermal cell membrane, and reduce the leakage and loss of water (Guerreiro et al., 2017). TSS and TA are core indicators that determine the flavor quality of apples, and their changes in content reflect the dynamic balance of sugar and acid Metabolism in the fruit after harvesting (Wu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2021). For TSS, the Meta-analysis results showed that ‘Soaked in containing 80% PVA and 20% UFMU solution for 2 min’ had the best retention effect on TSS (SUCRA = 100.0). PVA has excellent film forming ability and strong oxygen barrier ability; UFMU could form a denser composite membrane with PVA, reduce the O2 permeability, moderately increase the CO2 concentration in the fruit, effectively inhibit the aerobic respiration intensity of the fruit, and reduce the respiratory decomposition rate of TSS (Saleh et al., 2026). In terms of fruit TA, ASA, TPC, O2− content and PPO enzyme activity, soaking in a 1500 μmol/L MeJA solution for 5 min had the most significant effect. MeJA, as a potent substance, can be absorbed by cells through the fruit epidermis, activate the endogenous jasmonic acid signaling pathway, regulate fruit ripening and aging metabolism, antioxidant defense system, phenylpropanoid synthesis pathway, and cell structural stability at transcription and enzyme activity levels, thereby significantly promoting the activity of TA, ASA, TPC, and PPO enzymes, and having a significant clearance effect on O2− (Min et al., 2024). PPO is the core enzyme of enzymatic browning in apples, which can catalyze the oxidation of phenolics substances to quinones, and then polymerize to form brown substances. The higher the activity, the faster the oxidation rate of phenolics substances, and the more likely the flesh/peel is to brown, darken, and turn black, leading to a rapid decline in quality (Ottaviani et al., 2023; Tinello & Lante, 2018). As an important characterization of the internal acid-base environment of fruits, pH changes are closely related to sugar and acid metabolism and enzyme activity regulation (Yang et al., 2022). The Meta-analysis results showed that the treatment of ‘Placed fruits without AVG coating in paraffin coated paper boxes’ had the most significant effect on maintaining the pH of apple fruits (Hedges's g = 0.33, 95%CI [0.27, 0.40]). Paraffin coated cardboard constructs a passive controlled microenvironment through a dual physical barrier of gas and water vapor, thereby delaying the degradation of organic acids and stabilizing the intracellular acid-base balance from the metabolic source. (Saleh et al., 2026); The L*, a* and b* value are the visual indicators reflecting the degree of browning of apple skin and flesh (Li et al., 2019). The results of this study showed that the ‘soaked in a 1.5% pullulan polysaccharide solution for 7 min’ had the best effect on maintaining the color of apple fruit, and had the most significant effect on the L* value (SUCRA = 0.0) and a* value (SUCRA = 87.7) of apple fruit. Pullulan coating can form a dense oxygen barrier film on the surface of the fruit, significantly inhibiting the deterioration of skin color during storage and reducing abnormal fluctuations in L * and a * values (Synowiec et al., 2014). The higher the b* value, the more obvious the yellowing of apple peel/flesh, indicating high maturity, aging, aggravated browning, and decreased freshness ‘soaked in a slightly acidic electrolyzed water solution containing 5% apple extract microcapsules for 2 min’ processing can significantly suppress b* value, this solvent can be oxidized to modify the surface PPO activity of the fruit peel, reducing the degree of enzymatic browning and thereby preventing abnormal changes in the b* value (Luo et al., 2024).

Ethylene, as a key ripening hormone in respiratory climacteric fruits, directly regulates the ripening and aging process of apples through its release rate (Wang, Zhang, et al., 2023). Soaked in 100 μmol/L MeJA solution for 5 min showed the best inhibitory effect on ethylene release (SUCRA = 0). Respiratory rate is a core indicator reflecting the energy metabolism intensity of apples after harvesting, and its level directly determines the rate of nutrient consumption and aging process of the fruit (Wang, Zhang, et al., 2023). Meta-analysis showed that ‘Soaked in 100 μmol/L MeJA solution for 5 min’ has the best effect on slowing down respiratory rate (SUCRA = 12.4). A high respiratory rate means that the fruit consumes more nutrients such as sugars and organic acids, which is consistent with the conclusions drawn from TSS and TA earlier. The superoxide anions (O2−) in fruits are the core members of ROS, and at low concentrations, they act as key signaling molecules to regulate processes such as ripening and disease resistance; At high concentrations, it can cause oxidative damage, leading to aging and quality deterioration (González-Gordo et al., 2020). The Meta-analysis results showed that ‘soaked in 1500 μmol/L MeJA solution for 5 min’ had excellent inhibitory effects on the accumulation of O2− (SUCRA = 0.0). H2O2 is a core member of the reactive oxygen species family, and its content level is an important indicator of the degree of fruit oxidative stress. Moderate H2O2 can act as a signaling molecule to regulate physiological processes, but excessive accumulation can cause oxidative damage, damage cell membrane structure, and degrade biomolecules (Büchele et al., 2023). The results of this study indicated that ‘soaked apple chunks in plasma water at a voltage of 500 V' significantly inhibited the accumulation of H2O2 (SUCRA = 9.4) and promoted the activity of SOD (SUCRA = 97.4). SOD is the first line of defense in the reactive oxygen species scavenging system, whose main function is to convert superoxide anion radicals into H2O2 and O2− (Rossi et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025). In the regulation of antioxidant enzyme systems, there are SOD as the first line of defense for reactive oxygen species scavenging systems and CAT as the core enzyme for scavenging H2O2 (Kaur & Bhatla, 2023; Pinilla et al., 2019). The plasma-active substances can activate the antioxidant defense pathway of the fruit, upregulated the gene expression and catalytic activity of antioxidant enzymes such as SOD, enhanced the ROS clearance ability, and ultimately reduced the accumulation of intracellular H2O2. POD is involved in the clearance of reactive oxygen species and also in the enzymatic browning process of fruits (Wang et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2019). Soaked in 15 μmol/L NCA solution for 5 min can significantly promote POD enzyme activity (SUCRA = 99.4). It may be that Nitroxyl released by NCA up regulates the activities of antioxidant defense enzymes such as pod through post transcriptional activation modification, and participates in the maintenance of oxidative homeostasis in fruits (Palma et al., 2019).

Based on the above content, it can be concluded that different post-harvest treatments synergistically regulate the ripening and aging process of apples through multiple targets and pathways. Among them, MeJA treatment is the most effective comprehensive preservation strategy for regulating post-harvest ripening and aging of apples. Different concentrations and treatment durations can optimize different physiological indicators in a targeted manner. Specifically, soaked in 100 μmol/L MeJA for 5 min significantly inhibited ethylene release and respiration rate, effectively delaying the initiation of fruit ripening and nutrient consumption; Soaked in 1500 μmol/L MeJA for 5 min showed the best performance in inhibiting the production of O2− and reducing the activity of PPO, thereby significantly delaying the enzymatic browning rate and maintaining fruit color and commercial value. In terms of antioxidant defense system, soaked in 1.0% pullulan polysaccharide solution for 6 min can significantly inhibit the accumulation of membrane lipid peroxidation product MDA and protect the integrity of cell membrane; Soaked in 15 μmol/L NCA solution for 5 min can significantly promote the activity of POD and enhance the ability to clear ROS; The 500 V plasma water treatment has a significant effect on inhibiting the accumulation of hydrogen peroxide H2O2. It is worth noting that none of the treatments have a promoting effect on catalase CAT. Overall, MeJA treatment exhibits multi-target and multi-level preservation effects by synergistically regulating ethylene biosynthesis, respiratory metabolism, browning key enzyme activity, and redox balance. It has the greatest potential for delaying fruit aging and inhibiting browning.

Based on the sensory appearance of fresh-cut apples and the changes in L*, a* and b* values, it can be concluded that during the 8 d storage process, the control group (T1) showed a significant increase in flesh browning and wrinkling with prolonged storage time. The L* value decreased from 60.2 to 50.64 (the largest decrease), the a* value increased from 3.95 to 6.86, and the b* value increased from 19.41 to 27.8 (the highest increase in each group), indicating a rapid decrease in flesh brightness, a significant increase in redness and yellowing, and the fastest deterioration process of browning; The single treatment of PUL and MeJA, as well as the combination treatment of both, can effectively delay the color deterioration and texture shrinkage of fresh cut apples. The combination treatment group showed the best performance, with only a slight dull color of the fruit flesh at the end of storage, and no obvious browning or shrinkage. The changes in L*, a* and b* values were significantly lower than those in the CK and the single treatment group, indicating that the combination treatment of PUL + MeJA can more effectively inhibit enzymatic browning and water loss of fresh-cut apples through synergistic effects. It maintained better sensory and color quality during the 8 d storage period, and the preservation effect was significantly better than that of the control group and the single treatment method.

Based on the comprehensive physiological and quality indicators of whole and fresh-cut apples, the combined treatment of MeJA and PUL achieved a systematic synergistic preservation effect in both post-harvest storage systems through a complete physiological pathway of ‘activating the antioxidant defense system-clearing excess reactive oxygen species-maintaining cell membrane structure and functional stability-delaying post-harvest quality deterioration’. The combined treatment significantly increased the activity of the three core antioxidant enzymes CAT, SOD, and POD, efficiently cleared the accumulation of H2O2, significantly inhibited membrane lipid peroxidation (reduced MDA content), and maintained cell membrane integrity from the source (Naguib et al., 2022); This stable structure not only blocks the contact between PPO and phenolics substrates, significantly inhibiting enzymatic browning (especially in fresh-cut apples), but also delays the degradation of fruit flesh cell walls (maintaining fruit firmness), inhibits transpiration and water loss (reducing weight loss rate), reduces the oxidative loss of nutrients such as VC and TPC, delays the metabolic degradation of TSS and TA, inhibits abnormal pH rise, and comprehensively maintains the flavor, nutrition, and commercial value of the fruit; Comparing the two storage systems, it can be seen that fresh cut apples experience much stronger oxidative stress and accelerated quality deterioration than whole fruits due to mechanical damage. However, the PUL + MeJA combined treatment exhibits stronger stress relief ability in the fresh cut system, which can effectively weaken the negative effects caused by mechanical damage.

Conclusion

Clarifying efficient, stable, and adaptable preservation intervention technologies for industrial scenarios is of great practical significance for solving the problem of apple postharvest loss. This study relied on the Bayesian random effects model to conduct a network meta-analysis on 48 types of apple postharvest preservation treatments and 21 quality and physiological indicators. The potential combined treatments were evaluated through storage validation experiments of intact fruits and fresh cut apples. The NMA results showed that the separate application of MeJA and PUL exhibited the best performance in maintaining fruit quality and regulating physiological metabolism. Based on this, we combined the two treatment methods and verified that the PUL + MeJA combined treatment has the dual adaptability of long-term storage of whole fruits and short-term preservation of fresh cut apples, especially suitable for alleviating oxidative stress and rapid aging induced by mechanical damage of fresh cut apples. In terms of quality control, PUL + MeJA treatment can effectively reduce fruit weight loss, maintain fruit flesh hardness, significantly inhibit enzymatic browning and color deterioration, stabilize fruit pH value, and better preserve key flavors and nutrients such as TSS, TA, VC, TPC, etc.; At the physiological regulatory level, this combined treatment can significantly enhance the activities of SOD, CAT, and POD antioxidant enzymes, reduce the accumulation of H ₂ O ₂ and MDA, while inhibiting PPO activity, respiratory rate, and ethylene release rate. By activating the endogenous antioxidant defense system of the fruit, it effectively blocks the postharvest aging process of apples at the physiological level. In summary, its preservation effect is significantly better than MeJA, PUL single treatment, and other conventional preservation methods. From the perspective of industrial transformation value, this optimal joint preservation mode can provide practical and feasible technical references for green preservation and standardized production of fresh cut apples, effectively solving the problems of short shelf life and high postharvest losses of fresh cut fruits. In addition, based on the existing research gaps in the dataset of this study, there are still data gaps in the corresponding combinations of preservation treatment and detection indicators, especially in the molecular mechanism of the effect of PUL + MeJA combined treatment. Therefore, future research does not need to expand into new experimental directions. Instead, standardized validation experiments can be conducted around existing data gaps, focusing on optimizing the application process parameters of joint treatment, supplementing effect data under different varieties and storage environments, improving the applicable system of this preservation technology, and further enhancing its potential for industrial application.
