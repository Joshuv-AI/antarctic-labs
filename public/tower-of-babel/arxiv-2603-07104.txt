Stochastic analysis for the Dirichlet–Ferguson process
Günter Last and Babette Picker

Abstract
We study a Dirichlet–Ferguson process ζ \zeta on a general phase space. First we reprove the chaos expansion from [ 22 ] , providing an explicit formula for the kernel functions. Then we proceed with developing a Malliavin calculus for ζ \zeta . To this end we introduce a gradient, a divergence and a generator which act as linear operators on random variables or random fields and which are linked by some basic formulas such as integration by parts. While this calculus is strongly motivated by Malliavin calculus for isonormal Gaussian processes and the general Poisson process, the strong dependence properties of ζ \zeta require considerably more combinatorial efforts. We apply our theory to identify our generator as the generator of the Fleming–Viot process and to describe the associated Dirichlet form explicitly in terms of the chaos expansion. We also establish the product and chain chain rule for the gradient and an integral representation of the divergence. Finally we give a short direct proof of the Poincaré inequality.



Keywords:

Dirichlet–Ferguson process, Dirichlet distribution,
Mecke type equation, chaos expansion, Malliavin calculus, Fleming–Viot process,
Poincaré inequality

2
The Dirichlet–Ferguson process

We work on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
.
Let
({\mathbb{X}},{\mathcal{X}},\rho)
be a measure space where
\theta:=\rho({\mathbb{X}})>0
and
\theta<\infty
.
Let
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}\equiv{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})
be the space of all probability measures on

{\mathbb{X}}
equipped with the smallest
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{M}_{1}
such that the mapping

\mu\mapsto\mu(B)
from
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}
to
[0,1]
is measurable for each

B\in{\mathcal{X}}
. A
Dirichlet–Ferguson process
(DF Process)
\zeta
on
{\mathbb{X}}
with parameter
measure
\rho
is a random probability measure

\zeta
on
{\mathbb{X}}
(a random element of the space
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})
)
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}((\zeta(B_{1}),\ldots,\zeta(B_{n}))\in\cdot)={\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho(B_{1}),\ldots,\rho(B_{n})),

(2.1)

whenever
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, form a measurable partition of
{\mathbb{X}}
.
Recall that the
Dirichlet distribution

{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})

with
n\geq 1
parameters
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in[0,\infty)

such that
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}>0
is
the probability measure on

\Delta_{n}:=\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:p_{1}+\dots+p_{n}=1\}

defined as follows. If
n\geq 2
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in(0,\infty)
,
then
{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
has the density

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\cdots\Gamma(\alpha_{n})}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}-1}

with respect to (properly normalized)
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\Delta_{n}
.
Here
\Gamma(\alpha):=\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{a-1}e^{-t}\,\mathrm{d}t
,
\alpha>0
, denotes the Gamma function.
If
n=1
, then
{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_{1}):=\delta_{1}
. If
n\geq 2
and

\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{k}=0
for some
k\leq n-1
, then

{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}):=\delta_{0}^{\otimes k}\otimes{\mathrm{Dir}}(\alpha_{k+1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
.
A similar definition applies if some other set of the
\alpha_{i}
vanish.
In the extreme case
\rho=\delta_{x}
for some
x\in{\mathbb{X}}
we have
\zeta=\delta_{x}
.
The distribution of a DF process
is denoted by
{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho)\equiv{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho,\cdot)
, a probability measure on
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})
.
Sometimes we write
\zeta_{\rho}:=\zeta
.

Given a measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{X}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define a measure
\mu^{[n]}
on
{\mathbb{X}}^{n}

by

\mu^{[n]}(B)=\idotsint{\mathbf{1}}_{B}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{n-1}})(\mathrm{d}x_{n})\cdots(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}})(\mathrm{d}x_{2})\mu(\mathrm{d}x_{1}).

(2.2)

Note that

\mu^{[n]}(B^{n})=\mu(B)(\mu(B)+1)\cdots(\mu(B)+n-1)=\mu(B)^{(n)},\quad B\in{\mathcal{X}},

where, for
w\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
w^{(n)}:=w(w+1)\cdots(w+n-1)
is the
n
-th rising factorial of
w
.
As usual we set
w^{(0)}:=1
.
It can be shown that
\mu^{[n]}
is a symmetric measure.

Let
f\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})\times{\mathbb{X}}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be measurable.
Then the multivariate Mecke-type equation for the DF process says that

\displaystyle\iint

\displaystyle f(\mu,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,\mu^{n}(\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\,{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho,\mathrm{d}\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{(n)}}\iint f(\mu,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{n}},\mathrm{d}\mu)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})).

(2.3)

This result can be found in
[
8
]
, at least in an implicit form.
It can be proved inductively, starting with the Mecke-type equation from

[
3
,
17
]
.
Equation (
2
) shows in particular that the
n
-th moment measure
of
\zeta
is given by

\mathbb{E}\zeta^{n}(B)=\frac{1}{\theta^{(n)}}\rho^{[n]}(B),\quad B\in{\mathcal{X}}^{n}.

(2.4)

Sometimes it is convenient to write (
2
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int f(\zeta_{\rho},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\,\zeta_{\rho}^{n}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{(n)}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int f(\zeta_{\rho,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\bigg],

where
\zeta_{\rho,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}}:=\zeta_{\rho+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{n}}}
is a DF process with
directing measure
\rho+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{n}}
.

3
Chaos expansion

In this section we reprove the chaos expansion from
[
22
]
and derive an explicit
formula for the kernel functions.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we let
\mathbf{H}_{n}
denote the set of all symmetric functions
g\in L^{2}(\rho^{[n]})
satisfying

\int g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x)(\rho+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{n-1}})(\mathrm{d}x)=0,\quad\rho^{[n-1]}\text{-a.e.\ $(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{X}}^{n-1}$.}

(3.1)

For
n=1
this has to be interpreted as
\int g(x)\,\rho(\mathrm{d}x)=0
.

Since we consider
\mathbf{H}_{n}
as a subspace of
L^{2}(\rho^{[n]})
, the symmetry of

f\in L^{2}(\rho^{[n]})
means that

coincides with a symmetric function

\rho^{[n]}
-a.e. An important property of the spaces
{\mathbf{H}}_{n}
is

\displaystyle\int g(x)h(y)\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y))={\mathbf{1}}\{m=n\}n!\int g(x)h(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x),\quad g\in\mathbf{H}_{m},h\in\mathbf{H}_{n},

(3.2)

for

. More general versions of these orthogonality relations are proved in the appendix.
Together with (
2.4
) this shows that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int g(x)\zeta^{m}(dx)\int h(y)\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}y)\right]={\mathbf{1}}\{m=n\}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int g(x)h(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x).

(3.3)

As a consequence the spaces
\mathbf{F}_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
defined by

\mathbf{F}_{n}=\left\{\int g(x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}x):g\in\mathbf{H}_{n}\right\}

and

are pairwise orthogonal subspaces of
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
.
We call
{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
the
n
-th
chaos
associated with
\zeta
.
Let
L^{2}(\zeta)
be the space of all measurable
F\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

such that
\mathbb{E}F(\zeta)^{2}<\infty
.

Convention 3.1
.

Given
F\in L^{2}(\zeta)
we often write

.
If

is the canonical probability space

({\mathbf{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho))

Convention 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
{\mathbb{X}}
.
The space
{\mathbb{X}}^{0}
is identified with a singleton (as

, for instance)
and a

can be identified with a constant

Then it is both natural and useful to set

.
With this convention (

or

upon interpreting

The main result in

]
(cf. equation (6) therein)

where the convergence is in

-a.e. From (

\mathbb{E}FG=(\mathbb{E}F)(\mathbb{E}G)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)\,\quad F,G\in L^{2}(\zeta),

where

Given

Somewhat similar to the Poisson case

Lemma 3.5

Let

It is convenient

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

. Then

\int T_{F,k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1},x)\,(\rho+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{k-1}})(\mathrm{d}x)=(\theta+k-1)T_{F,k-1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1})

-a.e.

Proof.

We first consider the case

, the Mecke-type equation (

) and the definition of

Let

By definition of

\displaystyle g(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})\,(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k-1}})(\mathrm{d}x_{k})\,\rho^{[k-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\int\mathbb{E}F(\zeta_{\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k}}})g(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{k}).

By the Mecke-type equation (

Using once again the Mecke-type equation and the definition of

\displaystyle F(\zeta_{\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k-1}}})g(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})\,\rho^{[k-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=(\theta+k-1)\int T_{F,k-1}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})g(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})\,\rho^{[k-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{k-1}).

Let

4
Malliavin operators

In this section we use the chaos expansion to introduce Malliavin operators acting on
random variables and fields, which depend measurably on
\zeta
. Our approach is
inspired by the Malliavin calculus for classical isonormal Gaussian processes (see
[
20
]
)
and the general Poisson process (see
[
15
]
).
In the next section we shall see that the Malliavin operators admit a natural
interpretation in the context of Fleming–Viot processes.

4.1
The gradient

Let
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
denote the set of all
F\in L^{2}(\zeta)
with chaos decomposition
(
3.4
) such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty.

(4.1)

Define
\nabla\colon\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)\rightarrow L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
by

\displaystyle(\nabla F)

\displaystyle(\omega,x)

(4.2)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\int f_{n}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1})\,\zeta^{n-1}(\omega,\mathrm{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-1}))-\int f_{n}(y)\,\zeta^{n}(\omega,\mathrm{d}y)\right).

This definition is justified by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
.
Then the right-hand side of (
4.2
) converges in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
.

Proof.

Given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
H_{n}\colon\Omega\times{\mathbb{X}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be defined by

\displaystyle H_{n}(\omega,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int f_{n}(x,\mathbf{y}_{n-1})\,\zeta^{n-1}(\omega,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n-1})-\int f_{n}(\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{n}(\omega,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}).

Taking
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\displaystyle\int H_{m}(\omega,x)

\displaystyle H_{n}(\omega,x)\,C_{\zeta}(\mathrm{d}(\omega,x))=\mathbb{E}\int H_{m}(x)H_{n}(x)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int\left(\int f_{m}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1})\,\zeta^{m-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1})-\int f_{m}(\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{m}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\right)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\left(\int f_{n}(x,\mathbf{z}_{n-1})\,\zeta^{n-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{n-1})-\int f_{n}(\mathbf{z})\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z})\right)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\right].

By (
2.4
), this expectation equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+n-1)}}\int f_{m}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1})

\displaystyle f_{n}(x,\mathbf{z}_{n-1})\,\rho^{[m+n-1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1},\mathbf{z}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+n)}}\int f_{m}(\mathbf{y}_{m})f_{n}(\mathbf{z}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{y}_{m},\mathbf{z}_{n})).

According to Corollary
A.2
, this reduces to

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}\{m=n\}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(n-1)!}{\theta^{(2n-1)}}\int f_{n}(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})-\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})\right)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{1}}\{m=n\}\frac{(n-1)!}{\theta^{(2n)}}(\theta+n-1)\int f_{n}(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).

Hence, we obtain for each
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle\int\left(\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}nH_{n}(\omega,x)\right)^{2}\,C_{\zeta}(\mathrm{d}(\omega,x))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{0}}\mathbb{E}\int nmH_{n}(x)H_{m}(x)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}\frac{n^{2}(n-1)!}{\theta^{(2n)}}(\theta+n-1)\int f_{n}(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).

Since
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
,
we conclude that

\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}nH_{n}
,
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
and therefore convergent.
The limit is
\nabla F
.
Moreover, we see that

\mathbb{E}\int\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nH_{n}(x)\right)^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}(\theta+n-1)\int f_{n}(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).\qed

(4.3)

The operator
\nabla
is linear in the following (natural) sense. If
F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)

and
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
aF+bG\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and
\nabla(aF+bG)=a\nabla F+b\nabla G
holds

C_{\zeta}
-a.e. We work with a measurable version of the gradient
(which can be constructed via a
C_{\zeta}
-a.e. convergent subsequence)
and often suppress the dependence on
\omega
,
by writing
\nabla_{x}F
,
x\in{\mathbb{X}}
, for the random variable
\omega\mapsto(\nabla F)(\omega,x)
.
The next proposition provides an important isometry property.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose that
F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int\nabla_{x}F\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}g_{n}),\quad F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),

(4.4)

where
f_{n}
(resp.
g_{n}
),
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are the kernel functions of
F
(resp.
G
).

Proof.

For
F=G
we obtain the asserted formula from (
4.3
).
The general case can either be derived by the same calculation or by polarization.
∎

The gradient is pathwise centred w.r.t.
\zeta
, that is

\int\nabla_{x}F\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=0,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4.5)

To see this, we may define for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{X}}
the random variable
H^{m}_{x}

by truncating the infinite series (
4.2
) at
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By definition, we then have

\int H^{m}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=0
. On the other hand, it follows from the
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
-convergence and
Jensen’s inequality (applied to
\zeta
) that
\int H^{m}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)
converges to

\int\nabla_{x}F\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
as
m\to\infty
. Therefore,
(
4.5
) follows.

The next result shows that the gradient is a
closed operator
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F_{n}\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and assume that
(\nabla F_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

forms a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
.
Then there exists
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
with

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}F_{n})^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=0

(4.6)

and
F_{n}-\mathbb{E}F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
.

Proof.

Let for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the chaos expansion (
3.4
) of
F_{n}\in L^{2}(\zeta)
be given by

F_{n}(\zeta)=\mathbb{E}F_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int f_{n,k}(x)\,\zeta^{k}(\mathrm{d}x).

The assumed convergence and (
4.4
) yield

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int(\nabla_{x}F_{m}-\nabla_{x}F_{n})^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{m,n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{kk!}{\theta^{(2k)}}(\theta+k-1)\int(f_{m,k}(x)-f_{n,k}(x))^{2}\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}x).

Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
be
the vector space of all sequences
g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that
g_{k}\in L^{2}(\rho^{[k]})
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{kk!(\theta+k-1)}{\theta^{(2k)}}\int g_{k}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty.

Equipped with the norm

\|g\|_{\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}}:=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{kk!(\theta+k-1)}{\theta^{(2k)}}\int g_{k}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad g=(g_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{H}},

the space
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
becomes a Hilbert space as a countable direct sum of Hilbert spaces.
As we have seen above, the sequence formed by
f_{n}:=(f_{n,k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
is a Cauchy sequence in
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
.
Hence, there exists a limit
f=(f_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}
, i.e.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{kk!(\theta+k-1)}{\theta^{(2k)}}\int(f_{n,k}(x)-f_{k}(x))^{2}\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}x)=0.

In particular, we obtain for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that
f_{n,k}\to f_{k}
as
n\to\infty
in
L^{2}(\rho^{[k]})

so that Lemma
3.7
shows
f_{k}\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
.
Thus,

F:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int f_{k}(x)\,\zeta^{k}(\mathrm{d}x),

belongs to
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and satisfies (
4.6
).

The second assertion follows from (
3.5
) and (
4.4
), showing that
the convergence of
\nabla F_{n}\to\nabla F
in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
implies the asserted

L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
-convergence.
∎

4.2
The divergence

In this subsection we introduce and discuss the
divergence operator

acting as an adjoint of
\nabla
.
To do so, it is convenient to introduce the canonical version
C^{\prime}_{\zeta}
of the
Campbell measure (
1.2
), which is the measure on
{\mathbf{M}}_{1}\times{\mathbb{X}}
defined by

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{\zeta}:=\iint{\mathbf{1}}\{(\mu,x)\in\cdot\}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathbb{P}(\zeta\in\mathrm{d}\mu).

(4.7)

Let

\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
denote the set of all

H\in L^{2}(C^{\prime}_{\zeta})
for which there exists a
c\geq 0
such that

\mathbb{E}\int H(\zeta,x)\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\leq c\,\|G(\zeta)\|_{2},\quad G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
. Since
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)

is dense in
L^{2}(\zeta)
, we can apply the Riesz representation theorem to obtain for each

H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
the existence of an
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. uniquely determined

\sigma(\zeta)
-measurable
\delta(H)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
satisfying

\mathbb{E}\int H_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}\delta(H)G,\quad G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),

(4.8)

where we write
H_{x}:=H(\zeta,x)
,
x\in{\mathbb{X}}
. This equation is often referred to as

partial integration
. It implies that
\delta
is a linear operator.
Choosing
F\equiv 1
shows that
\mathbb{E}\delta(H)=0
.

We wish to identify a reasonably large subset
of
\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
along with an explicit formula for the divergence
in terms of chaos expansion. The next lemma is crucial.
In the proof and also later we use the notation

\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}
to indicate a summation
over
r
-tupels
(i_{1},\dots,i_{r})\in[m]^{r}
(
m,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
) with pairwise different entries.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that
h\in L^{2}(\rho^{[n+1]})
such
that
h(x,\cdot)\in{\mathbf{H}}_{n}
for each

Z:=(\theta+n)\int h(z)\,\zeta^{n+1}(\mathrm{d}z)-\iint h(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\,(\rho+\delta_{y_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{y_{n}})(\mathrm{d}x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})).

Then
\mathbb{E}Z^{2}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta n!}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2})+\frac{\theta nn!}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\int h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\ \rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{n!(n^{2}-\theta)}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,\mathbf{x}_{n})\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{nn!(n^{2}-\theta)}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2(n+1)(\theta+n)nn!}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{nn!(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{nn!(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})h(x_{2},\mathbf{x}_{n})\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n}).

Proof.

By (
2.4

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int(h\otimes h)(\mathbf{x})\,\rho^{[2n+2]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2(\theta+n)}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\iint h(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})h(z,\mathbf{y}_{n})\,(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{n}})(\mathrm{d}z)\,\rho^{[2n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\theta^{(2n)}}\iiint h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,\mathbf{y}_{n})\,(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{n}})(\mathrm{d}x)(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{n}})(\mathrm{d}y)\,\rho^{[2n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{n},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle A_{1}=

\displaystyle\;\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in[n]}\int h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n}})\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle+n\;\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1})\in[n]}\int h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,x,x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n-1}})\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle+n\;\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-1})\in[n]}\int h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,y,x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n-1}})\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle\eqqcolon

\displaystyle+nn!\int h(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})).

Next we consider

\displaystyle A_{2}=

.

A.1

\displaystyle=\;\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in[n+1]}\iint h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(z,x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n}})\,(\delta_{x}+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{n}})(\mathrm{d}z)\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}A_{1}-\frac{2(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+1)}(\theta+n)}A_{2}+\frac{1}{\theta^{(2n)}}A_{3}

Lemma 4.5
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
4.4

be satisfied. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\mathbb{E}Z^{2}\leq C\frac{(n+1)!(\theta+n)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h(z)^{2}\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}z).

Proof.

We need to bound the terms occurring in Lemma
4.4
. By symmetry of
\rho^{[n]}
,

\displaystyle\int h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\ \rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int h(x_{i},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2}),

where we have used the recursion (
A.1
) to obtain the inequality.
Multiplying this inequality with

\frac{nn!}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}=\frac{nn!(\theta+2n+1)}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}
,
we see that the required inequality holds.
Next we bound

\displaystyle 2\int

\displaystyle|h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n})h(y,\mathbf{x}_{n})|\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\int h(x,\mathbf{x}_{n}^{2})\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))+\int h(y,\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n}))

\displaystyle=2(\theta+n+1)\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2}).

Multiplying this inequality with
\frac{n!|n^{2}-\theta|}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}
yields the requested inequality.
Next we bound

\displaystyle 2\int

\displaystyle|h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})|\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\theta+n)\int h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle=2(\theta+n)\int h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})\leq\frac{2(\theta+n)}{n}\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2}),

where the final inequality has been derived above. Multiplying this with

\frac{nn!|n^{2}-\theta|}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}
yields the requested bound.
Next we bound

\displaystyle 2\int

\displaystyle|h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})|\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

\displaystyle\leq(\theta+n)\int h(x,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))+\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2}).

Here the first term as well as

\int|h(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})h(y,x,\mathbf{x}_{n-1})|\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,y,\mathbf{x}_{n-1}))

can be treated as above. Finally we can bound

\displaystyle 2\int|h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})h(x_{2},\mathbf{x}_{n})|\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\int h(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})+\int h(x_{2},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\rho^{[n+1]}(h^{2}),

finishing the proof.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, be measurable functions such that

n\in{\mathbb{N}}

(4.14)

converges in
L^{2}(C^{\prime}_{\zeta})

Proof.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we define
H_{n}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

H_{n}(\omega,x)\coloneqq\int h_{n}(x,\mathbf{y}_{n})\,\zeta^{n}(\omega,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n}).

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
m\geq n
. By (
2.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int H_{m}(\omega,x)

\displaystyle H_{n}(\omega,x)\,C_{\zeta}(\mathrm{d}(\omega,x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+n+1)}}\int h_{m}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m})h_{n}(x,\mathbf{z}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m},\mathbf{z}_{n})).

According to Corollary
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle\int h_{m}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m})h_{n}(x,\mathbf{z}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n+1]}(\mathrm{d}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m},\mathbf{z}_{n}))

\displaystyle={\mathbf{1}}_{\{m=n\}}m!\left(\int h_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m+1})^{2}\,\rho^{[m+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{m+1})+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int h_{m}(x_{r},\mathbf{x}_{m})^{2}\,\rho^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{m})\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}+{\mathbf{1}}_{\{m=n+1\}}(n+1)!\int h_{n+1}(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})h_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1}).

Hence, we obtain for
n_{0},n_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n_{0}\leq n_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\int\bigg(\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\int h_{n}(x,\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\bigg)^{2}\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum_{m,n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\mathbb{E}\int H_{m}(x)H_{n}(x)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\bigg(\int h_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})+\sum_{r=1}^{n}\int h_{n}(x_{r},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}-1}\frac{(n+1)!}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\int h_{n+1}(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})h_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\eqqcolon S_{1}+S_{2}.

We treat the sums
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
individually and establish upper bounds for both.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\sum_{r=1}^{n}\int h_{n}(x_{r},\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})=\iint h_{n}(y,\mathbf{x}_{n})^{2}\,\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{n}}(\mathrm{d}y)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n})\leq\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2}),

h_{n}
in its last
n
arguments. Therefore,

S_{1}\leq 2\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2}).

To bound
S_{2}
we can use the inequalities

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle h_{n+1}(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})h_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int h_{n+1}(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})+\frac{1}{2}\int h_{n}(\mathbf{x}_{n+1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})

and

(n+1)\int h_{n+1}(x_{1},\mathbf{x}_{n+1})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+1})\leq\int h_{n+1}(\mathbf{x}_{n+2})^{2}\,\rho^{[n+2]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{n+2}),

where we used the symmetry of
h_{n+1}
in its last
n+1
arguments. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}-1}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\big(\rho^{[n+2]}(h_{n+1}^{2})+(n+1)\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{n_{1}}\frac{(n-1)!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}-1}\frac{(n+1)!}{\theta^{(2n+2)}}\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2}).

Combining our findings, we arrive at

\mathbb{E}\int\left(\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\int h_{n}(x,\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\right)^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{n_{1}}\frac{n!(4+\frac{1}{2}\theta)}{\theta^{(2n+1)}}\rho^{[n+1]}(h_{n}^{2}).\qed

where the series converges in

. The proof of Theorem
4.9
and Lemma
4.4

show that these two statements hold under more general (but more technical) assumptions
on the functions
h_{n}
.
It suffices to have convergence of the series, arising by summing the
terms constituting
\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{n})^{2}
(
=\mathbb{E}Z^{2}
) in Lemma
4.4
. Given the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\geq 0}
it seems difficult
to find simple necessary and sufficient
conditions for
H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
. In contrast to the classical Gaussian and Poisson cases,
the summands in (
4.19
) are not orthogonal.

Our next lemma provides some information on the chaos expansion
of
H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
, provided that
H
is given by
(
4.14
) and (
4.13
) holds. Note that (
4.13
) is significantly
weaker than (
4.15
) assumed in Theorem
4.9
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose that the functions
h_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are given as
in Lemma
4.6
. Assume that (
4.13
) holds
and let
H
be given by (
4.14
).
Assume that
H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and let
F\in{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
for some

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\delta(H)F=\mathbb{E}(\delta(H_{n-1})+\delta(H_{n})+\delta(H_{n+1}))F,

where
H_{i}(\mu,x):=\int h_{i}(\mu,x)\,\mu^{n}(\mathrm{d}x)
for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\delta(H_{i})
are given by (
4.17
).

Proof.

Define
H_{\leq k}:=H_{0}+\cdots+H_{k}
for

k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. By partial integration and Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\delta(H)F=\mathbb{E}\int H(\zeta,x)\nabla_{x}F\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\int H_{\leq k}(\zeta,x)\nabla_{x}F\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

By Corollary
4.10
, we have
H_{\leq k}\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and hence,
by partial integration,

\mathbb{E}\delta(H)F=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{\leq k})F.

We have seen in the proof of Theorem
4.9
that

\delta(H_{m})\in\mathbf{F}_{m-1}\oplus\mathbf{F}_{m}\oplus\mathbf{F}_{m+1}

for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since
F\in{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
, we therefore obtain

\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{\leq k})F=\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{\leq n+1})F,\quad k\geq n+2,

and hence

\mathbb{E}\delta(H)F=\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{\leq n+1})F.

Since
\mathbb{E}\delta(H_{m})F=0
for
m\leq n-2
, the assertion follows.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

Assume that
H
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.13
.
For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
let
\pi_{n}
denote the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
to

{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
. Lemma
4.13
implies

\pi_{n}\delta(H)=\pi_{n}\delta(H_{n-1})+\pi_{n}\delta(H_{n})+\pi_{n}\delta(H_{n+1}).

In principle, these projections can be computed with Lemma
3.10
, thus
providing the chaos expansion of
\delta(H)
.
We omit further details.

4.3
The Fleming–Viot operator

Let
\operatorname{dom}(L)
stand for the set of all
F\in L^{2}(\zeta)
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\theta+n-1)^{2}n^{2}n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty,

(4.20)

where the kernel functions
f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are again given by (
3.4
).
A comparison with (
4.1
) shows that

\operatorname{dom}(L)\subset\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
.
Justified by the following lemma we define for
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)

LF:=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\theta+n-1)n\int f_{n}(x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}x).

(4.21)

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
with chaos expansion (
3.4
). Then the series
on the right-hand side of (
4.21
)
converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
.

Proof.

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
m\leq n
. The orthogonality relations (
3.3
) yield

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n}k(\theta+k-1)\int f_{k}(x)\,\zeta^{k}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{2}=\sum_{k=m}^{n}\frac{k^{2}(\theta+k-1)^{2}k!}{\theta^{(2k)}}\int f_{k}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}x).

Consequently, the partial sums of the series under consideration form a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
if and only if
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
.
∎

Since (
4.21
) provides the chaos expansion of
LF
, we immediately have

\mathbb{E}(LF)G=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x),\quad F\in\operatorname{dom}(L),\,G\in L^{2}(\zeta).

(4.22)

In particular, we see that
L
is negative semi-definite and that

\mathbb{E}(LF)G=\mathbb{E}F(LG),\quad F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(L).

(4.23)

Since
LF
is the
L^{2}
-limit of centred random variables, we further
have
\mathbb{E}LF=0
for each
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
.

For reasons that will become clear in the next section, we refer
to the linear mapping
L\colon\operatorname{dom}(L)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{P})

as the
Fleming–Viot operator
. It plays a similar role as the
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator in a Gaussian or Poisson context.
This fact is supported by our next theorem.

Theorem 4.16
.

Suppose that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. Then

\nabla F\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
iff
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
. In this case

\delta(\nabla F)=-LF,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(4.24)

Proof.

Let
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
with chaos expansion (
3.4
).

Assume first that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
.
Consider identity (
4.4
) for
G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. Applying
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we see that the right-hand side is bounded by

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{n^{2}n!(\theta+n-1)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int g_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again, this series can be bounded by

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}n!(\theta+n-1)}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int g_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
and
G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\int\nabla_{x}F\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\leq c\|G\|_{2},

(4.25)

where
c=\|LF\|_{2}<\infty
. Thus,
\nabla F
is an element of
\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
.

Assume, conversely, that
\nabla F\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
.
Then we obtain from the integration by parts formula (
4.8
) that

\mathbb{E}\delta(\nabla F)G=\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F)(\nabla_{x}G)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

(4.26)

for all
G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. We set
H\coloneqq\delta(\nabla F)
.
Let the chaos expansions of
H
and
G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
be given by

H=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int h_{n}(x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad G=\mathbb{E}G+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int g_{n}(x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}x),

respectively, where we recall that
\mathbb{E}H=0
. By (
3.5
),

\mathbb{E}\delta(\nabla F)G=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int h_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x).

(4.27)

Therefore, we obtain from (
4.26
) and (
4.4
)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nn!(\theta+n-1)}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int h_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)

(4.28)

and consequently

\int(n(\theta+n-1)f_{n}(x)-h_{n}(x))g(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)=0

for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and each
g\in\mathbf{H}_{n}
. Choosing
g=f_{n}-h_{n}
, gives

h_{n}=n(\theta+n-1)f_{n},\quad\rho^{[n]}\text{-a.e.},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(4.29)

Since
H\in L^{2}(\zeta)
, the convergence of

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int h_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!n^{2}(\theta+n-1)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)^{2}\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)

yields
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
. Finally we obtain from
(
4.27
) and (
4.29
) that

\mathbb{E}\delta(\nabla F)G=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!n(\theta+n-1)}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int f_{n}(x)g_{n}(x)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}(-L(F))G.

Since this is true for all
G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
, we obtain that
\delta(\nabla F)

and
-LF
coincide
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
∎

5
The Fleming–Viot process

In this section we assume
{\mathbb{X}}
to be a locally compact Polish space equipped with the Borel
\sigma
-field

\mathcal{X}
. The probability measure
{\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho)
is the unique invariant
distribution of the measure-valued Fleming–Viot process
(with parent-independent mutation) as
introduced and studied in
[
9
]
.
The generator of this process can be explicitly defined on smooth functions
as follows.
Let
\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})
denote the space of all bounded and measurable functions
f\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
{\mathbf{S}}
denote the space of all functions
F\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
of
the form
F(\mu)=\varphi(\mu(f_{1}),\ldots,\mu(f_{d}))
, where
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

f_{1},\ldots,f_{d}\in\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
For such an
F
we define
L_{\rho}F\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\displaystyle(L_{\rho}F)(\mu):=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{i,j}\varphi)\left(\mu(f_{1}),\ldots,\mu(f_{d})\right)\CV_{\mu}(f_{i},f_{j})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{i}\varphi)\left(\mu(f_{1}),\ldots,\mu(f_{d})\right)\mu(Af_{i}),\quad\mu\in{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}}),

where
\partial_{i}\varphi
and
\partial^{2}_{i,j}\varphi
denote the first and second order partial derivatives of
\varphi

and the
mutation operator

A\colon\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})\to\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})
is defined by

Af(x):=\int(f(y)-f(x))\,\rho(\mathrm{d}y),\quad x\in{\mathbb{X}}.

(5.1)

We will see below that

(L_{\rho}F)(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}LF,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},\,F\in{\mathbf{S}}.

(5.2)

In fact, we shall describe the closure (in
L^{2}({\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho)
) of the bilinear form associated with
L_{\rho}

explicitly in terms of Malliavin operators. Moreover, we shall see that
\frac{1}{2}L
is
the generator of this form. We start with the following lemma from

[
22
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The set
{\mathbf{S}}
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho))
.

Proof.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})
. Then

\mu\mapsto\mu^{m}(h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{m})
is an element of
{\mathbf{S}}
.
Furthermore,
{\mathbf{S}}
contains the constant functions. Since
{\mathbf{S}}
is a linear space,
the assertion follows from
[
22
, Lemma 2]
.
∎

Let us introduce a bilinear form

{\mathcal{E}}\colon\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)\times\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)\to{\mathbb{R}}

by

{\mathcal{E}}(F,G):=\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F)(\nabla_{x}G)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x),\quad F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla).

(5.3)

By (
4.5
), we can write this as

{\mathcal{E}}(F,G)=\mathbb{E}\CV_{\zeta}(\nabla F,\nabla G),

(5.4)

where we use the (quite natural) notation

\CV_{\zeta}(H,\tilde{H}):=\int H_{x}\tilde{H}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\int H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\int\tilde{H}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

for measurable functions
(\omega,x)\mapsto H_{x}(\omega)
and
(\omega,x)\mapsto\tilde{H}_{x}(\omega)
in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
.
As usual we abbreviate
{\mathcal{E}}(F):={\mathcal{E}}(F,F)
.
The following lemma shows that the form
{\mathcal{E}}
is
closed
; see e.g.
[
11
]
. This means that

\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
equipped with the scalar product

(F,G)\mapsto\mathcal{E}(F,G)+\mathbb{E}FG
is complete.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
F_{n}\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and assume that
\lim_{m,n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(F_{m}-F_{n})=0

as well as
\lim_{m,n\to\infty}(\mathbb{E}F_{m}-\mathbb{E}F_{n})=0
.
Then there exists
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}(F-F_{n})=0
and
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
.

Proof.

The assertion is a consequence Lemma
4.3
.
∎

For
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
we can use Theorem
4.16

and partial integration to obtain that

\mathbb{E}\int\nabla_{x}F\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}(-LF)G,\quad G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),

(5.5)

that is

\mathbb{E}(-LF)G={\mathcal{E}}(F,G),\quad F\in\operatorname{dom}(L),G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla).

(5.6)

We wish to identify
{\mathcal{E}}
with the
Dirichlet form
associated with
the generator

L_{\rho}
; see
[
11
]
for an introduction into
the theory of Dirichlet forms
and
[
21
]
for a thorough study of Dirichlet forms
associated with the Fleming–Viot
process and some of its generalisations.
In order to do so, we need to relate our Malliavin gradient to a
pathwise defined gradient defined on
{\mathbf{S}}
; see e.g.
[
29
,
25
]
.

For
F\in{\mathbf{S}}
we define
\nabla^{*}F\colon\Omega\times{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\nabla^{*}F(\omega,x):=\sum^{d}_{i=1}\partial_{i}\varphi(\zeta(\omega)(g_{1}),\ldots,\zeta(\omega)(g_{d}))(g_{i}(x)-\zeta(g_{i})).

(5.7)

Similarly as before we
denote the random variable
\omega\mapsto\nabla^{*}F(\omega,x)
by
\nabla^{*}_{x}F
.
Clearly we have

\nabla^{*}_{x}F=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F((1-t)\zeta+t\delta_{x})\Big|_{t=0},\quad x\in{\mathbb{X}},

showing that
\nabla^{*}_{x}F
can be interpreted as a directional derivative.

The following proposition shows
that
\nabla
and
\nabla^{*}
coincide on
{\mathbf{S}}
almost everywhere with respect to
C_{\zeta}
.
In particular, the definition
(
5.7
)
does
C_{\zeta}
-a.e. not depend on the chosen
representation of
F
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
F\in{\mathbf{S}}
. Then
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and

\nabla F=\nabla^{*}F,\quad C_{\zeta}\text{-a.e.}

Proof.

First we examine
\nabla^{\ast}F
.
Since
F
can be represented as

F(\mu)=\varphi\big(\mu(f_{1}),\ldots,\mu(f_{m})\big),\quad\mu\in\mathbf{M}_{1}(\mathbb{X}),

with
f_{i}\coloneqq h_{i}
,
i\in[m]
, and
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
,
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}
,
it is an element of
{\mathbf{S}}
and formula (
5.7
) yields

\nabla_{x}^{\ast}F=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big(\partial_{i}\varphi\big)\big(\zeta(h_{1}),\ldots,\zeta(h_{m})\big)\big(h_{i}(x)-\zeta(h_{i})\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(x)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{m}\zeta(h_{j})-m\zeta^{m}(h).

Second we consider
\nabla F
.
Let
x\in\mathbb{X}
.
By Lemma
3.10
, we have
F\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathbf{F}_{i}
. Consequently,
F
belongs to
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and
we have

\nabla_{x}F=\sum_{n=1}^{m}n\int f_{n}(x,\mathbf{y}_{n-1})\,\zeta^{n-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n-1})-\sum_{n=1}^{m}n\int f_{n}(\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\eqqcolon S(x)-\int S(z)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}z),

where the kernel functions of
F
are denoted by
f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Note that only the
terms in
S
that explicitly depend on
x
are relevant here. Indeed, since the
integral of
S
with respect to the probability measure
\zeta
is
subtracted, the terms not depending on
x
do not contribute to the
difference.
By (
3.9
), we have that
S(x)
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-1)!}\Bigg[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}}{(\theta+j)^{(m)}}\sum_{1<i_{1}<\ldots<i_{j}}\int(\rho+\delta_{y_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+\delta_{y_{i_{j}}})^{[m]}(h)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}}{(\theta+j)^{(m)}}\sum_{1<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{j}\leq n}\int(\rho+\delta_{x}+\delta_{y_{i_{2}}}+\cdots+\delta_{y_{i_{j}}})^{[m]}(h)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n})\Bigg]

\displaystyle\eqqcolon S_{1}+S_{2}(x).

Since
S_{1}
does not depend on
x
, this term does not contribute to
\nabla_{x}F
.
By Corollary
B.3
(recall definition (
B.9
)), the term
S_{2}(x)
equals

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}}{(\theta+j)^{(m)}}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\bigg(\rho^{[m]}(h)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\int h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{j}(\mathbf{y}_{j-1},x)\,\zeta^{j-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{j-1})\rho^{[m-r]}(h^{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}})\bigg)\eqqcolon S_{2,1}+S_{2,2}(x).

Only the term
S_{2,2}(x)
contributes to the gradient.
Swapping the order of summation gives

S_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{n=j}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{n-j}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}c_{n,j}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\zeta^{j-1}(h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{j}(\cdot,x))\rho^{[m-r]}(h^{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}),

where
c_{n,j}\coloneqq(\theta+2n-1)(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}((\theta+j)^{(m)})^{-1}
.
Applying Lemma
C.1
to the sum over
n
and inserting the definition of

h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{j}
for
j,r\in[m]

and pairwise distinct
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\in[m]
shows that
S_{2,2}(x)
equals

\displaystyle S_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{r,j=1}^{m}

\displaystyle\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\frac{(-1)^{m-j}}{(j-1)!(m-j)!}\times

(5.8)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{1\leq l_{1}\leq\cdots\leq l_{r}\leq j}\int h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}(y_{l_{1}},\ldots,y_{l_{r}})\,\zeta^{j-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\rho^{[m-r]}(h^{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}),

where we set
y_{j}\coloneqq x
,
j\in[m]
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose
1\leq l_{1}\leq\cdots\leq l_{r}\leq j

for some
j\in[m]
. To exploit the symmetry in (
5.8
),
we decompose the summation
as follows. First we choose
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
such that

l_{r-i+1}=\cdots=l_{r}=j
and
l_{r-i}<j
.
For
s\in[r-i]
let
n_{s}
be the number of distinct elements
of the set
\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{r-i}\}
which occur
s
times
as component of the vector
(l_{1},\ldots,l_{r-i})
. Then
k:=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r-i}

is the cardinality of
\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{r-i}\}
and
\sum^{r-i}_{s=1}sn_{s}=r-i
.
If
k\geq 1
(i.e.
i<r
), then
we write the non-zero entries of
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r-i})
in weakly descending order
as
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
. The case
k=0
is
encoded by
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\coloneqq\emptyset
.
In accordance with the literature, we call
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
a
partition

of
r-i
of
length

k
. Given such a partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
with length
k
there are

\binom{j-1}{k}k!(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\lambda_{s}!)^{-1}
sequences

1\leq l_{1}\leq\cdots\leq l_{r-i}\leq j-1
with
k
different elements associated with
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
Since

y\mapsto\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}(y)\rho^{[m-r]}(h^{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}})

is a symmetric function on
{\mathbb{X}}^{r}
, equation (
5.8
) can be written as

S_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{r,j=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{m-j}}{(j-1)!(m-j)!}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{(j-1)\land(r-i)}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{k,r-i}}\binom{j-1}{k}\frac{k!}{\prod_{s=1}^{k}\lambda_{s}!}I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},k,r,i}(x),

where for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
,
\mathcal{P}_{k,r-i}
is the set of all partitions of
r-i
of size
k
and

\displaystyle I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},k,r,i}(x)

\displaystyle:=\int\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}(\underbrace{y_{1},\ldots,y_{1}}_{\lambda_{1}\text{ times}},\ldots,\underbrace{y_{k},\ldots,y_{k}}_{\lambda_{k}\text{ times}},\underbrace{x,\ldots,x}_{i\text{ times}})\rho^{[m-r]}(h^{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}})\,\zeta^{k}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{k}).

(For
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\emptyset
, that is
r=i
and
k=0
, we set
\prod_{s=1}^{k}\lambda_{s}!:=1
.)
Changing the order of summation and simplifying the combinatorial coefficients gives

S_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{r-i}\sum^{m}_{j=k+1}\frac{(-1)^{m-j}}{(j-1)!(j-1-k)!}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{k,r-i}}\frac{1}{\prod_{s=1}^{k}\lambda_{s}!}I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},k,r,i}(x).

Here the sum over
j
comes to
0
unless
k=m-1
, in which case it equals
1
. Therefore,

S_{2,2}(x)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{r}{\mathbf{1}}\{m-1\leq r-i\}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{m-1,r-i}}\frac{1}{\prod_{s=1}^{m-1}\lambda_{s}!}I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},m-1,r,i}(x).

The constraint
m-1\leq r-i
implies that either
i=0
or
i=1
. In the first case

I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},m-1,r,i}(x)
does not depend on
x
and does not contribute to
\nabla_{x}F
.
If
i=1
, then the constraint implies
r=m
. Therefore,
it remains to consider

S^{\prime}(x):=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{P}_{m-1,m-1}}\frac{1}{\prod_{s=1}^{m-1}\lambda_{s}!}I_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},m-1,m,1}(x).

If
m=1
, then
S^{\prime}(x)
boils down to just
h_{1}(x)
.
If
m\geq 2
, then
\mathcal{P}_{m-1,m-1}
contains only the single element
(m-1)
, so that

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=I_{(m-1),m-1,m,1}(x)=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\int\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in[m]}h_{\otimes i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(\mathbf{y}_{m-1},x)\,\zeta^{m-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{m}=1}^{m}h_{i_{m}}(x)\int h^{\otimes i_{m}}(\mathbf{y}_{m-1})\,\zeta^{m-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1}).

In conclusion we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}F

\displaystyle=S(x)-\int S(z)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(x)\int h^{\otimes i}(\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{m-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})-\int\sum_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(z)\int h^{\otimes i}(\mathbf{y})\,\zeta^{m-1}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y})\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}z),

which equals
\nabla_{x}^{\ast}F
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
F\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be measurable.
The authors of
[
10
]
have introduced a
discrete space-size gradient
(x,t)\mapsto D_{x,t}F
of
F
by

D_{x,t}F(\mu):=F((1-t)\mu+t\delta_{x})-F(\mu),\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{X}}\times[0,1],\,\mu\in{\mathbf{M}}_{1}({\mathbb{X}}).

(5.9)

Proposition
5.3
shows that

\nabla_{x}F(\zeta(\omega))=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}D_{x,t}F(\zeta(\omega))\Big|_{t=0},\quad C_{\zeta}\text{-a.e.\ $(\omega,x)$},

provided that
F\in{\mathbf{S}}
. As far as we can see, there is no simple way to relate the associated discrete divergence from

[
10
]
with our Malliavin divergence; see also Remark
6.3
.

Let us introduce a bilinear form
\mathcal{E}_{1}
on

\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)\times\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)

by

\mathcal{E}_{1}(F,G)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}F(\zeta)G(\zeta)+\mathcal{E}(F,G),\quad F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla).

Proposition 5.5
.

The closure of
{\mathbf{S}}
with respect to

\mathcal{E}_{1}
coincides with
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
.

Proof.

We already know from Lemma
5.2
and Proposition
5.3

that
{\mathbf{S}}\subset\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and that
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
is closed.
It remains to show that each
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
can be approximated by members of

{\mathbf{S}}
. By (
3.5
) and (
4.4
), we have

\mathcal{E}_{1}(F,F)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{n!+(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}^{2}\big).

Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=k+1}\frac{n!+(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}^{2}\big)\leq\varepsilon.

We consider the square integrable random variable

F_{0}:=\sum^{k}_{n=0}\zeta^{n}(f_{n}).

Let us introduce the subspace
{\mathbf{S}}_{0}
of
{\mathbf{S}}
spanned by
the functions

\mu\mapsto\mu(h_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot\mu(h_{m}),

where
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\in\mathbf{F}_{b}({\mathbb{X}})
.
We have seen in the proof of Lemma
5.1
that

{\mathbf{S}}_{0}
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathrm{Dir}}(\rho))
. Hence, there exists

\tilde{G}\in{\mathbf{S}}_{0}
such that
\mathbb{E}(F_{0}-\tilde{G}(\zeta))^{2}\leq c^{-1}\varepsilon
, where

c:=1+(\theta+k-1)k
. Let
G
be the orthogonal projection of

\tilde{G}(\zeta)
onto
{\mathbf{F}}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathbf{F}}_{m}
.
Then
\mathbb{E}(F_{0}-G)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}(F_{0}-\tilde{G})^{2}
.
Let
g_{n}
,
0\leq n\leq k
, denote the kernel functions of
G
.
By definition of
{\mathbf{S}}_{0}
, Lemma
3.10
and Corollary
B.3
,
we have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. that
G=G_{0}(\zeta)
for some
G_{0}\in{\mathbf{S}}_{0}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(F-G,F-G)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{n!+(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}-g_{n}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\sum^{k}_{n=0}\frac{n!+(\theta+n-1)nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}-g_{n}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+c\sum^{k}_{n=0}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}-g_{n}\big)^{2}=\varepsilon+c\,\mathbb{E}(F_{0}-G)^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Define a bilinear form

{\mathcal{E}}^{*}\colon{\mathbf{S}}\times{\mathbf{S}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(F,G):=\mathbb{E}\int\nabla^{*}_{x}F\nabla^{*}_{x}G\,\zeta(dx),\quad F,G\in{\mathbf{S}}.

(5.10)

It follows from Propositions
5.3
and
5.5
that

(\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)\times\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),{\mathcal{E}})

is the closure of
({\mathbf{S}}\times{\mathbf{S}},{\mathcal{E}}^{*})
; see
[
11
]

for terminology. Therefore, we obtain from the general theory in
[
11
]

(and elementary properties of
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}
) that

(\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),{\mathcal{E}})
is a Dirichlet form; see also
[
29
]
.

The
generator

\tilde{L}
associated with the Dirichlet form
(\operatorname{dom}(\nabla),{\mathcal{E}})

is a linear mapping from a subspace of
\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
into
L^{2}(\zeta)

which is defined as follows. The domain

\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{L})
of
\tilde{L}
is the set of all
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)

such that there exists
H\in L^{2}(\zeta)
satisfying

{\mathcal{E}}(F,G)=\mathbb{E}HG,\quad G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla).

(5.11)

In this case one defines
\tilde{L}(F):=H(\zeta)
. Our next theorem shows that
\tilde{L}
coincides
with the operator
L
, introduced by (
4.21
) via chaos expansion. This justifies
to refer to
L
as Fleming–Viot operator.

Theorem 5.7
.

We have

\operatorname{dom}(L)=\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{L})
and

\tilde{L}F=LF,\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},\,F\in\operatorname{dom}(L).

(5.12)

Proof.

The inclusion
\operatorname{dom}(L)\subset\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{L})

and
\tilde{L}F=LF
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
follow from
(
5.6
).

Conversely, let
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\tilde{L})
,

G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and
H\in L^{2}(\zeta)
satisfy
(
5.11
). Choosing
G\equiv 1

shows
\mathbb{E}H=0
. Let
h_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote the kernel
functions in the chaos expansion of
H
. We can proceed exactly as in
the proof of Theorem
4.16

(cf. (
4.27
) and
(
4.28
)) to show
h_{n}=(\theta+n-1)f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Because of
H\in L^{2}(\zeta)
, the series

\mathbb{E}H(\zeta)^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}(h_{n}^{2})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!n^{2}(\theta+n-1)^{2}}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}^{2})

converges. Thus,
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

It was shown in
[
29
]
that

(L_{\rho}F)(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{L}F
holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for each
F\in{\mathbf{S}}
.
Hence, we obtain (
5.2
) from Theorem
5.7
.
Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between
closed symmetric forms and non-positive definite
self-adjoint operators (cf. Theorem 1.3.1
in
[
11
]
) and
\mathcal{E}
is generated by
L
,
we further obtain that
L
is the closure of
2L_{\rho}
.

Given
t\geq 0
we introduce a linear operator
T_{t}\colon L^{2}(\zeta)\to L^{2}(\zeta)
,
by

T_{t}(F):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}\int f_{n}(x)\,\zeta^{n}(\mathrm{d}x),

where the
f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are the kernel function of
F\in L^{2}(\zeta)
. By (
3.5
) and dominated convergence, this series converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
and we have

T_{0}F=F
and the contraction property

\mathbb{E}T_{t}(F)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}F^{2},\quad t\geq 0.

Furthermore, we have the semigroup property
T_{s}(T_{t}F)=T_{s+t}F
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
s,t\geq 0
,
the
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
-convergence
T_{t}F\to F
as
t\to 0
and the symmetry property

\mathbb{E}T_{t}(F)G=\mathbb{E}FT_{t}(G),\quad F,G\in L^{2}(\zeta).

(5.13)

Proofs are straightforward and left to the reader. These properties
make
\{T_{t}:t\geq 0\}
a strongly
continuous semigroup in the sense of
[
11
]
.
The following result shows that
L
is the generator of this semigroup.

Proposition 5.9
.

Suppose that
H\in L^{2}(\zeta)
. Then

t^{-1}(T_{t}F-F)\to H
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
as
t\to 0
if and only if

F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
. In this case
LF=H

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Proof.

Let
f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, denote the kernel functions of
F
.
Assume that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
.
By the definitions and orthogonality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}(T_{t}(F)-F)-L(F)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}\left(e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}-1\right)+n(\theta+n-1)\right)\zeta^{n}(f_{n})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}-1}{t}+n(\theta+n-1)\right)^{2}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}^{2}).

Since
|t^{-1}(e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}-1)|\leq n(\theta+n-1)
for all

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
t\geq 0
, we can apply dominated convergence to see that
the latter series tends to
0
as
t\to 0
.

Assume now that
t^{-1}(T_{t}(F)-F)\to H
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
as
t\to 0
,
and denote the kernel functions of
H
by
h_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Since

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}(T_{t}(F)-F)-H\right)^{2}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\int\left(\frac{e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}-1}{t}f_{n}(x)-h_{n}(x)\right)^{2}\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}x),

we obtain that
f_{0}=h_{0}
and, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}\big(e^{-n(\theta+n-1)t}-1\big)f_{n}(x)=n(\theta+n-1)f_{n}(x)=h_{n}(x),\quad\rho^{[n]}\text{-a.e.\ $x$}.

We conclude that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(L)
and
\mathbb{P}(H=L(F))=1
.
∎

6
Further properties of the Malliavin operators

In this section we provide further properties of gradient and divergence.
The results are similar to the Gaussian case, but some proofs
require different arguments.
We begin with an analogue of
[
20
, Proposition 1.3.3]
;
see also
[
19
, Proposition 2.5.4]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and

H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
. Assume that

\mathbb{E}F^{2}\delta(H)^{2}<\infty
and
\mathbb{E}\int F^{2}H^{2}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
. Then

FH\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and

\delta(FH)=F\delta(H)-\int\nabla_{x}FH_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(6.1)

Proof.

Our argument is based on the useful product rule

\nabla(FG)=(\nabla F)G+F(\nabla G),\quad C_{\zeta}\text{-a.e.}

(6.2)

for
F,G\in{\mathbf{S}}
. This follows from Proposition
5.3

and the elementary fact that
\nabla^{*}
(defined by (
5.7
)) satisfies
this rule everywhere on
\Omega\times{\mathbb{X}}
.

We first assume that
F\in{\mathbf{S}}
. We take
G\in{\mathbf{S}}
and note that

FG\in{\mathbf{S}}\subset\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
.
We then obtain from
(
6.2
) and partial integration that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}G)FH_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\int\nabla_{x}(GF)H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F)GH_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}GF\delta(H)-\mathbb{E}G\int(\nabla_{x}F)H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Since
G\in{\mathbf{S}}
is arbitrary, this shows both

FH\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and (
6.1
).

Now we take a general
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. By Proposition
5.5
,
there exist
F_{n}\in{\mathbf{S}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
and

\nabla F_{n}\to\nabla F
in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
. We have already proved that

\delta(F_{n}H)=F_{n}\delta(H)-\int(\nabla_{x}F_{n})H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(6.3)

We treat both sides of this equation separately and take
G\in{\mathbf{S}}
. By partial integration,

\mathbb{E}G\delta(F_{n}H)=\mathbb{E}\int F_{n}H_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Furthermore,

\bigg|\mathbb{E}\int FH_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\mathbb{E}\int F_{n}H_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|\leq c\,\mathbb{E}|F-F_{n}|\bigg|\int H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|,

where
c
is an upper bound for
|\nabla^{*}G|
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, this can be further bounded by

c\,\|F-F_{n}\|_{2}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq c\,\|F-F_{n}\|_{2}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int H^{2}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)\bigg)^{1/2}

which tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}G\delta(F_{n}H)=\mathbb{E}\int FH_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Turning to the right-hand side of (
6.3
),
we note first that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}GF_{n}\delta(H)=\mathbb{E}GF\delta(H)

since
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
and
G
is bounded. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{E}G\int(\nabla_{x}F)

\displaystyle H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\mathbb{E}G\int(\nabla_{x}F_{n})H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg|\leq c\,\mathbb{E}\int|\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}F_{n}||H_{x}|\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\bigg(\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}F_{n})^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\int H^{2}_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\bigg)^{1/2}

which goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
. Summarising, we obtain from (
6.3
) that

\mathbb{E}\int FH_{x}\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}GF\delta(H)-\mathbb{E}G\int(\nabla_{x}F)H_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Hence, the assertions follow from our integrability assumptions.
∎

The next result provides a pathwise representation of
\delta(H^{\prime})
for specific

H^{\prime}\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
. Similarly as in the classical Gaussian case,

\delta(H^{\prime})
is the difference of a stochastic integral and a divergence term;
see
[
20
, Proposition 1.3.5]
or
[
19
, eqn. (2.5.3)]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose
F^{\prime}\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and
h\in L^{2}(\rho)
.
Define
H^{\prime}(\mu,x):=F^{\prime}(\mu)h(x)
. Assume that
F^{\prime}
is bounded. Then
H^{\prime}\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and

\delta(H^{\prime})=\int h(x)F^{\prime}\,(\theta\zeta-\rho)(\mathrm{d}x)-\int h(x)\nabla_{x}F^{\prime}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(6.4)

Proof.

We wish to apply Proposition
6.1
with
H(\mu,x)
replaced
by
H^{*}(\mu,x):=h(x)
. By Corollary
4.10
, the function
H^{*}
is in the domain of
\delta
and we have

\delta(H^{*})=\delta(h)=\theta\int h(x)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\int h(x)\,\rho(\mathrm{d}x);

see Remark
4.11
.
Since
|F^{\prime}|
is bounded by some
c\geq 0
, say, we have

\mathbb{E}(F^{\prime})^{2}\delta(H^{*})^{2}<\infty
and

\mathbb{E}\int(F^{\prime})^{2}(H^{*}_{x})^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)\leq c^{2}\mathbb{E}\int h(x)^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{c^{2}}{\theta}\int h(x)^{2}\,\rho(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty.

(6.5)

Therefore, we obtain the assertion from Proposition
6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Formula (
6.4
) can be written as

\delta(H^{\prime})=\int h(x)(\theta F^{\prime}-\nabla_{x}F^{\prime})\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)-\int h(x)F^{\prime}\,\rho(\mathrm{d}x),\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

which is of a similar form as the discrete gradient defined in
[
10
]
.

We are now in a position to extend the product rule (
6.2
).

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose that
F,G\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)

and assume that
FG\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. Then (
6.2
) holds.

Proof.

Let

H(\mu,x):=h(x)F^{\prime}(\mu)
, where
h\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is measurable
and bounded and
F^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{S}}
. By Proposition
6.2
,

H\in\operatorname{dom}(\delta)
and (
6.4
) shows that

\delta(H)
is bounded. Therefore, we can apply
Proposition
6.1
with both
F
and
G
to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\int H_{x}(\nabla_{x}F)G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)+\mathbb{E}\int H_{x}F\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}\delta(GH)F+\mathbb{E}\delta(FH)G

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\delta(H)FG-\mathbb{E}F\int\nabla_{x}GH_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)+\mathbb{E}\delta(H)FG-\mathbb{E}G\int\nabla_{x}FH_{x}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Since
FG\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
, this means that

\mathbb{E}\int H_{x}(\nabla_{x}F)G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)+\mathbb{E}\int H_{x}F\nabla_{x}G\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x)=\mathbb{E}\int H_{x}\nabla_{x}(FG)\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

(6.6)

The space
\mathbf{G}
of all functions
H
of the above type is closed under
(pointwise) multiplication and generates the
\sigma
-field

\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{X}}
. On the other hand,

\mathbf{G}
is contained in the space of all bounded and measurable

H\colon{\mathbf{M}}_{1}\times{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying (
6.6
).
Therefore, a monotone class theorem (see e.g.
[
16
, Theorem A.4]
)
shows that (
6.6
) holds for all bounded and measurable functions
H
.
This concludes the proof of (
6.2
).
∎

We continue with a chain rule which looks exactly the same as in the
Gaussian case.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and suppose that
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
.
Let
\varphi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuously differentiable with bounded partial derivatives.
Then
\varphi(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
and

\nabla(\varphi(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}))=\sum^{k}_{i=1}\partial_{i}\varphi(F)\nabla F_{i},\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

(6.7)

Proof.

If
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
, then the result
follows from Proposition
5.3
and the properties of
\nabla^{*}
.
To lighten the notation, we prove the general case in the case
k=1
, writing
F:=F_{1}
.
As in the proof of Proposition
6.1
we take
F_{n}\in{\mathbf{S}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, such that
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
and

\nabla F_{n}\to\nabla F
in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
. Let us abbreviate the norm in
L^{2}(C_{\zeta})
by

\|\cdot\|_{\zeta}
. We have

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{\prime}(F)\nabla F-\nabla(\varphi(F_{n}))\|_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi^{\prime}(F)\nabla F-\varphi^{\prime}(F_{n})\nabla F_{n}\|_{\zeta}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\varphi^{\prime}(F)-\varphi^{\prime}(F_{n}))\nabla F\|_{\zeta}+\|\varphi^{\prime}(F_{n})(\nabla F-\nabla F_{n})\|_{\zeta}.

The squared second term on the above right-hand side equals

\mathbb{E}\varphi^{\prime}(F_{n})^{2}\int(\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}F_{n})^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x),

which tends to zero since
\varphi^{\prime}
is bounded.
The squared first term equals

\mathbb{E}(\varphi^{\prime}(F)-\varphi^{\prime}(F_{n}))^{2}\int(\nabla_{x}F)^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

Since
\varphi^{\prime}
is bounded, the integrand is bounded by an integrable random variable.
Since
F_{n}\to F
in probability, it follows from dominated convergence that this first
term tends to zero as well. Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi^{\prime}(F)\nabla F-\nabla(\varphi(F_{n}))\|_{\zeta}=0.

Therefore, Lemma
4.3

implies the assertions.
∎

7
Covariance identities

Equation (
3.5
) can be written as

\CV(F,G)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}\big(f_{n}g_{n}\big),\quad F,G\in L^{2}(\zeta).

(7.1)

Combining this with our expressions (
3.6
) for the kernel functions,
we can easily derive explicit covariance formulas. If
F
is in a fixed chaos and
G
is in a finite
sum of chaoses, then their covariance is of a particularly appealing form:

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose that
h\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be measurable and bounded.
Then

\displaystyle\CV

\displaystyle[\zeta^{k}(h),\zeta^{m}(h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{m})]

(7.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+k)}}\sum^{m}_{r=1}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\rho^{[m-r]}\bigg(\bigotimes_{j\notin\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}}h_{j}\bigg)\int h^{k}_{\otimes_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}}(\mathbf{x})h(\mathbf{x})\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}),

where
h^{k}_{\otimes_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}}
is given by (
B.10
).

Proof.

We abbreviate
g:=\otimes^{m}_{j=1}h_{j}
. Since
g
is bounded, we have
\zeta^{m}(g)\in L^{2}(\zeta)
.
By (
7.1
) and orthogonality,

\CV[\zeta^{k}(h),\zeta^{m}(g)]=\frac{k!}{\theta^{(2k)}}\rho^{[k]}\big(hf_{k}\big),

(7.3)

where
f_{k}
is the
k
-th kernel function of
\zeta^{m}(h^{\otimes m})
. This function is
given by (
3.9
). Since
h\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
, we have

\rho^{[k]}\big(hf_{k}\big)=\frac{\theta+2k-1}{k!}\frac{(\theta+k)^{(k-1)}}{(\theta+k)^{(m)}}\int(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}})^{[m]}(g)h(\mathbf{x})\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).

The combinatorial coefficient in front of the integral
simplifies to
1/\theta^{(m+k)}
.
Moreover, by Proposition
B.2
and symmetry of the measures
\rho^{[n]}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle(\rho+

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathbf{x}})^{[m]}(g)=\rho^{[m]}(g)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\rho^{[m-r]}\bigg(\bigotimes_{j\notin\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}}h_{j}\bigg)\sum_{1\leq n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}\leq k}h_{i_{1}}(x_{n_{1}})\cdot\ldots\cdot h_{i_{r}}(x_{n_{r}}).

Since
\rho^{[k]}(h)=0
, we obtain the assertion.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Consider the assumptions of
Theorem
7.1
and assume
m<k
. By definition of

h^{k}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}
and since
h\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
, we have

\int h^{k}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}(\mathbf{x})h(\mathbf{x})\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x})=0

for
r<k
, so that
\CV(\zeta^{k}(h),\zeta^{m}(g))=0
.
This is in accordance with
\zeta^{m}(h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{m})\in{\mathbf{F}}_{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathbf{F}}_{m}

(see Lemma
3.10
) and the fact that the latter space is orthogonal to
{\mathbf{F}}_{k}
.

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose that
h\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let

f\colon{\mathbb{X}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be measurable and bounded and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\displaystyle\CV

\displaystyle(\zeta^{k}(h),\zeta^{m}(f^{\otimes m}))

(7.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+k)}}\sum^{m}_{r=1}\frac{m!}{(m-r)!}\rho^{[m-r]}(f^{\otimes(m-r)})\sum^{r}_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=1\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=r\end{subarray}}}\int f(x_{1})^{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot f(x_{k})^{j_{k}}h(\mathbf{x})\,\rho^{[k]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}).

Proof.

We apply Theorem
7.1
with
h_{1}=\cdots=h_{m}=f
. Then

f^{k}_{\otimes_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}\leq\cdots\leq n_{r}\leq k}f(x_{n_{1}})\cdot\ldots\cdot f(x_{n_{r}})=\sum^{r}_{{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=r\end{subarray}}}f(x_{1})^{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot f(x_{k})^{j_{k}},

independently of
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
. Hence, we obtain (
7.4
) from (
7.2
)
using that
h\in{\mathbf{H}}_{k}
.
∎

In the special case

yields the following result.

Suppose that

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\theta^{(m+2)}}\sum^{m}_{r=1}\frac{m!}{(m-r)!}\rho^{[m-r]}(f^{\otimes(m-r)})\sum^{r-1}_{j=1}\int h(x_{1},x_{2})f(x_{1})^{j}f(x_{2})^{r-j}\,\rho^{2}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},x_{2})).

The result follows from (

8
The Poincaré inequality

The Poincaré inequality for functions of the DF process was derived
in
[
29
]
using the Poincaré inequality for the Dirichlet distribution
(proved in
[
26
]
) and
a suitable approximation. We can derive here this inequality directly from
the chaos expansion (
3.4
).

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{dom}(\nabla)
. Then

\BV(F(\zeta))\leq\frac{1}{\theta}\,\mathbb{E}\int(\nabla_{x}F)^{2}\,\zeta(\mathrm{d}x).

(8.1)

Equality holds if and only if
there exists
g\in L^{2}(\rho)
such that
F(\zeta)=\zeta(g)

\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

Proof.

From (
3.5
) we obtain that the left-hand side of (
8.1
) equals

\mathbb{E}(F(\zeta)-\mathbb{E}F(\zeta))^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n!}{\theta^{(2n)}}\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}^{2}),

where
f_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, are the kernel functions of
F
.
By (
4.4
), the right-hand side of (
8.1
) equals

\frac{1}{\theta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{nn!}{\theta^{(2n)}}(\theta+n-1)\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}^{2}).

Since
1\leq\theta^{-1}n(\theta+n-1)
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, inequality
(
8.1
) follows. The latter inequality becomes an
equality if and only if
n=1
.
Therefore, we have equality in (
8.1
) if and only if

\rho^{[n]}(f_{n}^{2})=0,\quad n\geq 2.

By Theorem
3.3
, this is in turn equivalent to
F\in\mathbf{F}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{F}_{1}
.
∎

Appendix A
Orthogonality relations

Let
\mu
be a
\sigma
-finite measure on
{\mathbb{X}}
.
Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
B\in{\mathcal{X}}^{\otimes(n+m)}
. A key ingredient of our proof is the recursion

\displaystyle\mu^{[n+m]}(B)=\iint{\mathbf{1}}_{B}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m})

\displaystyle\,(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{m}})^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{m+1},\ldots,x_{m+n}))

(A.1)

\displaystyle\times\mu^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})).

This can be proved by induction.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
m,n,r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
so that
r<n
. Further, let
f\colon{\mathbb{X}}^{m+r+n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
f\in L^{1}(\rho^{[m+r+n]})
and

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+r},\cdot)\in{\mathbf{H}}_{n}
for
\rho^{[m+r]}
-almost all
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+r})\in{\mathbb{X}}^{m+r}
. Then

\displaystyle\int f

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-r})\in[m]^{[n-r]}}\int f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n-r}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\,\rho^{[m+r]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}))

whenever
m\geq n-r
.
If
m<n-r
, the integral on the left-hand side vanishes.

Proof.

In the case
m\geq n-r

the recursion (
A.1
) and the defining property of
{\mathbf{H}}_{n}
yields

\displaystyle\int f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle=\iiint f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n})\,(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{m}}+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{n-1}})(\mathrm{d}y_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n-1]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{y}_{n-1},\mathbf{x}_{m}))

\displaystyle=\iint\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{m}f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n-1},x_{i_{1}})\,(\rho+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{n-1}})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}_{m})\,\rho^{[n-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{n-1}).

Further iterations give

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle=\int\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n-r}\in[m]}f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{r},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n-r}})\,\rho^{[m+r]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r})),

as asserted.
If
m<n-r
, then the above argument gives

\displaystyle\int f

\displaystyle(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n})\,\rho^{[m+n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle=m!\int f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{r},\mathbf{y}_{n-m},\mathbf{x}_{m})\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{n-m})).

This comes to zero after applying the recursion (
A.1
) one more time.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

Let
n,l,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
n\geq l\geq k
. Then
g\in{\mathbf{H}}_{n}
and

satisfy

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle g(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l-k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})\,\rho^{[l+n-k]}(\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l-k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}))

\displaystyle={\mathbf{1}}_{\{l=n\}}(n-k)!\int g(z)h(z)\,\rho^{[n]}(\mathrm{d}z).

Proof.

If
k=n
, then

and there is nothing to prove.
Now assume
k<n
and define

by

.
Since

and

are square-integrable, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality shows that

.
Applying Lemma

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x}_{l-k},\mathbf{y}_{k})g(\mathbf{y}_{n})\,\rho^{[l-k+n]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{l-k},\mathbf{y}_{n}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-k})\in[l-k]^{[n-k]}}\int h(\mathbf{x}_{l-k},\mathbf{y}_{k})g(\mathbf{y}_{k},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n-k}})\,\rho^{[l]}(\mathrm{d}(\mathbf{x}_{l-k},\mathbf{y}_{k})).

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\mu
be a

\sigma
-finite measure on
\mathbb{X}
and
x\in\mathbb{X}
. Then we have for each

m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and each measurable

that

(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m]}(g)=\mu^{[m]}(g)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m-1]}(g_{i}(x,\cdot)).

(B.4)

This remains true for all measurable and integrable
g\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

which are integrable with respect to
\mu+\delta_{x}
.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion
by induction over
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
The base case is

(\mu+\delta_{x})(g)=\mu(g)+g(x).

We now assume that the assertion hols for all measurable functions

g\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
. Let

g\colon\mathbb{X}^{m+1}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be measurable.
From the recursion (
A.1
) we obtain

(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m+1]}(g)=\iint g(\mathbf{y}_{m+1})\,(\mu+\delta_{x}+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{m}})(\mathrm{d}y_{m+1})\,(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m}).

Applying the induction hypothesis to the mapping

\mathbf{y}_{m}\mapsto(\mu+\delta_{x}+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{m}})(g(\mathbf{y}_{m},\cdot))

and using the recursion (
A.1
) gives

\displaystyle(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m+1]}

\displaystyle(g)=\int g(\mathbf{y}_{m+1})\,\mu^{[m+1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m+1})+\int g(\mathbf{y}_{m},x)\,\mu^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\iint g_{i}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1},y_{m+1})\,(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{m-1}}+\delta_{x})(\mathrm{d}y_{m+1})\,(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int g_{i}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1},x)\,(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m+1]}(g)

\displaystyle=\mu^{[m+1]}(g)+\mu^{[m]}(g_{m+1}(x,\cdot))+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m]}(g_{i}(x,\cdot))

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int g_{m+1,i}(x,x,\mathbf{y}_{m-1})\,(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m-1]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m-1}).

(B.5)

An application of the induction hypothesis to the function
\mathbf{y}_{m}\mapsto g_{m+1}(x,\mathbf{y}_{m})
shows

\displaystyle(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m]}(g_{m+1}(x,\cdot))=\mu^{[m]}(g_{m+1}(x,\cdot))+\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu+\delta_{x})^{[m-1]}(g_{m+1,i}(x,x,\cdot)).

Inserting this into (
B.5

Assume now that

.
Applying (
B.4
) to the function
|g|
shows that all resulting terms in (

) are finite.
Therefore, we obtain the assertion from (
B.4

into its negative
and positive part.

The next proposition deals with the addition of several Dirac measures.

Proposition B.2
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure. Then we have for all
m,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
and all
measurable functions
f\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\to[0,\infty]

that

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle f(z)\,(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{k}})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}z)=\int f(z)\,\mu^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}z)

(B.6)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},z_{1},\ldots,z_{m-r})\,\mu^{[m-r]}(\mathrm{d}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m-r})).

This remains true for all measurable
f\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

which are integrable with respect to
\mu+\delta_{x_{1}}+\cdots+\delta_{x_{k}}
.

We prove the assertion by induction on
m
. If
m=1
and

f\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\to[0,\infty]
is measurable, then

(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k}})\left(f\right)=\mu\left(f\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(x_{i})=\mu\left(f\right)+f^{k}_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})

by definition of
f^{k}_{1}
.
For the induction step we assume that the assertion holds for some

m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{X}
as well as
all measurable
f\colon\mathbb{X}^{m}\to[0,\infty]
. To establish the formula for

m+1
, we use induction on
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Take a measurable
f\colon\mathbb{X}^{m+1}\to[0,\infty]
and
x_{1}\in{\mathbb{X}}
.
By Lemma
B.1
,

(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}})^{[m+1]}(f)=\mu^{[m+1]}(f)+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\int f_{i}(x_{1},\mathbf{y}_{m})\,(\mu+\delta_{x_{1}})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m}).

Let
i\in[m+1]
. The induction hypothesis in the induction on
m

applied to the function

\mathbf{y}_{m}\mapsto f_{i}(x_{1},\mathbf{y}_{m})

shows that the second term on the above right-hand side equals

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\int f_{i}(x_{1},\mathbf{y}_{m})\,\mu^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m})+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r+1}\in[m+1]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r+1}}^{1}(x_{1},\mathbf{z}_{m-r})\,\mu^{[m-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m-r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m+1]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{1}(x_{1},\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})\,\mu^{[m+1-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r}).

This finishes the base case of the induction on
k
.
Next, we assume that the assertion holds for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k+1}\in{\mathbb{X}}
. By
Lemma
B.1
,

(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k+1}})^{[m+1]}(f)=(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k}})^{[m+1]}(f)+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\int f^{(i)}(\mathbf{y}_{m})\,(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k+1}})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m}),

where
f^{(i)}(\mathbf{y}_{m})\coloneqq f_{i}(x_{k+1},\mathbf{y}_{m})
for
\mathbf{y}_{m}\in\mathbb{X}^{m}
and
i\in[m+1]
.
Using the induction hypothesis in the induction on
k
, we obtain

(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k}})^{[m+1]}(f)=\mu^{[m+1]}(f)+\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m+1]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{k}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})\,\mu^{[m+1-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r}).

Let
i\in[m+1]
. The induction hypothesis in the induction on

\displaystyle\int

\displaystyle f^{(i)}(\mathbf{y}_{m})\,(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k+1}})^{[m]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}_{m})

Combining these findings, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k+1}})^{[m+1]}(f)=\mu^{[m+1]}(f)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m+1]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{k}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})\,\mu^{[m+1-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\mu^{[m]}(f^{(i)})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m]}\int(f^{(i)})_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x}_{k+1},\mathbf{z}_{m-r})\,\mu^{[m-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m-r})

\displaystyle=:(\mu+\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{k+1}})^{[m+1]}(f)+A+B.

We need to show that

\sum_{r=1}^{m+1}\quad\sideset{}{{}^{\neq}}{\sum}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\in[m+1]}\int f_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}}^{k+1}(\mathbf{x}_{k+1},\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})\,\mu^{[m+1-r]}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{z}_{m+1-r})=A+B.

Appendix C
A summation formula for rising factorials

The following formula has been used in the proof of Proposition
5.3
.
Since we have not found the result in the literature, we give the simple proof.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\theta>0
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
m\geq 2
.
For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
it holds

\sum_{n=j}^{m}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-j)!}(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}=(-1)^{m-j}\frac{(\theta+j)^{(m)}}{(m-j)!}.

Proof.

Let
S
denote the left-hand side of the asserted identity.
We have

\displaystyle S=(\theta+2j-1)(\theta+j)^{(j-1)}

\displaystyle-(\theta+2j+1)(\theta+j)^{(j)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-j)!}(\theta+j)^{(n-1)}.

Using the definition of rising factorials, we can write
S
as

S=(\theta+j)^{(j)}\left(-(\theta+2j)+\sum_{n=j+2}^{m}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-j)!}\prod_{k=j}^{n-2}(\theta+k+j)\right).

In particular, we obtain the assertion for
j=m-1
.
In the case
j\leq m-2
we have

S=(\theta+j)^{(j+1)}\left(-1+\frac{\theta+2j+3}{2!}+\sum_{n=j+3}^{m}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-j)!}\prod_{k=j+1}^{n-2}(\theta+k+j)\right),

that is

S=\frac{(\theta+j)^{(j+2)}}{2}\left(1+2\sum_{n=j+3}^{m}(-1)^{n-j}\frac{\theta+2n-1}{(n-j)!}\prod_{k=j+2}^{n-2}(\theta+k+j)\right).

In particular, we obtain the assertion for
j=m-2
.
The general case follows inductively.
∎
