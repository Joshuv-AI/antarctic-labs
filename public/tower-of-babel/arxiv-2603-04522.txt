Regge trajectories from the adjoint sector of Matrix Quantum Mechanics
Igor R. Klebanov, Henry W. Lin, Pavel Meshcheriakov

Abstract
We reexamine the large N N limit of SU ( N ) (N) symmetric quantum mechanics of a Hermitian matrix whose singlet sector is well known to be exactly solvable via free fermions. When the Fermi level approaches a maximum of the potential, there is critical behavior corresponding to string theory in two dimensions. We uncover new phenomena in the adjoint sector by solving the Marchesini-Onofri equation both numerically and analytically using semiclassical approximations: at criticality, the spectrum is governed by Regge trajectories with energy eigenvalues growing according to Δ 2 ∼ n / α ′ \Delta^{2}\sim n/\alpha^{\prime} . In the dual 2D string theory, we interpret these states as oscillatory excitations of a “short” folded open string. Up to sub-leading corrections, this Regge behavior is essentially universal and is insensitive to the particular potential we choose to approach criticality. Slightly away from criticality, the highly excited states transition into “long strings” that extend far into the Liouville direction.

II
Marchesini-Onofri equation near criticality

For the SU
(N)
invariant (singlet) sector of MQM, it is well known
8
that the system is described by
N
non interacting fermions. The ground state is obtained by filling the first
N
energy levels in potential
V
.
In the WKB approximation, the singlet excitation gap is given by the classical frequency
\omega(g)

\frac{2\pi}{\omega(g)}=\frac{1}{\pi}\oint\limits\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\rho(x)},\qquad\rho(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{2(\mu_{F}-V(x))}\ .\\

(4)

Here
\rho
is the eigenvalue density (or the fermion density
8
) in the ground state, which is supported between
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, the classical turning points of the potential
V(x)
(for even potentials
x_{2}=-x_{1}=a
).
For the first non-trivial representation of SU
(N)
, which is the adjoint representation, the situation is more complicated. In
28
, it was shown that the spectrum of the adjoint sector, in the large-
N
limit, is structured as follows: for each singlet state with energy
E_{s}^{\rm singlet}
, there is an infinite tower of bound states in the adjoint sector with energies
E_{(s,n)}^{\rm adjoint}

E_{(s,n)}^{\rm adjoint}(g)=E_{s}^{\rm singlet}(g)+\Delta_{n}(g)\ ,\quad n=1,2,\dots

(5)

The structure of this tower does not depend on the choice of the
s
-th singlet state:
\Delta_{n}(g)
is a universal gap between the
n
-th state in the tower and the singlet state.

To study these gaps in the large
N
limit, Marchesini and Onofri
28
derived the singular integral equation

\Delta_{n}\,\Phi_{n}(x)=\fint_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\mathrm{d}y\,\rho(y)\frac{\Phi_{n}(x)-\Phi_{n}(y)}{(x-y)^{2}}\ ,

(6)

where
\Phi_{n}(x)
are the eigenfunctions associated with the corresponding adjoint states.

The MO equation (
6
) resembles the well-known ’t Hooft equation
34
, which describes mesons in two-dimensional large
N
QCD. In fact, for
V(x)=0
, the MO equation becomes equivalent to the ’t Hooft equation for massless quarks, and
\Delta
is then proportional to the meson mass-squared. In this case, the spectrum consists of two Regge trajectories of mesons
15
;
24
;
3
. This is qualitatively similar to the two Regge trajectories (
3
).
For the ’t Hooft model the trajectories are asymptotically linear, and their string theoretic interpretation is in terms of longitudinal oscillations of 2D open strings with moving endpoints
5
.

The eigenfunctions
\Phi_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
, are orthonormal with respect to the measure
\rho(x)\mathrm{d}x
, e.g.,
\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\mathrm{d}x\,\rho(x)\,\Phi^{*}_{n}(x)\Phi_{m}(x)=\delta_{nm}\,

while the adjoint representation constraint
28
;
26

\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\mathrm{d}x\,\rho(x)\,\Phi_{n}(x)=0

removes the constant eigenfunction with eigenvalue
0
. The lowest eigenvalue
\Delta_{1}
corresponds to
\Phi_{1}(x)
, which is an odd function for quartic potential (
1
).

After introducing
\phi_{n}(x)=\rho(x)\,\Phi_{n}(x)
, the MO equation becomes

\Delta_{n}\,\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{H}\,\phi_{n}(x)
. The effective Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
is defined by
28

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\,\phi_{n}(x)=-\rho(x)\fint_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\phi_{n}(y)}{(x-y)^{2}}+\eta(g,x)\phi_{n}(x)\ ,

(7)

where
\eta(\mu,x)
defined below plays the role of “potential,”

\eta(g,x)\equiv\fint_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{\rho(y)}{(x-y)^{2}}\ ,\qquad\eta(g)=\frac{\omega(g)}{\pi}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\limits\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\pi}\frac{\eta(g,x)}{\rho(x)}\ ,

(8)

and the quantity
\eta(g)
in (
2
) corresponds to the average potential over one period.
To study the MO equation,
we will find it convenient to switch from

to the time of flight
\tau

\mu_{F}

\tau=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{x}\limits\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\rho(y)}\ .

(9)

This change of variables has proven useful for studying the singlet sector of MQM

;
23

For the quartic potential,
\tau\in[-\tau_{\rm max}(g),\,\tau_{\rm max}(g)]
, and
\tau_{\rm max}
diverges at criticality as

The potential

A natural question is whether our results depend sensitively on the UV. Since the continuum string is emerging from the discrete sum over planar diagrams, we may ask whether the Regge trajectory depends on whether we use a triangular random lattice, etc. This is equivalent to asking whether our results depend on the matrix potential

and
\log|\mu|
. For the cubic potential

The crossover level

may be estimated by matching the Regge (short-string) trajectory to the long-string scale, equivalently by
\Delta_{\rm max}\simeq\eta_{\rm max}
. From the point of view of the 2D string dual,

is the scale at which the tip of the fold reaches the value where the Liouville potential becomes significant. This occurs when

(20)

3

III
Discussion

In this paper, we clarified the structure of adjoint states in the SU
(N)
symmetric matrix quantum mechanics tuned near the critical coupling and proposed their interpretation in terms of 2D string theory. It is of obvious interest to study also the SU
(N)
representations higher than the adjoint, whose dimensions grow faster than
N^{2}
. In general, the allowed representations are all those whose Young diagrams have the number of boxes divisible by
N

7
. The four representations whose dimensions grow as
N^{4}
are the self-conjugate representations
B_{2}
of dimension
\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(N+3)(N-1)
and
C_{2}
of dimension
\frac{1}{4}N^{2}(N-3)(N+1)
, as well as
A_{2}
of dimension
\frac{1}{4}(N^{2}-4)(N^{2}-1)
and its conjugate.
We find that the energy levels in the self-conjugate representations are given by sums of MO eigenvalues,
\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{j}
, up to
1/N
corrections. The string theoretic interpretation of these states involves two folded strings, and higher presentations involve multiple folds
4
. A more detailed study of the representations higher than the adjoint, which are useful for the thermodynamics of the MQM, will be reported later.

In
18
;
19
, it was suggested that the contributions of non-singlet states to the free energy lead to a thermal phase transition. The non-singlets are suppressed at low temperatures, but they become important above the critical temperature, making this similar to the deconfinement transition in QCD. Evidence from the bootstrap for the existence of a critical temperature was reported in
12
. From the point of view of the discretized string world sheet, this is expected to be the BKT transition caused by the vortices
18
;
19
. However, an estimate of the critical temperature in
19
based on the WKB approximation appeared to produce a factor of
2
difference from the continuum formula
T_{\rm BKT}=\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}
.
Due to our new finding that the WKB approximation (
2
) is valid only for
n\gtrsim n_{\rm max}
, the equally spaced spectrum of quartic theory begins at
\Delta\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\log\frac{4}{|g_{c}|\mu}
, which is twice the value assumed in
19
. This appears to change the estimate of the critical temperature in MQM, but we leave a detailed study for the future.

We expect that the scattering phase of long strings
26
;
16
;
4
is conceptually related to the discrete “bound states” in our MO analysis via a Lüscher-like relation. In MQM in the double scaling limit, where one naively takes the approximation
V=-\mathrm{tr}\,X^{2}
, there is only the Liouville wall in the IR and we have a continuum of “long string” scattering states. But in the MQM that one actually gets from approaching
g\to g_{c}
there is a “UV wall” in addition to the IR Liouville wall. So we have bound states instead of a continuum, but as
g\to g_{c}
the continuum scattering phase shift can nevertheless be extracted from the theory in a finite box. Conversely, the scattering phase shift should allow us to smoothly interpolate between the low-energy Regge regime and the high-energy WKB approximation; such states have an energy
\Delta=\Delta_{\text{max}}+\mathcal{O}(1)
. Note that there are
\sim\log\mu
states in this transition region, which is large but significantly less than the total number of Regge levels
\sim\log^{2}\mu
. We hope to report on the physics of this transition region in future work.

Appendix A
Analytic results for high-energy WKB

In this appendix, we collect the explicit expressions for high-energy WKB parameters
\omega(g)
and
\eta(g)
entering (
2
) for the cubic and double-well potentials. We use the complete elliptic integrals of the first kind,
K(k)
, and second kind,
E(k)
, with their asymptotics near
k\to 1

\begin{aligned} K(k)=&\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{m}}{m!}\right)^{2}(1-k^{2})^{m}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{1-k^{2}}+\psi(1+m)-\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}+m\right)\right]\ ,\\
E(k)=&1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{1}{2})_{m}(\frac{3}{2})_{m}}{(m+1)!\,m!}(1-k^{2})^{m+1}\left[\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{1}{1-k^{2}}+\psi(1+m)-\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}+m\right)-\frac{1}{(2m+1)(2m+2)}\right],\end{aligned}

where
(n)_{m}
is the Pochhammer symbol,
\psi(n)
is the digamma function.

For the cubic potential, let
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
be the three real turning points defined as the roots of
\mu_{F}=V_{\rm cubic}(x)
, and introduce the elliptic modulus
k_{\rm cubic}^{2}=\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{x_{3}-x_{1}}
. Then

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm cubic}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\sqrt{2|g|(x_{3}-x_{1})}}{2K(k_{\rm cubic})},

(22)

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm cubic}(g)

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\omega_{\rm cubic}(g)}{\pi^{2}}K(k_{\rm cubic})\bigg[\frac{1+6gx_{3}}{g(x_{3}-x_{1})}\,K(k_{\rm cubic})-6E(k_{\rm cubic})+

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4g(x_{2}-x_{1})K(k_{\rm cubic})}\left(\frac{x_{2}+3gx^{2}_{2}}{x_{3}-x_{2}}-\frac{x_{1}+3gx^{2}_{1}}{x_{3}-x_{1}}\right)\bigg]\ .

Near criticality,
\mu\to 0
, the corresponding asymptotics are

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm cubic}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\log\frac{8}{g_{c}^{2}\mu}}-\frac{15\pi g_{c}^{2}\mu}{\log\frac{8}{g_{c}^{2}\mu}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\mu}{\log^{2}\mu}\right),

(23)

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm cubic}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\frac{8}{g_{c}^{2}\mu}-\frac{3}{\pi}-\frac{\pi}{\log\frac{8}{g_{c}^{2}\mu}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\mu\log\mu\right).

For the double-well potential, one must distinguish
\mu<0
and
\mu>0
. When
\mu<0
(so
\mu_{F}=-\mu>0
), the equation
\mu_{F}=V_{\rm dw}(x)
has two real turning points
\pm a
, while the remaining roots are purely imaginary
\pm ib
(with
a>b>0
); we define
k_{\rm dw^{-}}^{2}=\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}\geq 1
. In this case,

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm dw^{-}}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\frac{\sqrt{2g}\,b}{K(ik_{\rm dw^{-}})},

(24)

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm dw^{-}}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\omega_{\rm dw^{-}}(g)}{\pi^{2}}\,K(ik_{\rm dw^{-}})\left[(3+k_{\rm dw^{-}}^{2})K(ik_{\rm dw^{-}})-4E(ik_{\rm dw^{-}})-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4K(ik_{\rm dw^{-}})}\right].

For
\mu\to 0^{-}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm dw^{-}}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\log\frac{4}{g_{c}|\mu|}}+\frac{3\pi g_{c}|\mu|}{\log\frac{4}{g_{c}|\mu|}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\mu|}{\log^{2}|\mu|}\right)\ ,

K(ik)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}K\left(\frac{k}{\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}\right)

\pm a,\pm b

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm dw^{+}}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\sqrt{2g}\,b}{K(k_{\rm dw^{+}})}\ ,

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm dw^{+}}(g)

Appendix B
Numerical diagonalization

We start with the MO equation (
6
) for the quartic potential (
1
).
We rescale the
x\to ax
coordinate and introduce
A=\frac{ga^{4}}{\mu_{F}}\leq 1
such that

\Delta\,\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-A}}{\fint_{-1}^{1}\limits\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{(1-y^{2})(1+Ay^{2})}}{(x-y)^{2}}}\left(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\right)\ .

(33)

We discretize the integrand on a grid

x_{k}=\cos\left(\phi_{k}\right)\ ,\quad\phi_{k}=\frac{k\pi}{M+1}\ ,\quad k=1,2,\dots,M\ .

(34)

Then the MO equation is

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\,\phi_{n}(x_{j})

\displaystyle=K_{jk}\phi_{n}(x_{k})\ ,

(35)

\displaystyle K_{jk}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{M+1}\frac{\sin^{2}\phi_{k}}{\sqrt{1-A}}\frac{\sqrt{1+A\cos^{2}\phi_{k}}}{(\cos\phi_{j}-\cos\phi_{k})^{2}},\qquad j\neq k\ ,

(36)

\displaystyle K_{jj}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\neq j}K_{jk}\ .

(37)

For numerical stability, it is more convenient to conjugate this operator
K\to H=DKD^{-1}
, where
H
is a Hermitian operator and
D
is the diagonal matrix
D_{jk}=\delta_{jk}{\sin\phi_{k}(1+A\cos^{2}\phi_{k})^{1/4}}
.

H_{jk}=-\frac{1}{M+1}\frac{\sin\phi_{j}\sin\phi_{k}}{\sqrt{1-A}}\frac{(1+A\cos^{2}\phi_{k})^{1/4}(1+A\cos^{2}\phi_{k})^{1/4}}{(\cos\phi_{j}-\cos\phi_{k})^{2}}\ ,\quad j\neq k\ ,

(38)

The low-lying spectrum can be efficiently computed using the Lanczos algorithm.
We then performed a large
M
extrapolation for each eigenvalue that was obtained, e.g., we fit a function

\Delta^{M}_{n}=\Delta_{n}+\frac{1}{M}a_{n}+\frac{1}{M^{2}}b_{n}+\frac{1}{M^{3}}c_{n}+\frac{1}{M^{4}}d_{n}\ .

(39)

The results of this large
M
extrapolation are reported in Figure
2
. For this plot we used values of
M
ranging from
M=\{25000,26000,27000,\dots,40000\}
. Note that the eigenfunctions are highly oscillatory for large energies and therefore, to probe the “long string” states, one has to consider rather large values of
M
to accurately find these states for small
\mu
.

M=40000
to 46000 with increments of 2000. We performed a large
M

\Delta_{n}^{M}=\Delta_{n}+a_{n}/M+b_{b}/M^{2}
. We used these slightly different parameters since at
\mu=10^{-14}

n_{\text{max}}
is appreciably lower in the cubic than in the quartic; hence the eigenvalues at

.

Appendix C
Effect of the linear dilaton background

Let us consider the world sheet action of 2D string theory, which includes the linear dilaton background,

S\left[\phi\right]=\int d^{2}\sigma\sqrt{-\operatorname{det}g}\left[\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}g^{ab}(-\partial_{a}X^{0}\partial_{b}X^{0}+\partial_{a}\phi\partial_{b}\phi)+\frac{1}{4\pi}Q\phi R(g)-\mu e^{2\phi/\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}\right]\ .

(40)

With these conventions,
c_{\phi}=1+6\alpha^{\prime}Q^{2}
, so for
c_{\phi}=25
we should set
Q=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\alpha^{\prime}}}
.
We work in the static gauge where
X^{0}=\tau
and set
\mu=0
so that we focus on the dynamics far from the Liouville wall.
A classical solution
26
is

\phi(\tau,\sigma)=\phi_{0}^{\prime}-Q\alpha^{\prime}\log\left(\cosh\frac{\tau}{\alpha^{\prime}Q}+\cosh\frac{\sigma}{\alpha^{\prime}Q}\right)\ .

(41)

So the tip
\phi_{\text{tip}}=\phi(\tau,0)
at
\sigma=0
moves according to

\phi(\tau)=\phi_{0}-2Q\alpha^{\prime}\log\cosh(\frac{\tau}{\alpha' Q})\ .

(42)

From this solution we can derive the space-time energy, which in static gauge is just
E=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int\mathrm{d}\sigma
. This quantity is divergent; to regulate it we cut off the world sheet at
\phi=\phi_{c}
. Then the spacetime energy for the folded string is

E=\frac{1}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}(\phi_{0}-\phi_{c})=\frac{\phi-\phi_{c}}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{Q}{\pi}\log(1-v^2)\ .

(43)

Here
v=\mathrm{d}\phi/\mathrm{d}\tau
.
We can rewrite the Hamiltonian in canonical variables, by using the relation
v=\dot{\phi}=\partial H/\partial P
or
\mathrm{d}P=\frac{1}{v}\mathrm{d}E
:

P=\int\frac{1}{v}\frac{\partial E}{\partial v}\,\mathrm{d}v=\frac{2Q}{\pi}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{1-v^{2}}=\frac{2Q}{\pi}\text{ arctanh\,}v\ .

(44)

Thus
1-v^{2}=1/\cosh^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{2Q}P\right)
, or

E=\frac{\phi-\phi_{c}}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{2Q}{\pi}\log\cosh\left(\frac{\pi}{2Q}P\right)\ .

(45)

Expanding at small
P
, we get a term

E\supset\frac{1}{2M}P^{2},\quad M=\frac{2Q}{\pi}\ .

(46)
