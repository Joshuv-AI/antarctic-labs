Band mixing and particle-hole asymmetry in moiré fractional Chern insulators
Nicolás Morales-Durán, Jingtian Shi, Cristian Voinea, Paweł Potasz, Jennifer Cano

Abstract
We investigate the effect of remote band mixing on the stability of fractional Chern insulators in a family of models that approximate continuum descriptions of moiré materials. Our results suggest that the experimentally observed asymmetry between filling fractions ν = 1 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=1/3 and ν = 2 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=2/3 in twisted MoTe 2 originates from a competition between a fractional Chern insulator, an electron Wigner crystal, and a hole Wigner crystal. In the absence of band mixing, the leading instability at ν = 1 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=1/3 is the electron crystal, whereas at ν = 2 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=2/3 the main competing phase is the hole crystal. Remote band mixing substantially lowers the energy of the electron crystal but has only a weak effect on the hole crystal. Consequently, it destabilizes the fractional Chern insulator at ν = 1 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=1/3 more strongly than at ν = 2 / 3 \displaystyle\nu=2/3 . This mechanism also provides an explanation for the emergence of re-entrant integer quantum anomalous Hall states in moiré MoTe 2 for fillings ν > 1 / 2 \displaystyle\nu>1/2 .

In this work we propose a mechanism for this observation: remote band mixing preferentially stabilizes electron Wigner crystals over hole Wigner crystals. We reach this conclusion by investigating the many-body ground states and low-lying spectra of a family of Hamiltonians that become progressively better approximations to continuum descriptions of semiconductor moiré materials

;
Shi et al. 2024

\displaystyle\nu=1/3
and
\displaystyle\nu=2/3

;
Shi et al. 2024

\displaystyle\nu=1/3

\displaystyle\nu=2/3

We trace this behavior to a shift in the wave vector of the magnetoroton minimum, which manifests as a level crossing between low-lying excited states in the many-body spectrum. We argue that this level crossing reflects a competition between electron and hole Wigner crystal instabilities, providing a natural explanation for the enhanced robustness of the
\displaystyle\nu=2/3

2

II
Many-body Hamiltonians

We now describe the Hamiltonian for all three models studied here. The single-particle Hamiltonian is that of a Schrödinger particle subject to a periodic magnetic field
\displaystyle B({\bm{r}})
and a periodic potential
\displaystyle U({\bm{r}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{1}{2m}\Pi_{-}\Pi_{+}+U({\bm{r}}),

(1)

with kinetic-momentum operators given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}=[p_{x}+A_{x}({\bm{r}})]\pm i[p_{y}+A_{y}({\bm{r}})],

(2)

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}=-i\partial_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\alpha=x,y

\displaystyle\displaystyle B({\bm{r}})=\nabla\times{\bm{A}}({\bm{r}})=B_{0}+\delta B({\bm{r}}).

(3)

IV
Aharonov-Casher bands

We now turn to the AC band, where the magnetic field
\displaystyle B(\bm{r})
in Eq. (
1

\displaystyle U(\bm{r})
is absent. For concreteness, we keep only the first harmonic
\displaystyle B_{1}

4

\displaystyle n=0

\displaystyle n=1

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}

\displaystyle\kappa\equiv(e^{2}/\varepsilon\ell)/\Delta_{g}

VII
Discussion

Previous work
Yu et al. 2024
;
Abouelkomsan et al. 2024
;
Gonçalves et al. 2025
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
He et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025b
;
Luo et al. 2025
;
Hou and Nevidomskyy 2025
has established that incorporating remote bands is essential for correctly reproducing the experimental phenomenology of tMoTe
2
.
However, the mechanism driving this effect was unclear.
Motivated by experimental observations of RIQAH states and the particle-hole asymmetry in the phase diagram of tMoTe
2
, we have investigated the effect of remote band mixing on the nature of the FCI ground states at filling fractions
\displaystyle\nu=1/3
and
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
. By exploiting the mapping between continuum moiré TMD models and an effective Landau level problem
Morales-Durán et al. 2024
, we leveraged known results on CDWs in the fractional quantum Hall effect to propose an explanation for the particle–hole asymmetry observed in numerical studies of tMoTe
2
continuum Hamiltonians, which may also account for recent experimental findings
Pan et al. 2026
.

All the models that we studied here, namely Landau levels, AC bands, adiabatic bands and continuum moiré models, exhibit a larger many-body gap at
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
than at
\displaystyle\nu=1/3
and a two-step behavior in the multi-band projected gap at
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
as a function of band mixing.
This characteristic behavior of the
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
gap is due to a crossing between the two lowest excited energy levels, which we attribute to a change in the dominant CDW instability of the FCI state. In the context of Landau levels, LL-mixing allows for electron Wigner crystals to lower their energy significantly, while the energy of the hole crystals remains largely unchanged
MacDonald 1984
. Thus, at filling
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
and
\displaystyle\kappa\to 0
, the hole crystal initially has lower energy than the electron crystal
MacDonald and Murray 1985
,
but at a critical value of
\displaystyle\kappa
, the electron crystal becomes the lowest-energy competing CDW phase. This transition is reflected in the many-body spectrum by a change in the position of the magnetoroton minimum, and is accompanied by shift in the wave vector where the FQH structure factor is peaked. The increase of the magnetoroton minimum wave vector is consistent with the unit cell of the main competing CDW becoming smaller. A recent VMC study of LLs on the sphere confirms this picture
Zhu et al. 2026
. Going beyond LLs, we have shown that this behavior persists in the AC and adiabatic models and even in some regimes of the continuum model for tMoTe
2
, although the confluence of finite band width and non-trivial quantum geometry makes it harder to ascribe the particle-hole asymmetry to a single cause. The mechanism described here offers an explanation for why the inclusion of the second band in previous ED studies
Abouelkomsan et al. 2024
;
Yu et al. 2024
;
Xu et al. 2024
;
Gonçalves et al. 2025
weakens the state at
\displaystyle\nu=1/3
more than at
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
.

It is also worth noting that the impact of band mixing on the FCI ground state may depend on the Chern number and quantum geometry of the remote band. In nearly all cases considered here, the two bands share the same Chern number and can therefore be viewed as deviations from a generalized Landau level structure. However, in certain experimentally relevant twist-angle regimes of tMoTe
2
, the two topmost bands carry opposite Chern numbers
Xu et al. 2025b
;
Park et al. 2025
– as in the model shown in Fig.
8
(b) – for which a Haldane-like description is more appropriate
He et al. 2025
. While such differences in band structure may lead to quantitative modifications, we expect the central mechanism identified here – namely that remote band mixing preferentially stabilizes electron Wigner crystals over hole Wigner crystals – to remain robust. This provides a natural explanation for the enhanced stability of the
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
FCI and the proliferation of RIQAH states in tMoTe
2
when the density is slightly away from
\displaystyle\nu=2/3
. Finally, we note that disorder and strain effects, which are not included in our study but are present in experiments, may further contribute to the observed particle–hole asymmetry.
