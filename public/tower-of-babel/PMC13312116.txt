Spirulina platensis and β-glucan mitigate cyclophosphamide-induced Immunosuppression in broilers: Differential effects of preventive and therapeutic strategies via redox–inflammatory modulation
Authors: Khloud Adel Mohamed Elshaitany, Abd-Allah A. Mokhbatly, Emad W. Ghazy, Mohamed F. Abou ELazab, Mustafa Shukry, Doaa H. Assar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Immunosuppression remains a major constraint in intensive poultry production, compromising growth performance and increasing susceptibility to disease. This study investigated the comparative preventive and therapeutic efficacy of dietary Spirulina platensis and β-glucan in broiler chickens subjected to cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression. A total of 240 one-day-old Sasso broiler chicks were casually allotted into six experimental groups (n = 40/group; 4 replicates of 10 birds). Treatments included a control, a cyclophosphamide-treated group, and groups supplemented with Spirulina or β-glucan in either preventive or therapeutic regimens. Growth performance, lymphoid organ indices, hematological parameters, phagocytic activity, oxidative stress biomarkers, inflammatory gene expression, and histopathological alterations were evaluated on days 21 and 28.Cyclophosphamide administration significantly impaired growth performance, reduced lymphoid organ indices, suppressed leukocyte counts and phagocytic activity, and induced oxidative stress, as evidenced by augmented malondialdehyde levels and reduced glutathione content and catalase activity. These changes were accompanied by upregulation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and downregulation of interleukin-10 (IL-10) expression in lymphoid tissues. Dietary supplementation with Spirulina or β-glucan significantly ameliorated these adverse effects, restoring growth performance, enhancing immune function, improving antioxidant status, and modulating inflammatory responses. Histopathological examination confirmed marked recovery of lymphoid tissue architecture, with more pronounced improvements observed under preventive supplementation.In conclusion, Spirulina platensis and β-glucan effectively mitigate cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression in broiler chickens, with preventive administration providing superior protection. These findings support their application as functional feed additives to enhance immune competence and productivity in poultry production systems.

Introduction

Immunosuppression is a systemic and nonspecific adaptive response that occurs when organs or tissues are exposed to stressors or stimuli exceeding their physiological tolerance, leading to exhaustion of immune defenses and depletion of the body’s energy reserves (Perch, et al., 2025). This condition adversely affects metabolic homeostasis and organ function, ultimately impairing productivity, immune competence, and disease resistance, which may increase susceptibility to morbidity and mortality (Banerjee, et al., 2023). In intensive poultry production systems, broiler chicks are continuously exposed to multiple environmental and management-related stressors, including ammonia exposure (Zhang, et al., 2022), thermal stress (Wei, et al., 2018), high stocking density (Houshmand, et al., 2012), and infectious diseases (Gimeno and Schat, 2018). These stressors can collectively compromise immune function and induce immunosuppression, resulting in reduced feed intake, metabolic disturbances, impaired growth performance, and weakened immune responses (Odio, et al., 1986). Among the major immunosuppressive diseases affecting poultry, infectious bursal disease (IBD), caused by infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV), is considered one of the most economically significant viral diseases in the poultry industry due to its high transmissibility and severe immunosuppressive effects (Cubas-Gaona, et al., 2020). Moreover, IBDV-induced immunosuppression may facilitate secondary infections and exacerbate disease prevalence in poultry flocks (Eladl, et al., 2020).

Cyclophosphamide (CYP) is a well-established immunosuppressive agent that has been extensively used to induce immunosuppression in poultry models experimentally. Previous studies demonstrated that CYP administration in newly hatched chicks primarily targets B lymphocytes and induces marked humoral immunosuppression (Sun, et al., 2022). Therefore, CYP-induced immunosuppression models are commonly employed to evaluate the immunomodulatory potential of natural products and plant-derived compounds (Sun, Wang, Liu, Liu, An, Xue, Wang, Xia, Chen and Cao, 2022). In addition to its immunosuppressive effects, CYP disrupts intestinal mucosal integrity and alters gut microbial composition, thereby negatively affecting intestinal health and immune function (Chen, et al., 2021). Consequently, the CYP-induced immunosuppressed broiler model was selected in the present study to investigate the potential protective effects of dietary interventions against oxidative and immunological damage. CYP administration has been reported to markedly reduce the relative weights of primary lymphoid organs, including the thymus and bursa of Fabricius, indicating severe structural and functional impairment of the avian immune system (Su, et al., 2024). The bursa of Fabricius, a primary lymphoid organ unique to avian species, is highly sensitive to CYP-induced toxicity. CYP treatment causes severe bursal atrophy and substantial reductions in bursal weight, leading to impaired B-cell development and immune dysfunction. Administration of CYP during the first week of life can induce long-lasting bursal damage, resembling the effects of chemical bursectomy in chicks (Schat, 2022). Furthermore, CYP may also reduce thymus size and impair T-cell maturation, although its effects on the thymus are generally less persistent than those observed in the bursa. Chicks treated with CYP frequently exhibit significantly reduced thymus relative weights compared with healthy controls, reflecting suppression of cellular immune function.

Natural feed additives with antioxidant and immunomodulatory properties have recently attracted considerable attention as potential alternatives for improve poultry health and minimize the adverse effects of immunosuppressive conditions. Spirulina (SP) is a small type of filamentous cyanobacterium that gets its name from the helical or spiral shape of its filaments. The Aztecs were known to use it, and its consumption dates back many centuries (Dillon, et al., 1995). Spirulina platensis is a nutrient-rich blue-green microalga containing high levels of proteins, vitamins, minerals, essential fatty acids, carotenoids, and phenolic compounds. Previous studies demonstrated that Spirulina possesses potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and immunostimulatory activities, which can enhance growth performance, improve immune responses, and protect against oxidative stress in poultry (Deepika, et al., 2021). Similarly, β-glucans are naturally occurring polysaccharides found in the cell walls of yeast, fungi, and cereals and are recognized as effective immunomodulatory agents that activate macrophages, enhance cytokine production, and improve both innate and adaptive immunity (Singh and Bhardwaj, 2023b). Dietary β-glucan supplementation has also been associated with improved disease resistance, antioxidant status, and intestinal health in poultry species (Singh and Bhardwaj, 2023b).

Despite growing evidence of the beneficial effects of Spirulina and β-glucan, limited information is available on their comparative preventive and therapeutic roles against CYP-induced immunosuppression in broiler chickens. Therefore, the present study was designed to investigate the preventive and therapeutic effects of Spirulina platensis and β-glucan supplementation on cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression in Sasso broiler chickens by evaluating growth performance, immune organ indices, oxidative stress biomarkers, hematological parameters, cytokine-related gene expression, and histopathological alterations.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Cyclophosphamide was acquired from Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co., Ltd. (Lianyungang, Jiangsu, China). Spirulina was acquired in powdered form from the Algal Biotechnology Unit at the National Research Unit in Dokki, Cairo, Egypt. Batch No 2809115 of β-glucan powder extract, procured from the Pharma Health Company, is presented as a fine brown powder with a distinctive odor and flavor. The concentration is 83% with an extraction ratio of 1:12.

Experimental birds and housing

A total of 240 one-day-old Sasso broiler chicks were inadvertently assigned to six experimental groups (n = 40 birds each group). Each group was divided into four replicate pens, with ten birds allocated per pen. A random number generator was used to assign the birds. Each set of animals was housed in its own pen, which served as the experimental unit for measuring performance. This study's sample size was determined by reviewing prior research that found a 20% difference with a power of 0.8.

Experimental design

Birds were randomly allocated to pens via randomization, with each pen serving as the experimental unit for growth performance metrics, while individual birds were used for biochemical and molecular assessments.

Group 1: Birds fed on a basal diet during the whole experiment (28 days)

Group 2: Birds fed on a basal diet and intramuscularly injected Cyclophosphamide (CYP) (80 mg/kg of BW) in the thigh muscle on days 16, 18, and 20 of the experiment (Cheng, et al., 2017).

Group 3: Birds fed on a basal diet mixed with spirulina (SP) (10 gm/Kg diet) for 28 days (MOKHBATLY, et al., 2018) and IM injected with CYP (80 mg/kg of BW) on days 16, 18, and 20 of the experiment (preventive manner).

Group 4: Birds fed on a basal diet mixed with β-glucan (400 mg /kg diet) for 28 days (E lTawab, et al., 2019) and IM injected with CYP (80 mg/kg of BW) on days 16, 18, and 20 of the experiment (preventive manner)

Group 5: Birds fed on a basal diet only for 15 days, then were supplemented with a diet mixed with spirulina (SP) 10 gm/Kg diet from the 16th day of the experiment to the end of the study, and IM injected with CYP (80 mg/kg of BW) on days 16, 18, and 20 of the experiment (therapeutic manner)

Group 6: Birds fed on a basal diet only for 15 days, then were supplemented with a diet mixed with a β-glucan (400 mg /kg diet) from the 16th day of the experiment to the end of the study, and IM injected with CYP (80 mg/kg of BW) on days 16, 18, and 20 of the experiment (therapeutic manner).For diet preparation, the required amount of each supplement was first premixed with a small amount of the basal diet to ensure uniform distribution, then gradually mixed with the remaining feed until complete homogenization was achieved. The supplemented diets were freshly prepared and administered daily according to the preventive or therapeutic treatment protocol. Sasso broiler chicks were selected for the present study because they exhibit strong adaptability, relatively high disease resistance, and suitability for immunological and nutritional investigations under experimental conditions. In addition, their slower growth rate compared with conventional commercial broilers allows better evaluation of physiological, oxidative, and immune responses during prolonged experimental periods. For days 0–21, the birds were kept in a white-light environment with a 23L:1D photoperiod (darkness from 23:00 to 24:00 hours). Sampling procedures

The body weight of broiler chickens was recorded on days 1, 21, and 28 of the experiment. Body weight gain (BWG) was calculated as the difference between final and initial body weights. Feed intake (FI) was determined by calculating the difference between the amount of feed offered and the residual feed remaining in each pen during the experimental period. Feed conversion ratio (FCR) was calculated as the ratio of feed intake to body weight gain (FCR = FI/BWG). Each pen served as the experimental unit for growth-related measurements. Every single bird that was chosen had its blood drawn from a vein in its wing. We performed hematological tests and evaluated phagocytic activity using heparinized blood samples.

Tissue samples from the spleen, bursa, and cecal tonsils underwent precise weighing after extraction to determine the immunological organ index (IOR) using the method:

Tissue index (g/kg) = Tissue weight (g)/BW (kg). Then, the spleen, bursa, and cecal tonsil tissues were rapidly split into two sections; one was cryogenically preserved in liquid nitrogen at −80°C for mRNA extraction and determination of oxidative injury and antioxidant biomarkers, while the other was collected in neutral formalin for histopathological analysis.

Sampling on days 21 and 28 was designed to evaluate both early- and later-stage responses to cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression, as well as the progression of the protective effects of Spirulina platensis and β-glucan supplementation. Due to the rapid physiological and immunological changes occurring in broiler chickens during growth, measurable alterations in immune, oxidative stress, and histopathological parameters may occur within relatively short experimental intervals.”

Immuno-hematological analysis

Neubauer hemocytometers filled with Natt-Herrick fluid were used to quantify RBCs and WBCs, following the detailed procedure described by Natt and Herrick, 1952. Regularly made Giemsa-stained blood films were also subjected to differential leukocyte counts using the cross-sectional method (Grant, 1987). While the standard cyanmethemoglobin method was used to quantify hemoglobin, the microhematocrit method was used to determine hematocrit. The RBC indices, which include MCV, MCH, and MCHC, were eventually calculated from the data.

Leukocyte phagocytic activity against Candida albicans was tested in vitro according to the protocol described by Abo-Al-Ela, et al., 2017. Carefully mixed were 100 µl each of fresh blood, C. albicans inoculum (1 × 10^6 CFU/mL), and fetal bovine serum (South American, CE). The mixture was then incubated at 37°C for 30 minutes. A 10-minute centrifugation at 1500 rpm was then applied to the solution. After that, 5 µL of the resuspended cells were used to make a blood smear, which was then stained with Giemsa stain. The following formulae were used to quantify phagocytic activity (PA) as the proportion of phagocytic cells that devoured yeast cells, and to define phagocytic index (PI) as the total number of phagocytized yeast cells relative to the number of phagocytic cells:

Oxidative stress biomarkers

Homogenates of spleen, bursa, and cecal tonsil tissues were produced to evaluate the MDA (CAT. NO MD 25 29) and GSH (CAT. NO GP2524), as well as CAT (CAT. NO E-BC-K031-S) activity, as illustrated below: After cleansing with 0.9% normal saline, 250 mg of spleen, bursa, and cecal tonsils were mixed with 2.5 mL of ice-cold phosphate buffer (pH 7.5) using a PT 3100 Polytron homogenizer from Kinematica Instruments in Lucerne, Switzerland. After centrifuging at 3000 rpm for 20 minutes, the solution was poured off to collect the supernatant for further examination.

Histopathological examination

After blood collection, chicks were sacrificed, and then the spleen, bursa, and cecal tonsil tissues were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, processed through a gradient of alcohol concentrations, cleared with xylene, and subsequently embedded in paraffin to make wax blocks. Thereafter, they were mounted on glass slides and stained with hematoxylin and eosin following random sectioning at 5 µm for histological analysis (Ateufack, et al., 2015). Specimens were assessed by two independent observers, blinded to the experimental circumstances, utilizing light microscopy and a scoring system (Liu, et al., 2016).

Gene expression analysis

Using the RNeasy Mini kit (Qiagen) to extract total RNA from tissues such as the bursa, cecal tonsils, and spleen. We used the Nanodrop for RNA purity testing and a 1% agarose gel for RNA integrity testing. With the use of Quantiscript reverse transcriptase, 2 milligrams of the extracted RNA were converted into complementary DNA. In the next step, a real-time PCR reaction was performed using the Step One Plus real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA) and gene-specific primers (Table 2), with cDNA as the template. Statistical analysis

Data are shown as the average ± standard deviation (SD). To ensure the data were normal and homogeneous before statistical analysis, the Shapiro-Wilk and Levene's tests were used. While hematological, biochemical, immunological, molecular, and histopathological studies were conducted on individual birds, growth performance characteristics were evaluated using the pen as the experimental unit. To compare the experimental groups, we used a one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test for multiple comparisons. The statistical analyses were carried out using the latest version of GraphPad Prism (10.1.1), developed by GraphPad Software, Boston, MA, USA. At the P < 0.05 significance level, differences were deemed statistically significant.

Discussion

Poultry meat and eggs are a big source of high-quality animal protein. They make up almost 20% of the world's intake of animal-derived proteins (Wood, et al., 2024). On the other hand, intensive poultry production systems stress birds by exposing them to environmental stressors, infectious agents, and conditions that weaken their immune systems. All of these things make it harder for birds to grow, get vaccinated, and fight off disease. So, using safe dietary immunomodulators to boost immune competence has become an important way to improve poultry health and productivity and reduce immunosuppression (Ntsongota, et al., 2025).

Cyclophosphamide (CYP) is commonly used as an experimental immunosuppressive agent in poultry owing to its potent cytotoxic effects on rapidly proliferating immune cells, particularly B lymphocytes (Shan and Miao, 2022). CYP causes the bursa of Fabricius, spleen, and thymus to shrink a lot. These are primary and secondary lymphoid organs. It also causes a lack of lymphoid tissue, cell death, and immune system dysfunction.

Body weight, hematological parameters, phagocytic activity, phagocytic index, and immunological organ indices were all shown to decrease as a result of CPA therapy, suggesting toxicity and myelosuppressive qualities (Kanno, et al., 2009). CPA worsened histopathological damage and inflammatory signaling. These findings indicate the successful establishment of a CYP-induced immunosuppression model in broiler chickens(Su, Xue, Wang, Zhang, Zhang, Yang and Chu, 2024).The spleen and bursa of Fabricius are crucial organs in the immune system of chickens. The strength of the immune system is shown by the lymphocyte proliferation, which is impacted by the organ index(Wang, et al., 2022). The weight loss in the CPA-treated birds was significant, as was the weight loss in their bursa and spleen, according to our results. The bursa, spleen, and caecal tonsil all showed significant necrosis and lymphocyte depletion, which led to these complications. These findings have already been documented by other studies (Kim et al., 2003), who found that CYP treatment suppressed the humoral antibody response. CYP-treated broilers displayed lymphoid depletion, follicular atrophy, fibrosis, and necrosis in the bursa of Fabricius, spleen, and cecal tonsils, indicating humoral and cellular immune system destruction. These data are consistent with earlier studies demonstrating that CYP inhibits the development of immune organs and disrupts lymphocyte proliferation by hindering hematopoiesis and inducing apoptosis in immune cells (Shu, et al., 2021). The reduction in phagocytic activity and the phagocytic index confirm the impairment of innate immune function, which is essential for the rapid detection and eradication of pathogens. Adding spirulina (SP) or β-glucan to the diet, either preventively or therapeutically, significantly alleviated CPA-induced growth retardation and immune dysfunction. Birds treated with SP or β-glucan showed much better body weight gain and normal bursa and spleen indices than birds treated with CYP. Similar impact of spirulina and β-glucan on growth and immune restoration was observed in poultry and other animal models subjected to oxidative or immunological stress (Attia, et al., 2023).

One of the active metabolites of cyclophosphamide (CP) is phosphoramide mustard (PM), which has anticancer properties; the other is acrolein, a toxic metabolite that causes hematological and myelotoxicity (Kawabata, et al., 1990). In myelosuppression, sometimes called bone marrow suppression, the creation of new blood cells is reduced because the bone marrow is not actively producing them. CPA lowers blood cell levels, which increases myelosuppression and anemia (Khazaei, et al., 2020). It also inhibits bone marrow antioxidant enzyme activity, thereby promoting oxidative stress. We found that blood cells, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and lymphocyte percentage were reduced in hens given CPA in this study. Animals' total WBC and differential leucocyte, among their reactions to and adaptations from stressful situations, provide insight into their systemic health (Coles, 1986). Taking into account that white blood cells are involved in antibody formation and play an important part in the immune system (phagocytosis) (Weiss and Wardrop, 2010). Current findings of lymphocytopaenia point to a decrease in lymphoid cell counts caused by CYP toxicity, which destroys and blocks the division of immune-differentiated lymphocytes as well as B and T cells (Hengstler, et al., 1997). Histopathology confirmed this, showing that the CYP-treated hens had severe shrinkage of lymphoid follicles and increased lymphoid necrosis in the spleen and cecal tissues. There is a wide range of cell types that cyclophosphamide can damage; however, lymphoid and hemopoietic cells are especially vulnerable, Hengstler et al. (1997) leading to B-cell depression and depletion and T-cell suppression and necrosis

Histopathological analysis confirmed these findings, demonstrating substantial restoration of lymphoid architecture in the bursa, spleen, and cecal tonsils in the SP- or β-glucan-supplemented groups. The elevated lymphocyte count, diminished necrosis and fibrosis, and restoration of normal tissue architecture signify enhanced immune recovery and defense against CYP-induced tissue damage. Similar histological improvements following the use of natural immunostimulants have been reported previously. The change was more obvious in birds that got extra help from the beginning of the experiment (preventive manner). This shows how important it is to help the immune system early on.

The significant increase in the phagocytic activity and phagocytic index of peripheral blood mononuclear cells further illustrated the enhancement of innate immune responses by SP and β-glucan. CYP lowers immune surveillance by blocking phagocytosis, an important defense mechanism against pathogens. The restoration of phagocytic function after supplementation may be ascribed to the immunostimulatory characteristics of β-glucan, which activate macrophages through pattern recognition receptors, for instance, dectin-1 and toll-like receptors, thereby enhancing microbial clearance and cytokine production (Zhao, et al., 2024). SP also contains bioactive compounds such as phycocyanin, polysaccharides, and phenolic antioxidants that enhance macrophage function, support white blood cell growth, and boost the immune system's response (Vasudevan, et al., 2021).

One of the main ways that CYP can cause immunotoxicity is by inducing oxidative stress. Lipid peroxidation, increased ROS generation, and oxidative stress are all consequences of chronic phenol exposure (CPA) and its metabolites, which can inhibit GSH and its antioxidant activity and cellular damage (Abukhalil, et al., 2025). The current study illustrated that CYP administration significantly increased MDA levels, while GSH content and CAT activity, indicating substantial oxidative stress. These findings support earlier research demonstrating that CYP disrupts redox homeostasis and exacerbates tissue damage in avian and mammalian models.

Both SP and β-glucan substantially diminished oxidative stress by reducing MDA levels and normalizing GSH and CAT activity. The antioxidant effect can be attributed to spirulina's rich array of antioxidant components, including phycocyanin, carotenoids, vitamins, and phenolic compounds, which counteract free radicals and enhance innate antioxidant defenses (Park, et al., 2018). Similarly, β-glucan has been demonstrated to safeguard tissues from oxidative injury and enhance the function of antioxidant enzymes by modulating immune-redox signaling pathways. The reduction in oxidative stress probably helped the immune system and tissues get better.

In lymphoid tissues, a state of inflammation was indicated by molecular-level correlations between Immunosuppression, downregulation of IL-10, and overexpression of NF-κB are all brought about by CYP. One of the transcription factors that regulates inflammation-causing genes is NF-κB. ROS triggers the activation of NF-κB, which in turn causes the overexpression of inflammatory genes, including TNF-α and IL-6 (Varthya, et al., 2021). The activation of NF-κB is essential in inflammation, apoptosis, and immune dysregulation under oxidative stress and exposure to immunotoxic substances. The heightened NF-κB expression in CYP-treated avians may elucidate the pronounced histological inflammation, potentially revealing mechanisms of infiltration and tissue damage (Sadati, et al., 2025).

Dietary supplementation with SP or β-glucan enhanced IL-10 expression while preventing NF-κB activation in the bursa, spleen, and cecal tonsils. IL-10 is a significant immunoregulatory cytokine that helps control inflammation and maintain immune system stability. The interaction between IL-10 and NF-κB indicates that SP and β-glucan protect cells by altering the function of inflammatory and antioxidant signaling pathways. Previous research has demonstrated similar cytokine-modulating effects of spirulina and β-glucan (Singh and Bhardwaj, 2023a).

The present study collectively illustrates that dietary SP and β-glucan effectively alleviate cyclophosphamide-induced immunosuppression in broiler chickens by enhancing growth performance, restoring lymphoid organ architecture, augmenting innate immune responses, diminishing oxidative stress, and modulating critical inflammatory and anti-inflammatory signaling pathways. The protective effects were apparent when supplements were given both preventively and therapeutically, although earlier supplementation yielded greater benefits.

This study has some limitations. Only a limited number of cytokines were evaluated, and additional inflammatory markers such as TNF-α and IL-6 could provide stronger mechanistic insight. Moreover, microbiota analysis was not performed, even though it may have contributed to the observed immunomodulatory effects. In addition, the experimental period and the sampling interval were relatively short. Therefore, future studies with longer durations and wider sampling intervals are recommended to assess better the long-term effects of Spirulina platensis and β-glucan supplementation.

Conclusion

Spirulina and β-glucan are promising natural immunostimulants that can be added to poultry diets to boost immune function, fight off immunosuppression, and improve overall health and productivity via raising the immune system and hens' overall growth and development through improved phagocytic activity, phagocytic index, total leukocytic count, modified differential leukocytic count, and higher body weight. They have the potential to replace synthetic immunomodulators in contemporary chicken farming systems in a sustainable, risk-free manner.
