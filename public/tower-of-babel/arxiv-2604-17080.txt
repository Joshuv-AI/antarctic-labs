Abstract
. We prove a stabilization result for the 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -dimension of spaces of morphisms between supersingular Drinfeld modules, filtered by degree: for any two supersingular rank- 2 2 Drinfeld 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[T] -modules in characteristic 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} of degree d d , the dimension m s m_{s} of the space of morphisms of τ \tau -degree at most s s satisfies m s = 2 ​ ( s + 1 ) − ( d − 1 ) m_{s}=2(s+1)-(d-1) for all s ≥ d − 2 s\geq d-2 . This is proved using the theory of Brandt matrices and properties of L L -functions of automorphic forms for GL 2 \GL_{2} over function fields. The stabilization formula, combined with an analysis of zero entries in Brandt matrices and a hyperplane-avoidance argument, yields semifield rank-metric codes. We also describe an efficient algorithm for computing the relevant Brandt matrices.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Notation and basic definitions

We first introduce the notation and definitions needed
for the statement of the main results of this paper. This notation will be used throughout the paper.

We denote by
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
the polynomial ring in indeterminate
T
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
A_{+}

denote the set of monic nonzero polynomials in
A
.
Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
be the fraction field of
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
.
Given a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{n}\lhd A
, we denote by the same symbol
the unique monic generator of
\mathfrak{n}
. The
primes
of
A
are the maximal ideals of
A
.
Given a prime
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
, we write
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}=A/\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
k
be a finite field equipped with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\gamma\colon A\rightarrow k
. The
A
-characteristic of
k
is

\chr_{A}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\ker(\gamma)
; it is prime of
A
. We denote
\mathfrak{p}=\chr_{A}(k)
and
t=\gamma(T)
.

Let
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be the
twisted polynomial ring
, i.e., the noncommutative
polynomial ring in
\tau
with coefficients in
k
, subject to the commutation rule
\tau a=a^{q}\tau
for all
a\in k
.
The map
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}\longmapsto f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}
gives a ring isomorphism between
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}

and the ring
k\langle x\rangle
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomials, where multiplication on
k\langle x\rangle
is defined by composition.

A
Drinfeld module
of rank
r\geq 1
over
k
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi\colon A

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\},

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\longmapsto\phi_{a}=\gamma(a)+g_{1}(a)\tau+\cdots+g_{n}(a)\tau^{n},

such that for
a\neq 0
we have
n=\deg(a)r
and
g_{n}(a)\neq 0
. Note that

\phi
is uniquely determined by
\phi_{T}
, so to define a Drinfeld module over
k

one simply chooses
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in k
with
g_{r}\neq 0
and sets
\phi_{T}=t+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r}\tau^{r}
.

The group of
morphisms
\phi\rightarrow\psi
between two Drinfeld modules defined over
k
is

\displaystyle\Hom_{k}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\{u\in k\!\left\{\tau\right\}\mid u\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}u\text{ for all }a\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{u\in k\!\left\{\tau\right\}\mid u\phi_{T}=\psi_{T}u\}.

The
endomorphism ring
of
\phi
is

\End_{k}(\phi)=\Hom_{k}(\phi,\phi).

For simplicity, we denote
\Hom(\phi,\psi)=\Hom_{\bar{k}}(\phi,\psi)
– the group of all possible morphisms
\phi\rightarrow\psi

over the algebraic closure
\bar{k}
of
k
.
Given
u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)
and
a\in A
, we define
a\circ u=u\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}u
.
It is easy to check that
a\circ u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)
, so
\Hom(\phi,\psi)
is an
A
-module. It is known
that
\Hom(\phi,\psi)
is a free
A
-module of rank
\leq r^{2}
; see
[
Pap23
, Thm. 3.4.1]
.
A nonzero morphism
u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)
is called an
isogeny
.
An isogeny
u
is an
isomorphism
if it is invertible in
K\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, i.e.,
u\in K^{\times}
.

Through
\phi
,
\bar{k}
acquires an
A
-module structure, where
a\in A
acts on
\beta\in\bar{k}
by
a\ast\beta=\phi_{a}(\beta)
. Denote this module by
{{}^{\phi}}{\bar{k}}
. Given
0\neq a\in A
,
the
a
-torsion submodule

\phi[a]
is the set of roots of
\phi_{a}(x)
; it is
an
A
-submodule of
{{}^{\phi}}{\bar{k}}
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
does not divide
a
, then
\phi[a]\cong(A/aA)^{r}
; see
[
Pap23
, Cor. 3.5.3]
.

1.2.
Rank-metric codes from Drinfeld modules

In rank-metric coding, the codewords are taken from the space
\Mat_{m,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of
m\times n
-matrices (
m\leq n
), with
distance
\dist(X,Y)=\rank(X-Y)
.
A rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\subset\Mat_{m,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with minimum distance
d=\dist(\mathcal{C})

satisfies the
Singleton-type bound

\left|\mathcal{C}\right|\leq q^{n(m-d+1)}
;
a code attaining this bound is a
Maximum Rank Distance
(
MRD
)
code
.
MRD codes exist for all
d\leq m\leq n
and any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; see
[
Del78
]
.
We assume throughout that our codes are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.
In the special case
d=m=n
, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear MRD code corresponds to a
semifield
(a
finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra with unity in which multiplication is not
necessarily commutative or associative); see
[
She20
, Prop. 1]
. We call such MRD codes

semifield codes
. Rank-metric codes have attracted renewed interest
due to applications in network coding, distributed data storage,
and cryptography; cf.
[
SKK08
]
,
[
BHL+22
]
,
[
She19
]
.

In
[
MP26
]
, we established a general method for constructing
rank-metric codes from Drinfeld modules. The basic setup is as follows. Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
over
a finite field
k
, let
\mathcal{M}\subset\End(\phi)
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace, and let
\mathfrak{q}\neq\chr_{A}(k)
be a prime.
Each
u\in\mathcal{M}
acts
A
-linearly on the torsion module
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]\cong\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{r}
, giving a homomorphism

\mathcal{M}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\End_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}}(\phi[\mathfrak{q}])\cong\Mat_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}).

If every
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
(i.e., as an invertible

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear transformation)
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=r\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{q}),

then the image of
\mathcal{M}
is a semifield code; see
[
MP26
,
\lx@sectionsign
1]
for the details.

Example 1.1
.

We demonstrate the basic idea with a simple example from
[
MP26
,
\lx@sectionsign
1.2]
.
Define
\phi
by
\phi_{T}=\tau^{r}
. The rank of
\phi
is
r
,
\chr_{A}(k)=T
, and
\End(\phi)=k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, where
k=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\neq T
be a prime of degree
s+1
and let

\mathcal{M}_{s}=\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi)=\{u\in\End(\phi)\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\}.

Every
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
acts invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
: if
0\neq\beta\in\phi[\mathfrak{q}]

is annihilated by
u
, then the
A
-submodule of
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
generated by
\beta
has size
q^{s+1}
, yet
\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s
implies
\#\ker(u)\leq q^{s}
, a contradiction.
Since
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}=r(s+1)=r\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{q})
, the image of
\mathcal{M}_{s}
in
\Mat_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}})
is a semifield code.

1.3.
Main results

The Drinfeld module in Example
1.1
is
supersingular
; see Section
2
for definition.
The distinguishing feature of supersingular Drinfeld modules is that they have the largest endomorphism rings
among the Drinfeld modules of given rank. The results of this paper came out from trying to understand
the role that the supersingularity plays in the construction of Example
1.1
. It turns out that supersingularity
is important
but
also the fact that the
A
-characteristic of
k
has degree 1 is important.
What makes Example
1.1
work is that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}
is exactly
r\cdot(s+1)
, which matches the semifield dimension. In general, for a
supersingular Drinfeld module
\phi
of rank
r
the space
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi)=\{u\in\End(\phi)\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\}
has dimension strictly less
than
r(s+1)
.

In this paper, we investigate the construction of rank-metric codes from
supersingular Drinfeld modules of rank
2
. The endomorphism algebra of such
\phi
is a quaternion
division algebra
D
over
F
ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\infty
, and
\End(\phi)
is a maximal
A
-order in
D
.
The key question is:
what is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension of the space

\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)=\{u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\}

for a given pair of supersingular Drinfeld modules over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
?

Our main result answers this question for
r=2
and
s
in a certain “stable” range.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
A
of degree
d
, and let
\phi
and
\psi
be supersingular
rank-
2
Drinfeld modules over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)=2(s+1)-(d-1)\qquad\text{for all}\quad s\geq d-2.

The proof combines the theory of Brandt matrices (which encode the arithmetic of
isogenies between supersingular Drinfeld modules) with the decomposition of theta series into Eisenstein and cuspidal parts,
and a key input from the theory of automorphic forms: the
L
-function of a cuspidal
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
-invariant harmonic cochain on the
Bruhat-Tits tree of
\PGL_{2}(F_{\infty})
is a polynomial in
q^{-s}
of degree
d-3
, a consequence of
deep results of Drinfeld, Deligne, and Grothendieck.
This forces the dimensions
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
to stabilize for
s\geq d-2
. (In the range
1\leq s<d-2
, the
numbers
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
do not exhibit patterns persistent over all supersingular pairs.)

Remark 1.3
.

For supersingular Drinfeld modules
\phi
and
\psi
of rank
r\geq 2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we expect that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)=r(s+1)-r\frac{(r-1)(d-1)}{2}

for all
s\geq(r-1)(d-1)-1
, generalizing Theorem
3.5
and consistent with the explicit computation in Section
3.3

for the Drinfeld module
\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{r}
. In fact, Proposition
3.10
establishes this formula for

\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{r}
, whenever it is supersingular over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
(equivalently, whenever
\gcd(r,d)=1
, according to Lemma
3.7
).
The general case we intend to pursue in a future paper.

In Section
3.4
, we describe an efficient algorithm for computing the Brandt matrices
B(\mathfrak{q})

for the division quaternion algebra
D
over
F
ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\infty
.
Our algorithm relies crucially on the twisted polynomial ring
k\{\tau\}
in which supersingular Drinfeld modules live: isogenies
are elements of
k\{\tau\}
, and norm testing reduces to right-division in this ring.
This makes the algorithm specific to function fields, with no direct analogue over number fields,
where classical algorithms for Brandt matrices rely on lattice enumeration in quaternion orders over
\mathbb{Z}
; see Pizer
[
Piz80
]
and Voight
[
Voi21
, Ch. 41]
. Since Brandt matrices represent the action of Hecke operators on spaces of
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
-invariant harmonic cochains on the Bruhat–Tits tree of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(F_{\infty})
, the algorithm also has applications to the computation of automorphic forms for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
over function fields. Some explicit examples are given in Section
3.4
.

Finally, in Section
4
we consider applications of Theorem
3.5
to the construction of semifield codes,
which initially motivated our investigation. Theorem
3.5
implies that for large
s
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}
is
close to but less than
2(s+1)
, the dimension required for a semifield code on

\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
with
\deg(\mathfrak{q})=s+1
. As
s
increases, the deficit
d-1
becomes negligible relative to
2(s+1)
,
suggesting that
\mathcal{M}_{s}
should yield semifield codes after some modifications. We use two strategies depending on the parity of
d
. If
d
is odd, we choose a prime
\mathfrak{q}
with
2\deg(\mathfrak{q})=2(s+1)-(d-1)
and look for a zero entry in the Brandt matrix
B(\mathfrak{q})
indexed by
(\phi,\psi)
; all nonzero elements of
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
then map
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
invertibly onto
\psi[\mathfrak{q}]
, producing a semifield code in
\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}})
. When
d
is even, the dimension has the wrong parity, so instead we choose
\mathfrak{q}
with
2\deg(\mathfrak{q})=2(s+1)-(d-1)-1
and search for a good hyperplane in
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
, i.e., a codimension-1 subspace containing no bad elements (elements that fail to act invertibly on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
). We demonstrate both strategies with explicit examples for
d\leq 4
computed using our Magma implementation.

2.
Supersingular Drinfeld modules

In this section we collect some facts about supersingular Drinfeld modules. We keep the notation of
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
.
In particular,
k
is a finite field equipped with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\gamma\colon A\rightarrow k
and
\mathfrak{p}=\ker(\gamma)
.

For
u=a_{h}\tau^{h}+\cdots+a_{n}\tau^{n}\in k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
with
0\leq h\leq n
,
a_{h}\neq 0
and
a_{n}\neq 0
, the
height
of
u
is
\Ht(u)=h
and the
degree
of
u
is
\deg_{\tau}(u)=n
. Given two Drinfeld modules
\phi
and
\psi
over
k
and an isogeny
u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)
, let

\ker(u)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\alpha\in\bar{k}\mid u(\alpha)=0\},

which is the set of distinct roots of the polynomial
u(x)\in k\langle x\rangle
. This is a finite
A
-module, with the action of
A
defined by
a\circ\beta=\phi_{a}(\beta)
.
For a finite
A
-module
M
we define
\chi(M)
as the product of the invariant factors of
M
, which are assumed to be monic.
The
norm
of
u
is (cf.
[
Gek91
, p. 194]
)

(2.1)

\mathfrak{N}(u)=\mathfrak{p}^{\Ht(u)/\deg(\mathfrak{p})}\cdot\chi(\ker u).

By
[
Pap23
, Prop. 3.3.4]
,
\deg(\mathfrak{p})
divides
\Ht(u)
. Thus,
\mathfrak{N}(u)
is a monic
polynomial in
A
. It is immediate from the definitions that

(2.2)

\deg_{\tau}(u)=\deg_{T}\mathfrak{N}(u).

Let
\phi\colon A\rightarrow k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
.
The
height
of
\phi
is defined as
H(\phi)=\Ht(\phi_{\mathfrak{p}})/\deg(\mathfrak{p})
; this is a positive
integer satisfying
1\leq H(\phi)\leq r
(see
[
Pap23
, Lem. 3.2.11]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A Drinfeld module
\phi
over
k
of rank
r
is called

supersingular
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions
(see
[
Gek91
, Sec. 4]
):

(1)

H(\phi)=r
.

(2)

\phi[\mathfrak{p}]=0
.

(3)

\dim_{F}(\End(\phi)\otimes_{A}F)=r^{2}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

A supersingular Drinfeld module
\psi
of rank
r
over
\bar{k}
is isomorphic to a supersingular
Drinfeld module
\phi
defined over the degree
r
extension
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{r}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and such that all endomorphisms
of
\phi
over
\bar{k}
are already defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{r}}
.

Proof.

The following argument is very similar to the argument of the proof of
[
Gek91
, Prop. 4.2]
.

Suppose
\psi_{T}=t+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r}\tau^{r}
. Let
\alpha
be a fixed
(q^{r}-1)
-th root of
g_{r}
and
let
\phi=\alpha\psi\alpha^{-1}
. Then
\phi_{T}=t+f_{1}\tau+\cdots+\tau^{r}
, and
\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\tau^{rd}
. Let
u=u_{0}+u_{1}\tau+\cdots+u_{s}\tau^{s}

be an endomorphism of
\phi
over
\bar{k}
. The relation
\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}u=u\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}

implies
u_{i}^{q^{rd}}=u_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq s
. Thus,
u\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{r}}\!\left\{\tau\right\}
. Since every
\phi_{a}
lies in
\End(\phi)
, we conclude that

\phi
itself is defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{r}}
.
∎

The previous lemma implies that the number of supersingular Drinfeld modules of rank
r
over
\bar{k}
, up to isomorphism, is finite.
Moreover, it is known that any two supersingular Drinfeld modules of rank
r
over
\bar{k}
are isogenous; see
[
Pap23
, Lem. 4.4.3]
.

The endomorphism ring of a supersingular Drinfeld module enjoys the following
maximality property, due to Gekeler
[
Gek91
, Thm. 4.3]
:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be a supersingular rank
r
Drinfeld module over
\bar{k}
.

(1)

D=\End(\phi)\otimes_{A}F
is a central division algebra over
F
of dimension
r^{2}

with invariants
1/r
and
-1/r
at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\infty
, respectively, and
0
at all other places.

(2)

\End(\phi)
is a maximal
A
-order in
D
.

(3)

The left ideal classes of
\End(\phi)

are in bijection with the isomorphism classes of supersingular rank
r

Drinfeld modules in characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
.

Now we concentrate on the rank
2

2
defined by

\phi_{T}=t+g\tau+\Delta\tau^{2},

its

. It is easy to check that two Drinfeld modules

are isomorphic over

.

Consider the Drinfeld module

\phi_{T}=t+\tau+x\tau^{2}

over

, where

These polynomials are Gekeler’s Deuring polynomials

Theorem 2.4
.

(1)

The polynomial

\begin{cases}\frac{q^{d}-1}{q^{2}-1},&\text{if $d$ is even},\\
\frac{q^{d}-q}{q^{2}-1},&\text{if $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

The roots of

Since all such

is supersingular

) and the results in

(reproduced in English in

\alpha=\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}(x_{0})
and
\beta=\Nr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}(y_{0})
.

(1)

The elements
\alpha
and
\beta
lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

(2)

All morphisms
\phi\rightarrow\psi
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
if and only if
\alpha=\beta
. In particular, all
endomorphisms of
\phi
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
.

(3)

In general, all morphisms
\phi\rightarrow\psi
are defined over the
(q-1)
-th degree extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
, i.e., over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2d(q-1)}}
.

Proof.

Comparing
the coefficient of
\tau^{2d+1}
on both sides of
\phi_{T}\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}\phi_{T}
, we get
\alpha^{q}=\alpha
.
Thus,
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Similarly,
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This proves (1).

Let let
u=u_{0}+u_{1}\tau+\cdots+u_{s}\tau^{2}
be a morphism
\phi\rightarrow\psi
.
From
u\phi_{\mathfrak{p}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}u
we get
\alpha u_{i}=\beta u_{i}^{q^{2d}}
for all
0\leq i\leq s
, because
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Thus,
u_{i}=u_{i}^{q^{2d}}
for all
0\leq i\leq s
if and only if
\alpha=\beta
. This proves (2).

In general, when
\alpha
is not necessarily equal to
\beta
, for
u_{i}\neq 0
we get
u_{i}^{(q^{2d}-1)(q-1)}=1
.
We claim that if
b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
satisfies
b^{(q^{2d}-1)(q-1)}=1
,
then
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2d(q-1)}}
, and moreover
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2d(q-1)}}
is the smallest extension of

\mathbb{F}_{q}
containing all such
b
.

Set
N=(q^{2d}-1)(q-1)
. We need to find the smallest
n
such that

N\mid q^{n}-1
. A necessary condition is

(q^{2d}-1)\mid(q^{n}-1)
, which holds if and only if
2d\mid n
.
Write
n=2dm
for some positive integer
m
. Then
N\mid(q^{2dm}-1)
if and only if
(q-1)
divides

\frac{q^{2dm}-1}{q^{2d}-1}=1+q^{2d}+q^{4d}+\cdots+q^{2d(m-1)}.

Since each term
q^{2dj}
is congruent to
1
modulo
q-1
, we have

\frac{q^{2dm}-1}{q^{2d}-1}\equiv m\pmod{q-1}.

The smallest positive
m
divisible by
q-1
is
q-1
, giving
n=2d(q-1)
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

In Example
2.5
, all
Drinfeld modules defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are isogenous over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
, but those Drinfeld modules which are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}

properly are not necessarily isogenous over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
.

3.
Brandt matrices

Fix a prime
\mathfrak{p}\lhd A
of degree
d
.
Let
D
be the quaternion algebra over
F
ramified only at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\infty
. Fix a supersingular
Drinfeld module
\phi
of rank
2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
\mathcal{O}=\End(\phi)\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left\{\tau\right\}
.
By Theorem
2.3
,
\mathcal{O}
is isomorphic to a maximal
A
-order in
D
.

3.1.
Definitions

Two left ideals
I
and
J
of
\mathcal{O}
are in the same class if
J=Ib
with
b\in D^{\times}
. The set of left ideal
classes is finite and its order
n
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{O}
. Let
\{I_{1},\dots,I_{n}\}
be the set
of left ideals representing the distinct ideal classes, with
I_{1}=\mathcal{O}
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\mathcal{O}_{i}
be the
right order of
I_{i}
. Then each conjugacy class of maximal orders in
D
is represented (once or twice)
in the set
\{\mathcal{O}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{O}_{n}\}
. We let
\mathbf{t}\leq n
be the number of distinct conjugacy classes of maximal
orders in
D
. Then
n
is the
class number
of
D
and
\mathbf{t}
is the
type number
of
D
. With this notation

\deg H_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=\begin{cases}n&\text{if $d$ is even};\\
n-1&\text{if $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Moreover, the number of irreducible factors of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[x]
is
\mathbf{t}
if
d
is even, and
\mathbf{t}-1
if
d
is odd; see

[
Gek91
, Prop. 4.6]
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n}
be the representatives of supersingular Drinfeld modules of rank
2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
arranged so that
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\End(\phi_{i})
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}
be any monic element of
A
. Put

(3.1)

b_{i,j}(\mathfrak{m})=\frac{1}{\#\Aut(\phi_{j})}\#\left\{u\in\Hom(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})\mid(\mathfrak{N}(u))=(\mathfrak{m})\right\},

where the norm
\mathfrak{N}(u)
of the isogeny
u
was defined in (
2.1
). We recall that

\Aut(\phi)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}&\text{if $j(\phi)\neq 0$};\\
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}&\text{if $j(\phi)=0$}.\end{cases}

Definition 3.1
.

The
n\times n
matrix
B(\mathfrak{m})=(b_{i,j}(\mathfrak{m}))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\Mat_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is called the
\mathfrak{m}
-th
Brandt matrix
in
A
-characteristic
\mathfrak{p}
.

Remark 3.2
.

b_{i,j}(\mathfrak{m})
counts the number of isogenies
u\colon\phi_{i}\rightarrow\phi_{j}
with
\mathfrak{N}(u)=(\mathfrak{m})
, up to equivalence, where
two isogenies are equivalent if they have the same kernel, so differ by an automorphism of
\phi_{j}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Consider
\mathcal{O}_{ij}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}I_{j}^{-1}I_{i}
, which is a left ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{j}=\End(\phi_{j})
with right order
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\End(\phi_{i})
.
One can show that there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{ij}\cong\Hom(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})
of left
\mathcal{O}_{i}
and right
\mathcal{O}_{j}
modules.
Choose a generator

\mathfrak{n}_{ij}\in F
of the reduced norm ideal
\Nr(\mathcal{O}_{ij})=\langle\Nr(u)\mid u\in\mathcal{O}_{ij}\rangle_{A}
of
\mathcal{O}_{ij}
; here
\Nr\colon D\rightarrow F

is the reduced norm on
D
. Denote

w_{i}=\#\Aut(\phi_{i})/(q-1)=\#(\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\times})/(q-1).

Then
b_{ij}(\mathfrak{m})

can be alternatively defined, using only the data of the quaternion algebra
D
, as

(3.2)

b_{ij}(\mathfrak{m})=\frac{\#\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{ij}\mid(\Nr(u)/\mathfrak{n}_{ij})=(\mathfrak{m})\}}{(q-1)w_{j}}.

The fact that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are the same numbers is proved in
[
Pap05
, Prop. 2.7]
using the results
from
[
Gek91
]
, especially
[
Gek91
, Lem. 3.10]
.

3.2.
Theta series

Fix two supersingular Drinfeld modules
\phi_{i},\phi_{j}
of rank
2
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and denote

\mathcal{O}_{ij}=\Hom(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})
. Let

\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{ij}\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space, so we can define

m^{ij}_{s}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s}.

The goal of this subsection is to compute
m^{ij}_{s}
for
s
sufficiently large compared to
d
.

Because
\deg_{\tau}(u)=\deg_{T}(\mathfrak{N}(u))
(see (
2.2
)), we can write

\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s}=\bigsqcup_{m=0}^{s}\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg(\mathfrak{m})=m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{\mathfrak{m}},

where

\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{ij}\mid(\mathfrak{N}(u))=(\mathfrak{m})\}.

Thus,

(3.3)

\#\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s}=\#\mathcal{M}^{ij}_{s-1}+\#\Aut(\phi_{j})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg(\mathfrak{m})=s\end{subarray}}b_{ij}(\mathfrak{m}).

Assuming that
\phi_{i}
and
\phi_{j}
are fixed, we will omit the double indices from
b_{i,j}(\mathfrak{m})
.
Let
\varpi_{\infty}
be a fixed uniformizer at the place
\infty
. Define Fourier coefficients
[
WY11
, p. 735]
(see also
[
Pap05
, p. 271]
),

\displaystyle c_{0}(\varpi_{\infty}^{k})

\displaystyle=q^{-k}/w_{j},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z};

\displaystyle c(\mathfrak{m}\varpi_{\infty}^{k})

\displaystyle=b(\mathfrak{m})q^{-k},\quad k\geq 0.

These Fourier coefficients define a unique
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
-invariant harmonic cochain
\Theta
on the Bruhat-Tits tree of
\PGL_{2}(F_{\infty})
,
where
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
is the Hecke congruence subgroup of
\GL_{2}(A)
of level
\mathfrak{p}
. We refer
to
[
WY11
]
and
[
Pap05
]
for the definition of harmonic cochains, the precise definition of
\Theta
, and the proof of the claim
that
\Theta
is harmonic and
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
-invariant.

The
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
H(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})}
of
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})
-invariant harmonic cochain on
\mathcal{T}
decomposes as (see
[
Pap05
, Prop. 3.4]
)

H(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})}=\mathbb{C}E_{\mathfrak{p}}\bigoplus H_{!}(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})},

where
H_{!}(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})}
are the cuspidal harmonic cochains and
E_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the Eisenstein series
defined by

\displaystyle c_{0}(E_{\mathfrak{p}},\varpi_{\infty}^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\left|\mathfrak{p}\right|-1}{q^{2}-1}q^{-k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z};

\displaystyle c(E_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{m}\varpi_{\infty}^{k})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})q^{-k},\quad k\geq 0,

where

\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in A_{+}\\
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mid\mathfrak{m},\ \mathfrak{p}\nmid\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\right|.

The cusp forms in
H(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})}
are characterized by the vanishing of their “constant” Fourier coefficients
c_{0}
; thus,

\Theta_{c}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}w_{j}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{p}\right|-1}{q^{2}-1}\Theta-E_{\mathfrak{p}}

is a cusp form with Fourier coefficients

\displaystyle c(\Theta_{c},\mathfrak{m}\varpi_{\infty}^{k})

\displaystyle=\left(w_{j}\frac{\left|\mathfrak{p}\right|-1}{q^{2}-1}b(\mathfrak{m})-\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})\right)q^{-k}

\displaystyle=c(\mathfrak{m})q^{-k}.

For a cusp form
f\in H_{!}(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{C})^{\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{p})}
, following Weil, one defines its
L
-function as (see
[
Pap05
, p. 273]
)

L(f,s)=\sum_{\beta\text{ pos. div.}}c(f,\beta)\left|\beta\right|^{-s},\qquad\left|\beta\right|=q^{\deg(\beta)},

where the sum is over all non-negative divisors, including those with an
\infty
-component. In the case of
\Theta_{c}
, we get

\displaystyle L(\Theta_{c},s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}}c(\mathfrak{m})q^{-k}\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|^{-s}q^{ks}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}}\frac{c(\mathfrak{m})}{\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|^{s}}\right)\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{-k(1-s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-q^{1-s}}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}}\frac{c(\mathfrak{m})}{\left|\mathfrak{m}\right|^{s}}\right).

If
d\leq 2
, there are no nonzero cusp forms, so
\Theta=\frac{w_{j}(q^{2}-1)}{|p|-1}E_{\mathfrak{p}}

exactly, i.e.,
c(\mathfrak{m})=0
identically. In that case, the same computation as below give the formula
m_{s}^{ij}=2(s+1)-(d-1)
for all
s\geq 0
.

On the other hand, for
d\geq 3
,
it follows from deep results of Drinfeld, Deligne and Grothendieck that
L(f,s)
is a polynomial in
q^{-s}
of degree
d-3
; see
[
Pap03
, Ch. 2]
.
Let

L(\Theta_{c},s)=a_{0}+a_{1}q^{-s}+\cdots+a_{d-3}(q^{-s})^{d-3}\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{-s}].

Writing
x
for
q^{-s}
, we get

(a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{d-3}x^{d-3})(1-qx)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg\mathfrak{m}=m\end{subarray}}c(\mathfrak{m}).

This implies that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg\mathfrak{m}=m\end{subarray}}c(\mathfrak{m})=0\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq d-1,

or equivalently

(3.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg\mathfrak{m}=m\end{subarray}}b(\mathfrak{m})=\frac{1}{w_{j}}\frac{q^{2}-1}{\left|\mathfrak{p}\right|-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg\mathfrak{m}=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})\quad\text{for}\quad m\geq d-1.

The following lemma follows from a standard argument:

Lemma 3.4
.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg\mathfrak{m}=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})=\begin{cases}\frac{q^{m}(q^{m+1}-1)}{q-1},&\text{for }m\leq d-1,\\
\frac{q^{2m-d+1}(\left|\mathfrak{p}\right|-1)}{q-1},&\text{for }m\geq d-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Denote the sum in question by
s(m)
and consider the generating function

f(x)=\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in A_{+}}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})x^{\deg(\mathfrak{m})}=\sum_{m\geq 0}s(m)x^{m}.

Write
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{p}^{a}\mathfrak{n}
with
a\geq 0
and
\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{p})=1
. Since
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{m})=\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})
, we have

f(x)=\left(\sum_{a\geq 0}x^{ad}\right)\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{n}\in A_{+}\\
\gcd(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{p})=1\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{n})x^{\deg(\mathfrak{n})}=\frac{1}{1-x^{d}}\cdot g(x).

Since
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
is multiplicative on polynomials coprime to
\mathfrak{p}
,

g(x)=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}}\left(\sum_{e\geq 0}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{q}^{e})x^{e\deg\mathfrak{q}}\right).

Let
\delta=\deg\mathfrak{q}
. Then
\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{q}^{e})=\frac{q^{(e+1)\delta}-1}{q-1}
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{e\geq 0}\sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{q}^{e})x^{e\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{\delta}-1}\left(\frac{q^{\delta}}{1-q^{\delta}x^{\delta}}-\frac{1}{1-x^{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x^{\delta})(1-q^{\delta}x^{\delta})}.

Using the standard identities

\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\frac{1}{1-x^{\deg\mathfrak{q}}}=\frac{1}{1-qx},\qquad\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\frac{1}{1-q^{\deg\mathfrak{q}}x^{\deg\mathfrak{q}}}=\frac{1}{1-q^{2}x},

we obtain

g(x)=\frac{(1-x^{d})(1-q^{d}x^{d})}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}.

Thus,

f(x)=\frac{1}{(1-x^{d})}\frac{(1-x^{d})(1-q^{d}x^{d})}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}=\frac{1-q^{d}x^{d}}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}.

Given a series
H(x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}h(m)x^{m}
, denote by
[x^{m}]H(x)=h(m)
, i.e., “extract the
m
-th coefficient” function. With this notation, since

\frac{1}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\frac{q}{1-q^{2}x}-\frac{1}{1-qx}\right),

we get

[x^{m}]\frac{1}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}=\frac{1}{q-1}(q\cdot q^{2m}-q^{m})=\frac{q^{m}(q^{m+1}-1)}{q-1}.

Thus,

s(m)=[x^{m}]f(x)=[x^{m}]\frac{1}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}-q^{d}[x^{m-d}]\frac{1}{(1-qx)(1-q^{2}x)}.

For
m<d
, the second term vanishes, giving
s(m)=\frac{q^{m}(q^{m+1}-1)}{q-1}
. On the other hand, for
m\geq d
,

\displaystyle s(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{m}(q^{m+1}-1)}{q-1}-\frac{q^{d}\cdot q^{m-d}(q^{m-d+1}-1)}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2m-d+1}(q^{d}-1)}{q-1}.

Finally, for the borderline case
m=d-1
, note that both expressions are equal, i.e.,

\frac{q^{d-1}(q^{(d-1)+1}-1)}{q-1}=\frac{q^{2(d-1)-d+1}(q^{d}-1)}{q-1}.\qed

Combining (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and Lemma
3.4
, we obtain

q^{m_{s}}=q^{m_{s-1}}+q^{2s-d+1}\frac{\#\Aut(\phi_{j})}{(q-1)w_{j}}(q^{2}-1),\qquad\text{for}\quad s\geq d-1.

Thus,

q^{m_{s-1}}(q^{m_{s}-m_{s-1}}-1)=q^{2s-d+1}(q^{2}-1),\qquad\text{for}\quad s\geq d-1.

Since
(q^{2}-1)
and
(q^{m_{s}-m_{s-1}}-1)
are coprime to
q
, we get

m_{s-1}=2s-(d-1)
. Thus, we have proved the following:

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

m_{s}^{ij}=2(s+1)-(d-1)\qquad\text{if}\quad s\geq d-2.

In particular,

m_{d-2}^{ij}=d-1.

Remark 3.6
.

Generally, the values
m_{0}(\phi),m_{1}(\phi),\dots,m_{d-3}(\phi)
do not exhibit specific patterns persistent over all supersingular
Drinfeld modules
\phi
. For example, for
q=3
and
\mathfrak{p}=T^{5}+2T+1
, explicit calculations show that the following triples are
the possible
(m_{0}(\phi),m_{1}(\phi),m_{2}(\phi))
as
\phi
varies among the isomorphism classes of supersingular Drinfeld modules:

(2,2,4),\quad(1,1,3),\quad(1,2,3),\quad(1,1,2).

3.3.
Example

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and let
g=\gcd(r,d)
.
In this subsection,
\phi
denotes the Drinfeld module of rank
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
defined by

\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{r}.

Lemma 3.7
.

With notation as above, we have
H(\phi)=r/g
. In particular,
\phi
is supersingular if and only if
g=1
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be the Drinfeld module over
F
defined by
\Phi_{T}=T+\tau^{r}
, so that
\phi
is the reduction of
\Phi
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
.
Set
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[T]
and
F^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}(T)
. The prime
\mathfrak{p}
decomposes in
A^{\prime}
into a product of primes

\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{P}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{P}_{g},

where each
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
has degree
d/g
. Viewing
\Phi
as a Drinfeld module over
F^{\prime}
, the reduction of
\Phi
modulo
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
is isomorphic to
\phi
over the degree
r/g
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, via the natural embedding of residue fields
A/\mathfrak{p}\hookrightarrow A^{\prime}/\mathfrak{P}_{1}
.

On the other hand,
\Phi
over
F^{\prime}
is a Drinfeld
A^{\prime}
-module of rank
1
, and the reduction of a rank
1
Drinfeld module has height
1
. Therefore,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\equiv\tau^{rd/g}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{P}_{1})
.
For
2\leq i\leq g
, the constant term of
\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}_{i}}
is
\mathfrak{P}_{i}
, which is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{P}_{1}
. Letting
\alpha\neq 0
denote the product of the images of
\mathfrak{P}_{2},\ldots,\mathfrak{P}_{g}
in
A^{\prime}/\mathfrak{P}_{1}
, we compute

\displaystyle\Ht(\phi_{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle=\Ht(\Phi_{\mathfrak{p}}\bmod{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})

\displaystyle=\Ht(\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}_{1}}\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}_{2}}\cdots\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}_{g}}\bmod{\mathfrak{P}_{1}})

\displaystyle=\Ht(\alpha\,\tau^{rd/g}+\text{higher degree terms})

\displaystyle=rd/g.

Thus,
H(\phi)=r/g
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The previous lemma strengthens the statement in
[
Pap23
, Example 4.4.5]
and simplifies the argument in that example.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume
g=1
, so that
\phi
is supersingular. Then

\End(\phi)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[\phi_{T},\tau^{d}].

Proof.

We again set
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[T]
and
F^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}(T)
.

The inclusion
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[\phi_{T},\tau^{d}]\subseteq\End(\phi)
is clear. The ring
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[\phi_{T},\tau^{d}]
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}A^{\prime}[\pi]/(\pi^{r}-\mathfrak{p})
, where
\pi
and the elements of
A^{\prime}
satisfy the commutation relation

\pi(f_{0}+f_{1}T+\cdots+f_{s}T^{s})=(f_{0}^{q^{d}}+f_{1}^{q^{d}}T+\cdots+f_{s}^{q^{d}}T^{s})\pi.

Since
g=1
, the automorphism of
F^{\prime}
induced by
\pi
generates
\Gal(F^{\prime}/F)
, so
D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}\otimes_{A}F
is a cyclic
F
-algebra of dimension
r^{2}
and
\mathcal{O}
is an
A
-order in
D
. A direct computation from this presentation shows that the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}
is
\mathfrak{p}^{r(r-1)}
, which implies that
\mathcal{O}
is a maximal
A
-order in
D
; see
[
BG16
, Sec. 6]
. Therefore,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[\phi_{T},\tau^{d}]=\End(\phi)
.
∎

As before, define

\mathcal{M}_{s}=\{u\in\End(\phi)\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\},

and let
m_{s}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Assume
g=1
, so that
\phi
is supersingular. Then

m_{s}=r(s+1)-r\frac{(r-1)(d-1)}{2}

for all
s\geq(r-1)(d-1)-1
.

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
, so
m_{0}=r
. For any
s\geq 0
, the difference of two elements of
\tau
-degree
s+1
in

\End(\phi)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}[\phi_{T},\tau^{d}]
, scaled appropriately by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{r}}
, lies in
\mathcal{M}_{s}
, so
m_{s+1}\leq m_{s}+r
. More precisely,
m_{s+1}=m_{s}+r
if
\End(\phi)
contains an element of
\tau
-degree exactly
s+1
, and
m_{s+1}=m_{s}
otherwise. Moreover,
the former occurs if and only if
s+1
is representable as
nr+md
with
n,m\geq 0
.

Since
\gcd(r,d)=1
, the Sylvester–Frobenius theorem
[
Syl84
]
implies that every integer
\geq(r-1)(d-1)
is representable.
Moreover, in the range
1,\ldots,(r-1)(d-1)-1
exactly
(r-1)(d-1)/2-1
integers are representable.
It follows that for

1\leq s\leq(r-1)(d-1)-1
, the dimension
m_{s}
increases by
r
at exactly
(r-1)(d-1)/2-1
steps, while
m_{s}=m_{s-1}+r
holds for all
s\geq(r-1)(d-1)
. Combining with
m_{0}=r
, we get

\displaystyle m_{(r-1)(d-1)-1}

\displaystyle=r\frac{(r-1)(d-1)}{2}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle m_{s}

\displaystyle=m_{s-1}+r\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq(r-1)(d-1).

This is equivalent to the stated formula in the proposition.
∎

Note that for
r=2
, the formula in Proposition
3.10
specializes to the formula in Theorem
3.5
.
Moreover, it is easy to compute directly that for
\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{2}
and
d
odd we have

m_{s}=2\lceil(s+1)/2\rceil
for all
0\leq s\leq d-2
.

3.4.
Computing Brandt matrices

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be supersingular Drinfeld modules over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Our goal in this subsection is to give an algorithm which efficiently computes the space

\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)=\{u\in\Hom(\phi,\psi)\mid\deg_{\tau}(u)\leq s\}

as a subspace of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, and the Brandt matrices
B(\mathfrak{q})
for
\deg(\mathfrak{q})\leq s
. We will assume that
\phi
and
\psi
have rank
2
, although the algorithm can be extended to higher ranks.

First, choose convenient models for
\phi
and
\psi
. If
j(\phi)\neq 0
, then we
assume that
\phi
is defined by an equation
\phi_{T}=t+\tau+x\tau^{2}
,
where
x
is a root of
H_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)
(cf. (
2.3
)). If
j(\phi)=0
, then we assume that

\phi
is defined by
\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{2}
. For such
\phi
, by Proposition
2.6
, we know that the Drinfeld module and all its endomorphisms are
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
. More generally, by Proposition
2.6
, all isogenies
\phi\rightarrow\psi
are in many cases defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
,
and in the worst case scenario they are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2(q-1)}}
.
Let
k=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
if we are computing endomorphism, and let
k=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2(q-1)}}
if we are computing isogenies between non-isomorphic
Drinfeld modules.

Write a general element
u\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
in the form

u=\sum_{i=0}^{s}u_{i}\tau^{i},\qquad u_{i}\in k.

The unknowns are the coefficients
u_{0},\dots,u_{s}
. The products

u\phi_{T}
and

\psi_{T}u

can be expanded explicitly as skew polynomials in
\tau
. Since
\phi_{T}
and
\psi_{T}

have
\tau
-degree
2
, both sides are supported in degrees
\leq s+2
. Equating coefficients of
\tau^{j}
in the identity

u\phi_{T}=\psi_{T}u

for all
0\leq j\leq s+2
yields a system of homogeneous linear equations in the
coefficients
u_{i}
and their Frobenius twists
u_{i}^{q^{r}}
.

Fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of the finite field
k
. Each coefficient
u_{i}\in k
is then
represented by an
m
-tuple of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
m=[k:\mathbb{F}_{q}]
. After this
identification, the commutation relations become a homogeneous linear system over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
with

(s+1)m

unknowns and
(s+3)m
equations.

The solution space of this system is precisely
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
.
For increasing values of
s
we compute the spaces

\mathcal{M}_{0}(\phi,\psi)\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(\phi,\psi)\subset\mathcal{M}_{2}(\phi,\psi)\subset\cdots

using nested basis. More precisely, a basis of
\mathcal{M}_{s-1}(\phi,\psi)
is embedded into the
ambient space for
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
by appending
m
zero coordinates. This padded
basis is then extended to a full basis of
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
.
The result is a nested
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis, which is essential for efficiently enumerating
isogenies of exact
\tau
-degree
s
.

Now let
\mathfrak{q}
be a prime of
A
of degree
s
such that
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
. To determine
whether
u\in\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
has norm
\mathfrak{q}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{N}(u)=\mathfrak{q}
, it is sufficient to check whether

u
right-divides
\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}
in
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, which is easy to do using the division algorithm. Indeed,

\ker(u)
is an
A
-module under the action of
\phi
. Since
\mathfrak{q}
is prime,
\ker(u)\cong A/\mathfrak{q}
if and only
if
\ker(u)\subset\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}
, which is equivalent to right-divisibility as
u
and
\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}
are separable.

Since we can efficiently generate all isogenies
\phi\rightarrow\psi
of degree
s
, we can compute the entry of the
Brandt matrix
B(\mathfrak{q})
corresponding to
(\phi,\psi)
. Note that because the entries of the
i
-th row of
B(\mathfrak{q})

correspond to submodule-schemes of
\phi_{i}
isomorphic to
A/\mathfrak{q}
and
\phi_{i}[\mathfrak{q}]\cong A/\mathfrak{p}\times A/\mathfrak{q}

contains
\left|\mathfrak{q}\right|+1
such submodules, we have

\sum_{j}b_{ij}(\mathfrak{q})=\left|\mathfrak{q}\right|+1=q^{s}+1.

This equality provides a useful consistency check in explicit computations.

Remark 3.11
.

We briefly analyze the complexity. Fix
s
and let
m=[k:\mathbb{F}_{q}]
. Computing
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
requires solving a linear system over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of size
O(sm)\times O(sm)
, which costs
O(s^{\omega}m^{\omega})
operations in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
\omega\leq 3
is the matrix multiplication exponent.
For fixed
q
, we have
m=O(d)
when computing endomorphisms (
k=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
) and
m=O(qd)
when computing isogenies (
k=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2(q-1)}}
).
To compute the full entry
b_{ij}(\mathfrak{q})
with
\deg(\mathfrak{q})=s
, we enumerate
O(q^{m_{s}})
elements of
\mathcal{M}_{s}
and test each by a right-division in
k\!\left\{\tau\right\}
, which costs
O(s^{2}m)
operations. The overall cost to compute one entry of
B(\mathfrak{q})
is thus

O(q^{m_{s}}\cdot s^{2}m)=O(q^{2s}s^{2}m).

We implemented the above algorithm in
Magma
and the following examples were computed using that program.

4.
Constructing semifield codes

We keep the notation of
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
.
Let
\phi
and
\psi
be two Drinfeld modules over
k
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\Hom(\phi,\psi)
. Let
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
be a fixed prime. An element
u\in\mathcal{M}

maps
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
into
\psi[\mathfrak{q}]
, thus induces a linear transformation
u\colon\phi[\mathfrak{q}]\rightarrow\psi[\mathfrak{q}]
between
two
2
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}
-vector spaces. Fixing
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}
-bases of
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
and
\psi[\mathfrak{q}]
,
we get a homomorphism
\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}})
. If the image of each
0\neq u
in
\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}})
is an invertible matrix,
then we say that
\mathcal{M}
is
invertible
on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
. If
\mathcal{M}
is invertible and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}=2\cdot\deg(\mathfrak{q}),

then
\mathcal{M}\subset\Mat_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}})
is a semifield code; see
[
MP26
]
.

In this section, we use the spaces
\mathcal{M}_{s}=\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
to construct semifield codes.
We know that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{M}_{s}=2(s+1)-(d-1)
for all
s\geq d-2
.
Note that
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
for any prime
\mathfrak{q}
of degree
s+1
by the same argument as Example
1.1
,
but this does not give a semifield code if
d\geq 2

because
2(s+1)-(d-1)<2(s+1)
.

By a
hyperplane
in
\mathcal{M}_{s}
we mean a codimension-
1
subspace. Given
\mathcal{M}_{s}
and a prime
\mathfrak{q}
, we call
0\neq u\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
a
bad element
if
u
is not invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
, and we call a hyperplane
H\subset\mathcal{M}_{s}

good
if
H
contains no bad elements.

We use the following strategy to construct semifield codes:

(1)

If
d>1
is odd, take a prime
\mathfrak{q}
of degree
s^{\prime}
such that
2s^{\prime}=2(s+1)-(d-1)
. In the Brandt matrix
B(\mathfrak{q})
find a zero entry, if such exists.
Let
(\phi,\psi)
be the pair corresponding to the chosen zero entry. By definition,
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
is invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
, so
gives a semifield code.

(2)

If
d
is even, take a prime
\mathfrak{q}
of degree
s^{\prime}
such that
2s^{\prime}=2(s+1)-(d-1)-1
. Because of the Singleton bound,
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)

is never invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
for any
\phi
and
\psi
. Instead, find a good hyperplane in
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}
, if such exists.
The subspace corresponding to the good hyperplane is invertible on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
and gives a semifield code.

Below we demonstrate this idea on some examples for
d\leq 4
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We again call a subspace
H\subset V
of dimension
n-1
a
hyperplane
.
Let
S
be a set of
nonzero
vectors in
V
,
n\geq 2
, blocking all hyperplanes, i.e.,
every hyperplane contains at least one element of
S
. Let
S
be such a blocking set of minimal cardinality.
We claim that
\left|S\right|=q+1
. Indeed, let
W\subset V
be a
2
-dimensional subspace. The number of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
W
is
q+1
. Choose one nonzero vector from each of these
1
-dimensional subspaces, and let
S^{\prime}
be the set of these vectors.
Since any hyperplane
H\subset V
intersects
W
in a subspace of dimension
\geq 1
, it contains at least one element of
S^{\prime}
.
Thus
\left|S\right|\leq q+1
. To get the reverse inequality,
consider the subspaces

s^{\perp}=\{f\in V^{\ast}:f(s)=0\},\quad s\in S.

These are hyperplanes in the dual space
V^{\ast}
. The condition that every hyperplane of
V
contains an element of
S
is equivalent to the statement that every nonzero functional
f\in V^{\ast}
lies in
s^{\perp}
for some
s\in S
.
Thus the space
V^{\ast}
is covered by the hyperplanes
s^{\perp}
. A vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
cannot be covered by fewer than
q+1
proper subspaces, so we must have

\left|S\right|\geq q+1
.

The point of this remark is that if the set of bad elements in
\mathcal{M}_{s}
has cardinality
\leq q
, then a good hyperplane is guaranteed to exist.
Unfortunately, in practice this is never the case, and the number of bad elements grows with
s
. It is the geometric distribution of bad elements
that determines the existence of good hyperplanes. In particular, for
d
even the existence of a good hyperplane in
M_{s}(\phi,\psi)

is not guaranteed in general and depends on the arithmetic of the specific pair

(\phi,\psi)
; the examples in Sections
4.2
and
4.4
suggest that good hyperplanes
exist in practice but we have no general theorem to this effect.

4.1.
Case
d=1

This case is easy since
m_{s}=2(s+1)
for all
s\geq 0
,
so if we take any prime
\mathfrak{q}
of degree
s+1
, then
\mathcal{M}_{s+1}
is invertible
on
\phi[\mathfrak{q}]
and is a semifield code. In this case, there is only one supersingular
j
-invariant, namely
\phi_{T}=t+\tau^{2}
.

4.2.
Case
d=2

In this case we again have only one supersingular Drinfeld module, up to isomorphism over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
A representative of the supersingular class defined over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is given by

\phi_{T}=t+\tau+\frac{1}{t^{q}-t}\tau^{2}.

In this case, for all
s\geq 0
,

m_{s}=2s+1.

Let

q=3
and let

\mathfrak{p}=T^{2}+2T+2.

Let
w^{2}+t=0
, so
w
generates
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We fix

\mathfrak{q}=T^{4}+T^{3}+2T+1.

Let
s=4
and let
\mathcal{M}_{4}\subset\End(\phi)
be the
\mathbb{F}_{3}
-vector space of endomorphisms of degree at most
4
.
This space is
9
-dimensional, with the following basis computed using the algorithm of Section
3.4
.

\displaystyle b_{1}=1,\quad b_{2}=w+(2w)\tau,\quad b_{3}=t^{5}w+t^{5}w\,\tau,

\displaystyle b_{4}=t^{5}+2\tau+t\tau^{2},\quad b_{5}=\tau^{2},\quad b_{6}=w\tau^{2}+(2w)\tau^{3},

\displaystyle b_{7}=t^{5}w\tau^{2}+t^{5}w\tau^{3},\quad b_{8}=t^{5}+2\tau+2\tau^{3}+t\tau^{4},\quad b_{9}=\tau^{4}.

Next, our program cycled through all nonzero elements of
\mathcal{M}_{4}
and tested whether they are coprime to
\phi_{\mathfrak{q}}
in the skew polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}\{\tau\}
; this is easy to do using the division algorithm. The computation produced exactly

164
nonzero bad elements in
\mathbb{F}_{3}^{9}
.
We then searched for hyperplanes avoiding all of these bad elements. The total number of hyperplanes is

(3^{9}-1)/(3-1)=9841.
The computation showed that the number of good hyperplanes is

8832.

Thus, the overwhelming majority of hyperplanes is good.
In particular, the very simple hyperplane

x_{1}+x_{4}=0

already avoids the entire set of bad elements.

4.3.
Case
d=3

In this case
m_{s}=2s
for all
s\geq 1
. Here we take a prime
\mathfrak{q}
with
\deg(\mathfrak{q})=s

and look for a zero entry in the Brandt matrix
B(\mathfrak{q})
.
Then
\mathcal{M}_{s}(\phi,\psi)
corresponding to that zero entry gives a semifield code.
In Example
3.12
, this works for
s=1,2,3
, but not for
s=4
.

Remark 4.2
.

In Example
3.12
, the strategy of finding a zero entry in
B(\mathfrak{q})
works for

\deg(\mathfrak{q})\leq 3=d
but fails for
\deg(\mathfrak{q})=4>d
. We expect this to be a
general phenomenon: heuristics from the theory of representation of elements of
A

by quadratic forms suggest that
B(\mathfrak{q})
has no zero entries once
\deg(\mathfrak{q})>d
,
though we do not know the exact threshold. This is related to the question of
completeness of supersingular isogeny graphs: a zero entry
b_{ij}(\mathfrak{q})=0
means
there is no
\mathfrak{q}
-isogeny
\phi_{i}\rightarrow\phi_{j}
, i.e., the supersingular isogeny
graph of level
\mathfrak{q}
is not complete. One therefore expects these graphs to become
complete once
\deg(\mathfrak{q})
is sufficiently large relative to
d
.

4.4.
Case
d=4

In this case
m_{s}=2s-1
for all
s\geq 2
. Let
q=3
and
\mathfrak{p}=T^{4}+T+2
. Let
w
be a fixed root of
x^{2}+t
, so
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}=\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}[w]
.

We will shrink
\mathcal{M}_{3}
to get a semifield. We consider the rank-
2
Drinfeld module over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
defined by

\phi_{T}=t+\tau+t^{26}\tau^{2}\;\in\;\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\{\tau\}.

Let

\mathfrak{q}=T^{2}+T+2
be as before. The space
\mathcal{M}_{3}\subset\End(\phi)
is
5
-dimensional and is spanned over
