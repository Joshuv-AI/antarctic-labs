61 Abstract

62 We describe a recoil-carbon polarimeter for the BNL Booster synchrotron capable of measur-
63 ing the transverse polarization of both the polarized proton beam and the polarized 3 He ion beam
64 throughout the acceleration cycle. The instrument addresses a critical gap in the BNL polarized
65 beam program: no independent polarization measurement currently exists in the injector chain
66 between the 200 MeV Linac polarimeter and the AGS. The proposed new polarimeter will pro-
67 vide continuous, absolute polarization measurements at the Booster stage, supplying additional
68 anchor points for the polarization transmission through the accelerator chain. The polarimeter

 2

 69 is based on elastic pC and 3 He C scattering from a thin internal carbon target, with detection of
 70 the recoil 12 C nucleus in a fixed six-station silicon detector ring. A single detector geometry pro-
 71 vides continuous kinematic coverage from injection to extraction for both beam species without
 72 mechanical adjustment. For pC scattering, the available data and the Bonin parametrization
 73 provide a well-established basis for estimating the analyzing power and figure of merit over the
 74 Booster energy range; for 3 He C the sole experimental anchor is a measurement at 443 MeV,
 75 and the Booster polarimeter itself is identified as the instrument to map the analyzing power
 76 across the remainder of the acceleration ramp by a ramp-and-return calibration that anchors
 77 the absolute 3 He polarization scale to a few percent. Provision for future deuteron polarime-
 78 try is incorporated in the chamber design without modification to the existing geometry. With
 79 appropriate detector upgrades, the same recoil-carbon technique can be extended to deuteron
 80 beams, for which analyzing power data are already available, and, with future analyzing-power
 81 measurements, also to 6 Li and 7 Li beams, making the polarimeter a versatile instrument for the
 82 full range of light polarized ion species anticipated at BNL.

 83 1 Introduction

 84 The BNL polarized beam program requires high polarization at the Electron Ion Collider (EIC),
 85 placing stringent demands on polarization transmission through every stage of the injector chain.
 86 RHIC completed its final run in February 2026, after which the complex transitions to EIC con-
 87 struction while the Alternating Gradient Synchrotron (AGS), in continuous operation since 1960,
 88 undergoes a multi-year refurbishment including extensive recabling and hardware upgrades. During

 93 Booster is required to monitor and optimize the polarization of both the polarized proton beam

116 This polarimeter operates in a regime fundamentally different from the Coulomb-Nuclear Inter-
117 ference (CNI) polarimeters used at the AGS and RHIC. The CNI analyzing power formalism [7]
118 is valid only in the Regge regime of hadronic scattering, where the forward elastic amplitude is
119 dominated by Pomeron exchange and varies smoothly and slowly with energy, and the total cross
120 section and the real-to-imaginary ratio ρ of the hadronic amplitude are well-defined slowly-varying
121 quantities. This smooth behavior sets in above plab ∼ 1 GeV/c to 2 GeV/c; below this threshold
122 the amplitude is dominated by individual baryon and meson resonances, the total cross section
123 varies rapidly with energy, and the CNI parametrization is no longer applicable. At Booster ener-
124 gies (p ∼ 0.6 GeV/c to 2.3 GeV/c for protons) nuclear elastic scattering with a measured analyzing
125 power is therefore the correct approach, and the Booster polarimeter and the AGS pC CNI polarime-
126 ter [8, 9] are complementary by necessity, covering energy regimes where fundamentally different
127 reaction mechanisms are at work.
128 A key requirement for Booster operation is that the detector geometry must provide continuous
129 coverage over the full energy range of both beams listed in Table 1, without mechanical adjustment
130 during the acceleration cycle. Because the recoil-carbon kinematics scale smoothly with beam
131 momentum, a fixed detector configuration can satisfy this requirement, as discussed in detail in
132 Sec. 2.

146 clean identification of elastic events over the full Booster energy range listed in Table 1.

172 2.2 Recoil energy, detector requirements for pC and 3 HeC elastic scattering

173 Because the carbon mass is much larger than the proton mass and still significantly larger than the
174
 3 He mass, the recoil carbon nuclei emerge predominantly at large laboratory angles. Over the full

175 Booster energy range given in Table 1, the recoil carbon angles remain concentrated near

                                                 θC ≈ 70◦ to 90◦ .                                        (10)

176 This is a key feature enabling a fixed recoil detector geometry.
177 The recoil kinetic energy is obtained exactly from Eq. (8). At fixed beam energy it increases as
178 the forward projectile angle increases, or equivalently as the recoil angle decreases away from 90◦ .
179 For the proton beam, the recoil carbon kinetic energies range from the sub-MeV region near
180 injection to several tens of MeV near extraction. For the 3 He ion beam, the recoil energies are
181 correspondingly larger due to the larger projectile mass and momentum at the same magnetic
182 rigidity.
183 The forward scattered projectile emerges at relatively small laboratory angles. The relation
184 between forward scattering angle and beam momentum follows directly from the exact two-body
185 kinematics discussed above and is illustrated for the relevant beam species and energies in Figs. 1
186 and 2.
187 These kinematic properties form the basis of the polarimeter design. The correlation between
188 recoil carbon laboratory angle θC and projectile laboratory angle θ is shown in Fig. 2 for pC and
189
 3 HeC elastic scattering at Booster injection and extraction energies. As seen in the figure, over the

190 full Booster energy range and for projectile scattering angles up to about 30◦ , the recoil carbon nuclei
191 are confined to a relatively narrow angular interval near 70◦ to 90◦ , with only a weak dependence
192 on beam species and beam energy. This allows the recoil carbon nuclei to be detected in silicon
193 detectors placed at fixed laboratory angles near 90◦ throughout the Booster acceleration cycle.
194 Optional forward detectors placed at small laboratory angles can be used to detect the scattered
195 proton or 3 He ion beam particles in coincidence. The unique correlation between recoil angle and
196 recoil energy ensures clean identification of elastic events and separation from inelastic background
197 over the full Booster energy range.

225 3 Data Situation for pC and 3 HeC Elastic Scattering

226 The polarimeter performance depends on the availability of experimental data for the differential
227 cross section and analyzing power Ay for elastic scattering in the Booster energy range. These
228 quantities determine the expected event rates, the achievable statistical precision, and the sensitivity
229 of the polarimeter. Since a pC polarimeter already exists at 200 MeV behind the Linac, the primary
230 emphasis here is on the higher energies approaching Booster extraction. In addition, the available
231 data for 3 HeC elastic scattering in the Booster energy range must be assessed to establish the
232 feasibility of polarized 3 He measurements.

233 3.1 pC Elastic Scattering

234 Elastic pC scattering has been studied extensively over a wide range of beam energies. Differential
235 cross section and analyzing power data exist in the energy region relevant for Booster operation,
236 including energies approaching and exceeding 1.5 GeV. These data provide the basis for predict-
237 ing the recoil carbon angular and energy distributions and for determining the analyzing power
238 accessible to the Booster polarimeter.
239 For quantitative Booster polarimeter performance estimates we require both the analyzing power
240 Ay and the differential yield for pC scattering in the angular domain relevant for recoil-carbon
241 detection. In the present context, we restrict the projectile laboratory scattering angle to θ ≤ 30◦ ,
242 which corresponds to recoil-carbon laboratory angles θC ≃ 70◦ to 90◦ as established by the elastic
243 kinematics in Sec. 2 and Fig. 2. The analyzing power is described by the Bonin parametrization [14],
244 which extends the McNaughton form [4] and provides an analytic representation of Ay as a function
245 of beam momentum and scattering angle in the energy range relevant for the Booster. In this ap-

 9

246 proach, Ay is expressed in terms of a smooth function of θ with momentum-dependent coefficients,
247 fitted to a large body of proton–carbon polarization data and constrained by dedicated measure-
248 ments [3, 15]. In addition to Ay , the event yield must be modeled through the differential cross
249 section, or an equivalent differential efficiency. Bonin et al. [14] provide an analytic parametrization
250 of the inclusive pC scattering yield in the momentum range relevant for the Booster, which can
251 be used as a practical surrogate for dσ/dΩ for rate estimates and optimization of the polarimeter
252 acceptance.

438 4.1 Measurement concept and event identification

439 The Booster polarimeter is based on elastic nuclear scattering of the circulating beam from a thin
440 internal carbon target, with detection of the recoil 12 C nucleus. The transverse beam polarization
441 is determined from the azimuthal asymmetry of the recoil carbon yield, which is modulated by the
442 analyzing power Ay of the elastic reaction according to

 dN
                                    = N0 (θ, ϕ) [1 + Ay (θ) (Py cos ϕ − Px sin ϕ)] ,                    (41)
 dϕ

443 where N0 (θ, ϕ) is the azimuthally symmetric unpolarized yield, Ay (θ) is the analyzing power, Py
444 and Px are the vertical and horizontal components of the transverse beam polarization, and ϕ
445 is the azimuthal angle of the recoil nucleus measured positive from the x-axis toward y. The
446 sign convention follows from the normal to the scattering plane n̂ = (− sin ϕ, cos ϕ, 0), giving
447 P⃗ · n̂ = Py cos ϕ − Px sin ϕ. The polarization components are extracted from the amplitudes of
448 the cos ϕ and sin ϕ modulations of the recoil yield after integrating over θ within the detector accep-
449 tance. Since the recoil carbon emerges at azimuthal angle ϕC opposite to the scattered projectile,
450 the scattering plane normal constructed from the recoil direction satisfies n̂recoil = −n̂forward ; the
451 analyzing power Ay in Eq. (41) must therefore be taken with opposite sign when the Bonin or
452 McNaughton parametrizations, which are based on forward proton detection, are used directly.
453 The coordinate system used throughout this section is beam-based: the z-axis points along the
454 beam direction, y is vertical (upward), x is horizontal. The polar angle θ is measured from z and
455 the azimuthal angle ϕ from the x-axis toward y. Recoil carbon angles θC and forward scattering
456 angles θ are both defined in the laboratory frame.
457 Each silicon pad detector is segmented into strips. In the recoil ring the strips run radially,
458 subdividing the azimuthal dimension of each station and providing a measurement of ϕ. Since θC
459 is already determined by the energy deposit TC through the elastic kinematic relation in Eqs. (8)
460 and (9), no strip segmentation in the polar direction is required. In the forward ring the strips run
461 azimuthally, subdividing the radial dimension of each station and providing a measurement of θ
462 for the kinematic coincidence constraint. The azimuthal position of a forward hit is determined by
463 which station fired, in the same way as for the recoil ring.

475 4.1.2 Elastic event identification for 3 HeC scattering

476 For 3 He C elastic scattering, the recoil carbon signal alone is insufficient for reliable event identifica-
477 tion. The 3 He nucleus has a binding energy of only 7.72 MeV, and breakup into p+d or p+p+n final
478 states is energetically accessible at all Booster energies. Recoil carbon nuclei produced in breakup
479 reactions, where one or more nucleons are emitted forward and the remainder is absorbed into the
480 nucleus, can populate the same (θC , TC ) region as elastic scattering and cannot be distinguished
481 from elastic recoils using the recoil detector alone.
482 Detection of the forward-scattered 3 He in coincidence with the recoil carbon resolves this ambi-
483 guity. In elastic scattering the forward 3 He angle θ and the recoil carbon angle θC are uniquely
484 correlated through the two-body kinematics: for a given θC there is exactly one θ satisfying mo-
485 mentum conservation, and the measured pair (θ, θC ) must lie on the elastic locus. Breakup events
486 distribute projectile momentum among multiple final-state particles, violating the two-body con-
487 straint and failing the coincidence condition. The forward detector ring is therefore essential for
488
 3 He C polarimetry and is the primary motivation for the two-ring detector concept described in the

489 following subsections.

490 4.2 Recoil detector ring

491 The recoil 12 C nuclei are detected by a ring of six silicon pad detectors arranged azimuthally around
492 the beam axis. The detector centers are located at a radial distance r = 100 mm from the beam
493 axis and at a longitudinal position z = 17.4 mm downstream of the target, placing the detector-
494 face center at a recoil angle θC = arctan(100/17.4) ≈ 80.1◦ . This is close to the centroid of the
495 elastic recoil angular distribution over the Booster energy range and corresponds to the region of
496 large figure of merit identified in Sec. 3.2.3. The six stations are equally spaced in azimuth at
497 ∆ϕ = 60◦ , with opposite pairs oriented to measure the up–down and left–right components of the
498 beam polarization.
499 Each detector is a 40 × 40 mm2 silicon pad of 500 µm thickness. The detector faces are tilted to
500 be perpendicular to the recoil direction at θC ≈ 80◦ , which maximizes the geometric efficiency and
501 ensures that recoil tracks enter the active volume at normal incidence. With this orientation, the
502 recoil ring covers the polar-angle range θC = 69.0◦ –91.3◦ .
503 The recoil 12 C kinetic energy at θC = 70◦ ranges from TC ≈ 7 MeV at the Booster injection
504 energy of Tp = 200 MeV to TC ≈ 72 MeV at the extraction energy of Tp = 1.5 GeV. The 500 µm
505 silicon thickness provides a stopping range of approximately 59 MeV for 12 C nuclei, sufficient to
506 fully stop recoil carbons throughout the pC operating range (as shown in Fig. 3) and allowing both
507 the energy and position of the recoil to be measured simultaneously. This enables event-by-event
508 elastic identification via the TC –θC correlation as discussed in Sec. 4.1.1.
509 For 3 He C scattering at the Booster extraction energy of 832 MeV/u, the recoil carbon kinetic
510 energy at θC = 70◦ reaches approximately 230 MeV, far exceeding the stopping range of 500 µm
511 silicon. In this regime the detector operates as a ∆E element, providing a measurement of the
512 energy deposited in the active volume together with the hit position, which is used in conjunction
513 with the forward coincidence condition described in Sec. 4.1.2 for elastic event identification.

614 4.6.1 Kinematic comparison with 3 HeC scattering

615 At the maximum Booster rigidity Bρ = 7.507 T m the deuteron (md = 1875.613 MeV/c2 , q = +1)

618 than 3 He, the forward-scattered deuteron is deflected to larger laboratory angles than 3 He for the
619 same recoil carbon angle. Over the recoil detector acceptance θC = 69◦ –91◦ , the forward deuteron

621 bulk of the useful acceptance, where the recoil carbon energy is sufficient for reliable detection

623 range lies entirely above the 3 He forward ring acceptance of 7.5◦ –14.8◦ , so the existing forward ring
624 at z = 750 mm provides no useful coincidence coverage for dC elastic scattering in the region of
625 good recoil statistics.

626 4.6.2 Provision for a second forward ring

627 For deuteron polarimetry the forward coincidence serves two purposes. First, it provides the kine-
628 matic constraint needed to identify elastic dC events: the forward deuteron angle θd and the recoil
629 carbon angle θC are uniquely related through the two-body kinematics, and requiring the measured
630 pair (θd , θC ) to lie on the elastic locus suppresses inelastic and quasi-elastic backgrounds. Second, it
631 provides rejection of deuteron breakup events. The deuteron breaks up into p + n with a separation
632 energy of only 2.22 MeV, making breakup energetically accessible throughout the Booster range.
633 In a breakup event the neutron escapes undetected, while the proton carries a large fraction of the

 24

634 beam momentum and may be emitted at a forward angle close to the elastic θd . Without a forward
635 detector such events cannot be distinguished from elastic scattering on the basis of the recoil car-
636 bon signal alone, since the recoil carbon from a quasi-elastic pC collision within the deuteron can
637 populate the same (θC , TC ) region as true elastic dC scattering. The forward coincidence rejects
638 these events because the proton from breakup does not satisfy the dC elastic kinematic locus.
639 To accommodate future deuteron polarimetry the chamber design therefore includes blank flanges
640 at z = 400 mm downstream of the target, at the azimuthal positions of the existing forward stations.
641 At this longitudinal position the forward deuteron angles θd = 14◦ –34◦ correspond to radii r = 100–
642 270 mm, as listed in Table 11. The inner edge at r = 100 mm matches the beam clearance of the
643 existing rings. The radial extent of approximately 170 mm can be covered by two 98 × 98 mm2
644 Hamamatsu silicon pads per station, the same detector type used in the 3 He forward ring. The dC
645 ring at z = 400 mm is not expected to shadow the present 3 He forward ring at z = 750 mm: the 3 He
646 ring subtends θ = 7.5◦ –14.8◦ , which at z = 400 mm projects to r = 53–104 mm, i.e. largely within

754 5.4.1 Inelastic background in pC scattering

757 the first excited state (J π = 2+ ) at Ex = 4.44 MeV, the Hoyle state (J π = 0+ ) at Ex = 7.65 MeV,
758 and the J π = 3− state at Ex = 9.64 MeV [32], where J π denotes the spin and parity of the
759 state. These states are excited in pC scattering and contribute to the inclusive forward proton
760 yield. However, the Bonin parametrization of the differential yield ε(θ, ec , p) [14] was obtained
761 from measurements with thick carbon analyzer blocks and is explicitly inclusive; it parametrizes
762 the total forward-scattered proton yield including contributions from elastic scattering and from
763 excitation of these 12 C states. Similarly, the McNaughton parametrization of the analyzing power
764 Ay (θ, p) [4] was derived from measurements that did not kinematically separate elastic from inelastic
765 events. The two parametrizations are therefore internally consistent: the analyzing power applies
766 to the inclusive yield that the Bonin parametrization describes, and no correction for inelastic
767 contamination is required.
768 This inclusive nature of the pC parametrizations is in fact an advantage for the Booster polarime-
769 ter: the recoil ring operates in singles mode, detecting all recoil carbon nuclei within the kinematic
770 acceptance without requiring an event-by-event elastic selection. The TC –θC kinematic correlation
771 provides a natural filter against non-carbon backgrounds (beam-gas scattering, secondary parti-
772 cles), but the separation of elastic from inelastic 12 C final states is not necessary for an unbiased
773 polarization measurement with the Bonin and McNaughton parametrizations.

797 5.5.1 Inelastic background for 3 He C elastic scattering

798 Two classes of background reactions must be controlled for 3 He C elastic polarimetry: excitation of
799 the 12 C nucleus to low-lying inelastic states, and breakup of the 3 He projectile.
800 For 12 C excitation, the relevant states are the same as those listed in Sec. 5.4.1 for the pC case:
801 the first excited state at 4.44 MeV, the Hoyle state at 7.65 MeV, and the state at 9.64 MeV [32].
802 In 3 He C scattering, excitation of these states shifts the recoil carbon angle θC to smaller values
803 relative to the elastic locus at the same forward angle θ3 He . This occurs because the heavier effective
804 target mass (MC + Ex ) reduces the transverse momentum transfer to the carbon nucleus at fixed
805 forward angle, deflecting the recoil to a smaller polar angle. The shift is ∆θC ≲ −3◦ within the
806 forward detector acceptance and grows with increasing θ3 He , as illustrated in Fig. 12.
807 For 3 He breakup, the two-body channel 3 He → p + d has a threshold of 5.49 MeV and is open
808 throughout the Booster energy range. When the proton fragment scatters off 12 C, the lighter
809 effective projectile mass results in less forward focusing of the recoil carbon, shifting θC to larger
810 values relative to the elastic locus. The sign of the shift is opposite to that of 12 C excitation, so
811 the two background classes appear on opposite sides of the elastic locus in the (θ3 He , θC ) plane.
812 The three-body continuum 3 He → p + p + n, with threshold at 7.72 MeV, populates a broad region
813 bounded by the two-body p + d envelope and is open throughout the Booster energy range. A full
814 treatment of this three-body continuum would require a dedicated Monte Carlo simulation and is
815 beyond the scope of the present analysis; the p + d channel is used as a conservative estimate of the
816 breakup background extent.
817 The forward detector provides two independent handles for background rejection. First, the
818 kinematic constraint in the (θ3 He , θC ) plane places elastic events on a narrow locus that is well
819 separated from both the inelastic and breakup populations, as shown in Fig. 12. With the detector
820 segmentation and geometry adopted for the recoil and forward rings, the elastic locus is resolved
821 from the 4.44 MeV inelastic state across the full acceptance. Second, the forward detector measures
822 the energy deposit ∆E of the forward-scattered particle. In the 320 µm silicon forward detector,
823 an intact 3 He++ ion deposits of order 10 MeV, whereas a breakup proton deposits only of order
824 0.5 MeV. This large separation in ∆E, arising primarily from the Z 2 dependence of the Bethe–Bloch
825 stopping power, provides particle identification independent of the kinematic-locus cut and cleanly
826 rejects 3 He breakup events in which the forward particle is a proton rather than an intact helion.

828 The width of the carbon strip is constrained by the rate capability of the recoil-ring readout rather
829 than by target fabrication. The Timepix3 readout chip sustains a hit throughput of up to about
830 40 Mhits/s/cm2 by design, with 20 Mhits/s/cm2 demonstrated in first measurements [33]; beyond
831 this the per-pixel dead time grows and the output bandwidth saturates. Because the counting rate
832 scales linearly with the geometric overlap fraction, and therefore with the strip width w through
833 Eq. (51), a wide strip would push the recoil ring into this regime: at the AGS strip widths of 75 µm
834 to 250 µm the per-station recoil rate at the upper Booster energies would reach several tens of MHz,

836 strip of width w = 10 µm, matching the carbon ribbons already in routine use in the RHIC CNI

917 6 Light-ion polarimetry at Booster energies: uncharted territory

918 The use of 3 He+C elastic scattering as a polarimeter requires knowledge of the analyzing power Ay
919 across the full Booster energy range. As established in Sec. 3.3 and Fig. 5, the sole experimental
920 anchor point is the measurement by Kamiya et al. at 443 MeV [10], which yielded Ay reaching
921 0.63 near θlab ≈ 6◦ –7◦ with the figure-of-merit peak falling within the forward detector acceptance
922 as discussed in Sec. 4.3. Above 450 MeV, no 3 He+C analyzing power data exist anywhere in the
923 literature, and no calibrated optical model parametrization extends into this regime. The follow-
924 ing subsections discuss the expected behavior of Ay in this uncharted range and the strategy for
925 determining it experimentally using the Booster polarimeter itself.

926 6.1 Missing data, theoretical expectations, and kinematic constraints

927 Global optical potential parametrizations for 3 He projectiles, including those of Trost et al. [34] and
928 Pang et al. [35], are calibrated to energies below 220 MeV and 250 MeV respectively, and cannot be
929 extrapolated reliably into the Booster range. The optical model fit reported in Ref. [10] parametrizes
930 the spin-orbit interaction through a Woods-Saxon derivative form factor whose surface thickness
931 is characterized by a diffuseness parameter aSO : a small value implies a sharp, surface-confined
932 spin-orbit force, while a large value implies a spatially extended interaction. The fit at 443 MeV
933 yielded aSO = 0.6–0.8 fm, in contrast to the much smaller values of 0.2–0.3 fm found in optical model
934 analyses at lower energies, indicating that the spin-orbit interaction evolves significantly with energy
935 already below 500 MeV. The behavior of Ay throughout most of the Booster acceleration ramp is
936 therefore unknown both experimentally and theoretically.
937 Some qualitative guidance follows from the better-studied p+C system. The Bonin parametriza-
938 tion, [4,14] describes the smooth exponential forward peak of the p+C differential cross section and
939 shows that Ay remains in the range 0.1 to 0.3, with a relatively weak momentum dependence of the
940 figure of merit over the range 1.0 to 2.0 GeV/c. For 3 He+C the situation differs in three important
941 respects. First, the larger effective interaction radius leads, within standard optical-model system-
942 atics [36, 37], to a narrower diffraction peak, shifting the useful angular range to smaller laboratory
943 angles. This is consistent with the forward location of the Ay maximum near 6◦ –7◦ at 443 MeV.
944 Second, the analyzing power originates predominantly from the spin–orbit interaction and can be
945 related, in the impulse approximation, to the underlying nucleon–nucleon amplitudes [37, 38], pro-
946 viding a physical basis for a non-vanishing Ay at higher energies. Third, by analogy with α+C
947 elastic scattering, [37], the differential cross section becomes increasingly forward-peaked with en-
948 ergy, concentrating the figure of merit near the forward maximum.
949 A further kinematic concern is the migration of the figure-of-merit peak toward smaller laboratory
950 angles with increasing beam momentum. Since the forward scattering peak in the CM frame scales
951 approximately as 1/pCM , the laboratory angle of peak Ay shifts from 6◦ –7◦ at 443 MeV toward
952 approximately 3◦ –4◦ at Booster extraction energy, well inside the inner edge of the forward detector
953 acceptance at 7.5◦ (Fig. 6). This migration motivates the far-forward detector ring discussed in
954 the Conclusion, and must be taken into account in any evaluation of the effective figure of merit
955 integrated over the forward detector acceptance at the highest Booster energies.

956 6.2 Determining Ay in the presence of depolarization resonances

957 During acceleration in the Booster the 3 He beam traverses a series of spin-depolarizing resonances as
958 the spin tune νs = Gγ sweeps from −4.193 at injection through −4.5 after bunch merging (Table 1,
959 note a) to −7.904 at extraction. Simulations and resonance analysis presented by Hock [29] show
960   that three imperfection resonances are crossed up to |Gγ| = 7.5 and two intrinsic resonances are
961   encountered up to |Gγ| = 10.5. Resonance crossing is managed through harmonic orbit correction
962 and AC dipole techniques. Full spin-flip has been demonstrated in simulation at the intrinsic

 35

 963   resonances |Gγ| = 12 − νy and 6 + νy with AC dipole integrated strengths of 1.65 mT · m and
 964 2.05 mT · m respectively [29]. Nevertheless, partial depolarization at individual resonances cannot
 965 be excluded, particularly where the required corrector currents approach the supply limits.
 966 Determining Ay at an energy where it is unknown requires the beam polarization at that energy,
 967 which the intervening resonances render uncertain. The polarimeter resolves this by exporting the
 968 absolute polarization scale from the single reference energy of 443 MeV, where the 3 He+C analyzing
 969 power is known from Kamiya et al. [10], to any other energy in the ramp through a ramp-and-return
 970 calibration. In its elementary form, the beam is prepared at the reference energy A = 443 MeV with
 971 known polarization PA , ramped to the target energy B where the scattering asymmetry is measured,
 972 and returned to A where the polarization PA′ is measured a second time, so that the round-trip loss
 973 ∆P = PA − PA′ is obtained directly. Since depolarization cannot increase the polarization on either
 974 leg of the excursion, the polarization at B is bracketed by the two measurements, PA′ ≤ PB ≤ PA ,
 975 independently of how the loss is distributed between the up- and down-ramps. The midpoint
 976 PbB = 21 (PA + PA′ ) is the estimate of PB with the smallest worst-case error, ∆P/2, and Ay (B) then
 977 follows from the measured asymmetry as εB /PbB .
 978 Rather than bridging the full range in a single excursion, the scale is propagated by a ladder
 979 of short, adjacent round-trips, A → B → A, B → C → B, C → D → C, and so on, anchored at
 980 A = 443 MeV and extending both upward toward extraction and downward as an independent
 981 chain. Each rung rests only on the polarizations measured at its own two endpoints and on its own
 982 round-trip loss. With only a few resonances spread across the ramp, most rungs cross none and
 983 return a ∆P consistent with zero, while the few that straddle a resonance isolate a single one and
 984 carry the loss; each rung is thereby self-validating, an anomalous crossing appears as a large local
 985 ∆P and implicates only that rung. Steps of order 100 MeV ensure that no rung contains more than
 986 one resonance. Because the calibration is chained, the uncertainty on the absolute polarization
 987 accumulates along the ladder. With each rung verified to keep, for instance, ∆P ≤ 0.01 and
 988 estimated by its midpoint, the per-rung errors are independent and add in quadrature, so that
 √
 989 after n rungs the accumulated uncertainty is σn ≈ (∆P/2) n. Reaching extraction at T ≈ 2.5 GeV

 996 The quadrature scaling relies on the per-rung brackets being free of a common bias. This holds

1000 Repeating the ladder with deliberately irregular step sizes slices the same fixed resonances into
1001 different rungs, averaging down any residual bias and providing a direct diagnostic at the energies
1002 where the partitions disagree. This calibration by excursion has been demonstrated at the IUCF
1003 Cooler Ring [39], where it was used to export the polarization scale for pp elastic analyzing-power

1036 7 Conclusion

1037 A recoil-carbon polarimeter concept has been developed for the BNL Booster synchrotron to measure
1038 the transverse polarization of both the polarized proton beam and the polarized 3 He ion beam
1039 throughout the acceleration cycle. The polarimeter consists of coaxial recoil and forward silicon
1040 detector rings with six shared azimuthal stations, as summarized in Table 6. The detector geometry
1041 provides continuous kinematic coverage for proton and 3 He polarimetry over the Booster ramp.
1042 The design is staged for commissioning: Stage 1 instruments only the recoil ring, which is sufficient
1043 for pC polarimetry with event identification based on the recoil kinematics alone; Stage 2 adds the
1044 forward ring, enabling 3 He C polarimetry through the forward-coincidence condition together with
1045 ∆E identification, which suppresses both 3 He breakup backgrounds and inelastic 12 C excitation.
1046 The two stages are mechanically independent and can be commissioned sequentially, allowing proton
1047 polarimetry to proceed while the forward ring is being prepared.
1048 For pC elastic scattering, analyzing-power and differential-cross-section data from multiple exper-
1049 iments across the Booster energy range establish a well-characterized figure of merit in the recoil
1050 acceptance. The corresponding acceptance-averaged quantities and dwell times are summarized
1051 in Table 13 and Fig. 11. Over the representative proton energies considered here, the dwell time
1052 required to reach an absolute statistical precision δP = 1 × 10−2 remains within or comparable to
1053 the 75 ms ramp time. Proton polarimetry in the Booster is therefore statistically feasible within a
1054 single ramp cycle over essentially the full relevant energy range, so that the total uncertainty budget
1055 is expected to be dominated by systematic effects from the outset.
1056 For 3 He C elastic scattering, the sole experimental anchor in the Booster energy range is the
1057 Kamiya et al. measurement at 443 MeV [10]. The corresponding acceptance-averaged quantities
1058 and dwell time are summarized in Table 13 and Fig. 11. In contrast to the proton case, the dwell

 37

1059 time required to reach an absolute statistical precision δP = 1 × 10−2 greatly exceeds the 75 ms
1060 ramp time, so 3 He polarimetry necessarily requires accumulation over many ramp cycles.
1061 As the 3 He beam energy increases along the ramp, the forward-angle structure of the 3 He C
1062 elastic-scattering observables shifts to smaller laboratory angles. At the 443 MeV Kamiya anchor
1063 energy, the present forward ring samples the useful forward-angle region relevant for polarimetry.
1064 At higher 3 He energies, however, the most favorable angular region moves closer to the beam. The
1065 chamber design already includes additional flanges that could support an extension into this region,
1066 so the basic mechanical provision is in place. The present 98 × 98 mm2 pad detectors cannot be
1067 used for this purpose, since their azimuthal extent would cause adjacent stations to overlap if the
1068 inner active edge were moved substantially inward. An extension toward smaller forward angles
1069 would therefore require only the corresponding detector instrumentation with smaller sensors and
1070 an optimized layout.
1071 The chamber design includes blank flanges at z = 400 mm at the azimuthal positions of the
1072 existing forward stations, providing a mechanical provision for future deuteron polarimetry without
1073 affecting current operation. At the maximum Booster rigidity Bρ = 7.507 T m the elastic dC
1074 kinematic locus maps the recoil acceptance θC = 69◦ –82◦ to forward deuteron angles θd = 14◦ –34◦ ,
1075 a range entirely above the existing 3 He forward ring acceptance and covered by populating these
1076 flanges with silicon detectors. With six azimuthal stations, both the cos ϕ and cos 2ϕ modulations
1077 of the dC yield are resolved, enabling simultaneous measurement of vector and tensor polarization
1078 should a deuteron program at EIC be pursued in the future.
1079 The Booster polarimeter provides an operationally independent polarimetry capability at a time
1080 when the AGS is undergoing an extended refurbishment, including a major recabling effort. With
1081 the AGS pC polarimeter unavailable during this period, the Booster instrument allows polarimetry
1082 development, Ay calibration campaigns, and spin-resonance studies to proceed for both the proton
1083 and 3 He programs. More generally, the continuous polarization monitoring it provides throughout
1084 the Booster ramp is directly relevant to optimizing polarization transmission into the AGS and,
1085 ultimately, into the EIC hadron storage ring.
