Compliance with preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis in cesarean delivery and its impact on surgical site infections: A national retrospective study of Jordan
Authors: Suhaib Muflih, Marah Hamadneh, Ahmad Alrawashdeh, Sawsan Abuhammad, Mohammad Abu Assab
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSurgical site infections (SSIs) are significant complications following cesarean delivery, and preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis (PAP) is crucial for prevention. Limited data exist on PAP compliance in low- to middle-income countries such as Jordan. This study aimed to assess the compliance with PAP guidelines in women undergoing cesarean delivery in Jordan and evaluate its impact on the incidence of SSIs.MethodsA retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted using electronic health records from 60,212 women who underwent cesarean delivery between 2015 and 2022 in 20 Ministry of Health hospitals in Jordan. PAP compliance was defined as the administration of cefazolin (1–2 g) within one hour before surgery for patients without penicillin allergy, or appropriate alternatives for those with an allergy, following USAID and MOH protocols. Multilevel logistic regression models with hospital code as a random effect were used to identify factors associated with PAP compliance and SSIs.ResultsOnly 19.4% of patients received PAP as recommended. The monthly rate of women who received antibiotics increased slightly over time by an average of almost 1.0% per month (95% CI: 0.07–0.09; p < 0.001). Factors positively associated with receiving PAP included age, undergoing elective cesarean delivery (adjusted odds ratio [aOR]: 2.60; 95% CI: 2.43–2.79; p < 0.001), and longer time between admission and surgery (aOR per hour: 1.02; 95% CI: 1.02–1.02; p < 0.001). Patients who received PAP had 41% lower odds of readmission due to SSIs (aOR: 0.59; 95% CI: 0.39–0.89; p = 0.012). The monthly rate of SSIs decreased by approximately 1.6% over the study period (incidence rate ratio: 0.984; 95% CI: 0.979–0.989; p < 0.001).ConclusionPAP compliance in women undergoing cesarean delivery in Jordan is suboptimal, with less than one-fifth receiving prophylaxis per guidelines. Compliance with PAP is significantly associated with reduced SSIs. Interventions to improve PAP compliance, such as enhancing guideline awareness, implementing antibiotic stewardship programs, and improving preoperative planning, are urgently needed to enhance patient outcomes and reduce postoperative complications.Keywords: Caesarean delivery, Surgical site infection, Preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis, Compliance, Incidence rate, Risk factors, Jordan

Introduction

Cesarean delivery is one of the most common surgical procedures worldwide, and its rates have significantly risen over the last decades for both emergency and elective deliveries.1, 2 The world's rates of cesarean delivery have increased from approximately 12% in the year 2000 to more than 21% in 2015. A higher incidence has been recorded for areas such as North America and South America.3 In Jordan, the rate of cesarean delivery has increased since 1982, reaching approximately 31% during 2015–2017.4 While cesarean delivery may be lifesaving when medically indicated for both mother and child, the increasing number of cesarean deliveries without clear clinical justification raises concerns about risks and complications.5

Cesarean delivery involves risks in the immediate, short, and long run concerning maternal and neonatal health, particularly in areas with limited access to obstetric care.6 Common complications are postoperative hematoma, seroma, wound dehiscence, and SSIs.7, 8 SSIs, as defined by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), are an incision site infection occurring within 30 days post-surgery.9 They are a major factor in morbidity and mortality, with rates ranging from 3% to 15% and as high as 25% in some studies. 10, 11

In developing countries, SSIs are among the most common nosocomial infections in obstetrics and gynecology wards. 12, 13SSIs increase hospital length of stay, health expenses, and economic burden.14 Proper implementation of preoperative antibiotic prophylaxis (PAP) is a well-established evidence-based strategy for SSI prevention.15 PAP administered before surgical incision can reduce intraoperative microbial contamination of a surgical wound when an effective antimicrobial agent is used.16 However, inappropriate antibiotic timing, dosage, and duration of antibiotics may fail to prevent SSIs and cause the emergence of antibiotic-resistant organisms, posing a significant threat to public health. 17, 18, 19

Healthcare providers' poor compliance with PAP guidelines is a worldwide issue, as several studies have reported low compliance with recommended guidelines. 20, 21 Consistent with previous findings, poor compliance with surgical antimicrobial prophylaxis has also been reported in Jordan, highlighting an important gap in evidence-based practice. 17 Although national data on cesarean delivery trends in Jordan are limited, the global rise in cesarean delivery highlights the need to assess compliance with CS protocols within local maternal health settings, including Jordan. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the extent of compliance with PAP standards in women undergoing cesarean deliveries in public hospitals across Jordan, to identify factors associated with compliance. Based on national data on SSIs following cesarean deliveries in Jordan, this study also examined how effectively PAP compliance translated into a lower infection rate. Understanding how compliance with PAP guidelines affects SSIs may support strategies to enhance PAP compliance, reduce the local rates of SSI, and improve maternal health outcomes.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective cross-sectional study used routinely collected electronic health records to evaluate compliance with PAP guidelines among women who underwent cesarean delivery in Jordan. The study included records from 20 Ministry of Health hospitals participating in the Electronic Healthcare Solutions (EHS) program through the Hakeem Medical Information System between January 2015 and December 2022. The EHS system standardizes patient-level clinical, surgical, medication, and outcome data across participating public hospitals. The analysis was designed to estimate the proportion of eligible cesarean deliveries that met PAP criteria, describe temporal trends in antibiotic use and SSI-related readmission, and identify patient-, procedure-, medication-, and hospital-level factors associated with PAP compliance and SSI-related readmission.

Study sample and setting

The study included all women who underwent CS, whether it was an elective or an emergency, at the participating ministry of health (MOH) hospitals between January 2015 and December 2022. Inclusion criteria consisted of patients regardless of age, comorbidities, penicillin allergy status, or the presence of any pre-existing infection. The only exclusion criteria included cases outside this period and those with missing medication data in the EHS system.

A total of 105,312 patients underwent cesarean delivery during the study period. After excluding incomplete medication records from a total sample, the final analytic sample included 60,212 women. The data were obtained from the Obstetrics and gynecology departments at participating MOH hospitals. Cesarean delivery is an obstetrics and Gynecology service provided through specialized or tertiary hospitals. The MOH has updated the protocol of PAP for cesarean delivery by collaborating with the United States Agency for International Development (USAID).22 According to this protocol, all women undergoing cesarean delivery who do not have β-lactam allergy (i.e., penicillin, cephalosporin) must receive intravenous cefazolin 60 min prior to the skin incision, at a dose of 1 g for women weighing less than 80 kg and 2 g for those weighing > = 80 kg. Women with beta-lactam allergy should receive a dose of either Clindamycin 600 mg IV or Vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV plus Gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV should be provided an hour before the surgery. If intraoperative blood loss exceeds 1500 mL or the procedure lasts more than 3 h, a second dosage of antibiotic should be administered 3–4 h following the first dose. The hospitals included in this study participated in data sharing with EHS on different dates during the study period, anonymized hospital codes and patient numbers are provided in Supplementary Appendix Table A1.

Data collection

First, all eligible cesarean delivery records were extracted from the EHS for all women who underwent cesarean delivery during the study period. Extracted variables included age, comorbidities, surgical characteristics, hospital code, medication administration records, and post-discharge outcomes. Comorbidities included diabetes mellitus, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disorders, neurologic disorders, hematologic disorders, endocrine disorders, renal disease, gastrointestinal conditions, immunologic disorders, musculoskeletal disorders, reproductive health conditions, and oncologic conditions. Second, surgical variables were classified according to cesarean delivery type (elective or emergency), surgery timing (weekday or weekend; daytime [6 AM−6 PM] or nighttime [6 PM−6 AM]), time from hospital admission to cesarean delivery, and anonymized hospital code.

Third, medication records were reviewed to identify PAP and other medications administered during hospitalization. PAP compliance was defined as receipt of the recommended antibiotic, dose, and timing according to the USAID/MOH protocol: cefazolin 1–2 g within one hour before surgery for patients without penicillin allergy, or clindamycin 600 mg IV or vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV plus gentamicin 1.5 mg/kg IV for patients with allergy. Other antibiotics were classified as cephalosporins excluding PAP doses, penicillin, metronidazole, or other antibiotics. Additional medication categories included analgesics, anticoagulants, NSAIDs, antihypertensive or antiarrhythmic agents, steroids, diabetes medications, diuretics, opioids, and uterotonic agents.

Fourth, outcomes were identified from EHS records. SSI-related readmission was defined as readmission within 30 days after cesarean delivery with an SSI diagnosis, consistent with the CDC definition of infection at the incision site within 30 days after surgery. SSIs included superficial incisional, deep incisional, and organ/space infections and were identified using documented clinical or microbiologic evidence, including purulent drainage, positive microbiologic testing, deliberately opened incision, abscess formation, or localized signs such as pain, redness, and swelling.

Study outcome measures

The primary outcome was compliance with PAP guidelines, defined as administration of the recommended antibiotic at the appropriate dose and within the recommended preoperative timing window. Secondary outcomes were 30-day all-cause readmission after discharge, 30-day SSI-related readmission, time from discharge to readmission, and 30-day post-cesarean mortality.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were conducted using Stata version 16 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA), with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. Continuous variables were summarized using means with standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges, and categorical variables using frequencies and percentages. Group comparisons by PAP status used chi-square tests for categorical variables, independent t-tests for normally distributed continuous variables, and Mann-Whitney U tests for non-normally distributed continuous variables. Monthly trends in antibiotic administration, PAP compliance, all-cause readmission, and SSI-related readmission were evaluated using Poisson regression and reported as incidence rate ratios (IRRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Univariable and multivariable multilevel logistic regression models were used to identify factors associated with PAP compliance and SSI-related readmission, with hospital code included as a random effect to account for clustering. Multivariable models adjusted for age group, comorbidities, cesarean delivery type, surgery timing, time from admission to surgery, calendar month, and medications received during hospitalization. Results are reported as odds ratios (ORs) or adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% CIs. Model fit was assessed using likelihood ratio tests, and multicollinearity was examined using variance inflation factors.

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study sample based on PAP status

A total of 105,312 women underwent cesarean delivery between 2015 and 2022 across 20 MOH hospitals. After excluding 45,100 records due to lack of medication data, 60,212 were included in this study. The mean age was 29.3 years (SD=6.3), and 53.3% of women were younger than 30 years. At least one comorbidity was recorded for 15.9% of women; the most common medical conditions recorded were hematologic disorders (2.9%), diabetes mellitus (2.7%), gastrointestinal conditions (2.4%), and musculoskeletal disorders (2.4%). Elective cesarean deliveries accounted for 52.9% of procedures, 15.5% occurred on weekends, and 19.3% occurred at night. The median time from admission to cesarean delivery was 21 h.

Overall, 52.3% of women received antibiotics other than PAP. The most frequently recorded medication categories were opioids (52.3%), cephalosporins excluding PAP doses (46.5%), uterotonics (45.5%), metronidazole (41.3%), analgesics (41.3%), and anticoagulants (20.4%). Thirty-day all-cause readmission occurred in 1.73% of women (n = 1042), and 0.42% (n = 254) were readmitted due to SSIs. Overall, 11,658 women (19.4%) received PAP as recommended, whereas 48,554 (80.6%) did not. Among the 11,658 women who received PAP, 6476 (55.6%) received prophylaxis for ≤ 24 h and 5182 (44.4%) received prophylactic antibiotics for > 24 h after cesarean delivery. Further, weekdays cesarean deliveries were more likely to receive PAP compared to the weekend cesarean delivered [OR = 1.56, 95% CI: 1.47–1.65, p < 0.001], as well as daytime cesarean deliveries compared to nighttime cesarean deliveries [OR = 2.35, 95% CI: 2.20–2.51, p < 0.001]. The PAP group had fewer 30-day all-cause readmissions than the non-PAP group [OR = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.70–0.98, P = 0.015] and fewer SSI-related readmissions [OR = 0.60, 95% CI: 0.42–0.8, p = 0.024]. No significant difference in 30-day mortality was observed between groups. Baseline characteristics by PAP status are shown in Table 1.

Factors associated with PAP compliance

Table 2 presents the univariable and multivariable multilevel logistic regression models for factors associated with receiving PAP. The multicollinearity of the multiple logistic model showed variance inflation factor values ranging from 1 to 2.3 for the majority of variables. The likelihood ratio test for the model fit after removing non-significant variables was non-significant (LR χ²(4)= 2,53, p = 0.6.296). Based on the variables identified in the univariable analysis, the multivariable model showed that women aged between 30 and 34 years had 12% higher odds of receiving PAP compared to those aged less than 25 years (aOR: 1.12; 95% CI: 1.04–1.20; p = 0.003). Patients who underwent elective cesarean delivery were more than twice as likely to receive PAP compared to those who underwent emergency cesarean delivery (aOR: 2.60; 95% CI: 2.43–2.79; p < 0.001). Further hospitals with lower delivery volumes demonstrated more compliance with PAP (aOR: 1.039; 95% CI: 1.032–1.048; p < 0.001).

The findings showed that undergoing cesarean delivery during the weekend and nighttime was associated with lower odds of receiving PAP by 13% (aOR: 0.87; 95% CI: 0.80–0.94; p = 0.001) and 25% (aOR: 0.75; 95% CI: 0.69–0.82; p < 0.001), respectively. Patients with cardiovascular disease had 19% lower odds of receiving PAP compared to those without cardiovascular disease (aOR: 0.81; 95% CI: 0.66–0.99; p = 0.049). Similarly, patients who received any antibiotics other than PAP during hospitalization had 48% lower odds of receiving PAP (aOR: 0.52; 95% CI: 0.44–0.63; p < 0.001). Specific medications associated with lower odds of receiving PAP included cephalosporins (aOR: 0.14; 95% CI: 0.11–0.17; p < 0.001), penicillins (aOR: 0.87; 95% CI: 0.77–0.99; p = 0.034), antihypertensives/antiarrhythmics (aOR: 0.85; 95% CI: 0.74–0.97; p = 0.020), and diuretics (aOR: 0.73; 95% CI: 0.52–1.03; p = 0.071). Fig. 2 depicts the monthly rate of patients who received any antibiotics (excluding PAP) and those who received PAP over the study period. The monthly rate of patients who received antibiotic increased slightly over time by almost 1.0% per month (IRR:1.008; 95% CI 1.007–1.009; p < 0.001). Among the study sample, only 11,658 (19.4%) patients receive PAP per the USAID protocol. The monthly compliance rate with PAP fluctuated around 20% and reduced by an average of 0.4% each month (IRR: 0.996; 95% CI 0.995–0.997; p < 0.001).

Univariable and multivariable mixed-effects logistic regression models for factors associated with SSI-related readmission (N = 60,212).

In the multivariable model, adherence to PAP was the only variable associated with lower odds of readmission due to SSI. Patients who received PAP as recommended had a 41% lower likelihood of readmission due to SSI (aOR: 0.59; 95% CI: 0.39–0.89; p = 0.012). The findings also showed that women with diabetic had nearly twice the probability of being readmitted for an SSI compared to those without a history of diabetes mellitus (aOR: 1.98; 95% CI: 1.13–3.46; p = 0.016). Patients who underwent emergency cesarean delivery had more than double the odds of being readmitted for an SSI compared to those who underwent elective cesarean delivery (aOR: 2.32; 95% CI: 1.71–3.16; p < 0.001).

Discussion

This national retrospective study addresses an important evidence gap on PAP compliance in cesarean delivery within Jordan's public health system, which constitutes a barrier towards acting effectively in an effort to enhance patients' outcomes. The main findings were that PAP compliance was low, prolonged postoperative prophylactic antibiotic use was common among women who received PAP, and PAP compliance was associated with lower odds of SSI-related readmission. These findings support the clinical relevance of timely, guideline-concordant prophylaxis and indicate that antibiotic stewardship and perioperative workflow improvement are needed in maternal health services.

Only 19.4% of women received PAP as recommended. This rate is similar to reported antimicrobial prophylaxis compliance in Saudi Arabia (19.5%),23 higher than other low- to middle-income countries, such as Ghana (2.5%),24 and lower than some hospitals in India (52%).25 Compared to high-income countries, PAP compliance in Jordan is still suboptimal, with studies reporting 26% compliance in Slovenia 26 and up to 58% in Greece following educational interventions.27 Differences across settings surgical settings may reflect variation in stewardship infrastructure, guideline dissemination, staff training, electronic prescribing support, and audit-and-feedback systems. The finding that 44.4% of women who received PAP continued prophylactic antibiotics beyond 24 h also indicates that deviations from guideline-concordant prophylaxis involve both underuse before incision and prolonged use after surgery, a pattern also described in other low- and middle-income settings. 28, 29, 30, 31 These findings may reflect physicians’ perceived risk of infection, as well as the lack of perceived risk of prolonged antibiotic prophylaxis.17, 21, 32

Procedure-related and workflow factors appeared important. Elective cesarean delivery, longer time between admission and surgery, and lower number of cesareans deliver per hospital were associated with higher odds of PAP compliance, whereas emergency, weekend, and nighttime surgeries and higher cesarean volume were associated with lower odds. These patterns are consistent with the possibility that scheduled procedures allow more time for preoperative preparation and may reduce psychological stress son healthcare providers, whereas urgent procedures and after-hours care may disrupt standard workflows. Previous studies have linked weekend or nighttime surgery with challenges related to staffing, fatigue, teamwork, and access to multidisciplinary support.33, 34 These findings suggest that interventions should focus not only on clinical knowledge but also on reliable systems for antibiotic ordering, pharmacy availability, checklist use, and communication during emergency and off-hour procedures.

PAP compliance was associated with a 41% reduction in the odds of SSI-related readmission. This finding is consistent with the established role of appropriately timed prophylactic antibiotics in reducing postoperative infections.35, 36 Interestingly, although the overall compliance with PAP declined over time, a concurrent reduction in SSIs was also observed over time. This discrepancy may reflect the influence of multiple factors beyond the compliance with PAP on SSI prevention, including wound care, sterilization, surgical technique, patient education, and changes in surveillance or readmission practices patterns.37 Prolonged or non-guideline antibiotic use may also have affected infection outcomes, but such practice is not recommended because it lacks clear additional benefit and may increase antimicrobial resistance risk.21, 38

The cumulative SSI-related readmission incidence was 0.42%, which is lower than rates reported in several low- and middle-income countries.36 This estimate should be interpreted as SSI-related readmission observed rather than all SSIs, because infections managed in outpatient settings or at other facilities may not have been captured. Diabetes mellitus and emergency cesarean delivery were independently associated with higher odds of SSI-related readmission, which is consistent with impaired wound healing among patients with diabetes and the challenges of infection prevention during emergency procedures.39, 40

These results have practical implications for policy and practice. Improving PAP compliance may require multidisciplinary strategies, including standardized cesarean delivery order sets, pre-incision antibiotic prompts, perioperative safety checklists, audit-and-feedback cycles, pharmacist-led stewardship, and targeted training for emergency, weekend, and nighttime care teams.41 Such strategies may reduce unnecessary antibiotic exposure, support antimicrobial resistance mitigation, lower SSI-related readings, and improve maternal outcomes. 30, 31, 42, 43, 44

Limitations

There are several limitations to this study. First, being a retrospective observational study, it carries biases in the accuracy and completeness of the data. The exclusion of 45,100 patients because of missing medication data could introduce selection bias that would affect the representativeness of the sample. Second, reliance on electronic health records will result in misclassification or underreporting of variables like comorbidities, medication administration, and SSIs, especially if patients were treated at various facilities post-discharge. Third, we could not control several potential confounders, such as body mass index, smoking status, nutritional status, or even more detailed surgical factors, like the duration of surgery or intraoperative complications. These can affect not only the likelihood of receipt of PAP but also the risk of SSIs. Finally, the fact that PAP compliance decreases over time suggests there are systemic issues or perhaps an overall shift in practice patterns that might not have been captured in our analysis. This further limits our understanding of such low adherence rates.

Conclusions

This large national retrospective study among 60,212 women undergoing cesarean deliveries across 20 hospitals in Jordan from the year 2015–2022 showed low compliance with PAP guidelines. Only 19.4% received PAP as recommended, and compliance with PAP was associated with a 41% reduction in SSI-related readmission. These findings highlight the need to strengthen guideline awareness, effective antibiotic stewardship, preoperative planning, and standardized preoperative workflows, and systemic changes within the healthcare infrastructure to foster compliance with PAP guidelines, particularly for emergency, weekend, and nighttime cesarean deliveries, as a means of mitigating postoperative complications and combating antibiotic resistance.
