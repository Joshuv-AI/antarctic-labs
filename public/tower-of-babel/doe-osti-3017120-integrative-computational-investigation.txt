I. Executive Summary
The major goal of this research project was to advance theoretical and computational capability
to describe the dynamics and the spectroscopy of molecular excitons as reliably and efficiently as
possible. Outcomes of the project contributed to new understanding of the interaction between
light and molecular materials and ensuing dynamics of excited electronic states. These can as-
sist experimentalists in developing novel methods to utilize quantum properties of molecules and
molecular excitons for new energy harvesting and quantum information processing.

II. Summary of Project Outcomes
As direct outcomes, the project resulted in 11 completed works, 1–11 one work under revision, 12 and
three works 13–15 that continue being developed.
IIA. Completed Works
Ten outcomes 1–10 were published, and one outcome was submitted 11 for review. Summaries of
these works and their implications are provided below.

  1. A simple generalization of the energy gap law for nonradiative processes: 1 The energy gap
 (EG) law is a seminal theory first developed by Englman and Jortner, 16 and has for decades
 been playing a central role for explaining and modeling nonradiative decay rates of electronic
 excited states in a wide range of molecular systems. As the first outcome 1 of the project,
 the PI reviewed assumptions involved in the original EG theory, 16 clarified their implications
 in the context of modern quantum rate theories, and derived a new generalized EG (GEG)
 law. This generalization accounted for contributions of low frequency vibrational modes that
 were missing in the original EG law, 16 and demonstrated that near exponential dependence
 of the nonradiative decay rate on the value of the energy gap can still be observed even in
 the presence of such additional low frequency vibrational modes.
 Figure 1 provides representative model calculations that compare EG and GEG theories with
 exact numerical evaluation. These show that the GEG reproduce the mean behavior of the
 exact result quantitatively well even for the limit of weak vibronic coupling (panel a), where
 both EG and GEG are not expected to work well. On the other hand, the results of GEG
 agree very well with the exact result for moderate vibronic coupling limit (panel b). On the
 other hand, the results of the EG law systematically underestimate the exact results in both
 cases. These calculations show that the near exponential dependence of the nonradiative
 decay is more universal than the assumption implicit in the original EG law. At the same
 time, they also demonstrate that naive use of the original EG law 16 for quantitative model-

 ing of experimental data can incur significant overestimation of the nonadiabatic electronic
 coupling responsible for the nonradiative behavior. The new GEG law 1 improves upon this
 issue and helps experimentalists understand and model trends of the nonradiative decay of
 various excited state molecules more accurately.

2. Excitons: Energetics and spatiotemporal dynamics: 2 This is an editorial article for a Special
 Topic issue of the Journal of Chemical Physics of the same title, which the PI co-organized
 with three colleagues. This work provides perspectives and synopses of 41 papers published
 for the Special Topic, which encompassed advances in experimental tools to characterize exci-
 tons, first principles electronic structure calculations of excitons, theoretical studies of exciton
 transfer rates and spectral lineshapes, computational modeling of the exciton spectroscopy,
 quantum dynamics of excitons, and nanostructured systems of molecular excitons. Thus, this
 outcome offers a succinct summary of the current status of these key areas of research on
 molecular excitons, and clarifies important key theoretical and experimental challenges that
 need to be addressed for better understanding and control of molecular excitons.

3. Partially polaron-transformed quantum master equation for exciton and charge transport dy-
 namics: 3 This work is the most advanced development in our series of works 17–21 on polaron
 transformed quantum master equation (PQME) approaches for exciton dynamics. Earlier
 PQMEs were shown to be effective and quantitatively reliable for describing both coherent
 and incoherent exciton dynamics across a broad range of exciton-environment couplings, and
 have served as some of the most efficient quantum dynamics methods for non-Markovian open

 by introducing a general weighting function that allows partial polaron transformation in
 a flexible manner. This work 3 provided complete formal derivations of general expressions

4. Modified Fermi’s golden rule rate expressions: 4 Fermi’s golden rule (FGR) offers one of the
 most important theoretical frameworks for understanding the spectroscopy and quantum
 dynamical processes such as energy and electron transfer processes. The utility of FGR has
 been demonstrated through decades of experimental confirmation. However, there still remain
 important cases where the evaluation of a FGR rate is ambiguous or ill-defined. Examples
 are cases where the rate has divergent terms due to the sparsity in the density of final states
 or time dependent fluctuations of system Hamiltonians. This work 4 clarified these issues
 and derived new modified FGR rate expressions that can still be defined as effective rates.
 These new rate expressions enable quantitative characterization of energy and charge transfer
 processes for more broad range of systems than the original FGR.

6. Simple and general unitarity conserving numerical real time propagators of time dependent
 Schrödinger equation based on Magnus expansion: 6 There are many well established numerical
 time evolution methods for quantum dynamics, but simple and reliable ones preserving all
 the important properties of quantum mechanics for general time dependent Hamiltonian
 are somewhat lacking. The Magnus expansion offers a superior formal framework in this

 3

 respect because it naturally satisfies the unitarity of quantum time evolution for general
 Hamiltonians even though it is truncated at finite order. However, despite the fact that
 it was developed more than 70 years ago, it has been relatively underutilized as quantum
 dynamics methods in the chemical physics community because of some formal and practical
 difficulties associated with its implementation. These issues were addressed by this work 6
 through systematic investigation of the Magnus expansion and obtaining a series of new
 high order approximations that are straightforward to calculate. In particular, a simple
 fourth order expression was identified, which involves only one mid-point evaluation of the
 Hamiltonian in addition to end points. Outcomes of this work will serve as important tools
 for quantum dynamics calculations for time dependent Hamiltonian, which include quantum
 control of excitons by time dependent fields and real-time ab initio quantum dynamics in
 general.

7. Dynamic embedding of effective harmonic normal mode vibrations in all-atomistic energy gap
 fluctuations: case study of light harvesting 2 complex: 7 At present, most theories of quantum
 dynamics for studying molecular excitons in complex environments represent the environ-
 mental effects by linearly coupled baths of harmonic oscillators. Let alone the fundamental
 issue of understanding how such models are accurate in general, how to construct the most
 effective and reliable model of such kind is not well established. In this work 7 led by Young
 Min Rhee’s group at Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), where
 the PI worked during sabbatical leave and collaborated on both method development and
 analysis of results, we developed a method to extract bath spectral densities representing an
 effective bath of harmonic oscillator and residual anharmonic degrees of freedom. To this end,
 we employed the energy gap correlation function between the excited and ground states from
 ab initio molecular dynamics simulation. While this method was demonstrated specifically
 for a light harvesting 2 (LH2) complex of purple bacteria, it is broadly applicable to various
 molecular exciton systems and therefore will serve as an important method for constructing
 more reliable coarse-grained models for quantum dynamics of molecular excitons in complex
 environments.

8. Coherence in chemistry: foundations and frontiers: 8 Since the development of quantum me-
 chanics, there have been a huge body of theoretical and experimental works investigating and
 addressing the phenomena and effects of coherence in chemistry. In particular, the recognition
 of the importance of quantum coherence in molecular excitons for the past decades resulted
 in many exciting new discoveries and as yet unsettled issues. This comprehensive review 8 led
 by David Beratan’s group at Duke University is a timely and an important contribution in
 this respect. The PI’s contribution to this work includes review and clarification of molecular
 exctions, open system quantum dynamics methods, and some path integral calculation meth-
 ods, which help broader community understand the current status of research on quantum
 dynamics of molecular excitons.

10. A simple fourth order Magnus expansion propagator based on the Magnus expansion in the
 Liouville space: Application to a Λ-system and assessment of the rotating wave approxima-
 tion: 10 In this work, we extended the simplest fourth order propagator based on the Magnus
 expansion we have derived previously 6 to the Liouville space for the Lindbald dynamics. In
 addition to straightforward expressions with commutators, we also derived new commutator-
 free expressions that are more accurate and widely applicable. We applied our forth order
 expression with commutator to a Λ-system subject to time dependent control and probe fields,
 with parameters in the regime where electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) and co-
 herent population trapping (CPT) are observed. We also assessed the accuracy of the rotating
 wave approximation (RWA), an approximation widely used for matter-radiation interaction.
 Our assessment confirms the accuracy of RWA in the conventional weak matter-radiation
 interaction regime, but shows existence of subtle errors in an intermediate system-bath cou-
 pling regime where conventional EIT can be observed, and qualitatively incorrect predictions
 of RWA in the strong control field regime where important CPT processes have been predicted
 based on the RWA. Numerical results of this work 10 thus provide new insights into effects of
 strong control field. New methods of quantum dynamics developed in this work 10 will play
 important roles for conducting quantum dynamics calculations for the control of excitons by
 time dependent fields.

11. Minimization of disorder as a key design principle for natural sizes of light harvesting 2 com-
 plexes: 11 This is a culmination of long and steady investigations by the PI on LH2 complex of
 purple bacteria, and is a more complete version of a previous work 23 in that more satisfactory
 first principles level calculations were employed. LH2 complex of purple bacteria is a repre-
 sentative molecular exciton system engineered by nature, and has excellent energy conversion

 5

 efficiency. Clarifying the design principle behind such efficiency is important for understand-
 ing its structure-function relationship, and also has practical implications for potential design
 of artificial light harvesting systems. To this end, we conducted comprehensive computational
 investigation of the dynamical and statistical nature of electronic excited states of pigment
 molecules in a natural LH2 complex with 9-fold symmetry and its two non-natural analogues
 with 6- and 12-fold symmetries (see Fig. 4). To ensure reliable and efficient all-atomistic
 molecular dynamics simulations, we combined a well established interpolation approach for
 the construction of the potential energy surface with a neural network machine learning ap-
 proach.
 Outcomes of these calculations clarified that non-natural forms of LH2-type complexes have
 significantly larger quasistatic disorder than those for the natural one. In addition, non-
 natural systems have more disruptions of the hydrogen bonding, underscoring its crucial
 role for reducing the disorder. On the other hand, local environmental dynamics are rela-
 tively insensitive to the structural changes although there is moderate enhancement in the
 anharmonic or interatomic components for the synthetic ones. These findings provide direct
 computational evidence that the structure and sizes of natural LH2 complexes are designed
 to minimize the energetic disorder, and in turn to facilitate robust exciton dynamics in and
 across LH2 complexes. Methods used for this work are applicable to other types of natu-
 ral and synthetic molecular exciton systems. Lessons learned from this work also offer key
 insights into the successful functionality of the natural light harvesting complex.

IIB. Works in progress
There is one work that requires significant revision 12 and three works 13–15 that require additional
efforts for completion.

  1. Computational investigation of excitons in minimal aggregates of dimethylated diketopyrrolopy-
 rroles: 12 In this work, we conducted extensive computational study of dimers and tetramers
 of dimethylated diketopyrrolopyrroles (mDPPs), the aggregates of which exhibited interest-
 ing singlet fission properties according to results from Adam Braunschweig’s group at the
 Advanced Science Research Center of the City University of New York. Figure 5 provides
 transition densities of electronic excitations for two types of dimers, which were extracted from
 the X-ray crystallography data. Through detailed analyses of electronic structure calculation
 data and modeling of spectral lineshapes, we have uncovered that the conventional labeling
 of H and J aggregates based on structural features do not apply. On the other hand, we
 identified many features that make the nature of excitons in these systems more complicated

 6

  than those expected from a simple Frenkel exciton theory. A manuscript reporting this work
  was submitted to the Journal of Chemical Physics in March 2022, for which we have received
  valuable feedback from reviewers. We have been working on revision of this manuscript and
  plan to resubmit it in the near future.

3. Theoretical investigation of the dynamics and spectroscopy of excitons in oligofluorene-C60
 complex: 14 We conducted electronic structure calculation of excitons formed in dyads of
 oligofluorene and fullerene with different lengths of oligofluorenes, and estimated exciton

 7

 transfer rates from oligofluorene units to fullerene. This was to understand the experimen-
 tal observation of fast exciton transfer by the John Miller group at Brookhaven National
 Laboratory, 24 which reported experimental rates much faster than the prediction of Förster
 theory especially for short oligofluoene units. Our results can explain some of experimental
 observations, but there remain some key differences between experimental results and our
 best theoretical estimates that need to be clarified.

  4. Computational study of the effects of the structural features and arrangements on multichro-
 mophoric exciton energy transfer: 15 In this work, we calculated multichromophoric Förster
 resonance energy transfer (MC-FRET) rates 25 between linear and circular aggregates of dif-
 ferent sizes. Results of these calculations offer new insights into the relationship between
 structural features and spatial arrangement of multichromophoric systems and their capabil-
 ity to transfer excitons. Outcomes of this work will help identify design principles for efficient
 light harvesting.

III. Summary and Implications of Research Project Outcomes
The outcomes of the research project described above encompass new theory developments, ad-
vances in computations methods, and new applications to systems ranging from organic dye
molecules to biological complexes where intricate quantum effects play significant roles. The sup-
port contributed to the PI’s summer and sabbatical research, training of two PhD students and
two postdoctoral researchers in the PI’s group, and research presentations in 13 invited work-
shops/conferences and 19 invited seminars. Some of the research outcomes were completed in
conjunction with the support of the National Science Foundation (NSF) project to the PI, which
was focused on different objectives and involves efforts complementary to the present project. Over-
all, these research outcomes have made unique contributions to theoretical understanding and more
reliable computational studies of molecular excitons and their control mechanisms.
