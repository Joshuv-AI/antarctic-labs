Right groups and the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation
A. Albano, A. Facchini, M. Mazzotta, P. Stefanelli

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide techniques to obtain left non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang–Baxter equation, drawing on the class of right groups. To this end, we introduce the new algebraic structures of left R ​ G RG -semibraces, which include left (cancellative) semibraces as a proper subclass.

Introduction

The
Yang–Baxter equation
was first introduced by Yang
[
22
]
in Theoretical Physics and, independently, by Baxter
[
3
]
in Statistical Mechanics. Since then, the search for its solutions has drawn the attention of many researchers and remains an open problem. Due to the complexity of known solutions, Drinfel’d
[
13
]
suggested focusing on a simplified subclass. Namely, if
X
is a set, a map

r:X\times X\to X\times X
is a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang–Baxter equation
, briefly a
solution
, if the identity

(YBE)

\displaystyle\left(r\times\operatorname{id}_{X}\right)\left(\operatorname{id}_{X}\times\,r\right)\left(r\times\operatorname{id}_{X}\right)=\left(\operatorname{id}_{X}\times\,r\right)\left(r\times\operatorname{id}_{X}\right)\left(\operatorname{id}_{X}\times\,r\right)

is satisfied. We will write
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\lambda_{x}\left(y\right),\rho_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, where
\lambda_{x}
and
\rho_{y}
are maps from
X
into itself, for all
x,y\in X
. Then

r
is a solution if and only if for all
x,y,z\in X

(Y1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\lambda_{y}(z)=\lambda_{\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)}\lambda_{\rho_{y}\left(x\right)}\left(z\right)

(Y2)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rho_{\lambda_{y}\left(z\right)}\left(x\right)}\rho_{z}\left(y\right)=\rho_{\lambda_{\rho_{y}\left(x\right)}\left(z\right)}\lambda_{x}\left(y\right)

(Y3)

\displaystyle\rho_{z}\rho_{y}(x)=\rho_{\rho_{z}\left(y\right)}\rho_{\lambda_{y}\left(z\right)}\left(x\right)

We say that a solution
r
is
bijective
if
r
is a bijective map and, in particular,
involutive
if
r^{2}=\operatorname{id}_{X\times X}
;

left non-degenerate
if
\lambda_{x}\in\operatorname{Sym}_{X}
, for all
x\in X
;

right non-degenerate
if
\rho_{x}\in\operatorname{Sym}_{X}
, for all
x\in X
;
non-degenerate
if it is both left and right non-degenerate. It has only recently been proven in
[
17
]
that all non-degenerate solutions are bijective.

The theory of braces was introduced in 2007 by Rump
[
21
]
as an algebraic approach to study involutive non-degenerate solutions. A
left brace
is a set
B
endowed with two binary operations, usually denoted by
+
and
\circ
, such that
(B,+)
is an abelian group,
(B,+)
is a group, and the compatibility condition

(1)

\displaystyle a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c

holds, for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
-a
denotes the inverse of any
a
in
(B,+)
. Later, Guarnieri and Vendramin
[
15
]
introduced
skew left braces
as a generalization of braces, removing the assumption that the group
(B,+)
is abelian. Any skew left brace yields non-degenerate solutions.

Several further generalizations of braces have been proposed in the literature. Among them, we mention
left (cancellative) semibraces
, introduced in
[
7
]
. In the definition of skew left brace, instead of requiring
(B,+)
to be a group, one assumes that
(B,+)
is a left cancellative semigroup, and the compatibility condition becomes

(2)

\displaystyle a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+a\circ\left(a^{-}+c\right),

for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
a^{-}
denotes the inverse of
a
in the group
(B,\circ)
. Note that (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent in skew left brace structures; hence, every skew left brace is a left (cancellative) semibrace. The motivation for studying these structures lies in the fact that they still produce solutions that are left non-degenerate. A more detailed study of these algebraic structures can be found in
[
6
]
.

Subsequently, Jespers and Van Antwerpen
[
18
]
relaxed the condition on the additive structure by introducing
left semibraces
and assuming that
(B,+)
is an arbitrary semigroup. These structures do not necessarily determine solutions (see
[
9
, Theorem 3]
). We also mention that, to study left non-degenerate solutions (not necessarily bijective), the authors in
[
11
]
introduced the algebraic structure of YB-semitrusses, which forms a subclass of the category of semitrusses as introduced by Brzeziński
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we show that the class of right groups yield a wider class of left non-degenerate solutions than those arising from a left (cancellative) semibrace
B
whose additive structure is necessarily a right group (see
[
7
, p. 167]
). Recall that right groups are semigroups in which all left translations are bijective maps, or, equivalently, they are semigroups isomorphic to the external direct product of a group and a non-empty right zero semigroup (see
[
10
, Section 1.11, Theorem 1.27]
). To our end, we introduce and investigate the algebraic structure which we call a
left
RG
-semibrace
. It is a triplet
(B,+,\circ)
, where both
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are right groups and the identity

(3)

\displaystyle a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+(-a+a\circ c)

holds, for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
-a+a\circ c
is the unique element
x
of
B
such that
a+x=a\circ c
. We prove that (
3
) can be written as (
2
) and hence left (cancellative) semibraces form a subclass of left
RG
-semibraces.

By using structural properties of the two right groups
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
and the proof technique developed in
[
12
, cf. Theorem 2.15]
, we determine a left non-degenerate solution
r_{B}
associated with
B
. Additionally, we deduce that the operation
+
can influence the behaviour of
r_{B}
by providing solutions that satisfy
r_{B}^{2n+1}=r_{B}
, with
n
depending on the additive groupal component of
B
.
In particular, under certain conditions, we obtain cubic solutions, i.e.,
r_{B}^{3}=r_{B}
, or, more specifically, idempotent solutions.

Furthermore, exploiting the natural connection between averaging operators and right groups
[
23
, cf. Proposition 3.8]
, we employ them to obtain various classes of examples of
RG
-semibraces. Formally, we recall that a
left averaging operator
on a group
B
is a map
\varphi:B\to B
such that the equality

\varphi(a)\varphi(b)=\varphi\left(\varphi(a)b\right)
is satisfied, for all
a,b\in B
. Note that any idempotent endomorphism of a group is an averaging operator on that same group. Finally, we extend the matched product of left semibraces, originally introduced in
[
8
]
, to the wider framework of left
RG
-semibraces, and we specifically focus on the semidirect product as a particular case.

1.
Left
RG
-semibraces

1.1.
Basic notions on right groups

In this subsection, we provide basic notions on the class of right groups necessary throughout the paper.

To this end, let us recall that a
right simple semigroup

(S,\,\cdot)
is a semigroup such that
a\cdot S=S
, for all
a\in S
.
According to
[
10
, Lemma 1.26]
, every idempotent of a right simple semigroup
(S,\cdot)
is a left identity for
S
.

In particular, a class of right simple semigroups is given by
right zero semigroups
, i.e., those semigroups
(S,\,\cdot)
such that
a\cdot b=b
, for all
a,b\in\penalty\ S
. Since the operation
\cdot
corresponds to the second canonical projection
\pi_{2}\colon S\times S\to S
, right zero semigroups are those of the form
(S,\pi_{2})
for some set
S
. Similarly,
a\,\pi_{1}\,b=a
determines a
left zero semigroup
.
The full subcategory of the category of semigroups whose objects are all right zero semigroups is clearly isomorphic to the category
\mathsf{Set}
of sets, because every mapping between two right zero semigroups is a semigroup morphism.

The semigroups satisfying the equivalent conditions of he following result are called
right groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
10
, Section 1.11, Theorem 1.27]
The following assertions on a semigroup
(S,\,\cdot)
,
S\neq\emptyset
, are equivalent:

(a)

S
is right simple and left cancellative.

(b)

For every
a,b\in S
, there exists a unique element
x\in S
such that
a\cdot x=b
.

(c)

S
is right simple and contains at least an idempotent.

(d)

S
is isomorphic to the external direct product of
a group
G
and a non-empty right zero semigroup
E
.

Notice that from

Theorem

1.1
(d)

G
and
E
are both isomorphic to homomorphic images and subsemigroups of a right group
S
. Namely, if
\sigma_{e}
is the idempotent endomorphism of
S
defined by setting
\sigma_{e}(s)=s\cdot e
, for all
s\in S
and every idempotent
e\in S
, then the image of
\sigma_{e}
is the subgroup
S\cdot e
of
S
that is isomorphic to
G
.
In the same way, if
\ell_{s}:S\to S
is the map defined by
\ell_{s}(a)=s\cdot a
, for all
a,s\in S
, by
Theorem

1.1
(b), there is an idempotent endomorphism
\sigma
of the semigroup
S
defined by
\sigma(s)=\ell_{s}^{-1}(s)
, for every
s\in S
, whose image is the subsemigroup
E
of
S
. Also, if
\Delta\colon S\to S\times S
and
\mu\colon S\times S\to S
are the mappings defined by
\Delta(s)=(s,s)
and
\mu(s,s^{\prime})=s\cdot s^{\prime}
, for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S
, respectively, then

\mu(\sigma_{e}\times\sigma)\Delta=\operatorname{id}_{S},

for every
e\in E
. It is in this sense that
S
is a direct product of
G
and
E
. Note that, in general,
\mu(\sigma\times\sigma_{e})\Delta
is different from
\operatorname{id}_{S}
.

For any right group
(S,\,\cdot)
with a fixed idempotent element
0\in S
, there is a congruence
\sim
on the semigroup
S
defined by setting
a\sim b
if and only if
a\cdot 0=b\cdot 0
, for every
a,b\in S
. Notice that the equivalence
\sim
is compatible with both the operations
\cdot
and
\backslash
on
S
, where
\backslash
is the inverse operation of
\cdot
.
Note that the congruence
\sim
does not depend on the choice of the idempotent
0
.
The equivalence class of any
a\in S
modulo
\sim
is
a\cdot E:=\{\,a\cdot e\mid e\in E\,\}
, so that there is a partition
\{\,a\cdot E\mid a\in S\,\}
. A complete irredundant set of representatives of the congruence classes of
S
modulo
\sim
is the set
S\cdot 0:=\{\,a\cdot 0\mid a\in S\,\}
, which is a subgroup of
(S,\,\cdot)
with identity
0
. Moreover,
S\cdot 0
and
S/{\sim}
are canonically isomorphic groups.

Finally, notice that in the full subcategory
\mathsf{RGrp}
of the category of semigroups whose objects are all right groups, every semigroup morphism also respects the operation
\backslash
, that is, for all right groups
S,S^{\prime}
and all semigroup morphism
f\colon S\to S^{\prime}
, one necessarily has that
f(s_{1}\backslash s_{2})=f(s_{1})\backslash f(s_{2})
, for every
s_{1},s_{2}\in S
.

1.2.
Basics on left
RG
-semibraces

In this subsection, we introduce left
RG
-semibraces and investigate some of their structural properties.

Definition 1.2
.

A
left
RG
-semibrace
is a triplet
(B,+,\circ)
, where
B
is a set and
+,\circ
are binary operations on
B
, such that both
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are right groups,
and the identity

(4)

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+(-a+a\circ c)

holds, for all
a,b,c\in B
, where
-a+a\circ c
is the unique element
x
of
B
such that
a+x=a\circ c
.

Hence,
-a+a\circ c
is
a\backslash(a\circ c)
, where
\backslash
is the inverse of the operation
+
of the right group
(B,+)
.

Lemma 1.3
.

If
B
is a left
RG
-semibrace, then every idempotent element in the right group
(B,\circ)
is also idempotent in the right group
(B,+)
.

Proof.

Let
e
be any fixed idempotent in
(B,+)
and
f
be any idempotent in
(B,\circ)
. Then
f\circ(e+e)=f\circ e+(-f+f\circ e)
implies that
e+e=e+(-f+e)
because
f
is a left identity in
(B,\circ)
. Now
e
is a left identity for
(B,+)
, so
e=-f+e
. Therefore,
f+e=e
. From the decomposition
B=(B+e)+E
, where
B+e
is a group with respect to the operation
+
and
E
is the set of all idempotent elements in
(B,+)
, we can write any element
b\in B
in a unique way as
b=g_{b}+e_{b}
,
where
g_{b}=b+e\in B+e
and
e_{b}\in E
. Here
e_{b}=b\backslash b
, where
\backslash
is the inverse operation of the operation
+
of the right group
(B,+)
. For
b=f
, it follows that
f=(f+e)+e_{f}=e+e_{f}=e_{f}\in E
. This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In what follows, we will denote by
0
a fixed idempotent in
(B,\circ)
, hence necessarily idempotent in
(B,+)
as well.

From
Theorem

1.1
(d) we have that
B=G+E
where
G=B+0
is a subgroup of
(B,+)
with identity
0
and
E
is the set of all idempotents of
(B,+)
. Accordingly, we can write any element
b\in B
in a unique way as

b=g_{b}+e_{b}

with
g_{b}\in G
and
e_{b}\in E
. Here
g_{b}=b+0
, and
e_{b}
is the unique element of
B
such that
b+e_{b}=b
. Notice that the opposite
-g_{b}
of
g_{b}
in the additive group
B+0
is the unique element
x\in B
such that
b+x=0
. We have the two projections
B\to G
and
B\to E
defined by
b\mapsto g_{b}=b+0
and
b\mapsto e_{b}
, respectively. These projections are semigroup homomorphisms.
Similarly, we also have
B=H\circ F
where
H=B\circ 0
is a subgroup of
(B,\circ)
with identity
0
and
F
is the set of all idempotents of
(B,\circ)
. Thus, any element
b\in B
can be also written in a unique way as

b=h_{b}\circ f_{b}

with
h_{b}\in H
and
f_{b}\in F
. We have that
h_{b}=b\circ 0
, and
f_{b}
is the unique element of
B
such that
b\circ f_{b}=b
. We also have the projections
B\to H
and
B\to F
defined by
b\mapsto h_{b}
and
b\mapsto f_{b}
, respectively. Hereinafter, in the group
H=B\circ 0
, we will denote by
h_{b}^{-}
the inverse of
h_{b}
. Thus
h_{b}^{-}
is the unique element
y\in B
such that
b\circ y=0
.

Also, in any left
RG
-semibrace
B
, we know that
F\subseteq E
by Lemma
1.3
. As a consequence, if
(B,+)
is a group, then
(B,+,\circ)
is a skew left brace.

In these notations, the elements
-a+a\circ c
and
-g_{a}+a\circ c
coincide, for all
a,c\in B
. In fact,
a+(-g_{a}+a\circ c)=g_{a}+e_{a}+(-g_{a}+a\circ c)
, and because all elements of
E
are left identities in
(B,+)
, it follows that
e_{a}+(-g_{a}+a\circ c)=-g_{a}+a\circ c
. Therefore
a+(-g_{a}+a\circ c)=g_{a}+(-g_{a}+a\circ c)=a\circ c
. This proves that

-a+a\circ c=-g_{a}+a\circ c,

for every
a,c\in B
.

Consequently,
Definition

1.2
can be equivalently stated in a different way, as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

A
left
RG
-semibrace
is a triplet
(B,+,\circ)
, where
B
is a set and
+,\circ
are binary operations on
B
, such that both
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are right groups, there exists an element
0\in B
that is idempotent
both in
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
, and

(5)

a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-g_{a}+a\circ c,

for all
a,b,c\in B
.

According to
[
7
, p. 167]
and equation (2) in
[
6
, p. 134]
, any left (cancellative) semibrace is a left
RG
-semibrace for which
F\subsetneq E
. In particular, all skew left braces are left
RG
-semibraces.

Any right group
(B,\cdot)
determines a
trivial
left
RG
-semibrace if we consider the triplet
(B,\cdot,\cdot)
.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace. The assignment
a\mapsto g_{a}
can also be seen as an idempotent endomorphism
\sigma\colon B\to B
of the additive right group
(B,+)
. In this notation, our condition (
5
) in Definition

becomes equivalent to
a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-\sigma(a)+a\circ c
, for all
a,b,c\in B
. Thus, our definition of left

-semibrace is strictly related to the definition of

[

since, in this context, the three elements
-a+a\circ c
,

and

.

As usual in brace theory, we now introduce the lambda map associated with a left
RG
-semibrace

, through which identity (

) can also be rewritten.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace. Define a map
\lambda_{a}:B\to B
by setting

\lambda_{a}(b)=-g_{a}+a\circ b,

for all
a,b\in B
.
Then the following hold:

(a)

\lambda_{a}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
with inverse given by
\lambda_{a}^{-1}=\lambda_{h_{a}^{-}}
, for every
a\in B
.

(b)

\lambda_{a}(b)=a\circ(h_{a}^{-}+b)
, for all
a,b\in B
.

(c)

the map
\lambda:B\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
,
a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
, is a semigroup homomorphism from
(B,\circ)
into
\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)
.

(d)

\lambda_{a}=\lambda_{h_{a}}
, for all
a\in B
.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in B
and first note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}(b+c)

\displaystyle=-g_{a}+a\circ(b+c)=-g_{a}+a\circ b-g_{a}+a\circ c=\lambda_{a}(b)+\lambda_{a}(c)\,.

Next, for all
a,b\in B
define
\tau_{a}(b)=h_{a}^{-}\circ(a+b)
.
Then, the following hold:

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\tau_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=-g_{a}+a\circ\tau_{a}(b)=-g_{a}+h_{a}\circ h_{a}^{-}\circ(a+b)=b\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{a}\lambda_{a}(b)

\displaystyle=h_{a}^{-}\circ(a+\lambda_{a}(b))=h_{a}^{-}\circ(g_{a}-g_{a}+a\circ b)=b,

for all
a,b\in B
. Moreover, if
a,b\in B
then we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)=h_{a}^{-}\circ(a+b)=h_{a}^{-}\circ a-g_{h_{a}^{-}}+h_{a}^{-}\circ b=-g_{h_{a}^{-}}+h_{a}^{-}\circ b=\lambda_{h_{a}^{-}}(b).

This concludes the proof of (a).
Now, let
a,b\in B
and note that

\displaystyle a\circ\left(h_{a}^{-}+b\right)

\displaystyle=h_{a}\circ f_{a}\circ h_{a}^{-}-g_{a}+a\circ b=-g_{a}+a\circ b,

thus proving (b). Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{a\circ b}(c)

\displaystyle=a\circ b\circ(h_{b}^{-}\circ h_{a}^{-}+c)=a\circ\left(b\circ h_{b}^{-}\circ h_{a}^{-}-g_{b}+b\circ c\right)

\displaystyle=a\circ\left(h_{a}^{-}+\lambda_{b}(c)\right)=\lambda_{a}\lambda_{b}(c),

thus proving (c).
Now, since
\lambda_{f}=\operatorname{id}_{B}
, for all
f\in F
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}=\lambda_{h_{a}\circ f_{a}}=\lambda_{h_{a}}\lambda_{f_{a}}=\lambda_{h_{a}},

for all
a\in B
, concluding the proof.
∎

Notice that
\lambda_{a}(b)
is
a\backslash(a\circ b)
, where
\backslash
is the inverse of the operation
+
of the right group
(B,+)
.

Remark 1.7
.

Let
\mathsf{Grp}
denote the category of groups and
\mathsf{SGrp}
the category of semigroups. If
B
is a left

-semibrace, it is not difficult to show that the group

is isomorphic to the direct product
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Grp}}(B+0,+)\times\operatorname{Sym}_{E}
, where
\operatorname{Sym}_{E}
denotes the group of all permutations of the set

To this aim, it is sufficient to consider the group homomorphism

\mathcal{F}\colon\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{SGrp}}(B,+)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Grp}}(B+0,+)\times\operatorname{Sym}_{E}

that associates to every

the pair
\mathcal{F}(f)=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where

is defined by

, for every

, and

is the restriction of

.
The inverse of
\mathcal{F}
is the map
\mathcal{G}\colon\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Grp}}(B+0,+)\times\operatorname{Sym}_{E}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{SGrp}}(B,+)

the mapping

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(g,h):B\to B\,,\,\,b\mapsto g(b+0)+h(e_{b})\,,

for all

.

Now, if we put together the following three facts:

(1)

\lambda\colon(B,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{SGrp}}(B,+)

(2)

(B,\circ)=(B\circ 0)\circ F
;

(3)

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{SGrp}}(B,+)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Grp}}(B+0,+)\times\operatorname{Sym}_{E}

then we get that

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathsf{Grp}}(B+0,+)\,,\,\,b\in B+0\mapsto(\lambda_{b})_{1}

\displaystyle(B\circ 0,\circ)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Sym}_{E}\,,\,\,b\in B+0\mapsto(\lambda_{b})_{2}\,.

Hence,

on the group

and an action of the group
(B\circ 0,\circ)
on the set

.

The following shows how the
\lambda

to the operation
\circ
and conversely.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace. Then, for all
a,b\in B
:

(a)

a\circ b=a+\lambda_{a}(b)
.

(b)

a+b=a\circ\lambda_{h_{a}^{-}}(b)
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in B
. Then we have that

Let

As recalled in the preliminaries, every right group admits a congruence that is independent of the chosen idempotent. We now conclude this section by establishing some properties of this relation in the setting of left

Remark 1.12
.

For a left
RG
-semibrace

we have:

(1) For the right group
(B,+)
a congruence
\sim_{+}
defined, for every

(2) For the right group

is

(B\circ 0,\circ)
and

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a left

-semibrace. Then:

(a)

b\circ F\subseteq b+E
, for every
b\in B
.

(b)

The equivalence
\sim_{\circ}
is contained in the equivalence
\sim_{+}

[b]_{\sim_{\circ}}=b\circ F\mapsto[b]_{\sim_{+}}=b+E,

for every

(c)

As far as the complete irredundant set of representatives

and

are concerned, there is a canonical injective mapping

, defined by

, for every

.

(d)

The composite mapping of the injective mapping

in

, the group isomorphism

and the surjective map

Proof.

(a) If

1.8
(a) it follows that

(b) Follows from (a) and the fact that the equivalence classes modulo

(c) Let

2.
Constructions of left
RG
-semibraces

2.1.
Left
\mathbf{RG}
-semibraces from left averaging operators on groups

In this subsection, we recall the notion of averaging operators on groups, as recently introduced in
[
23
]
, and use it to provide a class of examples of left
RG
-semibraces. We underline that the study of averaging operators was initiated by Reynolds
[
20
]
in connection with turbulence theory and experienced an algebraic renaissance in the work of Aguiar
[
1
]
.

A
left averaging operator
on a group
B
is a map
{\varphi}:B\to B
such that the identity

\displaystyle{\varphi}(a){\varphi}(b)={\varphi}\left({\varphi}(a)b\right)

holds, for all
a,b\in B
.

Averaging operators on groups are a useful resource to construct right groups, see
[
23
, Proposition 3.8]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\varphi}:B\to B
be a left averaging operator on a group
B
and define a new operation
\vdash
on
B
by setting

\displaystyle a\vdash b:={\varphi}(a)b\,,

for all
a,b\in B
.
Then,
(B,\vdash)
is a semigroup. Moreover, if there exists
e\in B
such that
\varphi(e)=1_{B}
, then
(B,\vdash)
is a right group.

Let
{\varphi}:B\to B
be a left averaging operator on a group
B
and consider the operation
\vdash
defined in
Lemma

2.1
.
If we set
\ker({\varphi}):=\{a\in B\mid{\varphi}(a)=1_{B}\}
, then it is straightforward to verify that
\ker({\varphi})=E(B,\vdash)
.
Accordingly, if we assume that
\ker(\varphi)
is non-empty and fix
e\in\ker({\varphi})
then each element
a\in B
can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle a=\left({\varphi}(a)e\right)\vdash\left({\varphi}(a)^{-1}a\right)\,,

where
{\varphi}(a)e
and
{\varphi}(a)^{-1}a
are the groupal and idempotent components of
a
, respectively, in relation to the decomposition of
(B,\vdash)
induced by the fixed idempotent
e
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
B
be a group, consider two left averaging operators
{\varphi},{\psi}:B\to B
on
B
and assume that
\ker(\psi)\neq\varnothing
.
If
+
and
\circ
are the operations induced by
{\varphi}
and
{\psi}
, respectively, then the structure
(B,+,\circ)
is a left
RG
-semibrace if and only if

(6)

\displaystyle{\psi}(a){\varphi}(b)={\varphi}\left({\psi}(a)b\right){\varphi}(a)^{-1}{\psi}(a)

holds, for all
a,b\in B
.

Proof.

Assume first that (
6
) holds and note that if
a\in\ker(\psi)
then

\displaystyle\varphi(a)=\psi(a)\varphi(a)=\varphi\left(\psi(a)a\right)\varphi(a)^{-1}\psi(a)=\varphi(a)\varphi(a)^{-1}\psi(a)=1_{B}\,.

It follows that
\ker(\varphi)\neq\varnothing
so that the pairs
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
are right groups.
Now, let us note that if
a,b\in B
then
-g_{a}+b
coincides with the unique
x\in B
such that
a+x=b
, from which follows that
-g_{a}+b={\varphi}(a)^{-1}b
.
With this observation, it is straightforward to verify that (
6
) implies (
5
).
Conversely, if we assume that
(B,+,\circ)
is a left
RG
-semibrace, then an analogous reasoning shows that (
6
) is equivalent to (
5
), proving the assertion.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
B
be a group,
{\varphi}
and
{\psi}
two idempotent endomorphisms of

such that
{\varphi}{\psi}={\varphi}
and
\operatorname{Im}({\varphi})\subseteq Z(B)
. Then, by

2.2
, the structure

is a left

-semibrace, with operations

and

defined by

and

for all

.

A concrete non-trivial example is given by the Klein group

and
{\varphi},{\psi}:B\to B
the endomorphisms of

obtained by setting
{\varphi}(x)=1
,

, and
{\psi}(x)=1
,

In the previous example, neither

This section aims to extend the construction of the matched product of left semibraces, introduced in

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(B,\cdot),(C,\cdot^{\prime})
be two right groups. For every

, let
\sigma_{u}:B\to B,a\mapsto\sigma_{u}(a)
be a semigroup homomorphism. Suppose that
\sigma\colon(C,\cdot^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,\cdot),\,u\mapsto\sigma_{u}
is a semigroup homomorphism from
(C,\cdot^{\prime})
into
\operatorname{Aut}(B,\cdot)

endowed with the following operation

\displaystyle(a,u)(b,v):=\left(a\cdot\sigma_{u}(b),\,u\cdot^{\prime}v\right),

for all
(a,u),(b,v)\in B\times C
, is a right group.

Proof.

It is a routine computation to verify that it is a semigroup. Hence, using
Theorem

1.1
(b), it is sufficient to note that, if
(a,u),(b,v)\in B\times C
and
x\in B
and

are the unique elements such that
a\cdot x=b
and
u\cdot^{\prime}y=v
, set

, we obtain that
(t,y)
is the unique element in
B\times C
such that
(a,u)(t,y)=(b,v)
.
∎

Assuming that
B=G_{B}\cdot E_{B}
and
C=G_{C}\cdot^{\prime}E_{C}
are the decompositions of the two right groups
(B,\cdot)
and
(C,\cdot^{\prime})
, respectively, as in
Theorem

1.1
(d), it follows from the definition that
\sigma_{e}=\operatorname{id}_{B}
, for all

. As a consequence, we have that the idempotent part is given by
E_{B\times C}=E_{B}\times E_{C}
.
Moreover, the groupal part
G_{B\times C}
is computed in the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
B\rtimes_{\sigma}C
is the semidirect product of two right groups
(B,\cdot)
and
(C,\cdot^{\prime})
via
\sigma
, then
G_{B\rtimes C}=G_{B}\rtimes_{\sigma}G_{C}
.
In particular,
g_{(a,u)}=(g_{a},g_{u})
, for all
(a,u)\in B\times C

Proof.

The first part of the statement is obvious. Besides, we have that

g_{(a,u)}=(a,u)(0_{B},0_{C})=(g_{a}\sigma_{g_{u}}(0_{B}),g_{u}),

for all
(a,u)\in B\times C
. Note that the inverse of
g_{(a,u)}
is

\displaystyle\left(\sigma_{g_{u}}^{-1}(g_{a}^{-}),g_{u}^{-}\right)=\left(((\sigma^{-1}_{g_{u}}(g_{a}^{-})\cdot 0_{B})\cdot\sigma_{g^{-1}_{u}}(0_{B}),\,g^{-1}_{u}\right)\in G_{B\rtimes_{\sigma}C}\,.

\displaystyle(0_{B},0_{C})=\left(((\sigma^{-1}_{g_{u}}(g_{a}^{-})\cdot 0_{B})\cdot\sigma_{g^{-1}_{u}}(0_{B}),g^{-1}_{u}\right)(g_{a}\cdot\sigma_{g_{u}}(0_{B}),g_{u})=(\sigma^{-1}_{g_{u}}(0_{B}),0_{C})

we get that
\sigma^{-1}_{g_{u}}(0_{B})=0_{B}
.
Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
and
(C,+^{\prime},\circ^{\prime})
be two left

\sigma:(C,\circ^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,+,\circ),\,u\mapsto\sigma_{u}

endowed with the following operations

\displaystyle(a,u)\,\hat{+}(b,v):=(a+b,u+v),

\displaystyle(a,u)\,\hat{\circ}\,(b,v):=\left(a\circ\sigma_{u}(b),\,u\circ^{\prime}v\right),

and denote it by

Proof.

Clearly,

and

are right groups by the previous lemma.
Moreover, since by
Lemma

2.8

g_{(a,u)}=(g_{a},g_{u})
, for all

, we obtain

\displaystyle(a,u)\hat{\circ}\left((b,v)\hat{+}(c,w)\right)

\displaystyle=(a\circ\sigma_{u}(b+c),\,u\circ^{\prime}(v+w))

\displaystyle=(a\circ\sigma_{u}(b)-g_{a}+a\circ\sigma_{u}(c)),\,u\circ^{\prime}v-^{\prime}g_{u}+^{\prime}u\circ^{\prime}w))

\displaystyle=(a\circ\sigma_{u}(b),u\circ^{\prime}v)\,\hat{-}\,(g_{a},g_{u})\,\hat{+}\,(a\circ\sigma_{u}(c),u\circ^{\prime}w)

Thanks to

, it is possible to construct left

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
(B,\circ)
be a group,
\left(C,+^{\prime}\right)
a right group,
and consider a semigroup homomorphism

3.
Solutions associated to left
RG
-semibraces

In this section, we prove that any left
RG
-semibrace gives rise to a left non-degenerate solution.

To prove the main result, we make use of the notion of
twist
of a shelf and the description of left non-degenerate solutions in terms of twists
(cf.
[
12
, Definition 2.14]
).
First, recall that a
(
left
)
shelf

(X,\triangleright)
is a set
X
equipped with a left self-distributive binary operation
\triangleright
such that

(9)

x\triangleright(y\triangleright z)=(x\triangleright y)\triangleright(x\triangleright z),

for all
x,y,z\in X
.
A
shelf homomorphism
between two shelves is defined as any map that preserves the shelf operations on each underlying set.
If we denote with
L_{x}:X\to X,y\mapsto x\triangleright y
the operator of left multiplication by
x
, for all
x\in X
, then equation (
9
) is equivalent to requiring that
L_{x}
be a shelf homomorphism. If, in addition, the maps
L_{x}
are bijective,
(X,\triangleright)
is a
(left) rack
. Moreover, we say that a shelf
(X,\triangleright)
is a
(left) spindle
if
x\triangleright x=x
, for all
x\in X
. A rack
(X,\triangleright)
that is also a spindle is a
left quandle
. For more details on the topic, see, for instance,
[
14
]
.

If
(X,\triangleright)
is a shelf, then a map
\varphi:X\to\operatorname{Aut}(X,\triangleright)
is called a
twist
of
(X,\triangleright)
if the following identity

(10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{a}\varphi_{b}=\varphi_{\varphi_{a}(b)}\varphi_{\varphi^{-1}_{\varphi_{a}(b)}\left(\varphi_{a}(b)\,\triangleright\,a\right)},

holds, for all
a,b\in X
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
12
, cf. Theorem 2.15]

Let
(X,\triangleright)
be a shelf and
\varphi:X\to\operatorname{Sym}_{X}
a map.
If we define
r:X\times X\to X\times X
by setting

\displaystyle r(a,b)=\left(\varphi_{a}(b),\varphi^{-1}_{\varphi_{a}(b)}\left(\varphi_{a}(b)\triangleright a\right)\right)\,,

for all
a,b\in X
, then
(X,r)
is a left non-degenerate solution if and only if
\varphi
is a twist.
Moreover, any left non-degenerate solution can be obtained that way.

We now introduce a particular spindle that will be used, together with
Lemma

3.1
, to prove the theorem on solutions. The proof of the following lemma is omitted, since it consists of a straightforward computation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(S,\,\cdot)
be a right group and
\triangleright
the binary operation on

-semibrace

\displaystyle\rho_{b}(a)=h_{\lambda_{a}(b)}^{-}\circ a\circ b,

.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace. Then the map
r_{B}:B\times B\to B\times B
defined by
r_{B}(a,b)=\left(\lambda_{a}(b),\rho_{b}(a)\right),

for all
a,b\in B
, is a left non-degenerate solution.

Proof.

Let us prove that the map
\lambda
is a twist of the conjugation spindle
(B,\triangleright)
where
a\triangleright b:=-g_{a}+b+a
, for all
a,b\in B
.
By
Proposition

1.6
(a),

\displaystyle g_{\lambda_{x}(a)}=\lambda_{x}(a)+0=\lambda_{x}(g_{a})+\lambda_{x}(e_{a})+0=\lambda_{x}(g_{a})+0,

for all
x,a\in B
. Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}(a\triangleright b)=\lambda_{x}(-g_{a})+\lambda_{x}(b)+\lambda_{x}(a)=-g_{\lambda_{x}(a)}+\lambda_{x}(b)+\lambda_{x}(a)=\lambda_{x}(a)\triangleright\lambda_{x}(b)

for all
x,a,b\in B
, namely, for all
x\in B
,
\lambda_{x}
is a shelf homomorphism and since it is bijective,
\lambda_{x}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B,\triangleright)
.
Now, let us observe that

\displaystyle\lambda_{x}\rho_{\lambda^{-1}_{y}(x)}(y)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{x}\left(h^{-}_{\lambda_{y}\lambda_{h^{-}_{y}}(x)}\circ y\circ\lambda_{h^{-}_{y}}(x)\right)=\lambda_{x}\left(h^{-}_{x}\circ(h_{y}+x)\right)

\displaystyle=-g_{x}+x\circ h^{-}_{x}\circ(h_{y}+x)=-g_{x}+h_{y}+x=-g_{x}+g_{h_{y}}+x

\displaystyle=-g_{x}+g_{y}+x=x\triangleright y,

for all
x,y\in B
, where in the second last equality we use
Proposition

1.9
(a).
Therefore, by the bijectivity of the maps
\lambda_{x}
, we obtain that

\rho_{y}(x)=\lambda^{-1}_{\lambda_{x}(y)}\left(\lambda_{x}(y)\triangleright x\right),

for all
x,y\in B
. As a consequence, equality (
10
) follows by
Proposition

1.6
(c) and
Proposition

3.3
(a).
Therefore,
r_{B}
is a left non-degenerate solution by
Lemma

3.1
. ∎

From Remark
1.7
and Proposition
3.4
(h), we get the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace. Then the corresponding left non-degenerate solution
r_{B}\colon B\times B\to B\times B
can be identified with the mapping

r_{B}\colon(G\times E)\times(H\times F)\to(G\times E)\times(H\times F)

defined by

\left(\left(g_{a},e_{a}\right),\left(h_{b},f_{b}\right)\right)\mapsto\left(\left(\left(\lambda_{g_{a}+e_{a}}\right)_{1}(g_{h_{b}\circ f_{b}}),e_{(g_{a}+e_{a})\circ(h_{b}\circ f_{b})}\right),\left(\rho^{\prime}_{h_{b}\circ f_{b}}(h_{g_{a}+e_{a}}),f_{b}\right)\right).

Proof.

For all
a,b\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}\left(b\right)

\displaystyle=-g_{a}+a\circ b+e_{a\circ b}=-g_{a}+a\circ\left(g_{b}+e_{b}\right)+e_{a\circ b}

\displaystyle=-g_{a}+a\circ g_{b}+\lambda_{a}\left(e_{b}\right)+e_{a\circ b}=\lambda_{a}\left(g_{b}\right)+e_{a\circ b},

where in the last equality we apply that
\lambda_{a}\left(e_{b}\right)\in E
.
Hence, following the notation in Remark
1.7
,
g_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=\left(\lambda_{a}\right)_{1}(b)
and
e_{\lambda_{a}(b)}=e_{a\circ b}
. Moreover, by
Proposition

1.6
(d),

\displaystyle\rho_{b}(a)=h^{-}_{\lambda_{h_{a}}(b)}\circ a\circ b=h^{-}_{\lambda_{h_{a}}(b)}\circ h_{a}\circ b\circ f_{b}=\rho_{b}(h_{a})\circ f_{b}.

Thus,
h_{\rho_{b}(a)}=\rho^{\prime}_{b}\left(h_{a}\right)
and
f_{\rho_{b}(a)}=f_{b}
.
Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
(B,\cdot)
be a right group that is not a group and consider the trivial left
RG
-semibrace
(B,\cdot,\cdot)
. Then the map
r_{B}(x,y)=\left(y,\,h^{-}_{y}\cdot h_{x}\cdot y\right)
is a left non-degenerate solution on
B
that cannot arise as a solution associated with a (cancellative) semibrace on
B
.

Let us now focus on the order of solutions associated to a left
RG
-semibrace. To this purpose, we recall the notions of
index
and
period
of a solution
r
introduced in
[
9
]
that are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\ind{\left(r\right)}:=\min\left\{\left.j\,\right|\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\exists\,l\in\mathbb{N}\ r^{j}=r^{l},\ j\neq l\right\},

\displaystyle\per{\left(r\right)}:=\min\left\{\left.k\,\right|\,k\in\mathbb{N},\,r^{\ind{\left(r\right)}+k}=r^{\ind{\left(r\right)}}\right\}.

While being slightly different from the classical ones (cf.
[
16
, p. 10]
), these definitions are functional to distinguish bijective solutions, having index
0
, from non-bijective ones, having index a positive integer.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
B
is a left
RG
-semibrace and
r_{B}
is the solution associated with
B
, then the following hold:

(1)

r_{B}
has finite index and period if and only if
G/Z(G)
is periodic.

(2)

If
n:=\exp(G/Z(G))
is finite, then
r_{B}^{2n+1}=r_{B}
.

In particular,
r_{B}=2n
if and only if
|E|=1
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.1
and
[
12
, Lemma 2.12]
, it is enough to prove the statement for the solution associated to the conjugation spindle on the additive right group
(B,+)
, namely, to the map
r_{\triangleright}:B\times B\to B\times B
defined by
r_{\triangleright}(a,b)=(b,\,-g_{b}+a+b)
, for all
a,b\in B
.
To this end, note that
r_{\triangleright}
is the solution associated to the trivial
RG
-semibrace
(B,+,+)
, hence, by
Corollary

3.6
, we can identify the map
r_{\triangleright}
with

\displaystyle r_{\triangleright}:\,

\displaystyle(G\times E)\times(G\times E)\to(G\times E)\times(G\times E),

\displaystyle\left((g_{a},e_{a}),(g_{b},e_{b})\right)\mapsto\left((g_{b},e_{b}),(g_{b}\blacktriangleright g_{a},e_{b})\right)

where
(G,\blacktriangleright)
is the conjugation quandle of the additive group
(G,+)
.
As a consequence, the solution
r_{\triangleright}
is the direct product
r_{\blacktriangleright}\times r_{E}
where
r_{\blacktriangleright}
is the solution associated with
(G,\blacktriangleright)
and
r_{E}
is the idempotent solution associated to the
RG
-semibrace
(E,+,+)
.
Thanks to
[
9
, Theorem 9]
, we deduce that
r_{\triangleright}
has finite order if and only if
r_{G}
has finite order.
Moreover, by
[
2
, Corollary 1.20]
,
r_{G}
has finite order if and only if
G/Z(G)
is periodic.
In particular, by
[
9
, Proposition 10]
in this case we have that
\ind{\left(r_{\triangleright}\right)}\in\{0,1\}
and
\per{\left(r_{\triangleright}\right)}=2n
, where
n:=\exp(G/Z(G))
. Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Note that solutions obtained from left
RG
-semibraces
have the same index and same order as those arising from left (cancellative) semibraces, cf.
[
9
, Corollary 13]
.

Let us notice that index of
r_{B}
depends exactly by the substructure
(E,+,\circ)
of the arbitrary
RG
-semibrace
(B,+,\circ)
. On the other hand, although by
Proposition

1.11
the structure
(G,+,\circ)
is not generally a substructure of a left
RG
-semibrace
B
, the period of
r_{B}
depends exactly on the additive group
(G,+)
.
More specifically, the following result aligns with
[
9
, Corollary 13]
and
[
18
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
B
be a left
RG
-semibrace and
r_{B}
the solution associated with
B
. Then
r_{B}
is cubic, i.e.,
r_{B}^{3}=r_{B}
, if and only if
(G,+)
is an abelian group. In particular,
r_{B}
is idempotent if and only if
G=\{0\}
, namely,
(B,+)=(E,+)
is a left zero semigroup.

Example 3.11
.

Let
(B,\circ)
be a right group and define
a+b:=b
, for all
a,b\in B
, i.e.,
(B,+)
is a right zero semigroup. Then,
(B,+,\circ)
is an
RG
-semibrace and the solution associated to
B
is the map
r_{B}:B\times B\to B\times B
given by

\displaystyle r_{B}(a,b)=(a\circ b,\,f_{b}),

for all
a,b\in B
. Consistently with
Corollary

3.10
,
r_{B}
is idempotent.

As for
3.7
, the solution arising from the following example of left
RG
-semibrace does not arise from a left (cancellative) semibrace.

Example 3.12
.

Let
B
the left
RG
-semibrace in
2.3
. Observe that the solution
r_{B}
cannot be obtained from any left (cancellative) semibrace structure
\left(B,+^{\prime},\circ^{\prime}\right)
on the same underlying set
B
. Indeed, since
F=\{1,x\}
, we have that
\lambda_{1}(1)=1
and
\lambda_{x}(x)=x
. Hence, the only possible candidates for the identity element
0
of the group
\left(B,\circ^{\prime}\right)
are
1
and
x
(this is a consequence of the fact that in any left (cancellative) semibrace
\lambda_{0}(0)=0
). Assume that
0
is
1
, then considering that, for all
a,b\in B
,
a\circ^{\prime}b=a+^{\prime}\lambda_{a}(b)
, an easy computation shows that the only idempotent with respect to the operation
+^{\prime}
is
0
itself.
The same argument applies if
0=x
. Then
\left(B,+^{\prime},\circ^{\prime}\right)
would actually be a skew brace, and this is a contradiction because the solution
r_{B}
is not bijective.
