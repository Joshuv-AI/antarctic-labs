Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain bounds for the best constants in two inequalities which can be seen as analogues of the Lieb-Thirring inequality, but with the Dirac operator, on the n − n- sphere. We then apply these results in order to improve the known upper bounds on the classical Lieb-Thirring constant on the n n -sphere for n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 .



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 35P15. Secondary: 26D10

1.
Introduction

Lieb-Thirring inequalities, first introduced in
[
22
]
, offer bounds on the
\gamma
-moments of the negative eigenvalues for the Schrödinger operator:

(1)

\Psi=-\Delta-V,

acting in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. These inequalities assert the existence of constants
L_{\gamma,n}>0
such that

(2)

\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq 0}|\lambda_{j}|^{\gamma}\leq L_{\gamma,n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)^{\gamma+\frac{n}{2}}dx,

where
V\geq 0
is a potential function that diminishes rapidly at infinity, and
\gamma\geq\max\{1-\frac{n}{2},0\}
. These inequalities have numerous applications in fields such as dynamical systems (
[
10
,
17
,
23
]
) and quantum mechanics. The exact values for
L_{\gamma,n}
, especially for
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, were determined in
[
21
]
and are expressed as:

(3)

L^{cl}_{\gamma,n}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{2^{n}\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(\gamma+n/2+1)}.

This inequality is equivalent to a related inequality for orthonormal families of functions. Specifically, there is a constant
k_{n}>0
such that for any orthonormal set of functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and with
\rho(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}
, we have:

(4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho(x)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}dx\leq k_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\nabla\psi_{j}\|^{2}_{2}.

The best constants
k_{n}
are connected to
L_{\gamma,n}
as follows (see
[
23
,
22
]
):

k_{n}=\frac{2}{n}\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}L^{\frac{2}{n}}_{1,n}.

These types of inequalities can also be generalized to manifolds. For a smooth manifold
M
of dimension
n
, let
k_{M}>0
denote the smallest constant for which the following inequality holds for any set of orthonormal functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H^{1}(M)
, with
\rho(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}
:

(5)

\int_{M}\rho(x)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}dx\leq k_{M}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\nabla\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

However, for compact manifolds
M
, the zero eigenvalue must be considered. Thus, instead of the operator (
1
), we use:

-\Delta-\Pi(V\Pi\cdot),

where
\Delta
now denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
and
\Pi
is the orthogonal projection given by:

\Pi\psi(x)=\psi(x)-\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}\psi(y)dy.

For further reading on this topic, see
[
8
,
16
,
18
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
19
]
.

In recent years Dirac operators with a potential as the natural relativistic counterpart of Laplacians received a lot of attention from the point of view of the spectral theory. These operators describe relativistic spin-1/2 particles propagating under strongly localized external potentials, such quark confinement. For instance, Dirac operators with singular electrostatic, Lorentz scalar and anomalous magnetic interactions supported on surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
have been studied in
[
2
]
, while Dirac operators with
\delta
-shell potential were treated in
[
3
]
. These operators also appear in the study of conductivity properties of single-layer graphene structures. The
n
-dimensional Dirac operator with potential as approximations of the Hamiltonians of
interactions of relativistic quantum particles with potentials has been studied in
[
24
]
.

In another direction the study of spectra of Dirac operators over Riemannian manifolds is a classic topic, see, e.g.
[
11
]
or
[
9
]
. In general only estimates are known, but in special cases like the
n
-dimensional sphere explicit values for the eigenvalues of the Dirac operator
\not{D}
are known
[
1
]
.

This leads to the question if we can obtain the corresponding analogue for the Lieb-Thirring inequality for the Dirac operator
\not{D}
. Since the spectrum of the Dirac operator contains both positive and negative eigenvalues such an analogue can only be obtained for the positive or negative part of the spectrum. Therefore, our concrete problem is given by:

Does there exist a constant
K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}>0
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(x)dx,

for any orthonormal family
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, each with mean value zero, and if so, what is the best constant
K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
? Hereby
\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})
denotes the projection onto the positive spectrum
\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})=\chi_{(0,+\infty)}(\not{D})
. The second question is if under the same conditions, do we have a best constant
K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}^{\prime}>0
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\not{D}\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\not{D}\psi_{j},\not{D}\psi_{j}\rangle\geq K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}(x)dx

and if so what is the best constant?

The technique employed in the proofs of this paper drew significant inspiration from
[
14
,
15
]
, where the authors established the upper limits
k_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{2\pi}
and
k_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\leq\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}
. Their approach incorporated concepts reminiscent of those initially presented by M. Rumin
[
25
]
, who considered the inequality in its original form (
2
), within a broader context and applied to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This methodology was subsequently refined for the Euclidean framework in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

Finally, making use of the fact that the Dirac operator coincides with the gradient operator for real-valued functions, we are able to estimate the classical Lieb-Thirring constant
k_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
from our estimates on
K_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}^{\prime}
and improve the best known upper bounds for
n\geq 5
presented in
[
20
]
. In particular, we prove that (see Theorem
3.6
):

Theorem 1.1
.

The best constant
k_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
satisfies

k_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\leq\left(\frac{2n^{4}+16n^{3}+42n^{2}+44n+16}{n^{4}-6n^{2}+6n+8}\right)n!^{-\frac{2}{n}}2^{\frac{2}{n}(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)-1}(\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{n}})^{-\frac{2}{n}},

for
n\geq 2
, where
\sigma_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
denotes the volume of the
n
-sphere.

2.
Preliminaries

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
be the
2^{d}
-dimensional universal real Clifford algebra over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
constructed from the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d}\}
under the usual relations

e_{k}e_{l}+e_{l}e_{k}=-2\delta_{kl},~1\leq k,l\leq d,

where
\delta_{kl}
is the Kronecker delta function. An element
f\in\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
can be represented as
f=\sum_{A}f_{A}e_{A},f_{A}\in\mathbb{R},
where
e_{A}=e_{j_{1}j_{2}\ldots j_{k}}=e_{j_{1}}e_{j_{2}}\ldots e_{j_{k}}
,
A=\{j_{1},j_{2},\ldots j_{k}\}
with
1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\cdots\leq j_{k}\leq d
, and
e_{0}=e_{\emptyset}=1
is the identity element of
\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
. Also consider the complexified Clifford algebra
\mathbb{C}_{d}=\mathbb{R}_{0,d}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. The elements of the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
for which
|A|=k
are called
k
-vectors, and similarly for
\mathbb{C}_{d}
. We denote the space of all
k
-vectors by

\mathbb{R}_{0,d}^{k}:=\text{ span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{e_{A}:|A|=k\},

and analogously
\mathbb{C}_{d}^{k}
for complex
k
-vectors.

For
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
, it is clear that the spaces
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{K}^{d}
can be identified with
\mathbb{K}_{0,d}^{0}
and
\mathbb{K}_{0,d}^{1},
respectively. Elements of the space
\mathbb{K}_{0,d}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{K}_{0,d}^{1}
are also called para-vectors (scalar+vector).

We define the conjugation as an anti-automorphism on
\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
and
\mathbb{C}_{d}
by

f\mapsto\overline{f}=\sum_{A}\overline{f_{A}}\overline{e_{A}},

where the conjugation on the basis elements
e_{A}
is defined by the properties:
\overline{e_{j}}=-e_{j},
for
j=1,\ldots,d
, and
\overline{ab}=\overline{b}\overline{a}
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
or
\mathbb{C}_{d}
, respectively.

In what follows, we will require two types of scalar products. First, we introduce the (complex-valued) scalar product of
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{0,d}
or
\mathbb{C}_{d}
, as the scalar part of their geometric product

a\cdot b:=[\overline{a}{b}]_{0}=\sum_{A}\overline{a_{A}}{b_{A}}.

As usual, when we set
a=b
we obtain the square of the modulus (or magnitude) of the multivector
a
:

|a|^{2}=[\overline{a}a]_{0}=\sum_{A}|a_{A}|^{2}.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a (bounded or unbounded) domain. Any function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{n}
can be represented by
f=\sum_{A}e_{A}f_{A}
with complex-valued component functions
f_{A}:\Omega\to\mathbb{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus notations such as
f\in C^{k}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}_{d})
,
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}_{d})
, can be understood both co-ordinate-wisely and directly.

In particular, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}_{d})
the right-linear module of all Clifford-valued functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{d}

with finite norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{d})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\int_{\Omega}|f(\mathbf{x})|^{p}d^{n}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&{1\leq p<\infty},\\
{\rm ess}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}|f(\mathbf{x})|,&{p=\infty},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
d^{n}\mathbf{x}=dx_{1}dx_{2}\ldots dx_{n}
represents the usual Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In the case of
p=2,
we shall also denote this norm by
\|f\|
.

Given two functions
f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}_{d}),
we define a Clifford-valued sesqui-linear form via

(f,g):=\int_{\Omega}{\overline{f(\mathbf{x})}g(\mathbf{x})}d^{n}\mathbf{x},

from which we construct the scalar inner product

(6)

\langle f,g\rangle:\,=\left[(f,g)\right]_{0}=\int_{\Omega}[\overline{f(\mathbf{x})}g(\mathbf{x})]_{0}d^{n}\mathbf{x}.

We remark that (
6
) satisfies the (Clifford-) Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

|\langle f,g\rangle|\leq K_{d}\|f\|\|g\|,\quad\forall f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{C}_{d}),

with best constant
K_{d}
given by (see
[
5
,
12
]
):

K_{d}=\begin{cases}2^{d/4}&\text{if }d\text{ is even},\\
2^{(d+1)/4}&\text{if }d\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Let us remark that in the special case where
f
or
g
are either vector- or para-vector-valued functions we have the classical Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

|\langle f,g\rangle|\leq\|f\|\|g\|.

We also need to point out that in general both inner products are needed. While
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
gives rise to a norm, for questions of duality the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
is needed. In particular, in the case of
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{n}
we have the spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{C}_{d})
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional sphere. As a compact manifold without boundary, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
we also consider the corresponding Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{C}_{d})=W^{s,2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{C}_{d})
, which as before can be understood both co-ordinate-wisely and directly.

In this paper we will consider the spherical Dirac operator
[
1
]
. For this we will introduce the Gamma operator

\Gamma=-\sum_{j<k}e_{jk}L_{jk}=-\sum_{j<k}e_{jk}(x_{j}\partial_{x_{k}}-x_{k}\partial_{x_{j}})

where
L_{jk}=x_{j}\partial_{x_{k}}-x_{k}\partial_{x_{j}}
are the angular momentum operators. Using this operator the spherical Dirac operator over
\mathbb{S}^{n}
has the form

\not{D}=\Gamma-\frac{n-1}{2}.

In particular, we have that its square satisfies

\not{D}^{2}=(\Gamma-\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}=(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}-\Gamma(n-1-\Gamma)=(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}-\Delta_{LB},

where
\Delta_{LB}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator over the sphere. In the notation of
[
1
]
this operator corresponds to the operator
D+\mu
with
\mu=1/2
and the above identity is known as the Weitzenböck formula (Lemma 2 in
[
1
]
).

3.
Main Results

In this section we present the main results obtained in this paper. Their proofs can be found in Section
4
.

For the rest of this paper, let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. Also, we denote by
\nabla
the spherical gradient, that is,
\nabla\equiv\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any orthonormal family
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{C}_{d})
, such that
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{j}=0
, for
1\leq j\leq N
, we have that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\rho(x)^{\frac{3}{2}}dx,

where

\rho(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2},
and
\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})=\chi_{(0,+\infty)}(\not{D})
is the projection onto the positive spectrum of the Dirac operator.

Next, we generalize Theorem
3.1
for the
n
-sphere, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary. The case
n=2
was treated separately in order to obtain better estimates in this case of great importance in physics and mathematics.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any orthonormal family
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{C}_{d})
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi_{j}=0
for
1\leq j\leq N
, we have that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq{{n!}^{\frac{1}{n}}2^{-\frac{1}{n}\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}}c_{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(x)dx,

where

\rho(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2},

\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})=\chi_{(0,\infty)}(\not{D})
is the projection onto the positive spectrum of the Dirac operator and where
c_{n}
is given by:

(7)

\displaystyle c_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}-\frac{(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2}{n}}}{{(n+1)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}}\frac{n(2\sqrt{n(n+1)}-n)}{n^{2}+3n+2},

and the integration is taken with respect to the normalized standard surface measure on the sphere.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any orthonormal family
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{C}_{d})
and
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi_{j}=0
for
1\leq j\leq N
, we have that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\not{D}\psi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{d})}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\not{D}\psi_{j},\not{D}\psi_{j}\rangle\geq c_{n}^{\prime}n!^{\frac{2}{n}}2^{{1}-\frac{2}{n}(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}(x)dx,

where

\rho(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}
,

(8)

c_{n}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}\frac{n^{4}-6n^{2}+6n+8}{2n^{4}+16n^{3}+42n^{2}+44n+16},&\text{ if }n\geq 2,\\
\frac{7}{360}&\text{ if }n=1,\end{cases}

and the integration is taken with respect to the normalized standard surface measure on the sphere.

4.
Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

First recall (see for instance
[
4
]
) that the set of non-constant normalised eigenfunctions
\{y_{k}^{j\pm}\}_{j,k}
for the Dirac operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
have corresponding eigenvalues

\lambda_{k}^{\pm}=\pm(k+1),\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

These eigenvalues have multiplicity

2(k+1),\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

each and we denote by
y^{j\pm}_{k}
the corresponding eigenfunctions. These eigenfunctions form an orthonormal basis for the set of functions with zero mean.
Moreover, the following identity also holds, for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
:

(11)

\sum_{j=1}^{2(k+1)}|y^{j-}_{k}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{2(k+1)}|y^{j+}_{k}(x)|^{2}=2(k+1).

We note that this identity differs slightly from the one present in
[
4
]
as we are considering the normalised surface measure on the sphere.

Next, we introduce the following notation for labeling the non-constant eigenfunctions and corresponding eigenvalues of the projection onto the positive spectrum of the Dirac operator with a single subscript counting multiplicities:

\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left\{y_{k}^{j+},\dots\right\},

and

(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}=((k+1),\dots),

where the positive eigenvalue
(k+1)
is repeated
2(k+1)
times.
For
E\geq 0
, define the spectral projections:

P_{E}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}<E}\langle\cdot,y_{j}\rangle y_{j}

and

P_{E}^{\perp}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq E}\langle\cdot,y_{j}\rangle y_{j}.

Then

(12)

{\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})}_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\langle\cdot,y_{j}\rangle y_{j},

where
{\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})}_{0}
denotes the restriction of
\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})
to the invariant subspace of functions orthogonal to constants, that is

{\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})}_{0}=P_{\lambda_{1}}^{\perp}\circ\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\circ P_{\lambda_{1}}^{\perp}.

Next, notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}-0)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}+(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}a_{j}+\dots=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq E}a_{j}dE,

therefore we have the spectral decomposition

(13)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq E}\langle\cdot,y_{j}\rangle y_{j}dE=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{E}^{\perp}dE.

In view of (
12
) and (
13
), for
\psi=(\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{N})
we have

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi,\psi\rangle=\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})_{0}\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\langle P_{E}^{\perp}\psi,\psi\rangle dE

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi\|^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}dxdE

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}dEdx.

Denoting by
\Gamma_{\psi}
the orthogonal projection:

\Gamma_{\psi}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\cdot,\psi_{j}\rangle\psi_{j},

we obtain

(14)

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dEdx,

where

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}.

Now let
B
be a neighbourhood around
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, with measure
|B|\leq 1
, and let
\chi_{B}
be the corresponding characteristic function. Then

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\rho(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\Gamma_{\psi}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}+\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}

(15)

\displaystyle=\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}+\left(\int_{B}\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\|\cdot\|_{HS}
denotes the usual Hilbert-Schmidt norm for operators.
Since
\|\Gamma_{\psi}\|=1
and both
P_{E}
and
\chi_{B}
are projections, we conclude that:

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}<E}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}|y_{j}(x)|^{2}\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k+1)<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{2(k+1)}{|y_{k}^{j+}(x)|^{2}}\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{addition_formula_s2_1}}}{{=}}|B|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k+1)<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}2(k+1)

(16)

\displaystyle=|B|(m^{2}+m),\,\quad\text{for }\quad m<E\leq m+1,\ m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where the last equality follows from the fact that for
m<E\leq m+1
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(k+1)<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}2(k+1)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k^{\prime}=1\end{subarray}}^{m}k^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{(m+1)m}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=m^{2}+m.

Next, let

(17)

C(E)\vcentcolon=m^{2}+m,\quad\text{for }\quad m<E\leq m+1,\ m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Substituting inequality (
16
) in (
4
), dividing both sides by
|B|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and letting
|B|\to 0
, we obtain

\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}\leq C(E)^{1/2}+\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}(x_{0})^{1/2},

for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Since
\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}\geq 0
, this implies

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}(x_{0})\geq(\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2},

for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, where the expression
(\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}
denotes the positive part of this difference. Integrating this expression over
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and applying (
14
) yields

(18)

\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi,\psi\rangle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\rho(x)^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dEdx.

We proceed to estimate the integral above as follows: for
\rho>0
, let

(19)

I(\rho)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\rho^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE.

For any fixed
\rho>0
, let
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the unique non-negative integer such that

M^{2}+M\leq\rho<(M+1)^{2}+(M+1).

Then

\displaystyle I(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\int_{m}^{m+1}(\rho^{1/2}-(m^{2}+m)^{1/2})^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}[\rho-2\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{m^{2}+m}+m^{2}+m]

(20)

\displaystyle=(M+1)\rho-2\sqrt{\rho}\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sqrt{m^{2}+m}+\frac{1}{3}M(M+1)(M+2).

Note that

\sqrt{(m+1)(m+2)}-\sqrt{m(m-1)}>2,

for
m\geq 1
, therefore

2\sqrt{m^{2}+m}<(m+1)\sqrt{m(m+2)}-m\sqrt{(m-1)(m+1)}.

Setting
g(m)\vcentcolon=(m+1)\sqrt{m(m+2)}
, the inequality above corresponds to
2\sqrt{m^{2}+m}<g(m)-g(m-1)
. Therefore

2\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sqrt{m^{2}+m}\leq g(M)=(M+1)\sqrt{M(M+2)},

so that

\displaystyle 2I(\rho)

\displaystyle\geq(M+1)\Big(2\rho-2\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{M(M+2)}+\frac{2}{3}M(M+2)\Big)

\displaystyle=(M+1)\Big(\rho+\Big(\sqrt{\rho}-\sqrt{M(M+2)}\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{3}M(M+2)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq(M+1)\Big(\rho-\frac{1}{3}M(M+2)\Big).

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{I(\rho)}{\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(M+1)\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{3}M(M+2)\rho^{-1}\Big)=\vcentcolon f(\rho,M).

Differentiating
f
with respect to
\rho
, we find that for each fixed
M
, its unique critical point lies at
\rho=M^{2}+2M\in[M^{2}+M,(M+1)^{2}+M+1)
, and that this critical point corresponds to a local maximum. Therefore
f(\rho,M)
attains its minimum over
[M^{2}+M,(M+1)^{2}+(M+1)]
at one of the endpoints of this interval.

Note that

f(M^{2}+M,M)=\frac{2M+1}{6\sqrt{M(M+1)}},

while

f((M+1)^{2}+M+1,M)=\frac{2M+3}{6\sqrt{(M+1)(M+2)}}.

Since the function
x\mapsto\frac{1}{6}\frac{2x+1}{\sqrt{x(x+1)}}
is decreasing for
x>0
, we conclude that
f(\rho,M)
attains its minimum over
[M^{2}+M,(M+1)^{2}+M+1]
at
(M+1)^{2}+M+1
. By continuity we get that

\inf_{\rho\in[M^{2}+M,(M+1)^{2}+M+1)}\frac{I(\rho)}{\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq\frac{2M+3}{6\sqrt{(M+1)(M+2)}}.

Finally, noting that
\frac{2M+3}{6\sqrt{(M+1)(M+2)}}
is decreasing with respect to
M
, we conclude that

(21)

\inf_{\rho>0}\frac{I(\rho)}{\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{2M+3}{6\sqrt{(M+1)(M+2)}}=\frac{1}{3}.

Since

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}I(\rho(x))dx

by (
18
), from (
21
) we conclude that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}(x)dx,

completing the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

First recall (see for instance
[
4
]
) that the non-constant normalised eigenfunctions
\{y_{k}^{j\pm}\}_{j,k}
for the Dirac operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
(which form an orthonormal basis for the set of functions with zero mean) have corresponding eigenvalues

(22)

\lambda_{k}^{\pm}=\pm\left(\frac{n}{2}+k\right),\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Each of these eigenvalues has multiplicity

(23)

m_{k}\vcentcolon=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\binom{k+n-1}{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The following identity also holds for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
:

(24)

\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}|y^{j-}_{k}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}|y^{j+}_{k}(x)|^{2}=m_{k}.

As before we note that this identity differs slightly from the one present in
[
4
]
as we are considering the normalised surface measure on the sphere.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, consider the following notation for labeling these eigenfunctions and corresponding eigenvalues of the projection onto the positive spectrum of the Dirac operator with a single subscript counting multiplicities:

\{y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left\{y_{k}^{j+},\dots\right\},

and

(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left(\frac{n}{2}+k,\dots\right),

where the positive eigenvalue
\frac{n}{2}+k
is repeated
m_{k}
times.
Analogous to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we then proceed to obtain

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dEdx,

where

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}.

We also have that for a small neighbourhood
B
around
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\chi_{B}
its characteristic function, the following inequality holds:

(26)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\rho(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}+\left(\int_{B}\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since both
\chi_{B}
and
P_{E}
are projections and
\|\Gamma_{\psi}\|=1
, we find that

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}<E}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|y_{j}(x)|^{2}\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{n}{2}+k<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}|y_{k}^{j+}(x)|^{2}\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{addition_formula_sn_1}}}{{=}}|B|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{n}{2}+k<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\cdot\binom{k+n-1}{k}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}|B|2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{n!}(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n},\qquad\,\text{if }E>\frac{n}{2},\\
0,\,\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where the last inequality is justified as follows. Note that the largest integer strictly less that
E-\frac{n}{2}
is given by
\lceil E-\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
. So for
E>\frac{n}{2}
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{n}{2}+k<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\binom{k+n-1}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil E-\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}\binom{k+n-1}{k}=\binom{\lceil E-\textstyle{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil-1+n}{\lceil E-\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}((\lceil E-\textstyle{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil-1+n)\cdots(\lceil E-\frac{n}{2}\rceil))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{n(\lceil E-\frac{n}{2}\rceil)+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}{n}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\left({\lceil E-\textstyle{\frac{n}{2}}\rceil+\frac{(n-1)}{2}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{n!}\left({E-\frac{n}{2}+1+\frac{(n-1)}{2}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\left({E+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{n},

where we applied the AM-GM inequality in the third line. Next, let

(28)

C(E)\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n},\,\quad\quad\qquad\text{if }E>\frac{n}{2}\\
0,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Substituting inequality (
27
) in (
26
), dividing both sides by
|B|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and letting
|B|\to 0
, we obtain

\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}\leq(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2}+\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}(x_{0})^{1/2},

for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
where

(29)

K_{n}=2^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\frac{1}{n!}.

Since
\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}\geq 0
, this implies

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}(x_{0})\geq(\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}-(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2},

for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where the expression
(\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}-(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}
denotes the non-negative part of the difference. Integration over
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and equality (
25
) implies

(30)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\rho(x)^{1/2}-(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dEdx.

Therefore, what is left is to estimate this last integral. We proceed as follows: for
\rho>0
, define

(31)

I(\rho)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\rho^{1/2}-(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE.

Then for
\rho_{n}=\rho/K_{n}
and
I_{0}(\rho_{n})\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}({\rho_{n}}^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE
, we have that

(32)

I_{0}(\rho_{n})\cdot K_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}({\rho}^{1/2}K_{n}^{-1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE\cdot K_{n}=I(\rho).

Then since
C(E)=0
for
0<E<\frac{n}{2}
and
E\mapsto(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n}
is increasing, for
\rho>(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2})^{n}=(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle I_{0}(\rho_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{2}}({\rho_{n}}^{1/2}-0)^{2}dE+\int_{\frac{n}{2}}^{+\infty}\left({\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}-{\left(E+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)_{+}^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\rho_{n}+\int_{\frac{n}{2}}^{\sqrt[n]{{\rho_{n}}}-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}(\rho_{n}^{1/2}-(E+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{n}{2}})^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2}\rho_{n}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{n}}(n+1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(n+2)}+\frac{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\rho_{n}-\frac{(n+1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{n}}(n+1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(n+2)}+\frac{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}-\frac{\rho_{n}}{2}-\frac{(n+1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}=\vcentcolon I_{1}(\rho_{n}).

But notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{n\cdot 2^{n+1}}x^{-\frac{2n+1}{n}}+\frac{1}{2n}x^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}-\frac{(n+1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{n\cdot 2^{\frac{n}{2}}}x^{-\frac{3n+2}{2n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}x^{-\frac{3n+2}{2n}}\left(\frac{(n+1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}x^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{(n+1)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}}{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon f_{n}(x).

Hence the critical points of
\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}
are given by the roots of
f_{n}(x)
, for
x>0
. By performing the change of variables
x={u}^{2}
, finding the positive roots of
f_{n}(x)=0
amounts to solving the quadratic equation in
u
given by:

(33)

2^{n}u^{2}-2^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(n+1)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}u+(n+1)^{n+1}=0.

The roots of this equation are:

u_{\pm}=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{n+1}(\sqrt{n+1}\pm\sqrt{n}),

corresponding to the critical points

x_{\pm}=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{{n}}({n+1})(\sqrt{n+1}\pm\sqrt{n})^{2}.

Next, note that

\displaystyle f_{n}(u^{2})=\frac{1}{n}u^{-\frac{3n+2}{n}}\frac{2^{n}u^{2}-2^{\frac{n+2}{2}}(n+1)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}u+(n+1)^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}u},

hence its sign is also determined by the previous upward pointing quadratic (
33
). Substituting
u=\sqrt{(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}}
in (
33
), we obtain

\displaystyle(n+1)^{n}-(n+1)^{n+1}<0,

so
f_{n}({\textstyle{(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}}})<0
and
(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
lies between the roots
x_{\pm}
. We conclude that
f_{n}
is negative for
(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}<x<x_{+}
and positive for
x>x_{+}
, hence the point
x_{+}
corresponds to a minimum for
\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}
for
x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\inf_{\rho_{n}>(\textstyle{\frac{n+1}{2}})^{n}}\frac{I_{0}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\rho_{n}>(\textstyle{\frac{n+1}{2}})^{n}}\frac{I_{1}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{1}(x_{+})}{x_{+}^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{{n}}({n+1})(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n})^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n+1}{n}}(n+1)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{2^{\frac{n}{2}-1}(n+2)}+\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{(\left({\textstyle{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\right)^{{n}}({n+1})(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n})^{2})^{-\frac{1}{n}}}{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{(n+1)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}(\left({\textstyle{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\right)^{{n}}({n+1})(\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt{n})^{2})^{-\frac{n+1}{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}-\frac{(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2}{n}}}{{(n+1)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}}\frac{n(2\sqrt{n(n+1)}-n)}{n^{2}+3n+2}

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon c_{n}.

On the other hand, for
0<\rho_{n}\leq(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
, we have that

\displaystyle I_{0}(\rho_{n})/(\rho_{n})^{\frac{n+1}{n}}=\frac{n}{2}\rho_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\geq\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{-1}=\frac{n}{n+1}.

Note that
c_{n}<\frac{n}{n+1}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, this is equivalent to proving that
c_{n}-\frac{n}{n+1}<0
, or equivalently, that

S(n)<\frac{n}{n+1}-\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+3n+2}=\frac{2n}{n^{2}+3n+2},

where

S(n)=-\frac{(\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2}{n}}}{{(n+1)^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}}\frac{n(2\sqrt{n(n+1)}-n)}{n^{2}+3n+2}.

Therefore it is sufficient to prove that
S(n)
is always negative. But this is clear since
\sqrt{n(n+1)}>n
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Consequently, we have that

(34)

\displaystyle I_{0}(\rho_{n})/(\rho_{n})^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\geq\min\left\{c_{n},\frac{n}{n+1}\right\}=c_{n},

for every
\rho_{n}>0
, from which it follows that

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{I(\rho)}{\rho^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}=\frac{I_{0}(\rho_{n})\cdot K_{n}}{(\rho_{n})^{\frac{n+1}{n}}\cdot(K_{n})^{\frac{n+1}{n}}}\geq c_{n}\cdot(K_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n}},

for every
\rho>0
.
Therefore, from equations (
30
), (
31
), (
34
), (
35
) and the definition of
K_{n}
, we obtain that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\Lambda^{+}(\not{D})\psi_{j},\psi_{j}\rangle\geq c_{n}\cdot{{n!}^{\frac{1}{n}}2^{-\frac{1}{n}\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+1}{n}}(x)dx,

concluding the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

The proof follows an argument analogous to the proof of Theorem
3.2
, by performing the following adaptations:

Consider the following alternative notation for labeling the non-constant eigenfunctions and corresponding eigenvalues of the Dirac operator with a single subscript counting multiplicities:

\{y_{j}^{\pm}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left\{y_{k}^{j\pm},\dots\right\},

and

(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}=\left(\frac{n}{2}+k,\dots\right),

where the positive eigenvalue
\frac{n}{2}+k
is repeated
m_{k}
times, and
m_{k}
is given as in (
23
).
Once again, for
E\geq 0
consider the corresponding spectral projections:

P_{E}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}<E}[\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{+}\rangle y_{j}^{+}+\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{-}\rangle y_{j}^{-}]

and

P_{E}^{\perp}=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\geq E}[\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{+}\rangle y_{j}^{+}+\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{-}\rangle y_{j}^{-}],

so that

(36)

\dot{\not{D}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}[\lambda_{j}\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{+}\rangle y_{j}^{+}-\lambda_{j}\langle\cdot,y_{j}^{-}\rangle y_{j}^{-}],

where
\dot{\not{D}}=P_{\lambda_{1}}^{\perp}\circ\not{D}\circ P_{\lambda_{1}}^{\perp}.
denotes the restriction of
\dot{\not{D}}
to the invariant subspace of functions orthogonal to constants.

Thus, analogously as before, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\not{D}\psi,\not{D}\psi\rangle=2\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}E\cdot|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2}dEdx.

Once again, with
\Gamma_{\psi}\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\cdot,\psi_{j}\rangle\psi_{j},

we obtain

(37)

\displaystyle\|\not{D}\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{0,d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}E\cdot\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dEdx,

where

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|P_{E}^{\perp}\psi_{j}(x)|^{2},
and for any small neighbourhood
B
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B}\rho(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\Gamma_{\psi}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}+\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}

(38)

\displaystyle=\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}+\left(\int_{B}\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}^{\perp}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\chi_{B}
denotes the characteristic function of
B
.
Then, similarly to the previous proof, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{\psi}P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{E}\chi_{B}\|_{HS}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}<E}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(|y_{j}^{+}(x)|^{2}+|y_{j}^{-}(x)|^{2})\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{n}{2}+k<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{m_{k}}(|y_{k}^{j+}(x)|^{2}+|y_{k}^{j-}(x)|^{2})\chi_{B}(x)dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{addition_formula_sn_1}}}{{=}}|B|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{n}{2}+k<E\\
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\end{subarray}}2^{(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)}\cdot\binom{k+n-1}{k}

(39)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}|B|2^{(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)}\frac{1}{n!}(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n},\qquad\,\text{if }E>\frac{n}{2},\\
0,\,\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\rho_{P_{E}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{E}^{\perp}}(x_{0})\geq(\rho(x_{0})^{1/2}-(K_{n}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2},

for almost every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where the
K_{n}^{\prime}=2^{(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)}\frac{1}{n!}
and
E\mapsto C(E)
is given by (
28
). Integrating the expression above, equality (
37
) then implies that

(40)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\not{D}\psi_{j},\not{D}\psi_{j}\rangle\geq 2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}E\cdot(\rho(x)^{1/2}-(K_{n}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dEdx.

In order to estimate this integral, let

(41)

I(\rho)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}E\cdot(\rho^{1/2}-(K_{n}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE.

Then putting
\rho_{n}=\rho/K_{n}^{\prime}
it is enough to estimate
I_{0}(\rho_{n})\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\infty}E\cdot({\rho_{n}}^{1/2}-C(E)^{1/2})_{+}^{2}dE
.

Since
C(E)=0
for
0<E<\frac{n}{2}
, and
E\mapsto(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n}
is increasing, for
\rho_{n}>(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2})^{n}=(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle I_{0}(\rho_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{2}}E\cdot({\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}-0)^{2}dE+\int_{\frac{n}{2}}^{+\infty}E\cdot\left({\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}-{\left(E+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)_{+}^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{8}\rho_{n}+\int_{\frac{n}{2}}^{\sqrt[n]{{\rho_{n}}}-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}E\rho_{n}-2\rho_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}E(E+{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}})^{\frac{n}{2}}+E(E+\frac{1}{2})^{n}dE

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{n}}{8}+\frac{n^{2}\rho_{n}}{2(n+2)}\left(\frac{\rho_{n}^{2/n}}{n+4}-\frac{\rho_{n}^{1/n}}{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\rho_{n}}\left(\frac{2}{n+4}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+4}{2}}-\frac{1}{n+2}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(n+1)^{n}(n^{2}+n-1)}{2^{n+2}(n+2)}

\displaystyle=\vcentcolon I_{1}(\rho_{n}).

Setting

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n+4}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+4}{2}}-\frac{1}{n+2}\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}},

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)^{n}(n^{2}+n-1)}{2^{n+2}(n+2)},

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+2)(n+4)}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+1)(n+2)}x^{-\frac{1}{n}}+\frac{1}{8}x^{-\frac{2}{n}}+2A_{n}x^{-\frac{n+4}{2n}}-B_{n}x^{-\frac{n+2}{n}},

for every
x>0
. Let
y=x^{-\frac{1}{n}}
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=\frac{I_{1}(y(x))}{y(x)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}y^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+1)(n+2)}y+\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+2)(n+4)}+2A_{n}y^{\frac{n+4}{2}}-B_{n}y^{n+2}

\displaystyle=q(y)+R(y),

where
q(y)
corresponds to the quadratic

q(y)=\frac{1}{8}y^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+1)(n+2)}y+\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+2)(n+4)},

while
R(y)
corresponds to the remainder

R(y)=2A_{n}y^{\frac{n+4}{2}}-B_{n}y^{n+2}.

Note that
q(y)
attains a global minimum at
y_{0}=\frac{2n^{2}}{(n+1)(n+2)}
. For
n=1
,
y_{0}=1/3
corresponds to
x=3>((1+1)/2)^{1}=1
. For
n\geq 2
, note that
x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
if and only if
y\in(0,\frac{2}{n+1})
. Moreover,

\frac{2n^{2}}{(n+1)(n+2)}=\frac{2}{(n+1)}\frac{n^{2}}{(n+2)}\geq\frac{2}{(n+1)},

therefore
q(y(x))
has a global minimum for
x_{0}<(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
, and since
q
is an upward pointing parabola, we conclude that

\inf_{x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}}q(y(x))=\begin{cases}q\Big(y\Big({(\textstyle{\frac{n+1}{2}}})^{n}\Big)\Big)=\frac{n^{4}-6n^{2}+6n+8}{2n^{4}+16n^{3}+42n^{2}+44n+16},&\text{ if }n\geq 2,\\
q(1/3)=\frac{7}{360}&\text{ if }n=1.\end{cases}

Next note that
R(y)>0
for every
x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
. Indeed, this is equivalent to
2A_{n}-B_{n}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}>0
for all such
x
. But since
x^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is decreasing and

\displaystyle 2A_{n}-B_{n}\left(\Big(\frac{n+1}{2}\Big)^{n}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{2}}\frac{n^{2}+2n-2}{(n+2)(n+4)}-\frac{(n+1)^{\frac{n}{2}}(n^{2}+n-1)}{2^{\frac{n}{2}+2}(n+2)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{1}{(n+2)}\left(\frac{(n+1)(n^{2}+2n-2)}{n+4}-\frac{n^{2}+n-1}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\frac{1}{(n+2)}\left(\frac{3n^{3}+7n^{2}-3n-4}{4(n+4)}\right),

and
7n^{2}\geq 3n+4
for every
n\geq 2
, we conclude that
R(y)>0
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
(the case
n=1
is also true by inspection). Consequently,

(42)

\inf_{x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}}\frac{I_{1}(x)}{x^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}\geq\inf_{x>(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}}q(y(x))=\begin{cases}\frac{n^{4}-6n^{2}+6n+8}{2n^{4}+16n^{3}+42n^{2}+44n+16},&\text{ if }n\geq 2,\\
\frac{7}{360}&\text{ if }n=1.\end{cases}

Similarly, for
0<\rho_{n}\leq(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
we have that

\displaystyle I_{0}(\rho_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{n}{2}}E\cdot({\rho_{n}}^{\frac{1}{2}}-0)^{2}dE

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{8}\rho_{n},

so

\frac{I_{0}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=\frac{n^{2}}{8}\rho_{n}^{-\frac{2}{n}}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+1)^{2}},

for
0<\rho_{n}\leq(\frac{n+1}{2})^{n}
. One can verify that the lower bound obtained in (
42
) is smaller than
\frac{n^{2}}{2(n+1)^{2}}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore,

\frac{I_{0}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}\geq c^{\prime}_{n}\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}\frac{n^{4}-6n^{2}+6n+8}{2n^{4}+16n^{3}+42n^{2}+44n+16},&\text{ if }n\geq 2,\\
\frac{7}{360}&\text{ if }n=1.\end{cases},

for all
\rho_{n}>0
. Hence,

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{I(\rho)}{\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=\frac{I_{0}(\rho_{n})\cdot K_{n}^{\prime}}{\rho_{n}^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\cdot(K_{n}^{\prime})^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}\geq c^{\prime}_{n}(K_{n}^{\prime})^{-\frac{2}{n}},

for every
\rho>0
. From (
40
) and (
41
) we conclude that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle\not{D}\psi_{j},\not{D}\psi_{j}\rangle\geq c^{\prime}_{n}n!^{\frac{2}{n}}2^{1-\frac{2}{n}(\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}(x)dx,

completing the proof.

∎
