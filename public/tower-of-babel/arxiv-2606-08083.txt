Positive Instantial Neighbourhood logic
Litan Kumar Das, Anupam Khanra, Sujit Kumar Sardar

Abstract
Instantial neighbourhood logic is a modal language for neighbourhood frames in which formulas can express information about the kinds of worlds occurring inside a neighbourhood of a given world. In this paper, we study a positive, negation-free version of instantial neighbourhood logic with two primitive instantial modalities, one of box-type and one of diamond-type. Since classical negation is not available, the two modalities are treated independently. We introduce the language and proof system of positive instantial neighbourhood logic (PINL) and interpret it over persistent two-sided neighbourhood models. We then define a typed persistent neighbourhood semantics, used as an auxiliary canonical semantics to control witness and co-witness conditions. This yields a truth lemma and the corresponding completeness result of PINL. On the algebraic side, we introduce 2 2 - DLIO \mathrm{DLIO} s, bounded distributive lattices equipped with two families of instantial operations, as the algebraic semantics of PINL. We prove algebraic soundness and completeness via the Lindenbaum 2 2 - DLIO \mathrm{DLIO} . Finally, we construct the canonical bitopological PINL-space and show that the algebra of its admissible positive opens is isomorphic to the Lindenbaum 2 2 - DLIO \mathrm{DLIO} . Thus the paper gives a canonical admissible-open representation of positive instantial neighbourhood logic, providing a first step toward a future duality theory.

Introduction

Positive logic is the negation-free fragment of propositional logic, constructed from propositional variables, the constants
\top
and
\bot
, and the lattice connectives
\wedge
and
\vee
. It may also be viewed as the finitary part of geometric logic
vickers1989topology
, where finite conjunctions and finite disjunctions are allowed. Algebraically, this positive base is naturally interpreted in bounded distributive lattices. Positive modal logic, initiated by Dunn
dunn1995positive
, extends positive propositional logic by adding two normal modal operators,
\Box
and
\Diamond
, while still avoiding classical negation. Subsequent research produced relational, algebraic, and duality-theoretic semantics for positive modal logics, including Priestley-style dualities and coalgebraic approaches; see, for example,
celani1997new
;
celani1999priestley
;
celani2012note
;
gehrke2005sahlqvist
;
kikot2018strictly
;
palmigiano2004coalgebraic
;
sadrzadeh2010positive
). More recently, de Groot studied positive monotone modal logic
de2021positive
as the positive fragment of monotone modal logic and proved a duality between suitable neighbourhood spaces and distributive lattices with monotone operators. These works show that positive modal logics are naturally connected with ordered spaces, distributive lattices, and coalgebraic duality.

Instantial neighbourhood logic was introduced in
van2017instantial
as a modal language for neighbourhood frames. Its modalities can express not only that a neighbourhood satisfies a scope condition, but also that it contains points satisfying specified instance formulas. A coalgebraic duality for INL was later developed in
bezhanishvili2020duality
. In that work, descriptive INL-frames were shown to be dual to Boolean algebras with instantial operators(
\mathrm{BAIO}
). The same paper also points to positive INL as a natural related formalism, where Boolean algebras are replaced by distributive lattices with instantial operators. It further suggests a geometric version of INL, guided by the slogan that geometric INL should arise from positive INL by adding Scott-continuity. This connects the present direction with coalgebraic geometric logic and with point-free topological approaches to modal logic
bezhanishvili2022coalgebraic
;
vickers2004double
;
vickers1989topology
.

The above literature leaves a natural gap. Classical INL and its coalgebraic duality are well developed, but a systematic positive, negation free version of INL still requires a proof system, a suitable positive neighbourhood semantics, an algebraic semantics over distributive lattices, and a canonical representation of the resulting algebra. The present paper addresses this gap.

We develop positive instantial neighbourhood logic, abbreviated PINL. The language has two primitive instantial modalities: one of
\Box
-type and one of
\Diamond
-type. Since negation is not available, the two modalities are not defined from one another. Instead, they are treated independently. This leads to a two-sided semantic and algebraic setting: the
\Box
-type modality is governed by witness conditions, while the
\Diamond
-type modality is governed by co-witness conditions.

The typed semantics used in this paper is an auxiliary canonical semantics. The typed semantics is used only as an auxiliary tool for the canonical construction; it is not intended to be a separate final semantics for PINL. In particular, formula labels keep track of which modal formula a finite neighbourhood set is intended to witness or to refute as a co-witness. This prevents an extensional neighbourhood set chosen for one modal formula from being incorrectly used for another modal formula. The typed construction is then converted, in the final representation section, into an admissible-open representation.

The main contributions of the study are as follows. First, we define the language and proof system of PINL and prove a canonical completeness using typed persistent neighbourhood semantics. Second, we introduce
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s as the algebraic semantics of PINL and show that the Lindenbaum algebra of PINL is a
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
. Third, we construct the canonical bitopological PINL-space from prime theory pairs. In this bitopological setting, the positive topology is generated by the sets
U_{\varphi}
, which record membership of formulas in the theory component of prime pairs. The negative topology is generated by the sets
V_{\varphi}
, which record membership of formulas in the counter-theoretic component. Finally, we prove that the algebra of admissible positive opens of this canonical bitopological space is isomorphic to the Lindenbaum
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
of PINL.

The scope of the paper is intentionally limited. We prove a canonical representation theorem, not a categorical duality theorem. A Priestley-style or Vietoris style duality for
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s would require a suitable category of descriptive PINL-space, appropriate morphisms, and a functorial treatment of the instantial neighbourhood structure. This is left for future work.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect the basic order-theoretic, algebraic, and semantics notions used throughout the paper. Since our aim is to develop a positive version of instantial neighbourhood logic, the propositional base will be distributive-lattice logic rather than classical propositional logic, and the modal semantics will be formulated over ordered two-sided neighbourhood structures. This choice is in line with the general positive-logic perspective, where one works without negation, any implication and treats derivability in consequence form, and it is also consistent with the positive direction suggested for INL in the literature on distributive lattices with instantial operators(see
bezhanishvili2020duality
).

Posets and upward closed sets

A partially ordered set is a pair
(W,\leq)
, where
\leq
is reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive. For
X\subseteq W
, we write up-set(*) of
X
as

\uparrow X=\{w\in W:\exists x\in X\text{ such that }x\leq w\}.

A subset
U\subseteq W
is called an upset or upward closed if
u\in U
and
u\leq v
then
v\in U
. Let
Up(W,\leq)
denote the set of all upsets of
W
.

Distributive lattice background

A bounded distributive lattice is an algebra

(D,\vee,\wedge,\bot,\top)

such taht
(D,\vee,\wedge)
is a distributive lattice with least element
\bot
and greatest element
\top
. The lattice order
\leq
is defined as

a\leq b\iff a\wedge b=a\iff a\vee b=b.

Since our logic is positive, the propositional fragment is based on distributive-lattice logic. Thus instead of taking all classical propositional logic axioms, we take as the propositional base the valid laws of bounded distributive lattices. This is exactly the positive analogue of the classical propositional basis, and it matches the Dunn’s general positive modal logic view point(
dunn1995positive
), where the underlying algebraic semantics is distributive-lattice based rather than Boolean.

Background on Instantial Neighbourhood Logic

We briefly recall the classical language of Instantial neighbourhood logic, following
van2017instantial
. Let
\mathsf{Prop}
be an arbitrary but fixed set of propositional letters. The language
\mathcal{L}_{INL}
of INL is generated recursively by

\varphi::=\top\mid p\mid\neg\varphi\mid(\varphi\wedge\varphi)\mid\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi),\quad p\in\mathsf{Prop},\ n\in\omega.

When
n=0
, we write simply
\Box(;\psi)
.
As usual, the connectives
\bot
,
\vee
,
\to
, and
\leftrightarrow
are defined from
\neg
and
\wedge
in the standard way.

A neighbourhood frame is a pair
\mathfrak{F}=(W,N)
, where
W
is a non-empty
set and
N:W\to\mathcal{P}\mathcal{P}(W)
is a neighbourhood function, where
\mathcal{P}(W)
is a power set. A
valuation on
W
is a map
V:\mathsf{Prop}\to\mathcal{P}(W)
. A neighbourhood
model is a triple
\mathfrak{M}=(W,N,V)
.

The truth of INL-formulas is defined inductively in the usual way for the
propositional connectives. For the instantial modal formula,

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\varphi)

iff there exists
S\in N(w)
such that every point of
S
satisfies

\varphi
, and for each
i\leq n
there exists some
s_{i}\in S
such that

\mathfrak{M},s_{i}\models\psi_{i}.

Equivalently, if
\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}
denotes the truth
set of a formula
\chi
, then

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\varphi)

iff there exists
S\in N(w)
such that

S\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\quad\text{and}\quad S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\neq\varnothing\ \text{for each }i\leq n.

Thus, the INL-modality expresses that there is a neighbourhood of the current
world whose elements all satisfy the scope formula
\varphi
, while each witness
formula
\psi_{i}
is realized somewhere inside that neighbourhood.

Instantial neighbourhood logic
van2017instantial
was introduced to enrich neighbourhood semantics by allowing formulas to express not only universal information about a neighbourhood, but also existential information about the different kinds of worlds occurring inside it.
The INL study
van2017instantial
emphasizes that ordinary neighbourhood semantics naturally supports a quantifier pattern of the form
\exists\forall
, while the instantial language refines this by tracking which kinds of worlds occur in the neighbourhood. It also explains that the completeness proof for classical INL proceeds by normal forms and matching canonical models.

This motivates the present positive setting. In our framework, we isolate two primitive instantial modal connectives, one of
\Box
-type and one of
\Diamond
-type, and study them independently over ordered neighbourhood structures. Since we do not assume negation, the system should be understood as a positive two-sided instantial neighbourhood logic rather than as a fragment of classical INL.

3
Positive Instantial Neighbourhood logic: syntax and semantics

In this section, we introduce the positive version of instantial neighbourhood logic. Classical INL extends propositional logic by an instantial modality interpreted over neighbourhood structures. In the present setting, however, we work over a positive propositional base and treat the two instantial modalities as independent primitive modal connectives. More precisely, we replace the classical propositional language by the language of bounded distributive lattices, and we interpret the resulting
modal language over persistent two-sided neighbourhood structures. One
neighbourhood component governs witness behaviour for the
\Box
-type
connective, while the other governs co-witness behaviour for the

\Diamond
-type connective. This separation is natural in the positive setting, since in the absence of negation the two modal connectives are no longer interdefinable.

3.1
The language of Positive INL

Let
\mathsf{Prop}
be an arbitrary but fixed set of propositional variables. The language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
of positive instantial neighbourhood logic is defined recursively by

\varphi::=\top\mid\bot\mid p\mid(\varphi\wedge\varphi)\mid(\varphi\vee\varphi)\mid\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\mid\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),

where
p\in\mathsf{Prop}
and
n\in\omega
.
When
n=0
, we write simply
\Box(\psi)
and
\Diamond(\psi)
.

Thus Positive INL is obtained from the classical instantial framework by replacing the negation-based propositional language with the positive language of bounded distributive lattices, and by taking two primitive instantial connectives, one of box-type and one of diamond-type, instead of relying on definability by negation.

Persistent two-sided neighbourhood frames and models

Definition 1
.

A persistent two-sided neighbourhood frame is a structure

\mathfrak{F}=(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond}),

where

(i)

(W,\leq)
is a partially ordered set and

N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond}:W\to\mathcal{P}(Up(W,\leq))

are neighbourhood functions, where
\mathcal{P}(Up(W,\leq))
is the power set of
Up(W,\leq)
. Thus, for each
w\in W
,

N^{\Box}(w)\subseteq Up(W,\leq)\text{ and }N^{\Diamond}(w)\subseteq Up(W,\leq);

(ii)

for every
w\leq v
,

N^{\Box}(w)\subseteq N^{\Box}(v)\,\text{ and }\,N^{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N^{\Diamond}(w).

Thus every
\Box
-neighbourhood and every
\Diamond
-neighbourhood is an upset, the
\Box
-side neighbourhood assignment is monotone upward and the
\Diamond
-side neighbourhood assignment is monotone downward.

Definition 2
.

A persistent two-sided neighbourhood model is a tuple

\mathfrak{M}=(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond},V),

where
(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond})
is a persistent two-sided neighbourhood frame and

V:\mathsf{Prop}\to Up(W,\leq)

is a valuation assigning to each propositional variable an upset of
W
.

Witness and co-witness

We recall the notion of witness and co-witness for neighbourhoods, following
bezhanishvili2020duality
.

Let
A_{1},....,A_{n},B\subseteq W
, and let
u\subseteq W
. We say that
u
witness
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B)
if

u\subseteq B\text{ and }u\cap A_{i}\neq\varnothing\;\text{ for each }i=1,\ldots,n.

We say that
u
co-witnesses
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B)
if

u\subseteq A_{i}\;\text{ for some }i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\;\text{ or }u\cap B\neq\varnothing.

3.2
Truth of PINL-formulas

Definition 3

(Truth of PINL-formula)
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond},V)
be a persistent two-sided neighbourhood model, let
w\in W
, and let
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
. We recursively define when
\mathfrak{M}
satisfies
\varphi
at the state
w\in W
, written

\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi

as follows:

(i)

for propositional variables and lattice connectives:

\mathfrak{M},w\models p\iff w\in V(p),

\mathfrak{M},w\models\top\;\text{ always },\mathfrak{M},w\models\bot\;\text{ never },

\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi\wedge\psi\iff\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi\;\text{ and }\;\mathfrak{M},w\models\psi,

\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi\vee\psi\iff\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi\;\text{ or }\;\mathfrak{M},w\models\psi.

(ii)

for the modal formulas:

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)

\quad\text{ iff}\quad\text{there exists }U\in N^{\Box}(w)\text{ such that for all }u\in U,\;\;\mathfrak{M},u\models\varphi,

\qquad\qquad\text{and for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{ there exists }u_{i}\in U\text{ such that }\mathfrak{M},u_{i}\models\varphi_{i},

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)

\quad\text{iff}\quad\text{for every }S\in N^{\Diamond}(w),\text{ either there exists }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{ such that }

\qquad\qquad\text{for all }s\in S\text{ we have }\mathfrak{M},s\models\varphi_{i},\text{ or there exists }s\in S\text{ such that }\mathfrak{M},s\models\varphi.

For each formula
\chi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
, we write

\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=\{w\in W:\mathfrak{M},w\models\chi\}

for the truth set of
\chi
in
\mathfrak{M}
.

Thus

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\iff\exists\;u\in N^{\Box}(w)\text{ such that }u\text{ witnesses }(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}};\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}).

Further,

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\iff\forall u\in N^{\Diamond}(w)\text{ co-witnesses}(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}};\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}).

Persistence of truth

The prsistence conditions imposed on the frame ensure that truth is monotone upward in the order.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond},V)
be a persistent two-sided neighbourhood model. Then for every formula
\varphi
, the truth set
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}
is an upset of
W
. Equivalently, if
w\leq v
and
\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi
, then
\mathfrak{M},v\models\varphi
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the complexity of
\varphi
. The propositional cases are immediate from the fact that valuations are upward closed and the lattice operations preserve upward closure.

Suppose

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\text{ and }w\leq v.

Then there exists
u\in N^{\Box}(w)
such that

u\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\text{ and }u\cap\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\neq\varnothing\text{ for each}i=1,2,\ldots,n.

Since
N^{\Box}(w)\subseteq N^{\Box}(v)
,
u\in N^{\Box}(v)
. Because
\psi
is a subformula of
\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
, by the induction hypothesis we have
\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}
is upward closed. Similarly, for each
i
, since
\varphi_{i}
is a subformula, by the induction hypothesis
\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}
is upward closed. Hence
u
witnesses

(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}};\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}).

Therefore

\mathfrak{M},v\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

Now suppose

\mathfrak{M},w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\text{ and }w\leq v.

Let
u\in N^{\Diamond}(v)
. Since
N^{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N^{\Diamond}(w)
, we also have
u\in N^{\Diamond}(w)
. Because
\mathfrak{M},w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
, the set
u
co-witnesses

(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}},\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}};\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}).

Therefore same is true when evaluated at
v
, and hence

\mathfrak{M},v\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

This completes the induction.
∎

4
A Proof System for Positive INL

In this section, we introduce the deductive system for positive instantial neighbourhood logic. Since the propositional base is positive and no implication is taken as primitive, it is most natural to formulate the system in entailment form rather than as a classical Hilbert system. Thus the formal objects of the claculus are sequents

\varphi\vdash\psi,

to be read as saying that
\varphi
entails
\psi
. We write
\varphi\dashv\vdash\psi
to mean

\varphi\vdash\psi\text{ and }\psi\vdash\varphi.

A PINL formula
\varphi
is called a theorem if the sequent
\top\vdash\varphi
is derivable in the system, denoted by
\vdash\varphi
.

The proof system consists of three parts:

1.

propositional axioms, given by the axioms of bounded distributive lattices;

2.

modal axioms for
\Box
;

3.

modal axioms for
\Diamond
.

The systems also contains the structural rules listed below.

Structural rules

We take the following rules of inference.

(CUT)

\frac{\varphi\vdash\psi\quad\;\psi\vdash\chi}{\varphi\vdash\chi}

(US) Uniform substitution

\frac{\varphi\vdash\psi}{\sigma(\varphi)\vdash\sigma(\psi)}

where
\sigma
is a uniform substitutation of formulas for propositional variables.

(RE) Replacement of equivalents

\frac{\alpha\dashv\vdash\beta\quad\;\varphi\vdash\psi}{\varphi[\alpha/\beta]\vdash\psi[\alpha/\beta]}

where
\varphi[\alpha/\beta]
denotes the result of replacing occurrences of
\alpha
in
\varphi
by
\beta
.

Propositional axioms

The propositional base of the system is the positive consequence relation of bounded distributive lattices. Thus the system contains, as axioms schemeta, all bounded distributive lattice entailments. In particular, the following are available:

(i)

reflexitivity:

\varphi\vdash\varphi;

(ii)

commutativity:

\varphi\wedge\psi\dashv\vdash\psi\wedge\varphi,\quad\;\varphi\vee\psi\dashv\vdash\psi\vee\varphi;

(iii)

associativity:

(\varphi\wedge\psi)\wedge\chi\dashv\vdash\varphi\wedge(\psi\wedge\chi),\quad\;(\varphi\vee\psi)\vee\chi\dashv\vdash\varphi\vee(\psi\vee\chi);

(iv)

idempotence:

\varphi\wedge\varphi\dashv\vdash\varphi,\quad\varphi\vee\varphi\dashv\vdash\varphi;

(v)

absorption:

\varphi\wedge(\varphi\vee\psi)\dashv\vdash\varphi,\quad\;\varphi\vee(\varphi\wedge\psi)\dashv\vdash\varphi;

(vi)

distributivity:

\varphi\wedge(\psi\vee\chi)\dashv\vdash(\varphi\wedge\psi)\vee(\varphi\wedge\chi),\quad\;\varphi\vee(\psi\wedge\chi)\dashv\vdash(\varphi\vee\psi)\wedge(\varphi\vee\chi);

(vii)

bounds:

\displaystyle\bot\vdash\varphi,\quad\;\varphi\vdash\top

\displaystyle\varphi\wedge\top\dashv\vdash\varphi,\quad\;\varphi\vee\bot\dashv\vdash\varphi,

\displaystyle\varphi\wedge\bot\dashv\vdash\bot,\quad\;\varphi\vee\top\dashv\vdash\top.

(viii)

conjunction elimination:

\varphi\wedge\psi\vdash\varphi,\quad\;\varphi\wedge\psi\vdash\psi;

(ix)

disjunction introduction:

\varphi\vdash\varphi\vee\psi,\quad\;\psi\vdash\varphi\vee\psi.

The following rules are admissible in the bounded distributive lattice fragment.

(i)

Conjunction introduction:

\frac{\alpha\vdash\beta\quad\;\alpha\vdash\gamma}{\alpha\vdash\beta\wedge\gamma};

(ii)

Disjunction elimination

\frac{\beta\vdash\alpha\quad\;\gamma\vdash\alpha}{\beta\vee\gamma\vdash\alpha};

(iii)

Cut:

\frac{\varphi\wedge\alpha\vdash\psi\quad\;\varphi\vdash\psi\vee\alpha}{\varphi\vdash\psi}.

Modal axioms for
\Box

The modal axioms for
\Box
are the following.

(i)

(
\Box
1 ): for each
k\leq n
,

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k-1},\bot,\varphi_{k+1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\bot,

(ii)

(
\Box
2) Permutation invariance: for every permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\dashv\vdash\Box(\varphi_{\pi}(1),\ldots,\varphi_{\pi}(n);\psi),

(iii)

(
\Box
3) Join-preservation in each left-side coordinate: for each
k\leq n
,

\displaystyle\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k-1},\alpha\vee\beta,\varphi_{k+1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)

\displaystyle\dashv\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k-1},\alpha,\varphi_{k+1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vee\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{k-1},\beta,\varphi_{k+1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),

(iv)

(
\Box
4) Meet-subadditivity in the scope coordinate:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\chi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\wedge\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi),

(v)

(
\Box
5) Scope absorption in a left coordinate:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n-1},\varphi_{n}\wedge\psi;\psi),

(vi)

(
\Box
6) Positive cover axiom: if

\top\dashv\vdash\gamma\vee\delta,

then

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi)\vee\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\delta),

(vii)

(
\Box
7) Weakening in arity:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n+1};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),

(viii)

(
\Box
8) Duplication:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n};\psi).

\Diamond

are dual in spirit, but adapted to the universal co-witness semantics.

(i)

()
\top
in a left coordinate: for each

(ii)

() Permutation invariance: for every permutation

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\dashv\vdash\Diamond(\varphi_{\pi(1)},\ldots,\varphi_{\pi(n)};\psi),

() Meet-preservation in each left coordinate: for each

(iv)

() Meet-subadditivity in the scope coordinate:

(vi)

(vii)

(viii)

In
\mathrm{PINL}

5
Soundness and Completeness for PINL

In this section, we study the relation between the proof system introduced in Section

4
and the semantics of positive instantial neighbourhood logic. The soundness part is
established with respect to the persistent two-sided neighbourhood model introduced
in Section
3
. For the completeness of PINL, however, we follow the standard positive
canonical strategy in the sense of Dunn’s positive modal logic
dunn1995positive
. More precisely,
since the PINL is formulated without negation, the canonical construction is based
not on maximally consistent theories, but on theories, counter-theories, and disjoint
theory pairs. In this setting, disjoint theory pairs play the role usually played by
consistent theories, while maximally disjoint theory pairs play the role of maximally
consistent sets. Moreover, every disjoint theory pair extends to a maximally disjoint
one, equivalently to a prime theory pair.
Methodologically, therefore,the completeness proof follows the Dunn-style positive
Henkin construction, but it is adapted to the present two-sided instantial neigh-
bourhood setting. At the same time, the extensional nature of persistent two-sided
neighbourhood semantics creates a specific obstacle for a direct canonical truth
lemma, and for this reason the completeness theorem below is formulated with respect
to an auxiliary typed persistent semantics. Our study already record this as the
appropriate intermediate setting for the canonical argument.

5.1
Soundness for persistent two-sided neighbourhood models

We now verify that the proof system for
\mathrm{PINL}
is sound for the persistent semantics.

Theorem 1
.

For all formulas
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
,

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi\Longrightarrow\models(\varphi\vdash\psi).

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the derivation of

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Consider an arbitrary persistent two-sided neighbourhood model
\mathfrak{M}
. We show that

\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\psi).

Since
\mathfrak{M}
was arbitrary, that will give
\models(\varphi\vdash\psi)
.

Propositional axioms

These are valid because in every model:

\displaystyle\llbracket\alpha\wedge\beta\rrbracket=\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\beta\rrbracket,\;\llbracket\alpha\vee\beta\rrbracket=\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\beta\rrbracket,

\displaystyle\llbracket\bot\rrbracket=\varnothing,\;\llbracket\top\rrbracket=W.

Hence every distributive-lattice law corresponds to a correct inclusion/equality between sets.

For example, the axiom
\bot\vdash\varphi
is valid because
\varnothing\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
. Similarly,
\varphi\wedge\psi\vdash\varphi
is valid because
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
.

Modal axioms for
\Box

Recall the semantics:

w\models\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\varphi)\iff\exists S\in N^{\Box}(w)(S\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\&\forall i\leq n:S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket\neq\varnothing).

We verify the
\Box
-axioms.

(i)

(\Box 1)
:
\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\vdash\bot
.

Assume
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\bot,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\vdash\bot
. Then there is
S\in N^{\Box}(w)
such that
S\cap\llbracket\bot\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
. But
\llbracket\bot\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
, impossible.

So no state/world satisfies
\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\bot,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
; i.e., its truth set is empty. Hence

\llbracket\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\bot,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\rrbracket=\varnothing\subseteq\llbracket\bot\rrbracket=\varnothing.

So the axiom is valid.

(ii)

(\Box 2)
: This is valid because the condition
\forall i,S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
does not depend on the order of the
\psi_{i}
.

(iii)

(\Box 3)
:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha\vee\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\dashv\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\vee\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi).

•

(\Rightarrow)
Assume
w\in W
. If
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha\vee\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
, there is
S\in N^{\Box}(w)
such that
S\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
with

S\cap(\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\beta\rrbracket)\neq\varnothing.

Hence
S\cap\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
or
S\cap\llbracket\beta\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
. In the first case, the same
S
witnesses
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
; in the second case, it witnesses
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)

w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\vee\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
.

•

(\Leftarrow)
If
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
, witnessed by some

, then

implies
S\cap(\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\beta\rrbracket)\neq\varnothing
, so
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha\vee\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
. Similarly if
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
then
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha\vee\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
. Thus the axiom
(\Box 3)
is valid.

(iv)

(\Box 4)

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\chi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\wedge\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi).

Fix
w\in W
and assume
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\chi)
. Then there is some
S\in N^{\Box}(w)
witnessed
(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket,\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\rrbracket;\llbracket\psi\wedge\chi\rrbracket)

S\subseteq\llbracket\psi\wedge\chi\rrbracket
, from this we have
S\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket
and
S\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket
. The witness-interaction conditions with left-coordinates are unchanged. Hence
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\wedge\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)
.

(v)

(\Box 5)

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\wedge\psi;\psi).

Fix
w\in W
and assume
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)
. Then there exists
S\in N^{\Box}(w)
such that
S\subseteq\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket
and
\forall i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
S\cap\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
. Let
x\in S\cap\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
. Since

, we also have

. Hence
x\in\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket=\llbracket\varphi_{i}\wedge\varphi\rrbracket
. So
S\cap\llbracket\varphi_{i}\wedge\varphi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
. Thus the same
S

(\llbracket\varphi_{1}\rrbracket,\ldots,\llbracket\varphi_{n}\wedge\varphi\rrbracket;\varphi)
. So the entailment is valid.

(vi)

(\Box 6)
Assume

, so semantically:

\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\delta\rrbracket=W.

We show:

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vee\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\delta).

Fix
w\in W
and assume
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
. Choose a witness
S\in N^{\Box}(w)
such that

S\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\;\text{ and }\forall iS\cap\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\neq\varnothing.

Now if
S\cap\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
, then
w\models\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi)
. If
S\cap\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket=\varnothing
then for every

, we have

. Since

, it follows

. Hence

. Together with

(vii)

Analogously both

are valid axioms.

Modal axioms for
\Diamond

Recall the semantics:

w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\iff\forall T\in N^{\Diamond}(w)((\exists i\leq n:T\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket)\text{ or }(T\cap\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing)).

We check the key axioms, the rest are analogous.

(i)

(\Diamond 3)
For a coordinate
k

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha\wedge\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\dashv\vdash\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\alpha,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi)\wedge\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\beta,\ldots,\varphi_{n};\varphi).

(ii)

(\Diamond 4)

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\chi)\vdash\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\wedge\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi).

Fix
w\in W
and assume
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\wedge\chi)
. Let

be arbitrary. Then either

•

T\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for some
i
or

•

T\cap\llbracket\psi\wedge\chi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
, which implies both
T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
and

.

So the defining condition for
\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
and
\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)
holds for every

. Hence the axiom is valid.

(iii)

(\Diamond 6)
Assume the side condition

\gamma\wedge\delta\dashv\vdash\bot.

Semantically this means

\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket\cap\llbracket\delta\rrbracket=\varnothing.

We prove that the sequent

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi)\wedge\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\vee\delta)\vdash\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

Fix
w\in W
and assume

w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi)\wedge\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\vee\delta).

Then we have both

w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi),

and

w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\vee\delta).

To prove
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
, we must show:

\forall T\in N^{\Diamond}(w),(\exists i\leq n,T\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\text{ or }T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing).

Consider an arbitrary
T\in N^{\Diamond}(w)
. Since
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi\vee\delta)
, and
T\in N^{\Diamond}(w)
, the semantic clause gives:

either

T\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket\text{ for some }i=1,\ldots,n,

or

T\cap\llbracket\psi\vee\delta\rrbracket\neq\varnothing.

But
\llbracket\psi\vee\delta\rrbracket=\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\delta\rrbracket
, So we have

T\cap(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\delta\rrbracket)\neq\varnothing.

(i)

Case 1:
T\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket
for some
i
.

Then
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
.

(ii)

Case 2:
T\cap(\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\cup\llbracket\delta\rrbracket)\neq\varnothing

Then either

T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing,

or

T\cap\llbracket\delta\rrbracket\neq\varnothing.

•

Suppose
T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
. Then again we are done i.e.,
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)
.

•

Suppose
T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket=\varnothing\text{ and }T\cap\llbracket\delta\rrbracket\neq\varnothing
.

Since
w\models\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\gamma;\psi)
, and

either

(1)

or

T\subseteq\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket,

(2)

or

T\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\neq\varnothing.

(3)

So

is impossible. Threfore only two possibilities remain: either

(2)

Thus we have shown that for the arbitrary

or

Soundness of inference rules

Now assume the induction hypothesis: all premises used above are valid, and show the conclusion is valid.

Rule 1: (Cut)

\frac{\varphi\vdash\psi\quad\;\psi\vdash\chi}{\varphi\vdash\chi}.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\psi)
and
\mathfrak{M}\models(\psi\vdash\chi)
. Then

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\text{ and }\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket.

By transitivity of
\subseteq
, we get
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket
, i.e.
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\chi)
. So Cut is sound.

Rule 2: (US) Uniform substitution

\frac{\varphi\vdash\psi}{\sigma(\varphi)\vdash\sigma(\psi)}.

Assume
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\psi)
. We show that
\mathfrak{M}\models(\sigma(\varphi)\vdash\sigma(\psi))
, where
\sigma:\mathrm{Prop}\to\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
is a uniform substitution sending each propositional variable
p
to some formula
\sigma(p)
.

Define a new valuation
V^{\sigma}
on variables by:

V^{\sigma}(p)=\llbracket\sigma(p)\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}.

Let
\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma}
be the model obtained from
\mathfrak{M}
by replacing
V
with
V^{\sigma}
.

Claim: For every formula
\theta
and every
w\in W
:

\mathfrak{M},w\models\sigma(\theta)\iff\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma},w\models\theta.

This is proved by straightforward induction on
\theta
using compositionality of
\wedge,\vee,\Box,\Diamond
.

Using the claim:

\llbracket\sigma(\varphi)\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma}},\quad\;\llbracket\sigma(\psi)\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}=\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma}}.

Since
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\psi)
holds for all valuations (in particular for
V^{\sigma}
), we have:

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}^{\sigma}}.

Thus:

\llbracket\sigma(\varphi)\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}\subseteq\llbracket\sigma(\psi)\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}},

i.e.
\mathfrak{M}\models(\sigma(\varphi)\vdash\sigma(\psi))
. Hence the rule US is sound.

Rule 3: (RE) Replacement of equivalents

\frac{\alpha\dashv\vdash\beta\;\;\varphi\vdash\psi}{\varphi[alpha/\beta]\vdash\psi[\alpha/\beta]}.

Assume
\mathfrak{M}\models(\alpha\vdash\beta)
and
\mathfrak{M}\models(\beta\vdash\alpha)
. Then

\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}=\llbracket\beta\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}.

We also assume
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi\vdash\psi)
, i.e.

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}.

Claim: if
\llbracket\alpha\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}=\llbracket\beta\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}
, then

\llbracket\varphi[\alpha/\beta]\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}},\quad\llbracket\psi[\alpha/\beta]\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}=\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}.

This is proved by induction on the structure of the formula: since all connectives
\wedge,\vee,\Box,\Diamond
are interpreted by operations on truth sets, replacing a subformula by another with the same truth set does not affect the truth set of whole formula.

Using the claim ,
(**)
becomes:

\llbracket\varphi[\alpha/\beta]\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi[\alpha/\beta]\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{M}},

i.e
\mathfrak{M}\models(\varphi[\alpha/\beta]\vdash\psi[\alpha/\beta])
. So the rule RE is sound.

Thus we have shown that

•

every axiom instance of
\mathrm{PINL}
is valid in every persistent two-sided neighbourhood model
\mathfrak{M}
, and

•

each inference rule (US, Cut, RE) preserves validity.

Therefore, by induction on derivations, every sequent provable in
\mathrm{PINL}
is valid in every persistent model, i.e.

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi\Longrightarrow\models(\varphi\vdash\psi).

Canonical pair Model and Typed Completeness

In this section we develop a canonical model construction for positive INL. The canonical strategy is inspired by Dunn’s methodology for positive modal logic
dunn1995positive
: in the absence of negation, canonical states are built not from ordinary maximal consistent sets, but from two-sided syntactic objects recording both positive and negative information. In the present setting, this leads naturally to prime theory pairs.

If one attempts to work directly with the original persistent two-sided neighbourhood semantics and canonical worlds given by prime theories
T
, then the intended truth lemma would be

\mathfrak{M}^{c},T\models\varphi\iff\varphi\in T.

However, this direct one-sorted canonical proof is obstructed by the extensional nature of ordinary neighbourhoods: neighbourhoods are given only as unlabelled upsets, and the same upset may accidentally witness or refute several different instantial modal formulas. As a consequence, the truth-to-membership direction of the truth lemma may fail for the modal clauses. To understand this fact, we consider an example below:

Example 1
.

Let
W^{c}=\text{the set of all prime theories}
. An order relation
\leq^{c}
on
W^{c}
is defined by

T\leq^{c}U\iff T\subseteq U,

and a valuation
V^{c}:\mathrm{Prop}\to\mathrm{Up}(W,\leq^{c})
is defined by

\text{for each $p\in\mathrm{Prop}$},V^{c}(p)=\{X\in W^{c}:p\in X\}.

So for every formula
\theta
in
\mathrm{PINL}

\llbracket\theta\rrbracket^{c}=\{X\in W^{c}:\theta\in X\}.

The canonical
\Box

N^{\Box,c}(T)=\{S\in Up(W^{c},\leq^{c}):\exists\Box(\theta;\rho)\in T\text{ such that }S\subseteq\llbracket\rho\rrbracket^{c}\text{ and }S\cap\llbracket\theta\rrbracket^{c}\neq\varnothing\},

and the canonical
\Diamond

N^{\Diamond,c}(T)=\{A\in Up(W^{c},\leq^{c}):\forall\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\in T,[(\exists i\leq n:A\subseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{c})\text{ or }(A\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{c}\neq\varnothing)]\}.

Then
(W^{c},\leq^{c},N^{\Box,c},N^{\Diamond,c},V^{c})
is a persistent two-sided neighbourhood model. Assume there are three prime theories

in

such that

Hence

Since

To avoid this difficulty, we introduce an auxiliary typed persistent two-sided neigbhourhhod model, in which each neighbourhood is labelled by the modal formula for which it is intended. In this typed setting the canonical worlds are prime theory pairs
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
, and the truth lemma takes the form

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau,c},a\models^{\tau}\varphi\Longrightarrow\varphi\in a_{1}.

Thus semantic truth in the canonical typed model coincides exactly with membership in the positive component of the canonical state. This yields a completeness theorem for Positive INL with respect to typed persistent two-sided neighbourhood models.

Definition 8
.

A typed persistent two-sided neighbourhood model is a structure

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}=(W,\leq,N_{\tau}^{\Box},N_{\tau}^{\Diamond},V)

such that

(i)

(W,\leq)
is a poset;

(ii)

V:\mathrm{Prop}\to\mathrm{Up}(W,\leq)
is a valuation;

(iii)

for each
w\in W
,

\displaystyle N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Form}_{\Box}\times\mathcal{P}(W),

\displaystyle N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Form}_{\Diamond}\times\mathcal{P}(W),

where
\mathrm{Form}_{\Box}
denotes the set of all formulas of the form

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),

and
\mathrm{Form}_{\Diamond}
denotes the set of all formulas of the form

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi);

(iv)

for any
w,v\in W
whenever
w\leq v
,

N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)\subseteq N_{\tau}^{\Box}(v)\text{ and }N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w).

Definition 9
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}=(W,\leq,N_{\tau}^{\Box},N_{\tau}^{\Diamond},V)
be a typed persistent model. The truth relation

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\varphi

is defined inductively in the usual way for
\top,\bot,p,\wedge,\vee
, and for the modal formulas by:

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)

iff there exists

(\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi),S)\in N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)

such that

S\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\text{ and }S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,n.

Further,

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\Diamond(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)

iff there is no pair

(\Diamond(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi),A)\in N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w)

such that

A\cap\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}=\varnothing\text{ and }A\not\subseteq\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,n.

Definition 10
.

For typed persistent models, we write

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}\models_{\tau}(\varphi\vdash\psi)

if

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\subseteq\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}.

We write

\models_{\tau}(\varphi\vdash\psi)

if this is holds in every typed persistent model.

Proposition 1

(Truth preserving typed expansion of an ordinary model)
.

Every persistent two-sided neighbourhood model canonically induces a typed persistent model preserving truth of all formulas in the language of
\mathrm{PINL}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{M}=(W,\leq,N^{\Box},N^{\Diamond},V)
be a persistent two-sided neighbourhood model. Define
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}=(W,\leq,N_{\tau}^{\Box},N_{\tau}^{\Diamond},V)
by

\displaystyle N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(\alpha,S)\in\mathrm{Form}_{\Box}\times\mathcal{P}(W):S\in N^{\Box}(w)\},

\displaystyle N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(\delta,A)\in\mathrm{Form}_{\Box}\times\mathcal{P}(W):A\in N^{\Diamond}(w)\}.

Then we show that
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}
is a typed persistent two-sided neighbourhood model and for every formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
and every
w\in W
,

\mathfrak{M},w\models\varphi\iff\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\varphi.

Since
N^{\Box}(w),N^{\Diamond}(w)\subseteq\mathrm{Up}(W,\leq)\subseteq\mathcal{P}(W)
, the two typed neighbourhood assignments have the required codomains. Furthermore, if
w\leq v
, then persistence of
\mathfrak{M}
gives

N^{\Box}(w)\subseteq N^{\Box}(v),\quad\;N^{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N^{\Diamond}(w).

Hence

N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)\subseteq N_{\tau}^{\Box}(v),\quad\;N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(v)\subseteq N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w).

Thus
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}
is a typed persistent model.

We now prove the truth equivalence by induction on
\varphi
.

The propositional cases are immediate because
\mathfrak{M}
amd
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}
have the same valuation and interpret the lattice connectives identically. Let

\alpha=\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi).

Using the induction hypothesis for
\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n},\chi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models\alpha

\displaystyle\iff\exists S\in N^{\Box}(w)\Bigl[S\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\text{ and }S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\neq\varnothing\text{ for every }i\Bigr]

\displaystyle\iff\exists(\alpha,S)\in N_{\tau}^{\Box}(w)\,\Bigl[S\subseteq\llbracket\chi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\text{ and }S\cap\llbracket\psi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\neq\varnothing\text{ for every }i\Bigr]

\displaystyle\iff\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\alpha.

Now let

\delta=\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

The ordinary semantic clause is equal to the non-existence of a neighbourhood failing the co-witness condition. Thus, using the induction hypothesis and the definition of
N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},w\models\delta

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\neg\exists A\in N^{\Diamond}(w)\,\Bigl[A\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}=\varnothing\text{ and }A\nsubseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}}\text{ for every }i\Bigr]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\neg\exists(\delta,A)\in N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(w)\,\Bigl[A\cap\llbracket\psi\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}=\varnothing\text{ and }A\nsubseteq\llbracket\varphi_{i}\rrbracket^{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}}\text{ for every }i\Bigr]

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\mathfrak{M}^{\tau},w\models_{\tau}\delta.

∎

The converse of Proposition
1
is not automatic and is discussed at the end of this section.

6.1
Canonical Preliminaries

We now turn to the canonical construction for the typed persistent semantics. Since the logic is positive, the most convenient canonical states are prime theory pairs rather than ordinary maximal consistent sets.

Definition 11
.

A theory is a set
T\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
such that

(i)

\top\in T
;

(ii)

if
\alpha,\beta\in T
, then
\alpha\wedge\beta\in T
;

(iii)

if
\alpha\in T
and
\alpha\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\gamma
, then
\gamma\in T
.

A theory
T
is proper if
\bot\notin T
.

Definition 12
.

A counter-theory is a set
F\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
such that

(i)

\bot\in F
;

(ii)

if
\alpha,\beta\in F
then
\alpha\vee\beta\in F
;

(iii)

if
\alpha\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\gamma
and
\gamma\in F
, then
\alpha\in F
.

A counter-theory
F
is proper if
\top\notin F
. The intution is that a theory is closed upward under provable consequence, while a couter-theory is closed downward.

Definition 13
.

A theory pair is a pair

a=(a_{1},a_{2})

such that
a_{1}
is a theory and
a_{2}
is a counter-theory.

It is called

•

disjoint if
a_{1}\cap a_{2}=\varnothing
,

•

proper if
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are both proper.

Definition 14
.

A proper theory
T
is called prime if

\varphi\vee\psi\in T\Longrightarrow\varphi\in T\text{ or }\psi\in T.

A proper counter-theory
F
is called prime if

\varphi\wedge\psi\in F\Longrightarrow\varphi\in F\text{ or }\psi\in F.

A prime theory pairs is a disjoint proper pair

a=(a_{1},a_{2})

such that
a_{1}
is a prime theory,
a_{2}
is a prime counter-theory, and every formula belongs to exactly one of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
.

Thus prime theory pairs are the positive counterparts of classical maximal consistent sets.

Definition 15
.

A set
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
is consistent if there is no
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge\gamma_{n}\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\bot.

Disjointness and prime-pair extension

We now recall the basic seperation criterion from
dunn1995positive
.

Lemma 3
.

A theory pair
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
is not disjoint if and only if there exist
\alpha\in a_{1}
and
\beta\in a_{2}
such that

\alpha\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\beta.

For a theory
T
and a formula
\chi
, let
T+\chi
denote the theory generated by
T\cup\{\chi\}
. Dually, for a counter-theory
F
, let
F+\chi
denote the counter-theory generated by
F\cup\{\chi\}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
be a disjoint theory pair and let
\chi
be any formula. Then at least one of the pairs

(a_{1}+\chi,a_{2})\text{ or }(a_{1},a_{2}+\chi)

is disjoint.

Lemma 5
.

Every disjoint theory pair extends to a prime theory pair.

Canonical state space and valuation

Let

. Define

Since every formula belongs to exactly one side of a prime pair, this is equivalent to

.

For each formula

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c}=\{a\in W^{c}:\varphi\in a_{1}\},

Lemma 6
.

For every formula

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{c}

If

By Lemma

Definition 17

For each canonical state

\displaystyle\bigl(\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),C\bigr)\in\operatorname{Form}_{\Diamond}\times\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(W^{c}):\;

Lemma 9

(Persistence of the canonical typed neighbourhood assignments)
.

Let

. If

Let

be canonical states such that

By the definition of the canonical order,

Since
a
and

We first prove the persistence property for the
\Box

16
, we have

\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)\in a_{1},

and

S\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(W^{c}),

We now prove the persistence condition for the
\Diamond
-neighbourhood assignment. Let

\bigl(\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),C\bigr)\in N_{\tau}^{\Diamond,c}(b).

By Definition

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\in b_{2},

Theorem 2

(Completeness with respect to typed persistent semantics)
.

For all formulas
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
,

\models_{\tau}(\varphi\vdash\psi)\Rightarrow\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive. Suppose that

\varphi\not\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi

(20)

Consider the theory pair

\bigl(\operatorname{Th}(\{\varphi\}),\operatorname{CTh}(\{\psi\})\bigr).

We claim that this pair is disjoint. If not then by Lemma
3
there exist formulas

\alpha\in\operatorname{Th}(\{\varphi\})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\in\operatorname{CTh}(\{\psi\})

such that

\alpha\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\beta.

(21)

By the definitions of the generated theory and generated counter-theory,

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\alpha\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

(22)

From (
21
) and (
22
), two applications of
\mathrm{Cut}
yield

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

contradicting (
20
). Hence

\bigl(\operatorname{Th}(\{\varphi\}),\operatorname{CTh}(\{\psi\})\bigr)

is disjoint. By Lemma
5
, we can extend this disjoint theory pair to a prime theory pair

such that

\operatorname{Th}(\{\varphi\})\subseteq a_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{CTh}(\{\psi\})\subseteq a_{2}.

In particular,

\varphi\in a_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi\in a_{2}.

(23)

Since
a
is a prime theory pair, its two components are disjoint. Therefore,

\psi\notin a_{1}.

(24)

Now we consider the canonical typed model

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau,c}=(W^{c},\leq^{c},N_{\tau}^{\Box,c},N_{\tau}^{\Diamond,c},V^{c}).

By Lemma
10
, and using (

\mathcal{M}^{\tau,c},a\models_{\tau}\varphi\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}^{\tau,c},a\not\models_{\tau}\psi.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
2
establishes the completeness of PINL with respect to the auxiliary typed persistent semantics used in the canonical construction.
This paper introduces typed semantics to establish Lemma
10
, but does not offer a general soundness theorem for all typed persistent models.

∎

Remark 2
.

Proposition
1
shows that every ordinary persistent two-sided neighbourhood model induces a typed persistent model preserving truth of all
\mathrm{PINL}
-formulas. Consequently,

The converse implication is not established here. Moreover, the typed semantics is used in this section as an auxiliary canonical semantics: a full sound-and-complete typed semantics would require additional
coherence conditions on formula-labelled neighbourhood assignments.

Algebraic Semantics of Positive INL

The algebraic counterpart of positive instantial neighbourhood logic is naturally formulated in terms of distributive lattices with instantial operators (DLIOs). This viewpoint is already sketched by Bezhanishvili et al.
bezhanishvili2020duality
in their coalgebraic treatment of instantial neighbourhood logic, where DLIOs are proposed as the positive algebraic structures corresponding to instantial neighbourhood semantics.

In this section, we use this
\mathrm{DLIO}
perspective as our starting point. However, our proof system treats
\Box
and
\Diamond
as two primitive instantial operators with seperate axiom schemes.Threfore, before passing to the full
\mathrm{DLIO}
setting with interaction principles, we first isolate the algebraic content of the independent
\Box
and
\Diamond
fragments. For this pupose we introduce the
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
concept.

A
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
is a bounded distributive lattice equipped with two families of finitary operations,

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}\qquad\;(g_{n})_{n\geq 1},

where
(f_{n})
interprets formulas of the form

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi),

and
g_{n}
interprets formulas of the form

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

The
(f_{n})
-laws are the algebraic translation of the
\Box
-axioms, while the
g_{n}
-laws are the algebraic translations of the
\Diamond
-axioms.

Thus the notion of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
used here should not be understood as replacing the
\mathrm{DLIO}
framework of Bezhanishvili et al.
bezhanishvili2020duality
. Rather, it is a preliminary independent-operator version of that framework, tailored to the positive INL proof system developed in Section
4
. Interaction axioms connecting the two operator families may then be added later to recover the
\mathrm{DLIO}
structure relevent for the duality programme.

The pupose of this section is therefore twofold. First, we formulate the exact algebraic semantics corresponding to our proof system. Second, we prove algebraic soundness and completeness with respect to the resulting class of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIOs}
. This gives a complete algebraic account of positive INL at the independent-operator level.

Definition 18
.

A
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
is an algebra

\mathcal{A}=(A,\wedge,\vee,0,1,(f_{n})_{n\geq 1},(g_{n})_{n\geq 1})

such that
(A,\wedge,\vee,0,1)
is a bounded distributive lattice and for every
n\geq 1
, the operations

f_{n},g_{n}:A^{n+1}\to A

satisfy the following conditions.

The
f_{n}
-laws
:

For all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b,c,d,\gamma,\delta\in A
, and every
k\leq n

(i)

f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},0,a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n};b)=0
,

(ii)

for every permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};b)=f_{n}(a_{\pi}(1),\ldots,a_{\pi}(n);b)
,

(iii)

f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},c\vee d,a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n};b)=f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},c,a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n},a_{n};b)\vee f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},d,a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n};b)

(iv)

f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};b\wedge c)\leq f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};b)\wedge f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};c)
,

(v)

f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};b)\leq f_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1},a_{n}\wedge b;b)
,

(vi)

if

:

(i)

g_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k-1},1,a_{k+1},\ldots,a_{n};b)=1

(ii)

for every permutation
\pi

g_{n}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};b)=g_{n}(a_{\pi}(1),\ldots,a_{\pi}(n);b)

(iii)

Theorem 3

Let

The Lindenbaum Algebra of Positive INL

We now construct an algebra of positive INL. The idea is to identify formulas that are provably equivalent. Thus two formulas
\varphi
and
\psi
are regarded as representing the same algebraic element whenever

\varphi\dashv\vdash\psi.

The algebraic operations are then induced by the logical operations
\wedge,\vee,\top,\bot,\Box,
, and
\Diamond
. In the present setting, the Lindenbaum algebra carries two families of instantial operations, and hence will be shown to be a
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}

Define

\varphi\equiv_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi\iff\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi\text{ and }\psi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\varphi.

Let
[\varphi]
denote the equivalence class of
\varphi

\mathrm{Fm}
denote the set of all well-formed formulas of positive INL. Then

\mathrm{Fm}/\equiv_{\mathrm{PINL}}

means the set of all equivalence classes of formulas under provable equivalence.

Definition 21
.

The Lindenbaum algebra of
\mathrm{PINL}
is the quotient

\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}=(\mathrm{Fm}/\equiv_{\mathrm{PINL}},\wedge,\vee,[\bot],[\top],(f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}})_{n\geq 1},(g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}})_{n\geq 1}),

where

\displaystyle\wedge[\psi]=[\varphi\wedge\psi],[\varphi]\vee[\psi]=[\varphi\vee\psi],

\displaystyle f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])=[\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)],

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])=[\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)].

Lemma 13
.

The operations

and

Proof.

Suppose

\psi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi^{\prime}\text{ and }\psi^{\prime}\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Using monotonicity rule for

\displaystyle\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Box(\varphi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\varphi_{n}^{\prime};\psi^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Box(\varphi_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\varphi_{n}^{\prime};\psi^{\prime})\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi).

Lemma 14
.

Let

\alpha\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\alpha

8
Bitopological PINL-spaces and Admissible-open Representation

The typed canonical semantics introduced in Section
6
was used to prove completeness.
However, the typed canonical model is still partly syntactic, because its neighbourhoods are labelled by formulas.

For a representation-theoretic treatment, this syntactic dependence should be removed. If two formulas are provably equivalent, then they determine the same element of the Lindenbaum
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
, and therefore they should determine the same data on the space side. The aim of this section is to replace formula labels by admissible positive opens and to show that the resulting canonical bitopological structure recovers the Lindenbaum
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
.

8.1
The canonical bitopological setting for Positive INL

Let
W^{c}
be the set of prime theory pairs
a(a_{1},a_{2})
. For each formula
\varphi
, define

U_{\varphi}=\{a\in W^{c}:\varphi\in a_{1}\},

and

V_{\varphi}=\{a\in W^{c}:\varphi\in a_{2}\}.

For each formula
\varphi
in positive instantial neighbourhood language, the set
U_{\varphi}
consists of those prime pairs whose positive component contains
\varphi
, while
V_{\varphi}
consists of those prime pairs whose negative component contains
\varphi
.

Definition 22
.

Define two topologies on
W^{c}
by

\tau^{+}=\text{ the topology generated by }\{U_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}\},

and

\tau^{-}=\text{ the topology generated by }\{V_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}\}.

Then
(W^{c},\tau^{+},\tau^{-})
is a bitopological space.

Note 1
.

The collection
\{U_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}\}
forms the lattice of admissible positive opens, and this lattice will serve as the underlying lattice for the algebraic semantics.

Let
A^{c}=\{U_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}\}
. Then
A^{c}
is closed under finite unions and finite intersections. Indeed,

\displaystyle U_{\varphi\wedge\psi}=U_{\varphi}\cap U_{\psi},

\displaystyle U_{\varphi\vee\psi}=U_{\varphi}\cup U_{\psi},

and

U_{\bot}=\varnothing,\;U_{\top}=W^{c}.

Hence,
A^{c}
satisfies the basis condition and also
(A^{c},\cap,\cup,\emptyset,W^{c})
is a bounded distributive lattice of admissible positive opens.

Let
B^{c}=\{V_{\varphi}:\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}\}
. Now,

V_{\bot}=W^{c},

because
\bot\in a_{2}
for every prime counter-theory
a_{2}
. Hence
B^{c}
covers
W^{c}
. Since
V_{\varphi\vee\psi}=V_{\varphi}\cap V_{\psi}
,
B^{c}
is closed under finite intersection. Therefore,
B^{c}
is a basis for
\tau^{-}
.

Also we have

V_{\varphi\wedge\psi}=V_{\varphi}\cup V_{\psi},\;V_{\top}=\emptyset.

Thus
B^{c}
is closed under finite intersections and finite unions and contains the bottom and top elements. Hence
(B^{c},\cap,\cup,\emptyset,W^{c})
is a bounded distributive lattice.

The seperation lemma for admissible opens

We shall need the following basic fact:

Lemma 15
.

For all formulas
\varphi,\psi
in the language of
\mathrm{PINL}
,

U_{\varphi}\subseteq U_{\psi}

if and only if

\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Consequently,

U_{\varphi}=U_{\psi}

if and only if

\varphi\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi
. Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in U_{\varphi}
. Then
\varphi\in a_{1}
. Since
a_{1}
is a theory and is closed under provable consequence,
\psi\in a_{1}
. Hence
a\in U_{\psi}
. Therefore
U_{\varphi}\subseteq U_{\psi}
.

Conversely, suppose that
U_{\varphi}\subseteq U_{\psi}
. We prove that
\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi
. Assume, for contradiction, that

\varphi\not\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Then
Th(\{\varphi\})
, the theory generated by
\varphi
and
CTh(\{\psi\})
, the counter-theory generated by
\psi
form a disjoint theory pair. Then by Lemma
5
, there exists a prime theory pair

a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in W^{c}

such that

\varphi\in a_{1}\;\text{ and }\;\psi\in a_{2}.

Since the pair is disjoint,
\psi\notin a_{1}
. Hence
a\in U_{\varphi}
, but
a\notin U_{\psi}
. This contradicts
U_{\varphi}\subseteq U_{\psi}
. Therefore
\varphi\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi
.

The equivalence
U_{\varphi}=U_{\psi}
if and only if
\varphi\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi
follows immediately by applying the first part in both directions.
∎

8.2
Typed bitopological
\mathrm{PINL}
-spaces

The prime-pair typed canonical model constructed in Section
6
suggests which data should be included in an abstract space-side structure. First, we need a bitopological space
(X,\tau^{+},\tau^{-})
, because the canonical states carry both positive and counter-theoretic information. Second, we need a distinguished bounded distributive latyice
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\tau^{+}
of admissible positive opens, since this is the part on which the
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
operations are to be interpreted. Third, we need typed neighbourhood assignments

and

(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B)
of admissible positive opens.

The labels are therefore taken from
\mathcal{A}
, rather than from formulas. This makes the structure depend only on the algebraic meaning of formulas and not on their particular syntactic form. In particular, provably equivalent formulas determine the same admissible open and hence the same space-side data. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 23
.

A typed bitopological PINL-space is a structure

\mathfrak{X}=(X,\tau^{+},\tau^{-},\mathcal{A},N_{\tau}^{\Box},N_{\tau}^{\Diamond})

such that

(i)

(ii)

\emptyset
and
\top

X
;

(iii)

for each

(iv)

for each
x\in X
,

An element of
N_{\tau}^{\Box}(x)
has the form

((A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B),S),

where
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n},B\in\mathcal{A}
and

. The tuple

(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B)

is the admissible-open label attached to the neighbourhod
S
. Similarly, an elements of
N_{\tau}^{\Diamond}(x)
has the form

((A_{1},\ldots,A_{N};B),C),

where

, is the neighbourhood set, while the tuple
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n};B)
is the admissible-open label attached to
C
.

The algebra of admissible opens

Let

For

S\cap A_{i}\neq\emptyset\;\text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,n.

Thus

-clause.

.

2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}

8.3
The canonical bitopological PINL-space

We now define a bitopological PINL-space from the prime-pair typed canonical model developed in Section

Corollary 1

(Independence of modal open labels from formula representatives)
.

Let
n\in\omega
, and let

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n},\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n},\chi,\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}.

Suppose that

U_{\varphi_{i}}=U_{\theta_{i}}\text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,n,

and

U_{\chi}=U_{\rho}.

Then

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Box(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho),

and

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Diamond(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho).

Consequently, for every prime theory pair
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in W^{c}
,

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\in a_{1}\iff\Box(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho)\in a_{1},

and

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\in a_{2}\iff\Diamond(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho)\in a_{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
,
U_{\varphi_{i}}=U_{\theta_{i}}
shows that

\varphi_{i}\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\theta_{I}\text{ for every }i=1,\ldots,n,

and
U_{\chi}=U_{\rho}
shows that

\chi\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\rho.

Since provable equivalents may be replaced in every coordinate of the instantial modal connectives, we obtain

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Box(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho),

and

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\Diamond(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho).

Now let
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in W^{c}
. Since
a_{1}
is a theory and is closed under provable consequence, we have

\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\in a_{1}\iff\Box(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho)\in a_{1}.

Similarly, since
a_{2}
is a counter-theory and is closed downward under provable consequence, we have

\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\chi)\in a_{2}\iff\Diamond(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n};\rho)\in a_{2}.

This proves the result.

Definition 25

(Canonical Bitopological PINL-space)

For each

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Box}^{c}(a):=\Bigl\{

\displaystyle\bigl((U_{\psi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\psi_{n}};U_{\chi}),S\bigr):

\displaystyle\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)\in a_{1},

\displaystyle S\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(W^{c}),

\displaystyle S\subseteq U_{\chi},

Induced instantial operations on admissible positive opens

First, we define two maps

and

and whose values are subsets of

\displaystyle\text{there exists }S\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(W^{c})\text{ such that}

Proposition 5
.

Let

.

(i)

For all formulas

For all formulas

Proof.

Let

. We show that

Let

,

\alpha\in a_{1}.

Since

7
gives a finite set

S\subseteq U_{\chi}.

and

\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)\in a_{1}.

f_{n}^{c}(U_{\psi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\psi_{n}};U_{\chi})=U_{\Box(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n};\chi)}

\delta\in a_{1}

Then by definition of
g_{n}^{c}
there exists a finite set

\bigl((U_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\varphi_{n}};U_{\psi}),C\bigr)\in\mathcal{N}_{\Diamond}^{c}(a).

Again by the definition of

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n},\rho\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}

\displaystyle U_{\theta_{i}}

\displaystyle=U_{\varphi_{i}}\qquad\text{for every }i=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle U_{\rho}

\displaystyle=U_{\psi},

Definition 26
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=(A,\wedge_{A},\vee_{A},0_{A},1_{A},(f_{n}^{A}){n\in\omega},(g_{n}^{A}){n\in\omega})
and
\mathbb{B}=(B,\wedge_{B},\vee_{B},0_{B},1_{B},(f_{n}^{B}){n\in\omega},(g_{n}^{B}){n\in\omega})

2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}

2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}

and every
n\in\omega

(i)

h

h(x\wedge_{A}y)=h(x)\wedge_{B}h(y);

(ii)

h

h(x\vee_{A}y)=h(x)\vee_{B}h(y);

(iii)

h
preserves the bounds:

(iv)

h
preserves the
\Box
-type instantial operations:

h(f_{n}^{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};z))=f_{n}^{B}(h(x_{1}),\ldots,h(x_{n});h(z));

(v)

preserves the
\Diamond
-type instantial operations:

h(g_{n}^{A}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};z))=g_{n}^{B}(h(x_{1}),\ldots,h(x_{n});h(z)).

2

\mathrm{DLIO}

2

\mathrm{DLIO}
isomorphism if

is bijective. In this case,
\mathbb{A}
and

are said to be isomorphic.

By Corollary
2
, the admissible-open algebra
\mathbb{A}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{PINL}}^{c})
is a 2-
\mathrm{DLIO}

\mathrm{DLIO}
of PINL, under the natural map sending an element of Lindenbaum algebra of PINL to its canonical positive open.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}
be the Lindenbaum
2

\mathrm{DLIO}
of PINL. Define a map

\eta:\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}(\mathfrak{X}_{\mathrm{PINL}}^{c})

\eta([\varphi])=U_{\varphi}.

\eta
is an isomorphism of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}

First, we show that
\eta
is well-defined. Suppose

. Then
\varphi\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi
. By Lemma
15
,
U_{\varphi}=U_{\psi}
. Hence,

\eta([\varphi])=\eta([\psi]).

So,
\eta
is well-defined. Next, we show that

preserves the bounded distributive lattice structure. We have

\displaystyle\eta([\varphi]\wedge[\psi])

\displaystyle=U_{\varphi\wedge\psi}

\displaystyle=U_{\varphi}\cap U_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\eta([\varphi])\cap\eta([\psi]).

Similarly,
\eta([\varphi]\vee[\psi])=\eta([\varphi])\cup\eta([\psi])
. Also,
\eta([\bot])=U_{\bot}=\varnothing
and
\eta([\top])=U_{\top}=W^{c}
.

Now we show that
\eta
preserves the
\Box
-type instantial operations. Let
n\in\omega
, and let

[\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}],[\psi]\in\mathfrak{L}_{\mathrm{PINL}}.

By definition of the Lindenbaum

\displaystyle\eta\bigl(f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])\bigr)

\displaystyle=\eta([\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)])\

\displaystyle=U_{\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)}.

Now

\displaystyle f_{n}^{c}(\eta[\varphi_{1}],\ldots,\eta([\varphi_{n}]);\eta([\psi]))

\displaystyle=f_{n}^{c}(U_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\varphi_{n}};U_{\psi}).

By Proposition
5
,

\displaystyle f_{n}^{c}(U_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\varphi_{n}};U_{\psi})

\displaystyle=U_{\Box(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\eta\bigl(f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])\bigr)

\displaystyle=f_{n}^{c}\bigl(\eta[\varphi_{1}],\ldots,\eta([\varphi_{n}]);\eta([\psi])\bigr).

Therefore,
\eta
preserves the
\Box
-type instantial operation
f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}
, for every
n\in\omega
.

We next verify that
\eta
preserves the
\Diamond
-type instantial operations. By definition of Lindenbaum
\Diamond
-type operation,

g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])=[\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\eta\bigl(g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])\bigr)

\displaystyle=\eta([\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)])\

\displaystyle=U_{\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)}.

Now

\displaystyle g_{n}^{c}(\eta[\varphi_{1}],\ldots,\eta([\varphi_{n}]);\eta([\psi]))

\displaystyle=g_{n}^{c}(U_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\varphi_{n}};U_{\psi}).

By Proposition
5
,

\displaystyle g_{n}^{c}(U_{\varphi_{1}},\ldots,U_{\varphi_{n}};U_{\psi})

\displaystyle=U_{\Diamond(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n};\psi)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\eta\bigl(g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}([\varphi_{1}],\ldots,[\varphi_{n}];[\psi])\bigr)

\displaystyle=g_{n}^{c}\bigl(\eta[\varphi_{1}],\ldots,\eta([\varphi_{n}]);\eta([\psi])\bigr).

Therefore,
\eta
preserves the
\Diamond
-type instantial operation
g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}}
, for every
n\in\omega
.

It remains to show that
\eta
is bijective. To prove injectivity, suppose
\eta([\varphi])=\eta([\psi])
. Then
U_{\varphi}=U_{\psi}
. By Lemma
15
,

\varphi\dashv\vdash_{\mathrm{PINL}}\psi.

Hence,
[\varphi]=[\psi]
. Thus
\eta
is injective.

To prove surjectivity, let
D\in A^{c}
. By definition of
A^{c}
, there exists a formula
\varphi
in the language of PINL such that

D=U_{\varphi}.

But then
D=\eta([\varphi])
. Hence
\eta
is surjective. Therefore
\eta
is a bijective map preserving the bounded distributive lattice operations and all the instantial operations
(f_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}},g_{n}^{\mathfrak{L}})
. Hence
\eta
is an isomorphism of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s.
∎

The preceding theorem is a canonical representation result. It shows that the Lindenbaum
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
of PINL is recovered as the algebra of admissible positive opens of the canonical bitopological PINL-space, and hence provides a first step toward a duality theory for PINL.

9
Conclusion and future work

In this study, we have developed Positive Instantial Neighbourhood Logic with two primitive modalities,
\Box
and
\Diamond
, over a distributive-lattice propositional base. We have introduced the syntax and proof system of the logic and proved soundness with respect to persistent two-sided neighbourhood semantics.
A direct canonical proof for the original persistent two-sided neighbourhood semantics is difficult, because neighbourhoods are not labelled by the modal formulas for which they are used. We have introduced an auxiliary typed persistent neighbourhood semantics, which yields the required truth lemma and typed completeness theorem.

We also formulated the algebraic semantics of PINL by means of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s. These algebras have a bounded distributive lattice reduct together with two families of instantial operations corresponding to the
\Box
- and
\Diamond
-modalities. It has been proved that the Lindenbaum algebra of formulae modulo provable equivalence is a
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
and the deductive system is algebraically sound and complete w.r.t. the class of
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s.

Finally, we have constructed the canonical bitopological PINL-space associated with the prime-pair construction. In this space, the positive opens
U_{\varphi}
record membership of
\varphi
in the positive component of prime pairs, while the negative opens
V_{\varphi}
record membership of
\varphi
in the negative component. The admissible positive opens carry the algebraic operations induced by the typed instantial neighbourhood structure. We have proved that the algebra of admissible positive opens of the canonical bitopological PINL-space is isomorphic to the Lindenbaum
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
of PINL. Thus the final topological result of the paper is a canonical admissible-open representation theorem.

There are several directions for future work. First, we can develop a Priestley-style or bitopological duality for
2
-
\mathrm{DLIO}
s. This direction is motivated by Priestley duality and its modal extensions
priestley1970representation
;
celani1999priestley
, by the duality between bitopological spaces and
d
-frames
jakl2018d
, and by the existing coalgebraic duality theory for INL
bezhanishvili2020duality
. To develop these duality, we will need to specify a suitable category of descriptive PINL-spaces, appropriate morphisms, and stability criteria that ensure the instantial neighbourhood structure is represented functorially.

Second, one may study additional interaction axioms between the two modal operations
\Box
and
\Diamond
, and compare the resulting algebras with DLIO framework arising in the duality theory of INL
bezhanishvili2020duality
.

Third, one may develop a geometric version of PINL. In that direction, the bounded distributive lattice of admissible positive opens should be replaced by a frame of opens, and the instantial operations should satisfy suitable Scott-continuity conditions. This would provide a basis for future work on the hypothesis that a geometric version of INL can be obtained from positive INL by requiring frame-theoretic continuity conditions.
