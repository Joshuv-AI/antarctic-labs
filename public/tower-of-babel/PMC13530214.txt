RCS-YOLOv8: an improved YOLOv8 for wind turbine blade defect detection
Authors: Yang Jiao, Chaobin Xu, Jingyu Zhao, Jiale Li, Zhicheng Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWind turbine blades are critical components of wind power systems, and early accurate detection of surface defects is essential for ensuring operational safety. However, automated detection faces significant challenges due to drastic illumination variations, complex background interference, and wide-ranging defect scales in real wind farm environments. This paper proposes RCS-YOLOv8, a multi-module collaborative optimization model for wind turbine blade defect detection. The model introduces RepViT as the backbone network, integrating local convolutional modeling with global context perception to enhance robustness against complex backgrounds. A Channel-Spatial Cooperative Attention Module (CAFM) is designed and embedded in the Neck layer, which performs dual-dimensional adaptive feature recalibration to focus on critical defect regions. SlimNeck with VoVGSCSP is adopted to reconstruct the feature pyramid network, strengthening multi-scale fusion between shallow localization information and deep semantic features. WIoU replaces CIoU as the bounding box regression loss, employing a dynamic non-monotonic focusing mechanism to improve recall for hard-to-detect defects. On a wind turbine blade dataset containing seven defect categories, RCS-YOLOv8 achieves 90.3%, outperforming the baseline YOLOv8n by 5.8% and the second-best method RT-DETR by 3.0% The recall reaches 87.5%, the highest among all compared models. Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of each module, and visualization analysis confirms that the model accurately focuses on defect regions while effectively suppressing background interference. The proposed method provides a reliable technical solution for automated wind turbine blade defect detection.Keywords: Wind turbine blade, Defect detection, RepViT, Attention mechanism, Deep learningSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As a vital component of the global clean energy system, wind power generation has experienced sustained growth in installed capacity, with its operational safety and generation efficiency directly affecting the levelized cost of energy and power system reliability1,2. Wind turbine blades, serving as the core components for wind energy capture, are subjected to the coupled effects of multiple environmental stresses during service, including alternating aerodynamic loads, ultraviolet radiation, extreme temperature cycling, and rain-hail erosion. These conditions render the blade surfaces and internal structures susceptible to various defects, such as cracks, corrosion (rusty), coating peeling, and deformity3,4. If left undetected, these defects can lead to crack propagation, structural stiffness degradation, and even catastrophic blade fracture, resulting in significant economic losses and safety hazards5. Therefore, early and accurate detection of blade defects is of critical engineering importance for ensuring the safe operation of wind turbine systems.

Currently, wind farms predominantly rely on traditional inspection methods, such as manual telescope-based patrol and close-range observation from elevated gondolas. These approaches suffer from low efficiency, high maintenance costs, and considerable safety risks associated with working at heights. Moreover, the detection results are heavily dependent on the subjective experience and skill level of engineers, leading to high miss rates for early-stage minor damage6. With the rapid advancement of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) platforms and computer vision technologies, deep learning-based automated defect detection methods have emerged as a prominent research focus in this field7,8.

In recent years, deep learning-based object detection algorithms have achieved remarkable progress in industrial surface defect detection9,10. Methods based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have demonstrated outstanding performance across various industrial scenarios, owing to their efficient local feature extraction capabilities. Cui et al11. proposed SDDNet, a fast and accurate detection network for steel surface defects, validating the feasibility of lightweight CNN architectures in industrial defect detection. Chen et al12. designed DCAM-Net, which achieves rapid detection of strip steel surface defects through the synergistic integration of deformable convolution and attention mechanisms. Li et al13. proposed ACPP-Net, which enhances multi-scale feature representation for strip steel surface defects through the collaborative design of adaptive Ghost convolution and channel-spatial pyramid pooling, while maintaining a balance between detection accuracy and speed. Hou et al14. proposed DSF-Net, which employs a dual-space frequency architecture and a Dynamic Inception Mixer backbone to achieve high-precision detection of spacecraft thermal control coating defects under complex lighting conditions, providing a novel technical paradigm for defect recognition on highly reflective industrial surfaces. Du et al15. introduced AFF-Net, which employs an adaptive feature focusing approach combining deformable convolution with attention mechanisms for effective detection of strip steel surface defects through diffusion-aggregation feature sampling and weighting. Chen et al16. proposed an efficient YOLOv5-based detector that integrates residual connections with multi-head attention and adaptive feature fusion, achieving a favorable balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency for cold-rolled steel strip inspection.

In the field of wind turbine blade inspection, Ran et al17. successfully applied an improved YOLOv5 model to blade defect detection, demonstrating the applicability of the YOLO series in this scenario. Lv et al18. constructed an end-to-end blade damage detector based on a deep learning framework, achieving a favorable trade-off between detection accuracy and inference efficiency. Liu et al19. recently developed a lightweight YOLOv5‑based detector for wind turbine blade surface defects, employing CBAM attention and GhostNetv2 to balance accuracy and efficiency – a strategy closely related to our CAFM module.

To enhance feature representation capability, attention mechanisms have been widely incorporated into defect detection frameworks. Liu et al20. integrated attention mechanisms with detection networks to improve the feature discrimination ability for blade surface defect detection. Yeung et al21. proposed an efficient fused-attention model for steel surface defect detection, significantly enhancing the response intensity of defect regions through joint channel and spatial attention modeling. Huang et al22. developed the SSA-YOLO model, achieving significant accuracy improvements for hot-rolled strip steel surface defect detection by combining convolutional attention mechanisms with Swin Transformer architectures and adaptive spatial feature fusion. Liu et al23. proposed a comprehensive approach combining global attention modules with cascade fusion networks, demonstrating improved robustness in detecting complex and variable steel strip surface defects. He et al24. achieved end-to-end steel surface defect detection by fusing multiple hierarchical features, improving the model’s adaptability in complex scenarios. Additionally, multi-scale feature fusion strategies represent another key approach for improving detection performance. Liu et al25. designed a real-time anchor-free detector from the perspective of global–local feature enhancement, improving the capture capability for small-scale defects. Lin et al26. enhanced the information transmission efficiency across different scales through an improved feature pyramid network design. Shen et al27. introduced MINet, which incorporates a multiscale interactive module combining depthwise convolution and pointwise convolution for multi-scale feature extraction and fusion, achieving real-time salient object detection of strip steel surface defects.

Regarding model robustness and generalization, Ye et al28. conducted a systematic study on the robustness of surface defect detection in interference environments, proposing improved strategies to enhance model resistance to disturbances. Liu et al29. proposed an environment-adaptive multimodal attention module (EAMM) for wind turbine blade defect detection under diverse weather conditions, achieving strong cross-domain generalization capability – which aligns with our cross-scenario design considerations. Under data-limited conditions, Wang et al30. explored few-shot defect detection methods, providing a viable solution for scenarios with scarce labeled samples. Bao et al31. proposed a triplet-graph reasoning network for few-shot metal surface defect segmentation, maintaining stable detection performance with limited data. Zhong et al32. designed LiFSO-Net, a lightweight feature screening optimization network that addresses the detection challenges for small defects and high aspect ratio defects through deformable context guidance and feature interaction optimization.

Drastic illumination variations, weather interference (rain, fog, and snow), and complex backgrounds (sky, clouds, and vegetation) in real wind farm environments can significantly degrade detection model performance, leading to high false positive and miss rates20,28. Existing models generally exhibit limited generalization capability against such real-world noise, failing to meet the reliability requirements for practical deployment.

To address the above challenges, this paper proposes RCS-YOLOv8, a multi-module collaborative optimization model for wind turbine blade defect detection. Built upon the YOLOv8 framework, the proposed model systematically enhances detection capability in complex wind farm environments through the synergistic optimization of an efficient backbone network, attention-based feature enhancement, lightweight multi-scale fusion, and adaptive bounding box regression. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

(1) A task-specific collaborative optimization framework is proposed for wind turbine blade defect detection. Unlike conventional approaches that focus on isolated module replacement, this framework systematically addresses the intertwined challenges of complex backgrounds, extreme scale variations, and ambiguous defect boundaries by orchestrating the synergy among backbone feature extraction, attention-based refinement, multi-scale fusion, and regression optimization.

(2) A Channel-Spatial Cooperative Attention Module (CAFM) is introduced and strategically positioned at the bottleneck of the backbone-neck junction. Unlike conventional attention placement strategies, this task-specific architectural decision enables the module to collaboratively interact with the RepViT backbone: it receives structurally rich features from RepViT and performs adaptive recalibration that effectively suppresses wind-farm-specific background noise (sky, clouds) while sharpening the network’s focus on small-scale defect details, thereby significantly reducing miss rates.

(3) A lightweight multi-scale feature fusion strategy, SlimNeck with VoVGSCSP, is developed. This structure synergistically interacts with the enhanced features from CAFM, strengthening cross-scale information flow to improve the detection of defects with weak visual signatures.

(4) A regression optimization strategy based on WIoU is incorporated. This loss function is specifically chosen to complement the improved feature representations, dynamically balancing precision and recall to enhance robustness for hard-to-detect samples in noisy real-world annotations.

The proposed method

Overall architecture

The proposed RCS-YOLOv8 model adopts a three-stage Backbone–Neck–Head architecture, as illustrated in Fig. 1. In the Backbone, RepViT serves as the feature extraction network, extracting hierarchical feature representations through five stages (RepViT0–4). A Spatial Pyramid Pooling-Fast (SPPF) module is appended at the end of the backbone to enlarge the receptive field, followed by the CAFM module to further enhance high-level semantic feature expression. In the Neck, a bidirectional multi-scale feature fusion pathway is constructed through the combination of upsampling, downsampling, and lateral connections, with VoVGSCSP modules integrated at critical fusion nodes to improve feature reorganization and multi-scale representation. In the Head, detection predictions are performed on three feature maps at scales P3, P4, and P5, enabling accurate identification of wind turbine blade defects across different scales.

The overall structure of RCS-YOLOV8.

The architecture is specifically designed to address the prevalent characteristics of wind turbine blade defects, including high proportion of small targets, ambiguous boundaries, strong background interference, and large-scale variations. Through the synergy between the lightweight backbone network and multi-scale feature enhancement modules, the model effectively improves detection accuracy and robustness in complex scenarios while maintaining computational efficiency.

The proposed framework is not a mere aggregation of existing modules but a deliberately orchestrated system. The RepViT backbone provides a robust and efficient feature hierarchy resilient to complex wind farm backgrounds. The CAFM module then purifies these features by suppressing noise and sharpening defect-related responses. Subsequently, SlimNeck efficiently propagates this refined semantic information across scales, and WIoU introduces a robust regression signal tolerant to annotation ambiguity. Together, these components act synergistically, yielding a detection framework whose collective performance exceeds the sum of its individual parts.

RepViT backbone

The backbone network is responsible for extracting multi-level feature representations from input images, and its expressive capability and computational efficiency directly influence subsequent defect detection performance. To make the backbone setting reproducible, this study adopts RepViT-M1 as the replacement backbone for YOLOv8n. RepViT-M1 is selected because it provides a favorable balance between feature representation capacity and computational cost. The overall structure of RepViT is illustrated in Fig. 2. The network consists of multiple cascaded stages, each composed of stacked RepViTBlocks. As the network deepens, spatial resolution is progressively reduced while semantic abstraction capability is enhanced, thereby achieving hierarchical modeling from shallow-level details to deep-level semantics.

CAFM feature enhancement module

Although RepViT can efficiently extract multi-level feature information, relying solely on the high-level features output by the backbone network remains insufficient to fully characterize the long-range dependencies and salient response features of defect regions in wind turbine blade detection scenarios. This limitation is particularly pronounced when defect boundaries are unclear, local contrast is low, and background textures are complex, as conventional convolution operations are constrained by their local receptive fields, resulting in inadequate representation capability for critical defect regions. To address this issue, we introduce Channel-Spatial Cooperative Attention Module (CAFM) at the end of the backbone to further enhance the contextual modeling capability and discriminative expression of high-level features.

SlimNeck with VoVGSCSP

For wind turbine blade defect detection tasks, different defect types exhibit significant variations in scale, shape, and spatial distribution. In particular, targets such as fine cracks, pitting, and edge damage typically occupy only a small number of pixels, and without effective multi-scale feature interaction, they are prone to being overwhelmed by background information during deep semantic propagation. To address the low efficiency of small-target feature propagation and the loss of shallow-level details inherent in the original PANet structure, this paper adopts SlimNeck to reconstruct the feature pyramid network in the Neck. SlimNeck strengthens the fusion of shallow localization information and deep semantic information through optimized cross-scale connection strategies, employing VoVGSCSP as the core feature aggregation unit to improve multi-scale feature representation efficiency while reducing computational overhead.

The VoVGSCSP module integrates the multi-path aggregation philosophy of VoVNet, the lightweight convolution characteristics of GSConv, and the cross-stage partial connection mechanism of CSPNet. Its overall structure is illustrated in Fig. 4. The module consists of three key components: a 1 × 1 convolution at the entry for channel adjustment, followed by a Split operation that divides features into a trunk branch and a bypass branch. The trunk branch comprises multiple cascaded GSBottleneck units, while the bypass branch directly preserves the original features to enhance gradient flow. Finally, intermediate outputs from all levels and bypass features are aggregated via Concat, followed by a 1 × 1 convolution for channel compression and mixing. The right side of Fig. 4 illustrates the internal structure of GSBottleneck, whose core consists of a residual connection between GSConv and a 1 × 1 convolution. GSConv achieves lightweight cross-channel information interaction through a combination of standard convolution, depth-wise separable convolution, and channel shuffle.

WIoU v3 bounding box regression loss

In object detection tasks, the optimization quality of bounding box regression is a critical factor affecting model localization accuracy. Existing IoU-based loss functions, such as GIoU, DIoU, and CIoU, have partially alleviated the gradient vanishing problem of conventional IoU loss in non-overlapping cases. However, these methods implicitly assume uniform annotation quality across training samples, defaulting all samples to participate in optimization in an approximately consistent manner. In practice, training data inevitably contains varying degrees of annotation noise and quality discrepancies due to data acquisition conditions, manual labeling errors, and target boundary ambiguity. Imposing excessively strong regression constraints on low-quality samples introduces unreliable gradient signals that interfere with model parameter updates.

To address this issue, this paper introduces Wise-IoU v3 (WIoU v3) as the bounding box regression loss function. WIoU v3 designs a dynamic non-monotonic focusing mechanism that adaptively adjusts the gradient contributions of samples with different quality levels through the introduction of an outlier degree metric, simultaneously suppressing the harmful interference from low-quality samples and preventing high-quality samples from excessively dominating the optimization direction in later training stages.

Dataset

The experiments in this paper are conducted on a wind turbine blade defect dataset in wind farm scenario sourced the Roboflow Universe public dataset repository. After re-integration,a new dataset is constructed. The dataset contains seven defect categories: burning, crack, deformity, dirty, peeling, rusty, and oil. All images have a uniform resolution of 640 × 640 pixels and are annotated using the X-AnyLabeling tool. After filtering out invalid and corrupted samples, a refined dataset comprising 4,467 valid images is constructed, with representative samples shown in Fig. 6. Effect of the RepViT backbone

With the sole introduction of the RepViT backbone, precision increases from 0.841 to 0.903 (+ 6.2%), recall from 0.766 to 0.807 (+ 4.1%), mAP@0.5 from 0.845 to 0.880 (+ 3.5%), and mAP@0.5:0.95 from 0.552 to 0.641 (+ 8.9%). These results demonstrate that RepViT effectively enhances the model’s capability for extracting wind turbine blade defect features by integrating local convolutional modeling with global context perception, establishing a solid feature foundation for subsequent module optimization.

Effect of the CAFM attention mechanism

Upon introducing CAFM on top of the RepViT backbone, precision further improves to 0.920 (+ 1.7%), and mAP@0.5:0.95 increases significantly from 0.641 to 0.692 (+ 5.1%). Through dual-dimensional adaptive feature recalibration along channel and spatial dimensions, CAFM enables the model to effectively focus on critical defect regions while suppressing complex background noise, with particularly notable improvements in multi-scale detection capability.

Effect of the SlimNeck neck structure

After introducing SlimNeck, precision reaches 0.937, the highest value among all configurations (+ 1.7%), and mAP@0.5 improves to 0.896 (+ 0.7%). SlimNeck further enhances the fusion efficiency between shallow localization information and deep semantic features through lightweight VoVGSCSP feature aggregation and optimized cross-scale connection strategies.

Effect of the WIoU loss function

Replacing CIoU with WIoU results in differentiated performance characteristics: precision decreases from 0.937 to 0.882 (−5.5%), while recall significantly improves from 0.832 to 0.875 (+ 4.3%), and mAP@0.5 increases from 0.896 to 0.903 (+ 0.7%). These results indicate that WIoU optimizes the bounding box regression strategy through its dynamic non-monotonic focusing mechanism, effectively improving the recall for small-scale and low-contrast hard-to-detect defects. This achieves a more favorable balance between precision and recall, better aligning with the practical engineering requirement of prioritizing miss rate reduction in wind turbine blade defect detection.

It is worth noting that WIoU reduces precision while significantly improving recall. From an engineering perspective, this trade-off is acceptable in wind turbine blade defect detection. In practical scenarios, missed detections are more critical than false positives, as undetected defects may propagate and lead to severe structural damage, whereas false positives can be easily filtered through manual inspection. Therefore, prioritizing recall helps reduce defect omission risk and improves inspection reliability, while the precision remains within an acceptable range.

In summary, each module contributes to model performance improvement from different dimensions in a collaborative manner. The final RCS-YOLOv8 (A + B + C + D) achieves improvements of 5.8 percentage points in mAP@0.5, 14.1 percentage points in mAP@0.5:0.95, and 10.9 percentage points in recall over the baseline YOLOv8n, validating the effectiveness of the proposed optimization strategies.

Overall performance analysis

RCS-YOLOv8 achieves 0.903 in mAP@0.5, outperforming the baseline YOLOv8n by 5.8 percentage points and the second-best method RT-DETR by 3.0 percentage points, representing the highest value among all compared models. In terms of model efficiency, RCS-YOLOv8 has 20.9 M parameters and 36.0 GFLOPs of computation, substantially lower than the 77.0 M/57.0 GFLOPs of RT-DETR, achieving a more favorable trade-off between detection accuracy and computational efficiency.

Per-category performance analysis

Among the seven defect categories, RCS-YOLOv8 achieves the highest mAP@0.5 in four categories: Crack, Dirty, Oil, and Rusty. The improvements are particularly notable for two critical defect types: Crack increases from 0.702 (YOLOv8n) to 0.860, an improvement of 15.8 percentage points, and Rusty increases from 0.859 to 0.946, an improvement of 8.7 percentage points. As the most threatening defect type for wind turbine blades, the substantial improvement in crack detection directly reflects the synergistic effect of RepViT’s global context modeling and CAFM’s attention focusing mechanism. For the Deformity and Peeling categories, RT-DETR achieves the highest values at 0.953 and 0.938, respectively, slightly outperforming RCS-YOLOv8’s 0.935 and 0.934. However, the margins do not exceed 2 percentage points, and RT-DETR incurs several times the computational overhead to achieve these results.

Comparative method analysis

The two-stage detector Faster R-CNN achieves only 0.789 in mAP@0.5 with a parameter count of 128.0 M, making it impractical for real-world deployment. SSD achieves only 0.615 in mAP@0.5 and merely 0.327 for the Crack category, exhibiting severe missed detection issues. Among the lightweight YOLO series models, YOLOv8n demonstrates the best overall performance with 0.845 mAP@0.5, yet achieves only 0.702 for the Crack category, indicating insufficient detection capability for critical defects. Notably, YOLOv12n achieves only 0.749 in mAP@0.5, the lowest among the YOLO series, suggesting that newer model versions do not necessarily adapt well to domain-specific tasks. Although YOLOv5n has the smallest parameter count (5.1 M), its mAP@0.5 of only 0.802 results in an overall accuracy-efficiency trade-off inferior to that of RCS-YOLOv8. RT-DETR achieves the best performance in the Burning, Deformity, and Peeling categories owing to its Transformer architecture, but its high computational overhead limits its applicability in engineering scenarios.

Conclusion

To address the challenges of complex environmental interference, missed detection of small-scale defects, and insufficient cross-scale detection capability in wind turbine blade defect detection, this paper proposes RCS-YOLOv8, a collaborative optimization detection model that integrates an efficient backbone network, attention-based feature enhancement, lightweight multi-scale fusion, and adaptive bounding box regression. The main conclusions are as follows:

The RepViT backbone network effectively enhances the model’s robustness against illumination variations and background texture interference through the collaborative design of local convolutional modeling and global context perception, achieving a 6.4 percentage point improvement in mAP@0.5:0.95 over the second-best backbone HGNetV2.

The RepViT backbone network effectively enhances the model’s robustness against illumination variations and background texture interference through the collaborative design of local convolutional modeling and global context perception, achieving a 6.4 percentage point improvement in mAP@0.5:0.95 over the second-best backbone HGNetV2.

The CAFM channel-spatial cooperative attention module effectively focuses on critical defect regions and suppresses complex background noise through dual-dimensional adaptive feature recalibration, contributing a 5.1 percentage point improvement in mAP@0.5:0.95.

The CAFM channel-spatial cooperative attention module effectively focuses on critical defect regions and suppresses complex background noise through dual-dimensional adaptive feature recalibration, contributing a 5.1 percentage point improvement in mAP@0.5:0.95.

The VoVGSCSP-based SlimNeck neck structure strengthens multi-scale information fusion efficiency through optimized cross-scale connections and lightweight feature aggregation, raising precision to the highest value of 0.937 in the ablation study.

The VoVGSCSP-based SlimNeck neck structure strengthens multi-scale information fusion efficiency through optimized cross-scale connections and lightweight feature aggregation, raising precision to the highest value of 0.937 in the ablation study.

The WIoU dynamic non-monotonic focusing loss function achieves adaptive gradient allocation through outlier degree modeling, improving recall by 4.3 percentage points while maintaining the highest mAP@0.5, effectively optimizing the balance between precision and recall.

Experimental results demonstrate that RCS-YOLOv8 achieves 0.903 mAP@0.5 on the wind turbine blade defect dataset, outperforming the baseline YOLOv8n by 5.8 percentage points and the second-best method RT-DETR by 3.0 percentage points, with a recall of 0.875, the highest among all compared models. The proposed method provides a reliable technical solution for automated wind turbine blade defect detection. Future work will focus on optimizing adaptability under extreme operating conditions and lightweight deployment, exploring real-time online detection schemes for edge computing platforms.Nevertheless, it is important to acknowledge that the current validation is confined to offline analysis on a curated dataset. The deployment of RCS-YOLOv8 in authentic wind farm environments, which involve dynamic UAV flight conditions, unstable data transmission, and real-time processing constraints, has not yet been fully verified. Therefore, future work will prioritize the optimization of model adaptability under extreme on-site conditions and the implementation of a lightweight, real-time detection system on edge computing devices. Extensive field tests on operational wind turbines will be conducted to evaluate the model’s robustness, inference latency, and practical reliability in a production setting.
