Abstract

1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on studying projection to a restricted family of
hyperplanes for any analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We first fix several notations.
Denote by
\pi_{W}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to W
the orthogonal projection to the (
k
-dimensional,
k=1,\cdots,n-1
) subspace
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and by
T_{x}M
the tangent space at
x\in M
of a differentiable manifold
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The notation
\dim
will always refer to Hausdorff dimension.

In
[
3
]
, Fässler and Orponen conjectured that

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
Z
be an analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
{\gamma}\in C^{2}([0,1],S^{2})
be a non-degenerate curve, that is

(1.2)

{\rm span}\{\gamma(\theta),\dot{\gamma}(\theta),\ddot{\gamma}(\theta)\}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

Then

(i)

\dim[\pi_{{\rm span}\{\gamma(\theta)\}}(Z)]=\min\{\dim Z,1\},\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}\mbox{-\rm a.e.}\ \theta\in[0,1];

(ii)

\dim[\pi_{T_{\gamma(\theta)}S^{2}}(Z)]=\min\{\dim Z,2\},\quad\mathcal{H}^{1}\mbox{-\rm a.e.}\ \theta\in[0,1].

Conjecture
1.1
(i) was confirmed by Pramanik-Yang-Zahl
[
18
]
and Conjecture
1.1
(ii) was confirmed by Gan-Guo-Guth-Harris-Maldague-Wang
[
4
]
.

A higher
dimensional extension of Conjecture
1.1
(i) was studied in
[
13
]
, which states that

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
Z
be an analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold with sectional curvature
>1
.
Then

\dim[\pi_{{\rm span}\{x\}}(Z)]=\min\{\dim Z,1\},\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mbox{-\rm a.e.}\ x\in\Sigma.

This paper focuses on higher dimensional extension of Conjecture
1.1
(ii) in the spirit of Theorem
1.3
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For
n\geq 4
, let
Z
be an analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold with sectional curvature
>1
.
Then

\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{n-1}}(Z)]=\min\{\dim Z,n-1\},\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\ a.e.\ x\in\Sigma.

In this paper, we show that Conjecture
1.4
is true if
\dim Z\leq n-2
and we also make partial progress when
\dim Z>n-2
. Note that the non-degenerate condition (
1.2
) is equivalent to
\gamma
having non-vanishing geodesic curvature (c.f.
[
13
, Page 3]
).

Theorem 1.5
.

For
n\geq 3
, let
Z
be an analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim Z\leq n-2
and
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold having

•

non-vanishing geodesic curvature if
n=3
;

•

sectional curvature
>1
if
n\geq 4
.

Then, for any
s<\dim Z
, we have

\dim\{x\in\Sigma:\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{n-1}}(Z)]<s\}\leq s.

In particular,

\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{n-1}}(Z)]=\dim Z,\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mbox{-\rm a.e.}\ x\in\Sigma.

Remark 1.6
.

When
n=3
, under the same assumption on
\Sigma
, Gan-Guo-Guth-Harris-Maldague-Wang
[
4
, Theorem 1]
showed that for any
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

\dim\{x\in\Sigma:\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{2}}(Z)]<s\}\leq s+1-\dim Z.

Thus, if
\dim Z<1
, Theorem
1.5
improves their estimate.

Theorem 1.7
.

For
n\geq 3
, let
Z
be an analytic set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim Z>n-2
and
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be as in Theorem
1.5
.
Moreover, if there exists
y\in S^{n-1}
such that

(1.8)

\dim[\pi_{T_{y}S^{n-1}}(Z)]\leq n-2,

then,

\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{n-1}}(Z)]=\min\{\dim Z,n-1\},\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mbox{-\rm a.e.}\ x\in\Sigma.

Remark 1.9
.

(i) Notice that for sets
Z
with
n-2<\dim Z\leq n-1
, it is easy to see that
\dim[\pi_{W}(Z)]\geq\dim Z-1
for all hyperplanes
W
. Since
\dim Z-1\leq n-2
, assumption (
1.8
) is realisable.

(ii) If one wants to fully resolve Conjecture
1.4
, it suffices to further consider sets
Z
with
n-2<\dim Z\leq n-1
such that for some
y\in S^{n-1}
,

(1.10)

\dim[\pi_{T_{y}S^{n-1}}(Z)]\leq s\mbox{ for some $s<\dim Z$}.

Otherwise,
\dim[\pi_{T_{x}S^{n-1}}(Z)]=\dim Z
everywhere and the conjecture holds for
Z
trivially.
Thus, if one could relax the condition (
1.8
) in Theorem
1.7
to (
1.10
), one resolves Conjecture
1.4
.

Example 1.11
.

(i) Circles in
S^{2}
that are not equators have non-vanishing geodesic curvature. Similarly,
(n-2)
-dimensional spheres that are not equatorial spheres have sectional curvature
>1
.
In particular, let
\Sigma_{c}:=S^{n-1}\cap\{x_{n}=c\}
for
c\in(0,1)\cup(-1,0)
. Indeed, it is straightforward to check that
\Sigma_{c}
has geodesic curvature
\pm c/\sqrt{1-c^{2}}\neq 0
(depending on the orientation of
\Sigma_{c}
) when
n=3
and
sectional curvature
1/(1-c^{2})>1
when
n\geq 4
.

(ii) Other examples include small
C^{2}
-perturbations of circles and spheres in (i), since the curvature condition in Theorem
1.5
is stable under small perturbations.

1.1.
Background of (Restricted) Projections

Projection properties was first established by Marstrand
[
14
]
who showed that the orthogonal projection of any Borel set
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
to almost every line has dimension
\min\{1,\dim Z\}
. For all
n\geq 2
and
1\leq m\leq n
, Mattila
[
15
]
showed that the orthogonal projection of any Borel set
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
to almost every
W\in G(n,m)
has dimension
\min\{m,\dim Z\}
where
G(n,m)
is the
m(n-m)
-dimensional Grassmann manifold of all

m
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and “almost every” refers to the
m(n-m)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
G(n,m)
.

Restricted projection focuses on studying orthogonal projection of a set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a family of
m
-subspaces which forms a submanifold
\Sigma\subset G(n,m)
. Noting that when
m=1
or
m=n-1
,
G(n,m)
can be naturally identified with the unit sphere
S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, when
n=3
,
\dim G(3,1)=\dim G(3,2)=2
, thus the only meaningful submanifold
\Sigma\subset G(3,m)
(
m=1,2
) to consider in the restricted projection is
1
-dimensional, which is a curve in
S^{2}
. These cases are relatively well understood by the aforementioned Conjecture
1.1
and its resolution in
[
18
,
4
]
, together with other previous partial progress
[
10
,
3
,
9
,
1
,
8
,
17
,
6
,
7
,
11
]
, etc.
For the case
\Sigma\subset G(n,1)
being a
C^{\infty}
-curve, certain restricted projection property was established by Gan-Guo-Wang
[
5
]
.
In general, when
2\leq m\leq n-2
and
1\leq\dim\Sigma\leq\dim G(n,m)
, the study of restricted projection are widely open.

Remark 1.12
.

We remark that the case
n=3
in Theorem
1.5
is also a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
(see also
[
3
, Proposition 1.5]
) using the sublevel set technique pioneered by Kaufman
[
12
]
. One could also run this technique for
n\geq 4
in Theorem
1.5
(e.g. following Page 8-9 in
[
3
]
) and it however, only works for sets
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim Z\leq 1
instead of
\dim Z\leq n-2
in Theorem
1.5
. The reason is that the proof relies on a sublevel set estimate established in
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
for
n=3
. When
n\geq 4
, one could use the corresponding sublevel set estimate
[
2
, Lemma 3.4]
and obtain that Theorem
1.5
holds for
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\dim Z\leq 1
.

1.2.
Ideas of the Proof of Theorem
1.5

Let
Z\subset B(o,1/2)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an analytic set with

t:=\dim Z\in(0,n-2].

By proving the theorem on each local chart of
\Sigma
, we can assume
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism from
[0,1]^{n-2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and therefore throughout the paper we assume
\Sigma\in C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2},\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be a
C^{2}
diffeomorphism.
Thus the differential
\nabla\Sigma(x)
at
x
maps
\{\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{n-2}\}
(standard basis of
[0,1]^{n-2}
) to a basis

\{\nabla\Sigma(x)\cdot\partial_{1},\cdots,\nabla\Sigma(x)\cdot\partial_{n-2}\}

of
T_{\Sigma(x)}\Sigma
.
Write

(1.13)

e_{i}=e_{i}(x)=M_{\Sigma}^{-1}\nabla\Sigma(x)\cdot\partial_{i},\quad\forall i=1,\cdots,n-2

where
M_{\Sigma}:=\max_{x\in\Sigma}\{\nabla\Sigma(x)\}

and

(1.14)

P_{x}:=T_{\Sigma(x)}S^{n-1}=T_{\Sigma(x)}\Sigma\oplus{\rm span}\{\nu\}={\rm span}\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n-2},\nu\}

where
\nu
is the unit normal of
\Sigma
in
S^{n-1}
.
Thus, in coordinates, the projection
\pi_{P_{x}}
can be written as

\pi_{P_{x}}(z)=(e_{1}\cdot z,\cdots,e_{n-2}\cdot z,\nu\cdot z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Indeed,
P_{x}
is a distorted orthogonal projection and for any set
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\pi_{P_{x}}(Z)
has the same dimension as the orthogonal projection of
Z
onto
T_{\Sigma(x)}S^{n-1}
.

If
z\in B(o,1/2)
is fixed, then the map

(1.15)

[0,1]^{n-2}\ni x\mapsto f_{z}(x):=(e_{1}(x)\cdot z,\cdots,e_{n-2}(x)\cdot z,\nu(x)\cdot z)\in[0,1]^{n-1}

maps
[0,1]^{n-2}
into
[0,1]^{n-1}
.
The graph
f_{z}
of this map is an
(n-2)
-dimension manifold in
[0,1]^{2n-3}
:

\mbox{graph}(f_{z})(x):=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},e_{1}(x)\cdot z,\cdots,e_{n-2}(x)\cdot z,\nu(x)\cdot z)\quad x\in[0,1]^{n-2}.

For instance, if
n=3
,
\mbox{graph}(f_{z})
is a curve in
[0,1]^{3}
for each
z\in Z
.
Observing that

\bigcup_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2}}\pi_{P_{x}}(Z)=\bigcup_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2}}\bigcup_{z\in Z}\pi_{P_{x}}(z)=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\bigcup_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2}}(x,\pi_{P_{x}}(z))=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\mbox{graph}(f_{z}),

we see that the study of restricted projection can be transferred to the study of the union of graphs of
\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}
.

To be precise,
in order to show Theorem
1.5
, fix
0<s<s^{\prime}\leq\dim Z\leq n-2
and choose any subset
X\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
with
\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}(X)>0
.
Then the fact that Theorem
1.5
holds is equivalent to that

(1.16)

\dim(\pi_{P_{x}}(Z))=\dim(\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})])\geq s,\quad\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}\mbox{-a.e. }x\in X.

For any
z\in Z
, let
X_{z}\subset X
with
\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}(X_{z})\sim\mathcal{H}^{s^{\prime}}(X)
and consider the set

(1.17)

\mathcal{E}:=\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(X_{z}\times[0,1]^{n-1})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})]=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\bigcup_{x\in X_{z}}(x,f_{z}(x))\subset[0,1]^{2n-3}.

We will further verify (
1.16
) by showing that
\mathcal{E}
has dimension lower bound

(1.18)

\dim\mathcal{E}\geq s^{\prime}+s.

This dimension estimate will be done by a
\delta
-discretised proof on the following estimate:

Theorem 1.19
.

For
0<s\leq s^{\prime}\leq 1
and
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon,s,s^{\prime},n)\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
:

|\mathcal{E}|_{\delta}\geq\delta^{16\epsilon-s-s^{\prime}}

where
|\cdot|_{\delta}
stands for the
\delta
-covering number of a set.

We briefly explain how to show Theorem
1.19
. Given
\eta>0
and a subdomain
D\subset[0,1]^{m}
, the vertical
\eta
-neighborhood of the graph of
f:D\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
is

(1.20)

f^{\eta}(D):=\{(x,y)\in D\times\mathbb{R}^{l}:|(x,y)-(x,f(x))|=|y-f(x)|<\eta\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+l}.

Noting that
\mathcal{E}
is a union of subsets contained in graphs of
\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}
. To get the desired lower bound,
the core of the proof of Theorem
1.19
is to upper bound the volume of the intersection
f^{\delta}_{z}\cap f^{\delta}_{z^{\prime}}
of two
\delta
-vertical neighbourhoods in
[0,1]^{2n-3}
.
We have the following estimate.

Lemma 1.21
.

We have

\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}(f^{\delta}_{z}([0,1]^{n-2})\cap f^{\delta}_{z^{\prime}}([0,1]^{n-2}))\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2n-3}}{\|f_{z}-f_{z^{\prime}}\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}^{n-2}}

where
\mathcal{L}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional Lebsegue measure.

In fact, we will show the estimate holds for a more general family of maps and also show Theorem
1.19
holds in a more general setting. See Lemma
2.8
in Section
2
and Theorem in Section
3
respectively.

The estimate in Lemma
1.21
shows that the intersection looks like the
\delta
-neighbourhood of the intersection of two
(n-2)
-dimensional planes intersecting at a point (e.g. span
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
and span
\{x_{3},x_{4}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
when
n=3
).
The proof relies on the fact that the image

(1.22)

\Sigma^{\ast}:=\nu([0,1]^{n-2})\subset S^{n-1}

of the normal vector field
\nu:[0,1]^{n-2}\to S^{n-1}

is a
C^{2}
-manifold with non-vanishing geodesic curvature if
n=3
or sectional curvature
>1
if
n\geq 4
, which is a consequence of
\Sigma
having non-vanishing geodesic curvature/sectional curvature
>1
(see Lemma
2.16
in Section
2
). We will call
\Sigma^{\ast}
the dual manifold of
\Sigma
.

This completes the outline of the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

1.3.
Ideas of the Proof of Theorem
1.7

Let
Z\subset B(o,1/2)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an analytic set with

t:=\dim Z\in(n-2,n-1].

By the same argument as in the previous section and under the same notation, recalling (
1.16
) - (
1.18
),
the fact that Theorem
1.7
holds is equivalent to that

(1.23)

\dim(\pi_{P_{x}}(Z))=\dim(\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})])=t,\ \mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mbox{-a.e. }x\in[0,1]^{n-2}.

Again, for each
z\in Z
, let
X_{z}\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
with
\mathcal{L}^{n-2}(X_{z})\sim 1
and consider the set

(1.24)

\mathcal{Z}:=\bigcup_{z\in Z}[(X_{z}\times[0,1]^{n-1})\cap\mbox{graph}(f_{z})]=\bigcup_{z\in Z}\bigcup_{x\in X_{z}}(x,f_{z}(x))\subset[0,1]^{2n-3}.

and (
1.23
) will be verified by showing that
\mathcal{E}
has dimension

(1.25)

\dim\mathcal{Z}=n-2+\dim Z=n-2+t,

which will be further guaranteed by the following lemma.

Lemma 1.26
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon)\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
such that the following holds for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
:

|\mathcal{Z}|_{\delta}\gtrsim\delta^{3\epsilon-(n-2+t)}.

We give the heuristic idea to show Lemma
1.26
. We choose a
\delta
-separated subset of
Z
(still denoted by
Z
) with cardinality
\sim\delta^{-t}
and in the following of this section
Z
will always refers to this discrete set .
It suffices to show

\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}(\bigcup_{z\in Z}f_{z}^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2}))\gtrsim\delta^{O(\epsilon)+2n-3-(t+n-2)}=\delta^{O(\epsilon)+n-1-t}.

However, only the volume estimate in Lemma
1.21
is not enough for this lower bound. We also need to control the total number of pairs
(z,z^{\prime})\in Z\times Z
such that
f_{z}^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})\cap f_{z^{\prime}}^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})\neq\emptyset
. This is because when
t>n-2
, the total number of pairs in
Z\times Z
is
\delta^{-2t}>\delta^{-2(n-2)}
which is large.
Hence,
we will obtain Lemma
1.21
by establishing a line-cone incidence estimate together with the help of the assumption (
1.8
) in Theorem
1.7
. We sketch the ideas below.

Note that
f_{z}=f_{z^{\prime}}
at
x
, i.e.
f_{z}(x)=f_{z^{\prime}}(x)
is equivalent to that

\pi_{P_{x}}(z)=\pi_{P_{x}}(z^{\prime}),

which, together with
P_{x}^{\perp}=(T_{\Sigma(x)}S^{n-1})^{\perp}=
span
\{\Sigma(x)\}
implies that

z^{\prime}=z+u_{z}\Sigma(x)\quad\mbox{ for some $u_{z}\neq 0$},

since all points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which have same image as
z
under
\pi_{P_{x}}
are exactly the line
z+\mathbb{R}\Sigma(x)
.
This shows that for a fixed point
z\in Z
, all other points
z^{\prime}\in Z\subset B(o,1/2)
such that
f_{z}\cap f_{z^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
are contained in

\mathcal{C}_{z}:=\bigcup_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2},r\in[-1,1]}z+r\Sigma(x)=:\bigcup_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2}}L_{z,x},

which is a cone with apex
z
and cross-section
\Sigma
.
If
\Sigma
is the
(n-2)
-sphere
\Sigma_{c}
in Example
1.11
(i),
\mathcal{C}_{z}
is a circular cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with opening angle
\arccos c
.
We call the line segments
\{L_{z,x}\}_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2}}
generatrices of
\mathcal{C}_{z}
. Obviously,
\mathcal{C}_{z}
is the union of all its generatrices.
We conclude that

f_{z}\cap f_{z^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{z}.

Thus in the
\delta
-discretised version, we will prove the following relation: for any
z\in Z
,

(1.27)

|\{z^{\prime}\in Z:f_{z}^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})\cap f_{z^{\prime}}^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})\neq\emptyset\}|\sim|\{z^{\prime}\in Z:z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{z}^{\delta}\}|.

where
A^{\delta}
be the
\delta
-neighbourhood in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We have the following theorem on the incidence of a line and the cone
\mathcal{C}_{x}
. Recall that the angle of a line and a subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the angle of the line and its orthogonal projection to that subspace.

Theorem 1.28
.

Let
\Sigma
be as in Theorem
1.7
.
If a line
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies
\min_{p\in\mathcal{C}_{z}}\{\angle(L,T_{p}\mathcal{C}_{z})\}\geq a
for some
a\gg\delta>0
, then

\mathcal{L}^{n}(\mathcal{C}_{z}^{\delta}\cap L^{\delta})\lesssim_{a}\delta^{n}.

where
\angle(L,T_{p}\mathcal{C}_{z})
is the Euclidean angle between
L
and the tangent space
T_{p}\mathcal{C}_{z}
.

The proof of this theorem is geometric and relies on the curvature condition of
\Sigma
in Theorem
1.7
.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.28
, recalling that
Z
is
\delta
-separated, we know that

(1.29)

|\mathcal{C}_{z}^{\delta}\cap L^{\delta}\cap Z|\lesssim_{a}1,\quad\forall z\in Z.

Finally, under the assumption (
1.8
), that is,

(1.30)

s:=\dim[\pi_{T_{y}S^{n-1}}(Z)]\leq n-2<t,

we can efficiently upper bound the quantity in (
1.27
) as follows.

Let
Z_{y}:=\pi_{T_{y}S^{n-1}}(Z)
. By (

2.
Cinematic Maps and Their Intersections

We define a class of cinematic maps and show that the intersection of any two cinematic maps
satisfies the upper bound in Lemma
1.21
. Then, we check that the maps
\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}
arising from the restricted projection are cinematic maps.

2.1.
Cinematic Maps

Definition 2.1

(Cinematic Map)
.

Let
F\subset C^{2}(U,R^{l})
where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a domain,
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
with
l\geq k
. We say
F
is a cinematic family of
maps, with cinematic constant
K
and doubling constant
D
if the following conditions
hold:

(1)

F
is contained in a ball of diameter
K
under the usual metric on
C^{2}(U,R^{l})
;

(2)

F
is a doubling metric space, with doubling constant at most
D
;

(3)

For all
f,g\in F
, we have

\inf_{(x,\xi)\in U\times S^{k-1}}\{|f(x)-g(x)|+|\nabla_{\xi}f(x)-\nabla_{\xi}g(x)|\}\geq K^{-1}\|f-g\|_{C^{2}(U)}

where
\nabla_{\xi}h=\nabla h\cdot\xi
for
h=f,g
, noting that
\nabla h
is a linear map from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{l}
and
\xi\in S^{k-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is considered as a direction of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We make a remark about (3) in Definition
2.1
. Let us assume
|f(x)-g(x)|=0
at some
x
. Then (3) implies that

\inf_{\xi\in S^{k-1}}|\nabla_{\xi}f(x)-\nabla_{\xi}g(x)|\sim_{K}\|f-g\|_{C^{1}(U)}.

This condition guarantees that whenever
f(x)=g(x)
, the smallest singular value of
\nabla(f-g)(x)
is not zero, i.e.
\nabla(f-g)(x)
as an
(l\times k)
-matrix has full rank.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\eta>0
,
n\geq 3
.
Let

F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2},[0,1]^{n-1})
be a cinematic family with cinematic constant
K
and
f,g\in F
.
If
U\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
is a (closed) dyadic cube such that its diameter is smaller than
\frac{\eta}{2K}
, and
k
is one of the functions
|f-g|,|\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
for some
\xi\in S^{n-3}
, then
k
satisfies either one of the following two inequalities:

(S)

k(x)<\eta\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\mbox{ for all }x\in U
.

(L)

k(x)\geq\eta\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}/2\mbox{ for all }x\in U
.

Proof.

Write
d=\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}
.
Since
F
is contained in a ball of diameter
K
, we know

t\leq K
. In particular, we know Lip
(f-g)(x)\leq K
and Lip
|\nabla(f-g)|(x)\leq K
for all
x\in U
. We have

|k(x)-k(x^{\prime})|\leq\max_{y\in U}\mbox{{\rm Lip} }k(y)\cdot|x-x^{\prime}|\leq K\operatorname{diam}U<\frac{\eta}{2},\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in U,

where
k
is one of the functions
|f-g|,|\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
.

Next, suppose that the
alternative (L) fails for
k=f-g
. So there is
x_{0}\in U
such that
|k(x_{0})|<\eta d/2
. And for any
x\in U
, since
|x-x_{0}|<\eta/(2K)
, by defining
l=k/d
, we deduce that Lip
{}_{[0,1]^{n-2}}l\leq 1
and

\frac{|k(x)|}{d}\leq\frac{|k(x_{0})|}{d}+\left|\frac{|k(x)|}{d}-\frac{|k(x_{0})|}{d}\right|<\frac{\eta}{2}+|l(x)-l(x_{0})|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}+\mbox{ [Lip${}_{[0,1]^{n-2}}l$}]|x-x_{0}|\leq\eta.

Thus the alternative (S) holds for
k
. The proof of the case
k=|\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)|
is the
same.
∎

The above lemma gives the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let

F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2},[0,1]^{n-1})
be a cinematic family of maps with cinematic constant
K
,
f,g\in F
and
h:=f-g
. Then for each dyadic cube
U\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
with
{\rm diam\,}U<\frac{1}{4K^{2}}
,

(A):
at least one of the following two conditions hold:

(i)

|h(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2K}\|h\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}
for all
x\in U
.

(ii)

|\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\geq\frac{1}{2K}\|h\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}
for all
x\in U
and
\xi\in S^{n-3}
.

(B):
Moreover, if (A)(ii) holds, then

(2.5)

|h(x)|+|h(y)|\geq\frac{\|h\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}}{4K}|x-y|,\quad\forall x,y\in U.

Proof.

We first show (A):
We apply Lemma
2.3
to
h
with
\eta=1/(2K)
. As a result, we know for each dyadic cube
U\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
with diameter
{\rm diam\,}U<\frac{1}{4K^{2}}
,
either (S) or (L) holds for
k
and all
x\in U
, where
k
is one of the
functions
h
, or
\nabla_{\xi}h
. On the other hand,
since
F
is a cinematic family of functions, if the alternative (L) does not hold for the functions
h
, then by Definition
2.1
(3), for all
\xi\in S^{n-2}
, (L) holds for
\nabla_{\xi}h=\nabla_{\xi}(f-g)
.

We show (B). Fix
x,y\in U
. Note that if
x=y
, (
2.5
) holds trivially. We thus assume

x\neq y
and write

y=x+\xi_{y}|y-x|,\mbox{ where }\xi_{y}=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\in S^{n-3}

By Definition
2.1
(1), we know that

d:=\|h\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\leq 2K<\infty

and hence for any
s\in[0,|x-y|]
, we have

|\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x+s\xi_{y})-\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x)|\leq{\rm Lip}(\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h)\cdot|x+s\xi_{y}-x|\leq ds.

Let

v(s):=\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x+s\xi_{y})-\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x),s\in[0,|x-y|].

We deduce that

(2.6)

|v(s)|\leq ds\leq d|x-y|,\quad s\in[0,|x-y|].

We compute

\displaystyle|h(x)|+|h(y)|

\displaystyle\geq|h(y)-h(x)|=|\int_{0}^{|x-y|}\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x+s\xi_{y})\,ds|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{|x-y|}\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x)\,ds+\int_{0}^{|x-y|}v(s)\,ds|

\displaystyle\geq|\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x)||x-y|-|\int_{0}^{|x-y|}v(s)\,ds|.

Since
\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h
satisfies (A)(ii), we have
|\nabla_{\xi_{y}}h(x)|\geq\frac{d}{2K}
. Combining (
2.6
), we deduce that

|h(x)|+|h(y)|\geq\frac{d}{2K}|x-y|-d|x-y|^{2}=d|x-y|(\frac{1}{2K}-|x-y|).

Since
\operatorname{diam}U<\frac{1}{4K^{2}}
, we know
\frac{1}{2K}-|x-y|>\frac{1}{4K}
, which gives (
2.5
) as desired.

The proof is complete.
∎

Recall from (
1.20
) that for arbitrary
\eta>0
and
open set
D\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
, the vertical
\eta
-neighborhood of the graph of
f:D\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
is

f^{\eta}(D):=\{(x,y)\in D\times\mathbb{R}^{l}:|(x,y)-(x,f(x))|=|y-f(x)|<\eta\}.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
f\in C^{1}(D,\mathbb{R}^{l})
, then

\mathcal{L}^{n-2+l}(f^{\eta}(D))\lesssim\eta^{l}\cdot\|f\|_{C^{1}(D)}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n-2}(D).

Proof.

Note that

f^{\eta}(D)=\bigcup_{|\eta^{\prime}|<\eta}(D,f(D)+\eta^{\prime}).

Write
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{l})
where
f_{i}\in C^{1}(D)
,
i=1,\cdots,l
.
We furthermore have

f^{\eta}(D)=\bigcup_{|\eta^{\prime}|<\eta}(D,f_{1}(D)+\eta^{\prime},\cdots,f_{l}(D)+\eta^{\prime}).

Moreover, since the set
(D,f(D))
has
(n-2)
-Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}((D,f(D)))\leq\|f\|_{C^{1}(D)}\mathcal{L}^{n-2}(D)
, we deduce that

\mathcal{L}^{n-2+l}(f^{\eta}(D))\lesssim\eta^{l}\cdot\|f\|_{C^{1}(D)}\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n-2}(D),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\delta>0
,

F\subset C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2},[0,1]^{n-1})
be a cinematic family with cinematic constant
K
. Then, for any
f,g\in F
with
d:=\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\geq\delta
, we have

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}[f^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})\cap g^{\delta}([0,1]^{n-2})]\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2n-3}}{\|f-g\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}^{n-2}}.

Proof.

By
decomposing
[0,1]^{n-2}
into dyadic cubes
\{U\}
with with
{\rm diam\,}U<\frac{1}{4K^{2}}
to prove (
2.9
), it suffices to show

\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}(f^{\delta}(U)\cap g^{\delta}(U))\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2n-3}}{d^{n-2}}

for each
U\subset[0,1]^{n-2}
.
In the following, we fix
U
and write
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}=f^{\delta}(U)\cap g^{\delta}(U)
for simplicity.

If
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}=\emptyset
, then there is nothing to prove. Also, since
d\in[\delta,1]
, if
\delta\leq d\leq 4K\delta
,
then using Lemma
2.7
and noting that
\|f\|_{C^{1}(U)}\leq K
, we know that

\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}(f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta})\leq\mathcal{L}^{2n-3}(f^{\delta})\sim\delta^{n-1}\sim\frac{\delta^{n-1}d^{n-2}}{d^{n-2}}\sim\frac{\delta^{2n-3}}{d^{n-2}}.

Thus in the following, we assume

(2.10)

d>4K\delta.

Assume
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
P_{f,g}^{U}
be the orthogonal projection of

f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}
from
U\times[0,1]^{n-1}
to
U
.
We have

f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}\subset f^{\delta}(P_{f,g}^{U}).

Thus
by Lemma
2.7
,
we need to show

(2.11)

\mathcal{L}^{n-2}(P_{f,g}^{U})\lesssim\frac{\delta^{n-2}}{d^{n-2}}.

Write
h=f-g
. By
f^{\delta}\cap g^{\delta}\neq\emptyset
, we know there exists
x\in U
such that
|h(x)|\leq\delta<\frac{d}{4K}
which implies
|h|
does not enjoy Corollary
2.4
(i).
Hence Corollary
2.4
(A)(ii) holds and
we can apply Corollary (
2.4
) (B) to deduce that

|h(y)|\geq\frac{d}{4K}|x-y|-|h(x)|\geq\frac{d}{4K}|x-y|-\delta.

Thus if
|x-y|>16K^{2}\delta/t
,

(2.12)

|h(y)|=|f(y)-g(y)|\geq 4K\delta.

We claim that if
|x-y|>16K^{2}\delta/d
, then
(y,g(y))\notin f^{\delta}
, that is, for all
z\in U
we have

(2.13)

|(y,g(y))-(z,f(z))|>\delta.

Assume the claim holds. Then we can infer that

y\notin P_{f,g}^{U},\mbox{ if }|x-y|>16K^{2}\delta/d.

As a consequence

(2.14)

P_{f,g}^{U}\subset B(x,16K^{2}\delta/d)\subset U\subset[0,1]^{n-2},

which gives (
2.11
) as desired.

We are left to show (
2.13
).
Noting that if
|z-y|>\delta
, then (
2.13
) can not hold.
Thus it only suffices to consider
z\in U
with
|z-y|\leq\delta
.

Since
\|f\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\leq K
, we know

|f(z)-f(z^{\prime})|\leq K|z-z^{\prime}|,\quad z,z^{\prime}\in U.

Thus for all
z\in U
with
|z-y|\leq\delta
, recalling (
2.12
), we have

|g(y)-f(z)|\geq|g(y)-f(y)|-|f(z)-f(y)|\geq 3K\delta,

which implies that

|(y,g(y))-(z,f(z))|\geq|g(y)-f(z)|\geq 3K\delta,\quad z\in B(y,\delta)\cap U.

Thus (
2.13
) holds and
the proof of Lemma
2.8
is complete.
∎

2.2.
\{f_{z}\}_{z\in Z}
is a Cinematic Family

Recall the unit normal
\nu
defined in (
1.14
) and the dual surface
\Sigma^{\ast}=\nu([0,1]^{n-2})\subset S^{n-1}
in (
1.22
).

Example 2.15
.

Recall from Example
1.11
(i) that

\Sigma_{c}:=S^{n-1}\cap\{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}):x_{n}=c\},\quad c\in(-1,0)\cup(0,1).

Then one could easily check that the dual
\Sigma^{\ast}_{c}
of
\Sigma_{c}
is

\Sigma_{c}^{\ast}=\Sigma_{\sqrt{1-c^{2}}}.

We also note that
(\Sigma_{c}^{\ast})^{\ast}=\Sigma_{c}
.

We study the geometric property of the dual
\Sigma^{\ast}
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
be an
(n-2)
-dimensional
C^{2}
manifold having

(i)

non-vanishing geodesic curvature if
n=3
;

(ii)

sectional curvature
>1
if
n\geq 4
.

Then,
\Sigma^{\ast}
also satisfies (i) and (ii) respectively.

Moreover,

(a)

if
\kappa_{1}
,
\cdots
,
\kappa_{n-2}
are the geodesic curvature (
n=3
) or principal curvatures (
n\geq 4
) at
x\in\Sigma
, then
\kappa_{1}^{-1}
,
\cdots
,
\kappa_{n-2}^{-1}
are the geodesic curvature/principal curvatures at
\nu(x)\in\Sigma^{\ast}
;

(b)

for each
x\in\Sigma
, we have

T_{\nu(x)}\Sigma^{\ast}=T_{x}\Sigma\mbox{ and }T_{\nu(x)}S^{n-1}=T_{\nu(x)}\Sigma^{\ast}\oplus{\rm span}\{x\}.

Proof.

If
n=3
, the lemma is already known in
[
4
, Page 9]
. Thus we only prove the case
n\geq 4
and for
(a),(b)
, the case
n=3
is an easier modification of the following proof. Since
\Sigma\subset S^{n-1}
, Gauss’ equation reads as

K_{\xi_{i},\xi_{j}}(x)=\kappa_{i}(x)\kappa_{j}(x)+1,\quad x\in\Sigma

where
\{\xi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n-2}\subset T_{x}\Sigma
are the
n-2
principal directions,

K_{e_{i},e_{j}}(x)
is the sectional curvature of
\Sigma
at
x
spanned by
\xi_{i}
and
\xi_{j}
and
\kappa_{i}(x)
is the principal curvature in the direction
\xi_{i}
. Thus the sectional curvature
>1
is equivalent to that the
n-2
principal curvatures at each
x\in\Sigma
,
\kappa_{1}(x),\cdots,\kappa_{n-2}(x)
are non-vanishing and have same sign. Fixing an orientation of the normal vector
\nu
, we assume
\kappa_{i}(x)>0
for all
x\in\Sigma
and
i=1,\cdots,n-2
.

It suffices to check that principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}^{\ast}
of the dual manifold
\Sigma^{\ast}
are positive at all
y\in\Sigma^{\ast}
.

For each
y\in\Sigma^{\ast}
,
y=\nu(x)
for some
x\in\Sigma
.
The tangent space
T_{y}\Sigma^{\ast}
of
\Sigma^{\ast}
at
y
is the space
\nabla\nu(T_{x}\Sigma)
. Since
\nabla\nu
is the Gauss map,
\nabla\nu
is also the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
and thus has real eigenvalues, which are precisely the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,n-2
. Recalling that the principal directions

\{\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}(x)\}_{i=1,\cdots,n-2}
are the eigenvectors of
\nabla\nu(x)
, we have

(2.17)

\xi_{i}^{\ast}:=\nabla\nu(\xi_{i})=\kappa_{i}\xi_{i},\quad i=1,\cdots,n-2.

Thus
\{\xi_{i}^{\ast}\}_{i=1,\cdots,n-2}
also forms a basis of
T_{y}\Sigma^{\ast}
.
Noting that

\mathbb{R}^{n}={\rm span}\{x,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n-2},\nu(x)=y\}={\rm span}\{x,\nu(x)=y\}\oplus T_{y}\Sigma^{\ast},

we deduce that
the normal vector
\nu^{\ast}(y)
which satisfies

\nu^{\ast}(y)\subset T_{y}S^{n-1}\mbox{ and }\nu^{\ast}(y)\perp T_{y}\Sigma^{\ast}

is
x
, i.e.
\nu^{\ast}(y)=\nu^{\ast}(\nu(x))=x
for all
y=\nu(x)
,
x\in\Sigma
. Thus (b) holds.
In particular,
\nu^{\ast}\circ\nu=
Id and
\nabla(\nu^{\ast}\circ\nu)=\nabla
Id
=
Id where Id is the identity map.

We then compute the principal curvatures of
\Sigma^{\ast}
at
y
, which are the eigenvalues of
\nabla\nu^{\ast}(y)
.
Recalling (
2.17
), we have

\nabla\nu^{\ast}(y)(\xi_{i}^{\ast})=\nabla\nu^{\ast}(\nu(x))(\xi_{i})=\xi_{i}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}(x)}\xi_{i}^{\ast},\quad i=1,\cdots,n-2.

As a result,
the principal curvatures of
\Sigma^{\ast}
at
y
are
\{\kappa_{i}^{-1}(x)\}_{i=1,\cdots,n-2}
. Thus (a) holds and the sectional curvature spanned by
\xi_{i}^{\ast}
and
\xi_{j}^{\ast}
is

K_{\xi_{i}^{\ast},\xi_{j}^{\ast}}(y)=\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}(x)\kappa_{j}(x)}+1>1,\quad y=\nu(x),x\in\Sigma.

The proof is complete.
∎

In the following of the paper, we let

\underline{\kappa}:=\min_{x\in\Sigma,i=1,\cdots,n-2}\{\kappa_{i}(x)\}\mbox{ and }\bar{\kappa}:=\max_{x\in\Sigma,i=1,\cdots,n-2}\{\kappa_{i}(x)\}

where
\kappa_{i}(x)
are the principle curvatures of
\Sigma
at
x
.
Under the assumption of Theorem

, we know

\bar{\kappa}\geq\underline{\kappa}>0.

By Lemma
2.16
, we know that principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}^{\ast}
of the dual manifold
\Sigma^{\ast}
satisfy

(2.18)

\frac{1}{\bar{\kappa}}=\min_{x\in\Sigma^{\ast},i=1,\cdots,n-2}\{\kappa_{i}^{\ast}(x)\}\mbox{ and }\frac{1}{\underline{\kappa}}=\max_{x\in\Sigma,i=1,\cdots,n-2}\{\kappa_{i}^{\ast}(x)\}.

Remark 2.19
.

Recalling that the second fundamental form of the normals
\nu
of
\Sigma
and
\nu^{\ast}
of
\Sigma^{\ast}
are

T\Sigma^{\ast}\ni\zeta\mapsto(\nabla_{\zeta}\nu\cdot\zeta)(x),\ x\in\Sigma\mbox{ and }T\Sigma^{\ast}\ni\zeta\mapsto(\nabla_{\zeta}\nu^{\ast}\cdot\zeta)(x),\ x\in\Sigma^{\ast}

and the eigenvalues of the second fundamental form are precisely principal curvatures,
we deduce from (

) that

(2.20)

(\nabla_{\zeta}\nu\cdot\zeta)(x)\leq\bar{\kappa}|\zeta|^{2},\quad\forall x\in\Sigma,\ \forall\zeta\in T_{x}\Sigma

and

(2.21)

Remark 2.22

Lemma 2.23

Let

Proof.

First we show (1) in Definition

.
Since

\sup_{f_{z}\in F}\|f_{z}\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\leq\|\Sigma\|_{C^{2}([0,1]^{n-2})}\max_{z\in Z}|z|\lesssim 1

where the implicit constant only depends on
\Sigma
, (1) holds.

Fix

. We show (3) in Definition
2.1
. Similar as (

\|f_{y}-f_{z}\|_{C^{1}([0,1]^{n-2})}\lesssim|y-z|.

. We prove (3) by showing

(2.26)

\inf_{x\in[0,1]^{n-2},\xi\in S^{n-3}}\{|h(x)|+|\nabla_{\xi}h(x)|\}\gtrsim|y-z|.

We recall from Remark
2.22
that
w=\Sigma(x)
and

\{\tilde{e}_{i}(w):=e_{i}(\Sigma^{-1}(w))=\nabla\Sigma(x)\cdot\partial_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n-2}\mbox{ is a basis of $T_{w}\Sigma$}.

for

Case 2.

|I_{2}|\leq|h_{\ast}(I_{1})|+2\delta.

Thanks to (
4.10
), we deduce that

|I_{2}|\leq\max_{s\in I_{1}}\{|h^{\prime}_{\ast}(s)|\}|I_{1}|+2\delta<\frac{a}{10}|I_{1}|+2\delta\lesssim_{a}\delta.

Finally, since in this subcase
\sin\angle(L,T_{y}\mathcal{C})\sim\angle(L,T_{y}\mathcal{C})\gtrsim a
, thanks to the second inequality in (
4.9
), we conclude that

|I|\sim_{a}|I_{2}|\lesssim_{a}\delta

as desired.

Case 2.
I\cap B(o,10\delta)\neq\emptyset
.

In this case, since the interval
I\subset L
, we let

I_{1}^{\prime}:=I\cap B(o,10\delta)\mbox{ and }I_{2}^{\prime}:=I\setminus B(o,10\delta).

Thus

|I|=|I_{1}^{\prime}|+|I_{2}^{\prime}|.

We immediately have
|I_{1}^{\prime}|\leq\operatorname{diam}B(o,10\delta)\leq 20\delta.

In a similar way as in Case 1, one has
|I_{2}^{\prime}|\lesssim_{a}\delta
, which then implies that

|I|\lesssim_{a}\delta
as desired.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.1

Lemma 5.4

Under the assumption on

>1

-equatorial sphere which has sectional curvature
\equiv 1

∎

Proof of Lemma
