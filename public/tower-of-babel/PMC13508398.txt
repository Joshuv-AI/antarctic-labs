Microglial dysregulation and spatiotemporal dynamics of inflammation in multiple sclerosis white matter: an integrative transcriptomic analysis
Authors: Keqiang Ma, Qing Zhang, Hong Du, Huimin Peng, Jiaxuan Kang, Yisheng Cai, Lijun Tan, Xiangding Chen, Can Bian, Xiaochao Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveWhite matter damage in Multiple Sclerosis (MS) exhibits significant heterogeneity. The cellular and molecular underpinnings of this heterogeneity are not fully understood. This study investigated cell-specific changes and spatial heterogeneity in MS white matter by integrating single-cell and spatial transcriptomics (ST) to guide potential clinical interventions.MethodsSingle-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) identified key cell types, regulons, and cellular functional heterogeneity using Gene Regulatory Network (GRN) analysis, cell communication, subpopulation classification, functional enrichment, and pseudotime analysis. ST explored functional heterogeneity and cell type distribution in MS white matter niches via correlation, enrichment analysis, and deconvolution.ResultssnRNA-seq analysis identified module M3, showing transcription factor dysregulation in MS white matter compared to controls. These genes were predominantly expressed in microglia and enriched in inflammation related signaling pathways; IKAROS Family Zinc Finger 1 (IKZF1) was identified as a candidate transcriptional regulator connected to these changes. Microglia exhibited heterogeneity, existing in dynamic regulation from homeostatic to pro-inflammatory phenotypes, the latter showing Disease-associated Microglia (DAM) characteristics. Spatial transcriptomic analysis revealed strong heterogeneity within MS white matter with distinct niche functions. The Lesion Rim (LR), a transition zone, correlated strongly with microglia and was highly enriched with DAM characteristics. The LR was identified as an inflammatory hotspot enriched for DAM and altered IKZF1 regulon activity, supporting snRNA-seq findings.ConclusionIKZF1 regulon inactivation in microglia coincides with altered white matter lesions in MS and dysregulated inflammatory pathways. Microglial heterogeneity in MS, including DAM phenotypes, extends beyond traditional polarization models. MS white matter displays significant spatial heterogeneity. Hindered remyelination in the LR may reflect inflammation from sustained microglial activation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08360-2.

Introduction

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic disease of the central nervous system (CNS), characterized by immune-mediated demyelination of nerve axons, axonal degeneration, and chronic inflammation [1]. Research indicates that, compared to changes in gray matter, white matter in MS exhibits more complex physiological and pathological alterations [2]. White matter consists of myelinated nerve fibers that facilitate rapid communication between different brain regions, playing a critical role in coordinating neurological functions. In patients with MS, the immune system erroneously attacks these myelin sheaths, resulting in demyelination and the formation of focal, confluent demyelinating plaques. This process disrupts the conduction of nerve impulses, manifesting as symptoms such as fatigue, visual impairment, and motor dysfunction [3].

Microglia, the sole resident immune cells within the CNS, are responsible for immune surveillance and defense of the nervous system; they secrete a multitude of cytokines and neurotrophic factors, thereby modulating inflammatory responses and tissue repair [4, 5]. Consequently, microglia have been extensively investigated as therapeutic targets for MS. However, the inherent developmental and functional heterogeneity among microglial subpopulations poses significant challenges to the implementation of such therapeutic strategies [6].

Accordingly, the present study utilized single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) datasets to perform a comprehensive gene regulatory network (GRN) analysis on cells derived from the white matter tissue of 79 individuals with MS and 13 control (CTRL) subjects. Our investigations revealed the specific expression of the IKZF1 regulon within microglia, which correlates with the progression of white matter damage and is accompanied by changes in inflammatory response pathways. Furthermore, the spatial localization of both the IKZF1 regulon and IKZF1 was validated in situ using data from four spatial transcriptomic samples.

Materials and methods

Data sources

White matter tissue snRNA-seq data were obtained from the Zenodo database (10.5281/zenodo.8338963) with 13 CTRL and 79 MS white matter tissue samples [2]. To provide an independent line of support, we also processed the human white matter snRNA-seq dataset with 5 CTRL and 15 MS from Jäkel et al. (GSE118257) [7]. Spatial transcriptome data were obtained from GSE279183 in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database [8]. Slides containing 1 CTRL, 3 MS subcortical white matter were analyzed for subsequent analysis. Detailed information is provided in Supplementary Tables 1 & 2.

Single-cell data processing

The raw count matrix was loaded into a Seurat object (v5.1.0), with all functions executed using default parameters unless otherwise specified [9]. Cells expressing fewer than 300 unique genes or possessing Unique Molecular Identifier counts below 500 were excluded from downstream analysis, as these were considered potentially low-quality. To mitigate batch effects, the RunHarmony function from the Harmony package (v1.2.0) was employed, setting the group.by.vars parameter to ‘sample_id’ to facilitate integration across samples [10]. For cell clustering, the FindNeighbours and FindClusters functions were executed with the resolution parameter set to 0.1. This resolution was selected after iterative testing across multiple values to balance cluster stability and biological interpretability, ensuring clear separation of major cell types while avoiding over-fragmentation of transcriptionally similar populations. The resultant clusters were visualized using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). The UMAP embedding projects high-dimensional transcriptomic similarity into two dimensions, such that cells with similar gene expression profiles are positioned closer together, while the axes themselves do not correspond to specific biological variables. Marker genes for each cluster were subsequently identified using COSG [11]. Cell types were assigned to each cluster based on the expression of established marker genes: oligodendrocytes (PLP1, MAG, MOG, OPALIN); oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs; PDGFRA, BCAN); astrocytes (FGFR3, GFAP, SLC14A1, AQP4); microglia (P2RY12, SPP1, CSF1R, IRF8); excitatory neurons (SLC17A7, FEZF2, RORB); inhibitory neurons (GAD1, ADARB2, LHX6); endothelial cells (CLDN5, FLT1, EPAS1); pericytes (EPS8, LAMA2); B cells (IGHG1); and T cells (IL7R). For each cluster, log-normalized gene expression values were calculated and then scaled to a range of -2 to 2 across all clusters. The identity of each cell cluster was confirmed by selecting the cell type that demonstrated the highest average scaled expression of its established marker genes.

pySCENIC-based gene regulatory network construction

pySCENIC is a computational methodology for reconstructing GRNs and identifying cellular states from single-cell transcriptomic data through co-expression and motif analyses [12]. The human transcription factor (TF) gene set was sourced from the cisTarget database (https://resources.aertslab.org/cistarget). Co-expression networks, established via GRNs analysis, serve to delineate potential TF-target regulatory relationships. A minimum of 10 genes per regulon was stipulated. Target genes of regulons were scored using AUCell, and the regulon activity score (RAS) for each regulon was computed, followed by dimensionality reduction of the cells using UMAP. To comprehend the regulon-based clustering, TF-target credibility, assessed by feature importance, was imported; regulon information and correlation data were merged, and the number of targets for each TF was calculated. Cis-regulatory motif analysis was performed for each co-expression module using RcisTarget. Transcription factor binding motifs significantly enriched within the gene lists were identified [13]. AUCell was employed to score the activity of each regulon in every cell and to calculate the RAS. The distribution of RAS in cellular subpopulations of CTRL and MS white matter was visualized using UMAP plots. Correlations of RAS between different subpopulations were computed. Subsequently, the interconnected Connection Specificity Index was calculated. The results of hierarchical clustering were processed and plotted, with visualization of these clustering outcomes facilitated by a heatmap. The mean activity for each TF module was plotted, and individual modules were visualized using UMAP plots.

Identification and enrichment analysis of microglia-associated regulons

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted on the microglia data using clusterProfiler (v4.14.4) against the HALLMARK gene set [14–16]. To further identify microglia-specific regulons, the Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) between regulon activity distributions and cell type distributions was calculated using philentropy (v0.9.0) [17]. This JSD was then converted into a similarity score by computing 1-JSD.

To explore the impact of the IKZF1 regulon in microglia, cells were scored based on a gene set comprising the target genes of the IKZF1 regulon. Based on the median score, microglia were categorized into high IKZF1 score and low IKZF1 score groups. Differential analysis between the high and low IKZF1 score microglia groups was performed using the FindMarkers function to obtain differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Based on these DEGs, Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) enrichment analysis was performed on the low IKZF1 score microglia using g: Profiler [18].

Cell communication analysis

To investigate potential interactions among different cell types, we utilized the CellChat (v2.1.2), providing a merged Seurat object containing microglia and other cell types as input for the algorithm [19]. Subsequent to the creation of a CellChat object, a reference database founded on secreted signaling pathways was employed. The functions computeCommunProb and computeCommunProbPathway were then used to infer specific ligand-receptor interactions and their corresponding communication probabilities between distinct cell types, respectively.

Construction of microglial trajectory model

Microglia were selected using Seurat for further subclustering, yielding a total of five subclusters, and characteristic genes were identified using COSG. Subsequently, a single-cell pseudotime trajectory of microglial subtypes was constructed using the Monocle (v2.28.0) [20, 21]. Highly variable genes, identified using the VariableFeatures function in Seurat, were employed to order the cells within the trajectory. The origin of the trajectory was then determined based on the state of cellular differentiation. To this end, the CytoTRACE2 (v1.1.0) algorithm was utilized to predict and visualize cellular differentiation potential scores along the trajectory [22, 23]. The branch displaying high cellular differentiation potential scores, indicative of a less differentiated state, was selected as the starting point. Cells were subsequently ordered from this differentiation origin using the orderCells function in Monocle.

Independent validation dataset analysis

To assess the reproducibility of our findings in an independent cohort, the human white matter snRNA-seq dataset from Jäkel et al. (GSE118257; 5 CTRL and 15 MS) was processed using the same Seurat-based workflow described above, applying identical quality control thresholds, normalization, dimensionality reduction, and graph-based clustering at a resolution of 0.1. Major cell types were annotated using the same canonical marker gene panels applied to the main dataset. IKZF1 regulon activity was then scored on a per-cell basis using AUCell, with the IKZF1 regulon gene set derived from the pySCENIC analysis of the main cohort (Supplementary Table 3). Differences in IKZF1 expression and regulon activity between MS and control microglia were subsequently compared with those observed in the main snRNA-seq cohort to evaluate cross-dataset concordance.

Spatial transcriptomics data processing

For each tissue section, data filtering was performed using scanpy (v1.10.3) [24]. This procedure involved the exclusion of spots containing fewer than 200 genes and the retention of genes expressed in more than three spots. To elucidate the spatial distribution of distinct cell populations, cell types identified from snRNA-seq data were mapped onto their corresponding spatial coordinates. The abundance of these cell types at each spot was subsequently estimated using cell2location (v.0.1.3) [25]. Based on snRNA-seq data derived from the same batch of MS and CTRL tissues, cell types were first determined through processing with scanpy; thereafter, reference expression signatures for major cell types were inferred using regularized negative binomial regression. Subsequently, deconvolution analysis was conducted on each section employing a hierarchical Bayesian model, with hyperparameters configured as N_cells_per_location = 5 and detection_alpha = 20, as recommended in the official cell2location documentation [25]. Following this, the proportion of each cell type per spot was calculated by dividing the abundance of a specific cell type by the total cellular abundance at that particular spot. Finally, correlation analyses were performed between these derived cell type proportions and various defined ecological niches, and the results were visualized as a correlation heatmap.

Lesion areas were subdivided into distinct regions using Loupe Browser (v.6.3.0, 10x Genomics). These regions were defined as follows: the lesion core (LC), or central region, encompassing the demyelinated area; the lesion rim (LR), representing the distinct border between the demyelinated lesion center and the adjacent myelinated white matter; and the periplaque white matter (PPWM), referring to the surrounding myelinated white matter. Additionally, normal-appearing white matter (WM), normal-appearing gray matter (GM), and areas of vascular infiltration (VI), characterized by inflammatory cell infiltration, were delineated [8]. To assess the spatial expression patterns and enrichment of IKZF1 and its regulon, visualization was performed using OmicVerse (v1.6.9) [26].

Identification of disease-associated microglia

Inflammatory responses within microglia are inextricably linked to Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM). Accordingly, microglia were scored based on the DAM gene set (TYROBP, APOE, B2M, TREM2, LPL, CST7, AXL, ITGAX, SPP1, CD9, CCL15, CSF1) [27, 28]. In the snRNA-seq data, differential expression analysis was performed on the microglial subpopulation 3 (Micro3), which exhibited the highest DAM score, to identify DEGs. Subsequently, GOBP enrichment analysis of these DEGs was conducted using WebGestalt [29].

For ST data, slides were scored using the DAM signature gene set. To investigate the potential role of DAM within the LR, regions were dichotomized into high-score and low-score areas based on the median DAM score. Previous studies in Experimental Autoimmune Encephalomyelitis (EAE) have indicated that the biological functions of DAM predominantly involve the electron transport chain, energy-coupled proton transmembrane transport against the electrochemical gradient, lipid metabolism, iron metabolism, and oxidative phosphorylation, including the mitochondrial electron transport from reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) to ubiquinone [30]. Therefore, AUCell was employed to calculate enrichment scores for these pathways in the high-DAM and low-DAM score regions within the LR, and differences in these pathway scores between the high- and low-DAM regions were subsequently determined.

To further validate these findings using a microglial signature directly defined within human MS white matter, we additionally scored the spatial transcriptomic data using the microglia inflamed in MS (MIMS) signature reported by Absinta et al., which was derived from MRI-informed single-nucleus RNA sequencing of MS chronic active lesion edges and was explicitly shown by the authors to overlap with the canonical DAM phenotype [31]. The MIMS gene set used (TREM2, APOE, LPL, CD68, CD9, CD74, GRN, TYROBP, TIMP2, SPP1, CTSZ, CTSB, FTH1, C1QA, C1QB, C1QC) was applied with identical scoring parameters and pathway analyses as those used for the DAM signature.

Statistical analysis

Unless otherwise specified, all other statistical analyses were conducted using R (v4.4.1) or Python (v3.10.8). * P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001; and ns, not significant.

Results

The heterogeneous cellular composition between multiple sclerosis and control white matter

To investigate the cellular diversity of white matter in MS and CTRL white matter samples, we integrated data from 79 MS samples and 13 CTRL samples. After quality control and batch effect removal, we acquired a total of 280,973 nucleus from MS and 54,721 nucleus from control white matter (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, 335,694 nuclei were classified into eight distinct cell types based on classical marker genes: oligodendrocytes, oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs COPs, including oligodendrocyte progenitor cells and committed oligodendrocyte precursors), astrocytes, microglia, excitatory neurons, inhibitory neurons, neurovascular cells (Endo Peri, including endothelial cells and pericytes), and immune cells (including T cells and B cells) (Fig. 1D). Interestingly, the proportion of neurovascular cells was comparable between MS and CTRL. However, compared to CTRL, we observed an accumulation of astrocytes and a reduction in oligodendrocytes in MS (Fig. 1B&C).

GRNs in multiple sclerosis and control white matter

We analyzed the differences in transcriptional regulation between MS and control white matter using pySCENIC, and constructed GRNs comprising 406 key regulators and their target genes. The generated clustered heatmap displays a total of 14 modules (Fig. 2A). For each module, we examined the RAS of its associated regulators. When the average activity score of each module was projected onto the UMAP (Supplementary Fig. 1), a comparison between MS and control white matter revealed that microglia in MS exhibited significantly lower RAS in module M3 compared to controls, suggesting a potential key role in MS white matter pathology (Fig. 2B). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis was performed for each transcription factor module. Module M3 was primarily enriched in pathways related to T cell receptor signaling, chemokine signaling, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, primary immunodeficiency, and MAPK signaling pathway (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Identification of TFs and signaling pathways related to white matter damage in multiple sclerosis

GSEA using the HALLMARK gene set was performed on microglia from MS and CTRL white matter samples. The results revealed that the activated signaling pathways included inflammatory response, TNFα signaling via NFκB, IFN-γ response, IL2 STAT5 signaling pathway, apoptosis, and KRAS signaling activation (Fig. 2C). Previous studies have shown a close association between microglia and neuroinflammation in MS. Therefore, we hypothesize that among the enriched pathways, the inflammatory response, IFN-γ response, and IL2 STAT5 signaling pathway are related to microglial involvement in white matter pathology in MS. Enrichment analysis results indicated that the inflammatory response was primarily mediated by the FLI1, SPI1, IKZF1, TAL1, IRF8, and RUNX1 regulons (Fig. 3A). Similarly, the IL2-STAT5 signaling pathway was primarily mediated by the FLI1, IKZF1, RUNX3, SPI1, TAL1, and ETV6 regulons (Fig. 3B), while the IFN-γ response was predominantly mediated by the FLI1, IKZF1, RUNX3, ETV6, NFATC2, and TAL1 regulons (Fig. 3C). Furthermore, GSEA performed separately for the inflammatory response, the IL2-STAT5 signaling pathway, and the IFN-γ response demonstrated that all three pathways were significantly upregulated in microglia (Fig. 3D-F). Analysis identified a transcriptional regulatory network wherein IKZF1, SPI1, NFATC2, IRF8, RUNX1, and STAT5A regulate the inflammatory response. Additionally, IKZF1, RUNX3, and TAL1 were observed to jointly regulate both the IL2-STAT5 signaling pathway and the IFN-γ response (Fig. 3G-I).

Critical role of the key regulon IKZF1 in white matter damage in multiple sclerosis

Although both the FLI1 and IKZF1 regulons were prominently ranked, JSD analysis revealed that the IKZF1 regulon exhibits strong specificity almost exclusively within microglia, whereas the FLI1 regulon demonstrates high specificity in both microglia and neurovascular cells (Supplementary Fig. 3 & Fig. 4A). This observation is consistent with a stronger cell type specific association between IKZF1 and the pathology of MS white matter damage. Consequently, IKZF1 regulon was selected as a primary target for subsequent investigation. UMAP revealed elevated transcriptional activity of the IKZF1 regulon within microglia from the CTRL white matter (Fig. 4B). Relative to cells derived from the MS condition, the IKZF1 regulon demonstrated significantly higher activity within both microglia and other immune cell populations from the control group (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, the present study observed that IKZF1 expression levels were significantly reduced in MS microglia and immune cells compared to other groups (Fig. 4D), a finding congruent with the outcomes of the GRN analysis. This observation is consistent with an association between IKZF1 and maintenance of microglial homeostasis under normal physiological conditions, whereas diminished IKZF1 expression in MS coincides with the inflammatory response.

Analysis of an independent human white matter snRNA seq-dataset revealed a consistent reduction of IKZF1 expression and IKZF1 regulon activity in microglia from multiple sclerosis samples compared with controls (Supplementary Fig. 4), supporting the reproducibility of our findings across datasets [7].

Functional characteristics of microglia exhibiting low IKZF1 regulon scores

Microglia were stratified into high and low-IKZF1 score groups based on the activity of the IKZF1 regulon and its putative target genes (Supplementary Table 3). Subsequent GOBP analysis revealed that pathways predominantly enriched within the low score microglial cohort included aerobic electron transport chain, ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport, respiratory electron transport chain, oxidative phosphorylation, and aerobic respiration and mitochondrial electron transport, NADH to ubiquinone (Fig. 4E). The g: Profiler was additionally utilized to identify enriched GOBP pathways within the low IKZF1 score immune cell population. Enriched signaling pathways encompassed B cell receptor signaling pathway, B cell activation, as well as aerobic electron transport chain, ATP synthesis coupled to electron transport, mitochondrial electron transport, NADH to ubiquinone, and respiratory electron transport chain (Fig. 4F). The enrichment of the oxidative phosphorylation pathway in low score microglia is consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction signatures. Furthermore, the similarity in enriched pathways observed between low score immune cells and low score microglia is consistent with a correlation between functional inactivation of the IKZF1 regulon and coordinated transcriptional alterations involving both microglial and immune cell populations.

Although Schirmer et al. have previously delineated the intercellular communication landscape involving highly heterogeneous immune and glial cell populations within the MS cortex, the pivotal cell-cell communication events driving disease progression specifically within the white matter remain to be systematically elucidated [32]. To decipher the reciprocal influences among cellular constituents in the white matter, we constructed cell interaction networks utilizing putative receptor-ligand pairs identified in both MS and CTRL conditions. Microglia exhibiting low IKZF1 regulon scores demonstrated heightened communication intensity within the MS environment compared to the CTRL (Fig. 5A), engaging predominantly in interactions with astrocytes (Fig. 5B). Relative to the CTRL, astrocytes in MS appear subject to modulation via the SPP1-(ITGAV+ITGB1) interaction pathway originating from these low-IKZF1-score microglia; furthermore, these microglia were also observed to interact with oligodendrocytes via the PSAP-GPR37 axis (Fig. 5C).

Microglia exist within a state of dynamic homeostasis

Microglia exhibit considerable dynamism under diverse pathological conditions, and the conventional M1 pro-inflammatory versus M2 anti-inflammatory polarization paradigm represents an oversimplification of their intricate functional states [33]. To perform an in-depth dissection of microglial heterogeneity within the context of MS, a re-clustering analysis was conducted on 40,137 microglial cells(Fig. 6A), comprising 35,476 cells derived from MS white matter and 4,661 cells from control white matter (Fig. 6B). This analysis partitioned the microglia into five distinct subpopulations. Each of these subpopulations was represented in both the MS and control groups (Fig. 6C).

The functional roles of each subpopulation were delineated based on their respective characteristic genes (Supplementary Table 4). The Micro3 subpopulation, characterized as pro-inflammatory microglia, expresses marker genes indicative of specific functions: VEGFA, known to modulate angiogenesis and vascular permeability; HIF1A-AS2, signifying a response to hypoxic conditions; and NAMPT, which is implicated in nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide metabolism and cellular survival [34]. Collectively, these findings suggest that Micro3 represents a microglial phenotype potentially involved in promoting neuroinflammation and vascular regulation. This subpopulation exhibited enrichment related to DNA-binding transcription repressor activity, monosaccharide binding, and aromatic amino acid transmembrane transporter activity. Based on their respective marker genes, microglial subpopulation 1 (Micro1), annotated as stress associated microglia, showed elevated expression of stress response and proteostasis related genes, including molecular chaperones/heat shock pathway genes (e.g., HSP90AA1, DNAJB1, HSPH1 and BAG3), consistent with a stress responsive state. In line with this, Micro1 displayed functional enrichment for GTPase regulator activity and nucleoside triphosphatase regulator activity (Fig. 6D) [35]. Microglial subpopulation 2 (Micro2) was identified as homeostatic microglia, supported by canonical homeostatic markers such as CX3CR1, P2RY12, GPR34, and CSF1R (Supplementary Table 4) [36]. Microglial subpopulation 4 (Micro4) was categorized as phagocytic microglia, supported by prominent expression of innate immune/phagocytosis associated markers (e.g., CR1, MARCO, F13A1, and CD163) and enrichment for immune and antigen presentation related functions (Fig. 6D) [37]. Microglial subpopulation 5 (Micro5), designated as reparative microglia, is principally implicated in processes involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and axon regeneration. (Fig. 6D)

Construction of the microglial trajectory model

To elucidate the differentiation status of microglia and the temporal dynamics of key regulatory factors throughout the progression of white matter damage, cellular trajectory modeling was performed using Monocle, complemented by the assessment of differentiation potential for each subpopulation via the CytoTRACE2 algorithm. UMAP visualization revealed that Micro1 exhibited the highest differentiation potential score, suggesting it may represent the initial state of microglia; consequently, Micro1 was designated as the origin of the differentiation trajectory. (Fig. 7A&B) Pseudotime analysis employing Monocle revealed that microglia exhibited a continuous differentiation trajectory along the pseudotime axis (Fig. 7C, right panel). This trajectory could be segmented into five principal cellular states (Fig. 7C, middle panel), with Micro1 positioned at the trajectory origin, whereas Micro3 and Micro5 occupied the terminal endpoints (Fig. 7C, left panel), suggesting these two subpopulations may represent the terminally differentiated states of microglia during the progression of white matter damage. Notably, a significant reduction in the activation level of the IKZF1 regulon was observed with advancing pseudotime (Fig. 7E), a finding consistent with the preceding observation of decreased IKZF1 transcriptional activation levels within microglia from MS samples. This trend is consistent with an association between MS white matter damage progression, progressively diminished IKZF1 regulatory activity, and dysregulated microglial inflammatory responses related to neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration. Furthermore, IKZF1 expression levels were also significantly reduced in MS microglia (Fig. 7D), lending additional support to its association with the pathology of MS white matter damage.

Given that the pathways enriched (Fig. 4E) exhibited a pronounced similarity to those characteristic of Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM), DAM scores were computed for each microglial subpopulation [30]. The scoring results indicated that Micro3, which is notably the subpopulation with the most pronounced pro-inflammatory functions among microglia, exhibited the strongest DAM signature. Differential gene expression analysis was performed on Micro3 (Supplementary Fig. 5). Subsequent GOBP analysis of the resultant DEGs revealed that the enriched pathways were predominantly related to lipid metabolism, vascular and tissue regulation, and inflammatory responses (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Spatial heterogeneity of multiple sclerosis white matter damage

Given the pronounced spatial heterogeneity inherent to white matter damage in MS, which cannot be adequately delineated by single-nucleus transcriptomics alone, ST analysis was performed to investigate cellular localization and gene expression patterns [38]. Regions were demarcated based on distinct anatomical niches into white matter (WM), grey matter (GM), peri-plaque white matter (PPWM), lesion rim (LR), lesion core (LC), and vascular infiltration (VI) zones (Fig. 8A). The analysis revealed a notably higher abundance of microglia within the LR, a finding consistent with the active inflammatory processes characteristic of this region. Conversely, astrocytes exhibited a higher proportional representation within the LC, a feature indicative of glial scarring (Fig. 8B). Furthermore, a strong positive correlation was observed between microglial abundance and the LR, further substantiating their pivotal role in chronic inflammation and the progression of white matter damage (Fig. 8C).

Within MS white matter, the enrichment activity of the IKZF1 regulon was markedly higher in LR regions compared to LC regions. In control white matter, the enrichment activity of the IKZF1 regulon surpassed that observed in MS tissue (Fig. 8D), a finding consistent with our results at the single-cell level (Fig. 4C). Additionally, the spatial domain of transcription factor IKZF1 expression was reduced in MS white matter relative to control tissue and predominantly concentrated within LR regions, further corroborating its association with MS pathogenesis (Fig. 8E). These results indicate that diminished activity of the IKZF1 regulon correlates with inflammatory responses and the progression of white matter damage.

Disease-associated microglia are enriched within the lesion rim

Utilizing marker genes for Disease-Associated Microglia (DAM) to perform enrichment scoring on tissue sections, it was found that regions exhibiting high DAM scores demonstrated a pronounced spatial congruence with the LR (Fig. 9A). Subsequent stratification of the lesion rim based on the median DAM score into low-score and high-score regions revealed highly significant differences between these delineated groups (Fig. 9B). Further investigation centered on six pathways previously established as strongly linked to DAM in MS: the electron transport chain, energy-coupled transmembrane proton transport against the electrochemical gradient, mitochondrial electron transfer from NADH to ubiquinone, oxidative phosphorylation, iron metabolism, and lipid metabolism (Fig. 9C). Within the LR, the activity of all these enumerated pathways was significantly elevated, to varying degrees, in DAM high-score regions relative to DAM low-score regions. An analogous analysis using the human MS-specific MIMS (microglia inflamed in MS) signature defined by Absinta et al., which the authors demonstrated to overlap with the canonical DAM phenotype, yielded consistent results: within the LR, regions with high MIMS scores exhibited significantly elevated activity of the same six DAM-associated pathways relative to regions with low MIMS scores (Supplementary Fig. 7). These observations indicate that the accumulation of DAM within the LR accompanies the expansion of MS white matter lesions and activation of these established DAM-signature pathways.

Discussion

White matter damage, predominantly manifesting as neuroinflammation-associated demyelination and axonal injury, represents a cardinal pathological feature of MS [39]. Nevertheless, the precise molecular mechanisms governing white matter damage in MS are yet to be fully elucidated. Accordingly, this study integrated snRNA-seq and ST to elucidate the cellular and molecular underpinnings of heterogeneity in MS white matter damage. Our integrative analyses suggest an association between altered IKZF1 regulon activity and microglial inflammatory states, and indicate that the lesion rim represents an anatomical niche where these molecular and cellular features are preferentially observed (Fig. 10) [40].

A primary finding of this research is the identification of dysregulation in GRNs within MS white matter, particularly module M3, which was significantly altered in microglia and enriched for inflammation-related signaling pathways. Within this module, IKZF1 emerged as a candidate transcriptional regulator. The activity of the IKZF1 regulon was notably diminished in microglia from MS patients compared to controls. The IKZF1 gene encodes the Ikaros protein, a zinc-finger DNA-binding transcription factor that plays a critical role in regulating lymphocyte differentiation and proliferation, and is generally considered to be closely connected to hematological malignancies such as acute lymphoblastic leukemia [41]. In recent years, research has revealed that IKZF1 is not only implicated in hematological disorders but also plays roles in autoimmune diseases and neurological disorders [42, 43]. Ballasch et al. observed in Ikzf1 knockout mice morphologically altered microglia and neuroinflammation compared to wild-type mice [44]. This is consistent with IKZF1 activity being characteristic of microglial homeostatic states, while IKZF1 downregulation in MS coincides with pro-inflammatory responses and white matter damage. Both immune cells and microglia exhibiting low IKZF1 regulon scores are enriched in mitochondria-related pathways, indicating that IKZF1 regulon inactivation in microglia is accompanied by peripheral immune cell involvement and mitochondrial injury signatures in white matter damage [45–47]. This aligns with previous research implicating microglial dysfunction in MS pathogenesis but provides a more specific molecular insight into the IKZF1 regulon [48].

Our investigation into microglial heterogeneity revealed that these cells exist in a dynamic spectrum of activation states, transitioning from homeostatic to pro-inflammatory phenotypes which exhibit DAM characteristics. DAM were initially defined in Alzheimer’s disease models and subsequently recognized across diverse neurodegenerative and neuroinflammatory conditions, suggesting a conserved microglial response program to chronic tissue stress [28]. Across diseases, DAM like states are commonly linked to reduced homeostatic signatures, lipid handling and phagolysosomal remodeling programs, and metabolic adaptation accompanied by mitochondrial stress [49, 50]. Consistent with this broader literature, our MS white matter microglia show DAM like features together with enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation and electron transport related signatures, aligning with reports that microglial activation is coupled to metabolic reprogramming and mitochondrial dysfunction [51]. This observation challenges the traditional M1/M2 polarization model and underscores the complexity of microglial responses in MS [33]. The pseudotime trajectory analysis further supported this, with stress microglia (Micro1) at the origin and pro-inflammatory (Micro3, high DAM score) and reparative microglia (Micro5) at the trajectory endpoints. Significantly, the activation of the IKZF1 regulon decreased along this pseudotime trajectory, correlating with the progression towards more pathological microglial states seen in MS [30]. Microglia exhibiting low IKZF1 regulon scores also showed altered communication patterns, particularly increased interaction with astrocytes in the MS environment via pathways such as SPP1-(ITGAV+ITGB1). This indicates an association between IKZF1 dysregulation in microglia, altered glial communication, and overall pathology.

The integration of ST provided crucial anatomical context to our single-cell findings. We observed significant spatial heterogeneity in MS white matter, with distinct cellular compositions and molecular signatures in different lesion-associated niches. The LR was identified as a particularly active region, characterized by a high abundance of microglia and a strong correlation with DAM signatures. Although the overall activity of the IKZF1 regulon was higher in control white matter, ST analysis revealed a relative enrichment of IKZF1 regulon activity and IKZF1 expression within the LR compared to the LC in MS tissue. Importantly, these spatial patterns mirror the microglia specific IKZF1 dysregulation identified in the much larger snRNA-seq cohort, suggesting concordance between cellular and spatial levels of analysis. The LR also showed enrichment of inflammation-related pathways. Together, these findings suggest that the LR represents an inflammatory niche in which DAM accumulation and altered IKZF1-associated regulatory programs in microglia coincide with ongoing tissue stress and impaired remyelination. Furthermore, the enrichment of oxidative phosphorylation and metabolism pathways in high DAM regions within the LR is consistent with established metabolic features of DAM observed across neurodegenerative diseases [30, 47]. Nevertheless, given the limited ST sample size, these spatial observations should be interpreted as providing contextual and hypothesis-generating support for the snRNA-seq findings, and will require validation in larger, independent spatial transcriptomic cohorts.

Our study also confirmed broader cellular changes in MS white matter, such as an accumulation of astrocytes and a reduction in oligodendrocytes compared to controls, consistent with known MS pathology involving gliosis and demyelination. The specific finding of increased astrocyte abundance in the LC supports the concept of glial scar formation [52, 53].

In addition to spatial heterogeneity, patient specific clinical and pathological variables have also been connected to the diversity of microglial states observed in MS white matter. Factors such as disease duration, treatment exposure, and lesion age or activity state have been shown to influence microglial activation profiles and inflammatory signaling pathways [48, 54, 55]. Owing to the retrospective nature of the publicly available datasets analyzed in this study, such metadata were not uniformly available and therefore could not be systematically incorporated into the analyses. As a result, we cannot exclude the possibility that some of the transcriptional differences observed, including variation in IKZF1 regulon activity and DAM enrichment, may be modulated by these patient-specific factors. Importantly, rather than detracting from the findings, this heterogeneity likely reflects the complex and dynamic nature of MS pathology, within which microglia respond to evolving inflammatory and neurodegenerative cues. Future studies integrating detailed clinical annotation, lesion staging, and longitudinal sampling will be critical to disentangle the relative contributions of disease stage, treatment history, and intrinsic regulatory programs such as IKZF1 to microglial dysfunction in MS.

Strengths of this study include the comprehensive approach of combining snRNA-seq with ST, allowing for a multi-resolution view of MS pathology from single cells to tissue architecture. The use of advanced bioinformatic techniques, including GRN analysis, cell communication modeling, and pseudotime trajectory inference, provided deep insights into the molecular and cellular dynamics. However, the study has limitations. We observed evidence of IKZF1 dysregulation in microglia, and concordance with an independent human white matter snRNA-seq dataset provides additional support for the internal consistency of these associations. However, this evidence does not substitute for cell type specific functional validation, and further in vitro or in vivo studies will be required to causally link IKZF1 activity to MS pathogenesis and to assess its therapeutic potential. While ST data provided valuable spatial context for the snRNA-seq findings, the small sample size may limit the generalizability of spatial conclusions, particularly with respect to inter-individual variability, lesion heterogeneity, and disease stage. Therefore, spatial patterns observed in specific niches such as the lesion rim should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Future studies with larger ST cohorts could help to validate and expand upon these findings across a broader spectrum of MS patients and lesion types. Future research should aim to clarify how IKZF1 inactivation in microglia relates to downstream cellular changes and neuronal health. Investigating the specific signals within the LR microenvironment that coincide with IKZF1 downregulation and DAM accumulation could also inform future therapeutic research.

Conclusion

This study provides integrative transcriptomic and spatial evidence that altered activity of the IKZF1 regulon correlates with microglial inflammatory dysregulation in multiple sclerosis white matter, particularly within the lesion rim. Our analyses highlight pronounced microglial heterogeneity and suggest that reduced IKZF1 regulon activity accompanies a transition toward pro-inflammatory, disease-associated microglial states. By spatially localizing these transcriptional and cellular features, we identify the lesion rim as a key inflammatory niche where microglial activation, metabolic reprogramming, and impaired remyelination may converge. While our findings do not establish a causal role for IKZF1, they nominate IKZF1 as a candidate regulatory factor related to microglial inflammatory states in MS and provide a framework for future functional studies aimed at elucidating its mechanistic role in disease progression.
