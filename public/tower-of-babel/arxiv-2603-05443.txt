Cross-free families have linear size
István Tomon

Abstract
Two subsets A A and B B of a ground set X X are crossing if none of the four sets A ∖ B , B ∖ A , A ∩ B , X ∖ ( A ∪ B ) A\setminus B,B\setminus A,A\cap B,X\setminus(A\cup B) are empty. Almost fifty years ago, Karzanov and Lomonosov conjectured that every family of subsets of an n n -element ground set with no k k -pairwise crossing members has size O ⁡ ( k ​ n ) O(kn) . We prove the bound O k ​ ( n ) O_{k}(n) , settling (arguably) the main problem about the growth rate of such families.



1
Introduction

Let
X
be an
n
-element ground set. Two sets
A,B\subset X
are
crossing
if none of the four sets
A\setminus B,B\setminus A,A\cap B,
and
X\setminus(A\cup B)
are empty. Equivalently,
A
and
B
are crossing if their Venn diagram has no empty regions. In this paper, we address a long-standing conjecture of Karzanov and Lomonosov
[
13
]
regarding the maximum size of families containing no
k
pairwise-crossing members, referred to as
k
-cross-free
families.

Note that the definition of crossing is invariant under complementation of its arguments; if
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
is
k
-cross-free and
A\in\mathcal{F}
, one may include
X\setminus A
without violating the
k
-cross-free property. A 2-cross-free family is equivalent to a
laminar
family (after accounting for complements), where a family is laminar if any two members are either disjoint, or one contains the other. A classical result of Edmonds and Giles
[
9
]
states that the maximum size of a laminar family is
2n
, implying the bound of
4n-2
for 2-cross-free families.

The importance of 3-cross-free families stems from the locking theorem of Karzanov and Lomonosov
[
13
]
(1978), which characterizes the families of subsets
\mathcal{F}\subset X
that can be simultaneously ”locked” by a multiflow — meaning the flow value across each cut
(A,X\setminus A)
for
A\in\mathcal{F}
achieves the cut’s minimum capacity. They proved that a family is lockable in every graph if and only if it is 3-cross-free, providing a powerful multiflow generalization of the Max-flow min-cut theorem. This motivated the search for a general bound for the size of
k
-cross-free families. Karzanov and Lomonosov
[
12
,
13
]
, and Pevzner
[
16
]
conjectured that the maximum size of a
k
-cross-free family on an
n
-element ground set is
O(kn)
. Early progress by Lomonosov provided an elegant
O(kn\log n)
upper bound, derived from the observation that any element
x\in X
can be contained in at most
k-1
sets of a fixed size
\ell<n/2
. For the case
k=3
, Pevzner
[
16
]
proved a linear bound, and a very short argument for the bound
10n
was given by Fleiner
[
10
]
. This was subsequently sharpened to the optimal
8n-20
by Dress, Koolen, and Moulton
[
8
]
.

The existence of a linear bound for
k\geq 4
remained open. Grünewald, Koolen, Moulton and Wu
[
11
]
proved linearity for specific subclasses of 4-cross-free families, but the general
O(kn\log n)
bound persisted as the state-of-the-art for decades. In 2019, Kupavskii, Pach, and Tomon
[
14
]
achieved the first general improvement
O_{k}(n\log^{*}n)
, coming tantalizingly close to linearity. In this work, we close the gap.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
k
, there exists
c_{k}>0
such that the following holds. Let
X
be a set of size
n
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be a
k
-cross-free family. Then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq c_{k}n
.

In their survey of research problems in discrete geometry, Brass, Moser, and Pach
[
4
]
highlight interesting connections between the Karzanov-Lomonosov conjecture and geometric crossing problems. A fundamental question in the theory of graph drawings is whether there exists a constant
c_{k}
such that every
geometric graph
(i.e. graph drawn in the plane with straight line edges) on
n
vertices with at least
c_{k}n
edges contains
k
pairwise crossing edges, where crossing is meant in the usual geometric sense.

By Euler’s polyhedral formula, a geometric graph with no
2
crossing edges has at most
3n-6
edges. The existence of
c_{3}
was proved by Agarwal, Aronov, Pach, Pollack, and Sharir
[
2
]
(see also
[
3
,
15
]
), and the existence of
c_{4}
was confirmed by Ackerman
[
1
]
. However, for
k\geq 5
, the problem remains open with the best known general bound being
O_{k}(n\log n)
due to Valtr
[
18
]
.

Although ”crossing” in the geometric sense differs from the set-theoretic definition, the two concepts coincide in case the vertices are in convex position. Capoyleas and Pach
[
5
]
proved that a geometric graph on
n
vertices in convex position with no
k
pairwise crossing edges has at most
2(k-1)n-\binom{2k-1}{2}
edges, and this bound is optimal for
n\geq 2k
. This result is equivalent to stating that if
X
is a cyclically ordered set and
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
is a
k
-cross-free family of intervals, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 4(k-1)n-2\binom{2k-1}{2}
. Our main result extends this linearity from the restricted case of intervals to arbitrary families of subsets.

Beyond geometry and optimization,
k
-cross-free families play a structural role in phylogenetic combinatorics, particularly in the study of split systems and circular networks
[
7
]
. The remainder of the paper is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect a few simple results and introduce our notation. First, we show that the notion of crossing can be slightly relaxed. Two sets
A
and
B
are
weakly-crossing
if none of the three sets
A\cap B,A\setminus B,B\setminus A
are empty. A family
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
is
weakly-
k
-cross-free
if it does not contain
k
sets that are pairwise weakly-crossing. The next simple lemma shows that in order to prove
Theorem

3.1
, it is enough to consider weakly-
k
-cross-free families.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be a
k
-cross-free family. Then there exists a weakly-
k
-cross-free family
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset 2^{X}
of size at least
|\mathcal{F}|/2
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
be arbitrary and let
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{A\in\mathcal{F}:x\not\in A\}
. Note that the union of no two sets in
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is
X
, so
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is weakly-
k
-cross-free. If
|\mathcal{F}_{0}|\geq|\mathcal{F}|/2
, then
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\mathcal{F}_{0}
suffices. Otherwise, let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{X\setminus A:A\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{0}\}
. Then
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is also
k
-cross-free and no element of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
contains
x
, so it is also weakly
k
-cross-free, and has size at least
|\mathcal{F}|/2
.
∎

We recall some standard terminology about set-systems. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
.

•

Two sets
A,B\subset X
are
comparable
if
A\subset B
or
B\subset A
, otherwise incomparable.

•

\mathcal{F}
is a
chain
if any two if its members are comparable. Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is a chain if there is an enumeration
A_{1}\subset\dots\subset A_{s}
of the elements of
\mathcal{F}
. Also,
\mathcal{F}
is a
continuous chain
if
|A_{i+1}|=|A_{i}|+1
for
i=1,\dots,s-1
.

•

\mathcal{F}
is an
antichain
if any two elements of
\mathcal{F}
are incomparable.

•

\mathcal{F}
is
intersecting
if
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
for every
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
.

Dilworth’s theorem
[
6
]
states that any partially ordered set (such as a set-system ordered by the subset relation) can be partitioned into as many chains as the size of the largest antichain. This has the following simple corollary about weakly-
k
-cross-free families.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be a weakly-
k
-cross-free intersecting family. Then
\mathcal{F}
can be partitioned into
k-1
chains.

Proof.

If
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
and
A
and
B
are incomparable, then
A
and
B
are weakly-crossing. Therefore,
\mathcal{F}
does not contain an antichain of size
k
. But then Dilworth’s theorem
[
6
]
implies that
\mathcal{F}
is the union of
k-1
chains.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be a weakly-
k
-cross-free family such that
|A|=\ell
for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq(k-1)|X|/\ell
.

Proof.

By simple averaging, some
x\in X
is contained in at least
|\mathcal{F}|\ell/|X|
elements of
\mathcal{F}
. Any two
\ell
-element sets containing
x
are weakly-crossing, so we must have
|\mathcal{F}|\ell/|X|\leq k-1
.
∎

Finally, we make repeated use of the following formulation of Turán’s theorem
[
17
]
from extremal graph theory.

Lemma 2.4
.

Every
n
vertex graph of average degree
d
contains an independent set of size at least
\frac{n}{d+1}
.

3
Weakly-cross-free families

In this section, we prove the slight relaxation of
Theorem

1.1
in which
k
-cross-free is replaced by weakly-
k
-cross-free. By
Section

2
, then
Theorem

1.1
follows immediately as well.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
k
, there exists
c_{k}>0
such that the following holds. Let
X
be a set of size
n
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be a weakly-
k
-cross-free family. Then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq c_{k}n
.

We prove
Theorem

3.1
by induction on
k
. In case
k=2
,
\mathcal{F}
is a laminar family, and we can prove that
c_{2}=2
suffices by an elementary inductive argument. In the rest of the proof, we assume that
k\geq 3
and that
c_{k-1}
exists. The proof consists of two main parts. First, we show that if
\mathcal{F}
is extremal with respect to being weakly-
k
-cross-free, and
\mathcal{F}
has
cn
elements for some sufficiently large constant
c
, then we can find a dense collection of long, continuous chains. Second, we build a tree encoding certain special comparabilities between the chains. We argue that if we are able to build a sufficiently large such tree, then we can extract
k
pairwise weakly-crossing sets, reaching a contradiction.

Say that
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
is
extremal
, if
\mathcal{F}
is weakly-
k
-cross-free, and
\mathcal{F}
maximizes the quantity
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|/|X^{\prime}|
among all weakly-
k
-cross-free families
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subset 2^{X^{\prime}}
with
|X^{\prime}|\leq|X|
. Clearly, in order to prove
Theorem

3.1
, it is enough to only consider extremal families. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset 2^{X}
be an extremal family, and assume that
|\mathcal{F}|\geq cn
for some sufficiently large constant
c>0
specified later. Our goal is to arrive to a contradiction. First, we show that
\mathcal{F}
contains a dense collection of long continuous chains.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
h,\alpha
be integers, then the following holds if
c
is sufficiently large with respect to
h
and
\alpha
. The family
\mathcal{F}
contains a collection
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of pairwise disjoint continuous chains such that
|I|\geq\alpha n
and
|C_{i}|=h+1
for
i\in I
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
, and consider the family
\mathcal{F}_{x}=\{A\setminus\{x\}:A\in\mathcal{F}\}\subset 2^{X\setminus\{x\}}
. Observe that
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is also weakly-
k
-cross-free. Therefore, as
\mathcal{F}
is extremal, we have

\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}_{x}|}{n-1},

which implies
|\mathcal{F}_{x}|\leq|\mathcal{F}|-|\mathcal{F}|/n
. Therefore, there are at least
|\mathcal{F}|/n
sets
A\in\mathcal{F}
such that
x\not\in A
and
A\cup\{x\}\in\mathcal{F}
as well.

Define the auxiliary directed graph
H
on vertex set
\mathcal{F}
, where there is an edge pointing from
A
to
B
if
B=A\cup\{x\}
for some
x\in X\setminus A
. The previous implies that
H
has at least
n\cdot(|\mathcal{F}|/n)=|\mathcal{F}|
edges.

Say that a set
A\in\mathcal{F}
is
exceptional
if there exist
x,y\in X\setminus A
,
x\neq y
, such that
A\cup\{x\},A\cup\{y\}\in\mathcal{F}
. Equivalently,
A
is exceptional if the outdegree of
A
in
H
is at least 2. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the family of exceptional sets.

Claim 3.3
.

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq kc_{k-1}n+kn
.

Proof.

Remove all members of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
of size at most 2, and let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}
be the resulting family. By
Section

2
, we have
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}|\geq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|-(1+n+(k-1)n/2)\geq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|-kn
. Let
Q
be the graph on vertex set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}
in which
A,B\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}
are joined by an edge if
|A|=|B|
and
|A\Delta B|=2
. Observe that if
B_{1},B_{2}
are neighbors of
A
in
Q
, then
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
weakly-cross. Therefore, the maximum degree of
Q
is at most
k-1
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}
be a maximal independent set of
Q
, then
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}|\geq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}|/k
by Turán’s theorem (
Section

2
).

The main observation is that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}
is weakly-
(k-1)
-cross-free. Indeed, assume to the contrary that
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}
are pairwise weakly-crossing, and assume that
|A_{k-1}|
is minimal among
|A_{1}|,\dots,|A_{k-1}|
. As
A_{k-1}
is exceptional, there exist
x,y\in X\setminus A_{k-1}
,
x\neq y
, such that
A_{k-1}\cup\{x\},A_{k-1}\cup\{y\}\in\mathcal{F}
. But then
A_{1},\dots,A_{k-2},A_{k-1}\cup\{x\},A_{k-1}\cup\{y\}
are
k
pairwise weakly-crossing sets. This is fairly straightforward to check. For example,
A_{i}
and
A_{k-1}\cup\{x\}
are weakly-crossing for
i\in[k-2]
: they intersect as
A_{i}\cap A_{k-1}\neq\emptyset
; we cannot have
A_{k-1}\cup\{x\}\subset A_{i}
as
A_{k-1}\not\subset A_{i}
; and we cannot have
A_{i}\subset A_{k-1}\cup\{x\}
as either
|A_{i}|>|A_{k}|
or
|A_{i}|=|A_{k-1}|
and
|A_{i}\Delta A_{k-1}|\geq 4
.

In conclusion,
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}
is weakly-
(k-1)
-cross-free, so
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}|\leq c_{k-1}n
. But then

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{1}|+kn\leq k|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{2}|+kn\leq kc_{k-1}n+kn.

∎

Let
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and let
H_{1}
be the subgraph of
H
spanned by
\mathcal{F}_{1}
. Observe that every vertex of
H
, with the possible exception of the empty set, has at most
2(k-1)
neighbors. This is true as any two outneighbors of a non-empty set weakly-cross, and any two inneighbors of a set of size at least 3 weakly-cross. Therefore,
H_{1}
has at least

e(H)-2(k-1)|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|-n\geq|\mathcal{F}|-dn

edges for some constant
d=d(k)
, where the
-n
term accounts for the possible removal of the empty set. In
H_{1}
, the outdegree of every vertex is at most 1, and the indegree of every vertex is at most
k-1
. The former implies (after also noting that
H
contains no directed cycles) that every connected component of
H_{2}
is a directed rooted tree, whose edges are directed towards the root.

Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a maximum sized collection of pairwise disjoint continuous chains in
\mathcal{F}_{1}
, each
C_{i}
of size
h+1
. Each such chain
C_{i}
is a directed path of length
h
in
H_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}C_{i}
, and let
H_{2}
be the subgraph of
H_{1}
induced by
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Then the number of edges of
H_{2}
is at least

e(H_{2})\geq e(H_{1})-\sum_{i\in I}2(k-1)|C_{i}|\geq|\mathcal{F}|-dn-2(k-1)(h+1)|I|.

By the maximality of
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
H_{2}
does not contain a directed path of length
h+1
. But each connected component of
H_{2}
is a directed rooted tree, whose edges are directed towards the root, and each vertex has indegree at most
k-1
. If such a tree does not contain a directed path of length
h+1
, its size is at most
1+(k-1)+\dots+(k-1)^{h-1}=:K
. This implies that the total number of edges of
H_{2}
is at most

e(H_{2})\leq\frac{K-1}{K}|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\leq\frac{K-1}{K}|\mathcal{F}|.

Comparing the lower and upper bounds on the number of edges of
H_{2}
, we get

|\mathcal{F}|-dn-2(k-1)(h+1)|I|\leq\frac{K-1}{K}|\mathcal{F}|,

which implies

|I|\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}|-Kdn}{2(k-1)(h+1)K}\geq\frac{c-Kd}{2(k-1)(h+1)K}n.

Set
c:=2\alpha(k-1)(h+1)K+Kd
. Then
c
only depends on
k,h,\alpha
, we have
|I|\geq\alpha n
, and thus the collection
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I}
satisfies the required properties.
∎

Let
h=(2k)^{2k^{2}+1}
and
\alpha=32(h+2)!k^{h+2}
, and let
c
be the constant guaranteed by
Section

3
. Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i\in I_{0}}
be a collection of pairwise disjoint continuous chains in
\mathcal{F}
, each of size
h+1
. For every
i\in I_{0}
, the members of
C_{i}
can be written as

A\subset A\cup\{x_{1}\}\subset A\cup\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\subset\dots\subset A\cup\{x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\}

with a suitable set
A
and elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\in X\setminus A
. Define

X_{i}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\}.

Moreover, for
x\in X_{i}
, let
C_{i}(x)
denote the largest set in
C_{i}
not containing
x
. Then
C_{i}(x),C_{i}(x)\cup\{x\}\in C_{i}
.

Next, we prove that we can select a dense subcollection of these chains with several additional properties. In particular, we want to find
I\subset I_{0}
such that the following conditions are satisfied.

C1

If
i,j\in I
and
x\in X_{i}\cap X_{j}
, then
C_{i}(x)
and
C_{j}(x)
are comparable.

C2

There is a total ordering
\prec
of
X
such that if
i\in I
and
x,y\in X_{i}
with
x\prec y
, then
C_{i}(x)\subset C_{i}(y)
.

C3

If
i,j\in I
and
X_{i}\cap X_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then either every member of
C_{i}
has smaller size than every member of
C_{j}
, or the other way around.

C4

If
i\in I
, then every member of
C_{i}
has size at least
3kh
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
I\subset I_{0}
of size
|I|\geq(kh+1)n
such that
C1
-
C4
are satisfied.

Proof.

For
x\in X
, let

S_{x}=\{C_{i}(x)\cup\{x\}:i\in I_{0},x\in X_{i}\}.

Then
S_{x}
is a weakly-
k
-cross-free intersecting family. By
Section

2
,
S_{x}
can be partitioned into
k-1
chains. Let
D_{x}
be one of these chains, chosen randomly from the uniform distribution on the
k-1
chains. Let
I_{1}
be the set of indices
i\in I_{0}
such that for every
x\in X_{i}
, the set
C_{i}(x)\cup\{x\}
is in the selected chain
D_{x}
. Then each
i\in I_{0}
is included in
I_{1}
with probability
(k-1)^{-h}
, so
\mathbb{E}|I_{1}|=|I_{0}|(k-1)^{-h}
. Fix a choice of chains
D_{x}
such that
|I_{1}|\geq\mathbb{E}|I_{1}|
, and observe that
I_{1}
satisfies
C1
.

Let
\prec
be a random ordering of
X
, chosen from the uniform distribution on all
n!
orderings. Let
I_{2}
be the set of indices
i\in I_{1}
such that for every
x,y\in X_{i}
, if
x\prec y
, then
C_{i}(x)\subset C_{i}(y)
. Then for every
i\in I_{1}
, we have

with probability
1/h!
. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}|I_{2}|=|I_{1}|/h!
. Fix an ordering
\prec
such that
|I_{2}|\geq\mathbb{E}|I_{2}|
. Then
I_{2}
satisfies
C2
.

Let
H
be the auxiliary graph on
I_{2}
in which
i
and

are joined by an edge if
X_{i}\cap X_{j}\neq\emptyset
, and some member of
C_{i}
has the same size as some member of
C_{j}
. Then every

has degree at most

. Indeed, for every
x\in X_{i}
, the sets
\{C_{j}(x):j\in I_{2},x\in X_{j}\}
form a chain, so there are at most
4h
indices

with

and
||C_{j}(x)|-|C_{i}(x)||\leq 2h
. But if
||C_{j}(x)|-|C_{i}(x)||>2h
, then no member of
C_{i}
can have the same size as a member of
C_{j}
. Let
I_{3}
be a maximal sized independent set of

, then

satisfies
C3
as well. By Turán’s theorem,
|I_{3}|\geq|I_{2}|/(4h^{2}+1)
.

Finally, remove all indices

such that the minimum of

has size less than

, and let

be the resulting set. By

, we have
|I|\geq|I_{3}|-\sum_{\ell=1}^{3kh}(k-1)n/\ell\geq|I_{3}|-3k^{2}hn
.
Therefore,

|I|\geq\frac{|I_{0}|}{(4h^{2}+1)h!(k-1)^{h}}-3k^{2}hn\geq(kh+1)n.

∎

In the rest of the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, we fix

of size

satisfying

-

. With the help of these chains, we define a tree encoding comparabilities between the chains, with a number of other advantageous properties.

We introduce some notation and terminology related to trees. Given a rooted tree
T
, we imagine
T
being drawn in the plane without crossing edges such that the root is at the bottom, and vertices of the same
depth
(i.e. distance from the root) lie on a horizontal line (see

The following object is the heart of the proof of
Theorem

3.1
. A
cross-support tree
is a perfect rooted tree
T
with the following properties:

T1

Every vertex
v\in V(T)
is an element of
I
, and each edge
uv\in E(T)
receives a label
\rho(uv)\in X
. (Formally, each vertex
v
is labeled with an element of
I
, so we allow different vertices to have the same label. To simplify notation, we view
v
as an element of
I
, which should not lead to any confusion.)

T2

If
v\in V(T)
, the label of every edge with endpoint
v
is an element of
X_{v}
. If
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
are the children of
v
from left-to-right, then
\rho(vu_{1})\succ\dots\succ\rho(vu_{r})
.

T3

If
v
is not a root or a leaf with parent
p
and leftmost child
u_{1}
, then
\rho(pv)=\rho(vu_{1})
. Define

\phi(v):=\rho(pv)=\rho(vu_{1})

and

S_{v}:=C_{v}(\phi(v)).

T4

Let
v\in V(T)
be a non-leaf and let
u
be a child of
v
. If
x=\rho(vu)
, then
C_{v}(x)\subsetneq C_{u}(x)

T5

Let
u,u^{\prime}\in V(T)
be distinct vertices at the same depth such that
u
is to the left of
u^{\prime}
. Let
a
be their common ancestor, and let
v\neq v^{\prime}
be the children of
a
, where
v
is the ancestor of
u
(or
v=u
), and
v^{\prime}
is the ancestor of
u^{\prime}
(or
v^{\prime}=u^{\prime}
). If
u
is a leftmost vertex in
T[v]
, then
S_{u^{\prime}}\subseteq S_{u}
.

See
Figure

1
for an example of a cross-support tree. Let
T
be a cross-support tree with root
r
and height at most
k
. Then we can deduce the following additional properties of
T
.

T6

Let
u,v\in V(T)
such that
v\neq r
and
u
is a leftmost vertex
T[v]
. Then
\phi(u)=\phi(v)
and if
p
is the parent of
v
, then

C_{p}(x)\subsetneq S_{v}\subsetneq S_{u}.

T7

For any
u,v\in V(T)
and
A\in C_{u}
and
B\in C_{v}
,
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
.

T8

If
u,v\in V(T)
such that
v
is an ancestor of
u
, then
|S_{v}|<|S_{u}|
.

T9

After removing some children of
r
together with all their descendants, the resulting tree is also a cross-support tree.

Lemma 3.5
.

T
satisfies
T6
-
T9
. Moreover,
T6
-
T8
is implied by
T1
-
T4
alone.

Proof.

T6:

Let
v=v_{0},\dots,v_{q}=u
be a path from
v
to
u
. Then
v_{i}
is the leftmost child of
v_{i-1}
for
i=1,\dots,q
, so
\phi(v_{i})=\phi(v_{i-1})
by
T3
. But then writing
x=\phi(v)=\phi(v_{i})
, we also have
S_{v_{i}}=C_{v_{i}}(x)
, so
S_{v}=S_{v_{0}}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq S_{v_{q}}=S_{u}
follows form
T4
. Moreover,
\rho(pv)=\phi(v)=x
, so
C_{p}(x)\subset C_{v}(x)=S_{v}
also follows from
T4
.

T7:

Let
B
be the smallest element of
C_{r}
. We prove by induction that if
u
is at depth
d
and
A\in C_{u}
, then
|A\cap B|\geq|B|-dh
. As
|B|\geq 3kh
by
C4
and the height of
T
is at most
k
, this implies
|A\cap B|>|B|/2
, which then implies
T7
. The statement holds for
d=0
, so assume that
d\geq 1
. Let
p
be the parent of
u
, then
C_{p}(x)\subset C_{u}(x)
for
x=\rho(pu)
by
T4
. By our induction hypothesis,
|C_{p}(x)\cap B|\geq|B|-(d-1)h
, so
|C_{u}(x)\cap B|\geq|B|-(d-1)h
as well. But
|C_{u}(x)\Delta A|\leq h
, so
|A\cap B|\geq|B|-dh
.

T8:

It is enough to prove the statement in case
v
is the parent of
u
, as then the claim follows by induction. But this follows from
C3
, as
X_{u}\cap X_{v}
contains
x=\rho(uv)
, and
C_{v}(x)\subsetneq C_{u}(x)
by
T4
.

T9:

It is clear that the resulting tree
T^{\prime}
satisfies
T1
-
T4
. In order to see that
T5
is also true, note that for any
v\in V(T^{\prime})
with
v\neq r
, we have
T[v]=T^{\prime}[v]
.

∎

The following lemma is the first key result about cross-support trees, showing that they contain many pairwise weakly-crossing sets.

Lemma 3.6
.

If there exists a cross-support tree
T
of height
k
where every non-leaf vertex has at least
k
children, then
\mathcal{F}
contains
k
pairwise weakly-crossing sets.

Proof.

Let
r=v_{0},\dots,v_{k}
be a root-to-leaf path such that
v_{i}
is not the leftmost children of
v_{i-1}
, and
\phi(v_{i})
is distinct from
\phi(v_{1}),\dots,\phi(v_{i-1})
. We can find such a path greedily by noting that for every
v\in V(T)
, the edges from
v
to the children of
v
recieve different labels (
T2
). For
i=1,\dots,k
, let

A_{i}:=S_{v_{i}}\cup\{\phi(v_{i})\}\in C_{v_{i}}.

We prove that
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
are weakly-crossing for every
1\leq i<j\leq k
. As

\displaystyle|A_{i}|=|S_{v_{i}}|+1<|S_{v_{j}}|+1=|A_{j}|\hskip 20.0pt\mbox{by {T8}}

\displaystyle(A_{i}\cap A_{j})\supset(S_{v_{i}}\cap S_{v_{j}})\neq\emptyset\hskip 20.0pt\mbox{by {T7}}

,

we have either that
A_{i}\subset A_{j}
, or
A_{i}
and
A_{j}
are weakly-crossing. We show that the former is impossible. Let
w
be the leftmost vertex in
T[v_{i}]
on the same depth as
v_{j}
. Then
\phi(w)=\phi(v_{i})
by
T6
. Let
x=\phi(w)=\phi(v_{i})
, and recall that
x\neq\phi(v_{j})
. As
v_{i+1}
is not an ancestor of
w
(as the ancestor of
w
is the leftmost child of
v_{i}
), the common ancestor of
w
and
v_{j}
is
v_{i}
. Therefore,
T5
implies that
S_{v_{j}}\subset S_{w}
. Crucially, as
x\not\in S_{w}
, we have
x\not\in S_{v_{j}}
and thus
x\not\in S_{v_{j}}\cup\{\phi(v_{j})\}=A_{j}
. As
x\in A_{i}
, we cannot have
A_{i}\subset A_{j}
.
∎

To finish the proof, we construct a cross-support tree of height
k
, whose every non-leaf vertex has at least
k
children. We build such a tree by induction.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\ell=0,1,\dots,k
, there exists
I_{\ell}\subset I
of size
|I_{\ell}|\geq|I|-\ell hn
, and for every
i\in I_{\ell}
, there exists a cross-support tree
T_{\ell,i}
such that

(1)

the height of
T_{\ell,i}
is
\ell
,

(2)

the root of
T_{\ell,i}
is
i
,

(3)

every non-leaf vertex has at least
h/(2k)^{k\ell}
children.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
\ell
. The case
\ell=0
follows by taking
I_{0}=I
, and defining
T_{0,i}
to be the single vertex tree, whose root is
i
. Now assume that
\ell>0
and that the statement holds for
\ell-1
instead of
\ell
.

For every
i\in I_{\ell-1}
, let
Y_{i}
be the set of labels on the edges connecting the root
i
of
T_{\ell-1,i}
to its children, then
Y_{i}\subset X_{i}
by
T2
and
|Y_{i}|\geq h/(2k)^{k(\ell-1)}
. Let
Z_{i}
be the second half of
Y_{i}
with respect to the ordering
\prec
of
X
, so
Z_{i}\subset Y_{i}
and
|Z_{i}|\geq|Y_{i}|/2
. For each
x\in X
, let

J_{x}=\{i\in I_{\ell-1}:x\in Z_{i}\}.

By
C1
, the family
\{C_{i}(x):i\in J_{x}\}
is a chain. Let
J^{\text{top}}_{x}\subset J_{x}
be the set of indices corresponding to the top
h
elements of this chain, or
J^{\text{top}}_{x}=J_{x}
if
|J_{x}|<h
. Define

I_{\ell}:=I_{\ell-1}\setminus\bigcup_{x\in X}J_{x}^{\text{top}},

then
|I_{\ell}|\geq|I_{\ell-1}|-hn\geq|I|-h\ell n
.

For every
i\in I_{\ell}
, we define the desired tree
T_{\ell,i}
as follows. For every
x\in Z_{i}
, there are at least
h
indices
j\in I_{\ell-1}
such that
x\in Z_{j}
and
C_{i}(x)\subset C_{j}(x)
. This is true because
i\in J_{x}\cap I_{\ell}
, which means that
|J_{x}^{\text{top}}|=h
and every
j\in J_{x}^{\text{top}}
works. Therefore, for each
x\in Z_{i}
, we can pick an index
j_{x}\in J_{x}^{\text{top}}
such that
j_{x}\neq j_{y}
for
x\neq y\in Z_{i}
. For each tree
T_{\ell-1,j_{x}}
, remove all children of the root
j_{x}
(and their descendants), whose joining edge is labeled by an element of
X
that comes before
x
with respect to the ordering
\prec
, and let
T^{\prime}_{x}
be the resulting tree. By
T9
,
T^{\prime}_{x}
is also a cross-support tree. Recalling that
x\in Z_{j_{x}}
,
x
is in the first half of
Y_{j_{x}}
with respect to
\prec
, so the root of
T_{x}^{\prime}
has degree at least
h/2(2k)^{k(\ell-1)}>h/(2k)^{k\ell}
. To summarize, we have the following properties of
T_{x}^{\prime}
:

•

T_{x}^{\prime}
is a cross-support tree with root
j_{x}
,

•

the leftmost edge from the root is labeled with
x
,

•

C_{i}(x)\subset C_{j_{x}}(x)
,

•

every non-leaf vertex has degree at least
h/(2k)^{k\ell}
.

Now define the tree
T^{\prime}
by taking the disjoint union of the trees
T_{x}^{\prime}
for
x\in Z_{i}
, and joining the root
j_{x}
of
T_{x}
to the new root
i
. The trees
T_{x}^{\prime}
,
x\in Z_{i}
, are drawn from left to right with respect to the reverse of the order
\prec
on
X
. We label the edge
ij_{x}
by
x
. Clearly, the highlighted properties of the trees
T_{x}
ensure that
T^{\prime}
satisfies
T1
-
T4
, which then imply
T6
-
T8
by
Section

3
. However,
T5
does not necessarily hold, so we remove some further children of the root of
T^{\prime}
.

For
d=0,\dots,\ell-1
, let
i_{d,x}
be the leftmost vertex of
T^{\prime}_{x}
at depth
d
. The family

\{S_{i_{d,x}}:x\in Z_{i}\}

is intersecting by
T7
. Therefore, applying
Section

2
in a total of
\ell
times, there exists
Q\subset Z_{i}
of size at least

|Q|\geq\frac{|Z_{i}|}{(k-1)^{\ell}}\geq\frac{|Y_{i}|}{2(k-1)^{\ell}}\geq\frac{h/(2k)^{(\ell-1)k}}{2(k-1)^{\ell}}>\frac{h}{(2k)^{k\ell}}

such that

D_{d}=\{S_{i_{d,x}}:x\in Q\}

is a chain for every
d=0,\dots,\ell-1
. Let
T=T_{\ell,i}
be the tree we get by keeping only those subtrees
T^{\prime}_{x}
of
T^{\prime}
for which
x\in Q
.

Claim 3.8
.

T
satisfies
T5
.

Proof.

First, we show that if
x,y\in Q
and
y\prec x
, then
S_{i_{d,y}}\subset S_{i_{d,x}}
. As
D_{d}
is a chain, we have that
S_{i_{d,x}}
and
S_{i_{d,y}}
are comparable. As
i_{d,x}
is a leftmost vertex in
T_{x}^{\prime}=T[j_{x}]
, we have
C_{i}(x)\subset S_{j_{x}}\subset S_{i_{d,x}}
by
T6
. But
y\prec x
, so
C_{i}(y)\cup\{y\}\subset C_{i}(x)
by
C2
, and in particular
y\in C_{i}(x)
. As
i_{d,y}
is a leftmost vertex in
T_{y}^{\prime}
, we have
\phi(i_{d,y})=y
by
T6
, so
S_{i_{d,y}}=C_{i_{d,y}}(y)
. In particular,
y\not\in S_{i_{d,y}}
. Thus, as
S_{i_{d,x}}
and
S_{i_{d,y}}
are comparable, we must have
S_{i_{d,y}}\subset S_{i_{d,x}}
.

In order to prove that
T
satisfies
T5
, we need to verify the following. Let
u,u^{\prime}\in V(T)
be distinct vertices at the same depth such that
u
is to the left of
u^{\prime}
. Let
a
be their common ancestor, and let
v\neq v^{\prime}
be the children of
a
, where
v
is the ancestor of
u
(or
v=u
), and
v^{\prime}
is the ancestor of
u^{\prime}
(or
v^{\prime}=u^{\prime}
). If
u
is a leftmost vertex
T[v]
, then
S_{u^{\prime}}\subseteq S_{u}
.

If the common ancestor
a
is not
r
, then this is true using that
u
and
u^{\prime}
lie in some cross-support subtree
T^{\prime}_{x}
,
x\in Q
. Hence, assume that
a=r
, then
v=j_{x}
and
v^{\prime}=j_{y}
for some
x,y\in Q
,
y\prec x
. Also,
u
is a leftmost vertex in
T_{x}^{\prime}
, so
u=i_{d,x}
for some
d
. We have
S_{u^{\prime}}\subset S_{i_{d,y}}
using that
u^{\prime},i_{d,y}
are vertices of the cross-support tree
T_{y}^{\prime}
. But then
S_{u^{\prime}}\subset S_{i_{d,y}}\subset S_{i_{d,x}}=S_{u}
, verifying the required claim.
∎

In conclusion,
|I_{\ell}|\geq|I|-\ell hn
, and for every
i\in I_{\ell}
, the tree
T=T_{\ell,i}
is a cross-support tree of height
\ell
in which every non-leaf vertex has at least
h/(2k)^{k\ell}
children. This finishes the proof.
∎

Setting
\ell=k
, and noting that
I_{k}
is nonempty, there exists a cross-support tree
T
of height
k
in which every non-leaf vertex has at least
h/(2k)^{k^{2}}\geq k
children. By
Section

3
,
\mathcal{F}
contains
k
pairwise weakly-crossing sets, contradiction. Thus, we must have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq cn
, finishing the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.
