A Characterization of α \alpha -Convex Functions with Sharp Coefficient and Schwarzian Estimates
Víctor Bravo, Pablo Carrasco, Rodrigo Hernández, Osvaldo Venegas

Abstract
. The class M α M_{\alpha} of α \alpha -convex functions, introduced by Mocanu in 1969, interpolates between starlike and convex functions. We prove a characterization of M α M_{\alpha} that extends a theorem of Chuaqui, Duren, and Osgood from the convex case to the full class, and determine sharp values of β \beta for which M α ⊂ C β M_{\alpha}\subset C_{\beta} and C β ⊂ M α C_{\beta}\subset M_{\alpha} . We also obtain a sharp Fekete–Szegő inequality, bounds for the order and the Schwarzian norm, and an explicit formula for the Schwarzian norm of the α \alpha -Koebe function for α = 1 / n \alpha=1/n , n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} , which we verify for n ≤ 9 n\leq 9 and conjecture to hold in general.



Key words:

\alpha
-convex functions, univalent functions, Schwarzian derivative.

30C45, 30C55, 30C80.

1.
Introduction

The class
M_{\alpha}
of
\alpha
-convex functions
was introduced by Mocanu
[
9
]
in 1969 as a generalization of convex functions within the theory of univalent functions that interpolate continuously between starlike and convex mappings, controlled by the real parameter
\alpha
. A function
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+\cdots
, analytic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}
, belongs to
M_{\alpha}
if

(1)

\operatorname{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}=\operatorname{Re}\left\{(1-\alpha)\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}+\alpha\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
\alpha=0
recovers starlikeness and
\alpha=1
convexity. Mocanu proved that
M_{\alpha}\subseteq C
for
\alpha\geq 1
, and Miller, Mocanu, and Reade
[
7
]
proved that
M_{\alpha}\subset S^{*}
for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, where
C
and
S^{*}
denote the classical classes of
convex and starlike functions. Moreover, for
\alpha\geq 1
the same authors showed that
M_{\alpha}\subseteq S^{*}_{\beta}
, where

\beta=\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)},

and
S^{*}_{\beta}
denotes the class of starlike functions of order
\beta
,
defined by

\operatorname{Re}\left\{J_{0}[f](z)\right\}=\operatorname{Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\beta,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The bound is sharp, with equality for the
\alpha
-Koebe function

(2)

k_{\alpha}(z)=\left[\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\xi^{1/\alpha-1}(1-\xi)^{-2/\alpha}\,d\xi\right]^{\alpha}.

In fact, the authors in
[
6
]
show that
f\in M_{\alpha}
(
\alpha>0
) if and only if there exists
g\in S^{*}
such that

(3)

f(z)=\left[\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{(g(\xi))^{1/\alpha}}{\xi}\,d\xi\right]^{\alpha}.

The previous example holds when
g(z)
is the Koebe functions
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
.

It is therefore natural to ask whether a more precise statement can be made on the convex side: for
\alpha\geq 1
, does every
f\in M_{\alpha}
belong to
C_{\beta}
for some
\beta>0
? We shall prove that the answer is yes, with
\beta=\beta(\alpha)\in[0,1)
explicit and sharp, where
C_{\beta}
denotes the class of convex functions of order
\beta
, characterized
by

\operatorname{Re}\left\{J_{1}[f](z)\right\}=\operatorname{Re}\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>\beta,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
together make the reciprocal connection between
M_{\alpha}
and
C_{\beta}
precise.

The main result of this paper is the following characterization of
M_{\alpha}
, which
generalizes a theorem of Chuaqui, Duren, and Osgood
[
1
]
to this setting.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Then
f\in M_{\alpha}
if and only if

(4)

\operatorname{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}\geq\frac{1}{4}(1-|z|^{2})\left|(1-\alpha)\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)}{zf(z)}+\alpha\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|^{2},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The class
M_{\alpha}
is not linearly invariant, which makes the usual invariant quantities, such as the order or the norm of the Schwarzian derivative, difficult to compute or estimate. In this paper, we give a complete determination of the order of
M_{\alpha}
in terms of
\alpha
, and a partial answer for the Schwarzian norm. Concretely, we establish a sharp coefficient estimate for
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots
in
M_{\alpha}
:

|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{1+2\alpha}\left[1+\left(\frac{|1-\alpha^{2}|-|1+\alpha|^{2}}{4}\right)|a_{2}|^{2}\right],

which refines existing bounds and highlights the influence of
\alpha
on the coefficients of functions in
M_{\alpha}
. When
\alpha=1
, the inequality reduces to the classical bound for
C
. In the final subsection, we compute the Schwarzian norm of the
\alpha
-Koebe function given by (
2
) for
\alpha=1/n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and formulate a conjecture for the full class
M_{\alpha}
.

2.
Results

Let
f
be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, normalized by
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, with
f(z)f^{\prime}(z)/z\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
f
is
\alpha
-convex if it satisfies (
1
), and we write
f\in M_{\alpha}
. Note that
M_{0}=S^{*}
and
M_{1}=C
. By
[
5
]
, every
function in
M_{\alpha}
is univalent and starlike, and
M_{\alpha}\subseteq C
whenever
\alpha\geq 1
.

The classes
S^{*}_{\beta}
and
C_{\beta}
of starlike and convex functions of order
\beta
(
0\leq\beta<1
) are classical extensions of
S^{*}
and
C
. They satisfy
C_{\beta}\subset S^{*}_{\gamma}
, where

(5)

\gamma=\gamma(\beta)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1-2\beta}{2(2^{1-2\beta}-1)}&\text{if }\beta\neq\dfrac{1}{2},\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{2\ln 2}&\text{if }\beta=\dfrac{1}{2},\end{cases}

see
[
8
,
3
]
. When
\beta=0
this reduces to the classical inclusion
C\subset S^{*}_{1/2}
. Notice that from (
1
) and the fact that

M_{\alpha}\subseteq S^{*}_{\delta}
(see
[
7
]
) with

\delta=\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)},

we have that

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}>\left(\dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\right)\text{Re}\left\{z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)\right\}>\left(\dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\right)\cdot\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}.

We have proved the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in M_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\geq 1
, then
f\in C_{\beta}
, where

\beta=\left(\dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\right)\cdot\dfrac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}.

The fact that
M_{\alpha}\subseteq C
for all
\alpha\geq 1
and that
C_{\beta}\subset C=M_{1}
for all
0\leq\beta<1
allows us to formulate the next question: given
f\in C_{\beta}
, does exist
\alpha=\alpha(\beta)
such that
f\in M_{\alpha}
? The next proposition gives a positive answer as a direct consequence of (
5
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
f\in C_{\beta}
with
0\leq\beta<1
, and
\gamma
is given in (
5
). Then
f\in M_{\alpha}
with

\alpha=1+\dfrac{\beta}{\gamma-\beta}.

Proof.

Let
0\leq\beta<1
. We know that if
f\in C_{\beta}
then

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}>\beta.

By (
5
) we conclude that
f\in S^{*}_{\gamma}
so that

\text{Re}\left\{z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)\right\}>\gamma.

If
\alpha=1+\beta/(\gamma-\beta)
then

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left\{(1-\alpha)z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)+\alpha\left(1+z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle>(1-\alpha)\gamma+\alpha\beta

\displaystyle=\left(1-\left(1+\dfrac{\beta}{\gamma-\beta}\right)\right)\gamma+\left(1+\dfrac{\beta}{\gamma-\beta}\right)\beta

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-\beta\gamma}{\gamma-\beta}+\dfrac{\beta\gamma}{\gamma-\beta}=0.

Then the proof is finished.
∎

Now we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
f
satisfies (
4
), then in particular
\operatorname{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}\geq 0
, so
f\in M_{\alpha}
. Conversely, suppose
f\in M_{\alpha}
. Then
J_{\alpha}[f](z)
belongs to the Carathéodory class
P
of holomorphic functions with positive real part normalized by
J_{\alpha}[f](0)=1
. Every function in
P

is subordinate to
(1+z)/(1-z)
, so there exists a holomorphic

\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\varepsilon(0)=0
such that

(6)

J_{\alpha}[f]=(1-\alpha)z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)+\alpha\left(1+z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)=\dfrac{1+\varepsilon(z)}{1-\varepsilon(z)},\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Subtracting
1
from both sides of equation (
6
) and considering that, by Schwarz lemma,
\varepsilon(z)=z\varepsilon_{0}(z)
, for some
\varepsilon_{0}
holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
with
|\varepsilon_{0}(z)|\leq 1
, we have:

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}[f](z)-1=\dfrac{1+\varepsilon(z)}{1-\varepsilon(z)}-1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(1-\alpha)\left(z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)-1\right)+\alpha z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{2\varepsilon(z)}{1-\varepsilon(z)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(1-\alpha)\left(\dfrac{zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z)}{f(z)}\right)+\alpha z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\dfrac{2z\varepsilon_{0}(z)}{1-z\varepsilon_{0}(z)}.

Since
f(z)\neq 0
for
z\neq 0
, which is guarantee by the univalence and normalization, we defined
(zf^{\prime}(z)-f(z))/f(z)=zh(z)
. Then

(1-\alpha)zh(z)+\alpha z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\dfrac{2z\varepsilon_{0}(z)}{1-z\varepsilon_{0}(z)}\Leftrightarrow(1-\alpha)h(z)+\alpha\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\dfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}(z)}{1-z\varepsilon_{0}(z)}.

Let
E=E(\alpha,z)=(1-\alpha)h(z)+\alpha(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)
. Then:

(7)

\varepsilon_{0}(z)=\dfrac{E}{2+zE}.

Since we know that
\lvert\varepsilon_{0}(z)\rvert\leq 1
, applying this to (
7
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\lvert\dfrac{E}{2+zE}\right\rvert\leq 1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\lvert E\rvert^{2}\leq\lvert 2+zE\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\lvert E\rvert^{2}\leq 4+\lvert z\rvert^{2}\cdot\rvert E\lvert^{2}+4\text{Re}\{zE\}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\lvert E\rvert^{2}\leq 4+4\text{Re}\{zE\}.

Notice that
zE=J_{\alpha}[f](z)-1
, so we have:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\lvert E\rvert^{2}\leq 4+4\text{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)-1\}\Leftrightarrow(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\lvert E\rvert^{2}\leq 4\text{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}.

Finally, we can conclude:

\text{Re}\{J_{\alpha}[f](z)\}\geq\dfrac{1}{4}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\cdot\lvert E\rvert^{2}.

∎

2.1.
Fekete–Szegő Inequality

The Fekete–Szegő inequality
[
2
]
has been extensively studied in geometric function theory. For univalent mappings
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots
it gives
|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq 1
, while for convex mappings (
M_{1}=C
) the classical sharp bound due by S. Trimble in
[
12
]
shows that
|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq(1-|a_{2}|^{2})/3
holds. The following results generalize this inequality to
\alpha
-convex class.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots\in M_{\alpha}
, then

|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq\dfrac{1}{1+2\alpha}\left[1+\left(\dfrac{|1-\alpha^{2}|-|1+\alpha|^{2}}{4}\right)|a_{2}|^{2}\right].

The bound is sharp and holds when, for
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
g(z)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{z}\left(\dfrac{1+t}{1-t}\right)^{\lambda}\dfrac{1}{1-t^{2}}dt
in the integral representation given by (
3
).

Proof.

Since
f
is starlike, we have that
z\dfrac{f^{\prime}}{f}(z)=\dfrac{1+\omega}{1-\omega}
with
\omega:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and
\omega(0)=0
, hence
\omega(z)=z\omega_{0}(z)
. This implies

(8)

\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}=\dfrac{2\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}+\dfrac{2\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}.

However,
f\in M_{\alpha}
, therefore there exists
\varepsilon:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
with
\varepsilon(0)=0
such that
J_{\alpha}[f](z)=(1+\varepsilon(z))/(1-\varepsilon(z))
, from which we have that

(9)

\dfrac{1+\omega}{1-\omega}+\alpha\dfrac{2z\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}=\dfrac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon},

Since
\varepsilon(0)=0
, its follows that
\varepsilon(z)=z\varepsilon_{0}(z)
. Thus, we have that

\frac{\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}+\frac{\alpha\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon},

and using equation (
9
) we have

(10)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\alpha\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}-\omega_{0}}{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\omega)}.

But
\omega^{\prime}(z)=\omega_{0}(z)+z\omega_{0}^{\prime}(z)
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}(z)=2\omega_{0}^{\prime}(z)+z\omega_{0}^{\prime\prime}(z)
. Thus, using equation (
8
), we have that

a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{\prime\prime}(0)-\omega^{\prime}(0)^{2},

and by (
10
) we have that
\alpha\omega^{\prime}(0)=\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)-\omega^{\prime}(0)
and from this,

\omega^{\prime}(0)=\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)}{1+\alpha}.

Additionally, differentiating (
10
), we obtain

\alpha\dfrac{\omega^{\prime\prime}(1-\omega^{2})+2\omega\omega^{\prime 2}}{(1-\omega^{2})^{2}}=\dfrac{(\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}-\omega_{0}^{\prime})(1-\varepsilon)(1-\omega)+(\varepsilon_{0}-\omega_{0})\left(\varepsilon^{\prime}(1-\omega)+\omega^{\prime}(1-\varepsilon)\right)}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}(1-\omega)^{2}},

evaluating at
z=0
yields

(11)

\displaystyle\alpha\omega^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)-\omega^{\prime}(0)+(\varepsilon_{0}-\omega_{0})(\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)+\omega^{\prime}(0)).

But,
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}(0)
and
\omega^{\prime\prime}(0)=2\omega_{0}^{\prime}(0)
. Using these relationships in (
11
), we get

\omega^{\prime\prime}(0)=\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(0)}{1+2\alpha}+\dfrac{2\alpha(2+\alpha)\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}(1+2\alpha)}.

Substituting, we have that:

\displaystyle a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(0)}{1+2\alpha}+\dfrac{2\alpha(2+\alpha)\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}(1+2\alpha)}-\dfrac{2\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2(1+2\alpha)}\left[\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(0)-\dfrac{2(1-\alpha^{2})\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}\right].

If we write
\varepsilon(z)=\varepsilon_{1}z+\varepsilon_{2}z^{2}+\cdots=z(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}z+\cdots)
and using that
|\varepsilon_{2}|\leq 1-|\varepsilon_{1}|^{2}
, we have that
|\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}(0)|\leq 2-2|\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)|^{2}
, and with this

\displaystyle|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2(1+2\alpha)}\left[2-2|\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+\dfrac{2|1-\alpha^{2}||\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)|^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1+2\alpha}\left[1+\left(\left|\dfrac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}\right|-1\right)|\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)|^{2}\right].

Since
\varepsilon^{\prime}(0)=(1+\alpha)\omega^{\prime}(0)
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=4\omega^{\prime}(0)
(see Eq. (
8
)), we conclude that

|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq\dfrac{1}{1+2\alpha}\left[1+\left(\dfrac{|1-\alpha^{2}|-|1+\alpha|^{2}}{4}\right)|a_{2}|^{2}\right].

The equality holds if we consider that

\varepsilon_{0}(z)=\frac{z+\lambda}{1+\lambda z},\quad 0\leq\lambda\leq 1,

which implies that, in the integral representation (
3
) the corresponding function
g
is given by

g^{\prime}(z)=\left(\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{\lambda}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-z^{2}}

∎

2.2.
The Order of a function.

On the other hand, the
order
of a mapping
f
is defined as the supremum of the modulus of the second Taylor coefficient of the function:

\frac{f(\sigma_{a}(z))-f(a)}{f^{\prime}(a)(1-|a|^{2})},

where
\sigma_{a}(z)=(z+a)/(1+\overline{a}z)
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
. This invariant is denoted by
A_{f}
and is given by

A_{f}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{2}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-\overline{z}\right|.

It is not hard to see that
A_{f}\geq 1
for any
f
and
A_{f}=1
if and only if
f
is a convex mappings. Moreover, if
f
is univalent, then
A_{f}\leq 2
. The reader can see Pommerencke’s classical paper
[
10
]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f
in
M_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in[0,1]
, then its order satisfies that
A_{f}\leq 2-\alpha.

Proof.

Subtracting
1
from both sides of equation (
9
) we obtain

\dfrac{2\omega}{1-\omega}+\dfrac{2\alpha z\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}=\dfrac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\dfrac{2\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}=\dfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}-\dfrac{2\alpha\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}.

Replacing the right side of the last equation in equation (
8
), we have

\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}=\dfrac{2\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}+\dfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}-\dfrac{2\alpha\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}=\dfrac{2\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}+\dfrac{2(1-\alpha)\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}.

Then (here
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\omega
, and
\omega^{\prime}
are evaluating at
z
)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-\overline{z}

\displaystyle=(1-|z|^{2})\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}+(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}\right)-\overline{z}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}-\overline{z}}{1-z\varepsilon_{0}}+(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}(1-|z|^{2})}{1-\omega^{2}}.

Applying Schwarz-Pick lemma and the fact that
|(\varepsilon_{0}-\overline{z})/(1-z\varepsilon_{0})|<1
for all
|z|<1
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}(z)-\overline{z}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}-\overline{z}}{1-z\varepsilon_{0}}\right|+(1-\alpha)\left|\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}(1-|z|^{2})}{1-\omega^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+(1-\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})\dfrac{|\omega^{\prime}|}{1-|\omega|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(1-\alpha)\dfrac{1-|z|^{2}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{1-|\omega|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}=1+(1-\alpha)=2-\alpha.

Thus, taking supremum, we have that
A_{f}(z)\leq 2-\alpha
, which completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Schwarzian Derivative

The Schwarzian derivative of a locally univalent mapping
f
is

S_{f}(z)=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}.

This is one of the most thoroughly studied invariant in complex analysis; see
[
1
]
and the references therein. Since every function in
M_{\alpha}
is univalent, its Schwarzian norm satisfies

\|Sf\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|Sf(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq 6.

We seek a sharper bound in terms of
\alpha
. As we mentioned in the introduction, if
\alpha\geq 1
, then
M_{\alpha}\subset S^{\ast}_{\delta}
and
M_{\alpha}\subset C_{\beta}
, where
\beta=(1-1/\alpha)\delta(\alpha)
and
\delta(\alpha)
is the increasing function given by

(12)

\delta(\alpha):=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\cdot\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})}.

It is not hard to see that
0\leq\delta(\alpha)\leq 1
for all
\alpha\geq 1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
f\in M_{\alpha}
, then
\|Sf\|\leq s(\alpha)
, where

(13)

s(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}6,&0\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}\\[8.5359pt]
\dfrac{2}{\alpha}\left(2-\alpha^{2}\right),&\alpha_{0}<\alpha<1.\\[8.5359pt]
2,&1\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}.\\[8.5359pt]
8\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\delta(\alpha)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\delta(\alpha)\right),&\alpha>\alpha_{1}.\\[8.5359pt]
\end{array}\right.

Here
\alpha_{0}=(\sqrt{17}-3)/2\sim 0.56
and
\alpha_{1}
is the number that satisfies
\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{1}}\right)\delta(\alpha_{1})=1/2
(
\alpha_{1}\sim 3.27
).

Proof.

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
we have that
f\in S^{\ast}
then
zf^{\prime}(z)/f(z)=(1+\omega(z))/(1-\omega(z))
and

(14)

1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{z\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1+\omega(z)}+\frac{z\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-\omega(z)}+\frac{1+\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}.

Also
J_{\alpha}[f](z)=(1+\varepsilon(z))/(1-\varepsilon(z))
, therefore

(15)

1+z\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{1+\varepsilon(z)}{1-\varepsilon(z)}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{1+\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}.

Recall that
\varepsilon(z)=z\varepsilon_{0}(z)
and
\omega(z)=z\omega_{0}(z)
as before. Thus, (we omitted the argument
(z)
),

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}=\frac{2}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}-\frac{(1-\alpha)\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}\right).

Then,

Sf=\frac{2}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}+\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}-(1-\alpha)\frac{\omega_{0}^{\prime}+\omega_{0}^{2}}{(1-\omega)^{2}}\right)-\dfrac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}-\dfrac{(1-\alpha)\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}\right)^{2},

which is equivalent to

Sf=\frac{2}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}-(1-\alpha)\frac{\omega_{0}^{\prime}}{(1-\omega)^{2}}\right)-\dfrac{2(1-\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{1-\varepsilon}-\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{1-\omega}\right)^{2}.

Using equation (
9
), we have that

Sf=\frac{2}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{\prime}}{(1-\varepsilon)^{2}}-(1-\alpha)\frac{\omega_{0}^{\prime}}{(1-\omega)^{2}}\right)-2(1-\alpha)\left(\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}}{1-\omega^{2}}\right)^{2}.

Using Schwarz-Pick Lemma and
1-|\varphi_{g}(z)|^{2}=(1-|g(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})/|1-zg(z)|^{2}
, where
\varphi_{g}(z)=(\overline{z}-g(z))/(1-zg(z))
, we have that

(16)

\begin{array}[]{lll}|Sf(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}&\leq&\dfrac{2}{\alpha}\left(1-|\varphi_{\varepsilon_{0}}(z)|^{2}+(1-\alpha)(1-|\varphi_{\omega_{0}}(z)|^{2})\right)+2(1-\alpha)\left(\dfrac{|\omega^{\prime}|(1-|z|^{2})}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2}\\[8.5359pt]
&\leq&\dfrac{2}{\alpha}\left(1+1-\alpha\right)+2(1-\alpha)=\dfrac{2}{\alpha}\left(2-\alpha^{2}\right).\end{array}

Note that
|Sf(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\leq 6
whenever
\alpha\geq(\sqrt{17}-3)/2\approx 0.56
, then the first two part of the
s(\alpha)
has been proved. On the other hand, when
\alpha\geq 1
, we have that
f\in C_{\beta}
, given by
\beta=(1-1/\alpha)\delta(\alpha)
. In fact, Suita in
[
11
]
proved that, for any
f\in C_{\beta}
, then the norm of the Schwarzian derivative is bounded by 2 when
0\leq\beta\leq 1/2
, and
8\beta(1-\beta)=8\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\delta(\alpha)\left(1-\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\delta(\alpha)\right)
when
1/2<\beta<1
. As
\beta=1/2
occur when
\left(1-\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\right)\delta(\alpha_{0})=1/2
, the proof is completed.
∎

2.3.1.
The Schwarzian derivative of
\alpha
-Koebe function

We end this work by showing an example associated with the integral representation of any given function in
M_{\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
. In
[
6
]
the authors shows that
f\in M_{\alpha}
(
\alpha>0
) if and only if there exists
g\in S^{*}
such that

(17)

f(z)=\left[\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{(g(\xi))^{1/\alpha}}{\xi}\,d\xi\right]^{\alpha}.

This integral representation allows us to construct
\alpha
-convex functions given any starlike function. In particular, if
k(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
(
z\in\mathbb{D}
) is the Koebe function we obtain the
\alpha
-convex Koebe function

(18)

k_{\alpha}(z)=\left[\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{z}\xi^{1/\alpha-1}(1-\xi)^{-2/\alpha}\,d\xi\right]^{\alpha}.

It is well known that the Koebe function maximizes many important geometric properties in certain classes of univalent functions; in particular,
k_{\alpha}
yields sharp growth estimates in
M_{\alpha}

[
4
]
.

From an algebraic point of view in equation (
3
), let
G(z)=\int_{0}^{z}(g(\xi))^{1/\alpha}\cdot\xi^{-1}\,d\xi
, then
f(z)=[(1/\alpha)\cdot G(z)]^{\alpha}
then

\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=(\alpha-1)\dfrac{G^{\prime}}{G}(z)+\dfrac{G^{\prime\prime}}{G^{\prime}}(z).

Thus, we can obtain an expression for the Schwarzian derivative in terms of
G

(19)

Sf(z)=\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)^{\prime}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)^{2}=SG(z)-\dfrac{(\alpha-1)^{2}}{2}\left(\dfrac{G^{\prime}}{G}(z)\right)^{2}.

Notice that
G
is not necessarily locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Now if we let
n=1/\alpha
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
, then (
2
) can be expressed as

(20)

k_{n}(z)=\left[n\int_{0}^{z}\xi^{n-1}(1-\xi)^{-2n}\,d\xi\right]^{1/n}=[n\text{B}(z;n,1-2n)]^{1/n},

where
B(z;a,b)
is the incomplete beta function. Notice that the function in the integrand is rational so if we integrate, the expression (
20
) can also be represented as

k_{n}(z)=\left[n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{n-1}{k}\left(\dfrac{1-(1-z)^{k-2n+1}}{k-2n+1}\right)\right]^{1/n}.

In this case
G(z)=B[z;n,1-2n]
and apply (
19
) with
\alpha=1/n
, we obtain

(21)

Sk_{n}(z)=\dfrac{1-n}{2z^{2}}\left[1+n\left(\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\right)^{2}\right]+\left(\dfrac{n^{2}-1}{2n^{2}}\right)\left(\dfrac{G^{\prime}}{G}(z)\right)^{2}.

It is not hard to see that
Sk_{n}(0)=6n(n-1)/((n+2)(n+1))
, then

\sup_{f\in M_{\alpha}}\|Sf\|\geq\frac{6n(n-1)}{(n+2)(n+1)}.

For
\alpha=1/n
, the expression
Sk_{n}
is a rational function of
z
and
G(z)
. This allows us to compute the Schwarzian norm numerically for each
n
. For
n=1,\ldots,9
, a direct computation (using Mathematica) yields

(22)

\|Sk_{n}\|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|Sk_{n}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{2}=2\left(3-\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right),\quad\quad n=1,\ldots,9.

Numerical evidence for
10\leq n<50
is consistent with this formula, as Figure

illustrates. Thus, we conjectured that
