Derivation and external validation of a prognostic nomogram for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 negative early young breast cancer in women
Authors: Ziqing Kong, Meiling Wang, Xixi Wang, Yutong Wang, Deqin Huang, Liqun Liu, Xunhao Zhang, Guangyu An, Weihong Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundYoung breast cancer (YBC) tends to have more aggressive phenotypes and poorer survival outcomes. This study aimed to derive and validate a prognostic nomogram for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative early YBC.MethodsA total of 7863 patients diagnosed with HER2-negative early YBC between 2010 and 2015 from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database were enrolled. Patients were randomly divided into a training and an internal validation set. To further evaluate the performance of the nomogram, we enrolled patients diagnosed with YBC at Chinese PLA General Hospital using the same inclusion and exclusion criteria. In training set, univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were performed to select independent prognostic factors, which were then used to develop a nomogram to predict the 3- and 5-year overall survival (OS). Calibration curves, concordance index (C-index), receiver operating characteristic curves (ROC), decision curve analysis (DCA) and Kaplan–Meier survival analysis were used to assess the accuracy and clinical utility of the model. The accuracy and predictive ability of the model were confirmed through validation with both the internal and external validation sets.ResultsMultivariate Cox analysis revealed that grade, estrogen receptor, histology, T stage, N stage, and surgery were independent prognostic factors, which were used to construct the nomogram. The C-indexes of the nomogram in the training, internal and external validation sets were 0.739 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.723–0.755), 0.748 (95% CI 0.723–0.773) and 0.711 (95% CI 0.573–0.848) respectively. The results of ROC curve analysis indicated good discrimination of the nomogram. Calibration curves revealed the high forecast precision of the nomogram. DCA curves and results of risk stratification showed the clinical usability of the nomogram, with better OS in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group (all P < 0.05).ConclusionsOur prognostic nomogram has good accuracy, forecasting precision, and clinical usability for predicting OS in HER2-negative early YBC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05398-y.

Background

According to GLOBOCAN2020, breast cancer (BC) has surpassed the lung cancer and become the most commonly diagnosed malignancy and leading cause of cancer-related deaths in women, making it a public health issue on a global scale [1, 2]. Related to ageing, BC affects mostly women over the age of 65 years. However, approximately 4% of newly diagnosed BC cases occur in women aged < 40 years, which is defined as young BC (YBC) according to the European Society of Medical Oncology (ESMO) guidelines [3, 4]. Notably, China has a higher proportion of YBC than that in western countries. Large retrospective studies have reported that the rate in Chinese population ranged from 7% to 16.4% [5, 6]. Compared to BC in elderly patients, YBC is reportedly more aggressive with features including higher expression of Ki67, poor differentiation grade, more vascular invasion, higher T stage, and more lymph node involvement with poor survival outcomes [7]. In recent years, due to changing lifestyles as well as the development of screening technologies, the incidence of YBC has increased alarmingly. Despite advances in therapeutic modalities, YBC is prone to relapse and is associated with a high risk of death. Hence, further studies are needed to better understand YBC at both the clinical and molecular levels.

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a transmembrane protein, whose amplification and overexpression promotes the invasion and metastasis of BC by activating downstream pathways such as PI3K/Akt and Ras/Raf/MEK [8]. Unfortunately, the traditional anti-HER2 drugs have little effective on HER2-negative BC [9]. Recently, a novel antibody–drug conjugate named T-DXd was reported to significantly prolong progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in patients with HER2 low metastatic BC, who were classified as HER2-negative traditionally [10]. The definition of ‘HER2-low’ was proposed with highly concerning and inconsistent results. However, few large-scale studies have focused on HER2-negative early YBC. Little is known about the prognostic factors for early YBC with HER2-negative status and the prognostic role of HER2-low in YBC patients, especially in the Chinese population. Therefore, in this study, we used population-based data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to derive and validate a nomogram for predicting OS in early YBC with HER2-negative status. An external validation cohort from the Chinese PLA General Hospital was used to validate the performance of the nomogram and compare the clinicopathological characteristics between the Chinese population and those in the United States.

Methods

Data collection and patient selection

The Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database is a publicly accessible cancer reporting system and an authoritative source for cancer statistics, encompassing about 48% of the total cancer patients in the US population [11, 12]. It is a collaborative effort between the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the National Cancer Institute, and regional and state cancer registries [13]. In our study, clinicopathological information was extracted from the SEER database (Incidence—SEER Research Plus Data, 18 registries, Nov 2021 Sub, 2000–2020) (http://seer.cancer.gov/) using SEER*Stat software (version 8.3.5, National Cancer Institute, Bethesda, MD). The inclusion criteria were: (1) year of diagnosis from 2010 to 2015; (2) pathologically confirmed invasive HER2-negative BC; (3) women aged 18–40 years at first diagnosis; (4) BC as the first primary tumour; (5) OS duration over 1 month; (6) patients without neoadjuvant therapy before surgery; Patients were excluded if: (1) diagnosed with inflammatory BC or Paget’s disease; (2) bilateral BC; (3) cases with incomplete information; (4) cases with any metastasis diseases when first diagnosis.

To evaluate the generalizability of the nomogram, we collected an independent external validation cohort from the First Medical Center, General Hospital of the Chinese People's Liberation Army. Between 2011 and 2015, clinicopathological data of 138 patients meeting the same inclusion criteria were retrospectively collected. The external validation cohort was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of the Chinese People's Liberation Army (Hospital Ethics No. S2023-307–1) in accordance with the Medical Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of the Chinese People's Liberation Army and the Declaration of Helsinki.Due to the retrospective nature of this study, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the same committee.

Variables

The following variables were extracted from SEER database: Patient characteristics including age (“18–35”, “35–40”) and marital status (“Married”, “Unmarried”); Tumour histology type (“Invasive ductal cancer, IDC”, “Invasive lobular cancer”, “Other”), primary tumour location, grade, American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage, T stage, N stage, oestrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PR) status; Treatment modalities including surgery (“Breast-conserving surgery, BCS”, “Mastectomy”, “No surgery”), chemotherapy, and radiotherapy; Prognostic information including vital status and survival time. The HER2-negative status was determined based on the 'Derived HER2 Recode (2010 +)' variable, which reflects a summary status derived from IHC and/or ISH/FISH testing recorded in the clinical records according to contemporary American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and the College of American Pathologists (CAP)(ASCO/CAP) guidelines. Patients categorized as HER2-negative in this derived field were enrolled in the study.

For the independent external validation cohort from the Chinese PLA General Hospital, clinicopathological data were retrospectively collected. The HER2 IHC and ISH/FISH results were independently reviewed and confirmed by two senior pathologists at our institution. In accordance with the ASCO/CAP guidelines, HER2-low was defined as IHC 1 + or IHC 2 + with negative ISH/FISH results. Importantly, all IHC 2 + cases were subjected to reflexive ISH/FISH testing to ensure the absence of HER2 amplification before inclusion.

Objective

The primary aim was to (i) compare the clinicopathological characteristics of YBC in a Chinese population with those in the United States and (ii) establish a prognostic nomogram for predicting survival outcome in HER2-negative stage Ⅰ-Ⅲ YBC. Survival outcomes were presented as overall survival (OS), defined as the time between the initial diagnosis of BC and death from all causes or the last follow-up. We also did an exploratory analysis to evaluate the prognostic role of ‘HER2-Low’ for early YBC in Chinese population.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to check the distribution of the measurement data. The data with normal distribution were expressed as means ± standard, and a t‐test was used for comparison between groups. Continuous measurement variables with skewed distributions were presented as medians with interquartile ranges, and differences between groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages, and comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariate and multivariate Cox analysis was used for identifying independent prognostic factor of OS. Variables that showed a P—value < 0.1 in univariate analysis in the training set were entered into the multivariate Cox model. Those with a P—value < 0.05 in the multivariate Cox analysis were deemed independent factors of OS in the training set, which were finally incorporated into the construction of the nomogram. Hazard ratios (HR) were presented with 95% confidence intervals (CI). The performance of the nomogram was evaluated using the concordance index (C-index), area under the curve (AUC) in receiver operating characteristic (ROC), calibration curves. Furthermore, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed to determine the practical clinical utility of the model. The specific clinical decision scenario anchored for the DCA was postoperative risk stratification to guide either intensified adjuvant systemic therapy or more frequent surveillance. OS was assessed using the Kaplan–Meier method and log-rank test.

All tests were two-sided, and results with P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was conducted using the R programming language and environment version 4.2.3 (http://cran.r-project.org). The main R packages used in our study were gtsummary (version 1.7.0), survival (version 3.5–3), rms (version 6.3–0), time ROC (version 0.4), and ggplot2 (version 3.4.0).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 7863 patients were included in the SEER cohort and YBC accounted for 5.03% of the SEER cohort. To construct and validate the prognostic nomogram for HER2-negative early YBC, patients were randomly divided into a training set (n = 5507) and an internal validation set (n = 2356) at a ratio of 7:3. To further evaluate the performance of the nomogram, we enrolled patients diagnosed with stage I-III YBC between January 2011-December 2015 at the Chinese PLA General Hospital using the same inclusion and exclusion criteria. We also obtained the HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) results for the participants enrolled from our institution. Finally, 138 patients were enrolled in the external validation set, and YBC accounted for 13.8% of the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort. Construction of the nomogram

Based on the results of the multivariate Cox analysis, we constructed a survival nomogram including grade, ER status, histology type, T stage, N stage, and surgery to predict the 3-year and 5-year OS in the training cohort. As shown in the nomogram presented in Fig. 2, the N stage had the strongest effect on OS, followed by the T stage, histology, grade, ER, and surgery. The nomogram was used to predict the 3- and 5-year OS using the total scores obtained from each variable.

Performance and validation of the nomogram

The C-index calculated based on the nomogram was 0.739 (95% CI, 0.723–0.755) for the training set. Time-dependent ROC curves at 3 and 5 years demonstrated AUC values of 0.804 and 0.765, respectively. These results indicated the excellent predictive accuracy and discrimination of the nomogram, as shown in Fig. 3. The calibration curves in Fig. 3 illustrate a high degree of conformity between the predicted and actual probabilities of OS in the training set. The performance of the nomogram was further validated in both internal and external validation cohorts. The C-index values for the internal and external validation cohorts were 0.748 (95% CI 0.723–0.773) and 0.711 (95% CI 0.573–0.848), respectively. Time-dependent ROC curves in the internal and external validation cohorts, as shown in Fig. 3, exhibited AUC values of 0.810 (3-year OS, internal validation cohort), 0.784 (5-year OS, internal validation cohort), 0.745 (3-year OS, external validation cohort), and 0.732 (5-year OS, external validation cohort). To further evaluate the clinical superiority of the developed model, we compared the AUC value of the nomogram with the conventional AJCC staging system and its individual components (T and N stages) (Figure S1, Table S1-S2). As a result, the nomogram exhibited superior prognostic value compared to other indexes in the training set (Figure S1A, Table S1-S2). Consistent results were observed in the internal and external validation sets (Figure S1B-C, Table S1-S2). Remarkably, good calibration and clinical usability were observed in both the internal and external validation cohorts. As shown in Fig. 4, the nomogram demonstrated superior net benefit over both the treat-all and treat-none strategies. In the external validation cohort, this benefit spanned a threshold probability range of approximately 5% to 25% for both 3-year and 5-year overall survival, while in the SEER-based training and internal validation cohorts, it extended further to approximately 40%. These ranges indicate that the nomogram maintains clinical utility whether a clinician adopts a conservative threshold of 10% predicted mortality risk or a more stringent threshold of 20–25% to select patients for intensified postoperative management.

Risk stratification ability of nomogram

The patients were divided into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score according to the nomogram. Kaplan–Meier curves were used to show survival differences between the high- and low-risk groups. Discussion

Breast cancer (BC) is a malignant disease characterized by high heterogeneity, resulting in widely varying clinical outcomes [14]. Compared to their elderly counterparts, YBC patients have a lower incidence but demonstrate more aggressive behavior with poorer survival outcomes. Therefore, multidisciplinary care and comprehensive management are necessary for YBC. In this study, we compared the demographic and clinicopathological characteristics of YBC between the SEER database cohort and the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort. We also developed a prognostic model, providing guidance for personalized treatment and intensive interventions of YBC.

Firstly, we observed a higher proportion of YBC in the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort compared to the SEER database cohort (13.8% vs. 5.03%). This higher proportion of YBC in the Chinese population is consistent with the 14.6% reported in the Fudan University cohort in 2017 [5]. Additionally, a multicenter retrospective study based on Chinese population also reported a higher proportion of YBC, with rates of 26.1% and 34.1% in 2015 and 2017, respectively [6]. In addition, a higher proportion of grade 2 and N3 stages was observed in the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort, along with a greater number of PR-positive patients. Regarding the choice of surgical method, the proportion of patients undergoing BCS was only 15% in the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort, which was significantly lower than that in the SEER database cohort. Consistent with our study, a retrospective study based on 14,782 Chinese individuals with early BC reported the proportion of BCS as 19.5% in their cohort [15]. The differences observed above can be explained by a combination of genetic heterogeneity, race, geography, and economic status.

Five common clinicopathological parameters including grade, histology, T stage, N stage, ER status and surgery, were selected based on multivariate Cox analysis to predict 3-year and 5-year OS in HER2-negative early YBC. The variables depicted in the nomogram were partially aligned with findings from previous studies. Liu et al. developed a nomogram to determine cancer-specific survival (CSS) in early YBC based on race, tumour location, grade, T, N, and molecular subtype [16]. Similarly, Huang et al. included grade, T, N, lymph node ratio, and ER, PR, and HER2 status in their nomogram to predict OS in YBC [17]. However, patients with HER2-positive status whose disease course could be completely changed by targeted HER2 therapy were excluded in our study [18]. Moreover, the SEER database does not provide information on targeted therapies. Therefore, we limited our cohort to patients with HER2-negative status to avoid the influence of HER2 expression and HER2 targeted therapy. ER alone is a potential strong prognostic factor in BC as reported previously [19], whereas little is known about the independent prognostic role of PR, whose expression is dependent on estrogen receptor [20]. It has reported that the gene expression profile of ER-negative/PR-positive/HER2-negative BC is similar to that of triple-negative (ER-negative/PR-negative/HER2-negative) BC [21, 22]. In our study, the status of ER and PR was statistically significant for OS in univariate analysis but not in multivariate analysis, indicating that PR alone was not an independent prognostic factor, which supports the results at the molecular level.

Whether surgical methods influence the survival of patients with BC has been debated. Several studies have indicated that BCS does not affect OS or DFS in early YBC [15, 23]. However, surgical method was an independent prognostic factor for HER2-negative early YBC in our study. Consistent with our study, Pu et al. constructed two survival nomograms to predict OS and CSS in YBC patients with liver metastases, and reported that surgery was an independent factor of OS and CSS [24]. In our study, patients who underwent BCS had the lowest risk of death compared to those who underwent mastectomy or those who did not undergo surgery. These results may also be attributed to BCS at an earlier disease onset, lack of lymph node involvement, and administration of radiotherapy following surgery. It is important to note that since the surgical method was identified as an independent prognostic factor and incorporated into our nomogram, the model is specifically designed for postoperative risk stratification rather than preoperative planning. Furthermore, the observed survival differences across surgical methods may partially reflect selection bias rather than a direct causal effect. Patients selected for BCS inherently possess more favorable baseline tumor characteristics, which confounds the association. Consequently, our nomogram should be utilized strictly for postoperative risk stratification based on the treatment already received, and should not be misconstrued as a tool to guide the selection of the surgical approach itself.

Furthermore, the prognostic variables incorporated in our nomogram are conventional clinicopathological parameters that do not fully capture the biological heterogeneity of breast cancer. Recent studies have highlighted that metabolic–immune remodeling and microenvironmental biomarkers contribute substantially to breast cancer progression beyond what is reflected by traditional staging variables. For instance, FAM111B has been implicated in metabolic–immune crosstalk and proliferative signaling in breast ductal carcinoma [25]; TMEM132A was identified as a mediator of metabolic reprogramming and macrophage-oriented immune remodeling associated with breast cancer progression [26]; CH25H exhibited context-dependent prognostic associations and coupling with anti-tumor immune in breast cancer [27]; and POSTN, a stromal matricellular protein, was shown to be closely linked to cancer-associated fibroblast and macrophage infiltration in ER + breast cancer [28]. These emerging biomarkers underscore the importance of metabolic and immune microenvironmental factors in shaping breast cancer outcomes. Future iterations of prognostic models may benefit from integrating such molecular and microenvironmental features alongside clinicopathological variables to further refine risk stratification.

To ensure the reliability and applicability of our prognostic model in real-world scenarios, we further evaluated the performance of the prognostic model in internal and external validation set. The internal validation set revealed a C-index of 0.748 (95% CI 0.723–0.773), indicating strong discriminatory power. ROC at 3 and 5 years displayed impressive AUC values of 0.804 and 0.765, respectively, highlighting the model's predictive accuracy and discriminative ability. Crucially, our comparative analysis underscores the superior prognostic accuracy of the developed nomogram over the conventional AJCC staging system alone as demonstrated by the time-dependent ROC curves. This advantage was particularly pronounced in the external validation cohort, where the 3-year and 5-year AUC values for the AJCC stage alone dropped to 0.601 and 0.603, respectively, whereas our nomogram maintained robust discriminative power with AUCs of 0.745 and 0.732. This notable improvement indicates that integrating critical biological intrinsic features and treatment factors with traditional TNM parameters provides a more granular, multidimensional assessment, making it a more reliable tool for postoperative risk stratification in clinical practice. Moreover, the nomogram demonstrated superior net benefit over the treat-all and treat-none strategies across threshold probabilities of 5–40% in the training and internal validation cohorts, and 5–25% in the external validation cohort. The more limited range in the external cohort is most likely attributable to its smaller sample size, which reduced the number of high‑risk patients at higher thresholds and made the net benefit estimates unstable beyond 25%. Importantly, the 5–25% range still encompasses the clinically most relevant decision thresholds, supporting the practical utility of the nomogram. One highlight of this study is that we effectively extended the performance evaluation of the model to an independent external validation dataset derived from diverse population of patients. Through this independent validation process, we confirmed the validity of the model and demonstrated its predictive accuracy across various patient cohorts, thereby providing a robust foundation for clinical decision making.

Recently, the definition of HER2 low has been proposed, which challenged the traditional classification of HER2 IHC score. HER2 low was reported to be a heterogeneous population containing hormone receptor-positive and triple-negative BC, and the clinicopathological features are associated with disease stage, race, and hormone receptor status [29]. There is no clear evidence suggesting that HER2 low is an independent subtype of BC, especially in YBC [30, 31]. Outcomes of several studies have supported the notion that HER2 low has better survival outcomes compared to HER2 IHC0 [32]. In contrast, some other studies have indicated that there are no significant differences related to survival between the two groups [33] or that poor OS is observed in the HER2 low [34]. Therefore, we conducted exploratory research to analyze the prognostic role of HER2-low in YBC in the Chinese PLA General Hospital cohort. As a result, no significant differences were observed in DFS or OS among the entire cohort, hormone receptor-positive subgroup, or triple-negative breast cancer subgroup. Our results indicated that HER2 low alone may not be an independent prognostic factor for early YBC.

Despite the outstanding performance of the nomogram, our study had several limitations. Firstly, the SEER database lacks the information such as HER2 IHC scores, Ki-67 indices, BRCA mutation status, and detailed therapy information such as endocrine therapy, all of which significantly impact the survival outcomes of YBC. Secondly, our external validation cohort from the Chinese PLA General Hospital is relatively small with a limited number of death events. This small sample size contributes to wider confidence intervals in our performance metrics, such as the C-index and AUC values, and may reduce the stability and generalizability of the model’s calibration and clinical utility assessments. Similarly, the analysis of the prognostic role of HER2-low status was based on this limited cohort, which may lack sufficient statistical power to draw definitive conclusions. Therefore, to gain a deeper understanding of the prognostic significance of HER2-low expression in YBC, future multi-center studies with larger cohorts and extended follow-up periods are warranted.

Conclusions

In summary, our prognostic nomogram reliably stratifies postoperative risk in patients with HER2‑negative early YBC. It identifies high‑risk individuals after surgery, helping guide intensive postoperative management and long-term surveillance.Furthermore, our results suggest that HER2-low in YBC may not be an independent prognostic factor.
