27 February 1990

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT: El Salvador: Update #3 on Jesuit Killings

1. The investigation and preparation of the government’s
case against nine Army personnel charged with the murders
continued, but there were few new developments. The government’s
Special Investigations Unit (SIU) has requested that the Army
High Command order those senior officers who attended.a meeting
at the National Intelligence Directorate early on 16 November to
be available for interrogation. In addition, a possible
witness--a man who claims to have been in the night watchman’s
house near the Jesuit residence on the night of the murders-~-has
been in contact wit and as of 16 February was
writing down his recollections.

2. The SIU briefed ten presidents of US Jesuit
universities, as well as the CODEL led by Congressman Moakley, on
the investigation. All appeared satisfied with the SIU’s
professionalism, although the CODEL suggested that SIU chief
Lieutenant Colonel Rivas be polygraphed to "clear the air" of any
allegations of a cover-up. € Colonel
Benavides confessed in private to Rivas to having ordered the
murders :

3. Except for one deserter, the Army personnel charged in
the case remain in military custody. According to Salvadoran

law, they must be cashiered from the milita before can be
turned over to civilian authorities.
Colonel Benavides is living in comfortable quarters and

frequently entertains colleagues who visit to discuss the case.
Benavides also allegedly has been permitted to travel toa
coastal resort owned by the armed forces.
