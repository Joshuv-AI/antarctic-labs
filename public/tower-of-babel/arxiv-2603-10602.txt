On the inner radius of the nonvanishing set for eigenfunctions of complex elliptic operators
Omer Friedland, Henrik Ueberschär

Abstract
. Let Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} be any open set. We consider solutions of H ​ ψ λ = λ ​ ψ λ H\psi_{\lambda}=\lambda\psi_{\lambda} , λ ∈ ℂ \lambda\in\mathbb{C} , where H H is an m m th order complex constant-coefficient elliptic partial differential operator. 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and context

A classical theme in nodal geometry, going back to Courant, is to understand how the zero set of Laplace eigenfunctions partitions a domain or a manifold. Besides counting nodal domains, a central geometric quantity is the
inner radius
of a nodal domain, i.e. the radius of the largest Euclidean ball that can be inscribed in the domain.
For Laplace–Beltrami eigenfunctions on closed manifolds, Mangoubi obtained general lower bounds for the inner radius of
each
nodal domain, with the optimal wavelength scale
\lambda^{-1/2}
in dimension
2
and polynomially weaker bounds in higher dimensions
[
5
,
6
]
. More recently, using techniques influenced by Logunov–Malinnikova’s work, Charron–Mangoubi proved that in dimensions
d\geq 3
every nodal domain contains a ball of radius
c\,\lambda^{-1/2}(\log\lambda)^{-(d-2)/2}
, centered at a point of maximal amplitude in that nodal domain
[
1
]
.

A complementary theme relates
mass distribution
of an eigenfunction to geometric lower bounds on inscribed balls. In particular, Georgiev
[
3
]
discusses how an
L^{2}
–distribution condition on coverings by “good cubes” forces a nodal domain to have large inradius, and Hezari
[
4
]
shows that Mangoubi–type inradius bounds improve along quantum ergodic sequences under small–scale equidistribution hypotheses.

The present paper is of a different nature. We work on an arbitrary open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(no boundary regularity is assumed) and consider
constant–coefficient elliptic operators

H
of order
m
, allowing complex coefficients and complex spectral parameter
\lambda
. For complex-valued solutions the classical decomposition into nodal domains may be degenerate (the complement of the zero set can be connected), so we focus on the inner radius of the
nonvanishing set

\Sigma_{\lambda}=\{\psi_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
.

Our main result is a quantitative inequality relating
\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})
to the proportion of
L^{2}
–mass carried by the interior
\Omega_{-r_{\lambda}}
at the scale
r_{\lambda}\asymp|\lambda|^{-1/m}
.
We also prove a localized form valid on any open subset
A\subset\Omega
.
Finally, we give an application showing that we have a uniform lower bound
\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\gtrsim r_{\lambda}
, unless 100% of the
L^{2}
-mass of
\psi_{\lambda}
concentrates in the boundary layer
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{-r_{\lambda}}
, as
|\lambda|\to+\infty
.

1.2.
Setting and notation

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let

H=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha},\quad c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C},

be a constant–coefficient differential operator of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
whose principal part is homogeneous of degree
m
.
We use the standard notation

D^{\alpha}=(i\partial_{x_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(i\partial_{x_{d}})^{\alpha_{d}},\quad|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{d}.

Let

P(\xi):=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}

be the (principal) symbol.
We assume
H
is
elliptic
: there exists
c_{\rm ell}>0
such that

|P(\xi)|\ \geq\ c_{\rm ell}|\xi|^{m},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1)

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
and let
\psi_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a distributional solution of

H\psi_{\lambda}=\lambda\psi_{\lambda}\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(2)

By interior elliptic regularity,
\psi_{\lambda}
is smooth in
\Omega
.

Define the nonvanishing set

\Sigma_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\Omega:\psi_{\lambda}(x)\neq 0\}.

Since
\psi_{\lambda}
is continuous,
\Sigma_{\lambda}
is open.
We define its inner radius by

\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda}):=\sup\{\rho>0:\exists x\in\Sigma_{\lambda}\ \text{with}\ B(x,\rho)\subset\Sigma_{\lambda}\}.

For
r>0
we define the
r
–interior of
\Omega
by

\Omega_{-r}:=\{x\in\Omega:\ B(x,r)\subset\Omega\}.

For an open set
A\subset\Omega
we write

A_{-r}:=\{x\in A:\ B(x,r)\subset A\}.

Finally, we set

r_{\lambda}:=|\lambda|^{-1/m}.

1.3.
Main results

Our main theorem is the following quantitative inradius estimate.
We state it in a scale–invariant form (no global normalization is required).

Theorem 1.1

(Quantitative inradius bound)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
H
be a homogeneous constant–coefficient elliptic operator of order
m
satisfying (
1
).
Then there exists a constant
c_{d,H}>0
(depending only on
d
and
H
) such that for every
|\lambda|\geq 1
and every nonzero solution
\psi_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
of (
2
) we have

\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\ \geq\ c_{d,H}r_{\lambda}\frac{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{-r_{\lambda}})}}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

(3)

The following localized version is obtained by applying Theorem
1.1
on subsets.

Theorem 1.2

(Localized inradius bound)
.

Let
\Omega
,
H
and
\lambda
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
A\subset\Omega
be open and assume
\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(A)}>0
.
Then

\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda}\cap A)\ \geq\ c_{d,H}r_{\lambda}\frac{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(A_{-r_{\lambda}})}}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(A)}}.

(4)

A simple consequence of Theorem
1.1
, which is the subject of the following corollary, is that we either have a uniform lower bound
\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\gtrsim r_{\lambda}
, as
|\lambda|\to+\infty
, or 100% of the
L^{2}
-mass of
\psi_{\lambda}
concentrates in the boundary layer
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{-r_{\lambda}}
.

Corollary 1.3

(Boundary layer concentration)
.

Let
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda_{j}|\to\infty
and let
\psi_{\lambda_{j}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
solve (
2
) with
\|\psi_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.
If

\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda_{j}})=o(r_{\lambda_{j}}),\quad\text{as}\;j\to\infty,

then

\|\psi_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{-r_{\lambda_{j}}})}\ \longrightarrow\ 0,

or, equivalently, 100% of the
L^{2}
-mass concentrates in the boundary layer

\Omega\setminus\Omega_{-r_{\lambda_{j}}}
of thickness
\asymp r_{\lambda_{j}}
.

2.
Two elementary geometric lemmas

We record two simple lemmas that convert Lipschitz control and
L^{2}
–mass into an inscribed nonvanishing ball.

2.1.
A nonvanishing ball from Lipschitz control

Lemma 2.1

(Lipschitz nonvanishing ball)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open and let
G:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
be Lipschitz on
\Omega
with Lipschitz constant
L
, i.e.

|G(x)-G(y)|\leq L|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\Omega
.
Fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
and assume
|G(x_{0})|\geq\eta>0
.
Set

\rho:=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\eta}{2L},\ \operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\Omega^{c})\Bigr\}.

Then

B(x_{0},\rho)\ \subset\ \{x\in\Omega:\ G(x)\neq 0\}.

Proof.

By definition of
\rho
, we have
B(x_{0},\rho)\subset\Omega
.
For
x\in B(x_{0},\rho)
we estimate

|G(x)|\ \geq\ |G(x_{0})|-|G(x)-G(x_{0})|\ \geq\ \eta-L|x-x_{0}|\ \geq\ \eta-L\rho\ \geq\ \eta-\frac{\eta}{2}\ =\ \frac{\eta}{2}>0.

∎

2.2.
Finding a point of large amplitude from an
L^{2}
lower bound

Lemma 2.2

(Pointwise lower bound from
L^{2}
mass)
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(B(0,R))
. Then

\sup_{B(0,R)}|u|\ \geq\ \frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,R))}}{\operatorname{vol}(B(0,R))^{1/2}}.

Proof.

Since
|u|^{2}\leq\big(\sup_{B(0,R)}|u|\big)^{2}
almost everywhere on
B(0,R)
,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,R))}^{2}=\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}\leq\operatorname{vol}(B(0,R))\Big(\sup_{B(0,R)}|u|\Big)^{2}.

Taking square roots yields the claim.
∎

3.
A uniform local Lipschitz bound at bounded spectral parameter

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
we need a uniform interior
C^{1}
bound for solutions of

Hu=\mu u
in a fixed ball, with
\mu
ranging over a compact set.
We obtain this by invoking a local derivative estimate from
[
2
]
.
Throughout this section we fix once and for all

\delta:=\frac{1}{2}.

(5)

3.1.
A local derivative estimate from
[
2
]

Theorem 3.1

(Local derivative bound
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
H=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}
be a constant–coefficient elliptic operator of order
m
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with symbol
P(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
.
Fix
\delta
by (
5
).
Let
x\in\Omega
,
r\in(0,1)
, and assume
B(x,r)\subset\Omega
.
If
\psi
solves
H\psi=\lambda\psi
in
\Omega
with
|\lambda|\geq 1
, then for every multi-index
\gamma
,

|D^{\gamma}\psi(x)|\ \leq\ C_{d,r,\gamma,H}|\lambda|^{|\gamma|/m}\Bigl(1+\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}^{1/2}\Bigr)\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(B(x,r))},

where

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:=\#\Bigl\{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |P(\xi)-\lambda|\leq|\xi|^{m-1+\delta}\Bigr\}.

Lemma 3.2

(Uniform boundedness of
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
on compact sets)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be compact. Then

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}\ <\ \infty.

Proof.

Write
P(\xi)=P_{m}(\xi)+P_{m-1}(\xi)
, where
P_{m}
is the homogeneous principal part and
P_{m-1}
has degree at most
m-1
.
By ellipticity of
P_{m}
there exists
c_{0}>0
such that
|P_{m}(\xi)|\geq c_{0}|\xi|^{m}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
P_{m-1}(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{m-1})
, there exists
R_{0}\geq 1
such that

|P(\xi)|\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}|\xi|^{m}\quad\text{for all }|\xi|\geq R_{0}.

Let
M:=\sup_{\lambda\in K}|\lambda|<\infty
.
If
|\xi|\geq R_{0}
and
|P(\xi)-\lambda|\leq|\xi|^{m-1+\delta}
for some
\lambda\in K
, then

\frac{c_{0}}{2}|\xi|^{m}\ \leq\ |P(\xi)|\ \leq\ |\lambda|+|P(\xi)-\lambda|\ \leq\ M+|\xi|^{m-1+\delta}.

Since
\delta=\tfrac{1}{2}<1
, the right-hand side grows strictly slower than
|\xi|^{m}
as
|\xi|\to\infty
, hence this inequality can hold only for
|\xi|\leq R_{1}
for some
R_{1}=R_{1}(K,H)
.
Therefore, for every
\lambda\in K
,
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
counts lattice points in the finite set
\{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |\xi|\leq R_{1}\}
, so
\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}
is uniformly bounded on
K
.
∎

3.2.
A uniform
C^{1}
bound on
B(0,3/4)

Proposition 3.3

(Uniform local Lipschitz bound)
.

Let
H
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
Then there exists a constant
L_{d,H}>0
such that for every
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
with
1\leq|\mu|\leq 2

and every solution
u\in L^{2}(B(0,1))
of

Hu=\mu u\quad\text{in }B(0,1),

we have

\sup_{x\in B(0,3/4)}|\nabla u(x)|\ \leq\ L_{d,H}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}.

In particular,
u
is Lipschitz on
B(0,3/4)
with Lipschitz constant at most

L_{d,H}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in B(0,3/4)
. Then
B(x,1/4)\subset B(0,1)
.
Apply Theorem
3.1
with
r=1/4
,
\lambda=\mu
, and with
\gamma
any first-order multi-index.
Since
1\leq|\mu|\leq 2
, we obtain

|\nabla u(x)|\ \leq\ C_{d,1/4,1,H}|\mu|^{1/m}\bigl(1+\mathcal{N}_{\mu}^{1/2}\bigr)\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(x,1/4))}.

Because
\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(x,1/4))}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}
and
|\mu|^{1/m}\leq 2^{1/m}
, it remains to bound
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
uniformly for
1\leq|\mu|\leq 2
.
This follows from Lemma
3.2
with
K=\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:\ 1\leq|\mu|\leq 2\}
.
Absorbing constants gives the claim.
∎

4.
A bounded overlap cover and a mass ratio lemma

Lemma 4.1

(Bounded overlap cover)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
r>0
.
There exists a (finite or countable) set
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subset E
with the following properties:

(1)

E\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}B(x_{j},r/2)
.

(2)

The family of dilated balls
\{B(x_{j},r)\}_{j\in J}
has bounded overlap: there exists
N_{d}\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
d
such that each point of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to at most
N_{d}
balls
B(x_{j},r)
.

Proof.

Choose
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subset E
maximal with respect to the property that the balls
B(x_{j},r/4)
are pairwise disjoint.
Maximality implies
E\subset\bigcup_{j\in J}B(x_{j},r/2)
: otherwise one could add a point of
E
not covered and still preserve disjointness of the
r/4
–balls.

For the overlap bound, fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider the set
J(x):=\{j\in J:\ x\in B(x_{j},r)\}
.
Then
x_{j}\in B(x,r)
for all
j\in J(x)
, so the disjoint balls
B(x_{j},r/4)
,
j\in J(x)
, are all contained in
B(x,5r/4)
.
Comparing volumes yields

\#J(x)\operatorname{vol}(B(0,r/4))\ \leq\ \operatorname{vol}(B(0,5r/4)),

hence
\#J(x)\leq 5^{d}
.
Thus one may take
N_{d}:=5^{d}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Existence of a good ball)
.

Let
r>0
and set
E:=\Omega_{-r}
.
Let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy
f\geq 0
and
\int_{\Omega}f>0
.
Define
M:=\int_{E}f
.
Let
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subset E
be as in Lemma
4.1
, so that
E\subset\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},r/2)
and the balls
B(x_{j},r)
have overlap bounded by
N_{d}
.
Then there exists
j_{0}\in J
such that

\frac{\int_{B(x_{j_{0}},r/2)}f}{\int_{B(x_{j_{0}},r)}f}\ \geq\ \frac{M}{2N_{d}\int_{\Omega}f}.

(6)

Proof.

If
M=0
the claim is vacuous, so assume
M>0
.
Since
E\subset\bigcup_{j}B(x_{j},r/2)
we have

M\leq\sum_{j\in J}\int_{B(x_{j},r/2)}f.

Argue by contradiction and assume that for every
j\in J
,

\int_{B(x_{j},r/2)}f\ <\ \alpha\int_{B(x_{j},r)}f,\quad\text{where }\alpha:=\frac{M}{2N_{d}\int_{\Omega}f}.

Then

M<\alpha\sum_{j\in J}\int_{B(x_{j},r)}f.

By bounded overlap,

\sum_{j\in J}\int_{B(x_{j},r)}f\ \leq\ N_{d}\int_{\Omega}f.

Hence
M<\alpha N_{d}\int_{\Omega}f=M/2
, a contradiction. Therefore (
6
) holds for some
j_{0}
.
∎

5.
Proofs of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\psi_{\lambda}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a nonzero solution of (
2
).
Set
r:=r_{\lambda}=|\lambda|^{-1/m}
and define

M:=\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{-r})}^{2},\quad N:=\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}>0.

If
M=0
then (
3
) is trivial. Assume
M>0
.

Step 1: choose a good ball at scale
r
.

Apply Lemma
4.2
with
f=|\psi_{\lambda}|^{2}
,
E=\Omega_{-r}
.
Since
\int_{\Omega}f=N
, there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega_{-r}
such that

\frac{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r/2))}^{2}}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r))}^{2}}\ \geq\ \frac{M}{2N_{d}N}.

(7)

Because
x_{0}\in\Omega_{-r}
, we have
B(x_{0},r)\subset\Omega
.

Step 2: rescale to unit scale.

Define the rescaled and normalized function
u:B(0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
by

u(y):=\frac{r^{d/2}\psi_{\lambda}(x_{0}+ry)}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r))}}.

(8)

Then
\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}=1
, and similarly

\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1/2))}^{2}=\frac{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r/2))}^{2}}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(x_{0},r))}^{2}}\ \geq\ \frac{M}{2N_{d}N}.

(9)

Since
H
is homogeneous of degree
m
, the rescaling (
8
) converts (
2
) into

Hu=\mu u\quad\text{in }B(0,1),\quad\mu:=r^{m}\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{|\lambda|}.

(10)

In particular,
|\mu|=1
.

Step 3: find a point where
|u|
is large.

By Lemma
2.2
with
R=1/2
and (
9
), there exists
y_{0}\in B(0,1/2)
such that

|u(y_{0})|\ \geq\ \frac{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1/2))}}{\operatorname{vol}(B(0,1/2))^{1/2}}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(B(0,1/2))^{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{M}{2N_{d}N}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

(11)

Step 4: use a uniform Lipschitz bound to inscribe a nonvanishing ball.

Since
|\mu|=1
, Proposition
3.3
(with
\|u\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}=1
) yields

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq L_{d,H}|x-y|\quad\text{for all }x,y\in B(0,3/4).

Apply Lemma
2.1
to the open set
\Omega^{\prime}=B(0,3/4)
, the function
G=u|_{\Omega^{\prime}}
, and the point
x_{0}=y_{0}\in B(0,1/2)\subset\Omega^{\prime}
.
Since
\operatorname{dist}(y_{0},(\Omega^{\prime})^{c})=\frac{3}{4}-|y_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{4}
, Lemma
2.1
gives a ball

B(y_{0},\rho_{0})\subset\{u\neq 0\},\quad\rho_{0}:=\min\Bigl\{\frac{|u(y_{0})|}{2L_{d,H}},\ \frac{1}{4}\Bigr\}.

Scaling back via
x=x_{0}+ry
yields

B(x_{0}+ry_{0},\ r\rho_{0})\subset\{\psi_{\lambda}\neq 0\}=\Sigma_{\lambda},

so
\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\geq r\rho_{0}
.

Since
\sqrt{M/N}\leq 1
, the elementary inequality
\min\{at,b\}\geq\min\{a,b\}t
for
t\in[0,1]

together with (
11
) yields

\rho_{0}\geq\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2L_{d,H}\operatorname{vol}(B(0,1/2))^{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2N_{d}}\Bigr)^{1/2},\ \frac{1}{4}\Bigr\}\Bigl(\frac{M}{N}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Therefore

\operatorname{inrad}(\Sigma_{\lambda})\geq c_{d,H}r\Bigl(\frac{M}{N}\Bigr)^{1/2}=c_{d,H}r_{\lambda}\frac{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{-r_{\lambda}})}}{\|\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},

where

c_{d,H}:=\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2L_{d,H}\operatorname{vol}(B(0,1/2))^{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2N_{d}}\Bigr)^{1/2},\ \frac{1}{4}\Bigr\}>0.

This proves (
3
).
∎
