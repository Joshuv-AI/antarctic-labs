Soft Algebra for
𝒩

Abstract
Scattering amplitudes of n n particles in nonabelian gauge theories admit factorizations of the general form 𝒜 n = 𝒜 n soft × 𝒜 n hard \mathcal{A}_{n}\;=\;\mathcal{A}^{\rm soft}_{n}\times\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n} , where 𝒜 n soft \mathcal{A}^{\rm soft}_{n} is IR divergent, while 𝒜 n hard \mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n} is IR finite and encodes the higher loop corrections to scattering. We specify a particular all-orders definition of this factorization for planar 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 super Yang-Mills (SYM) and argue that the resulting 𝒜 n hard \mathcal{A}_{n}^{\rm hard} obeys an uncorrected tree-level soft theorem. Moreover it furnishes a representation of the undeformed tree-level 𝒮 \cal S -algebra generated by a tower of soft gluons. The results follow from several commonly invoked assumptions for 𝒩 = 4 {\cal N}=4 SYM, including BDS one-loop exponentiation of the splitting function and amplitude/Wilson-loop duality.

(2.2)

2.2
Soft from collinear

Having determined the collinear limit of the remainder function, we would like to determine its soft limit. Formally, the soft limit is a particular case of the collinear limit. However, it corresponds to a degenerate limit with
z\sim s\to 0
in the language of the splitting function. When discussing the splitting properties of scattering amplitudes, one assumes
s\to 0
while keeping
z
finite. Terms invisible in the collinear limit may contribute when
z\sim s\to 0
. Consider, for definiteness, the six-point remainder function
R_{6}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, which depends on three independent cross-ratios

u_{1}=\frac{x^{2}_{13}x_{46}^{2}}{x_{14}^{2}x_{36}^{2}},~~u_{2}=\frac{x^{2}_{24}x^{2}_{15}}{x^{2}_{25}x^{2}_{14}},~~u_{3}=\frac{x^{2}_{35}x^{2}_{26}}{x^{2}_{36}x^{2}_{25}}.

(2.17)

We always approach the collinear and soft limits from the Euclidean region; namely, all separations
x_{ij}
are taken to be space-like unless
i
and
j
are consecutive, in which case the separation is null. In terms of dual coordinates, a soft limit corresponds to two points colliding. For example, consider

. In terms of cross-ratios, the soft limit corresponds to

\text{Soft:}~~~~~u_{1}=1+{\cal O}(\delta),~~~u_{2}={\cal O}(\delta),~~~u_{3}={\cal O}(\delta)

(2.18)

Consider now the collinear limit. In terms of dual coordinates, we take

(2.19)

We can then reach the soft limit through the collinear limit by further taking
u\to 0
. This, of course, is a subtle limit, as subleading corrections to the collinear limit may be enhanced as we take

2.3
Collinear, half-collinear and soft limits

3
Non-MHV amplitudes

In this section, we extend the preceding discussion to non-MHV amplitudes. It turns out that the ingredients used above also extend to this case, and hence non-MHV amplitudes are also expected to satisfy the leading soft theorem. This will be explicitly checked in various examples.

3.1
Argument for soft behaviour

Non-MHV amplitudes in
{\cal N}=4
SYM can be conveniently described by introducing an on-shell superspace
[
78
,
60
,
5
,
49
]
. The on-shell superfield
\Phi
, depending on a Grassmann variable
\eta^{A}
, with
A=1,2,3,4
, is such that the positive- and negative-helicity gluons are its top and bottom components, respectively,

\Phi=G^{+}+\eta^{A}\Gamma_{A}+\cdots+\frac{1}{4!}\eta^{A}\eta^{B}\eta^{C}\eta^{D}\epsilon_{ABCD}G^{-}

(3.1)

In on-shell superspace, all color-ordered amplitudes are combined into a single superamplitude
{\cal A}_{n}(\Phi_{1},\cdots,\Phi_{n})
. Individual helicity components can be extracted by expanding in the Grassmann variables
\eta^{A}_{i}
. The MHV superamplitude
{\cal A}_{n,MHV}
is given by

{\cal A}_{n,MHV}=i\frac{\delta^{(4)}(\sum_{i}p_{i})\delta^{(8)}(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{i}^{A})}{\langle 12\rangle\cdots\langle n1\rangle}M_{n},

(3.2)

where
M_{n}
is exactly the normalized amplitude introduced in (
2.2
) and contains all the loop information. External particles are labelled by
(\lambda_{i},\tilde{\lambda}_{i},\eta_{i})
such that their null momenta are given by
p_{i}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}^{\dot{\alpha}}
. The Grassmann delta function is given by

\delta^{(8)}(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{i}^{A})=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{2}\prod_{A=1}^{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{\alpha}\eta_{i}^{A}\right),

(3.3)

so that the coefficient in front of a given
(\eta_{i})^{4}(\eta_{j})^{4}
reproduces the familiar
\langle ij\rangle^{4}
in the numerator of the Parke-Taylor formula. We can now consider the ratio of the superamplitude to the super MHV amplitude

{\cal A}_{n}={\cal A}_{n,MHV}{\cal P}_{n}.

(3.4)

The infrared divergences are entirely contained in
{\cal A}_{n,MHV}
. As a result, the ratio
{\cal P}_{n}
is infrared finite. We will also assume the following, related to Assumption 1, for which
[
49
]
presented strong evidence.

Assumption 1
′
: Scattering amplitudes in planar MSYM possess dual superconformal symmetry.

While the symmetry is anomalous due to IR divergences, the anomalies in
{\cal A}_{n}
are fully encoded in the MHV superamplitude. Hence
{\cal P}_{n}
is dual superconformal invariant. Furthermore, as discussed in
[
69
]
, collinear factorisation also holds for the superamplitude, with the same splitting function as for the MHV amplitude, to all loops.

In the on-shell superspace approach, the collinear limit
(\lambda_{j},\tilde{\lambda}_{j},\eta_{j})+(\lambda_{j+1},\tilde{\lambda}_{j+1},\eta_{j+1})\xrightarrow{\text{coll}}(\lambda,\tilde{\lambda},\eta)
is described by
[
50

\displaystyle(\lambda_{j},\tilde{\lambda}_{j},\eta_{j})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle(\sqrt{z}\,\lambda,\sqrt{z}\,\tilde{\lambda},\sqrt{z}\,\eta)

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\lambda_{j+1},\tilde{\lambda}_{j+1},\eta_{j+1})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle(\sqrt{1-z}\,\lambda,\sqrt{1-z}\,\tilde{\lambda},\sqrt{1-z}\,\eta)

so that the momenta become collinear with
p_{j}\to zp,p_{j+1}\to(1-z)p
as usual, while
\lambda_{j}\eta_{j}+\lambda_{j+1}\eta_{j+1}\to\lambda\eta

in (

{\cal P}_{n}(\cdots,\lambda_{j},\tilde{\lambda}_{j},\eta_{j},\lambda_{j+1},\tilde{\lambda}_{j+1},\eta_{j+1},\cdots)\xrightarrow{\text{coll}}{\cal P}_{n-1}(\cdots,\lambda,\tilde{\lambda},\eta,\cdots).

(3.6)

Next we can study corrections to this leading collinear behaviour. In the MHV case, we saw that these corrections are governed by the operator product expansion of polygonal null Wilson loops. The superamplitude can also be mapped to supersymmetrised Wilson loops
[
31
,
76
]
. Furthermore, one can develop an OPE for these superloops

[

, but decorated by (dual) supersymmetric invariants with very simple behaviour in the soft limit. This motivates the expectation

{\cal P}_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{soft}}{\cal P}_{n-1}.

(3.7)

We will now see this explicitly in various examples.

3.2

We now discuss a variety of soft-limit examples in which we show that

(3.8)

Let us discuss

3.3
Half-collinear behaviour

For the discussion on the

To see this, consider for example (

to become half-collinear, so that

(3.34)

from which (

2.3
. In the variables of that section, the limit corresponds to
z_{s}\to z_{2}
with
\bar{z}_{s},\omega_{s}
fixed, or equivalently

with
\tau,\phi_{e}\to\infty
and
\tau-\phi_{e},\sigma
fixed. By the superamplitude/super Wilson-loop duality
[
31
,
76
]
, each Grassmann component of
{\cal P}_{n}
admits a superloop OPE expansion
[
81
,
42
,
45
]
. In the channel where the edges carrying

and
2

{\cal P}_{n}(\tau,\sigma,\phi,{\bf v};\chi)={\cal P}_{n-1}({\bf v};\chi)+\sum_{\Psi\neq 0}C_{\Psi}(\sigma,{\bf v};\chi)\,e^{-E_{\Psi}(a)\tau+ip_{\Psi}(a)\sigma+im_{\Psi}\phi},

(3.35)

where the first term is the vacuum exchange and the sum runs over non-vacuum super-flux-tube states. The Grassmann variables only dress the OPE coefficients; the dependence on

\boxed{\displaystyle{\cal P}_{n}(\cdots,s,2,\cdots)\xrightarrow{\text{half-coll}}{\cal P}_{n-1}(\cdots,p,\cdots).}

for two consecutive positive-helicity gluons is uncorrected from its tree value, and together with the corresponding result for

Soft/hard factorization

(4.2)

with

\displaystyle G_{n}=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a^{\ell}\left(\frac{f^{(\ell)}}{(\ell\epsilon)^{2}}+\frac{g^{(\ell)}}{(\ell\epsilon)}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{-s_{i,i+1}}{\mu_{IR}^{2}}\right)^{-\ell\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}f(a)F^{(1-loop)}_{n}+h_{n}(a)+\cal{O}(\epsilon).

obeys the uncorrected tree-level leading soft theorem

14

5
S
-algebra

In this section, we argue that, even though the SYM soft theorems themselves may (or may not) have loop corrections beyond leading order, the commutator algebra of the tower of soft theorems obeyed by
\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}
is the undeformed tree-level
S
-algebra. We expect similar conclusions to pertain to the
{\cal N}=4
extension of
S
, but we shall not consider it here.

The soft theorems relate two distinct objects. First, there are soft gluon insertions in an (
n+1
)–point amplitude, denoted by
\mathsf{S}
. Their short-distance OPE algebra is read off of the half-collinear splitting function. Second, there are the differential operators that appear on the right-hand side of a soft theorem, denoted by
\mathsf{T}
; these act on the
n
–point amplitude with the soft insertion removed. We will
see that the collection of all amplitudes of fixed
n
forms a representation (or module) of
\mathsf{T}
, while
\mathsf{S}
is an intertwiner connecting different representations.

5.1
Soft Algebras

The tower of
\mathsf{S}
generators and their commutators can be described either in plane wave basis of energy eigenstates, or in a Lorentz/conformal basis of boost eigenstates. At loop level, the energy basis becomes hard to define due to
\log\omega
corrections. The analysis appears simpler in the conformal basis, where the leading holomorphic singularity in the gluon OPE is just the Mellin transform of this collinear pole in (
4.7
)
[
63
,
87
]
. We briefly recap this construction here. The boost-weight-

\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,+}(z,\bar{z})=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\omega^{\Delta-1}\,a^{a}_{+}(\omega,z,\bar{z}),

(5.1)

where

is given in (
2.38

\mathsf{A}_{n}^{\rm hard}

\mathsf{A}_{n}^{\rm hard}(\Delta_{1},z_{1},{\bar{z}}_{1};\ldots\Delta_{n},z_{n},{\bar{z}}_{n})=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega_{k}\,\omega_{k}^{\Delta_{k}-1}\,\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}(p_{1};\ldots p_{n}).

(5.2)

OPEs of the
\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,+}
are then obtained from the collinear expansion of the hard celestial amplitudes. Since the leading pole in the splitting function is unchanged, the leading OPE is given by the tree-level result

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta_{1},+}(z_{1},\bar{z}_{1})\,\mathcal{O}^{b}_{\Delta_{2},+}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})\sim{}

\displaystyle-\frac{if^{ab}{}_{c}}{z_{12}}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}B(\Delta_{1}-1+r,\Delta_{2}-1)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\times\frac{\bar{z}_{12}^{r}}{r!}\,\bar{\partial}_{2}^{r}\mathcal{O}^{c}_{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1,+}(z_{2},\bar{z}_{2})+O(z_{12}^{0}).

All displayed antiholomorphic descendants remain at order
1/z_{12}
and are needed for the mode algebra.
Loop effects can still change less singular terms in the OPE, or equivalently the detailed action of subleading soft operators on hard data, but they do not change the pole which fixes the insertion-mode algebra.
15
15

15

Indeed, as argued in section
2.3

The beta function in (

(5.4)

The leading soft current discussed above has

as modes of

16

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}^{p,a}_{\bar{m},m}

\displaystyle=\Gamma(p-\bar{m})\Gamma(p+\bar{m})\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,z^{m+1-p}\oint\frac{d\bar{z}}{2\pi i}\,\bar{z}^{\bar{m}-p}\,\oint\limits_{\Delta=3-2p}\frac{d\Delta}{2\pi i}\mathcal{O}^{a}_{\Delta,+}(z,\bar{z}),

(5.5)

where the contours are around the poles and the residues vanish outside the wedge

5.2
Soft theorems

Now we turn to soft theorems. These relate amplitudes with two different multiplicities: an
(n+1)
–point amplitude with a conformally soft gluon
\mathsf{S}
equals the action of
\mathsf{T}
on an
n
–point amplitude. Schematically,

\left\langle\mathsf{S}^{p,a}_{\bar{m},m}\,\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}\right\rangle_{\rm hard}=\mathsf{T}^{p,a}_{\bar{m},m}\,\left\langle\prod_{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}\right\rangle_{\rm hard}.

(5.7)

In a general setting, especially for
p>1
,
\mathsf{T}
can receive loop-dependent corrections:

\mathsf{T}^{p,a}_{\bar{m},m}=\mathsf{T}^{p,a,{\rm tree}}_{\bar{m},m}+\Delta\mathsf{T}^{p,a}_{\bar{m},m}.

(5.8)

The tree-level term is a differential operator in
\bar{z}
of degree
2p-2
and lowers the conformal weight by
2p-2
. An explicit expression may be obtained by commuting the leading operator with special conformal transformations
[
70
,
82
]
. We define

\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{i}(z,\bar{z})\equiv\Bigl((\bar{z}-\bar{z}_{i})\bar{\partial}_{i}-2\bar{h}_{i}+1\Bigr)e^{-\partial_{\Delta_{i}}}.

(5.9)

and

\mathsf{T}^{p,a,{\rm tree}}(z,\bar{z})={1\over\Gamma(2p-1)}\sum_{i}T_{i}^{a}\,{1\over z-z_{i}}\,\bar{\mathcal{D}}_{i}(z,\bar{z})^{2p-2}.

(5.10)

Then one finds that
T^{p,a,{\rm tree}}_{\bar{m},m}
are the mode coefficients of
\mathsf{T}^{p,a,{\rm tree}}(z,\bar{z})
. Explicitly,

\mathsf{T}^{p,a,{\rm tree}}_{\bar{m},m}=\Gamma(p-\bar{m})\Gamma(p+\bar{m})\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,z^{m+1-p}\oint\frac{d\bar{z}}{2\pi i}\,\bar{z}^{\bar{m}-p}\,\mathsf{T}^{p,a,{\rm tree}}(z,\bar{z}).

(5.11)

In the previous subsection, we argued that the soft insertions on the left-hand side of (
5.7
) obey an uncorrected
S
-algebra. This implies that the full
\mathsf{T}
action on the right-hand side obeys the same uncorrected algebra. The explicit form of the tree-level generators (
5.10
) then implies integrability conditions on any corrections
\Delta\mathsf{T}
. Explicit checks of this would be of interest.

6
Discussion

We have shown that, given stated assumptions, color-ordered amplitudes in planar
{\cal N}=4
SYM admit a factorized form
\mathcal{A}_{n}\;=\;\mathcal{A}^{\rm soft}_{n}(\epsilon)\;\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n}
is IR-finite and obeys the uncorrected tree-level leading soft theorem. This result is the starting point for a discussion of soft algebras and theorems for planar
{\cal N}=4
SYM, and there are many interesting open questions.

•

Can we understand
\mathcal{A}^{\rm soft}_{n}(\epsilon)
as IR dressings or a soft-sector CFT
2
along the lines of
[
7
,
73
,
62
,
35
,
33
,
36
]
? In principle, one may hope to find a 2D theory which fully reproduces it, as was done for the much simpler cases of QED and gravity. A related goal would be to understand the IR-finite
\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n}
as the amplitudes for suitably dressed states.

•

The leading soft theorems agree with those of the tree-level amplitudes. Subleading theorems, on the other hand, appear to be deformed at loop level, and in the appendix we have computed the leading logarithmic contribution. This deformation should be closely connected to the holomorphic anomaly, which causes the usual special conformal transformations to be anomalous at loop order
[
15
]
. Indeed, at tree level, special conformal transformations together with the leading soft theorem generate the tower of soft theorems
[
70
,
82
]
. It would be very interesting to better understand both the anomaly of special conformal transformations in planar
{\cal N}=4
SYM and its connection to subleading soft theorems.

•

Subleading soft theorems in momentum space contain logarithms of the soft momentum. It would be interesting to study the effect of these logarithms in the celestial basis, where logs translate into higher poles. The celestial basis has been instrumental in finding the
S
-algebra, and we expect it to play an important role in understanding potential deformations. The recent discussion
[
82
]
gives another useful perspective on how 4D conformal symmetry organizes the tower.

•

The hard amplitude gives evidence that the
S
-algebra itself is not deformed, even though the subleading soft theorem can be. This distinction is important in comparing with examples where soft algebras are genuinely deformed by additional interactions/backgrounds
[
77
,
57
,
40
,
88
,
39
]
.

•

In some examples, e.g.,
[
82
]
, 4D commutators, 2D commutators, and double-soft limits are all equal or proportional. It would be very interesting to derive the undeformed closure directly from double-soft limits of
\mathcal{A}^{\rm hard}_{n}
.

•

It would be very interesting to develop the OPE for Wilson loops into a tool to study soft corrections to scattering amplitudes in planar
{\cal N}=4
SYM. In the present paper we have barely scratched the surface of this.
