 This paper develops the cosmological consequences of the replication-driven cosmogenesis frame-
 work introduced in Paper I and the emergent geometric structure established in Paper II. After
 the replication epoch freezes out, the coherent sector occupies a finite spectral band and contains
 a population of excited states. The relaxation of these excited coherent configurations does not
 produce coherent radiation; instead, all released energy flows into the incoherent substrate, where
 the randomizer acts as a rapid phase-scrambling mechanism. This process generates an effectively
 thermal radiation bath, providing a natural reheating mechanism that requires neither inflaton oscil-
 lations nor scalar-field potentials, and can be contrasted with standard scenarios of nonperturbative
 reheating dynamics [1]. Subsequent symmetry-breaking transitions in the coherent vacuum inject
 additional radiation, yielding a multi-stage thermal history with well-defined energy transfers. We
 derive the effective equations of state for each component—the cosmological vacuum, the coherent
 vacuum, and the radiation bath—and show how their interplay produces an FRW-like expansion.
 The discrete sequence of coherent-state relaxations imprints a distinctive multi-peaked stochastic
 gravitational-wave background, whose spectral structure reflects the underlying hierarchy of coher-
 ent frequencies. Potential observational signatures in the LISA and mid-band frequency ranges
 are highlighted, providing concrete avenues to test this replication-based cosmological framework
 in the context of standard cosmological gravitational-wave backgrounds [2, 3] and LISA-oriented
 forecasts [4].

 I. INTRODUCTION

 Papers I and II developed a pre-geometric framework in which a finite spectral substrate, endowed with stochastic
fluctuations and a replication rule 1 → n, gives rise to temporal order, spatial adjacency, an effective metric structure,
and a Lorentzian spacetime of emergent dimension deff ≃ 3. The replication epoch generates a finite network of
coherent units whose connectivity encodes the geometry, while the spectral bounds (ω min , ωmax ) enforce a minimal
time resolution and maximal causal speed. Once replication ceases, the resulting spacetime manifold (M, d) becomes
the geometric arena for the subsequent evolution of the Universe.
 In this third paper we analyze the cosmological consequences of the spectral dynamics underlying this framework.
The finite replication epoch depletes the high-frequency reservoir of the spectral substrate and drives the system
toward a configuration with two distinct vacuum layers: (i) a cosmological vacuum associated with the lowest-frequency
band (ωmin , ω1 ), which inherits the large-scale geometry constructed during replication, and (ii) a coherent vacuum
corresponding to the lowest coherent band (ω1 , ω2 ), which later serves as the ground state for field excitations. The
freeze-out of replication therefore marks the transition from a purely spectral substrate to a Universe with a geometric
background and a field-theoretic vacuum.
 A central result of this work is that the long-wavelength mode generated by the replication front acts as an
imprinting mechanism: as replication propagates through the spectral interval from ω max toward ω1 , it leaves behind
a slowly varying, spatially uniform low-frequency component. When replication terminates at ω 1 this mode becomes
the cosmological vacuum, providing the smooth background that carries the inherited metric and Lorentzian structure
at large scales. Coherent excitations—which will later be identified with matter and radiation fields—propagate on
this inherited geometry rather than generating it.
 Beyond vacuum formation, the depletion of the high-frequency reservoir leads to a controlled release of energy during
and after the freeze-out process. This generates reheating phases associated with successive spectral bands (ω 2 , ω3 ),
(ω3 , ω4 ), and so on, eventually producing a population of excitations that seed the matter and radiation content of the
post-replication Universe. The combined effect of geometric inheritance, vacuum stratification, and reheating yields
a cosmological evolution that naturally transitions to an effective FRW-like description without invoking inflationary
dynamics, directly comparable to the standard FRW framework of cosmology [5, 6].
 The goals of this paper are therefore:

 1. to describe the freeze-out of replication and the formation of the cosmological and coherent vacua;

 2. to analyze the energy transfer mechanisms governing reheating and the emergence of matter and radiation
 sectors;

 3. to derive the large-scale expansion history implied by the inherited metric and the cosmological vacuum energy;

 4. to connect the resulting dynamics to an effective FRW cosmology; and

 5. to identify observational signatures, particularly in the stochastic gravitational-wave background, that may
 distinguish this scenario from inflationary models.

 In this way, Paper III completes the transition from a pre-geometric spectral substrate to a macroscopic cosmological
spacetime with vacuum structure, matter content, and dynamical evolution. The resulting picture differs conceptually
from standard inflationary cosmology, yet it produces an expansion history compatible with present observations and
leads to potentially testable deviations in the gravitational-wave spectrum.

 II. FORMATION OF THE COSMOLOGICAL VACUUM

  Following the emergence of a three-dimensional spatial manifold in the sense developed in Paper II, the excitation
and randomization operators acquire a new representation. Whereas during the replication epoch both E and R acted
exclusively on spectral amplitudes a(ω), their action is now defined on functions a(x, ω) over the emergent spatial
manifold. This transition from a purely spectral to a mixed spatial–spectral domain constitutes the beginning of the
cosmological phase and sets the dynamical conditions under which the vacuum structure of the Universe forms.

 D. Formation of the Level-I vacuum

 During the transition, the substrate relaxes toward a configuration in which the dynamical operators are stable
under repeated application at the coarse- grained level. This relaxation drives the coherent population toward the
lowest effective frequency ω1eff , producing a uniform background configuration that we identify as the Level-I vacuum.

 1. It is spatially homogeneous due to the uniform coupling of E and R to the global mode R(t).
 2. It carries a residual energy density associated with the lowest coherent frequencies in the system.
 3. It remains stable under the combined spatial–spectral action of E and R once replication has ceased.
 4. By construction, it is not capable of forming localized excitations.
  The Level-I vacuum is thus the first coherent field configuration supported by the emergent spacetime and constitutes
the background on which reheating and particle-like excitations later develop.

 E. Distinction between Level-I and Level-II vacua

  Paper I identified two conceptually distinct layers of vacuum structure:
 • Level-II vacuum: the coherence-carrying band associated with the structured sector formed during the repli-
 cation epoch. This band encodes the internal consistency conditions that allow coherent units to replicate and
 interact.
 • Level-I vacuum: the uniform, spatially extended field formed after dimensional emergence, which supports
 excitations and cosmological dynamics.
 After spatialization, the Level-II sector becomes embedded within the Level-I vacuum as a substructure that governs
the behavior of localized excitations but does not participate directly in the cosmological expansion. The effective
gravitational and kinematical properties of the Universe arise from the Level-I sector; particle-scale properties remain
tied to the Level-II coherence conditions.

 III. VACUUM HIERARCHY AND COHERENT VACUUM FORMATION

 The termination of replication produces a qualitative reorganization of the substrate. Once the replication front
has traversed the entire emergent three-dimensional domain, the operators E and R act no longer on a purely spectral
space but on a fully spatialized configuration: the frequency manifold becomes a field defined over the emergent spatial
geometry. In this setting the long-wavelength component left behind by the causal front forms a spatially extended,
slowly varying background that occupies the lowest frequency interval (ω min , ω1 ). This background constitutes the
Level-I, or cosmological, vacuum. Above this band, the remaining degrees of freedom retain sufficient bandwidth and
locality to form stable coherent configurations once E and R act within the space–frequency domain rather than the
frequency domain alone. The resulting band (ω1 , ω2 ) relaxes toward a metastable ground configuration, the Level-II
coherent vacuum, which later serves as the substrate for excitations, symmetry-breaking transitions, and the origin of
matter and radiation. This section develops the structure, stability, and cosmological role of these two vacuum layers.

 C. Formation of the coherent vacuum

  Once replication halts, the spectral band (ω1 , ω2 ) relaxes toward its lowest-energy configuration under the combined
action of the randomizer, phase diffusion, and the stabilization mechanisms analyzed in Paper I. This relaxation
produces a coherent ground state with the following properties:
 1. Local coherence: Modes within this band have coherence lengths comparable to the geometric correlation
 scale L∗ , allowing the formation of localized excitations (particles, fields) with well-defined propagation on the
 inherited geometry.
 2. Spectral stability: No further replication occurs in this band after tf . The coherent vacuum acts as a
 stationary reference configuration for subsequent spectral transitions.
 3. Metric compatibility: Because the geometric structure is inherited from the cosmological vacuum, modes
 in the coherent band propagate on the same Lorentzian manifold (M, d) with causal speed c ∗ . The coherent
 vacuum does not determine geometry; it propagates on the geometry inherited by the cosmological vacuum.
  The coherent vacuum may therefore be interpreted as the field-theoretic vacuum that will later support the effective
quantum fields of the post-replication Universe.

 D. Coexistence of the two vacuum layers

  The cosmological and coherent vacua coexist on the same manifold M but play distinct roles:
 • The cosmological vacuum governs the large-scale curvature and acts as the background spacetime.
 • The coherent vacuum provides the ground state for matter and radiation excitations with wavelengths shorter
 than the geometric correlation scale.
  Level-II inherits the metric indirectly, because the dynamics of excitations are constrained by the geometric structure
imprinted in the Level-I vacuum.

 E. Preparation for reheating and field emergence

  The coherent vacuum sets the stage for the next dynamical phase: reheating. Transitions involving frequencies
above ω2 release energy into the coherent band, generating localized excitations that eventually populate the matter
and radiation sectors. The details of these processes are described in Section IV.
  At this point, the Universe consists of:
 (i) the inherited geometry, (ii) the cosmological vacuum, and (iii) the coherent vacuum.

 IV. REHEATING AND EMERGENCE OF MATTER AND RADIATION

 Once replication freezes out, the Universe contains a population of coherent units occupying the spectral band
(ω1 , ωmax ). During the replication epoch, excitation processes populate high-frequency components, so many coherent
units are born in excited configurations. After freeze-out, these excited states relax. Crucially, this relaxation does not
emit coherent radiation; all released energy is transferred to the incoherent substrate, where it is rapidly scrambled
into an effectively thermal bath.

 A. Absence of coherent radiation

 A coherent unit is a phase-stable object whose internal consistency depends on decoupling from randomizing
interactions. Any radiation emitted with coherent phase structure would constitute a new coherent unit, contradicting
the freeze-out constraint. Thus, relaxation cannot produce coherent or weakly coherent excitations.

Remark. This prohibition follows directly from the freeze-out condition: any coherent emission would constitute a
new coherent unit, violating the global termination of replication. Consequently, all decay products must enter the
incoherent sector, guaranteeing rapid thermalization.
 All energy released from excited coherent states therefore flows into the incoherent substrate. Because the ran-
domizer injects rapid phase diffusion (Paper I), this injected energy thermalizes almost immediately. The result is a
relativistic radiation bath with p = ρ/3.

 VI. GRAVITATIONAL-WAVE PRODUCTION AND OBSERVABLE SIGNATURES

  The replication-driven cosmology developed in this work produces gravitational waves (GWs) through several
mechanisms, each tied to distinct spectral and dynamical epochs. Because the replication front imprints the large-
scale geometry and the coherent sector undergoes a sequence of transitions, the resulting GW background carries a
characteristic multi-peaked structure that can be contrasted with standard cosmological GW backgrounds reviewed
in Refs. [2, 3]. In this section we identify the primary sources of GWs, derive their qualitative spectral properties,
and outline the mapping to present-day observables.

 A. Sources of gravitational waves

 Four distinct processes generate GWs in this framework:
 a. (i) Replication freeze-out When replication halts at ω1 , the transition from an actively driven spectral con-
figuration to a frozen cosmological vacuum creates large-scale perturbations in the emergent geometry. Because the
replication front is a long-wavelength, coherent disturbance, the resulting GW signal peaks at very low frequencies.
This component is analogous to the “IR tail” expected in some pre-inflationary or pre–big-bang scenarios.
 b. (ii) Decay of excited coherent states (primary reheating) The rapid internal relaxation of coherent units from
high-frequency support (ωk , ωmax ) down to the floor frequency ω1 generates localized, time-dependent quadrupole
perturbations on the emergent geometry. The corresponding GW emission has characteristic frequencies set by the
excited–ground transitions and produces a series of peaks associated with the spectral decomposition {ω k }.
 c. (iii) Symmetry-breaking transitions of the coherent vacuum Each symmetry-breaking event (SB j ) in the co-
herent vacuum reorganizes the ground-state structure and releases a fraction ∆ρ SB,j of energy into excitations. Such
vacuum transitions naturally excite tensor modes, producing sharp contributions to the GW spectrum. These resem-
ble the GW signatures predicted for first-order phase transitions in standard cosmology [4, 7, 8], though here they
arise from the intrinsic spectral-coherence structure rather than from a scalar-field potential.
 d. (iv) Interactions and oscillations of coherent units Residual coherent units can interact, merge, or undergo
shape oscillations. These processes excite localized tensor modes and contribute a stochastic high-frequency tail.
Although typically subdominant, they imprint small-scale structure on the overall spectrum.

 D. Comparison with inflationary GW backgrounds

  The GW background predicted by this model differs qualitatively from the inflationary one in several key ways:
 1. Multi-peaked structure: inflation typically produces a smooth, nearly scale-invariant spectrum, whereas the
 replication-driven model yields a series of peaks associated with excited-state decays and vacuum transitions.
 2. Absence of a primordial tensor tilt: the lowest-frequency component originates from replication freeze-out
 rather than quantum fluctuations of a scalar field.
 3. Distinct high-frequency behavior: coherent-unit interactions produce a UV tail unlike the exponential
 suppression seen in many inflationary models.
 4. Phase-transition-like features without a scalar potential: symmetry-breaking transitions of the coherent
 vacuum lead to strong GW production without invoking thermal bubbles or scalar-field potentials.
 These differences provide a clear path to distinguishing the replication-driven cosmology from both inflationary and
alternative high-energy models.

 E. Assessing detectability

  Given the diversity of characteristic frequencies, the model has potential signatures across multiple gravitational-
wave observatories:
 • PTA band (nHz): replication freeze-out; first symmetry-breaking event.
 • LISA band (mHz): intermediate excited-state decays; mid-sequence symmetry breakings.
 • Einstein Telescope / Cosmic Explorer (10–1000 Hz): late symmetry-breakings; coherent-unit interac-
 tions; UV tail.
 • GHz detectors (CMB-based or resonant experiments): extremely high-frequency remnants of early
 excited-state transitions (if any spectral weight reaches sufficiently high frequencies).
  The multi-peaked nature of the predicted spectrum is particularly suited to identification: the relative positions
and amplitudes of peaks encode the spectral hierarchy {ωk } and the energies ∆ρk and ∆ρSB,j of the underlying
transitions. In this sense, the gravitational-wave background acts as a direct observational probe of the pre-geometric
spectral structure.

 VII. DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK

  The replication-driven cosmology developed in this work provides a coherent sequence of dynamical phases linking
the pre-geometric spectral substrate to the large-scale, FRW-like Universe that emerges after reheating. In this
section we summarize the main conceptual results, emphasize the distinctive observational predictions, and outline
the theoretical directions that remain to be explored.

 11

 A. Summary of the cosmological sequence

  The model describes the early Universe as a finite spectral manifold (ωmin , ωmax ) endowed with excitation and
randomization dynamics (Papers I and II). A spontaneous coherent seed initiates an exponential replication cascade,
which imprints a Lorentzian geometry on the substrate and generates a long-wavelength cosmological vacuum. When
replication halts at the floor frequency ω1 , the emergent manifold (M, d) inherits its causal structure, maximal
propagation speed, and initial correlation length.
  The post-replication Universe contains a population of coherent units with spectral support throughout (ω 1 , ωmax ).
Their internal relaxation produces a phase of primary reheating, in which excited states decay to the ground coherent
band near ω1 . The resulting radiation bath propagates on the inherited geometry. A later sequence of symmetry-
breaking transitions reorganizes the ground coherent vacuum, generating additional matter and radiation. The large-
scale dynamics are well described by an effective Friedmann equation with three components: a cosmological vacuum,
a metastable coherent vacuum, and a matter–radiation sector.

 B. Distinctive predictions

  The spectral nature of the replication-driven model leads to several clear, qualitative predictions:

 • Multi-layered vacuum structure: the cosmological vacuum (ωmin , ω1 ) and the coherent vacuum near ω1
 play distinct dynamical roles, leading to stepwise reductions in vacuum energy during symmetry breaking.
 • Emergent causal and geometric scales: the maximal propagation speed c∗ , correlation length L∗ , and
 initial horizon scale are determined by the spectral properties of the replication freeze-out rather than by a
 fundamental action or inflaton potential.
 • Multi-peaked gravitational-wave background: replication freeze-out, excited-state decays, and symmetry-
 breaking transitions each produce characteristic peaks in the GW spectrum. The positions and amplitudes of
 these peaks encode the spectral hierarchy of the coherent sector.
 • Non-inflationary early expansion: accelerated expansion arises from replication rather than from slow-roll
 dynamics, providing a distinct mechanism for generating an effectively homogeneous and isotropic geometry.

Vacuum hierarchy and observables. Because the cosmological and coherent vacua originate from disjoint spectral
intervals, their relative densities encode information about the freeze-out dynamics. This hierarchy determines both
the late-time vacuum domination and the amplitude pattern of the GW peaks.
 These signatures offer several avenues for distinguishing this model from both inflationary and alternative non-
inflationary scenarios.

 C. Relation to existing theoretical frameworks

 The present work shares conceptual features with multiple approaches to emergent spacetime—including causal-set
theory, group-field theory, and condensed-matter-inspired models—yet differs in several key respects. The replication
mechanism yields a Lorentzian geometry without invoking a discrete spacetime or fundamental microscopic action.
The vacuum hierarchy emerges from the spectral structure rather than from scalar potentials, and reheating is driven
by internal transitions of coherent units rather than by inflaton decay.
 Despite these differences, the effective post-reheating dynamics align closely with standard cosmology, enabling
direct comparison with observational data while maintaining a fundamentally distinct origin for spacetime and vacuum
structure.

 D. Open theoretical questions

  Several aspects of the replication-driven cosmology remain to be investigated:

 • Perturbations and structure formation: an analysis of fluctuations around the emergent FRW background
 is required to determine the power spectrum of density perturbations and its relation to the spectral statistics
 of the reheating phases.

 • Particle content and interactions: the detailed mapping from the coherent sector’s symmetry-breaking
 cascade to effective field species remains open, as does the emergence of interaction strengths and masses.

 • Microscopic completion: although a Lagrangian formulation is not required for the spectral cosmology,

 foundation.

 • Numerical simulations: the replication dynamics, coherent-unit decays, and GW production merit direct
 simulation on discretized spectral lattices to quantify the predicted gravitational-wave spectrum.

  Addressing these questions will further clarify the viability and predictive power of the model.

 E. Outlook

 The replication-driven cosmology presented in this series of papers offers a novel and internally coherent alternative
to inflationary scenarios, grounded in a finite spectral substrate and a replication mechanism that generates both
geometry and causal structure. It naturally yields a two-layer vacuum hierarchy, a multi-peaked gravitational-wave
background, and a clear sequence of reheating processes.
 The next steps include a quantitative analysis of the gravitational-wave signals, a study of perturbations and
structure formation, and the development of numerical implementations that can test the dynamics of the coherent
sector and its symmetry-breaking cascade. Given the growing sensitivity of GW observatories across multiple frequency
bands, the model’s distinctive spectral imprints offer promising avenues for empirical testing.

 VIII. CONCLUSION

 In this paper we have completed the construction of a replication-driven cosmology, linking the pre-geometric
spectral substrate of Papers I and II to the large-scale cosmological evolution characteristic of our Universe. The
framework naturally generates spacetime geometry, vacuum layering, reheating, matter–radiation production, and an
effective FRW-like expansion without the need for a fundamental inflaton or scalar potential.
 The decay of excited coherent units yields an initial reheating phase, while a cascade of symmetry-breaking tran-
sitions in the ground coherent band produces a structured thermal history and a multi-peaked gravitational-wave
spectrum. The cosmological vacuum inherited from replication freeze-out acts as a geometric background analogous
to a cosmological constant, ensuring late-time accelerated expansion, while the coherent vacuum near ω 1 is treated
as a matter-like, wcoh ≃ 0 component whose energy density redshifts as a−3 (t) and does not contribute to ρΛ . Only
the cosmological vacuum contributes a true w = −1 term in the effective stress–energy tensor; the coherent vacuum
behaves as a nonrelativistic component once the replication cascade has frozen out.
 Together, these elements provide a self-contained cosmological narrative that is both conceptually novel and compat-
ible with the phenomenological structure of standard cosmology. The model’s distinctive gravitational-wave signatures
offer a promising path toward observational tests, particularly in current and future multi-band GW experiments.
This work is currently progressing though a complete re-examination of the foundational assumptions of Paper I in
the attempt to define a more structured formalization of the replication mechanism.
