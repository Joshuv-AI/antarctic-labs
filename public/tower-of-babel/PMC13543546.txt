The role of DNMT3B, GSK-3β and GJA3 in lens epithelial cell apoptosis and age-related cataract
Authors: Xiaoya Chen, Dongmei Su, Gaobo Zhang, Zihan Xiang, Yanjiang Fu, Zhaoyi Sun, Yuzhu Hu, Sijia Li, Yue Zhang, Xu Ma, Shanshan Hu, Qianqiu Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAge-related cataract (ARC) is a degenerative change following the aging of the lens, with its specific pathogenesis still unclear. This study investigates the expression of GSK-3β, DNMT3B, and GJA3 in ARC through in vivo and in vitro studies, elucidating the regulatory mechanisms among them. Additionally, it preliminarily explores the role of DNMT3B in the epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG)-mediated delay of apoptosis in lens epithelial cells (LECs). Immunohistochemistry and Western blot results showed that the protein expression level of DNMT3B was elevated, while the protein expression levels of GJA3 and p-GSK-3β (Ser9) were decreased in the ARC group and the aged mouse group (P < 0.05). CCK-8 and Hoechst 333,42 apoptosis assays indicated that DNMT3B, GJA3, and p-GSK-3β (Ser9) were involved in H2O2-induced apoptosis of LECs. Further investigation revealed that DNMT3B regulates lens epithelial cell apoptosis by modulating GJA3 expression, and the expression of DNMT3B is regulated by GSK-3β. Additionally, we found that EGCG effectively alleviates DNMT3B-mediated apoptosis of LECs (P < 0.05). In summary, our study indicates that p-GSK-3β, DNMT3B, and GJA3 play significant roles in ARC and oxidative stress-induced apoptosis of LECs, with interrelated regulatory mechanisms. Introduction

Cataract is characterized by a decrease in lens transparency, leading to a decline in optical quality. ARC is the most common type of cataract, predominantly affecting middle-aged and elderly individuals over 50 years old. The etiology and pathogenesis of ARC are complex. With the gradual aging of the population and the improvement of living standards in China, increasing social attention is being directed towards ARC, and its treatment market holds enormous potential. The risk factors for cataract include aging (systemic diseases, oxidative stress, etc.), trauma, metabolic disorders (such as diabetes and galactosemia), and genetic factors, with aging being the most significant risk factor [1, 2]. As age increases, the metabolism of the lens gradually decreases, and the activity and levels of antioxidants decline. This imbalance between the production and elimination of reactive oxygen species (ROS) leads to oxidative stress [3]. Studies have shown that dysfunction of DNA oxidative stress repair in LECs is one of the major causes of ARC [4], but the specific molecular mechanisms remain unclear.

Epigenetics is a branch of genetics that studies molecular modifications on DNA that can regulate gene activity without altering the DNA sequence and remain stable during mitosis. DNA methylation is the most extensively studied epigenetic mechanism and plays a crucial role in the regulation of gene expression [5, 6]. This process is catalyzed by the DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) family, which transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the 5th carbon of cytosine residues, forming 5-methylcytosine (5mC). DNMT3B is an important member of the DNMTs family and is involved in the de novo methylation process, contributing to gene expression silencing [6]. Research indicates a strong association between DNMT3B and the development of tumors such as breast cancer, thyroid cancer, and leukemia. However, its specific mechanisms in ocular diseases remain largely unexplored [7–10]. Scholars have detected the expression of DNMT3B in retinoblastoma and pterygium [11, 12]. Additional studies have confirmed through microarray analysis that DNMT3B expression is significantly elevated in patients with ARC compared to normal individuals, although the precise mechanisms remain unclear [13]. Therefore, we will target DNMT3B for further in-depth research to determine whether it plays a critical role in the development and progression of ARC.

Connexins (Cx) are transmembrane proteins that facilitate intercellular communication through hemichannels and gap junction channels. Among them, connexin46 (Cx46, encoded by the GJA3 gene) is a component of gap junctions in lens fibers [14]. GJA3 plays a regulatory role in various cellular processes, including cell growth, proliferation, differentiation, protection, and apoptosis. It has been proven to be crucial for normal lens growth and the maintenance of lens transparency [15]. The lens growth in GJA3 knockout mice is normal, but they exhibit dense central opacities, characteristic of “nuclear cataracts” [16]. Furthermore, research indicates that single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the GJA3 gene are linked to an increased risk of ARC and may play a role in the onset and progression of posterior subcapsular cataracts [17]. Our research group previously discovered that GJA3 plays a crucial role in H2O2-induced apoptosis of human lens epithelial cells (HLECs), and its downregulation is associated with the occurrence of ARC [18]. Additionally, abnormal methylation of the GJA3 gene promoter region is closely related to the development of ARC [19]. However, whether DNMT3B can mediate the expression of GJA3 in ARC has not yet been discussed in previous literatures.

Glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK-3β) is an evolutionarily conserved serine/threonine kinase that is involved in various signaling pathways, including Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/PTEN/AKT, and Notch. It can inhibit apoptosis through these signaling pathways [20, 21]. The activity of GSK-3β is determined by the phosphorylation status at different sites. Phosphorylation of serine-9 (Ser-9) at the N-terminal tail of GSK-3β inhibits the binding of primed substrates to the primed substrate binding domain of GSK-3β, thereby inactivating it [22]. Reports have indicated that upregulating Nrf2 activation by modulating GSK-3β phosphorylation can protect retinal ganglion cells from H2O2-induced oxidative stress damage [23]. Another study demonstrated that SB 216763 (GSK-3β inhibitor) significantly alleviates ferroptosis in LECs by inhibiting GSK-3β, thereby reducing iron accumulation and ROS generation [24]. Researchers have also found that the GSK-3β inhibitor 2i is associated with the DNA methylation levels of DNMT3B [25]. Therefore, we will further investigate the regulatory mechanism of GSK-3β in ARC and whether it can regulate the occurrence of ARC by mediating DNMT3B.

Epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), which is abundant in green tea, is one of the main components of catechin derivatives [26]. Among them, EGCG has the highest content and is the most potent antioxidant. It can scavenge ROS, inhibit apoptosis, and improve many oxidative stress-related diseases [27]. Additionally, numerous studies have reported on the effects of EGCG in ocular tissues. EGCG can reduce the survival and migration of human primary pterygium cells without damaging conjunctival cells, alleviate corneal, stromal, and endothelial edema, and reduce wound inflammation. Furthermore, it has a strong protective effect against UVB radiation-induced oxidative damage in mouse corneas and apoptosis in HLECs [28–31]. Another study indicates that EGCG inhibited mitochondria-mediated H2O2-induced apoptosis of LECs by regulating the signaling pathway [32]. Additionally, EGCG promotes phosphorylation of GSK-3β in Parkinson’s rat neurons [33], but a direct interaction between the two was not confirmed.In tumor cells, EGCG inhibits tumor growth by suppressing DNMT3B gene expression, which in turn reduces the methylation levels of promoter regions of downstream target genes of DNMT3B [34]. Our research group was the first to discover the inhibitory effect of EGCG on lens opacity in mice, effectively reducing apoptosis in lens epithelial cells of aged mice [35]. Therefore, we will further explore whether EGCG can target GSK-3β/DNMT3B protein expression to inhibit lens epithelial cell apoptosis and the occurrence of ARC.

This study aims to investigate the regulatory mechanisms of GSK-3β, DNMT3B, and GJA3 in oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis of LECs and the occurrence of ARC using cellular, animal, and clinical samples. We will conduct preliminary exploration on the potential of EGCG to target GSK-3β/DNMT3B and delay LECs apoptosis, providing a robust basis for understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying ARC.

Materials and methods

Collection of subjects and tissues

A total of 30 eyes of age-related cataract patients who attended Hongqi Hospital affiliated to Mudanjiang Medical University and underwent cataract ultrasonic emulsification surgery between January 2022 and June 2023 were collected as the ARC group. The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients aged 50–70 years (mean age 63 ± 4.6 years). The ARC group included 30 patients (30 eyes, n = 30) diagnosed with age-related cataract by slit-lamp examination and scheduled to undergo phacoemulsification combined with intraocular lens implantation. The normal control group consisted of 30 patients (30 eyes, n = 30) with lens dislocation caused by concurrent ocular trauma. The exclusion criteria were listed below: poorly controlled diabetes, hypertension, or metabolic eye diseases; a medical history of ocular trauma, glaucoma, uveitis, retinopathy, or intraocular surgery; concomitant congenital cataract, complicated cataract, or drug-induced cataract; and unqualified specimens, tissue autolysis, or other invalid samples. Prior to surgery, one-on-one preoperative informed communication was conducted by specialized researchers. All harvested tissue specimens were explicitly clarified to be used only for basic scientific research without any commercial application, and all patients’ personal privacy was strictly protected throughout the study. All participants were fully informed that anterior lens capsule collection was a standardized conventional intraoperative maneuver, which would not induce additional surgical trauma or delay postoperative recovery. Intraoperatively, the anterior lens capsule was routinely obtained by the same senior attending ophthalmologist following unified surgical standard procedures. The isolated tissue samples were equally divided into two parts. One aliquot was immersed in pre-cooled 4% paraformaldehyde solution for fixation at room temperature to prepare for subsequent immunohistochemical detection. The other tissue portion was immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently transferred to an ultra-low temperature refrigerator at − 80 °C for standby use in Western blot assay.The samples collected for this study followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Mudanjiang Medical University, and all participants were informed about the study and signed an informed consent form.

Establishment of animal model and tissue collection

C57BL/6J male mice were purchased from Viton Lever (Beijing, China). Thirty young mice (3 months, n = 30) and thirty old (Two animals died) (> 20 months, n = 28) male C57BL/6J mice were housed in an environment with an average temperature of 22 ± 2 °C and humidity of 55% ± 5%, with free access to food and water. A 12 h light/12 h dark cycle was performed and lens opacification was observed and graded using a slit‑lamp microscope, and the Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III) was applied to quantitatively evaluate the severity of lens opacity.The experimental protocol was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Mudanjiang Medical University and followed the ARVO statement on the use of animals in ophthalmic and vision research. Mice were euthanized by carbon dioxide asphyxiation combined with cervical dislocation, and the entire eyeball of each mouse was removed. An incision was made at the site where the optic nerve enters the eye, and the sclera was cut to expose the lens. Ciliary body debris attached to the equatorial plane of the lens was cleared, and the intact anterior capsular membrane of the mouse lens was torn with surgical microscope forceps.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tissue specimens were embedded in paraffin and sectioned prior to staining. The sections were baked in an oven at 65 °C for 2 h, deparaffinized with xylene and graded ethanol, and then subjected to high-temperature antigen retrieval in 0.01 mol/L citrate buffer (pH 6.0) by continuous boiling for 30 min, followed by natural cooling to room temperature. Subsequently, sections were incubated in 3% hydrogen peroxide solution at room temperature to eliminate endogenous peroxidase activity. After rinsing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), sections were blocked with fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) for 30 min. Thereafter, primary antibodies were added and incubated overnight at 4 °C, including anti-DNMT3B (1:50, Abcam, UK), anti-GSK-3β (1:50, Abcam, UK), anti-p-GSK-3β (1:50, Abcam, UK), and anti-GJA3 (1:50, Abcam, UK). On the next day, corresponding horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H + L) secondary antibody (1:200, ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China) was applied. Following incubation, diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogenic reagent (Cell Signaling Technology, USA) was used for color development, with identical reaction duration maintained across all sections. A total of 30 human samples and animal samples(30 young mice and 28 aged mice) were included in this study. All sections were dehydrated through graded ethanol and xylene, mounted with neutral balsam, and photographed and observed under a Nikon Eclipse 80i microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Immunohistochemical scoring criteria: All staining results were independently evaluated and determined by two researchers using a double-blind method. A semi-quantitative scoring system was applied. The proportion of positive cells was calculated and the average value was recorded: scores were defined as 0 for < 10%, 1 for 10%–25%, 2 for 26%–50%, 3 for 51%–75%, and 4 for 76%–100%. Staining intensity was assessed as follows:0 points for no staining, 1 point for pale yellow, 2 points for tan/yellowish-brown, and 3 points for dark brown. The final score was calculated by multiplying the positive cell proportion score by the staining intensity score.Grading standards: final score 0–1: (−); 2–4: (+); 5–8: (++); 9–12: (+++). Specimens graded from (+) to (+++) were defined as positive protein expression. Detailed statistical analyses are provided in Supplementary Material 2.

Western blot

Total protein from mice and different groups of HLE-B3 cells were extracted in RIPA lysed buffer with protease inhibitor cocktail (Pierce, USA). BCA Protein Concentration Assay Kit detects protein concentration, SDS-polyacrylamide electrophoresis gel separates proteins, and then total protein (40 µg) is transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane(Millipore, Billerica, MA, USA). PVDF membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C in the refrigerator along with anti-DNMT3B (1:1000, Abcam), anti-GSK-3β (1:1000, Abcam), anti-p-GSK-3β (1:1000, Abcam) and anti-GJA3 (1:1500, Abcam) antibodies. The following day, the corresponding secondary antibody (at a dilution of 1:3000) was introduced for incubation. Chemiluminescence reagents were activated under dark conditions, and three independent experiments were conducted.

Cell culture and plasmids

HLE-B3 cells were donated by Professor Qi (Harbin Medical University), and DMEM with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin were cultured in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C. Cells were pretreated with 10 µM SB 216763 (GSK-3β inhibitor) for 1 h and then exposed to H2O2 (200 µM). When the cell density reached 80%, the plasmid DNMT3B overexpression and interference plasmid (purchased from the Beijing Youbao Biological Company) was transfected with Lipomax reagent (Sudgen, Nanjing, China). After 4 h of cell starvation, 10% fetal bovine serum was added, and the cells were treated with H2O2 (200 µM).

CCK8 assay

Cells were cultured by inoculating 6,000 cells/well into 96-well plates. 24 h later, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 2 h at 37 °C in an incubator. Subsequently, cell activity was assessed by measuring the absorbance at 450 nm with an enzyme labeller instrument. Each set of experiments was repeated at least 3 times.

Apoptosis assay

HLE-B3 cells were cultured in 6-well plates, and after 24 h, the cells were fixed with formaldehyde, and Hoechst 333,42 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) was used to observe the morphological changes of the nuclei and detect apoptosis. Five fields of view were randomly selected and apoptotic cells were counted under a fluorescence microscope (TS100, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

RNA extraction, reverse transcription and qRT-PCR

Cells were cultured in 6-well plates for 24 h. Total RNA was extracted from the cells according to the manufacturer’s protocol (RC112, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). reverse transcriptase kit (R323, Vazyme, Nanjing, China) reverse transcribed 1 µg of total RNA. qRT-PCR was performed according to the kit (Q712, Vazyme Nanjing, China) using SYBR Green, on a real-time quantitative PCR instrument (BIO-RAD, iQ5, USA), and the data were analysed using the 2-ΔΔCt method. GJA3 gene forward primer 5’-ATGGGCGACTGGGAGCTTTC-3’ and reverse primer 5’- TGCTGGGTGTTGCAGGTGA-3’. β-Actin gene forward primer 5’-TCGTGCGTGACATTAAGGAG-3’ and reverse primer 5’-ATGCCAGGGTACATGGTGGGT-3’.

Molecular docking analysis

The structural file for EGCG was obtained from the PubChem website, and the receptor protein GSK-3β was retrieved from the PDB database. In the present study, molecular docking between EGCG (ID: 65064) and GSK-3β (ID: 1I09) was performed using AutoDock Vina. Taking the active pocket of the GSK-3β protein as the center, the docking grid coordinates were defined as follows: center X = 20.42, center Y = 15.86, center Z = − 10.83. The grid box dimensions were set to size X = 22.5 Å, size Y = 22.5 Å, and size Z = 22.5 Å. For reference, a grid size of 20–28 Å is commonly used for a single small‑molecule active pocket, while 30–40 Å is applied for broad‑spectrum pockets or macromolecular ligands. The docking exhaustiveness was set to 32 (conventional range: 16–32). The number of output optimal conformations (num modes) was set to 20 (normal standard range: 9–20). The energy range threshold was defined as 3 kcal/mol (fixed standard range: 2–3 kcal/mol). Molecular docking between the receptor protein and the ligand molecule was performed using AutoDock Vina 1.1.2, and the docking results were analyzed with LigPlot. The docking interactions were visualized using PyMOL.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA) was applied for statistical analyses, and Image J software was used for quantitative protein expression analysis. If the distribution of quantitative variables obeyed normal distribution, they were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and differences between two groups were tested by independent samples t-test, and multi-sample means were compared by one-way ANOVA, which was statistically significant when P < 0.05.

Result

DNMT3B is highly expressed in the anterior capsule cells of the lens in ARC patients, aged mice, and in the apoptosis of HLE-B3 cells

Immunohistochemical staining revealed that DNMT3B expression was significantly elevated in the anterior lens capsule cells of ARC patients and aged mice compared to the normal group and young mice, respectively (Fig. 1a, b). Western blot analysis further confirmed increased DNMT3B protein expression in aged mice, p = 0.006, Hedges’ g = 1.626 (Fig. 1c). Our previous studies have demonstrated that treatment with 200µM H2O2-induces apoptosis in HLE-B3 cells [36]. In this study, western blot results confirmed that DNMT3B protein levels are elevated during H2O2-induced apoptosis in LECs, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 2.196 (Fig. 1d).

Overexpression and interference experiments demonstrated that DNMT3B played a key role in regulating LECs apoptosis

To further investigate the regulatory role of DNMT3B in oxidative damage-induced apoptosis of LECs, we constructed the pcDNA3.1-DNMT3B plasmid and transfected it into HLE-B3 cells. After overexpression of DNMT3B, Western blot results showed a significant increase in DNMT3B protein levels, as shown in Fig. 2a, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 4.726. The extremely large effect size observed here may be attributed to the overexpression manipulation, whereby overexpression drives the abundance of this target protein from an initially extremely low endogenous baseline level to a markedly higher expression level. CCK8 assay results indicated that overexpression of DNMT3B inhibited the viability of LECs, p = 0.014, Hedges’ g = 2.691. It indicates that the cell activity is significantly inhibited(Fig. 2b). Hoechst 333,42 staining results suggested that overexpression of DNMT3B led to apoptosis of LECs, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 5.197 (Fig. 2c, d). At the same time, we also constructed the pSilencer3.1-DNMT3B plasmid and transfected it into HLE-B3 cells. After DNMT3B interference, Western blot results showed a significant decrease in DNMT3B protein levels, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 4.581. Demonstrating that the DNMT3B-targeting shRNA plasmid exhibited reliable and specific knockdown efficiency, validating its applicability for follow-up functional experiments in this study. (Fig. 2e). CCK8 and Hoechst 333,42 staining results showed that, compared to the group treated with H2O2 alone, the group with DNMT3B interference followed by H2O2 treatment had increased cell viability, p < 0.001, η² = 0.959 (Fig. 2f) and a reduced number of apoptotic HLE-B3 cells, p < 0.001, η² = 0.974 (Figs. 2g, h).

DNMT3B mediates GJA3 expression

To investigate whether DNMT3B can modulate the expression levels of GJA3, we measured the mRNA and protein expression level of GJA3 using pcDNA3.1-DNMT3B and pSilencer3.1-DNMT3B plasmids. Overexpression of DNMT3B in HLE-B3 cells resulted in a significant downregulation of both mRNA and protein expression levels of GJA3, as demonstrated by qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses (Fig. 3a, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 3.664; Fig. 3b, p < 0.001, Hedges’ g = 2.315). When pSilencer3.1-DNMT3B was combined with the H2O2 group, a significant upregulation of both mRNA and protein expression levels of GJA3 was observed compared to the group treated with H2O2 alone (Fig. 3c, p < 0.001, η² = 0.924; Fig. 3d, p < 0.001, η² = 0.995).

The expression of DNMT3B is regulated by GSK-3β

The above results had confirmed that p-GSK-3β expression is downregulated in H2O2-treated apoptosis LECs (Fig. 5f). When serine 9 of GSK-3β is phosphorylated, its activity is reduced [22]. Therefore, we used its inhibitor SB 216763 to confirm the regulatory mechanism between GSK-3β and DNMT3B. Compared to the group with H2O2-induced apoptosis alone, the protein expression of DNMT3B was inhibited in the group with the inhibitor SB 216763 (Fig. 6a, p < 0.001, η² = 0.996), and the cell viability inhibited by H2O2 was improved (Fig. 6b, p < 0.001, η² = 0.973). At the same time, Hoechst 333,42 staining results showed that the number of apoptotic cells in the inhibitor SB 216763 group was significantly reduced compared to the group with H2O2-induced apoptosis alone, p < 0.001, η² = 0.955 (Fig. 6c, d).

EGCG targeted GSK-3β/DNMT3B to inhibit the apoptosis of LECs

The molecular docking study shows that the calculated binding free energy between EGCG and GSK-3β is -8.6 kcal/mol, indicating a potential strong binding affinity that is consistent with the energy requirements for possible drug-target interactions. Various potential non-covalent interactions may be formed between EGCG and the active site of GSK-3β based on the docking results. In the optimal conformation predicted by docking, EGCG is deeply embedded in the simulated active site of GSK-3β, with its hydrophobic region nestled within the hypothetical hydrophobic pocket of GSK-3β and its polar region oriented toward the surface. The hydroxyl groups of EGCG are predicted to establish hydrogen bonds with residues 64Asn, 85Lys, 200Asp, 135Val, 62Ile, 141Arg, and 138Thr on GSK-3β in the docking model. If hydrogen bond strength is defined by the number of hydrogen bonds between donor and acceptor, the predicted interactions from 141Arg and 64Asn are particularly strong in the simulated model. Meanwhile, if bond strength is defined by bond length, 85Lys and 138Thr are predicted to exhibit stronger interactions in the docking conformation (Fig. 7a–c). Previous studies showed that mutations or modifications of sites such as Asn64 and Lys85 significantly reduced GSK3-β activity [37–39]. Therefore, we hypothesize that the direct interaction of EGCG with amino acid residues 64ASN and 85LYS may lead to decreased GSK3-β activity. In addition, we examined the expression of the downstream response gene DMNT3B as a measure of GSK3-β activity. EGCG has antioxidant damage effects and can inhibit H2O2-induced oxidative damage [35]. However, whether EGCG can target DNMT3B to reduce LECs apoptosis was explored preliminarily. Western blot results showed that the protein DNMT3B expression level in the EGCG intervention group was significantly lower than that in the only H2O2-treated group (Fig. 7d, p < 0.001, η² = 0.995.). To further elucidate the role of EGCG, we transfected pcDNA3.1-DNMT3B plasmid. The CCK8 experiment showed that EGCG can mitigate the inhibitory effects of H2O2 on cell viability; however, this effect is significantly diminished when DNMT3B is overexpressed. (Fig. 7e, p < 0.001, η² = 0.926.). Meanwhile, Hoechst 333,42 staining results show that EGCG can delay H2O2-induced apoptosis in LECs. However, when DNMT3B is overexpressed, the protective effect of EGCG is reduced, leading to increased cell apoptosis, p < 0.001, η² = 0.944. (Fig. 7f, g).

Discussion

ARC is a degenerative disease of the lens. China has the largest number of cataract patients and cataract blind people in the world. Currently, there are many drugs on the market for the treatment of ARC, but there is no effective drug that can restore normal lens metabolism and promote the absorption of lens turbidity. Surgery is still the main treatment method. At the same time, postoperative complications and surgical costs have brought a heavy burden to patients and the social economy [40, 41]. Therefore, many researchers have explored the pathogenesis of ARC at the molecular level, but the exact cause has not been fully elucidated. Oxidative stress damage-induced lens cell protein modification and aggregation, DNA damage and cell apoptosis, etc., leading to cell dysfunction, are considered to be most closely related to the occurrence of cataracts [42, 43]. Studies have shown that H2O2-induced ROS accumulation in LECs promotes cell apoptosis, leading to the occurrence of ARC [44]. However, whether signal transduction pathways are involved in this process, and how the specific regulatory mechanisms work, will be the focus of our investigation.

Palsamy et al. found that DNMT3B can lead to DNA demethylation in the promoter region of Keap1, a negative regulator of Nrf2. Thereby disrupting Nrf2-dependent antioxidant protection, leading to cataract formation [45]. However, they did not confirm the correlation between DNMT3B and cataract development in animal and clinical samples. We not only confirmed the correlation between abnormal expression of DNMT3B and cataract development through clinical samples and animal models (Fig. 1a-c), but also found that the protein expression level of DNMT3B was increased in H2O2-induced LECs apoptosis (Fig. 1d). Subsequently, we constructed DNMT3B overexpression and interference plasmids. The experimental results showed that after DNMT3B overexpression, its protein expression level was significantly increased, cell activity was reduced, and the number of apoptotic cells was significantly increased (Fig. 2a-d). After DNMT3B interference, its protein expression level was significantly reduced, cell activity was increased, and the number of apoptotic cells was significantly reduced compared with the H2O2 group alone (Fig. 3e-h). These experimental results confirm that DNMT3B regulates cataract development by regulating lens epithelial cell apoptosis. This study reveals in detail the important role of DNMT3B in the development of ARC.

Banerjee et al. confirmed that GJA3 protects human lens epithelial cells from hypoxia-induced apoptosis [46]. In this study, we detected a decrease in GJA3 protein expression levels in the anterior capsule cells of ARC patients and aged mice (Fig. 4a-c). Cell experiments showed that GJA3 protein expression was downregulated in LECs undergoing H2O2-induced apoptosis (Fig. 4d). We detected the mRNA and protein expression levels of GJA3 through overexpression and interference experiments of DMMT3B, and the results confirmed that DNMT3B can affect the gene expression level of GJA3, and there is an upstream and downstream regulatory relationship between the two (Fig. 3a-d). This study primarily investigates whether DNMT3B can regulate GJA3-induced apoptosis in lens epithelial cells at the protein and mRNA levels. Previous studies have screened for aberrantly methylated genes associated with age-related cataract using methylation microarrays and conducted subsequent validation, which revealed that the aberrant methylation and expression of GJA3 are correlated with this disease [19, 47]. Using the MethPrimer software (available at: http://www.urogene.org/methprimer/index1.html), we identified a large methylated CpG island within the promoter region of the GJA3 gene (Supplementary Material 2 Fig. 1.). However, whether DNMT3B, as a DNA methyltransferase, can regulate GJA3 expression by modulating the methylation status of this region remains a hypothesis, which we will further verify in follow-up studies.

We found that the total protein expression level of GSK-3β remained unchanged in the anterior capsule cells of ARC patients, aged mice, and H2O2-induced LECs, while the protein expression level of p-GSK-3β (Ser9) was decreased (Fig. 5a-f). Some studies have reported that treatment of HLE-B3 cells with SB 216763 significantly inhibits GSK-3β activity [48]. Therefore, we used SB 216763 to detect LECs apoptosis. The results showed that after adding the inhibitor SB 216763 compared with the H2O2 group alone, cell activity increased, and the number of apoptotic cells significantly decreased (Fig. 6b-d), accompanied by a decrease in DNMT3B expression. Yang et al. found that adding 2i would lead to downregulation of DNMT3B in mouse embryonic stem cells and a significant decrease in DNA methylation [25]. Our results confirmed that DNMT3B was downregulated by GSK-3β in LECs (Fig. 6a), and the results are consistent with previous study in another cells [25]. This study has clearly confirmed that inhibiting GSK-3β activity can significantly downregulate DNMT3B protein expression, thereby alleviating oxidative stress-induced apoptosis of LECs and the progression of ARC. The specific molecular mechanisms by which GSK-3β activity mediates the protein expressionf DNMT3B have not been thoroughly explored. Previous studies have verified that GSK-3β can promote the degradation of IκBα, the inhibitory subunit of NF-κB, thereby enhancing the nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of the NF-κB p65 subunit. When GSK-3β is inhibited, the nuclear accumulation of NF-κB is markedly reduced, and the transcriptional activity of its downstream target genes is correspondingly decreased [49]. As a core transcription factor, NF-κB can directly bind to the promoter region of the DNMT3B gene and upregulate its transcriptional expression. During inflammatory responses and tumorigenesis, activation of NF-κB significantly induces DNMT3B expression, which further silences tumor suppressor genes or negative regulatory genes of inflammation through DNA methylation [50]. Thus, we propose that GSK-3β transcriptionally regulates DNMT3B gene expression through the modulation of NF-κB.We will experimentally validate this hypothesis in future studies.

EGCG has antioxidant effects and participates in changes in cell cycle, development, and apoptosis by regulating multiple signaling pathways [51]. In addition to its protective effects in the cornea and retina, the mechanism of action of EGCG in the lens is being continuously explored [31, 52]. Wu et al. found that EGCG can inhibit UVB-induced apoptosis of HLECs through the AIF/Endo G signaling pathway [53]. Previously, our research group has confirmed that EGCG can effectively delay H2O2-induced apoptosis of LECs [41]. In addition, EGCG could phosphorylate GSK-3β (Ser9) in H9c2 cells, resulting in decreasing activity of GSK-3β [54]. This is consistent with our hypothesis that EGCG can decrease GSK-3β activity, but this article does not confirm whether EGCG binds directly to GSK-3β (Ser9). We performed molecular docking to preliminarily predict that EGCG forms hydrogen bonds with the residues 64Asn, 85Lys, 200Asp, 135Val, 62Ile, 141Arg and 138Thr on GSK‑3β, which may lead to a reduction in GSK‑3β activity (Fig. 7a–c). Subsequently, we will further confirm the direct interaction between EGCG and GSK-3β by experiments in future.

Numerous studies have shown that EGCG inhibits DNA methyltransferases and thereby regulates gene expression in various cancers, but there are no reports in ophthalmic diseases [55]. Notably, DNMT3B gene in this study is a DNA methyltransferase, and we found that EGCG can downregulate DNMT3B to alleviate oxidative damage-induced apoptosis of LECs (Fig. 7d). Furthermore, by transfecting overexpression plasmids, we further confirmed that DNMT3B overexpression can effectively inhibit the apoptosis-relieving effect of EGCG on LECs (Fig. 7e-g). This study demonstrated that EGCG could inhibit apoptosis by suppressing the activity of GSK-3β and regulating the expression of DNA methyltransferase DNMT3B, which also has certain implications for the regulation of DNA methylation status in cataract and provides a basis for the development of cataract drugs at the epigenetic level. Nevertheless, as a natural polyphenol compound, EGCG was predicted to specifically bind to the active center of GSK-3β via molecular docking analysis in the present study. However, we cannot completely rule out the potential off-target effects of EGCG, which are elaborated as follows. First, in addition to the GSK-3β/DNMT3B/GJA3 axis identified in this study, EGCG may exert its biological functions by regulating other signaling pathways, such as the PI3K/AKT and Nrf2/ARE pathways. These pathways may exert synergistic or antagonistic effects on the core pathway investigated in the present study. These potential off-target effects have not been thoroughly validated in this study, which constitutes one of the limitations of our work. Second, previous in vivo studies have confirmed that EGCG can effectively alleviate lens opacity in aged mice [35]. However, it remains to be clarified whether EGCG alleviates cataract formation mainly by targeting the GSK-3β/DNMT3B pathway. In the present study, we only verified the regulatory effect of EGCG on the GSK-3β/DNMT3B axis at the cellular level, and no relevant in vivo validation experiments were performed. Our future research will focus on the following verification: the regulatory effect of EGCG on the GSK-3β/DNMT3B pathway will be further investigated in a naturally aging mouse model. We plan to construct GSK-3β wild-type transgenic mice, eye-specific GSK-3β-S9A transgenic mice, and DNMT3B transgenic mice. Further experiments will be conducted to verify the specific role of this signaling axis in the pathogenesis of ARC, so as to provide more rigorous experimental evidence for the clinical translation of EGCG.

Conclusion

As shown in Fig. 7h, DNMT3B is highly expressed in the anterior capsule cells of the lens in ARC patients, aged mice, and in the apoptosis of HLE-B3 cells. Down-regualtion of p-GSK-3β and GJA3 are also associated with age-related cataract. Furthermore, DNMT3B regulates GJA3 expression, which leads to lens epithelial cells apoptosis. Meanwhile the upstream regulatory mechanism of DNMT3B is also studied, The results showed that the expression of DNMT3B is regulated by GSK-3β. Furthermore, we predicted the interaction between EGCG and GSK-3β, and that inhibition of GSK-3β activity can significantly downregulate the protein expression of DNMT3B, thereby attenuating oxidative stress-induced apoptosis of LECs and the progression of ARC. In a word, the GSK-3β/DNMT3B/GJA3 pathway plays a major role in ARC pathogenesis and LECs apoptosis. EGCG can target GSK-3β/DNMT3B pathway to reduce apoptosis in lens epithelial cells. As a drug with both medicinal and food properties, EGCG also provides a new research direction for the early prevention and treatment of ARC, laying a solid theoretical foundation for the clinical translation of cataract drugs in the future. This has important theoretical and practical significance for maximizing the protection of patients’ visual function, improving their quality of life, and reducing the burden on patients and society.
