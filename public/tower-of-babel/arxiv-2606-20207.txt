Solutions of the 3D inhomogeneous incompressible Navier-Stokes system with initial velocity in VMO − 1 \rm VMO^{-1}
Ruilin Hu, Quoc-Hung Nguyen, Feng Shao, Dongyi Wei, Ping Zhang, Zhifei Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish local existence of strong solutions for the three-dimensional inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with initial data ( ρ 0 , u 0 ) (\rho_{0},u_{0}) lying in ( C 1 , L 2 ∩ VMO − 1 ) (C^{1},L^{2}\cap\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}) , where ρ 0 \rho_{0} has a positive lower bound. Furthermore, if ρ 0 ∈ C 2 \rho_{0}\in C^{2} and ‖ ρ 0 − 1 ‖ L ∞ + ‖ u 0 ‖ BMO − 1 \|\rho_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}} is sufficiently small, we prove global existence of the solution. To achieve this, we employ an estimate for the transport equation to obtain regularity for the density and apply a new freezing coefficient method for the momentum equation, as in [ 12 ] .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the existence of solutions to the initial value problem for the following three-dimensional inhomogeneous incompressible Navier–Stokes system:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)-\Delta u+\nabla P=0,\\[4.0pt]
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\[4.0pt]
(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0}).\end{cases}

Here
(\rho,u,P)
denote the density, velocity, and pressure, respectively. This system can be used to describe a mixture of several immiscible incompressible fluids with varying densities; we refer to
[
31
]
for a detailed derivation of (
1.1
).

A simplified case is
\rho\equiv 1
, which reduces the system to the classical Navier–Stokes equations. One observes that, just as in the classical case, system (
1.1
) also possesses a scaling invariance. More precisely, if
(\rho,u,P)
is a solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
, then

(1.2)

(\rho(t,x),u(t,x),P(t,x))\mapsto\bigl(\rho(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\lambda u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\lambda^{2}P(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\bigr)

is also a solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T/\lambda^{2}]
. A Banach space
X
is called
critical
if its norm is invariant under the scaling transformation (
1.2
). Some classical critical spaces in three dimensions include the Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, the Lebesgue space
L^{3}
, the Besov space
\dot{B}_{p,q}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
, and the spaces
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
and
\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}
, which will be recalled later.

Let us first survey some known results for the classical Navier–Stokes equations
(NS)
. The research dates back to
[
30
]
, where Leray established the existence of global weak solutions for
L^{2}
data. In the critical setting, Fujita and Kato
[
23
]
proved local well-posedness in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and Kato
[
27
]
proved local well-posedness for
L^{3}
data. For data with negative regularity, the authors
[
8
]
established the global well-posedness of
(NS)
with small initial data in the non-endpoint case
\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
. Although the endpoint space
\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{-1}
is ill-posed
[
7
]
, Koch and Tataru
[
29
]
proved the global well-posedness of
(NS)
with small initial data in the subspace
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
.

It is natural to expect that if
\rho_{0}
is close to a constant, then some of these well-posedness results should also hold for the inhomogeneous system. Danchin
[
16
]
proved that if
\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}_{2,1}^{-1+\frac{d}{2}}}
is sufficiently small and
\rho_{0}
is close to a constant in the sense of
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{d}{2}}
, then (
1.1
) admits a unique global solution. The existence result was extended to
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}
and
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{\frac{d}{p}}
for
p\in(1,2d)
, and the uniqueness result for
p\in(1,d]
in
[
1
]
. Corresponding results were extended to the half-space case in
[
17
]
. The gap for the uniqueness with
p\in(d,2d)
was solved by Danchin and Mucha
[
18
]

in a more general functional framework.
The restriction on the initial density can in fact be relaxed and need not be confined to a small neighborhood of a constant. The study of such problems originated with Kazhikhov
[
28
]
, who in 1974 established global existence of strong solutions for densities bounded away from zero and initial velocity
u_{0}\in H^{1}
. Subsequently,
[
2
]
proved local and global well-posedness for data
(u_{0},\rho_{0})
with
u_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\rho_{0}\in\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{3}{2}}
possessing a positive lower bound. This result was later extended to the
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}
framework in
[
3
]
; see also
[
4
,
33
]
for further developments. In
[
32
]
, Paicu, Zhang, and Zhang obtained global existence in two dimensions for initial density in
L^{\infty}
with a positive lower bound and initial velocity in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
s>0
. In three dimensions, they proved global existence for
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
velocity under the assumption that
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
is sufficiently small. The three-dimensional result was later improved to
H^{s}
velocity for
s>\frac{1}{2}
in
[
14
]
. The first result allowing the initial density
\rho_{0}
to be merely bounded (and bounded away from zero) while the velocity lies in a critical space is due to Zhang
[
35
]
, who established global existence for small initial velocity in
\dot{B}_{2,1}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Uniqueness for this class of solutions was subsequently proved in
[
20
]
, where the authors also generalized the existence result to initial velocities in the larger critical spaces
\dot{B}_{p,1}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}
. An interesting related direction shows that smallness of two velocity components can also suffice for global existence; see
[
10
,
21
,
26
,
35
]
for details.

All the aforementioned results require the density to be bounded away from zero, as the presence of vacuum introduces substantial additional difficulties. A significant advance was made by Simon
[
34
]
in 1990, who improved the results of
[
28
]
by removing the requirement of a positive lower bound for the density. Lions
[
31
]
further generalized these results to the case of density-dependent viscosity (formulated via a renormalized continuity equation), establishing the existence of global weak solutions for initial density in
L^{\infty}
and initial velocity in
L^{2}
. Subsequently, Danchin and Mucha
[
19
]
proved global existence for
L^{\infty}
densities and velocities in
H^{1}(\Omega)
on a bounded domain
\Omega
. More recently, Hao et al.
[
24
]
obtained a global existence result in two dimensions for
L^{\infty}
densities and velocities in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
; in three dimensions, they required the initial velocity to be small in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This result was soon extended in
[
25
]
, where global existence was established under the smallness condition
\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mathbb{P}(\rho_{0}u_{0})\|_{\dot{B}_{p,\infty}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}}\ll 1
for
3<p<\infty
.

1.1.
Functional setting and main results

We recall the
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
setting as in
[
29
]
. For
b>0
, we define the localized
\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}
norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_{b}}:=\sup_{t\leq b^{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{t\Delta}u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,0<R<b}R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{B(x,R)}|e^{t\Delta}u(y)|^{2}\,dy\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2}.

For the case
b=\infty
, the norm reduces to the
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
norm, and we denote the corresponding norm simply by
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
. The space
\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}
is defined as

\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}:=\bigl\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:\ \ \lim_{b\to 0}\|f\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_{b}}=0\bigr\}.

For simplicity, we introduce the following norm for a function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\|u\|_{V_{T}^{b}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,0<R<b}R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B(x,R)}|u(t,y)|^{2}\,dy\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2},

which satisfies
\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{V_{\infty}^{b}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_{b}}
.

To capture both critical and subcritical behaviors, we introduce the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{T}^{1}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{V_{T}^{b}}+\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{s<t\leq T}\Bigl(s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\Bigr),

where
\varkappa_{1}=\frac{18}{35}>\frac{1}{2}
and
\kappa=\frac{1}{700}
, and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\leq T}\Bigl(t^{\frac{5}{12}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{12}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\Bigr),

with
\varkappa_{2}=\frac{5}{7}>\varkappa_{1}
. The values of
\kappa
,
\varkappa_{1}
, and
\varkappa_{2}
remain fixed throughout the paper. It is easy to verify that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}^{1}}
is critical under the scaling (
1.2
) when
b=\infty
, while
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}^{2}}
is a subcritical norm. We define the following mixed norm

(1.3)

\|u\|_{X_{T}}:=\|u\|_{X_{T}^{1}}+\|u\|_{X_{T}^{2}}

for some
T\leq 1
. We will use the subcritical norm
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}^{2}}
to estimate the remainder term
u_{R}
in (
1.10
) below, for which we may gain nearly one additional derivative. Further details will be explained in the next subsection.

By the properties of the transport equation, if the Lipschitz norm of the convection velocity field is integrable in time, then the regularity of the solution is inherited from the initial data (see
[
5
]
). However, in our case, the
L^{\infty}
norm of the gradient to the velocity behaves like
t^{-1}
, which is not integrable, leading to a loss of space regularity in the density. In what follows, we will use the auxiliary norm

(1.4)

\|u\|_{T}:=\sup_{t\leq T}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+t\|u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1}}\right)

to control the flow map associated with
u
.

For the lower-order remainder terms that appear in the next subsection, we define another mixed norm without the
\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}
structure, namely

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y_{T}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{t\leq T}\Big(t^{\frac{2}{5}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+t^{\frac{5}{12}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{12}}+t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sup_{s<t\leq T}\Bigl(s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\Bigr)+\sup_{s<t\leq T}\Bigl(s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\Bigr).

Since
Y_{T}
contains subcritical terms, it follows readily from the definition of the
\|\cdot\|_{V_{T}^{b}}
norm that for any
T<1
,

\|u\|_{X_{T}}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y_{T}}.

Our first main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\rho_{0}\in C^{1}
satisfy

(1.5)

0<C_{1}<\rho_{0}<C_{2}<\infty

for some positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}\cap\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}
. There exist
b\in(0,1)
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that if

\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}_{b}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

then system (
1.1
) admits a solution
(\rho,u,P)
on
[0,T(\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}},u_{0},\varepsilon_{0},b)]
satisfying

(1.6)

\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq C\epsilon_{0}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}},

and

(1.7)

\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\bigl\|\bigl(u(t),t^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla u(t),tu_{t}(t)\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{2}}+\bigl\|\bigl(\nabla u,t^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{t},t\nabla u_{t}\bigr)\bigr\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\right)\leq C(\varepsilon_{0},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}).

Remark 1.1
.

Moreover, we note that
T
can be chosen such that
C_{1}^{\prime}b^{2}\leq T\leq C_{2}^{\prime}b^{2}
for some universal constants
C_{1}^{\prime}
and
C_{2}^{\prime}
(in the sequel, we write
T\sim b^{2}
). This scaling is consistent with the parabolic nature of the equations.

Remark 1.2
.

At present, establishing the uniqueness of solutions under the assumptions that
\rho_{0}\in C^{1}
has a positive lower bound,
u_{0}\in L^{2}\cap\mathrm{VMO}^{-1}
, and the quantity
\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}}
is sufficiently small remains a significant open problem. As noted in
[
25
, Remark 1.2]
, the methodology employed in that paper cannot be adapted to prove weak-strong uniqueness in the
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
setting. The principal difficulty arises from the lack of an effective decomposition
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
for a function
f\in L^{2}\cap\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
, where
f_{1}
belongs to a suitable subcritical space. It is worth mentioning that weak-strong uniqueness can be established under the stronger assumption
u_{0}\in L^{p}\cap\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
for some
p>2
. In this case, one can approximate
u_{0}
by a sequence
\{u_{0,N}\}\subset L^{q}
with
q>3
such that
\|u_{0}-u_{0,N}\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
, allowing the application of the method from
[
25
]
. This argument, however, fails for the critical case
p=2
.

For the classical Navier–Stokes equations, uniqueness for small initial data in
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
was proved by Koch and Tataru
[
29
]
. Their approach, which relies on a fixed-point argument that simultaneously yields global existence and uniqueness, can not be extended to our inhomogeneous setting, as our existence proof does not imply uniqueness. To the best of our knowledge, even in the classical Navier–Stokes setting, weak-strong uniqueness for
u_{0}\in L^{2}\cap\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
(without a smallness condition) remains a major open problem. For recent progress, we refer to
[
6
,
9
,
22
]
. Notably, Coiculescu and Palasek
[
15
]
have very recently proved the non-uniqueness of solutions to the classical Navier–Stokes equations for large initial data in
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
.

By assuming that the initial density is nearly constant and the initial velocity is sufficiently small in
\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}
, we obtain the following global existence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M>0
. Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and
\rho_{0}\in C^{2}
satisfy
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{2}}\leq M
. There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1/2)
, depending only on
M
, such that if

(1.8)

\|\rho_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{-1}}<\varepsilon_{2},

then (
1.1
) admits a global strong solution.

Remark 1.3
.

Here in Theorem
1.2
, we require
\rho_{0}\in C^{2}
to have higher regularity. This is because in the a priori estimate of Theorem
1.1
, the maximal existence time
T
depends on
\varepsilon_{0}
even if
\varepsilon_{0}
is small. More explanation can be seen in Section
6
and Remark
6.1
.

Remark 1.4
.

A notable aspect of Theorem
1.2
is that the required smallness of the initial data is measured in terms of the norms
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{2}}
. Whether an “unconditional” global existence result can be established remains an open problem.

1.2.
Ideas and outline

We explain the main ideas of the proof and the motivation behind the choice of the norm
\|u\|_{X_{T}}
.

We apply the freezing coefficient method developed in
[
12
]
. For any fixed
x_{0}
, applying the Leray projection operator
\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Id}-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\nabla\cdot
to the momentum equation in (
1.1
) and freezing the coefficient at
x_{0}
, we obtain

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\rho+u\cdot\nabla\rho=0,\\[4.0pt]
\rho_{0}(x_{0})\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=-\mathbb{P}\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)-\mathbb{P}\partial_{t}\bigl[(\rho-\rho_{0})u\bigr]\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\nabla\Delta^{-1}\bigl(\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot\partial_{t}u\bigr)-(\rho_{0}-\rho_{0}(x_{0}))u_{t},\\[4.0pt]
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\[4.0pt]
(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\end{array}\right.

where
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(y)
denotes the initial density, and we have used the notation
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(y)
in the convolution kernels below.

Let
\varrho:=\rho-\rho_{0}
and let
K_{x_{0}}(t,x)
be the fundamental solution to

(1.9)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{0}(x_{0})\partial_{t}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)-\Delta K_{x_{0}}(t,x)=0,\\[4.0pt]
K_{x_{0}}(t,x)|_{t=0}=\delta.\end{array}\right.

With this definition, we can write

(1.10)

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=K_{x_{0}}(t)\ast u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau)\ast\mathbb{P}\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau)\ast\nabla\Delta^{-1}\partial_{\tau}(\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot u)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau)\ast\mathbb{P}\partial_{\tau}(\varrho u)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)(\rho_{0}(y)-\rho_{0}(x_{0}))\partial_{\tau}u(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=:u_{L,x_{0}}+u_{N,x_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}u_{Ri,x_{0}},

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Since (
1.10
) holds for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it holds in particular for
x_{0}=x
. If we define
\tilde{u}_{\star}(t,x)=u_{\star,x_{0}}(t,x)|_{x_{0}=x}
for
\star\in\{L,N,R1,R2,R3\}
, then

(1.11)

u(t,x)=\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}\tilde{u}_{\star}(t,x).

With a slight abuse of notation, we will use
u_{R}
and
\tilde{u}_{R}
to denote any of the components
u_{R1,x_{0}},u_{R2,x_{0}},u_{R3,x_{0}},\tilde{u}_{R1},\tilde{u}_{R2},\tilde{u}_{R3}
in the sequel. From (
1.10
), we observe that
u_{L,x_{0}}
and
u_{N,x_{0}}
are the critical terms, while the remainder terms
u_{R}
enjoy supercritical regularity due to the extra smoothing from
\rho_{0}
and
\varrho
.

Here and throughout, we denote
\delta_{\alpha}f(t,x):=f(t,x+\alpha)-f(t,x)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\delta_{a}^{t}f(t,x):=f(t+a,x)-f(t,x)
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. A key observation is that

(\delta_{\alpha}\nabla u_{R3})|_{x_{0}=x}\neq\delta_{\alpha}\nabla(u_{R3}|_{x_{0}=x}).

From (
1.10
), we obtain the following estimates:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}\|\tilde{u}_{\star}(t)\|_{L^{p}},\quad\forall p\in[1,\infty],

\displaystyle\|\nabla|D|^{\kappa}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}\|(\nabla|D|^{\kappa}u_{\star,x_{0}})|_{x_{0}=x}\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\frac{\|u(t)-u(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}\lesssim\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{\star}(t)-\tilde{u}_{\star}(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}.

Here and below, we always denote
|D|^{\alpha}
to be Fourier multiplier with symbol
|\xi|^{\alpha}.

For the
V_{T}^{b}
norm, note that for any fixed
z
, (
1.10
) yields

|u(t,x)|\lesssim\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}|u_{\star,z}(t,x)|,

which implies

\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B(z,R)}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,d\tau\lesssim\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2,R3}\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B(z,R)}|u_{\star,z}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,d\tau

for any
R<b
. Consequently,

\|u\|_{V_{T}^{b}}\lesssim\sup_{z}\sum_{\star=L,N,R1,R2}\|u_{\star,z}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sup_{R<b}R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B(z,R)}|u_{R3,z}(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The estimates for the density
\rho
established in Section
2
allow us to complete the proof, provided that we control the norm
\|u\|_{T}
, and in particular the
C^{1}
component. According to the decomposition (
1.10
), the solution
u
comprises three parts: the linear term from the initial data
u_{L,x_{0}}
, the bilinear term
u_{N,x_{0}}
, and the remainder
u_{R,x_{0}}
. The terms
u_{L,x_{0}}
and
u_{N,x_{0}}
constitute the critical components of the analysis. To establish the required a priori bounds for
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|u\|_{\dot{C}^{1+\kappa}}
, we employ the strategy introduced by Koch and Tataru. This analysis is carried out in Appendix
7.2
.

The term
u_{R2}
can be expressed as

u_{R2}=\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\mathbb{P}(\varrho u)(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau+\mathbb{P}(\varrho u)(t,x).

Although the first integral can be estimated by making a difference, the second term—despite its favorable temporal decay—poses difficulties due to its limited spatial regularity. To circumvent this issue, we adapt the method from
[
11
]
, specifically Lemma
4.1
, by introducing time-weighted Hölder norms in
X_{T}^{1}
and
X_{T}^{2}
. This allows us to compensate for the spatial regularity loss from
\rho
by fully exploiting the available time regularity.

A second challenge lies in estimating the time-weighted Hölder norm of

u_{R1}=\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\nabla\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot u)(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau+\nabla\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot u)(t,x),

particularly the latter term. To address this, we employ energy estimates (developed in Appendix
7.1
) to obtain

\sup_{t\leq T}t\|u_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}<\infty,

which, via interpolation, supplies sufficient regularity for the desired bound.

The inclusion of the
\|u(t)\|_{L^{12}}
norm in our functional setting is motivated by the fact that the projection
\mathbb{P}
does not preserve
L^{\infty}
but is bounded on
L^{p}
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
. All newly introduced norms for
u_{N,x_{0}}
and
u_{L,x_{0}}
can be controlled using the energy inequality in Lemmas
7.1
-
7.3
together with standard interpolation.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
establishes estimates for the transport equation governing the density. Sections
3
and
4
provide the analysis for the critical terms and the remainder terms, respectively. Section
5
synthesizes these a priori estimates and establishes the local existence of a solution, namely, Theorem
1.1
. Section
6
extends the result to the global case, namely, Theorem
1.2
. Finally, Appendix
7
presents several weighted
L^{2}
energy estimates and proves a bilinear estimate of Koch–Tataru type.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we derive a priori estimates for the density
\rho
, which satisfies the following transport equation:

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)+(u\cdot\nabla\rho)(t,x)=0,\\[4.0pt]
\rho|_{t=0}(x)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is assumed to be divergence-free and Lipschitz for any
t>0
. Below we always assume that

(2.2)

\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{T}\leq\varepsilon_{1},

where
\|\cdot\|_{T}
is defined in (
1.4
), and
\varepsilon_{1}
is a small constant to be determined later.

We first establish the spatial Hölder regularity of
\rho
for small times.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\rho
be a smooth enough solution of (
2.1
). Then, under condition (
2.2
), for any
T\leq 1
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
depending only on
\gamma
such that if
\|u\|_{T}\leq\varepsilon_{1}
, one has

(2.3)

\sup_{t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}},

where
C>1
is a constant depending only on
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
X(t,x)
be the trajectory map associated with
u
, defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X(t,x)=u(t,X(t,x)),\quad X(0,x)=x.

Then the transport equation implies that

(2.5)

\rho(t,X(t,x))=\rho_{0}(x),\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad\rho(t,x)=\rho_{0}(X_{t}^{-1}(x)),

where
X_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is defined by
X_{t}(x)=X(t,x)
.

Note that

-\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}|X(t,x)-X(t,y)|\leq\frac{d}{dt}|X(t,x)-X(t,y)|\leq\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}|X(t,x)-X(t,y)|.

By Grönwall’s lemma, we obtain

(2.6)

|X(s,x)-X(s,y)|\leq\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau\right)|X(t,x)-X(t,y)|,\quad\forall s<t.

Moreover, from the definition (
1.4
) of
\|u\|_{T}
, we have

\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau\leq C\|u\|_{T}\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right),

which together with (
2.6
) yields

(2.7)

|X(s,x)-X(s,y)|\leq\exp\Bigl(C\|u\|_{T}\log\bigl(\frac{t}{s}\bigr)\Bigr)|X(t,x)-X(t,y)|,\quad\forall s<t.

Now we use this inequality to derive the Hölder estimate of
\rho
. Let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then there exist
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\rho_{0}(\overline{x}),\quad x=X(t,\overline{x}),

\displaystyle\rho(t,y)=\rho_{0}(\overline{y}),\quad y=X(t,\overline{y}).

Consequently, for any
\tau<t
, by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle|\rho(t,x)-\rho(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}|X(\tau,\overline{x})-X(\tau,\overline{y})|+2\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}|X(\tau,\overline{x})-X(\tau,\overline{y})|+2\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{T}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For any
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
t\in[0,T]
, we take
\tau=t\frac{|x-y|^{2\gamma}}{1+2|x-y|^{2\gamma}}
. Then it follows from (
2.7
) and assumption (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle|\rho(t,x)-\rho(t,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\exp\left(\log\Bigl(2+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\gamma}}\Bigr)\|u\|_{T}\right)|X(t,\overline{x})-X(t,\overline{y})|

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{t}\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{T}|x-y|^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\exp\bigl(-C_{0}\gamma\varepsilon_{1}\log(|x-y|)\bigr)|x-y|+2\sqrt{t}\varepsilon_{1}|x-y|^{\gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}|x-y|^{\gamma},\quad\forall|x-y|<1/2,

for some universal
C_{0}
, provided we choose
\varepsilon_{1}
sufficiently small such that
1-C_{0}\gamma\varepsilon_{1}\geq\gamma
. Note that a direct corollary of (
2.5
) is

(2.8)

\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

This completes the proof (
2.3
).
∎

Furthermore, it is important to establish the time regularity of
\rho
. In the remainder of this paper, we denote

(2.9)

|D|^{\eta}f(x):=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}f(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{\eta}\widehat{f}(\cdot))(x)=C_{d,\eta}\int\frac{\delta_{\alpha}f(x)}{|\alpha|^{d+\eta}}\,d\alpha,\quad\forall\eta\in(0,2),

noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1-e^{i\xi\cdot\alpha}}{|\alpha|^{d+\eta}}\,d\alpha=C_{d,\kappa}|\xi|^{\eta}.

Since for
\kappa\in(0,1)
we have
\|u\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa}}=\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\kappa}}
, Proposition
2.1
implies that

\||D|^{\eta}\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}<\infty,\quad\forall\eta\in(0,1).

We next quantify the time regularity of fractional derivatives of
\rho
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
0\leq\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. Assume that
\rho
is the solution of (
2.1
) with
\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{T}<\infty
. Then for any
0<s<t<T
, we have

\||D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(t)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}(1+\|u\|_{T})\min\Bigl\{(t-s)^{\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}},s^{-\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}{2}}(t-s)^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\Bigr\}.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a standard mollifier. Denote
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-3}\psi(\cdot/\varepsilon)
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\rho\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(t)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(s)=

\displaystyle\bigl(|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(t)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)\bigr)

\displaystyle-\bigl(|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(s)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)\bigr)+\bigl(|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)\bigr).

For the mollification error, using standard inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle||D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(x)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\bigl(|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(x)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho(y)\bigr)\psi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\||\cdot|^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}.

Moreover, the mollified function
\rho_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}+\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast(u\cdot\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon})+\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast\nabla\cdot\bigl(u(\rho-\rho_{\varepsilon})\bigr)=0,

hence

\displaystyle||D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(t)-|D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(s)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl||D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast\int_{s}^{t}(\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon}\cdot u)(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr|+\Bigl||D|^{\alpha^{\prime}}\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast\int_{s}^{t}(\rho-\rho_{\varepsilon})u(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{T}\int_{s}^{t}\varepsilon^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-1}\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{T}\,\varepsilon^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}-1}\min\bigl\{s^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s),(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr\},

where we used the estimate

|\nabla\rho_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim||D|^{\alpha}\rho\ast\nabla|D|^{-\alpha}\psi_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\||D|^{\alpha}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\varepsilon^{-1+\alpha}.

Optimizing with respect to
\varepsilon
by taking
\varepsilon=\min\bigl\{s^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t-s),(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr\}
yields the desired estimate.
∎

For any
\eta>-d
, we call
\mathcal{P}^{\eta}
an
\eta
-order Fourier multiplier if
\mathcal{P}^{\eta}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(P^{\eta}\widehat{f})
, where
P^{\eta}
is an
\eta
-order homogeneous function. We list some properties of the heat kernel
K_{x_{0}}(t,x)
in the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
0<C_{1}<\rho_{0}(x)<C_{2}<\infty
. Let
K_{x_{0}}(t,x)
be determined by (
1.9
). Then we have the pointwise estimate

(2.10)

\sup_{x_{0}}|\partial_{t}^{m}\mathcal{P}^{\eta}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)|\leq\frac{C_{m,\eta}(C_{1},C_{2})}{(\sqrt{t}+|x|)^{d+2m+\eta}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\,\eta>-d.

Furthermore, we have the time Hölder estimate

(2.11)

\sup_{x_{0}}\|\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}\mathcal{P}^{\eta}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\leq C_{m,\eta}(C_{1},C_{2})t^{-m-\frac{\eta}{2}}\min\left\{1,\frac{a}{t}\right\},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\,\eta>0.

Proof.

The estimate (
2.10
) follows from a classical decomposition into low and high frequencies; details can be found in
[
12
]
. For (
2.11
), we observe that

\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{t}^{m}\mathcal{P}^{\eta}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)=\int_{t}^{t+a}\partial_{t}^{m+1}\mathcal{P}^{\eta}K_{x_{0}}(s,x)\,ds,

and then (
2.11
) follows directly from (
2.10
).
∎

3.
Estimates for critical terms

In this section, we establish the main estimates for
u_{L,x_{0}}
and
u_{N,x_{0}}
defined in (
1.10
), following the approach of Koch and Tataru in
[
29
]
.

We will frequently use the following property, whose proof can be found in Section 2.4 of
[
5
]
:

\|u\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa}}\sim\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{\kappa}}\sim\||D|^{\kappa}u\|_{\dot{B}_{\infty,\infty}^{0}}\lesssim\||D|^{\kappa}u\|_{L^{\infty}},\quad\forall\kappa\in(0,1).

For the linear term
u_{L,x_{0}}
, by the properties of the heat kernel, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}
satisfy
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Then there exists
T\sim b^{2}
such that for
\tilde{u}_{L}
defined in (
1.11
), the following estimates hold:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{L,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}+\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)-u_{L,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|(\nabla D^{\kappa}u_{L,x_{0}})|_{x_{0}=x}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{5}{12}}\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}.

Proof.

We first deduce from (
1.9
) that
\widehat{K}_{x_{0}}(t,\xi)=e^{-\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}|\xi|^{2}},
from which, we infer

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\Bigl(\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigl\|e^{\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}},\quad\mbox{if}\ \ T\leq\rho(x_{0})b^{2},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}

\displaystyle t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|(|D|^{1+\kappa}u_{L,x_{0}})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}(x_{0})\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\Bigl(\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1+\kappa}{2}}\bigl\|(|D|^{1+\kappa}e^{\frac{t}{2\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}e^{\frac{t}{2\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}(x_{0})\sup_{t\leq T}\left(\Bigl(\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigl\|e^{\frac{t}{2\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}(x_{0})\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}},\quad\mbox{if}\ \ T\leq 2\rho(x_{0})b^{2}.

Observe that for
t\geq s
, if
t-s\leq\frac{s}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}-e^{\frac{s}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{t-\frac{s}{2}}\partial_{\tau}\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}\right)e^{\frac{s}{2\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}d\tau\Bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{t-\frac{s}{2}}\bigl\|e^{\frac{\tau}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}\Delta e^{\frac{s}{2\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\bigr\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-1}(t-s)\|e^{\frac{s}{4\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}(t-s)\|u_{0}\|_{BMO^{-1}_{b}},\quad\mbox{if}\ \ T\leq 4\rho(x_{0})b^{2}.

While it follows from (
3.2
) that

\left\|e^{\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}-e^{\frac{s}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}},\quad\mbox{if}\ \ s\leq t\leq T\leq\rho(x_{0})b^{2}.

As a result, it comes out

(3.3)

\left\|e^{\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}-e^{\frac{s}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\min\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\}s^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}.

For the case
t-s\geq\frac{s}{2}
, (
3.3
) follows from (
3.2
).

By virtue of (
3.3
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)-u_{L,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\lesssim&\frac{\min\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}s^{\varkappa_{1}}\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}\\
\lesssim&\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}},\end{split}

where we used the fact that if
t-s\leq s,
there holds

\frac{\min\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}s^{\varkappa_{1}}\leq(t-s)^{-1+\varkappa_{1}}s^{1-\varkappa_{1}}\leq 1\quad\forall\varkappa_{1}\in(0,1),

whereas if
t-s\geq s,
one has

\frac{\min\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}s^{\varkappa_{1}}\leq(t-s)^{-\varkappa_{1}}s^{\varkappa_{1}}\leq 1\quad\forall\varkappa_{1}\in(0,1).

For
\|u_{L,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}},
we get, by using changes of variable, that

\displaystyle\|u_{L,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}=

\displaystyle R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B(x,R)}\bigl|e^{\frac{t}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\Delta}u_{0}(y)\bigr|^{2}\,dy\,dt\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(x_{0})R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\bigl\{\frac{T}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})},\frac{R^{2}}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\bigr\}}\int_{B(x,R)}\bigl|e^{s\Delta}u_{0}(y)\bigr|\,dy\,ds\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim

\displaystyle C(\rho_{0}(x_{0}))R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\bigl\{\frac{T}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})},\frac{R^{2}}{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}\bigr\}}\int_{B\bigl(x,\frac{R}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}}\bigr)}\bigl|e^{s\Delta}u_{0}(y)\bigr|^{2}\,dy\,\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}

where in the last step, we used the fact that the ball with radius
R
can be covered by finite balls with radius
\frac{R}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x_{0})}}
, and with a constant multiple to
T,b
with respect to
\|\rho_{0}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

As a consequence, we deduce that,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|(|D|^{1+\kappa}u_{L,x_{0}})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)+\|u_{L,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sup_{x_{0}}\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{L,x_{0}}(t)-u_{L,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}}.

On the other hand, we observe that for any function
K(t,s,x,z)\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,x-y,x)u_{0}(y)\,dy\Bigr)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigl(\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,x-y,x)|u_{0}(y)|^{2}\,dy\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,x-y,x)\,dy\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\|K(t,s,\cdot,z)\|_{L^{1}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,x-y,x)|u_{0}(y)|^{2}\,dydx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,\cdot,z)\|_{L^{1}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

We now take
K(t,s,x,z)=K(t,x,z)=\frac{\rho_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}(z)}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp(-\frac{\rho_{0}(z)|x|^{2}}{4t}),
then it follows from (
1.5
) that

\sup_{z\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}K(t,s,x,z)\leq\frac{C_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{C_{1}|x|^{2}}{4t}\Bigr)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,\cdot,z)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 1,\quad\mbox{for any}\ t\geq 0.

Hence we obtain

\sup_{t\leq T}\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

from which and (
3.4
), we infer

(3.5)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\leq T}&t^{\frac{5}{12}}\left\|(K_{x_{0}}\ast u_{0})|_{x_{0}=x}(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{12}}\\
\leq&\Bigl(\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|(K_{x_{0}}\ast u_{0})(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\Bigr)^{\frac{5}{6}}\Bigl(\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\sup_{t\leq T}\|\widetilde{u}_{L}(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{6}}\\
\lesssim&\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{BMO}_{b}^{-1}}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}.\end{split}

For the term involving time difference of
\widetilde{u}_{L},
we take

\displaystyle K(t,s,x,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\rho_{0}(z))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(4\pi t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\rho_{0}(z)|x|^{2}}{4t}\Bigr)-\frac{(\rho_{0}(z))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(4\pi s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\rho_{0}(z)|x|^{2}}{4s}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\tau}\Bigl(\frac{(\rho_{0}(z))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(4\pi\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\rho_{0}(z)|x|^{2}}{4\tau}\Bigr)\Bigr)\,d\tau,

from which and (
1.5
), we infer

\bigl\|\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t,s,\cdot,z)\bigr\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\min\bigl\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\bigr\},\quad\mbox{for any}\ t\geq s\geq 0,

so that we obtain

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\min\bigl\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\bigr\}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\min\bigl\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\bigr\}\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}.

As a consequence, we deduce that

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\\
&\lesssim\sup_{s<t<T}\Bigl(\frac{s^{\varkappa_{2}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\bigl(s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr)^{\frac{5}{6}}\|\tilde{u}_{L}(t)-\tilde{u}_{L}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\Bigr)\\
&\lesssim\frac{s^{\varkappa_{2}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}{\min\{1,s^{-1}(t-s)\}}\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}}\\
&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{BMO_{b}^{-1}}^{\frac{5}{6}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{6}},\end{split}

By summarizing the estimates (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we complete the proof of (
3.1
).
∎

For
u_{N,x_{0}}
, we have the following estimates:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
T\sim b^{2}<1
. Let
(\rho,u,P)
be a smooth enough solution of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
and
u_{N,x_{0}}
be defined in (
1.10
). We assume that
\|u\|_{X_{T}}<\varepsilon_{0}
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0.
Then we have the following estimates:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{N,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}+\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|(\nabla|D|^{\kappa}u_{N,x_{0}})|_{x_{0}=x}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{5}{12}}\|\tilde{u}_{N}(t)\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{N}(t)-\tilde{u}_{N}(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}.

Proof.

Define the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{1,b}}:=\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,R\leq b}R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(R)}|f(\tau,y)|^{2}\,dy\,d\tau\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}:=\sup_{t\leq T}t\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,R\leq b}R^{-3}\int_{0}^{\min\{T,R^{2}\}}\int_{B_{x_{0}}(R)}|f(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau.

Thanks to the approach of Koch and Tataru, we have the following lemma (see also
[
5
, Lemma 5.36]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

B(f,g):=\int_{0}^{t}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau)\ast\mathbb{P}\nabla\cdot(f\otimes g)(\tau)\,d\tau.

Then, for some
T\sim b^{2}
, we have

\|B(f,g)\|_{Z_{T}^{1,b}}\leq C\|f\otimes g\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\leq C\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{1,b}}\|g\|_{Z_{T}^{1,b}},

where the implicit constant is independent of
x_{0}
.

The proof of this lemma is postponed to the appendix.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that

(3.8)

\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\|u_{N,x_{0}}\|_{V_{T}^{b}}\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}.

Let us turn to the estimate of the remaining norms of
u_{N,x_{0}}
in the space
X_{T},
which we will handle term by term below.

(i): The
C^{1+\kappa}
norm of
u_{N,x_{0}}
.

We split the time interval into two parts as

\displaystyle|D|^{\kappa}\nabla u_{N,x_{0}}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t/2}+\int_{t/2}^{t}\right)\int|D|^{\kappa}\mathbb{P}\nabla^{2}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle u_{N1,x_{0}}+u_{N2,x_{0}}.

Note that by Proposition
2.1
, if
\|u\|_{X_{T}}<\varepsilon_{0}
for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, then

\|\rho\|_{C^{\gamma}}\lesssim C(\gamma,\varepsilon_{0})\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}},\quad\forall\gamma<1.

For
u_{N1}
, we use a method similar to that in the Appendix
7.2
. In order to do so, for all
(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3},
we define

(3.9)

\Gamma_{R}^{1}(t,x):=\mathbf{1}_{|x|>R}\frac{1}{|x|^{3+\alpha}},\qquad\Gamma_{R}^{2}(t,x):=\mathbf{1}_{|x|<R}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t}+|x|)^{3+\alpha}},

for some
\alpha>0
. It is easy to observe that

||D|^{\kappa}\mathbb{P}\nabla^{2}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)|\lesssim\Gamma_{R}^{1}(t,x)+\Gamma_{R}^{2}(t,x),

with
\alpha=2+\kappa
. Then

\displaystyle|u_{N1,x_{0}}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\Gamma_{R}^{1}(x-y)|\rho u\otimes u|(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\Gamma_{R}^{2}(t-\tau,x-y)|\rho u\otimes u|(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau:=u_{N1,x_{0}}^{1}+u_{N1,x_{0}}^{2}.

Taking
R=\sqrt{t}
in (
7.10
) of Lemma
7.4
gives

\|u_{N1,x_{0}}^{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-1-\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|\rho u\otimes u\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\|u_{N1,x_{0}}^{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{|y|\leq\sqrt{t}}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t})^{5+\kappa}}|\rho u\otimes u|(\tau,y)\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{t^{\frac{5+\kappa}{2}}}\|\rho u\otimes u\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\lesssim t^{-1-\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|\rho u\otimes u\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Combining the estimates for
u_{N1}^{1}
and
u_{N1}^{2}
, and noting that
\|\rho u\otimes u\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\lesssim\|u\|_{Z_{T}^{1,b}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we obtain

(3.10)

\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|u_{N1,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

For
u_{N2}
, we have

\displaystyle|u_{N2,x_{0}}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl|\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbb{P}\nabla^{2}|D|^{\kappa}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\bigl((\rho u\otimes u)(\tau,y)-(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau,x)\bigr)\,dy\,d\tau\Bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\mathbb{P}\nabla^{2}|D|^{\kappa}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\right||x-y|^{\kappa^{\prime}}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-1-\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2}}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\,d\tau.

for some
\kappa^{\prime}\in(\kappa,1)
.
Yet we get, by using interpolation inequality in Hölder space and the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}}
given by (
1.3
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{1+\kappa}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1+\kappa}}^{\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{1+\kappa}}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\kappa^{\prime}\right)}\|u\|_{X_{T}},

from which and the law of product in Hölder space (see
[
5
]
), we infer

(3.11)

\begin{split}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\lesssim&\|\rho u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}+\|\rho u\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
\lesssim&\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}+\|\rho\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim\tau^{-1-\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.\end{split}

As a consequence,
we obtain

(3.12)

|u_{N2,x_{0}}(t,x)|\\
\lesssim t^{-1-\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}

Thus, by (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), we have proved

\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|\nabla|D|^{\kappa}u_{N,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Note that all of the estimates above are uniform with respect to
x_{0}
. Therefore, for
T\sim b^{2}
, we have

\sup_{t\leq T}t^{1+\frac{\kappa}{2}}\|(\nabla|D|^{\kappa}u_{N,x_{0}})|_{x_{0}=x}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

(ii): The non-critical
L^{12}
norm
.

We first observe from Proposition
2.3
that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}K_{x_{0}}(t,x)|\leq\frac{C(C_{1},C_{2})}{\left(\sqrt{t}+|x|\right)^{4}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|\nabla_{x}K_{x_{0}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}},

from which and
(
1.11
), we get, by direct computation, that

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}_{N}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int\bigl|\nabla_{y}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x,x-y)\bigr|^{\frac{11}{12}}\Bigl(\bigl|\nabla_{y}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x,x-y)\bigr|^{\frac{1}{12}}|\rho u\otimes u(\tau,y)|\Bigr)dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{11}{24}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\bigl|\nabla_{y}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x,x-y)\bigr||\rho u\otimes u(\tau,y)|^{12}\,dy\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{12}}d\tau.

Taking the
L^{12}
norm with respect to
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}_{N}(t)\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{11}{24}}\Bigl(\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\bigl|\nabla_{y}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x,x-y)\bigr||\rho u\otimes u(\tau,y)|^{12}\,dy\,dx\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{12}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{L^{12}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{-\frac{11}{12}}\,d\tau\,\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{5}{12}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{12}}^{2}\leq\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{12}}\lesssim\tau^{-\frac{11}{12}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}.

(iii): The time difference for
L^{\infty}
.

For the quantity
\frac{\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}
, it suffices to consider the case
t-s<\frac{s}{4}
, since otherwise, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\frac{1}{2}}{\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{t<T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Let
a:=t-s
and write

(3.13)

\displaystyle u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\mathbb{P}\nabla K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau)\ast(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{0}^{s/2}+\int_{s/2}^{s}\right)\delta_{a}^{t}\mathbb{P}\nabla K_{x_{0}}(s-\tau)\ast(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau

\displaystyle=:Du_{N,x_{0}}^{1}(t,s)+Du_{N,x_{0}}^{2}(t,s)+Du_{N,x_{0}}^{3}(t,s).

By virtue of (
3.11
), for any
\eta\in(0,1),
we get, by first taking
\kappa^{\prime}\in(1-\eta,1)
and then using the interpolation inequality in Besov spaces, that

(3.14)

\begin{split}\|D^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim&\|(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{\dot{B}^{1-\eta}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\theta}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{\dot{C}^{\kappa^{\prime}}}^{\theta}\\
\lesssim&\bigl(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\tau\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\bigr)^{1-\theta}\tau^{1-\theta}\times\tau^{-\left(1+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{2}\right)\theta}\bigl(\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\bigr)^{\theta}\\
\lesssim&\tau^{-\frac{3-\eta}{2}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2},\end{split}

where
\theta=\frac{1-\eta}{\kappa^{\prime}}.

While for
\Gamma_{\eta}(t):=\mathbb{P}\nabla|D|^{\eta-1}K_{x_{0}}(t)
with some
\eta\ll\kappa
sufficiently small, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{\eta}(t,x)|\leq\frac{C(C_{1},C_{2})}{(\sqrt{t}+|x|)^{3+\eta}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|\Gamma_{\eta}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}.

As a consequence, we deduce that

\|Du_{N,x_{0}}^{1}(t,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\|\Gamma_{\eta}(t-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\|D^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau\lesssim a^{1-\frac{\eta}{2}}s^{-\frac{3-\eta}{2}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Similarly, for
Du_{N,x_{0}}^{3}(t,s)
, by (
2.11
) we have

(3.15)

\|\delta_{a}^{t}\Gamma_{\eta}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\min\left\{t^{-\frac{\eta}{2}},at^{-1-\frac{\eta}{2}}\right\}.

In particular, for
0<\varkappa_{1}<1-\frac{\eta}{2},
we deduce from (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\|Du_{N,x_{0}}^{3}(t,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{s/2}^{s}\|\delta_{a}^{t}\Gamma_{\eta}(s-\tau)\|_{L^{1}}\|D^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{\varkappa_{1}}\int_{s/2}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{\eta}{2}-\varkappa_{1}}\tau^{-\frac{3-\eta}{2}}\,d\tau\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{\varkappa_{1}}s^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varkappa_{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

For
Du_{N,x_{0}}^{2}(t,s)
, we write

Du_{N,x_{0}}^{2}(t,s)=\int_{0}^{s/2}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}\nabla K_{x_{0}}(\gamma-\tau)\,d\gamma\right)\ast(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\tau.

Denote
H(\tau):=\partial_{\gamma}\mathbb{P}\nabla K_{x_{0}}(\tau)
; then
H(\gamma-\tau)\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\gamma-\tau}+|y|)^{6}}
. Using the same decomposition as in Lemma
7.4
, we write
H=H^{1}+H^{2}
, with
H^{1}\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{|y|\geq R}\frac{1}{|y|^{6}}
and
H^{2}\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{|y|\leq R}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\gamma-\tau}+|y|)^{6}}
. Take
R=\sqrt{s}<b
. By (
7.10
) in Lemma
7.4
with
\alpha=3
, we obtain

\left|\int_{0}^{s/2}\int_{s}^{t}H^{1}(\gamma-\tau)\ast(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\gamma\,d\tau\right|\lesssim\frac{t-s}{R^{3}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Moreover, for the part concerning
H^{2},
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{s/2}\int_{s}^{t}H^{2}(\gamma-\tau)\ast(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\,d\gamma\,d\tau\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{s/2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{|y|\leq R}\frac{1}{(\gamma-\tau)^{3}}|\rho u\otimes u|(\tau,x-y)\,dy\,d\gamma\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\frac{R^{3}}{(\gamma-\frac{s}{2})^{3}}\,d\gamma\,\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\lesssim\frac{(t-s)R^{3}}{s^{3}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

As a result, since
R=\sqrt{s}
, it comes out

\|Du_{N,x_{0}}^{3}(t,s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{t-s}{s^{3/2}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Since all the estimates above are uniform with respect to
x_{0}
and
t-s<\frac{s}{4}
, we conclude

\sup_{x_{0}}\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{1}{2}+\varkappa_{1}}\frac{\|u_{N,x_{0}}(t)-u_{N,x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{1}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

(iv): The time difference for
L^{12}
.

For any
\eta\in(0,1),
we get, by using (
2.9
), that

\displaystyle\left\||D|^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)\right\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\left\|\delta_{a}(\rho u\otimes u)\right\|_{L^{12}}}{|\alpha|^{4-\eta}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\frac{\|\delta_{\alpha}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{12}}}{|\alpha|^{4-\eta}}d\alpha+\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\delta_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{12}}}{|\alpha|^{4-\eta}}d\alpha

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\delta_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{\alpha}\|u(\cdot-\alpha)\|_{L^{12}}}{|\alpha|^{4-\eta}}d\alpha

As a result, it comes out

(3.16)

\begin{split}\||D|^{1-\eta}&(\rho u\otimes u)(t)\|_{L^{12}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{12}}\|\rho(t)\|_{C^{1-\eta}}\\
&+\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\eta}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1}}^{1-\eta}\|u(t)\|_{L^{12}}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}t^{-\left(\frac{17}{12}-\frac{\eta}{2}\right)}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}.\end{split}

Let us now turn to the estimate of
\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{N}(t)-\tilde{u}_{N}(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}.
We again use the corresponding decomposition in (
3.13
). Let
a=t-s<\frac{s}{4}
. Then we deduce from (
2.10
) and (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\|D\tilde{u}_{N}^{1}(t,s)\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\|\tilde{\Gamma}_{\eta}(t-\tau,z,y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}(L^{\infty}_{z})}\||D|^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)(\tau)\|_{L^{12}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{1-\frac{\eta}{2}}s^{-\frac{2-\eta}{2}-\frac{5}{12}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}.

Here, we denote
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\eta}(t,z,y):=(\nabla|D|^{\eta-1}K_{x_{0}}(t,y))|_{x_{0}=z}
for
0<\eta\ll\kappa
and
T\leq 1
.

Similarly, we deduce from (
2.11
) and (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\|D\tilde{u}_{N}^{3}\|_{L^{12}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s/2}^{s}\|\delta_{a}^{t}\tilde{\Gamma}_{\eta}(s-\tau,z,y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}L^{\infty}_{z}}\|D^{1-\eta}(\rho u\otimes u)\|_{L^{12}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{1-\theta}\int_{s/2}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\theta-\frac{\eta}{2}}\tau^{-\left(\frac{17}{12}-\frac{\eta}{2}\right)}\,d\tau\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\theta}s^{-\theta-\frac{5}{12}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2},

for any
\theta<1-\frac{\eta}{2}.

For
u_{N,x_{0}}^{2}
, using the fact that
\|\rho u\otimes u(t)\|_{L^{12}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{11}{12}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}
and (
3.15
), we infer

\displaystyle\|D\tilde{u}_{N}^{2}(t,s)\|_{L^{12}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s/2}\|\delta_{a}^{t}\tilde{\Gamma}_{1}(s-\tau,z,y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}(L^{\infty}_{z})}\|\rho u\otimes u(\tau)\|_{L^{12}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{\varkappa_{2}}\int_{0}^{s/2}(s-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varkappa_{2}}\tau^{-\frac{11}{12}}\,d\tau\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{\varkappa_{2}}s^{-\varkappa_{2}-\frac{5}{12}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Thus we conclude that

\sup_{s<t<T}s^{\frac{5}{12}+\varkappa_{2}}\frac{\|\tilde{u}_{N}(t)-\tilde{u}_{N}(s)\|_{L^{12}}}{(t-s)^{\varkappa_{2}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\|\rho_{0}\|_{C^{1}}.

Combining the results (i)–(iv) above with (
3.8
), we complete the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

4.
Estimates for remainder terms

In this section we derive estimates for the remainder terms
u_{R_{i},x_{0}}

(i=1,2,3)
that appear in the decomposition (
1.10
). Recall the definitions

(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{R1,x_{0}}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\nabla\Delta^{-1}(\nabla\rho_{0}\cdot\partial_{\tau}u)(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau,

\displaystyle u_{R2,x_{0}}(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\mathbb{P}\partial_{\tau}(\varrho u)(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau,

\displaystyle u_{R3,x_{0}}(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)\partial_{\tau}\bigl((\rho_{0}(y)-\rho_{0}(x_{0}))u(\tau)\bigr)\,dy\,d\tau.

We denote
\tilde{u}_{Ri}(t,x):=(u_{Ri,x_{0}})|_{x_{0}=x}
and
K(t,x,y):=(K_{x_{0}}(t,y))|_{x_{0}=x}
. These terms arise from the spatial inhomogeneity of the density
\rho
and the localization in
x_{0}
. We shall prove that each
u_{R_{i},x_{0}}
satisfies an estimate of the same type as (
3.1
) and (
3.7
),
with additional small factors depending on the regularity of
\rho
. All constants appearing in this section are independent of
x_{0}
and
b
.

To prove the estimates of time differences, we introduce the following technical lemma. The proof is direct, and a similar version can be found in Lemma 2.3 of
[
11
]
and Lemma 2.2 of
[
13
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0\leq\eta,\sigma<1
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Define

g_{x_{0}}(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\int\partial_{\tau}K_{x_{0}}(t-\tau,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau,

where
K_{x_{0}}
is defined in (
1.9
), and define

\tilde{g}(t,x):=g_{x_{0}}(t,x)|_{x_{0}=x}.

Then we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{x_{0}}\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|g_{x_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{x_{0}}\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|g_{x_{0}}(t)-g_{x_{0}}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}},

and for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|\tilde{g}(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|\tilde{g}(t)-\tilde{g}(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}.

Proof.

For simplicity, we only prove (
4.3
), since (
4.2
) can be proved along the same line. Denote that
\partial_{t}K(t,x,y)=(\partial_{t}K_{x_{0}}(t,y))|_{x_{0}=x}
. First, for the
L^{p}
norm, we write

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)\bigl(f(\tau,y)-f(t,y)\bigr)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+f(t,x)-\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}K(t/2,x,x-y)f(t,y)\,dy.

Note that for any
h(x)
and
m,n\geq 0
, if we denote
\mathbf{h}(t,x):=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{y}^{m_{2}}K(t,x,x-y)h(y)\,dy
, then by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\mathbf{h}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{y}^{m_{2}}K(t,x,x-y)|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{y}^{m_{2}}K(t,x,x-y)|^{\frac{1}{p}}|h(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|\sup_{z}\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{m_{2}}K(t,z,x)\bigr\|_{L_{x}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{y}^{m_{2}}K(t,x,x-y)||h(y)|^{p}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which leads to

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{h}(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|\sup_{z}\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{m_{2}}K(t,z,x)\bigr\|_{L_{x}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\iint_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}|\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{y}^{m_{2}}K(t,x,x-y)||h(y)|^{p}\,dy\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl\|\sup_{z}\partial_{t}^{m_{1}}\nabla_{x}^{m_{2}}K(t,z,x)\bigr\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\|h\|_{L^{p}}.

Note that, for any
x_{0}
and
t
, one has

\left\|K(t,x_{0},x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}=1,

so that it follows from (
4.4
) that

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K({t}/{2},x,x-y)f(t,y)\,dy\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\lesssim\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim t^{-\eta}\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}.

By (
4.4
) and the pointwise estimate (
2.10
), we find

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{g}(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(\int_{0}^{t/2}(t-\tau)^{-1}\tau^{-\eta}\,d\tau+t^{-\eta}\Bigr)\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle+\int_{t/2}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-1+\sigma}\tau^{-\sigma-\eta}\,d\tau\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\eta}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}\Bigr).

For the time differences, let
a:=t-s
. For the same reason as in the previous section, we only need to consider the case
a<\frac{s}{4}
. We write

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau:=I(t,x)+II(t,x).

For
I(t)
, since

\displaystyle I(t)-I(s)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s/2}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau,

by (
2.10
) and (
4.4
), one obtains

\displaystyle{\|I(t)-I(s)\|_{L^{p}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{0}^{s/2}\min\bigl\{(s-\tau)^{-1},a(s-\tau)^{-2}\big\}\tau^{-\eta}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{s/2}^{t/2}(t-\tau)^{-1}\tau^{-\eta}\,d\tau\Bigr)\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}},

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{s/2}^{t/2}(t-\tau)^{-1}\tau^{-\eta}\,d\tau\lesssim at^{-1}s^{-\eta}\lesssim a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}\Bigl(\frac{a}{t}\Bigr)^{1-\sigma}\lesssim a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}.

Furthermore, notice that

\displaystyle II(t)-II(s)

\displaystyle=-\int_{s/2}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t/2}^{s}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle=-\int_{s/2}^{t/2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)f(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t/2}^{s}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)\left(f(\tau,y)-f(s,y)\right)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)\left(f(\tau,y)-f(t,y)\right)\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\int_{t/2}^{s}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)f(s,y)\,dy\,d\tau\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{\tau}K(t-\tau,x,x-y)f(t,y)\,dy\,d\tau\right)

\displaystyle:=DII_{1}(t,s)+DII_{2}(t,s)+DII_{3}(t,s)+DII_{4}(t,s).

Observing that

\sup_{x}\|\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,y)\|_{L_{y}^{1}}\lesssim(s-\tau)^{-1},

and on the time interval
(s/2,t/2)
, there holds

, we deduce from (
4.4
) and

, that

\displaystyle\|DII_{1}(t,s)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{-1}\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{\frac{t}{2}}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\,d\tau\lesssim s^{-1}\int_{\frac{s}{2}}^{\frac{t}{2}}\tau^{-\eta}\,d\tau\sup_{\tau\leq T}\tau^{\eta}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)s^{-1-\eta}\sup_{\tau\leq T}\tau^{\eta}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}\sup_{\tau\leq T}\tau^{\eta}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{p}}.

By (
2.10
) and (
4.4
) (pointwisely for each
\tau
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl\|\int_{t/2}^{s}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\delta_{a}^{t}\partial_{\tau}K(s-\tau,x,x-y)\bigl(f(\tau,y)-f(s,y)\bigr)\,dy\,d\tau\bigr\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-1+\sigma}\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\Bigr\}\,\tau^{-\sigma-\eta}\,d\tau\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}},

where we used the fact that

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-1+\sigma}\min\Bigl\{1,\frac{a}{s-\tau}\Bigr\}\,\tau^{-\sigma-\eta}\,d\tau=

\displaystyle a\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{2s-t}(s-\tau)^{-2+\sigma}\tau^{-\sigma-\eta}\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{2s-t}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-1+\sigma}\tau^{-\sigma-\eta}\,d\tau\lesssim a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}.

Then along the same line to the proof of (
4.5
), we find

\displaystyle\|DII_{2}(t,s)\|_{L^{p}}+\|DII_{3}(t,s)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim(t-s)^{\sigma}s^{-\eta-\sigma}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}\Bigr).

For the term
DII_{4}(t,s)
, we write

\displaystyle DII_{4}(t,s)=

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(t-s,x,x-y)\bigl(f(s,y)dy-f(t,y)\bigr)\,dy\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(s-\frac{t}{2},x,x-y)f(s,y)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(\frac{t}{2},x,x-y)f(s,y)dy\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\left(f(t,x)-f(s,x)\right).

Then by (
3.15
) and (
4.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|DII_{4}(t,s)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}+\|f(s)\|_{L^{p}}\sup_{x}\left\|K(s-{t}/{2},x,y)-K({t}/{2},x,y)\right\|_{L_{y}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim a^{\sigma}s^{-\sigma-\eta}\Bigl(\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\eta}\|f(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\sup_{s<t\leq T}s^{\eta+\sigma}\frac{\|f(t)-f(s)\|_{L^{p}}}{(t-s)^{\sigma}}\Bigr).

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

7.1.
Energy estimates

In this section, we derive energy estimates for smooth solutions
(\rho,u,P)
to the inhomogeneous Navier–Stokes system (
1.1
). For the classical Navier–Stokes equations (
\rho\equiv 1
), it is well known that the equation satisfies the energy inequality
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
for any
t\geq 0
. In the inhomogeneous and non-vacuum case, a similar property holds; see Lemma
7.1
. For the two-dimensional case, the
L^{2}
norm is critical, which allows one to obtain global regularity for large initial data (see
[
24
]
and references therein). In three dimensions, however, the
L^{2}
norm is supercritical. Nevertheless, we will use the
L^{2}
norm to estimate the lower-order remainder terms
u_{R}
, which enjoy additional regularity, making the loss of regularity from the critical level to
L^{2}
manageable. We derive these energy estimates because they help control terms with negative regularity, such as
\|\nabla\Delta^{-1}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{2}{3}}
, and because the Leray projection
\mathbb{P}
is bounded on
L^{p}
only for
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in L^{2}
and that

(7.1)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq T}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+t^{1+\frac{\eta}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1+\eta}}\right)\leq M\quad\hbox{and}\quad\\
&C_{1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq C_{2}\ \ \forall\ (t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3},\end{split}

for some
M>1
,
T<+\infty
, and
\eta\in(0,1)
. These quantities arise respectively from (
2.8
) and the definition of the
\|u\|_{X_{T}^{1}}
norm. Below the constant
C
may depend on
C_{1}
and
C_{2}.

Lemma 7.1

(Basic Energy Estimate)
.

Let
(\rho,u,P)
be a smooth enough solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in L^{2}
. Then

\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product of the momentum equation with
u
and using
\nabla\cdot u=0
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx\right)+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Integrating in time and applying (
7.1
) yields the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 7.2

(Estimates with Time Weights)
.

Let
(\rho,u,P)
be a smooth enough solution of (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in L^{2}
such that (
7.1
) holds. Then

\sup_{t\leq T}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}\leq C(1+M)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product of the momentum equation with
\partial_{t}u
, we obtain

\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)\cdot\partial_{t}u\,dx.

Multiplying both sides by
t
and applying Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(t\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)+t\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+t\|\rho u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}^{2}t\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating over
(0,T)
and using (
7.1
) together with Lemma
7.1
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7.3

(Higher Regularity Estimate)
.

Let
(\rho,u,P)
be a smooth enough solution of (
1.1
) with
u_{0}\in L^{2}
such that (
7.1
) holds. Then

\sup_{t\leq T}t\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|t\nabla\partial_{t}u\right\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}\leq C(1+M)^{2}M^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Differentiating (
1.1
) in time gives

\rho\partial_{tt}u-\partial_{t}\Delta u=-\partial_{t}\rho\,\partial_{t}u-\partial_{t}\rho\,\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)-\rho\partial_{t}u\cdot\nabla u-\rho u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u+\nabla\partial_{t}P.

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product with
\partial_{t}u
and multiplying the resulting inequality by
t^{2}
, we obtain

(7.2)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(t^{2}\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)+t^{2}\|\partial_{t}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=t\|\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)(u\otimes\partial_{t}u)(t,x):\nabla u(t,x)\,dx\\
&\quad-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(t,x)(\partial_{t}u\otimes\partial_{t}u)(t,x):\nabla u(t,x)\,dx\\
&:=I(t)+II(t)+III(t)+IV(t).\end{split}

By Lemma
7.1
, Lemma
7.2
, and (
7.1
), we have

(7.3)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(|I(t)|+|IV(t)|\bigr)\,dt\lesssim&\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}\|t\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\rho}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&C(1+M)^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

where we used (
7.1
) and interpolation inequality so that

\|t\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}}\|t^{1+\frac{\eta}{2}}u(t)\|_{\dot{C}^{1+\eta}}^{\frac{1}{1+\eta}}\lesssim M.

For
II
, note that by the transport equation for
\rho
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and (
7.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle|II(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|2t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(t,x)(u\otimes\partial_{t}u)(t,x):\partial_{t}\nabla u(t,x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|t^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|t\nabla\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}t^{2}\|\partial_{t}\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CM^{2}t\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which together with Lemma
7.2
yields

(7.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|II(t)|\,dt\leq\frac{1}{8}\|t\partial_{t}\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+M)^{2}M^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
III
, using the transport equation for
\rho
in (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\operatorname{div}(\rho u)(u\otimes\partial_{t}u):\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle=-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(u\cdot\nabla u\otimes\partial_{t}u):\nabla u\,dx-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(u\otimes u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}u):\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle-t^{2}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\rho(u\otimes\partial_{t}u):(u\cdot\nabla(\nabla u))\,dx

\displaystyle:=III_{1}+III_{2}+III_{3}.

By (
7.1
), we deduce

\displaystyle|III_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{t}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|t\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{2}\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}.

Then Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
7.2
lead to

(7.5)

\int_{0}^{T}|III_{1}(t)|\,dt\lesssim(1+M)^{2}M^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, (
7.1
), and Lemma
7.1
, we obtain

(7.6)

\int_{0}^{T}|III_{2}(t)|\,dt\leq CM^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|t\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{2}}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{16}\|t\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+CM^{4}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

For
III_{3}
, we first need an estimate for
\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. From (
1.1
), we have

-\Delta u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\partial_{t}u+\rho u\cdot\nabla u).

Thus, by singular integral theory, together with (
7.1
), Lemma
7.1
, and Lemma
7.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{t}\nabla^{2}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies

(7.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|III_{3}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sqrt{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\sqrt{t}\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}\|\sqrt{t}\nabla^{2}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+M)^{2}M^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It follows from (
7.5
), (
7.6
), and (
7.7
) that

(7.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|III(t)|\,dt\leq\frac{1}{16}\|t\nabla\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{T}^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+M)^{2}M^{2}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Substitutining (
7.3
), (
7.4
), and (
7.8
) into (
7.2
) yields the desired result.
∎

7.2.
Proof of Lemma
3.1

In this appendix we give a self-contained proof of Proposition
3.1
. The reader is referred to
[
5
]
for a more detailed exposition. Before proceeding with the proof, we need some preparations.

We denote the linear functional
Lf=(L^{m}f)_{m=1,2,3}
as the solution to the following system

(7.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}L^{m}f-\Delta L^{m}f+\nabla P=\partial_{m}f,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot L^{m}f=0,

\displaystyle L^{m}f|_{t=0}=0.

We can prove the following version of
[
5
, Proposition 5.37]
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\Gamma^{i}(t,x),i=1,2,
be given by (
3.9
). There exists
C>0
such that for any
R<b
and
\alpha>0
,

(7.10)

\|\Gamma_{R}^{1}\ast_{t,x}f\|_{L^{\infty}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\alpha}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

For
R<b
and
0<\alpha<2
, there holds

\|\Gamma_{R}^{2}\ast_{t,x}f\|_{L^{\infty}([R^{2},\infty]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\alpha}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Proof.

First, for any
R<b
, we split the convolution integral into annuli
C(0,2^{p}R,2^{p+1}R)
, which yields

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{R}^{1}\ast f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{C(0,2^{p}R,2^{p+1}R)}\frac{1}{|y|^{3+\alpha}}|f(\tau,x-y)|\,dy\,d\tau.

Note that
C(0,2^{p}R,2^{p+1}R)
can be covered by balls
B(x^{\prime},R)
with center
x^{\prime}
and radius
R
, and the number of such
x^{\prime}
is approximately
2^{3p}
for any
t\leq R^{2}
. Consequently,

|\Gamma_{R}^{1}\ast f(t,x)|\leq\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(2^{p}R)^{-3-\alpha}2^{3p}\sup_{x^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{B(x^{\prime},R)}|f(\tau,x-y)|\,dy\lesssim\frac{C}{R^{\alpha}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Second, we write

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{R}^{2}\ast f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\min\{t/2,R^{2}\}}^{t}+\int_{0}^{\min\{R^{2},t/2\}}\right)\int_{B_{0}(R)}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t-\tau}+|x-y|)^{3+\alpha}}|f(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle:=\Gamma_{R}^{21}(f)+\Gamma_{R}^{22}(f).

For
\Gamma_{R}^{22}(f)
, since in this regime
\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t-\tau}+|x-y|)^{3+\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}
, we obtain

|\Gamma_{R}^{22}(f)|\lesssim\frac{R^{3}}{t^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{R^{3}}\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{B_{0}(R)}|f(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau\right)\lesssim\frac{R^{3}}{t^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},\quad\forall t\geq R^{2}.

For the remaining part, note that

\int_{B_{0}(R)}\frac{1}{(a+|y|)^{3+\alpha}}\,dy\lesssim\frac{1}{a^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|)^{3+\alpha}}\,dy.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{R}^{21}(f)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\min\{t/2,R^{2}\}}^{t}\int_{B_{0}(R)}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t-\tau}+|x-y|)^{3+\alpha}}\|f(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\left(\int_{R^{2}}^{t}\frac{1}{(t-\tau)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}+\int_{t/2}^{t}\frac{R^{3}}{t^{\frac{3+\alpha}{2}}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},\quad\forall t\geq R^{2},\;0<\alpha<2.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Assume that
\theta\in\mathcal{S}
. Define
f_{\theta}(t,\cdot):=t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\theta\!\left(\frac{\cdot}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\ast f(t,\cdot)
. Then

\|f_{\theta}\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x\in B_{0}(R)
. For any
R<b
, we have

(7.11)

\displaystyle|f_{\theta}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\theta\!\left(\frac{x-y}{\sqrt{t}}\right)|\mathbf{1}_{B_{0}(2R)}f(t,y)|\,dy

\displaystyle+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}}\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|/\sqrt{t})^{4}}\frac{t}{R^{2}}|f(t,y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left|\theta\!\left(\frac{\cdot}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\ast\mathbf{1}_{B_{0}(2R)}f(t,\cdot)\right|+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sup_{t\leq b^{2}}t\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Integrating over
[0,R^{2}]\times B_{0}(R)
, we obtain

\frac{1}{R^{3}}\|f_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times B_{0}(R))}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

The
L^{\infty}
estimate also follows from (
7.11
) for any
t<b^{2}
.
∎

Finally, to estimate the local
L^{1}
norm, we need the following technical lemma.

Lemma 7.6
.

For any
R<b
and any function
f:[0,R^{2}]\times B_{0}(2R)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there holds

\sup_{R<b}R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|L(f)(t)\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

Proof.

We decompose
f
into low and high frequencies. Precisely, let
\theta
be a function whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\theta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies
\widehat{\theta}|_{B_{0}(1)}\equiv 1
. Write

f=f^{l}+f^{h},\qquad f^{l}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl(\widehat{\theta}(t^{1/2}\cdot)\widehat{f}(t,\cdot)\bigr)(x).

Then

\displaystyle\|f^{h}\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}];\dot{H}^{-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|1-\widehat{\theta}(\sqrt{t}\xi)|^{2}}{t|\xi|^{2}}\,t|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{R^{2}}t\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sup_{t<R^{2}}t\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}},

and then the classical energy estimate for the Stokes equations yields

(7.12)

\|Lf^{h}\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim R^{3}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}^{2}.

The hardest part is
Lf^{l}
. Observe that if we define

\widetilde{f^{l}}(t,x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl(e^{t|\cdot|^{2}}\widehat{\theta}(\sqrt{t}|\cdot|)\widehat{f}(t,\cdot)\bigr)(x),

then

Lf^{l}=\nabla\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)\Delta}\mathbb{P}f^{l}(\tau)\,d\tau=\nabla e^{t\Delta}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau,

where
\mathbb{P}
denotes the Leray projection operator to the divergence free vector field space.
Since
\widehat{\theta}
is compactly supported, there exists a Schwartz function
\widetilde{\theta}
such that

(7.13)

\widetilde{f^{l}}=t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\widetilde{\theta}\!\left(\frac{\cdot}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\ast f(t,\cdot).

Direct calculation gives

(7.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f

\displaystyle:=\|Lf^{l}\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\langle\nabla e^{t\Delta}\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(s),\nabla e^{t\Delta}\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\bigr\rangle\,d\tau\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\bigl\langle\Delta e^{2t\Delta}\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau),\widetilde{f^{l}}(s)\bigr\rangle\,d\tau\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}\left\langle\left(\int_{s}^{R^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}e^{2t\Delta}\,dt\right)\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau),\widetilde{f^{l}}(s)\right\rangle\,d\tau\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R^{2}}\left\langle(e^{2s\Delta}-e^{2R^{2}\Delta})\int_{0}^{s}\mathbb{P}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau,\widetilde{f^{l}}(s)\right\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{f^{l}}\|_{L_{T}^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\sup_{s<R^{2}}\left\|(e^{2s\Delta}-e^{2R^{2}\Delta})\mathbb{P}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

A simple consequence of (
7.13
) are that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f^{l}}\|_{L_{T}^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|{f^{l}}\|_{L_{T}^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},

and

\displaystyle\left\|e^{2R^{2}\Delta}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-3}\left\|e^{2R^{2}\Delta}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau\right\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle R^{-3}\|f\|_{L^{1}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}.

For the remaining term, note that

\left|e^{2s\Delta}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau,x)\,d\tau\right|\leq s^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\int_{B({\bf n}\sqrt{s},\sqrt{s})}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2s}}\mathbf{1}(y)\left|\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau,y)\right|\,d\tau\,dy,

where
B({\bf n}\sqrt{s},\sqrt{s})
denotes the ball with center
{\bf n}\sqrt{s}
and radius
\sqrt{s}
. Using translation invariance, we may assume without loss of generality that
x=0
. Then

(7.15)

\displaystyle\left|e^{2s\Delta}\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau)\,d\tau(0)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{|{\bf n}|>2}e^{-\frac{|{\bf n}|^{2}}{4}}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{B({\bf n}\sqrt{s},\sqrt{s})}|\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\sum_{|{\bf n}|\leq 2}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{B({\bf n}\sqrt{s},\sqrt{s})}|\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl(1+\sum_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|n|^{2}}{4}}\Bigr)\|\widetilde{f^{l}}\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
7.5
. Here we also used
\sqrt{s}<R<b
, and the fact that
\int_{0}^{s}\int_{B{(\bf n}\sqrt{s},\sqrt{s})}|\widetilde{f^{l}}(\tau,y)|\,dy\,d\tau\lesssim s^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\widetilde{f^{l}}\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}
for any
n
. Combining (
7.14
)–(
7.15
), we obtain

\|Lf^{l}\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\lesssim R^{3}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}}^{2},

which together with (
7.12
) completes the proof of Lemma
7.6
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Obviously, it suffices to prove that
L
(defined in (
7.9
)) maps
Z_{T}^{2,b}
continuously into
Z_{T}^{1,b}
. By the Duhamel principle,
Lf
admits the explicit representation (see
[
5
]
for instance)

(L^{m}f)^{k}(t,x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\int_{0}^{t}\int\Gamma_{m,\ell}^{k}(t-\tau,x-y)f^{\ell}(\tau,y)\,dy\,d\tau,

where
\Gamma_{j,\ell}^{k}
is a kernel satisfying

|\Gamma_{j,\ell}^{k}(t,x)|\lesssim\Gamma_{R}^{1}(t,x)+\Gamma_{R}^{2}(t,x),\quad\forall R>0,

with
\Gamma_{R}^{1}
and
\Gamma_{R}^{2}
defined in (
3.9
). By Lemma
7.4
with
\alpha=1
and
R=\sqrt{t}
, we obtain

\|Lf(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},\quad\forall t<b^{2}.

Furthermore, we need to estimate the local
L^{2}
norm. Without loss of generality, we may restrict our attention to a neighborhood of the origin. Note that

Lf=L(\mathbf{1}_{B(0,2R)}f)+L(\mathbf{1}_{B^{c}(0,2R)}f).

For the exterior part, note that when
|x|<R
and
|y|>2R
, we have
\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t}+|x-y|)^{4}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sqrt{t}+|y|)^{4}}
. The first estimate in Lemma
7.4
then yields

R^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|L(\mathbf{1}_{B^{c}(0,2R)}f)\|_{L^{2}([0,R^{2}]\times B(0,R))}\lesssim R\|L(\mathbf{1}_{B^{c}(0,2R)}f)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,R^{2}]\times B(0,R))}\lesssim\|f\|_{Z_{T}^{2,b}},\quad\forall R<b.

The corresponding estimate for
L(\mathbf{1}_{B_{0}(2R)}f)
follows from Lemma
7.6
. We thus complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎
