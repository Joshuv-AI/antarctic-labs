A Projective Resolution of the Symplectic Steinberg Module
Urshita Pal

Abstract
Borel–Serre proved that for a number ring R R with fraction field K K , the symplectic group Sp 2 ​ n ​ ( R ) \mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R) is a virtual duality group of degree quadratic in n n , and that the symplectic Steinberg module St 2 ​ n ω ​ ( K ) \mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K) is its dualizing module. We construct a projective resolution of this symplectic Steinberg module as an Sp 2 ​ n ​ ( R ) \mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R) -representation, that is similar in form to a resolution of Lee–Szczarba for the special linear group, but whose construction is more involved. When R R is a Euclidean number ring, we use this resolution to compute the top degree cohomology of principal level- p p congruence subgroups of Sp 2 ​ n ​ ( R ) \mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R) , for primes p ∈ R p\in R such that the natural map R × → ( R / ( p ) ) × R^{\times}\to(R/(p))^{\times} is surjective.

Introduction

The cohomology of arithmetic groups plays a fundamental role in algebraic K-theory and number theory. Examples of arithmetic groups include special linear and symplectic groups over a number ring and their finite-index subgroups.
An important class of finite-index subgroups are
principal congruence subgroups
. These are the kernels
\Gamma_{n}(J)
and
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(J)
of the maps
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)\to\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R/J)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R/J)
, respectively, that reduce coefficients mod
J
, where
J\subset R
is an ideal such that
R/J
is finite. When
J=(p)
is a principal ideal, we will denote
\Gamma_{n}(J)
and
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(J)
simply as
\Gamma_{n}(p)
and
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)
, respectively. In this paper we construct a resolution that enables us to compute certain cohomology groups of finite-index subgroups of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
for
R
a number ring. This resolution is similar in form to one due to Lee–Szczarba in
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, but whose construction is more involved and requires new ideas. We also use this resolution to compute the top degree cohomology of some principal congruence subgroups
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)\subset\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
when
R
is Euclidean.

Borel-Serre duality.

Borel–Serre
[
3
]
proved that when
R
is a number ring and
\Gamma
is a finite-index subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
or
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
, then
\Gamma
is a rational duality group of some dimension
\nu
, which implies that there are natural isomorphisms of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\nu-i}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{H}_{i}(\Gamma;\mathfrak{D}\otimes\mathbb{Q})\text{ for all }i

for a
\Gamma
-module
\mathfrak{D}
called the
dualizing module
. This statement also holds with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients if
\Gamma
is torsion-free.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\nu}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{H}_{0}(\Gamma;\mathfrak{D}\otimes\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathfrak{D}\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\Gamma},

where the subscript
\Gamma
indicates that we are taking
\Gamma
-coinvariants.

Suppose
R
has fraction field
K
. For the special linear group
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
, the degree
\nu
is given by

\displaystyle\nu=r\binom{n+1}{2}+cn^{2}-n-r-c+1

and for
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
,
\nu
is given by

\displaystyle\nu=r(n^{2}+n)+2cn^{2}-n

where

•

r
is the number of embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}

•

c
is the number of pairs of complex conjugate embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
that do not factor through
\mathbb{R}
.

Steinberg modules.

In the case of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
for
R
a number ring, the dualizing module
\mathfrak{D}
has a beautiful combinatorial description.
For
K
a field, let
\mathbb{T}_{n}(K)
be the
Tits building
for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(K)
, that is, the simplicial complex whose
k
-simplices are flags of vector subspaces of
K^{n}

\displaystyle 0\subsetneq V_{0}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq V_{k}\subsetneq K^{n}

The Solomon–Tits theorem
[
14
,
5
]
says that
\mathbb{T}_{n}(K)
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres of dimension
(n-2)
. The
Steinberg module
for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(K)
, denoted
\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)
, is
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n-2}(\mathbb{T}_{n}(K))
.
The action of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(K)
(and its subgroups) on
\mathbb{T}_{n}(K)
descends to an action on
\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)
. When

is a finite-dimensional vector space over

, we will use
\mathbb{T}(V)
and
\mathrm{St}(V)
, respectively, to denote the Tits building and the Steinberg module associated to
V
. Thus
\mathbb{T}(K^{n})\coloneq\mathbb{T}_{n}(K)
and
\mathrm{St}(K^{n})\coloneq\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)
.

When
K
is the field of fractions of a number ring
R
, Borel–Serre
[
3
]
proved that the dualizing module for a finite-index subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
is
\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)
.

We have similar notions for symplectic groups. For
K
a field, let
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
be the
symplectic Tits building
for
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(K)
, that is, the simplicial complex whose
k
-simplices are flags of isotropic subspaces of
K^{2n}
(i.e. subspaces where the standard symplectic form
\omega
on

restricts to 0 – see Definition
2.1
)

\displaystyle 0\subsetneq V_{0}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq V_{k}\subset K^{2n}

The Solomon–Tits theorem
[
14
,
5
]
says that
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres of dimension
(n-1)
. The
symplectic Steinberg module
for
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(K)
, denoted
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
, is
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}_{n-1}(\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K))
. The action of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(K)
and its subgroups on
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
descends to an action on
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
. When
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
K
with a non-degenerate alternating bilinear form
\omega
, we will use
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}(V)
and
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)

V
. Thus
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}(K^{2n})\coloneq\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
and
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(K^{2n})\coloneq\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
.

When
K
is the field of fractions of a number ring
R
, Borel–Serre
[

]
proved that the dualizing module for a finite-index subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)

\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)

The Sharbly resolution.

One way to compute the twisted homology groups
\mathrm{H}_{i}(\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R);\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})
and
\mathrm{H}_{i}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R);\mathrm{St}_{2n}^{\omega}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})
is by constructing projective resolutions of
\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathrm{St}_{2n}^{\omega}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
over
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
and
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
, respectively, and computing homology after taking the coinvariants of these resolutions under the respective group actions. In practice, for computational feasibility, one needs the coinvariants of the terms of the resolution to not be too large.

In
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, Lee–Szczarba constructed an
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)
-resolution of
\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)
, for
K
the field of fractions of a PID
R
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lee–Szczarba,
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
{R}
be a PID, and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
V
is a vector field over
K
of dimension
n
.
Then we have a resolution of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SL}_{n}({R})]
-modules, that we will refer to as the
Sharbly resolution of
\mathrm{St}(V)

\displaystyle\dots\to\mathrm{Sh}_{k}(V)\to\dots\to\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(V)\to\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(V)\to\mathrm{St}(V)\to 0

where the degree
k
term
\mathrm{Sh}_{k}(V)
is the quotient of the free abelian group on ordered spanning sets of lines of
V
of size
n+k
, quotiented by the relation that

\displaystyle[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{n+k}\rangle]=(-1)^{\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)}[\langle\vec{v}_{\sigma(1)}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{\sigma({n+k})}\rangle]

for any permutation
\sigma
.
Furthermore, if
{R}
has finitely many units, then tensoring the above resolution with
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
in fact yields a resolution of projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{SL}_{n}({R})]
-modules.
See Proposition
2.7
for a description of the differential on the resolution.

Lee–Szczarba used this resolution to prove the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Lee–Szczarba,
[
11
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
)
.

1.

Let
R
be a Euclidean domain with multiplicative norm, and
K
its field of fractions. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{0}(\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R);\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})=(\mathrm{St}_{n}(K)\otimes\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{SL}_{n}(R)}\cong 0

2.

For
n\geq 3
, we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{0}(\Gamma_{n}(3);\mathrm{St}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}))=(\mathrm{St}_{n}(\mathbb{Q}))_{\Gamma_{n}(3)}\cong\mathrm{St}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{3})

The proof for the second statement crucially uses the fact that the map
\mathbb{Z}^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times}
is surjective. Indeed, the above statement is known to be false for primes
p>3
(See for instance, Miller–Patzt–Putman
[
12
, Theorem A]
).

In higher degrees of homology, the groups in Lee–Szczarba’s resolution seem too large to be practical for computations (see
[
11
, Section 4]
). However, recent work by Ash–Miller–Patzt
[
1
, Theorem A]
used their resolution to prove the existence of a Hopf algebra structure on the rational cohomology of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Independently, Brown–Chan–Galatius–Payne
[
4
, Theorem 1.7]
established a Hopf algebra structure on these groups using completely different methods.

A Sharbly analogue for the symplectic group.

In this paper we construct a resolution of the symplectic Steinberg module that is in some sense analogous to Lee–Szczarba’s construction from Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with field of fractions
K
. There is a resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)]
-modules, where the degree
d
term of the resolution is given by

\displaystyle\bigoplus\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m}),

where the direct sum ranges over (ordered) direct sum decompositions

\displaystyle V=W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}

of
V
into perpendicular symplectic subspaces,
\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{i}}(W_{i})
is the
k_{i}
-th term in the Sharbly resolution for
W_{i}
, and

\displaystyle d=k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m

The resolution has the property that if
R
has finitely many units, then tensoring with
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
gives a resolution of projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)]
-modules. (For a description of the differential on the resolution, see Theorem
2.9
).

As a corollary, we obtain a presentation for the symplectic Steinberg module. A similar presentation was constructed by Tóth
[
15
, Theorem 2]
. Tóth in fact constructs a presentation of Steinberg modules over many more Chevalley groups and uses it to deduce a cyclicity result for these Steinberg modules, thus generalizing results of Ash–Rudolph
[
2
]
and Gunnells
[
9
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with field of fractions
K
. There is a presentation of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
over
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)]
:

\displaystyle V_{1,2}\oplus V_{1,1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}V_{0}\to\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)

where
V_{0},V_{1,2},V_{1,2}
are as follows:

•

V_{0}
is the direct sum of all terms of
\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{n})
, where
W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{n}=V
is a decomposition of
V
into mutually perpendicular genus 1 symplectic subspaces.

•

V_{1,1}
is the direct sum of terms of the form
\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(W_{i})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{n})
, where
W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{n}=V
is a decomposition of
V
into mutually perpendicular genus 1 symplectic subspaces.

•

V_{1,2}
is the direct sum over all terms of the form
\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(W_{n-1})
, where
V=W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{n-1}
is a decomposition into mutually perpendicular subspaces, where all but one of the
W_{i}
have genus 1 and the remaining one has genus 2.

The map
\partial_{1}
is as follows:

•

[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\in V_{1,1}
maps to the sum

\displaystyle\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right)-\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right)

\displaystyle+\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\otimes\dots[\langle\vec{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle]\otimes\dots\otimes[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right).

•

[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{i,0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i,1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i,2}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i,3}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]\in V_{1,2}
maps to a sum of ’omit terms’ as defined in Proposition
3.12
:

\displaystyle(-1)^{\nu}[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{x}_{i}\rangle][\langle\vec{y}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{z}_{i}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]

where
\vec{w}_{i},\vec{x}_{i}
are a subset of the vectors
\vec{v}_{i,0},\vec{v}_{i,1},\vec{v}_{i,2},\vec{v}_{i,3}
spanning a genus 1 subspace of
W_{i}
, and
\vec{y}_{i}
and
\vec{z}_{i}
are the projections of the other two vectors onto its perpendicular subspace. The sign
\nu
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\nu=j_{1}+j_{2}+n-1-i

where
j_{1},j_{2}
are the indices of the
\vec{v}_{i,t}
that correspond to
\vec{w}_{i}
and
\vec{x}_{i}
.

If
R
has finitely many units, then tensoring with
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
gives a presentation of projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)]
-modules.

We remark here that though this presentation is quite general, it comes at the cost of its groups being fairly large in many cases, and it is very hard to construct small presentations in general. In work of Brück–Patzt–Sroka
[
6
]
, they give a presentation for the symplectic Steinberg module
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{Q})
over
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})
where it is much easier to calculate coinvariants. They used this presentation to show the vanishing of the rational cohomology groups
\mathrm{H}^{n^{2}-1}(\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z});\mathbb{Q})
for
n\geq 2
. Their ideas were inspired by Church–Putman’s proof in
[
8
]
of the Bykovskii presentation for
\mathrm{St}_{n}(\mathbb{Q})
, where they relate the construction of such presentations to the high connectivity of suitably defined simplicial complexes. These complexes tend to get highly intricate and technical, and it is not clear whether it is feasible to carry out these constructions for rings other than
\mathbb{Z}
. The presentation from Corollary
1.4
can be thought of as a weaker analogue of Brück–Patzt–Sroka’s presentation in
[
6
]
, but for symplectic Steinberg modules over arbitrary fields.

When
R
is a Euclidean domain and
K
its field of fractions, we use the presentation from Corollary
1.4
to compute the coinvariants of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
by the level
p
principal congruence subgroup of
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
for certain primes
p\in R
. Our result is as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
R
be a Euclidean domain and
K
its field of fractions. Let
\mathbb{F}=R/(p)
for a prime
p\in R
. Suppose the map
R^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
is surjective. Then for all
n\geq 1
, we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle(\mathrm{St}_{2n}^{\omega}(K))_{\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)}\cong\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})

Combined with Borel-Serre duality
[
3
]
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
R
be a Euclidean number ring with fraction field
K
, and
p\in R
a prime. Let
\mathbb{F}
be the quotient field
R/(p)
. If the natural map
R^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
is surjective, then we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\nu}(\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p);\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})\otimes\mathbb{Q}

where
\nu
is given by:

\displaystyle\nu=r(n^{2}+n)+2cn^{2}-n

where

•

r
is the number of embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}

•

c
is the number of pairs of complex conjugate embeddings
K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
that do not factor through
\mathbb{R}
.

If
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)
is torsion-free, this result holds integrally, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{\nu}(\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})

There are many examples of pairs of rings and primes
(R,p)
that satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.5
, including:
(\mathbb{Z},3),(\mathbb{Z}[\iota],1+2\iota),(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}],1+\sqrt{-2})
,
(\mathbb{Z}[\omega],2\omega+1)
,
(\mathbb{Z}[\omega],3\omega+1)
,
(\mathbb{Z}[\omega],3\omega+2)
, and so on.
A simple way to generate such examples is to take a Euclidean domain
R
that has multiplicative norm, and take an element
p\in R
with minimal norm such that
p
is not a unit. Any such
p
will necessarily be prime, and will satisfy the conditions of Theorem
1.5
.

We expect that it is also possible to use the presentation from Corollary
1.4
to show vanishing of the coinvariants
(\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{Q}))_{\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z})}
in a similar way to Lee–Szczarba’s result in
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
, but we have not pursued this in the present article. This result is already known, for example, by work of Gunnells
[
9
]
, with a new proof via studying group actions on simplicial complexes by Brück–Sroka
[
7
]
.

Proof outline.

An obstruction to constructing an analogue of Lee–Szczarba’s resolution from Theorem
1.1
is that there is no clear way to generalize their proof to the symplectic group. We overcome this obstruction by studying a genus filtration on a simplicial complex closely related to the Tits buildings. The associated spectral sequence yields a finite resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
involving (symplectic) Steinberg modules of vector spaces of strictly smaller dimension, which may be a result of independent interest. The combinatorial arguments used to analyze the associated spectral sequence are inspired by methods used in Section 10 of
[
6
]
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
K
be a field and let
V
be a symplectic
K
-vector space of genus
n
. We have a resolution of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(K)]
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathrm{St}(V)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=n-1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})\longrightarrow\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)\to 0

where the direct sums are taken over symplectic subspaces of
V
, and
g(W)
denotes the genus of a symplectic subspace
W\subset V
.

In work in preparation, Miller–Scalamandre–Sroka proved that the Koszul dual of an equivariant ring built out of symplectic Steinberg modules (see
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
) are the even Steinberg modules for the special linear group. From this perspective, the resolution constructed in Proposition
1.7
can be viewed as a Koszul resolution. Likely, one can use Proposition
1.7
to give an alternative derivation of Miller–Scalamandre–Sroka’s computation of the Koszul dual of symplectic Steinberg modules.

Proposition
1.7
is proved in Section
2.3
, and a description of the differential on it is given in Proposition
3.7
.

The above resolution then allows us to inductively assemble resolutions of its individual terms to get a resolution of the symplectic Steinberg module. The process of assembling resolutions is described in Section
3.1
, and uses a fairly standard homological algebra argument.

The next obstruction then is to get an explicit description of the differential on this resolution. This is a fairly involved process – owing to the large number of indices involved in the description of the groups of the resolution and the number of maps that arise from the assembly process described in Section
3.1
– and takes up most of Section
3
. See Theorem
2.9
for a description of the differential.

Theorem
1.5
is proved in Section
4
. We outline the proof as follows: Suppose
\mathbb{F}
is the quotient of a Euclidean domain
R
by a prime
p\in R
and let
K
be the field of fractions of
R
. There is a map
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)\to\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
that intersects an isotropic subspace of
K^{2n}
with
R^{2n}
, and then reduces it mod
p
. Passing to homology, and observing that the map is
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)
-invariant (with
\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)
acting trivially on the codomain), we get a map

\displaystyle(\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K))_{\Gamma^{\omega}_{2n}(p)}\to\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})

It is not hard to see that this map is surjective. We use the presentation of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})
given by our resolution to show that this map is in fact an isomorphism when all units of
\mathbb{F}
can be lifted to units of
R
. This property is crucial in our proof, and in fact this map is known to not be injective when for example,
R=\mathbb{Z}
and
p>3
, by forthcoming work of Capovilla-Searle.

Our proof requires a cyclicity result for
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
as an
\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)
-module when
R
is a Euclidean domain, which was proven by Gunnells
[
9
, Theorem 4.11]
, with a new proof using high-connectivity of certain simplicial complexes later given for the case
R=\mathbb{Z}
by Brück–Sroka
[
7
]
.

is

if it is equipped with a symplectic bilinear form
\omega
, i.e.
\omega
is bilinear, alternating, and non-degenerate.
A finite dimensional symplectic vector space must necessarily have even dimension.
These conditions on
\omega
are equivalent to the condition that there is a basis
\{\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{\bar{1}},\vec{e}_{2},\vec{e}_{\bar{2}},\dots,\vec{e}_{n},\vec{e}_{\bar{n}}\}
for the bilinear form
\omega
such that for
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\omega(\vec{e}_{i},\vec{e}_{j})=0=\omega(\vec{e}_{\bar{i}},\vec{e}_{\bar{j}}),

\displaystyle\omega(\vec{e}_{i},\vec{e}_{\bar{j}})=0=\omega(\vec{e}_{\bar{j}},\vec{e}_{i})\text{ for }i\neq j,

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\omega(\vec{e}_{i},\vec{e}_{\bar{i}})=-\omega(\vec{e}_{\bar{i}},\vec{e}_{i})=1

•

Symplectic Bases.

For a symplectic
K
-vector space
V
, we call a set
\{\vec{v}_{1},\vec{v}_{\bar{1}},\dots,\vec{v}_{n},\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\}\subset V
a
symplectic basis
if there is a symplectic (i.e., form-preserving) automorphism
V\to V
that sends
\vec{e}_{i}\mapsto\vec{v}_{i}
and
\vec{e}_{\bar{i}}\mapsto\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
We use
\mathrm{Sp}(V)
to denote the group of all such symplectic automorphisms of
V
.

•

Radical of a Subspace.
Given a symplectic
K
-vector space
(V,\omega)
and a subspace
W\subset V
, the
radical of
W
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{rad}(W):=\{\vec{v}\in W\text{ such that }\omega(\vec{v},\vec{w})=0\hskip 7.11317pt\forall\vec{w}\in W\}

•

Genus of a Subspace.
Given a symplectic vector space
V
of dimension
2n
, we say
V
has
genus

n
.
In general, for a subspace
W\subset V
, the
genus of
W
is half the rank of the matrix of the restriction of the form
\omega|_{W}
.

Later we will need to index certain sums by symplectic subspaces of
V
(i.e. subspaces
W
where the restriction of the form
\omega|_{W}
to
W
is non-degenerate). For this reason, we shall use
g(W)
to denote the genus of a symplectic vector subspace
W\subset V
.

•

Isotropic Subspaces.
For a symplectic
K
-vector space
V
, a subspace
J\subset V
is
isotropic
if the restriction of the form
\omega|_{J}
is identically zero.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
V
be a genus
n
symplectic vector space over a field
K
. Suppose
W
is a symplectic subspace of
V
of genus
k
. Then
W^{\perp}
is a symplectic subspace of genus
n-k
, and

.

We shall also sometimes need to work with symplectic spaces over PIDs, as opposed to fields. For this we make the following definition.

(Solomon–Tits,
[

,

We now state a slight variant of Lee–Szczarba’s construction of a

-resolution of the Steinberg module.

Proposition 2.7

(Lee–Szczarba,
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
{R}
be a PID, and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
V
is a vector field over
K
of dimension
n
.
Then we have a resolution of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SL}_{n}({R})]
-modules, that we will refer to as the
Sharbly resolution of
\mathrm{St}(V)

\displaystyle\dots\to\mathrm{Sh}_{k}(V)\to\dots\to\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(V)\to\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(V)\to\mathrm{St}(V)\to 0

where:

•

The degree
k

is the quotient of the free abelian group on ordered spanning sets of lines of
V
of size

, quotiented by the relation that

\displaystyle[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{n+k}\rangle]=(-1)^{\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)}[\langle\vec{v}_{\sigma(1)}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{\sigma_{n+k}}\rangle]

.

•

For

maps a set of vectors
[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{n+k}\rangle]
to the usual sum where we omit one vector at a time – if omitting a vector results in the span becoming strictly smaller than

•

The map
\mathrm{Sh}_{0}(V)\to\mathrm{St}(V)
maps a set of vectors
[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle]
to the apartment class determined by the basis
\vec{v}_{1},\dots,\vec{v}_{n}
.

Furthermore, if
{R}
has finitely many units, then tensoring the above resolution with
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
in fact yields a resolution of projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{SL}_{n}({R})]
-modules.

Lee–Szczarba’s original version dealt with ordered sets of unimodular vectors of

as opposed to lines of

. But the version that uses lines can be shown via the same proof. The projectivity statement can be shown by using, for example,
[

Notation 2.8
.

We will denote the tensor products

We can now state our constructed resolution of the symplectic Steinberg module. To avoid clutter, we will write a Sharbly
[\langle v_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle v_{k}\rangle]
simply as

.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with field of fractions
K
. Suppose
V
is a symplectic
K
-vector space of genus
n
. Then we have a resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)
of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(R)]
-modules such that:

1.

The degree-

\displaystyle\bigoplus\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m}),

where the direct sum ranges over (ordered) direct sum decompositions

\displaystyle V=W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}

of

is the

\displaystyle d=k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m

Here

2.

Suppose that

\displaystyle[{v}^{1}_{0},{v}^{1}_{1},\dots,{v}^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[{v}^{m}_{0},{v}^{m}_{1},\dots,{v}^{m}_{d_{m}}]\in\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})

is a simple tensor in one of the terms of the resolution. Then the image of the differential on this term is the sum of the following two types of terms:

•

Omit-terms:
Suppose

is such that the span of
{v}^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{{v}^{j}_{i}},\dots,{v}^{j}_{d_{j}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{d_{1}-1+\dots+d_{j-1}-1}(-1)^{i}[v^{1}_{0},v^{1}_{1},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i}},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},v^{m}_{1},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

•

.
Let

\displaystyle\langle w^{j}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle w^{j}_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle v^{j}_{i_{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle v^{j}_{i_{p}}\rangle

respectively onto

.
Then the

corresponding to these vectors is

\displaystyle\hskip-30.00005pt(-1)^{\nu}[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{0}}},v^{j}_{i_{0}+1},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{1}}},\dots,v^{j}_{i_{p}-1},\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{p}}},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}]\otimes[w^{j}_{0},\dots w^{j}_{p}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

where

2.2
Simplicial Complexes Associated to Symplectic Vector Spaces

Here and throughout the rest of this paper, we will define simplicial complexes by specifying that their simplices are certain sets.
What we mean by this is that the
k
-simplices are such sets containing
(k+1)
-elements, and the face relations between simplices are simply inclusions of sets.

Definition 2.10
.

•

For a poset
\mathcal{P}
, let
|\mathcal{P}|
denote its geometric realization. Thus
|\mathcal{P}|
is the simplicial complex whose vertices correspond to elements of
\mathcal{P}
, and
k
-simplices correspond to length
(k+1)
ordered chains
p_{0}<p_{1}<\dots<p_{k}
of elements in
\mathcal{P}
.
For an order-preserving map of posets
f:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
, let
|f|:|\mathcal{P}|\to|\mathcal{Q}|
denote the corresponding map on geometric realizations.

•

Suppose
X
is a simplicial complex. We shall use
\mathcal{P}(X)
to denote the associated poset, i.e. the elements of
\mathcal{P}(X)
correspond to simplices of
X
, and the order relation comes from face relations.

•

Suppose
f:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
is an order-preserving map of posets. For an element
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, let the
downward fiber of
q
, denoted
f_{\leq q}
, be the subposet of
\mathcal{P}
consisting of all elements
p
such that
f(p)\leq q
.

Remark 2.11
.

For a simplicial complex
\mathbb{X}
, we have
|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})|
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{X}
. Specifically, we have a simplicial isomorphism
|\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{X})|\cong\mathrm{sd}(\mathbb{X})
, where
\mathrm{sd}(\mathbb{X})
denotes the barycentric subdivision of
\mathbb{X}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
K
be a field, and
V
a symplectic
K
-vector space.

1.

Let
\mathcal{K}(V)
be the simplicial complex whose vertices correspond to lines in
V
, and such that every finite set of vertices forms a simplex.

2.

Let
\mathcal{L}(V)
be the subcomplex of
\mathcal{K}(V)
consisting of those simplices whose vertices span a
proper
subspace of
V
.

3.

Let
\mathcal{I}^{g}(V)
be the subcomplex consisting of simplices spanning a genus
\leq g
subspace.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
V
be a symplectic vector space over a field
K
of genus
n
.

1.

\mathcal{K}(V)
is contractible.

2.

\mathcal{L}(V)
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{T}(V)
.

3.

\mathcal{I}^{0}(V)
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}(V)
.

Proof.

1.

Pick any vertex
v
in
\mathcal{K}(V)
, and note that
\sigma\cup\{v\}
is a simplex in
\mathcal{K}(V)
for any simplex
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}(V)
. Thus
\mathcal{K}(V)
is a cone on
v
and hence contractible.

2.

Define a map of posets
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}(V))\to\mathcal{T}(V)
that sends a simplex
\sigma=\{\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{k}\rangle\}
to the span of its vertices
\langle\vec{v}_{0},\dots,\vec{v}_{k}\rangle
. Note that this is an order-preserving map, and that for any subspace
W\subset V
the downward fiber
f_{\leq W}
can be identified with
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(W))
, and is hence contractible. Hence by Quillen’s Fiber Lemma (see for example,
[
10
, Proposition 2.5]
),
|f|
is a homotopy equivalence.

3.

This proof is exactly similar to the previous one. We have a map of posets
f:\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(V))\to\mathcal{T}^{\omega}(V)
that sends a simplex of
\mathcal{I}^{0}(V)
to the span of the set of lines corresponding to its vertices. For any isotropic subspace
J
of
V
, its downward fiber
f_{\leq J}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}(J))
, and is hence contractible. Thus
|f|
is a homotopy equivalence.

∎

2.3
A Resolution of (Symplectic) Steinberg Modules

Now we will prove Proposition
1.7
, which we restate here for convenience.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
K
be a field, and
V
a symplectic

-vector space of genus

. We have a resolution of

-modules

0\rightarrow\mathrm{St}(V)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=n-1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})\longrightarrow\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)\to 0

(1)

where the direct sums are taken over symplectic subspaces of

We shall prove Proposition

defined in Definition

To do this, we need to determine the relative homology groups

.
Our result is as follows.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose
V
is a genus
n

K
. For each

is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{k}(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{W\subset V\text{ symplectic, }g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})&k=n+g\\

We will prove this proposition by defining certain intermediate complexes
\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)
such that
\mathcal{I}^{g}(V)\subset\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)\subset\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V)
, and analyzing the homologies of the pairs
(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V))
and
(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
.

Definition 2.16
.

Suppose
V
is a symplectic
K
-vector space of genus
n
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)
be the subcomplex consisting of
\mathcal{I}^{g}(V)

.

Thus we have

.

Example 1
.

Let
V=\langle\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{\bar{1}},\dots,\vec{e}_{n},\vec{e}_{\bar{n}}\rangle
. The simplex
\{\langle\vec{e}_{1}+\vec{e}_{2}\rangle,\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{2}}\rangle\}

\mathcal{I}^{1}(V)
but not in

First we analyse the quotient
\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V)/\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)
.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
V
be a symplectic
K
-vector space of genus
n
. For

,
\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V)/\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)

Our proof will make use of the following Lemma from Miller–Patzt–Wilson (
[
13
]
):

Lemma 2.18

(Miller–Patzt–Wilson
[
13
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Suppose
X
is a simplicial complex with a subcomplex Y, and suppose there is a set of simplices
S
of
X
such that the following conditions are met:

1.

Suppose
\sigma
is a simplex in
X

\sigma
is a simplex in

\sigma

.

2.

If
\sigma
and
\tau

Then

\displaystyle X/Y\simeq\bigvee_{\sigma\in S}\Sigma^{\dim\sigma+1}\mathrm{Link}_{X}(\sigma).

To prove Lemma
2.17
, we shall construct a filtration

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)=F_{2g+2}\subset F_{2g+3}\subset...\subset F_{m}\subset...\subset\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\geq 2g+2}F_{i}=\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V)

and show that each successive quotient

is contractible.

We make some definitions as follows.

Notation 2.19
.

•

For a simplex
\sigma
in
\mathcal{K}(V)
, let

be the number of vertices in
\sigma
. (Thus

=

).

•

Let
\mathrm{rad}(\sigma)
denote the radical of the subspace spanned by the vectors of
\sigma
, and let

be the number of vertices of
\sigma

\mathrm{rad}(\sigma)
.

Example 2
.

If
\sigma=\{\langle\vec{e}_{1}+\vec{e}_{2}\rangle,\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{2}}\rangle\}
, then

. The radical of the subspace
\langle\vec{e}_{1}+\vec{e}_{2},\vec{e}_{\bar{1}},\vec{e}_{\bar{2}}\rangle
is
\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{1}}-\vec{e}_{\bar{2}}\rangle
, and so
\mathrm{rad}(\sigma)=\langle\vec{e}_{\bar{1}}-\vec{e}_{\bar{2}}\rangle
. Since no vertices of

Remark 2.21
.

F_{i}
is closed under taking faces because both
\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)
and the set

Proof of Lemma

.

We shall first show that

is contractible for each

.
We will use Lemma

. Let

1.

Suppose

First we show that if

, then no face of it is in

.

If

, then this implies that
\sigma\in Y
, and since all faces of

. So assume that

\sigma\in Y
.
Suppose that some face
\tau

and
\tau\not\in\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)

Then by Remark (
2.21
), we have
n(\sigma)-n(\mathrm{rad}(\sigma))\geq n(\tau)-n(\mathrm{rad}(\tau))\geq i+1
. Since
\sigma\in Y=F_{i}=\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)\cup\{\sigma\in\mathcal{I}^{g+1}|n(\sigma)-n(\mathrm{rad}(\sigma))\leq i\}
, it follows that the only way that
\sigma
can be in

is if
\sigma\in\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V)
. But this gives us a contradiction. Thus no face of

can be in

.

Now suppose that no face of
\sigma
is in

. Suppose that

\tau
be the face of

.

To now apply Lemma (

), let us now examine the links of the simplices in

Now we will analyse the homology of the pair
(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
.

Lemma 2.22
.

Suppose
V
is a genus
n
symplectic vector space over a field
K
. For
g\geq 0
, the homology of the pair
(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
is concentrated in degree
n+g
, and the homology group in this degree is equal to

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})

where the direct sum is over symplectic subspaces
W\subset V
.

Proof.

The relative homology groups
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
are calculated from the chain complex whose chain groups are

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{C}_{m}(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V))}{\mathrm{C}_{m}(\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))}\cong\bigoplus_{p+q=m-1}\mathbb{Z}[\langle\vec{w}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{w}_{p}\rangle,\langle\vec{l}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{l}_{q}\rangle]

where the direct sum is over sets of vectors
\{\vec{w}_{0},\dots,\vec{w}_{p},\vec{l}_{0},\dots,\vec{l}_{q}\}
such that

\displaystyle\vec{w}_{0},\dots,\vec{w}_{p}\text{ span a symplectic subspace $W\subset V$ with $g(W)=g+1$}

\displaystyle\vec{l}_{0},\dots,\vec{l}_{q}\in W^{\perp}\text{ are mutually perpendicular vectors}

When we apply the differential to a term
[\langle\vec{w}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{w}_{p}\rangle,\langle\vec{l}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{l}_{q}\rangle]
and we omit a
\vec{w}_{i}
so that the span of
\vec{w}_{0},\dots,\widehat{\vec{w}}_{i},\dots,\vec{w}_{p}
is strictly smaller than
W
, then this lands us in
\mathrm{C}_{m-1}(\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
.
Thus the chain complex that computes
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
is quasi-isomorphic to the following sum across tensor products of chain complexes, shifted up by one degree:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{K}(W),\mathcal{L}(W))\otimes\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(W^{\perp}),\{*\})

That is, the chain group in degree
m
can be identified with

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1,p+q=m-1}\mathrm{C}_{p}(\mathcal{K}(W),\mathcal{L}(W))\otimes\mathrm{C}_{q}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(W^{\perp}),\{*\})

Consider the spectral sequence associated to this double complex. By Lemma
2.13
, each
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{K}(W),\mathcal{L}(W))
has homology equal to
\mathrm{St}(W)
in degree
2(g+1)-2+1=2g+1
, and 0 otherwise, so filtering the double complex in the
p
-direction gives us an
\mathrm{E}^{1}
page with

\displaystyle E^{1}_{p,q}=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{C}_{q}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(W^{\perp}))&\text{ if }p=2g+1\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Also by Lemma
2.13
, each
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(W^{\perp}),\{*\})
has homology concentrated in degree
n-(g+1)-1=n-g-2
equal to
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})
, so now filtering in the
q
-direction gives us an
\mathrm{E}^{2}
page with

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{2}_{p,q}=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})&\text{ if }p=2g+1\text{ and }q=n-g-2\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Thus the spectral sequence collapses on the
\mathrm{E}^{2}
page, and so the homology of this double complex is

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{g(W)=g+1}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})

in degree
(2g+1)+(n-g-2)=n+g-1
. Since we are considering a shift of this complex up by 1 degree, the result follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.15
.

Lemma
2.17
says that the homology of the pair
(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V))
is 0 in every degree. Thus if we consider the long exact sequence in homology associated to the triple
(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g+1}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))\cong\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g+\frac{1}{2}}(V),\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))

and so the result follows immediately from Lemma

.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.14
.

Take the spectral sequence associated to the filtration
\mathcal{I}^{0}(V)\subset\mathcal{I}^{1}(V)\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{I}^{n}(V)=\mathcal{K}(V)
.
The
\mathrm{E}^{1}
page of the sequence is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{1}_{p,q}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{H}_{p+q}(\mathcal{I}^{p}(V),\mathcal{I}^{p-1}(V))&p\geq 1\\
\mathrm{H}_{q}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(V))&p=0\end{cases}

Lemma
2.15
says that for

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{p+q}(\mathcal{I}^{p}(V),\mathcal{I}^{p-1}(V))=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{W\subset V\text{ symplectic, }g(W)=p}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})&p+q=n+p-1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Thus for
p\geq 1
, the only non-zero groups among
\mathrm{E}^{1}_{p,q}
are all in row
q=n-1
, given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{1}_{p,n-1}=\bigoplus_{W\subset V\text{symplectic, }g(W)=p}\mathrm{St}(W)\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})

In the
p=0
column the only nonzero entries are

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{1}_{0,0}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{0}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(V))\cong\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}^{1}_{0,n-1}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{n-1}(\mathcal{I}^{0}(V))\cong\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)

where these equalities follow from Lemma
2.13
.
The differential on the
\mathrm{E}^{1}
-page goes horizontally from
\mathrm{E}^{1}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{E}^{1}_{p-1,q}
. Since the
\mathrm{E}^{1}
page only has nonzero entries in the row
q=n-1
and at the entry

, we conclude that the spectral sequence has to collapse on page
\mathrm{E}^{2}
. Since
\mathcal{K}(V)
is contractible, the only nonzero entry on the
\mathrm{E}^{\infty}

\mathrm{E}^{\infty}_{0,0}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, the

\mathrm{E}^{2}

\mathrm{E}^{1}

∎

3
The Symplectic Sharbly Resolution

In this section we will prove Theorem
2.9
. It will be easier to state and prove a rational version of Theorem
2.9
where we tensor everything with
\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and assume that
R
has finitely many units, but the argument turns out to be essentially the same for the general case. For this reason, we will describe the proof in detail for the rational version, and then briefly describe how to handle the integral case in Section

3.1
Piecing projective resolutions together

In this subsection we deduce the groups we get in our symplectic Sharbly resolution:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let
V=M\otimes_{{R}}K
. There is a resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}
of projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Sp}(M)]
-modules, which we shall refer to as the
symplectic Sharbly resolution
, whose degree-
d
term is given by

\displaystyle\bigoplus\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})_{\mathbb{Q}},

where the direct sum ranges over (ordered) direct sum decompositions

\displaystyle V=W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}

of
V
into perpendicular symplectic subspaces,
\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{i}}(W_{i})
is the
k_{i}
-th term in the Sharbly resolution for
W_{i}
(as defined in Proposition
2.7
), and

\displaystyle d=k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m

with
d
and
n
fixed,
1\leq m\leq n
, and
k_{1},\dots k_{m}
varying in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

We proceed by induction on
n
. Note that we always have
1\leq m\leq n
. So for
n=1
, the only possibility is that
m=1
, and in this case we recover the usual Sharbly resolution for
V\cong K^{2}
over
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)=\mathrm{Sp}_{1}(K)
.
For the induction step, note that we have now established the following resolution:

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathrm{St}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=n-1}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{g(W)=1}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to 0

We will use this to obtain a projective
\mathbb{Q}[\mathrm{Sp}(M)]
-resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}
by taking projective resolutions of each term
\bigoplus\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and ‘piecing them together’.
To do this we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring. Suppose we have a resolution of left

-modules

\displaystyle\dots\xrightarrow{\partial_{3}}F_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}F_{0}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}}M_{g}\xrightarrow{d_{g}}M_{g-1}\xrightarrow{d_{g-1}}\dots\xrightarrow{d_{2}}M_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}M\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\dots\xrightarrow{\partial_{3}^{\prime}}F_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}^{\prime}}F_{1}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}^{\prime}}F_{0}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\partial_{0}^{\prime}}M_{g}\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle W_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}=W^{\perp}

To apply Lemma

\displaystyle F_{a,b}=\bigoplus\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{2}}(W_{2})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})_{\mathbb{Q}}

where the direct sum ranges over:

\displaystyle g(W)=a

\displaystyle W_{2}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}=W^{\perp}\text{ is a decomposition into symplectic subspaces}

\displaystyle k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m-(a-1)=b\implies k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m=a+b-1

Thus applying Lemma

gives us a resolution whose degree

implies that

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\dim W_{i}^{\prime}=\dim W_{i}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1}^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m}^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathrm{Sp}(W_{1})\times\dots\times\mathrm{Sp}(W_{m})}^{\mathrm{Sp}(V)}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})

where the direct sum ranges over all symplectic decompositions

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\dim W_{i}^{\prime}=\dim W_{i}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1}^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\dots\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m}^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Q}}

Lemma 3.8

Let

n

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\partial\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket=\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2g+1}(-1)^{i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]

The span of the vectors
\vec{v}_{0},\dots,\hat{\vec{v}}_{i},\dots,\vec{v}_{2g+1}
is a codimension 1 subspace of
\langle\vec{v}_{0},\dots,\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle
and must have one-dimensional radical. Let
\vec{a}_{i}
be a vector spanning this radical. The vector
\vec{a}_{i}
may or may not be present among the
\vec{v}_{j}
, for
j\neq i
.

Consider the simplex
[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]
, where we replace
\vec{v}_{i}
with
\vec{a}_{i}
. The span of these vectors has genus
g
, and so lies in
\mathcal{I}^{g}
. In particular, the boundary of this simplex forms a boundary in
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g})
.

From this we have the following equivalence:

\displaystyle(-1)^{i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]=

\displaystyle(-1)^{i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{a}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}(-1)^{j-1}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]

Now,
\vec{a}_{i}
lies in the span of
\vec{v}_{0},\dots,\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i},\dots,\vec{v}_{2g-1}
, and so is a linear combination of them. Take the minimal set of
\vec{v}_{j}
’s so that
\vec{a}_{i}
is a linear combination of them - in other words, take those
\vec{v}_{j}
’s that have non-zero coefficient when we write down
\vec{a}_{i}
as a linear combination of these vectors. For
\vec{v}_{j}
that lie in this minimal set, call the pair
(i,j)

good

If
(i,j)
is
not

[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]
has a span of dimension

, with a non-zero radical (since
\vec{a}_{i}
is in the radical), and thus must have genus
\leq g-1
, and thus this chain is in
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g-1}(V))
.

On the other hand, for
(i,j)
good, the span of
[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]
has genus
g
, and can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(-1)^{\sigma}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]*[\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle],

where
\sigma
is the sign of the permutation that takes

to the right end (Thus
\sigma=2g-i+1
if

, and

otherwise). In doing so, we have rewritten the simplex as a join of an apartment representative for a genus
g
symplectic space, and a vector perpendicular to it.

Note that the above shows that
[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]
spans a genus
g
subspace if and only if
(i,j)
is good. This implies
(i,j)
being good is equivalent to saying that
i\sim j
, and also that
\sim
is symmetric: i.e.

.

For each such pair
i,j
with
i\sim j
, (and suppose WLOG that
i<j

\displaystyle(-1)^{j-1}(-1)^{2g-i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]*[\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{i-1}(-1)^{2g-j+1}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]*[\langle\vec{a}_{j}\rangle]

\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{j}\rangle

\vec{v}_{0},\vec{v}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i},\dots,\vec{v}_{2g+1}

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{j}=c\vec{a}_{i}+\text{ a linear combination of }\{\vec{v}_{0},\dots,\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i},\vec{v}_{i+1},\dots,{\vec{v}_{j-1}},\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j},\dots,\vec{v}_{2g+1}\}

\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle=\langle\vec{a}_{j}\rangle

.
Thus we have

\displaystyle[\langle\vec{a}_{j}\rangle]-[\langle\vec{a}_{i}\rangle]=\llbracket\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}

And so we have the following equivalence on chains

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt\partial\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket=\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2g+1}(-1)^{i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2g+1}(-1)^{i}[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{a}}_{i}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle]

\displaystyle\quad\simeq\sum_{i\sim j}(-1)^{i+j}\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket*\llbracket\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle\rrbracket^{w}

as desired.

tells us that with respect to
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g}(V))
and its subcomplex
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g-1}(V))
, we have the following equivalence of chains:

\displaystyle\partial\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket\simeq\sum_{i\sim j}(-1)^{i+j}\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket*\llbracket\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}

where
\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle
are the projections of
\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{j}\rangle
respectively onto
W_{ij}^{\perp}\subset W
.

Using this, and the fact that
c*\llbracket\langle\vec{u}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{u}_{n-g-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\overline{n-g-1}}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}
is a chain in
\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g-1}(V))
for any chain
c\in\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{I}^{g-1}(V))
, we get that:

\displaystyle\partial(\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket*\llbracket\langle\vec{u}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{u}_{n-g-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\overline{n-g-1}}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\left(\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket\right)*\llbracket\langle\vec{u}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{u}_{n-g-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\overline{n-g-1}}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\sim j}(-1)^{i+j}\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket*\llbracket\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}*\llbracket\langle\vec{u}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{u}_{n-g-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\overline{n-g-1}}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i\sim j}(-1)^{i+j}\llbracket\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{j-1}\rangle,\langle\widehat{\vec{v}}_{j}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{2g+1}\rangle\rrbracket*\llbracket\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{j}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\bar{1}}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{u}_{n-g-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{u}_{\overline{n-g-1}}\rangle\rrbracket^{\omega}

This almost completes the proof – the only problem is that for a pair of indices
i<j
, the sign of the shuffle taking
\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle
and
\langle\vec{v}_{j}\rangle
to positions 0 and 1 is
(-1)^{i+j-1}
, not
(-1)^{i+j}
.
But note that this is not really an issue, because if we have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

\displaystyle\dots\xrightarrow{\partial_{k+1}}M_{k}\xrightarrow{\partial_{k}}M_{k-1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{k-1}}\dots

then replacing the
\partial_{k}
with
-\partial_{k}
does not change exactness.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

We chose to state the differential in Proposition
3.7
using the sign
(-1)^{i+j-1}
instead of
(-1)^{i+j}
to make results more consistent to state in Section
3.4
.

3.3
Preliminaries on the differential

In this section, we deduce preliminary information about the differential on the symplectic Sharbly resolution, before obtaining a full description of it in Section
3.4
.

We remark that we will be following the anticommutativity sign convention for chain maps between chain complexes, i.e. when we say that
g_{\bullet}:(\mathrm{C}^{1}_{\bullet},\partial^{1}_{\bullet})\to(\mathrm{C}_{\bullet}^{2},\partial^{2}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{k}\circ g_{k}+g_{k-1}\circ\partial^{1}_{k}:\mathrm{C}^{1}_{k}\to\mathrm{C}^{2}_{k-1}

is 0 for all
k\geq 1
.
In this convention, the differential on the tensor product of these chain complexes is simply the sum
\partial^{1}_{\bullet}+\partial^{2}_{\bullet}
, without having to take signs into consideration.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let
V=M\otimes_{R}K
. For
1\leq a\leq n-1
, let
F_{a,\bullet}
denote the resolution for
\bigoplus_{g(W)=a}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where the sum is taken over symplectic subspaces of
V
, and let
d^{i}_{0,-1}:F_{i,j}\to F_{i,j-1}
denote the differential on
F_{i,\bullet}
. Then, the degree
d
term in the resolution for
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}
is
\sum_{i+j=d+1}F_{i,j}
. For every
i
, and for
0<r\leq i-1
, there exist maps

\displaystyle d^{i}_{-r,r-1}:F_{i,j}\to F_{i-r,j+r-1}

such that

\sum_{r+s=t}d^{i-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{i}_{-r,r-1}=0

(3)

for every fixed

.
Given a choice of such maps, the sum of these maps gives a choice of differential for the resolution so obtained for
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

This is a direct consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let
V=M\otimes_{R}K
. For
1\leq a\leq n-1
, let
F_{a,\bullet}
denote the resolution for
\bigoplus_{g(W)=a}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where the sum is taken over symplectic subspaces of
V
, and let
d^{i}_{0,-1}:F_{i,j}\to F_{i,j-1}
denote the differential on
F_{i,\bullet}
. Define
F^{\prime}_{a,j}
as in Equation (
2
) above and let
\partial^{\prime}_{a}
denote the differentials
F^{\prime}_{a,j}\to F^{\prime}_{a,j-1}
. Then we have
F^{\prime}_{a,k}=\bigoplus_{a\leq i\leq\min(n,a+k)}F_{i,a+k-i}
. There exist maps of the form

\displaystyle d^{i}_{-r,r-1}:F_{i,j}\to F_{i-r,j+r-1}

where

\displaystyle 0<r\leq i-a

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=t}d^{i-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{i}_{-r,r-1}=0

for every fixed
t
.
On a summand
F_{i,j}
of
F^{\prime}_{a,k}
with
i\geq a
, the differential
\partial^{\prime}_{a}
is the sum of these maps
\sum_{0\leq r\leq i-a}d^{i}_{-r,r-1}
.

Proof.

Set
a=n-b
and induct on
b
, with the base case being
b=0
.
When
b=0
, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{n,\bullet}=F_{n,\bullet}

and

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{n}=d^{n}_{0,-1},

and the claim is trivially true.
So now assume the result is true for some
a=n-b\leq n
. We shall prove the assertion for
a-1=n-(b+1)
.

Note that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{a-1,k}=F_{a-1,k}\oplus F^{\prime}_{a,k-1}\text{ for }k\geq 1

and

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{a-1,0}=F_{a-1,0}

We have maps
\delta_{a}:F^{\prime}_{a,k}\to F_{a-1,k}
such that

d^{a-1}_{0,-1}\circ\delta_{a}+\delta_{a}\circ\partial^{\prime}_{a}=0

(4)

Recall that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{a,k}=\bigoplus_{a\leq i\leq\min(n,a+k)}F_{i,a+k-i}

Thus the existence of
\delta_{a}:F^{\prime}_{a,k}\to F_{a-1,k}
implies that for all
a\leq i\leq n
we have maps

\displaystyle d^{i}_{-(i-a+1),i-a}:F_{i,j}\to F_{a-1,j+i-a}

such that

\displaystyle\delta_{a}|_{F^{\prime}_{a,k}}=\sum_{a\leq i\leq\min(n,a+k)}d^{i}_{-(i-a+1),i-a}

Now Equation (
4
) implies that

\displaystyle 0=d^{a-1}_{0,-1}\circ d^{i}_{-(i-a+1),i-a}+\left(\sum_{r+s=i-a+1,s>0}d^{i-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{i}_{-r,r-1}\right):F_{i,j}\to F_{a-1,j+i-a-1}\hskip 14.22636pt(i\geq a)

This is equivalent to saying that

\sum_{r+s=i-a+1}d^{i-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{i}_{-r,r-1}=0\hskip 14.22636pt(i\geq a)

(5)

The differential
\partial^{\prime}_{a-1}
is given by:

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{a-1}|_{F_{a-1,k}}

\displaystyle=d^{a-1}_{0,-1}:F_{a-1,k}\to F_{a-1,k-1}

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{a-1}|_{F_{a,k}}

\displaystyle=\partial^{\prime}_{a}+\delta_{a}:F_{a,k}\to F_{a,k-1}\oplus F_{a-1,k}

It follows that on a term
F_{i,j}
with
i\geq a-1
, the differential
\partial^{\prime}_{a-1}
is the sum of maps

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq r\leq i-a+1}d^{i}_{-r,r-1}

It remains to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=t}d^{i-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{i}_{-r,r-1}=0

for every fixed
t
, and
i\geq a-1
.

When
i\geq a
and
t\leq i-a
, this holds by the inductive hypothesis on
F^{\prime}_{a,\bullet}
.
When
i=a-1
, this is just the assertion that
d^{a-1}_{0,-1}\circ d^{a-1}_{0,-1}=0
, which is true since
d^{a-1}_{0,-1}
is the differential on
F_{a-1,\bullet}
.
And finally, for
i\geq a
and
t=i-a+1
, this is precisely Equation (
5
).
∎

3.4
The Differential

We are now ready to state the differential on our resolution.

Keeping track of signs will be important in proving our result about the differential, so we first recall the convention for the differential on the tensor product of two chain complexes.
Suppose that
(\mathrm{C}^{1}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{1})
and
(\mathrm{C}^{2}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{2})
are two projective resolutions of
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
respectively over a ring
\mathcal{R}
. Then recall that the tensor product of these resolutions gives us a projective
\mathcal{R}
-resolution of
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
, with the differential given by:

\displaystyle\partial^{1}_{p}+(-1)^{p}\partial^{2}_{q}:\mathrm{C}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{C}_{p-1,q}\oplus\mathrm{C}_{p,q-1}

(6)

We shall in particular make use of the case when
M_{1},M_{2}
are equal to
\mathrm{St}(W)
for some symplectic vector space
W
, with the
(\mathrm{C}^{i}_{\bullet},\partial_{\bullet}^{i})
being the Sharbly resolution.
Note that the degree of a Sharbly term
[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{l}\rangle]
in the resolution for
\mathrm{St}(W)
is

\displaystyle(l+1)-\dim(W)

Since
W
is symplectic,
\dim(W)
is even, and so the degree has the same parity as
l+1
. Thus we don’t need to know the dimension of
W
to plug into (
6
).

Though we have to take signs into account when tensoring two Sharbly resolutions, we will still use the anticommutativity sign convention for chain maps between resolutions, as mentioned in the beginning of Section
3.3
. It will be important to keep track of which convention applies in which case as we proceed through our proof.

Now, recall from Proposition
3.1
that the degree
d
term in the symplectic Sharbly resolution is a direct sum over terms of the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})_{\mathbb{Q}}

where

\displaystyle W_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus W_{m}=V

is a decomposition of
V
into mutually perpendicular symplectic subspaces, and

\displaystyle d=k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}+n-m

Our differential will reduce the degree either by reducing one of the
k_{i}
’s by 1, or by increasing
m
by 1.
For brevity, in this section we shall denote a vector using lowercase letters (for eg,
v
instead of
\vec{v}
), and write simply
[{{v}_{0}},\dots,{{v}_{l}}]
instead of
[\langle\vec{v}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle\vec{v}_{l}\rangle]
to represent a Sharbly term. We will also use the convention that for a permutation
\sigma
,
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=0
if
\sigma
is an even permutation and
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=1
if
\sigma
is odd (as opposed to
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=\pm 1
).

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let
V=M\otimes_{R}K
. Suppose that

\displaystyle[{v}^{1}_{0},{v}^{1}_{1},\dots,{v}^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[{v}^{m}_{0},{v}^{m}_{1},\dots,{v}^{m}_{d_{m}}]\in\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})_{\mathbb{Q}}

is a simple tensor in the symplectic Sharbly resolution for
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(V)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then the image of the differential on this term is the sum of the following two types of terms:

•

Omit-terms:
Suppose
{v}^{j}_{i}
is such that the span of
{v}^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{{v}^{j}_{i}},\dots,{v}^{j}_{d_{j}}
is the same as the span of
v^{j}_{0},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}
. Then the
omit-term
corresponding to
v^{j}_{i}
is

\displaystyle(-1)^{d_{1}-1+\dots+d_{j-1}-1}(-1)^{i}[v^{1}_{0},v^{1}_{1},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i}},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},v^{m}_{1},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

•

Split-terms:
Suppose
v^{j}_{i_{0}},v^{j}_{i_{1}},\dots,v^{j}_{i_{p}}
are vectors such that
v^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{0}}},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{p}}},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}
spans a proper nonzero symplectic subspace
X_{j}
of
W_{j}
.
Let

\displaystyle\langle w^{j}_{0}\rangle,\dots,\langle w^{j}_{p}\rangle

be the projections of

\displaystyle\langle v^{j}_{i_{0}}\rangle,\dots,\langle v^{j}_{i_{p}}\rangle

respectively onto
X_{j}^{\perp}\subset W_{j}
.
Then the
split-term
corresponding to these vectors is

\displaystyle(-1)^{\nu}[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{j}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{0}}},v^{j}_{i_{0}+1,}\dots,v^{j}_{i_{p}-1,}\widehat{v^{j}_{i_{p}}},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}]\otimes[w^{j}_{0},\dots w^{j}_{p}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

with

\displaystyle\nu=(d_{1}-1+d_{2}-1+\dots+d_{j-1}-1)+pd_{j}+\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)+(d_{j+1}+d_{j+2}+\dots+d_{m})

(7)

where
\sigma
is the shuffle permutation that shuffles
v^{j}_{i_{0}},\dots,v^{j}_{i_{p}}
to the first
p+1
positions in
[v^{j}_{0},\dots,v^{j}_{d_{j}}]
.

In the case when the genus
n=1
, we are forced to have
m=1
, and we can only have omit-terms, in which case we recover the usual Sharbly resolution for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
.

We shall prove the general case by inducting on
n
. We will break the proof up into two lemmas.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let
V=M\otimes K
. Suppose Proposition
3.12
holds for all genus
<n
symplectic spaces.
Recall from Section
3.3
that
F_{a,\bullet}
denotes the resolution of
\bigoplus_{g(W)=a}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
d^{a}_{0,-1}
denotes the differential on it.
Then the differential
d^{a}_{0,-1}
on a term
[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]
is the sum of all the omit-terms on it, plus all the split-terms where
j>1
, i.e. the splitting is
not
taking place on
[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]
.

Proof.

Recall that
F_{a,\bullet}
is obtained by tensoring the resolutions for
\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
and adding them across all
W
with
g(W)=a
. Let
\partial_{1}
denote the differential on the Sharbly resolution for
\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
\partial_{2}
the differential on the symplectic Sharbly resolution of
\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then the differential on a term

\displaystyle[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

is

\displaystyle\partial_{1}+(-1)^{d_{1}-1}\partial_{2}

Now note that applying
\partial_{1}
gives us the omit-terms where the omitted vector is in
[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]
, and applying
(-1)^{d_{1}-1}\partial_{2}
gives us all the omit-terms and the split-terms where the omitted or split vectors are in one of the

.
∎

We now describe the maps
d^{a}_{-r,r-1}
described in Lemma
3.11
, for

.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
{R}
be a PID with finitely many units and
K
its field of fractions. Suppose
M\cong{R}^{2n}
is a symplectic
{R}
-module and let

. Recall from Section
3.3

F_{a,\bullet}
denotes the resolution of
\bigoplus_{g(W)=a}\mathrm{St}(W)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\mathrm{St}^{\omega}(W^{\perp})_{\mathbb{Q}}

denotes the differential on it. Suppose
[v^{1}_{0},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]\in\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{1}}(W_{1})_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathrm{Sh}_{k_{m}}(W_{m})_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a term in some

. Then for

, the map

\displaystyle d^{a}_{-r,r-1}:F_{a,\bullet}\to F_{a-r,\bullet+r-1}

\displaystyle(-1)^{\nu}[v^{1}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{1}_{i_{0}}},v^{1}_{i_{0}+1,}\dots,v^{1}_{i_{p}-1},\widehat{v^{1}_{i_{p}}},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}]\otimes[w^{1}_{0},\dots w^{1}_{p}]\otimes[v^{2}_{0},\dots,v^{2}_{d_{2}}]\otimes\dots\otimes[v^{m}_{0},\dots,v^{m}_{d_{m}}]

where

is given by Equation (
7

\displaystyle v^{1}_{0},\dots,\widehat{v^{1}_{i_{0}}},v^{1}_{i_{0}+1},\dots,v^{1}_{i_{p}-1},\widehat{v^{1}_{i_{p}}},\dots,v^{1}_{d_{1}}

span a genus
a-r
symplectic subspace of the genus
a
symplectic space
W_{1}
.

Proof.

We need to check that on setting
r=1
that the given description of the map

extends the differential described on Equation (

3
) is satisfied. It is straightforward to verify the former, so let us turn to showing that Equation (

\displaystyle\sum_{r+s=t}d^{a-r}_{-s,s-1}\circ d^{a}_{-r,r-1}=0

for every fixed

.
When

, this equation becomes

\displaystyle d^{a}_{0,-1}\circ d^{a}_{0,-1}=0

which holds since Lemma
3.13

Proof of Theorem

We now outline how to prove Theorem

Definition 4.4

(Tits building)
.

Let
{R}
be a commutative ring and let
V\cong{R}^{2n}
be equipped with the standard symplectic form. Define
\mathcal{T}^{\omega}(V)
to be the poset of isotropic summands of
V
, ordered by inclusion. Also, let
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}(V)
denote the geometric realization of
\mathcal{T}(V)
, viewed as a simplicial complex. For
n\geq 1
, we will write
\mathcal{T}^{\omega}_{2n}({R})=\mathcal{T}^{\omega}({R}^{2n})
and
\mathbb{T}^{\omega}_{2n}({R})=\mathbb{T}^{\omega}({R}^{2n})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
{R}
is a PID, and
K
its field of fractions. For
n\geq 1
, we have
\mathcal{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(R)\cong\mathcal{T}^{\omega}_{2n}(K)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that there is a bijection between subspaces of
K^{2n}
and direct summands of
{R}^{2n}
taking a subspace
V\subset K^{2n}
to
V\cap R^{2n}
and a direct summand
W\subset R^{2n}
to
W\otimes_{R}K
, and
V
is isotropic if and only if
W
is.
∎

For our proof we will need to know a cyclicity result for

when

is the field of fractions of a Euclidean domain, which is due to Gunnells
[

]

. We summarize this in the following lemma.

\displaystyle[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}+a\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{i+1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]

is equal to (up to sign)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}\rangle][\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{i+1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]

\displaystyle\text{}-[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}+a\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{i+1}}\rangle][\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}-a\vec{v}_{\overline{i+1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]

\displaystyle\text{}-[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{{i+1}}+a\vec{v}_{i}\rangle][\langle\vec{v}_{i+1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{i}}-a\vec{v}_{\overline{i+1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n-1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\overline{n-1}}\rangle]

(29)

Finally, suppose we have an element of
V_{1,1}

\displaystyle[\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{u}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]

If this element is non-zero, then the vectors
\vec{v}_{i},\vec{w}_{i}
must be linearly independent and thus have nontrivial pairing. We then have
\vec{u}_{i}=a\vec{v}_{i}+b\vec{w}_{i}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{F}
. Thus the image of
\partial_{1}
on this element is

\displaystyle\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right)-\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle a\vec{v}_{i}+b\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{w}_{i}\rangle]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\text{}+\left([\langle\vec{v}_{1}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{1}}\rangle]\dots[\langle a\vec{v}_{i}+b\vec{w}_{i}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{i}\rangle,]\dots[\langle\vec{v}_{n}\rangle,\langle\vec{v}_{\bar{n}}\rangle]\right)

(30)

Now using the fact that any symplectic basis of
\mathbb{F}^{2n}
lifts to a symplectic basis for
{R}^{2n}
, and that all units of
\mathbb{F}
lift to units of
{R}
, we see that the three sums (
28
), (
29
), and (
30
) we got above all lift to the relations (
25
), (
27
), and (
26
), respectively, described in Lemma
4.6
. This completes the proof.
∎
