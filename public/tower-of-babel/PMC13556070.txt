Evolution, hotspots, and future directions of global research on ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) care interventions: a visual analysis using CiteSpace and VOSviewer (2005–2025)
Authors: Ying Mei, Haixiao Zhang, Yinfeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVentilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in critically ill patients. This bibliometric study aimed to map the global research landscape and intellectual evolution of nursing care interventions for VAP from January 1, 2005 to December 31, 2025.MethodsPublications were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Scientometric analysis and visualization were performed using CiteSpace and VOSviewer to examine publication trends, collaboration networks (countries, institutions, authors), and thematic evolution through keyword co-occurrence, burst detection, and timeline analysis.ResultsAnnual publication output showed a steady increase, with a notable rise after 2022. Collaboration networks revealed distinct regional clusters centered in Europe/North America, Asia, and Latin America/Europe. The most cited publications were clinical practice guidelines. Research themes evolved from foundational epidemiology and risk factors to the development and evaluation of preventive “care bundles.” Recent frontiers emphasize implementation science, with “oral hygiene,” “management,” and “implementation” as current research hotspots.ConclusionsResearch on VAP nursing interventions has matured, solidifying care bundles as a core prevention strategy. The field is now shifting towards optimizing implementation and practical integration of evidence into clinical workflows. Future efforts should foster greater global and interdisciplinary collaboration to enhance the adaptability and impact of these interventions across diverse healthcare settings.

Introduction

Ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) is one of the most common healthcare-associated infections in patients receiving mechanical ventilation. It typically develops more than 48 hours after endotracheal intubation or tracheostomy [1, 2]. Despite advances in critical care, VAP remains a major contributor to prolonged ICU stay, increased healthcare costs, and higher mortality rates [3]. Epidemiological data show that the incidence of VAP varies significantly across countries and regions [4], yet the clinical management challenges it poses are shared globally.

The development of VAP is primarily linked to pathways such as bacterial colonization in the oropharynx followed by aspiration, and the leakage of subglottic secretions around the endotracheal tube cuff. As a key strategy to interrupt these infection routes, nursing interventions have gained increasing attention. With the rapid growth of published literature, traditional narrative reviews or systematic evaluations often focus on the effectiveness of single interventions, making it difficult to capture the overall evolution of research trends and emerging topics from a macro perspective. In contrast, bibliometric analysis combined with scientific knowledge mapping can quantitatively reveal the developmental trajectory and structural characteristics of a research field through visualization.

Although some bibliometric studies on VAP have been conducted, comprehensive visual analyses specifically focusing on the dimension of nursing interventions—and covering the post-pandemic era and the period of smart nursing transformation (2023–2025)—remain limited. Therefore, this study aims to use CiteSpace and VOSviewer to systematically analyze and explore relevant literature on VAP nursing interventions published between 2005 and 2025 in the Web of Science Core Collection. The goal is to uncover the global distribution of research efforts, identify key research themes across different periods, and detect potential future trends, thereby providing insights for clinical nursing management and research planning.

Data and methods

Data source and search strategy

To ensure the authority of the literature data and the completeness of citation analysis, this study used the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) as the data source. The search strategy combined subject terms with free-text keywords to optimize both recall and precision. The time span was set from January 1, 2005 to December 31, 2025. The specific search query was: (“ventilator-associated pneumonia” OR “ventilator associated pneumonia” OR “VAP”) AND (“nurs*” OR “nursing care” OR “nursing intervention*” OR “nursing practice*” OR “nurse-led” OR “bundle*”). Inclusion and exclusion criteria

To ensure the analysis focuses on high-quality scholarly publications, strict screening criteria were applied:

Document Type: Only “Articles” and “Reviews” were included. Conference abstracts, editorials, letters, corrections, and other non-research items were excluded.

Language: Only English-language publications were included.

Content Relevance: Titles and abstracts were manually reviewed to exclude studies that were not nursing-led, focused solely on pathophysiological mechanisms, or unrelated to nursing care in mechanically ventilated patients.

The final set of eligible records was exported in “Full Record and Cited References” format as a plain text file. Duplicate entries were removed before data import into the analysis tools.

Analysis tools and parameter settings

This study combined the strengths of VOSviewer (v1.6.19) in cluster visualization with those of CiteSpace (v6.2.R4) in time-based trend detection, allowing for complementary insights.

VOSviewer parameter settings

VOSviewer was used to construct collaboration networks among countries and institutions, as well as co-occurrence maps of keywords. The full counting method was applied. Node thresholds were adjusted based on term frequency to ensure map clarity. Clusters were visualized using the LinLog (modularity) layout algorithm, with different colors representing distinct clusters. Node size reflects term frequency, and line thickness indicates the strength of association (Total Link Strength).

CiteSpace parameter settings

CiteSpace was used to generate timeline views and detect burst terms, helping to identify emerging research fronts over time. The time span was set from 2005 to 2025 with one-year slices (Years Per Slice = 1). The node type was set to “Keyword.” A g-index threshold (k = 5) was applied to select high-impact nodes within each time slice. The Pathfinder and Pruning sliced networks algorithms were used to simplify and clarify network structure.

Initial exploration phase (2005–2008): The number of publications increased steadily from 4 to 31 per year, indicating a slow growth rate and an early stage of research development.

Steady growth phase (2009–2021): Publication output gradually rose from 32 to 69 articles per year. A brief decline occurred during 2014–2015, but overall, the body of research continued to expand.

In the early phase (2006–2012), burst keywords were mainly related to fundamental clinical issues, including “nosocomial infections”, “pneumonia”, “risk factors”, and “evidence based guidelines”. During the middle phase (2013–2020), bursts shifted toward practical interventions and quality enhancement, with rising prominence of terms such as “quality improvement”, “infection control”, “intervention”, and “care bundle”. Timeline view of research themes

The keyword timeline map generated by CiteSpace clearly shows that research on VAP nursing interventions from 2005 to 2025 has evolved in a phased, deepening, and expanding manner.

From 2005 to 2010, the focus was on terms such as “intensive care unit”, “nosocomial infection”, and “critical care”. This period primarily explored the clinical settings, epidemiological characteristics, and fundamental issues related to VAP in critical care.

From 2011 to 2020, research shifted toward practical interventions. Keywords like “infection control”, “ventilator-associated pneumonia”, and “care bundle” became prominent, reflecting growing attention to bundled nursing strategies and infection prevention practices.

From 2021 to 2025, the field further extended toward Precision Management and Full-Cycle Care. Terms such as “oral care”, “mechanical ventilation”, and “nursing care” remained consistently active. Meanwhile, the emergence of “trial” and “implementation” suggests increasing emphasis on empirical validation and real-world execution of intervention protocols.

Discussion

This study used CiteSpace and VOSviewer to conduct a visual analysis of global research on nursing interventions for ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) from 2005 to 2025. The findings systematically explore the developmental trends, core themes, and collaboration networks in this field, which are further discussed in light of existing evidence and clinical contexts.

Research evolution over time

From 2005 to 2025, the number of publications on VAP nursing interventions showed a fluctuating upward trend. This pattern may reflect broader advances in critical care practice. During the initial exploration phase (2005–2008), publication growth was relatively slow. This may suggest that academic interest in this topic was still emerging, with research mainly focused on the epidemiological features and key risk factors of VAP.

In the steady growth phase (2009–2021), the number of studies continued to rise. A number of highly cited papers on care bundles and infection control strategies were published during this period. At the same time, the release and promotion of international guidelines—such as the 2007 IDSA/ATS consensus on pneumonia management [12]—may have helped standardize clinical practices and encouraged more research on standardized nursing protocols. However, the exact impact of these guidelines on research development requires further empirical validation.

However, there was a slight decline from 2014 to 2015. Early studies on bundle care and infection control were mainly conducted by core institutions in Europe and America. Some relevant studies were completed or integrated, which led to the temporary drop in publication volume.

Since 2022, there has been a clear increase in publication output, reaching a high level by 2025. This trend may be related to the increased need for mechanical ventilation after the COVID-19 pandemic. During the pandemic, the use of prone positioning and prolonged mechanical ventilation became more common, posing new challenges to traditional VAP prevention methods. These changes may have promoted research into remote monitoring, smart early-warning systems, and other emerging nursing models [13, 14]. However, this association remains observational. Other confounding factors cannot be ruled out.

Core research themes

Keyword clustering and burst detection show that VAP nursing intervention research has formed a system centered on “prevention,” with “care bundles” as a key strategy and multiple thematic extensions. The central role of the term “prevention” highlights that the main goal of VAP nursing is to block infection pathways. This aligns well with the pathogenesis of VAP, such as bacterial colonization in the oropharynx and aspiration [15].

Care bundles (“care bundle,” “bundle”) remain a persistent research focus. Several highly cited studies support their effectiveness. For example, Hajiyeva A et al. concluded through meta-analysis and systematic review that care bundles can reduce the incidence of VAP [16]. A 2019 study published in Critical Care Medicine, which involved 15,000 adult patients, confirmed that implementing multiple measures together is more effective than single interventions [17].

Recently, the rising bursts of keywords such as “oral hygiene”, “implementation”, and “management” reflect a shift from “proposing strategies” to “refining implementation.” This change may respond to ongoing challenges in clinical practice. Although care bundle protocols have been widely agreed upon, low compliance rates and inconsistent details in practice still affect outcomes [18]. Future research should therefore focus on optimizing specific aspects—such as choice of oral care solutions, frequency of procedures, and precise cuff pressure management—to improve standardization and practicality [19, 20]. In addition, reviews on ICU infection management show that fungal pathogens including Candida, Aspergillus and other molds play an increasingly important role in infection burden among critically ill patients. Patients with immunosuppression, long-term mechanical ventilation or broad-spectrum antibiotic use face higher risks of fungal colonization and infection. Such infections may worsen illness, prolong hospital stay and increase treatment difficulty.

Therefore, VAP-related research should focus on epidemiological characteristics, early diagnosis and intervention strategies of fungal pathogens.

In addition, the co-occurrence of terms like “antimicrobial resistance” and “nosocomial infection” highlights the interdisciplinary nature of VAP nursing. The high citation count (2,777 times) of the 2016 guideline on antibiotic stewardship programs supports the link between rational antibiotic use and VAP care [21]. Preventing VAP requires collaboration among nursing, infectious disease specialists, and pharmacy teams to build an integrated system combining “nursing protocols + pathogen monitoring + antibiotic stewardship” [22]. This approach is consistent with the World Health Organization’s framework for multidisciplinary hospital infection control [23]. Reviews on ICU infection management indicate that fungal pathogens such as Candida, Aspergillus and other molds are gaining growing importance in the infection burden of critically ill patients, especially those with immunosuppression or receiving broad-spectrum antibiotics.

Collaboration network characteristics

Analysis of institutional and author collaboration networks shows a pattern of “regional clustering with limited cross-regional linkage.” The blue cluster, composed mainly of universities in Europe and North America, has produced many influential studies, especially in guideline development and basic research. This may be due to their relatively strong research systems and resources. The red cluster, made up of clinical institutions in Asia, focuses more on clinical nursing practice, suggesting possible differences in research priorities across regions. The green cluster, formed by universities in Latin America and Europe, shows some transregional collaboration.

Therefore, there is a need to establish a collaborative framework that promotes knowledge exchange. Such a system could allow high-income countries’ technological innovations to complement low-cost, context-specific innovations from developing countries, together forming a more complete evidence-based decision-making network. For example, Metersky et al. (2025) developed a virtual nursing education simulation system through international cooperation among Canada, Finland, and other countries, adapting it to different legal and clinical environments [24].

Obvious regional differences exist in global studies on VAP nursing interventions. They are mainly related to research systems, resource distribution, clinical practice and policy backgrounds.

On the one hand, universities in Europe and America have sound research facilities and sufficient funds. They tend to carry out high-impact basic research and guideline formulation. Medical institutions in Asia focus more on practical intervention with strong operability and practicability. Universities in Latin America and Europe have frequent cross-regional cooperation yet limited overall research scale. On the other hand, differences in ICU settings, nursing procedures and infection prevention policies vary across countries. These factors affect research priorities and lead to diverse research outputs in different regions. Combined with the above reasons, global VAP nursing intervention research shows obvious regional aggregation and insufficient cross-regional collaboration.

In the author network, small teams led by key scholars such as Rello Jordi and Klompas Michael dominate collaboration. While this model may support in-depth research in specific areas, it may also limit broader integration of knowledge across fields or regions. Previous studies have shown that major advances in critical care—such as monitoring infection rates or validating intervention protocols—often depend on large-scale, multicenter collaborations that combine data from diverse settings to strengthen the reliability of findings [25, 26].

Future research directions

With the further development of research on ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP), future studies need to focus on the following key directions.First, monitor dynamic changes of infectious pathogen spectra. Most current studies focus on bacterial pathogens. However, the burden of fungal pathogens in VAP and mechanically ventilated patients keeps rising. Colonization and invasive infection of Candida, Aspergillus and other fungi have drawn increasing clinical attention. Existing systematic reviews and clinical observations confirm that airway Candida colonization is linked to poor outcomes of ventilated patients. But its exact role in VAP pathogenesis remains unclear. High-quality evidence is still needed to clarify the epidemiology, diagnostic criteria and clinical significance of filamentous fungi such as Aspergillus. Future research shall prioritize rapid fungal diagnosis, optimized antifungal regimens, and interaction mechanisms of bacterial-fungal co-infection [27, 28].

Second, conduct more multicenter, large-sample and high-quality cohort studies across different regions and healthcare systems. These studies can clarify the impacts of ICU environment, nursing interventions and antimicrobial strategies on VAP incidence and pathogen distribution.

In addition, emerging technologies show great potential in early VAP diagnosis, pathogen identification, drug resistance detection and precise prevention. Molecular diagnosis, artificial intelligence-assisted analysis and biomarker-based comprehensive evaluation are worthy of clinical verification in empirical studies. Finally, deepen interdisciplinary and cross-regional cooperation. It helps establish unified research frameworks and promote the optimization and revision of global VAP prevention and control guidelines.

This study has several limitations. First, the data were limited to the Web of Science Core Collection, which may miss studies published in non-English or regional journals. Second, bibliometric analysis mainly reflects changes in research volume and topic popularity, not the actual clinical effectiveness of interventions. Third, the interpretation of research trends—such as links to the post-pandemic era—is based on temporal overlap. The causal relationships require further validation through qualitative analysis or policy document reviews. And most included literatures are general infection control guidelines or guidelines for hospital-acquired pneumonia and ventilator-associated pneumonia. Original studies focusing specifically on nursing interventions account for a relatively low proportion.

Although relevant nursing intervention papers have practical value in VAP prevention and control, their academic influence should be interpreted cautiously. Future studies could expand the data sources and conduct deeper thematic analyses. Regional comparative studies could also help provide tailored guidance for healthcare systems with different resource levels.

Summary

In summary, research interest in VAP nursing interventions has grown in three phases: initial exploration, steady increase, and rapid rise. The collaboration network shows “regional clustering with limited cross-regional connection,” forming three core clusters: one led by Western universities, one by Asian clinical institutions, and one by Latin American and European universities. Most leading authors work within small, focused teams.

Highly cited literature is often led by international guidelines. Practice-oriented topics such as care bundles and infection control receive wide attention. Research hotspots have formed a multidimensional system, with “VAP prevention” at its center and “care bundles” as the main intervention method, covering clinical practice, infection control, and critical care management. Keyword evolution shows an early focus on identifying basic issues, a mid-phase shift toward building intervention packages, and a recent emphasis on refined management and implementation. Terms such as “oral hygiene,” “implementation,” and “management” have become current core topics.
