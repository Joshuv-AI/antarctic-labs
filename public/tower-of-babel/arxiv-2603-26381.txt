Average block nonlinear Kaczmarz methods with adaptive momentum for nonlinear systems of equations Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Renjie Ding, Dongling Wang, Jun Zou

Abstract
The Kaczmarz method is widely recognized as an efficient iterative algorithm for solving large-scale linear systems, owing to its simplicity and low memory requirements. However, the development of its nonlinear extensions for solving large-scale nonlinear systems has seen limited progress. In this work, we introduce a new family of momentum-accelerated averaging block nonlinear Kaczmarz methods tailored for large-scale nonlinear systems and ill-posed problems. Our contributions are twofold: (1) We develop an adaptive strategy for selecting step sizes and momentum coefficients, leading to a new average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with adaptive momentum (ABNKAm). This algorithm achieves high computational efficiency by requiring only minimal inner-product computations per iteration, which significantly reduces both arithmetic complexity and memory usage. (2) We establish rigorous convergence of the ABNKAm under mild assumptions, proving that the method converges exponentially to the unique solution nearest to the initial point. Moreover, under suitable conditions, we provide a theoretical justification of acceleration of the proposed ABNKAm with momentum. Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate that ABNKAm outperforms existing nonlinear Kaczmarz variants in terms of both iteration count and computational time, with particularly notable gains in large-scale problems.

Introduction

Consider solving the following system of nonlinear equations:

F(x)=0,

(1)

where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a nonlinear vector-valued function and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an unknown vector.
The system of nonlinear equations
F
can be rewritten in component form as
F_{i}
for
i=1,\cdots,m
, where
F_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the
i
-th component of
F
.

Solving large-scale systems of the form (
1
) arises widely and significantly in applications such as electrical impedance tomography, circuit design, nonlinear programming
[
15
]
, systems of nonlinear differential equations
[
27
]
, and machine learning
[
5
,
16
,
19
]
. Therefore, the efficient solution of the large-scale system of nonlinear equations (
1
) is of utmost importance. Classical iterative methods, such as Newton-type methods, are widely used but often entail high computational costs or become inapplicable for large-scale or ill-conditioned problems. Thus, there is a pressing need for efficient, low-cost iterative methods.
For the solution of large-scale linear systems, the Kaczmarz algorithm
[
14
,
33
]
, as a low cost and efficient algorithm, has been remarkably developed. When
F=Ax-b
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the Kaczmarz method orthogonally projects the current iteration point
x_{k}
onto the constraint set
\{x|A_{i_{k}}x=b_{i_{k}}\}
, namely it updates the iteration as

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{A_{i_{k}}x_{k}-b_{i_{k}}}{\|A_{i_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}}(A_{i_{k}})^{T},

(2)

where the index
i_{k}
is selected via random or cyclic sampling from
[m]:=\{1,\dots,m\}
.
This approach significantly reduces the computational cost and storage requirements per iteration.

The Kaczmarz method was extended for solving large-scale nonlinear systems
[
35
]
,
with a family of nonlinear Kaczmarz algorithms. These include the nonlinear Kaczmarz (NK), the nonlinear randomized Kaczmarz (NRK), and the nonlinear uniformly randomized Kaczmarz (NURK) methods. Notably, they established a connection between the NURK method and the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) method
[
13
,
31
]
. These methods are based on the following iterative scheme:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k}).

(3)

Here
\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x)
denotes the gradient function of
F_{i_{k}}(x)
. However, selecting index sets from
[m]
via random or cyclic order strategies may be inefficient and may lead to the selection of indices corresponding to small residual values. To address this limitation, several approximate greedy strategies have been proposed
[
21
,
25
,
40
,
41
]
, which first construct a controlled index subset
\mathcal{J}_{k}
and then select an index from
\mathcal{J}_{k}
based on given probability.
To further accelerate the convergence of the NK method, a relaxation parameter
\alpha
in the iterative formula (
3
) is introduced in
[
20
]
to enhance convergence. Specifically, the proposed iteration scheme is formulated as:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha\frac{F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k}).

(4)

The Nonlinear Greedy Randomized Kaczmarz method with momentum (NGRKm), integrating a Polyak heavy ball momentum
[
29
]
into the NGRK framework, was further developed in
[
21
]
:

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha\frac{F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}\nabla F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})+\beta(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),

(5)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are the step size and momentum parameter, respectively.
Numerical experiments demonstrated the accelerance of NGRKm over NGRK
[
21
]
.

Although the above methods have shown improvements over the classical nonlinear Kaczmarz method,
they suffer from two significant drawbacks:
(i) The selection of relaxation parameters
\alpha
and momentum parameters
\beta
lacks theoretical guidance.
(ii) These methods update the solution in each iteration by exploiting the knowledge from
just a single equation. Compared with the block method to be introduced below, which updates
the approximation by leveraging information from a set of equations at each iteration, this inherent limitation causes their convergence rate to deteriorate substantially as the problem scale increases. This will be
demonstrated clearly through several typical examples numerically in Section
4
.

With respect to the drawback (i),
there are many interesting and in-depth studies
[
1
,
22
,
24
,
32
,
39
,
6
]

for the linear Kaczmarz method for linear systems.
In particular, an adaptive stochastic heavy-ball momentum method was developed in
[
39
]
,
with a significant feature that the choice of the momentum parameter is independent
of the singular values of the coefficient matrix. However,
to the best of our knowledge, there is still no similar efficient algorithm available
for the nonlinear Kaczmarz method.

Building on the success of the linear block Kaczmarz method
[
9
,
4
,
25
,
26
,
30
]
, which has demonstrated superiority in handling large-scale linear systems, some studies have extended the concept to the nonlinear equations and developed a nonlinear block Kaczmarz method
[
34
,
40
,
41
,
37
]
.
This approach utilizes multiple equations simultaneously in each iteration, with the iterative formula defined as

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{\dagger}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}),

(6)

where
F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})^{\dagger}
denotes the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the submatrix of
F^{\prime}(x_{k})
and the block index set
\mathcal{J}_{k}\subset[m]
. Notably, the maximum-residual block nonlinear Kaczmarz method (MRBNK) can also be regarded as an iterative sketch-and-project method applied to the sketched Newton system
[
38
,
40
]
. However, these methods require the computing of
the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the sub-matrix of the Jacobian matrix in each iteration, which increases the computational cost of the methods. To avoid computing the pseudoinverse, a variation of the block Kaczmarz method was proposed
by first projecting the current iterate onto each row of the selected block matrix, then computing the next iterate
as a convex combination of these projections:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}}\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\right),k\geq 0,

(7)

where the weights
\omega_{k}^{(i)}\in[0,1]
and satisfy
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}=1
and
\alpha_{k}\in(0,2)
.
The method constructed based on iterative formula (
7
) is named the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method (ABNK)
[
36
]
. Notably, while the averaging technique in block iteration formula (
7
) achieves lower computational cost compared to the pseudo-inverse based block scheme (
6
), its precision may be compromised. This observation motivates the necessity to enhance the convergence rate of averaging techniques without sacrificing their computational efficiency.

In this work, we propose a novel class of averaged block nonlinear Kaczmarz methods with momentum to design more efficient nonlinear Kaczmarz-type algorithms. From a geometric perspective, the selection of step size and momentum parameters is investigated, enabling adaptive update of these parameters using only information from the current iterate. The main contributions are:

•

We introduce a new framework that integrates averaging block techniques with momentum acceleration for solving nonlinear systems. This framework is highly flexible, with some important existing methods like ABNK
[
36
]
and NGRKm
[
21
]
as the special cases. A key component is its adaptive scheme for selecting the step size and momentum parameters, which overcomes the limitation of manual tuning in prior work and enhances the convergence rate of the basic averaging block method from
[
36
]
. The proposed methods also exhibit superior scalability compared to existing nonlinear Kaczmarz approaches.

•

The proposed method combines the benefits of momentum techniques with averaging block methods to achieve faster convergence while maintaining computational efficiency. Convergence guarantees are provided for the algorithm, and numerical experiments demonstrate practical effectiveness and superior performance compared to existing nonlinear Kaczmarz approaches.

The paper is structured as follows: Section
2
presents the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with momentum, including its general framework and adaptive-parameter variants.
Section
3
provides a rigorous convergence analysis for the proposed method with convergence rate.
In Section
4
, numerical experiments are conducted to verify the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms, with
a comprehensive comparison among the proposed methods and existing nonlinear Kaczmarz methods.
Section
5
presents some concluding remarks.

2
The average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with momentum

In this section, we propose a new average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method incorporating momentum, and
an adaptive selection for both the step size and momentum parameters, leading to an adaptive momentum variant
of the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method.

2.1
General framework

To reduce the per-iteration computational cost in (
6
), we employ a block-averaged nonlinear Kaczmarz strategy. Furthermore, to accelerate convergence, we introduce a momentum term into the iterative scheme (
7
), leading to
the new method of the general form:

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}}\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\right)+\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),\quad k\geq 0,

(8)

where
\alpha_{k}
is the step size,
\beta_{k}
is the momentum parameter,
\omega_{k}^{(i)}\in[0,1]
are weights satisfying
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}=1
, and
\mathcal{J}_{k}
is the index set selected at the
k
-th iteration. To prioritize the reduction of larger-magnitude components in the residual vector
r_{k}=-F(x_{k})
, we adopt a greedy criterion for selecting
\mathcal{J}_{k}
, following the approach in
[
3
,
7
,
24
,
40
,
36
,
34
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k}=\left\{i_{k}\left||F_{i_{k}}(x_{k})|^{2}\geq\theta\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|F_{i}(x_{k})|_{2}^{2}\right.\right\},

(9)

where
\theta\in(0,1]
controls the selectivity of the index set.
This defines the general framework of the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with momentum (ABNKm).

Remark 1
.

The cardinality of the index set
\mathcal{J}_{k}
evolves dynamically with the iteration, and it is governed by the control parameter
\theta
.
It is crucial to design an algorithm that is robust with respect to parameter
\theta
.

Remark 2
.

The resulting ABNKm gives rise to some popular existing algorithms, with appropriate selections of the step size
\alpha_{k}
,
the momentum parameter
\beta_{k}
and the weight
\omega_{k}
.
The simplest form of the algorithm may use constant step sizes and momentum parameters.
In particular, if we select
\beta_{k}=0
, the proposed ABNKm reduces to the ABNK scheme.
More appropriate choices for these key parameters,
especially those that can improve the convergence, will be discussed in the subsequent section.

2.2
The average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with adaptive momentum

In this section, we propose some effective adaptive strategies for selecting both the step size and momentum parameters, leveraging these mechanisms to accelerate the convergence of the newly proposed ABNKm method (
8
).
The resulting algorithm, which explicitly incorporates these adaptive strategies,
is designated as ABNKAm.

We now address the selection of the step size parameter
\alpha_{k}
and momentum parameter
\beta_{k}
in
(
8
). Our objective is to determine
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
such that the error
x_{k+1}-x_{*}

is minimized as follows:

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha,\,\beta\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\|x-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

(10)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x=x_{k}-\alpha d_{k}+\beta(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),

where
d_{k}:=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}}\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})
.
If the condition

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}:=\|d_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}-\left\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rangle^{2}\neq 0

(11)

holds, then the solution to the optimization problem (
10
) is explicitly given by

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha_{k}&=\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle-\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle}{\Delta_{k}},\\
\beta_{k}&=\frac{\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},d_{k}\rangle\langle x_{k}-x_{*},d_{k}\rangle-||d_{k}||_{2}^{2}\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle}{\Delta_{k}}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(12)

Unfortunately, the update formulae (
12
) require the knowledge of the true solution
x_{*}

to equation (
1
), and this is clearly impractical.
We next discuss how to handle the two terms that involves
x_{*}
, i.e.,

\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle
and
\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle
for all
k\geq 1
.

On the one hand, from a geometric perspective of the minimization (
10
) we can view

x_{k}
for
k\geq 2
as the orthogonal projection of
x_{*}
onto the affine set

\displaystyle\Pi_{k-1}:=x_{k-1}+\text{Span}\{d_{k-1},x_{k-1}-x_{k-2}\}\,,

(13)

hence we have
\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle=0
for
k\geq 2
.
While for
k=1
, we obtain
x_{1}
from the optimization:

\displaystyle\min_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\|x-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x=x_{0}-\alpha d_{0}.

This implies that the orthogonality condition
\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle=0
holds for all
k\geq 1
.

On the other hand, in order to deal with
\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle
in (
12
),
we may linearize the nonlinear operator
F
at the current iterate
x_{k}
via Taylor expansion,

F(x)\approx F(x_{k})+F^{\prime}(x_{k})(x-x_{k}).

Then we have the approximation

\displaystyle\langle d_{k},x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}}\nabla F_{i}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|^{2}}\langle\nabla F_{i}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})}{||\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})||_{2}^{2}}F_{i}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})^{2}}{||\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})||_{2}^{2}}\,.

(15)

Using the above deductions, we can rewrite (
12

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha_{k}=\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})^{2}}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}}{\Delta_{k}},\\
\beta_{k}=\frac{\langle x_{k}-x_{k-1},d_{k}\rangle\cdot\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{k}}\omega_{k}^{(i)}\frac{F_{i}(x_{k})^{2}}{\|\nabla F_{i}(x_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}}{\Delta_{k}}.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(16)

as defined in (

3
Convergence analysis

In this section, we establish the convergence properties of the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method with adaptive momentum
in subsection
2.2
. We begin by introducing some necessary assumptions and preliminary results
that are used in our subsequent analysis.

Hypothesis 5
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded closed domain,
x_{0}\in D
and
F:D\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
a nonlinear vector-valued function. The following conditions are assumed:

(i)

Each component
F_{i}:D\to\mathbb{R}
of
F=(F_{1},\dots,F_{m})^{\mathsf{T}}

is differentiable.

(ii)

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})\subseteq D
.

(iii)

For all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
x,\widetilde{x}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
, there exists
\eta_{i}\in[0,\eta)
with

\eta=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\eta_{i}<{1}/{2}
such that

\left|F_{i}(x)-F_{i}(\widetilde{x})-\nabla F_{i}(x)^{\mathsf{T}}(x-\widetilde{x})\right|\leq\eta_{i}\left|F_{i}(x)-F_{i}(\widetilde{x})\right|.

(24)

(iv)

For the constant
\delta_{0}
from (ii), it holds that

B_{\delta_{0}/4}(x_{0})\cap\ker(F)\neq\emptyset\,.

(25)

(v)

For the same
\delta_{0}
, there exists
\widetilde{x}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
such that

F^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})^{\mathsf{T}}e_{i}\neq 0\quad\text{for all }i\in{1,\dots,m},

(26)

where
e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
form the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Assumptions
5
are standard in the literature
[
11
,
10
,
15
,
34
]
.
Condition
(iii)
is the so-called local tangential cone condition, a natural requirement for Kaczmarz-type iterative methods; detailed explanations and heuristic justifications can be found in
[
15
, page 12]
. Moreover, the condition

\eta<{1}/{2}
plays a pivotal role in ensuring convergence of the nonlinear Kaczmarz method, as will become evident in Lemmas
16
.
Conditions
(ii)
and
(iv)
together imply the existence of a unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution, as will be shown in Lemma
7
.
Finally, Assumption
(v)
is necessary for the validity of several of our subsequent key results,
as established in Lemma
8
. In fact, condition
(v)
is less restrictive
than the usual condition that requires the Jacobian matrix of
F
to be row-bounded below or to satisfy full column rank conditions.

Lemma 6
.

[
11
,
10
,
15
,
34
,
36
]

Let
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Under Assumption
5
(ii)-(iii), it holds for all
x,\widetilde{x}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\eta}\|F_{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(x)(x-\widetilde{x})\|_{2}\leq\|F_{\mathcal{J}}(x)-F_{\mathcal{J}}(\widetilde{x})\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-\eta}\|F_{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}(x)(x-\widetilde{x})\|_{2},

\displaystyle\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}}(x)-F_{\mathcal{J}}(\widetilde{x})-F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}(x)(x-\widetilde{x})\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\eta^{2}\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}}(x)-F_{\mathcal{J}}(\widetilde{x})\right\|_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}}(x)-F_{\mathcal{J}}(\widetilde{x})\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{1+\eta^{2}}\left\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}(x)(x-\widetilde{x})\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Lemma 7
.

[
15
,
34
]

Let
x_{0}\in D
and the vector-valued function
F
satisfies Assumption
5
(ii)-(iv). Then

(i) For any
x,\widetilde{x}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
with
F(x)=F(\widetilde{x})
, it holds that
x-\widetilde{x}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x))=\ker(F^{\prime}(\widetilde{x}))
.

(ii) There exists a unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution
x_{*}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
to (
1
), satisfying

x_{*}-x_{0}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}.

(27)

The second part of Lemma
7
establishes that
x_{*}
is the unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm element in
\ker(F)\cap B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
and satisfies condition (
27
). Indeed, by Lemma
7
(i) and (
27
), if there exists another point
x^{\dagger}\in\ker(F)\cap B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
, then

\displaystyle\|x^{{\dagger}}-x_{0}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\|x^{{\dagger}}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}+\|x_{*}-x_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+2\langle x^{{\dagger}}-x_{*},x_{*}-x_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|x^{{\dagger}}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}+\|x_{*}-x_{0}\|^{2}_{2}>\|x_{*}-x_{0}\|^{2}.

(28)

Furthermore, in the linear case
F(x)=Ax-b
, condition (
27
) implies that
x_{*}
corresponds to the best approximation of
x_{0}
onto the solution manifold
\{x\mid Ax=b\}
.

Lemma 8
.

[
34
]

Let
x_{0}\in D
and suppose
F
satisfies Assumptions
5
(ii)–(iii). Then, for every
i\in{1,2,\dots,m}
,
F_{i}(x)=0
for all
x\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
if and only if there exists
\widetilde{x}_{i}\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
such that
F^{\prime}(\widetilde{x}_{i})^{\top}e_{i}=0
.

Lemma
8
demonstrates the necessity of condition (v) in Assumption
5
. If condition (v) is not satisfied, then by Lemma
8
, there would exist some
i_{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
such that
F_{i_{0}}(x)=0
for all
x\in B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
.

Lemma 9
.

[
12
]

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be any nonzero real matrix. For every vector
u\in\text{range}(A)
, we have

\sigma_{\min}^{2}(A)\|u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|A^{T}u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sigma_{\max}^{2}(A)\|u\|_{2}^{2}
,
where
\text{range}(A)
,
\sigma_{\min}(A)
and
\sigma_{\max}(A)
are the column space, the nonzero minimum and maximum singular values of

, respectively.

3.1
Convergence analysis of Algorithm
1

We first establish the convergence of Algorithm
1
.
A key step in our analysis is to derive a recursive estimate for the error sequence
x_{k}-x_{*}
,
which will be shown to satisfy certain contraction conditions. We shall need the following basic
recurrence estimate.

Lemma 12
.

[
22
]

Fix
E_{1}=E_{0}\geq 0
and let
\{E_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a nonnegative real sequence satisfying that

E_{k+1}\leq a_{1}E_{k}+a_{2}E_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 1
,
where
a_{2}\geq 0
,
a_{1}+a_{2}<1
and at least one of the coefficients
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
is positive. Then the sequence satisfies the estimae
E_{k+1}\leq q^{k}(1+\xi)E_{0}
for all
k\geq 1
, where
q=\frac{a_{1}+\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2}}}{2}
and
\xi=q-a_{1}\geq 0
. Moreover, we have

a_{1}+a_{2}\leq q<1
and the equality holds if and only if
a_{2}=0
(or
q=a_{1}
and
\xi=0
).

Following Lemma
12
, we now derive a recursive estimate
of the form
E_{k+1}\leq a_{1}E_{k}+a_{2}E_{k-1}
associated with Algorithm
1
,
and then achieve the convergence of Algorithm
1
(see Theorem
14
).

Lemma 13
.

Suppose that
F(x)
and
x_{0}
satisfies Assumption
5

is the unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution of the nonlinear equation (

Assume that

.
Then the new approximation

generated by the iteration (

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+3\beta_{k}+2\beta_{k}^{2})\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}+(2\beta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k})\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}

(29)

\displaystyle-(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})\frac{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}},

where
\theta_{k}:=\arccos\left(\frac{F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})(x_{k}-x_{k-1})}{\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|\cdot\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|}\right)

Proof.

It follows from iteration (

\displaystyle\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\frac{(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))\|^{2}}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left\langle x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}-x_{*},\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We now estimate
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
one by one. Firstly, we can directly derive

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\left\langle\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}},x_{k}-x_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\left\|\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left\langle-\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}\left[F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})-F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{*})-F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})(x_{k}-x_{*})\right]+

\displaystyle\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{4}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}\left[\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}\left\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\right].

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
6
(ii) and Lemma
9
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{4}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}\left[\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}\left\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}-2\right]

For

, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle x_{k}-\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}-x_{*},\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\beta_{k}\langle x_{k}-x_{*},x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle-2\left\langle\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}},\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle I_{3,2}

\displaystyle=-2\left\langle\alpha_{k}\frac{(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})}{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}},\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{4}_{2}}{\Delta^{2}_{k}}\left\langle F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{4}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{4}_{2}\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{k}}{\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{4}_{2}\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{4}_{2}(1-\cos^{2}\theta_{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-2\frac{\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{k}}{\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))(1-\cos^{2}\theta_{k})^{2}}\,,

while we can estimate
I_{2}
and
I_{3,1}
together as follows:

\displaystyle I_{2}+I_{3,1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}^{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta_{k}\langle x_{k}-x_{*},x_{k}-x_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}^{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta_{k}\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta_{k}\langle x_{k}-x_{*},x_{*}-x_{k-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\beta_{k}^{2}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\beta_{k}\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\beta_{k}\left\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-\beta_{k}\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\beta_{k}\left\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\beta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k})\left\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}+(2\beta_{k}^{2}+3\beta_{k})\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|_{2}^{2}

where we have used in the third equality above the identity that

2\langle A-C,C-B\rangle=\|A-B\|_{2}^{2}-\|C-B\|_{2}^{2}-\|A-C\|_{2}^{2}

for any vectors
A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By combining all the estimates for

and

To further investigate the acceleration mechanism of the momentum effect, we now derive some direct comparison
between the rate of convergence
of Algorithm

(under
\Delta_{k}\neq 0
for all

(the scheme (

For this purpose, we first introduce some constants.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{\eta(1+\eta^{2})+\sqrt{\eta^{2}(1+\eta^{2})^{2}+(1+\eta^{2})(1-2\eta^{2}-4\eta^{3})})}{1-2\eta^{2}-4\eta^{3}}

where

\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)

For any

and

\displaystyle C_{2}(\eta,\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\min\Big\{\frac{\sqrt{(1+3d^{2})^{2}+8(1+d^{2})(d^{4}+d^{2}c-d^{2})}-(1+3d^{2})}{4(1+d^{2})},\frac{\sqrt{1+c}-1}{2}\Big\},

We can easily verify that

Theorem 14
.

Let
F(x)
satisfy Assumption
5
with
x_{*}
being the unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution of
F(x)=0
. Assume
\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))\subseteq\ker(F^{\prime}(x))
for all
x\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})
, and let
\{x_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
1
with
\Delta_{k}\neq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.
We assume that there exists
\gamma\in(\varepsilon_{0}/2,\varepsilon_{0})
such that the following conditions hold for all
k
.

(C1)

\kappa(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}k}(x_{k}))\leq K
for some
K\in[1,C_{1}(\eta,\gamma))
,

(C2)

\beta_{k}\in(0,\beta_{\max})
for some
\beta_{\max}\in(0,C_{2}(\eta,\gamma))
,

(C3)

\sin^{2}\theta_{k}\leq\gamma
,

then the following conclusions hold:

(i)
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
,

(ii) If
F(x_{k})\neq 0
for some
k
, then
x_{k+1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
and

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq q^{k}(1+\xi)\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

where
q=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+4b_{2}}}{2}
,
\xi=q-b_{1}
with

b_{1}=1+3\beta_{\max}+2\beta_{max}^{2}-\frac{1-2\eta\delta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}\delta^{2}},\,b_{2}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+\beta_{\max}
.

Furthermore, the estimate
q<d^{2}<1
holds for the rate
q
of convergence.

Proof.

We apply the mathematical induction.
From Assumption
5
(iv) and Lemma
16
, we have
x_{0}\in B_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{4}}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0}),x_{1}\in B_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
and
x_{0}-x_{*},x_{1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
.
Accordingly, we may assume without loss of generality that
x_{j}\in B_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0}),\,x_{j}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
hold for all
j\in\{0,1,\cdots,k\}
. It remains
to show that
x_{k+1}\in B_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
and
x_{k+1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
.
It follows from (
29
) that

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\beta_{k}+2\beta_{k}^{2})\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}+(2\beta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k})\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle-(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})\frac{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}.

For the last term on the right-hand side, we can rewrite and estimate as follows:

\displaystyle-(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})\frac{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})\frac{\left\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})-F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{*})\right\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})}{(1+\eta^{2})\sigma^{2}_{\max}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}\left\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})(x_{k}-x_{*})\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}}\left\|x_{k}-x_{*}\right\|^{2}_{2},

where we have used Lemma
6
in the first inequality and Lemma
9
in the second respectively.
This implies

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\beta_{k}+2\beta_{k}^{2}-\frac{(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}})\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+(2\beta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k})\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq b_{1}\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}+b_{2}\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

where
b_{1}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+3\beta_{\max}+1-\frac{1-2\eta\delta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}\delta^{2}}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+3\beta_{\max}+1-c
and
b_{2}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+\beta_{\max}
.

Noting that
\beta_{\max}<C_{2}(\eta,\gamma)\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+c}-1}{2}
,
it is easy to verify that
b_{1}+b_{2}=4\beta_{\max}^{2}+4\beta_{\max}+1-c<1
and
b_{2}>0
. Therefore, by Lemma
12
, we
readily derive

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq q^{k}(1+\xi)\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

(30)

where
q=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+4b_{2}}}{2}
,
\xi=q-b_{1}
and
b_{1}+b_{2}\leq q<1.

Next, we verify that
q<d^{2}
. In fact, we only need to show that
d^{4}-d^{2}b_{1}-b_{2}>0
.
Substituting the expressions for
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, and using the conditions
\beta_{\max}<C_{2}(\eta,\gamma)\leq\frac{-(1+3d^{2})+\sqrt{(1+3d^{2})^{2}+8(1+d^{2})(d^{4}+d^{2}c-d^{2}})}{4(1+d^{2})}
and
K<C_{1}(\eta,\gamma)
, we can easily get that
q<d^{2}
.

On the other hand, we can deduce from (
30
) that

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{q^{k}(1+\xi)}\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}\leq 2\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},

(31)

That is to say
x_{k+1}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
. According to the iteration (
22
), we can write

\displaystyle x_{k+1}-x_{*}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-x_{*}+\frac{\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}_{2}\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\Delta_{k}}(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})

(32)

\displaystyle+\beta_{k}(x_{k}-x_{*}+x_{*}-x_{k-1}).

By means of Lemma
16
, (
32
) and
x_{j}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*}),\,x_{j}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
for all
j\in\{0,1,\cdots,k\}
, we derive

\displaystyle x_{k+1}-x_{*}

\displaystyle\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}+Im(F^{\prime}(x_{k})^{T})+\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}+\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{k}))^{\perp}

\displaystyle\subseteq\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}.

This completes the proof.

We easily see from Theorem
14
that the rate of convergence of ABNKAm (Algorithm
1
)
is given by
q=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+4b_{2}}}{2}
,
whereas the rate of convergence of
the average block nonlinear Kaczmarz method, say the variant ABNK2 with an adaptive step size,
is given by
[
36
]

\rho_{k}=1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))}
, with

\rho_{k}\geq 1-\frac{1-2\eta}{1+\eta^{2}}=d
. We know from Theorem
14
that
q<d^{2}<d
.
This concludes that the rate of convergence of the averaged block Kaczmarz method with adaptive momentum
is strictly smaller than that of the averaged block Kaczmarz method without adaptive momentum.

3.2
Convergence analysis of Algorithm
2

We have derived in the subsection
3.1
the rate of convergence of Algorithm
1

(cf. Theorem
14
). We are now going to estimate the rate of convergence of
Algorithm
2
. We first present a very important result for our subsequence analysis.

Lemma 15
.

Fix
E_{1}=E_{0}\geq 0
and let
\{E_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
be a nonnegative real sequence. For each
k\geq 1
, the sequence satisfies one of the following two inequalities:
(i)
E_{k+1}\leq a_{1}E_{k}+a_{2}E_{k-1}
, where
a_{2}\geq 0
,
a_{1}+a_{2}<1
and at least one of the coefficients
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
is positive;
(ii)
E_{k+1}\leq a_{3}E_{k}
, where
a_{3}>0
.
Let

t_{k-1}=\{\text{The inequality (ii) occurs exactly }t_{k-1}\text{ times for any }k\geq 1\}.

Then the sequence
\{E_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfies the estimate

E_{k+1}\leq q^{k-t_{k}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(1+\xi)E_{0}\text{ for all }k\geq 1,

where
q=\frac{a_{1}+\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+4a_{2}}}{2}
and
\xi=q-a_{1}\geq 0
. Moreover, we have

q\geq a_{1}+a_{2}
and the equality holds if and only if
a_{2}=0
(or
q=a_{1}
and
\xi=0
).

Proof.

We can easily find that
\xi
satisfies the following two properties:
\xi\geq 0
and
a_{2}\leq(a_{1}+\xi)\xi
.
Let the indices corresponding to the
t_{k}
occurrences of Case (ii) be denoted as
i_{1}<\dots<i_{t_{k}}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
t_{k}<k+1
. Under these settings, the following relations hold:

E_{k+1}+\xi E_{k}\leq(a_{1}+\xi)E_{k}+a_{2}E_{k-1}\leq(a_{1}+\xi)(E_{k}+\xi E_{k-1})=q(E_{k}+\xi E_{k-1}).

(33)

In conjunction with Case (ii) and recurrence relation (
33
), the following relations can be derived:

E_{k+1}\leq E_{k+1}+\xi E_{k}
and

\displaystyle E_{k+1}+\xi E_{k}\leq q(E_{k}+\xi E_{k-1})\leq q^{k-i_{t_{k}}+1}(E_{i_{t_{k}}}+\xi E_{i_{t_{k}}-1})\leq q^{k-i_{t_{k}}+1}(a_{3}+\xi)E_{i_{t_{k}}-1}.

(34)

To further carry out the recurrence for (
34
), we must examine whether
i_{t_{k}}-1=i_{t_{k}-1}
holds. In fact, regardless of which scenario occurs, the following relation always holds:

\displaystyle q^{k-i_{t_{k}}+1}(a_{3}+\xi)E_{i_{t_{k}}-1}\leq q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{2}(E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1}+\xi E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-2}).

(35)

Indeed, (
35
) stems from the following fact:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle q^{k-i_{t_{k}}+1}(a_{3}+\xi)E_{i_{t_{k}}-1}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)a_{3}E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1},&i_{t_{k}}-1=i_{t_{k}-1};\\
q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)(E_{i_{t_{k}-1}}+\xi F_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1}),&i_{t_{k}}-1\neq i_{t_{k}-1};\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{2}E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1},&i_{t_{k}}-1=i_{t_{k}-1};\\
q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{2}E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1},&i_{t_{k}}-1\neq i_{t_{k}-1};\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{2}(E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1}+\xi E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-2}),&i_{t_{k}}-1=i_{t_{k}-1};\\
q^{k-i_{t_{k}-1}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{2}(E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-1}+\xi E_{i_{t_{k}-1}-2}),&i_{t_{k}}-1\neq i_{t_{k}-1}.\end{cases}

Thus, by repeating the reasoning from (
33
) to (
35
), we can obtain the following relation:

\displaystyle E_{k+1}\leq q^{k-t_{k}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(E_{1}+\xi E_{0})\leq q^{k-t_{k}}(a_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(1+\xi)E_{0}.

(37)

This completes the proof.

Recalling the fact that Algorithm
2
updates the approximate solution using (
23
),
we next introduce the following convergence result that is needed for our subsequent analysis. It is based on a modified version of Theorem 1 in
[
34
]
and Theorem 3.2 in
[
36
]
, but tailored for our special purposes here.

Lemma 16
.

[
34
,
36
]

Suppose
F(x)
satisfies Assumption
5
and
\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))\subseteq\ker(F^{\prime}(x))
for all
x\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})
,
where
x_{*}
is the unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution of
F(x)=0
. Then

(i) The iterative sequence
\{x_{k}\}
generated by

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\|(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})

satisfies the inclusion relation

x_{k}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})\text{ for all }k\geq 0.

(ii) If
F(x_{k})\neq 0
for some
k\geq 0
, we have
x_{k+1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}

and

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))}\right)\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}.

(38)

We next present the rate of convergence of the practical variant of ABNKAm (Algorithm
2
),
which also exhibits exponential convergence.
For the sake of simplicity, we first introduce some constants. Let

\varepsilon_{1}=\min\Big\{\frac{\eta(1+\eta^{2})+\sqrt{\eta^{2}(1+\eta^{2})^{2}+(1+\eta^{2})(1-2\eta^{2}-4\eta^{3})}}{1-2\eta^{2}-4\eta^{3}},\frac{-\eta+\sqrt{2\eta^{2}+2\eta}}{\eta^{2}+2\eta}\Big\}.

We can check that
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
.
For any
\gamma\in(\varepsilon_{1}/2,\varepsilon_{1})
, we define

C_{3}(\eta,\gamma):=\min\left\{\sqrt{\frac{1-2\eta\gamma+(1-2\eta)\gamma^{2}}{(1+\eta^{2})\gamma^{2}}},\sqrt{\frac{(1-2\eta\gamma)(1+\eta^{2})}{(1-2\eta^{2}-4\eta^{3})\gamma^{2}}}\right\}
,
and

\displaystyle C_{4}(\eta,\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\min\Big\{\frac{-(1+3d^{2})+\sqrt{(1+3d^{2})^{2}+8(1+d^{2})(d^{4}+d^{2}c-d^{2})}}{4(1+d^{2})},\frac{\sqrt{1+c}-1}{2},

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\sqrt{(4-3b_{3})^{2}+8(2-b_{3})(1-b_{3})(c-b_{3})}-(4-3b_{3})}{4(2-b_{3})}\Big\}.

We can easily verify that
C_{3}(\eta,\gamma)>1
and
C_{4}(\eta,\gamma)>0
.

Theorem 17
.

Let
F(x)
satisfy Assumption
5
with
x_{*}
being the unique
x_{0}
-minimum-norm solution of
F(x)=0
. Assume
\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))\subseteq\ker(F^{\prime}(x))
for all
x\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})
, and let
\{x_{k}\}
be the sequence generated by Algorithm
2
.
Assume there exists a constant
\gamma\in(\varepsilon_{1}/2,\varepsilon_{1})
such that the following conditions hold for all
k
:

(C1)

\kappa(F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}))\leq K
for some
K\in[1,C_{3}(\eta,\gamma))
,

(C2)

\beta_{k}\in[0,\beta_{\max})
for some
\beta_{\max}\in(0,C_{4}(\eta,\gamma))
,

(C3)

\sin^{2}\theta_{k}\leq\gamma
,

then the following conclusions hold:

(i)
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
for all
k\geq 0
.

(ii) If
F(x_{k})\neq 0
for some
k
, then
x_{k+1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
and

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq Q(k,t_{k})\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

where
t_{k}
is defined as in Lemma
15
and
Q(k,t_{k})
is given by
Q(k,t_{k})=q^{k-t_{k}}(b_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(1+\xi)
and

\displaystyle q=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+4b_{2}}}{2},\xi=q-b_{1},b_{1}+b_{2}\leq q<d^{2}<1,b_{3}+\xi<1

\displaystyle b_{1}=1+3\beta_{\max}+2\beta_{\max}^{2}-\frac{1-2\eta\delta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}\delta^{2}},b_{2}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+\beta_{\max},b_{3}=1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}}.

(iii) For sufficiently large
k
, the rate of convergence
Q(k,t_{k})
admits the following upper bound estimates depending on
the range of
t_{k}
:

•

Q(k,t_{k})\leq q
for
1\leq t_{k}\ll k+1
;

•

Q(k,t_{k})\leq\sqrt{q(b_{3}+\xi)}<d
for
1<t_{k}<\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor
;

•

Q(k,t_{k})\leq b_{3}+\xi
for
\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor\leq t_{k}\leq k+1
.

Proof.

We apply the mathematical induction.
From Assumption
5
(iv) and Lemma
16
, we have
x_{0}\in B_{\delta_{0}/4}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0}),x_{1}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
and
x_{0}-x_{*},x_{1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
.
We now assume without loss of generality that
x_{j}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0}),\,x_{j}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
for all
j\in\{0,1,\cdots,k\}
hold. Then it remains to show that
x_{k+1}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
and
x_{k+1}-x_{*}\in\ker(F^{\prime}(x_{*}))^{\perp}
.

Because the iterative formula (
23
) involves two different situations, namely (i)
\Delta_{k}\geq\varepsilon
and
\beta_{k}\in(0,\beta_{\max})
and (ii)
\Delta_{k}<\varepsilon
or
\beta_{k}\notin(0,\beta_{\max})
. We will discuss each case separately.

(i)
\Delta_{k}\geq\varepsilon
and
\beta_{k}\in(0,\beta_{\max})
.
Using the iterative formula (
22
) at the
k
-th step,
we follow the proof of Theorem
14
to deduce

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\beta_{k}+2\beta_{k}^{2}-\frac{(1-2\eta+\cot^{2}\theta_{k})}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))\sin^{2}\theta_{k}})\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

(39)

\displaystyle+(2\beta_{k}^{2}+\beta_{k})\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq b_{1}\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}+b_{2}\|x_{k-1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

where
b_{1}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+3\beta_{\max}+1-\frac{1-2\eta\delta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}\delta^{2}}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+3\beta_{\max}+1-c
and
b_{2}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+\beta_{\max}
. Noting that
0<\beta_{\max}<\frac{\sqrt{1+c}-1}{2}

it is easy to verify that
b_{1}+b_{2}=4\beta_{\max}^{2}+4\beta_{\max}+1-c<1
and
b_{2}>0
.

(ii)
\Delta_{k}<\varepsilon
or
\beta_{k}\notin(0,\beta_{\max})
.
In this case, we have

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\frac{\|F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}{\|F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})\|^{2}_{2}}F^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k})^{T}F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}(x_{k}).

Then according to Lemma
16
, we know (with
b_{3}=1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}}
)

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})\kappa^{2}(F_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}^{\prime}(x_{k}))})\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq b_{3}\|x_{k}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

(40)

Combining (
39
), (
40
), Theorem
14
and Lemma
15
, we obtain
the following estimate:

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2}\leq q^{k-t_{k}}(b_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(1+\xi)\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}^{2},

where
q=\frac{b_{1}+\sqrt{b_{1}^{2}+4b_{2}}}{2}
,
\xi=q-b_{1}
,
b_{1}+b_{2}\leq q<d^{2}<1
, and
b_{1}=1+3\beta_{\max}+2\beta_{max}^{2}-\frac{1-2\eta\delta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}\delta^{2}},\,b_{2}=2\beta_{\max}^{2}+\beta_{\max}
,
b_{3}=1-\frac{1-2\eta}{(1+\eta^{2})K^{2}}
.

Next, we verify that
b_{3}+\xi<1
. Indeed, we only need to verify that
b_{2}+(1-b_{3})b_{1}-(1-b_{3})^{2}<0
.
Substituting the expressions for
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, and using the conditions

.

On the other hand, we easily see

\|x_{k+1}-x_{*}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{q^{k-t_{k}}(b_{3}+\xi)^{t_{k}}(1+\xi)}\|x_{0}-x_{*}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}

(41)

that is to say
x_{k+1}\in B_{\delta_{0}/2}(x_{*})\subseteq B_{\delta_{0}}(x_{0})
.

Remark 18
.

Although both Theorems
14
-
17
require
\beta_{\text{max}}
to stay
in some certain range, we have observed very favorable results by simply
setting
\beta_{\text{max}}=+\infty
in all our numerical experiments (see Section
4
).

According to Theorem
17
, we know that the rate of convergence of Algorithm
2
depends
on the concrete values of
t_{k}
. If
t_{k}\ll k+1
, we always have
\rho<d^{2}
. This condition is relatively easy to satisfy, as evidenced by the numerical experiments (see Figure
2
) in Section
4
. If
t_{k}<\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor
, we have
\rho\leq\sqrt{q(b_{3}+\xi)}<d
.
If
t_{k}>\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor
, the upper bound estimate of the rate we have provided is
b_{3}+\xi
. But at the extreme case (i.e.,
t_{k}=k+1
), the ABNKAm degenerates to the ABNK2, and it is obvious to see that their rates of convergence are the same in this scenario. Therefore, we can always conclude that
the rate of convergence of the ABNKAm is less than or equal to that of the ABNK2.

(44)

we set

44

Further comments on the performance comparison between ABNKAm and other methods

From the numerical results presented above, we have observed significant variation in performance
among existing Kaczmarz-type methods across different problems. Specifically, row-action methods (NGRKm, MRNK, and NURK) are substantially less effective than block-oriented approaches (ABNK2, RB-CNK, MRBNK, and ABNKAm), with a notable decline in performance as problem size increases.

For sparse problems (i.e., those with sparse Jacobian matrices, excluding Problem
4.3
), the MRBNK method based on update formula (
6
) achieves the best CPU time on Problems
4.1
and
4.2
. On Problem
4.1
, MRBNK requires approximately 90 iterations, compared to 126 for RB-CNK and 2913 for ABNK2. On Problem
4.2
, iteration counts
range between 288 and 332 for MRBNK, between 399 and 470 for RB-CNK, and between 322 and 386 for ABNK2
across different scales. In contrast, the RB-CNK method, which employs a different greedy criterion than MRBNK but uses the same update formula, shows an advantage on Problem
4.4
, converging in about 83 iterations versus 100-120 for MRBNK, while ABNK2 fails to converge completely.

Notably, although the ABNK2 method derived from (
7
) avoids pseudoinverse computations, its per-iteration cost reduction is offset by increased iteration counts. Moreover, its performance degrades significantly on Problem
4.4
, and like all other existing Kaczmarz-type methods, it fails to solve Problem
4.5
.
For the dense Jacobian case (Problem
4.3
), ABNK2 leverages the averaging strategy to outperform pseudoinverse based methods RB-CNK and MRBNK in computational time. As the problem size grows from
10^{3}
to
10^{5}
, ABNK2’s runtime increases from 1 to over 2200 seconds, while RB-CNK and MRBNK increase from 6.3 to over 12000 and from 4.2 to nearly 8000 seconds, respectively.

In contrast to the performance variability exhibited by existing methods, the proposed ABNKAm method consistently outperforms all others in both iteration count and CPU time across all examples (Problems
4.1
-
4.5
). On sparse Problems
4.1
and
4.2
, it converges in approximately 9 and 180 iterations, respectively, surpassing MRBNK with speed-up factors of about 15 and 3. For Problem
4.4
, it requires only 24 iterations, outperforming RB-CNK by a factor of 4. Most notably, ABNKAm is the only method that successfully solves Problem
4.5
, converging within about 28 iterations. On the dense Problem
4.3
, it also surpasses ABNK2, achieving a nearly speed-up
2
. In summary, we have seen that ABNKAm converges stably, effectively and
consistently for all problems in this subsection, while the convergence behavior of all other numerical methods
is quite uncertain: each method may work well for some nonlinear examples, but may perform very poorly for other examples.

5
Concluding remarks

We have proposed a novel class of averaged block nonlinear Kaczmarz methods enhanced with momentum for solving nonlinear systems. An adaptive strategy for selecting step sizes and momentum parameters has been developed, relying solely on information from the iterative sequence. The proposed ABNKAm method has been validated through extensive numerical experiments, demonstrating not only high effectiveness but also remarkable competitiveness in both iteration count and computational time compared to existing nonlinear Kaczmarz-type methods.
Looking forward, the influence of greedy criteria on ABNKAm performance suggests a promising research direction. Developing specialized greedy criteria or efficient index subset selection strategies tailored to the ABNKAm framework could yield further computational gains, representing an interesting focus for our future work.
