Trivariate Splines on Fans of Hyperplane Arrangements and Koszul Homology
Carles Checa, Michael DiPasquale, Pablo Mazón, Thái Thành Nguy Ê ~ \tilde{\text{\^{E}}} n, Liana Sega, Prajwal Udanshive, Adam Van Tuyl, Nelly Villamizar

Abstract
. We study the space of splines 𝒮 𝒓 ​ ( Σ 𝒜 ) \mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}) where 𝒓 {\bm{r}} denotes a smoothness distribution and Σ 𝒜 \Sigma^{\mathscr{A}} is the fan of a central hyperplane arrangement 𝒜 \mathscr{A} in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . This is the first step in the analysis of splines on three-dimensional cross-cut partitions, which naturally generalize planar cross-cut partitions. We show that the Hilbert function of 𝒮 𝒓 ​ ( Σ 𝒜 ) \mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}) is bounded by an expression that involves the dimensions of specific Koszul homology modules constructed from the defining equations of the hyperplane arrangement 𝒜 \mathscr{A} and the smoothness distribution function. By exploiting this connection with Koszul homology, we are able to: 1) compute the dimension of the spline space in high degrees, 2) compute all values of the dimension of the spline space if 𝒜 \mathscr{A} is generic with five or fewer hyperplanes, and 3) compute the Hilbert function of the spline space if 𝒜 \mathscr{A} is a generic arrangement with sufficiently many hyperplanes and 𝒓 {\bm{r}} is a constant distribution. As an application of our methods, we compute dim 𝒮 d 0 ​ ( Σ 𝒜 ) \dim\mathcal{S}^{0}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}) and dim 𝒮 d 1 ​ ( Σ 𝒜 ) \dim\mathcal{S}^{1}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}) for all values of d d when 𝒜 \mathscr{A} is a generic arrangement.

1.
Introduction

For our purposes, a multivariate spline is a piecewise polynomial function on a subdivision of some domain in

\mathbb{R}^{m}
with prescribed smoothness across faces of the subdivision. Splines play a central
role in geometric modeling, finite element methods, and approximation
theory (see, e.g.,
[
25
]
for general background).

The study of splines has long been shaped by
connections with commutative and homological algebra, beginning with
the work of Chui and Wang
[
9
]
, Billera
[
2
]
, and
Billera and Rose
[
3
]
, which introduced algebraic and
homological techniques for understanding the dimension and structure
of spline spaces.
Schenck and Stillman refined these methods, making a crucial connection to ideals generated by powers of linear forms

[
32
,
34
,
35
,
22
]
.
Within the last decade, these ideas have been extended to include splines with enhanced smoothness across lower-dimensional
faces
[
43
]
and explicit lower bounds on the
dimension of splines on three-dimensional complexes

[
15
,
16
]
.
Additional developments include geometric
conditions on two-dimensional partitions under which the dimension of the spline space
admit a combinatorial formula
[
17
,
29
]
and an important counterexample to the Schenck-Stiller ‘
2r+1
’ conjecture, showing that the dimension of spline spaces on triangulations can have unexpected dimension in relatively large degree

[
36
,
44
]
.
These approaches have further been generalized to semialgebraic splines
[
41
,
13
,
14
]
, to algebraic frameworks for geometrically
continuous splines
[
27
]
, and continue to inspire
ongoing work, much of which is discussed in the surveys
[
33
]
and
[
26
]
.

In this paper, we consider splines on a subdivision of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
induced by a collection of planes through the origin. More precisely, let
\mathscr{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
where each
H_{i}
is a plane that contains the origin;
\mathscr{A}
is called a
central hyperplane arrangement
, where the adjective
central
indicates that each hyperplane contains the origin. The hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}
subdivides
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into a collection of regions. For instance, one plane divides
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into two regions, two distinct planes divide
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into four regions, and three distinct planes may divide
\mathbb{R}^{3}
into six regions (if the three planes contain a common line) or eight regions (if the three planes do not contain a common line). For an arbitrary hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the data of the subdivision induced by
\mathscr{A}
is encoded by the
fan

\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
of
\mathscr{A}
(see
Section

2
). We consider a smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and we study the space of functions
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
which are piecewise polynomial on the subdivision of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
induced by
\mathscr{A}
and continuously differentiable to order
{\bm{r}}(H_{i})
across each plane
H_{i}
. We say that a function in
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is a
spline on the fan

\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
. For a given non-negative integer
d
, our primary objective is to compute the dimension of the space
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
of functions
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
where the restriction of
F
to each region is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
.

Our setting is motivated by planar
cross-cut partitions
; these are partitions of a planar region by straight lines joining boundary points
[
9
,
35
]
.
The natural generalization to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a partition of a region by a collection of planes; let us call this a
three-dimensional cross-cut partition
. Our aim in this paper is to study the local case of a three-dimensional cross-cut partition. That is, if we consider a point at which some number of planes meet in a three-dimensional cross-cut partition and the cells of the partition surrounding that point, we obtain (up to translation) a partition by planes that pass through the origin. A full study of the local case is a natural first step in the analysis of three-dimensional cross-cut partitions, which will be a topic of future research. This local-to-global analysis for splines is well established; for instance, Schumaker’s formula for splines on two-dimensional cells yields the fundamental non-trivial expression in his lower bound
[
37
]
. See also the two sets of companion papers
[
13
,
14
]
and
[
15
,
16
]
which both follow the local-to-global strategy.

Planar cross-cut partitions are some of the first partitions for which dimension formulas were computed in all degrees. For constant smoothness distribution
\bm{r}
, the dimension of the spline space over a cross-cut partition was computed by Chui and Wang
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
when no three lines of the cross-cut partition meet at a point. For arbitrary cross-cut partitions, Schenck and Stillman
[
35
, Theorem 5.3]
proved that the module of splines is a free module over the polynomial ring and thus the dimension of splines over a cross-cut partition is always given by a well-known lower bound of Schumaker
[
37
]
.

The situation for splines on the fan
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is significantly more complicated.
The module
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is not usually free and even if some genericity conditions are imposed on
\mathscr{A}
, we show in
Theorem

8.2
that
\dim\mathcal{S}^{1}_{4}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
can exhibit a dependence on geometry that is strikingly similar to the well-known Morgan-Scott split (c.f. Section 6 of
[
1
]
).

The novelty in this paper is to exhibit
a connection between the Hilbert functions of these
spline spaces and the first Koszul homology modules constructed
from the linear forms defining
\mathscr{A}
.
To state one of our main results,
if
\mathscr{A}
is the collection of hyperplanes

\mathscr{A}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
, let
\ell_{i}
be a linear form in
R=\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]

that vanishes on
H_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Suppose
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is a smoothness distribution on
\mathscr{A}
.
We let

\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}=(\ell_{1}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1},\ldots,\ell_{n}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{n})+1})

denote the sequence of powers of these linear forms.
The following is the main result of the paper in simplified form when
\mathscr{A}
is
generic
(that is, no three hyperplanes intersect along a line).

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.3
)
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is a generic hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
n
hyperplanes and
{\bm{r}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution. If
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
is the fan of
\mathscr{A}
,
then for all
d\geq 0
the dimension of the spline space
\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is given by

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}+\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d}-\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d},

where
{\sf{H}}_{i}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
denotes the
i
-th Koszul homology module
of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
(see
Section

4
).

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we use the
Billera-Schenck-Stillman chain complex
, which is a standard tool to study splines from an algebraic and homological perspective (see
Section

3
). The main point is that
\mathcal{S}_{d}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
is (essentially) the top homology of a chain complex
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
. Thus, using the Euler-Poincaré characteristic, one can compute
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma)
provided one knows the Hilbert functions of the modules in the chain complex
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
and the Hilbert functions of the remaining homology modules of
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
(see
Proposition

3.2
). Our main insight is that, in the case of the fan
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
of a hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}
, the module
H_{1}(\mathcal{J}[\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}]_{\bullet})

can be related to the Koszul homology of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
.
In particular, we show in
Theorem

4.2
that
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
can be bounded by an expression that involves the dimension of Koszul homology modules. This bound is an equality if
\mathscr{A}
is generic, giving the statement of
Theorem

1.1
.

By unlocking this connection between
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})

and
{\sf{H}}_{i}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
, we can derive new results
about the Hilbert function of
\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})

by employing results and techniques related to Koszul homology.
We highlight some of these new results.

In one direction, if
d\gg 0
, then the
dimensions of the Koszul homology modules in
Theorem

1.1

become zero, so the formula for
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})

reduces to the first two terms in the statement. In fact, this is true for arbitrary arrangements (not just generic arrangements), as we show in
Theorem

4.3
. We next take up how large
d
must be for this vanishing to occur. This is given by the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the Koszul homology modules. The regularity of the Koszul homology modules can be bounded by the regularity of the ideal generated by the elements of the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}
, which in turn can be bounded by the values of the smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}
and an interpolation statistic of the hyperplane arrangement (see
Theorem

5.6
).

In the remainder of the paper (
Section

6
to
Section

8
) our objective is to compute
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
for
all
integers
d\geq 0
for certain
generic
arrangements. For instance, if
\mathscr{A}
is generic and has three, four, or five hyperplanes, we compute

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
for all
d
. For
the case of three or four hyperplanes (
Theorem

6.4
), we can explicitly
compute the dimensions of the Koszul homology modules.
In the case of five hyperplanes given in
Theorem

6.5
,
we show that computing the desired Hilbert function reduces
to computing the Hilbert function of
R/\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
, where
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
is the ideal generated by the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
.
For five hyperplanes, we describe an algorithm that uses inverse
systems to reduce the computation to computing the Hilbert function
of a set of fat points on a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. One
can then use the algorithm of Catalisano
[
7
]

to compute this new Hilbert function.

If
\mathscr{A}
is generic and has
three, four, or five hyperplanes, then
the Hilbert function of
\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
depends only upon
the number of hyperplanes and the smoothness distribution. However,
as we show in
Example

6.6
and
Theorem

8.2
, if
\mathscr{A}
is generic with six or more hyperplanes,
then one must take into consideration the equations of the hyperplanes. This is a consequence of the fact that, even if
\mathscr{A}
is generic,
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})

depends upon the choice of the linear forms
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}

if
n\geq 6
.

We next restrict to the case when
\mathscr{A}
has a constant smoothness distribution (that is,
{\bm{r}}(H_{i})=r
for all
1\leq i\leq n
for a fixed integer
r\geq 0
) and many hyperplanes. More precisely, if
\mathscr{A}
is sufficiently generic and has at least
\binom{r+3}{2}
hyperplanes, in
Theorem

7.3
we compute
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
for all
d\geq 0
. In
Section

8
, we close out the paper by applying our results to compute the dimension of
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
splines on fans of generic arrangements in all degrees.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the relevant definitions and results from the theory of cones, fans, hyperplane arrangements, and graded modules.

Throughout, let
R=\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
be the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra of polynomials in
m
variables, graded by total degree, and let
R_{d}
denote the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
.
A
linear form
means an element of
R_{1}
(hence homogeneous, with no constant term).

2.1.
Cones, fans, and hyperplane arrangements

We follow
[
45
]
for cones and fans, and we use
[
30
]
as a standing reference for hyperplane arrangements (see also
[
40
]
for a standard introductory survey on hyperplane arrangements).

2.1.1.
Hyperplanes, half-spaces, and cones

A
hyperplane

H=H_{\ell}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the zero locus of a linear form
\ell\in R_{1}
, i.e.

H_{\ell}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ell(\bm{x})=0\}.

The associated closed half-spaces are

(2.1)

H_{\ell,+}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ell(\bm{x})\geq 0\},\qquad H_{\ell,-}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ell(\bm{x})\leq 0\},

and we also write
H_{\ell,0}=H_{\ell}
. Observe that
H_{\ell,+}\cap H_{\ell,-}=H_{\ell,0}
.

A

cone
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a finite intersection of half-spaces of the form (
2.1
); its
dimension
is the dimension of its linear span.
A
face
of a cone is the intersection of the cone with a supporting hyperplane (hence again a cone).
A cone is called
essential
if
\{\bm{0}\}
is a face of the cone; equivalently, it contains no positive-dimensional linear subspace.

Definition 2.1
.

A
fan

\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a collection of cones
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s}\}
with the two properties:

(1)

every face of a cone in
\Sigma
is also in
\Sigma
, and

(2)

the intersection of any two cones in
\Sigma
is a face of both.

We call a fan
\Sigma

complete
if
\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma=\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

For a fan
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we put
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma
.
The
dimension
of
\Sigma
is the maximal dimension of a cone in
\Sigma
.
Maximal cones under inclusion are called
facets
, and
\Sigma
is
pure
if all facets have the same dimension.
We call
\Sigma

essential
if
\{\bm{0}\}
is a cone of
\Sigma
(equivalently, every cone of
\Sigma
is essential).

Remark 2.2
.

Ziegler
[
45
]
calls a cone (resp
.
fan)
pointed
if
\{\bm{0}\}
is a face of the cone (resp. a cone of the fan).
We use the adjective
essential
to align terminology with that of hyperplane arrangements.

If
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a pure
m
-dimensional fan, a cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is an
interior face
if it is contained in the intersection of two facets of
\Sigma
, and a
boundary face
otherwise.
For
0\leq i\leq m
, let
\Sigma_{i}
be the set of
i
-dimensional cones of
\Sigma
, and let
\Sigma_{i}^{\circ}
be the subset of interior
i
-dimensional cones.

A pure
m
-dimensional fan
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
hereditary
if, for any two facets
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
,
\sigma
can be connected to
\sigma^{\prime}
by a sequence of facets

\sigma=\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k}=\sigma^{\prime}
so that
\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\sigma_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
\dim(\sigma_{i}\cap\sigma_{i+1})=m-1
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

Remark 2.3
.

A complete fan
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is always hereditary. Henceforth, when we refer to a fan
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, it will always be pure and
m
-dimensional.

2.1.2.
Arrangements

A
hyperplane arrangement
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}

is a finite collection of hyperplanes
\mathscr{A}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
. By definition, each hyperplane is a linear space and so contains the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

The
intersection lattice
of
\mathscr{A}
is
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})=\{\cap_{H\in F}H:F\subseteq\mathcal{A}\}

that is, the collection of all subspaces obtained by intersecting some subset
of hyperplanes in
\mathscr{A}
(including
\mathbb{R}^{m}
itself, as the empty intersection),
partially ordered by reverse inclusion. We write
\mathscr{L}_{i}(\mathscr{A})=\{X\in\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{A}):\mathrm{codim}(X)=i\}
.

We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
essential
if
\mathrm{codim}(\bigcap_{H\in\mathscr{A}}H)=m
, equivalently,
\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{A}}H=\{\mathbf{\bm{0}}\}.

An arrangement
\mathscr{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
generic
if for every choice of

k\leq m
distinct hyperplanes
H_{i_{1}},\dots,H_{i_{k}}
in
\mathscr{A}
gives a rank
k
flat, i.e., we have

\mathrm{codim}(H_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i_{k}})=k.

Observe that a generic arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with at least
m
hyperplanes is necessarily essential.

2.1.3.
The fan of an arrangement

For a central arrangement
\mathscr{A}=\{H_{\ell_{1}},\ldots,H_{\ell_{n}}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{n})\in\{-,0,+\}^{n}
, define

C_{\epsilon}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}H_{\ell_{i},\epsilon_{i}}

and
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}=\{\,C_{\epsilon}:\epsilon\in\{-,0,+\}^{n}\},

where
H_{\ell_{i},\epsilon_{i}}
are the spaces defined in
Equation

2.1
.
One can check that
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
is a fan, which we call the
fan of

\mathscr{A}
. It is a complete, pure,
m
-dimensional fan.
Moreover,
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
is essential if and only if
\mathscr{A}
is essential.

The facets of
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
are the closures of the connected components of

\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathscr{A}}H
, i.e. the
regions
(often called
chambers
)
of
\mathscr{A}
.

2.2.
Graded modules and Hilbert function

Following the notation above, the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
carries its standard grading
R=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}R_{d},

where multiplication maps
R_{i}\times R_{j}
into
R_{i+j}
.
In a similar way, an
R
-module
M
is called
graded
if there is a direct sum decomposition
M\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
, where
M_{i}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and there is a multiplication map
R_{i}\times M_{j}\to M_{i+j}
given by
(f,m)\to fm
.
Our main reference of this section is
[
18
, Chapter 1]
, see also
[
6
, Chapter 1]
.

Definition 2.4
.

If
M=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M_{i}
is a graded
R
-module, the
Hilbert function
of
M

is given by the dimensions of its graded pieces,
i\mapsto\dim M_{i}
.
The
Hilbert series
of
M
, denoted
\mathrm{Hilb}_{M}(t)
, is the generating function of the Hilbert function, namely

\mathrm{Hilb}_{M}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim(M_{i})t^{i}
.

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
M(a)
as the module isomorphic to
M
whose grading is defined by
M(a)_{i}=M_{a+i}
.
We say that
M(a)
is the module
M
with grading
shifted
by
a
.

If
M
is a finitely generated graded
R
-module, then the Hilbert function
\dim M_{i}
of
M
is an alternating sum of binomial coefficients of the form
\binom{i+m-1-a}{m-1}
, for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, where we adopt the convention that
\binom{A}{B}=0
whenever
A<0
or
B>a
. If
i\gg 0
then the values of
\dim M_{i}
agree with a polynomial in the variable
i
, called the
Hilbert polynomial
of
M
and denoted
{\sf{HP}}_{M}(i)
.

Moreover, if
M
is a finitely generated graded
R
-module, the Hilbert series of
M
is a rational function and has the form

\mathrm{Hilb}_{M}(t)=\frac{p(t)}{(1-t)^{m}},

where
p(t)
is a polynomial with integer coefficients (e.g.,
see
[
6
, Proposition 4.4.1]
).

2.3.
Conventions for direct sums

Let
\{M_{w}\}_{w\in W}
be a finite collection of
R
-modules indexed by a finite set
W
, with
|W|
elements. An element of

\bigoplus_{w\in W}M_{w}
is written either as
(m_{w})_{w\in W}
or as

\sum_{w\in W}m_{w}b_{w}
, where
b_{w}
denotes the standard basis symbol of the

w
-th summand. We also write
\bigoplus_{w\in W}M_{w}b_{w}
.
In the special case where
M_{w}=M
for all
w\in W
, we also write
\bigoplus_{w\in W}Mb_{w}
as either
M^{W}
or
M^{|W|}
.
For example, if
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a pure
m
-dimensional fan with
f
facets, we write either
R^{\Sigma_{m}}
or
R^{f}
for
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}Rb_{\sigma}
.

Suppose now that each
M_{w}
is a graded cyclic
R
-module with homogeneous generator
e_{w}
of degree
a_{w}
. By abuse of notation, we also denote by
e_{w}

the corresponding generator of the

\bigoplus_{w\in W}M_{w}\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W}Re_{w}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigoplus_{w\in W}R(-a_{w})b_{w}\cong\bigoplus_{w\in W}Re_{w},

Let

Definition 2.5

Let

(2.2)

Remark 2.6
.

Splines are usually defined as piecewise polynomial functions that are differentiable to some order. In fact,
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
consists of those functions

so that

{\bm{r}}(\tau)

If

that for every face

Remark 2.7

In this paper, we consider smoothness distributions that may vary across codimension one faces. 2.5.
Reduction to the essential case

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a fan which is
not
essential, then there is a non-trivial linear space
V
of dimension
m-i
(
i\geq 1
) which is contained in all of the cones of
\Sigma
. Changing coordinates if necessary, we may assume that
V
is defined by the vanishing of the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}
. Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{i}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{i}
defined in coordinates by
\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
. Since
V\subset\sigma
for every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the collection of cones
\pi(\Sigma)=\{\pi(\sigma):\sigma\in\Sigma\}
is a fan in
\mathbb{R}^{i}
. Moreover,
\sigma\to\pi(\sigma)
is a bijection between
j
-dimensional cones
\sigma\in\Sigma_{j}
and
(j-(m-i))
-dimensional cones
\pi(\Sigma)_{j-(m-i)}
, for
m-i\leq j\leq m
, with inverse
\sigma^{\prime}\to\pi^{-1}(\sigma^{\prime})
. It follows that a smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}:\Sigma^{\circ}_{m-1}\to\mathbb{R}
naturally induces a smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}^{\prime}:\pi(\Sigma)^{\circ}_{i-1}\to\mathbb{R}
via
{\bm{r}}^{\prime}={\bm{r}}\circ\pi^{-1}
. We have the following structural result.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a pure
m
-dimensional fan which is not essential. Without loss of generality, suppose that every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
contains the vector space
V
defined by the vanishing of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}
and let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{i}
be the orthogonal projection defined by
\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
. Then

\displaystyle S^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)\cong\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}^{\prime}}(\pi(\Sigma))\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}],

\displaystyle\dim S^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\binom{j+m-i}{m-i}\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}^{\prime}}_{d-j}(\pi(\Sigma)),

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{and }\quad\mathrm{Hilb}_{\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)}(t)=\frac{\mathrm{Hilb}_{\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}^{\prime}}(\pi(\Sigma))}(t)}{(1-t)^{m-i}}.

Proof.

The proof that
S^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)\cong S^{\bm{r}}(\pi(\Sigma))\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}]
is essentially the same as the proof of
[
38
, Proposition 3.1]
, so we only give a sketch. Take any spline
F\in\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}^{\prime}}(\pi(\Sigma))
and pull it back via
\pi
to a function
F\circ\pi:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
. One then checks that
F\circ\pi\in\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma)
. Then define the map

\phi:\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}^{\prime}}(\pi(\Sigma))\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{R}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}]\to\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma)

on simple tensors by
\phi(F\otimes p)=(F\circ\pi)\cdot p
. This is an injective map, and one needs to show that
\phi
is surjective. This can be done by considering the constituents of a spline
G\in\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma)
as polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}]
with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}[x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{m}]
. One shows that the terms of each constituent with a fixed coefficient fit together to make a spline in
\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma)
which is the image of a simple tensor under
\phi
, establishing surjectivity. See the proof of
[
38
, Proposition 3.1]
for further details. The equations for the Hilbert function and Hilbert series of
S^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
follow from the first isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Suppose that
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a pure, three-dimensional fan. If
\Sigma
is not essential, then we may assume that every cone of

contains the line defined by the vanishing of

and

3.
Homological methods for splines

In this section we recall homological methods for splines that were introduced by Billera
[
2
]
and were later refined in an important way by Schenck and Stillman
[
35
]
. We also prove a presentation for a particular homology module that is crucial for the remainder of the paper.

3.1.
Chain complexes for splines

Throughout this section we use the conventions outlined in
Section

2.3
. Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a pure, hereditary,
m
-dimensional fan. Denote by
\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
the chain complex

\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}\colon\quad 0\longrightarrow R^{\Sigma_{m}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{m}}R^{\Sigma^{\circ}_{m-1}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{m-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}R^{\Sigma^{\circ}_{1}}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}R^{\Sigma_{0}^{\circ}}\longrightarrow 0\ ,

which is the cellular chain complex (relative to its boundary) of the finite CW complex
X
obtained by intersecting
\Sigma
with a solid disc of radius
1
.
We shall also use the notation
\mathcal{R}_{i}=R^{\Sigma_{i}}
.
The matrices representing the differentials
\partial_{i}
have entries which are all
0,1,
or
-1
. As
\Sigma
is not compact, some care must be taken here – the homology modules of
\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
compute the cellular homology of the CW complex
X
relative to its boundary with coefficients in
R
(the so-called
Borel-Moore
homology of
\Sigma
relative to its boundary). We will need an explicit description of the cellular boundary maps only when
m=3
in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
, and we will explicitly describe them there. Readers may refer to
[
11
]
for a general description of these boundary maps.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a complete and essential fan. For each
\tau\in\Sigma_{m-1}
, let
{\ell}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}
be a linear form vanishing on
\tau
, and define

J(\tau)=\langle{\ell}_{\tau}^{{\bm{r}}(\tau)+1}\rangle
.
For all
\beta\in\Sigma_{i}
, where
0\leq i\leq m-1
, define

J(\beta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Sigma_{m-1}\\
\tau\supseteq\beta\end{subarray}}J(\tau).

Collecting these ideals yields a subcomplex of
\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
denoted
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
, given by

\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}\colon\quad 0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}J(\sigma)=0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\circ}_{m-1}}J(\tau)\xrightarrow{\partial_{m-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Sigma^{\circ}_{1}}J(\beta)\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}J(\bm{0})\longrightarrow 0\ .

The chain complex introduced by Billera in
[
2
]
and modified by Schenck and Stillman in
[
35
]
is the quotient of the complex
\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
by
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
, which we denote by
\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
:

(3.1)

\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}\colon 0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{m}}{R}\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{m}}\bigoplus_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\circ}_{m-1}}{R}/J(\tau)\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{m-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{2}}\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Sigma^{\circ}_{1}}R/J(\beta)\xrightarrow{\bar{\partial}_{1}}\dfrac{R}{J(\bm{0})}\rightarrow 0.

If the fan
\Sigma
is fixed, we will write
\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}
,
\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}
, and
\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}
to denote the chain complexes
\mathcal{R}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
,
\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
, and
\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\bullet}
, respectively. We will sometimes write

\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}}
if we want to highlight the specific smoothness
condition we are considering.

A key observation here is that
Equation

2.2
implies that the top homology module of the chain complex
Equation

3.1
satisfies

{\mathrm{H}}_{m}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})\cong S^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
.
We summarize some well-known properties of
\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}[\Sigma]_{\,\bullet}
when
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a fan (see for instance
[
32
,
35
,
43
,
15
]
).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a fan with smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}
, then the following hold:

(1)

\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)\cong{\mathrm{H}}_{3}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})\cong R\oplus{\mathrm{H}}_{2}(\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})
;

(2)

{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})={\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})=0
;

(3)

{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})\cong{\mathrm{H}}_{i-1}(\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})
for
i=1,2
.

The Hilbert function of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
can be computed from the Hilbert functions of the terms of
\mathcal{J}_{\bullet}
and its lower homologies (this was Billera’s crucial insight in
[
2
]
). We record the three-dimensional version of this observation, which is sufficient for our purposes. The proof is straightforward homological algebra; see e.g.
[
15
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a complete fan with smoothness distribution
{\bm{r}}
, then

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{1}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}-\dim(R/J(\bm{0}))_{d}+\dim{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}_{\bullet})_{d}.

In particular,

\dim\mathcal{S}^{r}_{d}(\Sigma)\geq 2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{1}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}-\dim(R/J(\bm{0}))_{d}.

3.2.

Proof.

For each ray

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an essential hyperplane arrangement and

the fan of

{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}/{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}\cong{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}/{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}.

In particular,
{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}[\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}]_{\bullet})\cong{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}/{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}
.

Proof.

The final statement is immediate from Lemma
3.3
, so we prove the isomorphism
{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}/{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}\cong{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}/{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}.
Consider the
R
-module homomorphism

\phi:\bigoplus\limits_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}Re_{\tau}\to\bigoplus\limits_{H\in\mathscr{A}}Re_{H}
defined by
\phi(e_{\tau})=e_{H}
if
\tau\subseteq H
. This is well-defined since a codimension one cone
\tau
can only be contained in one hyperplane of
\mathscr{A}
.
In the remainder of this proof we let
H_{\tau}
be the unique hyperplane in
\mathscr{A}
which contains
\tau
. We also have that
\phi
is a graded map since we assume
{\bm{r}}
is constant along hyperplanes.

We first claim that
\phi(V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma})=V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Clearly if
\sum f_{\tau}e_{\tau}\in V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}
then

\phi(\sum\limits_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}}f_{\tau}e_{\tau})=

\sum\limits_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}}f_{\tau}e_{H_{\tau}}
is in
V^{\bm{r}}_{\mathscr{A}}.

So
\phi(V^{\bm{r}}_{\Sigma})\subseteq V^{\bm{r}}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Now suppose that
\sum_{H\in\mathscr{A}}f_{H}e_{H}\in V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Then, by definition,
\sum_{H\in\mathscr{A}}f_{H}{\ell}_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}=0
. Now we may take any collection
\{\tau_{H}\}_{H\in\mathscr{A}}\subseteq\Sigma_{2}
so that
\tau_{H}\subseteq H
. Then
\sum\limits_{H\in\mathscr{A}}f_{H}e_{\tau_{H}}\in\bigoplus\limits_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}}Re_{\tau}
and
\sum f_{H}{\ell}_{\tau_{H}}^{{\bm{r}}(\tau_{H})+1}=\sum f_{H}{\ell}_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}=0
. So
\phi(V^{\bm{r}}_{\Sigma})=V^{\bm{r}}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Next we claim that
\phi(V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma})=V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
. First suppose that
\sum\limits_{\tau\supseteq\gamma}f_{\tau}e_{\tau}\in V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}
is one of the
R
-module generators of

. Then
\phi(\sum\limits_{\tau\supseteq\gamma}f_{\tau}e_{\tau})=\sum\limits_{\tau\supseteq\gamma}f_{\tau}e_{H_{\tau}},
and
\sum f_{\tau}{\ell}_{H_{\tau}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{\tau})+1}=\sum f_{\tau}{\ell}_{\tau}^{{\bm{r}}(\tau)+1}=0,
and the hyperplanes
H_{\tau}
all intersect along the line
W
which is the span of the ray
\gamma
. So
\phi(\sum\limits_{\tau\supseteq\gamma}f_{\tau}e_{\tau})\in V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}.
Now suppose that
W\in\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathscr{A})
and
\sum\limits_{H\supseteq W}f_{H}e_{H}\in V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
is one of the
R
-module generators of
V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
. Since
\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}
is the fan induced by
\mathscr{A}
, there is a ray
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
so that the span of
\gamma
is
W
and for every

so that

there exists a
\tau_{H}\in\Sigma_{2}
which contains
\gamma
and whose span is
H
. Then observe that

\sum\limits_{\tau_{H}\supseteq\gamma}f_{H}{\ell}_{\tau_{H}}^{{\bm{r}}(\tau_{H})+1}=\sum\limits_{\tau_{H}\supseteq\gamma}f_{H}{\ell}_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}=0,

so
\sum\limits_{\tau_{H}\supseteq\gamma}f_{H}e_{\tau_{H}}\in V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}
. Clearly
\phi(\sum\limits_{\tau_{H}\supseteq\gamma}f_{H}e_{\tau_{H}})=\sum\limits_{H\supseteq W}f_{H}e_{H}
. So
\phi(V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma})=V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

Now consider
\ker(\phi)
– clearly this is generated as an
R
-module by the differences

D_{1}=\{e_{\tau}-e_{\tau^{\prime}}:\tau,\tau^{\prime}\subseteq H\mbox{ for some }H\in\mathscr{A}\}.

Since
{\ell}_{\tau}={\ell}_{\tau^{\prime}}
if
\tau,\tau^{\prime}
are both subsets of the same hyperplane,

whenever
e_{\tau}-e_{\tau^{\prime}}\in\ker(\phi)
. Since these span

\ker(\phi)\subseteq V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}
.

Now we claim that
\ker(\phi)\subseteq V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}
also. For this we observe that
\ker(\phi)
is also generated as an
R
-module by the differences

D_{2}=\{e_{\tau}-e_{\tau^{\prime}}:\tau,\tau^{\prime}\subseteq H\mbox{ for some }H\in\mathscr{A}\mbox{ and }\tau\cap\tau^{\prime}=\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}\}.

To prove this, suppose that
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}

, which is the hyperplane arrangement in the linear space

Putting everything together, we have

\dfrac{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}}\cong\dfrac{V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}/\ker(\phi)}{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma}/\ker(\phi)}\cong\dfrac{\phi(V^{{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma})}{\phi(V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\Sigma})}\cong\dfrac{V^{\bm{r}}_{\mathscr{A}}}{V^{1,{\bm{r}}}_{\mathscr{A}}}.\qed

4.
Connection to Koszul Homology

In this section we use
Lemma

3.4
to relate the homology module
{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}[\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}]_{\bullet})
to a standard tool in commutative algebra, namely
Koszul homology
. We first recall the definition of Koszul homology and a few of its salient properties.

Let
{\Bbbk}
be a field (
{\Bbbk}=\mathbb{R}
for our purposes),
R={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
in
R
. We let
I
be the ideal generated by
{\bm{y}}
.
Denote by
{\sf{K}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
the Koszul complex on
{\bm{y}}
and by
{\sf{H}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
its homology. To describe
{\sf{K}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
, we let
F
denote a free
R
-module with basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
and we let
\varphi\colon F\to I
denote the
R
-module homomorphism with
\varphi(e_{i})=y_{i}
. Then we have
{\sf{K}}_{i}({\bm{y}})=\bigwedge^{i}F
and the differential
\partial=\partial^{{\sf{K}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})}
of
{\sf{K}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
is described for
z_{1},\dots,z_{i}\in F
by

\partial_{i}(z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge z_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(-1)^{j+1}\varphi(z_{j})z_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{z_{j}}\wedge\cdots\wedge z_{i}.

It is clear that
{\sf{H}}_{0}({\bm{y}})=R/I
. Furthermore,
{\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}})=\ker(\partial_{1})/\text{Im}(\partial_{2})
can be described as follows. The module
\ker(\partial_{1})
is the
module of syzygies
of
{\bm{y}}
, namely

(4.1)

\ker(\partial_{1})=\ker(\varphi)=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}e_{i}\in F\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}y_{i}=0\Big\}.

The map
\partial_{2}\colon F\wedge F\to F
is described for
z,z^{\prime}\in F
by

\partial_{2}(z\wedge z^{\prime})=\varphi(z)z^{\prime}-\varphi(z^{\prime})z,

and its image is generated by the elements of
F
of the form
y_{i}e_{j}-y_{j}e_{i}
with
i,j\in[n]
,
i<j
, which are called
Koszul syzygies
of
{\bm{y}}
.

When
{\bm{y}}
is a minimal generating set of
I
, Koszul homology is independent, up to isomorphism, on the choice of
{\bm{y}}
and in this case we also write
{\sf{H}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}(I)={\sf{H}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
.

It is known, see
[
6
, Proposition 1.6.5(b)]
, that the ideal
I
annihilates
{\sf{H}}_{\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}}({\bm{y}})
. In particular, if
R/I
is finite length, then
{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})
is finite length for all
i
.

4.1.
Koszul homology and generic three-dimensional hyperplane arrangements

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a central hyperplane arrangement and
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
a smoothness distribution (c.f.,
Definition

2.9
). We put

and denote by
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
the sequence
(\ell_{H_{i}}^{r_{i}+1}:1\leq i\leq n)
and by
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
the ideal generated by the
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
.

Recall that a hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is called

if no three hyperplanes of
\mathscr{A}
intersect along a line. We now come
to one of the main results of the paper that
shows a tight connection between

and Koszul homology. In particular, we can now relate some of the homological groups that appear in Section

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an essential hyperplane arrangement and
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution. Then

(1)

{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}}_{\bullet})
is a quotient of the first Koszul homology
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
.

(2)

{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}}_{\bullet})
has finite length.

(3)

If
\mathscr{A}
is generic, then
{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}}_{\bullet})\cong{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
.

Proof.

We apply Koszul homology to the elements
of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
. In particular,
we have the following maps

\cdots\longrightarrow{\sf{K}}_{2}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\sf{K}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}I\longrightarrow 0.

We first prove
(1)
.
By
Lemma

3.4
,

{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}})=V_{\mathscr{A}}^{\bm{r}}/V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
.
Since
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})=\ker(\partial_{1})/\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})
, it suffices to prove that
\ker(\partial_{1})=V_{\mathscr{A}}^{\bm{r}}
and
\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})\subseteq V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
. By (
4.1
), we have

\ker(\partial_{1})=\Big\{\sum_{H\in\mathscr{A}}\alpha_{H}e_{H}\in F\colon\sum_{H\in\mathscr{A}}\alpha_{H}\ell_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}=0\Big\}.

Note we are using
\mathscr{A}
for our indexing
set of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
rather than
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
But the set on the right is exactly how

V_{\mathscr{A}}^{\bm{r}}
was defined in
(
3.2
), thus giving

\ker(\partial_{1})=V^{\bm{r}}_{\mathscr{A}}
.

The image
\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})
is generated by elements of the form

\ell_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}e_{H_{1}}-\ell_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}e_{H_{2}}
.
Since
W=H_{1}\cap H_{2}\in\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathscr{A})
,
\ell_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}e_{H_{1}}-\ell_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}e_{H_{2}}\in V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
. Thus
\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})\subseteq V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
.

For
(2)
, recall that
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
annihilates
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
. Since
\mathscr{A}
is essential, there are three linearly independent linear forms among
\{\ell_{H}:H\in\mathscr{A}\}
. It follows that, up to a change of coordinates, we may assume that
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
has a power of
x
, a power of
y
, and a power of
z
among its generators. Hence a power of the maximal ideal
\langle x,y,z\rangle
is contained in
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
and so annihilates
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
. It follows that
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
has finite length.

For
(3)
, suppose
\mathscr{A}
is generic. If
W\in\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathscr{A})
is a line, then (since
\mathscr{A}
is generic)
W
is contained in only two hyperplanes, say
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. By definition
Equation

3.3
,
we have

\displaystyle V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\sum_{H\supseteq W}f_{H}e_{H}:W\in\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathscr{A}),\sum_{H\supseteq W}f_{H}{\ell}_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}=0\Big\},\text{\; hence}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{f_{H_{1}}e_{H_{1}}+f_{H_{2}}e_{H_{2}}:W\in\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathscr{A})~\mbox{and}~H_{1},H_{2}\supseteq W,f_{H_{1}}{\ell}_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}+f_{H_{2}}{\ell}_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}=0\right\}.

Because
\ell_{H_{1}}
and
\ell_{H_{2}}
are linear forms,

f_{H_{1}}{\ell}_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}+f_{H_{2}}{\ell}_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}=0
if and only if

\ell_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}\mid f_{H_{1}}
and

\ell_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}\mid f_{H_{2}}
. Using
this observation, it can be shown that as an
R
-module,

V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
is generated by elements of
the form
\ell_{H_{2}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}e_{H_{1}}-\ell_{H_{1}}^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}e_{H_{2}}
. But these elements
are also the generators of

\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})
. Consequently,
\mbox{im}(\partial_{2})=V_{\mathscr{A}}^{{\bm{r}},1}
, and so
{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{{\bm{r}}})\cong{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
.
∎

As a consequence of
Theorem

4.2
and
Proposition

3.2
, we relate the dimension of spline spaces on the fan of a hyperplane arrangement to Koszul homology.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an essential hyperplane arrangement and
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution. If
d\geq 0
, then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})\leq 2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{1}^{\mathscr{A}}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}+\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d}-\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d}.

If
d\gg 0
, then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}.

If
\mathscr{A}
is generic and
d\geq 0
, then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}+\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d}-\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})_{d}.

Proof.

The first inequality is immediate from
Proposition

3.2
,
Theorem

4.2
(1)
,
and the fact that
R/J(\bm{0})={\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
. The equality for
d\gg 0
follows from
Proposition

3.2
since
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\ell^{r})
has finite length (
Theorem

4.2
(2)
) and
R/J(\bm{0})
has finite length.

For the final equality, suppose
\mathscr{A}
is generic. By
Theorem

4.2
(3)
,
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})\cong{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\mathcal{J}^{\bm{r}}_{\bullet})
.
Thus by
Proposition

3.2
, the inequality
given in the previous paragraph is an equality in
the case that
\mathscr{A}
is generic. To complete the proof it
suffices to show that

2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}^{\mathscr{A}}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{1}^{\mathscr{A}}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}=2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}.

Clearly
2\dim R_{d}=2\binom{d+2}{2}
. We show that

(4.2)

\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma_{2}^{\mathscr{A}}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma_{1}^{\mathscr{A}}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}=2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}.

To prove
Equation

4.2
, observe that if
\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
is contained in a hyperplane
H
, then
J(\tau)=\langle\ell_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}\rangle
and so

\dim J(\tau)_{d}=\binom{d+1-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})}{2}.

Since
\mathscr{A}
is generic, every ray
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
is contained in the intersection of a unique pair of hyperplanes
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}
. It follows that
J(\gamma)=\langle\ell^{{\bm{r}}(H_{1})+1}_{H_{1}},\ell^{{\bm{r}}(H_{2})+1}_{H_{2}}\rangle
and so

\dim J(\gamma)_{d}=\binom{d+1-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})}{2}+\binom{d+1-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}-\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}.

The term
\binom{d+1-{\bm{r}}(H)}{2}
appears once in the formula for
\dim J(\tau)_{d}
for every
\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
with
\tau\subseteq H
and once in the formula for
\dim J(\gamma)_{d}
for every
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
with
\gamma\subseteq H
. Clearly there is a bijection between the number of two dimensional cones
\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
contained in
H
and the number of rays
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
contained in
H
. So the terms of the form
\binom{d+1-{\bm{r}}(H)}{2}
all cancel on the left-hand side of
Equation

4.2
, leaving just the terms
\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}
for each ray
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
. For each pair of hyperplanes
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}
there are two rays in
H_{1}\cap H_{2}
, leading to the coefficient of
2
on the right hand side of
Equation

4.2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Recall from
Section

2.2

that the
Hilbert polynomial
of an
R
-module

M
is a polynomial
{\sf{HP}}_{M}(d)
such that
{\sf{HP}}_{M}(d)=\dim M_{d}

for all
d\gg 0
, that is, it agrees with the Hilbert function
of
M
for almost all integers
d
.
For an essential arrangement
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
Theorem

4.3
shows that, for
d\gg 0
, the value of
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})

only relies on the first three terms since both
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})

and
{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
have finite length. In particular, if
\mathscr{A}
is generic,
we can determine the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)

directly from the smoothness distribution of
{\bm{r}}
:

{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)=(d+2)(d+1)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}(d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2}))(d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})-1).

If
\mathscr{A}
is not generic, it is still possible to determine the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma)
directly from
{\bm{r}}
and the intersection lattice of
\mathscr{A}
– i.e. the containments between lines and planes. The main technical hurdle is to compute the Hilbert polynomial of the ideal
J(\gamma)
, where
\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
. This is done by Geramita-Schenck in
[
22
]
; we omit the details but see
Example
A.1
for an illustration of the computation.

5.
Dimension computations in high degree via regularity

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is a hyperplane arrangement with at least three linearly independent hyperplanes. If
d
is sufficiently large, the dimension of the spline space
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
coincides with the
Hilbert polynomial

{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)
, as in
Remark

4.4
. According to
Theorem

4.3
, the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is determined by
local data
, namely the Hilbert polynomials of the ideals
\{J(\tau):\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\}
and
\{J(\gamma):\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}\}
.

The values of
d
for which
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is given by
{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)
can be bounded by the maximum degree in which the Koszul homologies
{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})
and
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})
are non-zero. This is determined by the (Castelnuovo-Mumford) regularity of the two modules. Recall that
{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})=R/I
, where
I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}\rangle=\langle\ell_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}:H\in\mathscr{A}\rangle
. In this section, we first provide a bound on the regularity of
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})
using the regularity of
I
, and then we provide several bounds on the regularity of
I
. Putting these together, we derive bounds in
Theorem

5.6
on the values of
d
for which
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is given by
{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)
.

If
M
is a graded
R
-module, we use
\mathrm{reg}(M)
to denote the regularity of
M
. We refer to standard texts (e.g.
[
6
,
18
,
19
]
) for the definition of regularity; for our purposes we need the following properties.

Lemma 5.1
.

[
19
, Section 4B]

Let
R
be a graded ring.

(1)

If
M
is an finite length
R
-module, then
\mathrm{reg}(M)=\max\{t\>:M_{t}\neq 0\}
.

(2)

\mathrm{reg}(A\oplus B)=\max(\mathrm{reg}(A),\mathrm{reg}(B))
.

(3)

If
I\subset R
is a homogeneous ideal, then
\mathrm{reg}(I)=\mathrm{reg}(R/I)+1
.

5.1.
Bounding the regularity of the first Koszul homology

The following result allows us to compute the Koszul homology of the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
in terms of the Koszul homology of a minimal generating set for the ideal generated by the sequence.
It follows from a direct adaptation of the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 1.6.21]
to a graded setting.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R
be a graded ring and
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
a sequence in
R
with
\deg(y_{i})=r_{i}+1
for all
i\in[n]
. Suppose that
\bm{y}^{\prime}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})

where
\langle\bm{y}\rangle=\langle\bm{y^{\prime}}\rangle
and
p\leq n
.
Then there is an isomorphism of graded modules

(5.1)

{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{y})\cong{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{y}^{\prime})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=p+1}^{n}\frac{R}{\langle\bm{y}^{\prime}\rangle}(-r_{i}-1).

Formulas for the regularity of Koszul homologies of ideals were studied in
[
4
,
5
,
10
]
. We use
Proposition

5.2
and
[
4
, Proposition 3.3]
to deduce a bound on the regularity of
{\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}})
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring with standard grading. Let
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
be a sequence in
R
with
\deg(y_{i})=r_{i}+1
for all
i\in[n]
, set
I=\langle{\bm{y}}\rangle
, and assume that
R/I
has finite length. Suppose that
\bm{y}^{\prime}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})

where
\langle\bm{y}\rangle
is minimally generated by
\bm{y^{\prime}}
.

If
n=p
then
\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}))\leq 2\mathrm{reg}(I)
. If
n>p
, assume that
r_{p+1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
. Then,

\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}))\leq\mathrm{reg}(I)+\max\left\{\mathrm{reg}(I),r_{n}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
R/I
has finite length,
[
4
, Proposition 3.3]
gives
\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}^{\prime}))\leq 2\mathrm{reg}(I)
. If
n=p
, we are done. Otherwise, using also
Proposition

5.2
and
Lemma

5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}))=

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}\bigg({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}^{\prime})\oplus\bigoplus_{i=p+1}^{n}(R/I)(-r_{i}-1)\bigg)=\max\{\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}^{\prime})),\mathrm{reg}(R/I)+r_{n}+1)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{reg}(I)+\max\{\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}}^{\prime}))-\mathrm{reg}(I),r_{n}\}\leq\mathrm{reg}(I)+\max\{\mathrm{reg}(I),r_{n}\}.

∎

As the next result shows, we can bound when
the Hilbert function and the Hilbert polynomial of

\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma^{A})
agree in terms of the regularity
of the ideal

I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}\rangle=\langle\ell_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}:H\in\mathscr{A}\rangle
.
The following theorem can be deduced from the regularity bound in
Proposition

5.3
and the dimension formula in
Theorem

4.3
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}=\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\}
is an essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
{\bm{r}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution, and put
{\bm{r}}(H_{i})=r_{i}
. Assume that the ideal
I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
is minimally generated by
\ell_{1}^{r_{1}+1},\dots,\ell_{p}^{r_{p}+1}
. Suppose that either
n=p
and
d>2\mathrm{reg}(I)
or
n>p
,
r_{p+1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
, and
d>\mathrm{reg}(I)+\max\{\mathrm{reg}(I),r_{n}\}
.
Then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}.

If
\mathscr{A}
is generic, then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}.

5.2.
When does the dimension of the spline space become polynomial?

As just shown in
Theorem

5.4
,
we can bound the degrees
d
for which
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is given by its Hilbert polynomial
{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)
in
terms of the regularity of the ideal

I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}\rangle=\langle\ell_{H}^{{\bm{r}}(H)+1}:H\in\mathscr{A}\rangle
. We further
refine this result in this section by showing how
either the smoothness function
{\bf r}
or
geometric information related to
I
can give bounds
on
{\rm reg}(I)
.

Throughout the rest of the section (and also in
Section

6
), we use that the dual point of the linear form
\ell_{i}=\alpha_{0}\,x+\alpha_{1}\,y+\alpha_{2}\,z
is
P_{i}=[\alpha_{0}:\alpha_{1}:\alpha_{2}]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is an essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
n
hyperplanes and
{\bm{r}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution. Let
I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
.

(1)

Let
i,j,k
be indices so that
\ell_{i},\ell_{j},\ell_{k}
are linearly independent. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R/I)\leq r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{k}.

(2)

Suppose there are indices
1\leq i\leq j\leq s\leq t\leq n
so that every proper subset of
\{\ell_{i},\ell_{j},\ell_{s},\ell_{t}\}
is linearly independent. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R/I)\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{s}+r_{t}}{2}\right\rfloor.

(3)

Suppose
{\bm{r}}
is constant, so
r_{i}=r
for all
1\leq i\leq n
for some integer
r
. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R/I)\leq r\left(\dfrac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\right),

where
\alpha
is the smallest degree of a non-zero homogeneous polynomial vanishing on the dual set of points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
.

Proof.

For the first two inequalities, we use that
R/I
has finite length (since
\mathscr{A}
is essential), so if
J\subseteq I
is an ideal and
R/J
has finite length, then
\mathrm{reg}(R/I)\leq\mathrm{reg}(R/J)
.

For the first inequality,
J_{1}=\langle\ell_{i}^{r_{i}+1},\ell_{j}^{r_{j}+1},\ell_{k}^{r_{k}+1}\rangle\subseteq I
and
J_{1}
is a complete intersection, so
\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{1})=r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{k}
.

For the second inequality,
J_{2}=\langle\ell_{i}^{r_{i}+1},\ell_{j}^{r_{j}+1},\ell_{s}^{r_{s}+1},\ell_{t}^{r_{t}+1}\rangle\subseteq I
and, by the hypotheses, there is a change of coordinates so that
J_{2}=\langle x^{r_{i}+1},y^{r_{j}+1},z^{r_{s}+1},(x+y+z)^{r_{t}+1}\rangle
. With
J_{1}=\langle x^{r_{i}+1},y^{r_{j}+1},z^{r_{s}+1}\rangle
, a result of Stanley
[
39
]
shows that
R/J_{1}
has the strong Lefschetz property and
x+y+z
is a strong Lefschetz element. Consequently, the computation of
[
28
, Lemma 2.5]
applies, yielding

\mathrm{reg}(R/J_{2})=\left\lfloor(r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{s}+r_{t})/2\right\rfloor.

For the third inequality, let
X=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
and
I_{X}
be the ideal of homogeneous polynomials vanishing on
X
. It follows from
[
15
, Proposition 3.11]
(see also
[
12
, Corollary 4.22 and Example 4.23]
) that
\mathrm{reg}(S/I)\leq\frac{\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})r}{\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})-1}
, where
\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})
is the so-called
Waldschmidt constant
of
I_{X}
. Since Chudnovsky’s conjecture is known for points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(see e.g.
[
24
, Proposition 3.1]
), it follows that
\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})\geq(\alpha(I_{X})+1)/2
, where
\alpha(I_{X})=\alpha
is the smallest degree of a non-zero homogeneous polynomial which vanishes on
X
. Thus

\mathrm{reg}(S/I)\leq\dfrac{\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})r}{\widehat{\alpha}(I_{X})-1}\leq\dfrac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}r.

∎

We now come to the main result of this section, which refines
Theorem

4.3
by bounding how large
d
must be for
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
to agree with
{\sf{HP}}_{\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})}(d)
, using either
the smoothness distribution or the geometry
of the dual points.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose
\mathscr{A}
is an essential hyperplane arrangement in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
of
n
hyperplanes and
{\bm{r}}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution. Assume that the ideal
I=\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
is minimally generated by
\ell_{1}^{r_{1}+1},\dots,\ell_{p}^{r_{p}+1}
. We further assume that
r_{1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{p}
and
r_{p+1}\leq\cdots\leq r_{n}
. Suppose that one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

d>r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+1+\max\{r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+1,r_{n}\}
.

(2)

There are indices
i,j,s,t
so that every subset of size three of
\{\ell_{i},\ell_{j},\ell_{s},\ell_{t}\}
is linearly independent and
d>\lfloor(r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{s}+r_{t})/2\rfloor+1+\max\{\lfloor(r_{i}+r_{j}+r_{s}+r_{t})/2\rfloor+1,r_{n}\}
.

(3)

{\bm{r}}
is constant with
r_{i}=r
for
i=1,\ldots,n
for some non-negative integer
r
and
d>2r\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}+2
.

Then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\dim R_{d}+\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{2}}\dim J(\tau)_{d}-\sum_{\gamma\in\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}}_{1}}\dim J(\gamma)_{d}.

If
\mathscr{A}
is generic, then

\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathscr{A}\\
H_{1}\neq H_{2}\end{subarray}}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(H_{1})-{\bm{r}}(H_{2})}{2}.

Proof.

Apply
Theorem

4.3
,
Proposition

5.3
, and
Proposition

5.5
, keeping in mind that
\mathrm{reg}(I)=\mathrm{reg}(S/I)+1
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

The condition that there are four linear forms
\ell_{i},\ell_{j},\ell_{s},\ell_{t}
so that every subset of size three of
\{\ell_{i},\ell_{j},\ell_{s},\ell_{t}\}
is linearly independent is equivalent to saying that the hyperplane arrangement
\mathscr{A}
is
indecomposable
. That is
\mathscr{A}
is not a product of a one-dimensional and a two-dimensional hyperplane arrangement.

Remark 5.8
.

Suppose
{\bm{r}}
is constant with
r_{i}=r
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then the bound in
Theorem

5.6
part
(2)
is
d>4r+2
. The bound in part
(3)
performs at least as well as the bound in part
(2)
as long as
\alpha\geq 3
. If
\alpha>3
(that is, the dual points do not lie on a cubic) then the bound in part
(3)
is strictly better.

Remark 5.9
.

Let
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},{\bm{r}}}
be the maximum value at which the Hilbert function of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is not equal to the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
. This is sometimes called the
postulation number
. The three parts of
Theorem

5.6
give three bounds on
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},{\bm{r}}}
. These bounds are best when
\mathscr{A}
is a generic arrangement, but even in the generic setting the bounds are not always optimal. For instance, if
\mathscr{A}
is generic and
{\bm{r}}=\bm{0}
assigns the constant
0
to every hyperplane, then
Theorem

8.1
implies that
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},\bm{0}}=1
. However, the bounds
(1),(2),
and
(3)
in
Theorem

5.6
only give
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},\bm{0}}\leq 2
. If
\mathscr{A}
is generic with four hyperplanes and
{\bm{r}}
assigns the constant
r
to every hyperplane, then
(2)
and
(3)
both yield
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},\bm{r}}\leq 4r+2
. However,
Theorem

6.4
implies that
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},{\bm{r}}}=4r+1
. (The discrepancy appears to be caused by the fact that the bound
\mathrm{reg}({\sf{H}}_{1}(I))\leq 2\mathrm{reg}(I)
is off by exactly one.) On the other hand, the bound in (3) tends to be tight if
\mathscr{A}
has many hyperplanes and is very generic. According to
Theorem

7.3
, if
\mathscr{A}
is generic,
{\bm{r}}
assigns the constant
r
to every hyperplane, and
\langle\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
, then
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},{\bm{r}}}=2r+2
. This agrees with the bound in
(3)
if
\alpha>4r+1
. However, the geometry of
\mathscr{A}
can have an impact here. For instance, if
{\bm{r}}=\bm{1}
and the dual points of
\mathscr{A}
all lie on a conic,
Theorem

8.2
implies that
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},\bm{1}}=4
. However,
(3)
only implies that
d^{*}_{\mathscr{A},\bm{1}}\leq 8
.

6.
Complete dimension computation for small generic hyperplane arrangements

In this section, we use
Theorem

4.3
to provide a formula for the dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
(for all
d\geq 0
) by establishing the Hilbert function of the Koszul homology modules
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
when
\mathscr{A}
is generic and has five or less hyperplanes.

In the results in this section, a combinatorial property of Koszul homology modules is also established: for generic hyperplane arrangements with five or less hyperplanes, the dimension of
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}})
only depends on the smoothness distribution. We say that the obtained formula for
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is
combinatorial
because it does not depend on the exact linear forms defining
\mathscr{A}
, but only on the fact that
\mathscr{A}
is generic. This is no longer true for six hyperplanes: we provide in
Example

6.6
two generic hyperplane arrangements of six hyperplanes whose corresponding spline modules have different Hilbert functions (for constant smoothness
r=2
).

The arguments in this section require properties of Koszul homology modules that go beyond the foundations established so far; we record them next. Recall that the grade of an ideal is the length of the longest regular sequence contained in the ideal.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
{\Bbbk}
be a field,
R={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
in
R
with
\deg(y_{i})=r_{i}+1
for all
i\in[n]
. Set
\overline{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\deg(y_{i})=n+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}
. If
\mathrm{grade}(\langle{\bm{y}}\rangle)=m
, then

(1)

{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})_{j}\cong{\sf{H}}_{n-m-i}({\bm{y}})_{\overline{r}-m-j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
.

(2)

Assume
m=3
. For each
d\geq 0
,

\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}})_{d}-\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}({\bm{y}})_{d}=\sum_{i=2}^{n-3}(-1)^{i}\dim{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})_{d}-\!\sum_{A\subseteq[n]}(-1)^{|A|}\binom{d+2-|A|-\!\sum_{i\in A}r_{i}}{2}.

Proof.

Set
I=\langle{\bm{y}}\rangle
.
To prove Statement (1), we use
[
8
, Lemma 5.7]
, that gives

(6.1)

{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})^{\vee\vee}(\overline{r}+a_{S})\cong{\sf{H}}_{n-m-i}({\bm{y}})^{\vee}\quad\text{for all $i$,}

where
a_{S}=-m
is the
a
-invariant of
R
and
(-)^{\vee}=\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{m}(-,\omega_{R})
with
\omega_{R}
the canonical module of
R
. For any finite length
R
-module
U
, the module
U^{\vee}
is the Matlis dual of
U
, where
U^{\vee}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{{\Bbbk}}(U,{\Bbbk})
and
(U^{\vee})_{j}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{{\Bbbk}}(U_{-j},{\Bbbk})
for all
j
; see
[
6
]
for basics of Matlis duality. Further, since
\mathrm{grade}(I)=m
and
I
annihilates Koszul homology, the modules
{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})
are finite length for all
i\geq 0
.
Consequently,
Equation

6.1
gives an isomorphism of graded
R
-modules

{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})(\overline{r}-m)\cong{\sf{H}}_{n-m-i}({\bm{y}})^{\vee}

that translates to an isomorphism of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces

{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})_{j+\overline{r}-m}\cong{\sf{H}}_{n-m-i}({\bm{y}})_{-j}\quad\text{for all $i,j$}.

After a re-indexing, this yields Statement (1).

For Statement (2), we use the fact that in a finite complex of finitely generated graded
R
-modules, the alternating sum of the Hilbert functions of the homologies is equal to the alternating sum of the Hilbert functions of the original modules in the complex. Applying this to the Koszul complex
{\sf{K}}_{\bullet}({\bm{y}})
we obtain

(6.2)

\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{y}})_{d}-\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}({\bm{y}})_{d}=\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{i}\dim{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})_{d}-\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\dim{\sf{K}}_{i}({\bm{y}})_{d}\,.

The grade sensitivity property of Koszul homology, see
[
6
, Theorem 1.6.17(b)]
, gives
{\sf{H}}_{i}({\bm{y}})=0
for
i>n-3
.
The formula follows since
m=3
and
{\sf{K}}_{i}({\bm{y}})\cong\bigoplus\limits_{A\subseteq[n],|A|=i}R(-\sum\limits_{i\in A}(r_{i}+1))
.
∎

6.1.
Powers of linear forms and fat points

The following result elegantly transforms the problem of studying ideals generated by powers of linear forms into the geometric problem of studying “fat points”, i.e., finding the number of degree
d
curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
that pass through a specified set of points with prescribed multiplicities. For a linear form
\ell=\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z\in R
, we define its dual point as

(6.3)

P=[\alpha:\beta:\gamma]\in\mathbb{P}^{2}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

The defining ideal of this point is given by
\mathfrak{p}=\langle\alpha Y-\beta X,\beta Z-\gamma Y,\gamma X-\alpha Z\rangle\subset S
.

Theorem 6.2

(Theorem IIA
[
20
]
)
.

Let
I=\langle\ell_{1}^{r_{1}+1},\dots,\ell_{n}^{r_{n}+1}\rangle\subset R
be an ideal generated by powers of linear forms, and let
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\subset S
be the defining ideal of the point
P_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
dual to
\ell_{i}
.
With the convention that
\mathfrak{p}^{m}=S
when
m\leq 0
, we have

\dim(R/I)_{d}=\dim\Big(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{d-r_{i}}\Big)_{d}\quad\text{for all $d\geq 0$}.

Theorem

6.2
is a consequence of the
apolarity
action; see the excellent survey
[
21
]
.

6.2.
The case of three, four and five hyperplanes

We provide formulas for
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
when
d=3,4,5
. In all of these cases, the fomulas are combinatorial.

Remark 6.3
.

If
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a generic hyperplane arrangement with
n
hyperplanes and

, then
\mathrm{grade}(I)=\min\{n,3\}
. Indeed, up to a change of variables, we may assume that

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{g}\}\subseteq\{\ell_{H}\colon H\in\mathscr{A}\}
, where

In what follows, we set
{\bm{r}}(\emptyset)=0
and

Theorem 6.4

(
Three and four hyperplanes).

Suppose

is a generic hyperplane arrangement with

In particular,
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is a combinatorial invariant.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{grade}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})=3
by
Remark

6.3
, the statement follows directly from
Theorem

4.3
and
Proposition

6.1
(3).
∎

Theorem 6.5

(
Five hyperplanes).

Suppose
\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a generic hyperplane arrangement with
n=5
hyperplanes and
{\bm{r}}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is a smoothness distribution which is constant along hyperplanes. Then for all
d\geq 0

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=

\displaystyle 2\binom{d+2}{2}+2\sum_{A\subseteq\mathscr{A},|A|=2}\binom{d-{\bm{r}}(A)}{2}-\sum_{A\subseteq\mathscr{A}}(-1)^{|A|}\cdot\binom{d+2-|A|-{\bm{r}}(A)}{2}

\displaystyle+\dim(R/\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle)_{{\bm{r}}(\mathscr{A})+|\mathscr{A}|-3-d}.

Furthermore, there is an algorithm to compute

for any

, which only depends on
{\bm{r}}
. In particular,
\dim\mathcal{S}^{{\bm{r}}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{grade}(\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle)=3
by

6.3
. Using

(1) with

4.3
and

(3).
Since we assume that the hyperplane arrangement is generic, no three of the points

are aligned. In particular, the dual point

lie on a nonsingular (irreducible) conic. An algorithm due to Catalisano

∎

6.3.
The case of six hyperplanes

We provide an example in which we show that in the case of six hyperplanes, the formula for
\dim\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is not combinatorial. Namely, there exist two sequences of six hyperplanes and a smoothness distribution

have different Hilbert series.

Example 6.6
.

Let
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
be a solution of the equation

. Consider the six linear forms
\tilde{\ell}_{1}=x,\tilde{\ell}_{2}=y,\tilde{\ell}_{3}=z,\tilde{\ell}_{4}=x+y+z
,
\tilde{\ell}_{5}=x-\phi y+(\phi+1)z
, and
\tilde{\ell}_{6}=x+(\phi+1)y-\phi z
and let
{\bm{r}}
be the constant smoothness distribution

. Computing with

equals

\mathrm{Hilb}_{{\sf{H}}_{1}({\tilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\bm{2}}})}(t)=3t^{4}+15t^{5}+17t^{6}+10t^{7}+t^{8}.

However, for the sequence
\ell_{1}=x,\ell_{2}=y,\ell_{3}=z,\ell_{4}=x+y+z,\ell_{5}=x+2y+2z,\ell_{6}=2x+y+2z
, the Hilbert series of

equals

\mathrm{Hilb}_{{\sf{H}}_{1}({\bm{\ell}^{\bm{2}}})}(t)=3t^{4}+15t^{5}+17t^{6}+9t^{7}.

Both sequences define a generic arrangement, meaning that no three of the hyperplanes intersect on a line. Therefore, by
Theorem

4.3
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{S}^{2}_{7}(\Sigma^{\tilde{\mathscr{A}}})=

\displaystyle 2\binom{9}{2}+2\binom{6}{2}\binom{3}{2}+\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\tilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\bm{2}})=72+90+10=172\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{S}^{2}_{7}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})=

\displaystyle 2\binom{9}{2}+2\binom{6}{2}\binom{3}{2}+\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{2}})=72+90+9=171,

where
\tilde{\mathscr{A}}
and
\mathscr{A}
are the hyperplane arrangements
corresponding to the sequences
\tilde{\bm{\ell}}
and
\bm{\ell}
.

We also observe that a distinguishing feature between the two sequences is that while for
\tilde{\bm{\ell}}^{\bm{2}}
there exists a linear sygygy in the module of second Koszul differentials, this does not happen for the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{\bm{2}}
. This seems to be a tip of the iceberg situation, as we have observed a similar behaviour for higher constant smoothness distributions.

Remark 6.7
.

If
\mathscr{A}
is a generic arrangement with seven or more hyperplanes, the dimension of
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{4}(\Sigma^{\mathscr{A}})
is not combinatorial – see
Theorem

8.2
. The geometry in the case of

8.2
is clear;

has one value when the dual points all lie on a conic and a different value otherwise. On the other hand, we have no explanation for the geometry that contributes to the different dimensions of

arising in

6.6
. This suggests that describing

could be a fundamental challenge when

is a generic arrangement.

7.
The generic case with constant smoothness and many hyperplanes

In this section, we compute the exact dimension of the spline space
\mathcal{S}^{\bm{r}}_{d}(\Sigma)
under two assumptions:

(1)

The smoothness distribution
\bm{r}:\mathscr{A}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
is constant, that is,
{\bm{r}}(H_{i})=r
for
i\in[n]
.

(2)

The ideal
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle
is equal to the ideal
\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
.

In particular, (1) implies that all elements of the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
have the same degree
r+1
. For condition (2) to hold, there must be a subset of
\{\ell_{i}^{r+1}:1\leq i\leq n\}
with cardinality
\binom{r+3}{2}
which is linearly independent. Equivalently, via apolarity, there is no homogeneous polynomial of degree
r+1
which vanishes on the dual points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
. This is true for ‘most’ collections of at least
\binom{r+3}{2}
points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Since
\langle\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
, the
0
’th Koszul homology of the sequence satisfies
\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})_{d}=\binom{d+2}{2}
if
d\leq r
and
\dim{\sf{H}}_{0}(\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}})_{d}=0
if
d>r
. In order to provide an analogous description of the first Koszul homology of
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
, we first describe the Hilbert function of the first Koszul homology of
\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

The first Koszul homology of
\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
has Hilbert function

\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})_{d}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }d\leq r\text{ or }d\geq 2r+3\\
\binom{r+3}{2}\binom{d+1-r}{2}-\binom{d+2}{2}&\text{if }r+1\leq d\leq 2r+1\\
\binom{r+3}{2}\binom{d+1-r}{2}-\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{\binom{r+3}{2}}{2}&\text{if }d=2r+2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
R=\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
. Consider the short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Syz}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})\to R(-r-1)^{\binom{r+3}{2}}\to\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}\to 0.

From this we deduce that

\dim\mathrm{Syz}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})_{d}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }d\leq r\\
\binom{r+3}{2}\binom{d+1-r}{2}-\binom{d+2}{2}&\text{if }d\geq r+1\end{cases}.

By
Section

4
(see
Equation

4.1
),
{\sf{H}}_{1}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})
is the quotient of
\mathrm{Syz}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})
by the Koszul syzygies of the minimal monomial generators of
\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
, and the Koszul syzygies all have degree
2r+2
. Thus the formula follows for
d\leq 2r+1
.

In degree
d=2r+2
, we claim the Koszul syzygies are linearly independent. Since there are
\binom{\binom{r+3}{2}}{2}
of them, the formula follows for
d=2r+2
once we establish this claim. Let
\lambda=\binom{r+3}{2}
and order the degree
r+1
monomials of
\mathbb{R}[x,y,z]
lexicographically as
m_{1}=x^{r+1},\ldots,m_{\lambda}=z^{r+1}
. The Koszul syzygies are the syzygies
K_{ij}=m_{j}b_{i}-m_{i}b_{j},1\leq i\leq j\leq\lambda
in the free module
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\lambda}R(-r-1)b_{i}
, where
b_{i}
is a standard basis symbol corresponding to the monomial
m_{i}
. Suppose to the contrary that there is a non-trivial linear dependency
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\lambda}c_{ij}K_{ij}=0
where
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq\lambda
. Since the linear dependency is non-trivial, there are
s,t
satisfying
1\leq s<t\leq\lambda
so that
c_{st}\neq 0
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda}c_{si}m_{i}b_{s}=0
, implying that there is a linear dependency among the monomials of degree
r+1
. This is a contradiction since the monomials of degree
r+1
are linearly independent. Thus the Koszul syzygies are linearly independent and the formula follows for
d=2r+2
.
The fact that
\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1})_{d}=0
for
d\geq 2r+3
follows from
[
5
, Corollary 3.7]
.
∎

Now we describe the Hilbert function of the first Koszul homology of the sequence
\bm{\ell}^{{\bm{r}}}
.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
n\geq p=\binom{r+3}{2}
and
\bm{\ell}^{\bm{r}}=(\ell_{1}^{r+1},\ldots,\ell_{p}^{r+1},\ell_{p+1}^{r+1},\ldots,\ell_{n}^{r+1})

We have a graded isomorphism

In particular,

\dim{\sf{H}}_{1}(\bm{\ell}^{r})_{d}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }d\leq r\text{ or }d\geq 2r+3\\
n\binom{d+1-r}{2}-\binom{d+2}{2}&\text{if }r+1\leq d\leq 2r+1\\
\binom{r+3}{2}\binom{d+1-r}{2}-\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{\binom{r+3}{2}}{2}&\text{if }d=2r+2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
I=\langle\ell_{1}^{r+1},\ldots,\ell_{p}^{r+1},\ell_{p+1}^{r+1},\ldots,\ell_{n}^{r+1}\rangle=\langle x,y,z\rangle^{r+1}
,
