The Oort Cloud as a Gravitational Detector for Primordial Black Holes
Sohrab Rahvar

Abstract
Planetary systems can act as sensitive gravitational detectors for dark matter. We investigate the gravitational scattering of Oort cloud objects by primordial black holes (PBHs) as a potential component of the Galactic dark matter halo. Calculating the rates at which PBH encounters eject objects from the Oort cloud or inject them into Earth - crossing orbits, we find a linear scaling Γ ∝ m PBH \Gamma\propto m_{\mathrm{PBH}} for m PBH ≳ 10 − 10 ​ M ⊙ m_{\mathrm{PBH}}\gtrsim 10^{-10}M_{\odot} . For m PBH ∼ 10 3 ​ M ⊙ m_{\mathrm{PBH}}\sim 10^{3}M_{\odot} , PBHs constituting all local dark matter would eject ∼ 1.3 × 10 12 \sim 1.3\times 10^{12} objects over the Solar System’s lifetime—comparable to the total Oort cloud population—and inject ∼ 2.6 × 10 10 \sim 2.6\times 10^{10} objects into Earth - crossing orbits. Comparing these rates with observational constraints from long - period comet fluxes and terrestrial impact records, we derive upper limits on the PBH dark matter fraction f PBH f_{\mathrm{PBH}} . Our most stringent constraints exclude f PBH = 1 f_{\mathrm{PBH}}=1 for 10 2 ​ M ⊙ ≲ m PBH ≲ 10 5 ​ M ⊙ 10^{2}M_{\odot}\lesssim m_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 10^{5}M_{\odot} , with f PBH ≲ 0.002 f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 0.002 at m PBH = 10 3 ​ M ⊙ m_{\mathrm{PBH}}=10^{3}M_{\odot} . For the asteroid - mass window ( 10 17 10^{17} – 10 23 10^{23} g), scattering rates are far too low to produce observable effects. These Solar System - based constraints complement existing astrophysical probes and demonstrate that planetary systems can serve as sensitive gravitational detectors for compact dark matter.

II
Scattering Formalism

We model a PBH–Oort object encounter as a hyperbolic two
-
body scattering in the impulsive approximation. An Oort cloud object of mass
m
(typical
10^{16}
–
10^{18}
g) is on a nearly circular orbit at heliocentric distance
r\sim 10^{4}
AU with speed
v_{c}=\sqrt{GM_{\odot}/r}\approx 0.3
km/s. PBHs in the Galactic halo have a dispersion
v_{\infty}\approx 220
km/s
1
and are far faster than Oort objects, so we work in the rest frame of the Oort object.

For impact parameter
b
and relative velocity
\vec{v}_{\infty}\approx\vec{v}_{\mathrm{pbh}}
, the scattering angle in the center
-
of
-
mass frame is

\Theta=2\arctan\!\left(\frac{GM}{bv_{\infty}^{2}}\right),

(1)

with total mass
M=m+m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
. The impulse imparted to the Oort object is

\Delta\vec{v}=\beta\big[(1-\cos\Theta)\,\hat{i}-\sin\Theta\,\hat{j}\big],

(2)

where
\beta=(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M)v_{\infty}
,
\hat{i}\parallel\vec{v}_{\infty}
, and
\hat{j}
is perpendicular in the scattering plane. The magnitude simplifies to

|\Delta\vec{v}|=\frac{2Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{bv_{\infty}\sqrt{1+(GM/bv_{\infty}^{2})^{2}}}.

(3)

Immediately after the encounter the object’s heliocentric velocity is
\vec{v}_{\mathrm{new}}=\vec{v}_{c}+\Delta\vec{v}
while its position
\vec{r}
is unchanged. The new specific orbital energy
\mathcal{E}
and angular momentum
\mathbf{h}
determine the post
-
encounter Keplerian elements. The change in specific energy,

\Delta\mathcal{E}=\vec{v}_{c}\cdot\Delta\vec{v}+\tfrac{1}{2}|\Delta\vec{v}|^{2},

(4)

controls whether the object remains bound (
\mathcal{E}<0
), is ejected (
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
), or is placed on an orbit with small perihelion
q=a(1-e)
.

III
Encounter Rates and Outcomes

Let us introduce the mass ratio
\alpha=m/m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
. For stellar
-
mass PBHs we have
\alpha\ll 1
; then
M\approx m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
,
\beta\approx v_{\infty}
, and for small
-
angle scattering (
x\equiv GM/bv_{\infty}^{2}\ll 1
) we obtain

\Delta\mathcal{E}\approx-\frac{2Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{b}\,\frac{v_{c,j}}{v_{\infty}},

(5)

where
v_{c,j}
is the component of
\vec{v}_{c}
along
\hat{j}
, with
\langle v_{c,j}^{2}\rangle=v_{c}^{2}/3
.

III.1
Ejection

Ejection requires
\Delta\mathcal{E}\geq|\mathcal{E}_{0}|=v_{c}^{2}/2
. From Eq. (5) the maximum impact parameter is

b_{\mathrm{ej}}\sim\frac{4Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{v_{\infty}v_{c}},

(6)

giving a cross
-
section
\sigma_{\mathrm{ej}}=(\pi/2)b_{\mathrm{ej}}^{2}
(the factor
1/2
accounts for equal probability of energy gain/loss). With PBH number density
n_{\mathrm{pbh}}=\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}/m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
, the ejection rate per object is

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,obj}}=n_{\mathrm{pbh}}v_{\infty}\sigma_{\mathrm{ej}}=8\pi G^{2}\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}}{v_{\infty}v_{c}^{2}}\,m_{\mathrm{pbh}}.

(7)

Numerically,
\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}=0.008M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc}^{-3}
,
v_{c}=0.3
km/s,
v_{\infty}=220
km/s, gives
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,obj}}=1.9\times 10^{-13}(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}
. Then, for the entire Oort cloud (
N=10^{12}
objects) the total ejection rate is

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,tot}}\approx 0.19\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.

(8)

Over the Solar System’s age
T_{\odot}=4.5
Gyr,
N_{\mathrm{ej}}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,tot}}T_{\odot}\approx 1.3\times 10^{12}(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/10^{3}M_{\odot})
. Thus PBHs of
10^{3}M_{\odot}
would eject the entire Oort cloud if PBHs made up all DM.

For comparison, stellar encounters (
n_{*}\approx 0.1
pc
-3
,
m_{*}\approx 0.5M_{\odot}
,
v_{*}\approx 30
km/s) produce
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,*}}\approx 6.5
yr
-1
. The PBH ejection rate becomes comparable to the stellar rate for
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}\gtrsim 20M_{\odot}
(Fig.
1
).

III.2
Delivery to the Inner Solar System

Delivery to Earth
-
crossing orbits (
q\lesssim 1
AU) requires a substantial reduction of angular momentum. The typical impulse for
q\sim 1
AU corresponds to
|\Delta v|\sim v_{c}
, leading to an impact parameter

b_{\mathrm{del}}\approx\frac{2Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{v_{\infty}v_{c}}\approx 0.5\,b_{\mathrm{ej}}.

(9)

Not every encounter with
b<b_{\mathrm{del}}
yields a small perihelion; the required geometry (impulse opposite to
\vec{v}_{c}
) occurs with probability
p_{\mathrm{del}}\sim 1/15
(see Appendix). Then the delivery rate per object is

\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,obj}}=n_{\mathrm{pbh}}v_{\infty}\pi b_{\mathrm{del}}^{2}p_{\mathrm{del}}=\frac{4\pi G^{2}p_{\mathrm{del}}}{v_{\infty}v_{c}^{2}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}m_{\mathrm{pbh}}.

(10)

With
p_{\mathrm{del}}=0.06
we obtain
\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,obj}}=5.8\times 10^{-15}(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})
yr
-1
, and for the whole cloud

\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,tot}}\approx 5.8\times 10^{-3}\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1},

(11)

see Fig.
1
.

III.2.1
Velocity distribution anisotropy

The other observable due to PBH interaction with the Oort objects is the anisotropy of the comets that we may observe. Let us take the Sun that moves through the halo with
v_{0}\simeq 220
km/s. On the other hans PBHs follow an isotropic Maxwellian with dispersion
\sigma\simeq v_{0}
with respect to the rest frame of halo. So the relative speed distribution
f(v,\theta)
would be anisotropic and since
\Gamma_{\mathrm{del}}\propto 1/v
, the event rate is enhanced for slow encounters from behind. Averaging over
v
reduces the delivery rate by a factor
\langle 1/v\rangle/(1/v_{0})\approx 0.68
. The same averaging applies to the ejection rate, reducing
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,tot}}
to
0.13\,(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})
yr
-1
and the corrected total delivery rate is

\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,tot}}\approx 3.9\times 10^{-3}\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}.

(12)

The interesting feature is that the rate per unit solid angle exhibits a strong dipole: the anti
-
apex direction receives
\sim 13
times more injections than the apex. This distinctive signature could be tested with future surveys. For example, LSST is expected to discover
\sim 10^{4}
new Long-period Comets (LPCs) over ten years
9
. A
5\sigma
detection of the predicted anisotropy would require a sample of
\sim 300
comets with known perihelion directions, readily achievable by LSST. Stellar encounters, with their much lower velocity dispersion, produce an essentially isotropic flux, providing a clear discriminant between PBH
-
dominated and stellar
-
dominated perturbations.

III.3
Mild Perturbations

Encounters that change the orbital energy by a fraction
f\lesssim 0.1
(
|\Delta\mathcal{E}|\approx fv_{c}^{2}/2
) have impact parameter
b_{\mathrm{mild}}=4Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}/(\sqrt{3}fv_{\infty}v_{c})
. The rate per object is
\Gamma_{\mathrm{mild,obj}}\approx 1.5\times 10^{-11}(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})
yr
-1
and Over
T_{\odot}
only a tiny fraction of the cloud is mildly perturbed unless
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
is extremely large.

III.4
Light PBHs (
\alpha\gg 1
)

Now we come to PBHs with small mass. For PBH masses in the asteroid window,
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}\ll m
, the impulse
\Delta v\approx 2Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}/(bv_{\infty})
is independent of
m
. Critical impact parameters still scale as
b_{X}\propto m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
, so cross
-
sections scale as
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}^{2}
and rates per object as
n_{\mathrm{pbh}}\sigma_{X}\propto\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}m_{\mathrm{pbh}}
. However, for
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}\sim 10^{20}
g (
5\times 10^{-14}M_{\odot}
) the ejection rate per object is
\sim 5\times 10^{-27}
yr
-1
—utterly negligible over the age of the Solar System. Hence asteroid
-
mass PBHs produce no observable signatures.

IV
Constraints on the PBH Dark Matter Fraction

We now place upper limits on
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}=\rho_{\mathrm{PBH}}/\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}
by requiring that PBH
-
induced perturbations do not exceed observed or inferred rates. Here we examine three different observables.

IV.1
Oort Cloud Survival

The stellar ejection rate
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,*}}\approx 6.5
yr
-1
has depleted only
\sim 3\%
of the Oort cloud over
T_{\odot}
. We conservatively require that the PBH ejection rate does not exceed the stellar rate:

f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,tot}}(f_{\mathrm{PBH}}=1)<\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,*}}.

(13)

With
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ej,tot}}(1)=0.13\,(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})
yr
-1
(after velocity averaging), this gives

f_{\mathrm{PBH}}<50\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{-1}.

(14)

For
m_{\mathrm{pbh}}=10^{3}M_{\odot}
, we have
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 0.05
and for
10^{4}M_{\odot}
,
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 0.005
. An alternative, less stringent limit comes from requiring that PBH ejections remove at most half of the initial Oort cloud population, giving
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 850\,(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})^{-1}
.

IV.2
Long-Period Comet Flux

Observations of dynamically new LPCs with

0.4

f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,tot}}(1)<0.1\,\Gamma_{\mathrm{LPC,obs}}.

(15)

Using
\Gamma_{\mathrm{del,tot}}(1)=3.9\times 10^{-3}(m_{\mathrm{pbh}}/M_{\odot})

and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{LPC,obs}}=0.4

f_{\mathrm{PBH}}<10\left(\frac{m_{\mathrm{pbh}}}{M_{\odot}}\right)^{-1},

which gives
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 0.01

IV.3
Terrestrial Impact Record

The Phanerozoic impact rate for

V
Conclusion

The Oort Cloud—a relic of our Solar System’s formation—serves as a powerful gravitational detector for compact dark matter. By investigating gravitational scattering between primordial black holes and Oort cloud objects, we have established a novel, Solar System
-
based method to constrain PBH dark matter across the stellar
-
mass window.

Our analysis reveals that PBH–Oort object encounter rates follow a linear scaling
\Gamma\propto m_{\mathrm{PBH}}
for
m_{\mathrm{PBH}}\gtrsim 10^{-10}M_{\odot}
, leading to potentially observable effects for stellar
-
mass PBHs. Through three independent observational probes—Oort cloud survival, long
-
period comet fluxes, and terrestrial impact records—we derive stringent upper limits on
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}
. Our most robust constraints exclude PBHs as the dominant dark matter component (
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}=1
) for
10^{2}M_{\odot}\lesssim m_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 10^{5}M_{\odot}
, with
f_{\mathrm{PBH}}\lesssim 0.001
at
m_{\mathrm{PBH}}=10^{3}M_{\odot}
from terrestrial impact constraints. These limits are particularly significant in the
10^{2}
–
10^{4}M_{\odot}
range, where they complement and, in some regions, surpass existing microlensing and CMB bounds.

Beyond setting constraints, our work demonstrates that planetary systems can serve as sensitive gravitational laboratories for fundamental physics. The predicted anisotropic signature in comet injection directions—a 13:1 ratio between anti
-
apex and apex directions—offers a distinctive testable prediction that could distinguish PBH perturbations from stellar encounters. LSST is poised to discover thousands of new long
-
period comets, enabling a
5\sigma
detection of the anisotropy with only a few years of operation.

Looking forward, the combination of Solar System gravitational detectors, gravitational wave observations from LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA, and cosmological probes will continue to illuminate the nature of dark matter. The Oort Cloud reminds us that sometimes the most profound discoveries come from looking at familiar places with new eyes.

Appendix A
Geometry of PBH-Induced Injection into the Inner Solar System

In this Appendix we estimate the probability
p_{\mathrm{del}}
that a PBH encounter delivers an Oort object to
q<1
AU. In the small
-
angle limit
\Delta\vec{v}\approx-(2Gm_{\mathrm{pbh}}/(bv_{\infty}))\,\hat{j}
. The circular velocity
\vec{v}_{c}
is perpendicular to
\vec{r}
. Let
\gamma
be the angle between
\hat{i}
(PBH direction) and
\hat{r}
. Then
v_{c,j}=v_{c}\sin\gamma
. The post
-
encounter angular momentum is
\vec{h}=\vec{r}\times(\vec{v}_{c}+\Delta\vec{v})
, leading to
h\approx rv_{c}(1-\cos\gamma)
. For a near
-
parabolic orbit,
q\approx h^{2}/(2GM_{\odot})\approx r(1-\cos\gamma)^{2}
. Setting
q=1
AU,
r=10^{4}
AU gives
|\gamma|\lesssim 0.2
rad. For an isotropic distribution of
\hat{i}
,
\gamma
is uniformly distributed in
\cos\gamma
, so the probability that
|\gamma|<0.2
is
P_{\mathrm{del}}\approx\frac{1}{2}(1-\cos 0.2)\approx 0.01
.
