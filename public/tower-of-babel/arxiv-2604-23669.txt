Strategically Robust Aggregative Games
Andreas Feik, Nicolas Lanzetti, Saverio Bolognani, Florian Dörfler, Dario Paccagnan

Abstract
In many multiagent settings, such as electric vehicle charging and traffic routing, agents must make decisions in the face of uncertain behavior exhibited by others. Often, this uncertainty arises from multiple sources, such as incomplete information, limited computation, or bounded rationality, ultimately impacting the aggregate behavior. To tackle this challenge, we follow recent work on strategically robust game theory and postulate that agents seek protection directly against deviations around the emergent behavior, as opposed to explicitly modeling all sources of uncertainty. Specifically, we propose that each agent protects itself against the worst-case aggregate behavior within an optimal-transport-based ambiguity set centered at the emergent aggregate population behavior. This leads to a novel equilibrium concept, called strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium, that enables one to interpolate between standard Wardrop equilibria (no robustness) and security strategies (maximum robustness). In the setting of convex aggregative games, we establish the existence of a pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium and provide tractable computational tools for computing it. Through an application in electric vehicle charging, we demonstrate that strategically robust Wardrop equilibria lead to better decisions, protecting agents against the uncertain aggregate behavior of the population. Remarkably, we also observe that strategic robustness can lead to lower equilibrium costs for all agents, uncovering a “coordination-via-robustification” effect.



I
Introduction

In many of today’s large-scale systems, agents must make decisions while uncertain about others’ aggregate behavior.
For instance, in a traffic network, agents must route themselves to their corresponding destinations. However, perfect anticipation of the expected aggregate behavior, which impacts travel times and costs, is often impossible.
It is therefore natural for agents to seek protection against this uncertainty.
When this uncertainty is
exogenous

(e.g., an unknown disturbance)
, agents can model (or estimate) it and incorporate it into their decision-making.
For this, various approaches have been proposed,
such as robust games and, more recently, distributionally robust games;

e.g., see
[
1
,
14
,
10
,
3
,
19
,
18
,
17
,
23
]
.

However, this uncertainty is often
endogenous
. Agents are uncertain about the aggregate behavior of the population, which they themselves impact, both directly (because the aggregate behavior includes each agent’s behavior) and indirectly (because each agent’s behavior impacts that of others).

Additionally, the nature of the uncertainty is rarely known; e.g., uncertain behavior may emerge due to bounded rationality, imperfect knowledge, and limited computation. Given the presence of all these sources of uncertainty, it is natural for the agents to seek decisions that protect themselves against deviations around the aggregate behavior itself rather than trying to explicitly model each of them.

In this paper, we follow this approach initiated in
[
9
]
, and propose that each agent takes decisions in the face of the worst-case aggregate behavior within a suitably-defined ambiguity set, which is itself centered around the emergent aggregate behavior that is so implicitly defined.
Motivated by its expressivity and computational properties, we construct ambiguity sets via optimal transport. These ambiguity sets contain all aggregate behaviors (i.e., probability distributions over
possible aggregates
) that are not too dissimilar from the emergent aggregate behavior, as measured through the so-called
Wasserstein distance

[
22
]
.
The radius of this ambiguity set allows us to tune the level of robustness. When the radius collapses to
0
, the ambiguity set only contains the original aggregate behavior, and we recover the classical notion of Wardrop equilibrium. When, instead, the radius approaches infinity, players protect themselves against
any
aggregate behavior, thereby recovering the
notion of security strategy.

We present our results in the setting of
N
-player convex aggregative games, widely used for interactions in large-scale systems with strategic agents, e.g., see
[
15
]
and references therein.
This setting effectively allows us to expand the scope of
[
9
]
, which instead focuses on Nash equilibrium problems, in three ways.
First, strategic robustness as proposed in
[
9
]
requires knowledge about each agent’s action, which is impractical in large-scale systems. Thanks to the aggregate setting, we instead require knowledge about the aggregate behavior.
Second, the computational complexity of
[
9
]
scales with the number of agents. This makes the analysis of games with many agents challenging. The aggregative formulation we propose, instead, allows us to design algorithms whose computational cost is independent of the number of agents.
Third, robustifying against the aggregate can capture effects such as that of a new player entering the game, which cannot be easily modeled in a standard
N
-player game.

Contributions

Our contributions are threefold.

C1.
We introduce the notion of strategically robust Wardrop equilibria and, for convex games, we prove existence of a
pure
strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium (
Sections

II-A
and
II-B
).

Existence of standard (pure) Wardrop equilibria usually follows from tools from variational inequalities
[
6
]
, which however demand differentiability of the costs.
Unfortunately, worst-case costs
generally fail to meet this property.
Thus, we resort to Kakutani’s fixed-point theorem, which solely relies on continuity arguments, together with Berge’s Maximum Theorem and the composition properties of set-valued maps.

C2.

We show that the problem of finding a strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium can be reformulated into the problem of finding
standard
Wardrop equilibria of an appropriately modified convex aggregative game, which is finite-dimensional
(
Sections

II-C
and
II-D
).

The computation of strategically robust Wardrop equilibria appears challenging; e.g.,
the mere evaluation of worst-case costs is an infinite-dimensional optimization problem over the space of probability distributions.

We bypass this complexity as in strategically robust game theory
[
9
, Section 4]
:
strategically robust Wardrop equilibria are standard Wardrop equilibria of an appropriately modified convex aggregative game.
While the cost function of this modified game involves solving an optimization problem, we use duality in distributionally robust optimization to further reformulate it as a standard convex aggregative game with augmented action spaces. This way, we can compute strategically robust Wardrop equilibria using standard equilibrium-seeking algorithms.

C3.
We study the role of strategic robustness in an electric vehicle charging game (
Section

III
).
Our results show not only that strategic robustness protects agents against uncertainty, but also uncover a remarkable
“coordination-via-robustification effect”
whereby strategic robustness yields lower equilibrium costs for
all
agents.
In some settings, we even demonstrate that, when appropriately calibrated, strategic robustness can coordinate agents to the social optimum, driving the price of anarchy to 1.

II
Strategically Robust Wardrop Equilibria

We consider a game in which each player
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
takes action
x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Each agent aims to minimize the cost
J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)
, which depends on their action
x^{i}
and the aggregate
\sigma:\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of all players’ actions, e.g., the “average”
\sigma=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x^{i}
.
Since the cost only depends on an aggregate of all players’ actions and not on the action of each player individually, the game is called
aggregative
. We operate under the standard convex game assumptions:

Assumption 1
(Convex game)

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
:

1.

The action space
\mathcal{X}^{i}
is a non-empty compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.

The function
x^{i}\mapsto J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)
is convex for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.

The aggregate function
\sigma:\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is continuous.

4.

The cost
(x^{i},\sigma)\mapsto J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)
is continuous.

We denote this game by
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
.

II-A

Strategically Robust Wardrop Equilibrium

We consider players that seek protection against uncertainty in the aggregate
\sigma
. We suppose players know that the aggregate
\sigma
takes values in a non-empty set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Assumption 2
(Compactness)

The set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
is compact and satisfies
\sigma(\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N})\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
.

When each player is aware of the others’ action spaces, a natural choice is
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}=\sigma(\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N})
, which is compact being the image of a compact set under a continuous map.

In practice, however, players might not know the others’ action spaces, so we present our results for a general compact set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
ensuring that
\sigma(\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N})\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
.

Ambiguity set

To robustify against deviations in the aggregate behavior
\sigma(x)
, each player constructs an ambiguity set of probability distributions over the aggregate behavior, centered at the nominal one (i.e., the Dirac delta
\delta_{\sigma(x)}
).
We construct this ambiguity set via optimal transport: For
p\geq 1
, the type-
p
Wasserstein distance between
\mu
and
\delta_{\sigma}
is

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu,\delta_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\delta_{\sigma})}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}}\norm{\aggregate_1-\aggregate_2}^{p}\mathrm{d}\gamma(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\Gamma(\mu,\delta_{\sigma})
is the set of joint probability distributions whose first marginal is
\mu
and whose second marginal is
\delta_{\sigma}
. Intuitively, the Wasserstein distance quantifies the minimum cost to morph
\mu
into
\delta_{\sigma}
when transporting a unit of mass from
\sigma_{1}
to
\sigma_{2}
costs
\norm{\aggregate_1-\aggregate_2}^{p}
, where
\norm{\cdot}
is any norm
on
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
.

The ambiguity set is defined as the set of all distributions whose distance from
\delta_{\sigma}
, as measured through
W_{p}
, is at most
\varepsilon
:

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma})\coloneqq\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}):W_{p}(\mu,\delta_{\sigma})\leq\varepsilon\}.

The radius
\varepsilon
allows us to tune the size of the ambiguity set and, therefore, the resulting level of robustness.
When
\varepsilon=0
, the ambiguity set only contains
\delta_{\sigma}
; in this case, players are not robust. Conversely, when
\varepsilon\to+\infty
, the ambiguity set includes all possible distributions on
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
, meaning that players are protected against any possible aggregate behavior.

Equilibrium definition

We now introduce the notion of strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium. In such an equilibrium allocation, each player selects an action minimizing the worst-case cost in the face of all distributions
in this ambiguity set.

Definition 1
(Pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium)

Given an aggregative game
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
and a set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
,
the action profile
x_{W}=(x_{W}^{1},\ldots,x_{W}^{N})\in\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N}
is a
pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium
with robustness level
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
if for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}

x_{W}^{i}\in\argmin_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i}}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma(x_{W})})}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\sigma}\sim\mu}[J^{i}(x^{i},\hat{\sigma})].

(1)

In particular, when
\varepsilon=0
, we call
x_{W}
a Wardrop equilibrium.

A few remarks on
Definition

1
are in order.

Protection against mixed and pure aggregate behaviors

Our ambiguity sets contain
probability distributions
over possible aggregate behaviors, and not only deterministic ones. This way, players are not only protected against different aggregate behaviors but also when the aggregate behavior is stochastic.

Interpolation between Wardrop and security strategies

When
\varepsilon=0
, the ambiguity set
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma(x_{W})})
collapses to
\{\delta_{\sigma(x_{W})}\}
and we recover the standard definition of Wardrop equilibrium.
When instead
\varepsilon\to\infty
, the ambiguity set
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma(x_{W})})
contains all possible aggregate behaviors and we recover security strategies. Thus, the radius of the ambiguity set interpolates between these two concepts.

Wardrop vs Nash

The key distinction between our strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium and strategically robust equilibrium in
[
9
]
lies in the assumption that the effect of player
i
’s action
x^{i}
on the aggregate
\sigma(x)
is negligible (e.g., because of a large population of agents), as in the standard setting of Wardrop equilibria. That is, when an agent deviates from
x^{i}
to
\tilde{x}^{i}
, the aggregate action
\sigma(x)
does
not
change and continues to define the center of our ambiguity sets.

On the equilibrium condition (
1
)

The strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium condition (
1
) is a fixed point of distributionally robust optimization problems with decision-dependent ambiguity sets, which raises concerns regarding the existence of fixed points and computational tools to compute them. We will resolve them in the remainder of the paper.

Uniform robustness levels

We use the same robustness level
\varepsilon
and set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
for all players. However, all our results readily extend to the case where these are player-specific.

II-B

Existence of
Pure Strategically Robust Wardrop Equilibria

In this section, we establish existence of
pure
strategically robust Wardrop equilibria under the
very same
assumptions required for existence of a standard pure Wardrop equilibrium.

Theorem 1

(Existence)
.

Consider a convex aggregative game
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
, a robustness level
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and a compact set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
.
Then, a pure strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium exists.

Note that, due to the non-smoothness of the function
x^{i}\mapsto\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma})}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\sigma}\sim\mu}[J^{i}(x^{i},\hat{\sigma})]
for fixed
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(because of the supremum), standard tools from (smooth) variational inequalities
[
6
]
cannot be applied to establish existence of strategically robust Wardrop equilibria.
In contrast, our proof, deferred to the appendix, follows from recent results in strategically robust game theory, in combination with tools from set-valued analysis and Kakutani’s fixed point theorem.

II-C

Equivalent Game Formulation

Unfortunately, the existence of pure strategically robust equilibria does not suffice to yield a finite-dimensional problem: The equilibrium condition (
1
) still requires the solution of a maximization problem in the space of probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
, which is infinite-dimensional.
To bypass this complexity, we follow
[
9
, Section 4]
and use duality for distributionally robust optimization (e.g., see
[
7
, Theorem 1]
) to obtain a finite-dimensional counterpart of (
1
).
In particular, we reformulate the problem of finding strategically robust Wardrop equilibria as the problem of finding standard Wardrop equilibria of an appropriately augmented game.

Proposition 1

(Equivalent convex aggregative game)
.

Consider a convex aggregative game
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
, a compact set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
, and a robustness level

\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
be the associated convex aggregative game defined by the augmented action spaces

\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}^{i}\times[0,M^{i}],\quad\text{with}\quad M^{i}\coloneqq 2\max_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i},\sigma\in\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}}\absolutevalue{J^i\bigl(x^i,\aggregate\bigr)}/\varepsilon^{p}<+\infty,

the same aggregative function
(i.e.,
\tilde{\sigma}_{\varepsilon}((x^{1},\lambda^{1}),\ldots,(x^{N},\lambda^{N}))=\sigma(x^{1},\ldots,x^{N})
)
,
and the cost functions

\tilde{J}_{\varepsilon}^{i}((x^{i},\lambda^{i}),\sigma)\coloneqq\max_{\hat{\sigma}\in\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}}\Bigl\{J^{i}\bigl(x^{i},\,\hat{\sigma}\bigr)-\lambda^{i}\norm{\aggregate-\hat\aggregate}^{p}\Bigr\}+\lambda^{i}\varepsilon^{p}.

(2)

Then,
(x_{W}^{1},\dots,x_{W}^{N})
is a pure
strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium of
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
with set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
and robustness level
\varepsilon
if and only if there exists

(\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{N})
,
with
\lambda^{i}\in[0,M^{i}]
for all
i
, so that

\bigl((x_{W}^{1},\lambda^{1}),\dots,(x_{W}^{N},\lambda^{N})\bigr)
is a pure Wardrop equilibrium of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition

1
, which we prove in the appendix, suggests that strategically robust Wardrop equilibria of a game
\mathcal{G}
can be computed as
standard
Wardrop equilibria of an appropriately modified convex aggregative game
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
.
Although the prospect of designing computational tools is enticing, the mere evaluation of the costs
\tilde{J}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
involves solving a possibly non-convex optimization problem, which is generally prohibitive.
This complexity prompts us to focus on an instance where we can efficiently handle the maximization problem – namely when the function
\sigma\mapsto J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)
is concave for all
x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i}
and
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}=\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{k}(\sigma)\leq 0\,\forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\}
for lower semi-continuous convex
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
This setting is motivated by games in markets with linear pricing functions
[
11
]
or power minimization games in multi-user communication settings
[
20
]
, where the cost is a concave function of the aggregate.
When
J^{i}
is concave in
\sigma
and
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
is convex, the maximization problem in (
2
) is a convex problem.
We can therefore utilize duality for convex optimization
[
25
, Theorem 2]
to reformulate the maximization problem in
\tilde{J}^{i}_{\varepsilon}
.

Corollary 1

(
J^{i}
concave in
\sigma
,
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
convex)
.

Consider a convex aggregative game
\mathcal{G}(\{\mathcal{X}^{i},J^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},\sigma)
, a robustness level
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and a set
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
.
Assume that (i)
\sigma\mapsto J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)
is concave for all
x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i}
and (ii)
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}=\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:f_{k}(\sigma)\leq 0\,\;\forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\}
for lower semi-continuous convex
f_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
.
Then,
\tilde{J}_{\varepsilon}^{i}
in (
2
) can be cast as the convex program:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v,z_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\tau_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\lambda^{i}\varepsilon^{p}+(-J^{i})^{*,2}(x^{i},v)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\tau_{k}f_{k}^{\ast}\!\left(\frac{z_{k}}{\tau_{k}}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle-\sigma^{\top}\left(v+\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_{k}\right)+\lambda^{i}\psi_{p}\left(\frac{v+\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_{k}}{\lambda^{i}}\right),

where the functions
f_{k}^{*}(v)\coloneqq\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{v^{\top}\sigma-f_{k}(\sigma)\right\}
and
(-J^{i})^{*,2}(x^{i},v)\coloneqq\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{v^{\top}\sigma+J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)\}
are the convex conjugate of
f_{k}
and of
-J^{i}
(w.r.t. the second argument), and

\psi_{p}(w)=\frac{(q-1)^{q-1}}{q^{q}}\norm{w}_{*}^{q}
if
p>1

(
\norm{\cdot}_{*}
is the dual norm of
\norm{\cdot}
and
q
is the conjugate exponent of
p
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
)
and
\psi_{p}(w)=0
if
\norm{w}_{*}\leq 1
and
+\infty
otherwise if
p=1
.

This reformulation as a minimization problem is attractive since it allows us to augment the players’ action space with the variables
\tau_{k},v,z_{k}
and obtain a game whose costs do
not
“include” an optimization problem. As we discuss in
Section

II-D
below, this enables the design of algorithms to compute strategically robust Wardrop equilibria.
Inspired by our application in EV charging (see
Section

III
),
we study aggregative games with affine price functions as an example.

Example 1
(Games with affine prices)

Let

J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{i}p_{k}(\sigma_{k}),

where the price function
p_{k}
is
p_{k}(\sigma_{k})=\alpha_{k}\sigma_{k}+\beta_{k}
.

Let

\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}=\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{k}\in[0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{\text{max}}}]\}
,
p=2
, and
\norm{\cdot}
be the Euclidean norm.
Then, the cost function
\tilde{J}_{\varepsilon}^{i}
reads

\displaystyle\min_{\tau^{\mathrm{l}},\tau^{\mathrm{u}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}}\lambda^{i}\varepsilon^{2}+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}x^{i}_{k}\large(\alpha_{k}\sigma_{k}+\beta_{k}\large)+\sigma_{k}\large(\tau^{\mathrm{l}}_{k}-\tau_{k}^{\mathrm{u}})+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{\text{max}}}\tau^{\mathrm{u}}_{k}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\lambda^{i}}\norm{\tau^\mathrm{l}-\tau^\mathrm{u}+\alpha\odot x^i}^{2},

where
\odot
denotes element-wise multiplication. We defer the derivation to the appendix.

II-D

Solution Methods

Armed with an equivalent game reformulation, we now design an iterative algorithm to compute strategically robust Wardrop equilibria.
We employ an iterative scheme based on
proximal-best responses
, whereby at each iteration players perform a proximal step to minimize their own cost.
Concretely, at each step
t
, each player
i
selects
(x^{i}_{t+1},\lambda^{i}_{t+1})
as

\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i},\\
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lambda^{i}\in[0,M^{i}]}\end{subarray}}\Bigl\{\tilde{J}_{\varepsilon}^{i}\bigl((x^{i},\lambda^{i}),\sigma(x_{t})\bigr)+\frac{1}{2\rho}\norm{(x^i,\lambda^i) - (x^i_t,\lambda^i_t)}^{2}\Bigr\},

where
\rho>0
can be interpreted as the step size of the scheme.
Our convex reformulations allow us to write
\tilde{J}_{\varepsilon}^{i}
as a minimization problem, so a proximal step effectively consists of solving a finite-dimensional optimization problem, which can be done efficiently with off-the-shelf solvers.
If this algorithm converges, then the solution is a strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium.

The study of its convergence properties, and, more broadly, decentralized algorithms for strategically robust Wardrop equilibria, is left for future research.

III
EV charging: Coordination via Robustification

III-A

Problem Formulation

General problem setting

We revisit the canonical electric vehicle (EV) charging game studied in
[
11
,
16
]
.
We consider the charging of a population of
N
plug-in EVs over a horizon of
n
time periods.
Each EV decides their charging profile
x^{i}\coloneqq(x^{i}_{1},\ldots,x_{n}^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
x^{i}_{k}
denotes the amount charged by EV
i
at time
k
.
At each time
k
,
x^{i}_{k}
must be nonnegative and cannot exceed an upper bound
\bar{x}^{i}_{k}
, which captures battery constraints and the inability to charge at given times (in which case
\bar{x}^{i}_{k}=0
).
The players aim at charging their batteries to their desired amounts
\theta^{i}
by the end of the horizon.
Accordingly, the players’ action spaces are

\mathcal{X}^{i}\coloneqq\left\{x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq x_{k}^{i}\leq\bar{x}_{k}^{i},\quad\forall k=1,\ldots,n\\
\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{i}\geq\theta^{i}\end{array}\right\}.

Each EV seeks to minimize the charging cost, which, at each time, results from the product of the charging
x_{k}^{i}
and the price of electricity at time
k
, denoted by
p_{k}(\sigma_{k})
. We model it as

p_{k}(\sigma_{k})=\frac{\sigma_{k}+d_{k}}{\kappa_{k}},

where
d_{k}
represents the non-EV demand, which we take from
[
11
]
and show in
Fig.

2
,
\sigma_{k}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{k}^{i}
is the aggregate charging at time
k
, normalized by the number of EVs, and
\kappa_{k}
is the normalized total production capacity.
The players’ cost functions are then

J^{i}(x^{i},\sigma):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}^{i}p_{k}(\sigma_{k}).

For our strategically robust game, we suppose players do not have full knowledge about the others’ action spaces and assume that the normalized aggregate charging lies in

\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{k}\in[0,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}]\}

for some
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}}>0
.
Provided it is sufficiently large, it can be shown that the value of
\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}
does not affect the strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium.

Concluding Remarks

Our application in electric vehicle charging adds to the games studied in
[
9
]
that feature a “
coordination-via-robustification
” effect.
A full characterization of this effect is a natural area for future research.
To conclude, just as in standard robust optimization, robustness might ease computation, as observed in

,
24
]
in multi-agent reinforcement learning. Accordingly, the study of the computational properties of strategically robust Wardrop equilibria is a promising research direction.

-A

Proof of
Theorem

1

We split the proof into four steps. Throughout the proof, let
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N}
and
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}=\sigma(\mathcal{X})
. In general,
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}
need not coincide with
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
, as we defined the former to be the image of
\mathcal{X}
through
\sigma
(i.e.,
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}
is the set of all possible values of
\sigma(x)
) and the latter to be a general compact set (cf.
2
).

Step 1: Strategically robust best responses are upper hemicontinuous and have non-empty, compact, and convex values

For each player
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we define the strategically robust best response
r_{\mathrm{sr}}^{i}:\bar{\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{X}^{i}}
as

\sigma\mapsto r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}(\sigma)\coloneqq\arg\min_{x^{i}\in\mathcal{X}^{i}}\bar{J}^{i}(x^{i},\sigma),

where the function
\bar{J}^{i}:\mathcal{X}^{i}\times\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(x^{i},\sigma)\mapsto\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{\sigma})}\mathbb{E}^{\hat{\sigma}\sim\mu}[J^{i}(x^{i},\hat{\sigma})],

where
\bar{\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}}
is a non-empty compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, being the image of a non-empty compact set through a continuous map.
Moreover, by the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
, the map
\bar{J}^{i}
is continuous in
(x^{i},\sigma)
and convex in
x^{i}
for a fixed
\sigma
(in particular, the results apply since, by
2
,
\mathcal{X}_{\sigma}
is compact).
Then, Berge’s Maximum Theorem
[
21
, Theorem 9.14]
establishes that the best response map
r_{\mathrm{sr}}^{i}
is upper hemicontinuous, nonempty, and compact valued.
Finally, convexity of the set
r_{\mathrm{sr}}^{i}(\sigma)
for all
\sigma
follows from convexity of the function
\bar{J}^{i}
and the action spaces
\mathcal{X}^{i}
.

Step 2: Upper hemicontinuity of
x\mapsto\bar{\sigma}(x)\coloneqq\{\sigma(x)\}

By
[
2
, Lemma 17.6]
and continuity of
\sigma
, the set-valued map
\bar{\sigma}:\mathcal{X}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{X}^{N}\to 2^{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}}
which maps each
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
to the singleton
\{\sigma(x)\}\subset\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}
is upper hemicontinuous.

Step 3: The strategically robust best response has closed graph and is non-empty and convex valued

We define the strategically robust best response map
\Omega:\mathcal{X}\to 2^{\mathcal{X}}
by

\Omega(x)\coloneqq\{(r^{1},\ldots,r^{N}):r^{i}\in r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}(\sigma(x))\,\forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\}.

We now prove that
\Omega
has closed graph.
The map
\Omega
can be interpreted as the composition of the set-valued maps
r_{\mathrm{sr}}^{i}
and
\bar{\sigma}
, defined in Steps 1 and 2, respectively.
By Step 1, the correspondence
r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}
is upper hemicontinuous with nonempty and closed (or compact) values for each player
i
.
By Step 2,
\bar{\sigma}:\mathcal{X}\to 2^{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\sigma}}
is upper hemicontinuous.
Then, we can leverage
[
2
, Theorem 17.23]
to conclude each composition
r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}\circ\bar{\sigma}
is upper hemicontinuous.
Since
\mathcal{X}^{i}
is Hausdorff (being a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and the values of
r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}
are closed (by compactness),
[
2
, Theorem 17.11]
implies that the graph of each
r^{i}_{\mathrm{sr}}\circ\bar{\sigma}
is closed.
Consequently, the graph of
\Omega
, being the Cartesian product of these closed graphs, is itself closed.
To prove that
\Omega
has non-empty convex values, it suffices to observe that, for each
i
and
\sigma
, the set of best responses
r_{\mathrm{sr}}^{i}(\sigma)
is non-empty and convex. Since the Cartesian product of non-empty convex sets is itself non-empty and convex, we conclude.

Step 4: Kakutani’s fixed point theorem

Since
\Omega:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow 2^{\mathcal{X}}
has (i) non-empty compact and convex domain, (ii) nonempty convex values, and (iii) a closed graph, we can use Kakutani’s Fixed Point Theorem (e.g., see
[
2
, Corollary 17.55]
) to ensure the existence of an
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
x^{*}\in\Omega(x^{*})
.
This is a strategically robust Wardrop equilibrium.
