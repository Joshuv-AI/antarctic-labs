Remimazolam vs. propofol for prevention of postoperative nausea and vomiting in laparoscopic gynecological surgery: a randomized non-inferiority trial
Authors: Yaodong Zhang, Qiqing Song, Kangjia Xie, Zhaojie Ou, Liqiu Mo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) is a prevalent complication, occurring in approximately 30% of surgical patients and reaching 80% in high-risk individuals. PONV is associated with prolonged recovery, increased healthcare costs, and reduced patient satisfaction. Although propofol can reduce the risk of PONV, it can cause adverse effects such as injection pain and hemodynamic instability. Remimazolam, a novel ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine, has a favorable safety profile, with more stable hemodynamics. While recent studies suggest that remimazolam may reduce PONV, the evidence comparing its antiemetic efficacy directly with propofol remains inconclusive, largely due to limited sample size. Therefore, this non-inferiority trail was conducted to determine whether remimazolam is non-inferior to propofol for the prevention of PONV.MethodsThis was a single-center, prospective, randomized, double-blind, non-inferiority trial. A total of 334 women aged 18–65 years, with American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–III, undergoing elective laparoscopic gynecological surgery were enrolled. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either remimazolam or propofol for anesthesia induction and maintenance. The primary outcome was the incidence of PONV within 24 h. Secondary outcomes included the incidence of rescue antiemetic therapy, the types of PONV, the incidence of injection pain, the incidence of hypotension, the incidence of bradycardia, postoperative pain, and patient satisfaction.ResultsIn the per-protocol analysis (n = 326), the incidence of PONV was 58.3% (95/163) and 57.1% (93/163) in the remimazolam and propofol groups, respectively. The absolute difference was 1.2% (95% CI–9.5% to 12.0%), and the upper confidence limit exceeded the predefined non-inferiority margin of 5%. Remimazolam significantly reduced injection pain (9.6% vs. 54.5%; P < 0.001), hypotension within 10 min after induction (16.8% vs. 28.1%; P = 0.013), and intraoperative bradycardia (25.6% vs. 52.5%; P < 0.001). No significant differences were observed in the use of rescue antiemetics, PONV types, postoperative pain scores, or patient satisfaction.ConclusionAlthough non-inferiority of remimazolam to propofol for PONV prevention was not demonstrated in this high-risk population, remimazolam provided a superior safety profile, with significantly reduced injection pain and improved hemodynamic stability. These advantages position remimazolam as a valuable alternative for general anesthesia in settings where such adverse effects are of particular concern.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300076452,09/10/2023).Keywords: Remimazolam, Propofol, Postoperative nausea and vomiting

Introduction

Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) is a common complication. Studies have reported that approximately 30% of patients experience PONV after anesthesia, with the incidence increasing to 80% among high-risk individuals (Elvir-Lazo et al. 2020; Apfel et al. 1999). PONV may prolong post-anesthesia care unit stays (Habib et al. 2006), delay hospital discharge, cause electrolyte disturbances, exacerbate pain, increase the risk of wound dehiscence and aspiration pneumonia, raise healthcare costs (Weibel et al. 2020; Apfel et al. 2012), and reduce patient satisfaction (Macario et al. 1999).Therefore, effective prevention of PONV remains important. Established risk factors include female sex, a history of PONV or motion sickness, non-smoking status, age < 50 years, opioid analgesia, and inhalation anesthesia (Apfel et al. 2012). Several strategies have been recommended, including one that uses propofol infusions for maintenance of anesthesia (Gan et al. 2020).

Propofol, the most commonly used intravenous anesthetic agent for induction and maintenance over the past three decades, can reduce the risk of PONV by 19% in high-risk patients compared with inhalational anesthesia (Apfel et al. 2004). However, propofol has drawbacks including injection pain, dose-dependent hypotension, respiratory depression, and reliance on hepatic and renal metabolism (Sahinovic et al. 2018).

Remimazolam, a novel benzodiazepine approved for general anesthesia in 2020, is structurally similar to midazolam and exhibits high affinity for γ-aminobutyric acid type A (GABA) receptors (Masui et al. 2020). Its rapid hydrolysis by tissue esterases enables faster recovery. Remimazolam can also reduce the risks of hypotension and respiratory depression, avoid injection pain, and can be reversed by its antagonist (flumazenil) (Kim et al. 2022). In addition, a randomized controlled trial showed that remimazolam reduced PONV (Hari et al. 2022). Nonetheless, no significant difference in PONV has been observed between remimazolam and propofol (Matsumoto et al. 2023; Choi et al. 2022; Kim et al. 2023), which may be due to small sample sizes and an insufficient ability to detect differences. Thus, whether remimazolam is equivalent or non‑inferior to propofol remains unclear. Given its multiple advantages, if remimazolam also provides acceptable antiemetic efficacy, it would broaden the range of available anesthetic options. Accordingly, we conducted this non-inferiority trial to compare remimazolam with propofol for the prevention of PONV.

Methods

This single-center, prospective, randomized, double-blind, non-inferiority trial was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300076452).

Ethics

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Clinical Research and Animal Trials of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University on February 16, 2023. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before surgery. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patients

Patients were eligible if they met all of the following criteria: scheduled for laparoscopic gynecological surgery under general anesthesia with an expected duration of more than 30 min, aged 18–65 years, and ASA physical status of I–III. Patients were excluded if they had any of the following: a history of allergy to remimazolam or propofol, schizophrenia or depression, emergency surgery, massive hemorrhage, shock lasting more than 30 min, or refusal to participate in this trial.

Randomization and blinding

This study used a 1:1 random allocation to assign participants to either the remimazolam or propofol group. Before recruitment, an independent anesthetist generated a computer-based randomization code using R software. The primary outcome was assessed using a double-blind approach, in which both subjects and data collectors were blinded. The data collector performed telephone follow-up. Secondary outcome assessment used a single-blind approach, in which only patients were blinded. Because drug dosages needed adjustment, the researcher administering sedative drugs was not blinded and recorded vital signs, adverse events, and procedural outcomes during surgery and recovery. The trial was unblinded if participants experienced severe adverse reactions, such as severe allergic reactions or shock. A stepwise unblinding procedure was used for statistical analysis. In the first step, only the participants’s group assignments were indicated without revealing the specific group identities. After the results were generated, the second step disclosed the specific group allocations.

Intervention

Anesthesia was administered routinely in our institution. Briefly, after entering the operating room, patients were monitored using noninvasive blood pressure (NIBP), pulse oximetry, and electrocardiography. In the remimazolam group, after 3 min of preoxygenation, anesthesia was induced by sequential intravenous administration of remimazolam 0.4 mg kg− 1, sufentanil 0.3 µg kg− 1 and cisatracurium 0.2 mg kg− 1, followed by maintenance with continuous remimazolam infusion (0.4 mg kg− 1 h− 1), sevoflurane 1-1.5%, and remifentanil (0.075–0.2 µg kg− 1 min− 1). In the propofol group, anesthetic induction was achieved through sequential intravenous administration of 2 propofol mg kg− 1, sufentanil 0.3 µg kg− 1 and cisatracurium 0.2 mg kg− 1, followed by maintenance with propofol (2 mg kg− 1 h− 1), sevoflurane 1–1.5%, and remifentanil (0.075–0.2 µg kg− 1 min− 1). In both groups, all patients were intubated 4–5 min after drug administration. flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg and butorphanol 0.01 mg kg− 1 were administered before sugeryto relieve postoperative pain 30 min before the end of the procedure. If patients reported severe pain upon arrival in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU), sufentanil 0.05–0.1 µg kg− 1 was administered as rescue analgesia.

To avoid interfering with the assessment of PONV, no prophylactic antiemetics were given intraoperatively. If a patient experienced more than two episodes of vomiting or retching, persistent nausea persisted 30 min, or unbearable nausea requiring postoperative treatment, an intravenous injection of metoclopramide hydrochloride 10 mg was administered; if symptoms still persist after 30 min, dexamethasone 5 mg is given (Gan et al. 2020). Patients received postoperative intravenous analgesic pumps for 2–3 days, using hydromorphone and flurbiprofen axetil. Based on each patient’s body weight and the extent of surgical trauma, the hydromorphone dose was 4–10 mg (approximately 2–4 mg/day), and flurbiprofen axetil dose was 200–300 mg (100 mg/day). These were added to normal saline to prepare a 100–150 mL solution (50 mL/day). The pump parameters settings were as follows: loading dose of 0.5–1 mg hydromorphone plus 50–100 mg flurbiprofen axetil; a bolus dose of 2–4 mL (containing approximately 0.1–0.2 mg hydromorphone and 4–8 mg flurbiprofen axetil); a lockout interval of 15 min; an hourly limit of 10–16 mL; and no background infusion.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome was the incidence of PONV within 24 h, defined as any occurrence of nausea, retching, or vomiting in a single patient as one event; no additional counting was performed if more than one event occurred.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes included the incidence of antiemetic therapy, the types of PONV (assessed as follows: no nausea or vomiting; nausea; retching; vomiting) (Hu et al. 2020), the incidence of injection pain, the incidence of hypotension (defined as a systolic pressure decreased more than 25% of baseline within 10 min after induction), incidence of bradycardia (heart rate <60 beats per minute), postoperative pain (assessed using a 4 point scale: 0, none; 1, mild; 2, moderate; 3, severe), and patient satisfaction (assessed with a numeric rating scale from 0 to 10) (Klare et al. 2015).

Any adverse events occurring during hospitalization were recoreded.

Statistical analysis

Since no direct comparative study between the two regimens under inhaled anesthesia was available, the expected PONV rates were derived from separate published studies: the rate of PONV when remimazolam is used for induction and maintenance in women undergoing laparoscopic surgery is approximately 30% (Hari et al. 2022), and propofol approximately 40% (Bansal et al. 2022). Given that a prior trial reported an absolute difference of 10.6% in PONV incidence between propofol and inhalation anesthesia (Kampmeier et al. 2021), and considering that PONV is an unpleasant symptom without life-threatening consequences, we set a non-inferiority margin at 5% (approximately half of 10.6%). A one-sided type I error of 0.025 and 80% power were used. The sample size was calculated to be 318 cases using PASS 21. Accounting for a 5% dropout rate, 334 patients were enrolled. The primary outcome was analyzed using R software. Non-inferiority was concluded if the upper limit of the 95% confidence interval (CI) was lower than the non-inferiority margin of 0.05. Analyses were performed on a per-protocol (PP) population, defined as all patients who were randomized and treated without major protocol deviations.

Secondary outcomes and baseline characteristics were analyzed using R software. The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Continuous variables with normal distributions are presented as mean (standard deviation), and between-group comparisons were performed using a two-tailed t-test, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Continuous variables not normally distributed are presented as median (interquartile range) and compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test as appropriate and are reported as frequencies (percentages). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05.

Results

Baseline and characteristics

A total of 352 women were screened for eligibility between March 2024 and November 2024 at the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, and 338 met the inclusion criteria. Four patients declined participation, resulting in 334 patients being randomized in a 1:1 ratio to the remimazolam (n = 167) or propofol (n = 167) group. The intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis included all randomized patients, whereas the per-protocol (PP) analysis excluded 8 patients due to protocol deviations (4 received prophylactic antiemetics before surgery and 4 underwent unexpected surgical procedure changes) (Fig. 1). Baseline characteristics were well balanced between groups (Table 1). The mean age was 42.6 (9.5) years in the remimazolam group and 42.2 (9.6) years in the propofol group (P = 0.705). Most patients had ASA physical status I (53.9% vs. 49.7%) or II (44.3% vs. 48.5%), with only 1.8% classified as ASA III (P = 0.456). PONV risk factors assessed by the Apfel score were comparable: 86.8% of patients in the remimazolam group and 85.6% in the propofol group had ≥ 3 risk factors (P = 0.460). Surgical procedures included hysterectomy (37.1% vs. 34.7%), myomectomy (22.2% vs. 22.2%), and oophorocystectomy (13.8% vs. 12.0%) (P = 0.775). The median duration of anesthesia was 135 (110–180) minutes in the remimazolam group and 136 (108–180) minutes in the propofol group (P = 0.962), with similar surgical durations (110 [80–153] vs. 105 [80–151], P = 0.855). Baseline hemodynamics were comparable, with baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) of 115.2 (11.5) mmHg versus 114.1 (13.4) mmHg (P = 0.456) and baseline diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of 73.7 (8.9) mmHg versus 74.0 (8.7) mmHg (P = 0.819).

Primary outcome

The incidence of PONV within 24 h after surgery was evaluated in the PP population. In the PP analysis (n = 326), 58.3% of patients in the remimazolam group and 57.1% in the propofol group experienced PONV, resulting in a difference of 1.2% (95% CI -9.5% to 12.0%). The upper limit of the 95% CI exceeded the predefined non-inferiority margin of 5% (P = 0.245) (Table 2).

Remimazolam demonstrated superior hemodynamic tolerance during induction: 28 (16.8%) patients in the remimazolam group experienced a >25% reduction in SBP within 10 min of induction, compared with 47 (28.1%) in the propofol group (P = 0.013; Table 3). Bradycardia (heart rate < 60 bpm) occurred in 25.6% versus 52.5% of patients (P < 0.001), whereas tachycardia (heart rate > 100 bpm) did not differ significantly (9.0% vs. 8.2%, P = 0.813).

Injection pain was significantly less frequent with remimazolam: 90.4% reported no pain compared with 45.5% in the propofol group (P < 0.001). Severe pain occurred in 1.2% and 13.2% of patients, respectively (Table 3).

Pain scores in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) were comparable: 75.6% of patients in the remimazolam group and 70.7% in the propofol group reported no pain (P = 0.302), with similar distributions of moderate pain. In the ward, 25.0% versus 18.6% of patients reported no pain (P = 0.178), and fewer than 1% in each group required rescue opioids for severe pain (Table 3). Patient satisfaction, assessed using a 0–10 numeric rating scale, was high and identical between groups (P = 0.841). Patients had similar satisfaction levels, and no serious adverse events were reported. No patient experienced delayed emergence, and none required flumazenil.

Discussion

This randomized trial did not demonstrate the non-inferiority of remimazolam compared with propofol for preventing PONV in laparoscopic gynecological surgery. The observed PONV incidence was 58.3% with remimazolam versus 57.1% with propofol (difference 1.2% [-9.5% to 12.0%]), with the upper confidence limit exceeding the predefined 5% non-inferiority margin. The higher-than-expected PONV rate reduced the study’s statistical power, which had been based on an expected PONV incidence of 30% in the remimazolam group (Hari et al. 2022) and 40% in the propofol group (Bansal et al. 2022). The lower PONV incidence observed in those studies may be related to reduced opioid use.

Several studies have shown that there is no significant difference in PONV between remimazolam and propofol (Matsumoto et al. 2023; Choi et al. 2022; Kim et al. 2023). A systematic review and meta-analysis involving 3,450 participants also demonstrated no significant difference in PONV (Barbosa et al. 2024). However, two retrospective analyses have paradoxically associated remimazolam with higher PONV rates than propofol (Suzuki et al. 2023; Kaneko 2024). These conflicting findings may be attributable to heterogeneity in surgical procedures, patient populations, and insufficient statistical power due to small sample sizes. In our larger study, the rates of antiemetic rescue use and the types of PONV were comparable between treatment groups, suggesting a similar effect of remimazolam and propofol on PONV. Meta-analyses have indicated that benzodiazepines reduce the incidence of PONV (Kaneko et al. 2024; Au et al. 2024). The antiemetic effects of benzodiazepines may involve two mechanisms: (1) suppression of dopaminergic activity in the chemoreceptor trigger zone (CRTZ) via adenosine-mediated pathways that inhibit dopamine synthesis, release, and postsynaptic signaling; and (2) GABA receptor-mediated reduction of dopamine neuron firing, leading to decreased 5-HT3 release. These effects may be further enhanced by benzodiazepine-induced inhibition of adenosine reuptake (Habib 2002; Jabalameli et al. 2012). Thus, remimazolam, a highly selective GABA receptor agonist, may reduce PONV through similar pathways. Propofol is a potent 5-HT3 receptor antagonist (Carpenter et al. 1990; Miner et al. 1986; Barann et al. 1993; Aldrete 1995) and may also modulate the dopaminergic system in the CRTZ (Hammas et al. 1998). Propofol also suppresses the limbic system (Hammas et al. 1998), which may alter cortical reflexes and ultimately affect the vomiting center. In short, remimazolam shares overlapping underlying antiemetic mechanisms with propofol.

We found that remimazolam demonstrated superior hemodynamic stability, with fewer patients experiencing a > 25% reduction in systolic blood pressure during induction (16.8% vs. 28.1%; P = 0.013). This finding is consistent with results from a meta-analysis (Zhao et al. 2023). A study reported that severe hypotension occurred in 9% of patients within 10 min after induction (Reich et al. 2005). Intraoperative hypotension may lead to cerebrovascular events (Gregory et al. 2021) or renal injury (Ahuja et al. 2020). Propofol affects blood pressure through multiple pathways: (1) attenuation of sympathetic nervous system activity; (2) modulation of calcium ion flux in vascular smooth muscle, resulting in vasodilation (Sahinovic et al. 2018; Soleimani et al. 2017); and (3) reduction of left ventricular preload, leading to decreased stroke volume (Soleimani et al. 2017; Hammarén et al. 1995). Blood pressure may also be reduced by inhibiting thromboxane A2 synthesis in platelets and increasing nitric oxide synthesis (Tsikas et al. 2015). A meta-analysis examining the association between propofol and survival suggested that propofol may increase the risk of mortality by 10% (Kotani et al. 2023), potentially related to hemodynamic instability (Hammarén et al. 1995). The mechanism by which remimazolam affects blood pressure remains unclear. The relatively stable hemodynamics associated with remimazolam may help reduce adverse outcomes (Ebert et al. 1992).

The incidence of injection pain with remimazolam was significantly lower than with propofol (9.6% vs. 54.5%, P < 0.001). Propofol-induced injection pain is thought to stem from its activation of transient receptor potential (TRP) channels, particularly TRPA1 and TRPV1, on nociceptive neurons (Matta et al. 2008; Cornett et al. 2008). Injection pain may also be associated with the concentration of propofol in the aqueous phase (Tan et al. 1998). High concentrations of unbound propofol can directly stimulate nerve endings along the venous endothelium, leading to pain (Darandale et al. 2017). In addition, propofol may exert direct effects on vascular endothelial cells by activating the kallikrein–kinin system, thereby inducing vasodilation and increased vascular permeability. This enables unbound (free) propofol to interact with subendothelial nerve endings, ultimately producing pain perception—a mechanism that may also underlie delayed pain induced by propofol (Ohmizo et al. 2005). According to surveys of American anesthesiologists, injection pain associated with propofol administration is ranked as the seventh most significant adverse effect in clinical anesthesia practice (Macario et al. 1999). Reducing injection pain can improve patient comfort, which may represent an advantage of remimazolam.

This study has several limitations. First, its single-center design may restrict generalizability due to potential population homogeneity, and thus multicenter trials are needed for validation. Second, the 24-hour postoperative observation period limits assessment of delayed PONV (e.g., 48-hour incidence). Nevertheless, delayed PONV is more strongly associated with postoperative opioids and is less likely to be driven by intraoperative anesthetics (Tramèr et al. 1997). Third, in light of safety considerations in clinical anesthesia, inadequate blinding (only single-blinding) was used during the collection of certain secondary outcomes (bradycardia and hypotension), which may have influenced the objectivity of the findings to some extent. However, these outcomes were objective indicators, which may have reduced potential bias. Fourth, the sample size remains small and may be insufficient to establish noninferiority between remimazolam and propofol. Fifth, because inhalational anesthetics are a risk factor for PONV, the concomitant use of sevoflurane may confound interpretation. The combination of intravenous anesthesia and inhalational anesthesia is a common approach that can mitigate many adverse effects, such as hypotension and delayed emergence. As sevoflurane was administered in both groups, any potential effect of sevoflurane on PONV would apply equally to both groups.

Conclusion

This study did not demonstrate the non-inferiority of remimazolam to propofol in preventing PONV after laparoscopic gynecological surgery. Nonetheless, remimazolam provides more stable hemodynamics and results in less injection pain.
