Hybrid vision transformer and ensemble machine learning framework for automated atherosclerotic plaque classification in intravascular ultrasound imaging
Authors: Hazim Sheet Jasem, Zahra Rezaei, Rabah Nori Farhan, Mehdi Hamidkhani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Atherosclerotic plaque classification in intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) imaging is crucial for cardiovascular disease diagnosis and treatment planning. This paper proposes a novel hybrid framework that combines Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture with traditional machine learning approaches for automated plaque classification. Our three-stage ensemble system leverages the global context understanding capabilities of ViT with the local texture analysis strengths of Gabor filter-based traditional ML methods. The framework consists of: (1) a ViT model with Mixup augmentation for global feature extraction, (2) Gabor filter banks combined with Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF) classifiers for texture-based analysis, and (3) a weighted ensemble approach that optimally combines predictions from both stages. Evaluated on a comprehensive dataset of 3,867 IVUS images across three plaque categories (Mild Stenotic, Stenotic, Normal Vertebral Artery), our hybrid framework achieves superior performance with 89.39% accuracy and 88.45% F1-score, significantly outperforming individual component models: ViT (79.12%), Gabor-SVM (85.52%), and Gabor-RF (77.78%). Comprehensive comparison with published state-of-the-art methods for IVUS plaque classification demonstrates the effectiveness of our approach, with our hybrid framework achieving superior or competitive performance compared to existing deep learning and traditional ML approaches that establishing new benchmarks for automated IVUS plaque classification. The framework’s ability to leverage complementary strengths of transformer-based global learning and traditional texture analysis provides a robust solution for clinical cardiovascular imaging applications.

Introduction

Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) imaging has become an essential diagnostic modality for evaluating cardiovascular diseases, offering high-resolution cross-sectional images of coronary arteries and facilitating intricate visualization of atherosclerotic plaques1. Correctly classify plaque word structure is crucial for calculate away how defective the disease be, plan intervention, and calculate away how probably a patient with coronary artery disease be to have a heart and soul attack2. Nonetheless, manual interpretation of IVUS images necessitates specialized expertise and is prone to inter-observer variability3, thereby prompting the creation of automated classification systems4. Recent improvements in deep learning, especially Vision Transformers (ViTs), have demonstrated remarkable capability in medical image analysis5. ViTs use self-attention mechanisms to pick up on long-range dependencies and global context information. This makes them particularly suitable for complex medical imaging tasks6. At the same time, traditional machine learning methods that use hand-crafted features, like Gabor filters, remain effective for texture analysis tasks, particularly in capturing local patterns and fine-grained details7. Even with these improvements, the current methods for classifying IVUS plaques face several limitations. Consequently, pure deep learning methods may struggle with insufficient training data and may fail to capture fine textural details essential for plaque characterizations8. Conversely, traditional machine learning methods perform well for texture analysis but may not capture global contextual information that could enhance classification accuracy9. This drives the founding of hybrid strategies that can leverage the strengths of both approaches10.

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 17.9 million deaths annually according to the World Health Organization. Atherosclerosis, characterized by plaque accumulation in arterial walls, underlies the majority of coronary artery disease cases. The Global Burden of Disease Study reports that ischemic heart disease caused by atherosclerotic plaques accounts for 16% of total global deaths. In developed countries, approximately 30–40% of adults over age 50 show evidence of coronary atherosclerosis on imaging studies. Accurate plaque characterization is critical for risk stratification, with vulnerable plaques responsible for 60–70% of acute coronary syndromes. These global statistics underscore the critical need for reliable, automated diagnostic tools for atherosclerotic plaque assessment.

This paper introduces a novel three-stage hybrid framework that uniquely combines Vision Transformer architecture with Gabor filter-based traditional machine learning for automated atherosclerotic plaque classification in IVUS images. Our key innovations include: 1. A novel integration of Vision Transformer with Gabor-based texture analysis for IVUS plaque classification, extending prior Gabor + CNN work6. 2. An optimized weighted ensemble strategy with empirically determined fusion weights. 3. A Comprehensive validation on 3,867 real-world IVUS images.

Related work

The field of automated IVUS image analysis has witnessed significant development over the past decade, with approaches ranging from traditional computer vision techniques to state-of-the-art deep learning methods11. Early work focused on handcrafted feature extraction using texture analysis, statistical measures, and morphological operations12. More recent approaches have leveraged deep learning architectures, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to automatically learn discriminative features for plaque classification13. The importance of automated IVUS analysis has been well-established in clinical practice, with studies showing that accurate plaque characterization can significantly improve treatment planning and patient outcomes14.

Traditional machine learning methodologies

Conventional methods for IVUS analysis have predominantly relied on feature engineering and traditional machine learning algorithms. Gabor filters are widely employed for texture analysis in medical imaging applications due to their ability to capture oriented patterns and spatial frequencies15. Gabor filters have been extensively employed in medical imaging, demonstrating their effectiveness in various applications, including tissue characterization and boundary detection16. Support Vector Machines (SVMs) are widely employed for medical image classification due to their effectiveness in high-dimensional spaces and their ability to handle non-linear decision boundaries through kernel functions17. Numerous studies have applied SVMs to IVUS plaque classification, yielding promising results18. Random Forest classifiers have also demonstrated strong performance in the high-dimensional feature spaces characteristic of medical image analysis19.

Deep learning techniques

Deep learning has transformed automated diagnostic systems by enabling sophisticated analysis of medical images20. Convolutional Neural Networks have been effectively utilized in IVUS image analysis, exhibiting enhanced performance relative to conventional techniques21. Moreover, recent research has investigated attention mechanisms, transfer learning, and ensemble methods to enhance classification accuracy22. Numerous studies have evaluated various deep learning architectures for IVUS analysis. For example23, evaluated different CNN architectures and found that ResNet-based models achieved the best performance, with an accuracy of 84.7%24. demonstrated that U-Net architectures could accurately segment plaque regions, which is a critical prerequisite for classification.

Vision transformers in medical imaging

Vision Transformers are a new way of looking at computer vision that is different from CNN-based architectures25. Their success in processing natural images has led to increasing adoption in medical imaging26.ViTs are great at picking up on global dependencies and contextual information, making them well-suited for medical image analysis tasks27.

Hybrid and ensemble methods

Recent epoch research lines have concentrated on hybrid and ensemble method that incorporate various technique, acknowledging the constraint of singular approach28. These strategies seek to leverage the synergistic advantages of multiple methodologies while mitigating their individual limitations29,30. demonstrated that deep learning-based plaque characterization in IVUS can effectively classify attenuated and calcified plaques, achieving 98% maximal accuracy at frame-level classification31. developed an optimal deep transfer learning framework combining DenseNet-169 feature extraction with stacked autoencoders, achieving 97.19% accuracy for atherosclerotic plaque classification.

Methodology

Our proposed hybrid framework consists of three sequential stages designed to leverage the complementary strengths of Vision Transformers and traditional machine learning approaches. The system architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Interpretability and clinical validation of heatmaps

Grad-CAM interpretability analysis shows the algorithm is both reliable and makes sense from a medical perspective. The heatmaps it makes as in Fig. 8, approves the model is finding and concentrating on the actual shape of the artery, specifically pinpointing the boundary between the inside layers of the artery wall and the amount of plaque caused by atherosclerosis. Importantly, the way this is shown on the image proves the network isn’t taking a shortcut to learn; it successfully disregards strong contrasts that aren’t important, like the ultrasound catheter in the middle and dark areas from sound bouncing back. When a vessel is seriously narrowed, the areas the model is most sure about will precisely follow the outline of the plaque pressing in on the opening, and this matches what doctors would expect to see. But in vessels with little or no narrowing, or with only mild disease, the model correctly focuses its “attention” on the slight differences in the healthy artery wall. This detailed, disease-based focus is clear visual proof that the algorithm’s high accuracy in classification comes from learning real indicators of heart disease, and not just oddities within the image data (Tables 3 and 4).

Discussions

Important results and insights

Experimental results reveal significant insights concerning the hybrid Vision Transformer and ensemble machine learning methodology for IVUS plaque classification: Complementary Strengths: The combination of ViT and Gabor-based traditional ML effectively leverages the complementary strengths of both approaches. ViT excels at capturing global context and spatial relationships. Gabor filters, conversely, excel at extracting local texture patterns critical for plaque characterization. This complementarity is evident in the substantial performance enhancement (10.27% compared to ViT-only and 3.87% compared to Gabor-SVM-only) .Optimal Weight Configuration:: The empirically established weight configuration (w_ViT = 0.6, w_Gabor_SVM = 0.4) signifies the comparative significance of global and local features. The higher weight assigned to ViT suggests that global context contributes more significantly to overall classification accuracy, while local texture features play a crucial role in fine-grained discrimination .Per-Class Performance: Analysis of confusion matrices reveals notable class-specific behaviors achieves perfect recall for normal vertebral arteries but struggles with mild stenotic cases, frequently misclassifying them as stenotic. This indicates that global features alone are insufficient for nuanced plaque differentiation. The Gabor-SVM component strengthens the ensemble by ensuring balanced performance across all classes.

Future cross-dataset validation

We plan validation on external IVUS datasets from different medical centers, IVUS device manufacturers (e.g., Boston Scientific, Philips, Volcano), and diverse patient populations.

Clinical implications

The proposed framework has several significant clinical implications: Diagnostic Accuracy: The 89.39% accuracy approaches the threshold required for effective clinical decision support systems. This level of accuracy could substantially assist cardiologists in IVUS interpretation, particularly in challenging cases with ambiguous findings. Consistency and Reproducibility: Automated classification reduces inter-observer variability, a persistent challenge in medical imaging interpretation. The framework provides consistent and reproducible results that can support standardized diagnostic protocols. Increased Efficiency: Automated classification can significantly reduce the time required for IVUS image analysis. This improvement could enhance catheterization laboratory workflow efficiency and enable more thorough patient evaluations.

Limitations

Despite the promising results, several limitations must be acknowledged: Dataset Specificity: We conducted our evaluation on a specific IVUS dataset focusing on vertebral artery plaques. Additional validation is required to assess generalizability across different vessel types and imaging protocols. Computational Complexity: The hybrid approach requires processing through both ViT and Gabor feature extraction pipelines, which may increase computational overhead compared to individual methods. Class Imbalance: Although our dataset is relatively balanced, real-world clinical scenarios may present different class distributions, potentially affecting performance. Interpretability: The multi-component nature of the framework may render it more difficult to interpret than simpler methods, which could be important for clinical acceptance and regulatory approval.

Experimental results demonstrate that the hybrid approach effectively addresses limitations of individual methods. The ViT component captures global spatial relationships and contextual information, while the Gabor filter-based traditional ML components provide robust texture analysis capabilities. The weighted ensemble approach optimally combines these complementary strengths, resulting in superior classification performance. The comprehensive comparison with 54 existing methods validates the effectiveness of our approach across different methodological categories, including traditional ML, CNN-based deep learning, transformer architectures, and hybrid approaches. The statistical significance analysis confirms that the observed improvements are meaningful and reliable. From a clinical perspective, the framework’s high accuracy and balanced performance across plaque categories suggest potential for practical deployment in cardiovascular imaging workflows. Automated classification could enhance IVUS-guided interventions by improving diagnostic consistency, reducing inter-observer variability, and supporting clinical decision-making. Future work will focus on multi-modal integration, explainable AI implementation, real-time processing optimization, and cross-dataset validation to further enhance the framework’s clinical utility and broaden its applicability.

A federated learning approach could enable collaborative model development while preserving patient privacy. This could facilitate the broader adoption of AI-assisted cardiovascular diagnosis. In conclusion, our hybrid Vision Transformer and ensemble machine learning framework represents a significant advancement in automated IVUS plaque classification. It provides a robust, accurate, and clinically relevant approach for cardiovascular image analysis. The integration of modern deep learning methods with established traditional approaches represents a promising direction for future medical imaging applications.
