Exponential Quantum Space Advantage for Approximating Max- k k SAT in the Streaming Setting
Haoyu Wang, Guangxu Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we give a one-pass quantum streaming algorithm for Max- k k SAT that uses polylog ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{polylog}(n) space and achieves a 0.7172 0.7172 -approximation on instances with n n variables. In contrast, prior work by Chou, Golovnev, and Velusamy (FOCS 2020) implies that achieving an approximation ratio better than 2 / 2 ≈ 0.7071 \sqrt{2}/2\approx 0.7071 for Max- k k SAT requires Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(\sqrt{n}) space for any classical streaming algorithm. Therefore, it yields an exponential quantum space advantage for Max- k k SAT in the streaming setting. We further give a one-pass quantum streaming algorithm for Max-2OR that uses polylog ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{polylog}(n) space and achieves a 0.7425 0.7425 -approximation on instances with n n variables. Combining with the known results, it gives a complete classification of quantum space advantages for all Boolean Max-2CSPs.

1.3
Technical overview

Our proof extends the snapshot-to-pseudosnapshot framework of Kallaugher, Parekh, and Voronova
[
14
]
from Max-DiCut to Max-
k
SAT. At a high level, we first reduce Max-
k
SAT to estimating a weighted snapshot, then replace this final-stream object by a one-pass pseudosnapshot, and finally estimate the pseudosnapshot using quantum sketches.

Step 1: From Max-
k
SAT to a weighted snapshot.

The first step is to reduce Max-
k
SAT to estimating a weighted snapshot. The snapshot is designed for a fixed oblivious rounding rule: each variable is placed into a bucket according to its weighted signed bias, and the rounding probability of each literal is then determined only by this bucket and the literal sign.

Snapshot-based estimation was introduced for Max-DiCut by Saxena, Singer, Sudan, and Velusamy
[
20
]
, who asked whether the approach can be extended beyond Max-DiCut. We answer this question for Max-
k
SAT. Interestingly, combining our approach with the streaming algorithm of
[
20
]
also yields a
0.7425
-approximation classical streaming algorithm for Max-2SAT in
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{n})
space, thereby resolving an open problem from
[
7
]
. Since this paper focuses on quantum advantage, we omit the details of this classical variant.

An edge in Max-DiCut has only two ordered endpoints, whereas a SAT clause is characterized by both its length and the signs of its literals. We therefore defined a
weighted snapshot
. For each variable, we compute a weighted signed bias from its occurrences in clauses with length at most three; the weights make endpoints in shorter clauses count more. Each endpoint is then assigned a sign-bucket pair, consisting of its literal sign and the bias bucket of its variable, and the weighted snapshot
\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi)
records the resulting histograms over these pairs.

The goal of this step is to construct a linear score
L_{\leq k}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi))
such that

\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\leq L_{\leq k}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi))\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Compared with
[
14
]
, the main barrier is that in Max-
k
SAT, clauses have different lengths. As the number of endpoints processed simultaneously increases, the operations and analysis associated with quantum algorithms become extremely complex and intractable. In order to bypass this technical barrier, we handle clauses of length at least four separately. Under the certified rounding rule, every such clause is satisfied with probability at least the target ratio
\rho
. Thus these long clauses get the fixed contribution
\rho m_{\geq 4}
, and all nontrivial snapshot work is only for clauses of length at most three. In other words,

L_{\leq k}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi))=L_{\leq 3}(\mathrm{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}.

The main difficulty inside
L_{\leq 3}(\mathrm{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))
is the ternary case. The exact satisfaction probability of a ternary clause depends on three endpoints at once, but directly querying three endpoints would still make the quantum bookkeeping hard to control
1
1

1

Our focus is on demonstrating a quantum advantage rather than optimizing the ration
\rho=0.7172
; better score functions and more involved quantum operations may further improve the approximation ratio.
. We therefore replace the exact ternary score by a pairwise-decomposable surrogate, namely a linear combination of one-endpoint and two-endpoint quantities. The unary and binary scores are kept as their exact independent-rounding satisfaction probabilities.

We certify that this surrogate is still good enough: the resulting short-clause score
L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi))
achieves ratio
\rho=0.717275
. This is done by solving a finite linear program and verifying the resulting rational certificate exactly over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Step 2: The weighted snapshot and pseudosnapshot are close.

The weighted snapshot uses final variable biases, which are only known after the stream ends. As in
[
14
]
, we replace it by a weighted
pseudosnapshot
, whose endpoint sign-bucket pairs are assigned when a clause arrives so that the it can be estimated in one pass.

The idea is to use prefix information at the time of arrival together with exact suffix information: the sketch encodes the prefix before the query, and once a query selects a endpoint, the remaining suffix counts can be computed exactly.

For Max-
k
SAT, the data being tracked are different from Max-DiCut. We must track tagged total degrees and tagged positive counts, instead of only directed degree and out-degree quantities, and the same sign-bucket assignment rule must handle unary, binary, and ternary clauses. Our modification is to duplicate each endpoint from a short clause into multiple copies, called tagged units. If the clause length is
\ell
, the endpoint creates
w_{\ell}
copies; this multiplicity records the clause length. Each copy also carries a positive/negative tag, recording the literal sign. Thus every variable has a tagged total degree and a tagged positive count, which play the role of the degree and out-degree quantities in
[
14
]
. We then use the same high-degree/low-degree split: On high-degree scales, the prefix total degree is rounded to the lower end of its scale, and the positive prefix count is sampled randomly. On low-degree scales, both prefix quantities are kept exactly.

To compare these arrival-time sign-bucket pairs with a fixed bucket assignment, we add smoothing noise to the final biases and prove that the pseudosnapshot is
O(\varepsilon m)
-close to the fixed-label snapshot in
\ell_{1}
distance. The fixed bucket assignment obtained this way may not satisfy the original bucket constraints exactly; they are only
\varepsilon
-admissible. We therefore prove a robust version of the finite certificate, which tolerates these approximate labels and loses only
O(\varepsilon m)
in the snapshot score.

Step 3: Quantum estimation of the pseudosnapshot.

The third step is to estimate the weighted pseudosnapshot in one pass. Kallaugher, Parekh, and Voronova
[
14
]
estimate the Max-DiCut pseudosnapshot by running a hidden-matching-style sketch separately for each pair of head/tail degree ranges. Their sketch maintains four degree-test families of basis states, corresponding to lower and upper degree tests for the head endpoint and lower and upper degree tests for the tail endpoint; sampled out-degree information is encoded in separated counter blocks. Edge updates are implemented by swaps. After each edge measurement, a cleanup measurement handles boundary basis states so that the state keeps the invariant needed for the lower/upper inclusion-exclusion over the queried degree ranges.

We follow the same high-level idea, but use a different quantum implementation. For the current clause, our sketch tests either one endpoint or a pair of endpoints. Each test asks whether certain threshold basis states are present in the current superposition. The updates are elementary shifts along threshold-state chains, and the measurements are only single-threshold or pair-threshold queries. Thus the coordinates of the weighted pseudosnapshot are estimated by the same basic operation: test whether the relevant threshold basis states are present when the clause arrives.

For each tested endpoint, the sketch needs two prefix numbers of the underlying variable: the tagged total prefix degree
d
and a positive prefix count
c
. On high-degree scales,
c
is the sampled positive prefix count; on low-degree scales, it is the exact positive prefix count. To implement the update by shifts, we encode these two numbers into one counter

\Gamma(d,c)=d+Bc,

where the multiplier
B
is chosen randomly for the current degree range. The price of this simple encoding is impersonation. An endpoint whose true prefix degree is outside the queried scale may still land on a queried level of
\Gamma
and be counted by mistake. The random choice of
B
makes these accidental hits rare. We charge impersonation and show that its total contribution is
O(\varepsilon m)
. This avoids the separate cleanup measurement used in
[
14
]
.

Combining the first three steps gives a constant-success one-pass quantum algorithm for Max-
k
SAT. Finally, we amplify over independent public seeds and take a median, obtaining success probability
1-\delta
while keeping polylogarithmic space.

Preliminaries

Definition 1
.

For an instance
\Phi
of a Max-CSP(
\Pi
) problem, we denote the number of constraints in
\Phi
by
m=|\Phi|=\operatorname{poly}(n)
. We denote the set of Boolean variables
of
\Phi
by
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
A constraint in
\Phi
consists of a predicate from the constraint language
\Pi
applied to a tuple of variables from
\mathcal{V}
.

For an assignment
\sigma:\mathcal{V}\to\{0,1\}
of the variables of
\Phi
, we denote the number of constraints of
\Phi
satisfied by
\sigma
as
\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi}(\sigma)
. We denote the maximum number of simultaneously satisfiable constraints in
\Phi
as
\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)
:

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)=\max_{\sigma}\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi}(\sigma).

For
\alpha\in[0,1]
, an algorithm
\mathcal{A}
is an
\alpha
-approximation to the Max-CSP(
\Pi
) problem if on any input
\Phi
,
\mathcal{A}
outputs
Z
, such that with probability
1-\delta
, it holds that

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\geq Z\geq\alpha\cdot\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

For example, when
\alpha=1
, the algorithm solves Max-CSP(
\Pi
) exactly with probability
1-\delta
.

Suppose each variable
v\in\mathcal{V}
has been assigned a bucket index
i(v)\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}
. Given a rounding vector
r=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{L-1})\in[0,1]^{L}
, let
\mathcal{R}_{r}(\Phi)
be the product distribution over assignments defined by

\Pr[X_{v}=1]=r_{i(v)},\qquad\Pr[X_{v}=0]=1-r_{i(v)},

independently for all
v\in\mathcal{V}
. The independent rounding value of
\Phi

with respect to
r
is

\mathcal{E}_{r}(\Phi)=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma\sim\mathcal{R}_{r}(\Phi)}\left[\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi}(\sigma)\right].

As a well-known specific example, the Max-
k
SAT problem is a special case of Max-CSP where a constraint is a clause, and a clause in
\Phi
is a disjunction (OR) of at most
k
literals (where a literal is either a variable
x_{i}
or its negation
\neg x_{i}
).

Quantum streaming for Max-CSP

In this paper, we consider the Max-CSP(
\Pi
) problem processed by a one-pass quantum streaming algorithm. The algorithm has sequential access to an input instance
\Phi
, which it processes left-to-right using a limited quantum workspace.

First, the algorithm receives the number of variables
n
and the stream length
m
(equivalently, any constant-factor upper bound on
m
). It then initializes a quantum work register
W
consisting of
s(n)
qubits to a fixed state
\rho_{0}
, typically

\rho_{0}=\ket{0^{s(n)}}\bra{0^{s(n)}}
.

The algorithm receives the constraints
C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}
in
\Phi
one by one (in a possibly adversarial order). Let
\Sigma
be the set of all possible constraints on
n
variables. For each constraint
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the algorithm possesses a corresponding quantum channel
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
acting on
W
. As the stream is processed, the state of the work register evolves as:

\rho_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{C_{i}}(\rho_{i-1}),\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,m.

After the last update, the algorithm measures the work register
W
. Based on the measurement outcome, it outputs a value
Z
.

Throughout this framework, the space complexity of the algorithm is the number of qubits
s(n)
stored in the work register between two consecutive constraint stream updates, where
s
is typically a small function of
n
. We place no restriction on the running time or computational complexity of the quantum update channels. We also allow the algorithm read-only access to public random bits, and these random bits are not stored in the work register and are not charged to the streaming space.

3
Reducing Max-
k
SAT to weighted snapshot estimation

In this section, we reduce Max-
k
SAT to the problem of estimating a weighted snapshot. The snapshot is designed to support a fixed oblivious rounding rule: each variable is first assigned a type according to its signed bias, and the rounding probability of each literal is then determined only by this type and by the literal sign.

We first simplify clauses. First, we can delete the repeated literals. Clauses that contain both a literal and its negation are always satisfied, so they are removed from the stream and accounted for by an exact offset maintained separately. For the remaining simplified instance
\Phi
, let

\Phi_{\leq 3}:=\{C\in\Phi:|C|\in\{1,2,3\}\},\qquad\Phi_{\geq 4}:=\{C\in\Phi:|C|\geq 4\},\qquad m_{\geq 4}:=|\Phi_{\geq 4}|.

Only the short-clause subinstance
\Phi_{\leq 3}
is used to define these variable types and the snapshot score. Clauses in
\Phi_{\geq 4}
do not enter the snapshot; since they have at least four literals, they are handled separately by a deterministic floor
\rho\cdot m_{\geq 4}
, where
\rho
is a constant determined later.

Before defining the ”snapshot” of instances, we first define the bias of the variables in
\Phi_{\leq 3}
.

Definition 2
.

For
\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
P_{v}^{(\ell)}
and
N_{v}^{(\ell)}
denote the numbers of positive and negative occurrences of
v
in
\ell
-clauses of

\Phi_{\leq 3}
. Set

p_{v}:=4P_{v}^{(1)}+2P_{v}^{(2)}+P_{v}^{(3)},\qquad q_{v}:=4N_{v}^{(1)}+2N_{v}^{(2)}+N_{v}^{(3)},\qquad d_{v}:=p_{v}+q_{v}.

The bias of
v
is defined as

b_{v}:=\begin{cases}(p_{v}-q_{v})/d_{v},&d_{v}>0,\\
0,&d_{v}=0.\end{cases}

Note that
b_{v}\in[-1,1]
. The optimized weights
(4,2,1)
determine how strongly unary, binary, and ternary endpoints contribute to the signed type of a variable.

The next definition turns these signed types into a rounding rule.

Definition 3

(Short-clause rounding probability)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{0},\ldots,I_{L-1}\}
be a partition of
[-1,1]
into a constant number of intervals, written with deterministic tie-breaking as

I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i})\quad(i<L-1),\qquad I_{L-1}=[\ell_{L-1},u_{L-1}].

Let
r=(r_{0},\ldots,r_{L-1})\in[0,1]^{L}
be a vector of rounding probabilities. For a literal sign
s\in\{+,-\}
and a bucket
i\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}
, define

q_{+}(i):=r_{i},\qquad q_{-}(i):=1-r_{i}.

For a label
\alpha=(s,i)
, write
q_{\alpha}:=q_{s}(i)
.

Once
[-1,1]
is partitioned into buckets, a variable
v
with
b_{v}\in I_{i}
is rounded to true with probability
r_{i}
. Thus, a positive literal is satisfied with probability
r_{i}
, while a negative literal is satisfied with probability
1-r_{i}
. The snapshot will only remember the literal sign and the bucket index, not the exact value of
b_{v}
.

After the rounding probabilities are fixed, every endpoint of a short clause has a constant-size label
\alpha=(s,i)
: the literal sign
s
, together with the bucket
i
of its variable. The snapshot below records only the histograms of these labels that will be needed. We only keep one-endpoint and two-endpoint projections, because these are the statistics that the quantum estimator can handle.

Definition 4

(Weighted snapshot)
.

Let
i(v)
be the unique bucket containing
v
’s bias
b_{v}
. Order every clause canonically, say by increasing variable index. Denote the variable on the
j
-th endpoint of clause
C
by
v_{j}(C)
. Write the sign-bucket pair by

\alpha_{j}(C)=(s_{j},i(v_{j}(C)))\in\{+,-\}\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\},

where
s_{j}
is the literal sign of the
j
-th endpoint. The weighted short snapshot
\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
consists of:

•

one-endpoint coordinates

U_{\ell,j,\alpha}(\Phi):=\#\{C\in\Phi:|C|=\ell,\ \alpha_{j}(C)=\alpha\},\qquad 1\leq j\leq\ell\leq 3;

•

two-endpoint coordinates

P_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}(\Phi):=\#\{C\in\Phi:|C|=\ell,\ \alpha_{j}(C)=\alpha,\ \alpha_{t}(C)=\beta\},\qquad 1\leq j<t\leq\ell\leq 3;

•

the ternary clause count
M_{3}(\Phi):=\#\{C\in\Phi:|C|=3\}
.

The true satisfaction probability of a ternary clause,
1-\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}})
, is a three-variable function. If we used it directly, the snapshot would have to store a full three-dimensional histogram of ternary endpoint labels. To avoid using ternary endpoint labels, we define a pairwise-decomposable score
H
that relies only on a one-endpoint function,

c_{0}\in\mathbb{Q},\qquad u_{j}:\{+,-\}\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\to\mathbb{Q}\quad(j=1,2,3)

and a two-endpoint function,

p_{jt}:\bigl(\{+,-\}\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\bigr)^{2}\to\mathbb{Q}\quad(1\leq j<t\leq 3).

Definition 5

(Pairwise-decomposable function)
.

The function
H:\bigl(\{+,-\}\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\}\bigr)^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

H(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}):=c_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}u_{j}(\alpha_{j})+\sum_{1\leq j<t\leq 3}p_{jt}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{t}).

Thus, the total ternary contribution can be written using only
M_{3}
, the one-endpoint marginals
U_{3,j,\alpha}
, and two-endpoint marginals
P_{3,j,t,\alpha,\beta}
.

We can now assign a numerical value to a weighted snapshot.

Definition 6

(Weighted snapshot score)
.

Fix the parameters
\Theta=(\mathcal{I},r,c_{0},\{u_{j}\}_{j=1,2,3},\{p_{jt}\}_{1\leq j<t\leq 3})
. For a short instance
\Phi_{\leq 3}
, its weighted snapshot score is

\displaystyle L^{\Theta}_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\alpha}U_{1,1,\alpha}q_{\alpha}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}P_{2,1,2,\alpha,\beta}\bigl(1-(1-q_{\alpha})(1-q_{\beta})\bigr)

\displaystyle+c_{0}\,M_{3}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{\alpha}u_{j}(\alpha)U_{3,j,\alpha}+\sum_{1\leq j<t\leq 3}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}p_{jt}(\alpha,\beta)P_{3,j,t,\alpha,\beta}.

For a full simplified Max-
k
-SAT instance
\Phi
, define

L^{\Theta}_{\leq k}(\Phi):=L^{\Theta}_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho\,m_{\geq 4}.

The first line scores unary and binary clauses by their exact independent-rounding satisfaction probabilities. The second line scores ternary clauses using the pairwise-decomposable function
H
, expanded in terms of the coordinates stored in
\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
.

Lemma 1

(Finite short-clause certificate)
.

There exists a tuple
\Theta^{*}=(\mathcal{I}^{*},r^{*},c^{*},u^{*},p^{*})
such that every short instance
\Phi_{\leq 3}
satisfies

\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\leq L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\leq\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi_{\leq 3}).

where
\rho=0.717275
and
\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
denotes the expected number of clauses satisfied by the independent randomized assignment induced by
r^{*}
.

Proof.

Step 1: Finding conditions for upper bound:
Fix any parameter tuple
\Theta=(\mathcal{I},r,c_{0},u,p)
.
For unary and binary clauses, the score appearing in
L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))
is already the exact independent-rounding satisfaction probability. Therefore, the only extra condition needed for the upper bound concerns
ternary clauses.

For a ternary clause, if
\Theta
satisfies

H_{\Theta}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\leq 1-\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}})\qquad\text{for every ternary label triple }(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}),

then
L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))
is at most the expected number of satisfied clauses
\mathcal{E}_{r}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
under the independent randomized assignment induced by
\Theta
.

Step 2: Finding conditions for lower bound:

Fix an optimal assignment
x^{*}
for the short instance
\Phi
. We refine each clause into
typed atoms
. A typed atom
c
consists of a clause length

\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
, endpoint signs

(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})\in\{+,-\}^{\ell}
, endpoint buckets

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell})\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^{\ell}
, and reference-assignment bits

(\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{\ell})\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}
. We write

c=(\ell;s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell};i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell};\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{\ell})
. For
\alpha_{j}=(s_{j},i_{j})
, define the local snapshot score of
c
by

h_{\Theta}(c):=\begin{cases}q_{\alpha_{1}},&\ell=1,\\[2.58334pt]
1-(1-q_{\alpha_{1}})(1-q_{\alpha_{2}}),&\ell=2,\\[2.58334pt]
H_{\Theta}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}),&\ell=3.\end{cases}

Its optimal-assignment contribution is

o(c):=\mathbf{1}\!\left[\bigvee_{j=1}^{\ell}\operatorname{Lit}(s_{j},\tau_{j})=1\right],

where
\operatorname{Lit}(+,\tau)=\tau
,
\operatorname{Lit}(-,\tau)=1-\tau
.

For each typed atom
c
, let
N_{c}
be the number of clauses of
\Phi
whose length, endpoint signs, endpoint buckets, and endpoint bits under
x^{*}
are described by
c
. Then the typed atoms give the exact
decomposition

L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))=\sum_{c}N_{c}h_{\Theta}(c),\qquad\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})=\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi}(x^{*})=\sum_{c}N_{c}o(c).

Now fix a bucket
I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i}]
and a bit
\tau\in\{0,1\}
. Let
D_{i,\tau}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:\ i(v)=i,x_{v}^{*}=\tau\end{subarray}}(p_{v}+q_{v})
be the total endpoint mass in bucket
i
among variables whose optimal bit is
\tau
, and let
A_{i,\tau}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v:\ i(v)=i,x_{v}^{*}=\tau\end{subarray}}(p_{v}-q_{v}).
be the corresponding signed endpoint mass. Endpoint masses use the same weights as the bias:
w_{1}=4,w_{2}=2,w_{3}=1
.

Writing
\chi(+)=1
and
\chi(-)=-1
, an endpoint of an
\ell
-clause with sign
s_{j}
, bucket
i_{j}
, and optimal bit
\tau_{j}
contributes
w_{\ell}
to
D_{i_{j},\tau_{j}}
and
w_{\ell}\chi(s_{j})
to
A_{i_{j},\tau_{j}}
. The bucket-feasibility constraints are

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}:=\ell_{i}D_{i,\tau}-A_{i,\tau}\leq 0,\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}:=A_{i,\tau}-u_{i}D_{i,\tau}\leq 0.

These are the global constraints, saying that variables assigned to bucket
I_{i}
must have average bias inside that interval.

The atom-level contributions to these constraints are defined by

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\ell\\
i_{j}=i,\ \tau_{j}=\tau\end{subarray}}w_{\ell}(\ell_{i}-\chi(s_{j})),\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\ell\\
i_{j}=i,\ \tau_{j}=\tau\end{subarray}}w_{\ell}(\chi(s_{j})-u_{i}).

Thus, the global constraints also decompose exactly as

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(\Phi_{\leq 3})=\sum_{c}N_{c}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c),\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(\Phi_{\leq 3})=\sum_{c}N_{c}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c).

Suppose there exist nonnegative multipliers
\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau},\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}\geq 0
such that every typed atom
c
satisfies

h_{\Theta}(c)-\rho\,o(c)+\sum_{i,\tau}\left(\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c)+\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c)\right)\geq 0.

Multiplying this inequality by
N_{c}
and summing over all typed atoms gives

L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))-\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})+\sum_{i,\tau}\left(\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(\Phi_{\leq 3})+\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\right)\geq 0.

Since
\Phi_{\leq 3}
is a valid instance, all bucket constraints are nonpositive, and since all multipliers are nonnegative, the correction term is nonpositive.
Therefore

L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\geq\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3}).

So the lower bound reduces to finding a tuple
\Theta
, a ratio
\rho
, and nonnegative multipliers
\lambda
satisfying the typed-atom inequalities.

Step 3: Solving and verifying a finite LP:

After fixing the certificate form, all unknowns are finite-dimensional:

r,c_{0},\quad u_{j}(\alpha),\quad p_{jt}(\alpha,\beta),\quad\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau},\quad\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau},\quad\rho.

This makes the search space finite and manageable. We first fix a constant-size uniform bucket partition
\mathcal{I}
of the bias domain
[-1,1]
and the rounding vector
r
by a separate finite numerical search. The choices of
I
and
r
are recorded in Appendix
B
.

After this choice is fixed, all quantities
q_{\alpha}
, the unary/binary satisfaction probabilities, and the ternary upper bounds
1-\prod_{j}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}})
are rational constants. The resulting linear program maximizes
\rho
subject to two classes of constraints:

•

for every ternary endpoint-label triple,

H_{\Theta}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\leq 1-\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}});

•

for every typed atom
c
,

h_{\Theta}(c)-\rho\,o(c)+\sum_{i,\tau}\left(\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c)+\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c)\right)\geq 0.

Because the bucket set and the atom types are both finite, this is a finite LP. We use codes to solve the LP and verify the
\Theta^{*}
that satisfies both conditions. The corresponding code is available in the repository:
https://github.com/Guangxu-Yang/maxksat-lp-certificate
.

We first solve the LP numerically, obtaining a floating-point candidate
(c,u,p,\lambda,\rho)
. It is used only to find a good candidate certificate, not to prove the theorem. We then round all floating-point parameters to nearby rational values and run the exact verifier. This script checks every ternary upper constraint and every typed-atom inequality exactly over
\mathbb{Q}
. The exact rational certificate used here has
\rho=0.717275
. The exact rational tuple that passes all checks is denoted by
\Theta^{*}
. Therefore,
\Theta^{*}
simultaneously satisfies the upper bound condition and the lower bound condition.

Applying Step 1 and Step 2 to this verified tuple
\Theta^{*}
gives

\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\leq L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\leq\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi_{\leq 3}).

Here
\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
denotes the expected number of satisfied clauses under the independent randomized assignment induced by
r^{*}
.
∎

Lemma 2

(Reduction to weighted snapshot estimation)
.

Fix the certificate
\Theta^{*}
from Lemma
1
, then every Max-
k
SAT instance
\Phi
with
k\geq 2
satisfies

\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\leq L_{\leq k}^{\Theta^{*}}(\Phi)\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Proof.

Recall that

L_{\leq k}^{\Theta^{*}}(\Phi)=L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho\,m_{\geq 4}.

Upper bound.
We first handle the long clauses. Under the rounding vector
r^{*}
, every literal fails with probability at most
0.72917

2
2

2

Under the certified rounding vector
r^{*}
, recorded in Appendix
B
, every literal is satisfied with probability at least
0.27083
. Therefore, every clause of length
d\geq 4
is satisfied with probability at least

1-\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}})\geq 1-(0.72917)^{d}\geq 1-(0.72917)^{4}>0.717275=\rho.

Thus, the term
\rho\cdot m_{\geq 4}
is a valid lower floor for the long clauses, and is also at most their expected contribution under the same independent
rounding.

Apply the independent randomized assignment determined by
\Theta^{*}
.
Lemma
1
gives

L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\leq\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi_{\leq 3}).

The long-clause estimate above shows that
\rho\cdot m_{\geq 4}
is at most the
expected number of satisfied long clauses. Therefore,

L_{\leq k}^{\Theta^{*}}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{r^{*}}(\Phi)\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Lower bound.

Let
x^{*}
be an optimal assignment for
\Phi
. Let
S^{*}
be the number of
short clauses satisfied by
x^{*}
, and let
G^{*}
be the number of long
clauses satisfied by
x^{*}
. Then

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)=S^{*}+G^{*}.

Since the restriction of

to

is feasible for the short subinstance,
\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\geq S^{*}
. Hence, Lemma

gives

L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\geq\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\geq\rho S^{*}.

Also

Adding the last two inequalities yields

L_{\leq k}^{\Theta^{*}}(\Phi)=L_{\leq 3}^{\Theta^{*}}(\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho\cdot m_{\geq 4}\geq\rho\cdot(S^{*}+G^{*})=\rho\cdot\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

4
Closeness of the Weighted Snapshot and Pseudosnapshot

In section
3
, the Max-
k
-SAT value was reduced to the weighted snapshot score
L_{\leq 3}
together with the deterministic long-clause term
\rho\cdot m_{\geq 4}
. The obstacle is that the true short snapshot
\operatorname{WSnap}
depends on final variable biases, which are not available during the stream.

To obtain a one-pass streaming algorithm, we replace the weighted snapshot by a weighted pseudosnapshot that can be estimated in the streaming setting. Throughout this subsection, we fix the optimized tuple
\Theta^{*}
and the ratio
\rho
from Lemma
1
and Lemma
2
. We suppress the superscript
\Theta^{*}
from
L_{\leq 3}
.

Definition 7

(Tagged unit)
.

For a short clause
C
with
\ell=|C|\leq 3
, its
j
-th endpoint
(C,j)
creates the tagged units

(C,j,h),\qquad h\in[w_{\ell}],

where
w_{1}=4
,
w_{2}=2
, and
w_{3}=1
. The tagged unit
(C,j,h)
is associated to the variable
v_{j}(C)
, and is positive iff the
j
-th literal of
C
is positive. A tagged unit is simply a copy of the
j
-th endpoint with index
h\in[w_{\ell}]
. Unless necessary, we don’t distinguish between the endpoint and its tagged units.

We define the degree of a variable
v_{j}(C)
as the total number of occurrences of its associated tagged units. We then partition the degree range into the following scales.
Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1/100)
, and define geometric scales for

D_{0}:=0,\qquad D_{a}:=\lfloor(1+\varepsilon^{3})^{a}\rfloor\quad(a=1,\ldots,A),

where
A
is the smallest interger that satisfies
D_{A}\geq 4m
. In particular
A=O_{\varepsilon}(\log m)
. When a positive tagged unit arrives in the streaming, our algorithm employs a hash function to probabilistically decide whether it should be counted. This selective counting strategy is designed to save memory space.

Definition 8

(Hash function)
.

For each scale
a
with
2D_{a}\geq\kappa
, let
f_{a}
be a fully independent hash function on positive tagged units with selection probability
\kappa/(2D_{a})
, where
\kappa=\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)
is a sufficiently large constant. Namely, for any positive tagged unit
(C,j,r)
,

f_{a}(C,j,r)=\begin{cases}1\;\;\text{ with probability }\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}.\\
0\;\;\text{ with probability }1-\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}.\end{cases}

When
f_{a}=1
, we say
(C,j,r)
is selected by
f_{a}
. Otherwise we say it’s not selected.

When
2D_{a}<\kappa
, the cost of counting the exact degree is cheap, so no hash function is needed for these scales. Let
g:V\to[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
be independent uniform smoothing noise. We define the weighted pseudobias as follows.

Definition 9

(Weighted pseudobias)
.

For a short clause
C
incident to a variable
v
, let
d_{v}^{\leq C},p_{v}^{\leq C}
and
d_{v}^{>C},p_{v}^{>C}

denote the tagged total and positive counts up to and including
C
, and strictly after
C
, respectively. Empty scales with
D_{a}=D_{a+1}
are ignored. Let
a
be the smallest nonempty scale index satisfying

D_{a}<d_{v}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1}.

If
2D_{a}<\kappa
, set

\widetilde{d}_{v}^{\leq C}:=d_{v}^{\leq C},\qquad\widetilde{p}_{v}^{\leq C}:=p_{v}^{\leq C}.

If
2D_{a}\geq\kappa
, set
\widetilde{d}_{v}^{\leq C}:=D_{a}
, let
s_{v}^{\leq C}
be the number of positive tagged units in the prefix selected by
f_{a}
, and set

\widetilde{p}_{v}^{\leq C}:=\frac{2D_{a}}{\kappa}\,\min\{s_{v}^{\leq C},\kappa\}.

The weighted pseudobias at the arrival of
C
is

\widetilde{b}_{v}^{\,C}:=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\left(\frac{2(\widetilde{p}_{v}^{\leq C}+p_{v}^{>C})}{\widetilde{d}_{v}^{\leq C}+d_{v}^{>C}}-1+g(v)\right).

We now use these arrival-time pseudobiases to assign the same sign-bucket labels that were used in the weighted snapshot, but separately for each endpoint at the moment its clause arrives.

Definition 10

(Weighted pseudosnapshot)
.

For a clause
C
of length
\ell
, write
v_{j}(C)
and
s_{j}(C)
for the variable and literal sign of its
j
-th endpoint. Let
\widetilde{i}_{j}(C)
be the unique bucket index satisfying
\widetilde{b}_{v_{j}(C)}^{\,C}\in I_{\widetilde{i}_{j}(C)}
, using the same half-open bucket convention as Definition
3
, and set

\lambda_{j}(C):=\bigl(s_{j}(C),\widetilde{i}_{j}(C)\bigr)\in\{+,-\}\times\{0,\ldots,L-1\}.

The weighted pseudosnapshot
\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
is a vector with the following coordinates:

U_{\ell,j,\alpha}:=\#\{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}:|C|=\ell,\ \lambda_{j}(C)=\alpha\},\qquad 1\leq j\leq\ell\leq 3,

\displaystyle P_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle:=\#\{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}:|C|=\ell,\ (\lambda_{j}(C),\lambda_{t}(C))=(\alpha,\beta)\},\;\;\;\;\;1\leq j<t\leq\ell\leq 3,

and

M_{3}:=\#\{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}:|C|=3\}.

Thus
\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
has the same coordinate set as
\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
, but endpoint labels are assigned using the arrival-time pseudobias
\widetilde{b}_{v}^{\,C}
rather than the final bias
b_{v}
.

The pseudosnapshot still uses labels that may vary with the arrival time of a clause. For the analysis, it is useful to compare it with a simpler object in which every variable has one fixed label throughout the whole stream. We obtain this fixed label by applying the same smoothing noise to the final bias
b_{v}
.

Definition 11

(Smoothed labels and fixed-label snapshot)
.

For each variable
v
, define
\bar{b}_{v}:=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}(b_{v}+g(v))
. Let
\beta_{v}
be the unique bucket index such that
\bar{b}_{v}\in I_{\beta_{v}}
, using the half-open bucket convention from Definition
3
. The fixed-label short snapshot
\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
is defined using the same coordinates as
\operatorname{WSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
, except that every endpoint of variable
v
is assigned the bucket label
\beta_{v}
.

Next, we show that with high probability over the public randomness, the pseudosnapshot is close to a fixed-label snapshot. The fixed labels need not be the true final buckets, but they are close enough: if a variable is labeled by bucket
I_{i}
, then its true bias lies within an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
I_{i}
.

Lemma 3

(Short-clause pseudosnapshot stability)
.

With probability at least
1-O(\varepsilon)-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
, the label map
\beta
from the smoothed labels construction satisfies

\left\lVert\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})-\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\right\rVert_{1}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\varepsilon m

for an absolute constant
C_{\mathrm{st}}
. Moreover, if
I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i}]
, then
\beta
is
\varepsilon
-admissible in the sense that whenever
\beta_{v}=i
,
b_{v}\in[\ell_{i}-\varepsilon,u_{i}+\varepsilon]
.

Proof.

Fix a variable
v
. An endpoint of a short clause
C
incident to
v
uses scale
a=a(C,v)
if
D_{a}<d_{v}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1}
. Call this endpoint
overflowing
if
2D_{a}\geq\kappa
and
s_{v}^{\leq C}\geq\kappa
.

We first show that, except for a small initial segment and possible overflow endpoints, the pseudobias is close to the final smoothed bias. If
d_{v}<1/\varepsilon
, then all prefix counts are exact once
\kappa>2/\varepsilon
. If
d_{v}\geq 1/\varepsilon
, discard the first
\lfloor\varepsilon d_{v}\rfloor
tagged units of
v
; their total mass is at most
\varepsilon d_{v}
.

After this discard, the prefix degree of
v
visits only
O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-3}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)
nonempty geometric intervals. Fix one sampled interval
(D_{a},D_{a+1}]
, and set
\theta_{a}=\kappa/(2D_{a})
. At the first tagged unit in the interval, let
p
and
s
be the true and sampled positive prefix counts. Then
\mathbb{E}[s]\leq\theta_{a}D_{a+1}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{3})\kappa/2
, so Bernstein’s inequality gives

|s-\theta_{a}p|\leq c_{1}\varepsilon^{3}\kappa

except with probability
e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
. During the rest of the interval, the true degree changes by only

D_{a+1}-D_{a}=O(\varepsilon^{3}D_{a}),

So a Chernoff bound shows that the number of additionally sampled positive tagged units is
O(\varepsilon^{3}\kappa)
, except with probability
e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{3}\kappa)}
. Hence, throughout this interval,

\left|s_{v}^{\leq C}-\theta_{a}p_{v}^{\leq C}\right|\leq c_{2}\varepsilon^{3}\kappa

for every non-overflow endpoint
C
.

Scaling by
2D_{a}/\kappa
and using
D_{a}<d_{v}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1}
gives

\left|\widetilde{p}_{v}^{\leq C}-p_{v}^{\leq C}\right|\leq c_{3}\varepsilon^{3}d_{v}^{\leq C}.

(1)

Also, in sampled intervals,

\left|\widetilde{d}_{v}^{\leq C}-d_{v}^{\leq C}\right|=d_{v}^{\leq C}-D_{a}\leq D_{a+1}-D_{a}\leq c_{4}\varepsilon^{3}d_{v}^{\leq C}.

(2)

In low-degree intervals, both prefix quantities are exact, so the same bounds hold with left-hand side
0
. A union bound over the intervals visited by
v
, with the polynomial factor in
1/\varepsilon
absorbed into the exponent by choosing the constant in
\kappa=\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)
large enough, shows that (
1
) and (
2
) hold for every non-overflow endpoint of
v
after the discarded prefix, except with probability
e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
.

On this concentration event, write

p_{v}=p_{v}^{\leq C}+p_{v}^{>C},\qquad d_{v}=d_{v}^{\leq C}+d_{v}^{>C}.

Since
0\leq p_{v}\leq d_{v}
, the two prefix estimates (
1
) and (
2
) imply

\displaystyle\left|\frac{2(\widetilde{p}_{v}^{\leq C}+p_{v}^{>C})}{\widetilde{d}_{v}^{\leq C}+d_{v}^{>C}}-\frac{2p_{v}}{d_{v}}\right|\leq O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{3}d_{v}^{\leq C}}{d_{v}}\right)=O(\varepsilon^{3}).

The clipping map is
1
-Lipschitz. Therefore

\left|\widetilde{b}_{v}^{\,C}-\bar{b}_{v}\right|=O(\varepsilon^{3}),\qquad\bar{b}_{v}:=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}(b_{v}+g(v)),

for every such endpoint.

This bias estimate implies label agreement unless the smoothed bias lies very close to a bucket boundary. If
b_{v}+g(v)
is farther than
c\varepsilon^{3}
from every bucket boundary, then every endpoint satisfying the concentration event receives the same bucket in
\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
as in
\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
. Since
g(v)
is uniform on
[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
and there are only constantly many bucket boundaries,

\Pr\bigl[\mathrm{dist}(b_{v}+g(v),\partial\mathcal{I})\leq c\varepsilon^{3}\bigr]=O(\varepsilon^{2}).

It remains to show that the exceptional endpoints have small total mass. First consider overflow. If
2D_{a}<\kappa
, overflow is impossible. If
2D_{a}\geq\kappa
, then

s_{v}^{\leq C}\sim\operatorname{Bin}\!\left(p_{v}^{\leq C},\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}\right),\qquad\mathbb{E}[s_{v}^{\leq C}]\leq\frac{\kappa D_{a+1}}{2D_{a}}\leq 0.6\kappa

for
\varepsilon<1/100
. Thus

\Pr[s_{v}^{\leq C}\geq\kappa]\leq e^{-\Omega(\kappa)}.

Let
W_{\mathrm{of}}
be the total tagged mass of overflowing endpoints. Since
\sum_{v}d_{v}=4m_{1}+4m_{2}+3m_{3}\leq 4m
,

\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{of}}]\leq e^{-\Omega(\kappa)}m.

Increasing the constant in
\kappa=\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)
if necessary, we may assume
\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{of}}]\leq\varepsilon^{2}m
. Markov’s inequality gives

\Pr[W_{\mathrm{of}}>\varepsilon m]\leq\varepsilon.

(3)

Call a tagged unit bad if it is in a discarded initial segment, its variable fails the concentration event, its endpoint overflows, or
b_{v}+g(v)
is within
c\varepsilon^{3}
of a bucket boundary. These four classes have small total mass. The discarded initial segments have mass at most

\varepsilon\sum_{v}d_{v}\leq 4\varepsilon m.

The concentration-failure class has an expected mass at most
e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}\sum_{v}d_{v}
and hence has mass
O(\varepsilon m)
with failure probability
e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
, after the same adjustment of the constant in
\kappa
. The boundary class has expected mass
O(\varepsilon^{2})\sum_{v}d_{v}
, and is
O(\varepsilon m)
with probability
1-O(\varepsilon)
. The overflow class is controlled by (
3
). Thus, the total bad tagged mass is
O(\varepsilon m)
with probability

1-O(\varepsilon)-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
.

Now delete, only for the analysis, every short clause containing a bad tagged unit. Each short clause contains at most four tagged units, so the number of deleted clauses is
O(\varepsilon m)
. On every remaining endpoint, the label used by
\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
is exactly the bucket of
\bar{b}_{v}
, hence it is the same label used by
\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
. Since each short clause contributes to only constantly many snapshot coordinates, the deleted clauses contribute only
O(\varepsilon m)
to the
\ell_{1}
distance:

\left\lVert\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})-\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})\right\rVert_{1}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}\varepsilon m

for a constant
C_{\mathrm{st}}
.

Finally, if
\beta_{v}=i
, then
\bar{b}_{v}\in I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i}]
. Since
|g(v)|\leq\varepsilon
, we obtain
b_{v}\in[\ell_{i}-\varepsilon,u_{i}+\varepsilon]
.
Hence
\beta
is
\varepsilon
-admissible.
∎

Lemma
3
reduces the analysis of the pseudosnapshot to the analysis of a fixed-label snapshot whose labels are only approximately feasible. We therefore need a robust version of the finite certificate from Lemma
1
: the certificate should still give a good lower bound when the bucket constraints hold with a small additive slack.

Lemma 4

(Approximate-feasibility short-clause certificate)
.

Let
\Psi=\Phi_{\leq 3}
. Let
\beta
be a
\xi
-admissible label map for its variables and
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
be the independent assignment that sets
x_{v}=1
with probability
r^{*}_{\beta_{v}}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{cert}}
, depending only on the verified finite certificate, such that for every assignment
x
,

\rho\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(x)-C_{\mathrm{cert}}\xi|\Psi|\leq L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Psi))\leq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}[\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(X)].

Proof.

For the upper bound, unary and binary clauses are counted with their exact satisfaction probabilities under
\mathcal{D}_{\beta}
. For ternary clauses,
the verified constraint

H_{\Theta^{*}}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\leq 1-\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1-q_{\alpha_{j}})

shows that the surrogate contribution is at most the true satisfaction probability. Summing over clauses gives

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Psi))\leq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}[\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(X)]\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Psi).

For the lower bound, fix an assignment
x
. Refine every clause into typed atoms using the labels from
\beta
and the endpoint bits of
x
. More
explicitly, a typed atom records the clause length
\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
, the endpoint signs
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}
, the endpoint labels

\beta_{v_{1}},\ldots,\beta_{v_{\ell}}
, and the bits

x_{v_{1}},\ldots,x_{v_{\ell}}
. Let
N_{c}
be the number of clauses of
\Psi
of typed atom
c
. As in the exact certificate, write
h(c)
for the local contribution of
c
to
L_{\leq 3}
, and
o(c)
for whether the clause represented by
c
is satisfied by
x
. Then

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Psi))=\sum_{c}N_{c}h(c),\qquad\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(x)=\sum_{c}N_{c}o(c).

The bucket constraints decompose over the same typed atoms. Let
w_{1}=4,w_{2}=2,w_{3}=1
, and write
\chi(+)=1
,
\chi(-)=-1
. For
I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i}]
, define
D_{i,\tau}
and
A_{i,\tau}
using the label
map
\beta
and the reference assignment
x
: an endpoint of an
\ell
-clause with sign
s_{j}
, label
i_{j}
, and bit
\tau_{j}
contributes
w_{\ell}
to
D_{i_{j},\tau_{j}}
and
w_{\ell}\chi(s_{j})
to
A_{i_{j},\tau_{j}}
. The atom contributions

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\ell\\
i_{j}=i,\ \tau_{j}=\tau\end{subarray}}w_{\ell}(\ell_{i}-\chi(s_{j})),\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\ell\\
i_{j}=i,\ \tau_{j}=\tau\end{subarray}}w_{\ell}(\chi(s_{j})-u_{i})

satisfy

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)=\sum_{c}N_{c}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(c),\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)=\sum_{c}N_{c}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(c).

Multiplying the verified atom inequality by
N_{c}
and summing over all typed atoms gives

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Psi))-\rho\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(x)+\sum_{i,\tau}\left(\lambda^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)+\lambda^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)\right)\geq 0.

Since
\beta
is
\xi
-admissible, every variable assigned to bucket
I_{i}=[\ell_{i},u_{i}]
satisfies

\ell_{i}-\xi\leq b_{v}\leq u_{i}+\xi.

After summing over variables in bucket
i
and bit class
\tau
, this gives

g^{\rm lo}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)\leq\xi D_{i,\tau},\qquad g^{\rm up}_{i,\tau}(\Psi)\leq\xi D_{i,\tau}.

Because all multipliers are fixed nonnegative constants and
\sum_{i,\tau}D_{i,\tau}\leq 4|\Psi|
, the correction term is at most
C_{\mathrm{cert}}\xi|\Psi|
. Therefore

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Psi))\geq\rho\operatorname{Val}_{\Psi}(x)-C_{\mathrm{cert}}\xi|\Psi|.

∎

We now combine the two ingredients. Lemma
3

shows that
\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
is close to

\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3})
for an
\varepsilon
-admissible label map
\beta
. Lemma
4
then applies to this fixed-label snapshot and loses only
O(\varepsilon m)
. Since
L_{\leq 3}
is a
fixed linear functional, the
L_{1}
-closeness of the two snapshots changes the score by only
O(\varepsilon m)
. Adding the deterministic long-clause term
\rho m_{\geq 4}
gives the approximate value guarantee below.

Lemma 5

(From weighted pseudosnapshot to value)
.

There exist constants
C_{0},C_{1}>0
, depending only on the certificate, such that with probability at least
1-O(\varepsilon)-e^{-\Omega(\varepsilon^{6}\kappa)}
,

\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)-C_{1}\varepsilon m\leq L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}-C_{0}\varepsilon m\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Proof.

Work on the good event of Lemma
3
. Since
L_{\leq 3}
is a fixed linear functional with constant coefficients,

\left|L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{lin}}\varepsilon m

for some absolute constant
C_{\mathrm{lin}}
.

For the upper bound, apply Lemma
4
with
\xi=\varepsilon
and
\Psi=\Phi_{\leq 3}
:

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\leq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}[\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi_{\leq 3}}(X)].

Moreover, the same label map
\beta
defines an independent randomized assignment on all variables of
\Phi
. Under this assignment, every long clause has a satisfaction probability at least
\rho
. Hence

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}\leq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_{\beta}}[\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi}(X)]\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Together with the previous display, this gives

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)+C_{\mathrm{lin}}\varepsilon m.

Subtracting a sufficiently large multiple of
\varepsilon m
yields the upper bound.

For the lower bound, let
x^{*}
be an optimal assignment for
\Phi
. Let
S^{*}
and
G^{*}
be the numbers of satisfied short and long clauses, respectively, so that
\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)=S^{*}+G^{*}
. Applying Lemma
4
with
\Psi=\Phi_{\leq 3}
,
\xi=\varepsilon
, and
x=x^{*}|_{\Phi_{\leq 3}}
, we obtain

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\geq\rho S^{*}-C_{\mathrm{cert}}\varepsilon m.

Also
G^{*}\leq m_{\geq 4}
, so
\rho m_{\geq 4}\geq\rho G^{*}
. Therefore

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{\beta}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}\geq\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)-C_{\mathrm{cert}}\varepsilon m.

Using the linear closeness bound again,

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}\geq\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)-C_{1}^{\prime}\varepsilon m

for some constant
C_{1}^{\prime}>0
. After subtracting
C_{0}\varepsilon m
, we obtain

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))+\rho m_{\geq 4}-C_{0}\varepsilon m\geq\rho\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)-C_{1}\varepsilon m.

Finally, every nonconstant clause is satisfied by a uniformly random assignment with probability at least
1/2
, so
\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\geq m/2
. Hence, the additive loss
C_{1}\varepsilon m
is an
O(\varepsilon)\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)
multiplicative loss.
∎

5
Quantum algorithm for weighted pseudosnapshot estimation

In this section, we give a quantum streaming algorithm for the pseudosnapshot.

Lemma 6

(Quantum estimation)
.

For any fixed constant
\varepsilon\in(0,1/100]
, there is a one-pass quantum streaming algorithm for estimating
L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))
using
O_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\log^{5}(n+m)\right)
qubits and
O_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\log^{5}(n+m)\right)
classical bits. The output

satisfies

\left|\widehat{L}_{\leq 3}-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|\leq\varepsilon m

with probability at least
15/16
.

Throughout this section, we use the tagged units from
Definition
7
, the public hash functions

from
Definition
8
, the weighted pseudobias notation from
Definition
9
, and the buckets from
Definition
3
. We take
A
to be the smallest index with

. Hence every

and

\kappa
to be a sufficiently large positive integer.

For a clause
C
and

, let
v_{j}(C)
be the variable of the
j
-th literal. The endpoint

has a sign

, and the pseudobias
\widetilde{b}_{v_{j}(C)}
.
\widetilde{b}_{v_{j}(C)}
can be computed at the arrival of

denote the sign-bucket pair. All clauses considered in this section are short clauses, with

. Each endpoint of an

For later reference, define the true scale of the endpoint

actually belongs

We will use a scale refinement of the coordinates of

. Moreover, we partition clauses by scales
(D_{a},D_{a+1}]

\displaystyle U_{1,1,\alpha}^{(a)}

\displaystyle:=\#\Bigl\{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}:\begin{subarray}{c}|C|=1,D_{a}<d_{v_{1}(C)}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1},\ \lambda_{1}(C)=\alpha\end{subarray}\Bigr\},

\displaystyle P_{2,1,2,\alpha,\beta}^{(a,b)}

\displaystyle:=\#\Bigl\{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}:\begin{subarray}{c}|C|=2,D_{a}<d_{v_{1}(C)}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1},D_{b}<d_{v_{2}(C)}^{\leq C}\leq D_{b+1},(\lambda_{1}(C),\lambda_{2}(C))=(\alpha,\beta)\end{subarray}\Bigr\},

With this scale refinement, the weighted pseudosnapshot score admits the decomposition

\displaystyle L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}U_{1,1,\alpha}^{(a)}+\sum_{a,b}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\bigl(1-(1-q_{\alpha})(1-q_{\beta})\bigr)\cdot P_{2,1,2,\alpha,\beta}^{(a,b)}

(4)

\displaystyle+c_{0}M_{3}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{a}\sum_{\alpha}u_{j}(\alpha)U_{3,j,\alpha}^{(a)}+\sum_{1\leq j<t\leq 3}\sum_{a,b}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}p_{jt}(\alpha,\beta)\cdot P_{3,j,t,\alpha,\beta}^{(a,b)}.

The coefficients

are fixed constants from Lemma

. The term

is the number of length-

classical bits.

It remains to estimate the four kinds of scale-refined coordinates above. We call

and

coordinates, because they test one endpoint of a clause. We call

and

coordinates. For each coordinate, we run a quantum sketch algorithm, described in Sections
5.2
and
5.3
. The sketches are combined to form the target quantum algorithm, and we prove Lemma
6
in Section
5.4
. We first record the quantum-register notation used by all sketches.

5.1
Quantum register

We use a
label

S
to denote the corresponding basis state
\ket{S}
. For convenience, we do not distinguish between them unless necessary. Let
U
be the set of all labels. If a basis state has a nonzero amplitude, we call it
live
. Let
T\subseteq U
be the set of live non-anchor labels. We write

\ket{Q(T)}:=\frac{\ket{\bot}+\sum_{x\in T}|x\rangle\,}{\sqrt{1+|T|}}.

Here
\ket{\bot}
is a fixed anchor basis state. It is never measured; it is included only so that after every failed measurement, the post-measurement state is still valid, including the case
T=\emptyset
. In all sketches below, we will use the quantum register with size

M:=\lceil C_{\varepsilon}m\rceil,

where
C_{\varepsilon}
is a large enough constant.

We mainly use the following three quantum operations in our algorithm.

•

Permutation.
For a permutation
\pi
of
U
, apply the unitary that fixes
\ket{\bot}
and sends
\ket{x}
to
\ket{\pi(x)}
.

•

Single-query.
For
x\in U
, measure
\ket{Q(T)}
with
\{\ket{x}\!\bra{x},I-\ket{x}\!\bra{x}\}
.

For
x\in T
: if we get
\ket{x}
, output
1
, and the quantum state collapses. Otherwise, output 0 and the new
state becomes
\ket{Q(T\setminus\{x\})}
;

For
x\notin T
: it outputs 0, and the state is unchanged.

•

Pair-query.
For distinct
x,y\in U
, measure
\ket{Q(T)}
with

\ket{\psi^{+}_{x,y}}:=\frac{\ket{x}+\ket{y}}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad\ket{\psi^{-}_{x,y}}:=\frac{\ket{x}-\ket{y}}{\sqrt{2}},

and their orthogonal complement.
The query outputs
1
on outcome
\ket{\psi^{+}_{x,y}}
, outputs
-1
on outcome
\ket{\psi^{-}_{x,y}}
, and outputs
0
on the orthogonal-complement outcome.
The state update and probabilities are:

1.

If both
x
and
y
belong to
T
, then

\Pr[+1]=\frac{2}{1+|T|},\qquad\Pr[-1]=0.

If we get the orthogonal complement, the new state becomes
\ket{Q(T\setminus\{x,y\})}
.

2.

If exactly one of them, say
z
, belongs to
T
, then

\Pr[+1]=\Pr[-1]=\frac{1}{2(1+|T|)}.

If the outcome is the orthogonal complement, the new state is

\ket{Q(T\setminus\{z\})}
.

3.

If neither belongs to
T
, the state is unchanged.

Formulas of single/pair-query.

The single-query and pair-query operations satisfy the following expectation formulas. If the state before the query is
\left|Q(T)\right\rangle
, then:

•

if
x\in U
and
\zeta_{x}\in\{0,1\}
is the output of the single-query, then

\mathbb{E}[\zeta_{x}\mid\left|Q(T)\right\rangle]=\frac{1}{|T|+1}\,\mathbf{1}[x\in T];

•

if
x,y\in U
and
\chi_{x,y}\in\{-1,0,+1\}
is the output of the pair-query, then

\mathbb{E}[\chi_{x,y}\mid\left|Q(T)\right\rangle]=\frac{2}{|T|+1}\,\mathbf{1}[x\in T]\,\mathbf{1}[y\in T].

5.2
One-endpoint estimation

Fix a one-endpoint coordinate
U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}
, where
\alpha=(s_{\alpha},i_{\alpha})
denotes a sign-bucket pair. Recall that when
D_{a}\geq\kappa/2
, we call
U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}

high-degree
. Otherwise, so
D_{a}<\kappa/2
, call it
low-degree
. We handle these two cases with slightly different strategies in Section
5.2.1
and Section
5.2.2
, respectively.

The rough idea is to sample a clause during the stream with low probability. If a sampled clause belongs to
U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}
, the output is scaled by
M
or
M/2
to cancel the low probability; otherwise it outputs
0
.

Thus, to test if the clause belongs to
U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}
, we should check all the relative constraints on the scale
a
, clause length
\ell
, endpoint position
j
, literal sign
s_{\alpha}
and bucket index
i_{\alpha}
. Indeed, the clause length, endpoint position, and literal sign are known when the clause arrives. The scale test is handled by quantum labels that track prefix degree. The bucket test is evaluated only after a successful query, using the queried candidate prefix value and the exact suffix counts.

Moreover, for the scale and bucket tests, the sketch needs the prefix total degree
d
and the prefix positive count
c
. To implement the updates by simple shifts, we store these two counts through the single threshold value
\Gamma_{a}(d,c)=d+B_{a}c
. A different pair
(d^{\prime},c^{\prime})
can sometimes reach the same queried threshold, especially when the endpoint is outside the queried scale. These accidental threshold crossings are the only extra counts considered below. We define the degree condition under which they can occur and record the properties needed later.

Random-base threshold encoding.

Fix a sufficiently large constant
K_{\mathrm{base}}:=K_{\mathrm{base}}(\varepsilon)
. For every nonempty scale
a
, choose an independent public-random integer
B_{a}
uniformly from

\Bigl\{\lceil K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{a+1}\rceil,\,\lceil K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{a+1}\rceil+1,\,\ldots,\,\lceil 2K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{a+1}\rceil\Bigr\}.

In particular, after enlarging
K_{\mathrm{base}}
if needed, we have
B_{a}>D_{a+1}+1
for every scale
a
. For a prefix positive count
c
, define

\Gamma_{a}(d,c):=d+B_{a}c.

In a high-degree sketch,
c
is the sampled positive-prefix count
s_{v}^{\leq C}
, while in a low-degree sketch,
c
is the exact positive-prefix count
p_{v}^{\leq C}
.

For every
q\in\{0,\ldots,\kappa-1\}
and every in-range degree
D_{a}<d\leq D_{a+1}
,

\mathbf{1}[\Gamma_{a}(d,c)\geq B_{a}q+D_{a}+1]-\mathbf{1}[\Gamma_{a}(d,c)\geq B_{a}q+D_{a+1}+1]=\mathbf{1}[c=q].

(5)

Likewise, for every
D_{a}<d,d_{0}\leq D_{a+1}
and every
c_{0}\geq 0
,

\mathbf{1}[\Gamma_{a}(d,c)\geq B_{a}c_{0}+d_{0}]-\mathbf{1}[\Gamma_{a}(d,c)\geq B_{a}c_{0}+d_{0}+1]=\mathbf{1}[d=d_{0},\ c=c_{0}].

(6)

These are the only identities needed on genuine scale-
a
endpoints. For the high-degree one-endpoint coordinate output, we will also use the cumulative
form: for every
q\geq 1
, every integer
c\geq 0
, and every

D_{a}<d\leq D_{a+1}
,

\mathbf{1}[\Gamma_{a}(d,c)\geq qB_{a}]=\mathbf{1}[c\geq q].

(7)

This follows from
B_{a}>D_{a+1}
.

For later use, set the largest multiplier of
B_{a}
that can appear in the false-scale condition

H_{a}:=\begin{cases}\kappa,&D_{a}\geq\kappa/2,\\
D_{a+1},&D_{a}<\kappa/2.\end{cases}

Impersonation.

We say that an endpoint with prefix degree
d

impersonates scale
a
if
d>D_{a+1}
and

d\in\bigcup_{h=1}^{H_{a}}\bigl[hB_{a}+D_{a}+1,\ hB_{a}+D_{a+1}\bigr].

(8)

To make the subsequent formulas more concise, let
\mathsf{Imp}^{a}_{C,r}
be the event that endpoint
(C,r)

impersonates scale
a
.

This condition is deliberately broader than the event that the endpoint actually gives a nonzero contribution to the wrong queried scale. Every such nonzero contribution from outside the queried scale satisfies this condition, but some endpoints satisfying it may still contribute zero because their positive-prefix count does not put the encoded value
d+B_{a}c
at the queried level.

Lemma 7

(Impersonation)
.

Assume
D_{a}<\kappa/2
. Fix a queried low-degree level
B_{a}c_{0}+d_{0}
, where
D_{a}<d_{0}\leq D_{a+1}
and
0\leq c_{0}\leq d_{0}
. Let
d^{\ast},c^{\ast}
be nonnegative integers. If

\Gamma_{a}(d^{\ast},c^{\ast})=B_{a}c_{0}+d_{0}

and
d^{\ast}\notin(D_{a},D_{a+1}]
, then
d^{\ast}>D_{a+1}
and the endpoint with prefix degree
d^{\ast}
impersonates scale
a
.

Thus, in a low-degree single-level query, every nonzero contribution from outside the queried scale is counted by the impersonation condition.

Proof.

Because
B_{a}>D_{a+1}+1
, both
d_{0}
and every positive
d^{\ast}\leq D_{a}
are below
B_{a}
. If
d^{\ast}=0
, equality modulo
B_{a}
is also impossible because
d_{0}>0
. Thus
d^{\ast}\leq D_{a}
cannot satisfy
d^{\ast}+B_{a}c^{\ast}=B_{a}c_{0}+d_{0}
: equality modulo
B_{a}
would force
d^{\ast}=d_{0}>D_{a}
.

It remains to consider
d^{\ast}>D_{a+1}
. From the equality,

d^{\ast}=(c_{0}-c^{\ast})B_{a}+d_{0}.

The integer
h:=c_{0}-c^{\ast}
must be positive; otherwise
d^{\ast}\leq d_{0}\leq D_{a+1}
. Also
h\leq c_{0}\leq d_{0}\leq D_{a+1}=H_{a}
in the low-degree case. Hence

d^{\ast}\in[hB_{a}+D_{a}+1,\ hB_{a}+D_{a+1}]

for some
1\leq h\leq H_{a}
, which is exactly the event that the endpoint impersonates scale
a
.
∎

5.2.1
High-degree case

For each

, we maintain an independent

q
-Sketch
. The case

accounts for all endpoints with

\min\{s_{v}^{\leq C},\kappa\}

Each

-Sketch

-Sketch
. Their outputs are combined to give an estimate for the high-degree coordinate.

We describe the explicit form of the quantum register as follows. There are six kinds of labels/basic states in

. We use the global register size
M=\lceil C_{\varepsilon}m\rceil
, with

chosen large enough for the fixed

1.

The fixed anchor basis state
\ket{\bot}
;

2.

Spare labels:
S_{1},\ldots,S_{M}
;

3.

-labels:

, where

,

;

4.

-labels:
E(v,t)
, where
v\in[n]
,
t\in[M]
;

5.

H
-garbage-labels:
G_{H}(v,t)
,where
v\in[n]
,
t\in[M]
;

6.

-garbage-labels:
G_{E}(v,t)
,where
v\in[n]
,
t\in[M]
;

Besides, we maintain a pointer

for the spare labels via classical register, which costs

bits. Because the basis state is specified by a variable index and a level in
[M]
, this quantum register costs
O(\log(nm))=O(\log n)
qubits, for
m=\mathrm{poly}(n)
.

What the labels are for.

The spare labels are only temporary labels used to make every update a permutation. They are not queried directly.

The
H
-labels stores the prefix total degree information. Every time a tagged unit on
u
arrives, we increase the
H(u,\cdot)
labels, so the queried label
H(u,t)
should be thought of as the level
t
of the prefix total degree.The queries on
H
-labels will tell us whether the prefix degree has already entered the scale
(D_{a},D_{a+1}]
.

The
E
-labels stores the encoded quantity. Besides the one-step update for every tagged unit, a selected positive tagged unit applies
B_{a}
additional one-step shifts to the
E
-labels. So the queried label
E(u,t)
should be thought of as recording

\Gamma_{a}(d_{u}^{\leq C},s_{u}^{\leq C})=B_{a}s_{u}^{\leq C}+d_{u}^{\leq C}.

The queries on
E
-labels will tell us which sampled positive-prefix count is consistent with the current prefix.

The garbage labels do not store any statistic we need. They are only there so that every update is a permutation on a finite set of basis labels.

Definition 12

(Increment operation)
.

Given a variable
u\in[n]
and the current pointer
\mathcal{P}
, define
\mathrm{inc}(H,u,1)
as follows.

1.

Apply the cyclic permutation of basis labels whose cycle is

S_{\mathcal{P}}\to H(u,1)\to H(u,2)\to\cdots\to H(u,M)\to G_{H}(u,M)\to\cdots\to G_{H}(u,1)\to S_{\mathcal{P}}.

We call this one elementary shift. Its quantum part is unitary because it is
a permutation of basis labels.

2.

Move the pointer to the next spare label. If
\mathcal{P}<M
,
set
\mathcal{P}\leftarrow\mathcal{P}+1
. If
\mathcal{P}=M
, set

\mathcal{P}\leftarrow 1
. Thus the pointer runs through

S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{M}
and then starts again from
S_{1}
.

For any integer
r\geq 1
,
\mathrm{inc}(H,u,r)
means repeating this elementary shift
r
times, updating
\mathcal{P}
after each repetition, saying

\mathrm{inc}(H,u,r):=\underbrace{\mathrm{inc}(H,u,1)\circ\cdots\circ\mathrm{inc}(H,u,1)}_{r\text{ times}}.

For
E
-labels, we use the same shift operation on the chain
E(u,\cdot)
; in particular,
\mathrm{inc}(E,u,B_{a})
means applying this one-step shift
B_{a}
times, so it advances
\mathcal{P}
by
B_{a}
.

-Sketch (

-Sketch (

Initialization:

The quantum register is initialized as:

\left|Q(T_{0})\right\rangle=\frac{|\perp\rangle+|S_{1}\rangle+\cdots+|S_{M-1}\rangle}{\sqrt{M}},

where the live set

Initialize

.

When a clause
C

Update.

Decompose the clause
C
into tagged units and handle them one by one. Suppose the tagged unit is on variable
u
and the live set is

Step-1.

\mathrm{inc}(H,u,1).

Step-2.

\mathrm{inc}(E,u,1).

Step-3.
If this tagged unit is positive and selected by

(See Definition

Branch 1:

. Then do nothing and end.

Branch 2:

1.

store the variable

2.

store the nonzero measurement outcome
\chi_{C}^{\ell}

3.

store
q

in the following streaming of clauses, count the suffix total degree

and suffix positive count

The classical part costs

bits. We say a sub-sketch is

Finally, the Lower-

-Sketch

In all other cases, the Lower-
q
-Sketch
(q\geq 1)

.

-Sketch

Correctness.

Fix a one-endpoint sub-sketch and a label

that it may query, where

could denote

or

-labels.
We first show that, as long as the pointer has not wrapped around,

is live exactly when

. Lemma

will then show that the assumption fails with probability at most

.

For this fixed

and

, the sub-sketch queries only this level

. Just before such a query, let

count the calls
\operatorname{inc}(F,u,1)
, and let

count the calls
\operatorname{inc}(F,u,B_{a})
. If this family has no such

calls, set

.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
t<M
, and suppose that before every query of this same label, the whole sub-sketch has used fewer than
M
elementary shifts in total. Then, with
d_{u}
and
c_{u}
as defined above, immediately before every such query,

F(u,t)\in T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_{u}+B_{a}c_{u}\geq t.

Remark 1
.

The same proof also applies to the
Z_{1}
- and
Z_{2}
-labels used later, because those sketches also update first and query afterward. See Section
5.3
for details. Therefore, we will not repeat the proof for the two-endpoint case, but instead directly cite this lemma.

Proof.

First ignore failed queries. Because the whole sub-sketch has used fewer than
M
elementary shifts, the pointer has not wrapped around. On the chain
F(u,\cdot)
, the calls counted by
d_{u}
and
c_{u}
move live labels forward by
d_{u}+B_{a}c_{u}
levels. Hence
F(u,t)
is live exactly when
d_{u}+B_{a}c_{u}\geq t
.

It remains to check that failed queries do not break this statement. A failed query can only remove the label being tested. Suppose it removes
F(u,t)
, and suppose the same label is queried again later. That later query can occur only on a later clause whose queried endpoint is again
u
. In the algorithm, the update step of that later clause is performed before the query, and this update applies at least one one-step shift to the same chain
F(u,\cdot)
. Since the total number of shifts is still below
M
, the pointer has not returned to a previously used spare position.

If
t=1
, that shift moves a live spare label into
F(u,1)
. If
t>1
, then
F(u,t-1)
was live when
F(u,t)
was removed: the live labels had
already reached level
t
, and this sub-sketch does not query level
t-1
of the same chain. The next shift moves that live label into
F(u,t)
. Thus a failed deletion of
F(u,t)
is repaired before the same label is queried again. The same condition for being live therefore holds before every query of
F(u,t)
.
∎

The previous lemma needs two assumptions: the queried level
t
is below
M
, and the total number of elementary shifts in the sub-sketch is below
M
. The next lemma checks both. The level check is deterministic after
C_{\varepsilon}
is chosen large enough; the shift bound is the part that uses the public randomness. Although the low-degree one-endpoint sketches are described later, we include them in the same statement because they use the same shift operation.

Lemma 9

(No-wrap event for one-endpoint sketches)
.

Choose the constant
C_{\varepsilon}
in
M=\lceil C_{\varepsilon}m\rceil
large enough. Then every queried level in these sub-sketches lies in
[M]
. Moreover, there is an event
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, depending only on the public hash functions, such that
\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{1}]\geq 1-1/160
, and on
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, every one-endpoint sub-sketch performs fewer than
M
elementary shifts during the whole stream.

Proof.

The stream contains at most
4m
tagged units in total. If
m=0
, no update is applied and the claim is immediate. Assume
m\geq 1
.

First consider a low-degree sub-sketch, so
D_{a}<\kappa/2
. Then
D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
. Every tagged unit applies
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
elementary shifts: it always applies the one-step updates, and a positive tagged unit applies at most
B_{a}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
additional shifts. Therefore the total number of elementary shifts in this sub-sketch is deterministically
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
.

Now consider a high-degree scale
a
, so
D_{a}\geq\kappa/2
. Let
X_{a}
be the number of positive tagged units selected by the public hash
f_{a}
in the whole stream. There is some
N_{a}\leq 4m
such that

X_{a}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\!\left(N_{a},\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}\right),\qquad\mathbb{E}[X_{a}]\leq\frac{2\kappa m}{D_{a}}.

Choose a large constant
K_{\varepsilon}
. A Chernoff bound gives

\Pr\!\left[X_{a}>K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right]\leq\exp\!\left(-c_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right)

for some constant
c_{\varepsilon}>0
.

On the event

X_{a}\leq K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}},

the extra number of elementary shifts caused by sampled positive tagged units is at most

B_{a}X_{a}\leq\bigl(2K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{a+1}+1\bigr)\cdot K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}=O_{\varepsilon}(m),

because
D_{a+1}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{3})D_{a}+1
. The deterministic one-step updates contribute another
O(m)
shifts, so the total number of elementary shifts is
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
.

It remains to check the queried levels. In the low-degree one-endpoint sketches, the queried levels are

B_{a}c+d,\qquad B_{a}c+d+1,

where
D_{a}<d\leq D_{a+1}
and
0\leq c\leq d
. Since
D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
, these levels are
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
.

In the high-degree one-endpoint sketches, the queried levels are

D_{a}+1,\qquad D_{a+1}+1,\qquad qB_{a}\quad(0\leq q\leq\kappa).

Here
D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(m)
,
B_{a}=O_{\varepsilon}(D_{a+1})
, and
\kappa=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
, so these levels are
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
. After increasing
C_{\varepsilon}
if necessary, all queried levels lie in
[M]
.

The event above depends only on the scale
a
, not on the endpoint position, sign-bucket label, or the value of
q
. Thus, once it holds for scale
a
, every one-endpoint sub-sketch on this scale uses
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
elementary shifts. After increasing
C_{\varepsilon}
again if necessary, this number is less than

M
.

Now let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
be the event that
X_{a}\leq K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}
holds for every high-degree scale
a
. Low-degree scales need no probability bound, as shown above. Because the scales are geometric, there are only

O_{\varepsilon}(1)
scales in each dyadic range
D_{a}\in[m/2^{r+1},m/2^{r}]
. There are also
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
high-degree scales with
m<D_{a}\leq 4m
. Their total failure probability can be made no larger than the first term of the dyadic sum by increasing
K_{\varepsilon}
. Thus,

\sum_{a:\,D_{a}\geq\kappa/2}\Pr\!\left[X_{a}>K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right]\leq C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\sum_{r\geq 0}\exp(-c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}2^{r})<\frac{1}{160}

after increasing
K_{\varepsilon}
if necessary. Therefore,
\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{1}]\geq 1-\frac{1}{160}
on
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, every one-endpoint sub-sketch uses fewer than
M
elementary shifts over the whole stream, and every queried level lies in
[M]
. This proves the lemma.
∎

If
\mathcal{G}_{1}
fails, the algorithm still runs legally: the pointer is cyclic, each update is still a permutation of the finite set of labels, and each query is still one of the measurements defined earlier. What may fail is only the simple interpretation of queried labels by prefix counts.

On
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, Lemma
8
applies to every label queried by a one-endpoint sketch. Thus, immediately before each query, an
H
-label is live exactly when the corresponding prefix degree has reached its queried level, and an
E
-label is live exactly when the prefix value encoded by
\Gamma_{a}
has reached its queried level. These are the only facts about the live set used in the estimates below.

Lemma 10

(Active probability)
.

Fix one sub-sketch and fix the public randomness. Suppose that, conditioned on the event that this sub-sketch is active just before the query on a clause
C
, its live set

has size
M^{\prime}-1
, where

.
Then

\Pr[\text{this sub-sketch is active just before the query on }C]=\frac{M^{\prime}}{M}.

Proof.

List the earlier queries of this sub-sketch as
\text{Query}_{1},\dots,\text{Query}_{r}
. For
i=1,\dots,r+1
, let
M_{i}
be the value of
1+|T|
just before
\text{Query}_{i}
, conditioned on the event that the sub-sketch is still active at that time. Let
M_{r+1}=M^{\prime}
. Then
M_{1}=M
.

A failed query can delete zero, one, or two queried live labels, so

M_{i}-M_{i+1}\in\{0,1,2\}
. A failed query leaves the sub-sketch active. Therefore

\Pr[\text{the sub-sketch is active after }\text{Query}_{i}\mid\text{it is active before }\text{Query}_{i}]=1-\frac{M_{i}-M_{i+1}}{M_{i}}=\frac{M_{i+1}}{M_{i}}.

Multiplying these conditional probabilities over

gives

\Pr[\text{the sub-sketch is still active just before the query on }C]=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{M_{i+1}}{M_{i}}=\frac{M^{\prime}}{M}.

∎

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{M^{\prime}}\bigl(F_{q}(C)-F_{q-1}(C)\bigr)\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C}]\cdot\mathbf{1}[H(v,D_{a}+1)\in T]\cdot\mathbf{1}[E(v,qB_{a})\in T].

For each clause

Summing over all pairs

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}(C)\right]=\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C},\,D_{a}<d_{v}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1},\,\lambda_{j}(C)=\alpha\right]

For a fixed clause

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}(C)\right]-\mathbf{1}\!\left[\begin{subarray}{c}\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C},\ D_{a}<d_{v}^{\leq C}\leq D_{a+1},\ \lambda_{j}(C)=\alpha\end{subarray}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C}]\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Imp}^{a}_{C,j}].

(14)

Recall that

denotes the event that endpoint

impersonates scale

.

Hence

\left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}\right]-U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}\right|\leq\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C},\mathsf{Imp}^{a}_{C,j}\right].

(15)

5.3
Two-endpoint estimation

Fix a two-endpoint coordinate

\alpha=(s_{\alpha},i_{\alpha})
and

(D_{a},D_{a+1}]

We call a clause

For the first endpoint

For the second endpoint

Impersonation also plays an important role in the error counting here. Recall that

denotes the true scale of the endpoint

For any queried nonempty scale

To see this, let

For a wrong-scale nonzero contribution, the integer

is one of the values allowed in (

). Therefore this endpoint is included in
the larger set defined by the impersonation condition. The converse is not claimed: an endpoint may satisfy the interval condition in (

at the queried level.

The coordinate output defined in this section is the actual algorithmic output for

a
and

can differ from the target coordinate only in the following two ways:

can still give a nonzero term to the coordinate output for

5.3.1
High-degree on both endpoints

We first discuss the case

In this case both endpoints use the sampled positive-prefix counts

Each of the four sub-sketches has an identical quantum register. We use the global register size

in a

In the one-endpoint sketch, the

For

\left|Q(T_{0})\right\rangle=\frac{|\perp\rangle+|S_{1}\rangle+\cdots+|S_{M-1}\rangle}{\sqrt{M}},\qquad T_{0}:=\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{M-1}\}.

And initialize

This register uses

Update.

Process the tagged units of the current clause one by one. Suppose the tagged unit is on variable

1.

\mathrm{inc}(Z_{1},u,1)
;

2.

\mathrm{inc}(Z_{2},u,1)
;

3.

if this tagged unit is positive and selected by

\mathrm{inc}(Z_{1},u,B_{a})
;

4.

if this tagged unit is positive and selected by
f_{b}

\mathrm{inc}(Z_{2},u,B_{b})
.

If the current clause
C

The Lower-Lower-
(\sigma,\tau)
-Sketch applies the pair-query to

The other three sub-sketches use the same pair-query with the corresponding threshold:

\displaystyle\Bigl(Z_{1}\bigl(u,B_{a}\sigma+D_{a}+1\bigr),Z_{2}\bigl(v,B_{b}\tau+D_{b+1}+1\bigr)\Bigr),

\displaystyle\Bigl(Z_{1}\bigl(u,B_{a}\sigma+D_{a+1}+1\bigr),Z_{2}\bigl(v,B_{b}\tau+D_{b}+1\bigr)\Bigr),

\displaystyle\Bigl(Z_{1}\bigl(u,B_{a}\sigma+D_{a+1}+1\bigr),Z_{2}\bigl(v,B_{b}\tau+D_{b+1}+1\bigr)\Bigr).

Denote the four outputs of pair-query by

\chi^{\ell\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C),\qquad\chi^{\ell u}_{\sigma,\tau}(C),\qquad\chi^{u\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C),\qquad\chi^{uu}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\in\{-1,0,1\}.

If the pair-query output is nonzero, the sub-sketch computes

\widetilde{b}_{u}:=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\left(\frac{2\left(\frac{2D_{a}}{\kappa}\sigma+p_{u}^{>C}\right)}{D_{a}+d_{u}^{>C}}-1+g(u)\right),\qquad\widetilde{b}_{v}:=\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\left(\frac{2\left(\frac{2D_{b}}{\kappa}\tau+p_{v}^{>C}\right)}{D_{b}+d_{v}^{>C}}-1+g(v)\right),

\displaystyle F_{\sigma,\tau}(C):=\mathbf{1}\left[\widetilde{b}_{u}\in I_{i_{\alpha}},\;\widetilde{b}_{v}\in I_{i_{\beta}}\right].

If a sub-sketch never has a successful pair-query, its output is

.

Correctness.

The same no-wrap issue appears here. Each two-endpoint sub-sketch has two label families,
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
, but they share one pointer. The analysis only needs the following fact: each queried label represents its intended threshold inequality as long as the sub-sketch performs fewer than
M
elementary shifts in total and every queried level lies in
[M]
. Under these two conditions, the pointer never returns to an earlier spare position, so the queried labels still represent the thresholds of
\Gamma_{a}
and
\Gamma_{b}
. The following lemma verifies these two conditions for all two-endpoint sub-sketches.

Lemma 11

(No-wrap event for two-endpoint sketches)
.

Choose the constant
C_{\varepsilon}
in
M=\lceil C_{\varepsilon}m\rceil
large enough. Then every level queried in these
sub-sketches lies in
[M]
. Moreover, there is an event
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, depending only on the public hash functions, such that

\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{2}]\geq 1-\frac{1}{160},

and on
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, in every sub-sketch appearing in the two-endpoint estimation, the total number of elementary shifts applied during all update steps, counting both the
Z_{1}
- and
Z_{2}
-families, is less than
M
.

Proof.

The stream contains at most
4m
tagged units in total. If
m=0
, no update is applied and the claim is immediate. Assume
m\geq 1
.

First, consider a low-degree side, say
D_{a}<\kappa/2
. Then
D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
. The deterministic one-step updates for this family apply
O(m)
elementary shifts over the whole stream, and every positive tagged unit applies at most
B_{a}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
additional elementary shifts. Therefore, this side applies
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
elementary shifts deterministically. The same argument applies to the second side when
D_{b}<\kappa/2
.

Now consider a high-degree scale
a
, so
D_{a}\geq\kappa/2
. Let
X_{a}
be the number of positive tagged units selected by the public hash
f_{a}
in the whole stream. There is some
N_{a}\leq 4m
such that

X_{a}\sim\mathrm{Bin}\!\left(N_{a},\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}\right),\qquad\mathbb{E}[X_{a}]\leq\frac{2\kappa m}{D_{a}}.

Choose a large constant
K_{\varepsilon}
. A Chernoff bound gives

\Pr\!\left[X_{a}>K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right]\leq\exp\!\left(-c_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right)

for some constant
c_{\varepsilon}>0
.

On the event

X_{a}\leq K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}},

the extra number of elementary shifts coming from the sampled positive tagged units on this scale is at most

B_{a}X_{a}\leq\bigl(2K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{a+1}+1\bigr)\cdot K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}=O_{\varepsilon}(m),

because
D_{a+1}\leq(1+\varepsilon^{3})D_{a}+1
. The deterministic one-step updates contribute another
O(m)
elementary shifts. If a two-endpoint sub-sketch has two high-degree sides, the total number of elementary shifts is the sum of the two corresponding bounds, and is still
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
.

It remains only to check that the queried levels themselves are below
M
. On a low-degree side with scale
a
, the queried levels are

B_{a}c+d,\qquad B_{a}c+d+1,

with
D_{a}<d\leq D_{a+1}
and
0\leq c\leq d
. Since

D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
, these levels are
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
.

On a high-degree side with scale
a
, the queried levels are

B_{a}\sigma+D_{a}+1,\qquad B_{a}\sigma+D_{a+1}+1\quad(0\leq\sigma\leq\kappa-1),

and the setup at the start of this section gives
D_{a+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(m)
. Since
B_{a}=O_{\varepsilon}(D_{a+1})
and
\kappa=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
, these levels are
O_{\varepsilon}(m)
. The same bounds hold for the second side. After enlarging
C_{\varepsilon}
if needed, all queried levels in both families are below
M
deterministically.

The event above depends only on the scale
a
. It controls the number of elementary shifts caused by selected positive tagged units for every two-endpoint sub-sketch that uses scale
a
on either side, because these sub-sketches use the same selected positive tagged units.

Now let
\mathcal{G}_{2}
be the event that
X_{a}\leq K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}
holds for every high-degree scale
a
. Low-degree sides need no probability bound, as shown above. Because the scales are geometric, there are only
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
scales in each dyadic range
D_{a}\in[m/2^{r+1},m/2^{r}]
. There are also
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
high-degree scales with
m<D_{a}\leq 4m
. Their total failure probability can be made no larger than the first term of the dyadic sum by increasing
K_{\varepsilon}
. Thus, the following union bound covers all high-degree scales:

\sum_{a:\,D_{a}\geq\kappa/2}\Pr\!\left[X_{a}>K_{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{D_{a}}\right]\leq C^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\sum_{r\geq 0}\exp(-c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}2^{r})<\frac{1}{160}

after enlarging

On

∎

As in the one-endpoint case, the correctness argument only needs the fact from

that a fixed queried label has
the intended threshold meaning before each query. Work on

, fix one elementary sub-sketch, and let

A failed pair-query can delete one queried label from the

5.3.2
Other cases

Assume now that at least one of

For each endpoint we use the exact positive-prefix count on a low-degree scale and the sampled positive-prefix count on a high-degree scale. Accordingly, set

Update.

Query.

If the current clause

If the first endpoint is low-degree and

\displaystyle F_{\sigma,\tau}(C):=\mathbf{1}\Biggl[

\displaystyle\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\left(\frac{2(\widehat{p}_{u}+p_{u}^{>C})}{\widehat{d}_{u}+d_{u}^{>C}}-1+g(u)\right)\in I_{i_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\left(\frac{2(\widehat{p}_{v}+p_{v}^{>C})}{\widehat{d}_{v}+d_{v}^{>C}}-1+g(v)\right)\in I_{i_{\beta}}\Biggr].

Estimation.

All the following argument is on the event

from Lemma

For each clause

\widehat{P}^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}(C):=\sum_{(\sigma,\tau)\in\Sigma_{a}\times\Sigma_{b}}\Bigl(Y^{\ell\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{\ell u}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{u\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)+Y^{uu}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\Bigr),

For a clause that is not target-shape, all four outputs are

, and write

Suppose the live set just before the query in the current sub-sketch has size

The Lower-Lower-

-Sketch queries the pair

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{\ell\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\mid|Q(T)\rangle]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{\ell u}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\mid|Q(T)\rangle]

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{M^{\prime}}F_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}]\cdot\mathbf{1}[N_{a}(C)\geq L_{a}(\sigma)]\cdot\mathbf{1}[N_{b}(C)\geq U_{b}(\tau)],

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{u\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\mid|Q(T)\rangle]

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{M^{\prime}}F_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}]\cdot\mathbf{1}[N_{a}(C)\geq U_{a}(\sigma)]\cdot\mathbf{1}[N_{b}(C)\geq L_{b}(\tau)],

\mathbb{E}\!\left[Y^{\ell\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{\ell u}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{u\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)+Y^{uu}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\right]=F_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\cdot\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}]\cdot\Delta_{a}(C,\sigma)\cdot\Delta_{b}(C,\tau),

(that is, if

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[Y^{\ell\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{\ell u}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)-Y^{u\ell}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)+Y^{uu}_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=F_{\sigma,\tau}(C)\cdot\mathbf{1}\bigl[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}\bigr]\cdot\mathbf{1}[\text{ the first endpoint is consistent with }\sigma]

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{P}^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}\bigr]-P^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}\right|

5.4
Estimating the weighted pseudosnapshot score

Run in parallel, during the same pass over the stream, all sketches defined for the one-endpoint and two-endpoint coordinates, together with the exact classical counter for
M_{3}
. Let
Z
denote the output of one such full run after applying exactly the same linear combination as

L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))
:

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a}\sum_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{1,1,\alpha}+\sum_{a,b}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\bigl(1-(1-q_{\alpha})(1-q_{\beta})\bigr)\widehat{P}^{(a,b)}_{2,1,2,\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle+c_{0}M_{3}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{a}\sum_{\alpha}u_{j}(\alpha)\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{3,j,\alpha}+\sum_{1\leq j<t\leq 3}\sum_{a,b}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}p_{jt}(\alpha,\beta)\widehat{P}^{(a,b)}_{3,j,t,\alpha,\beta}.

Fix the public randomness used for the hash functions, smoothing noise, and the random bases
B_{a}
, and call it
\omega

\omega
. In the rest of the proof, whenever
\omega
is fixed, every expectation and variance is taken only over the outcomes of the quantum measurements.

The constants are fixed in the following order. First, choose the positive integer
\kappa
large enough for the sampled-prefix estimates and the overflow bound used below. With this
\kappa
fixed, choose
K_{\mathrm{base}}
for the impersonation bound, choose
K_{\varepsilon}
for the no-wrap tail bounds, and finally choose
C_{\varepsilon}
in
M=\lceil C_{\varepsilon}m\rceil
so that every update count and queried level fits in the finite register.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{1}
be the good event from Lemma
9
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{2}
be the good event from Lemma
11
. Define

\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}_{1}\cap\mathcal{G}_{2}.

By the two lemmas and a union bound,

\Pr[\mathcal{G}^{c}]\leq\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{1}^{c}]+\Pr[\mathcal{G}_{2}^{c}]\leq\frac{1}{160}+\frac{1}{160}=\frac{1}{80}.

For every short-clause endpoint
(C,r)
, let
a(C,r)
be its true scale, and let

\mathcal{I}(C,r):=\{s:\ s\text{ is nonempty},\ s\neq a(C,r),\ (C,r)\text{ impersonates scale }s\}

be the set of wrong queried scales for which
(C,r)
satisfies the
impersonation condition. Also write

\mathcal{R}(C,r):=\{a(C,r)\}\cup\mathcal{I}(C,r)

for the true scale of
(C,r)
together with these wrong queried scales. Recall that
\mathsf{Imp}^{s}_{C,r}
denotes the event that endpoint
(C,r)

impersonates scale
s
. Define

W_{\mathrm{imp}}:=\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\sum_{r=1}^{|C|}|\mathcal{I}(C,r)|=\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\sum_{r=1}^{|C|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\text{ nonempty}\\
s\neq a(C,r)\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Imp}^{s}_{C,r}].

Thus
W_{\mathrm{imp}}
counts all endpoint-scale pairs that may contribute to a wrong queried scale. It may count too many endpoint-scale pairs, but it does not miss any endpoint-scale pair that gives a nonzero contribution to a wrong queried scale.

Let
W_{\mathrm{of}}
be the total tagged weight of short-clause endpoints whose true scale
a(C,r)
satisfies
2D_{a(C,r)}\geq\kappa
and whose sampled positive-prefix count at that scale is at least
\kappa
:

W_{\mathrm{of}}:=\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\sum_{r=1}^{|C|}w_{|C|}\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[2D_{a(C,r)}\geq\kappa\text{ and }s_{v_{r}(C)}^{\leq C}\geq\kappa\right].

The expectations in the next two auxiliary bounds are over the public randomness before
\omega
is fixed. After these bounds, expectations again follow the convention stated at the start of the proof.

Lemma 12

(Overflow weight bound)
.

\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{of}}]\leq e^{-\Omega(\kappa)}m.

Proof.

Fix one short-clause endpoint
(C,r)
, and let
a=a(C,r)
. If
2D_{a}<\kappa
, then this endpoint is not counted by
W_{\mathrm{of}}
. If
2D_{a}\geq\kappa
, then

s_{v_{r}(C)}^{\leq C}\sim\operatorname{Bin}\!\left(p_{v_{r}(C)}^{\leq C},\frac{\kappa}{2D_{a}}\right),\qquad\mathbb{E}[s_{v_{r}(C)}^{\leq C}]\leq\frac{\kappa D_{a+1}}{2D_{a}}\leq 0.6\kappa

for sufficiently small fixed
\varepsilon
. A Chernoff bound gives
\Pr[s_{v_{r}(C)}^{\leq C}\geq\kappa]\leq e^{-\Omega(\kappa)}
. The sum of the tagged weights of all short-clause endpoints is at most
4m
, so linearity of expectation gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 13

(Impersonation-count bound)
.

There is a constant
C^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}>0
depending only on
\varepsilon
such that

\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{imp}}]\leq C^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{m\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)}{K_{\mathrm{base}}}.

Proof.

Fix one endpoint
(C,r)
with prefix degree
d
and true scale
a(C,r)
. Fix a wrong nonempty scale
s\neq a(C,r)
. If
(C,r)
impersonates scale
s
, then for some
1\leq h\leq H_{s}
,

d\in[hB_{s}+D_{s}+1,\ hB_{s}+D_{s+1}].

Equivalently,

B_{s}\in\left[\frac{d-D_{s+1}}{h},\ \frac{d-D_{s}-1}{h}\right].

For a fixed
h
, this interval contains
O((D_{s+1}-D_{s}+1)/h+1)=O_{\varepsilon}(D_{s+1}/h)
integers, whereas
B_{s}
is uniform on an interval of length
\Theta(K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{s+1})
. Hence

\Pr[(C,r)\text{ impersonates scale }s\text{ through this }h]=O_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\frac{1}{K_{\mathrm{base}}h}\right).

Summing over
1\leq h\leq H_{s}
gives

\Pr[\mathsf{Imp}^{s}_{C,r}]=O_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\frac{1}{K_{\mathrm{base}}}\right).

The harmonic factor is absorbed into
O_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
, because
H_{s}\leq\kappa=\text{poly}(\varepsilon)
on high-degree scales and
H_{s}=D_{s+1}=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
on low-degree scales.

Moreover, impersonating scale
s
implies

d\leq H_{s}(2K_{\mathrm{base}}D_{s+1}+1)+D_{s+1}.

Notice that
D_{s}
grows exponentially by
s
. If
s
is high-degree then
H_{s}=\kappa
, so only the
O_{\varepsilon}(\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2))
scales with

D_{s+1}\in\left[\frac{d}{2K_{\mathrm{base}}\kappa+O_{\varepsilon}(1)},\,d\right)

are relevant. If
s
is low-degree then
D_{s+1}<\kappa=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
, so there are only
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
such scales. Therefore

\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{I}(C,r)|]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\text{ nonempty}\\
s\neq a(C,r)\end{subarray}}\Pr[\mathsf{Imp}^{s}_{C,r}]=O_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\frac{\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)}{K_{\mathrm{base}}}\right).

Since there are at most
O(m)
short-clause endpoints,

\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{imp}}]\leq C^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{m\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)}{K_{\mathrm{base}}}

for some constant
C^{\ast}_{\varepsilon}
depending only on
\varepsilon
.
∎

The same geometric argument also bounds the number of scales in
\mathcal{R}(C,r)
: there is a constant
C^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|\mathcal{R}(C,r)|\leq C^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\varepsilon}\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)\qquad\text{for every endpoint }(C,r).

(18)

Indeed, applying the same argument to a fixed endpoint
(C,r)
, with

d=d^{\leq C}_{v_{r}(C)}
, shows that low-degree wrong scales contribute only

O_{\varepsilon}(1)
possibilities, while the bound above leaves only

O_{\varepsilon}(\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2))
possible high-degree scales.

On
\mathcal{G}
, Lemma
8
says that each queried label is live exactly when its threshold inequality is true. The bounds for fixed coordinates proved in
(
13
),
(
15
),
(
16
), and
(
17
) give the following facts:

1.

every high-degree one-endpoint coordinate output is unbiased for its true coordinate;

2.

a low-degree one-endpoint coordinate output can differ from its target only when
the queried endpoint satisfies the impersonation condition for the queried scale;

3.

a two-endpoint coordinate output can differ from its target only when an endpoint
satisfies the impersonation condition for one of the queried scales, or when a
true high-degree endpoint overflows.

We now combine these fixed-coordinate bounds into a bound for the full linear
form. Recall that
\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C}
denotes the event that
C
is the target-type for
U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}
;
\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}
denotes the event that
C
is the target-type for
P^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}
.

One-endpoint contribution.

The high-degree one-endpoint coordinates are unbiased, and the counter
M_{3}
is exact on
\mathcal{G}
. Therefore only the low-degree one-endpoint coordinates can contribute here. (
15
) gives, for every fixed low-degree coordinate
(\ell,j,\alpha,a)
,

\left|\mathbb{E}\!\left[\widehat{U}^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}\right]-U^{(a)}_{\ell,j,\alpha}\right|\leq\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\mathbf{1}\!\left[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,\alpha}_{C}\right]\cdot\mathbf{1}\left[\mathsf{Imp}^{a}_{C,j}\right].

Fix an event that
(C,r)
impersonates scale
a
, where
a
is a wrong queried scale in
\mathcal{I}(C,r)
. Once the clause length, the endpoint position, and the sign of that literal are fixed, only a constant number of one-endpoint coordinates can use this event: the bucket index ranges over a constant-size set, and the coefficients
q_{\alpha}
and
u_{j}(\alpha)
in (
4
) are fixed constants. Therefore there is a constant
C^{\mathrm{one}}_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the total weighted one-endpoint error is at most
C^{\mathrm{one}}_{\varepsilon}W_{\mathrm{imp}}
.

Two-endpoint error from wrong scale pairs.

(
16
) and
(
17
) give, for every fixed
coordinate
(\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta,a,b)
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\widehat{P}^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}\bigr]-P^{(a,b)}_{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{C\in\Phi_{\leq 3}}\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Tgt}^{\ell,j,t,\alpha,\beta}_{C}]\Bigl(\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Imp}^{a}_{C,j}]+\mathbf{1}[\mathsf{Imp}^{b}_{C,t}]+\mathbf{1}[a=a(C,j),\ D_{a}\geq\kappa/2,\ s^{\leq C}_{v_{j}(C)}\geq\kappa]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{1}[b=a(C,t),\ D_{b}\geq\kappa/2,\ s^{\leq C}_{v_{t}(C)}\geq\kappa]\Bigr).

We first sum the part caused by endpoints counted by the impersonation condition for a scale
that is not their true scale.

Fix a clause
C
and one ordered pair of relevant endpoints
(C,j)
and
(C,t)
with
1\leq j<t\leq|C|
. A queried scale pair
(a,b)
can receive a nonzero term from this clause even

(a,b)
is not the true scale pair only in one of the following two ways:

•

the first endpoint impersonates the queried first scale, so
a\in\mathcal{I}(C,j)
, while the second queried scale must still belong to
\mathcal{R}(C,t)
;

•

the second endpoint impersonates the queried second scale, so
b\in\mathcal{I}(C,t)
, while the first queried scale must belong to
\mathcal{R}(C,j)
.

Hence the total number of queried scale pairs that can receive such an extra
nonzero term from this ordered pair is at most

|\mathcal{I}(C,j)|\,|\mathcal{R}(C,t)|+|\mathcal{I}(C,t)|\,|\mathcal{R}(C,j)|.

Using (
18
), this is at most

C^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\varepsilon}\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)\bigl(|\mathcal{I}(C,j)|+|\mathcal{I}(C,t)|\bigr).

The clause length, the ordered pair
(j,t)
, and the bucket indices
(\alpha,\beta)
all range over constant-size sets, and the coefficients in (
4
) are fixed constants. Therefore there is a constant
C^{\mathrm{two}}_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the total weighted wrong-scale error in all two-endpoint coordinates is at most

C^{\mathrm{two}}_{\varepsilon}\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)\,W_{\mathrm{imp}}.

Overflow contribution.

The weight
W_{\mathrm{of}}
counts the high-degree endpoints whose sampled positive-prefix count is at least
\kappa
. Overflow does not affect one-endpoint coordinates. For two-endpoint coordinates, an overflowing endpoint matters only on its true queried scale, not on wrong scales. Therefore each overflowing endpoint can affect only the constantly many true two-endpoint coordinates that use that endpoint inside its clause. Since all coefficients in (
4
) are fixed constants, there is a constant
C_{\mathrm{of}}>0
such that the total weighted overflow error is at most
C_{\mathrm{of}}W_{\mathrm{of}}
.

Combining the one-endpoint, two-endpoint, and overflow bounds, there is a constant
C_{\mathrm{imp}}=O_{\varepsilon}(\log(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2))
such that for every

\omega\in\mathcal{G}
,

\left|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega]-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|\leq C_{\mathrm{imp}}W_{\mathrm{imp}}+C_{\mathrm{of}}W_{\mathrm{of}}.

(19)

Choose
K_{\mathrm{base}}
large enough so that the
O_{\varepsilon}(\log^{2}(K_{\mathrm{base}}+2)/K_{\mathrm{base}})
bound from Lemma
13
gives

C_{\mathrm{imp}}\,\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{imp}}]\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{320},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{imp}}]\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{320C_{\mathrm{imp}}}.

The initial choice of
\kappa=\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)
also ensures

\mathbb{E}[W_{\mathrm{of}}]\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{320C_{\mathrm{of}}};

this follows from Lemma
12
.

Now define

\mathcal{S}:=\left\{W_{\mathrm{imp}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{4C_{\mathrm{imp}}}\right\},\text{ and }\mathcal{O}:=\left\{W_{\mathrm{of}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{4C_{\mathrm{of}}}\right\}.

By Markov’s inequality,

\Pr[\mathcal{S}^{c}]\leq\frac{1}{80},\text{ and }\Pr[\mathcal{O}^{c}]\leq\frac{1}{80}.

On
\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}
, (
19
) gives

\left|\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega]-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon m}{2}\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}.

(20)

We now bound the variance of one full run, still with the public randomness fixed. There are
A=O(\log(n+m))
scales. The number of one-endpoint coordinates is
O(\log(n+m))
, and the number of two-endpoint coordinates is
O(\log^{2}(n+m))
, because the clause lengths, endpoint positions, signs, and bucket indices all range over constant-size sets.

For a fixed coordinate, the number of elementary sub-sketches is
O_{\varepsilon}(1)
: for one-endpoint coordinates it is
2\kappa+2
, and for two-endpoint coordinates it is
4|\Sigma_{a}||\Sigma_{b}|=O_{\varepsilon}(1)
. Every elementary sub-sketch outputs either
0
or a number whose absolute value is at most
M=O_{\varepsilon}(m)
. Hence the output for one fixed coordinate has variance
O_{\varepsilon}(m^{2})
. Since different coordinates use independent work registers,

\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid\omega)=O_{\varepsilon}(m^{2}\log^{2}(n+m))\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal{G}.

(21)

Take
R=C^{\mathrm{rep}}_{\varepsilon}\log^{2}(n+m)
independent full runs, in parallel during the same pass, with the same public randomness
\omega
, and average their outputs:

\overline{Z}:=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}Z_{r}.

Then, for every
\omega\in\mathcal{G}
,

\mathbb{E}[\overline{Z}\mid\omega]=\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega],\text{ and }\operatorname{Var}(\overline{Z}\mid\omega)=O_{\varepsilon}(m^{2}).

Choosing
C^{\mathrm{rep}}_{\varepsilon}
large enough, Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\Pr\left[\left|\overline{Z}-\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega]\right|>\frac{\varepsilon m}{4}\ \middle|\ \omega\right]\leq\frac{1}{40}\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal{G}.

Hence on the event
\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}\cap\{|\overline{Z}-\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega]|\leq\varepsilon m/4\}
, the triangle inequality and (
20
) give

\left|\overline{Z}-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|\leq\frac{3\varepsilon m}{4}<\varepsilon m.

Also, as shown above,

\Pr[\mathcal{G}^{c}]\leq\frac{1}{80}.

Even on
\mathcal{G}^{c}
, the algorithm still runs legally. What fails there is only the estimation guarantee stated above. Therefore

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\left|\overline{Z}-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|>\varepsilon m\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr[\mathcal{G}^{c}]+\Pr[\mathcal{S}^{c}]+\Pr[\mathcal{O}^{c}]+\mathbb{E}_{\omega}\!\left[\mathbf{1}[\omega\in\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{O}]\Pr\left[\left|\overline{Z}-\mathbb{E}[Z\mid\omega]\right|>\frac{\varepsilon m}{4}\ \middle|\ \omega\right]\right].

Using the four bounds above,

\Pr\left[\left|\overline{Z}-L_{\leq 3}(\operatorname{WPsSnap}(\Phi_{\leq 3}))\right|>\varepsilon m\right]\leq\frac{1}{80}+\frac{1}{80}+\frac{1}{80}+\frac{1}{40}=\frac{1}{16}.

So the averaged output
\widehat{L}_{\leq 3}:=\overline{Z}
succeeds with probability at least
15/16
.

Space complexity.

One sub-sketch uses a quantum register of size
O(\log(n+m))
qubits and, after a successful query, only
O(\log(n+m))
additional classical bits for storing the variable name, the relevant indices, and the suffix total/positive degree. One full run contains
O_{\varepsilon}(\log^{2}(n+m))
elementary sub-sketches, so one full run uses
O_{\varepsilon}(\log^{3}(n+m))
qubits and the same order of classical working bits. The averaging factor
R=\Theta_{\varepsilon}(\log^{2}(n+m))
multiplies this by another factor
\Theta_{\varepsilon}(\log^{2}(n+m))
. Therefore, the overall algorithm uses
O_{\varepsilon}(\log^{5}(n+m))
qubits, and the classical working space is of the same asymptotic order. Public random coins are shared and are not charged to the working space, as in the rest of the paper. This proves Lemma
6
.

6
Quantum algorithm for Max-
k
SAT

We now prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\rho=0.717275
. Since the theorem only needs a constant
0.7172
, we fix
\varepsilon>0
small enough to absorb all
O(\varepsilon)
losses below.

Let
\Phi_{\mathrm{orig}}
be the input instance. As the stream arrives, the algorithm applies the online simplifications from Subsection
3
: it removes repeated literals, discards tautological clauses while counting them in an exact counter
T
, and keeps an exact counter
m_{\geq 4}
for simplified clauses of length at least four. These counters use
O(\log m)=O(\log n)
classical bits, and we let
\Phi
denote the remaining nonconstant instance.

We first describe one constant-success trial. The trial samples fresh public randomness for the hash functions and smoothing noise used in the pseudosnapshot construction. It then runs the quantum estimator of Lemma
6
with a fixed constant failure probability, say
1/16
, obtaining an estimate
\widehat{L}_{\leq 3}
. The trial outputs

Y:=\max\left\{0,\,\widehat{L}_{\leq 3}+\rho m_{\geq 4}-C\varepsilon m\right\},

where
C
is a sufficiently large constant depending only on the fixed certificate and on the constants in Lemma
5
.

Call a trial valid if both Lemma
5
holds and the estimator error is at most
\varepsilon m
. By choosing
\kappa=\operatorname{poly}(1/\varepsilon)
large enough, the first event holds with probability at least
15/16
, and Lemma
6
gives the second event with probability at least
15/16
. Thus, a trial is valid with probability at least
9/10
.

On every valid trial, the estimator error is absorbed by the shift
C\varepsilon m
. Lemma
5
gives

\rho\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)-O(\varepsilon m)\leq Y\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Every remaining nonconstant clause is satisfied by a uniformly random assignment with probability at least
1/2
, so
\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\geq m/2
. Thus the additive loss is an
O(\varepsilon)\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)
loss, and our choice of
\varepsilon
gives

0.7172\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\leq Y\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)

on every valid trial.

To boost the success probability, run
R_{\mathrm{out}}=\Theta\!\left(\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)

independent trials in parallel during the same pass, using independent public seeds and independent quantum estimator randomness. Return the median of their outputs, denoted
Z_{0}
. By a Chernoff bound, with probability at least
1-\delta
, a strict majority of the trials are valid. In that case, the median also lies in the interval

0.7172\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\leq Z_{0}\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Finally, return
Z:=T+Z_{0}
.

Since the removed tautological clauses contribute exactly
T
to every assignment, adding
T
preserves both inequalities:

0.7172\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\mathrm{orig}})\leq Z\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{\mathrm{orig}}).

One trial uses
O(\log^{5}n)
qubits because Lemma
6
is invoked with constant failure probability. The outer amplification runs
R_{\mathrm{out}}
trials in parallel, so the total space is
O\!\left(\log^{5}n\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)
qubits, plus
O(\log n)
classical bits for the exact counters. Public random coins are shared and are not charged to the working space.
∎

Appendix A
Quantum space lower bound for Max-
k
SAT

We use the following promise-gap form of the quantum streaming lower bound for Max-Cut of Kallaugher and Parekh
[
15
]
.

Theorem 3
.

[
15
]

For every constant
\varepsilon>0
, any one-pass quantum streaming algorithm that, on an
n
-vertex graph with
m_{E}
edges, distinguishes

\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)=m_{E}\qquad\text{from}\qquad\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\leq(1/2+\varepsilon)m_{E}

with constant advantage requires
\Omega(n)
qubits of space.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We reduce from this Max-Cut promise problem. For a graph
G=(V,E)
and an assignment
\sigma:V\to\{0,1\}
, define

\operatorname{Cut}_{G}(\sigma):=\bigl|\{\{u,v\}\in E:\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)\}\bigr|,\qquad\operatorname{MaxCut}(G):=\max_{\sigma}\operatorname{Cut}_{G}(\sigma).

Let
m_{E}:=|E|
. Given the edge stream of
G
, we construct a Max-
k
-SAT stream
\Phi_{G}
online by replacing each edge
\{u,v\}
with the two clauses

(x_{u}\vee x_{v}),\qquad(\neg x_{u}\vee\neg x_{v}).

Since
k\geq 2
, these are valid Max-
k
-SAT clauses. The reduction is one-pass and online, and uses only
O(\log(n+m_{E}))
additional classical space. The resulting instance has
M=2m_{E}
clauses. In what follows,
m_{E}
always denotes the number of Max-Cut edges, not the number of Max-
k
SAT clauses.

For every assignment
\sigma
, each edge contributes

1+\mathbf{1}[\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)]
satisfied clauses: if
\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)
, both clauses are satisfied, whereas if
\sigma(u)=\sigma(v)
, exactly one is satisfied. Summing over all edges gives

\operatorname{Val}_{\Phi_{G}}(\sigma)=m_{E}+\operatorname{Cut}_{G}(\sigma).

Taking the maximum over
\sigma
, we obtain

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{G})=m_{E}+\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)
.

Now suppose, toward contradiction, that there is a one-pass quantum streaming algorithm
\mathcal{A}
using
o(n)
qubits and achieving an approximation ratio

3/4+\gamma
for Max-
k
-SAT. Choose
\varepsilon<2\gamma
, and run
\mathcal{A}
on the stream
\Phi_{G}
produced above.

If
\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)=m_{E}
, then

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{G})=2m_{E}=M,

so a successful run of
\mathcal{A}
outputs

Z\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}+\gamma\right)2m_{E}=\left(\frac{3}{2}+2\gamma\right)m_{E}.

If instead
\operatorname{MaxCut}(G)\leq(1/2+\varepsilon)m_{E}
, then

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{G})\leq m_{E}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)m_{E}=\left(\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon\right)m_{E},

and every valid approximation output satisfies
Z\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi_{G})
.

Because
\varepsilon<2\gamma
, the two output ranges are separated by a threshold. Hence
\mathcal{A}
would yield an
o(n)
-space quantum streaming algorithm distinguishing the corresponding Max-Cut gap instances. This contradicts Theorem
3
. Therefore, any one-pass quantum streaming algorithm beating
3/4
for Max-
k
SAT requires
\Omega(n)
qubits of space.
∎

SAT framework.

The optimization is instead over a Max-2SAT-specific rounding certificate: the unary endpoint coefficient, the nonuniform bias partition, and the finite list of nine rounding profiles are chosen so that the best profile certifiably beats every feasible mixture of
unary and binary typed atoms.

We consider simplified unweighted Max-2SAT instances, meaning finite multisets of unary clauses
(x_{v}=s)
and binary clauses
(x_{u}=s)\vee(x_{v}=t)
with
s,t\in\{+,-\}
, after replacing repeated-literal binary clauses by the corresponding unary clause and removing tautologies. Let
|\Phi|
denote the number of remaining clauses.

Before defining the ”snapshot” of instances, we first define the bias of the variables in
\Phi
. The certificate uses the unary endpoint coefficient
w_{\mathrm{u}}:=\frac{67}{20}
; this is part of the bias definition, not an input clause weight.

Definition 13
.

For each variable
v
, let
P_{v}^{(1)},\ N_{v}^{(1)}
and

P_{v}^{(2)},\ N_{v}^{(2)}
be its positive/negative unary occurrence counts and positive/negative binary-endpoint counts. Define

p_{v}:=w_{\mathrm{u}}P_{v}^{(1)}+P_{v}^{(2)}\qquad q_{v}:=w_{\mathrm{u}}N_{v}^{(1)}+N_{v}^{(2)}\qquad d_{v}:=p_{v}+q_{v},

and the signed bias

b_{v}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{p_{v}-q_{v}}{d_{v}},&d_{v}>0,\\[4.30554pt]
0,&d_{v}=0.\end{cases}

Fix the nonuniform partition
-1=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{450}=1

from the artifact file

I_{i}=[t_{i},t_{i+1})\quad(0\leq i\leq 448)
and

I_{449}=[t_{449},t_{450}]
.

Definition 14
.

For
s,t\in\{+,-\}
and
0\leq i,j\leq 449
, define the unary and binary snapshot coordinates

U_{s,i}(\Phi):=\sum_{C=(x_{v}=s)\in\Phi}\mathbf{1}[b_{v}\in I_{i}],

S_{s,t,i,j}(\Phi):=\sum_{C=(x_{u}=s)\vee(x_{v}=t)\in\Phi}\mathbf{1}[b_{u}\in I_{i}]\mathbf{1}[b_{v}\in I_{j}].

Define the snapshot as

\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi):=\Bigl(U_{s,i}(\Phi),\,S_{s,t,i,j}(\Phi)\Bigr)_{s,t\in\{+,-\},\ 0\leq i,j\leq 449}

Now, we can define the snapshot score.

Definition 15

(Snapshot score)
.

The certificate evaluates nine fixed rounding profiles

p^{(1)},\dots,p^{(9)}\in[0,1]^{450}

from the codes
4

4

The file
artifacts/max2sat/multirounding_L450_9_rounding_curves.json

. For each rule
r
, let
q^{(r)}_{+}(i):=p_{i}^{(r)}
and
q^{(r)}_{-}(i):=1-p_{i}^{(r)}
, define the rule-
r
snapshot score by

\displaystyle A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\sum_{i=0}^{449}q^{(r)}_{s}(i)U_{s,i}(\Phi)

\displaystyle+\sum_{s,t\in\{+,-\}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{449}\Bigl(1-(1-q^{(r)}_{s}(i))(1-q^{(r)}_{t}(j))\Bigr)S_{s,t,i,j}(\Phi),

and let

A_{\max}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi)):=\max_{1\leq r\leq 9}A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi)).

We use nine profiles rather than one because no single oblivious rounding curve is best for all mixtures of unary and binary clauses. The profiles are fixed
in advance by the certificate. Each
A_{r}
is the expected number of satisfied clauses under the corresponding independent rounding rule, expressed as a linear function of the snapshot;
A_{\max}
then chooses the best of these constant many certified rules.

Lemma 14
.

For every nonempty simplified unweighted Max-2SAT instance
\Phi
,

\rho_{2}\,\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)\leq A_{\max}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi),

where
\rho_{2}=0.74252
.

Proof.

The upper bound is immediate: for every profile
r
,

A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))
is the expected number of satisfied clauses in
\Phi
under the
corresponding independent rounding rule, and is therefore at most

\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)
. Hence
A_{\max}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))\leq\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)
.

Fix an optimal assignment
x^{*}
. Refine every unary or binary clause according to its signs, bucket labels, and endpoint bits under
x^{*}
. Thus, a typed atom
is either

c=(\mathrm{U},s,i,\tau)\qquad\text{or}\qquad c=(\mathrm{S},s,t,i,j,\tau,\upsilon),

where
s,t\in\{+,-\}
,
i,j\in\{0,\dots,449\}
, and

\tau,\upsilon\in\{0,1\}
. Let
x_{c}
be the number of clauses of type
c
.

Write
\chi(+)=+1
,
\chi(-)=-1
, and

\chi_{x}(\tau)=\begin{cases}+1,&\tau=1,\\
-1,&\tau=0.\end{cases}

Define the optimum contribution of an atom by

o(\mathrm{U},s,i,\tau)=\mathbf{1}[\chi(s)=\chi_{x}(\tau)]

and

o(\mathrm{S},s,t,i,j,\tau,\upsilon)=1-\mathbf{1}[\chi(s)\neq\chi_{x}(\tau)]\mathbf{1}[\chi(t)\neq\chi_{x}(\upsilon)].

For rounding profile
r
, define its atom contribution
R_{r}(c)
by

R_{r}(\mathrm{U},s,i,\tau)=q_{s}^{(r)}(i)

and

R_{r}(\mathrm{S},s,t,i,j,\tau,\upsilon)=1-\bigl(1-q_{s}^{(r)}(i)\bigr)\bigl(1-q_{t}^{(r)}(j)\bigr).

Then, by construction,

\sum_{c}x_{c}o(c)=\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi),\qquad\sum_{c}x_{c}R_{r}(c)=A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi)).

We also need the bucket feasibility constraints. For each bucket
a
and bit
\theta
, let
D_{a,\theta}(c)
and
B_{a,\theta}(c)
be the total bias-weighted endpoint
count and signed bias-weighted endpoint count contributed by atom
c
to bucket
a
and bit class
\theta
. For unary atoms, this contribution uses the coefficient
w_{\mathrm{u}}/2
, and for binary endpoints, the coefficient
1/2
. If

I_{a}=[\ell_{a},u_{a}]
, set

g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c):=\ell_{a}D_{a,\theta}(c)-B_{a,\theta}(c),\qquad g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}(c):=B_{a,\theta}(c)-u_{a}D_{a,\theta}(c).

For the real instance, after grouping variables with
b_{v}\in I_{a}
and

x_{v}^{*}=\theta
, the sums

D_{a,\theta}:=\sum_{c}x_{c}D_{a,\theta}(c),\qquad B_{a,\theta}:=\sum_{c}x_{c}B_{a,\theta}(c)

are common scalar multiples of

\sum_{v:\,b_{v}\in I_{a},\ x_{v}^{*}=\theta}d_{v}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{v:\,b_{v}\in I_{a},\ x_{v}^{*}=\theta}d_{v}b_{v}.

The common scalar is
1/2
, since unary endpoints carry coefficient
w_{\mathrm{u}}/2
and binary endpoints carry coefficient
1/2
in the atom
bookkeeping.
Hence
B_{a,\theta}/D_{a,\theta}
, when
D_{a,\theta}>0
, is a weighted
average of biases lying in
I_{a}
. Therefore

\sum_{c}x_{c}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c)\leq 0,\qquad\sum_{c}x_{c}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}(c)\leq 0.

The finite factor-revealing LP behind the certificate is

\displaystyle\eta^{*}:=\min

\displaystyle z

(22)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sum_{c}o(c)x_{c}=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{c}g_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c)x_{c}\leq 0\qquad\forall i,\tau,

\displaystyle\sum_{c}g_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{up}}(c)x_{c}\leq 0\qquad\forall i,\tau,

\displaystyle\sum_{c}R_{r}(c)x_{c}\leq z\qquad\forall r=1,\dots,9,

\displaystyle x_{c}\geq 0\qquad\forall c.

Here, the normalization
\sum_{c}o(c)x_{c}=1
scales the optimum value to one, and
z
upper-bounds the value achieved by each of the nine rounding rules. This finite LP is solved by the accompanying search code
5
5

5

scripts/max2sat_multi_rounding_certificate_search.py
in codes
. The numerical solution is used only to find a candidate certificate; the proof below uses the
rational Farkas-dual witness checked by the exact verifier:

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\rho

(23)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}R_{r}(c)-\rho o(c)+\sum_{i,\tau}\lambda_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{lo}}g_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c)+\sum_{i,\tau}\lambda_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{up}}g_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{up}}(c)\geq 0\qquad\forall c,

\displaystyle\alpha_{r}\geq 0\ (1\leq r\leq 9),\qquad\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}=1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{lo}},\lambda_{i,\tau}^{\mathrm{up}}\geq 0.

In particular, the checker certifies nonnegative numbers

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{9}
, with
\sum_{r}\alpha_{r}=1
, and nonnegative multipliers
\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}},\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}
, such that for every typed atom

\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}R_{r}(c)-\rho_{2}o(c)+\sum_{a,\theta}\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c)+\sum_{a,\theta}\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}(c)\geq 0.

Multiplying by
x_{c}
and summing over all atoms gives

\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))-\rho_{2}\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi)+\sum_{a,\theta}\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}\sum_{c}x_{c}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{lo}}(c)+\sum_{a,\theta}\lambda_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}\sum_{c}x_{c}g_{a,\theta}^{\mathrm{up}}(c)\geq 0.

The two bucket-feasibility sums are nonpositive, and all multipliers are nonnegative. Hence

\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))\geq\rho_{2}\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).

Since
\alpha

A_{\max}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))\geq\sum_{r=1}^{9}\alpha_{r}A_{r}(\operatorname{WSnap}_{t}(\Phi))\geq\rho_{2}\operatorname{OPT}(\Phi).
