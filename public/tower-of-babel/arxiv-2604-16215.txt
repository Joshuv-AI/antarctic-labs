Log-Conformal Projective Manifolds
Maurício Corrêa, Alex Massarenti

1.
Introduction

Holomorphic conformal geometry occupies a singularly distinguished place within complex geometry, at once intersecting the theory of locally homogeneous geometric structures, uniformisation by Hermitian symmetric domains, the birational geometry of projective manifolds, and, at a more conceptual level, the twistorial perspective in which null directions and their incidence relations take precedence over any preferred metric representative. In the foundational work of Kobayashi and Ochiai
[
14
]
, holomorphic conformal structures are treated as geometric structures modelled on the hyperquadric, and this leads to striking rigidity and uniformisation phenomena. From a rather different direction, the Penrose programme
[
21
]
and its mathematical development by Atiyah–Hitchin–Singer
[
2
]
and LeBrun
[
18
]
emphasise that conformal geometry is governed not by a distinguished metric, but by its null cones and null curves. In LeBrun’s complex-geometric reformulation
[
18
]
, the space of null geodesics becomes an auxiliary incidence space, intimately linked to contact geometry, through which the underlying manifold may, at least in principle, be recovered. In the projective setting, Ye
[
25
]
showed that this null geometry admits a genuinely algebro-geometric incarnation inside
\mathbb{P}(TX)
: the divisor of null directions, the natural contact structure, and suitable families of rational curves together furnish the proper language in which conformal geometry may be recast in birational terms. From this vantage point, spaces of rational curves and their deformation theory do not appear as external machinery, but rather as the natural moduli spaces of the “complex light rays” distinguished by the conformal tensor.

The logarithmic setting is best regarded as the natural continuation of this circle of ideas. Already in dimension two, Kobayashi and Naruki
[
13
]
showed that one may replace everywhere regular conformal structures by generalised conformal tensors with controlled logarithmic behaviour along a boundary divisor, thereby bringing orbifold singularities, Hilbert modular cusps, and ramified coverings within the scope of the theory. More recently, Langer
[
17
]
has shown that the logarithmic world likewise carries natural contact-geometric structures, thereby lending further support to the expectation that the classical conformal, contact, and twistorial pictures admit a meaningful logarithmic extension. Motivated by this perspective, we study smooth complex projective simple normal crossing pairs
(X,\Delta)
endowed with an everywhere nondegenerate logarithmic conformal tensor on
T_{X}(-\log\Delta)
. The governing problem is to determine how much of the rigidity of the classical conformal theory persists once the boundary is incorporated into the geometry
ab initio
. More concretely, one asks whether the familiar rigid models continue to exhaust the possibilities, or whether phenomena of a genuinely logarithmic nature necessarily arise. In the numerically trivial direction, this question also fits naturally with the recent analysis of holomorphic tensors on log Calabi–Yau manifolds carried out by Collins and Guenancia
[
9
]
.

This logarithmic formalism is also natural from the birational standpoint. Indeed, within the minimal model programme singular models are unavoidable
[
15
]
, and one is therefore led quite naturally to consider a conformal structure on a log resolution together with the problem of descending it, after contracting the boundary in codimension at least two, to a reflexive conformal structure on the singular model downstairs. In this way, logarithmic conformal geometry furnishes a bridge between smooth pairs with boundary and compact singular models. This perspective is already reflected in the final part of the present article, where boundary contractions and compact singular outputs arise naturally from the geometry under investigation.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a smooth complex projective simple normal crossing pair of dimension
n\geq 3
, and let

g\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N\bigr)

be a fibrewise nondegenerate logarithmic conformal tensor, where
N
is a line bundle on
X
.
Our first main result gives a definitive description of the non-nef regime. It shows that the logarithmic conformal condition remains strikingly rigid: apart from the two familiar Picard-rank-one models, namely the smooth quadric and projective space with hyperplane boundary, the only further possibility is an even-dimensional fibrational geometry governed by a maximal isotropic distribution. More precisely, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a smooth complex projective simple normal crossing pair of dimension
n\geq 3
carrying an everywhere nondegenerate log-conformal tensor. Assume that
K_{X}+\Delta
is not nef. Then one of the following mutually exclusive alternatives occurs:

(1)

\Delta=\varnothing
and
X\simeq Q^{n}
;

(2)

X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\Delta
is a hyperplane;

(3)

n=2m
is even and there exists a rational maximal isotropic fibration
\pi\colon X\dashrightarrow Y
whose geometric generic fibre is the log pair
(\mathbb{P}^{m},H)
. Moreover, on dense open subsets
X^{\circ}\subset X
and
Y^{\circ}\subset Y
such that the restriction
\pi^{\circ}:=\pi|_{X^{\circ}}\colon X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is a projective bundle, there exist a line bundle
L
on
Y^{\circ}
and an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\circ}}\to V\to T_{Y^{\circ}}\otimes L^{-1}\to 0
such that
(X^{\circ},\Delta^{\circ})\simeq\bigl(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathbb{P}(T_{Y^{\circ}}\otimes L^{-1})\bigr)
and
N|_{X^{\circ}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\pi^{\circ}}(1)\otimes(\pi^{\circ})^{*}L
.

Our second main result concerns the numerically trivial case. Under natural metric hypotheses on the open part
M:=X\setminus\Delta
, we show that a logarithmic conformal structure with trivial conformal line bundle forces the restricted holonomy to be trivial. Under an additional trivial-monodromy hypothesis on the induced flat connection, it follows that
M
is semi-abelian and that
(X,\Delta)
arises as its toroidal compactification.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
, and assume that
M
carries a complete Ricci-flat Kähler metric
\omega
satisfying
(B)
(Bochner extension) and
(I)
(irreducible restricted holonomy). Assume moreover that
(X,\Delta)
carries a log-conformal tensor
(N,g)
with
N\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and that the induced flat connection on
T_{M}
has trivial monodromy. Then
M
is semi-abelian and
(X,\Delta)
is a toroidal compactification of
M
.

Taken together, these results show that logarithmic conformal geometry retains a substantial part of the rigidity of the classical theory, while simultaneously exhibiting phenomena that arise intrinsically from the presence of the boundary. In the non-nef case, one is led to two rigid Picard-rank-one models and to a genuinely logarithmic maximal isotropic fibrational alternative, the latter having no direct counterpart in the boundaryless setting. In the numerically trivial case, the picture is instead governed by holonomy, Bochner-type arguments, and the monodromy of the induced flat connection, culminating in a semi-abelian uniformisation theorem.

The proof of the non-nef classification rests on a combination of several ingredients. We first extract the numerical constraint imposed by the log-conformal tensor on its conformal line bundle, and then turn to
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curves spanning negative extremal rays. A logarithmic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-normal form for such curves, together with the pull-back of the log-conformal tensor, produces a sharp dichotomy in the splitting of
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)
and in the behaviour of the associated nullity section. This dichotomy is the decisive mechanism behind the classification: the null case leads to the quadric model, the equality case to the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},H)
, and the remaining case to the maximal isotropic fibration. We also place the resulting geometries in a logarithmic Cartan-theoretic perspective and relate them to boundary contractions and to reflexive conformal structures on singular targets.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we introduce log-conformal structures, establish the fundamental numerical constraint on the conformal line bundle, and collect the deformation-theoretic input concerning
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curves, including the logarithmic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-normal form. We also discuss the explicit model geometries that subsequently reappear in the classification. In Section
3
we study the restriction of the logarithmic tangent bundle to a minimal rational curve and derive the splitting and nullity dichotomy on which the remainder of the argument rests. Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the classification in the non-nef case, distinguishing the quadric case, the projective-space-with-hyperplane case, and the even-dimensional maximal isotropic fibrational case. In Section
5
we turn to the numerically trivial situation and prove, under the stated Bochner, holonomy, and monodromy hypotheses, the semi-abelian uniformisation theorem. Section
6
recasts the principal geometric outcomes in logarithmic Cartan-geometric terms. Finally, Section
7
discusses boundary contractions, reflexive conformal structures on singular targets, and explicit surface-level examples illustrating both the non-nef and the numerically trivial regimes.

2.
Log-conformal structures, minimal curves and
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-normal form

In this section we collect the basic geometric and deformation-theoretic tools that will be used throughout the proof of the main theorem. We first recall the notion of a log-conformal structure and the numerical constraint it imposes on the associated line bundle. We then pass to
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curves spanning a negative extremal ray, use Langer’s logarithmic
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-normal form to control their contact with the boundary, and derive the corresponding splitting properties of the logarithmic tangent bundle along such curves. These results provide the starting point for the case-by-case analysis carried out in the subsequent sections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a smooth projective simple normal crossing pair,
\dim X=n\geq 3
.
Set
E:=T_{X}(-\log\Delta),\,E^{\vee}=\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)
. A
log-conformal structure
on
(X,\Delta)
is a pair
(N,g)
where
N
is a line bundle on
X
and
g\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}(E^{\vee})\otimes N\bigr)
is fibrewise nondegenerate. In other words, contraction with
g
induces an isomorphism

(2.2)

g^{\sharp}:E\xrightarrow{\sim}E^{\vee}\otimes N.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume Definition
2.1
. Then

(2.4)

N^{\otimes n}\simeq\det(E)^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}\bigl(-2(K_{X}+\Delta)\bigr).

In particular, for any morphism
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
with image curve
C=f(\mathbb{P}^{1})
,

(2.5)

n\cdot\deg(f^{*}N)=2\cdot\bigl(-(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot C\bigr).

Proof.

Taking determinants in (
2.2
) gives

\det(E)\simeq\det(E^{\vee})\otimes N^{\otimes n}\simeq\det(E)^{-1}\otimes N^{\otimes n},

hence
N^{\otimes n}\simeq\det(E)^{\otimes 2}
.
For a simple normal crossing divisor
\Delta
one has
\det\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+\Delta)
, hence
\det E\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))
.
Intersecting with
C
gives (
2.5
).
∎

Varieties of minimal rational tangents

Let
X
be a uniruled projective manifold and let
\mathcal{K}
be an irreducible dominating family of minimal rational curves on
X
. For a general point
x\in X
, let
\mathcal{K}_{x}
be the subfamily of curves in
\mathcal{K}
passing through
x
. Fix a base point
o\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. For a general member

f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X,\qquad\text{with }f(o)=x,

the tangent direction at
x
defines a point
[df_{o}(T_{o}\mathbb{P}^{1})]\in\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
. This gives the tangent map

\tau_{x}:\mathcal{K}_{x}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X),\qquad[f]\longmapsto[df_{o}(T_{o}\mathbb{P}^{1})].

The closure of its image
\mathcal{C}_{x}:=\overline{\tau_{x}(\mathcal{K}_{x})}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
is called the
variety of minimal rational tangents
, VMRT for short, at
x
.

Remark 2.6
.

In the two basic examples discussed later, the VMRT is completely explicit. For
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
H
the family of lines, one has
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\mathbb{P}(T_{x}\mathbb{P}^{n})\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
for every point
x
.

For
X=Q^{n}
and
H
the family of lines on the quadric, one has
\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}Q^{n})
equal to the smooth null quadric.

We now work out explicitly the models appearing in the classification theorem, first globally and then in local coordinates.

Proposition 2.7
.

Set
q(z_{0},\dots,z_{n+1}):=z_{0}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n+1}^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
, and let

X:=Q^{n}:=\{[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{n+1}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n+1}\mid q=0\}.

Assume
n\geq 3
and that
Q^{n}
is smooth. Set
\Delta:=\varnothing
and
N:=\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2)
.
Then
(Q^{n},\varnothing)
carries a natural log-conformal structure in the sense of Definition
2.1
, namely
g_{Q}\in H^{0}\bigl(Q^{n},\Sym^{2}\Omega_{Q^{n}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2)\bigr)
, and
g_{Q}
is fibrewise nondegenerate. Moreover
K_{Q^{n}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(-n),\,N^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2n)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}\bigl(-2K_{Q^{n}}\bigr)
.

Proof.

We have
K_{Q^{n}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(-n)
and
\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}\bigl(-2K_{Q^{n}}\bigr)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2n)=N^{\otimes n}
. Consider the affine cone

\widehat{Q}:=\{z=(z_{0},\dots,z_{n+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+2}\setminus\{0\}\mid q(z)=0\},

and let
p:\widehat{Q}\longrightarrow Q^{n}
be the quotient by scalar multiplication. On
\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
consider the constant symmetric bilinear form
\beta:=dz_{0}^{2}+\cdots+dz_{n+1}^{2}
. Fix
z\in\widehat{Q}
. Since
dq_{z}(v)=2\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}z_{i}v_{i}
, the tangent space to the cone at
z
is

T_{z}\widehat{Q}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{n+2}\ \middle|\ \sum_{i=0}^{n+1}z_{i}v_{i}=0\right\}.

Since
q(z)=0
, the radial vector
z
itself belongs to
T_{z}\widehat{Q}
.
We claim that the restriction
\beta|_{T_{z}\widehat{Q}}
has radical exactly
\mathbb{C}z
. Indeed, if
w\in T_{z}\widehat{Q}
satisfies
\beta(w,v)=0\,\text{for every }v\in T_{z}\widehat{Q}
, then
w
is orthogonal to the hyperplane

T_{z}\widehat{Q}=z^{\perp}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{n+2}\ \middle|\ \sum z_{i}v_{i}=0\right\}

with respect to the standard symmetric form on
\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
. Hence
w\in(z^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathbb{C}z
. Conversely, since
z\in z^{\perp}
and
q(z)=0
, one has
\beta(z,v)=\sum z_{i}v_{i}=0\,\text{for every }v\in T_{z}\widehat{Q}
. So
\mathrm{rad}\bigl(\beta|_{T_{z}\widehat{Q}}\bigr)=\mathbb{C}z
. Therefore
\beta
induces a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on the quotient
T_{z}\widehat{Q}/\mathbb{C}z\simeq T_{p(z)}Q^{n}
. This is the desired conformal tensor at the point
p(z)
.
Under scalar multiplication
z\mapsto\lambda z
, the differentials transform as
dz_{i}\mapsto\lambda\,dz_{i}
.
Hence
\beta\mapsto\lambda^{2}\beta
. So the bilinear form induced on the quotient
T_{z}\widehat{Q}/\mathbb{C}z
is not invariant, but transforms with weight
2
. In other words, it descends to a global section
g_{Q}\in H^{0}\bigl(Q^{n},\Sym^{2}\Omega_{Q^{n}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2)\bigr)
. Fibrewise nondegeneracy follows from the fact proved above that
\beta|_{T_{z}\widehat{Q}}
has radical exactly
\mathbb{C}z
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

We write down the tensor of Proposition
2.7
in local coordinates. Work on the standard affine chart

U_{0}:=\{z_{0}\neq 0\}\subset Q^{n},\qquad u_{i}:=\frac{z_{i}}{z_{0}}\quad(i=1,\dots,n+1).

Then
U_{0}
is cut out by
1+u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n+1}^{2}=0
. Fix the open subset
U_{0,n+1}:=U_{0}\cap\{u_{n+1}\neq 0\}
. On
U_{0,n+1}
we may solve for
u_{n+1}
as a holomorphic function of
(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
by the implicit function theorem, since
\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{n+1}}\bigl(1+u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n+1}^{2}\bigr)=2u_{n+1}\neq 0
. Write
u_{n+1}=h(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. Differentiating the equation
1+u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n}^{2}+h^{2}=0
gives
2u_{1}\,du_{1}+\cdots+2u_{n}\,du_{n}+2h\,dh=0
, hence

(2.9)

dh=-\frac{u_{1}}{h}\,du_{1}-\cdots-\frac{u_{n}}{h}\,du_{n}.

Now trivialize
\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2)
on
U_{0}
by the section
z_{0}^{2}
. In this trivialization, the conformal tensor
g_{Q}
is represented by the ordinary symmetric
2
-form
\widetilde{g}_{Q}:=du_{1}^{2}+\cdots+du_{n}^{2}+dh^{2}
. Using (
2.9
), we obtain
dh^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}}{h}\,du_{i}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{h^{2}}\,du_{i}\,du_{j}
. Therefore

(2.10)

\widetilde{g}_{Q}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{ij}+\frac{u_{i}u_{j}}{h^{2}}\right)du_{i}\,du_{j}.

The matrix of
\widetilde{g}_{Q}
in the coordinate basis
(du_{1},\dots,du_{n})
is
I_{n}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\,u\,{}^{t}\!u,\,u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{t}
. Its determinant is

\det\!\left(I_{n}+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\,u\,{}^{t}\!u\right)=1+\frac{u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n}^{2}}{h^{2}}=\frac{h^{2}+u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n}^{2}}{h^{2}}=-\frac{1}{h^{2}}\neq 0,

since
1+u_{1}^{2}+\cdots+u_{n}^{2}+h^{2}=0
. So the tensor is explicitly nondegenerate in local coordinates.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
\ell\subset Q^{n}
be a line. Since
\Delta=\varnothing
, we work with the ordinary tangent bundle. The normal exact sequence
0\to N_{\ell/Q^{n}}\to N_{\ell/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\to N_{Q^{n}/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}|_{\ell}\to 0
becomes
0\to N_{\ell/Q^{n}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\to 0
, since
N_{\ell/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}
and
N_{Q^{n}/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Q^{n}}(2)
. A straightforward computation gives
N_{\ell/Q^{n}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. Using
0\to T_{\ell}\to T_{Q^{n}}|_{\ell}\to N_{\ell/Q^{n}}\to 0
and
T_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)
, we obtain
T_{Q^{n}}|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. This is exactly the splitting that appears in the quadric side of the main theorem.

We now turn on the projective-space model with hyperplane boundary.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let

X:=\mathbb{P}^{n},\Delta:=H:=\{Z_{0}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
N:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2).

Then
(\mathbb{P}^{n},H)
carries a natural log-conformal structure
g_{H}\in H^{0}\bigl(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Sym^{2}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\bigr)
, and
g_{H}
is fibrewise nondegenerate. Moreover
K_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-n-1),\,K_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}+H\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-n)
, so
N^{\otimes n}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2n)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\bigl(-2(K_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}+H)\bigr)
.

Proof.

For
i=1,\dots,n
define

(2.13)

\omega_{i}:=\frac{Z_{0}\,dZ_{i}-Z_{i}\,dZ_{0}}{Z_{0}}.

This is a global section of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
. Indeed, the numerator has homogeneous degree
2
in the affine cone variables, and dividing by
Z_{0}
lowers the weight by one; the only possible pole is along
Z_{0}=0
, and it is logarithmic. Set
z_{i}:=\frac{Z_{i}}{Z_{0}}
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Then
U_{0}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}
and
H\cap U_{0}=\varnothing
.
In the trivialization of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
given by
Z_{0}
, one computes
\omega_{i}=d\!\left(\frac{Z_{i}}{Z_{0}}\right)=dz_{i}
. So on
U_{0}
the forms
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}
are exactly the standard coordinate
1
-forms.

Now, work on
U_{1}:=\{Z_{1}\neq 0\}
. Use coordinates
u_{0}:=Z_{0}/Z_{1},\,u_{i}:=Z_{i}/Z_{1},\,i=2,\dots,n
. Then
H\cap U_{1}=\{u_{0}=0\}
. We compute the local expressions of the
\omega_{i}
in the trivialization of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
given by
Z_{1}
. For
i=1
we have

\omega_{1}=\frac{Z_{0}\,dZ_{1}-Z_{1}\,dZ_{0}}{Z_{0}}.

Since
Z_{0}=u_{0}Z_{1}
, we have
dZ_{0}=du_{0}\cdot Z_{1}+u_{0}\,dZ_{1}
, hence
Z_{0}\,dZ_{1}-Z_{1}\,dZ_{0}=u_{0}Z_{1}dZ_{1}-Z_{1}(du_{0}\cdot Z_{1}+u_{0}dZ_{1})=-Z_{1}^{2}\,du_{0}.
Dividing by
Z_{0}=u_{0}Z_{1}
gives

(2.14)

\omega_{1}=-\,\frac{du_{0}}{u_{0}}\qquad\text{in the local frame }Z_{1}.

For
i\geq 2
, since
Z_{i}=u_{i}Z_{1}
, we have
dZ_{i}=du_{i}\cdot Z_{1}+u_{i}\,dZ_{1}
. Therefore

Z_{0}\,dZ_{i}-Z_{i}\,dZ_{0}=u_{0}Z_{1}(du_{i}\cdot Z_{1}+u_{i}dZ_{1})-u_{i}Z_{1}(du_{0}\cdot Z_{1}+u_{0}dZ_{1})=Z_{1}^{2}(u_{0}\,du_{i}-u_{i}\,du_{0}),

and dividing by
Z_{0}=u_{0}Z_{1}
gives

(2.15)

\omega_{i}=du_{i}-\frac{u_{i}}{u_{0}}\,du_{0}=du_{i}-u_{i}\,\frac{du_{0}}{u_{0}},\qquad i=2,\dots,n.

Equations (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) show explicitly that the
\omega_{i}
have only logarithmic poles along
H
. On
U_{0}
, this is clear since
\omega_{i}=dz_{i}
. On
U_{1}
, the local generators of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
are
\frac{du_{0}}{u_{0}},\ du_{2},\ \dots,\ du_{n}
. From (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), we recover these generators from the
\omega_{i}
:
\frac{du_{0}}{u_{0}}=-\omega_{1},\,du_{i}=\omega_{i}-u_{i}\omega_{1},\,i=2,\dots,n
. Hence
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}
form a basis of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
on
U_{1}
. Therefore
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{\oplus n}
, and after dualizing,

(2.16)

T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(-\log H)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)^{\oplus n}.

Define

(2.17)

g_{H}:=\omega_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\omega_{n}^{2}\in H^{0}\bigl(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Sym^{2}\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(2)\bigr).

Since the
\omega_{i}
form a local frame of
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}^{1}(\log H)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)
, the matrix of
g_{H}
in this frame is the identity matrix.
Hence
g_{H}
is fibrewise nondegenerate.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

On the affine chart
U_{0}=\{Z_{0}\neq 0\}
, where
\omega_{i}=dz_{i}
, the tensor of Proposition

Remark 2.20

Let

Remark 2.28

3.
Splitting and nullity dichotomy

In this section we study the splitting type of the logarithmic tangent bundle along a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curve and the behaviour of the associated nullity section. The logarithmic differential determines a distinguished saturated line subbundle, and bend-and-break imposes strong restrictions on the remaining summands.

Combined with the self-duality furnished by the log-conformal tensor, this leads to a sharp dichotomy between the null case, where the nullity section vanishes identically, and the equality case, where the splitting is entirely rigid. This dichotomy provides the central structural input for the analysis of the three possibilities considered in the sections that follow.

Recall Definition
2.1
and let
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
be as in Proposition
2.34
.
Set
D_{\mathrm{red}}=(f^{-1}\Delta)_{\mathrm{red}}
and
\delta=\deg D_{\mathrm{red}}\leq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The morphism
f
induces a logarithmic differential

df^{\log}:T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})\to f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta).

Let
L:=\Sat\,\mathrm{Im}(df^{\log})\subset f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)
.
Then
L\simeq T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2-\delta)
.

Proof.

On a smooth curve,
\mathrm{Im}(df^{\log})
is torsion-free of rank
1
, hence a line bundle
F
.
The map
T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})\to F
is generically an isomorphism, hence
F\simeq T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})(-Z)
for some effective
Z
.
Saturation removes torsion in the quotient supported on
Z
, so
L\simeq F(Z)\simeq T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})
.
Finally
T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})\simeq\mathcal{O}(2-\delta)
for
\delta\in\{0,1\}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Write
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b_{j}),\,b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
. Then:

(i)

b_{1}=2-\delta
;

(ii)

b_{j}\leq 1
for all
j\geq 2
.

Proof.

Fix distinct points
p,q\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\Supp(D_{\mathrm{red}})
.
If some
b_{j}\geq 2
for
j\geq 2
, then
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(b_{j}-2))\neq 0
and hence

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)(-p-q))\neq 0
, producing a nontrivial deformation fixing
p,q
.
Bend-and-break with two fixed points
[
16
, Section II.5]
yields a reducible cycle
C\equiv C_{1}+C_{2}
with
[C_{i}]\in R
,
contradicting minimality as in Lemma
2.32
. Hence
b_{j}\leq 1
for
j\geq 2
.
By Lemma
3.1
, there is a subbundle
\mathcal{O}(2-\delta)
, so
b_{1}\geq 2-\delta
.
If
b_{1}\geq 3-\delta
, the same bend-and-break argument gives a contradiction, so
b_{1}=2-\delta
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Set

q_{f}:=g(df^{\log},df^{\log})\in H^{0}\Bigl(\mathbb{P}^{1},\ f^{*}N\otimes(T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}}))^{-2}\Bigr).

Then
T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2-\delta),
and
\deg\Bigl(f^{*}N\otimes(T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}}))^{-2}\Bigr)=\deg(f^{*}N)-4+2\delta
.

Proof.

The contraction of
df^{\log}
with
g\in\Sym^{2}(E^{\vee})\otimes N
gives
q_{f}
; the degree computation is straightforward.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

In the setup above we have
\deg\Bigl(f^{*}N\otimes(T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}}))^{-2}\Bigr)\leq 0
and exactly one of the following occurs:

(i)

q_{f}\equiv 0
;

(ii)

\delta=1
and
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}
.

Proof.

Pull back
E\simeq E^{\vee}\otimes N
to obtain
f^{*}E\simeq(f^{*}E)^{\vee}\otimes f^{*}N
.
With splitting degrees
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
, self-duality gives
\deg(f^{*}N)=b_{1}+b_{n}
.
By Proposition
3.2
,
b_{1}=2-\delta
and
b_{n}\leq 1
, hence
\deg(f^{*}N)\leq 3-\delta
.
Thus
\deg(f^{*}N)-4+2\delta\leq(3-\delta)-4+2\delta=-1+\delta\leq 0
, using Lemma
3.3
and
\delta\leq 1
.
If the degree is negative then
q_{f}\equiv 0
. If it is
0
, then
\delta=1
and
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, forcing
b_{n}=1
and hence all
b_{j}=1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
\delta=0
. Then
q_{f}\equiv 0
and
\deg(f^{*}N)\in\{1,2\}
. Moreover, if
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, then
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, and in particular
\ell(R)=n
.

Proof.

Write
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b_{j})
with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
. Since
\delta=0
, Proposition
3.2
gives
b_{1}=2
and
b_{j}\leq 1
for every
j\geq 2
. Let
d:=\deg(f^{*}N)
. Pulling back the logarithmic conformal isomorphism
T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N
and using uniqueness of the splitting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we get
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=d
for every
i
. In particular,
d=b_{1}+b_{n}=2+b_{n}
. Since
b_{2}\leq 1
and
b_{n-1}\leq 1
, we also have
d=b_{2}+b_{n-1}\leq 2
. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.3
,
nd=2\ell(R)
, and since
R
is
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative, one has
\ell(R)>0
, hence
d>0
. Therefore
d\in\{1,2\}
.

Now, Lemma
3.3
gives
\deg\bigl(f^{*}N\otimes(T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}}))^{-2}\bigr)=d-4
. Since
\delta=0
and
d\leq 2
, this degree is negative. Hence the line bundle carrying
q_{f}
has no nonzero global sections, and therefore
q_{f}\equiv 0
.

Assume finally that
d=2
. Then
b_{n}=d-b_{1}=0
. For every index
2\leq i\leq n-1
one has
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=2
, while both summands are at most
1
. Hence
b_{i}=b_{n+1-i}=1
for all such
i
. This proves that
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. Moreover, since
d=2
, Lemma
2.3
gives
2\ell(R)=2n
, hence
\ell(R)=n
.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Let
U
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
carrying a holomorphic conformal structure, that is, a line subbundle
[g]\subset\Sym^{2}\Omega_{U}^{1}
whose nonzero local sections are nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms.

Let
\gamma:C\to U
be a nonconstant holomorphic curve, where
C
is a smooth complex curve.
We say that
\gamma
is a
null geodesic
if for one, hence any, local holomorphic representative
g_{0}\in H^{0}(V,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{U}^{1})
of the conformal class on an open set
V\subset U
meeting
\gamma(C)
, the following two conditions hold on
\gamma^{-1}(V)
:

(i)

the tangent direction is null,
g_{0}(d\gamma,d\gamma)=0
;

(ii)

\gamma
is an unparametrized geodesic for
g_{0}
, that is, if
t
is a local coordinate on
C
and
\dot{\gamma}:=d\gamma\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)
, then
\nabla^{g_{0}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\dot{\gamma}
is everywhere proportional to
\dot{\gamma}
.

Remark 3.7
.

The notion above is independent of the chosen local representative
g_{0}
of the conformal class.
Indeed, replacing
g_{0}
by
g_{1}=e^{\varphi}g_{0}
does not change the null condition
g_{1}(d\gamma,d\gamma)=e^{\varphi}g_{0}(d\gamma,d\gamma)=0
, and the Levi-Civita connections of
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
have the same unparametrized null geodesics.
Thus the notion of null geodesic is conformally invariant. This is the notion used by Ye in
[
25
, Section 2]
.

Notation 3.8
.

Let
U
be a complex manifold and let
W
be an irreducible family of rational curves on
U
. We denote by
\locus_{U}(W)
the closure in
U
of the union of the images of members of
W
. If
x\in U
, we denote by
W_{x}
the subfamily of members of
W
passing through
x
, and by
\locus_{U}(W,x)
the closure in
U
of the union of the images of members of
W_{x}
.

The next lemma is the local geometric input from Ye that will be used whenever the relevant family is known to consist generically of null geodesics.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
U
be a complex manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
carrying a holomorphic conformal structure. Let
W
be an irreducible family of rational curves on
U
such that the general member of
W
is a null geodesic in the sense of Definition
3.6
. Assume moreover that
W
is the family of minimal rational curves associated with a
K_{U}
-negative extremal ray in the sense of
[
Ye92
, Section 2]
.
Then for a general point
x\in\locus_{U}(W)
one has
\dim\locus_{U}(W,x)=n-1
. More precisely,
\locus_{U}(W,x)
is locally a hypersurface, and its tangent cone at
x
is the cone over the smooth null quadric in
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}U)
.

Proof.

This is exactly the content of
[
25
, Lemma 2.5 and Lemma 2.7]
.
∎

The next lemma explains why the hypotheses of Lemma
3.9
are satisfied in our setting when
\delta=0
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume
\delta=0
, and let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. Assume moreover that
V
is unsplit. Then every member of
V
whose image meets
U:=X\setminus\Supp\Delta
is entirely contained in
U
and satisfies
q_{g}\equiv 0
.

If, in addition,
V
is the family of minimal rational curves spanning the extremal ray
R
, then the general member of
V
is a null geodesic on
U
. Consequently, for a general point
x\in\locus(V)
one has
\dim\locus(V,x)=n-1
, and
\locus(V,x)
is locally a hypersurface whose tangent cone at
x
is the cone over the smooth null quadric in
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}U)
.

Proof.

Since
\delta=0
, we have
f^{*}\Delta=0
. Let
[g]\in V
be such that
g(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cap U\neq\varnothing
. Then
g(\mathbb{P}^{1})
is not contained in
\Delta
. Since all members of
V
have the same numerical class as
C
, we have
\Delta\cdot g(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\Delta\cdot C=0
. On the other hand,
g^{*}\Delta
is an effective divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, hence it has degree
0
and therefore
g^{*}\Delta=0
. It follows that
g(\mathbb{P}^{1})\subset U
.

Let
d:=\deg(f^{*}N)
. Since
[g(\mathbb{P}^{1})]=[C]
, one has
\deg(g^{*}N)=d
. By Lemma
3.5
,
d\in\{1,2\}
. Since
\delta=0
, Lemma
3.3
shows that
q_{g}
is a section of a line bundle of degree
d-4<0
. Hence
q_{g}\equiv 0
.

Assume now that
V
is the family of minimal rational curves spanning the extremal ray
R
. Since every general member of
V
is contained in
U
and
K_{U}=K_{X}|_{U}
, this family may be regarded as a family of minimal rational curves on the conformal manifold

. By
[
Ye92
, Theorem 1]
, the general member of

is a null geodesic on

. Therefore all hypotheses of Lemma

are satisfied, and the stated conclusions follow.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Assume
\delta=1
and write
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b_{j}),\qquad b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

b_{1}=1
and
b_{j}\leq 1
for every
j\geq 2
.

(ii)

If
d:=\deg(f^{*}N)
, then
d\in\{1,2\}
.

(iii)

If
d=2
, then
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}
.

(iv)

If
d=1
, then
n=2m
is even, one has
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus m}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus m}
, and the nullity section
q_{f}
vanishes identically.

Proof.

Part (i) is Proposition
3.2
. Pulling back the log-conformal isomorphism
T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N
along
f
and using uniqueness of the splitting on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we obtain
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=d:=\deg(f^{*}N)\,\text{for every }i
. Summing over
i
gives
\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}=\frac{n}{2}\,d
.

Since every
b_{j}\leq 1
by (i), we have
\sum_{j}b_{j}\leq n
, hence
d\leq 2
. On the other hand
b_{1}=1
, so
d\neq 0
. This proves (ii). If
d=2
, then
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=2
and every
b_{j}\leq 1
. Hence every
b_{j}=1
, proving (iii).
Assume now
d=1
. Then
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=1
for all
i
, so every
b_{j}
is either
1
or
0
. Since the total sum is
n/2
, there are exactly
n/2
summands equal to
1
and exactly
n/2
summands equal to
0
; in particular
n=2m
is even and the splitting has the stated form.
Finally, Lemma
3.3
gives
\deg\Bigl(f^{*}N\otimes T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\log D_{\mathrm{red}})^{-2}\Bigr)=d-2=-1
, so the section
q_{f}
must vanish identically. This proves (iv).
∎

4.
Classification when
K_{X}+\Delta
is not nef

We keep the notation of Sections
2
and
3
. Thus
R\subset\NE(X)
is a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray,
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
is a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curve spanning
R
, and after Proposition
2.33
we may and will assume that
\delta:=\deg D_{\mathrm{red}}\leq 1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
then
\ell(R)=n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
one has
2\ell(R)=n\deg(f^{*}N)
. If
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, this gives
2\ell(R)=2n
, hence
\ell(R)=n
.
∎

The case
\delta=1

Assume throughout this subsection that
\delta=1
. Since
D_{\mathrm{red}}
consists of a single point, the scheme-theoretic divisor
f^{-1}\Delta
is supported at one point as well. We denote it by
Z:=f^{-1}\Delta=mp
for some closed point
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
and some integer
m\geq 1
. Notice that
m=\deg(f^{*}\Delta)=\Delta\cdot C
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\delta=1
and write
Z=f^{-1}\Delta=mp
scheme-theoretically. Then there is a natural exact sequence

(4.3)

0\to f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\to f^{*}T_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to 0.

Proof.

Since
(X,\Delta)
is a smooth simple normal crossing pair and
f(\mathbb{P}^{1})\not\subset\Delta
, the image of
f
meets the smooth locus of exactly one irreducible component of
\Delta
at the point
f(p)
. Shrinking analytically around
f(p)
, we may choose local coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
on a neighborhood
U\subset X
of
f(p)
such that
\Delta\cap U=\{x_{1}=0\}
. On
U
the logarithmic tangent bundle is generated by
x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
, whereas the ordinary tangent bundle is generated by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
. Therefore one has the standard exact sequence

0\to T_{X}(-\log\Delta)|_{U}\to T_{X}|_{U}\to\mathcal{O}_{\Delta\cap U}\to 0,

where the quotient is generated by the class of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}
.

Choose a local coordinate
t
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
centered at
p
. Since
f^{-1}\Delta=mp
scheme-theoretically, the pull-back
f^{*}x_{1}
vanishes to order exactly
m
at
p
. After replacing
x_{1}
by a unit multiple we may assume
f^{*}x_{1}=t^{m}
. Pulling back the local generators above, we see that
f^{*}T_{X}
is generated near
p
by
e_{1}:=f^{*}\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right),\,e_{i}:=f^{*}\!\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\ (i\geq 2)
, while
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)
is generated by
t^{m}e_{1},\ e_{2},\dots,e_{n}
.
Hence the quotient is

\frac{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},p}e_{1}}{t^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},p}e_{1}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},p}/(t^{m})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Z}.

Away from
p
the curve does not meet
\Delta
, so the logarithmic and ordinary tangent bundles coincide along
f
. Therefore the local description globalizes to the exact sequence (
4.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
\delta=1
and
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
. Then the ray
R
is also
K_{X}
-negative, the curve
C:=f(\mathbb{P}^{1})
is
K_{X}
-minimal in
R
, and
-K_{X}\cdot C=n+1,\,\Delta\cdot C=1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
one has
\ell(R)=n
, that is,
-(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot C=n
. Since
\Delta\cdot C=m\geq 1
, we get
-K_{X}\cdot C=-(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot C+\Delta\cdot C=n+m>0
, so
R
is
K_{X}
-negative.
We claim that
C
is minimal in the ray
R
also with respect to
-K_{X}
. Let
\Gamma
be any rational curve with
[\Gamma]\in R
. Since
R
is one-dimensional, there exists a rational number
a>0
such that
[\Gamma]=a[C]
in
N_{1}(X)
. Intersecting with the Cartier divisor
K_{X}+\Delta
gives
-(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot\Gamma=a\,\ell(R)=an
. The left-hand side is a positive integer, since
R
is
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative and
K_{X}+\Delta
is Cartier. Since
n=\ell(R)
is the smallest positive value of
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
on curves spanning

, one must have

, hence
a\geq 1
. Therefore
-K_{X}\cdot\Gamma=a(-K_{X}\cdot C)=a(n+m)\geq n+m
. This proves that
C
is
K_{X}
-minimal in the ray
R
.

Now, apply Mori’s length bound to the
K_{X}
-negative extremal ray
R
on the smooth projective manifold
X
; see
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
16
, Chapter II, Theorem 1.2]
. Since
C
is
K_{X}
-minimal in
R
, we obtain
-K_{X}\cdot C\leq n+1
. Together with
-K_{X}\cdot C=n+m
and
m\geq 1
, this yields
n+m\leq n+1
, hence

. Therefore
\Delta\cdot C=1
and
-K_{X}\cdot C=n+1
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Assume
\delta=1
and
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
. Then
f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}
and
f^{*}T_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-1)}
.

Proof.

The first splitting is Lemma
3.11
(iii). By Lemma
4.4
, one has
\deg Z=\Delta\cdot C=1
. Therefore Lemma
4.2
becomes

(4.6)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus n}\to f^{*}T_{X}\to k(p)\to 0.

In particular

. Write
f^{*}T_{X}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d_{i})
with

. Since

is nonconstant, the differential

is nonzero, hence

. On the other hand, the injection in (

(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=(2,1,\dots,1)

∎

Lemma 4.9

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
S\subset Q^{2m-2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2m-1}
be an irreducible smooth subvariety of dimension
m-1
. Assume that, for a general point
y\in S
, the embedded projective tangent space
\mathbb{T}_{y}S
is a maximal linear subspace of the quadric
Q^{2m-2}
. Then
S
is linear.

Proof.

Fix a general point
y\in S
. Since the statement is local near
y
, we work in an affine chart of the quadric centered at
y
. There exist affine coordinates
(u,v,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{C}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{C}
, with the origin corresponding to
y
, such that the quadric is given by
z=u\cdot v=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}u_{i}v_{i}
.

After a linear change of coordinates preserving this equation, we may assume that the affine tangent space to
S
at the origin is
L:=\{v=0,\ z=0\}
. Since
S
is smooth of dimension
m-1
and tangent to
L
at the origin, after shrinking we may write
S
as the graph of a holomorphic map
v=\varphi(u)
, hence
z=u\cdot\varphi(u)
, with
\varphi(0)=0
and
d\varphi_{0}=0
. For a nearby point
u
and a vector
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{m-1}
, the corresponding tangent vector to
S
is
\bigl(\xi,\ d\varphi_{u}(\xi),\ \varphi(u)\cdot\xi+u\cdot d\varphi_{u}(\xi)\bigr)
. Since, by assumption, the affine tangent space to
S
at a general point is contained in the quadric, for every
\xi
the affine line through the point
(u,\varphi(u),u\cdot\varphi(u))
in the direction above is contained in the quadric. Substituting
(u,\varphi(u),u\cdot\varphi(u))+t\bigl(\xi,\ d\varphi_{u}(\xi),\ \varphi(u)\cdot\xi+u\cdot d\varphi_{u}(\xi)\bigr)
into the equation
z=u\cdot v
and comparing the coefficient of
t^{2}
, we obtain
\xi\cdot d\varphi_{u}(\xi)=0
for every
\xi
. Polarizing, this gives
\xi\cdot d\varphi_{u}(\eta)+\eta\cdot d\varphi_{u}(\xi)=0
for all
\xi,\eta
, so the Jacobian matrix
d\varphi_{u}
is skew-symmetric for every
u
.

Now write
A_{ij}(u):=\partial\varphi_{i}/\partial u_{j}
. Since
d\varphi_{u}
is skew-symmetric, one has
A_{ij}=-A_{ji}
. Differentiating, we obtain
\frac{\partial A_{ij}}{\partial u_{k}}=\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{i}}{\partial u_{k}\partial u_{j}}
. The right-hand side is symmetric in
j
and
k
, since it is a second derivative, and skew-symmetric in
i
and
j
, since
A_{ij}=-A_{ji}
. Hence, if we set
T_{ijk}:=\partial^{2}\varphi_{i}/\partial u_{j}\partial u_{k}
, then
T_{ijk}=T_{ikj}
and
T_{ijk}=-T_{jik}
. Cycling the indices gives

T_{ijk}=-T_{jik}=-T_{jki}=T_{kji}=T_{kij}=-T_{ikj}=-T_{ijk},

so
T_{ijk}=0
for all
i,j,k
. Therefore all second derivatives of
\varphi
vanish, hence
d\varphi
is constant. Since
d\varphi_{0}=0
, we conclude that
d\varphi\equiv 0
, so
\varphi\equiv 0
.
Thus, near
y
, the variety
S
coincides with the linear space
L
. Since
L
is Zariski closed and
S\cap L
contains a nonempty analytic open subset of the irreducible variety
S
, it follows that
S\subset L
. Since
\dim S=\dim L=m-1
, we conclude that
S=L
. Hence
S
is linear.
∎

F_{y}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{m}
. Since
\ell
is a line in
\mathbb{P}^{m}
, one has
T_{F_{y}}|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}
, hence
T_{F_{y}}(-\log(\Delta|_{F_{y}}))|_{\ell}
has degree
(m+1)-d
. Comparing with the previous paragraph, we obtain
(m+1)-d=m
, hence
d=1
. Therefore
\Delta|_{F_{y}}
is a hyperplane in
F_{y}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{m}
.
∎

The case
\delta=0

The remaining case is
\delta=0
. We first exclude the subcase
\deg(f^{*}N)=1
, and then treat the only possible residual case
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
.

Lemma 4.18
.

Assume
\delta=0
. Then
\deg(f^{*}N)\neq 1
. Equivalently, in the case
\delta=0
one necessarily has
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\delta=0
and
\deg(f^{*}N)=1
. Write

f^{*}T_{X}=f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(b_{j})

with
b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
. By Lemma
3.5
, one has
q_{f}\equiv 0
and
\deg(f^{*}N)\in\{1,2\}
. Since we are assuming
\deg(f^{*}N)=1
, the self-duality relation
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=1
holds for every
i
, while Proposition
3.2
gives
b_{1}=2
and
b_{j}\leq 1
for
j\geq 2
. It follows that
b_{n}=-1
. For every index
2\leq i\leq n-1
, the relation
b_{i}+b_{n+1-i}=1
together with
b_{i}\leq 1
forces each middle pair to be
(1,0)
. In particular
n
is even, say
n=2m
, and

f^{*}T_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1).

Let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
, and let
\widetilde{V}
be the normalization of the closure of its image in the Chow variety.

We first show that
V
is a covering family. Since
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},f^{*}T_{X})=0
, the space
V
is smooth at
[f]
, of dimension
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},f^{*}T_{X})=3m
. Now fix a general point
x\in\locus(V)
, and let
g:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
be a general member of the family through
x
, with
g(0)=x
. By semicontinuity,
g^{*}T_{X}
has the same splitting type as above. Hence
g^{*}T_{X}(-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2)
, so
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},g^{*}T_{X}(-1))=0
and
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},g^{*}T_{X}(-1))=m+1
. Therefore the germ of the space of pointed maps
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)\to(X,x)
at
[g]
is smooth of dimension
m+1
, and passing to unparametrized curves gives
\dim\widetilde{V}_{x}=m-1
.

Since
\delta=0
, Proposition
2.34
(ii) applies to
V
. Thus
\dim V\leq\dim\locus(V)+\dim\locus(V,x)+1
. Using
\dim V=3m
and
\dim\locus(V,x)=m-1
, we obtain
3m\leq\dim\locus(V)+m
. Hence
\dim\locus(V)\geq 2m=n
, and therefore
\locus(V)=X
. Thus
\widetilde{V}
is a covering family. It is also unsplit by Lemma
2.32
, since here
\Delta\cdot C=0
and the minimality with respect to
K_{X}+\Delta
is the same as the minimality with respect to
K_{X}
.

Let
x\in U:=X\setminus\Supp\Delta
be a general point, and let
\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
be the VMRT of the family
\widetilde{V}
at
x
. Since
\dim\widetilde{V}_{x}=m-1
, the tangent map is generically finite onto its image by
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, and therefore
\dim\mathcal{C}_{x}=m-1
.

Now, let
g:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
be a general member of the family through
x
, with
g(0)=x
. The positive part of
g^{*}T_{X}
is
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}
. The pulled-back conformal tensor restricts on this positive part to a section of
\Sym^{2}\!\bigl((\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)})^{\vee}\bigr)\otimes g^{*}N
.
Since
g^{*}N\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
and every summand of this bundle has negative degree, this section vanishes identically. Therefore the image of the positive part at
0
is a maximally isotropic
m
-dimensional subspace of the quadratic space
(T_{x}X,g_{x})
.

By
[
10
, Proposition 2.6]
, for a general point of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
the embedded projective tangent space to
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is precisely the projectivization of this positive tangent space. Hence, at a general point, the tangent space to
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is a maximal linear subspace of the smooth null quadric

\mathcal{N}_{x}:=\{[v]\in\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)\mid g_{x}(v,v)=0\}.

By Lemma
4.14
, every irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is therefore a linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
.

We can now apply
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
to the covering unsplit family
\widetilde{V}
. It follows that there exists a dense open subset
X^{\circ}\subset X
and a
\mathbb{P}^{m}
-bundle
\pi^{\circ}:X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
such that every member of
\widetilde{V}
meeting
X^{\circ}
is a line in a fibre of
\pi^{\circ}
. Let
\ell
be a general such line. Since
\ell
lies in a fibre
\mathbb{P}^{m}
of
\pi^{\circ}
, one has
T_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}
, and the normal bundle of the fibre in
X^{\circ}
is trivial of rank
m
. Hence
T_{X}|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus m}
. On the other hand, every general member of the family has splitting type
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(m-1)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
, which is impossible. This contradiction proves that
\deg(f^{*}N)\neq 1
.
∎

Assume throughout this subsection that
\delta=0
. By Lemma
4.18
, one has
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
. Then
f(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cap\Delta=\varnothing
, so
\Delta\cdot C=0
and the whole family lies in
U:=X\setminus\Supp\Delta
. By Lemma
3.5
, one has

f^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)=f^{*}T_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}},

and
q_{f}\equiv 0
. Moreover, Lemma
4.1
gives
\ell(R)=n
.

Notation 4.19
.

For every point
x\in U
, the restriction of the log-conformal tensor to
U
induces an ordinary holomorphic conformal structure. We denote by

\mathcal{N}_{x}:=\{[v]\in\mathbb{P}(T_{x}U)\mid g_{x}(v,v)=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}U)

the corresponding smooth null quadric.

The next lemma is the first structural consequence of the equality
\ell(R)=n
in the case
\delta=0
.

Lemma 4.20
.

Assume
\delta=0
and
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
. Then
\dim Y\leq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{R}:X\to Y
be the contraction of the extremal ray
R
. Since
\Delta\cdot C=0
, the ray
R
is
K_{X}
-negative as well. Indeed, for every curve class
\Gamma\in R
one has
(K_{X}+\Delta)\cdot\Gamma<0
and
\Delta\cdot\Gamma=0
, hence
K_{X}\cdot\Gamma<0
.

By Lemma
4.1
, the ray
R
has length
\ell(R)=n
. We now apply the Ionescu–Wiśniewski inequality to the
K_{X}
-negative extremal contraction
\varphi_{R}
. Let
E
be an irreducible component of the exceptional locus of
\varphi_{R}
, and let
F
be an irreducible component of a nontrivial fibre of
\varphi_{R}|_{E}
. Then
\dim E+\dim F\geq\dim X+\ell(R)-1=2n-1
.

Since
\dim E\leq n
and
\dim F\leq n
, this forces
\dim F\geq n-1
. If
\varphi_{R}
were birational, then every irreducible component of the exceptional locus would satisfy
\dim E\leq n-1
, and the inequality above would give
n-1+\dim F\geq 2n-1
, hence
\dim F\geq n
, which is impossible for a nontrivial fibre in an
n
-dimensional variety. Therefore
\varphi_{R}
is of fibre type.

Now, let
F
be a general fibre of
\varphi_{R}
. Since
\varphi_{R}
is of fibre type, the exceptional locus is all of
X
, so we may take
E=X
in the inequality above. Thus
n+\dim F\geq 2n-1
, hence
\dim F\geq n-1
. Therefore
\dim Y=n-\dim F\leq 1
, as claimed.
∎

The next lemma excludes the case in which the contraction of
R
has one-dimensional base.

Lemma 4.21
.

Assume
\delta=0
,
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, and let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. Assume moreover that
V
is unsplit. Then
\dim Y\neq 1
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\dim Y=1
, and let
F
be a general fibre of
\varphi_{R}
. Since
\varphi_{R}
is a contraction from the smooth variety
X
onto a normal curve, the base
Y
is smooth, and the general fibre
F
is a smooth subvariety of dimension
n-1
.

By Lemma
3.10
, the general member of
V
is a null geodesic on
U:=X\setminus\Supp\Delta
. Therefore Lemma
3.9
applies, and for a general point
x\in\locus(V)
one has
\dim\locus(V,x)=n-1
. More precisely, the projectivized tangent cone of
\locus(V,x)
at
x
is a smooth quadric hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
.

Now, choose a general point
x\in F\cap U
. Since every curve in the family
V
has numerical class in the ray
R
, every member of
V
passing through
x
is contracted by
\varphi_{R}
and hence is contained in the fibre
F
. Therefore
\locus(V,x)\subset F
. Since both
\locus(V,x)
and
F
have dimension
n-1
, the inclusion above implies that
\locus(V,x)
contains an open neighborhood of
x
inside
F
. In particular,
\mathbb{P}\bigl(C_{x}\locus(V,x)\bigr)=\mathbb{P}(T_{x}F)
. But
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}F)
is a projective space of dimension
n-2
, whereas Lemma
3.9
says that the same projective variety is a smooth quadric hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
. This is impossible. Hence
\dim Y\neq 1
.
∎

The previous two lemmas force the contraction of the extremal ray to be trivial.

Lemma 4.22
.

Assume
\delta=0
,
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, and let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. Assume moreover that
V
is unsplit. Then
Y
is a point. In particular,
\rho(X)=1
and
X
is Fano.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.20
, one has
\dim Y\leq 1
, while Lemma
4.21
shows that
\dim Y\neq 1
. Therefore
\dim Y=0
, so the contraction
\varphi_{R}:X\to Y
maps
X
to a point.
Hence the relative Picard number of
\varphi_{R}
is
\rho(X/Y)=\rho(X)=1
. Since
R
is
K_{X}
-negative, the divisor
-K_{X}
has positive degree on every nonzero effective curve class. By Kleiman’s ampleness criterion,
-K_{X}
is ample. Therefore
X
is Fano.
∎

Notation 4.23
.

Assume
\delta=0
, let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
, and let
\widetilde{V}
be the normalization of the closure of its image in the Chow variety of
X
. For a general point
x\in U:=X\setminus\Supp\Delta
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
the VMRT of the family
\widetilde{V}
at

.

The next lemma identifies the VMRT in the case
\delta=0
with the null quadric of the conformal structure on
U
.

Lemma 4.24
.

Assume
\delta=0
,
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, and let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. Assume moreover that
V
is unsplit. Then, for a general point
x\in U
, the VMRT
\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
of the family
\widetilde{V}
is exactly the null quadric
\mathcal{N}_{x}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.22
, the contraction of the ray
R
is to a point. Hence the family
\widetilde{V}
dominates
X
. Fix a general point
x\in U
, and let
g:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
be a general member of the family passing through
x
, with
g(0)=x
. By Lemma
3.5
, since
\deg(g^{*}N)=2
, one has
g^{*}T_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. Therefore
g^{*}T_{X}(-1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus(n-2)}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
, so
H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},g^{*}T_{X}(-1))=0
and
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},g^{*}T_{X}(-1))=n
.
Thus the germ of the space of pointed maps
(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)\to(X,x)
at
[g]
is smooth of dimension
n
. Passing to unparametrized curves amounts to quotienting by
\Aut(\mathbb{P}^{1},0)
, which has dimension
2
. Hence the family of curves through
x
has dimension
\dim\widetilde{V}_{x}=n-2
.

By
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, the tangent map of the family at
x
is generically finite onto its image. Therefore the VMRT
\mathcal{C}_{x}
has dimension
n-2
.

On the other hand, by Lemma
3.10
, the general member of
V
is a null geodesic on
U
. In particular, since

, the tangent direction of a general member of the family at

\mathcal{C}_{x}\subset\mathcal{N}_{x}
.

Now
\mathcal{N}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)

. Since
\mathcal{C}_{x}
is an irreducible closed subset of

∎

The next proposition identifies the case
\delta=0
with the standard quadric model.

Proposition 4.25
.

Assume
\delta=0
,
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, and let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. Assume moreover that
V
is unsplit. Then
\Delta=\varnothing
and
X\simeq Q^{n}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.22
, the variety
X
is Fano and
\rho(X)=1
. By Lemma
4.24
, for a general point
x\in X
the VMRT of the dominating unsplit family
\widetilde{V}
is the smooth quadric hypersurface
\mathcal{N}_{x}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{x}X)
.

We may therefore apply the Cartan–Fubini type extension theorem for Fano manifolds of Picard number one whose VMRT is a smooth hyperquadric; see
[
19
, Section 4]
. It follows that
X\simeq Q^{n}
.

It remains to show that
\Delta=\varnothing
. Let
\ell\subset Q^{n}
be a line belonging to the family
\widetilde{V}
. Since
\delta=0
, one has
\Delta\cdot\ell=0
. On the other hand,
\rho(Q^{n})=1
, so every effective divisor on
Q^{n}
is linearly equivalent to
dH_{Q}
for some integer
d\geq 0
, where
H_{Q}
is the hyperplane class. If
d>0
, then
\Delta\cdot\ell=d(H_{Q}\cdot\ell)=d>0
, since
H_{Q}\cdot\ell=1
. Therefore
d=0
, and hence
\Delta=\varnothing
.
∎

Definition 4.26
.

A
rational maximal isotropic fibration
of a log-conformal pair
(X,\Delta,g)
of dimension
2m
is a dominant rational map

\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y

for which there exist dense open subsets
X^{\circ}\subset X
and
Y^{\circ}\subset Y
such that
\pi^{\circ}:X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is smooth and

T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}(-\log\Delta^{\circ})\subset T_{X^{\circ}}(-\log\Delta^{\circ})

is a maximally isotropic subbundle with respect to
g|_{X^{\circ}}
.

The previous results combine to give the following classification.

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a smooth complex projective simple normal crossing pair of dimension
n\geq 3
carrying an everywhere nondegenerate log-conformal tensor. Assume that
K_{X}+\Delta
is not nef. Then one of the following mutually exclusive alternatives occurs.

(i)

\Delta=\varnothing
and
X\simeq Q^{n}
.

(ii)

X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\Delta
is a hyperplane.

(iii)

n=2m
is even and there exists a rational maximal isotropic fibration

\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y

whose geometric generic fibre is the log pair
(\mathbb{P}^{m},H)
. Moreover, on dense open subsets
X^{\circ}\subset X
and
Y^{\circ}\subset Y
such that the restriction

\pi^{\circ}:=\pi|_{X^{\circ}}:X^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}

is a projective bundle, there exist a line bundle
L
on
Y^{\circ}
and an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\circ}}\to V\to T_{Y^{\circ}}\otimes L^{-1}\to 0

such that

(X^{\circ},\Delta^{\circ})\simeq\bigl(\mathbb{P}(V),\mathbb{P}(T_{Y^{\circ}}\otimes L^{-1})\bigr),

and

N|_{X^{\circ}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\pi^{\circ}}(1)\otimes(\pi^{\circ})^{*}L.

Proof.

Choose a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal rational curve spanning a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-negative extremal ray
R
, let
f:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to X
be its normalization, and replace
f
by the morphism given by Proposition
2.33
. Then
\delta\leq 1
.

Assume first that
\delta=0
. Let
V\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X)
be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. By Lemma
2.32
, the component
V
is unsplit. By Lemma
4.18
, one has
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
. Therefore Proposition
4.25
yields
\Delta=\varnothing
and
X\simeq Q^{n}
. This is case (i).

Assume now that
\delta=1
. By Lemma
3.11
(ii), one has
\deg(f^{*}N)\in\{1,2\}
. If
\deg(f^{*}N)=2
, then Theorem
4.8
yields
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\Delta
is a hyperplane. This is case (ii).

Assume finally that
\deg(f^{*}N)=1
, and let

W\subset\Hom(\mathbb{P}^{1},X;f^{*}\Delta\subset\Delta)

be the irreducible component containing
[f]
. By Lemma
4.16
, the family
W
is unsplit and covering. By Theorem
4.17
, there exists a morphism

\phi:=\phi_{R}:X\to Y

such that, for a general point
y\in Y
, the fibre
F_{y}:=\phi^{-1}(y)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{m}
, the restriction
\Delta|_{F_{y}}
is a hyperplane in
F_{y}
, and every member of
\widetilde{W}
meeting
F_{y}
is a line in
F_{y}
.

After shrinking
Y^{\circ}
if necessary, we may assume that every fibre of
\phi^{\circ}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{m}
and that every member of
\widetilde{W}
meeting a fibre is a line in that fibre. We now identify the boundary on the fibres. Let
y\in Y^{\circ}
, let

F_{y}:=(\phi^{\circ})^{-1}(y)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{m},

and let
\ell\subset F_{y}
be a line belonging to the family
\widetilde{W}
. Since
\ell
is a general member of the third case, Lemma
3.11
(iv) gives

T_{X}(-\log\Delta)|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus m}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus m},

so
\deg\bigl(T_{X}(-\log\Delta)|_{\ell}\bigr)=m
.
Since
F_{y}
is a smooth fibre of
\phi^{\circ}
, its normal bundle in
X^{\circ}
is trivial of rank
m
. Let
D_{y}:=\Delta^{\circ}|_{F_{y}}
, where
\Delta^{\circ}:=\Delta\cap X^{\circ}
. Restricting the logarithmic relative tangent sequence to
\ell
, we obtain

0\to T_{F_{y}}(-\log D_{y})|_{\ell}\to T_{X}(-\log\Delta)|_{\ell}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus m}\to 0.

Hence
\deg\bigl(T_{F_{y}}(-\log D_{y})|_{\ell}\bigr)=m
.
Since
F_{y}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{m}
, there exists an integer
d\geq 0
such that
D_{y}\sim dH
, where
H
denotes the hyperplane class on
F_{y}
. Moreover,

T_{F_{y}}|_{\ell}\simeq T_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus(m-1)}.

Therefore

\deg\bigl(T_{F_{y}}(-\log D_{y})|_{\ell}\bigr)=\deg(T_{F_{y}}|_{\ell})-\deg(D_{y}|_{\ell})=(m+1)-d.

Comparing the two expressions gives
d=1
. Thus
D_{y}
is a hyperplane in
F_{y}
.
Shrinking
Y^{\circ}
once more if necessary, we may assume that
\Delta^{\circ}\to Y^{\circ}
is smooth and that, for every
y\in Y^{\circ}
, the fibrewise divisor
\Delta^{\circ}|_{F_{y}}
is a hyperplane in
F_{y}
. We may therefore define the logarithmic relative tangent bundle

P^{\circ}:=T_{X^{\circ}/Y^{\circ}}(-\log\Delta^{\circ})\subset T_{X^{\circ}}(-\log\Delta^{\circ}).

Then
\rk P^{\circ}=m
.
Let

\sigma:=g|_{P^{\circ}\otimes P^{\circ}}\in H^{0}\bigl(X^{\circ},\Sym^{2}((P^{\circ})^{\vee})\otimes N|_{X^{\circ}}\bigr).

Let again
\ell\subset F_{y}
be a line contained in a fibre. Since
(F_{y},\Delta^{\circ}|_{F_{y}})\simeq(\mathbb{P}^{m},H)
, one has

P^{\circ}|_{\ell}=T_{F_{y}}(-\log(\Delta^{\circ}|_{F_{y}}))|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)^{\oplus m}.

Moreover, since
\deg(f^{*}N)=1
, one has
N|_{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
. Hence

\Sym^{2}\bigl((P^{\circ}|_{\ell})^{\vee}\bigr)\otimes N|_{\ell}\simeq\Sym^{2}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus m}\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)^{\oplus\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}.

Therefore

H^{0}\Bigl(\ell,\Sym^{2}\bigl((P^{\circ}|_{\ell})^{\vee}\bigr)\otimes N|_{\ell}\Bigr)=0,

so

5.
Numerically trivial case: semi-abelian uniformisation

The previous sections deal with the case in which
K_{X}+\Delta
is not nef. We now turn to the numerically trivial case
K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
under additional metric hypotheses on the open part
M:=X\setminus\Delta
. The result proved in this section is conditional: under a Bochner-type extension property and an irreducibility assumption on the restricted holonomy of a complete Ricci-flat Kähler metric on
M
, the existence of a log-conformal tensor forces the restricted holonomy to be trivial. Under an additional trivial-monodromy hypothesis on the induced flat connection, this implies that the logarithmic tangent bundle is trivial, and hence that
(X,\Delta)
is a toroidal compactification of a semi-abelian variety.

This point of view is motivated in part by recent work of Collins and Guenancia
[
9
]
, which shows, in the setting of log Calabi–Yau complements endowed with complete Ricci-flat Kähler metrics, that Bochner-type phenomena and holonomy properties may depend in a delicate way on the geometry of the boundary. The formulation adopted here isolates the abstract Bochner–holonomy package needed for the uniformisation argument below.

Throughout this section,
(X,\Delta)
is a smooth projective simple normal crossing pair of dimension
n\geq 2
, and we set
M:=X\setminus\Delta
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\omega
be a complete Ricci-flat Kähler metric on
M
. We say that
(M,\omega)
satisfies:

(B)

Bochner extension
if for every
p,q\geq 0
and every logarithmic tensor bundle

\mathcal{E}_{p,q}:=T_{X}(-\log\Delta)^{\otimes p}\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)^{\otimes q},

restriction induces a bijection between
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}_{p,q})
and the space of
\nabla^{\omega}
-parallel sections of
\mathcal{E}_{p,q}|_{M}
;

(I)

irreducible restricted holonomy
if the connected restricted holonomy group
\Hol^{\circ}(\omega)
acts irreducibly on
T_{M,x}
for some, hence any, point
x\in M
.

Definition 5.2
.

A
toroidal compactification
of a semi-abelian variety
G
is a smooth projective simple normal crossing pair
(X,\Delta)
endowed with an algebraic action of
G
on
X
whose open orbit is
X\setminus\Delta
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume
K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
, and assume that
M
carries a complete Ricci-flat Kähler metric
\omega
satisfying
(B)
and
(I)
. Assume moreover that
(X,\Delta)
carries a log-conformal tensor
(N,g)
with
N\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Then
\Hol^{\circ}(\omega)=\{1\}
. In particular,
\omega
is flat.

Proof.

Since
N\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the log-conformal tensor gives a nonzero section
g\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\bigr)
. By (B), its restriction
g|_{M}
is
\nabla^{\omega}
-parallel.

Fix a point
x\in M
, and write
V:=T_{M,x}
. Since
g|_{M}
is parallel, the tensor
g_{x}\in\Sym^{2}(V^{\vee})
is invariant under
\Hol^{\circ}(\omega)
. By Berger–Simons theory, the restricted holonomy of an irreducible Ricci-flat Kähler metric is one of the following:
\{1\}
,
\mathrm{SU}(n)
, or, if
n
is even,
\mathrm{Sp}(n/2)
; see
[
3
,
22
,
11
]
. In the standard representation, neither
\mathrm{SU}(n)
nor
\mathrm{Sp}(n/2)
preserves a nonzero element of
\Sym^{2}(V^{\vee})
. Therefore
g_{x}\neq 0
forces
\Hol^{\circ}(\omega)=\{1\}
. Hence
\omega
is flat.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume
K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
, and assume that
M
carries a complete Ricci-flat Kähler metric
\omega
satisfying
(B)
and
(I)
. Assume moreover that
(X,\Delta)
carries a log-conformal tensor
(N,g)
with
N\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and that the flat connection on
T_{M}
has trivial monodromy. Then
M
is semi-abelian and
(X,\Delta)
is a toroidal compactification of
M
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
, the metric
\omega
is flat. Since the flat connection on
T_{M}
has trivial monodromy, the tangent bundle
T_{M}
admits a global frame of parallel holomorphic vector fields. By (B), these vector fields extend uniquely across the boundary to global logarithmic vector fields
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\bigr)
. Their restrictions form a frame of
T_{M}
, so the wedge
s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n}
is a nonzero global section of
\det T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}\bigl(-(K_{X}+\Delta)\bigr)
.

Since
K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
, the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))
is numerically trivial. Let
D
be the zero divisor of
s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n}
. Then
D
is an effective divisor numerically equivalent to zero. This forces
D=0
: indeed, if
A
is any ample divisor on
X
and
D\neq 0
, then
A^{n-1}\cdot D>0
, contradicting the numerical triviality of
D
. Therefore
s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{n}
is nowhere vanishing, and the sections
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
trivialise
T_{X}(-\log\Delta)
. Hence
T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus n}
.

By Winkelmann’s criterion
[
24
, Theorem 1]
, triviality of the logarithmic tangent bundle on a smooth projective simple normal crossing compactification is equivalent to the existence of a semi-abelian algebraic group acting on
X
with open orbit
M
. Thus
M
is semi-abelian and
(X,\Delta)
is a toroidal compactification of
M
in the sense of Definition

.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

A corresponding statement up to finite étale cover holds provided the hypotheses above are preserved after passing to the cover. More precisely, if after replacing
M
by a finite étale cover and
(X,\Delta)
by a smooth simple normal crossing compactification of that cover, the pulled-back metric still satisfies (B) and (I), the pulled-back line bundle
N
becomes trivial, and the pulled-back flat connection has trivial monodromy, then Theorem
5.4
applies to the cover.

6.
A logarithmic Cartan-geometric interpretation

We conclude with a conceptual complement to the non-nef classification established in Sections
2
–
4
. The aim of this subsection is not to introduce a further classification result, but rather to show that the three possibilities arising there admit a natural interpretation within a unified conformal-geometric formalism. More precisely, the quadric case, the affine-logarithmic projective case, and the split-null case may all be viewed through the lens of conformal Cartan geometry in the logarithmic category. We record the relevant formalism here in a form sufficiently explicit for the induced logarithmic tensor to be described completely.

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
n+2
endowed with a nondegenerate quadratic form
q
, let

G:=SO(V,q),

and let
P\subset G
be the stabilizer of a fixed isotropic line
L_{0}\subset V
. Then the smooth quadric
Q^{n}
is the homogeneous space

(6.1)

Its Lie algebra admits a
|1|
-grading

(6.2)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1},\qquad\mathfrak{g}_{0}\simeq\mathfrak{co}(n,\mathbb{C}),\qquad\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1},

which is the standard algebraic package underlying holomorphic conformal geometry and its Cartan-geometric formulation; see
[
23
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. For the logarithmic extension of Cartan geometry to a simple normal crossing boundary, compare
[
4
, §3.1]
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a smooth projective simple normal crossing pair. A

of type
(G,P)
on
(X,\Delta)
consists of a holomorphic principal

-bundle
\pi:\mathcal{G}\to X
together with a

-valued logarithmic

\omega_{\log}\in H^{0}\bigl(\mathcal{G},\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{G}}(\log\mathcal{G}_{\Delta})\otimes\mathfrak{g}\bigr),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{\Delta}:=\pi^{-1}(\Delta),

such that:

(1)

for every
u\in\mathcal{G}
, the induced linear map

\omega_{\log,u}:T_{\mathcal{G},u}(-\log\mathcal{G}_{\Delta})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{g}

is an isomorphism;

(2)

for every
p\in P
, one has
R_{p}^{*}\omega_{\log}=\operatorname{Ad}(p^{-1})\omega_{\log}
;

(3)

if
A\in\mathfrak{p}
and
\zeta_{A}
is the fundamental vector field on
\mathcal{G}
generated by
A
, then

(6.5)

\omega_{\log}(\zeta_{A})=A.

An
exact logarithmic Weyl scale
is a reduction of structure group from

to
G_{0}:=CO(n,\mathbb{C})
, equivalently a section of the quotient bundle
\mathcal{G}/\exp(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to X
, whose restriction to

is an ordinary exact Weyl structure in the sense of parabolic conformal geometry; see
[
6
]
and
[
7
, Chapter 5]
. Compare also the general Cartan-geometric background in
[
23
, Chapter 2]
and the logarithmic Cartan-geometric setup in
[

.

On the open part

, an exact Weyl scale decomposes the Cartan connection into the familiar three pieces

\sigma^{*}\omega=\theta+\gamma+\rho,

where
\theta
is the soldering form with values in
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
,
\gamma
is the Weyl connection with values in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, and

is the Rho-tensor with values in
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
; compare
[
7
, Chapter 5]
. In the logarithmic setting one asks that these pieces extend across

Proposition 6.6
.

Assume that
(X,\Delta)

(6.7)

and a canonically induced section

which is fibrewise nondegenerate.

Proof.

We divide the argument into four steps.

Step 1: the logarithmic tangent exact sequence.

Let

\pi:\mathcal{G}\to X
is a holomorphic principal
P

(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}_{\Delta})\simeq(U\times P,(\Delta\cap U)\times P).

Choose coordinates
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
on

\Delta\cap U=\{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}=0\}

for some

(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
on

(6.9)

The first

, while the last

and are killed by

. Hence the local coordinate description shows that

restricts to a surjective morphism

(6.10)

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{G}/X}\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{G}}(-\log\mathcal{G}_{\Delta})\xrightarrow{\,d\pi\,}\pi^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\longrightarrow 0,

whose kernel is precisely
T_{\mathcal{G}/X}
. Since exactness is local on the base, this yields the claimed global exact sequence of locally free sheaves.

Step 2: quotienting the Cartan form by
\mathfrak{p}
.

At every point
u\in\mathcal{G}
, the logarithmic Cartan form gives an isomorphism

\omega_{\log,u}:T_{\mathcal{G},u}(-\log\mathcal{G}_{\Delta})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{g}.

By (
6.5
), the subspace
T_{\mathcal{G}/X,u}
is mapped isomorphically onto
\mathfrak{p}
. Hence the composition with the quotient map
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}
vanishes precisely on
T_{\mathcal{G}/X}
. Using (
6.10
), it descends to an isomorphism

(6.11)

\pi^{*}T_{X}(-\log\Delta)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{G}\times(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}).

The
P
-equivariance of
\omega_{\log}
implies that this descended map is
P
-equivariant. Passing to associated bundles yields (
6.8
).

Step 3: the quadratic form on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}
.

The tangent space to the quadric
Q^{n}=G/P
at the base point
[L_{0}]
identifies with

(6.12)

T_{[L_{0}]}Q^{n}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(L_{0},L_{0}^{\perp}/L_{0}),

and the isotropy representation of
P
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}
is the natural one on this tangent space; see
[
5
, §4.1–§4.2]
and
[
7
, Chapter 4]
. Since
L_{0}
is isotropic, the ambient quadratic form
q
induces a nondegenerate quadratic form

\overline{q}\in\Sym^{2}\bigl((L_{0}^{\perp}/L_{0})^{\vee}\bigr).

Given
\varphi,\psi\in\operatorname{Hom}(L_{0},L_{0}^{\perp}/L_{0})
and
\ell\in L_{0}
, we define

B(\varphi,\psi)(\ell\otimes\ell):=\overline{q}\bigl(\varphi(\ell),\psi(\ell)\bigr).

This yields a
P
-equivariant, fibrewise nondegenerate symmetric bilinear map

(6.13)

B:\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow(L_{0}^{\vee})^{\otimes 2}.

By construction, the associated weight line is precisely
N=L^{-2}
.

Step 4: passage to the associated bundle on
X
.

Using (
6.8
), the pairing (
6.13
) descends to a global section

g\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}(T_{X}(-\log\Delta)^{\vee})\otimes N\bigr)=H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N\bigr).

Its fibrewise nondegeneracy follows from the nondegeneracy of
\overline{q}
. This is exactly the desired logarithmic conformal tensor.
∎

Remark 6.14
.

Proposition
6.6
only proves the implication

(6.15)

\text{logarithmic conformal Cartan geometry}\Longrightarrow\text{log-conformal tensor}.

The converse is not established in this article. In particular, for a general tensor

g\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{2}\Omega_{X}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N\bigr)

We do not claim that the ordinary conformal Cartan geometry on
X\setminus\Delta
extends globally to a logarithmic Cartan connection on
(X,\Delta)
. The point of the present discussion is merely that, in the explicit cases singled out by the classification, the geometry admits a natural and concrete Cartan-geometric interpretation.

Remark 6.16
.

The three outputs of the non-nef classification may now be read as three manifestations of the same package, and the point here is only to reorganize the results already proved in Sections
2
–
4
.

(1) The homogeneous flat case.
When
\Delta=\varnothing
and
X\simeq Q^{n}
, one recovers the ordinary flat conformal Cartan geometry on the homogeneous space
G/P
of (
6.1
); see also Proposition
2.7
and Proposition
2.27
. In this case one may take
\mathcal{G}=G\to G/P
and
\omega_{\log}=\omega_{\mathrm{MC}}
, the Maurer–Cartan form. Proposition
6.6
then yields exactly the quadric tensor described explicitly in Section
2
.

(2) The affine-logarithmic projective case.
Proposition
2.12
already constructs the logarithmic tensor on
(\mathbb{P}^{n},H)
, together with the global logarithmic coframe (
2.13
), the local boundary expression (
2.19
), and the tangent-theoretic interpretation recorded in Remark
2.20
. On the affine chart
\mathbb{P}^{n}\setminus H\simeq\mathbb{A}^{n}
, Remark
2.18
shows that this tensor is simply the constant flat quadratic form. From the present Cartan-geometric perspective, the pair
(\mathbb{P}^{n},H)
should therefore be understood not as a second compact homogeneous model, but rather as the logarithmic compactification of the flat affine chart in an exact Weyl gauge; compare also the affine-logarithmic discussion in
[
4
, §4]
.

(3) The split-null case.
The third alternative in Theorem
4.27
is the even-dimensional case described geometrically in Theorem
4.17
; a concrete product model was already exhibited in Proposition
2.22
and Remark
2.23
. For the present interpretation, the essential point is that the relevant decomposition is of Witt type:

T_{X}(-\log\Delta)=A\oplus B,\qquad\rk A=\rk B=m,\qquad g|_{A}=0,\qquad g|_{B}=0.

The mixed part of
g
then induces a perfect pairing

(6.17)

A\otimes B\longrightarrow N,

or, equivalently, an isomorphism

A\xrightarrow{\sim}B^{\vee}\otimes N\qquad\text{or, symmetrically,}\qquad B\xrightarrow{\sim}A^{\vee}\otimes N.

This should not be understood as asserting that
g
vanishes on every pair of vectors arising from the splitting. Rather, each summand is isotropic in itself, whereas the mixed pairing between them is perfect. Fibrewise, if
u,u^{\prime}\in A_{x}
and
v,v^{\prime}\in B_{x}
, then

g_{x}(u,u^{\prime})=0,\qquad g_{x}(v,v^{\prime})=0,\qquad g_{x}(u,v)\ \text{may be nonzero},

and the induced map
A_{x}\to B_{x}^{\vee}\otimes N_{x}
is an isomorphism. Accordingly, in a local frame adapted to the splitting, the matrix of
g
has the block form

\begin{pmatrix}0&M\\
M^{t}&0\end{pmatrix},

with
M
invertible. After choosing a local trivialisation of
N
and local frames of
A
and
B
which are dual with respect to the pairing (
6.17
), one may normalise this matrix to

\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{m}\\
I_{m}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus the nondegeneracy of the tensor arises precisely from the fact that the two isotropic summands are mutually dual. This is exactly the mechanism already visible in the explicit model of Proposition
2.22
; see also the fibrational interpretation in Remark
2.23
. The split-null case should therefore be understood not as a new compact flat model, but as an internal reduction of the same conformal structure in even dimension, from
CO(2m,\mathbb{C})
to the subgroup preserving an ordered Witt decomposition.

7.
Boundary contractions and compact singular models

In the present setting, the only birational contractions that are relevant for the later discussion are those for which the boundary is contracted to codimension at least two. In that case, no divisorial boundary survives on the target, and the natural downstairs object is a reflexive conformal structure on a singular variety.
If
X
is a normal variety, we write
T_{X}
for its reflexive tangent sheaf. Equivalently, if

j:X_{\mathrm{reg}}\hookrightarrow X

denotes the inclusion of the smooth locus, then

T_{X}\simeq j_{*}T_{X_{\mathrm{reg}}}.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(\widetilde{X},\Delta)
be a smooth projective pair with simple normal crossings boundary, endowed with a logarithmic conformal structure

(7.2)

g^{\sharp}:T_{\widetilde{X}}(-\log\Delta)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{\widetilde{X}}^{1}(\log\Delta)\otimes N.

Let

\mu:\widetilde{X}\to Y

be a birational contraction onto a normal projective variety. Assume that
\mu
restricts to an isomorphism

\widetilde{X}\setminus\Delta\xrightarrow{\sim}Y\setminus\mu(\Delta)

and that

\operatorname{codim}_{Y}\mu(\Delta)\geq 2.

If the conformal tensor induced by (
7.2
) on the open set

U:=\widetilde{X}\setminus\Delta\simeq Y\setminus\mu(\Delta)

descends to
Y
, then the descended structure is necessarily reflexive. More precisely, there exists a line bundle
L
on
Y
and an isomorphism

(7.3)

q_{Y}^{\sharp}:T_{Y}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}\otimes L.

Proof.

Over
U
, the logarithmic conformal structure is an ordinary conformal structure. Therefore (
7.2
) restricts to an isomorphism

g^{\sharp}|_{U}:T_{U}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{U}^{1}\otimes N|_{U}.

Since the complement of
U
in
Y
has codimension at least two and
Y
is normal, any tensor on
U
descending to
Y
extends uniquely as a reflexive tensor on
Y
. The associated morphism therefore extends uniquely to a morphism

q_{Y}^{\sharp}:T_{Y}\to\Omega_{Y}^{[1]}\otimes L.

Since it is an isomorphism on the big open set
U
, it is an isomorphism of reflexive sheaves on all of
Y
. This yields (
7.3
).
∎

Proposition
7.1
shows that, once the image of the boundary has codimension at least two, the downstairs object is no longer logarithmic.

Remark 7.4
.

The basic smooth compact model corresponding to case (iii) of Theorem
4.27
was already exhibited in Proposition
2.22
. Indeed, if
A
is an abelian variety of dimension
r
and
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a hyperplane, then

(\widetilde{X},\Delta)=\bigl(A\times\mathbb{P}^{r},\;A\times H\bigr)

is a smooth projective simple normal crossing pair of dimension
2r
endowed with an everywhere nondegenerate logarithmic conformal tensor, and the natural projection to
A
has general fibre
\mathbb{P}^{r}
with hyperplane boundary. Thus this pair furnishes the basic smooth compact model for the third case of the classification theorem.

Example 7.5

(Generalized cones)
.

Let
Z
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
r
, let
\mathcal{M}
be an ample line bundle on
Z
, and consider the projective bundle

Y:=\mathbb{P}_{Z}\bigl(\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\oplus r}\bigr)

with projection

\pi:Y\to Z.

Let

E:=\mathbb{P}_{Z}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_{Z}^{\oplus r}\bigr)\subset Y.

Then every fibre of
\pi
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, and the restriction of
E
to each fibre is a hyperplane. Accordingly, the pair
(Y,E)
realises exactly the fibrewise geometry of case (iii).

For
m\gg 0
, the semiample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(1)
induces a birational contraction

e:Y\to X,

where
X
is the corresponding generalized cone, and
e
contracts
E
onto a copy of
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
.If
\dim Z=r
, then
\dim Y=2r
and

\operatorname{codim}_{X}e(E)=r+1\geq 2.

Hence the boundary disappears completely downstairs. Therefore, whenever
(Y,E)
carries a logarithmic conformal structure compatible with the fibration, Proposition
7.1
yields a reflexive conformal structure on the singular cone,

(7.6)

q_{X}^{\sharp}:T_{X}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\otimes L.

Accordingly, generalized cones should be regarded as compact singular manifestations of case (iii). The most natural such examples arise when
Z
is an abelian variety of dimension
r
, in view of Proposition
2.22
, which already exhibits the corresponding split-null geometry in the trivial product case.
