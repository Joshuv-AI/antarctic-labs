Factors associated with perceived healthcare access difficulties among older adults in Türkiye
Authors: Kübranur Çebi̇ Karaaslan, Hülya Di̇ğer, Seda Ağgül, Kanan Aslanov
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn Türkiye, the demand for healthcare services is rising quickly due to the country’s aging population, which calls for a review of the sustainability, equity, and accessibility of its healthcare system. The aging population brings a number of structural and individual problems in terms of access to healthcare services. The success of active aging policies, health outcomes, and the quality of life of the elderly are all significantly correlated with access to healthcare services. Therefore, examining the barriers to healthcare that older adults face will help develop more focused healthcare policies. With this motivation, the present study aims to offer an analytical view point on factors associated with older adults’ access to healthcare and is the first to use a micro-dataset of older adults representative of Türkiye.MethodsThe Turkish Statistical Institute’s 2023 Türkiye Older Adults Profile Survey micro-dataset was used in this study. Given the dichotomous nature of the dependent variable (perceived difficulty in accessing healthcare services), a binary logit model was employed to identify factors associated with healthcare access difficulties among older adults. This approach is widely used in empirical studies analyzing healthcare access using survey-based data.ResultsThe findings indicate pronounced socioeconomic, health-related, and organizational disparities in healthcare access among older adults. Older adults are more likely to have limited access to healthcare services if they receive an old-age pension, are able to prepare meals on their own, have partial or severe vision problems, complete difficulty walking, trouble with pedestrian transportation, difficulty obtaining medications, communicating with medical staff (such as doctors, nurses, or clerks), scheduling appointments, consulting the doctor of their choice, experience difficulties with waiting times, and encounter physical barriers within healthcare facilities (such as elevators, stairs, and stretchers). On the other hand, regarding health status and functional capacity indicators, older adults who report good and moderate health are 24.1% and 14.8% less likely, respectively, to experience difficulties compared with those reporting poor health. Compared with those without any formal education, older adults who graduated from high school, and university are 31.3%, and 24.5% less likely, respectively, to experience difficulties in healthcare access. Older adults who do not live alone are 14.5% less likely to face access difficulties than those who live alone.ConclusionHealth policies developed by the Ministry of Health that take into account the factors associated with healthcare access among older adults are crucial to improving service usage in this sociodemographic. This study presents empirical evidence that can help the Ministry of Health build effective access initiatives for older adults. In this sense, health policies that focus on criteria such as health status and health literacy may help to reduce obstacles to healthcare access and promote a more equitable delivery of services.

Introduction

Globally, increased life expectancy at birth, along with improving health literacy and healthier lifestyles, has resulted in a growing population of older adults. This demographic transition has prompted healthcare systems to adjust to the requirements of the elderly. As a result, effective health initiatives and policies are being developed to ensure that older adults have access to high-quality healthcare services. In Türkiye, older adults are given various priorities in healthcare provision (for example, precedence in appointments and examinations), which improves their access to services.

Primary healthcare institutions are particularly responsible for facilitating older adults’ access to healthcare. Within this framework, family physicians in primary care are in charge of monitoring certain diseases, administering immunizations, providing home healthcare services, and referring patients to hospitals as necessary. Thus, primary healthcare facilities play an important role in meeting the healthcare needs of older adults. It is therefore expected that primary healthcare facilities should first and foremost be physically accessible.

In Türkiye, family health centers, classified as primary healthcare institutions, are located in accordance with specific regulations (e.g., the Regulation on Non-Sanitary Establishments, the Regulation on Spatial Plans, and the Environmental Impact Assessment Regulation) [1–3]. In this regard, Article 12, paragraph 2 of the Regulation on Spatial Plans stipulates that: “In zoning plans, children’s playgrounds, open neighborhood sports areas, family health centers, nurseries, kindergartens, and primary schools shall be planned within approximately 500 meters; middle schools within approximately 1.000 meters; and high schools within approximately 2.500 meters, considering walking distance within the service impact area” [2]. This regulation thus guides the spatial distribution of family health centers.

However, physical proximity is not the only factor associated with access to healthcare institutions. A variety of sociodemographic and economic factors might influence accessibility. In this context, the present study aims to provide insight into the factors that influence older adults’ access to healthcare institutions. Furthermore, as part of the Healthy Türkiye Century Program, the Ministry of Health has implemented incentives to encourage the public to use primary healthcare facilities, as well as measures to monitor older adults in these institutions. These endeavors constitute the main motivation behind this study. The aim of this study is to identify the factors associated with perceived difficulties in accessing healthcare services among older adults in Türkiye. Accordingly, this study addresses the following research question: Which sociodemographic and economic characteristics, health status and functional capacity indicators, and healthcare service usage and organizational experience factors are associated with perceived difficulties in accessing healthcare institutions/facilities or hospitals among older adults in Türkiye? In line with this aim, the study examines how these factors shape the likelihood of reporting access difficulty, thereby providing an analytical assessment of factors associated with perceived healthcare access difficulties rather than a descriptive prevalence estimate.

No study in literature at the national level has been found with the same objective to date. Furthermore, it is a study that is representative at the national level and is the first to examine the subject. Furthermore, the rise in life expectancy, combined with ongoing changes and transformations in health policies and systems aimed at older adults, highlights the lack of studies assessing the effectiveness of policies and their role in healthcare accessibility for older adults in Türkiye. These aspects underscore the originality of the present study.

Literature review and research gap

Spatial accessibility, care services, quality of life, chronic diseases, malnutrition, resource efficiency, health outcomes, healthcare demands, and subjective well-being are some of the themes explored in previous studies on older adults’ access to healthcare facilities. A thorough review of the literature reveals that accessibility has been studied using a variety of methodological approaches. In this regard, relevant studies have applied regression analysis [4–13], Mann-Whitney U tests [14], Kruskal-Wallis tests [14], correlation analysis [9, 14], data envelopment analysis [9], and ordinary least squares [12].

Almulhem (2023) investigated the factors and barriers related to mobile health adoption among older adults [15]. Jiang et al. (2023) examined the phased relationship between the development of sustainable care institutions for older adults and spatial environmental factors [4]. Qin et al. (2023) explored care services for older adults [5]. Yildirim et al. (2023) studied the relationship between the burden of diabetes and health-related quality of life among older adults with diabetes [14]. Alodhialah et al. (2024) evaluated pain severity and quality of life in older patients [6]. Döhring et al. (2024) investigated the quality of life of high-risk older adults in primary healthcare institutions [7]. Liang et al. (2024) analyzed the effects of chronic diseases on health-related quality of life among older adults [8]. Sheffer Hilel et al. (2024) examined family physicians’ knowledge and practices concerning malnutrition in older adults [16]. Bao et al. (2025) studied efficiency and equity in the allocation of care resources for older adults [9]. Di and Wang (2025) investigated the impact of access to care services on quality of life [10]. Fukuda et al. (2025) explored oral health-related quality of life among older adults [17]. Kostadinovic et al. (2025) examined sociodemographic factors associated with physical functioning in the older adults [11]. Liu et al. (2025) analyzed synergistic evaluation systems in smart care institutions for older adults [18]. Mora et al. (2025) studied the effects of influenza vaccination on health outcomes in older adults [19]. Szewczyczak et al. (2025) assessed the needs of older adults [20]. Wan and Di (2025) explored the factors affecting older adults’ demands for care services [13]. Wan et al. (2025) investigated the effect of care services for older adults on subjective well-being among them, with end-of-life stress serving as a mediating factor [12]. Studies on healthcare accessibility have been conducted in various countries, including China [4–6, 9–11, 13], Türkiye [14], Saudi Arabia [6] and Serbia [11].

According to a review of the literature, no study has investigated whether health policies aiming to improve healthcare processes for older adults at the national level have achieved their intended goals. Furthermore, at the national level there are no studies that identify the key variables that significantly impact accessibility, or that explore how these variables affect, guide, and shape healthcare processes. Therefore, this study is significant in filling these gaps in the literature, providing potential time and cost savings, and contributing to positive health outcomes among older adults. Identifying the factors associated with difficulties in accessing healthcare institutions may enable policymakers and system designers to develop practical recommendations, offer a transparent perspective on healthcare accessibility, and evaluate the functionality of health policies implemented in primary healthcare institutions specifically targeting older adults. Accordingly, this study examines the factors associated with perceived healthcare access difficulties among older adults in Türkiye. The scope of the study also includes identifying the extent to which sociodemographic and economic factors play a decisive role in the emergence of these difficulties and how they do so.

Methods

Data source

The dataset used in this study is the 2023 Türkiye Older Adults Profile Survey micro-dataset, provided by the Turkish Statistical Institute (TurkStat). The aim of the Türkiye Elderly Profile Research is to obtain concrete data to serve as a basis for developing elderly policies in Türkiye, to compile existing data, and to derive indicators of elderly welfare from the research results. The Türkiye Elderly Profile Research was conducted under a bilateral protocol signed on May 10, 2023, between the Turkish Statistical Institute and the General Directorate of Services for the Disabled and Elderly of the Ministry of Family and Social Services. Fieldwork for this research, the first of its kind in Türkiye, was carried out by Turkstat across Türkiye between October 23 and December 18, 2023. The sample size was calculated to produce estimates based on the total population of Türkiye and 12 regions according to the NUTS 1 level. Those defined as institutional population, such as those residing in university dormitories, nursing homes, orphanages, and prisons, as well as those residing in barracks and military residences, were not included in the research. The sampling method of the study was multi-stage cluster sampling [21]. Individuals aged 65 years and over who participated in the survey were included in the analysis.

Variables

The dependent variable of the study is healthcare accessibility among older adults. It was measured using a self-reported survey item asking respondents. It was measured with the question: “Do you experience difficulties in accessing a healthcare institution/facility or hospital? (Yes; No; Not applicable)”. Responses marked as “Not applicable” were excluded from the analysis because they do not directly reflect perceived difficulty in accessing healthcare services within the binary structure of the dependent variable. Responses were coded as 1 = yes and 0 = no [22]. As the item is formulated broadly in the survey, it reflects an overall perceived access barrier rather than a specific dimension. This measure captures individuals’ perceived difficulties in accessing healthcare services. It reflects the respondents’ subjective evaluation of barriers they may face when attempting to reach healthcare institutions, including hospitals and other healthcare facilities. While the survey question does not distinguish between different types of access barriers, it provides an overall indicator of perceived healthcare access difficulties among older adults. Because the survey item is formulated broadly, it aggregates several potential barriers to healthcare access into a single outcome variable. These barriers may include physical constraints (e.g., distance to healthcare facilities), organizational barriers (e.g., appointment availability or waiting times), logistical issues such as transportation difficulties, or communication and informational barriers. Therefore, the variable should be interpreted as an indicator of overall perceived difficulty in accessing healthcare services, rather than a measure of specific dimensions of healthcare accessibility.

Descriptions of the independent variables included in the study are presented in Table 1. The classification of independent variables in this study was guided by Andersen’s Behavioral Model of Health Services Use, which conceptualizes healthcare access as a function of predisposing, enabling, and need-related factors [23]. The Andersen Behavioral Model was developed by Ronald Andersen in 1968 [24]. With this model, Ronald Andersen introduced a conceptual and functional dimension to access to health care. Within the model, the issue of access to healthcare is evaluated under two headings: potential access and perceived access [23]. The Andersen Behavioral Model has undergone various updates over time.

Accordingly, the variables were grouped into three main domains: sociodemographic and economic characteristics, health status and functional capacity, and healthcare service usage and organizational experiences. As the analysis is based on secondary survey data, the selection and grouping of variables are necessarily limited to those available in the dataset.

Some independent variables included in the analysis reflect specific experiences encountered when interacting with healthcare services (e.g., obtaining medication, scheduling appointments, or communicating with healthcare personnel). While these factors may also be considered components of healthcare accessibility, they were included in the analysis to capture organizational and service-related experiences that may shape individuals’ overall perception of access difficulty.

Empirical strategy

Microsoft Excel was used to organize and prepare the data obtained from the Turkish Statistical Institute for analysis, and Stata 16 was employed for statistical analyses. After obtaining descriptive statistics from the dataset, a binary logit model, one of the discrete choice models, was applied due to the dichotomous (binary) structure of the dependent variable [26]. Estimates were obtained taking into account the sample weights provided by the Turkish Statistical Institute [33]. In this way, sociodemographic and economic characteristics, health status and functional capacity, as well as healthcare utilization experiences that may affect the difficulties older adults face in accessing healthcare services, were examined in detail.

Binary logit model estimation and marginal effect estimation results

The overall model was found to be statistically significant (P < 0.0001), and all independent variables in the model had VIF values below 5. Due to the structure of discrete choice models, the quantitative interpretation of the coefficients is conducted through marginal effects. Marginal effects are reported and interpreted using the 1%, 5%, and 10% significance thresholds.

In terms of sociodemographic and economic characteristics, it was found that gender and marital status had no statistically significant effect on the likelihood of difficulty accessing healthcare. Older adults who have completed high school, and university were found to be 31.3%, and 24.5% less likely to have barriers to healthcare access than those who have not completed any formal education. It was observed that older adults who did not live alone were 14.5% less likely to have accessibility issues than those who lived alone. Those who stated that their expenditures had increased or stayed the same over the past three years were 17.3% and 13.7% less likely, respectively, to face difficulties than those who reported decreased expenditures. Older adults who received an old-age pension were found to be 8.9% more likely to face difficulties than those who did not.

In terms of health status and functional capacity indicators, older adults who reported good and moderate health were found to be 24.1% and 14.8% less likely to have difficulties accessing healthcare, respectively, than those who reported poor health. It was determined that older adults who had severe difficulty walking or were unable to walk were 17.5% and 23.1% more likely to face access difficulties than those who did not have such problems. Similarly, older persons who had severe vision problems and no vision were 26.2% and 50.2% more likely to suffer difficulties than those who did not have visual impairments. Those who had challenges with pedestrian transportation were 46.6% more likely to have access issues than those who did not. Older adults who were able to prepare their own meals were 19.4% more likely to experience difficulties than those who could not. Additionally, older adults who did not require home care were found to be 22% less likely to face challenges than those who did. Although the findings identified in the study generally support expectations, the higher likelihood of difficulties encountered by elderly individuals who can prepare their own meals is an unexpected result. However, this situation is largely based on assumptions. It is also thought that this relationship may stem from unobservable variables (rural settlement, selective use of health services, heterogeneous structure, care arrangements, etc.).

In terms of healthcare service usage and organizational experiences indicators, older adults who reported difficulty obtaining medication were found to be 43.3% more likely to difficulties in healthcare access than those who did not. Those who experienced communication challenges with healthcare workers, such as doctors, nurses, and clerks, were 88.2% more likely to experience problems. Older adults who reported difficulty scheduling appointments were 35% more likely to experience issues than those who did not. Those who had difficulty consulting their preferred physician were 15.1% more likely to have problems. Similarly, those who reported issues with wait times were 29% more likely to experience difficulties. Finally, older adults who reported physical barriers in healthcare facilities, such as elevators, stairs, or stretchers, were 29% more likely to experience access difficulties than those without such issues.

Discussion and limitations

The present study’s research question and implementation were based on health policies and efforts targeted at improving older adults’ health in Türkiye. The rise in life expectancy at birth and the aging of the population have forced changes to the healthcare system to accommodate the unique demands of older adults. In this context, policies such as monitoring older adults’ health status in primary healthcare institutions, effectively managing the healthcare process, providing home healthcare services, and ensuring various hospital priorities are intended to improve older adults’ access to health care. Furthermore, studies and initiatives aimed at ensuring equitable access to healthcare have been underway for many years, with justice and fairness serving as essential guiding concepts. In particular, the World Health Organization’s “Health for All by the Year 2000” target has played an important role in emphasizing financial accessibility.

The Turkish health system, which aims to provide universally accessible healthcare services, designs health programs for disadvantaged groups (such as older adults, disabled people, and children) based on their unique characteristics. In this context, the role of family physicians in monitoring and guiding older adults through healthcare processes is especially important. Understanding the factors associated with healthcare access among older adults, who are the target of personalized health policies, was deemed critical for offering a clear perspective to health policy and system makers, prompting the implementation of this study. The research evaluated the difficulties older adults face in accessing healthcare institutions based on sociodemographic indicators, health status, daily life indicators, and healthcare utilization experiences.

Sociodemographic & economic characteristics

Accordingly, it has been determined that educated older adults are less likely to experience difficulties accessing healthcare services compared to uneducated older adults. This finding may be related to individuals’ ability to manage and navigate healthcare processes. Many stages of healthcare services, particularly through the Central Physician Appointment System (MHRS), are now conducted online. Moreover, literacy is crucial for understanding hospital signage and completing administrative procedures. Education is expected to positively influence accessibility, and the study’s findings support this expectation. It is therefore assumed that older adults receiving such pensions are classified as needy and manage their expenditures accordingly. In addition, although primary healthcare services are provided free of charge in Türkiye, and various financial facilities are available, the finding that older adults still experience difficulties in access may be associated with their level of health literacy. Indeed, according to the “Türkiye Health Literacy and Related Factors Research” conducted in 2024 by the General Directorate of Health Promotion under the Ministry of Health, 52.2% of individuals aged 65 and over have inadequate health literacy levels [28]. The finding obtained in this study may be related to these factors. Similar results have also been reported in the literature [11].

Older adults who did not live alone were less likely to have difficulty accessing healthcare than those did. Older adults’ healthcare requirements can be frequent and unpredictable and home healthcare services play an important role in addressing them. However, living alone may make it difficult for people to report their health problems or get help when they need it. This study’s findings support this view, stressing those older adults living alone may struggle to communicate their healthcare needs, which can have a substantial impact on their accessibility. This finding is also supported by the related literature [7].

Older adults who reported increased expenditures over the previous three years were found to be less likely to have difficulty accessing healthcare than those who reported decreased expenditures. This finding may be associated with individuals’ increased health-related spending. Although the General Health Insurance system in Türkiye reduces out-of-pocket expenses, some co-payments for medical consultations and additional charges for prescriptions remain. Furthermore, alternative medicine products may play an important part in how individuals manage their healthcare procedures. Consequently, increased expenses by providing the opportunity people to become more health-conscious, influencing their access to healthcare. The findings of the study support this interpretation.

Older adults who received old-age pensions were found to be more likely to experience difficulties in accessing healthcare compared to those who did not. This finding is thought to be related to the characteristics of receiving an old-age pension. In this context, under the “Law on Granting Pensions to Needy, Weak, and Lonely Turkish Citizens Over the Age of 65,” implemented in 2016, old-age pensions are provided to older individuals in Türkiye. According to the law, Turkish citizens aged 65 and over who are determined by the Social Assistance and Solidarity Foundations to be in need are entitled to receive this pension for as long as their condition of need continues [29]. The health status of elderly individuals in Türkiye is monitored by family physicians, and there is no co-payment in the financing of primary healthcare services. Although there are no barriers to access in terms of finances or physician follow-up, it is thought that the finding that elderly individuals experience difficulties in accessing services may be due to their lack of sufficient knowledge about the aforementioned health policies. Indeed, according to the “Türkiye Health Literacy and Related Factors Research” conducted in 2024 by the General Directorate of Health Promotion under the Ministry of Health, 52.2% of individuals aged 65 and over have inadequate health literacy levels [28]. Therefore, the finding obtained in this study may be related to these factors.

Health status & functional capacity

Undoubtedly, an individual’s health status has a significant impact on their access to healthcare services. In this sense, whether older adults are in good or poor health may influence how they manage their healthcare needs and access medical facilities. When evaluating findings related to characteristics that can be examined in this context, it was discovered that older adults in good or moderate health were less likely to face difficulty in accessing healthcare than those in poor health. Similarly, older adults who had trouble walking or were unable to walk were more likely to face accessibility challenges than those who did not. Older adults with severe vision problems were more likely to have accessibility issues than those without visual impairments. It was found that those who had trouble walking outdoors were more likely to have accessibility issues than those who did not. Furthermore, older adults who were able to prepare their own meals were found to be more likely to have difficulty accessing healthcare than those who were not. The findings of the study indicate that certain aspects of the health policies developed by the Turkish Ministry of Health for individuals aged 65 and over are consistent with the existing literature and practices. In this regard, the idea that individuals may have difficulty accessing healthcare, as well as the active tasks assigned to family physicians in addressing these obstacles, underline once more the determining role of these actors in the healthcare process. Furthermore, the findings of the present study agree with those reported in the literature [5].

It was found that older adults who did not require home care were less likely to experience difficulties in healthcare access compared to those who did. It is thought that this finding might be related to policies concerning healthcare and home care services. Although individuals in need of home care face difficulties in accessing healthcare institutions, the Ministry’s home healthcare services allow care to be provided within individuals’ own homes. In this respect, home care services were first introduced in Türkiye in 1993 in Ankara, Istanbul, Izmir, and Adana. By 2004, home healthcare services had been expanded to all public hospitals [30]. Within this framework, home care services play an active role in providing healthcare to those in need; however, given the aging population and the inclusion of other disadvantaged groups (such as individuals with disabilities or patients undergoing continuous treatment), certain delays in service provision may occur. The findings of this study support this assumption.

Older adults who experienced difficulties in obtaining medication were found to be more likely to face challenges in healthcare access compared to those who did not. This finding may be related to the financial distress experienced by individuals, as previously noted. Although there are additional payments required for certain medications, healthcare services for individuals whose monthly income is less than one-third of the minimum wage are financed by the state under the General Health Insurance system. Despite the financial accessibility ensured within Türkiye’s healthcare system, older adults’ difficulties in obtaining medication can be explained by their inadequate levels of health literacy, a finding further supported by both previous studies on national health literacy and the present study’s findings.

Healthcare service usage & organizational experiences

It was observed that older adults who experienced difficulties communicating with healthcare professionals, such as doctors, nurses, or secretaries, were more likely to have healthcare accessibility issues compared to those who did not. The foundation of the healthcare process lies in effectively communicating one’s health needs. In this regard, healthcare services differ from other service types, as they are primarily defined by the provider (the physician) rather than the recipient (the patient). Effective communication in healthcare can therefore have both positive and negative effects on health outcomes. Moreover, factors such as sociodemographic and economic characteristics may play a decisive role in how individuals communicate their healthcare needs. The findings of the present study support these considerations.

Individuals aged 65 and over are entitled to certain priorities within the healthcare process in Türkiye. These priorities encompass every stage of healthcare services, including appointments, waiting times for consultations, and the conduct of necessary tests and examinations. Accordingly, it may be expected that older adults would experience a smoother and more efficient service process. However, older patients who are unaware of these priorities, or who lack a relative to assist or guide them in this regard, may in fact face various challenges during the consultation process. Within the variables evaluated in this study, it was found that older adults who experienced difficulties in making appointments, consulting their preferred physician, or while waiting in line were more likely to face difficulties in healthcare access compared to those who did not undergo such difficulties. Therefore, the study highlights once again the importance of informing older adults about health policies designed specifically for them. Otherwise, these policies may fail to achieve their intended goals, and older adults may struggle or even be unable to manage the healthcare process effectively. The findings of this study are consistent with those reported in the related literature [4].

Older adults who experienced difficulties with the physical features of healthcare institutions, such as elevators, stairs, or stretchers, were found to be more likely to face challenges in healthcare access compared to those who did not. These difficulties may likewise be associated with older adults’ level of awareness. Each hospital employs staff responsible for welcoming, informing, and guiding patients, and, when necessary, accompanying them to the appropriate units. The difficulties older adults encounter with physical infrastructure within healthcare facilities once again emphasize the importance of their knowledge and awareness levels. Previous studies in the literature support this finding [4].

It is also important to acknowledge certain measurement limitations regarding the dependent variable. The dependent variable does not define the type of “difficulty” (e.g., physical, bureaucratic, transportation-related, or financial). Therefore, respondents may have interpreted the item differently, which may introduce heterogeneity in the measurement of perceived access difficulties. In addition, the survey question did not specify a time frame for these difficulties, which may further contribute to variation in respondents’ interpretations.

Healthcare accessibility in this study was measured using a single self-reported item capturing perceived difficulty in accessing healthcare services. As such, the measure may encompass a broad range of potential barriers, including physical distance to health facilities, transportation problems, financial constraints, organizational issues such as waiting times, or informational barriers. However, the dataset does not allow these dimensions of healthcare access to be distinguished separately. Furthermore, because the variable is perception-based, responses may reflect subjective evaluations and personal experiences rather than objective measures of healthcare accessibility. Despite these limitations, perception-based indicators are widely used in population surveys and provide valuable insights into how individuals experience and interpret barriers to healthcare access.

Another important limitation concerns the conceptual proximity between the dependent variable and some explanatory variables included in the analysis, particularly those related to healthcare service usage and organizational experiences (e.g., medication procurement, appointment scheduling, communication with healthcare personnel, waiting times, and physical conditions of healthcare institutions). These variables may partly reflect dimensions of healthcare accessibility itself rather than entirely external determinants. Although they were included to capture organizational and experiential factors shaping individuals’ perceptions of healthcare access, this conceptual overlap may affect the interpretation of the findings. Therefore, the results should be interpreted as associations related to perceived healthcare access difficulties rather than strictly causal determinants. In addition, excluding “Not applicable” responses may introduce potential bias if these responses are systematically associated with characteristics such as health status or healthcare utilization.

Conclusion

In Türkiye, the healthcare processes of older adults are generally managed within primary healthcare institutions, where routine check-ups for these patients are also included among the monitored conditions. Accordingly, health policies tailored specifically for older adults are designed to facilitate their healthcare experiences, and the health system is adapted to meet their needs. However, the health problems they experience, and the requirements involved in managing and navigating healthcare processes may lead them to encounter certain challenges. In this context, it was considered that identifying the key factors association with older adults’ difficulties in accessing healthcare could help lay the groundwork for improved health outcomes, and the study was carried out with this aim. This study aimed to identify the associated with perceived healthcare access difficulties among older adults in Türkiye. The scope of the study also includes identifying the extent to which sociodemographic and economic factors play a decisive role in the emergence of these difficulties and how they do so.

The present study utilized data from the Turkish Statistical Institute’s Türkiye Older Adults Profile Survey. The findings of the study are largely consistent with those in the related literature. According to the findings, sociodemographic and economics characteristics, health status and functional capacity, and healthcare use experiences play an important role in the difficulties older adults face in accessing healthcare institutions.

In terms of sociodemographic indicators, gender and marital status were found to have no statistically significant effect on the likelihood of experiencing difficulties in healthcare access. Older adults who have received any education are less likely to experience difficulties accessing healthcare services compared to those who have not received any education. It was observed that older adults who did not live alone were less likely to experience difficulties compared to those who did. Older adults who reported increased expenditures over the past three years were less likely to experience difficulties in accessing healthcare compared to those whose expenditures decreased. Older adults who received old-age pensions were more likely to face difficulties compared to those who did not. These findings once again emphasize the importance of knowledge and awareness in ensuring full accessibility to healthcare institutions. In this regard, the study supports the view that health policies and initiatives designed to facilitate the healthcare process for older adults can only be functional when they or their relatives are adequately informed about them. Therefore, it is thought that future initiatives in this direction can enhance the effectiveness of health policies.

In terms of health status and functional capacity indicators, older adults in good or moderate health were found to be less likely to experience difficulties in healthcare access compared to those in poor health. Older adults who had great difficulty walking or were unable to walk were more likely to experience access difficulties compared to those without mobility problems. Similarly, older adults who had some or severe problems with vision were found to be more likely to experience access challenges compared to those without. Those who had difficulty walking outdoors were more likely to experience difficulties compared to those who did not. Older adults who were able to prepare their own meals were more likely to experience difficulties compared to those who could not. Older adults who did not require home care were less likely to experience difficulties in accessing healthcare compared to those who did. The findings of the study highlight the necessity of home healthcare services and follow-up programs that take into account the health status of older adults. In this regard, the findings support the importance of the existing health initiatives and services in Türkiye that aim to facilitate older adults’ access to healthcare.

In terms of healthcare use experience indicators, older adults who experienced difficulties in obtaining medication were found to be more likely to face challenges in accessing healthcare compared to those who did not. Older adults who had communication issues with healthcare professionals such as doctors, nurses, or secretaries were more likely to face access challenges compared to those who did not. Similarly, older adults who experienced difficulties in making appointments, consulting their doctor of choice, or while waiting in line were more likely to experience difficulties compared to those who did not. Older adults who faced difficulties with the physical features of healthcare institutions, such as elevators, stairs, or stretchers, were also found to be more likely to face challenges in healthcare access compared to those who did not. Despite these findings, policy frameworks aimed at improving geographical, economic, and institutional access to healthcare services exist in Türkiye. However, the impact of these policies on accessibility may vary at the implementation level. The finding that these policies may not always achieve their intended outcomes is thought to be related to the low level of health literacy, as supported by the Türkiye Health Literacy Research. It is believed that improvements in health literacy through targeted interventions could lead to significant progress in this area.
