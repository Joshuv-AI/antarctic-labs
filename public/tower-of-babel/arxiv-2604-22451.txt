Analytic spectral flow formula for unitaries and Levinson’s theorem
Angus Alexander, Alan Carey, Galina Levitina, Adam Rennie

Abstract
We prove an integral formula for the spectral flow of differentiable loops of unitaries of the form Id + {\rm Id}+ Schatten. Our formula is in terms of a regularised winding number, expressed in terms of exact differential forms, and we show how the formula extends to non-closed paths. Applying these ideas to the scattering operator of Schrödinger scattering systems yields explicit formulae for the number of bound states, possibly modified by the presence of resonances, of the system in terms of the potential. We finish by briefly considering the paths of unbounded operators obtained from unitary loops via the Cayley transform. These include cases of moving domain as well as paths with non-constant Hilbert space.

Introduction

Starting from the invention by Lusztig, the notion of spectral flow played a significant role in geometry as demonstrated by Atiyah, Patodi and Singer
[
6
,
7
]
. Initially defined in topological terms as an intersection number, later an analytical definition was given by Phillips
[
42
,
43
]
. Exploiting an essential contribution of Getzler
[
25
]
, the papers
[
19
,
21
]
established spectral flow formulae as integrals of one-forms on affine subspaces of the Banach manifold of self-adjoint Fredholms.
In this paper we aim to establish such an integral formula for spectral flow of a path of unitaries which are Schatten
p
-class perturbations of
the identity,
p\geq 1
. We are motivated by scattering theory where a path of scattring matrices is a Schatten norm continuous path
S(\lambda)
of
d
-Schatten unitaries depending on the spectral variable.

The spectral flow along a path of unitaries is well-established. We utilise the definition and several features of known approaches to spectral flow for unitaries appearing in
[
8
,
15
,
16
,
23
,
35
]
. Other approaches, such as Pushnitski’s
[
45
]
, necessarily agree with ours but have quite different definitions.

For the spectral flow of a differentiable loop
U_{\bullet}
of unitaries which are trace-class perturbations of the identity, the following integral formula holds
[
16
,
23
,
35
]
:

\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(U_{t}^{*}\dot{U}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t,

(1.1)

showing that the spectral flow can be computed via the integral of an exact one form. A refinement of the formula (
1.1
) for Schatten class unitaries was proposed in
[
34
]
, and the formula suggested there is what we ultimately obtain, and now describe.

In this paper (see Theorem
2.9
below) we show that if
U_{\bullet}
is a Schatten differentiable loop of unitaries, such that
U_{t}-\Id
is in the Schatten
p
-class for some
p\geq 1
, then
for
r\geq(p-1)/2
or
n\geq p-1
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})&=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})|U_{t}-{\rm Id}|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})(U_{t}-{\rm Id})^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

(1.2)

Here the integrand in both integrals is an exact differential form on the Banach-Lie group of unitaries of the form
{\rm Id}+
Schatten. Notably, Equation (
1.2
) holds for any loop of unitaries indexed by any interval (bounded or not) on the real line. For example, if
U_{\bullet}
is a differentiable loop of unitaries indexed by
[0,\infty)
, such that
U_{s}-\Id
is in the Schatten
p
-class for some
p\geq 1
for any
s\in[0,\infty)
, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})&=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})|U_{s}-{\rm Id}|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})(U_{s}-{\rm Id})^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(1.3)

provided that the integrand defines an integrable function on
[0,\infty)
.

We also present an alternative formula for the spectral flow of unitaries in terms of regularised determinants in Theorem
2.13
, again first suggested in
[
34
]
. This states that for
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
U_{\bullet}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
a differentiable loop of
p
-Schatten unitaries we have

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}t.

We extend these spectral flow formulae to non-closed paths. The usual procedure, which for self-adjoint Fredholm operators yields eta invariants of the end-points, depends on the choice of paths from the identity to the endpoints. While this dependence can not in general be removed, we use the known cut-locus of the unitary groups to identify a convention when the end-points are finite rank perturbations of the identity. This convention is natural and produces known answers in examples, as we demonstrate in the following section.

By connecting the endpoints of a non-closed path to the identity via geodesic paths, we prove an analytic formula for the spectral flow along the path in terms of regularised winding number formula and end-point correction terms (see Section
2.5
).

In Section
3

we apply our spectral flow results to Schrödinger scattering systems. We formulate the celebrated theorem of Levinson in terms of spectral flow along the path of unitaries given by the scattering matrix. Levinson’s theorem relates the number of bound states of the Schrödinger operator
H=-\Delta+V
to the scattering matrix
S(\lambda)
of the pair
(-\Delta,-\Delta+V)
.

There have been many interpretations of Levinson’s theorem, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
39
]
. Notably, for each energy level
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
, the scattering matrix
S(\lambda)
is a unitary operator on an appropriate Hilbert space, so that
S(\bullet)
is a path of unitaries indexed by
[0,\infty)
. Furthermore, under suitable assumptions on
V
,
S(\lambda)
is a trace-class perturbation of the identity. However, the trace of
S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)
is usually not integrable on
[0,\infty)
, making it impossible to apply the spectral flow formula (
1.1
). However, as we show in Section
3
the spectral flow formulae (
1.3
), may be applied in this situation.

For nice potentials
V
, we show in all generality (see Theorem
3.15
below) that

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(S(\bullet))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{n}(0)=-N-N_{res},

(1.4)

where
N
is the number of eigenvalues of
H=-\Delta+V
and
N_{res}
is an integer correction arising in dimensions 2 and 4 only in the presence of zero energy resonances. In particular, (
1.4
) holds in all dimensions, whether resonances are present or not.
The polynomials
P_{n}(\lambda),
and
p_{n}(\lambda)
are explicitly defined in term of the potential
V
.

Given a loop of unitaries
U_{\bullet}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
we can apply the Cayley transform to this loop of unitaries, to obtain a path of unbounded operators. These paths, while well-behaved in many ways, have the disturbing feature that the operators at each point may fail to be densely-defined on
\mathcal{H}
. By completing the domains of definition, we obtain paths of self-adjoint operators on moving Hilbert spaces, where the standard definition of the spectral flow is not applicable. Nevertheless, we can define a sensible notion of spectral flow for such paths, via the unitary spectral flow.

Our results for paths of operators encompass known cases of gap continuous varying boundary conditions,
[
16
,
52
]
. To understand better what the abstract case of paths of self-adjoint operators on moving Hilbert spaces says, we formulate our results using Kasparov’s bivariant K-theory.

Kasparov’s stabilisation theorem
[
32
]
shows that any Hilbert module
X
over
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
consists of a continuous field of Hilbert spaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}
over
\mathbb{R}
, where each
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a closed subspace of a fixed Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Given an unbounded self-adjoint regular operator
T
on
X
, we characterise the continuity of the family
\{T_{t}\}
on the continuous field
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}
, and when
\{T_{t}\}
defines a Kasparov module. Our characterisation, found in Proposition
4.8
and Theorem
4.18
, is in terms of continuous paths in a Banach manifold arising from the Banach manifold
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
of unitaries of the form
{\rm Id}+
compact via the Cayley transform.

The discussion of exact forms and our formulae for spectral flow are in Section
2
. The application to scattering theory is in Section
3
, and the paths of unbounded operators are discussed in Section
4
.

2
Spectral flow for Schatten unitaries

Throughout the paper we denote by
\mathcal{H}
a separable Hilbert space and the algebra of all bounded (respectively, compact) linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
(respectively, by
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
). The standard trace on the algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is denoted by
\Tr
. For
p\geq 1
let
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})
be the Schatten
p
-class of compact operators
T
on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\Tr(|T|^{p})<\infty
.

Our aim is to study the spectral flow of moderately smooth paths
(U_{t})
of
\Id+
Schatten unitaries through the point
-1
in the spectrum. The issue has been addressed before, and a good introduction is provided in
[
23
]
.
We start with the definition of spectral flow for a path of self-adjoint (bounded) Fredholm operators due to Phillips
[
42
,
43
]
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})/\penalty\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
denote the quotient onto the Calkin algebra and let
\chi=\chi_{[0,\infty)}
be the characteristic function of the interval
[0,\infty)
.

Assume that
(T_{t}),t\in[0,1],
is any norm continuous path of bounded self-adjoint Fredholm operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
\pi(\chi(T_{t}))=\chi(\pi(T_{t}))
. Since the spectrum of the
\pi(T_{t})
are bounded away from zero, the path
\chi(\pi(T_{t}))
is continuous. By compactness (and
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
), we can choose a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\pi(\chi(T_{t}))-\pi(\chi(T_{s}))\right|\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}

for all
t,s\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
and
0\leq i\leq k
. Defining the projection
P_{i}=\chi(T_{t_{i}})
we find that
P_{i-1}P_{i}:P_{i}\mathcal{H}\to P_{i-1}\mathcal{H}
is Fredholm. The following is the definition due to Phillips
[
42
,
43
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
t\in[0,1]
let
(T_{t})
be any norm continuous path of bounded self-adjoint Fredholm operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. For a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}=1
of the interval
[0,1]
define the operators
P_{i}=\chi(T_{t_{i}})
. Then we define the
spectral flow
of the path
(T_{t})
by

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(T_{t})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\textup{Ind}(P_{i-1}P_{i})}.

This definition of spectral flow is independent of the choice of partition
[
38
,
42
,
43
]
and agrees with the topological definition used in
[
6
,
7
]
when both apply.

If
D_{t}
is a path of densely-defined self-adjoint operators with

t\mapsto F_{t}:=D_{t}(1+D_{t}^{2})^{-1/2}
operator norm continuous, then we say that the path
D_{t}
is Riesz continuous. If the path of graph projections
P_{D_{t}}
are norm continuous, we say that the path
D_{t}
is gap continuous.
Gap continuity is equivalent to norm resolvent continuity,
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Spectral flow for Riesz continuous paths of unbounded self-adjoint Fredholm operators
D_{t}
is defined as the spectral flow of the path of bounded transforms
F_{t}
using Phillips’ definition above.

In
[
16
, Section 2.1]
it is also shown that, via the Cayley transform, the spectral flow of a gap continuous path
\{D_{t}\}
of unbounded self-adjoint Fredholm operators can be defined as the winding number of the associated path of unitaries
t\mapsto(D_{t}+i)(D_{t}-i)^{-1}
. For a gap continuous path,
[
16
, Definition 2.12]
shows that Phillips’ definition still applies.

2.1
Spectral flow of unitaries

Now we move on to an analytical definition of spectral flow for a path of unitaries. We follow the discussions of
[
15
,
16
,
23
,
35
]
where the spectral flow of unitaries and the relation to spectral flow of closed unbounded Fredholm operators is presented. Pushnitski’s treatment,
[
45
]
, also deals with the Schatten class unitaries that we consider, but having different aims develops a quite different approach. In particular, Pushnitski deals with the discontinuity of integer-valued dimension-type functions on the circle by building a space of such functions, together with a natural covering space. By defining spectral flow in terms of lifts of maps to the covering space, Pushnitski obtains homotopy invariance.

Our approach will be closer to the “naive” definition that counts the net number of eigenvalues passing through
-1
as we traverse a loop of unitaries. This approach can be made rigorous by a Phillips-style definition presented in
[
15
]
and
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
, which is our starting point. We refer to
[
16
, Section 2]
and
[
35
]
for more discussion and additional references.

Our basic definitions will make sense for the “Fredholm unitaries”, see
[
16
, Corollary 1.8]
and
[
35
, Section 6.1]
, namely those Hilbert space unitaries for which
-1
is not contained in the essential spectrum. The Fredholm unitaries do not form a group, and its elements do not have sufficiently nice behaviour for us to define our differential forms.
It is shown in
[
35
, Lemma 6.1]
and
[
23
, Proposition 3.7.2]
that the unitaries of the form
{\rm Id}+K
with
K
compact are a deformation retract of the Fredholm unitaries.

To define one-forms on a Banach manifold of unitaries, we will require
p
-Schatten class compacts, which we introduce next. We note that these are again homotopy equivalent to the unitaries of the form
{\rm Id}+K
,
[
22
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p\geq 1
. We say a unitary
U
on
\mathcal{H}
is
p
-Schatten
if
U=\Id+T
for some
T\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})
. We say that a path
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto U_{t}
of
p
-Schatten unitaries is piecewise
differentiable
(or piecewise
C^{1}
) if the path is continuously differentiable at all but finitely many points
t\in(0,1)
in the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and is continuous on the whole interval
[0,1]
in the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
. We denote by
\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
the set of Schatten unitaries on
\mathcal{H}
with the topology coming from the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

For the remainder of this section we fix
p\geq 1
.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})

is a Banach-Lie group with Lie algebra the skew-adjoint subspace of
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})
,
[
22
]
. Similarly, the space
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
of unitaries of the form
{\rm Id}+X,\ X\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
with the norm topology is a Banach-Lie group.

Let
U_{t}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
be a continuous path. By
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
there exists a partition
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
of the interval and positive real
numbers
0<\epsilon_{j}<\pi
,
j=1,\dots,n
, such that
\ker(U_{t}-e^{i(\pi\pm\epsilon_{j})})=\{0\}
for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq t_{j}
.
Introduce the notation

k(t,\epsilon_{j}):=\sum_{0\leq\theta<\epsilon_{j}}\dim\ker(U_{t}-e^{i(\pi+\theta)}).

The sum defining
k(t,\epsilon_{j})
is necessarily finite.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U_{t}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
be a continuous path. For a finite partition
\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=1\}
of the interval and positive real
numbers
0<\epsilon_{j}<\pi
,
j=1,\dots,n
, such that
\ker(U_{t}-e^{i(\pi\pm\epsilon_{j})})=\{0\}
for
t_{j-1}\leq t\leq t_{j}
, define the
spectral flow
for
U_{\bullet}
by setting

\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})=\textup{wind}(U_{\bullet})=\sum^{n}_{j=1}k(t_{j},\epsilon_{j})-k(t_{j-1},\epsilon_{j}),

As shown in
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
this definition is independent of the choice of the partition of the interval and of the choice of the
\epsilon_{j}
.
In
[
16
,
23
,
35
]
, they recall the formula for the winding number on
\mathcal{U}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
. For a differentiable loop
U_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{U}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
based at
{\rm Id}_{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\textup{wind}(U_{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(U_{t}^{*}\dot{U}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathbb{Z}.

Our aim in this section is to extend this formula to all
\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
with
1\leq p<\infty
.

2.2
Exact one-forms

To prove the integral formula (
1.2
), we firstly introduce two different one-forms and show that they are exact. In the following, we fix the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and write
\mathcal{U}_{p}=\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
and similarly for Schatten ideals.

The tangent space
T_{\rm Id}\mathcal{U}_{p}
to the identity in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
is the skew-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Given two tangent vectors
X,Y\in T_{\rm Id}\mathcal{U}_{p}
the Lie bracket is given by
(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]
where
[X,Y]=XY-YX
is the standard commutator.
The exponential map
\exp:T_{\rm Id}\mathcal{U}_{p}\to\mathcal{U}_{p},X\mapsto\exp(X)
from the tangent space
T_{\rm Id}\mathcal{U}_{p}
, is onto and a local diffeomorphism,
[
22
, Proposition II.15]
.

The exponential map takes tangent vectors
X\in T_{\rm Id}\mathcal{U}_{p}
to curves through an arbitrary point
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
with tangent vector at
U
a translation of
X
to the point
U
, so

\exp(sX)\cdot U=Ue^{sX},\quad U\in\mathcal{U}_{p},\quad X=-X^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}.

Now we define the two one-forms on
the
tangent space to
\mathcal{U}_{p}
at the point
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}.

Definition 2.4
.

For
x\geq 0
let

C_{x}=\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(x+1/2)}.

Let
n
be an integer
n\geq p-1
and let
r
be real such that
r\geq(p-1)/2
. Define one-forms on
tangent vectors
X\in T_{U}\mathcal{U}_{p}
at the point
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
by

\alpha_{U,n}(X):=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Tr(X(U-\Id)^{n})

and

\beta_{U,r}(X):=-iC_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2r+1}\Tr(X|U-\Id|^{2r}).

Remark 2.5
.

We note that since
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
, the Hölder inequality implies that
(U-\Id)^{n}\in\mathcal{L}^{\frac{p}{n}}
and
|U-\Id|^{2r}\in\mathcal{L}^{\frac{p}{2r}}
. Since
X\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
and
n\in\mathbb{N},r\in\mathbb{R}
are chosen such that
n\geq p-1
and
r\geq(p-1)/2
, another application of the Hölder inequality implies that both operators under the trace in Definition
2.4
are trace-class operators, so that both one-forms are well-defined. In what follows, we will repeatedly use this argument without further reference.

In the following two lemmas we show that the forms
\alpha_{U,n}
and
\beta_{U,n}
are exact. We start with the one-form
\alpha_{U,n}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\geq p-1
the one-form
\alpha_{U,n}
is exact.

Proof.

Let
U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
and and let
Y=-Y^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
be such that
U_{1}=e^{Y}
. Setting
U_{t}=e^{tY}
.
t\in[0,1]
, we define the function
\Theta:\mathcal{U}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
by setting

\Theta(U_{1})=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y(U_{t}-\Id)^{n})\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.1)

By the Hölder inequality, the function
t\mapsto Y(U_{t}-\Id)^{n}
,
t\in[0,1]
, is a continuous trace-class valued function, and so the integral above is well-defined.

We claim that
d\Theta_{U_{1}}(X)=\alpha_{U_{1},n}(X)
for any
X\in T_{U_{1}}\mathcal{U}_{p}
.
We firstly note that since
Y=U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}U_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}
, we can rewrite the definition of
\Theta
as follows:

\Theta(U_{1})=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\left(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}U_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t}-\Id)^{n}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t.

To compute differentials at the point
U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
, we first compute that

\Theta(U_{1}e^{sX})=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(e^{-(t-1)Y}e^{-sX}U_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y})}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Noting that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}=U_{1}Xe^{(t-1)Y}=U_{1}Xe^{-Y}U_{0}^{*}U_{0}e^{tY}=U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}

and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}e^{-(t-1)Y}e^{-sX}U_{1}^{*}=-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}

we infer that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}e^{-(t-1)Y}e^{-sX}U_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y})}{\mathrm{d}t}=U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}U_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}U_{t}}{\mathrm{d}t}=0.

Since, in addition, we have that

e^{-(t-1)Y}e^{-sX}U_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y})}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{s=0}=Y,

we obtain that

(-1)^{n}2\pi i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Theta(U_{1}e^{sX})=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(Y\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Writing

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}

\displaystyle(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-1)^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(U_{1}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id)^{k}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}(U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id)\Big)(U_{1}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id)^{n-k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}(U_{t}-\Id)^{n-k-1},

we find that

(-1)^{n}2\pi i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Theta(U_{1}e^{sX})=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(Y\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}(U_{t}-\Id)^{n-k-1}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using repeatedly the cyclicity of the trace and unitarity of
U_{t}
we conclude that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}2\pi i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Theta(U_{1}e^{sX})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{t}Y(U_{t}-\Id)^{n-k-1}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(U_{t}-\Id)^{n}\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\Tr(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}(U_{1}-\Id)^{n})-\Tr(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}(U_{0}-\Id)^{n}))

\displaystyle=\Tr(X(U_{1}-\Id)^{n})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}2\pi i\,\alpha_{U_{1},n}(X),

proving the claim that
d\Theta_{U_{1}}(X)=\alpha_{U_{1},n}(X)
, and thus showing that the one-form
\alpha_{U,n}
is exact.
∎

Next we examine the one-forms
\beta_{U,r}
with
r
real. The proof of exactness for these forms requires the spectral theorem to handle non-integer powers.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
such that
r\geq(p-1)/2
, the one-form
\beta_{U,r}
on
\mathcal{U}_{p}
is exact.

Proof.

Let
n
be the integer part of
r
so
r=n+x
with
0\leq x<1
. For
U_{t}=e^{tY}
,
t\in[0,1]
, define
\Xi:\mathcal{U}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}
at the point
U_{1}
by

\Xi(U_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2r})\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2x})\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.2)

By the Hölder inequality, the function
t\mapsto Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2r}
,
t\in[0,1]
, is a continuous trace-class valued function, and so the integral above is well-defined.

Now, for
x\neq 0
, by the integral formula (see e.g.
[
41
, 1.3.7]
)

T^{-\alpha}=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\alpha}(1+\lambda T)^{-1}T\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,\quad 0<\alpha<1,0\leq T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),

we can write

|U_{t}-\Id|^{2x}=\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(2.3)

As the integral is absolutely convergent in operator norm with both the integrand and the value of the integral
|U_{t}-\Id|^{2x}\in\mathcal{L}^{p/2x}
and
Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2x}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
, we may interchange the trace and integral to find that

\Xi(U_{1})=\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t.

We now compute the exterior derivative of
\Xi
at
U_{1}
. As before, for
X\in T_{U_{1}}\mathcal{U}_{p}
we have

\mathrm{d}\Xi_{U_{1}}(X)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Xi(U_{1}e^{sX}).

We recall that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}|U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}(2-U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-e^{-(t-1)Y}e^{-sX}U_{1}^{*})

\displaystyle=-(U_{1}Xe^{(t-1)Y}-e^{-(t-1)Y}XU_{1}^{*})=-(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}),

and inspection shows that the derivative lies in
\mathcal{L}^{p}
.
The remaining elements needed to compute the differential are then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}|U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id|^{2n}

\displaystyle\qquad=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2k}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*})|U_{t}-\Id|^{2(n-k-1)}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}(1+\lambda|U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{1}{s}(1+\lambda|U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}\Big(\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2}-\lambda|U_{1}e^{sX}e^{(t-1)Y}-\Id|^{2}\Big)(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\lambda(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}\big(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Xi_{U_{1}}(X)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Xi(U_{1}e^{sX})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\Bigg(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}))

\displaystyle-Y\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2k}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*})|U_{t}-\Id|^{2(n-k-1)}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})

\displaystyle+Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n}\lambda(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}\big(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})\Bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t.

As
U_{t}
is normal and
YU_{t}=U_{t}Y
, we can rearrange these expressions using cyclicity of the trace to find

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Xi_{U_{1}}(X)=-\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2x-2}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-n\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2x-2}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x+1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-2}\big(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t.

For the last term, we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}=\lambda(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-2}(U_{t}Y-YU_{t}^{*})

so

\displaystyle\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x+1}\Tr\big(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-2}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\big(|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big((1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}\big)U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t.

Integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2}(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}\Big)U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2}\Big)(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\big(|U_{1}-\Id|^{2n+2}(1+\lambda|U_{1}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle+i\frac{\sin(x\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-x}\Tr\Big(\big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|U_{t}-\Id|^{2k}(U_{t}Y-U_{t}^{*}Y)|U_{t}-\Id|^{2(n-k)}\big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times(1+\lambda|U_{t}-\Id|^{2})^{-1}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\Tr(X|U_{1}-\Id|^{2r})+(n+1)\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\big((U_{t}Y-U_{t}^{*}Y)|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2x-2}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\Tr\big(X|U_{1}-\Id|^{2r})+(n+1)\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(Y|U_{t}-\Id|^{2n+2x-2}(U_{1}XU_{1}^{*}U_{t}-U_{t}^{*}U_{1}XU_{1}^{*})\big)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Combining our calculations now shows that

\mathrm{d}\Xi_{U_{1}}(X)=\Tr(X|U_{1}-\Id|^{2r})

and so normalising by
-i(1/2)^{2r+1}C_{r}
we see that
\beta_{r}
is exact.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

In general the functions
\Theta,\Xi
depend on the choice of path
e^{tY}
joining a unitary
U=e^{Y}
to the identity. In turn the choice of path depends on the choice of
Y
such that
U=e^{Y}
. Near the identity, there is a unique such
Y
, but in general there will exist multiple choices. Nevertheless, the differentials
\alpha,\beta
of
\Theta,\Xi
are independent of this choice. We return to this issue in subsection
2.5
.

2.3
Integral formula for the spectral flow of loops of unitaries

Having established that the one-forms
\alpha_{U,n}
and
\beta_{U,r}
are exact for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we will now show that the spectral flow for a path of unitaries, as defined in Definition
2.3
, can be written as an integral of these exact one-forms.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
p\geq 1
and let
(0,1)\ni t\mapsto U_{t}
be a Schatten differentiable path in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
such that
U_{0}=U_{1}=\Id
. Then for
r\geq(p-1)/2
or
n\geq p-1
we have

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})|U_{t}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})(U_{t}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t,

whenever the integrals converge.

Proof.

We will start with the first equality. To this end fix
r\geq(p-1)/2
and on the Banach manifold
C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{U}_{p})
of (piecewise)
C^{1}
-loops in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
introduce the function
B_{r}
by setting

B_{r}(U_{\bullet})=-i\frac{1}{2^{2r+1}}C_{r}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})|U_{t}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\beta_{r}\left(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})\right)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto U_{t,\bullet}
is a differentiable path in
C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{U}_{p})
.
Note that the chain rule gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=s_{0}}B_{r}(U_{s,\bullet})=dB_{r}\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=s_{0}}U_{s,\bullet}\Big).

Computing the differential at
X\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
yields

\mathrm{d}B_{r,U_{s_{0},\bullet}}(X)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\beta_{r}(U_{s_{0},t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{s_{0},t}),X)\,\mathrm{d}t=0

since
\beta_{r}
is closed by Lemma
2.7
. Hence
B_{r}
is locally constant and so homotopy invariant. Furthermore, the function
B_{r}
satisfies concatenation by construction.

To see that
B_{r}
is integer-valued, let
P
be a rank
k,k\in\mathbb{N},
projection on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and define the loop

U_{t}^{(k)}={\rm Id}-P+Pe^{i2\pi t},t\in[0,1].

(2.4)

As
k
eigenvalues go anti-clockwise through
-1
on this path, the spectral flow for this loop is
k
. To compute
B_{r}
for this loop we note that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}U_{t}^{(k)}=i2\pi Pe^{i\pi t}
and
U_{t}^{(k)}-\Id=P(-1+e^{i2\pi t})
. Hence, we can compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}

\displaystyle\Tr\Big((U_{t}^{(k)})^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t}^{(k)})|U_{t}^{(k)}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\big(2\pi iP|-1+e^{i2\pi t}|^{2r}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=2\pi ik\int_{0}^{1}|-1+e^{i2\pi t}|^{2r}\,\mathrm{d}t=ik\pi 2^{2r+1}\int_{0}^{1}\sin^{2r}(t\pi)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=ki2^{2r+1}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}{\Gamma(r+1)},

where the last line follows from a standard integral. Recalling the definition of the constant
C_{r}
(see Definition
2.4
), we infer that

B_{r}(U_{t}^{(k)})=k=\textup{sf}(U_{t}^{(k)}).

(2.5)

Now
\mathcal{U}_{p}
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathcal{U}_{1}
, and each class in
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is represented by one of the paths
U_{t}^{(k)}
or its reverse path. So for any loop
V_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{U}_{p}
we have a homotopy
V_{s,\bullet}
with
V_{0,\bullet}=V_{\bullet}
and
V_{1,\bullet}=U^{(k)}_{\bullet}
. By the homotopy invariance of
B_{r}
and the computation above

B_{r}(V_{0,\bullet})=B_{r}(V_{1,\bullet})=k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Referring to the normalisation check in (
2.5
), we see that
B_{r}=\textup{sf}
on every loop in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
. This is analogous to, but simpler than,
Lesch’ uniqueness characterisation of the spectral flow.

To prove the second asserted equality, fix
n\geq p-1
and introduce the function
A_{n}
on the Banach manifold
C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{U}_{p})
by setting

A_{n}(U_{\bullet})=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t})(U_{t}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\alpha_{n}(U_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t}))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto U_{t,\bullet}
be a differentiable path in
C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathcal{U}_{p})
. Using an identical proof as for the function
B_{r}
, one can show that the function is locally constant, homotopy invariant, descends to a map on
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
and satisfies concatenation.
For the simple path
U_{t}^{(k)}
introduced in (
2.4
) we can check that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big((U_{t}^{(k)})^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(U_{t}^{(k)})(U_{t}^{(k)}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\big(2\pi iP(-1+e^{i2\pi t})^{k}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t=(-1)^{n}2\pi ik

and so we again have
A_{n}(U_{\bullet}^{(k)})=k=\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet}^{(k)})
, showing that the function
A_{n}
satisfies the same normalisation as the spectral flow. Thus,
A_{n}(U_{\bullet})=\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})
, proving the second asserted equality.
∎

In Theorem
2.9
, the integral formula for spectral flow is presented for a loop of unitaries indexed by the interval
[0,1]
. In many application (particularly those considered in Section
3
below) it is convenient to consider this formula for paths of unitaries indexed by
[0,\infty)
or by
\mathbb{R}
. For this reason, we state the version of this formula in this setting.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
p\geq 1
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
r\geq(p-1)/2
and
n\geq p-1
. Let
(U_{s}),s\in[0,\infty)
be a path in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
such that

1.

(U_{s})
is
\mathcal{L}^{p}
-differentiable on any compact interval of
[0,\infty)
;

2.

The limit
\lim_{s\to\infty}U_{s}=:U_{\infty}
exists in the Schatten norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
U_{0}=U_{\infty}=\Id
;

3.

The functions
s\mapsto\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})|U_{s}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)
and
s\mapsto\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})(U_{s}-\Id)^{n}\Big)
are integrable on
[0,\infty)
.

Then

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})|U_{s}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})(U_{s}-\Id)^{n}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

A similar result holds for a path indexed by
\mathbb{R}
(in which case we assume that the limits in the Schatten norm as
s\to\pm\infty
coincide and are the identity).

Proof.

Consider the change of variable
t=f(s)=1-(1+s)^{-\alpha/2}
,
s\in[0,\infty)
and the path
V_{f(s)}=U_{s}
. Then the loop
V_{f(s)}
is a continuous loop in
\mathcal{U}_{p}(H)
with
V_{0}=V_{1}=\Id
. Since
(U_{\bullet})
is differentiable on any compact interval of
[0,\infty)
we have that
V_{\bullet}
is differentiable on
[0,1)
.
Note that
U^{\prime}_{t}=V^{\prime}_{f(s)}f^{\prime}(s)
, so that
U^{*}_{t}U^{\prime}_{t}\frac{dt}{ds}=V^{*}_{s}V^{\prime}_{s}
. Hence, by Theorem
2.9
we have that

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(U_{s})_{s\in[0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\textup{sf}(V_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr\Big(V_{t}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(V_{t})|U_{t}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{2^{2r+1}}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(r+1/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\Big(U_{s}^{*}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}(U_{s})|U_{s}-\Id|^{2r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

as required.

For the case when the path is indexed by
\mathbb{R}
the argument is similar with a possible change of variables given by
t=\frac{1}{1+e^{-s}}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

2.4
Regularised determinants

We now aim to rewrite the right-hand side of the integral formula in Theorem
2.9
in terms of regularised determinants of
U_{t}
. We recall some basic definitions and results for regularised determinants and refer to
[
26
,
51
]
for the general theory of regularised determinants.

Let, as before,
\mathcal{H}
be a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space.
Let
\{h_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
denote an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}
and suppose
A\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
. For each

N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{N}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
denote the matrix with entries

\delta_{j,k}+(h_{j},Ah_{k})_{\mathcal{H}},\quad 1\leq j,k\leq N.

(2.6)

The sequence
\{\textup{Det}(M_{N})\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
has a limit as
N\to\infty
, and its value does not depend on the orthonormal basis chosen. One defines the Fredholm determinant

\textup{Det}(\Id+A):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\textup{Det}(M_{N}).

(2.7)

The Fredholm determinant is Fréchet differentiable with respect to
A
(cf., e.g.,
[
51
, Theorem 5.2]
).
Moreover, if
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is an open set and
A:\Omega\to\mathcal{L}^{1}
is analytic, then the function
\textup{Det}(\Id+A(\,\cdot\,))
is analytic in
\Omega
, and on a dense open subset of
\Omega

\frac{d}{dz}\Log\big(\textup{Det}(\Id+A(z))\big)=\tr\big((\Id+A(z))^{-1}A^{\prime}(z)\big).

(2.8)

Here we use the convention that
\Log:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

\Log(re^{it})=\ln(r)+it\quad\mbox{for}\quad-\pi<t\leq\pi.

(2.9)

This convention means that
\Log\textup{Det}
has jump discontinuities, but is otherwise smooth.

For an operator
U
with
U-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
, the operator
U\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}(U-\textup{Id})^{\ell}\right)-\textup{Id}
defines a trace-class operator and so we can define the
p
-determinant of
U
by

\displaystyle\textup{Det}_{p}(U)

\displaystyle=\textup{Det}\left(U\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}(U-\textup{Id})^{\ell}\right)\right).

If
p\geq 2
and
U-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{p-1}
also, we have the recursion formula (see e.g.
[
51
, Theorem 9.2]
)

\displaystyle\textup{Det}_{p}(U)

\displaystyle=\textup{Det}_{p-1}(U)\exp\left(\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{p-1}\textup{Tr}\left((U-\textup{Id})^{p-1}\right)\right),

(2.10)

and if further
U-{\rm Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{q}
for all
1\leq q\leq p
we have

\displaystyle\textup{Det}_{p}(U)

\displaystyle=\textup{Det}(U)\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((U-\textup{Id})^{\ell}\right)\right).

(2.11)

In the following we shall need a version of Equation (
2.8
) for the regularised determinant
\textup{Det}_{p}
and a path of unitaries indexed by a real variable
t
. We start with an auxiliary lemma. The version of this lemma for analytic Schatten-valued maps is well-known (see e.g.
[
51
, Lemma 9.1]
).

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
t\mapsto U_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
be a path of
p
-Schatten unitaries which is differentiable in Schatten norm on
(0,1)
. Define

R_{t}:=U_{t}\exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(U_{t}-\Id)^{j}\right)-{\rm Id}.

Then
R_{t}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
, the map
t\mapsto R_{t}
is differentiable in the trace-class norm on
(0,1)
, and

\Tr\bigl((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime}\bigr)=(-1)^{p-1}\Tr\bigl(U_{t}^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{p-1}U_{t}^{\prime}\bigr),\qquad t\in(0,1).

Proof.

Define a scalar function

h(z):=(1+z)\exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}z^{j}\right)-1,

so that
R_{t}=h(U_{t}-\Id).

It is well know that
h(z)=z^{p}g(z)
for an entire function
g
(see e.g. the proof of
[
51
, Lemma 9.1]
). In particular, the Hölder inequality implies that
R_{t}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}
. Computing
h^{\prime}(z)
yields

h^{\prime}(z)=(1+h(z))\left(\frac{1}{1+z}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}z^{j-1}\right).

Note that

\frac{1}{1+z}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}z^{j-1}=(-1)^{p-1}\frac{z^{p-1}}{1+z},

and so

h^{\prime}(z)=(1+h(z))\,(-1)^{p-1}\frac{z^{p-1}}{1+z},

or, equivalently,

(1+h(z))^{-1}h^{\prime}(z)=(-1)^{p-1}\frac{z^{p-1}}{1+z}.

(2.12)

Next, we show that
t\mapsto R_{t}
is differentiable in the trace norm and compute
R_{t}^{\prime}
. We write

h(z)=\sum_{m=p}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m},

where
a_{m}
are such that
g(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}.

For each integer
m\geq 1
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}(U_{t}-\Id)^{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{t}^{\prime}(U_{t}-\Id)^{m-1-k}

in the
\mathcal{L}^{p/m}
-norm.
Hence we obtain

R_{t}^{\prime}=\sum_{m=p}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{t}^{\prime}(U_{t}-\Id)^{m-1-k}

in
\mathcal{L}^{1}
.

Since
\Id+R_{t}=U_{t}\exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j}(U_{t}-\Id)^{j}\right)
and
U_{t}
is unitary, it follows that
\Id+R_{t}
is invertible. Therefore

(\Id+R_{t})^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}.

Using the above formula for
R_{t}^{\prime}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\Tr\bigl((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=p}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\Tr\left((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{k}U_{t}^{\prime}(U_{t}-\Id)^{m-1-k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=p}^{\infty}ma_{m}\Tr\left((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{m-1}U_{t}^{\prime}\right),

where in the last equation we used the cyclicity of the trace and that
R_{t}
commutes with
U_{t}
.
Since

h^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{m=p}^{\infty}ma_{m}z^{m-1},

we deduce that

\Tr\bigl((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime}\bigr)=\Tr\left((\Id+R_{t})^{-1}h^{\prime}(U_{t}-\Id)U_{t}^{\prime}\right).

By (
2.12
) we have that

(\Id+R_{t})^{-1}h^{\prime}(U_{t}-\Id)=(-1)^{p-1}U_{t}^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{p-1}.

Substituting this into the trace identity above yields

\Tr\bigl((I+R_{t})^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime}\bigr)=(-1)^{p-1}\Tr\bigl(U_{t}^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{p-1}U_{t}^{\prime}\bigr),

as required.
∎

The following lemma relates spectral flow formula to regularised determinants, which in Section
3
will allow us to rewrite Levinson’s theorem as a spectral flow formula.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose that
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
U_{\bullet}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
is a path of
p
-Schatten unitaries which is differentiable in
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-norm on
(0,1)
.
Then for almost all
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p-1}\textup{Tr}\left(U^{*}_{t}U^{\prime}_{t}(\textup{Id}-U_{t})^{p-1}\right).

(2.13)

Proof.

Assume first that
p=1
.
Since
U_{t}
is trace-class differentiable, we can write

U_{t+\varepsilon}=U_{t}+\varepsilon U_{t}^{\prime}+O(\varepsilon^{2})

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Applying the determinant to both sides and using multiplicativity of the determinant, we obtain

\textup{Det}(U_{t+\varepsilon})=\textup{Det}(U_{t})\textup{Det}\bigl(\Id+\varepsilon U_{t}^{*}U_{t}^{\prime}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\bigr).

By
[
50
, p. 268]
we have that

\textup{Det}\bigl(\Id+\varepsilon U_{t}^{*}U_{t}^{\prime}+O(\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)=1+\varepsilon\Tr(U_{t}^{*}U_{t}^{\prime})+O(\varepsilon^{2}).

Substituting this into the previous expression yields

\textup{Det}(U_{t+\varepsilon})=\textup{Det}(U_{t})+\varepsilon\textup{Det}(U_{t})\Tr(U_{t}^{*}U_{t}^{\prime})+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

so that

\frac{\textup{Det}(U_{t+\varepsilon})-\textup{Det}(U_{t})}{\varepsilon\,\textup{Det}(U_{t})}=\Tr(U_{t}^{*}U_{t}^{\prime})+O(\varepsilon).

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, it follows that

\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}(U_{t})}=\Tr(U_{t}^{-1}U_{t}^{\prime}),

which proves the formula for
p=1
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary. Arguing similarly we have that there is a decomposition of the interval
[0,1]
into finitely many closed intervals intersecting only in singletons such that
\Log\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})
is differentiable on each interval. Let
R_{t}
as in Lemma
2.11
. By Lemma
2.11
,
R_{t}
is a trace-class differentiable function with

\Tr(R_{t}^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime})=(-1)^{p-1}\Tr\bigl(U_{t}^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{p-1}U_{t}^{\prime}\bigr).

Therefore, since
\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})=\textup{Det}(R_{t})
we infer that

\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}=\Tr(R_{t}^{-1}R_{t}^{\prime})=(-1)^{p-1}\Tr\bigl(U_{t}^{-1}(U_{t}-\Id)^{p-1}U_{t}^{\prime}\bigr),

as required.
∎

Combining Theorem
2.9
and Lemma
2.12
we have the following result.

Theorem 2.13
.

Suppose that
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
U_{\bullet}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
is a
C^{1}
loop of
p
-Schatten unitaries. Then

\displaystyle\textup{sf}(U_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Proof.

Comparing the integral with the result of Theorem
2.9
completes the proof.
∎

We record the following result which follows immediately from equality (
2.11
).

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose that
U_{\bullet}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{U}_{1}
is a trace-class path of unitaries. Then

\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}=\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}(U_{t})}+\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\Tr\big((U_{t}-\Id)^{\ell}\big).

2.5
Spectral flow for general paths

So far we have considered spectral flow for loops of unitaries. In this section, we consider spectral flow for path of
p
-Schatten unitaries. Here the idea is to connect the endpoints of the path to the identity via a simple path.

Let
U_{\bullet}
be a
C^{1}
-path of unitaries in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
for some
p\geq 1
which is not closed (i.e. not a loop). Set
U_{0}=e^{Y}
,
U_{1}=e^{Z}
for some skew
Y,Z\in\mathcal{L}^{p}
. Define paths
V_{t}=e^{tY}
and
W_{t}=e^{(1-t)Z}
for
t\in[0,1]
. The choice of
Y,Z
is not unique and we address the choices below.

Definition 2.15
.

With
U_{\bullet}
a path in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
and
V_{\bullet}
,
W_{\bullet}
as above, we define

\textup{sf}_{Y,Z}(U_{\bullet}):=\textup{sf}(V_{\bullet}*U_{\bullet}*W_{\bullet})

where
*
denotes concatenation.

Proposition 2.16
.

Recall
\Theta,\Xi
defined in Equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) respectively.
With
U_{\bullet}
,
V_{\bullet}
,
W_{\bullet}
as in Definition
2.15
, for any
n\geq p-1
we have the formulae

\displaystyle\textup{sf}_{Y,Z}(U_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\alpha(U^{*}_{t}\dot{U}_{t})\,dt+\Theta(U_{0})-\Theta(U_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\beta(U^{*}_{t}\dot{U}_{t})\,dt+\Xi(U_{0})-\Xi(U_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(U_{t}^{*}\dot{U}_{t}(U_{t}-\Id)^{n})dt+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(V_{t}^{*}\dot{V}_{t}(V_{t}-\Id)^{n})dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{1}\Tr(W_{t}^{*}\dot{W}_{t}(W_{t}-\Id)^{n})dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(U_{t})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(V_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(V_{t})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\textup{Det}_{p}(W_{t})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(W_{t})}\,dt\Big].

Proof.

These follow from
Theorems
2.9
and
2.13

and the relations
d\Theta=\alpha
and
d\Xi=\beta
.
∎

The functions
\Theta,\Xi
play the same role as the eta and kernel correction terms
[
19
,
21
]
in spectral flow formulae for paths of self-adjoint Fredholm operators.

Remark 2.17
.

We now address the dependence of spectral flow for a (non-closed) path on the choice of the paths connecting endpoints to the identity. In the self-adjoint case, any two choices of admissible paths in the affine space between two fixed points are homotopic
[
19
]
. In particular, there is no ambiguity when connecting two endpoints of the path to the same point. In the case of unitaries, this is no longer the case and two different loops starting and ending at the identity may have different spectral flow.

In Definition
2.15
we have made a choice to connect the endpoints to the identity via geodesics.
Recall, that the exponential map
\exp:T\mathcal{U}_{p}\to\mathcal{U}_{p}
is surjective, but not injective. Thus in general our definition of spectral flow for a non-closed path depends on how we represent the endpoints.

Nevertheless, when the endpoints are finite rank perturbations of the identity, we can make a convention to fix these choices. The tangent cut locus of
U(n)
is
[
49
, Section 3]

Ad_{G}\Big\{\frac{i\pi}{X}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}E_{jj}:\,\sum_{j}x_{j}^{2}=1,\ X=\max_{j}|x_{j}|\Big\}\subset T_{{\rm Id}}U(n)

where the
E_{jj}
are the standard diagonal matrix units.

Given a unitary finite rank perturbation of the identity
U
, we may diagonalise
U
to be of the form
{\rm diag}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}},1,1,\dots)
. If
U
is a cut point, then at least one
e^{i\theta_{j}}=-1
. We then choose to represent this point as
-1=e^{i\pi}
(not
e^{-i\pi}
).
This choice is consistent with the (discontinuous) principal branch of
\Log
extended to the negative real axis from above as in Equation (
2.9
). We will adopt this normalisation in the next section.

3
Application to scattering theory

In this section we apply spectral flow for unitaries to the scattering operator
S
of Schrödinger systems. In this setting, the scattering matrix
S(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
is a differentiable path of
p
-Schatten unitaries. Using Theorem
2.9
, we will reformulate Levinson’s theorem as a spectral flow formula. The interesting feature here is that as a function of energy,
\lambda\mapsto S(\lambda)-{\rm Id}
is pointwise trace-class, but the trace of
S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)
is not integrable. This makes it impossible to apply the classical winding number formula for the spectral flow in this setting. Including additional powers of
S(\lambda)-\Id
in the integral formula for the spectral flow provides the appropriate correction.

3.1
Scattering theory for Schrödinger operators

Consider the free Laplacian
H_{0}=-\sum_{j}\partial_{j}^{2}
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with domain the second Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we can define the Schrödinger operator

H=H_{0}+V
,
also with domain
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The spectrum of
H_{0}
is entirely absolutely continuous and given by
\sigma_{ac}(H_{0})=[0,\infty)
and the spectrum of
H
consists of the absolutely continuous part
\sigma_{ac}(H)=\sigma_{ac}(H_{0})=[0,\infty)
and a finite number of non-positive eigenvalues with finite multiplicity.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
we denote the resolvent of
H_{0}
by

R_{0}(z)=(H_{0}-z)^{-1}
,
considered as a bounded operator from
H^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
v=|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
u=\textup{sign}(V)
. Then for
\lambda>0
the limiting absorption principle
[
1
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
tells us that the limits

\displaystyle vR_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)v

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}vR_{0}(\lambda\pm i\varepsilon)v\quad\textup{ and }\quad(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i\varepsilon)v)^{-1}

(3.1)

exist in the topology of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

The wave operators are

W_{\pm}:=\mathop{\slim}_{t\to\pm\infty}\mathrm{e}^{itH}\mathrm{e}^{-itH_{0}}
,
and satisfy the following properties (see
[
56
, Theorem 1.6.2]
and
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

The wave operators are isometries and
\textup{Range}(W_{\pm})=P_{ac}(H)L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence they are Fredholm operators with index

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{\pm})

\displaystyle=-N,

(3.2)

where
N
is the total number of eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) of
H
.
Moreover the scattering operator

S=W_{+}^{*}W_{-}

is a unitary operator which commutes with
H_{0}

Define the unitary operator
F_{0}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
by

\displaystyle[F_{0}f](\lambda,\omega)

\displaystyle=2^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle x,\omega\rangle}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.3)

The operator
F_{0}
diagonalises
H_{0}
as multiplication by the spectral variable in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
(see
[
56
, Section 1.2]
), and so there exists a family of unitary operators
\{S(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, defined by the relation

[S(\lambda)f](\lambda,\omega)=[F_{0}SF_{0}^{*}f](\lambda,\omega)
.
The operator
S(\lambda)
is called the scattering matrix at energy
\lambda

and has the representation

\displaystyle S(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}-2\pi iF_{0}(\lambda)v(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}vF_{0}(\lambda)^{*},

(3.4)

where
F_{0}(\lambda)v:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
is given by
[F_{0}(\lambda)vf](\omega)=[F_{0}vf](\lambda,\omega)
.

In the following proposition we collect some basic properties of the scatting matrix ensuring that it is a path of unitaries for which we can consider the spectral flow formulae.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
56
, Proposition 8.1.5 and Proposition 8.1.9]

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We have that
S(\lambda)-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
S(\lambda)
is
k
-times differentiable in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|S(\lambda)-\textup{Id}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d-1}{2p}},

(3.5)

for some constants
C>0
and
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}>0
. In particular,
S(\lambda)\to\textup{Id}
in
\mathcal{L}^{q}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
for all
q\geq d
and the function
\lambda\mapsto\Tr_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}((S(\lambda)-\textup{Id})^{p})
is absolutely integrable on
[0,\infty)
for
p>d+1
.

By Proposition
3.2
we have that
S(\lambda)
is a locally differentiable path of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
-class unitaries, but the limit
S(\infty)=\Id
holds only in
\mathcal{L}^{d}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
.

The final item we require before we can apply the spectral flow formula of Theorem
2.9
is the low energy behaviour of
S(\bullet)
. The low energy behaviour is dictated by the presence (or absence) of zero energy resonances of
H
, namely distributional solutions of
H\psi=0
, and whether
d\geq 2
.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If
d\neq 2
we say there is an
s
-resonance if there exists a non-zero bounded distributional solution to
H\psi=0
. If
d=2
we say there is a
p
-resonance if there exists a non-zero distributional solution
\psi
to
H\psi=0
with
\psi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
q>2
. We say that there is an
s
-resonance if there exists a non-zero bounded distributional solution
\psi
to
H\psi=0
with
\psi\notin L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
q<\infty
.

General bounds on the resolvent of
H

[
29
]
show that there can be no resonances for dimension
d\geq 5
. The naming convention for the types of resonances is due to the fact that for spherically symmetric potentials,
s
-resonances can only occur with angular momentum zero and
p
-resonances with angular momentum one (see
[
12
,
36
]
).

The following result describes the low energy behaviour of the scattering matrix in the norm of
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
. The low energy behaviour of the scattering operator has been obtained in dimension
d=1
in
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
(see also
[
33
, Proposition 9]
and
[
24
, Theorem 2.15]
), in dimension
d=2
in
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
48
, Theorem 1.1]
, in dimension
d=3
in
[
28
, Section 5]
and in dimension
d\geq 4
in
[
4
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for
d\neq 1,3
we have
S(0)=\textup{Id}
. For
d=3
we have
S(0)=\textup{Id}-2P_{s}
, where
P_{s}
is the projection onto spherical harmonics of order zero in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
if there exists a resonance and
P_{s}=0
otherwise. For
d=1
we have

\displaystyle S(0)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}

if there does not exist a resonance and if there does exist a resonance, there exists
c_{+},c_{-}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
with
c_{+}^{2}+c_{-}^{2}=1
and

\displaystyle S(0)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2c_{+}c_{-}&c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}\\
c_{-}^{2}-c_{+}^{2}&2c_{+}c_{-}\end{pmatrix}.

In the following, we shall require a little more detailed analysis, showing that the values of
S(0)
in Theorem
3.4
are, in fact, the trace-class limit of
S(\lambda)
as
\lambda\to 0
. The result below is folklore and we briefly outline the proof for completeness.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let, as before,
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose that
d\geq 2
and
d\neq 3
. Then
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}S(\lambda)=\Id
in the trace-class norm. If
d=3
, then
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}S(\lambda)=\Id-2P_{s}
in the trace-class norm. For
d=1
,
S(0)=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}S(\lambda)
in the trace-class norm.

Proof.

Since for
d=1
the scattering matrix is a finite-dimensional marix, we immediately have the equality in the trace-class norm.

By equality (
3.4
) we have that

S(\lambda)-\Id=-2\pi iF_{0}(\lambda)v(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}vF_{0}(\lambda)^{*}.

It follows from
[
56
, equality 1.2.5]
that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that

(F_{0}(\lambda)vf)(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}\,\widehat{(vf)}(\sqrt{\lambda}\,\omega).

Therefore, the integral kernel
K(\lambda;\omega,x)
of the operator
F_{0}(\lambda)v
is given by

K(\lambda;\omega,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(2\pi)^{-d/2}\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}e^{-i\sqrt{\lambda}\,\omega\cdot x}v(x).

A direct computation implies that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm can be estimated as

\|F_{0}(\lambda)v\|_{2}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}})\to 0,\quad\lambda\to 0.

(3.6)

If
d\geq 5
, then the operator

(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}
has asymptotic expansion as
\lambda\to 0
in the operator norm satisfying
(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\lambda})
(see
[
4
]
). Therefore, the asymptotic in (
3.6
) guarantees that
S(\lambda)\to\Id
in the trace-class norm.

For dimensions
2\leq d\leq 4
the key is the expansion of
(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}
as
\lambda\to 0
.
By the threshold expansions of
(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}
, one may write

(u+vR_{0}(\lambda+i0)v)^{-1}=L(\lambda)+G(\lambda),

where
L(\lambda)
denotes the leading part and
G(\lambda)
is finite rank for
\lambda
near
0
(see
[
12
]
and
[
48
]
for
d=2
,
[
28
, Section 5]
for
d=3
, and
[
4
]
for
d=4
).
Hence

S(\lambda)-I=-2\pi i\,F_{0}(\lambda)\,v\,L(\lambda)\,v\,F_{0}(\lambda)^{*}-2\pi i\,F_{0}(\lambda)\,v\,G(\lambda)\,v\,F_{0}(\lambda)^{*}.

The behaviour of the first term is already known from the proofs of the operator-norm
limits of
S(\lambda)
at threshold: in dimensions
d=2
and
d=4
it tends to
0
(
[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
48
, Theorem 1.1]
for
d=2
and
[
4
, Theorem 2.15]
for
d=4
),
while in dimension
d=3
it converges to the finite-rank resonance correction

-2P_{s}

[
28
, Section 5]
. Thus no additional argument is needed for the leading part.

For the second term, since
G(\lambda)
is finite rank, the operator

F_{0}(\lambda)\,v\,G(\lambda)\,v\,F_{0}(\lambda)^{*}

is finite rank as well. Therefore, the existing operator-norm convergence results in fact prove trace-norm convergence.
It follows that the operator-norm threshold limits of
S(\lambda)
automatically upgrade
to trace-norm limits:

S(\lambda)\to I\quad\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{1}\ \text{for }d=2,4,

and

S(\lambda)\to I-2P_{s}\quad\text{in }\mathcal{L}_{1}\ \text{for }d=3.\qed

3.2
Determinants of the scattering operator

To relate Levinson’s theorem to spectral flow we firstly need to establish certain formulas for regularised determinants of the scattering matrix.

By Proposition
3.2
, we have that
S(\lambda)-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))
for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and so the determinant of
S(\lambda)
is well-defined. We have the following more precise statement, due to Guillopé
[
27
, Theorem III.1]

Lemma 3.6

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose that
q_{1},q_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\big(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)(S(\lambda)-\Id)^{d-1}\big)\,d\lambda=(-1)^{d-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Det_{d}(S(\lambda))}{\Det_{d}(S(\lambda))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{d}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{l=1}^{d-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\Tr\big((S(0)-\Id)^{\ell}\big)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{d}(0).

Proof.

We introduce the notation

H_{d}(\lambda):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\Tr\big((S(\lambda)-I)^{\ell}\big),\lambda\in[0,\infty).

Since for any
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
we have that

is a trace-class operator, Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
2.14
implies that

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}

\displaystyle\Tr\big(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)(S(\lambda)-\Id)^{d-1}\big)=\frac{\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Det_{d}(S(\lambda))}{\Det_{d}(S(\lambda)}=\frac{\frac{d}{d\lambda}\Det(S(\lambda))}{\Det(S(\lambda)}+\frac{d}{d\lambda}H_{d}(\lambda).

Using Lemma
2.12
once again, we have that

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}

\displaystyle\Tr\big(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)(S(\lambda)-\Id)^{d-1}\big)=\Tr(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda))+\frac{d}{d\lambda}H_{d}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\Big[\Tr(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda))-p_{d}(\lambda)\Big]+\Big[\frac{d}{d\lambda}H_{d}(\lambda)+p_{d}(\lambda)\Big].

By Theorem
3.12
the integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)-p_{d}(\lambda)\right)\right)\,d\lambda

is well-defined.
Since
p_{d}(\lambda)=P_{d}^{\prime}(\lambda)
, Proposition
3.11
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\frac{d}{d\lambda}H_{d}(\lambda)+p_{d}(\lambda)\Big]\,d\lambda=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}(H_{d}(\lambda)+P_{d}(\lambda))-\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}(H_{d}(\lambda)+P_{d}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=-H_{d}(0)-P_{d}(0).

Thus,

\displaystyle(-1)^{d-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\big(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)(S(\lambda)-\Id)^{d-1}\big)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)-p_{d}(\lambda)\right)\right)\,d\lambda-\frac{1}{2\pi i}H_{d}(0)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{d}(0),

proving that the integral converges.
∎

Proof.

2.13
and Lemma

\displaystyle\spf

\displaystyle(S(\bullet))=(-1)^{d-1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Tr\big(S^{*}(\lambda)S^{\prime}(\lambda)(S(\lambda)-\Id)^{d-1}\big)\,d\lambda

Assume now

Assume first that

4
Spectral flow for paths of non-densely defined operators

In this section we show that translating from unitaries in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
using the Cayley transform allows us to meaningfully discuss spectral flow of paths of non-densely defined operators, or equivalently, paths of self-adjoint operators on varying Hilbert spaces. We then use these tools to describe the unbounded operators on fields of Hilbert spaces defining classes in
KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Kasparov’s stabilisation theorem shows that Hilbert modules of the form
X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}
are (sections of) continuous fields of Hilbert spaces
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}
over
\mathbb{R}
. Using the stabilisation theorem to make an identification of
X
with such a continuous field, we provide a characterisation of the families of operators on
\{\mathcal{H}_{t}\}
which define Kasparov modules. The same comments apply to any locally compact Hausdorff space in place of
\mathbb{R}
.

4.1
The Cayley transform of
\mathcal{U}_{p}

It is well-known that the Cayley transform allows to translate between unitaries and (unbounded) self-adjoint operators.
A careful comparison of spectral flow on unbounded self-adjoint Fredholm operators in different topologies appears in
[
16
]
, in part using the spectral flow for unitaries and the Cayley transform. Importantly, the class of unitaries considered in
[
16
]
includes only unitaries
U
with
U-\Id
injective. Under this assumption the Cayley transform translates to self-adjoint operators which are densely defined on a fixed Hilbert space
H
. In
[
16
]
, the Cayley transform is shown to be continuous between the gap topology and norm topology, while the bounded transform
x\to x(1+x^{2})^{-1/2}
is continuous, by definition, between the Riesz topology and norm topology.

In this subsection, we aim to show what class of unbounded operators is associated via Cayley transform with the unitaries in
\mathcal{U}_{p}
without the assumption that
U-\Id
is injective. This will allow to define spectral flow of paths of non-densely defined operators, or equivalently, paths of self-adjoint operators on varying Hilbert spaces.

One obvious application encompassed by our results, which we do not explore here, is to spectral flow for boundary value problems, as discussed in
[
16
,
52
]
. In these cases the Hilbert space is fixed, but the domains of the operators are changing in a gap continuous manner.

We start by introducing the notation for the Cayley transform and its inverse. As before, we assume that
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a self-adjoint unbounded operator
D:\Dom(D)\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, the inverse Cayley transform of
D
is the unitary
C^{-1}(D):=(D+i)(D-i)^{-1}
.

Given a unitary
U\in B(\mathcal{H})
, the Cayley transform of
U
is the unbounded self-adjoint operator
C(U):(U-\Id)\mathcal{H}\to\overline{(U-\Id)\mathcal{H}}
given by
C(U)=i(U+\Id)(U-\Id)^{-1}
.

Remark 4.2
.

We have departed from some standard conventions regarding which is the Cayley transform and which its inverse.

The Cayley transform is ubiquitous. For us, the relevant aspects are the applications of the Cayley transform in studying properties of unbounded self-adjoint operators (see e.g.
[
37
]
), leveraging the well-understood spectral theory of unitaries.

Next we introduce the class of unbounded operators we shall consider in this section.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
.
Given a closed subspace
V\subset\mathcal{H}
, we define

\mathcal{F}_{p}^{sa}(V)=\{T:\Dom(T)\subset V\to V\ \mbox{self-adjoint}:\,(T-i)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(V)\}.

Define the set

\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H}):=\bigcup_{V\subset\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{F}_{p}^{sa}(V),

where the union is taken over all closed subspaces
V\subset\mathcal{H}
. We define
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
similarly by assuming that

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that

are mutually inverse bijections between

and
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})

is the orthogonal projection from
\mathcal{H}
onto the closed subspace
V\subset\mathcal{H}

Proof.

Let

. Then the operator, defined as

C(U)=i(U+{\rm Id})(U-{\rm Id})^{-1}:(U-{\rm Id})\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}_{U}

is densely-defined and self-adjoint on

[
16
,
17
]
, and therefore,

maps
\mathcal{U}_{p}
into
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})

) is unitary and belongs to

For

we have a natural basepoint given by the identity. For

Lemma 4.5
.

For

Proof.

The domain of the Cayley transform of

Given a path

below, extends the characterisation of gap continuity of paths of self-adjoint operators on a fixed space

in

Definition 4.6
.

Let

.
Given

The following lemma show that the topology on
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
inherited from
\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
can be equivalently described as continuity of the projections
P_{T}
in the respective Schatten class.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. A path
t\mapsto T_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
is continuous in
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if the path
t\mapsto P_{T_{t}}
is continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})
. In particular,
U_{t}
is a continuous path in
\mathcal{U}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
P_{C(U_{t})}
is a continuous path in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})
. In this case, the path
t\mapsto(T_{t}\pm i)^{-1}
extended to an operator on
\mathcal{H}
by zero on
\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}^{\perp}
is continuous in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}).

Proof.

Let
t\mapsto T_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
be a continuous path in
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
.

By definition of the topology on
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
, the convergence
T_{t}\to T_{t_{0}}
in
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
means that

(T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\to(T_{t_{0}}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{0}}}\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})

as
t\to t_{0}
.
Set

A_{t}:=(\Id+T_{t}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}},\qquad B_{t}:=T_{t}(\Id+T_{t}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}},

and similarly

A_{0}:=(\Id+T_{t_{0}}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t_{0}}}},\qquad B_{0}:=T_{t_{0}}(\Id+T_{t_{0}}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t_{0}}}}.

The projection
P_{T_{t}}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{T_{t}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(\Id+T_{t}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}&T_{t}(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\\
T_{t}(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}&\Id_{\mathcal{H}_{t}}-(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\end{pmatrix}+0\oplus\Id_{\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{t}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}(\Id+T_{t}^{2})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}&T_{t}(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\\
T_{t}(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}&\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-(\Id+T^{2}_{t})^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}A_{t}&B_{t}\\
B_{t}&\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-A_{t}\end{pmatrix},

and similarly,

P_{T_{t_{0}}}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{0}&B_{0}\\
B_{0}&\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-A_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

(T_{t}-i)^{-1}=(T_{t}+i)(\Id+T_{t}^{2})^{-1},\qquad(T_{t_{0}}-i)^{-1}=(T_{t_{0}}+i)(\Id+T_{t_{0}}^{2})^{-1},

we have

\displaystyle(T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}=B_{t}+iA_{t},\qquad(T_{t_{0}}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t_{0}}}}=B_{0}+iA_{0}.

(4.2)

Furthermore,

A_{t}=\frac{(T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}-\big((T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\big)^{*}}{2i},\qquad B_{t}=\frac{(T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}+\big((T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}\big)^{*}}{2},

and, similarly, for
A_{0},B_{0}
. Hence
T_{t}\to T_{t_{0}}
in
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
implies that

A_{t}\to A_{0},\qquad B_{t}\to B_{0}

in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})
and so

P_{T_{t}}-P_{T_{t_{0}}}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{t}-A_{0}&B_{t}-B_{0}\\
B_{t}-B_{0}&-(A_{t}-A_{0})\end{pmatrix},

(4.3)

ensuring that
P_{T_{t}}\to P_{T_{t_{0}}}
in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})
.

Conversely, assume that

P_{T_{t}}\to P_{T_{t_{0}}}\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})

as
t\to t_{0}
. Then, by Equation (
4.3
) we have that
A_{t}\to A_{0},\ B_{t}\to B_{0}
as
t\to t_{0}
. Equality (
4.2
) implies that

(T_{t}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t}}}-(T_{t_{0}}-i)^{-1}P_{\mathcal{H}_{T_{t_{0}}}}=(B_{t}-B_{0})+i(A_{t}-A_{0})\to 0

in
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H})
, proving that
T_{t}\to T_{t_{0}}
in
\mathcal{F}_{p}(\mathcal{H})
, as required.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
t\mapsto T_{t}
be a continuous path in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
with
\mathcal{H}_{t}=\overline{\Dom(T_{t})}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

The graph projections
P_{G(T_{t})}
are strongly continuous.

2.

The projections onto
\mathcal{H}_{t}
are strongly continuous.

Proof.

The assertion follow immediately from the norm continuity of

P_{T_{t}}\oplus\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1_{\mathcal{H}_{t}^{\perp}}\end{smallmatrix}\big)
.
∎

In the following lemma we specify how continuity of a unitary path translates to the continuity of the associated path of self-adjoint operators under an additional assumption that they are all densely defined on a fixed Hilbert space.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
t\mapsto U_{t}
be a continuous
p
-Schatten path of unitaries. Then if
C(U_{t})
are all defined on a fixed
\mathcal{H}

t\mapsto C(U_{t})
is
p
-norm resolvent continuous on
\mathcal{H}

\|(C(U_{t})-\lambda)^{-1}-(C(U_{s})-\lambda)^{-1}\|_{p}\to 0
as
t\to s
for all
\lambda\notin{\rm spec}(C(U_{s}))

Proof.

We will prove the result for
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{R}
, which ensures that
\lambda\not\in{\rm spec}(C(U_{t}))
. The case of real
\lambda\not\in{\rm spec}(C(U_{t}))
follows similarly using
[
47
, Section VIII.7]
. Normality of
U_{t}
and the resolvent identity yields

\displaystyle(C(U_{t})-\lambda)^{-1}-(C(U_{s})-\lambda)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(U_{t}-1)(i(U_{t}+1)-\lambda(U_{t}-1))^{-1}-(U_{s}-1)(i(U_{s}+1)-\lambda(U_{s}-1))^{-1}

\displaystyle=(i(U_{t}+1)-\lambda(U_{t}-1))^{-1}(U_{t}-1)-(U_{s}-1)(i(U_{s}+1)-\lambda(U_{s}-1))^{-1}

\displaystyle=(i(U_{t}+1)-\lambda(U_{t}-1))^{-1}\Big((U_{t}-1)(i(U_{s}+1)-\lambda(U_{s}-1))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-(i(U_{t}+1)-\lambda(U_{t}-1))(U_{s}-1)\Big)(i(U_{s}+1)-\lambda(U_{s}-1))^{-1}

\displaystyle=(i(U_{t}+1)-\lambda(U_{t}-1))^{-1}\Big((i+\lambda)(U_{t}-U_{s})\Big)(i(U_{s}+1)-\lambda(U_{s}-1))^{-1}

\displaystyle=((i-\lambda)U_{t}+(i+\lambda))^{-1}(i+\lambda)(U_{t}-U_{s})((i-\lambda)U_{s}+(i+\lambda))^{-1}.

Now as
\lambda\not\in\mathbb{R}
we have
(\lambda+i)(\lambda-i)^{-1}\not\in\mathbb{T}
and the spectral mapping property then gives (for
\lambda\neq i

\displaystyle\|(C(U_{t})

\displaystyle-\lambda)^{-1}-(C(U_{s})-\lambda)^{-1}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{i+\lambda}{(i-\lambda)^{2}}\Big|\|U_{t}-U_{s}\|_{p}\,d({\rm spec}(U_{t}),(\lambda+i)(\lambda-i)^{-1})\,d({\rm spec}(U_{s}),(\lambda+i)(\lambda-i)^{-1}),

where

is the usual distance in
\mathbb{C}
. For
\lambda=i

\|(C(U_{t})-\lambda)^{-1}-(C(U_{s})-\lambda)^{-1}\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|U_{t}-U_{s}\|_{p}.\qed

Every bounded continuous function of
C(U_{t})
is strongly continuous and every
C_{0}
function of
C(U_{t})
is norm continuous by the same proof as
[
47
, VIII.20]
.
In particular, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.11

Let

4.2
Kasparov modules

We now relate our constructions to Kasparov theory. Namely, we will show that unbounded Kasparov modules
(\mathbb{C},X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T)
can be described precisely using paths of operators
T_{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
which vanish at infinity in the operator norm.

Recall that an unbounded Kasparov module
(\mathbb{C},X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T)
representing a class in
KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
consists of a right Hilbert module
X
over
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
T:\Dom(T)\subset X\to X
a self-adjoint regular operator whose resolvent
(T\pm i)^{-1}
is a compact endomorphism of
X
. All of these notions can be found in
[
37
]
.

For a complex
C^{*}
-algebra
A
,
[
17
, Section 3]
shows that the Cayley transform gives an explicit isomorphism
K_{1}(A)\to KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},A)
.
In the case
A=C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, elements of
K_{1}(A)
are represented by elements of
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
. Using the identification of
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
provides most of the information we require.

Theorem 4.18
.

Every unbounded Kasparov module
(\mathbb{C},X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T)
defining a class in
KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
is unitarily equivalent to a cycle
(\mathbb{C},P_{\bullet}(\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})),\tilde{T}_{\bullet})
with
t\mapsto P_{t}
a strongly continuous path of projections on
\mathcal{H}
and
t\mapsto\tilde{T}_{t}
a continuous path in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
vanishing at infinity.

Conversely, every continuous path
t\mapsto T_{t}
in
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
vanishing at infinity whose graph projections
P_{G(T_{t})}
are strongly continuous defines an unbounded Kasparov module
(\mathbb{C},(\mathcal{H}_{\bullet})_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T_{\bullet})
and so a class in
KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
. Here

\mathcal{H}_{t}\subset\mathcal{H}
is the subspace on which
T_{t}
is densely-defined.

Proof.

Given an unbounded Kasparov module
(\mathbb{C},X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T)
, let
v:X\to\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be an isometry for some separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
: such a
v
exists by Kasparov’s stabilisation theorem
[
32
]
. Then the unbounded cycle
(\mathbb{C},X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})},T)
is unitarily equivalent, via
v
, to

(\mathbb{C},vv^{*}(\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})),vTv^{*}).

The map

\begin{pmatrix}0&v^{*}\\
v&0\end{pmatrix}:\begin{pmatrix}X\\
\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\end{pmatrix}\to\begin{pmatrix}X\\
\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\end{pmatrix}

is a bounded self-adjoint operator, and so too then is its square,

\begin{pmatrix}{\rm Id}_{X}&0\\
0&vv^{*}\end{pmatrix}

as is the composition with the evaluation at
t
, denoted
ev_{t}
. For the second component
vv^{*}
we recall from
[
46
, Section 2.3]
that

\End_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}(\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))\cong C_{b}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*-s})

where
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})_{*-s}
is the bounded operators with the
*
-strong topology. Hence
t\mapsto ev_{t}\circ vv^{*}
is a strongly continuous family of projections.
Let
v_{t}:X\to\mathcal{H}
be the composition
ev_{t}\circ v
.

The compactness of the resolvent of
T
gives the compactness of
(\pm ivv^{*}+vTv^{*})^{-1}
. Using the results of
[
46
, Section 2.3]

\End_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}^{0}(\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))\cong C_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))

we see that
t\mapsto(\pm iv_{t}v_{t}^{*}+v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{-1}
is a norm continuous family of compacts vanishing at infinity. As the domain of
v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}
is the image of
(\pm iv_{t}v_{t}^{*}+v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{-1}
, all paths of vectors in the domain of
v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}
converge to zero as
t\to\pm\infty
. Thus
v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}
vanishes at infinity.

Moreover, the family of graph projections

P_{G(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})}=\begin{pmatrix}(v_{t}v_{t}^{*}+(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{2})^{-1}&v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}(v_{t}v_{t}^{*}+(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{2})^{-1}\\
v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}(v_{t}v_{t}^{*}+(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{2})^{-1}&v_{t}v_{t}^{*}-(v_{t}v_{t}^{*}+(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{2})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

is strongly continuous, and
P_{G(v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})}\oplus\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
0&1-v_{t}v_{t}^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\big)
is norm continuous. Hence the family

t\mapsto v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*}
is in
F_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
, or equivalently, the path of unitaries

t\mapsto\begin{cases}(iv_{t}v_{t}^{*}+v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})(-iv_{t}v_{t}^{*}+v_{t}Tv_{t}^{*})^{-1}&v_{t}v_{t}^{*}\mathcal{H}\\
(1-v_{t}v_{t}^{*})&(1-v_{t}v_{t}^{*})\mathcal{H}\end{cases}

is in
\mathcal{U}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
.

For the converse, let
t\mapsto T_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{F}_{\infty}(\mathcal{H})
be a continuous path, and let
\mathcal{H}_{t}
be the closure of
\Dom(T_{t})
in
\mathcal{H}
. Let

X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}=\{\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{H}:\,\sigma\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}),\,\sigma(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{t}\}

with the multiplication action of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and inner product

(\sigma_{1}\mathbin{|}\sigma_{2})_{X}(t)=(\sigma_{1}(t)\mathbin{|}\sigma_{2}(t))_{\mathcal{H}}.

Then
X
is a Hilbert module, in particular complete, by
[
46
, Example 2.13]
.

Let
T_{\bullet}
be the unbounded operator on
X_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}
given by

\Dom(T_{\bullet})=\{\sigma\in X:\,\sigma(t)\in\Dom(T_{t})\},\qquad(T_{\bullet}\sigma)(t)=T_{t}(\sigma(t)).

Then
T_{\bullet}
is regular and self-adjoint. To see this, consider the graph projection
P_{G(T_{\bullet})}:X\oplus X\to X\oplus X
. By the local-global principle of Pierrot and Kaad-Lesch
[
30
,
44
]
, the submodule
P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X)
is complemented if and only if
[
40
, Proposition 1.6]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\big((P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X))^{\perp}\big)_{t}=(P_{G(T_{t})}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}))^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H}.

Given
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\in(P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X))^{\perp}
, for every
\rho\in\Dom(T_{\bullet})
we have for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

0=((\rho,T_{\bullet}\rho)\mathbin{|}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}))_{X\oplus X}(t)=((\rho(t),T_{t}\rho(t))\mathbin{|}(\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t)))_{\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}}.

Thus
(\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t))\in(P_{G(T_{t})}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}))^{\perp}
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and we have the inclusion
\big((P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X))^{\perp}\big)_{t}\subset(P_{G(T_{t})}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}))^{\perp}
.

Conversely, let
(\xi_{t},\zeta_{t})\in(P_{G(T_{t})}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}))^{\perp}
. By assumption, the graph projections are strongly continuous, so choosing any

(\xi,\zeta)\in(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
whose evaluation at

(\xi_{t},\zeta_{t})
, we obtain a continuous path
(1-P_{G(T_{\bullet})})(\xi,\zeta)

(1-P_{G(T_{\bullet})})(\xi,\zeta)\in(P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X))^{\perp}
and

. Thus
\big((P_{G(T_{\bullet})}(X\oplus X))^{\perp}\big)_{t}\supset(P_{G(T_{t})}(\mathcal{H}_{t}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{t}))^{\perp}
, and
T_{\bullet}
is regular. Self-adjointness now follows from the local-global principle and the pointwise
self-adjointness of the
T_{t}
.

Moreover
t\mapsto{\rm Id}_{\mathcal{H}}-C^{-1}(T_{t})=-2i(T_{t}-i)^{-1}
converges to zero in norm, and for each

is a compact operator on
\mathcal{H}

\mathbb{R}
with fibre at

the compacts
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{t})
on
\mathcal{H}_{t}

is contained in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}))=\End^{0}_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}(\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R}))
. Thus
(T_{\bullet}-i)^{-1}
is a compact endomorphism on
\mathcal{H}\otimes C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
commuting with the projection
Q_{\bullet}
onto the sections of the Hilbert space bundle

.
Hence
(T_{\bullet}-i)^{-1}
is a compact operator on
