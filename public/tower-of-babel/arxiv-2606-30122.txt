A Polynomial Moment Approach to a Rank Condition for Continuous-Stage Runge–Kutta Methods
Yuto Miyatake

Abstract
In the study of energy-preserving methods for Hamiltonian systems, polynomial continuous-stage Runge–Kutta methods play an important role. Necessary and sufficient conditions for such methods to be energy-preserving have already been established. They are energy-preserving if the matrix M ∈ ℝ s × s M\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times s} defining the method is symmetric, and the converse holds under the assumption that a certain s × ∞ s\times\infty matrix Φ CSRK \Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}} has full row rank. It was conjectured in ( Miyatake and Butcher 2016 , Remark 3) that the full-rank assumption should always hold for every consistent polynomial continuous-stage Runge–Kutta method. In this paper, we prove the conjecture by showing that the matrix Φ CSRK \Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}} has full row rank under the standard consistency condition. The proof is a direct application of the polynomial moment problem solved by Pakovich and Muzychuk Pakovich and Muzychuk 2009 .

Introduction

Continuous-stage Runge–Kutta (CSRK) methods are one-step numerical integrators for the initial value problem of ordinary differential equations

\dot{y}=f(y),\qquad y(0)=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a sufficiently smooth vector field.
In particular, a polynomial CSRK method
y_{0}\mapsto y_{1}
is defined as

\displaystyle Y_{\tau}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+h\int_{0}^{1}A_{\tau,\zeta}f(Y_{\zeta})\,\mathrm{d}\zeta,\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq 1,

(2)

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=y_{0}+h\int_{0}^{1}B_{\tau}f(Y_{\tau})\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,(=Y_{1}),

(3)

where
A_{\tau,\zeta}
is a bivariate polynomial satisfying
A_{0,\zeta}=0
and
B_{\tau}=A_{1,\tau}
is a univariate polynomial.
It is called an
s
-degree polynomial CSRK method if
A_{\tau,\zeta}
is of degree at most
s
in
\tau
.
The abscissa polynomial is defined by

C(\tau)=C_{\tau}:=\int_{0}^{1}A_{\tau,\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

(4)

Then
A_{0,\zeta}=0
implies
C(0)=0
.
A method is said to be consistent if

\int_{0}^{1}B_{\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=1,

or equivalently if

C(1)=1.

(5)

CSRK methods provide a flexible framework for constructing numerical integrators for ordinary differential equations.
They go back to Butcher’s algebraic theory of integration methods
Butcher 1972
; see also Butcher’s monograph on B-series
Butcher 2021
.
In particular, CSRK formulations have appeared in the study of energy-preserving integrators for Hamiltonian systems, including the average-vector-field collocation method and Hamiltonian boundary value methods
Hairer 2010
;
Brugnano et al. 2010
, continuous-stage energy-preserving methods
Miyatake 2014
;
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
, and related Runge–Kutta type constructions
Tang and Sun 2012
;
Tang and Sun 2014
.

Both Runge–Kutta (RK) methods and CSRK methods are special cases of the more general class of B-series methods, and they share many properties.
For example, one can apply the B-series theory to both.
In addition, the computational costs of
s
-stage implicit RK methods and
s
-degree polynomial CSRK methods are comparable.
Nevertheless, there are also important differences between the two classes.
From the viewpoint of geometric integration, RK methods can be symplectic for Hamiltonian systems, but they cannot preserve the energy of arbitrary Hamiltonian systems exactly.
By contrast, CSRK methods can be energy-preserving for Hamiltonian systems, but they cannot preserve the symplectic structure of arbitrary Hamiltonian systems exactly.

It is well known that an
s
-stage RK method is symplectic if

b_{i}a_{ij}+b_{j}a_{ji}=b_{i}b_{j},\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,s,

where
a_{ij}
and
b_{i}
are the coefficients of the RK method
Lasagni 1988
;
Sanz-Serna 1988
;
Suris 1988
.
This condition is also necessary if the method is irreducible, i.e., if there are no equivalent stages (see
Hairer 1994
and
(
Hairer et al. 2006
, Section VI.7.3)
).
The irreducibility condition is equivalent to the full row rank of a certain
s\times\infty
matrix.

Then, an RK method is irreducible if and only if
\Phi^{\mathrm{RK}}
has full rank
s
(see
(
Hairer et al. 2006
, Section VI.7.3)
).

Interestingly, a similar rank condition appears in the characterization of energy-preserving polynomial CSRK methods
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
.
We represent the kernel as

A_{\tau,\zeta}=\begin{bmatrix}\tau&\tau^{2}/2&\cdots&\tau^{s}/s\end{bmatrix}M\begin{bmatrix}1&\zeta&\cdots&\zeta^{s-1}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}},

(9)

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{s\times s}
.
Then, it is straightforward to verify that the method is energy-preserving if
M
is symmetric.
Conversely, this condition is also necessary if the method is consistent and the matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
defined below has full row rank
s
:

\Phi_{i,k}^{\mathrm{CSRK}}=\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{i-1}C_{\tau}^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad i=1,\ldots,s,\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

(10)

It was conjectured in
(
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
, Remark 3)
that this assumption should always hold for every consistent polynomial CSRK method.

In this paper, we prove in Section
2
that the moment matrix (
10
) has full row rank
s
under the standard consistency condition (
5
).
We also note that the rank condition is not directly related to the reducibility of continuous-stage methods.
We discuss this distinction in more detail in Section
3
.

Notation.

For
m\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}_{m}
denotes the real vector space of polynomials of degree at most
m
.
We also write
\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.

2
Polynomial moment problem and proof of the rank condition

2.1
The moment map and the rank condition

We formulate this rank condition as a moment non-degeneracy statement.
For a real polynomial
C
, we define

\mathcal{M}_{C}:\mathbb{P}_{s-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{+}},\qquad q\mapsto\left(\int_{0}^{1}q(\tau)C(\tau)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right)_{k\geq 1}.

(11)

As Lemma
1
below shows,
the full rank condition for the matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
in (
10
) is equivalent to the injectivity of the map
\mathcal{M}_{C}
.
Note that if the map (
11
) is not injective, there exists a non-zero polynomial
q\in\mathbb{P}_{s-1}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}q(\tau)C(\tau)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=0,\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.

(12)

Lemma 1
.

Let
C
be a real polynomial, and define
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
by (
10
). The following are equivalent:

(i)

the moment map
\mathcal{M}_{C}:\mathbb{P}_{s-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is injective;

(ii)

the matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
has full row rank
s
.

Proof.

If
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
does not have full row rank, there exists
0\neq\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
such that
\alpha^{\mathsf{T}}\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}=0
. Set

q(\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}\tau^{i-1}.

(13)

Then
0\neq q\in\mathbb{P}_{s-1}
and, for all
k\geq 1
,

\int_{0}^{1}q(\tau)C(\tau)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{i-1}C(\tau)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=(\alpha^{\mathsf{T}}\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}})_{k}=0.

(14)

Thus
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is not injective. Conversely, if
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is not injective, take
0\neq q\in\mathbb{P}_{s-1}
satisfying (
12
) and write
q(\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}\tau^{i-1}
. Then
\alpha\neq 0
and
\alpha^{\mathsf{T}}\Phi=0
, so
\Phi
cannot have full row rank.
∎

2.2
Full rank property

The main claim of the paper is the following.

Theorem 2
.

Let
C
be a real polynomial satisfying

C(0)=0,\qquad C(1)=1.

(15)

Then, for every
s\geq 1
, the moment map
\mathcal{M}_{C}:\mathbb{P}_{s-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is injective. Consequently, the matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
in (
10
) has full row rank
s
for every consistent polynomial CSRK method.

Theorem
2
can be seen as a direct application of the solution of the polynomial moment problem by Pakovich and Muzychuk
Pakovich and Muzychuk 2009
.

Theorem 3

(Pakovich–Muzychuk
Pakovich and Muzychuk 2009
)
.

Let
P
be a complex polynomial, and let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
with
a\neq b
.
There exists a nonzero complex polynomial
q
satisfying

\int_{a}^{b}P(z)^{k}q(z)\,\mathrm{d}z=0,\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(16)

if and only if
P(a)=P(b)
.

Theorem
3
is a direct consequence of the solution of the polynomial moment problem in
Pakovich and Muzychuk 2009
.
More precisely, the main theorem therein expresses every nonzero solution as a sum of reducible solutions associated with compositional right factors
W_{j}
satisfying
W_{j}(a)=W_{j}(b)
; this forces
P(a)=P(b)
, and the converse is obtained by taking functions of
P
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is not injective. Then there exists
0\neq q\in\mathbb{P}_{s-1}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}q(\tau)C(\tau)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=0,\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.

(17)

Set

\widetilde{q}(\tau)=C(\tau)q(\tau).

Since
C(1)=1
, the polynomial
C
is not identically zero; hence
\widetilde{q}
is non-zero. Moreover, (
17
) gives, for every
m=0,1,2,\ldots
,

\int_{0}^{1}C(\tau)^{m}\widetilde{q}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{1}q(\tau)C(\tau)^{m+1}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=0.

Thus
\widetilde{q}
is a non-zero solution of the polynomial moment problem (
16
) with
P=C
,
a=0
, and
b=1
. By Theorem
3
, this implies
C(0)=C(1)
, contradicting
C(0)=0
and
C(1)=1
. Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{C}
is injective. The full row rank of
\Phi
follows from Lemma
1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

The moment map (
11
) uses only the moments with
k\geq 1
, whereas Theorem
3
is stated for
k\geq 0
. The multiplication by
C
in the proof is the simple device that converts the vanishing of positive moments for
q
into the vanishing of all moments for
Cq
.

2.3
Consequence for energy-preserving methods

We recall the characterization of energy-preserving polynomial CSRK methods from
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
. Consider Hamiltonian systems

\dot{y}=S\nabla H(y),

(18)

where
S
is a constant non-singular skew-symmetric matrix.
Combining Theorem 3.5 of
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
, summarized in Section
1
, with Theorem
2
yields the following characterization of energy-preservation for consistent polynomial CSRK methods.

Corollary 4
.

Let a consistent polynomial CSRK method have the representation (
9
). Then the method is energy-preserving for all Hamiltonian systems of the form (
18
) if and only if

M=M^{\mathsf{T}}.

(19)

3
Relation with pointwise reducibility

The rank condition studied above is reminiscent of the full-rank characterizations that appear in finite-stage RK theory.
However, for continuous-stage methods the direct analogue of stage reducibility is pointwise reducibility.
It is a condition on the equality of stage values, not on the moment matrix (
10
).

Definition 1

(Pointwise equivalence and pointwise reducibility)
.

Two parameters
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in[0,1]
are called pointwise equivalent if, for every sufficiently smooth initial value problem (
1
) and every sufficiently small step size for which the stage equation (
2
) is uniquely solvable, the corresponding stage curve satisfies

Y_{\tau_{1}}=Y_{\tau_{2}}.

(20)

The method is called pointwise reducible if there exist
\tau_{1}\neq\tau_{2}
that are pointwise equivalent, and pointwise irreducible otherwise.

Butcher discusses the concepts of equivalence and reducibility for more general integration methods in
(
Butcher 2021
, Section 4.4)
.
He defines
A
-equivalence, and the pointwise equivalence introduced above is a special case of
A
-equivalence for polynomial CSRK methods.

Pointwise irreducibility is a natural continuous-stage analogue of irreducibility for finite-stage RK methods.
By contrast,
the moment non-degeneracy tests whether a non-zero polynomial stage direction can be invisible to all powers of the single abscissa polynomial
C
.
The pointwise reducibility depends on the full kernel
A_{\tau,\zeta}
, whereas the moment non-degeneracy depends only on
C
.

The following example shows that, although every consistent polynomial CSRK method is moment nondegenerate, such a method may still be pointwise reducible.

Example 1

(Pointwise reducible while
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
is full rank)
.

Let

\rho(\tau)=2\tau^{2}-\tau

(21)

and define

A_{\tau,\zeta}=\rho(\tau)+\rho(\tau)^{2}\left(\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(22)

Then
A_{0,\zeta}=0
and

B_{\zeta}=A_{1,\zeta}=\zeta+\frac{1}{2},\qquad\int_{0}^{1}B_{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=1.

(23)

Thus the method is consistent. Its abscissa polynomial is

C(\tau)=\int_{0}^{1}A_{\tau,\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=\rho(\tau).

(24)

For this example, it follows that
\rho(0)=\rho(1/2)=0
, and hence
Y_{0}=Y_{1/2}=y_{0}
for every problem.
This indicates that the method is pointwise reducible.

Example
1
illustrates why pointwise reducibility and the full-rank property of
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
should be kept conceptually separate.
The former identifies redundant stage parameters.
The latter excludes invisible polynomial directions in the moment matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
(
10
).
These are different tests, and in the consistent polynomial setting Theorem
2
implies that the matrix
\Phi^{\mathrm{CSRK}}
is always full rank.

4
Concluding remarks

We proved that the moment matrix appearing in the characterization of energy-preserving polynomial CSRK methods has full row rank under the standard consistency conditions. Equivalently, the associated moment map is injective on the polynomial stage-direction space.
The proof is a direct application of the solution to the polynomial moment problem obtained by Pakovich and Muzychuk.
Consequently, the full-rank conjecture in
(
Miyatake and Butcher 2016
, Remark 3)
follows, and the rank assumption in the necessity part of the energy-preserving condition can be removed.

We also clarified that this moment non-degeneracy is not the same as pointwise irreducibility. Pointwise reducibility concerns equality of stage values and depends on the full kernel
A_{\tau,\zeta}
. The rank condition treated in this paper concerns only the abscissa polynomial
C
and the finite-dimensional polynomial stage-direction space.
