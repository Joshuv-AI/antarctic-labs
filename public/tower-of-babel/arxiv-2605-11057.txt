The Poincaré series of Coxeter folding subgroups
Camilo Villamil, Edward Richmond

Abstract
. Folding subgroups give a way to realize non-simply-laced Coxeter groups as subgroups of simply-laced Coxeter groups. In this paper, we study how folding subgroups of finite and affine type are distributed length-wise by calculating the length generating function of the subgroup with respect the length of the ambient group. These generating functions have surprisingly nice formulas in terms of q q -integers and give rise to interesting combinatorial identities on polynomials involving length statistics of both the ambient group and folding subgroup.



1.
Introduction

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system and let
\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denote its length function. Let
\widehat{S}
be a set partition of
S
such that for each
I\in\widehat{S}
, the parabolic subgroup
W_{I}=\langle s\ |\ s\in I\rangle
is a finite subgroup. Let
r_{I}
denote the maximal length element in
W_{I}
and define
\widehat{W}
as the subgroup of
W
generated by
R:=\{r_{I}\ |\ I\in\tilde{S}\}
.
We say the set partition
\widehat{S}
is
admissible
if for every
w\in\widehat{W}
and
I\in\widehat{S}
, we have either
\ell(ws)=\ell(w)+1
for each
s\in I
or
\ell(ws)=\ell(w)-1
for each
s\in I
. The following theorem is proved by Mühlherr in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Mühlherr, 1992)
.

Let
(W,S)
be as above and let
\widehat{S}
be an admissible partition of
S
. Then the pair
(\widehat{W},R)
is a Coxeter system.

We call the Coxeter subgroup
\widehat{W}
from Theorem
1.1
a folding subgroup of
W
. Folding subgroups appear naturally in the study of simple Lie groups and their associated Weyl groups. For example, the natural embedding of the symplectic group into the special linear group
Sp(2n)\subset SL(2n)
induces a folded embedding of the Weyl group of
Sp(2n)
into the Weyl group of
SL(2n)
.
The term “folding” comes from the way the set partition
\widehat{S}
collects vertices in the Coxeter graph of
W
to obtain the Coxeter graph of
\widehat{W}
. The folding of Coxeter groups is a natural consequence of the folding of root systems which were described by Steinberg in
[
8
]
for finite crystallographic root systems and later by Lusztig for general finite root systems in
[
5
]
.

The folding process establishes a relation between the canonical generators of both
\widehat{W}
and
W
. Since any element
w\in\widehat{W}
can be expressed as a word in
R-
generators and in
S-
generators, we distinguish these expressions by considering the embedding map

\phi:\widehat{W}\rightarrow W.

If
w
is an expression in the generators in
R
, then
\phi(w)
will denote the corresponding expression in the generators in
S
. We call
\phi(w)
the
unfolding
of
w
. We study how folding subgroups are distributed by length in their ambient group. To measure this distribution, we make the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
W
be a Coxeter group with folding subgroup
\widehat{W}
. Define the
unfolding series

U_{\widehat{W}}^{W}(q):=\sum_{w\in\widehat{W}}q^{\ell(\phi(w))}.

If
\widehat{W}
is finite, then
U_{\widehat{W}}^{W}(q)
is the
unfolding polynomial
.

We focus on folding subgroups of finite and affine type and discuss each type separately in the following two subsections.

Unfolding polynomials studied in this paper have a close connection with other distribution functions for Coxeter groups. In
[
7
]
, Reiner studies the
q
-Eulerian distribution for general Coxeter groups. As part of his work, he calculates a certain distribution involving the frequency certain generators appearing in reduced words (see Proposition
4.1
). We show in Section
4
, that some unfolding polynomials can be realized a specialization of these distributions (in fact, they play a critical role in the proof of Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
).

We remark that admissible set partitions and folding subgroups extend beyond the cases of finite and affine Coxeter groups. As far as the authors know, unfolding polynomials are largely unknown for general folding subgroups. Several examples of admissible partitions beyond the finite and affine types are showcased by Elias and Heng in
[
4
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
W
be a
Coxeter group
with simple generating set
S
of rank
n
. Specifically,
W
is generated by
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
with relations of the form

(s_{i}s_{j})^{m(s_{i},s_{j})}=e

for some value
m(s_{i},s_{j})\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{\infty\}
where
m(s_{i},s_{j})=1
if and only if
i=j
. Each Coxeter group
W
is equipped with a
length function

\ell:W\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
where
\ell(w)
denotes the minimum number of generators in
S
needed to express
w
. Such a minimum-length expression is called a
reduced expression
for
w
. The
Bruhat partial order

\leq
on
W
is defined as follows: For
v,w\in W
, we define
v\leq w
if every reduced expression for
w
has a subword that is a reduced expression for
v
. As a consequence,
v<w
implies
\ell(v)<\ell(w)
and hence the Bruhat order respects length.

For any
J\subseteq S
, let
W_{J}=\langle J\rangle
denote the corresponding parabolic subgroup of
W
generated by
J
. Let
W^{J}
denote the set of minimal length coset representatives of
W/W_{J}
. Equivalently, we have

W^{J}=\{w\in W:ws>w\text{ for all }s\in J\}.

Every
w\in W
has a unique factorization
w=w^{J}\cdot w_{J}
such that
w^{J}\in W^{J}
and
w_{J}\in W_{J}
and
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{J})+\ell(w_{J})
(see
[
1
]
). This factorization is called the
parabolic decomposition of
w
with respect to
J
.
The fact that parabolic decompositions are length preserving implies the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
J\subseteq S
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in W}q^{\ell(w)}=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{v\in W^{J}}q^{\ell(v)}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{u\in W_{J}}q^{\ell(u)}\right).

2.1.
Folding subgroups and parabolic decompositions

Let
\widehat{W}
be a folding subgroup of
W
corresponding to an admissible set partition
\widehat{S}
of
S
. The corresponding partition function gives a map
\pi:S\rightarrow R
where
R
denotes the generators of
\widehat{W}
.
In particular, we have that
\pi(s)=r
if and only if
s\leq\phi(r)
where
\phi:\widehat{W}\rightarrow W
denotes the embedding homomorphism. The following properties are proved by Mühlherr in
[
6
]
and were later (independently) proved in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\widehat{W}
be a folding subgroup with the notation above and let
w=r_{i_{1}}\cdots r_{i_{k}}\in\widehat{W}
be a reduced expression in the generators of
R
. Then the following are true:

(1)

\displaystyle\ell(\phi(w))=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\ell(\phi(r_{i_{j}})).

(2)

Let
s\in\pi^{-1}(r)
. Then
w\cdot r<w
in
\widehat{W}
if and only if
\phi(w)\cdot s<\phi(w)
in
W
.

The primary tool we use to prove Theorem
1.3
is to show that the unfolding polynomial respects the factorization of the Poincaré polynomial in Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\widehat{J}\subseteq R
and define

J:=\{s\in\pi^{-1}(r):r\in\widehat{J}\}\subseteq S.

Consider the parabolic subgroups
\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}
and
W_{J}
of
\widehat{W}
and
W
, respectively. Then the following are true:

(1)

If
w\in\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}
, then
\phi(w)\in W_{J}
.

(2)

If
w\in\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
, then
\phi(w)\in W^{J}
.

Proof.

Part 1 follows immediately from the definition of
J
. To prove part 2, let
w\in\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
and suppose
r
is right descent of
w
(i.e.
w\cdot r<w
). Then
r\notin\widehat{J}
and, by Lemma
2.2
,
\phi(w)\cdot s<\phi(w)
for all
s\in\pi^{-1}(r)
. Since
r\notin\tilde{J}
, we have
s\notin J
and thus
\phi(w)\in W^{J}
.

∎

One consequence of Proposition
2.3
is that if
w=vu
is a parabolic decomposition with respect to
\widehat{J}
in
\widehat{W}
, then
\phi(w)=\phi(v)\phi(u)
is a parabolic decomposition with respect to
J
in
W
. For any
K\subseteq\widehat{W}
, we define the unfolding series of
K
in
W
as

U_{K}^{W}(q):=\sum_{w\in K}q^{\ell(\phi(w))}.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\widehat{W}
be a folding subgroup of
W
with
\widehat{J}\subset R
and
J\subset S
as in Proposition
2.3
. Then

U_{\widehat{W}}^{W}(q)=U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{W}(q)\cdot U_{\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}}^{W_{J}}(q)

Proof.

The corollary follows from Propositions
2.1
and
2.3
.
∎

3.
Coxeter foldings of finite type

In this section, we present a proof of Theorem
1.3
by inducting on the formula found in Corollary
2.4
through a careful choice of
\widehat{J}\subset R
. For parts (1)-(3) in Theorem
1.3
, we find an explicit formula for
U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{W}(q)
and observe that the second factor
U_{\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}}^{W_{J}}(q)
belongs to a lower rank case of the same calculation. For parts (4) and (5) of Theorem
1.3
, we find explicit formulas for both
U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{W}(q)
and
U_{\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}}^{W}(q)
.

3.1.
Classical foldings

Since the folding subgroup
\widehat{W}
is of type
B_{n}
for each classical folding, we start by describing the parabolic subgroup
\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}
and the respective system of minimal coset representatives
\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
. Then we will describe their respective unfolding in the group
W
for each type:
A_{2n-1}
,
A_{2n}
, and
D_{n+1}
.

Write
\widehat{W}=B_{n}
with generating set
R=\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\}
. The Coxeter relations on
B_{n}
are given by

(r_{i}r_{i+1})^{3}=(r_{n-1}r_{n})^{4}=e\quad\text{for $1\leq i<n-1$}\quad\text{and}\quad(r_{i}r_{j})^{2}=e\quad\text{otherwise.}

Let
\widehat{J}=R\setminus\{r_{1}\}
. Then the parabolic subgroup
\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}
of
\widehat{W}=B_{n}
generated by
\widehat{J}
is isomorphic to a Coxeter group of type
B_{n-1}
. We write
\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}=B_{n-1}
. The next lemma gives an explicit description of
\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
. We leave the proof as a simple exercise.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
. If
u\neq e
, then
u
is one of the following:

•

r_{i}r_{i-1}\cdots r_{2}r_{1}
, for some
1\leq i\leq n

•

r_{i}r_{i+1}\cdots r_{n-1}r_{n}r_{n-1}\cdots r_{2}r_{1}
, for some
1\leq i<n
.

Proof of Thoerem
1.3
parts (1)-(3).

For part (1), let
W=A_{2n-1}
with generating set
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{2n-1}\}
and consider the folded embedding
\phi:B_{n}\rightarrow A_{2n-1}
given by

\phi(r_{i})=s_{i}s_{2n-i}\ \text{for $i<n$}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(r_{n})=s_{n}.

Since
\pi^{-1}(r_{1})=\{s_{1},s_{2n-1}\}
, we let
J=S\setminus\{s_{1},s_{2n-1}\}
as in Proposition
2.3
. The parabolic subgroup
W_{J}
is isomorphic to
A_{2n-3}
and Corollary
2.4
says that

U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)=U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)\cdot U_{B_{n-1}}^{A_{2n-3}}(q).

Lemma
3.1
implies

U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q^{2i}+\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}q^{2i-3}=[2n-1]_{-q}[2n]_{q}.

By induction, we have

U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)=[2]_{q}[3]_{-q}[4]_{q}[5]_{-q}\cdots[2n-1]_{-q}[2n]_{q}

which proves part (1).

For part (2), let
W=A_{2n}
and consider the folded embedding
\phi:B_{n}\rightarrow A_{2n}
given by

\phi(r_{i})=s_{i}s_{2n-i+1}\ \text{for $i<n$}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(r_{n})=s_{n}s_{n+1}s_{n}.

Since
\pi^{-1}(r_{1})=\{s_{1},s_{2n}\}
, we let
J=S\setminus\{s_{1},s_{2n}\}
as in Proposition
2.3
. The parabolic subgroup
W_{J}
is isomorphic to
A_{2n-2}
and Corollary
2.4
implies

U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n}}(q)=U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{2n}}(q)\cdot U_{B_{n-1}}^{A_{2n-2}}(q).

Using Lemma
3.1
, we get

U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{2n}}(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q^{2i}+\sum_{i=n}^{2n-1}q^{2i+1}=[2n]_{q}[2n+1]_{-q}.

Again by induction, we have

U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n}}(q)=[2]_{q}[3]_{-q}[4]_{q}[5]_{-q}\cdots[2n-1]_{-q}[2n]_{q}[2n+1]_{-q}.

This completes the proof of part (2).

For part (3) of Theorem
1.3
, write
W=D_{n+1}
with generators
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n+1}\}
and consider the folded embedding
\phi:B_{n}\rightarrow D_{n+1}
given by

\phi(r_{i})=s_{i}\ \text{for $i<n$}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi(r_{n})=s_{n}s_{n+1}.

First note that if
n=2
, then the folded embedding
\phi:B_{2}\rightarrow D_{3}
is equivalent to
\phi:B_{2}\rightarrow A_{3}
as in part (1) and hence

U_{B_{2}}^{D_{3}}(q)=U_{B_{2}}^{A_{3}}(q)=[2]_{q}[3]_{-q}[4].

Now assume
n\geq 3
. Since
\pi^{-1}(r_{1})=\{s_{1}\}
, we let
J=S\setminus\{s_{1}\}
as in Proposition
2.3
. Then the subgroup
W_{J}
of
D_{n+1}
generated by
J
is isomorphic to
D_{n}
and write
W_{J}=D_{n}
. Corollary
2.4
says that

U_{B_{n}}^{D_{n+1}}(q)=U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{D_{n+1}}(q)\cdot U_{B_{n-1}}^{D_{n}}(q)

and Lemma
3.1
yields

U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{D_{n+1}}(q)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq 2n\\
i\neq n\end{subarray}}q^{i}=[2n+1]_{q}-q^{n}.

By induction, we have

U_{B_{n}}^{D_{n+1}}(q)=[2]_{q}[3]_{-q}[4]\prod_{k=3}^{n}\left([2k+1]_{q}-q^{k}\right).

∎

With the formulas for
U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)
and
U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n}}(q)
, we can prove Corollary
1.5
. Note that for the full rank function on the groups
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
, we have

A_{n}(q):=\sum_{w\in A_{n}}q^{\ell_{A}(w)}=[1]_{q}[2]_{q}\cdots[n]_{q}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n}(q):=\sum_{w\in B_{n}}q^{\ell_{B}(w)}=[2]_{q}[4]_{q}\cdots[2n]_{q}.

Corollary
1.5
follows from the identities

B_{n}(-q)\cdot U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)=A_{2n-1}(-q)\cdot B_{n}(q)\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n}(-q)\cdot U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n}}(q)=A_{2n}(-q)\cdot B_{n}(q).

3.2.
Lusztig foldings

The dihedral group
\widehat{W}=I_{2}(n+1)
is generated by
R=\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
subject to the Coxeter relations

r_{1}^{2}=r_{2}^{2}=(r_{1}r_{2})^{n+1}=e.

Consider the embedding
\phi:I_{2}(n+1)\to A_{n}
given by

\displaystyle\phi(r_{1})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in S\\
i\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}s_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\phi(r_{2})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{i}\in S\\
i\text{ even}\end{subarray}}s_{i}

where
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
denote the generators of
A_{n}
.

Proof of Thoerem
1.3
parts (4) and (5).

Let
\widehat{J}=\{r_{2}\}
. Then the parabolic subgroup
\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}=\{e,r_{2}\}
and the set of minimal length coset representatives

\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
consists of the identity element and all expressions
\underset{j}{\underbrace{\ldots r_{2}r_{1}r_{2}r_{1}}}
for some
1\leq j\leq n
. In particular,
\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}
contains a unique element of length
j
for every
0\leq j\leq n
.
Let
J=\{s_{i}\ |\ i\text{ is even}\}
. Then by Corollary
2.4
,

U_{I_{2}(n+1)}^{A_{n}}(q)=U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{n}}(q)\cdot U_{\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}}^{W_{J}}(q).

Suppose
n
is even and write
n=2m
. Then
\ell_{A}(\phi(r_{1}))=\ell_{A}(\phi(r_{2}))=m
and hence

U_{\tilde{W}_{\tilde{J}}}^{W_{J}}(q)=1+q^{m}=[2]_{q^{m}}

and

U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{n}}(q)=1+q^{m}+q^{2m}+\cdots+q^{nm}=[n+1]_{q^{m}}.

Otherwise, suppose
n
is odd and write
n=2m-1
. Then
\ell_{A}(\phi(r_{1}))=m
and
\ell_{A}(\phi(r_{2}))=m-1
. This implies

U_{\widehat{W}_{\widehat{J}}}^{W_{J}}(q)=1+q^{m-1}=[2]_{q^{m-1}}

and

U_{\widehat{W}^{\widehat{J}}}^{A_{n}}(q)=(1+q^{m})(1+q^{n}+q^{2n}+\cdots+q^{(m-1)n})=[2]_{q^{m}}\cdot[m]_{q^{n}}.

These calculations imply parts (4) and part (5) of Theorem
1.3
which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Coxeter foldings of affine type

In this section we prove Theorems
1.7
,
1.8
, and
1.9
on the unfolding series for irreducible Coxeter groups of affine type. Unlike the finite case, we do not apply Corollary
2.4
in our calculations (although Corollary
2.4
holds for affine foldings). We first show that Theorem
1.7
follows from a direct calculation based on the Poincaré series of affine type A. For Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
we apply a certain distribution formula described by Reiner in
[
7
]
(see Proposition
4.1
).

4.1.
The unfolding of
\widetilde{A}_{n-1}

For any
m\geq 1
, we consider the embedding
\phi:\widetilde{A}_{n-1}\rightarrow\widetilde{A}_{mn-1}
given by

\phi(r_{i})=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}s_{i+jn}

where
R=\{r_{0},\ldots,r_{n-1}\}
and
S=\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{mn-1}\}
denote the generators of
A_{n-1}
and
\widetilde{A}_{mn-1}
respectively (see Table
6
). We also let
\ell
and
\ell_{\widetilde{A}}
represent the length functions on
\widetilde{A}_{kn-1}
and
\widetilde{A}_{n-1}
, respectively. In
[
2
]
, Bott shows that the Poincaré series of the group
\widetilde{A}_{n-1}
is given by

(1)

\widetilde{A}_{n-1}(q)=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{A}_{n-1}}q^{\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(w)}=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{[k]_{q}}{1-q^{(k-1)}}

Note that
\ell(\phi(r))=m\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(r)
for any
r\in R
and therefore Lemma
2.2
implies
\ell(\phi(w))=m\ell_{\widetilde{A}_{n-1}}(w)
for any
w\in\widetilde{A}_{n-1}
. Substituting
q\mapsto q^{m}
in Equation (
1
) gives

U_{\widetilde{A}_{n-1}}^{A_{mn-1}}(q)=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{A}_{n-1}}q^{\ell(\phi(w))}=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{A}_{n-1}}q^{m\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(w)}=\widetilde{A}_{n-1}(q^{m})=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{[k]_{q^{m}}}{1-q^{(k-1)m}}.

This proves Theorem
1.7
.

(resp.

) in type
\widetilde{B}_{n}
(resp.

w\in\widetilde{B}_{n}

denote then number of times
r_{0}
appears in a reduced word of
w

w\in\widetilde{C}_{n}

r_{C}(w)
and

denote the number of times
r_{0}
and
r_{n}
appear in a reduced word of
w
respectively. The following formulas appear in
[
7
]
where Reiner studies
q
-Eulerian distribution functions on Coxeter groups.

Proposition 4.1

(Reiner, 1992)
.

Denote the
q
-Pochhammer symbol by

(x;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k}x).

Then the following formulas hold:

(2)

\widetilde{B}_{n}(a,q):=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}a^{r_{B}(w)}q^{\ell_{\widetilde{B}}(w)}=\dfrac{(-aq;q)_{n}(-q;q)_{n-1}[n]_{q}!}{(aq^{n};q)_{n}}

(3)

\widetilde{C}_{n}(a,b,q):=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{C}_{n}}a^{r_{C}(w)}b^{s_{C}(w)}q^{\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)}=\dfrac{(-aq;q)_{n}(-bq;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!}{(abq^{n+1};q)_{n}}

a\mapsto q
and
q\mapsto q^{2}
in Equation (
2
) yields

U_{\widetilde{B}_{n}}^{\widetilde{D}_{2n+1}}(q)=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}q^{\ell_{\widetilde{D}}(\phi(w))}=\sum_{w\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}q^{r(w)}q^{2\ell_{\widetilde{B}}(w)}=\widetilde{B}_{n}(q,q^{2})=\dfrac{(-q^{3};q^{2})_{n}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{n-1}[n]_{q^{2}}!}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{n}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

The proof of Theorem
1.9
follows the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
1.8
. In this case, we apply Equation (
3
) to each of the seven foldings of
\widetilde{C}_{n}
given in Table
8
. Below, we list the corresponding relationships between length functions and leave the details of the calculations as an exercise.

(1)

\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(\phi(w))=2\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)+r(w)+s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{A}_{2n+1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(q,q,q^{2})

(2)

\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(\phi(w))=2\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)+r(w)-s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{A}_{2n}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(q,q^{-1},q^{2})

(3)

\ell_{\widetilde{A}}(\phi(w))=2\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)-r(w)-s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{A}_{2n-1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(q^{-1},q^{-1},q^{2})

(4)

\ell_{\widetilde{B}}(\phi(w))=\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)+s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{B}_{n+1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(1,q,q)

(5)

\ell_{\widetilde{D}}(\phi(w))=\ell_{\widetilde{C}}(w)+r(w)+s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{D}_{n+2}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(q,q,q)

(6)

\ell_{\widetilde{C}_{2n+1}}(\phi(w))=2\ell_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}(w)+s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{C}_{2n+1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(1,q,q^{2})

(7)

\ell_{C_{2n}}(\phi(w))=2\ell_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}(w)-s(w)
and
U_{\widetilde{C}_{n}}^{\widetilde{C}_{2n}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(1,q^{-1},q^{2})

∎

Remark 4.2
.

If we set
a=0
in Equation (
3
), then we recover the unfolding polynomials of finite type from Theorem
1.3
parts (1)-(3). In particular, we have

U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n-1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(0,q,q^{2}),\quad U_{B_{n}}^{A_{2n}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(0,q^{-1},q^{2}),\quad U_{B_{n}}^{D_{n+1}}(q)=\widetilde{C}_{n}(0,q,q).

More generally,

\widetilde{C}_{n}(0,t,q)=\sum_{w\in B_{n}}t^{s_{B}(w)}q^{\ell_{B}(w)}=(-tq;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!

recovers a well known formula for the
(t,q)
-distribution for the number of signs and inversions on signed permutations.
