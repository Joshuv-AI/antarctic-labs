Project Overview

Many pulsed-power and high-energy-density plasma simulations must track particle distributions
across physical space, velocity, time, and uncertain parameters. The number of values in a conven-
tional grid then grows so rapidly that otherwise desirable kinetic models become too expensive to
store or solve. The PULSE-TN tri-lab project investigated tensor networks, which replace a very
large multidimensional array with a compact set of connected factors when the problem contains
suitable low-rank structure. The Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) branch of the
project led work on performance-portable tensor software, adaptive solvers, uncertainty quantifi-
cation, tensor completion, and collisional plasma physics. Sandia National Laboratories advanced
their application-code infrastructure and high-order discretizations, contributing complementary
end-to-end demonstrations of challenging realistic benchmark problems
LLNL released BoBa Guthrey et al. 2026, an open-source library that supplies tensor formats, nu-
merical building blocks, and specialized solvers on modern heterogenous architectures. BoBa was
connected to Sandia’s Camellia finite-element software Roberts 2014, 2019 and used to develop a
block low-rank solver for the structured systems produced by stable high-order plasma discretiza-
tions. LLNL also demonstrated high-dimensional uncertainty quantification without constructing
the full parameter array, developed methods for reconstructing and querying incomplete count
data, and created adaptive compressed solvers for stiff collisional kinetic equations. The collision
studies preserved important physical quantities and showed that collisions can suppress fine-scale
filamentation and limit rank growth, so adding needed physics can sometimes improve rather than
degrade compression - a very interesting result.
Once the BoBa code was fully integrated into Sandia’s application code, Sandia demonstrated
direct compressed operator assembly for robust high order discretizations. This allowed achieve-
ments establish reusable software and algorithms while also identifying the remaining challenges:
a fully coupled, production-scale, low-rank Vlasov–Maxwell solver with collisions and relativity
still requires integration of the collisional physics solvers into the Vlasov discretization as well as
continued development of relativistic plasma modeling capabilities.

Mission Impact

This research gives LLNL a performance-portable capability for exploring high-dimensional kinetic,
transport, and uncertainty problems on advanced computing platforms, including the El Capitan
architectures. The methods can reduce memory and time-to-solution for selected models while
retaining kinetic detail needed to simulate high energy density science applications, strengthening
the scientific computing base used to maintain the safety, security, and effectiveness of the United
States nuclear stockpile and to develop science and technology capabilities for future national-
security challenges. The open-source BoBa library, its integration into the Camellia code, and
