Self-supervised cross frame-rate event-frame depth learning without ground truth
Authors: Arun Kumar Arigela, D. David Neels Ponkumar, Martin Margala, K. Jayasri, A. Leela Sravanthi, Prasun Chakrabarti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Event-based cameras provide high-temporal-resolution motion information and wide dynamic range, making them suitable for depth estimation in challenging dynamic environments. However, existing depth learning approaches often rely on ground-truth supervision, handcrafted event representations, or computationally expensive fusion strategies, limiting scalability and real-time deployment. Recent self-supervised methods partially address annotation dependency but struggle with cross-frame-rate alignment, temporal instability, and inefficient fusion of event and frame modalities. To overcome these limitations, this study proposes a novel Self-Supervised Event–Frame Self-Attention Transformer (EF-SAT) framework for depth estimation without ground-truth depth supervision. The proposed method employs a Vision Transformer-based encoder to jointly model event streams and intensity frames, while a cross-frame-rate self-attention mechanism captures spatiotemporal dependencies across heterogeneous data rates. The framework further carries revolutionary self-sampling based primarily on bilinear interpolation, timescale-consistent intensity decoding, and ego-motion optimization that uses absolute trajectory error (ATE) and relative pose error (RPE) constraints to improve geometric balances in successive frames. Depth and pose are learned using photometric reconstruction, event–frame alignment, temporal consistency, and smoothness constraints in a fully self-supervised manner. Experiments conducted on the DAVIS-240 C event–frame dataset demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves superior performance, attaining an Absolute Relative Error of 0.114 and RMSE of 0.163, outperforming state-of-the-art deep learning baselines. Overall, the proposed EF-SAT model demonstrates robust, temporally stable, and accurate depth estimation in dynamic scenes, highlighting its potential for real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and event-driven perception systems.

Introduction

The correct recognition of the structure of a three-dimensional scene is an essential requirement in computer vision tasks like autonomous navigation, robotics and augmented reality1. Conventional methods of depth estimation use frame-based cameras which record intensity images in constant frame rates. Nevertheless, these traditional sensors have automatic constraints in tough environments, such as high motion rate, low light, and an environment with sudden change of illumination2,3. Conversely, event cameras deliver asynchronous spatial-temporal data recording the change of pixel intensities in the resolution of microseconds4. The characteristic feature makes it possible to capture fast dynamic scenes with minimum motion blur and maximum temporal fidelity5. Combining data of event cameras with low-rate intensity images has become one of the most promising approaches to address the limitations of regular cameras, which can enable dense depth estimation, and maintain a temporal consistency between frames6. Although event-frame fusion has a potential, there are a number of challenges that remain in the existing methodologies7. Traditionally, early methods of depth estimation made use of supervised learning models which needed extensive ground-truth depth annotations, which are costly and time-intensive to acquire8.

Recent study has explored into the self-supervised learning models to reduce the reliance on labelled datasets, using photometric consistency and cross-modal losses on alignment9. CNN-based systems have been widely used in the depth prediction of images and events, but have been known to have weaknesses in capturing spatial and temporal dependencies of long-range, especially in an asynchronous stream of events10. Models with self-attention mechanisms built on transformers offer a compelling alternative to model global correlations among multi-modality and multi-temporal data11. However, current transformer methods of depth estimation have been highly one-sided either in image sequence or event stream and have made no major use of cross-frame-rate fusion between an asynchronous event stream and a low-frame-rate image stream12. The gap shows that there is the need to have a unified model that is capable of acquiring feature-rich spatial-temporal representations using a combination of multimodal in a self-supervised fashion, and that can produce accurate depth estimates without the use of ground-truth depth labels. The proposed paper presents a self-supervised EF-SAT dense depth estimation, that is, a fusion between asynchronous streams of events and low-frame-rate intensity images. Through the cross-frame-rate self-attention, the framework is able to capture the long-range spatial-temporal dependence and therefore prediction of depth is obtained accurately even without ground-truth annotations.

Recent innovation and its limits

Recent depth estimation progress has investigated self-supervised architecture, event-camera fusion and transformer-based architecture. Event cameras deliver asynchronous data of high temporal resolution, which makes it possible to have motion-robust perception of dynamic scenes13. Self-supervised methods have minimized the use of labelled depth maps by using photometric consistency and cross-modal alignment. Transformer models have been shown to be superior in the capability of capturing the long-range dependencies when compared with convolutional networks. Nonetheless, the existing innovations have significant drawbacks. The current transformer-based approaches tend to treat each single modality separately, and make poor use of cross-modal information between events and frames14. Furthermore, the majority of methods are limited to regular frame rates, which prevents their use in asynchronous sensor data tasks. The limited feature fusion mechanisms and sparse representations of events are impediments to accurate depth reconstruction especially in complicated dynamic environments. These difficulties lead to the need to have a single framework which can cross-frame-rate fuse to achieve proper self-supervised depth learning.

Problem statement

The precise estimation of depth is also a basic issue in dynamic and complicated settings and the use of classical frame-based cameras. Traditional frame based techniques are affected by motion blur, poor light sensitivity and performance loss when there is a sudden change in illumination15. Even though event cameras have excellent temporal resolution and resistance to changes in light, due to their sparsity and asynchronous nature, direct depth information cannot be easily inferred. The current deep learning models are mostly either single-modality based or are based on CNN-based models which do not fully capture long-range spatial-temporal interactions between event-frame data16. Moreover, the majority of approaches assume equal frame rates and rely on supervised learning that requires expensive and hard to obtain ground-truth depth labels. These are scalability and practicality limitations. In order to overcome these issues, this study presents a self-supervised EF-SAT that is able to effectively merge asynchronous event streams and the low-frame-rate intensity images that can densely estimate depth without the need to use ground-truth depth labels.

Research motivation

The rationale behind the current research is the need to eliminate the shortcomings of the current depth estimation methods in dynamic and low-light conditions. With the combination of asynchronous event streams and low-frame rate intensity images, the proposed framework will help capture rich spatial-temporal representations. The use of self-supervised learning methods removes the need to use expensive ground-truth depth annotations, and the transformer-based self-attention allows effective cross-modal and time-scale fusion. The aim of this approach is to give correct and real-time depth information of robotics and autonomous navigation systems.

Research significance

This study makes a contribution to the field of depth estimation by proposing a single unified, self-supervised framework to combine event and frame information. The developed EF-SAT model overcomes the shortcomings of traditional CNN and single-modality transformer-based methods enabling the model to provide robust results through dynamic, low light, and high-speed conditions. It is able to remove the need to use ground-truth depth annotations, thereby lowering the cost of data acquisition and improving scalability. The method has a great potential in autonomous systems, robotics, and real-time vision application that needs precise and reliable depth perception.

Related works

Event-based video reconstruction and representation learning

Lu et al.17 suggest a self-supervised system to deal with rolling shutter artifacts and low frame rate with conventional cameras by reconstructing high-frame-rate video with event cameras. The aim of the study is to retrieve distortion-free slow-motion videos with minimum bandwidth without relying on large scale real-world supervised datasets. The authors present a representation of displacement fields that represents dense and non-linear spatiotemporal motions of pixels in case of exposure, with which rolling shutter and global shutter images can be mutually reconstructed. The framework combines rolling shutter- global shutter mapping with inverse global shutter- rolling shutter reconstruction and rolling shutter warping to obtain self-supervision. Experiments using four datasets and user experiments show that removing distortions are successful, bandwidth is reduced by 94% and inference can occur in 16 ms per frame. Nonetheless, its major limitation is that it has not been quantitatively validated on real-world data, with experiments mainly done on simulated data, which is a limitation to real-world applicability.

Qu et al.16 introduce a self-supervised self-data-driven data-driven approach to enhance event-stream representations to event-based vision tasks that overcomes the drawbacks of noise-sensitive manual representations. The aim of the study is to improve the quality and strength of event-stream representation in order to be able to apply to the usual learning models more efficiently. The authors introduce EvRep, a spatiotemporal statistical representation, and a self-supervised representation generator, RepGen that leverages the theoretical link of asynchronous event-streams with synchronous APS frame to generate polished representations called EvRepSL. Comprehensive testing on event-based classification and optical flow datasets shows significant improvement in performances compared to the current techniques and good generalizations to various event cameras and tasks. Nevertheless, the main limitation is that the performance of noise reduction is limited by the challenge of acquiring correct event-wise ground truth in practice.

Self-supervised event-based depth estimation

To address the shortcomings of using expensive and time-intensive depth annotations, Zhu et al.18 suggest a self-supervised event-based monocular depth estimation that they refer to as EMoDepth. The idea of the study is to make proper depth estimation in high-speed and problematic light scenes when the regular cameras have a negative performance. The suggested approach takes advantage of cross-modal consistency between asynchronous events and pixel-aligned intensity frames during training and uses only events at inference time. Multiscale skip-connection architecture is an architecture that is meant to combine features and ensure high-inference speeds. The experiments that are carried out on the MVSEC and the DSEC datasets indicate that EMoDepth has a state of art performance that is superior to the supervised event-based and unsupervised frame-based approaches. Nevertheless, the major limitation is low utilization of dynamic scene information, since the management of moving objects and creation of smaller yet more informative event representations are issues.

Xiao et al.15 introduce a multimodal monocular dense depth estimation system to address the drawbacks of frame cameras in abnormal light circumstances. Research is intended to enhance the accuracy of depth prediction methods by making good use of the complementary advantages of event cameras and traditional frame data. The presented approach utilizes the transformer-based design that comprises of a multimodal encoder and a joint decoder, in which self-attention networks learn local and global relationships between frame patches and event tensors, and then integrate multi-scale features to provide slow-scale depth reconstruction. The results of the experiments conducted with the Event Scape and MVSEC data sets indicate that the approach has the state-of-the-art performance, and it is significantly better than the existing one. The major disadvantage is though that compared to transformer-based multimodal fusion, there is a higher computational complexity that can restrict its application in real time in resource-constrained systems.

Zhang et al.19 suggest a depth estimation system that is energy-efficient to deal with the issue of event-camera data, such as non-standard spiking outputs and small labelled datasets. The objective of the study is to obtain correct depth estimation with low computational and energy expenses to resource-limited systems. To overcome data scarcity, the authors present a Spike-Driven Transformer Network (SDT) that has spike-based attention and residual mechanisms, a multi-stage fusion depth head, and cross-modality knowledge distillation based on a pre-trained vision foundation model. Experiment results on both synthetic and real-world event data have demonstrated strong performance improvement, such as a 49% decrease in absolute relative error, 39.77% decrease in squared relative error, 70.2% decrease in energy usage and 42.4% decrease in model parameters. Nevertheless, there is a potential limitation of validation being limited to a smaller set of datasets and platforms, and the implementation on specialized spiking devices and larger real-world validation remains not implemented.

Duan et al.20 discuss the drawbacks of monocular depth estimation in dynamic scenes and difficult lighting environments by capitalizing on the defining advantages of event cameras and traditional frame cameras. The research objective is to enhance the accuracy and sensitivity of depth estimation by using good multimodal fusion. The authors introduce a new Coordinate Attention Gated Recurrent Unit (CAGRU), comprising of coordinate attention as an attention gate with a traditional convolutional gate to learn spatial, temporal, and inter-channel dependencies altogether. The design increases the information density of sparse event data, and allows effective frame information fusion with features. Experimental tests on various publicly available data sets show that the performance is significantly better than the current methods. Nevertheless, the major limitation is the use of synthetic data, which does not perfectly match the real-world data distributions and can cause bias and restrict generalization in the context of practical applications.

Event–frame calibration and motion analysis

Hu et al.21 examine the calibration of event-frame stereo camera systems to optimize on the complementary benefits of event cameras and traditional frame cameras. The objective of the research is to obtain the precise and real time dynamic calibration of such hybrid stereo configurations. The authors suggest a dynamic calibration scheme that jointly uses circle-grid pattern recognition, a sliding-window based time matching approach to pairing events with frames and an event warping and synchronization algorithm based on patch-size motion compensation, is computationally efficient. A pose- landmark graph is thereafter formed to optimize global pose between two cameras. The experimental findings on self-recorded datasets indicate that it has high calibration accuracy, good real-time performance and can be deployed in a practical manner. Nevertheless, the method has less robustness in difficult environments like the presence of strongly noisy scenes, overloaded events or even non parallel planes of pixels between the two cameras, and this can impact the calibration stability and accuracy.

By using bio-inspired event cameras, Zhao et al.22 solve the problems with motion of objects and tracking them in rapidly moving scenarios and with changes in illumination. The goal of the study is to enhance the strength of detection, continuity of tracking, and range estimation of dynamic scenes. The authors suggest a mixed framework, which is the combination of event frames and standard frames as the case of object detection with probabilistic and colour cues, then a detection-based tracking algorithm with event frames and better kernel correlation filter to minimize missed detections. Also, another distance estimation technique which uses event-frame tracking and the similar triangle theory is presented. The experimental results prove the successful moving object detection, dependable track performance, and the distance measurement accuracy. Nevertheless, one of the major weaknesses is the quality of event-frame filtering and the capability to resist any complex changes in illumination because noise and adverse lighting conditions may deteriorate detection and tracking abilities.

Transformer-based spatial–temporal modelling

To overcome the temporal inconsistency and feature misalignment problem in the case of fast moving aerial motion, Yongsheng et al.23, proposed SiamCTCA for UAV tracking. To enhance spatial–temporal feature correspondence between consecutive frames, the study adopted a Cross-Temporal Correlation Aggregation mechanism embedded into a Siamese network, based on the UAV tracking benchmark datasets. The architecture improved the robustness to occlusion, and enhanced motion-aware feature learning for robustness under fast viewpoint variation. The results of experimental evaluation showed that the tracker has high tracking precision and temporal stability than the traditional Siamese tracker. Similarly, Wang et al.24, proposed a multi-channel complex residual network with adaptive sphere-space decision boundaries for embedded RF fingerprint interpretation. The technique used involved complex residual feature extraction and adaptive boundary optimization for increasing the reliability of classification in noisy environments. The experimental results showed the identification accuracy improvement and feature discrimination ability. However, the main tasks of the aforementioned studies were focusing on tracking and/or signal interpretation rather than geometric depth inference, which restricts the direct applicability to self-supervised event-frame depth estimation.

To address these research challenges, the proposed EF-SAT framework proposes cross-frame-rate self-attention fusion to jointly capture spatial geometry and asynchronous motion information and enhances temporal consistency, computational efficiency, and self-supervised depth estimation accuracy.

Proposed event frame self attention transformer framework

The proposed approach introduces a unified self-supervised system of depth estimation which builds upon the complementary nature of event-based and frame-based vision sensors. The system is trained to be depth-agnostic using time-varying information on geometry and consistency of appearances. With synchronized intensity frames and event streams, which have high temporal resolution, as inputs, the framework performs the modality specific preprocessing to provide spatial temporal alignment. Both modalities are converted to patch-based token representations which are augmented with space and time positional encodings. These tokens are further fed into the EF-SAT which is the main learning unit. In EF-SAT, cross frame rate and self-attention on latent features allow the effective combination of frame-based spatial structure with asynchronous information on events. The resulting combined representation of latent represents both scene geometry and motion dynamics. The next step is parallel decoding branches that forecast dense depth maps and relative camera motion that are employed to impose self-supervised learning constraints by view synthesis and cross-modal consistency. In inference, the trained system simply allows generating depth maps on unseen data without any other supervision. This design is end-to-end allowing it to be robust during high velocity motion and adverse illumination, but also be scaled to real-time deployment. Data collection

This study utilizes publicly available event-based vision datasets, which are compiled in the event-based-camera-dataset repository available in GitHub, which contains several benchmark datasets recorded with DAVIS event cameras [25]. These datasets offer synchronized asynchronous event streams and low-frame-rate intensity images, which can be successfully fused with events to produce depth learning. The data set is gathered in a wide range of interior and external conditions, including fast motion and dynamic scenes in combination with different levels of light. Streams of events can document the pixel-level changes of brightness at a microsecond time resolution whereas traditional grayscale pictures can provide complementary structural data. Some sequences also provide ground-truth depth or camera pose, not used to be supervised, but to be evaluated, and this also falls within the paradigm of self-supervised learning followed in this paper. The variability of the motion patterns, geometry of the scene, and light variations in the dataset makes the process of training robust and thorough validation of the suggested Event Frame Self-Attention Transformer in conditions of real scenarios.

Data preprocessing

Effective fusion of the stream of events and intensity frames, preprocessing is necessary to deal with the inherent disparities in temporal resolution, data density, and representation of these two streams. Before the encoding of features and the fusion through transformers, the raw sensor values are converted into aligned and noise-reduced and structurally compatible values. The preprocessing stage provides the temporal coherence between asynchronous and synchronous frames as well as the high-frequency motion information of event cameras. The subsequent subsections outline the preprocessing pipeline, comprising event stream normalization, temporal segmentation, event representation construction, frame preparation, and cross-modal alignment, that find applications in all the phases of enabling stable and meaningful self-supervised learning in the proposed EF-SAT Transformer framework.

Event–frame self-attention transformer layer

The main learning element of the proposed self-supervised depth estimation system is the EF-SAT. It is mainly designed to integrating effectively the complementary information of two basic sensing modalities; namely low-frame-rate intensity images which code the rich spatial appearance and scene structure, and high-temporal-resolution event streams that encode the fine-grained motion dynamics. In contrast to traditional convolutional networks, which use local receptive fields and find it challenging to define long distance dependencies, EF-SAT layer uses self-attention to define more global spatial-temporal relationships between event and frame representations. This feature is necessary especially in-depth estimation without ground-truth guidance where the geometric knowledge is indirectly learned by examining motion and appearance consistency. The EF-SAT layer makes cross-frame-rate interaction modelling, joint encoding of modality-specific cues and the generation of unified latent representations, which underlie the basis of the proposed self-supervised learning strategy, possible, and facilitates effective depth and pose learning by view synthesis and time constraints. The EF-SAT architecture is designed not just to enhance the learning of spatial–temporal features, but also to be computationally efficient. The proposed cross-frame-rate attention mechanism reduces unnecessary attention computations by selectively focusing on the event regions where motion information is important and the frame regions where spatial information is important. Besides, light-weight decoding layers and shared latent feature representations help to reduce parameter complexity and floating-point operations (FLOPs), maintaining the geometric learning capacity while allowing to improve the performance of real-time inference. Event–frame latent feature alignment

The main role of aligning heterogeneous sensory data into unified latent representation that can be used in cross-frame-rate reasoning is played in the proposed self-supervised event-frame depth learning framework, where the EF-SAT encoder acts as the primary element. The tokenized intensity frames and event-based representations in this stage have wide differences in terms of temporal density, spatial sparsity, and information distribution in the model. Whereas intensity frames capture mostly the static appearance and the geometry of a scene, event streams encode the high-frequency motion-related information of capturing the slight alterations of brightness due to camera or object motion. The explicit encoding approach would result in representational imbalance and inefficient interaction of attention were there to be direct fusion of these modalities. This is especially significant in the depth learning via motion parallax because closer objects are likely to produce a more intense and quicker event responses than far-off areas. The encoded features implicitly encode depth relations between features therefore do not use explicit depth annotations.

In the framework of the present research, the EF-SAT encoder is essential in stabilizing self-supervised learning. The richer latent representations that were produced by the encoder enable the model to predict camera motion and scene geometry across time that directly enables the calculation of photometric and event-alignment losses that are utilized during training. The EF-SAT encoder provides a solid base to proper depth estimation in extreme situations, like rapid motion and low-illumination, where the conventional frame-based systems tend to perform poorly due to the joint embedding of spatial layout, temporal dynamics, and information of a particular modality.

Cross-frame-rate spatial–temporal dependency modelling

The cross-frame-rate self-attention mechanism is the major part of the proposed structure that will integrate both the spatial appearance information gained on intensity frames and the high-temporal-resolution motion information gained on event streams. The novelty of EF-SAT is that it can be used to attend simultaneously frame and event tokens, although the two have different temporal properties. The EF-SAT attention in contrast to traditional methods of attention where the attention mechanisms are based on homogeneous stimuli is specifically created to address the intrinsic imbalance between the temporal and representational properties of low-frame-rate images compared to asynchronous events. The attention mechanism in this study allows the model to selectively match event responses caused by motion to the corresponding spatial locations in the frame, which allows the model to infer depth in a self-supervised fashion and using motion parallax. Multi-head self-attention makes the model correlate the motion cues generated by the event with the corresponding regions of the image, which enables the model to infer depth when their frame information is ambiguous. Geometry-aware depth and motion inference

Depth and pose decoding are the last step of prediction in the proposed EF-SAT-based framework, which makes self-supervised learning possible without ground-truth depth maps. The latent representation of the cross-frame-rate self-attention layer is unified and contains the spatial geometry and the motion information of an event represented through event-frame fusion. Nevertheless, this latent representation does not directly give the supervisory signals. Rather, depth and pose decoders convert the latent features to physically meaningful representations dense depth maps and relative camera motion and can then be used to generate novel views, and provide geometric consistency between timestamps. The depth decoder is developed to predict a rich depth map of every single intensity frame through the gradual transformation of the unified latent features into pixel-wise depth estimations. Given the predicted depth \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{D}_{t}$$\end{document} and pose \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{T}}_{t\to\:t+1}$$\end{document}, pixels from frame \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{I}_{t+1}$$\end{document} are projected into the coordinate frame of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{I}_{t}$$\end{document}, enabling reconstruction of the target image. The major self-supervised learning signal is photometric discrepancies between the reconstructed and original frame. This process enables the model to improve the quality of depth and pose estimates by minimizing rebuilding error, and this does not need any ground-truth depth labels. In the framework of this research, depth and pose joint estimation is the key of effective utilization of event frame data. Frames give spatial information that can be used to construct depth, whereas events give accurate temporal motion data which levels off pose estimation. Through the combination of these predictions, the suggested framework will guarantee that the learned depth maps become geometrically consistent through time as well as matched with the observed motion patterns. It leads to the trustworthy depth learning under difficult real-world conditions, such as high-speed motion and low-light conditions, which meets the essential goal of the self-supervised cross-frame-rate event-frame depth estimation. To quantitatively demonstrate the effectiveness of joint depth-pose learning, accuracy of the ego-motion estimation is assessed based on Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE). The ATE metric computes the overall deviation of the global trajectory of the predicted camera motion from the reference camera motion, while the RPE computes the local deviation of the camera motion throughout the frames. The lower the ATE and the RPE values, the more consistent are the geometries and the more reliable the pose estimation is in self-supervised optimization. These metrics are used to ensure the stability of the proposed depth–pose closed loop learning under dynamic motion.

Inference for event–frame depth estimation

The presented framework is based on the inference of dense depth maps and relative camera motion of completely unseen sequences, using the trained Event Frame Self-Attention Transformer in the absence of any additional supervision. Each input consists of a low-frame-rate intensity image \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{I}_{t}$$\end{document}and its temporally aligned event segment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathcal{E}}_{t}$$\end{document}, corresponding to the time interval \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:[{T}_{t},{T}_{t+1})$$\end{document}. These statistics confirm that the loss combination formulation is effective in overseeing the EF-SAT model and facilitates depth estimation reliably in the absence of ground-truth supervision and strengthens the usefulness and effectiveness of the implemented approach.

The ablation experiment analyses the effect of important elements in the proposed EF-SAT framework. The full EF-SAT setup is always outperforming all the reduced versions and proves that the combination of event-frame fusion, transformer-based attention, and self-supervised constraints are necessary to achieve stable and time-synchronous depth estimation.

Discussion

This study shows that the combination of event streams and intensity frames using a self-supervised transformer architecture permits the estimation of depth without ground-truth oversight and makes the estimation of depth accurate and temporally stable. The suggested EF-SAT model is consistent in providing depth predictions in dynamic environments. The success of the EF-SAT can be explained by the fact that it is able to utilize complementary spatial information in frames and high-temporal-resolution motion data in events. This synergy enables the model to deal with high motion, change of illumination and motion blur better than single modality methods. Every element of the framework helps in bringing value to the performance. Motion sensitivity is offered by event-based representations which give up structural context provided by frame-based cues. The self-attention scheme is useful in the feature integration of cross-frames-rate to allow coherent representations of depth. Event streams are discretized during the pre-processing stage but the proposed framework preserves the essential temporal motion patterns using the temporal bin encoding as well as the positional-aware attention learning. While voxelization helps to make the computation more tractable and the optimization of the transformer more stable, some temporal smoothing of extremely high-frequency motions may occur due to quantization over fixed bins. However, such significant spatio-temporal inconsistencies and asynchronies are well maintained for reliable cross-frame-rate depth estimation. Event-only EF-SAT, as compared to frame-only and conventional fusion-based deep learning models, has lower depth errors and higher temporal consistency. Due to transformer-based fusion, global context modelling is possible, which is frequently lacking in CNN-based baselines. The weight distribution of attention shows that the model dynamically lays emphasis on event information in high-motion regions and frame information in static regions that enhances understanding of fusion behaviour. The model is robust to different intensity of motion and this implies that it is stable in response to abrupt changes in a scene which generally causes poor monocular depth estimation. Even though the trained models are trained on one dataset, the modality-agnostic design and self-supervised formulation is promising of generalizing to non-homogeneous environments. In general, EF-SAT provides a good paradigm of self-supervised learning of event-frame depth. However, performance may be affected by extreme event noise, and large-scale cross-dataset validation remains a direction for future work.

Conclusion and future work

The proposed study introduced a self-supervised event-frame depth learning model, which is based on an EF-SAT. The suggested method is successful in combining both asynchronous streams of events and synchronous intensity frames to teach depth representations without ground-truth supervision. The framework overcomes issues related to motion blur, variation in illumination and sparse temporal sampling when performing depth estimation in traditional frame-based depth estimation, by exploiting the cross-frame-rate self-attention. The outcomes of the experiments prove that the suggested EF-SAT model can be used to obtain consistent predictions of depth in a dynamic scene with a high degree of temporal consistency. The model is efficient in preparing event-based motion cues to boost the level of depth estimates especially where there is rapid motion. Quantitative and qualitative results indicate that the current deep learning-based depth estimates use better performance than the existing deep learning-based depth estimates. The main findings in this research are the development of an integrated self-supervised learning framework that aims to learn event-frame depth, the presentation of a transformer-based cross-frame-rate attention system, and the proof of the practicality to work in real time without affecting the depth quality. The framework suggested can be used in the real-time where robust depth estimation in dynamic environments is essential, including autonomous navigation, robotic perception, and augmented reality. The model used now presupposes that there is correct temporal synchronization between event streams and frames. Also, the performance can be reduced when the event conditions are very noisy.

The research will be continued in the future by investigating the extension of the proposed EF-SAT framework to more complex and diverse real-life situations. The combination of dynamic object segmentation may also be seen as one of the promising directions to be taken further in order to enhance depth estimation in highly non-rigid environments. Multi-camera and stereo event-frame systems can also be made to fit into the model and increase the consistency of scales to the geometric accuracy. Also, by investigating light variants of transformers and hardware-conscious optimization techniques, it will become possible to use it on edge and embedded platforms. Another useful direction that has been discovered to enhance reliability of safety-critical applications is to incorporate uncertainty estimation and robustness analysis in the face of extreme noise and illumination conditions.
