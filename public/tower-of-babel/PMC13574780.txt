Semaglutide treatment in MOSH is associated with altered DNA methylation patterns of genes related to glycolipid metabolism
Authors: Yunchong Guo, Junlei Su, Lijun Shen, Chenzhao Ding, Yaqing Wen, Zeting Li, Fangping Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Male obesity-associated secondary hypogonadism(MOSH) is a common disease among severely obese male patients. Although surgical interventions have demonstrated clinical benefits, a subset of patients continue to experience MOSH following surgery. Therefore, this study aims to investigate epigenetic changes associated with the use of the weight-loss drug Semaglutide in MOSH, focusing on DNA methylation and miRNA expression. In this exploratory study, samples were classified into three groups: a control group (n = 2), a MOSH group (n = 7), and a follow-up group (n = 4). DNA methylation analysis was performed on all samples, while miRNA sequencing was conducted on a subset of the samples: 2 from the control group, 7 from the MOSH group, and 2 from the follow-up group. Differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) were analyzed through the R package “limma”, and the methylation level of CpG sites was analyzed based on the methylation β value, obtaining differentially methylated genes (DMGs). The functional enrichment analysis of miRNA target genes and methylation change genes was conducted using the R package “clusterProfiler”. Finally, the regulatory networks of miRNA and methylation genes as well as the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network were analyzed. A total of 6 DEMs were screened out. The target genes of these DEMs were mainly enriched in pathways such as ATP binding, phosphorylation, cell adhesion, and Glycosphingolipid biosynthesis. Eighty DMGs were identified, and the largest number of DMGs were found in the X chromosome. In the regulatory network of DMGs and DEMs, hsa-miR-423-5p regulates most of these DMGs. Moreover, the PPI network shows that DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C, and CNTNAP2 are the proteins with the strongest connectivity. Notably, differential CpG methylation changes were observed on chromosome 7, indicating a potential region of epigenetic alteration in MOSH; however, the biological and functional relevance of these changes remains unclear. Collectively, these findings suggest that Semaglutide treatment in MOSH may be associated with concurrent alterations in DNA methylation and miRNA expression, implicating genes related to energy and glycolipid metabolism, including DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C, and CNTNAP2. These results are exploratory and hypothesis-generating, providing preliminary observations to inform future validation studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-50299-3.

Introduction

Male obesity-associated secondary hypogonadism(MOSH) in male obesity is one of the most common endocrine complications in severely obese men, and its incidence significantly increases with the increase of BMI1. Clinical evidence indicates that morbidly obese men (BMI ≥ 40 kg/m²) exhibit a strikingly high incidence of MOSH, ranging from 40% to 70% based on endocrine evaluations. This disorder is characterized by low testosterone levels (< 3.0ng/mL) accompanied by decreased gonadotropins. The main mechanisms involve leptin resistance caused by obesity, enhanced aromatase activity and dysfunction of hypothalamic Kisspeptin neurons2. MOSH can cause dysfunction of multiple systems, leading to a series of progressively deteriorating clinical symptoms in patients. In terms of the reproductive system, patients with MOSH show significant spermatogenic dysfunction and decreased libido. Their low serum testosterone level can directly lead to oligospermia or even azoospermia, seriously affecting fertility. In terms of metabolism, the patient exhibited central obesity with insulin resistance, dyslipidemia (elevated low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and decreased high-density lipoprotein (HDL)), and fat redistribution. Moreover, obesity has been shown to disrupt sex hormone balance, with obese men displaying significantly lower sex hormone levels compared to their non-obese counterparts.

Accumulating evidence indicates that obesity is consistently associated with reduced circulating sex hormone levels in men3. Meanwhile, the BMI of men was negatively correlated with semen parameters, and when BMI > 30, men had more immature sperm and sperm with damaged chromosomes4. Obesity is also associated with alterations in sperm DNA methylation, and the transmission of such methylation modifications to offspring increases the risk of chronic diseases and autism in the next generation5,6. Previous studies have shown that obesity can also affect the abnormal function of adipose tissue. Meanwhile, abnormal glucose metabolism promotes fat synthesis and accumulation, forming a vicious cycle. Together, they drive the occurrence of metabolic syndrome, creating a vicious cycle7. Among them, obesity mainly regulates the changes of glycolipid metabolism in individuals through miRNA8.

Epigenetics is a discipline that studies heritable changes in gene expression. These changes do not rely on alterations in DNA sequences but regulate gene activity through mechanisms such as DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA regulation. These modifications can affect chromatin structure, determine whether genes are turned on or off, and can change dynamically in response to environmental factors. They can even be passed on to offspring and play a key role in development, diseases and environmental adaptation9. This study focused on two epigenetic patterns, namely DNA methylation and miRNA.

Protein methylation represents one type of post-translational modification (PTM) that regulates protein function by adding methyl groups to amino acid residues, whereas DNA methylation constitutes a distinct epigenetic regulatory mechanism10. The methylation modifications of DNA, RNA and proteins jointly constitute a multi-level epigenetic regulatory network, playing a core role in key biological processes such as cell differentiation, developmental programming, metabolic balance and stress response. DNA methylation occurs in different sequence contexts, including CpG, CHG, and CHH, with CpG methylation representing the predominant form in mammalian genomes and being widely implicated in transcriptional regulation. DNA methylation affects chromatin structure and transcriptional activity by dynamically modifying CpG islands and gene regulatory regions. Its abnormality is closely related to many kinds11. Studies have shown that a large number of methylation variations at CpG sites related to lipid metabolism are closely associated with abnormal blood lipid levels, obesity and the occurrence and development of metabolic diseases. Large-scale genomic investigations, including genome-wide and epigenome-wide association analyses, have pinpointed numerous methylation loci demonstrating robust statistical associations with metabolic phenotypes, such as CpG sites located in the regulatory regions of key lipid metabolism genes. Changes in their methylation levels can affect gene expression and thereby regulate physiological processes such as cholesterol synthesis and triglyceride metabolism12. Recent studies have shown that genome-wide methylation analysis has revealed a large number of differentially methylated regions (DMRs) in obese patients. These epigenetic alterations are mainly enriched in the regulatory regions of genes related to energy metabolism, inflammatory response, and adipocyte differentiation13,14.

microRNA (miRNA) is a type of endogenous non-coding RNA molecule with a length of approximately 18–25 nucleotides. As a key regulatory factor of gene expression, it is widely involved in various biological processes through post-transcriptional regulatory mechanisms. miRNA precisely regulates the gene expression level by partially complementary binding to the 3’ untranslated region (3’-UTR) of the target gene mRNA, inducing mRNA degradation or translation inhibition. Studies have shown that a single miRNA can simultaneously regulate hundreds of target genes, and the same gene may also be jointly regulated by multiple miRNAs, forming a complex gene regulatory network. These microRNA molecules play important roles in basic life activities such as cell differentiation, proliferation, apoptosis and metabolic balance15. Abnormal alterations in miRNA expression profiles are closely related to a variety of human diseases, including cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and neurodegenerative diseases. Due to its highly conserved and tissue-specific expression patterns, miRNAs have been explored as potential biomarkers and therapeutic candidates in various diseases16. Studies have shown that miRNAs related to obesity play an important role in the occurrence and development of metabolic disorders by regulating the expression of key genes in glucose and lipid metabolism. These miRNAs present specific expression patterns in adipose tissue, liver and circulatory system of obese patients8. However, miR-143 interferes with the membrane localization of the glucose transporter GLUT4 by targeting and regulating the AKT signaling pathway, resulting in insulin resistance. Furthermore, miR-27b and miR-130b, etc. have been confirmed to play a key regulatory role in the process of adipocyte differentiation. Abnormal expression of them can lead to adipose tissue dysfunction and ectopic lipid deposition. These metabolism-related miRNAs not only participate in the energy metabolism regulation of local tissues, but also can communicate intercellular through vesicles such as exosomes, forming a systematic metabolic regulation network17–19. miRNAs have been widely studied for their therapeutic potential across a range of diseases20. Due to the important role of DNA methylation and miRNA in obesity-related diseases, this study focused on the mechanism of changes related to DNA methylation and miRNA in patients with MOSH.

For patients with MOSH, weight loss is a very crucial factor in treatment. At present, metabolic and bariatric surgery is an effective treatment option for MOSH21. However, although bariatric surgery can reduce patients’ weight, 64% of men still suffer from MOSH after the operation22. Therefore, in current clinical practice, drugs are also chosen for weight control. Semaglutide is a new type of weight loss drug for weight management, which can lower blood sugar levels and liver fat accumulation23–26. Clinical trials also suggest it improves metabolic health markers beyond weight reduction, such as insulin sensitivity and lipid profiles27. Studies have shown that Semaglutide can reduce the body weight of rodents through distributed neural pathways28, and at the same time, it can reduce alcohol consumption and regulate the transmission of central gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) nerves29. Improve BNIP3-mediated mitochondrial dysfunction to alleviate doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity30. Semaglutide can down-regulate pro-inflammatory miRNAs and up-regulate insulin-sensitive related miRNAs, thereby improving insulin resistance, inhibiting adipogenesis and promoting β -cell function31. Meanwhile, studies have confirmed that Semaglutide can significantly improve sperm morphology, total testosterone levels, and symptoms of hypogonadism32. However, the exact molecular mechanism of Semaglutide in the treatment of MOSH is not yet fully understood.

Although Semaglutide, as a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, has shown significant weight loss and metabolic improvement effects in the treatment of obesity, its epigenetic mechanism of action in the treatment of MOSH and its potential impact on the health of offspring remain to be explored.

In this study, DNA methylation patterns were analyzed in Semaglutide-treated MOSH patients, untreated MOSH patients, and healthy controls, and corresponding miRNA expression changes across the three groups were explored. However, whether Semaglutide is associated with epigenetic alterations in glycolipid metabolism–related genes in patients with MOSH has not been systematically investigated. Therefore, the present study was designed as an exploratory analysis to examine DNA methylation and miRNA expression patterns in a small, well-characterized MOSH cohort before and after Semaglutide treatment. We aimed to explore potential epigenetic signals involving genes related to glucose and lipid metabolism, rather than to establish causal or mechanistic relationships. By characterizing these epigenetic features, this study seeks to generate hypotheses and provide preliminary insights that may inform future mechanistic and validation studies.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

This study strictly screened and included 9 subject samples that met the research criteria, including 2 healthy controls (c3, c4) and 7 clinically diagnosed MOSH patients (p2, p5, p6, p7, p8, p9, p10). All patients with MOSH underwent standardized clinical assessment to rule out hypogonadism caused by other reasons. The Patients with MOSH inclusion criteria were: male, age range 20–40 years, BMI ≥ 28 (applying the obesity criteria for Chinese men), and testosterone (T) < 12 nmol. The detailed information is provided in Supplementary Table 2. In particular, four patients (p2, p5, p7, and p10) were followed up after Semaglutide treatment. Paired samples were collected respectively during the baseline period (p series) and after treatment (f2, f5, f7, and f10) to dynamically evaluate epigenetic changes. Whole Genome Bisulfite Sequencing was performed on all samples (2 healthy controls, 7 MOSH and 4 follow-up patients), a subset of samples was selected for the small RNA sequencing(2 healthy controls, 7 MOSH and 2 follow-up patients). Given the limited sample size, this study was designed as an exploratory analysis to identify potential epigenetic signals rather than to draw definitive conclusions.

We applied strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to minimize potential bias and ensure the representativeness of the study population. Only patients who met clearly defined diagnostic standards for MOSH(male, age range 20–40 years, BMI ≥ 28, and testosterone (T) < 12 nmol) and who had not received prior interventions that could affect DNA methylation or miRNA expression were included, while individuals with confounding conditions such as uncontrolled diabetes, severe metabolic disorders, or ongoing medication use with known epigenetic effects were excluded. In addition, sample collection and processing were carefully standardized to reduce technical variability. Blood (serum) and semen samples were collected between 8:00 and 10:00 a.m. under fasting conditions after 2–7 days of abstinence. Semen was obtained by masturbation and preserved in liquid nitrogen within 2 min.All blood samples were collected under fasting conditions at the same time of day to minimize circadian and dietary influences. Uniform protocols were followed for handling, storage, and processing of biological materials, including immediate stabilization of RNA and DNA to prevent degradation and batch effects. These measures helped to ensure the reliability, reproducibility, and comparability of the downstream sequencing and bioinformatics analyses.

Furthermore, all methods were declared in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations. All the experimental protocols had been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Seventh Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Shenzhen) on July 1, 2024, with approval number KY-2024-257-02. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to participation in the study. All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Methylation quantitative analysis

Whole Genome Bisulfite Sequencing (WGBS) was performed using the Illumina HiSeq (Illumina, San Diego). Comprehensively analyze the DNA methylation map of the sample at single-base resolution. The experiment strictly followed the standard procedures of the International Epigenomics Consortium (IHEC) : Firstly, Bismark software (v0.23.0) was used for the quality control of the original data, the junction sequences were removed and low-quality reads (Q value < 30) were filtered; Subsequently, the bisulfite transformed double-ended reads were compared with the human reference genome (GRCh38/hg38) through Bowtie2 (v2.4.2), allowing a maximum of 2 mismatches; The methylation levels (coverage depth ≥ 10×) of each CpG, CHG and CHH site were calculated using the bismark_methylation_extractor module. To ensure data reliability, we adopt multi-level quality control: (1) Remove incompletely transformed reads (conversion rate < 99%); (2) Exclude mitochondrial and repetitive sequence regions; (3) Filter out potential PCR duplicate fragments. The identification of differentially methylated regions (DMR) was accomplished through the callDMR function in the DSS software package (v2.42.0). The parameters were set as follows: the sliding window was 200 bp, the distance between adjacent CPGS was ≤ 300 bp, the q value was < 0.05, and the absolute value of the methylation difference between groups Δβ was > 0.2. This analysis process has been verified by the standard substance (NA12878), and the correlation coefficient r between technical replicates is greater than 0.98, ensuring the accuracy and repeatability of the test results.

Sequencing and data processing of miRNA

The small RNA sequencing analysis of the samples was performed using the BGI Manufacturing DNBSEQ-T7 high-throughput sequencing platform, with a focus on detecting the miRNA expression profile. The experimental process strictly follows the ENCODE Alliance standard: Firstly, the Library was constructed using the TruSeq Small RNA Library Prep Kit, and cDNA was amplified by polymerase chain reaction. Subsequently, the library quality inspection was conducted using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer to ensure that the fragment size was concentrated within the range of 140–160 bp. The library concentration is greater than 2nM and there is no obvious joint dimer contamination. The original data were subjected to quality control processing by fastp software (v0.23.2), including: 1) Removing low-quality reads; 2) Cut off the 3’ end joint sequence; 3) Filter reads with a length of less than 18nt or more than 32nt. After quality control, the data were compared with the Human Reference Genome (GCF_000001450.39_GRCH38.p13) using Bowtie2 (v2.4.4), and the parameters were set as: -- local-N 1-L 16. Eventually, an average comparison rate of 83.6% was obtained. To improve the accuracy of the analysis, we further used miRDeep2 (v2.0.1.2) for novel miRNA prediction and annotated the known miRNAs based on miRBase (v22.1). This process has been verified by ERCC RNA Spike-In Mix. The Pearson correlation coefficient between technical replicates is > 0.95, and the CV value is < 15%, ensuring the reliability and reproducibility of the data.

Difference analysis and functional annotation

In this study, the standard procedures of bioinformatics were adopted to standardize the miRNA expression data and conduct difference analysis. Firstly, the edgeR software package is used for data normalization processing. The specific steps include: (1) The scale factors between samples are calculated through the calcNormFactors function, and the TMM (trimmed mean of M-values) method is adopted to correct the differences in library size; (2) Apply the voom transformation to process the counting data to make it satisfy the variance assumption of the linear model. Subsequently, the limma package was used for differential expression analysis. When constructing the linear model, the batch effect was included as a covariate, and the empirical Bayes square difference was adjusted through the eBayes function. The final screening criterion was set as |log2FC| > 0.5 and p < 0.05. This analysis process has been verified through a simulated dataset, maintaining detection sensitivity while controlling the false positive rate33,34. In-silico validation was performed to assess the robustness of the differential expression analysis pipeline implemented in edgeR through simulation. A synthetic count matrix was generated using the polyester R package, mimicking the statistical properties (library sizes, dispersion) of our actual dataset. We introduced 1,000 known differentially expressed genes (5% of total genes) with predefined log2-fold changes (± 1). Applying our standard edgeR workflow to this simulated data demonstrated that the false discovery rate (FDR) was well-controlled at approximately 5% while achieving a sensitivity of 92%.

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis annotates gene functions through gene ontology system, revealing significant enrichment patterns of gene sets in molecular functions, biological processes and cellular components35. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis maps genes to signaling pathways, visually demonstrating the regulatory role of key genes in specific biological pathways and their association with phenotypes36. Visualization of gene functional enrichment analysis was performed by the “clusterprofile” R package37. The significance of enrichment was assessed using a hypergeometric test. To control for multiple testing and reduce the false positive rate, p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Enriched terms with adjusted p-values (false discovery rate, FDR) < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Construction of protein-protein networks

STRING (https://string-db.org/) is a comprehensive protein-protein interaction network analysis database that integrates experimental validation, computational prediction, and literature mining data on protein-protein interactions by constructing PPI networks. Explore the functional associations and regulatory mechanisms of genes and proteins in biological systems38. This study constructed the PPI network by setting the confidence score at 0.4. Subsequently, the PPI network was visualized using Cytoscape.

Prediction of miRNA target genes

MiRTarBase (http://mirtarbase.mbc.nctu.edu.tw/) is a after experimental verification of microRNAs - target gene interaction database, which includes reliable data from the literature and experimental, covering several species, including humans, mice and rats, The regulatory information of miRNA on mRNA was provided, including binding sites and regulatory effects39. In this study, the miRTarBase database was used to predict the regulatory genes of miRNA.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.2.1). The choice of statistical tests was based on the data type and distribution. For normally distributed continuous variables (assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test), comparisons between two groups were conducted with Student’s t-test; otherwise, the Mann–Whitney U test was applied. For categorical variables, the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used as appropriate. Assumptions of homogeneity of variance were evaluated using Levene’s test. A significance threshold of p < 0.05 was applied. To account for multiple comparisons in enrichment and high-throughput analyses, the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method was employed to control the false positive rate. Given the limited sample size, statistical analyses were performed in an exploratory manner without the intention of drawing definitive or generalizable conclusions.

Results

miRNA differential analysis and target gene prediction

Among the patients in the MOSH group, 49 miRNAs were significantly differentially expressed (Fig. 1A). There were 17 significantly differentially expressed miRNAs in the follow-up group (Fig. 1B). Subsequently, six miRNAs were identified, namely hsa-miR-451a, hsa-miR209-3p, hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR235-3p, hsa-miR-423-5p and hsa-miR110-3p. These miRNAs underwent significant differential changes simultaneously in the MOSH and follow-up groups (Fig. 1C). Among them, hsa-miR110-3p was significantly highly expressed in the control group, while hsa-miR-451a, hsa-miR-423-5p, hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR235-3p and hsa-miR209-3p were significantly highly expressed in a subset of the MOSH group. After Semaglutide treatment, the expression of DEMs, which was highly expressed in the MOSH group, decreased significantly (Fig. 1D; Table 1). Subsequently, 570 targeted genes of DEMs were collected in the mirtarbase database (Supplementary Table 1). Interestingly, currently, there are only the targeted genes of hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR-423-5p and hsa-miR-451a in the database, and hsa-miR-423-5p is found to regulate more target genes (Fig. 1E). However, it should be noted that only three of the six DEMs have experimentally validated targets in miRTarBase, which limits the completeness of the constructed regulatory network. The lack of target gene annotations for the remaining three miRNAs may limit the comprehensiveness of the regulatory network; therefore, the presented regulatory interactions should be interpreted with this limitation in mind.

Functional enrichment analysis of DEMs

Through a systematic analysis of molecular regulatory networks, this study explores epigenetic alterations associated with Semaglutide treatment in MOSH, providing preliminary, hypothesis-generating observations related to metabolic regulation. Firstly, based on high-throughput sequencing data, we screened differentially expressed miRNAs (DEMs) associated with Semaglutide treatment and subsequently predicted their target genes. It is notable that the analysis of PPI networks revealed that only 159 genes among these target genes formed a tight interaction network (Fig. 2A), and these results highlight epigenetic changes that may be relevant to metabolic pathways in MOSH.

To gain an in-depth understanding of the biological functions of these core target genes, we conducted multi-level enrichment analyses. GO functional annotation shows that these genes are significantly enriched in basal metabolic processes such as ATP binding, protein phosphorylation and cell adhesion (Fig. 2B). It is particularly worth noting that KEGG pathway analysis revealed a significant enrichment of these genes in the glycosphingolipid biosynthesis pathway (Fig. 2C), which is known to affect insulin sensitivity by regulating ceramide metabolism. This enrichment pattern is consistent with pathways previously reported to be involved in metabolic regulation, although functional relevance was not directly assessed in this study. These results suggest that Semaglutide treatment in MOSH may be associated with changes in the expression of hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR-423-5p, and hsa-miR-451a, which are linked to pathways related to energy metabolism.

The difference in methylation patterns

DNA methylation was detected across CpG, CHG, and CHH contexts in both MOSH and follow-up samples. The methylation of all samples is almost CpG methylation (Fig. 3A,B). Subsequently, the methylation changes in different chromosomes were compared in different groups of samples. The results showed that the number of CpG methylation sites in chr7 of the MOSH group samples decreased. After treatment with Semaglutide, The number of CpG methylation sites in chr7 increased (Fig. 3C), and the same changes occurred in chromosomes such as chr6, chr5 and chr19. In CpG methylation, the methylation patterns of CGI and UP3K account for the lowest proportion. The methylation patterns of DOWN3K, exon, Intergenic, intron and repeat are several types of methylation patterns with the highest frequency in MOSH patients (Fig. 3D). Although no statistically significant differences were observed in the overall CpG, CHG, and CHH methylation patterns among the three groups, the data suggested the presence of potential MOSH-associated methylation features.

Analysis and functional annotation of differential methylation

Compared with the control group, 3,899 hypermethylated CpG sites in 1,988 genes and 597 hypomethylated sites in 447 genes were identified in the MOSH group (Fig. 4A). Compared with the MOSH group, in the follow-up group, 171 hypermethylated CpG sites among 119 genes and 192 CpG sites among 127 genes were identified (Fig. 4B). Subsequently, We performed a statistical analysis of differential DNA methylation across CpG sites between the MOSH group and the follow-up group. It was identified that 659 methylation sites of 61 genes showed hypermethylation in the MOSH group, and 516 methylation sites across 19 genes exhibited hypomethylation. Notably, these methylation sites exhibited opposite methylation trends in the follow-up group, and at the same time, the number of differentially methylated genes (DMGs) on the X chromosome was the largest (Fig. 4C; Table 1). After functional annotation of these methylation sites, we observed that the genes with hypermethylation in the MOSH group and follow-up hypomethylation were significantly enriched in transmembrane transporter binding and NAD+ nucleotidase. Related functions such as cyclic ADP ribose generating and dystrophin−associated glycoprotein complex (Fig. 4D). The hypomethylated and follow-up hypermethylated genes in the MOSH group were significantly enriched in related functions such as neuromuscular process and sarcolemma (Fig. 4E).

Genes regulated jointly by DNA methylation and miRNA

Through the analyses described above, we observed that Semaglutide treatment in MOSH was associated with changes in the expression of hsa-miR-451a, hsa-miR-209-3p, hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR-235-3p, hsa-miR-423-5p, and hsa-miR-110-3p, as well as with altered DNA methylation patterns in a subset of genes. These observations suggest that the integrated analysis of miRNA expression and DNA methylation may be useful for exploring epigenetic associations related to Semaglutide treatment in MOSH. The intersection of the target genes of important miRNAs and the DNA methylation genes with significant differential changes in the follow-up group was taken. The results are shown in Fig. 5A. hsa-miR-451a, hsa-miR-7-1-3p and hsa-miR-423-5p have obvious regulatory relationships with DNA methylation genes. Among them, hsa-miR-423-5p regulates 7 hypermethylated genes and 33 hypomethylated genes. Subsequently, mapping these genes to a protein-protein interaction network revealed that DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C, and CNTNAP2 exhibited high connectivity within the network(Fig. 5B). This also shows that DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C and CNTNAP2 may represent candidates for further investigation as potential components of epigenetically regulated pathways in MOSH.

Through chromosomal localization analysis of key metabolism-related genes, our results indicate that methylation sites of multiple genes exhibited regional clustering across specific chromosomes (Fig. 5C,D). Specifically, the methylation sites of CNTNAP2 and DPP6 were both located on chromosome 7. Among them, CNTNAP2 showed the most significant methylation changes, with the number of differential methylation sites reaching 23, which was much higher than that of other genes. Notably, the methylation sites of CACNA1C were concentrated on chromosome 12, while those of DPP10 were mainly distributed on chromosome 2. This distribution pattern was consistent with the previous results of whole-genome methylation analysis, in which chromosome 7 exhibited the most pronounced differences in DNA methylation levels between pre- and post-treatment samples in patients with MOSH.

It is particularly worth noting that the two genes CNTNAP2 and DPP6 on chromosome 7 both play significant roles in the development of the nervous system and the regulation of energy metabolism. As a member of the Neurexin family, CNTNAP2 has been reported to be involved in synaptic transmission within the hypothalamic–pituitary axis; however, the potential relevance of its altered methylation status to gonadal function was not directly examined in the present study. While DPP6 affects the electrical activity of pancreatic islet β cells by regulating potassium ion channels. Combined with the correlation between the methylation changes of chr7 and the serum testosterone level that we discovered earlier, these observations raise the possibility that chromosome 7 may represent a region of interest for future epigenetic studies in MOSH. However, given the limited sample size, these observations should be interpreted cautiously and require validation in larger cohorts and functional studies. This chromosomal region-specific methylation pattern may reflect specific three-dimensional chromatin structure changes or regional epigenetic regulatory mechanisms during the pathogenesis of MOSH, providing a new research direction for understanding disease heterogeneity. Subsequent studies need to further analyze the causal relationship between the chr7 methylation landscape and the clinical phenotype of MOSH, as well as how Semaglutide specifically regulates the epigenetic status of this region.

Discussion

Given the extremely small sample size, all findings presented here should be considered exploratory and interpreted with caution. DNA methylation and miRNA expression are known to influence gene transcription and have been implicated in obesity-related diseases in previous studies40–42. Multiple studies have reported that DNA methylation and miRNAs are implicated in obesity-related diseases and may represent potential targets for future therapeutic exploration43–45. Semaglutide is an effective weight-loss agent, and examining changes in DNA methylation status and miRNA expression during its use in MOSH may provide a useful exploratory approach for investigating epigenetic associations related to this treatment. Therefore, in this study, DNA methylation measurement and miRNA expression sequencing were conducted on three groups of samples: control, MOSH, and follow-up. The changes in DNA methylation levels and miRNA expression levels in different groups of samples were analyzed, and the biological functions affected by these important changes were elucidated. Subsequently, the regulatory networks of miRNA and differential DNA methylation were mapped, and the key genes of hypermethylated and hypomethylated genes were explored in the PPI network.

Semaglutide can alleviate chronic low-grade inflammation of visceral adipocytes, which inhibits the nuclear translocation of NF-κB by activating the AMPK signaling pathway and reduces the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors such as TNF-α and IL-6; On the other hand, by regulating the polarization of macrophages in adipose tissue, the pro-inflammatory M1 type is transformed into the anti-inflammatory M2 type. In terms of endoplasmic reticulum stress, Semaglutide can significantly down-regulate the expression of endoplasmic reticulum stress markers such as CHOP and GRP78. The mechanism involves restoring the homeostasis of the IRE1α/XBP1 signaling pathway, thereby improving the insulin sensitivity of adipocytes46. The mechanistic pathways discussed above are based on prior literature and were not directly assessed in the present study.

However, there is still a lack of relevant analytical research on its influence in the directions of DNA methylation and miRNA. It affected the expression of six miRNAs (hsa-miR-451a, hsa-miR209-3p, hsa-miR-7-1-3p, hsa-miR235-3p, hsa-miR-423-5p and hsa-miR110-3p). Functional enrichment analysis of miRNA target genes indicated that energy metabolism–related biological functions were predominantly associated with skeletal muscle–related pathways, including abdominal muscle–associated processes. Adipose tissue dysfunction is the core of obesity metabolic dysfunction. ATP binding can regulate the expression of ATP-binding cassette transporter A1 (ABCA1) in adipose tissue47. Glycosphingolipid biosynthesis is one of the key pathways for energy metabolism regulation, and its metabolites are involved in the fine regulation of glucose and lipid homeostasis through multiple mechanisms. Studies have shown that glycolipid substances play a dual role in insulin signaling: On the one hand, ceramides can induce insulin resistance in sk1eletal muscle and adipose tissue by activating protein kinases and inhibiting the Akt/PKB pathway; On the other hand, specific glycosphingolipid derivatives can regulate the localization of insulin receptors in the lipid raft of the cell membrane and affect their signal transduction efficiency. In liver tissues, abnormal glycosphingolipid metabolism promotes steatosis, and the mechanism involves the abnormal activation of Sterol regulatory element-binding protein-1c (SREBP-1c) and impaired mitochondrial β -oxidation function48–50. Taken together, existing evidence and our findings suggest that Semaglutide treatment in MOSH may be associated with changes in epigenetic features related to energy metabolism; however, these observations should be interpreted as preliminary associations rather than evidence of direct mechanistic or clinical effects. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as preliminary associations rather than evidence of specific mechanistic pathways.

In the DMGs and DEMs regulatory networks, the majority of DMGs associated with Semaglutide treatment exhibited a hypomethylated state. Furthermore, PPI network analysis highlighted DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C, and CNTNAP2 as potentially important functional proteins. DPP6 can affect the sensitivity of GLP-1 receptor in pancreatic islet β cells and enhance the glucose-dependent insulin secretion function. Meanwhile, by prolonging the active duration of GLP-1, DPP6 not only promotes insulin release but also inhibits the secretion of glucagon and delays gastric emptying51,52. The gene polymorphism of DPP10 can also affect the BMI index53, but the specific influence mechanism lacks specific research. CACNA1C can reduce insulin secretion in β cells54, thereby affecting the glycolipid metabolism of patients. CNTNAP2 can regulate diet-induced obesity55. These genes are involved in pathways related to body weight regulation, which may be consistent with the known metabolic effects of Semaglutide. Meanwhile, these genes have a relatively close relationship with glycolipid metabolism when regulating body weight, and all indirectly control the process of glycolipid metabolism. Collectively, these findings suggest that Semaglutide treatment in MOSH may be associated with alterations in DNA methylation and miRNA expression patterns implicating genes involved in glycolipid metabolism, including DPP6, DPP10, CACNA1C, and CNTNAP2.

Our findings extend previous studies by providing preliminary, hypothesis-generating observations on DNA methylation and miRNA expression associated with Semaglutide treatment in MOSH. While earlier research primarily focused on metabolic outcomes, our study highlights specific epigenetic targets and regulatory pathways modulated by Semaglutide, offering a deeper understanding of its molecular mechanisms. These findings contribute preliminary observations that may inform future studies aimed at identifying epigenetic markers or regulatory pathways relevant to MOSH.

This study has several important limitations, including the small sample size, the absence of an independent validation cohort, and the observational nature of the epigenetic analyses, all of which preclude causal inference. In particular, the extremely limited sample size substantially restricts the reliability and generalizability of the findings. Accordingly, the present results should be interpreted strictly as exploratory and should not be considered evidence of mechanistic effects or therapeutic efficacy. The number of analyzed samples should be expanded in subsequent analyses. Future studies should validate the epigenetic effects of Semaglutide in larger MOSH cohorts and explore the functional roles of the identified miRNAs and DNA methylation changes. These efforts will require expanded sample sizes and targeted validation approaches, such as bisulfite sequencing or RT-qPCR, to confirm our preliminary findings.
