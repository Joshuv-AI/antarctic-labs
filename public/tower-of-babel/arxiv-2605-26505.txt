A note on the exact partition polytope of Frieze and Teng Journal: Operations Research Letters
Krishna Narayanan, Tamon Stephen

Abstract
In 1994, Frieze and Teng [ 2 ] proposed an integer linear programming formulation of the 𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{NP}} -Complete Exact Partition problem, whose LP-relaxation they claimed was non-degenerate. Contrary to their claim, we show how an instance of Exact Partition can produce a degenerate polytope, and study conditions for which this can happen. We then give details of one of the smallest such degenerate Frieze-Teng polytopes, along with a closely related non-degenerate Frieze-Teng polytope that encodes an equivalent problem. We note that for the purposes of the complexity results in [ 2 ] and [ 6 ] , these degenerate polytopes can be avoided via a simple preprocessing step.

Introduction

Exact Partition
is a variant of the well-known
{\mathsf{NP}}
-Complete
Partition
problem, which was one of Karp’s 21
{\mathsf{NP}}
-Complete problems
[
4
]
.
Partition
asks if in a given set of integers, we can obtain two subsets of equal sum.
Exact Partition
additionally requires the subsets to be of equal size.

In
[
2
]
, Frieze and Teng modified a version of an integer linear programming formulation of
Exact Partition
proposed by Korte and Schrader in
[
5
]
. The polytope arising from the LP-relaxation of Frieze and Teng’s formulation is used in the context of studying the computational complexity of diameters and monotone diameters of polytopes.
Recent activity highlighting complexity questions on simple polytopes includes results by the authors of this note
[
6
]
and by Black and Steiner
[
1
]
.

Frieze and Teng claimed that their polytope is non-degenerate (Proposition 2,
[
2
]
). We identify an issue in their proof, but note that the problematic instances can be sidestepped in the derivation of complexity results.
We describe a minimal counterexample of
Exact Partition
with four elements that gives a degenerate Frieze-Teng polytope, and compare it to an equivalent
Exact Partition
instance whose Frieze-Teng polytope is non-degenerate. We finish with a discussion of conditions that can cause degeneracy in this polytope.

2
Preliminaries and Frieze and Teng’s formulation

We begin with some definitions. An
\mathcal{H}
-polytope
(henceforth, simply a
polytope
) is the intersection of finitely many closed halfspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is
bounded
, i.e., it contains no ray
\{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf x$}}+t\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf y$}}:t\geq 0\}
for
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf x$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf y$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf y$}}\neq\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf 0$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf 0$}}
. The
dimension
of a polytope is the dimension of its affine hull. A
d
-dimensional polytope is
simple
if every vertex is adjacent to exactly
d
edges in the 1-skeleton, or equivalently, contained in exactly
d
facets. When a vertex is at the intersection of more than
d
facets, we call it a
degenerate
vertex. If a polytope contains any degenerate vertices, then we call it a
degenerate polytope
.

The
Exact Partition
problem is defined as follows.

Problem:
Exact Partition

Input:
A finite set
\mathcal{A}=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{2m}\}
of integers.

Question:
Does there exist a subset
\mathcal{A^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{A}
with
|\mathcal{A^{\prime}}|=m
such that

\sum_{s\in\mathcal{A^{\prime}}}s=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A^{\prime}}}t?

Korte and Schrader
[
5
]
studied this problem via a
0/1
linear programming formulation with two knapsack-like constraints. The first limits the number of elements in the set to
m
and the second limits the sum of the selected elements to
\displaystyle\frac{S}{2}
where
S:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}s_{i}
.
Frieze and Teng
[
2
]
noted that the polytope arising from this formulation, which they call
ILP1
, is highly degenerate. They provide another formulation,
ILP2
, which they claim is non-degenerate.

Let
s_{\max}
be the largest element in a given problem instance
\mathcal{A}
. Take
M:=S+1
,
d_{i}:=s_{\max}-s_{i}
and
\epsilon:=\frac{1}{2M}
. The Frieze-Teng formulation of
Exact Partition
is:

(ILP2)

Maximize

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}x_{i}\text{ with }x_{i}\in\{0,1\}\qquad\qquad\qquad

subject to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+s_{i})x_{i}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}S+mM+\epsilon\qquad

(K1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+d_{i})x_{i}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}+mM+\epsilon

(K2)

Observe that (K1) limits the sum of elements in the chosen subset, while (K2) limits the number of selected elements. We assume that the elements of
\mathcal{A}
are positive.
If this does not hold we can translate the problem to this scenario by adding an appropriate constant to each element of
\mathcal{A}
.

Let us denote the polytope arising from the linear programming relaxation of ILP2 by
P_{R}
. Then
P_{R}
is a polytope obtained from the unit
2m
-cube
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
by cutting it with two knapsack-type constraints (K1) and (K2) with positive co-efficients. We will refer to the constraints
0\leq x_{i}\leq 1
as
box constraints
, and to (K1) and (K2) as the
knapsack constraints
.

2.1
Some characteristics of
P_{R}
and related notation

For a given vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in P_{R}
, we use the following notation in the remainder of this document.

1.

I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})=\{i\in[2m]\colon x_{i}=1\}

2.

I_{0}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})=\{i\in[2m]\colon x_{i}=0\}

3.

I_{f}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})=\{i\in[2m]\colon 0<x_{i}<1\}

Additionally, for a vertex
\textstyle\bf v
, let us also denote
I^{*}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})=I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})\cup I_{f}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})
.

Frieze and Teng claim that
P_{R}
is a simple polytope.

Claim 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

When
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2M}
, the polytope defined by the linear programming relaxation of ILP2 is simple (non-degenerate).

The above claim is equivalent to saying that only
2m
of the constraints are active at any vertex of
P_{R}
.

Following Lemma 5 of
[
2
]
, the vertices of
P_{R}
can be partitioned into three classes:

V_{0}
- Vertices lying on
2m
box constraints. Any such point is integer, and does not satisfy (K1) or (K2) due the choice of
\epsilon
.

V_{1}
- Vertices lying on exactly
2m-1
box constraints. These will lie on at least one of (K1) or (K2).

V_{2}
- Vertices lying on exactly
2m-2
box constraints. These will lie on both (K1) and (K2).

We now outline Frieze and Teng’s proof of Claim
2.1
and identify an issue.

Proof.

Assume that
P_{R}
is degenerate, which implies that there is a vertex
\textstyle\bf x
in
P_{R}
which lies on
2m+1
active constraints.

If
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf x$}}\in V_{0}
, then
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}for\quad i=1,\ldots,2m
. The choice of
\epsilon
in the definitions of (K1) and (K2) is set so that they do not contain any integer points and
\textstyle\bf x
lies on exactly
2m
constraints.
If
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf x$}}\in V_{2}
, since there are only two remaining constraints,
\textstyle\bf x
lies on at most
2m
constraints.

Now suppose
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf x$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf x$}}\in V_{1}
. Then
|\{x_{i}\in\{0,1\}\}|=2m-1
. Without loss of generality, assume that
0<x_{1}<1
. For
\textstyle\bf x
to be degenerate, both (K1) and (K2) need to be active.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+s_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}S+mM+\epsilon

(K1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+d_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}+mM+\epsilon

(K2)

Frieze and Teng claim that after eliminating
x_{1}
, they obtain an integer
c
such that

\epsilon=\frac{c}{2d_{1}}

which contradicts the assumption that
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2M}
. However, we observe that this expression obtained for
\epsilon
is not accurate. In particular, when we isolate
x_{1}
, we obtain
x_{1}=\frac{c_{1}}{(s_{\max}-2s_{1})}
, where
c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and by substituting
x_{1}
, we obtain
\epsilon=\frac{c_{3}}{2(d_{1}-s_{1})}
, where
c_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We detail this below.

Recall that by our assumption, both (K1) and (K2) are active at
\textstyle\bf x
. We follow Frieze and Teng to eliminate
x_{1}
by isolating it. By subtracting (K1) from (K2), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(d_{i}-s_{i})x_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}-\frac{1}{2}S

Substituting
d_{i}=s_{\max}-s_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}((s_{\max}-s_{i})-s_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(s_{\max}-s_{i})-\frac{1}{2}S

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(s_{\max}-2s_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(2m\cdot s_{\max}-S\right)-\frac{1}{2}S

If
s_{\max}=2s_{1}
then
x_{1}
drops out of the equation. When
s_{\max}\neq 2s_{1}
, we can proceed to isolate
x_{1}

\displaystyle(s_{\max}-2s_{1})x_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(s_{\max}-2s_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=m\cdot s_{\max}-S

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{m\cdot s_{\max}-S-\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(s_{\max}-2s_{i})x_{i}}{(s_{\max}-2s_{1})}

Since
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for all
i\in\{2,...,2m\}
,
ms_{\max}-S-\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(s_{\max}-2s_{i})x_{i}
in the numerator is an integer. Let this integer be
c_{1}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{(s_{\max}-2s_{1})}

Recalling
0<x_{1}<1
, we substitute
x_{1}
into (K1) to isolate
\epsilon
.

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+s_{i})x_{i}-\frac{1}{2}S-mM

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=(M+s_{1})x_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(M+s_{i})-\frac{1}{2}S-mM

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=(M+s_{1})\frac{c_{1}}{(s_{\max}-2s_{1})}+\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(M+s_{i})-\frac{1}{2}S-mM

Once more, since
x_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for all
i\in\{2,...,2m\}
, we have
\displaystyle c_{2}=\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(M+s_{i})x_{i}-mM
such that
c_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=(M+s_{1})\frac{c_{1}}{(s_{\max}-2s_{1})}+c_{2}-\frac{1}{2}S

Since
d_{1}=s_{\max}-s_{1}
, we have
s_{\max}-2s_{1}=d_{1}-s_{1}
. We then have

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=(M+s_{1})\frac{c_{1}}{(d_{1}-s_{1})}+c_{2}-\frac{1}{2}S

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{2((M+s_{1})c_{1}+c_{2}(d_{1}-s_{1}))-S(d_{1}-s_{1})}{2(d_{1}-s_{1})}

Clearly,
c_{3}=2((M+s_{1})c_{1}+c_{2}(d_{1}-s_{1}))-S(d_{1}-s_{1})
is an integer. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}}{2(d_{1}-s_{1})}

which contradicts the assumption that
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2M}
and the proof follows.

∎

We adjust Frieze and Teng’s lemma to remove the offending case:

Lemma 2.2
.

When
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2M}
and there is no
i\in\{1,\ldots,2m\}
such that
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
, the polytope defined by the linear programming relaxation of ILP2 is simple.

When the conditions for the above Lemma are satisfied, we have the following alternate characterization of
V_{0}
,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
respectively. This is implied in
[
2
]
and described in
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
.

1.

V_{0}
- Vertices that lie only on the box constraints and not on either of (K1) or (K2).

2.

V_{1}
- Vertices that lie on either of (K1) or (K2), but not both, as these vertices lie on exactly
2m-1
box constraints and a total of
2m
constraints.

3.

V_{2}
- Vertices that lie on both (K1) and (K2). The simplicity of
P_{R}
implies that there are exactly two fractional components by a similar argument as in
V_{1}
.

For the case where
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
, it turns out we
can
get degenerate polytopes. In particular, we obtain polytopes where vertices with one fractional variable lie on both (K1) and (K2), which violates the above implication of simplicity. In Section
3
we study one such polytope and generalize the conditions under which degenerate vertices occur.

2.2
Preprocessing to avoid degenerate polytopes in complexity reductions

Frieze and Teng introduce this lemma to prove that computing a vertex’s
eccentricity
(or
radius
), the maximum shortest pivot distance to any other vertex on a polytope, is
{\mathsf{NP}}
-hard via a reduction from
Exact Partition
. Similarly, the authors of this note use this in the context of
monotone
eccentricity
[
6
]
.

We remark that this still holds, as the degenerate polytopes that can occur in this reduction can be avoided by simple pre-processing.
Recall from Section 2 that we assumed that all input integers in the
Exact Partition
instance are non-negative, and if not, can be translated accordingly by adding an appropriate constant to every element of the instance.

A degenerate vertex requires an even
s_{\max}
in the
Exact Partition
instance. To prevent this, adding one to each element ensures the new
s_{\max}
is odd, making
s_{i}=s_{\max}/2
impossible while preserving its status as the largest element. By Lemma
2.2
, this transformation guarantees the resulting exact partition polytope contains no degenerate vertices.

3.2
Conditions for degeneracy in
P_{R}

In this section, we briefly elaborate conditions for
P_{R}
to have degenerate vertices.
The first, of course, is that
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
for some
i\in[2m]
.
We note that the example provided is for a polytope with an exact partition, and that the degenerate vertex contains
m
ones, corresponding to this exact partition. In fact, this turns out to be required for such a degenerate vertex, as we will see.

Recall that vertices in
V_{0}
and
V_{2}
cannot be degenerate (as justified in the proof of Claim
2.1
), so we focus our attention on vertices in
V_{1}
, i.e. those
\textstyle\bf v
with
|\{v_{i}\in\{0,1\}\}|=2m-1
. Note that for any vertex
\textstyle\bf v
of
P_{R}
in
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
,
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|\geq m-1
(Lemma 8,
[
6
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

A vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in V_{1}
in
P_{R}
with
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|=m-1
cannot be degenerate.

Proof.

Assume that a vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in V_{1}
with
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|=m-1
is degenerate. Then
\textstyle\bf v
lies on
2m+1
active constraints, and has one remaining fractional index. Without loss of generality, this is the first co-ordinate, i.e.
0<v_{1}<1
.
When there is no element
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
, then we are in the situation of Lemma
2.2
, and no vertices are degenerate. Further, if
i\neq 1
, Frieze and Teng’s analysis of Claim
2.1
gives that
\textstyle\bf v
does not lie on both (K1) and (K2).

So we consider the situation when
s_{1}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
.
If both (K1) and (K2) are active, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+s_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}S+mM+\epsilon

(K1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2m}(M+d_{i})x_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}+mM+\epsilon

(K2)

We define a parameter
\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}
which denotes the difference between the sum of variables in the support of a vertex
\textstyle\bf v
and the target sum of the input instance of

. Symbolically, we have at a vertex
\textstyle\bf v

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}=\frac{S}{2}-\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}s_{i}

From (K1), we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+s_{i})+(M+s_{1})v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2}+mM+\epsilon

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}s_{i}+(M+s_{1})v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2}+M+\epsilon

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}+M+\epsilon}{M+s_{1}}\text{}

(1)

Recall that
d_{i}=s_{\max}-s_{i}
and
\sum d_{i}=2m\cdot s_{\max}-S
. We have the following from (K2):

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+d_{i})+(M+d_{1})v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}+mM+\epsilon

\displaystyle(M+s_{\max}-s_{1})v_{1}

\displaystyle=s_{\max}+M-\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}+\epsilon

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{s_{\max}+M-\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}+\epsilon}{M+s_{\max}-s_{1}}

Since
s_{1}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
,
M+s_{\max}-s_{1}=M+\frac{s_{\max}}{2}=M+s_{1}
, and from (K2), this becomes

\displaystyle v_{1}=\frac{s_{\max}+M-\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}+\epsilon}{M+s_{1}}

(2)

Equating the two expressions for

, which now share a common denominator, we get

\alpha_{v}+M+\epsilon=s_{\max}+M-\alpha_{v}+\epsilon

Solving for

gives
\alpha_{v}=\displaystyle\frac{s_{\max}}{2}=s_{1}
. But since

, we have
v_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{M+s_{1}+\epsilon}{M+s_{1}}>1
, violating the box constraint. The statement follows.

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in P_{R}
where
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|=m
, the elements at indices
I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})
necessarily encode an exact partition. This is implicit from Lemma 4 in
[
2
]
. We elaborate it below following the additional details in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

For a vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in V_{0}
, if
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|=m
, then the elements at indices
I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})
represent an exact partition in the input instance.

Proof.

Consider
\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}=\frac{S}{2}-\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}s_{i}
as before. The statement is equivalent to
\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}=0
.
We can see that we can’t have
\alpha_{v}<0
, as the constraint (K1) would be violated.

So assume
\alpha_{v}>0
.
Recall that
d_{i}=s_{\max}-s_{i}
by definition in ILP2. In (K2), we have the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+d_{i})v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{2m}d_{i}}{2}+mM+\epsilon

\displaystyle m(M+s_{\max})-\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}s_{i}

\displaystyle\leq m(M+s_{\max})-\frac{S}{2}+\epsilon

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{S}{2}+\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}s_{i}+\epsilon

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(-\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}})+\epsilon

Hence
\alpha_{v}\leq\epsilon
, which in turn is less than 0.5 by construction. But this is impossible since
\alpha_{v}
is positive and at least 0.5.

Therefore,
\alpha_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}}=0
and the statement follows.

∎

Let us now suppose that there is an exact partition in the input instance and that the elements at indices
\{1,\dots,m\}
are a valid subset. Let vertex
\textstyle\bf v
be a corresponding vertex in
V_{0}
whose support consists of variables at indices
\{1,\dots,m\}
.

Observation 3.3
.

Consider a vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in V_{0}
where
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})|=m
. Then, at
\textstyle\bf v
, the slack in the RHS of (K1) and (K2) is
\epsilon
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have at
\textstyle\bf v
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+s_{i})v_{i}=mM+\frac{S}{2}

(1)

Therefore, in (K1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+s_{i})v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{S}{2}+mM+\epsilon

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+s_{i})v_{i}-\frac{S}{2}-mM

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\epsilon.

Similarly, we have:

\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+d_{i})v_{i}=mM+m\cdot s_{\max}-\frac{S}{2}

(2)

Therefore, in (K2), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+d_{i})v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq mM+\frac{\sum d_{i}}{2}+\epsilon

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})}(M+d_{i})v_{i}

\displaystyle\leq mM+m\cdot s_{max}-\frac{\sum s_{i}}{2}+\epsilon

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\epsilon.

∎

Recall a vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}}\in V_{1}
(where
|\{v_{i}\in\{0,1\}\}|=2m-1
) is degenerate when both (K1) and (K2) are active. Consider
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}\in V_{1}
where
|I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}})|=m
, indices
\{1,\dots,m\}
form a valid subset, and there is an index
i\in I_{f}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}})
. If at least one
i\in\{m+1,\dots,2m\}
satisfies
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
, the coefficients of
v^{*}_{i}
in (K1) and (K2) are equal since
M+s_{i}=M+d_{i}=M+s_{max}-s_{i}
. We show
\textstyle\bf v^{*}
is degenerate.

Theorem 3.4
.

Vertex
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}\in V_{1}
(as defined above, with
s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2}
for some
i\in\{m+1,\ldots,2m\}
) is degenerate.

Proof.

Recall that
I^{*}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})=I_{1}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})\cup I_{f}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v$}})
. Since
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}\in V_{1}
, let constraint (K1) be tight at
\textstyle\bf v^{*}
. At the index
i\in I_{f}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}})
,
v^{*}_{i}
is as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I^{*}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}})}(M+s_{k})v^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{S}{2}+mM+\epsilon

\displaystyle(M+s_{i})v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\quad(\because\text{by Observation \ref{obs}})

\displaystyle v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{(M+\frac{s_{\max}}{2})}

\displaystyle v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon}{(2M+s_{\max})}\quad(\because s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2})

Evaluating the LHS of (K2) at this point, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I^{*}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\bf v^{*}$}})}(M+d_{k})v^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=mM+\frac{\sum d_{i}}{2}+(M+d_{i})v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle\quad\quad(\because\text{Lemma \ref{epv}})

Given that the slack in the RHS of (K2) is
\epsilon
(per Observation
3.3
), the value of
v^{*}_{i}
required to bridge this slack is:

\displaystyle(M+d_{i})v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\epsilon

\displaystyle v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{(M+s_{\max}-s_{i})}\quad(\because d_{i}=s_{\max}-s_{i})

\displaystyle v^{*}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\epsilon}{(2M+s_{\max})}\quad(\because s_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{2})

Since the obtained values of
v^{*}_{i}
from (K1) and (K2) are equal, both constraints are tight at vertex
\textstyle\bf v^{*}
apart from
2m-1
box constraints. The statement follows.
∎
