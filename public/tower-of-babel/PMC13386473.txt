Safety of a feed additive consisting of Macleaya cordata (Willd.) R. Br. extract and leaves (Sangrovit® Extra) for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae (Phytobiotics Futterzusatzstoffe GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Maria de Lourdes Bastos, Emilio Benfenati, Paul Brantom, Andrew Chesson, Josef Schlatter, Johannes Westendorf, Paola Manini, Birgit Dusemund
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety of a feed additive consisting of Macleaya cordata (Willd.) R. Br. extract and leaves (Sangrovit® Extra) as a zootechnical feed additive for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae. In its previous assessment, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) had no safety concerns for the target species when the additive is used at the recommended level of 0.750 mg sanguinarine/kg complete feed for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae. However, owing to the presence of the DNA intercalators sanguinarine and chelerythrine, a concern for genotoxicity was identified for the additive and the FEEDAP Panel could not conclude on the safety for the consumer. The use of the additive was considered safe for the environment. The additive was shown to be irritant to the eyes but not irritant to skin or a skin sensitiser. In the current assessment, the applicant provided an in vitro mutagenicity assay in mammalian cells with Macleaya cordata extract. Although the study gave negative results, these would not change the conclusions on the genotoxicity of sanguinarine and chelerythrine reached in the previous assessment. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of Sangrovit® Extra for consumers when used in feed for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae.Keywords: chelerythrine, Macleaya cordata extract, mutagenicity, other zootechnical additives, safety, Sangrovit® extra, sanguinarine

Additional information

The additive Sangrovit® Extra consists of Macleaya cordata extract and leaves. The FEEDAP Panel issued two opinions on the safety and efficacy of Sangrovit® Extra for all poultry species (excluding laying and breeding birds) and for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023, 2024).

The additive is currently authorised for use in poultry for fattening (4d26). 2

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of supplementary information 3 to a previous application on the same product. 4 The dossier was received on 12/01/2026 and the general information and supporting documentation are available on Open.EFSA at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2026‐00016.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment regarding the methods used for the control of the sanguinarine (SG) in Sangrovit® Extra, in premixtures and feedingstuff are valid and applicable for the current application. 5

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety of Macleaya cordata extract and leaves (Sangrovit® Extra) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 6 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017), Statement on the genotoxicity assessment of chemical mixtures (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2019a), Guidance on the use of the Threshold of Toxicological Concern approach in food safety assessment (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2019b) and General approach to assess the safety for the target species of botanical preparations which contain compounds that are genotoxic and/or carcinogenic (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021).

ASSESSMENT

The additive under assessment (Sangrovit® Extra) consists of a M. cordata (Willd.) R. Br. extract and M. cordata processed leaves. The additive is formulated with wheat flour, to achieve a target concentration of the sum of M. cordata alkaloids of 1.25%, with 0.5% sanguinarine (SG, selected as the marker substance), together with lignosulfonate and sorbic acid. The additive is specified to contain a minimum of 4000 mg SG/kg additive and a maximum 7000 mg SG/kg additive. The additive was fully characterised in former opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023, 2024). The additive is intended for use as a zootechnical additive (functional group: other zootechnical additives) in feed for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae.

In its previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that Sangrovit® Extra is safe for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae and for the environment. However, due to concerns about genotoxicity because of the presence of the DNA intercalators SG and chelerythrine (CH), the Panel could not conclude on consumer safety. Regarding user safety, the Panel concluded that the additive is irritant to the eyes but not to skin nor is it a skin sensitiser and exposure should be minimised. The Panel concluded that the additive had the potential to be efficacious in suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae.

In the current dossier, the applicant submitted additional data aimed at demonstrating the safety of the additive for the consumer.

Safety for the consumer

In its former assessments, the FEEDAP Panel evaluated genotoxicity studies performed with M. cordata extract (MCE) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023, 2024) and with the formulated additive (Sangrovit®) (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023). The in vitro studies conducted with MCE were negative but showed some limitations related to the prevalent cytotoxic activity of the test item, which possibly did not allow the expression of a potential genotoxicity. In particular, the FEEDAP Panel noted that ‘…the outcome of mutagenicity tests for compounds with bactericidal or bacteriostatic properties depends on the balance between their mutagenic potential and their antibacterial activity. A negative result obtained with a bacterial reverse mutation assay cannot be considered truly negative, because antibacterial activity at the tested concentration might suppress the expression of mutations’.

For the current assessment, the applicant submitted an in vitro mammalian gene mutation assay with MCE, which is described below.

In vitro mammalian gene mutation test

To investigate the potential of MCE to induce gene mutations in mammalian cells, an in vitro L5178Y Gene Mutation Assay at the hypoxanthine‐guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (hprt) Locus test was performed following the Organisation for Economic Co‐operation and Development (OECD) Testing Guideline (TG) 476 and claimed good laboratory practice (GLP) compliant. 7

Based on the results obtained in a preliminary cytotoxicity assay, at least five concentrations were tested in the main experiment ranging from ■■■■■ to ■■■■■ and ■■■■■ to ■■■■■, in the absence and presence of metabolic activation (S9‐mix), respectively. The relative survival values observed at the highest concentrations tested were 11% and 24% in the absence and presence of metabolic activation, respectively, compared to the vehicle controls. No statistically significant increases in the mean mutant frequency (MF) were observed at any of the test concentrations compared to the concurrent mean vehicle control values. No concentration‐related response was detected in the presence of metabolic activation, while a positive linear trend (p ≤ 0.05) was observed in the absence of S9‐mix. However, the increase in MF was less than two times the values observed in the vehicle control cultures. The FEEDAP Panel considers it not biologically relevant and concludes that the MCE did not induce gene mutations in mammalian cells under the experimental conditions applied in the study.

Overall evaluation of the genotoxicity data

The FEEDAP Panel took account of the negative results of the newly presented study (Section 3.1.1). Overall, the in vitro studies with MCE were negative. However, the Panel considered that, in line with the principles of the EFSA statement on the genotoxicity of mixtures (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2019a), the negative results observed with the extract could be due to the presence of multiple chemicals in the mixture affecting the sensitivity of the assays. In addition, consumers will not be exposed to MCE, but to residues of SG (and CH). Thus, the conclusion on genotoxicity should be based on the evidence available for SG (for details, see EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023).

Negative results for SG were reported in a gene mutation assay in mammalian cells (mouse Lymphoma assay L5178Y Gene Mutation Assay), in some gene mutation tests in bacteria and in an in vitro micronucleus test with human peripheral lymphocytes, whereas positive results were reported in some other Ames tests and in an in vitro Comet assay in four different cell lines (including human cells). Co‐treatment with antioxidants reduced the effect. In vivo, SG induced a statistically significant, dose‐related increase in the frequency of chromosomal aberrations and sister chromatid exchanges (SCE) in the bone marrow of mice after i.p. administration.

Overall, data show that SG (i) intercalates between DNA base pairs; (ii) induces in vitro and in vivo DNA strand breaks associated with oxidative damage; (iii) induces in vivo structural chromosome aberrations; (iv) increases the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Therefore, SG has the potential to produce ROS in proximity of the double helix of DNA and induce oxidative DNA damage. The FEEDAP Panel notes that although the induction of oxidative DNA damage through the increased formation of ROS is the most likely cause of the observed genotoxic effects of SG, based on the available data, it is not possible to discriminate definitively between the genotoxic effects due to the direct binding of SG to DNA and the induction of DNA damage through other mechanisms.

Conclusions on the safety for the consumer

The FEEDAP Panel confirms that a concern for genotoxicity is identified owing to the presence of the DNA intercalator SG in MCE. Based on the structural similarity with SG, the same conclusions apply to CH.

No new data were submitted that would allow a reconsideration of the previous exposure assessment or the selection of the reference point (i.e. the TTC for genotoxic and carcinogenic substances).

Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of Sangrovit® Extra for consumers when used in feed for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae.

CONCLUSIONS

The FEEDAP Panel cannot conclude on the safety of Sangrovit® Extra for consumers when used in feed for suckling and weaned piglets and other growing Suidae.
