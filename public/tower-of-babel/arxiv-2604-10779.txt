3.
Stack-Sorting on
S_{n}^{\prime}

In this section, we examine the evolution of
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
under repeated applications of
s
. We begin by defining
column
and
block
sequences, which arise from identifying the entries of
s^{i-1}(\pi)
sent to the right of
0
after a single application of
s
.

Definition 3.1
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
and
1\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle
, define the recurrent column sequence
C_{\pi,i}=(c_{\pi,i,j})_{j\geq 1}
such that

c_{\pi,i,1}:=\max(s^{i-1}(\pi)[:\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(0)])

and

c_{\pi,i,j}:=\max(s^{i-1}(\pi)[\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(c_{\pi,i,j-1})+1:\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(0)])

for all
j\geq 2
so long as
\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(c_{\pi,i,j-1})+1<\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(0)
; otherwise, the recursion terminates.

Definition 3.2
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
and
1\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle
, define the block sequence
B_{\pi,i}=(b_{\pi,i,j})_{j=1}^{|C_{\pi,i}|}
such that

b_{\pi,i,1}:=s^{i-1}(\pi)[:\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(c_{\pi,i,1})]

and

b_{\pi,i,j}:=s^{i-1}(\pi)[\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(c_{\pi,i,j-1})+1:\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(c_{\pi,i,j})]

for all
2\leq j\leq|C_{\pi,i}|
.

Immediately, we make three basic observations on the properties of
C_{\pi,i}
and
B_{\pi,i}
.

(1)

C_{\pi,i}
is a sequence of right-to-left maxima on the prefix of
s^{i-1}(\pi)
to the left of
0
.

(2)

c_{\pi,i,j}
is greater than
\max(b_{\pi,i,j})
for all
1\leq j\leq|C_{\pi,i}|
.

(3)

s^{i-1}(\pi)[:\ind_{s^{i-1}(\pi)}(0)]=b_{\pi,i,1}c_{\pi,i,1}\dots b_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|}c_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|}
.

We next express the prefix of
s^{i}(\pi)
to the left of
0
strictly in terms of
B_{\pi,i}
. The following lemma is adapted from Lemma 3.2 of Chen, Luo, and Zhang
[
1
]
to the setting of
s
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
and
1\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle
,

s^{i}(\pi)[:\ind_{s^{i}(\pi)}(0)]=s(b_{\pi,i,1})s(b_{\pi,i,2})\dots s(b_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|}).

Proof.

Let
t_{j}:=j+2(|b_{\pi,i,1}|+|b_{\pi,i,2}|+\dots+|b_{\pi,i,j}|)
for all
0\leq j\leq|C_{\pi,i}|
. By induction on
j
, we prove that when
s
is applied to
s^{i-1}(\pi)
,

•

the remaining input permutation begins with
b_{\pi,i,j+1}\dots c_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|}0,

•

the stack consists of
c_{\pi,i,j}c_{\pi,i,j-1}\dots c_{\pi,i,1}
from top to bottom, and

•

the output permutation is
s(b_{\pi,i,1})s(b_{\pi,i,2})\dots s(b_{\pi,i,j})

after
t_{j}
push and pop operations. For
j=0
, the claim is trivial. Now assume that the claim holds for
j-1\geq 0
. After
t_{j-1}
operations, the remaining input permutation begins with
b_{\pi,i,j}c_{\pi,i,j}
where
c_{\pi,i,j}>\max(b_{\pi,i,j})
. Further, the top element of the stack becomes
c_{\pi,i,j-1}>\max(b_{\pi,i,j})
if
j-1>0
, and the stack is empty otherwise. In either case, elements already in the stack can never be popped out, and
c_{\pi,i,j}
can never be pushed in until after all elements of
b_{\pi,i,j}
are processed. Thus, in the following
2|b_{\pi,i,j}|+1=t_{j}-t_{j-1}
additional operations,
s(b_{\pi,i,j})
is appended to the output permutation, and then
c_{\pi,i,j}
is pushed into the stack, which completes the inductive proof.

In particular, by applying the claim to
j=|C_{\pi,i}|
, we observe that the output permutation is
s(b_{\pi,i,1})s(b_{\pi,i,2})\dots s(b_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|})
when
0
is the first element of the remaining input permutation. Once
0
is pushed in and immediately popped out by the stack, we finally conclude that

s^{i}(\pi)[:\ind_{s^{i}(\pi)}(0)]=s(b_{\pi,i,1})s(b_{\pi,i,2})\dots s(b_{\pi,i,|C_{\pi,i}|}).

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
,

\bigsqcup_{1\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle}\left\{c_{\pi,i,j}:1\leq j\leq|C_{\pi,i}|\right\}=[n].

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.4
,
\left\{c_{\pi,i,j}:1\leq j\leq|C_{\pi,i}|\right\}
is precisely the set of all elements sent to the right of
0
when
s
is applied to
s^{i-1}(\pi)
. Since every element of
[n]
is eventually sent to the right of
0
, the union of all such sets over
1\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle
must be
[n]
. Moreover, because elements that are sent to the right of
0
remain there under subsequent applications of
s
, these sets are pairwise disjoint.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}^{\prime}
and
2\leq i\leq\langle\pi\rangle
, the sequence
C_{\pi,i}
is a subsequence of
(\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j}))_{j=1}^{|C_{\pi,i-1}|}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.4
, both
C_{\pi,i}
and
(\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j}))_{j=1}^{|C_{\pi,i-1}|}
occur as subsequences of
s^{i-1}(\pi)
. It therefore suffices to prove that
C_{\pi,i}
is a subset of
(\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j}))_{j=1}^{|C_{\pi,i-1}|}
. Let
j_{k}
be the unique index for which
c_{\pi,i,k}\in b_{\pi,i-1,j_{k}}
for all
1\leq k\leq|C_{\pi,i}|
. If
c_{\pi,i,k}\neq\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j_{k}})
, then
\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j_{k}})
must be between
c_{\pi,i,k}
and
0
in
s^{i-1}(\pi)
by
Lemma

3.4
, which contradicts the definition of
c_{\pi,i,k}
. Hence,
c_{\pi,i,k}=\max(b_{\pi,i-1,j_{k}})
for all
1\leq k\leq|C_{\pi,i}|
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
and
k\geq 0
. The permutation

is sorted, and its elements are all greater than
\pi_{n}
.

Proof.

We induct on
k
. For
k=0
, the permutation
\pi[\ind_{\pi}(\pi_{n})+1:]
is empty, so the claim is vacuously true. Now assume that the claim holds for
k-1\geq 0
. Consider the moment immediately after
\pi_{n}
is popped from the stack when
s
is applied to
s^{k-1}(\pi)
. Let the remainder of the input permutation be
r=r_{1}r_{2}\dots r_{i}
, and let the current elements of the stack from top to bottom be
t=t_{1}t_{2}\dots t_{j}
. By the induction hypothesis,
r
is sorted, and its elements are all greater than
\pi_{n}
. Furthermore, because the stack always maintains a strictly increasing order and because
\pi_{n}
was just popped out of the stack,
t
must be sorted, and its elements are also all greater than
\pi_{n}
.

Now, if
t_{1}<r_{1}
, then
t_{1}
is popped out. If
t_{1}>r_{1}
, then
r_{1}
is pushed into the stack and popped out immediately. In either case, the least element is always appended to the output permutation. Thus, after
i+j
iterations, the subpermutation of
s^{k}(\pi)
to the right of
\pi_{n}
will be sorted, and its elements are all greater than
\pi_{n}
.
∎

s
to
s^{k-1}(\pi[:n])
. Thus, we conclude that the ordering of
s^{k-1}({\pi[:n]})
upon applying
s
is never disrupted by adding
\pi_{n}
, and
s^{k}(\pi)
always includes the subpermutation
s^{k}(\pi[:n])
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
and
1\leq m\leq n
. The permutation
s(\pi_{\geq m})
occurs as a subpermutation of
s(\pi)
.

Proof.

We use strong induction on
n
. For
n=1
, the claim trivially holds. Now assuming that the claim holds true for all
n\leq\ell-1
where
\ell\geq 2
, we aim to prove that the claim holds for all
\pi\in S^{\ell}
. If we express
\pi
as
LnR
, then
s(\pi)=s(L)s(R)n
by the recursive definition of the stack-sorting map. Similarly, if we express
\pi_{\geq m}
as
L_{\geq m}nR_{\geq m}
, then
s(\pi_{\geq m})=s(L_{\geq m})s(R_{\geq m})n.
Notably,
L_{\geq m}
and
R_{\geq m}
are subpermutations of
L
and
R
respectively, so under the induction hypothesis,
s(L_{\geq m})
and
s(R_{\geq m})
occur as subpermutations of
s(L)
and
s(R)
respectively since
|L|,|R|\leq\ell-1
. Therefore, we conclude that
s(\pi_{\geq m})=s(L_{\geq m})s(R_{\geq m})n
occurs as a subpermutation of
s(\pi)=s(L)s(R)n.

∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\pi\in S_{n}
,
1\leq m\leq n
, and
k\geq 0
. The permutation
s^{k}(\pi_{\geq m})
occurs as a subpermutation of
s^{k}(\pi)
.

Proof.

We induct on
k
. For
k=0
, the claim trivially holds. Now suppose that the claim holds for
k-1
. Then,
s^{k-1}(\pi_{\geq m})
occurs as a subpermutation of
s^{k-1}(\pi_{\geq m})
. More precisely, observe that

s^{k-1}(\pi_{\geq m})=s^{k-1}(\pi)_{\geq m},

so by applying
Lemma

5.3
, we find that
s(s^{k-1}(\pi)_{\geq m})=s^{k}(\pi_{\geq m})
is a subpermutation of
s(s^{k-1}(\pi))=s^{k}(\pi)
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For all
\pi\in S_{n}
, it holds that
\langle\pi\rangle=\max(\langle\pi[:n]\rangle,\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle)
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

5.2
and
Corollary

5.4
, it is evident that

\langle\pi\rangle\geq\max(\langle\pi[:n]\rangle,\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle)

because
s^{k}(\pi[:n])
and
s^{k}(\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}})
are both subpermutations of
s^{k}(\pi)
for all
k\geq 0
. If
\langle\pi\rangle>\langle\pi[:n]\rangle
, then
\pi_{n}
must be the only element out of order in
s^{\langle\pi[:n]\rangle}(\pi)
. Note that
\pi_{n}
is always to the right of elements less than
\pi_{n}
, so when the subpermutation
s^{\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle}(\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}})
is sorted,
s^{\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle}(\pi)
must then also be sorted.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.5
.

We induct on
n
. For
n=1
, the claim is trivial because
\pi[:2]_{\geq\pi_{1}}=\pi
. Now assume that the claim holds for
n-1\geq 1
. By
Lemma

5.5
,

\displaystyle\langle\pi\rangle

\displaystyle=\max(\langle\pi[:n]\rangle,\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\max\left(\max_{i\in[n-1]}(\langle\pi[:n][:i+1]_{\geq\pi[:n]_{i}}\rangle),\langle\pi_{\geq\pi_{n}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{i\in[n]}(\langle\pi[:i+1]_{\geq\pi_{i}}\rangle).

∎

6.
Future Directions

We conclude by providing some insight into how one may generalize our approach to
S_{n}
. Note that

1.5
implies

\langle\pi\rangle=\max_{i\in[n]}\left(w\left(\alpha_{\std\left(\pi[:i+1]_{\geq\pi_{i}}\right)}\right)\right).

Thus, exploring the evolution of the sequence

, which prohibits the same dynamic programming argument from succeeding.
