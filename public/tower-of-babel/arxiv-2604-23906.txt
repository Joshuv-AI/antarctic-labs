Proof strategy

Rosenfeld’s

The backward direction is immediate from the definition.

3.
Sieving techniques

In order to carry out our proof strategy, we need to verify that
J(k,p)=\emptyset
.
The first approach is to check all tuples in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{pl}
to verify that
I(k,p,l)=\emptyset
,
but this entails checking approximately
(pl)^{k}
tuples.
Moreover, for reasons to be given in Remark
3.2
,
we need

, so this approach becomes very laborious even for
k=8

[

Before we motivate them, let us at once formalize and validate the two techniques that allows for efficient verification in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 2
and prime
p

Let

We also have

•

Proof.

The second case is obvious. In the first case, note that

\begin{split}\pi_{p}S^{\prime}&=\pi_{p}\left(\pi_{clp\rightarrow lp}^{-1}S\cap I(k,p,cl)\right)\\
&=\pi_{p}\pi_{clp\rightarrow lp}^{-1}S\cap\pi_{p}I(k,p,cl)\\
&=\pi_{p}S\cap\pi_{p}I(k,p,cl).\end{split}

As

is a subset of
\pi_{p}S
by assumption and a subset of
\pi_{p}I(k,p,cl)

it is also a subset of
\pi_{p}S^{\prime}
.

The speed tuple

As

3.2.
Backward projection

Backward projection is just the application of
\pi_{p}
to pull back the speed tuples to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}_{p}
.
Just as lifting can reduce the size of the search space, projection achieves a similar effect.
This is due to the fact that for any set
S
,

\left|\pi_{p}S\right|\leq\left|S\right|.

The only time projecting becomes counterproductive is if we lift afterwards with the same constant
c
that was previously used,
because then we are intersecting with the same improper set and hence no speed tuples can be further eliminated.

Therefore, in general, we can lift with
c=2
successively, then project back,
then lift with
c=3
successively and project back, and so on.
This is the strategy that we use in our verification algorithms in Subsection
5.2
.

4.
Eventual properness of

(1,2,\ldots,k)

According to Remark
3.2
, when
k+1
is prime, we have to lift with the multiplier
c=k+1
.
This lift is extremely computationally intensive: for
k=12
, the
c=k+1
lift requires checking
(13/2)^{12}\approx 5\cdot 10^{9}

times more tuples than the

lift (for fixed

If we are to verify the conjecture for

in a reasonable time, we need to avoid doing this lift computationally.
For many primes

with
c=2
several times and projecting back,
the only tuples that cannot be shown eventually proper is, up to symmetry,
(1,2,\ldots,10)
.
The analogous observation holds when
k=12
.

Therefore, the aim of this section is to determine the eventual properness
of
(1,2,\dots,k)
.
We emphasize that our method still requires

with
c=k+1
.
In fact, we merely are finding a solution for speed tuples in

in the

-ansatz,
but we do so analytically instead of computationally.

First, we prove the following Proposition

This proposition is combinatorially interesting in its own right.
For convenience, we write

N_{k}:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{k}:\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}\text{ and }\mathbf{v}\text{ has at least one zero coordinate }\right\}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
k+1
be an odd prime. For all vectors
\mathbf{v}\in N_{k}
,
there are
s
and

\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{\times}
such that

s\mathbf{v}+r(1,2,\dots,k)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}.

Proof.

In this proof, we work in the field
\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
.
Define

V_{m}=\left\{v_{i}+mi:i\in\left\{1,\ldots,k\right\}\right\}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{\times}

We say

is

and

otherwise.
Let
G
be the set of good

Now, as
\mathbf{v}
contains at least one zero coordinate, it contains at most

For each non-zero coordinate

such that

,
whereas for each zero coordinate, there cannot be such

Thus, there are at most

This implies that
\left|G\right|\geq 1
.

We continue the proof by contrapositive. Assume that for all

then we will show that
\mathbf{v}=0

Let

Thus, there is an index

As

As

.

From
V_{m}=\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{\times}
when
m
is good, we have that

k!=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}+mi)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}i\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}i^{-1}+m)=k!\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}i^{-1}+m),

which gives

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}i^{-1}+m)=1.

On the other hand, when
m
is bad, there is an index
i
such that
v_{i}+mi=0
, hence

\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}i^{-1}+m)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}+mi)i^{-1}=0.

Therefore, the polynomial

P(X):=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(v_{i}i^{-1}+X)

is the indicator function of
G
on
\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{\times}
.
Note also that since
\mathbf{v}
contains at least one zero coordinate,
P(0)=0
.

As
k+1
is prime, Fermat’s Little Theorem gives another indicator function of
G
, which is

Q(X):=\sum_{m\in G}(1-(X-m)^{k}),

where the sum is not empty as
\left|G\right|\geq 1
.
It also follows that
Q
is a polynomial of degree
k
.

As
P(0)=0=Q(0)
,
P
and
Q
are polynomials of degree at most
k
that agree on
k+1
points, hence
P=Q
.
In particular, their leading coefficients must be equal, that is

1\equiv-\left|G\right|\pmod{k+1}
. Since
\left|G\right|\leq k
as integers, we must have that
\left|G\right|=k
.
Therefore, there is no bad
m
. Specifically, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
v_{i}+mi\neq 0

for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.
However, this implies
v_{i}=0
for all
i
, that is,
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}
.
∎

The strategy we will later use to prove Proposition
4.4
is decoupling and limiting dependency on the prime
p
.
Instead of considering times of the form
t=\frac{a}{(k+1)p}
, we decompose them into the partial fractions
t=\frac{s}{k+1}+\frac{r}{p}
.
The former term has no dependence of
p
. To capture the effect of
p
of the latter term, we define

\mathbf{r}_{k}(t):=\left(\lfloor(k+1)\{t\}\rfloor,\;\lfloor(k+1)\{2t\}\rfloor,\;\ldots,\;\lfloor(k+1)\{kt\}\rfloor\right)

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Intuitively,
\lfloor(k+1)\{it\}\rfloor=x
means that the runner with speed
i
is the arc

\left[\frac{x}{k+1},\frac{x+1}{k+1}\right)
of the unit circle at time
t
.
With this discretization, we state Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k+1
be an odd prime. For every
\mathbf{v}\in N_{k}
, there are
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
for which

s\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{r}_{k}(\tfrac{r}{k+1})\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}

when considered modulo
k+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}
be any speed tuple in
N_{k}
.
By Proposition
4.1
,
there exist
s,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}^{\times}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
such that

s\mathbf{v}+r(1,2,\dots,k)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}.

By definition,

\begin{split}\mathbf{r}_{k}\left(\tfrac{r}{k+1}\right)&=\left(\left\lfloor(k+1)\left\{\tfrac{r}{k+1}\right\}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor(k+1)\left\{\tfrac{2r}{k+1}\right\}\right\rfloor,\ldots,\left\lfloor(k+1)\left\{\tfrac{kr}{k+1}\right\}\right\rfloor\right)\\
&=(r,2r,\ldots,kr)\\
&=r(1,2,\dots,k)\end{split}

modulo
k+1
. Therefore,

s\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{r}_{k}\left(\tfrac{r}{k+1}\right)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k},

modulo
k+1
.
∎

A corollary of Lemma
4.2
is that
(1,2,\dots,k)
is eventually
(k,k+1)
-proper, but this is trivial.
Indeed, for
p=k+1
, we only need that

s\mathbf{v}+r(1,2,\dots,k)\in\{1,\ldots,k\}^{k}

to show eventual
(k,p)
-properness of
(1,2,\dots,k)
.
However, proving the stronger statement that

s\mathbf{v}+r(1,2,\dots,k)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}

in Proposition
4.1
allows us to extend the result to larger primes via the next lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k+1
and
p>k(k+1)
be odd primes. For every
\mathbf{v}\in N_{k}
,
there are
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
for which

s\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{r}_{k}(r/p)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}.

when considered modulo
k+1
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.2
, we have that there is
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
for which

s\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{r}_{k}(r/(k+1))\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}.

Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathbf{r}_{k}(\tfrac{1}{k+1}\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathbf{r}_{k}(\tfrac{1}{p}\mathbb{Z})
for all
p>k(k+1)
.

Fix
p
to be a prime greater than
k(k+1)
. Notice that
\mathbf{r}_{k}
is a piecewise constant function. Moreover,

x
is a discontinuity of
\mathbf{r}_{k}
if and only if it is a discontinuity of
\lfloor(k+1)\{ix\}\rfloor
for some
i
.
Those points of discontinuity are precisely points of the form
\frac{a}{i(k+1)}
for integer
a
and
1\leq i\leq k
.

Choose an integer
n
and denote
x:=\tfrac{n}{k+1}
. Observe that
x
is a discontinuity of
\mathbf{r}_{k}
and let
y
be
the smallest number greater than
x
that is also a discontinuity of
\mathbf{r}_{k}
. We know that
y
can be written as

\tfrac{a}{i(k+1)}
for some integer
a
and
1\leq i\leq k
, so it follows that

y-x=\frac{a}{i(k+1)}-\frac{n}{k+1}=\frac{a-ni}{i(k+1)}\geq\frac{1}{i(k+1)}\geq\frac{1}{k(k+1)}.

As
y
was chosen to be the smallest such discontinuity,
\mathbf{r}_{k}
is constant on the interval
[x,y)
.
Since the interval’s width is at least
\tfrac{1}{k(k+1)}>\tfrac{1}{p}
,
there must be an integer
m
such that
\tfrac{m}{p}\in[x,y)
.
For such value
m
,
\mathbf{r}_{k}(\frac{n}{k+1})=\mathbf{r}_{k}(\frac{m}{p})
.
As
n
is arbitrary, the desired inclusion follows.
∎

With this lemma, we finally obtain the headline result that assures eventual properness of
(1,2,\dots,k)
for
p>k(k+1)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that
k+1
and
p>k(k+1)
are odd primes.
Then, every speed tuple in
\pi_{(k+1)p\rightarrow p}^{-1}(1,2,\dots,k)
is
(k,p,k+1)
-proper,
so in particular
(1,2,\dots,k)
is eventually
(k,p)
-proper.

Proof.

We will show that every
\mathbf{u}\in\pi_{p(k+1)\to p}^{-1}(1,2,\dots,k)
is
(k,p,k+1)
-proper,
and hence
(1,2,\dots,k)
is eventually
(k,p)
-proper.
Let
u_{i}=a_{i}p+i
for some
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{k+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
.
By considering a time of the form
t=\frac{s}{k+1}+\frac{r}{p}
for
s\in\mathbb{Z}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
note that the fractional part of
tu_{i}
is given by

\{tu_{i}\}=\left\{\left(\frac{s}{k+1}+\frac{r}{p}\right)(a_{i}p+i)\right\}=\left\{\frac{su^{\prime}_{i}}{k+1}+\frac{ri}{p}\right\}

where
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}:=\pi_{p(k+1)\to k+1}\mathbf{u}
.

If
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
, then it follows that
k+1\mid\gcd(\mathbf{u})
, thus

k+1\mid\gcd(k+1,\mathbf{u})

hence
\mathbf{u}
is
(k,p,k+1)
-proper because it satisfies the gcd condition.

If
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
contains no zero coordinate, then pick
r=0
and
s=1
and we have

\{tu_{i}\}=\left\{\frac{u^{\prime}_{i}}{k+1}\right\}.

Since
u_{i}^{\prime}\neq 0
,
\lVert tu_{i}\rVert\geq\frac{1}{k+1}
for all
i
.

Otherwise,
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in N_{k}
, so by Lemma
4.3
there is a choice of
r
and
s
such that

s\mathbf{u}^{\prime}+\mathbf{r}_{k}(r/p)\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}^{k}

when considered modulo
k+1
.
This means that for each coordinate
i
, there are
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
such that

\{\frac{sv_{i}}{k+1}\}=\frac{A_{i}}{k+1}
and

\{\frac{ri}{p}\}\in\left[\frac{B_{i}}{k+1},\frac{B_{i}+1}{k+1}\right)

where
C_{i}:=A_{i}+B_{i}
is in
\left\{1,\ldots,k-1\right\}
when considered modulo
k+1
.
Therefore,

\{tu_{i}\}=\left\{\frac{su_{i}}{k+1}+\frac{ri}{p}\right\}\in\left[\frac{C_{i}}{k+1},\frac{C_{i}+1}{k+1}\right)

and in particular the set on the right-hand side does not intersect with
[0,\frac{1}{k+1})\cup(\frac{k}{k+1},1)
.
Since this holds for every
i
,
\mathbf{u}
has the LR property.

As there exists a witness time
t=\frac{s}{k+1}+\frac{r}{p}\in\frac{1}{p(k+1)}\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbf{u}
is
(k,p,k+1)
-proper.
∎

Proposition
4.4
implies Proposition
1.4
:
suppose that
\gcd(\mathbf{v})=1
and
\pi_{p}(\mathbf{v})=(1,2,\dots,k)
, then we have shown in Proposition
4.4

that
\pi_{(k+1)p}(\mathbf{v})
is
(k,p,k+1)
-proper.
If it is proper due to the gcd condition, then as
\gcd(\mathbf{v})=1

),

\mathbf{v}
has the LR property.
Otherwise, there is a time

such that

for all

.

5.
Implementation

5.1.
Equivalence of speed tuples

It is clear that some speed tuples in an ansatz are essentially the same, so we only need to verify properness for one of them.
We say that two speed tuples
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p,1}^{k}
are
equivalent
if
\mathbf{v}
can be obtained from
\mathbf{u}

by performing a finite number of the following actions:

•

Permuting the coordinates of
\mathbf{u}

•

Flipping the sign of a coordinate
u_{i}
to
-u_{i}

•

Multiplying
\mathbf{u}
by some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

The first two actions are used in both
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
.
For completeness, we show that this equivalence indeed preserves eventual properness.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
p
be prime and
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p,1}^{k}
be equivalent.
If
\mathbf{u}
is eventually
(k,p)
-proper, then so is
\mathbf{v}
.

Proof.

We only need to show that each of the aforementioned actions preserves eventual properness.
This is trivial for permuting.
Suppose
\mathbf{u}
is eventually proper and
l
is the integer for which every element in
\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{u})
is proper,
where
\pi:=\pi_{lp\rightarrow p}
. Now let
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{v})
.

If
\mathbf{v}=(u_{1},\ldots,-u_{i},\ldots,u_{k})
,
then
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=(a_{1}p+u_{1},\ldots,a_{i}p-u_{i},\ldots,a_{k}p+u_{k})
for some integers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
.
In this case, pick

\mathbf{u}^{\prime}:=(a_{1}p+u_{1},\ldots,(l-a_{i})p+u_{i},\ldots,a_{k}p+u_{k})\in\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{u}).

If
\mathbf{v}=a\mathbf{u}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
,
then by Chinese Remainder Theorem there is
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{lp}
such that
b\equiv a^{-1}\pmod{p}
and
b\equiv 1\pmod{l}
.
In particular,
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{lp}^{\times}
and

\mathbf{u}^{\prime}:=b\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\pi^{-1}(\mathbf{u})

because
\pi(b\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=\pi(b)\pi(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})=a^{-1}(a\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{u}
. We need
b
to be a unit because if a time
t
is a witness for
\mathbf{u}
, then the time
b^{-1}t
is a witness for
\mathbf{v}
.

Then, whichever way
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
satisfies properness,
it can be checked that
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}
also satisfies properness in the same way.
Therefore every speed tuple in
\pi(\mathbf{v})
is proper, and thus
\mathbf{v}
is eventually proper.
∎

Therefore, we may only verify the eventual properness for a single element in each equivalence class.
We implement this by fixing by ordering the speeds ascendingly,
limiting the speeds to be in the first half of residues modulo
p
, and fixing
v_{1}
to be
1
.
In other words, we check all tuples of integers
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
with

1=v_{1}\leq v_{2}\leq\cdots\leq v_{k}\leq\frac{p-1}{2}.

We should stress that this reduction is essential to obtaining our results.
There are approximately
p^{k}
tuples in the
(k,p,1)
-ansatz,
but with this reduction we only need to check a much smaller number of approximately

\binom{p/2}{k-1}\approx\frac{p^{k}}{2^{k}(k-1)!}.

Compared to the implementation of
[
10
]
and
[
15
]
,
we reduce the number of tuples to check by a factor of approximately
p/k
.

5.2.
Verification algorithms

Fix
k\in\{10,11,12\}
and a prime
p
. Our goal is to verify that
J(k,p)=\emptyset

in order to apply Corollary
2.5
. To do this, we use a verification algorithm that consists of two phases:
computing
I(k,p,1)
and repeated lifting.

The first phase is done by choosing representative speed tuples as described in the previous subsection.
We use lifting diagram to explain the second phase. In a lifting diagram, the first set
S_{1}
is always the precomputed
I(k,p,1)
.
Two kinds of arrows correspond to two actions from Section
3
:

•

S_{n}\xrightarrow{\times c}S_{n+1}
indicates lifting from
S_{n}
to
S_{n+1}
with constant
c
.

•

S_{n}\xrightarrow{\div d}S_{n+1}
indicates projecting with
\pi_{dp\rightarrow p}
.

Since we only perform these two actions, Proposition
3.1
inductively asserts that

J(k,p)\subseteq\pi_{p}S_{n}
for every
n
.

For
k=11

k+1=12
only has small prime factors, namely
2
and
3
,
it is convenient to lift through its prime factors (and some extra sieves). Hence, the lifting diagram is

S_{1}\xrightarrow{\times 2}S_{2}\xrightarrow{\times 2}S_{3}\xrightarrow{\times 2}S_{4}\xrightarrow{\times 2}S_{5}\xrightarrow{\times 3}S_{6}\xrightarrow{\times 3}S_{7}.

If
S_{7}
is empty, we conclude that

is empty.

For

,

is prime.
In order to avoid the lift with

Therefore, the lifting diagram for

7.
Concluding remarks

In Section
4
, we mentioned that,
surprisingly, we only need to perform
c=2
lifts (more than once) to eliminate all speed tuples apart
from those whose projection modulo
p
is
(1,2,\dots,k)
, which is a
tight
speed tuple.
From Goddyn and Wong
[
7
]
, a speed tuple
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{k}
is said to be tight if the strict inequalities cannot simultaneously hold in
the statement of Conjecture
1.2
.

An easy continuity argument shows that if
\mathbf{v}
is not tight, then there is
T
and
\epsilon>0

such that every
t\in(T,T+\epsilon)
is a witness time for
\mathbf{v}
. Therefore, for any non-tight speed tuple
\mathbf{v}
, there is
L
such that for any
l\geq L
there is a witness time in
\frac{1}{lp}\mathbb{Z}
. Our observation suggests a far stronger statement, that there is a universal
L
for every non-tight, coprime
\mathbf{v}
.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
k+1
be a positive integer. There exists a constant
D
such that, for any integer
d\geq D
, every non-tight speed tuple
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}^{k}
with
\gcd(\mathbf{v})=1
has a witness time in
\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
.

On a different note, the algebraic method in Proposition
4.1
may be extensible to verify Conjecture
1.2
for other classes of speed tuples.

Looking ahead, the primary bottleneck in extending our results to
k=13
is the efficient computation of
I(k,p,1)
.
Progress here likely requires a better understanding of speed tuples that do not have a witness time in an ansatz,
which in turn could yield stronger pruning conditions and make the initial sieve more tractable.
