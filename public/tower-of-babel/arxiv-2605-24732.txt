Non-extendably shellable skeleta of simplices
Davide Bolognini and Paolo Sentinelli

Abstract
We disprove a long-standing open conjecture due to Simon stating that all skeleta of simplices are extendably shellable. In particular, for every d ⩾ 3 d\geqslant 3 we provide a pure d d -dimensional shellable simplicial complex which is not shelling completable.

Introduction

The simple idea of building a simplicial complex one piece after another is the reason why shellability plays a prominent role in combinatorics. It is impossible to provide an account of its importance here, due to the very large literature on this topic, but it is sufficient to recall that this notion could be useful to prove that a simplicial complex is Cohen-Macaulay over every field, connected in codimension one or with the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres.

Every pure simplicial complex of dimension
d
on
n
vertices lives in the so-called
d
-skeleton

S_{d,n}
of the
(n-1)
-simplex. The
d
-skeleton of a simplex is a shellable simplicial complex (it is the independence complex of a uniform matroid) and it appears to be a very regular object. In a paper
[
16
]
published in 1994, Simon conjectured that a property stronger than shellability, called
extendable shellability
, holds for
S_{d,n}
.

Simon’s Conjecture

[
16
, Conjecture 4.2.1]
:

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leqslant d<n
. Then
S_{d,n}
is extendably shellable.

A simplicial complex is extendably shellable if every partial shelling can be extended to a complete shelling, so Simon’s Conjecture states that a shelling of every shellable simplicial complex can be extended to a shelling of the whole
d
-skeleton in which it lives. If
d\in\{0,1,n-2,n-1\}
the statement is trivial. For
d=2
, Björner and Eriksson in
[
3
, Corollary 2]
proved the more general fact that the independence complexes of all rank
3
matroids are extendably shellable, so in particular
S_{2,n}
is, because it is the independence complex of the uniform matroid of rank
3
. They conjectured that all independence complexes of matroids have this property, but in
[
14
]
Hall proved that the boundary complex of an
11
-dimensional cross-polytope (a matroid of rank
12
) is not extendably shellable. In the present paper we show that the conjecture of Björner-Eriksson is also false for rank
\geqslant 4
uniform matroids, see Remark
4.4
. Bigdeli, Yazdan Pour and Zaare-Nahandi in
[
2
, Corollary 3.8]
and Dochtermann in
[
8
, Corollary 4.4]
proved the case
d=n-3
. For a simpler proof see
[
6
]
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors proposed a conjecture implying Simon’s conjecture. In
[
1
]
Benedetti and the first author found an infinite class of counterexamples to this stronger conjecture.

A related research line focused on the so-called
shelling completable
simplicial complexes, i.e. shellable complexes from which it is possible to complete the shelling of the skeleton. In
[
5
]
it is proved that vertex-decomposable simplicial complexes are shelling completable. This result was generalized to
1
-decomposable complexes in
[
12
]
, up to adding new vertices.

We recall here Stanley’s words from
[
17
, pag. 84]
: "Though this conjecture is probably false, so far it has resisted all attempts to find a counterexample". In this paper we find such a counterexample.

In Section
2
we provide a brief and essential background on notation and preliminaries. Section
3
introduces the crucial construction (see Definition
3.8
, Theorems
3.10
and
3.11
): given a
2
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Gamma
with suitable properties, we construct a
3
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta_{\Gamma}
, which is a counterexample to Simon’s conjecture, i.e. a shellable not shelling completable simplicial complex. In Theorem
4.2
we provide a contractible
d
-dimensional counterexample for
d\geqslant 3
. In Section
4.2
we list explicitly the facets of a
3
-dimensional counterexample on
16
vertices and its shelling.

2
Notation and preliminaries

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
,
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
0\leqslant d<n
. A pure simplicial complex
\Delta
on
n
vertices of dimension
d
is defined by a collection of subsets of
[n]
of cardinality
d+1
called
facets
.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
the set of facets of
\Delta
. Then

\Delta=\bigcup\limits_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}\mathcal{P}(F),

where
\mathcal{P}(X)
is the power set of a set
X
. For every
S\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
, let
\Gamma_{S}
be the simplicial complex defined by
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma_{S})=S
. We denote by
\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
the set of the minimal non-faces of
\Delta
with respect to inclusion, i.e.

\mathcal{N}(\Delta)=\min(\mathcal{P}([n])\setminus\Delta).

We write a facet by listing its vertices between brackets
\{\ldots\}
or
(\ldots)
. Given a face
F\in\Delta
, the
link
of
F
in
\Delta
is defined by

\mathrm{link}_{\Delta}(F):=\{G\in\Delta:F\cup G\in\Delta,F\cap G=\varnothing\}.

The
f
-vector
of
\Delta
is
f(\Delta)=(1,f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{d})
where
f_{i}:=|\{F\in\Delta:|F|=i+1\}|.
The
h
-vector

h(\Delta)=(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots,h_{d+1})
of
\Delta
is defined by the relation

h_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{d+1-j}{i-j}f_{j-1},

for every
0\leqslant i\leqslant d+1
.

We denote by
S_{d,n}
the
d
-skeleton of the
(n-1)
-simplex, i.e. the pure simplicial complex whose facets are

\mathcal{F}(S_{d,n})=\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|=d+1\}.

The
complement complex

\overline{\Delta}
of a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta\subseteq S_{d,n}
is defined by setting

\mathcal{F}(\overline{\Delta})=\mathcal{F}(S_{d,n})\setminus\mathcal{F}(\Delta).

Recall that a pure
d
-dimensional simplicial complex is
shellable
if and only if there exists a
shelling

F_{1},\cdots,F_{r}
of its facets, i.e. a linear order such that for every
j<i
, there exists
v\in F_{i}\setminus F_{j}
and
k<i
such that
F_{i}\setminus F_{k}=\{v\}
. The addition of the facet
F_{i}
to
\Gamma_{i-1}:=\Gamma_{\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}\}}
is called
i
-th shelling step. It is easy to prove that
F_{i}
contains a unique minimal non-face of
\Gamma_{i-1}
and that the intersection
\Gamma_{i-1}\cap\Gamma_{F_{i}}
is pure
(d-1)
-dimensional.

For a shellable simplicial complex
\Delta
we have
h_{i}\geqslant 0
and
h_{d+1}
equals the number of facets glued along all their boundary in their shelling step. Moreover,
\Delta
is contractible if and only if
h_{d+1}=0
.

Notice that if
\Delta
is shellable, then
\mathrm{link}_{\Delta}(F)
is shellable for every face
F\in\Delta
. In fact, given a shelling
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
of
\Delta
, a shelling of the link is
F_{1}\setminus F,\ldots,F_{r}\setminus F
, where we keep only
F_{i}
’s with
F\subseteq F_{i}
. See
[
18
, Lemma 8.7]
.

We say that
\Delta
is
extendably shellable
if it is shellable and, for every
S\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
such that
\Gamma_{S}
is shellable, there exists a shelling of
\Delta
starting with the facets of
S
.

The first example of shellable
2
-dimensional simplicial complex not extendably shellable is given in
[
16
, pag. 386]
. Other examples are given in
[
4
, Exercise 7.37]
,
[
13
, Proposition 5.7]
and
[
15
, Theorem A]
. It follows from results in
[
7
]
that any 2-dimensional sphere is extendably shellable. Ziegler
[
19
]
showed that there exist simplicial 4-polytopes that are not extendably shellable. Moreover, in
[
6
]
it is proved that all shellable
d
-dimensional complexes on at most
d+3
vertices are extendably shellable. See also
[
8
, Proposition 4.5]
for a numerical condition ensuring extendable shellability.

The following conjecture concerning the skeleta of the simplices is
[
16
, Conjecture 4.2.1]
. See also
[
17
, Ch. 2]
and
[
18
, Exercise 8.24(iii)*]
.

Conjecture 2.1

(Simon’s Conjecture)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leqslant d<n
. Then
S_{d,n}
is extendably shellable.

We say that a shellable pure simplicial complex
\Delta\subseteq S_{d,n}
is
shelling completable
(see
[
5
, Definition 1.2]
) if every shelling of
\Delta
can be completed to a shelling of
S_{d,n}
. In other words, Simon’s conjecture states that every shellable simplicial complex is shelling completable. Simon’s Conjecture is known for
d\leqslant 2
and
d\geqslant n-3
. Hence the first open case of the conjecture is for
n=7
and
d=3
.

3
Austere simplicial complexes

In this section we introduce the class of
austere
and the class of
quiet
simplicial complexes, and a construction, the
echo
, which provides an austere complex given a quiet one.

Definition 3.1
.

A pure
d
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta\subsetneq S_{d,n}
is austere if every
F\in\mathcal{F}(\overline{\Delta})
contains at least two minimal non-faces of
\Delta
.

The interest of this definition is due to the fact that an austere simplicial complex is not shelling completable, because no new facet may be added to a shelling of
\Delta
to provide a longer shelling. This provides the following formulation of Simon’s Conjecture.

Conjecture 3.2

(Simon’s conjecture rephrased)
.

Every austere simplicial complex is not shellable.

There are several examples of austere simplicial complexes. The trivial ones are pairs of disjoint facets, for instance
(1,2,3)
and
(4,5,6)
. It is not difficult to prove that every austere
2
-dimensional simplicial complex is disconnected. This does not hold for higher dimensions, as the following example shows.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq S_{3,6}
be the simplicial complex with facets

(1,2,3,4),(1,2,5,6),(3,4,5,6).

This is an example of austere connected simplicial complex. Notice that this example is not connected in codimension one.

The next examples provide austere simplicial complexes with various properties.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq S_{3,7}
be the simplicial complex with facets

\displaystyle(1,2,3,4),(2,3,4,5),(3,4,5,6),(4,5,6,7),(1,2,3,7),(1,2,6,7),(1,5,6,7).

This is an example of austere simplicial complex that is connected in codimension one, but it is not Cohen-Macaulay.

Example 3.5
.

Let
\Delta\subseteq S_{3,10}
be the simplicial complex with facets

\displaystyle(1,2,3,6),(1,2,3,8),(1,2,3,10),(1,2,5,6),(1,2,6,10),(1,2,8,10),

\displaystyle(1,3,6,7),(1,3,6,10),(1,3,8,10),(1,4,5,9),(1,4,8,10),(1,4,9,10),

\displaystyle(1,5,6,7),(1,5,6,9),(1,6,9,10),(2,3,4,6),(2,3,4,9),(2,3,6,10),

\displaystyle(2,3,8,9),(2,3,8,10),(2,7,8,10),(3,4,5,9),(3,4,6,7),(3,4,7,9),

\displaystyle(3,5,8,9),(3,5,8,10),(4,6,7,8),(4,7,8,10),(4,7,9,10),(5,6,7,8),

\displaystyle(5,6,8,9),(5,7,8,10)

This is an example of austere simplicial complex that is Cohen-Macaulay on a field of characteristic
\neq 2
. Since
\widetilde{H}_{1}(\Delta,\mathbb{F}_{2})\neq 0
, it is not shellable.

In the following proposition we prove that a
d
-dimensional austere simplicial complex has no minimal non-faces of cardinality
d+1
and it contains all possible vertices.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Delta
be a pure
d
-dimensional austere simplicial complex. Then for every
N\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
we have
2\leqslant|N|\leqslant d+2
with
|N|\neq d+1
.

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathcal{N}(\Delta)
. It is clear that
|N|\leqslant d+2
, otherwise
\Delta
has dimension greater than
d
. By contradiction, assume that
|N|=d+1
. This implies that
N\setminus\{v\}\in\Delta
, for every
v\in N
. Hence
N
is a facet of
\overline{\Delta}
and can be glued along all its boundary, i.e. it contains itself as unique minimal non-face. This contradicts austerity. Assume now
|N|=1
and let
v
be a missing vertex of
\Delta
. Given any
(d-1)
-dimensional face
e
of
\Delta
, then
e\cup\{v\}
contains only
v
as minimal non-face of
\Delta
. This contradicts the austerity of
\Delta
and we conclude.
∎

The main goal of next sections is to construct an austere shellable simplicial complex.

3.1
Quiet simplicial complexes and their echoes

Our aim is to construct austere
3
-dimensional simplicial complexes starting from pure
2
-dimensional simplicial complexes. To do this, we introduce the class of quiet simplicial complexes.

Definition 3.7
.

A pure
2
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Gamma\subsetneq S_{2,n}
is quiet if
S_{1,n}\subseteq\Gamma
and every
F\in\mathcal{F}(S_{3,n})
contains at most two facets of
\Gamma
.

Now we introduce a construction that, given
k\in\N
and a pure
2
-dimensional simplicial complex on
[k]
, provides a pure
3
-dimensional simplicial complex on
[2k]
. For
i\in[k]
define

C_{i}:=\{2i-1,2i\}.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq S_{2,k}
be a pure
2
-dimensional simplicial complex. The echo of
\Gamma
is the pure
3
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta_{\Gamma}\subseteq S_{3,2k}
defined by the following property:

F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{\Gamma})\iff\{i\in[k]:C_{i}\cap F\neq\varnothing\}\in\Gamma.

Example 3.9
.

Let
k=4
and consider the simplicial complex
\Gamma
such that
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)=\{(1,2,3),(2,3,4)\}
. Then
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is a simplicial complex on
[8]
. Its facets are

1.

the facets corresponding to the edges of
\Gamma
:

C_{1}\cup C_{2},C_{1}\cup C_{3},C_{2}\cup C_{3},C_{2}\cup C_{4},C_{3}\cup C_{4},

i.e.
(1,2,3,4),(1,2,5,6),(3,4,5,6),(3,4,7,8),(5,6,7,8)
;

2.

the facets corresponding to
(1,2,3)
:

\displaystyle(1,2,3,5),(1,2,3,6),(1,2,4,5),(1,2,4,6),(1,3,4,5),(1,3,4,6)

\displaystyle(2,3,4,5),(2,3,4,6),(1,3,5,6),(1,4,5,6),(2,3,5,6),(2,4,5,6);

3.

the facets corresponding to
(2,3,4)
:

\displaystyle(3,4,5,7),(3,4,5,8),(3,4,6,7),(3,4,6,8),(3,5,6,7),(3,5,6,8)

\displaystyle(4,5,6,7),(4,5,6,8),(3,5,7,8),(3,6,7,8),(4,5,7,8),(4,6,7,8).

It is clear from the definition that
S_{1,k}\subseteq\Gamma\implies S_{1,2k}\subseteq\Delta_{\Gamma}
,
i.e. if the
1
-skeleton of
\Gamma
is a complete graph, then the
1
-skeleton of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is a complete graph, because in such case
C_{i}\cup C_{j}
is a facet of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
for every
i,j\in[k]
,
i\neq j
.

Our interest in constructing echoes is due to the following result.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a quiet simplicial complex. Then
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is austere.

Proof.

Consider a facet
F\in\overline{\Delta_{\Gamma}}
, i.e.
F\notin\Delta_{\Gamma}
and
|F|=4
. From our assumptions, it follows that
\Delta_{\Gamma}
has full
1
-skeleton. Then, by definition of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
, we have two possibilities:

•

|\{i\in[k]:C_{i}\cap F\neq\varnothing\}|=3
. In this case there exists
i\in[k]
such that
F=C_{i}\cup\{a,b\}
, where
a\in C_{j},b\in C_{k}
, with
j\neq k
different from
i
. Since
S_{1,k}\subseteq\Gamma
, we have that
C_{i}\cup\{a\}
and
C_{i}\cup\{b\}
are faces of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
, and
\{2i-1,a,b\}
,
\{2i,a,b\}
are distinct minimal non-faces of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
contained in
F
.

•

|\{i\in[k]:C_{i}\cap F\neq\varnothing\}|=|\{a,b,c,d\}|=4
. In this case there are at least two facets of
\overline{\Gamma}
in
\{a,b,c,d\}
, say
\{a,b,c\}
and
\{a,b,d\}
, because
\Gamma
is quiet. Then in
F
there are at least two minimal non-faces of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
.

This proves that
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is austere.
∎

Consider now a pure shellable contractible simplicial complex
\Gamma
of dimension
2
. Given a shelling order
T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}
for
\Gamma
,
we provide a linear order of some facets of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
associated to
T\in\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is pure and contractible, we have only three possibilities.

•

T=T_{1}=\{a,b,c\}
. Assume
a<b<c
. Order lexicographically the set
\{C_{a}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{b},j\in C_{c}\}
. Then continue with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{b}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{c}\}
and then with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{c}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{b}\}
. End the linear order with
C_{a}\cup C_{b}<C_{a}\cup C_{c}<C_{b}\cup C_{c}
.

•

T=\{a,b,c\}
introduces exactly two new edges
\{a,c\}
and
\{b,c\}
. Assume
a<b
and order lexicographically the set
\{C_{a}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{b},j\in C_{c}\}
. Then continue with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{b}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{c}\}
and then with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{c}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{b}\}
. End the linear order with
C_{a}\cup C_{c}<C_{b}\cup C_{c}
.

•

T=\{a,b,c\}
introduces exactly one new edge
\{a,c\}
.
Assume
a<c
and order lexicographically the set
\{C_{b}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{c}\}
. Then continue with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{a}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{b},j\in C_{c}\}
and then with the lexicographic order of
\{C_{c}\cup\{i,j\}:i\in C_{a},j\in C_{b}\}
. End the linear order with
C_{a}\cup C_{c}
.

In the following theorem we use the linear order defined above, arising from a shelling order of a pure
2
-dimensional contractible simplicial complex
\Gamma
, to provide a shelling order for
\Delta_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a pure contractible shellable
2
-dimensional simplicial complex. Then
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is shellable.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is shellable, consider a shelling order
T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}
. Combining the linear orders arising from any facet
T_{i}
as described above, we obtain a linear order on the facets of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
. We prove that this is in fact a shelling order.

Consider two facets
F<G
of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
. If
|F\cap G|=3
there is nothing to prove. Then assume
|F\cap G|\leqslant 2
.
Let
T=\{i,j,r\}
be a facet of
\Gamma
from which
G
arises and
C_{i}=\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
,
C_{j}=\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
,
C_{r}=\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
.
We have three cases to treat.

1.

G=C_{i}\cup C_{j}
. Consider a vertex
v\in G\setminus F
. Then
(G\setminus\{v\})\cup\{2r\}
is a facet of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
and it appears before
G
in the linear order.

2.

G=C_{i}\cup\{j_{1},r_{1}\}
and
F
arises from
T
. If both
F
and
G
contain
C_{i}
, we have
F\cap G=C_{i}
. Without loss of generality assume
j<r
. Notice that
j_{1}\in G\setminus F
because
|F\cap G|\leqslant 2
. Then
(G\setminus\{j_{1}\})\cup\{j_{2}\}
is a facet of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
and it appears before
G
in the linear order. On the other hand, suppose that
F
contains
C_{j}
. Since
F<G
in our linear order and
F
arises from
T
as the facet
G
does,
F
is not of the type
C_{x}\cup C_{y}
and then there exists
i_{1}\in C_{i}
such that
C_{j}\cup\{i_{1}\}\subseteq F
and
i_{2}\in G\setminus F
. Therefore
(G\setminus\{i_{2}\})\cup\{j_{2}\}=C_{j}\cup\{i_{1},r_{1}\}\in\Delta_{\Gamma}
. Since the facet
F
contains
C_{j}
and
F<G
, we have that
C_{j}\cup\{i_{1},r_{1}\}<G
in our linear order.

3.

G=C_{i}\cup\{j_{1},r_{1}\}
and
F
does not arise from
T
. Then
F
arises from a facet
H
appearing before
T
in the shelling order of
\Gamma
. Hence there exists
a\in T\setminus H
and a facet
S<T
of
\Gamma
such that
T\setminus S=\{a\}
. There are two cases to be considered.

•

a\neq i
. If
a=j
then
T\cap S=\{i,r\}
and
j_{1}\in G\setminus F
. Let
S\setminus T=\{s\}
. Hence
(G\setminus\{j_{1}\})\cup\{2s\}
is a facet of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
and this facet appears before
G
in our linear order. We proceed analogously if
a=r
.

•

a=i
. In this case
T\cap S=\{j,r\}
. Let
S\setminus T=\{s\}
.
Notice that
2i\in G\setminus F
.
Assume first that
\{j,r\}
is the unique old edge. Then
\{i,j\}
and
\{i,r\}
are new edges introduced by
T
. In this case
(G\setminus\{2i\})\cup\{j_{2}\}=C_{j}\cup\{2i-1,r_{1}\}\in\Delta_{\Gamma}
and
C_{j}\cup\{2i-1,r_{1}\}

appears before
G
in our linear order.

Now assume that
\{j,r\}
is not unique as an old edge.
Let
\{i,j\}
be the other one. Then
\{i,r\}
is the unique new edge introduced by
T
.
Hence
(G\setminus\{2i\})\cup\{j_{2}\}=C_{j}\cup\{2i-1,r_{1}\}\in\Delta_{\Gamma}
and
C_{j}\cup\{2i-1,r_{1}\}
appears before
G
in our linear order.
If
\{i,j\}
is the unique new edge introduced by
T
then
(G\setminus\{2i\})\cup\{r_{2}\}=C_{r}\cup\{2i-1,j_{1}\}\in\Delta_{\Gamma}
and this facet appears before
G
in our linear order.

This ensures the shellability of
\Delta_{\Gamma}
and we are done.
∎

In the following example, we make clear that in the previous result we cannot drop the assumption "contractible".

Example 3.12
.

Let
\Gamma
be the simplicial complex with facets

(1,2,3),(1,2,4),(1,3,4),(2,3,4).

It is clearly a shellable

4
Counterexamples to Simon’s conjecture

By Theorems
3.10
and
3.11
, to find a counterexample to Simon’s conjecture it is sufficient to find a quiet shellable contractible complex
\Gamma
. In this case the
f
-vector of
\Gamma
is

f(\Gamma)=\left(1,k,\binom{k}{2},f_{2}\right).

It follows that the
h
-vector of
\Gamma
is

h(\Gamma)=\left(1,k-3,\binom{k-2}{2},f_{2}-\binom{k-1}{2}\right).

This immediately provides the condition
f_{2}=\binom{k-1}{2}
, due to the fact that
\Gamma
is shellable and contractible.

Notice that
k\geqslant 3
. For
k=3
,
\Gamma
has a unique facet
(1,2,3)
and
\Gamma
is not quiet. For
k=4
the unique possible choice (up to relabeling) is to consider the complex
(1,2,3),(1,2,4),(1,3,4)
, which is not quiet. For
k=5
, a simple numerical argument shows that a quiet complex on
5
vertices could have at most
5
facets, but we want one with
6
. A request to Chat GPT 5.5 Thinking for a quiet
2
-dimensional shellable simplicial complex on
k\geqslant 6
vertices satisfying these numerical constraints produced a simplicial complex
\Gamma
on
8
vertices and with
f
-vector
(1,8,28,21)
, whose facets are

\displaystyle(1,4,8),(3,4,8),(2,3,4),(3,4,7),(3,6,8),(3,5,6),(1,6,8),

\displaystyle(3,5,7),(2,3,6),(1,4,7),(1,6,7),(1,5,6),(2,6,7),(2,5,7),

\displaystyle(2,4,5),(1,2,5),(4,5,8),(1,2,8),(2,7,8),(1,2,3),(4,5,6).

It can be easily verified with Macaulay2 or Sagemath that this is a shelling of
\Gamma
, that
\Gamma
is quiet and that no facet of
\Gamma
is glued along all its boundary, i.e.
\Gamma
is contractible. A presentation of this simplicial complex is given in Section
4.1
.

By Theorems
3.10
and
3.11
, it follows that the echo
\Delta_{\Gamma}
is shellable and austere. This is our first counterexample to Simon’s conjecture in dimension
3
. Its
f
-vector is
(1,16,120,280,280)
and its
h
-vector is
(1,12,78,84,105)
. In Section
4.2
, we provide an explicit shelling of this simplicial complex by following the construction of the linear order presented above.

The next result provides counterexamples to Simon’s conjecture with a number of facets smaller than the previous.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a
d
-dimensional shellable austere simplicial complex. Then there exists a contractible shellable not shelling completable simplicial complex with the same
(d-1)
-skeleton of
\Delta
.

Proof.

If
\Delta
is contractible we have nothing to show, because austere simplicial complexes are not shelling completable. If it is not contractible, then in the shelling there are
h_{d+1}(\Delta)
facets attached along all their boundary in the corresponding shelling step. All these facets may be removed while preserving the
(d-1)
-skeleton. For this reason, no other facets may be attached to the simplicial complex obtained, except the removed ones. By construction, the resulting complex is also shellable and this concludes the proof.
∎

In other words, given the austere shellable simplicial complex above, we may remove all the
105
facets attached along their boundary (the facets in blue in the shelling of Section
4.2
) to obtain a shellable contractible not shelling completable simplicial complex with
f
-vector
(1,16,120,280,175)
and
h
-vector
(1,12,78,84,0)
.

Our last result ensures that, for every dimension
d\geqslant 3
, there exists a
d
-skeleton that is not extendably shellable.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
d\geqslant 3
. Then there exists a not shelling completable pure simplicial complex of dimension
d
on
d+13
vertices with
175
facets. In particular, the skeleton
S_{d,d+13}
is not extendably shellable, for
d\geqslant 3
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be our
3
-dimensional shellable not shelling completable counterexample with
175
facets. It is sufficient to prove that the cone
C(\Delta)
is not shelling completable. By contradiction, assume that
C(\Delta)
is shelling completable. Then there exists a shelling of
S_{4,17}
starting with the facets of
C(\Delta)
. Let
v
be the new vertex, i.e.
\mathrm{link}_{S_{4,17}}(v)=S_{3,16}
. Recall that a shelling of
\mathrm{link}_{S_{4,17}}(v)
can be induced by a shelling of
S_{4,17}
. From the equality
\mathrm{link}_{C(\Delta)}(v)=\Delta
, it follows that
\Delta
is shelling completable, but this is a contradiction. By iterating the cone operation we conclude.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Notice that Theorems
3.10
,
3.11
and
4.2
provide a machinery to produce shellable not vertex-decomposable simplicial complexes, thanks to

Remark 4.4
.

It is worth mentioning that

with a smaller example with respect to the classical counterexample constructed by Hall

.

Remark 4.5
.

Let
