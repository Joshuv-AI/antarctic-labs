The Extremum Stack is a Minimal Sufficient Statistic for Rate-Independent Functionals: A Kolmogorov Complexity Characterisation Journal: Information Processing Letters
Piotr Frydrych

Abstract
We prove that the extremum stack Π n \Pi_{n} of a discrete sequence u 0 : n ∈ 𝒢 L n + 1 u_{0:n}\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{n+1} is a minimal sufficient statistic for the class ℛ \mathcal{R} of all computable, causal, rate-independent functionals, in the sense of Kolmogorov complexity. Specifically, we establish: K ( Π n ) − O ( 1 ) ≤ K ℛ ( u 0 : n ) ≤ K ( Π n ) + O ( 1 ) , K(\Pi_{n})-O(1)\;\leq\;K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\;\leq\;K(\Pi_{n})+O(1), where K ℛ ( u 0 : n ) K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n}) is the length of the shortest program answering every query in ℛ \mathcal{R} , and the O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) overhead is independent of both the sequence length n n and the stack depth k k . Sufficiency follows from the classical wiping property of the Preisach hysteresis operator. Minimality is established via a finite indicator family whose rate-independence is verified explicitly. Any compression of a hysteresis-driven stream that preserves the full class ℛ \mathcal{R} must therefore retain at least K ⁡ ( Π n ) − O ⁡ ( 1 ) K(\Pi_{n})-O(1) bits; the stack-based representation is asymptotically optimal.



1
Introduction

A functional
F
on sequences is
rate-independent
if it
responds only to the sequence of local extrema, not to the
temporal spacing or absolute position of values

[
1
,
8
]
.
Rate-independence appears in ferromagnetic hysteresis, elastoplastic
systems, financial threshold models
[
2
]
, and —
more recently — in sequence-modelling architectures based on the
Preisach operator
[
4
]
.

A central data structure for computing rate-independent functionals
is the
extremum stack

\Pi_{n}
: the alternating sequence
of local maxima and minima of
u_{0:n}
, maintained in decreasing
order.
It is known that
\Pi_{n}
is a
sufficient
statistic for
all Preisach functionals
[
8
]
— that is, the
Preisach output at time
n
depends only on
\Pi_{n}
, not on
the full history
u_{0:n}
.

Contribution.

We prove that
\Pi_{n}
is also
minimal
: no strictly shorter
representation suffices for the full class
\mathcal{R}
of computable
rate-independent functionals.
The proof uses Kolmogorov complexity theory

[
6
]
and an explicitly constructed finite
indicator family.
We further show that the overhead incurred by the stack
representation is
O(1)
— a constant independent of both the
sequence length
n
and the stack depth
k
.
This tightens an earlier informal claim of
O(\log n)
overhead
and also an intermediate
O(\log K(\Pi_{n}))
bound: the correct
bound follows from the prefix-free invariance theorem

K(f(x))\leq K(x)+O(1)
for computable
f
, giving
O(1)
.

Implications.

The minimality result has two direct consequences:
(i) any lossless compression scheme for hysteresis-driven streams
that preserves
\mathcal{R}
-query answering must retain at least

K(\Pi_{n})-O(1)
bits, making the stack asymptotically optimal;
(ii) the stack provides a principled basis for an
O(n)
-time,

O(k)
-space online compression algorithm, where
k\leq n
is the
current stack depth.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Discrete sequences and the grid

Throughout, we fix a resolution
L\geq 2
and work with sequences
over the discrete grid

\mathcal{G}_{L}=\{0,\Delta,2\Delta,\ldots,1\}
,
\Delta=1/L
.
Restricting to
\mathcal{G}_{L}
is necessary: over
\mathbb{R}
, the indicator
family constructed in
section

4
is uncountable and
the Kolmogorov framework does not apply directly.

2.2
Rate-independent functionals

Definition 1

(Rate-independence)
.

A functional
F:\mathcal{G}_{L}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}
is
rate-independent
if

\Pi_{n}(u)=\Pi_{m}(v)\;\Longrightarrow\;F[u](n)=F[v](m)

for all
u\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{n+1}
,
v\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{m+1}
.
That is,
F
depends on the input history only through its extremum
stack, not through absolute timing, speed of change, or values at
non-extremal positions.
Let
\mathcal{R}
denote the class of all computable rate-independent
functionals on
\mathcal{G}_{L}^{*}
.

A functional is
causal
if
F[u](n)
depends only on the
prefix
u_{0},\ldots,u_{n}
; since
F
takes a prefix as input
throughout this paper, causality is implicit.

Remark 2

(Relation to time-reparameterisation)
.

In continuous time, rate-independence is classically stated via
non-decreasing surjections
\varphi:[0,T]\to[0,T]

[
1
]
.
In discrete time, a bijection
\varphi:\{0,\ldots,m\}\to\{0,\ldots,n\}
with
m=n
must be the identity, making
the condition vacuous.
The stack-based definition above is the correct discrete-time
analogue: two sequences are equivalent if and only if they produce
the same extremum stack, which captures the spirit of
“same order of extrema, regardless of timing.”

2.3
The extremum stack

Definition 3

(Extremum stack)
.

The
extremum stack
of
u_{0:n}\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{n+1}
is the
sequence

\Pi_{n}=[(M_{1},m_{1}),(M_{2},m_{2}),\ldots,(M_{k},m_{k})],

where pairs are ordered from
oldest
(bottom, index 1) to

newest
(top, index
k
).
Each pair
(M_{i},m_{i})
records a dominant local maximum
M_{i}

and the most recent local minimum
m_{i}
that followed it and
survived wiping.
The maxima are strictly decreasing from bottom to top:

M_{1}>M_{2}>\cdots>M_{k}
.
The minima are strictly
increasing
from bottom to top:

m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{k}
(each newer minimum is less extreme
than its predecessor).
Each pair satisfies
M_{i}>m_{i}
.
The depth
k=|\Pi_{n}|
satisfies
k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1
.

Remark 4

(Ordering convention)
.

The decreasing-maxima, increasing-minima ordering follows the
classical Preisach staircase geometry
[
8
]
:
the outermost loop has the largest maximum and smallest minimum;
inner loops are strictly contained.
In our notation,
(M_{1},m_{1})
is the outermost (oldest) pair and

(M_{k},m_{k})
is the innermost (newest).

The stack is updated in amortised
O(1)
time per step
(
algorithm

1
): each element is pushed and popped at most once.

Remark 5

(Correctness of Algorithm
1
)
.

A push occurs only when a direction reversal is detected,
i.e. when
e_{\mathrm{prev}}
is a
confirmed
local extremum.
During a monotone run (
d_{\mathrm{new}}=d
),
e_{\mathrm{prev}}

is updated but nothing is pushed, preserving the invariants
of Definition

The depth satisfies

since each push requires a direction reversal.
The algorithm is initialised with

2.4
Kolmogorov complexity

We use standard Kolmogorov complexity
[
6
]
.

K(x)
denotes the length of the shortest self-delimiting program
that outputs
x
on a fixed universal Turing machine
\mathcal{U}
.
For a class of functionals
\mathcal{R}
, we define:

Definition 6

(
\mathcal{R}
-query complexity)
.

K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})=\min\bigl\{|p|:\text{for all }F\in\mathcal{R},\;\mathcal{U}(p,F)=F[u](n)\bigr\},

the length of the shortest program that, given any
F\in\mathcal{R}

as an additional input, computes the answer
F[u](n)
.

3
Sufficiency of the Extremum Stack

Proposition 7

(Stack sufficiency)
.

Every
F\in\mathcal{R}
satisfies
F[u](n)=f(\Pi_{n})

for some computable function
f
.

Proof.

Immediate from
definition

1
: rate-independence means
F[u](n)

depends on
u_{0:n}
only through
\Pi_{n}(u)
.
For the connection to the Preisach integral,

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}[u](n)=\iint_{\mathcal{T}}\hat{\gamma}_{\alpha,\beta}[u](n)\,\mu(\alpha,\beta)\,d\alpha\,d\beta

depends only on
\Pi_{n}
by the classical wiping property

[
8
]
.
∎

Corollary 8

(Upper bound)
.

K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\leq K(\Pi_{n})+O(1)
.

Proof.

By
proposition

7
, any
F[u](n)
is computable from
\Pi_{n}

by a fixed program of length
O(1)
(independent of
n
,
k
,
F
).
Since
K
is defined via a prefix-free universal machine,
the standard invariance property gives
K(f(x))\leq K(x)+O(1)

for any computable
f

[
6
]
.
Embedding the shortest prefix-free program for
\Pi_{n}
and
appending the
O(1)
-length post-processor suffices; the prefix-free
encoding is self-delimiting so no additional overhead is needed.
∎

Remark 9

(On the overhead:
O(1)
vs
O(\log K)
)
.

An
O(\log n)
overhead is sometimes suggested informally.
This is incorrect: the depth
k
is implicit in the number of pairs
and need not be encoded separately.
A subtler claim of
O(\log K(\Pi_{n}))
overhead arises from
the chain rule
K(x,y)\leq K(x)+K(y)+O(\log\min\{K(x),K(y)\})

[
6
]
, which applies to pairs of
independently
chosen
objects.
Here, however,
F[u](n)
is obtained from
\Pi_{n}
by a

fixed
computable function; the invariance theorem

K(f(x))\leq K(x)+O(1)
applies directly, giving the tighter

O(1)
bound.

4
Minimality of the Extremum Stack

4.1
Indicator family

For each pair
(M,m)\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2}
with
M\geq m
, define the

indicator functional
:

F_{(M,m)}[u](n)=\mathbf{1}\bigl[(M,m)\in\Pi_{n}\bigr].

(1)

Lemma 10

(Rate-independence of indicators)
.

Each
F_{(M,m)}
defined in (
1
) belongs to
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

If
\Pi_{n}(u)=\Pi_{m}(v)
, then

(M,m)\in\Pi_{n}(u)\Leftrightarrow(M,m)\in\Pi_{m}(v)
,
so
F_{(M,m)}[u](n)=F_{(M,m)}[v](m)
.
Rate-independence follows directly from
definition

1
.
∎

Remark 11
.

F_{(M,m)}
is
not
the Preisach relay

\hat{\gamma}_{M,m}[u](n)
, which indicates whether the relay with
thresholds
(M,m)
is active, not whether
(M,m)
appears as a
stack entry.
The relay may be active even when
(M,m)\notin\Pi_{n}

(if
M
is not a local maximum of
u_{0:n}
).
The indicator (
1
) is a distinct, valid
rate-independent functional.

4.2
Minimality theorem

Lemma 12

(Stack reconstruction)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be any set and

R:\mathcal{G}_{L}^{n+1}\to\mathcal{S}
a representation such that
every
F\in\mathcal{R}
is computable from
R(u_{0:n})
.
Then there exists a computable function

\Phi:\mathcal{S}\to(\mathcal{G}_{L}\times\mathcal{G}_{L})^{*}

such that
\Phi(R(u_{0:n}))=\Pi_{n}
.

Proof.

The indicator family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{(M,m)}\}_{M\geq m,\,(M,m)\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2}}
is
finite
(at most

\binom{L}{2}+L=O(L^{2})
elements) and lies in
\mathcal{R}

by
lemma

10
.
Since
R
suffices for all
F\in\mathcal{R}
, for each

(M,m)\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2}
the value
F_{(M,m)}[u](n)=\mathbf{1}[(M,m)\in\Pi_{n}]
is computable from

R(u_{0:n})
.
Define

\Phi(R(u_{0:n}))=\bigl\{(M,m)\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{2}:F_{(M,m)}[u](n)=1\bigr\}.

This set equals
\Pi_{n}
by construction, and
\Phi
is
computable (finite enumeration over
O(L^{2})
pairs).
∎

Theorem 13

(Minimality of the extremum stack)
.

Let
u_{0:n}\in\mathcal{G}_{L}^{n+1}
and
\Pi_{n}
its extremum stack.
Then:

K(\Pi_{n})-O(1)\;\leq\;K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\;\leq\;K(\Pi_{n})+O(1).

(2)

The extremum stack is, up to an additive constant, the
shortest program that answers every
\mathcal{R}
-query.

Proof.

Lower bound.

By
lemma

12
, there exists a computable
\Phi
with

\Phi(R^{*}(u_{0:n}))=\Pi_{n}
, where
R^{*}
is the

K_{\mathcal{R}}
-optimal representation.
Therefore:

K(\Pi_{n})\leq K(R^{*}(u_{0:n}))+O(1)=K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})+O(1),

since
\Pi_{n}
is computable from the optimal representation
by a program of fixed length
O(1)
(the description of
\Phi
).
Rearranging gives the lower bound.

Upper bound.

The upper bound follows directly from
corollary

8
:
since every
F[u](n)
is computable from
\Pi_{n}
by a fixed

O(1)
-length program, the prefix-free invariance theorem gives

K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\leq K(\Pi_{n})+O(1)
.
∎

Discussion

5.1
Tightness of the bounds

The bounds in (
2
) are tight up to the additive
constant: there exist sequences for which

K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\leq K(\Pi_{n})+O(1)
and

K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\geq K(\Pi_{n})-O(1)
simultaneously
(e.g. when
\Pi_{n}
has a short description that immediately
answers all indicator queries).
The
O(1)
gap is the unavoidable overhead of prefix-free
encoding on a universal machine
[
6
]
.

5.3
Independence from
n

The
O(1)
overhead in (
2
) is independent of both
n
and
k
.
For slowly varying signals (small
k
), the compression ratio

n/k
can be arbitrarily large while the optimality gap remains
bounded by a constant.
PSTACK is therefore well-suited for streams with long monotone runs:
industrial sensor data (SCADA), EEG, and financial tick data.

5.4
Connection to the Preisach operator

The sufficiency part of
theorem

13
is a consequence of
the classical
wiping property
of the Preisach hysteresis
operator
[
8
]
: a new extremum erases all prior
extrema of smaller magnitude, and the output depends only on the
surviving stack.
The minimality part — which appears to be new — shows that this
compression is not merely sufficient but
necessary
.
No computable rate-independent functional "sees" less than

\Pi_{n}
.

5.5
Open questions

1.

Adversarial update complexity.

algorithm

1
runs in amortised
O(1)
per step, but
an adversarial input stream could force
O(n)
total pops
followed by a single push, giving worst-case
\Theta(n)
cost
per step.
Does there exist an online stack-maintenance algorithm
with
O(\log n)
worst-case (non-amortised) cost per step,
while preserving the
K_{\mathcal{R}}
minimality guarantee?

2.

Vector extension.
Does an analogue of

theorem

13
hold for the vector Preisach operator

[
3
]
with two-dimensional input signals?
The joint representation
(\Pi^{x}_{n},\Pi^{y}_{n})
of two
independent stacks is a computable pairing, so the overhead
for answering all vector
\mathcal{R}
-queries should remain
O(1)

by the same invariance argument.
Does this extend to the full vector Preisach measure

\mu(\alpha,\beta,\theta)
over directions
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
?

3.

KV-cache reduction.
Can PSTACK replace the
key-value cache in Preisach Attention
[
4
]
,
reducing memory from
O(n\cdot d)
to
O(k\cdot d)

without loss of expressiveness for rate-independent tasks?

6
Conclusion

We proved that the Preisach extremum stack
\Pi_{n}
is a
minimal sufficient statistic for the class of computable
rate-independent functionals, in the Kolmogorov complexity sense.
The minimality bound
K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\geq K(\Pi_{n})-O(1)

is established via a finite indicator family.
The matching upper bound
K_{\mathcal{R}}(u_{0:n})\leq K(\Pi_{n})+O(1)

follows from the prefix-free invariance theorem
K(f(x))\leq K(x)+O(1)
for computable
f
, tightening earlier
O(\log n)

and
O(\log K(\Pi_{n}))
claims to an additive constant.
The stack-based compression algorithm implied by the result
carries a Kolmogorov optimality guarantee that none of the
standard time-series compression methods provide.
