1.
Introduction

Nevanlinna
[
5
]
established the theory now bearing his name for meromorphic functions on the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
in 1926. His theory relates the characteristic function of a meromorphic function to the counting functions of its zeros, poles, and other value distributions. The growth of the characteristic function reflects the behavior of the function near the infinity point. Since then, many authors have developed Nevanlinna theory further and applied it to various problems concerning the growth and value distribution of meromorphic functions. One particularly interesting direction is the study of the relationship between the characteristic functions of meromorphic functions that share certain values or small functions. To state some results in this direction, we first recall the following notation.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
. We denote by
T(r,f)
the characteristic function of
f
(see Section 2 for the definition), and by
S(r,f)
any quantity satisfying

\|\ S(r,f)=o(T(r,f)).

Here, the notation “
\|P
” means that the statement
P
holds for all
r\in(0,+\infty)
outside a Borel subset of finite Lebesgue measure. A meromorphic function
a
is called a small function with respect to
f
if

\|\ T(r,a)=S(r,f).

Let
k
be a positive integer or
+\infty
, and let
S
be a subset of
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}={\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\infty\}
. We denote by
E_{k}^{S}(a,f)
the set of all
a
-points of
f
outside
S
, where an
a
-point of multiplicity
n
is counted
n
times if
n\leq k
, and counted
k+1
times if
n>k
. Moreover, if
S=\emptyset
(resp.
k=+\infty
), we write
E_{k}(a,f)
(resp.
E^{S}(a,f)
) instead of
E_{k}^{S}(a,f)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two meromorphic functions. We say that
f
and
g
weakly weighted share the value
a
with level
k
if there exists a discrete subset
S\subset\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
satisfying

\|\ N(r,S)=o(T(r,f)+T(r,g)),

such that

E_{k}^{S}(a,f)=E_{k}^{S}(a,g).

In the special case where
S=\emptyset
, we simply say that
f
and
g
weighted share the value
a
with level
k
.

When
k=\infty
(resp.
k=1
), we say that
f
and
g
weakly share the value
a
counting multiplicity (CM) (resp. ignoring multiplicity (IM)).

In 1995, E. Mues
[
6
]
conjectured that if two meromorphic functions
f
and
g
share three distinct values counted with multiplicity then

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}T(r,f)\leq T(r,g)\leq 2T(r,f)+o(1)

for every
r\in[1,+\infty)
outside a finite linear measure set, and the numbers
\dfrac{1}{2},2
are sharp.

In 1998, P. Li and C. C. Yang
[
9
]
established this conjecture. They proved that if two non-constant meromorphic functions
f
and
g
share
0,1,\infty
CM. Then for every positive
\epsilon
, we have the following

\displaystyle T(r,g)\leq(2+\epsilon)T(r,f)+S(r,f).

Later, many related results were obtained for two meromorphic functions that weighted share or weakly weighted share few values (see
[
1
,
3
,
8
,
11
]
). These results differ in their assumptions on the weights and in the applications derived from them. In particular, in 2005, I. Lahiri
[
7
]
generalized the previous results by introducing weaker conditions on the weights, and proved the following theorem.

Theorem A.
(see
[
7
]
)
Let
f
and
g
be two non-constant meromorphic functions that weighted share
0,1,\infty
with levels
1,m,k
respectively, so that
mk-m-k-3>0
. Then

\displaystyle T(r,g)\leq 2T(r,f)+S(r,f)\text{ and }T(r,g)\leq 2T(r,f)+S(r,g).

Recently, Quang and Ky considered a further weakening of the sharing condition. They introduced the notion of two meromorphic functions weakly sub-weighted sharing a value, defined as follows.

Let
f
be a meromorphic function,
a
a value in
{\mathbb{C}}
and
k
a non-negative integer or infinity. Denote by
E^{S}_{k)}(a,f)
the set of all
a
-points of
f
outside a discrete subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}}
, where an
a
-point of multiplicity
m
is counted
m
times if
m\leq k
and is omitted if
m>k
(i.e., not counted). Two meromorphic functions
f
and
g
are said to weakly sub-weighted share the value
a
with level
k
if there exists a discrete subset
S
of
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
of the counting function equal to
o(T(r,f)+T(r,g))
such that
E_{k)}^{S}(a,f)=E_{k)}^{S}(a,g).

Quang and Ky proved the following theorem.

Theorem B.
(see
[
10
]
)
Let
f,g
be two non-constant meromorphic functions. Assume that
f
and
g
weakly sub-weighted share three distinct values
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in{\mathbb{C}}
with levels
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
respectively, where
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
are positive integer or maybe infinity. Then

(1-\epsilon-\delta_{\epsilon})T(r,f)\leq(2+\epsilon+\delta_{\epsilon})T(r,g)+S(r,g)

for every positive number
\epsilon
, where
\delta_{\epsilon}=(2^{4/\epsilon^{2}}+16/\epsilon^{4})\left(\dfrac{1}{k_{1}+1}+\dfrac{1}{k_{2}+1}+\dfrac{1}{k_{3}+1}\right)
.

Our purpose of this paper is to extend Theorems A and B by considering the case where two functions weakly share three distinct small functions with bi-weights. Now, we recall the following.

Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function and
a
be a meromorphic function. For
S
to be a discrete subset of
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
and
n,k
two positive integers. Denote by
E^{S}_{n,k)}(a,f)
the set of all zeros of
f-a
outside
S
with multiplicity not exceeding
k
, where each zero of multiplicity
m\leq k
is counted
m
times if
m\leq n
and
n+1
times if
m>n
,and all zeros of multiplicity
m>k
are disregarded. Note that

E^{S}_{n,+\infty)}(a,f)=E^{S}_{n}(a,f).

We say that
f
and
g
weakly share the small functions
a
with the bi-weight
(n,k)
if there exists a discrete subset
S
of
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}
of the counting function equal to
o(T(r,f)+T(r,g))
such that
E^{S}_{n,k)}(a,f)=E^{S}_{n,k)}(a,g)
.

In this paper, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be two non-constant meromorphic functions and
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
three small functions with respect to
f
and
g
. Let
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3},k
be four positive integers (
k
may be
+\infty
) and
\Delta:=n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-2>0
. If
f
and
g
weakly share
a_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq 3)
with bi-weights
(n_{i},k)
then

(1-\epsilon-\delta_{\epsilon})T(r,f)\leq(2+\epsilon+\delta_{\epsilon})T(r,g)+S(r,g)

for every
\epsilon>0
, where
\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}=(2^{4/\epsilon^{2}}+16/\epsilon^{4})\left(\frac{2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{3}{k+1}\right)
.

Remark.(1) Let
n_{1}=1
and let
k=+\infty
then Theorem
1.1
recovers Theorem A.

(2) Let
n_{1}=n_{2}=n_{3}=n
and let
n\longrightarrow\infty
then we get the Theorem B for the case
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=k
.

Some lemmas and auxiliary results from Nevanlinna theory

For a divisor
\nu
on
{\mathbb{C}}
, we consider
\nu
as a function on
{\mathbb{C}}
with values in
\mathbb{Z}
so that the set
\overline{\{z|\nu(z)\neq 0\}}

\nu

N(r,\nu)=\int\limits_{1}^{r}\dfrac{n(t)}{t}dt\quad(1<r<\infty),\text{ where }n(t)=\sum\limits_{|z|\leq t}\nu(z).

For two positive integers
k,M
(maybe
M=\infty
), we set

\nu^{[M]}_{\leq k}(z)=\begin{cases}\min\{M,\nu(z)\}&\text{ if }\nu(z)\leq k\\
0&\text{ for otherwise. }\end{cases}

and write
N^{[M]}_{k)}(r,\nu)
for
N(r,\nu^{[M]}_{\leq k})
. We neglect character
[M]
(resp.
\leq k

(resp.

). In the same way, we define
\nu^{[M]}_{\geq k}
and write
N^{[M]}_{(k}(r,\nu)

\overline{N}(r,\nu)

.

Let
f
be a non-zero holomorphic function. For each
z_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, expanding

as

f(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}(z-z_{0})^{i}
around
z_{0}
, then we define
\nu^{0}_{f}(z_{0}):=\min\{i\ :\ b_{i}\neq 0\}
.

Let
\varphi
be a non-constant meromorphic function. Then there are two holomorphic function
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
without common zeros such that
\varphi=\dfrac{\varphi_{1}}{\varphi_{2}}
. We define

\nu_{\varphi}^{0}:=\nu_{\varphi_{1}}^{0}
and
\nu_{\varphi}^{\infty}:=\nu_{\varphi_{2}}^{0}
and
\nu_{\varphi}=\nu^{0}_{\varphi}-\nu^{\infty}_{\varphi}
. The proximity function of
\varphi
is defined by:

m(r,\varphi):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\log^{+}|\varphi(re^{i\theta})|d\theta\ \ (r>1),

here
\log^{+}x=\max\{1,\log x\}
for

. The Nevanlinna characteristic function of

T(r,\varphi):=m(r,\varphi)+N(r,\nu^{\infty}_{\varphi}).

.

\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}

Proof of Theorem 1

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
and
g
be non-constant meromorphic functions,
f

. Assume that
f
and
g
weakly share three values
a_{1}=0,a_{2}=1,a_{3}=\infty

(n_{i},k)

Proof.

.
We define
h_{1}:=\dfrac{f}{g}
and
h_{2}:=\dfrac{f-1}{g-1}
. It easy to see that
T(r,h_{i})\leq T(r)
,

. Because

\beta=\dfrac{h_{1}^{\prime}}{h_{1}}-\dfrac{h_{2}^{\prime}}{h_{2}}\not\equiv 0

\displaystyle\begin{split}N(r,\beta)&\leq N(r,\nu_{1})\leq\sum_{h=f,g}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\overline{N}_{(n_{i}+1,k)}(r,\nu_{h}^{a_{i}})+\overline{N}_{(k+1}(r,\nu_{h}^{a_{i}})\right]\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}T(r,\alpha)&\leq T\left(r,\dfrac{h_{2}^{\prime}}{h_{2}}\right)+T(r,\beta)=N\left(r,\dfrac{h_{2}^{\prime}}{h_{2}}\right)+N(r,\beta)+S(r)\\

where
\nu_{1}
is the reduced divisor counting all points

\nu_{f}(z)\neq\nu_{g}(z)\text{ or }\nu_{f-1}(z)\neq\nu_{g-1}(z).

It is easy to compute that

\displaystyle f=\dfrac{h_{1}-h_{1}h_{2}}{h_{1}-h_{2}}=\dfrac{h_{2}^{-1}-1}{h_{2}^{-1}-h_{1}^{-1}}

Thus

\displaystyle f-\alpha=\dfrac{(1-\alpha)h_{2}^{-}1+\alpha h_{1}^{-1}-1}{h_{2}^{-1}-h_{1}^{-1}}

Now, we set
F_{1}=(\alpha-1)h_{2}^{-1}-\alpha h_{1}^{-1}+1,F_{2}=(1-\alpha)h_{2}^{-1},F_{3}=\alpha h_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle f-\alpha=\dfrac{F_{1}}{h_{2}^{-1}-h_{1}^{-1}}

By using Claim 3.11 in

\displaystyle\begin{split}N\left(r,\dfrac{1}{f-\alpha}\right)&-N(r,f-\alpha)\\

We consider the following two cases:

\displaystyle c_{2}^{\prime}=(1-\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}),c_{3}^{\prime}=(1-\frac{c_{3}}{c_{1}}).

\ T(r,f)\leq T(r,g)+T(r,\alpha)+O(1).

Combining this inequality with the inequality (

\displaystyle N\Big(r,\dfrac{1}{f-\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq T(r,F_{2}^{-1}-F_{1}^{-1})-N\Big(r,\dfrac{1}{F_{2}^{-1}-F_{1}^{-1}}\Big)+N(r,f)-2N(r,\nu_{1})

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\leq m\Big(r,\dfrac{1}{F_{2}^{-1}-F_{1}^{-1}}\Big)+T(r,\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})+S(r)

\displaystyle\leq m\Big(r,\dfrac{1}{F_{2}^{-1}-F_{1}^{-1}}\Big)+N(r,\nu_{1})+S(r).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

If
f
is a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
then

T(r,f)=T(r,g)+S(r),

and hence we get the conclusion of theorem.Therefore, we may assume that
f
is not a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
. Moreover,without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{1}=0,a_{2}=1,a_{3}=\infty
.

With
h_{1},h_{2}
are defined in Lemma
3.1
, then

T(r,h_{i})\leq T(r)(i=1,2).

If there exist integers
s,t
(
\|s\|+\|t\|>0
) such that
h_{1}^{s}h_{2}^{t}-1\equiv 0)
then
f^{s}(f-1)^{t}=g^{s}(g-1)^{t}
, thus
T(r,f)=T(r,g)
and we get the conclusion of the theorem.

Therefore, we may suppose that
h_{1}^{s}h_{2}^{t}-1\not\equiv 0
for all integers
s,t
(
\|s\|+\|t\|>0
), then by Lemma
3.1
, one of the fowlloing inequalities holds:

(3.6)

\displaystyle T(r,f)\leq T(r,g)+\left(\dfrac{8\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{5}{k+1}\right)T(r)+S(r),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}T(r,f)+T(r,g)\leq N(r,g)+N\left(r,\dfrac{1}{g-1}\right)+N\left(r,\dfrac{1}{g}\right)+N_{0}(r)\\
+\left(\frac{116\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{78}{k+1}\right)T(r)+S(r).\end{split}

\bullet
If the inequality (

) holds then we completely get the conclusion of the theorem.

\bullet

2.3
, for any positive integer
