Search for direct pair production of top squarks in p ​ p pp collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV and 13.6 13.6 TeV in events with two oppositely charged leptons using the ATLAS detector


Abstract
This paper presents the search for direct pair production of top squarks decaying into two on-shell top quarks and two neutralinos in final states with two oppositely charged leptons (electrons or muons), b b -jets and large missing transverse momentum. The search uses the full Run 2 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 fb -1 of proton–proton collisions at a centre-of-mass energy of s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV collected by the ATLAS detector from 2015 to 2018, and the early Run 3 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 53 fb -1 of proton–proton collisions at s = 13.6 \sqrt{s}=13.6 TeV collected in 2022 and 2023. Machine-learning-based classifiers are used to distinguish the signal from Standard Model backgrounds, to enhance signal discrimination across a wide kinematic phase space. No significant excess is observed with respect to the Standard Model prediction and 95% confidence level limits are set on top squark and neutralino mass combinations reaching up to 1060 GeV for the former and 560 GeV for the latter, improving mass limits by about 10% compared to previous ATLAS analyses in the same channel.

ATLAS detector

The ATLAS detector
[
29
,
30
]
at the LHC covers nearly the entire solid angle around the collision point.
1
1

1

ATLAS uses a right-handed coordinate system with its origin at the nominal interaction point (IP) in the centre of the detector and the
z
-axis along the beam pipe.
The
x
-axis points from the IP to the centre of the LHC ring, and the
y
-axis points upwards.
Polar coordinates
(r,\phi)
are used in the transverse plane,
\phi
being the azimuthal angle around the
z
-axis.
The pseudorapidity is defined in terms of the polar angle
\theta
as
\eta=-\ln\tan(\theta/2)
and is equal to the rapidity
y=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{E+p_{z}}{E-p_{z}}\right)
in the relativistic limit.
Angular distance is measured in units of
\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}
.

It consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer incorporating three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.

The inner-detector system (ID) is immersed in a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
axial magnetic field
and provides charged-particle tracking in the range
|\eta|<2.5
.
The high-granularity silicon pixel detector covers the vertex region and typically provides four measurements per track,
the first hit generally being in the insertable B-layer.
It is followed by the SemiConductor Tracker, which usually provides eight measurements per track.
These silicon detectors are complemented by the transition radiation tracker (TRT),
which enables radially extended track reconstruction up to
|\eta|=2.0
.
The TRT also provides electron identification information
based on the fraction of hits (typically 30 in total) above a higher energy-deposit threshold corresponding to transition radiation.

The calorimeter system covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<4.9
.
Within the region
|\eta|<3.2
, electromagnetic calorimetry is provided by barrel and
endcap high-granularity lead/liquid-argon (LAr) calorimeters,
with an additional thin LAr presampler covering
|\eta|<1.8

to correct for energy loss in material upstream of the calorimeters.
Hadronic calorimetry is provided by the steel/scintillator-tile calorimeter,
segmented into three barrel structures within
|\eta|<1.7
, and two copper/LAr hadronic endcap calorimeters.
The solid angle coverage is completed with forward copper/LAr and tungsten/LAr calorimeter modules
optimised for electromagnetic and hadronic energy measurements, respectively.

The muon spectrometer (MS) comprises separate trigger and
high-precision tracking chambers measuring the deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by the superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.
The field integral of the toroids ranges between
2.0
and
6.0\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

across most of the detector.
Three layers of precision chambers, each consisting of layers of monitored drift tubes, cover the region
|\eta|<2.7
.
These were complemented in the innermost layer of the endcap region by cathode-strip chambers in Run 2,
which were replaced in Run 3 by layers of small-strip thin-gap chambers and Micromegas chambers, both providing precision tracking in the region
1.3<|\eta|<2.7
.
The muon trigger system covers the range
|\eta|<2.4
with resistive-plate chambers in the barrel region, thin-gap chambers in the endcap regions,
and, in Run 3, the aforementioned small-strip thin-gap chambers and Micromegas chambers in the innermost layer of the endcap.

The luminosity is measured mainly by the LUCID–2 detector that records Cherenkov light produced in the quartz windows of photomultipliers located close to the beam pipe.

Events were selected by the first-level trigger system implemented in custom hardware,
followed by selections made by algorithms implemented in software in the high-level trigger
[
31
,
32
]
.
The first-level trigger accepted events from the
40\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
bunch crossings at a rate close to
100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
,
which the high-level trigger further reduced in order to record complete events to disk at about
1.25\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
and
3\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
in Run 2 and Run 3, respectively.

The Run 3 detector configuration benefits from several upgrades compared with that of Run 2 to maintain high detector performance at the higher pile-up levels of Run 3.
The improvements include a new innermost layer of the muon spectrometer in the endcap region, which provides higher redundancy and a large reduction in fake muon triggers.
The trigger system also benefits from a new digital electronics readout of the LAr calorimeters with significantly increased granularity.
Other updates and further details are provided in Ref.
[
30
]
.

A software suite
[
33
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition systems of the experiment.

Event reconstruction

Primary vertices are reconstructed from ID tracks with
p_{\text{T}}>$500\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$

[
86
,
87
]
.
The primary vertex with the largest sum of squared track
p_{\text{T}}
is chosen as the hard-scatter vertex, and serves as the origin for all the objects described below.

Leptons (electrons or muons) are classified as either baseline or signal.
Baseline leptons are used in the calculation of the missing transverse momentum, to resolve ambiguities between the analysis objects in an event, and to define 2-lepton and 3-lepton analysis regions.
Baseline electrons are reconstructed from energy deposits (clusters) in the electromagnetic calorimeter matched to tracks in the ID
[
88
,
89
,
90
]
.
Baseline electron candidates are required to have a pseudorapidity
|\eta|<2.47
, a transverse energy
E_{\text{T}}>$4.5\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
, to satisfy a
Loose
likelihood-based identification requirement
[
91
]
, and to have
|z_{0}\sin\theta|<0.5
mm, where
z_{0}
is the longitudinal impact parameter relative to the reconstructed primary vertex.
They are calibrated based on the procedure and results described in Ref.
[
89
,
90
]
. Additional corrections to the in-situ energy scale and resolution are applied to account for residual differences between data and simulation.
Baseline muon candidates are reconstructed by matching ID tracks in the pseudorapidity region
|\eta|<2.4
with MS tracks and energy deposits in the calorimeter compatible with a minimum-ionising particle.
The resulting tracks are required to have a
p_{\text{T}}>$4\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and to satisfy
|z_{0}\sin\theta|<0.5
mm.
Baseline muon candidates are required to satisfy the
Medium
identification requirement, defined in Ref.
[
92
]
, based on the number of hits in the different ID and MS subsystems, and on the precision of the charge-to-momentum ratio
q/p
measurement.

Jets are reconstructed using the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm
[
93
]
as implemented in FastJet
[
94
]
with a jet radius parameter of
R=0.4
. The inputs to this algorithm are particle flow (PFlow) objects
[
95
]
, which combine measurements from the ATLAS ID and calorimeters
[
96
]
to improve the jet energy resolution and increase the jet reconstruction efficiency, especially at low jet
p_{\text{T}}
.
The reconstructed jets are then calibrated by the application of a jet energy scale and resolution correction derived from data and simulation
[
97
]
.
Only jet candidates with
p_{\text{T}}>$25\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
and
|\eta|<2.5
are considered.
2

2

No attempt is made to identify hadronic
\tau
-lepton decays, so they are treated as jets.
To reduce the effects of pile-up, jets with
p_{\text{T}}<$120\text{\,}\mathrm{GeV}$
are required to satisfy the jet-to-vertex tagger (JVT)
[
98
]

?
Tight
?
working point requirements. The tagger is configured to identify jets from the hard-scatter vertex with 92% efficiency.
Jets originating from the hadronisation of
b
-quarks are identified using the GN2
b
-tagging algorithm
[
99
,
100
]
at a working point corresponding to a
77\%
efficiency for
b\text{-jets}
, with a misidentification rate below
0.06
(
0.002
) for charm (light-flavour) jets in simulated
t\bar{t}
events. The
b

[
101
,
102
,
103
]
. Jets failing this

To avoid reconstruction ambiguities and double counting of analysis objects, an overlap removal procedure is applied to the baseline leptons and jets in the following order.
First, calorimeter-tagged muons, reconstructed from a track in the ID matched to an energy deposit in the calorimeter compatible with a minimum-ionising particle, are removed if sharing the track with electrons and, next, all electrons sharing an ID track with a muon are removed.
Jets that lie within a cone of
\Delta R=0.2
around an electron candidate are removed.
Finally, any lepton candidate is removed in favour of a jet candidate if it is closer than
\Delta R<\mathrm{min}(0.4,0.04+10\penalty\ \text{GeV}/{p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell}})
to a jet, where
{p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\ell}}
is the
p_{\text{T}}
of the lepton.

The missing transverse momentum (
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}
), with magnitude
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
, is defined as the negative vector sum of the transverse momenta of all baseline electrons, muons and jets.
Low-momentum tracks from the primary vertex that are not associated with reconstructed analysis objects are also considered in the calculation, by including them in a dedicated soft term
[
104
]
.
Linked to the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
is the ‘object-based
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
significance’
[
105
]
, referred to as ‘
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}\ \mathrm{significance}
’ in this paper.
This quantity measures the significance of the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
based on the transverse momentum resolution of all objects used in the calculation of the
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}
.
The
E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}\ \mathrm{significance}
improves the discrimination between events with true
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
, arising from weakly interacting particles such as neutralinos, and those where the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
is consistent with a particle mismeasurement and resolution or identification inefficiencies, thus providing better background rejection.

Signal leptons are a subset of the baseline leptons, satisfying stricter reconstruction quality and kinematic selections, and are used for the final event selection.
Signal electrons are required to satisfy a
Tight
likelihood-based identification requirement
[
91
]
and the track associated with a signal electron is required to have a significance
|d_{0}|/\sigma(d_{0})<5
, where
d_{0}
is the transverse impact parameter relative to the reconstructed primary vertex and
\sigma(d_{0})
is its uncertainty.
To further reduce the background from non-prompt leptons and mis-identified hadrons, signal electrons are also required to be isolated in the tracking system and the calorimeter, as described in Ref.
[
89
]
.
For signal muons, a significance in the transverse impact parameter
|d_{0}|/\sigma(d_{0})<3
is required.
Isolation criteria applied to muons require low activity in the tracker around the muon track, as well as in the calorimeters
[
106
]
.

5
Event selection and analysis strategy

The search reported in this paper selects events with a pair of leptons with opposite electric charge.
Events are required to contain at least two jets, with one identified as a
b\text{-jet}
.
The leading lepton is required to have a
p_{\text{T}}>27\,\text{GeV}
to be in the plateau region of the used triggers, while the subleading lepton is required to have a
p_{\text{T}}>20\,\text{GeV}
.
A selection is also applied to the invariant mass of the two leptons,
m_{\ell\ell}>20\,\text{GeV}
, to reject events coming from low-mass resonances.

Six Neural Networks (NNs) are trained on MC simulated samples of the main SM backgrounds (
t\bar{t}
and
t\bar{t}Z
) and the signal models, only including events satisfying the selection described above.
The signal models are grouped into three categories according to the mass splitting
\Delta m(\tilde{t}_{1},\mathchoice{\displaystyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\textstyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptstyle\raise 0.90417pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptscriptstyle\raise 0.64583pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}})
:

The variable
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}
is used to bound the masses of a pair of identical particles that have each decayed into a visible and an invisible particle.
Different variants of
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}
can be computed, using different combinations of visible objects. For the
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}
calculation, the invisible particles are assumed to be massless. The two signal leptons are used for the calculation of
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}^{\ell\ell}

In dileptonic events, the
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}^{\ell\ell}
distribution is expected to have an endpoint corresponding to the
W

t\bar{t}

E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}

110

The NN input variables include the kinematic variables (
p_{\text{T}}
,
\eta
,
\phi
) of the signal leptons and the two most energetic jets in the final state, complemented by a set of additional observables that provide further discrimination between the background and the signal, which are defined in the following.
The lepton variables include the lepton flavour and the invariant mass (
m_{\ell\ell}
) of the two leptons. For jets, the pseudo-continuous
b
-tagging score is also considered: it consists of the discrete output of the GN2
b
-tagging algorithm (see
Section

4

b
-tagging efficiency range (
65\%
,
70\%
,
77\%
,
85\%

90\%

At event-level, the input variables include the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}

The angular separation (
\Delta\phi
) between all the combinations of two considered final state objects (the two leptons, the leading and the subleading
b\text{-jet}

m_{\mathrm{T}}
) between the missing transverse momentum and each of the two leptons.
Additionally, the variable
\mathbf{p}^{\ell\ell}_{\mathrm{T,\mathrm{boost}}}
is defined as the vectorial sum of
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}
and the leptons transverse momentum vectors
\mathbf{p_{\text{T}}}(\ell_{1})
and
\mathbf{p_{\text{T}}}(\ell_{2})
.
Its magnitude,
p^{\ell\ell}_{\mathrm{T,boost}}
, can be interpreted as the magnitude of the vector sum of all the transverse hadronic activity in the event.
The azimuthal angle between the
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}
vector and the
\mathbf{p}^{\ell\ell}_{\mathrm{T,\mathrm{boost}}}
vector is defined as
\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{boost}}
.
This variable is useful for selecting events where the two signal leptons are in the same direction of the
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}

p^{\ell\ell}_{\mathrm{T,boost}}
as an input to the NNs
[
111
]

Finally, different variants of
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}
are considered, including all the possible combinations of leptons and
b
-tagged jets as visible particles in the calculation of the variable.
For the calculation of the variables described above, when two
b
-tagged jets are required and only one is reconstructed, the leading light-jet is used instead of the subleading
b
-tagged jet.
According to metrics provided by the SHAP package
[
112

m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}^{\ell\ell}
and
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
significance.

The outputs of the NNs are classification scores ranging between
0

6
Background estimation

The MC predictions for the dominant SM background processes
t\bar{t}
and
t\bar{t}Z
, with an invisible decay of the
Z
boson (
Z\rightarrow\nu\bar{\nu}
), are improved using a data-driven normalisation procedure, while non-dominant processes are estimated directly using MC simulation.
MC simulation is also used to account for background events arising from processes that do not contain two leptons at scattering level, but for which two leptons are selected by the analysis criteria, either because of hadrons
misidentified as leptons or non-prompt leptons from decays of hadrons (mainly from semileptonic
t\bar{t}
events).
The modelling of the processes with one or more fake or non-prompt leptons (hereafter referred to as FNP) was validated in data using a sample with two same-charge signal leptons, which is naturally enriched in this background.

For each
\Delta m(\tilde{t}_{1},\mathchoice{\displaystyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\textstyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptstyle\raise 0.90417pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptscriptstyle\raise 0.64583pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}})
category, a simultaneous profile likelihood fit
[
115
]
is used to constrain the MC yields with the observed data in dedicated background control regions (CRs).
The CRs are kinematic regions enriched in the targeted background. Their definitions aim at keeping the contamination of other backgrounds and signals as small as possible, while being kinematically close to the SRs to reduce the extrapolation error.
The CRs and the SRs are required to be orthogonal to each other. In the fit, implemented with the HistFitter and PyHF frameworks
[
116
,
117
,
118
]
, the normalisation of the targeted backgrounds are allowed to float in an independent way for the Run 2 and Run 3 datasets, while the MC simulation is used to describe the distribution of kinematic variables. Systematic uncertainties that could affect the expected yields in the different regions are taken into account in the fit through nuisance parameters.
Each uncertainty source is described by a single nuisance parameter constrained by a Gaussian distribution whose width corresponds to the size of the uncertainty, which is shared across analysis regions and background processes, when the related uncertainties are considered correlated.
A list of the systematic uncertainties considered in the fit is provided in Section
7
.
The SM background estimate is validated in dedicated validation regions (VRs), which are disjoint from both CRs and SRs.

The
t\bar{t}
CRs and VRs are defined applying selections to the NN scores for each
\Delta m(\tilde{t}_{1},\mathchoice{\displaystyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\textstyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptstyle\raise 0.90417pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptscriptstyle\raise 0.64583pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}})
category. Consequently, different CRs and VRs are defined for events with one and more than one
b
-tagged jet.
Specifically, CRs contain events with lower NN signal score, and the VRs cover the gap between the CRs and the SRs, as depicted in
Figure

2
.
A selection on the NN
t\bar{t}
score is added to the definition of the
t\bar{t}
CRs, to remove the events with NN
t\bar{t}
score close to zero, thus increasing the purity of the region for the
t\bar{t}
process.

t\bar{t}Z
events with invisible decays of the
Z
boson are expected to dominate the tail of the
m_{\mbox{\tiny T2}}^{\ell\ell}
distribution in the SRs and are normalised in the dedicated control region
\mathrm{CR}_{t\bar{t}Z}
.
Given the difficulty in achieving enough purity for this SM process because of the high contamination from
t\bar{t}
events, a strategy based on a three-lepton final state is adopted.
Events are selected if characterised by three charged leptons including at least one pair of same-flavour leptons with opposite-sign electric charge (SFOS leptons) having an invariant mass consistent with that of the
Z
boson (
|m_{\ell\ell}-m_{Z}|<20

GeV
). If more than one pair is identified, the one with
m_{\ell\ell}
closest to the
Z
boson mass is chosen. Events are further required to have a jet multiplicity,
n_{\mathrm{jets}}
, greater than or equal to three with at least two
b
-tagged jets.
These selections target
t\bar{t}Z
production with the
Z
boson decaying into two leptons and
t\bar{t}
decaying in the semileptonic channel. To select
t\bar{t}Z
events whose kinematics, regardless of subsequent
t\bar{t}
and
Z
decays, emulate the kinematics of this background in the SRs, the momenta of the two leptons of the SFOS pair
(\mathbf{p}(\ell^{\mathrm{Z}}_{1}),\mathbf{p}(\ell^{\mathrm{Z}}_{2}))
are vectorially added to the
\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}
, effectively treating them as a neutrino pair from the
Z
boson decay. A variable called
E_{\mathrm{T,\mathrm{corr}}}^{\mathrm{miss}}=\left|\left(\mathbf{p}^{\mathrm{miss}}_{\mathrm{T}}+\mathbf{p}(\ell^{\mathrm{Z}}_{1})+\mathbf{p}(\ell^{\mathrm{Z}}_{2})\right)_{\mathrm{T}}\right|
is constructed.
Events characterised by high
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
selected by the NN scores in the SRs are emulated by requiring
E_{\mathrm{T,\mathrm{corr}}}^{\mathrm{miss}}\ >140\penalty\ \text{GeV}
in
\mathrm{CR}_{t\bar{t}Z}
.

The expected signal contamination in the CRs is generally below
\sim 1\%
and less than
10\%
in the VRs.

7
Systematic uncertainties

The uncertainty on the yield predictions in the analysis regions has roughly similar contributions from theoretical and modelling uncertainties of the simulated background, and the experimental limitations in the reconstruction of the physics objects.
The statistical uncertainty of the simulated events is also taken into account.

The main sources of experimental uncertainty are related to the jet energy scale (JES) and the jet energy resolution (JER).
The JES and JER uncertainties are derived as a function of the
p_{\text{T}}
and
\eta
of the jet, as well as of the pile-up conditions and the jet-flavour composition of the selected jet sample, and are evaluated using a series of simulation-based techniques and in situ measurements, documented in Ref.
[
97
]
.
Uncertainties associated with the modelling of the
b
-tagging efficiencies for
b
-jets,
c
-jets and light-flavour jets are also considered
[
103
,
102
,
101
]
. The systematic uncertainties related to the modelling of the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
are estimated by propagating the uncertainties in the energy and momentum scales of electrons, muons, and jets, and the uncertainties in the resolution and scale of the soft term
[
120
]
.
Other detector-related systematic uncertainties, including those arising from lepton reconstruction efficiency, energy scale, energy resolution and in the modelling of the trigger efficiency
[
89
,
90
,
121
,
39
,
38
]
, together with the ones due to the pile-up reweighting
[
42
]
and JVT
[
122
]
are also considered, and found to have a small impact on the results.
Finally, the uncertainty on the full Run 2 integrated luminosity amounts to
0.83\%

[
36
]
, while the uncertainty on the combined 2022–2023 integrated luminosity is estimated to be
2\%

[
35
]
, and is uncorrelated with that of Run 2.

The uncertainty on the modelling of the
t\bar{t}
background due to initial-state radiation (ISR) was estimated by simultaneously varying the parameter that regulates the high-
p_{\text{T}}
radiation against which the
t\bar{t}
system recoils, and the normalisation and factorisation scales
[
123
]
.
Variations of the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{S}
applied to the modelling of initial-state radiation are evaluated as described in Ref.
[
124
]
.
The impact of final-state radiation (FSR) was evaluated by varying the renormalisation scale for emissions from the parton shower up or down by a factor two.
The uncertainties on
t\bar{t}Z
production also include the effects of QCD scale uncertainties. Additionally, the uncertainties due to possible additional ISR contributions are evaluated by varying the
Var3c
parameter in the
A14

Pythia
tune
[
52
]
.
For both
t\bar{t}
and
t\bar{t}Z
production, the impact of using a different parton shower and hadronisation model was evaluated
by comparing simulated samples generated using the two different showering models implemented in
Pythia
and in
Herwig

[
125
,
126
]
.
For single top production in the
Wt
channel, an uncertainty is assigned to account for the interference with
t\bar{t}
production, which is estimated by comparing samples generated using the diagram removal (nominal) and the diagram subtraction prescriptions
[
127
]
.
For all the processes mentioned above, the PDF uncertainties
[
128
]
were evaluated and found to be negligible.
For minor backgrounds, the uncertainty on the cross section used for the normalisation is included.

A summary of the impact of the systematic uncertainties on the background expectation in each SR bin is presented in
Figure

5
.
The total systematic uncertainty in the SR bins ranges between 10% and 23% for Run 2 samples, and between 14% and 29% for Run 3 samples. The dominant sources of uncertainty are the JES and JER uncertainties, the theoretical uncertainties, the MC statistical uncertainty and the uncertainty in the background normalisation.
All systematic uncertainties are considered uncorrelated between Run 2 and Run 3, and this choice is not found to impact the final results.

Figure 5:
Comparison of the relative uncertainty for the total background yield in each SR for the (a) Run 2 and (b) Run 3 datasets, including the contribution from the different sources of uncertainty, calculated as results of the profile likelihood fit.
The category “Other experimental” includes minor detector uncertainties, such as the ones related to leptons, flavour tagging and pile-up modelling. The total uncertainty is calculated considering the correlation across the various systematic components, and therefore it does not necessarily correspond to the sum of the single components.

The SUSY signal cross section uncertainty is evaluated from an envelope of the cross section predictions using different PDF sets and factorisation and renormalisation scales as described in Ref.
[
129
,
54
]
.
The SUSY theory uncertainties affecting the signal acceptance in the SRs are computed from the variation of the renormalisation and factorisation scales. Additionally, an uncertainty of 10% is added in the SRs to take into account the uncertainties due to the variation of the fragmentation and merging scales.
This uncertainty is estimated with alternative samples for selected benchmark signal models.

The analysis results are interpreted in terms of both model-dependent exclusion limits and model-independent upper limits on new physics cross section.
Model-dependent exclusion limits are derived for the signal scenarios considered in the analysis.
As a first step, separate model-dependent limits are derived using only the regions from either the low, the medium or the high categories. For each combination of SUSY particle masses,
only the category with the best expected sensitivity is then considered for the final limit setting. The hypothesis tests include the expected signal yields and their associated uncertainties in both the CRs and SRs. All exclusion limits are quoted at 95% confidence level (CL) using the CL
s
method
[
131
]
.
When setting limits, CRs and the SR bins within each
\Delta m(\tilde{t}_{1},\mathchoice{\displaystyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\textstyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptstyle\raise 0.90417pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptscriptstyle\raise 0.64583pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}})
-category are statistically combined.

\tilde{t}_{1}

9
Conclusions

This paper presents a search for the direct top squark pair production, with each top squark decaying directly into a top quark and the lightest neutralino,
\tilde{t}_{1}\to\penalty\ t\mathchoice{\displaystyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\textstyle\raise 1.72218pt\hbox{$\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptstyle\raise 0.90417pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}{\scriptscriptstyle\raise 0.64583pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}$}}
.
The analysis targets final states containing exactly two oppositely charged leptons,
b
-tagged jets and missing transverse momentum.
The search uses the full LHC Run 2 dataset, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
140\penalty\ \mbox{fb${}^{-1}$}
of
pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{TeV}
collected by the ATLAS experiment between 2015 and 2018, and
53\penalty\ \mbox{fb${}^{-1}$}
of
pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13.6\penalty\ \text{TeV}
collected in 2022 and 2023 during the Run 3 of the LHC.

Compared to previous searches, a significant improvement in the expected sensitivity, especially at high
\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}
masses, is obtained through the inclusion of the additional integrated luminosity, the improvement in the event reconstruction techniques, and the development of a new analysis strategy based on machine-learning techniques. For a benchmark signal point, considering a
1\penalty\ \text{TeV}

\tilde{t}_{1}
and a
400\penalty\ \text{GeV}

\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}
, the expected improvement of the upper limit on the signal strength arising from the improved reconstruction and new analysis strategy is 50%. A further 20% is then reached thanks to the addition of Run 3 data. These improvements allow the dilepton channel to be competitive in terms of sensitivity with the single-lepton analysis,
despite the latter having an advantage in the branching fraction.

The observed data are found to be consistent with the Standard Model expectations. Exclusion limits at the 95% confidence level are set on
\tilde{t}_{1}
and
\textstyle\tilde{\chi}^{0}_{1}
masses, up to approximately
1060\penalty\ \text{GeV}
and
560\penalty\ \text{GeV}
, respectively. These limits are slightly weaker than expected due to the presence of small excesses of data over the predicted background, compatible with statistical fluctuations.
The analysis extends the previous ATLAS limits obtained in the dilepton channel by
\approx 10\%
in mass reach.
