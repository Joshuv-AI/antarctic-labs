Expressivity of congruence-based architectures for DNNs on positive-definite matrices
Antonin Oswald, Estelle Massart

Abstract
This work studies neural architectures for classifying symmetric positive-definite matrices, focusing on congruence-like layers, in which the input matrix is multiplied on the left and right by a (possibly rectangular) weight matrix 𝑾 \boldsymbol{W} and its transpose. Such layers lie at the core of the celebrated SPDNet and have also been employed independently for dimensionality reduction on positive-definite data. We show that the (semi)-orthogonality constraint commonly imposed on 𝑾 \boldsymbol{W} limits the expressivity of these layers: for certain activation functions, the resulting architecture collapses to a one-hidden-layer equivalent. This lack of expressivity follows from a loss of spectral diversity in congruence-like layers for semi-orthogonal W W and is a direct consequence of Poincaré’s separation theorem. We then examine the choice of the final classifier, comparing several Riemannian classifiers and discussing their compatibility with the feature maps produced by congruence-like layers.

Model Expressivity for semi-orthogonal weight matrices

We next address the case where the weight matrices in (

Theorem 1

(Poincaré Separation Theorem, see

Let

VI
Conclusion

We explored DNN architectures for SPD matrices that involve a structure-preserving feature extraction block made of congruence-like layers and nonlinear matrix functions, followed by a final classifier. We showed that imposing (semi)-orthogonal constraints on congruence weights can collapse deep architectures to one-hidden-layer networks for certain activation function, reducing expressivity. We also noted that several common distances between SPD matrices are invariant under congruences, preventing the resulting classifiers to separate data from different classes. Future work could explore the design of new activation functions to improve expressivity.
