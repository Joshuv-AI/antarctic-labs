Concentrated solutions to fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system with non-homogeneous potentials
Lintao Liu, Haidong Yang

Abstract
. This paper mainly investigates several limit properties of normalized solutions for the fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system, including existence, concentration behaviors and local uniqueness. It is worth noting that our results on the existence and asymptotic behaviors of normalized solutions are obtained in a doubly nonlocal setting and without assuming homogeneity of the potential, which generalize the results in [ 19 ] in several aspects and improve our previous work in [ 34 ] . Meanwhile, some precise properties of solution sequence such as energy estimates, decay estimates and uniform regularity are also established by introducing some new techniques.



Keywords:
Concentrated solutions; Fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system; Non-homogeneous.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
35A02, 39A14.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the solutions with prescribed mass for the following fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=(-\Delta)^{s}\psi+V(x)\psi-\phi\psi-a^{p-2}|\psi|^{p-2}\psi&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\\
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=\psi^{2}&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the time dependent wave function,
s\in(\frac{3}{4},1)
,
a>0
and
2<p<2+\frac{4s}{3}
. We seek standing wave solutions to (
1.1
), which are given by
\left(\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\mu t}u(x),\phi(x)\right)
for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. The pair
(u(x),\phi(x))
thus satisfies the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=\mu u+\phi u+a^{p-2}|u|^{p-2}u&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\\
(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=u^{2}&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is the corresponding Lagrange multiplier.
(-\Delta)^{s}
is a nonlocal operator defined by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=C_{s}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{3+2s}}dy,

and the symbol
P.V.
stands for the Cauchy principal value of the singular integral,
C_{s}
is a suitable normalization constant. Laskin
[
26
,
27
]
originally proposed a method that generalizes the Feynman path integral from Brownian-like quantum paths to Lévy-like ones, and this generalization has practical implications for the energy spectrum of hydrogen-like atoms, such as the fractional Bohr atom. It is a well-established fact that system (
1.2
) holds profound physical significance, as it arises naturally in the context of semiconductor theory. Specifically, in (
1.2
), the first equation is a nonlinear stationary equation, whose nonlinear term models the inter-particle interactions of many-body systems, and this equation is coupled with the Poisson equation and must be satisfied by the function
\phi
. This dependency implies that the potential is determined by the charge density of the wave function
[
36
]
.

Over the past few years, numerous scholars have extensively studied the existence and multiplicity of positive solutions, ground state solutions, sign-changing solutions and semiclassical states for the fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system (
1.2
), and have developed several effective methods for handling equations or systems containing local terms, such as the perturbation approach, quantitative deformation lemma, global compactness lemma, Pohozaev–Nehari manifold, approximation method and Ljusternik–Schnirelmann theory. We refer the readers to see

,
43

42

and the references therein for more details.

It is noted that the research findings of scholars in the aforementioned studies are mainly obtained without imposing any constraints on the
L^{2}
-norm. However, in this paper, we focus more on the existence, asymptotic behavior and uniqueness of the normalized solutions for the fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system (
1.2

where

-subcritical,

[

23

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}u+\lambda\phi u=\alpha u+\mu|u|^{q-2}u+|u|^{2_{s}^{\ast}-2}u&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\\
(-\Delta)^{t}\phi=u^{2}&\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\end{array}\right.

where

satisfy

,

,
\lambda,\mu>0
are parameters and

Under the subcritical perturbation, the authors obtained the existence of multiple normalized solutions by means of the truncation technique, concentration-compactness principle and the genus theory. In the critical regime, they proved the existence results by applying the Jeanjean theory, Pohozaev manifold method and Brezis-Nirenberg technique. In the supercritical perturbation, they proved two different results for normalized solutions when parameters satisfy different assumptions, by employing the constrained variational methods and the mountain pass theorem.
These results comprehensively address the existence of normalized solutions for the fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system in the autonomous case, with no existing results on the concentration behavior and local uniqueness of normalized solutions in the non-autonomous case.

Following a literature review, the existence, concentration behavior and local uniqueness of normalized solutions for non-autonomous fractional Schrödinger-Poisson systems have received little attention in existing literature. Even for the existing results on classical elliptic equations, they are mainly obtained under the condition of unbounded harmonic potentials.

Therefore, it is nature to ask whether the normalized solutions of (
1.2
) admit similar limit properties when potential function is bounded and lack of homogeneity.
Using the Lax-Milgram theorem, the fractional Schrödinger-Poisson system (
1.2
) can be transformed into a single fractional Schrödinger equation with a nonlocal term. Indeed, given any fixed
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(-\Delta)^{s}\phi=u^{2}

\phi_{u}\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\phi_{u}(x)=c_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3-2s}}dy\quad\hbox{with}\quad c_{s}=\pi^{-\frac{3}{2}}2^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{3-2s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s)}.

(1.5)

In the sequel, we often omit the constant
c_{s}
for convenience. Substituting (
1.5

1.2
), it reduces to a single fractional Schrödinger equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=\mu u+\phi_{u}u+a^{p-2}|u|^{p-2}u\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},

(1.6)

where
s\in(\frac{3}{4},1)
,
a>0
and
2<p<2+\frac{4s}{3}
. The main purpose of this paper is to focus on the existence and asymptotic behavior of normalized solutions to problem (
1.6

\mu

1.6

e_{m}(a):=\inf_{u\in S_{m}}E_{a}(u)\quad\hbox{$a>0$},

(1.7)

where
S_{m}:=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|u\|_{2}^{2}=m\}

E_{a}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2})dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3-2s}}dxdy-\frac{2a^{p-2}}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{p}dx.

(1.8)

Remark 1.5
.

Compared with the study of the concentration and uniqueness of normalized solutions in classical elliptic problems
[
16
,
18
,
29
,
47
]
, the fractional Laplacian operator introduces some additional difficulties, which require the development of new analytical methods.

(1)
Compared with our previous work
[
34
]
, we obtain the asymptotic behaviors and local uniqueness without conditions of homogeneity and unboundedness of the potential, while the potential is
|x|^{2}
in
[
34
]
. Our results relax the global condition on the potential function to the local condition, which only depends on the asymptotic behaviors of the potential function near its minimum point. Similar enhancements have also been widely applied in the study of classical elliptic equations, we refer to
[
5
,
30
]
. This improvement enables our results to be applied more widely.

(2)
The proof of Theorem
1.3
follows from optimal energy estimates

\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{1}(a)}{\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{\ast}}}\right)^{\frac{4s(p-2)}{4s+6-3p}}}=\frac{3p-6-4s}{6-(3-2s)p},

where
a^{\ast}:=\|Q\|_{2}^{2}
and
Q>0
is the unique positive solution of (
1.10
). However, when obtaining a lower bound estimate for
e_{1}(a)
, the
L^{2}
-subcritical nonlinearity term renders the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality inapplicable directly. To address this, we have to employ the fact that

\frac{e_{1}(a)}{\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^{\ast}}}\right)^{\frac{4s(p-2)}{4s+6-3p}}}\geq\tilde{e}_{1}(\sqrt{a^{\ast}})+o(1)\quad\hbox{as $a\rightarrow\infty$},

where
\tilde{e}_{1}(\sqrt{a^{\ast}})
is a new auxiliary minimization problem defined in (
3.3
), see Lemma
3.1
.

(3)
The inapplicability of classical local elliptic estimates leads to a significant increase in the difficulty of deriving the decay estimate for
|\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}(x)|
, where
\hat{u}_{ik}
be as in (
4.4
). Inspired by
[
3
, Theorem 12.2.4]
, we obtain the upper bound estimate for
\|\hat{u}_{ik}(y)\|_{C^{2s}(B_{1}(x))}
by combining the
L^{\infty}
-estimate and the nonlocal term estimate, thereby deriving the decay property of
|\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}(x)|
, see Lemma
A.1
. Moreover, the decay estimate of
\hat{\eta}_{k}
serves as key information for establishing the local uniqueness of the constrained minimizers via contradiction, where
\hat{\eta}_{k}
is defined in (
4.5
). However, due to the complexity of the equation satisfied by
\hat{\eta}_{k}
, classical methods for handling fractional elliptic equations, such as Nash-Moser methods and Bessel kernel properties, are no longer applicable. To address this challenge, we have to develop a new comparison principle, see Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
A.2
.

(4)
The Pohozaev-type identity plays an important role in proving the local uniqueness of constrained minimizers. Nevertheless, the non-locality of the fractional Laplacian operator makes it impossible to apply integration by parts and other conventional methods directly. To overcome this difficulty, we employ the extension method proposed by Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
1
]
, which transforms the nonlocal problem into a local one in higher dimensions. Through precise integral estimates, we establish the Pohozaev-type identity, see Lemma
4.3
, Lemma
A.3
and Lemma
A.4

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{3-2s}}dxdy\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{\frac{12}{3+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{3+2s}{3}}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{6-4s}{4s}}.

(2.4)

Using the Young’s inequality, we deduce from (
1.9
) and (
2.4
) that for any

\begin{split}E^{0}_{a}(u)&\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx-C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{6-4s}{4s}}-Ca^{p-2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{3p-6}{4s}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}dx-C_{a},\end{split}

(2.5)

which shows that
E^{0}_{a}(u)
is bounded from below.

Now consider
m\in(0,\infty)
and a sequence
\left\{m_{n}\right\}\subset\left(0,\infty\right)
such that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}m_{n}=m
. There exists a sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset S_{m}
such that

. Moreover, (
2.5

\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let
w_{n}:=\sqrt{\frac{m_{n}}{m}}u_{n}
, one has
w_{n}\in S_{m_{n}}
and

\begin{split}E^{0}_{a}(w_{n})=&\frac{m_{n}}{m}E^{0}_{a}(u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{m_{n}}{m}\left(1-\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u_{n}(x)|^{2}|u_{n}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3-2s}}dxdy\\
&-\frac{2a^{p-2}}{p}\frac{m_{n}}{m}\left(1-\left(\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx.\end{split}

Them we have

\begin{split}\left|E^{0}_{a}(w_{n})-E^{0}_{a}(u_{n})\right|\leq&\left|\frac{m_{n}}{m}-1\right|\left|E^{0}_{a}(u_{n})\right|+C\left|1-\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{\frac{12}{3+2s}}dx\right)^{\frac{3+2s}{3}}\\
&+C\left|1-\left(\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u_{n}|^{p}dx\\
\leq&C\left|\frac{m_{n}}{m}-1\right|+C\left|\left(\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-1\right|.\end{split}

(2.6)

Thus

e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)\leq E^{0}_{a}(w_{n})\leq E^{0}_{a}(u_{n})+C\left|\frac{m_{n}}{m}-1\right|+C\left|\left(\frac{m_{n}}{m}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-1\right|,

(2.7)

which implies that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E^{0}_{a}(u_{n})=e^{0}_{m}(a).

(2.8)

Further, for all
n
, there exists
v_{n}\in S_{m_{n}}
such that

e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)\leq E^{0}_{a}(v_{n})\leq e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)+\frac{1}{n},

from which and (
2.7
) we deduce that
E^{0}_{a}(v_{n})
is uniformly bounded. Then (
2.5
) shows that
\left\{v_{n}\right\}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Let

, similar to (
2.6
), one derive that

\left|E^{0}_{a}(h_{n})-E^{0}_{a}(v_{n})\right|\leq C\left|\frac{m}{m_{n}}-1\right|+C\left|\left(\frac{m}{m_{n}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-1\right|.

Thus we have

\displaystyle e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)

\displaystyle\geq E^{0}_{a}(v_{n})-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\geq E^{0}_{a}(h_{n})-C\left|\frac{m}{m_{n}}-1\right|-C\left|\left(\frac{m}{m_{n}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-1\right|-\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\geq e^{0}_{m}(a)-C\left|\frac{m}{m_{n}}-1\right|-C\left|\left(\frac{m}{m_{n}}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-1\right|-\frac{1}{n}

which means that

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)\geq e^{0}_{m}(a).

Combining with (

), we obtain that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{0}_{m_{n}}(a)=e^{0}_{m}(a)

as
m_{n}\rightarrow m
, this completes the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

\begin{split}&e^{0}_{\theta\lambda}(a)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E^{0}_{a}(\sqrt{\theta}v_{n})\\

<&\theta e^{0}_{\lambda}(a).\end{split}

(2.11)

It then follows from (

.

(2.16)

Similar to (
2.4
)-(
2.5

\left\{u_{n}\right\}\subset S_{m}
such that

\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C,\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{a}(u_{n})=e_{m}(a)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=m.

Applying the concentration compactness lemma, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by

This implies that

which contradicts (

Now we claim that there exists some

This implies that

e_{m}(a)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{a}(u_{n})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E^{\infty}_{a}(u_{n})\geq e^{\infty}_{m}(a),

which contradicts (
2.16

2.2

\left\{x_{n}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and

We now prove that

A.2
), one derive that

\begin{split}\|\hat{u}_{ik}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{3}(x))}&\leq\frac{C}{1+|y|^{3+2s}}\leq\frac{C}{1+(|x|-3)^{3+2s}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{3+2s}}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{for $|x|\geq R$ as $k\rightarrow\infty$}.\end{split}

(A.4)

Applying (
A.2
) again, it holds that

\begin{split}&\int_{|z|\geq 3}\frac{|\hat{u}_{ik}(x+z)|}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\hat{u}_{ik}(x+z)|}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\\
=&C\int_{B^{c}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz+C\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz.\end{split}

(A.5)

By
[
20
, Lemma A.3]
, one has

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{B^{c}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\right|\\
\leq&C\int_{B^{c}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\\
\leq&C\left(\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{3+2s}}+\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{|x|^{2s}}\right)\\
\leq&\frac{C}{(1+|x|)^{3+2s}}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{for $|x|\geq R$ as $k\rightarrow\infty$}.\end{split}

(A.6)

Furthermore, a direct calculation implies that

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}dz\right|\\
\leq&C\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{(1+|z|)^{3+2s}}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}dz\\
\leq&\frac{C}{\left(1+\frac{|x|}{2}\right)^{3+2s}}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{1+|x+z|^{3+2s}}dz\\
\leq&\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{1+|y|^{3+2s}}dy\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{as $k\rightarrow\infty$},\end{split}

(A.7)

where we have used the fact that
|z|\geq|x|-|x-z|\geq|x|-\frac{|x|}{2}=\frac{|x|}{2}
for
z\in B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)
. Combining (
A.3
)-(
A.7
), we observe that

|\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}(x)|\leq\|\hat{u}_{ik}(y)\|_{C^{2s}(B_{1}(x))}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{for $|x|\geq R>3$ as $k\rightarrow\infty$}.

(A.8)

Moreover, we also have

|\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}(x)|\leq\frac{1+R^{3+2s}}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\|\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{for $|x|\leq R$ as $k\rightarrow\infty$}.

(A.9)

Thus, (
A.8
) and (
A.9
) imply (
A.1
), and the proof of Lemma
A.1
is completed.

To establish the decay estimate of
\hat{\eta}_{k}(x)
, we develop the following comparison principle.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
|g(x)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{|x|^{3+2s}}
, and let
R>0
be sufficiently large so that
\inf\limits_{|x|>R}\rho(x):=\rho_{0}>0
. If
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-\Delta)^{s}u+\rho(x)u=g(x)\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\\
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(x)=0.\end{array}\right.

(A.10)

Then the following decay estimate holds

|u(x)|\leq\frac{C}{1+|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}.

(A.11)

Proof.

For
s\in(0,1)
and
\lambda>0
, let

\varphi_{\lambda}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda^{-3-2s}&\quad\hbox{$|x|<\lambda$},\\
|x|^{-3-2s}&\quad\hbox{$|x|\geq\lambda$}.\end{array}\right.

We first claim that for any
\lambda>0
, there exist
R_{0}>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that

-C_{1}\lambda^{-2s}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\leq(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\leq-C_{1}^{-1}\lambda^{-2s}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\quad\hbox{for $x\in B^{c}_{R_{0}}(0)$}.

(A.12)

In fact, assume that
\lambda>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfies
|x|>3\lambda
, one has

(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=-\frac{C_{s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy.

(A.13)

For
y\in B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(0)
, we have that
|x\pm y|\geq\lambda
. By Taylor expansion, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(0)}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\right|\\
=&|x|^{-3-4s}\left|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}(0)}\frac{\left|y+\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}+\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}-2}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\right|\\
\leq&C_{2}|x|^{-3-4s}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}(0)}\frac{|y|^{2}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\leq C_{3}|x|^{-3-4s}.\end{split}

(A.14)

For
y\in B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\backslash B_{\lambda}(x)
, we see that
|x+y|\geq|x-y|\geq\lambda
. Moreover, we also have
\left|y+\frac{x}{|x|}\right|\geq\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|
for
y\in B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\backslash B_{\frac{\lambda}{|x|}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)
and
|y|\geq\frac{2}{3}
for
y\in B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)
. Then we obtain that

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\backslash B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
=&\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\backslash B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{|x+y|^{-3-2s}+|x-y|^{-3-2s}-2|x|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}\\
=&|x|^{-3-4s}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\backslash B_{\frac{\lambda}{|x|}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\frac{\left|y+\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}+\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}-2}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
\leq&C_{4}|x|^{-3-4s}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\backslash B_{\frac{\lambda}{|x|}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}dy\leq C_{5}\lambda^{-2s}|x|^{-3-2s}.\end{split}

(A.15)

Moreover, note that
|y|\leq\frac{4}{3}
for
y\in B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\backslash B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
\geq&|x|^{-3-4s}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\backslash B_{\frac{\lambda}{|x|}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\frac{\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy-\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\frac{2}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\right)\\
\geq&C_{6}|x|^{-3-4s}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\backslash B_{\frac{\lambda}{|x|}}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)}\left|y-\frac{x}{|x|}\right|^{-3-2s}dy-C_{7}|x|^{-3-4s}\\
\geq&C_{8}\lambda^{-2s}|x|^{-3-2s}-C_{9}|x|^{-3-4s}.\end{split}

(A.16)

For
y\in B_{\lambda}(x)
, we can see that
|x+y|>\lambda>|x-y|
and
|y|\geq|x|-\lambda\geq\frac{2|x|}{3}
. Then we have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
=&\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{|x+y|^{-3-2s}+\lambda^{-3-2s}-2|x|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
\leq&2\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{\lambda^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\leq C_{10}\lambda^{-2s}|x|^{-3-2s},\end{split}

(A.17)

and

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{|x+y|^{-3-2s}+\lambda^{-3-2s}-2|x|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\\
\geq&\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}\frac{-2|x|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\geq-C_{11}\lambda^{3}(|x|-\lambda)^{-3-2s}|x|^{-3-2s}\geq-C_{12}|x|^{-3-4s}.\end{split}

(A.18)

The same method allows us to estimate the integral domains in
B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(-x)\backslash B_{\lambda}(-x)
and
B_{\lambda}(-x)
. For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\left[B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(0)\cup B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\cup B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(-x)\right]
, we have
|x\pm y|\geq\frac{|x|}{3}
for
|x|>3\lambda
. Thus

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\left[B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(0)\cup B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(x)\cup B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(-x)\right]}\frac{|x+y|^{-3-2s}+|x-y|^{-3-2s}-2|x|^{-3-2s}}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\right|\\
\leq&C_{13}|x|^{-3-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash B_{\frac{|x|}{3}}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\leq C_{14}|x|^{-3-4s}.\end{split}

(A.19)

Combining (
A.14
)-(
A.19
), it then follows from the fact
|x|>3\lambda
that

C_{15}^{-1}\lambda^{-2s}|x|^{-3-2s}-C_{15}|x|^{-3-4s}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\varphi_{\lambda}(x+y)+\varphi_{\lambda}(x-y)-2\varphi_{\lambda}(x)}{|y|^{3+2s}}dy\leq C_{15}\lambda^{-2s}|x|^{-3-2s}.

Let
R_{0}>3\lambda
such that
C_{15}^{-1}\lambda^{-2s}-C_{15}|x|^{-2s}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{15}^{-1}\lambda^{-2s}
for
|x|\geq R_{0}
, then (
A.13
) shows that (
A.12
) holds, and claim is proved. We now prove (
A.11
). Since
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then there exists
M>0
such that
|u(x)|\leq M
. Take
R\geq R_{0}>3\lambda
large enough, then
\rho(x)\geq\rho_{0}>0
for
|x|\geq R
. Choose
\lambda>\left(\frac{C_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2s}}
, where
C_{1}>0
is given by (
A.12
). Define

v(x):=\frac{C_{16}}{|x|^{3+2s}}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad C_{16}\geq\max\left\{\frac{C_{0}}{\rho_{0}-C_{1}\lambda^{-2s}},R^{3+2s}M\right\}.

It then follows from (
A.12
) that

(-\Delta)^{s}v+\rho(x)v\geq\frac{-C_{1}C_{16}\lambda^{-2s}}{|x|^{3+2s}}+\frac{\rho_{0}C_{16}}{|x|^{3+2s}}\geq\frac{C_{0}}{|x|^{3+2s}}\geq|g(x)|\quad\hbox{for $|x|\geq R$}.

(A.20)

Together with (
A.10
), this yields that

(-\Delta)^{s}\dot{w}(x)+\rho(x)\dot{w}(x)\leq 0\quad\hbox{for $|x|\geq R$},

(A.21)

where
\dot{w}(x):=|u(x)|-v(x)
. Note that

v(x)=\frac{C_{16}}{|x|^{3+2s}}\geq\frac{C_{16}}{R^{3+2s}}\geq M\geq|u(x)|\quad\hbox{for $|x|\leq R$},

then we deduce that
\dot{w}(x)\leq 0
in
B_{R}(0)
. We now claim that
\dot{w}(x)\leq 0
in
B^{c}_{R}(0)
. Otherwise, there exists some global maximum point
x_{1}\in B^{c}_{R}(0)
satisfying
\dot{w}(x_{1})>0
. It then follows from (
A.21
) that

(-\Delta)^{s}\dot{w}(x_{1})\leq-\rho(x_{1})\dot{w}(x_{1})\leq-\rho_{0}\dot{w}(x_{1})<0.

However, we have

(-\Delta)^{s}\dot{w}(x_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\dot{w}(x_{1})-\dot{w}(y)}{|x-y|^{3+2s}}dy\geq 0.

This is a contradiction. Hence, we infer that
|u(x)|\leq v(x)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which further implies that (
A.11
) holds. The proof of Lemma
A.2
is completed.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\hat{u}_{ik}
be as in (
4.4
), there holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dx=0.

Proof.

We employ the
s
-harmonic extension technique following the approach in
[
1
]
. Deonte

\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{s}(x-z,t)\hat{u}_{ik}(z)dz\quad\hbox{$(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}$ and $i=1,2$},

of solves

\begin{cases}&-{\rm div}(t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})=0\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}$},\\
&\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(x,0)=\hat{u}_{ik}(x)\quad\hbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\\
&-\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(x,t)=(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\quad\hbox{on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$},\end{cases}

where
i=1,2
, and

\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}:=\{(x,t):x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},t>0\},~~\mathcal{P}_{s}:=\beta(s)\frac{t^{2s}}{(|x|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{3+2s}{2}}},

\beta(s)
is a constant such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{s}(x,1)\mathrm{d}x=1
. For
R>1
, set

\displaystyle\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\left[\frac{1}{R},\infty\right):\>|x|^{2}+t^{2}\leq R^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\left\{t=\frac{1}{R}\right\}:\>|x|^{2}\leq R^{2}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\left[\frac{1}{R},\infty\right):\>|x|^{2}+t^{2}=R^{2}\right\}.

Clearly, one has

\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}=\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}\cup\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\nu=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(0,0,0,-1)&\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}$},\\
\frac{(x,t)}{R}&\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}$},\end{array}\right.

where
\nu
denotes the unit outward normal vector of
\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}
. Direct calculation shows that

{\rm div}\left(t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)={\rm div}\left(t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right)\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}+t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\right).

Applying the divergence theorem, it follows that

\int_{\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)dxdt=\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dS,

(A.22)

where we have used the fact that
{\rm div}(t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})=0
. Similar to the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 4.1]
, we know that
\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4})
. Simple calculation yields that

\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\right)}{\partial x_{j}},

from which and (
A.22
) we deduce that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}dxdt=\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dS.

(A.23)

Integrating by parts, one derive that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\left(\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}dxdt&=\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\nu_{j}dS-\int_{\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\partial_{x_{j}}(t^{1-2s})dxdt\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}t^{1-2s}\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\nu_{j}dS,\end{split}

(A.24)

where we have used the facts that
\partial_{x_{j}}(t^{1-2s})=0

and
\nu_{j}=0
on
\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}
. Note that

\left|(x,t)\right|=R,\quad\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(x,t)\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left|(x,t)\right|^{3+2s}},\quad\hbox{and}\quad t\geq\frac{1}{R}\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}$},

then we have

\left|\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}t^{1-2s}\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|^{2}\nu_{j}dS\right|\leq CR^{2s-1}R^{-6-4s}R^{3}\rightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as $R\rightarrow\infty$}.

(A.25)

We proceed to estimate the RHS of (
A.23
). Clearly

\begin{split}&\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{+}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dS\\
=&\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dS+\int_{\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}t^{1-2s}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dS:=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(A.26)

Since
\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}=-\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial t}
on
\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}
, we thus obtain that

I_{1}=-\left(\frac{1}{R}\right)^{1-2s}\int_{|x|\leq\sqrt{R^{2}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}}}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\left(x,\frac{1}{R}\right)}{\partial t}\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\left(x,\frac{1}{R}\right)}{\partial x_{j}}dx.

Taking
R\rightarrow\infty
, one can see that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}I_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dx.

(A.27)

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial\nu}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\cdot\nu\right|\leq\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left|(x,t)\right|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}$},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\left|(x,t)\right|^{3+2s}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad t\geq\frac{1}{R}\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}$}.

We thus derive that

\left|I_{2}\right|\leq CR^{2s-1}R^{-6-4s}R^{3}\rightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as $R\rightarrow\infty$}.

(A.28)

Combining (
A.23
)-(
A.28
), we can conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\frac{\partial\hat{u}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}dx=0,

this therefore completes the proof of Lemma
A.3
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

x_{1k}
is the unique global maximum point of

, and
\hat{u}_{ik}
is given in (

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}[(x-x_{1k})\cdot\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}]dx=\frac{2s-3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\hat{u}_{ik}\right|^{2}dx.

, for

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\left[\frac{1}{R},\infty\right):|x-x_{1k}|^{2}+t^{2}\leq R^{2}\right\}

{\rm div}(t^{1-2s}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})=0
, and

\displaystyle\text{div}\left(t^{1-2s}\left[(X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}X\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(s-\frac{3}{2}\right)t^{1-2s}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2},

\displaystyle\text{div}\left(\frac{3-2s}{2}t^{1-2s}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3-2s}{2}t^{1-2s}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}.

By divergence Theorem, we have

\int_{\partial D^{+}_{R,\frac{1}{R}}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS=\int_{D^{+}_{R,\frac{1}{R}}}\text{div}\mathbf{P}\,dxdt=0.

The boundary integral can be divided into two parts as follows

\int_{\partial D^{+}_{R,\frac{1}{R}}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS=\int_{\partial D^{1}_{R,\frac{1}{R}}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS+\int_{\partial D^{2}_{R,\frac{1}{R}}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS.

Since
\nu=(0,0,0,-1)
on
\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}
, we then deduce from the definition of
\mathbf{P}
that

\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu=-t^{1-2s}\left[(X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})\partial_{t}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}t\right]-\frac{3-2s}{2}t^{1-2s}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}\partial_{t}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}.

As
R\to\infty
,
t\to 0^{+}
, and
X=(x-x_{0},t)\to(x-x_{0},0)
, it is easy to check that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}-(X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})(t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})&=-((x-x_{0})\cdot\nabla_{x}\hat{u}_{ik})\cdot\left(-(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\right)\\
&=((x-x_{0})\cdot\nabla\hat{u}_{ik})(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}.\end{split}

Since
s<1
, one can see that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2}t^{2-2s}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}=0
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}-\frac{3-2s}{2}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})=-\frac{3-2s}{2}\hat{u}_{ik}\cdot\left(-(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\right)=\frac{3-2s}{2}\hat{u}_{ik}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}.

Using the above facts, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{1}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu dS=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\left((x-x_{0})\cdot\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}\right)(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}+\frac{3-2s}{2}\hat{u}_{ik}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}\right]dx.

Note that

|X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|\leq R|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|\quad\hbox{and}\quad t\geq\frac{1}{R}\quad\hbox{on $\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}$},

then using the decay property of
|\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|

and
|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|
, we proceed with the integral estimation

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}\left(t^{1-2s}\left[\frac{(X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})^{2}}{R}-\frac{R}{2}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}\right]+\frac{3-2s}{2R}t^{1-2s}\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}(X\cdot\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik})\right)dS\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}t^{1-2s}\left(\frac{(R^{2}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|)^{2}}{R}+\frac{R}{2}|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}+C|\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}||\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|\right)dS

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}t^{1-2s}\left(\frac{3}{2}R|\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|^{2}+C|\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}||\nabla\tilde{\hat{u}}_{ik}|\right)dS

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(R^{-3-2s}+R^{-4-2s}\right),

which implies that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\left|\int_{\partial D_{R,\frac{1}{R}}^{2}}\mathbf{P}\cdot\nu\,dS\right|=0.

Till now, one can conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{s}\hat{u}_{ik}[(x-x_{1k})\cdot\nabla\hat{u}_{ik}]dx=\frac{2s-3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\hat{u}_{ik}\right|^{2}dx,

this therefore completes the proof of Lemma
A.4
. ∎
