Efficacy of remimazolam besylate versus propofol on hemodynamics during anesthetic induction in elderly patients undergoing bronchoscopy: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Danni Li, Yang Cheng, YuanYuan Zhou, Siyi Wei, Dan Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe effect of remimazolam besylate versus propofol on hemodynamics during bronchoscopy remains uncertain.AimTo test the hypothesis that remimazolam besylate can provide a more stable hemodynamic profile than propofol in the elderly for bronchoscopy under general anesthesia and calculate the optimal induction doses.MethodsA total of 232 patients aged ≥ 65 years who underwent bronchoscopy were enrolled. Participants were randomly divided into the remimazolam besylate group or propofol group for sedation. The patients received titrated induction with remimazolam besylate or propofol combined with remifentanil and maintenance with assigned agents. Vital signs and bispectral index (BIS) changes were monitored. The cognitive test was performed before and after operation. The primary outcome was the incidence of hypotension during induction. Secondary key outcomes included average real variability of mean arterial pressure, titrated dose of remimazolam besylate and propofol during induction, and the BIS changes. Other outcomes included baseline characteristics, intraoperative anesthesia-related data, and other adverse events.ResultsThe remimazolam besylate group had a significantly lower incidence of hypotension during anesthetic induction (28.44% vs. 73.33%,95% CI 1.88–3.56, P < 0.001). The average real variability of mean arterial pressure of remimazolam besylate group was 8.25 ((Interquartile range [IQR] 6.33–11.50) mmHg/min smaller than that 12.33 (IQR 9.00–14.67) mmHg/min in propofol group (P < 0.001). The optimal dose of remimazolam besylate by titration during induction was 0.16 (IQR 0.15–0.18) mg/kg. The mean BIS values in remimazolam besylate group was higher than that in propofol group [59.35 (IQR 53.15–65.11) vs. 55.13 (IQR 51.72–61.75), P < 0.001].ConclusionRemimazolam besylate was superior to propofol in terms of hemodynamics for the elderly undergoing bronchoscopy. Furthermore, we proposed a titrated dose of remimazolam besylate 0.16 mg/kg during induction in this patient population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-026-03966-x.

Introduction

In China, flexible bronchoscopy (FB) is performed under sedation for most elderly patients suffering from multiple pulmonary disorders [1]. FB is traditionally performed under topical anesthesia. An invasive stimulation may lead to anxiety, cough, pain, and hemodynamic instability, which is poorly tolerated [2]. The spreading of medical comfort care as well as the increasing complexity of bronchoscopy procedures encourage medical staff to turn to sedation or general anesthesia. This has been widely used to minimize complications such as cardiac arrhythmias and hypertension and improve the tolerability of patients during operation [3].

Hypotension is common during induction of general anesthesia, which mainly results from hypovolemia and anesthetic drugs and is expected to be prevented [4]. Due to a rapid turnover of patients in bronchoscopy, it is difficult to optimize the volume status in a relatively short period, the selection of anesthetics and vasoactive drugs use may be more therapeutically significant. Clinically, propofol and midazolam are the most commonly used outpatient sedative agents. Rapid onset, short action, and low risk of postoperative nausea and vomiting make both of them acceptable sedatives [5]. However, propofol may induce significant hemodynamic changes, respiratory depression, and injection pain [6, 7]. Especially the elderly with vascular stiffening, cardiac dysfunction, and multiple comorbidities, are vulnerable to the effects of anesthetics [8]. Compared with propofol, midazolam has a lower risk of cardiorespiratory depression and less injection pain [9]. Unfortunately, repeated injection or continuous infusion will lead to unpredictable recovery time which is associated with cognitive dysfunction [10–12].

Remimazolam besylate is a novel hypnotic drug with remarkable advantages, including rapid onset and short-acting, extra hepatic–renal esterase metabolism, minimal cardiorespiratory depression, and availability with a reversal drug [13]. Previous studies have suggested that it is a promising sedative in terms of safety and efficacy [14]. In particular, it might be a preferable selection in ambulatory surgery where the outpatients choose to be discharged on the surgical day [13]. Nevertheless, concerning the hemodynamic profile during bronchoscopy, no large interventional studies comparing remimazolam and propofol have been conducted before. Therefore, we hypothesized that the use of remimazolam besylate might reduce the risk of hypotension during induction compared with propofol in patients aged ≥ 65 years. We also explored appropriate anesthetic induction doses and subsequently compared the bispectral index (BIS) changes in both groups.

Methods

Study design and approval

This single-center, prospective, randomized clinical trial was registered before enrollment at chictr.org.cn (ChiCTR2200063276, date of registration: September 2, 2022) and approved by Ethics Committee of Sichuan Academy of Medical Sciences and Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (Number 2022304) on August 3, 2022. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects before they entered the trial. The study adhered to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2010 guidelines.

Patient population

Patients aged 65–80 years scheduled for fiberoptic bronchoscopy under general anesthesia between October 1, 2022 and January 30, 2023 were recruited for this study at the Department of Respiratory at Sichuan Academy of Medical Sciences and Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status (ASA) IV or higher; (2) Asthma attack, hemoptysis, myasthenia gravis, or severe central airway stenosis; (3) a history of mental disease, neurologic disorders, drug dependency, or alcoholism; (4) inability to understand and complete the cognitive screening tests; (5) allergic history of benzodiazepines or propofol or other anesthetics; (6) body mass index ≥ 30 kg/m2.

Randomization and blinding

Patients were randomized 1:1 to the remimazolam besylate group or the propofol group according to a computer-generated random sequence using the SPSS 23.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). The treatment allocations were sealed in opaque envelopes, which were kept in the investigator who was unaware of the assignment and were not opened until induction of anesthesia on the surgical day. The participants, surgical team, and outcome assessors were all blinded to the allocation. To ensure patients’ safety, the anesthesiologists who participated in the conduct of anesthesia without assessing outcomes were unblinded.

Study interventions

Preoperative medicine with dexamethasone and an albuterol inhaler were used when clinically needed. An electrocardiogram, non-invasive blood pressure (BP), pulse oximetry, and BIS monitoring were performed. Baseline BP was recorded in the supine position as the average of 3 consecutive measurements, with a difference of < 5 mmHg. All patients received preoxygenation for 5 min. Induction started with an initial bolus of either 0.1 mg/kg remimazolam besylate (Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd, China) or 1 mg/kg propofol (Aspen Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd, Ireland) intravenously over 30 s. The patient’s shoulder would be shaken mildly every 30 s to verify consciousness. Either remimazolam besylate (0.05 mg/kg) or propofol (0.5 mg/kg) was added for each additional administration until loss of consciousness (LOC), which was defined as Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alert (MOAA/S) ≤ 1. After the LOC, remifentanil (1 ug/kg) combined with succinylcholine (1.5 mg/kg) or cisatracurium (0.05 mg/kg) was administered and then the laryngeal mask (LMA) was inserted under the guidance of FB.

Immediately after intubation, all patients underwent mechanical ventilation in volume-controlled ventilation mode. Initial ventilator settings were standardized across groups: target tidal volume of 6–8 ml/kg, and the inspired oxygen fraction was set at 0.4. Ventilatory frequency was adjusted to maintain end-tidal carbon dioxide between 35 and 45 mmHg, with an inspiratory-to-expiratory ratio of 1:1.5.

For certain surgery including bronchoscopy, bronchoalveolar lavage, and transbronchial brushing, we selected succinylcholine, while for the remaining surgical types, we chose cisatracurium. Before the operation, local anesthesia of 3 ml of 2% lidocaine was sprayed on the airway. Maintenance was started by infusions of remimazolam besylate (1 mg/kg/h) or propofol (6 mg/kg/h) according to the group assignment. Intraoperative analgesia was started with infusions of remifentanil (8 ug/kg/h). The infusions of maintenance agents were adjusted to maintain sedation based on clinical signs (PRST score) [15]. The PRST score contains systolic blood pressure, heart rate, sweating, and tears. One of the following signs indicated light anesthesia: sudden reactive hypertension and/or tachycardia (a rise of ≥ 25% from baseline systolic BP and heart rate over 5 min), sweating, and tearing [15]. Additional remimazolam besylate (0.05 mg/kg) or propofol (0.5 mg/kg) or remifentanil (20–40 ug) boluses were performed for light anesthesia in two groups, respectively. If total boluses of remimazolam besylate were insufficient to maintain adequate sedation with a maximum of 5 doses during any 15-min window, the rescue sedation of propofol was initiated and the patient was withdrawn from the study. When hypotension occurred, an intravenous bolus of ephedrine (3–6 mg) was administered if it was accompanied by heart rate (HR) ≤ 45 bpm. Otherwise, it was treated with phenylephrine (50–100 ug) if it was accompanied by HR > 45 bpm. In any case, when HR dropped below 40 bpm, the patient was treated with administering atropine (0.2–0.5 mg). Cisatracurium was routinely reversed by neostigmine (0.02–0.05 mg/kg) and remimazolam besylate was reversed by flumazenil (0.2 mg). Recovery from anesthesia was defined as three consecutive MOAA/S values of 5, assessed every 1 min following the end of the surgery. Then patients were extubated in the operating room and transferred to the postoperative care unit. The Hopkins Verbal Learning Test-Revised (HVLT-R) was performed before drug administration and within 5 min after recovering fully alert. The Modified Brice Questionnaire for intraoperative awareness was performed within 10 min after the patient fully recovered from anesthesia.

Study outcomes assessments

The primary outcome was the incidence of hypotension during induction. Hypotension was defined as a decrease of mean arterial pressure (MAP) of ≥ 30% from baseline. Secondary outcomes included the area under the curve (AUC) of threshold (≤ 65 mmHg, ≤ 60 mmHg, ≤ 50 mmHg), average real variability of MAP (ARV-MAP) and time-weighted average MAP (TWA-MAP) throughout the procedure, a maximum decrease of MAP during induction (MAPdec), the incidence of hypotension during maintenance, titrated dose of remimazolam besylate and propofol during induction, the BIS value when the MOAA/S score ≤ 1 (BISLOC), the average BIS value during maintenance (BISmai) and cognitive function changes before and after surgery. Other outcomes included baseline characteristics, intraoperative anesthesia-related data, and other adverse events.

Vital signs (BP, MAP, HR) were monitored every 1 min during induction and every 2 min until patients recovered from anesthesia, which were recorded at the following time points: arrival at the operating room (T0); at the start of drug administration (T1); 1 min after drug administration (T2); 2 min after drug administration (T3); 3 min after drug administration (T4); at the end of anesthetic induction (T5); at the start of surgery (T6); at 5th min during surgery (T7); at 10th min during surgery (T8); at 15th min during surgery (T9); at 20th min during surgery (T10); at 25th min during surgery (T11); at 30th min during surgery (T12); recovery from anesthesia (T13). The BIS value was monitored every 1 min in this procedure and recorded at the following time points: arrival at the operating room (T0); at the start of drug administration (T1); 1 min after drug administration (T2); 2 min after drug administration (T3); 3 min after drug administration (T4); at the end of anesthetic induction (T5); at the start of surgery (T6); at 5th min during surgery (T7); at 10th min during surgery (T8); at 20th min during surgery (T9); at 30th min during surgery (T10); at 40th min during surgery (T11); recovery from anesthesia (T12).

The area under the threshold for MAP ≥ 65 mmHg, ≥ 60 mmHg, ≥ 50 mmHg was calculated using the trapezoidal rule: for each consecutive pair of MAP measurements, if the MAP value was ≥ 65 mmHg, the excess pressure (MAP—65 mmHg) was multiplied by the time interval (minutes) between measurements, and these products were summed to generate the cumulative AUC [15].

We used the following algorithm to calculate ARV-MAP, which represents the average of absolute changes between adjacent BP readings [16].\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{ARV}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\mid\ BP_{k+1}-BP_k\mid\ mmHg/min$$\end{document}

Where T is the total time, N is the number and k is the order of BP measurements, respectively.

TWA-MAP was calculated as the area under the curve of the MAP measurements divided by the total time [16]. The MAPdec during induction was defined as a maximum MAP reduction from baseline value. We also recorded the BIS value when the MOAA/S score ≤ 1 and the mean BIS value during maintenance, which were defined as BISLOC and BISmai, respectively. Time to LOC and recovery time were the intervals from the drug administration to MOAA/S score ≤ 1 and from the end of operation to patients reaching full alert, respectively.

Statistical analysis

The sample size was calculated using PASS 11.0 (NCSS Statistical Software, East Kaysville, USA). The incidence of hypotension during induction with propofol was 70% [17]. Another study showed remimazolam besylate with an induction dose of 0.4 mg/kg led to hypotension during induction in 34% of subjects [11]. Based on our preliminary trial results, we assumed that an 18% absolute reduction in remimazolam besylate group would be reasonable. At an α of 0.8 and a β of 0.2, a total sample size of 232 patients (116 in each group) was required in this study.

The SAS 9.4 software (SAS Institute Inc, North Carolina, USA) was used for data analysis. Normality was tested using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Continuous variables with normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation; non–normal distribution was reported by median (interquartile range) and was analyzed using the t-test and the Mann–Whitney U test, respectively; Categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage) and were analyzed by the χ2 test. Notably​, we used the Hodges-Lehmann estimator, a non-parametric statistic of median pairwise differences between two groups, which provided a robust measure of location shift—critical for non-normal physiological endpoints [15]. There were no missing data. A 2-tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

From October 1, 2022 to January 30, 2023, a total of 232 patients were recruited in this study, of whom 8 were excluded and 224 patients were randomized to two groups. Later, 10 patients discontinued the trial. Finally, 109 patients in remimazolam besylate group and 105 patients in propofol group were analyzed. Hemodynamic parameters

The primary outcome showed a significantly lower incidence of hypotension during induction in remimazolam besylate group (28.44% vs. 73.33%, Risk ratio: 2.59, 95%CI 1.88–3.56, P < 0.001) (Table 2), as well as during maintenance (52.29% vs. 74.29%, Risk ratio: 1.42, 95%CI 1.51–1.76, P < 0.001) (Table 2). Median area under the MAP ≤ 65 mmHg was 4 (0–13.5) mmHg·min in the propofol group versus 0 (0–0.5) mmHg·min in the remimazolam besylate group (P < 0.001), with a Hodges-Lehmann estimated location shift of −3 mmHg·min (95% CI −5 to 0) (Fig. 2). The median area under the MAP ≤ 60 mmHg was 0 (0–7) mmHg·min in the propofol group versus 0 (0–0) mmHg·min in the remimazolam besylate group (P < 0.001; Hodges-Lehmann estimated location shift 0 mmHg·min [95% CI 0 to 0]). For the threshold of MAP ≤ 50 mmHg, the median area under the curve was 0 (0–0) mmHg·min in both the propofol and remimazolam besylate groups (P = 0.0067; Hodges-Lehmann estimated location shift: 0 mmHg·min [95% CI 0–0]). ARV-MAP during induction in remimazolam besylate group was 8.44 (IQR 6.33–11.50) mmHg/min versus 12.33 (IQR 9.00–14.67) mmHg/min in propofol group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3b). No difference in ARV-MAP between the two groups was observed during maintenance (P = 0.83). The MAPdec during induction was 24.68 ± 10.74 mmHg in remimazolam besylate group versus 36.76 ± 12.38 mmHg in propofol group (P < 0.001). The TWA-MAP of the remimazolam besylate group was higher than that in the propofol group [82.45 (IQR 77.23–88.13) mmHg vs. 78.24 (IQR 73.27–83.79) mmHg, P < 0.001] (Table 2). Intraoperative anesthesia-related data

We proposed optimal titrated dose of remimazolam besylate was 0.16 (IQR 0.15–0.18) mg/kg and propofol was 1.44 (IQR 1.33–1.57) mg/kg. The time to LOC of remimazolam besylate was longer [1.5 (IQR 1.5–2.0) min vs. 1.0 (IQR 1.0–1.5) min, P < 0.001] but the recovery time was shorter with reversal of flumazenil [4.0 (IQR 3.0–6.0) min vs. 8.0 (IQR 6.0–9.0) min, P < 0.001] (Table 2). There was no difference in pre-and postoperative cognitive performance (P = 0.98). None of the patients in remimazolam besylate group developed respiratory depression versus 10.48% in propofol group (P < 0.001). Injection pain was reported in 49.52% of the propofol group versus 4.59% in remimazolam besylate group (P < 0.001). More details of adverse events are presented in Supplementary Table 3.

The BIS changes

The BIS changes during this trial were as follows (Fig. 3a). The BISLOC value of 67.85 ± 9.13 in remimazolam besylate group was higher than 55.99 ± 11.85 in propofol (P < 0.001). The mean BISmai values during anesthetic maintenance were 59.35 (IQR 53.15–65.11) in remimazolam besylate group and 55.13 (IQR 51.72–61.75) in propofol group, respectively (P < 0.001).

Discussion

We compared two protocols for anesthetic management in geriatric patients ≥ 65 years and concluded that remimazolam besylate had a superior hemodynamic profile compared with propofol. Based on efficacy and safety for bronchoscopy under general anesthesia, induced titration of remimazolam besylate was optimal at a dose of 0.16 mg/kg for this population. The exploratory outcome showed the mean BIS value was higher in remimazolam besylate group.

Hypotension frequently occurred during anesthesia induction due to the drugs peak effect for administration of bolus doses. The results indicated that titrated induction with remimazolam besylate had a significantly lower incidence of hypotension than propofol. Previous studies have drawn similar conclusions [18, 19]. Indeed, the incidence of hypotension depends on the selected definition for different goals [20]. In this study, hypotension was defined as a decrease of MAP ≥ 30% from baseline. While baseline BP, vascular autoregulation, and comorbidities varied considerably among patients, individualized BP targets using a relative threshold were chosen in this study [20]. Elderly patients with chronic hypertension were more sensitive to BP fluctuations and required a higher target at perioperative BP [20]. A MAP decrease of ≥ 30% from baseline significantly increased the risk of perioperative stroke and neurocognitive disorders [21, 22]. We focused on the result that remimazolam besylate (versus propofol) could reduce the risk of hypotension to improve severe outcomes. Our study found that the incidence of hypotension during maintenance was higher than that during induction. An operation may cause a cough. However, once the operation suddenly becomes gentle or interrupted, airway irritation disappears and the corresponding sedative effect continues. As a tertiary teaching hospital undertaking educational tasks, all procedures were conducted under the guidance of mid-level physicians, ensuring that no differences in the qualifications of physicians between the two groups. A shared airway surgery poses a significant challenge to anesthetic management. Surgical experience and team collaboration are critical to a successful outcome.

Moreover, we studied blood pressure variability (BPV), attempting to achieve a novel goal to provide the potential beneficial role of BP management. BPV increases with aging and even brief periods of high BPV may result in cardiovascular events [23, 24]. Various measurements of BPV have been proposed. ARV accounts for the sequence in BP measurements and was a powerful index to reflect short-interval, consecutive BP changes [16, 23]. The ARV of remimazolam besylate during induction was smaller than that of propofol. Our study first investigated that remimazolam besylate had the superiority for reducing hemodynamic fluctuations and thus achieved a smooth induction. However, there was no difference in ARV-MAP between the two groups. Indeed, continuous infusions of drugs brought more stable hemodynamics than bolus administration.

An absolute MAP was also calculated considering that short–term intraoperative MAP < 60–70 mmHg was associated with damage to vital organs such as myocardial injury, and acute kidney injury [25]. TWA-MAP featuring time changes is more accurate than a simple average. The higher TWA-MAP of remimazolam besylate could optimize organ perfusion to some extent.

Our study calculated that the titrated induction dose of remimazolam besylate to LOC was 0.16 mg/kg for patients aged over 65 years. A study proposed optimal doses of 0.19–0.25 mg/kg in patients aged 60–80 years, which was consistent with our results [26]. Elderly patients may require personalized administration because of aging-related changes in the pharmacological effects of anesthetic agents. This titration regime reduced the consumption of anesthetics, minimized side effects, and enhanced patients’ recovery.

Our study indicated that remimazolam besylate produced higher BIS values at equivalent sedation levels compared with propofol. When the MOAA/S score was ≤ 1, average BIS values exceeded 60 and were generally below 60 during the maintenance. We used the MOAA/S and PRST score, the widely accepted and reliable tools, to assess sedation level. Propofol is well known to decrease BIS index values but the correlation between the BIS index and remimazolam besylate was still uncertain [27]. We reported that remimazolam showed a weak correlation with BIS31. Based on the original finding, remimazolam potentiates β-wave activity in the electroencephalogram, resulting in artificially elevated BIS values; consequently, BIS lacks sufficient reliability as a standalone metric for sedation depth assessment during remimazolam anesthesia, necessitating complementary evaluation through unprocessed EEG parameters or pupillary diameter [28, 29]. Therefore, we emphasized caution when relying solely on BIS to monitor sedation with remimazolam, suggesting the potential benefit of complementary monitoring approaches for this anesthetic. This finding underscores the need for further research on remimazolam's pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, particularly across different patient populations.

There were some limitations in our study. Firstly, since the trial was a single-center study, further multi-institutional, prospective research is needed. Secondly, the administration of remifentanil contributed to hypotension. Although the dosage was similar between groups, the hemodynamic effects of opioids represent a potential confounding factor that could have influenced the blood pressure profile;thirdly, although the incidence of hypotension was observed, there was no focus on the long-term outcomes of those patients who experienced severe hypotension.

Conclusion

The ideal sedatives for bronchoscopy should have a rapid onset, short duration of action, quick recovery, and a safety profile, and thus remimazolam besylate has the potential due to its efficacy and safety.
