Information Asymmetry Across Language Varieties: A Case Study on Cantonese-Mandarin and Bavarian-German QA


Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are becoming a common way for humans to seek knowledge, yet their coverage and reliability vary widely. Especially for local language varieties , there are large asymmetries, e.g., information in local Wikipedia that is absent from the standard variant. However, little is known about how well LLMs perform under such information asymmetry , especially on closely related languages. We manually construct a novel challenge question-answering (QA) dataset that captures knowledge conveyed on a local Wikipedia page, which is absent from their higher-resource counterparts—covering Mandarin Chinese vs. Cantonese and German vs. Bavarian. Our experiments show that LLMs fail to answer questions about information only in local editions of Wikipedia. Providing context from lead sections substantially improves performance, with further gains possible via translation. Our topical, geographic annotations, and stratified evaluations reveal the usefulness of local Wikipedia editions as sources of both regional and global information. These findings raise critical questions about inclusivity and cultural coverage of LLMs. Keywords: question answering, language varieties, information asymmetry, large language models

Large Language Models (LLMs) are becoming a commonly used channel through which humans find and learn new information

Another information source is Wikipedia,

Till now, far less is understood about how LLMs handle facts relevant to specific cultural or linguistic communities that are less visible in global discourse
(
32

Cross-lingual retrieval for information-seeking questions remains challenging for LLMs.
Answer retrieval during post-training remains the main bottleneck even when provided with contextual information
(
16

.
Such challenges are particularly pronounced in open-book question answering (QA), where additional external information is provided, in contrast to closed-book QA, which relies on knowledge already in the model’s training
(
47
)
.
LLMs also struggle to share knowledge across languages, i.e., when the question is proposed in a language different from the context language
(
17
)
.

In this work, we address cross-lingual context retrieval on regional knowledge.
Specifically, we focus on how well LLMs can answer questions drawn from knowledge in local-language Wikipedia pages but which are absent from standard-language pages.
This is similar to the
Information Asymmetry
analyzed in
39
;
40
.
However, rather than focusing on the number of articles covered and the length of articles in English vs. non-English Wikipedia versions, we frame it as a QA task, seeking local information that is absent from the standard Wikipedia editions.
This aligns with
3
, who observe that questions about entities from other cultures may lack answers in the questioner’s language due to cultural bias, and with the notion of a “content gap” introduced by
12
.

To address this gap, we conduct a case study of two pairs of higher-resource (
standard
) versus lower-resource (
local
) language varieties spoken in overlapping regions: Mandarin Chinese versus Cantonese, and German versus Bavarian.
These pairs allow us to probe whether LLMs capture regional facts beyond what is available in dominant, higher-resource variants.
We experiment with closed-book QA, i.e., relying on LLMs’ internal parametric knowledge, and open-book QA, i.e., explicitly providing the extracted local information.
We focus on Wikipedia lead sections, which provide concise yet systematically curated knowledge that are length-friendly to test LLMs’ capability in open-book QA.

We construct a new evaluation dataset,
WiLoVa-QA
, consisting of facts that appear in local lead sections but are absent in their standard counterparts. Our dataset includes 294 lead section QA pairs for Cantonese–Mandarin and 178 for Bavarian–German.
Furthermore, we annotate a smaller set of document-level QA pairs for which the information is extracted from the full local Wikipedia article beyond the local lead section, yet absent from the aligned standard Wikipedia page.
These document-level annotations introduce an additional evaluation scenario for seeking local information beyond the provided context.

Our experiments yield four key findings. First, LLMs consistently fail to answer questions derived from local Wikipedia lead sections in closed-book QA, suggesting that such knowledge is systematically underrepresented in models.
Second, providing lead sections from local Wikipedia editions as context substantially improves performance, indicating that LLMs can reason over that knowledge when it is explicitly supplied. Translating these lead sections into the standard variant, i.e., from Cantonese to Mandarin, further boosts performance, highlighting the importance of context language for knowledge integration.
Third, we evaluate on a cross-lingual dataset, ECLeKTic
(
17
)
, and demonstrate that QA performance on local language varieties still lags behind when compared to seeking information in widely spoken languages.
Lastly, we manually categorize
WiLoVa-QA
articles to assess their topic and regional relevance and their effects on model performance.
Better performance is achieved on articles discussing more regional topics than on others.
Our results corroborate earlier findings
(
39
;
40
;
43
)
that local Wikipedia editions are not only useful as sources of regional knowledge but can also provide additional information on more region-independent topics.
We release our dataset and code to facilitate future research on cultural representation and coverage in LLMs.

3.1.  Target Language Varieties

We focus on two
local
versus
standard
language pairs:
3
3

3

For the sake of simplicity, we use the terms ‘local’ vs. ‘standard’.
However, this terminology is somewhat flawed, as Cantonese is arguably a standardized language, especially in Hong Kong. However, speaker attitudes towards the status of Cantonese as a language (relative to Mandarin) vary greatly

.
Cantonese (ISO 639-3:
yue
) vs. Mandarin Chinese (
cmn
) and Bavarian (
bar
) vs. German (
deu
).
Both pairs are spoken in overlapping regions, where the standard language prevails in formal communication, while the local variety serves as a marker of regional and cultural identity.

Cantonese is a Sinitic language spoken primarily in the Guangdong province, Hong Kong, and Macau, as well as in diaspora communities outside of China.
Bavarian is a group of non-standardized Upper German dialects spoken in southeastern Germany (Bavaria), most of Austria, and in northern Italy (South Tyrol).
Though Cantonese and Bavarian have over 10 million speakers and are among the top 100 most spoken languages
(
13
)
, their digital presence is relatively sparse.

23
labeled Cantonese and Bavarian as “1 - The Scraping-Bys” (i.e., with some amount of unlabeled data) in a 0-5 scale for language resource richness (0 the rarest and 5 the richest), whereas Mandarin and German are both “5 - The Winners” (i.e., quintessential resource-rich languages).
For example, there are 10 times more pages on the Mandarin Chinese Wikipedia (1.5M) than on the Cantonese Wikipedia (146K), and 100 times more on the German Wikipedia (3.0M) than on the Bavarian Wikipedia (27K).
Though underrepresented, these local pages still provide information that does not appear in standard variety pages, as shown in Table
1
.
This asymmetry makes Cantonese and Bavarian instructive test cases for studying how knowledge in regional Wikipedia editions is represented or omitted in LLMs.

3.2.  Preprocessing Wikipedia Pages

Aligning Wikipedia pages

We follow
30
to extract and align Wikipedia pages from both the local and standard varieties.
We obtain 111.6K and 24.6K aligned Wikipedia pages for Cantonese-Mandarin (
yue
-
cmn
) and Bavarian-German (

-

Local-heavy filtering

Since our study seeks to manually annotate a small portion of information uniquely present on local pages, we further filter to local-heavy pairs.
Namely, we only keep Wikipedia pairs for which both the
lead section
(typically the introductory paragraphs)
4
4

4

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Lead_section
and the whole document in the local language edition are longer than those of the corresponding page in the standard language.
This resembles the

We used the Wikipedia API

After filtering, only 5.38% (6.2K/111.6K) and 4.07% (1.0K/24.6K) of

This simple filtering has some limitations.
Our preprocessing and annotation only ensure that the information on the local-language page is absent from the
paired

It remains unknown whether such information is attested on other Wikipedia pages of the standard language, in resources outside of Wikipedia, or even in other languages beyond our study.
Although other external sources of information may exist, our experiments and results clearly show that such information from these local Wikipedia pages remains difficult for LLMs to recall.

Removing direct translations and structural mismatches

Before our annotations, we noticed that some page pairs are not ideal for our information asymmetry annotation, even after our local-heavy filtering.
These include cases that are (i)
translated
, if the local page seems to be a direct translation of the standard page (i.e., containing almost identical information and having a similar structure), regardless of whether the translated local variety sounds natural or superficial, and (ii)

structural mismatch
if either the local page or the standard page is a disambiguation page or a page without a lead section, lacking sufficient content for our information-asymmetry annotation.
We ask annotators to exclude these two cases from QA annotation.
We found that more than half of inspected
bar
-
deu

yue
-
cmn
pages.
The proportion of structural mismatch pages is also higher in
bar
-
deu
(28.6%) than
yue

cmn
(10.5%).
As a result, only 18.9% (94/497) of
bar
-
deu
pairs contain distinctive content in the local page for QA annotation; comparatively, more than half of
yue
-
cmn
pairs, 59.8% (137/229), deliver such information.

3.3.  Question-Answering Annotation

We frame our local-heavy information-asymmetry annotation as a question-answering (QA) task.
For each filtered Wikipedia pair, we ask annotators to compare both editions and identify information present only on the local page but absent from the standard.
137
yue
-
cmn
and 94
bar
-
deu
article pairs were annotated.

We conduct QA annotations at two levels.
The main contributions are the
lead section
annotations.
Wikipedia lead sections typically range from a few dozen to a few hundred tokens (much shorter than full Wikipedia documents), making it feasible for annotators to compare pages from two language varieties and extract information present only in the local one.
Because our study evaluates LLMs on answering such locally specific information—and compares performance with and without reference contexts in the prompt—using concise lead sections also keeps the experiments tractable.
We additionally annotate a smaller number of
document-level
QAs to assess LLMs’ capability to seek out-of-context information, which could be useful for shortcut learning.

Lead section QA annotations

For each pair of lead sections, we ask annotators to identify 2-3 pieces of information that are present only on the local side and missing on the standard side.
We follow
33
in encouraging annotators to pursue their natural curiosity in discovering information asymmetry between versions.
Annotators are instructed to (i) formulate a
question
in the standard language that addresses the information, (ii)
answer
the question in both the standard language and the local variety, and (iii)
highlight
texts on the local section from which the answer was drawn.
We ask annotators to create questions that (a) are as diverse as possible, (b) can be answered with a short phrase, and (c) make sense as stand-alone questions.
For example, we ask annotators to avoid frequently composing questions that ask for the year/date of an event or require multi-hop reasoning.

WiLoVa-QA
includes 294
yue
-
cmn
and 178
bar
-
deu
lead section QA annotations.

Verifying information asymmetry in the full document

For each lead section QA annotation, we ask annotators to verify whether the answer can be found in the full document of the standard page via a quick string search.
If an answer is found, the supporting sentence(s) are copied into our annotation.
Among 294
yue
-
cmn
and 178
bar
-
deu
lead section QA annotations, the majority, 200 (68.0%) and 100 (56.2%),
cannot
be answered even using the full content of the standard page.

Document-level QA annotations

We supplement the
WiLoVa-QA
dataset with 80 and 46 document-level QA annotations for
yue
-
cmn
and
bar
-
deu
.
We ask annotators to skim through the whole Wikipedia page of the local and standard language varieties and find one piece of information from the local page that is (a) not on the standard page and (b) not already annotated at the lead section level.
Document-level QA annotation is conducted only when some additional information from the local page can be observed within 10-15 minutes; thus, it is not annotated on every Wikipedia page pair.

Annotators

We recruited two master’s students in computational linguistics, one fluent in Cantonese and Mandarin and one fluent in Bavarian and German.
Two annotators were hired and compensated at national salary rates for 50 hours of annotation per language.
Both annotators report that annotating each lead-section QA pair takes 5-10 minutes and that annotating each document-level QA takes 10-15 minutes, mostly due to reading the lead section and skimming both pages of the document.
Because a larger portion of
bar
–
deu
pages were translations or structurally mismatched, more documents had to be screened and discarded, resulting in fewer QA annotations than for
yue
–
cmn
.

Quality Control

Since the annotators were asked to extract a few pieces of information they found prominent from paired Wikipedia pages, their QA annotations remain non-exhaustive and open-ended.
This makes traditional overlap-based inter-annotator agreement (IAA) inappropriate to evaluate the quality of
WiLoVa-QA
.

We follow
33
and ask two project coordinators, one native Mandarin speaker and one native German, to review the elicited questions and answers that are written in Mandarin or in German.
Our quality check includes three aspects: (a)
grammaticality
: if any of the
question
or
answer
is ungrammatical or non-fluent;
(b)
stand-alone validity
: if a
question
cannot function as an independent, context-free information-seeking question;
and (c)
answer appropriateness
: if an answer does not directly and fully address its paired question when considered in isolation.
Only a few questions and answers were flagged and rewritten on the entire dataset, demonstrating the overall high quality of our original QA annotations.

Datasets

We use our lead section and document QA annotations to evaluate LLMs’ ability to answer information-seeking questions.
We also evaluate on ECLeKTic

Unlike our manual QA annotations, the ECLeKTic dataset generated knowledge-seeking questions in 12 source languages using Gemini

Questions and answers are translated into the other 11 languages to evaluate LLMs in cross-lingual knowledge transfer.
To compare with our

It is also worth noting that the extracted context in ECLeKTic includes the first 10 sentences of the Wikipedia pages, which differ slightly from the lead sections in

Metrics

We use standard evaluation metrics for Natural Language Generation (NLG) tasks to measure the similarity between the predicted and reference answers at the lexical, character, and semantic levels.
ROUGE-L
(
28
)
evaluates lexical overlap of the longest common subsequence, chrF++
(
35
)
captures character-level similarity that is robust across languages and morphological variants, and BERTScore
(
49
)
measures token-level semantic similarity using contextualized embeddings from pretrained language models. We report F1 for ROUGE-L and BERTScore.

To complement traditional NLG evaluation metrics, we also employ LLM-as-a-judge (LLMaJ,
10
;
51
).
The judge model (gpt-oss-20b) takes the question, the reference answer, and the generated answer as input and determines whether the generated answer is correct.
We adapt the evaluation prompt from
17
, which is provided in Appendix
C
.
To verify the reliability and consistency of LLMaJ as an evaluation method, we manually inspected 50 QA instances evaluated by LLMaJ and found only one misjudgment (see Appendix
D
).

6.  Conclusion

This paper introduces
WiLoVa-QA
, a Wikipedia-based question-answering dataset that addresses information asymmetry across language varieties.
Through experiments on Cantonese–Mandarin and Bavarian–German, we reveal that current LLMs often fail to recall local knowledge in closed-book settings, but can retrieve it effectively once the relevant local context is provided—highlighting a clear information asymmetry between high- and low-resource varieties.
The findings suggest that knowledge gaps in LLMs parallel those observed on the web, where regional content remains underrepresented.
Beyond QA,
WiLoVa-QA
offers a foundation for studying cross-variety understanding, cultural bias, and regional knowledge access in multilingual LLMs.
