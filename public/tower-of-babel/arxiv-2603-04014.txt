Non-Derivability Results in Polymorphic Dependent Type Theory
Herman Geuvers

Abstract
In the pure Calculus of Constructions (CC) one can define data types and function over these, and there is a powerful higher order logic to reason over these functions and data types [ 11 , 6 ] . This is due to the combination of impredicativity and dependent types, and most of these features can already be observed in polymorphic (second order) dependent type theory λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 . The impredicative encoding of data types (in λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 or CC) is powerful but not fully satisfactory: for example, the induction principle is not provable. As a matter of fact, it can be shown that induction is not provable for whatever possible representation of data types [ 13 ] . In [ 2 ] , Awodey, Frey and Speight show that in an extension of λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 with a Σ \Sigma -types, identity types with uniqueness of identity proofs and function extensionality, it is possible to define data types for which the induction principle is provable. In [ 9 ] it has been shown that in this extension of λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 , also quotient types can be defined with the proper induction principle, and, using quotient types, coinductive types can be defined with the proper coinduction principle. This leaves various questions open: Are quotient types with induction principle not definable in the original λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 ? And how about coinduction types, is it impossible to get a strong coinduction principle in λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 ? Looking at it from the other side: which of the extensions used in [ 2 ] are really needed to make induction and coinduction work? In this paper, we contribute partial answers to these questions: parametric quotient types are not definable in λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 and the well-known definable stream type does not have a coinduction principle. For the latter question we show that, if we just extend λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 with Σ \Sigma -types and identity types with uniqueness of identity proofs, we still cannot prove an induction principle for the natural numbers. So function extensionality is crucial in making induction provable. We show these results by studying models of λ ​ P ​ 2 \lambda P2 where the types representing these principles are empty, so these models act as counter models to the derivability of the principles. Most of the well-studied models for polymorphic type theory are parametric or in any case quite extensional [ 3 , 22 , 15 ] , so in these models, induction and coinduction just hold. An interesting exception is work by Berardi and co-authors [ 7 , 4 , 8 ] , who describe non-parametric models of (non-dependent) polymorphic type theory. The models we study here are of a more syntactic nature and have been described and studied in [ 13 , 12 ] . They can be seen as a collection of term models where specific choices can be made to construct counter models. 1 1 1 Acknowledgments: Thanks to the referees for their useful and constructive comments.

3
Model construction for
\lambda P2

The model notion for
\lambda P2
we use is the same as in
[

,
12
]

extended to a class of models for the Calculus of Constructions, which

[
24

Our models are a kind of realizability semantics in the sense that types will be interpreted as sets of realizers, where the realizers are taken from a combinatory algebra. The terms will be interpreted as the realizers. To cater for dependent types, our models are built from
weakly extensional
combinatory algebras
(WECA). A

(CA for short) is a tuple

{\bf A}
a set,

\cdot

(as usual

{\cal A}
a combinatory algebra, the
set of
terms over
{\cal A}

{\cal T}({\cal A})
contain infinitely many variables
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots
and
distinct elements

for every
a\in{\bf A}
, and letting
{\cal T}({\cal A})

be closed under application (the operation
\cdot
). Given a
term
t
and a valuation
\rho

variables to elements of
{\bf A}

interpretation of

in

{\bf A}
under
\rho

\llbracket{t}\rrbracket^{{\cal A}}_{\rho}

\llbracket{t_{1}}\rrbracket^{{\cal A}}_{\rho(x:=a)}=\llbracket{t_{2}}\rrbracket^{{\cal A}}_{\rho(x:=a)}

We now extend the polyset model of Definition

Definition 5.1
.

We define
{\Lambda}{{\sf id}}
by extending the untyped
\lambda
-calculus with two constants
{\sf J}
and
{\sf refl}
with the following reduction rules.

\begin{array}[]{rclccrcl}{\sf J}\ c\ a\ b\ {\sf refl}&\longrightarrow_{J}&c\ a&&{\sf refl}\ q&\longrightarrow_{{\sf refl}}&{\sf refl},\\
\pi_{1}\ \langle a,b\rangle&\longrightarrow_{\Sigma}&a&&\pi_{1}\ {\sf refl}&\longrightarrow_{{\sf refl}}&{\sf refl},\\
\pi_{2}\ \langle a,b\rangle&\longrightarrow_{\Sigma}&b&&\pi_{2}\ {\sf refl}&\longrightarrow_{{\sf refl}}&{\sf refl}.\end{array}

The rule for

represents the reduction rule for identity types that combines the
{\sf J}
eliminator and the
{\sf refl}
constructor, the rules for
\longrightarrow_{\Sigma}
represent the
\beta
-rules for
\Sigma
-types.
The rules for
\longrightarrow_{{\sf refl}}
does not occur in a typed setting, as
{\sf refl}\ M
and
\pi_{1}\ {\sf refl}
are never well-typed, but it is harmless, in the sense that it doesn’t spoil the Church-Rosser property (see
[
19
,
17
]
) and it allows us to define an interesting model.

The extension of the interpretation to
\Sigma
-types can be done by
extending the notion of polyset-structure (Definition

3.2
) to incorporate dependent sums in a straightforward

{\Lambda}{{\sf id}}
and the the

{\cal P}^{{\Lambda}{{\sf id}}}(\{{\sf refl}\})
. So we will define the

\Sigma
-types into
{\cal P}^{{\Lambda}{{\sf id}}}(\{{\sf refl}\})

Definition 5.2
.

We take

to be

\Sigma_{t\in X}.F(t):=\{q\mid\pi_{1}\ q\in X\wedge\pi_{2}\ q\in F(\pi_{1}\ q)\}.

6
Conclusion and Discussion

We have shown a number of
non-derivability results
in
polymorphic dependent type theory
\lambda P2
. We believe that the proofs
go through without change if we replace
\lambda P2
with the pure
Calculus of Constructions CC.

In Section
4
we have looked at quotient types and coinductive types, in particular the data type of streams.
It would be interesting to generalize these results in the following directions.

•

To prove that there exists no stream type in
\lambda P2
with a
coinduction principle. Our Lemma
4.3
just shows that
the well-known definable streams don’t have coinduction principle,
but maybe one can give a smarter encoding of streams.

•

Theorem
4.4
proves that parametric
quotients don’t exists. On the other hand, Example

2.8
gives a concrete example of a
non-parametric quotient, but there the relation is really trivial:
just the identity itself. Are there other non-parametric quotients
(besides the trivial one)? Taking
R\ x\ y:=\top
we can define
a
{\sf cls}
function and
\widehat{f}
, but
{\sf IndQuot}
is already
unclear. In
[
21
]
it has been studied which quotient types
are definable and which ones are not. But this is in predicative
type theory with inductive types, which has more expressive power.

•

With functional extensionality, the definable quotient is
parametric, as shown in
[
9
]
. But to construct a
strong quotient type, we add
\Sigma
-types, identity types and

UIP
. Can one prove that there are no strong quotients in
\lambda P2+{\sf FunExt}
?

In Section
5
