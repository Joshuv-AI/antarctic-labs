Global Strichartz Estimates for Wave Equations with time-dependent structured Lipschitz coefficients Thanks: Funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – Project-ID 258734477 – SFB 1173
Dorothee Frey, Yonas Mesfun

Abstract
. We establish global-in-time Strichartz estimates without loss of derivatives for wave equations with time-dependent Lipschitz coefficients, which satisfy an additional structural assumption. Our approach is based on a parametrix construction through the Phillips functional calculus. We furthermore obtain the well-posedness of such wave equations with Lipschitz coefficients in H 1 H^{1} .



1.
Introduction and Main Results

In this article, we prove global-in-time Strichartz estimates for variable-coefficient wave equations with time-dependent Lipschitz-regular coefficients under a structural condition.
More precisely, we consider wave equations in space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}=\{(t,x)\colon t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
with spatial dimension
d\geq 2
, which are of the form

(1.1)

(D_{t}^{2}-P(t,x,D_{x}))u(t,x)=F(t,x),\quad u(0,x)=g(x),\quad D_{t}u(0,x)=h(x).

Here,
D_{t}\coloneqq-\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}
,
D_{x}\coloneqq(D_{x_{1}},\dots,D_{x_{d}})\coloneqq-\mathrm{i}\nabla_{x}
and
P(t,x,D_{x})
is an elliptic second order differential operator of the form

P(t,x,D_{x})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{x_{j}}c_{j}(t,x)D_{x_{j}}\quad\text{or}\quad P(t,x,D_{x})\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}(t,x)D_{x_{j}}^{2}.

The coefficients
c_{1},\dots,c_{d}
are merely assumed to be Lipschitz-regular and to satisfy some structural and an asymptotic flatness condition (see Assumption
1.1
below).

Strichartz estimates control space-time
\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}
-norms of the solution of a linear dispersive equation in terms of (
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-based) Sobolev norms of its initial data. These estimates turn out to be particularly useful in establishing the well-posedness of nonlinear dispersive equations (see e.g.
[
36
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
3
]
for an account of nonlinear wave equations). For a Schwartz solution
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the classical wave equation in spatial dimension
d\geq 2
(which corresponds to
c_{j}=1
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, i.e.,
P=-\Delta_{x}
in (
1.1
)),

(1.2)

(D_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{x})u=F\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad u(0,\cdot)=g,\quad D_{t}u(0,\cdot)=h,

they read

(1.3)

\||D_{x}|^{1-\alpha}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\||D_{x}|^{\tilde{\alpha}}F\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
|D_{x}|^{\beta}
is the Fourier multiplier operator associated with the symbol
|\xi|^{\beta}
for
\beta\in\{1-\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}\}
and
\|g\|_{\dot{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneqq\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
denotes the homogeneous Sobolev norm. The integrability exponents
\tilde{p}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{q}^{\prime}
in (
1.3
) are the Hölder conjugate exponents of
\tilde{p}
and
\tilde{q}
, respectively, and the triples
(p,q,\alpha),(\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{\alpha})\in[0,\infty]^{3}
satisfy the admissibility conditions

(1.4)

2\leq p\leq\infty,\,2\leq q<\infty,\quad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{d}{q}=\frac{d}{2}-\alpha,\quad\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d-1}{q}\leq\frac{d-1}{2}.

Triples satisfying (
1.4
) are called
(wave-admissible) Strichartz triples
and they are referred to as
strict
if the fourth condition in (
1.4
) holds with an equality. Time-translation invariance, scaling symmetry and the Knapp example show that the conditions in (
1.4
) are in fact necessary for (
1.3
) to hold (see
[
39
]
). We preclude
q=\infty
in (
1.4
) because in general, (
1.3
) fails to hold in this case (see
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
).
Observe that strict Strichartz triples with
p=\infty
, i.e.,
(p,q,\alpha)=(\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{\alpha})=(\infty,2,0)
correspond to the well-known energy inequality (see e.g.
[
36
, p.13]
). At the other extreme, strict Strichartz triples with
p=2
, i.e.,
(2,\frac{2(d-1)}{d-3},\frac{d+1}{2(d-1)})
for
d\geq 4
, are called
endpoint
Strichartz triples. Strichartz estimates for non-strict Strichartz triples follow from those for strict ones by Sobolev embedding.

Strichartz estimates for the wave equation have a long history. In his seminal work, Strichartz
[
38
]
proved (
1.3
) in the case
p=q
by means of Fourier restriction. Later, Ginibre–Velo
[
14
]
and Lindblad–Sogge
[
26
]
independently established (
1.3
) for non-endpoint Strichartz triples. Finally, Keel–Tao
[
23
]
developed an abstract approach to Strichartz inequalities, thereby settling the subtle endpoint case.

The primary goal of this article is to obtain sharp global-in-time Strichartz estimates for wave equations as in (
1.1
), where the coefficients
c_{j}
are merely Lipschitz continuous. To this end, we impose the following assumptions on the coefficients
c_{j}
.

Assumption 1.1
.

We have

(1.5)

c_{j}(t,x)\coloneqq b_{j}(t)a_{j}(x_{j}),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\,j\in\{1,\dots,d\},

where
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{d}
are Lipschitz functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
with the following properties.

\bullet

There exist constants
0<m_{1}\leq m_{2}<\infty
such that

(1.6)

m_{1}\leq a_{j}(x)\leq m_{2}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }j\in\{1,\dots,d\}.

Moreover, we assume that

(1.7)

m_{3}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\big\|\tfrac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}x}\log(a_{j})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}<4.

\bullet

For some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, we have

(1.8)

1-\varepsilon_{0}\leq b_{j}(t)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }j\in\{1,\dots,d\}.

We set
m_{4}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\|b_{j}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty
. Moreover, we assume that there exists some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(m_{1},m_{2},m_{4})>0
such that

(1.9)

\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\|b_{j}^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Thus,
P(t,x,D_{x})
is of the form

(1.10)

P(t,x,D_{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)D_{x_{j}}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}\quad\text{or}\quad P(t,x,D_{x})=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}^{2}.

Due to the low regularity of the coefficients, it is a priori not clear if (
1.1
) admits a distributional solution even for very regular initial data (cf. for instance
[
6
, Theorem 10]
in the case of Hölder continuous coefficients). Therefore, in a first step, we address the well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the latter denoting the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-based (inhomogeneous) Sobolev space of order
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. To this end, we view (
1.1
) as a vector-valued Cauchy problem in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
. It is convenient to write
P(t)
instead of
P(t,x,D_{x})
, and we think of
P\coloneqq(P(t))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
as a family of differential operators acting on functions of
x
. We use the following notion of a weak solution.

Definition 1.2

(Weak Solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose that
g\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha},h\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}
and
F\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1})
. Then, a function
u\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1})\cap W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2})
is called a
weak solution
of (
1.1
) (in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
) if

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} D_{t}^{2}u(t)&=P(t)u(t)+F(t)&&\text{in }\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}\quad\text{for a.e. $t\in\mathbb{R}$},\\
u(0)&=g,\\
D_{t}u(0)&=h.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, we obtain the following well-posedness result.

Theorem 1.3

(Well-posedness in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in[-1,2]
and suppose that
g\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
,
h\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}
and
F\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1})
. Then, there exists a unique weak solution
u
to (
1.1
). Moreover, for each bounded interval
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
0\in J
, the map

\Phi\colon\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}\times\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}\times\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1})\to C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}),\quad(g,h,F)\mapsto u

is Lipschitz continuous.

We note that for Theorem
1.3
we do not need the smallness of
\varepsilon_{0}
as stated in (
1.8
). In fact, it is enough that the coefficients
b_{j}
are just bounded from above and below by positive constants, see Remark
4.4
below.

For the weak solution from Theorem
1.3
, we are able to derive a representation formula, see Theorem
4.2
below. This representation then allows us to prove global-in-time Strichartz estimates, which is our main result:

Theorem 1.4

(Global-In-Time Strichartz Estimates for Weak Solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{1}
)
.

Let
(p,q,\alpha)
be a wave-admissible Strichartz triple and
\alpha\in[0,2]
. Suppose that
g\in\mathrm{H}^{1}
,
h\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
and
F\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{2})
. Then, the weak solution to the wave equation (
1.1
) satisfies the global-in-time Strichartz estimate

(1.11)

\||D_{x}|^{1-\alpha}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}+\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{2})}.

In
[
13
, Theorem 2]
, Schippa and the first author obtained Theorem
1.4
in the special case where
P
as in (
1.10
) is time-independent, i.e.,
b_{j}=1
for all
j\in\{1\dots,d\}
. Theorem
1.4
extends this result to the case of time-dependent coefficients of multiplicative form (
1.10
).

One striking feature of Theorem
1.4
is the mere Lipschitz regularity of the coefficients. Indeed, Strichartz estimates for wave equations with low-regularity coefficients have been intensively studied in the literature, leading to the insight that
C^{2}
- or at least
C^{1,1}
-regularity of the coefficients is the minimal requirement for Strichartz estimates to be valid in general. Indeed, for more general wave equations

(1.12)

(D_{t}^{2}-\tilde{P}(t,x,D_{x}))u(t,x)=F(t,x),\quad u(0,x)=g(x),\quad D_{t}u(0,x)=h(x),

in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with elliptic differential operator
\tilde{P}(t,x,D_{x})=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}D_{x_{j}}c_{jk}(t,x)D_{x_{k}}
, Smith
[
34
]
first obtained local(-in-time)

1.3
) holds with

replaced by
\||D_{x}|^{1-\alpha}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(I;\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
or
\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{1-\alpha}u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(I;\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
for some finite interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
containing

.
Strichartz estimates in spatial dimensions
d=2,3
in the homogeneous case (
F=0
), provided that
c_{jk}\in C^{1,1}
. Later, Tataru
[
40
]
established local Strichartz estimates under a
C^{2}
-assumption in all dimensions
d\geq 2
, and Metcalfe–Tataru
[
31
]
proved that these estimates are global-in-time under an additional asymptotic flatness condition. For
C^{r}
-coefficients with
r\in[0,2)
, Strichartz estimates hold true with loss of derivatives
[
40
]
, in which case the regularity parameter
\alpha
of the Strichartz triple
(p,q,\alpha)
in (

) has to be replaced by
\alpha+\frac{\sigma_{r}}{p}
with
\sigma_{r}\coloneqq\frac{2-r}{2+r}
. Moreover, the counterexamples constructed in
[

]
show that this loss is generally unavoidable. In light of these results, Theorem
1.4
shows that for the wave equations under consideration in this article, Strichartz estimates in fact do hold without loss of derivatives. The reason for this essentially lies in the crucial structural assumption (
1.5
), see also Subsection
1.1

1.1.
Methods

In our approach, we make use of the abstract result by Keel–Tao
[
23
]
, which states that Strichartz estimates follow from uniform
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-bounds and dispersive estimates for the solution operators to (
1.12
). However, one of the the main difficulties in our situation is to find a good enough approximation of the solution operator, i.e., a parametrix.
In case of the classical wave equation, the solution operator to (
1.2
) can be represented in terms of Fourier multiplier operators of the form

(1.13)

\big[\tilde{T}(t)f\big](x)\coloneqq\big[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t|D_{x}|}\psi(D_{x})f\big](x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(x\xi+t|\xi|)}\psi(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

with
0\neq\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported away from the origin. While in this case the uniform
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-bounds are an immediate consequence of Plancherel’s Theorem, the dispersive estimates are considerably more delicate. In order to derive them, we may rewrite (
1.13
) as a convolution according to

(1.14)

\big[\tilde{T}(t)f\big](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{K}_{t}(y)f(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{K}_{t}(y)(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot D_{x}}f)(x)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
\tilde{K}_{t}(y)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t|\cdot|}\psi)(y)

and
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot D_{x}})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
denotes the translation group acting on functions of
x
.
The crucial dispersive estimate for the operator
\tilde{T}(t)
,

(1.15)

\|\tilde{T}(t)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

then follows from the bound
|\tilde{K}_{t}(y)|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}
, which in turn follows from the theory of oscillatory integrals (see e.g.
[
37
, Chapter 8]
,
[
19
, Section 7.7]
).

For variable-coefficient wave equations,
one has to rely on more sophisticated tools from microlocal analysis, namely Fourier integral operators (FIOs) in the case of smooth coefficients (see
[
21
]
,
[
32
]
), or, in the case of coefficients with limited regularity, on phase space methods such as the FBI transform (see
[
40
]
,
[
41
]
). Those latter methods are not readily available if the regularity of the coefficients of
P
is lower than
C^{2}
.

The assumption that allows us to fall below the ’
C^{2}
-barrier’ is the structural assumption (
1.5
), which goes back to
[
12
]
on fixed-time
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-estimates for rough wave equations and which opens the door to a construction of a parametrix via tools from functional calculus. Indeed, (
1.5
) means that
P(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}
, where
L_{1},\dots,L_{d}
are commuting differential operators given by

L_{j}\coloneqq D_{x_{j}}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}\quad\text{or}\quad L_{j}\coloneqq a_{j}(x_{j})D^{2}_{x_{j}}.

It turns out that
\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}=\mathrm{i}(\sqrt{L_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{L_{d}})
is the generator of a
d
-parameter
C_{0}
-group
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
on
\mathrm{L}^{p}\coloneqq\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, and, in analogy to (
1.14
), we are able to define a parametrix for (
1.1
) in terms of operators of the form

(1.16)

T(t)f\coloneqq(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t}}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{t}(y)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}y

with a suitable one-homogeneous phase function
\varphi_{t}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and the kernel
K_{t}
given by
K_{t}(y)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t}}\psi)(y)\coloneqq
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(y\cdot\xi+\varphi_{t}(\xi))}\psi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi
.
Let us emphasize that this representation cannot be achieved through a simple bi-Lipschitz change of variables, see
[
12
, Remark 4.5]
.
The representation then allows to reduce the crucial dispersive estimate for
T(t)f
to a corresponding oscillatory integral estimate for
K_{t}
, namely

(1.17)

|K_{t}(y)|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}.

We deduce (
1.17
) from decay estimates for the Fourier transform of a surface-carried measure. This is where (
1.8
) enters the picture. Indeed, the smallness of
\varepsilon_{0}
as in (
1.8
) guarantees that the hypersurface
\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\varphi(\xi)=1\}
is a small perturbation of the unit sphere, and as such inherits its nonvanishing Gaussian curvature. An application of stationary phase then yields (
1.17
).

1.2.
Outline

We briefly describe the structure of this article. In Section
2
, we recall the operator-theoretic framework, which was introduced in
[
12
]
. In particular, we shortly review the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
and recall basic facts and tools of this calculus. In Section
3
, we use these tools in order to construct parametrices, i.e., approximate solution operators for (

). Following e.g.
[

]
(see also
[

. Finally, we prove in Section

1.4
. Our proof is based on the Keel–Tao argument

.

1.3.
Notation

Since the underlying first order operators are Dirac operators as in
[
12
]
(cf.
[
11
]
and the references therein), we define the second order elliptic operator
P(t)
from (
1.10
) as the matrix-valued operator

P(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)D_{x_{j}}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}&0\\
0&\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

and implicitly work with
\mathbb{C}^{2}
-valued functions. This allows to treat the divergence-form and standard-form versions of
P(t)
simultaneously.
To ease noation, we suppress the target space and write
\mathrm{L}^{p}\coloneqq\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
for
p\in[1,\infty]
,
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
, etc. We denote the Schwartz space by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the Fourier transform on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\mathcal{F}
, where we use the convention

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f(\xi)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\xi}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\xi}g(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

for
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The extension of
\mathcal{F}
onto
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the space of tempered distributions, is still denoted by
\mathcal{F}
. We set
\mathcal{F}\mathrm{L}^{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of complex-valued Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite variation norm; we put
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}}\coloneqq\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}
. For
\lambda>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set
\langle\lambda\rangle\coloneqq(1+\lambda^{2})^{1/2}
and
\langle\xi\rangle\coloneqq\langle|\xi|\rangle
, respectively. By a slight abuse of notation, we denote both the dual operator (in Banach spaces) and the adjoint operator (in Hilbert spaces) of
T
by
T^{\ast}
. We reserve the letter
\lambda>0
to denote either the size
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in frequency space or a scaling parameter in physical space. Finally,
A\lesssim_{M}B
means
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
depending on a set
M
of parameters, and
A\simeq_{M}B
denotes the relations
A\lesssim_{M}B
and
B\lesssim_{M}A
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some properties of the Phillips functional calculus used in this article. For more information on this calculus, we refer the reader to e.g.
[
16
, Section 3.3]
,
[
20
, Section 10.7]
.

2.1.
Functional Calculus

For each
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define the operator
L_{j,p}
in
\mathrm{L}^{p}=\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

\displaystyle L_{j,p}\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}D_{x_{j}}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}&0\\
0&a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}^{2}&\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\mathsf{Dom}(L_{j,p})\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}\mid D_{x_{j}}^{k}u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}\text{ for }k\in\{0,1,2\}\}.

The operators
L_{j,p}
are sectorial operators in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
by
[
29
, Proposition 1.1]
and
[
33
, Section 7.3, Theorem 3.2]
(the latter theorem is only formulated for bounded domains
\Omega
but can be modified for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see
[
30
, Proposition 2.2.2]
). By the holomorphic functional calculus for sectorial operators, the square roots
\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{j,p}}}
are well-defined and we write
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{p}}}\coloneqq(\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1,p}}},\dots,\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{d,p}}})

Proposition 2.1

(Generation of
d
-parameter
C_{0}
-Group)
.

Let

. Then, the operator
\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{p}}}

-group
\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{p}}}}\big)_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}

We set

Since

for

Definition 2.2

(Phillips Functional Calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
)
.

Let

. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}
, we define
\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p})

\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\colon\mathrm{L}^{p}\to\mathrm{L}^{p},\quad\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varphi}(y),

where

We will mostly work with

, for which (
2.1
) is just

(2.2)

\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi)(y)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}y\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}).

Proposition 2.3

(Properties of the Phillips Functional Calculus)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. The following statements hold true.

(a)

The map
\Phi\colon\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p}),\>\varphi\mapsto\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})
, defines a unit-preserving, bounded homomorphism of Banach algebras. Moreover,
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}te_{j}\cdot})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{j}}}}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

(b)

Let
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.Then, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, we have
\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\in\mathsf{Dom}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}^{\alpha})
and

(2.3)

\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}^{\alpha}\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\varphi_{\alpha}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,

where
\varphi_{\alpha}(\xi)\coloneqq\xi^{\alpha}\varphi(\xi)
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover,
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}^{\alpha}\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}^{\alpha}f
if

.

(c)

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}\mathrm{L}^{1}
. Then,

\|\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p})}\lesssim_{M_{p}}\|\varphi(D_{x})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p})},

where
\varphi(D_{x})\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}M_{\varphi}\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier multiplier operator associated with the symbol
\varphi
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M_{2}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{p})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M_{p},p,d}\sup_{|\alpha|\leq[d/2]+1}\big\|\,|\cdot|^{|\alpha|}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\varphi(\cdot)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}},\quad p\in(1,\infty).

Proof.

See
[
30
, Proposition 2.1.10]
. ∎

Remark 2.4
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}\mathrm{L}^{1}
is even in every component, i.e., if

\varphi(\xi)=\varphi^{(\mathrm{e})}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{d}}\sum_{(\delta_{j})_{j=1}^{d}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}\varphi(\delta_{1}\xi_{1},\dots,\delta_{d}\xi_{d})\quad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}),

then the same is true for
\rho\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
. In this case, we have by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{(\mathrm{e})}(y)\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y_{1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1}}}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y_{1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1}}}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y^{\prime}\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L^{\prime}}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{(\mathrm{e})}(y)\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y_{1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1}}}}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y_{1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1}}}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y^{\prime}\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L^{\prime}}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{(\mathrm{e})}(y)\mathrm{Cos}(y_{1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{1}}})\big)\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y^{\prime}\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L^{\prime}}}}}f\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\cdots=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{(\mathrm{e})}(y)\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(y)\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

with the
d
-parameter cosine function
\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{Cos}(y_{j}\sqrt{\smash[b]{L_{j}}})
.

Thus for such functions
\varphi
, we are free to replace
\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
by
\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})
in the integral which defines
\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})
. This observation turns out to be useful when combined with the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5

(
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x}\mathrm{L}^{1}_{y}
-Estimate)
.

Let

.

(a)

We have

\|\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{1}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}),

with an implicit constant depending on
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
from Assumption
1.1
.

(b)

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty})
with
\varphi=\varphi^{(\mathrm{e})}
, then

\|\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{1}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}).

Proof.

For a proof of (a), we refer the reader to
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
30
, Theorem 2.2.8 and Remark 2.2.9 (3)]
). To prove (b), let
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{\infty})
with

. Then, by Remark
2.4
, Hölder’s inequality and part (a), we have for

and a.e.

∎

2.2.
An Adapted Scale of Sobolev spaces

Fourier multiplier operators can be viewed as the Phillips functional calculus for
D_{x}
. These operators can be efficiently studied in the scale of
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
, using Plancherel’s Theorem and the characterization of these spaces within this calculus, i.e.,
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\colon\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\}

(\alpha\in\mathbb{R})
. As we replace the Fourier multiplier calculus (for
D_{x}
) by the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
, it is more convenient to replace the classical Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
by a scale of spaces which is better adapted to
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
. To this end, we consider the operator
L
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
defined by

(2.4)

L\coloneqq L_{1,2}+\cdots+L_{d,2}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{j=1}^{d}D_{x_{j}}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}&0\\
0&\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}(x_{j})D_{x_{j}}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

with domain
\mathrm{H}^{2}=\mathrm{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Note that
L
is self-adjoint with respect to the equivalent scalar product

(2.5)

\langle u,v\rangle_{A}\coloneqq\langle A^{-1}u,v\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}},\quad\text{with}\quad A=A(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&a(x)\end{pmatrix},\quad a(x)\coloneqq a_{1}(x_{1})\cdots a_{d}(x_{d}).

Moreover,
L
is injective with spectrum contained in
[0,\infty)
. In particular, the fractional powers
L^{\alpha}
are defined for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.
[
16
, Section 3.2]
). As in
[
24
, Appendix E]
, we may therefore use the following definition.

Definition 2.6

(The Spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the
L
-adapted (
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-based) Sobolev space of order
\alpha
by

(\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}})\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\mathsf{Dom}(L^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}),\|({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\|_{2}),&\alpha\geq 0,\\
(\mathrm{L}^{2},\|({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\|_{2})^{\sim},&\alpha<0.\end{cases}

Here,
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})^{\sim}
denotes the completion of a normed space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
. We also set
\mathrm{H}^{\infty}_{L}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
and
\mathrm{H}^{-\infty}_{L}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
.

Note that
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}\subseteq\mathrm{H}^{\beta}_{L}
for
-\infty\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq\infty
. To ease notation, we will frequently write
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}}
. The notation
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
is motivated by the observation that
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{-\Delta_{x}}=\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
It follows from
[
24
, Proposition 15.23]
that the fractional power
({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
extends to an operator
({\mathrm{Id}}+\mathscr{L})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
on
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{-\infty}
such that
({\mathrm{Id}}+\mathscr{L})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-\beta}_{L}
is a bounded isomorphism for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We write
\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\beta}\coloneqq({\mathrm{Id}}+\mathscr{L})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
. We need the following lemma to extend the Phillips functional calculus to the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
, at least for compactly supported smooth functions.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Let further
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then:

(a)

({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=(\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\alpha}\varphi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f
, and if
f\in\mathsf{Dom}(L^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})
, then
({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})({\mathrm{Id}}+L)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f
,

(b)

L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\psi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=(|\cdot|^{\beta}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f
. Moreover, if
f\in\mathsf{Dom}(L^{\frac{\beta}{2}})
, then
L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\psi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\psi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}f
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathrm{e}^{-tL}=G_{t}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})
, where
G_{t}(\xi)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-t|\xi|^{2}}
. Then, one may use subordination formulas such as
[
16
, Proposition 3.3.5]
to prove (a). Part (b) can be proved similarly.
∎

For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
, the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
extends to
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
by letting

(2.6)

\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\coloneqq(\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{-\alpha}\varphi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})g,\quad g\coloneqq\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha}f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}.

By Lemma
2.7
(a), this definition does not depend on
\alpha
and is therefore reasonable. In view of Lemma
2.7
(b) and (
2.6
), we see that

(2.7)

L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}\psi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=(|\cdot|^{\beta}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\quad(f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and all smooth
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported away from the origin. It follows similarly that

(2.8)

\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\beta}\varphi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=(\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle^{\beta}\varphi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\quad(f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We use the following result in order to switch from the
L
-adapted scale to the classical one.
The
L
-adapted
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-based homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{\beta,q}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as in
[
24
, Definition 15.21]
with
A
replaced by the closure of the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-part of
\sqrt{\smash[b]{L}}
and
\alpha
replaced by
\beta
therein.

Proposition 2.8
.

(a)

For
\alpha\in[-2,2],
we have
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}=\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
and

\|\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\simeq\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}u\|_{2}\quad(u\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}).

(b)

For all
\beta\in[-1,1]
and
q\in(1,\infty)
, we have
\dot{W}^{\beta,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\dot{W}^{\beta,q}_{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with equivalent norms
\|L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}u\|_{q}\simeq\||D_{x}|^{\beta}u\|_{q}
.

Proof.

Consider first the case
\alpha=2
. Then,
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{2}=\mathsf{Dom}(L)=\mathrm{H}^{2}
by definition. The estimate
\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}
is trivial (by the Lipschitz continuity of the
a_{j}
), and the reverse one then follows from the open mapping theorem. Thus,
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{L}=\mathrm{H}^{2}
with equivalent norms. For
\alpha=0
, there is nothing to prove. For intermediate values
\alpha\in(0,2)
, one argues by interpolation, as both
(\mathrm{H}^{\beta}_{L})_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
and
(\mathrm{H}^{\beta})_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
form complex interpolation scales
[
28
, Theorem 4.17]
. The case
\alpha\in[-2,0)
follows by duality. The second assertion for
q\in(1,\infty)
and
\beta=1
follows from the Kato square root problem, see
[
2
, Corollary 5.19]
for the case of Lipschitz coefficients. ∎

2.3.
Littlewood–Paley-type Estimates

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we rely on results of Littlewood–Paley type for the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
. Proofs can be found in
[
30
, Section 2]
. In order to state the results, we fix some standard (homogeneous) Littlewood–Paley partition of unity, i.e., some radially symmetric
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\}
such that we have

(2.9)

\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{\lambda}(\xi)=1\quad\text{for all}\quad\xi\neq 0,\quad\text{where }\psi_{\lambda}(\xi)\coloneqq\psi\bigg(\frac{\xi}{\lambda}\bigg).

Proposition 2.9

(Calderón Reproducing Formula in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
. Then,

\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi_{\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=f.

In particular,

(2.10)

\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\{\psi(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\mid\psi,f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\}

belongs to
\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{Dom}(L^{\beta})\cap\bigcap_{p\in(1,\infty)}\mathrm{L}^{p}
and is dense in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
.

Proposition 2.10

(
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-Boundedness of Almost Orthogonal Operators)
.

Let
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
h\coloneqq(h_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a sequence of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
h_{\lambda}
is smooth on an open neighborhood of
K_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\|h\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\|h_{\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(K_{\lambda})}<\infty
. Then,

T\colon\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}\to\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-\gamma},\quad Tf\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle\lambda\rangle^{\gamma}(h_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

is a well-defined linear bounded operator with
\|T\|\lesssim\|h\|_{\infty}
. Moreover, for
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
, there holds

\|Tf\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-\gamma}_{L}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\|(h_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})g\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\|\psi_{\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})g\|_{2}^{2}\simeq\|f\|^{2}_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}},

where
g\coloneqq\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha}f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
.

Lemma 2.11

(Differentiation under the Sum)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Suppose that
\{h_{t,\lambda}\mid\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a set of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
which satisfy the following assumptions:

(i)

For all
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and each
\xi\in K_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\lambda})
, the map
t\mapsto h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)
belongs to
C^{n}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
and

|\partial_{t}^{k}h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)|\leq C(t)\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\quad(0\leq k\leq n)

with some locally bounded
function
t\mapsto C(t)
.

(ii)

For each fixed
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\big\|\partial_{t}^{n}h_{t,\lambda}-\partial_{t}^{n}h_{t_{0},\lambda}\big\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}(K_{\lambda})}\rightarrow 0\quad(t\to t_{0}).

Then, for all
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, the map

u\colon t\mapsto T(t)f\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(h_{t,\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

belongs to
C^{k}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-k})
and it holds

u^{(k)}(t)=\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\partial_{t}^{k}h_{t,\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,\quad\|u^{(k)}(t)\|_{\alpha-k}\lesssim C(t)\|f\|_{\alpha}\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
2.10
and dominated convergence in
\ell^{1}(2^{\mathbb{Z}})
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

We also have a straightforward modification of Lemma
2.11
in the case when
t\mapsto h_{t,\lambda}
is only Lipschitz continuous (i.e., only differentiable a.e.). More precisely, let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
and suppose that
\{h_{t,\lambda}\mid\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a set of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
which satisfy: For all
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and each
\xi\in K_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\lambda})
, the map
t\mapsto h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)
is Lipschitz continuous and

|\partial_{t}h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)|\leq C(t)\langle\lambda\rangle^{n}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R},

where
t\mapsto C(t)
is some locally bounded function.

Then, the map

u\colon t\mapsto T(t)f\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(h_{t,\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

belongs to
W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-n})
and it holds

u^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\partial_{t}h_{t,\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,\quad\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{\alpha-n}\lesssim C(t)\|f\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for a.e. $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Proposition 2.13

(Squarefunction Characterization of the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-norm)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then,

\left\|\bigg(\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big|\psi_{\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f(x)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\simeq\|f\|_{p}\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathrm{L}^{p}.

Proof.

See
[
13
, Proposition 5]
.
∎

Lemma 2.14

(Extension of the Operator
L_{j}
to
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Then, the operator
(L_{j})^{n}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}\to\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
extends uniquely to a bounded operator from
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
to
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-n}_{L}
. In particular, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
P(t)\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L}
.

Proof.

Uniqueness is clear, since
\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
is dense in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
by Proposition
2.9
. Now, just note that the operator

T\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-n}_{L},\quad Tf\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\langle\lambda\rangle^{n}\big(\big(\tfrac{\xi_{j}}{\langle\lambda\rangle}\big)^{n}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

is bounded by Proposition
2.10
and that by Proposition
2.3
(b) and Proposition
2.9
, we have
Tf=(L_{j})^{\frac{n}{2}}f
for
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
. The second assertion follows from the identity
P(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}
and the first assertion with
n=2
.
∎

3.
Parametrix Construction

We collect the time-dependent coefficients
b_{j}
in a matrix

(3.1)

B(t):=\mathrm{diag}(b_{1}(t),\dots,b_{d}(t))\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

In this section, for fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{s}\in\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}
, we want to construct a parametrix for the equation

(3.2)

(D_{t}^{2}-P(t))u(t)=0\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}),\quad u(s)=u_{s}.

Recall that
P(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}
. In view of Proposition
2.3
(b),
P(t)
is associated with the symbol
p(t,\xi)\coloneqq(B(t)\xi|\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)\xi_{j}^{2}
,
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and thus the ODE in (
3.2
) corresponds in the frequency space to the equation

(3.3)

(D_{t}^{2}-p(t,\xi))v(t,\xi)=0\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

For fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, (
3.3
) is a second-order linear ODE in
t
. In order to find an approximate solution, we make, as in Lax’s parametrix construction (see
[
25
]
,
[
8
, Chapter VIII.64]
), the ansatz
v(t,\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}
with an appropriate one-homogeneous phase function
\varphi_{t,s}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, whose partial derivatives
\partial_{t}^{k}\varphi_{t,s}

(k\in\{0,1,2\})
are also one-homogeneous with respect to
\xi
. Formally, we then have

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}(D_{t}^{2}-p(t,\xi))\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}=(\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi))^{2}-p(t,\xi))+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\partial^{2}_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi).

Viewing the right-hand side as a function of the frequency variable
\xi
, we observe that
(\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi))^{2},p(t,\xi)\simeq|\xi|^{2}
and
|\partial^{2}_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|\simeq|\xi|
by the homogeneity of
\varphi_{t,s}
. Therefore, we might think of
\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\partial^{2}_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)
as a term of
lower
order (at least in the relevant high-frequency regime
|\xi|\gg 1
). Neglecting this term, we arrive at the approximation

\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}(D_{t}^{2}-p(t,\xi))\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}\approx(\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi))^{2}-p(t,\xi))\overset{!}{=}0,

which yields
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)=\pm p(t,\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\pm(B(t)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Choosing the initial condition
v(s,\xi)=1
in (
3.3
) gives
\varphi_{s,s}(\xi)=0
, so that an integration with respect to
t
naturally leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(Definition of Phase Function)
.

Let
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. We define the phase function

(3.4)

\varphi_{t,s}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)\coloneqq\int_{s}^{t}(B(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

For later reference, we state some useful properties of
\varphi_{t,s}
.

Lemma 3.2

(Regularity of the Phase Function)
.

The following assertions hold true.

(a)

For each
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, the phase function
\varphi_{t,s}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth away from the origin and positively homogeneous of degree one. Moreover, we have the bounds

(3.5)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha}|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}|t-s|.\\

(b)

For fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, the map
t\mapsto\varphi_{t,s}
belongs to
C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
with

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|\simeq|\xi|,\quad|\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\,|\xi|.

Here,
\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)
exists for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\varphi_{t,s}=-\varphi_{s,t}
and thus
\partial_{s}^{k}\varphi_{t,s}=-\partial_{\tau}^{k}\varphi_{\tau,t}\mid_{\tau=s}
for all
k\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Proof.

For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the function
f_{\tau}(\xi)\coloneqq(B(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Clearly,
f_{\tau}
is positively homogeneous of order one and smooth away from the origin. Thus, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f_{\tau}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\tau}|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}\quad\text{with}\quad C_{\alpha,\tau}\coloneqq\sup_{|\xi|=1}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f_{\tau}(\xi)|.

By (
1.8
), we have
\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{Id}}\leq B(\tau)\leq\frac{3}{2}{\mathrm{Id}}
, which yields
C_{\alpha}\coloneqq\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}C_{\alpha,\tau}<\infty
. Therefore, if
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with
s\leq t
, then

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|\leq\int_{s}^{t}|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}f_{\tau}(\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}\int_{s}^{t}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}|t-s|.

The same estimate holds if
s>t
, since
\varphi_{t,s}=-\varphi_{s,t}
. This proves (a). To prove (b), fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and observe that
t\mapsto\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)
is differentiable with

\partial_{t}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)=f_{t}(\xi)\simeq|\xi|.

Moreover, since
t\mapsto f_{t}(\xi)
is Lipschitz continuous, we have for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}

|\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)|=|\partial_{t}f_{t}(\xi)|=\frac{|(B^{\prime}(t)\xi|\xi)|}{2f_{t}(\xi)}\leq\frac{\|B^{\prime}(t)\||\xi|^{2}}{2f_{t}(\xi)}\simeq\|B^{\prime}(t)\||\xi|.

∎

Now, our natural candidate for a parametrix of (
3.2
) would be
T^{\pm}(t,s)u_{s}=(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})u_{s}
. However, this expression is not well-defined within the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
as
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\notin\mathcal{F}\mathbf{M}
. To circumvent this subtlety, we define
T^{\pm}(t,s)
as a limit of Littlewood–Paley sums as in Proposition
2.10
. To this end, we fix a standard Littlewood–Paley partition of unity
(\psi)_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}
as defined in (
2.9
) for the rest of this article.

Proposition 3.3

(Boundedness of Parametrices I)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the linear operators

T^{\pm}(t,s)\colon\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}\to\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L},\quad T^{\pm}(t,s)f=\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

are bounded with operator norms uniformly bounded in
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
and put
h_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}
for
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since
|h_{\lambda}|=1
for all
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, the assertion immediately follows from Proposition
2.10
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

One may wonder why we use the Phillips functional calculus for
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
in place of the Borel functional calculus for the self-adjoint operator
L
. There are at least two reasons. First, while
\rho(|\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}|)=\rho(L)
for
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,\infty))
, the phase function
\varphi_{t,s}
is generally not radially symmetric, so that
T^{\pm}(t,s)
cannot be meaningfully expressed within the Borel functional calculus for
L
. Second, the integral representation (
2.2
) is concrete enough to deduce dispersive estimates for
T^{\pm}(t,s)
which are foundational to prove Strichartz estimates.

Clearly, the operator
\Box_{P}\coloneqq D_{t}^{2}-P(t)
is a differential operator of order two and therefore loses two derivatives in the
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
-scale, i.e.,
\Box_{P}\colon\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}
boundedly. In the following, we show that
T^{\pm}(t,s)
are approximate solution operators to the equation
\Box_{P}u=0
, in the sense that
\Box_{P}T^{\pm}(t,s)
only loses one derivative in the
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
-scale, i.e.,
\Box_{P}T^{\pm}(t,s)\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}
boundedly, see Theorem
3.7
below.
The derivative gain allows us to construct a weak solution to (
1.1
) by an iterative procedure. We need the following lemmas as a preparation.

Lemma 3.5

(Strong Differentiability of Parametrices I)
.

Let
s_{0},t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and assume
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the maps

t\mapsto T^{\pm}(t,s_{0})f\quad\text{and}\quad s\mapsto T^{\pm}(t_{0},s)f

belong to
C_{b}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})\cap C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-2})
, and for all
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, we have

\displaystyle D_{t}^{k}T^{\pm}(t,s_{0})f={}\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big(D_{t}^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s_{0}}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,

\displaystyle D_{s}^{k}T^{\pm}(t_{0},s)f={}\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big(D_{s}^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t_{0},s}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{k}T^{\pm}(t,s_{0})f\|_{\alpha-k}+\|D_{s}^{k}T^{\pm}(t_{0},s)f\|_{\alpha-k}\lesssim\|f\|_{\alpha}.

Proof.

Let
s_{0},t_{0},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
. Since
T^{\pm}(t,s)=T^{\mp}(s,t)
for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, it suffices to consider the maps
u^{\pm}\colon t\mapsto T^{\pm}(t,s_{0})f
. But in this case, the corresponding statement is just a straightforward consequence of Lemma
2.11
, Remark
2.12
, and Lemma
3.2
. Indeed, put
h_{t,\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{\pm i\varphi_{t,s_{0}}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R},\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then,
h_{t,\lambda}
is smooth away from the origin and by Lemma
3.2
, we have the bounds

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)|=1,\\
&|D_{t}h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)|=|D_{t}\varphi_{t,s_{0}}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle,\\
&|D_{t}h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)-D_{t}h_{t_{0},\lambda}(\xi)|\lesssim\|B(t)-B(t_{0})\||\xi|+|t-t_{0}||\xi|^{2},\\
&|D_{t}^{2}h_{t,\lambda}(\xi)|=\big|\big(D_{t}\varphi_{t,s_{0}}(\xi)\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{i}}D_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t,s_{0}}(\xi)\big|\lesssim|\xi|^{2}+\|B^{\prime}(t)\||\xi|\lesssim_{m_{4}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2}.\end{split}

Lemma
2.11
and the first three bounds in (
3.7
) yield
u^{\pm}\in C_{b}^{k}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-k})
for
k\in\{0,1\}
, while Remark
2.12
and the last bound in (
3.7
) give
D_{t}u^{\pm}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-2})
. The claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Derivative Gain on Dyadic Frequencies)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\lambda\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\cdot r_{t,\lambda}\cdot\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,

where
r_{t,\lambda}
is smooth away from the origin with
\|r_{t,\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(K_{\lambda})}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|
and
K_{\lambda}\coloneqq\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
and dominated convergence, we have for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}

(3.8)

D_{t}^{2}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=(D_{t}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f.

Recalling that
\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)=\int_{s}^{t}(B(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau
, we compute for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}

D_{t}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}=D_{t}((B(t)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)})=\big((B(t)\xi|\xi)+D_{t}(B(t)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}.

Plugging this into (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle{}D_{t}^{2}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle((B(t)\xi|\xi)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f+\lambda(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}D_{t}\big(B(t)\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}|\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f.

By Proposition
2.3
(b), the first term on the right-hand side is equal to

\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)(\xi_{j}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=P(t)(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f.

We therefore conclude

\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=\lambda\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}D_{t}(B(t)\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}|\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f.

But by (
3.6
), we have

|(D_{t}(B(t)\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}|\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{2}}|=|D_{t}^{2}\varphi_{t,s}(\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda})|\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\,|\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}|\simeq\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\quad(\xi\in K_{\lambda}),

so the assertion follows setting
r_{t,\lambda}(\xi)\coloneqq D_{t}(B(t)\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda}|\tfrac{\xi}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Derivative Gain I)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the operator

\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T^{\pm}(t,s)\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\to\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}

is bounded for a.e.
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
with an operator norm uniform in
s
and
t
. Moreover, we have the estimates

\displaystyle(i)\quad

\displaystyle\|\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T^{\pm}(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\cdot\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle(ii)\quad

\displaystyle\|\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T^{\pm}(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the estimates (i) and (ii). Let
s,t,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
. To ease notation, we just give the proof for
T\coloneqq T^{+}
(the proof for
T^{-}
is analogous). By Lemma
3.5
, we have
D_{t}^{2}T(t,s)f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L}
almost everywhere. On the other hand, Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
2.14
yield
P(t)T(t,s)f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L}
. Thus,
(D_{t}^{2}-P(t))T(t,s)f
is a well-defined element in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L}
(for a.e.
t
). Now, Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
imply

\displaystyle\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T(t,s)f={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}r_{t,\lambda}\cdot\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda(h_{t,s,\lambda}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

with

h_{t,s,\lambda}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C},\quad h_{t,s,\lambda}(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}r_{t,\lambda}(\xi)

satisfying

\|h_{t,s,\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(K_{\lambda})}=\|r_{t,\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(K_{\lambda})}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|

uniformly in
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. It therefore follows from Proposition
2.10
that
\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T(t,s)f
in fact belongs to
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}
(for a.e.
t
) and that

\|\big(D_{t}^{2}-P(t)\big)T(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}.

This proves (i). Estimate (ii) follows by integrating (i) with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Based on the operators
T^{\pm}(t,s)
, we define the following two families of operators
C(t,s)
and
S(t,s)
to adjust for the initial values
T(t,t)
and
D_{t}T(t,s)|_{t=s}
. They play a similar role as
\mathrm{Cos}((t-s)\sqrt{L})
and
\mathrm{i}(t-s)\mathrm{Sinc}((t-s)\sqrt{\smash[b]{L}})
do in the time-independent case.

Definition 3.8

(Parametrices II)
.

Let
s,t,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. For
f\in\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}
, we define

\displaystyle C(t,s)f\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\hphantom{i}\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big((\cos\circ\varphi_{t,s})\cdot\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,

(3.9)

\displaystyle S(t,s)f\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\bigg(\frac{\sin\circ\varphi_{t,s}}{\partial_{s}\varphi_{t,s}}\cdot\psi_{\lambda}\bigg)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f.

The properties established for
T^{\pm}(t,s)
extend to
C(t,s)
in a natural way. The situation for
S(t,s)
is slightly different. In view of the factor
|\partial_{s}\varphi_{t,s}|^{-1}\simeq|\xi|^{-1}
in (
3.9
), the mapping properties of
S(t,s)
are even improved by one order in the
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
-scale (see Lemma
3.9
(b) (iv) below). This will be crucial in Section
4
. Another difference is that the very same factor reduces the strong differentiability of
s\mapsto S(t,s)
by one order, which fortunately turns out to be irrelevant for our purposes.

Lemma 3.9

(Relation between Parametrices)
.

Let
s,t,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

We have

(3.10)

C(t,s)f=\frac{1}{2}\big(T^{+}(t,s)+T^{-}(t,s)\big)f\quad(f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}).

(b)

Define for
f\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}

\tilde{T}(t,s)f\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\tilde{h}_{t,s}\psi_{\lambda}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,\quad\tilde{h}_{t,s}(\xi)\coloneqq\langle\xi\rangle\frac{\sin(\varphi_{t,s}(\xi))}{\partial_{s}\varphi_{t,s}(\xi)}.

Then:

(i)

\tilde{T}(t,s)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})
with
\|\tilde{T}(t,s)\|\lesssim\langle t-s\rangle
.

(ii)

The map
\tau\mapsto\tilde{T}(\tau,s)f

Proof.

(t\in\mathbb{R})

Lemma 4.1

(Volterra Operator)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
G\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
. Put

V(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Then,
V\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})\cap W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L})
. Moreover,

(D_{t}^{2}-P(t))V(t)=\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{i}}[(\mathbf{Id}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{R})G](t)

for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
where

\mathbf{R}\colon\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})\to\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}),\quad(\mathbf{R}G)(t)=\int_{0}^{t}R_{2}(t,s)G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

has operator norm
\|\mathbf{R}\|\leq C\|B^{\prime}\|_{1}
with a constant
C
depending only on
m_{1},m_{2}
and
m_{4}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
G\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
. We write

V(t)=\int_{0}^{t}v(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}),

where we have set
v(t,s)\coloneqq S(t,s)G(s)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and a.e.
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
by Theorem
3.10
, we have for a.e.
s\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle v(\cdot,s)\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})\cap W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-k}_{L})\quad\text{with }

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{k}v(t,s)\|_{\alpha-k}\lesssim\langle t-s\rangle^{|k-1|}\|G(s)\|_{\alpha-1},\quad k\in\{0,1,2\}.

First Step
:
\mathbf{V\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}

Fix
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We have to show
V(t)\to V(t_{0})
in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
as
t\to t_{0}
. We consider the limit
t\downarrow t_{0}
(the limit
t\uparrow t_{0}
can be treated similarly). Observe that for
t\geq t_{0}
,

(4.3)

\|V(t)-V(t_{0})\|_{\alpha}\leq{}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|v(t,s)\|_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|v(t,s)-v(t_{0},s)\|_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Now for the first integral on the right-hand side of (
4.3
), we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|v(t,s)\|_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim\langle t-t_{0}\rangle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|G(s)\|_{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\rightarrow 0\quad(t\downarrow t_{0}),

where we used (
4
) with
k=0
and then dominated convergence. To estimate the second integral on the right-hand side of (
4.3
), note that once again by (
4
) (with
k=0
), the integrand converges to zero as
t\downarrow t_{0}
for a.e.
s
and is controlled by
\langle t_{0}-s\rangle\|G(s)\|_{\alpha-1}\lesssim\langle t_{0}\rangle\|G(s)\|_{\alpha-1}
if

. Thus, we conclude once again that

\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\|v(t,s)-v(t_{0},s)\|_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\rightarrow 0\quad(t\downarrow t_{0})

.

Second Step
:
\mathbf{V\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};H^{\alpha-1}_{L})}

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\frac{V(t+h)-V(t)}{\mathrm{i}h}={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+h}\frac{v(t+h,s)-v(t,s)}{\mathrm{i}h}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}h}\int_{t}^{t+h}v(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\Delta_{1,h}+\Delta_{2,h}.

Now, it follows again from (
4

k=1
and dominated convergence that

Turning to
\Delta_{2,h}
, we have to be a bit more careful since

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{2,h}\|_{\alpha-1}={}

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{(t,t+h)}(s)\bigg(\frac{s-t}{h}\bigg)\frac{S(t,s)-S(t,t)}{s-t}G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|_{\alpha-1}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t+h}\frac{D_{t}v(t+h,s)-D_{t}v(t,s)}{\mathrm{i}h}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}h}\int_{t}^{t+h}D_{t}v(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\|D_{t}S(t,s)G(s)-G(s)\|_{\alpha-2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}\|G(s)-G(t)\|_{\alpha-2}\,\mathrm{d}s

is just defined as in Definition

with
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
in place of
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
.
.

Theorem 4.2

(Well-posedness in
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}
be the constant from (
1.9
) and suppose that
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{C})
,
where

is the constant from Lemma

. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose that

,

, and

) in

. Moreover, there exists

u(t)=C(t,0)g+S(t,0)h+\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}),

and we have the estimate

\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}+\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}+\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}.

Furthermore, for each bounded interval

with

(4.6)

\Phi\colon\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}\times\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}\times\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})\to C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}),\quad(g,h,F)\mapsto u

is Lipschitz continuous.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose that
g\in\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}
,
h\in\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}
, and
F\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
. Uniqueness of weak solutions and the continuous dependence of the initial data and the driving force as in (

below. To prove existence, define for a given function
G\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
the function
u
by the right-hand side of (
4.5
). Then, by Theorem
3.10
and Lemma

,

with

and

. Now Theorem

and Lemma

imply that

) in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
if and only if for a.e.

F(t)=((D_{t}^{2}-P(t))u(t)=R_{1}(t,0)g+R_{2}(t,0)h+\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\big[(\mathbf{Id}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{R})G\big](t),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\big(\mathbf{Id}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{R}\big)G(t)=F(t)-R_{1}(t,0)g-R_{2}(t,0)h.

Since
\|\mathbf{R}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}))}\leq C\|B^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\leq C\varepsilon_{1}<1

\displaystyle\|R_{1}(t,0)g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}},

\displaystyle\|R_{2}(t,0)h\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim\|B^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}}\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}}

it follows that

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}

\displaystyle\|R_{1}(t,0)g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}+\|R_{2}(t,0)h\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}+\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}+\|h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}+\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}.

∎

By Proposition

, we can identify the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
and

for

, so Theorem

.

Corollary 4.3

(Existence and Uniqueness of Weak Solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
be as in Theorem
4.2
and suppose additionally
-1\leq\alpha\leq 2
. Assume that
g\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
,

, and

. Then, the function
u
as in Theorem

defines the unique weak solution to (
1.1
) in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}
.

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
4.2
and hence Corollary
4.3
are also true under milder conditions on the coefficients
b_{1},\dots,b_{d}
. First, observe that up to now, we did not use the smallness assumption of
\varepsilon_{0}
as in (
1.8
). Thus, it would be enough if the coefficients
b_{j}
are bounded from above and below by a positive constant, just as the coefficients
a_{j}
are. Second, the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1}>0
was only needed in the proof of Theorem
4.2
in order to invert the operator
\mathbf{Id}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{R}
on
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
(using a Neumann-series argument). However,
without
assuming the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1}
, we can invert this operator
locally
in time, i.e., on the space
\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
, where
J\coloneqq(-T,T)
for some fixed
T\in(0,\infty)
. Indeed, a straightforward induction on
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
yields

(4.7)

\|\mathbf{R}^{k}G\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}\lesssim\frac{(C_{J}\,T)^{k}}{k!}\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}_{0})

with

C_{J}\coloneqq\sup_{t,s\in J}\|R_{2}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim\sup_{t,s\in J}\|B^{\prime}(t)\|\leq m_{4},

where we used Theorem
3.11
. Thus, the operator
A\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\mathrm{i}^{k}\mathbf{R}^{k}
converges in
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}))
(with operator norm bounded by a constant times
\mathrm{e}^{m_{4}T}
) and defines the inverse of
\mathbf{Id}+\mathrm{i}\mathbf{R}
. Following the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we obtain a unique weak solution on
J
, i.e., a unique function
u_{J}\in C(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})\cap C^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})\cap W^{2,1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-2})
with
u(0)=g
,
D_{t}u(0)=h
and
D_{t}^{2}u=P(t)u(t)+F(t)
for a.e.
t\in J
. Exhausting
\mathbb{R}
by bounded open intervals
J
then gives the claim. Observe that this gives only
G\in\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})
in the representation formula for
u
.
However, the smallness of
\varepsilon_{1}
ensures that
G\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})
, which in turn means that the energy
\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}
stays globally bounded with respect to

. In general, this is nontrivial (see e.g.
[
42
]
). However, it is a minimum requirement in order to prove global-in-time Strichartz estimates, since the energy estimates are a special case of the latter.

5.
Uniqueness of Weak Solutions

The classical energy inequalities imply the uniqueness of weak solutions to linear wave equations with
C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-coefficients in the scale of standard Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
As the coefficients of our wave operator
\Box_{P}
possess only limited regularity, these energy inequalities are applicable only in a restricted range of exponents
\alpha
. However, combining them with Proposition
2.8
gives a first result.

Lemma 5.1

(Classical Energy Inequalities)
.

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded open interval with
0\in J
. If

u\in C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L})\cap C^{1}(\overline{J};\mathrm{L}^{2})\cap W^{2,1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{-1}_{L})

with
\Box_{P}u=(D_{t}^{2}-P(t))u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{L}^{2})
, then

(5.1)

\|u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L})}\lesssim_{|J|}\|u(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}}+\|D_{t}u(0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\|\Box_{P}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{L}^{2})}.

Proof.

We first observe that (
5.1
) holds with
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
replaced by the classical Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}^{1}
. Indeed, a careful inspection of the proof of
[
36
, Chapter I, Theorem 3.1]
shows that the latter is applicable with
s=0
in the assumptions of that theorem (the coefficients
c_{j}(t,x)=b_{j}(t)a_{j}(x_{j})
of
P(t)
are Lipschitz, so taking one time derivative and integrating by parts once with respect to
x
is justified to make the energy inequality work). But
\mathrm{H}^{\pm 1}_{L}=\mathrm{H}^{\pm 1}
by Proposition
2.8
, so the claim follows.
∎

Note that Lemma
5.1
in particular implies the uniqueness of weak solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
. In the following, we lift this result to general exponents
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. The reason why we can do this is that, while
\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\alpha}
does not commute with
P(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}
for large
\alpha
(by the limited regularity of the coefficients
a_{j}
), the fractional power
\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha}
in fact
does
(on appropriate subsets of functions). In order to exploit this fact, we need the following approximation lemma. To state it, we introduce some notation first. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded open interval. Put

\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha}\coloneqq\begin{cases}W^{m,p}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})&\text{for }p\in[1,\infty),\\
C^{m}(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})&\text{for }p=\infty.\end{cases}

Let
u\in\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then, we set
\mathbb{Z}_{n}\coloneqq\{-n,\dots,n\}
and define

(\Pi_{n}u)(t)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}}\psi_{\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})u(t)\quad\text{for all }t\in\overline{J}

if
p=\infty
. If
p\in[1,\infty)
, we define
\Pi_{n}u
by the same expression in an almost everywhere sense. If
I\Subset J
is an open subinterval and
4^{-n}<\mathrm{dist}(I,J^{c})
, we define

(S_{n}u)(t)\coloneqq(\varphi_{n}\ast u)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{n}(t-s)u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for }t\in\overline{I},

where
\varphi_{n}(t)\coloneqq 4^{n}\varphi(4^{n}t)
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subseteq(-1,1)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=1
.

Lemma 5.2

(Approximation Lemma)
.

Let
I\Subset J
be bounded open intervals in
\mathbb{R}
and
m,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0},p\in[1,\infty],\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
\Pi_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha},\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha+\gamma})
with
\|\Pi_{n}\|\lesssim_{\gamma}2^{(n+1)\gamma_{+}}
. Moreover,
\Pi_{n}\to{\mathrm{Id}}
strongly on
\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha}
as
n\to\infty
.

(b)

For all
n\geq n_{0}
, we have
S_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha},\mathcal{W}^{m+\ell,p}_{I,\alpha})
with
\|S_{n}\|\lesssim 4^{n\ell_{+}}
, where
n_{0}
is such that
4^{-n_{0}}<\mathrm{dist}(I,J^{c})
. Moreover, if
u\in\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha}
, then
(S_{n}u)^{(k)}=S_{n}u^{(k)}
for
k\leq m
and
S_{n}u\to u|_{I}
in
\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{I,\alpha}
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose first that
p\in[1,\infty)
. If
\gamma\leq 0
, then
\|\Pi_{n}\|\leq 1
since
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\alpha+\gamma}_{L}
in this case. Therefore, we may suppose
\gamma>0
. By Lemma
2.7
(a) and Proposition
2.3
(c), we then have

\|\Pi_{n}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha+\gamma}_{L}}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}}\langle\lambda\rangle^{\gamma}\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}}\lesssim_{\gamma}2^{(n+1)\gamma}\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}}

for a.e.
t\in J
. Raising this inequality to the
p
-th power and integrating it with respect to
t\in J
, we deduce the bound
\|\Pi_{n}\|\lesssim 2^{(n+1)\gamma}
. This proves the first claim. To prove the second claim, note that we have proved that
(\Pi_{n})_{n}
is in particular uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{W}_{J,\alpha}^{m,p}
. Let
v\in D\coloneqq C^{\infty}(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})
. Then,
\Pi_{n}v\to v
in
\mathcal{W}_{J,\alpha}^{m,p}
by Proposition
2.9
and the uniform continuity of
v
. Now, since
D
is dense in
\mathcal{W}_{J,\alpha}^{m,p}
(see e.g.
[
5
, Corollary 1.4.37]
), the strong convergence of
(\Pi_{n})_{n}
to
{\mathrm{Id}}
on
\mathcal{W}^{m,p}_{J,\alpha}
follows from a standard density argument using uniform boundedness. The case
p=\infty
is proved similarly.

(b) These assertions are proved exactly as in the case of scalar-valued functions (see e.g.
[
19
, Section 1.3]
).
∎

Proposition 5.3

(Energy Estimates in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
)
.

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded open interval with
0\in J
. Suppose further that for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
u
belongs to
C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})\cap C^{1}(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})\cap W^{2,1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L})
. If
\Box_{P}u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})
, then

\|u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})}\lesssim_{|J|}\|u(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}+\|D_{t}u(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}}+\|\Box_{P}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})}.

Proof.

Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{R},\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
u
be as in the statement of the proposition. Fix some open interval
I
with
0\in\overline{I}\subseteq J
. Now, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
4^{n}\geq(\mathrm{dist}(I,J^{c}))^{-1}
, we define
u_{n}\colon\overline{I}\to\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}
by

u_{n}(t)\coloneqq(S_{n}\Pi_{n}u)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{n}(t-s)\Pi_{n}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad(t\in\overline{I}).

Observe that
u_{n}
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\infty}_{L})
. Indeed, since
u\in C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})
, it follows that
\Pi_{n}u\in C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\beta}_{L})
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
by Lemma
5.2
(a) and therefore
u_{n}\in C^{k}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\beta}_{L})
by Lemma
5.2
(b) for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This proves
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\infty}_{L})
. But then also
v_{n}\coloneqq\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha-1}u_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\infty}_{L})
and
\Box_{P}v_{n}\in C^{0,1}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\infty}_{L})\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{L}^{2})
(note that the loss of regularity in
t
arises from the fact that the
b_{j}
are only Lipschitz). Applying Lemma
5.1
to
v_{n}
, we obtain

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|_{C(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})}&=\hphantom{{}_{|I|}}{}\|v_{n}\|_{C(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{L}}^{1})}\\
&\lesssim_{|I|}\|v_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}}+\|D_{t}v_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\|\Box_{P}v_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{L}^{2})}\\
&=\hphantom{{}_{K}}{}\|u_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}}+\|D_{t}u_{n}(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}+\|\Box_{P}u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})},\end{split}

where, for the last equality, we used that
\Box_{P}
and
\langle D_{L}\rangle^{\alpha-1}
commute when applied to
u_{n}
. Now, we want to pass to the limit
n\to\infty
in (
5.2
). By Lemma
5.2
, we infer
u_{n}\to u
in
C(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})
and
D_{t}u_{n}\to D_{t}u
in
C^{1}(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})
.
We claim that

(5.3)

\|\Box_{P}u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim_{|I|}\|\Box_{P}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}+2^{-n}\|u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}.

Taking (
5.3
) for granted, we may let
n\to\infty
in (
5.2
) to obtain

(5.4)

\|u\|_{C(\overline{I};\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha})}\lesssim_{|I|}\|u(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha}}+\|D_{t}u(0)\|_{\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1}}+\|\Box_{P}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(J;\mathrm{H}_{L}^{\alpha-1})},

and we infer the claim from (
5.4
) by simply exhausting
J
by open, compactly contained subintervals
I\Subset J
. So it remains to prove (
5.3
). First, note that since
u\in W^{2,1}(J;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L})
by assumption, Lemma
5.2
(b) implies

(5.5)

D_{t}^{2}u_{n}=D_{t}^{2}(\varphi_{n}\ast\Pi_{n}u)=(\varphi_{n}\ast\Pi_{n}D_{t}^{2}u)=S_{n}\Pi_{n}D_{t}^{2}u

in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-2}_{L})
. On the other hand, since
P(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}(t)L_{j}
, one may verify that

(5.6)

(Pu_{n})(t)=(S_{n}\Pi_{n}Pu)(t)-Ru(t)\quad\text{for }t\in\overline{I}

with
Ru=\sum_{j=1}^{d}R_{j}u
and

R_{j}u(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{n}(t-s)(b_{j}(t)-b_{j}(s))(L_{j}\Pi_{n}u)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Note that by Lemma
2.14
and Lemma
5.2
(a)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|R_{j}u(t)\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}}\leq{}&\|b_{j}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\Pi_{n}u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha+1}_{L})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\varphi_{n}(t-s)||t-s|\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\lesssim{}&m_{4}\cdot\big(2^{(n+1)}\|\Pi_{n}u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}\big)\cdot 4^{-n}\|\varphi\|_{1}\\
\lesssim{}&2^{-n}\|u\|_{C(\overline{J};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}\end{split}

for each
t\in\overline{I}
and thus

(5.7)

\|Ru\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}\lesssim_{|I|}2^{-n}\textbf{}\|u\|_{C(\overline{J},\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}.

Combining (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we conclude

\Box_{P}u_{n}=(D_{t}^{2}-P)u_{n}=S_{n}\Pi_{n}\Box_{P}u-Ru\quad\text{in }\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}),

and applying Lemma
5.2
and (
5.7
) gives

\displaystyle\|\Box_{P}u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}

\displaystyle\leq{}\|S_{n}\Pi_{n}\Box_{P}u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}+\|Ru\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{|I|}{}\|\Box_{P}u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(I;\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})}+2^{-n}\|u\|_{C(\overline{J},\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L})}

as desired. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.4

(Uniqueness of Weak Solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose that
g\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L},h\in\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L}
, and
F\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{H}^{\alpha-1}_{L})
. If
u
and
v
are weak solutions to (
1.1
) in
\mathrm{H}^{\alpha}_{L}
, then
u=v
.

Proof.

Define
J_{n}\coloneqq(-n,n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying Proposition
5.3
to
w_{n}\coloneqq(u-v)|_{\overline{J_{n}}}
gives
w_{n}=0
. Letting
n\to\infty
yields
w=0
and thus
u=v
as desired.
∎

6.
Global Strichartz Estimates

In this section, we prove Theorem

following the approach in the proof of

. Recall the parametrices from Section

,

T^{\pm}(t,s)=\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}T^{\pm}_{\lambda}(t,s)=\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}).

We first establish a Strichartz inequality for

in Subsection

using the Keel–Tao framework

, see Proposition

6.3
, we patch these estimates together to deduce global Strichartz estimates for
T^{\pm}(t,s)
(see Theorem
6.8
(a) below). This then immediately translates to Strichartz estimates for the parametrices
C(t,s)
and
S(t,s)
, which, when combined with Theorem
4.2
and the Christ–Kiselev lemma, then yields the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

6.1.
Global Strichartz Estimates for Unit Frequencies

In this subsection, we prove global Strichartz estimates for
T^{\pm}_{1}(t,s)
. More precisely, we show:

Proposition 6.1

(Global Strichartz Estimates for
T_{1}^{\pm}(t,s)
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
from (
1.8
) be sufficiently small and suppose that
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,

(6.1)

\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}),

with an implicit constant independent of
s
.

Our proof is based on the celebrated result by Keel–Tao
[
23
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we restate it here. Recall that, for given
\gamma>0
, an exponent pair
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]^{2}
is called
\gamma
-admissible
if
(p,q,\sigma)\neq(2,\infty,1)
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\gamma}{q}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}
. Note that if
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple, then
(p,q)
is
\frac{d-1}{2}
-admissible (cf. (
1.4
)).

Theorem 6.2

(
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a measure space and
H
be a Hilbert space. Suppose that
U(t)\colon H\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is a linear operator for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

Uniform Boundedness: For all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in H

\|U(t)f\|_{\mathrm{L}_{x}^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}.

(ii)

Truncated Decay: For some
\gamma>0
, we have

\|U(s)U(t)^{\ast}g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{x}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim(1+|t-s|)^{-\gamma}\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}_{x}^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all }s,t\in\mathbb{R},g\in S,

where
S\subseteq\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
denotes a dense subset in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Then, the estimates

\|U(t)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\Omega))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}\quad(f\in H)

and

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}U(t)U(s)^{\ast}F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\Omega))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H}\quad(F\in\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{x}(\Omega))),

hold true for any
\gamma
-admissible exponent pairs
(p,q),(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
.

We want to apply Theorem
6.2
with
U_{\pm}(t)\coloneqq L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})
(for fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}
). Condition (i) in Theorem
6.2
is straightforward to verify using the Phillips functional calculus. However, for condition (ii), we need an oscillatory integral estimate, whose proof we postpone to Section
7
. In order to state it, we set for
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{\tilde{B}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\mid\tilde{B}(t)\text{ is diagonal for all $t\in[0,1]$ and }\|\tilde{B}-{\mathrm{Id}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\}

and associate with
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
the phase function

(6.2)

\varphi_{\tilde{B}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi_{\tilde{B}}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}(\tilde{B}(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

To ease notation, we just write
\tilde{\varphi}\coloneqq\varphi_{\tilde{B}}
, if
\tilde{B}
is clear from the context.
Observe that, if
\varphi_{t,s}
denotes the phase function from Definition
3.1
, then a simple change of variables shows that

(6.3)

\varphi_{t,s}=(t-s)\tilde{\varphi}_{t,s},\,\text{where }\tilde{\varphi}_{t,s}\coloneqq\varphi_{\tilde{B}_{t,s}},\;\tilde{B}_{t,s}(\tau)\coloneqq B((t-s)\tau+s)\quad(\tau\in[0,1]),

and
\tilde{B}_{t,s}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
by (
1.8
).

Theorem 6.3

(Oscillatory Integral Estimate)
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that the following holds. For any
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported away from the origin, there exists some constant
C=C(\tilde{\psi},d)>0
such that we have the decay estimate

|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi})(y)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
and
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

Equipped with Theorem
6.2
and Theorem
6.3
, we are in the position to prove Proposition
6.1
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

Let
(p,q,\alpha)
be a wave-admissible Strichartz triple and fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Set
\tilde{\psi}\coloneqq|\cdot|^{-\alpha}\psi
. By Lemma
2.7
(b), we have for
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
that

\displaystyle L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f={}

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\eqqcolon U_{\pm}(t)f.

Thus, it remains to show

(6.4)

\|U_{\pm}(t)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}),

for which we invoke Theorem
6.2
. To ease notation, we provide the proof for
U\coloneqq U_{+}
(the proof for
U_{-}
is analogous). We verify conditions (i) and (ii) from Theorem
6.2
. Property (i) is easy since

\|U(t)f\|_{2}\lesssim_{M_{2}}\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\tilde{\psi}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}\lesssim_{\psi,d,\alpha}\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2},t\in\mathbb{R})

by Proposition
2.3
(c). As
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
and therefore also
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
is self-adjoint w.r.t. (
2.5
), we have
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}})^{\ast}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
and therefore

(U(\sigma))^{\ast}=\big(\overline{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\sigma,s}}\tilde{\psi}}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})=\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\sigma,s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}).

Thus,

(6.5)

U(\tau)(U(\sigma))^{\ast}=\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\varphi_{\tau,s}-\varphi_{\sigma,s})}\tilde{\psi}^{2}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})=\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\tau,\sigma}}\tilde{\psi}^{2}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})

for
\tau,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, by Proposition
2.5
(b), we have for
g\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}
that

(6.6)

\|U(\tau)(U(\sigma))^{\ast}g\|_{\infty}=\big\|\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\tau,\sigma}}\tilde{\psi}^{2}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})g\big\|_{\infty}\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\tau,\sigma}}\tilde{\psi}^{2})\|_{\infty}\|g\|_{1}.

In view of (
6.3
), we have
\varphi_{\tau,\sigma}=(\tau-\sigma)\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau,\sigma}
with
\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau,\sigma}=\varphi_{\tilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}}
and
\tilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.
Hence, we obtain from Theorem
6.3

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{\tau,\sigma}}\tilde{\psi}^{2})\|_{\infty}=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\tau-\sigma)\tilde{\varphi}_{\tau,\sigma}}\tilde{\psi}^{2})\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\tilde{\psi},d}(1+|\tau-\sigma|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|g\|_{1}\quad(\tau,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}).

Using this in (
6.6
) shows condition (ii) in Theorem
6.2
. Applying the latter theorem (separately in both components of
\mathrm{L}^{2}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2})
), we obtain (
6.4
) as desired.
∎

6.2.
Global Strichartz Estimates for
T_{\lambda}^{\pm}(t,s)

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. For
\lambda>0
, we define the dilation operator
\delta_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{L}^{p}\to\mathrm{L}^{p}
by
(\delta_{\lambda}f)(x)\coloneqq f_{\lambda}(x)\coloneqq f(\frac{x}{\lambda})
and the rescaled operator

\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}\coloneqq(L_{1,\lambda},\dots,L_{d,\lambda})
by

L_{j,\lambda}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}D_{j}a_{j,\lambda}D_{j}&\\
0&a_{j,\lambda}D_{j}^{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad a_{j,\lambda}(x_{j})\coloneqq a_{j}\big(\tfrac{x_{j}}{\lambda}\big).

The following lemma shows that
T^{\pm}_{\lambda}(t,s)
is obtained from
T^{\pm}_{1}(t,s)
by rescaling.

Lemma 6.5

(Scaling)
.

Let
\lambda>0
.

(a)

We have

(6.7)

\delta_{\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{y}{\lambda}\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\sqrt{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}\delta_{\lambda}.

In particular,

(6.8)

\sup_{\lambda>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\sqrt{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})}\leq 1.

(b)

We have

\big[\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big]_{\lambda}=\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\psi\big)(\sqrt{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}})f_{\lambda}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}).

Proof.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\lambda>0
.

(a)

A straightforward computation reveals the identity
\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-2}\mathbf{L})\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}
, which implies
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}=\delta_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}
by the holomorphic functional calculus for sectorial operators. By standard semigroup theory (see e.g.
[
7
, Sections II.2.1 and II.2.2]
), it follows that
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}
generates a
d
-parameter
C_{0}
-group on
\mathrm{L}^{p}
given by

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}}=\delta_{\lambda}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{y}{\lambda}\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\quad(y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Now, (
6.8
) is a straightforward consequence, since

\big\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\sqrt{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}\big\|=\big\|\delta_{\lambda}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{y}{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}}\big)\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\big\|\leq\big\|\delta_{\lambda}\big\|\big\|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{y}{\lambda}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbf{L}}}\big\|\big\|\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\big\|\leq\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}\cdot 1\cdot\lambda^{-\frac{d}{2}}=1,

where in the above display
\|\cdot\|
denotes the norm in
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})
.

(b)

Let
s,t\in
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
. By Remark
2.4
, we have

(6.9)

\big[(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big]_{\lambda}={}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(y)\big(\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big)_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Observe that
\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(y)=\lambda^{d}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\lambda y)
by the change of variables
\xi\mapsto\lambda\xi
and the positive homogeneity of
\varphi_{t,s}
. Moreover,
\big(\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big)_{\lambda}=\mathrm{Cos}(\lambda y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}))f_{\lambda}
by (
6.7
). Using these relations in (
6.9
) gives

\displaystyle\big[(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big]_{\lambda}={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda^{d}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\lambda y)\mathrm{Cos}(\lambda y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}))f_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(y)\mathrm{Cos}(y\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}))f_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}y=(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\psi)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}})f_{\lambda}.

∎

Lemma 6.6

(Global Strichartz Estimates for
T_{\lambda}^{\pm}(t,s)
)
.

Suppose that
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda>0
. Then,

(6.10)

\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}),

with an implicit constant independent of
s
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
(p,q,\alpha)
be a wave-admissible Strichartz triple,
\lambda>0
, and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
. As in the proof of Proposition
6.1
, set
\tilde{\psi}\coloneqq|\cdot|^{-\alpha}\psi
. Then, by Lemma
2.7
(b) and Lemma
6.5
(b), we have

\displaystyle L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\alpha}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\tilde{\psi}_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\alpha}\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\varphi_{t,s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}})\delta_{\lambda}f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\alpha}\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi^{\lambda}_{\lambda t,\lambda s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}})\delta_{\lambda}f,

where
\varphi^{\lambda}_{t,s}
is just defined as
\varphi_{t,s}
, with
B(\tau)
replaced by
B_{\lambda}(\tau)=B(\frac{\tau}{\lambda})
, see Definition
3.1
.
Now, applying the change of variables
x\mapsto\frac{x}{\lambda}
,
t\mapsto\frac{t}{\lambda}
first and then Remark
6.4
as well as Proposition
6.1
with
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}}
in place of
\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}
(note that this is justified since Assumption
1.1
is invariant under the scaling
a_{j}\mapsto a_{j,\lambda}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\alpha}\|\delta_{\lambda^{-1}}\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi^{\lambda}_{\lambda t,\lambda s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}})\delta_{\lambda}f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\big(\alpha+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\big)}\|\big(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi^{\lambda}_{t,\lambda s}}\tilde{\psi}\big)(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}_{\lambda}}})\delta_{\lambda}f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\big(\alpha+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\big)}\|\delta_{\lambda}f\|_{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-\big(\alpha-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{d}{p}\big)}\|f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2},

where the last equality follows from the fact that
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple.
∎

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We recall the following lemma, which is due to Christ–Kiselev.

Lemma 6.7

(
[
39
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
X,Y
be Banach spaces and let
K\in C(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R};\mathcal{L}(X,Y))
. Suppose that
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
is such that

\bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}K(t,s)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R};Y)}\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)}

for all
F\in\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)
. Then, one also has

\bigg\|\int_{-\infty}^{t}K(t,s)F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};Y)}\lesssim\|F\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)}.

Theorem 6.8

(Strichartz Estimates for Parametrices I)
.

Suppose that
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple.

(a)

The estimate

\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}})

holds uniformly in
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

If
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{\alpha})
is another wave-admissible Strichartz triple with
\tilde{p}^{\prime}<p
, then

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}L^{-\tfrac{\alpha+\tilde{\alpha}}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))}\lesssim\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

for all measurable functions
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
of compact support.

Proof.

Let
(p,q,\alpha)
be a wave-admissible Strichartz triple.

(a)

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, Proposition
2.13
followed by an application of Minkowski’s inequality (note that
p,q\geq 2
in view of (
1.4
)) gives

(6.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\simeq{}&\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big|\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\\
\lesssim{}&\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f\big\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.\end{split}

Note that by the Phillips functional calculus and the fact that the
\psi_{\lambda}
are almost disjointly supported, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}(|\cdot|^{-\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda}\psi_{\mu})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in I_{\mu}}L^{-\alpha}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f,

where
I_{\mu}\coloneqq\{\tfrac{\mu}{2},\mu,2\mu\}
. So the sum on the right-hand side of (
6.11
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle{}3\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\lambda\in I_{\mu}}\big\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 3\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\mu\in I_{\lambda}}\big\|L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\varphi_{t,s}}\psi_{\lambda})(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle 3\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{\mu\in I_{\lambda}}\big\|\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big\|^{2}_{2}=9\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\big\|\psi_{\mu}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\big\|^{2}_{2}\simeq\|f\|_{2}^{2},

where we used Lemma
6.6
and Proposition
2.10
in the last line of the above display. This proves (a).

(b)

Let
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{\alpha})
be another wave-admissible Strichartz triple with
\tilde{p}^{\prime}<p
. Fix
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
is dense in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by Proposition
2.9
, it follows from (a) that

\mathcal{T}_{s}\colon\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\tilde{p}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{q}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad(\mathcal{T}_{s}f)(t)=\mathrm{L}^{-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,s)f

is a bounded linear operator. Recalling the scalar product (
2.5
) on
\mathrm{L}^{2}
, we may define the equivalent dual pairing

\langle F,G\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle F(\tau),G(\tau)\rangle_{A}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

on
\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\tilde{p}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{q}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Since
\mathcal{T}_{s}
is bounded, the dual operator
\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\ast}
and thus the composition
\mathcal{T}_{s}\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\ast}
are bounded, too. Let
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
be measurable with compact support. Then, a straightforward computation reveals

\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\ast}G=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{T}_{\tau}(s)G(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

and using that

T^{\pm}(t,s)T^{\pm}(s,\tau)=T^{\pm}(t,\tau),\quad T^{\pm}(t,s)^{\ast}=T^{\pm}(s,t)\quad\text{for }\quad t,s,\tau\in\mathbb{R}

as identities on
\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})
, we conclude

(\mathcal{T}_{s}\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\ast}G)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}L^{-\frac{\alpha+\tilde{\alpha}}{2}}T^{\pm}(t,\tau)G(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for a.e.
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, the claim follows from the boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{s}\mathcal{T}_{s}^{\ast}
and Lemma
6.7
.

∎

Corollary 6.9

(Strichartz Estimates for Parametrices II)
.

Suppose that
(p,q,\alpha)
is a wave-admissible Strichartz triple.

(a)

We have

(6.12)

\|L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}C(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|L^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L})

and

(6.13)

\|L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}S(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}\quad(f\in\mathrm{L}^{2})

uniformly in
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

Suppose that
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q},\tilde{\alpha})
is another Strichartz triple with
\tilde{p}^{\prime}<p
. Then, there holds the inhomogeneous estimate

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}L^{\tfrac{1-\alpha-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}}S(t,s)G(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})))}\lesssim\|G\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

for
G\in\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}_{t}(\mathrm{L}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

Proof.

Let
(p,q,\alpha)
be wave-admissible and let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
. By Lemma
3.9
(a) and Lemma
2.7
(b), we may write

L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}C(t,s)f=\tfrac{1}{2}L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{+}(t,s)L^{\frac{1}{2}}f+\tfrac{1}{2}L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{-}(t,s)L^{\frac{1}{2}}f,

so Theorem
6.8
gives
\|L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}C(t,s)f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|L^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{2}
uniformly in
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Now, (
6.12
) follows as
\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
is dense in
\mathrm{H}_{L}^{1}
(in particular, the left-hand side
L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}C(t,s)f
is to be understood as a limit in
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{t}(\mathbb{R};\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
). This proves (
6.12
). Let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0\leq\rho\leq 1
and
\rho=1
on
B(0,1)
. For
\lambda\geq 1
, put
\chi_{\lambda}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\chi_{\lambda}(\xi)\coloneqq\rho(\frac{\xi}{\lambda})-\rho(\lambda\xi)
. Then, by the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}}}
, we have
\chi_{\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f=f
for
\lambda=\lambda(f)\geq 1
sufficiently large. In view of (
3.8
), we may then write

L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}S(t,s)f=\tfrac{1}{2}L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{+}(t,s)m_{s,\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f-\tfrac{1}{2}L^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}T^{-}(t,s)m_{s,\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f

with
m_{s,\lambda}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{|\xi|}{(B(s)\xi|\xi)^{1/2}}\chi_{\lambda}(\xi)
. Since
\|m_{s,\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})f\|_{2}\lesssim\|m_{s,\lambda}\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{2}
by Proposition
2.3
(c), we argue as above to infer (
6.13
). Finally, (b) is proved similarly as Theorem
6.8
(b), using that
\|m_{s,\lambda}(\sqrt{\smash[b]{\mathbf{L}}})\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1
by Proposition
2.3
(c).
∎

From the preceding corollary and Theorem
4.2
, we obtain Strichartz estimates for weak solutions in
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}
.

Theorem 6.10

By Corollary

\displaystyle\|L^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}C(t,0)g(x)\|_{\mathrm{L}_{t}^{p}(\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|L^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|_{2}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}_{L}},

∎

7.
Oscillatory Integral Estimate

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
6.3
, which was one of the key ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. For the convenience of the reader, we restate it here. Recall that we set for
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\{\tilde{B}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\mid\tilde{B}(t)\text{ is diagonal for all $t\in[0,1]$ and }\|\tilde{B}-{\mathrm{Id}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\}

and define for
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
the phase function

(7.1)

\varphi_{\tilde{B}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\varphi_{\tilde{B}}(\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}(\tilde{B}(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

To ease notation, we just write
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi_{\tilde{B}}
as in Section
6
.

Theorem 7.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that the following holds. For any
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported away from the origin, there exists some constant
C=C(\tilde{\psi},d)>0
such that we have the decay estimate

|\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi})(y)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
and
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

For
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
and associated phase function
\tilde{\varphi}
, let us define the hypersurface

(7.2)

\mathbb{S}\coloneqq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)=1\}.

We also define the hypersurface

(7.3)

\Sigma\coloneqq\{\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\xi\neq 0\}.

Theorem
7.1
can be reduced to decay estimates for the Fourier transform of the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}
. It is well-known that these are linked to the Gaussian curvature on
\mathbb{S}
(see e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
19
, Theorem 7.7.14]
). Therefore, we first collect some important geometrical properties of
\mathbb{S}
, see Proposition
7.2
below. We then use these to prove Theorem
7.4
below, from which Theorem
7.1
follows.

Observe that
\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)=\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(\xi)\xi
for
\xi\neq 0
, where

(7.4)

\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(\xi)\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}(\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{1}(\xi),\dots,\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}(\xi)),\quad\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{j}(\xi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{b}_{j}(\tau)}{(\tilde{B}(\tau)\xi|\xi)^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
\|\tilde{B}-{\mathrm{Id}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, it follows immediately from (
7.1
) and (
7.4
) that

(7.5)

\mathbb{S}\subseteq\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|\xi|\simeq 1\big\},\quad\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{j}(\xi)\simeq|\xi|^{-1},\quad\Sigma\subseteq\big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|\xi|\simeq 1\big\}.

Proposition 7.2

(Properties of
\mathbb{S}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, and
\mathbb{S}
and
\Sigma
as defined in (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), respectively.

(a)

\mathbb{S}
is a smooth, compact hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with normal space at each
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
given by

(7.6)

N_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})=\mathrm{span}\big\{\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)\big\}\quad(\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)\in\Sigma).

Moreover,
\mathbb{S}
is the boundary of the compact, strictly convex set

K\coloneqq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)\leq 1\}.

(b)

The Gauss map on
\mathbb{S}
given by

n\colon\mathbb{S}\to S^{d-1},\quad n(\omega)\coloneqq\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)}{|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)|}

is a diffeomorphism with
n(-\omega)=-n(\omega)
. In particular, for each
\nu_{0}\in S^{d-1}
there exists exactly one
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that
\pm\nu_{0}=n(\pm\omega_{0})
.

(c)

Let
n
be the Gauss map from (b) and
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small. Then, there exists some
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon_{0})>0
such that in each point
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
, the principal curvatures
\kappa_{1}(\omega),\dots,\kappa_{d-1}(\omega)
with respect to
-n
satisfy
\kappa_{j}(\omega)\geq\kappa
.

Proof.

The assertions in (a) are straightforward to prove (see e.g.
[
43
, Proposition 5.1]
). To prove (c), let
n
be the Gauss map as defined in (b) and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
. Recall that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{1}(\omega),\dots,\kappa_{d-1}(\omega)
with respect to
-n
are the eigenvalues of the self-adjoint shape operator

L_{\omega}\colon T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\to T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S}),\quad L_{\omega}(v)=D_{v}n(\omega)

(see e.g.
[
43
, Section I.9]
). Here,
D_{v}
denotes the directional derivative along the tangent vector
v\in T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
. Thus, we have to show that there exists some
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon_{0})\in(0,1)
such that
\kappa_{j}(\omega)~\geq~\kappa
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
. Equivalently, we need to show that the bilinear form associated with
L_{\omega}
, namely the second fundamental form in
\omega
, defined by

\mathrm{II}_{\omega}\colon T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\times T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\to\mathbb{R},\quad\mathrm{II}_{\omega}(v,\tilde{v})=(L_{\omega}v|\tilde{v}),

is positive definite uniformly in
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
. Unfortunately, the strict convexity of
K
, as shown in (a), only implies that
\mathrm{II}_{\omega}
is positive
semi
-definite. To prove strict positive definiteness, we argue by perturbation. Fix
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})\in\mathbb{S}
. A computation yields for any
v\in T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})

(7.7)

|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)|\cdot\mathrm{II}_{\omega}(v,v)=(\nabla^{2}_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)v|v)=(\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(\omega)v|v)-(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v)

with

(7.8)

\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)=(\tilde{r}_{j,k})_{j,k=1}^{d}=(r_{jk}(\omega)\omega_{j}\omega_{k})_{j,k=1}^{d},\quad r_{jk}(\omega)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{b}_{j}(\tau)\tilde{b}_{k}(\tau)}{(\tilde{B}(\tau)\omega|\omega)^{3/2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Therefore, in view of (
7.5
),

\mathrm{II}_{\omega}(v,v)\gtrsim(\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(\omega)v|v)-(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v)\gtrsim|v|^{2}-(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v).

So it is enough to show that

(7.9)

|(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v)|=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{0})\cdot|v|^{2}\quad(v\in T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})).

Put
\nu\coloneqq\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega)=\tilde{\mathsf{B}}(\omega)\omega
. Without restriction, we may assume
\nu_{d}\neq 0
. Then,
T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})=\mathrm{span}\{v_{1},\dots,v_{d-1}\}
with
v_{k}\coloneqq\nu_{d}e_{k}-\nu_{k}e_{d}
. Now, if
v\in T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
, then there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
such that
v=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\lambda_{k}v_{k}
and thus

(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v_{k}|v_{j})

with

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v_{k}|v_{j})={}

\displaystyle\big[\big(r_{jk}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}-r_{jd}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}+\big(r_{dd}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}-r_{dk}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{j}\big]\omega_{j}\omega_{k}|\omega_{d}|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\big[\big(r_{jk}-r_{jd}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}^{2}+r_{jd}\big(\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}-\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}\big]\omega_{j}\omega_{k}|\omega_{d}|^{2}

\displaystyle+{}

\displaystyle\big[\big(r_{dd}-r_{dk}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{j}\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}+r_{dk}\big(\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}-\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}\big)\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{j}\big]\omega_{j}\omega_{k}|\omega_{d}|^{2}.

By assumption, we have
\|\tilde{b}_{k}-\tilde{b}_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\tilde{b}_{k}-1\|_{\infty}+\|\tilde{b}_{j}-1\|_{\infty}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}
for all
j,k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
. Hence, since
|\omega|\simeq 1
by (
7.5
),

\displaystyle|(r_{jk}-r_{jd})(\omega)|\leq{}

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tilde{b}_{j}(\tau)}{(\tilde{B}(\tau)\omega|\omega)^{3/2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)\cdot\|\tilde{b}_{k}-\tilde{b}_{d}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

and similarly

|\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{d}(\omega)-\tilde{\mathsf{B}}_{k}(\omega)|\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(\tilde{B}(\tau)\omega|\omega)^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)\cdot\|\tilde{b}_{k}-\tilde{b}_{d}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

We conclude that
|(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v_{k}|v_{j}|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}|\omega_{j}||\omega_{k}||\omega_{d}|^{2}
and since
|\omega_{d}|\simeq|\nu_{d}|
by (
7.5
), we obtain

|(\tilde{\mathsf{R}}(\omega)v|v)|\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}|\nu_{d}|^{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}|\omega_{j}||\omega_{k}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}|\nu_{d}|^{2}|\lambda|^{2}|\omega|^{2}\simeq\varepsilon_{0}|\nu_{d}|^{2}|\lambda|^{2}\leq\varepsilon_{0}|v|^{2}

(the last inequality follows from the identity
|v|^{2}=|(\lambda|\nu^{\prime})|^{2}+|\nu_{d}|^{2}|\lambda|^{2}
, which is readily verified).
This proves (
7.9
) and therefore the claim.

Finally, we prove (b). Note that by (c),
\,\mathrm{d}n_{\omega}\colon T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\to T_{n_{\omega}}(S^{d-1})\simeq T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
has nonvanishing determinant for each
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
, so
n
is a local diffeomorphism. Also,
n(-\omega)=-n(\omega)
for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
follows immediately from the definition of
n
. Therefore, it suffices to show that
n
is bijective.

Injectivity
: Suppose that
\nu\coloneqq n(\omega_{1})=n(\omega_{2})
for some
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{S}
. By (a), the set
K
is strictly convex and
n
is the outer unit normal vector field on the boundary of
K
. Therefore, we must have
K\backslash\{\omega_{j}\}\subseteq H_{j}
, where
H_{j}
is the open supporting hyperplane at
\omega_{j}
defined by

H_{j}\coloneqq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon(\xi-\omega_{j}|\nu)<0\}\quad(j\in\{1,2\}).

But this is only possible if
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
.

Surjectivity
: Let
\nu\in S^{d-1}
. We consider the linear function

f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\quad f(\omega)=\nu\cdot\omega.

By the extreme value theorem and the compactness of
K
, there are extremal points
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in K
in which
f
attains a maximum and a minimum. The function
f
, being linear, cannot attain extremal values in the interior of
K
and thus
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
have to lie on
\partial K=\mathbb{S}
. In particular,
f(\omega_{1})
and
f(\omega_{2})
are local extremas on
\mathbb{S}
. Therefore,
\nu=\nabla_{\omega}f(\omega_{j})
must belong to
N_{\omega_{j}}(\mathbb{S})
by the Lagrange multiplier theorem. Combining this with (a), we conclude
\pm n(\omega_{1})=\nu=\pm n(\omega_{2})
. If
n(\omega_{1})=n(\omega_{2})
, then
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
by the already shown injectivity. But then
f
would have to be constant (since minimum and maximum of
f
would be equal), contradicting the fact that
f\neq 0
is linear. So either
\nu=n(\omega_{1})
or
\nu=n(\omega_{2})
, which shows that
n
is surjective.
∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\kappa=\kappa(\varepsilon_{0})
be as in Proposition
7.2
(c). Then, there are constants
c_{\kappa}
and
c_{d,\kappa}>0
such that the following holds. For all
\delta\in(0,c_{\kappa}^{-1})
and
\omega,\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{S}
with
|\omega\pm\omega_{0}|\geq\delta
, there exists some
v\in T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
with
|v|=1
and

|(v|n(\omega_{0}))|\geq c_{d,\kappa}\delta.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{S}
. Recall that the principal curvatures
\kappa_{1}(\omega),\dots,\kappa_{d-1}(\omega)
with respect to
-n
are the eigenvalues of the self-adjoint shape operator
L_{\omega}\colon T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\to T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
,
L_{\omega}=D_{v}n
. Under the identification
T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})\simeq T_{n(\omega)}(S^{d-1})
, we have
\,\mathrm{d}n_{\omega}=L_{\omega}
and thus by the inverse function theorem that
n^{-1}
is smooth with
\|\,\mathrm{d}(n^{-1})_{\nu}\|=\|(\,\mathrm{d}n_{n^{-1}(\nu)})^{-1}\|\leq\kappa^{-1}
for all
\nu\in S^{d-1}
. Thus,

|n^{-1}(\nu)-n^{-1}(\nu_{0})|\leq\|\,\mathrm{d}n^{-1}\|_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}_{S^{d-1}}(\nu,\nu_{0})\leq\tfrac{\pi}{2\kappa}|\nu-\nu_{0}|

for
\nu,\nu_{0}\in S^{d-1}
. In particular,
n^{-1}
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\tfrac{\pi}{2\kappa}
. Thus, if
\delta>0
and
\omega,\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{S}
with
|\omega\pm\omega_{0}|\geq\delta
, then

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{2\kappa\delta}{\pi}\big)^{2}\leq(\tfrac{2\kappa}{\pi}|\omega-\omega_{0}|)^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle|n(\omega)-n(\omega_{0})|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle|n(\omega)|^{2}+|n(\omega_{0})|^{2}-2(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0}))

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2\big(1-(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0}))\big),

which implies
(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0}))\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{2\kappa\delta}{\pi})^{2}
. Replacing
\omega_{0}
by
-\omega_{0}
and using that
n(-\omega_{0})=-n(\omega_{0})
, we also get
-(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0}))\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{2\kappa\delta}{\pi})^{2}
and thus

|(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0}))|\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\tfrac{2\kappa\delta}{\pi}\big)^{2}\eqqcolon r

Put
c_{\kappa}\coloneqq\sqrt{2}\pi^{-1}\kappa>0
so that
r\in(0,1)
for
\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{c_{\kappa}})
. Now choose an orthonormal basis
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d-1})
of
T_{\omega}(\mathbb{S})
. Then,

\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}|(v_{j}|n(\omega_{0}))|^{2}=|n(\omega_{0})|^{2}-|(n(\omega)|n(\omega_{0})|^{2}\geq 1-r^{2}\geq 1-r.

Thus, there must exist some
j\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
such that

|(v_{j}|n(\omega_{0}))|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{d-1}(1-r)=\tfrac{1}{2(d-1)}\big(\tfrac{2\kappa\delta}{\pi}\big)^{2}\eqqcolon c_{d,\kappa}^{2}\delta^{2},

proving the claim.
∎

Let
\tilde{\psi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be supported away from the origin. For
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\tilde{B}\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
and associated phase function
\tilde{\varphi}
as in (
6.2
), let us define for
t\in\mathbb{R}
the kernel
K_{t}
by

K_{t}(y)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\tilde{\varphi}}\tilde{\psi})(y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(y\cdot\xi-t\tilde{\varphi}(\xi))}\tilde{\psi}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\quad(y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Theorem
7.1
is then a consequence of the following result.

Theorem 7.4

(Decay Estimate for
K_{t}
)
.

Let
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
as in Proposition
7.2
(c) be sufficiently small. Then, there are constants
C_{N}=C_{N}(\tilde{\psi})>0

(N\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
such that the following estimates hold true for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and all
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

(7.10)

\big|K_{t}(y)\big|\leq C_{N}(1+|y|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}(1+\mathrm{dist}(y,t\Sigma))^{-N}.

In particular,

(7.11)

\|K_{t}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\tilde{\psi}}(1+|t|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Proof.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The claim is trivial if
t=0
, for then
K_{t}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\tilde{\psi}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t\Sigma=\{0\}
. We may therefore suppose
t\neq 0
in the following. We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1: Rescaling

Define the full phase function
\tilde{\Phi}(z,\xi)\coloneqq z\cdot\xi-\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)
for
(z,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,

(7.12)

K_{t}(y)={}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\tilde{\Phi}\big(\frac{y}{t},\,\xi\big)}\tilde{\psi}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\eqqcolon\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}I\big(t,\tfrac{y}{t}\big),

where

I(\lambda,z)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda\tilde{\Phi}(z,\xi)}\tilde{\psi}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\quad(z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}),

and (
7.10
) would follow from the uniform estimates

(7.13)

\big|I(\lambda,z)\big|\lesssim_{N,\tilde{\psi}}(1+|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}(1+|\lambda|\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma))^{-N}\quad(z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\lambda\in\mathbb{R}).

This is what we will show next.

Step 2 : The Proof of Estimate (
7.13
)

We deal first with the easier case
z=0
and then proceed with the case
z\neq 0
.

Case 1
:
z=0
.

Put
\delta\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(0,\Sigma)>0
. Then,
\delta\simeq 1
by (
7.5
). Now, observe that

(7.14)

|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\Phi}(0,\xi)|=|0-\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)|\geq\mathrm{dist}(0,\Sigma)=\delta\simeq 1

and that by the positive homogeneity of
\tilde{\varphi}
, we have for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\geq 2

(7.15)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\tilde{\Phi}(y,\xi)|=|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\tilde{\varphi}(\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha}|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}\lesssim 1\quad\text{on $\mathrm{supp}(\tilde{\psi})$}.

Therefore, nonstationary phase (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 7.7.1]
) implies

|I(\lambda,0)|\lesssim_{N,d,\tilde{\psi}}(1+|\lambda|)^{-N}\lesssim(1+|\lambda|\,\delta)^{-N}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{R})

which is exactly (
7.13
).

Case 2
:
z\neq 0
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and set
\nu_{z}\coloneqq\frac{z}{|z|}\in S^{d-1}
. Using
[
9
, Theorem 3.11]
(see also
[
30
, Proposition A.0.1 (iv)]
), we may integrate the integrand of
I(\lambda,z)
along the level sets of
\tilde{\varphi}
. This gives

I(\lambda,z)={}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda}\bigg(\int_{\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(r)}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\lambda z\cdot\omega}\tilde{\psi}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\,\mathrm{d}\omega\coloneqq|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}|^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
(the latter denoting the
d-1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on the hypersurface
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(r)
). Since
\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(r)=r\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}(1)=r\mathbb{S}
by the positive homogeneity of
\tilde{\varphi}
, the integral in the above display is equal to

\displaystyle I(\lambda,z)={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}r\lambda z\cdot\omega}\tilde{\psi}(r\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigg)r^{d-1}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda}J(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,

where
\tilde{\chi}(r)\coloneqq\chi(r)r^{d-1}
and
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
such that
\chi=1
on
\mathrm{supp}(r\mapsto\tilde{\psi}(r\omega))
, and for fixed
r>0
,

J(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mu\nu\cdot\omega}\psi(r\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\quad(\mu\in\mathbb{R},\nu\in S^{d-1}).

By Proposition
7.2
(b), there is exactly one
\omega_{z}\in\mathbb{S}
such that
\pm\nu_{z}=n(\pm\omega_{z})
. We localize
J(\mu,\nu_{z})
around
\pm\omega_{z}
: To this end, we choose
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\rho=1
on
(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2})
,
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq(-1,1)
and for some
\delta>0
, we split
J(\mu,\nu_{z})={}J_{1}(\mu,\nu_{z})+J_{2}(\mu,\nu_{z})+J_{3}(\mu,\nu_{z})
, where

\displaystyle J_{1}(\mu,\nu_{z})\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mu\nu_{z}\cdot\omega}\rho\big(\tfrac{|\omega-\omega_{z}|}{\delta}\big)\psi(r\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

\displaystyle J_{2}(\mu,\nu_{z})\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mu\nu_{z}\cdot\omega}\rho\big(\tfrac{|\omega+\omega_{z}|}{\delta}\big)\psi(r\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

\displaystyle J_{3}(\mu,\nu_{z})\coloneqq{}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mu\nu_{z}\cdot\omega}\psi(r\omega)\bigg(1-\rho\big(\tfrac{|\omega-\omega_{z}|}{\delta}+\rho\big(\tfrac{|\omega+\omega_{z}|}{\delta}\big)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

Then, we obtain the splitting
I(\lambda,z)=I_{1}(\lambda,z)+I_{2}(\lambda,z)+I_{3}(\lambda,z)
with

I_{k}(\lambda,z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda}J_{k}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r

for
k=\{1,2,3\}
. We estimate each of these terms separately. Let
k\in\{1,2\}
. Note that by Euler’s relation for
\tilde{\varphi}
,

\displaystyle\nu_{z}\cdot\omega_{z}=n(\omega_{z})\cdot\omega_{z}=\frac{\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})\cdot\omega_{z}}{|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})|}=\frac{\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})}{|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})|}=\frac{1}{|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})|}\eqqcolon\frac{1}{\sigma}

and therefore

I_{k}(\lambda,z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda(1-\frac{|z|}{\sigma})}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda\frac{|z|}{\sigma}}J_{k}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Hence, integrating by parts and applying stationary phase (see
[
19
, Theorem 7.7.5, Theorem 7.7.14]
) with
\delta=\delta(\kappa(\varepsilon_{0}))>0
sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\big(\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big)\big)^{N}I_{k}(\lambda,z)\big|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg((-D_{r})^{N}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda(1-\frac{|z|}{\sigma})}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda\frac{|z|}{\sigma}}J_{k}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda(1-\frac{|z|}{\sigma})}D_{r}^{N}\bigg(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\frac{\lambda|z|}{\sigma}}J_{k}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg|D_{r}^{N}\bigg(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda\frac{|z|}{\sigma}}J_{k}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\bigg)\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+r|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}|\partial_{r}^{N-\ell}\tilde{\chi}(r)|\,\mathrm{d}r\lesssim_{N,\tilde{\psi}}(1+|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}.

This yields

|I_{k}(\lambda,z)|\lesssim_{N,\tilde{\psi}}(1+|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\big(1+\big|\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big)\big|\big)^{-N}

for
k\in\{1,2\}
. For
k=3
, we use the principle of nonstationary phase. Indeed, Lemma
7.3
implies (for
\delta<c_{\kappa}^{-1}
, which we may assume without restriction) that
f\colon\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{R},

f(\omega)=\nu_{z}\cdot\omega
satisfies

\|\,\mathrm{d}f_{\omega}\|\geq c_{d,\kappa}\,\delta

uniformly
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\omega_{z}\in\mathbb{S}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}\setminus(B_{\delta}(\omega_{z})\cup B_{\delta}(-\omega_{z}))
. Thus, by nonstationary phase (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 7.7.1]
),

|D_{r}^{\ell}J_{3}(\mu,\nu_{z})|\lesssim_{\ell,M}(1+|\mu|)^{-M}\quad(M,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}).

Following the above argument in the case
k\in\{1,2\}
, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle\big|\big(\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big)\big)^{N}I_{3}(\lambda,z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg|D_{r}^{N}\bigg(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda\frac{|z|}{\sigma}}J_{3}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\tilde{\chi}(r)\bigg)\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{N,d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},\\
|\ell|=N\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|D_{r}^{\ell_{1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}r\lambda\frac{|z|}{\sigma}}\big|\cdot\big|D_{r}^{\ell_{2}}J_{3}(r\lambda|z|,\nu_{z})\big|\cdot\big|D_{r}^{\ell_{3}}\tilde{\chi}(r)\big|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{N,M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3},\\
|\ell|=N\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\lambda z|^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}(1+r|\lambda z|)^{-M}\cdot\big|D_{r}^{\ell_{3}}(\tilde{\chi}(r))\big|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle(1+|\lambda z|)^{-(M-N)}.

Choosing
M=2N
gives

|I_{3}(\lambda,z)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|\lambda z|)^{-N}\big(1+\big|\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big)\big|\big)^{-N}.

Since
|I(\lambda,z)|\leq|I_{1}(\lambda,z)|+|I_{2}(\lambda,z)|+|I_{3}(\lambda,z)|
, we have proved that

|I(\lambda,z)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\big(1+\big|\lambda\big(1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big)\big|\big)^{-N}.

Now it just remains to recall that
\sigma=|\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})|\simeq 1
by (
7.5
) as well as
z=\frac{|z|}{\sigma}\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})
, which gives

\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma)\leq\big|z-\nabla_{\xi}\tilde{\varphi}(\omega_{z})\big|=\sigma\big|1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big|\simeq\big|1-\tfrac{|z|}{\sigma}\big|.

This proves (
7.13
) as desired.

Step 3 : The Proof of (
7.10
)

Recall from (
7.5
) that

(7.16)

\Sigma\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\tilde{c}_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq\tilde{c}_{2}\}

for some
\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2}>0
.
Therefore, we deduce from (
7.13
)
the uniform bounds

(7.17)

|I(\lambda,z)|\lesssim(1+|\lambda|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Indeed, if
\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma)\leq\tfrac{\tilde{c}_{1}}{2}
, then (
7.16
) implies
|z|\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}}{2}
, so (
7.13
) with
N=0
yields

|I(\lambda,z)|\lesssim(1+|\lambda z|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\lesssim_{\tilde{c}_{1},d}(1+|\lambda|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

On the other hand, if
\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma)\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}}{2}
, then choosing some
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N\geq\frac{d-1}{2}
in (
7.13
) yields

|I(\lambda,z)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|\lambda|\mathrm{dist}(z,\Sigma))^{-N}\lesssim_{\tilde{c}_{1},N}(1+|\lambda|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\quad(\lambda\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This proves (
7.17
). Now, (
7.10
) is an immediate consequence of (
7.17
) in view of (
7.12
). The proof is complete.
∎
