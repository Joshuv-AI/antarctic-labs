Split Tallies: A Discrete Certificate Calculus for Auditing Dynamic Ordered Sets in Constant Memory
Faruk Alpay, Levent Sarıoğlu

Abstract
A dynamic ordered set is maintained by one party and consulted by another. The maintainer executes insertions, deletions, membership tests, predecessor and successor queries, and extremum queries, and announces an answer for each; the answers are used immediately and verified never. We ask how little an auditor needs in order to certify, after the fact, that every announced answer was correct. The auditor we construct is passive: it watches the operation–answer stream, requires the maintainer to append a constant number of machine words per operation to a public, append-only tally , and keeps a private stock of five words and a flag. At audit time the maintainer discloses its claimed live intervals; the auditor accepts or rejects in one pass. Honest maintainers are accepted with probability one. A maintainer that misreported even a single answer among T T operations is accepted with probability at most ( 4 ​ T + 1 ) / p (4T+1)/p over the auditor’s one secret field element, where p p may be taken as a 61 61 -bit prime, with no assumption on the maintainer’s computational power. The certificate calculus rewrites the gaps of the ordered set—its maximal vacant intervals—under an indenture discipline that timestamps every gap by a public counter. A discrete normal-form theorem identifies these gaps with the unique unit path in the interval acyclic graph, equivalently with an incidence vector of boundary 𝟏 0 − 𝟏 U + 1 \mathbf{1}_{0}-\mathbf{1}_{U+1} ; insertions and deletions are then exactly the one-vertex refinements and coarsenings of that path. Both pillars of the construction are provably indispensable: any deterministic auditor with end-of-session soundness must keep at least 2 ​ n − log 2 ⁡ ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) 2n-\log_{2}(2n+1) bits of state, the same floor binds every perfectly complete auditor whose coin tosses are visible to the maintainer, and removing the timestamp rule admits an explicit three-operation forgery that achieves exact balance and is accepted with probability one. A leaf-oriented ( 2 , 4 ) (2,4) -tree realizes the maintainer’s side in O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) worst-case time and one extra word per element, and a potential-function argument shows that its splits, fusions, shares, and root events number at most 2 ​ m 2m over any m m updates—a bound the auditor itself can certify by counting one-word attestations. Audits may be placed at arbitrary checkpoints at cost linear in the current size, and accepted epochs compose with additive error. All constants are explicit, and a complete six-operation session, together with its forged twin, is worked to the last residue. Keywords: dynamic ordered sets; transcript auditing; fingerprinting; interval decompositions; incidence vectors; amortized analysis; ( 2 , 4 ) (2,4) -trees; lower bounds.



1
Introduction

A growing share of the data structures that programs rely on are no longer
executed where they are trusted. The ordered dictionary at the heart of an
index, a scheduler, or a routing table may live in a procured library, behind
a remote interface, on a rented machine, or inside an accelerated component
whose internals are opaque to its caller. The caller issues operations,
receives answers, and acts on them at once. Correctness, when it is
established at all, is established by testing the component in isolation—a
guarantee about
some
executions, not about
this
one.

This paper develops the opposite guarantee for the dynamic ordered set: a
certificate that travels with the execution itself. We place between the
maintainer of the structure and the consumer of its answers a third party of
deliberately minimal power, the
auditor
. The auditor never touches the
structure, never sends a message, and stores five machine words and a flag.
It watches the stream of operations and announced answers, and it obliges the
maintainer to append, for each operation, a short record—a

notch
—to a public, append-only sequence we call the
tally
.
At a moment of the auditor’s choosing the maintainer must
disclose
the
intervals it claims are currently vacant, and the auditor renders a verdict.
If every announced answer was correct, the verdict is
accept
, always.
If even one answer was wrong, the verdict is
reject
, except with
probability at most
(4T+1)/p
over a single field element the auditor drew in
private at the start; with a
61
-bit prime this is below
2^{-38}
for any
session of up to a million operations. No bound is assumed on the
maintainer’s computational resources, and no key, signature, or hardness
assumption appears anywhere in the construction.

The design borrows its vocabulary, and its architecture, from a very old
auditing instrument. For roughly six centuries the English Exchequer recorded
debts on wooden tally sticks: the amount was cut as notches across the stick,
the stick was split lengthwise through the notches, the creditor kept one
half—the
stock
—and the debtor the other—the
foil
—and at
settlement the halves were laid together; only the genuine counterpart would
mesh, grain and notch, with its twin (see, e.g.,
Jenkinson
[
12
]
and Baxter
[
3
]
). The scheme
below is a split tally for order semantics. The tally is the public notch
stream; the foil is the bookkeeping the maintainer carries inside its
structure; the stock is the auditor’s five private words; the audit is the
meshing of the halves. None of the parts is useful alone, and a forged half,
we prove, meshes with probability at most
(4T+1)/p
.

1.1
What is certified, and against whom

The object under audit is a set
S
over a finite ordered universe,
manipulated through a totalized interface:
insert
,
delete
,

member
,
pred
,
succ
,
min
,
max
, each returning an
answer whose correct value is a function of the operation sequence alone
(Section
2
). The maintainer is adversarial in the strongest
sense: it may answer and notch arbitrarily, adaptively, with unbounded
computation, and it may even choose the operation sequence itself. The
auditor is passive and silent until the audit; in particular the maintainer
learns nothing about the auditor’s secret during the run. Soundness is
retrospective: a false answer is not interrupted at the instant it is uttered,
but it is, with the stated probability, exposed at the audit, and audits may
be placed as densely as desired (Section
9
).

The technical core is a calculus of
gaps
. A set
S
over the
augmented universe partitions the order into
|S|+1
maximal vacant
intervals, and every interface operation acts on this family by a local
rewrite: an insertion splits one gap in two, a deletion merges two adjacent
gaps into one, and every query is answered by exhibiting a single gap
(Section
3
). Section
4
isolates the
underlying discrete object: the current gap family is the unique directed
unit path from
0
to
U+1
in the interval acyclic graph, and the split and
merge rules are precisely its one-vertex refinements and coarsenings. The
auditor therefore certifies not the set but the evolving path of gaps. It
does so with two product accumulators in a prime field—one absorbing every
gap
birth
, one absorbing every gap
consumption
—and a public
counter that stamps each birth with a fresh
code
. The citation rules, which we call the
indenture
discipline
, force every operation to consume the gaps it rewrites, citing
their codes, and force every answer to be the unique value determined by the
citation (Section
5
). Acceptance reduces to one equality
between accumulators, and the soundness analysis to unique factorization in

\mathbb{F}_{p}[z]
(Section
6
).

Mechanism design of this kind earns its keep only if its parts are necessary,
so Section
7
is devoted to impossibility. First, no
deterministic auditor can do this job in small space: end-of-session
soundness for sets of size
n
over a universe of size
2n
forces at least

\log_{2}\binom{2n}{n}\geq 2n-\log_{2}(2n+1)
bits of auditor state
(Theorem
7.2
), so randomness is not a convenience but a
requirement. Second, the randomness must be
hidden
: every perfectly
complete auditor whose coins are visible to the maintainer inherits the same
floor, with soundness error one below it
(Corollary
7.3
). Third, the timestamp rule is load-bearing on
its own: if the auditor checks everything else but accepts citations of
not-yet-issued codes, an explicit three-operation strategy forges a
membership answer, restores
exact
multiset balance—not merely a
collision—and is accepted with probability one; the same example shows that
end-of-session meshing cannot substitute for the temporal rule
(Proposition
7.4
).

The maintainer’s side must also be realizable without penalty, and
Section
8
supplies a concrete one: a leaf-oriented

(2,4)
-tree whose leaves are doubly linked and carry one extra word each—the code of the gap to the right. Every operation runs in
O(\log n)

worst-case time, emits its notch in
O(1)
additional work, and never touches
a code during rebalancing. The rebalancing itself is then made auditable:
we prove, by an explicit potential function with constants

\varphi(1{:}5)=(3,1,0,2,4)
, that splits, fusions, shares, and root events
number at most
2m
over any
m
updates from an empty structure
(Theorem
8.2
). If the maintainer appends a one-word

attestation
per structural event, the auditor certifies the
amortized-cost claim by counting—amortization with a public envelope.
Section
9
composes audits into epochs with halt-on-reject
semantics and additive error, and Section
10
executes a full
six-operation session and its forged twin numerically, every residue shown.

1.2
Relation to prior work

The fingerprinting mechanics descend directly from the offline memory
checkers of Blum, Evans, Gemmell, Kannan, and Naor
[
5
]
, who
certified a random-access memory—an address-to-value map—with an

\varepsilon
-biased multiset hash and write timestamps, in the
program-checking tradition of Blum and Kannan
[
6
]
. We
transplant the invariant from address semantics to
order
semantics.
The transplant is not a relabeling: a memory checker certifies that the value
read at an address is the value last written there, whereas the queries
here—absence with its bracketing neighbors, predecessor, successor,
extrema—are properties of the
arrangement
of keys, not of any stored
cell. The gap calculus of Section
3
is what converts
arrangement into a checkable ledger: adjacency becomes an object that can be
born, cited, and consumed, the unique-citation arguments of
Section
6
replace last-write reasoning, and the answers
themselves become forced functions of the citations. On top of this sit
results with no memory-checking counterpart: the deterministic and
visible-coin floors of Section
7
, the balance-restoring
replay forgery, the audited rebalancing envelope, and closed-form constants
throughout.

Certifying algorithms
[
13
]
ask a program to emit a witness that
a
single
output is correct, checkable by a simple verifier; the LEDA
system
[
14
]
demonstrated the practice at scale. The
present setting is the dynamic analogue with an adversarial prover: thousands
of interleaved updates and queries, a verifier that may not retain the
structure, and witnesses that must compose across time. Authenticated data
structures in the Merkle tradition
[
15
,
20
]
solve a
different problem—public verifiability of
individual
answers against
a published digest—and pay for it with cryptographic assumptions and
logarithmic-size proofs per query; here proofs are constant-size, the
guarantee is information-theoretic, and the verdict covers the entire history
at once. Goodrich, Kornaropoulos, Mitzenmacher, and
Tamassia
[
10
]
use the word
audit
for a related but
distinct forensic goal: reconstructing what an intruder learned from a
structure, rather than certifying the structure’s answers to its user.

In the annotated-data-stream model of Chakrabarti, Cormode, McGregor, and
Thaler
[
7
]
, a prover annotates a stream so that a small-space
verifier can compute a function of it, and strong lower bounds trade
annotation length against verifier space for
online
soundness. Our
auditor escapes those trade-offs by design, not by contradiction: detection
here is retrospective, settled at the audit rather than at the moment of each
answer. Whether small-state
online
checking is possible at all
without cryptography is precisely the question Naor and
Rothblum
[
16
]
tied to the existence of one-way functions,
with the trade-offs sharpened by Dwork, Naor, Rothblum, and
Vaikuntanathan
[
9
]
; Section
11
states the exact
boundary this places on any strengthening of the present results. The
algebraic identity test underlying the audit is the classical one of
Schwartz, Zippel, and DeMillo–Lipton
[
18
,
23
,
8
]
,
and the multiset-equality use of a secret evaluation point is in the lineage
of Wegman and Carter
[
22
]
. On the maintainer’s side, the

(2,4)
-tree is the classical small-order B-tree
[
4
,
1
]
,
its constant amortized rebalancing was established by Huddleston and
Mehlhorn
[
11
]
, and our envelope theorem packages that
phenomenon, with the credit method of
[
21
,
19
]
, into a
form an external counter can certify; skip lists
[
17
]
would serve as
an alternative maintainer with expected bounds. Kinetic data
structures
[
2
]
also maintain a family of certificates alongside a
structure, but theirs expire with the motion of the data and trigger repairs,
whereas an indentured gap is consumed only by the operation that rewrites it.

The model

2.1
States, operations, answers

Fix an integer
U\geq 1
and let the universe be

\mathcal{U}=\{1,2,\dots,U\}
with the natural order. Augment it with two

0

U+1

0<1<\dots<U<U+1

is a subset

is a function of the state, hence of the operation prefix.

Definition 2.1

(Operation semantics)

Let

•

\textsf{mem}(x)

•

\textsf{pred}(x)

\max\{s\in S:s<x\}

•

\textsf{succ}(x)

No operation other than a fresh insertion or an effective deletion changes

A

We write

2.2
Parties, streams, words

Three parties appear. The

holds whatever private data it likes, announces an
answer for every operation, and appends to a public append-only sequence,
the

passively reads the merged stream of triples

(o_{t},\textit{answer}_{t},\textit{notch}_{t})
and keeps a private state; it
sends no messages during the session. At the audit the maintainer appends a
final notch sequence, the

, and the auditor outputs

We work in the word model: a

holds an integer of magnitude

For soundness the adversary is given every power short of clairvoyance: a

may choose the operations themselves, the answers,
the notches, and the disclosure, adaptively as a function of the entire
visible history. The auditor’s only secret is one field element
z

uniformly before the session and never used in any visible action until the
final verdict; consequently the realized transcript is a random variable
independent of
z
. (Mid-session, the auditor’s flag can change only through
checks that do not mention
z

chained audits, in Sections
6
and
9
.)

3
The gap calculus

Order semantics becomes checkable once the set is replaced by the family of
its maximal vacant intervals.

Definition 3.1

(Gaps)
.

For a state
S\subseteq\mathcal{U}
let
\bar{S}=S\cup\{0,U+1\}
. A
gap
of

S
is a pair
\langle a,b\rangle
with
a,b\in\bar{S}
,
a<b
, and

(a,b)\cap S=\emptyset
; equivalently,
a
and
b
are consecutive in

\bar{S}
. Write
\Gamma(S)
for the set of gaps of
S
.

Definition 3.2

(Interlocking chain)
.

A finite set
G
of pairs over
\{0,\dots,U+1\}
is an
interlocking
chain
if, listing its pairs as

\langle a_{1},b_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle a_{k},b_{k}\rangle
in increasing order of left endpoint,
one has
a_{1}=0
,
b_{i}=a_{i+1}
for
1\leq i<k
,
b_{k}=U+1
, and
a_{i}<b_{i}
for
all
i
. We then say the pairs
mesh
.

Lemma 3.3

(Tiling)
.

(i)
|\Gamma(S)|=|S|+1
for every state
S
.
(ii) A set of pairs equals
\Gamma(S)
for some state
S
if and only if it is
an interlocking chain.
(iii) The map
S\mapsto\Gamma(S)
is a bijection from states to interlocking
chains; its inverse reads off the interior endpoints:

S=\{a_{2},\dots,a_{k}\}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{k-1}\}
.

Proof.

List
\bar{S}
as
0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{n}<s_{n+1}=U+1
where
n=|S|
. By
Definition
3.1
,
\Gamma(S)=\{\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle:0\leq i\leq n\}
,
which has
n+1
members and is an interlocking chain; this proves (i) and one
direction of (ii). Conversely, an interlocking chain

\langle a_{1},b_{1}\rangle,\dots,\langle a_{k},b_{k}\rangle
determines

S=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{k-1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
(interior endpoints are neither
0

nor
U+1
because endpoints strictly increase along the chain), and the chain
of consecutive pairs of
\bar{S}
is exactly the given one. The two
constructions invert each other, giving (iii).
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Rewrites)
.

Let
S
be a state and
x\in\mathcal{U}
.

(i)

If
x\notin S
there is a unique gap
\langle a,b\rangle\in\Gamma(S)
with

a<x<b
, and

\Gamma(S\cup\{x\})=\bigl(\Gamma(S)\setminus\{\langle a,b\rangle\}\bigr)\cup\{\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle\}
.

(ii)

If
x\in S
there are unique gaps
\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle\in\Gamma(S)
,
and

\Gamma(S\setminus\{x\})=\bigl(\Gamma(S)\setminus\{\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle\}\bigr)\cup\{\langle a,b\rangle\}
.

Proof.

Immediate from the description
\Gamma(S)=\{\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle\}
in the proof
of Lemma
3.3
: adjoining
x
refines exactly the consecutive pair
that straddles it; removing
x
coalesces exactly the two pairs incident to
it.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Query characterizations)
.

Let
S
be a state and
x\in\mathcal{U}
.

(a)

x\in S
iff some gap of
S
has left endpoint
x
, iff some gap of
S

has right endpoint
x
.
x\notin S
iff some gap
\langle a,b\rangle
of
S
has

a<x<b
.

(b)

There is exactly one gap
\langle a,b\rangle\in\Gamma(S)
with
a<x\leq b
, and
for it
a=\max\{s\in S:s<x\}
when that set is nonempty, while
a=0
exactly
when it is empty. Symmetrically, exactly one gap satisfies
a\leq x<b
, and
its right endpoint is the correct answer of
\textsf{succ}(x)
, with
b=U+1

exactly when no successor exists.

(c)

Exactly one gap has left endpoint
0
, namely
\langle 0,\min S\rangle
if

S\neq\emptyset
and
\langle 0,U+1\rangle
otherwise; symmetrically for right
endpoint
U+1
and
\max S
.

Proof.

All three parts read off the sorted listing

0=s_{0}<\dots<s_{n+1}=U+1
. For (b): the left endpoints

s_{0}<s_{1}<\dots<s_{n}
are distinct, and
s_{i}<x\leq s_{i+1}
holds for exactly
one
i
, namely
i=\max\{j:s_{j}<x\}
; then
s_{i}
is the largest member of

\bar{S}
below
x
, which is the largest member of
S
below
x
unless that
set is empty, in which case
s_{i}=0
. Part (a) restates consecutiveness:
an interior point of a gap is in no state, and an element of
S
is the right
endpoint of one gap and the left endpoint of the next. Part (c) is the case

at the boundary:
s_{0}=0
begins exactly one gap and
s_{n+1}=U+1
ends

∎

of Definition

Corollary 3.6

(Traffic)
.

Under the discipline of Section
5
, every operation
consumes at most two gaps and creates at most two; over a session of
T

2T+1
gaps are ever created (including the initial gap
of the empty state) and at most

4
Combinatorial normal forms

The preceding lemmas already determine the protocol, but the discrete
structure behind them is useful to state explicitly. The gaps of a set are
not an arbitrary collection of intervals: they are a unit path in a finite
acyclic interval graph. This section records the path normal form and the
uniqueness of the two update rewrites. These statements are the part of the
paper that belongs most directly to discrete mathematics; the field
fingerprint of Section
5
is only a compact way to audit
this finite path calculus.

Definition 4.1

(Interval graph and boundary)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{U}
be the directed acyclic graph with vertex set

\{0,1,\dots,U+1\}
and one directed edge
a\to b
for every
a<b
.
We identify the edge
a\to b
with the interval
\langle a,b\rangle
. For a finite
set
H
of such edges define its discrete boundary

\partial H(v)=\operatorname{out}_{H}(v)-\operatorname{in}_{H}(v),\qquad v=0,1,\dots,U+1.

The support
\operatorname{supp}(H)
is the set of endpoints incident to some edge of
H
.

Proposition 4.2

(Incidence characterization of chains)
.

For a finite set
H
of intervals over
\{0,\dots,U+1\}
, the following are
equivalent.

(i)

H
is an interlocking chain from
0
to
U+1
.

(ii)

H
is a
\{0,1\}
-valued unit flow in
\mathcal{I}_{U}
with boundary

\partial H=\mathbf{1}_{0}-\mathbf{1}_{U+1}.

Equivalently,
\partial H(0)=1
,
\partial H(U+1)=-1
, and

\partial H(v)=0
for every interior vertex
v
.

Consequently, checking that a disclosed family meshes is the same as checking
one finite incidence-vector identity in the interval acyclic graph.

Proof.

If
H=\{\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle:0\leq i\leq k-1\}
with

0=s_{0}<\cdots<s_{k}=U+1
, then
0
has one outgoing and no incoming edge,

U+1
has one incoming and no outgoing edge, and every intermediate
s_{i}
has
one incoming and one outgoing edge; vertices not in the support have neither.
Thus (i) implies (ii).

Conversely assume (ii). Regard the indicator of
H
as an integral flow of
value one from
0
to
U+1
. Because all edges of
\mathcal{I}_{U}
point from smaller
to larger vertices, the induced graph is acyclic. Starting at
0
, there is
one outgoing unit of flow; following any positive-flow edge and using
conservation at each interior vertex must eventually reach
U+1
, since an
acyclic walk cannot continue forever and no other vertex has negative
boundary. This gives a directed
0
-to-
U+1
path
P
contained in
H
.
Subtract the indicator of
P
from the indicator of
H
. The remaining edge
set, if nonempty, would be a nonnegative integral circulation in an acyclic
directed graph: its boundary is zero at every vertex. But any nonempty
finite acyclic directed graph has a smallest vertex incident to an edge with
no incoming remaining edge; at that vertex the remaining boundary is positive,
a contradiction. Hence no edge remains, so
H=P
, an interlocking chain.
∎

Theorem 4.3

(One-vertex normal form)
.

Let
C
be an interlocking chain and let
V(C)
be its ordered endpoint set.

(a)

If
x\notin V(C)
, there is a unique interlocking chain with endpoint
set
V(C)\cup\{x\}
: if
\langle a,b\rangle
is the unique edge of
C
with

a<x<b
, it is

C^{+}_{x}=(C\setminus\{\langle a,b\rangle\})\cup\{\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle\}.

(b)

If
x\in V(C)\setminus\{0,U+1\}
, there is a unique interlocking
chain with endpoint set
V(C)\setminus\{x\}
: if

\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle
are the two edges of
C
incident to
x
, it is

C^{-}_{x}=(C\setminus\{\langle a,x\rangle,\langle x,b\rangle\})\cup\{\langle a,b\rangle\}.

Thus insertion and deletion are not merely local operations that happen to
work; they are the only possible one-key refinements and coarsenings of the
chain normal form.

Proof.

Write the endpoint set of
C
as

0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{k}=U+1
. If
x\notin V(C)
, there is a unique index

i
with
s_{i}<x<s_{i+1}
; any chain whose endpoints are

V(C)\cup\{x\}
must connect consecutive endpoints in their sorted order,
so the only changed edge is

\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle
, replaced by

\langle s_{i},x\rangle
and
\langle x,s_{i+1}\rangle
. The deletion case is the same
argument with
x=s_{i}
for some
0<i<k
: consecutive endpoints on the two
sides of
x
must become consecutive after
x
is removed. Uniqueness also
follows from Proposition
4.2
, because the prescribed
endpoint set admits only one unit path.
∎

Corollary 4.4

(Uniqueness of local witnesses)
.

For every state
S
and every operation of Definition
2.1
,
the gap or gap pair that can justify the correct branch is unique. Moreover,
a state-preserving query can only cite the unique chain edge selected by the
corresponding inequality in Lemma
3.5
; an effective update can
only perform the refinement or coarsening of Theorem
4.3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
,
\Gamma(S)
is the unit path whose interior
vertices are exactly
S
. Membership in
S
is therefore endpoint membership
in that path, absence is strict containment in one path edge, predecessor and
successor are the two one-sided containment queries of the same ordered path,
and extrema are the two sentinel-anchored edges. The uniqueness statements
are exactly Lemma
3.5
plus the path-refinement normal form of
Theorem
4.3
.
∎

5
The indenture discipline

The auditor cannot afford to remember the gap family, so it remembers a
fingerprint of its
history
: every gap, when created, is stamped with a
fresh code; every operation must
cite
the codes of the gaps it
rewrites or reads; and the two sides of the ledger—births and
consumptions—are folded into two field accumulators. This section defines
the discipline purely behaviorally; no structure that might implement it is
mentioned, and none needs to be. Any maintainer whose notches obey the rules
is treated identically.

5.1
Codes and the public clock

The auditor keeps a counter
\kappa
, initially
0
. Whenever a gap is
created—we say
born
—it receives the current value of
\kappa
as its

code
, and
\kappa
increments. Before the first operation, the gap

\langle 0,U+1\rangle
of the empty state is born with code
0
. Within an event,
all cited codes are checked first, against the value
\kappa_{\mathrm{start}}

that
\kappa
had when the event began; births then occur in the fixed order
prescribed by the matching rule below. An
indentured gap
is a triple

\langle a,b\rangle@{c}
: a gap together with its code.

Lemma 5.1

(Public clock)
.

The code assigned to every birth is a deterministic function of the public
transcript prefix. Consequently any party reading the tally—in particular
a maintainer—can reproduce all code assignments exactly, with
O(1)

bookkeeping per event and no communication from the auditor.

Proof.

The counter starts at a fixed value, increments only at births, and the
number and order of births in each event are fixed by the operation and the
notch shape, both public.
∎

5.2
Encoding

Codes, endpoints, and gaps are folded into a prime field through one
injective map.

Definition 5.2

(Encoding)
.

Fix a prime
p
. For
a,b\in\{0,\dots,U+1\}
and a code
c\geq 0
define

\nu(a,b,c)\;=\;1+c\,(U+2)^{2}+a\,(U+2)+b\pmod{p}.

Lemma 5.3

(Injectivity)
.

If
p>(2T+2)(U+2)^{2}
then
\nu
is injective on

\{0,\dots,U+1\}^{2}\times\{0,\dots,2T+1\}
, which contains every triple that
can arise in a session of
T
operations.

Proof.

For
a,b\leq U+1
one has
0\leq a(U+2)+b\leq(U+1)(U+2)+(U+1)=(U+1)(U+3)<(U+2)^{2}
, so the integer
1+c(U+2)^{2}+a(U+2)+b
determines
c
as a quotient
and
(a,b)
as a remainder: distinct triples give distinct integers. All
such integers lie in
[1,(2T+2)(U+2)^{2}]\subseteq[1,p-1]
, so reduction
modulo
p
identifies nothing. Corollary
3.6
bounds codes by

2T+1
.
∎

Remark 5.4

(A concrete instantiation)
.

On a
64
-bit machine take
p=2^{61}-1
. Then
Lemma
5.3
holds whenever
(2T+2)(U+2)^{2}<2^{61}-1
, e.g. for
every
U\leq 10^{6}
and
T\leq 10^{6}
simultaneously, and every quantity in the
scheme fits in one word with single-word modular arithmetic. The choice is
parametric: nothing below depends on it beyond the inequality.

6
The audit theorem

Two deterministic lemmas carry the weight: balance forces every citation to
be
live
, and live citations force every answer to be correct. The
field then converts balance into one probable equality.

Definition 6.1

(Ledger)
.

Fix a well-notched transcript. For
0\leq t\leq T
let
\mathcal{L}_{t}
be the
multiset of indentured gaps born in events
0,\dots,t
(event
0
being the
initial birth) minus those consumed in events
1,\dots,t
. A citation at
event
t
is
live
if the cited triple belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{t-1}
.

Lemma 6.2

(Liveness)
.

In a well-notched, balanced transcript:

(a)

\mathcal{B}
is a set: no two births share a code, and each code
determines its gap.

(b)

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{B}
is likewise a set; in particular no indentured gap
is consumed twice, during the run or at disclosure.

(c)

Every citation in the run is live, and the disclosure equals

\mathcal{L}_{T}
as a set.

Proof.

(a) Codes are assigned by a strictly increasing counter, one per birth, and
the gap of a birth is fixed at the moment the code is issued. (b) Balance
says the multisets are equal; a multiset equal to a set is a set. Thus each
code appears exactly once on each side, and the matched pairs carry the same
gap by (a).

(c) Consider a citation of
\langle a,b\rangle@{c}
at event
t
, during the run or
at disclosure. By (b) there is exactly one birth with code
c
, and it is the
birth of the very gap
\langle a,b\rangle
. By the temporal rule
(during the run) or the disclosure code bound (at the audit), the birth of
code
c
occurred strictly before the citing event began, so the triple
entered the ledger before event
t
. By (b) it is consumed exactly once in
the whole transcript, namely here; hence it had not been consumed earlier and

\langle a,b\rangle@{c}\in\mathcal{L}_{t-1}
. For the final claim:

\mathcal{L}_{T}=\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{run}}
, and balance gives

\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{run}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{disc}}
, the disclosed
multiset.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Forcing)
.

In a well-notched, balanced transcript, for every
0\leq t\leq T
the gaps of
the ledger are exactly the gaps of the true state,

\{\langle a,b\rangle:\langle a,b\rangle@{c}\in\mathcal{L}_{t}\}\;=\;\Gamma(S_{t})\quad\text{(with all multiplicities equal to one),}

and every announced answer in the transcript is correct.

Proof.

Induction on

. At

,
\mathcal{L}_{0}=\{\langle 0,U+1\rangle@{0}\}
and

\Gamma(\emptyset)=\{\langle 0,U+1\rangle\}
. Assume the claim after event

on key

. By Lemma

.

Suppose

and the notch matched

(a); the forced answer

. If instead the notch matched (N1): the citation is

by the same lemma (a gap contains

Theorem 6.4

(Audit)
.

Fix

,
T
, and a prime

. The scheme of

5
–

is perfectly complete, and it
is
\varepsilon

\varepsilon\;\leq\;\frac{4T+1}{p}

against every maintainer strategy, with no bound on the maintainer’s
computational power. The auditor stores five words and a flag and performs

O(1)
word operations per event and per disclosed triple.

Proof.

Completeness.
The honest maintainer maintains the true state, cites
at each event the unique gap or gap pair that Lemmas
3.4

and
3.5
prescribe, with the codes those gaps actually carry—reproducible by Lemma

—and at the audit discloses

\Gamma(S_{T})
with its live codes. Every predicate of

holds by construction, every announced answer is the
forced one, the temporal rule holds because cited gaps were born at earlier
events, the disclosure meshes by Lemma

. The transcript is balanced—every birth is eventually
consumed, in the run or at disclosure—so
\mathsf{A}
and
\mathsf{R}
are products
over identical multisets and agree for
every

. Acceptance is
certain; no randomness is consumed by completeness.

Soundness.
Fix a maintainer strategy. Since the auditor emits
nothing during the session and its flag depends only on the public
transcript, the realized transcript
\tau
is independent of
z
. Condition
on
\tau
. If
\tau
is not well-notched, the flag is lowered and the
auditor rejects with probability one. So assume
\tau

\mathcal{B}
and consumption multiset
\mathcal{C}
determined by

\tau
. Define the polynomial

P(Z)\;=\;\prod_{\beta\in\mathcal{B}}\bigl(Z-\nu(\beta)\bigr)\;-\;\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}}\bigl(Z-\nu(\gamma)\bigr)\;\in\;\mathbb{F}_{p}[Z],

so that the auditor accepts iff
P(z)=0
.

If
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{C}
then

and the auditor accepts; but by

6.3
every answer in

is then correct, so this case
contributes nothing to the soundness event. If

, then

; both being products of monic linear factors, unique

gives

. Its degree is at most

. By Corollary

,

; consumptions during the run number at most

), so

. A nonzero

has at most

is

and independent of

Averaging over
\tau
in the event that the transcript is incorrect (which,
as shown, forces

whenever the flag survived) yields
the bound. The cost accounting is immediate from
Figure

.
∎

Remark 6.5

(Calibration)
.

(Meshing)

Outside the failure event, acceptance implies balance, and

identify the disclosure

and its gap family with

7
Necessity

Each pillar of Theorem

temporal rule—is now shown to be irreplaceable. For the lower bounds we
allow the auditor an arbitrary notch language: the maintainer may append any
finite binary string per event, and the auditor is any machine over such
streams.

Definition 7.1

(Deterministic auditor)

7.1

8.2
The attestation envelope

The maintainer of Theorem

Theorem 8.2

(Attestation envelope)
.

Over any sequence of
m

Proof.

Assign to each internal node of degree

over all internal nodes;

of a step as its number of structural events plus the

\Phi

Insertions.

•

\Delta\Phi=0

•

\leq 2
.

•

Definition 8.3

(Attested mode)

(Session tally)

11
Concluding remarks

The results form a closed account of the constant-memory audit of dynamic
ordered sets. The gap calculus (Lemmas
3.3
–
3.5
)
and its incidence normal form (Proposition
4.2
and
Theorem
4.3
) turn order semantics into a finite path
rewriting system whose every answer is the forced image of one citation. The
indenture discipline prices the certificate: at most four words of notch per update, three per query, three
per element at disclosure, against a stock of five words and a flag. The
audit theorem (Theorem
6.4
) delivers perfect completeness and
soundness error
(4T+1)/p
against unbounded adaptive maintainers; the
calibration is explicit, and a
61
-bit Mersenne prime covers a million
operations over a million keys with error below
2^{-38}
. The necessity
results close the design space from below:
\binom{2n}{n}
states for any
deterministic auditor (Theorem
7.2
), the same wall for visible
coins (Corollary
7.3
), and certain forgery without the
temporal rule (Proposition
7.4
). The maintainer’s theorem and
the attestation envelope (Theorems
8.1

and
8.2
) settle the other side of the ledger: the discipline
costs the structure one word per element and
O(1)
work per operation, and
even its amortized rebalancing budget—at most
2m
structural events—can
be policed by the auditor’s counter rather than taken on faith. Epoch
composition (Theorem
9.2
) makes audit frequency a dial with
additive error.

Two boundary lines deserve plain statement. First, detection here is
retrospective by design, and the necessity results say something about how
far that can be improved: an auditor below the
\binom{2n}{n}
floor must
keep its randomness hidden, and whether such an auditor can be made

online
—rejecting a false answer the moment it is announced rather
than at the covering audit—is not a gap in the present analysis but a
recognized frontier: Naor and Rothblum
[
16
]
proved that
online memory checking with small secret state and nontrivial query cost
implies the existence of one-way functions, with the quantitative trade-offs
mapped in
[
9
]
. Within the information-theoretic setting of this
paper, retrospective is therefore the correct word, and
Theorem
9.2
prices the latency exactly. Second, the scope is
the seven-operation interface of Definition
2.1
. The gap
calculus certifies arrangement—adjacency, bracketing, extremality—and
does so completely; positional statistics such as the rank of a key are
aggregate properties of the arrangement, not local ones, and lie outside
what a two-word citation can force. The interface boundary is drawn where
the uniqueness arguments of Lemma
3.5
end, and within that
boundary nothing has been left conditional: every constant is explicit,
every protocol total, and both halves of the tally—the construction and
its impossibility counterparts—have been cut from the same stick.
