SUBJECT: Energy loss of a short electron bunch in hollow-core silica fiber channel.

Wake potentials of a short electron bunch and impedances in a hollow-core fiber channel are calculated
with CST wakefield solver. The energy loss of the bunch passing through the fiber channel of a given
length is found for a few bunch lengths.

1. Introduction.

We consider propagation of a short electron bunch along the axis of a hollow-core (HC) silica fiber
channel [1]. More specifically, the HC fiber under consideration has a Kagome lattice structure [2] shown
in Fig. 1. The lattice formed by thin (1.5 µm) silica inner walls has inner boundary with the curvature that
is in an opposite direction to the curvature of the outer thick (relatively thick, ~ 70 µm) cylindrical layer.
This is the so-called negative curvature structure, which allows the light to propagate through the channel
with low losses [1]. Important dimensions of the fiber channel in Fig. 1 are its outer radius 209.7105 µm,
the inner radius of the outer layer 138.193 µm, and the minimum radius of the inner boundary 56.135 µm.

Figure 1: Cross section of a hollow-core silica fiber with Kagome lattice [2]. Marked dimensions are listed
under the photograph.

The task is to calculate the energy lost by a short bunch of electrons with the energy 43 MeV as it passes
through such a fiber channel. We will apply the wakefield solver in the CST Studio [3] to solve this
problem, using an approximation of the bunch by a rigid Gaussian on-axis charge distribution with a fixed
r.m.s. length σz. Wake potentials of the approximated bunch and beam coupling impedances of the fiber
channel will be calculated as well.

The fiber channel inner structure was modeled in CST Studio [3] in two ways: (a) a simplified
approximation with filling by thin-wall cylinders, as shown in Fig. 2a, and (b) a structure resembling the
original filling in Fig. 1. The latter was built in CST using results of point-coordinate measurements [4]
in Fig. 1; see Fig. 2b. In case (a), the wall thickness for inner structure was taken to be 2 µm; for (b), the
original thickness of 1.5 µm was kept. The wall material is silica glass (SiO2) with complex dielectric
permittivity ε = ε′ + i ε′′ = 3.8 + i 0.0002 at 10 GHz [5]. One can take ε′ to be constant in the frequency
range of interest, up to 1 THz.

2. Wake calculations.

For wake calculations we model the electron bunch by a rigid Gaussian line charge distribution traveling
on the channel axis with velocity v ≈ c (ultra-relativistic). We neglect the electron bunch transverse size
(~90 µm) in this approximation, which can be justified for our goal to estimate electron energy loss. The
bunch FWHM length of 3 ps can be approximated by the Gaussian distribution r.m.s. length σt = 1.3 ps,
or in space σz = cσt = 390 µm. Another possible choice is to take 4σt = 3 ps, which results in σz = 225 µm.
We will also consider shorter distribution lengths, down to σz = 100 µm, to better resolve higher
frequencies in impedance calculations.

The wake potentials from a CST wakefield solver run with the Gaussian bunch of σz = 225 µm in a fiber
channel of length 5 cm are shown in Fig. 3 as a function of the distance s behind the bunch head. The
shape of the Gaussian distribution is shown in red (unnormalized).

The energy loss ΔW of a bunch passing through a structure is related to the structure longitudinal loss
factor kL as ΔW = kLq2, where q is the bunch charge. For N electrons in the bunch, q = Ne, with e being
the electron charge. Then the energy loss per particle ΔW1 = ΔW/N = kLqe. The definition of the loss factor
 ∞
is k L = ∫ dzW ( z )λ ( z ), where W(z) is the wake potential of a distribution in the structure and λ(z) is the
 −∞
charge distribution line density [7]. Essentially, it is an overlap integral of the wake potential and the
charge density distribution (Reference Pulse), see the insets in Figs. 3. The CST wakefield solver
calculates the loss factors. The results for a few bunch lengths are summarized in Table 1. All the results
assume 5-cm long channels, and, for particle energy loss calculation, we take the bunch charge q = 1 nC.

Most of the CST results above were obtained without account of dielectric losses, since the loss tangent
for silica is very low, tan δd = 5.26∙10-5 [5]. As a check, we performed a run with dielectric losses taken
into account for the case of empty channel. The wake and loss results were practically the same as without
dielectric losses; however, the run took about 3 times longer than in the case without dielectric losses.

Regarding wake solver run times, most of the runs for 5-cm structures took about an hour on a PC with
48 CPU cores (the CST wakefield solver does not use GPU), or just a few minutes for empty channels.
The mesh step in the transverse directions x, y is defined by the thin (1.5-2 µm) walls of the fiber filling
resolution; there should be at least 2 steps within this thickness. The longitudinal mesh step depends on
the bunch length: by default, the wake solver puts at least ten mesh steps over the bunch length and uses
the same mesh step along the structure. These settings resulted in a rather large mesh, 208M mesh points
for one-quarter of structure in the case of fiber lattice of Fig. 2b with the short bunch, σz = 100 µm. That
wake solver run took 17 hours.

One can see from Table 1 that the particle energy loss depends strongly on the bunch length in the
explored range. The reason for such a dependence is related to the strength of excitation of structure
resonances shown in Figs. 5-6. For short bunches (here σz = 100 µm), the bunch frequency spectrum
extends to the resonance frequency, so the resonance is excited by the higher spectrum harmonics, which
increases the bunch energy loss significantly. The longer bunches have very little (σz = 225 µm) or
negligible (σz = 390 µm) harmonic amplitudes at resonance frequencies, which results in low energy
losses.

3. Summary.

We explored the energy losses of short electron bunches passing through the hollow-core fiber channel.
Wake potentials and energy loss factors are calculated with CST wakefield solver for a few bunch
lengths. The results are presented in Sec. 2 and summarized in Table 1. The estimated beam energy loss
for the relevant bunch lengths is small, no more than ~200 keV for 1-nC bunch charge, compared to the
beam energy 43 MeV. The beam energy loss in the fiber channel is directly proportional to the bunch
charge.
