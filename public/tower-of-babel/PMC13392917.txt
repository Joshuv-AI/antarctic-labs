Assessment of the feed additive consisting of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate (3b606) for all animal species for the renewal of its authorisation (Zinpro Animal Nutrition (Europe), Inc.)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Jaume Galobart, Maria Vittoria Vettori, Paola Manini, Alberto Navarro Villa, Fabiola Pizzo, Piera Valeri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the application for renewal of authorisation of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate as a feed additive for all animal species. The applicant has provided data demonstrating that the additive currently in the market complies with the conditions of authorisation. There is no new evidence that would lead the FEEDAP Panel to reconsider its previous conclusions. Thus, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive remains safe for the target species and consumers. Regarding user safety, the additive is an eye irritant. It is also considered a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised. The FEEDAP Panel is not in a position to conclude on the environmental safety of the additive. The conclusions of the forthcoming opinion on the environmental risk assessment of trace elements are expected to be directly applicable to the present assessment and will inform the final evaluation. There is no need to assess the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from Zinpro Animal Nutrition (Europe), Inc. 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate, when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: nutritional additives; functional group: compounds of trace elements).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 17 July 2025 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2025‐00444. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 16 September 2025.

Additional information

The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for all animal species (3b606). 3

EFSA issued three opinions on the safety and efficacy of this product when used in feed for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2013, 2021b).

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 5 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 6 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 12 November to 03 December 2025 for which no comments were received.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 8 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021b).

ASSESSMENT

The additive zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate is currently authorised as a nutritional additive (functional group: compounds of trace elements) for use in feed for all animal species. The current application regards the renewal of its authorisation. Unless otherwise indicated, in this opinion, zinc refers to zinc(II).

Characterisation

Characterisation of the active substance

A Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) or the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) name could not be identified for the additive under assessment, zinc chelate of amino acid hydrate. It can be described as a covalent coordinate complex of bivalent zinc with amino acids derived from hydrolysed soy. The chemical formula is [R‐NH2‐CH‐ COO](1‐3)Zn–X, where R is the residual group of any amino acid and X is a negative charged ion (e.g. Cl− or HSO4 −). The molecular weight will vary depending on the amino acid linked to the zinc molecule; the weight range will vary from 176 to 281 Dalton (Da) with an estimated average of 230 Da.

The current authorisation sets specifications for the active substance to contain a maximum of 10% of the molecules exceeding 1500 Da, which correspond to the fraction of proteins which are not hydrolysed and will not take part in the formation of the Zn chelate. Although the applicant did not provide any data to show compliance with this requirement, the analytical data provided for preparations of the additive (Table 1) indicate that the difference between the percentage of total amino acids and the percentage of free amino acids is 3.5%–4.5%, suggesting that less than 10% of total amino acids is present in the active substance in the form of peptides and proteins, whose molecular weight exceeds 1500 Da.

The applicant provided data on five batches of the active substance, which showed a mean content of zinc of 21.9% and of amino acids of 45.4%. 9

Characterisation of the preparations

The additive is currently authorised as a preparation of zinc amino acid complex where the zinc and the amino acids derived from soya protein are chelated via coordinate covalent bonds, as a powder with a minimum content of 10% zinc.

The applicant stated that no changes in the manufacturing process of the additive have been introduced since the last authorisation. 10 However, the applicant requested to extend the source of the amino acids to any plant‐based proteins, such as hydrolysed corn gluten or potato protein. The applicant provided analytical data on five batches of the additive with amino acids derived from each of the proposed sources of hydrolysed proteins (soy, corn gluten and potato).

The applicant provided examples of two solid preparations, which are specified to contain 10% and 18% zinc, respectively. Safety

The safety of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate (including alternative hydrolysed protein sources) was already assessed by the FEEDAP Panel in its previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2013, 2021a). In those opinions, the Panel concluded that the additive is safe for the target species and the consumer when used up to the maximum authorised level of Zn in complete feeds. The Panel considered that the use of the additive is of no concern for its use in aquaculture and for the soil compartment but could not exclude a potential environmental concern related to drainage and run‐off of zinc to surface water. The FEEDAP Panel also concluded that the additive should be considered as a skin and eye irritant and as a skin sensitiser.

The applicant states that no incidents or safety issues have been documented or reported since the previous authorisation. 17

The applicant provided a literature search on the safety of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate for the target animals, consumers, users and the environment (see Section 3.2.1).

Extensive literature search

The applicant performed a literature search to provide evidence that the additive remains safe under the approved conditions for the target species, 18 consumers, 19 users 20 and the environment. 21 The search covered the period 2016–2025 and the following databases were used: Web of Science, PubMed, Agricola and Google Scholar. The search protocol described the inclusion and exclusion criteria applied for the screening process. A total of 441 publications were retrieved, of which 70 were considered relevant for the safety for the target species, 45 for the consumer, 3 for the environment and 3 for the user. The FEEDAP Panel reviewed the publications retrieved in the search and concluded that none of them identified new information that would lead to reconsider its previous conclusions.

Safety for target species

In the previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2021a), the Panel considered zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate (including alternative hydrolysed protein sources) as a safe source of zinc for all animal species, provided that the maximum zinc contents authorised in complete feed are respected. Following a review of the 70 publications identified in the literature search, the FEEDAP Panel notes that there was no study reporting any adverse effects from the use of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate as a source of dietary zinc when used to meet the nutritional needs of the animals at current authorised levels. Considering the above, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the use of zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate derived from soya protein remains safe for the target species under the authorised conditions of use. This conclusion applies also to the additive produced using corn gluten and potato protein as sources of hydrolysed proteins.

Safety for the consumer

In a previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b), the Panel concluded that the use of the additive as a source of zinc in animal nutrition would not lead to different zinc concentrations in food of animal origin compared with the standard authorised zinc sulfate and would therefore not modify the exposure of consumers to dietary zinc. The FEEDAP Panel concluded consequently that the additive is safe for consumers when used as a supplemental source of zinc up to the maximum authorised levels for total zinc in feedingstuffs.

In the current application, no specific studies to support the safety of the additive for the consumer were submitted. From the literature search conducted by the applicant (see Section 3.2.1), several papers were retrieved. One publication reported data on zinc deposition and was considered by the FEEDAP Panel to be relevant for the current assessment and is described below.

In a 35‐day feeding study, broiler chickens were fed diets supplemented with zinc from the additive under assessment at levels of 50 or 100 mg Zn/kg feed (Kakhki et al., 2016). Liver, breast and thigh muscle samples were collected at slaughter (day 35 of the experiment). Zinc supplementation resulted in increased zinc concentrations in all tissues analysed. At the highest supplementation level (100 mg Zn/kg feed, approx. 135 mg Zn/kg feed analysed), zinc concentrations were 138.6 mg Zn/kg in liver, 32.1 mg Zn/kg in breast muscle and 58.5 mg Zn/kg in thigh muscle.

The Panel notes that the Zn deposition levels reported in this publication are somewhat higher than those reported in previous opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2015), in which zinc concentrations were 29 mg Zn/kg in liver and 21 mg Zn/kg in breast muscle. However, this should be considered in light of the Zn concentration in feed (approximately 135 mg/kg complete feed), which exceeded the maximum authorised level (120 mg/kg feed) and was about twice the level reported in the previous opinion (75 mg/kg feed). Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel considers that use of the additive under assessment would not be expected to result in significant differences in Zn deposition in other edible tissues compared with previously reported levels.

The toxicological properties of zinc compounds have been extensively described by the Scientific Committee on Food (SCF) (EC SCF, 2003), by Sandstead (2015) and in previous FEEDAP Panel opinions (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012a, 2012b, 2012c, 2012d, 2012e, 2015). No substantial differences between inorganic and organic zinc compounds are expected concerning zinc toxicity. Dietary upper levels (ULs) for zinc have been established by the Scientific Committee on Food (SCF) for different population categories (EC SCF, 2003): 25 mg/day in adults, 22 mg/day for adolescents and 7 mg/day for children (1–3 years). No ULs are set for infants, the elderly and very elderly.

Intestinal zinc absorption is saturable, and the rate of zinc uptake is inversely proportional to intake within its large range of homeostatic regulation. Therefore, animal supplementation of zinc above its requirements and within the authorised levels will not substantially change zinc deposition in edible tissues, except in liver and kidney, where limited increases in deposition can be observed with increased intake. The EFSA FEEDAP and NDA Panels previously summarised the zinc intake of the European populations and the possible contribution of animal zinc supplementation to human zinc dietary exposure (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2014, 2015; EFSA NDA Panel, 2014). Products of animal origin contributed to about 40%–50% of total zinc intake in all consumer age groups. Previous assessments showed that there is no indication that the current dietary intake of zinc in the European population, which already includes food commodities from zinc‐supplemented animals, may be higher than the UL (EFSA NDA Panel, 2014).

In re‐assessing the consumer exposure and safety of the additive, the FEEDAP Panel considered the following: –no new data were made available on possible zinc deposition in animal tissues following the administration of the additive under assessment and considering the maximum authorised zinc content in complete feed;–there is no sufficient certainty on the differences in the bioavailability of inorganic and organic zinc sources leading to a different residue formation potential;–the toxicological profile of zinc is well known and depressed copper uptake with associated copper deficiency is the most sensitive and well‐characterised effect of chronic excess of zinc intake in humans and animals;–the conclusion of the EFSA NDA Panel (EFSA NDA Panel, 2014) indicates that current maximum authorised levels for zinc in feed do not cause concern with regard to consumer exposure to zinc.

Based on the above, the FEEDAP Panel considers that an update of the consumer exposure is not necessary and concludes that zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate derived from soya protein remains safe for the consumers under the current authorised conditions of use in feed. This conclusion also applies to the additive produced using corn gluten and potato protein as sources of hydrolysed proteins.

Safety for users

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b, 2021a) in the absence of data, the Panel concluded that the additive (including alternative hydrolysed protein sources) should be considered a skin and eye irritant and a skin sensitiser.

In the current assessment, no experimental data were provided and the literature search did not identify any relevant papers for the safety of the additive for the users.

Based on the information present in the safety data sheet, 22 the additive is irritant to the eyes.

The FEEDAP Panel notes that the additive preparation contains nickel (see Section 3.1.2). The Directive (EU) 2022/431 23 set an occupational exposure limit (OEL) of 0.01 and 0.05 mg/m3 for both respirable and inhalable fraction, respectively, as nickel meets the criteria for classification as carcinogenic (category 1A).

Therefore, the Panel concludes that the additive is an eye irritant. It is also considered a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised.

Safety for environment

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b), the FEEDAP Panel concluded that ‘The use of zinc as a feed additive does not pose a direct concern for the soil compartment’. However, there is a potential environmental concern related to drainage and run‐off of zinc to surface water. Most vulnerable to these processes are acidic sandy soils.

The use of zinc‐containing feed additives in aquaculture up to the maximum authorised zinc level in feeds is not expected to pose an appreciable risk to the environment.

No information was provided by the applicant to allow the Panel to perform an assessment in line with the requirements of the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019); therefore, the FEEDAP Panel is not in a position to conclude on the environmental safety of the additive.

The Panel notes that an Opinion addressing the environmental risk assessment of trace elements, including zinc, is currently ongoing, following a mandate from the European Commission. The conclusions of the forthcoming opinion are expected to be directly applicable to the present assessment and will inform the final evaluation. 24

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additives. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additives in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 25 and good manufacturing practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The applicant provided evidence that the additive currently in the market complies with the existing conditions of the authorisation. The Panel notes that the proposed modifications on the alternative sources of hydrolysed proteins (corn gluten and potato protein) for the additive are considered to be of no concern.

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the use of the additive zinc chelate of amino acids hydrate in animal nutrition remains safe for the target animals and consumer under the approved conditions of the authorisation.

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additive is an eye irritant and a skin and respiratory sensitiser. Exposure of users by any route is considered a risk and should be minimised.

The FEEDAP Panel is not in a position to conclude on the environmental safety of the additive. The conclusions of the forthcoming opinion on the environmental risk assessment of trace elements are expected to be directly applicable to the present assessment and will inform the final evaluation.

There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

RECOMMENDATION

The FEEDAP Panel recommends removing the specifications of the active substance to contain a maximum of 10% of molecules exceeding 1500 Da and instead including a minimum content of free amino acids in the additive of at least 20%.
