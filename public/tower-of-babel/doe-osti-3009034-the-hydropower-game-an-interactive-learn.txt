Executive Summary
This report documents a Seedling project supported by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE)
Water Power Technologies Office (WPTO), conducted by Argonne National Laboratory (ANL)
and the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR). The Seedling and Sapling Program provides
small grants over short duration to enable early-stage research at national laboratories. This
funding mechanism is intended to cultivate innovative ideas and expand research road maps in
hydropower and marine energy technologies [1].
 Under this Seedling award, the project team developed an educational outreach tool, or
“serious game” [2–5], built in Python and powered by the Pygame library, aimed at teaching
fundamental hydropower concepts through an engaging, interactive learning experience. The
game features multiple lessons covering several hydropower topics while allowing for expansion
and customization in the instance of future funding availability:

 • Hydropower Plant Types: Players explore and compare the mechanics and applications of
 run-of-river, dam hydropower, and pumped-storage hydropower.

 • Flow Dynamics and Power Generation: Interactive tasks demonstrate how release rate
 and hydraulic head combine to determine power output.

 • Grid Operations and Load Following: Simulations illustrate how hydropower facilities
 respond to fluctuating electricity demand to balance the grid.

 • Market Integration: Levels scaffold understanding of how hydropower interfaces with the
 broader energy market, including operational and economic considerations.

 The tool was showcased at several science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM)
outreach events, where it was demoed to students, educators, and the general public. These
events provided valuable opportunities to collect feedback on the gameplay, storyline, and ed-
ucational objectives from a wide audience. The game was presented at STEMapalooza [6],
Introduce a Girl to Engineering Day [7], STEMCON [8], and Clean Currents 2025 [9]. Insights
gathered during these demonstrations informed refinements to the game’s user experience and
strengthened its effectiveness as an educational tool for teaching hydropower concepts.
 This report outlines the game’s design philosophy, educational objectives, technical imple-
mentation, user experience insights, and potential for broader deployment within educational
and workforce development contexts. It emphasizes how gamified learning can demystify com-
plex hydropower science and inspire interest in water-power technologies.
 The project’s success demonstrates the value of Seedling funding in fostering creative, low-
cost educational tools that support DOE’s mission to advance energy literacy and innovation.
The report concludes with recommendations for expanding the tool by adding new levels, inte-
grating assessment metrics, and exploring commercialization or deployment pathways through
future Sapling funding.
 The official webpage of the Hydropower Game, which includes a link to the educational tool,
can be accessed at www.anl.gov/hydropower/hydropower-game [10].

1 Introduction
Hydropower is one of the most versatile and reliable renewable energy sources in the United
States, providing critical services to the electric grid, including load balancing, frequency reg-
ulation, and energy storage. However, despite its importance, the fundamental principles of
hydropower generation, operation, and integration into the broader energy system remain unfa-
miliar to much of the general public. Increasing awareness and understanding of these concepts
is essential to support the U.S. DOE mission of fostering innovation and advancing the nation’s
energy system.
 Moreover, workforce demographics within the hydropower industry underscore the urgency
of educational outreach. Approximately 26% of the hydropower workforce is age 55 or older and
is expected to reach retirement age within the next decade. It is estimated that nearly 9,000
workers will retire by 2030 and approximately 13,000 by 2040 [11–13]. This aging workforce,
particularly among engineers and managers, highlights the need to inspire and prepare the next
generation of hydropower professionals.
 To address both the public knowledge gap and emerging workforce challenges, the WPTO
funded a Seedling project jointly awarded to ANL and NLR. Seedling projects are small-scale,
short-duration efforts designed to explore early-stage, high-risk, and potentially high-reward
ideas. Their purpose is to stimulate innovative approaches that can later be expanded through
larger-scale research and development initiatives [1].
 Under this Seedling award, the project team developed an interactive educational tool in
the form of a “serious game” [2], an approach that combines entertainment with structured
learning objectives. Serious games have gained significant traction in recent years as effective
tools for teaching complex concepts in an engaging way. They have been applied across a va-
riety of domains, including mathematical optimization [3], renewable energy systems [4], and
environmental challenges such as fish passage [5]. By leveraging this pedagogical approach,
the Hydropower Game introduces users to the basic principles of hydropower through a playful,
story-driven experience that encourages exploration, experimentation, and problem-solving. Im-
plemented in Python and powered by the Pygame library, the game is organized into a sequence
of progressively challenging levels that guide players step by step into core hydropower topics:

 • Types of hydropower plants: explore the design and purpose of run-of-river, dam-based,
 and pumped-storage hydropower (PSH) systems.
 • Power generation fundamentals: understand how hydraulic head and release rate combine
 to determine power output.
 • Operational strategies: learn how hydropower facilities are operated to follow electricity
 demand and maintain grid stability.
 • Integration into energy markets: discover how hydropower interacts with the broader
 energy market and contributes to reliable and resilient energy supply.

 By combining interactive gameplay with scientifically accurate content, the project aims
to make complex technical topics accessible to a wider audience, ranging from students and
educators to energy professionals and the general public. Beyond raising awareness, the game
seeks to spark curiosity about hydropower’s role in the grid and to help cultivate a future
workforce equipped to replace aging professionals in the field.

 The remainder of this report details the project objectives, game design and development,

2 Game Concept and Educational Goals
2.1 Target Audience
The game is designed to reach a wide audience, including students, educators, energy profes-
sionals, and members of the general public interested in renewable energy and power systems.
While the primary target audience is high-school students who are beginning to explore future
career paths in STEM, the game has been developed to remain accessible and engaging for a
much broader spectrum of learners.
 Improving energy literacy among younger audiences is a critical motivation behind this
project. Studies have shown that U.S. secondary students demonstrate limited understanding of
basic energy concepts and technologies, including hydropower [14]. For example, authors in [14]
found that students’ cognitive energy literacy scores averaged only 42%, indicating widespread
misconceptions about where electricity comes from and how different energy technologies work.
This highlights the need for innovative and engaging educational tools capable of bridging the
gap between concern for energy issues and actual understanding.
 Beyond students, the game can be integrated into classrooms and outreach activities by
educators to enrich STEM curricula. Energy professionals and stakeholders may also use it as
a communication tool to explain hydropower fundamentals to non-experts in an intuitive and
interactive way. Finally, members of the general public can play the game independently to
improve their understanding of hydropower’s role within the evolving energy landscape.
 By addressing audiences with different backgrounds and levels of expertise, the game sup-
ports the DOE’s broader goals of improving renewable energy literacy and inspiring the next
generation of hydropower professionals.

2.2 Learning Objectives
The game is structured around several key learning objectives that guide the player through the
fundamental concepts of hydropower and its role in modern energy systems:

 • Link between moving water and electricity: the player learns that hydropower
 generation relies on harnessing the energy of moving water and converting it into electricity
 through turbines and generators.

 • Types of hydropower plants: the player explores different hydropower technologies,
 including run-of-river facilities, dam-based hydropower plants, and PSH systems, gaining
 insight into their design and applications.

 • Fundamental hydropower equation: the game introduces the core relationship gov-
 erning hydropower generation through the equation

 P = ηρgQh

 where power output P depends on turbine efficiency η, water density ρ, gravitational
 acceleration g, water flow rate Q, and hydraulic head h. This illustrates how changes in
 flow and head directly impact electricity production.

 3

 • Hydropower operations and grid stability: the player learns how hydropower facil-
 ities adjust their output in real time to follow electricity demand and help stabilize the
 grid. Because electricity is a commodity that cannot be stored, market operators clear
 supply and demand simultaneously, making hydropower’s flexibility especially valuable.

 • Environmental considerations: the game highlights the potential environmental im-
 pacts of hydropower operations on river ecosystems, such as effects on fish populations and
 habitat disruption. It also explains how operational rules and environmental regulations
 are designed to mitigate these impacts.

 • Integration into broader energy markets: the player discovers hydropower’s role
 alongside other generation technologies. When combined with resources such as nuclear,
 natural gas, or geothermal, hydropower enhances grid reliability and flexibility by pro-
 viding fast ramping and storage capabilities that complement both baseload and variable
 energy resources.

2.3 Pedagogical Approach
The game adopts a narrative-driven approach to enhance player engagement and promote active
learning. The storyline follows a young hydropower engineer on their first day of work in the
fictional town of Blue Rapids, a community powered by several hydropower plants of different
types.
 Players step into the role of this new engineer and progress through a series of levels de-
signed to simulate real-world challenges in hydropower operations. As they explore the different
facilities around Blue Rapids, players gradually acquire knowledge about the fundamentals of
hydropower, including plant types, power generation, operational strategies, environmental con-
siderations, and integration into the broader energy market. Each successfully completed level
represents a new skill acquired, and the character’s job title evolves accordingly, progressing
from a new hire to a more senior operator with increasing responsibilities. At the end of each
level, players also receive a performance score that reflects how effectively they completed the
tasks, providing feedback on their learning progress and encouraging replay for improvement.
 To reinforce a realistic learning experience, the player is guided and mentored by a senior
engineer who provides context, explanations, and feedback throughout the game. This mentor-
ship component mirrors real-world workforce development pathways and highlights the role of
experienced professionals in training the next generation. By combining an immersive storyline
with progressive learning objectives, the game aims to spark curiosity about hydropower careers
and inspire students to consider entering the hydropower workforce.
 The development of this tool also draws on the concept of serious games, which combine
entertainment with educational or training objectives. Serious games are widely recognized
for their ability to increase engagement, improve knowledge retention, and provide interactive
environments for learning complex concepts [2]. By applying these principles, the Hydropower
Game bridges the gap between traditional instruction and experiential learning, making abstract
energy concepts more accessible and memorable.

3 Game Design and Development
3.1 Software Architecture
The Hydropower Game was developed in Python using a combination of standard and third-
party libraries, including Pygame, NumPy, Matplotlib, OpenCV, and OR-Tools. The interactive
graphical user interface (GUI) was built with Pygame [15], while Matplotlib was used to create
data visualization components integrated into the gameplay. Small optimization models were
formulated using Google OR-Tools MathOpt [16] and solved with the Google Linear Optimiza-
tion Package (GLOP) solver.
 To ensure broad accessibility and straightforward deployment, the game was packaged using
PyInstaller for major desktop platforms. A standalone executable file (.exe) was created for
Windows, and an application bundle (.app) was generated for macOS. Each distribution embeds
the Python interpreter and all required third-party libraries (e.g., Pygame, NumPy), eliminating
the need for users to manually install dependencies. This cross-platform packaging strategy
minimizes setup time for classrooms and outreach events, enables offline use on standard lab or
personal machines, and ensures consistent performance across different hardware configurations.

3.2 Level Descriptions
The Hydropower Game is designed to follow the structure of a traditional video game, providing
players with a familiar and intuitive experience. It begins with a main menu offering options such
as starting a new game, continuing a saved game, adjusting settings, or viewing credits. Upon
selecting a new game, players proceed to a character selection screen where they can choose

 5

an avatar, enter their name, or opt for a randomly assigned character. The gameplay itself
is organized into a sequence of levels, each introducing new learning objectives and challenges
that build upon one another. Progression through the levels reflects the player’s growth in
knowledge and skills, reinforcing the narrative of a young engineer advancing in their career.
This conventional structure ensures that players can easily navigate the game while engaging
with the educational content in a gradual and rewarding manner.

3.2.1 Main menu
The game opens with a simple main menu designed to provide easy navigation for players of
all backgrounds. From this interface, players can start a New Game to begin their journey
as a young hydropower engineer in Blue Rapids, or select Continue Game to pick up where
they previously left off. The Settings option allows players to customize elements such as audio
and display preferences to suit their experience. The Credits section acknowledges the project
team, contributors, third-party source codes and graphics, and the U.S. DOE’s WPTO funding.
This design ensures that players can quickly access core features while maintaining a clean and
user-friendly interface.

 Figure 3.2: Main menu

3.2.2 Choose your character
After selecting New Game, players are taken to a dedicated character selection interface where
they can personalize their in-game identity before beginning their journey. They can choose
from eight distinct characters, each designed with various visual styles and backgrounds to
allow players to select an avatar they most identify with. For those who prefer an element of
surprise, a question mark icon is available at the center of the selection grid, enabling the game

to randomly assign a character. Players can also enter their name in the input field at the top
of the interface to further personalize their experience. Once satisfied with their choices, they
can confirm their selection by clicking the confirmation button at the bottom of the screen, and
transition into the start of the storyline.

 Figure 3.3: Choose your character

3.2.3 Level 0: Welcome to Blue Rapids
Level 0 serves as the introductory stage of the game, setting the context for the player’s journey.
Upon arrival in the town of Blue Rapids, the young engineer is welcomed by a senior engineer
who will act as a mentor throughout the storyline. In this level, the senior engineer provides a
tour of the local hydropower facility, guiding the player into the turbine room and offering an
initial overview of how the plant operates. This stage does not include gameplay challenges or
decision-making; rather, it establishes the narrative, introduces the senior engineer who serves
as mentor, and familiarizes the player with the setting before the interactive learning begins in
later levels.

 Figure 3.4: Level 0: Welcome to Blue Rapids

3.2.4 Level 1: The Run-of-River plant
In Level 1, the player is introduced to the fundamental principle of hydropower generation at a
Run-of-River (RoR) plant. The senior engineer explains a simplified relationship for this type
of facility:
 P ≈ aQ,
where P represents the electrical power output, Q is the water release rate through the turbine,
and a is a proportionality factor that accounts for efficiency and physical constants. This
expression conveys that, in a RoR context, power generation is approximately proportional to
the flow of water through the turbine.
 After learning this concept, the player is guided to an exploration screen that illustrates the
components of a RoR plant. Here, they can click on elements such as the penstock, turbine,
and generator to discover their roles within the plant.
 Then, the player is invited to take control of the plant. The gameplay focuses on controlling
the plant’s power output by adjusting the angle of the wicket gates, which regulates the water
release rate. The main objective of this level is to match the hydropower generation to the
electricity load in real time. By adjusting turbine flow to match demand, the player develops a
practical understanding of how water flow governs hydropower generation and how supply and
demand must be balanced in real time on the electricity grid.

 Figure 3.5: Level 1: The Run-of-River plant

3.2.5 Level 2: The Dam Hydropower plant
In Level 2, the player is introduced to a more complete representation of hydropower generation
through the fundamental equation:
 P = η ρ g Q h,
where P is the power output, η the turbine efficiency, ρ the density of water, g the gravitational
constant, Q the water release rate, and h the hydraulic head. This formulation expands on the
simplified relation presented in Level 1 (P ≈ aQ), highlighting the critical role of hydraulic head
in determining generation capacity.
 The player first encounters this relationship through a cross-section diagram of a dam, where
the variables P , Q, and h are visually displayed to reinforce the physical meaning of each
term. They are then invited to interact with a three-dimensional visualization that dynamically
illustrates how changes in flow and head affect hydropower output.
 After this introduction, the player is taken to an exploration screen of a dam hydropower
facility, where they can first examine its components before taking control of its operation. By
opening and closing a set of gates, the player manages the release of water, directly influencing
both the hydraulic head and the power output. Through this interactive exercise, the player
develops a practical understanding of water management and water budget, the link between
water reservoir volume and water surface elevation, and the interplay between power output,
release rate, and hydraulic head.
 This level emphasizes the operational complexity of dam hydropower plants, where genera-
tion decisions must balance water availability and system flexibility.

 Figure 3.6: Level 2: The Dam Hydropower plant

3.2.6 Level 3: The Pumped-Storage Hydropower plant
In Level 3, the player is introduced to a PSH plant, which operates like a large-scale water bat-
tery. The senior engineer explains that PSH facilities have two reservoirs at different elevations
and can alternate between two operating modes: generating electricity by releasing water from
the upper reservoir to the lower one, and pumping water back up to the upper reservoir using
electricity from the grid. This ability to both charge and discharge makes PSH an essential
technology for storing energy and supporting grid flexibility.
 The player is shown a simplified diagram of a PSH plant to visualize these two operating
modes and understand the energy flows involved. Then, they take control of a PSH facility
and learn to switch between generation and pumping based on electricity demand and market
conditions. Through this interactive experience, the player develops a practical understanding
of how PSH plants help balance supply and demand, help integrate other energy sources, and
enhance overall grid reliability.

 Figure 3.7: Level 3: The Pumped-Storage Hydropower plant

3.2.7 Level 4: Complying with environmental rules
In Level 4, the player is introduced to the environmental impacts of hydropower operations and
the importance of regulated water management. Through guidance from the senior engineer,
the player learns that unregulated hydropower generation can disrupt river ecosystems, affect
fish populations, and damage habitats that rely on stable flow conditions. To mitigate these
impacts, hydropower plant operators implement a series of operating rules designed to balance
electricity production with environmental stewardship.
 The player learns about three key types of operational constraints:

 • Daily release volume limits to ensure that the total water released each day remains
 within agreed-upon thresholds and avoids reservoir depletion.

 • Maximum release rates to prevent downstream flooding and protect sensitive habitats
 that depend on stable river flows.

 • Ramping rate limits to avoid sudden changes in flow that could shock aquatic ecosys-
 tems, cause bank erosion, or displace fish populations.

 After understanding these constraints, the player is invited to interact with the hourly water
release schedule chart of a dam hydropower plant. Their objective is to maximize hydropower
revenues by aligning production with electricity price signals, while simultaneously complying
with the environmental rules governing daily volumes, release rates, and ramping rates. This
level demonstrates the trade-offs operators face between economic objectives and environmental
responsibilities, helping the player appreciate the complexity of hydropower management.

 11

 Figure 3.8: Level 4: Complying with environmental rules

3.2.8 Level 5: Hydropower in the energy market
In this level, the player is introduced to the role of hydropower within the electricity market.
Building on the concepts from earlier levels, the player now manages both a dam hydropower
facility and a PSH plant. The central objective is to schedule energy production and storage
operations in response to fluctuating price signals, maximizing economic returns while ensuring
reliability.
 The player interacts with a scheduling chart that displays hourly electricity prices along-
side plant operations. They must adjust the hydropower release schedule and PSH pump-
ing/generation cycles to follow market signals, while still respecting fundamental physical con-
straints (such as water balance, turbine capacity, and hydraulic head) and environmental re-
quirements (including release rate limits and ramping restrictions).
 Through this exercise, the player gains a practical understanding of how hydropower plants
participate in energy markets, balancing technical feasibility, environmental stewardship, and
economic optimization. This level highlights hydropower’s unique role as a flexible generator
and a large-scale energy storage.

3.3 Graphics and Visualization
The visual design of the Hydropower Game was developed using a combination of AI-generated
and 3D-rendered pictures to create an engaging and visually rich learning experience. AI-
generated assets were produced using Google’s Whisk [17] and OpenAI’s ChatGPT image gen-
eration capabilities [18], while detailed 3D hydropower visualizations were provided by IKM
Testing [19].

 12

 The style of the AI-generated images was carefully selected to be vibrant, colorful, and
expressive, aiming to appeal to both students and adults. The visuals were designed to strike a
balance between realism and approachability: they are detailed enough to represent hydropower
systems accurately, yet stylized in a way that feels inviting and engaging for a wide range of
audiences. This artistic direction was chosen deliberately to make the game visually attractive
for younger players while maintaining a polished, professional aesthetic suitable for educators
and adult learners.
 The 3D visuals were pre-rendered and integrated into the game as static assets using a 2.5D
projection approach [20]. This development strategy was chosen as a trade-off between achieving
high-quality, realistic 3D visuals and avoiding the additional complexity of developing a fully
interactive 3D environment. Notably, the 3D renderings provided by IKM Testing build upon a
previous collaboration with NLR on the REDi Island project [21], allowing the game to leverage
existing WPTO-funded high-fidelity hydropower models for educational purposes.
 Animations and real-time interactions within the game were implemented using the Pygame
library [15], which powers the graphical user interface, player actions, and dynamic elements
such as turbine control and water flow simulation. This combination of pre-rendered 3D vi-
suals, AI-generated pictures, and Pygame-based interactivity provides a visually appealing yet
computationally efficient solution for an educational game.

3.4 Testing and Debugging
The development of the Hydropower Game followed an iterative process involving frequent
testing, continuous feedback, and progressive refinements to both gameplay and educational
content. Biweekly project team meetings were held to review the overall game design, storyline,
and alignment with the intended learning objectives. These discussions helped ensure that the
educational goals remained central while maintaining an engaging and coherent narrative.
 In parallel, daily meetings with the development team focused on technical components and
implementation details. Topics included file size optimization for efficient packaging, validation
of the underlying physical models (e.g., the fundamental hydropower equation and the reservoir
water budget equation), and refinements to the Pygame-based modeling of turbines, flows, and
user interactions. This frequent coordination allowed for rapid identification and resolution of
issues during development.
 The game was also demoed at multiple STEM outreach events, where participants provided
valuable feedback on gameplay mechanics, storyline clarity, and overall usability. These real-
world testing opportunities helped validate the game’s accessibility for students and educators
and informed final adjustments to improve both the player experience and the educational
effectiveness of the tool.

4 Deployment and User Testing
4.1 Public Release and Beta Testing
A beta version of the game was released on August 15, 2025, and made available to the public
through a dedicated online link. The beta version is also accessible via the official project
webpage [10]. To support continuous improvement, the official webpage includes a short survey
where testers can share their feedback, report potential issues, and suggest enhancements to the
gameplay, storyline, and learning objectives.
 The release was announced on social media to invite both internal and external participants
to test the game and provide feedback on its usability, clarity, and educational effectiveness.
 As of October 17, 2025, the release announcement received a total of 715 reactions on
LinkedIn and was reposted by the National Hydropower Association [22], the International
Hydropower Association [23], and International Water Power & Dam Construction [24]. The
release was directly featured on the DOE WPTO newsletter [25], International Water Power
& Dam Construction [26], and Factor This, an industry media outlet previously known as
Renewable Energy World [27].
 As of October 17, 2025, a total of 480 downloads were recorded from participants located
across 55 countries. A total of 44 completed surveys were received, providing insights into
the background of the respondents. 86% of participants reported a post-high school background,
7% were middle school students, and the remaining 7% included grade school and high school
participants. The range of respondent backgrounds ensured that feedback captured perspectives
from both younger students and adult learners.

4.2 Feedback and Iteration
Throughout development, feedback played a central role in refining both the educational content
and the overall player experience. Initial responses highlighted the strengths of the interactive,
gamified approach and its ability to engage audiences with hydropower concepts. At the same
time, participants provided valuable suggestions to improve the instruction clarity, organize
visual elements more effectively, and better balance gameplay difficulty. These insights were
carefully integrated into the final version of the game to enhance usability and learning outcomes.
 These insights were collected in part from several STEM outreach events where the Hy-
dropower Game was showcased to students, educators, and members of the public:

 • STEMapalooza, Golden, CO, November 8-9, 2024 [6]

 • Introduce a Girl to Engineering Day, Lemont, IL, February 20, 2025 [7]

 • STEMCON, Glen Ellyn, IL, April 26, 2025 [8]

 • Clean Currents 2025, Pittsburgh, PA, October 14-17, 2025 [9]

 These events provided opportunities to collect feedback from a broad audience, ranging from
middle school and high school students to educators, engineers, and industry professionals. The
most common suggestions included:

 • Building a strong storyline to make the overall game experience more compelling and
 immersive.

 • Providing clear instructions about the objectives in each level.

 • Improving the organization and readability of visual elements within the gameplay inter-
 face.

 • Offering multiple control options, such as arrow keys, scroll wheel integration, and virtual
 buttons.

 • Simplifying player controls to make the game more intuitive for first-time users.

 This iterative process of showcasing, testing, and refining the game helped ensure that the
final product is both accessible and educationally effective, while maintaining an engaging game-
play experience for a wide range of audiences.

5 Broader Impacts and Future Work
5.1 Educational Potential
The Hydropower Game can be used as an educational tool in a variety of contexts, includ-
ing classrooms, outreach events, and DOE-supported initiatives aimed at strengthening the
hydropower and energy workforce pipeline. By presenting hydropower concepts through an in-
teractive and engaging format, the game lowers the barrier to understanding complex energy
systems and helps students, educators, and the general public build a foundational knowledge
of hydropower technology.
 In addition to classroom deployment, the game can complement educational and workforce
development efforts supported by the U.S. DOE’s WPTO. For example, initiatives such as
the Hydropower Collegiate Competition [28] challenge university teams to solve real-world hy-
dropower and renewable energy problems, fostering technical expertise and interdisciplinary
collaboration. The Hydropower Game can serve as an entry point for these programs by in-
troducing players to fundamental hydropower principles before they engage in more advanced
engineering and policy challenges. It can also provide a gaming pathway to engage with K-12
classrooms and/or the general public.
 By making hydropower concepts accessible to a broad audience, the game supports public
awareness, enhances STEM engagement, and helps inspire the next generation of energy profes-
sionals. Through integration with formal and informal educational initiatives, it also aligns with
DOE’s broader goals of advancing energy literacy and preparing a skilled workforce to support
the nation’s energy security and future energy needs.

5.2 Future Extensions
Several potential future developments could expand the scope and impact of the Hydropower
Game, enhancing its educational value, interactivity, and accessibility:

 • Cascading reservoir operations: Introduce levels focused on hydropower systems with
 multiple connected reservoirs, where the player would learn about the coupling dynamics
 between upstream and downstream facilities. This would demonstrate how operational
 decisions at one reservoir propagate through the system and influence generation, storage,
 and environmental flows across the entire cascade.

 • Advanced energy market interactions: Extend the game to simulate more complex
 interactions between hydropower and other energy technologies, such as nuclear, natural
 gas, and other renewable energy sources. While the current version introduces market price
 signals, future levels could allow players to explore bidding strategies, ancillary services,
 and how hydropower contributes to grid reliability and energy security.

 • Fully interactive 3D environments: Transition from the current 2.5D projection to a
 fully interactive 3D environment, providing players with immersive visuals and enhanced
 gameplay. This would enable more intuitive exploration of hydropower plants, river sys-
 tems, and grid infrastructure while maintaining scientific accuracy.

 17

 • Integration into online educational platforms: Host the game on widely used online
 platforms to reach a broader audience and remove technical barriers associated with down-
 loading and running an executable file on a local machine. This transition would make the
 tool compatible across multiple operating systems and facilitate its use in classrooms and
 outreach events. This transition could also support direct linkages to the WPTO-funded
 REDi Island project [21], a virtual, renewable-energy-powered world home to more than
 a dozen water power waystations.

 • Multiplayer gameplay: Enable collaborative or competitive multiplayer modes through
 online platforms, allowing students to work together to manage shared hydropower sys-
 tems or compete to optimize operations. This feature would introduce social learning
 dynamics, foster teamwork, and make the game more engaging in classroom and outreach
 environments.

 • Expanded learning pathways and assessment: Develop additional educational re-
 sources, such as quizzes, progress dashboards, and integrated assessments, to help in-
 structors measure learning outcomes. These features would make the game a stronger
 complement to formal STEM curricula and DOE-supported workforce development pro-
 grams.

5.3 Scaling and Deployment
The Hydropower Game provides a strong foundation for future DOE outreach activities and has
the potential to evolve into a more comprehensive and widely adopted learning platform. With
additional investment, the game could be expanded to include more advanced topics, improved
interactivity, and integration with online educational ecosystems to reach broader audiences,
from K–12 students to university classrooms and industry professionals.
 Future scaling could be supported through opportunities such as the WPTO’s Sapling Pro-
gram [1] or larger lab call funding opportunities that focus on education, outreach, and workforce
development for water power-specific audiences as well as to meet multi-technology hybrid energy
systems interests. These investments would enable the development of additional levels, richer
3D environments, and advanced learning pathways that align with DOE’s goals of improving
energy literacy and preparing a skilled workforce.
 By leveraging these potential funding opportunities, the game could grow beyond a stan-
dalone educational tool into a versatile platform for nationwide deployment, serving as both a
classroom resource and a public engagement tool to raise awareness of hydropower’s role in the
nation’s evolving energy landscape.

6 Conclusions
This report presents the development of the Hydropower Game, an interactive educational tool
designed to make hydropower concepts accessible to a broad audience while inspiring interest in
energy-related careers. Developed with the support from the U.S. DOE WPTO Seedling Pro-
gram, the game combines scientific accuracy, engaging gameplay, and narrative-driven learning
to teach fundamental principles of hydropower generation, operation, and integration into the
nation’s energy system. The game was designed with flexibility to ensure it remains aligned
with DOE WPTO goals and priorities.
 The game guides players through a progressive learning experience, from understanding
basic relationships between water flow and power generation to exploring the dynamics of dam
operations, PSH facilities, energy markets, and environmental considerations. By embedding
these concepts into an interactive storyline, the game transforms complex topics into intuitive
learning experiences, making it suitable for students, educators, and lifelong learners alike.
 Throughout its development, the game was iteratively refined based on continuous feedback
from the project team, technical experts, and public demonstrations at STEM outreach events
such as STEMapalooza [6], Introduce a Girl to Engineering Day [7], STEMCON [8], and Clean
Currents [9]. These engagements validated the game’s usability and educational impact while
informing improvements to the gameplay, storyline, and interface.
 The Hydropower Game establishes a foundation for broader DOE-supported outreach and
workforce development initiatives by lowering the barrier to understanding hydropower tech-
nologies and operations. By making technical concepts approachable and engaging, the tool
supports the DOE’s objectives of advancing energy literacy, fostering public awareness of hy-
dropower’s role in the national energy landscape, and inspiring the next generation of energy
professionals.
 Looking forward, there is significant potential to expand and scale the platform with addi-
tional investment. Future developments could include new levels exploring cascading reservoir
systems and advanced market interactions, integration with online educational platforms for
broader accessibility, and the transition to fully interactive 3D environments for a richer user
experience. Such scaling could be supported through opportunities like the WPTO Sapling
Program [1] or larger lab call fundings dedicated to education, outreach, and workforce develop-
ment. These efforts would transform the Hydropower Game from a standalone offline learning
tool into a versatile online platform supporting multi-player competition, formal curricula, pub-
lic engagement, and DOE’s broader strategy to strengthen the nation’s energy workforce and
ensure long-term energy security.
 In summary, the Hydropower Game demonstrates the potential of interactive, narrative-
driven tools to bridge the gap between public outreach, STEM education, and workforce de-
velopment. By combining scientific rigor with engaging gameplay, it serves as a model for how
educational innovation can empower learners, strengthen energy literacy, and support national
priorities in water power and energy systems.
