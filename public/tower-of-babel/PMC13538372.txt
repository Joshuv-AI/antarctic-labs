A hybrid CNN–LSTM approach for large-scale Twitter sentiment analysis with explainable artificial intelligence
Authors: Zeynep Kılıç, Emrah Aslan, Yıldırım Ozupak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sentiment analysis of social media data has become an essential research topic due to the widespread use of platforms such as Twitter for expressing public opinion. However, the short, informal, and context-dependent nature of tweets poses significant challenges for accurate sentiment classification. In this study, a comprehensive sentiment analysis framework is proposed using a hybrid Convolutional Neural Network–Long Short-Term Memory (CNN–LSTM) architecture. The proposed model integrates the local feature extraction capability of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with the sequential and contextual modeling strength of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The framework is evaluated on a large-scale and balanced Twitter dataset containing over 690,000 tweets labeled as positive, negative, and neutral. Multiple deep learning models, including CNN, LSTM, Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM), and the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid model, are systematically compared using standard evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Experimental results demonstrate that the CNN–LSTM hybrid model achieves the best overall performance, with an accuracy of 97.73%, precision of 97.74%, recall of 96.73%, and an F1-score of 97.73%, outperforming all baseline models. Furthermore, model interpretability is enhanced using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), which reveal that sentiment predictions are driven by semantically meaningful and sentiment-relevant words. The experimental findings suggest that the proposed approach provides a strong balance between predictive performance and interpretability for large-scale Twitter sentiment analysis tasks.

Introduction

The rapid growth of social media platforms has fundamentally transformed the way individuals express opinions, share experiences, and engage with public discourse. Among these platforms, Twitter (now X) has emerged as one of the most influential microblogging services, where users communicate thoughts and reactions in real time through short textual messages1. As a result, Twitter has become a valuable source of large-scale, user-generated data for analyzing public opinion, brand perception, political attitudes, and social trends. In this context, sentiment analysis has gained increasing importance as a computational technique for automatically identifying and categorizing the emotional polarity of textual content2.

Despite its significance, sentiment analysis on Twitter data remains a challenging task. Tweets are typically short, informal, and noisy, often containing slang, abbreviations, emojis, hashtags, and ambiguous expressions. Moreover, sentiment is frequently context-dependent, and the same word or phrase may convey different emotional meanings depending on its surrounding context3. These characteristics limit the effectiveness of traditional lexicon-based approaches and conventional machine learning methods, which rely heavily on handcrafted features and struggle to capture complex semantic relationships. The challenge becomes even more pronounced when distinguishing neutral sentiment, which often lacks explicit emotional cues and is expressed through uncertainty or informational language.

Recent advances in deep learning have led to significant improvements in sentiment analysis performance4. Models such as CNNs have demonstrated strong capability in capturing local lexical patterns and sentiment-bearing n-grams, while LSTM networks and their bidirectional variants have proven effective in modeling sequential dependencies and contextual information in text5. However, relying on a single deep learning architecture may not be sufficient to fully exploit the diverse linguistic characteristics of social media data6. CNN-based models may overlook long-range dependencies, whereas recurrent models may fail to explicitly emphasize local patterns that are critical for sentiment discrimination7.

To address these limitations, hybrid deep learning architectures that integrate multiple modeling strategies have attracted growing attention8. By combining CNNs and LSTMs, hybrid models aim to jointly capture both local feature representations and long-range contextual dependencies, offering a more comprehensive understanding of sentiment expressions9. Nevertheless, there remains a need for systematic evaluation of such hybrid approaches on large-scale and balanced Twitter datasets, as well as for improving the interpretability of deep learning–based sentiment classifiers, which are often criticized as black-box models.

Motivated by these challenges, this study proposes a comprehensive Twitter sentiment analysis framework based on a CNN–LSTM hybrid architecture. The proposed approach leverages convolutional layers to extract salient local lexical features and LSTM layers to model sequential and contextual information. In addition, multiple deep learning models including CNN, LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and the proposed hybrid model are evaluated and compared under a unified experimental setting. To enhance transparency and trustworthiness, model interpretability is further investigated using SHAP, enabling an analysis of how individual words contribute to sentiment predictions.

The main contributions of this study can be summarized as follows: (i) a large-scale and balanced Twitter sentiment dataset is employed to ensure robust and unbiased evaluation; (ii) a comparative analysis of multiple deep learning architectures is conducted to assess their effectiveness in sentiment classification; (iii) a CNN–LSTM hybrid model is proposed and shown to outperform standalone architectures across multiple performance metrics; and (iv) SHAP-based interpretability analysis is incorporated to provide insights into the model’s decision-making process.

Unlike existing CNN–LSTM sentiment analysis studies that mainly focus on domain-specific or relatively small datasets, the proposed framework is designed and evaluated on a large-scale and balanced multi-class Twitter dataset containing more than 690,000 tweets. In addition, this study combines comparative benchmarking, SHAP-based explainability, and hybrid deep learning modeling within a unified experimental framework. Another important contribution is the detailed analysis of neutral sentiment behavior, which is often overlooked in prior studies. Furthermore, the proposed framework emphasizes computational efficiency and practical deployability by achieving high classification performance without relying on computationally expensive transformer architectures.

The section “Results and discussion” presents and discusses the experimental results. Finally, the section “Discussion” concludes the paper and outlines directions for future research.

Literature review

In recent years, sentiment analysis of social media data has attracted considerable attention from researchers due to its wide range of applications in areas such as marketing, finance, public opinion analysis, and decision support systems. With the rapid growth of platforms like Twitter, numerous studies have explored different computational approaches for extracting sentiment from short and informal text. Existing research spans a broad spectrum of methodologies, including lexicon-based techniques, traditional machine learning algorithms, and deep learning–based models. This section reviews representative studies in the literature, focusing on their methodological approaches, datasets, and key findings, and highlights the research gaps that motivate the proposed hybrid CNN–LSTM framework10.

Biradar et al. utilized Apache Hadoop and Hive to process large-scale Twitter data for brand sentiment analysis. Their methodology includes unsupervised clustering into domains followed by n-gram/TF-IDF feature extraction and classification, achieving 80% accuracy and 1.5x faster speeds than traditional databases11. Amirshahi and Lahmiri proposed a hybrid LSTM-CNN-Attention model for crypto price prediction, integrating Twitter sentiment data. Using 14–21 day sequences yielded the lowest RMSE. Findings show sentiment significantly improves predictions for over 70% of cryptocurrencies12. Aljedaani et al. proposed a hybrid sentiment approach for the airline industry, combining lexicon-based methods with deep learning. They found that TextBlob annotations outperformed original labels, with an LSTM-GRU hybrid achieving a peak 0.97 accuracy, surpassing VADER and Afinn13. Bibi et al. proposed an unsupervised framework using Concept-based Sentiment Analysis and hierarchical clustering. By ensembling different linkage methods, they achieved results comparable to supervised learning without manual labeling14. Leelawat et al. analyzed English tweets on Thai tourism during COVID-19. SVM was best for sentiment (77.4%), while Random Forest led intention analysis (95.4%). Results showed pandemic and politics are the main travel barriers15.

Context-aware hybrid frameworks such as RoBERTa-BiLSTM have shown strong performance in sentiment analysis by combining transformer-based contextual embeddings with sequential modeling capabilities1. In parallel, NLP-based automatic evaluation systems have increasingly utilized deep learning and semantic representation techniques to improve text understanding and prediction accuracy in educational assessment tasks16,17. Furthermore, recent advancements in abstractive text summarization have highlighted the capability of attentional peephole convolutional LSTM architectures to effectively capture both local and long-range textual dependencies18,19.

Sunitha et al. analyzed COVID-19 sentiments from India and Europe using an ensemble of GRU and CapsNet. Combining TF-IDF and Word2Vec embeddings, the model achieved up to 97.28% accuracy in classifying emotions like anger, sad, and joy20. Wang et al. provide a comprehensive survey of Twitter Sentiment Analysis (TSA), categorizing recent algorithms and applications in text mining. The study maps current research trends, focusing on how computers process the subjective nature of opinions and sentiments in microblogging data21. Hassan et al. used lexicon sentiment analysis and supervised machine learning on 15,000 tweets to explore cryptocurrency through emotion theory. They found 33% positive sentiment and 18% anticipation, suggesting high investor confidence and reaction to price bubbles22. Albladi et al. systematically reviewed TSA using NLP, focusing on Transformers (BERT, GPT). They evaluate machine, deep, and hybrid models, emphasizing how pre-processing and feature extraction impact sectors like marketing and health23. Karim et al. developed an ensemble learning model (LRD) using TF-IDF weighting on Kaggle data. By combining multiple classifiers like SVM and Logistic Regression, they achieved 90.5% accuracy, significantly improving sentiment-based recommendation systems24.

Alsekait et al. combined five ML and three DL algorithms, including LSTM, CNN, and Random Forest, on Facebook and Twitter data. LSTM proved superior with high accuracy, boosting overall framework performance by up to 20.9% over existing models25. Ahamad and Mishra developed the ESIHE_AML model to convert handwriting images to digital text and analyze emotions. Achieving over 90% accuracy on IAM and Twitter datasets, it classifies both polarity and complex emotions like contempt and disgust26. Smith-Mutegi et al. analyzed three years of X posts (2020–2023) regarding AI in STEM education. Using machine learning sentiment analysis, they found that public perception is mostly neutral to positive, though some wariness exists. These findings aim to guide policymakers in improving public awareness and AI implementation27. Saravanos and Kanavos developed a model predicting U.S. stock indices by merging historical prices with Twitter sentiment. Using Deep Neural Networks, they found that tweet diffusion and influencer activity significantly refine accuracy, effectively handling high-dimensional data without sparsity issues28. Sam et al. analyzed 8,855 tweets to gauge customer perceptions of the metaverse in hospitality. Using Python, they found 60.9% positive sentiment. Topic modeling identified six key themes: gaming, virtual events, sightseeing, travel, business, and blockchain, marking a pioneering study in social media-based metaverse CX29.

Several recent studies have explored aspect-based sentiment analysis by integrating contextual transformer representations with graph-based dependency modeling. For instance, hybrid BERT-GCN frameworks have demonstrated strong capability in capturing semantic and syntactic relationships for comprehensive sentiment dissection tasks30. Similarly, recent research on Urdu NLP has highlighted the effectiveness of transfer learning, dual graph convolutional networks, graph attention mechanisms, and biaffine attention models for aspect-category sentiment detection and opinion extraction in low-resource languages31–34. In addition, named entity recognition and corpus construction studies such as the Uzbek News Corpus35 and deep learning–based Urdu aspect targeting approaches36 have contributed valuable resources and methodologies for multilingual NLP research. Recent generative implicit opinion mining studies have further shown that prompt-based contextual learning can significantly improve the extraction of hidden sentiment expressions37.

Materials and methods

This section describes the materials and methods employed in this study to perform Twitter sentiment analysis. It outlines the dataset used, the preprocessing and feature representation steps, the deep learning architectures adopted, and the training and evaluation strategies applied.

Dataset description

The dataset used in this study is a large-scale Twitter sentiment analysis dataset published on the Kaggle platform38. It contains over 690,000 tweets, each manually labeled according to its expressed sentiment as Positive, Negative, or Neutral. Due to the diversity of topics covered in the tweets, the dataset is not limited to a specific domain and is therefore suitable for a wide range of natural language processing and sentiment analysis tasks, including supervised machine learning and deep learning applications. One of the key strengths of this dataset is its relatively balanced distribution across sentiment classes. As presented in Table 1, positive is represented in nearly equal proportions, while neutral tweets also constitute a substantial portion of the data. This balanced class distribution is particularly important for reducing classification bias and improving the robustness and generalizability of sentiment classification models. Consequently, the dataset provides a reliable foundation for training, validating, and evaluating sentiment analysis models in experimental research settings. To minimize potential data leakage and evaluation bias, duplicate tweets, empty entries, and highly repetitive samples were removed prior to model training. Furthermore, stratified train-validation-test splitting was applied after preprocessing to ensure that no overlap existed between training and evaluation subsets.

Text preprocessing and feature representation

Social media texts are inherently noisy due to informal language usage, spelling variations, abbreviations, emojis, hashtags, user mentions, and hyperlinks. Therefore, effective text preprocessing is a crucial step to improve the performance of sentiment analysis models. Although URLs and user mentions were removed during preprocessing, hashtags and emojis were partially preserved when they carried explicit sentiment information. Preliminary experiments indicated that retaining sentiment-rich emojis and emotionally expressive hashtags improved classification performance, particularly for informal and highly emotional tweets. In this study, raw tweet texts were subjected to a series of preprocessing operations, including lowercasing, removal of URLs and user mentions, elimination of unnecessary punctuation marks, and normalization of repeated characters. These steps aim to reduce noise while preserving sentiment-relevant information. Following text cleaning, the processed texts were tokenized and transformed into numerical representations suitable for deep learning models. A fixed vocabulary size of 10,000 most frequent words was used, and all sequences were padded or truncated to a uniform length. For feature representation, an Embedding layer was employed to map discrete tokens into dense, continuous vector spaces of 128 dimensions, enabling the models to capture semantic and syntactic relationships between words.

The vocabulary size of 10,000 was selected based on preliminary experiments balancing model complexity and coverage of sentiment-bearing expressions. Increasing the vocabulary beyond this threshold resulted in marginal performance improvements while significantly increasing computational cost and sparsity.

Deep learning-based models

This study investigates multiple deep learning architectures for Twitter sentiment classification, including CNN, LSTM, Bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM), and a hybrid CNN+LSTM model. These models were selected due to their proven effectiveness in capturing local patterns, sequential dependencies, and contextual information in textual data39.

CNN model

The CNN model is designed to capture local n-gram features within the text. The architecture begins with an Embedding layer initialized with a vocabulary size of 10,000 and 128-dimensional word vectors. Feature extraction is performed using a one-dimensional convolutional layer with 128 filters and a kernel size of 5, followed by a Global Max Pooling layer to reduce dimensionality and retain the most salient features40. The pooled features are passed through a fully connected Dense layer with 64 neurons and ReLU activation, along with a Dropout rate of 0.3 to mitigate overfitting. The output layer uses a Softmax activation function to perform multi-class sentiment classification41.

LSTM model

The LSTM model is employed to capture long-range dependencies and sequential patterns in textual data. After the Embedding layer (10,000 vocabulary size, 128-dimensional vectors), a single LSTM layer with 64 hidden units is applied42. To prevent overfitting, both dropout and recurrent dropout rates are set to 0.2. The final output layer utilizes the Softmax activation function to generate class probabilities for sentiment prediction43.

Bidirectional LSTM model

The Bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) model extends the standard LSTM architecture by processing the input sequence in both forward and backward directions. This design enables the model to capture contextual information from both preceding and succeeding words. The model consists of an Embedding layer with a 10,000-word vocabulary and 128-dimensional vectors, followed by a bidirectional LSTM layer with 64 hidden units. A Softmax-activated output layer is used for multi-class sentiment classification44.

CNN+LSTM hybrid model

To leverage the complementary strengths of CNNs and LSTMs, a hybrid CNN–LSTM architecture is employed. In this model, text inputs are first transformed through an Embedding layer with 128-dimensional vectors. A Conv1D layer with 64 filters, a kernel size of 3, and ReLU activation is applied to capture local lexical patterns, followed by a MaxPooling1D layer for dimensionality reduction. The extracted features are then passed to an LSTM layer with 64 hidden units to model sequential and contextual dependencies. The classification stage consists of a Dense layer with 64 neurons and ReLU activation, followed by a Softmax output layer.

Training strategy and hyperparameters

All models were trained using the Adam optimization algorithm, which adaptively updates learning rates by utilizing first- and second-order moment estimates of the gradients. This adaptive mechanism improves convergence speed and training stability, making Adam particularly suitable for deep learning–based text classification tasks. The categorical cross-entropy loss function was employed to address the multi-class sentiment classification problem and to optimize the probability distribution produced by the Softmax output layer. Model training was conducted for 10 epochs with a batch size of 64, a setting chosen to ensure efficient learning while maintaining model generalization and reducing the likelihood of overfitting.

Although the dataset is relatively large, preliminary experiments showed that performance improvements became marginal after approximately 8–10 epochs, while longer training increased the risk of overfitting and computational overhead. Therefore, the epoch number was limited to 10 and supported with early stopping mechanisms.

Experimental setup and reproducibility

To ensure experimental reproducibility, the dataset was randomly divided into training (80%), validation (10%), and testing (10%) subsets using stratified sampling to preserve class distribution across all splits. Duplicate tweets and null entries were removed prior to training in order to reduce potential data leakage and redundancy bias. The tokenizer was configured using the Keras Tokenizer API with a vocabulary size of 10,000 and an out-of-vocabulary (OOV) token for unseen words. All sequences were padded to a fixed maximum length of 50 tokens.

Random seeds were fixed to ensure reproducibility across experiments (NumPy = 42, TensorFlow = 42, Python random = 42). Early stopping with a patience value of 3 epochs and ReduceLROnPlateau scheduling were employed to improve convergence stability and prevent overfitting. All experiments were conducted using Python 3.11, TensorFlow 2.15, and Scikit-learn libraries on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM), Intel Core i9 processor, and 64 GB RAM. The average training time for the CNN–LSTM model was approximately 48 min.

SHapley Additive exPlanations

To enhance the interpretability of the deep learning–based sentiment classification models, SHAP were employed. SHAP is a model-agnostic explanation framework grounded in cooperative game theory, specifically based on Shapley values, which fairly distribute the contribution of each feature to the final prediction. In the context of sentiment analysis, SHAP quantifies the contribution of individual words or tokens to a model’s predicted sentiment class. SHAP values were computed using the DeepExplainer method with a randomly selected background sample of 1,000 tweets from the training set. Due to the computational complexity of SHAP analysis for deep learning models, explanations were generated on representative subsets rather than the entire dataset. This approach provided a practical balance between interpretability and computational efficiency. Each SHAP value represents how much a specific input feature increases or decreases the prediction score relative to a baseline (i.e., the average model output). Positive SHAP values indicate that a word contributes positively toward a particular sentiment class, while negative values indicate a suppressing effect. One of the key advantages of SHAP is its ability to provide both local explanations, which clarify individual predictions, and global explanations, which reveal overall model behavior across the dataset. This dual capability allows for the identification of sentiment-bearing words that consistently influence predictions, as well as the analysis of why a specific tweet was classified as positive, negative, or neutral46.

Results and discussion

This section presents and discusses the experimental results obtained from the proposed Twitter sentiment analysis framework. The results are analyzed through exploratory data analysis, model performance evaluation, comparative assessment of deep learning architectures, and interpretability analysis.

Exploratory data analysis results

The exploratory data analysis reveals that the Twitter dataset exhibits clear structural and linguistic characteristics that are closely related to sentiment expression. Variations in tweet length across sentiment classes indicate that users employ different communication styles depending on emotional polarity. Statistical observations from the dataset indicate that negative tweets exhibit slightly higher average sequence lengths compared to positive tweets. Neutral tweets, on the other hand, demonstrate a more restrained and informational style. These structural differences suggest that sentiment classification models must be capable of handling variable-length sequences and capturing both local and long-range contextual information. Lexical analysis further highlights distinct vocabulary patterns associated with each sentiment category. Negative sentiment is dominated by words reflecting dissatisfaction, problems, and adverse experiences, while positive sentiment is characterized by strongly evaluative and emotionally charged terms related to satisfaction, improvement, and approval.

Figure 3 presents the distribution of tweet lengths across different sentiment classes. As observed in the figure, the majority of tweets, regardless of sentiment, are relatively short, with lengths predominantly concentrated below approximately 300 characters. This observation is consistent with the inherent constraints of the Twitter platform and reflects typical user behavior. A comparison across sentiment classes reveals notable differences in text length distributions. Negative tweets tend to exhibit slightly longer lengths on average, with a more pronounced distribution in the higher character ranges compared to positive and neutral tweets. This suggests that users may employ more detailed or explanatory language when expressing negative opinions. In contrast, positive tweets are more densely concentrated in shorter lengths, indicating that positive sentiments are often conveyed more concisely. Neutral tweets display a relatively narrower distribution, suggesting more factual or informational content with limited elaboration.

Model performance analysis

The experimental results indicate that all deep learning models achieve high performance in Twitter sentiment classification, confirming the effectiveness of neural network–based approaches for this task. Among the baseline models, CNN, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM show comparable results, with Bi-LSTM providing a slight improvement due to its ability to capture bidirectional contextual information. The CNN–LSTM hybrid model achieves the best overall performance across all evaluation metrics. This improvement can be attributed to the combination of convolutional layers for local feature extraction and LSTM layers for modeling long-range dependencies. In addition, the close alignment between training and validation results indicates stable learning behavior and strong generalization capability, demonstrating that the hybrid model effectively balances accuracy and robustness.

Statistical reliability analysis

To further evaluate the robustness and statistical reliability of the proposed sentiment classification framework, all experiments were repeated five independent times using different random initialization seeds. Repeated experimentation is particularly important in deep learning–based studies because model performance may vary depending on weight initialization, stochastic optimization behavior, and batch sampling processes. Therefore, reporting only a single experimental outcome may not fully reflect the stability and reproducibility of a model.

Table 2 summarizes the mean and standard deviation values obtained across multiple runs for all evaluated architectures, including CNN, LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid model. The relatively small standard deviation values observed for all models indicate stable training behavior and consistent predictive performance. Among all evaluated approaches, the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid architecture achieved the highest overall performance while also exhibiting the lowest variability across repeated experiments. This finding confirms the robustness and strong generalization capability of the proposed framework.

In addition to repeated experimental evaluation, paired statistical significance tests were conducted between the proposed CNN–LSTM model and the baseline architectures. Specifically, paired t-tests were applied to the accuracy scores obtained from multiple runs. The resulting p-values were lower than 0.05, demonstrating that the performance improvements achieved by the proposed hybrid model are statistically significant rather than arising from random variation. These results provide additional evidence supporting the reliability and effectiveness of the proposed architecture for large-scale Twitter sentiment classification.

Discussion

This section discusses the experimental findings in depth by interpreting the observed results and relating them to existing studies in the literature. The discussion focuses on explaining the performance differences among the evaluated models, highlighting the advantages of the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid architecture, and addressing challenges such as neutral sentiment classification. In addition, the implications of the findings and potential directions for future research are considered.

Interpretation of key findings

The findings of this study demonstrate that the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid model achieves superior performance in Twitter sentiment classification compared to standalone deep learning architectures. This improvement can be primarily attributed to the complementary nature of convolutional and recurrent components. Convolutional layers are effective in capturing local lexical patterns and sentiment-bearing n-grams, while LSTM layers model long-range dependencies and contextual relationships within the text. The integration of these two mechanisms enables the hybrid model to construct richer and more discriminative representations, which directly contributes to improved classification performance. In contrast, models relying solely on convolutional or recurrent structures show limited gains, suggesting that sentiment expression in social media text cannot be fully captured by a single modeling strategy. The results indicate that both local and contextual features play a critical role in accurately identifying sentiment polarity, particularly in short and informal texts such as tweets.

Comparison with previous studies

The results of this study align with existing research highlighting the superiority of hybrid deep learning architectures for Twitter sentiment analysis. By combining convolutional layers for local feature extraction with LSTM for sequential dependencies, the proposed CNN+LSTM model captures both short-range lexical patterns and longer contextual relationships in noisy, short-form social media text. This complementary design leads to superior performance (97.73% accuracy) compared to standalone CNN, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM models on the same dataset. Table 5 compares the proposed model with 8 recent studies using deep learning for Twitter/X sentiment analysis. The proposed CNN+LSTM hybrid ranks among the top performers, achieving results comparable to advanced hybrid models on domain-specific data while using a lighter architecture suitable for general-purpose applications.

The proposed model performs exceptionally well on a large, diverse dataset, nearly matching BERT-enhanced hybrids on specialized topics (e.g., COVID-19, cryptocurrency) while avoiding the computational overhead of transformers. This supports the effectiveness of straightforward CNN-LSTM hybrids for robust, generalizable sentiment classification. Recent advancements in Twitter sentiment analysis emphasize hybrid deep learning models that integrate convolutional and recurrent components to address the challenges of short, noisy, informal text. Convolutional layers excel at detecting local n-gram patterns and sentiment-bearing phrases, while LSTM (or Bi-LSTM) layers model long-range dependencies and contextual flow. Attention mechanisms and pre-trained embeddings further enhance performance, especially in domain-specific contexts where language patterns are more consistent. The proposed CNN+LSTM model achieves 97.73% accuracy on a balanced, large-scale dataset covering diverse topics, demonstrating excellent generalization. This result is particularly notable given the inherent difficulty of multi-class tasks with a neutral category, which often introduces ambiguity and reduces overall accuracy compared to binary classification.

Challenges and class-level observations

Despite the overall strong performance, the analysis reveals that neutral sentiment remains the most challenging class to predict accurately. Unlike positive and, neutral tweets often lack explicit sentiment-bearing keywords and instead rely on ambiguous or speculative language. This linguistic characteristic leads to increased confusion between neutral and weakly polarized classes, as observed in the classification results. These findings suggest that neutral sentiment cannot be effectively captured using keyword-based cues alone and requires deeper contextual understanding. Models capable of capturing subtle semantic and sequential patterns, such as Bi-LSTM and CNN–LSTM architectures, are therefore better suited for addressing this challenge, although some ambiguity remains inherent to the data.

Practical implications and model robustness

From a practical perspective, the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid model demonstrates strong robustness and generalization capability. The stable alignment between training and validation performance indicates that the model effectively avoids overfitting and maintains consistent performance on unseen data. This robustness makes the model suitable for real-world applications involving large-scale social media analysis. Potential application domains include brand sentiment monitoring, public opinion analysis, customer feedback evaluation, and trend detection. The ability of the model to handle diverse sentiment expressions and maintain high classification accuracy highlights its practical value for decision-support systems and social media analytics platforms.

Limitations and future work

Although the proposed approach yields strong results, certain limitations should be acknowledged. This study focuses on deep learning architectures based on CNN and LSTM models and does not incorporate transformer-based approaches, which have recently shown promising performance in natural language processing tasks. Additionally, the analysis is limited to English-language tweets, which may restrict the applicability of the findings to multilingual contexts. Future research may explore the integration of transformer-based models, more extensive hyperparameter optimization, and cross-lingual sentiment analysis. Incorporating explainability methods more deeply into model evaluation may also provide further insights into decision-making processes and enhance model transparency.

Another limitation of this study is the use of randomly initialized embedding representations instead of pretrained embeddings such as Word2Vec, GloVe, or contextual transformer embeddings. Although the proposed architecture achieved strong performance, pretrained semantic representations may further improve contextual understanding and cross-domain generalization.

Future studies may further explore multilingual and low-resource language sentiment analysis, transformer-integrated hybrid architectures, graph neural networks, and attention-based mechanisms. In addition, integrating large language models and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) frameworks may provide improved contextual understanding and explainability for complex sentiment analysis tasks.

Computational efficiency and scalability

From a deployment perspective, computational efficiency is an important consideration for large-scale Twitter sentiment analysis systems. Compared to transformer-based architectures, the proposed CNN–LSTM framework requires substantially fewer trainable parameters and lower GPU memory consumption. The hybrid architecture achieves high classification accuracy while maintaining moderate training and inference costs, making it suitable for real-time or large-volume social media analytics applications. The average inference time per tweet was approximately 2.1 ms on GPU hardware, demonstrating the scalability of the proposed approach for high-throughput sentiment monitoring systems.

Ethical considerations

Although the dataset used in this study is publicly available, ethical considerations remain important in large-scale social media analytics. User-generated content may contain sensitive opinions, demographic biases, or harmful language patterns that can influence model behavior. Therefore, sentiment analysis systems should be deployed responsibly, with attention to fairness, transparency, and potential societal impacts. Future work may incorporate bias detection and fairness-aware learning strategies to improve ethical robustness.

Conclusion

In this study, a comprehensive sentiment analysis framework for Twitter data was proposed and evaluated using multiple deep learning architectures, including CNN, LSTM, Bi-LSTM, and a hybrid CNN–LSTM model. Extensive experiments conducted on a large-scale and balanced dataset demonstrated that deep learning–based approaches are highly effective in capturing sentiment patterns in social media text. Exploratory data analysis revealed distinct structural and lexical characteristics across sentiment classes, highlighting the importance of both local and contextual information in sentiment classification. The experimental results showed that while standalone CNN and recurrent models achieve strong performance, the CNN–LSTM hybrid architecture consistently outperforms all baseline models across multiple evaluation metrics. This performance gain confirms that integrating convolutional layers for local feature extraction with recurrent layers for sequential modeling leads to more robust and discriminative representations. Moreover, training and validation analyses indicated stable convergence and strong generalization capability, suggesting that the proposed hybrid model is reliable and resistant to overfitting. In addition to predictive performance, model interpretability was addressed using SHAP analysis, which provided valuable insights into the contribution of individual words to sentiment predictions. The interpretability results confirmed that the model relies on semantically meaningful and sentiment-relevant features, enhancing transparency and trustworthiness. The findings of this study demonstrate that the proposed CNN–LSTM hybrid model offers an effective and interpretable solution for large-scale Twitter sentiment analysis and can serve as a strong foundation for future research and real-world social media analytics applications.
