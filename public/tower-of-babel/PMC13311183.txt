Decade-scale contrasts in sediment metal(loid)s across Qinghai–Xizang Plateau lakes
Authors: Jun Chen, Ziyao Zhou, Libo Chen, Chengshuo Hua, Xuebin Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau capture shifting metal(loid) dynamics within an alpine ecosystem uniquely vulnerable to climate change and anthropogenic pressures. However, plateau-wide ecological risk assessments incorporating quantitative source apportionment remain scarce, leaving a critical gap that hinders targeted ecosystem conservation and precise policy interventions. Here we present a plateau-scale analysis of sediment geochemistry across 110 lakes over two decadal periods (2014 and 2024), utilizing a hybrid positive matrix factorization–random forest framework. Arsenic serves as the primary diagnostic sentinel, persistently exceeding baseline thresholds across both decades. Although mean chromium concentrations declined from 85.7 to 68.0 mg kg−1, the broader multi-metal pool exhibits remarkable regional stability, with low baseline ecological risks dominated by cadmium toxicity alongside localized high-risk anomalies in Lakes Tosu, Gahai, and Xiligou. Hybrid source apportionment demonstrates that a mixed footprint of transport emissions, transboundary atmospheric influx, and lithogenic weathering increasingly drives geochemical variance, surging from 44% to 66% over ten years, whereas the independent arsenic source remains invariant at approximately 15%. These patterns reveal that while natural weathering constrains the regional background, localized anthropogenic signatures are rapidly intensifying around emerging infrastructure corridors. This predictive framework establishes a transferable template for tracking contaminant dynamics in vulnerable alpine headwaters, providing a vital baseline for targeted, source-specific mitigation globally.

Introduction

Potentially toxic metals (hereafter referred to as “heavy metal(loid)s” for brevity, including the metalloid As and the heavy metals Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, Cd, and Pb) are persistent aquatic contaminants that can bioaccumulate and threaten ecosystem and human health [1,2]. In lakes, sediments serve as an archive and a reactive sink and source. Lake sediments are a key sink for heavy metal(loid)s, where binding to organic matter and other reactive phases controls their mobility, toxicity, and bioavailability; yet shifts in redox and geochemical conditions can remobilize these sediment-bound elements—partly via complexation with dissolved organic matter—and release them back to the water column, making sediments a potential secondary source of contamination [3,4]. However, these sediment-bound phases are not permanently stable. Shifts in redox conditions, pH, salinity, or organic-matter degradation can destabilize them and remobilize labile metal(loid)s into pore water and overlying water, thereby stressing benthic communities [5]. Therefore, sediment records can capture Anthropocene signals, although their detectability and attribution remain contested.

Metal(loid) burdens in lake sediments vary substantially across regions because anthropogenic inputs are superimposed on the natural background supply [6]. In industrialized and densely populated catchments, enrichment of Cd, Pb, Zn, and Cu is commonly associated with mining and smelting, fossil-fuel combustion, urban runoff, and agricultural activities [7,8]. In contrast, remote high-mountain lakes show appreciable accumulation of Ni and Cu owing to lithogenic materials delivered by snowmelt and glacial melt, even in the absence of local point sources [9]. Additionally, large-scale surveys further illustrate the breadth of this issue. In the United States, the National Lakes Assessment classified many sediments as “fair” based on multi-metal(loid)s guideline quotients for As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, Ni, and Zn [10,11]. Comparable concentration ranges have been reported in major temperate systems and in coastal lagoon lakes influenced by watershed inputs and hydrodynamic exchange. In comparison, Asian lowland lakes often exhibit higher maxima under intensive land use and rapid industrialization [12,13]. In China, lake-sediment metal(loid)s pollution shows pronounced regional contrasts that reflect differences in geomorphology, land use, and industrial structure [6]. In the middle and lower Yangtze River floodplain, eutrophication frequently co-occurs with metal(loid) enrichment; likewise, high organic-matter loading together with Fe and Mn redox cycling can enhance the release of particle-bound metal(loid)s under seasonal hypoxia [14,15]. Importantly, this process complicates risk assessments based solely on total concentrations [8]. Pb is a neurotoxic heavy metal. Susceptible populations, such as fetuses, children, and pregnant women, may experience greater adverse effects, and elevated placental Pb has been associated with reduced fetal body weight and body height [16]. By contrast, lithology-aware baselines are required for Yunnan–Guizhou karst lakes because carbonate dissolution and groundwater–lake exchange can regulate metal(loid)s mobility and apparent enrichment. Thus, while Northeast cold-region lakes illustrate the roles of ice cover and freeze–thaw cycling in driving sediment geochemical shifts [17,18], those on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau remain underrepresented in China-wide syntheses. Therefore, this data gap persists despite the plateau's strategic importance to regional water security and ecological integrity.

The Qinghai–Xizang Plateau hosts the world's highest-altitude and most extensive plateau lake region [19]. As such, these lakes constitute a core component of the Asian Water Tower and serve as sensitive sentinels of climate change [20,21]. Consequently, cold, arid, high-elevation lakes are experiencing rapid warming and increasing human pressures. Glacier retreat and permafrost thaw can enhance erosion, mineral exposure, and hydrological connectivity, thereby increasing lithogenic metal(loid) inputs and promoting the remobilization of previously stabilized metal(loid)s [9]. This shift elevates bioavailability and ecological risk [22]. Meanwhile, infrastructure development, mining and resource extraction, and traffic and tourism increase emissions and dust transport, amplifying anthropocene signals in remote headwaters [23,24]. Existing case studies on the plateau have also reported both anthropogenic and geogenic fingerprints. For example, moderate Cd-related ecological risk has been linked to fertilizer inputs in Xizang's “one river–two tributaries” system [25]. Catchment-scale controls on As associated with volcanic lithology and identified speciation shifts have been reported for Yamdrok Lake [26]. In addition, strong lithological control on Cr and Ni has been documented in Yarlung Tsangpo sediments and attributed to ultramafic rock weathering [27]. However, three knowledge gaps remain. First, current evidence is largely confined to localized case studies and therefore fails to capture the plateau-wide spatial heterogeneity of heavy metal(loid)s. Second, the available data rely on snapshot datasets collected with inconsistent methodologies, which preclude harmonized inter-period comparisons and robust decadal inferences. Third, this evidentiary base remains dependent on single-source apportionment approaches, making it difficult to distinguish anthropogenic inputs from natural weathering and climate-related remobilization in this complex cryospheric environment [28,29]. Addressing these three gaps requires an integrative framework that combines multi-lake evidence to resolve spatial heterogeneity, harmonized period-based comparisons to enable decadal inference, and quantitative source apportionment together with cautious driver screening to separate anthropogenic from natural and climate-related signals.

Source apportionment is critical for designing targeted strategies to mitigate sediment metal(loid) pollution. Recent sediment studies in coastal and riverine systems have further demonstrated the value of integrating source apportionment analysis with ecological risk assessment to disentangle mixed natural and anthropogenic metal(loid) signals [30,31]. These cross-system studies reinforce the methodological relevance of such integrated frameworks, although comparable applications to high-altitude lake sediments remain scarce. Positive matrix factorization (PMF) incorporates measurement uncertainty and non-negativity constraints to quantify source contributions and has been applied to plateau riverine systems [[32], [33], [34]]. Nevertheless, PMF may not capture nonlinear relationships between metal(loid) enrichment and environmental gradients [32]. While random forest (RF) models accommodate nonlinearities in complex source–response relationships, they lack the capacity to independently resolve physically interpretable sources [35,36]. Coupling source-contribution estimation with nonlinear predictive refinement via a hybrid PMF–RF strategy addresses this limitation, providing an integrative workflow for diagnosing source patterns in complex, high-elevation environments. The application of such an approach remains a notable gap in the study of lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau.

To address these gaps, this study compiled a plateau-wide database of sediment metal(loid) concentrations (Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, and Pb) from 110 lakes (A total of 152 observations were yielded) across the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau. A coupled PMF–RF modeling framework was implemented across two harmonized comparison periods to: (1) characterize spatial heterogeneity and concentration variability; (2) apportion the relative contributions of natural weathering, anthropogenic activities, and traffic-related emissions while tracking shifts in source structures; and (3) identify high-risk lake zones by exploring their associations with socioeconomic, hydrochemical, biological, and sedimentary variables. These findings support targeted protection efforts and provide a transferable template for high-elevation lake systems worldwide.

Materials and methods

Study area

The Qinghai–Xizang Plateau (26°00′–39°47′ N, 73°19′–104°47′ E) is the world's highest and largest plateau, with a mean elevation exceeding 4000 m [19]. It contains the planet's most extensive high-altitude lake region, comprising approximately 1200 lakes with a combined area of 45,000 km2, accounting for about 52% of China's total lake area [20,37]. Under the combined influence of the mid-latitude westerlies and the Indian monsoon, the region exhibits a typical alpine cold-arid climate. Mean annual temperature decreases from approximately 20 °C in the southeast to below −6 °C in the northwest, and precipitation declines from approximately 2000 mm to less than 50 mm along the same gradient. Plateau lakes are mainly recharged by glacier meltwater, precipitation, and groundwater, resulting in pronounced seasonal water-level fluctuations. A diversity of lake types occurs, including freshwater, brackish, and saline lakes. Most lakes formed in fault-depression or glacial-erosion basins associated with long-term collision between the Indian and Eurasian plates. The surrounding landscapes are dominated by alpine meadow and desert vegetation and exhibit clear elevational zonation of biodiversity. Endemic species, including the Grus nigricollis and the Pantholops hodgsonii, depend on lake–wetland ecosystems. In recent decades, glacier retreat has driven the expansion of some lakes, whereas intensified animal husbandry and tourism have imposed increasing ecological pressure on littoral zones.

We briefly summarize population distribution and transportation infrastructure to provide a regional-scale background on anthropogenic activity across the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau. This background is relevant to the subsequent analysis of socio-economic characteristics (SECs) and the interpretation of traffic-related sources. The Qiji Line, a population-geographical dividing line connecting Qilian County and Jilong County, separates a densely populated, better-connected southeastern sector from a sparsely populated northwestern sector. Although the two sectors are broadly comparable in area, approximately 93% of the population is concentrated in the southeastern half [38,39]. Transportation infrastructure shows a similar pattern, with a denser road network in the east and major corridors centered on Xining and Lhasa, along the principal national highways [40]. This description is intended only to characterize broad human-activity gradients at the regional scale and to provide context for later source interpretation, rather than to imply a simple direct correspondence between population distribution and sediment heavy metal(loid) concentrations.

Data sources

Heavy-metal(loid) data for lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau were compiled from the Scientific Expedition Report on Lakes of the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau [41] and additional literature-derived records. These were then subsequently harmonized for cross-study and cross-period comparisons.

Data compilation and period definition

We obtained primary heavy-metal(loid) data for lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau collected during 2019–2023 from the Scientific Expedition Report on Lakes of the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau [41]. In addition, the remaining records were compiled from published sources identified through a systematic literature search and reference snowballing. Key analytical metadata extracted from these sources, including digestion procedures, instrumental platforms, detection limits, and quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) information (Supplementary Table S1). This report provides surface-sediment heavy-metal(loid) concentrations for 110 lakes. We also compiled a database of heavy-metal(loid) concentrations in lake sediments across the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau and supplemented it with records identified through a systematic Web of Science search conducted on August 1, 2025. The search covered the Web of Science Core Collection, MEDLINE, Data Citation Index, SciELO Citation Index, and Chinese Science Citation Database, and was restricted to English-language articles and reviews. Search terms combined plateau names, lake sediments, and target metal(loid)s: (Qinghai–Xizang Plateau OR Qinghai–Tibet Plateau) AND lake∗ AND sediment∗ AND (heavy metal(loid)∗ OR trace metal(loid)∗ OR Cu OR Zn OR Cd OR Pb OR Cr OR Ni OR As). The seven target elements—Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, and Pb—were selected because they were the most consistently reported total metal(loid)s across the compiled datasets. These elements represent the principal environmentally relevant source signals currently recognized on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau, including lithogenic, atmospheric, agricultural, and traffic-related inputs. This specific suite strikes a deliberate balance between regional relevance and cross-study comparability, facilitating a robust plateau-scale interperiod analysis.

Based on peer-reviewed literature, the snowball method was further used to trace additional published records and, where explicitly cited in the literature, publicly accessible government datasets. Thus, except for the 2019–2023 expedition dataset, the remaining records were compiled from published sources; unpublished data were not used. Records were retained only when they met the following criteria: (i) the sample represented surface lake sediment, or the reported interval could be harmonized to the upper 0–20 cm; (ii) at least one of the seven target heavy metal(loid)s was reported as a total concentration on a dry-weight basis, or could be converted to that basis; (iii) the lake identity and sampling year were clearly reported, allowing assignment to the 2014 or 2024 period; and (iv) duplicate reports of the same lake and survey were removed, retaining the record with the more complete elemental coverage and clearer analytical metadata.

To facilitate comparisons of changes in metal(loid) concentrations in lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau, the compiled data were grouped into two periods: before and including 2014 (the 2014 period) and 2015–2024 (the 2024 period) (Fig. 1). We selected 2014 was selected as the final pre-breakpoint year because 2015 is widely recognized as a step change in China's water-environment governance and enforcement. This shift included the implementation of the revised Environmental Protection Law and the issuance of the Water Pollution Prevention and Control Action Plan (known as the Water Ten Plan), which are commonly treated as policy shocks in empirical environmental studies [[42], [43], [44]]. Temporal harmonization and cross-study comparability

Since the sediment database was compiled from multiple literature sources and the Second Tibetan Plateau Scientific Expedition report [41], identical field and laboratory protocols could not be assumed across all records. Even for lakes represented in both periods, comparability should be understood primarily at the harmonized lake level. The retained data refer only to surface sediments, or to core-top intervals that could be converted to a 0–20 cm equivalent, rather than to complete sediment-core profiles or strictly identical repeated sampling points. To improve temporal comparability, we applied the following harmonization procedures before dividing the dataset into the 2014 and 2024 periods. First, only surface-lake-sediment data were retained, and all concentrations were converted to dry-weight units (mg kg−1). Second, when the reported sampling interval did not fall within 0–20 cm, a weighted-average approach was used to approximate surface-sediment concentrations and improve vertical comparability. Specifically, for studies reporting multiple contiguous sub-intervals within the upper 20 cm, the harmonized concentration was calculated as C0–20=∑(mihi)/∑hi, where mi is the concentration in sub-interval i and hi is the thickness of its overlap with the target 0–20 cm layer. If a study reported only a single surface interval entirely within the upper 20 cm (e.g., 0–5, 0–10, or 0–15 cm), that value was retained directly as the best available surface-sediment observation. This procedure preserves the relative contribution of each sublayer and reduces bias caused by unequal sampling intervals. Third, studies were screened to retain only records reporting scientifically valid total metal(loid) determinations in sediments. Where available, metadata on digestion procedures, analytical platforms, detection limits, and quality-control information were extracted and summarized in Supplementary Table S1. Because reporting varied across sources, these details are summarized by analytical method category and element-specific detection limit range, rather than for each individual record. Finally, sensitivity analyses were performed using (i) the 42 lakes available in both periods, (ii) a method-restricted subset with more complete analytical metadata, and (iii) resampling procedures to evaluate the influence of unequal sample sizes between periods. These steps do not eliminate all cross-source heterogeneity, but they improve the robustness of the period-based comparison. All analyses in this study were based on total concentrations of heavy metal(loid)s in surface lake sediments (mg kg−1, dry-weight basis) compiled from published datasets and scientific expedition reports. No sequential extraction data, speciation data, or bioavailable fraction data were included. The pollution and ecological-risk assessments presented here reflect total metal(loid) burdens rather than specific chemical forms.

To evaluate whether the harmonized datasets differed across the full sample, we compared heavy metal(loid) concentrations, geo-accumulation index (Igeo) values, and potential ecological risk index (PERI) values between the 2014 and 2024 periods using two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests. This nonparametric approach was adopted because the compiled datasets were unbalanced across periods, and most variables exhibited strongly skewed, non-normal distributions (Table 1). To account for multiple comparisons, P-values were further adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Statistical significance was defined at adjusted P < 0.05. These tests were intended to compare the overall distributions of the harmonized period-based datasets rather than to represent strict repeated-measures changes at identical lakes. Assessment of sediment pollution levels

To characterize the spatial heterogeneity and ecological implications of heavy metal(loid)s in lake sediments on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau, we applied three complementary methods. We used Igeo to quantify the degree of anthropogenic enrichment relative to natural backgrounds, and PERI to integrate contamination levels with metal(loid) toxicity. All index calculations were based on total concentrations in surface sediments (on a dry-weight basis) and on the unified background-value system described below. In addition, we conducted a Monte Carlo simulation to propagate parameter uncertainty and derive probabilistic risk estimates.

The background values used for pollution and ecological risk assessment were primarily taken from the published literature rather than from an independent, plateau-wide geochemical baseline survey. Specifically, the background values of As, Cd, Cr, Cu, Pb, and Zn used for Igeo and PERI were derived from the preindustrial lacustrine sediment record reported for four sediment cores from Qinghai Lake, using concentrations from the approximately 1920 interval as a regional low-impact baseline [45]. Previous work indicated relatively weak anthropogenic disturbance before 1950 and relatively stable ecological risk in that sediment record, supporting the use of these published core-based values as representative pre-anthropogenic backgrounds for plateau lake sediments. Because Ni was not reported in that sediment-core dataset, its background value was taken from the published regional topsoil background of the Lake Qinghai catchment rather than from the national soil background, to better reflect the geochemical characteristics of the northeastern Qinghai–Xizang Plateau [46].

Igeo index

The Igeo index, proposed by Müller [48], is a widely used metric for assessing the pollution status of sediments and other depositional media. It is defined as:(1)Igeo=log2(Ci1.5Cb,i)where Igeo is the geo-accumulation index; Ci (mg kg−1) is the measured total concentration of element i in surface sediment; Cb,i (mg kg−1) is the corresponding regional background value for element i; and 1.5 is a correction factor introduced to account for possible lithogenic variability in the background. Thus, the Igeo calculation assumes that the measured sediment concentration can be evaluated relative to a pre-anthropogenic geochemical background after correction for natural variability. The classification of Igeo and the background values used are shown in Supplementary Table S3.

PERI

The potential ecological risk index (PERI), developed by Hakanson [49], evaluates the potential threat posed by heavy metal(loid)s from a sedimentological perspective and has been widely applied to soils and aquatic sediments [50]. Here, PERI was calculated from measured total concentrations in surface sediments, relative to the regional background values described above, and then weighted by conventional toxic-response coefficients to reflect the relative hazard of different elements. Accordingly, PERI is therefore used as a screening-based comparative indicator of potential ecological risk rather than as an absolute quantification of toxicity. The method is expressed as follows:(2)PIi=CiCb,i(3)Er,i=Tr,i×PIi(4)PERI=∑i=1nEr,iwhere PIᵢ is the pollution index of metal(loid) i; Er,i is the ecological risk factor of metal(loid) i; Tr,i is the toxic-response coefficient of metal(loid) i; and n is the number of metal(loid)s considered. Following the conventional Hakanson framework [49], we used toxic-response coefficients of 5 for Cu, 5 for Pb, 1 for Zn, 5 for Ni, 2 for Cr, 10 for As, and 30 for Cd. Because the element assemblage analyzed here differs slightly from the original PERI formulation, the ecological-risk classification thresholds were adjusted with reference to the corresponding toxicity coefficients [51]. Moreover, these were interpreted primarily as relative screening categories rather than plateau-specific absolute ecological thresholds. The classification of PERI is shown in Supplementary Table S4.

Monte Carlo simulation

Monte Carlo simulation is a probabilistic technique that propagates uncertainty in model input parameters into a distribution of output risk levels and is therefore suitable for ecological risk assessment of heavy metal(loid)s [52]. After specifying the functional relationships in the PERI-based evaluation, 10,000 iterations were conducted to account for variability in key parameters, such as toxic-response coefficients and background values, and to derive risk estimates at the 95% confidence level. The procedure comprised four steps: identifying random variables in the assessment model (e.g., metal(loid) concentrations and background values); assigning appropriate probability distribution functions to these variables; repeatedly sampling from these distributions to generate PERI values; and using the simulated PERI distribution to estimate the likelihood of different ecological-risk grades.

Source apportionment

We implemented a three-tier analytical framework to identify the primary drivers of heavy metal(loid)s in sediments across the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau. Initially, Mantel and correlation analyses were used to screen for broad associations between metal(loid) patterns and environmental or anthropogenic variables. This screening provided the groundwork for a PMF receptor model, which was used to extract latent source factors and their corresponding scores. These outputs were then used to train RF regression models; by using the PMF-derived scores as predictors of observed concentrations, the RF step provided a nonlinear evaluation of the framework's performance. Rather than functioning independently, the RF model served to refine the source–element relationships and confirm how effectively the PMF factors captured the observed geochemical variance Supplementary Fig. S1.

Mantel test

The Mantel test is a nonparametric procedure that evaluates the correlation between two distance (or dissimilarity) matrices. It has been widely used in ecology, microbial community analyses, and environmental pollution studies because it can accommodate complex multivariate structures. In this study, heavy-metal(loid) data from lake sediments were converted into a distance matrix. We constracted five additional distance matrices to represent major groups of environmental and anthropogenic factors: (i) SECs, represented by population density and per-capita income; (ii) lake physical characteristics (PCs), including lake area, elevation, and water transparency; (iii) water-quality and hydrochemical characteristics (WQHCs), including pH, salinity, nutrients, chlorophyll a, dissolved organic matter, suspended solids, and some major ions; (iv) biotic community characteristics (BCs), including zooplankton biomass and diversity indices; and (v) basic sediment characteristics (SCs), including sediment total phosphorus and major elemental contents. In addition, we computed pairwise distance matrices among heavy-metal(loid) species to examine their internal associations.

All data were obtained from the Scientific Expedition Report on Lakes of the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau [41] and related peer-reviewed literature. Before analysis, we addressed missing data in the Mantel framework at two levels. First, at the lake-record level, we retained only lakes with concurrent auxiliary information for socioeconomic conditions, lake characteristics, water quality, biological communities, and sediment properties. We initially assembled 54 lakes for the 2014 period and 98 for the 2024 period for Mantel screening; after excluding lakes with excessive missingness in the auxiliary variables, 32 and 81 lakes were retained for the final Mantel analysis, respectively (Supplementary Table S5). Second, at the variable level, missing values were handled on a per-category basis. Numerical variables with <20% missingness were retained and imputed using the median, whereas non-core variables with >20% missingness were removed. Core variables, including population density, lake area, water pH, water salinity, dissolved organic matter, bacterial biomass, and total phosphorus in sediments, were retained and imputed using multiple imputation or k-nearest neighbors approaches to reduce bias from discarding key information. Thus, imputation was applied only to auxiliary explanatory variables within the Mantel framework and not to the compiled heavy-metal(loid) concentration dataset itself.

PMF model

Positive matrix factorization (PMF), a receptor model recommended by the United States Environmental Protection Agency, is a quantitative method for resolving latent source profiles and estimating their contributions to receptor samples without requiring a priori source information [53]. The PMF model can be expressed as:(5)xi,j=∑k=1pgi,kfk,j+ei,j(6)Q=∑i=1n∑j=1m(ei,jui,j)2(7)ui,j={56Ljxi,j≤Lj(δxi,j)2+Lj2xi,j≥Ljwhere X = (xi,j), G = (gi,k), F = (fk,j), and E = (ei,j) denote the sample concentration, source-contribution, source-profile, and residual matrices, respectively; Q is the objective function defined in PMF, and a value close to the degrees of freedom of the dataset indicates a better fit; U = (ui,j) is the uncertainty matrix; Lj is the method detection limit for element j; δ is the relative standard deviation; i, j, k denote samples, elements, and potential sources, respectively; and n, m, and p are the corresponding numbers of samples, elements and potential sources.

In this study, concentration data for seven heavy metals (Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, and Pb) in sediment from the 2014 and 2024 periods were used as inputs to the PMF model. The summed metal(loid) load (SUM) was used only as a descriptive auxiliary metric and as an RF response variable, rather than as a species used to define the PMF source profile. To reduce the influence of less-reliable species, Cd and Ni were downweighted by treating them as weak variables and inflating their uncertainties to three times their original values. Four samples exhibiting abnormal fluctuations were excluded before final model fitting. Candidate solutions with three to seven factors were evaluated using multiple diagnostic criteria, including the agreement between the robust and expected objective functions (Qrobust, Qexpected, and Qrobust/Qexpected), displacement (DISP) swaps, and bootstrap (BS) mapping percentages (Supplementary Table S6). The final four-factor solution was selected based on overall fit, solution stability, and environmental interpretability. Although some higher-factor solutions produced slightly lower Qrobust/Qexpected values, they showed weaker stability and/or factor splitting, particularly for the 2024 dataset, and did not provide a better balance between statistical performance and source identifiability.

Residual diagnostics for the selected four-factor solution are presented in Supplementary Fig. S2. The scaled residuals of the seven metal(loid)s were generally unimodal and approximately centered around zero, indicating an acceptable overall fit and no evidence of severe model misfit. Rotational ambiguity was further assessed by scanning the rotational parameter (Fpeak) and examining changes in Qrobust/Qexpected (Supplementary Fig. S3). For the 2014 dataset, the minimum value occurred at approximately Fpeak = −0.2, whereas the 2024 dataset was relatively stable over the Fpeak range of −0.2 to 0.2. We therefore used Fpeak = −0.2 for the final runs to maintain cross-period comparability while preserving solution stability and interpretability. The selected solution also passed DISP and bootstrap diagnostics, supporting the robustness of the PMF results and their suitability for subsequent coupling with RF.

For clarity, FAC1–FAC4 denote the four PMF-resolved factors within a given period and are used here as model-internal labels rather than predefined source categories. Their environmental meanings were assigned post hoc using a weight-of-evidence approach based on dominant element loadings in the PMF profiles, spatial coincidence of high-contribution areas, element co-variation and Mantel/correlation evidence, consistency with published regional source signatures, and the stability of characteristic elements after RF refinement [34,53]. Because PMF factor numbering is arbitrary, the same factor number does not necessarily represent the same environmental source in the 2014 and 2024 periods. The lake-specific factor scores from the selected PMF solution were then exported and used as predictor variables in the subsequent RF models.

RF

RF is an ensemble learning algorithm that constructs multiple decision trees from bootstrap samples of the original dataset and aggregates their predictions to improve generalization performance [54]. In this study, RF was used as a regression tool rather than a classification model, and was not treated as an independent source-apportionment method. PMF-derived factor scores from the selected four-factor solution were used as predictor variables. In contrast, the observed concentrations of the seven-target metal(loid)s (Cr, Ni, Cu, Zn, As, Cd, and Pb) and their summed load (SUM) were used as response variables. Thus, the predicted values were generated by the RF model from PMF-resolved factor scores, rather than directly by the PMF model itself.

Within this coupled framework, RF served two complementary purposes: to evaluate how well the PMF-resolved latent factors reproduced the observed metal(loid) concentrations and to capture nonlinear source–element relationships that may not be fully represented by PMF alone. Because PMF is a receptor model rather than a supervised concentration-prediction model, a strict one-to-one numerical comparison between PMF-only predictions and RF predictions is not entirely appropriate. Instead, the complementarity between PMF and RF was evaluated by assessing whether the dominant element–factor relationships identified by PMF were preserved after RF-based refinement of the contribution estimates, and by evaluating the cross-validated predictive performance of the PMF–RF framework. RF variable-importance output was therefore not used as an independent basis for source identification in this study.

RF models were implemented in R using the mlr3 framework. The selected PMF solution resolved four latent factors; thus, each RF model used four PMF-derived source scores as predictors. We therefore tuned the number of variables (Mtry) considered at each split over the full feasible range, from 1 to 4. The number of trees (Ntree) was tuned from 200 to 600 to ensure stable ensemble predictions without unnecessary computational cost, and the minimum terminal-node size was tuned from 2 to 10 to balance local fitting capacity and overfitting risk. Hyperparameter tuning was performed separately for each response variable using a 10-fold cross-validation scheme with 50 randomized searches, and the final setting for each model was defined as the combination yielding the lowest cross-validated root mean squared error (RMSE). Model performance was evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R2), RMSE, and mean absolute error (MAE) based on 10-fold cross-validated predictions, rather than training-set fits, thereby reducing the risk of overfitting and allowing a more reliable assessment of the predictive ability of the PMF–RF framework.

Results and discussion

Spatial distribution of heavy metal(loid)s

Descriptive statistics for the seven-sediment metal(loid) samples from Qinghai–Xizang Plateau lakes during the 2014 and 2024 periods are summarized (Table 1). The interperiod pattern is most clearly illustrated by grouping the metal(loid)s into three descriptive response categories. At the same time, full-sample statistical testing indicated no significant distributional shift for the evaluated metal(loid)s between 2014 and 2024 (Supplementary Table S2). The temporal evolution of sediment metal(loid)s is characterized by divergent shifts in spatial distribution, most notably for As. However, its full-sample difference was not statistically significant, as evidenced by a pronounced expansion of its upper tail and elevated coefficients of variation and kurtosis, implying that an emerging subset of hotspots is driving regional variability. Cr did not mirror this pattern of increasing heterogeneity; instead, Cr moved toward a more homogenized state in 2024, evidenced by lower spatial dispersion and the attenuation of extreme values across the studied lakes. Consistent with this trend of stabilization, the concentrations of Cd, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn remained largely unchanged from the baseline period. These elements were characterized by damped extremes and a lack of significant interperiod shifts at the full-sample level (Supplementary Table S2), suggesting that for the majority of target metals, the plateau's geochemical landscape remains relatively stable. Zn shows a higher median, while its maximum decreases, consistent with broader redistribution at low levels rather than the emergence of new extreme inputs.

The contrasting behavior of As relative to the other metal(loid)s suggests process-driven remobilization rather than a uniform increase in external loading. The strengthening of As hotspots is consistent with episodic remote atmospheric transport of industrial emissions and with erosion associated with permafrost degradation during freeze–thaw cycles, both of which can mobilize and redeposit legacy As in specific catchments and sediment-focusing areas [28,55]. By contrast, the relative stability and increasing homogeneity of Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn are consistent with the stable mixed natural and traffic source proposed by Guo et al. [25], implying that modest, persistent traffic emissions together with steady bedrock weathering constrain regional variability. The decline in Cr observed in this study may be related to its predominant origin from rock weathering in the region. In addition, changes in hydrological conditions and sediment transport processes can lower the apparent Cr concentration through dilution and mixing, without requiring emission-reduction measures [50]. Benchmark comparisons indicate that As is a dominant environmental concern. Mean As exceeds the regional sediment background value compiled for the Plateau [45] and surpasses multiple guideline thresholds, including threshold effect level, agricultural thresholds, human health and ecological thresholds, and probable effect level. This pattern implies that ecological effects are plausible in many lakes and that localized risk likely increased as the high-concentration tail expanded. In comparison, the other metal(loid)s are generally near background and remain below major effect levels on average. However, maxima indicate that isolated hotspots can still exceed management or effects thresholds. Overall, the results support a natural-source-dominant setting, with localized As superposition and stronger upper-tail variability plausibly linked to climate-related remobilization and episodic atmospheric inputs [28,55].

Spatial distributions of heavy-metal(loid) concentrations in lake sediments were characterized for both the 2014 and 2024 periods (Fig. 2). Because the compiled 2024 dataset contains more eligible lake records than the 2014 dataset, these spatial comparisons should be interpreted primarily as an assessment of changes in hotspot locations, spatial spread, and concentration classes following temporal harmonization (Fig. 2). We evaluated formal interperiod comparability separately using the harmonized full-sample tests (Supplementary Table S2), sensitivity analyses based on the 42 lakes present in both periods, and resampling checks to account for unequal sample sizes. As is the core diagnostic element in both periods, showing elevated concentrations and an expanded area of influence. In the 2014 period, sediments from lakes in Ngari Prefecture exhibited slightly higher Cd, Cr, and Cu contents than in the 2024 period, whereas changes elsewhere were minor. By the 2024 period, Pb and Zn concentrations were higher in some lakes in Naqu, Shannan, and Xigazê prefectures than in the 2014 period, while Ni showed a broadly similar magnitude and spatial pattern across the plateau. The observed enhancement in As diffusion and enrichment likely reflects the combined effects of regional source characteristics and atmospheric inputs. Lv et al. [56] documented a regional expansion of atmospheric As deposition, consistent with our observation that As-enriched sediments expanded from localized areas in northern Xizang and the Qaidam Basin in the 2014 period to most lakes in northern Xizang by the 2024 period. High concentrations of Cd, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn were consistently observed in lake sediments across Gar County, Xigazê, Lhasa, Shannan, and the Qaidam Basin during both periods. These clusters define Northern Xizang as the region's principal enrichment belt, mirroring the findings of Luo et al. [50], who identified natural bedrock weathering as the primary driver of Cr and Ni distributions. The increases in Pb and Zn in Naqu and adjacent areas may be linked to the localized intensification of traffic-related emissions from the Qinghai–Xizang railway [57]. The slightly higher Cd and Cr levels in Ngari Prefecture in the 2014 period may reflect episodic inputs of weathering products transported by glacier meltwater, consistent with Che et al. [58], who reported that glacier-fed rivers can elevate heavy-metal(loid) concentrations in receiving lake sediments. Taken together, the observed spatial heterogeneity appears to be shaped by three broad groups of drivers: first, remote atmospheric transport, reflected in the regional spread of As and part of the Pb signal; second, geological background and cryosphere-related weathering, which likely explains the persistent enrichment belt of Cr and Ni in northern Xizang and the Qaidam Basin; and third, localized human activity, especially transport corridors and hub effects, which may contribute to Pb–Zn enrichment in Naqu and adjacent areas. Ecological risk and its spatial distribution

During both the 2014 (Fig. 3a) and 2024 (Fig. 3b) periods, most heavy metal(loid)s in lake sediments fell within the unpolluted (Igeo ≤ 0) and slightly polluted (0 < Igeo ≤ 1) classes. Only a few metal(loid)–site combinations reached the mildly to moderately polluted range (1 < Igeo ≤ 3), and no samples were classified as moderately to heavily polluted (3 < Igeo ≤ 4) or heavily to extremely polluted (Igeo > 4). No significant upward shift in Igeo values across the full sample was detected between the two periods. Instead, minor metal(loid)-specific fluctuations were observed, while the full-sample comparison indicated an overall stable level of pollution (Supplementary Table S2). This pattern supports the persistence of natural source dominance. Luo et al. [50] reported that natural sources, such as acidic and basic rocks, contribute more than 60% of heavy metal(loid)s to plateau soils, implying limited short-term variability in bedrock weathering rates. Meanwhile, the stability of Igeo may also reflect effective control of anthropogenic inputs. The potential ecological risk index (PERI) likewise indicated a generally low-risk situation. In both the 2014 (Fig. 4a) and 2024 (Fig. 4c) periods, most sites were categorized as low risk (PERI <150), followed by a smaller proportion as moderate risk (150 ≤ PERI <300). Sites in high-risk or higher grades (PERI ≥300) were comparatively rare. Qualitatively compared with published sediment-based PERI studies from other lake systems, the overall ecological risk level observed here appears relatively low. A national-scale synthesis of Chinese lake sediments reported an average moderate potential ecological risk. A recent study from the Lake Keluke–wetland system in the northeastern Qinghai–Xizang Plateau reported generally low ecological risk except for Cd. The plateau-wide pattern identified in this study falls at the lower-risk end of existing lake sediment PERI datasets, and preserves the well-established pattern of Cd dominating total ecological risk [59,60]. PERI component analysis for the two periods (Fig. 4b–d) showed that Cd was the principal contributor in both years, and the set of high-contribution metal(loid)s remained unchanged. Source apportionment

Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis was first conducted to evaluate element co-variation and identify preliminary linkages between heavy metal(loid)s and environmental drivers, thereby supporting subsequent PMF–RF interpretation. In the 2014 period, strong positive correlations were observed among Cr, Cu, Ni, and Pb. Correlation coefficients exceeded 0.90 for the Ni–Cr, Zn–Cu, and Zn–Pb pairs, suggesting that these elements likely shared a common mixed source. By contrast, As showed no significant correlation with the other metal(loid)s, and Cd was significantly correlated only with Zn (Fig. 6a). Across the five groups of auxiliary factors, SECs were significantly correlated with As. PCs were significantly correlated with Cd, Cu, Pb, and Zn, and WQHCs were significantly correlated with Pb. BCs showed positive correlations with all seven metal(loid)s. SCs were generally negatively correlated with As, Cd, Cu, Pb, and Zn, although these relationships were not statistically significant. Together with Gao et al. [63], these patterns suggest that, in the 2014 period, plateau lake sediments primarily received inputs from natural bedrock weathering and traffic-related emissions. The absence of significant correlations between As and the other metal(loid)s supports the view of Guo et al. [25] that As on the plateau is predominantly introduced via remote atmospheric transport from West and South Asia. PMF and RF models

Among the three-to-seven-factor solutions tested, the four-factor model was the first for which further increases in the number of factors yielded only marginal reductions in Qrobust/Qexpected. At the same time, the residual distributions remained well-behaved and the factor profiles retained clear environmental meaning. Most scaled residuals for the seven metal(loid)s were distributed symmetrically around zero and fell within ±3, indicating an acceptable fit. Error-estimation diagnostics showed limited factor swapping and no evidence of severe rotational instability, supporting the robustness of the selected four-factor solution.

Overall, the PMF analysis indicates four interpretable source categories controlling variability in sediment heavy metal(loid) concentrations on the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau. Based on dominant PMF loadings, spatial distribution, element associations, and published regional source signatures, these factors can be grouped into: (1) a mixed source integrating traffic-related metal(loid)s, remote atmospheric input, and lithogenic weathering; (2) an As-dominated atmospheric/industrial source related mainly to coal combustion and smelting; (3) a Pb-dominated long-range atmospheric-deposition source; and (4) a transitional factor shifting from geogenic Cr in the 2014 period to traffic- or mining-related Cu in the 2024 period.

Because factor numbers are model-internal labels, the same factor number does not necessarily denote the same environmental source in the 2014 and 2024 periods; the dominant factor loadings and their corresponding source interpretations are summarized in Supplementary Table S7. Numerous studies have demonstrated that As is a typical tracer of fossil-fuel combustion, particularly coal burning, as well as metal(loid)-smelting processes [71,72]. Elevated As has also been detected in river sediments on the plateau [73], indicating sustained fluvial delivery to lakes. Ma et al. [74] estimated that coal burning, especially high-As coal, contributes up to 36.8% of total atmospheric As deposition on the plateau. At the same time, industrial smelting releases additional As-bearing emissions that can be transported and deposited via the monsoon. As is redox-sensitive and is readily adsorbed onto Fe, Al, and Mn oxides [75], which can enhance its retention in surface sediments. Given the plateau's proximity to high-emission regions in South Asia, remote monsoon-driven transport of As-rich aerosols has been confirmed by isotopic analyses [25,58]. In the 2024 period, the As-dominated factor shifted to FAC1 (85.0% in PMF; 73.0% in RF) but retained a pollution signature similar to that of 2014-FAC2, with source contributions distributed across the central, western, and northern lakes. In the 2014 period, the third factor (FAC3) was mainly represented by Pb (60.8% in PMF; 67.5% in RF). High-contribution areas occurred in the eastern, western, and northern parts of the plateau. In contrast, contributions were lower in the central belt (Fig. 8a). In the 2024 period, Pb again dominated the corresponding factor (FAC4), with contributions of 69.0% (PMF) and 75.0% (RF), and high-value areas were more widely distributed. Pb is widely used as an indicator of atmospheric deposition and legacy anthropogenic emissions, including traffic-related and industrial sources [76,77]. Although direct human activity on the plateau is limited, Pb can be efficiently transported to the region via the westerlies and the South Asian monsoon [78]. Historical records indicate that anthropogenic Pb accumulation fluxes on the plateau over the past 50 years have been comparable to those in the Northern Hemisphere, with >90% of Pb attributed to atmospheric deposition [79]. The westerlies and summer monsoon have historically transported Pb from Central Asia to the interior of the Qinghai–Xizang Plateau via the Yarlung Tsangpo Gorge [28]. The stability of Pb-related factors observed in the 2014 and 2024 periods reflects the persistence of these large-scale atmospheric pathways, which operate alongside legacy traffic emissions and steady geogenic backgrounds. Finally, in the 2014 period, the fourth factor (FAC4) was dominated by Cr, with contribution rates of 76.8% (PMF) and 80.7% (RF), and was primarily attributed to the natural geological background. The Yarlung Tsangpo suture zone in southern Xizang is rich in ophiolitic sequences, and high-Cr chromite, formed by mixing depleted and ancient mantle during subduction, can be transported to lakes via surface runoff [80,81]. Sediment records from Qinghai Lake also indicate that before 2014, Cr mainly originated from bedrock weathering, with anthropogenic enrichment factors close to background values over long periods [25,82]. Although traffic activity can introduce Cr, the low road density and the predominance of small-scale mining in the 2014 period suggest that such inputs were negligible, consistent with the reported dominance of residual-state Cr in Yamdrok Lake [58]. In the 2024 period, however, Cu became the characteristic element of FAC3, contributing 57.1% (PMF) and 67.3% (RF). Rapid tourism growth and the expansion of road networks (e.g., G214 and G109) likely increased traffic-derived Cu inputs. Cu from tire and brake abrasion can be deposited into the atmosphere and delivered to lakes via runoff. Roadside soils have been reported to show exponential increases in Cu with traffic volume [57]. At the same time, intensified mineral exploitation in the Bangong–Nujiang suture zone may provide an additional Cu source [83]. Although weathering in permafrost-affected areas still supplies a natural Cu component, its contribution has reportedly declined to below 30% [56]. The spatial coincidence between FAC3 hotspots and traffic hubs, such as Delingha, further supports the increasing importance of anthropogenic Cu sources, consistent with source-apportionment results from the northwest industrial park [63]. Accordingly, the source categories and their temporal evolution identified here are receptor-model-based inferences supported by spatial patterns and previous regional evidence, rather than direct observations of source emissions.

Model evaluation

The reliability of the PMF solution was evaluated across three complementary aspects: the number of factors selected, residual behavior, and rotational stability. Among the tested three-to-seven-factor solutions, the four-factor model provided the best compromise among statistical fit, solution stability, and environmental interpretability (Supplementary Table S6). For both periods, the four-factor solution substantially improved model fit relative to the three-factor solution. Although Qrobust/Qexpected decreased further in some five-to seven-factor solutions, these improvements were limited and were accompanied by reduced robustness, including factor swaps and/or lower stability in the uncertainty diagnostics, particularly for the 2024 dataset. Higher-factor solutions frequently resulted in source splitting and obscured environmental interpretations, establishing the four-factor solution as the most parsimonious and interpretable representation of source structure for both periods. Residual diagnostics further supported the adequacy of the selected four-factor solution. The scaled residuals for the seven heavy metal(loid)s were generally concentrated near zero and displayed approximately unimodal distributions in both the 2014 and 2024 periods (Supplementary Fig. S1). Most species showed no obvious extreme tails, strong multimodality, or severe asymmetry, indicating that the model captured the main structure of the observed data. Minor skewness or slight offsets from zero were observed for a few species, especially As, Cd, and Pb in the 2014 period and Ni in the 2024 period, suggesting limited species-specific bias but no evidence of substantial model failure. The influence of rotational ambiguity was assessed by Fpeak sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Fig. S2). In the 2014 period, Qrobust/Qexpected reached its minimum at approximately Fpeak = −0.2. In contrast, in the 2024 period, the curve was relatively flat over the Fpeak range of −0.2 to 0.2, indicating that the four-factor solution was not highly sensitive to small rotational forcing. Considering both numerical stability and the need for consistent comparison between the two periods, Fpeak = −0.2 was selected for the final solution. Together with the DISP and Bootstrap diagnostics, these results indicate that the PMF solution was stable and sufficiently robust for source interpretation.

Compared with the PMF-only solution, RF provided a complementary refinement that preserved the original identities of characteristic elements. This synergy is reflected in the comparison of contribution structures (Fig. 7), where the preservation of dominant factor–element relationships occurs alongside a sharpening of individual source signatures. For example, in the 2014 period, the contribution of Cr to FAC4 increased from 76.8% in PMF to 80.7% after RF refinement, whereas in the 2024 period, the contribution of Pb to FAC4 increased from 69.0% to 75.0%. In a few cases, RF slightly reduced the contribution of the dominant element, such as Cu in FAC1 in the 2014 period and As in FAC1 in the 2024 period, reflecting redistribution of explained variance when multiple predictors had similar explanatory power. Importantly, the reported R2, RMSE, and MAE values were calculated from 10-fold cross-validated predictions rather than from training-set fits (Fig. 9). The RF models showed consistently strong predictive performance for both periods, with R2 values of 0.927–0.983 in the 2014 period and 0.936–0.985 in the 2024 period for individual metal(loid)s, and 0.980–0.981 for SUM. The corresponding RMSE and MAE values were also low relative to the target elements' concentration ranges. The close agreement between observed and predicted concentrations indicates that the PMF-derived latent factors explained a substantial proportion of the variability in sediment heavy metal(loid) concentrations. Because hyperparameter tuning and model evaluation were conducted within a 10-fold cross-validation framework, model performance was assessed on held-out subsets rather than on the full dataset, reducing the risk of overfitting. Overall, these results confirm the robustness of PMF-derived patterns at the system level, identify which elements benefit from nonlinear learning, and indicate that prediction uncertainty is concentrated in extreme samples. The two models therefore exhibit clear complementarity, consistent with findings from combined receptor-model applications [53]. PMF resolved four source categories, and RF preserved and refined the dominant factor–element relationships associated with these PMF-derived categories: (i) a mixed source comprising traffic emissions, remote atmospheric transport, and natural weathering; (ii) an As source associated with fossil-fuel combustion and South Asian transport; (iii) a cross-regional atmospheric Pb source; and (iv) a mixed natural and anthropogenic Cu source. This agreement indicates that the core pollution sources identified for plateau lakes are stable and that differences are primarily quantitative and algorithm-driven. This evolution may be partially related to the expansion of the Delingha transportation hub [57], the increase in industrial emissions in South Asia [58,84], and the emergence of anthropogenic Cr inputs reported in previous studies [68,85]. PMF retained its ability to resolve natural sources despite localized, high-uncertainty data, underscoring its robustness in complex plateau settings [81]. RF, although showing comparably strong predictive performance, tended to overestimate traffic contributions in some areas due to its sensitivity to local-scale variables, a behavior also noted by Gao et al. [63]. Overall, PMF appears better suited to primary-source apportionment in plateau lake sediments. In contrast, RF provides a complementary tool for refining element-specific contributions and quantitatively evaluating the explanatory power of PMF-derived latent factors. The combined PMF–RF framework provides robust technical support for tracing complex heavy-metal(loid) sources in the Third Pole, aligning these findings with recent sediment-based source fingerprinting and risk-mapping studies in other aquatic systems [86].

The source apportionment results in this study are based primarily on PMF–RF inference, the correlation structure, the spatial coincidence of source hotspots, and support from previously published regional studies, rather than on the direct assimilation of independent emission inventories, land-use change datasets, or atmospheric trajectory analyses. Natural redistribution processes—including hydrological dilution, sediment focusing, resuspension, burial, and climate-related remobilization—complicate surface–sediment metal(loid) concentrations. This inherent complexity mandates that interpreted source transitions and anthropogenic intensification signals be regarded as model-supported, process-consistent inferences rather than direct observational evidence of source change.

Limitations and future directions

Although this study provides a plateau-scale assessment based on a harmonized comparison between the 2014 and 2024 periods, several limitations should be acknowledged. The interpretation of our findings is subject to several methodological considerations. Primarily, the temporal dimension of this study relies on two harmonized comparison periods rather than standardized continuous monitoring. Consequently, the term decadal changes refers to decade-scale differences between period-based datasets rather than to continuous trajectories; this framing leaves short-term fluctuations or episodic events within each period unresolved. Closely linked to this temporal structure is the inherent uncertainty arising from the heterogeneity of compiled datasets. Despite our harmonization procedures, variations in sampling design, analytical methods, and QA/QC reporting across published records mean this study should be viewed as a harmonized comparison rather than a strict repeated-measures assessment. Furthermore, the statistical robustness of these comparisons must be weighed against the unequal sample sizes between the 2014 and 2024 periods, which may affect model stability despite our sensitivity analyses and resampling tests. Finally, the scope of our ecological assessment is limited to total concentrations. Without data on speciation or bioavailability, the reported pollution and risk levels represent burden-based screening estimates rather than form-specific ecological responses.

More importantly, the source-apportionment results remain model-supported, process-consistent inferences rather than direct observational evidence. The interpreted source transitions are supported mainly by the combined PMF–RF framework, correlation or Mantel analyses, spatial coincidence of hotspots, and consistency with published regional studies, but not by direct assimilation of independent emission inventories, atmospheric-deposition measurements, land-use change data, or trajectory-based transport analyses. Moreover, natural redistribution processes, including hydrological dilution, sediment focusing, burial, resuspension, and climate-related remobilization, may also alter surface-sediment signatures and complicate interperiod interpretation. Future work should therefore prioritize standardized long-term monitoring across representative plateau lakes under unified field and analytical protocols, while more explicitly coupling climatic, hydrological, redox, and biogeochemical controls with sediment records. Integrating isotope tracing, atmospheric deposition observations, and process-based environmental data with receptor models and machine-learning-assisted refinement would further improve quantitative source discrimination and provide a stronger scientific basis for precise prevention and control of heavy-metal(loid) contamination in high-altitude lakes.

Conclusion

The results showed that differences between the two harmonized comparison periods were characterized mainly by As hotspot behavior and spatial redistribution rather than by uniform multi-metal(loid) enrichment. Overall contamination remained low and temporally stable based on Igeo, while localized ecological-risk hotspots persisted; Cd consistently dominated PERI despite the prominence of As in benchmark-based concern. Source apportionment resolved four source categories that were stable in type but shifted in relative strength between periods, with mixed anthropogenic and natural influences becoming more pronounced around key hubs. The coupled PMF–RF framework provided complementary and internally consistent source diagnostics at the plateau scale.

Overall, detectable Anthropocene signals in this high-elevation lake system were expressed mainly as localized superposition on a broadly natural-source-dominant background. The identified priority elements, hotspot regions, and source types provide a practical basis for risk-oriented monitoring and source-focused management on the plateau. More broadly, these findings also have implications for the environmental-security dimension of the Asian Water Tower, since plateau lakes function as sensitive sentinels of contaminant accumulation and remobilization in climatically vulnerable headwater environments. Although the present sediment-based results should not be considered direct evidence of widespread downstream water-quality deterioration, they indicate that water-resource protection on the plateau should place greater emphasis on early warning, hotspot-oriented surveillance, and source-focused intervention in lakes exhibiting persistent or expanding risk signals. In particular, as hotspots and their apparent northward expansion warrant prioritized surveillance, Cd should remain a core risk indicator because it dominates PERI, and northern lakes and hub regions such as Delingha deserve continued management attention. In this sense, protecting the quality of plateau lakes is also relevant to safeguarding the long-term environmental resilience of the Asian Water Tower.
