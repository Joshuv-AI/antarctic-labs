1.
Introduction

A classical theme in commutative algebra is to detect homological invariants of modules over Noetherian rings through vanishing conditions. In characteristic
p>0
, the Frobenius map and its iterates provide a powerful tool for such investigations. Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring of characteristic
p
, and let
\operatorname{F}:R\to R
denote the Frobenius map given by
a\mapsto a^{p}
. For each integer
e\geq 0
, we write
{}^{e}R
for the
R
-algebra whose structure is induced by the
e
-th iterate
\operatorname{F}^{e}:R\to R
.

In a foundational work, Herzog
[
9
]
established the following celebrated criterion:

Theorem 1.1

(Herzog)
.

Let
R
be an
F
-finite local ring of characteristic
p
, and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. If
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}({}^{e}R,M)=0
for all
i>0
and infinitely many
e
, then
\operatorname{id}_{R}M<\infty
.

While elegant, Herzog’s theorem requires checking infinitely many modules
{}^{e}R
—one for each Frobenius iterate—to conclude finite injective dimension. This raises a natural question: can the entire family
\{{}^{e}R\}_{e\geq 0}
be encoded into a single test module that simultaneously detects finite injective dimension?

The perfect closure of
R
, defined as the direct limit

R^{\infty}:=\varinjlim\bigl(R\xrightarrow{\operatorname{F}}R\xrightarrow{\operatorname{F}}R\xrightarrow{\operatorname{F}}\cdots\bigr)=\varinjlim\bigl(\{{}^{e}R\}_{e\geq 0}),

is a natural candidate. This
R
-algebra is the colimit of all Frobenius twists and has remarkable homological properties. Bhatt and Scholze
[
5
]
showed that
R^{\infty}
has finite global dimension in a suitable sense, while
[
2
]
established that every
R^{+}
-module has finite flat dimension over
R^{+}
. Here, the absolute integral closure of an integral domain
R
, denoted by
R^{+}
, is the integral closure of
R
inside an algebraic closure of its fraction field.

The main purpose of this note is to prove that the perfect closure
R^{\infty}
indeed serves as a universal test module for finite injective and projective dimensions. Our first main result is the following:

Theorem A)

Let
R
be a complete local ring of prime characteristic, and let
N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R^{\infty},N)=0
for all
i>0
,

(2)

\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty
.

This result provides a single-test-module analogue of Herzog’s criterion Theorem
1.1
.
Analogously, we establish a projective dimension criterion:

Theorem B)

Let
R
be a complete local domain of prime characteristic, and let

N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{+},N)=0\qquad\text{for all }i>0,

(2)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{+},N)=0\qquad\text{for all }i\geq\dim(R),

(3)

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty.

It is well-known that
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,
for some
i\geq d
, then
\operatorname{Tor}_{j}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,
for all
j\geq i
(see
[
7
]
).
It is well known from
[
1
]
that if

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,

then
R
is regular, or equivalently,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(k)<\infty.
Thus, Theorem B) has the following consequence:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain of prime characteristic, and let

N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Suppose

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{\infty},N)=0

for all
i\gg 0.

Then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty.

Corollary
1.2
reveals that by passing to the infinite cover
R^{\infty}
of the family
\{{}^{e}R\}_{e\geq 0}
, one obtains a single test module, which refines the following classical result of Herzog, (which is converse to a famouse result of
Peskine and Szpiro
[
13
]
):

Theorem 1.3

(Herzog)
.

Let
R
be a of prime characteristic
p
, and let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
If

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,{}^{e}R)=0

for any
i>0
and infinitely many
e
, then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty.

2.
Perfect closure detects finite homological dimensions

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Noetherian complete local ring of prime characteristic
p
.
The notation
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(-)
(resp.
\operatorname{id}_{R}(-)
) stands for the projective (resp. injective) dimension of
(-)
.

Notation 2.1
.

Let
(-)^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(-,E_{R}(k))
denote the Matlis duality
functor.

Fact 2.2
.

(See
[
12
, 4.1]
).

Let
L
,
L^{\prime}
, and
N
be
R
-modules such that
N
is Noetherian. Since
E_{R}(k)
is injective we have following isomorphisms:

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(N,L^{\prime})^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(N,L^{\prime\vee}),\qquad\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(L,L^{\prime})^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(L,L^{\prime\vee}),

where the second isomorphism is a consequence of Hom–tensor adjointness.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
R
be a complete local ring of prime characteristic, and let

N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R^{\infty},N)=0\qquad\text{for all }i>0,

(2)

\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.
It follows by the so called Bass’ conjecture (see
[
6
, 9.6.2]
), and from
\operatorname{id}_{R}(N)<\infty
that
R
is Cohen–Macaulay. Let
\underline{x}:=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be a parameter sequence. Then
\underline{x}^{[q]}:=x_{1}^{[q]},\ldots,x_{d}^{[q]}
is an
R
-regular sequence. In other words,
\underline{x}
is
R^{1/q}
-regular, i.e.,
{}^{e}R
is Cohen-Macaulay. Taking direct limit,

R^{\infty}
is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay module.
By
[
12
, Corollary 7.7]
we obtain the claim.
Here is a more elementary argument.
Any module of finite injective dimension, has finite
\omega
-resolution (see
[
6
, 3.3.28(b)]
), hence we may assume
that

N=\omega_{R}.

Recall that
\underline{x}
is a parameter sequence. Then the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow\oplus R_{x_{i}}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow R_{x_{1}\ldots x_{d}}\longrightarrow\omega_{R}^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0

remains exact after tensoring with
R^{\infty}
, since
R^{\infty}
is balanced
big Cohen–Macaulay and the homologies are just the local cohomology of
R^{\infty}
. Hence

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},\omega_{R}^{\vee})=0.

Therefore, taking a Matlis dual, and using second isomorphism in Fact
2.2
, we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R^{\infty},\omega_{R})=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R^{\infty},\omega_{R}^{\vee\vee})=0.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
.
There is a spectral sequence (see
[
11
, Theorem 10.74]
):

E_{2}^{pq}=\operatorname{Ext}_{R^{\infty}}^{\,p}\!\left(L,\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,q}(R^{\infty},N)\right)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p+q}(L,N),

where
L
is any
R^{\infty}
-module.
By assumption (1), the spectral sequence collapses at
q=0
.
Hence, we lead to the following isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}_{R^{\infty}}^{\,p}(L,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{\infty},N))\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p}(L,N)\quad(\ast)

for every
p
.
Recall from Bhatt–Scholze
[
5
]
that

g:=\operatorname{gldim}(R^{\infty})<\infty.

Set
L:=R^{\infty}/\mathfrak{m}R^{\infty}
. Then as an
R
-module, we have
L=\oplus R/\mathfrak{m}
, since
\mathfrak{m}L=0
.
Therefore, the left hand side of
(\ast)
is zero for all
p>g
. It follows

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p}(L,N)=\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p}(\oplus R/\mathfrak{m},N)=\prod\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p}(R/\mathfrak{m},N)=0\qquad\text{for }p\gg 0.

Thus,
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{\,p}(R/\mathfrak{m},N)=0
for all
p\gg 0
.
By Roberts’ theorem (see
[
10
, Theorem 2]
),
\mu^{n}(\mathfrak{m},N)
is nonzero only in the range

\bigl[\operatorname{depth}(N),\,\operatorname{id}(N)\bigr].

Hence

\operatorname{id}(N)<\infty.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a local ring of prime characteristic, and let

L
be an Artinian
R
-module. Suppose that

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},L)=0

for all positive
i
.
Then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(L)<\infty,

and consequently
R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

Proof.

In view of second isomorphism in Fact
2.2
, we obtain

0=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},L)^{\vee}\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R^{\infty},L^{\vee}).

Since
L
is Artinian,
L^{\vee}
is finitely generated. Hence, by the previous
proposition,

\operatorname{id}_{R}(L^{\vee})<\infty.

By previous result,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(L^{\vee\vee})<\infty.

Since
L
is Artinian,

L^{\vee\vee}\cong L,

and therefore

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(L)<\infty.
Finally, the existence of a nonzero Artinian module of finite projective
dimension implies that
R
is Cohen–Macaulay (see e.g.
[
4
, Observation 3.15]
).
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
R
be a local ring of prime characteristic. Then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{+}(R^{\infty},R)=0
.

It is well known from
[
1
]
that if

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,

then
R
is regular, or equivalently,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(k)<\infty.
Also, it is well-known that
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,
for some
i\geq d
, then
\operatorname{Tor}_{j}^{R}(R^{\infty},k)=0,
for all
j\geq i
(see
[
7
]
).
These suggest the following question.

Question 2.6
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Suppose that

\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(R^{\infty},M)=0
for all
i\geq\dim(R)
.
Must it follow that

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(M)<\infty?

The following result provides an affirmative answer.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain of prime characteristic, and let

N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{+},N)=0\qquad\text{for all }i>0,

(2)

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{+},N)=0\qquad\text{for all }i\geq\dim(R),

(3)

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty.

Proof.

Replacing
N
by a sufficiently high syzygy, we may need to prove
(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
.

(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.
This is in
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
, as
R^{+}
is a balanced big Cohen-Macaulay algebra (see
[
8
]
).

(1)\Rightarrow(3)
.
There is a spectral sequence (
[
11
, Theorem 10.73]
)

E_{2}^{pq}=\operatorname{Tor}^{R^{+}}_{\,p}\!\left(L,\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,q}(R^{+},N)\right)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p+q}(L,N),

where
L
is any
R^{+}
-module.
By assumption (1), the spectral sequence collapses at
q=0
.
Hence

\operatorname{Tor}^{R^{+}}_{\,p}(L,R^{+}\otimes N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p}(L,N)\quad(\ast)

for every
p
.
Recall from
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
that
any
R^{+}
-module has
finite flat dimension.
Set
L:=R^{+}/\mathfrak{m}R^{+}
. Then as an
R
-module, we have
L=\oplus R/\mathfrak{m}
.
Therefore, the left hand side of
(\ast)
is zero for all
p>d:=\operatorname{w.dim}(R^{+})
. It follows

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p}(L,N)=\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p}(\oplus R/\mathfrak{m},N)=\oplus\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p}(R/\mathfrak{m},N)=0\qquad\text{for }p\gg 0.

Thus,
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{\,p}(R/\mathfrak{m},N)=0

for
p\gg 0.
Hence

\operatorname{pd}(N)<\infty.

∎

Similarly, one has:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be a complete local domain of prime characteristic, and let

N
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Suppose

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{\infty},N)=0

for all
i\gg 0.

Then

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(N)<\infty.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the previous result, and so omitted.
∎

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic. Then
R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{\infty},\omega_{R})=0

for all
i\gg 0.

Proof.

Suppose

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{\infty},\omega_{R})=0
. By Proposition
2.8
,

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\omega_{R})<\infty.
By Auslander-Buchsbaum,
\omega_{R}
is free. This implies that
R
is Gorenstein.
The reverse implication is clear.
∎
