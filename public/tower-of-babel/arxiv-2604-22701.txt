Gel’fand integration of 𝓑 ⁡ ( 𝓔 , 𝓕 ∗ ) \EuScriptBold B(E,F^{*}) -valued functions with emphasis on ( q , p ) (q,p) -summing operators Journal: Bulletin of Iranian Mathematical Society
Matija Milović, Stefan Milošević

Abstract
We generalize results concerning Gel’fand integration of functions taking values in the space of operators on Hilbert spaces to certain Banach spaces. Building on ideas from [ 10 ] we provide sufficient conditions for the Gel’fand integral to be ( q , p ) − (q,p)- summing and we use the developed techniques to answer a question posed in the mentioned article. Applications to positive operator-valued functions between certain function spaces are also given.

Introduction

Throughout the paper,
E
and
F

|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!\cdot\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{E}
and
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!\cdot\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{F},

we will denote the topological dual of the space

By an operator

we will always mean a bounded linear operator with its operator norm denoted by

and the space of all such operators will be denoted by
\EuScriptBold B(E,F).
In the case when
E
and
F
are Banach lattices, an operator

will be called positive and denoted by
T\geqslant 0
if
Tx\geqslant 0
for every

such that
x\geqslant 0.
Also, by
\ell_{p}(E)
and
\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
(for
p\geqslant 1
), we will denote Banach spaces of
p
-summable sequences in
E
and weakly
p
-summable sequences in
E,
respectively. Banach space
\ell_{p}(\mathbb{C})
will be denoted by
\ell_{p}.
If there is no risk of ambiguity, we will use
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!\cdot\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{p}
and
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!\cdot\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{p,w}
to denote norms in
\ell_{p}(E)
and
\ell^{w}_{p}(E).
Moreover, for
p\geqslant 1,
we denote by

the conjugate exponent, i. e., the number satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.
As we will need some results from the duality of sequence spaces, we state the following lemma for the comfort of the reader. Details can be found in
[
6
, Proposition 1.3.3]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
1\leqslant p<+\infty
the following duality holds
\bigl({\ell_{p}(E)}\bigr)^{*}\cong\ell_{p^{\prime}}(E^{*})
in the sense that every
\Phi\in\bigl({\ell_{p}(E)}\bigr)^{*}
is of the form
\Phi((x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi_{n},x_{n}\rangle,
for some
(\phi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}(E^{*})
such that
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!\Phi\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|=|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(\phi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{p^{\prime}}.

As a direct consequence, we get the following

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
(\phi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in a dual Banach space
E^{*}
and
1\leqslant p<+\infty
. Then,
(\phi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}(E^{*})
if and only if for every
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}(E)
holds
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle\phi_{n},x_{n}\rangle|<+\infty.

Proof.

The proof of one implication follows directly for Holder’s inequality, while we prove the converse by contradiction using the Uniform Boundedness Principle and Theorem
1.1
.
∎

For a norm
\alpha

E\otimes F,
by
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\alpha}F
we will denote the completion of the tensor product under that norm. By a
\varepsilon,\pi
and
\Delta_{p}
we will denote, respectively, the injective norm, the projective norm, and the natural norm on the

-integrable functions (see Chapter 7 in

\ell_{p}(E)=\ell_{p}\widehat{\otimes}_{\Delta_{p}}E,
while
\ell_{p}\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}E
is isomorphic to a subspace of
\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
(proofs and more details can be found in sections 8 and 9 of
[

). Also, we will make use of the duality

\bigl({E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F}\bigr)^{*}\cong\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*}),

(1.1)

proof of which can be found in
[
2
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Definition 1.3
.

An operator
T\colon E\to F
is said to be
(q,p)
-summing, for
1\leqslant p\leqslant q\leqslant+\infty,
if there exists
C>0
such that

\biggl({\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!Tx_{k}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{F}^{q}}\biggr)^{1/q}\leqslant C\cdot\biggl({\sup_{\|y\|_{E^{*}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\langle y,x_{k}\rangle|^{p}}\biggr)^{1/p},

for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E.
The least
C
such that the previous inequality holds will be denoted by
\pi_{q,p}(T)
and the class of all
(q,p)
-summing operators will be denoted by
\Pi_{q,p}(E,F).

Before moving on, we note that in the case
q=+\infty
a straightforward calculation shows that every
T\in\EuScriptBold B(E,F)
is
(\infty,p)
-summable with
\pi_{\infty,p}(T)=|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!T\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|.
So we will only deal with the case
q<+\infty,
although most of the statements remain valid considering that
\Pi_{\infty,p}(E,F)=\EuScriptBold B(E,F)
isometrically.

If an operator
T\colon E\to F
is
(q,p)
-summing and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
by the previous definition, the sequence
(Tx_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
\ell_{q}(F).
Therefore, each
(q,p)
-summing operator induces an operator
\widehat{T}\colon\ell^{w}_{p}(E)\to\ell_{q}(F)
defined by
\widehat{T}((x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})=(Tx_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and it is straightforward to see that
\|\widehat{T}\|=\pi_{q,p}(T).
Also, since finite sequences belong to
\ell^{w}_{p}(E),
if the operator
\widehat{T}
from
\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
to
\ell_{q}(F)
is defined in a previously described manner, the operator
T
is
(q,p)
-summing. Moreover, by the Closed Graph Theorem, it can be shown that
T
is
(q,p)
-summing if and only if
\widehat{T}(\ell^{w}_{p}(E))\subset\ell_{q}(F)
(details can be found in
[
4
, Proposition 2.1]
). Furthermore, due to the connection of the injective tensor norm and
\ell^{w}_{p}(E),
the previous definition can also be reformulated as (details can be found in
[
2
, Proposition 11.1]
and
[
2
, Exercise 11.20]
).

Lemma 1.4
.

An operator
T\colon E\to F
is
(q,p)
-summing if and only if the operator

id\otimes T\colon\ell_{p}\otimes_{\varepsilon}E\to\ell_{q}\otimes_{\Delta_{q}}F

is continuous, where
id
denotes the canonical embedding of
\ell_{p}
into
\ell_{q}
for
1\leqslant p\leqslant q<+\infty.

For the comfort of the reader, we provide the following lemma that will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 1.5
.

An operator
T\colon E\to F^{*}
is
(q,p)
-summing if and only if for every
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
and
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{q^{\prime}}(F)
holds

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}|<+\infty.

In that case we have the following formula

\pi_{q,p}(T)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{p,w}\leqslant 1\\
\|(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{q^{\prime}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}|.

(1.2)

Proof.

If
T
is
(q,p)
-summing, as already noted,
(Tx_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{q}(F^{*})
for every
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
and
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(Tx_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{q}\leqslant\pi_{q,p}(T)\cdot|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{p,w}.
Hence

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}|\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!Tx_{n}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{F^{*}}\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!y_{n}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{F}\\
\leqslant\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({Tx_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{q}\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({y_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{q^{\prime}}\leqslant\pi_{q,p}(T)\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({x_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{p,w}\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({y_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{q^{\prime}}<+\infty.

Conversely, let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{w}(E)
be arbitrary. Due to the assumption, by applying Lemma
1.2
to the sequence
(Tx_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},
we get that it belongs to
\ell_{q}(F^{*}),
implying proclaimed
(q,p)
-summability of the operator
T.

If the assumptions hold, for every
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
in the closed unit ball of in
\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
we get

\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({Tx_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{q}=\sup_{\|(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{q^{\prime}}\leqslant 1}\Biggl|{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\Biggr|=\sup_{\|(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{q^{\prime}}\leqslant 1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}|,

due to Theorem
1.1
. Finally,

\pi_{q,p}(T)=\sup_{\|(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{p,w}\leqslant 1}\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!\bigl({Tx_{n}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{q}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{p,w}\leqslant 1\\
\|(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{q^{\prime}}\leqslant 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\langle Tx_{n},y_{n}\rangle}|,

completing the proof.
∎

Also, for

For a measure space
(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu)
(notation that will also be used in the sequel), a function
f\colon\Omega\to E^{*}
will be called weakly
∗
measurable (integrable) if for every
x\in E
the scalar function
t\to\langle f(t),x\rangle
is measurable (integrable). In the case of weakly
∗
integrable function, due to
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
, for every
E\in\mathfrak{M}
there exists
x_{E}^{*}\in E^{*}
such that for every
x\in E
holds
\langle x_{E}^{*},x\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\langle f(t),x\rangle d\mu(t).
We will denote
x_{E}^{*}
by
\int_{E}f(t)d\mu(t)
and is also called the Gel’fand integral or the weak
∗
integral of
f
over
E.
Likewise, a weakly
∗
integrable function will also be called Gel’fand integrable. Furthermore, we say that two functions
f,g\colon\Omega\to E^{*}
are weakly
∗
equivalent if for every
x\in E
holds
\langle f(t),x\rangle=\langle g(t),x\rangle
almost everywhere, and by
\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,E^{*})
we denote the space of classes of weakly
∗
equivalent Gel’fand integrable functions
f\colon\Omega\to E^{*}
with norm defined by

\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!f\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{\mathbb{G}}=\sup_{|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!x\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{E}\leqslant 1}\int_{\Omega}\bigl|{\langle f(t),x\rangle}\bigr|d\mu(t).

In this paper, our primary focus will be on functions that take values in
\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
which we will also refer to as operator valued (o.v.) functions. Since this space is a dual of a Banach space, as already noted in (
1.1
), we can define the Gel’fand integral for
\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
-valued functions. Similarly to
[
7
, Lemma 1.1]
, the conditions for measurability and integrability for
\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
-valued functions can be simplified. More precisely, the following holds.

Lemma 1.8
.

An operator-valued function
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\to\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
is weakly
∗
measurable (integrable) if and only if for every
x\in E
and
y\in F
scalar functions
t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle
are measurable (integrable).

Proof.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be weakly
∗
measurable (integrable) and
x\in E
and
y\in F
be arbitrary. As
x\otimes y\in E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F,
the function
t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},x\otimes y\rangle=\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle
is measurable (integrable).

Conversely, let the scalar functions
t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle
be measurable, for all
x\in E
and
y\in F.
Every
z\in E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F
can be written in the form
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}\otimes y_{n},
for some sequences
(x_{n})_{n{\in\mathbb{N}}}
in
E
and
(y_{n})_{n{\in\mathbb{N}}}
in
F
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!x_{n}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{E}\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!y_{n}\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{F}<+\infty.
It follows that the series
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},z\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle
absolutely converges implying weak
∗
measurability of
{\mathscr{A}},
since all summands are measurable functions. If, in addition, all functions
t\mapsto\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle
are integrable, we define a bilinear mapping
B\colon E\times F\to L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)
by
B(x,y)(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle,
for
t\in\Omega.
To prove that
B
is bounded, it is sufficient to prove that it is separately continuous, by the
[
2
, 1.2.]
. For
y\in F
we define a linear operator
B_{y}\colon E\rightarrow L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)
by
B_{y}(x)=B(x,y).
We prove that
B_{y}
is continuous using the Closed Graph Theorem. Let
\|x_{n}-x\|_{E}+\|B_{y}(x_{n})-\varphi\|_{L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)}\rightarrow 0,
as
n\rightarrow\infty,
for some
\varphi\in L_{1}(\Omega,\mu),
and
x,x_{n}\in E,
for
n{\in\mathbb{N}}.
Then, there is a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})_{k{\in\mathbb{N}}}
such that
B_{y}(x_{n_{k}})(t)=\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}},y\rangle\rightarrow\varphi(t),
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for
\mu
a.e.
t\in\Omega.
For such
t\in\Omega,
as
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F),
we have
B_{y}(x_{n_{k}})(t)=\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n_{k}},y\rangle\rightarrow\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle,
as
k\rightarrow\infty,
implying
B_{y}(x)(t)=\varphi(t)
for
\mu
a.e.
t\in\Omega.
Thus,
B_{y}
is a bounded linear operator. In the same fashion, we prove that for every
x\in E
the linear operator
B_{x}\colon F\rightarrow L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)
defined by
B_{x}(y)=B(x,y)
is also continuous, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 1.9
.

In
[
1
]
, the authors introduce the space of pointwise Dunford integrable functions, denoted by
L_{pD}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(E,F)),
where
F
is not required to be a dual space. The conditions defining this space (see Definition 2.8, and also Proposition 2.9 in the mentioned paper) are similar to, though stronger than, those in the previous lemma, and they apply to
\EuScriptBold B(E,F),
for all Banach spaces
E
and
F.
We also note that in the case where
F
is reflexive, the spaces
L_{pD}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(E,F))
and
\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\EuScriptBold B(E,F))
are the same, according to Lemma
1.8
.

For the o.v. function
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(E,F),
we define an o.v. function
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(F^{*},E^{*})
by
({\mathscr{A}}^{*})_{t}={\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*},
for
t\in\Omega.
The next proposition deals with the weak
∗
measurability and integrability of
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(E,F^{*})
be weakly
∗
measurable function, where
F
is a reflexive Banach space. Then the o.v. function
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold B(F^{**},E^{*})
is also weak
∗
measurable. If, in addition,
{\mathscr{A}}
is weakly
∗
integrable, so is
{\mathscr{A}}^{*},
and for every
S\in\mathfrak{M}
holds
(\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu)^{*}=\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu.

Proof.

Let
x\in E
and
y^{**}\in F^{**}
be arbitrary. Since
F
is reflexive, there exists (unique)
y\in F,
such that for every
y^{*}\in F^{*}
holds
\langle y^{**},y^{*}\rangle=\langle y^{*},y\rangle.
Now, we have

\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{**},x\rangle=\langle y^{**},{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\rangle=\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle.

Thus, a weak
∗
measurability (integrability) of
{\mathscr{A}}
implies a weak
∗
measurability (integrability) of
{\mathscr{A}}^{*}.
In the case of integrability, for
S\in\mathfrak{M}
we have

\left\langle\left(\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu\right)y^{**},x\right\rangle=\int_{S}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}^{*}y^{**},x\rangle d\mu(t)=\int_{S}\langle y^{**},{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x\rangle d\mu(t)\\
=\int_{S}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle d\mu(t)=\left\langle\left(\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\right)x,y\right\rangle=\left\langle y^{**},\left(\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\right)x\right\rangle

proving that
(\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu)^{*}=\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}^{*}d\mu,
as proclaimed.
∎

Remark 1.11
.

If the Banach space E is additionally assumed to be reflexive, the results align with those established in
[
1

.

In Lemma
1.8
, we concluded that the weak

are entirely determined by its associated bilinear form. A similar result holds for evaluating

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
be weakly
∗
integrable. Then, the following holds

\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\mathbb{G}}=\sup_{\|x\|_{E}=\|y\|_{F}=1}\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle|d\mu(t).

(1.4)

Proof.

As for unit vectors
x\in E
and
y\in F
holds
\|x\otimes y\|_{E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F}=\|x\|_{E}\cdot\|y\|_{F}=1,
we have

\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle|d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},x\otimes y\rangle|d\mu(t)\leqslant\sup_{\|T\|_{E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F}=1}\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},T\rangle|d\mu(t)=\|{\mathscr{A}}\|_{\mathbb{G}}.

By taking the supremum over all unit vectors
x
and
y
we get the "
\geqslant
" in (
1.4
).

To prove the converse inequality, let
T\in E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F
be such that
\|T\|_{E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F}=1
and
\varepsilon>0.
Then, we can find sequences
(x_{n})_{n{\in\mathbb{N}}}
in
E
and
(y_{n})_{n{\in\mathbb{N}}}
in
F
of nonzero vectors, such that
T=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}\otimes y_{n}
and
\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{E}\cdot\|y_{n}\|_{F}\leqslant 1+\varepsilon.
Now,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t},T\rangle|d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle\right|d\mu(t)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\|x_{n}\|_{E}\cdot\|y_{n}\|_{F}\cdot\int_{\Omega}\left|\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|_{E}},\frac{y_{n}}{\|y_{n}\|_{F}}\right\rangle\right|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\sup_{\|x\|_{E}=\|y\|_{F}=1}\int_{\Omega}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle|d\mu(t).

By letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and taking the supremum over
\|T\|_{E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F}=1
we get the "
\leqslant
" in (
1.4
), completing the proof.
∎

For a Banach Lattice
E
its dual space
E^{*}
has a natural order given by
y\geqslant 0
iff
\langle y,x\rangle\geqslant 0
for every positive
x\in E.
Moreover,
E^{*}
is also a Banach lattice. Also, if
E
and
F
are Banach lattices, an operator
T\colon E\to F^{*}
is positive (by definition) if
Tx\geqslant 0
for
x\geqslant 0
and by previous discussion
Tx\geqslant 0
if and only if
\langle Tx,y\rangle\geqslant
for every
0\leqslant y\in F.
Thus,
T\colon E\to F^{*}
is positive if and only if
\langle Tx,y\rangle\geqslant 0
for every positive
x\in E
and
y\in F.
Final conclusion provides us with the following

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach lattices and
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\to\EuScriptBold{B}(E,F^{*})
weakly
∗
integrable function such that
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\geqslant 0
for almost every
t\in\Omega.
Then, for every measurable
S\subset\Omega,
holds
\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\geqslant 0.

Proof.

First, we note that
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\rangle\geqslant 0
a.e. for positive
x\in E
and
y\in F
as
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are positive operators a. e. Hence, by the definition of the Gel’fand integral we have

\left\langle\biggl({\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu}\biggr)x,y\right\rangle=\int_{S}\left\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x,y\right\rangle d\mu(t)\geqslant 0,

for positive
x\in E
and
y\in F.
Based on the discussion preceding the Lemma, the operator
\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu
is positive.
∎

Lemma 2.5

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space and
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})

|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\leqslant 1.

can be written in the form

x_{n}=f_{n}+g_{n}+h_{n},

where
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(h_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
are orthonormal bases of
{\mathcal{H}}.

Proof.

Due to the isometry given by
[
2

defined by
A(e_{n})=x_{n}
(where

is the

\ell_{2}
) is bounded and
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!A\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|=|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\leqslant 1.
According to
[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
, as the spaces
{\mathcal{H}}
and
\ell_{2}
are isomorphic Hilbert spaces, for the operator
A
holds
A=U+V+W,
where

are unitary operators and therefore

x_{n}=Ae_{n}=Ue_{n}+Ve_{n}+We_{n}.

Since the operators
U,V,W
are unitary, the sequences
({Ue_{n}})_{n=1}^{\infty},({Ve_{n}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
({We_{n}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
are orthonormal bases of
{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

Finally, the previous lemma and the Baire Category Theorem establish the finiteness of the mentioned supremum.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a separable Hilbert space and let
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\to{\EuScriptBold B}({\mathcal{H}})
be weakly
∗
measurable function such that
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}\in{\EuScriptBold C}_{\Phi}({\mathcal{H}})
for almost all
t\in\Omega
and for every orthonormal sequences
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
holds

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{n},f_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\,d\mu(t)<+\infty.

(2.5)

Then

\sup_{e,f\in B}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{n},f_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\,d\mu(t)<+\infty.

(2.6)

Proof.

First, we demonstrate that under the assumptions of the Theorem, the integral
\int_{\Omega}\Phi({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})\,d\mu(t)
remains finite for sequences
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
belonging to
\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}}).
Indeed, let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\leqslant 1
and
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\leqslant 1.
By the Lemma
2.5
, we have the bases
f^{i}
and
g^{j}
for
i,j=1,2,3
such that
x_{n}=f^{1}_{n}+f^{2}_{n}+f^{3}_{n}
and
y_{n}=g^{1}_{n}+g^{2}_{n}+g^{3}_{n}.
Hence
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f^{i}_{n},g^{j}_{n}\rangle
and by the triangle inequality for
\Phi
holds
\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f^{i}_{n},g^{j}_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}}).
Integration further gives us

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\,d\mu(t)\leqslant\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f^{i}_{n},g^{j}_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\,d\mu(t)<+\infty

(2.7)

where last inequality holds by the (
2.5
). By homogeneity, we get the claimed result for all
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}}).

Next, we prove that
\int_{\Omega}\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})\,d\mu(t)
is uniformly bounded for all
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w},|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\leqslant 1.
Now, let us denote by
Z=\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})\times\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})
a Banach space endowed with the norm
\|(x,y)\|_{Z}=\max\{|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w},|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\},
and for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
Z_{M}=\{(x,y)\in Z:\int_{\Omega}\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})\,d\mu(t)\leqslant M\}.
By the (
2.7
) we get

To prove that
Z_{M}
is closed, let
(x^{m},y^{m})\in Z_{M},
for every

such that
(x^{m},y^{m})\to(x,y)
as

As
x^{m}\to x

\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}}),
for every

and every

such that
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!h\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant 1
we get

\bigl|{\langle x^{m}_{n}-x_{n},h\rangle}\bigr|\leqslant\biggl({\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\langle x^{m}_{n}-x_{n},h\rangle}\bigr|^{2}}\biggr)^{1/2}\leqslant\sup_{|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!h\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant 1}\biggl({\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\langle x^{m}_{n}-x_{n},h\rangle}\bigr|^{2}}\biggr)^{1/2}.

By taking the supremum over

we get
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!x^{m}_{n}-x_{n}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!x^{m}-x\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{2,w}\to 0.
Moreover, as operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are continuous, we also get
\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle\to\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle,
as

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every

Now, for

and sequence

\Phi

\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle^{N}})_{n=1}^{\infty}})\to\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle^{N}})_{n=1}^{\infty}}),
and by monotonicity properties of
\Phi
we finally get

\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle^{N}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle^{N}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\\
=\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle^{N}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\leqslant\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr).

(2.8)

As the last term in (
2.8
) is independent of
N,
by taking supremum over
N\in\mathbb{N},
again due to monotonicity properties of
\Phi
we get

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle^{N}}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)=\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)\leqslant\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr).

Furthermore, by integrating the last inequality and by the Fatou’s lemma, follows

\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)d\mu(t)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)d\mu(t)\\
\leqslant\varliminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\bigl({\bigl({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x^{m}_{n},y^{m}_{n}\rangle}\bigr)_{n=1}^{\infty}}\bigr)d\mu(t)\leqslant M

(2.9)

where the last estimate holds due to
(x^{m},y^{m})\in Z_{M}.
Therefore
(x,y)
belongs to
Z_{M},
thus
Z_{M}
is closed. We note that details and proofs of mentioned properties of s.n. functions can be found in sections 3. and 4. of Chapter III of
[
5
]
.

Now, by the Baire’s Category Theorem, there exists
M_{0}
such that the open ball
B((x^{0},y^{0}),r)\in Z_{M_{0}}.
For the ease of notation, let us denote by
S(x,y)=\int_{\Omega}\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})d\mu(t)
and we note that (due to the triangle inequality for
\Phi
) for
S
holds
S(x+z,t)\leqslant S(x,t)+S(z,t)
and
S(\alpha x,y)=|{\alpha}|S(x,y),
and similar for the second variable. Therefore, for
(x,y)\in Z
such that
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(x,y)\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{Z}\leqslant 1,
we get

\displaystyle 4S\bigl({x,y}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{r^{2}}S\bigl({rx+x_{0}+rx-x_{0},ry+y_{0}+ry-y_{0}}\bigr)\leqslant

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{r^{2}}

\displaystyle\Bigl(S\bigl({x_{0}+rx,y_{0}+ry}\bigr)+S\bigl({x_{0}+rx,y_{0}-ry}\bigr)+\Bigr.

\displaystyle\Bigl.S\bigl({x_{0}-rx,y_{0}+ry}\bigr)+S\bigl({x_{0}-rx,y_{0}-ry}\bigr)\Bigr)\leqslant\tfrac{4}{r^{2}}\cdot M_{0},

(2.10)

where last inequality in (
2.10
) holds due to
S(x_{0}\pm rx,y_{0}\pm ry)\in Z_{M_{0}}.
Finally, by Bessel’s inequality, every orthonormal sequence
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
belongs to
\ell_{2}^{w}({\mathcal{H}})
and satisfies
\|(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\|_{2,w}\leqslant 1.
Equivalently,
B\times B\subset\{(x,y)\in Z:\|(x,y)\|_{Z}\leqslant 1\},
hence

\sup_{e,f\in B}\!\int_{\Omega}\!\!\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}e_{n},f_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})d\mu(t)\!\leqslant\!\!\sup_{\|(x,y)\|_{Z}\leqslant 1}\!\int_{\Omega}\!\!\Phi({({\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}x_{n},y_{n}\rangle})_{n=1}^{\infty}})d\mu(t)\!\leqslant\!\frac{M_{0}}{r^{2}}\!<\!\!+\infty.

∎

In the case of positive operators, the conclusions of previous Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
hold under weaker assumptions, at least in one particular case. Due to Lemma
1.6
, we can provide alternative sufficient conditions for Gel’fand integrability in the case of operators between continuous and
p
-integrable functions. More precisely,

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
K
be a compact space,
(X,\nu)
be a measure space,
1\leqslant p\leqslant q<+\infty
and
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\to\EuScriptBold B(C(K),L_{q}(X,\nu))
be a weakly
∗
integrable o.v. function. If operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are also positive, then for every measurable
S
the operator
\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu
belongs to
\Pi_{q,p}(C(K),L_{q}(X,\nu)).
Moreover, in the case
q=p
holds
{\mathscr{A}}\in\mathbb{G}(\Omega,\mathfrak{M},\mu,\Pi_{p}(C(K),L_{p}(X,\nu)).

Proof.

Due to Proposition
1.13
, the operator
\int_{S}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu
is positive for every
S\in\mathfrak{M}
and hence, by the Lemma
1.6
belongs to
\Pi_{q,p}(C(K),L_{q}(X,\nu)).
Moreover, we will prove that
{\mathscr{A}}
satisfies the condition (
2.1
), implying proclaimed Gel’fand integrability in the case
p=q.

Let
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{p}^{w}(C(K))
and
(\varphi_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{q^{\prime}}(L_{q^{\prime}}(X,\nu))
be arbitrary, and let
A:=\int_{\Omega}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu.
Since
|{\;\!\!\!\!\;|{\;\!\!\>\!(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\>\;\!\!}|\!\!\;\;\!\!}|_{p,w}=\sup_{s\in K}(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f_{n}(s)|^{p})^{1/p}
(see the proof of
[
4
, Examples 2.9(a)]
) we note that the sequence
(|{f_{n}}|)_{n=1}^{\infty}
also belongs to
\ell_{p}^{w}(C(K)).
Also, by the definition of the space
\ell_{q^{\prime}}(L_{q^{\prime}}(X,\nu)),
we have that
(|\varphi_{n}|)_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\ell_{q^{\prime}}(L_{q^{\prime}}(X,\nu)).
Furthermore, as the operators
{\mathscr{A}}_{t}
are positive and
L_{q}(X,\nu)
is Dedekind complete (details can be found in
[
13
, Example 12.5]
), by
[
9
, Proposition 2.2.6]
we get
|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f_{n}|\leqslant{\mathscr{A}}_{t}|f_{n}|,
for every
n{\in\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in\Omega.
Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f_{n},\varphi_{n}\rangle|d\mu(t)=\int_{\Omega}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\int_{X}({\mathscr{A}}_{t}f_{n})(s)\!\cdot\!\varphi(s)d\nu(s)d\mu(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{X}\!|{\mathscr{A}}_{t}f_{n}|(s)\!\cdot\!|\varphi_{n}|(s)d\nu(s)d\mu(t)\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\int_{X}\!({\mathscr{A}}_{t}|f_{n}|)(s)\!\cdot\!|\varphi_{n}|(s)d\nu(s)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{t}|f_{n}|,|\varphi_{n}|\rangle d\mu(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle A|f_{n}|,|\varphi_{n}|\rangle<+\infty,

due to Lemma
1.5
, as operator
A
is
(q,p)
-summing, being positive.

∎

Without the assumption of positivity, the conclusions of the previous Theorem do not hold. More precisely, condition (
2.1
) needs not to be satisfied, at least for some appropriate compact set
K
and measure space
(X,\nu),
as the following example shows.

Example 2.8
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{N}
with
\mu
being the counting measure. By the Lemma
1.8
function
{\mathscr{A}}\colon\Omega\to\EuScriptBold B(E,F^{*})
is weak
∗
integrable if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x,y\rangle}\bigr|<+\infty

for every
x\in E
and
y\in F.
For
1\leqslant p\leqslant+\infty,
let
T\in\EuScriptBold B(E,\ell_{p})
and let us denote
P_{n}\colon\ell_{p}\to\ell_{p}
an operator defined by

P_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots)=(0,\ldots,0,x_{n},0,\ldots),

for every
n{\in\mathbb{N}}.
Now, we define an o.v. function by
{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=P_{n}T.
Then for
x\in E
and
y\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}
(it is enough to take
y\in c_{0}
in the case
p=1
) holds

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x,y\rangle}\bigr|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\biggl|{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}({P_{n}Tx})_{m}y_{m}}\biggr|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{({Tx})_{n}y_{n}}\bigr|\\
\leqslant\sqrt[p]{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{({Tx})_{n}}\bigr|^{p}}\cdot\sqrt[p^{\prime}]{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigl|{y_{n}}\bigr|^{p^{\prime}}}=\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!Tx\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{p}\cdot\bigl|{\;\!\!\!\!\;\bigl|{\;\!\!\>\!y\>\!\;\!\!}\bigr|\!\!\;\;\!\!}\bigr|_{p^{\prime}}.

(2.11)

Therefore
{\mathscr{A}}
is weak
∗
integrable, so for every
x\in E
and
y\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}
holds

\left\langle\left(\int_{\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu\right)x,y\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{N}}\langle{\mathscr{A}}_{n}x,y\rangle d\mu(n)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle P_{n}Tx,y\rangle=\langle Tx,y\rangle,

where the last equality holds because
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}T
converges in weak
∗
topology to
T.
Thus,
\int_{\mathbb{N}}{\mathscr{A}}d\mu=T.

Next, for every
1\leqslant p<2
(see
[
4
, Remark 3.8(a)]
) and
2<p<+\infty
(see
[
8
, Theorem 7]
), there exists
T\in\EuScriptBold B(C(K),\ell_{p})
(for appropriate compact
K
) that is not
p
-summing. For such
T,
the previously defined o.v. function
n\mapsto{\mathscr{A}}_{n}=P_{n}T
cannot satisfy condition (
2.1
). Moreover, we note that
P_{n}T
is
p
-summing for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
being a rank-one operator.
