Early hemodynamic assessment and longitudinal imaging for predicting graft outcomes post-transplant
Authors: Ziqian Wu, Songxiu Li, Siyu Ouyang, Jingyi Hu, Yuan Qiang, Jie Ding, Qiu Guo, Jidong Gao, Wei Wei, Ke Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe early identification of early allograft dysfunction (EAD) and the long-term prediction of graft-related adverse event-free survival (GRAEFS) are crucial for effective post-transplant management.MethodsThe study encompassed two complementary analyses: (1) an early hemodynamic assessment, which evaluated the diagnostic efficacy of hepatic artery ultrasound parameters for the identification of EAD; and (2) a longitudinal imaging prognostic analysis, which developed radiomics and Vision Transformer Base/16-based (Vit b/16) nomograms utilizing Delta features extracted from serial grayscale ultrasound images at six key time points relative to day 1, for the prediction of 3-year GRAEFS. The performance of the models was externally validated and compared with clinical baseline models.ResultsA hepatic artery resistive index (RI) below 0.55 within 4 days post-transplant is linked to EAD, showing a high negative predictive value (92.2%) for ruling out EAD. The Vit b/16 nomogram excelled in predicting 3-year GRAEFS, with AUCs of 0.951, 0.847, and 0.831 in training, internal, and external validation cohorts, respectively, surpassing the radiomics nomogram. In external validation, it outperformed the best clinical model (AUC: 0.831 vs. 0.808, p = 0.048) with a net reclassification improvement of 0.307 (p = 0.028). Both models were stable in sensitivity analyses excluding tumor recurrence. Interpretability showed the radiomics nomogram emphasized tissue heterogeneity, while the Vit b/16 nomogram focused on liver margin and intrahepatic vascular structures.ConclusionThis study shows that a drop in the hepatic artery RI soon after surgery is linked to EAD, and a Vit b/16-based nomogram from ultrasound images effectively predicts 3-year GRAEFS. These results need prospective validation before clinical use.

Introduction

Liver transplantation (LT) is vital for treating end-stage liver disease [1], but complications like early allograft dysfunction (EAD) can impact patient prognosis and graft survival [2]. EAD occurs in 20–40% of cadaveric liver transplants [3]. Current EAD prediction models like Early Allograft Function (MEAF) [4] and Liver Graft Assessment Following Transplantation (L-GrAFT) [5], despite being validated, struggle with issues like complexity [6], lack of external validation [7], and diverse cohorts [5], hindering their clinical adoption.

Successful LT requires reconstructing multiple blood vessels and maintaining hemodynamic stability for liver function recovery. Ultrasound is integral to LT due to its simplicity, non-invasiveness, and real-time capabilities. It is particularly useful post-surgery for assessing the graft at the bedside and detecting blood flow issues with pulsed Doppler technology [8]. Sun et al. found that contrast-enhanced ultrasound parameters can predict EAD, with the hepatic artery-portal vein arrival interval being a significant risk factor [9]. Meng et al. developed a deep learning model to analyze ultrasound blood flow for diagnosing EAD [10]. However, most studies focus on single-time-point data, the functionality of the transplanted liver is influenced by a multitude of dynamic factors following LT. To comprehensively evaluate the function of the transplanted liver and the patient’s prognosis, it is essential to monitor its progression using longitudinal ultrasound.

Nevertheless, the visual information derived from LT ultrasound images is constrained and heavily reliant on the subjective interpretation of physicians, posing diagnostic challenges. In response to these challenges, radiomics, which leverages machine learning, has demonstrated considerable promise by extracting high-throughput features from images to quantify disease staging, grading, and prognosis [11]. Deep learning has notably enhanced the capacity to represent complex tasks through multi-level neural networks, thereby refining the feature extraction process [12]. The Vision Transformer Base/16 (Vit b/16), which utilizes a global self-attention mechanism, was pre-trained on the ImageNet-21K dataset and has demonstrated superior performance in various tasks, including disease diagnosis [13], tumor segmentation [14], and medical image analysis [15]. Consequently, it has emerged as a significant contributor to the advancement of medical imaging technology.

Given the poor prognosis of EAD, we aimed to assess if early postoperative ultrasound flow parameters could detect it early. However, these parameters were only effective in the first postoperative week, limiting their long-term prognostic use. Therefore, we explored whether additional imaging features from longitudinal grayscale ultrasound could predict long-term graft-related adverse event-free survival (GRAEFS) outcomes, aiming for a comprehensive solution from early detection to long-term follow-up. Materials and methods

Research design and participants

This study is a multicenter retrospective investigation conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Institutional Review Board of Xiamen University Affiliated Xiang’an Hospital (Xiamen Center), Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital (Guiyang Center), and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Dalian Medical University (Dalian Center). Given the retrospective and non-invasive nature of the study, informed consent was waived. The study report was meticulously prepared following the TRIPOD + AI guidelines [16]. Based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 341 patients who underwent orthotopic LT from deceased donors were included in the study, comprising recipients of grafts from both brain-dead and cardiac-dead donors (E1.1). To investigate the application of artificial intelligence in the imaging prognosis of LT, patients from the Xiamen and Guiyang Centers were randomly allocated into a training set and an internal validation set in a 7:3 ratio, while patients from the Dalian Center served as an external validation set (Figure S1).

Inclusion criteria for the study were as follows:patients undergoing orthotopic cadaveric LT;availability of comprehensive clinical data;availability of complete and uninterrupted ultrasound examination data and image records within 1-year post-surgery.

Postoperative laboratory parameters (ALT, AST, TBIL, INR) and grayscale ultrasound images of the transplanted liver were systematically collected at seven specific time points (1, 7, 30, 60, 90, 180, and 365 days). Additionally, hemodynamic parameters of the transplanted liver, including peak systolic velocity (PSV), end diastolic velocity (EDV), and resistive index (RI) of the left hepatic arteries (LHA) and right hepatic arteries (RHA), were dynamically monitored at 1–7 days, and at 30, 60, 90, 180, and 365 days postoperatively. Detailed descriptions of the examination methods and instruments employed are provided in (E1.2) and (Table S1).

The study involves two separate data analysis components:Early hemodynamic analysis: This examines daily Doppler ultrasound data from the first postoperative week (Days 1–7) to identify hemodynamic biomarkers for early detection of EAD before a biochemical diagnosis.Longitudinal imaging prognostic analysis: This evaluates grayscale ultrasound images of the liver at seven time points (Days 1, 7, 30, 60, 90, 180, and 365). It calculates ‘Delta features’ to assess changes over time, using these to develop predictive models for long-term GRAEFS outcomes. Notably, this component of the analysis does not incorporate ultrasound blood flow monitoring data.

All patients were monitored for a minimum of three years following LT, with the final follow-up conducted on October 1, 2025. The GRAEFS is calculated from the initiation of the LT procedure until the first occurrence of any of the aforementioned graft-related adverse events or the conclusion of the study follow-up period. For details regarding GRAEFS as the basis for constructing composite endpoints, please refer to E1.3.

Ultrasound image preprocessing and radiomics analysis

Two professional sonographers (WW and SXL), blinded to the patients’ EAD status and GRAEFS outcomes, manually delineated regions of interest (ROIs) along the boundaries of the liver parenchyma on grayscale images using the 3D Slicer software (version 4.10.1, available at www.slicer.org) [20]. During the deep learning pre-training phase, a series of image augmentation techniques were employed to mitigate overfitting. These techniques included random horizontal and vertical flipping, random affine transformations, random brightness adjustments, and random contrast adjustments.

Utilizing the PyRadiomics software package (version 3.0), a total of 1409 radiomics features were extracted from the ultrasound images at each time point following LT for each patient. To quantify the longitudinal dynamic changes, we calculated the Delta radiomics characteristic, which is defined as the net relative change between the characteristic values on postoperative days 7, 30, 60, 90, 180, and 365, compared to the baseline value on postoperative day 1. To prevent time-related bias, this study uses a landmark analysis framework (E1.4).

Deep learning analysis

This study employs a Vit b/16 pre-trained model for transfer learning. Based on the GRAEFS score following LT, patients were categorized into two groups: a poor prognosis group (GRAEFS < 36 months) and a good prognosis group (GRAEFS ≥ 36 months) [21]. To address the issue of class imbalance, we innovatively combined a weighted random sampler with a cross-entropy loss function that incorporates class frequency weighting. The weighted random sampler is introduced at the data loading stage to balance the class distribution within each batch by oversampling minority class samples. Meanwhile, the cross-entropy loss function, adjusted by the inverse of class frequency weighting, is employed at the loss function level to enhance the algorithm’s focus on minority classes. The model training process employed the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e–4. Detailed methodologies are provided in Section E1.5 and illustrated in Figure 2.

Feature selection and nomogram construction

The dimensionality reduction of Delta features adhered to the protocol established in Section E1.6. Delta radiomics scores and Delta deep learning scores were computed through a weighted summation based on the selected features and their respective weights within the model. The Delta radiomics and Delta deep learning risk scores were used as independent predictors in a Cox regression model. A nomogram converted regression coefficients into a visual scoring scale, allowing clinicians to calculate a patient’s 3-year postoperative GRAEFS probability by summing risk scores at different times. The model predicts outcomes using Delta features available at each time, with later data updating and refining predictions rather than being necessary for the model to function.

Model evaluation and interpretability

In both the training and validation cohorts, the predictive performance of radiomics nomogram and Vit b/16 nomogram for GRAEFS was assessed. Optimal cutoff values for each model score were determined using X-Tile software, allowing for the stratification of patients into high- and low-risk groups. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was subsequently employed to compare differences in GRAEFS between these groups.

This study utilized interpretable artificial intelligence techniques, including SHAP and Grad-CAM, to elucidate the decision-making framework of the model in predicting adverse events within three years post-LT. The analysis identified the most prognostic imaging features and anatomical structures in ultrasound images, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for future clinical translation (E1.7).

Incremental value assessment

This study assessed the added predictive value of longitudinal ultrasound imaging over traditional clinical indicators by developing three Cox regression models: Model 1 (Baseline Clinical) with MELD score, IVC occlusion duration, recipient age, and intraoperative blood loss; Model 2 (Clinical + Early Postoperative Biochemical) adding postoperative EAD status, AST, ALT, INR, and bilirubin levels on Day 7; and Model 3 (Clinical + Biochemical + Doppler) adding hepatic artery RI <0.55 status within 4 days to Model 2. For the basis of variable inclusion, please refer to E1.8.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics 25.0 and R 4.2.0 software. For continuous variables, either the Student’s t test or the Mann–Whitney U test was employed for inter-group comparisons, contingent upon their distribution characteristics. For categorical variables, the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was applied. In the early hemodynamic evaluation, an RI < 0.55 within four days post-transplant was assessed for its ability to identify EAD, using biochemical criteria as the gold standard. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value were calculated, and a receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) curve with area under the curve (AUC) was plotted. The RI < 0.55 cutoff was based on previous research [22]. Multivariate logistic regression, determined if RI < 0.55 is an independent EAD risk factor, for the basis of variable inclusion, please refer to E1.9. Longitudinal imaging prognosis involved univariate and multivariate Cox regression models at various landmark times. Since tumor recurrence events are linked to primary hepatic malignancies rather than graft function, a sensitivity analysis excluding these events will be conducted to reassess the predictive accuracy of the radiomics and Vit b/16 nomograms. The DeLong test will evaluate the statistical significance of AUC differences between the models across cohorts, and net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were calculated. Calibration will be assessed using calibration curves, intercepts, and slopes. Statistical significance was established at p < 0.05 for all tests.

Results

Early hemodynamic characteristics and the identification of EAD

In this study cohort, preoperative baseline data were comparable between the EAD and non-EAD groups (p > 0.05) as presented in Table S2. Postoperative ultrasound hemodynamic analysis revealed that the EAD group demonstrated distinctive changes in hepatic artery blood flow during the early postoperative period (days 1–7), as illustrated in Figure 2. These changes were characterized by relatively higher EDV and significantly lower RI, with most comparisons yielding p values less than 0.05. This disparity persisted until 30 days postoperatively (LHA-EDV, p = 0.025; RHA-RI, p = 0.004), after which it gradually diminished, resulting in no significant differences in the later stages, as detailed in Table S3. Intergroup comparisons of laboratory parameters revealed statistically significant differences only during the first postoperative week, with no significant differences observed thereafter, as shown in Table S4.

Figure 3A–D displays the early postoperative graft hepatic artery spectrograms for patients in both the EAD and non-EAD groups. Using a hepatic artery RI < 0.55 within 4 days post-surgery as a diagnostic criterion, the sensitivity for detecting EAD was 73.2%, specificity was 83.3%, positive predictive value was 53.6%, negative predictive value was 92.2%, and AUC was 0.783 (95% CI: 0.718–0.848). The high negative predictive value indicates an 92.2% probability of not developing EAD for patients with an RI ≥ 0.55, proving effective for ruling out the diagnosis. After adjusting for various factors, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that an RI < 0.55 within 4 days post-surgery is independently linked to EAD, indicating that low resistance in the hepatic artery is a risk factor (Table S5).

Model performance based on longitudinal ultrasound images

According to landmark analysis principles, Delta features and model evaluations were limited to patients without a graft-related adverse event by each time point (Table S7). This study utilized a transfer learning framework based on the Vit b/16 architecture. The model underwent training for 50 epochs, with continuous monitoring of the F1 score and AUC on the validation set to identify the optimal model. Consequently, the model from epoch 26 was selected as the deep learning feature extractor, achieving the highest F1 score (0.868) and AUC (0.822) on the validation set, thereby demonstrating superior classification performance and discriminative capability. Subsequently, this model was employed to extract deep learning features from all images. Detailed information on Delta feature selection (Table S8) and the risk scoring formula (E1.10) is available in the (supplementary materials Figure 4). Based on the Delta feature risk scores at six distinct time points, we developed radiomics nomogram (Figure 3A) and Vit b/16 nomogram (Figure 3B).

The model’s performance evaluation results in the training cohort, as well as the internal and external validation cohorts, are presented in Figure 4. The Vit b/16 nomogram exhibited superior discrimination, calibration, and overall predictive accuracy across all cohorts when compared to the radiomics nomogram. In the external validation cohort, the Vit b/16 nomogram achieved a 3-year AUC of 0.831 (95% CI: 0.730–0.933), surpassing that of the radiomics nomogram, which recorded an AUC of 0.779 (95% CI: 0.661–0.898). DeLong tests confirmed that this difference was statistically significant in both the internal validation cohort (p = 0.047) and the external validation cohort (p = 0.032). In terms of calibration, the Vit b/16 nomogram produced a calibration intercept of 0.347 and a calibration slope of 0.628 in the external validation cohort; its Brier score was 0.213, which is lower than the 0.238 observed for the radiomics nomogram, indicating reduced predictive error. A sensitivity analysis, conducted after excluding tumor recurrence events, revealed that the AUCs for both models across all cohorts varied by less than 0.05 compared to the primary analysis. This finding suggests that the model performance is not significantly affected by the inclusion of the tumor recurrence component within the GRAEFS composite endpoint, thereby affirming the robustness of the study’s conclusions (Table 2).

Model interpretability

To further investigate the decision-making mechanism of the predictive model, this study assessed the contribution of each component within the two nomograms. As illustrated in Figure 5A–C, the Delta1–7 RiskScore exhibited the highest contribution weight in both the radiomics nomogram and the Vit b/16 nomogram, accounting for 39.91% and 31.90%, respectively. This finding underscores the significant impact of incremental features derived from early postoperative ultrasound grayscale images on the overall predictive efficacy of the nomogram.

Moreover, this study employed the SHAP algorithm to assess the predictive significance of radiomic features comprising the Delta1–7 RiskScore (Figure 5D). The findings revealed that the most influential features predominantly originated from the wavelet-transformed images, with tissue heterogeneity indicators, particularly ‘Entropy,’ being the most prominent.

To further investigate the interpretability of deep learning features, this study utilized Grad-CAM to produce visual interpretations (Figure 5E). This technique calculates the contribution of each feature map to the predicted category, generates a weighted activation heatmap, and overlays it onto the original image, thereby visually highlighting the key regions that inform the model’s decisions. The Grad-CAM analysis revealed that for ultrasound images of patients accurately predicted on postoperative day 7, the model’s attention was predominantly concentrated on the surrounding region of the transplanted liver and the intrahepatic vascular structures.

Incremental value analysis

In the external validation cohort, the Vit b/16 nomogram showed a higher 3-year AUC (0.831) than Model 3 (0.808, p = 0.048), while the radiomics nomogram’s AUC (0.779) was not significantly different from Model 3 (p = 0.675) (Table 3). The Vit b/16 nomogram significantly improved risk reclassification over Model 3 in both validation cohorts, with NRIs of 0.459 (p = 0.026) and 0.307 (p = 0.028), and IDIs of 0.024 (p = 0.041) and 0.054 (p < 0.001). The radiomics nomogram also added value in the external cohort (NRI = 0.336, p = 0.004; IDI = 0.058, p < 0.001) but not in the internal cohort (NRI = 0.198, p = 0.948) (Table S9).

Prognostic stratification efficacy of nomogram

To evaluate the prognostic stratification capability of radiomics and the Vit b/16 nomogram, the study categorized patients into high-risk and low-risk groups based on their calculated risk scores. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that the GRAEFS curves for the different risk groups, as determined by the two nomograms, exhibited significant differences across the training, internal validation, and external validation cohorts (Figure 5F–K; all Log-rank tests p < 0.05). Furthermore, the hazard ratios consistently indicated that the nomogram possesses strong discriminative power (refer to Table S10 for details). To further investigate the predictive efficacy of the model within specific clinical subgroups, we performed subgroup analyses on populations with and without EAD. The findings indicated that both nomograms exhibited excellent predictive performance in patients without EAD. Conversely, among patients with EAD, who present with more complex clinical conditions, only the Vit b/16 nomogram independently and significantly predicted prognosis (HR = 2.315, p = 0.007), whereas the radiomics nomogram did not achieve statistical significance in this subgroup (p = 0.431). This result underscores the unique and indispensable value, as well as the enhanced predictive capability, of the Vit b/16 nomogram in high-risk clinical populations where traditional models have proven inadequate (Figure S5).

Discussion

The study reveals that patients with EAD have worse long-term outcomes, highlighting the need for early detection and monitoring. A decrease in the hepatic artery RI shortly after surgery serves as an effective early diagnostic marker for EAD. However, this marker’s usefulness is limited to the first postoperative week, after which standard ultrasound parameters no longer differentiate between EAD and non-EAD patients. This indicates that conventional hemodynamic monitoring alone cannot fully account for the ongoing differences in long-term outcomes. The gap identified through clinical observation led to the second phase of our study, where we hypothesized that grayscale imaging could reveal deeper prognostic insights from the evolving texture and structure of transplanted liver tissue. We developed a predictive model using longitudinal ‘Delta’ imaging features, and its successful validation showed that radiomics and deep learning can detect microstructural changes linked to long-term survival without graft-related issues, offering a valuable tool for managing high-risk patients.

In the ultrasound evaluation following LT, PSV, EDV, and RI are essential parameters for assessing the perfusion status of the hepatic artery [22]. Through dynamic monitoring, this study identified that the EAD group exhibited a characteristic low-resistance state of the hepatic artery during the early postoperative period (days 1–7), marked by a significant increase in EDV and a notable decrease in RI. This difference was particularly pronounced within the first four days post-surgery (p < 0.001) and persisted until day 30 post-surgery. Contrary to conventional understanding, this hemodynamic manifestation of ‘low resistance and high flow’ does not indicate an effective increase in liver parenchymal perfusion. Instead, it may reveal a pathological condition described as ‘false normalization of hepatic artery diastolic blood flow’ [23]. The underlying mechanism involves severe microcirculatory disturbances, such as swelling of hepatic sinusoidal endothelial cells and microthrombus formation, which obstruct systolic blood flow distally, causing backflow into the main channel and resulting in ‘ineffective blood flow’ characterized by increased EDV. The inadequate perfusion fails to satisfy the tissue’s oxygen requirements, potentially exacerbating ischemia–reperfusion injury and impeding the recovery of graft function [24]. While current research predominantly emphasizes the early detection of elevated RI as an indicator of vascular complications [8], this study elucidates the clinical significance of low RI values observed in the early postoperative period. This parameter has a high negative predictive value (92.2%) and moderate sensitivity, similar to tools like D-dimer for venous thromboembolism [25]. Clinicians can use this non-invasive ultrasound measure to quickly identify patients unlikely to develop EAD, allowing them to concentrate monitoring efforts on those with an RI < 0.55.

Given the substantial prognostic disparity between the two patient groups (p < 0.001), this study further investigates the additional prognostic value provided by ultrasound grayscale imaging. The findings of this study indicate that both the Vit b/16-based nomogram and the radiomics-based nomogram exhibit strong discriminative ability, calibration, and clinical net benefit in predicting 3-year GRAEFS, with the Vit b/16 nomogram demonstrating superior performance. The radiomics-based and Vit b/16 nomograms allow for early risk stratification by the seventh postoperative day. As follow-up continues, new ultrasound images can be added to the model for ongoing risk assessment of developing graft-related adverse events. This study’s incremental value analysis shows that the Vit b/16 nomogram, outperforms the best clinical model that includes clinical data, biochemical markers, and Doppler RI. This suggests that deep learning-derived liver parenchyma features offer valuable prognostic insights beyond traditional clinical information. To address the prevalent issue of class imbalance in clinical data, this study implemented targeted strategies at both the data loading and loss function levels to bolster the model’s capability to accurately identify minority classes, thereby enhancing its applicability in real-world clinical settings. Our findings align with a growing body of research that supports the integration of deep learning and radiomics in prognostic modeling. For instance, Wu et al. developed a CT-based deep learning radiomics nomogram aimed at enhancing the risk stratification of early recurrence following LT surgery for hepatocellular carcinoma. The model achieved a C-index of 0.829 in the validation cohort [26]. In a similar vein, Nie et al. demonstrated that intratumoral heterogeneity features derived from radiomics can effectively predict the prognosis of hepatocellular carcinoma patients post-LT [27]. Collectively, these studies indicate that high-throughput features extracted from medical images have the potential to enhance the precision of clinical diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and predictive decision-making.

To further investigate the decision-making mechanisms underlying the two nomograms, it was found that the Delta1–7 RiskScore was the most significant contributor in both models. This finding suggests that early postoperative hemodynamic parameters and high-dimensional features present in grayscale images are critical variables for predicting long-term prognosis. Additionally, the SHAP algorithm was employed to perform attribution analysis on the radiomics incremental features encompassed within the Delta1–7 RiskScore. The findings indicate that the most influential features predominantly originate from the wavelet-transformed images, with the tissue heterogeneity index, represented by ‘Entropy,’ playing a pivotal role. Previous research has suggested that an increase in entropy may be associated with factors such as intratumoral structural disorder and heterogeneous angiogenesis [28]. This study further demonstrates that the microstructural complexity and texture heterogeneity of transplanted liver, as analyzed in the multi-scale wavelet domain, serve as potential long-term prognostic imaging markers. To investigate the interpretability of the deep learning model, this study employed Grad-CAM to produce visual heatmaps. During prediction, the model focused on the marginal regions of the transplanted liver and its intrahepatic vascular structures, areas linked to complications like hepatic artery thrombosis and anastomotic leakage [29,30]. While Grad-CAM highlights image regions influencing the model’s decisions, it doesn’t indicate biological reasoning. However, this attention aligns with clinical assessment focal points, offering some interpretability.

Nonetheless, this study has certain limitations. Although it included samples from three centers, the overall sample size remains limited. Secondly, retrospective study designs are inherently prone to selection bias, necessitating the integration of multimodal data in future research for prospective validation. Thirdly, The GRAEFS endpoint includes diverse events with varying pathological mechanisms and ultrasound associations, suggesting a need for further validation in larger, more uniform datasets. Fourthly, although existing nomograms exhibit commendable predictive performance, manual intervention remains necessary for certain tasks, such as the segmentation of transplanted livers in ultrasound images. This requirement is primarily due to the inadequate accuracy of current automated segmentation models. Future initiatives should focus on incorporating automation technologies to develop an end-to-end prognostic prediction system, thereby minimizing human intervention. Fifthly, the variability in ultrasound image acquisition protocols may introduce heterogeneity; despite the implementation of standardized preprocessing procedures to mitigate this issue, its potential impact warrants further investigation.

This multicenter retrospective study found that a hepatic arterial RI below 0.55 within four days post-surgery is strongly linked to EAD, indicating its potential as an early diagnostic marker. The Vit b/16 nomogram showed superior prediction of 3-year survival without graft-related issues compared to a radiomics model. Despite these findings, the study’s retrospective nature and endpoint variability necessitate further validation to confirm the models’ value and address potential ‘time leakage.’ Prospective multicenter validation and clinical impact assessments are needed before these tools can be used in routine postoperative care.
