Abstract

Introduction

The Takesaki duality theorem for crossed products of von Neumann algebras, pioneered by M. Takesaki in 1973, represents a profound symmetry inherent to von Neumann algebras equipped with group actions.
Recall that a W*-dynamical system is a triple
(G,M,\alpha)
consisting of a von Neumann algebra
M
, a locally compact group
G
, and a continuous homomorphism
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(M)
, where
\mathrm{Aut}(M)
is the group of
*
-automorphisms of
M
with pointwise weak* topology. We denote by
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
the crossed product of
M
by the action
\alpha
of
G
on
M
.
If, in addition,
G
is Abelian, then there exists a dual action
\hat{\alpha}
of
\hat{G}
on
M\rtimes_{\alpha}G
. The Takesaki duality theorem (
[
32
, Theorem4.5]
) says that the iterated crossed product
(M\rtimes_{\alpha}G)\rtimes_{\hat{\alpha}}\hat{G}
is
*
-isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))\bar{\otimes}M
, where
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
is the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
L^{2}(G)
and
\bar{\otimes}
is the von Neumann algebraic tensor product. This theorem not only deepened the understanding of the structure of von Neumann algebras but also became an indispensable tool in their classification (see
[
33
]
).

Inspired by Takesaki duality theorem for crossed products of von Neumann algebras, H. Takai proved a duality theorem for crossed products of
C^{*}
-algebras in 1975. The Takai duality theorem says that the iterated crossed product
(A\rtimes_{\alpha}G)\rtimes_{\hat{\alpha}}\hat{G}
is
*
-isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G))\otimes A
,
where
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(G))
denotes the algebra of compact operators on
L^{2}(G)
.

In 2014, N. C. Phillips asked a problem if there is an
L^{p}
-version of the Takai duality theorem (see
[
26
, Problem 8.7]
). S. Zhu and I proved that the Takai duality theorem can not be generalized to crossed products of
L^{p}
-operator algebras when
p\neq 2
(see
[
36
]
).

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that a Banach algebra
A
is an
L^{p}
-operator algebra
if
it is isometrically isomorphic to a norm closed subalgebra of the algebra
\mathcal{B}(E)
of all bounded linear operators on some
L^{p}
-space
E
.
The study of
L^{p}
-operator algebras traces back to C. Herz’s influential work on harmonic analysis of group algebras in the 1970’s
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
Recently, people renew interest in
L^{p}
-operator algebras due to the work of N. C. Phillips.
In the passing ten years, N. C. Phillips introduced and studied
L^{p}
-operator algebras
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. These studies encourage many authors to participate in the research of
L^{p}
-operator algebras. This includes the work on group
L^{p}
-operator algebras
[
12
,
38
]
; groupoid
L^{p}
-operator algebras
[
13
]
;

-Roe type algebras
[

]
;

-operator crossed products

,
37
]
and the

-Toeplitz algebra
[

]
.

Since von-Neumann algebras are weak* closed
L^{2}
-operator algebras, it is natural to ask if there is a Takesaki duality theorem for crossed products of weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebras.
The aim of this paper is study this problem.

Let

be a weak* closed

-operator algebra dynamical system, we will introduce the weak* closed

-operator crossed product

of

of

It is natural to consider the following problem.

Problem 1.1

Is it the iterated weak* closed

-operator crossed product

-finite separable

Let

Remark 1.5

This shows that Takesaki duality theorem of von-Neumann algebras can be generalized to weak* closed

-operator algebras, but this theorem can not be generalized to weak* closed

-operator algebras when

2.
Crossed products of weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebras

In this section, we introduce crossed products of weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebras and their basis properties.

Let
E
be a
\sigma
-finite separable
L^{p}
-space and
E^{\prime}
be the dual space of
E
.
Let
\mathcal{N}(E)=E^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}E
denote the space of nuclear operator operators on
E
, where
\widehat{\otimes}
is the projective tensor product. Then
\mathcal{B}(E)
is the dual space of
\mathcal{N}(E)
by way of dual paring

\langle T,\xi\otimes\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,T\eta\rangle

where
\xi\in E^{\prime}
and
\eta\in E
.
We say that a net
(T_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{B}(E)
converges weak* to an operator
T
in
\mathcal{B}(E)
if

\langle T_{\alpha},\omega\rangle\rightarrow\langle T,\omega\rangle

for all
\omega\in E^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}E
.

A
weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebra
A
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(E)
which is weak* closed in
\mathcal{B}(E)
.
Let
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
be the group of isometric automorphisms of
A
. We consider the pointwise weak* topology on
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
, that is, a net
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Aut}(A)
converges to
\varphi_{0}\in\mathrm{Aut}(A)
if

\langle\varphi_{\lambda}(a)-\varphi_{0}(a),\omega\rangle\rightarrow 0

for all
a\in A
and
\omega\in\mathcal{N}(E)
. A
weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebra dynamical system
is a triple
(G,A,\alpha)
consisting of a locally compact group
G
, a weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebra
A
, and a weak* continuous isometric action of
G
on
A
, that is,
\|\alpha_{t}(a)\|=\|a\|
and
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(A)
is continuous, which means that for each
a\in A
, the function

\alpha(a):G\rightarrow A;t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(a)

is continuous on
G
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group, then there exists a left Haar measure
\mu
on
G
.
Denote by
C_{c}(G,A,\alpha)
the vector space of continuous compactly supported functions
G\rightarrow A
, made into an algebra over
\mathbb{C}
with product given by twisted convolution, that is,

(f*g)(t):=\int_{G}f(s)\alpha_{s}(g(s^{-1}t))d\mu(s)

for all
f,g\in C_{c}(G,A,\alpha)
and
t\in G
.

Given two measure spaces
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
, we denote by
L^{p}(X,\mu)\otimes_{p}L^{p}(Y,\nu)
the
L^{p}
-tensor product, which can be identified with
L^{p}(X\times Y,\mu\times\nu)
via
\xi\otimes\eta(x,y)=\xi(x)\eta(y)
for
\xi\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
and
\eta\in L^{p}(Y,\nu)
.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{B}(E)
be a weak* closed subalgebra, and
we denote by
\pi_{0}:A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
be the canonical representation of
A
. Its associated
regular covariant representation
is the pair
(\pi,\lambda_{p}^{E})
given by

\pi(a)(\xi)(s):=\pi_{0}\left(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a)\right)(\xi(s))\quad

and

\lambda_{p}^{E}(s)(\xi)(t):=\xi(s^{-1}t)

for all
a\in A
,
\xi\in L^{p}(G,E)\cong L^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E
, and
s,t\in G
.
For any
t\in G
and
a\in A
, one can check that

\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)\pi(a)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t^{-1})=\pi(\alpha_{t}(a)).

The
integrated form
of
(\pi,\lambda_{p}^{E})
is an isometric representation
\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}:C_{c}(G,A,\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E))
given by

\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f):=\int_{G}\pi(f(t))\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)d\mu(t)

for all
f\in C_{c}(G,A,\alpha)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{B}(E)
be a weak* closed subalgebra and let
\alpha
be a weak* continuous isometric action of

on
A
. The weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator crossed product of

Since the crossed products of von-Neumann algebras were used to denote by

The pseudomeasure algebras are special case of weak* closed

We let

\lambda_{p}(s)g(t)=g(s^{-1}t),\penalty\ \penalty\ \rho_{p}(s)g(t)=g(ts)

(i)

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
(G,A,\alpha)
be a weak*
L^{p}
-operator algebra dynamical system, where
G
is a countable discrete group,
A\subset\mathcal{B}(E)
be a weak* closed subalgebra, and
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(A)
. Then
W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha)\subset\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{0}:A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
is the canonical representation, and let
(\pi,\lambda_{p}^{E})
be its associated regular representation.
For each

, from the definition of
\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)
, we have

\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)=\lambda_{p}(t)\otimes I_{E},

where
\lambda_{p}:G\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))
is the left regular representation, and

is the identity operator on

. This shows that
\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)\in\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
.

Let
\delta_{t}
be the canonical basis of

, and let
e_{t,t}
is the rank one projection in
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))
satisfying
e_{t,t}(\sum_{s\in G}a_{s}\delta_{s})=a_{t}\delta_{t}
for all
\sum_{s\in G}a_{s}\delta_{s}\in l^{p}(G)
.
Identifying

with the

-direct sum
\bigoplus_{t\in G}E
, we may simply take the
L^{p}
-direct sum representation

\pi(a)=\bigoplus_{t\in G}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a))\in\mathcal{B}(\bigoplus_{t\in G}E).

In the spatial
L^{p}
-operator tensor product picture, we have

\pi(a)=\sum_{t\in G}e_{t,t}\otimes\pi_{0}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a))\in\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}A.

This finish the proof of this Lemma.
∎

3.
Dual action

If
G
is abelian, then we let
\hat{G}
denote the dual group of
G
. For each
\gamma\in\hat{G}
, we denote by
V_{\gamma}
the invertible isometric operator on

(V_{\gamma}f)(s)=\overline{\gamma(s)}f(s)

for all

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\pi_{0}:A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
be the canonical representation, and let
(\pi,\lambda_{p}^{E})
be its associated regular representation.
For each
\gamma\in\hat{G}
and

(i)

\left(V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E}\right)\left(\lambda_{p}^{E}(s)\right)\left(V_{\gamma^{-1}}\otimes I_{E}\right)=\overline{\gamma(s)}\lambda_{p}^{E}(s)
;

(ii)

(V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\pi(a)(V_{\gamma^{-1}}\otimes I_{E})=\pi(a).

Proof.

(i)
Recall that
\lambda_{p}:G\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(G))
is the left regular representation.
To prove (i), it suffices to show that
V_{\gamma}\lambda_{p}(s)V_{\gamma^{-1}}=\overline{\gamma(s)}\lambda_{p}(s)
for all
s\in G
and
\gamma\in\hat{G}.

For each

, we have that

\displaystyle\left(V_{\gamma}\lambda_{p}(s)V_{\gamma^{-1}}f\right)(t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(t)}\left(\lambda_{p}(s)V_{\gamma^{-1}}f\right)(t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(t)}\left(V_{\gamma^{-1}}f\right)(s^{-1}t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(t)}\cdot\overline{\gamma^{-1}(s^{-1}t)}f(s^{-1}t)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(s)}\left(\lambda_{p}(s)f\right)(t).

This implies that
V_{\gamma}\lambda_{p}(s)V_{\gamma^{-1}}=\overline{\gamma(s)}\lambda_{p}(s)
.

(ii) If
\xi\in L^{p}(G,E)
, then
\left((V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\xi\right)(s)=\overline{\gamma(s)}\xi(s)
.
Then we get

\displaystyle\left((V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\pi(a)\xi\right)(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(s)}\left(\pi(a)\xi\right)(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\gamma(s)}\pi_{0}(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(s))\xi(s)

\displaystyle=\pi_{0}(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(s))(V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\xi(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\pi(a)(V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\xi\right)(s).

Hence
(V_{\gamma}\otimes I_{E})\pi(a)(V_{\gamma^{-1}}\otimes I_{E})=\pi(a).

)

C^{*}
-core is invariant under isometric isomorphism between
L^{p}
-operator algebras.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
. Let
G
be a countable discrete group,
A\subset\mathcal{B}(E)
be a weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator algebra, and
\alpha
be a weak* continuous isometric action of
G
on
A
. Then the canonical embedding
A\subset W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha)
identifies the
C^{*}
-core of
W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha)
with that of
A
, that is,

\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha))=\mathrm{core}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{0}:A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
be the canonical representation, and let
(\pi,\lambda_{p}^{E})
be its associated regular representation.
For each
s,t\in G
and
\xi\in E
,
we have

\lambda_{p}^{E}(s)(\delta_{s}\otimes\xi)=\delta_{st}\otimes\xi

and

\pi(a)(\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)=\delta_{t}\otimes\pi_{0}(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(a))(\xi).

We define
F:\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
by

F(T)(\xi)=(T(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi))(e)

for all
T\in\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E))
and
\xi\in E
, where
e
is the unit of
G
.

Claim 1.

F
is weak* continuous.

Let
\{T_{\alpha}\}
be a net in
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E)
such that
\{T_{\alpha}\}
converges to
T
in weak* topology.
For each
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\in E^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}E
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F(T_{\alpha}),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},F(T_{\alpha})\eta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},T_{\alpha}(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\delta_{e}\otimes\xi_{i},T_{\alpha}(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle T_{\alpha},\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi_{i})\otimes(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle T,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi_{i})\otimes(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\delta_{e}\otimes\xi_{i},T(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},T(\delta_{e}\otimes\eta_{i})(e)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},F(T)\eta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F(T),x\rangle.

This proves Claim 1.

Claim 2.
We have
F\circ(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E})=\pi_{0}\circ F
, that is, the following diagram commutates:

\begin{CD}W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha)@ >\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}>>\mathcal{B}(l^{p}\otimes_{p}E)\\
@V{}V{E_{e}}V@V{}V{F}V\\
A@>{\pi_{0}}>{}>A.\end{CD}

Since all the maps involved are weak* continuous, it suffices to prove this by showing

F\circ(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E})(f)=\pi_{0}\circ F(f)

for all
f\in C_{c}(G,A,\alpha)
. Let
f=\sum_{s\in G}a_{s}\delta_{s}
and
\xi\in E
, a direct computation shows that

\pi_{0}\circ E_{e}(\sum_{s\in G}a_{s}\delta_{s})=a_{e},

and

\displaystyle F\circ(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E})(f)\xi

\displaystyle=(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E})(f)(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in G}\pi(a_{s})\lambda_{p}^{E}(s)(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in G}(\delta_{s}\otimes\pi_{0}(\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a_{s}))\xi)(e)

\displaystyle=a_{e}\xi.

This proves Claim 2.

Claim 3.

Let
E=L^{p}(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite separable
L^{p}
-space. If
\eta:G\rightarrow L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
is a bounded function with associated multiplication operator
M_{\eta}\in\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E)
, then
F(M_{\eta}\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))=0
when
t\neq e
.

For each
\xi\in E
and
t\in G,t\neq e
, we have

\displaystyle F(M_{\eta}\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))(\xi)

\displaystyle=(M_{\eta}\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))(\delta_{e}\otimes\xi)(e)

\displaystyle=\eta(e)((\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)(e))

\displaystyle=0.

This proves Claim 3.

Claim 4.
Let
f\in\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha))
. Then
E_{e}(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))=0
when
t\neq e
.

Note that
\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f)\in\mathrm{core}(\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E))
, it follows from
[
5
, Example 2.11]
that there exists a bounded function
\eta:G\rightarrow E
such that
M_{\eta}=\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f)
. For each
t\in G,t\neq e
,
by Claim 2 and Claim 3, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{0}(E_{e}(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)))

\displaystyle=F(\pi\rtimes\lambda_{p}^{E}(f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))

\displaystyle=F(M_{\eta}\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))

\displaystyle=0.

This proves Claim 4.

Now we shall prove this proposition. Obviously, we have
\mathrm{core}(A)\subset\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha))
. If
f\in\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha))
, to finish the proof of this proposition, it suffices to show that
E_{e}(f)=f
. By
[
5
, Proposition 2.16]
, we have
E_{e}(f)\in\mathrm{core}(A)
.Thus for each
t\in G\setminus\{e\}
, we have

E_{e}(f\lambda_{p}^{E}(t))=0=E_{e}(E_{e}(f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t)).

For
t=e
, we have

E_{e}(f\lambda_{p}^{E}(e)=E_{e}(f)=E_{e}(E_{e}(f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(e)).

This shows that

E_{t}(E_{e}(f)-f)=E_{e}((E_{e}(f)-f)\lambda_{p}^{E}(t^{-1}))=0

for each
t\in G
. Therefore,
E_{e}(f)=f
.
∎

5.
The Gelfand theory of
p
-pseudomeasure algebras

In this section, we study the Gelfand theory of
p
-pseudomeasure algebra

when

is a locally compact abelian group. The reader will see that then Takesaki duality heavily depend on Gelfand theory. All the new phenomena of Takesaiki duality for weak* closed
L^{p}
-operator crossed products originally come from the Gelfand theory of
p
-pseudomeasure algebras.
Firstly, we look into the
C^{*}
-core of
PM_{p}(G)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)\setminus\{2\},
and let
G
be a locally compact group. If
PM_{p}(G)=CV_{p}(G)
, then
\mathrm{core}(PM_{p}(G))=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

The algebras
PM_{p}(G)
and
CV_{p}(G)
are always unital (see
[
15
]
). For any
f\in(PM_{p}(G))_{h}
, then
f
is a multiplication operator on
L^{p}(G)
. Since
f\in PM_{p}(G)=CV_{p}(G)
, it follows that

f\rho_{p}(t)=\rho_{p}(t)f

for all
t\in G
, where
\rho_{p}
is the right regular representation of
G
. This implies that

f(s)=f(st)

for all
s,t\in G
. Then
f
is a real-valued constant function. Therefore,
\mathrm{core}(PM_{p}(G))=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Secondly,
we introduce
p
-incompressible
L^{p}
-operator algebras. This property is invariant under isomorphisms of
L^{p}
-operator algebras. The definition is inspired by N. C. Phillips and B. Johnson’ work on incompressible Banach algebras
[
29
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
A
be an
L^{p}
-operator algebra.
We say that
A
is
p
-incompressible
if whenever
E
is an
L^{p}
-space and
\varphi:A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(E)
is a bounded injective homomorphism, then
\varphi
is bounded below.

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

Let
A
and
B
be two
L^{p}
-operator algebras. Assume that
A
and
B
are isomorphic, then
B
is
p
-incompressible if and only if
A
is
p
-incompressible.

(ii)

The
M_{n}^{p}
and
C^{*}
-algebras are examples of
p
-incompressible
L^{p}
-operator algebras (see
[
29
]
).

Lemma 5.4

(
[
4
, Exercise 5 on page 93]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and let
T\in\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
. Then there is a constant
c>0
such that
\|Tx\||\geq c\|x\|
for all
x\in X
if and only if
\ker T=\{0\}
and
\mathrm{ran}T
is closed.

The algebra
L^{\infty}(G)
is a dual space of
L^{1}(G)
, then there is a natural weak* topology of
L^{\infty}(G)
viewed as the dual space of
L^{1}(G)
. Also, there is a natural multiplicative representation
M:L^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(G));f\mapsto M_{f}
, where
M_{f}
is the multiplicative operator on
L^{p}(G)
. So there is a weak* topology on
L^{\infty}(G)
viewed as the operators on
L^{p}(G)
, and we call this topology
operator weak* topology
. In the following lemma, we will show that this two weak* topology coincide.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
p>1
and let
G
be a locally compact group. The natural weak* topology on
L^{\infty}(G)
coincides with operator weak* topology.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{\alpha}\}
be a net in
L^{\infty}(G)
converges to
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
in weak* topology. By uniform boundedness principle we may assume
that
\{f_{\alpha}\}
is uniformly bounded. For each
\xi\in L^{q}(G)
and
\eta\in L^{p}(G)
, by H
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
lder inequality, we have
\xi\eta\in L^{1}(G)
. Then

\langle\xi,M_{f_{\alpha}}\eta\rangle=\langle f_{\alpha},\xi\eta\rangle\rightarrow\langle f,\xi\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,M_{f}\eta\rangle.

Since the operator weak* topology coincides with weak operator topology on bounded sets, it follows that the net
\{f_{\alpha}\}
converges to
f
in operator weak* topology.

On the other hand, let
\{f_{\alpha}\}
be a net in
L^{\infty}(G)
converges to
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
in operator weak* topology. For each
g\in L^{1}(G)
, we let
g^{\frac{1}{p}}(t)=g(t)|g(t)|^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda(t)
and
g^{\frac{1}{q}}(t)=g(t)|g(t)|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}
, where
g(t)=\lambda(t)|g(t)|
. Then we have
g^{\frac{1}{p}}\in L^{p}(G)
and
g^{\frac{1}{q}}\in L^{q}(G)
. Therefore, one can check that

\langle f_{\alpha},g\rangle=\langle f_{\alpha},g^{\frac{1}{p}}g^{\frac{1}{q}}\rangle=\langle g^{\frac{1}{q}},M_{f_{\alpha}}g^{\frac{1}{p}}\rangle\rightarrow\langle g^{\frac{1}{q}},M_{f}g^{\frac{1}{p}}\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle.

Hence the net
\{f_{\alpha}\}
converges to
f
in weak* topology.
∎

\Phi_{1}:C_{c}(\hat{G}\times G,A)\rightarrow C_{c}(G\times\hat{G},A)
given by

\left(\Phi_{1}(x)\right)(t,\tau)=\tau(t)x(\tau,t).

Furthermore, we define an isometry

given by

Then for each

and
\xi\in L^{p}(\hat{G}\times G,E)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\Phi_{1}(x)(W\xi)\right)(t,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\hat{G}}\overline{\gamma(t)}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(\Phi_{1}(x)(s,\gamma))W\xi(s^{-1}t,\gamma^{-1}\tau)d\nu(\gamma)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\hat{G}}\overline{\gamma(t)}\alpha_{t^{-1}}(\gamma(s)x(\gamma,s))\overline{(\gamma^{-1}\tau)(s^{-1}t)}\xi(\gamma^{-1}\tau,s^{-1}t)d\nu(\gamma)d\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\tau(t)}\int_{\hat{G}}\int_{G}\gamma(s)\alpha_{t^{-1}}(x(\tau,s))\xi(\gamma^{-1}\tau,s^{-1}t)d\mu(s)d\nu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\overline{\tau(t)}(x\xi)(\tau,t)=W(x\xi)(t,\tau).

This implies that
\Phi_{1}(x)=WxW^{-1}.

For each
x\in W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha),\hat{\alpha})
, we still define
\Phi_{1}(x)=WxW^{-1}.

Claim 1.
If
\{x_{n}\}
is a sequence in

such that
\{x_{n}\}
converges to

in weak* topology, then

converges to

Let

be given as in Claim 1.
For each

, then

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{1}(x_{n}),\omega\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\zeta_{i},Wx_{n}W^{-1}\theta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle W^{\prime}\zeta_{i},x_{\alpha}W^{-1}\theta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x_{n},\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}W^{\prime}\zeta_{i}\otimes W^{-1}\theta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\langle x,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}W^{\prime}\zeta_{i}\otimes W^{-1}\theta_{i}\rangle

This prove Claim 1.
Using similar method from Claim 1, one can check that

is weak* continuous. Obviously,
\Phi_{1}
is isometric.

6.2.
The Gelfand transform properties of
\Phi_{2}

In this subsection, we study the properties of
\phi_{2}
. This map is essentially induced by Gelfand transform
\Gamma_{p}:PM_{p}(\hat{G})\rightarrow l^{\infty}(G)
. We will see that
\Phi_{2}
share similar properties of
\Gamma_{p}
. To show the Gelfand transform is
p
-completely contractive, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
A\subset\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(X,\mu))
be a norm closed subalgebra, and let
B=L^{\infty}(Y,\nu)\subset\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(X,\nu))
. If
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
is a bounded linear map, then
\|\varphi\|_{cb}=\|\varphi\|.

Proof.

Since
L^{p}
-operator algebras are
p
-operator spaces, we will use the axioms (see
[
8
, axiom
\mathcal{M}_{p}
]
) of
p
-operator spaces to prove this Lemma.
Let
a=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes a_{i,j}\in M_{n}^{p}\otimes_{p}A
.
For each
\beta=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}\}^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we let
\beta^{*}=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{n}\},
then we have the following

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{id}_{M_{n}^{p}}\otimes\varphi(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes a_{i,j})\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(e_{i,j}\otimes\varphi(a_{i,j})\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in Y}\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes\varphi(a_{i,j})(y)\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in Y,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\alpha\|_{p}=\|\beta\|_{q}=1}\left\langle\beta,\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes\varphi(a_{i,j})(y)\right)\alpha\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in Y,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\alpha\|_{p}=\|\beta\|_{q}=1}\left|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}\varphi(a_{i,j})(y)\alpha_{i}\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in Y,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\alpha\|_{p}=\|\beta\|_{q}=1}\left|\varphi\left(\beta^{*}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes a_{i,j}\right)\alpha\right)(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\|\alpha\|_{p}=\|\beta\|_{q}=1}\left\|\beta^{*}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes a_{i,j}\right)\alpha\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\left\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes a_{i,j}\right\|.

This shows that
\|\mathrm{id}_{M_{n}^{p}}\otimes\varphi\|\leq\|\varphi\|
for all positive integer
n
, and thus
\|\mathrm{id}_{M_{n}^{p}}\otimes\varphi\|=\|\varphi\|
.
∎

Let
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha
be the action of
G
on
l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
given by

(\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha)_{s}(f)(t)=\alpha_{s}(f(s^{-1}t))

for all

.
Recall that
\beta
is the action of
G
on

given by

for all

,
\tau\in\hat{G}
and

.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group.
Let
\varphi_{2}:=\Gamma_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}:W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota)\rightarrow l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
. Then the following hold:

(i)

the map
\varphi_{2}
is weak* continuous
p
-completely contractive;

(ii)

the map
\varphi_{2}
is injective with dense range in weak* topology;

(iii)

the map
\varphi_{2}
is surjective if and only if either
G
is finite or
p=2
;

(iv)

the map
\varphi_{2}
is isometrically isomorphic if either
p=2
or
G
is trivial;

(v)

\varphi_{2}
is equivariant for the action
\beta
of
G
on
W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota)
and the action
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha
of

on

.

Proof.

(i) Note that
\iota
is the trivial action of
\hat{G}
on
A
, then we have that
W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota)=PM_{p}(\hat{G})\bar{\otimes}A
.
Let
\Gamma_{p}:PM_{p}(\hat{G})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(G)
be the Gelfand transform. By Lemma
6.4
, it follows that
\Gamma_{p}
is
p
-completely contractive. Then by Lemma
2.7
, there exists a weak* continuous
p
-completely contractive homomorphism
\hat{\Gamma}_{p}:PM_{p}(\hat{G})\bar{\otimes}A\rightarrow L^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
such that
\hat{\Gamma}_{p}(f\otimes a)=\Gamma_{p}(f)\otimes a
for all

and

. We denote by
\varphi_{2}=\Gamma_{p}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}=\hat{\Gamma}_{p}
. This proves (i).

(ii) Obviously,
\varphi_{2}
has dense range.
Now we shall prove that
\varphi_{2}
is injective.
For each

, we assume that
\varphi_{2}(F)=0
. Let
F_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{m(n)}f_{i}^{(n)}\otimes a_{i}^{(n)}\in PM_{p}(\hat{G})\otimes_{alg}A
such that

converges to

in weak* topology. By uniform boundedness principle we may assume that

8

where

.
Note that the inclusion

then it suffices to show that

Since

is weak* continuous, it follows that

\displaystyle(\sigma\circ\Gamma_{p})(F_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m(n)}\sum_{t\in G}\Gamma_{p}(f_{i}^{(n)})(t)\sigma(t)\eta(a_{i}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\sigma\otimes\eta(\varphi_{2}(F_{n}))\rightarrow 0.

. Hence

(iii) Let

. By Theorem
5.6
, it follows that

G

is surjective if and only if

(iv) If either

2.7

(v) The Gelfand transformation

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\beta_{s}(g)(\gamma)\overline{\gamma(t)}d\mu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{G}}\gamma(s)\alpha_{s}(g(\gamma))\overline{\gamma(t)}d\mu(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}(\int_{\hat{G}}g(\gamma)\overline{\gamma(s^{-1}t)}d\mu(\gamma))

\varphi_{2}
,

By Lemma
2.9

Proposition 6.6
.

With the notations given above, then we have the following

(i)

the map
\Phi_{2}
is injective and has dense range in weak* topology;

(ii)

the map
\Phi_{2}
is surjective if and only if

(iii)

Choose

6.3.
The weak* continuous isometry
\Phi_{3}

Let
\mathrm{id}
be the trivial action of
G
on
A
. Then we can form the tensor product action of

(\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id})_{t}f(s):=f(t^{-1}s)

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id}
be defined as above. If, in addition,

is a

such that

for all

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{3}:l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A\rightarrow l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
be given by

\varphi_{3}(f)(t)=\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f(t))

for all
f\in l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
. By Lemma
2.9
, it suffices to prove that
\varphi_{3}
is a weak* continuous,
p
-completely isometric, equivariant map for the action
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha
of
G
on
l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
and the action
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id}
of
G
on
l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
.
Indeed, if we let
\Phi_{3}=\varphi_{3}\rtimes\mathrm{id}
, this proves Proposition
6.7
.
Then we will prove these by showing the following three Claims.

Claim 1.

\varphi_{3}
is weak* continuous.

Assume that
f_{n},f\in l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
such that
f=\lim_{\mathrm{weak}*,n}f_{n}
. For each
t\in G
,
\zeta\in L^{q}(X,\mu)
and
\theta\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\zeta,f_{n}(t)\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\delta_{t}\otimes\zeta,f_{n}(\delta_{t}\otimes\theta)\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\langle\delta_{t}\otimes\zeta,f(\delta_{t}\otimes\theta)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\zeta,f(t)\theta\rangle.

This implies that
f_{n}
is uniformly bounded, so there exists a positive number
M
such that
\|f_{n}\|\leq M.

Since
\alpha
is a weak*continuous action of
G
on
A
, it follows that

\langle\zeta,\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f_{n}(t))\theta\rangle\rightarrow\langle\zeta,\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f(t))\theta\rangle.

Let
x=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\xi_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\in(l^{q}(G)\otimes_{q}L^{q}(X,\mu))\widehat{\otimes}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}L^{p}(X,\mu))
.
Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}|\langle\xi_{i}(t),\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f_{n}(t))\eta_{i}(t)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}|\langle\xi_{i}(t),\eta_{i}(t)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}\|\xi_{i}(t)\|_{q}\|\eta_{i}(t)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\sum_{t\in G}\|\xi_{i}(t)\|_{q}^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}(\sum_{t\in G}\|\eta_{i}(t)\|_{p}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=M\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\|\xi_{i}\|_{q}\|\eta_{i}\|_{p}<\infty,

it follows from Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem that

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{3}(f_{n}),x\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},\varphi_{3}(f_{n})\eta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}\langle\xi_{i}(t),(\varphi_{3}(f_{n})\eta_{i}(t))\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}\langle\xi_{i}(t),\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f_{n}(t))\eta_{i}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\rightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}\langle\xi_{i}(t),\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f(t))\eta_{i}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{t\in G}\langle\xi_{i}(t),\varphi_{3}(f)\eta_{i}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\langle\xi_{i},\varphi_{3}(f)\eta_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi_{3}(f),x\rangle.

This proves Claim 1.

Claim 2.

\varphi_{3}
is
p
-completely isometric.

For each
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes f_{i,j}\in M_{n}^{p}\otimes_{p}A

is a

-completely isometric action of

on

, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{id}_{M_{n}^{p}}\otimes\varphi_{3}(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes f_{i,j})\|

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes\varphi_{3}(f_{i,j})\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in G}\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes\alpha_{t^{-1}}(f_{i,j}(t))\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in G}\|\sum_{ij=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes f_{i,j}(t)\|

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e_{i,j}\otimes f_{i,j}\|.

This proves Claim 2.

Claim 3.

\varphi_{3}
is equivariant map for the action
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha
of
G
on

and the action
\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id}
of
G
on
l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A
.

For each

,
a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}((\mathrm{lt}\otimes\alpha)_{s}(f))(t))

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}_{t}(\alpha_{s}(f(s^{-1}t)))

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}_{s^{-1}t}(f(s^{-1}t))

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id})_{s}(\varphi_{3}(f))(t).

This proves Claim 3.
∎

6.4.
The weak* continuous isometry
\Phi_{4}

Proposition 6.8
.

There exists a weak* continuous isometrical isomorphism
\Phi_{4}
from
W^{*}_{p}(G,l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A,\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id})
onto
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}A.

Proof.

We define an invertible isometry
V:l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E\rightarrow l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E
as
\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi\mapsto\delta_{st}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi
, where
\delta_{s}
is the canonical basis of
l^{p}(G)
for all
s\in G
and
\xi\in E
.
We denote by
I_{l^{p}(G)}
and
I_{E}
the identity operator on
l^{p}(G)
and
E
, respectively.

Let
\pi_{0}:l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G)\otimes_{p}E)
be a representation given by
\pi(f\otimes a)=M_{f}\otimes a
for all
f\in l^{\infty}(G)
and
a\in A
, where
M_{f}
is the multiplication operator on

\displaystyle V\pi(f\otimes a)(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=V(\delta_{s}\otimes\pi_{0}((\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id})_{s^{-1}}(f\otimes a))(\delta_{t}\otimes\xi))

\displaystyle=V(\delta_{s}\otimes M_{(\mathrm{lt})_{s^{-1}}(f)}\delta_{t}\otimes a\xi))

\displaystyle=V(\delta_{s}\otimes f(st)\delta_{t}\otimes a\xi)

\displaystyle=f(st)\delta_{st}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes a\xi

\displaystyle=(M_{f}\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes a)(\delta_{st}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=(M_{f}\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes a)(V(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)).

It follows that

(1)

\displaystyle V\pi(f\otimes a)V^{-1}=M_{f}\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes a.

It is easy to check that
\lambda_{p}^{l^{p}(G,E)}=\lambda_{p}\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E}
, where
\lambda_{p}:G\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))
is the left-regular representation of
G
on
l^{p}(G)
.
For each

\displaystyle V(\lambda_{p}(r)\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E})V^{-1}(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=V(\lambda_{p}(r)\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E})(\delta_{st^{-1}}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=V(\delta_{rst^{-1}}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi)

\displaystyle=\delta_{rs}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{p}(r)\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E})(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\xi).

It follows that

(2)

\displaystyle V(\lambda_{p}(r)\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E})V^{-1}=\lambda_{p}(r)\otimes I_{l^{p}(G)}\otimes I_{E}.

Since
f\in l^{\infty}(G)
and
V
is an isometry, by (
1
) and (
2
), we have the following weak* continuous isometric isomorphism

V(W^{*}_{p}(G,l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A,\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id}))V^{-1}\cong\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}\mathbb{C}I_{l^{p}(G)}\bar{\otimes}A.

Obviously,
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}\mathbb{C}I_{l^{p}(G)}\bar{\otimes}A
is weak* continuous isometrically isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}A
. We denote by
\Phi_{4}
the weak* continuous isometric isomorphism from
W^{*}_{p}(G,l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}A,\mathrm{lt}\otimes\mathrm{id})
onto
\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}A
.
∎

Now we shall prove that
\Phi=\Phi_{4}\circ\Phi_{3}\circ\Phi_{2}\circ\Phi_{1}
is equivariant.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the double dual action
\hat{\hat{\alpha}}
of
G
on
W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},W^{*}_{p}(G,A,\alpha),\hat{\alpha})
and the action
(\mathrm{rt}\otimes\alpha)\otimes\mathrm{id}
of

Proposition 6.9

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}\circ\Phi_{2}\circ\Phi_{1}(F)(s,t)

Note that isometric isomorphisms between

-operator algebras preserve

-cores, then Theorem

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

(i)

\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota),\beta))=\mathrm{core}(A)

\mathrm{core}(\mathcal{B}(l^{p}(G))\bar{\otimes}(A))=l^{\infty}(G)\bar{\otimes}\mathrm{core}(A)

Proof.

(i) By Proposition
4.4

\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(G,W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota),\beta))=\mathrm{core}(W^{*}_{p}(\hat{G},A,\iota))

Since
\iota
is a trivial action of
\widehat{G}
on

For each

(see
[
2

5

.

f(.,x)
is a real-valued function in

.

5.1
