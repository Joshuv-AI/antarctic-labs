CLEIA-based type IV collagen 7S for second-step assessment of MRE-defined clinically significant fibrosis in MASLD with indeterminate FIB-4
Authors: Shiho Murakami, Miwa Kawanaka, Kento Imajo, Syohei Shiota, Nozomi Miyake, Masahiko Sue, Takuya Adachi, Yasuto Takeuchi, Hideki Onishi, Akinobu Takaki, Noriyo Urata, Hirofumi Kawamoto, Motoyuki Otsuka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Stepwise MASLD pathways based on the fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index leave a large indeterminate subgroup requiring second-step assessment. We evaluated automated chemiluminescent enzyme immunoassay (CLEIA)-based type IV collagen 7S (COL4-7S) as a candidate second-line test for MRE-defined clinically significant fibrosis (≥F2). In this retrospective derivation-validation study, adults with MASLD underwent clinically indicated magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) with serum biomarkers measured within 30 days in a two-center derivation cohort (n=347) and an independent external validation cohort (n=126). COL4-7S was compared with ELF, M2BPGi, and FIB-4 using MRE-defined fibrosis stage (F0-F4). In the derivation cohort, MRE liver stiffness correlated with COL4-7S (r=0.59), ELF (r=0.58), M2BPGi (r=0.62), and FIB-4 (r=0.49; all p<0.001), with similar ordinal discrimination across F0-F4. Within the indeterminate FIB-4 range, second-step COL4-7S correctly classified 90/136 patients (66%) for MRE-defined ≥F2; ELF and M2BPGi correctly classified 90/136 (66%) and 73/136 (54%), respectively. In external validation, COL4-7S and ELF correctly classified 32/50 (64%) and 34/50 (68%) patients, respectively. These findings indicate numerically similar second-step performance of CLEIA-based COL4-7S and ELF for MRE-defined ≥F2 fibrosis in this retrospective cohort. Prospective studies are needed to determine whether COL4-7S improves workflow, cost-effectiveness, and patient outcomes in routine MASLD pathways.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58113-w.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the most common chronic liver disease worldwide, and fibrosis stage is the principal determinant of liver-related and overall outcomes1–4. Accordingly, noninvasive identification of clinically meaningful fibrosis has become a key requirement in routine care. This includes not only advanced fibrosis (≥F3), which typically prompts specialist referral and intensified surveillance5,6, but also clinically significant fibrosis (≥F2), which is increasingly relevant to treatment decisions in non-cirrhotic MASH7,8.

Current clinical pathways generally employ a stepwise noninvasive testing strategy anchored by the fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index9,10 because of its simplicity, low cost, and broad availability. However, its major limitation is the large indeterminate (“gray-zone”) range, in which first-line assessment does not provide sufficiently actionable triage and a reliable second-step test is required5,6,11. Although elastography-based modalities can address this gap, limited access and throughput continue to support the need for serum-based second-line tests12–14. The Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF) test15–17 is an established option, particularly for prioritizing referral for advanced fibrosis, but its reliance on three analytes may constrain implementation depending on laboratory infrastructure, workflow, and cost18.

At the same time, the role of second-step testing in patients with indeterminate FIB-4 is changing. As emerging care pathways and pharmacologic strategies increasingly focus on non-cirrhotic MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis, particularly F2-F3 disease7,8, second-line testing may be useful not only for referral-oriented triage of advanced fibrosis but also for identifying patients in the gray zone who are likely to have treatment-relevant fibrosis and may require confirmatory assessment or therapeutic consideration. Thus, the optimal positioning of second-line tests may depend on the clinical endpoint.

Type IV collagen 7S (COL4-7S) is a serum fibrosis marker reflecting basement membrane collagen turnover and extracellular matrix remodeling. Although historically measured by radioimmunoassay (RIA)19–24, it can now be quantified by chemiluminescent enzyme immunoassay (CLEIA) on routine automated platforms, improving suitability for high-throughput clinical practice25,26. However, evidence for CLEIA-based COL4-7S in MASLD remains limited, particularly in direct comparison with established noninvasive tests and in two-step strategies focused on the indeterminate FIB-4 population.

In this study, we compared CLEIA-measured COL4-7S with ELF, M2BPGi27–29, and FIB-4 using an MRE-defined, rather than histologic, ordinal fibrosis reference and ordinal discrimination metrics, including the distance-weighted Obuchowski measure30,31. We further assessed its performance in patients with indeterminate FIB-4 values to explore whether CLEIA-based COL4-7S could serve as a second-line test for identifying MRE-defined treatment-relevant fibrosis (≥F2, particularly F2-F3), while benchmarking its performance for advanced fibrosis against established second-line markers. No formal equivalence or non-inferiority hypothesis was prespecified; therefore, between-marker findings were interpreted descriptively.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective derivation-validation study included a two-center derivation cohort and an independent external validation cohort. For the derivation cohort, we retrospectively identified adults with MASLD at Kawasaki Medical School General Medical Center and Okayama University Hospital who underwent clinically indicated liver MRE between March 2022 and October 2025 and had serum fibrosis markers measured within 30 days of MRE. After application of the eligibility criteria, 347 patients were included in the final derivation cohort. Patient enrollment was completed before manuscript preparation and analysis finalization.

For the external validation cohort, we retrospectively identified 126 adults with MASLD at Shin-Yurigaoka General Hospital who underwent clinically indicated MRE between 2024 and 2025 and had serum fibrosis markers measured within 30 days of MRE. The same clinical definition of MASLD and the same eligibility framework were applied to both cohorts. The manuscript was prepared after completion of the study period; guidance published after patient enrollment was cited only to contextualize treatment-relevant F2-F3 disease and was not used for patient selection, staging, or statistical analysis.

MASLD was defined as imaging evidence of hepatic steatosis plus at least one cardiometabolic risk factor, after exclusion of other causes of chronic liver disease, in accordance with contemporary consensus criteria32. Key exclusion criteria included hepatitis B or C infection, autoimmune liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, and inherited or metabolic liver diseases. Alcohol intake was required to be low (<30 g/day for men and <20 g/day for women), as assessed by structured clinical interview and chart review.

Ethics

This retrospective multicenter study was conducted under centralized ethics review and approved by the Ethics Committee of Okayama University, which served as the central institutional review board for all participating institutions, including Kawasaki Medical Center and Shin-Yurigaoka General Hospital (approval No. 2508-029, and 2508-030). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Because of the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was obtained using an opt-out approach, as approved by the ethics committee.

MRE acquisition and fibrosis staging

Liver MRE was performed on 3.0-T MRI systems (Siemens) using standard clinical sequences with a vibration frequency of 60 Hz. Liver stiffness maps were reconstructed using vendor-provided software. Regions of interest were placed in the right hepatic lobe while avoiding large vessels, bile ducts, and artifact-prone areas, and mean liver stiffness was recorded in kilopascals (kPa). Because MRE was used as the reference standard, all diagnostic endpoints in this study are reported as MRE-defined fibrosis categories and should not be interpreted as histologic diagnoses.

MRE-defined fibrosis stage (F0-F4) was assigned using established MRE thresholds for MASLD derived from an individual participant data meta-analysis: <2.65 kPa (F0), 2.65-<3.14 kPa (F1), 3.14-<3.53 kPa (F2), 3.53-<4.45 kPa (F3), and ≥4.45 kPa (F4)12. These fixed thresholds were used for analytic consistency, but they may not fully capture histologic heterogeneity or biopsy-defined fibrosis stage.

Serum biomarkers and clinical variables

Serum COL4-7S was measured using a chemiluminescent enzyme immunoassay (CLEIA; Fujirebio, Tokyo, Japan)25,26. The ELF score was calculated from hyaluronic acid, procollagen III N-terminal peptide, and tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-1 measured on the Siemens platform15–17. M2BPGi was measured using a lectin-antibody immunoassay and reported as a cut-off index (Sysmex, Kobe, Japan)27,29. COL4-7S and ELF were measured using the same commercial assay systems in the derivation and external validation cohorts; M2BPGi was available for the derivation analysis but was not included in the external validation analysis. Blood sampling was performed as part of routine clinical care within the 30-day MRE window, and assays were conducted according to manufacturer-recommended procedures at the participating institutions. Preanalytical handling was based on local routine laboratory workflow rather than a centrally standardized research protocol.

The FIB-4 index was calculated as: FIB-4 = [age (years) × AST (U/L)] / [platelet count (10^9/L) × √ALT (U/L)]9,10. Demographic characteristics, comorbidities, available medication information, and routine laboratory data were extracted from electronic medical records.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as median (range) and categorical variables as number (%). Associations between MRE liver stiffness and each serum marker were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. For visualization and regression-based summaries, liver stiffness and/or biomarker values were log-transformed as appropriate to reduce skewness and improve interpretability. To characterize transportability, baseline characteristics of the derivation and external validation cohorts were summarized side by side, including the prevalence of MRE-defined ≥F2 and ≥F3 fibrosis (Supplementary Table S1).

Overall ordinal discrimination across MRE-defined fibrosis stages (F0-F4) was summarized using the distance-weighted Obuchowski measure30,31 with inverse-distance weights (w = 1/|i-j|), which integrates pairwise discrimination across all stage combinations while penalizing larger stage misclassifications more heavily than adjacent-stage errors. Stage-stratified bootstrap resampling (2,000 iterations) was used to estimate 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Because no equivalence or non-inferiority margin was prespecified, overlapping CIs were not interpreted as evidence of equivalence, and between-marker results were described as numerical differences rather than proof of comparability. Where applicable, resampling was stratified by center.

Diagnostic performance for MRE-defined clinically significant fibrosis (≥F2) and MRE-defined advanced fibrosis (≥F3) was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and AUROCs were reported. In the derivation cohort, data-driven operating points were additionally explored using the adjusted Youden index (AYI)33 as a sensitivity analysis, and corresponding sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) with 95% CIs were calculated. To reduce overinterpretation of data-derived cutoffs, these thresholds were considered cohort-specific exploratory operating points rather than universal clinical cutoffs.

For evaluation of the two-step algorithm, analyses were restricted to patients with indeterminate FIB-4 values (1.30-2.66). In the derivation cohort, second-line markers (COL4-7S, ELF, and M2BPGi) were assessed using the ≥F2 operating points identified in the sensitivity analysis. In the external validation cohort, the prespecified ≥F2 cutoffs derived in the derivation cohort were applied without re-estimation. Classification performance against MRE-defined ≥F2 was summarized using overall correct classification and related measures, and concordant and discordant classifications were visualized using quadrant plots.

All analyses were performed in R (version 4.5.2). All statistical tests were two-sided, and P values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Complete-case analysis was used; there were no missing data for the primary variables, including MRE liver stiffness and serum fibrosis markers.

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 347 adults with MASLD in whom liver fibrosis stage was noninvasively estimated by MRE were included in the derivation analysis (Supplementary Table S2). The median age was 62 years (range 20–86), and 159 (46%) were male. The median body mass index was 26.9 kg/m2 (range 18–40.3). Type 2 diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and dyslipidemia were present in 62%, 47%, and 85% of patients, respectively. Median liver enzymes were ALT 37 U/L (11–298), AST 31 U/L (11–154), and γ-GTP 39 U/L (11–538), with a median platelet count of 22 ×104/µL (8.6–28.6). Median serum fibrosis markers were COL4-7S 3.8 ng/mL (1.4–12), ELF 9.4 (7.2–13.1), M2BPGi 0.75 C.O.I. (0.24–9.1), and FIB-4 1.3 (0.2–5.9), and the median MRE liver stiffness was 2.6 kPa (1.18–12.57) (Supplementary Table S2). Using the predefined MRE thresholds (<2.65, 2.65–<3.14, 3.14–<3.53, 3.53–<4.45, and ≥4.45 kPa)12, fibrosis stage distribution was F0: 173 (49.9%), F1: 43 (12.4%), F2: 27 (7.8%), F3: 43 (12.4%), and F4: 61 (17.6%) (Supplementary Table S3). A side-by-side comparison of the derivation and external validation cohorts is provided in Supplementary Table S1. The prevalence of MRE-defined ≥F2 fibrosis was 37.8% in the derivation cohort and 35.7% in the validation cohort, and the prevalence of MRE-defined ≥F3 fibrosis was 30.0% and 30.2%, respectively.

Second-line stratification within the indeterminate FIB-4 range

We next focused on patients only in the indeterminate FIB-4 range (1.30–2.66; n=136), a clinically important bottleneck in stepwise MASLD pathways, to evaluate a pragmatic two-step strategy in which a second-line serum marker refines risk against the MRE reference (Fig. 4). Within this gray zone, MRE classified 72 patients as ≤F1 and 64 patients as ≥F2. Using the ≥F2 operating points (AYI-derived; Table 1), quadrant-based plots showed that COL4-7S and ELF yielded the same overall correct classification for MRE-defined ≥F2: COL4-7S correctly classified 90/136 patients (66%; TP=53, TN=37), and ELF likewise correctly classified 90/136 (66%; TP=52, TN=38). Collectively, these findings suggest that, among patients left “uncertain” after first-line screening, COL4-7S provided second-step enrichment for MRE-defined treatment-relevant fibrosis (≥F2) with a sensitivity-oriented profile and an overall correct classification rate numerically similar to ELF. However, the modest overall accuracy and the presence of false-positive and false-negative classifications indicate that COL4-7S should not be interpreted as a definitive diagnostic test.

External validation of second-step serum marker stratification within the indeterminate FIB-4 range

Finally, we externally evaluated COL4-7S and ELF as second-step serum markers for identifying MRE-defined clinically significant fibrosis (≥F2), a treatment-relevant stage, among patients with indeterminate FIB-4 values. An independent MASLD cohort comprising 126 patients with paired MRE and serum biomarker data was analyzed. The median age was 59 years (range, 19–83), 78 patients (61%) were male, and the median body mass index was 26.8 kg/m2 (range, 18–43.5). Median values were 3.6 ng/mL (range, 0.7–12.0) for COL4-7S, 9.5 (range, 6.9–13.1) for ELF, 1.5 (range, 0.3–6.7) for FIB-4, and 2.6 kPa (range, 1.67–12.0) for MRE (Supplementary Table S4). Compared with the derivation cohort, the external validation cohort had a higher proportion of men and lower prevalences of type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and dyslipidemia, whereas BMI, median MRE liver stiffness, and the prevalences of MRE-defined ≥F2 and ≥F3 fibrosis were broadly similar (Supplementary Table S1). Based on the predefined MRE thresholds (<2.65, 2.65–<3.14, 3.14–<3.53, 3.53–<4.45, and ≥4.45 kPa), fibrosis stages were distributed as F0 in 64 patients (50.8%), F1 in 17 (13.5%), F2 in 7 (5.6%), F3 in 17 (13.5%), and F4 in 21 (16.7%) (Supplementary Table S5).

Among these 126 patients, 50 (39.7%) had indeterminate FIB-4 values (1.30–2.66) and underwent second-step stratification (Fig. 5). Within this subgroup, MRE classified 26 patients as ≤F1 and 24 as ≥F2. Using the prespecified ≥F2 cutoffs derived in the derivation cohort (AYI-derived; Table 1), COL4-7S and ELF showed numerically similar second-step classification performance in the external validation cohort. Discussion

In this retrospective derivation-validation study of adults with MASLD staged by MRE, CLEIA-measured COL4-7S showed numerically similar overall performance to ELF, M2BPGi, and FIB-4. Notably, within the clinically important indeterminate FIB-4 range5,6, COL4-7S provided second-step classification for MRE-defined clinically significant fibrosis (≥F2) with correct classification rates close to ELF, while retaining the practical feature of being a single-analyte automated assay. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory and descriptive rather than as evidence of equivalence.

Across the full cohort, all markers correlated with MRE liver stiffness and increased in a stage-dependent manner, supporting their relevance as noninvasive indicators of fibrosis burden. At the same time, the substantial overlap observed between adjacent fibrosis stages, particularly in the lower-to-intermediate range, underscores an important limitation of serum markers and simple indices: although they reflect fibrosis severity on a continuum, they do not fully resolve early-stage disease at the individual level. This observation reinforces the rationale for stepwise clinical pathways5,6, in which first-line screening identifies patients who require second-step refinement rather than definitive staging by any single test.

To account for the ordinal structure of the MRE reference, we used the distance-weighted Obuchowski measure30,31 to summarize discrimination across F0-F4 while assigning greater penalty to wider stage misclassification. By this metric, COL4-7S, ELF, M2BPGi, and FIB-4 showed close point estimates with overlapping 95% confidence intervals. However, overlapping confidence intervals do not establish equivalence, and no formal non-inferiority or equivalence margin was prespecified. Therefore, the clinical value of these tests should be considered in relation to specific endpoints and intended use rather than in terms of a single universally superior marker.

From a clinical standpoint, the most informative analysis was that restricted to patients within the indeterminate FIB-4 range (1.30–2.66), which remains a major decision bottleneck in contemporary MASLD care pathways5,6. In the derivation cohort, COL4-7S and ELF yielded the same overall correct classification for MRE-defined ≥F2, whereas M2BPGi performed less favorably. These findings suggest that COL4-7S may be a useful second-line option for refining risk among patients who remain unclassified after first-line screening, particularly when the goal is to identify individuals likely to harbor MRE-defined treatment-relevant fibrosis and therefore warrant confirmatory assessment and therapeutic consideration.

This numerical pattern was also observed in the external validation cohort. Among patients with indeterminate FIB-4 values, second-step classification for MRE-defined ≥F2 remained close between COL4-7S and ELF, with correct classification in 64% and 68% of patients, respectively. Although the balance between false-positive and false-negative classifications differed modestly between the two markers, the external dataset supports the reproducibility of the two-step signal beyond the derivation cohort, while still requiring prospective confirmation in routine clinical pathways.

The appeal of COL4-7S is supported by practical assay features but should not be overstated. Whereas ELF is a multi-analyte composite score16,17, COL4-7S can be measured as a single analyte on routine automated CLEIA platforms25,26. In this context, the transition from historical RIA-based assays to CLEIA may facilitate integration into high-throughput laboratory workflows. However, the present study did not directly evaluate turnaround time, cost, adoption barriers, reproducibility, or workflow advantages over ELF. Therefore, COL4-7S has practical features that may facilitate implementation, but formal evaluation of feasibility, cost-effectiveness, and workflow impact is still needed.

Several limitations merit consideration. First, PPV and NPV are inherently prevalence-dependent, and both cohorts comprised patients referred for clinically indicated MRE; accordingly, operating characteristics may differ in lower-prevalence primary care settings. Second, although MRE is well validated and provides whole-liver assessment, it is not a histologic gold standard13,14,34. Outcomes in this study therefore represent MRE-defined fibrosis categories based on fixed thresholds, not biopsy-proven fibrosis stage, and these thresholds may misclassify some patients relative to histology. Third, despite external validation, the study was retrospective and the validation cohort was modest in size; AYI-derived thresholds33 should therefore be interpreted as cohort-specific exploratory operating points rather than universal cutoffs. Fourth, preanalytical handling followed routine local laboratory workflows rather than a centrally standardized research protocol, and medication-use data were not uniformly structured across centers for a reliable comparative analysis. Finally, this study did not assess treatment decisions, workflow implementation, cost, reproducibility, or prospective clinical outcomes. Prospective validation in routine stepwise pathways, ideally incorporating feasibility and health-economic considerations, will be important.

In conclusion, CLEIA-based COL4-7S demonstrated ordinal discrimination in the same numerical range as established noninvasive tests and, within the indeterminate FIB-4 gray zone, provided second-step classification for MRE-defined ≥F2 with performance close to ELF in both derivation and validation cohorts. As a single-analyte automated assay, COL4-7S may be a candidate second-line option for MASLD risk stratification, but its workflow, cost-effectiveness, and impact on treatment decisions and patient outcomes require prospective evaluation.
