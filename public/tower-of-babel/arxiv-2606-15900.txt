Square-root cancellation for sums of coefficients of GL ⁡ ( m ) \operatorname{GL}(m) -automorphic forms over values of random polynomials
Dimitrios Chatzakos, Efthymios Sofos

Abstract
. For arbitrary m ⩾ 2 m\geqslant 2 and d ⩾ 1 d\geqslant 1 , we establish square-root cancellation for sums of Fourier coefficients of GL ⁡ ( m ) \mathrm{GL}(m) -automorphic forms averaged over values of random degree- d d polynomials.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

11F30, 11N37.

1.
Introduction

The study of sums of arithmetic functions evaluated along polynomial sequences, given by

(1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n)),

where
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
and
P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
,
represents a cornerstone of analytic number theory with
significant connections to other fields. A very important case is when
g
is the normalized Fourier coefficient
\lambda_{\pi}
of an automorphic form
\pi
(which are closely related to Hecke eigenvalues). For linear polynomials
P
, the study of sums of the form (
1.1
) reduces to mean value estimates for those Fourier. This problem has been attacked using the theory of summation formulas and
L
-functions (see for instance the survey
[
16
]
for the rich history of this problem).

When
\pi
is a
\mathrm{GL}(2)
cusp form and
P
is quadratic, sums of the form (
1.1
)
were studied by Blomer
[
1
]
,
Templier
[
22
]

and Templier–Tsimerman
[
23
]
.
The work in
[
1
]

obtained the error term
O(x^{6/7+\varepsilon})

via Kuznetsov’s trace formula, while
in
[
23
]
the bound

O(x^{1/2+\theta+\varepsilon})
is proved
via sup norm bounds for Fourier coefficients;
here
\theta
is the exponent depending only on the specific progress on the
Selberg’s eigenvalue conjecture. Assuming this conjecture one can take
\theta=0
,
while
\theta\leqslant 7/64
is currently known

[
2
,
14
]
. We also refer to
[
18
]
for an extended discussion on sums arising from higher degree polynomials.

As the general problem is intractable, it is natural to examine its behaviour under generic conditions.
In the present work, we introduce alternative methods
to establish unconditionally
that for any
\mathrm{GL}(m)
cusp form and for almost all polynomials
P
of arbitrary degree, we have square-root cancellation, i.e.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(P(n))=O(x^{1/2}).

In order to do so, we order the polynomials
P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]

by the height

\displaystyle|P|:=\textrm{ maximum of absolute values of its
coefficients}.

In order to state our results, fix arbitrary
d\in\mathbb{N},m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2},\varepsilon>0
and denote

\alpha:=\min\left\{\frac{2}{2d(2m+3)+3m+4},\frac{2}{d(m^{2}-1)}\right\}.

When
m\geqslant 7
and
d\neq 1

we always have
\alpha=2/(d(m^{2}-1))
. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi
be an
automorphic irreducible cuspidal
representation of
\mathrm{GL}(m)

over
\mathbb{Q}
with unitary central character.
Then for all
x\leqslant H^{\alpha-\varepsilon}

we have

\frac{1}{\sharp\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t],\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(P(n))\right|^{2}\ll x

where the implied constant depends
only on
d,\pi
and
\varepsilon
.

This is proved in §
5

by feeding work
of Jiang–Lü–Wang
[
12
]

into Theorem
4.1
(verified in §
4
.) The condition
P(n)>0
is
imposed because otherwise
\lambda_{\pi}(P(n))
would not be defined for all
\mathrm{GL}(m)
forms;
however, the proof is
easily adapted to accommodate these cases whenever the
terms are well-defined.

Let us denote by
C_{\pi}
the limit

C_{\pi}=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}.

It is known from Rankin–Selberg theory that this constant essentially equals
the residue of the
Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})
at
s=1
,
where
\tilde{\pi}
is the
dual of
\pi
. In the
\mathrm{GL}(2)
and
\mathrm{GL}(3)

cases we show that for random polyomials
P

one has an asymptotic rather than a bound:

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\pi}(P(n))\right|\sim(C_{\pi}x)^{1/2}.

We
improve cases of Theorem
1.1

in the following manner:
Let

\beta(2)=\frac{1}{2+3d},\ \ \ \beta(3):=\frac{1}{7d+4}

and fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

\varepsilon>0
and
N>0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m
be
2
or
3
and suppose
\pi
is an
automorphic irreducible cuspidal
representation of
\mathrm{GL}(m)

over
\mathbb{Q}
with unitary central character.
Then for all

x\leqslant H^{\beta(m)-\varepsilon}

we have

\frac{1}{\sharp\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}=C_{\pi}x+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{N}}\right)

where the implied constant depends only on
d,\pi,\varepsilon
and
N
.

Remark 1.3
.

We would like to highlight the impressive
recent progress made on special
GL(2)
cases of the Rankin–Selberg problem by Huang
[
9
]
and Dasgupta–Leung–Young
[
6
]
. While these important results bring us closer, they don’t quite yield an immediate improvement for
\beta(2)
in Theorem
1.2
, for reasons we outline in Remark
5.1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2

is in §
5
.
The
\mathrm{GL}(3)
case
uses
work of Miller
[
17
]
,
who studied the additive twists

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathrm{e}^{2i\gamma n},\ \ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R},

whereas for the
\mathrm{GL}(2)
case we use
Wilton’s analogous bound (see
[
10
, Theorem 8.1]
).
This input is then fed into
Theorem
3.1
that is proved in §
3
.
It is worth noting that Theorem
3.1

is the first result in the literature giving
asymptotics
for the second moment of sums of
general

arithmetic functions
over values of random polynomials.

Remark 1.4
.

It should be noted that, in a diffenent direction, sums of the form

(1.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{\pi}(|P(n)|)|

behave quite differently as one can only hope for a logarithmic saving.
They are related to various other important questions in the theory of automorphic forms,
the QUE conjecture and Manin’s conjecture on counting rational points. We refer to
[
25
]
and references therein for a detailed discussion on the case
m=2
,
d\geqslant 1
and its applications.

Remark 1.5
.

The study of sums of the form (
1.1
) over the values
of random polynomials has seen recent developments.
Work has so far focused on
the von Mangoldt function

[
20
,
4
,
15
,
3
]
,
the Möbius and Liouville functions

[
4
,
15
,
24
]
,
the norm representation function
[
4
]
,
and the square-free indicator function
[
19
,

,

]
.
Square-root cancellation is not a priori obvious. Indeed, one has
worse
than square-root cancellation for
\Lambda

[
3
]
,
better
than square-root cancellation for
\mu^{2}

[
21
]
, and square-root cancellation for
\mu

[
24
,
25
]
.

2.
Estimates for Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms

In this section we recall all the necessary estimates for Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms that we need for the proofs of our results.

2.1.
Rankin-Selberg theory

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\hbox{GL}(m,\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. As usually, we denote by
A_{\pi}(n_{1},...,n_{m-1})
the Fourier coefficient of
\pi
and let

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pi}(n):=A_{\pi}(n,1,1,...,1)

normalized such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pi}(1)=1.

Asymptotic estimates for the second moment sums of
\lambda_{\pi}(n)
are known using the classical Rankin-Selberg theory, i.e. the analytic properties of the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})
.
In particular, for
m\geqslant 2
the classical convexity error term for the Rankin–Selberg problem in
\mathrm{GL}(m)
for general
m
is the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
and let
\pi
be an
automorphic irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}(m)
over
\mathbb{Q}
with unitary central character.
For fixed
\varepsilon>0
and all
x\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}=C_{\pi}x+O(x^{a_{m}+\varepsilon}),

with
a_{m}=(m^{2}-1)/(m^{2}+1)
, where the implied constant depends at most on
\varepsilon
and
\pi
and
C_{\pi}
equals
the residue of the
Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})
at
s=1
.

The proof of the lemma follows along the lines of
[
11
, page 26]
, for example. It uses a Perron-type argument applied to the
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\tilde{\pi})
of degree
m^{2}
. For more details on the Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms and on higher rank Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions we refer to
[
8
]
.

2.2.
Twisted linear sums

Let
\pi(z)
be a
\hbox{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
cusp form with Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{\pi}(n)
. Wilton’s result states that for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
the following bound holds uniformly on
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant X}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathrm{e}^{2i\gamma n}\ll_{\pi}X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

This result was improved by Jutila
[
13
]
to
O(X^{1/2})
. When
\pi
is a
\hbox{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, an analogous result was deduced by
[
17
]
, who proved that for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we have the following bound uniformly on
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant X}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathrm{e}^{2i\gamma n}\ll_{\pi}X^{3/4+\varepsilon}.

For the higher rank case
m\geqslant 3
only weaker results are known. When
\pi
is an automorphic irreducible cuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}(m)

over
\mathbb{Q}
with unitary central character, the best known uniform bound for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
in the literature is due to Jiang, Lü and Wang
[
12
]
who proved the bound

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leqslant X}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\gamma in}\ll_{m}\frac{X}{\log X}.

One may wish for a uniform upper bound in (
2.2
) with a power saving
O(X^{1-\delta_{m}+\varepsilon})
, for some
\delta_{m}>0
. As explained in
[
17
, Theorem 7.1]
, such a bound is equivalent to an explicit ‘Ramanujan type’-uniform upper bound for a period integral.

Nevertheless, for twisted linear sums with rational angle
\gamma=a/q\in\mathbb{Q}
, Jiang, Lü and Wang
[
12
]
proved a weaker upper bound which suffices for our purposes.

Lemma 2.2

(Jiang–Lü–Wang)
.

Suppose
\pi
is as in Lemma
2.1
.
For any
a\in\mathbb{Z},q\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\lambda_{\pi}(n)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\frac{a}{q}n}\ll(qx)^{\frac{m+1}{m+2}+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends at most on
\varepsilon
and
\pi
.

Finally, we recall
the following recent improvement in the error term of the Rankin–Selberg problem:

[
6
, Corollary 1.3]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Dasgupta–Leung–Young)
.

Let
\pi
be a fixed Hecke-Maass cusp form for

SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
For fixed
\varepsilon>0
and all
x\geqslant 1
we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}|\lambda_{\pi}(n)|^{2}=C_{\pi}x+O(x^{4/7+\varepsilon}),

where the implied constant depends at most on
\varepsilon
and
\pi
.

3.
Second moment with minor arcs

Assume that there are constants

\gamma,\delta\in[0,1),C\in\mathbb{C}

and that
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(3.1)

{\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant x}\mathrm{e}^{i\lambda n}g(n)\right|\ll x^{\gamma}}

and

(3.2)

{\sum_{n\leqslant x}|g(n)|^{2}=Cx+O(x^{\delta}).}

Fix any
d\in\mathbb{N},\gamma,\delta,\varepsilon,N>0
and denote

\eta:=\min\left\{\frac{1-\gamma}{1+d(1+\gamma)},\frac{1-\delta}{d\delta}\right\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
3.1
)-(
3.2
)
and all
x\leqslant H^{\eta-\varepsilon}
we have

\frac{1}{\sharp\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,\\
|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}=Cx+O\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{N}}\right).

The implied constant depends at most on
N,C,\gamma,\delta,\varepsilon
and
the implied constants in
(
3.1
)-(
3.2
).

The proof is based on the ideas in
[
20
, §3]
. We start by
expanding

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t],\deg(P)=d\\
|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t],P(n_{1}),P(n_{2})>0\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\overline{g(P(n_{1}))}g(P(n_{2}))

and then
use the circle method identity
to write the sum over
P
in
the right-hand side as

\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{(-\pi,\pi]^{2}}\overline{S_{\overline{g}}(\alpha_{1})S_{g}(\alpha_{2})}\prod_{j=0}^{d}D_{H}({n_{1}}^{j}\alpha_{1}+{n_{2}}^{j}\alpha_{2})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\alpha}

as in
[
20
, Lemma 3.4]
.
Here, for
any
F:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
and

t\in\mathbb{R}
we denote

D_{H}(t):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\in\mathbb{Z}\\
|c|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}^{ict}\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ S_{F}(t):=\sum_{c\leqslant(d+1)\mathscr{M}^{d}H}F(c)\mathrm{e}^{ict}

where
\mathscr{M}:=\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
.

For the non-diagonal terms
n_{1}\neq n_{2}

we apply

[
20
, Lemma 3.5]

to obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t],\deg(P)=d\\
|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\overline{g(P(n_{1}))}g(P(n_{2}))\ll\|S_{\overline{g}}\|_{\infty}S_{|g|}(0)H^{d-1}\frac{\mathscr{M}(\log H)^{2}}{|n_{2}-n_{1}|}

with an implied constant
independent of
n_{i}
and
H
.
By (
3.1
) we get

\|S_{\overline{g}}\|_{\infty}\ll(\mathscr{M}^{d}H)^{\gamma}
.
Furthermore, Cauchy’s inequality and (
3.2
)
shows that
S_{|g|}(0)\ll H\mathscr{M}^{d}
. Thus, the contribution of the non-diagonal terms towards
Theorem
3.1
is

\ll H^{d+\gamma}(\log H)^{2}\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leqslant x}\frac{\mathscr{M}^{1+d(1+\gamma)}}{|n_{2}-n_{1}|}\ll H^{d+\gamma}(\log H)^{2}x^{d(1+\gamma)}\sum_{n_{2}\leqslant x}n_{2}\sum_{n_{1}\in[1,n_{2})}\frac{1}{n_{2}-n_{1}}.

The sum over
n_{1}
in the
outmost right-hand side is

O(\log n_{2})
, hence,
the overall bound is

\ll H^{d+\gamma}(\log H)^{2}x^{d(1+\gamma)+2}(\log x).

This is
O(H^{d+1}x/(\log x)^{N})

if
x\leqslant H^{\eta-\varepsilon}
.

We have proved

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}=O\left(\frac{H^{d+1}x}{(\log x)^{N}}\right)+\sum_{n_{1}\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t],P(n_{1})>0\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}|g(P(n_{1}))|^{2}.

Write
P(n_{1})=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}{n_{1}}^{j}
with
|c_{j}|\leqslant H

and
N=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}{n_{1}}^{j}
. Letting
t=c_{0}+N

we use (
3.2
) to write
the sum over
P
in the right-hand side as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|c_{j}|\leqslant H\forall j\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
t\in[N-H,N+H]\end{subarray}}|g(t)|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|\mathbf{c}|_{\infty}\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left(2CH+O((|N|+H)^{\beta})\right)=(2H)^{d+1}C+O(H^{d}(Hx^{d})^{\delta}).

The error term is

O(H^{d+1}/(\log x)^{N})
if

x\leqslant H^{\eta-\varepsilon}
.
This concludes the proof
of Theorem
3.1
.

4.
Second moment without minor arcs

In this section we weaken the
assumptions of Theorem
3.1

and prove a weaker conclusion.
Assume that there are constants

\sigma>0
and
\tau\in[0,1)

such that the arithmetic function

g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}

satisfies the following for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\geqslant 1
:

(4.1)

{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{n\leqslant x}g(n)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\frac{a}{q}n}\right|\ll q^{\sigma}x^{\tau},}

with an implied that is independent of
q
and
x
.
Let

(4.2)

\rho:=\min\left\{\frac{1-\tau}{d(1+\tau)+\max\{\sigma,1/2\}+1/2},\frac{1-\delta}{d\delta}\right\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume

g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies
(
3.2
)
and
(
4.1
). Fix any
\varepsilon>0
and

d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for all

x\leqslant H^{\rho-\varepsilon}

we have

\frac{1}{\sharp\{P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]:\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}\ll x,

where the implied constant
depends at most on
C,d,\varepsilon,\sigma,\tau

and the implied constants in (
3.2
)
and
(
4.1
).

While Theorem 2.2 in
[
4
]
provides a
non-trivial bound, achieving the square-root cancellation bound

O(x)
seems to require some modifications in its proof.
The proof of Theorem
4.1
is based on
the use of Gauss kernels
imposed on the random coefficients of
P
,
as introduced in

[
7
, §2]
. While the weights
in §
3
enable the circle method to
function under (
4.1
) rather
than (
3.1
), they yield only upper
bounds instead of the asymptotic in Theorem
3.1
.

The Jacobi theta function is defined
for
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Im(w)>0

by

\theta(z;w):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(\pi in^{2}w+2\pi inz).

For any
H\geqslant 1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

we let
K_{H}(\alpha):=\theta(\alpha/2\pi;i/H^{2})
.
Using Poisson summation one can verify various properties of
K_{H}
, for example, that

K_{H}(\alpha)
is a non-negative real number, see
the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 2.15]
.
A straightforward calculation shows that
for any integer
c

the Fourier transform
is given by
\widehat{K}_{H}(c)=\mathrm{e}^{-\pi c^{2}/H^{2}}
. Now writing
P(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}t^{j}
we
upper bound
\mathds{1}_{[-H,H]}(c_{j})
by

O(\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j}))
for each
j
. This results in

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}\ll\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P_{\mathbf{c}}(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n))\right|^{2}

where
P_{\mathbf{c}}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}t^{j}
.
Opening up the square we obtain

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\\
P_{\mathbf{c}}(n_{1}),P_{\mathbf{c}}(n_{2})>0\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n_{1}))\overline{g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n_{2}))}.

As in
[
7
, Equation (2.20)]
we may
now see that

(4.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\\
\deg(P)=d,|P|\leqslant H\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leqslant x\\
P(n)>0\end{subarray}}g(P(n))\right|^{2}\ll\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leqslant x}\int_{(-\pi,\pi]^{2}}\overline{S(\alpha)}S(\beta)\left(\prod_{k=0}^{d}K_{H}(n_{1}^{k}\alpha-n_{2}^{k}\beta)\right)\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta,

where for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we let

S(\gamma)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant(d+1)x^{d}H\end{subarray}}g(t)\mathrm{e}^{i\gamma t}.

Note that

|S(\gamma)|\ll\sum_{t\leqslant(d+1)x^{d}H}|g(t)|\ll x^{d}H

by Cauchy’s inequality and (
3.2
).

Let
\|\gamma\|
denote the distance of
\gamma
from the closest integer multiple of
2\pi
.

Lemma 4.2

(Minor arcs for non-diagonal terms)
.

The contribution of
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
and
\alpha,\beta
satisfying

(4.4)

\|\alpha-\beta\|>\frac{\log x}{H}\ \ \ \mathrm{or}\ \ \ \|n_{1}\alpha-n_{2}\beta\|>\frac{\log x}{H}

towards the right-hand side of
(
4.3
)
is
\ll H^{d+1}/x
, where the implied constant is independent of

n_{1},n_{2},x
and
H
.

Proof.

We apply the bound

|K_{H}(\alpha)|\ll|K_{H}(0)|\ll H
for
k=2,3\ldots,d
to get

\ll x^{2}(x^{d}H)^{2}H^{d-1}\int_{\eqref{Abbassa l'orgoglio}}K_{H}(\alpha-\beta)K_{H}(n_{1}\alpha-n_{2}\beta)\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta.

By
[
7
, Lemma 2.9]
this is

\ll x^{2}(x^{d}H)^{2}H^{d-1}\int_{\max\{\|\alpha\|,\|\beta\|\}>(\log x)/H}K_{H}(\alpha)K_{H}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta.

Denote
\delta=(\log x)/H
.
Using the bound

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}K_{H}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta\ll 1
that is verified
at the end of the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 2.15]

we obtain the bound

\ll x^{2}(x^{d}H)^{2}H^{d-1}\int_{\|\alpha\|>\delta}K_{H}(\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha\ll\frac{x^{2d+2}H^{d+1}}{\delta H\mathrm{e}^{(\delta H)^{2}/(4\pi))}},

where the last bound is proved via Poisson summation
in
[
7
, Lemma 2.15]
.
With our choice for

\delta=(\log x)/H
we have

x^{2d+4}\leqslant x^{-1}\delta H\mathrm{e}^{(\delta H)^{2}/(4\pi))}
.
This means that the overall contribution is at most

\ll H^{d+1}/x
, which is admissible. ∎

\rho

Lemma 4.3

(Major arcs for non-diagonal terms)
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
and
x\leqslant H^{\zeta-\varepsilon}
,
the contribution of
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
and
\alpha,\beta
with

(4.5)

\|\alpha-\beta\|\leqslant\frac{\log x}{H}\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \|n_{1}\alpha-n_{2}\beta\|\leqslant\frac{\log x}{H}

towards the right-hand side of
(
4.3
)
is
\ll H^{d+1}x
, where the implied constant is independent of

n_{1},n_{2},x
and
H
.

Proof.

Let
q:=n_{2}-n_{1}
.
By
[
7
, Lemma 2.11]
the condition
(
4.5
) implies that

\left|\alpha-\frac{2\pi a}{q}\right|\ll\frac{(\log x)}{H}\frac{|n_{2}|}{|q|}\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ \left|\beta-\frac{2\pi b}{q}\right|\ll\frac{(\log x)}{H}\frac{|n_{1}|}{|q|}

for some integers

a,b
.
Letting

A(T)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\leqslant T\end{subarray}}g(t)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi iat/q}

we then
use partial summation to see that for
\eta:=\alpha-2\pi a/q

one has

S(\alpha)\ll|A((d+1)x^{d}H)|+|\eta|\int_{1}^{(d+1)x^{d}H}|A(t)|\mathrm{d}t.

By (
4.1
) we get
the overall bound

S(\alpha)\ll|q|^{\sigma}(x^{d}H)^{\tau}+|q|^{\sigma}|\eta|(x^{d}H)^{1+\tau}
, which is

\ll|n_{2}-n_{1}|^{\sigma}(x^{d}H)^{\tau}+|n_{2}-n_{1}|^{\sigma-1}(\log x)x^{1+d(1+\tau)}H^{\tau}=:\mathfrak{F}_{1}+\mathfrak{F}_{2},

say. Thus,
|S(\alpha)S(\beta)|\ll\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{2}+\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{2}
,
hence, the contribution towards
the right-hand side of
(
4.3
) is

\ll\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leqslant x}(\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{2}+\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{2})\int_{(-\pi,\pi]^{2}}\prod_{k=0}^{d}K_{H}(n_{1}^{k}\alpha-n_{2}^{k}\beta)\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathrm{d}\beta.

We use the trivial bound

K_{H}(\gamma)\ll K_{H}(0)\ll H
for all
k\notin\{0,1\}

and then apply
[
7
, Lemma 2.9]
to
get the overall bound

\ll\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leqslant x}(\mathfrak{F}_{1}^{2}+\mathfrak{F}_{2}^{2})H^{d-1}\ll x^{2d\tau}H^{d-1+2\tau}\mathscr{A}+(\log x)^{2}x^{2+2d(1+\tau)}H^{d-1+2\tau}\mathscr{B},

where

\mathscr{A}=\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leqslant x}|n_{2}-n_{1}|^{2\sigma}\ll x^{2+2\sigma}\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ \mathscr{B}=\sum_{n_{1}\neq n_{2}\leqslant x}|n_{2}-n_{1}|^{2\sigma-2}\ll x^{\max\{1,2\sigma\}}\log x.

If

x\leqslant H^{\zeta-\varepsilon}

then one can verify

x^{2d\tau}H^{d-1+2\tau}\mathscr{A}\ll H^{d+1}x\ \ \ \textrm{ and }\ \ \ (\log x)^{2}x^{2+2d(1+\tau)}H^{d-1+2\tau}\mathscr{B}\ll H^{d+1}x.\qed

Lemma 4.4

(Diagonal terms)
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
For all
x\leqslant H^{\rho-\varepsilon}
we have

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}\\
P_{\mathbf{c}}(n)>0\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)|g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n))|^{2}\ll H^{d+1}x,

with an implied constant that is independent of
x
and
H
.

Proof.

By (
3.2
) with
t=x

we get

|g(t)|^{2}\ll t
, hence,

|g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n))|^{2}\ll|\mathbf{c}|x^{d}.

Then for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mathbf{c}|>\lambda H\\
P_{\mathbf{c}}(n)>0\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)|g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n))|^{2}\ll x^{1+d}\sum_{|\mathbf{c}|>\lambda H}|\mathbf{c}|\prod_{j=0}^{d}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi c_{j}^{2}/H^{2}}.

With no loss of generality we can assume that
|c_{0}|=|\mathbf{c}|
, hence,
we obtain

\ll x^{1+d}\left(\sum_{c\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi c^{2}/H^{2}}\right)^{d}\sum_{m>\lambda H}m\mathrm{e}^{-\pi m^{2}/H^{2}}\ll x^{1+d}H^{d+1}\int_{\lambda H}^{\infty}t\mathrm{e}^{-\pi t^{2}/H^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\ll xH^{d+1}\frac{x^{d}H^{2}}{\mathrm{e}^{\pi\lambda^{2}}}.

This is

O(H^{d+1}x)
if

\lambda:=[2d(\log(xH))^{1/2}]
.
Thus, we are left with treating

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\mathbf{c}|\leqslant 2dH(\log(xH))^{1/2}\\
P_{\mathbf{c}}(n)>0\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)|g(P_{\mathbf{c}}(n))|^{2}.

Fix
n,c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}
, let
N=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}n^{j}
and

t=c_{0}+N
. Then the last sum equals

\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|c_{j}|\leqslant 2dH(\log(xH))^{1/2}\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t>0\\
|t-N|\leqslant\ 2dH(\log(xH))^{1/2}\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi(t-N)^{2}/H^{2}}|g(t)|^{2}.

We upper-bound the last sum over
t
by

\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant 2d(\log(xH))^{1/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi j^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}jH\leqslant|t-N|\leqslant(j+1)H\end{subarray}}|g(t)|^{2}.

The sum over
t
is over over a union of at most
4

intervals of
length
\gg H

and centre
N\ll x^{d}H(\log(xH))^{1/2}
. By (
3.2
)
we know that
for
1\leqslant y\leqslant x
one has

\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}|g(n)|^{2}=Cy+O(x^{\delta})

so that if
y>x^{\delta}
then
\sum_{x<n\leqslant x+y}|g(n)|^{2}\ll y
.
Hence, if

H\gg(x^{d}H(\log(xH))^{1/2})^{\delta}
then the sum over
t
is

\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant 2d(\log(xH))^{1/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi j^{2}}H\ll H.

The overall estimate then becomes

\ll\sum_{n\leqslant x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|c_{j}|\leqslant H\log(xH)\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\widehat{K}_{H}(c_{j})\right)H\ll xH^{d+1}

owing to the bound

\sum_{c\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{K}_{H}(c)\ll H
.
The proof concludes by noting that the required
inequality

H\gg(x^{d}H(\log(xH))^{1/2})^{\delta}
holds by the assumption

x\leqslant H^{\rho-\varepsilon}
.
∎
