Geometric classification of primes modulo a (bend) congruence
Netanel Friedenberg, Kalina Mincheva

Abstract
. In this paper we continue the program to develop the algebraic foundations of tropical (algebraic) geometry. We give strong characterizations of prime congruences containing a given congruence on a toric semiring. We give four applications of this result. (1) We prove an analogue of the strong Nullstellensatz for congruences with finite tropical basis. This extends the existing result of [ 20 ] to cases, such as the bend congruence of a tropical(ized) ideal, where the congruence is not finitely generated. (2) We show that, if I I is the ideal of an affine variety not contained in the coordinate hyperplanes, then 𝕋 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] / Bend ⁡ ( trop ⁡ I ) \mathbb{T}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I)} is cancellative. This result has applications to the integral closure (as defined in [ 35 ] ) of 𝕋 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] / Bend ⁡ ( trop ⁡ I ) \mathbb{T}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I) which we explore in a forthcoming paper [ 12 ] . (3) We show that 𝕋 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] / Bend ⁡ ( trop ⁡ I ) \mathbb{T}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I)} is the tropical function semiring on trop ⁡ V ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{trop}V(I) , which creates a bridge between the algebraic approach to non-embedded tropicalization in the work in [ 33 ] and [ 34 ] and the bend congruence approach of [ 14 ] and [ 24 ] . (4) As a consequence of one of our lemmas, we describe the closure of a polyhedron in a tropical toric variety even when the polyhedron is not compatible with the fan defining the tropical toric variety.



1.
Introduction

Tropical geometry replaces algebraic varieties (the vanishing loci of polynomial equations) with tropical varieties (tropical vanishing loci of tropical polynomials), thus allowing for the use of a broader range of (combinatorial) tools in the study of algebraic varieties. While tropical geometry has been very successful in providing a new approach to classical problems, those applications have been focused on using the combinatorics and geometry and have ignored the underlying (semiring) algebra.

In recent years, there has been a lot of effort dedicated to developing the necessary tools for commutative semiring algebra to understand the geometry of tropical varieties; see
[
14
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

,
[

[

[

[

[

. One particular approach, introduced in
[

]
, focuses on constructing an analogue of a coordinate semiring which allows us to remember more information about the original algebro-geometric objects and are able to define tropical objects intrinsically.

There are compelling results pointing to the power of semiring methods in tropical geometry.

Tropicalizations of ideals in polynomial rings, or equivalently the bend congruences of such tropicalized ideals, have been shown to preserve more information than the tropicalizations of the corresponding varieties. For example, the tropical variety retains the information of the dimension and degree, whereas the whole Hilbert polynomial is determined by the tropical ideal; see
[
14
]
,
[
23
]
, and
[
24
]
. Maclagan and the second author, in unpublished work, give concrete ways to use tropical ideals to distinguish planar quadric curves that have the same tropical variety.

A recent paper,
[
3
]
, focuses on the tropicalization of the function field of the variety over a discretely valued field and, extending a result of Baker and Rabinoff for curves, shows that this is the same as the semifield of tropical rational functions.
They also show that this semifield is finitely generated. These results lead them to new proofs of the discretely valued cases of a recent result by Ducros, Hrushovski, Loeser and Ye
[
7
]
and of the faithful tropicalization theorem by Gubler, Rabinoff and Werner
[
17
]
.

In
[
35

Tolliver provides an application to number theory – he gives a purely semiring-theoretic proof for the extension of valuations of fields.

One way to endow tropical varieties (which are not necessarily tropicalizations of algebraic varieties) with algebraic structure is introduced in
[
14
]
wherein the authors define a semiring analogue of the coordinate ring of a variety. As shown in
[
23
]
, these coordinate semirings carry the same information as that of particular ideals, which the authors call tropical ideals, that are almost always not finitely generated. More precisely, let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be a toric monoid, and let
I
be any subset of
S[M]
. The variety of
I
is denoted
V(I)
and is defined in terms of a “tropical vanishing” condition. Towards forming a corresponding coordinate semiring, we can form the
bend congruence

\operatorname{Bend}(I)
of
I
; see Definition
2.8
. The coordinate semiring is then defined to be
S[M]/\operatorname{Bend}(I)
. We can then recover the variety of
I
as
\operatorname{Hom}_{S-\mathrm{alg}}(S[M]/\operatorname{Bend}(I),\mathbb{T})\cong V(I)
.

Coming from algebraic geometry, one may wonder why these congruences are necessary - maybe we should just be quotienting by ideals, i.e., setting everything in the ideal equal to 0. If we do this, however, then affine space would only have finitely many subvarieties. Moreover, different ideals would have the same quotients.

In analogue with classical algebraic geometry one would like to more deeply study the connection between tropical varieties and their coordinate semirings.

A natural first question concerns a strong Nullstellensatz statement for congruences. At the moment one only exists for finitely generated congruences; see
[
20
]
. However, if
I
is a tropical ideal (or even the tropicalization of an ideal in a polynomial ring) then
\operatorname{Bend}(I)
is almost never finitely generated.
Similarly, at the moment there is also no strong Nullstellensatz for the corresponding tropical ideals.

One object that has recently been of interest is the semifield of functions on a tropical variety; see
[
3
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
etc. In the case where the variety is all of tropical affine space, Theorem 4.9 (iv), (v) and Theorem 4.14 (iv) in
[
20
]
show that this is the same as the semifield of fractions of
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\sqrt{\Delta}
where
\Delta
is the trivial congruence and the radical
\sqrt{C}
of a congruence
C
is the intersection of all prime congruences containing
C
. However, no explicit algebraic presentation is known for the semifield of functions on any tropical variety other than tropical toric varieties. Moreover, it isn’t known whether
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(I)}
is cancellative and so whether the total semiring of fractions of
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(I)}
is even a semifield.

In this paper we resolve both of these. In doing so, we also given an algebraic description of the congruences that are considered in
[
18
]
, where they are defined purely geometrically.

Our original motivation for this work was to be able to describe integral closure in the context of coordinate semirings. We continue this project in upcoming work.

1.1.
Results

We first introduce different versions of tropical vanishing loci. Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
and let
M
be a toric monoid corresponding to a (strictly convex rational polyhedral) cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Given
f=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in M}f_{u}\chi^{u}\in S[M]
and
(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
, we let
\widetilde{f}(r,x)=\displaystyle\max_{u\in M}\left(r\log(f_{u})+_{\mathbb{R}}\left\langle x,u\right\rangle\right)
. For any
E\subseteq(S[M])^{2}
and
I
ideal of
S[M]
we define

\begin{split}&\widetilde{V}(E)=\{w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\;:\;\widetilde{f}(w)=\widetilde{g}(w)\text{ for all }(f,g)\in E\}.\\
\end{split}

This is a refinement of the “usual” congruence variety

V(E)=\{a\in N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma):f(a)=g(a),\text{ for all }(f,g)\in E\}

that also takes into account the variety of the corresponding congruence with
\mathbb{B}
coefficients.

If
I
is the ideal of a subvariety
X
of the torus and
E=\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I)
, the set
\widetilde{V}(E)
exactly coincides with Gubler’s tropicalization
\operatorname{Trop}_{W}(X)
defined in
[
16
]
.

Our first results are Corollary
4.2
and Corollary
4.4
, which are a Nullstellensatz-type statements for congruences with finite tropical basis, i.e., congruences whose varieties can be described as a finite intersection of varieties of a single pair
(f,g)
; see Definition
3.1
for the precise definition of finite tropical basis and Definition
2.16
for tropical ideal.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
with a finite tropical basis
\mathcal{B}
. Then the finitely generated congruence
C=\left\langle\mathcal{B}\right\rangle
satisfies
\sqrt{E}=\sqrt{C}
. In particular, if
I\subseteq S[M]
is a tropical ideal then there is a finitely generated ideal
J\subseteq I
such that
\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(I)}=\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(J)}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Nullstellensatz for congruences with a finite tropical basis)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
and
M
a toric monoid. Let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
that has a finite tropical basis. Suppose
(f,g)
is such that
\widetilde{f}(w)=\widetilde{g}(w)
for all
w\in\widetilde{V}(E)
. Then there are
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
h\in S[M]
such that
\big[(f+g)^{i}+h\big](f,g)\in E
.

We next show in Corollary
5.18
that
S[M]/\sqrt{E}
, which in view of Appendix
A
can be called the function semiring of a well-behaved congruence variety, is cancellative.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be the toric monoid corresponding to the cone
\sigma
, and let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
with a finite tropical basis. Suppose that
\widetilde{V}(E)
is the closure of
\widetilde{V}(E)\cap(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
. Then
S[M]/\sqrt{E}
is cancellative.

In particular, we have the following corollary (see Remark
5.14
).

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
S
be a sub-semifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
M
be the toric monoid corresponding to the cone
\sigma
, and let
I\subseteq S[M]
be the tropicalization of an ideal in

for some valued field

. Suppose that
V(I)
is the closure of
V(I)\cap N_{\mathbb{R}}
in the tropical toric variety
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
. Then
S[M]/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(I)}
is cancellative.

We suspect that Corollary 1.4 is also true when

is a tropical ideal; see Remark

The above statements are consequences of a stronger structure result; Theorem

states that, in the situation of Theorem

has trivial-ideal kernel. Our proof also gives a recipe to produce those primes. In Appendix

shows that

and

The above results allow us to express the rational function semifields of

[

,
[

[

]

in terms of quotients of polynomial semirings. This links the non-embedded tropicalization of those works with the bend congruence approach of

Our last result concerns the closure of a polyhedron in a tropical toric variety. Until now, such results were limited to cases where the polyhedron was compatible with the fan defining the tropical toric variety; see, for example,

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
w\in N_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)\backslash N_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
\mathfrak{L}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a polyhedron such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v=w
.

2.
preliminaries

A
semiring
is a set
R
with two binary operations (addition
+
and multiplication
\cdot
) satisfying the same axioms as rings except the existence of additive inverses, as well as the axioms that
a\cdot 0=0
and
0\cdot a=0
. In this paper, a semiring is always assumed to be commutative. A semiring
(R,+,\cdot)
is a
semifield
if
(R\backslash\{0_{R}\},\cdot)
is a group. A semiring
R
is called
additively idempotent
if for all
a\in R
we have that
a+a=a
.

Key assumption:
All semirings will be assumed to be additively idempotent.

Any additively idempotent semiring comes with a partial order defined by
a\leq b\iff a+b=b
. Both addition and multiplication preserve this partial order. One consequence of this is the following well-known fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be an additively idempotent semiring, then it is zero-sum free, i.e., if
a,b\in A
with
a+b=0
, then
a=b=0
.

Proof.

If
a+b=0
, then
a+a+b=a+0
and so
a+b=a
implying that
b\leq a
and by symmetry
a\leq b
. So
a=b
and
a+b=a+a=a=0
.
∎

We will denote by
\mathbb{B}
the semifield with two elements
\{1,0\}
, where
1
is the multiplicative identity,
0
is the additive identity and
1+1=1
.
The
tropical semifield
, denoted
\mathbb{T}
, is the set
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
with the
+
operation defined to be the maximum and the
\cdot
operation defined to be the usual addition, with
-\infty=0_{\mathbb{T}}
.

In order to distinguish when we are considering a real number as being in
\mathbb{R}
or being in
\mathbb{T}
we introduce some notation. For a real number
a
, we let
t^{a}
denote the corresponding element of
\mathbb{T}
. In the same vein, given
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
\log(\mathfrak{a})
for the corresponding element of
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
. This notation is motivated as follows.
Given a non-archimedean valuation
\nu:K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
on a field and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda>1
, we get a non-archimedean absolute value
|\cdot|_{\nu}:K\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by setting
|x|_{\nu}=\lambda^{\nu(x)}
. Since
\mathbb{T}
is isomorphic to the semifield
\big([0,\infty),\max,\cdot_{\mathbb{R}}\big)
, we use a notation for the correspondence between elements of
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and elements of
\mathbb{T}
that is analogous to the notation for the correspondence between
\nu(x)
and
|x|_{\nu}
. This notation is also convenient as we get many familiar identities such as
\log(1_{\mathbb{T}})=0_{\mathbb{R}}
and
t^{a}t^{b}=t^{a+_{\mathbb{R}}b}
.

2.1.
Congruences

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a semiring and let
a\in A\backslash\{0\}
. We say that
a
is a
cancellative element
if, for all
b,c\in A
, whenever
ab=ac
then
b=c
. If all elements of
A\backslash\{0\}
are cancellative, then we say that
A
is a
cancellative semiring
.

Definition 2.3

(cf. Definition 2.3 and Proposition 2.10 in
[
20
]
)
.

A
congruence
on a semiring
A
is an equivalence relation on
A
that respects the operations of
A
. The
trivial congruence
on
A
is the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq A\times A
, for which
A/\Delta\cong A
. We call a congruence
C
on
A

cancellative
if
A/C
is a cancellative semiring. We call a proper congruence
P
of an idempotent semiring
A

prime
if
A/P
is totally ordered and cancellative

Let
C
be a congruence on a semiring
A
. The
ideal-kernel
of
C
is the set of elements
a\in A
such that
(a,0_{A})\in C
. It is easy to see that the ideal-kernel of
C
is an ideal of
A
. If the ideal-kernel of
C
is the zero ideal, then we say that
C
has
trivial ideal-kernel
.

Let
\varphi:A\to A^{\prime}
be a homomorphism of semirings. The
congruence-kernel
of
\varphi
, denoted
\ker(\varphi)
, is the pullback
\varphi^{*}(\Delta)
of the trivial congruence on
A^{\prime}
.
That is,
\ker(\varphi):=\{(f,g)\in A\times A\,:\,\varphi(f)=\varphi(g)\}
.
If
A^{\prime}
is totally ordered and cancellative, then
\ker(\varphi)
is prime. The
ideal-kernel
of
\varphi
is the set of those
a\in A
such that
\varphi(a)=0_{A^{\prime}}
. Clearly, the ideal-kernel of
\varphi
is the same as the ideal-kernel of
\ker(\varphi)
.

Notation 2.4
.

Let
R
be semiring and let
P
be a prime congruence on
R
. For two elements
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
we say that
r_{1}\leq_{P}r_{2}
(resp.
r_{1}<_{P}r_{2}
, resp.
r_{1}\equiv_{P}r_{2}
) whenever
\overline{r_{1}}\leq\overline{r_{2}}
(resp.
\overline{r_{1}}<\overline{r_{2}}
, resp.
\overline{r_{1}}=\overline{r_{2}}
) in
R/P
. Here by
\overline{r}
we mean the image of
r
in
R/P
.

Note that
P
is determined by the relation
\leq_{P}
on
R
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}
is a set of additive generators for
R
, then
P
is determined by the restriction of
\leq_{P}
to
\mathcal{G}
.

.

Let
P
be a prime congruence on a semiring
A
. The
residue semifield of
A
at
P
, denoted
\kappa(P)
, is the total semiring of fractions

This is obtained via the usual
construction as equivalence classes of pairs. For more details see Definition 2.15 in

of

Since

When
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
we describe the prime congruences on
S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
in terms of their
defining matrices
. Every prime congruence on
S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
has a defining matrix; see
[
20
]
, where it is shown that the matrix can be taken to be particularly nice.

A
k\times(n+1)
real valued matrix
\Theta
such that the first column of
\Theta
is lexicographically greater than or equal to the zero vector gives a prime congruence on

S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]

as follows:
For any monomial
m=t^{a}\chi^{u}\in S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
we let
\Phi(m)=\Theta\begin{pmatrix}a\\
u\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, where we view
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as a column vector. We call
\begin{pmatrix}a\\
u\end{pmatrix}
the
exponent vector
of the monomial
m
. For any nonzero
f\in S[\mathbb{Z}^{n}]
, write
f
as a sum of monomials
m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
and set
\Phi(f)=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\Phi(m_{i})
, where the maximum is taken with respect to the lexicographic order. Finally, set
\Phi(0)=-\infty
. We specify the prime congruence
P
by saying that
f
and
g
are equal modulo
P
if
\Phi(f)=\Phi(g)
. In this case we say that
\Theta
is a defining matrix for
P
. The first column of
\Theta
is called the column corresponding to the coefficient or the column corresponding to
S
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we say that the
(i+1)^{\text{st}}
column of
\Theta
is the column corresponding to
x_{i}
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
S[\mathbb{Z}^{2}]=\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1}]
. The matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}

represents the lexicographic order on this semiring with
x_{1}\gg x_{2}
. The corresponding prime congruence contains
(f,g)
if
f
and
g
have the same initial term with respect to this order.

\diamondsuit

In contrast, the congruences on
S[\mathbb{N}^{n}]
are allowed to have non-trivial ideal-kernel. We account for this by allowing
-\infty
as an entry of the matrix, so long as the whole column consists of
-\infty
’s as seen in the next example. Here we use the convention that
0\cdot(-\infty)=0
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
S[\mathbb{N}^{n}]=\mathbb{T}[x_{1},x_{2}]
. The congruence
C=\left<(x_{1},1),(x_{2},0)\right>

is given by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-\infty\end{pmatrix}.

When a congruence has non-trivial ideal-kernel, we note that it has the same quotient as a congruence with trivial ideal-kernel on a smaller semiring. In this example, if we let
C^{\prime}=\left<(x_{1},1)\right>
on
\mathbb{T}[x_{1}]
then
\mathbb{T}[x_{1},x_{2}]/C=\mathbb{T}[x_{1}]/C^{\prime}
. Geometrically, the rows of a matrix with infinities in the same columns are in the same stratum of a toric variety.

\diamondsuit

Definition 2.8

(adapted from Definition 5.1.1 in
[
14
]
)
.

Let
A
be an idempotent semiring, let
M
be a monoid, and let
f\in A[M]
. We let
\operatorname{supp}(f)
denote the support of
f
and, for
i
in
\operatorname{supp}(f)
, we write

for the result of deleting the

. Then the set of

is the set of pairs

\operatorname{bend}(f)=\{(f,f_{\hat{\imath}})\}_{i\in\operatorname{supp}(f)}.

For any set
I\subseteq A[M]
, the
bend congruence
of
I
, denoted
\operatorname{Bend}I
, is the congruence generated by the bend relations of all
f\in I
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
C
be a congruence. The
radical of
C
, denoted
\sqrt{C}
, is the intersection of all prime congruences containing
C
. If
C=\sqrt{C}
, we say that that
C
is a
radical
congruence.

Proposition 2.10

(Proposition 3.10
[
20
]
)
.

Let
A
be an idempotent semiring. Then every cancellative congruence on
A
is radical.

Corollary 2.11
.

For any congruence
C
of an additively idempotent semiring
A
, we have that

\sqrt{C}=\left\{(x,y)\in A\times A\,:\,\text{there are }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\text{ and }c\in A\text{ such that }\left((x+y)^{k}+c\right)(x,y)\in C\right\}

Proof.

This follows from Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 5.2 in
[
20
]
.
∎

2.2.
Polyhedral geometry and basics of tropical toric varieties

In this section we recall the definition of classical tropical toric varieties.

For a general introduction to toric varieties see
[
6
]
or
[
13
]
.
In this paper we follow conventions from
[
29
]
.
Let
\Lambda
be a finitely generated free abelian group and denote by
\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
the vector space
\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. We write
N:=\Lambda^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{Z})
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{R})\cong N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. We will use the pairing
N_{\mathbb{R}}\times\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
(v,u)\mapsto\left\langle v,u\right\rangle:=v(u)
.
A cone
\sigma
is a
strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
if
\sigma=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}v_{i}
for
v_{i}\in N
and
\sigma
contains no line. We write
\tau\leq\sigma
to mean that
\tau
is a face of
\sigma
.
We denote by
\sigma^{\vee}
the dual cone of

Lemma 2.18
.

For any
f,g\in S[M]
,
V(fg)=V(f)\cup V(g)
.

Proof.

We first show
V(f)\cup V(g)\subseteq V(fg)
. By symmetry, it suffices to show
V(f)\subseteq V(fg)
. If
f
is constant, we get
V(f)\subseteq V(fg)
by considering cases as to whether
f=0
or not. If
f
is a non-constant monomial and
w\in V(f)
then
f(w)=-\infty
, so
(fg)(w)=-\infty
, so
w\in V(fg)
.

Now suppose
f
has multiple terms. If
w\in V(f)
, then there are terms
m_{1},m_{2}
of
f
such that
f(w)=m_{1}(w)=m_{2}(w)
. Let
\mu
be any term of
g
for which
g(w)=\mu(w)
. Then
(fg)(w)=(\mu m_{1})(w)=(\mu m_{2})(w)
, with
\mu m_{1},\mu m_{2}
terms of
fg
, so
w\in V(fg)
.

For the other direction, suppose that
w\in N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
is not in
V(f)\cup V(g)
; we want to show that
w
is not in
V(fg)
. We cannot have
f=0
or
g=0
, for then
V(f)=N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
or
V(g)=N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
. If
f
(or
g
) is a nonzero constant then
V(f)=\emptyset
(resp.
V(g)=\emptyset
) and
V(fg)=V(g)
(resp.
V(fg)=V(f)
), so we are done. If
f
and
g
are both monomials then
f(w)\neq-\infty
and
g(w)\neq-\infty
so
(fg)(w)\neq-\infty
, giving us
w\notin V(fg)
.

So now suppose that
f,g
are both nonzero and that at least one of
f,g
has multiple terms; we assume without loss of generality that
f
has multiple terms. In this case
fg
has multiple terms, so we want to show that the maximum in
fg(w)
is attained only once. Since
w\notin V(f)
, there is a term
m
of
f
such that, for all other terms
m^{\prime}
of
f
,
m^{\prime}(w)<m(w)
. In particular,
m(w)\neq-\infty
. Since
w\notin V(g)
, there is a term
\mu
of
g
such that
g(w)=\mu(w)
,
\mu^{\prime}(w)<\mu(w)
for all other terms
\mu^{\prime}
of
g
(if there are any other terms), and
\mu(w)\neq-\infty
(whether or not there are any other terms).

Any term of
fg
other than

can be written as
m^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}
where either
m^{\prime}\neq m
or
\mu^{\prime}\neq\mu
. If
m^{\prime}\neq m
then
m^{\prime}(w)<m(w)
, so
m^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}(w)\leq m^{\prime}\mu(w)<m\mu(w)
. Similarly, if
\mu^{\prime}\neq\mu
then
\mu^{\prime}(w)<\mu(w)
so
m^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}(w)\leq m\mu^{\prime}(w)<m\mu(w)
. Thus any term of

evaluates to less than

. So

∎

Lemma 2.19

If

or

and

Now suppose that one of

Now suppose that both

of

∎

The idea of the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.7

Let

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{D}_{-1}\leq\mathcal{D}_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{D}_{k})

be a (

-admissible) neighborhood of

such that

Proof.

Note that the

P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}\subseteq\widetilde{V}(E)
if and only if there is a
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
with
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}\subseteq\widetilde{V}(C)
if and only if
P\supseteq C
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be a sub-semifield and let
M
be a toric monoid.
Let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
with a finite tropical basis. Then the radical of
E
is the intersection of the rank 1 prime congruences containing
E
, i.e.,

\sqrt{E}=\displaystyle\bigcap_{w\in\widetilde{V}(E)}P_{w}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a tropical basis for
E
and let
C=\left\langle\mathcal{B}\right\rangle
.
By Theorem
3.3
,
P\supseteq C
if and only if there is a flag of cones
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}
such that
P=P_{\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}\subseteq\widetilde{V}(C)
for all
j
. So for any rank-one prime
P_{w}
, we have
P_{w}\supseteq C
if and only if
w\in\widetilde{V}(C)
.
By
[
20
, Theorem 5.4]
we know that the radical of a finitely generated congruence is the intersection of the rank-one primes containing it. Thus

\bigcap_{w\in\widetilde{V}(E)}P_{w}=\bigcap_{w\in\widetilde{V}(C)}P_{w}=\sqrt{C}=\sqrt{E},

where the last equality is true by Corollary
4.2
.
∎

In
[
20
]
the authors prove a tropical Nullstellensatz for finitely generated congruences. The statement below extends that result to congruences whose variety can be cut out by finitely many pairs, such as the bend congruences of tropical ideals.

Corollary 4.4

(Gleichstellensatz for congruences with a finite tropical basis)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be a sub-semifield and let
M
be a toric monoid.
Let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
that has a finite tropical basis.
Suppose
(f,g)
is such that
\widetilde{f}(w)=\widetilde{g}(w)
for all
w\in\widetilde{V}(E)
. Then there are

i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

and
h\in S[M]
such that
\big[(f+g)^{i}+h\big](f,g)\in E
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
(f,g)\in\displaystyle\bigcap_{w\in\widetilde{V}(E)}P_{w}
, which Corollary
4.3
says is
\sqrt{E}
.
So
Corollary
2.11
tells
us that there are
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
h\in S[M]
such that
\big[(f+g)^{i}+h\big](f,g)\in E
.

∎

The following example demonstrates that in Theorem
3.3
, Corollary
4.3
,
and Corollary
4.4
the finite tropical basis hypothesis is necessary.

Example 4.5
.

Let
S=\mathbb{T}
and
M=\mathbb{N}
so
S[M]=\mathbb{T}[x]
. Let
E
be the prime congruence on
\mathbb{T}[x]
given by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
.
Then
\widetilde{V}(E)=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\{0\}
.
So, if we let
f(x)=x
and
g(x)=1
then
\widetilde{f}(w)=\widetilde{g}(w)
for all
w\in\widetilde{V}(E)
. Now suppose there are some
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
h\in\mathbb{T}[x]
such that
[(1+x)^{i}+h](1,x)\in E
. Since
E
is prime but
(1,x)\notin E
, this implies that
\left((1+x)^{i}+h,0\right)\in E
. But
E
has trivial ideal-kernel, so this is impossible.

\diamondsuit

5.
Minimal primes containing a congruence

Let
S
be a subsemifield of
\mathbb{T}
, let
\Gamma
be the corresponding additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
, and let
M
be a toric monoid.

We know from a straightforward generalization of
[
20
, Theorem 4.14]
that every prime congruence of
S[M]
contains a prime with trivial ideal-kernel. In this section we show that the same is true for prime congruences of
S[M]/E
, when
E
satisfies certain geometric conditions. In particular, it is true for the coordinate semirings of tropicalizations of varieties under very mild geometric assumptions. We start with some motivating examples of prime congruences with trivial ideal-kernel contained in a prime congruence with non-zero ideal-kernel.

Example 5.1
.

Consider

A=\mathbb{B}[x,y,z]/E
, where
E=\left<(x+x^{2}y^{2}+z^{3},x^{2}z+x^{2}+y)\right>
.
Let
P
be the prime congruence corresponding to the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}-\infty&-\infty&-\infty\end{pmatrix}
, i.e., the prime generated by
(x,0)
,
(y,0)
, and
(z,0)
. Note that
P
contains

E
.
The primes with trivial ideal-kernel that contain

E

and are contained in
P
correspond to the
following
matrices:

\displaystyle C_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&-1&-1/3\end{pmatrix},\alpha\leq-1,\quad C_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\
0&-1&-1/3\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle C_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1&\gamma\end{pmatrix},\gamma<1,\quad\quad\ \ \ C_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
-1&-1&0\end{pmatrix}

\diamondsuit

Example 5.2
.

Consider

A=\mathbb{T}[x,y,z]/E
, where
E

is the congruence generated by the bend relations of the polynomial
f=x^{2}+txy+y^{2}+x^{2}y^{2}
, i.e.,

E=\left<\operatorname{bend}(f)\right>
.
Let
P
be the prime corresponding to the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&-\infty&-\infty\end{pmatrix}
, i.e., the prime generated by
(x,0)
and
(y,0)
. Note that
P
contains

E
.

A prime with trivial ideal-kernel that contains
E
and is contained in
P
corresponds to the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&-1\\
1&0&-1\end{pmatrix}
. This can be written as
\begin{pmatrix}0&v\\
1&\widehat{w}\end{pmatrix}
where
v=(-1,-1)
is a point on the recession of
V(E)
that "points toward"
(-\infty,-\infty)
and
\widehat{w}=(0,-1)
is a point in the relative interior of a maximal cell of
V(E)
whose closure contains
(-\infty,-\infty)
. The proof of Theorem
5.3
will proceed by finding similar
v
s and
\widehat{w}
s.

Furthermore, if
k
is a field with valuation
v:k\to\mathbb{T}
and
F\in k[x,y]
is any polynomial whose tropicalization is
f=x^{2}+txy+y^{2}+x^{2}y^{2}
, then the above example works verbatim if we replace

E

with
\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}\left<F\right>)
.

\diamondsuit

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
E
be a congruence on
S[M]
with a finite tropical basis.
Suppose that
\widetilde{V}(E)
is the closure of
\widetilde{V}(E)\cap(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
.
Then every minimal prime congruence of
S[M]/E
has trivial ideal-kernel.

To prove Theorem
5.3
we consider a prime congruence
P
of
S[M]
containing
E
, then construct a prime
Q
with trivial ideal-kernel, and show that
E\subseteq Q\subseteq P
. In order to show that
(f,g)\in E
implies
(f,g)\in Q
, we consider
Q
-leading terms of
f
and
g
.
To facilitate this, we now explore initial forms of any
f\in S[M]
.

We first define
a very general type of initial form.
Let
\displaystyle f=\sum_{u\in M}t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}\in S[M]
. The support of
f
is the set
\operatorname{supp}{f}=\{u\in M:a_{u}\neq-\infty\}
.
Given
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
, we define the following initial form:

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}(f)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\\
\left<(a_{u},u),w\right>\text{ is maximized}\end{subarray}}t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}.

Note that, since we do not require
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
,
\left\langle(a_{u},u),w\right\rangle
may sometimes be
-\infty
.
Our initial form
is different from the definition that Maclagan and Rincón give in

[
24
, Definition 3.1]
; their initial form
\mathrm{in}_{w}(f)
is in
\mathbb{B}[M]
.
The relationship between the two definitions is

\mathrm{in}_{w}(f)=\pi_{\mathbb{B}}(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}(f)),
where
\pi_{\mathbb{B}}
is the map that sends all non-zero coefficients of the polynomial to
1_{\mathbb{B}}
.
We now define an initial form with respect to any prime congruence
P
on
S[M]
, which recovers the previous definition if the prime is defined by a matrix that has a single row.

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{P}(f)=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\operatorname{supp}{f}\\
|t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}|_{P}\text{ is maximized}\end{subarray}}t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}.

Note that if
P
is given by
\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
w_{1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}
then
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{P}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w_{k}}(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w_{k-1}}(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w_{0}}(f)\cdots))
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
f\in S[M]
and
v\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
. There is a finite set
\Xi\subset\Gamma\times M
and, for each
m\in\Xi
, a positive number
\mathcal{V}_{m}
such that the following holds. For any
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and any real number
\mathcal{N}
, if
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{V}_{m}>-\left(\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right)
for all
m\in\Xi
then
\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}v)=\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
and
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}v}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
.

The same is true if we allow
v,w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
so long as
v
and
w
are in the same stratum.

Proof.

Write
f=\displaystyle\sum_{u\in M}t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}
. Let
\Xi=\left\{(a_{u},u)\;:\;u\in\operatorname{supp}(f)\,\&\,u\notin\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)\right\}
. For any
m=(a_{u},u)\in\Xi
, we have
\widetilde{f}(v)>\left\langle m,v\right\rangle
because
u\in\operatorname{supp}(f)
but
u\notin\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
. We let
\mathcal{V}_{m}:=\widetilde{f}(v)-\left\langle m,v\right\rangle>0
.

Now fix
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and suppose that
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{V}_{m}>-\left(\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right)
for all
m\in\Xi
. We will show that
\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}v)=\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
and
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}v}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
by evaluating each term of
f
at
w+\mathcal{N}v
. So consider
u\in\operatorname{supp}(f)
and let
m=(a_{u},u)
.

If
u\notin\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
then
m=(a_{u},u)\in\Xi
and so

\mathcal{N}\left(\widetilde{f}(v)-\left\langle m,v\right\rangle\right)>-\left(\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right),

i.e.,
\left\langle m,w+\mathcal{N}v\right\rangle<\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
.

Say
u\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
. We first consider the case when
u\notin\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)\right)
. Then
\left\langle m,w+\mathcal{N}v\right\rangle=\left\langle m,w\right\rangle+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)<\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
. Finally, if
u\in\operatorname{supp}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)\right)
, then
\left\langle m,w+\mathcal{N}v\right\rangle=\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
.

Thus
\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}v)=\max\{\left\langle m,w+\mathcal{N}v\right\rangle\;:\;u\in\operatorname{supp}(f)\,\&\,m=(a_{u},u)\}=\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}\widetilde{f}(v)
and this maximum occurs at
m=(a_{u},u)
if and only if the term
t^{a_{u}}\chi^{u}
occurs in
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}v}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Fix
\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in S[M]
. Then there is a full-dimensional polyhedron
\mathfrak{Q}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that, if
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1},\mathcal{N}_{k})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}
then we have

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f_{i})\right)\cdots\right)\right)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}}(f_{i})

for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.
Moreover, if
0\ll\mathcal{N}_{1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{2}\ll\cdots\ll\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{k}
, then
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1},\mathcal{N}_{k})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}
.

Proof.

We will prove a slightly stronger statement: under the above hypotheses, if we define
h_{i,j}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
and
0\leq j\leq k
by
h_{i,k}=f_{i}
and

h_{i,j-1}=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{j}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f_{i})\cdots\right)

for
1\leq j\leq k
, then we also have

\widetilde{f_{i}}\big(\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}\big)=\widetilde{h_{i,0}}(\xi_{0})+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widetilde{h_{i,1}}(\xi_{1})+\mathcal{N}_{2}\widetilde{h_{i,2}}(\xi_{2})+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{h_{i,k}}(\xi_{k}).

We prove this by induction on
k
. The statement for
k=1
is immediate from Lemma
5.4
.

Now assume the statement is true for
k-1
; we want to show it is true for
k
. We first consider the case
\ell=1
which is where we have a single
f\in S[M]
. Let
g=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)
and define
h_{k-1}:=g=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)
and, for
1\leq j\leq k-1
,

h_{j-1}:=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{j}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}(g)\cdots\right)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{j}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)\right)\cdots\right).

By the inductive hypothesis there is a full-dimensional polyhedron
\mathfrak{P}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
such that, if
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{P}
then

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}(g)\cdots\right)\right)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}}(g)

and

\widetilde{g}\big(\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}\big)=\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widetilde{h_{1}}(\xi_{1})+\mathcal{N}_{2}\widetilde{h_{2}}(\xi_{2})+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\widetilde{h_{k-1}}(\xi_{k-1})

and if
0\ll\mathcal{N}_{1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{2}\ll\cdots\ll\mathcal{N}_{k-1}
then
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{P}
.

By Lemma
5.4
applied with
v=\xi_{k}
, there is a finite set
\Xi\subset\Gamma\times M
and, for each
m\in\Xi
, a positive number
\mathcal{V}_{m}
such that the following holds. For any
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
, if
\mathcal{N}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{m}>-\left(\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right)
for all
m\in\Xi
then
\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k})=\widetilde{\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)}(w)+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{f}(\xi_{k})
and
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)\right)
.
That is,
if
\mathcal{N}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{m}>-\left(\widetilde{g}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right)
for all
m\in\Xi
then
\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k})=\widetilde{g}(w)+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{f}(\xi_{k})
and
\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}}(f)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}(g)
.

Suppose
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{P}
and let
w=\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}
. If

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{m}

\displaystyle>-\left(\widetilde{g}(w)-\left\langle m,w\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\widetilde{g}\left(\xi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\xi_{j}\right)-\left\langle m,\xi_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\xi_{j}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\widetilde{h_{j}}(\xi_{j})-\left(\left\langle m,\xi_{0}\right\rangle+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\left\langle m,\xi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\left(\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})-\left\langle m,\xi_{0}\right\rangle\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\left(\widetilde{h_{j}}(\xi_{j})-\left\langle m,\xi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\right)

for all
m\in\Xi
, then we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\widetilde{f}(\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k})=\widetilde{g}(w)+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{f}(\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{g}(\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k})+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{f}(\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widetilde{h_{1}}(\xi_{1})+\mathcal{N}_{2}\widetilde{h_{2}}(\xi_{2})+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\widetilde{h_{k-1}}(\xi_{k-1})+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widetilde{f}(\xi_{k})

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}}(f)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w+\mathcal{N}_{k}\xi_{k}}(f)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{w}(g)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\xi_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\xi_{k-1}}(g)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}(g)\cdots\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k-1}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f)\right)\cdots\right)\right)

Now let

\mathfrak{Q}:=\left\{(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{l}(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})\in\mathfrak{P}\;\&\\
\mathcal{N}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{m}\geq-\left(\left(\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})-\left\langle m,\xi_{0}\right\rangle\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\left(\widetilde{h_{j}}(\xi_{j})-\left\langle m,\xi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\right)\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\text{ for all }m\in\Xi\lx@intercol\end{array}\right\}

and

\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}:=\left\{(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{l}(\mathcal{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})\text{ is in the interior of }\mathfrak{P}\;\&\\
\mathcal{N}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{m}>-\left(\left(\widetilde{h_{0}}(\xi_{0})-\left\langle m,\xi_{0}\right\rangle\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathcal{N}_{j}\left(\widetilde{h_{j}}(\xi_{j})-\left\langle m,\xi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\right)\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\text{ for all }m\in\Xi\lx@intercol\end{array}\right\}.

Note that
\mathfrak{Q}
is a polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and, if
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is nonempty then
\mathfrak{Q}
is full-dimensional and
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}
. But if
0\ll\mathcal{N}_{1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{2}\ll\cdots\ll\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{k}
, then
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{P}
and so, because the
\mathcal{V}_{m}
are all positive,
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1},\mathcal{N}_{k})
is in
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
. So
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is nonempty. This completes the proof for the case where
\ell=1
.

Now let
\ell
be arbitrary. Given
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in S[M]
let
g_{i}=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\xi_{k}}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. By the inductive hypothesis, there is one polyhedron
\mathfrak{P}
that works for
g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}
. Then, as above, we get
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}^{\circ}
that work for
f_{i}
. Now let
\mathfrak{Q}=\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{Q}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}=\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{Q}_{i}^{\circ}
.
Then
\mathfrak{Q}
is a polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and, if
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is nonempty, then
\mathfrak{Q}
is full-dimensional and
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}
. But if
0\ll\mathcal{N}_{1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{2}\ll\cdots\ll\mathcal{N}_{k-1}\ll\mathcal{N}_{k}
, then
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k-1},\mathcal{N}_{k})
is in each
\mathfrak{Q}_{i}^{\circ}
and so is in
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
. Thus
\mathfrak{Q}^{\circ}
is nonempty.
∎

Having analyzed initial forms, we now turn to the hypothesis of Theorem
5.3
and so investigate closures in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
of subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and some corresponding polyhedral geometry.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\tau\leq\sigma
and let
w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
. If
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a cone such that
w_{i}\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}(L)
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
, then there are
v,\widehat{w}_{0},\widehat{w}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{k}\in L
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v=w_{i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
. Moreover, if we let
\pi_{\tau}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
be the quotient map, then the sets
L\cap(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})^{-1}(w_{i})
and
L\cap[\{0_{\mathbb{R}}\}\times(\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau)]
are nonempty,
\widehat{w}_{i}
can be any element of
L\cap(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})^{-1}(w_{i})
, and
v
can be any element of
L\cap[\{0_{\mathbb{R}}\}\times(\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau)]
.

Proof.

Note that, by restricting our attention to the open set
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\sigma=\tau
.

For
\rho\leq\tau=\sigma
we let
\pi_{\tau,\rho}:N_{\mathbb{R}}/\rho\to N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
be the quotient map. We also write
\pi_{\tau}:=\pi_{\tau,\{0\}}:N_{\mathbb{R}}=N_{\mathbb{R}}/\{0\}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
and let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\}
be a set of generators for
\Lambda\cap\tau^{\vee}
. For each neighborhood
U
of
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
and each
m\in\mathbb{R}
we let

C(U,m):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\rho\leq\tau}\{(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}/\rho\;:\;(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau,\rho})(r,x)\in U\ \&\ \left\langle u_{j},x\right\rangle<m\text{ for }u_{j}\in\rho^{\perp}\backslash\tau^{\perp}\}.

The
C(U,m)
s form a neighborhood basis for the topology of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
at
w
; see
[
28
, Remark 3.4]
.
We let

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}(U,m)

\displaystyle:=C(U,m)\cap(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle=\{(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(r,x)\in U\ \&\ \left\langle u_{j},x\right\rangle<m\text{ for }u_{j}\notin\tau^{\perp}\}.

We now prove a series of claims.

Claim 1: For each
i=0,1,\ldots,k
there is a
\widehat{w}_{i}\in L
such that
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(\widehat{w}_{i})=w_{i}
. Otherwise, we can pick an
i
for which this fails. Since
\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau}
is linear,
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(L)
is a polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
. Thus
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(L)
is a closed set of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
and, by hypothesis,
w_{i}\notin(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(L)
. So there is a neighborhood
U
of
w_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
that does not meet
\tau
. But then, for any
m
,
C(U,m)
is a neighborhood of
w_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
that does not meet
L
, contradicting
w_{i}\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)}(L)
. This proves Claim 1.

We now let
L_{0}:=\{v\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;(0,v)\in L\}
.

Claim 2:
L_{0}^{\vee}\cap(-\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp})=\emptyset
. Towards seeing this, note that for any
u\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
we have
u\in L_{0}^{\vee}
if and only if
L_{0}\subseteq u^{\vee}
which, in turn, happens if and only if there is an
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
L\subseteq(a,u)^{\vee}
.

Now fix
u\in-\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
; we want to show that
u\notin L_{0}^{\vee}
. For any
a\in\mathbb{R}
we set
\mathcal{H}(a,u):=\{(r,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)\;:\;-ar+\left\langle-u,x\right\rangle\geq 0\},
which is a well-defined closed subset of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
because
-u
is a continuous function on
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
. Since
\mathcal{H}(a,u)\cap\big(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\big)=(a,u)^{\vee}
,
we have that
u\in L_{0}^{\vee}
if and only if there is an
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that
L\subseteq\mathcal{H}(a,u)
.

Fix any
i=0,1,\ldots,k
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
-u\in\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
and
w_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
we have
\left\langle-(a,u),w_{i}\right\rangle=-\infty\not\geq 0_{\mathbb{R}}
, so
w_{i}\notin\mathcal{H}(a,u)
. Because
w_{i}\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)}(L)
and
\mathcal{H}(a,u)
is closed in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)
, this implies that
L\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}(a,u)
. Since this is true for all
a
, we conclude that
u\notin L_{0}^{\vee}
. This completes the proof of Claim 2.

Claim 3:
L_{0}\cap\operatorname{rel.int.}(\tau)\neq\emptyset
. If not then
L_{0}\cap\tau
is contained in a proper face of
\tau
.
That is, there is some
u\in\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
such that
L_{0}\cap\tau\subseteq u^{\perp}
.
In particular
L_{0}\cap\tau\subseteq-u^{\vee}
, so
-u\in(L_{0}\cap\tau)^{\vee}=L_{0}^{\vee}+\tau^{\vee}
. Thus, there exists
\widetilde{u}\in\tau^{\vee}
such that
-u\in L_{0}^{\vee}+\widetilde{u}
, so
-(u+\widetilde{u})\in L_{0}^{\vee}
. But
u\in\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
and
\widetilde{u}\in\tau^{\vee}
, so
-(u+\widetilde{u})\in-\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
, contradicting Claim 2. This proves Claim 3.

By Claim 1 we have
\widehat{w}_{0},\widehat{w}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{k}
such that
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(\widehat{w}_{i})=w_{i}
for all
i
. By Claim 3, we can pick
v^{\prime}\in L_{0}\cap\operatorname{rel.int.}(\tau)
. Let
v=(0,v^{\prime})\in L
.

Since
v^{\prime}\in\tau
, we have
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(v)=0
, so for all
\mathcal{N}\in\mathbb{R}
,
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v)=w_{i}
. Because
v^{\prime}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}(\tau)
, for any
u\in\tau^{\vee}\backslash\tau^{\perp}
we have
\left\langle u,v^{\prime}\right\rangle<0
.
For each neighborhood
U
of
w_{i}
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
, the previous two sentences together with the definition of
\widetilde{C}(U,m)
show us that

\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v\in\widetilde{C}(U,m)
for all
\mathcal{N}\gg 0
. That is,
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v=w_{i}
.
∎

We now deduce from this a corollary for closures of polyhedra in (usual) tropical toric varieties.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
w\in N_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)\backslash N_{\mathbb{R}}
. If
\mathfrak{L}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a polyhedron such that
w\in\operatorname{cl}_{N_{\mathbb{R}}(\Sigma)}(\mathfrak{L})
, then there are
\widehat{w}\in\mathfrak{L}
and
v\in\operatorname{rec}(\mathfrak{L})
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v=w
.

Proof.

Fix any
\sigma\in\Sigma
with
w\in N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
. Then
w\in\operatorname{cl}_{N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}\mathfrak{L}
so we can work entirely in
N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
.
Let

L\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the closed cone over
\mathfrak{L}
. Applying Lemma
5.6
to
L
and
(1,w)
we get that there are
(a,\widehat{w}),(b,v)\in L
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}(a,\widehat{w})+\mathcal{N}(b,v)=(1,w)
. Thus
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v=w
and, because
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}a+\mathcal{N}b=1
, we must have
a=1
and
b=0
. Thus
\widehat{w}\in\mathfrak{L}
and
v\in\operatorname{rec}(\mathfrak{L})
.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
v,\widehat{w}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)
and suppose that
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v=w
. Then, for any
u\in\mathbb{R}\times M
,

(1)

\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=-\infty
if and only if
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<0
.

(2)

\left\langle u,w\right\rangle\neq-\infty
if and only if
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=0
. If these occur, then
\left\langle u,\widehat{w}\right\rangle=\left\langle u,w\right\rangle
.

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathbb{R}\times M
we have

\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\left<u,\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v\right>=\left<u,\widehat{w}\right>+\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\mathcal{N}\left<u,v\right>.

Since
\left\langle u,w\right\rangle
is either a finite number or
-\infty
, we must have
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=0
or
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<0
. If
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=0
then

\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=\left<u,\widehat{w}\right>+\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\mathcal{N}\left<u,v\right>=\left\langle u,\widehat{w}\right\rangle,

which is a finite number. If
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle<0
then

\left\langle u,w\right\rangle=\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\left<u,\widehat{w}+\mathcal{N}v\right>=\left<u,\widehat{w}\right>+\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\mathcal{N}\left<u,v\right>=-\infty.

∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\varphi:\mathfrak{V}\to\mathfrak{W}
be a linear map of (finite dimensional) real vector spaces. Let
\Sigma
be a fan in
\mathfrak{V}
and define a set-valued function on
\mathfrak{W}
by

\displaystyle\Phi(w)

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;w\in\varphi(\rho)\right\}.

Then
\Phi
has the following two properties.

(1)

There is a complete fan
\Pi
in
\mathfrak{W}
such that
\Phi
is constant on the relative interior of each cone in
\Pi
. In particular, the map
\Phi
is piecewise constant.

(2)

For every
w\in\mathfrak{W}
, there is a neighborhood
\mathfrak{N}
of
w
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{N}
,
\Phi(\xi)\subseteq\Phi(w)
. (This is a semicontinuity-type property
4
4

4

In fact, this property says that
\Phi
is
upper hemicontinuous

[
2
, Definition 17.2]
when the topology on
\Sigma
is, for example, the discrete topology.
.)

Proof.

Fix
\rho\in\Sigma
and pick a fan
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
that subdivides the cone
\varphi(\rho)
in
\mathfrak{W}
; if
\varphi(\rho)
is strictly convex, we can take
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
to be the fan of faces of
\varphi(\rho)
. Let
\Pi_{\rho}
be any completion of
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
(see for the existence of completions of rational fans
[
27
, page 18]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
30
]
). For any
w\in\mathfrak{W}
there is a unique
\tau\in\Pi_{\rho}
with
w\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau
and
\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\tau\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
.

Now let
\Pi
be the common refinement of the
\Pi_{\rho}
for
\rho\in\Sigma
. That is,

\Pi=\left\{\displaystyle\bigcap_{\rho\in\Sigma}\tau_{\rho}\;:\;\tau_{\rho}\in\Pi_{\rho}\text{ for all }\rho\in\Sigma\right\}.

Since each
\Pi_{\rho}
is complete,
\Pi
is complete. For each
\lambda\in\Pi
and
\rho\in\Sigma
there is a unique smallest cone of
\Pi_{\rho}
that contains
\lambda
; we call this cone
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)
. Note that
\operatorname{rel.int.}\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)
. So if
w\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\lambda
then
\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
.

Now fix
\lambda\in\Pi
and suppose
w,v\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\lambda
. Then

\displaystyle\Phi(w)

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(v)\neq\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=\Phi(v),

proving (1).

Fix
w\in\mathfrak{W}
and let
\mathfrak{N}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Pi\\
\lambda\ni w\end{subarray}}\left(\operatorname{rel.int.}\lambda\right)
be the open star of
w
in
\Pi
. Since
\Pi
is complete,
\mathfrak{N}
is open. Let
\theta
be the smallest cone of
\Pi
with
w\in\theta
, so
w\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\theta
.

Now fix
\xi\in\mathfrak{N}
; we show that
\Phi(\xi)\subseteq\Phi(w)
. So there is a
\lambda\in\Pi
with
w\in\lambda
and
\xi\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\lambda
. Since
\theta
is the smallest cone of
\Pi
containing
w
,
\theta
must be a face of
\lambda
. In particular, for any
\rho
we have that
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)\in\Pi_{\rho}
contains
\lambda
and therefore contains
\theta
. Since
\tau_{\rho}(\theta)
is the smallest cone of
\Pi_{\rho}
that contains
\theta
,
\tau_{\rho}(\theta)
must be a face of
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)
.

Now suppose
\rho\in\Phi(\xi)
; we want to show
\rho\in\Phi(w)
. By definition of
\Phi
,
\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(\xi)\neq\emptyset
, which we know is equivalent to
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
. Since
\tau_{\rho}(\theta)
is a face of
\tau_{\rho}(\lambda)
and
\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
is a fan,
\tau_{\rho}(\theta)\in\widetilde{\Pi}_{\rho}
. Equivalently,
\rho\cap\varphi^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset
, so
\rho\in\Phi(w)
.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The above proof also works when
\varphi:\mathfrak{V}\to\mathfrak{W}
is a linear map of (finite dimensional) half-spaces.

In the proof of our next lemma, it will be convenient to have the following definition.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
\varphi:X\to Y
be an affine map of real vector spaces and let
\Pi
be a polyhedral complex in
Y
. The
pullback of
\Pi
along
\varphi
is the polyhedral complex in
X
given by

\varphi^{*}(\Pi):=\{\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{Q})\;:\;\mathfrak{Q}\in\Pi\}.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
\Pi
be a polyhedral complex whose support is a convex cone
\lambda=\operatorname{supp}(\Pi)
. Suppose
w_{0},\ldots,w_{k}\in\lambda
. Then there are a full-dimensional simplex
\mathfrak{S}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
of the form

\mathfrak{S}=\{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\;:\;0\leq\delta_{1}\leq a_{1}\text{ and }0\leq\delta_{i}\leq a_{i}\delta_{i-1}\text{ for }i=2,\ldots,k\}

for some positive constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and a polyhedron
\mathfrak{P}\in\Pi
such that, for all
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\mathfrak{S}
,
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}
is contained in
\mathfrak{P}
and,
if
\delta_{k}\neq 0
,
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
.

Proof.

We prove this statement by induction on
k\geq 1
.

For the base case, let
k=1
. Let
\varphi
be the map from
\mathbb{R}
to the ambient space of
\Pi
given by
\varphi(t)=w_{0}+tw_{1}
. Since
w_{0},w_{1}\in\lambda
and
\lambda=\operatorname{supp}(\Pi)
is a cone, the support of
\varphi^{*}(\Pi)
contains
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Now consider the polyhedra of
\varphi^{*}(\Pi)
that contain
0_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since the support of
\varphi^{*}(\Pi)
contains
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, there must be one such polyhedron that also contains positive numbers; call this polyhedron
I
. Then
I
is an interval with one endpoint a positive number that we call
\alpha
. Let
a_{1}=\alpha/2
. So
[0,a_{1}]
is contained in
I
and
(0,a_{1}]
is contained in
\operatorname{rel.int.}I
.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be the smallest polyhedron of
\Pi
with
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{P})=I
and let
\mathfrak{S}=[0,a_{1}]
. Certainly, if
\delta_{1}\in\mathfrak{S}
then
w_{0}+\delta_{1}w_{1}=\varphi(\delta_{1})\in\mathfrak{P}
. If
\delta_{1}\neq 0
, then
I
is the smallest polyhedron of
\varphi^{*}(\Pi)
containing
\delta_{1}
, so any polyhedron
\mathfrak{Q}\in\Pi
that contains
\varphi(\delta_{1})
must have
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{Q})\supset I
; since
I
is full-dimensional in
\mathbb{R}
, this means
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{Q})=I
. So, because
\mathfrak{P}
is the smallest polyhedron of
\Pi
with
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{P})=I
,
\mathfrak{P}
is the smallest polyhedron of
\Pi
containing
\varphi(\delta_{1})
. In particular,
\varphi(\delta_{1})
is not in any proper face of
\mathfrak{P}
, so
w_{0}+\delta_{1}w_{1}=\varphi(\delta_{1})\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
. This completes the proof of the base case.

Now suppose the result is true for
k
and that we have
w_{0},\ldots,w_{k},w_{k+1}\in\lambda
. By the inductive hypothesis there are a full-dimensional simplex
\mathfrak{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
of the form

\mathfrak{R}=\{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\;:\;0\leq\delta_{1}\leq a_{1}\text{ and }0\leq\delta_{i}\leq a_{i}\delta_{i-1}\text{ for }i=2,\ldots,k\}

for some positive constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and a polyhedron
\mathfrak{O}\in\Pi
such that, for all
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\mathfrak{R}
,
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}
is contained in
\mathfrak{O}
and, if
\delta_{k}\neq 0
,
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{O}
. Let
b_{0}=1
and for
i=1,\ldots,k
let
b_{i}=a_{i}b_{i-1}
, i.e.,
b_{i}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}
. For
i=0,\ldots,k
let
\zeta_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}b_{j}w_{j}
. Then
\zeta_{i}\in\mathfrak{O}
for
i=0,\ldots,k
and
\zeta_{k}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{O}
.

Applying the base case with
\zeta_{k}
and
w_{k+1}
, we find that there is a positive real number
b_{k+1}
and a
\mathfrak{P}\in\Pi
such that, if
0\leq t\leq b_{k+1}
then
\zeta_{k}+tw_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{P}
and if
t\neq 0
then
\zeta_{k}+tw_{k+1}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
. We let
\zeta_{k+1}:=\zeta_{k}+b_{k+1}w_{k+1}
, so
\zeta_{k+1}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
. Now
\zeta_{k}=\zeta_{k}+0w_{k}\in\mathfrak{P}\cap\mathfrak{O}
and so, because
\mathfrak{O}
is the smallest polyhedron in
\Pi
containing
\zeta_{k}
,
\mathfrak{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}\cap\mathfrak{O}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{O}\subseteq\mathfrak{P}
. Thus
\zeta_{i}\in\mathfrak{P}
for
i=0,\ldots,k+1
.

Let
a_{k+1}:=\dfrac{b_{k+1}}{b_{k}}
and let

\mathfrak{S}=\{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}\;:\;0\leq\delta_{1}\leq a_{1}\text{ and }0\leq\delta_{i}\leq a_{i}\delta_{i-1}\text{ for }i=2,\ldots,k+1\}.

Fix
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k+1})\in\mathfrak{S}
; we will show that
v:=w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\mathfrak{P}
. Let
r_{0}:=1-\dfrac{\delta_{1}}{b_{1}}
,
r_{i}:=\dfrac{\delta_{i}}{b_{i}}-\dfrac{\delta_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, and
r_{k+1}=\dfrac{\delta_{k+1}}{b_{k+1}}
. Note that each
r_{i}
is non-negative. For
r_{k+1}
this is clear and for
r_{0}
this follows from
\dfrac{\delta_{1}}{b_{1}}=\dfrac{\delta_{1}}{a_{1}}\leq 1
. For
i=1,\ldots,k
we have
\dfrac{\delta_{i+1}}{a_{i+1}}\leq\delta_{i}
and dividing through by
b_{i}
gives
\dfrac{\delta_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}=\dfrac{\delta_{i+1}}{a_{i+1}b_{i}}\leq\dfrac{\delta_{i}}{b_{i}}
, so
r_{i}\geq 0
.

Note that
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}r_{i}=1
and, for
0<j\leq k+1
,
\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}^{k+1}r_{i}=\dfrac{\delta_{j}}{b_{j}}
. So we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}r_{i}\zeta_{i}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}\left(r_{i}\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}b_{j}w_{j}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq j\leq i\leq k+1}r_{i}b_{j}w_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}r_{i}\right)b_{0}w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=j}^{k+1}r_{i}\right)b_{j}w_{j}

\displaystyle=w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\dfrac{\delta_{j}}{b_{j}}b_{j}w_{j}

\displaystyle=w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\delta_{j}w_{j}=v.

Thus
v
is a convex combination of
\zeta_{0},\ldots,\zeta_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{P}
and, since
\mathfrak{P}
is convex,
v\in\mathfrak{P}
, as claimed.

Continuing with
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k+1})\in\mathfrak{S}
, we now suppose
\delta_{k+1}>0
; we will show that
v\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
. Consider a nonzero dual vector
u
and a real number
c
with
\mathfrak{P}\subseteq\{\xi:\left\langle u,\xi\right\rangle\leq c\}
; we will show that
v
is not contained in the proper face
\mathfrak{P}\cap\{\xi:\left\langle u,\xi\right\rangle=c\}
of
\mathfrak{P}
. Since
\zeta_{k+1}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
,
\left\langle u,\zeta_{k+1}\right\rangle<c
. So

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}r_{i}\left\langle u,\zeta_{i}\right\rangle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}r_{i}c+r_{k+1}\left\langle u,\zeta_{k+1}\right\rangle<\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k+1}r_{i}c=c.

Thus
v
is not contained in any proper face of
\mathfrak{P}
, so
v\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
X^{\circ}
be the support of a fan in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
X=\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}X^{\circ}
.
Let
\tau
be a face of
\sigma
, let
\theta
be a cone contained in
X\cap[\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)]
, and let
w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\theta
.
Then there are
v,\widehat{w}_{0},\widehat{w}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and an open set
U\subseteq(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{k+1}
such that

•

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v=w_{i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
,

•

whenever
1\gg\varepsilon_{0}\gg\varepsilon_{1}\gg\cdots\gg\varepsilon_{k}
, we have
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
, and

•

if
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
then
v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\widehat{w}_{i}\in X^{\circ}
.

Proof.

Fix a fan structure
\Sigma
on
X^{\circ}
and let
\pi_{\tau}:N_{\mathbb{R}}\to N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau
be the quotient map. We define a set-valued function
\Phi
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
with values that are subsets of
\Sigma
as follows. For any
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
,

\displaystyle\Phi(w)

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\rho\cap(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})^{-1}(w)\neq\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\rho\in\Sigma\;:\;\text{for some }\widehat{w}\in\rho,\ (\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})(\widehat{w})=w\right\}.

Lemma
5.9
and Remark
5.10
now tell us that

(1)

there is a complete fan
\Pi
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
such that
\Phi
is constant on the relative interior of each cone in
\Pi
and

(2)

for every
w\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
, there is a neighborhood
\mathfrak{N}
of
w
such that, for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{N}
,
\Phi(\xi)\subseteq\Phi(w)
.

Since
\operatorname{supp}(\Pi)=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times(N_{\mathbb{R}}/\tau)
is a convex cone, Lemma
5.12
tells us that there is a full-dimensional simplex
\mathfrak{S}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
of the form

\mathfrak{S}=\{(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\;:\;0\leq\delta_{1}\leq a_{1}\text{ and }0\leq\delta_{i}\leq a_{i}\delta_{i-1}\text{ for }i=2,\ldots,k\}

for some positive constants
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and a cone
\mathfrak{P}\in\Pi
such that, for all
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\mathfrak{S}
,
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\mathfrak{P}
and, if
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{S}
then
w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{P}
. Letting
\Omega=\left\{w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\;:\;(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{k})\in\operatorname{rel.int.}\mathfrak{S}\right\}
, we have that the value
\Phi(\omega)
is independent of
\omega\in\Omega
; we call this value
\mathfrak{t}
.

For
i=0,\ldots,k
we let
b_{i}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}
; we have
b_{0}=1
. Now for
i=0,\ldots,k
we let
\zeta_{i}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}b_{j}w_{j}
.
Note that each
\zeta_{i}
is in the closure of
\Omega
, i.e., every neighborhood of
\zeta_{i}
meets

\Omega
. So (2) tells us that
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\Phi(\zeta_{i})
.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
. Since
w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\theta
, we also have

\omega=w_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{i}w_{i}\in\theta\subseteq X=\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}(X^{\circ})=\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}(\operatorname{supp}(\Sigma))=\displaystyle\bigcup_{L\in\Sigma}\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}(L),

so we can fix an
L\in\Sigma
with
\omega\in\operatorname{cl}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)}(L)
. Lemma
5.6
now tells us that
L\in\Phi(\omega)=\mathfrak{t}
and
L\cap[\{0_{\mathbb{R}}\}\times(\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau)]\neq\emptyset
. Fix
v\in L\cap[\{0_{\mathbb{R}}\}\times(\operatorname{rel.int.}\tau)]
.

For each
i=0,\ldots,k
we have
L\in\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\Phi(\zeta_{i})
so there is a
\widehat{\zeta}_{i}\in L
with
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{i}\right)=\zeta_{i}
.
Let
\widehat{w}_{0}=\widehat{\zeta}_{0}
and, for
i=1,\ldots,k
,
\widehat{w}_{i}=\dfrac{1}{b_{i}}\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{i}-\widehat{\zeta}_{i-1}\right)
. Since
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})
is linear, we have

(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{w}_{i}\right)=\dfrac{1}{b_{i}}\left((\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{i}\right)-(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{i-1}\right)\right)=\dfrac{1}{b_{i}}\left(\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{i-1}\right)=w_{i}

for
i=1,\ldots,k
and
(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{w}_{0}\right)=(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}\times\pi_{\tau})\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{0}\right)=\zeta_{0}=w_{0}
.
Now Lemma
5.6
tells us that
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v=w_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,k
.

Let

U=\left\{(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\;:\;0<\varepsilon_{0}<1\text{ and }0<\varepsilon_{i}<a_{i}\varepsilon_{i-1}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,k\right\}.

Certainly, if
1\gg\varepsilon_{0}\gg\varepsilon_{1}\gg\cdots\gg\varepsilon_{k}
then
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
.

Say
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
. We claim that
\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}<\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i-1}}{b_{i-1}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Recall that
b_{0}=1
and
b_{i}=a_{i}b_{i-1}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. So, for
i=1
, we have
\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{b_{1}}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{a_{1}}<\varepsilon_{0}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{b_{0}}
. For
i>1

\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{a_{i}}<\varepsilon_{i-1}
so multiplying by
\dfrac{1}{b_{i-1}}
we get

\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}=\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{a_{i}b_{i-1}}<\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i-1}}{b_{i-1}}
.

Thus, the numbers
\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}-\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}
for
i=0,\ldots,k-1
and
\dfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{a_{k}}
are all positive. We let
\widehat{\zeta}_{-1}=0
so that
\widehat{w_{0}}=\dfrac{1}{b_{0}}\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{0}-\widehat{\zeta}_{-1}\right)
. Now, if
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
then

\displaystyle v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\widehat{w}_{i}

\displaystyle=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}\left(\widehat{\zeta}_{i}-\widehat{\zeta}_{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i-1}

\displaystyle=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=-1}^{k-1}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i}

\displaystyle=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i}+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{b_{k}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}-\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{b_{0}}\widehat{\zeta}_{-1}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}\widehat{\zeta}_{i}

\displaystyle=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i}}{b_{i}}-\dfrac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{b_{i+1}}\right)\widehat{\zeta}_{i}+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{b_{k}}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}-0

\displaystyle\in L\subseteq X^{\circ}

where the last line holds because
L
is a cone and
v,\widehat{\zeta}_{0},\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\zeta}_{k}\in L
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Because the prime congruences of
S[M]/E
are in bijection with the prime congruences of
S[M]
containing
E
, it suffices to show that, if
P
is a prime congruence of
S[M]
with

then there is a prime congruence
Q
on
S[M]
such that

and
Q
has trivial ideal-kernel. Let
\tau=\tau_{P}
.

Since

, Theorem
3.3
tells us that we can write

for a flag of cones
\mathcal{C}_{\bullet}=(\mathcal{C}_{-1}\leq\mathcal{C}_{0}\leq\cdots\leq\mathcal{C}_{k})

\begin{pmatrix}w_{0}\\
w_{1}\\
\vdots\\
w_{k}\end{pmatrix}
.
Applying Lemma
5.13
with
X^{\circ}=\widetilde{V}(E)\cap(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}})
,
X=\widetilde{V}(E)
and
\theta=\mathcal{C}_{k}
, we get that there are

v,\widehat{w}_{0},\widehat{w}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{w}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}
and an open set
U\subseteq(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{k+1}
such that

•

\displaystyle\lim_{\mathcal{N}\to\infty}\widehat{w}_{i}+\mathcal{N}v=w_{i}
for
i=0,1,\ldots,k
,

•

whenever
1\gg\varepsilon_{0}\gg\varepsilon_{1}\gg\cdots\gg\varepsilon_{k}
, we have
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
, and

•

if
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
then
v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\widehat{w}_{i}\in\widetilde{V}(E)
.

Let
Q
be the prime congruence on
S[M]
defined by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\widehat{w}_{0}\\
\widehat{w}_{1}\\
\cdots\\
\widehat{w}_{k}\end{pmatrix}
.
Since

,

has trivial ideal-kernel.

Fix
(f,g)\in E
; we will show that
(f,g)\in Q
. Given
(\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{k})\in U
, let
\omega=v+\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}\widehat{w}_{i}
; since
\omega\in\widetilde{V}(E)
and

, we have
\widetilde{f}(\omega)=\widetilde{g}(\omega)
.

Since
Q
is prime,
(f,g)\in Q
if and only if
(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{Q}(f),\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{Q}(g))\in Q.
Recall that

\displaystyle\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{Q}(f)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k-1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)\cdots\right)\right)

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{Q}(g)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k-1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(g)\right)\cdots\right)\right).

Applying Corollary
5.5
with
f_{1}=f
,
f_{2}=g
,
\xi_{j}=\widehat{w}_{k-j}
for
0\leq j\leq k
and
\xi_{k+1}=v
, we get that there is a full-dimensional polyhedron
\mathfrak{Q}
in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}
such that, if
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k},\mathcal{N}_{k+1})
is in the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}
then we have

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k-1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(f)\right)\cdots\right)\right)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widehat{w}_{k-1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widehat{w}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{k+1}v}(f)

and

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k-1}}\left(\cdots\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{0}}\left(\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{v}(g)\right)\cdots\right)\right)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widehat{w}_{k-1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widehat{w}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{k+1}v}(g).

Moreover, if

, then
(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{N}_{k},\mathcal{N}_{k+1})
is in the interior of

.

Note that, if

is positive and

. So if we let

and

\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\widehat{w}_{k}+\mathcal{N}_{1}\widehat{w}_{k-1}+\cdots+\mathcal{N}_{k}\widehat{w}_{0}+\mathcal{N}_{k+1}v}(g)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\varepsilon_{k}\widehat{w}_{k}+\varepsilon_{k-1}\widehat{w}_{k-1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{0}\widehat{w}_{0}+v}(g)=\widehat{\operatorname{in}}_{\omega}(g)

Also, the condition of

\widehat{w}_{0}\\

\begin{pmatrix}v\\

\widehat{w}_{1}\\

w_{1}\\
\vdots\\

[
3
]
to more general polyhedral complexes. They show that, if
\Pi\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a polyhedral complex in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the tropical function semifield
\operatorname{Rat}(|\Pi|)
can be found as follows. Let
R
be the semiring of tropical Laurent polynomial functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(by
[
20
, Proposition 5.5]
, this is
\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]/\sqrt{\Delta}
), and let
F
be the semifield of fractions of
R
. Then
\operatorname{Rat}(|\Pi|)
is the quotient of
F
determined by restricting functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
|\Pi|
. That is,
\operatorname{Rat}(|\Pi|)=F/E(|\Pi|)
where, for any
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
E(V)=\{(f,g)\in F^{2}\,:\,f(w)=g(w),\forall w\in V\}
.

J. Song proves structure results for

\operatorname{Rat}(V)

and establishes certain relations to corresponding tropical curves. She shows that, for a tropical curve
\Gamma
, the geometric structure of
\Gamma
as a tropical curve is determined by the
\mathbb{T}
-algebra structure of
\text{Rat}(\Gamma)
.

Our results in this paper allow us to relate Song’s rational function semifield to bend congruences.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
k
be a valued field, let
I\subseteq k[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be an ideal, Let
X=V(I)
,
Y=\operatorname{Trop}(X)
, and
\operatorname{Rat}(Y)
denote the tropical rational function semifield of
Y
as per
[
31
]
. Let
C=\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I)
. Then
\operatorname{Rat}(Y)\cong\operatorname{Frac}\left(\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]\big/\sqrt{C}\right)
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Rat}(\operatorname{Trop}V(I))\cong\operatorname{Frac}\left(\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]\Big/\sqrt{\operatorname{Bend}(\operatorname{trop}I)}\right).

Proof.

Let

E_{\pm}(Y):=\{(f,g)\in(\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}])^{2}\,:\,f(y)=g(y)\text{ for all }y\in Y\}
. Then, by the comment between Lemmas 3.28 and 3.29 of
[
31
]
, we have

\operatorname{Rat}(Y)=\operatorname{Frac}\left(\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]/E_{\pm}(Y)\right)
.
But by Corollary
A.13
,
E_{\pm}(Y)
, being the congruence kernel of the evaluation map
\mathbb{T}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]\to\operatorname{CPA}(Y)
, is equal to
\sqrt{C}
.
∎
