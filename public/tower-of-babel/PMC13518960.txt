HOA-OBL: hybrid opposition-based hippopotamus optimization framework for efficient UAV task allocation
Authors: G Keerthana, R Padmanaban
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are widely deployed in public and civic areas, especially in environments where human presence is restricted due to safety concerns. UAVs play a vital role in maximizing resource utilization and minimizing operational redundancy in applications such as disaster recovery, infrastructure inspection, and surveillance. However, the conventional task-allocation frameworks often suffer from suboptimal resource utilization and limited adaptability in dynamic operational environments. To resolve these problems, this paper presents a hybrid opposition based Hippopotamus Optimization (HOA-OBL) algorithm framework for efficient UAV task allocation optimization. Particularly, Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA) is one of the widely known population-based algorithms, motivated by the inherent behavior of hippopotamuses, but it has a limitation of local optima in certain iterations. To enhance the exploration, the Opposition-Based Learning (OBL) is integrated with HOA to explore the search space to prevent it from converging too early to local optima. We performed simulation experiments in dynamic environments with 20 UAVs and 100 tasks. The experimental results imply that the proposed framework achieves reductions of about 17% to 33% in energy consumption and performance enhancements of about 33% to 50% in load balancing when compared with existing models such as HOA, GA, PSO, and GWO. The results demonstrate that the proposed framework provides a more balanced and scalable approach to task assignment to UAVs, thereby improving efficiency and consistency in real-world UAV missions.Keywords: Unmanned aerial vehicles, Hippopotamus optimization, Task allocation, Opposition-based learning, Load balancingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are gaining significant focus as a groundbreaking technology for defense and civilian applications due to their flexibility, fast deployment and reduced operational risks. Many UAVs are equipped with cutting-edge sensors, communication modules and autonomous control mechanisms, which enable real-time sensing, coordination and decision making1,2. These technological advancements allow multiple UAVs to operate collaboratively and accomplish complex missions with high efficiency and reliability. As a result, UAVs are widely adopted in diverse applications, such as precision farming, environmental monitoring, infrastructure inspection, emergency response, and surveillance3,4.

Recently, next-generation smart aerial systems require multitasking capability to operate autonomously and handle complex tasks.Optimization algorithms are essential in practical UAV deployment supporting efficient scheduling, path planning, energy management and adaptive mission execution in real- world scenarios such as disaster response, surveillance, infrastructure inspection, logistics and agriculture. However, it is challenging for a single UAV to manage tasks simultaneously. Hence, UAV swarm systems have emerged as an effective solution to handle multitasking efficiently by offering several benefits such as improved fault tolerance, faster mission completion, expanded area coverage, and enhanced resilience against individual UAV failures5,6. For instance, in search-and-rescue operations, a UAV swarm can simultaneously explore multiple regions, significantly enriching the efficiency of locating victims. Consequently, in real-time tracking and surveillance scenarios, several UAVs can share the sensing workload with each other to continuously track targets without interruption.

Regardless of the benefits of the UAV swarm, there remain some operational challenges, such as task allocation, energy constraints, communication limits, collision avoidance, and safety issues. Of the various challenges to achieve the better coordination of swarms, task allocation is the crucial one. In real multi-UAV missions, the task allocation involves more than just a task-to-agent matching. It has to take into account the heterogeneity of platform capabilities, a lack of on-board energy, the unreliable communication links, the dynamic arrival of tasks, the possible failure of UAVs, the changing priorities of tasks, and environmental constraints such as no-fly zones and complex terrain. The combinatorial complexity of the assignment problem is significantly increased by the interaction of these factors, and this requires optimization strategies that are efficient, adaptive, and feasibility-aware for reliable real-world deployment.The process involves allocating tasks to the right UAVs in a mission which are distributed in space and time sensitive. The task allocation strategy affects directly the mission duration, the energy consumption, and team coordination7–9. When tasks have different priorities and UAVs have different capabilities, efficiently allocating tasks becomes more challenging. In circumstances where there exist hundreds of tasks in a big mission, it quickly becomes hard to find the best allocation. The space for assigning tasks grows exponentially as the number of UAVs and tasks grows. Beyond the computational load, UAV task allocation must include mission constraints such as battery endurance, non-uniform travel times, no-fly zones, sensor range limits, communication delays, and UAV conflicts.

Owing to these limitations, optimization-based task allocation approaches have become very popular. Many researchers from various domains utilized optimization algorithms to solve their domain problems. In particular, the ant colony optimization algorithm10, grey wolf optimization11, particle swarm optimization12 and so on have been used to solve the task allocation problem in heterogeneous aerial vehicles.In spite of these optimization approaches demonstrated promising performance, yet many of them rely on static environments and simplified assumptions. Therefore they often suffer from challenges such as premature convergence, scalability issues and lack of adequate feasibility handling in large scale NP hard UAV task assignment problems.The algorithm has problems like premature convergence, poor global exploration ability and lack of dynamic mission inputs despite acceptable results.Especially in UAV task allocation, as swarms grow in size, the discrete nature of the feasibility of assignments, the complex interactions among constraints, and the rapidly expanding search space become more intense. When assigning tasks to multiple UAVs, the search landscapes are commonly high-dimensional, nonlinear and multi-modal. Optimization algorithms need to strike a fine balance between exploration and exploitation if they are to avoid getting stuck in local optima. The necessity for greater diversity, strong global search behavior and faster convergence has led to the development of hybrid and advanced metaheuristic methods.Recent developments in metaheuristics have introduced new frameworks such as Draco Lizard Optimizer (DLO)13, which mimics gliding and adaptive environmental strategies to attain efficient global optimization, and the Cape Lynx Optimizer (CLO)14, which enhances biologically inspired mechanisms for balancing exploration and exploitation. DLO has demonstrated strong benchmark performance and competitive computational efficiency on contemporary test suites, reflecting the rapid evolution of swarm intelligence design.However, despite these advances, such optimizers remain predominantly validated on benchmark and continuous optimization problems, with limited investigation into constrained, discrete multi UAV task allocation that involves dynamic mission uncertainty, feasibility repair, and heterogeneous resource matching.

To address this requirement, the present study introduces a hybrid Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm combined with Opposition-Based Learning (HOA–OBL) for the allocation of tasks within UAV swarms. The Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA)15 is a recently introduced bio-inspired metaheuristic that mimics the cooperative and competitive social behaviors of hippopotamuses. During its exploration phase, HOA performs a global search, while in the exploitation phase, it rapidly intensifies the search around promising regions. However, like many swarm-based algorithms, it is also prone to getting stuck in local optima due to the gradual loss of population diversity over time. This restriction limits its ability to explore the best global regions in very complicated task allocation landscapes.In contrast to many recent optimizers developed for continuous benchmarks, HOA provides structured behavioral phases that balance exploration and exploitation. This framework is more appropriate for constrained assignment problems, when combined with discrete repair mechanisms.

To enhance the performance of HOA, this study integrates opposition-based learning into HOA. OBL is a well-established technique that improves the optimization efficiency by comparing a candidate solution to its opposite at the same time, thereby increasing the probability of identifying better search regions at an early stage. In addition, OBL increases the probability of initializing the population nearer to the global optimum and enriches the exploration in concurrent iterations.To enhance HOA’s search diversity and reduce stagnation at local optima, Opposition Based Learning is incorporated during both the initialization and iterative search phases. This integration enables broader coverage of the search space and sustains diversification beyond the one time improvement achieved at initialization. By integrating OBL with HOA, enriches the population diversity, accelerates convergence, and minimizes the premature stagnation in local optima. This makes the hybrid system perfect for assigning tasks to multiple UAVs. In addition, it is noteworthy to specify that various researchers have effectively utilized the OBL approach in numerous evolutionary computation frameworks to enrich their exploration phase. However, its integration with HOA remains unexamined in the realm of UAV swarm coordination. Also, many current UAV task allocation frameworks only look at optimizing one objective while overlooking other critical factors that significantly influence mission execution. These limitations motivate the development of a novel hybrid approach that integrates the most effective features of HOA with OBL that enhances search diversity and global exploration capability. The main motive of this hybrid approach is to develop a well-balanced task allocation approach that reduces energy consumption and mission completion time, thereby enriching suitability for real-world UAV swarm operations.Although the optimization algorithms have been developed remarkably, studies on UAV task allocation so far have not provided integrated approaches to address multidimensional capability assessment, dynamic mission uncertainty, constraint feasibility repair, and scalability toward swarm level deployment. To overcome these shortcomings, the HOA-OBL framework is introduced.

Related work

Traditional methods

In UAV swarm systems, traditional ways of assigning tasks mostly used deterministic mathematical models such as the Assignment Problem (AP), the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), and the Multiple Traveling Salesman Problem (MTSP). In these models, tasks are assigned by minimizing total mission cost and travel time. Exact optimization methods like branch-and-bound, Hungarian assignment, and mixed integer linear programming16 give the best answers, but they quickly become too hard to compute for real-world UAV swarm situations as the model is derived from NP-hard problems. Nam and Shell17 proposed exact allocation strategies to reduce resource contention. Centralized UAV task assignment strategies have been applied using deterministic formulations such as the vehicle routing protocol and the traveling salesman problem to order reconnaissance and logistics tasks for small UAV teams; however these methods incur substantial computational delays as the number of UAVs and tasks grows, limiting their real-time applicability18. Moreover, classical centralized solvers usually assume static mission maps and tightly synchronized communication, assumptions that are not valid in real UAV swarms where platform states, battery levels and communication links change asynchronously19. Several traditional optimization studies have focused on UAV/ARS trajectory and communication reliability in complex environments. For instance, outage probability analysis has been conducted for downlink NOMA ARS-assisted cooperative systems with trajectory optimization20 performance enhancement has been explored in NOMA multi-user networks using aerial reconfigurable intelligent surfaces and aerial relay21 and outage performance has been studied for UAV-aided V2V-NOMA communication systems over double Rayleigh channels22. These works highlight the importance of trajectory and communication optimization in UAV networks. However, they remain centered on communication outage and trajectory planning rather than heterogeneous multi-UAV task allocation, which motivates our focus on heuristic and metaheuristic frameworks for adaptive task scheduling. In cell-free multi-ARS communication systems, several traditional optimization studies have addressed advanced resource allocation challenges. Pham Thanh Hiep et.al23 power optimization and ARS selection strategies for downlink multi-ARS systems .Hiep et al24,25 , developed data power optimization algorithms for small-cell environments, and introduced a spatial pilot reassignment algorithm to improve channel estimation in cell-free multi-ARS systems. These works highlight the importance of resource allocation in complex communication infrastructures. However, their focus remains on ARS and cell-free communication optimization rather than heterogeneous UAV task allocation under dynamic mission constraints.Consequently, these limitations have motivated research toward faster, more scalable allocation strategies based on heuristic optimization techniques proficient in managing extensive and intricate mission environments.

Heuristic methods

Heuristic-based task allocation methods were introduced to provide the solutions to the problems with traditional models that make them hard to scale. These approaches provide fast, approximate solutions derived from decentralized bidding and rule-based decision frameworks. One of the most important among these is the Consensus-Based Bundle Auction (CBBA)7 . In this auction UAVs bid for tasks and share bundle information to ensure global consistency. CBBA is powerful and converges under diminishing marginal gains, but it can be very communicative and does not always give the best global allocations. Zhao et al.9 further improved decentralized coordination even more with the Performance Impact and PI-Softmax algorithm. These algorithms use exploration-driven distributed decision-making and work better in time-critical rescue situations. Other heuristic scheduling methods, like willingness-based task allocation5 along with distributed time-constrained rescheduling2, show that they can work well in practice, but they are limited since they rely on local information, can’t handle multi-objective optimization, and don’t work as well in environments with a lot of tasks. Recent heuristic and hybrid optimization studies have successfully extended UAV system design toward deployment-oriented applications. For instance, Nguyen et al.26 introduced a hybrid deep learning–heuristic framework that integrates adaptive multi-start PSO with a hybrid U-Net to optimize 3D UAV placement in multi-user systems, achieving near-optimal throughput with significantly reduced inference time. Similarly, PSO-based joint power and position optimization strategies27 have been shown to enhance fairness and throughput in multi-UAV small-cell environments. These approaches show the practical value of swarm-inspired heuristics for UAV deployment and communication optimization. However, their scope is still largely limited in the area of deployment geometry and network level performance, leaving the problem of heterogeneous multi-UAV task allocation in the presence of dynamic mission constraints under addressed.Thus, heuristic methods are fast and reliable, but they are not able to perform the global search needed for complex UAV swarm coordination.

Metaheuristic methods

Metaheuristic optimization techniques have become very popular for allocating tasks to UAVs because they can quickly search large solution spaces and deal with multiple objectives, such as the total cost of the mission, the maximum workload for the UAV, and the efficiency of the path. Genetic algorithms28,29 and particle swarm optimization-based methods30,31 have been extensively utilized for multi-UAV routing and workload distribution; however, they frequently experience premature convergence and require meticulous parameter tuning. Ant Colony Optimization variants, such as memetic ACO32 and the Multi-Objective ACO System (MOACS)33, exhibit robust routing capabilities and deliver enhanced Pareto fronts compared to NSGA-II and MOPSO in various benchmark instances. Wolf Pack Search (WPS)34 and hybrid PSO–GA models are two more bio-inspired methods that improve global search ability, but they still lose diversity and get stuck in later iterations. Geng et al.35 proposed a task allocation method in UAV clusters using a quantized particle swarm optimization (QPSO) algorithm. However, complexity can make it more challenging to implement and tune correctly for specific problems, particularly for complex multivariable optimization tasks. Duan et al.36 presents a predator–prey particle swarm optimization (PP-PSO) method that is integrated with dynamic game theory for task assignment in multi-UAV combat. The PP-PSO is used to compute the mixed Nash equilibrium at every decision step, generating a flexible plan for engaged parties in combat. Although PP-PSO improved convergence, resolving Nash equilibrium remains computationally complex for a large scale.

Lizhi Chen et al.10 proposed a multi-objective ACO (MOACO) algorithm to solve the complex task of assigning cooperative tasks to different types of UAVs. The problem is formulated as a constrained multi-objective optimization problem with three main goals: to minimize task benefit, UAV damage, and resource use. However, MOACO problem is that it tends to converge too quickly on suboptimal solutions. Gao et al.37 also suggested a grouping ACO algorithm to solve the hard combinatorial optimization problem. They also came up with a categorization model for different types of targets to improve representational fidelity.

Wu et al.38 introduced a new task allocation approach combining a double-chromosome GA with simulated annealing to reduce computation time and improve efficiency; however, it struggles in handling dynamic situations during task execution. Wang et al.39 proposed a multi-UAV task allocation problem where Improved Grey Wolf Optimization (IGWO) can solve the local optima problem well and suit large-scale complex environments. However, the algorithm suffers from increased computational complexity compared to other algorithms. Wei et al.40 employed the firefly optimization algorithm with multi-neighbor search and simulated annealing to escape local optima, but the method remains sensitive to premature convergence and lacks responsiveness in highly dynamic environments.

OBL enhanced optimization methods

Several studies have integrated OBL with PSO, GA, GWO, Firefly, harmony search algorithm, DE, and other algorithms, demonstrating the performance by improving the convergence rate, global search capability, and solution quality. For instance, Opposition-Based Learning was integrated with Particle Swarm Optimization to create the Opposition-Based Particle Swarm Optimization (OPSO) algorithm for cloud task scheduling, which improved convergence speed, load balancing, and energy efficiency compared to standard PSO, mPSO, GA, and heuristic schedulers41. The genetic algorithm was hybridized with OBL to form an opposition-based genetic algorithm with double chromosome encoding, which improves the population variety for enhancing the global exploration capability42. The firefly algorithm integrated with OBL was also employed to accelerate the convergence and improve the FA’s exploration capability and also demonstrated superior performance in small- to medium-scale dimensions, while maintaining comparatively moderate efficacy in higher-dimensional scenarios43. Similarly, the Opposition-Based Learning Parameter-Adjusting Harmony Search (OLPDHS) algorithm44was proposed to address multi-UAV cooperative task allocation in complex battlefield environments. The authors have developed a heterogeneous task assignment framework that classifies missions into surveillance, attacks, and evaluation categories, integrating considerations such as mission utility, execution cost, payload limitations, attack range, and terrain constraints. At the same time, an Improved Adaptive Differential Evolution (IADE) algorithm that used Opposition-Based Learning was introduced45 to make global search more efficient and speed up convergence in difficult optimization situations.The framework combines OBL-based population initialization with adaptive modulation of control parameters to keep the population diversity and avoid premature convergence. Moreover, Ntakolia et al.46 proposed a new hybrid algorithm called Fick’s Law Algorithm with Opposition-Based Learning (FLA-OBL). The algorithm balances exploration and exploitation in searching for better solutions. These OBL-enhanced methods demonstrate the utility of adding opposite solutions at the start and at each iteration.

Furthermore, the existing approaches do not adequately consider the real-time operational constraints related to energy availability, task urgency, environmental disturbances, and the heterogeneous capabilities of UAVs in a comprehensive optimization framework. The proposed model is aimed to overcome these problems by combining the adaptive search algorithms with the oppositional based learning. Despite its proven effectiveness in different application domains, opposition based learning has not been systematically integrated into the HOA for the UAV task allocation. This study fills this gap by adding OBL to the HOA framework to make solutions more diverse, speed up convergence and create stronger task-UAV mappings. While OBL and HOA have significant potentials, there is no research on combining OBL and HOA for UAV task allocation, which is a specific research gap that this paper aims to fill.

Proposed algorithm

The proposed model for allocating tasks to UAVs relies on the necessity for missions that consider capabilities, uses energy efficiently, and adapt to the situation. Five important key factors are considered for the UAV’s weight: sensor capability, transmission quality, wind impact factor, remaining battery life, and available payload capacity. The UAV’s ability to do surveillance, monitoring, and inspect tasks depends on performance of the sensor being able to gather important data. The quality of transmission shows the efficency of the UAV to keep a stable connection with ground control. This is important for stable data transfer while it is in use. The wind-impact factor tells the reliability of the UAV, the capability of the system to maintain flight stability under various environment conditions, and the amount of energy it uses. The UAV’s ability to complete its mission without running out of power is also affected by the left over battery power. The remaining payload capacity is also important for keeping UAVs from being overloaded, which let them carry equipment that are made for certain tasks. Five metrics are considered to figure out the task weight: task completion time, distance to task from UAVs, estimated energy consumption, task priority score and resources required. The time it takes to do a task and the distance between the UAV and the task both show how much time and space it takes to finish the task. This lets the allocation model give tasks to UAVs that can get to them and finish them with the least amount of delay. The estimated energy use makes sure that tasks are only given to UAVs that have enough energy left, which reduces the chance of failures during the mission. Task urgency, which is based on deadlines and mission-critical needs, helps determine which tasks are more important for operations. Finally, the required resources metric takes into account the specific needs of a task, including its payload, to make sure that the capabilities are right for the task. When examined together, these UAV and task metrics give a overall depiction of both the mission’s limits and the UAV’s capabilities. This helps the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm work better by adding Opposition-Based Learning , which makes it possible to do UAV-task allocations that are energy-efficient yet adaptable to the situation.

Weight coefficient determination using analytic hierarchy process (AHP)

To minimize subjective bias in assigning weighting coefficients for multidimensional UAV evaluation, the Analytic Hierarchy Process was applied to systematically derive the relative importance of each criterion. Solution representation and encoding framework for UAV task allocation

Given that the initial Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm works in a continuous search space, a framework is needed to decompose it into smaller parts in order to solve the NP-hard and combinatorial UAV task allocation problem. In this study, each candidate solution is represented as a real-valued vector , where N denotes the number of tasks, and each dimension reflects a UAV-selection preference for a specific task. A valid assignment vector is created by applying a deterministic integer mapping to each HOA position update.

Specifically, rounding and boundary clamping are used to make each task variable discrete ensuring that each task is given to one UAV index that is within the range of what is possible. A constraint-repair mechanism is also used to move any task-UAV pair that can’t be done to the nearest feasible alternative, making sure that the payload and battery limits are met. This encoding successfully shifts HOA from ongoing optimization to distinct UAV–task allocation. For binary search spaces, sigmoid or V-shaped transfer functions are usually used. Due to the fact that the task allocation involves multi-class integer assignments , an integer discretization scheme that is better suited for and more commonly used for assigning multiple UAVs.

Constraint repair mechanism

Although,the encoding function in Eq. (26) guarantees that each task is mapped to a valid UAV index. The HOA essentially works in a continuous search space while the UAV task allocation is a discrete constrained combinatorial problem. Hence, candidate solutions produced during the optimization process can still violate assignment feasibility conditions that relate to sensing compatibility, residual battery energy, payload capacity, operational range, or workload balancing constraints. Thus, a dedicated constraint repair mechanism is employed after the solution decoding and before the fitness evaluation to enforce the solution feasibility.A constraint repair mechanism is used after the solution decoding and before the fitness evaluation to ensure a valid task allocation.The repair process requires the following feasibility conditions :

Task uniqueness: a task should be assigned to exactly one UAV. When more than one UAV is assigned to the same task, the one with the highest suitability score is retained and the conflicting assignments are re-assigned to the next best feasible UAVs. Task completion: every unassigned task is assigned to the nearest feasible UAV that satisfies the capability, payload and energy requirements.

Energy feasibility: the lower priority tasks are assigned iteratively until the energy constraint is met when the total assigned task energy of a UAV exceeds its residual battery capacity.

Operational feasibility: if a mission violates payload, communication, or mission-range limitations, it is replaced with the next highest feasible candidate that is compatible with the UAV-task.

This repair strategy guarantees that all candidate solutions meet mission-critical constraints and preserves as much optimization structure as possible. The proposed approach improves the feasible solution ratio, improves the convergence stability and reduces the computational waste by correcting the infeasible assignments rather than discarding the whole candidate solutions.

Conclusion and future work

In this paper, an enhanced UAV swarm task allocation framework based on the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm with Opposition Based Learning was proposed. The framework was designed to address key challenges in multi UAV environments including heterogeneous capability matching, premature convergence and constrained task allocation. By combining HOA’s exploration-exploitation dynamics with opposition based candidate generation, a multidimensional weighted evaluation model, and feasibility aware constraint repair, the approach significantly improves allocation quality , convergence efficiency, and operational robustness compared with both classical and recent metaheuristics. Experimental results show that HOA-OBL delivers a 17.7% improvement in mission completion efficiency, reduces the overall mission span to 265 seconds, achieves a 100% task completion rate, and consistently produces the highest mean fitness value of 0.9480, while maintaining strong statistical robustness across repeated runs. Although opposition based learning and repair mechanisms introduce moderate computational overhead, the framework achieves a good balance between runtime and optimization quality, validating its scalability and appropriateness for complex UAV swarm coordination settings. The strong performance of HOA-OBL is primarily attributed to the complementary integration of its key mechanisms. Opposition based learning results in increased population diversity and reduced stagnation in local optima. The weighted evaluation framework upgrades the heterogeneity assessment of UAV-task compatibility . The constraint repair strategy enhances feasible assignment generation under realistic mission constraints. Moreover, the dynamic scenario evaluations reveal that the framework has a good reassignment capability under random task arrivals, UAV failures and constrained operational environments. These results extend its importance beyond static settings of task allocation. Despite these promising results, several limitations still exist. The present validation is simulation based with partial simplifications of communication and environmental assumptions including abstraction of sensing uncertainty, local wind estimation and limited dynamic models. It did not fully consider real-world deployment factors like decentralized communication delays, non-uniform 3D terrain, dense urban obstacles, and hardware-specific uncertainties. Furthermore, the current formulation considers weighted single-objective optimization, which may not capture broader Pareto trade-offs among energy, latency and resilience. Future work will extend HOA-OBL to fully decentralized real- time UAV swarm coordination, multi-objective Pareto optimization, and more realistic 3D mission environments including mountainous terrain, urban no-fly zones, and communication-impaired settings. Overall, the proposed HOA-OBL framework provides a strong and scalable foundation for next-generation UAV swarm task allocation and shows strong potential for wider autonomous mission planning applications.
