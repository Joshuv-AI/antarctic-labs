1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
p
,
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
d>0
,
I
an ideal of
R
. Let
F:R\to R;x\mapsto x^{p}
be Frobenius endomorphism of the ring. The nilpotent part under the Frobenius endomorphism modulo
I
is called the
Frobenius closure
of
I
, defined by
I^{F}=\{x\mid x^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for some }e\geq 0\}
, where
I^{[p^{e}]}=(x^{p^{e}}\mid x\in I)
is the
Frobenius closure
of
I
. By the Noetherianness of
R
there is an integer
e
, depending on
I
, such that
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. The
Frobenius test exponent
of
I
is defined by
\mathrm{Fte}(I)=\min\{e\mid(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}\}
. Under mild conditions,
R
is
F
-pure if and only if
\mathrm{Fte}(I)=0
for all
I
by Hochster
[
H77
]
. In general, we cannot expect a uniform bound for all
\mathrm{Fte}(I)
by Brenner
[
B06
]
. Restricting to the class of parameter ideals, for any local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
, we define the
Frobenius test exponent for parameter ideals

\mathrm{Fte}(R)
as follows

\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\min\{e\mid(\mathfrak{q}^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[p^{e}]}\,\text{for all parameter ideals }\,\mathfrak{q}\},

and
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\infty
if we have no such integer. Katzman and Sharp asked whether
\mathrm{Fte}(R)<\infty
for any (equidimensional) local ring (i.e. there exists an integer
C
such that
\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq C
).

There are several classes of rings for which
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
is finite, such as Cohen-Macaulay rings (cf.
[
KS06
]
), generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings (cf.
[
HKSY06
]
),
F
-nilpotent rings (i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
) (cf.
[
Q19
]
) and generalized weakly
F
-nilpotent rings, i.e.
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
has finite length for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
in
[
M19
]
. In general, for any local ring
R
, we have
\mathrm{Fte}(R)\geq\mathrm{HSL}(R)
in
[
HQ19
]
.

In another context, we consider the question of whether there exists a uniform bound for the Frobenius test exponent of certain classes of ideals. When
R
has positive finiteness dimension
t
, we proved that
[
HQ22
, Theorem 3.6]
there exists an integer
C
such that for any filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
of length at most
t
we have
\mathrm{Fte}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\leq C
. The number
C
is related to the invariant
\mathrm{HSL}(R)
, which is always finite and is defined by
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
for
i=1,\ldots,\mathrm{dim}(R)
(see the next section for details).

It should be noted that the Cohen-Macaulay rings have
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\mathrm{HSL}(R)
, while in generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings the second author
[
Q19
]
proved that

\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil\;+\;\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{dim}(R)}\binom{\mathrm{dim}(R)}{i}\mathrm{HSL}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)),

where
n_{0}
is the integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}\,H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
, and
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x
.
In
[
Ma15
, Theorem 1.1]
, it was proved that if
R
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring then we have
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=0
if and only if
\mathrm{HSL}(R)=0
. In this case,
R
becomes a Buchsbaum ring.
Let
R
be an excellent generalized Cohen-Macaulay ring that is
F
-injective on the punctured spectrum. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
, and
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}0^{F}_{H^{\mathrm{dim}(R)}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}=0
. We have

\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil+\mathrm{HSL}(R),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x

[
HQ23
, Corollary 3.6]
.
The condition that
R
is
F
-injective in the punctured spectrum guarantees that
0^{F}_{H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\mathrm{dim}(R)}(R)}
has finite length. We wonder whether this condition can be omitted in order to consider the problem for generalized Cohen–Macaulay rings
[
HQ23
, Question 3.7]
.

The aim of this paper is to provide a better upper bound for the Frobenius test exponent of all ideals generated by filter sequences of length at most the finiteness dimension of the ring. As a consequence, we obtain a sharp bound for
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
when
R
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.

The main results of this paper are the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p
and of positive finiteness dimension
t>0
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<t
. Then for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of
R
we have

\mathrm{Fte}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil+\mathrm{HSL}(R),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a generalized Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic
p
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
. Then

\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil+\mathrm{HSL}(R),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x
.

In the next section, we recall the basic notions and relevant materials. We prove the main results in the last section.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Standard sequences

In this subsection, the ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is not necessarily of prime characteristic.
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
is a sequence of elements of
R
.
We recall the notion of a filter regular sequence, introduced by Cuong, Schenzel, and Trung in
[
CST78
]
, which generalizes the notion of a regular sequence. The sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
is a
filter regular sequence
on
M
if for every
1\leq i\leq s
we have

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M:_{M}x_{i}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\geq 1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}.

If the sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
is a filter regular sequence on
M
then the sequence
x_{1}^{n_{1}},\ldots,x_{s}^{n_{s}}
is a filter regular sequence on
M
for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{s}\geq 1
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,
M
a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
d>0
, and
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
a parameter ideal of

. Then

(1)

is called

if there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
i<d
.

(2)

The parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
is called
standard
if for all
i+j<d
we have

\mathfrak{q}H^{j}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})M)=0.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,
M
a finitely generated
R
-module of dimension
d>0
and
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
a parameter ideal of
M
. Set
\mathfrak{q}_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})

(1)

(2)

I(M):=\sup\;\{\ell(M/\mathfrak{q}M)-e(\mathfrak{q};M)\}<\infty,

where
\mathfrak{q}
runs through all parameter ideals of

(3)

There exists a positive integer

\mathfrak{q}_{i-1}M:_{M}x_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{i-1}M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}^{n}

for every system of parameters of

.

(4)

There exists a standard system of parameters

of M. In this case, we have

\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{d})

(5)

Every system of parameters

of

(6)

is equidimensional and

are Cohen-Macaulay for all

Then the first four assertions are equivalent, and they are also equivalent to the last two provided that R is a homomorphic image of a Cohen–Macaulay local ring.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated module over a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})

The

of

For convenience, we adopt the convention that

Definition 2.4
.

Let

be a finitely generated module over a local ring

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\,H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}\!\left(\frac{M}{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})M}\right)=0

Remark 2.5

(1)

Let

provided that

(3)

If

We need also splitting results for local cohomology

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring,

R
-module of dimension

Theorem 2.7

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})

M

Corollary 2.8

Let

. Let

be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0\quad\text{for all }i<t

Take any filter regular sequence

2.2.
The limit closure

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
be a sequence in
R
. There is a useful way to describe the top local cohomology. It can be given as the direct limit
of Koszul cohomologies

H^{t}_{I}(M)\cong\varinjlim M/(x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{t}^{n})M,

with the map in the system

\varphi_{n,m}:M/(x_{1}^{n},\ldots,x_{t}^{n})M\longrightarrow M/(x_{1}^{m},\ldots,x_{t}^{m})M
is multiplication by
(x_{1}\cdots x_{t})^{m-n}
for all
m\geq n
. The kernel of the map is
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M
where

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\left((x_{1}^{n+1},\ldots,x_{t}^{n+1})M:_{M}(x_{1}\cdots x_{t})^{n}\right)

limit closure
of
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
in
M
and it does not depend on the choice of its generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of ideal
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
. It contains a lot of information of the ring.
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module iff
\mathfrak{q}^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}=\mathfrak{q}
for some (all) parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
of
M
. If
M
is unmixed, then
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay iff

\sup\{e(\mathfrak{q},M)-\ell(M/\mathfrak{q}^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M})\}<\infty

for all parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
of
M
(cf.
[
CN03
, Corollary 3.3]
). Moreover,
M
is Buchsbaum iff
\ell(\mathfrak{q}^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/\mathfrak{q}M)
is a constant for all parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
of
M
, see
[
MQ22
, Theorem 1.1]
. By
[
CHL99
, Theorem 5.1]
, if
M
is a generalized Cohen-Macaulay module, then for every system of parameters
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
of

, we have

\ell_{R}\!\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})M\right)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\binom{d}{i}\,\ell_{R}\!\left(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M)\right).

Moreover, the equality occurs if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is standard.

For a Buchsbaum module

(cf.
[
G83

\mathfrak{q}^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}=\mathfrak{q}M+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{d})M:_{M}x_{i}.

This result is true for standard parameter ideals by

If

is a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all

. For every filter regular sequence

2.3.
The Frobenius action on local cohomology

In this subsection, let
R
be a Noetherian ring containing a field of prime characteristic
p
. Let
F:R\to R,x\mapsto x^{p}
denote the Frobenius endomorphism.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
, we define

(1)

The
e
-th Frobenius power
of
I
is
I^{[p^{e}]}=(x^{p^{e}}\mid x\in I)
.

(2)

The
Frobenius closure
of
I
,
I^{F}=\{x\mid x^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for some }e\geq 0\}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
. By the Noetherianness of
R
there is an integer
e
, depending on
I
, such that
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. The smallest number
e
satisfying the condition is called the
Frobenius test exponent of
I
, and denoted by
\mathrm{Fte}(I)
,

\mathrm{Fte}(I)=\min\{e\mid(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}\}.

A problem of Katzman and Sharp
[
KS06
, Introduction]
asks in its strongest form: does there exist an integer
e
, depending only on the ring
R
, such that for every ideal
I
we have
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}
. A positive answer to
this question, together with the actual knowledge of a bound for
e
, would give an algorithm to compute the Frobenius closure
I^{F}
. Unfortunately, Brenner
[
B06
]
gave two-dimensional normal standard graded domains with no Frobenius test exponent. In contrast, Katzman and Sharp showed the existence of Frobenius test exponent if we restrict to the class of parameter ideals in a Cohen-Macaulay ring. Therefore, it is natural to ask the question whether there exists an integer
e
such that for every parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
we have
(\mathfrak{q}^{F})^{[p^{e}]}=\mathfrak{q}^{[p^{e}]}
. We define the
Frobenius test exponent for parameter ideals
of
R
,
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, the smallest integer
e
satisfying the above condition and
\mathrm{Fte}(R)=\infty
if no such integer
e
exists. It should be noted that the authors used the finiteness of
\mathrm{Fte}(R)
, if it exists, to find an upper bound of the multiplicity of a local ring
[
HQ20
]
.

For any ideal
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
, the Frobenius endomorphism
F:R\to R
and its localizations induce a natural Frobenius action on local cohomology
F:H^{i}_{I}(R)\to H^{i}_{I^{[p]}}(R)\cong H^{i}_{I}(R)
for all
i\geq 0
. In general, let
A
be an Artinian
R
-module with Frobenius action
F:A\to A
. The
Frobenius closure
0^{F}_{A}
of the zero submodule
of
A
is defined to be the submodule of
A
consisting of all elements
z
such that
F^{e}(z)=0
for some
e\geq 0
. Hence
0^{F}_{A}
is the nilpotent part of
A
under the Frobenius action. By
[
HS77
, Proposition 1.11]
,
[
L97
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
Sh07
]
we have the following.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, and
A
be an Artinian
R
-module with a Frobenius action
F:A\to A
. Then there exists a non-negative integer
e
such that
0^{F}_{A}=\operatorname{Ker}(A\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}A)
.

Definition 2.13
.

(1)

Let
A
be an Artinian
R
-module with Frobenius action
F
. The
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{HSL}(A)
and is defined as

\mathrm{HSL}(A)=\min\{e\mid 0^{F}_{A}=\operatorname{Ker}(A\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}A)\}.

(2)

Notice that
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is always Artinian for all
i\geq 0
. We define the
Hartshorne-Speiser-Lyubeznik number
of a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
as follows

\mathrm{HSL}(R):=\min\{e\mid 0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}=\operatorname{Ker}(H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\overset{F^{e}}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))\text{ for all }i=0,\ldots,d\}.

3.
Main result

We first combine the splitting results for local cohomology, Theorems 2.7, 2.8, with the theory of limit closure. The following result was proved in the Buchsbaum case using different methods, those methods cannot be extended to the generalized Cohen–Macaulay case (see
[
GMM26
, Lemma 2.6]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module of positive finiteness dimension
t>0
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(M)=0
for all
j<t
. Then for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of
M
contained in
\mathfrak{m}^{2n_{0}}
, we have

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})_{M}^{\mathrm{lim}}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)^{\binom{t}{i}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.7
,

((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}})/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\cong\big(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)^{\binom{t}{i}}\big)\,\,\oplus\,\,0:_{H^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}.

By
[
NS95
]
,
H^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)\cong H^{0}_{\mathfrak{m}}(H^{t}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})}(M))
is a submodule of
H^{t}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})}(M)
. Moreover, we have

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}\subseteq(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}.

This implies that the kernel of the map
M/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\to H^{t}_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})}(M)
is

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M

that is contained in

((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M:_{M}\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}})/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M.

The map

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\to 0:_{H^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)}\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}

is a zero map. Hence,

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})_{M}^{\mathrm{lim}}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)^{\binom{t}{i}}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.9
we have

\ell_{R}\!\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}_{M}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\binom{t}{i}\,\ell_{R}\!\left(H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(M)\right).

Combining the above observations, we obtain the following

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})_{M}^{\mathrm{lim}}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})M\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)^{\binom{t}{i}}.

∎

Applying the previous lemma to
R
, we obtain the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of positive finiteness dimension
t>0
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{j}(R)=0
for all
j<t
. Then for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of
R
contained in
\mathfrak{m}^{2n_{0}}
, we have

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)^{\binom{t}{i}}.

If we further assume that
R
has prime characteristic
p
, then the above isomorphism is compatible with the Frobenius action. Hence

((x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})^{F})/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}{0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}}^{\binom{t}{i}}.

Let
I
be an arbitrary ideal of
R
, and
e
an integer. Notice that
(I^{F})^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
, so
\mathrm{Fte}(I)\leq\mathrm{Fte}(I^{[p^{e}]})+e
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of prime characteristic
p
and of positive finiteness dimension
t>0
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<t
. Then for every filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
of
R
we have

\mathrm{Fte}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil+\mathrm{HSL}(R),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x
.

Proof.

Set
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})
.

Consider an exact sequence

0\to(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})/I\to I^{F}/I\to I^{F}/(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})\to 0.

Moreover,
I^{F}/(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})\cong(I^{\mathrm{lim}}+I^{F})/I^{\mathrm{lim}}\hookrightarrow 0^{F}_{H^{t}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}
by
[
GMM26
, Lemma 3.3]
.
If the filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2n_{0}}
, by Lemma
3.2
we have

(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})/I\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t-1}{0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)}}^{\binom{t}{i}}.

Applying
F^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}(-)
to the above equivalences, we see that
F^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}(0^{F}_{H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)})=0
for all
i\geq 0
and thus
((I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})/I)^{[p^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}]}=(I^{F}/(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F}))^{[p^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}]}=0
. In other words,

I^{[p^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}]}=(I^{\mathrm{lim}}\cap I^{F})^{[p^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}]}=(I^{F})^{[p^{\mathrm{HSL}(R)}]}.

Hence,
\mathrm{Fte}(I)\leq\mathrm{HSL}(R)
for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}\in\mathfrak{m}^{2n_{0}}
.
Taking any filter regular sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
, set
e_{0}=\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil
, then
I^{[p^{e_{0}}]}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2n_{0}}
and thus
\mathrm{Fte}(I)\leq\mathrm{Fte}(I^{[p^{e_{0}}]})+e_{0}
.
Combining all the above observations, we complete the proof.
∎

Applying Theorem
3.3
to generalized Cohen–Macaulay local rings, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a generalized Cohen-Macaulay local ring of prime characteristic
p
. Let
n_{0}
be a positive integer such that
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
i<\mathrm{dim}(R)
. Then we have

\mathrm{Fte}(R)\leq\lceil\log_{p}(2n_{0})\rceil+\mathrm{HSL}(R),

where
\lceil x\rceil
is the smallest integer that is greater than or equal to
x
.

Together with the inequality
\mathrm{HSL}(R)\leq\mathrm{Fte}(R)
proved in
[
HQ19
]
, our result shows that the obtained upper bound is essentially sharp.
