An exponentially small gap of the Perron vector on independent sets
Hongzhang Chen, Jianxi Li, Yongtao Li, Lele Liu, Bo Ning

Abstract
A classical result of Cioabă states that if G G is a connected graph with the unit Perron vector 𝒙 \bm{x} , then any independent set S S of G G satisfies ∑ v ∈ S x v 2 ⩽ 1 2 \sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2} , with equality if and only if G G is a bipartite graph and S S is one of the partite sets. Let χ ⁡ ( G ) = k \chi(G)=k be the chromatic number of G G . A well-known conjecture of Gregory asserts that any independent set S S of G G satisfies 1 2 − ∑ v ∈ S x v 2 = Ω ⁡ ( ( k / n ) 1 / 2 ) \frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=\Omega((k/n)^{1/2}) . Recently, Liu and Ning [ J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 176 (2026) ] disproved Gregory’s conjecture by constructing a graph G G and an independent set S S such that 1 2 − ∑ v ∈ S x v 2 = O ⁡ ( k 5 / n 3 ) \frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=O(k^{5}/n^{3}) . Furthermore, they conjectured that this bound is tight up to a constant factor. In this paper, we first show that any cycle C n C_{n} with odd integer n ⩾ 7 n\geqslant 7 provides a simple counterexample to Gregory’s conjecture. Second, we establish that for any independent set S S , we have 1 2 − ∑ v ∈ S x v 2 = q 4 ​ λ − 2 ​ q \frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{q}{4\lambda-2q} , where λ \lambda is the spectral radius of G G , and q q is the Rayleigh quotient of 𝒙 \bm{x} restricted to S ¯ := V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ S \bar{S}:=V(G)\setminus S . Third, we construct a graph with arbitrarily large chromatic number and find an independent set S S such that ∑ v ∈ S x v 2 \sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2} can be arbitrarily close to 1 2 \frac{1}{2} , with an exponentially small gap. Our construction shows that there is no universal lower bound of the form Ω ⁡ ( k α / n β ) \Omega(k^{\alpha}/n^{\beta}) for any α , β > 0 \alpha,\beta>0 . This settles both Gregory’s original conjecture and the modified conjecture of Liu and Ning in the negative. Finally, we show the tightness of our construction and provide some local weighted lower bounds.



Keywords:
Spectral radius; Perron vector; Gregory’s conjecture; Liu–Ning’s conjecture.

1
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple connected graph of order
n
. The
adjacency matrix
of
G
is
defined as
A(G)=[a_{ij}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
, where
a_{ij}=1
if
ij\in E(G)
, and
a_{ij}=0
otherwise.
The
spectral radius

\lambda(G)
is defined as the maximum modulus of eigenvalues of
A(G)
.
The Perron–Frobenius theorem implies that
the spectral radius
\lambda(G)
is the largest eigenvalue of
A(G)
.
For a connected graph
G
, there exists a positive unit eigenvector
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V}
satisfying
\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}^{2}=1
with
x_{v}>0
for all
v\in V
. This vector is called
the
Perron vector
(or principal eigenvector) of
G
.

Spectral graph theory is an important area as it reveals deep connections between the combinatorial structure of a graph and the algebraic properties of its associated matrices.
In recent years, spectral graph theory has also found very important applications in extremal combinatorics
[
9
,
10
]
.
By studying eigenvalues and eigenvectors, it provides powerful tools for analyzing graph properties that are otherwise hard to compute. In particular, spectral methods yield bounds on fundamental parameters such as the independence number
[
7
,
8
]
and the chromatic number
[
20
,
2
,
21
]
.
Let
\alpha(G)
be the independence number, and let
n_{\geqslant 0}(G)
be the minimum number
of non-negative eigenvalues taken over all Hermitian weighted adjacency matrices of
G
.
The well-known inertia bound states that
\alpha(G)\leqslant n_{\geqslant 0}(G)
.
Recently, Kwan and Wigderson
[
11
]
proved that this bound is far from tight. They obtained the largest possible gap between
\alpha(G)
and
n_{\geqslant 0}(G)
, showing that there exists an
n
-vertex regular graph
G
with
\alpha(G)=O(n^{3/4})
and
n_{\geqslant 0}(G)=\Omega(n)
.
Furthermore, Tang, Zhang and Elphick
[
19
]

constructed an
n
-vertex graph
G
such that

\alpha(G)\leqslant 2
and
n_{\geqslant 0}(G)=\Omega(n^{1/9})
.
Consequently, we see that
n_{\geqslant 0}(G)
cannot be upper bounded by any function of
\alpha(G)
.

In this paper, we investigate the distribution of the Perron vector on independent sets for graphs with given chromatic number.
For a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
,
the
Perron mass
of
S
is denoted by

\sigma(S):=\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}.

The
independent Perron mass
of
G
is defined as

\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G):=\max_{S}\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2},

where the maximum is taken over all independent sets
S\subseteq V(G)
.
This parameter is closely related to the local properties of
G
, and is of considerable interest.
It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
) that if
G
is a bipartite graph with
V(G)=A\sqcup B
, then
\sigma(A)=\sigma(B)=\frac{1}{2}
. This identity is useful in the proof of a spectral stability result for a Turán-type problem
[
14
, Lemma 2.5]
.
The study of the coordinates of the Perron vector has always been an important problem that has attracted great attention in spectral graph theory; see
[
17
,
6
]
for recent progress.

A well-known result in spectral graph
theory states that a connected graph
is bipartite if and only if the negative of the largest eigenvalue is also an eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix.
In 2010, Cioabă
[
3
]
proved that for any connected graph
G
, we have

\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)\leqslant 1/2
, with equality if and only if
G
is bipartite.
To some extent,
this result provides an interesting characterization involving
\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)
for a connected graph
G
to be
bipartite. Moreover,
this result naturally raises an intriguing
problem of bounding the quantitative gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)

for a graph
G
with higher chromatic number
\chi(G)=k\geqslant 3
. David A. Gregory proposed the following conjecture,
which was collected and introduced by Cioabă
[
4
]
at an online conference on spectral graph theory.

Conjecture 1.1

(Gregory, cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices with chromatic number
\chi(G)=k\geqslant 3
. Then

\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)\leqslant\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(S_{k-1,n})
,
where
S_{k-1,n}:=K_{k-1}\vee I_{n-k+1}
is the split graph obtained by joining a clique
K_{k-1}
to an independent set of size
n-k+1
.

In other words, the split graph
S_{k-1,n}
achieves the maximum value of
\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)
among all
n
-vertex graphs
G
with chromatic number
k
.
Incidentally, we mention here that split graphs often serve as the extremal graphs of several spectral graph problems in the literature; we refer the readers to
[
18
,
5
,
13
,
12
,
15
]
.
By direct computation, we can check that (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
)

\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(S_{k-1,n})=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k-2}{2\sqrt{(k-2)^{2}+4(k-1)(n-k+1)}}.

Equivalently, Conjecture
1.1
can be rewritten as the following numerical form.

Conjecture 1.2

(Gregory, cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices with
\chi(G)=k
, and let
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V}
be the unit Perron vector of
G
.
Suppose that
S
is an independent set of
G
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\frac{k-2}{2\sqrt{(k-2)^{2}+4(k-1)(n-k+1)}}.

(1)

This conjecture was also selected by Liu and Ning
[
16
, Conjecture 23]
as one of “
Unsolved problems in spectral graph theory
”.
In the case
k=2
, the right-hand side in (
1
) is
0
, which is consistent with
Cioabă’s theorem. In the case
k\geqslant 3
, the bound (
1
) implies
\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}>0
. The aim of Conjecture
1.2
is to provide a
nontrivial universal lower bound.

In 2026, Liu and Ning
[
17
]
made the first progress on Conjecture
1.2
.
They surprisingly disproved Conjecture
1.2
by constructing a graph
G

and exhibiting an independent set
S
in
G
such that

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=O(k^{5}/n^{3})
, which is much smaller than the
conjectured bound of (
1
).
Inspired by this result, they further proposed a modified conjecture as follows.

Conjecture 1.3

(Liu–Ning
[
17
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
with chromatic number
\chi(G)=k\geqslant 3
.
Suppose that
S
is an independent set of
G
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\Omega\left(\frac{k^{5}}{n^{3}}\right).

1.1
An exponentially small gap

In this paper, we construct a simple graph with arbitrarily large chromatic number
k
and an independent set
S
for
which the gap between
\frac{1}{2}
and
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}

is exponentially small.
Our construction not only refutes the original conjecture of Gregory, but also disproves the modified
polynomial-gap conjecture of Liu and Ning.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every integer
k\geqslant 3
and all sufficiently large
n
, there exist a connected
graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\chi(G)=k
and an independent set
S\subseteq V(G)

such that

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\leqslant e^{-\Omega_{k}(n\log n)}.

The exponential decay established in Theorem
1.4
is far smaller than any polynomial bound. Thus,
Theorem
1.4
implies that no universal lower bound of the form
\Omega(k^{\alpha}/n^{\beta})

can hold for any reals
\alpha,\beta>0
. We state it as the following corollary.
Consequently, no lower bound depending polynomially on
n
and
k
can hold in this setting.

Corollary 1.5
.

There do not exist real numbers
C>0
and
\alpha,\beta>0
such that every connected graph
G
of order
n
with
\chi(G)=k
satisfies
\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant Ck^{\alpha}/n^{\beta}
for every independent set
S
.

In fact, we shall prove the following more general result than Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k\geqslant 3
,
\ell\geqslant 2
and
m\geqslant 2k
.
There exist a connected graph
G=G(m,\ell,k)
with order
n:=2m+2\ell+k
and

\chi(G)=k
,
and an independent set
S
with

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\leqslant\frac{30k}{(m-1)^{4\ell+3}}.

1.2
Description of the construction in Theorem
1.6

We define the graph
G=G(m,\ell,k)
in the following simple way.

Definition 1.7
.

Take a complete bipartite graph
K_{m,m}
with bipartition

(S_{A},Y_{A})
, where
|S_{A}|=|Y_{A}|=m
.
Take a path
P
of order
2\ell
with vertices

p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{2\ell}
.
Connect
p_{1}
to a fixed vertex
y^{*}\in Y_{A}
.
Take a clique
K_{k}
with vertex set
C=\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k}\}
. Connect
p_{2\ell}
to
c_{1}
; see Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The graph
G(m,\ell,k)

The graph
G(m,\ell,k)
has
n=2m+2\ell+k
vertices.
Since it contains a clique
K_{k}
, we have
\chi(G)\geqslant k
.
Conversely, the path
P_{2\ell}
and the bipartite subgraph
K_{m,m}
can be colored using colors already available outside the clique, so
we get
\chi(G)=k
. Moreover, the set

S_{\text{path}}:=\{p_{1},p_{3},\dots,p_{2\ell-1}\}

contains no adjacent pair and has no edges to
S_{A}
, hence

S:=S_{A}\cup S_{\text{path}}

(2)

is independent.
We will prove that
S
is the desired independent set with exponentially small gap.

Motivation.

Before presenting the proof argument,
we would like to give an explanation of the original ideas behind such a construction. Motivated by Cioabă’s bound
\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)\leqslant{1}/{2}
,
with equality if and only if
G
is bipartite, one expects from a stability perspective that if
\sigma_{\mathrm{ind}}(G)
is close to
{1}/{2}
, then
G
should be close to a large bipartite graph after removing a small number of edges. So we embed a large complete bipartite subgraph
K_{m,m}
into
G
.
To guarantee
\chi(G)=k
, we simply embed a clique
K_{k}
into
G
. To reduce the mutual influence between the coordinates of the Perron vector
\bm{x}
on the bipartite graph
K_{m,m}
and the coordinates on the clique
K_{k}
, we should connect a sufficiently long path between them. Moreover, adding such a path is a natural and direct way to ensure that
G
is connected.

Our approach.

The key insight of our construction is that the
exponential decay of the Perron vector along a long path
makes the coordinate of
p_{2\ell}
and all coordinates of vertices in the clique
K_{k}
sufficiently small.
This forces the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
to be exponentially tiny, where we write
\sigma(S)=\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
for short.
We shall show that the coordinates of the vertices of
S_{A}
and
Y_{A}
are of order
m^{-1/2}
. In contrast, the
i
-th path vertex
p_{i}
is of order
m^{-i-1/2}
; in particular,
the last path vertex
p_{2\ell}
is of order
m^{-2\ell-1/2}
, which yields that the vertices of the clique are of order
m^{-2\ell-3/2}
and thereby become negligible when the length
\ell
is sufficiently large; see Lemma
4.1
.
Consequently, we can see that

\sum_{v\in S_{A}}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}-O(\frac{1}{m})
and
\sum_{v\in Y_{A}}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}-O(\frac{1}{m})
.
Moreover, we prove that the total contribution from the path and clique is negligible, i.e.,
R:=\sum_{i=1}^{2\ell}x_{p_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{c_{j}}^{2}=O_{k}(\frac{1}{m^{2}})
. These estimates imply a slightly weak bound
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)<\frac{6}{m}
; see Proposition
4.2
.
Therefore, the main difficulty in our argument lies in deriving an exponentially small gap between
\frac{1}{2}
and
\sigma(S)
. To overcome this, we need to capture the essential relation to the structure of the induced subgraph
G[\bar{S}]
and establish an identity for the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
; see Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
.

Organization.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide a simple counterexample for Conjecture
1.2
. In Section
3
, we establish two useful identities for
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
. In Section
4
, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
.
In Section
5
, we demonstrate the tightness of the bound in Theorem
1.4
by showing
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)\geqslant e^{-O(n\log n)}
; see Theorem
5.1
.

2
A simple counterexample for Gregory’s conjecture

Although Liu and Ning
[
17
]
already provided a general construction disproving
Conjecture
1.2
,
we next show that
odd cycles provide a simple and transparent
counterexample.

Example 2.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 7
be an odd integer and
C_{n}
be the cycle on
n
vertices.
Choose
S
to be any maximum independent set of
C_{n}
. Then
\chi(C_{n})=3
, and
Conjecture
1.2
is false.

Proof.

Since
C_{n}
is
2
-regular, we know that

\lambda(C_{n})=2
and the unit Perron eigenvector satisfies

x_{v}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
for all
v\in V(C_{n})
.
Let
S
be a maximum independent set with
|S|=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then

\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{n-1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{n}=\frac{n-1}{2n}
.
The right-hand side of (
1
) with
k=3
becomes

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{8n-15}}
.
It is easy to verify that

\frac{n-1}{2n}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{8n-15}}
since

n\geqslant 7
. Thus, Conjecture
1.2
fails.
∎

Remark.

The odd cycle counterexample captures the essential reason: the conjectured gap in (
1
) is of order
\Theta(n^{-1/2})
, whereas for
C_{n}
, the actual gap is of order
O(n^{-1})
.

3
Two exact identities for the gap

For a connected graph
G
, let
S
be an independent set
of
G
. We denote its complement by
\bar{S}=V\setminus S
.
We write
B
for the

(S,\bar{S})
-incidence matrix, where
b_{vu}=1
if
v\in S
,
u\in\bar{S}
and

vu\in E(G)
; and
b_{vu}=0
otherwise.
Let
A_{\bar{S}}
denote the adjacency matrix of the induced subgraph
G[\bar{S}]
.
Since
S
is an independent set, the adjacency matrix
A:=A(G)

can be partitioned as

A=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\
B^{\mathsf{T}}&A_{\bar{S}}\end{pmatrix}.

Correspondingly,
we partition the Perron
vector
\bm{x}
as
\bm{x}=\binom{\bm{y}}{\bm{z}}
, where
\bm{y}
and
\bm{z}
correspond to the vertices of
S
and
\bar{S}
, respectively.
Then
A\bm{x}=\lambda\bm{x}
gives

\displaystyle\lambda\bm{y}=B\bm{z}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda\bm{z}=B^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{y}+A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}.

(3)

In what follows, we establish an identity for

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
that
involves the spectral radius of
G
and the Rayleigh quotient of
\bm{z}
restricted to the complement
\bar{S}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a connected graph
G=(V,E)
, let
S\subseteq V
be an
independent set of
G
, and set
\bar{S}=V\setminus S
. With the notation above, let

q:={\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}/{\|\bm{z}\|^{2}}
.
Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\frac{q}{4\lambda-2q}.

Proof.

Note that
\|\bm{y}\|^{2}=\sigma(S)
and
\|\bm{z}\|^{2}=1-\sigma(S)
.
From (
3
), we have

\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}B\bm{z}=\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}(\lambda\bm{y})=\lambda\|\bm{y}\|^{2}=\lambda\,\sigma(S).

By the definition of
q
, we have

\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}=q\|\bm{z}\|^{2}=q(1-\sigma(S)).

Since
\bm{x}
is a unit Perron vector of
G
,
and
S
is an independent set, we obtain

\lambda=\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A\bm{x}=2\bm{y}^{\mathsf{T}}B\bm{z}+\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}=2\lambda\,\sigma(S)+q(1-\sigma(S)).

Rearranging gives

\lambda-q=2\lambda\,\sigma(S)-q\,\sigma(S)=(2\lambda-q)\sigma(S)
.
Since
A_{\bar{S}}
is a principal submatrix of
A(G)
, the Rayleigh quotient satisfies

q\leqslant\lambda(G[\bar{S}])\leqslant\lambda
. Hence, we get
2\lambda-q\geqslant\lambda>0
. Then

\sigma(S)=\frac{\lambda-q}{2\lambda-q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{4\lambda-2q}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Since
q
is nonnegative, Theorem
3.1

implies
\sigma(S)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}
for any independent set
S
,
recovering Cioabă’s bound.
In addition, applying Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.2
.

For a connected graph
G
, let
S\subseteq V
be an
independent set of
G
, and set
\bar{S}=V\setminus S
. Let

\mu
be the smallest
eigenvalue of the induced subgraph
G[\bar{S}]
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)\geqslant\frac{\mu}{4\lambda-2\mu},

with equality if and only if
\bm{z}

is an eigenvector of
A_{\bar{S}}
corresponding to
\mu
.

Proof.

Denote
f(q):=\frac{q}{4\lambda-2q}

for each
q\leqslant\lambda
.
We can see that
f(q)
is strictly increasing on
(-\infty,\lambda)
.
The Rayleigh quotient

q={\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}/{\|\bm{z}\|^{2}}

satisfies

\mu\leqslant q\leqslant\lambda
,
which yields

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)\geqslant\frac{\mu}{4\lambda-2\mu}.

Equality occurs when
q=\mu
and

\bm{z}
is an eigenvector of
A_{\bar{S}}

corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu
.
∎

Next, we present a simple equivalent identity.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with the spectral radius
\lambda
and unit Perron vector
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
. Let
S\subseteq V(G)
be an independent set and denote
\bar{S}:=V(G)\setminus S
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}.

Proof.

Since
S
is an independent set, by the eigenvalue equation, we have

\lambda\,\sigma(S)=\lambda\sum_{u\in S}x_{u}^{2}=\sum_{u\in S}x_{u}\cdot\lambda\,x_{u}=\sum_{u\in S}x_{u}\sum_{v\in N(u)}x_{v}=\sum_{uv\in E(S,\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}.

Also, we see that

\lambda=\bm{x}^{\mathsf{T}}A(G)\bm{x}=2\sum_{uv\in E(S,\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}+2\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}.

Substituting the first identity into the second gives

\lambda=2\lambda\,\sigma(S)+2\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}.

By rearranging, we get the desired identity immediately.
∎

4
Proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6

In this section, we give the proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
.
To begin with, we present the upper bounds on all coordinates of the Perron vector of the graph
G(m,\ell,k)
.

4.1
Spectral analysis of the Perron vector

Let
G
be a graph with minimum degree
\delta(G)
and maximum degree
\Delta(G)
. It is well-known that
\delta(G)\leqslant\lambda(G)\leqslant\Delta(G)
, where equality holds if and only if
G
is a regular graph.
For
G=G(m,\ell,k)
and
\lambda=\lambda(G)
, the graph properly contains
K_{m,m}
as a subgraph, so
\lambda>m
. Also
\Delta(G)=m+1
and
G
is not regular, so we get
\lambda<\Delta(G)=m+1
. Thus
m<\lambda<m+1
.

We now analyze the coordinates of the Perron vector of

G
.
The following lemma roughly says that the Perron vector of
G
decays exponentially along the path
P
starting from the bipartite subgraph
K_{m,m}
to the clique
K_{k}
. The coordinates of the path vertices decrease by a factor related to
\lambda
at each step, the clique vertices are strictly smaller than the last path vertex
p_{2\ell}
, and most of the mass of
\bm{x}
is concentrated on the bipartite subgraph
K_{m,m}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The unit Perron vector
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
of
G

satisfies the following properties:

(i)

Denote
p_{0}:=y^{*}\in Y_{A}
.
For every
i=1,2,\dots,2\ell
,

x_{p_{i}}<\frac{x_{p_{i-1}}}{\lambda-1}.

(ii)

Every vertex
c_{i}
in the clique
C
satisfies that for all
j=1,2,\dots,k
,

x_{c_{j}}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{\lambda-k+1}.

(iii)

By symmetry,
all vertices in
S_{A}
share the same eigenvector coordinate,
denoted by
\alpha
;
all vertices in
Y_{A}\setminus\{y^{*}\}
share the
same coordinate, denoted by
\beta
.
Write
\beta^{*}:=x_{y^{*}}
. Then

\tfrac{1}{3}m^{-1/2}<\alpha<m^{-1/2},\qquad\tfrac{1}{4}m^{-1/2}<\beta<m^{-1/2},\qquad\tfrac{1}{4}m^{-1/2}<\beta^{*}<4m^{-1/2}.

(iv)

We have
x_{p_{2\ell}}<4(m-1)^{-2\ell-1/2}
and
x_{c_{j}}<8(m-1)^{-2\ell-3/2}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,k
.

Proof of (i).

We denote the ratio
\rho_{i}:=\frac{x_{p_{i}}}{x_{p_{i-1}}}
for each
i
.
The goal is to establish
\rho_{i}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}

for all
i=1,\dots,2\ell
.
We shall proceed by reverse induction on
i
, starting from
i=2\ell
to
i=1
.

Recall that
P=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{2\ell}\}
and
C=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
.
By Definition
1.7
, the vertices
c_{2},\dots,c_{k}
in the clique
C
are symmetric, so they have the same eigenvector coordinate. We may assume that

x_{c}:=x_{c_{2}}=\dots=x_{c_{k}}
.
The eigenvector equation at
c_{1}
reads as

\lambda x_{c_{1}}=\sum_{u\in N(c_{1})}x_{u}=x_{p_{2\ell}}+(k-1)x_{c}.

(4)

For any other clique vertex, say
c_{j}
with
j\geqslant 2
, we have

\lambda x_{c}=\sum_{u\in N(c_{j})}x_{u}=x_{c_{1}}+(k-2)x_{c},

which yields
x_{c}=\frac{x_{c_{1}}}{\lambda-k+2}
.
Substituting
x_{c}
into (
4
) gives

\lambda x_{c_{1}}=x_{p_{2\ell}}+\frac{k-1}{\lambda-k+2}\,x_{c_{1}},

and rearranging yields

x_{c_{1}}=\gamma\,x_{p_{2\ell}},\quad\text{where}\quad\gamma:=\frac{\lambda-k+2}{\lambda^{2}-(k-2)\lambda-(k-1)}.

(5)

Since
\lambda^{2}-(k-2)\lambda-(k-1)=(\lambda+1)(\lambda-k+1)
and
\lambda>m\geqslant 2k
, the denominator is positive, and one can obtain
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
by a simple calculation.

The eigenvector equation at
p_{2\ell}
is

\lambda x_{p_{2\ell}}=x_{p_{2\ell-1}}+x_{c_{1}}.

Using
x_{c_{1}}=\gamma x_{p_{2\ell}}
from (
5
)
and setting

\rho_{2\ell}:=\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{x_{p_{2\ell-1}}}
,
we obtain
(\lambda-\gamma)x_{p_{2\ell}}=x_{p_{2\ell-1}}
and

\rho_{2\ell}=\frac{1}{\lambda-\gamma}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1},

(6)

where the strict inequality follows directly from
\gamma<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}<\frac{1}{2}
.

For any vertex
p_{i}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant 2\ell-1
, the eigenvector equation is

\lambda x_{p_{i}}=x_{p_{i-1}}+x_{p_{i+1}}
.
Dividing by
x_{p_{i}}
both sides, we obtain

\lambda=\frac{x_{p_{i-1}}}{x_{p_{i}}}+\frac{x_{p_{i+1}}}{x_{p_{i}}}=\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}+\rho_{i+1}.

Rearranging yields the recurrence

\rho_{i}=\frac{1}{\lambda-\rho_{i+1}}.

(7)

We now prove that

\rho_{i}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
by the reverse induction on
i=2\ell,2\ell-1,\dots,1
.
The base case
i=2\ell
is exactly (
6
).
Assume that
the inequality holds for
i+1
, i.e.,

\rho_{i+1}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
.
From (
7
), we get

\rho_{i}<\frac{1}{\lambda-\frac{1}{\lambda-1}}=\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda-1}.

To complete the induction step, it suffices to check that
\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda^{2}-\lambda-1}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
.
This is trivial since
\lambda>m\geqslant 6
.
Thus,
\rho_{i}<\frac{1}{\lambda-1}
.
Consequently, we conclude that for every
i=1,2,\dots,2\ell
,

x_{p_{i}}<\frac{x_{p_{i-1}}}{\lambda-1}.

Proof of (ii).

Now consider the clique
C=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}
.
We sum the eigenvector equations over all its vertices.
The vertex
c_{1}
is adjacent to
p_{2\ell}

and the other
k-1
clique vertices, while each
c_{j}
(
j\geqslant 2
)
is adjacent only to the other
k-1
clique vertices.
Summing these equations yields

\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{c_{i}}=x_{p_{2\ell}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in C,u\neq c_{i}}x_{u}.

Since the subgraph induced by
C
is a clique
K_{k}
,
every vertex coordinate
x_{c_{i}}
appears exactly
k-1
times
in the double sum on the right-hand side.
Hence the equation simplifies to

\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{c_{i}}=x_{p_{2\ell}}+(k-1)\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{c_{i}}.

Rearranging, we obtain the sum of the Perron weights

\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{c_{i}}=\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{\lambda-k+1}.

Since every
x_{c_{j}}>0
, it follows that
each coordinate
x_{c_{j}}
satisfies

x_{c_{j}}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{\lambda-k+1}\quad\text{for all }j=1,\dots,k.

Proof of (iii).

For any
v\in S_{A}
, the neighbors of
v
are the
vertices of
Y_{A}
, so

\lambda\,\alpha=(m-1)\,\beta+\beta^{*}.

(8)

For any
v\in Y_{A}\setminus\{y^{*}\}
,
the neighbors are precisely the
m
vertices of
S_{A}
, giving

\lambda\,\beta=m\,\alpha~~\text{and}~~\beta=\frac{m\,\alpha}{\lambda}.

(9)

Invoking the fact
m<\lambda<m+1
,
we get from (
9
) that

\beta<\alpha~~\text{and}~~\beta=\frac{m}{\lambda}\,\alpha>\frac{m}{m+1}\alpha.

For the vertex
y^{*}\in Y_{A}
, its neighbors consist of all vertices of
S_{A}
and the vertex
p_{1}
,
hence

\lambda\,\beta^{*}=m\,\alpha+x_{p_{1}}=\lambda\,\beta+x_{p_{1}}~~\text{and}~~\beta^{*}=\beta+\frac{x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda}.

(10)

Substituting
\beta
and
\beta^{*}
into (
8
) gives

\lambda\,\alpha=m\beta+\frac{x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda}=m^{2}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}+\frac{x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda}
. Then

(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})\,\alpha=x_{p_{1}}.

(11)

Using (
9
), (
10
), (
11
) and

m<\lambda<m+1
again, we have

\beta^{*}=\beta+\frac{x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda}=\frac{m+(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})}{\lambda}\alpha<\frac{3m+1}{m}\alpha\leqslant 4\alpha.

From the above discussion, we conclude that

\frac{3}{4}\alpha<\beta<\alpha
and

\beta<\beta^{*}<4\alpha
.
To prove
\alpha,\beta,\beta^{*}=\Theta(m^{-1/2})
, it suffices to show
\alpha=\Theta(m^{-1/2})
.
Clearly,
we have

m\alpha^{2}<1
and

\alpha<m^{-1/2}
.

Since
\bm{x}
is a unit vector, i.e.,
\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}^{2}=1
,
we have

m\,\alpha^{2}+(m-1)\,\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}+R=1,

(12)

where
R:=\sum_{i=1}^{2\ell}x_{p_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{c_{j}}^{2}\geqslant 0
.

From part (i), iterating the ratio estimate gives

x_{p_{i}}<\frac{x_{p_{i-1}}}{\lambda-1}<\cdots<\frac{x_{p_{0}}}{(\lambda-1)^{i}}

for all
i\geqslant 1
.
Since
x_{p_{0}}\leqslant 1

and
\lambda>m\geqslant 6
, the contribution of the path to
R
satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{2\ell}x_{p_{i}}^{2}<\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{2i}}=\frac{1}{(\lambda-1)^{2}-1}\leqslant\frac{1}{(m-1)^{2}-1}<\frac{2}{m^{2}}.

(13)

By part (ii), each
x_{c_{j}}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{\lambda-k+1}<\frac{1}{m-k+1}
.
Then

\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{c_{j}}^{2}<\frac{k}{(m-k+1)^{2}}\leqslant\frac{4k}{m^{2}},

where the last inequality holds since
m\geqslant 2k
. Therefore

R<\frac{2}{m^{2}}+\frac{4k}{m^{2}}\leqslant\frac{5k}{m^{2}}.

(14)

Note that
\beta<\alpha
and
\beta^{*}<4\alpha
. By (
12
), we have

1<m\alpha^{2}+(m-1)\alpha^{2}+16\alpha^{2}+R=(2m+15)\alpha^{2}+R,

which implies

\alpha^{2}>\frac{1-(5k)/m^{2}}{2m+15}\geqslant\frac{1}{9m}.

Thus, we obtain that

\frac{1}{3}m^{-1/2}<\alpha<m^{-1/2}
.
Combining with
\frac{3}{4}\alpha<\beta<\alpha
,
we get
\frac{1}{4}m^{-1/2}<\beta<m^{-1/2}
.
Moreover, using
\beta<\beta^{*}<4\alpha
yields

\frac{1}{4}m^{-1/2}<\beta^{*}<4m^{-1/2}
, as desired.

Proof of (iv).

From part (i), we have
x_{p_{i}}<\frac{x_{p_{i-1}}}{\lambda-1}

for
i=1,\dots,2\ell
.
Iterating this inequality yields

x_{p_{2\ell}}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell-1}}}{\lambda-1}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell-2}}}{(\lambda-1)^{2}}<\cdots<\frac{x_{p_{0}}}{(\lambda-1)^{2\ell}}.

Recall that
p_{0}=y^{*}
and
x_{y^{*}}=\beta^{*}<4m^{-1/2}
.
Since
\lambda>m
, we have

x_{p_{2\ell}}<4(m-1)^{-2\ell-1/2}
.
By part (ii), each coordinate of the clique satisfies

x_{c_{j}}<\frac{x_{p_{2\ell}}}{\lambda-k+1}<\frac{4(m-1)^{-2\ell-1/2}}{m-k+1}<8(m-1)^{-2\ell-3/2}
.
∎

4.2
Warm up for a weak bound

As a warm-up and to illustrate our method, we next give a quick proof of a weak bound, saying that
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)<\frac{6}{m}
.
This follows directly from the estimates in Lemma
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under (
2
) and Definition
1.7
,
we have
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)<\frac{6}{m}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\|\bm{y}\|^{2}=\sigma(S)
and
\|{\bm{z}}\|^{2}=1-\sigma(S)
,
where

\sigma(S)=\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=\sum_{v\in S_{A}}x_{v}^{2}+\sum_{v\in S_{\mathrm{path}}}x_{v}^{2}.

Using (
13
), we obtain

\sum_{v\in S_{\mathrm{path}}}x_{v}^{2}<\sum_{i=1}^{2\ell}x_{p_{i}}^{2}<{2}/{m^{2}}
, which yields

m\alpha^{2}<\sigma(S)<m\alpha^{2}+\frac{2}{m^{2}}.

(15)

Recall in (
14
) that
R<{(5k)}/{m^{2}}
.
Then

1-\sigma(S)=(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}+\sum_{v\in\bar{S}_{\mathrm{path}}}x_{v}^{2}+\sum_{v\in C}x_{v}^{2}<(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}+\frac{5k}{m^{2}}.

We next show that
\sigma(S)
and
1-\sigma(S)
are
almost equal. From the above discussion, our goal is to estimate the difference between
m\alpha^{2}
and
(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}
.
From (
9
) and (
10
), we have

(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}=(m-1)\frac{m^{2}\alpha^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\left(\frac{m\alpha}{\lambda}+\frac{x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda}\right)^{2}=\frac{m^{3}\alpha^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2m\alpha\,x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{x_{p_{1}}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}-m\alpha^{2}=m\alpha^{2}\!\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{2m\alpha\,x_{p_{1}}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{x_{p_{1}}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

(16)

Recall in (
11
) that

x_{p_{1}}=(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})\alpha
.
Substituting this into (
16
) yields

\displaystyle(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}-m\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{m\alpha^{2}(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2m\alpha^{2}(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}(\lambda^{2}-m^{2})(m+\lambda^{2}-m^{2})}{\lambda^{2}}.

Since
m<\lambda<m+1
, we have
0<\lambda^{2}-m^{2}<2m+1
.
Together with
\alpha^{2}<\frac{1}{m}
, we obtain

\left|(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}-m\alpha^{2}\right|<\frac{\frac{1}{m}\cdot(2m+1)\cdot(3m+1)}{m^{2}}<\frac{7}{m}.

Consequently, we get

1-\sigma(S)<(m-1)\beta^{2}+(\beta^{*})^{2}+\frac{5k}{m^{2}}<m\alpha^{2}+\frac{10}{m}.

(17)

Adding (
15
) and (
17
) yields

1<2m\alpha^{2}+\frac{12}{m}
,
which implies

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{6}{m}<m\alpha^{2}<\sigma(S)
, as needed.
∎

Remark.

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.6

In Proposition
4.2
, we obtained a weak bound
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)<\frac{6}{m}
. To improve this bound and establish an exponentially small gap, a key ingredient is to utilize the exact identities from Theorems
3.1
or
3.3
.
Here, we prefer to use the simple version in Theorem
3.3
, which states

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}.

(18)

We now use the estimates provided by Lemma
4.1
.

Claim 4.1
.

We have
\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}\leqslant 30k\cdot(m-1)^{-4\ell-2}
.

Proof of claim.

Recall that
\bar{S}=Y_{A}\cup\bar{S}_{\mathrm{path}}\cup C
, where

Y_{A}
is an independent set of
K_{m,m}
,
and
\bar{S}_{\mathrm{path}}=\{p_{2},p_{4},\dots,p_{2\ell}\}

consists of even-indexed vertices,
which are pairwise non-adjacent, and
C
forms a clique
K_{k}
. Therefore,
the edges of
G[\bar{S}]
are of the following two types:

•

the
\binom{k}{2}
edges inside the clique
C
: By Lemma
4.1
(iv), and
m\geqslant 2k
, we obtain

\sum_{uv\in E(C)}x_{u}x_{v}\leqslant\binom{k}{2}\cdot 64(m-1)^{-4\ell-3}<16k\cdot(m-1)^{-4\ell-2}.

•

the bridge edge
p_{2\ell}c_{1}
: The contribution of this edge is given as

x_{p_{2\ell}}x_{c_{1}}\leqslant 4(m-1)^{-2\ell-1/2}\cdot 8(m-1)^{-2\ell-3/2}\leqslant 32(m-1)^{-4\ell-2}.

So we obtain

\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}=\sum_{uv\in E(C)}x_{u}x_{v}+x_{p_{2\ell}}\,x_{c_{1}}\leqslant 30k\cdot(m-1)^{-4\ell-2}
, as needed.
∎

Applying the identity (
18
),
and combining with Claim
4.1
, we get

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)\leqslant\frac{30k}{\lambda\,(m-1)^{4\ell+2}}<\frac{30k}{(m-1)^{4\ell+3}},

where the last inequality holds since
\lambda>m
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

4.4
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In what follows, we show that Theorem
1.4
is a direct consequence of Theorem
1.6
. Indeed, we assume that
k
is fixed and
n
is sufficiently large.
In the case that
n-k
is even,
we can choose
m:=\lfloor{(n-k)}/{4}\rfloor
and
\ell:=\lceil{(n-k)}/{4}\rceil
.
Then
2m+2\ell+k=n
. So Theorem
1.6
gives

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=O_{k}(m^{-4\ell-3})=e^{-\Omega_{k}(n\log n)}.

To obtain the bound for all sufficiently large
n
,
we use the same construction of Definition
1.7
with a connecting path of order
2\ell+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
is chosen so that
n-k-\varepsilon
is even. Define
S:=S_{A}\cup\{p_{i}:i\text{ is odd}\}
. The estimates in Lemma
4.1
and Claim
4.1
are unchanged up to absolute constants, with
2\ell
replaced by
2\ell+\varepsilon
. Taking
m=\lfloor(n-k-\varepsilon)/4\rfloor
and

gives a graph on exactly
n
vertices and yields

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\leqslant e^{-\Omega_{k}(n\log n)}
. This completes the proof.

5
Tightness of Theorem
1.4
and lower bounds

In Theorem
3.1
,
we obtained that for any independent set
S
,

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\frac{q}{4\lambda-2q},

(19)

where
q=\frac{{\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}}{\|\bm{z}\|^{2}}
is the Rayleigh quotient of the Perron vector on
\bar{S}
.
Thus, bounding the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
from below reduces to bounding quadratic form
q
from below. As an application, we now prove that the bound in Theorem
1.4

cannot
be improved beyond a factor in the exponent.

Theorem 5.1
.

If
G
is a non-bipartite graph of order
n
,
then for any independent set
S
of
G
,

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant e^{-O(n\log n)}.

Proof.

Since
G
is non-bipartite, we see that
\bar{S}
contains at least one edge
uv\in E(G)
. This gives

{\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}=2\sum_{ij\in E(\bar{S})}x_{i}x_{j}\geqslant 2x_{u}x_{v}
.
From the eigenvalue equation
A\bm{x}=\lambda\bm{x}
, for the edge
uv
, we have

x_{v}=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x_{u}+\sum_{w\sim v,w\neq u}x_{w})\geqslant\frac{1}{\lambda}x_{u}
. It follows that

x_{v}\geqslant\lambda^{-1}x_{u}
.

Assume that all coordinates of the Perron vector are sorted in the order
x_{v_{1}}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant x_{v_{n}}
.
Let
d(v_{1},v_{n})
be the distance between
v_{1}
and
v_{n}
.
By inducting a shortest path starting from
v_{1}
to
v_{n}
, the above discussion gives

x_{v_{n}}\geqslant\lambda^{-d(v_{1},v_{n})}x_{v_{1}}
.
Invoking the fact
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{v_{i}}^{2}=1
, we obtain

x_{v_{1}}\geqslant{1}/{\sqrt{n}}
. We write
D
for the diameter of
G
.
Thus, we conclude that
x_{v_{n}}\geqslant\lambda^{-D}/\sqrt{n}
. Then

q=\frac{{\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}}{{\|\bm{z}\|^{2}}}\geqslant{\bm{z}^{\mathsf{T}}A_{\bar{S}}\bm{z}}\geqslant 2x_{u}x_{v}\geqslant\frac{2}{\lambda^{2D}n}.

Since
D<n
and
\lambda\leqslant\Delta<n
, we have
\frac{2}{\lambda^{2D}n}>\frac{2}{n^{2n+1}}
. Combining with (
18
),
we obtain

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)>\frac{q}{4n}\geqslant\frac{1}{2n^{2n+2}}=e^{-O(n\log n)}.\qed

It is worth noting that even in the non-bipartite case, the above lower bound can be quite weak.
As an illustration, we consider the complete
k
-partite graph
K_{n/k,\ldots,n/k}
, where
k
divides
n
. Then
D=2
and
\lambda=\frac{k-1}{k}n
. By symmetry, we obtain
x_{v}=1/\sqrt{n}
.
So a maximum independent set gives
\sigma(S)=\frac{n}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{k}
and

\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}
.
However, in the proof of Theorem
5.1
,
we see that
q\geqslant{2}{\lambda^{-2D}/n}
. Then (
18
) gives
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)=\Omega(1/n^{6})
, which is a polynomial bound.

The construction in Theorem
1.4
exhibits exponential decay due to its diameter being
\Theta(n)
. For graphs with bounded diameter, such as complete
k
-partite graphs, the gap stays polynomial in terms of
n
.
This difference suggests that the size of the gap may be determined by the diameter, rather than the chromatic number. Inspired by this observation, a more appropriate framework is possibly to fix the diameter
D
, and ask how the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
depends on
D
and
n
jointly.

In the sequel, we provide some lower bounds for
\frac{1}{2}-\sigma(S)
, where
S
is an arbitrary independent set of
G
.
Applying Theorem
3.3
, we obtain
a local weighted lower bound.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
, let
\bm{x}
be its unit Perron vector, let
S
be an independent set. Let
\lambda=\lambda(G)
, choose a vertex
v^{\ast}\in V(G)
such that

x_{v^{\ast}}:=\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}
,
and define

\rho(u):=\operatorname{dist}_{G}(u,v^{*})
for each
u\in V(G)
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}\lambda^{-\rho(u)-\rho(v)-1}.

Proof.

For a vertex
u\in V(G)
, let
v^{\ast}:=w_{0}\to w_{1}\to\cdots\to w_{\ell}:=u
(where
\ell=\rho(u)
)
be a shortest path starting from
v_{\ast}
to
u
. For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell
, the eigenvalue equation at
w_{i}
gives
\lambda x_{w_{i}}=\sum_{z\sim w_{i}}x_{z}\geqslant x_{w_{i-1}}
,
hence
x_{w_{i}}\geqslant x_{w_{i-1}}\lambda^{-1}
.
Iterating along the path yields
x_{u}\geqslant x_{v^{*}}\,\lambda^{-\rho(u)}
.
Note that
x_{v^{*}}^{2}\geqslant 1/n
since
\bm{x}
is unit.
Therefore, for every edge
uv
in
G[\bar{S}]
,

x_{u}x_{v}\geqslant x_{v^{*}}^{2}\,\lambda^{-\rho(u)-\rho(v)}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\lambda^{-\rho(u)-\rho(v)}.

Combining with Theorem
3.3
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}x_{u}x_{v}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{uv\in E(\bar{S})}\lambda^{-\rho(u)-\rho(v)-1},

which gives the desired inequality.
∎

Next, we give an corollary of Theorem
5.2
. We present a lower bound on
\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
in terms of the diameter and the chromatic number of
G
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
5.2
,
let
D
be the diameter and
\chi(G)=k
be the chromatic number of
G
, where
k\geqslant 3
. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\frac{\binom{k-1}{2}}{n\,\lambda^{2D+1}}.

Proof.

For every edge
uv\in E(\bar{S})
, we have
\rho(u),\rho(v)\leqslant D
.
Now assume
\chi(G)=k\geqslant 3
. Since
S
is independent, we have
\chi(G[\bar{S}])\geqslant k-1
.
A graph
H
is
color-critical

if
\chi(H^{\prime})<\chi(H)
for every proper subgraph
H^{\prime}
of
H
. By taking a color-critical subgraph of
G[\bar{S}]
,
we may assume
\delta(H)\geqslant\chi(H)-1
. Otherwise, if
\delta(H)\leqslant\chi(H)-2
, then
let
v\in V(H)
be a vertex with minimum degree, since
H\setminus\{v\}
can be colored by at most
\chi(H)-1
colors, and the neighbors of
v
use at most
\chi(H)-2
colors, so
H
can be colored by
\chi(H)-1
colors, a contradiction.

From the above discussion, we have
\delta(H)\geqslant\chi(H)-1\geqslant k-2
, so

2e(H)\geqslant(k-2)|V(H)|\geqslant(k-2)(k-1).

Hence

e(G[\bar{S}])\geqslant e(H)\geqslant\binom{k-1}{2}.

Applying Theorem
5.2
gives

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant{\binom{k-1}{2}}/{\big(n\,\lambda^{2D+1}\big)}
, as needed.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
with chromatic number
k\geqslant 3
, let
\bm{x}
be its unit Perron vector, and let
S
be an independent set. Then

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}>\frac{k-2}{n^{2n-2k+5}}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{S}:=V(G)\setminus S
and
v^{\ast}\in V(G)
be a vertex defined as in Theorem
5.2
. Let
H
be a color-minimal subgraph of
G[\bar{S}]
with
\chi(H)\geqslant k-1
. Such a subgraph exists because
\chi(G[\bar{S}])\geqslant k-1
.

Choose a vertex
u\in V(H)
such that the distance
d(v^{*},u)
is minimum. Denote
d:=d(v^{*},u)
. From the proof of Corollary
5.3
, we know that
d_{H}(u)\geqslant k-2
.
For each
w\in N_{H}(u)
, we get
d(v^{*},w)\leqslant d(v^{*},u)+d(u,w)\leqslant d+1
. Applying Theorem
5.2
yields

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{w\in N_{H}(u)}\frac{1}{\lambda^{\rho(u)+\rho(w)+1}}\geqslant\frac{k-2}{n\,\lambda^{2d+2}}.

It remains to bound
d
. By the minimality of
d(v^{*},u)
, a shortest path from
v^{*}
to
u
meets
H
only at its last vertex
u
. Thus, this path contributes
d
vertices outside
H
, so

n\geqslant d+|V(H)|
.
Since
\chi(H)\geqslant k-1
, we have
|V(H)|\geqslant k-1
, and therefore
d\leqslant n-k+1
.
Consequently, we get

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}\geqslant\frac{k-2}{n\,\lambda^{2n-2k+4}}.

Finally, invoking the fact
\lambda(G)\leqslant\Delta(G)<n
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper,
we first showed that cycles
C_{n}
(with odd integers
n\geqslant 7
) serve as counterexamples to Gregory’s conjecture; see Example
2.1
. This is much simpler than the construction recently provided by Liu and Ning
[
17
]
.
Second, we established two identities for

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
; see Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
.
These identities capture the intrinsic relation between the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}

and the graph structure of
G[\bar{S}]
.
Third, we constructed a graph
G
with chromatic number
k
and found an independent set
S
such that
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
can be arbitrarily close to
1/2
, with a small gap decaying as
\exp(-\Omega(n\log n))
; see Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
.
Finally, we illustrated that the bound in Theorem
1.4
is tight up to a constant factor in the exponent; see Theorem
5.1
.
Moreover, we provide some local weighted lower bounds involving the distances of vertices; see Theorem
5.2
.

Our construction essentially consists of two parts. One part is a large complete bipartite graph, which is designed to make
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
close to
1/2
. The other part is a clique, which is used to satisfy the chromatic number constraint; see Definition
1.7
.
As for the key techniques, we employ a long path to attenuate the contributions of the vertices outside the bipartite graph, and then apply the above identity to bound the gap

\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
accurately.
Our construction not only completely disproves the polynomial lower bound conjectured by Liu and Ning, but also demonstrates that
\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
cannot be separated from
1/2
by any function of polynomial order in
n
and
k
.

Let
C_{n,k}
be the minimum value of the gap
\frac{1}{2}-\sum_{v\in S}x_{v}^{2}
, where
G
is an
n
-vertex connected graph with chromatic number
k\geqslant 3
and unit Perron vector
\bm{x}
, and
S
is an independent set of
G
.
Combining Theorems
1.4
and
5.1
, we see that
\log C_{n,k}=-\Theta_{k}(n\log n)
. Therefore, it would be interesting to determine an exact constant
\delta_{k}
such that
\log C_{n,k}=-\big(\delta_{k}+o(1)\big)\,n\log n
.
