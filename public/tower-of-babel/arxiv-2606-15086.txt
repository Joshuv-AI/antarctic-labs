Finite-Window Singularity Audits and Local-to-Clean Defect Transfer for Navier–Stokes
Runlong Yu

Abstract
We develop a finite-window audit and transfer framework for the local regularity problem of the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The paper has two logically separate components. First, a finite-scale critical ledger estimate shows that a persistent non-CKN branch cannot survive without cumulative untaxed critical supply or accumulated localization leakage. Second, a local-to-clean transfer theorem shows how a clean finite-window anti-phantom gap gives a localized coercive estimate for Navier–Stokes defect packages, provided three structural inputs hold: quotient-lifting stability, componentwise detection comparison, and a normalized residual budget. The technical contribution is an explicit bookkeeping of the residuals that can obstruct this transfer. Pressure tails, cutoff leakage, truncation loss, nonlinear cutoff mismatch, reproduction drift, gauge mismatch, and profit discrepancy are separated as finite-window ledger entries and then assembled into a concrete absorption criterion. The main transfer theorem is algebraic once these entries satisfy the stated normalized bounds; the PDE-facing work left open is precisely to prove the corresponding component compatibility estimates against the localized quotient distance. Thus the article is a conditional reduction. Its conclusion is structural: any surviving obstruction must either defeat the stated compatibility/growth controls or appear as an NS-realizable, cleaned, scale-critical, combined-invisible, profitable, reproducible defect cascade.

Navier–Stokes equations is usually phrased in terms of scale-critical

Thus a possible singular point must avoid that decay basin through an infinite
sequence of parabolic windows. This paper asks a more structural question:

If a non-CKN branch persists across scales, what precise mechanism pays for
that persistence, hides from the available finite-window observations, reproduces
from scale to scale, and remains realizable by the Navier–Stokes equation?

The answer is formulated as a finite-window singularity audit coupled to a
local-to-clean transfer theorem. The audit side shows that persistent critical
badness cannot survive for free: along a finite chain of windows it must generate
untaxed critical supply or accumulated leakage. The transfer side then asks
whether a clean finite-window anti-phantom gap can be transported back to a
localized Navier–Stokes package without hiding pressure tails, cutoff leakage,
truncation error, reproduction drift, gauge mismatch, or profit discrepancy.

The result is not a proof of Navier–Stokes regularity and not a construction of
a singular solution. Its contribution is a theorem-level reduction: a surviving
singular branch must either defeat finite-window observability and residual
absorption, or organize itself as an NS-realizable, cleaned, scale-critical,
combined-invisible, profitable, reproducible defect cascade.

1.3
Main innovations

The paper has four connected innovations.

I1.

Finite-scale payment ledger.
Persistent reservoir badness is
converted into a quantitative alternative: cumulative untaxed supply or leakage
must appear along any long non-CKN branch.

I2.

NS-realizable defect audit.
A dangerous obstruction is not an
arbitrary kernel vector. It must satisfy coarse momentum, pressure compatibility,
Reynolds positivity, and the energy-flux identity inherited from suitable weak
solutions.

I3.

Local-to-clean transfer.
A clean anti-phantom gap is transferred

I4.

Residual-budget closure map.
Pressure, localization, truncation,
nonlinear, reproduction, gauge, and profit errors are placed into a single

problem against the localized quotient distance.

1.4
Boundary of the paper

The paper deliberately does not claim a new proof of Navier–Stokes regularity.
It proves a finite-scale ledger estimate and an algebraic local-to-clean transfer
theorem, then records the exact compatibility estimates that would be needed to
turn this finite-window mechanism into a regularity argument. Conversely, any
counterexample mechanism surviving this framework would have to satisfy the same
compatibility, cleaning, observability, profitability, and reproduction tests.

2
Suitable weak solutions, CKN coordinates, and bad branches

For a space-time point
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
, write

Q_{r}(z_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}).

When
z_{0}=(0,0)
, write
Q_{r}=Q_{r}(0,0)
.

A pair
(u,p)
is a suitable weak solution if

u\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}\cap L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x},\qquad p\in L^{3/2},

solves (NS) distributionally, is divergence-free, and satisfies the local energy inequality: for every nonnegative
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\int|u(x,t)|^{2}\phi(x,t)\,dx+2\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|\nabla u|^{2}\phi\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int|u|^{2}(\partial_{s}\phi+\Delta\phi)\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int(|u|^{2}+2p)u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx\,ds.

(2.1)

The scale-critical quantities are

\displaystyle A(z_{0},r)

\displaystyle=r^{-1}\esssup_{t_{0}-r^{2}<t<t_{0}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle E(z_{0},r)

\displaystyle=r^{-1}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle C(z_{0},r)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|u|^{3}\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle D(z_{0},r)

\displaystyle=r^{-2}\int_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}|p-(p)_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(t)|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt.

We also set

\Psi(z_{0},r)=C(z_{0},r)+D(z_{0},r),\qquad\Phi(z_{0},r)=A(z_{0},r)+E(z_{0},r)+C(z_{0},r)+D(z_{0},r).

The CKN criterion gives a universal
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}>0
such that

\Psi(z_{0},r)\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\text{ is regular in a smaller cylinder.}

Therefore a non-CKN branch is a sequence of scales
r_{k}\downarrow 0
for which
\Psi(z_{0},r_{k})>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{CKN}}
for all relevant
k
.

Definition 2.1

(Admissible scale-window chain)
.

Fix
0<\theta<1
. A sequence

r_{k}=\theta^{k}r_{0},\qquad Q_{k}=Q_{r_{k}}(z_{k}),

is called an admissible scale-window chain if
Q_{k+1}\subset Q_{k}
and the centers have controlled parabolic drift:

|x_{k+1}-x_{k}|\leq\eta r_{k},\qquad|t_{k+1}-t_{k}|\leq\eta r_{k}^{2}.

The reservoir badness used by the ledger is

B_{k}=A_{k}+C_{k}+D_{k},

where
A_{k},C_{k},D_{k}
denote the scale-critical quantities on
Q_{k}
. The dissipation
E_{k}
is not placed in the reservoir; it appears mainly as a tax.

3
Payment: the finite-scale critical ledger

This section records the first answer to the audit question. If badness persists, it must be paid for. The payment appears as untaxed critical supply or localization leakage.

Choose cutoffs
\phi_{k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{k})
satisfying

0\leq\phi_{k}\leq 1,\qquad\phi_{k}\equiv 1\text{ on }Q_{k+1},

and

|\nabla\phi_{k}|\leq C_{\theta}r_{k}^{-1},\qquad|\partial_{t}\phi_{k}|+|\Delta\phi_{k}|\leq C_{\theta}r_{k}^{-2}.

Define transition ledger coordinates

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}

\displaystyle=r_{k}^{-1}\int_{Q_{k}}|u|^{2}|u\cdot\nabla\phi_{k}|\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle\Pi_{k}^{\mathrm{press}}

\displaystyle=r_{k}^{-1}\int_{Q_{k}}|p-(p)_{B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})}(t)|\,|u\cdot\nabla\phi_{k}|\,dx\,dt,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=r_{k}^{-1}\int_{Q_{k}}|u|^{2}(|\partial_{t}\phi_{k}|+|\Delta\phi_{k}|)\,dx\,dt.

The first is nonlinear flux supply, the second is pressure-transport supply, and the third is cutoff/window leakage.

Lemma 3.1

(Local energy ledger)
.

For every suitable weak solution and every transition
Q_{k}\to Q_{k+1}
,

A_{k+1}+2E_{k+1}\leq\theta^{-1}\bigl(\Lambda_{k}+\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}+2\Pi_{k}^{\mathrm{press}}\bigr).

Proof.

Apply the local energy inequality (
2.1
) with the cutoff
\phi_{k}
in the suitable-weak-solution sense of the CKN framework
[
5
,
15
]
, first with a standard time cutoff that approaches the characteristic function of the terminal time interval and then pass to the usual almost-everywhere time representatives. Taking the essential supremum over terminal times in the smaller cylinder gives the
A_{k+1}
term, while the spacetime part gives
E_{k+1}
. Since
\phi_{k}\equiv 1
on
Q_{k+1}
, the left-hand side controls the smaller-scale energy and dissipation. The right-hand side separates into the cutoff leakage term, the nonlinear flux term, and the pressure-transport term. The spatial average of the pressure may be subtracted because
\nabla\cdot u=0
and, for each time,
\int u\cdot\nabla\phi_{k}\,dx=0
after the standard compact-support approximation.
∎

The velocity and pressure reservoirs are closed by the standard interpolation and pressure-decay estimates:

\displaystyle C_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{I,\theta}\Bigl((\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}+2\Pi_{k}^{\mathrm{press}})^{3/2}+\Lambda_{k}^{3/2}\Bigr),

(3.1)

\displaystyle D_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{P}\theta D_{k}+C_{P}\theta^{-2}C_{k}.

(3.2)

The first follows by applying the scale-invariant interpolation inequality

C_{k+1}\lesssim A_{k+1}^{3/4}E_{k+1}^{3/4}+A_{k+1}^{3/2}

to
Section

3
and absorbing constants depending only on the fixed scale ratio
\theta
. The second is the usual local Calderon–Zygmund plus harmonic-pressure decomposition
[
6
,
11
,
15
]
.

Fix
0<\lambda<1
and choose
\theta
so that

\delta_{D}=(1-\lambda)-C_{P}\theta>0.

Define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sup}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}(\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}+2\Pi_{k}^{\mathrm{press}})+C_{I,\theta}(\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{flux}}+2\Pi_{k}^{\mathrm{press}})^{3/2}+C_{P}\theta^{-2}C_{k},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tax}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}

\displaystyle=2E_{k+1}+(1-\lambda)A_{k}+(1-\lambda)C_{k}+\delta_{D}D_{k},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Leak}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}\Lambda_{k}+C_{I,\theta}\Lambda_{k}^{3/2}.

Theorem 3.2

(Full critical ledger inequality)
.

Along every admissible scale-window chain,

B_{k+1}-(1-\lambda)B_{k}\leq\mathrm{Sup}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}-\mathrm{Tax}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}+\mathrm{Leak}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Add the energy transition estimate, the interpolation bound (
3.1
), and the pressure decay estimate (
3.2
). Subtract
(1-\lambda)(A_{k}+C_{k}+D_{k})
. The terms are exactly those grouped into supply, tax, and leakage.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Finite-scale survival alternative)
.

Assume that

B_{k}\geq\varepsilon,\qquad 0\leq k\leq N-1.

Then

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Sup}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}-\mathrm{Tax}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}\bigr)_{+}\geq\lambda\varepsilon N-B_{0}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{Leak}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}.

(3.4)

Proof.

Let
M_{k}=B_{k+1}-(1-\lambda)B_{k}
. Summing gives

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}M_{k}=B_{N}-B_{0}+\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}B_{k}\geq-B_{0}+\lambda\varepsilon N.

On the other hand, (
3.3
) implies

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}M_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Sup}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}-\mathrm{Tax}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}\bigr)+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{Leak}^{\mathrm{full}}_{k}.

Since
X\leq X_{+}
for every real number
X
, (
3.4
) follows.
∎

The meaning of (
3.4
) is the first central conclusion:

\boxed{\text{long survival of critical badness forces positive average untaxed supply or leakage.}}

In particular, if leakage has vanishing average along an infinite branch, then a persistent bad orbit requires positive-density untaxed supply.

4
Hiding: NS-realizable defect packages and combined observability

The ledger tells us that badness must be paid for. The next question is how a paid mechanism can remain hidden. The answer requires passing from raw scale quantities to equation-generated defect packages.

4.1
Dyadic rescaling and coarse graining

At a point
z_{0}=(0,0)
, let
r_{n}=2^{-n}
and define the rescaled fields

u^{(n)}(x,t)=r_{n}u(r_{n}x,r_{n}^{2}t),\qquad p^{(n)}(x,t)=r_{n}^{2}p(r_{n}x,r_{n}^{2}t).

The CKN quantities are invariant under this scaling.

Let
S_{\ell}
be an interior parabolic mollifier at scale
\ell
. Define

U_{n,\ell}=S_{\ell}u^{(n)},\qquad P_{n,\ell}=S_{\ell}p^{(n)},

and the Reynolds defect

R_{n,\ell}=S_{\ell}(u^{(n)}\otimes u^{(n)})-U_{n,\ell}\otimes U_{n,\ell}.

Because
S_{\ell}
is an averaging operator,
R_{n,\ell}
is nonnegative as a quadratic form:

R_{n,\ell}\geq 0.

The unresolved energy density is

\kappa_{n,\ell}=\frac{1}{2}\Tr R_{n,\ell}\geq 0.

The coarse-grained fields satisfy the exact identity

\partial_{t}U-\Delta U+\nabla\cdot(U\otimes U)+\nabla P=-\nabla\cdot R,\qquad\nabla\cdot U=0.

(4.1)

Taking divergence gives pressure compatibility:

-\Delta P=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(U_{i}U_{j}+R_{ij}).

(4.2)

The active pressure
P^{\mathrm{act}}
is the chosen local solution generated by the source in (
4.2
); the retained harmonic pressure is

P^{\mathrm{har}}=P-P^{\mathrm{act}},\qquad\Delta P^{\mathrm{har}}=0

in the local pressure window. Only functions of time are pure pressure gauge. Spatially harmonic pressure is not discarded; it still contributes to momentum and pressure work.

The coarse-grained flux is

\Pi_{n,\ell}=-R_{n,\ell}:\nabla U_{n,\ell}.

Dotting (
4.1
) with
U
gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}-\Delta\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}+|\nabla U|^{2}+\nabla\cdot\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}|U|^{2}+P\right)U+RU\right]=-\Pi.

(4.3)

Thus the package

D_{n,\ell}=(U_{n,\ell},P_{n,\ell};P^{\mathrm{act}}_{n,\ell},P^{\mathrm{har}}_{n,\ell},R_{n,\ell},\Pi_{n,\ell})

is not an arbitrary finite-dimensional defect. It is
NS-realizable
: it comes from an actual suitable weak solution and satisfies (
4.1
), (
4.2
),
R\geq 0
, and (
4.3
). The coarse-grained flux and local energy-transfer viewpoint is closely related to the Constantin–E–Titi commutator method, Eyink’s local energy-transfer formulation, and the Duchon–Robert defect-measure perspective

4.2
Combined observation channels

A finite observation window is a tuple

W=(n,\ell,\Lambda,\chi,s_{*}),

where
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional frequency or test window,
\chi
is a cutoff, and
s_{*}
is a selected time. Let
Y_{W}
denote the cleaned finite-dimensional constrained defect space obtained from the projected variables after imposing the finite-window versions of momentum, pressure compatibility, and flux identity, and after quotienting only exact gauge directions.

The combined observation map has four channels:

\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{comb}}_{W}=(\mathcal{O}^{P}_{W},\mathcal{O}^{F}_{W},\mathcal{O}^{E}_{W},\mathcal{O}^{T}_{W}).

Here

•

\mathcal{O}^{P}_{W}
observes the active pressure source;

•

\mathcal{O}^{F}_{W}
observes the linearized interscale flux;

•

\mathcal{O}^{E}_{W}
observes the positive energy/dissipation channel;

•

\mathcal{O}^{T}_{W}
observes selected-time trace directions, equivalently through the adjoint map
A_{W}^{*}
.

The hierarchy is

\text{active pressure}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{flux}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{energy}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{adjoint trace}.

Definition 4.1

(Combined-invisible direction)
.

A nonzero
d\in Y_{W}
is called combined-invisible in the window
W
if

\mathcal{O}^{P}_{W}d=\mathcal{O}^{F}_{W}d=\mathcal{O}^{E}_{W}d=\mathcal{O}^{T}_{W}d=0.

It is called a true finite-window phantom if, in addition, it lies in the closure of cleaned projections of NS-realizable packages.

This distinction is essential. A formal finite-dimensional kernel is not automatically a Navier–Stokes obstruction. It becomes dangerous only after intersection with the range of actual NS-derived packages.

Proposition 4.2

(Fixed-window observability criterion)
.

For a fixed finite window
W
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

there is no nonzero combined-invisible direction in
Y_{W}
;

(ii)

there exists a finite constant
M_{W}
such that

\left\|d\right\|_{Y_{W}}\leq M_{W}\Bigl(orm{\mathcal{O}^{P}_{W}d}{}+orm{\mathcal{O}^{F}_{W}d}{}+orm{\mathcal{O}^{E}_{W}d}{}+orm{\mathcal{O}^{T}_{W}d}{}\Bigr)

for all
d\in Y_{W}
.

Proof.

This is the compactness of the unit sphere in the finite-dimensional space
Y_{W}
. If a nonzero invisible direction exists, the estimate fails. If not, the sum of observation norms is a strictly positive continuous function on the unit sphere, hence has a positive minimum.
∎

The moving-window problem is the question of whether the constants
M_{W}
can be controlled along a sequence of windows
W_{n}
selected from a possible singular branch. Non-effective growth of these constants is itself an obstruction.

5
Realization: excluding formal phantoms

The third audit test is realizability. Many algebraic phantoms are artifacts of the chosen finite-dimensional model. They are not dangerous unless they can be produced by the Navier–Stokes equation.

Definition 5.1

(NS-realizable finite-window direction)
.

A finite-window direction
d\in Y_{W}
is NS-realizable if there exists a sequence of suitable weak solutions, dyadic scales, coarse-graining parameters, and cleaning choices whose projected normalized packages converge to
d
in
Y_{W}
.

The NS-realizability filter has three parts:

(i)

the direction must satisfy the constrained coarse momentum equation;

(ii)

its pressure must be compatible with the active source and retained harmonic pressure;

(iii)

its stress and flux must come from a nonnegative Reynolds covariance and the identity
\Pi=-R:\nabla U
.

This is why the defect package is stronger than a formal envelope. A formal left singular vector of an observation map might be invisible, but it may fail pressure compatibility, positivity, local energy balance, or vertical momentum compatibility. Such a vector is a model phantom, not an NS obstruction.

6
Reproduction: from invisible directions to cascade mechanisms

A single-scale invisible direction is still not enough. A singularity is a scale-recursive phenomenon. The relevant object is therefore a mechanism that reproduces.

Definition 6.1

(PRV mechanism)
.

A finite-window mechanism is called profitable, reproducible, and verified, or PRV, if it has the following properties:

(i)

Verified:
the projected package satisfies the finite-window Navier–Stokes residual constraints up to an error below the window tolerance;

(ii)

Cleaned:
it is not a pressure gauge, cutoff artifact, harmonic leakage artifact, or finite-projection tail;

(iii)

Invisible:
the combined observation strength is below the window tolerance;

(iv)

Profitable:
its ledger profit
\mathrm{Sup}-\mathrm{Tax}
has positive averaged contribution after leakage is removed;

(v)

Reproducible:
the package transfers from one scale to the next with small reproduction residual.

The key point is that a serious bad mechanism cannot be merely static. It must be dynamically profitable and repeatable. In this sense, the singularity problem becomes a recurrence problem in a scale-critical state space.

The one-component degeneration problem is a special but important audit test. It is connected to one-component and one-entry regularity criteria for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations

V=(V_{h},0),\qquad\nabla_{h}\cdot V_{h}=0,

\partial_{t}V_{h}-\Delta V_{h}+(V_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})V_{h}+\nabla_{h}Q=0,\qquad\partial_{3}Q=0.

This is not the usual two-dimensional Navier–Stokes system, because

The pressure topology is the decisive point. One should not demand

p\approx Q+h,\qquad\Delta h(\cdot,t)=0.

Thus the natural excess is measured in the harmonic-pressure quotient:

X^{\mathrm{har}}_{\rho}(u,p;M)=\inf_{(V,Q)}\inf_{h:\Delta h=0}\left[\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|u-V|^{3}\,dx\,dt+\rho^{-2}\int_{Q_{\rho}}|p-Q-h|^{3/2}\,dx\,dt\right].

The quotient is forced by local pressure decomposition: the Calderon–Zygmund part is compact under strong

7.1
Vertical duality as an anti-phantom principle

In the strict-shadow reduction, finite-window trace-cost duality has the following form. Let

A:H\to Y

be the active trace-defect map between finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. For an active residual
g\in Y
, define

\mathrm{Cost}_{\mathrm{tr}}(g)=\inf\{\left\|\xi\right\|_{H}^{2}:A\xi=-g\}.

Then

\mathrm{Cost}_{\mathrm{tr}}(g)^{1/2}=\sup_{A^{*}y\neq 0}\frac{|\left\langle g,y\right\rangle|}{\left\|A^{*}y\right\|_{H}},

with value
+\infty
if
g\notin\Range A
.

Thus the active residual is removable at small trace cost precisely when

|\left\langle g,y\right\rangle|\leq r\left\|A^{*}y\right\|_{H}\qquad\text{for every }y\in Y^{*}.

The vertical-duality target says that residuals produced by the full Navier–Stokes vertical momentum equation do not excite strict phantom quotient directions. This is the one-component version of the broader anti-phantom problem.

Old observables, no-rate branches, and Schur visibility

A second diagnostic result is negative. The standard old observable package–scale quantities, harmonic-pressure quotient distance, covariance stress, unresolved variance, good-time trace functionals, finite-window residuals, and trace cost–does not by itself force a logarithmic or power selected-trace rate. The strict-shadow and Schur-visibility terminology used in this diagnostic layer follows the related one-component modules

In an abstract old trace skeleton, one can construct branches with arbitrarily slow selected-trace convergence while all fixed finite-window residuals vanish. This is not a Navier–Stokes counterexample. It is an insufficiency theorem: old observables alone do not prove the desired quantitative selection estimate.

The obstruction separation is as follows:

(i)

parabolic trace-drop removes elementary high-frequency escape, provided drop times intersect admissible good times;

(ii)

fixed-window analyticity removes ordinary finite-dimensional arbitrary-slow behavior through Lojasiewicz-type control;

(iii)

the surviving obstruction is an all-order finite-mode flat branch with potentially non-summable finite-stage constants;

(iv)

the Schur trace-projectability problem asks whether this residual branch is a true NS obstruction or only a strict-shadow phantom;

(v)

relaxed vertical-pressure visibility and vertical duality are the NS-specific mechanisms expected to eliminate the surviving phantom.

This module explains why the audit needs the realization and anti-phantom filters. Formal invisibility is not enough; the hidden object must be NS-realizable and must remain invisible after all compatibility relations among pressure, flux, energy, and trace are imposed.

10
What remains open

The reduction turns the broad question “Can a singularity occur?” into a shorter list of theorem targets.

Problem 10.1

(Uniform taxation or observable depletion)
.

Show that every NS-realizable critical supply is uniformly taxed, or else produces pressure/flux/energy/trace activity which depletes a finite local budget, up to summable leakage.

Problem 10.2

(Finite-window NS-realizability exclusion)
.

In each controlled finite window, prove that the combined-invisible kernel contains no nonzero direction lying in the closure of NS-realizable cleaned defect packages.

Problem 10.3

(Vertical-duality and anti-phantom closure)
.

For one-component strict-shadow degeneration and for the more general PFET quotient, prove that active residuals generated by the Navier–Stokes equation lie in the trace-defect range with small trace cost. Equivalently, prove the dual estimate

|\left\langle g,y\right\rangle|\leq r\left\|A^{*}y\right\|

for all reduced active dual directions.

Problem 10.4

(Moving-window growth control)
.

Control the growth of finite-window observability, cleaning, pressure-transfer, and trace-cost constants along scale-selected windows strongly enough to prevent non-summable escape.

Problem 10.5

(Critical recurrence diagnostics)
.

Develop diagnostics for the three remaining sustaining mechanisms: vortex-stretching production, active pressure work, and coarse-grained interscale flux. Show either that one of these must be visibly depleting, or that a sign-coherent hidden recurrence can be constructed.

11
The singularity-audit conclusion

The main conclusion is not that singularities have been ruled out. It is that a singularity, if it exists within this framework, must be far more organized than a generic energy or pressure concentration. It must pay through untaxed critical supply or leakage; it must hide from active pressure, flux, energy, and trace observations; it must be realized by the exact structures of the Navier–Stokes equation; and it must reproduce across scales as a profitable recurrence.

Thus the problem has been transformed from

does a Navier–Stokes singularity exist?

into the more structured question

does there exist an NS-realizable, cleaned, combined-invisible, profitable critical recurrence?

A positive regularity route must prove that this final object is impossible. A counterexample route must construct it while preserving pressure compatibility, Reynolds positivity, local energy inequality, and scale-to-scale reproduction. Either direction would be highly informative. The value of the audit framework is that it identifies exactly what remains to be proved or constructed.

12
From singularity audit to local-to-clean transfer

The audit part of the paper identifies the last dangerous object: a cleaned,
NS-realizable, profitable, reproducible, combined-invisible defect cascade. To
make this statement usable, one must compare two finite-window descriptions.
The localized description is the one produced directly from Navier–Stokes: it
contains cutoffs, harmonic pressure tails, projection tails, recentring choices,
and moving-window leakage. The clean description is a finite model in which a
quotient anti-phantom gap can be tested without those local artifacts.

The rest of the paper proves the following transfer principle at the finite-window
level:

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{clean quotient gap}+\text{ quotient lifting}+\text{ detection comparison}+\text{ residual absorption}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{localized non-gauge defects are quantitatively detected.}\end{gathered}}

Thus the first half of the paper answers
what
a surviving obstruction must
look like, while the second half proves
how
a clean anti-phantom gap would
be transported back to localized Navier–Stokes packages. The proof is kept
finite-window throughout: no scale-uniform conclusion is claimed unless the
corresponding compatibility and growth controls are explicitly assumed.

Lemma 14.5

(Pressure mismatch decomposition)
.

Fix a pressure-transfer datum. Let

f_{k,ij}=u_{i}^{(k)}u_{j}^{(k)},\qquad F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}}=U_{k,i}U_{k,j}+R_{k,ij},

and suppose that the normalized localized pressure satisfies on the core

where

Then on the core,

\displaystyle p^{(k)}-p_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle=p_{k}^{\rm harm}+\Bigl[R_{i}R_{j}(\eta_{k}f_{k,ij})-R_{i}R_{j}(f_{k,ij})\Bigr]

\displaystyle+R_{i}R_{j}(f_{k,ij}-F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})+\Bigl[R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},k}^{\mathrm{cl}}R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})\Bigr].

The four terms are, respectively, the harmonic pressure tail, the cutoff–Riesz

Proof.

Starting from the pressure splitting, add and subtract

R_{i}R_{j}(f_{k,ij})\qquad\text{and}\qquad R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})

R_{i}R_{j}(\eta_{k}f_{k,ij})
and

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},k}^{\mathrm{cl}}R_{i}R_{j}(F_{k,ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})

displayed identity. The only PDE input used here is the stated active/harmonic
pressure decomposition on the core; the rest is algebraic bookkeeping.

Definition 14.8

Lemma 14.9

(Boundedness and best approximation of the harmonic projection)
.

The map
\Pi_{{\rm harm},M}
is a bounded linear projection on

\|\Pi_{{\rm harm},M}h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}\leq\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}.

\|(I-\Pi_{{\rm harm},M})h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}=\inf_{q\in\mathcal{H}_{M}(B_{1/2})}\|h-q\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{M}(B_{1/2})
is a closed subspace of the Hilbert space

\Pi_{{\rm harm},M}

idempotent, and have operator norm at most one, which gives the displayed

For the best-approximation identity, decompose

h=\Pi_{{\rm harm},M}h+(I-\Pi_{{\rm harm},M})h,

where the residual is orthogonal to

and

p^{\rm harm}(t,\cdot)
is harmonic in
B_{3/4}
for almost every
t\in I
,
then

\|(I-\Pi_{{\rm harm},M})p^{\rm harm}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(B_{1/2}))}\leq\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{M+1}\|p^{\rm harm}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(B_{3/4}))}.

Proof.

We first prove the spatial estimate. By the standard spherical-harmonic
expansion for
L^{2}
-harmonic functions on a ball,

h(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}h_{\ell}(x)

in
L^{2}(B_{\rho})
for every
\rho<3/4
, where each
h_{\ell}
is a
homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree
\ell
. The summands are
orthogonal in
L^{2}(B_{\rho})
, and homogeneity gives, for

0<\rho\leq 3/4
,

\|h_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}=\left(\frac{\rho}{3/4}\right)^{2\ell+3}\|h_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{3/4})}^{2}.

Set

T_{M}h:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}h_{\ell}.

Then
T_{M}h\in\mathcal{H}_{M}(B_{1/2})
. Since
\Pi_{{\rm harm},M}
is the

L^{2}(B_{1/2})
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{M}(B_{1/2})
,

\|(I-\Pi_{{\rm harm},M})h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}\leq\|h-T_{M}h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}.

Using orthogonality and the preceding scaling identity with
\rho=1/2
,

\displaystyle\|h-T_{M}h\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell>M}\|h_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1/2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell>M}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2\ell+3}\|h_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{3/4})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2M+2}\sum_{\ell>M}\|h_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{3/4})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{2M+2}\|h\|_{L^{2}(B_{3/4})}^{2}.

Taking square roots proves the spatial estimate. Applying this estimate for
almost every
t\in I
to
h=p^{\rm harm}(t,\cdot)
and integrating in time
gives the stated
L^{2}(I;L^{2})
bound.
∎

Under the hypotheses of

Section

14.3
,

\|(I-\Pi_{{\rm harm},M})p^{\rm harm}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\leq|I|^{1/6}|B_{1/2}|^{1/6}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{M+1}\|p^{\rm harm}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(B_{3/4}))}.

Thus, in the fixed normalized geometry, the harmonic-tail contribution to

is controlled by the interior
L^{2}
-size of the harmonic
pressure tail on

, with the explicit factor shown above.

Proof.

Combine

Section

14.3
with the

L^{2}(I;L^{2})
estimate in

Section

14.3
.
∎

Remark 14.18

(Status of the harmonic approximation estimate)
.

Section

14.3
and

Section

14.3
are fixed-geometry
statements on the nested balls
B_{1/2}\Subset B_{3/4}
. The estimate uses
interior harmonicity on

and the default

Lemma 14.19

(Cutoff–Riesz commutator harmonicity)
.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}
satisfy
\eta\equiv 1
on a neighborhood of a spatial
core
B_{\rm core}
. Let
f_{ij}\in L^{3/2}_{\rm loc}
, and assume that

(1-\eta)f_{ij}
is supported away from
B_{\rm core}
. Define

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f):=R_{i}R_{j}(\eta f_{ij})-\eta R_{i}R_{j}(f_{ij}).

Then
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)
is spatially harmonic on
B_{\rm core}
in the
distributional sense.

Proof.

On
B_{\rm core}
,
\eta=1
, hence

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)=R_{i}R_{j}(\eta f_{ij})-R_{i}R_{j}(f_{ij})=-R_{i}R_{j}((1-\eta)f_{ij}).

The distribution
(1-\eta)f_{ij}
is supported away from
B_{\rm core}
.
Therefore, for every test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\rm core})
,

\langle-\Delta\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f),\varphi\rangle=-\langle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}((1-\eta)f_{ij}),\varphi\rangle=0,

because the supports of
(1-\eta)f_{ij}
and
\varphi
are disjoint. Thus

\Delta\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)=0
on
B_{\rm core}
distributionally.
∎

Remark 14.20

(What the commutator lemma does not prove)
.

The lemma proves harmonicity on the separated core, not smallness in any
pressure norm. Norm bounds for
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)
require additional
information about the pressure observation norm, the cutoff separation, and the
source norm. If the clean pressure solve is periodized rather than whole-space,
an additional periodization residual must be included in the pressure budget.

Definition 14.21

(Normalized local pressure geometry)
.

Let

B_{1/2}\subset B_{3/4}\subset B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad A_{3/4,1}:=B_{1}\setminus\overline{B}_{3/4}.

A normalized pressure cutoff is a function

\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and

\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{3/4}
. For a tensor source
f=(f_{ij})
, define

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f):=R_{i}R_{j}(\eta f_{ij})-\eta R_{i}R_{j}(f_{ij}),

with summation over
i,j
.

Lemma 14.22

(Normalized cutoff–Riesz commutator estimate)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a finite time interval, let
\eta
be a normalized
pressure cutoff, and let
f=(f_{ij})
be such that the commutator in

Section

14.3
is defined distributionally.
Set

g_{ij}:=(1-\eta)f_{ij}.

Assume that
g_{ij}
is supported in
A_{3/4,1}
for almost every

t\in I
, and that

g\in L^{3/2}\bigl(I;L^{3/2}(A_{3/4,1})\bigr).

Then
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)
is spatially harmonic on
B_{1/2}
in the
distributional sense for almost every
t\in I
, and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm nlp}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|g_{ij}\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(A_{3/4,1}))}

\displaystyle=C_{\rm nlp}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|(1-\eta)f_{ij}\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(A_{3/4,1}))}.

Here
C_{\rm nlp}<\infty
depends only on the fixed normalized geometry and
the Calderon–Zygmund kernel, not on
f
. The estimate is fixed-scale; no
smallness or scale-uniformity is asserted.

Proof.

On
B_{1/2}
, we have
\eta=1
. Hence, in distributions on

B_{1/2}
,

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)=R_{i}R_{j}(\eta f_{ij})-R_{i}R_{j}(f_{ij})=-R_{i}R_{j}(g_{ij}).

Since
g_{ij}
is supported in
A_{3/4,1}
, its spatial support is
separated from
B_{1/2}
. Therefore the principal-value singularity of the
Riesz kernel is never encountered on
B_{1/2}
. Writing
K_{ij}
for the
whole-space kernel of
R_{i}R_{j}
away from the origin, we have, for

x\in B_{1/2}
,

\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)(x,t)=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{A_{3/4,1}}K_{ij}(x-y)g_{ij}(y,t)\,dy.

The separation gives

d_{0}:=\operatorname{dist}(B_{1/2},A_{3/4,1})=\frac{1}{4},

and hence

M_{K}:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in B_{1/2}\\
y\in A_{3/4,1}\end{subarray}}|K_{ij}(x-y)|<\infty.

It follows that, for almost every
t\in I
,

|\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)(x,t)|\leq M_{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|g_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(A_{3/4,1})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})
norm and using Holder’s inequality on the
annulus,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{1/2}|^{2/3}M_{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|g_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(A_{3/4,1})}

\displaystyle\leq|B_{1/2}|^{2/3}|A_{3/4,1}|^{1/3}M_{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|g_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(A_{3/4,1})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(I)
norm gives the stated bound with

C_{\rm nlp}=|B_{1/2}|^{2/3}|A_{3/4,1}|^{1/3}M_{K}.

It remains to record harmonicity. Let

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1/2})
. Since

\operatorname{supp}g_{ij}(\cdot,t)\cap\operatorname{supp}\varphi=\emptyset
,

\langle-\Delta\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle=-\langle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}g_{ij}(\cdot,t),\varphi\rangle=-\langle g_{ij}(\cdot,t),\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle=0.

Thus
\Delta\mathcal{C}_{\eta}(f)=0
on
B_{1/2}
distributionally for
almost every
t
. The proof uses only the fixed normalized separation and
the annular
L^{3/2}
source norm.
∎

Lemma 14.23

(Normalized active pressure source residual)
.

Work in the normalized geometry of

Section

14.3
, and let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a
finite time interval. Let
f=(f_{ij})
and
F^{\mathrm{cl}}=(F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})
be
tensor sources and set

G_{ij}:=f_{ij}-F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Assume that the zero extension of
G
is supported in
B_{1}
and belongs to

L^{3/2}\bigl(I;L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\bigr).

Then

\|R_{i}R_{j}G_{ij}\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))}\leq C_{\rm CZ}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|G_{ij}\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1}))}.

If the clean active pressure
R_{i}R_{j}F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and the clean pressure
projection
\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
are fixed, define

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm active}:=C_{\rm CZ}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|f_{ij}-F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}}\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1}))}

and

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm proj}:=\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}\bigl(R_{i}R_{j}(F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})\bigr).

Then the active-source and clean-projection mismatch is bounded by

\displaystyle\|R_{i}R_{j}(f_{ij}-F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))}

\displaystyle+\bigl\|R_{i}R_{j}(F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}R_{i}R_{j}(F_{ij}^{\mathrm{cl}})\bigr\|_{L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))}\leq\mathsf{Err}_{\rm active}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm proj}.

Proof.

For
1<p<\infty
, the Calderon–Zygmund operator
R_{i}R_{j}
is bounded on

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Applying this with
p=3/2
at almost every time gives

\|R_{i}R_{j}G_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{\rm CZ}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|G_{ij}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Restricting the left-hand side to
B_{1/2}
, using the support of
G
in

B_{1}
, and then taking the
L^{3/2}(I)
norm proves the first estimate.
The second displayed inequality follows by adding this estimate to the
definition of
\mathsf{Err}_{\rm proj}
. The projection residual is not estimated
away; it remains an explicit pressure error.
∎

Remark 14.24

(Status of the active source estimate)
.

Section

14.3
proves a fixed-scale
Calderon–Zygmund estimate for the active pressure source residual in the

14.4
Clean pressure projection residual

Convention 14.25

(Clean pressure projection model)
.

On a finite time interval
I
, let

Y_{\rm prs}=L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))

be the default pressure observation space from

Section

14.3
. For a finite integer

N\geq 0
, fix a finite-dimensional clean pressure observation space

V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset Y_{\rm prs}

and a linear projection

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{\rm prs}\to V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}},\qquad(\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}})^{2}=\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

The projection is part of the clean pressure model. It removes only the
finite-dimensional clean pressure modes in
V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
; the
remaining component is retained as an observable projection residual.

Lemma 14.26

(Bounded clean pressure projection)
.

For every finite-dimensional subspace

V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset Y_{\rm prs}
, there exists a bounded linear
projection

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{\rm prs}\to V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

In particular, after fixing such a projection, there is a finite constant

C_{{\rm proj},N}<\infty
such that

\|\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\leq C_{{\rm proj},N}\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\qquad\text{for all }q\in Y_{\rm prs}.

Proof.

Because
V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is finite-dimensional, choose a basis

\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{d}
of
V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Let

\ell_{a}^{V}
,
1\leq a\leq d
, be the coordinate functionals on

V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, so that

v=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\ell_{a}^{V}(v)\psi_{a}\qquad\text{for }v\in V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Each
\ell_{a}^{V}
is continuous on the finite-dimensional normed space

V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. By Hahn–Banach, extend
\ell_{a}^{V}
to a bounded
linear functional
\ell_{a}
on
Y_{\rm prs}
. Define

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q:=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\ell_{a}(q)\psi_{a}.

Then
\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q\in V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, and if

q\in V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
then the construction gives

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q=q
. Hence

(\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}})^{2}=\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Finally,

\|\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\leq\sum_{a=1}^{d}\|\ell_{a}\|_{(Y_{\rm prs})^{*}}\|\psi_{a}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}},

which proves boundedness with

C_{{\rm proj},N}:=\sum_{a=1}^{d}\|\ell_{a}\|_{(Y_{\rm prs})^{*}}\|\psi_{a}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

∎

Definition 14.27

(Clean pressure projection residual)
.

Given the clean pressure projection model in

Section

14.4
, define

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}(q):=\|q-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}},\qquad q\in Y_{\rm prs}.

Lemma 14.28

(Well-defined clean projection residual)
.

For every
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
, the quantity

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}(q)
is a finite, well-defined pressure error.
Moreover,

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}(q)\leq(1+C_{{\rm proj},N})\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

Proof.

Since
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
and

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}:Y_{\rm prs}\to Y_{\rm prs}
is bounded,

q-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}q\in Y_{\rm prs}
. Therefore the norm in

Section

14.4
is finite. The displayed bound
follows from the triangle inequality and

Section

14.4
.
∎

Lemma 14.29

(Hilbert clean pressure best approximation)
.

Let

Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}:=L^{2}(I;L^{2}(B_{1/2})).

Suppose
V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}
is finite-dimensional
and
\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2}
is the

Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}
-orthogonal projection onto

V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Then for every
q\in Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}
,

\|q-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}}=\inf_{v\in V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}}\|q-v\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}}.

Proof.

The Hilbert-space projection theorem gives the orthogonal decomposition

q=\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2}q+(I-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2})q,

where the residual is orthogonal to
V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. For

v\in V_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, Pythagoras gives

\|q-v\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}}^{2}=\|(I-\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2})q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}}^{2}+\|\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2}q-v\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{(2)}}^{2}.

The infimum is attained at

v=\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl},2}q
, proving the identity.
∎

Remark 14.30

(Banach status of the clean pressure projection residual)
.

The default pressure observation norm is

Y_{\rm prs}=L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2}))
, which is a Banach norm rather than
the Hilbert norm used in

Section

14.4
. In the default

Y_{\rm prs}
setting, the manuscript uses only boundedness of

\mathsf{P}_{{\rm prs},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
and the residual norm

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. No best-approximation identity, smallness of

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm proj},N}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, or scale-uniform truncation statement is claimed

14.5
Pressure mean normalization residual

Convention 14.31

(Default pressure mean normalization)
.

In the normalized pressure observation space

Y_{\rm prs}=L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})),

the default pressure representative is the one with zero spatial mean over the
observation core
B_{1/2}
at almost every time. For

q\in Y_{\rm prs}
, write

\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t):=\frac{1}{|B_{1/2}|}\int_{B_{1/2}}q(x,t)\,dx

and define the mean-normalization map

\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}q:=q-\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}.

Here
\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}
is regarded as a function of
(x,t)
that
is constant in
x
on
B_{1/2}
.

Lemma 14.32

(Bounded pressure mean residual)
.

For every
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
, the mean residual

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm mean}(q):=\|\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}

is well defined and satisfies

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm mean}(q)\leq\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

Moreover,
\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}
is a bounded projection onto the closed
subspace of
Y_{\rm prs}
consisting of functions with zero spatial mean over

B_{1/2}
for almost every
t
, and

\|\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\leq 2\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

Proof.

For almost every
t\in I
, Holder’s inequality gives

|\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)|\leq|B_{1/2}|^{-2/3}\|q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}.

Since
\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)
is constant in
x
, its spatial

L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})
norm is

\|\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}=|B_{1/2}|^{2/3}|\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)|.

Combining the two displays yields

\|\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}\leq\|q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(I)
norm proves

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm mean}(q)\leq\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}
.

The identity

\langle\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}=0

shows that
\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}
maps into the zero-mean subspace, and if

q
already has zero mean then
\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}q=q
. Thus

\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}^{2}=\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}
. The triangle inequality and
the mean estimate give

\|\mathsf{Z}_{\rm mean}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}\leq\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm mean}(q)\leq 2\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

Finally, the zero-mean subspace is the kernel of the bounded mean map, and is
therefore closed.
∎

Lemma 14.33

(Vanishing of the mean residual under consistent normalization)
.

If
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
is already normalized so that

\int_{B_{1/2}}q(x,t)\,dx=0\qquad\text{for almost every }t\in I,

then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm mean}(q)=0.

Proof.

The stated normalization is precisely

\langle q\rangle_{B_{1/2}}(t)=0
for almost every
t
. Hence the
constant-in-space mean function vanishes in
Y_{\rm prs}
.
∎

Remark 14.34

(Status of the pressure mean residual)
.

Section

14.5
is a fixed-core normalization
statement in the default pressure observation norm. It does not estimate any
nonconstant pressure component, does not prove smallness of the pressure, and
does not provide scale-uniform control under dyadic rescaling. It only records
the cost of changing pressure representatives by a time-dependent spatial
constant on
B_{1/2}
.

14.6
Pressure periodization residual

Convention 14.35

(Default pressure periodization model)
.

Fix a unit torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
containing a copy of the normalized core
B_{1/2}

inside one coordinate chart. Let

Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}:=L^{3/2}(I;L^{3/2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

The default pressure periodization map is the zero-extension operator

\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}:Y_{\rm prs}\to Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per},

defined by

(\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q)(x,t)=\begin{cases}q(x,t),&x\in B_{1/2},\\
0,&x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\setminus B_{1/2}.\end{cases}

This is only a fixed pressure-observation periodization convention. It is not
a claim that the zero extension is a smooth periodic pressure or that it solves
a periodic pressure equation.

Lemma 14.36

(Bounded pressure periodization)
.

The map

\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}:Y_{\rm prs}\to Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}

is linear and bounded, with

\|\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}}=\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}.

Proof.

Linearity is immediate from the definition. For almost every
t
,
zero-extension gives

\|\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\|q(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{1/2})}.

Taking the
L^{3/2}(I)
norm proves the identity and hence boundedness.
∎

Definition 14.37

(Pressure periodization residual)
.

Given a local pressure observation
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
and a clean periodic
pressure representative
q^{\rm per}\in Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}
, define

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm per}(q,q^{\rm per}):=\|\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q-q^{\rm per}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}}.

Lemma 14.38

(Well-defined periodization residual)
.

For
q\in Y_{\rm prs}
and
q^{\rm per}\in Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}
, the
quantity
\mathsf{Err}_{\rm per}(q,q^{\rm per})
is a finite, well-defined pressure
error. Moreover,

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm per}(q,q^{\rm per})\leq\|q\|_{Y_{\rm prs}}+\|q^{\rm per}\|_{Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}}.

If the clean periodic representative is chosen to be

q^{\rm per}=\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q
, then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm per}(q,q^{\rm per})=0.

Proof.

Boundedness of
\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}
gives

\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q\in Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}
, so the difference

\mathsf{Per}_{\rm prs}q-q^{\rm per}
belongs to
Y_{\rm prs}^{\rm per}
.
The displayed estimate is the triangle inequality together with

Section

14.6
. The vanishing statement follows

14.7
Energy–flux localization leakage

Convention 14.45

(Normalized energy–flux cutoff)
.

For the energy–flux localization leakage model, set

Q_{1}:=B_{1}\times(-1,0),\qquad Q_{1/2}:=B_{1/2}\times(-1/4,0).

Fix a cutoff

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1}),\qquad 0\leq\chi\leq 1,\qquad\chi\equiv 1\ \text{on }Q_{1/2},

and define the fixed cutoff constant

C_{\chi}:=\|\partial_{t}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|\Delta\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}+\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}.

This convention is fixed-scale and normalized. No scale-uniform family of
cutoffs is asserted here.

Definition 14.46

(Energy–flux localization leakage errors)
.

For a velocity-pressure pair
(u,p)
on
Q_{1}
, define

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm en}(u):=\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{2}\bigl(|\partial_{t}\chi|+|\Delta\chi|\bigr),

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm flux}(u):=\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{2}|u\cdot\nabla\chi|,

and

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm prs}(u,p):=\int_{Q_{1}}|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(t)|\,|u|\,|\nabla\chi|,

where

(p)_{B_{1}}(t):=\frac{1}{|B_{1}|}\int_{B_{1}}p(x,t)\,dx.

Lemma 14.47

(Fixed-scale energy–flux leakage estimates)
.

Let
u\in L^{3}(Q_{1})\cap L^{2}(Q_{1})
, and let

p-(p)_{B_{1}}(\cdot)\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1}).

Then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm en}(u)\leq C_{\chi}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}^{2},

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm flux}(u)\leq C_{\chi}\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{3},

and

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm prs}(u,p)\leq C_{\chi}\|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}.

Proof.

The energy leakage estimate follows directly from the definition and the
boundedness of
\partial_{t}\chi
and
\Delta\chi
:

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm en}(u)\leq\bigl(\|\partial_{t}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Delta\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigr)\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{2}\leq C_{\chi}\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}^{2}.

For the flux leakage,

|u|^{2}|u\cdot\nabla\chi|\leq\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}|u|^{3},

and integration over
Q_{1}
gives the claimed bound. Finally, Holder’s
inequality with exponents
3/2
and
3
gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm prs}(u,p)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{Q_{1}}|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(t)|\,|u|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}\|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}.

∎

Definition 14.48

(Normalized CKN quantities on
Q_{1}
)
.

On
Q_{1}=B_{1}\times(-1,0)
, set

A:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{-1<t<0}\int_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx,

C:=\int_{Q_{1}}|u|^{3},\qquad D:=\int_{Q_{1}}|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(t)|^{3/2}.

Corollary 14.49

(CKN-normalized leakage bounds)
.

Under the hypotheses of

Section

14.7
,

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm en}(u)\leq C_{\chi}A,

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm flux}(u)\leq C_{\chi}C,

and

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm prs}(u,p)\leq C_{\chi}C^{1/3}D^{2/3}.

Proof.

Since the normalized time interval has length one,

\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}^{2}=\int_{-1}^{0}\int_{B_{1}}|u(x,t)|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq A.

The flux estimate is exactly

\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{3}=C.

For the pressure leakage, use

\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}=C^{1/3},\qquad\|p-(p)_{B_{1}}(\cdot)\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}=D^{2/3}.

Substitution into

Section

14.7
proves the three bounds.
∎

14.8
Momentum residual localization leakage

Convention 14.51

(Default momentum residual test norm)
.

For the momentum localization residual, use the same normalized cylinder

Q_{1}
and cutoff
\chi
as in

Section

14.7
. The default test norm is

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm mom}}:=\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})},\qquad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3}).

For a vector-valued distribution
T

\|T\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm mom}^{*}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm mom}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\langle T,\varphi\rangle|.

This is a fixed-scale negative norm. It is chosen only to record the
distributional size of cutoff-generated momentum errors.

Definition 14.52

(Cutoff momentum residual)
.

Let

p^{\circ}(x,t):=p(x,t)-(p)_{B_{1}}(t).

For a velocity-pressure pair
(u,p)
on
Q_{1}
, define the cutoff momentum
residual

\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm mom}(u,p):=\partial_{t}(\chi u)-\Delta(\chi u)+\nabla\cdot(\chi\,u\otimes u)+\nabla(\chi p^{\circ})

in the sense of distributions on
Q_{1}
. Its localization leakage size is

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm mom}(u,p):=\|\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm mom}(u,p)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm mom}^{*}}.

Lemma 14.53

(Cutoff momentum residual identity)
.

Assume that
(u,p)
satisfies the incompressible Navier–Stokes momentum
equation

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0

distributionally on
Q_{1}
, with

u\in L^{2}(-1,0;H^{1}(B_{1}))\cap L^{3}(Q_{1}),\qquad p^{\circ}\in L^{3/2}(Q_{1}).

Then, for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm mom}(u,p),\varphi\rangle=\int_{Q_{1}}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\chi-\Delta\chi)\,u\cdot\varphi-2\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{j}\chi)\,\partial_{j}u\cdot\varphi

\displaystyle+\bigl((u\otimes u)\nabla\chi\bigr)\cdot\varphi+p^{\circ}\,\nabla\chi\cdot\varphi.

Proof.

Since
p^{\circ}=p-(p)_{B_{1}}(t)
, one has

\nabla p^{\circ}=\nabla p
. Expanding the localized residual by the product
rule gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm mom}(u,p)=

\displaystyle\chi\bigl(\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(u\otimes u)+\nabla p^{\circ}\bigr)

\displaystyle+(\partial_{t}\chi-\Delta\chi)u-2\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\partial_{j}\chi)\partial_{j}u+(u\otimes u)\nabla\chi+p^{\circ}\nabla\chi.

The term on the first line vanishes distributionally by the momentum equation.
The remaining terms belong to
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
under the stated hypotheses,
so pairing with
\varphi
gives the displayed identity.
∎

Lemma 14.54

(Fixed-scale momentum residual leakage estimate)
.

Under the hypotheses of

Section

14.8
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}_{\rm loc}^{\rm mom}(u,p)\leq C_{\chi}\Bigl(\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}+\|p^{\circ}\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\Bigr).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. By

Section

14.8
,

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm mom}(u,p),\varphi\rangle|\leq C_{\chi}(

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle+\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}+\|p^{\circ}\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}).

The first two terms use Cauchy’s inequality. The last two terms use Holder’s
inequality with exponents
3/2
and
3
. If

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm mom}}\leq 1
, the right-hand side is bounded by
the displayed quantity. Taking the supremum over such
\varphi
proves the
estimate.
∎

,

, and

14.10
Truncation leakage

Convention 14.61

(Finite-window truncation datum)
.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}
, fix a Banach observation space

\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}

and a finite-dimensional clean truncation space

V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}.

The object to be truncated is denoted by

z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\in\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}.

This datum is finite-window and norm-level. It does not specify a Fourier
basis, does not impose a dyadic tail estimate, and does not assert that
truncation errors decay with
N
.

Lemma 14.62

(Bounded finite-dimensional truncation projection)
.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}
, there exists a bounded linear projection

\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}:\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}\to V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Proof.

Fix
k
. Since
V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is finite-dimensional, choose a
basis
e_{1,k},\dots,e_{m_{k},k}
. The coordinate functionals on

V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
are continuous because all norms on a
finite-dimensional space are equivalent. By Hahn–Banach, extend these
coordinate functionals to bounded linear functionals

\ell_{1,k},\dots,\ell_{m_{k},k}
on
\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}
. Define

\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}y:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m_{k}}\ell_{\alpha,k}(y)e_{\alpha,k}.

This map is bounded and linear. If
y\in V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, the
coordinate representation gives

\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}y=y
. Hence
\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}
is a
projection onto
V_{{\rm tr},N,k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.
∎

Definition 14.63

(Truncation residual)
.

Given the bounded projections from

Section

14.10
, define the
single-scale truncation residual

\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm tr},k}(\mathfrak{D}):=\|z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})-\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}.

For nonnegative finite-window truncation weights
\omega_{{\rm tr},k}\geq 0
,
define

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm tr}(\mathfrak{D}):=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}}\omega_{{\rm tr},k}\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm tr},k}(\mathfrak{D}).

Lemma 14.64

(Finite-window truncation leakage bound)
.

Let

\mathcal{B}_{\rm tr}^{\rm norm}(\mathfrak{D}):=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}}\omega_{{\rm tr},k}\bigl(1+\|\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}\bigr)\|z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}.

Then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm tr}(\mathfrak{D})\leq\mathcal{B}_{\rm tr}^{\rm norm}(\mathfrak{D}).

Proof.

For each
k
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}_{{\rm tr},k}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle=\|z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})-\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}

\displaystyle\leq\|z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}+\|\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1+\|\mathsf{T}_{{\rm tr},N,k}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}\to\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}\bigr)\|z_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{{\rm tr},k}}.

Multiplying by
\omega_{{\rm tr},k}\geq 0
and summing over the finite window
gives the result.
∎

Remark 14.65

(Status of truncation leakage)
.

Section

14.10
is a norm-level
finite-window truncation estimate. It proves that the truncation residual is

operator norm. It does not prove that

is small, does not

T
, set

\|T\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm nl}^{*}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm nl}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}|\langle T,\varphi\rangle|.

The gradient term is part of the norm because the mismatch is measured after
taking a divergence.

Definition 14.67

(Nonlinear cutoff-flux mismatch)
.

Define

\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm nl}(u):=\nabla\cdot\left((\chi u)\otimes(\chi u)-\chi\,u\otimes u\right)

as a distribution on
Q_{1}
. Its fixed-scale mismatch size is

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(u):=\|\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm nl}(u)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm nl}^{*}}.

Lemma 14.68

(Fixed-scale nonlinear cutoff-flux mismatch)
.

If
u\in L^{3}(Q_{1})
, then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(u)\leq C_{\chi}\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}.

In normalized CKN variables, this is

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(u)\leq C_{\chi}C^{2/3}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{1};\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Since

(\chi u)\otimes(\chi u)-\chi\,u\otimes u=\chi(\chi-1)u\otimes u,

distributional integration by parts gives

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{R}_{\chi}^{\rm nl}(u),\varphi\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q_{1}}\chi(\chi-1)(u\otimes u):\nabla\varphi\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi(\chi-1)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\|u\otimes u\|_{L^{3/2}(Q_{1})}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm nl}}.

Taking the supremum over

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\rm nl}}\leq 1
proves the first estimate. The
normalized form follows from
\|u\|_{L^{3}(Q_{1})}^{2}=C^{2/3}
.
∎

Definition 14.69

(Finite-window nonlinear mismatch budget)
.

For nonnegative finite-window weights
\omega_{{\rm nl},k}^{\rm cut}\geq 0
,
define

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(\mathfrak{D}):=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}}\omega_{{\rm nl},k}^{\rm cut}\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(u_{k}),

and

\mathcal{B}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(\mathfrak{D}):=C_{\chi}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}}\omega_{{\rm nl},k}^{\rm cut}C_{k}^{2/3}.

Lemma 14.70

(Finite-window nonlinear cutoff budget)
.

If every normalized component satisfies
u_{k}\in L^{3}(Q_{1})
, then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(\mathfrak{D})\leq\mathcal{B}_{\rm nl}^{\rm cut}(\mathfrak{D}).

Proof.

Apply
Section

14.11
to each
normalized component, multiply by the nonnegative weights, and sum over the
finite scale window.
∎

Remark 14.71

(Status of the nonlinear cutoff-flux mismatch)

Definition 14.72

For each

This models only the finite-window remainder left after the already estimated

Lemma 14.73

(Finite-dimensional nonlinear remainder bound)
.

Under
Section

14.11
, set

14.12
Reproduction drift budget

Definition 14.75

(Abstract finite-window reproduction maps)
.

Let

\Lambda_{\rm adj}:=\{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}:k+1\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}\}

be the adjacent-scale index set. For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}
, fix
finite-dimensional normed local and clean component spaces

\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\quad\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}},

bounded linear local-to-clean component charts

\Theta_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}},\qquad\Theta_{k+1}:\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}},

and bounded linear reproduction maps

R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

This is the default abstract finite-window reproduction model. The bounded
linearity of the component charts is part of the datum and is used in the drift
identity below. The definition does not assert that either reproduction map is
generated by dyadic Navier–Stokes rescaling.

Definition 14.76

(Localized and clean reproduction residuals)
.

For a finite-window localized package

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{loc}}=(D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}})_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}},\qquad D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\in\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}},

define

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm loc}(\mathfrak{D})^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}}^{2}.

The clean reproduction residual of the charted package is

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}.

Definition 14.77

(Chart-commutation drift)
.

For
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}
, define

\displaystyle{\rm Drift}_{k}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle:=\Theta_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}-\Theta_{k+1}\bigl(D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{k+1}R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}.

The reproduction drift budget is

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}(\mathfrak{D}):=\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|{\rm Drift}_{k}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 14.78

(Finite-window reproduction residual comparison)
.

Let

C_{\rm rep}:=\max_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}},

with
C_{\rm rep}=0
if
\Lambda_{\rm adj}
is empty. Then

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})\leq C_{\rm rep}\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm loc}(\mathfrak{D})+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}(\mathfrak{D}).

Proof.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}
, the definition of the drift gives

\displaystyle\Theta_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\Theta_{k+1}\bigl(D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)+{\rm Drift}_{k}(\mathfrak{D}).

Taking the
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{\rm adj})
norm of these clean residuals and
using the triangle inequality gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}(D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm rep}\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}(\mathfrak{D}).

The last displayed expression is

C_{\rm rep}\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm loc}(\mathfrak{D})+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}(\mathfrak{D})
.
∎

Remark 14.79

(Status of reproduction drift)
.

Section

14.12
is an algebraic
finite-window comparison for abstract reproduction maps. It does not assert
that
R_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
or
R_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is the dyadic Navier–Stokes rescaling
map, does not prove that the chart-commutation drift is small, and does not

Convention 14.80

(Normalized adjacent dyadic rescaling)
.

In this subsection only, fix adjacent radii

r_{k+1}=2^{-1}r_{k}.

If
(y,s)
denotes normalized coordinates at scale
k+1
, then the
corresponding normalized coordinates at scale
k
are

\iota_{k\to k+1}(y,s)=(2^{-1}y,2^{-2}s).

Equivalently, the inverse expansion from the
k
-window coordinates to the

(k+1)
-window coordinates is

. For a
velocity–pressure representative
(u,p)
at scale

\mathcal{Z}_{k\to k+1}(u,p)(y,s)=\left(2^{-1}u(2^{-1}y,2^{-2}s),2^{-2}p(2^{-1}y,2^{-2}s)\right).

This convention fixes only the model-level adjacent rescaling. It does not
assert that the finite-window defect coordinates are generated by an actual
Navier–Stokes solution across scales.

Definition 14.81

(Local and clean dyadic shift operators)
.

A dyadic shift reproduction datum is the abstract reproduction datum of

Section

14.12
together with the
rescaling convention of
Section

14.13
and
bounded linear operators

S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}},\qquad k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}.

Definition 14.82

(Dyadic reproduction residual and drift)
.

For a finite-window localized package

\mathfrak{D}^{\mathrm{loc}}=(D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}})_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm sc}}
, define the local
dyadic reproduction residual by

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm loc}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}}^{2}.

The corresponding clean dyadic residual of the charted package is

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}^{\rm dy}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}.

The dyadic chart-commutation drift is

{\rm Drift}_{k}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D}):=\Theta_{k+1}S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}=(\Theta_{k+1}S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k})D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}.

Set

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D}):=\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|{\rm Drift}_{k}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 14.83

(Finite-window dyadic reproduction comparison)
.

For a fixed dyadic shift reproduction datum, let

C_{\Theta,{\rm dy}}:=\max_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}},

with
C_{\Theta,{\rm dy}}=0
if
\Lambda_{\rm adj}
is empty. Then

\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}^{\rm dy}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})\leq C_{\Theta,{\rm dy}}\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm loc}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D}).

Proof.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}
, add and subtract

\Theta_{k+1}S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. This gives

\displaystyle\Theta_{k+1}D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\Theta_{k+1}\bigl(D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\bigr)+{\rm Drift}_{k}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D}).

Taking the
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{\rm adj})
norm and using the triangle
inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{Rep}_{\rm cl}^{\rm dy}(\Theta\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|\Theta_{k+1}(D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Theta,{\rm dy}}\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|D_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}-S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{loc}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D}).

The last expression is the asserted bound.
∎

Definition 14.85

Lemma 14.86

(Finite-dimensional dyadic drift bound)
.

Assume that the component spaces

\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}
are finite-dimensional normed
spaces and that the maps

\Theta_{k},\quad\Theta_{k+1},\quad S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\quad S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}

appearing in the dyadic shift datum are bounded linear maps on their stated
domains. Then, for every
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}
,

\|K_{k}^{\rm dy}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}<\infty.

Consequently, if

C_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}:=\max_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|K_{k}^{\rm dy}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}},

with
C_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}=0
when

is empty, then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})\leq C_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}\left(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}}\|D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The operator
K_{k}^{\rm dy}
is the difference of the two linear maps

\Theta_{k+1}S_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad S_{k}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Each composition is bounded because it is a composition of bounded linear maps.
Hence
K_{k}^{\rm dy}
is a bounded linear map. Since the spaces are
finite-dimensional, its operator norm is finite.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{\rm adj}

gives

\|{\rm Drift}_{k}^{\rm dy}(\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}=\|K_{k}^{\rm dy}D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}}\leq C_{\rm rep}^{\rm dy}\|D_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{k}^{\mathrm{loc}}}.

Squaring this inequality, summing over

The localized residual map
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
measures projected
divergence, momentum, pressure, ledger, and gluing compatibility. The cleaning

G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}

removes only chosen cutoff, harmonic-gauge, recentring, coarse-graining, and

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})=\inf_{a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}\|\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}.

The reproduction residual and profit functional are denoted by

\mathsf{Rep}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and

.

15
Local-to-Clean Charts

15.1
Chart datum

Definition 15.1

(Local-to-clean chart datum)
.

A local-to-clean chart datum consists of the following finite-window choices:

(i)

a core restriction operator;

(ii)

a cutoff-flattening convention;

(iii)

a harmonic-gauge projection;

(iv)

a periodization operator;

(v)

a clean Fourier/time projection;

(vi)

coordinate extraction maps into
\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
.

A chart datum also fixes a chart domain

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}

and finite-dimensional normed intermediate spaces so that these operations
compose. We write the successive maps schematically as

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}}X_{2}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}}X_{3}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}}X_{4}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}}X_{5}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}}\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Here
\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}
denotes core restriction,

\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}
cutoff flattening,
\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}
harmonic-gauge
projection,
\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}
periodization,

\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
clean finite-window projection, and

\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}
clean coordinate extraction. Each map is part of the
chosen datum and is assumed continuous on its stated domain. The resulting
chart is

\Theta_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}},\qquad\Theta_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}.

The datum is called linear if all six component maps are linear.

Lemma 15.2

(Bounded chart map)
.

For a fixed local-to-clean chart datum, the map

\Theta_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is a
well-defined finite-dimensional map on its chart domain. If the chart datum
is linear and
\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, then

\Theta_{\Lambda}
is a bounded linear map. More precisely, there is a
finite constant
C_{\Theta,\Lambda}<\infty
, depending only on the fixed
chart datum and the chosen norms, such that

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq C_{\Theta,\Lambda}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}\qquad\text{for all }\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}.

Proof.

The chart datum includes the chart domain, the intermediate spaces, and the
component maps with compatible domains and codomains. Therefore the
composition

\Theta_{\Lambda}=\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}

is defined for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, and its values
lie in
\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. This proves well-definedness.

Assume now that the datum is linear and that

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. Since all spaces in the
finite-window chart are finite-dimensional normed spaces, every linear
component map has a finite operator norm. Let

M_{R}=\|\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}\|,\quad M_{F}=\|\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}\|,\quad M_{H}=\|\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}\|,\quad M_{\rm Per}=\|\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}\|,\quad M_{P}=\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\|,\quad M_{E}=\|\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}\|.

Then, for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathsf{E}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\mathsf{Per}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{H}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{F}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{R}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}

\displaystyle\leq M_{E}M_{P}M_{\rm Per}M_{H}M_{F}M_{R}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

Thus the estimate holds with

C_{\Theta,\Lambda}=M_{E}M_{P}M_{\rm Per}M_{H}M_{F}M_{R}<\infty.

The composition of linear maps is linear, so
\Theta_{\Lambda}
is a bounded
linear map. This proves only boundedness of the fixed chart; it does not
prove quotient-distance stability, near-isometry, or small localized error.
∎

15.2
Stable chart hypotheses

Assumption 15.3

(Stable chart bound)
.

There are constants
C_{\Theta,\Lambda}<\infty
,

\varepsilon_{\rm chart}\geq 0
, and
\Delta_{\rm chart}\geq 0
such that, for
all localized packages in the chart domain,

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq C_{\Theta,\Lambda}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

When a perturbative normalization is available, this may be strengthened to

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart})\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}+\Delta_{\rm chart}.

Lemma 15.4

(Bounded chart estimates and perturbative normalization)
.

Assume the chart datum is linear and

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. Then the first estimate in

Section

15.2
holds with the constant

C_{\Theta,\Lambda}
obtained in
Section

15.1
. The sharper
perturbative estimate

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart})\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}+\Delta_{\rm chart}

is an additional structural input unless it is proved from the concrete chart
geometry. In particular, on a full vector-space chart domain, if the above
estimate holds for all
\mathfrak{D}
, then

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\|_{\mathrm{loc}\to\mathrm{cl}}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart}.

Proof.

The bounded estimate is precisely the conclusion of

Section

15.1
. It gives

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq C_{\Theta,\Lambda}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}

for all
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Now suppose that the perturbative estimate with additive constant

\Delta_{\rm chart}
holds on the full vector space

\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. Fix any nonzero direction

\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and apply the estimate to

t\mathfrak{D}
,
t>0
. Since the chart is linear,

t\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}=\|\Theta_{\Lambda}(t\mathfrak{D})\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart})t\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}+\Delta_{\rm chart}.

Dividing by
t
and sending
t\to\infty
gives

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{cl}}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart})\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

Taking the supremum over
\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}=1
yields

\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\|_{\mathrm{loc}\to\mathrm{cl}}\leq 1+\varepsilon_{\rm chart}.

Finite-dimensional boundedness alone only gives the finite constant

C_{\Theta,\Lambda}
; it does not imply that this constant is close to

1
. Thus the perturbative normalization is a genuine structural
hypothesis unless supplied by a separate argument.
∎

15.3
Gauge compatibility defect

The chart must also be compared with the chosen local and clean cleaning
spaces. Fix a clean finite-window cleaning map

G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}:\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

For every localized cleaning direction
a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
such that

G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, define the gauge compatibility
defect by

\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a):=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}\left(\Theta_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a,\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\right).

(15.1)

Thus
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a)
measures how much of a localized cleaning direction
survives as a non-clean direction after applying the local-to-clean chart. A
perfectly compatible chart would satisfy
\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a)=0
for all such

a
.

Define the unit-scale gauge compatibility constant

\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\\
\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}\leq 1\\
G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a).

(15.2)

Lemma 15.5

(Finite gauge compatibility defect)
.

Assume that the chart datum is continuous on its chart domain, that

G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and
G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
are finite-dimensional linear
cleaning maps, and that the set

B_{\rm gauge}:=\left\{a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\|a\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}\leq 1,\ G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\right\}

is closed in
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. Then

\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}<\infty
. If
B_{\rm gauge}
is nonempty,
the supremum in (
15.2
) is attained.

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is finite-dimensional. Therefore the
closed unit ball is compact, and the closed subset
B_{\rm gauge}
is compact.
The localized cleaning map
G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is continuous, the chart

\Theta_{\Lambda}
is continuous on its chart domain, and the clean cleaning
image
\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is a finite-dimensional linear subspace of

\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, hence closed. The distance to a closed subspace is a
continuous function. Consequently
a\mapsto\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a)
is continuous
on
B_{\rm gauge}
.

If
B_{\rm gauge}
is empty, the supremum is taken over the empty set and the
constant may be set to
0
by convention. If
B_{\rm gauge}
is nonempty,
the extreme value theorem gives a finite maximum of
\Gamma_{\Lambda}
on

B_{\rm gauge}
. This proves finiteness and attainment.
∎

Remark 15.6

(Status of gauge compatibility)
.

The constant
\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}
is only a finite-window defect
constant. The lemma does not prove that it is small, scale-uniform, or
absorbable into the localized transfer budget. Smallness of this quantity is a
separate structural input.

Definition 15.7

(Finite-dimensional gauge mismatch operator)
.

In the linear finite-window gauge datum, assume

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, the chart is linear, and the
clean image
\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
is equipped with the quotient norm on

\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Let

Q_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}:\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}

be the quotient map. Define

K_{\rm gauge}:=Q_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}:\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}.

Then, for each localized cleaning direction
a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a)=\|K_{\rm gauge}a\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}}.

Lemma 15.8

(Finite-dimensional selected gauge mismatch bound)
.

Assume the linear finite-window gauge datum of

Section

15.3
. Then

\|K_{\rm gauge}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}}<\infty.

If, in addition, a bounded linear selected-cleaning map

A_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}

is fixed and

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}^{\rm sel}(\mathfrak{D}):=\Gamma_{\Lambda}(A_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}),

then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}^{\rm sel}(\mathfrak{D})\leq C_{\rm gauge}^{\rm fd}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad C_{\rm gauge}^{\rm fd}:=\|K_{\rm gauge}\|\,\|A_{\Lambda}\|.

Proof.

The maps
G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,
\Theta_{\Lambda}
, and
Q_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
are
linear on finite-dimensional normed spaces in the stated datum. Therefore the
composition

K_{\rm gauge}=Q_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}

is a linear map between finite-dimensional normed spaces, and hence has finite
operator norm.

The quotient norm is by definition the distance to the clean gauge image:

\|Q_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}d\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}/\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}}=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(d,\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}).

Taking
d=\Theta_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a
gives

\|K_{\rm gauge}a\|=\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\Theta_{\Lambda}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a,\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}})=\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a).

For the selected cleaning
a=A_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}
, boundedness of

K_{\rm gauge}
and
A_{\Lambda}
gives

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}^{\rm sel}(\mathfrak{D})=\|K_{\rm gauge}A_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}\|\leq\|K_{\rm gauge}\|\,\|A_{\Lambda}\|\,\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

This is the claimed finite-dimensional selected-gauge bound.
∎

Remark 15.9

(Status of the selected gauge bound)
.

Section

15.3
bounds only the
gauge mismatch generated by a chosen selected-cleaning map. It does not prove
that the full detection error
\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}
is equal to

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}^{\rm sel}
, does not prove smallness, does not give
scale-uniform control, and does not absorb the gauge term into

\eta_{\Lambda}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}+\Delta_{\Lambda}
.

The relation between the finite gauge defect and the later error budget is not
automatic. The following elementary absorption lemma records one clean way in
which the constant
\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}
may enter the normalized
gauge error. It is included only to keep the bookkeeping honest; the
hypotheses below are structural inputs, not consequences of finite-dimensionality.

Lemma 15.10

(Gauge defect absorption into the normalized budget)
.

Assume that the chart domain is compatible with localized cleanings and that
there are constants
L_{\rm gauge,\Lambda},M_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}\geq 0
and a
choice of localized cleaning parameter
a_{\mathfrak{D}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}

for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
such that

G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a_{\mathfrak{D}}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\|a_{\mathfrak{D}}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}\leq L_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+M_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}.

Assume also that the gauge contribution in the detection comparison satisfies

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}(\mathfrak{D})\leq\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a_{\mathfrak{D}})+\eta_{\rm gauge}^{0}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+\Delta_{\rm gauge}^{0}.

If, in addition, the gauge defect is homogeneous along the selected cleaning
parameters, so that

\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a_{\mathfrak{D}})\leq\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}\|a_{\mathfrak{D}}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}},

then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}(\mathfrak{D})\leq\eta_{\rm gauge}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+\Delta_{\rm gauge},

where

\eta_{\rm gauge}=\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}L_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}+\eta_{\rm gauge}^{0},\qquad\Delta_{\rm gauge}=\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}M_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}+\Delta_{\rm gauge}^{0}.

Proof.

The assumptions give

\displaystyle\mathsf{Err}_{\rm gauge}(\mathfrak{D})

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{\Lambda}(a_{\mathfrak{D}})+\eta_{\rm gauge}^{0}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+\Delta_{\rm gauge}^{0}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}\|a_{\mathfrak{D}}\|_{\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}}+\eta_{\rm gauge}^{0}\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+\Delta_{\rm gauge}^{0}

\displaystyle\leq(\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}L_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}+\eta_{\rm gauge}^{0})\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})+\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}M_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}+\Delta_{\rm gauge}^{0}.

This is exactly the claimed normalized gauge-error bound.
∎

Remark 15.11

(Gauge constants versus quotient lifting)
.

The constants
\delta_{\rm gauge,\Lambda}
,
\eta_{\rm gauge}
, and

\delta_{G}
play different roles. The first measures how localized cleaning
directions survive under the chart; the second is the part of the detection
error budget assigned to that mismatch; the third is an additive loss in the
quotient-distance comparison. None of them is automatically small. In a
concrete localized Navier–Stokes model one must prove separate estimates
showing that they are either absorbable in
\eta_{\Lambda}
or harmless inside

\Delta_{\Lambda}^{\prime}
.

Assumption 15.12

(Quotient-lifting stability)
.

There are constants
0\leq\varepsilon_{G}<1
and
\delta_{G}\geq 0
such that the
following lifting property holds. For every

\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and every

b\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
, there exists
a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
with

\|\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\|_{\mathrm{loc}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}b\|_{\mathrm{cl}}+\frac{\delta_{G}}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}.

Lemma 15.13

(Quotient-distance comparison from lifting)
.

Assume
Section

15.3
. Then every

\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
satisfies

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}})\geq(1-\varepsilon_{G})\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})-\delta_{G}.

Proof.

Fix
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and

b\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. By

Section

15.3
, there is

a\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
such that

\|\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}a\|_{\mathrm{loc}}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}b\|_{\mathrm{cl}}+\frac{\delta_{G}}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}.

Since the localized quotient distance is the infimum over all localized
cleaning corrections,

\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}b\|_{\mathrm{cl}}+\frac{\delta_{G}}{1-\varepsilon_{G}}.

Multiplying by
1-\varepsilon_{G}>0
gives

(1-\varepsilon_{G})\operatorname{dist}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathfrak{D},\operatorname{Im}G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}})-\delta_{G}\leq\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{D}-G_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}b\|_{\mathrm{cl}}.

Taking the infimum over
b\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
proves the stated
quotient-distance comparison.
∎

Remark 15.14

(Status of quotient lifting)
.

The lifting assumption is the central structural input behind quotient-distance
stability. It is not a consequence of boundedness of

\Theta_{\Lambda}
or of finiteness of the gauge defect. It asserts that clean
gauge corrections can be represented, up to the explicit loss

(\varepsilon_{G},\delta_{G})
, by localized cleaning corrections. Concrete
Navier–Stokes estimates for this lifting remain outside the finite-window
argument.

Remark 16.5

(Pressure obstruction alternatives)
.

The pressure module leaves three honest possibilities. First, harmonic and
commutator directions may be compatible with the chosen cleaning space and
enter the budget as absorbable errors. Second, they may be controlled but not
small, in which case they remain in

absorbed coefficient. Third, they may produce pressure-observable near-kernel
directions; those directions should then be treated as genuine pressure
phantom candidates, not hidden as gauge.

K_{\rm prof}:=\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}-\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}.

Then

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm prof}(\mathfrak{D})=|K_{\rm prof}\mathfrak{D}|.

Lemma 16.20

(Finite-dimensional profit discrepancy bound)
.

Under the linear finite-window profit datum of

Section

16.3
,

\|K_{\rm prof}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}}<\infty.

Consequently, with

C_{\rm prof}^{\rm fd}:=\|K_{\rm prof}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}},

every localized package
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
satisfies

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm prof}(\mathfrak{D})\leq C_{\rm prof}^{\rm fd}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

In particular,

C_{\rm prof}^{\rm fd}\leq\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}}+\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\to\mathbb{R}}\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}}.

Proof.

The map
K_{\rm prof}
is the difference of the two linear scalar maps

\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}},\qquad\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}.

The first is bounded by assumption. The second is bounded because it is the
composition of the bounded linear chart
\Theta_{\Lambda}
and the bounded
linear clean profit functional
\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}
. Hence

K_{\rm prof}
is a bounded linear scalar functional. Since the domain is
finite-dimensional, its operator norm is finite.

For every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,

\mathsf{Err}_{\rm prof}(\mathfrak{D})=|K_{\rm prof}\mathfrak{D}|\leq\|K_{\rm prof}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\mathbb{R}}\|\mathfrak{D}\|_{\mathrm{loc}}.

This proves the displayed bound with

C_{\rm prof}^{\rm fd}=\|K_{\rm prof}\|
. The triangle inequality and
submultiplicativity of operator norms also give

\displaystyle\|K_{\rm prof}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|+\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\Theta_{\Lambda}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{loc}}\|+\|\mathsf{P}_{\Lambda}^{\mathrm{cl}}\|\,\|\Theta_{\Lambda}\|.

∎

18
Final synthesis and closure targets

We finish by combining the conceptual audit with the local-to-clean transfer
result. This final statement is best read as a synthesis corollary: the main
finite-window coercive estimate is
Theorem

17.4
, while
the corollary below records how that estimate fits into the larger singularity
audit. A persistent non-CKN branch must pass the payment test, the
combined-observability test, the NS-realizability test, the reproduction test,
and the localized residual-budget test. If any of these tests closes uniformly,
the branch must enter a CKN scale. If none closes, the remaining object is no
longer a generic concentration of energy or pressure; it is an NS-realizable,
cleaned, combined-invisible, profitable recurrence.

Corollary 18.1

(Unified singularity-audit and transfer alternative)
.

Let
(u,p)
be a suitable weak solution near a point
z_{0}
. Suppose a dyadic
branch based at
z_{0}
remains outside the CKN smallness regime. Assume dyadic
defect extraction, the finite-scale ledger, observable depletion or visible-supply
taxation with summable errors and a lower-bounded depletion budget, moving-window
growth control, local-to-clean quotient lifting, componentwise detection
comparison, concrete residual-budget absorption, and a near-kernel extraction
principle that converts persistent cleaned near-invisibility with small
reproduction residual into an NS-realizable PRV cascade. Then one of the following
alternatives occurs:

(a)

accumulated localization/window leakage is non-negligible;

(b)

positive average untaxed supply persists after the available taxes are
charged;

(c)

moving-window observability constants or cleaning constants grow too fast
to close the argument;

(d)

a nonzero NS-realizable, cleaned, scale-critical, combined-invisible,
profitable, reproducible defect cascade exists;

(e)

in a one-component degeneration, the obstruction appears as a vertical-duality
or anti-phantom failure for the active residual quotient.

Consequently, if leakage is negligible, visible supply is depleted or taxed,
moving-window constants are controlled, normalized absorption holds, and such
PRV cascades are excluded, then the branch must enter a CKN scale and
z_{0}
is
regular. The theorem is conditional in precisely the hypotheses listed above;
it does not assert that the depletion, absorption, or near-kernel extraction
inputs have been proved in this paper.

Proof.

The proof combines the earlier conditional alternatives. The finite-scale ledger gives the payment alternative: persistent reservoir badness forces untaxed supply or leakage. If leakage is non-negligible, (a) holds. If positive average supply remains after the available taxes are charged, (b) holds. Otherwise the visible part of the supply is taxed through the pressure, flux, energy, or trace channels. The observable-depletion input, its lower-bounded budget, and the summability of the error terms forbid an infinite controlled branch with a non-summable amount of visible observation. If the moving-window constants lose effectiveness before this conclusion is reached, (c) holds. If not, the branch has a subsequence of cleaned localized packages approaching the combined-invisible NS-realizable kernel. The local-to-clean transfer theorem supplies the localized coercive comparison under normalized absorption, and the reproduction comparison supplies the scale-to-scale residual control. The near-kernel extraction principle then produces the PRV cascade in (d), with profitability inherited from the ledger. In the one-component channel, failure of the required vertical-duality closure is recorded as (e). If all alternatives are excluded, the original non-CKN branch cannot persist, and therefore a CKN scale occurs.
∎

Problem 18.2

(Localized quotient compatibility)
.

The concrete theorem target left by this paper is to prove component
compatibility estimates of the form

\mathsf{Err}_{\bullet}(D)\leq\eta_{\bullet}\dist_{\mathrm{loc}}(D,\operatorname{Im}G^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\Lambda})+\Delta_{\bullet},

with coefficients summing below the clean gap margin. This requires either a

Problem 18.3

(NS-realizable anti-phantom closure)
.

Prove that the combined-invisible finite-window kernel contains no nonzero
direction in the closure of cleaned NS-realizable packages, or classify the
remaining kernel directions as genuine PRV mechanisms.

Problem 18.4

(Moving-window growth control)
.

Control the scale dependence of observability, cleaning, pressure-transfer,
truncation, and reproduction constants strongly enough to prevent non-summable
escape along selected moving windows.

19
Conclusion

The paper develops a finite-window language for separating genuine
Navier–Stokes obstructions from artifacts of cutoff, pressure normalization,
finite projection, and clean-model representation. The unconditional PDE part
is the critical ledger: persistent reservoir badness must be paid for by
untaxed supply or leakage. The main algebraic part is the local-to-clean
anti-phantom transfer theorem: a clean quotient gap becomes a localized coercive
estimate once quotient lifting, componentwise detection comparison, and residual
absorption are available. The remaining blocker is therefore sharply localized. One must either prove
component compatibility estimates against the localized quotient distance, with
coefficients below the clean gap margin, or construct an NS-realizable recurrence
that survives those tests. In this sense a possible singularity, within the
present framework, is forced to be highly organized: it must be cleaned,
combined-invisible, profitable, reproducible, and compatible with the momentum,
pressure, flux, gauge, and residual ledgers. Closing or realizing that final
object is the next theorem-level step.
