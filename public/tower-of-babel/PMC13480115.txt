Sotatercept in Congenital Heart Disease–Associated Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension and Eisenmenger Syndrome
Authors: Gillian P. Leung, Elaine C. Major, Sydney C. Cason, Andrew R. Kolodziej, David C. Booth, J. Shaun Smith
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEisenmenger syndrome (ES) is a form of World Health Organization Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH). Initial therapy typically involves monotherapy with an endothelin-receptor antagonist or a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor, whereas evidence for advanced therapies remains limited. Sotatercept, a first-in-class therapy, modulates vascular cell proliferation. Phase 3 results were promising; however, they excluded patients with ES. Concerns exist that sotatercept could exacerbate pre-existing erythrocytosis and bleeding risk. This case series describes the authors’ experience with the use of sotatercept in patients with ES and illustrates its feasibility.Case SummaryA retrospective chart review was performed for all adult patients with PAH and ES seen at the PAH clinic from June 1, 2024, to May 30, 2025. This is a consecutive case series of patients treated with sotatercept. Six patients with ES initiated sotatercept. Five continued therapy, whereas one discontinued because of family preference. Adverse effects included diarrhea and headaches, which were manageable. No major safety signals were observed in the cohort. No patient experienced treatment-limiting erythrocytosis or bleeding.DiscussionThis case series describes a single-center experience with sotatercept in PAH and ES. All patients who continued therapy have sustained improvement in their functional capacity. No major safety signals were observed. Patients with ES are heterogeneous, the results are hypothesis-generating. Clinicians should carefully assess patient-specific physiology when selecting treatments.Take-Home MessageThis case series suggests the feasibility of sotatercept in patients with ES.

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a frequent complication of congenital heart disease associated with increased pulmonary blood flow through left-to-right shunting, which results in endothelial dysfunction. Chronic shunting can result in plexogenic pulmonary arteriopathy, an increase in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), shunt reversal, and systemic hypoxemia, known as Eisenmenger syndrome (ES).1Take-Home Messages•The single-center, retrospective case series suggests that the use of sotatercept in patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension and Eisenmenger syndrome is feasible.•This case series is hypothesis-generating; an individualized approach should be taken for patients with Eisenmenger syndrome.

Treatment approaches for ES remain an area of research as these heterogeneous patients are excluded from most randomized controlled trials focussed on PAH. Current data on the management of ES are derived from small randomized controlled or open-label trials.2 Based on the data from a small randomized controlled trial showing hemodynamic and functional improvement,3 bosentan is recommended by the 2022 European Society of Cardiology/European Respiratory Society guidelines on pulmonary hypertension as a treatment option for symptomatic patients with ES.4 Other endothelin-receptor antagonists or a combination with phosphodiesterase 5 inhibitors could be considered if no improvement is achieved. Other combinations and escalation of therapies exist, but because of the paucity of data, treatment decisions should be individualized.1,4 Similar uncertainty exists for sotatercept, a first-in-class activin signaling inhibitor.

Sotatercept binds to endogenous transforming growth factor-beta superfamily ligands, which balances proproliferative activin receptor IIA/Smad2/3 and antiproliferative bone morphogenetic protein receptor type II/Smad 1/5/8 signaling to modulate vascular cell proliferation and reverse vascular and right ventricular (RV) remodeling.5 It is proposed that through reversal of vascular remodeling, sotatercept could reduce PVR and improve pulmonary blood flow. Despite promising results in the phase 3 STELLAR trial, because patients with ES were not included, it is unclear whether the therapy is appropriate for patients with ES, especially given the safety profile of hemoglobin increase and platelet count reduction. Patients with ES experience hypoxemia, which could lead to an increase in hemoglobin concentration, along with dysfunction in the coagulation pathway and platelets that lead to a higher risk of bleeding. Careful monitoring is recommended.

Study Design and Methods

This is a case series of sotatercept use in patients with ES that was approved by the medical institutional review board with an exemption certification. We retrospectively evaluated all adult patients with PAH and ES seen at the pulmonary hypertension clinic from June 1, 2024, to May 30, 2025, who were treated with sotatercept. Six patients were initiated on sotatercept therapy in addition to background therapy. Consent from patients was obtained for this publication. The objective of this case series is to showcase the feasibility and safety of sotatercept in patients with PAH and ES.

Efficacy and Safety

SpO2 = oxygen saturation.

The patient received 3 doses and family requested to terminate therapy because of a reduction in the platelet count.

Case 1

A 46-year-old female with trisomy 21 presented with WHO Group 1 PAH and ES with an unrepaired ventricular septal defect (VSD). Pre-sotatercept hemodynamics included a mean pulmonary arterial pressure (mPAP) of 66 mm Hg and a PVR of 9.46 WU, following stable background triple therapy. An echocardiogram demonstrated a mildly dilated right heart with RV hypertrophy and low-normal RV systolic function.

Since the initiation of sotatercept, the patient no longer takes breaks to walk 1 block. Oxygen saturation with activity increased from the 70% range to above 85%. SpO2 at rest was maintained at 96% on room air. The patient experiences 3 to 4 episodes of diarrhea daily.

Case 2

A 47-year-old female presented with WHO Group 1 PAH and ES following childhood VSD closure. The 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) on background triple therapy was 366 m. Pre-sotatercept hemodynamics included an mPAP of 69 mm Hg and a PVR of 9.9 WU following stable triple therapy. An echocardiogram revealed a severely dilated RV with normal systolic function and an estimated RV systolic pressure of 72 mm Hg.

Since the initiation of sotatercept, the 6MWD improved by 45.7 m at 6 months. The patient reports increased ease with climbing stairs. SpO2 at rest increased from the low 90% to 98% on room air. SpO2 with activity increased from 80% to 90%-92%.

Case 3

A 52-year-old female with trisomy 21, WHO Group 1 PAH, and ES due to a primum atrial septal defect (ASD), inlet VSD, patent ductus arteriosus, and a common atrioventricular valve with mild-to-moderate regurgitation was evaluated. Pretreatment hemodynamics included an mPAP of 71 mm Hg and a PVR of 13.3 WU. An echocardiogram following stable dual therapy showed a small RV with severe hypertrophy and grossly normal RV systolic function. An endothelin-receptor antagonist was previously avoided because of the risk of volume overload. Other pertinent past medical history included chronic gout and wheelchair dependence.

Since the initiation of sotatercept, the patient’s family reported improved exertional dyspnea and oxygenation with an increased SpO2, from the upper 70% to low 80%, to now 91%-93% on 5 L/min of supplemental oxygen. In addition, the patient reported reduced cyanosis. The patient had frequent diarrhea requiring antidiarrheal therapy.

Case 4

A 27-year-old female with trisomy 21 presented with WHO Group 1 PAH and ES with a secundum ASD and a membranous VSD. Pretreatment hemodynamics included an mPAP of 50 mm Hg and a PVR of 21 WU. An echocardiogram following stable background triple therapy showed a severely dilated, subpulmonic RV with severely reduced systolic function, and an RV systolic pressure of 91 mm Hg. Since the initiation of sotatercept, the patient’s family reported improved dyspnea and functional capacity. The patient was able to sit on the edge of the bed and roll herself onto a wheelchair independently. The platelet count progressively reduced from 106 × 103 cells/μL at baseline by a mean of 10.5 × 103 cells/μL with each dose, down to 64 × 103 cells/μL. At that time, the family decided to terminate sotatercept and not reattempt therapy. No ecchymosis or bleeding was noted.

Case 5

A 67-year-old female with WHO Group 1 PAH and ES with an unrepaired VSD was evaluated. Pretreatment hemodynamics included an mPAP of 69 mm Hg and a PVR of 14.8 WU. An echocardiogram following stable background triple therapy showed a moderately dilated hypertrophic RV with normal systolic function. The patient had an acute decline in breathing with a reduced SpO2 to the upper 60% with activity. Selexipag dosage was increased from 1,400 to 1,600 μg twice a day, and sildenafil was increased from 40 to 60 mg three times a day. Fifty days later, sotatercept was initiated.

Since the initiation of therapy, the patient experienced progressive improvement and was able to perform activities of daily living with increased ease. Exertional SpO2 increased from the upper 60% to 86%-90% on room air.

Case 6

A 59-year-old female with WHO Group 1 PAH and ES after ASD closure was evaluated. Pretreatment hemodynamics included an mPAP of 58 mm Hg and a PVR of 13.7 WU. Of note, the mid-left main coronary artery had sustained 90% stenosis due to pulsatile dynamic external compression by a severely enlarged pulmonary artery, which required placement of a drug-eluting stent. An echocardiogram on the background triple therapy revealed a dilated, hypertrophic RV and grossly normal systolic function. The 6MWD pre sotatercept was 366 m.

Since the initiation of sotatercept, the patient experienced significant improvement in functional capacity and exertional dyspnea. She no longer required supplemental oxygen and was able to walk her dog. The 6MWD increased to 411.5 m. Side effects included occasional headache.

Discussion

Treatment of late-stage Eisenmenger PAH constitutes a challenging disease in which options such as transplantation may be limited. This case series describes our experience with sotatercept use in 6 patients with PAH and ES. Food and Drug Administration–approved dosing strategies were strictly followed using actual body weight. Five patients (83%) are still on therapy to date. All patients who continued therapy have sustained improvement in their functional capacity. Reported adverse drug effects are manageable. One patient discontinued therapy after the platelet count reduced to 64 × 103/μL. The decision was made by the family to terminate therapy despite the platelet count not reaching the package insert cutoff of 50 × 103/μL. Since the termination of therapy, the platelet count has recovered to 98 × 103/μL. Based on family preference, therapy was not reattempted. This patient was concurrently on a prostacyclin pathway agent, which increased the risk of thrombocytopenia.

Concerns exist with the use of sotatercept in this patient population because of baseline erythrocytosis and known drug-related risk of erythrocytosis and bleeding. Data from the 6 patients suggest that sotatercept can be safe in the ES population with close monitoring of blood counts. No major safety signals were observed in this limited cohort. Although clinically insignificant, the increase in hemoglobin in response to sotatercept could have a positive impact on oxygenation. Lastly, no major or minor bleeding event was reported.

Limitations of this case series are the retrospective nature and small sample size. The retrospective nature of this case series did not allow standardized endpoints. It is worth noting that the patients included were heterogeneous, and clinicians should carefully assess patient-specific physiology when selecting treatments. Although the patients showed improvement, these observations may reflect natural variability or background therapy. In addition, we did not observe any major safety signals in the limited cohort; however, results are hypothesis-generating and true safety cannot be inferred. Future studies with a larger sample size and a prospective, randomized design will be necessary to demonstrate the extent of benefit for sotatercept in the management of Group 1 PAH ES. A study of sotatercept for patients with ES or unrepaired shunt-associated PAH resistant to vasodilator therapy (NCT07356778) is ongoing, which will evaluate the use of sotatercept in the ES population.6
