Abstract
. In this paper, we study the dynamics of commuting transcendental entire functions f f and g g , where g g is of the form a ​ f p + b af^{p}+b with a , b ∈ ℂ a,b\in\mathbb{C} , p ∈ ℕ p\in\mathbb{N} , and a ≠ 0 , 1 a\neq 0,1 . We establish that the escaping sets, filled Julia sets, and bungee sets of f f and g g all coincide. As an immediate consequence, we obtain in particular that the Julia sets of f f and g g are identical. Our theorem extends the 1998 result of Poon and Yang. Furthermore, following Wang and Yang, we consider a non-constant polynomial Q Q and permutable entire functions f f and g g satisfying the relation Q ⁡ ( g ) = a ​ Q ​ ( f ) + b Q(g)=aQ(f)+b , where a ( ≠ 0 , 1 ) , b ∈ ℂ a(\neq 0,1),b\in\mathbb{C} . In this more general setting, we also prove that the escaping sets, filled Julia sets, and bungee sets of f f and g g are equal.

1.
Introduction

Let
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
be a transcendental entire function and let

f^{n}
denote the
n
-th iterate of
f
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The dynamical
behavior of
f
is described by partitioning the complex plane into two
fundamental sets viz., the Fatou set and the Julia set. The Fatou set of
f
is denoted by
F(f)
and is defined by

F(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\{f^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is normal in some neighborhood of }z\},

and its complement
J(f)=\mathbb{C}\setminus F(f)
is called the Julia set.
Points in the Fatou set exhibit stable dynamical behavior, whereas the Julia
set represents chaotic dynamics and sensitive dependence on initial
conditions. Basic properties and structural aspects of these sets are well
documented in the literature
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
.

Apart from the classical decomposition of the complex plane into Fatou and
Julia sets, one may further classify points according to their orbits under iteration. The escaping set of
f
is denoted by
I(f)
and is defined by

I(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:f^{n}(z)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty\}.

It was introduced by Eremenko
[
6
]
and consists of all those points whose
forward orbit tends to infinity. Also, he proved that
I(f)
is non-empty and
that every connected component of
\overline{I(f)}
is unbounded. The filled Julia set of
f
is denoted by
K(f)
and is defined by

K(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\{f^{n}(z)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is bounded}\}.

That is, it contains those points that have bounded orbit
[
4
]
. In addition, the bungee set of
f
is
denoted by
BU(f)
and is defined to be the collection of those points whose orbit
display mixed dynamical behavior
[
8
]
. That is, the orbit admits at least two subsequences such that one subsequence remains bounded while another subsequence tends to infinity.

Two functions
f
and
g
are said to be commuting (permutable) if

f\circ g=g\circ f
. Fatou proved that such permutable rational functions have
identical Fatou sets
[
2
]
. Under analogous special hypothesis, Hua and Wang
[
7
]
, answered a question originally posed by Baker
[
1
]
. This naturally leads to the question of whether similar kind of results also hold for the escaping set, the filled Julia set and the bungee set which has been addressed in this article.

Motivated by these developments, the present work studies the dynamics of certain special classes of transcendental entire functions together with their affine modifications. It has been shown that under suitable hypothesis, two commuting entire functions
f
and
g
have the same filled Julia set, escaping set and bungee set. Our results extend the work of Poon and Yang
[
9
]
, by establishing the equality of the three dynamical sets viz., the escaping set, the filled Julia set and the bungee set for a wider class of commuting transcendental entire functions. Additionally, we obtain the equality of the Julia sets of these commuting transcendental entire functions.

2.
The equality of dynamical sets

As shown in
[
9
]
, if
f
and
g
are permutable transcendental entire functions, then
F(f)=F(g),
where
g(z)=af(z)+b,\,a,b\in\mathbb{C}
are constants and
a\neq 0
. In addition,
|a|=1
. Analogous conclusion for the filled Julia set, the escaping set and the bungee set are then naturally explored in a more general setting.
The following elementary lemma, which will be used frequently in the sequel, describes how affine mappings perform on bounded and unbounded sets.

Observation :

Let
P(z)=az+b
with
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
a\neq 0
. Then
P
sends every bounded subset (respectively, an unbounded subset) of
\mathbb{C}
to a bounded set (respectively, an unbounded set).
With the help of above Lemma
2
, we investigate the filled Julia set, the escaping set and the bungee set of two permutable transcendental entire functions.

Before proving the next result, we first state the following lemma, which clarifies the connection between
f
,
g
and the affine function
P(z)=az+b,\,0\neq a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are two commuting transcendental entire functions where
g=af^{p}+b,\,a,b\in\mathbb{C}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and
a\neq 0,1
. Then,

f(az+b)=af(z)+b.

Proof.

We have
f\circ g(w)=g\circ f(w)
.
As
g(w)=af^{p}(w)+b
, we obtain

\displaystyle f\circ g(w)

\displaystyle=f(af^{p}(w)+b).

On the other hand, we have
g\circ f(w)=af^{p}(f(w))+b
. Substituting
f^{p}(w)=z
, this leads to the following functional equation
f(az+b)=af(z)+b
.
∎

The next lemma provides an expression for the iterates of
g
in terms of the iterates of
f
and the affine map
P
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are commuting transcendental entire functions where
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b,\,a,b\in\mathbb{C}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and
a\neq 0,1
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
g^{n}(z)=P^{n}\circ f^{np}(z)
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle g^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=g(g(z))

\displaystyle=af^{p}(af^{p}(z)+b)+b

\displaystyle=a^{2}f^{2p}(z)+ab+b.

By induction, we obtain

g^{n}(z)=a^{n}f^{np}(z)+b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}.

Now, consider the affine map
P(z)=az+b
. Then, the function
g
can be expressed as
g=P\circ f^{p}
.
As
P^{n}(z)=a^{n}z+b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}
, this implies that
P^{n}(f^{np})=a^{n}f^{np}+b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}
which is same as
g^{n}(z)
and the result follows.
∎

For two permutable entire functions, Poon and Yang established, under suitable conditions, that the Fatou set of
f
coincides with the Fatou set of
g
, as stated in
[
9
]
and reproduced below.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are permutable transcendental entire functions. If

g(z)=af(z)+b,

where
a,b
are constants and
a\neq 0
, then

F(f)=F(g).

Moreover,
|a|=1
.

We now generalize this result to obtain the equality of the filled Julia sets of two permutable entire functions. The next result establishes the equality of the filled Julia sets for two commuting transcendental entire functions.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
f
and
g
be commuting transcendental entire functions where
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C},\,p\in\mathbb{N}
with
a\neq 0,1
.
Then
K(f)=K(g)
.

Proof.

Let
z\in K(f)
. Then
f^{n}(z)
is bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
that is, there exists
A>0
such that
|f^{n}(z)|\leq A
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From Lemma
2.2
, we have
g^{n}(z)=P^{n}\circ f^{np}(z)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Lemma
2
,
P^{n}
sends bounded set to bounded set. As a result, there exists
M>0
such that
|g^{n}(z)|\leq M
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
which implies that
z\in K(g)
. Thus
K(f)\subset K(g)
.

On the other hand, observe that
f^{np}(z)=P^{-n}(g^{n}(z))
is bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As
f
is continuous, we have
f^{k}(f^{np}(z))
is bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=1,2,\dots p-1.
Consequently,
f^{n}(z)=\{f^{np}(z)\}\cup\{f(f^{np}(z))\}\cup\dots\cup\{f^{p-1}(f^{np}(z))\}
is bounded for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We deduce that
K(g)\subset K(f)
. Hence,
K(f)=K(g)
.
∎

In the next result, we show the equality of the escaping set of two permutable entire functions.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are commuting transcendental entire functions where
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C},p\in\mathbb{N}
with
a\neq 0,1
. Then

I(f)=I(g)
.

Proof.

Let
z\in I(f)
. Then
f^{n}(z)\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. By Lemma
2.2
,
g^{n}(z)=P^{n}\circ f^{np}(z)
. Using Lemma
2
,
P
sends unbounded sets to unbounded sets (and therefore, so does
P^{n}
). It follows that
P^{n}(f^{np}(z))\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
z\in I(g)
which shows that
I(f)\subset I(g)
. On similar lines, we obtain
I(g)\subset I(f)
. Therefore,
I(f)=I(g)
.
∎

Using the above two results, we now demonstrate that the corresponding results also apply to the bungee set as shown in the remark below.

Remark 2.6
.

Suppose
f
and
g
are commuting transcendental entire functions, where
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C},p\in\mathbb{N}
with
a\neq 0,1
.
Then the bungee set
of
f
and
g
are identical. For, if
z\in BU(f)
, then
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(K(f)\cup I(f)).
Accordingly,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(K(g)\cup I(g))=BU(g)
.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.5
, we obtain that
\partial I(f)=\partial I(g)
, where
\partial U
denotes the boundary of the set
U
. Eremenko in
[
6
]
, proved that
\partial I(f)=J(f)
. Thus we obtain
J(f)=J(g)
. This gives another proof of the equality of the Julia sets of two permutable entire functions under the given hypothesis as in
[
9
]
. In fact, our results are more general in the sense that on taking
p=1
, we get the result obtained in
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Finally, We illustrate our results with an example

Example 2.7
.

Let
f=\lambda\,\sin z
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, and
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b
where,
a,b\in\mathbb{C},\,p\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\neq 0,1
.
It can be seen that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

g^{n}(z)=a^{n}f^{np}(z)+b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}.

The growth of
g^{n}
and
f^{np}
are comparable, as
|a|=1
,
a\neq 1
and the term
b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}
is bounded. Hence,
I(f)=I(g)
. On similar lines, it can seen that their filled Julia sets and the bungee sets are also identical.

Remark 2.8
.

The aforementioned results shows how the dynamics of
f
entirely controls the dynamical behavior of
g(z)=af^{p}(z)+b,\,a,b\in\mathbb{C},\,p\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\neq 0,1
.

We now present a family of permutable entire functions whose Julia set, escaping set, filled Julia set, and the bungee set of
f
and
g
all coincide.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
f
satisfies
f(d-z)=d-f(z)
for some constant
d
, and we define
g(z)=d-f(z)
, then
f
and
g
commute.

Starting from any odd function
G
(i.e.,
G(-w)=-G(w)
), we can construct such a function
f
as follows.

Define

f(z)=\frac{d}{2}+G\!\left(z-\frac{d}{2}\right).

Substituting
d-z
in place of
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle f(d-z)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}+G\!\left(d-z-\frac{d}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}+G\!\left(\frac{d}{2}-z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}-G\!\left(z-\frac{d}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{2}-\left[\frac{d}{2}+G\!\left(z-\frac{d}{2}\right)\right]+\frac{d}{2}

\displaystyle=d-f(z).

Consequently, the function
g(z)=d-f(z)
commutes with
f
. Also,
f
satisfies the property
f(az+b)=af(z)+b
.

Example 2.10
.

Suppose
f(z)=1+\sin(z-1)
and
g(z)=2-f(z)=1-\sin(z-1)
are commuting functions. Introduce the affine map
h(z)=2-z
, so that

g(z)=2-f(z)=h(f(z)).

Moreover,

\displaystyle f(h(z))

\displaystyle=1+\sin(1-z)

\displaystyle=1-\sin(z-1)

\displaystyle=g(z).

hence
f\circ h=h\circ f
. Using the commutativity of
f
and
h
, we have

g^{n}=(h\circ f)^{n}=h^{n}\circ f^{n}.

Since
h^{2}=\mathrm{id}
, we obtain

g^{n}=\begin{cases}f^{n},&n\text{ is even},\\[4.0pt]
h\circ f^{n},&n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

As
h
is conformal, it preserves the normality of families of functions. Hence, the family
(g^{n})
is normal if and only if the family
(f^{n})
is normal, and therefore
F(f)=F(g)
. Using the conformality of
h
once more, we see that a point escapes under iteration of
g
if and only if it escapes under iteration of
f
, so
I(f)=I(g)
. By the same reasoning, a point is in the filled Julia set of
g
exactly when it is in the filled Julia set of
f
, which implies
K(f)=K(g)
. Likewise, we obtain
BU(f)=BU(g)
.

More generally, we can choose
f(z)=1+\sin(z-1)
and
g(z)=-f^{p}(z)+2
so that
f
and
g
commute. By applying the same line of reasoning as in the previous example, we obtain that the escaping set, the filled Julia set, and the bungee set of
f
and
g
all coincide.

Remark 2.11
.

As
I(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:f^{n}(z)\to\infty\}
. Let
z\in I(f)
then by definition
f^{n}(z)\to\infty
. We have
g^{n}(z)=h^{n}\circ f^{np}(z)
and because
h
is conformal it follows that
g^{n}(z)\to\infty
. Thus
I(f)\subseteq I(g)
, and by the same argument we obtain
I(g)\subseteq I(f)
. Therefore,
I(f)=I(g)
.

Analogously, we can prove that the filled Julia set and the bungee set of two permutable entire functions coincide.

Example 2.12
.

Consider
f(z)=1+(z-1)e^{(z-1)^{2}}
and
g(z)=-f(z)+2
. Clearly,
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
. Now consider the affine map
h(z)=2-z
. A straightforward computation shows that
f(h(z))=h(f(z))=g(z)
. Moreover,
f\circ g=g\circ f
. As illustrated in example
2.10
, one observes that
F(f)=F(g)
. Using the same reasoning as in the preceding example, we conclude that the escaping set, the filled Julia set, and the bungee set of
f
and
g
are all identical.

The next lemma from
[
10
]
is useful for proving theorem
2.14

Lemma 2.13
.

[
10
, Lemma 1]
Let
f
and
g
be two distinct transcendental entire functions that commute, and let
Q
be a non-constant polynomial. Suppose that

Q(g)=aQ(f)+b

for some
a(\neq 0),b\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, for every integer
n\geq 1
and every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

Q\bigl(g^{n}(z)\bigr)=a^{n}Q\bigl(f^{n}(z)\bigr)+b(a^{n-1}+a^{n-2}+\dots+a+1).

Proof.

We prove this lemma using a recurrence relation. Define
A_{n}(z)=Q(g^{n}(z))
. We start with the relation;

(2.1)

Q(g(z))=aQ(f(z))+b.

Now post-multiply both sides of equation
2.1
by
g^{n-1}
.

Q(g\circ g^{n-1}(z))=aQ(f\circ g^{n-1}(z))+b.

By applying the property of commutativity, we have:

Q(g^{n}(z))=aQ(g^{n-1}\circ f(z))+b.

Thus, we can express it as:

A_{n}(z)=aA_{n-1}(f(z))+b.

For
n=1
, we find:

A_{1}(z)=aA_{0}(f(z))+b=aQ(f(z))+b.

For
n=2
:

A_{2}(z)=aA_{1}(f(z))+b=a(aQ(f^{2}(z))+b)+b=a^{2}Q(f^{2}(z))+ab+b.

These patterns suggest the following general formula:

Q(g^{n}(z))=aA_{n-1}(f(z))+b=a^{n}Q(f^{n}(z))+b\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a^{k}.

If
a\neq 1
, we can simplify this further:

Q(g^{n}(z))=a^{n}Q(f^{n}(z))+b\frac{a^{n}-1}{a-1}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

In order to prove the following theorem we will use the above lemma.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
f
and
g
be two distinct permutable transcendental entire functions and
Q
be a non-constant polynomial. Suppose
Q(g)=aQ(f)+b
with
a(\neq 0),b\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, if
|a|=1
and
a\neq 1
, the following statements hold:

(1)

I(f)=I(g)
,

(2)

K(f)=K(g)
,

(3)

BU(f)=BU(g)
.

Proof.

We first show that
I(f)\subseteq I(g)
. Take any
z\in I(f)
. Then
|f^{n}(z)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Since
Q
is a non-constant polynomial, it maps unbounded sets to unbounded sets. Hence,
|Q(f^{n}(z))|\to\infty
for all
n\to\infty
. Now, by lemma
2.13
,

\displaystyle|Q(g^{n}(z))|

\displaystyle=\left|a^{n}Q(f^{n}(z))+b\frac{a^{n}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|a^{n}||Q(f^{n}(z))|-\left|b\frac{a^{n}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|Q(f^{n}(z))|-2\frac{|b|}{|a-1|},

where we have used
|a|=1
and the fact that
\left|\frac{a^{n}-1}{a-1}\right|\leq 2/|a-1|
. This shows that
|Q(g^{n}(z))|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, which in turn implies that
|g^{n}(z)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Thus
z\in I(g)
, and hence
I(f)\subseteq I(g)
. On similar lines, we obtain
I(g)\subseteq I(f)
. Thus we obtain the equality of
I(f)
and
I(g)
.

Equality of
K(f)
and
K(g)
can be done on similar lines as that of
I(f)
and
I(g)
.

We now show the equality of
BU(f)
and
BU(g)
analogous to the equality of
I(f),I(g)
and
K(f),K(g)
. To this end,
let
z\in BU(f)
. Then their exists at least two subsequences
\{m_{k}\}
and
\{n_{k}\}
such that
|f^{m_{k}}|\leq R,\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
|f^{n_{k}}|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. There exist some constant
R^{\prime}>0
, such that
|Q(f^{m_{k}})|\leq R^{\prime},\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
|Q(f^{n_{k}})|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Now,

\displaystyle|Q(g^{m_{k}}(z))|

\displaystyle=\left|a^{m_{k}}Q(f^{m_{k}}(z))+b\frac{a^{m_{k}}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|a^{m_{k}}||Q(f^{m_{k}}(z))|+\left|b\frac{a^{m_{k}}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|Q(f^{m_{k}}(z))|+2\frac{|b|}{|a-1|}.

As
\{Q(f^{m_{k}})\}
is bounded we obtain that
\{Q(g^{m_{k}}(z))\}
is bounded which further implies that
\{g^{m_{k}}(z)\}
is bounded. Now,

\displaystyle|Q(g^{n_{k}}(z))|

\displaystyle=\left|a^{n_{k}}Q(f^{n_{k}}(z))+b\frac{a^{n_{k}}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|a^{n_{k}}||Q(f^{n_{k}}(z))|-\left|b\frac{a^{n_{k}}-1}{a-1}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|Q(f^{n_{k}}(z))|-2\frac{|b|}{|a-1|}.

As
|Q(f^{n_{k}}(z))|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
we conclude that
|Q(g^{n_{k}}(z))|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. This further implies that
|g^{n_{k}}(z)|\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Hence,
BU(f)\subseteq BU(g)
. By similar reasoning we obtained
BU(g)\subseteq BU(f)
and hence the result.

∎
