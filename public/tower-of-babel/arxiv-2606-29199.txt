Improved Domination–Packing Bounds in Claw-Free Cubic Graphs and Unit Disk Graphs
Juan Gutierrez, Kaustav Paul

Abstract
Given a graph G G , the domination number γ ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G) is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set in G G , and the packing number ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) is the maximum cardinality of a set of vertices that are pairwise at distance at least 3 3 . The ratio between these parameters has been widely studied in several graph classes. It is known that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G) for claw-free subcubic graphs, up to finitely many exceptions, and that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 32 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq 32\rho(G) for unit disk graphs. In this paper, we improve the latter bound by showing that γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 16 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)\leq 16\rho(G) for a unit disk graph G G . For the former bound, we show that it can be improved in the cubic bridgeless setting; more precisely, every bridgeless claw-free cubic graph G G satisfies γ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 7 4 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) + 5 6 \gamma(G)\leq\frac{7}{4}\rho(G)+\frac{5}{6} . These results are not tight. In fact, we give example of an infinite family of bridgeless cubic graphs G G with γ ⁡ ( G ) = 5 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) / 4 \gamma(G)=5\rho(G)/4 and an infnite family of unit disk graphs G G in which γ ⁡ ( G ) = 3 ​ ρ ​ ( G ) \gamma(G)=3\rho(G) .

Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For any vertex
u\in V
, we denote by
N(u)
the neighborhood of
v
, and set
N[u]=\{u\}\cup N(u)
. Also,
N_{i}(u)
is the set of vertices at distance

from

and we put
N_{\leq i}(u)=N[u]\cup\cdots\cup N_{i}(u)

These definitions extend naturally to sets

N(S)=\bigcup_{u\in S}N(u),N[S]=S\cup N(S),N_{i}(S)=\bigcup_{u\in S}N_{i}(u)

A set

is a
dominating set
if every vertex of
G
is either in
D
or adjacent to a vertex in

. A set

is a

if
N[u]\cap N[v]=\emptyset
for any distinct
u,v\in P
. Equivalently, if every pair of vertices in
P
are at distance at least 3. The
domination number

\gamma(G)
is the minimum size of a dominating set, and the
packing number

\rho(G)
is the maximum size of a packing.

The relationship between
\gamma(G)
and
\rho(G)
has been extensively studied. The inequality
\gamma(G)\geq\rho(G)
holds and Henning et al. showed that
\gamma(G)\leq\Delta(G)\rho(G)
, where

is the maximum degree of

[
9
]
.
It is known that

is a tree
[
12
]
, a strongly chordal graph
[
6
]
, or a dually chordal graph
[
3
]
.
It is also known that
\gamma(G)\leq 2\rho(G)
holds if
G
is a cactus graph
[
11
]
or a biconvex graph
[
8
]
.
Regarding planar graphs, it was shown that

\gamma(G)\leq 3\rho(G)
for any maximal outerplanar

. Such bound was later improved to
2

[
1
]
.
For arbitrary planar graphs, it was shown that
\gamma(G)\leq 10\rho(G)

7

[

It was recently shown that

A unit disk graph is the intersection graph of equal-radius disks in the plane. Equivalently, it is a graph
G=(V,E)
for which there exists a representation
p:V\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
uv\in E

\|p(u)-p(v)\|_{2}\leq 1

S_{i}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\leq y<\frac{i+1}{\sqrt{2}}\}
