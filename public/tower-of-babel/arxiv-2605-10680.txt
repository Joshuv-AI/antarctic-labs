Exact Unlearning from Proxies Induces Closeness Guarantees on Approximate Unlearning
Virgile Dine, Teddy Furon

Abstract
This paper proposes a paradigm shift linking machine unlearning directly to the structure of the data distributions rather than a mere update of the neural network parameters. We show that inferring these distributions with precision enables distilling the exact unlearning signal induced by the modeling. Theoretical bounds on the Kullback-Leibler divergence from the ideal retrained model to our unlearned model, under verifiable admissibility criterion, reveal the soundness of our framework. This method is experimentally validated over three forgetting scenarios as reaching the closest classifier to the ideal retrained model when compared to competitors.

Introduction

Approximate machine unlearning removes the influence of some training data from an already trained classifier, without retraining it from scratch.
Application ranges from privacy issues, the right to be forgotten (European GDPR, Canadian PIPEDA or Californian CCPA), to security defenses removing poisoned training data.
Another scenario is the derivation of a restricted public model from a powerful private model learned on some sensitive data.

Classical methods construct asymmetrical loss that treat retain and forget samples differently. They are iterative, hyper-parameter sensitive, and without stopping criterion.
Their performances are often gauged by misleading accuracy metrics and lack of a measurable distance to the retrained model.

This paper first models the exact unlearning signal as the logits shift between the ideal and the initial NNs.
Our update is exact up to the quality of the chosen proxies modeling the posterior distributions (see Prop.
1
).
Section
3
bounds the gap to the ideal retrained model under verifiable assumptions about the proxies.
The bound tightens as the proxies quality increases.
Implementing this unlearning signal in the NN parameters is only a secondary matter achieved through distillation.

The key element of our method is the choice of the proxies modeling the posterior distributions.
Naive marginalisation is a first approximation which turns out to be limited in challenging scenarios.
Section
4
introduces some mathematical modeling specifically designed for machine unlearning.

Our contributions are the following:

•

An exact unlearning signal, approximately computed thanks to proxies with theoretical guarantees and computable bounds on the gap
to the ideal retrained model (Sect.
3
).

•

A series of fine-grained proxies modeling the posterior distributions that intrinsically learns the membership dependency from the data structure (Sect.
4
).
Among them, the feature agnostic unlearning
DIR-2C
naturally emerges from the empirical measure of the data.

•

A way to assess unlearning by the empirical evaluation of KL divergences (App.
G
).

•

An experimental validation across four architectures (including a foundation model backbone) and three unlearning scenarios: random data, subclass, or class forgetting.
(Sect.
5
)

Related work

Closed-form Modeling

of class-conditional densities has a rich history in statistics.
Gaussian discriminant analysis (LDA, QDA) provides the canonical example, with regularized variants
(
21
)
and Bayesian counterparts
(
56
;
23
)
.
More flexible options include per-class Gaussian mixtures
(
13
;
53
)
and constrained covariance structures
(
25
)
.
Our method leverages these fast closed-form approaches.
To our knowledge, no prior work has exploited the structure that arises when retain and forget samples within a class are modeled as two separate subclasses.

Machine unlearning

is the art of removing the influence of some training data without learning a new model from scratch (see App.
A
for a deeper overview). The gold standard is
exact removal
. As far as classifiers are concerned, exact techniques resort to specific data structures to avoid full retraining
(
4
;
65
)
. For instance,
3
propose the method SISA, which splits the training data into shards and stores intermediate checkpoints for each shard. The trade-off between the memory footprint and the complexity of unlearning is the main limitation at scale, especially when the training data to be forgotten spans multiple shards.

Approximate unlearning
aims at making the output of the unlearned model statistically indistinguishable from the ideal model that never saw the forget data.
The dominant paradigm, so-called first-order methods, resorts to gradient-based updates of the NN parameters.
Gradient ascent on the forget-set loss is the simplest primitive, used in many methods like
(
32
;
6
)
.
Knowledge-distillation variants enforce retention jointly with forgetting.
SCRUB trains a student by minimising KL divergence against the original model on the data to retain while maximising the loss on the data to forget
(
44
)
.

8
use an incompetent teacher guiding the student to suppress class knowledge.

67
propose an impair-repair scheme: an error-maximising noise degrades class knowledge, then a repair step restores retain-set accuracy.
SalUn restricts updates to the weights most responsible for forgotten data
(
19
)
.

Recent articles unify these first-order methods into either a multi-objective
(
76
;
61
)
or a constrained optimization
(
15
;
38
)
: unlearning the forget data while preserving the model utility.
These four papers outline the conflicting gradients of the objectives and propose ad-hoc combinations.
Yet, the iterative updates result in a trajectory with no clear stopping criterion.
In the end, opacity prevails since there is no characterization of the result in terms of data distribution.
Last but not least,
52
recently spotted that the dependence between the forget and retain data frequently prevents these first-order methods from performing machine unlearning properly.

From the theoretical point of view, KL divergence from the ideal model to the empirically unlearned NN has been adopted as a natural criterion
(
65
;
30
;
7
;
24
)
.
Yet, nearly all theoretical results are stated in terms of NN properties (Hessian approximation, Lipschitz constants of the loss) rather than of data distribution.
Indeed,
70
report from the NeurIPS unlearning competition that the field still relies on membership inference attacks (MIAs)
(
66
;
5
;
34
)

as a proxy for formal guarantees, underscoring the lack of interpretable, data-driven bounds.

Our positioning

24
propose data model matching as a way to predict the output of the retrained model but we show that modeling only the retrain set is sub-optimal.
45
give guarantees but primarily designed for the class-wise unlearning scenario. Our work departs from all the above by providing a closed-form decomposition of the expected KL divergence directly in terms of the quality of mathematical models fitted on the data.
First, exact unlearning signal is designed as a correction in the logit space from explicit mathematical models. For instance, the
LDA-Mix
and
QDA-Mix
constructions picture the retain and forget sub-populations within each class as two Gaussian distributions enabling the removal of order 2 statistic of the true unlearning signal. It yields a precise correction capturing intra-class structure invisible to standard methods. Our proxy
LDA-2C
go even further: doubling the label space to intrinsically capture the retain-forget distribution overlap, implicitly stabilizing the covariance on the forget set and extracting an even more precise unlearning signal.
The network weight update only emerges as a second distillation step.

Our method

3.1
Notations (summarised in Table
4
, App
B
)

The original training set
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{r}\cup\mathcal{D}_{f}
is composed by the forget set
\mathcal{D}_{f}
to be erased and the retain set
\mathcal{D}_{r}
to be preserved.
Given a neural network (NN)
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}
trained on
\mathcal{D}
, machine unlearning seeks an efficient procedure producing NN
\mathrm{f}_{\tilde{\theta}}
as if it had been trained on
\mathcal{D}_{r}
alone.

Here, NNs are functions from the input space
\mathcal{X}
to the
logit
space
\mathbb{R}^{C}
, with
C
classes.
The softmax operator yields predicted class probabilities conditioned on
x
, a.k.a.
probits
,

{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)\doteq\mathrm{softmax}({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x))
belonging to the simplex of probability distributions.
Conversely,
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)=\log({\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x))+\gamma(x)
with
\gamma(x)\doteq\mathrm{LSE}({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x))
,
\mathrm{LSE}
being the Log-Sum-Exponential function (see App.
C.2
).

We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the true joint distribution of the data
(Y,X)
.
The induced marginal and conditioned true distributions are denoted
\mathbb{P}_{X}
,
\mathbb{P}_{Y}
and
\mathbb{P}_{Y\mid X}
,
\mathbb{P}_{X\mid Y}
, or simply
\mathbb{P}
when the context is clear.

We introduce a third representation of the distribution of the data given by a proxy
\mathrm{P}_{X\mid Y}
,
i.e.
a parametric mathematical model of the true class-conditional distribution
\mathbb{P}_{X\mid Y}
. This proxy induces the following posterior and associated ‘logit’ vector
\mathrm{M}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
:

\forall y\in\llbracket C\rrbracket,\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\quad\mathrm{P}_{Y\mid X}\left(y|x\right)\doteq\frac{\mathrm{P}_{X\mid Y}\left(x|y\right)\mathbb{P}(y)}{\sum_{c=1}^{C}\mathrm{P}_{X\mid Y}\left(x|c\right)\mathbb{P}(c)},\quad\mathrm{M}(x)_{y}\doteq\log\mathrm{P}_{Y\mid X}\left(y|x\right).

(1)

Again, subscripts may be omitted if the context is clear.
Each of these objects has its counterpart denoted with subscript
r
when related to the retain set
\mathcal{D}_{r}
.
This includes the ideal NN
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}_{r}
learned from
\mathcal{D}_{r}
, the true distribution
\mathbb{P}_{r}
, and the proxy
\mathrm{P}_{r}
.

Kullback-Leibler divergence from distribution
p_{Y|X}
to
q_{Y|x}
is denoted as
\mathrm{KL}_{Y|x}\left(p\middle\|q\right)\doteq\sum_{y=1}^{C}p_{Y|x}(y)\log(p_{Y|x}(y)/q_{Y|x}(y)).
Its expectation over
\mathbb{P}
is denoted
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left(p\middle\|q\right)
.

3.2
A family of unlearned models

For any first-order method mentioned in Sect.
2
, the NN update

\Delta(x)=\mathrm{f}_{\tilde{\theta}}(x)-{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)
cannot be quantified in terms of
unlearning efficacy: it is a function of the optimization trajectory
and carries no information about the ideal classifier
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}_{,Y|x}
.
Our key idea is to replace
\Delta
by the proxy shift

\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)\doteq{\mathrm{M}_{r}}(x)-\mathrm{M}(x)
,
computed from the explicit parametric proxies of the data structure,
for which the shift is fully interpretable.
To our knowledge, this is the first work to provide a closed form, interpretable decomposition of the unlearning error in terms of end-to-end proxies quality.

We indeed consider a family of unlearned models defined in the logit space, for all
0\leq\eta\leq 1
:

\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)\doteq{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\Delta\mathrm{M}(x),\qquad\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)\doteq{\mathrm{M}_{r}}(x)-\mathrm{M}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

(2)

We find the optimal value
\eta_{\max}
as the upper bound of a theoretically safe regime (see App.
C.1
and
C.2
).
The final proposed unlearned model is then this precise
\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta_{\max}}
. The update
\eta_{\max}\Delta\mathrm{M}
may be implemented as a logit processor on top of the initial classifier
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}
taken as a black-box provided it outputs a logit vector.
If
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}
is a white box NN, then one may cast this update directly in its parameters.
The standard option fine-tunes the NN parametrized by
\theta
with a the teacher-student distillation:

\min_{\delta\theta}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta_{\max}}\middle\|\mathrm{p}_{\theta+\delta\theta}\right),\quad\text{with }{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta_{\max}}\doteq\mathrm{softmax}\left({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}+\eta_{\max}\Delta\mathrm{M}\right).

(3)

3.3
Assumptions

A1

The data are an independent sampling of the true distributions:
\mathcal{D}\sim\mathbb{P}^{\mid\mathcal{D}\mid}
and
\mathcal{D}_{r}\sim\mathbb{P}_{r}^{\mid\mathcal{D}_{r}\mid}
.

A2

The original true distribution is a mixture of form
(
45
)
:

\mathbb{P}=\pi_{r}\mathbb{P}_{r}+\pi_{f}\mathbb{P}_{f}\quad\text{with}\quad\pi_{r}+\pi_{f}=1.

(4)

A3

The initial NN perfectly captures the true distribution of the data:
\forall x\in\mathcal{X},\,{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)=\mathbb{P}_{Y\mid X}(\cdot\mid x)
.

Definition 1

(Admissible proxies)
.

We say that an ordered pair of proxy distributions
(\mathrm{P},{\mathrm{P}_{r}})
is
\mathbb{P}
-
admissible
if, and only if, it verifies the equation:

\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right).

(5)

A4

\mathrm{P}
is a better proxy for
\mathbb{P}
than
\mathrm{P}_{r}
, in the sense that
(\mathrm{P},{\mathrm{P}_{r}})
is
\mathbb{P}
-
admissible
.

Remarks.

Assumption A3 is the objective of cross-entropy training: minimizing it drives
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
to
\mathbb{P}_{Y\mid X}
(App.
E
, Fig.
3
). It enables splitting the learning from the unlearning errors (App.
D
bounds the cost of relaxing it by
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
).
Assumption A4 is verifiable experimentally before starting the unlearning process.
Our experiments in Sect.
5.2
show that the better the proxy, the more likely it will verify this assumption.

3.4
Theoretical guarantees

This section gives a sketch of the reasoning, which is fully detailed in App.
C
and
D
.

First, App.
C.1
justifies our choice (
2
) of unlearned models.
These are indeed a family of solutions to an optimization problem of information geometry, looking for an update similar to the initial NN
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
, but closer to the retain proxy
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
than the initial proxy
\mathrm{P}
. An interpretation is that an observer seeing samples drawn from the unlearned model
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}
is likely attributing them to
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
rather than
\mathrm{P}
.

Second, App.
C.2
seeks a safety range for
\eta
insuring that the unlearned
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}
provides an improvement.
The quantity
\eta_{\max}
denotes the biggest scale at which one can amplify the exact unlearning signal extracted through the proxies while transferring it into the NN.
This justifies our further choice to take
\eta=\eta_{\max}
in the experiments of Sect.
5
.
Furthermore, finding
\eta_{\max}
is simple with a 1-d line search over the empirical evaluation of a convex function.

The main quantity of the study,
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)
, gauges how close the probits
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}
of the unlearned model are from the ideal distribution given by the conditional true distribution
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}_{,Y|x}
.
We look for a proof of soundness showing that the unlearned model is closer to this ideal than the initial NN.

App.
D.2
first expands the divergence to exhibit an interpretation in terms of modeling shift:

Proposition 1
.

For any
\eta\in[0,\eta_{\max}]
, the divergence from the ideal retrained model to our unlearning target
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)
is tightly bounded by:

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|\mathbb{P}\right)}_{\text{initial divergence}}+\eta\left(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)}_{\text{modeling shift}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\pi_{f}).

(6)

The first term of the bound is close to zero either when the posteriors of
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
and
\mathbb{P}
are similar (nothing to unlearn), or when
\eta
is close to
1
.
The second term of the bound can be small even if the proxies
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
may not accurately model their respective targets
({\mathbb{P}_{r}},\mathbb{P})
.
It outlines that we should model both
\mathbb{P}
and
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
in a similar way so that their KL divergences compensate. Modeling only the retrain set
24
is sub-optimal.
As a final remark, (
6
) is our tightest bound. It indeed becomes an equality for
\eta=\eta_{\max}
(see App.
D.2
).

App.
D.3
states another bound which is less tight than (
6
) but it reflects the intuition of why modeling the conditional distributions
X|Y
proxies as accurate as possible is an advantage.

Proposition 2
.

For any
\eta\in[0,\eta_{\max}]
we have the intuitive bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)\leq\eta\left(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{X\mid Y}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{X\mid Y}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}(\pi_{f})+\mathcal{O}(1-\eta).

(7)

Finally, the appendix shows that
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)
can be exactly written as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\right)+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta\gamma(X,\eta)\right]}_{<0,\forall\eta\in[0,\eta_{\max}]}+\eta\left(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)}_{<0\text{ if $({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})$ is ${\mathbb{P}_{r}}$-admissible}}\right)

App.
D.1
shows that the second term is negative in the safety range.
The third term is negative if
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
is
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
-admissible,
i.e.

{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
is a better proxy for
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
than
\mathrm{P}
.
The proof of soundness reads as:

Proposition 3

(Provable decrease of KL)
.

For any
\eta\in[0,\eta_{\max}]
, if one further assumes that the pair of proxies
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
is
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
-admissible,

\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\right)

(8)

The above result is only a sufficient condition over the decreasing. However it suggests that by striving to achieve the best possible modeling of the data and create
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
an
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
-admissible pair of proxy, it can only improve the resulting unlearning.

Unlearning aware data modeling

This section shows how to take into account the unlearning scenario to construct proxies
(\mathrm{P},{\mathrm{P}_{r}})
that are close to their respective target distributions.
This is important for two reasons: i) Assumption A4 is valid, ii) the bound of Prop.
2
is small.
Our method can be applied to any type of parametric distributions.
This section illustrates it with Gaussian distributions to model
X|y

(Eq. (
1
) recovers the posteriors
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|x\right)
and
\mathrm{P}\left(\cdot|x\right)
), or empirical Dirac distribution to model
(X,Y)

4.1
Naive models

LDA
is a first model considering one distribution per class sharing a single covariance matrix, both for
\mathrm{P}
and
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
.
There are
2C
expectation vectors and 1 covariance matrix.

\mathrm{P}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{y};\Sigma),\quad{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{r,y};\Sigma).

(9)

QDA
is the variant using one covariance per class. There are
2C
expectation vectors and
C
covariance matrices:

\mathrm{P}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{y};\Sigma_{y}),\quad{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{r,y};\Sigma_{y}).

(10)

DIR
is an alternative based on the empirical distribution,
i.e.
a sum of Diracs

\mathrm{P}:(X,Y)\sim\mathcal{D},\quad{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:(X,Y)\sim\mathcal{D}_{r}.

(11)

App.
F
shows that this creates an unlearned model, independent of
\eta>0
, s.t.
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}(x)={\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)
if
x\notin\mathcal{D}_{f}
, and
for the forget set:

{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}(x)}_{c}=\begin{cases}0&c=y(x),\\
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)_{c}\,/\,(1-{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)_{y(x)})&c\neq y(x).\end{cases}

(12)

This is useless in practice.
Yet, the KL distillation drives the unlearned NN
towards
a total annihilation of the forget set.
The global effect is a strong decrease of the probit related to
y(x)
for the forget data.
This is interesting for class-wise unlearning but dramatic for the subclass forgetting (see Sect.
5.1
).

4.2
Mixture models

Better modeling takes into account the data from the forget set.
Following assumption A2, for each class
y
, we infer the distribution of the forget set
\mathcal{D}_{f}(y)
with a data model
\mathrm{P}_{f}\left(x|y\right)
.
For each class
y
, the rules of total probabilities gives:

\mathrm{P}(x\mid y)=(1-\pi_{f}(y)){\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(x|y\right)+\pi_{f}(y)\mathrm{P}_{f}\left(x|y\right),

(13)

where
\pi_{f}(y)=\nicefrac{{|\mathcal{D}_{f}(y)|}}{{|\mathcal{D}(y)|}}

is the true intra-class proportion of the forget set.
This proxy is more informative yielding an update
\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)
if and only if there exists a natural spread between the forget and the retain set at the raw data level.
It precisely checks if there is something to unlearn (or not).

LDA-Mix
considers one distribution per class and per state
s\in\{r,f\}
with a shared covariance per state.
There are
2C
expectation vectors and
2
covariance matrices:

{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{r,y};\Sigma_{r}),\quad\mathrm{P}_{f}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{f,y};\Sigma_{f}).

(14)

QDA-Mix
is the variant with one covariance per class and state.
There are
2C
expectation vectors and
2C
covariance matrices:

{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{r,y};\Sigma_{r,y}),\quad\mathrm{P}_{f}:X|y\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{f,y};\Sigma_{f,y}).

(15)

The method
DIR
stays unaffected by this awareness.

4.3
Doubling labels

Another refinement
artificially considers the variable
(y,s)\in\llbracket C\rrbracket\times\{r,f\}
as a label itself and produces a mixture on these
2C
labels. Doing so, the produced proxy on the initial data intrinsically estimate the quantity
\mathrm{P}\left(s|x\right)
which helps the unlearning.
To our knowledge, no other unlearning work lift the labeled data
(x,y)
in upper dimension while doubling the label dimension in order to have a richer mixture.
This subtle shift results in a final mixture on the posterior rather than on
X\mid y
.
App.
F
details the projection back to dimension
C
to update the initial model.

LDA-2C
applies a unique LDA on
(y,s)\in\llbracket C\rrbracket\times\{r,f\}
.
There are thus
2C
expectation vectors and
1
covariance matrix, whose estimation is more robust than for
LDA-Mix
.

\mathrm{P}:X|y,s\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{s,y};\Sigma),\quad{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:Y|x\sim\mathrm{P}(Y|x,r).

(16)

QDA
remains unaffected by double labels.

DIR-2C

\mathrm{P}:(X,Y,s)\sim\mathcal{D}\times\{r,f\},\quad{\mathrm{P}_{r}}:Y|x\sim\mathrm{P}(Y|x,r).

(17)

App.
F
shows that this creates an unlearned model, independent of
\eta>0
, s.t.
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}(x)={\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)
if
x\notin\mathcal{D}_{f}
, and
for the forget set:

{{{\mathrm{p}_{\tilde{\theta}}}}(x)}_{c}=\frac{|\mathcal{D}_{r}(y(x))|}{|\mathcal{D}(y(x))|}{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)_{c}+\frac{|\mathcal{D}_{f}(y(x))|}{|\mathcal{D}(y(x))|}\begin{cases}0&c=y(x),\\
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)_{c}\,/\,(1-{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)_{y(x)})&c\neq y(x).\end{cases}

(18)

This avoids the total canceling in (
12
). Again, this unlearned model is useless in practice, but it plays the important role of the teacher in the KL distillation.
The effect is a slight decrease of the probit related to
y(x)
for the forget data.

Criticism of the SOTA baselines.

Even if through the lense of accuracies, SOTA methods seems to perform unlearning, Figure

SCRUB is not stable in term of KL by design: the gradient ascent to move away from the teacher over the forget set implies a catastrophic behavior.
It reaches good statistics in Table

In practice, without this criterion, its unstable behavior makes it a poor baseline.
The other methods reach a ceiling because they quickly over-unlearned.
SalUn, GA, RL+FT, and GA+FT destroys the forget accuracy.
They cause catastrophic forgetting and without a clear stopping criterion, this instability makes them useless in practice.

shows that FT goes in the right direction but matching the ideal NN

6
Conclusion: advantages and limitations

Our theoretical framework introduces
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}={\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}+\eta\,\Delta\mathrm{M}
, a closed-form unlearning operator
emerging from an explicit constrained optimization problem (App.
C
). The extracted unlearning signal is derived from any data proxies. We derive bounds (tight in Prop.
1
and pedagogical in Prop.
2
) on the KL divergence to the retrained model under a
verifiable admissibility criterion A4.
The decreasing property in Prop.
3
is a theoretical guarantee that cannot be verified before the unlearning process, contrary to the previous bounds.
Three concrete advantages emerge:
(i)
the procedure unlearns
black-box networks outputting logits,
(ii)
the
distilled NNs systematically beats the analytic target itself
(Tab.
2
), and
(iii)
the Stein bound
(App.
G
) translates
{\mathrm{KL}}_{f}
into a minimum number of
attacker queries, replacing fragile empirical MIA scores with an
interpretable, closed-form privacy guarantee.

We see the following limitations.

(i)
The protocol assumes the data controller still holds

\mathcal{D}_{r}
to fit
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
. As a countermeasure,
DIR-2C
only needs
\mathcal{D}_{f}
and the proportions
\pi_{f}
label-wise.
Also, the distillation may use a data set
{\mathcal{D}_{r}}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{D}_{f}
different than
\mathcal{D}
.

(ii)
In high feature dimension, as for ResNet, estimating
\mathrm{LDA}
/
\mathrm{QDA}
covariances become ill-conditioned.
As a countermeasure, we resort to dimension reduction by projecting features onto a small subspace and restrict covariances to diagonal matrices (App.
H
).

(iii)
Admissibility A4 is checked on the empirical
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
before unlearning; admissibility on the true

\mathbb{P}
therefore holds only up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
of good training assumption A3.
(iv)
The decrease property
3
cannot be verified before unlearning because the unlearner do not have access to
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
to verify that
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
is
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
-admissible.

(v)
Our analysis proposes a short list of possible proxies without any claim about their optimality.

Appendix A
Machine unlearning overview

This appendix expands the related work presented in Sect.
2
.

A.1
Closed-form modeling

Closed-form modeling of class-conditional densities has a rich history in statistics.
Among these, LDA is the simplest closed-form maximum-likelihood classifier under class-conditional Gaussian data with a shared covariance
[
54
]
(see App.
E
).
Linear probing, which freezes a pretrained encoder and trains only the final linear layer, is a standard diagnostic for representation quality
[
1
,
41
]
.

43
show that full fine-tuning can distort strong pretrained features, reducing out-of-distribution accuracy, while last-layer probing preserves feature geometry.
Their findings have motivated last-layer strategies in transfer learning.
The neural collapse phenomenon
[
62
]
further justifies this: near convergence, within-class variability is small and approximately isotropic across classes, a regime in where a LDA head is nearly Bayes-optimal.

A.2
Machine unlearning

The literature is now broad enough that several survey papers
cover the field from complementary angles
[
59
,
74
,
50
,
49
]
.

Motivation and regulatory context.

The renewed interest in machine unlearning is driven by data-protection
regulations such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and its
“right to be forgotten”
[
72
]
,
together with the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and similar
frameworks worldwide. Beyond legal compliance, unlearning has been
identified as a tool for removing the influence of poisoned, biased,
mislabelled, copyrighted or otherwise undesirable training samples

[
4
,
3
,
28
]
,
and as a primitive for AI safety (e.g., suppressing hazardous knowledge in
foundation models)
[
47
]
.

Exact unlearning.

Machine unlearning was formalised by
4
, who introduced
the term and proposed summation-form algorithms that allow data removal
without full retraining. The gold standard remains exact removal, in
which the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from a model
retrained from scratch on the retained set

[
4
,
3
,
65
,
26
]
.

26
initiated the modern study of approximate
deletion in the specific case of
k
-means clustering, providing the first
formal definition of deletion efficiency.
3
achieve
exact unlearning in deep models via SISA training, which partitions the data
into shards so that only the affected shard needs to be retrained on
deletion. Although SISA reduces retraining cost by isolating deletions, it
requires storing intermediate checkpoints for every shard, and at scale this
storage overhead and the accuracy loss caused by aggressive sharding make it
impractical for large deep networks.

Gradient-based approximate unlearning.

To avoid retraining, the dominant paradigm relies on gradient-based weight
updates. Gradient ascent on the forget-set loss is the simplest baseline and
is used as a primitive in several methods

[
32
,
6
,
68
]
.

68
provide an unrolled-SGD analysis of the verification
error and show how a training-time penalty can ease subsequent unlearning.
Knowledge-distillation variants jointly enforce forgetting and retention:
SCRUB
[
44
]
trains a student that minimises the KL
divergence to the original model on the retained data while maximising the
loss on the forget set, and
8
use an
incompetent teacher whose soft labels guide the student to suppress class
knowledge. SalUn
[
19
]
restricts updates to the weights
identified as most responsible for the forgotten data via gradient-based
saliency, reaching state-of-the-art results on image classification and
generation, and
36
show that model sparsification
prior to unlearning closes the gap between exact and approximate unlearning
across multiple algorithms.
67
propose UNSIR, an
impair repair scheme in which an error-maximising noise first degrades
class knowledge and a repair step then restores retain-set accuracy, and

57
provide gradient-descent-based deletion
algorithms that handle adversarial sequences of deletion requests for convex
models. Beyond their empirical strengths, these methods share a structural
limitation: the update follows an optimisation trajectory that admits no
closed-form characterisation in terms of the data distribution or the
resulting predictive shift.

Fisher information and synaptic dampening.

Fisher-based methods identify which parameters encode the forgotten data and
selectively modify them.
30
derive a Fisher
Information Matrix (FIM) scrubbing procedure that perturbs weights in order
to remove cohort information, and
31

extend this analysis via a Neural Tangent Kernel linearisation that better
handles the null-space of the weights and bounds the information accessible
from input output observations.
20
propose Selective
Synaptic Dampening (SSD), a retrain-free two-step method that uses the FIMs
of the training and forget sets to select and dampen the parameters most
important to the forget set. SSD is computationally light and does not
require retained data at unlearning time, but its forgetting guarantee is
coupled to the quality of the diagonal FIM approximation, which does not
expose the structure of the data distribution.

55
introduce L-CODEC, a randomised
conditional-independence test that selects a Markov blanket of parameters,
making Newton-style updates tractable for very large vision and NLP models.

Influence functions and certified removal.

Influence functions
[
40
]
, borrowed from robust
statistics, approximate the leave-one-out parameter shift via a single
Newton step on the training loss.
33
exploit this
connection to derive certified data removal for
\ell_{2}
-regularised linear
models: a calibrated noise mechanism masks the residual Newton-step error,
yielding an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
differential-privacy certificate.

65
extend the theoretical analysis by bounding
generalisation after unlearning, and
35

propose a projection-based deletion algorithm whose cost is linear in
the feature dimension and independent of the dataset size for linear and
logistic models.
73
extend influence-based
unlearning to the deletion of features and labels, providing closed-form
updates and certified guarantees for strongly convex losses.
Beyond linear models, extending influence-function guarantees to non-convex
deep networks remains challenging
[
40
,
33
]
:
the Hessian approximation is hard to control, and no closed-form bound on
the predictive shift in terms of the data distribution is available. More
recent work such as
7
uses Langevin dynamics to
obtain certified unlearning without convexity assumptions, but the resulting
bounds still ignore the class-conditional structure of the data.

Weight-space and Bayesian methods.

A complementary line of work reasons about model uncertainty in parameter
space rather than via optimisation trajectories.

29
extend FIM scrubbing to a mixed-privacy setting by
targeting only non-core data, and
63
scale FIM-based
scrubbing to deep networks via rank-one Hessian approximations. The Laplace
approximation
[
12
]
provides a principled Gaussian
posterior over weights and has been adopted in Bayesian unlearning to
characterise the uncertainty introduced by data deletion

[
58
]
.
All of these methods operate entirely in weight space: their error
guarantees are coupled to Hessian-approximation quality and do not decompose
in terms of the quality of the class-conditional data model.

Geometry-based and training-free unlearning.

A recent direction exploits the geometric structure of trained
representations to obtain closed-form, training-free corrections.

45
build on Neural Collapse theory
[
62
]

to show that the orthogonal projection of a simplex Equiangular Tight Frame
(ETF) onto the subspace of retained classes remains an ETF in the
lower-dimensional space, which yields a provably optimal forgetting
operator. Their method, POUR-P, delivers this correction in closed form and
without gradient steps. In a similar spirit,

39
project model weights onto the
subspace orthogonal to the principal directions of the forget concepts,
rendering the model blind to that information without retraining. While
these methods are fast and elegant, the optimality guarantees of
projection-based approaches such as POUR rely on the terminal
neural-collapse regime, and neither family provides a data-distribution
bound on the residual unlearning error.

Theoretical guarantees and certification criteria.

33
measure unlearning success as

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-indistinguishability, mirroring differential privacy

[
16
]
. Total variation and KL divergence between the ideal
retrained model and the empirically unlearned model have since been adopted
as natural criteria

[
65
,
30
,
7
]
, but
nearly all theoretical results are stated in terms of algorithmic
properties (Hessian approximation quality, loss Lipschitz constants) rather
than in terms of the data distribution. A separate line of work studies the
auditability of unlearning definitions and the difficulty of certifying
forgetting in arbitrary training pipelines

[
69
]
,
underlining the gap between algorithmic and distributional guarantees.
To our knowledge, no prior work provides a closed-form decomposition of the
expected KL divergence directly in terms of the quality of distributional shift of
models fitted to the data (See Prop.
1
).

Membership inference as an evaluation tool.

Membership inference attacks (MIAs) were introduced by

66
, formally connected to overfitting by

75
, and sharpened by the likelihood-ratio attack
LiRA of
5
, which achieves the highest statistical
power at low false-positive rates thanks to held-out shadow models. MIAs
have become the standard black-box audit for unlearning efficacy

[
70
,
34
]
: an effective method
should prevent an adversary from distinguishing forgotten examples from
held-out non-members.
34
show that population-level MIAs
systematically overestimate the privacy protection of approximate methods,
and they propose U-LiRA, a per-example extension that serves as a stricter
worst-case audit. Complementary evaluation protocols have also been
developed:
27
introduce the Interclass
Confusion test together with the EU-
k
and CF-
k
baselines, and the
findings of the first NeurIPS unlearning competition

[
70
]
provide a broad empirical assessment of
the state of the art under a unified evaluation pipeline.

Unlearning beyond classification: generative models and LLMs.

Although the present work focuses on classification, machine unlearning has
recently been extended to generative models, where it is used to suppress
copyrighted, sensitive or harmful content. For diffusion models,

22
propose Erased Stable Diffusion (ESD),
a fine-tuning method that removes a target concept from the model weights
using only a textual description and negative guidance. SalUn

[
19
]
also covers concept erasure in diffusion models. For
large language models,
18
introduce the
“Who’s Harry Potter?” protocol for unlearning copyrighted content,

51
propose the TOFU benchmark of fictitious authors for
controlled evaluation, and
47
introduce the WMDP benchmark
for hazardous-knowledge unlearning together with the RMU representation
misdirection method. These efforts highlight that distributional unlearning
guarantees are even more elusive in the generative regime, since the
relevant “data distribution” is itself implicit in the model.

Federated and distributed unlearning.

A parallel literature studies unlearning in the federated setting, where
clients hold local data and only model updates are shared with a central
server. FedEraser
[
48
]
reconstructs the global model
from cached client updates while excluding the contribution of a target
client, and a growing number of follow-up methods address client-level and
sample-level deletion under heterogeneous data and asynchronous
participation;
64
provide a comprehensive
survey of methods, design guidelines and evaluation metrics. While these
contributions are largely orthogonal to ours, the federated
setting introduces communication and asynchronicity constraints absent in
the centralised case, they share with the centralised literature the
property that error guarantees are stated in terms of algorithmic quantities
rather than data-distribution quality.

Appendix C
Justifications

C.1
The unlearned model family

We look for a model
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}
, which is an update similar to the initial NN
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
, closer to the ideal retain distribution
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
than the initial distribution
\mathbb{P}
.
This objective is framed in terms of KL divergences in the following optimization problem:

\min_{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}}\;\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\right)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|\mathbb{P}\right),\quad\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)=1.

(19)

An interpretation is the following. Among all distributions
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}
that fall on the
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
side of the decision boundary (discriminating whether samples from
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}
are more likely to be sampled from
\mathbb{P}
or
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
), we seek the one closest to
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
in relative entropy
i.e.
the most
{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}
-like distribution that the Neyman-Pearson likelihood test would still attribute in expectation to
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
instead of
\mathbb{P}
.

By definition of the
{\mathrm{KL}}
one always has the identity

\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|\mathbb{P}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)\log\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|X\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(y|X\right)}.

Following
[
9
, Chap. 12]
, we write the Lagrangian with multipliers
\eta\geq 0,\nu
associated to (
19
) as

J({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}};\eta,\nu)\doteq\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)\log\frac{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)}{{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\left(y|X\right)}-\eta\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)\log\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|X\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(y|X\right)}+\nu\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right).

Cancelling the derivative with respect to
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)
, we obtain:
\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}

\mathbb{E}\log\frac{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)}{{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\left(y|X\right)}+1-\eta\mathbb{E}\log\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|X\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(y|X\right)}+\nu=0.

(20)

Rearranging the terms, the final expression can be expressed as:

\mathbb{E}\left[\underbrace{\log({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\left(\cdot|X\right))}_{\tilde{\mathrm{f}}(X)-\tilde{\gamma}(X;\eta)\mathbbm{1}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\underbrace{\log{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\left(\cdot|X\right)}_{{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(X)-\gamma(X)\mathbbm{1}}+\eta\underbrace{\log\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|X\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot|X\right)}}_{\text{exact unlearning signal }\Delta\mathbb{M}(X)}-\underbrace{\Delta\gamma(X;\eta)}_{\text{normalizing term}}\right].

(21)

Remark

In their work
9
,
\eta=\eta_{0}
is chosen precisely on the attacker decision boundary :

\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta_{0}}\middle\|\mathbb{P}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta_{0}}\middle\|{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\right)=0.

so that an attacker sampling from
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}
is not, in expectation, in capacity to decide whether the drawn samples are coming from
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
or
\mathbb{P}
.
By the symmetry
\eta\longleftrightarrow 1-\eta
, the same
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}
minimizes

\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\right)
constrained to the reversed inequality.

Estimation

One solution is to impose that (
21
) holds for any
x
.
This translates in the logit space to the solution:

\tilde{\mathrm{f}}(x)={\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\Delta\mathbb{M}(x),

a.k.a.
precisely our modeling of the unlearning family in Section
3
.
The true exact unlearning signal
\Delta\mathbb{M}(x)=\log{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|X\right)/\mathbb{P}\left(\cdot|X\right)
being inaccessible in practice,
our key idea consists in creating proxies
(\mathrm{P},{\mathrm{P}_{r}})
that approximate this quantity.

\Delta\mathrm{M}\approx\Delta\mathbb{M}

Rephrasing, given proxies
(\mathrm{P},{\mathrm{P}_{r}})
we obtain our unlearned model family as

\tilde{\mathrm{f}}(x)={\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)

More precisely, it is the unbiased solution of the following optimization problem:

\min_{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}}\;\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\right)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right),\quad\mathbb{E}\sum_{y}{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}\left(y|X\right)=1.

To conclude, we consider the following family of unlearned models:

\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)\doteq{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\Delta\mathrm{M}(x),\qquad\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)\doteq{\mathrm{M}_{r}}(x)-\mathrm{M}(x).

(22)

As introduced in Sect.
3.1
, logits and probits are related by:

{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}(x)\doteq\mathrm{softmax}(\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)),\qquad\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)=\log({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}(x))+\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta).

(23)

C.2
The safety range of
\eta

We first relate the normalization
\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)
to
\gamma(x)
, the normalization of the initial NN.

\mathrm{LSE}:\mathbb{R}^{C}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the Log-Sum-Exponential function defined as
\mathrm{LSE}(z)\doteq\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{C}e^{z_{i}}\right)
. It verifies,
\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{C},\forall\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\log(\mathrm{softmax}(z))

\displaystyle=z-\mathrm{LSE}(z)\mathbbm{1}

(24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{LSE}(z+\kappa\mathbbm{1})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{LSE}(z)+\kappa

(25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{LSE}(\log(z))

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{C}z_{i}\right)

(26)

where
\log(z)
is an abuse of notation defining the vector
(\log z_{1},\ldots,\log z_{C})
for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{C}_{>0}
, and
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{C}
is the all-one vector.

Indeed, applying
\mathrm{LSE}
to the logits of the unlearned model
\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)=\log{\mathrm{p}_{\tilde{\theta}}}_{\eta}(x)+\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)\mathbbm{1}
, isolates the normalization term due to (
25
) and (
26
):
\mathrm{LSE}(\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x))=\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)
. Since we forge the unlearned model by adding the proxy shift to the initial NN (
2
), we have:

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:AppUnlearn}}}{{=}}\mathrm{LSE}\left({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\log\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(\cdot|x\right)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:AppSoftmax}}}{{=}}\mathrm{LSE}\left(\log{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x)+\gamma(x)\mathbbm{1}+\eta\log\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(\cdot|x\right)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:LSE2}}}{{=}}\gamma(x)+\mathrm{LSE}\left(\log\left(\frac{{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}(x){\mathrm{P}_{r}}^{\eta}\left(\cdot|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}^{\eta}\left(\cdot|x\right)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{A3}}}{{=}}\gamma(x)+\mathrm{LSE}\left(\log\left(\frac{\mathbb{P}(x){\mathrm{P}_{r}}^{\eta}\left(\cdot|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}^{\eta}\left(\cdot|x\right)}\right)\right),

with component-wise multiplication and division between vectors.
This links the normalization offset
\tilde{\gamma}
of the unlearned model to
\gamma
, the one of the initial model.
Thanks to (
26
), we have:

\displaystyle\Delta\gamma(x;\eta)

\displaystyle\doteq\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)-\gamma(x)=\log\left(\sum_{y=1}^{C}\mathbb{P}(y|x)\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(y|x\right)}\right)^{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}(Y|x)}{\mathrm{P}(Y|x)}\right)^{\eta}\right]\right).

Note that
\Delta\gamma(x;0)=0,\,\forall x
, which is expected as the unlearning operator is void at
\eta=0
. Its derivative w.r.t.
\eta
equals

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Delta\gamma(x;\eta)}{\partial\eta}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\log\left(A(Y,x)\right)\right]}{E_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\right]},

with
A(Y,x)\doteq\nicefrac{{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}(Y|x)}}{{\mathrm{P}(Y|x)}}
, and because the derivative of
a^{x}=\exp(x\log(a))
equals
a^{x}\log(a),\,\forall a>0
. Note that, at point
\eta=0
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\Delta\gamma(x;\eta)}{\partial\eta}\right|_{\eta=0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[\log\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}(Y|x)}{\mathrm{P}(Y|x)}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{KL}_{Y|x}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)-\mathrm{KL}_{Y|x}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right).

On expectation over
\mathbb{P}_{X}
, we obtain:

\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\frac{\partial\Delta\gamma(X;\eta)}{\partial\eta}\right|_{\eta=0}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left(\mathbb{P}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{A4}}}{{\leq}}0,

if
\mathrm{P}
is a better proxy of
\mathbb{P}
than
\mathrm{P}_{r}
(see Sect.
3.3
, assumption A4).
This assumption is verified experimentally in Sect.
5.2
.

In the same way, the second derivative verifies,
\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
:

\frac{\partial^{2}\Delta\gamma(x;\eta)}{\partial^{2}\eta}=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\log^{2}\left(A(Y,x)\right)\right]}{E_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\right]}-\frac{\mathbb{E}_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\log\left(A(Y,x)\right)\right]^{2}}{\left(E_{Y|x\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[A^{\eta}(Y,x)\right]\right)^{2}}\geq 0

thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

To recap,
\Delta\gamma(\eta)\doteq\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta\gamma(X;\eta)\right]
enjoys the following properties:

•

\Delta\gamma(0)=0

•

\Delta\gamma^{\prime}(0)\leq 0

•

\Delta\gamma(\eta)
is convex over
[0,+\infty)
.

Consequently, there exist
\eta_{\max}>0
such that
\Delta\gamma(\eta)\leq 0
for all
0\leq\eta\leq\eta_{\max}
.

In practice,
\eta_{\max}
is found by a line search as the zero of the function:

h(\eta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{LSE}({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x)+\eta\,\Delta\mathrm{M}(x))-\mathrm{LSE}({\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}(x))\simeq\Delta\gamma(\eta).

(27)

Remark

The careful reader will spot that the derivative of
\Delta\gamma
in expectation cancels out exactly at
\eta_{0}
: the regime of indistinguishability between
\mathrm{P}
and
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
. Then
\eta_{0}\leq\eta_{\max}
as the negative derivative hypothesis indicates. Taking
\eta=\eta_{\max}
ensures we have been through the equality boundary and we are in the
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)>\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)
regime.

We now define the error of prediction between the true distribution
\mathbb{P}_{r,Y|x}
and the unlearned model
{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(y,x;\eta)

\displaystyle\doteq\log({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right))-\log({\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\left(y|x\right)).

This quantity is worth studying as it appears in the computation of
\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}_{Y\mid x}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=1}^{C}{\mathbb{P}_{r}}(y|x)\log\left(\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}(y|x)}{{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}(y|x)}\right)=\langle{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(\cdot|x\right),\mathcal{E}(\cdot,x,\eta)\rangle.

(28)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(x,y;\eta)

\displaystyle=\log({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right))-\tilde{\mathrm{f}}_{\eta}(x)+\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:delta-M}}}{{=}}\log({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right))-f(x)-\eta\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)+\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)

\displaystyle=\log({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right))-\log{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\left(y|x\right)-\gamma(x)-\eta\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)+\tilde{\gamma}(x;\eta)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{{\mathbb{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right)}{{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\left(y|x\right)}\right)-\eta\log\left(\frac{{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\left(y|x\right)}{\mathrm{P}\left(y|x\right)}\right)+\Delta\gamma(x;\eta)

(29)

Plugging (
29
) into (

) and taking the expectation leads to:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\tilde{\mathrm{p}}}_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{p}_{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta\gamma(X,\eta)\right]}_{<0,\forall\eta\in[0,\eta_{\max}]}+\eta\left(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{\mathrm{P}_{r}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|\mathrm{P}\right)}_{<0\text{ if $({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})$ is ${\mathbb{P}_{r}}$-admissible}}\right).

The first term is the KL divergence without unlearning,
i.e.
between the true retain distribution and the initial NN probits.
App.
C.2
shows that the second term is negative under A4 (
5
) for any
0\leq\eta\leq\eta_{\max}

The third term is negative with one extra assumption: if
({\mathrm{P}_{r}},\mathrm{P})
is
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
-admissible,
i.e.

{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
is a better proxy for
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}
than
\mathrm{P}
is.

This proves Prop.
3
.

We now take the expectation w.r.t.
{\mathbb{P}_{r}}_{Y|x}
and then the expectation w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}_{X}
.
As earlier, we assume A4 (

) holds and

so that

Appendix E
About discriminant analysis proxies

Homoscedastic Gaussian data.

Linear Discriminant Analysis (
\mathrm{LDA}
) assumes a generative model where each class follows a Gaussian distribution with a shared covariance:

x\mid y=k\;\sim\;\mathcal{N}(\mu_{k},\,\Sigma).

(34)

The posterior probability has the following expression
[
54
]
:

\mathbb{P}(y=k\mid x)=\frac{\exp(w_{k}^{\top}x+b_{k})}{\sum_{j=1}^{C}\exp(w_{j}^{\top}x+b_{j})},

with row weights
w_{k}=\Sigma^{-1}\mu_{k}
and biases
b_{k}=\ln\pi_{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}w_{k}\mu_{k}
, where
\pi_{k}
is the class prior.
The empirical moment
(\hat{\pi}_{k},\hat{\mu}_{k},\hat{\Sigma})
estimated from
\mathcal{D}
yield the Maximum Likelihood Estimator which exactly has the form of a linear layer
\left(W_{\mathrm{LDA}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{C})^{\top},b_{\mathrm{LDA}}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{C})^{\top}\right)
followed by the softmax operator.
Indeed, as

, this MLE coincides with the global minimizer of the cross-entropy loss over any linear model, making
\mathrm{LDA}
the Bayes-optimal classifier in this setting
[
54
,
62
]
. This explains the success of last-layer linear probing on strongly pre-trained features
[
43
,
1
]
: when frozen features are approximately class-conditional Gaussian distributed, fitting a linear head by cross-entropy is equivalent to computing the MLE in closed form.

Heteroscedastic Gaussian data.

When the homoscedasticity assumption is relaxed, each class
k
has its own covariance

x\mid y=k\;\sim\;\mathcal{N}(\mu_{k},\,\Sigma_{k}).

(35)

(36)

so that the posterior probability

[
54
]

Under the heteroscedastic Gaussian assumption and in the limit

[
54
]
.

Even when the Gaussian assumption is only approximate,

matters equally.

The data processing theorem states that any processing of the data
\hat{Y}
reduces the KL divergence. The attacker’s test is a processing, therefore:

\displaystyle\mathrm{KL}_{Y|x}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{{\mathrm{p}_{\tilde{\theta}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{KL}\left(\mathcal{B}(\alpha)\middle\|\mathcal{B}(1-\beta)\right)=(1-\alpha)\log\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\beta}\right)+\alpha\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\alpha=\beta)}}{{=}}(1-2\alpha)\log\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\right).

This inequality is at the root of the Stein lemma.

Querying
N>1
inputs brings more information. Provided they are independent (assumption A1), their KL divergences sum up.
This sum can be written
N\cdot\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{{\mathrm{p}_{\tilde{\theta}}}}\right)
where the expectation is indeed an average over the queries as reported experimentally in Sect.
5
.
At the end, we get a necessary number of queries to reach a reliability given by
1-\alpha
:

N\geq\frac{(1-2\alpha)\log\left(\nicefrac{{1-\alpha}}{{\alpha}}\right)}{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{KL}_{Y|X}\left({\mathbb{P}_{r}}\middle\|{{\mathrm{p}_{\tilde{\theta}}}}\right)}.

This number comes with two flavors. The attacker may query test data (resp. forget data) and we plug
{\mathrm{KL}}_{t}
(resp.
{\mathrm{KL}}_{f}
) in the formula.
For instance, for
\alpha=1/1\,000
,
N_{t}=138
for
{\mathrm{KL}}_{t}=0.05
,
N_{f}=69
for
{\mathrm{KL}}=0.1
, which are typical values reported in Sect.
5
.
The good question is to compare
N_{f}
with the actual number of forget data: if
|\mathcal{D}_{f}|<N_{f}
, then the attacker is unable to distinguish the two classifiers with forget data, and even if he knows all of them.

Appendix H
Handling large NN

This appendix shows that scaling our framework to networks whose hidden
representations are too high-dimensional for a direct
\mathrm{LDA}/\mathrm{QDA}
fit
requires
no theoretical change
. It is purely a question of choice of the proxies.

Composed proxy

The whole machinery of Sect.
3
only requires to model the input distribution so that

\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)={\mathrm{M}_{r}}(x)-\mathrm{M}(x)
can be computed in closed form.
Up to now we instantiated this proxy directly on
x
via

\mathrm{LDA}
or
\mathrm{QDA}
. When
x
lives in a space too large for a class-conditional
Gaussian fit, we simply compose:

\mathrm{P}\left(x|y\right)\;\doteq\;\mathrm{P}_{0}\left(F(x)|y\right),\qquad F\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{k},

where
\mathrm{P}_{0}
is the standard
\mathrm{LDA}
or
\mathrm{QDA}
proxy on the space
F(\mathcal{X})
and
F
is any fixed injection from the high-dimensional input space onto a low-dimensional
target. The retain proxy
{\mathrm{P}_{r}}
is built identically with the same
F
.
Plugging (
H
) into the framework yields a logit shift

\Delta\mathrm{M}(x)=\Delta\mathrm{M}_{0}\!\big(F(x)\big)

that is computed in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
All the closed-form properties of discriminant analysis are preserved.
Assumptions and the bounds of Sect.
3
carry over verbatim.
Any
F
that yields a low-dimensional representation on which
\mathrm{LDA}/\mathrm{QDA}
is accurate is admissible.

Practical instance for ResNet-18

The specific
F
we use in the ResNet-18 experiments is anecdotal — it is
one convenient choice among many. We take

F(x)\;=\;\Omega\,\phi^{(3)}(x),

(42)

where
\phi^{(3)}
is the activation just after the third residual stage
(
\dim\sim 8{\times}10^{3}
at our resolution) and

\Omega\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
(
k\!=\!512
) is a deterministic
semi-orthogonal Johnson–Lindenstrauss
sketch
[
37
,
11
]
obtained as the
orthogonal factor of a Gaussian matrix via QR decomposition, with a fixed seed shared
across all proxy variants. The hook depth (
\phi^{(3)}
) is the standard
mid-level/pre-classification choice: earlier stages have too small a
receptive field, later ones already encode logits and would make the proxy
redundant with
{\mathrm{f}_{\theta}}
.

F
enters only the proxy and enables recovering approximately Gaussian
per-class features, as the semi-orthogonal projection of the high-dimensional
activations induces a sub-Gaussian design on each class
conditional
[
14
,
71
]
.
The MSE finalizer regresses the per-sample target

\Delta(x)=\eta_{\max}\Delta\mathrm{M}_{0}(F(x))
on the network’s own penultimate
representation
\phi(x)
by
closed-form least squares.
The alternative finalizer, the KL distillation, distils the full logit target
into the network by backpropagation. The proxy and the
finalizer may live in different representations, exactly as in the DINOv2 pipeline.

Scalability

This framework scales to arbitrary backbones at fixed cost in
k
because the proxy fit is performed in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
regardless of the input
dimension.
Operator
F
is the only choice that needs revisiting for a new
architecture: any low-dimensional representation on which
\mathrm{LDA}/\mathrm{QDA}
is reasonable
(an intermediate activation, a learned embedding, a sketch of any of these)
is a valid drop-in. One has to construct
F
to make the good proxy assumptions hold.
