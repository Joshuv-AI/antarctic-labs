Application of the QUAL2K model for assessing water quality in the anthropogenically impacted Dil Stream, Turkey
Authors: Meltem Yağcıoğlu, Mehmet Salim Öncel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOne of the most important surface water sources in the districts of Gebze and Dilovası (Kocaeli, Turkey) is the Dil Stream and its tributaries. Today, while part of the Dil Stream has high-quality water, the southern section, which is open to settlement, has become low-quality water due to the controlled/uncontrolled discharge of agricultural, domestic, and industrial wastewater. Based on its characteristics and the QUAL2K model structure, the Dil Stream has been divided into a total of 11 reaches, including its tributaries. Samples were collected over 12 months from 28 sampling points in these 11 reaches. Model calibration was performed using April data and model validation was performed using November data, for the parameters pH, carbonaceous biological oxygen demand (CBOD), dissolved oxygen (DO) and total kjeldahl nitrogen (TKN). During the calibration, the R2 values were calculated as 0.72 for DO, 0.98 for CBOD, 0.96 for TKN and 0.92 for pH, while during the validation, they were calculated as 0.81 for DO, 0.92 for CBOD, 0.97 for TKN and 0.91 for pH. The RMSE and MAE values applied to evaluate model performance, in the calibration values were 0.99 and 0.80 for DO, 0.91 and 0.88 for CBOD, TKN 1.20 and 1.03, and pH 0.73 and 0.66. In the validation, the values were calculated as 1.22 and 1.09 for DO, 1.82 and 1.51 for CBOD, 1.80 and 1.63 for TKN and 0.40 and 0.39 for pH. The QUAL2K model, applied for the first time in Dil Stream, will provide a basis for water and environmental management in future decision-making processes for different scenarios.Keywords: Water quality, QUAL2K, Dil Stream, Calibration, ValidationSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Hydrology

Introduction

Turkey is among the countries with limited water resources. According to official data reported in the 2024 Performance Program Report, Türkiye’s technically and economically usable surface water potential is 91.9 billion m³. An examination of the sectoral distribution of water demand reveals that the largest share, 74%, belongs to agriculture, while the remaining 26% is allocated to drinking and domestic use, industry, and other water-related needs1. Although approximately 65% of these demands are currently met from surface water resources, considering Türkiye’s economic growth trajectory and the effects of climate change, it is likely that the country will be among those facing water scarcity in the coming years.

One of the primary reasons for the selection of the Gebze and Dilovası districts (Kocaeli, Turkey) as industrial zones in the 1960 s was their relatively high water potential. However, uncontrolled urban and industrial growth, the increasing number of industrial facilities, and the resulting rise in domestic and industrial wastewater discharge loads have led to significant deterioration in water quality. Given that industrial activities are expected to continue intensively in this region for many years, the risk of water scarcity poses a major threat. Moreover, the growing water demand of industrial enterprises and the associated increase in unit water prices have begun to create substantial economic challenges in the region.

In the Gebze and Dilovası districts, one of the most important surface water resources, despite their limited number, is the Dil Stream and its tributaries. While certain reaches of the Dil Stream exhibit high water quality and support diverse aquatic fauna and flora, several of its tributaries have become severely degraded. As a result of long-term excessive agricultural, domestic, and industrial water use, as well as controlled and uncontrolled wastewater discharges, these tributaries have effectively been transformed into turbid, malodorous wastewater channels devoid of aquatic life.

Water quality models provide a means of representing the relationships between water bodies and pollutant loads, as well as the concentrations of individual pollutants as a function of the characteristics of the receiving water body. The understanding of stream systems is greatly enhanced through the application of mathematical models and modern computational tools2. Widely used water quality models worldwide include BASINS, AQUATOX, WEAP, MIKE-11, MIKE-SHE, WASP, SWAT, and QUAL2K. The literature on one-dimensional river modeling is not limited to QUAL2K alone; numerous models serving different purposes have been developed, including Advection–Dispersion Equation (ADE)-based transport models, MIKE11, WASP, HEC-RAS, MASCARET, OTIS, and TOASTS. Some of these models are particularly strong in hydrodynamic–transport processes such as dispersion, tracer transport, unsteady flow, and transient storage3,4. In contrast, within this family of models, QUAL2K provides a suitable modeling framework that is widely used especially for the process-based assessment of river water quality, organic matter–oxygen balance, nutrients, and scenario-based pollution load analyses. The QUAL2K model represents the river as a laterally and vertically well-mixed one-dimensional system and calculates the transport of water quality variables based on advection, longitudinal dispersion, load inputs, load outputs, biochemical reactions, and mass transfer processes5. The model conceptualizes the simulated river as a series of end-to-end, well-mixed units connected to each other through transport and diffusion processes. Each unit is considered an ideally mixed reactor, and during the simulation, the relevant equations based on the law of mass balance are solved for this smallest computational unit. In this context, the mass balance includes the advective transport and dispersion of water6. In this study, the longitudinal dispersion coefficient was not treated as a calibration parameter; instead, the representation of transport processes was evaluated within QUAL2K’s hydraulic calculation structure. Therefore, in order to ensure the accurate representation of advection and dispersion processes in the model, hydraulic inputs such as flow rate, velocity, channel width, depth, and slope were defined for each river reach in accordance with field measurements and model geometry.

The QUAL2E model was developed by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (USEPA) for waste load distribution, discharge permit determination, and other pollution assessments7. Its new version, QUAL2K, began to be implemented worldwide in 2002. QUAL2K can model the flow of a stream system with multiple branches. This one-dimensional model can simulate the effects of point and non-point sources. In this study, QUAL2K was applied as a one-dimensional, water quality model capable of simulating the stream system for water quality management of the Dil Stream and its tributaries.

One of the first studies in the literature to apply QUAL2E was published in 1988 by the Kentucky Division of Water Department for Environmental Protection8. In the report titled “Water Quality Study of Little Bullskin Creek Basin to Determine Effluent Limits for Purnell Sausage Company and The City of Simpsonville,” a water quality study was conducted in the Little Bullskin Creek basin to calibrate the QUAL2E water quality model for the purpose of determining wastewater limits. The modeling results revealed that the discharge facility contributed to the sediment deposits near Purnell, based on Simpsonville’s high suspended solids concentration and the sediment deposits near Purnell. Subsequently, the number of studies in different countries and on different streams has increased9–18. QUAL2K has been widely applied in studies since 2002. A review of the literature reveals that the QUAL2K model has been used in a versatile manner in terms of the study areas where it has been applied, the number of observation stations, and the sampling time interval. While the model can be applied to stream networks with lengths between approximately 5 km and 16 km19–22, it has also been successfully applied to long streams ranging from 120 km to 350 km in length23–25. For model calibration, it has been observed that both extensive time-series datasets26–28 and data sets spanning only a few months have been used29–31. Furthermore, the literature indicates that many QUAL2K applications are characterized by a relatively small number of sampling stations32–34, generally fewer than 2535–38.

Literature investigations have shown that point sources exert their effects over short distances and that there is a research gap in the high-resolution modeling of small-scale watersheds under intense anthropogenic pressure. In small-scale streams such as the Dil Stream, point sources affect short distances and cause dramatic changes in water quality. By integrating the high-resolution dataset obtained in this study into the QUAL2K model, this study aims to evaluate the applicability of the model in the Dil Stream and, at the same time, to provide a management tool for similar watersheds discharging into sensitive marine environments such as the İzmit Bay.

Materials and methods

Study area

Approximately 18 km long, the Dil Stream has a catchment area of 131.54 km² and a dendritic drainage system. Flowing north to south, it passes through cattle and sheep farms, vegetable and fruit cultivation areas, residential areas, and industrial areas before emptying into the Sea of Marmara via the Gulf of Izmit.

Various highways, expressways, motorways, freeways, and railways connecting the Asian and European continents pass over the Dil Stream. The Dil Stream flows into the sea at an elevation of 396 m. It has an average gradient of 2%. Although Dilovası is one of the smallest districts in Kocaeli in terms of area, 5 of the 14 Organized Industrial Zones (OIZ) in Kocaeli Province are located in Dilovası.

To optimally reflect the stream system consisting of the Dil Stream and its tributaries in the model, the stream system shown in Fig. 1 was created using ArcGIS 10.5 and 28 sampling points were marked.

Sampling and analysis

Samples were collected from 28 sampling points in Dil Stream over a period of 12 months. The sampling method used was carried out in accordance with the “Regulation on Sampling and Biological Sampling from Surface Waters, Groundwater, and Sediment” published by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry of the Republic of Turkey in 201539. In-situ measurement parameters (pH, dissolved oxygen, water temperature), stream flow rate, and bottom width were measured in the field for the water samples collected. In the samples brought to the laboratory, analyses were performed for the parameters of biological oxygen demand (BOD5), ammonium nitrogen (NH3_N), nitrate nitrogen (NO3_N), and total Kjeldahl nitrogen (TKN).

In water samples, BOD5, oxygen consumed in 5 days at 20 °C, with nitrification inhibition (5210), Nitrate nitrogen, ammonium nitrogen, and total Kjeldahl nitrogen were analyzed using the 4500-NO3 B, 4500-NH3 C titrimetric method, and 4500-Norg B macro-Kjeldahl Standard Method, respectively40. All these analyses were performed at the Gebze Technical University Institute of Earth and Marine Sciences Laboratory.

Model application of Dil Stream

In the QUAL2K model, the study area is divided into reaches representing stream sections with fixed geometric characteristics (slope, bottom width, etc.). Each reach area is divided into segments that constitute the shortest parts of the model simulation. Depending on the stream characteristics and model structure, the study area is divided into a total of 11 reaches, including the main stream and tributaries. A representative schematic representation of the stream system created for Dil Stream and its tributaries, as defined for the QUAL2K model, is shown in Fig. 2. These 11 reaches are numbered sequentially downstream from the main tributary inflow (headwater-HW). Point sources, observation points, and headwaters were identified along the stream through field studies. Stoichiometric, kinetic, and physical parameters were determined at 28 sampling points (9 point sources, 14 observation points, 5 headwaters) for use as model input.

In the QUAL2K model, many parameters are required to simulate stream water quality. These include water quality parameters from point and non-point sources, meteorological data, and hydraulic data (flow, channel slope, bottom width, and Manning’s coefficient). Steady-state flow equilibrium is applied for each model access.

In the QUAL2K model, the use of carbonaceous biochemical oxygen demand (CBOD), rather than biochemical oxygen demand (BOD₅), is preferred to achieve more specific and accurate water quality simulations. The model distinguishes between fast-reacting and slow-reacting carbonaceous biochemical oxygen demand components, denoted as CBODf and CBODs, respectively. In the present study, measured BOD₅ values were converted to ultimate carbonaceous biochemical oxygen demand (CBODu) and subsequently assumed to be equivalent to the fast-reacting CBOD fraction (CBODf).

Results and discussion

Calibration and validation

In this study, the model was calibrated and validated using four water quality parameters (DO, pH, CBOD and TKN). The selected parameters are among the fundamental indicators that determine the suitability of water for industrial and agricultural purposes and directly influence water quality. To determine the model coefficients that best represent the study area, data from April were used for the calibration, and data from November were used for the validation, based on the 12 months of data obtained.

The calibrated values of the model coefficients are summarized in Table 2. Hydraulic parameters were determined using the channel slope, bottom width, and Manning’s n coefficient specific to the site. During dissolved oxygen calibration, the reaeration coefficient (k₂) was calculated using user-specified values based on the reach. In the model, the tributaries connected to the main stream were treated as point source loads23,28,43–46. The model calibration R² values for the selected water quality parameters DO, CBOD, TKN, and pH were found to be 0.72, 0.98, 0.98, and 0.84, respectively.

Dissolved oxygen is an important parameter that indicates the state of biological growth and the level of water pollution. According to QUAL2K outputs, the DO profile in Dil Stream shows that high oxygen inflow dominates in the northern section of the stream (18–13 km) in both calibration and validation graphs. Model outputs show that DO concentrations up to 8 km in the stream varied between April and November but remained close to each other without seasonal changes after 8 km (Fig. 4). The validation graph shows that the DO concentration stabilizes after the 8th kilometer. This can be explained by the turbulence created in the current during the rainy season in November, when the validation process was carried out, due to the increased discharge from the tributaries and the increased downstream flow velocity in the main riverbed. Turbulence can increase the reaeration coefficient by continuously renewing the surface area of the water, thereby enabling faster oxygen transfer. This situation is thought to be a possible cause for the stabilization of the concentration observed after the 8th km. Changes in DO concentrations are observed at the 12.8 km side branch, which is under industrial pressure, where it connects to the main branch. CBOD values, which indicate the biodegradability of certain pollutants in the stream, start at a concentration of 7 mg O2/L in Reach 1, which forms the headwaters of the Dil Stream basin, indicating good water quality. It is noteworthy that the CBOD value on the calibration graph increases from 7 mg O2/L to 15 mg O2/L when the first tributary joins the main stream at 12.8 km. Reach 2, located on this tributary, consists of the section of the stream where both industrial facilities discharge and small livestock farms are located. The increase in CBOD concentrations upon the connection of Reach 2, polluted with livestock wastewater, to the main branch is seen in the model outputs, indicating that the high organic matter and nitrogen load carried by the tributary increases oxygen consumption. Studies by Wen et al.47 and Cesoniene et al.48 also indicate an increase in BOD5 values due to the entry of wastewater from livestock activities into the stream. At the 7.5 km point of the stream, the dilution effect of the connecting tributary resulted in a limited decrease in CBOD values (12 mg O2/L), but the values remained high compared to the upper reaches of the stream (Fig. 3). The increase in CBOD concentration observed at the same point in the validation graph can be explained by the increase in the number of animals in local farms in November and the fact that this is a common practice throughout the region. The discharges from industrial and domestic wastewater treatment plants located on Reach 9 and Reach 10 cause an increase in organic load and CBOD concentration at 2.18 and 4.24 km of the stream. Supporting this finding, studies by Yang et al.49 and Ramalho et al.50 mention that treatment plant discharges cause an increase in BOD5 and organic load values in the stream.

The pH value in Reach 1, which forms the headwaters of the stream, starts at 9.4 and reaches the mouth of the stream with small fluctuations along the stream, ending at a value of 8.05 where it flows into the Gulf of Izmit. The validation graph shows a similar situation for the pH value. TKN values start at 8.6 mg/L in Reach 1, which forms the headwaters, increase up to 8 km, and reach a maximum value of 18.8 mg/L. At the point where it reaches Izmit Bay, this value drops to 11.87 mg/L. Similarly, in the validation graph, it starts at 18 mg/L in Reach 1 and reaches a maximum value of 36.3 mg/L. Reach 4, 5, 6, 7, and Reach 8 are located at the side branch connection point where the TKN value reaches its maximum value in both graphs. There are point sources in each of these reaches. The stream section that forms Reach 4 has an OIZ and the discharge point of this zone’s wastewater treatment plant. Reach 5 consists of the stream section where the sewage inlet belonging to the settlement is located, Reach 6 consists of the stream section where the leachate from the old landfill site is located, and Reach 7 consists of the stream sections where the discharge points of industrial facilities in various sectors, along with livestock activities, are located.

It has been observed that the model outputs produced by the QUAL2K model applied to the Dil Stream show similar results to QUAL2K studies conducted in streams worldwide that are exposed to comparable industrial and agricultural pressures. One of these studies is the work conducted by Zhang et al.51 on the Hongqi River, a polluted tributary in the Taihu Lake Basin in China. In this study, the QUAL2K model was applied to estimate the water quality and environmental capacity of the Hongqi River. Similar to the Dil Stream discharging into İzmit Bay, Taihu Lake, to which the Hongqi River is connected, also discharges into Meiliang Bay; furthermore, the main pollution sources of the Hongqi River were reported to be domestic and industrial wastewater, surface runoff from agricultural lands, and sewage discharges. In the calibration studies of the Hongqi River, which shows strong similarity to the Dil Stream, it was observed that the DO parameter, in particular, exhibited deviations from the observed values compared to other parameters, and that industrial discharges directly caused a decrease in the river’s DO concentration. In another similar study in the literature, Santy et al.52 evaluated the potential impacts of climate change and land use change on river water quality in the Ganga River in India using the QUAL2K model. In the 238 km long Ganga River, which is exposed to excessive pollutant discharges from various industries and agricultural runoff, scenario applications using the QUAL2K model identified DO as the most sensitive indicator. Unlike large-scale river systems such as the Ganga River, pollution loads in small-scale streams like the Dil Stream cause abrupt changes over much shorter distances. This situation makes the importance of the 28 sampling points used in our study in capturing pollution loads more evident compared to large-scale studies.

In this study, data obtained from 28 sampling points on the stream network formed by Dil Stream and its tributaries were entered into the model, and the model was run using data from 6 observation points selected from these sampling points. Calibration and validation studies were performed at different times (April-November). In terms of continuity and data quality in the operation of the model, April and November were selected as they stand out in terms of data completeness, hydrological representation, and model stability. The model performance results of the study within the framework of the above statistical approaches are shown in Table 3. The R2 values indicate the relationship between the observed and simulated values. These values were calculated as 0.72 for DO, 0.98 for CBOD, 0.96 for TKN, and 0.92 for pH during the calibration phase, and 0.81 for DO, 0.92 for CBOD, 0.97 for TKN, and 0.91 for pH during the validation phase. These high correlation coefficients between the observed and simulated values indicate that the simulated data are in acceptable agreement with the measured data and that the model can be reliably applied to the Dil Stream and its tributaries. Due to the fact that the study area comprises very different types of land use (agricultural land, livestock, residential, and industrial areas), dissolved oxygen is one of the parameters most affected by controlled and uncontrolled discharges at various points in the Dil Stream. Since DO is a parameter that responds very rapidly to hydrodynamic, biochemical, and atmospheric processes, it is an expected situation for the R2 value to be lower compared to other parameters.

According to Gültepe53, RMSE and MAE, being error measures, are metrics that show high performance inversely proportional to low results. For example, an RMSE value equal to zero indicates that the model performs well. However, the RMSE and MAE values are noticeably higher than expected. The slightly higher RMSE and MAE values corresponding to the R2 values of the QUAL2K model applied to water quality in Dil Stream indicate that this is due to sudden load increases caused by the effect of tributary inflows, particularly in the 12.8 km and 7.5 km areas. Field observations show that load inflows vary in terms of time and flow rate due to the impact of industrial and irregular discharges. Modeling load shocks and heterogeneous mixing conditions at such main branch connection points is challenging and can lead to increased point estimation errors in the model. However, the literature shows that RMSE results can range from 0.14–2.86 for DO and 0.24–5.65 for CBOD, respectively, while MAE results can range from 0.083–2.78 for DO and 0.08–4.44 for CBOD, respectively24,54–57. In addition to these three statistical criteria, performance evaluation criteria recommended by Moriasi et al.58, which are widely used and accepted as standard in water quality modeling worldwide, were utilized to determine acceptable error limits. The RSR value, which represents the ratio of the RMSE to the standard deviation, was calculated for all parameters. In the recommended criteria, an RSR value of ≤ 0.70 is specified as an ‘acceptable/satisfactory’ threshold for model performance. These values are classified as ‘very good’ for 0.00 ≤ RSR ≤ 0.50, ‘good’ for 0.50 ≤ RSR ≤ 0.60, and ‘satisfactory’ for 0.60 ≤ RSR ≤ 0.70. In this study, all RSR values calculated for the four separate parameters (CBOD, TKN, DO, pH) that underwent calibration and validation are below this threshold (≤ 0.70). For the calibration period, the RSR values calculated for TKN, CBOD, DO, and pH were 0.47, 0.24, 0.69, and 0.68, respectively, while for the validation period, the corresponding RSR values were calculated as 0.41, 0.32, 0.61, and 0.67, respectively. These results confirm that all parameters met the acceptable threshold (≤ 0.70) according to the performance ratings; TKN and CBOD were classified in the “Very Good” category, whereas DO and pH were classified as “Satisfactory”. After acceptable performance levels were obtained as a result of model calibration and validation, sensitivity analysis was performed using a ± 20% variation in the fast CBOD oxidation rate and a ± 20% variation in the ammonium nitrification coefficient in order to evaluate the response of model outputs to changes in key kinetic parameters. As a result of the analysis, it was determined that a 20% increase in the fast CBOD oxidation rate reduced CBOD concentration in the downstream sections by an average of 10.3%, while a 20% increase in the ammonium nitrification coefficient reduced DO concentration, particularly in the downstream section of the river, by an average of 14.1%. These findings indicate that, in addition to the calibrated model’s success in representing the observed data, it also generally reflects the expected hydrochemical responses to changes in kinetic parameters.

Conclusions

In this study, the QUAL2K water quality model was calibrated and validated based on data obtained from samples collected at 28 points on the Dil Stream network, which was divided into 11 reaches. The model outputs reveal that water quality parameters along the stream exhibit significant changes/breaks, particularly due to the influence of tributaries and point sources. The decreases in DO graphs parallel the increases seen in CBOD and TKN graphs, which is consistent with the model’s process-based approach representing water quality kinetic processes.

As is the case worldwide, Turkey is negatively affected in terms of natural resources by both migration from neighboring countries and internal population movement. The population-attracting urban centers that emerge from this population mobility sometimes make access to the most important resources of life difficult. The concentration of the population in these centers also brings needs such as industry, agriculture, and animal husbandry to these centers, affecting vital resources, primarily water.

One of the main objectives of this study is to model the Dil Stream, located in the Kocaeli/Dilovası–Gebze regions, which are population-attracting urban centers, using an applicable water quality model, and to take the first step toward evaluating its water quality under different possible scenarios.

Based on the calibrated and validated model results in this study, the local authority should establish a regular and sustainable water quality monitoring program in the basin. It is an important need to establish flow observation stations at critical locations and ensure continuous monitoring in order to determine the water potential carried by Dilderesi and to identify the amount of water and pollutant parameter loads originating from tributary confluence points. The implementation of an integrated basin management approach involving all stakeholders in the basin and increasing data sharing through university–public–industry cooperation will make an important contribution to environmental management processes. Along with these, it will be recommended to the local authority that the QUAL2K model, whose applicability has been demonstrated in this study, be used as a decision-support tool to evaluate future water quality improvement and pollution load reduction strategies and basin-scale water quality management actions.
