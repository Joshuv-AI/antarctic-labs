Tumefactive parenchymal lesion of neurosarcoidosis responsive to infliximab
Authors: Karn Dhillon, Shuvro Roy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Neurosarcoidosis is an uncommon manifestation of systemic sarcoidosis and can involve any part of the nervous system. Intraparenchymal tumefactive lesions are particularly rare and pose a diagnostic challenge due to their radiographic resemblance to high-grade gliomas or metastatic disease. Optimal management remains complex, especially in cases refractory to or dependent on glucocorticoids.

Introduction

This report presents a case of tumefactive parenchymal neurosarcoidosis, which is a rare and diagnostically challenging manifestation of sarcoidosis which can closely mimic malignant and demyelinating processes on imaging.

Case description

A 37-year-old female presented to establish care in our neurology clinic, complaining of right leg sensory changes, right foot weakness, and generalized fatigue. She was previously diagnosed with pulmonary sarcoidosis, confirmed by pathology specimen obtained from lung biopsy performed nine years prior to presentation. She had previously maintained clinical remission while on a treatment regimen of regular infliximab infusions and oral glucocorticoids. However, due to insurance denial she had been off infliximab therapy for approximately nine months at time of presentation, and had been solely on a regimen of oral prednisone during this nine-month period.

Neurologic examination was notable for 4/5 strength at the right iliopsoas, reduced vibration sensation and proprioception in the right lower extremity, and symmetric hyperreflexia 3+ in the bilateral upper and lower extremities. The patient was treated with intravenous methylprednisolone 1000 mg daily for 3 days, and was restarted on infliximab 900 mg (5 mg/kg) every 4 weeks. This treatment regimen was selected based on therapeutic guidelines supporting the use of high-dose intravenous methylprednisolone in combination with a second-line steroid-sparing agent in the treatment of severe or refractory cases of neurosarcoidosis. While methotrexate has been the most widely used steroid-sparing agent for neurosarcoidosis [1], infliximab was preferred by the treatment team as this agent was concordant with the patient’s prior therapy resulting in clinical remission.

Repeat examination one month following treatment with this regimen demonstrated a return to full strength, and complete resolution of her sensory symptoms and generalized fatigue. Brain MRI was conducted two months after restarting infliximab, which demonstrated marked interval radiographic improvement which was characterized by near resolution of the lesion and previously seen mass effect (Fig. 2). Discussion

The formulation of granulomas are the hallmark abnormality in sarcoidosis, with neurological involvement seen in approximately 5-10% of total cases [2]. On imaging, neurosarcoidosis (NS) mimics many infectious, neoplastic, demyelinating, and inflammatory disease and can be a difficult diagnosis to make. Per the diagnostic criteria established by the 2018 Neurosarcoidosis Consortium Consensus Group (NCCC), a definitive diagnosis of NS requires biopsy confirmation. However, neural tissue biopsy is infrequently performed due to the associated risks which can lead to permanent neurological impairments. It is therefore essential to prioritize the exploration of alternative targets, such as endobronchial tissue or lymph nodes. The presence of clinical features of NS in conjunction with a histopathological confirmation of a systemic disease meets diagnostic criteria for probable neurosarcoidosis based on the 2018 NCCC guidelines [3].

Tumefactive parenchymal CNS lesions represent an especially rare subset of sarcoidosis-associated CNS lesions, representing as few as 5% of lesions. When present, these parenchymal granulomas are usually less than 1 cm in size and have been associated with a high failure rate to treatment with most immunomodulatory treatments. However, as seen in this case, lesions can mimic high grade glioma, demyelinating disease, metastasis, hemorrhage, or other mass lesions which poses a diagnostic challenge. Meningeal inflammation is a common imaging manifestation which aids in distinguishing NS from these other entities, occurring in 16-69% of patients. Leptomeningeal involvement is more common than pachymeningitis [4].

Glucocorticoids have historically been the mainstay of treatment for neurosarcoidosis. However, achieving and sustaining an optimal treatment response often require high doses, and can still be insufficient due to disease severity [5]. In cases refractory to glucocorticoids or in steroid-dependent patients, the steroid-sparing immunosuppressive agent infliximab has been increasingly utilized as a treatment option. Infliximab is a chimeric mouse–human monoclonal antibody targeting tumor necrosis factor–alpha (TNF-α), a critical cytokine for granuloma formation and maintenance. TNF-α inhibitor therapy is generally targeted to remission, often continued for several years after radiologic remission in those with a history of severe disease. Surveillance for disease recurrence is of utmost importance, as recurrence has been seen to occur as early as 3-6 months after discontinuation of TNF-α inhibitor therapy [6].

This case highlights the importance of timely immunosuppressive therapy in tumefactive parenchymal NS, demonstrating that early re-initiation of infliximab can lead to rapid clinical and radiographic improvement, reinforcing its role as a treatment option in severe or steroid-refractory cases.
