A dimension-free interpolation of Caffarelli’s contraction theorem
Bader Ammari, Alessio Figalli

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

The
L^{2}
optimal transport (or Brenier-McCann) map between two absolutely continuous probability measures
\mu=f(x)\,dx
and
\nu=g(y)\,dy
is the unique solution of the Monge problem for quadratic cost:

\min\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-T(x)|^{2}f(x)\,dx:T_{\#}\mu=\nu\right\}.

Here
T_{\#}\mu=\nu
means that
\mu(T^{-1}(A))=\nu(A)
for every Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
By the Brenier-McCann theorem (see
[
3
,
13
]
), the solution of this quadratic optimal transport problem is the gradient of a convex function,
T=\nabla\varphi
. This mapping is a fundamental object in optimal transportation theory and has found applications in numerous fields such as functional and geometric inequalities, probability, and machine learning (see
[
10
,
1
,
2
]
).

In
[
4
]
, Caffarelli proved that if
f=e^{-V}
and
g=e^{-W}
for some
V,W\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

D^{2}V\leq\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad D^{2}W\geq\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id}

almost everywhere, where
0<\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)},\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}<+\infty
, then the Brenier-McCann map
T
pushing
f\,dx
forward to
g\,dy
is globally Lipschitz with

(1.1)

\|DT\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sqrt{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}/\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}}.

Caffarelli’s contraction theorem is notable for its dimension-free Lipschitz bound
Equation

1.1
. This dimension independence allows one to transfer functional inequalities, such as log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities, via the Lipschitz transport map between the two measures. For further references, extensions, alternative proofs, and applications, we refer to
[
12
,
11
,
7
,
14
,
6
,
8
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to obtain analogous estimates for a family of probability measures interpolating between polynomial-type densities and log-concave densities. Our global estimate is uniform not only in the dimension
n
, but also in the interpolation parameters. For
p\in[n,+\infty)
and a potential
U>-p
, set

\Theta_{p}(U):=p\log\Bigl(1+\frac{U}{p}\Bigr),\qquad Z_{U,p}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\Theta_{p}(U)}\,dx,

whenever the integral is finite, and define

\rho_{U,p}:=Z_{U,p}^{-1}e^{-\Theta_{p}(U)}=Z_{U,p}^{-1}\Bigl(1+\frac{U}{p}\Bigr)^{-p}.

We also set
\Theta_{\infty}(U)=U
,
Z_{U,\infty}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-U}\,dx
, and
\rho_{U,\infty}=Z_{U,\infty}^{-1}e^{-U}
. Since
\Theta_{p}(t)\to t
as
p\to+\infty
, the family
\rho_{U,p}
interpolates between the densities considered in
[
5
,
9
]
and the log-concave densities appearing in Caffarelli’s theorem.

For
p\in[n,+\infty)
, we use the global shorthand

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{V,p}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{|\nabla V|}{\sqrt{p}+|\cdot|}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{W,p}^{(1)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{|\nabla W|}{\sqrt{p}+|\cdot|}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{V,p}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+|\cdot|^{2}}{p+V}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{-1},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{W,p}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+|\cdot|^{2}}{p+W}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{V,p}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+V}{p+|\cdot|^{2}}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{W,p}^{(0)}

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+W}{p+|\cdot|^{2}}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

When a local bound is needed, we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{W,p}^{(1)}(R)

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{|\nabla W|}{\sqrt{p}+|\cdot|}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}^{2},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{V,p}^{(0)}(R)

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+|\cdot|^{2}}{p+V}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{V,p}^{(0)}(R)

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+V}{p+|\cdot|^{2}}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{W,p}^{(0)}(R)

\displaystyle:=\Biggl\|\frac{p+W}{p+|\cdot|^{2}}\Biggr\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}.

Here and below, we use the convention
B_{+\infty}(0)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and omit the argument when
R=+\infty
.
At the endpoint
p=+\infty
, for
R<+\infty
, we use the conventions

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{c}_{V,\infty}^{(0)}(R)=\mathbf{C}_{V,\infty}^{(0)}(R)=\mathbf{c}_{W,\infty}^{(0)}(R)=\mathbf{C}_{W,\infty}^{(0)}(R)=1,\\
\mathbf{C}_{V,\infty}^{(1)}=\mathbf{C}_{W,\infty}^{(1)}=0.\end{gathered}

Inequalities such as
U>-p
are understood as void when
p=+\infty
.

We shall allow different source and target parameters, denoted by
d
and
D
. More precisely, we will study the monotone transport map from
\rho_{V,d}
to
\rho_{W,D}
, where

d,D\in[n,+\infty],\qquad d\leq D.

With this notation, the polynomial-to-polynomial case studied in
[
5
,
9
]
corresponds to
D=d<+\infty
, while the case
D=d=+\infty
corresponds to Caffarelli’s setup.

Remark 1.1
.

The lower bound
n\leq d
is natural for the maximum-principle argument: after taking the Monge-Ampère equation to the power
1/d
, the concavity of
\det^{1/d}
is available.

The restriction
d\leq D
is also natural from the point of view of tails. Indeed, if
n\leq D<d<+\infty
and
V(x)=W(x)=|x|^{2}
, then the radial Brenier map
T(x)=t(|x|)x/|x|
from
\rho_{V,d}
to
\rho_{W,D}
satisfies

Z_{W,D}^{-1}\int_{t(r)}^{+\infty}\frac{s^{n-1}}{(1+s^{2}/D)^{D}}\,ds=Z_{V,d}^{-1}\int_{r}^{+\infty}\frac{s^{n-1}}{(1+s^{2}/d)^{d}}\,ds.

Thus,
t(r)\simeq r^{(2d-n)/(2D-n)}
as
r\to+\infty
, which is superlinear when
d>D
. In particular, in this range, no global Lipschitz bound can hold in general for
T
.

We first state a global Hessian estimate for the polynomial-to-polynomial case considered in
[
5
]
. Its constant is uniform in
n,d,D
. To keep the statement readable, we collect the following global structural quantities, understood in the extended sense; if they make
C_{\mathrm{glob}}=+\infty
, then the estimate is void:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{V}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\max\{1,\mathbf{C}_{V,n}^{(0)}\}}{\min\{1,\mathbf{c}_{V,n}^{(0)}\}},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{W}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\max\{1,\mathbf{C}_{W,n}^{(0)}\}}{\min\{1,\mathbf{c}_{W,n}^{(0)}\}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{V}

\displaystyle:=\min\{1,\mathbf{c}_{V,n}^{(0)}\},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{W}

\displaystyle:=\max\{1,\mathbf{C}_{W,n}^{(0)}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{V}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{C}_{V,n}^{(1)},\qquad

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{W}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{C}_{W,n}^{(1)}.

These quantities control all larger parameters. Indeed, for fixed
x
, the ratio

\frac{p+U(x)}{p+|x|^{2}}

moves monotonically towards
1
as
p
increases, and
(\sqrt{p}+|x|)^{-1}
is decreasing in
p
. Thus, the zeroth-order upper and lower constants are controlled by their values at
p=n
and by the endpoint value
1
, while the first-order constants are largest at
p=n
. The ratios
\mathbf{C}_{V,p}^{(0)}/\mathbf{c}_{V,p}^{(0)}
and
\mathbf{C}_{W,p}^{(0)}/\mathbf{c}_{W,p}^{(0)}
need not be monotone in
p
, but they are bounded by
\mathfrak{q}_{V}
and
\mathfrak{q}_{W}
.
Set

\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{glob}}:=10^{6}\,\mathfrak{q}_{V}^{2}\mathfrak{q}_{W}^{2},\qquad A_{\mathrm{glob}}:=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\mathfrak{C}_{W}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{glob}}}{\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\mathfrak{c}_{V}},\qquad B_{\mathrm{glob}}:=\frac{8\mathfrak{L}_{V}\mathfrak{L}_{W}\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{glob}}}{(\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)})^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{V}^{2}},

and

C_{\mathrm{glob}}:=\sqrt{A_{\mathrm{glob}}+B_{\mathrm{glob}}}+\sqrt{B_{\mathrm{glob}}}.

Theorem 1.2

(Global dimension-free Hessian estimate)
.

Let
d,D\in[n,+\infty]
with
d\leq D
. Let
V>-d
and
W>-D
, assume that the normalizing constants are finite, and let
\nabla\varphi
be the Brenier map pushing
\rho_{V,d}
forward to
\rho_{W,D}
. Assume that

D^{2}V\leq\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad D^{2}W\geq\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},

where
0<\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)},\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}<+\infty
.
Then

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\mathrm{glob}}.

Taking
D=d<+\infty
in
Theorem

1.2
gives the polynomial-to-polynomial estimate of
[
5
]
in a form that is uniform in the parameters
n,d,D
, contrary to the result in
[
5
]
which had an exponential dependence in
d
.

While this theorem is extremely robust, it does not recover the sharp Caffarelli bound (
1.1
) in the case
d=D=+\infty
. To recover such a sharp result, we need first to prove quantitative bounds on the Hessian of
\varphi
in a fixed ball
B_{R}(0)
, then we will let
D\to+\infty
and
d\to+\infty
at first and finally let
R\to+\infty
.

To prove this refined result, we need to introduce the quantities
\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)
and
\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R)
. These are finite-parameter local growth constants coming from Fathi’s argument in
[
9
]
.

For
d,D\in[n,+\infty]
with
d\leq D
, set

(1.2)

\Gamma_{d,D}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle+\infty,&D=d<+\infty,\\[8.00003pt]
\displaystyle\left(\frac{2d-D}{D-d}\right)_{+},&d<D<+\infty,\\[8.00003pt]
0,&D=+\infty.\end{cases}

For
d\leq D<+\infty
and
0<R<+\infty
, set

s_{d,R}:=\max\{R,\sqrt{d}\}

and

\mathfrak{m}_{d,R}:=\frac{\omega_{n}(3s_{d,R})^{n}}{Z_{V,d}\bigl(\mathbf{C}_{V,d}^{(0)}(10s_{d,R})\bigr)^{d}}\left(1+\frac{100s_{d,R}^{2}}{d}\right)^{-d}.

Here,
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Also, define

\Psi_{W,D}(r):=Z_{W,D}^{-1}\int_{B_{r}(0)^{c}}e^{-\Theta_{D}(W(y))}\,dy,

and

(1.3)

\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R):=3\inf\{r\geq 0:\Psi_{W,D}(r)\leq\mathfrak{m}_{d,R}\},\qquad\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R):=1+\frac{\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)^{2}}{D}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}_{d,R}>0
and
\Psi_{W,D}(r)\to 0
as
r\to+\infty
, the radius
\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)
is finite.

For the localized estimate, with
\Gamma_{d,D}
as in
Equation

1.2
, define

(1.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d,D}(R)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{W,D}^{(0)}(\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R))\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R),&D<+\infty,\\[8.00003pt]
1,&D=+\infty,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Xi_{d,D}(R)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{D}{d}\,\frac{\mathbf{C}_{W,D}^{(1)}(\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R))}{\mathbf{C}_{W,D}^{(0)}(\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R))},\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\Gamma_{d,D}\right\},&D<+\infty,\\[8.00003pt]
0,&D=+\infty.\end{cases}

Finally, set

\displaystyle A_{V,W}^{d,D}(R)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\Lambda_{d,D}(R)}{\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\mathbf{c}_{V,d}^{(0)}(R)},\qquad

\displaystyle B_{V,W}^{d,D}(R)

\displaystyle:=\frac{4\mathbf{C}_{V,d}^{(1)}\Lambda_{d,D}(R)\Xi_{d,D}(R)}{(\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)})^{2}(\mathbf{c}_{V,d}^{(0)}(R))^{2}}.

Theorem 1.3

(Localized Hessian estimate)
.

Let
d,D\in[n,+\infty]
with
d\leq D
, and let
0<R<+\infty
. Let
V>-d
and
W>-D
, assume that the normalizing constants are finite, and let
\nabla\varphi
be the Brenier map pushing
\rho_{V,d}
forward to
\rho_{W,D}
. Assume that

D^{2}V\leq\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad D^{2}W\geq\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},

where
0<\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)},\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}<+\infty
. With the constants just defined,

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq\sqrt{A_{V,W}^{d,D}(R)+B_{V,W}^{d,D}(R)}+\sqrt{B_{V,W}^{d,D}(R)}.

For fixed
0<R<+\infty
and
d<+\infty
, the constants in
Theorem

1.3
with
D<+\infty
converge to the choices
\Lambda_{d,\infty}(R)=1
and
\Xi_{d,\infty}(R)=0
in
Equation

1.4
. Indeed, the convergence and tightness of
\rho_{W,D}
as
D\to+\infty
keep the Fathi’s radius
\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)
bounded, while
\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R)\to 1
,
\mathbf{C}_{W,D}^{(0)}(\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R))\to 1
, and
\Xi_{d,D}(R)=0
once
D\geq 2d
.

As we now show, to obtain the sharp Caffarelli limit, the order of limits is important: one first keeps
R<+\infty
fixed and lets
D\to+\infty
and then
d\to+\infty
, and only afterwards sends
R\to+\infty
. In the parameterization
R=\rho\sqrt{d}
, this means that
\rho=R/\sqrt{d}
tends to zero during the parameter limit.

The polynomial-to-log-concave case
D=+\infty
gives a direct interpolation between the polynomial estimates and Caffarelli’s theorem. In this case, the growth factor disappears from the bound.

Corollary 1.4
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem

1.3
with
D=+\infty
, for every
0<R<+\infty
one has

(1.5)

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(0))}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}}{\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\mathbf{c}_{V,d}^{(0)}(R)}}.

In particular,

\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\R^{n})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}}{\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\mathbf{c}_{V,d}^{(0)}}}.

Starting from (
1.5
), passing first to
d\to+\infty
for fixed
R<+\infty
, and then to
R\to+\infty
, allows us to recover Caffarelli’s contraction theorem.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
f=e^{-V}
and
g=e^{-W}
are probability densities, with
V,W\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

D^{2}V\leq\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},\qquad D^{2}W\geq\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}\,\mathrm{Id},

then the Brenier map
T
pushing
f\,dx
forward to
g\,dy
satisfies

\|DT\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sqrt{\mathbf{C}_{V}^{(2)}/\mathbf{c}_{W}^{(2)}}.

The approaches of
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
, which inspired
[
9
]
, are distinct: the latter relies on monotonicity of the optimal transport and concentration inequalities to deduce growth estimates, while the former uses the Monge-Ampère equation and a maximum-principle argument. We combine these two viewpoints. After deriving the two-parameter maximum principle, we prove a local finite-parameter growth estimate by Fathi’s argument and insert this explicit bound into the localized maximum-principle estimate. The endpoint
D=+\infty
is obtained from the corresponding endpoint differential inequality, and the constants above are arranged so that the local finite-
D
estimates converge to it for fixed
R<+\infty
.

2.
Proofs of
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3

We first prove the estimates under the additional assumption that the potentials and the Brenier potential are smooth, and that the second directional derivative whose supremum gives
\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}
attains its maximum. This allows us to write the maximum-principle computation at the level of second derivatives. Then, at the end of the paper, we recall the argument used in
[
5
]
to circumvent these issues.

We begin with the finite-parameter case
n\leq d\leq D<+\infty
, assume that
V>-d
and
W>-D
, and let
\nabla\varphi
be the Brenier map pushing
\rho_{V,d}
forward to
\rho_{W,D}
.

Recalling that
\Theta_{p}(t)=p\log\left(1+\frac{t}{p}\right)
and
\Theta_{\infty}(t)=t
, we have

\Theta_{p}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{p}{p+t},\quad\Theta_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\frac{p}{(p+t)^{2}},\qquad\Theta_{\infty}^{\prime}(t)=1,\quad\Theta_{\infty}^{\prime\prime}(t)=0.

The Monge-Ampère equation reads

Z_{V,d}^{-1}e^{-\Theta_{d}(V)}=Z_{W,D}^{-1}e^{-\Theta_{D}(W\circ\nabla\varphi)}\det(D^{2}\varphi).

Setting
F=\det^{1/d}
, we can rewrite it as

(2.1)

\Bigl(\frac{Z_{V,d}}{Z_{W,D}}\Bigr)^{1/d}F(D^{2}\varphi)=\exp\left(\frac{\Theta_{D}(W\circ\nabla\varphi)-\Theta_{d}(V)}{d}\right).

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
V,W,\varphi
are smooth and that the function

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\ni(x,e)\mapsto\langle D^{2}\varphi(x)e,e\rangle

attains its maximum at
(\overline{x},e_{1})
. Set
\overline{y}=\nabla\varphi(\overline{x})
and
\lambda=\varphi_{11}(\overline{x})
. Then

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\geq{}

\displaystyle\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)W_{11}\lambda^{2}-\Theta_{d}^{\prime}(V)V_{11}-2\Theta_{d}^{\prime\prime}(V)V_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)^{2}}{d}+\Theta_{D}^{\prime\prime}(W)\right)W_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}-\frac{2\Theta_{d}^{\prime}(V)\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)}{d}V_{1}W_{1}\lambda,

where
V
and its derivatives are evaluated at
\overline{x}
, while
W
and its derivatives are evaluated at
\overline{y}
.

In particular, since
D\geq d
, if
W_{11}>0
then

(2.3)

\displaystyle W_{11}\lambda^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\frac{d}{D}\frac{D+W}{d+V}V_{11}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}\frac{V_{1}^{2}W_{1}^{2}}{W_{11}(d+V)^{2}},

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

At the maximum point,
e_{1}
is an eigenvector of
D^{2}\varphi(\overline{x})
, and hence

\nabla\varphi_{11}(\overline{x})=0,\qquad D^{2}\varphi_{11}(\overline{x})\leq 0,\qquad\varphi_{i1}(\overline{x})=0\quad\text{for }i\neq 1.

Let

H(x):=\frac{\Theta_{D}(W(\nabla\varphi(x)))-\Theta_{d}(V(x))}{d}.

By
Equation

2.1
, the right-hand side is
e^{H}
. Differentiating twice in the
e_{1}
direction and using the concavity and monotonicity of
F=\det^{1/d}
give

0\geq(e^{H})_{11}=e^{H}(H_{11}+H_{1}^{2}).

At
\overline{x}
, using
\varphi_{i1}=0
for
i\neq 1
and
\varphi_{i11}=0
for all
i
, this becomes exactly
Equation

2.2
.

Since

\frac{\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)^{2}}{d}+\Theta_{D}^{\prime\prime}(W)=\frac{D(D-d)}{d(D+W)^{2}}\geq 0,

we may drop this term, as well as the nonnegative
V_{1}^{2}
term, to get

\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)W_{11}\lambda^{2}\leq\Theta_{d}^{\prime}(V)V_{11}+\frac{2\Theta_{d}^{\prime}(V)\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)}{d}V_{1}W_{1}\lambda.

After division by
\Theta_{D}^{\prime}(W)
and using
\Theta_{d}^{\prime}(V)=d/(d+V)
, Young’s inequality gives
Equation

2.3
.
∎

We first prove the growth estimates needed to use
Equation

2.3
.
The next proposition is a slight reformulation of Fathi’s monotonicity estimate
[
9
, Theorem 2.3]
. We recall that
\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)
and
\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R)
are defined as in
(
1.3
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
n\leq d\leq D<+\infty
and
0<R<+\infty
, and let
\nabla\varphi
be the optimal transport map pushing
\rho_{V,d}
forward to
\rho_{W,D}
.
Then

|\nabla\varphi(x)|\leq\mathcal{R}_{d,D}(R)\qquad\text{for a.e. }\,x\in B_{R}(0),

and

\frac{d}{D}\frac{D+|\nabla\varphi(x)|^{2}}{d+|x|^{2}}\leq\mathcal{G}_{d,D}(R)\qquad\text{for a.e. }\,x\in B_{R}(0).

Proof.

Let
\mu_{V,d}
and
\mu_{W,D}
denote the measures with densities
\rho_{V,d}
and
\rho_{W,D}
, respectively, and let
s=s_{d,R}
. For
x\in B_{R}(0)
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, every
z\in B_{3s}(x+6su)
satisfies
|z|\leq 10s
. Hence

\mu_{V,d}\bigl(B_{3s}(x+6su)\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{Z_{V,d}\bigl(\mathbf{C}_{V,d}^{(0)}(10s)\bigr)^{d}}\int_{B_{3s}(x+6su)}\Bigl(1+\frac{|z|^{2}}{d}\Bigr)^{-d}\,dz\geq\mathfrak{m}_{d,R}.

Moreover,

\mu_{W,D}\bigl(B_{r}(0)^{c}\bigr)=\Psi_{W,D}(r)\qquad\forall\,r\geq 0.

If
\nabla\varphi(x)=0
, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, take
u=\nabla\varphi(x)/|\nabla\varphi(x)|
. The monotonicity of
\nabla\varphi
gives

\nabla\varphi\bigl(B_{3s}(x+6su)\bigr)\subset B_{|\nabla\varphi(x)|/3}(0)^{c}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{d,R}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{V,d}\bigl(B_{3s}(x+6su)\bigr)=\mu_{W,D}\Bigl(\nabla\varphi\bigl(B_{3s}(x+6su)\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{W,D}\bigl(B_{|\nabla\varphi(x)|/3}(0)^{c}\bigr)=\Psi_{W,D}(|\nabla\varphi(x)|/3),

Following

5

, we conclude this paper by recalling the standard argument that turns the preceding smooth maximum-principle computation into a proof under the stated hypotheses. By regularizing

and

, one may first assume that

For
\varepsilon>0
and

, set

\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x,e):=\varphi(x+\varepsilon e)+\varphi(x-\varepsilon e)-2\varphi(x).

After the usual compact truncation of the target measure,
\varphi^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,e)
tends to

at infinity, uniformly in

, and therefore attains its maximum at some point
(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon},\overline{e}_{\varepsilon})
. The first variation in

and

\nabla\varphi(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}\pm\varepsilon\overline{e}_{\varepsilon})=\nabla\varphi(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon})\pm\delta_{\varepsilon}\overline{e}_{\varepsilon},

where

\delta_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\nabla\varphi(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\overline{e}_{\varepsilon})-\nabla\varphi(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\overline{e}_{\varepsilon}),\overline{e}_{\varepsilon}\right\rangle.

By convexity,

(2.10)

\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi^{\varepsilon}=\varphi^{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}_{\varepsilon},\overline{e}_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\varepsilon\delta_{\varepsilon}.

The same discrete second-variation computation as in
[
5
]
, applied to the Monge-Ampère equation at the three points
\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}
,
\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\overline{e}_{\varepsilon}

\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\overline{e}_{\varepsilon}
, gives the incremental version of

2.2

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the points
\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}
remain bounded, then, up to subsequences,
\overline{x}_{\varepsilon}\to\overline{x}
and
\overline{e}_{\varepsilon}\to e_{1}
, and the incremental inequality converges to the maximum-principle inequality above.

It remains only to rule out escape of the maximizing points. In the finite case, this follows from
