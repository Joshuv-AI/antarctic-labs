Rank-Based Tests for Mutual Independence of High-Dimensional Random Vectors via L q L_{q} Norm


Abstract
We consider the problem of testing mutual independence among the components of a high-dimensional random vector. Building on the rank-based max-sum framework, we introduce fixed finite- L q L_{q} power-sum statistics under three general classes of rank-based correlations: simple linear rank statistics, non-degenerate rank-based U-statistics and degenerate rank-based U-statistics. The proposed statistics interpolate between the dense-alternative sensitivity of the L 2 L_{2} statistic and the sparse-alternative sensitivity of the L ∞ L_{\infty} statistic. We establish the asymptotic independence between any fixed finite- L q L_{q} block and the corresponding L ∞ L_{\infty} statistic, and combine L 2 , L 4 , L 6 L_{2},L_{4},L_{6} and L ∞ L_{\infty} p-values through a Cauchy rule. Numerical studies show that the resulting L 2 , 4 , 6 , ∞ L_{2,4,6,\infty} procedure is highly robust to the sparsity of the alternative and has strong empirical power across the considered designs.

Introduction

Let

\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})^{\top}

be a
p
-dimensional random vector, and suppose that independent copies

\bm{X}_{i}=(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{ip})^{\top},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

are observed. We study the global mutual independence hypothesis

(1.1)

H_{0}:X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}\hbox{ are mutually independent}.

The dimension
p
may be comparable to, or substantially larger than, the sample size
n
. This problem is central in high-dimensional inference because it determines whether a large collection of variables can be analyzed as mutually unrelated. It arises in network screening, genomics, neuroscience, finance and econometrics, where falsely imposing independence can distort uncertainty quantification, edge selection and risk assessment.

In low-dimensional Gaussian models, classical likelihood-ratio and covariance-matrix procedures are available; see, for example,
Anderson 2003
,
Roy 1957
and
Nagao 1973
. These procedures are not directly applicable when
p>n
, because the sample covariance matrix is singular. To address high-dimensional covariance and correlation testing,
Bai et al. 2009
,
Jiang, Jiang and Yang 2012
and
Jiang and Yang 2013
proposed likelihood-ratio adaptations based on random matrix theory. Sum-type procedures aggregate squared sample correlations and are naturally sensitive to dense alternatives;
Schott 2005
proposed a modified Nagao-type statistic based on Pearson correlations. Maximum-type procedures use the largest sample correlation and are designed for sparse alternatives;
Jiang 2004
,
Liu, Lin and Shao 2008
and
Cai and Jiang 2011
studied the corresponding extreme-value approximations for high-dimensional correlation matrices. Because the sparsity level of the unknown dependence structure is rarely known in applications,
Li and Xue 2015
and
Feng et al. 2022
proposed adaptive max-sum procedures that combine sum-type and maximum-type statistics.

Pearson-correlation procedures can be sensitive to marginal distributions, heavy tails and nonlinear dependence. A substantial body of work therefore replaces Pearson correlation by distribution-free or nonlinear dependence coefficients.
Wang, Zou and Wang 2013
proposed a necessary rank-correlation test for complete independence in high dimensions.
Mao 2014
and
Mao 2017
developed nonparametric and robust high-dimensional independence tests, and
Mao 2018
studied a Kendall’s
\tau
based complete-independence test.
Shi, Xu and Du 2023
proposed quadratic and maximum statistics based on Spearman’s footrule and combined them into a max-sum test. In the three-class rank framework used in this paper, classical examples include Spearman’s
\rho

(
Spearman 1904
)
, Kendall’s
\tau

(
Kendall 1938
)
, Hoeffding’s
D

(
Hoeffding 1948b
)
, Blum–Kiefer–Rosenblatt’s
R

(
Blum, Kiefer and Rosenblatt 1961
)
and Bergsma–Dassios–Yanagimoto’s
\tau^{\ast}

(
Bergsma and Dassios 2014
)
.
Han, Chen and Liu 2017
derived maximum-type rank tests for simple linear rank statistics and non-degenerate rank-based U-statistics.
Leung and Drton 2018
derived sum-type rank tests for non-degenerate and degenerate rank-based U-statistics.
Drton, Han and Shi 2020
established maximum-type theory for degenerate rank correlations.
Wang et al. 2024
unified these three classes through rank-based max-sum tests and proved the asymptotic independence between the rank-based
L_{2}
sum statistic and the
L_{\infty}
maximum statistic.

Other nonparametric coefficients provide complementary high-dimensional independence tools.
Székely, Rizzo and Bakirov 2007
introduced distance covariance and distance correlation, which characterize general dependence between random vectors.
Székely and Rizzo 2013
developed a high-dimensional distance-correlation
t
-test for independence between random vectors, and
Yao, Zhang and Shao 2018
constructed an
L_{2}
-type high-dimensional mutual-independence test based on pairwise distance covariance. Kernel-based approaches are also closely related:
Gretton et al. 2005
introduced Hilbert–Schmidt norm measures of dependence,
Gretton et al. 2008
proposed the HSIC independence test, and
Pfister et al. 2018
extended HSIC to joint independence through dHSIC.
Heller, Heller and Gorfine 2013
developed a multivariate distance-rank test of association, and
Pan et al. 2020
introduced ball covariance as a generic metric-space dependence measure. More recently,
Chatterjee 2021
proposed a rank correlation coefficient that is consistent against all alternatives,
Xia et al. 2025
developed quadratic and extreme-value complete-independence tests based on Chatterjee’s coefficient, and
Xia et al. 2026
combined Spearman’s
\rho
and Chatterjee’s
\xi
for high-dimensional independence testing.

Finite
L_{q}
norms offer a systematic way to interpolate between dense and sparse alternatives. The
L_{2}
statistic is most effective when many coordinates of the dependence structure are nonzero, whereas the
L_{\infty}
statistic is most effective when the signal is concentrated in a small number of coordinates. Fixed finite orders such as
q=4
and
q=6
target moderately sparse alternatives.
Xu et al. 2016
and
Wu, Xu and Pan 2019
studied sums of powers and related
L_{q}
-type procedures for high-dimensional testing.
He et al. 2021
constructed unbiased U-statistics for powers of high-dimensional parameters and established asymptotic independence across different
q
’s and with maximum-type statistics in several settings.
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
developed a general
L_{q}
-norm based U-statistic theory for kernels of order larger than one, including component-wise independence examples based on Kendall’s
\tau
and Spearman’s
\rho
. Their results give asymptotic normality and joint independence for several fixed
q
’s and show that larger finite
q
values are advantageous as the alternative becomes sparser.

This paper extends the rank-based max-sum framework from the two endpoint statistics,
L_{2}
and
L_{\infty}
, to a finite-
L_{q}
spectrum. We work under the same three rank-based classes as
Wang et al. 2024
; the named coefficients above serve only as representative examples. For U-statistic-based members of the framework, we use the finite-
L_{q}
Gaussian approximation of
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
. The new theoretical component is a rank-specific block independence result: under the null hypothesis, any fixed finite collection of rank-based
L_{q}
statistics is asymptotically independent of the corresponding
L_{\infty}
statistic. Building on
Liu and Xie 2020
, we then construct a Cauchy combination test that aggregates
L_{2},L_{4},L_{6}
and
L_{\infty}
p-values.

The main contributions are as follows. We formulate fixed finite-
L_{q}
power-sum tests under the three general classes of rank-based correlations. We prove block asymptotic independence between the finite-
L_{q}
vector and the maximum statistic, with explicit rate bounds that display the required relationship between
p
and
n
. We construct an adaptive Cauchy
L_{2,4,6,\infty}
procedure that retains sensitivity to dense, moderately sparse and very sparse alternatives. For implementation, we give closed finite-sample centering and variance formulas for Spearman’s
\rho
and Kendall’s
\tau
, and use high-precision null simulation for the degenerate rank correlations, whose high-order finite-sample moments are combinatorially much more complex.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the methodology and main theory, including the three rank-based classes, finite-
L_{q}
statistics, assumptions, null calibration, block independence, Cauchy combination and representative calibration formulas. Section
3
describes the simulation design for assessing empirical size and power across sparsity regimes. Section
4
concludes. Proofs are collected in the Appendix.

2
Methodology and main theory

2.1
Three rank-based classes and finite-
L_{q}
statistics

Let

\Lambda_{p}=\{(s,t):1\leq s<t\leq p\},\qquad N_{p}=|\Lambda_{p}|={p\choose 2}.

All expectations, variances and probabilities with subscript
0
are taken under (
1.1
). We assume throughout that the marginal distributions are continuous, so ranks are almost surely free of ties.

2.1.1
Simple linear rank statistics

Let
R^{s}_{ni}
be the rank of
X_{is}
among
X_{1s},\ldots,X_{ns}
. For score functions
f,g:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
, define

(2.1)

V_{st}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\left(\frac{R^{s}_{ni}}{n+1}\right)g\left(\frac{R^{t}_{ni}}{n+1}\right),\qquad(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}.

The statistic
V_{st}
measures agreement between the two rank sequences
(R^{s}_{n1},\ldots,R^{s}_{nn})
and
(R^{t}_{n1},\ldots,R^{t}_{nn})
through the scores
f
and
g
. Under
H_{0}
, the two rank vectors are independent uniform permutations of
1,\ldots,n
. Without loss of generality one may replace
f
and
g
by their centered discrete versions

f_{ni}=f\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{f}_{n},\qquad g_{ni}=g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n},

where

\bar{f}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right),\qquad\bar{g}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right).

This class includes Spearman’s
\rho

(
Spearman 1904
)
after a deterministic normalization of the linear scores.

2.1.2
Rank-based U-statistics and degeneracy

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{2m}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, symmetric, rank-based kernel. For
\bm{X}_{i}^{(st)}=(X_{is},X_{it})^{\top}
, define

U_{h,st}={n\choose m}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}h\{\bm{X}_{i_{1}}^{(st)},\ldots,\bm{X}_{i_{m}}^{(st)}\}.

Rank-based means that
h
depends on its arguments only through their coordinate-wise ranks among the
m
points. If an original kernel is not symmetric, it may be replaced by its symmetrization without changing the associated U-statistic.

For
1\leq r\leq m
, define the
r
th projection

h_{r}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})=\mathbb{E}_{0}\{h(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r},Z_{r+1},\ldots,Z_{m})\},

where
\bm{Z}_{1},\ldots,\bm{Z}_{m}
are iid copies of
\bm{X}^{(st)}=(X_{s},X_{t})^{\top}
under
H_{0}
. The kernel has degeneracy order
d\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
if

h_{1}=\cdots=h_{d-1}=0,\qquad h_{d}\not\equiv 0.

The case
d=1
is non-degenerate. The case
d\geq 2
is degenerate. The present paper follows
Wang et al. 2024
and focuses on the non-degenerate class
d=1
and the degenerate class
d=2
.

2.1.3
Non-degenerate rank-based U-statistics

When
d=1
, write

(2.2)

W_{h,st}=U_{h,st},\qquad(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}.

The first projection controls both the finite-
L_{q}
sum statistic and the maximum statistic. The factorization condition used in rank-based maximum theory requires

h_{1}(x_{s},x_{t})=\omega(x_{s})\gamma(x_{t})

for measurable functions
\omega
and
\gamma
. Kendall’s
\tau

(
Kendall 1938
)
is the canonical example with
m=2
and

h_{\tau}\{\bm{X}_{i_{1}}^{(st)},\bm{X}_{i_{2}}^{(st)}\}=\operatorname{sign}\{(X_{i_{1}s}-X_{i_{2}s})(X_{i_{1}t}-X_{i_{2}t})\}.

2.1.4
Degenerate rank-based U-statistics

When
d=2
, write

(2.3)

Q_{h,st}=U_{h,st},\qquad(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}.

Let
h_{2}
be the second-order projection. The degenerate maximum theory assumes the spectral representation

h_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{v}\phi_{v}(x)\phi_{v}(y),\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0,

where

\Lambda=\sum_{v=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{v}<\infty,\qquad\sup_{v\geq 1}\|\phi_{v}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Let
\mu_{1}
be the multiplicity of
\lambda_{1}
, and define

(2.4)

\kappa=\prod_{v>\mu_{1}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{v}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{-1/2}.

Hoeffding’s
D

(
Hoeffding 1948b
)
, Blum–Kiefer–Rosenblatt’s
R

(
Blum, Kiefer and Rosenblatt 1961
)
, and Bergsma–Dassios–Yanagimoto’s
\tau^{\ast}

(
Bergsma and Dassios 2014
)
are examples of this class.

2.1.5
Finite-
L_{q}
power sums

Let
A_{st}
denote a generic member of one of the three classes:

A_{st}\in\{V_{st},W_{h,st},Q_{h,st}\}.

Write

\widetilde{A}_{st}=A_{st}-\mathbb{E}_{0}(A_{st}).

For a fixed even integer
q\geq 2
, define the single-pair centering and variance

\mu_{A,q,n}=\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{\,q}),\qquad v_{A,q,n}=\operatorname{var}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{\,q}),

and the global finite-
L_{q}
statistic

(2.5)

S_{A,q}=\sum_{(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}}\{\widetilde{A}_{st}^{\,q}-\mu_{A,q,n}\},\qquad Z_{A,q}=\frac{S_{A,q}}{(N_{p}v_{A,q,n})^{1/2}}.

The statistic
Z_{A,2}
is the usual sum statistic. The values
q=4
and
q=6
are intermediate finite-
L_{q}
statistics between
L_{2}
and
L_{\infty}
.

Remark 2.1
.

For the U-statistic classes,
\widetilde{A}_{st}^{\,q}
is a V-statistic of order
qm
. Its diagonal-free version is the
L_{q}
U-statistic studied by
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
. Under bounded kernels and fixed
q
, the diagonal-deletion difference is of smaller order after the normalization in (
2.5
). We keep the power-sum form because it is the direct finite-
L_{q}
analogue of the
L_{2}
statistic in the rank-based max-sum paper and because it yields transparent centering and scaling formulas for concrete rank coefficients.

2.2
Mathematical assumptions

We state the assumptions in a form parallel to the rank-based max-sum paper and the general
L_{q}
U-statistic theory.

Assumption 2.1
.

As
n,p\to\infty
,

X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}\hbox{ have continuous marginal distributions},

N_{p}={p\choose 2}\to\infty,\qquad\mathcal{Q}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\}\subset 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}\quad\hbox{is fixed}.

Assumption 2.2
.

For
V_{st}
in (
2.1
), there exist constants
C,C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
such that

\|f\|_{\infty}+\|g\|_{\infty}\leq C,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n}\right]^{2}>0,

\log p=o(n^{1/3}),

f\hbox{ is differentiable and }\sup_{x\in(0,1)}|f^{\prime}(x)|\leq C,

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left|g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n}\right]^{2},

\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n}\right]^{3}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{C_{2}^{2}}{n}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[g\left(\frac{i}{n+1}\right)-\bar{g}_{n}\right]^{2}\right\}^{3}.

Assumption 2.3
.

For
W_{h,st}
in (
2.2
),

\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty,\qquad h\hbox{ is symmetric and rank-based},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{0}\{h(\bm{X}_{1}^{(st)},\ldots,\bm{X}_{m}^{(st)})\}=0,

\zeta_{1}^{h}:=\operatorname{var}_{0}\{h_{1}(\bm{X}_{1}^{(st)})\}>0,

\mathbb{E}_{0}\{h(\bm{X}_{i}^{(st)})\mid\bm{X}_{j}^{(s)},\bm{X}_{j^{\prime}}^{(t)}\}=0\quad\hbox{whenever }j,j^{\prime}\subset i,\quad\min(|j|,|j^{\prime}|)<1,

h_{1}(x_{s},x_{t})=\omega(x_{s})\gamma(x_{t}),\qquad\mathbb{E}_{0}\{\omega^{2}(X_{s})\}\mathbb{E}_{0}\{\gamma^{2}(X_{t})\}>0,

\log p=o(n^{1/3}).

Assumption 2.4
.

For
Q_{h,st}
in (
2.3
),

\|h\|_{\infty}<\infty,\qquad h\hbox{ is symmetric and rank-based},\qquad\mathbb{E}_{0}\{h(\bm{X}_{1}^{(st)},\ldots,\bm{X}_{m}^{(st)})\}=0,

h_{1}\equiv 0,\qquad\zeta_{2}^{h}:=\operatorname{var}_{0}\{h_{2}(\bm{X}_{1}^{(st)},\bm{X}_{2}^{(st)})\}>0,

\mathbb{E}_{0}\{h(\bm{X}_{i}^{(st)})\mid\bm{X}_{j}^{(s)},\bm{X}_{j^{\prime}}^{(t)}\}=0\quad\hbox{whenever }j,j^{\prime}\subset i,\quad\min(|j|,|j^{\prime}|)<2,

h_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{v\geq 1}\lambda_{v}\phi_{v}(x)\phi_{v}(y),\quad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq 0,\quad\sum_{v\geq 1}\lambda_{v}<\infty,\quad\sup_{v}\|\phi_{v}\|_{\infty}<\infty,

\phi_{v}(x_{s},x_{t})=\psi_{v}(x_{s})\varphi_{v}(x_{t}),\qquad v\geq 1.

If infinitely many
\lambda_{v}
are nonzero, assume

(2.6)

\log p=o(n^{\theta}),\qquad\theta<\sup\left\{k\in[0,1/3):\sum_{v>\lfloor n^{(1-3k)/5}\rfloor}\lambda_{v}=O(n^{-k})\right\}.

If only finitely many
\lambda_{v}
are nonzero, assume (
2.6
) with any
\theta<1/3
.

Assumption 2.5
.

For a U-statistic class with kernels
h_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathcal{L}=\Lambda_{p}
, degeneracy order
s\in\{1,2\}
, and every fixed
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, let

\sigma_{s}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=\operatorname{cov}_{0}\{h^{(s)}_{\ell_{1}}(X_{[s]}),h^{(s)}_{\ell_{2}}(X_{[s]})\},

\Delta_{s}(q)=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathcal{L}}\sigma_{s}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})^{q},\qquad\widetilde{\Delta}_{s}(q)=\operatorname{var}_{0}\left\{\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}H^{(s)}_{\ell,q}(X_{[qs]})\right\},

where
h^{(s)}_{\ell}
is the
s
th Hoeffding projection and
H^{(s)}_{\ell,q}
is the symmetrization of
\otimes^{q}h^{(s)}_{\ell}
as in
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
. The following conditions hold:

(2.7)

\Delta_{t}(q)=o\{n^{q(t-s)}\Delta_{s}(q)\},\quad s<t\leq m,\qquad\Delta_{s}(q)=O\{\widetilde{\Delta}_{s}(q)\},

\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{4}\in\mathcal{L}}\left|\sigma_{s}(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})\sigma_{s}(\ell_{3},\ell_{4})\sigma_{s}(\ell_{1},\ell_{4})\sigma_{s}(\ell_{2},\ell_{3})\right|^{q/2}=o\{\widetilde{\Delta}_{s}(q)^{2}\},

for
u=2,3,4
,

\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{u}\in\mathcal{L}}\left|\operatorname{cum}_{q}\{h^{(s)}_{\ell_{1}}(X_{[s]}),\ldots,h^{(s)}_{\ell_{u}}(X_{[s]})\}\right|\leq C_{q}\widetilde{\Delta}_{s}(q)^{u/2},

and, for
s>1
, for all
I_{1},\ldots,I_{4}\in P_{qs}(n)
such that every element of
I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{4}
appears in exactly two of the four index sets and
I_{a}\neq I_{b}
for
a\neq b
,

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\sum_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{4}\in\mathcal{L}}\prod_{a=1}^{4}(\otimes^{q}h^{(s)}_{\ell_{a}})(X_{I_{a}})\right]=o\{\widetilde{\Delta}_{s}(q)^{2}\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Assumption
2.5
is a compact restatement of Assumptions 3.2–3.3 of
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
for the present component-wise independence indexing set
\mathcal{L}=\Lambda_{p}
. It is used only to invoke the fixed-
L_{q}
U-statistic CLT. The new independence proof in this paper uses Assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
to control the maximum statistic and the exceedance-event expansion.

2.3
Null calibration for finite
L_{q}
statistics

The following proposition gives the exact null centering and variance of (
2.5
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Under
H_{0}
, for any fixed even
q\geq 2
,

\mathbb{E}_{0}(S_{A,q})=0,\qquad\operatorname{var}_{0}(S_{A,q})=N_{p}v_{A,q,n}.

Moreover, for fixed even
q_{1},q_{2}
,

\operatorname{cov}_{0}(S_{A,q_{1}},S_{A,q_{2}})=N_{p}\{\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{1}+q_{2}})-\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{1}})\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{2}})\}.

The next theorem gives the finite-
L_{q}
null approximation. The theorem is stated at the rank-correlation power-sum level. For U-statistic classes, the proof uses the diagonal-free
L_{q}
U-statistic theorem of
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
and the diagonal equivalence in Remark
2.1
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
hold and let
\mathcal{Q}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\}\subset 2\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be fixed.

(i)

If
A=V
and Assumption
2.2
holds, then (
2.9
) below holds.

(ii)

If
A=W
and Assumptions
2.3
and
2.5
hold with
s=1
, then (
2.9
) below holds.

(iii)

If
A=Q
and Assumptions
2.4
and
2.5
hold with
s=2
, then (
2.9
) below holds.

Define

\bm{Z}_{A,\mathcal{Q}}=(Z_{A,q_{1}},\ldots,Z_{A,q_{K}})^{\top}.

Then

(2.9)

\bm{Z}_{A,\mathcal{Q}}\Rightarrow N_{K}(0,\mathbf{\Gamma}_{A,\mathcal{Q}}),

where

(\mathbf{\Gamma}_{A,\mathcal{Q}})_{jk}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{j}+q_{k}})-\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{j}})\mathbb{E}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12}^{q_{k}})}{(v_{A,q_{j},n}v_{A,q_{k},n})^{1/2}},

whenever the limits exist. In particular, for every fixed even

Z_{A,q}\Rightarrow N(0,1).

Remark 2.3
.

The finite-
L_{q}
statistics are not generally mutually independent across different
q
values when the power-sum version (
2.5
) is used. This is immaterial for the Cauchy combination test, which only requires valid marginal p-values and is robust to dependence among the finite-
L_{q}
p-values. The key independence result needed here is the block independence between the entire finite-
L_{q}
vector and the maximum statistic.

For finite
q
, define the one-sided p-value

(2.10)

P_{A,q}=1-\Phi(Z_{A,q}),\qquad q\in 2\mathbb{Z}^{+},

where
\Phi
is the standard normal distribution function.

2.4
Maximum statistics and block asymptotic independence

For the simple linear and non-degenerate U-statistic classes, define

L_{A}=\max_{(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}}|\widetilde{A}_{st}|,\qquad A\in\{V,W\},

and let
\sigma_{A,n}^{2}=\operatorname{var}_{0}(\widetilde{A}_{12})
. The standardized maximum statistic is

M_{A}=\frac{L_{A}^{2}}{\sigma_{A,n}^{2}}-4\log p+\log\log p,\qquad A\in\{V,W\}.

Its limiting distribution function is

G(y)=\exp\{-(8\pi)^{-1/2}\exp(-y/2)\}.

For the degenerate class, define

L_{Q}=\max_{(s,t)\in\Lambda_{p}}\widetilde{Q}_{h,st},

and

M_{Q}=(n-1)\lambda_{1}^{-1}{m\choose 2}^{-1}L_{Q}-4\log p-(\mu_{1}-2)\log\log p+\Lambda/\lambda_{1}.

Its limiting distribution function is

F(y)=\exp\left\{-\frac{2^{\mu_{1}/2-2}\kappa}{\Gamma(\mu_{1}/2)}\exp(-y/2)\right\},

where
\kappa
is defined in (
2.4
).

.

Let

2.5
Cauchy combination of finite-
L_{q}
and maximum p-values

Let

\mathcal{B}=\{2,4,6,\infty\}.

For a generic rank class
A\in\{V,W,Q\}
, collect p-values

\{P_{A,a}:a\in\mathcal{B}\},

where finite-

) and the maximum p-values are defined by (

)–(

. Define

and

A default choice is

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
2.2
. Then the test

\phi_{A,\mathcal{B},\alpha}=\mathbf{1}\{P_{A,\mathcal{B}}\leq\alpha\}

is asymptotically valid in the Cauchy tail sense:

(2.13)

Spearman’s

Kendall’s

For each data-generating mechanism, we generate

. The dimensions are chosen from

(n,p)\in\{(100,100),(100,200),(200,200),(200,400)\}.

For each representative rank coefficient

T\in\{\rho,\tau,D,R,\tau^{\ast}\},

we compute the four p-values

P_{T,2},\qquad P_{T,4},\qquad P_{T,6},\qquad P_{T,\infty},

and the equal-weight combined p-value

P_{T,\{2,4,6,\infty\}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left\{\frac{1}{4}\sum_{a\in\{2,4,6,\infty\}}\tan\bigl[\pi\{1/2-P_{T,a}\}\bigr]\right\}.

Under the null hypothesis, the coordinates are mutually independent and the marginal distributions are generated from three models:

\mathrm{N}(0,1),\qquad t_{3}/\sqrt{3},\qquad(\chi^{2}_{1}-1)/\sqrt{2}.

The last two distributions are included to assess the robustness of the rank-based procedures under heavy tails and skewness.

Empirical power under sparse-to-dense alternatives

We next examine the finite-sample power of the proposed tests under sparse-to-dense alternatives. In this experiment,

n=100,\qquad p=200,\qquad\alpha=0.05,

Each configuration is based on
1000
Monte Carlo replications. The sparsity parameter is
k\in\{2,\ldots,16\}
. For each replication, an active set of
k
coordinates is selected uniformly at random. Conditional on the active set, the latent Gaussian vector
\bm{Z}=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{p})^{\top}
has unit marginal variances and pairwise correlations only inside the active block. The nonzero correlations are generated independently from
U(r_{\min},r_{\max})
, where

\displaystyle\text{(i) linear normal:}

\displaystyle r_{\min}=\{12k^{-1}\log(p)/n\}^{1/2},\quad r_{\max}=\{14k^{-1}\log(p)/n\}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\text{(ii) sine cube-root:}

\displaystyle r_{\min}=\{9\log(p)/(n\log((k+2)/2))\}^{1/2},

\displaystyle r_{\max}=\{10\log(p)/(n\log((k+2)/2))\}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\text{(iii) sine cubic:}

\displaystyle r_{\min}=\{19k^{-1/2}\log(p)/n\}^{1/2},\quad r_{\max}=\{20k^{-1/2}\log(p)/n\}^{1/2}.

The observed vector
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})^{\top}
is then generated from one of the following three models:

X_{j}=Z_{j},\qquad X_{j}=\sin\{2\pi\,\operatorname{sgn}(Z_{j})|Z_{j}|^{1/3}/3\},\qquad X_{j}=\sin(\pi Z_{j}^{3}/4).

We compare six pairwise dependence measures: Pearson’s
r
, Kendall’s
\tau
, Spearman’s
\rho
, the Bergsma–Dassios–Yanagimoto
\tau^{\ast}
, Hoeffding’s
D
, and the Blum–Kiefer–Rosenblatt
R
. For each dependence measure, we report tests based on
L_{2}
,
L_{4}
,
L_{6}
,
L_{\infty}
, the
L_{2,\infty}
combination and the
L_{2,4,6,\infty}
combination. Because the higher-order Pearson tests have inflated size under non-Gaussian null distributions, Pearson’s
r
is reported only for the
L_{2}
statistic in the power comparison. In the coefficient-level comparison, Pearson’s
r
uses
L_{2}
, whereas the five rank-based dependence measures use the
L_{2,4,6,\infty}
combination.

4
Discussion

We propose a finite-
L_{q}
extension of rank-based mutual independence testing and prove that a fixed finite-
L_{q}
block is asymptotically independent of the corresponding maximum statistic. The resulting
L_{2,4,6,\infty}
procedure combines distribution-free rank robustness with stable power over dense, moderately sparse and sparse alternatives.

Future work includes developing faster exact finite-sample calibration for high-order degenerate rank U-statistics, possibly using symbolic enumeration and dynamic programming ideas related to those in
Drton, Han and Shi 2020
and
Zhang, Wang and Shao 2025
. Another direction is to extend finite-
L_{q}
rank combinations to conditional, residual-based and kernel or distance-based independence settings; relevant starting points include
Wang et al. 2024
,
Yao, Zhang and Shao 2018
and
Pfister et al. 2018
.

Appendix A
Finite-sample moment calculation for degenerate rank correlations

This appendix describes the exact finite-sample moment calculation for the degenerate rank correlations Hoeffding’s
D
, Blum–Kiefer–Rosenblatt’s
R
and Bergsma–Dassios–Yanagimoto’s
\tau^{\ast}
. The formulas are written at the level of concrete rank kernels. The full
L_{4}
centering and variance calculation is printed for
\tau^{\ast}
because it is the shortest of the three degenerate examples and already shows the combinatorial complexity of exact finite-
n
calibration.

For
m\in\{4,5,6\}
, let
\bm{e}_{m}=(1,\ldots,m)
and let
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
be the permutation group on
m
elements. For
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
and
\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, define

\displaystyle A_{m,a}(u;\omega)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{1}}\leq u_{\omega_{a}})-\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{2}}\leq u_{\omega_{a}})\}\{\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{3}}\leq u_{\omega_{a}})-\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{4}}\leq u_{\omega_{a}})\},

\displaystyle B_{4}(u;\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{1}},u_{\omega_{3}}<u_{\omega_{2}},u_{\omega_{4}})+\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{2}},u_{\omega_{4}}<u_{\omega_{1}},u_{\omega_{3}})

\displaystyle\quad-\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{1}},u_{\omega_{4}}<u_{\omega_{2}},u_{\omega_{3}})-\mathbf{1}(u_{\omega_{2}},u_{\omega_{3}}<u_{\omega_{1}},u_{\omega_{4}}),

where
\mathbf{1}(a,b<c,d)=\mathbf{1}(a<c)\mathbf{1}(a<d)\mathbf{1}(b<c)\mathbf{1}(b<d)
. The three pattern kernels are

(A.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{D}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{5}}A_{5,5}(\bm{e}_{5};\omega)A_{5,5}(\sigma;\omega),\qquad\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{5},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{R}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{32}\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{6}}A_{6,5}(\bm{e}_{6};\omega)A_{6,6}(\sigma;\omega),\qquad\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{6},

(A.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau^{\ast}}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4!}\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{4}}B_{4}(\bm{e}_{4};\omega)B_{4}(\sigma;\omega),\qquad\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{4}.

Equivalently, the pattern-value tables are

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\hline\cr\hline\cr T&\psi_{T}(\sigma)&\#\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m_{T}}:\psi_{T}(\sigma)\}\\
\hline\cr D&1&8\\
D&0&96\\
D&-1/2&16\\
\hline\cr R&1&80\\
R&1/2&128\\
R&0&64\\
R&-1/4&320\\
R&-1/2&128\\
\hline\cr\tau^{\ast}&2/3&8\\
\tau^{\ast}&-1/3&16\\
\hline\cr\hline\cr\end{array}

Under
H_{0}
, the joint ranks of one coordinate pair are equivalent to
(1,\pi_{1}),\ldots,(n,\pi_{n})
with
\pi
uniform on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Therefore

(A.4)

T_{n}(\pi)={n\choose m_{T}}^{-1}\sum_{I\in{[n]\choose m_{T}}}\psi_{T}\{\operatorname{pat}_{I}(\pi)\},

where
{[n]\choose m_{T}}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=m_{T}\}
; for
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\}
,
\operatorname{pat}_{I}(\pi)\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
is the relative rank pattern of
(\pi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\pi_{i_{m}})
.

For integers
m,r,b
, let

\mathcal{I}_{m,r}(b)=\left\{(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}):I_{a}\in{[b]\choose m},\;1\leq a\leq r,\;\bigcup_{a=1}^{r}I_{a}=[b]\right\}.

For
T\in\{D,R,\tau^{\ast}\}
, define

(A.5)

\Omega_{T,r,b}=\frac{1}{b!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{b}}\sum_{(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\in\mathcal{I}_{m_{T},r}(b)}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\psi_{T}\{\operatorname{pat}_{I_{a}}(\pi)\},\qquad m_{T}\leq b\leq rm_{T}.

For
T\in\{D,R,\tau^{\ast}\}
define

(A.6)

\mathsf{P}_{T,r}(n)=\sum_{b=m_{T}}^{rm_{T}}{n\choose b}\Omega_{T,r,b},\qquad{n\choose b}=0\quad(b>n).

Then the exact single-pair moment is

(A.7)

M_{T,r}(n)=\mathbb{E}_{0}(T_{n}^{r})={n\choose m_{T}}^{-r}\mathsf{P}_{T,r}(n).

Indeed, expanding
T_{n}^{r}
and grouping the ordered kernel tuples according to the cardinality of their union gives (
A.5
) and hence (
A.7
). Equivalently, each summand can be written as an explicit rational function of
n
:

(A.8)

\frac{{n\choose b}}{{n\choose m_{T}}^{r}}\Omega_{T,r,b}=\Omega_{T,r,b}\frac{(m_{T}!)^{r}}{b!}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}(n-j)}{\{\prod_{j=0}^{m_{T}-1}(n-j)\}^{r}}.

Substituting (
A.1
)–(
A.3
) gives the coefficient-level formulas

(A.9)

\displaystyle\Omega_{D,r,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{16^{-r}}{b!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{b}}\sum_{(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\in\mathcal{I}_{5,r}(b)}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\sum_{\omega_{a}\in\mathfrak{S}_{5}}A_{5,5}(\bm{e}_{5};\omega_{a})A_{5,5}\{\operatorname{pat}_{I_{a}}(\pi);\omega_{a}\},

(A.10)

\displaystyle\Omega_{R,r,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{32^{-r}}{b!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{b}}\sum_{(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\in\mathcal{I}_{6,r}(b)}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\sum_{\omega_{a}\in\mathfrak{S}_{6}}A_{6,5}(\bm{e}_{6};\omega_{a})A_{6,6}\{\operatorname{pat}_{I_{a}}(\pi);\omega_{a}\},

(A.11)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},r,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{(4!)^{-r}}{b!}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{b}}\sum_{(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\in\mathcal{I}_{4,r}(b)}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\sum_{\omega_{a}\in\mathfrak{S}_{4}}B_{4}(\bm{e}_{4};\omega_{a})B_{4}\{\operatorname{pat}_{I_{a}}(\pi);\omega_{a}\}.

Equations (
A.9
)–(
A.11
) are the exact rational-constant calculations used to form
\mathsf{P}_{T,r}(n)
. The second-order constants, which verify the normalization against known formulas, are

\displaystyle\Omega_{D,2,5}=\frac{1}{10},\qquad\Omega_{D,2,6}=\frac{41}{45},\qquad\Omega_{D,2,7}=\frac{49}{30},\qquad\Omega_{D,2,8}=\frac{28}{45},

\displaystyle\Omega_{D,2,9}=\Omega_{D,2,10}=0,

\displaystyle\Omega_{R,2,6}=\frac{41}{180},\quad\Omega_{R,2,7}=\frac{287}{60},\quad\Omega_{R,2,8}=\frac{952}{45},\quad\Omega_{R,2,9}=\frac{154}{5},\quad\Omega_{R,2,10}=14,

\displaystyle\Omega_{R,2,11}=\Omega_{R,2,12}=0,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},2,4}=\frac{2}{9},\quad\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},2,5}=\frac{32}{45},\quad\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},2,6}=\frac{2}{5},\quad\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},2,7}=\Omega_{\tau^{\ast},2,8}=0.

gives

\displaystyle\zeta_{A}(p,r;x,y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{I_{1}<\cdots<I_{r}}\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})E_{I_{1}}\cdots E_{I_{r}}\}-\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})\}\mathbb{P}_{0}(E_{I_{1}}\cdots E_{I_{r}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})H_{A}(p,r;y)+2{N_{p}\choose r}\Pi_{p,r,\tau}(\varepsilon)

(C.7)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})H_{A}(p,r;y)+C_{A,\mathcal{Q},r,\tau}\varepsilon^{-\tau}p^{2r-\tau/2}.

By Theorem
2.1
,

\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})\leq 2r_{A,\mathcal{Q}}(n,p)+\sup_{\|\bm{u}-\bm{x}\|_{\infty}\leq 2\varepsilon}|\Phi_{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{A,\mathcal{Q}}}(\bm{u})-\Phi_{\mathbf{\Gamma}_{A,\mathcal{Q}}}(\bm{x})|.

Since a non-degenerate Gaussian distribution has a bounded density on compact rectangles,

(C.8)

\limsup_{n,p\to\infty}\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})\leq C_{\Gamma,K,x}\varepsilon.

Choose
\tau=6r
in (
C.7
). Then

\limsup_{n,p\to\infty}\zeta_{A}(p,r;x,y)\leq C_{\Gamma,K,x}\varepsilon\frac{C_{A,y}^{r}}{2^{r}r!}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

\lim_{n,p\to\infty}\zeta_{A}(p,r;x,y)=0\qquad(r\hbox{ fixed}).

The explicit
p
-rate before the last limit is

\zeta_{A}(p,r;x,y)\leq\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})\left\{\frac{C_{A,y}^{r}}{2^{r}r!}+2C_{A,y}^{r}p^{1-r}+C_{A,y,r}(2r)^{2r}p^{-1}\right\}+C_{A,\mathcal{Q},r}\varepsilon^{-6r}p^{-r}.

Let

B_{p}(y)=\bigcup_{I\in\Lambda_{p}}E_{I}=\{M_{A}>y\}.

For
m\geq 1
, Bonferroni’s inequality gives

(C.9)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})B_{p}(y)\}-\sum_{r=1}^{m}(-1)^{r+1}\sum_{I_{1}<\cdots<I_{r}}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})E_{I_{1}}\cdots E_{I_{r}}\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq H_{A}(p,m+1;y),

(C.10)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{B_{p}(y)\}-\sum_{r=1}^{m}(-1)^{r+1}H_{A}(p,r;y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq H_{A}(p,m+1;y).

Combining (
C.7
), (
C.9
) and (
C.10
), for any
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})B_{p}(y)\}-\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})\}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{B_{p}(y)\}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{r=1}^{m}\zeta_{A}(p,r;x,y)+2H_{A}(p,m+1;y)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{r=1}^{m}\left[\Delta_{p,\varepsilon}(\bm{x})H_{A}(p,r;y)+C_{A,\mathcal{Q},r,\tau_{r}}\varepsilon^{-\tau_{r}}p^{2r-\tau_{r}/2}\right]+2H_{A}(p,m+1;y).

Set
\tau_{r}=6r
. Using (
C.2
) and (
C.8
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{n,p\to\infty}

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})B_{p}(y)\}-\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})\}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{B_{p}(y)\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\Gamma,K,x}\varepsilon\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{C_{A,y}^{r}}{2^{r}r!}+2\frac{C_{A,y}^{m+1}}{2^{m+1}(m+1)!}.

Now

\lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n,p\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})B_{p}(y)\}-\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})\}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{B_{p}(y)\}\right|=0.

Thus

(C.11)

\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x}),M_{A}>y\}=\mathbb{P}_{0}\{D_{p}(\bm{x})\}\mathbb{P}_{0}\{M_{A}>y\}+o(1).

Together with Theorem
2.1
and the rank-based maximum limits, (
C.11
) proves Theorem
2.2
.

Appendix D
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Let

C_{a}=\tan\{\pi(1/2-P_{A,a})\},\qquad\mathcal{C}_{A,\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{B}}w_{a}C_{a}.

For
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\{C_{0}>t\}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(t)=\frac{1}{\pi t}+O(t^{-3}),

where
C_{0}
is a standard Cauchy random variable. Let

t_{\alpha}=\tan\{\pi(1/2-\alpha)\}.

Then

t_{\alpha}=(\pi\alpha)^{-1}\{1+O(\alpha^{2})\},\qquad\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(t_{\alpha})=\alpha.

The Cauchy combination theorem of
Liu and Xie 2020
gives, for the fixed set
\mathcal{B}
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\{\mathcal{C}_{A,\mathcal{B}}>t\}=\frac{1}{\pi t}\{1+\delta_{A}(t,n,p)\},\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\limsup_{n,p\to\infty}|\delta_{A}(t,n,p)|=0.

Therefore, with
t=t_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}(P_{A,\mathcal{B}}\leq\alpha)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A,\mathcal{B}}>t_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi t_{\alpha}}\{1+\delta_{A}(t_{\alpha},n,p)\}

\displaystyle=\alpha\{1+O(\alpha^{2})+\delta_{A}(t_{\alpha},n,p)+O(\alpha^{2}\delta_{A}(t_{\alpha},n,p))\}.

Equation (
2.13
) follows.

If for some
a_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
,

P_{A,a_{0}}\xrightarrow{P_{1}}0,

then

C_{a_{0}}=\tan\{\pi(1/2-P_{A,a_{0}})\}=\{\pi P_{A,a_{0}}\}^{-1}\{1+O(P_{A,a_{0}}^{2})\}\xrightarrow{P_{1}}+\infty.

For the remaining finite number of indices
a\neq a_{0}
,

\min_{a\neq a_{0}}P_{A,a}\geq\eta_{n}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\max_{a\neq a_{0}}|C_{a}|\leq C\eta_{n}^{-1},

and taking
\eta_{n}\downarrow 0
with

\mathbb{P}_{1}\{P_{A,a_{0}}\leq\eta_{n}^{2}\}\to 1

gives

\mathcal{C}_{A,\mathcal{B}}\geq w_{a_{0}}\{\pi P_{A,a_{0}}\}^{-1}\{1+o_{P_{1}}(1)\}-C\eta_{n}^{-1}\xrightarrow{P_{1}}+\infty.

Thus

P_{A,\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(\mathcal{C}_{A,\mathcal{B}})\xrightarrow{P_{1}}0.

This proves Theorem
2.3
.
