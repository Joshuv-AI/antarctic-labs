Large-scale harmonic measures and nontangential maximal functions in periodic homogenization
Zhongwei Shen, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the elliptic operators ℒ ε = − ∇ ⋅ ( A ( X / ε ) ∇ ) \mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\nabla\cdot(A(X/\varepsilon)\nabla) with periodic coefficients in a bounded domain Ω \Omega without any local smoothness assumption on A = A ⁡ ( Y ) A=A(Y) , where ε ≪ diam ⁡ ( Ω ) \varepsilon\ll\diam(\Omega) is a microscopic scale. Due to the irregularity of the coefficients at ε \varepsilon scale, we introduce the correct forms of the large-scale nontangential maximal functions for the Dirichlet, Neumann and regularity problems that measure the behaviors of solutions at an ε \varepsilon distance away from the boundary. The L p L^{p} estimates uniform in ε \varepsilon are established for these nontangential maximal functions for the same and optimal ranges of p p as the Laplace operator in the Lipschitz or C 1 C^{1} domains. With some additional regularity assumption on the coefficients, the large-scale estimates combined with the small-scale estimates recover the classical full-scale estimates of the nontangential maximal functions. Our proofs are based on the notion of large-scale ℒ ε \mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon} -harmonic measures, the periodic structure of operators in the transversal direction to the boundaries, and the homogenization tools, including convergence rates and large-scale regularity.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

Consider the elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\nabla\cdot(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla)
,
which arises in homogenization theory,
where
A^{\varepsilon}=A(X/\varepsilon)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is a small parameter.
Assume that the coefficient matrix
A
is real, symmetric, and satisfies the following conditions:

•

Ellipticity and boundedness: there exists
\Lambda\geq 1
such that for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.1)

\xi\cdot A(Y)\xi\geq\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\quad|A(Y)\xi|\leq\Lambda|\xi|.

•

Periodicity: for any
Z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(1.2)

A(\cdot+Z)=A(\cdot).

We are interested in the
L^{p}
Dirichlet and Neumann problems in a bounded domain
\Omega
,

(1.3)

(D)_{p}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
&u_{\varepsilon}=f\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.4)

(N)_{p}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\partial u_{\varepsilon}/\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}=n\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
and
n
represents the unit outer normal vector.
We will also consider the
L^{p}
regularity problem,

(1.5)

(R)_{p}\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
&u_{\varepsilon}=f\in W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

In the case
\varepsilon=1
without the periodicity condition, these problems have been studied
extensively since the late 1970’s.
Let
N(u)
denote the nontangential maximal function of
u
.
If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
it is well known that under certain smoothness conditions on the matrix
A
,
the Dirichlet problem
(D)_{p}
with
\varepsilon=1
and the natural regularity condition
N(u_{1})\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)

is solvable for
p_{0}<p\leq\infty
, while

(N)_{p}
and
(R)_{p}
with
\varepsilon=1
and
N(\nabla u_{1})\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
are solvable for
1<p<p_{1}
, where
p_{0}<2
and
p_{1}>2
depend on
A
and
\Omega

[
14
]
.
Under the periodicity condition (
1.2
),
these results were extended to the case
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

with nontangential-maximal-function estimates that are uniform in
\varepsilon

by C. Kenig and the first author.
Indeed, it was proved in
[
16
,
17
]
that
solutions of (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) satisfy the estimates,

(1.6)

\|N(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)},

respectively, and solutions of (
1.4
) satisfy

(1.7)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

where
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
.
See (
1.9
)-(
1.10
) for the definitions of
nontangential maximal functions
N(u)
and
\widetilde{N}(u)
.
We point out that uniform estimates (
1.6
)-(
1.7
)
cannot be expected to hold without some structure conditions on
A
,
as the same estimates for the case
\varepsilon=1
fail
without some smoothness conditions on
A
, even in a smooth domain

[
19
,
3
,
14
]
.

The primary purpose of this paper is to address the following question:
Under the periodicity condition on
A
,
are there large-scale nontangential-maximal-function estimates
for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
,
without additional smoothness condition on
A
?
The question is partially motivated by the observation that if
u_{\varepsilon}
is a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
B_{1}(0)=B(0,1)
, where
A
satisfies
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
), then

(1.8)

\fint_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},

for
\varepsilon\leq r\leq 1
,
where
C
depends only on
d
and
\Lambda
.
This estimate, which follows from
[
2
]
, is now referred to as the large-scale Lipschitz estimate.
By combining (
1.8
) with the small-scale estimate,

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(0)|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},

which holds with some smoothness condition on
A
by rescaling, one obtains
the uniform Lipschitz estimates for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
. The approach of deriving the large-scale estimates (due to periodic structure) and the small-scale estimate (due to the local smoothness of the coefficients) separately turns out to be natural and general in the study of uniform regularity in homogenization. Therefore, it is equally natural to pursue the large-scale nontangential-maximal-function estimates in the same spirit.

In this paper, we find the correct forms of the large-scale nontangential maximal functions and establish their uniform estimates independent of
\varepsilon
for operators with periodic and merely bounded measurable coefficients.

1.2.
Statement of main results

Recall the definitions of the nontangential maximal function,

(1.9)

N(F)(Q)=\sup\big\{|F(X)|:X\in\Gamma(Q)\big\}

and its modified version,

(1.10)

\widetilde{N}(F)(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}:X\in\Gamma(Q)\bigg\},

where
\delta(X)=\dist(X,\partial\Omega)
, the nontangential region
\Gamma(Q)
for
Q\in\partial\Omega
is given by

(1.11)

\Gamma(Q)=\left\{X\in\Omega:|X-Q|<\beta\delta(X)\right\},

and
\beta>1
is a large fixed constant depending on
\Omega
.
For
Q\in\partial\Omega
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, define the large-scale nontangential maximal function by

(1.12)

N_{\varepsilon}(F)(Q)=\sup\big\{|F(X)|:X\in\Gamma(Q)\text{ and }\delta(X)\geq 10\varepsilon\big\},

and its modified version by

(1.13)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(F)(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}:X\in\Gamma(Q)\text{ and }\delta(X)\geq 10\varepsilon\bigg\}.

For
Q\in\partial\Omega
, define

(1.14)

S_{\varepsilon}(f)(Q)=\sup\big\{|f(P)|:P\in\partial\Omega\text{ and }|P-Q|<\varepsilon\big\}.

The following theorems are the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
A
is real, symmetric and satisfies (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and

f\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)\cap C(\partial\Omega)
. Let

u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
\Omega
with the
Dirichlet condition

on
\partial\Omega
. Then there exists

,
depending on
d
,
\Lambda
and
\Omega
,
such that for
2-\delta<p\leq\infty
,

(1.15)

\|N_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|S_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

where
C
depends on
d
,
p
,
\Lambda
and
\Omega
.
Moreover, if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain,
the estimate (
1.15
) holds for
1<p\leq\infty
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
and
\Omega
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
\Omega

u_{\varepsilon}=f\in H^{1}(\partial\Omega)

on
\partial\Omega
. Then there exists
\delta>0
, depending only on

,

(1.16)

\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)},

,

,

and
\Omega
.
Moreover, if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1}
domain,
the estimate (

.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
and
\Omega
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of
the Neumann problem:
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
\Omega

\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=g

(1.17)

\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},

\Lambda
, and

.

If in addition,
A

N_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
and
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})

1.1

and
\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})

Corollary

2.
Large-scale
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure and Dirichlet problem

2.1.
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure

The
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure and Green function can be defined for the elliptic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}=-\nabla\cdot(A^{\varepsilon}\nabla)
in a bounded domain, as long as
A^{\varepsilon}
is bounded measurable and satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition. In particular, the periodic structure or regularity on
A^{\varepsilon}
is not needed.
Let
\{\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}\}_{X\in\Omega}
denote the family of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measures for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
. This means that if
f\in C(\partial\Omega)
, the classical solution of (
1.3
) can be expressed as

(2.1)

u_{\varepsilon}(X)=\int_{\partial\Omega}fd\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}.

Let
G_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)
be the Green function of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
, i.e., for each
Y\in\Omega
,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,Y))=\delta_{Y}(\cdot)
in the sense of distributions and
G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,Y)=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Moreover,
G_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)=G_{\varepsilon}^{*}(Y,X)
, where
G_{\varepsilon}^{*}
is the Green function for the adjoint operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{*}=-\nabla\cdot(A^{*}(Y/\varepsilon)\nabla)
. In other words,
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(G_{\varepsilon}(X,\cdot))=\delta_{X}(\cdot)
for each
X\in\Omega
.

We recall some basics of the
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure and Green function, which hold for any bounded measurable coefficients; see
[
14
]
for a collection of these materials.
Let
\Delta_{r}(Q)=\partial\Omega\cap B_{r}(Q)
. Let
A_{r}(Q)
be a point in
\Gamma(Q)
such that
|A_{r}(Q)-Q|\simeq r
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and
Q\in\partial\Omega
.

(i)

\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}
is doubling, i.e., for
X\in\Omega\setminus B_{4r}(Q)
,

(2.2)

\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{2r}(Q))\lesssim\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{r}(Q)).

(ii)

It holds

(2.3)

\omega_{\varepsilon}^{A_{r}(Q)}(\Delta_{r}(Q))\simeq 1.

(iii)

For
X\in\Omega\setminus B_{2r}(Q)
,

(2.4)

\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{r}(Q))\simeq r^{d-2}G_{\varepsilon}(X,A_{r}(Q)).

(iv)

For
X\in\Omega\setminus B_{2r}(Q)
,

(2.5)

G_{\varepsilon}(X,A_{r}(Q))\simeq\bigg(\fint_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(Q)}|G_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}.

(v)

There exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for
X,Y\in\Omega

(2.6)

G_{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\lesssim\frac{\delta(X)^{\alpha}}{|X-Y|^{d-2+\alpha}}.

2.2.
Localization

Though our main theorems are proved for bounded Lipschitz domains, it is standard to apply a localization argument to reduce the local boundary estimates to Lipschitz graph domains to avoid some technical issues. For example, for a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
,
D_{r}(P)=\Omega\cap B_{r}(P)
may not be a Lipschitz domain or even be disconnected. Here, we will briefly describe the localization argument.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. If we want to estimate
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)
, it suffices to estimate the truncated nontangential maximal function
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{R}(\nabla u)
. This is due to the following observation

(2.7)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(u)(Q)\leq\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{R}(u)(Q)+C_{R}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{R/2})}.

If we choose
R\simeq r_{0}\gg\varepsilon
, where
r_{0}
is a constant depending only on
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
, then the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.7
) can be easily controlled by the energy estimate.
Consequently, we may concentrate on the estimates on the boundary layer
\Omega_{2r_{0}}
. Next, we cover
\Omega_{2r_{0}}
by a finite number of balls
B(P_{i},10r_{0})
centered at
P_{i}\in\partial\Omega
with finite overlaps. This reduces the estimates to each
D_{10r_{0}}(P_{i})=B(P_{i},10r_{0})\cap\Omega
. By a translation and a rotation, we can assume

(2.8)

B(P_{i},10r_{0})\cap\Omega=B(0,10r_{0})\cap\left\{X=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\right\},

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function, representing the local graph of
\partial\Omega
. We will use
x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
to represent the first
d-1
components of
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x_{d},y_{d},z_{d}
the last components.

With the above localization and reduction, we only need to consider the boundary value problems in the Lipschitz graph domain
\Omega=\{X=(x^{\prime},x_{d}):x_{d}>\phi(x^{\prime})\}
, whose boundary is given by
\partial\Omega=\{X=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))\}
, where
\phi
is a Lipschitz function.
Without loss of generality, assume
O=(0^{\prime},0)\in\partial\Omega
, where
0^{\prime}
denotes the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
Given
Q=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))\in\partial\Omega
, let

(2.9)

I_{r}(Q)=\left\{P=(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})):|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|<r\right\}

and

(2.10)

T_{r}(Q)=\left\{Y=(y^{\prime},y_{d}):|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|<r,\phi(y^{\prime})<y_{d}<\phi(x^{\prime})+Mr\right\},

where
M=100d(1+\|\nabla\phi\|_{\infty}).

These are analogs of
\Delta_{r}(Q)
and
D_{r}(Q)
. Note that
T_{r}(Q)
is always a Lipschitz domain and
I_{r}(Q)
is the bottom boundary of
T_{r}(Q)
. The crucial fact is that the estimates in
I_{r}(Q)
or
T_{r}(Q)
are interchangeable with those in
\Delta_{r}(Q)
and
D_{r}(Q)
due to the following simple comparisons:

(2.11)

I_{cr}(Q)\subset\Delta_{r}(Q)\subset I_{r}(Q)\quad\text{and}\quad T_{cr}(Q)\subset D_{r}(Q)\subset T_{Cr}(Q),

for some constants
0<c<1<C
. Oftentimes, we will simply write
I_{r}
and
T_{r}
if
Q=O\in\partial\Omega
, where
O
is the origin.

Note that although
\Omega
is an unbounded graph domain, we can still define

\Omega_{r}=\{X\in\Omega:\delta(X)<r\}
. For each

by

(2.12)

\Gamma(Q)=\left\{Y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in\Omega:|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<(\tan\beta)(y_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime}))\right\}

for some

(representing the half-aperture of the cone), which is a harmless modification of the original nontangential maximal function in a bounded domain. By this, all kinds of nontangential maximal functions can be defined analogously as in bounded Lipschitz domains.
For example, we redefine the truncated large-scale nontangential maximal function by

(2.13)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(F)(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B(Y,\delta(Y)/2)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}:Y\in\Gamma(Q)\cap(\Omega_{r}\setminus\Omega_{10\varepsilon})\bigg\}.

Finally, we will see how the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
changes in the above localization. If
A^{\varepsilon}(X)=A(X/\varepsilon)
is
\varepsilon

1.2

\varepsilon
-periodic with respect to a rotated lattice. Here, we will introduce the idea in
[

]
that can handle more general cases for which
A^{\varepsilon}
only needs to be periodic in the
x_{d}
-direction (and has no structure assumption in the remaining

directions). Actually, if
A
satisfies (
1.2
), we can always choose a rational rotation in the above reduction to (
2.8
) such that
A^{\varepsilon}(X)
is still
\ell\varepsilon
-periodic in
x_{d}
-direction for some integer
\ell\geq 1
(the size of
\ell
depends on the Lipschitz character of
\partial\Omega
), i.e.,
A^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=A^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d}+z\ell\varepsilon)
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\ell=1
, which yields

(2.14)

A^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d})=A^{\varepsilon}(x^{\prime},x_{d}+\varepsilon z)\qquad\text{for any }z\in\mathbb{Z}.

Indeed, the results in this section are available whenever
A^{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
2.14
) in each localized graph domain, which is a weaker assumption than (
1.2
).

2.3.
L^{2}
Rellich estimates

There are two different approaches to achieve the large-scale
L^{2}
Rellich estimates. The first one was introduced in
[
16
]
using the difference operator under the assumption (
2.14
), and the second was introduced in
[
20
]
based on the convergence rates in homogenization theory. These two approaches have their own advantages and disadvantages, though they are both applicable to periodic (in all directions) coefficients. The approach in
[
16
]
relies on the strict periodicity in the
x_{d}
-direction (i.e., (
2.14
)); while the approach in
[
20
]
also applies to non-periodic coefficients, but needs the structure assumption on the coefficients in all directions so that the quantitative homogenization applies.
We also point out that the approach in
[
16
]
requires
A
to be symmetric, while the approach in
[
20
]
only requires symmetry for the Neumann condition. For simplicity, we will always assume that
A
is symmetric in this paper.

In order to be consistent with the class of operators for regularity and Neumann problems, we recall the large-scale
L^{2}
Rellich estimates in
[
16
]
based on the assumption (
2.14
).

Proposition 2.2

(
[
16

)
.

Let
\varepsilon<r
and
A^{\varepsilon}
satisfy (

) and (

be a solution of

(2.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{in }T_{3r},\\
&u_{\varepsilon}=f\quad\text{on }I_{3r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then for any
\varepsilon<t<r
,

(2.16)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{T_{r}\cap\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{I_{3r}}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{3r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon<r
and
A^{\varepsilon}
satisfy (
1.1
) and (
2.14
). Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of

(2.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{in }T_{3r},\\
&\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=g\quad\text{on }I_{3r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then for any
\varepsilon<t<r

(2.18)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{T_{r}\cap\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{I_{3r}}|g|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{3r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Remark 2.4
.

The above propositions are not stated in the same form as
[
16
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.4]
. In this remark, we clarify that they are actually equivalent. First, the above propositions are stated in a scaled version with period
\varepsilon
, which definitely is equivalent to the original version in
[
16
, Lemmas 3.2, 3.4]
with period 1. Second,

[
16
, Lemma 3.2, 3.4]
were stated for the flat boundary
\{x_{d}=0\}
, while the above propositions are stated over Lipschitz boundaries
\{x_{d}=\phi(x^{\prime})\}
. However, by a change of variables,
(x^{\prime},x_{d})\to(y^{\prime},y_{d})=(x^{\prime},x_{d}-\phi(x^{\prime}))

). Third, the estimates (

, instead of a fixed scale
t=\varepsilon
, because if
A_{\varepsilon}
is
\varepsilon
-periodic in

, then it is
m\varepsilon
-periodic for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and the desired estimates are valid for any

.

The above propositions can be converted to
D_{r}
in a bounded Lipschitz domain for the operator

using (

) and a covering argument. Consequently, they implies the global

) and (

\frac{1}{t}\int_{D_{cr_{0}}(P_{i})\cap\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\Delta_{r_{0}}(P_{i})}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}\int_{D_{r_{0}}(P_{i})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Choosing

with finite overlaps, then we sum over

for the above inequality and obtain

(2.20)

\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Omega_{t}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\lesssim\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Finally, the energy estimate yields

For an application to the Dirichlet problem in the next subsection, we state the following local estimate in

2.4.
Large-scale
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure

Define the large-scale
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure by

(2.22)

\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q):=\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q))}{\sigma(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q))}.

For a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, we have
\sigma(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q))\simeq\varepsilon^{d-1}
.
By the doubling property of
\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}
, we can show the doubling property of
\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
0<r<r_{0},\Delta_{r}=\Delta_{r}(P),X\in\Omega
and
|X-P|>5\max\{r,\varepsilon\}
, we have

(2.23)

\int_{\Delta_{2r}}\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)\lesssim\int_{\Delta_{r}}\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q).

Proof.

First, consider
r<10\varepsilon
. In this case, by the doubling property of
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}
, for any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Delta_{2\varepsilon}(P)
, we have
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q))\simeq\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\prime}))
.
Here we have used the assumption
|X-P|>5\varepsilon
.
This implies that
\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)\simeq\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\prime})
for any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\Delta_{2r}\subset\Delta_{2\varepsilon}(P)
. Thus, (
2.23
) follows.

Now consider the case
r\geq 10\varepsilon
. By Fubini’s Theorem, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{2r}}\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{2r}}\int_{\Delta_{2r+\varepsilon}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Q^{\prime}-Q|<\varepsilon\}}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\prime})|^{-1}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime})d\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Delta_{2r+\varepsilon}}\int_{\Delta_{2r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Q^{\prime}-Q|<\varepsilon\}}d\sigma(Q)\varepsilon^{1-d}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Delta_{2r+\varepsilon}}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{2r+\varepsilon})\lesssim\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{r-\varepsilon}),

where in the last line we have used the fact
2r+\varepsilon\leq 3(r-\varepsilon)
for
r\geq 10\varepsilon
and the doubling property of
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}
in Proposition
2.1

(i)
.

On the other hand, using Fubini’s Theorem again, we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{r}}\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{r}}\int_{\Delta_{r+\varepsilon}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Q^{\prime}-Q|<\varepsilon\}}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\prime})|^{-1}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime})d\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{\Delta_{r+\varepsilon}}\int_{\Delta_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Q^{\prime}-Q|<\varepsilon\}}d\sigma(Q)\varepsilon^{1-d}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime}).

Observe that for
Q^{\prime}\in\Delta_{r-\varepsilon}
,

(2.26)

\int_{\Delta_{r}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Q^{\prime}-Q|<\varepsilon\}}d\sigma(Q)=\sigma(\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q^{\prime}))\simeq\varepsilon^{d-1}.

It follows that

(2.27)

\int_{\Delta_{r}}\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)\gtrsim\int_{\Delta_{r-\varepsilon}}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q^{\prime})=\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(\Delta_{r-\varepsilon}).

Combining (
2.24
) and (
2.27
), we get (
2.23
).
∎

2.5.
Dirichlet problem

In this subsection, we consider the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) and prove Theorem
1.1
for Lipschitz domains. The following is the key theorem that explains how the large-scale
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure is involved.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
f\in C(\partial\Omega)
and

(2.33)

v_{\varepsilon}(X)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(Q)\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q).

Then
v_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of

(2.34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
&v_{\varepsilon}(Q)=\bar{f}(Q):=\int_{\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}fd\sigma\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
|\Delta_{\varepsilon}|=|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)|\simeq\varepsilon^{d-1}
.
Moreover, for any
p\in(2-\delta,\infty)
,

(2.35)

\|N_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Proof.

By Fubini’s Theorem,

(2.36)

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)|^{-1}f(Q)1_{\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)}(P)d\omega^{X}_{\varepsilon}(P)d\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\partial\Omega}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)|^{-1}f(Q)1_{\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)}(Q)d\sigma(Q)d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(P)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)}|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)|^{-1}f(Q)d\sigma(Q)d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(P)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\bar{f}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}.

This proves (
2.34
).

Next, we prove (
2.35
). Let
X\in\Gamma(Q)
and
\dist(X,\partial\Omega)>10\varepsilon
. Let
r_{0}=c|X-Q|
and
r_{j}=2^{j}r_{0}
. Let
R_{0}=\Delta_{r_{0}}(Q)
,
R_{j}=\Delta_{r_{j}}(Q)\setminus\Delta_{r_{j-1}}(Q)
for

, where
L
is the minimal integer such that
2^{L}r_{0}\geq c\diam(\Omega)
,
and
R_{L+1}=\partial\Omega\setminus\Delta_{r_{L}}
. Then

(2.37)

\partial\Omega=\bigcup_{j=0}^{L+1}R_{j}.

Observe that for every
j=0,1,\cdots,L+1
, we have
\dist(X,R_{j})\simeq 2^{j}r_{0}
. Thus, Corollary
2.8
and a covering argument yields

(2.38)

\bigg(\fint_{R_{j}}\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q)^{2+\delta}d\sigma(Q)\bigg)^{1/(2+\delta)}\lesssim\fint_{R_{j}}\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q)d\sigma(Q).

Now, we bound (
2.33

(2.39)

\displaystyle|v_{\varepsilon}(X)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{L+1}\int_{R_{j}}|f(P^{\prime})|\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(P^{\prime})d\sigma(P^{\prime})

where

.
By the definition of
\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}
and Proposition
2.1

(iii)

(v)
,

(2.40)

\int_{R_{j}}\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(P^{\prime})d\sigma(P^{\prime})\lesssim\int_{R_{j}}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}=\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(R_{j})\simeq 2^{-j\alpha}.

It follows from (

(2.41)

\displaystyle|v_{\varepsilon}(X)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{L+1}\bigg(\fint_{R_{j}}|f(P^{\prime})|^{p^{\prime}_{0}}d\sigma(P^{\prime})\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}_{0}}2^{-j\alpha}

Remark 2.10
.

The equations (

) can be written as

(2.44)

\int_{\partial\Omega}f(Q)\overline{\omega}^{X}_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\bar{f}(Q)d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q).

Remark 2.11
.

The proof of Theorem
2.9
gives the following result. If
\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}\in B_{p}(d\sigma)
for some
p>1
, namely, for
X\in\Omega\setminus B_{10r}(P)
,

(2.45)

\bigg(\fint_{\Delta_{r}(P)}\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q)^{p}d\sigma(Q)\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim\fint_{\Delta_{r}(P)}\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}(Q)d\sigma(Q),

then
v_{\varepsilon}
given by (
2.33
) satisfies

(2.46)

\|N_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}.

This large-scale estimate can be compared to the classical result for
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic functions; see e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
11
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We use the representation formula by
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure,

(2.47)

u_{\varepsilon}(X)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(P)d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}(P).

Observe that by the definition of
S_{\varepsilon}(f)
, we have
|f(P)|\leq S_{\varepsilon}(f)(Q)
for any
Q\in\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)
. Since
|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)|\simeq|\Delta_{\varepsilon}(Q)|
, we have

|f(P)|\lesssim\overline{S_{\varepsilon}(f)}(P)
.
Consequently, in view of (
2.44
),

(2.48)

|u_{\varepsilon}(X)|\lesssim\int_{\partial\Omega}\overline{S_{\varepsilon}(f)}d\omega_{\varepsilon}^{X}=\int_{\partial\Omega}S_{\varepsilon}(f)\overline{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{X}d\sigma.

Then (
1.15
) follows from this and (
2.35
) in Theorem
2.9
.
∎

Note that if
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain and
\varepsilon
is small enough (depending on
\Omega
), then
\Omega^{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}
is also a Lipschitz domain.
Applying Theorem
1.1
to the subdomain
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

(2.49)

\|N_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon})}.

Here, we have also used an observation that the large-scale nontangential maximal function on
\partial\Omega
can be bounded by that on
\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}
with slight modifications (enlarge
\beta
in (
1.11
) and replace
10\varepsilon
by
8\varepsilon
in (
1.12
)) on the definition for the latter.
We find a bi-Lipschitz map
\tau:\partial\Omega\mapsto\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}
such that
Q^{\varepsilon}=\tau(Q)
satisfies
B_{2\varepsilon}(Q_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{4\varepsilon}(Q)
.
Hence, by the interior
L^{\infty}
estimate, we have for
P\in\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}

(2.50)

S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(Q^{\varepsilon})=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon}\\
|P-Q^{\varepsilon}|<\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|u_{\varepsilon}(P)|\lesssim\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon}(Q^{\varepsilon})\cap\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\fint_{B_{4\varepsilon}(Q)\cap\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|=M_{4\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(Q).

It follows that
\|S_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega^{\varepsilon})}\lesssim\|M_{4\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}
, which together with (
2.49
) leads to

(2.51)

\|N_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|M_{4\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

This is an alternative version of the large-scale
(D)_{p}
estimates.

3.
Regularity and Neumann problems

Apart from the
L^{2}
Rellich estimate, for the regularity and Neumann problems, we also need the difference operator, which relies essentially on the periodicity of the coefficient matrix
A^{\varepsilon}
in the
x_{d}
-direction as in (
2.14
) in graph domains (after localization).

3.1.
Difference operator

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
T_{10r}=T_{10r}(O)
with
r>10\varepsilon
. Assume that
A^{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
2.14
). We will estimate
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
for
Q\in I_{r}
.

For
X=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\Omega
, define the difference operator by

(3.1)

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u)(X)=\frac{u(X+\varepsilon e_{d})-u(X)}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{x_{d}}^{x_{d}+\varepsilon}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{d}}(x^{\prime},t)dt.

The key observation for the difference operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}
is that if
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
with
A^{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
2.14
), then
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is also a solution in the domain where
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is defined. The following lemma reduces the local estimate of large-scale nontangential maximal functions to the difference estimate and a boundary layer term.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\lesssim r
and
I_{r}=I_{r}(O)
with
O\in\partial\Omega
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
T_{10r}
.
Then for any
Q=(z^{\prime},\phi(z^{\prime}))\in I_{r}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})))(Q)+\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(V_{\varepsilon})(Q),

where

(3.3)

V_{\varepsilon}(P)=\fint_{0}^{11\varepsilon}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+t)|dt,\ \text{with }P=(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})),

and

(3.4)

\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}f(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\fint_{I^{\prime}}|f|d\sigma:Q\in I^{\prime}\subset I_{3r}\bigg\}.

Proof.

Fix
Q_{0}=(z^{\prime},\phi(z^{\prime}))\in I_{r}
and
X\in\Gamma(Q_{0})\cap(\Omega_{r}\setminus\Omega_{10\varepsilon})
. Let
s:=\delta(X)\leq r
.
By the interior Caccioppoli inequality,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B(X,s/2)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\fint_{B(X,3s/5)}|u_{\varepsilon}-E|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s}\fint_{B(X,4s/5)}|u_{\varepsilon}-E|,

where
E
is an arbitrary constant to be specified later.

Let
Y=(y^{\prime},y_{d})\in B(X,4s/5)
.
Note that

(3.6)

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d})-E|

\displaystyle\leq|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d}-\varepsilon)|+|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d}-\varepsilon)-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d}-2\varepsilon)|

\displaystyle+\cdots+|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d}-m\varepsilon)-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)|+|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)-E|,

where
m
is an integer such that
y_{d}-m\varepsilon>\phi(y^{\prime})+10\varepsilon\geq y_{d}-(m+1)\varepsilon
, and
h\in[0,\varepsilon]
is a free parameter. On both sides taking average over
h\in[0,\varepsilon]
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d})-E|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\varepsilon|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(y^{\prime},y_{d}-j\varepsilon)|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},y_{d}-m\varepsilon)-u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)|dh

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)-E|dh

\displaystyle\leq(m-1)\varepsilon\sup_{\phi(y^{\prime})+10\varepsilon<t<\phi(y^{\prime})+3s}|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})(y^{\prime},t)|

\displaystyle+\int_{\phi(y^{\prime})}^{\phi(y^{\prime})+11\varepsilon}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},t)|dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)-E|dh.

Note that
m\varepsilon\simeq s
. We will write
u_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime})+h)=u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})
for
Q=(y^{\prime},\phi(y^{\prime}))
.

Integrating (
3.7
) over
Y\in B(X,4s/5)
, we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle\fint_{B(X,4s/5)}|u_{\varepsilon}-E|

\displaystyle\lesssim s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))d\sigma+\varepsilon\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}V_{\varepsilon}d\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}|u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})-E|d\sigma(Q)dh,

where
V_{\varepsilon}
is given by (
3.3
) and
(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))
is the vertical projection of
X=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
on
\partial\Omega
.
Let
E=E_{0}=\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}u_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q)
and
E_{h}=\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})d\sigma(Q)
. Then by the Poincaré inequality,

(3.9)

\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}|u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})-E_{h}|\lesssim s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})|d\sigma(Q),

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle|E_{h}-E|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}(u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})-u_{\varepsilon}(Q))d\sigma(Q)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x_{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})|dhd\sigma(Q).

By these two inequalities, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}|u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})-E|d\sigma(Q)dh

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{0}^{\varepsilon}\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}|u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})-E_{h}|d\sigma dh+\fint_{0}^{\varepsilon}|E_{h}-E|dh

\displaystyle\lesssim s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}\fint_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})|dhd\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}\fint_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x_{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(Q+he_{d})|dhd\sigma(Q)

\displaystyle\lesssim s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}V_{\varepsilon}(Q)d\sigma(Q).

Inserting this into (
3.8
), we get

(3.12)

\fint_{B(X,4s/5)}|u_{\varepsilon}-E|\lesssim s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))d\sigma+s\fint_{I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))}V_{\varepsilon}d\sigma.

Using the observation that
I_{s}(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))\subset I_{2s}(Q_{0})\subset I_{3r}(O)
due to
X\in\Gamma(Q_{0})\cap\Omega_{r}
, we have

(3.13)

\frac{1}{s}\fint_{B(X,4s/5)}|u_{\varepsilon}-E|\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})))(Q_{0})+\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(V_{\varepsilon})(Q_{0}),

which combined with (
3.5
) gives the desired estimate.
∎

3.2.
L^{2}
estimates

The pointwise estimate (
3.2
) and the
L^{2}
boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function imply

(3.14)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{3r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}d\sigma+\int_{I_{3r}}V_{\varepsilon}^{2}d\sigma.

Now it suffices to estimate the difference term with
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
, which is also a solution.

Recall that
T_{t}=T_{t}(O)=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d}):|x^{\prime}|<t,\phi(x^{\prime})<x_{d}<\phi(0)+Mt\}
is a Lipschitz domain.
Define
T_{t,\varepsilon}=(T_{t})_{\varepsilon}=\{X\in T_{t}:\dist(X,\partial T_{t})<\varepsilon\}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
T_{5r}
with
r\geq 10\varepsilon
. Then

(3.15)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{5r}\cap\Omega_{5\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\int_{T_{5r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Proof.

By the estimate (
2.51
) applied to
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
in
T_{t}
for every
t\in[4r,5r]
, we have

(3.16)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{\partial T_{t}}|N_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{t,4\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}.

Using (
3.1
), we see that

(3.17)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{t,4\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{t+\varepsilon,5\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Combining the last two inequalities, we obtain

(3.18)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{t+\varepsilon,5\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Note that the left-hand side is independent of
t
as
t
varies in
[4r,5r]
. Thus, we integrate the above inequality in
t
over
[4r,5r-\varepsilon]
and use Fubini’s Theorem to obtain

(3.19)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{5r}\cap\Omega_{5\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{5r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
T_{15r}
with
r\geq 10\varepsilon
. Then

(3.20)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{15r}\cap\Omega_{20\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{15r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Proof.

This follows from (
3.14
) and Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
for
p=2
.

First of all, by rotation and a localization argument as in Subsection
2.2
, the local estimate of large-scale nontangential maximal function (
3.20
), established in a graph domain, can be transferred into a form in a bounded Lipschitz domain, i.e.,

(3.21)

\int_{\Delta_{r}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D_{15r}\cap\Omega_{20\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{D_{15r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

By taking
r\simeq r_{0}
as in Subsection
2.2
and a covering argument, we have

(3.22)

\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{20\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now, if
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
1.5
), then the energy estimate implies

(3.23)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

The large-scale Rellich estimate (
1.18
) implies

(3.24)

\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{20\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial\Omega)}.

Combining these estimates together, we obtain (
1.16
) for
p=2
. Similarly, for the Neumann problem we apply the energy estimate and the large-scale Rellich estimate (
1.19
) to the right-hand side of (
3.22
) to obtain (
1.17
) for
p=2
.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(Localized
L^{2}

.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
-\nabla\cdot A^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
T_{2r}
. Then for

,

(3.25)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}^{r}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{I_{2r}}|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}d\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},

and

(3.26)

\int_{I_{r}}\widetilde{N}^{r}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{I_{2r}}\Big|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}\Big|^{2}d\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
3.3
and Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
.

3.3.
L^{p}
estimates for
1<p<2

It is classical that the
(R)_{2}
(resp.
(N)_{2}
) estimate will directly imply the
(R)_{p}
(resp.
(N)_{p}
) estimates for any
1<p<2
, by an interpolation between
L^{2}
estimate and
L^{1}
estimate (with data in atom Hardy space
H^{1}_{\rm at}(\partial\Omega)
); see
[
15
, Theorem 5.2, 6.2]
(also see
[
7
,
16
]
).
In this subsection, we will adjust the classical argument to the large-scale
L^{p}
estimate with
1<p<2
, only using the large-scale
(R)_{2}
or
(N)_{2}
estimates.

We first consider the regularity problem.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
f\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
\Omega
with

u_{\varepsilon}=f
on
\partial\Omega
, then

(3.27)

\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{H^{1}_{\rm at}(\partial\Omega)}.

It is sufficient to prove (
3.27
) for an atom function
f
, whose support is contained in
\Delta_{r}(P)
for some
P\in\partial\Omega
with
r>0
and
|\nabla_{\tan}f|\leq Cr^{1-d}
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
\Omega
with Dirichlet data
f
. Then it suffices to show

(3.28)

\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim 1.

First of all, by Hölder’s inequality and the
(R)_{2}
estimate, we have

(3.29)

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{10r}(P)}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|

\displaystyle\leq|\Delta_{10r}(P)|^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{\Delta_{10r}(P)}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{(d-1)/2}\bigg(\int_{\Delta_{r}(P)}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim 1.

Next, we decompose the rest of the boundary as

(3.30)

\partial\Omega\setminus\Delta_{10r}(P)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{L+1}R_{j},

where
R_{j}=\Delta_{r_{j}}(P)\setminus\Delta_{r_{j-1}}(P)
,
r_{j}=2^{j}10r
for
1\leq j\leq L
,
R_{L+1}=\partial\Omega\setminus\Delta_{r_{L}}
, and
L
is an integer such that
r_{L}\approx\diam(\Omega)
. We will estimate
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
for
Q\in R_{j}
for each
j\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
Q\in R_{j}
, we have

(3.31)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{cr_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)+\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-1+\alpha}},

where
c\in(0,1)
is a constant independent of
r_{j}
and
\varepsilon
.
Moreover, if
cr_{j}\leq 10\varepsilon
, then the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.31
) does not appear.

Proof.

Let
X\in\Gamma(Q)
with
\delta(X)\geq\max\{10\varepsilon,cr_{j}\}
. By the Caccioppoli inequality,

(3.32)

\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\delta(X)^{-1}\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\frac{3}{5}\delta(X))}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Note that for each
Y\in B(X,\frac{3}{5}\delta(X))
, we still have
\delta(Y)\geq\frac{2}{5}\max\{10\varepsilon,cr_{j}\}
. By using the representation by
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measure (
2.1
), we have

(3.33)

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(Y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Delta_{r}(P))}\omega^{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{r}(P))\lesssim r^{d-2}\omega^{Y}_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{r}(P))

\displaystyle\simeq G_{\varepsilon}(Y,A_{r}(P))\lesssim\frac{r^{\alpha}}{|Y-P|^{d-2+\alpha}},

where we have used Proposition
2.1

(iii)
and
(v)
in the last inequality.
Obviously,
|Y-P|\geq\delta(Y)\geq\frac{2}{5}cr_{j}
. Hence, for every
Y\in B(X,\frac{3}{5}\delta(X))
,

(3.34)

|u(Y)|\lesssim\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-2+\alpha}}.

Combining this with (
3.32
), we obtain that for any
X\in\Gamma(Q)
and
\delta(X)\geq\max\{10\varepsilon,r_{j}\}
,

(3.35)

\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-1+\alpha}}.

Thus, if
r_{j}\geq 10\varepsilon
, then we have (
3.31
). If
r_{j}<10\varepsilon
, we simply do not have the term
N_{\varepsilon}^{r_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
on the right-hand side.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

It suffices to prove (
3.28
).
We show on each
R_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq L+1
,

(3.36)

\int_{R_{j}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|d\sigma\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{r_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

If
cr_{j}\leq 10\varepsilon
, then by Lemma
3.6
and an integration over
R_{j}
, we directly get

(3.37)

\int_{R_{j}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|d\sigma\lesssim\int_{R_{j}}\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-1+\alpha}}d\sigma\lesssim\Big(\frac{r}{r_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

If
cr_{j}>10\varepsilon
, by Lemma
3.6
,

(3.38)

\int_{R_{j}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma\lesssim\int_{R_{j}}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{cr_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma+\Big(\frac{r}{r_{j}}\Big)^{\alpha}.

Note that
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega\setminus\Delta(P,r)
. We decompose
R_{j}
into a union of
\Delta_{cr_{j}}(Q_{k})
with finite overlaps. As
\Omega
is Lipschitz, the number of such
\Delta_{cr_{j}}(Q_{k})
depends only on
\Omega
. Then by (
3.25
) and the Caccioppoli inequality,

(3.39)

\int_{\Delta_{cr_{j}}(Q_{k})}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{cr_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{j}}\int_{D_{2cr_{j}}(Q_{k})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{r_{j}^{3}}\int_{D_{3cr_{j}}(Q_{k})}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.

Using the same argument as Lemma
3.6
, for

,

(3.40)

|u_{\varepsilon}(X)|\lesssim\frac{r^{\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-2+\alpha}}.

Inserting this into (
3.39
), we get

(3.41)

\int_{\Delta_{cr_{j}}(Q_{k})}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{cr_{j}}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{r^{2\alpha}}{r_{j}^{d-1+2\alpha}}.

Summing over
k
and combining this with (
3.38
) and the Hölder inequality, we arrive at (
3.36
).

Consequently, by (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and (
3.36
), we have

(3.42)

\int_{\partial\Omega}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|\leq\int_{\Delta_{10r}(P)}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|+\sum_{j}\int_{R_{j}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|d\sigma\lesssim 1.

The proof is complete.
∎

We first recall the Neumann function

; see

Proposition 3.7

If

3.4.
L^{p}
estimates for
2<p<2+\delta

It is classical that the
L^{p}
regularity and Neumann estimates for
2<p<2+\delta
follow from the
L^{2}
estimate and a real-variable argument; see
[
15
, Theorem 5.3, 6.3]
.
In this subsection, we prove the large-scale
L^{p}
regularity/Neumann estimates for
2<p<2+\delta
by modifying the real-variable argument in
[
16
]
. To this end, we first consider a local problem in the graph domain
T_{r}
. We introduce
a modified large-scale nontangential maximal function: for
Q\in\partial\Omega
,

(3.54)

\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(F)(Q):=\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(F)(Q)+M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(F)(Q),

where

(3.55)

M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(F)(Q)=\bigg(\fint_{T_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(Q)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

and

(3.56)

T_{r}^{\star}(Q)=\{X\in\Omega:\text{proj}(X)\in I_{r}(Q),\delta(X)<r\}.

Here
\proj(X)
denotes the vertical projection of
X=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
on
\partial\Omega
, i.e.,
\proj(X)=(x^{\prime},\phi(x^{\prime}))\in\partial\Omega
.
This modified large-scale nontangential maximal function includes the information of
F
on the boundary layer of thickness
10\varepsilon
and is useful in proving the reverse Hölder inequality.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
in
T_{4R}
satisfying either
u_{\varepsilon}=0
or
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=0
on
I_{4R}
with
R\geq 100\varepsilon
. Then there exists
p_{0}>2
such that

(3.57)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{R}}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p_{0}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/{p_{0}}}\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{2R}}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/{2}}.

Proof.

This is proved by the self-improving property of the reverse Hölder inequality.
Let
P\in I_{R}
and
I_{4r}(P)\subset I_{2R},r>0
. We only need to prove

(3.58)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3r}(P)}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{p_{*}},

where
p_{*}=\frac{2d}{d+2}<2
. Then (
3.57
) is a straightforward corollary of the self-improving property of the reverse Hölder inequality.

Case 1:
r<50\varepsilon
.
We will show that for this case, a reverse Hölder inequality holds for
\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
by its definition, irrelevant to the equation. To see this,
we first claim that if
r<50\varepsilon
, for each
Q\in I_{r}(P)
,

(3.59)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\fint_{I_{r}(Q)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma.

To show the claim, we fix
Q
and let
X\in\Gamma(Q)
be such that
\delta(X)>10\varepsilon
and
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
is attained at
X
, namely,

(3.60)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\simeq\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Now, in view of the definition of the cone
\Gamma(Q)
in graph domain (
2.12
), we see that there exists a subset
E\subset I_{r}(Q)
with
|E|\simeq|I_{r}(Q)|
such that for each
Q^{\prime}\in E
,
X\in\Gamma(Q^{\prime})
. This implies
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q^{\prime})
for each
Q^{\prime}\in E
. Hence,

(3.61)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma\lesssim\fint_{I_{r}(Q)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma,

which proves the claim (
3.59
).

The claim (
3.59
) then easily yields

(3.62)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma.

Next, by a property of the average operator (
B.5
) in Proposition
B.2
(the property is proved for
M_{\varepsilon}
, but it holds for
M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}
as well), for any
s\lesssim 10\varepsilon
, we have

(3.63)

M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\fint_{I_{s}(Q)}M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma.

Taking
s=r<50\varepsilon
and any
Q\in I_{r}(P)
, we have

(3.64)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma.

Combining (
3.62
) and (
3.64
), we obtain, for
r<50\varepsilon
,

(3.65)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma.

Case 2:
r>50\varepsilon
.
In this large-scale case, we first note that for each
Q\in\Delta_{r}
,

(3.66)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})d\sigma+\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q).

Indeed, if
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
is reached at a point
X
with
\delta(X)>r
, then, for the same reason as in Case 1,
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
is bounded by the first term of (
3.66
). If
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)
is attained at a point
X
with
\delta(X)<r
, then it is clearly bounded by the second term of (
3.66
).

We estimate the second term of (
3.66
).
Using either the local
(R)_{2}
estimate (
3.25
) if
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
I_{4R}
or the local
(N)_{2}
estimate (
3.26
) if
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=0
on
I_{4R}
,

(3.67)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{T_{2r}(P)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r}\bigg(\fint_{T_{3r}(P)}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{T_{3r}(P)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}},

where we have used the Caccioppoli inequality and Sobolev-Poincaré inequality and
1/p_{*}-1/d=1/2
. Note that in the case
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=0
, we need to replace
u_{\varepsilon}
by
u_{\varepsilon}-\fint_{T_{3r}(P)}u_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.67
).

Now we decompose the last integral into two parts

(3.68)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{T_{3r}(P)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{T_{3r}(P)\cap\{\delta(X)>10\varepsilon\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{T_{3r}(P)\cap\{\delta(X)<10\varepsilon\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}.

Note that the projection
T_{3r}
on
\partial\Omega
is
I_{3r}
. We point out that the average operator
M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}
is introduced in the modified nontangential maximal function in order to handle the boundary layer term in (
3.68
) that cannot be avoided in our large-scale analysis. In view of the definition of
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
, if
X\in T_{3r}\cap\{\delta(X)>10\varepsilon\}
has the projection
Q\in I_{3r}
, then by the large-scale Lipschitz estimate

(3.69)

M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(X)\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q).

Thus, by a property of
M_{\varepsilon}
in (
B.1
) of Proposition
B.1
and Fubini’s Theorem, we have

(3.70)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{T_{3r}(P)\cap\{\delta(X)>10\varepsilon\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{T_{3r}(P)\cap\{\delta(X)>10\varepsilon\}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3r}(P)}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}.

Moreover, by Fubini’s Theorem and Hölder inequality,

(3.71)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d}}\int_{T_{3r}(P)\cap\{\delta(X)<10\varepsilon\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon}{r^{d}}\int_{I_{3r}(P)}\fint_{T_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(Q)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}dXd\sigma(Q)\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon}{r^{d}}\int_{I_{3r}(P)}\bigg(\fint_{T_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(Q)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dX\bigg)^{p_{*}/2}d\sigma(Q)\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\fint_{I_{3r}(P)}|M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}.

Taking both (
3.70
) and (
3.71
) into (
3.68
), we have

(3.72)

\bigg(\fint_{T_{3r}(P)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{*}}\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3r}(P)}\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p_{*}},

which together with (
3.67
) yields

(3.73)

\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3r}(P)}\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p_{*}}.

To handle
M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
, by the
L^{2}
Rellich estimate in
T_{4r}(P)
for either the regularity problem or Neumann problem, we obtain

(3.74)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon r^{d-1}}\int_{T_{3r/2}\cap\Omega_{10\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{T_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3r}}\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})^{p_{*}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p_{*}},

where we have used the combined estimate of (
3.67
) and (
3.72
) in the last inequality.
This and (
3.73
) together give (
3.58
) for the case
r>50\varepsilon
.
Consequently, we have proved the reverse Hölder inequality (
3.58
) for all
I_{4r}(P)\subset I_{2R}
and
r>0
, as desired.
∎

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{R}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{3R}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/{2}}+\bigg(\fint_{I_{4R}\cap\Omega_{10\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/{2}}+\bigg(\fint_{I_{4R}}|g|^{p}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p}.

Again, this is proved by the real-variable argument.
Let
I_{r}(P)\subset I_{R}
with
4r<R
and
r>100\varepsilon
. Let

\lambda=|\partial T_{4R}\setminus I_{4r}(P)|^{-1}\int_{I_{4r}(P)}gd\sigma.

Then we define

. Then we have
\int_{\partial T_{4R}}g_{1}=0
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}^{1}
be the solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{1})=0
in
T_{4R}
and
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{1}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=g_{1}
on
\partial T_{4R}
. Decompose
u_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}^{1}+u_{\varepsilon}^{2}
and note that
\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=0
on
I_{4r}(P)
.

Let
F=\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}),F_{I_{r}}=\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{1}_{\varepsilon})
and
R_{I_{r}}=\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{2})
. By the
(N)_{2}
estimate in the Lipschitz domain
T_{4R}
and the large-scale
L^{2}
Rellich estimate (

), we have

(3.82)

\displaystyle\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}|F_{I_{r}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|I_{2r}(P)|}\int_{I_{2r}(P)}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u^{1}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|I_{2r}(P)|}\int_{I_{4R}}|g_{1}|^{2}\lesssim\fint_{I_{4r}(P)}|g|^{2}.

For
R_{I_{r}}
, Lemma

yields, for some

(3.83)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}(P)}|R_{I_{r}}|^{p_{0}}\bigg)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}|R_{I_{r}}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}|F_{I_{r}}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{2r}(P)}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{I_{4r}(P)}|g|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Now, we are in a situation similar to the regularity problem. By Theorem
C.2
and a localization argument as before, we can prove (
1.17
). The details are omitted.
∎

4.1.
Localized
L^{p}
Rellich estimates

In this subsection, we will establish the large-scale
L^{p}
Rellich estimates for any
p\in(2,\infty)
in
C^{1}
domains. Unlike the large-scale
L^{2}
Rellich estimate, which can be derived in graph domains under the assumption (

), the
L^{p}
Rellich estimates rely on the quantitative convergence rates in
C^{1}
domains. In the case of VMO coefficients, the large-scale
L^{p}
Rellich estimates have been established in
[
20
]
. Since in this paper we do not assume any regularity on the coefficients, we will work with the averaged gradient
M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
instead of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
to avoid the local irregularity caused by the rough coefficients. This crucial modification leads to a sequence of large-scale estimates and some of them are even new for harmonic functions.

For the localized Rellich estimates, it is more convenient to work in a
C^{1}
graph domain
\Omega
. For
Q\in I_{r}(Q)
, we redefine the boundary average operator as

(4.1)

M_{t}^{\partial}(f)(Q)=\bigg(\fint_{I_{t}(Q)}|f|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}.

The following are the main theorems of this subsection.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a
C^{1}
graph domain.
Let
1\gtrsim r\gtrsim\varepsilon
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }T_{2r},\\
&u_{\varepsilon}=f\in W^{1,2}(I_{2r})\quad\text{on }I_{2r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then for any

and
\varepsilon\leq t<r
,

(4.3)

\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Omega_{t}\cap T_{r/2}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim\bigg(\int_{I_{2r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\nabla_{\tan}f)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}+r^{\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}\bigg(\int_{T_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a
C^{1}
graph domain.
Let
1\gtrsim r\gtrsim\varepsilon
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0\quad\text{in }T_{2r},\\
&\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}=g\in L^{2}(I_{2r})\quad\text{on }I_{2r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then for any
p\in[2,\infty)
and
\varepsilon\leq t<r
,

(4.5)

\bigg(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\Omega_{t}\cap T_{r/2}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\lesssim\bigg(\int_{I_{2r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(g)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}+r^{\frac{d-1}{p}-\frac{d}{2}}\bigg(\int_{T_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

We will concentrate on the proof of Theorem
4.1
for the regularity problem. The proof of Theorem
4.2
is similar.
The proof relies on the nontangential maximal function of the homogenized solution and the optimal convergence rates in
L^{p}
spaces for the gradient
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}

We begin with a general lemma that relates the large-scale nontangential maximal function
\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}
defined by (

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
r>100\varepsilon
. Let

. Then for any

(4.6)

\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)(Q)\lesssim M_{10\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\widetilde{N}(\nabla u))(Q).

Proof.

First, by a reasoning similar to (
3.59

(4.7)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)(Q)\lesssim\fint_{I_{10\varepsilon}(Q)}\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)d\sigma\lesssim M_{10\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\widetilde{N}(\nabla u))(Q).

Next, by Fubini’s Theorem,

(4.8)

\displaystyle M_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(\nabla u)(Q)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\fint_{T_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(Q)}|\nabla u|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{10\varepsilon}{|T_{10\varepsilon}^{\star}(Q)|}\int_{I_{10\varepsilon}(Q)}\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{10\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\widetilde{N}(\nabla u))(Q).

These estimates together prove the lemma.
∎

The next proposition, which is our first ingredient from the
C^{1}
domains, is a large-scale version for the local
L^{p}
estimate of nontangential maximal function for the homogenized operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\nabla\cdot(\widehat{A}\nabla)
; see Appendix
A
for a brief introduction of the homogenized operator.

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that
\Omega
is a
C^{1}
graph domain. Let
1\geq r>100\varepsilon
.
Let
u
be a solution to

(4.9)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{0}(u)=0\quad\text{in }T_{2r},\\
&u=f\in W^{1,2}(\partial T_{2r})\quad\text{on }\partial T_{2r}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then for any

(4.10)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\widetilde{N}(\nabla u))|^{p}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{I_{2r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(\nabla_{\tan}f)|^{p}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p}+\bigg(\fint_{\partial T_{2r}\setminus I_{2r}}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}.

By a localization argument, the above proposition implies the following statement. If
\Omega

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{0}(u)=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\

(4.12)

Combining Proposition
4.4
and Lemma

Corollary 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.4

(4.13)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{I_{r}}|\widehat{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)|^{p}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p}

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

(4.65)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{r}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{T_{10r}\cap\Omega_{20\varepsilon}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{10r}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}.

Proof.

Recalling Lemma
3.1
, we have

(4.66)

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})))(Q)+\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(V_{\varepsilon})(Q),

where

,

and

are defined in (

) and (

), respectively.

For our application here, we need to slightly modify the estimate, by examining the proof of Lemma

(4.67)

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}(\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})))(Q)+\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}^{\varepsilon}(V_{\varepsilon})(Q),

where

(4.68)

\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}^{\varepsilon}f(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\fint_{I_{s}(P)}|f|d\sigma:Q\in I_{s}(P)\subset I_{3r}\text{ and }s\geq\varepsilon\bigg\}.

For this large-scale Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, we have

(4.69)

\int_{I_{r}}|\mathcal{M}_{I_{3r}}^{\varepsilon}f|^{p}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{I_{3r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(f)|^{p}d\sigma,

for any

. This can be proved in a similar manner as Proposition
B.4
, replacing the volume average
M_{\varepsilon}
in

by the surface average
M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}
on

.
Thus, from (

(4.70)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{r}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}(Q)d\sigma(Q)\lesssim\int_{I_{3r}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma+\int_{I_{3r}}|M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(V_{\varepsilon})|^{p}d\sigma.

We need to estimate the two terms on the right-hand side.

By the
(D)_{p}
estimate in Theorem
1.1
with

for
\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
in

for

and

, we have

(4.71)

\int_{I_{3r}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{\partial T_{t}}|N_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{\partial T_{t}}|M_{4\varepsilon}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma.

Now, integrating over

, we have

(4.72)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{3r}}|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}^{3r}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I_{10r-5\varepsilon}}|M_{4\varepsilon}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}d\sigma+\frac{1}{r}\int_{T_{10r-5\varepsilon}}|M_{4\varepsilon}(\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{p}dX.

(4.73)

(4.74)

(4.76)

(4.77)

M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}(V_{\varepsilon})(Q)\lesssim\fint_{I_{\varepsilon}(Q)\times(0,11\varepsilon)}M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})(X)dX

Remark 4.14
.

All the proofs in this section remain valid if the domain is convex (or even strongly quasiconvex in the sense of

) instead of

. This is because, as we mentioned at the beginning of this section, the only ingredients we need from the geometry of domains are the

and the large-scale boundary

for the gradient estimate of Neumann problem, while the gradient estimate of Dirichlet problem is classical). Thus Theorems

hold for convex domains for any

5.
Full-scale estimates

In the previous sections, we dealt with bounded measurable coefficients with periodic structure and established various large-scale estimates. A natural question is that if we assume some regularity on the coefficients at small scale (i.e., in each periodic cell), can we combine the large-scale estimate established previously with the small-scale estimates to obtain the classical full-scale estimates? In this section, we will answer this question positively by proving the estimates of the full-scale nontangential maximal functions in Corollary
1.4
, which also yield the classical Rellich estimates and the reverse Hölder inequalities of
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
-harmonic measures.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

We only consider the regularity problems in Lipschitz or
C^{1}
domains. The estimates for Neumann problems are similar. The Dirichlet problem follows by a classical duality that (full-scale)
(R)_{p}
directly implies (full-scale)
(D)_{p^{\prime}}
; see
[
15
, Theorem 5.4]
.

We first recall the local (small-scale) regularity estimate in Lipschitz domains. Let
A(X)
be Hölder continuous. Let
u
be a weak solution of

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla u)=0\quad\text{in }T_{1},\\
&u=f\in C^{1}(I_{1})\quad\text{on }I_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then by
(R)_{p}
estimates for elliptic operators with
C^{\alpha}
coefficients and a localization technique (
[
17
, Theorem 8.1]
for the case
p=2
), we have, for any
1<p<2+\delta
,

(5.2)

\int_{I_{1/2}}|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u)|^{p}\lesssim\int_{I_{1}}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{p}d\sigma+\int_{T_{1}}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Now, let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of (
1.5
) in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
. We will first consider
(R)_{2}
estimate. Let
P\in\partial\Omega
. By a blow-up argument and (
5.2
), we have

(5.3)

\int_{\Delta_{\varepsilon}(P)}|\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{\Delta_{100\varepsilon}(P)}|\nabla_{\tan}f|^{2}d\sigma+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{D_{100\varepsilon}(P)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}d\sigma,

where
\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(F)(Q)
is the truncated nontangential maximal function defined on the boundary layer:

(5.4)

\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(F)(Q)=\sup\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B(X,\delta(X)/2)}|F(Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}:X\in\Gamma(Q)\cap\Omega_{10\varepsilon}\bigg\}.

Integrating (
5.3
) over
P\in\partial\Omega
, we have

(5.5)

\|\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{100\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Note that
\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\leq\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})+\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
and the large-scale estimate for
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})
has been given in Theorem
1.2
. It follows that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{100\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

where the last inequality follows from the large-scale Rellich estimate (
1.18
). Hence, we have proved the full-scale
L^{2}
regularity estimate.

Next, as before, the full-scale
L^{p}
regularity estimate for
1<p<2
follows from an interpolation between
L^{2}
estimate and
L^{1}
estimate with boundary data in Hardy space. While the estimate for
2<p<2+\delta
follows from the self-improving property of the reverse Hölder inequality. These are generally the consequences of the full-scale
(R)_{2}
estimates; see
[
15
, Theorem 5.2, 5.3]
(also see
[
15
, Theorem 6.2, 6.3]
for Neumann problem).

Finally, in
C^{1}
domains, (
5.2
) holds for all
2<p<\infty
; see Appendix
D
. By blow-up and covering arguments (as well as a small-scale
W^{1,p}
estimate), we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{N}^{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{100\varepsilon}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla_{\tan}f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{100\varepsilon}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p},

where the last term can be handled by Theorem
4.10
. As a result, the desired
L^{p}
estimate for
2<p<\infty
follows similarly as (
5.6
).
∎

Remark 5.1
.

It is a well-known fact, see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
, that the full-scale
(D)_{p}
estimate is equivalent to the reverse Hölder inequality of
k_{\varepsilon}=d\omega^{X_{0}}_{\varepsilon}/d\sigma
, i.e., for any
\Delta\subset\partial\Omega
,

(5.8)

\bigg(\fint_{\Delta}k_{\varepsilon}^{p^{\prime}}d\sigma\bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\lesssim\fint_{\Delta}k_{\varepsilon}d\sigma.

Also, the full-scale
(R)_{p}
and
(N)_{p}
estimates yield the classical Rellich estimates

(5.9)

\|\nabla_{\tan}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\simeq\Big\|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Therefore, these estimates, for the corresponding range of
p
, hold under the assumptions of Corollary
1.4
.

Appendix A
Basics in periodic homogenization

In this appendix we provide the basics of periodic homogenization for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon}
for the reader’s convenience, while all of them can be found in the monograph
[
21
]
.

Let
A=A(Y)
be a bounded measurable matrix defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Due to the peridoicity, we can view
A
as a function defined on the flat torus (a periodic cell)
\mathbb{T}^{d}=[0,1)^{d}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}=-\nabla\cdot(A\nabla)
be an elliptic operator defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The correctors
\{\chi_{j}:j=1,2,\cdots,d\}
are 1-periodic functions satisfying

(A.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{1}(\chi_{j})=-\mathcal{L}_{1}(e_{j}\cdot Y),\quad\text{for }Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{j}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The homogenized (constant) coefficients
\widehat{A}=(\widehat{a}_{ij})
are defined by

(A.2)

\widehat{a}_{ij}=\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(a_{ij}(Y)+a_{ik}(Y)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\chi_{j}(Y)\Big)dY,

where the repeated index
k
is summed from
1
to
d
. The homogenized matrix
\widehat{A}
also satisfies the ellitpicity condition with the same constant. The homogenized operator is given by
\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\nabla\cdot(\widehat{A}\nabla)
. If
A
is symmetric, then
\widehat{A}
is also symmetric.

The flux correctors are defined as follows. Let
f=(f_{ij})
be the matrix-valued 1-periodic solution of

(A.3)

\Delta f(Y)=A(Y)+A(Y)\nabla\chi(Y)-\widehat{A}.

The
H^{2}
regularity for the Laplace operator implies that
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. The flux correctors are defined by
\phi=\nabla\times f
, or in the component form

(A.4)

\phi_{kij}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}f_{ij}-\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}f_{kj}.

The key properties of
\phi
are

(A.5)

\phi_{kij}=-\phi_{ikj}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}\phi_{kij}=0.

The latter is due to (
A.3
) and the observation that
f
is divergence free, i.e.,
\partial f_{kj}/\partial y_{k}=0
. The properties in (
A.5
) are useful for the calculation of (
4.29
). In this paper, we only need to use the fact that
\chi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
in the proof of Lemma
4.8
.

Appendix B
Basic properties of average operator

and smoothing operator

We introduce a convenient notation for the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of an open set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}):=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\dist(X,\mathcal{O})<\varepsilon\}
.
In this paper, we use different versions of average operators, including
M_{\varepsilon},M_{\varepsilon}^{\star}
and
M_{\varepsilon}^{\partial}
. We will only prove several properties of
M_{\varepsilon}
, while similar properties also hold for other average operators.

Proposition B.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for any
X\in\mathcal{O},|B_{\varepsilon}(X)\cap\mathcal{O}|\gtrsim|B_{\varepsilon}(X)|
. Then for any locally
L^{2}
function
f
, and any
1\leq p\leq 2

(B.1)

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|M_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}

Moreover, for any
q\geq 2
,

(B.2)

\|M_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Proof.

We first prove (
B.1
). By the Hölder inequality and Fubini’s Theorem, for
1\leq p\leq 2
,

(B.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}M_{\varepsilon}(f)^{p}dX

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{O}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(X)}|f(Y)|^{p}dYdX

\displaystyle\geq|B_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O})}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|X-Y|<\varepsilon\}}|f(Y)|^{p}dXdY

\displaystyle\geq|B_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\mathcal{O}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(Y)||f(Y)|^{p}dY

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(Y)|^{p}dY,

where we have used the assumption
|B_{\varepsilon}(Y)\cap\mathcal{O}|\gtrsim|B_{\varepsilon}(Y)|
for
Y\in\mathcal{O}
.

To prove (
B.2
), we use the Hölder inequality and Fubini’s Theorem again,

(B.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}|M_{\varepsilon}(f)|^{q}dX

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{O}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(X)}|f(Y)|^{q}dYdX

\displaystyle\leq|B_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O})}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|X-Y|<\varepsilon\}}|f(Y)|^{q}dXdY

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O})}|f(Y)|^{q}dXdY.

This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition B.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then for any
s\lesssim\varepsilon
,

(B.5)

M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X)\lesssim\fint_{B_{s}(X)}M_{\varepsilon}(f),

and

(B.6)

M_{s}(M_{\varepsilon}(f))(X)\lesssim M_{\varepsilon+s}(f)(X)\lesssim M_{2s}(M_{\varepsilon}(f))(X).

Proof.

Let
X=0
. Without loss of generality, assume
s<\varepsilon/10
. For any
\theta\in(0,1/2)
, there exists a cone
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:1-\theta<\frac{X}{|X|}\cdot v\leq 1\}
for some
v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

(B.7)

M_{\varepsilon}(f)(0)=\bigg(\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}|f(Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}\simeq\bigg(|B_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}|f(Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}.

Now, observe that if
\theta
is small enough and
s<\varepsilon/10
, then for any
X\in B_{s}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,
B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(X)
. This and (
B.7
) imply that for each
X\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
,

(B.8)

\displaystyle M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(X)}|f(Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(|B_{\varepsilon}|^{-1}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}|f(Y)|^{2}dY\bigg)^{1/2}\simeq M_{\varepsilon}(f)(0).

It follows that

(B.9)

M^{\varepsilon}(f)(0)\lesssim\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\mathcal{C}_{\theta}}M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X)dX\lesssim\fint_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X)dX.

This proves (
B.5
).

The first inequality in (
B.6
) follows from the simple fact that for each
Y\in B_{s}(X)
,
M_{\varepsilon}(f)(Y)\lesssim M_{\varepsilon+s}(f)(X)
. Taking
L^{2}
average of
M_{\varepsilon}(f)(Y)
over
B_{s}(X)
, we get the desired estimate. For the second inequality in (
B.6
), we apply Fubini’s Theorem as follows (assume
X=0
for simplicity),

(B.10)

\displaystyle M_{2s}(M_{\varepsilon}(f))(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{2s}||B_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{2s}(0)}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(Y)}|f(Z)|^{2}dZdY

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|B_{2s}||B_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\varepsilon+2s}(0)}|f(Y)|^{2}\int_{B_{2s}(0)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Y-Z|<\varepsilon\}}dZdY.

Now, the key observation is that for
Y\in B_{\varepsilon+s}(0)
, we have

(B.11)

\int_{B_{2s}(0)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{|Y-Z|<\varepsilon\}}dZ=|B_{2s}(0)\cap B_{\varepsilon}(Y)|\gtrsim|B_{s}(0)|.

It follows that

(B.12)

M_{2s}(M_{\varepsilon}(f))(0)\gtrsim\frac{1}{|B_{\varepsilon}|}\int_{B_{\varepsilon+s}(0)}|f(Y)|^{2}dY\simeq M_{\varepsilon+s}(f)(0),

as desired.
∎

Proposition B.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then given
K\geq 1
,

(B.13)

\|M_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|M_{K\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|M_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(B_{K\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))},

where the implicit constant depends on
d
and
K
.

Proof.

The first inequality is due to the observation

(B.14)

M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X)\lesssim M_{K\varepsilon}(f)(X)\quad\text{for any }X\in\mathcal{O}.

To see the second, we use the following covering property: there exists
Z_{1},Z_{2},\cdots,Z_{m}\in B_{K\varepsilon}(0)
with
m
depending only on
d
and
K
such that

(B.15)

B_{K\varepsilon}(0)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}B_{\varepsilon}(Z_{i}).

This implies that

(B.16)

M_{K\varepsilon}(f)(X)\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{m}M_{\varepsilon}(f)(X+Z_{i}).

Taking
L^{p}
norm on both sides in
X
over
\mathcal{O}
, we obtain the second inequality in (
B.13
).
∎

Define the large-scale Hardy-Littlewood maximal function by

(B.17)

\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(f)(X)=\sup\bigg\{\fint_{B_{r}}|f|:X\in B_{r},r>\varepsilon\bigg\}.

Proposition B.4
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
1<p\leq\infty
. Then

(B.18)

\|\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|M_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Suppose
r>\varepsilon
. Then for any
X\in B_{r}
, then
|B_{r}\cap B_{\varepsilon}(X)|\simeq|B_{\varepsilon}(X)|
. By Proposition
B.1
with
p=1
,

(B.19)

\fint_{B_{r}}|f|\lesssim\fint_{B_{r}}M_{\varepsilon}(f).

Thus, by the definition (
B.17
), we have
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}(f)(X)\lesssim\mathcal{M}(M_{\varepsilon}f)(X)
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Thus, it follows from the
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}
that (
B.18
) holds for any
1<p\leq\infty
.
∎

Recall that the smoothing operator is defined in (
1.23
). We have the following properties.

Proposition B.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set and
f
be a
W^{1,p}
function defined in
B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O})
. Then for
1\leq p<\infty
,

(B.20)

\|K_{\varepsilon}(f)-f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Proof.

Write, for
X\in\mathcal{O}
,

(B.21)

K_{\varepsilon}f(X)-f(X)=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\psi_{\varepsilon}(Y)\int_{0}^{1}\nabla f(X-sY)\cdot YdsdY.

By the Minkowski inequality,

(B.22)

\displaystyle\|K_{\varepsilon}f(\cdot)-f(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(0)}\psi_{\varepsilon}(Y)\int_{0}^{1}\|\nabla f(\cdot-sY)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}dsdY

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition B.6

(
[
21
, Proposition 3.1.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set and
f
be a function defined in
B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O})
. Let
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be a 1-periodic function. Then for any
1\leq p<\infty
,

(B.23)

\|g(X/\varepsilon)K_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Proposition B.7
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set and
f
be a function defined in
B_{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}):=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\dist(X,\mathcal{O})<\varepsilon\}
. Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
be a 1-periodic function. Then for any
2\leq p<\infty
,

(B.24)

\|M_{\varepsilon}(g(X/\varepsilon)K_{\varepsilon}(f))\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

Proof.

Define
T_{\varepsilon}f=M_{\varepsilon}(g(X/\varepsilon)K_{\varepsilon}(f))
. Clearly,
T_{\varepsilon}
is a sublinear operator. We prove the theorem by considering
p=2
and
p=\infty
, and then apply the real interpolation theorem.
For
p=2
, we have

(B.25)

\|T_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|g(X/\varepsilon)K_{\varepsilon}(f)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

For

,

(B.26)

\|T_{\varepsilon}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{O})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}\|M_{\varepsilon}(g(X/\varepsilon))\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2\varepsilon}(\mathcal{O}))}.

The desired estimate follows from Marcinkiewicz interpolation Theorem.
∎

Appendix C
The real-variable argument

The real-variable argument (originating from
[
4
]
) is built upon the following theorem taken from
[
21
, Theorem 4.2.3]
.

Theorem C.1
.

Let
B_{0}
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
F\in L^{2}(4B_{0})
. Let
q>2
and
f\in L^{p}(4B_{0})
for some
2<p<q
. Suppose that for each ball
B\subset 2B_{0}
with
|B|\leq c_{1}|B_{0}|
, there exist two measurable functions
F_{B}
and
R_{B}
on
2B
such that
|F|\leq|F_{B}|+|R_{B}|
on
2B
, and

(C.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bigg(\fint_{2B}|F_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq N_{1}\sup_{4B_{0}\supset B^{\prime}\supset B}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\\
&\bigg(\fint_{2B}|R_{B}|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\leq N_{2}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{\beta B}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\sup_{4B_{0}\supset B^{\prime}\supset B}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\},\end{aligned}\right.

where
N_{1},N_{2}>0,0<c_{1}<1
and
\beta>2
. Then
F\in L^{p}(B_{0})
and

(C.2)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
q
,
N_{1}
,
N_{2},c_{1}
, and
\beta
.

In this paper, we actually need a large-scale version of the real-variable argument, in which the assumption (
C.1
) holds only above a certain scale. In this case, we only have large-scale estimates above this particular scale. The following theorem or its similar versions also appears in
[
22
, Remark 4.2]
and
[
23
, Theorem 2.6]
.

Theorem C.2
.

Let
B_{0}
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
F\in L^{2}(4B_{0})
. Let
q>2
and
f\in L^{2}(4B_{0})
for some
2<p<q
. Suppose that there exists
t>0
such that for each ball
B\subset 2B_{0}
with
|B|\leq c_{1}|B_{0}|
and
\diam(B)>2t
, there exist two measurable functions
F_{B}
and
R_{B}
on
2B
such that
|F|\leq|F_{B}|+|R_{B}|
on
2B
, and (
C.1
) holds. Then
M_{t}(F)\in L^{p}(B_{0})
and

(C.3)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|M_{t}(F)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|F|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|M_{t}(f)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
p
,
q
,
N_{1}
,
N_{2},c_{1}
, and
\beta
.

Proof.

The proof is simple, using Theorem
C.1
. Actually, let
\overline{F}=M_{t}(F)
. Then
\overline{F}
satisfies
\overline{F}\leq M_{t}(F_{B})+M_{t}(R_{B})
and the assumption (
C.1
) with
F_{B},R_{B}
and
f
replaced by
M_{t}(F_{B}),M_{t}(R_{B})
and
M_{t}(f)
for any
B
with arbitrarily small radius, namely

(C.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bigg(\fint_{2B}|M_{t}(F_{B})|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\sup_{4B_{0}\supset B^{\prime}\supset B}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|M_{t}(f)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},\\
&\bigg(\fint_{2B}|M_{t}(R_{B})|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{\beta B}|\overline{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\sup_{4B_{0}\supset B^{\prime}\supset B}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|M_{t}(f)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\}.\end{aligned}\right.

In fact, if for
B=B_{s}
and
s>t
, then (
C.1
) directly implies (
C.4
), due to the comparison of the
L^{2}
norm of
F
and
M_{t}(F)
in Proposition
B.1
. For
s<t
, thanks to the first inequality in (
B.6
), we have

(C.5)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{2s}}|M_{t}(F_{B})|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{B_{t+2s}}|F_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Now
\diam(B_{t+2s})=2(t+2s)>2t
and we can apply the first condition in (
C.1
) at this scale and the second inequality in (
B.6
) to get

(C.6)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{2s}}|M_{t}(F_{B})|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\sup_{4B_{0}\supset B^{\prime}\supset B_{s}}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|M_{t}(f)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

This is a large-scale version of the first condition of (
C.1
).
Similarly, a large-scale version of the second condition in (
C.1
) is valid for
M_{t}(R_{B})
. Thus, applying Theorem
C.1
to
\overline{F}=M_{t}(F)
, we get (
C.3
). Note that we changed
M_{t}(F)
back to
F
on the right-hand side of (
C.3
) by (
B.2
).
∎

Appendix D
Regular elliptic operators in
C^{1}
domains

Consider the elliptic equation

\mathcal{L}_{0}(u)=0
in a bounded domain
\Omega
, where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is a second-order elliptic operator with constant and symmetric coefficients.
If
\Omega
is
C^{1}
and
1<p<\infty
, it was proved in
[
10
]
by the method of layer potentials that the Dirichlet problem,

(D.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{0}(u)=0\ \ \text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=f\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\ \text{ on }\partial\Omega\text{ and }N(u)\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\end{aligned}\right.

is solvable, and that the solution satisfies the estimate,

(D.2)

\|N(u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Moreover, if
f\in W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)
, then

(D.3)

\|N(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\partial\Omega)}.

Furthermore, if
\int_{\partial\Omega}gd\sigma=0
, the Neumann problem,

(D.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{L}_{0}(u)=0\ \ \text{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{0}}=g\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\ \text{ on }\partial\Omega\text{ and }N(\nabla u)\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)\end{aligned}\right.

is solvable for
1<p<\infty
, and
the solution satisfies

(D.5)

\|N(\nabla u)\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

We point out that the results above also follow from some general results in

[
8
,
