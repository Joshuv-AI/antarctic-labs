Executive Summary of CRADA Work:
 • Visolis is developing a commercial process for the bioproduction of a chemical
 intermediate, towards the derivative production of a portfolio of bio-based chemicals with
 large application potential, from drop-in commodity chemicals, to innovative specialty
 chemical offerings, to materials for multiple end markets and industries, which will
 ultimately benefit consumers and the public. Visolis already has several variants of a
 microbe that produce the chemical intermediate at distinct levels (i.e. high, medium, and

 low). They are currently pursuing transcriptomics analysis for some of these strains,
 towards a better understanding of the biology behind what makes certain strains perform
 better than others. There exist genome-scale library approaches for the microbe that
 could be used to screen gene disruption, overexpression, or repression candidates for
 perturbations to the production of the chemical intermediate. These approaches often
 reveal opportunities for further production improvement that are not otherwise accessible
 using hypothesis-driven metabolic engineering approaches. However, Visolis, while it
 could generate or procure such genome-scale libraries, does not have the automated
 strain engineering workflows required to screen thousands of variants, obtaining a
 production phenotype and a genotype for each. The purpose of this collaboration is to
 use LBNL and SNL capabilities to enable Visolis to effectively screen thousands of
 genome-scale library strain variants for phenotype/genotype relationships that will
 complement Visolis’ transcriptomic investigations.
 • The technical objectives of this work are to: 1) further develop the existing automated
 strain engineering workflow capabilities at LBNL and SNL, to additionally support the
 microbe chosen by Visolis, and in particular to the production of the chemical
 intermediate; and 2) to apply the developed workflow to screen thousands of colonies of
 genome-scale library variants for the production of the chemical intermediate, (a subset
 thereof for) proteomics, and genotyping.

Summary of Research Results:
 • Kickoff
 o A joint project kickoff meeting between Visolis, LBNL, and SNL participants was
 conducted. Work plan details were confirmed, and clarified and coordinated as
 needed. Visolis sent LBNL/SNL three existing strains that produce the chemical
 intermediate at high, medium, low levels.
 • Extend Workflows to the microbe chosen by Visolis
 o Research and development was performed to extend existing automated strain
 engineering workflows as needed to the microbe chosen by Visolis and to the
 production of the chemical intermediate. This included development and
 validation of the RapidFire tandem mass spectrometry (RF-MS-MS) method for
 the chemical intermediate (including sample prep).
 o We used microbial strains with known production titers (low, medium, high levels)
 of the target chemical to evaluate the high throughput culturing and analysis
 conditions. A few variations in culturing parameters were tested to find an
 optimized condition for the library screen. The results showed that, with the
 optimized condition, the automation workflow was able to distinguish different
 strains producing the target chemicals at low, medium, and high levels. However,
 the workflow could hardly distinguish slight titer differences among strains within
 the high producer group (Figure 1). The results also show that Coefficient of
 Variation across technical replicates of the same sample are mostly around 10%
 and below 20% (Figure 2).

 • Procure Libraries
 o Libraries of the microbe were obtained for prospective evaluation of chemical
 intermediate production, using existing Visolis base strain(s). These libraries
 were provided by Visolis to LBNL/SNL. The original scope of work proposed two
 full rounds of procure / screen / confirm. However, due to time and resource
 constraints, only one round was completed which impacted the subsequent
 screening and confirmation tasks below. The workflow for this project with Visolis
 involved several adjustments from previously established approaches. Additional
 developments and adjustments were required beyond the standard adaptations
 for a new microbe and chemical intermediate.
 § In earlier workflows, collaborators would provide the library as a pooled
 sample in a single tube. For this project, mutants were supplied as individual
 strains, each requiring separate plating. While this approach allowed direct
 handling of each strain, it also increased the workload. Plating requirements
 were approximately ten times higher and colony picking about three times
 higher. Given the project’s screening capacity, it was not feasible to process
 all biological replicates for each variant. To address this, hit-picking methods
 were developed to selectively cultivate a limited number of replicates from
 each mutant strain. Additionally, extensive preliminary tests (3 rounds) were
 required to optimize the plating concentration due to variation in the mutant
 strain density and growth differences. An example of the Qtray plating of the
 arrayed mutant library under these optimized plating conditions is shown in
 Figure 3.

Figure 3. An example of the Qtray plating of the arrayed mutant library. The 12 wells with green
highlights are control samples, which are left blank and plated on separate plates. The other wells contain
mutant strains. Yellow dots designate pickable colonies; red dots designate unpickable colonies; white
dot areas designate un-recognizable colonies by automation.

 § Secondly, changes in the input library type required development of a
 series of new automation workflows and programming scripts. The newly
 developed automation workflows include: i) high throughput Qtray plating
 (from 2 Qtrays to 6 Qtrays in a batch, increasing plating efficiency by over
 40%); ii) colony picking for arrayed libraries; iii) high throughput batch
 feeding; and iv) series dilution for sample preparation for RF-MS-MS
 analysis. The existing automation programs, such as the hit-pick, seed
 culture inoculation, production culture inoculation programs were adjusted
 to accommodate the needs of the target microbe.The newly developed
 programming scripts include i) automated hit-pick instruction for selecting
 3 out of 10 biological replicates for screening; ii) sample mapping
 connecting samples from the initial input mutant library, to hit-picked
 selected mutant plate, to QPix colony picking map, and to output culture
 plate; and iii) high-titer mutant selection based on RF-MS-MS analysis
 results.
 § Thirdly, the specific species in the project required batch feeding twice a
 day for continuous seven or more days, which requires extended labor
 time not in the initial planning.
 o Accommodating these issues consumed time and resources that would have
 otherwise been directed towards a second round of procure / screen / confirm.

• Screen
 o A total of 2,149 mutant samples (representing 998 mutant strains) were plated on
 outgrowth plates. 1,553 (representing 762 mutant strains) out of the 2,149
 samples resulted in single colonies, which were picked from outgrowth plates
 and cultured in parallel with two positive control strains in each screening plate.
 All samples in the screening plates were processed and analyzed for the titer of
 the chemical of interest by RF-MS-MS.
 o As a quality control step, Coefficient of Variation of the chemical titer from
 biological replicates in the same screening plates was analyzed for the two
 positive control strains (PC1 and PC2). Both PC1 and PC2 represent high
 producer strains for the target chemical from the preliminary results. PC1 showed
 less than 10% in CV for all screening plates except for one plate with a CV of
 16%, thus indicating good consistency in biological replicate titers; biological
 replicates for PC2 showed larger variation in titer, up to 26% (Figure 4). PC1 but
 not PC2 is used as the reference strain for positive candidate selection.

 Figure 4. Coefficient of Variation of biological replicates of the two positive
 control strains (PC1 and PC2) in all screening plates.

 o Based on RF-MS-MS analysis results, a total of 23 mutant strains were selected
 as positive candidates for further analysis for target chemical production. A
 candidate strain is identified if the average chemical titer from its biological
 replicates shows a 10% or more increase compared to average PC1 titer in the
 same screening plate. The titer information is listed in Table 1.
