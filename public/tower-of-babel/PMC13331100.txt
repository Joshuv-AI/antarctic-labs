Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrosing lung disease with a poor prognosis.1 An estimated 5%–15% of patients with IPF experience an acute exacerbation (AE-IPF) within 1 year,2 a life-threatening event with an in-hospital mortality that often exceeds 50%.3 4 Despite the availability of several therapeutic options for AE-IPF, none have been definitively proven effective in clinical trials, highlighting an urgent need for a validated treatment strategy.5 6

Systemic corticosteroids represent the most frequently employed therapeutic strategy for AE-IPF. While their efficacy has not been confirmed in randomised controlled trials (RCTs), international guidelines provide a conditional recommendation for their use,7 and they are commonly used in clinical practice. A common approach to administration involves an initial high-dose treatment, followed by a gradual dose reduction over several weeks or months.5

Despite this widespread acceptance, a significant gap in knowledge exists regarding the optimal corticosteroid administration strategy. Specifically, there is no established consensus on the tapering regimen for patients with AE-IPF, and prominent international guidelines do not provide specific recommendations on the method or duration of dose reduction.7 This lack of guidance creates a clinical challenge, as prolonged high-dose therapy increases the risk of serious adverse events (SAEs). While early tapering has proven beneficial in other inflammatory conditions by mitigating steroid-related toxicity without compromising outcomes,8 9 its efficacy and safety in the context of AE-IPF remain uninvestigated. Although our previous retrospective cohort study suggested a potential survival benefit with an early tapering approach,10 the limitations of observational data, such as confounding by indication and variability in treatment regimens, necessitate validation with a prospective, RCT.

This study, the RApid PrednIsolone Dose reduction for acute exacerbation of Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (RAPID-IPF) trial, aims to assess whether a rapid (6 weeks) prednisolone tapering regimen is non-inferior to a conventional (12 weeks) tapering regimen in terms of 90-day all-cause mortality in patients with AE-IPF. We hypothesise that the early tapering regimen will be non-inferior to the conventional regimen for 90-day mortality, while potentially reducing cumulative steroid exposure and related adverse events.

Trial design

The RAPID-IPF trial is a multi-centre, parallel-group, active-controlled, open-label, RCT designed to assess the non-inferiority of a rapid (6 weeks) prednisolone tapering regimen compared with a conventional (12 weeks) tapering regimen in patients with AE-IPF. The 12-week conventional tapering regimen was chosen as the comparator. This duration reflects current clinical practice, as a large international survey reported that the average duration of corticosteroid treatment for AE-IPF was 13 weeks.5 Based on this evidence and expert consensus within the study group, a 12-week regimen was established as a representative conventional treatment strategy.

Eligible patients will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to either the rapid tapering group or the standard tapering group.

The primary framework of the trial is non-inferiority, aiming to demonstrate that the 90-day all-cause mortality in the rapid tapering group is not unacceptably worse than that in the standard tapering group, with a prespecified non-inferiority margin of 10% for the risk difference. In the context of AE-IPF, where placebo-controlled trials are lacking to establish the effect of current empirical therapy, defining this margin is particularly challenging. Our margin of 10 percentage points was established through a consensus-based approach informed by data from a large international survey suggesting a high mortality even with current empirical clinical practice5 and was determined by expert consensus among the investigators. This margin is further supported by evidence from the literature demonstrating that a 10% non-inferiority margin for mortality outcomes falls within the range used in trials involving other high-mortality conditions.12,15 It balances the substantial potential benefits of the early tapering regimen—namely, a considerable reduction in cumulative steroid exposure and associated adverse events—against a modest and clinically acceptable degree of uncertainty regarding mortality. The trial incorporates a two-stage design: an initial stage to assess feasibility, followed by the main non-inferiority comparison.

Trial setting

This is a multicentre trial that will be conducted in tertiary care hospitals across Japan. Initially, patients will be recruited from Saiseikai Kumamoto Hospital, Kobe City Medical Center General Hospital and Iizuka Hospital. Additional participating sites, which will be tertiary care hospitals or specialised respiratory centres, will be added during the trial if interim analyses indicate that the trial is feasible to continue and expand, or if patient recruitment is slower than anticipated, to ensure an adequate sample size.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

In brief, this trial will enrol patients aged 40 years or older who are hospitalised for an AE-IPF and have initiated corticosteroid pulse therapy. Key exclusion criteria include exacerbations primarily due to other causes (eg, infection or heart failure), coexisting active malignancy and current mechanical ventilation. Interventions

Eligible participants will be randomly assigned to receive prednisolone according to either an accelerated 6-week tapering schedule (rapid tapering group) or a conventional 12-week tapering schedule (standard tapering group). Both interventions will be initiated following corticosteroid pulse therapy for AE-IPF. The initial daily dose of oral prednisolone will be 1.0 mg/kg of actual body weight for both groups, starting the day after the completion of pulse therapy. The subsequent tapering schedule for each group is detailed in table 1. After completion of the protocol-specified treatment period as detailed in table 1, prednisolone therapy can be either modified (eg, further tapered or maintained) or discontinued at the discretion of the attending physician at each participating site (local clinical practice). However, if there is no deterioration, the steroid dose should not be increased. The initial dose of 1 mg/kg/day was selected as a pragmatic choice reflecting one commonly used regimen in AE-IPF and prior trial precedent, including a published randomised trial (EXAFIP)16 and an ongoing trial (EXAFIP2; NCT05674994), rather than as the single predominant standard of care.

Administration and flexibility

Prednisolone will be administered systemically (eg, orally, intravenously), with the specific route and method at the discretion of the attending physician. Treatment will be initiated in an inpatient setting but may be transitioned to an outpatient basis on clinical improvement.

The protocol allows for a degree of flexibility in the administration of prednisolone to accommodate real-world clinical practice:

Dosage: minor variations from the prescribed daily dose are permissible, within ±10% of the calculated dose or ±5 mg, whichever is larger.

Duration: the duration of each tapering step may be adjusted within the specific day ranges indicated in parentheses for each step in table 1 (eg, to accommodate weekends or patient convenience).

All treatment decisions, including adherence to the allocated tapering schedule and management after the protocol-defined treatment period, will be made by the attending physicians at each participating site. No specific intervention manual beyond this protocol is provided.

Criteria for discontinuation of protocol-specified prednisolone therapy

The criteria under which protocol-specified prednisolone therapy will be discontinued are detailed in online supplemental additional file 1. Subsequent treatment will be at the discretion of the attending physician.

Adherence to intervention

No specific strategies to improve adherence to the allocated prednisolone regimens beyond routine clinical care are planned, as the initial treatment phase is predominantly hospital-based under direct medical supervision. Adherence will be assessed based on medication administration records during hospitalisation and investigator assessment of compliance with the tapering schedule. Any application of the protocol-defined flexibility (eg, dose or duration adjustments) as well as any other deviations from the protocol-specified treatment will be documented, along with the reasons for each instance.

Concomitant care

The use of concomitant medications to prevent potential steroid-related adverse events is strongly recommended. Standard medical care is permitted unless explicitly prohibited. A detailed list of prohibited medications and therapies is provided in online supplemental additional file 1.

For the purposes of this trial, disease worsening is defined as either an increase in oxygen requirement at rest or an increase in the dose or prolongation of corticosteroid therapy due to investigator-judged relapse of AE-IPF. Safety reporting

All SAEs must be reported promptly by the site investigator to the principal investigator/study secretariat for their awareness. The principal investigator will be responsible for reporting all other SAEs to the head of each research institution and the respective institutional review boards in accordance with institutional procedures and the applicable ethical guidelines.

Sample size calculation

The required sample size was calculated for the primary outcome of 90-day all-cause mortality. Based on a non-inferiority design with 80% power, a one-sided significance level of 0.2 and a 10% risk difference margin, a total of 64 participants (32 per group) are required. To account for potential dropouts or exclusions from the analysis, the target enrolment is set at 70 participants (35 per group). The detailed assumptions and justifications for these parameters are provided in online supplemental additional file 1.

Interim analyses

The trial incorporates two planned interim analyses. First, an interim analysis for feasibility will be conducted after the first 30 participants have completed their 90-day follow-up. This analysis will assess the proportion of loss to follow-up and protocol adherence. The threshold of 20% for loss to follow-up is based on established evidence that losses exceeding this level pose serious threats to trial validity.17 The threshold of 70% for protocol adherence was determined based on the collective clinical expertise and judgement of the research group, in accordance with the general framework for feasibility progression criteria.18 19 If the proportion of loss to follow-up exceeds 20% or the proportion of protocol adherence is below 70%, the trial may be discontinued or the protocol may be revised.

Second, an adaptive interim analysis for sample size re-estimation will be performed once 90-day primary outcome data are available for 60 participants. Following the method described by Uemura et al,20 the conditional power will be calculated based on the observed risk difference.

If the conditional power is less than 20%, the trial will be considered for early termination due to futility or will continue to the initially planned sample size of 70.

If the conditional power is 20% or greater, the sample size will be recalculated based on the interim data and enrolment will continue up to the revised target. The maximum total sample size is capped at 130 participants.

Recruitment

To achieve the target sample size, potentially eligible patients will be identified by screening all individuals hospitalised for AE-IPF at the participating centres. Attending physicians or study staff at each site will approach identified patients or their legally acceptable representatives to provide study information and obtain informed consent.

The primary strategy to ensure adequate and timely enrolment is the expansion of the trial network; additional collaborating hospitals will be invited to join if the recruitment rate is slower than anticipated. The study secretariat will regularly monitor enrolment progress to manage this process.

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the rapid or standard tapering group. Randomisation will be performed centrally at the JORTC Data Center using a computer-generated minimisation method. The allocation will be stratified by two factors: the use of anti-fibrotic medication (yes vs no) and long-term home oxygen therapy (yes vs no) at baseline.

Site investigators will enrol participants and receive the treatment allocation via a secure, web-based system, ensuring allocation concealment.

This is a fully open-label trial, and no personnel, including participants, investigators, outcome assessors or data analysts, will be blinded to treatment allocation.

Data collection and management

Data for all participants will be collected by site investigators or designated study staff and recorded in a centralised, secure, web-based eCRF system developed and maintained by the JORTC Data Center. The schedule of all assessments is detailed in table 2. While all required data can be entered manually, prespecified data such as laboratory results and medication records may also be extracted directly from the electronic health record systems of participating hospitals, where feasible, to supplement direct data entry and reduce administrative burden. In the event of discontinuation from the allocated intervention, participants will be encouraged to continue follow-up for key outcome assessments, particularly survival status, unless consent is fully withdrawn.

Data management will be conducted by the JORTC Data Centre in accordance with applicable national ethical guidelines and the principles of Good Clinical Practice. A separate, detailed data management plan will be developed prior to the trial. The eCRF system will have built-in data validation checks (eg, range checks), and a query management process will be used to ensure data quality. Access to all trial data will be restricted to authorised personnel. Participant data will be de-identified using a unique study ID, and all data will be stored securely and encrypted at the data centre. Essential trial documents will be archived for a minimum of 10 years after publication.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed by the study statistician according to the prespecified SAP. The main principles of the analysis are outlined here. All efficacy analyses will be conducted on the intention-to-treat population, which includes all randomised participants, analysed in the group to which they were originally assigned, regardless of adherence to the intervention. To assess the impact of protocol deviations, an adherence-adjusted analysis using inverse probability weighting will be conducted as a sensitivity analysis, with details specified in the SAP. A safety population, consisting of all participants who received at least one dose of prednisolone, will be used for the analysis of safety data.

Primary outcome analysis

The primary outcome, 90-day all-cause mortality, will be compared between the two groups. The proportion of deceased patients and its 95% CI in each group will be calculated using the Kaplan-Meier method, with the CI estimated using Greenwood’s formula. To test for non-inferiority, the risk difference between the groups will be assessed using a Wald test with a prespecified non-inferiority margin of 10 percentage points (0.1) and a one-sided significance level of 0.2.

Secondary outcome analyses

All secondary outcomes will be analysed on an exploratory basis to estimate treatment effects, without a formal hypothesis testing for statistical significance.

Sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the impact of the findings under different assumptions about the missing data mechanism.

Monitoring

An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will not be established for this trial. A DMC was considered unnecessary given that (1) the trial investigates different tapering strategies of a widely used medication (prednisolone) rather than a novel investigational product, (2) the trial has an open-label design, allowing for continuous safety monitoring by site investigators and (3) the primary outcome is short-term (90 days).

Trial monitoring will be conducted centrally by the JORTC Data Center following a prespecified monitoring plan. Any significant safety signals or data integrity issues identified will be communicated promptly to the principal investigator and the study statistician for review and action. No on-site monitoring or independent auditing is planned for this trial. The principal investigator reserves the right to terminate the trial at any time due to safety concerns, significant recruitment difficulties or compelling external evidence.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and public participants were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research. This was an investigator-initiated trial focused on a specific clinical question regarding treatment strategy, and formal mechanisms for patient and public involvement were not in place at the time of study design. Future trials should consider incorporating patient perspectives in the study design.

Discussion

The RAPID-IPF trial is the first prospective RCT designed to investigate the optimal tapering strategy for corticosteroids in patients with AE-IPF. Given the exploratory nature of this trial, the findings should be regarded as preliminary evidence. If the hypothesis of non-inferiority for the rapid (6 weeks) tapering regimen is confirmed, the results would provide important preliminary evidence to inform clinical practice and to support the design of future confirmatory trials, potentially enabling a shorter treatment duration with reduced cumulative corticosteroid exposure, without compromising survival. This would represent a meaningful step towards establishing a safer and more patient-centred management approach for this devastating condition.

A recent systematic review reported inconsistent findings regarding corticosteroid use in AE-IPF, with some observational studies suggesting a possible association between higher doses and increased mortality.21 However, this evidence is subject to substantial confounding by indication, as more severely ill patients are more likely to receive higher doses. The review itself concluded that randomised evidence is needed, and the RAPID-IPF trial contributes to this effort.

Strength and limitation

This trial has several strengths. The randomised, controlled design minimises selection bias and confounding inherent in previous observational studies. The multicentre approach enhances generalisability. Furthermore, the adaptive sample size re-estimation ensures adequate power despite uncertainty in the treatment effect.

However, we acknowledge several potential limitations. First, the open-label design may introduce bias. Its impact on mortality is likely minimal; however, subjective secondary outcomes—such as the decision to escalate corticosteroid therapy, dyspnoea scales and the Clinical Frailty Scale—may be more susceptible. Predefined criteria for disease worsening are specified to minimise subjectivity, but this potential for bias should be acknowledged. Second, a key methodological consideration is the choice of a one-sided significance level of 0.2 for the primary non-inferiority analysis. Given the complete absence of randomised evidence for corticosteroid tapering strategies in AE-IPF, this trial is more appropriately characterised as a phase II-type randomised trial aimed at generating preliminary evidence to inform future confirmatory research. The use of a one-sided α of 0.2 is consistent with recommendations for such exploratory designs.22 23 Nevertheless, this threshold increases the risk of a type I error. Furthermore, the combination of these design features—a permissive α, a 10% non-inferiority margin, adaptive sample size re-estimation, open-label design and absence of a DMC—may increase the risk of falsely reassuring conclusions, particularly given the small sample size. Accordingly, if non-inferiority is demonstrated, a finding should be interpreted as preliminary evidence to guide clinical practice and to inform the design of future confirmatory trials, rather than as definitive proof of non-inferiority. Third, recruitment is restricted to tertiary care centres in Japan, which may limit generalisability. Corticosteroid tapering practices, patient demographics and supportive care standards may differ internationally, and the 12-week comparator regimen may not represent approaches used in other regions. Fourth, missing data for certain secondary outcomes is anticipated, and our statistical approach, including the use of trimmed means, is designed to mitigate its impact, though interpretation will require care. Additionally, this trial was designed without formal patient and public involvement (PPI). Patient engagement in trial design is increasingly recognised as important for ensuring that research questions, outcome measures and trial procedures reflect the priorities and experiences of those most affected. The absence of PPI may have limited the patient-centredness of the trial design, including outcome selection and procedural acceptability. Finally, this trial is designed to compare two corticosteroid tapering strategies and does not address the broader question of whether corticosteroid tapering confers any benefit over no corticosteroid treatment following pulse therapy. This question would require a separate RCT with an appropriate comparator arm to address. More broadly, the efficacy of corticosteroids in AE-IPF itself remains unproven, and a placebo-controlled trial would represent the most rigorous approach to evaluating this question. However, recruitment to such a trial may be particularly challenging in AE-IPF. Corticosteroid use has become standard practice at many centres, and clinicians and patients confronted with this life-threatening condition may be reluctant to participate in a placebo-controlled trial.24 An ongoing placebo-controlled trial (EXAFIP2; NCT05674994) may help address this question.

Conclusion

The RAPID-IPF trial is designed to generate preliminary prospective evidence on the optimal corticosteroid tapering strategy for patients with AE-IPF and to inform the design of future confirmatory studies.
