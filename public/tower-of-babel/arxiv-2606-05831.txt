Abstract
. The skew ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n , 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r ) (\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r}) Howe duality states that the exterior algebra Λ ⁡ ( ℂ n ​ r ) \Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr}) admits a multiplicity-free decomposition under the natural actions of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n × 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r \mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r} . In this paper, by using certain Lagrange interpolation polynomials of degree r − 1 r-1 , we extend the action of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} on Λ ⁡ ( ℂ n ​ r ) \Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr}) to its loop algebra L ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) L(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}) . View Λ ⁡ ( ℂ n ​ r ) \Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr}) as a module for the loop algebra L ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ) L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}) of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{sl}_{n} by taking restriction. We prove that every highest weight vector of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n × 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r \mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r} in Λ ⁡ ( ℂ n ​ r ) \Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr}) generates a local Weyl module of L ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ) L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}) . Furthermore, we obtain in this way an explicit realization of all local Weyl modules for L ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 n ) L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}) .

1.
Introduction

Motivated by the study of finite-dimensional modules of quantum affine algebras, Chari and Pressley introduced in
[
CP
]

the notion of local Weyl modules for loop algebras. They conjectured therein that local Weyl modules are the classical limit of standard irreducible modules for quantum affine algebras, and further showed that this conjecture would follow from an additional conjecture concerning the dimensions of local Weyl modules.
The dimension conjecture has been proved via algebraic methods in
[
CP
,
CL
,
FL
]
for the simply laced case and in
[
N
]
for the non-simply laced case. These works also revealed close connections between local Weyl modules and several other important classes of modules, including Demazure modules and Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules.
As pointed out by Nakajima, the dimension conjecture can also be deduced from some deep geometric results in
[
BN
]
and
[
K
]
.

The notion of (local and global) Weyl modules has since been extended to various other interesting algebras, such as current Lie algebras
[
CFK
,
FL
,
N
]
, twisted loop algebras
[
CFS
]
, toroidal Lie algebras
[
MPS
]
, and loop superalgebras
[
CLS
]
. On the other hand, Feigin and Makedonskyi introduced generalized Weyl modules in
[
FM
]
by extending the notion of Weyl modules to arbitrary integral weights, and the connection between these modules and nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials was investigated in
[
FMO
]
.

Although local Weyl modules arise as the classical limits of irreducible modules for quantum affine algebras, they are generally not irreducible and possess rather intricate structures.
Several fundamental problems concerning local Weyl modules remain open, most notably their realization problem. The main goal of this paper is to provide a unified construction of local Weyl modules for the loop algebra
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
Our construction is motivated by the so-called skew
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
Howe duality on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
, which yields an explicit realization of all finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
-modules.

In representation theory, one of the prominent notions is that of dual pair for reductive Lie groups/algebras, first introduced by Howe
[
H1
]
. This concept has found numerous applications in invariant theory, automorphic representations, quantum groups, infinite-dimensional Lie algebras, and related areas.
In this paper, we focus on the skew Howe duality for complex general linear Lie algebras.
Specifically, it is known that
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
forms a reductive dual pair, namely a pair of maximal commuting reductive subalgebras, inside
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
. The natural action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
on the exterior algebra
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
of
\mathbb{C}^{nr}
therefore induces a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-action on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
.
The skew
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
Howe duality states that the exterior algebra
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
admits a remarkable multiplicity-free decomposition under this
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
-action.
This decomposition is determined by those highest weight vectors of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
in
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
, which are given explicitly by Grassmann monomials (see
[
H2
]
or Proposition
2.2
).

Motivated by the work of
[
CGT
]
,
we extend the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
to its loop algebra
L(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, in terms of finite sums of partial differential operators
glued together using certain Lagrange interpolation polynomials of degree
r-1
. Restricting this action, we may regard
the exterior algebra
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
as a module for the loop algebra
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. As the main result of this paper, we prove that every highest weight vector of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
in
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
generates a local Weyl module of
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
(see Theorem
4.2
).
Furthermore, by the factorization property of local Weyl modules, we obtain an explicit realization of all local Weyl modules for
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
(see Corollary
4.3
).

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is based on a realization of certain local Weyl modules for the

(r-1)
-th Takiff algebra
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})=\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{r}\mathbb{C}[t]
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.
Local Weyl modules for Takiff algebras attached to finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras were introduced and studied in
[
FMM
]
. It is also known that every local Weyl module for a loop algebra descends naturally to a module over an appropriate Takiff algebra of the same type (see
[
CFS
]
).

A key observation is that there is a natural embedding from the
Takiff algebra
T_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{r}\mathbb{C}[t]
into
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
(see
[
L
, Section 2]
for example).
Consequently, the natural action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
induces an action of
T_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
, and hence also of
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
.
We prove in Theorem
5.2
that the
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-submodule of
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
generated by a highest weight vector of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
is a local Weyl module.
On the other hand,
we show that the action of
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
descends to an action of
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.
Moreover, up to twisting by an automorphism, these two

T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module structures on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
coincide, thereby implying Theorem
4.2
.

Now we give an outline of the structure of this paper. In Section
2
, we review the skew Howe duality for general linear Lie algebras. In Section
3
, we recall some basics on local Weyl modules for
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, and then
state the main result in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we construct certain local Weyl modules for the
(r-1)
-th Takiff algebra
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
. Finally, in Section
6
, we prove the main result using the result established in Section
5
.

All the Lie algebras considered in this paper are over the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex
numbers. We denote the sets of integers, non-negative integers, positive integers,
complex numbers and non-zero complex numbers by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, respectively. And, for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
, we will use the notation
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{t})
to stand for the
universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{t}
.

2.
skew
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
Howe duality

In this section, let
n
and
r
be two positive integers. Here we recall the skew Howe duality for general linear Lie algebras from
[
H2
]
.

Write
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
for the complex general linear Lie algebra of rank
n
, which consists of all
n\times n
-matrices over
\mathbb{C}
. For every
1\leq i,j\leq n
, write
E_{i,j}
for the
n\times n
-matrix with
1
in the
(i,j)
-entry and zeros elsewhere. Note that there is a natural triangular decomposition

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{d}_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{n}^{-},

of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
,
where

\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}_{n}:=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n;\pm(j-i)>0}\mathbb{C}E_{i,j}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{d}_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}E_{i,i}.

As usual, by a partition, we mean an infinite sequence

\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)

of non-negative integers in non-increasing order and containing only finitely many non-zero terms. The number of non-zero terms in
\xi
is called its length, denoted by
\ell(\xi)
.
For every partition
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)
with
\ell(\xi)\leq n
, denote by
L_{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}(\xi)
the irreducible highest weight
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module generated by a (non-zero) highest weight vector
v_{\xi}
such that

\mathfrak{n}_{n}^{+}.v_{\xi}=0\quad\textrm{and}\quad E_{j,j}.v_{\xi}=\xi_{j}v_{\xi}\quad\text{for}\ 1\leq j\leq n.

Form the Fock space

(2.2)

\displaystyle V_{n,r}:=\Lambda[y_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,~0\leq j\leq r-1],

which is the exterior algebra in the variables
y_{i,j}
.
By identifying
V_{n,r}
with the exterior algebra
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
of
\mathbb{C}^{nr}
,
the natural action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
on
\mathbb{C}^{nr}
(and hence on
\Lambda(\mathbb{C}^{nr})
) induces a
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
-module structure on
V_{n,r}
with the action given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle E_{i+kn,j+tn}\mapsto y_{i,k}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,t}}\quad\text{for $1\leq i,j\leq n$ and $0\leq k,t\leq r-1$.}

It is well known that
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
forms a dual pair in
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
in the sense that:
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
is a pair of maximal commuting subalgebras in
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}

through the embedding

\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{nr},\quad E_{i,j}\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}E_{i+kn,j+kn},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{gl}_{r}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{nr},\quad E_{k,t}\mapsto\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}E_{\ell+(k-1)n,\ell+(t-1)n},

where
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
1\leq k,t\leq r
. This, together with (
2.3
), provides a
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
-module structure on
V_{n,r}
.
For later use, we write the explicit action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
V_{n,r}
as follows:

(2.4)

\displaystyle E_{i,j}\mapsto\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1}y_{i,\ell}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,\ell}}\quad\text{for $1\leq i,j\leq n$.}

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\xi
and
\eta
be two partitions with
\ell(\xi)\leq n
and
\ell(\eta)\leq r
.
A non-zero vector
v
in
V_{n,r}
is called a highest weight vector for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
with type
(\xi,\eta)
if

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{n}_{n}^{+}\times\mathfrak{n}_{r}^{+}).v=0\ \text{and}\ (E_{i,i},E_{j,j}).v=(\xi_{i}+\eta_{j})v\ \text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq r.

For every partition
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots)
, let
\eta^{\prime}=(\eta_{1}^{\prime},\eta_{2}^{\prime},\dots)

denote its transposed partition, where

\eta_{i}^{\prime}=\textrm{Card}\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mid\eta_{j}\geq i\}.

Write
\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
for the set of all partitions
\eta
such that
\ell(\eta)\leq n
and
\ell(\eta^{\prime})\leq r
.
Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)
be a partition in
\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
. Write

(2.5)

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{\xi}:=\bm{y}_{1}(\xi_{1})\bm{y}_{2}(\xi_{2})\cdots\bm{y}_{\ell(\xi)}(\xi_{\ell(\xi)})\in V_{n,r}.

Here, for
1\leq i\leq\ell(\xi)
,

\bm{y}_{i}(\xi_{i}):=y_{i,0}y_{i,1}\cdots y_{i,\xi_{i}-1}.

The structure of
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
-module
V_{n,r}
is completely determined by the highest weight vectors inside it.
In fact, the skew
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
Howe duality asserts that as a
(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r})
-module,

V_{n,r}
has the following multiplicity-free decomposition:

V_{n,r}\cong\bigoplus_{\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n,r}}L_{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}}(\xi)\otimes L_{\mathfrak{gl}_{r}}(\xi^{\prime}).

This decomposition can be deduced from the following result (cf.
[
H2
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
,
\bm{y}_{\xi}
is a highest weight vector for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
in
V_{n,r}
with type
(\xi,\xi^{\prime})
. Conversely, up to a scalar, every highest weight vector for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\times\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
in
V_{n,r}
has such a form.

3.
Local Weyl modules for loop algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}

In the rest of this paper, let
n
and
r
be two positive integers with
n\geq 2
.
In this section, we introduce some basics on local Weyl modules.

As in the introduction, denote by

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}=[\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n}]

the complex simple Lie algebra of type
A_{n-1}
. Note that
(
2.1
)
induces a
triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{sl}_{n}={\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}_{n}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{-},

where

\mathfrak{h}_{n}:=\mathfrak{d}_{n}\cap\mathfrak{sl}_{n}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{C}h_{i}\qquad(h_{i}:=E_{i,i}-E_{i+1,i+1}).

Write
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n-1}\}\subset(\mathfrak{h}_{n})^{\ast}
for the dual basis of
\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{n-1}\}
. Namely,

\omega_{i}(h_{j})=\delta_{i,j}\quad\textnormal{{for }}1\leq i,j\leq n-1.

Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle P_{+}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathbb{N}\omega_{i}\subset(\mathfrak{h}_{n})^{\ast}

is the set of dominant integral weights of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
.

For every complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, denote by

L(\mathfrak{a}):=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]

the loop algebra of
\mathfrak{a}
, where the Lie bracket is given by

\displaystyle[x(m),y(n)]=[x,y](m+n)\quad\textnormal{{for }}x,y\in\mathfrak{a}\textnormal{{ and }}m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Here and henceforth, we set

x(m)=x\otimes t^{m}\in L(\mathfrak{a})\quad\textnormal{{for }}x\in\mathfrak{a}\textnormal{{ and }}m\in\mathbb{Z}.

For every polynomial
\pi(z)
with constant term 1, define

\pi^{+}(z)=\pi(z)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\pi^{-}(z)=\frac{z^{\deg\pi(z)}\pi(z^{-1})}{(z^{\deg\pi(z)}\pi(z^{-1}))|_{z=0}}\in\mathbb{C}[z],

where
\deg\pi(z)
denotes the degree of
\pi(z)
.
Write
\mathcal{M}_{n}
for the set of all

(n-1)
-tuples

\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1}(z),\pi_{2}(z),\dots,\pi_{n-1}(z)),

where each
\pi_{i}(z)
is a polynomial with constant term
1
.
The local Weyl modules for
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
are parametrized by the elements in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
(see
[
CP
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1}(z),\pi_{2}(z),\dots,\pi_{n-1}(z))\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
The local Weyl module of
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
associated to
\bm{\pi}
, denoted by
W(\bm{\pi})
, is a cyclic
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module generated by
v
and subject to the defining relations

(3.3)

\displaystyle L({\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{+}).v=0,\quad h_{i}(0).v=(\deg\pi_{i}(z))v,\quad\Lambda_{i}^{\pm}(z).v=\pi_{i}^{\pm}(z)v,

and

(E_{i+1,i}(0))^{(\deg\pi_{i}(z))+1}.v=0\quad\textnormal{{for all }}1\leq i\leq n-1.

Here,

\Lambda_{i}^{\pm}(z):=\mathrm{exp}\left(-\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{h_{i}(\pm m)}{m}z^{m}\right)\in L(\mathfrak{h}_{n})[[z]].

For every
a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}\omega_{i}\in P_{+}
, set

(3.4)

\displaystyle\bm{\pi}_{\lambda,a}:=((1-az)^{\lambda_{1}},\dots,(1-az)^{\lambda_{n-1}})\in\mathcal{M}_{n}.

We remark that when
\bm{\pi}=\bm{\pi}_{\lambda,a}
,
the second and third defining relations in (
3.3
) are equivalent to
the relations

(3.5)

\displaystyle h_{i}(m).v=a^{m}\lambda_{i}v\quad\text{for all $m\in\mathbb{Z}$ and $1\leq i\leq n-1$.}

Finally, we recall the following factorization property of local Weyl modules for later use (see
[
CP
, Theorem 2]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1}(z),\dots,\pi_{n-1}(z))
,
\tilde{\bm{\pi}}=(\tilde{\pi}_{1}(z),\dots,\tilde{\pi}_{n-1}(z))\in\mathcal{M}_{n}

such that
\pi_{i}(z)
and
\tilde{\pi}_{j}(z)
are coprime for
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
. Then one has that

W(\bm{\pi\tilde{\pi}})\cong W(\bm{\pi})\otimes W(\bm{\tilde{\pi}})

as
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-modules, where

\bm{\pi\tilde{\pi}}=(\pi_{1}(z)\tilde{\pi}_{1}(z),\pi_{2}(z)\tilde{\pi}_{2}(z),\dots,\pi_{n-1}(z)\tilde{\pi}_{n-1}(z)).

4.
Main result

In this section, we state the main result of this paper.
Recall that
V_{n,r}
(see (
2.2
)) denotes the exterior algebra in the variables
y_{i,j}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
, we set

x_{i,j}:=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\,y_{i,r-1-k}\in V_{n,r}.

It is straightforward to see that

(4.1)

\displaystyle y_{i,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{r-j}\binom{r-j-1}{k-1}(-1)^{r-j-k}x_{i,k-1},

and so

V_{n,r}=\Lambda[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,~0\leq j\leq r-1]

is the exterior algebra in the variables
x_{i,j}
as well.

Let
a
be a non-zero complex number.
Define a linear map

\psi_{a,n,r}:L(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\to\textrm{End}(V_{n,r})

by letting

(4.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{a,n,r}(E_{i,k}(m))=a^{m}\sum_{j,s=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j,r}(m+s)x_{i,j}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{k,s}}}

for
1\leq i,k\leq n
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Here,

\displaystyle\ell_{j,r}(t)=\prod_{0\leq i\neq j\leq r-1}\frac{t-i}{j-i}

denotes the fundamental Lagrange interpolation polynomial of degree
r-1
at the points
j=0,1,\dots,r-1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The linear map
\psi_{a,n,r}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

For a polynomial
g(t)
with
\textnormal{{deg\,}}g(t)\leq r-1
, one has (cf.
[
CGT
, Lemma 4.4]
)

(4.3)

\displaystyle g(m+n)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}g(m+j)\ell_{j,r}(n)\quad\textrm{for all }m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, for
1\leq i_{1},i_{2},k_{1},k_{2}\leq n
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle[\psi_{a,n,r}(E_{i_{1},k_{1}}(m_{1})),\psi_{a,n,r}(E_{i_{2},k_{2}}(m_{2}))]

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\sum_{j_{1},s_{1},j_{2},s_{2}=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j_{1},r}(m_{1}+s_{1})\ell_{j_{2},r}(m_{2}+s_{2})\left[x_{i_{1},j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{1},s_{1}}},x_{i_{2},j_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{2},s_{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\delta_{i_{2},k_{1}}\sum_{j_{1},s_{2}=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j_{1},r}(m_{1}+j_{2})\ell_{j_{2},r}(m_{2}+s_{2})\right)x_{i_{1},j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{2},s_{2}}}

\displaystyle-a^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\delta_{i_{1},k_{2}}\sum_{j_{2},s_{1}=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{s_{2}=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j_{2},r}(m_{2}+s_{2})\ell_{s_{2},r}(m_{1}+s_{1})\right)x_{i_{2},j_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{1},s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\delta_{i_{2},k_{1}}\sum_{j_{1},s_{2}=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j_{1},r}(m_{1}+m_{2}+s_{2})x_{i_{1},j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{2},s_{2}}}

\displaystyle-a^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\delta_{i_{1},k_{2}}\sum_{j_{2},s_{1}=0}^{r-1}\ell_{j_{2},r}(m_{1}+m_{2}+s_{1})x_{i_{2},j_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k_{1},s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\psi_{a,n,r}\left([E_{i_{1},k_{1}}(m_{1}),E_{i_{2},k_{2}}(m_{2})]\right),

where in the second last equality we have used (
4.3
) with
g(t)=\ell_{j_{1},r}(t)
and
\ell_{j_{2},r}(t)
.

∎

Via the homomorphism
\psi_{a,n,r}
,
V_{n,r}
becomes an
L(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i,j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r-j}\binom{r-k}{j}(-1)^{r-k-j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i,k-1}}

for
1\leq i\leq n
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
.
This, together with (
4.1
), implies that

\displaystyle\psi_{a,n,r}(E_{i,j}(0))=\

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}x_{i,k}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{j,k}}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}x_{i,k}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{r-k-1}\binom{r-p-1}{k}(-1)^{r-k-p-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{r-p-1}\binom{r-p-1}{k}(-1)^{r-p-k-1}x_{i,k}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,p}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}y_{i,p}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,p}}

for
1\leq i,j\leq n
.
Thus, the action of
L(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
on
V_{n,r}
is an extension of the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
V_{n,r}
(see (
2.4
)).

From now on, we view
V_{n,r}
as an
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module by taking restriction, denoted by
V_{n,r}(a)
.
Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots)
be a partition in
\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
.
Denote by

(4.4)

\lambda(\xi):=(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\omega_{1}+(\xi_{2}-\xi_{3})\omega_{2}+\dots+(\xi_{n-1}-\xi_{n})\omega_{n-1}

the dominant integral weight of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
associated to
\xi
.
Then we have the local Weyl module
W({\bm{\pi}}_{\lambda(\xi),a})
for
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
(see (
3.4
)).
On the other hand, recall that there is a Grassmann monomial
\bm{y}_{\xi}
in
V_{n,r}
associated to
\xi
(see (
2.5
)).
Write

W_{a,\xi}
for the
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-submodule of
V_{n,r}(a)
generated by the element
\bm{y}_{\xi}
.

The following is the main result of this paper, whose proof will be presented in Section
6
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and let
\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
. Then

(4.5)

\displaystyle W_{a,\xi}\cong W(\bm{\pi}_{\lambda(\xi),a})

as
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-modules.

Finally, let
\bm{\pi}=(\pi_{1}(z),\pi_{2}(z),\dots,\pi_{n-1}(z))\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. For every
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we know

(4.6)

\displaystyle\pi_{i}(z)=\prod_{k=1}^{s_{i}}(1-b_{i,k}z)^{r_{i,k}}

for some (uniquely determined) non-zero distinct complex numbers
b_{i,1},b_{i,2},\dots,b_{i,s_{i}}
and positive integers
r_{i,1},r_{i,2},\dots,r_{i,s_{i}}
.
Let
a^{(1)},a^{(2)},\dots,a^{(m)}
be the distinct elements in the set
\{b_{i,k}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1,1\leq k\leq s_{i}\},
and let
\lambda^{(j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{(j)}\omega_{i}\in P_{+}
for
1\leq j\leq m
such that

\lambda_{i}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}r_{i,k},&\mbox{if }a^{(j)}=b_{i,k}\mbox{ for some }k,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then one has that

\bm{\pi}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m}\bm{\pi}_{\lambda^{(j)},a^{(j)}}
, and hence

(4.7)

\displaystyle W(\bm{\pi})\cong\otimes_{j=1}^{m}W(\bm{\pi}_{\lambda^{(j)},a^{(j)}})

by Proposition
3.2
.

Form the tensor
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module

V_{\bm{\pi}}:=\otimes_{j=1}^{m}V_{n,r^{(j)}}(a^{(j)}),

where
r^{(j)}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{(j)}
for
1\leq j\leq m
.
Note that

W_{\bm{\pi}}:=\otimes_{j=1}^{m}W_{a^{(j)},\xi^{(j)}}

is the submodule of
V_{\bm{\pi}}
generated by the vector
\otimes_{j=1}^{m}\bm{y}_{\xi^{(j)}}
,
where

\xi^{(j)}=(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{(j)},\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{(j)},\dots,\lambda_{n-1}^{(j)},0,\dots)\in\mathcal{P}_{n,r^{(j)}}\quad\text{for $1\leq j\leq m$}.

Combining Theorem
4.2
with (
4.7
), we have the following realization of all local Weyl modules for
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.

Corollary 4.3
.

With the notation given above, one has that

\displaystyle W(\bm{\pi})\cong W_{\bm{\pi}}

as
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-modules.

5.
Local Weyl modules for Takiff algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}

In this section we give a realization of local Weyl modules for the
(r-1)
-th Takiff algebra of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
,
which plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

For every complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, write

T_{r}(\mathfrak{a}):=\mathfrak{a}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{r}\mathbb{C}[t]

for the
(r-1)
-th Takiff algebra of
\mathfrak{a}
.
For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\overline{t^{i}}:=t^{i}+t^{r}\mathbb{C}[t]
.
Then

\{\overline{t^{i}}\mid 0\leq i\leq r-1\}

forms a basis of
\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{r}\mathbb{C}[t]
.

We remark that there is a natural embedding from
T_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
into
\mathfrak{gl}_{nr}
defined by

E_{i,j}\otimes\overline{t^{k}}\mapsto\sum_{s=0}^{r-k-1}E_{i+sn,j+(s+k)n}\quad\text{for $1\leq i,j\leq n$ and $0\leq k\leq r-1$.}

This, together with (
2.3
),
yields a
T_{r}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module structure on
V_{n,r}
with the action given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle E_{i,j}\otimes\overline{t^{k}}\mapsto\sum_{s=0}^{r-k-1}y_{i,s}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j,s+k}}\quad\text{for $1\leq i,j\leq n$ and $0\leq k\leq r-1$.}

By taking restriction,
V_{n,r}
becomes a
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module. We denote by
T_{r}(W_{\xi})
the
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-submodule of
V_{n,r}
generated by
{\bm{y}_{\xi}}
.

The following result is straightforward.

Lemma 5.1
.

The following relations hold in
T_{r}(W_{\xi}):

\displaystyle T_{r}({\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{+}).\bm{y}_{\xi}=0,\ (h_{i}\otimes\overline{t^{j}}).\bm{y}_{\xi}=\delta_{j,0}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1})\bm{y}_{\xi},\ \textrm{and}\ (E_{i+1,i}\otimes\overline{1})^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}+1}.\bm{y}_{\xi}=0,

where
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
.

Similar to Definition
3.1
,
one also has the notion of local Weyl modules for
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
(cf.
[
FMM
]
). For every

\lambda\in P_{+}
, denote by

W_{r}(\lambda)
the local Weyl module of
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
associated to
\lambda
, which by definition is the cyclic
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module generated by a vector
v
and subject to the defining relations

(5.2)

\displaystyle T_{r}({\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{+}).v=0,\quad(h_{i}\otimes\overline{t^{j}}).v=\delta_{j,0}\lambda(h_{i})v,\quad\textrm{and}\quad(E_{i+1,i}\otimes 1)^{\lambda(h_{i})+1}.v=0

for
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
0\leq j\leq r-1
.

The following result gives a realization of these local Weyl modules for
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
n,r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
n\geq 2
and let
\xi\in\mathcal{P}_{n,r}
. Then
one has that

T_{r}(W_{\xi})\cong W_{r}(\lambda(\xi))

as
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-modules.

Lemma
5.1
implies that the
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module
T_{r}(W_{\xi})
is a quotient of
W_{r}(\lambda(\xi))
. In addition,
it is known that (see
[
CL
, 1.5.1]
and
[
FMM
, Eq. (2.2.5)]
)

(5.3)

\displaystyle\dim W_{r}(\lambda(\xi))=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}.

Therefore, Theorem
5.2
follows from the following result.

Proposition 5.3
.

One has that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\dim T_{r}(W_{\xi})\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}.

The rest of this section is devoted to a proof of Proposition
5.3
. We will prove the proposition by constructing
\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}
linearly independent vectors in
T_{r}(W_{\xi})
. To this end, we first introduce some notations. Let
``\preceq"
denote the reversed lexicographic (total) order on
\mathbb{N}^{k}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. More precisely, for

\bm{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k}),\quad\bm{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k},

we write
\bm{i}\preceq\bm{j}
if either
\bm{i}=\bm{j}
, or the last non-vanishing difference
i_{s}-j_{s}
(
1\leq s\leq k
) is negative. In the latter case, we write
\bm{i}\prec\bm{j}
.

Let
m,p,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
p\leq q
. Denote by
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}
the subset of
\mathbb{N}^{m}
consisting of strictly increasing
m
-tuples,
and by
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(p,q)\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}
the set consisting of those
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{m})
satisfying the condition

p\leq k_{1}<\cdots<k_{m}\leq q.

When
m>0
and
q=p+m-1
, we have

\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(p,q)=\{(p)_{m}\}\quad((p)_{m}:=(p,p+1,\dots,p+m-1)).

Assume further that
1\leq m\leq q-p+1
. For every
\bm{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
, we define
S(p,q,\bm{d})

to be the subset of
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}
consisting of all
\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m})

such that

(u_{1}+d_{\sigma(1)},u_{2}+d_{\sigma(2)},\dots,u_{m}+d_{\sigma(m)})\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(p,q)\quad\textnormal{{for some }}\sigma\in S_{m}.

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume that
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\dots\leq d_{m}\leq q-p-m+1
. Then

(q-m+1-d_{m},\dots,q-1-d_{2},q-d_{1})

is the unique maximal element in
S(p,q,\bm{d})
with respect to the order
``\preceq"
on
\mathbb{N}^{m}
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from the fact that

(q-m+1)_{m}=(q-m+1,q-m+2,\dots,q)

is the unique maximal element in
\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(p,q)
with respect to
``\preceq"
.
∎

Let
1\leq i\leq n
,
0\leq m\leq r
, and
\bm{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(0,r-1)
.
Set
\bm{y}_{i,\bm{j}}=1
if
m=0
, and

\bm{y}_{i,\bm{j}}:=y_{i,j_{1}}\cdots y_{i,j_{m}}\in V_{n,r}

if
m>0
.
Then the set

\{\bm{y}_{1,\bm{j}_{1}}\bm{y}_{2,\bm{j}_{2}}\cdots\bm{y}_{n,\bm{j}_{n}}\mid\bm{j}_{t}\in\cup_{m=0}^{r}\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{m}(0,r-1),~1\leq t\leq n\}

forms a basis of
V_{n,r}
.

We first consider the case that
n=2
. Introduce the elements

\displaystyle Y(k,\bm{m})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}E_{2,1}\otimes\overline{t^{\xi_{1}-m_{k+1-i}-i}}\right).\bm{y}_{\xi}\in T_{r}(W_{\xi}),

where
0\leq k\leq\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}
and
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1)
. These elements are well-defined since
[E_{2,1}\otimes\overline{t^{i}},E_{2,1}\otimes\overline{t^{j}}]=0
for all
0\leq i,j\leq r-1
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathcal{P}_{2,r}
. Then the elements
Y(k,\bm{m})
for
0\leq k\leq\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}
and
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathrm{ord}}^{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1)
are linearly independent in
T_{r}(W_{\xi})
.

Proof.

It is clear that the assertion holds for the case that
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}
. Now assume that
\xi_{1}\neq\xi_{2}
.
Let
0\leq k\leq\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}
and
\bm{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1)
.
For
1\leq i\leq k
, we set
d_{i}=\xi_{1}-m_{k+1-i}-i
and write

\bm{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{k})
. Then one easily verifies that

d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq\dots\leq d_{k}\leq\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}-k.

For every
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1)
and
\sigma\in S_{k}
, we have that

\bm{u}\preceq(m_{1}+d_{\sigma(k)},m_{2}+d_{\sigma(k-1)},\dots,m_{k}+d_{\sigma(1)}).

Furthermore, the above is an equality if and only if
\bm{u}=(\xi_{1}-k,\xi_{1}-k+1,\dots,\xi_{1}-1)
and
d_{\sigma(i)}=d_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. This, together with (
5.1
) and Lemma
5.4
, implies that

\displaystyle Y(k,\bm{m})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{p}\in S(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1,\bm{d})}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\left(y_{2,p_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1,p_{1}+d_{\sigma(k)}}}y_{2,p_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1,p_{2}+d_{\sigma(k-1)}}}\dots y_{2,p_{k}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1,p_{k}+d_{\sigma(1)}}}\right).\bm{y}_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle c_{\bm{m}}\bm{y}_{1,(0)_{\xi_{1}-k}}\bm{y}_{2,(0)_{\xi_{2}}}\bm{y}_{2,\bm{m}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{q}\in\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{\xi_{1}-k}(0,\xi_{1}-1),\\
\bm{p}\prec\bm{m}\end{subarray}}c_{\bm{q},\bm{p}}\bm{y}_{1,\bm{q}}\bm{y}_{2,(0)_{\xi_{2}}}\bm{y}_{2,\bm{p}},

where
c_{\bm{q},\bm{p}}\in\mathbb{C}
and
c_{\bm{m}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. The proposition then follows immediately.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.3
:

We prove the theorem by using induction on
n
. When
n=2
, it follows from Proposition
5.5
that

\textrm{dim }T_{r}(W_{\xi})\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\textrm{Card\ }\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{k}(\xi_{2},\xi_{1}-1)=\sum_{k=0}^{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}\binom{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}}{k}=2^{\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}},

as desired.

Now assume that
n>2
. We set

\mathbb{N}(\xi)=\{\bm{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}\mid 0\leq k_{i}\leq\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1},~1\leq i\leq n-1\},

and introduce the following elements

\displaystyle Y(\bm{k},\bm{m}_{1},\dots,\bm{m}_{n-1})=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k_{j}}E_{n,j}\otimes\overline{t^{\xi_{j}-m_{j,k_{j}-i+1}-i}}\right).\bm{y}_{\xi}\in T_{r}(W_{\xi}),

where
\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}(\xi)
and
\bm{m}_{i}=(m_{i,1},\dots,m_{i,k_{i}})\in\mathbb{N}_{\textnormal{{ord}}}^{k_{i}}(\xi_{i+1},\xi_{i}-1)
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

We claim that these elements are linearly independent in
T_{r}(W_{\xi})
. Indeed, by a similar argument as that in Proposition
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle Y(\bm{k},\bm{m}_{1},\dots,\bm{m}_{n-1})=

\displaystyle\ c\bm{y}_{\bm{\xi}_{n}-\bm{k}}\bm{y}_{n,(0)_{\xi_{n}}}\bm{y}_{n,\bm{m}_{n-1}}\bm{y}_{n,\bm{m}_{n-2}}\cdots\bm{y}_{n,\bm{m}_{1}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{\textrm{ord}}^{\xi_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}}(0,r-1),\\
\bm{j}\prec((0)_{\xi_{n}},\bm{m}_{n-1},\bm{m}_{n-2},\dots,\bm{m}_{1})\end{subarray}}u_{\bm{j}}{\bm{y_{n,j}}},

where
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
u_{\bm{j}}\in V_{n-1,r}(\subseteq V_{n,r})
, and

\bm{\xi}_{n}-\bm{k}=(\xi_{1}-k_{1},\xi_{2}-k_{2},\dots,\xi_{n-1}-k_{n-1})\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1,r}.

This implies the claim.

On the other hand, view
V_{n,r}
as a
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n-1})
-module by restricting to
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n-1})
and consider the
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n-1})
-submodule generated by
\bm{y}_{\bm{\xi}_{n}-\bm{k}}
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{U}(T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n-1})).\bm{y}_{\bm{\xi}_{n}-\bm{k}}\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\binom{n-1}{i}^{\xi_{i}-k_{i}-\xi_{i+1}+k_{i+1}}.

In addition,
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n-1})
acts trivially on
y_{n,j}
for
0\leq j\leq r-1
. This, together with (
5
) and (
5.6
), gives that

\displaystyle\dim T_{r}(W_{\xi})\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}(\xi)}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\binom{n-1}{i}^{\xi_{i}-k_{i}-\xi_{i+1}+k_{i+1}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{\xi_{j}-\xi_{j+1}}{k_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}\binom{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}{k_{i}}\binom{n-1}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}-k_{i}}\binom{n-1}{i-1}^{k_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}},

where in the last equality we used the fact that

\binom{n}{i}=\binom{n-1}{i}+\binom{n-1}{i-1}.

This completes the proof. ∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

This section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
4.2
.
We start with the following technical result (cf.
[
CGT
]
).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m
and
s
be two non-negative integers with
m<s
, and let
g(t)
be a polynomial of degree
m
.
Then

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\binom{s}{k}(-1)^{s-k}g(k)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\binom{s}{k}(-1)^{s-k}k^{s}=s!.

Write
I_{r,a}
for the ideal of
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]
generated by
(t-a)^{r}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

One has that
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\otimes I_{r,a}\subseteq\ker\psi_{a,n,r}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
and (
4.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\psi_{a,n,r}(E_{i,k}\otimes(t-a)^{r})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(-a)^{r}\sum_{j_{1},s_{1}=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-j_{2}-1}\binom{r-1}{j_{2}}\ell_{j_{1},r}(r-j_{2}+s_{1}-1)\right)x_{i,j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k,s_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle 0,

where
1\leq i,k\leq n
.
It follows that

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\otimes I_{r,a}=[\mathfrak{gl}_{n},\mathfrak{gl}_{n}]\otimes I_{r,a}\subseteq\ker\psi_{a,n,r},

as desired.
∎

In view of Lemma
6.2
, the
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module
V_{n,r}(a)
descends to an

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\otimes\left(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]/I_{r,a}\right)
-module.
Then, via the canonical algebra isomorphism

T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\to\mathfrak{sl}_{n}\otimes\left(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]/I_{r,a}\right),\quad x\otimes\overline{t^{k}}\mapsto x\otimes((t-a)^{k}+I_{r,a}),

V_{n,r}(a)
becomes a
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module, denoted by

\overline{V_{n,r}(a)}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The action of
T_{r}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
on
\overline{V_{n,r}(a)}
is given by

E_{i,k}\otimes\overline{t^{m}}\mapsto a^{m}\sum_{p=m}^{r-1}y_{i,p-m}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}\quad\text{for $1\leq i,k\leq n$ and $0\leq m\leq r-1$.}

In particular, when
a=1
, it coincides with (
5.1
).

Proof.

By using Lemma
6.1
and (
4.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle E_{i,k}\otimes\overline{t^{m}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{j_{1},s_{1}=0}^{r-1}\left(\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j_{2}}\binom{m}{j_{2}}\ell_{j_{1},r}(j_{2}+s_{1})\right)x_{i,j_{1}}\left(\sum_{p=0}^{r-s_{1}-1}\binom{r-p}{s_{1}}(-1)^{r-p-s_{1}-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{j_{1},p=0}^{r-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{r-p-1}\left(\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-j_{2}+r-p-s_{1}-1}\binom{m}{j_{2}}\binom{r-p-1}{s_{1}}\ell_{j_{1},r}(j_{2}+s_{1})\right)x_{i,j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{j_{1},p=0}^{r-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{m+r-p-1}\left((-1)^{m+r-p-s_{1}-1}\sum_{j_{2}=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j_{2}}\binom{r-p-1}{s_{1}-j_{2}}\ell_{j_{1},r}(s_{1})\right)x_{i,j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{j_{1},p=0}^{r-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{m+r-p-1}\left((-1)^{m+r-p-s_{1}-1}\binom{m+r-p-1}{s_{1}}\ell_{j_{1},r}(s_{1})\right)x_{i,j_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{m+r-p-1}\left((-1)^{m+r-p-s_{1}-1}\binom{m+r-p-1}{s_{1}}x_{i,s_{1}}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle a^{m}\sum_{p=m}^{r-1}y_{i,p-m}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{k,p}}

as operators on
\overline{V_{n,r}(a)}
.
∎

By applying Lemma
6.3
, the following is straightforward.

Lemma 6.4
.

The following relations hold in the
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module
V_{n,r}(a)
:

L({\mathfrak{n}}_{n}^{+}).\bm{y}_{\xi}=0,\ h_{i}(m).\bm{y}_{\xi}=a^{m}(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1})\bm{y}_{\xi},\ \textrm{and}\ (E_{i+1,i}\otimes 1)^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}+1}.\bm{y}_{\xi}=0,

where
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
:

Lemma
6.4
implies that the
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module

W_{a,\xi}
is a quotient of the local Weyl module
W(\bm{\pi}_{\lambda(\xi),a})
(see Definition
3.1
and (
3.5
)).
Thus, it suffices to prove that

\dim W_{a,\xi}=\dim W(\bm{\pi}_{\lambda(\xi),a}).

On the one hand, it was shown in
[
CL
, Section 1.5.1]
that

\dim W(\bm{\pi}_{\lambda(\xi),a})=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}.

On the other hand, by twisting the automorphism

L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})\rightarrow L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n}),\quad x(m)\mapsto a^{m}x(m),

there is a new
L(\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
-module structure on
V_{n,r}(1)
, which is exactly
V_{n,r}(a)
.
This, together with Lemma
6.3
, Theorem
5.2
, and (
5.3
), gives that

\dim W_{a,\xi}=\dim W_{1,\xi}=\dim T_{r}(W_{\xi})=\dim W_{r}(\lambda(\xi))=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{i}^{\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}}.

Therefore, we complete the proof. ∎
