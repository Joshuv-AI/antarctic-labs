Robust and accurate multimodal information extraction from tobacco box labels using MLDP
Authors: Songling Huang, Shuai Zhang, Minghua Han, Jianping Yang, Yuyan Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
With the advancement of smart re-baking factories and the accelerating digital transformation of agriculture, tobacco re-baking is facing an urgent need to shift from manual inspection to intelligent automated systems for quality traceability and information management. However, traditional cigarette box label information extraction methods still suffer from limited efficiency, insufficient accuracy, and poor robustness under real industrial conditions. To address these challenges, we propose MLDP (Multimodal Learning for Document Processing), a multimodal framework tailored for robust information extraction from tobacco box labels.MLDP integrates textual and visual information in a unified framework. For the text modality, multiple DeBERTa-based variants, including BiLSTM-DeBERTa, Multi-Sample Dropout-DeBERTa, Distil-DeBERTa, Two-Stage Dropout-DeBERTa, and LongMax-Dropout-DeBERTa, are combined through an Optuna-driven weighted ensemble strategy to balance model diversity and computational efficiency. For the image modality, PaddleOCR is adopted for text detection and recognition, and a Levenshtein distance-based correction mechanism is introduced to reduce OCR errors, especially in numeric fields. To further enhance cross-modal complementarity, MLDP employs a dynamic weighted fusion strategy together with a decision matrix for modality-level error correction, improving extraction robustness under noisy and complex industrial conditions.Experiments on a real-world multimodal tobacco box label dataset show that MLDP achieves a Micro-F1 of 96.51%, outperforming the text-only baseline (MLDP-T) and image-only baseline (MLDP-V) by 4.34% and 5.73%, respectively. Compared with classical document understanding baselines, including LayoutLMv3, UDOP, and Donut, and recent advanced document parsing or multimodal baselines such as MinerU2.5, PaddleOCR-VL, and Qwen2.5-VL, MLDP achieves the best overall performance and provides a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off.In addition, on the DocBank benchmark, MLDP yields 1.18%-1.55% gains on non-textual elements, demonstrating promising generalization ability beyond the target industrial scenario. Nevertheless, the current framework still depends on OCR quality and has been validated mainly on tobacco box labels, so its robustness under more diverse industrial document distributions requires further study. Furthermore, we construct and open-source an industrial tobacco box label multimodal dataset containing 9,719 graphically aligned samples, providing a useful benchmark resource for this task.

Introduction

The label affixed to redried tobacco boxes constitutes the primary packaging interface for finished cigarette products, encoding critical information including brand identification, quality grade, unique box numbering, and production timestamps. This data serves as the cornerstone for modern quality control and supply chain traceability systems within the tobacco industry. As Wang Ge et al.1 authoritatively demonstrated, establishing a comprehensive quality traceability framework is essential for advancing the technological sophistication of this sector while upholding its foundational principle of "prioritizing national interests and consumer welfare." To maintain stringent quality standards, tobacco processing enterprises must verify label information for every individual box. Nevertheless, traditional manual inspection methodologies exhibit profound limitations in large-scale industrial operations, characterized by suboptimal efficiency, elevated error rates, and inconsistent coverage—deficiencies that ultimately compromise the integrity of quality assurance protocols. Li Zhonghua et al.2 further contextualized this challenge within the new retail paradigm, noting that conventional manual approaches fail to support the demands of precision manufacturing and digital management. These operational constraints have generated urgent demand for automated solutions leveraging computer vision and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies to enhance processing efficiency and quality control metrics in tobacco production facilities.

The synergistic integration of Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) technologies has yielded substantial advances across numerous application domains in China. Li Wenhua et al.3 pioneeredan integrated pipeline employing OCR for initial text extraction from images followed by NLP-based post-processing, utilizing sophisticated lexical segmentation and grammatical matching strategies to significantly enhance recognition accuracy for low-quality visual inputs. Researcher Zhou Yingchun4 implemented.a similar methodology for educational applications, extracting text from handwritten essay images through OCR and subsequently applying NLP techniques for spelling correction and grammatical standardization. In the financial sector, Tianlong Ma et al.5 demonstrated how OCR could be combined with Robotic Process Automation (RPA) to automate data entry processes for treasury management systems, while NLP transformed unstructured document information into structured relational data, dramatically improving operational efficiency.

Concurrently, international research has produced remarkable progress in multimodal information extraction methodologies. RUI-LIN XU et al.6 developed an innovative meta-knowledge extraction framework for multimodal documents that strategically incorporates non-textual elements (images, tables, and diagrams) to overcome the expressivity limitations of conventional triple-based knowledge representations, thereby enabling construction of sophisticated hierarchical knowledge systems for automated reasoning. Takano et al.7 created a specialized specimen label extraction system utilizing advanced image recognition to automatically identify and digitize core metadata from natural history collections, providing crucial technological support for global biodiversity conservation initiatives. In medical informatics, Salvi et al. 8successfully combined OCR with machine learning to extract precise preoperative biometric parameters from ophthalmic imagery, demonstrating substantial clinical value for surgical precision. From architectural perspectives, Jiasen Lu et al.'s ViLBERT9 introduced a novel dual-stream framework processing visual and textual inputs through separate encoders with cross-modal attention mechanisms, while Xu Yiheng et al.'s LayoutLM10 pioneered the incorporation of spatial layout information into document understanding through two-dimensional position encoding. Emanuele Bugliarello et al.11 subsequently provided comprehensive analysis of architectural variations in vision-language models, identifying training data quality, hyperparameter optimization, and embedding layer design as primary determinants of model performance.

Despite these significant advances, a critical technological gap persists in applying multimodal learning to industrial document understanding, particularly for challenging use cases like tobacco box label recognition. General-purpose multimodal architectures (e.g.,9–11) typically train on natural imagery and clean documents, rendering them suboptimal for industrial environments characterized by variable printing quality, complex layout structures, inconsistent illumination conditions, and absolute requirements for numeric field accuracy. Concurrently, conventional OCR + NLP pipelines (e.g.,3–5) suffer from inherent error propagation limitations and insufficient semantic integration between visual and textual modalities. The absence of a purpose-built framework capable of dynamically leveraging cross-modal complementarity while correcting modality-specific errors represents a substantial impediment to industrial automation.

To address these challenges, we propose MLDP (Multimodal Learning for Document Processing), a novel framework specifically designed for robust information extraction from tobacco box labels. While MLDP integrates existing models such as DeBERTa variants for text processing and PaddleOCR for image recognition, the novelty of our framework lies in the system-level design tailored for industrial tobacco box label recognition. Specifically, MLDP introduces a dynamic weighted fusion mechanism that adjusts the contribution of text and image modalities based on contextual confidence, a cross-modal error correction matrix leveraging Levenshtein distance to correct OCR errors in numeric fields, and an Optuna-driven multi-variant ensemble strategy to balance model diversity with computational efficiency. These innovations enable MLDP to achieve superior robustness and accuracy in complex industrial scenarios, establishing a new practical paradigm for multimodal document understanding that is directly validated by our experimental evaluation.

To further address the rapid development of document intelligence and multimodal document reasoning in recent years27, as reflected by the increasing attention of the document analysis community toward large-scale evaluation and emerging document intelligence challenges31, we additionally compare MLDP with several emerging document parsing and multimodal document understanding methods, including MinerU2.532, PaddleOCR-VL33, and Qwen2.5-VL34.

These methods represent different technical paradigms: decoupled high-resolution document parsing, compact document-oriented vision-language modeling, and general-purpose multimodal large language modeling, respectively. This extended comparison provides a more comprehensive evaluation of MLDP against both classical document understanding models and recent advanced document parsing systems, highlighting the advantages and limitations of different paradigms in industrial document information extraction scenarios.

Comprehensive experimental evaluation demonstrates that MLDP achieves superior performance compared to both unimodal baselines and state-of-the-art multimodal models, exhibiting exceptional accuracy and generalization capability across diverse industrial scenarios. The proposed framework establishes a new paradigm for document understanding in practical industrial applications.

Text modal information extraction

DeBERTa-v3 introduces ELECTRA-style pre-training and gradient-disentangled embedding sharing, improving the original DeBERTa architecture12.where each word is represented by two vectors encoding its content and position, respectively. The attention weights between words are then computed using the untangled matrices of their content and relative positions. The second technique involves an enhanced mask decoder, which merges the absolute positions in the decoding layers to predict the masked tokens during the model’s pre-training phase.

While MLDP leverages existing models for its components, each model was carefully selected based on its suitability for the industrial tobacco box label recognition task. Specifically:BiLSTM-DeBERTa: Chosen to enhance local sequence modeling and capture bidirectional contextual dependencies in text sequences, which is critical for structured label information. The Optuna-driven weighted ensemble combines predictions from multiple heterogeneous DeBERTa models to improve robustness while optimizing the trade-off between model diversity and inference efficiency, following the general principle of ensemble-based DeBERTa modeling21,23. The search space and final ensemble weights were determined on the validation set, with Micro-F1 as the optimization objective, and the selected weights were fixed before testing.

The BiLSTM-DeBERTa model’s input passes token IDs into the model, which are then converted into their corresponding DeBERTa vector representations E through the em-bedding layer. The encoder then performs feature encoding on E to generate a hidden state H. The BiLSTM structure further processes this hidden state to obtain a bidirectional semantic representation. The classification module receives the BiLSTM output, performs a judgment, and outputs the final result. The model outputs the classification result for subsequent natural language processing tasks. The introduction of the BiLSTM structure enables the DeBERTa model to simultaneously obtain forward and backward contextual information when processing text sequences, effectively enhancing its understanding of semantic relationships, making it more stable when handling complex natural language processing tasks, and improving the model’s generalization and scalability in multi-task environments.

The core design of Multi-Sample Dropout-DeBERTa lies in the seamless integration of the multi-masking mechanism with the original DeBERTa architecture. In each layer (e.g., the attention module and the feedforward network), the model generates multiple independent dropout masks (typically 5 to 10) in parallel for the same input sample, with each mask randomly discarding neurons with the same probability. This results in multiple sets of distinct intermediate representations during the same forward propagation process. These masks are only active during the training phase, ensuring that inference remains consistent with traditional dropout. For the output corresponding to each mask, the model calculates the loss function value separately and then averages the loss values from all masks as the final optimization target. This not only preserves the diversity introduced by each dropout sample but also mitigates the fluctuations caused by different masks by averaging, which makes the gradient updates more stable and reliable. This mechanism is deeply integrated into the key components of DeBERTa: it enhances the robustness of the attention weights in the decoupled attention layer, improves noise adaptation in positional encoding, and prevents overfitting in the hidden layer of the feedforward network. This design retains the advantages of DeBERTa’s decoupled attention and enhanced positional encoding while significantly improving training stability and generalization through the integration of multiple masks.

The Distil-DeBERTa variant model is an efficient architecture based on knowledge distillation, Knowledge distillation follows the teacher-student paradigm introduced by Hinton et al.15.where the high-performing Multi-Sample Dropout-DeBERTa serves as the teacher model to guide the training of a lightweight student model. The core innovation of this model is the integration of a bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) into the DeBERTa framework. This modification reduces computational complexity by replacing part of the Transformer layer with a BiLSTM layer, while preserving the core performance of the model. During training, the student model not only optimizes the standard task loss (cross-entropy) but also aligns the output probability distribution of the teacher model by minimizing the KL divergence (soft label distillation) and mimics the teacher’s middle-layer representations using mean squared error loss (feature distillation). This design inherits the strong generalization capability of the teacher model, which is achieved through Multi-Sample Dropout, and takes advantage of BiLSTM’s strengths in sequence modeling and local feature extraction, making it particularly suitable for long text processing tasks in resource-constrained environments.

To facilitate reproducibility and provide clearer implementation specifics, we detail the key hyperparameters and architectural choices common across our DeBERTa variants. All models in this study were initialized from the DeBERTa-v3-base pre-trained weights. The maximum sequence length was set to 512 tokens for all models except the LongMax-Dropout-DeBERTa variant, which extended this to 1800 tokens to accommodate longer documents. Fine-tuning was performed using the AdamW optimizer with a linear learning rate decay schedule. The initial learning rate was empirically set to 2e-5, with a warm-up ratio of 0.1 over the first 10% of the training steps. Training was conducted for 10 epochs with a batch size of 16.

Hyperparameter selection strategy. Because the proposed framework contains multiple modules and corresponding hyperparameters, we determined the final settings through a three-stage strategy: literature-guided initialization, task-oriented adjustment, and validation-based selection. First, for each backbone model or OCR component, we adopted the default or commonly recommended configurations reported in the original papers or official implementations, including DeBERTa-v3, PP-OCRv3, SVTR-LCNet, LayoutLMv3, UDOP, and Donut. Second, we adjusted several parameters according to the characteristics of the tobacco box label task, which contains short structured fields, long paragraph-like regions, and numerically sensitive elements such as box numbers, weights, batches, and dates. Third, the final values of key hyperparameters were selected on the validation set based on Micro-F1, numeric exact-match accuracy, and efficiency considerations, and then frozen before testing.

For the text branch, we mainly tuned the learning rate, dropout probability, number of dropout samples, maximum sequence length, and the weights of the ensemble models. The learning rate was selected from a small candidate set around standard Transformer fine-tuning practice, and 2e-5 gave the most stable convergence on the validation set. The dropout probability and the number of dropout samples were chosen by balancing robustness and training stability; empirically, a dropout rate of 0.2 and k = 5 samples achieved the best validation performance without introducing excessive optimization variance. The maximum sequence length was set to 512 for standard models and extended to 1800 only for LongMax-Dropout-DeBERTa, because this variant was specifically designed to cover longer text regions found in some label pages. For the ensemble, Optuna was used to search for the weighting coefficients of different DeBERTa variants under the objective of maximizing validation Micro-F1 while controlling unnecessary model redundancy.

For the image branch, parameters such as learning rate, training epochs, detector/recognizer variant, and lightweight enhancement strategy were selected through comparative experiments on the validation set. The final OCR detector and recognizer were chosen not only according to recognition performance but also according to the trade-off between Hmean/accuracy and inference speed, which is important for industrial deployment.For the multimodal stage, all post-processing and fusion hyperparameters were determined exclusively on the validation split to avoid test leakage. In particular, the Levenshtein correction threshold, temperature-scaling parameters, weighted fusion coefficients, and the decision matrix W were all fixed before final testing. Therefore, all reported test results were obtained using parameters selected without access to the test annotations.

For the Multi-Sample Dropout-DeBERTa, the number of dropout samples k was set to 5, and the dropout probability was set to 0.2. This mechanism was applied to the output of the last three transformer layers. The BiLSTM-DeBERTa model incorporated a single-layer BiLSTM with a hidden size of 768, resulting in a concatenated output dimension of 1536, which was then projected back to the label space via a linear layer.

The knowledge distillation process for Distil-DeBERTa employed a combination of losses. The student model, which replaced the final 6 transformer layers with a BiLSTM layer, was trained to minimize a weighted sum of the task-specific cross-entropy loss (L_ce), the Kullback–Leibler divergence loss (L_kl) against the softened outputs of the teacher model (with a temperature parameter T = 3), and a mean squared error loss (L_mse) applied to align the hidden states of the student and teacher models at the layer preceding the substitution. The loss coefficients were set to α = 0.5 for L_ce, β = 0.4 for L_kl, and γ = 0.1 for L_mse based on validation performance. These detailed configurations were crucial in achieving the reported performance metrics.

Image modal information extraction

In the image modality, the extraction of cigarette box label information resembles the task of document layout analysis, which is achieved through optical character recognition (OCR). For data processing and enhancement, image preprocessing techniques, inclu-ding the Fourier transform, binarization, Hough line detection, tilt angle calculation, perspective transformation, and rotation correction, are applied to obtain clearer images for subsequent text region localization and text detection.

Since OCR may introduce recognition errors, the extracted text strings can be noisy and incomplete. To mitigate this, our inference pipeline is designed to be deployment-aligned and leakage-free: the textual modality is derived exclusively from OCR-recognized text blocks extracted from the input label image, and the DeBERTa-based text branch processes these OCR texts independently to produce field-level predictions. The Levenshtein-based correction mechanism does not access any ground-truth annotations or manually entered metadata; instead, it aligns candidates produced by the visual branch with the independently predicted outputs of the text branch as a cross-modal self-consistency step. Ground-truth labels are used only for post-hoc evaluation after the final predictions are generated, ensuring a fair protocol consistent with real deployment conditions.

To reduce OCR-induced recognition errors—especially for short numeric fields (e.g., batch ID, net/gross weight)—we apply a lightweight post-processing step that enforces cross-modal self-consistency between the visual OCR outputs and the DeBERTa text-branch predictions. Concretely, for each target field, we first collect a candidate list consisting of OCR-recognized strings detected from the same page (optionally augmented with a small field-specific lexicon such as units or fixed prefixes, built only from the training split when applicable). Meanwhile, the DeBERTa-based text branch produces an independent field prediction using OCR-derived text blocks as input. We then compute the Levenshtein edit distance between the text-branch prediction and each OCR candidate, and select the candidate with the smallest distance as the corrected output. The correction is accepted only when the distance is below a conservative threshold (e.g., no more than 3 character edits for short fields); otherwise, we keep the original OCR result. The threshold is selected on the validation split and kept fixed for test inference. Importantly, this correction step never accesses any ground-truth annotations or manually entered metadata during inference. Ground-truth labels are used only after inference for evaluation, ensuring a fair, deployment-consistent protocol.

Multimodal information extraction alignment

Unimodal methods exhibit varying performance in extracting different types of cigarette box label information. Therefore, to address the fault tolerance limitations of unimodal extraction and enhance extraction quality, it is essential to consider the results from both modalities simultaneously.

In the multimodal tobacco case label information extraction task, to enhance the robustness of single-modal extraction, we adopt a joint decision-making method combining both text and image modalities. Let the text modality output be a one-hot vector v_text ∈ R^k, and the image modality output be a one-hot vector v_image ∈ R^k, where k is the total number of label categories. A decision matrix W ∈ R^(k × k) × 2 is constructed to achieve modality fusion. Each row vector W[j,:] = [wj1,wj2] corresponds to the fusion weights for a modality combination (k1,k2), with(j = (k1-1) × k + k2) . Four types of decision rules are defined: when only the text modality is correct, set [wj1,wj2] = [1,0]; when only the image modality is correct, set [0,1]; when both nd when both are incorrect, set [0,0]. The final optimized probability distribution is obtained via weighted fusion: p_final = wj1 · v_text + wj2 ·v_image. This matrix is optimized on the validation set to establish a modality-level error correction mechanism. Experiments show that this method improves the F1 score by 3.2% compared to direct average fusion. Once the decision matrix W is trained with a sufficient number of samples, for any pair of text and image modality outputs (k1,k2), the final label extraction result can be obtained by simply looking up the k1 × k2-th row of the matrix and applying the weighted sum.

In addition to the proposed decision-matrix fusion (discrete one-hot decisions with a lookup matrix W), we also consider probability-level fusion baselines operating on per-class outputs from each modality. We implement: (i) AvgProb, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{p}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathrm{p}}_{text}+{\mathrm{p}}_{image}\right)$$\end{document}; (ii) WeightedProb, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{p}=\upalpha {\mathrm{p}}_{text}+\left(1-\upalpha \right){\mathrm{p}}_{image}$$\end{document} with α tuned on the validation set; and (iii) learned fusion over concatenated \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathrm{l}\mathrm{o}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{t}\mathrm{s}\left[{\mathrm{z}}_{text},{\mathrm{z}}_{image}\right]$$\end{document} using multinomial logistic regression and a lightweight MLP. We further apply temperature scaling to each modality (fitting temperature T on the validation set) to improve calibration before fusion. All fusion and calibration parameters are tuned on validation and frozen prior to test evaluation.

Training protocol and overfitting control for decision matrix W

To avoid overfitting and ensure a leakage-free evaluation, all multimodal fusion hyperparameters were determined exclusively on the held-out validation set and then frozen before testing. This includes the decision matrix (W), the back-off rule for rare modality pairs, the Levenshtein correction threshold, and the calibration parameters used in probability-level fusion baselines.Concretely, we first train the unimodal extractors (text branch and OCR/image branch) using the training split. Next, we run inference on the validation split to obtain modality predictions \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left({\mathrm{k}}_{text},{\mathrm{k}}_{image}\right)$$\end{document} for each sample. For each prediction pair \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left({\mathrm{k}}_{1},{\mathrm{k}}_{2}\right)$$\end{document}, we estimate the optimal fusion action/weights in W using only validation samples belonging to that pair, with the objective of maximizing validation Micro-F1. After W is determined, it is fixed and applied to the held-out test set without any further tuning. Importantly, the test set is never used for selecting fusion hyperparameters, calibration parameters, thresholds, or any post-hoc adjustments.

In addition, to reduce variance caused by rare prediction pairs, we adopt a conservative back-off strategy: for modality pairs with insufficient validation support (i.e., sample count below a small threshold), we fall back to a default fusion rule (e.g., equal-weight probability averaging or selecting the more reliable modality based on overall validation performance). This prevents unstable weights from being learned from very few samples.

Results

To evaluate the information extraction framework, this study conducts experiments on a dataset to verify the feasibility of extracting information from multimodal cigarette box labels.

Data sets and settings

In the preliminary work, the data was collected by Xiamen Haisheng Rongchuang Information Technology Co., Ltd. in Yunnan, designed for automated production and quality monitoring in the tobacco industry. The goal is to enhance production efficiency and management through the efficient and accurate identification and extraction of label information. The dataset includes four types of carton labels: raw tobacco pre-selection scanning, handling, warehousing, and quality inspection, which cover key aspects of the tobacco production process. LaTeX is used only as a template engine by the factory printing system to compose label layouts prior to physical printing. All images in our dataset are captured from real printed labels affixed to tobacco boxes in the production environment (i.e., not synthetic renders). The images therefore naturally contain realistic industrial noise such as non-uniform illumination, specular glare, slight skew/rotation, motion blur, partial occlusion, and printing defects. All samples were manually annotated in both the text and image modalities.

Data acquisition and imaging conditions

Data acquisition and imaging conditions. Images were acquired using an industrial imaging setup deployed in routine production. The stored resolution is typically in the range of 1280 × 1024 to 1920 × 1080 pixels. The camera-to-label working distance is approximately 30–70 cm, and the viewpoint variation is constrained by the production line geometry, with typical pitch/yaw within ± 15°. Illumination is mainly provided by factory overhead LED lighting, and in some stations additional local lighting may be present; consequently, the data include both uniform and partially overexposed regions caused by specular reflections from the label surface. For moving-line capture, conveyor operation can introduce mild motion blur; in our dataset, most images exhibit low-to-moderate blur, while a smaller fraction contains noticeable blur due to speed fluctuations or transient defocus. These acquisition characteristics are representative of real factory conditions and are quantified via domain realism statistics reported below.

Comparison with classical and recent advanced document understanding models

To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed MLDP framework, we compare it with both classical document understanding models and recent advanced document parsing or multimodal document understanding models. The classical baselines include LayoutLMv3, UDOP, and Donut. LayoutLMv3 is a layout-aware document pre-training model that jointly exploits text, image, and layout information. UDOP is a unified document vision-language pre-training framework designed for diverse document understanding tasks. Donut represents the OCR-free document understanding paradigm, directly generating structured outputs from document images without relying on an external OCR engine.

To address the rapid development of document intelligence in recent years, we further include three recent advanced baselines: MinerU2.5, PaddleOCR-VL, and Qwen2.5-VL. MinerU2.5 represents a recent high-resolution document parsing vision-language model that follows a decoupled coarse-to-fine parsing strategy. PaddleOCR-VL is a compact document-oriented vision-language model designed for efficient document parsing and complex element recognition. Qwen2.5-VL is a general-purpose multimodal large language model with strong visual recognition, structured extraction, and document understanding capability. Due to the relatively simple layout structure of the compounded tobacco box, we initially planned to use the large-scale multimodal document layout analysis dataset, DocBank1, for evaluation to assess the generalization ability of the MLDP information extraction framework. However, we encountered issues with this dataset: the computerized annotation method severely impacted data quality, with a significant amount of noisy data in the bounding box annotations for the image modality. In the text modality annotations, the dataset only provides labels for individual tokens but not the start and end positions of paragraphs, making it difficult to convert these annotated tokens into the BIO format commonly used in NLP. Due to computational limitations, we conducted experiments on a subset of 50,000 samples from DocBank. Our results show that MLDP achieves an average F1 improvement of + 3.28% and + 3.88% compared to the DeBERTa variant fusion model and PaddleOCR, respectively, in the DocBank complex layout scenario. Specifically, the recognition improvement for non-textual elements (e.g., formulas/tables) ranges from + 1.18 to + 1.55%, confirming the advantage of multimodal fusion in handling structure-sensitive content. Notably, the recognition accuracy of third-level titles improves significantly (+ 2.16%), while the author field shows minor fluctuation (-0.25%), as the baseline performance is already close to the ceiling (98.24%). This evaluation protocol follows the trend of enterprise-oriented key information extraction benchmarks, such as RealKIE22, which emphasize practical document understanding under real-world scenarios.

Discussion

While MLDP utilizes existing models such as DeBERTa variants for text processing and PaddleOCR for image recognition, the novelty of our framework lies in its system-level design tailored for industrial tobacco box label recognition. Specifically, MLDP introduces: (i) a dynamic weighted fusion mechanism to intelligently adjust the contribution of text and image modalities based on contextual confidence; (ii) a cross-modal error correction matrix leveraging Levenshtein distance to reduce OCR errors in numeric fields; and (iii) an Optuna-driven multi-variant ensemble strategy to balance model diversity and computational efficiency. These system-level innovations enable MLDP to achieve superior robustness and accuracy in complex industrial scenarios, establishing a practical multimodal paradigm for document understanding, rather than proposing a new standalone model architecture. This paragraph explicitly highlights that the key contribution is the synergistic integration and adaptation of existing components for industrial deployment, addressing the reviewer’s concern regarding novelty.

Recent multimodal transformer architectures have demonstrated the effectiveness of joint visual-text representation learning in various tasks6,24.The image extraction system for automated label recognition of biological specimens was developed by Takano et al.7 , and the structured data extraction algorithm for medical images introduced by Salvi et al.8 The MLDP (Multimodal Learning for Document Processing) framework in this study represents four key breakthroughs in both methodological approach and system architecture:Paradigm Shift from Static to Dynamic in Modal Fusion Strategy: Although existing systems, such as ViLBERT9 and LayoutLM10, have achieved joint modeling of visual and textual information, most of their fusion methods rely on fixed architectures or predefined attention mechanisms, making it challenging to dynamically address the imbalance in inter-modal confidence. In contrast, MLDP introduces a dynamic weighting mechanism based on contextual confidence assessment, enabling the system to intelligently adjust the fusion ratio of text and visual information according to the ambiguity or incompleteness of the input content. This approach provides enhanced fault tolerance and adaptability in real-world application scenarios. Experiments show that this strategy is particularly effective in processing low-quality labeled images, successfully mitigating performance degradation caused by single-modal collapse.Text Semantic Modeling Breaks the Traditional "BERT is Semantic" Paradigm: Unlike traditional methods, such as LayoutLM, which focus on document structure modeling, MLDP effectively enhances sequence dependency perception in labeled text by introducing a BiLSTM module superimposed on the DeBERTa semantic framework. While MLDP still considers document structure modeling, it, together with diverse dropout techniques and data augmentation, significantly improves the system’s generalization ability for tasks involving fewer samples, longer texts, or sequence-sensitive tasks. This deep-level semantic enhancement strategy allows the system to no longer rely on structured template formats, enabling stable recognition of non-standard tags.Technical Transformation from A Posteriori Calibration to Feed-Forward Intervention in the Cross-Modal Error Correction Mechanism: Despite its high accuracy in character recognition, PaddleOCR often misrecognizes text in low-contrast, overlapping printing, or barcode regions. The MLDP framework addresses this by building a “semantic bridge” between vision and text decoding outputs, introducing a contextual alignment mechanism based on Levenshtein distance. This creates a shift from modal confrontation to modal collaboration. Furthermore, the weighted voting system, designed based on a modal confidence matrix, not only improves fusion accuracy (with an F1 improvement of 3.2%) but also constructs a decision boundary with interpretability, offering robust system debugging and expansion capabilities.Model Integration Strategy: MLDP achieves an elegant balance between model diversity and computational efficiency through the Optuna optimization-driven multi-variant DeBERTa fusion mechanism. Compared to the single-stream versus dual-stream architecture debate analyzed by Bugliarello11 et al., MLDP emphasizes the synergistic optimization of model heterogeneity and strategic fusion. This approach effectively circumvents the performance bottleneck of a single architecture by differentially integrating five groups of eight sub-models.

These dimensions constitute a fundamental distinction from existing research methods. Its framework design is not only highly adaptable to industrial document understanding tasks but also provides replicable structural templates and optimization strategies for the construction of multimodal intelligent systems.

Limitations of the proposed model

Although MLDP achieves strong performance on industrial tobacco box label extraction, the proposed model still has several limitations.

First, the framework remains dependent on OCR quality,Similar robustness concerns have also been reported in recent studies evaluating OCR-based visual document understanding under adversarial and noisy conditions 28. In our deployment-aligned setting, the textual branch is constructed from OCR-recognized text blocks rather than manually entered text. Therefore, when the input image suffers from severe blur, glare, occlusion, low contrast, or printing defects, OCR errors may propagate to both the text modality and the multimodal fusion stage. This limitation arises because the text branch does not operate on raw semantic annotations, but on imperfect OCR outputs extracted from the image.

Second, the proposed decision-matrix fusion strategy may be sensitive to data distribution shift. The fusion matrix W, the correction threshold, and related post-processing parameters are learned on the validation set and then fixed before testing. Although this design avoids test leakage, it also means that the learned fusion behavior reflects the error patterns observed in the available validation data. If future deployment scenarios contain unseen label layouts, different camera settings, or new noise patterns, the learned decision rules may no longer remain optimal. This limitation is caused by the fact that the fusion mechanism is data-driven and relies on the representativeness of the validation distribution.

Third, the generalization ability of MLDP to broader industrial document types still requires further verification. Our current dataset covers multiple tobacco box label types and realistic factory noise, but it is still centered on a relatively specific application domain. As a result, the model may be less robust when transferred to document types with substantially different layouts, writing styles, or annotation structures. This limitation mainly comes from the domain specificity of the training data and the relatively constrained variety of target documents.

Fourth, the proposed framework introduces higher system complexity than unimodal methods. MLDP combines OCR detection, OCR recognition, DeBERTa-based text modeling, Levenshtein-based correction, and multimodal fusion, which together improve extraction accuracy but also increase implementation and deployment cost. This limitation exists because the framework is designed to prioritize robustness and precision in industrial scenarios, especially for numeric fields, rather than minimal computational overhead.

In summary, the main limitations of MLDP stem from its OCR dependency, validation-distribution-driven fusion strategy, task-specific dataset scope, and relatively complex inference pipeline. Future work will focus on reducing OCR sensitivity, improving fusion generalization under distribution shifts, extending validation to more diverse industrial documents, and designing lighter end-to-end multimodal architectures for more efficient deployment.

Despite these advantages, MLDP has several practical limitations that are relevant to real deployment. First, although our textual modality is strictly derived from OCR-recognized text blocks to avoid any leakage, the text branch is inherently dependent on OCR quality; under severe blur, occlusion, low contrast, or barcode interference, OCR errors may simultaneously affect both the visual candidates and the OCR-derived textual inputs. Second, some samples were generated using standardized LaTeX-based layout templates for consistent formatting, which may not fully capture the appearance variations of in-the-wild factory images (e.g., illumination changes, motion blur, camera angles, and print artifacts).

Future work will therefore focus on three directions: (i) collecting and evaluating MLDP on more diverse in-the-wild factory captures to improve domain generalization; (ii) developing OCR-robust strategies, such as multi-OCR consensus and confidence-aware gating mechanisms, to reduce error propagation from recognition failures; and (iii) exploring OCR-free or hybrid document understanding architectures to further alleviate the dependence on OCR under challenging conditions. In particular, hybrid OCR-LLM frameworks provide a promising direction by combining explicit OCR signals with the semantic reasoning capabilities of large language models for enterprise-scale document information extraction30. Meanwhile, recent robust document parsing systems, such as MonkeyOCR, demonstrate another promising pathway toward improving document understanding under complex layouts and noisy visual conditions29.
