A solution to the Frankl–Kupavskii conjecture on the Erdős–Kleitman matching problem
Cheng Chi, Yan Wang

Abstract
For integers n ≥ s ≥ 2 n\geq s\geq 2 , let e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) be the maximum size of a family ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} with no s s pairwise disjoint members. The problem of determining e ⁡ ( n , s ) e(n,s) is closely related to its uniform counterpart, the Erdős Matching Conjecture. Frankl and Kupavskii conjectured an exact formula for e ⁡ ( ( m + 1 ) ​ s − ℓ , s ) e((m+1)s-\ell,s) when 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ ⌈ s / 2 ⌉ 1\leq\ell\leq\lceil s/2\rceil . We prove that for every fixed m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 and sufficiently large s s , the extremal families for e ⁡ ( ( m + 1 ) ​ s − ℓ , s ) e((m+1)s-\ell,s) are P ⁡ ( m , s , ℓ , L ) ≔ { A ⊆ [ n ] : | A | + | A ∩ L | ≥ m + 1 } ​ for some L with ​ | L | = ℓ − 1 P(m,s,\ell;L)\coloneqq\{A\subseteq[n]\colon|A|+|A\cap L|\geq m+1\}\text{ for some $L$ with }|L|=\ell-1 when 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ ( m + 1 2 ​ m + 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ s 1\leq\ell\leq(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}-o(1))s . In particular, this confirms the Frankl–Kupavskii conjecture for every fixed m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 and all sufficiently large s s . For m = 3 m=3 and sufficiently large s s , we determine the exact range of ℓ \ell for which P ⁡ ( 3 , s , ℓ , L ) P(3,s,\ell;L) is extremal, generalizing a theorem of Kupavskii and Sokolov.

Introduction

Let
[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
A
family
is a collection of sets.
For a set
X
and an integer
k
, we write
\binom{X}{k}
for the family of
all
k
-element subsets of
X
, and we write

\binom{X}{\geq k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geq k}\binom{X}{i}
.
Given an integer
n
, we also write
\binom{n}{\geq k}
for
\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}
.
A
matching
in a family is a collection of pairwise disjoint sets.
An
s
-
matching
is a matching of size
s
.
Given a family
\mathcal{F}
, its
matching number

\nu(\mathcal{F})
is the size of a
largest matching contained in
\mathcal{F}
.

Extremal problems concerning matching numbers in set systems date back to the classical work of Erdős in the 1960s.
One of the representative results is the classical Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
4
]
.
Erdős
[
2
]
proposed the following problem: determining the largest possible size of a
k
-uniform family that contains no given number of
pairwise disjoint members.
This problem is now known as the Erdős Matching Conjecture.

For integers
N,k,t
with
N\geq kt
, write

e_{k}(N,t)\coloneqq\max\left\{|\mathcal{G}|:\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}\text{ and }\nu(\mathcal{G})<t\right\}.

The Erdős Matching Conjecture
[
2
]
asserts that

e_{k}(N,t)=\max\left\{\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t+1}{k},\,\binom{kt-1}{k}\right\}.

Erdős also confirmed the conjecture holds for sufficiently large
N
when
k
and
t
are fixed
[
2
]
.
The conjecture is known for
k=2
as the Erdős–Gallai theorem

[
3
]
.
For
k=3
, it was proved for sufficiently large
N
by Łuczak and Mieczkowska
[
14
]
and then fully solved by Frankl
[
5
]
.
Despite this substantial progress, the full conjecture remains open for every
k\geq 4
.

In this paper, we study the non-uniform analogue of the Erdős Matching Conjecture.
For integers
n\geq s\geq 2
, define

e(n,s)\coloneqq\max\{|\mathcal{F}|:\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}\text{ and }\nu(\mathcal{F})<s\}.

The study of this problem, now called the Erdős–Kleitman problem, goes back to a question of Erdős answered by Kleitman, who determined
e(sm-1,s)
and
e(sm,s)
for all
m,s\geq 1

[
10
]
.
In contrast with these two residue classes, the other values of
e(n,s)
remain unknown and may depend heavily on
n\pmod{s}
.

The following construction explains the connection between the Erdős–Kleitman problem and its uniform analogue.
Write
n=(m+1)s-\ell
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{m}
contains no
\ell
pairwise disjoint
members.
Then the lifted family
\mathcal{G}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq m+1}
contains no
s
pairwise disjoint members.
Indeed, if an
s
-matching in this lifted family uses
q
sets from
\mathcal{G}
, then its total number of vertices is at least
qm+(s-q)(m+1)=n+\ell-q
.
Since the ground set has size
n
, this forces
q\geq\ell
, which would give an
\ell
-matching in
\mathcal{G}
.
Thus, when
n=(m+1)s-\ell
, an
m
-uniform family with no
\ell
-matching can be lifted to a non-uniform family with no
s
-matching by adding all sets of size at least
m+1
.
However, this lifting only accounts for the
m
-th layer together with all higher layers.
The construction below contains lower-layer sets, which makes the Erdős-Kleitman problem more difficult.
For each
L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
, define

P(m,s,\ell;L)\coloneqq\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|+|A\cap L|\geq m+1\}.

The size of this family is independent of the choice of
L
and we denote this
size by
|P(m,s,\ell)|
.

The family
P(m,s,\ell;L)
has no
s
pairwise disjoint members.
Indeed, if
A_{1},\ldots,A_{s}
were pairwise disjoint members of
P(m,s,\ell;L)
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{s}|A_{i}|\leq n=(m+1)s-\ell
and
\sum_{i=1}^{s}|A_{i}\cap L|\leq|L|=\ell-1
.
Adding the two inequalities gives
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\bigl(|A_{i}|+|A_{i}\cap L|\bigr)\leq(m+1)s-1
, contradicting the condition that

|A_{i}|+|A_{i}\cap L|\geq m+1
for every
i
.

Thus
P(m,s,\ell;L)
gives a natural lower bound for
e(n,s)
.
Frankl and Kupavskii conjectured that, in the following range, this lower bound is always sharp.

Conjecture 1.1

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose that
s\geq 2
,
m\geq 1
, and

n=(m+1)s-\ell
for some integer
\ell
with

1\leq\ell\leq\left\lceil s/2\right\rceil
.
Then

e(n,s)=|P(m,s,\ell)|.

They
[
6
]
also confirmed the conjecture for several
(s,\ell)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
6
]
)
.

e((m+1)s-\ell,s)=|P(m,s,\ell)|
holds in each of the following cases:

1.

\ell=2
and
s\geq 5
, and also
\ell=2
,
s=4
when
m
is even;

2.

m=1
;

3.

s\geq\ell m+3\ell+3
.

The case
m=2
was completely solved recently by Kupavskii and Sokolov

[
11
]
.
Thus
m=3
is the first unresolved value in the conjectured range.
Very recently, Kupavskii and Sokolov also determined the extremal families for
e(sm+c,s)
when
c
is sufficiently small compared to
s

[
12
]
.
Moreover, they show that
Section

1
holds for every
m
, every
\ell
with
1\leq\ell\leq(1/2-o(1))s
and sufficiently large
s

[
13
]
.
Therefore,
Section

1
holds asymptotically, provided that
s
is sufficiently large.

Our main contribution is to prove that, for every fixed
m\geq 3
and all
sufficiently large
s
, the family
P(m,s,\ell;L)
is the
unique extremal family when
1\leq\ell\leq(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}-o(1))s
.
This range properly contains the conjectured interval

1\leq\ell\leq\left\lceil s/2\right\rceil
and extends beyond it.
Thus we give an affirmative answer to
Section

1
due to Frankl-Kupavskii in a strong sense.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix
m\geq 3
and
\varepsilon>0
. There is an integer

s_{0}=s_{0}(m,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for all
s\geq s_{0}
.
Suppose

1\leq\ell\leq\left(\frac{m+1}{2m+1}-\varepsilon\right)s,\qquad n=(m+1)s-\ell.

If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P(m,s,\ell)|.

Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=P(m,s,\ell;L)

for some
L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
.

The general theorem is not sharp when
m=3
.
In this case, the exact three-uniform matching theorem allows the comparison to continue until a second natural construction becomes competitive.
Let
n=4s-\ell
for some
\ell
with
1\leq\ell\leq s
and
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
. Define

P^{\prime}(s,\ell;L^{\prime})=\binom{L^{\prime}}{3}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq 4}.

Its size is independent of the choice of
L^{\prime}
and we denote this size by
|P^{\prime}(s,\ell)|
.

Since
P(3,s,\ell;L)
and
P^{\prime}(s,\ell;L^{\prime})
agree on all layers of size at least
4
, it suffices to compare the size of third layers of
P(3,s,\ell;L)
and
P^{\prime}(s,\ell;L^{\prime})
, together with the additional contribution from the second layer of
P(3,s,\ell;L)
.
Let
t(s)
be the root of
|P(3,s,\ell)|=|P^{\prime}(s,\ell)|
with
t(s)>1
.
A direct calculation gives

t(s)\coloneqq\frac{17-18s+\sqrt{49-852s+1284s^{2}}}{20}=\frac{\sqrt{321}-9}{10}s+O(1)\approx 0.8916s+O(1).

For
m=3
, this leads to the following sharper theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists an integer
s_{0}
such that the following statements hold for
all
s,\ell
with
s\geq s_{0}
and
1\leq\ell\leq t(s)
.
Write
n\coloneqq 4s-\ell
.
If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, then

|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P(3,s,\ell)|.

Moreover, when
\ell<t(s)
, equality holds if and only if

\mathcal{F}=P(3,s,\ell;L)
for some
L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
.
If
t(s)
is an integer and
\ell=t(s)
, equality holds if and only if
either
\mathcal{F}=P(3,s,\ell;L)
for some

L\in\binom{[n]}{\ell-1}
or

\mathcal{F}=P^{\prime}(s,\ell;L^{\prime})
for some
L^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
.

The two quantities
|P(3,s,\ell)|
and

|P^{\prime}(s,\ell)|
are equal precisely at the two roots
\ell=1
and
\ell=t(s)
.
Moreover,
|P(3,s,\ell)|>|P^{\prime}(s,\ell)|
for
1<\ell<t(s)
, while

|P(3,s,\ell)|<|P^{\prime}(s,\ell)|
when
s\geq\ell>t(s)
.
Therefore, for
t(s)<\ell\leq s
, the construction
P^{\prime}(s,\ell;L^{\prime})
is better
than
P(3,s,\ell;L)
while still avoiding an
s
-matching, so

P(3,s,\ell;L)
is not extremal.
Thus
Theorem

1.4
determines the exact range of
\ell
for which

P(3,s,\ell;L)
is extremal.

Definitions and lemmas

In this section, we collect some useful notation and several lemmas.
For a family
G
, let
\tau(G)
denote its vertex-cover number, namely the minimum size of a set of vertices meeting every member of
G
; we use the convention
\tau(\emptyset)=0
.

We first record several theorems concerning the Erdős Matching Conjecture.

Lemma 2.1

(Erdős and Gallai
[
3
]
)
.

If
G\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}
with
\nu(G)\leq s
, then

|G|\leq\max\left\{\binom{2s+1}{2},\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-s}{2}\right\}.

Theorem 2.2

(Erdős
[
2
]
)
.

Fix integers
k\geq 2
and
t\geq 1
. There exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(k,t)
such that
the following holds for all
N\geq N_{0}
. If

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<t
, then

|\mathcal{G}|\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t+1}{k}.

Theorem 2.3

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
8
]
)
.

Fix an integer
k\geq 3
. Then there exists
s_{\mathrm{FK}}=s_{\mathrm{FK}}(k)
such that the
following holds for every integer
s\geq s_{\mathrm{FK}}
. If

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
with
\nu(\mathcal{G})\leq s
and

n\geq\frac{5}{3}ks-\frac{2}{3}s,

then

|\mathcal{G}|\leq\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s}{k}.

Theorem 2.4

(Frankl
[
5
]
)
.

Let
N
and
t
be positive integers with
N\geq 3t-1
.
If
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{3}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<t
, then

|\mathcal{G}|\leq\max\left\{\binom{N}{3}-\binom{N-t+1}{3},\binom{3t-1}{3}\right\}.

Moreover, if

\binom{3t-1}{3}>\binom{N}{3}-\binom{N-t+1}{3}

and equality holds with
|\mathcal{G}|=\binom{3t-1}{3}
, then

\mathcal{G}=\binom{U}{3}

for some
U\in\binom{[N]}{3t-1}
.

The following corollary combines the preceding results in the form needed below.
The point of this corollary is that it allows the matching number
t
to be small.

Corollary 2.5
.

Fix
k\geq 2
and
\rho>0
. There exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(k,\rho)
such that the
following holds for all integers
N\geq N_{0}
and
t\geq 1
. If

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{G})<t
and

N\geq\left(\frac{5k-2}{3}+\rho\right)t,

then

|\mathcal{G}|\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t+1}{k}.

Proof
.

If
t=1
, then
\nu(\mathcal{G})<1
, so
\mathcal{G}=\emptyset
and the desired bound is
immediate.

If
k=2
, then
Section

2
gives
|\mathcal{G}|\leq\max\{\binom{2t-1}{2},\binom{N}{2}-\binom{N-t+1}{2}\}
.
The second term dominates the first whenever

N\geq\frac{5t-2}{2}.

This follows from
N\geq(8/3+\rho)t
.
Hence the required bound holds for
k=2
.

Now suppose
k\geq 3
.
Let
s_{\mathrm{FK}}(k)
be the threshold in
Theorem
2.3
, and put
t_{0}=s_{\mathrm{FK}}(k)+1
.
If
t\geq t_{0}
, then
t-1\geq s_{\mathrm{FK}}(k)
and

\nu(\mathcal{G})<t
gives
\nu(\mathcal{G})\leq t-1
. Moreover, the hypothesis gives

N\geq\left(\frac{5k-2}{3}+\rho\right)t>\frac{5k-2}{3}(t-1),

so all hypotheses of Theorem
2.3
, with matching parameter

t-1
, are satisfied. The theorem yields

|\mathcal{G}|\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t+1}{k}.

It remains to handle
2\leq t<t_{0}
. For each such
t
,
Theorem
2.2
gives the same conclusion once

N\geq N_{0}(k,t)
. Taking the maximum of these finitely many thresholds
gives a single
N_{0}(k,\rho)
.
∎

For integers
k\geq 3
and
N\geq u+k
, put

h_{k}(N,u)\coloneqq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-u}{k}+1-\binom{N-u-k}{k-1}.

Now we record the following Hilton-Milner type results.
They can be seen as stability results concerning the Erdős Matching Conjecture.

Theorem 2.6

(Guo, Lu and Mao
[
9
]
)
.

There exists an integer
N_{0}
such that the following holds for all
integers
N\geq N_{0}
and
u\geq 1
with
N\geq 3u+2
.
If
G\subseteq\binom{[N]}{3}
satisfies

\nu(G)\leq u<\tau(G),

then

|G|\leq\max\left\{h_{3}(N,u),\binom{3u+2}{3}\right\}.

Theorem 2.7

(Frankl and Kupavskii
[
7
]
)
.

Suppose that
k\geq 3
. For every fixed
\eta>0
there exists an integer

s_{1}=s_{1}(k,\eta)
such that the following holds. Let
n
and
s
be
integers, and let
G\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
satisfy

\nu(G)=s<\tau(G)
. If either

n\geq(s+\max\{25,2s+2\})k,

or

s\geq s_{1}(k,\eta)\qquad\text{and}\qquad n\geq(2+\eta)sk,

then
|G|\leq h_{k}(n,s)
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Fix
k\geq 3
and
\rho>0
. There exists a constant

N_{0}=N_{0}(k,\rho)
such that the following holds for all integers

N\geq N_{0}
and
t\geq 1
. If

G\subseteq\binom{[N]}{k}
satisfies
\tau(G)>t\geq\nu(G)
and
N\geq(2k+\rho)t
, then

|G|\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t}{k}-\binom{N-t-k}{k-1}+N^{k-2}.

Proof
.

Put
u\coloneqq\nu(G)
. Since
\tau(G)>t\geq u
, we have
u<\tau(G)
.
Also
u\geq 1
, because otherwise
G=\emptyset
and
\tau(G)=0
.

We first verify that Theorem
2.7
applies to
G
with matching
parameter
u
. Let
\eta=\rho/k
, and let
u_{1}=s_{1}(k,\eta)
be the
threshold in the second part of Theorem
2.7
. If
u\geq u_{1}
,
then

N\geq(2k+\rho)t\geq(2k+\rho)u=\left(2+\frac{\rho}{k}\right)ku=(2+\eta)ku,

so the second part of Theorem
2.7
applies. If

1\leq u<u_{1}
, then the first part applies once

N\geq k\bigl(u+\max\{25,2u+2\}\bigr).

We therefore choose
N_{0}=N_{0}(k,\rho)\geq 1
; when
u_{1}>1
, we also require

N_{0}\geq\max_{1\leq v<u_{1}}k\bigl(v+\max\{25,2v+2\}\bigr).

Thus, for all
N\geq N_{0}
,
Theorem
2.7
gives

|G|\leq h_{k}(N,u)=\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-u}{k}+1-\binom{N-u-k}{k-1}.

Since
u\leq t
, we have
\binom{N-u}{k}\geq\binom{N-t}{k}
and
\binom{N-u-k}{k-1}\geq\binom{N-t-k}{k-1}
.
Therefore

|G|\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t}{k}-\binom{N-t-k}{k-1}+1\leq\binom{N}{k}-\binom{N-t}{k}-\binom{N-t-k}{k-1}+N^{k-2}.

This proves the claim.
∎

The next input converts the absence of a perfect matching into a lower bound on missing edges.
It is the mechanism that later pays for extra low-layer sets.

Lemma 2.9

(Baranyai
[
1
]
)
.

For integers
q,t\geq 1
, the complete
q
-uniform hypergraph on
qt

vertices can be decomposed into
\binom{qt-1}{q-1}
perfect matchings.

We use this consequence in the following blocker lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
q\geq 2
, let
G\subseteq\binom{X}{q}
, let
|X|=qt+d
with
t\geq 1

and
d\geq 0
, and put
Z\coloneqq\binom{X}{q}\setminus G
.
If
\nu(G)<t
, then

|Z|\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{t}\binom{qt+d}{q},\binom{d+q}{q}\right\}.

Proof
.

Let
U
be a uniformly random
qt
-subset of
X
. By
Lemma
2
, the family
\binom{U}{q}
decomposes into
\binom{qt-1}{q-1}
perfect matchings. Since
\nu(G)<t
, no such perfect matching can lie entirely in
G
.
Therefore

\left|Z\cap\binom{U}{q}\right|\geq\binom{qt-1}{q-1}.

Taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}\left|Z\cap\binom{U}{q}\right|=|Z|\,\frac{\binom{qt+d-q}{qt-q}}{\binom{qt+d}{qt}}\geq\binom{qt-1}{q-1}.

Hence

|Z|\geq\frac{\binom{qt+d}{qt}\binom{qt-1}{q-1}}{\binom{qt+d-q}{qt-q}}=\frac{1}{t}\binom{qt+d}{q}.

Let
M
be a maximum matching in
G
. Then
|M|\leq t-1
, and by
maximality every edge of
G
meets
V(M)
. Therefore every
q
-set
contained in
X\setminus V(M)
belongs to
Z
. Since

|X\setminus V(M)|\geq qt+d-q(t-1)=d+q,

we obtain
|Z|\geq\binom{d+q}{q}
.
∎

Comparison setup

Now we record the notation and assumptions used in the proof of

Theorem

1.3
.
The proof of
Theorem

1.4
in
Section

5
will use the same notation, but with a different
range for
\ell
.
Assume from now on that
\varepsilon
is given with
0<\varepsilon<(m+1)/(2m+1)
.
Put

\theta_{m}\coloneqq\frac{m+1}{2m+1}
and
\gamma\coloneqq\theta_{m}-\varepsilon
.
Choose
s_{0}=s_{0}(m,\varepsilon)
sufficiently large.
Let
s,\ell
be integers with
s\geq s_{0}
and
1\leq\ell\leq\gamma s
.
Let
n=(m+1)s-\ell
.
Put

a\coloneqq\ell-1
and
r\coloneqq n-a=n-\ell+1=(m+1)s-2\ell+1
.

Given a family
\mathcal{A}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
and a positive integer

\mathcal{A}_{i}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}\cap\binom{[n]}{i},\quad\mathcal{A}_{\geq k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=k}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i},\quad\mathcal{A}_{\leq k}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{A}_{i}.

For a set

, write
\mathcal{A}(\overline{E})\coloneqq\{Q\in\mathcal{A}:Q\cap E=\emptyset\}
for the subfamily of
\mathcal{A}
disjoint from

.
Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family with
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
.
Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the complement family of
\mathcal{F}
, that is,
\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq 2^{[n]}\setminus\mathcal{F}
.
Later, we shall frequently use the notation
\mathcal{F}_{i},\mathcal{Y}_{i},\mathcal{F}_{\leq m},\mathcal{F}_{\geq m+1},\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}
.

For a set
E
with
|E|\leq m
, define its
deficit
by

\Delta(E)\coloneqq m+1-|E|.

Thus the empty set has deficit
m+1
, a singleton has deficit
m
, an

(m-1)
-set has deficit
2
, and an
m
-set has deficit
1
.
For a family
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{\leq m}
, define its deficit by

\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\coloneqq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\Delta(Q).

For
0\leq j\leq m
, the number of
j
-sets in the canonical family
P(m,s,\ell;L)
is

\Lambda_{j}(a,r)\coloneqq\left|\left\{E\in\binom{[n]}{j}:|E\cap L|\geq m+1-j\right\}\right|=\sum_{u=m+1-j}^{j}\binom{a}{u}\binom{r}{j-u},

where impossible binomial coefficients are interpreted as zero. In particular,

\Lambda_{0}(a,r)=0,\qquad\Lambda_{m}(a,r)=\sum_{u=1}^{m}\binom{a}{u}\binom{r}{m-u}=\binom{n}{m}-\binom{r}{m}.

Put
\Lambda\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{m}\Lambda_{j}(a,r)
.
Thus
\Lambda
is the total contribution of the canonical family in the layers of size at most
m
.
Because
P(m,s,\ell;L)
contains every set of size at least
m+1
, the global comparison reduces to a single inequality: any surplus of
\mathcal{F}
in the first
m
layers must be paid for by missing sets of
\mathcal{F}
in the layers of size at least
m+1
.
Consequently,

|P(m,s,\ell)|=\Lambda+\sum_{i\geq m+1}\binom{n}{i}.

This reduction is captured by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|
,
then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P(m,s,\ell)|
.

Proof
.

Since

|\mathcal{F}_{\geq m+1}|=\sum_{i\geq m+1}\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|,

we have

|\mathcal{F}|=|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|+|\mathcal{F}_{\geq m+1}|\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|+\left(\sum_{i\geq m+1}\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|\right)=|P(m,s,\ell)|.

This proves the desired result.
∎

The next lemma records the coefficient comparison used below.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
0\leq\alpha\leq\theta_{m}-\varepsilon
,

\frac{(m+1-2\alpha)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}>\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{(m+1-\alpha)^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}

with a positive gap depending only on
m
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof
.

It is enough to prove the inequality at the larger endpoint

\alpha=\theta_{m}
. Indeed, the left-hand side is strictly decreasing in

\alpha
, while the right-hand side is strictly increasing on

[0,\theta_{m}]
. Therefore the inequality at
\alpha=\theta_{m}
implies it
throughout
[0,\theta_{m}-\varepsilon]
.
At this endpoint the desired inequality is equivalent to

(2m-1)^{m-1}>(m-1)2^{m-3}m^{m-2}.

Since
2m-1>2(m-1)
, the left-hand side is larger than

2^{m-1}(m-1)^{m-1}
. It is therefore enough to prove

4(m-1)^{m-2}>m^{m-2}.

For
m=3
this is
8>3
, while for
m\geq 4
it follows from
\left(\frac{m}{m-1}\right)^{m-2}<e<4
.
The strict inequality at the endpoint
and compactness of
[0,\theta_{m}-\varepsilon]
give the required positive gap.
∎

The deficit notation measures how many vertices are saved by using a low-layer set instead of an
(m+1)
-set.
If a disjoint collection of low-layer sets saves at least
\ell
vertices in total, then the remaining vertices have exactly the form required by the blocker lemma, and this forces many missing
(m+1)
-sets.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix an integer
m\geq 3
and a real number
0<\lambda<1
. There are
constants
c_{0}=c_{0}(m,\lambda)>0
and

such that
the following holds for all

1\leq\ell\leq\lambda s,\qquad n=(m+1)s-\ell.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
. If there is a
pairwise disjoint family
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}
such that

\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\geq\ell,

then
|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq c_{0}s^{m}
.

Proof
.

Choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily, still denoted by

\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\}
, with
\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\geq\ell
. Since the
chosen sets have size at most
m
, each deficit lies between
1
and

m+1
. Minimality gives
\ell\leq\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\leq\ell+m
. Moreover,
each
Q_{i}
has deficit at least one. If
q>\ell
, then after removing
any one of the
Q_{i}
the remaining family has deficit at least

, contradicting minimality. Hence

. Write

\Delta(\mathcal{Q})=\ell+d
, where

.
This choice ensures that the selected low-layer sets use exactly

(m+1)q-\Delta(\mathcal{Q})
vertices, so the remaining vertex set has the cardinality
required by the blocker lemma.

The sets

use

\sum_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}|=(m+1)q-\Delta(\mathcal{Q})

vertices. Let

be the remaining vertex set. Then

|W|=n-\bigl((m+1)q-\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\bigr)=(m+1)(s-q)+d.

Put

. Since

,

If
\mathcal{F}_{m+1}[W]

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq\frac{1}{t}\binom{(m+1)t+d}{m+1}\geq t^{m}\geq(1-\lambda)^{m}s^{m}.

The lemma holds with

.
∎

L_{m}\geq\frac{(m+1-2\alpha)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}s^{m-1}-O_{m,\varepsilon}(s^{m-2}).

(4.7)

In particular,
L_{m}>0
by the choice of
s_{0}
.

There are two possibilities. Either the low layers themselves contain enough
disjoint deficit to force many missing high-layer sets, or they do not. The
first possibility is dominated by
|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|
; the second forces
the
(m-1)
-layer to have small matching number.

We first consider the case in which there is a pairwise disjoint family

\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{m-1}

\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\geq\ell
.
By Lemma

,
|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq c_{0}s^{m}
.
On the other hand, since

,

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{n}{j}=O_{m}(s^{m-1}).

Thus, by the choice of
s_{0}
,

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{n}{j}<c_{0}s^{m}\leq|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|.

Using (
4.6
) and
L_{m}>0
, we obtain

|\mathcal{F}_{m}|<\binom{n}{m}-\binom{r}{m}=\Lambda_{m}(a,r).

Therefore

|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|=|\mathcal{F}_{m}|+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|<\Lambda_{m}(a,r)+|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|.

Thus (
4.1
) holds strictly in this subcase.

Hence, assume that there is no pairwise disjoint family

\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{0}\cup\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{F}_{m-1}

such that
\Delta(\mathcal{Q})\geq\ell
.
We now bound the lower layers. Put
T\coloneqq\left\lfloor a/2\right\rfloor
.
Since every
(m-1)
-set has deficit
2
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}
cannot
contain
T+1
pairwise disjoint members.
Hence
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})\leq T
.

If
a=1
, then
T=0
, so

\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})=0
and hence
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}=\emptyset
. Suppose
a\geq 2
.
Then
T+1\leq a
, and by (
4.5
),

\frac{n}{T+1}\geq\frac{n}{a}\geq 2m+\rho_{2}\geq\frac{5(m-1)-2}{3}+\rho_{2}.

By the choice of
s_{0}
, we also have
n\geq N_{0}(m-1,\rho_{2})
. Therefore
Corollary
2
applies to
\mathcal{F}_{m-1}
with

,

, and
t=T+1
. Since

\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m-1})<T+1
, it gives

|\mathcal{F}_{m-1}|\leq\binom{n}{m-1}-\binom{n-T}{m-1}.

This bound is also valid when
a=1
, because then both sides are zero.

Using

\binom{n}{m-1}-\binom{n-T}{m-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{T-1}\binom{n-1-i}{m-2}\leq T\binom{n}{m-2},

together with

T\leq\frac{a}{2}<\frac{\ell}{2}=\frac{\alpha s}{2}
and

,
we obtain

|\mathcal{F}_{m-1}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{(m+1-\alpha)^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}s^{m-1}.

(4.8)

It remains to compare the loss
L_{m}
in the
m
-layer with the largest possible contribution from the
(m-1)
-layer. After
normalizing by
s^{m-1}
, this is exactly the following coefficient
comparison.
Define

\Phi(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{(m+1-2\alpha)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{(m+1-\alpha)^{m-2}}{(m-2)!}.

Combining (
4.7
) and
(
4.8
), we get

L_{m}-|\mathcal{F}_{m-1}|\geq\Phi(\alpha)s^{m-1}-O_{m,\varepsilon}(s^{m-2}).

By Lemma
3
, there is a constant

c_{1}=c_{1}(m,\varepsilon)>0
such that
\Phi(\alpha)\geq c_{1}
for all
\alpha\leq\gamma
.
Thus, by the choice of
s_{0}
, we obtain

L_{m}-|\mathcal{F}_{m-1}|\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2}s^{m-1}.

By the choice of
s_{0}
again,

\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\binom{n}{j}<\frac{c_{1}}{2}s^{m-1}.

Therefore

L_{m}>|\mathcal{F}_{m-1}|+\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\binom{n}{j}\geq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|.

Finally, using (

),

|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|=|\mathcal{F}_{m}|+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|\leq\binom{n}{m}-\binom{r}{m}-L_{m}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|<\binom{n}{m}-\binom{r}{m}=\Lambda_{m}(a,r)\leq\Lambda\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|.

Thus (
4.1
) holds strictly in Case II.

4.3
Proof of Case III

In this case,
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m})\geq\ell
.
Write
q\coloneqq\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m})=\ell+d
for some

.
Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, we have
q\leq s-1
. Put

Choose
q

-sets

.
Let

Then

If

(m+1)
-sets, then

\mathcal{F}
. Hence

Since

, we have

. Applying

to

gives

Let

Then

. Since
\ell\leq\gamma s
, we have

Choose

such that

, which is possible because

(4.10)

We now split according to the excess

.

Subcase 1:
d\leq Kb^{m/(m+1)}
.

Since
b\leq s
, this gives

Also

q+1=\ell+d+1\leq\gamma s+o(s)
and
n=(m+1)s-\ell\geq(m+1-\gamma)s
.
Since

\frac{m+1-\gamma}{\gamma}>\frac{5m-2}{3},

we may choose
\rho_{4}=\rho_{4}(m,\varepsilon)>0
such that, by the choice of

s_{0}
,

and
n\geq N_{0}(m,\rho_{4})
,
where

is the threshold in Corollary
2
.
Since
\nu(\mathcal{F}_{m})=q
, Corollary

gives

|\mathcal{F}_{m}|\leq\binom{n}{m}-\binom{n-q}{m}=\binom{n}{m}-\binom{r-1-d}{m}.

Since

, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{m}|-\Lambda_{m}(a,r)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{r}{m}-\binom{r-1-d}{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\binom{r-1-i}{m-1}\leq(d+1)\binom{r}{m-1}=O_{m,\varepsilon}\bigl((d+1)s^{m-1}\bigr).

The layers below

contribute at most

\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}|\mathcal{F}_{j}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\binom{n}{j}=O_{m}(s^{m-1}).

Therefore

|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|-\Lambda\leq O_{m,\varepsilon}\bigl((d+2)s^{m-1}\bigr)=O_{m,\varepsilon}\bigl(s^{m-1+m/(m+1)}\bigr)=o(s^{m}).

(4.11)

On the other hand, since
q\geq\ell
, any
\ell
of the sets

Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
form a pairwise disjoint subfamily
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{m}
with

\Delta(\mathcal{Q})=\ell
. Lemma
3
gives

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq c_{0}s^{m}

for some
c_{0}=c_{0}(m,\varepsilon)>0
. Combining this with
(
4.11
), and enlarging

if necessary, we get

|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|<\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|.

Thus (
4.1
) holds strictly in this subcase.

Subcase 2:
d>Kb^{m/(m+1)}
.

In this case, (
4.9
) gives

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\geq\binom{d+m+1}{m+1}\geq\frac{d^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}.

Since

and

, the choice of

in
(

) implies

|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|>\frac{K^{m+1}(1-\gamma)^{m}}{(m+1)!}s^{m}>Cs^{m}\geq|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|.

Hence

|\mathcal{F}_{\leq m}|<|\mathcal{Y}_{m+1}|\leq\Lambda+|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|.

Thus (
4.1
) holds strictly in this subcase.

This completes Case III.

4.4
Completion of the proof and equality cases

The three cases prove (
4.1
) for every family with

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
. Hence

3
gives

|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P(m,s,\ell)|
. It remains to discuss equality.

Suppose first that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
and

. The good-set subcase of Case I and Cases II and
III are strict. Hence equality can occur only in the no-good subcase of

) forces
|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq m+1}|=0
and

.
Moreover equality must hold in Claim

. Thus

, and

for every

. Since
\mathcal{F}_{j}\subseteq B_{j}(M)
and the

for all

Together with

Finally, if an extremal family originally contained

, replacing

by a missing singleton gives another extremal family with no

contains no singleton for

. Hence no extremal

, and the equality case follows.

\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{Case 1:}&\tau(H)\leq a,\\[2.84526pt]

5.1

Let

For

(5.6)

By

and (

for some constant

. Since

, we have

5.3
Proof of Case 3

Write

q\coloneqq\nu(H)=\ell+d

for some
d\geq 0
. Since
\nu(\mathcal{F})<s
, we have
q\leq s-1
.

We first bound the possible excess of the first three layers over

\Lambda
. Unlike in the general proof, Frankl’s exact three-uniform
bound has two possible extremal terms, so both terms must be compared with
the canonical third layer. Since
q+1\leq s
and
s\leq(n+1)/3
,
Theorem
2.4
gives

|H|\leq\max\left\{\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-q}{3},\binom{3q+2}{3}\right\}.

For the first term,

n-q=4s-\ell-(\ell+d)=r-1-d,

and hence

\binom{n}{3}-\binom{n-q}{3}-\left(\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}\right)=\binom{r}{3}-\binom{r-1-d}{3}\leq(d+1)\binom{r}{2}=O((d+1)s^{2}).

For the second term,

\binom{3q+2}{3}-\binom{3\ell+2}{3}=O(ds^{2}),

and there is a constant
C_{0}
such that

\binom{3\ell+2}{3}-\left(\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}\right)\leq C_{0}s^{2}.

Indeed,
\binom{3\ell+2}{3}-A_{3}=O(s^{2})
, and

A_{3}-\left(\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}\right)\leq\binom{a}{2}=O(s^{2})

because
A_{3}\leq\Lambda=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}
. Since

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|\leq n+\binom{n}{2}=O(s^{2}),

there is an absolute constant
C
such that

|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|\leq\Lambda+C(d+1)s^{2}.

(5.7)

Choose
q

Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}\in H
.
Put
t_{0}\coloneqq s-q
and
W\coloneqq[n]\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}Q_{i}
.
Then
|W|=n-3q=4t_{0}+d
. If
\mathcal{F}_{4}[W]
contained
t_{0}

disjoint 4-sets, then those 4-sets together with

Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
would form an
s

\mathcal{F}
. Hence

\nu(\mathcal{F}_{4}[W])<t_{0}
. By

, with

|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|\geq\max\{T_{1},T_{2}\}.

Let

b\coloneqq s-\ell.

Then
t_{0}+d=b
. Since
\ell\leq t(s)
and

,
there is a constant
\beta>0
such that

It remains to show that
|\mathcal{Y}_{4}|
is larger than the error term in
(

d
is small, and the second blocker term is used when

is large. Choose a sufficiently large constant

.

Then

for all sufficiently large

On the other hand,

Thus

for all sufficiently large

Subcase b:

Since
s=O(b)
,

Choosing

In both subcases,

5.4
Completion of the proof and equality cases

The three cases above prove (
5.3
) for every family with

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
satisfying (
5.1
). Hence

|\mathcal{F}|\leq|P(3,s,\ell)|.

The canonical family
P(3,s,\ell;A)
has no
s
pairwise disjoint members
and has size
|P(3,s,\ell)|
. If
t(s)
is an integer and
\ell=t(s)
, then

|P(3,s,\ell;A)|=\Lambda+\binom{n}{\geq 4}=A_{3}+\binom{n}{\geq 4}=|P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime})|

for every
A^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
. Moreover,

P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime})
has no
s
pairwise disjoint members. Indeed, if an

s
-matching in
P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime})
contains
q
triples, then its total
number of vertices is at least

3q+4(s-q)=4s-q.

Since
n=4s-\ell
, this forces
q\geq\ell
. But all triples of

P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime})
lie inside
A^{\prime}
, and
|A^{\prime}|=3\ell-1
, so
A^{\prime}
cannot
contain
\ell
pairwise disjoint triples.

It remains to classify equality. The strict inequalities above show that
every subcase is strict except for two possibilities: the subcase with no good sets in
the coverable case, and the branch of Case 2 using the complete
3
-graph term with no lower-layer
sets. Suppose first that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
and

|\mathcal{F}|=|P(3,s,\ell)|
. Case 3 and the good-set subcase of Case 1 are
strict. In Case 2, Subcase a is strict, and Subcase b is strict whenever

\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}\neq\emptyset
. Hence equality can occur only in the
following two situations.

First, equality may occur in the subcase of Case 1 with no good sets. Then equality in
(
5.3
) forces

|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\mathcal{F}_{1}|+|\mathcal{F}_{2}|+|H|=\Lambda.

Therefore equality holds in
Section

5.1
. Hence, we obtain
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\emptyset
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\binom{A}{2}
and

H=\left\{T\in\binom{[n]}{3}:T\cap A\neq\emptyset\right\}

for some

with
|A|=a=\ell-1
and
\mathcal{F}_{\geq 4}=\binom{[n]}{\geq 4}
.
Thus

\mathcal{F}=P(3,s,\ell;A).

Second, equality may occur in Case 2, Subcase b, with

\mathcal{F}_{1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{2}=\emptyset
. In this case equality forces

\ell=t(s)
,
|H|=A_{3}=\binom{3\ell-1}{3}
, and
|\mathcal{Y}_{\geq 4}|=0
.
Since Case 2 gives
\nu(H)\leq a=\ell-1
, we have
\nu(H)<\ell
. At the
endpoint,

A_{3}-\left(\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}\right)=\binom{a}{2}>0,

because
\Lambda=A_{3}
and

\Lambda=\binom{a}{2}+\binom{n}{3}-\binom{r}{3}.

Moreover,

n-(3\ell-1)=4(s-\ell)+1>0,

so Theorem
2.4
applies with matching parameter
\ell
.
By the equality case in Theorem
2.4
, it follows that

H=\binom{A^{\prime}}{3}

for some
A^{\prime}\in\binom{[n]}{3\ell-1}
. Therefore

\mathcal{F}=\binom{A^{\prime}}{3}\cup\binom{[n]}{\geq 4}=P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime}).

Thus, if
\ell<t(s)
, equality is possible only for the canonical family

P(3,s,\ell;A)
. If
t(s)
is an integer and
\ell=t(s)
, equality is possible only for

P(3,s,\ell;A)
or
P^{\prime}(s,\ell;A^{\prime})
.
Finally, if an extremal family originally contained
\emptyset
, replacing

\emptyset
by a missing singleton gives another extremal family with no
empty set but with a singleton. This is impossible, since neither extremal
family described above contains a singleton. Hence no extremal family
contains
\emptyset
, completing the proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume (
5.1
). Let
j\in\{1,2\}
, put

p=\ell+j-4,\qquad N=n-j=4s-p-4.

Then, for all sufficiently large
s
,

N\geq 3p-1

and

\binom{N}{3}-\binom{N-p+1}{3}>\binom{3p-1}{3}.

Proof
.

We have

N-3p+1=4(s-\ell)+13-4j>0

for all sufficiently large
s
, since
\ell\leq t(s)<s
.
Hence
N\geq 3p-1
.

Put

R(p,s)\coloneqq 48s^{2}-36sp-20p^{2}+67p-108s+54.

Direct calculation gives

\binom{N}{3}-\binom{N-p+1}{3}-\binom{3p-1}{3}=\frac{p-1}{6}R(p,s).

Moreover,

\frac{\partial R}{\partial p}=-36s-40p+67<0

on the range
s/2-3<p=\ell+j-4\leq t(s)-2
. Hence

R(p,s)\geq R(t(s)-2,s).

Using

10t(s)^{2}+(18s-17)t(s)-24s^{2}+6s+6=0,

we get

\displaystyle R(t(s)-2,s)

\displaystyle=48s^{2}-36s(t(s)-2)-20(t(s)-2)^{2}+67(t(s)-2)-108s+54

\displaystyle=113t(s)-24s-148

\displaystyle=(113\alpha_{*}-24)s+O(1)>0.

Since
p-1>0
for all sufficiently large
s
, the desired inequality
follows.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Under (
5.1
), for all sufficiently large
s
,

\Lambda-h_{3}(n,a)>L_{12},

where
L_{12}
is defined in (
5.5
).

Proof
.

Direct calculation gives

2\bigl(\Lambda-h_{3}(n,a)-L_{12}\bigr)=5\ell^{2}-14\ell s+5\ell+9s^{2}-15s+8.

The quadratic part is

5\ell^{2}-14\ell s+9s^{2}=(s-\ell)(9s-5\ell).

Since
t(s)/s\to\alpha_{*}<1
, both
s-\ell
and
9s-5\ell

are bounded below by positive constant multiples of
s
throughout
the range
s/2<\ell\leq t(s)
. Hence the quadratic part is

\Omega(s^{2})
, and the lower-order terms are negligible. Therefore

\Lambda-h_{3}(n,a)>L_{12}
for all sufficiently large

.
∎

Proof of
Section

5.2
.

For the term
h_{3}(N_{i},p_{i}-1)
, direct expansion gives

\Lambda-L_{1}-h_{3}(n-1,\ell-4)-L_{12}=\frac{13\ell^{2}-46\ell s-11\ell+41s^{2}+17s+4}{2},

\Lambda-L_{2}-h_{3}(n-2,\ell-3)-L_{12}=\frac{5\ell^{2}-14\ell s+5\ell+9s^{2}-15s+8}{2}.

Their quadratic parts are respectively

\frac{13x^{2}-46x+41}{2}s^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{(x-1)(5x-9)}{2}s^{2},\qquad x=\ell/s.

Since
t(s)/s\to\alpha_{*}<1
, the relevant range satisfies

1/2<x\leq\alpha_{*}+o(1)
. On this range both quadratic coefficients
are bounded away from zero. Hence, for all sufficiently large

,

, direct expansion gives

\Lambda-L_{1}-\binom{3\ell-10}{3}-L_{12}=\frac{-20\ell^{3}-36\ell^{2}s+294\ell^{2}+48\ell s^{2}+54\ell s-1114\ell-117s^{2}+63s+1326}{6},

\Lambda-L_{2}-\binom{3\ell-7}{3}-L_{12}=\frac{-20\ell^{3}-36\ell^{2}s+210\ell^{2}+48\ell s^{2}+78\ell s-616\ell-165s^{2}+123s+492}{6}.

As functions of
\ell
, both have derivative with leading term

(-60x^{2}-72x+48)s^{2}
, where

. This leading coefficient is

. Thus both expressions are decreasing in

throughout

. It is enough to evaluate them at

, the two

respectively. Both coefficients are positive. Hence, for all

,

\Lambda-L_{1}-\binom{3\ell-10}{3}-L_{12}>0,\qquad\Lambda-L_{2}-\binom{3\ell-7}{3}-L_{12}>0.

∎
