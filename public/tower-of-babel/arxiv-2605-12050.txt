1.
Introduction

The fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
arises naturally as the first variation of the fractional Dirichlet energy, also referred to as the Gagliardo seminorm, defined for
N\geq 1
,
0<s<1
, and
1<p<\infty
by

\Phi_{s,p}(u)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

More precisely, for suitably regular functions
u
, the fractional
p
-Laplace operator is defined by

\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)&:=C(N,s,p)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,\\
&=C(N,s,p)\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|y-x|\geq\epsilon\}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,\end{aligned}\end{gathered}

(1.1)

where
C(N,s,p)
denotes a normalization constant. It is noteworthy that this operator, along with nonlocal integro-differential operators in general, appears in a wide range of applications, including optimization, finance, phase transitions, thin soft films, and image processing. Furthermore, the fractional
p
-Laplacian constitutes a fundamental model for certain jump Lévy processes in probability theory, for porous media in physics, and for various other phenomena. For a comprehensive account of the principal properties of such operators, we refer the reader to Bertoin
[
6
]
, Caffarelli and Vázquez
[

, De Pablo et al.
[

, Del Teso et al.
[

[
20
]
, Foghem
[
23
]
, Majda and Tabak
[
28
]
, Vázquez
[
34
]
and the references therein. Recently, the fractional
p
-Laplacian has attracted significant attention, primarily due to the intricate interplay between its non-linear and nonlocal features. Considerable attention has been devoted to the study of existence, uniqueness, regularity, and qualitative properties of solutions for a broad class of problems, including elliptic and parabolic boundary-value problems, as well as eigenvalue problems. This research encompasses several fundamental inequalities, such as those of Picone, Hardy, Sobolev, and Poincaré, which play a central role in the analysis of these problems. For comprehensive surveys and further references beyond those cited herein, we refer the reader to Bisci et al.
[

[

[

, without giving an exhaustive list. Concerning the normalization constant
C(N,s,p)
, in the particular case
p=2
, it is given by

C(N,s)=C(N,s,2):=\frac{s2^{2s}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+2s}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma(1-s)},

(1.2)

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. This specific choice ensures that the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}_{2}=(-\Delta)^{s}
has the Fourier symbol
|\xi|^{2s}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(see Di Nezza et al.
[
20

). For
p\neq 2
, the normalization constant
C(N,s,p)
is often chosen arbitrarily in much of the literature and is frequently set equal to
2
for the sake of simplifying computations. In the present work, however, it is essential to employ explicit expressions. Accordingly, we define the normalization constant in such a way that the following limits are satisfied

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)=|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x),\quad\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)=-\Delta_{p}u(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.3)

Hence, we define the normalization constant
C(N,s,p)
as follows (see Dyda et al.
[
21

C(N,s,p)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{sp2^{2(s-1)}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+sp}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(1-s\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)},&\text{if }s>\dfrac{1}{2},\\[10.0pt]
\dfrac{sp2^{2s-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+sp}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma\left(1-s\right)},&\text{if }s\leq\dfrac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

This choice of
C(N,s,p)
preserves several essential properties, including the validity of the limits in (

On the other hand, for

L_{\Delta}
, which constitutes the first-order correction in the asymptotic expansion as
s\to 0^{+}
. More precisely, for every
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+sL_{\Delta}u(x)+O(s)\quad\text{as }s\to 0^{+}\text{ in }L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad 1<q\leq\infty.

(1.5)

Furthermore, it was established in the same paper
[
15

admits the following equivalent representation:

\displaystyle L_{\Delta}u(x)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{ds}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\right|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=C(N)\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}dy-C(N)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{u(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}dy+\rho(N)u(x),

\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}

x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

C(N):=\pi^{-N/2}\Gamma\left(N/2\right),\qquad\rho(N):=2\ln 2+\psi\left(N/2\right)-\gamma,

where
\gamma:=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and
\psi:=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
is the digamma function. In fact, in the same work, the authors developed a comprehensive variational framework for Dirichlet problems posed on bounded domains, thereby providing a rigorous functional setting for the logarithmic Laplace operator
L_{\Delta}
. Within this framework, they investigated the associated eigenvalue problem and derived several fundamental qualitative properties. In particular, they established a Faber-Krahn type inequality, proved appropriate weak and strong maximum principles, and obtained continuity results for weak solutions to the corresponding Poisson problems up to the boundary. For the nonlinear counterpart, Dyda et al.
[
21

L_{\Delta}

L_{\Delta_{p}}

\displaystyle L_{\Delta_{p}}u(x)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u(x)\right|_{s=0}=C(N,p)\text{P.V.}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N}}dy

(1.6)

\displaystyle+C(N,p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))-|u(x)|^{p-2}u(x)}{|x-y|^{N}}dy+\rho(N,p)\left|u(x)\right|^{p-2}u(x),

where

C(N,p):=\frac{p\Gamma\left(N/2\right)}{2\pi^{N/2}},\qquad\rho(N,p):=2\ln 2+\frac{p}{2}\psi\left(N/2\right)-\gamma.

In analogy with the case
p=2
, the authors developed a rigorous variational framework to investigate Dirichlet problems involving
L_{\Delta_{p}}
on bounded domains. Within this framework, they elucidated the relationship between the first Dirichlet eigenvalue and corresponding eigenfunction of the fractional
p
-Laplacian and those of the logarithmic
p
-Laplacian. Furthermore, they extended the Faber-Krahn inequality, established comprehensive maximum principles, and derived a boundary Hardy-type inequality in the associated energy space.

The publication of these two pioneering works has sparked significant interest in boundary value problems involving the logarithmic Laplacian, although this area remains at an early stage of development. This growing interest is driven not only by the wide range of applications (see, for instance, Pellacci and Verzini
[
31
]
, Antil and Bartels
[
1
]
, as well as Feulefack and Jarohs
[

]
, where fractional models with very small order
s
in the operator (
1.1
) arise as an optimal choice in several contexts) but also by significant advances in the analysis of nonlocal phenomena in partial differential equations. For representative contributions, we refer to Arora et al.
[
2
,
5
]
and Chen et al.
[
13
]
in the case
p=2
, and to Arora et al.
[
3
,
4
]
for the more general setting
p>1
. In this direction, and motivated by the preceding discussion, a natural question arises:

Question 1.1
.

For
1<p<\infty
, is it possible to compute the first-order derivative

\left.\frac{d}{dt}(-\Delta)_{p}^{t}u(x)\right|_{t=s},

(1.7)

for an arbitrary order
0<s<1
and for a suitable function
u
?

In this context, Chen et al.
[
14
]
recently established the existence of the first-order derivative and introduced the logarithmic Laplacian, denoted by
(-\Delta)^{s+\log}
in the linear case (i.e., when
p=2
). This operator arises as the first-order correction term in the expansion (
1.5
) for any
0<s<1
and for suitable functions
u
. Precisely, for
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the logarithmic Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s+\log}
is defined by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s+\log}u(x)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}(-\Delta)^{t}u(x)\right|_{t=s}

\displaystyle=B(N,s)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)+C(N,s)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\big(-2\ln|x-y|\big)dy,

where
C(N,s)
is given by (
1.2
), and

B(N,s):=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\ln C(N,t)\big)\Big|_{t=s}=2\ln 2+\frac{1}{s}+\psi(1-s)+\psi\left(\frac{N+2s}{2}\right).

The authors present several equivalent formulations of the operator
(-\Delta)^{s+\log}
, including its characterization as a Fourier multiplier, its definition via spectral calculus, and a description based on an extension procedure. Furthermore, a comprehensive variational framework is developed to address problems both in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and on bounded smooth domains. This framework introduces the natural energy spaces associated with the operator and establishes the corresponding embedding results. In particular, a compact embedding at the critical exponent
2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
is obtained, a phenomenon that contrasts with the classical Sobolev and fractional Sobolev settings. Within this framework, existence and boundedness results for Poisson-type problems are rigorously derived, along with several related results. The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem is also investigated, and qualitative spectral properties are established. Motivated by the aforementioned studies, this article aims to extend the operator introduced above to the case
p>1
, specifically to the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian, denoted by
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}
, and to establish the associated functional framework.

A principal difficulty in the present analysis arises from the differentiation of the nonlocal operator under the integral sign. Introducing the notation
g_{p}(t):=|t|^{p-2}t
, a formal differentiation of the fractional kernel leads to the following pointwise identity:

\partial_{t}\left(\frac{g_{p}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+tp}}\right)\Bigg|_{t=s}=-p\frac{g_{p}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\ln|x-y|.

This logarithmic factor significantly enhances the singularity in a neighborhood of the origin. In the singular range
1<p<2
, the function
g_{p}
is only
(p-1)
-Hölder continuous, that is,
|g_{p}(a)-g_{p}(b)|\leq C|a-b|^{p-1}.
As a consequence, the resulting singular behavior is asymptotically of the form
|x-y|^{(p-1)-N-sp}|\ln|x-y||,
which necessitates stronger regularity assumptions, such as
u\in C_{c}^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\alpha>\frac{1-p(1-s)}{p-1}
, in order to ensure local integrability. Moreover, the logarithmic kernel changes sign at
|x-y|=1
. The associated energy functional admits the following decomposition into positive and negative contributions:

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)=\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)+\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u).

The negative part, up to equivalence, is characterized by

\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\sim\iint_{|x-y|>1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\ln|x-y|dxdy>0.

Analogously, the positive part is characterized by

\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)\sim\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}(-\ln|x-y|)dxdy>0.

Furthermore, the standard fractional contribution is given by

\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)\sim\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

This shows that the functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
is not globally nonnegative on
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
, which in turn invalidates the usual coercivity arguments and makes the application of the direct methods in the calculus of variations more delicate. To overcome these difficulties, we develop a robust functional analytic framework based entirely on real-variable methods. In the case
p=2
, Chen et al.
[
14
]
relied crucially on the Plancherel theorem and the Fourier symbol representation

\mathscr{F}\big((-\Delta)^{s+\log}u\big)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\ln(|\xi|^{2})\hat{u}(\xi).

However, in the case
p\neq 2
, the nonlinear structure of the problem precludes the use of Fourier transform techniques. The method developed in this work extends the corresponding linear theory to the quasilinear setting
1<p<\infty
. Among the main contributions, we establish the following sharp interpolation estimate: for every radius
r\in(0,1)
,

[u]_{s,p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)(-p\ln r)}\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
C(N,s,p)
denotes the standard fractional normalization constant defined in (
1.4
), and
\omega_{N}
denotes the surface measure of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, given by
\omega_{N}=\frac{2\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma\left(N/2\right)}.
This logarithmic control yields a strong compactness property at the critical fractional Sobolev exponent

p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-sp}
. In particular, we establish the compact embedding

W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega),

where
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
denotes the fractional logarithmic Sobolev space (see Section
2
bellow).
This framework represents a significant departure from the classical fractional Sobolev setting. Indeed, it rules out the formation of Dirac mass concentrations and makes the usual concentration-compactness limit measures unnecessary on bounded domains. Furthermore, we derive a structural Pohozaev-type identity. The introduction of the logarithmic weight breaks the exact homogeneity
\mathbf{K}_{s,p}(\lambda z)=\lambda^{-(N+sp)}\mathbf{K}_{s,p}(z)
which is satisfied by the classical fractional kernel. In this modified framework, the corresponding commutator identity is given by

z\cdot\nabla\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|z|)+(N+sp)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|z|)=-pC(N,s,p)|z|^{-N-sp},

where
\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}
is defined in (
2.3
) (see Section
2
). This formulation induces a strictly positive scale-breaking defect measure defined by

\Gamma_{s,p}(u):=\frac{C(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy>0.

The introduction of this defect measure leads to a modified Pohozaev identity of the form

\frac{N-sp}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy-\frac{N}{q}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}=\Gamma_{s,p}(u)-\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u),

which shows that the classical non-existence arguments for critical solutions in star-shaped domains are no longer applicable in the present framework. Under the geometric assumption
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-1/sp}
, the associated kernel remains strictly positive on
\Omega\times\Omega
.This property allows for a global integration of the identity and ensures both the positivity and simplicity of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}^{s+\log}
. We consider the associated nonlinear Dirichlet eigenvalue problem

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

The first eigenvalue is defined variationally as

\lambda_{1,p}^{s+\log}:=\inf\left\{\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u):\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\right\}.

Owing to the sign-changing character of the kernel
\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|z|)
for
|z|>e^{-1/sp}
, the standard comparison principle

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(|u|,|u|)\leq\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
as well as the classical Picone identity are no longer applicable. This difficulty is overcome by imposing the geometric condition
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-1/sp}
, which ensures that
\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)>0
holds strictly on
\Omega\times\Omega
. On the interaction region
\Omega\times\Omega^{c}
, the negative contribution of the kernel is exactly balanced by the boundary condition, in the sense that

|u(x)-0|^{p}-\big||u(x)|-0\big|^{p}=0.

Combined with the fractional Picone-type inequality
P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}
, this cancellation mechanism allows us to prove that
\lambda_{1,p}^{s+\log}>0
is simple, and that the corresponding eigenfunction
\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}
is strictly positive and globally bounded, satisfying

\|\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

To complete the analysis of these eigenfunctions, we establish a strong minimum principle for weak super-solutions satisfying
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u\geq 0
. This result extends the weak Harnack estimates of Di Castro et al.
[
19
]
by employing the test function
\eta=(u+\delta)^{1-p}\phi^{p}
in the weak formulation. The main argument relies on a precise control of the logarithmic term through an explicit computation of the associated singular integrals. In particular, we show that the logarithmic singularity exactly recovers the expected fractional scaling

r^{-p}\cdot r^{p(1-s)}\cdot r^{N}=r^{N-sp}.

More specifically, we derive the following quantitative estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(|x-y|)\left|\ln\left(\frac{\delta+u(x)}{\delta+u(y)}\right)\right|^{p}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{N-sp}\Bigg\{C(N,s,p)\omega_{N}^{2}2^{2N}\left[\frac{B(N,s,p)-p\ln r}{sp}-\frac{1}{s^{2}p}\right]

\displaystyle+\omega_{N}2^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\Big(\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}|y-x_{0}|\big)\Big)_{+}\big(u(y)\big)_{-}^{p-1}dy

\displaystyle+cC(N,s,p)\frac{\omega_{N}^{2}}{Np(1-s)}2^{N+2p(1-s)}\left[B(N,s,p)-p\ln(4r)+\frac{1}{1-s}\right]\Bigg\},

where
B(N,s,p)
is defined in (
2.1
) below. As a consequence, every non-negative and non-trivial weak supersolution satisfies

\operatorname*{ess}\inf_{K}u>0\qquad\text{for every compact set }K\Subset\Omega.

This, in turn, yields the strong minimum principle.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish the existence of the derivative of
(-\Delta)_{p}^{t}
with respect to its order at an arbitrary point
s\in(0,1)
. We then introduce the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian operator, investigate its main properties, and develop the associated functional framework. In addition, we study the corresponding energy forms and their fundamental properties. In Section
3
, we derive several key inequalities, including a regional Poincaré-type inequality, a boundary Hardy-type inequality, a Díaz-Saa inequality, and a Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. We also provide an elementary proof of a Sobolev-type inequality, together with a Pohozaev identity associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian. Section
4
is devoted to proving continuous and compact embedding results. We also establish suitable density results within the corresponding functional spaces. Finally, in Section
5
, we study the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem, with particular emphasis on the qualitative properties of the associated eigenvalues.

2.
The fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian and its associated functional setting

In this section, we introduce the fractional logarithmic Sobolev space
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which serves as the natural functional setting for the weak formulation of the problem under study. Since the structure of this space is intrinsically linked to the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian, we first establish the existence of the derivative appearing in (
1.7
) in the more general case
p>1
. We also recall the precise definition of the associated operator and characterize its kernel. We now state the following proposition:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, for each fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the mapping
t\longmapsto(-\Delta)^{t}_{p}u(x)
is of class
C^{1}
on the interval
(0,1)
. Furthermore, the derivative with respect to
t
at
t=s
is given by

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(-\Delta)_{p}^{t}u(x)\right|_{t=s}

\displaystyle=B(N,s,p)(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)

\displaystyle-pC(N,s,p)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,

where
C(N,s,p)
is the standard fractional normalization constant given in (
1.4
), and

B(N,s,p)=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\ln C(N,t,p)\big)\Big|_{t=s}=2\ln 2+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{p}{2}\psi\left(\frac{N+sp}{2}\right)+\psi(1-s),

(2.1)

with
\psi
denoting the digamma function.

Proof.

First, we note that the operator
(-\Delta)^{t}_{p}
is well-defined for every
t\in(0,1)
when
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Indeed, for a fixed
t\in(0,1)
, by setting
z=y-x
, the operator can equivalently be expressed as

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{t}_{p}u(x):=C(N,t,p)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(x+z)\right|^{p-2}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)}{|z|^{N+tp}}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{C(N,t,p)}{2}\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(x+z)\right|^{p-2}\left(u(x)-u(x+z)\right)+\left|u(x)-u(x-z)\right|^{p-2}\left(u(x)-u(x-z)\right)}{|z|^{N+tp}}dz.

Case 1:
1<p<2
.
Under the regularity assumption on
u
, we have
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\alpha>0
, which will be specified later. Moreover, noting that the mapping
\tau\mapsto\left|\tau\right|^{p-1}
is concave, an application of the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem yields

\displaystyle\frac{\Big|\ |u(x)-u(x+z)|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(x+z)\big)+|u(x)-u(x-z)|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(x-z)\big)\Big|}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2-p}\frac{\big|\big(u(x)-u(x-z)\big)+\big(u(x)-u(x+z)\big)\big|^{p-1}}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle=2^{2-p}\frac{\big|\big(Du(x-\zeta_{1}z)-Du(x+\zeta_{2}z)\big)\cdot z\big|^{p-1}}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle\leq C|z|^{(\alpha+1)(p-1)-N-tp},

for some
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in[0,1]
, and
C=C(p,\alpha,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})>0
is a constant. By choosing
\alpha>\frac{1-p(1-t)}{p-1}
, the right-hand side becomes integrable near
0
for any
t\in(0,1)
(see
[
27
, Proposition 2.12]
).

Case 2:
p\geq 2
.
By also using the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem together with the classical elliptic estimate

\bigl||a|^{p-2}a-|b|^{p-2}b\bigr|\leq C(p)\bigl(|a|+|b|\bigr)^{p-2}|a-b|,\quad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ with }|a|+|b|\neq 0,

and taking into account that
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Bigl||u(x)-u(x+z)|^{p-2}\bigl(u(x)-u(x+z)\bigr)-|u(x-z)-u(x)|^{p-2}\bigl(u(x-z)-u(x)\bigr)\Bigr|}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\frac{\Bigl(|u(x)-u(x+z)|+|u(x-z)-u(x)|\Bigr)^{p-2}\Bigl|(u(x)-u(x+z))-(u(x-z)-u(x))\Bigr|}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle=C(p)\frac{|z|^{p-2}\Bigl(|Du(x+\zeta_{1}z)|+|Du(x-\zeta_{2}z)|\Bigr)^{p-2}\Bigl|(Du(x-\zeta_{1}z)-Du(x+\zeta_{2}z))\cdot z\Bigr|}{|z|^{N+tp}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\mathrm{supp}(u)}|Du|^{p-2}\sup_{\mathrm{supp}(u)}|D^{2}u||z|^{p(1-t)-N},

for some
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in[0,1]
, and constant
C=C(p,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})>0
. It is straightforward to see that this expression is integrable near
0
for every
t\in(0,1)
.
Second, we aim to establish the existence of the first-order derivative of the fractional
p
-Laplace operator. To this end, for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}(-\Delta)^{t}_{p}u(x)\right|_{t=s}=\frac{\left.\frac{d}{dt}C(N,t,p)\right|_{t=s}}{C(N,s,p)}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)

(2.2)

\displaystyle+C(N,s,p)\left.\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+tp}}dy\right)\right|_{t=s}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{d}{dt}\big(\ln C(N,t,p)\big)\Big|_{t=s}}_{\textbf{I}_{1}}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)

\displaystyle+C(N,s,p)\underbrace{\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left.\frac{d}{dt}\big(|x-y|^{-N-tp}\big)\right|_{t=s}dy}_{\textbf{I}_{2}}.

We first calculate the term
\textbf{I}_{1}
. Taking into account the normalizing constant
C(N,t,p)
introduced in (
1.4
), we distinguish between the following two cases:

Case 1:

2s>1
. In this case, we have

C(N,t,p)=\dfrac{tp2^{2(t-1)}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+tp}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(1-t\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)}.

Taking logarithms gives

\displaystyle\ln C(N,t,p)

\displaystyle=\ln t+\ln p+2(t-1)\ln 2+\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{N+tp}{2}\right)-\frac{N-1}{2}\ln\pi-\ln\Gamma\left(1-t\right)-\ln\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right).

Differentiating with respect to
t
and using the definition of the digamma function
\psi
, we obtain

\displaystyle B(N,s,p):=\textbf{I}_{1}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\ln C(N,t,p)\big)\Big|_{t=s}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{s}+2\ln 2+\frac{p}{2}\psi\Big(\frac{N+sp}{2}\Big)+\psi\Big(1-s\Big).

Case 2:

2s\leq 1
. Here, we have

C(N,t,p)=\dfrac{tp2^{2t-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{N+tp}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma\left(1-t\right)}.

Similarly, differentiating gives

B(N,s,p)=\frac{d}{dt}\big(\ln C(N,t,p)\big)\Big|_{t=s}=\frac{1}{s}+2\ln 2+\frac{p}{2}\psi\left(\frac{N+sp}{2}\right)+\psi(1-s).

Next, we calculate
\textbf{I}_{2}
. It is easy to see that

\textbf{I}_{2}=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\big(-p\ln|x-y|\big)dy.

We emphasize that this integral is finite and that
\textbf{I}_{2}\in C(0,1)
. Indeed, since
u
has compact support, for sufficiently large
|y|
the integrand behaves like
\left|\ln|x-y|\right||x-y|^{-N-sp},
which is integrable at infinity for all
t\in(0,1)
. On the other hand, in a neighborhood of
x=y
, by exploiting the regularity properties of
u
together with the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem, the integrand behaves like
\left|\ln|x-y|\right||x-y|^{(\alpha+1)(p-1)-N-tp}
for
\alpha>\frac{1-p(1-t)}{p-1}
when
1<p<2
, and like
\left|\ln|x-y|\right||x-y|^{p(1-t)-N}
when
p\geq 2
. In both cases, the integral remains integrable near
x=y
for any
t\in(0,1)
. Hence, combining these observations and returning to (
2.2
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\big((-\Delta)^{t}_{p}u(x)\big)\right|_{t=s}

\displaystyle=B(N,s,p)(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)

\displaystyle-pC(N,s,p)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy.

This completes the proof.
∎

Based on Proposition
2.1
, we introduce our main operator, called the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian. It is defined on
C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with values in
\mathbb{R}
and is explicitly obtained as the derivative with respect to
s\in(0,1)
, given by:

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u(x):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}(-\Delta)_{p}^{t}u(x)\right|_{t=s},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Furthermore, this operator admits the following equivalent representations

(i)

Integral representation: For every
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u(x)

\displaystyle=B(N,s,p)(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle-pC(N,s,p)\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy.

(ii)

Kernel representation: For every
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u(x)=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(|x-y|)dy,

where the kernel
\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}
is explicitly given by

\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(r):=\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)-pC(N,s,p)\ln r}{r^{N+sp}},\quad r>0.

(2.3)

Remark 2.1
.

By the definitions of
C(N,s,p)
and
B(N,s,p)
, we observe the following properties:

(1)

In general, the kernel
\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}
is not guaranteed to be nonnegative.

(2)

The constant
B(N,s,p)
exhibits the asymptotic behavior

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}B(N,s,p)=+\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}B(N,s,p)=-\infty.

Moreover,
B(N,s,p)
is strictly decreasing as a function of
s
. In particular, since
B(N,1/2,p)>0
, there exists a threshold
s_{0}\in(1/2,1)
such that

B(N,s,p)\geq 0\quad\text{for all }s\in(0,s_{0}).

We next show that the operator
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}
enjoys the following properties.

\displaystyle=|u(x)|^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{N}}{\theta p}R^{-\theta p}|u(x)|^{p-1}\leq C|u(x)|^{p-1},

\bullet
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)
, with the additional assumption that
y\in\operatorname{supp}(u)
. In this setting, we still have
u(x)=0
. Moreover, since

, it follows that
|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2},
which implies
|x-y|\geq\frac{|x|}{2}>1.
Consequently, we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy\leq 2^{N}|x|^{-N}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-1}.

(2.13)

We also distinguish the following cases for the second term in (
2.11
):

\bullet
Let
x\in\mathscr{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
and
y\in\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)
. Consequently, under the assumption that
\alpha(p-1)>\theta p

\displaystyle\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p-\alpha(p-1)}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{N}}{\alpha(p-1)-\theta p}R^{\alpha(p-1)-\theta p}\leq C.

\bullet
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
and

with

u(x)=0
, and hence

\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy=\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy\leq 2^{N}|x|^{-N}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-1}.

(2.15)

Consequently, for any
1<q<\infty

2.15
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{E}_{1}^{q}dx\leq C\Bigg[\|u\|_{L^{q(p-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q(p-1)}+2^{qN+1}\frac{\omega_{N}}{N(q-1)}\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{N(1-q)}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q(p-1)}+\omega_{N}\left(\frac{R}{2}\right)^{N}\Bigg]<\infty,

while for
q=\infty

\left\|\mathbf{E}_{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\Bigg[\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-1}+2^{2N+1}R^{-N}\|u\|_{L^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p-1}+1\Bigg]<\infty.

Estimate of

\mathbf{E}_{2}
. In a similar manner, we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|\ln|x-y||}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy+\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}|\ln|x-y||}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy.

(2.16)

We follow the same method for
\mathbf{E}_{1}

\bullet
Let
x\in\mathscr{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}R^{-\theta p}\left(\frac{\ln R}{\theta p}+\frac{1}{(\theta p)^{2}}\right)|u(x)|^{p-1}\leq C|u(x)|^{p-1}.

\bullet
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)
and

, and assume in addition that

u(x)=0
and
|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}

For the second term in (

\displaystyle\mathrm{P.V.}

\displaystyle=\omega_{N}\left[R^{\alpha(p-1)-\theta p}\left(\frac{\ln R}{\alpha(p-1)-\theta p}-\frac{1}{\left(\alpha(p-1)-\theta p\right)^{2}}\right)+\frac{2}{\left(\alpha(p-1)-\theta p\right)^{2}}\right]\leq C.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{E}_{2}^{q}dx

\displaystyle<\infty.

and for

Estimate of

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}(\ln|x-y|)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy+\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}(\ln|x-y|)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy.

and

and

. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{R}(x)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}(\ln|x-y|)^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta p}}dy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathbf{E}_{3}^{q}dx

Now, with the operator and its underlying kernel
\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}
properly defined, we can now construct the associated functional framework. To provide the necessary baseline context, we first recall the standard definitions and norm structures of the classical fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. First, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function and let
p\in[1,+\infty)
. We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
the norm in the space
L^{p}(\Omega)
, defined by

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx.

The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

The space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
consists of functions

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\},

and it is endowed with the Gagliardo semi-norm

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=[u]_{s,p}=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

Remark 2.2
.

It is well-known that the fractional Poincaré inequality (see Di Nezza et al.
[
20
, Theorem 6.5]
) ensures that the norms
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)}
are equivalent on
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for
N>sp
, the results by Bisci et al.
[
7
]
and Di Nezza et al.
[
20
]
imply that
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for
1\leq r\leq p_{s}^{*}:=\frac{Np}{N-sp}
, with the embedding being compact whenever
1\leq r<p_{s}^{*}
.

We now introduce the fractional logarithmic Sobolev space
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the natural energy space corresponding to the positive part of the fractional logarithmic kernel

W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and }[u]_{s+\log,p}<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}.

(2.17)

Here, the Gagliardo seminorm is defined as

[u]_{s+\log,p}:=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the positive kernel
\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}
is given by (with the notation
(a)_{\pm}:=\max\left\{0,\pm a\right\}
)

\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(r):=\frac{C(N,s,p)(-\ln|r|)_{+}}{|r|^{N+sp}},\quad r\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

Notice that the full fractional logarithmic kernel defined previously in (
2.3
) admits the natural decomposition

\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(r):=B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)r^{-N-sp}+p\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(r)-p\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,-}_{p}(r),\quad r>0.

The corresponding Dirichlet subspace is defined as

W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega):=\Bigl\{u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ :\ u=0\ \text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\Bigr\}.

We point out that, by a new Poincaré inequality associated with our fractional logarithmic operator (see Proposition
3.1
below), the norms
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
and
[\cdot]_{s+\log,p}
are equivalent on
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Accordingly, throughout this work, we adopt the following convention:

\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}:=[\cdot]_{s+\log,p}.

In this paper, we denote by
d(\cdot)
the distance function to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, defined for any
x\in\Omega
by

d(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)=\inf_{y\in\partial\Omega}|x-y|.

We also consider the Hölder space
C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
, endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[u]_{0,\beta},

where the Hölder seminorm is defined by

[u]_{0,\beta}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}.

We now establish the fundamental topological and geometrical properties of these spaces.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. The fractional logarithmic Sobolev space
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, is a separable, uniformly convex, and reflexive Banach space. Consequently, the closed subspace
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
inherits the same geometric properties.

Proof.

Let
d\mu(x,y):=\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy
be the measure defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
associated with the positive logarithmic kernel. We consider the product space
E:=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},d\mu),
endowed with the natural product norm defined by

\|(v,w)\|_{E}:=\left(\|v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\|w\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},d\mu)}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
1<p<\infty
, both spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},d\mu)
are separable and uniformly convex Banach spaces (see Brezis
[
10
]
). Consequently, their Cartesian product
E
inherits separability and uniform convexity. Therefore, by the Milman-Pettis theorem (see Milman
[
29
]
and Pettis
[
32
]
), it follows that
E
is reflexive.

Now, consider the linear operator
T:W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to E
defined by

Tu(x,y):=\big(u(x),u(x)-u(y)\big).

By the definition of the corresponding norms, we obtain

\|Tu\|_{E}=\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}d\mu(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Hence,
T
is a linear isometry. Consequently, the space
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is isometrically isomorphic to its image
T\big(W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)\subset E
. To conclude that
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
inherits the completeness and geometric properties of
E
, it is sufficient to show that
T\big(W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
is a closed subspace of
E
. Let
(u_{n})
be a Cauchy sequence in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
Tu_{n}\to(v,w)
strongly in
E
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle\to v\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(2.18)

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\to w(x,y)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2N},d\mu).

(2.19)

From (
2.18
), we can extract a subsequence (still denoted by
(u_{n_{k}})
) such that
u_{n_{k}}\to v
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Hence,

u_{n_{k}}(x)-u_{n_{k}}(y)\to v(x)-v(y)\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{2N}.

On the other hand, (
2.19
) ensures the existence of a (possibly further) subsequence converging pointwise almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
(with respect to the measure
d\mu
) to
w(x,y)
. By the uniqueness of the pointwise limit, it follows that

w(x,y)=v(x)-v(y)\quad\text{for almost every }(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}.

Hence,
(v,w)=Tv
, which shows that the image
T\big(W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
is closed in
E
.
We therefore conclude that
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a separable, uniformly convex, and reflexive Banach space. Finally, since
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
is defined as a closed linear subspace of
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it inherits completeness, separability, uniform convexity, and reflexivity.
∎

We are now ready to present a detailed weak formulation for problems involving the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian. To this end, let
u,v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and we define

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,v):=\dfrac{p}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,v)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,v)\right)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2}\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,v),

(2.20)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\pm}(u,v)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,\pm}_{p}(x-y)dxdy,

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,v)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

It is worth noting that the full energy functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}
is, in general, not positive definite. Furthermore, the functionals
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}
,
\mathscr{J}_{\pm}
, and
\mathscr{J}_{s}
possess the following fundamental properties.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and let
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

The following estimate holds:

0\leq\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\leq\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{(sp)^{2}}C(N,s,p)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

(2)

Define

\mathrm{diam}(\Omega):=\sup\{|x-y|:x,y\in\Omega\}.

If
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<1
, then

\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\geq-\frac{2\omega_{N}}{sp}C(N,s,p)(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{-sp}\left(\ln(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))+\frac{1}{sp}\right)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

In particular, if
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
, then

\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\geq 0.

(3)

Let
0<r<1
. Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(4)

Assume that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
. Then the functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
is nonnegative definite on
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, and

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)=0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad u=0.

Moreover,
|u|\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, and

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(|u|,|u|)\leq\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u).

Equality holds if and only if
u
does not change sign.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Then, we have the following:

(1)
By exploiting the convexity of the function
\tau\mapsto\tau^{p}
together with the symmetry of the integrand, we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)

\displaystyle=C(N,s,p)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\left(-\ln|x-y|\right)_{-}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}C(N,s,p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left(-\ln|x-y|\right)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\right)dx.

Computing the inner integral yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left(-\ln|x-y|\right)_{-}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy=\frac{\omega_{N}}{(sp)^{2}}.

Consequently, we obtain the estimate

\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\leq\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{(sp)^{2}}C(N,s,p)\left\|u\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(2)
Since
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, by the definitions of
\mathscr{J}_{\pm}
we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)

\displaystyle=-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy}_{\textbf{I}_{1}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{-2C(N,s,p)\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\right)dx}_{\textbf{I}_{2}}.

Clearly,
\boldsymbol{I}_{1}
is non-negative, since
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<1
. We now turn to estimating the term
\boldsymbol{I_{2}}
. Indeed, for a fixed
x\in\Omega
, the inner integral can be decomposed as

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cap\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy-\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{1}(x))}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}(x))\cap\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{1}(x)}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega_{N}}{sp}(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{-sp}\left(\ln(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))+\frac{1}{sp}\right).

Consequently, we obtain

\boldsymbol{I}_{2}\geq-\frac{2\omega_{N}}{sp}C(N,s,p)(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{-sp}\left(\ln(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))+\frac{1}{sp}\right)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},

which implies

\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\geq-\frac{2\omega_{N}}{sp}C(N,s,p)(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{-sp}\left(\ln(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))+\frac{1}{sp}\right)\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

It is easy to verify that the preceding estimate is non-negative whenever
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}.

(3)
We follow the same argument as in
[
14
, Lemma 3.1]
. In fact, we obtain the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\iint_{\{|x-y|<r\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy}_{\mathbf{I}_{1}}+\underbrace{\iint_{\{|x-y|\geq r\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy}_{\mathbf{I}_{2}}.

Estimate of
\mathbf{I}_{1}
.

Observe that for
|x-y|<r<1
, one has

(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}=-\ln|x-y|\geq-\ln r.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{\ln r}\iint_{\{|x-y|<r\}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\frac{(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\iint_{\{|x-y|<r\}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}[u]^{p}_{s+\log,p}.

Estimate of
\mathbf{I}_{2}
.

Using once again the convexity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\tau^{p}
together with the symmetry, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\iint_{\{|x-y|\geq r\}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}+|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\{|x-y|\geq r\}}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Consequently, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}[u]^{p}_{s+\log,p}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(4)
Observing that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
, it follows directly from
(2)
that
\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)>0.
Moreover, recalling the definition of the full fractional energy and noting that
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
, we deduce

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u)\geq 0.

Assuming that
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)=0
, we then infer that

\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy=0.

Since
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, it immediately follows that
u=0
in
\Omega
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)

\displaystyle=-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle-2C(N,s,p)\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\geq-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{||u(x)|-|u(y)||^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle-2C(N,s,p)\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=-C(N,s,p)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{||u(x)|-|u(y)||^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\mathscr{J}_{+}(|u|,|u|)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(|u|,|u|).

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle 0\leq\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(|u|,|u|)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{p}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{+}(|u|,|u|)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(|u|,|u|)\right)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2}\mathscr{J}_{s}(|u|,|u|)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{p}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{+}(u,u)-\mathscr{J}_{-}(u,u)\right)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2}\mathscr{J}_{s}(u,u)=\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u).

We remark that the last equality holds if and only if
u
does not change sign.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In view of Proposition
2.4
(1) and (3), the energy functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian is well-defined on
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
.

We show that the space
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
can be viewed as an intermediate space between
W_{0}^{s+\varepsilon,p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
for every
0<\varepsilon<1-s
. More precisely, we have

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Then, for every
0<\varepsilon<1-s
, the following continuous embeddings hold:

W_{0}^{s+\varepsilon,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega).

Proof.

On the one hand, it follows from Proposition
2.4
(3), together with the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
3.1
below), that
W^{s+\log}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).
On the other hand, we argue in the same spirit as in
[
14
, Proposition 1.6]
. For any
0<\epsilon<1-s
, there exists
0<r_{\epsilon}<e^{-\frac{1}{ps}}
such that

|x-y|^{-N-sp}\bigl(-p\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}\leq|x-y|^{-N-p(s+\epsilon)}\quad\text{for all }0<|x-y|<r_{\epsilon}.

Consequently, a direct computation yields

\iint_{\{|x-y|<r_{\epsilon}\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigl(-p\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}dxdy\leq\iint_{\{|x-y|<r_{\epsilon}\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+p(s+\epsilon)}}dxdy\leq\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s+\epsilon}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Now, by exploiting the convexity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\tau^{p}
, together with symmetry, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{\{|x-y|>r_{\epsilon}\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigl(-p\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathscr{B}_{r_{\epsilon}}(x)}\dfrac{\bigl(-\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=2^{p}\omega_{N}\left[\frac{r_{\epsilon}^{-sp}}{sp}(-\ln r_{\epsilon})+\frac{1-r_{\epsilon}^{-sp}}{(sp)^{2}}\right]\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

Combining the above estimates, we deduce the existence of a constant
C_{\epsilon,p,s}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{C(N,s,p)}{p}\iint_{\{|x-y|<r_{\epsilon}\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigl(-p\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+C(N,s,p)\iint_{\{|x-y|>r_{\epsilon}\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigl(-\ln|x-y|\bigr)_{+}dxdy\leq C_{\epsilon,p,s}\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s+\epsilon,p}_{0}(\Omega)}.

In particular, for any
0<\epsilon<1-s
, it follows that
W^{s+\epsilon,p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s+\log}_{0}(\Omega).

∎

3.
Fundamental functional inequalities and the Pohozaev identity

In this section, we establish several fundamental functional inequalities, namely a Poincaré-type inequality, a boundary Hardy-type inequality, a Díaz-Saa inequality, and a Pohozaev identity. These results constitute the main analytical framework for the fractional logarithmic Sobolev space
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
.

3.1.
Poincaré-type inequality

We first derive a Poincaré inequality associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian under consideration. More precisely, we obtain the following result:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Assume that
u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then the estimate holds:

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy,

(3.1)

where
C=C(s,p,N,\Omega)
is a positive constant given by

C=\frac{N\big(\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\big)^{N+sp}}{C(N,s,p)\omega_{N}a^{N}\left(1-N\ln a\right)},

with
a=\min\big(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega),1\big)
, where
C(N,s,p)
denotes the fractional normalization constant defined in (
1.4
).

Proof.

Let
x\in\Omega
be fixed, and let
y\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}(x)
be such that
u(y)=0
. Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle C(N,s,p)|u(x)|^{p}(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}

\displaystyle=|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\frac{C(N,s,p)(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}|x-y|^{N+sp}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{N+sp}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\frac{C(N,s,p)(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}.

Integrating with respect to
y
over
\mathscr{B}_{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}(x)
yields

\displaystyle C(N,s,p)

\displaystyle\omega_{N}a^{N}\left(-\ln a+\frac{1}{N}\right)|u(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))^{N+sp}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\frac{C(N,s,p)(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy,

where
a=\min\big(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega),1\big)
. Finally, integrating over
\Omega
with respect to
x
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}dx\leq C(s,p,N,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathscr{B}_{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}(x)}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\frac{C(N,s,p)(-\ln|x-y|)_{+}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dydx,

where

C(s,p,N,\Omega)=\frac{N\big(\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)\big)^{N+sp}}{C(N,s,p)\omega_{N}a^{N}\left(1-N\ln a\right)}.

∎

As a consequence of Proposition
3.1
(Poincaré-type inequality), we establish a fractional Sobolev inequality within our functional framework. The presence of the logarithmic singularity at the origin implies that the fractional logarithmic seminorm yields a stronger control than the classical fractional Gagliardo seminorm (as shown in Proposition
2.5
). Consequently, we obtain the following critical Sobolev-type inequality.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
satisfy
N>sp
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,p,\Omega)>0
such that for every
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, the following critical Sobolev-type inequality holds:

\|u\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy.

(3.2)

Proof.

By means of the classical fractional Sobolev inequality (see Remark
2.2
), there exists a positive constant
C_{*}=C_{*}(N,s,p)>0
such that the fractional Gagliardo seminorm
[\cdot]_{s,p}
controls the critical Lebesgue norm, in the sense that

\|u\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\leq C_{*}[u]_{s,p}^{p}:=C_{*}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

(3.3)

On the other hand, it follows from Proposition
2.4
(3) that, for any
0<r<1
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Combining this estimate with the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
3.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}C(s,p,N,\Omega)\right)\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}.

(3.4)

Finally, coupling (
3.4
) with (
3.3
), we obtain the desired estimate. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Hardy-type inequality

In this subsection, we establish a logarithmic boundary Hardy inequality. Indeed, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,p,\Omega)>0
such that, for every
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d(x)^{sp}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{d(x)}\right)\right)_{+}dx\leq C\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy.

Proof.

For any
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, it holds that
u=0
almost everywhere in
\Omega^{c}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
. By decomposing the fractional logarithmic Gagliardo seminorm
[\cdot]_{s+\log,p}
, we obtain that

[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}\geq 2\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\left(\int_{\Omega^{c}}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dy\right)dx=:2\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\kappa_{\Omega}(x)dx,

(3.5)

where
\kappa_{\Omega}(x)
denotes the regional killing measure. In order to derive the desired inequality, it is sufficient to establish a suitable lower bound for
\kappa_{\Omega}(x)
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega
. Since
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, it satisfies the uniform exterior cone condition. Consequently, there exist constants
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
h\in(0,e^{-1/p})
such that, for every
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, one can associate an exterior cone
\mathscr{C}_{x_{0}}\subset\Omega^{c}
with vertex at
x_{0}
, height
h
, and opening angle
\theta
. Now, let
x\in\Omega
with
d(x)<h/4
, and choose
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that
|x-x_{0}|=d(x)
. Considering the truncated cone
\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{x_{0}}:=\mathscr{C}_{x_{0}}\setminus B_{2d(x)}(x_{0})
, any
y\in\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{x_{0}}
satisfies
|y-x_{0}|\geq 2d(x)
. By the triangle inequality,
\frac{1}{2}|y-x_{0}|\leq|y-x|\leq\frac{3}{2}|y-x_{0}|
. Passing to polar coordinates centered at
x
, the angular measure of the intersection is bounded from below, giving

\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\geq\int_{\widetilde{\mathscr{C}}_{x_{0}}}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dy\geq c_{1}p\int_{3d(x)}^{h}\frac{-\ln r}{r^{1+sp}}dr,

where
c_{1}>0
depends only on
N
and
\theta
. Evaluating the integral via integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{3d(x)}^{h}\frac{-\ln r}{r^{1+sp}}dr

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{r^{-sp}}{sp}(-\ln r)\right]_{3d(x)}^{h}-\frac{1}{sp}\int_{3d(x)}^{h}r^{-1-sp}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{(3d(x))^{-sp}}{sp}|\ln(3d(x))|-\frac{(3d(x))^{-sp}}{s^{2}p^{2}}+C(h,s,p).

As
d(x)\to 0^{+}
, the leading logarithmic term strictly dominates. Consequently, there exist a threshold
d_{0}\in(0,h/4)
and a constant
c_{2}>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
satisfying
d(x)<d_{0}
,

\kappa_{\Omega}(x)\geq c_{2}\frac{\left(\ln(1/d(x))\right)_{+}}{d(x)^{sp}}.

(3.6)

Defining the boundary strip
\Omega_{d_{0}}:=\{x\in\Omega:d(x)<d_{0}\}
and substituting (
3.6
) into (
3.5
), we secure the bound near the boundary

\int_{\Omega_{d_{0}}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d(x)^{sp}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{d(x)}\right)\right)_{+}dx\leq\frac{1}{2c_{2}}[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}.

(3.7)

For the interior region
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{d_{0}}
, the distance to the boundary is bounded strictly away from zero (
d(x)\geq d_{0}
), ensuring the continuous weight function is uniformly bounded:
d(x)^{-sp}\left(\ln(1/d(x))\right)_{+}\leq M(d_{0})
. Moreover, by applying the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
3.1
),
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C(s,p,N,\Omega)[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}
, we derive

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{d_{0}}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{d(x)^{sp}}\left(\ln\left(\frac{1}{d(x)}\right)\right)_{+}dx\leq M(d_{0})\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq M(d_{0})C(s,p,N,\Omega)[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}.

(3.8)

Summing (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) yields the desired global inequality with
C:=\frac{1}{2c_{2}}+M(d_{0})C(s,p,N,\Omega)
.
∎

3.3.
Díaz-Saa type inequality

In this subsection, we present a Díaz-Saa type inequality associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}
, which leads to uniqueness results. Precisely, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
1<r\leq p
, and
0<s<1
. Assume that
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
, where
B(N,s,p)
is defined in (
2.1
). Then, the following inequality holds in the sense of distributions:

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(u,\frac{u^{r}-v^{r}}{u^{r-1}}\right)-\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(v,\frac{v^{r}-u^{r}}{v^{r-1}}\right)\geq 0.

(3.9)

This inequality holds for all
u,v\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u>0
and
v>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and such that
u/v,\;v/u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if equality holds in (
3.9
), then the following assertions are satisfied:

(i)

\dfrac{u}{v}\equiv c>0
a.e. in
\Omega
;

(ii)

if, in addition,
r\neq p
, then
u\equiv v
a.e. in
\Omega
.

In order to establish a Díaz-Saa type inequality, we exploit the convexity properties of the associated energy functional. To this end, let
1<r\leq p
and consider the following functional

\mathscr{W}:\dot{V}_{+}^{r}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},\qquad\mathscr{W}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(w^{\frac{1}{r}},w^{\frac{1}{r}}\right).

Here,

\dot{V}_{+}^{r}:=\Bigl\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to(0,\infty)\;\Big|\;u^{\frac{1}{r}}\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\Bigr\}.

We next introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a real vector space and let
C\subset X
be a nonempty convex cone.
A functional
\mathscr{W}:C\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be ray-strictly convex (respectively, strictly convex) if

\mathscr{W}\big((1-t)u_{1}+tu_{2}\big)\leq(1-t)\mathscr{W}(u_{1})+t\mathscr{W}(u_{2}),\quad\text{for all }u_{1},u_{2}\in C,\;t\in(0,1),

with strict inequality unless
u_{1}/u_{2}\equiv c>0
(respectively, unless
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
).

According to the above definition, the functional
\mathscr{W}
satisfies the following convexity property:

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
. Then the functional
\mathscr{W}
is ray-strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
. Moreover, if
r\neq p
, then
\mathscr{W}
is strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
.

Proof.

First, we show that the set
\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
is a convex cone. Indeed, let
u,v\in\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\left|\left((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-\left((1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-u^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|v^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-v^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy,

where in the last inequality we use
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
. Consequently, we deduce that
(1-\lambda)u+\lambda v\in\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
. Now, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
and
t\in[0,1]
, and define
w=(1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2}
. Consequently, since
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
and by applying again
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{W}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathscr{J}_{+}(w^{\frac{1}{r}},w^{\frac{1}{r}})-\mathscr{J}_{-}(w^{\frac{1}{r}},w^{\frac{1}{r}})\Big)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2p}\mathscr{J}_{s}(w^{\frac{1}{r}},w^{\frac{1}{r}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{C(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\left|w^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\left|w^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle=-\frac{C(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\left|w^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|^{p}\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2p}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{\left|w^{\frac{1}{r}}(x)-w^{\frac{1}{r}}(y)\right|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad-C(N,s,p)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{w(x)\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{p}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{w(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\mathscr{W}(w_{1})+t\mathscr{W}(w_{2}).

Assume that equality holds. Using the condition
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
, we obtain

\big|w(x)^{\frac{1}{r}}-w(y)^{\frac{1}{r}}\big|^{p}=(1-t)\big|w_{1}(x)^{\frac{1}{r}}-w_{1}(y)^{\frac{1}{r}}\big|^{p}+t\big|w_{2}(x)^{\frac{1}{r}}-w_{2}(y)^{\frac{1}{r}}\big|^{p},\quad\text{a.e. }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(3.10)

We distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

p=r
. In this case, we obtain

\left|\|a\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{r}}-\|b\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{r}}\right|^{r}=\|a-b\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{r}}^{r},\quad\text{for a.e. }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{r}}
denotes the
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{r}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and

a=\big(((1-t)w_{1}(x))^{1/r},(tw_{2}(x))^{1/r}\big),\quad b=\big(((1-t)w_{1}(y))^{1/r},(tw_{2}(y))^{1/r}\big).

Since
r>1
, it follows that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
w_{2}=cw_{1}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Consequently, the functional
\mathscr{W}
is ray-strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
.

Case 2:

p\neq r
. Using (
3.10
) together with
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
and the strict concavity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\tau^{\frac{r}{p}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle(1-t)\left|w_{1}(x)^{1/r}-w_{1}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{r}+t\left|w_{2}(x)^{1/r}-w_{2}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\left((1-t)\left|w_{1}(x)^{1/r}-w_{1}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{p}+t\left|w_{2}(x)^{1/r}-w_{2}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{r}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left|w(x)^{1/r}-w(y)^{1/r}\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\left|w_{1}(x)^{1/r}-w_{1}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{r}+t\left|w_{2}(x)^{1/r}-w_{2}(y)^{1/r}\right|^{r}.

Hence, by Case 1, we deduce that
w_{2}=cw_{1}
for some constant
c>0
. Substituting this relation into (
3.10
), we obtain

\big((1-t)+ct\big)^{\frac{p}{r}}=(1-t)+tc^{\frac{p}{r}}.

By the strict convexity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\tau^{\frac{p}{r}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we necessarily have
c=1
. Hence,
w_{1}=w_{2}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Therefore, the functional
\mathscr{W}
is strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let
u,v\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
u>0
and
v>0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
, and let
\theta\in(0,1)
. Define
w:=(1-\theta)u^{r}+\theta v^{r}.
It is well-known, see
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
, that
w\in\dot{V}_{+}^{r}
. Hence, by Proposition
3.5
, the mapping
\theta\mapsto\Phi(\theta):=\mathscr{W}(w)
is convex and differentiable on
[0,1]
. For
\theta\in(0,1)
, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)=\frac{p}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{+}\left(w,\frac{v^{r}-u^{r}}{w^{r-1}}\right)-\mathscr{J}_{-}\left(w,\frac{v^{r}-u^{r}}{w^{r-1}}\right)\right)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2}\mathscr{J}_{s}\left(w,\frac{v^{r}-u^{r}}{w^{r-1}}\right).

Using the convexity of
\Phi
together with the identities
w=u^{r}
at
\theta=0
and
w=v^{r}
at
\theta=1
, we deduce that

\Phi^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)\leq\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)=\Phi^{\prime}(1),

which is equivalent to the inequality

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(u,\frac{u^{r}-v^{r}}{u^{r-1}}\right)-\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(v,\frac{v^{r}-u^{r}}{v^{r-1}}\right)\geq 0.

(3.11)

Finally, assume that equality holds in (
3.11
). Since
\Phi^{\prime}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is monotone, it follows that
\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)
is constant on
(0,1)
, and consequently
\Phi
is linear on
[0,1]
, that is,

\Phi(\theta)=\mathscr{W}(w)=(1-\theta)\Phi(0)+\theta\Phi(1)=(1-\theta)\mathscr{W}(u^{r})+\theta\mathscr{W}(v^{r}),\quad\forall\theta\in[0,1].

This implies that
u\equiv cv
for some constant
c>0
. Moreover, if
r\neq p
, then
u\equiv v
by Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

An alternative proof of Lemma
3.4
can be obtained by using the fractional Picone inequality (see
[
9
, Proposition 4.2]
), together with Young’s inequality. This approach leads directly to the derivation of (

). Indeed, we have (see
[

)

\displaystyle\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\left[\frac{u(x)^{r}-v(x)^{r}}{u(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{u(y)^{r}-v(y)^{r}}{u(y)^{r-1}}\right]

(3.12)

\displaystyle+\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)\left[\frac{v(x)^{r}-u(x)^{r}}{v(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{v(y)^{r}-u(y)^{r}}{v(y)^{r-1}}\right]\geq 0,\quad\text{for a.e. }(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega.

Since
\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
, we infer that

C(N,s,p)\left(-p\ln|x-y|+B(N,s,p)\right)>0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times\Omega.

Consequently, it follows from (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle-pC(N,s,p)\ln|x-y|\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\left[\frac{u(x)^{r}-v(x)^{r}}{u(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{u(y)^{r}-v(y)^{r}}{u(y)^{r-1}}\right]

\displaystyle+C(N,s,p)B(N,s,p)\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\left[\frac{u(x)^{r}-v(x)^{r}}{u(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{u(y)^{r}-v(y)^{r}}{u(y)^{r-1}}\right]

\displaystyle-pC(N,s,p)\ln|x-y|\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)\left[\frac{v(x)^{r}-u(x)^{r}}{v(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{v(y)^{r}-u(y)^{r}}{v(y)^{r-1}}\right]

\displaystyle+C(N,s,p)B(N,s,p)\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|^{p-2}\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)\left[\frac{v(x)^{r}-u(x)^{r}}{v(x)^{r-1}}-\frac{v(y)^{r}-u(y)^{r}}{v(y)^{r-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\text{for a.e. }(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega.

It is worth noting that this inequality, being a pointwise estimate, is stronger than Díaz-Saa inequality (
3.9
).

3.4.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

We establish a fractional logarithmic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality. This result provides an estimate for intermediate Lebesgue norms in terms of a convex combination of the base
L^{p}
norm and the full fractional logarithmic norm. In the broader context of nonlinear partial differential equations, this inequality is a fundamental tool for controlling power-type nonlinearities, deriving a priori energy estimates, and studying both the existence and qualitative properties of normalized solutions.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
satisfy
sp<N
. For any
q\in[p,p_{s}^{*}]
, there exists a constant
C=C(N,s,p,q)>0
such that, for every
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, one has

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta},

where
\theta\in[0,1]
is uniquely determined by

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{p}+\frac{\theta}{p_{s}^{*}}.

Proof.

We begin by recalling the classical fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality due to Brezis and Mironescu
[
11
]
. This result asserts the existence of a positive constant
S>0
such that, for every
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-noting that
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(see Proposition
2.5
)-the following estimate holds

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq S[u]_{s,p}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta}.

(3.13)

On the other hand, Proposition
2.4
(3) ensures that for every
0<r<1
,

\displaystyle{}_{s,p}^{p}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\|u\|^{p}_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant depending only on
N,s,p
and
r
. Taking the
p
-th root, we infer that

[u]_{s,p}\leq C_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Substituting this estimate into (
3.13
) gives

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq S\left(C_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta},

which implies

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta},

where the constant
C
is given by
C:=SC_{0}^{\frac{\theta}{p}}
.
∎

3.5.
Pohozaev-type identity

In this subsection, we derive a Pohozaev identity associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplace operator. This identity serves as a fundamental tool in the study of critical exponents and in the analysis of geometric properties related to the corresponding boundary value problems.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary. We define the weighted fractional logarithmic regularity space
\mathscr{C}_{s+\log}^{p}(\overline{\Omega})
by

\mathscr{C}_{s+\log}^{p}(\overline{\Omega}):=\left\{u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\;:\;\frac{u(x)}{d(x)^{s}\ln\bigl(e+1/d(x)\bigr)}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right\}.

Remark 3.2
.

The weight
d(x)^{s}\ln\bigl(e+1/d(x)\bigr)
accurately reflects the boundary decay associated with the logarithmic singularity of the operator
\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}
. In contrast to the standard fractional
p
-Laplacian, for which solutions typically satisfy
u(x)/d(x)^{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the presence of the logarithmic correction naturally leads to the modified functional space
\mathscr{C}_{s+\log}^{p}(\overline{\Omega})
. A rigorous characterization of the singular boundary flux
\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)
requires establishing the precise asymptotic behavior

0<\liminf_{x\to\partial\Omega}\frac{u(x)}{d(x)^{s}\ln(e+1/d(x))}\leq\limsup_{x\to\partial\Omega}\frac{u(x)}{d(x)^{s}\ln(e+1/d(x))}<\infty,

in analogy with the classical fractional setting developed by Ros-Oton and Serra
[
33
]
. In order to isolate the algebraic structure of the Pohozaev identity and the defect measure
\Gamma_{s,p}(u)
, we assume a priori that
u\in\mathscr{C}_{s+\log}^{p}(\overline{\Omega})
. The proof of this optimal boundary regularity remains an open problem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with a
C^{1,1}
boundary. Assume that
u\in\mathscr{C}_{s+\log}^{p}(\overline{\Omega})
is a sufficiently regular weak solution to the pure power Dirichlet problem

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u=|u|^{q-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

for some exponent
q>p
. Then
u
satisfies the following Pohozaev identity:

\frac{N-sp}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(|x-y|)dxdy-\frac{N}{q}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}=\Gamma_{s,p}(u)-\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u),

(3.14)

where the term
\Gamma_{s,p}(u)
represents the interior defect measure, defined by

\Gamma_{s,p}(u):=\frac{C(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{|x-y|<1}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy,

(3.15)

and
\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)
denotes the boundary flux contribution on
\partial\Omega
, given by

\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u):=C_{s,p}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\lim_{x\to\sigma}\frac{u(x)}{d(x)^{s}\ln(e+1/d(x))}\right)^{p}(x\cdot\nu(\sigma))d\sigma,

where
\nu(\sigma)
denotes the outward unit normal vector at
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
, and
C_{s,p}>0
is a dimensional constant.

Proof.

The proof is based on the multiplier method, where the equation is tested against the Pohozaev-type vector field defined by
v(x):=x\cdot\nabla u(x)
. Multiplying the governing equation by
v(x)
and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}(x\cdot\nabla u)(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}udx=\int_{\Omega}(x\cdot\nabla u)|u|^{q-2}udx.

(3.16)

For the right-hand side, standard integration by parts gives

\int_{\Omega}(x\cdot\nabla u)|u|^{q-2}udx=\frac{1}{q}\int_{\Omega}x\cdot\nabla(|u|^{q})dx=-\frac{N}{q}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{q}dx.

(3.17)

For the left-hand side, we extend the operator to the symmetric domain
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. Since
u
vanishes outside
\Omega
, the gradient
\nabla u
develops a singular jump across the boundary
\partial\Omega
. By isolating this boundary contribution, we obtain the surface term
\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)
(see Ros-Oton and Serra
[
33
]
for a detailed analysis of boundary blow-up phenomena). The remaining interior term can be written in the symmetric form

\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\big(x\cdot\nabla u(x)-y\cdot\nabla u(y)\big)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\mathbf{K}^{s+\log}_{p}(|x-y|)dxdy.

(3.18)

Introducing the vector
z=x-y
and applying the chain rule, the symmetrized fractional integration-by-parts commutator produces a term involving

z\cdot\nabla\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(z)+2N\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(z).

For
|z|<1
, we compute the exact spatial gradient of the full kernel

\displaystyle z\cdot\nabla\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(z)

\displaystyle=z\cdot\nabla\Big(C(N,s,p)|z|^{-N-sp}\big(B(N,s,p)-p\ln|z|\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=C(N,s,p)z\cdot\nabla\big(|z|^{-N-sp}\big)\big(B(N,s,p)-p\ln|z|\big)

\displaystyle\quad+C(N,s,p)|z|^{-N-sp}z\cdot\nabla\big(B(N,s,p)-p\ln|z|\big)

\displaystyle=-(N+sp)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(z)-pC(N,s,p)|z|^{-N-sp}.

Substituting this differential identity into the commutator integral, the term
-(N+sp)
combines with the symmetric dimensional contribution
2N
, yielding exactly
(N-sp)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(z)
. After dividing by the factor
2p
arising from the chain rule, the first term evaluates to

\frac{N-sp}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy.

The remaining contribution gives rise to the defect measure

\Gamma_{s,p}(u)=\frac{1}{2p}\iint_{|x-y|<1}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\Big(pC(N,s,p)|x-y|^{-N-sp}\Big)dxdy.

Consequently, the interior symmetric integral (
3.18
) evaluates exactly to

-\frac{N-sp}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy+\Gamma_{s,p}(u).

Recalling that the full left-hand side of (
3.16
) consists of this interior contribution minus the boundary flux
\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)
, we equate it with the right-hand side in (
3.17
) to deduce

-\frac{N-sp}{2p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy+\Gamma_{s,p}(u)-\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)=-\frac{N}{q}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Multiplying by
-1
and rearranging the terms yields the final identity (
3.14
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The identity (
3.14
) reveals a profound geometric difference between the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian and the standard fractional
p
-Laplacian. For the standard operator, the defect measure
\Gamma_{s,p}(u)\equiv 0
. Consequently, on strictly star-shaped domains where
\mathscr{B}_{s,p}(u)>0
, the standard Pohozaev identity immediately yields a contradiction for critical and supercritical exponents (
q\geq p_{s}^{*}
), proving non-existence. However, in our logarithmic setting, testing the weak formulation of the PDE with
u
yields the full energy balance

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy=\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Substituting this into (
3.14
), we obtain

\left(\frac{N-sp}{p}-\frac{N}{q}\right)\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)=\Gamma_{s,p}(u).

For
q\geq p_{s}^{*}
, the coefficient
\left(\frac{N-sp}{p}-\frac{N}{q}\right)
is nonnegative. Moreover, if
\Omega
is strictly star-shaped, then
\mathscr{B}_{s+\log,p}(u)>0
. Since the defect measure
\Gamma_{s,p}(u)
is strictly positive, the two positive contributions on the left-hand side may precisely balance the positive defect measure on the right-hand side. Consequently, the classical Pohozaev-type non-existence argument is no longer applicable. The logarithmic correction introduces a strictly positive defect energy, which may support the existence of critical and even supercritical solutions in star-shaped domains.

4.
Continuous and compact embeddings and density results

In this section, we establish continuous and compact embedding theorems into Lebesgue and Hölder spaces, which precisely characterize the integrability and regularity properties of functions in both the subcritical and supercritical regimes with respect to the spatial dimension. To address the classical loss of compactness in unbounded domains, we prove a Strauss-type compact embedding result for radially symmetric functions. Finally, we derive density results showing that smooth functions are dense in the spaces
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. We start with the following first result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
with
sp<N
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then, for any
q\in[1,p_{s}^{*}]
, the following assertions hold:

(i)

The space
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
L^{q}(\Omega)
, that is,
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega).

(ii)

The embedding
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
is compact.

Proof.

Part (i) follows directly from Theorem
3.2
together with Hölder’s inequality. To prove (ii), let
(u_{k})
be a bounded sequence in
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. By (i) and the fractional Rellich-Kondrachov theorem (see Di Nezza et al.
[
20
]
), the embedding
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega)
is compact. Consequently, there exist a subsequence, still denoted by
(u_{k})
, and a function
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
such that

u_{k}\to u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega).

(4.1)

Let
v_{k}:=u_{k}-u
. We invoke the fractional Sobolev inequality (see Remark
2.2
), which ensures the existence of a constant
C=C(N,s,p,\Omega)>0
such that

\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq C[v_{k}]_{s,p}^{p}.

Moreover, combining this estimate with Proposition
2.4
(3), we obtain that for every
0<r<1
,

\displaystyle\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}[v_{k}]_{s+\log,p}^{p}+\frac{C2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|v_{k}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(4.2)

Since
(v_{k})
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, we define
M:=\sup_{k}[v_{k}]_{s+\log,p}^{p}<\infty.
Taking the limit supremum as
k\to\infty
in (
4.2
) and combining it with (
4.1
), we obtain

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq-\frac{CM}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}.

Since
r\in(0,1)
is arbitrary, letting
r\to 0^{+}
yields

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}=0\Longrightarrow u_{k}\to u\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega).

Finally, for any
q\in[1,p_{s}^{*})
, the strong convergence in
L^{p_{s}^{*}}(\Omega)
, together with the standard Hölder interpolation inequality on the bounded domain
\Omega
, yields the compactness of the embedding in
L^{q}(\Omega)
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary, and suppose that the supercritical growth condition
sp>N
is satisfied. Define the Hölder exponent
\beta:=s-\frac{N}{p}\in(0,1)
. Then, the space
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into the Hölder space
C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
, namely,

W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega}).

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
be arbitrary. First, by applying Proposition
2.4
(3), we obtain that for
r\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{}_{s,p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Combining this estimate with the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
3.1
), we infer that

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}C(s,p,N,\Omega)\right)\|u\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}.

Consequently,
u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Under the supercritical assumption
sp>N
, the classical fractional Morrey inequality (see Di Nezza et al.
[
20
]
) guarantees that
u
admits a continuous representative, and its Hölder seminorm of order
\beta=s-N/p
is strictly controlled by the standard fractional Gagliardo seminorm

[u]_{0,\beta}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leq C_{Morrey}[u]_{s,p},

(4.3)

where
C_{Morrey}>0
depends only on
N,s
, and
p
. Furthermore, the Morrey embedding ensures uniform
L^{\infty}
control via the full fractional Sobolev norm

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\infty}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(4.4)

We now synthesize these bounds to evaluate the full
C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
norm. Utilizing (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and the subadditivity of the
p
-th root, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[u]_{0,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[u]_{s,p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+C_{Morrey}[u]_{s,p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\infty}\left(C_{P}[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}+C_{int}^{p}[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+C_{Morrey}C_{int}[u]_{s+\log,p}

\displaystyle=\left(C_{\infty}(C_{P}+C_{int}^{p})^{1/p}+C_{Morrey}C_{int}\right)[u]_{s+\log,p}.

(4.5)

Defining the global embedding constant
C_{emb}:=C_{\infty}(C_{P}+C_{int}^{p})^{1/p}+C_{Morrey}C_{int}
, we arrive at the bound

\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{emb}\|u\|_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}.

(4.6)

This establishes the continuous embedding
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\beta}(\overline{\Omega})
directly for any
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, completing the proof without requiring smooth density approximations.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
1<p<\infty
, and
0<s<1
such that
sp<N
. Let
W_{rad}^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denote the closed subspace of radially symmetric functions in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, for every exponent
q\in(p,p_{s}^{*})
, the continuous embedding

W_{rad}^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

is compact.

Proof.

Let
(u_{k})
be a bounded sequence in
W_{rad}^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, meaning there exists a constant
M>0
such that
\|u_{k}\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq M
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We first establish that this sequence is uniformly bounded in the standard fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By applying Proposition
2.4
(3), we obtain that for every
0<r<1
,

\displaystyle{}_{s,p}^{p}\leq-\frac{1}{C(N,s,p)\ln r}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}+\frac{2^{p}\omega_{N}}{sp}r^{-sp}\|u_{k}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Adding
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}
to both sides, we deduce the existence of a constant
C_{1}>0
, independent of
k
, such that
\|u_{k}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{1}\|u_{k}\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{1}M
. Since
W_{rad}^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a reflexive Banach space, there exists a subsequence of
(u_{k})
, still denoted by
(u_{k})
, and a radially symmetric function
u\in W_{rad}^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, by the weak lower semicontinuity of the norm, the limit function
u
satisfies
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{1}M.

To prove strong convergence in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we exploit the radial symmetry of the sequence. According to the classical fractional Strauss radial decay estimate, any radially symmetric function
v\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies a pointwise bound away from the origin. Specifically, there exists a constant
C_{S}>0
, depending only on
N,s
, and
p
, such that for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|x|\geq 1
,

|v(x)|\leq C_{S}|x|^{-\frac{N-sp}{p}}\|v\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

(4.7)

We apply this decay estimate to the difference
v_{k}:=u_{k}-u
. Since
\|v_{k}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\|u_{k}\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 2C_{1}M
, equation (
4.7
) yields the uniform pointwise bound

|v_{k}(x)|\leq 2C_{S}C_{1}M|x|^{-\frac{N-sp}{p}}\quad\text{for a.e. }|x|\geq 1.

(4.8)

Now, let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrary positive number. We split the
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm of
v_{k}
into an integral over a large ball
B_{R}(0)
and an integral over its complement
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)
for some
R>1

\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q}=\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v_{k}(x)|^{q}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|v_{k}(x)|^{q}dx.

(4.9)

For the integral over
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)
, we interpolate between
L^{p}
and
L^{\infty}
using the pointwise decay (
4.8
). Since
q>p
, we can write
|v_{k}(x)|^{q}=|v_{k}(x)|^{q-p}|v_{k}(x)|^{p}
and estimate as follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|v_{k}(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{|x|\geq R}|v_{k}(x)|^{q-p}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|v_{k}(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(2C_{S}C_{1}MR^{-\frac{N-sp}{p}}\right)^{q-p}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(2C_{S}C_{1}M)^{q-p}(2M)^{p}R^{-\frac{(N-sp)(q-p)}{p}}.

Since we assume that
q>p
, it follows that the exponent
\frac{(N-sp)(q-p)}{p}
is strictly positive. Consequently, the term
R^{-\frac{(N-sp)(q-p)}{p}}
tends to zero as
R\to\infty
. Therefore, one can choose a radius
R>1
, independent of
k
, sufficiently large such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(0)}|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|^{q}dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Having fixed such a radius
R
, we now consider the integral over the bounded domain
B_{R}(0)
. By the local compactness of the fractional logarithmic embedding established earlier, the restriction mapping

W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(B_{R}(0))

is compact for every
q\in[1,p_{s}^{*}]
. Since
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we deduce that
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{q}(B_{R}(0))
. Consequently, there exists an integer
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq K
,

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|^{q}dx<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Substituting the above two estimates into the decomposition (
4.9
), we infer for all
k\geq K
,
\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{q}<\varepsilon.
Consequently,
u_{k}\to u
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which yields the compactness of the radial embedding.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
0<s<1
. Then, the space of smooth functions with compact support,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, is dense in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We first approximate
u
by compactly supported functions. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a standard cut-off function satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{1}(0)
, and
\text{supp}(\eta)\subset B_{2}(0)
. For
R>0
, define
\eta_{R}(x):=\eta(x/R)
and
u_{R}:=u\eta_{R}
. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
u_{R}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
R\to\infty
. To bound the seminorm of the difference, we utilize the algebraic decomposition
u_{R}(x)-u(x)-(u_{R}(y)-u(y)=(u(x)-u(y))(\eta_{R}(x)-1)+u(y)(\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(y))
and the inequality
|a+b|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|a|^{p}+|b|^{p})
to obtain

\displaystyle[u_{R}-u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}|\eta_{R}(x)-1|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+2^{p-1}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|u(y)|^{p}|\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle=:2^{p-1}J_{1}(R)+2^{p-1}J_{2}(R).

Since
\eta_{R}\to 1
pointwise and is uniformly bounded by
1
, the Dominated Convergence Theorem implies

\lim_{R\to\infty}J_{1}(R)=0.

For
J_{2}(R)
, we exploit the Lipschitz bound
|\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(y)|\leq\frac{C}{R}|x-y|
and the fact that
\text{supp}(\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y))=\overline{B_{1}(0)}
to obtain

J_{2}(R)\leq\frac{C^{p}}{R^{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(y)|^{p}dy\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(z)(z)dz.

Because
p>sp
, the singularity at the origin is integrable, meaning
\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{p-N-sp}(-p\ln|z|)dz<\infty
. Hence,
J_{2}(R)\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}}{R^{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p}\to 0
as
R\to\infty
. This establishes that
u_{R}\to u
strongly in
W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We may therefore assume without loss of generality that
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
has compact support. Let
\rho_{\epsilon}(x):=\epsilon^{-N}\rho(x/\epsilon)
be a standard symmetric mollifier and define
u_{\epsilon}:=u*\rho_{\epsilon}
. Classically,
u_{\epsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{\epsilon}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For the seminorm convergence, applying Minkowski’s integral inequality to the difference yields

\displaystyle[u_{\epsilon}-u]_{s+\log,p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho_{\epsilon}(z)\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}|(u(x-z)-u(y-z))-(u(x)-u(y))|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy\right)^{1/p}dz

(4.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\epsilon}(0)}\rho_{\epsilon}(z)[\tau_{z}u-u]_{s+\log,p}dz,

where
\tau_{z}u(x):=u(x-z)
denotes the translation operator. Standard properties of translations in
L^{p}
spaces ensure that ensures that
\lim_{|z|\to 0}[\tau_{z}u-u]_{s+\log,p}=0
. Since
\rho_{\epsilon}
is supported entirely within
B_{\epsilon}(0)
and has integral
1
, taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
4.10
) implies
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}[u_{\epsilon}-u]_{s+\log,p}=0
, concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set, let
1<p<\infty
, and let
0<s<1
. Then the space
C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
is dense in
W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
and let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrary positive number. We construct a smooth approximation of
u
by employing a locally finite partition of unity and carefully controlled local mollifications. We begin by defining an exhaustion of the domain
\Omega
by strictly interior open subdomains. For each integer
j\geq 1
, we define the sets

\Omega_{j}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>1/j\right\},

and for notational convenience, we set
\Omega_{j}=\emptyset
for all
j\leq 0
. We then construct a sequence of annular open sets
V_{j}
that cover
\Omega
by defining
V_{j}:=\Omega_{j+2}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{j-1}
for all
j\geq 1
. By construction, the collection
\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms a locally finite open cover of
\Omega
. Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to this cover, meaning that
\psi_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(V_{j})
,
0\leq\psi_{j}\leq 1
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(x)=1
for all
x\in\Omega
.

We decompose the function
u
into a locally finite sum by defining
u_{j}(x):=u(x)\psi_{j}(x)
. We must first verify that each
u_{j}
belongs to
W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Since
\psi_{j}
is bounded by
1
, the
L^{p}(\Omega)
norm is trivially bounded:
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. To estimate the fractional logarithmic Gagliardo seminorm, we utilize the algebraic identity
u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)=\psi_{j}(x)(u(x)-u(y))+u(y)(\psi_{j}(x)-\psi_{j}(y))
and the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|a|^{p}+|b|^{p})
. This yields

\displaystyle[u_{j}]_{s+\log,p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|\psi_{j}(x)|^{p}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+2^{p-1}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}|u(y)|^{p}|\psi_{j}(x)-\psi_{j}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy.

(4.11)

The first integral on the right-hand side of (
4
) is bounded by
2^{p-1}[u]_{s+\log,p}^{p}
. For the second integral, we exploit the Lipschitz continuity of
\psi_{j}
. Let
L_{j}=\|\nabla\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
be the Lipschitz constant of
\psi_{j}
. Since the positive logarithmic kernel
k_{s+\log,p}^{+}(z)=|z|^{-N-sp}(-p\ln|z|)_{+}
is supported entirely within the unit ball
B_{1}(0)
, we can bound the second integral by

2^{p-1}L_{j}^{p}\int_{\Omega}|u(y)|^{p}\left(\int_{|x-y|\leq 1}|x-y|^{p}\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dx\right)dy.

Because
p-sp=p(1-s)>0
, the singularity at the origin is integrable, and the inner integral evaluates to a finite constant
C_{0}=\int_{|z|\leq 1}|z|^{p-N-sp}(-p\ln|z|)dz<\infty
. Thus, the second term is bounded by
2^{p-1}L_{j}^{p}C_{0}\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}
, confirming that
u_{j}\in W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
with compact support in
V_{j}
. Next, let
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a standard symmetric mollifier supported in
B_{1}(0)
, and define the scaled mollifier
\rho_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-N}\rho(x/\delta)
.

For each
j\geq 1
, we choose a sufficiently small mollification radius
\delta_{j}>0
such that two conditions are met. First, to ensure the mollified function remains strictly inside
\Omega
and respects the local finiteness of the cover, we require
\delta_{j}<\text{dist}(\text{supp}(\psi_{j}),\partial(\Omega_{j+3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{j-2}))
. Second, by the continuity of translations in
L^{p}
spaces, we can choose
\delta_{j}
small enough such that the approximation error satisfies

\|u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}}-u_{j}\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j}}.

(4.12)

The convergence of the seminorm under mollification is justified by Minkowski’s integral inequality. Specifically, the difference can be written as an integral of translations, and we have

[u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}}-u_{j}]_{s+\log,p}\leq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\rho(z)[\tau_{\delta_{j}z}u_{j}-u_{j}]_{s+\log,p}dz,

which vanishes as
\delta_{j}\to 0
due to the strong continuity of the translation operator
\tau_{h}u_{j}(x)=u_{j}(x-h)
in the space
L^{p}(\Omega\times\Omega,d\mu)
equipped with the measure
d\mu(x,y)=\mathbf{k}^{s+\log,+}_{p}(x-y)dxdy
.

Finally, we define our global smooth approximation as the infinite sum

v(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}})(x).

By our geometric restriction on
\delta_{j}
, the support of
u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}}
is contained within
\Omega_{j+3}\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{j-2}
. Consequently, for any compact subset
K\subset\Omega
, only a finite number of terms in the sum are non-zero. This local finiteness guarantees that
v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, since
u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
, we can apply the triangle inequality to the full fractional logarithmic norm to obtain

\|u-v\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(u_{j}-u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}})\right\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|u_{j}-u_{j}*\rho_{\delta_{j}}\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}.

Substituting the bound from (
4.12
) into this infinite series yields

\|u-v\|_{W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)}<\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{2^{j}}=\varepsilon.

Since
u\in W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
and
u-v\in W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
, it follows that
v\in W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Consequently, we have constructed a function
v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap W^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
which can be chosen arbitrarily close to
u
. This establishes the desired density result.
∎

5.
The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian

In this section, we investigate the fundamental properties of the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}
. More precisely, we consider the following nonlinear Dirichlet eigenvalue problem.

(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(P)

We study the weak formulation of the problem by seeking functions
u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,\varphi)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(5.1)

The functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}
is introduced in (
2.20
). A function
u
satisfying (
5.1
) is referred to as an eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
for problem (
P
). We now proceed to establish the global boundedness of such eigenfunctions. To this end, we make use of a classical iterative argument; see, for example,
[
30
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
, and assume that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
. Let
u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution to (
5.1
). Then,
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of (
5.1
). We define

v_{0}=\frac{u}{\rho\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}},\quad\text{where}\quad\rho=\max\left\{1,\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{-1},C^{\frac{1}{p\vartheta^{2}}}\right\},

(5.2)

where
C>0
will be specified later. Since
v_{0}\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\|v_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\rho^{-1}
, we introduce the sequence
(w_{k})
defined by

w_{0}(x):=(v_{0}(x))^{+},\qquad w_{k}(x):=\big(v_{0}(x)-1+2^{-k}\big)^{+},\text{for }k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

We first note the following elementary properties of the sequence
(w_{k})
:

w_{k}=0\quad\text{a.e.\ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad w_{k}\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega),

together with

\begin{cases}0\leq w_{k+1}(x)\leq w_{k}(x),&\text{a.e.\ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\[4.0pt]
v_{0}(x)<(2^{k+1}-1)w_{k}(x),&\text{for }x\in\{w_{k+1}>0\}.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Moreover, we have the inclusion

\{w_{k+1}>0\}\subseteq\{w_{k}>2^{-(k+1)}\},\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N},

which implies

\displaystyle\big|\{w_{k+1}>0\}\big|\leq 2^{p(k+1)}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

(5.4)

We now define
U_{k}:=\|w_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p},
and we claim that
U_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Indeed, under the assumptions
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
, and by using (
5.3
) together with the inequality

|a^{+}-b^{+}|^{p}\leq|a-b|^{p-2}(a-b)(a^{+}-b^{+}),\quad\text{for all }a,b\in\mathbb{R},

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(w_{k+1},w_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}\big(\mathscr{J}_{+}(w_{k+1},w_{k+1})-\mathscr{J}_{-}(w_{k+1},w_{k+1})\big)+\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)}{2}\mathscr{J}_{s}(w_{k+1},w_{k+1})

\displaystyle U_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{w_{k+1}>0\}}w_{k+1}^{p}dx\leq C(N,s,p,\Omega)\big|\{w_{k+1}>0\}\big|^{1-\frac{p}{p_{s}^{*}}}\|w_{k+1}\|_{W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p}

for some constants

U_{k}\leq\frac{\eta^{k}}{\rho^{p}},\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N},

(5.8)

where
\eta\in(0,1)
. The estimate follows by induction. Indeed, from (

U_{0}\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\frac{1}{\rho^{p}}.

Assuming (

Furthermore, since

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
, and let

be a bounded, open, and connected set. Let

in
\Omega
and

in
\Omega
. Then

a.e. in

It is well-known that if

is a local extremum of the functional
\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
under the constraint

is an eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda=\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)
, which is the smallest eigenvalue that can be obtained in this variational framework. For this reason, we refer to this value as the first eigenvalue, since this variational characterization naturally leads to the definition:

\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}:=\inf\left\{\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)\;:\;u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega),\ \|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\right\}.

(5.13)

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. The first eigenvalue
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
is attained, and its associated extremal function
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}
is an eigenfunction of (
P
) corresponding to
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
. Moreover, if
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and

(1)

The first eigenvalue
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
is strictly positive, and the corresponding eigenfunction
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}

\Omega
.

(2)

The eigenvalue
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}

v\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is another eigenfunction associated with
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}

such that

\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}=cv.

Let
u\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction associated with an eigenvalue
\lambda
such that

in
\Omega
. Then, it follows that
\lambda=\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
.

Proof.

A minimizer can be obtained via the direct method of the calculus of variations. To this end, we define

\mathbf{I}=\inf\left\{\Phi_{s+\log,p}(u)\;:\;u\in\mathscr{C}\right\},\quad\text{where}\quad\Phi_{s+\log,p}(u):=\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u),

By the definition of the infimum, there exists a sequence
(u_{n})\subset\mathscr{C}
such that
\Phi_{s+\log,p}(u_{n})\leq\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{n}\leq\mathbf{I}+1.
From Proposition
2.4
(1) and (3), the sequence

is bounded in
W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
. Hence, by Theorem
4.1
, there exist a function

. On the other hand, again by Proposition

, we have

\frac{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p}-(v+\varepsilon)^{p}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}-(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p}}{(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\in W^{s+\log,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Indeed, it suffices to distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1:

v(x)>v(y)
and
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)>\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)
.
By the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(x)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(x)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p\max\{(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x),(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)\}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(p-1)(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)\max\{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-2}(x),(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-2}(y)\}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)}|\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)-\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq p\left(\frac{v(x)+\varepsilon}{\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}|v(x)-v(y)|

\displaystyle\quad+(p-1)\left(\frac{v(y)+\varepsilon}{\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)+\varepsilon}\right)^{p}|\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)-\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(p,\left\|\frac{v+\varepsilon}{\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left(|v(x)-v(y)|+|\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)-\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)|\right).

Case 2:

v(x)<v(y)
and
v(x)>v(y)
(mixed monotonicity between
v
and
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}
).
A similar argument yields

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(x)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}-\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)}\right|\leq\frac{p\max\{(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x),(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)\}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)}|v(x)-v(y)|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(p-1)(v+\varepsilon)^{p}(y)\max\{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-2}(x),(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-2}(y)\}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(x)(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}(y)}|\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)-\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(p,\left\|\frac{v+\varepsilon}{\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left(|v(x)-v(y)|+|\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(x)-\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}(y)|\right),

for some constant
C>0
. We then choose the above test functions in (
5.1
), satisfied respectively by
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}
and
v
. Summing the resulting identities and applying Lemma
3.4
(noting that
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
imply
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{\frac{B(N,s,p)}{p}}
), we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p},\frac{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p}-(v+\varepsilon)^{p}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)-\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(v,\frac{(v+\varepsilon)^{p}-(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p}}{(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p})^{p-1}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}-\frac{v^{p-1}}{(v+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\left((\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}+\varepsilon)^{p}-(v+\varepsilon)^{p}\right)dx.

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, and using Fatou’s lemma together with the dominated convergence theorem, we deduce

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p},\frac{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p})^{p}-v^{p}}{(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p})^{p-1}}\right)-\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(v,\frac{v^{p}-(\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p})^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)=0.

Finally, by Lemma
3.4
, we conclude that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
\phi^{s+\log}_{1,p}=cv.

(3)
Let
u\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda
such that
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and let
\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}
be the positive,
L^{p}
-normalized first eigenfunction. By the variational characterization (
5.13
), we immediately have

\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}\leq\frac{\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,u)}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}=\frac{\lambda\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}=\lambda.

(5.17)

To establish the reverse inequality, we use the fractional Picone inequality (see
[
9
, Proposition 4.2]
). Indeed, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the nonnegative test function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}=\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}(u+\varepsilon)^{1-p}\in W_{0}^{s+\log,p}(\Omega).
Accordingly, by employing
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
as a test function in the weak formulation of the eigenvalue problem satisfied by
u
, we obtain

\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{u+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}dx=\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,\varphi_{\varepsilon}).

(5.18)

Let
P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
denote the fractional Picone difference

P_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left(\frac{\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}(x)}{(u(x)+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}-\frac{\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}(y)}{(u(y)+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right).

By virtue of the fractional Picone inequality, we have

P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\leq\left|\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)-\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(y)\right|^{p},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We next decompose the energy functional into the contribution associated with the interior domain and the corresponding cross-interaction terms as follows:

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,\varphi_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dydx.

(5.19)

On the interior domain
\Omega\times\Omega
, the condition
|x-y|\leq\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
ensures that the kernel is strictly positive,
\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)>0
. Thus, the inequality is preserved

\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\left|\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)-\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(y)\right|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dxdy.

(5.20)

On the cross-domain
\Omega\times\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\big)
, we note that
u(y)=\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(y)=0.
So, the Picone difference reduces to

P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=u(x)^{p-1}\frac{\big(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)\big)^{p}}{\big(u(x)+\varepsilon\big)^{p-1}}.

Moreover, under the assumptions
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)<e^{-\frac{1}{sp}}
and
B(N,s,p)\geq 0
, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{B(N,s,p)C(N,s,p)-pC(N,s,p)\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy

\displaystyle=C(N,s,p)\Bigg[B(N,s,p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{\ln|x-y|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\Bigg]>0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}P_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dydx

(5.21)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u(x)^{p-1}\frac{\big(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)\big)^{p}}{(u(x)+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dy\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\big(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)\big)^{p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dy\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\left|\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(x)-\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}(y)\right|^{p}\mathbf{K}_{p}^{s+\log}(|x-y|)dydx.

Substituting (
5.20
) and (
5.21
) into (
5.19
) yields the global bound

\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}(u,\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathscr{J}_{s+\log,p}\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log},\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)=\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}.

(5.22)

Combining (
5.18
) and (
5.22
), we obtain

\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{u+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}dx\leq\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}.

Since
u>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, the sequence of functions
\left(\frac{u}{u+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}
converges pointwise a.e. to
\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. By the Monotone Convergence Theorem, and passing to the limit while taking into account that
\mathrm{supp}(u)=\Omega
, we obtain

\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\right)^{p}dx\leq\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}.

Since
\|\phi_{1,p}^{s+\log}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
, it follows that
\lambda\leq\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
. Together with (
5.17
), this yields
\lambda=\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

Recall that
\phi^{s}_{1,p}
denotes the positive, normalized eigenfunction of the fractional
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
in
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
associated with the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{s}_{1,p}
, which admits the variational characterization

\lambda^{s}_{1,p}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\\
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{C(N,s,p)}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy.

It is well-known that
\lambda^{s}_{1,p}>0
and that the above infimum is attained. By contrast, as shown in Theorem
5.4
, the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
associated with the fractional logarithmic
p
-Laplacian
(-\Delta)_{p}^{s+\log}
is, in general, not necessarily positive (see Proposition
2.4
). However, under appropriate geometric conditions on the domain
\Omega
, one can guarantee the strict positivity of
\lambda^{s+\log}_{1,p}
.
