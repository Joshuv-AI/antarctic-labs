Comprehensive characterization and prognostic analysis of multifocal intracranial germ cell tumors
Authors: Yi Zhang, Xinyi Chai, Yue Hu, Kan Deng, Yong Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGiven the limited evidence on multifocal intracranial germ cell tumors (iGCTs)—a rare but clinically distinct entity—this study comprehensively characterizes their clinical features and identifies key prognostic factors.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 48 patients diagnosed with multifocal iGCTs from 2006 to 2025 in our center. Demographics, clinical presentation, MRI characteristics, pathology, treatment modalities, and survival outcomes were analyzed. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods. Prognostic factors were identified through univariate and multivariate analyses.ResultsMedian age at diagnosis was 13 years (range: 6–31), with male predominance (87.5%). The most common symptoms were diabetes insipidus (81.2%) and hemiparesis (56.2%). Significant imaging features included hemiatrophy (47.9%) and heterogeneous enhancement (68.8%). Germinoma was diagnosed in 66.7% of patients and combined chemoradiotherapy (CHT + RT) was administered to 58.3% of patients. The 10-year OS and PFS rates were 84.6% and 70.6%, respectively. In this exploratory analysis, factors associated with favorable outcomes included germinoma histology (HR = 0.06, 95% CI 0.01–0.53, P = 0.011), combined CHT + RT (HR = 0.09, 95% CI 0.01–0.76, P = 0.027), and absence of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (HR = 0.14, 95% CI 0.02–1.06, P = 0.050 for PFS).ConclusionMultifocal iGCTs represent a distinct clinical entity with outcomes that are poorer compared to solitary iGCTs. Germinoma, combined chemoradiotherapy, and lymphocyte infiltration may be associated with survival, warranting further investigation. These findings emphasize the importance of histology-driven, multimodal treatment strategies for optimizing outcomes in this rare disease.

Introduction

Intracranial germ cell tumors (iGCTs) are a group of rare neoplasms that predominantly affect children and adolescents, exhibiting a marked predilection for midline structures [1]. The pineal and suprasellar regions represent the two most prevalent locations, accounting for approximately 50–60% and 30–40% of cases, respectively, while a minority of tumors arise in the basal ganglia, thalamus, or cerebral parenchyma. Less than 10% of patients present with multifocal iGCTs (including bifocal iGCTs), characterized by synchronous lesions in two or more brain regions such as the pineal-suprasellar areas, the sellar-basal ganglia region and sellar-pineal-basal ganglia region [2].

The pathogenesis of multifocal iGCTs remains controversial, discussion primarily centered on whether multifocal iGCTs represent synchronous primary tumors at distinct sites or metastatic spread from a single primary tumor [3]. Although criteria, such as those proposed by Zhang et al., exist to classify these lesions [4], for the purpose of this study we defined multifocal iGCTs by the simultaneous presence of two or more tumors in different neuroanatomical regions, with no evidence of cerebrospinal fluid dissemination—confirmed by both the absence of tumor cells in cerebrospinal fluid and the lack of abnormal ependymal enhancement on MRI.

While previous studies have documented the epidemiological, clinical features and outcomes of solitary iGCTs thoroughly [5–7], emerging evidence suggests that multifocal iGCTs may represent a distinct clinical entity with potentially more aggressive behavior, as indicated by their higher rates of tumor seeding and inferior survival outcomes compared to solitary iGCTs [8]. However, despite these concerning observations, research on this rare condition has been challenged by limited case availability. Consequently, the literature on multifocal iGCTs remains limited to case reports and small series [9–11], leaving significant gaps in the understanding of their clinical manifestations and outcomes.

Therefore, to address the critical gap, we conducted a retrospective study of 48 patients who were diagnosed with multifocal iGCTs at Peking Union Medical College Hospital from 2006 to the present, aiming to provide an in-depth analysis of their survival outcomes. Despite the limited sample size inherent to the scarcity of the disease, we anticipate that this preliminary investigation will offer valuable insights to inform clinical understanding and practice, while laying a solid foundation for future research.

Materials and methods

Patients

This was a single-center retrospective analysis of a prospectively established database of patients who were diagnosed with multifocal iGCTs based on biopsy pathology at our center between January 2006 and October 2025.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients with (i) newly diagnosed, previously untreated multifocal iGCTs, defined by the synchronous presence of two or more distinct tumors in separate neuroanatomical regions as confirmed by MRI. (ii) surgical intervention (biopsy or debulking surgery) and pathological examination performed at our institution. (iii) definitive diagnosis of either germinoma or non-germinomatous cell tumor (NGGCT). The exclusion criteria were as follows: patients with (i) incomplete medical record, follow-up information or MRI information (ii) evidence of tumor dissemination, defined by either positive cerebrospinal fluid cytology for tumor cells or abnormal ependymal enhancement on MRI. (iii)with a history of prior treatment or presenting at relapse.Bifocal germinomas (e.g., sellar-pineal) were included as a subset of multifocal iGCTs.

This study was approved by the ethical review committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital. Due to the nature of this retrospective study, the informed consent from patients was waived.

Data collection

This study involved a retrospective collection of comprehensive data from medical records and MRI images for all patients enrolled. Baseline clinical information included patient’s age at diagnosis, gender, and the presence of key symptoms (cognitive disturbance, hemiparesis, diabetes insipidus, and hydrocephalus). Imaging variables included tumor size (maximum tumor diameter ≥ 3 cm, < 3 cm), T1- and T2-weighted signal intensity, contrast enhancement pattern, and the presence of calcification, cystic components, or parenchymal atrophy, tumor location and number of lesions.

Laboratory and pathological data included cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) levels of AFP and β-hCG(with elevations defined as ≥ 10 ng/mL and ≥ 5 IU/L, respectively). The pathological diagnosis was classified into germinoma and NGGCT, which was defined as yolk sac tumor, choriocarcinoma, or embryonal carcinoma, either alone or in combination with each other, with germinoma, or with teratoma. The presence of lymphocyte infiltration within tumor tissue was recorded in a binary manner (present vs. absent), the assessment of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) in our study was performed by neuropathologists using a standardized methodology on hematoxylin and eosin-stained histological sections. Treatment strategies included surgical approach, treatment modality and radiotherapy fields. Surgical approach included biopsy and debulking surgery while treatment modality was classified into combined chemoradiotherapy (CHT + RT) and others. Chemotherapy regimens included PE (Cisplatin + VP16), PEI (Cisplatin + VP16 + Ifosfamide), CE(Carboplatin + VP16), and VMPP (Cisplatin + Methotrexate + Vincristine + PYM). Radiotherapy modalities included CSI (Craniospinal irradiation), WBI (Whole Brain Irradiation), WVI (Whole ventricular irradiation), and PB (Primary Boost). Radiotherapy doses were administered as follows: WVI ranged from 18 to 24 Gy, CSI ranged from 18 to 36 Gy, WBI ranged from 20 to 24 Gy, PB alone ranged from 30.6 to 54 Gy depending on the treatment protocol.

Outcome measures included recurrence status, time to treatment failure, follow-up duration, final patient status, and treatment response. Treatment response was assessed according to the Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology (RANO) criteria for CNS tumors, categorized as: Complete Response (CR): disappearance of all lesions and normalized tumor markers for ≥ 4 weeks; Partial Response (PR): ≥ 50% reduction in tumor size for ≥ 4 weeks; No Change (NC): < 50% reduction or < 25% increase over 6–8 weeks; Progressive Disease (PD): ≥ 25% increase or new lesions. Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) served as the primary and secondary endpoints, respectively. OS was calculated from treatment until death or last known follow-up. PFS was defined as the time from treatment to the first radiographic evidence of disease progression on MRI. For patients with no evidence of progression, OS and PFS were considered equivalent.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R4.3.3. Categorical variables across the four prognostic groups (CR, PR, NC, PD) were compared using Fisher’s exact test. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis were included in a multivariate logistic regression model to identify potential prognostic factors. OS and PFS were assessed using the Kaplan–Meier method, and compared with the log-rank test. Univariate Cox regression was performed to quantify the effect for key prognostic factors identified in the logistic regression analysis. No missing data were present. The proportional odds assumption for ordinal logistic regression was tested and satisfied. Seven covariates with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate model.

Clinical presentation

The most frequent symptom was diabetes insipidus (81.2%), followed by hemiparesis (56.2%). In addition, 14 patients suffered from cognitive disturbance while another 14 patients presented hydrocephalus. The other abnormal neurological symptoms included anterior hypopituitarism (47.9%), headache (33.3%), nausea/vomiting (31.3%). The median duration of symptom was 9 months (interquartile range, 9–12 months). A delayed diagnosis of six months or more was observed in 34 patients (70.8%).

Radiologic features

All patients underwent presurgical MRI scans. The tumors in 44 patients (91.7%) demonstrated hypo- to iso-intensity on T1-weighted sequences, with all patients showing hyper-intensity on T2-weighted sequences. The most typical enhancement patterns were heterogeneous (68.8%) after administration of intravenous contrast material. Cystic formation was identified in 15 patients (31.2%), and calcification was present in 11 patients (22.9%). Notably, parenchymal atrophy was identified in 23 patients (47.9%), which mainly presented as cerebral hemiatrophy. Tumor maximum diameter exceeding 3 cm was observed in 25 patients (52.1%) and tumors involving three or more distinct sites were found in 15 patients (31.2%).

Diagnosis and treatment

All patients received AFP and β-hCG test in CSF. Elevated levels of CSF-HCG and CFS-AFP were noted in 18 (37.5%) and 5 (10.4%) patients, respectively. Lymphocyte infiltration within the tumor was present in 33 patients (68.8%). Of note, immunophenotyping (e.g., CD8 +, FoxP3 +, PD-1 + subsets) was not performed due to the retrospective nature of the study. Pathologically, germinoma was diagnosed in 32 patients (66.7%), while NGGCTs accounted for the remaining 16 cases (33.3%).

In terms of treatment modality, CHT + RT was administered to 28 patients (58.3%). The most common radiation strategy was craniospinal irradiation with a primary boost (CSI + PB), employed in 17 patients (35.4%). This was followed by craniospinal irradiation alone (CSI) in 10 patients (20.8%) and whole ventricle irradiation with a primary boost (WVI + PB) in 6 patients (12.5%). Primary boost (PB) alone was used in 5 patients (10.4%), and whole brain irradiation (WBI) was utilized in 2 patients (4.2%). Notably, 8 patients (16.7%) did not undergo radiotherapy due to poor patient compliance. Surgical intervention was predominantly biopsy (79.2%), with debulking surgery performed in 10 cases (20.8%).

Outcomes

Kaplan–Meier survival curves for PFS and OS were presented in Fig. 2. All patients underwent follow-up without loss. The median follow-up period for the entire cohort was 10 years (range: 0.1–17 years). During this period, disease recurrence occurred in 14 patients (29.2%), and 7 patients (14.6%) died. Subsequently, the statistically significant factors identified in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. On the premise that the regression model demonstrated good fitness and statistical significance, lymphocyte infiltration, treatment modality, and histological type were identified as independent prognostic factors for patients with multifocal iGCTs (p < 0.05).

To validate whether the independent prognostic factors identified above also influence long-term survival, we performed Kaplan–Meier analysis and univariate Cox regression analysis. Patients treated with CHT + RT had significantly lower risk of death (HR 0.09, 95% CI 0.01–0.76, p = 0.027) and progression (HR 0.13, 95% CI 0.04–0.47, p = 0.002) compared to those without combined therapy. Extended radiotherapy fields were also associated with improved OS (HR 0.14, 95% CI 0.02–1.00, p = 0.050) and PFS (HR 0.15, 95% CI 0.03–0.68, p = 0.013) relative to primary boost alone. For histological type, univariate Cox regression confirmed significantly better outcomes for germinoma compared to NGGCT, with HRs of 0.06 (95% CI 0.01–0.53, p = 0.011) for OS and 0.10 (95% CI 0.03–0.35, p < 0.001) for PFS, consistent with the Kaplan–Meier analysis (log-rank p < 0.01, Fig. 3). The absence of lymphocyte infiltration showed a trend toward improved PFS (HR 0.14, 95% CI 0.02–1.06, p = 0.050), consistent with the Kaplan–Meier results (log-rank p < 0.05, Fig. 3). When stratified by histology, germinoma patients receiving extended-field radiotherapy (n = 26) had no deaths or progressions during follow-up, resulting in 100% 10-year OS and PFS, compared to 80% and 40% for those without radiotherapy (n = 5). Among NGGCT patients, those receiving extended-field radiotherapy (n = 8) had 10-year OS and PFS rates of 75% and 50%, respectively, compared to 50% and 0% for those without radiotherapy (n = 4), and 33.3% and 0% for those receiving primary boost alone (n = 3)(p < 0.05).

Prognostic stratification by tumor topography and histology

The locations of the multifocal lesions in this cohort were heterogeneous. Classic bifocal tumors involved the sellar and pineal regions exclusively (n = 14). The remaining multifocal tumors involved various combinations of other intracranial sites, including the basal ganglia, thalamostriate region, cerebral parenchyma, and brain stem.

When patients were stratified into classic bifocal germinomas (sellar-pineal germinomas, n = 14) versus all other patients (n = 34), significant prognostic differences emerged. Classic bifocal germinomas exhibited outstanding outcomes with no deaths or recurrences (0%), whereas the remaining patients experienced substantially higher event rates (mortality: 20.6%, recurrence: 41.2%). All mortality (7/7) and progression events (14/14) in the entire cohort occurred within this latter group (PFS log-rank p = 0.006).

Discussion

Our study, through an in-depth analysis of the present largest multifocal iGCTs cohort, suggests that these tumors may represent a distinct clinical entity with distinct epidemiological, clinical, and imaging characteristics that set them apart from solitary iGCTs. The male predominance observed in our cohort aligns with the well-established epidemiological signature of iGCTs in general [12, 13]. However, it is noteworthy that male predominance persists even within the subset of patients with sellar/suprasellar involvement, which contrast to the documented female predominance in the sellar/suprasellar region [14, 15], suggesting that the multifocal nature of these tumors may be underpinned by a different biological predisposition that warrants further investigation. The clinical presentation of multifocal iGCTs depend on their tumor location. The high incidence of diabetes insipidus and other endocrine abnormalities can be attributed to the dysfunction of the hypothalamic pituitary axis caused by lesions in the suprasellar region. Increased intracranial pressure and hydrocephalus are often consequences of lesions in the pineal region obstructing the cerebral aqueduct [16]. The high frequency of hemiparesis and cognitive disturbances in our cohort stems from the involvement of the basal ganglia and thalamus in multifocal iGCTs, these insidious onsets and atypical clinical symptoms likely contribute to the considerable diagnostic delay observed in our patients [17–19].

A distinctive neuroimaging feature in our multifocal iGCTs cohort was the high prevalence of parenchymal atrophy—particularly cerebral hemiatrophy—observed in nearly half of patients. This atrophy was particularly associated with basal ganglia/thalamic involvement (64.3% vs. 25.0% in other locations, p = 0.017), which likely reflects Wallerian degeneration, the histopathological degeneration of axons and myelin sheaths [20] that highly suggest slow and invasive tumor growth, frequently associated with basal ganglia or thalamus iGCTs that infiltrate the nerve fiber tracts [18]. Previous study has described a characteristic imaging sequence where hemiatrophy presents as the initial MRI finding while a classic enhancing tumor mass developing only upon follow-up imaging after a delay of months to years in iGCTs [21]. In multifocal iGCTs, this subtle initial sign could be easily overlooked or misinterpreted, given the complex and aggressive nature of multifocal iGCTs, hemiatrophy and relating symptoms such as hemiparesis and cognitive dysfunction should be taken seriously. Moreover, the presence of cystic components and calcifications observed in our cohort further diversifies the imaging spectrum of multifocal iGCTs and aligns with features more commonly associated with NGGCTs.

The histological subtype was a significant characteristics associated to patient outcome in our cohort, which is consistent with the Matsutani classification and management principles for iGCTs [12, 22]. Patients with germinomas demonstrated significantly superior OS and PFS compared to those with NGGCTs, confirming that pathology remains an important outcome determinant in multifocal iGCTs [23–25]. However, a particularly intriguing finding from our multifocal iGCTs cohort was the significant association between the presence of lymphocyte infiltration within the tumor tissue and inferior survival outcomes. Conventionally, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes are regarded as a marker of active host anti-tumor immunity. This opposite observation in our study suggests a more complex and potentially immunosuppressive role of the tumor microenvironment in multifocal iGCTs. This viewpoint is supported by previous studies on solitary iGCTs which, despite associating high lymphocytic infiltration with better outcomes, have highlighted the immunosuppressive nature of such infiltration [26, 27]. Specifically, the microenvironment is characterized by an abundance of regulatory T cells and a functional exhaustion of cytotoxic T cells, a state often enforced by the prominent expression of immune checkpoints like PD-1 and PD-L1 [28–30]. These findings from the literature, together with our observations, lead to the hypothesis that immune checkpoint inhibitors might benefit a subset of patients with multifocal iGCTs, which warrants further investigation with prospective immunoprofiling. However, the very small subgroup size (e.g., NC group, n = 3) limits the robustness of this comparison, and this finding should be interpreted with particular caution. Therefore, the association between lymphocyte infiltration and survival observed in this study is exploratory only and warrants validation in larger prospective cohorts.

The distinct clinical and pathological profiles of multifocal iGCTs highlight the importance of a nuanced treatment strategy. Our study highlight that combined chemoradiotherapy is associated with for achieving better outcomes in multifocal iGCTs, which is consistent with and extends the findings from previous studies on solitary iGCTs [31, 32]. Furthermore, our analysis demonstrated a significant survival advantage for patients treated with extended-field radiotherapy compared to those receiving primary boost alone. Therefore, although dose reduction is preferred to decrease late toxicities in many study regarding solitary iGCTs, extended-field radiotherapy appears to be essential based on our findings for multifocal iGCTs to ensure disease control [5, 7, 33], which aligns with the aggressive behavior of iGCTs observed in our cohort. The implementation of this risk-adapted treatment strategy depend on an accurate histological diagnosis. Facilitated by the expertise in minimally invasive neurosurgical techniques, a vast majority of patients underwent biopsy surgery in our center, which allows tailored treatment for different subtypes. Beyond its diagnostic merits, debulking surgery was performed in a subset of NGGCT cases in our cohort, as NGGCTs are often less sensitive to chemoradiation [34], debulking surgery serves as a valuable approach to improve local control [35]. Therefore, an optimized surgical strategy for multifocal iGCTs should prioritize diagnostic biopsy for all patients, while actively considering tumor resection for those with NGGCTs.

Several limitations pertain in our study. The retrospective, single-center nature may introduce selection bias. Specifically, our institutional expertise in midline endoscopic surgery likely led to a higher proportion of patients presenting with sellar region symptoms. Second, while all patients underwent spinal MRI and CSF cytology at diagnosis, these standard staging modalities have limited sensitivity for detecting microscopic dissemination. The absence of routine serial CSF sampling further limits our ability to definitively distinguish multifocal primaries from early metastatic disease. Therefore, some cases classified as multifocal may represent undetected metastasis. Third, as this is a retrospective observational study, the treatment comparisons are associative rather than causal. Changes in treatment paradigms over the 20-year study period may have introduced bias, and multivariable adjustment for treatment era was not feasible due to the limited number of events. These findings should therefore be interpreted with caution. Fourth, the limited sample size (n = 48) and sparse events (7 deaths, 14 progressions) create a substantial risk of statistical overfitting, and our findings should therefore be considered exploratory. Nevertheless, this study represents the most comprehensive clinical characterization of multifocal iGCTs reported to date and reinforces the central role of histology-directed multimodal therapy in optimizing outcomes.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study provides a comprehensive characterization of multifocal intracranial germ cell tumors. While the possibility of early metastatic disease cannot be definitively ruled out, these multifocal presentations constitute a clinically distinct subgroup with unique features that necessitate tailored treatment strategies. Our exploratory findings suggest that germinoma, combined chemoradiotherapy and extended-field radiotherapy may be associated with favorable outcomes, but require validation. Notably, the unexpected association between tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and poorer prognosis generates the hypothesis of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in multifocal iGCTs, which requires validation in future studies. These findings underscore the necessity of tailored treatment strategies and highlight the critical role of accurate diagnosis through biopsy and surgical intervention. Future multicenter studies integrating immunophenotyping and molecular profiling are essential to validate our hypotheses and to refine risk stratification and therapeutic approaches for this rare and challenging disease.
