Karhunen-Loève Expansion for Fluid Antenna Systems: Information-Theoretic Optimal Channel Compression and Outage Analysis
Tuo Wu

Abstract
Fluid antenna systems (FAS) achieve spatial diversity by dynamically switching among N N densely packed ports, but the resulting spatially correlated Rayleigh channels render exact outage analysis intractable. Existing block-correlation models (BCM) impose structural approximations on the channel covariance matrix that can introduce optimistic performance bias. This paper proposes a principled Karhunen-Loève (KL) expansion framework that decomposes the N N -dimensional correlated FAS channel into independent eigenmodes and performs a controlled rank- K K truncation, reducing the outage analysis to a K K -dimensional integration with K ≪ N K\ll N . Closed-form outage expressions are derived for the rank-1 and rank-2 cases, and a general Gauss-Hermite quadrature formula is provided for arbitrary K K . On the theoretical front, it is proved via Anderson’s inequality that the KL approximation always overestimates the outage probability, providing a conservative guarantee essential for secure system design. Leveraging the Slepian–Landau–Pollak concentration theorem, it is established that only K ∗ = 2 ​ ⌈ W ⌉ + 1 K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1 eigenmodes are needed regardless of N N , where W W is the normalized aperture. It is further shown that the KL truncation achieves the Gaussian rate-distortion bound, certifying it as the information-theoretically optimal channel compression. Extensive numerical results confirm that (i) theoretical predictions match Monte Carlo simulations, (ii) the entropy fraction converges faster than the power fraction, (iii) the KL framework uniformly outperforms BCM in approximation accuracy while avoiding the optimistic bias inherent in block-diagonal models, and (iv) the effective degrees of freedom scale with the aperture rather than the number of ports.

Despite this rapid progress, a fundamental bottleneck persists in FAS outage analysis. Since adjacent ports share common scattering environments, the channel vector
\mathbf{g}=[g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}]^{T}
follows a correlated complex Gaussian distribution whose covariance matrix
\mathbf{R}
exhibits a Toeplitz structure under the widely adopted Jakes model
[
29
]
. Computing the outage probability therefore requires the joint CDF of
N
correlated Rayleigh random variables—an
N
-dimensional integral that is analytically intractable for general
N
and whose computational cost grows exponentially with
N
. This intractability is not merely a technical inconvenience: it fundamentally limits the ability to perform closed-form system optimization, derive diversity-multiplexing tradeoffs, or obtain design insights that scale with the system parameters.

To circumvent this difficulty, block-correlation models (BCM) have been proposed
[
30
,
31
]
, which partition the
N
ports into
D
independent blocks with a common intra-block correlation coefficient, reducing the
N
-dimensional integral to a product of
D
one-dimensional integrals. The variable BCM (VBCM) extends this by allowing block-specific correlation coefficients
[
32
]
, partially reducing the approximation error. While these models yield tractable closed-form expressions, they introduce a fundamental structural distortion: by imposing a block-diagonal constraint on
\mathbf{R}
, they eliminate all inter-block correlations present in the true Toeplitz matrix. This removal artificially inflates the effective diversity order—since the true channel is more correlated than the block-diagonal model suggests—and produces a systematic
optimistic bias
in outage estimation. The BCM/VBCM consistently underestimate the true outage probability, which is particularly dangerous in security-critical applications
[
25
,
21
]
: an underestimated outage implies an overestimated secrecy rate, potentially causing the system to transmit at a rate that is not actually secure. Moreover, as the number of ports
N
grows, the BCM/VBCM must increase the number of blocks
D
proportionally, making them inherently non-scalable.

The key observation motivating this paper is that the Jakes correlation matrix
\mathbf{R}
possesses a
rapidly decaying eigenvalue spectrum
that is fundamentally different from its block-diagonal approximation. Specifically, by the Slepian–Landau–Pollak concentration theorem
[
34
,
35
]
, the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{R}
exhibit a sharp phase transition at index
K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1
: the first
K^{*}
eigenvalues are
\Theta(N/K^{*})
while the remaining
N-K^{*}
eigenvalues decay super-exponentially to zero. Crucially,
K^{*}
depends only on the normalized aperture
W
, not on the number of ports
N
. This spectral concentration property suggests a principled alternative to BCM/VBCM: decompose the channel vector
\mathbf{g}
via its Karhunen-Loève (KL) expansion
\mathbf{g}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}
into
N
statistically independent eigenmodes
\{z_{k}\}
, retain only the
K
dominant ones, and reduce the
N
-dimensional correlated outage problem to a
K
-dimensional independent one. Unlike BCM/VBCM, this approach imposes no structural constraint on
\mathbf{R}
, works directly with the true eigenbasis, and admits a rigorous truncation error bound. The resulting
K
-dimensional integral can be evaluated efficiently via Gauss-Hermite quadrature, with closed-form expressions available for the rank-1 and rank-2 special cases.

Realizing this approach with full analytical rigor, however, requires overcoming three non-trivial challenges.
First
, while the KL truncation reduces the channel power, it is not immediately clear whether this reduction leads to a conservative or optimistic outage estimate. Establishing the direction of the bias requires invoking Anderson’s inequality
[
33
]
for symmetric convex sets—a result from geometric probability that, when applied to the outage event (a product of
N
disks in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
), proves that the KL-truncated outage is always an upper bound on the true outage, with monotone convergence as
K
increases.
Second
, establishing that
K^{*}=\mathcal{O}(2W+1)
suffices for near-exact outage prediction requires a careful asymptotic analysis connecting the Toeplitz eigenvalue distribution to the Szegő–Slepian spectral concentration theory, and making this connection precise for finite
N
.
Third
, certifying the KL truncation as information-theoretically optimal—in the sense that no other rank-
K
approximation of
\mathbf{R}
can achieve a lower channel compression distortion at the same rate—requires connecting the KL truncation to the Gaussian rate-distortion function via reverse water-filling, and showing that the block-diagonal constraint of BCM/VBCM prevents them from achieving this fundamental bound.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

KL expansion framework for FAS outage analysis:
This paper derives the outage probability of FAS under the KL-truncated channel model, reducing the
N
-dimensional correlated problem to a
K
-dimensional independent one. Closed-form expressions are obtained for the rank-1 and rank-2 cases, and a general Gauss-Hermite quadrature formula is provided for arbitrary
K
.

•

Conservative outage guarantee:
By invoking Anderson’s inequality on symmetric convex sets, this paper proves that the KL-truncated outage probability is always an upper bound on the true outage, with monotone convergence as
K
increases. This conservative property is critical for secure system design, where optimistic estimates can lead to security breaches.

•

Effective degrees of freedom:
Leveraging the Slepian–Landau–Pollak concentration theorem, this paper establishes that the effective number of spatial degrees of freedom is
K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1
, depending only on the normalized aperture
W
and independent of the port count
N
. This provides a theoretical foundation for the scalability of the KL approach.

•

Rate-distortion optimality:
This paper proves that the KL truncation achieves the Gaussian rate-distortion bound via reverse water-filling, confirming it as the information-theoretically optimal channel compression. In contrast, the BCM/VBCM, constrained to a block-diagonal structure, operates strictly above this bound.

•

Comprehensive numerical verification:
Extensive Monte Carlo simulations are provided to validate all theoretical predictions, including the conservative outage guarantee, the eigenvalue phase transition, the entropy-power duality, the ergodic capacity lower bound, and the rate-distortion optimality. A systematic comparison with BCM, VBCM, and i.i.d. models is also provided.

The system under consideration is a point-to-point wireless communication system in which a single-antenna base station (BS) transmits to a user equipped with a one-dimensional fluid antenna system (FAS) capable of switching among
N
discrete ports within a linear space of
W\lambda
, where
W
is the normalized antenna aperture and
\lambda
is the carrier wavelength. The system operates under quasi-static block Rayleigh fading.

II-A

Signal Model

The BS transmits the signal
s\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
with transmit power
P
. The received signal at the
n
-th port of the FAS is

\displaystyle y_{n}=\sqrt{P}\,d^{-a/2}\,g_{n}\,s+n_{n},\quad n=1,\ldots,N,

(1)

where
g_{n}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\eta)
is the complex channel coefficient from the BS to the
n
-th FAS port,
d
is the BS–user distance,
a
is the path-loss exponent, and
n_{n}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
is the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).

The FAS selects the optimal port that maximizes the instantaneous channel gain:

\displaystyle n^{*}=\arg\max_{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}|g_{n}|^{2}.

(2)

The resulting instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is

\displaystyle\gamma=\bar{\gamma}\cdot\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\frac{|g_{n}|^{2}}{\eta},

(3)

where
\bar{\gamma}\triangleq Pd^{-a}\eta/\sigma^{2}
denotes the average SNR.

II-B

FAS Channel Correlation Model

The channel vector
\mathbf{g}=[g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}]^{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{\Sigma})
is spatially correlated due to the close proximity of adjacent FAS ports. The covariance matrix is
\mathbf{\Sigma}=\eta\,\mathbf{R}
, where
\mathbf{R}
is the normalized correlation matrix. Under the Jakes model, the
(k,l)
-th entry of
\mathbf{R}
is

\displaystyle[\mathbf{R}]_{k,l}=J_{0}\!\left(\frac{2\pi|k-l|W}{N-1}\right),

(4)

where
J_{0}(\cdot)
is the zeroth-order Bessel function of the first kind. The matrix
\mathbf{R}
is Hermitian positive semi-definite with a symmetric Toeplitz structure. Its diagonal entries satisfy
[\mathbf{R}]_{n,n}=1
for all
n
, so
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{R})=N
.

II-C

Outage Probability

The outage probability is defined as the probability that the instantaneous SNR falls below a target threshold
\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{out}}=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\gamma\leq\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}\right)=F_{\max}\!\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}}\right),

(5)

where
F_{\max}(x)\triangleq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\max_{n}|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta\leq x\right)
is the CDF of the normalized maximum channel gain.

Remark 1
(Analytical Challenge)

Computing
F_{\max}(x)
requires the joint CDF of
N
correlated Rayleigh random variables, which involves the
N
-dimensional integral

\displaystyle F_{\max}(x)=\int\cdots\int_{\mathcal{D}(x)}f_{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{g})\,d\mathbf{g},

(6)

where
\mathcal{D}(x)=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta\leq x,\ \forall n\}
and
f_{\mathbf{g}}
is the multivariate complex Gaussian density. Due to the non-diagonal Toeplitz structure of
\mathbf{R}
, this integral does not admit a product form and is analytically intractable for general
N
. This motivates the KL expansion approach developed in the next section.

III
Karhunen-Loève Expansion of the FAS Channel

With the system model and the intractability of the exact outage integral established, this section develops the KL expansion of the FAS channel. The key idea is to diagonalize the correlated channel via its eigenbasis, enabling a controlled low-rank approximation that preserves the true spectral structure of
\mathbf{R}
without any block-diagonal constraint.

III-A

Eigendecomposition of the Correlation Matrix

The eigendecomposition of the correlation matrix is performed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^{H},

(7)

where
\mathbf{U}=[\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{N}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is the unitary eigenvector matrix and
\mathbf{\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})
contains the eigenvalues ordered as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0
. Since
\mathbf{R}
is a correlation matrix with unit diagonal, the eigenvalues satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{R})=N.

(8)

Let
u_{n,k}\triangleq[\mathbf{U}]_{n,k}
denote the
(n,k)
-th entry of
\mathbf{U}
. The unitarity of
\mathbf{U}
guarantees that for each port
n
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}|u_{n,k}|^{2}=1,\quad\forall\,n=1,\ldots,N.

(9)

III-B

KL Representation of the Channel

Using (
7
), the channel vector admits the Karhunen-Loève (KL) expansion:

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}\mathbf{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{z},

(10)

where
\mathbf{z}=[z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}]^{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})
is a vector of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) standard complex Gaussian random variables. The channel coefficient at port
n
is thus expressed as

\displaystyle g_{n}=\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,z_{k}.

(11)

Remark 2
(Physical Interpretation of Eigenmodes)

Each eigenmode
z_{k}
represents an independent spatial degree of freedom of the FAS channel. The eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
quantifies the power captured by the
k
-th mode. In the presence of strong spatial correlation (small
W
or large
N/W
), the eigenvalue spectrum decays rapidly: a few dominant modes capture most of the channel energy, while the remaining modes contribute negligible power. This spectral concentration is the key property that enables low-rank truncation.

III-C

Low-Rank Truncation

The channel is approximated by retaining only the
K
dominant eigenmodes (
K\ll N
):

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}=\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,z_{k},\quad n=1,\ldots,N.

(12)

The truncated channel vector is
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}_{K}\mathbf{\Lambda}_{K}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{K}
, where
\mathbf{U}_{K}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times K}
contains the first
K
columns of
\mathbf{U}
,
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{K}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{K})
, and
\mathbf{z}_{K}=[z_{1},\ldots,z_{K}]^{T}
.

The truncation error at port
n
is

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\triangleq g_{n}-\tilde{g}_{n}=\sqrt{\eta}\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,z_{k},

(13)

with variance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\Delta_{n}|^{2}]=\eta\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}\lambda_{k}|u_{n,k}|^{2}\leq\eta\lambda_{K+1},

(14)

where the inequality follows from
\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}|u_{n,k}|^{2}\leq 1
(by (
9
)) and
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{K+1}
for
k\geq K+1
.

Proposition 1
(Truncation Error Bound)

Let
F_{\max}(x)
and
\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)
denote the CDFs of
\max_{n}|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta
under the full and
K
-truncated KL expansions, respectively. The fraction of total channel power lost due to truncation is bounded as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{K}\triangleq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{g}-\tilde{\mathbf{g}}\|^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{g}\|^{2}]}=\frac{\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}\lambda_{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}}=1-\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}}{N}.

(15)

Proof:

Let
\mathbf{U}_{r}=[\mathbf{u}_{K+1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{N}]\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times(N-K)}
and
\bm{\Lambda}_{r}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{K+1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})
denote the matrices formed by the discarded eigenvectors and eigenvalues, respectively. The truncation residual is

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}-\tilde{\mathbf{g}}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}-\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}_{K}\bm{\Lambda}_{K}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{K}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Lambda}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{r},

(16)

where
\mathbf{z}_{r}=[z_{K+1},\ldots,z_{N}]^{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N-K})
is independent of
\mathbf{z}_{K}
. The expected squared norm of the residual is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{g}-\tilde{\mathbf{g}}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle=\eta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{z}_{r}^{H}\bm{\Lambda}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{U}_{r}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{r}\bm{\Lambda}_{r}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}_{r}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbf{z}_{r}^{H}\bm{\Lambda}_{r}\mathbf{z}_{r}\right]

\displaystyle=\eta\,\mathrm{tr}\!\left(\bm{\Lambda}_{r}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}_{r}\mathbf{z}_{r}^{H}]\right)

\displaystyle=\eta\,\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\Lambda}_{r})=\eta\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}\lambda_{k},

(17)

where the second equality uses
\mathbf{U}_{r}^{H}\mathbf{U}_{r}=\mathbf{I}_{N-K}
(since
\mathbf{U}
is unitary), and the third uses
\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}_{r}\mathbf{z}_{r}^{H}]=\mathbf{I}_{N-K}
. The total channel power is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{g}\|^{2}]=\eta\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{R})=\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}=\eta N,

(18)

where the last equality uses
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{R})=N
(unit diagonal). Dividing yields (
15
).
∎

Remark 3
(Choosing
K
)

Given a target approximation accuracy
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, the minimum number of retained modes is

\displaystyle K^{*}=\min\left\{K:\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{0})\,N\right\}.

(19)

For FAS channels under the Jakes model with moderate aperture
W
, the eigenvalue spectrum of
\mathbf{R}
decays rapidly, and typically
K^{*}=\mathcal{O}(\lceil 2W\rceil+1)
suffices to capture over 99% of the total power, regardless of
N
. Crucially,
K^{*}
depends on the aperture
W
but not on
N
, making this approach scalable to large port numbers.

IV
Outage Probability via KL Expansion

Having established the KL expansion and the truncation error bound, this section derives the outage probability under the rank-
K
truncated channel model. The derivation proceeds from the general
K
-dimensional integral formulation, through Gauss-Hermite quadrature evaluation, to closed-form expressions for the rank-1 and rank-2 special cases, and finally to the ergodic rate.

IV-A

General
K
-Dimensional Integral Formulation

Under the
K
-truncated KL expansion, the CDF of the normalized maximum channel gain is

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}_{K}}\!\left[\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\!\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,z_{k}\right|^{2}\leq x\right)\right],

(20)

where
\mathbf{1}(\cdot)
is the indicator function and
z_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
are i.i.d. This is a
K
-dimensional expectation over independent complex Gaussian variables, which reduces the original
N
-dimensional correlated problem to a
K
-dimensional independent one.

The corresponding outage probability is

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(K)}=\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}\!\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}}\right).

(21)

IV-B

Gauss-Hermite Quadrature Evaluation

To evaluate (
20
) numerically, each complex mode is written as
z_{k}=z_{k}^{R}+j\,z_{k}^{I}
where
z_{k}^{R},z_{k}^{I}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
are independent. The probability density kernel
e^{-(z_{k}^{R})^{2}-(z_{k}^{I})^{2}}
directly matches the Gauss-Hermite weight function. After substitution, (
20
) becomes a
2K
-dimensional real integral:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi^{K}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2K}}\Psi(\mathbf{t};\,x)\,\prod_{k=1}^{K}e^{-(t_{k}^{R})^{2}-(t_{k}^{I})^{2}}\,d\mathbf{t}^{R}\,d\mathbf{t}^{I},

(22)

where
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1}^{R},t_{1}^{I},\ldots,t_{K}^{R},t_{K}^{I})
and

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{t};\,x)=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\!\left(\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,(t_{k}^{R}+j\,t_{k}^{I})\right|^{2}\leq x\right).

(23)

Applying the
2K
-dimensional Gauss-Hermite quadrature with
Q
nodes per dimension:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)\approx\frac{1}{\pi^{K}}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{Q}\cdots\sum_{i_{2K}=1}^{Q}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{2K}w_{i_{l}}\right)\Psi(\mathbf{t}_{\mathbf{i}};\,x),

(24)

where
\{t_{j},w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{Q}
are the standard Gauss-Hermite quadrature nodes and weights, and
\mathbf{t}_{\mathbf{i}}
denotes the evaluation point. The computational complexity is
\mathcal{O}(Q^{2K}\cdot N)
.

Remark 4
(Complexity Comparison)

The original problem requires the
N
-dimensional joint CDF evaluation, which scales as
\mathcal{O}(Q^{2N})
– infeasible for even moderate
N
. The KL-based approach reduces this to
\mathcal{O}(Q^{2K})
, where
K\ll N
. For typical FAS parameters (
W\leq 5
),
K\leq 11
and with
Q=10
the computation is efficient. In contrast, the VBCM requires computing products of block CDFs involving Marcum Q-functions with numerical integration per block.

IV-C

Special Case: Rank-1 Approximation (
K=1
)

When only the dominant eigenmode is retained (
K=1
), the channel at port
n
reduces to

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}^{(1)}=\sqrt{\eta\lambda_{1}}\,u_{n,1}\,z_{1},

(25)

where
z_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
. The squared magnitude at port
n
is

\displaystyle|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(1)}|^{2}=\eta\lambda_{1}|u_{n,1}|^{2}|z_{1}|^{2}.

(26)

Since
u_{n,1}
and
\lambda_{1}
are deterministic, all
N
ports are perfectly correlated through the single random variable
z_{1}
. The maximum over all ports is therefore

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(1)}|^{2}=\eta\lambda_{1}\left(\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|u_{n,1}|^{2}\right)|z_{1}|^{2}=\eta\lambda_{1}c_{1}|z_{1}|^{2},

(27)

where
c_{1}\triangleq\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|u_{n,1}|^{2}\in(0,1]
is a deterministic constant determined by the peak magnitude of the dominant eigenvector. Since
z_{1}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
, it follows that
|z_{1}|^{2}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(|z_{1}|^{2}\leq t)=1-e^{-t}
for
t\geq 0
. The CDF of the normalized maximum channel gain is therefore

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\frac{\max_{n}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(1)}|^{2}}{\eta}\leq x\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\lambda_{1}c_{1}|z_{1}|^{2}\leq x\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(|z_{1}|^{2}\leq\frac{x}{\lambda_{1}c_{1}}\right)=1-e^{-x/(\lambda_{1}c_{1})}.

(28)

Substituting
x=\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}/\bar{\gamma}
, the outage probability is

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}=\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(1)}\!\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}}\right)=1-\exp\!\left(-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}\lambda_{1}c_{1}}\right).

(29)

Remark 5
(Interpretation of Rank-1)

The rank-1 approximation yields a simple Rayleigh fading model with an effective channel gain
\lambda_{1}c_{1}
. No spatial diversity is captured, since all ports are perfectly correlated under a single mode. The product
\lambda_{1}c_{1}
quantifies the
beamforming gain
:
\lambda_{1}
is the power concentration in the dominant mode, and
c_{1}
is the peak spatial focusing of the dominant eigenvector. This approximation is tight only when
\lambda_{1}\gg\lambda_{2}
, i.e., the channel is nearly rank-deficient.

IV-D

Special Case: Rank-2 Approximation (
K=2
)

Retaining two eigenmodes (
K=2
), the channel at port
n
becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{n}^{(2)}=\sqrt{\eta}\!\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\,u_{n,1}\,z_{1}+\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\,u_{n,2}\,z_{2}\right),

(30)

where
z_{1},z_{2}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
are independent. Expanding the squared amplitude:

\displaystyle\frac{|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(2)}|^{2}}{\eta}

\displaystyle=\left|\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\,u_{n,1}\,z_{1}+\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\,u_{n,2}\,z_{2}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}|u_{n,1}|^{2}|z_{1}|^{2}+\lambda_{2}|u_{n,2}|^{2}|z_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left(u_{n,1}u_{n,2}^{*}z_{1}z_{2}^{*}\right).

(31)

The cross-term
\mathrm{Re}(u_{n,1}u_{n,2}^{*}z_{1}z_{2}^{*})
couples
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, so the joint distribution of
(\tilde{g}_{1}^{(2)},\ldots,\tilde{g}_{N}^{(2)})
given
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
does not factorize, preventing a direct product-form CDF.

To make progress, the derivation conditions on
z_{1}
and treats
z_{2}
as the remaining random variable. Define

\displaystyle a_{n}(z_{1})\triangleq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\,u_{n,1}\,z_{1},\quad b_{n}\triangleq\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}\,u_{n,2},

(32)

so that
\tilde{g}_{n}^{(2)}/\sqrt{\eta}=a_{n}(z_{1})+b_{n}z_{2}
. The outage constraint at port
n
is
|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(2)}|^{2}/\eta\leq x
, which becomes

\displaystyle|a_{n}(z_{1})+b_{n}z_{2}|^{2}\leq x.

(33)

Dividing both sides by
|b_{n}|^{2}=\lambda_{2}|u_{n,2}|^{2}
(assuming
u_{n,2}\neq 0
) and completing the square:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{a_{n}(z_{1})}{b_{n}}+z_{2}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{x}{|b_{n}|^{2}},

(34)

which is equivalently written as

\displaystyle\left|z_{2}-\left(-\frac{a_{n}(z_{1})}{b_{n}}\right)\right|^{2}\leq r_{n}^{2},\quad r_{n}\triangleq\sqrt{\frac{x}{\lambda_{2}|u_{n,2}|^{2}}}.

(35)

This defines a disk
\mathcal{B}_{n}(z_{1})
in the complex
z_{2}
-plane centered at
c_{n}(z_{1})=-a_{n}(z_{1})/b_{n}
with radius
r_{n}
. Note that
r_{n}
is independent of
z_{1}
(it depends only on the fixed threshold
x
and the eigenvector entry
u_{n,2}
), while the center
c_{n}(z_{1})
shifts linearly with
z_{1}
.

The outage event
\{\max_{n}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(2)}|^{2}/\eta\leq x\}
requires all
N
port constraints to hold simultaneously, i.e.,
z_{2}\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}_{n}(z_{1})
. The conditional CDF given
z_{1}
is therefore

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(2)}(x\mid z_{1})=\mathbb{P}\!\left(z_{2}\in\bigcap_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}_{n}(z_{1})\,\Big|\,z_{1}\right),

(36)

where
z_{2}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
independently of
z_{1}
. The unconditional CDF is obtained by averaging over
z_{1}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(2)}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{z_{1}}\!\left[\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(2)}(x\mid z_{1})\right]=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(2)}(x\mid z_{1})\,\frac{e^{-|z_{1}|^{2}}}{\pi}\,d^{2}z_{1}.

(37)

The inner probability (
36
) is the probability that a
\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
variable falls within the intersection of
N
disks whose centers depend on
z_{1}
. Writing
z_{2}=t^{R}+jt^{I}
with
t^{R},t^{I}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/2)
, this becomes a 2D integral over the real plane, and the outer expectation over
z_{1}
adds another 2D integral—yielding a total 4D real integral evaluated via 2D Gauss-Hermite quadrature applied twice.

IV-E

Ergodic Rate Under KL Expansion

The ergodic rate (Shannon capacity) under port selection is

\displaystyle\bar{C}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\log_{2}\!\left(1+\bar{\gamma}\max_{n}\frac{|g_{n}|^{2}}{\eta}\right)\right].

(38)

Under the
K
-truncated KL expansion, this becomes

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(K)}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{z}_{K}}\!\left[\log_{2}\!\left(1+\bar{\gamma}\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\,u_{n,k}\,z_{k}\right|^{2}\right)\right],

(39)

which is a
K
-dimensional expectation over independent Gaussian modes. This can be evaluated via the same Gauss-Hermite quadrature framework of (
24
) by replacing the indicator function
\Psi
with the
\log_{2}(\cdot)
integrand.

For the rank-1 special case, using (
27
) and
|z_{1}|^{2}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
, let
t=|z_{1}|^{2}
so that
\max_{n}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(1)}|^{2}/\eta=\lambda_{1}c_{1}t
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\log_{2}\!\left(1+\bar{\gamma}\lambda_{1}c_{1}t\right)e^{-t}\,dt.

(40)

Let
\mu\triangleq\bar{\gamma}\lambda_{1}c_{1}
and substitute
s=1+\mu t
, so
t=(s-1)/\mu
and
dt=ds/\mu
:

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu\ln 2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\ln(s)\,e^{-(s-1)/\mu}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1/\mu}}{\mu\ln 2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\ln(s)\,e^{-s/\mu}\,ds.

(41)

Integrating by parts with
u=\ln s
and
dv=e^{-s/\mu}ds
, so
du=ds/s
and
v=-\mu e^{-s/\mu}
:

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\ln(s)\,e^{-s/\mu}\,ds

\displaystyle=\Big[-\mu\ln(s)\,e^{-s/\mu}\Big]_{1}^{\infty}+\mu\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-s/\mu}}{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=0+\mu\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-s/\mu}}{s}\,ds.

(42)

The remaining integral is recognized as
\mu\,E_{1}(1/\mu)
via the substitution
s\to\mu s^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-s/\mu}}{s}\,ds=\int_{1/\mu}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,du=E_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right).

(43)

Combining all steps:

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(1)}=\frac{e^{1/\mu}}{\ln 2}\,E_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right)=\frac{e^{1/(\bar{\gamma}\lambda_{1}c_{1})}}{\ln 2}\,E_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{\bar{\gamma}\lambda_{1}c_{1}}\right),

(44)

where
E_{1}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{-1}e^{-t}\,dt
is the exponential integral
[
37
]
.

V
Information-Theoretic Guarantees

The outage expressions derived in Section IV are computationally tractable, but they do not yet answer two fundamental questions: (i) is the KL approximation conservative or optimistic relative to the true outage? and (ii) how many modes
K
are actually needed, and is the KL truncation the best possible rank-
K
approximation? This section addresses both questions rigorously. The results establish that the KL framework is not merely a convenient approximation, but one with provable safety guarantees and information-theoretic optimality.

V-A

Conservative Outage Guarantee

The following theorem proves that KL truncation
always
overestimates the outage probability, making it a safe approximation for system design.

Theorem 1
(Conservative Outage Bound)

For any truncation order
K\leq N
and threshold
x>0
, the outage probability under the
K
-truncated KL expansion satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)\geq F_{\max}(x).

(45)

Equivalently,
\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(K)}\geq P_{\mathrm{out}}
for all
\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}>0
and
\bar{\gamma}>0
.

Proof:

Step 1: Loewner ordering of covariance matrices.

The rank-
K
truncated covariance matrix is
\eta\mathbf{R}_{K}=\eta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{H}
. The difference is

\displaystyle\eta(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K})=\eta\sum_{k=K+1}^{N}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{H}\succeq\mathbf{0},

(46)

since each term
\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{H}
is positive semidefinite (
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
). Therefore
\eta\mathbf{R}_{K}\preceq\eta\mathbf{R}
.

Step 2: Real-composite representation.

For a complex Gaussian vector
\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\eta\mathbf{R})
, write
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbb{R}}=[\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{g})^{T},\Im(\mathbf{g})^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. Then
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{\Sigma})
where

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}=\frac{\eta}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{R})&-\Im(\mathbf{R})\\
\Im(\mathbf{R})&\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{R})\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}.

(47)

Similarly, the truncated channel
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\eta\mathbf{R}_{K})
has real covariance

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{K}=\frac{\eta}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{R}_{K})&-\Im(\mathbf{R}_{K})\\
\Im(\mathbf{R}_{K})&\mathrm{Re}(\mathbf{R}_{K})\end{bmatrix}.

(48)

Since
\mathbf{R}_{K}\preceq\mathbf{R}
in the complex Loewner sense, the real-composite covariance matrices satisfy
\bm{\Sigma}_{K}\preceq\bm{\Sigma}
. This follows because for any real vector
\mathbf{v}=[\mathbf{v}_{1}^{T},\mathbf{v}_{2}^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}^{T}(\bm{\Sigma}-\bm{\Sigma}_{K})\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{2}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[(\mathbf{v}_{1}+j\mathbf{v}_{2})^{H}(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K})(\mathbf{v}_{1}+j\mathbf{v}_{2})\right]\geq 0,

(49)

where the last inequality uses
\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K}\succeq\mathbf{0}
.

Step 3: Symmetry and convexity of the outage set.

The outage event
\{\max_{n}|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta\leq x\}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{C}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{(x_{1},y_{1},\ldots,x_{N},y_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}:x_{n}^{2}+y_{n}^{2}\leq\eta x,\;\forall\,n\right\}.

(50)

This set is the Cartesian product of
N
closed disks of radius
\sqrt{\eta x}
centered at the origin. It is
symmetric
(i.e.,
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}\Rightarrow-\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{C}
) and
convex
(intersection of convex sets), hence a symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
.

Step 4: Application of Anderson’s inequality.

By Anderson’s inequality
[
33
]
: if
\mathbf{X}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{1})
and
\mathbf{Y}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{2})
with
\bm{\Sigma}_{1}\preceq\bm{\Sigma}_{2}
, then
\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{C})\geq\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{Y}\in\mathcal{C})
for any symmetric convex set
\mathcal{C}
. Applying this with
\bm{\Sigma}_{K}\preceq\bm{\Sigma}
:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{\mathbb{R}}^{(K)}\in\mathcal{C}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\mathbf{g}_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{C}\right)=F_{\max}(x),

(51)

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
(Monotone Convergence)

The KL outage approximation is monotonically non-increasing in
K
:

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}\geq\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(2)}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{P}_{\mathrm{out}}^{(N)}=P_{\mathrm{out}}.

(52)

Proof:

For
K_{1}<K_{2}\leq N
, it holds that
\mathbf{R}_{K_{1}}\preceq\mathbf{R}_{K_{2}}\preceq\mathbf{R}
. Applying Theorem
1
with the truncated channel of order
K_{2}
playing the role of the “full” channel yields
\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K_{1})}(x)\geq\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K_{2})}(x)
. The chain (
52
) follows by induction.
∎

Corollary 2
(Ergodic Capacity Lower Bound)

The ergodic capacity under the
K
-truncated KL expansion satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(K)}\leq\bar{C},

(53)

with equality if and only if
K=N
.

Proof:

Theorem
1
establishes
\tilde{F}_{\max}^{(K)}(x)\geq F_{\max}(x)
for all
x\geq 0
, which means
\max_{n}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(K)}|^{2}/\eta
is stochastically dominated by
\max_{n}|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta
. Since
h(x)=\log_{2}(1+\bar{\gamma}x)
is non-decreasing, the standard stochastic ordering result gives
\mathbb{E}[h(\max_{n}|\tilde{g}_{n}^{(K)}|^{2}/\eta)]\leq\mathbb{E}[h(\max_{n}|g_{n}|^{2}/\eta)]
, i.e.,
\bar{C}_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(K)}\leq\bar{C}
.

When
K=N
,
\mathbf{R}_{K}=\mathbf{R}
and the bound holds with equality. For
K<N
,
\bm{\Sigma}_{K}\prec\bm{\Sigma}
(strict ordering) and strict inequality follows from the strict version of Anderson’s inequality.
∎

Remark 6
(Design Implication)

Theorem
1
and its corollaries guarantee that the KL approximation always provides a
pessimistic
performance estimate. In secrecy analysis, where underestimating the eavesdropper’s capability or overestimating the legitimate user’s performance can lead to security breaches, such a conservative guarantee is particularly valuable. The monotone convergence (
52
) further ensures that increasing
K
systematically tightens the bound without ever “overshooting” the true value.

V-B

Effective Degrees of Freedom

This subsection provides a theoretical foundation for the empirical observation that
K^{*}=\mathcal{O}(2W+1)
eigenmodes suffice, independent of
N
.

Theorem 2
(Asymptotic Spectral Concentration)

Consider the
N\times N
Jakes correlation matrix
\mathbf{R}
with aperture
W
defined in (
4
). As
N\to\infty
with
W
fixed:

(a)

The eigenvalues of
\mathbf{R}
exhibit a phase transition at index
K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1
: the first
K^{*}
eigenvalues satisfy
\lambda_{k}=\Theta(N/K^{*})
for
k\leq K^{*}
, while
\lambda_{k}\to 0
exponentially fast for
k>K^{*}
.

(b)

The truncation error satisfies
\varepsilon_{K^{*}}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

(c)

The number of retained modes
K^{*}
is
independent of

N
.

Proof:

The entries
[\mathbf{R}]_{k,l}=J_{0}(2\pi|k-l|W/(N-1))
form a Toeplitz matrix generated by the sampling of
J_{0}(2\pi\tau W)
for
\tau\in[0,1]
. As
N\to\infty
, this matrix is asymptotically equivalent (in the Szegő sense) to the integral operator

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}f)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}J_{0}\!\left(2\pi W|x-y|\right)f(y)\,dy

(54)

on
L^{2}[0,1]
. By the Fourier transform of the Bessel kernel,
J_{0}(2\pi W\tau)=\int_{-W}^{W}e^{j2\pi f\tau}\,\frac{df}{\pi\sqrt{W^{2}-f^{2}}}
, which shows that the spectral density of the underlying process is supported on the band
[-W,W]
.

By the
Slepian–Landau–Pollak concentration theorem

[
34
,
35
]
, the eigenvalues of a time-frequency concentration operator on an interval of length
T
and bandwidth
B
exhibit a sharp phase transition at index
2TB+1
: the first
2TB+1
eigenvalues cluster near 1, and the remaining eigenvalues decay super-exponentially to zero. Mapping to the present setting with
T=1
(normalized aperture support) and
B=W
(spectral bandwidth), this gives
K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1
.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}=N
and the first
K^{*}
eigenvalues grow as
\Theta(N/K^{*})
while the tail eigenvalues are
o(1)
, it follows that
\sum_{k=1}^{K^{*}}\lambda_{k}\sim N
and thus
\varepsilon_{K^{*}}=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K^{*}}\lambda_{k}/N\to 0
.
∎

Remark 7
(Physical Interpretation)

Theorem
2
reveals that the number of independent spatial degrees of freedom in a FAS channel is determined by the
space–bandwidth product

2W
, which is a fundamental information-theoretic quantity. The Jakes correlation function
J_{0}(2\pi W\tau)
has an effective bandwidth of
W
, so the Shannon number (number of independent modes per unit length) is
\lceil 2W\rceil+1
. This is the spatial analogue of the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem: a FAS channel with aperture
W\lambda
has at most
\approx 2W+1
independent degrees of freedom, regardless of how densely the ports are sampled.

V-C

Rate-Distortion Optimality of KL Truncation

Theorem 3
(Optimal Low-Rank Approximation)

Among all rank-
K
approximations
\hat{\mathbf{R}}
of
\mathbf{R}
, the KL truncation
\mathbf{R}_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}\mathbf{u}_{k}^{H}
uniquely minimizes both the Frobenius norm error and the operator norm error:

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{K}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\mathrm{rank}(\hat{\mathbf{R}})\leq K}\|\mathbf{R}-\hat{\mathbf{R}}\|_{F},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{K}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\mathrm{rank}(\hat{\mathbf{R}})\leq K}\|\mathbf{R}-\hat{\mathbf{R}}\|_{2}.

(56)

The minimum errors are
\|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K}\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{k>K}\lambda_{k}^{2}}
and
\|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K}\|_{2}=\lambda_{K+1}
.

Proof:

This is the Eckart–Young–Mirsky theorem
[
36
]
. The Frobenius norm result follows from the unitary invariance of
\|\cdot\|_{F}
and the optimality of truncating the eigenvalue decomposition. The operator norm result follows from the Courant–Fischer min-max characterization.
∎

Proposition 2
(Information-Theoretic Interpretation)

The KL truncation achieves the Gaussian rate-distortion bound. Specifically, the minimum number of real-valued parameters (rate) needed to represent the channel
\mathbf{g}
within average distortion
D=\eta\sum_{k>K}\lambda_{k}
is

\displaystyle R(D)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}\!\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\theta}\right),\quad\text{where }\theta=\lambda_{K+1},

(57)

assuming
\lambda_{K}>\lambda_{K+1}
(i.e., the truncation level lies between consecutive eigenvalues). The KL truncation with
K
modes achieves this rate-distortion pair exactly, confirming that it is the
information-theoretically optimal
compression of the Gaussian source
\mathbf{g}
.

Proof:

For a complex Gaussian source
\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0},\eta\mathbf{R})
with eigenvalues
\eta\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\eta\lambda_{N}
, the rate-distortion function under MSE distortion is obtained by reverse water-filling
[
37
]
:

\displaystyle R(D)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\max\!\left(0,\,\log_{2}\!\left(\frac{\eta\lambda_{k}}{\theta}\right)\right),

(58)

where the water level
\theta
is chosen to satisfy
D=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\min(\eta\lambda_{k},\theta)
. Setting
\theta=\eta\lambda_{K+1}
(assuming
\lambda_{K}>\lambda_{K+1}
), the modes
k>K
are fully “flooded” (distortion
\eta\lambda_{k}
each), and the modes
k\leq K
are represented exactly (distortion 0). The total distortion is
D=\eta\sum_{k>K}\lambda_{k}
, matching (
15
) up to the factor
\eta N
. The achiever of this bound is precisely the transform-coding scheme that retains the top
K
KL coefficients and discards the rest — which is exactly the proposed truncation.
∎

Remark 8
(Comparison with BCM/VBCM)

The BCM/VBCM approximations impose a block-diagonal structure on
\hat{\mathbf{R}}
, which is a
structural constraint
not aligned with the eigenbasis of
\mathbf{R}
. For a given number of free parameters, the block-diagonal constraint prevents the approximation from minimizing the Frobenius error or the rate-distortion cost. In particular:

•

The BCM with
D
blocks of size
B=N/D
has
\|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{BCM}}\|_{F}\geq\|\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}_{K}\|_{F}
for any
K\leq N
. Equality is impossible unless
\mathbf{R}
is itself block-diagonal.

•

From an information-theoretic perspective, the BCM achieves a distortion strictly above the rate-distortion bound (
57
) at any finite rate, since the block-diagonal structure does not align with the principal components of
\mathbf{R}
.

Furthermore, the block-diagonal BCM introduces
D
independent sub-channels, which artificially
underestimates
the inter-block correlation present in the true Toeplitz matrix
\mathbf{R}
. Since positive off-diagonal entries in
\mathbf{R}
reduce the effective diversity (they make channels more “alike”), removing them inflates the apparent diversity, leading to the optimistic outage bias observed in Fig.
6
.

V-D

Mutual Information Characterization of the Truncation

Proposition 3
(Retained Information)

The mutual information between the full channel vector
\mathbf{g}
and its KL-truncated version
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}
is

\displaystyle I(\mathbf{g};\,\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log_{2}(\pi e\,\eta\lambda_{k})\quad\text{(in bits)}.

(59)

The fraction of total differential entropy captured by the truncation is

\displaystyle\frac{h(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)})}{h(\mathbf{g})}=\frac{K\log(\pi e)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\log(\eta\lambda_{k})}{N\log(\pi e)+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\log(\eta\lambda_{k})},

(60)

which approaches 1 as
K\to K^{*}
for large
N
, since the tail eigenvalues contribute negligible entropy.

Proof:

The KL expansion diagonalizes the channel:
\mathbf{g}=\sqrt{\eta}\,\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{1/2}\mathbf{z}
, where
z_{k}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)
are i.i.d. Since
\mathbf{U}
is unitary, the differential entropy is

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{g})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}h(\sqrt{\eta\lambda_{k}}\,z_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\log_{2}(\pi e\,\eta\lambda_{k}).

(61)

The truncated channel
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}
is a function of
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{K}\}
, and the tail
\bm{\Delta}=\mathbf{g}-\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}
is a function of
\{z_{K+1},\ldots,z_{N}\}
. Since all
z_{k}
are independent,
\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}\perp\bm{\Delta}
, so

\displaystyle I(\mathbf{g};\,\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)})

\displaystyle=h(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)})-h(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(K)}\mid\mathbf{g})

Remark 9
(Dual Metrics of Approximation Quality)

The truncation quality is captured by two complementary metrics:

•

Power fraction:
1-\varepsilon_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\lambda_{k}/N
measures the fraction of total signal power retained. This is a first-order (mean) metric.

•

Entropy fraction: (
60
) measures the fraction of information content retained. Since differential entropy is sensitive to the full eigenvalue distribution (not just the sum), the entropy fraction converges faster than the power fraction when the tail eigenvalues are small (because
\log\lambda_{k}\to-\infty
rapidly for
\lambda_{k}\to 0
, making the tail contribution to entropy negligible). This explains why even moderate
K
yields excellent outage approximations despite capturing less than 100% of the power.

VII
Conclusion

Building on the theoretical framework and numerical evidence presented in the preceding sections, this section summarizes the main findings and outlines directions for future research.

This paper has proposed a Karhunen-Loève expansion framework for the outage probability analysis of fluid antenna systems, offering a principled alternative to block-correlation models. By decomposing the spatially correlated FAS channel into independent eigenmodes and performing a controlled rank-
K
truncation, the proposed approach reduces the
N
-dimensional correlated outage analysis to a
K
-dimensional independent one, with
K\ll N
. Several information-theoretic guarantees have been established: the KL truncation provably overestimates the outage probability (conservative guarantee via Anderson’s inequality), requires only
K^{*}=2\lceil W\rceil+1
modes (Slepian–Landau–Pollak theorem), and achieves the Gaussian rate-distortion bound (optimal channel compression). Extensive numerical results have confirmed all theoretical predictions and demonstrated the systematic superiority of the KL framework over BCM/VBCM models in terms of both approximation accuracy and theoretical soundness.

Several promising directions remain for future work, including: integration with physical layer secrecy analysis where both the legitimate and eavesdropper channels are decomposed via KL; extension to multi-user FAS systems with inter-user correlation; asymptotic analysis under
N\to\infty
via Szegő’s theorem for Toeplitz eigenvalue distributions; and a hybrid KL-VBCM approach that leverages the KL eigenbasis to determine the optimal block partition for VBCM.
