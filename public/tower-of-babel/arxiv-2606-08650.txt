Restriction estimates for toral eigenfunctions and lattice points in spherical regions
Cheng Zhang and Zhifei Zhu

Abstract
. We establish new L 2 L^{2} restriction estimates for toral eigenfunctions. These estimates are sharp in certain cases, and thus prove a conjecture of Huang-Zhang for smooth submanifolds of large codimension. In particular, they provide new progress toward a conjecture of Bourgain-Rudnick. The proof combines a slicing and packing method with the approximation of the discrete spherical multiplier by Magyar-Stein-Wainger and Magyar.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a compact smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let

\Delta
be its Laplace–Beltrami operator. Suppose

-\Delta e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda},\qquad\lambda>1,

one may ask how large the restriction of
e_{\lambda}
can be on a fixed
submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
. Let
d=\dim M
, let
k=\dim\Sigma
, and
write
m=d-k
for the codimension. Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov

[
8
]
and Hu
[
16
]
proved that

(1.1)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\Lambda(m,\lambda)\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)},

where

\Lambda(m,\lambda)=\begin{cases}\lambda^{1/4},&m=1,\\
\lambda^{1/2}\sqrt{\log\lambda},&m=2,\\
\lambda^{(m-1)/2},&m\geq 3.\end{cases}

These estimates are sharp on the standard sphere, apart from the logarithmic
loss in codimension two. In the case of codimension one, if
\Sigma
has positive or
negative definite second fundamental form, the exponent
1/4
can be
improved to
1/6
.

Related developments include the microlocal background in the work of Greenleaf–Seeger
[
12
]
and Tataru
[
27
]
. Quasimode and
curved-hypersurface variants were developed by Tacy
[
26
]
and
Hassell–Tacy
[
13
]
. For curves and geodesics, see Reznikov

[
23
]
, Bourgain
[
3
]
, Sogge–Zelditch
[
25
]
,
Chen
[
9
]
, Chen–Sogge
[
10
]
, Xi–Zhang
[
30
]
,
Blair
[
1
]
, Zhang
[
31
]
, and Park
[
22
]
. In the flat
torus setting, one may refer to Bourgain–Rudnick
[
5
]
, Hezari–Rivière
[
15
]
,
Huang–Zhang
[
18
]
, Wang–Zhang
[
29
]
, and
Burq–Germain–Sorella–Zhu
[
7
]
. Other variants include
[
14
,
15
,
2
,
17
,
32
]
.

Let
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the standard flat torus. We write
\lambda
for the frequency radius, so the Laplace eigenvalue is
(2\pi\lambda)^{2}
. In this setting, eigenfunctions are
trigonometric polynomials whose frequencies lie on a lattice sphere:

e_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{\xi}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}.

Thus, restriction estimates of eigenfunctions are closely connected with the distribution of the lattice points in
\lambda S^{d-1}
.
This arithmetic structure allows one to improve general estimates
(
1.1
).

For curves

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
, Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov observed that
(
1.1
) can be improved to

(1.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},\qquad\forall\varepsilon>0,

using the corresponding
L^{\infty}
bound for eigenfunctions. They asked whether the factor
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
can be removed. Bourgain–Rudnick

[
5
]
proved such a uniform bound for curve segments with nonvanishing
geodesic curvature, and Huang–Zhang
[
18
, Theorem 2]
proved it for
the closed geodesics case. For a geodesic segment on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, this problem is closely related to the open problem of whether every
arc of length
\lambda^{1/2}
on the circle
|x|=\lambda
contains only

O(1)
lattice points.

Bourgain–Rudnick proposed the following higher-dimensional conjecture for real-analytic hypersurfaces.

Conjecture 1.1

(Bourgain–Rudnick
[
5
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a real-analytic
hypersurface. Then for
\lambda>1,

(1.3)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

If moreover
\Sigma
has nowhere vanishing curvature and

\lambda>\lambda_{\Sigma}
, then

(1.4)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\gtrsim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Bourgain–Rudnick proved the conjecture for
d=2,3
in the real-analytic
case with nowhere vanishing curvature. Hezari–Rivière
[
15
]
obtained
the upper bound along a density-one subsequence of eigenfunctions for smooth
embedded hypersurfaces with nonvanishing principal curvatures.

Recently, Huang–Zhang
[
18
]
proposed the following analogue in the case of smooth manifolds in higher
codimensions.

Conjecture 1.2

(Huang–Zhang
[
18
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
, and let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a smooth
submanifold of codimension
m\geq 2
. Then, for
\lambda>1
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.5)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{m}{2}-1+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Huang–Zhang proved this
bound (
1.5
) as well as (
1.3
) for rational totally geodesic submanifolds, while the problem remains
open for general submanifolds. The exponent is also sharp in general; see
Proposition
2.6
below.

Our first main result is Theorem
1.3
, which proves Conjecture
1.2
for
m\geq\frac{d+3}{2}
when
d\geq 5
. It also improves the restriction estimates (
1.1
) for totally geodesic submanifolds of all codimensions. In addition, we prove new restriction estimates for curves on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
; see Theorem
6.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
.

(i)
Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a compact totally geodesic
submanifold of codimension
m
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
,

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\alpha(m,d)+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

where

\alpha(1,d)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{12},&d=3,\\
\frac{1}{8},&d\geq 4,\end{cases}\qquad\alpha(2,d)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{3},&d=3,\\
\frac{3}{8},&d\geq 4,\end{cases}

\alpha(3,d)=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4},&d=4,\\
\frac{4}{5},&d=5,\\
\frac{13}{16},&d\geq 6,\end{cases}

and, for
m\geq 4
and
d\geq 5
,

\alpha(m,d)=\begin{cases}\frac{m}{2}-1,&d\leq 2m-3,\\
\frac{m}{2}-\frac{3}{4},&d=2m-2,\\
\frac{m}{2}-\frac{3}{4}+2^{-m-1},&d\geq 2m-1.\end{cases}

(ii)
If
d\geq 5
,
m\geq\frac{d+3}{2}
, and

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is any compact smooth submanifold of
codimension
m
, then for every
\varepsilon>0

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{m}{2}-1+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Part (ii) proves Conjecture
1.2
in the range

m\geq(d+3)/2
,
d\geq 5
. Part (i) gives new bounds for totally geodesic
submanifolds in all codimensions. When
d=3
, they agree with earlier bounds of Huang–Zhang

[
18
, Theorem 4]
. They also improve the general
Burq–Gérard–Tzvetkov estimate (
1.1
).

The proof of Theorem
1.3
combines a slicing and packing method with the approximation of the discrete spherical multiplier (Proposition
4.1
) due to Magyar-Stein-Wainger
[
21
]
and Magyar
[
20
]
. At present, our approach only partially confirms Conjecture
1.2
. For smaller codimension, the remainder term
\lambda^{(d-1)/2+\varepsilon}
in Proposition
4.1
prevents
this method from reaching the conjectural exponent. A full resolution of this conjecture would require a more efficient use of cancellation in the relevant exponential sums.

We also prove, in Theorem
6.2
, new restriction estimates for curves in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. If

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{3}
is a geodesic segment, a smooth curve segment
with nowhere vanishing torsion, or a real-analytic curve segment whose geodesic curvature is nowhere vanishing, then

(1.6)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

If
\Sigma
is a smooth planar curve segment with nowhere vanishing geodesic
curvature, then we can obtain the stronger bound
O_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{1/4+\varepsilon})
.
These estimates improve the general codimension-two bound in (
1.1
).
Our proof uses Bourgain–Rudnick’s lattice-point estimates in spherical caps
together with the van der Corput lemma. Notably, the bound (
1.6
) agrees with the one predicted by the endpoint Discrete Restriction Conjecture (Bourgain–Demeter

[
4
, Conjecture 2.6]
). See Remark
6.5
.

1.1.
Paper structure

Section
2
introduces the lattice-point counting problem associated with
totally geodesic restrictions and proves the reduction from restriction
estimates to band-counting estimates. It also records lower bounds showing
that the expected exponents are sharp. Section
3
proves the geometric
lattice-point estimates by slicing and packing. Section
4
uses the
Magyar–Stein–Wainger and Magyar spherical multiplier approximation to prove
restriction estimates for general smooth submanifolds. Section
5
combines the
geometric and multiplier estimates to prove Theorem
1.3
. Section
6

proves the curve estimates on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and discusses their relation to
the Discrete Restriction Conjecture.

1.2.
Notation

Throughout the paper,
X\lesssim Y
means that
X\leq CY
, where the
constant
C
is independent of
\lambda
. The implicit constant
C
may depend on the dimensional constant
d
, the fixed band-width
constant
C_{0}
, and the transversality constant
\nu
whenever they appear.

The subscripts indicate the allowed
dependence of the implicit constant. Thus,
X\lesssim_{\varepsilon}Y
means that the
constant may depend on
\varepsilon
. We write
X\approx Y
if

X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
. Finally,
X\gg Y
means that

X\geq CY
for a sufficiently large constant
C>0
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\lambda S^{d-1}
be the standard sphere in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of radius
\lambda
. If
\lambda^{2}\notin\mathbb{N}
, then

\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}=\varnothing
, so all estimates are trivial. We
therefore assume
\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

A unit band on
\lambda S^{d-1}
is a set of the form

B(u,x_{0})=\left\{x\in\lambda S^{d-1}:\left|\frac{u}{|u|}\cdot(x-x_{0})\right|\leq C_{0}\right\}.

For
1\leq k\leq d-1
, the bands are
\nu
-transverse if their unit
normals
n_{j}=u_{j}/|u_{j}|
satisfy

|n_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge n_{k}|\geq\nu.

Define

A_{k,d,\lambda}:=\sup\#\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}B(u_{j},x_{0,j})\right),

where the supremum is over all
\nu
-transverse
k
-tuples of bands. Indeed,
A_{k,d,\lambda}
is essentially the maximum number of lattice points in the intersection of the sphere
\lambda S^{d-1}
and the 1-neighborhood of a
(d-k)
-plane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. See Lemma
3.7
.

2.1.
Lower bounds

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write

r_{d}(n)=\#\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|x|^{2}=n\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 4
and let
\lambda\geq 3
be odd. Then
r_{d}(\lambda^{2})\gtrsim\lambda^{d-2}.

This lemma is known and can be proved using Jacobi’s four-square formula. For
d=4
and even
\lambda
, the lower bound does not need to hold. See Walfisz
[
28
]
and Bourgain-Rudnick
[
5
, Section 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 4
,
1\leq k\leq d-2
, and let
\lambda\geq 3
be odd. Then there exists
a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that

\#\{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-k}:|a_{0}|^{2}+|b|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\}\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}.

Proof.

Write
x=(a,b)
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-k}
. For each
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, set

F(a)=\#\{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-k}:|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\}.

Then

r_{d}(\lambda^{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\\
|a|\leq\lambda\end{subarray}}F(a).

The number of possible
a
is
\#\{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:|a|\leq\lambda\}\lesssim\lambda^{k}.

By Lemma
2.1
,
r_{d}(\lambda^{2})\gtrsim\lambda^{d-2}.

Therefore, the pigeonhole principle gives some
a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with
F(a_{0})\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}.

∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
, let
1\leq k\leq d-2
, and fix
C_{0}>0
and
0<\nu\leq 1
. Then

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{A_{k,d,\lambda}}{\lambda^{d-k-2}}\gtrsim 1.

In fact, for
d\geq 4
the lower bound holds for all sufficiently large odd integers
\lambda
.

Proof.

We first treat
d\geq 4
. Let
\lambda\geq 3
be odd. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
a_{0}=(a_{0,1},\dots,a_{0,k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
such that

\#\{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-k}:|a_{0}|^{2}+|b|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\}\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}.

For
j=1,\dots,k
, define the coordinate band

B_{j}=\{x\in\lambda S^{d-1}:|x_{j}-a_{0,j}|\leq C_{0}\}.

This is a unit band with normal
e_{j}
. The normals satisfy
|e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{k}|=1,

so the bands are
\nu
-transverse for every
0<\nu\leq 1
.

Every lattice point of the form
x=(a_{0},b)
with
|a_{0}|^{2}+|b|^{2}=\lambda^{2}

lies in
B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
. Hence

A_{k,d,\lambda}\geq\#\{b\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-k}:|a_{0}|^{2}+|b|^{2}=\lambda^{2}\}\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}.

It remains to treat
d=3
and
k=1
.
For every integer
\lambda\geq 1
, the point
(\lambda,0,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\lambda S^{2}.

The coordinate band
B=\bigl\{x\in\lambda S^{2}:|x_{1}-\lambda|\leq C_{0}\bigr\}

contains this point. Thus
A_{1,3,\lambda}\geq 1.

∎

Therefore, the following conjecture on the upper bounds seems reasonable.

Conjecture 2.4
.

For
d\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq d-1
,

(2.1)

A_{k,d,\lambda}\lesssim 1,\ \ \text{when}\ d-k=1,

and

(2.2)

A_{k,d,\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2+\varepsilon},\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\ \ \text{when}\ d-k\geq 2.

2.2.
Sharp restriction estimates

Throughout, the totally geodesic submanifolds are assumed to be bounded with fixed unit diameter, and they are essentially determined by their directions. Huang–Zhang
[
18
, Theorem 3]
reduced the restriction problem to the number
A_{k,d,\lambda}
, with a logarithmic loss. We can now remove this loss. The idea is to majorize the cutoff function by a function with compact Fourier support.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
d\geq 3
, and let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a totally geodesic submanifold of dimension
k
and fixed unit diameter. Then

(2.3)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\sqrt{A_{k,d,\lambda}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}.

Here the constant is independent of
\lambda
and the direction of
\Sigma
.
Moreover, for any fixed eigenvalue
\lambda
, there exist an eigenfunction
e_{\lambda}
and a totally geodesic submanifold
\Sigma
of dimension
k
and fixed unit diameter such that

(2.4)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\gtrsim\sqrt{A_{k,d,\lambda}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}.

Thus, the upper bound (
2.3
) is sharp.

Therfore, Conjecture
2.4
implies the upper bounds in Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
for totally geodesic submanifolds.

Proof.

We prove the upper bound. After covering
\Sigma
by
O(1)
coordinate patches, it is enough to treat a patch of the form

x=x_{0}+Lt,\qquad t\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{k},

where
L:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an isometric embedding and
\Omega
has bounded diameter. Thus, the columns
Le_{1},\ldots,Le_{k}
are orthonormal tangent vectors. Write

e_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\cdot x},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|n|=\lambda\}.

Then

\int_{\Sigma}|e_{\lambda}|^{2}\,d\sigma\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\cdot x_{0}}e^{2\pi it\cdot L^{T}n}\Big|^{2}dt.

Choose a fixed nonnegative function
\Psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
such that
\Psi\geq\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
,
\widehat{\Psi}\geq 0
, and
\widehat{\Psi}
is supported in a fixed small cube
[-c_{0},c_{0}]^{k}
, with
c_{0}\leq C_{0}
. Expanding the square after majorizing
\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}
by
\Psi
gives

\int_{\Omega}\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{n}e^{2\pi in\cdot x_{0}}e^{2\pi it\cdot L^{T}n}\Big|^{2}dt\leq\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{m}\overline{a_{n}}e^{2\pi i(m-n)\cdot x_{0}}\widehat{\Psi}(L^{T}(n-m)).

Here
\widehat{\Psi}(L^{T}(n-m))
is supported where

|(n-m)\cdot Le_{j}|\leq c_{0},\qquad j=1,\ldots,k.

For each fixed
m
, the possible
n
therefore lies in the intersection of the
k
unit-width bands on
\lambda S^{d-1}
centered at
m
with normals
Le_{1},\ldots,Le_{k}
. Since these normals are orthonormal, they are transverse, and the number of such
n
is at most
A_{k,d,\lambda}
. We have

\max_{m\in E_{\lambda}}\sum_{n\in E_{\lambda}}|\widehat{\Psi}(L^{T}(n-m))|\lesssim A_{k,d,\lambda}.

Schur’s test gives

\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}\left|a_{m}\overline{a_{n}}\widehat{\Psi}(L^{T}(n-m))\right|\lesssim A_{k,d,\lambda}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}|a_{n}|^{2}.

This proves (
2.3
).

Next, we prove the lower bound. Choose a transverse
k
-tuple of bands and a set

P\subset\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}B(u_{j},x_{0,j}),\ \text{with}\ \#P=A_{k,d,\lambda}.

Let
n_{j}=u_{j}/|u_{j}|
, and set

V=\operatorname{span}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
. Since

|n_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge n_{k}|\geq\nu
, the map

v\in V\longmapsto(n_{1}\cdot v,\ldots,n_{k}\cdot v)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

has bounded inverse norm. Hence, for all

p,p^{\prime}\in P
,
|\pi_{V}(p-p^{\prime})|\lesssim 1.

Choose an isometric embedding
L:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
range
V
. Let
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a fixed ball of sufficiently
small radius, depending only on
d,k,\nu,C_{0}
, such that

|t\cdot L^{T}(p-p^{\prime})|\ll 1\qquad(t\in\Omega_{0},\ p,p^{\prime}\in P).

Let
\Sigma
be a fixed unit-size totally geodesic patch parallel to
V

and containing the subpatch
L\Omega_{0}
. Define

e_{\lambda}(x)=A_{k,d,\lambda}^{-1/2}\sum_{p\in P}e^{2\pi ip\cdot x}.

Then
\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=1
.
On the subpatch
x=Lt
,
t\in\Omega_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\geq\int_{\Omega_{0}}|e_{\lambda}(Lt)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=A_{k,d,\lambda}^{-1}\sum_{p,p^{\prime}\in P}\int_{\Omega_{0}}e^{2\pi it\cdot L^{T}(p-p^{\prime})}\,dt\gtrsim A_{k,d,\lambda}

by the choice of
\Omega_{0}
.
This proves (
2.4
).
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a fixed totally geodesic submanifold of dimension
k
, with
m=d-k\geq 1
. Then there are sequence
\lambda_{j}\to\infty
and
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions
e_{\lambda_{j}}
such that

(2.5)

\|e_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\gtrsim 1+\lambda_{j}^{\frac{m}{2}-1}.

Proof.

If
m\leq 2
, we choose

e_{\lambda_{j}}(x)=e^{2\pi ijn_{0}\cdot x}
, with
0\neq n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
fixed
and
\lambda_{j}=2\pi j|n_{0}|
, and then
\|e_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\gtrsim 1.

It remains to consider
m\geq 3
and
d\geq 4
.

Let
U
be the tangent
k
-plane of
\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
is totally geodesic, on a coordinate patch we may write

x=x_{0}+Lt,\qquad t\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{k},

where
L:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an isometric embedding with range
U
. We may choose a ball
B(t_{0},r_{0})\subset\Omega

with
r_{0}>0
. Choose small
\delta>0
such that
\delta r_{0}\ll 1
.

For
d\geq 4
and odd
\lambda
, Lemma
2.1
gives
\#\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=r_{d}(\lambda^{2})\gtrsim\lambda^{d-2}
. Since the projection of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
onto
U
lies in a
k
-dimensional ball of radius
\lambda
, it may be covered by
O(\lambda^{k})

\delta
-balls in
U
. By the pigeonhole principle, one
\delta
-ball contains the projections of
\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}
points of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. We denote this set of points of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
by
S_{\lambda}
, and set
N_{\lambda}=\#S_{\lambda}\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}
.
Then there is a vector
\eta_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that

|L^{T}n-\eta_{\lambda}|\leq\delta,\qquad n\in S_{\lambda}.

Let

e_{\lambda}(x)=N_{\lambda}^{-1/2}\sum_{n\in S_{\lambda}}e^{-2\pi in\cdot(x_{0}+Lt_{0})}e^{2\pi in\cdot x}.

Then
\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=1
.
We have

|e_{\lambda}(x_{0}+Lt)|=N_{\lambda}^{-1/2}\Big|\sum_{n\in S_{\lambda}}e^{2\pi i(t-t_{0})\cdot(L^{T}n-\eta_{\lambda})}\Big|\gtrsim N_{\lambda}^{1/2}\,\qquad t\in B(t_{0},r_{0}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2}\geq\int_{B(t_{0},r_{0})}|e_{\lambda}(x_{0}+Lt)|^{2}\,dt\gtrsim N_{\lambda}\gtrsim\lambda^{d-k-2}.

This yields (
2.5
).
∎

3.
Slicing and packing

In this section, we use a slicing and packing method to establish new estimates for the number
A_{k,d,\lambda}
. Throughout this section, if
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a linear subspace, we write

\pi_{U}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to U
for the orthogonal projection onto
U
. We first show some elementary estimates for the number of integer points in
\lambda S^{d-1}
.

We shall use affine 2-planes to slice the spherical regions. Note that any affine 2-plane intersects the sphere
\lambda S^{d}
in a circle of radius at most
\lambda
. The following Lemma
3.1
follows immediately from
[
18
, Lemma 4]
, we include the statement for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an affine 2-plane, then for every
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.1)

\#\bigl(\Gamma\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}\bigr)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}.

The next lemma is a basic volume estimate for counting lattice points.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}=U\oplus U^{\perp}
be an orthogonal decomposition with
\dim U=q
. If

L\geq 1
,
C\geq 1
, and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

\#\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\pi_{U}(m-z)|\leq L,|\pi_{U^{\perp}}(m-z)|\leq C\}\lesssim L^{q}C^{d-q}.

The implicit constant only depends on
d
and
q
.

Proof.

Let

K=\{x:|\pi_{U}x|\leq L,\ |\pi_{U^{\perp}}x|\leq C\}.

Since
L,C\geq 1
, the convex symmetric body
K
contains the Euclidean unit
ball. The unit cubes centered at the lattice points in
z+K
are disjoint and
are contained in
z+K+B(0,\sqrt{d}/2)\subset z+(1+\sqrt{d}/2)K
. Therefore the
number of such lattice points is bounded by a dimensional constant times the volume

\vol(K)\approx L^{q}C^{d-q}
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

For
0\leq q\leq d-1
,
1\leq L\leq\lambda^{1/2}
, and fixed
C\geq 1
, let

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)
be the supremum of
\#P
over all finite sets

P\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}
for which there is an orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{d}=U\oplus U^{\perp}
,
\dim U=q
, such that

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{U}P)\leq L,\qquad\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{U^{\perp}}P)\leq C.

We call such sets
P

admissible
for
T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)
.

These admissible sets
P
will serve as standard bricks to count lattice points in spherical regions. We first show an estimate for the upper bound of
T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we have

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}L^{q(d-2)/d}\lambda^{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
V=U^{\perp}
. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, set

\rho(a)=C|\pi_{V}a|+L|\pi_{U}a|.

And consider the map
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by
T(a)=C\pi_{V}(a)+L\pi_{U}(a).

Since
V
are orthogonal to
U
, we have

|T(a)|\leq\rho(a)\leq\sqrt{2}|T(a)|\quad\text{ and }\quad|\det T|=C^{d-q}L^{q}.

We can choose linearly independent
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
so that

\rho(a_{1})\leq\cdots\leq\rho(a_{d})
, with

\prod_{j=1}^{d}\rho(a_{j})\lesssim L^{q}.

By definition,
C,L\geq 1
, and hence

\rho(a_{j})\geq|a_{j}|\geq 1

for every
j
. Therefore

\prod_{j=1}^{d-2}\rho(a_{j})\leq\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\rho(a_{j})\Big)^{(d-2)/d}\lesssim L^{q(d-2)/d}.

For
x,y\in P
, the assumptions give

|\pi_{V}(x-y)|\leq C,\qquad|\pi_{U}(x-y)|\leq L.

Hence, for
1\leq j\leq d-2
,

|a_{j}\cdot(x-y)|\leq|\pi_{V}a_{j}|\,|\pi_{V}(x-y)|+|\pi_{U}a_{j}|\,|\pi_{U}(x-y)|\leq\rho(a_{j}).

Since
a_{j}\cdot x
is an integer, it takes at most
O(\rho(a_{j}))

different values as
x
runs over
P
. Thus the number of possible value
lists

(a_{1}\cdot x,\ldots,a_{d-2}\cdot x)

is at most

\prod_{j=1}^{d-2}O(\rho(a_{j}))\lesssim L^{q(d-2)/d}.

Fix one of such value lists. The remaining points satisfy

a_{j}\cdot x=m_{j},\qquad 1\leq j\leq d-2,

for fixed integers
m_{j}
. The corresponding linear
equations have two free integer variables, and thus determine an affine 2-plane. Consequently by Lemma
3.1
, this fixed value list contributes at
most
O_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{\varepsilon})
points on
\lambda S^{d-1}
.
Summing over all value lists gives

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}L^{q(d-2)/d}\lambda^{\varepsilon}.

∎

Proposition
3.4
is a preliminary estimate. To obtain the exponent in Theorem
1.3
, we refine the estimate for
T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)
by establishing a recurrence in
q
.

Proposition 3.5
.

We have

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim L^{q-1+2^{-q}}.

Proof.

The case
q=0
is immediate. Indeed, a Euclidean ball of fixed radius contains only
O(1)
lattice points,
so
T_{d,0}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim 1
.

We now assume
q\geq 1
.
Let
P\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}
be admissible for

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)
, and let
N=\#P
. For
h\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, define

R(h)=\#\{x\in P:x+h\in P\}.

Every ordered pair
(x,y)\in P\times P
has a unique difference

h=y-x
. Equivalently, writing
y=x+h
, we have

N^{2}=\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}R(h).

If
R(h)>0
, then
h
is the difference of two points of
P
. Hence the
admissibility assumptions imply

|\pi_{U}h|\leq L,\qquad|\pi_{U^{\perp}}h|\leq C.

By Lemma
3.2
, the number of integer vectors satisfying
these two inequalities is
O(L^{q})
.

We separate bounded and unbounded differences. Fix a constant

H\geq 4C+10
. The contribution of small
|h|\leq H
is

O(N)
, since there are only
O(1)
such integer vectors and

R(h)\leq N
for each
h
.

Now suppose that
R(h)>0
and large
|h|>H
. From

|\pi_{U^{\perp}}h|\leq C
and
H\geq 4C
we have

|\pi_{U}h|=\bigl(|h|^{2}-|\pi_{U^{\perp}}h|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}\geq|h|/2.

In particular
\pi_{U}h\neq 0
. Set

\eta=\frac{\pi_{U}h}{|\pi_{U}h|}\in U.

For every
x
counted by
R(h)
, both
x
and
x+h
lie on

\lambda S^{d-1}
. Therefore

|x+h|^{2}=|x|^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2h\cdot x+|h|^{2}=0,

or

(3.2)

h\cdot x=-\frac{|h|^{2}}{2}.

Writing
x=x_{U}+x_{\perp}
, with
x_{U}=\pi_{U}x
and

x_{\perp}=\pi_{U^{\perp}}x
, equation
(
3.2
) gives

(3.3)

(\pi_{U}h)\cdot x_{U}=-\frac{|h|^{2}}{2}-(\pi_{U^{\perp}}h)\cdot x_{\perp}.

If
x,x^{\prime}
are both counted by
R(h)
, subtracting
(
3.3
) from
x
and
x^{\prime}
yields

|\pi_{U}h|\,|\eta\cdot(x_{U}-x^{\prime}_{U})|\leq|\pi_{U^{\perp}}h|\,|x_{\perp}-x^{\prime}_{\perp}|\leq C^{2}.

Since
|\pi_{U}h|\geq H/2
, the scalar coordinate

\eta\cdot x_{U}
has diameter
O_{C}(1)
on the set

P_{h}=\{x\in P:x+h\in P\}.

The remaining component of
x_{U}
in
U\cap\eta^{\perp}
has diameter

\leq L
, as
\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{U}P)\leq L
. The component in

U^{\perp}
is of diameter at most
C
. Hence
P_{h}
is admissible for

T_{d,q-1}(\lambda,L;C^{\prime})
, with respect to the orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{d}=(U\cap\eta^{\perp})\oplus(U^{\perp}\oplus\mathbb{R}\eta),

where
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(d,C)
. Thus, every large
|h|
satisfies

R(h)=\#P_{h}\leq T_{d,q-1}(\lambda,L;C^{\prime}).

Combining the estimates for small and large
|h|
gives

N^{2}\lesssim N+L^{q}T_{d,q-1}(\lambda,L;C^{\prime}).

Consequently,

N\lesssim 1+L^{q/2}T_{d,q-1}(\lambda,L;C^{\prime})^{1/2}.

Taking the supremum over all admissible
P
gives the recurrence

(3.4)

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim 1+L^{q/2}T_{d,q-1}(\lambda,L;C^{\prime})^{1/2}.

Because
q\leq d-1
, the constants produced by iterating
(
3.4
) depend only on
d
and
C
.

Starting from
T_{d,0}\lesssim 1
, define the exponents
\delta_{q}
by

\delta_{0}=0
and

\delta_{q}=\frac{q}{2}+\frac{\delta_{q-1}}{2}.

The recurrence gives
T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim L^{\delta_{q}}
, since

L\geq 1
. The explicit solution is

\delta_{q}=q-1+2^{-q}.

Indeed, this is true for
q=1
, and if

\delta_{q-1}=q-2+2^{-(q-1)}
, then

\delta_{q}=\frac{q}{2}+\frac{q-2}{2}+2^{-q}=q-1+2^{-q}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

For
1\leq L\leq\lambda^{1/2}
, we conjecture that the correct bounds should be
T_{d,1}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim 1
and

T_{d,q}(\lambda,L;C)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}L^{q-2}\lambda^{\varepsilon},\quad q\geq 2,\ \varepsilon>0.

These would imply Conjecture
2.4
, according to the proof of Propositions
3.9
and
3.11
. Cilleruelo-Córdoba
[
11
]
proved that

T_{2,1}(\lambda,\lambda^{\delta};C)\lesssim_{\delta}1,\ \ \delta<1/2.

The case
\delta=1/3
was proved earlier by Jarnik
[
19
]
. Bourgain-Rudnick
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
proved that
T_{2,1}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)\lesssim\log\lambda
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in S^{d-1}
such that

|n_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge n_{k}|\geq\nu
. For
1\leq j\leq k
, consider the bands

B_{j}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|n_{j}\cdot(x-x_{0,j})|\leq C_{0}\}

Put

V=\spanop(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}).

Then there is a point
p\in V
, depending on the bands, such that every

x\in B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
satisfies

|\pi_{V}x-p|\leq C,

where
C=C(d,k,\nu,C_{0})
. In particular, if

x,y\in B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
, then
|\pi_{V}(x-y)|\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Let
G=(n_{i}\cdot n_{j})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
.
For each
j
, write

\beta_{j}=n_{j}\cdot x_{0,j},\qquad\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}).

Since
\det G\geq\nu^{2}>0
, one may choose
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
so that

Gt=\beta,

and define

p=t_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+t_{k}n_{k}\in V.

Then

n_{j}\cdot p=\beta_{j},\forall 1\leq j\leq k.

We claim that
\pi_{V}x
stays close to
p
for all

x\in B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
. To see this, fix such an
x
. Since

n_{j}\in V
, we have

n_{j}\cdot\pi_{V}x=n_{j}\cdot x.

Therefore,

|n_{j}\cdot(\pi_{V}x-p)|=|n_{j}\cdot x-\beta_{j}|=|n_{j}\cdot(x-x_{0,j})|\leq C_{0}.

Now write

\pi_{V}x-p=s_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+s_{k}n_{k}.

If

c_{j}=n_{j}\cdot(\pi_{V}x-p),\qquad c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}),

then
Gs=c
, and

|s|\leq C(k,\nu)|c|\leq C(k,\nu,C_{0}).

Since the vectors
n_{j}
have length
1
,

|\pi_{V}x-p|=|s_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+s_{k}n_{k}|\leq C(d,k,\nu,C_{0}).

This proves the first claim. Now if
x,y\in B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{k}
, then

|\pi_{V}(x-y)|\leq|\pi_{V}x-p|+|\pi_{V}y-p|\leq C(d,k,\nu,C_{0}).

Hence
|\pi_{V}(x-y)|\lesssim 1
.
∎

The following lemma decomposes large spherical regions into a number of standard bricks that can be handled by Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}=V\oplus W
, with
m=\dim W\geq 2
. Suppose

\Omega\subset\{x\in\lambda S^{d-1}:|\pi_{V}x-p|\leq C\}

for some
p\in V
. Let
R=(\max(\lambda^{2}-|p|^{2},0))^{1/2}
.
Then one of the following holds.

(i)

If
R\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}
, then

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{W}\Omega)\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}
.

(ii)

If
R\gg\lambda^{1/2}
, then
\Omega
is covered by

O(\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}})
many sets
\Omega_{\alpha}
, each satisfying

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{V\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega_{\alpha}}\Omega_{\alpha})\lesssim 1,\qquad\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{W\cap\omega_{\alpha}^{\perp}}\Omega_{\alpha})\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}

for some unit vector
\omega_{\alpha}\in W
.

Proof.

Write every point
x\in\Omega
as

x=p+v+w,\qquad v\in V,\qquad w\in W,\qquad|v|\leq C.

Since
V\perp W
and
|x|=\lambda
, we have

(3.5)

|w|^{2}=\lambda^{2}-|p+v|^{2}.

Therefore,

\bigl||p+v|^{2}-|p|^{2}\bigr|\leq 2|p|\,|v|+|v|^{2}\lesssim_{C}\lambda.

By definition,

R^{2}=\max(\lambda^{2}-|p|^{2},0),

this implies the uniform shell estimate

(3.6)

\bigl||w|^{2}-R^{2}\bigr|\lesssim\lambda.

Indeed, if
|p|\leq\lambda
, then

R^{2}=\lambda^{2}-|p|^{2}
and the conclusion follows directly from
(
3.5
). If
|p|>\lambda
, then
R=0
and the same
bound follows because
\lambda^{2}-|p|^{2}<0
while

|w|^{2}=\lambda^{2}-|p+v|^{2}\geq 0
differs from

\lambda^{2}-|p|^{2}
by
O(\lambda)
.

If
R\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}
, then (
3.6
) gives

|w|^{2}\lesssim\lambda
, hence
|w|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}
for all
x\in\Omega
.
Thus,

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{W}\Omega)\leq 2\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\pi_{W}x|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}.

This proves (i).

We now assume
R\gg\lambda^{1/2}
. From
(
3.6
),

\bigl||w|-R\bigr|=\frac{\bigl||w|^{2}-R^{2}\bigr|}{|w|+R}\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{R}.

Thus,
\pi_{W}\Omega
lies in a radial shell around the sphere

\{w\in W:|w|=R\}
of thickness
O(1+\lambda/R)
.

Put
q=m-1=\dim W-1
. Choose a maximal
c\lambda^{1/2}
-separated set on
the sphere
\{w\in W:|w|=R\}
, where
c>0
is a small fixed constant. The
corresponding spherical caps of tangential radius
O(\lambda^{1/2})
cover
the sphere, and the number of such caps is

O((\frac{R}{\lambda^{1/2}})^{q}),

because
R\gg\lambda^{1/2}
. Let
R\omega
be the center of one cap
with
\omega\in W
and
|\omega|=1
.

Consider the portion of the shell that lies over this cap. For such a point

w
, write

w=(\omega\cdot w)\omega+w_{\perp},\qquad w_{\perp}\in W\cap\omega^{\perp}.

The angular localization gives

|w_{\perp}|\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}.

We will then estimate the range of the scalar coordinate
\omega\cdot w
. Since

w
is in a small cap,
\omega\cdot w
is comparable to
R
, and

|w|-\omega\cdot w=\frac{|w_{\perp}|^{2}}{|w|+\omega\cdot w}\lesssim\frac{\lambda}{R}.

The radial variable
|w|
itself varies over an interval of length

O(1+\lambda/R)
. Hence
\omega\cdot w
also varies over an interval of
length
O(1+\lambda/R)

on this cap. We partition that interval into subintervals of bounded
length. This introduces
O(1+\lambda/R)
many subpieces for each angular cap.

On each resulting piece
\Omega_{\alpha}
, the
V
-projection has bounded
diameter as
|\pi_{V}x-p|\leq C
, and the
\omega
-coordinate has bounded
diameter by construction. Therefore,

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{V\oplus\mathbb{R}\omega}\Omega_{\alpha})\lesssim 1.

At the same time the transverse component in
W\cap\omega^{\perp}
has
diameter
O(\lambda^{1/2})
, so

\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{W\cap\omega^{\perp}}\Omega_{\alpha})\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}.

See Figure

.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
m=d-k\geq 2
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

A_{k,d,\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\beta(m,d)+\varepsilon},

with

(3.7)

\beta(m,d)=\min\left\{\frac{(m-1)(d-1)}{d},\ m-\frac{3}{2}+2^{-m}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\lambda S^{d-1}
be as before. Again, put

V=\spanop(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
,
W=V^{\perp}
,
m=d-k
. If

P\neq\varnothing
, choose
x_{0}\in P
, and set
p=\pi_{V}x_{0}
.
Lemma
3.7
gives
|\pi_{V}x-p|\lesssim 1
for all
x\in P
, so
we may apply Lemma
3.8
. Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
gives

T_{d,q}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)\lesssim\lambda^{\gamma(q,d)+\varepsilon}

where

\gamma(q,d)=\min\left\{\frac{q(d-2)}{d},\ q-1+2^{-q}\right\}.

In case (i),
P
is admissible for

T_{d,m}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)
, hence

\#P\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\gamma(m,d)/2+\varepsilon}.

This exponent is no larger than both exponents in
(
3.7
).

In case (ii),
P
is covered by
O(\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}})
patches that are admissible for

T_{d,m-1}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)
. Therefore,

\#P\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\lambda^{\gamma(m-1,d)/2}\lambda^{\varepsilon}=\lambda^{\beta(m,d)+\varepsilon}.

∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{d-1}
be the transverse bands as in
Lemma
3.7
. Then

B_{1}\cap\cdots\cap B_{d-1}

is contained in a fixed-radius tube around some line
\ell
. Moreover, the part of this tube lying on
\lambda S^{d-1}
can be divided into at
most two pieces. On each piece, the projection to the direction of
\ell
has
diameter
O(\lambda^{1/2})
.

Proof.

Let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1}
be the unit normals of the bands, and put

V=\spanop(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d-1})
.
Then by Lemma
3.7
, the band intersection is contained in a fixed-radius tube around a line
\ell
parallel to
V^{\perp}
.

It remains to describe the intersection of this tube with the sphere. Write

\ell=p+\mathbb{R}v,\qquad|v|=1,\qquad p\perp v.

Every point in the tube can be written as

x=p+tv+y,\qquad y\perp v,\qquad|y|\leq C,

where
C
is fixed. If also
|x|=\lambda
, then

t^{2}+|p+y|^{2}=\lambda^{2}.

If the intersection is nonempty, then
|p|\leq\lambda+C
. Hence, as
y
ranges
over the bounded set
|y|\leq C
, the quantity
|p+y|^{2}
varies by

O(\lambda)
. Therefore
t^{2}
also varies by
O(\lambda)
.

Now split the spherical part of the tube into the two pieces
t\geq 0
and

t\leq 0
. On either piece,

|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq|t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}|^{1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{1/2}.

Thus the projection to the direction of
\ell
has diameter

O(\lambda^{1/2})
on each of the two pieces.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

For
d\geq 3
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

A_{d-1,d,\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\beta(1,d)+\varepsilon},

with

(3.8)

\beta(1,d)=\min\left\{\frac{d-2}{2d},\frac{1}{4}\right\}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.10
, the set is covered by two admissible sets for

T_{d,1}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)
. Proposition
3.5
gives

T_{d,1}(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2};C)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{1/2})^{\min\{(d-2)/d,1/2\}}\lambda^{\varepsilon}=\lambda^{\beta(1,d)+\varepsilon},

which is the asserted exponent.
∎

4.
Discrete spherical multiplier

In this section, we use an approximation of the discrete spherical multiplier to establish new restriction estimates for eigenfunctions.

Let
n=\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|\xi|=\lambda\}
. We write the discrete spherical multiplier

S_{n}(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}

We shall use the approximation of the discrete spherical multiplier
S_{n}(x)
from Magyar-Stein-Wainger
[
21
, Section 3]
and Magyar
[
20
, Lemma 1]
, involving Kloosterman sums from analytic number theory. For any integer
q\geq 1
, write
q=2^{h}q_{1}
for some integer
h\geq 0
and odd integer
q_{1}\geq 1
. Define

\rho(q,n)=2^{h}(q_{1},n),

where
(q_{1},n)
denotes the greatest common divisor of
q_{1}
and
n
.
Let
\sigma
denote normalized Euclidean surface measure on

S^{d-1}
. Its Fourier transform has the decay estimate

(4.1)

|\hat{\sigma}(x)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}.

We use the dimensional constant

(4.2)

C_{d}=\frac{1}{2}|S^{d-1}|=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}.

Proposition 4.1

(Magyar-Stein-Wainger
[
21
]
and Magyar
[
20
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
n=\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
the discrete spherical multiplier admits the decomposition

(4.3)

S_{n}(x)=C_{d}\lambda^{d-2}M_{n}(x)+E_{n}(x),

where

(4.4)

M_{n}(x)=\sum_{1\leq q\leq\lambda}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}K_{q}(m,n)\psi(qx-m)\widehat{\sigma}(\lambda(x-m/q)).

Here

(4.5)

K_{q}(m,n)=q^{-d}\sum_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{r\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{d}}e^{2\pi i(a(|r|^{2}-n)+m\cdot r)/q}.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.6)

|K_{q}(m,n)|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}q^{-\frac{d-1}{2}+\varepsilon}\rho(q,n)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

(4.7)

|E_{n}(x)|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Here

\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is supported on
\max_{j}|x_{j}|\leq 1/4
and equal to 1 on
\max_{j}|x_{j}|\leq 1/8
.

The estimate (
4.6
) is obtained from Weil’s bound
[
24
, Chapter 4]
for a Kloosterman sum or Salié sum, depending on whether
n
is even or odd. Thus, for
s=\frac{d-1}{2}
and every
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.8)

|S_{n}(x)|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{d-2}\sum_{1\leq q\leq\lambda}q^{-s+\varepsilon}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi(qx-m)|\bigl(1+\lambda|x-m/q|\bigr)^{-s}.

4.1.
Proof of restriction estimates

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a compact smooth
k
-dimensional submanifold. By a finite partition of unity, it is enough to work on a coordinate patch. Thus, let

\Gamma:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

be a smooth lift of a patch of
\Sigma
. After shrinking the patch, we may assume that
\Gamma
is bi-Lipschitz:

(4.9)

|\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v)|\approx|u-v|,\ \ u,v\in U.

We define
T_{\lambda}:\ell^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda})\to L^{2}(U)
as

(T_{\lambda}a)(u)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{\xi}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot\Gamma(u)}.

By a
TT^{*}
argument and Schur’s test, it suffices to estimate the integral

(4.10)

\sup_{u\in U}\int_{U}|S_{n}(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v))|dv.

Then the norm of
T_{\lambda}
will be bounded by the square root of this integral estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
1\leq q\leq\lambda
, let

I_{q}(u)=\int_{U}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi(q(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v))-m)|\bigl(1+\lambda|\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v)-m/q|\bigr)^{-s}dv.

Then we have

(4.11)

I_{q}(u)\lesssim\begin{cases}q^{k}\lambda^{-k},&s>k,\\
q^{k}\lambda^{-k}\log(2\lambda/q),&s=k,\\
q^{s}\lambda^{-s},&0<s<k.\end{cases}

Proof.

The support of
\psi
forces
m
to lie in a fixed-radius neighborhood of the
k
-dimensional set
q(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(U))
. This neighborhood contains
O(q^{k})
lattice points. For each such
m
, the set of
v
contributing to the integral is contained, up to finite multiplicity, in a ball of radius
O(1/q)
in the parameter variables. By (
4.9
), the integral is bounded by

\int_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:|z|\lesssim 1/q}(1+\lambda|z|)^{-s}\,dz\lesssim\begin{cases}\lambda^{-k},&s>k,\\
\lambda^{-k}\log(2\lambda/q),&s=k,\\
\lambda^{-s}q^{s-k},&0<s<k.\end{cases}

Multiplying by the
O(q^{k})
possible values of
m
gives the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
n=\lambda^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
. For every

\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{q\leq\lambda}q^{a}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\max(a+1,0)+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Write
q=2^{h}q_{1}
, where
q_{1}
is odd. Since

(q_{1},n)^{1/2}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\mid n\\
\delta\mid q_{1}\end{subarray}}\delta^{1/2},

we have

\sum_{q\leq\lambda}q^{a}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}\leq\sum_{2^{h}\leq\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\mid n\\
\delta\ {\rm odd}\end{subarray}}2^{h/2}\delta^{1/2}(2^{h}\delta)^{a}\sum_{r\leq\lambda/(2^{h}\delta)}r^{a}.

If
a>-1
, then the inner sum is

\sum_{r\leq\lambda/(2^{h}\delta)}r^{a}\lesssim(\lambda/(2^{h}\delta))^{a+1}

and then

\sum_{q\leq\lambda}q^{a}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{a+1}\sum_{2^{h}\leq\lambda}2^{-h/2}\sum_{\delta\mid n}\delta^{-1/2}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{a+1+\varepsilon}.

The factor
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
comes from the divisor bound.
If
a\leq-1
, then the inner sum is
O_{\varepsilon}((\lambda/(2^{h}\delta))^{\varepsilon})
for any
\varepsilon>0
, and the remaining sums are

O_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{\varepsilon})
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The factor
\rho(q,n)^{1/2}
does not worsen the power of
\lambda
beyond the
usual unweighted
q^{a}
-summation, apart from an
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
loss.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every

\varepsilon>0
,

\sup_{u\in U}\int_{U}|S_{n}(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v))|dv\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
s=\frac{d-1}{2}
. By (
4.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{U}|S_{n}(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v))|dv

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-2}\sum_{1\leq q\leq\lambda}q^{-s+\varepsilon}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}I_{q}(u)+\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}.

If
s>k
, Lemma
4.2
gives
I_{q}(u)\lesssim q^{k}\lambda^{-k}
, so Lemma
4.3
implies

\displaystyle\int_{U}|S_{n}(\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v))|dv

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2}\sum_{q\leq\lambda}q^{k-s+\varepsilon}\rho(q,n)^{1/2}+\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2+\max(k-s+1,0)+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}.

If
k\leq s-1
, this is
\lambda^{d-k-2+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}
. If

s-1<k<s
, the bound is
\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}
.

If
s=k
, then Lemma
4.2
gives

I_{q}(u)\lesssim q^{k}\lambda^{-k}\log(2\lambda/q)

and Lemma
4.3

again gives the bound
\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}
.

If
s<k
, then
Lemma
4.2
gives
I_{q}(u)\lesssim q^{s}\lambda^{-s}
, and Lemma
4.3

also implies the bound
\lambda^{s+\varepsilon}
.
These bounds are uniform in
u
. Combining the cases yields the result.
∎

Using the estimate for the norm of
T_{\lambda}
, we immediately obtain the following new restriction estimates for eigenfunctions.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
1\leq k\leq d-1
. Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a compact smooth
k
-dimensional submanifold. Then for any

\varepsilon>0
,

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{\frac{d-k-2}{2}+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{4}+\varepsilon})\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

In particular, when
k\leq\frac{d-3}{2}
we have the sharp estimate

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{d-k-2}{2}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Since
\frac{d-1}{4}<\frac{d-k-1}{2}
when
k<\frac{d-1}{2}
, these bounds improve (
1.1
) when
k<\frac{d-1}{2}
. We remark that if
\Sigma
is totally geodesic, then the implicit constants in Theorem
4.6
can be independent of the direction of
\Sigma
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, the following estimates on
A_{k,d,\lambda}
hold.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
1\leq k\leq d-1
. Then, for any

\varepsilon>0
,

A_{k,d,\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

In particular, when

we have the sharp estimate

A_{k,d,\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{d-k-2+\varepsilon}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
m=d-k\geq 1
be the codimension of the totally geodesic submanifold. By

2.5
, the restriction estimates follow from the

A_{k,d,\lambda}
in Propositions

,

,
4.7
.

If

, then Proposition

gives the

\beta(1,d)=\min\left\{\frac{d-2}{2d},\frac{1}{4}\right\}.

Dividing by two gives
\alpha(1,3)=1/12
and

for

.

gives the lattice exponent

\beta(m,d)=\min\left\{\frac{(m-1)(d-1)}{d},\ m-\frac{3}{2}+2^{-m}\right\}.

, Proposition

also gives

.

when

6.
Further discussions on restrictions to curves

In this section, we prove some new restriction estimates for curves and discuss their relation to the Discrete Restriction Conjecture (Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
, Conjecture 2.6]
). By Theorem
4.6
, for any curve segment
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
, when
d\geq 5
we have sharp estimates

(6.1)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{d-3}{2}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},

and when
d=4
, we have

(6.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{4})}.

We remark that the power
\frac{3}{4}
agrees with the one from the slicing and packing method in Proposition
3.9
. The conjectural power is
\frac{1}{2}
. See Conjecture
1.2
and Remark
6.5
.

When
d=2
, Bourgain-Rudnick proved the following result.

Theorem 6.1

(Bourgain-Rudnick
[
5
,
6
]
)
.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a geodesic segment, then

(6.3)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\sqrt{\log\lambda}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
is a smooth curve segment whose geodesic curvature is nowhere vanishing, then

(6.4)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

It is conjectured that the uniform bound should hold for any real-analytic curve. Bourgain-Rudnick’s proof in
[
5
]
actually gives the uniform bound (
6.4
) for any real-analytic curve that is not a geodesic. Thus geodesics are the only unsolved case for (
6.4
) among all real-analytic curves.

When
d=3
, we prove the following results using Bourgain–Rudnick’s cap estimates and the van der Corput lemma. These improve the general bound
O(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log\lambda})
in (
1.1
), while the conjectural bound is
O_{\varepsilon}(\lambda^{\varepsilon})
. See Conjecture
1.2
and Remark
6.5
.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{3}
is (i) a geodesic segment, or (ii) a smooth
curve segment whose torsion is nowhere vanishing, or (iii) a real-analytic
curve segment whose geodesic curvature is nowhere vanishing, then for any
\varepsilon>0

(6.5)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

If
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{3}
is (iv) a smooth planar curve segment whose
geodesic curvature is nowhere vanishing, then

(6.6)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Proof.

The geodesic case (i) follows from Theorem
1.3
. We prove the
remaining cases by a local
TT^{*}
argument. Write

e_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{\xi}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot x},\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}=\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\lambda S^{2},\qquad\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}=\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}|a_{\xi}|^{2}.

Choose a lift and an arclength parametrization

\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{3},\qquad|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|=1.

After a finite partition of unity it suffices to prove a weighted estimate
with
0\leq\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I)
. Put

K(h)=\int_{I}\chi(t)e^{2\pi ih\cdot\gamma(t)}\,dt.

Then

\int_{I}\left|\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{\xi}e^{2\pi i\xi\cdot\gamma(t)}\right|^{2}\chi(t)\,dt=\sum_{\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}a_{\xi}\overline{a_{\eta}}K(\xi-\eta).

Since
K(-h)=\overline{K(h)}
, Schur’s test reduces the proof to bounding

B_{\lambda}:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}|K(\xi-\eta)|.

We shall use Bourgain–Rudnick’s cap estimate
[
5
]
in the following two forms. If
F_{3}(\lambda,r)
denotes the maximal number of points of
\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap\lambda S^{2}
in a spherical cap of Euclidean diameter
r
, then for
1\leq r\leq 2\lambda
,

F_{3}(\lambda,r)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}(1+r).

Consequently, for every two-plane
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with

\pi_{P}
denoting orthogonal projection onto
P
,

N_{\xi}(U;P):=\#\{\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}:|\pi_{P}(\eta-\xi)|\leq U\}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\bigl(1+(\lambda U)^{1/2}\bigr),\qquad 1\leq U\leq 2\lambda.

Indeed, the set is contained in the intersection of
\lambda S^{2}
with a

U
-tube about the affine line
\xi+P^{\perp}
, and this intersection is
covered by
O(1)
caps of Euclidean diameter
C(\lambda U)^{1/2}
.

We also use the standard finite-type van der Corput lemma: if

\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|\phi^{(j)}(t)|\geq A

on an interval, with the amplitude in a fixed bounded
C^{1}
-family, then

\left|\int e^{i\phi(t)}a(t)\,dt\right|\lesssim A^{-1/m}.

Now Case (ii) and (iv) follow immediately.

(ii) Nowhere vanishing torsion case.

Assume

\det(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime},\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime})\neq 0

on
\operatorname{supp}\chi
. Then the finite-type van der Corput estimate gives the following.

|K(h)|\lesssim(1+|h|)^{-1/3}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq R\leq 2\lambda\\
R\ \mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}R^{-1/3}\#\{\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}:|\eta-\xi|\leq 2R\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq R\leq 2\lambda\\
R\ \mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}R^{-1/3}(1+R)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

Thus,

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(iv) Planar curved case.

Assume
\gamma(I)\subset x_{0}+P
, where
P
is a two-dimensional linear
plane, and assume that the curvature in
P
is nowhere zero in

\operatorname{supp}\chi
. Then

|K(h)|\lesssim(1+|\pi_{P}h|)^{-1/2}.

Hence, summing dyadically in
U=1+|\pi_{P}(\eta-\xi)|
,

\displaystyle B_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq U\leq 2\lambda\\
U\ \mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}U^{-1/2}N_{\xi}(2U;P)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq U\leq 2\lambda\\
U\ \mathrm{dyadic}\end{subarray}}U^{-1/2}\bigl(1+(\lambda U)^{1/2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

And Schur’s test gives

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/4+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Finally Case (iii) requires some further discussion.

(iii) Analytic curved case.

Assume now that
\gamma
is real-analytic and has nowhere vanishing geodesic curvature,

|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t)|\geq c_{0}>0.

Let

\tau(t)=\det(\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(t),\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)).

If
\tau\equiv 0
on a subinterval, then the curve is planar there, and the
planar estimate gives the stronger bound. Otherwise, the zeros of
\tau

are isolated and, hence, finite on the compact support after shrinking. Away
from these zeros we are in the nowhere vanishing torsion case. It remains to show for a small neighborhood of one zero, say
t=0
.

Set

P_{0}=\operatorname{span}\{\gamma^{\prime}(0),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(0)\}.

After a rigid motion, translation, and analytic reparametrization, absorbing
the Jacobian into the amplitude, we may write

\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s),z(s))=\bigl(s+O(s^{2}),\,c_{2}s^{2}+O(s^{3}),\,c_{k}s^{k}+O(s^{k+1})\bigr),

where

c_{2}c_{k}\neq 0,\qquad k\geq 4.

If no finite
k
occurs, then
z\equiv 0
near
0
, so the piece is
planar and is already handled.

Let

K_{0}(h)=\int\chi_{0}(s)e^{2\pi ih\cdot\gamma(s)}\,ds

with
\chi_{0}
supported in this neighborhood. Write

\rho=1+|h|,\qquad V=1+|\pi_{P_{0}}h|.

We claim

(6.7)

|K_{0}(h)|\lesssim\begin{cases}\rho^{-1/k},&V\leq\rho^{2/k},\\[5.69054pt]
\rho^{-\frac{1}{3(k-2)}}V^{-\frac{k-3}{3(k-2)}},&V>\rho^{2/k}.\end{cases}

Indeed,

\det(\gamma^{\prime}(0),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(0),\gamma^{(k)}(0))\neq 0,

so, after shrinking the support,

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|h\cdot\gamma^{(j)}(s)|\gtrsim|h|.

The finite-type van der Corput gives the first bound in (
6.7
).

Assume now
V>\rho^{2/k}
. The case
V=O(1)
is trivial, so assume

V\approx|\pi_{P_{0}}h|
. Write
h=(q,m)
, where

q\in P_{0}
and
m\in P_{0}^{\perp}
, and set
p(s)=(x(s),y(s))
. The phase is

h\cdot\gamma(s)=q\cdot p(s)+mz(s).

We split the integral at the scale

\delta=\left(\frac{V}{\rho}\right)^{1/(k-2)}.

We discuss in two cases for
|s|\leq c\delta
and
|s|\approx r
,
r\geq c\delta
.

Case 1.
On
|s|\leq c\delta
, the projected curve
p
has nowhere vanishing curvature,
so

\max\{|q\cdot p^{\prime}(s)|,\ |q\cdot p^{\prime\prime}(s)|\}\gtrsim|q|\approx V.

Also,

|mz^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim\rho|s|^{k-1},\qquad|mz^{\prime\prime}(s)|\lesssim\rho|s|^{k-2}.

Since
|s|\leq c\delta
and
\rho\delta^{k-2}=V
, we have

\max\{|mz^{\prime}(s)|,|mz^{\prime\prime}(s)|\}\lesssim c^{k-2}V.

Choosing
c>0
small gives

\max\{|(h\cdot\gamma)^{\prime}(s)|,\ |(h\cdot\gamma)^{\prime\prime}(s)|\}\gtrsim V

on the inner region. Hence the inner contribution is

\lesssim V^{-1/2}.

Case 2.
On a dyadic annulus
|s|\approx r
,
r\geq c\delta
, the normal form gives

|\det(\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}(s))|\approx r^{k-3}.

Since the first three derivatives of
\gamma
are bounded, this implies

\max_{1\leq j\leq 3}|h\cdot\gamma^{(j)}(s)|\gtrsim\rho r^{k-3}.

Thus the annular contribution is

\lesssim(\rho r^{k-3})^{-1/3}.

Summing over dyadic
r\geq c\delta
gives

\sum_{r\geq c\delta}(\rho r^{k-3})^{-1/3}\lesssim(\rho\delta^{k-3})^{-1/3}.

Moreover
V^{-1/2}\leq(\rho\delta^{k-3})^{-1/3}
when

V>\rho^{2/k}
. Therefore

|K_{0}(h)|\lesssim(\rho\delta^{k-3})^{-1/3}=\rho^{-\frac{1}{3(k-2)}}V^{-\frac{k-3}{3(k-2)}}.

This proves the claim (
6.7
).

We now prove the Schur bound for
K_{0}
. Fix

\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
, put
h=\xi-\eta
, and decompose dyadically
according to

\rho\leq 1+|h|<2\rho,\qquad 1\leq\rho\lesssim\lambda.

For the subregion
V\leq\rho^{2/k}
, using (
6.7
) and the projection
count,

\displaystyle S_{\rho}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{-1/k}N_{\xi}(C\rho^{2/k};P_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\rho^{-1/k}\bigl(1+(\lambda\rho^{2/k})^{1/2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

For the subregion
V>\rho^{2/k}
, set

\alpha=\frac{1}{3(k-2)},\qquad\beta=\frac{k-3}{3(k-2)}.

Then
\alpha+\beta=1/3
and
0<\beta<1/2
. Dyadic summation in

U\approx V
gives

\displaystyle S_{\rho}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U>\rho^{2/k}\\
U\ \mathrm{dyadic}\\
U\lesssim\rho\end{subarray}}\rho^{-\alpha}U^{-\beta}N_{\xi}(2U;P_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\rho^{-\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U>\rho^{2/k}\\
U\ \mathrm{dyadic}\\
U\lesssim\rho\end{subarray}}U^{-\beta}\bigl(1+(\lambda U)^{1/2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}+\lambda^{1/2+\varepsilon}\rho^{-\alpha}\rho^{1/2-\beta}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

Thus, for each dyadic
\rho
,

S_{\rho}^{(0)}+S_{\rho}^{(1)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

After summing over
O(\log\lambda)
dyadic
\rho
’s and absorbing the
logarithm into
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\sup_{\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}}|K_{0}(\xi-\eta)|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

Combining the finitely many pieces gives

B_{\lambda}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2/3+\varepsilon}.

Schur’s test therefore yields

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B_{\lambda}^{1/2}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

∎

6.1.
Discrete Restriction Conjecture

We briefly discuss the relation between the
L^{2}
restriction bounds and global
L^{p}
bounds for eigenfunctions, such as the Discrete Restriction Conjecture. This provides a different perspective for understanding the bounds in (
6.5
) and (
6.2
). See Remark
6.5
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a fixed smooth compact
submanifold of codimension
m
.
Assume that for some
2\leq p\leq\infty
one has the global estimate

(6.8)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq A_{p}(\lambda)\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Then

(6.9)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{m}{p}}A_{p}(\lambda)\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Proof.

Let

T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\dist(x,\Sigma)\leq C\lambda^{-1}\}

be a fixed constant multiple of the wavelength-scale tube around
\Sigma
.
The first step is the local tube estimate

(6.10)

\int_{\Sigma}|e_{\lambda}|^{2}\,d\sigma\lesssim\lambda^{m}\int_{T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)}|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

We prove (
6.10
). Fix
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and rescale around
x

at wavelength scale:

u(z)=e_{\lambda}\left(x+\frac{z}{\lambda}\right).

Then
u
satisfies

-\Delta_{z}u=(2\pi)^{2}u

on a fixed Euclidean ball. By the standard interior elliptic estimate,

|u(0)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{B(0,1)}|u(z)|^{2}\,dz.

Returning to
e_{\lambda}
, this gives

(6.11)

|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{d}\int_{B(x,\lambda^{-1})}|e_{\lambda}(y)|^{2}\,dy.

The factor
\lambda^{d}
comes from the change of variables

y=x+z/\lambda
.

Now integrate (
6.11
) over
x\in\Sigma
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{d}\int_{\Sigma}\int_{B(x,\lambda^{-1})}|e_{\lambda}(y)|^{2}\,dy\,d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|e_{\lambda}(y)|^{2}\sigma\{x\in\Sigma:\dist(x,y)\leq\lambda^{-1}\}\,dy.

Let
k=d-m
. Since
\Sigma
is a fixed smooth
k
-dimensional submanifold, a ball of
radius
\lambda^{-1}
intersects
\Sigma
in
k
-dimensional measure

\lesssim\lambda^{-k}.

Moreover, the inner set is empty unless

y\in T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)
. Therefore

\int_{\Sigma}|e_{\lambda}|^{2}\,d\sigma\lesssim\lambda^{d}\lambda^{-k}\int_{T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)}|e_{\lambda}(y)|^{2}\,dy,

which proves (
6.10
).

Next, since
\Sigma
has codimension
m
inside
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, its

\lambda^{-1}
-tube has volume

(6.12)

|T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)|\lesssim\lambda^{-m}.

By Hölder’s inequality and (
6.8
),

\displaystyle\int_{T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)}|e_{\lambda}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq|T_{\lambda^{-1}}(\Sigma)|^{1-2/p}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m(1-2/p)}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m(1-2/p)}A_{p}(\lambda)^{2}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

Combining this with (
6.10
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|e_{\lambda}|^{2}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{m}\lambda^{-m(1-2/p)}A_{p}(\lambda)^{2}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2m/p}A_{p}(\lambda)^{2}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

This yields
(
6.9
).
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Assume
d\geq 3
, and suppose that the endpoint discrete restriction
estimate

(6.13)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{c}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},\qquad p_{c}=\frac{2d}{d-2},

holds. Then for every smooth compact submanifold

\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
of codimension
m
,

(6.14)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{m}{2}-\frac{m}{d}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Remark 6.5
.

For every smooth curve
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have
m=d-1
and then the endpoint estimate (
6.13
)
would imply

(6.15)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{d-3}{2}+\frac{1}{d}+\varepsilon}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

The power is exactly
\frac{1}{3}
when
d=3
and
\frac{3}{4}
when
d=4
, which agrees with the power that we have obtained in (
6.5
) and (
6.2
). Since the conjecture (
6.13
) is still open (see Bourgain-Demeter
[
4
, Conjecture 2.6]
and references therein), it would be interesting to prove (
6.5
) for any smooth curve
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{T}^{3}
, without assuming (
6.13
).
