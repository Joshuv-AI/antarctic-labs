Protocol for enhancing production of human PSC-derived cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells with placental growth factor
Authors: Liang Liu, Phuong Thao Nguyen, Jie Liu, Makoto Sahara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryPlacental growth factor (PLGF) drives cardiomyogenesis and vasculogenesis during cardiogenesis. Here, we present a protocol for enhancing production of human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes (CMs) and endothelial cells (ECs). We describe steps for CM-/EC-directed hPSC differentiation with PLGF. Our approaches can achieve production of hPSC-CMs/-ECs by 2- to 2.5-fold compared with those in traditionally established protocols, through promoting proliferation and differentiation of cardiac and vascular progenitors.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Witman et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, Single Cell, Developmental biology, Stem Cells, Cell Differentiation

Innovation

We have recently identified that placental growth factor (PLGF) exerts a dual function of cardiomyogenesis and vasculogenesis in a spatiotemporal fashion during heart development through single-cell omics studies.1 Further utility and potentiality of PLGF have been tested in in vitro hPSC differentiation into cardiomyocytes (CMs) and endothelial cells (ECs). Here we innovate two unique approaches for enhancing production of hPSC-derived CMs and ECs with featuring PLGF. Traditionally, Wnt signaling modulation is conducted for generation of hPSC-CMs,3,4 and sequential activation or inactivation of several signaling pathways such as Wnt, Notch, transforming growth factor-β, and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is employed for generation of hPSC-ECs.5,6 Of interest, we discover that adding PLGF to these conventionally established protocols promotes induction and proliferation of mesodermal precursor cell-derived cardiac and vascular progenitors in an early stage. This is followed by rapid and large-scale production of hPSC-CMs and hPSC-ECs in a late stage by around two to 2.5-fold compared with those when only the conventionally established protocols are used without PLGF. In fact, we find that a single hPSC can generate approximately 81 ECs and 12 CMs, respectively, within 12 days in our approaches. Consequently, the current protocol will provide more reliable cell sources for future construction of vascularized heart tissue models, and thereby, may help with development of effective cardiovascular regenerative therapies.

The rationale for the experimental design

As described, the conventional hPSC-CM and hPSC-EC differentiation protocols mainly depend on stepwise modulation of developmental pathways, such as Wnt signaling for CM induction and mesoderm induction signaling followed by VEGF-based endothelial specification for EC induction. In the present study, PLGF was incorporated as an additional stage-specific signal based on our prior evidence.1 In the end, PLGF was added to the conventional protocols with the aim of enhancing lineage progression and increasing yields of the desired cell types such as CMs and ECs in a temporally controlled manner, rather than altering the overall developmental framework of hPSC differentiation.

Timing: 12 days

CM-directed differentiation of hPSCs with PLGF

Timing: 14 days

Traditional approaches for hPSC-CM differentiation employs Wnt signaling modulation.3,4 By adding the treatment steps with human recombinant protein of PLGF at a specific time point, mesodermal cardiac progenitor cells are more easily induced, and subsequently, hPSC-CMs can be produced within 12 days by approximately 2-fold increase compared with those generated by traditional approaches alone without PLGF.

Expected outcomes

PLGF-enhanced hPSC-ECs

Following the traditional hPSC-EC differentiation protocol (Figure 1A), CD31+VE-cad+ ECs appear on day 6, occupying around 50% of total differentiating cells (Figures 1B and 3). The generated ECs at this stage typically express endothelial progenitor markers, such as KDR and CD34 in a higher degree, and are thereby considered as immature (endothelial progenitor-like) ECs.6 Importantly, additional PLGF treatment during days 1 to 6 significantly enhances EC induction, as represented by the increased percentages and cell numbers of the generated ECs (Figures 3A and 3B). Reversely, a PLGF receptor, i.e., VEGF receptor-1 (VEGFR1) blocker ZM306416 downregulates these parameters, highlighting the importance of the PLGF-VEGFR1 axis in the context of hPSC-EC differentiation. After CD31+VE-cad+ ECs are sorted and replated on day 6 for EC expansion culture, continued PLGF treatment during the phase 3 (days 6 to 12) further enhances EC proliferation (Figures 4A and 4B). In the end, 2.0 × 105 hPSCs (e.g., hESC H9 or HES-3 line), initially seeded in one well of a 12-well plate, yielded approximately 1.62 × 107 ECs by day 12 in the PLGF-treated hPSC-EC differentiation protocol. This is corresponding to about 81 ECs per a single input hPSC. Compared with control, the present treatment with PLGF represents an approximately 2.5-fold increase in EC yield (Figure 4B, bottom). The generated ECs can form easily vascular tube formation in vitro, demonstrating their functional activity (Figures 4C and 4D). Importantly, PLGF treatment also enhances vascular tube formation quantitatively. These findings highlight the important role of the PLGF-VEGFR1 axis in the context of proliferation and function of hPSC-ECs. Finally, we measured quantitative gene expression of arterial and venous EC markers7 in hPSC-ECs treated with or without PLGF. As shown in Figure 5, hPSC-ECs tended to express arterial EC markers to higher degrees but venous EC markers to lower degrees compared to human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs). Of particular interest, the PLGF-treated hPSC-ECs expressed arterial EC markers to much higher degrees than control hPSC-ECs, indicating that PLGF treatment biases hPSC-ECs towards arterial-like ECs (Figure 5).

PLGF-enhanced hPSC-CM

Following the traditional hPSC-CM differentiation (Figure 2A), ISL1+ cardiac progenitors (CPs) appear on day 6, occupying around 90% of total differentiating cells (Figure 6A). Importantly, additional PLGF treatment during days 1 to 5 significantly enhances induction of ISL1+ CPs, as represented by the increased percentages and cell numbers of the generated CPs (Figures 6A and 6B). Reversely, ZM306416 downregulates these parameters, highlighting the importance of the PLGF-VEGFR1 axis in the context of hPSC-CP/CM differentiation and proliferation. Following this, TNNT2+ CMs appear on day 12 as a main population of differentiating cells (Figure 6A). PLGF treatment during days 1 to 5 also enhances induction of TNNT2+ CMs, as represented by the increased percentages and cell numbers of the generated CMs, while ZM306416 downregulates these parameters (Figures 6A and 6B). In the end, 6.5 × 105 hPSCs (e.g., hESC H9 or HES-3 line), initially seeded in one well of a 12-well plate, yielded approximately 7.74 × 106 CMs by day 12 in the PLGF-treated hPSC-CM differentiation protocol. This is corresponding to about 12 CMs per a single input hPSC. Compared with control, the present treatment with PLGF represents an approximately 2-fold increase in CM yield (Figure 6B, bottom). Immunostaining of differentiating cells on day 12 shows significantly proliferated CMs by PLGF treatment (Figure 2C). The hPSC-CMs on day 12 are spontaneously contracting in a robust and broad fashion (Methods Video S1). The PLGF-treated hPSC-CMs show more robust and broader beating in a regular basis (Methods Video S2), implying the importance of the PLGF-VEGFR1 axis in the context of hPSC-CM proliferation and function.

Limitations

HPSC differentiation in the present protocol is performed in two-dimensional culture conditions. While suitable for scalable production, monolayer systems do not fully recapitulate the spatial, mechanical, and multicellular complexity of in vivo cardiovascular development. Consequently, cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions may not reflect physiological contexts. Thus, there is still room for improvement to obtain larger numbers of hPSC-CMs and hPSC-ECs than those described here.

The magnitude of PLGF-mediated enhancement for production of hPSC-CMs and hPSC-ECs may vary depending on genetic background, epigenetic status, and baseline differentiation propensity of individual hPSC lines, as well as culture environment and conditions. Therefore, to get maximum absolute yield improvements in generation of those cells with PLGF, optimization and minor modification of the protocol described here (e.g., PLGF concentration and/or treatment periods, etc.) may be needed in each case.

Problem 5
