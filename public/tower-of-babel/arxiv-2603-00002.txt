Hedgehog Reconstruction of Polygons: Non-Central Sections and Slabs
Brendan Matthews

Abstract
We show that a polygon can be uniquely determined by the lengths of non-central sections supporting a piecewise-analytic hedgehog in the interior of the polygon. We also prove the analogous result for slab areas - centrally-symmetric polygon can be reconstructed based on the areas of slabs supporting an analytic centrally-symmetric hedgehog in the interior of the polygon.

a=\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle}{\langle m_{1},\xi\rangle}
,
b=\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle}{\langle\ell_{1},\xi\rangle}
,
c=\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle}{\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle}
and lastly
d=\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle}{\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}
. To determine the area
A_{\triangle}
of the triangle with vertices
u,q_{1},p_{1}
, we evaluate the following,

\displaystyle|u,q_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|u,u+bm_{1}\right|=|u,u|+b|u,m_{1}|=b|u,m_{1}|,

\displaystyle|q_{1},p_{1}|

\displaystyle=\left|u+bm_{1},u+a\ell_{1}\right|=|u,u|+a|u,\ell_{1}|+b|m_{1},u|+ab|m_{1},\ell_{1}|

\displaystyle=a|u,\ell_{1}|-b|u,m_{1}|+ab|m_{1},\ell_{1}|,

\displaystyle|p_{1},u|

\displaystyle=|u+a\ell_{1},u|=|u,u|-a|u,\ell_{1}|=-a|u,\ell_{1}|.

The sum yields

A_{\triangle}=\frac{1}{2}ab|m_{1},\ell_{1}|=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle)^{2}}{\langle\ell_{1},\xi\rangle\langle m_{1},\xi\rangle}\right)|m_{1},\ell_{1}|
. For the area
A_{\square}
of the quadrilateral with vertices
v,w,q_{1},p_{1}
, the corresponding determinants are

\displaystyle|v,w|

\displaystyle=-|w,v|,

\displaystyle|w,q_{2}|

\displaystyle=|w,w+dm_{2}|=|w,w|+d|w,m_{2}|=d|w,m_{2}|,

\displaystyle|q_{2},p_{2}|

\displaystyle=|w+dm_{2},v+c\ell_{2}|=|w,v|+c|w,\ell_{2}|-d|v,m_{2}|+cd|m_{2},\ell_{2}|,

\displaystyle|p_{2},v|

\displaystyle=|v+c\ell_{2},v|=|v,v|+c|\ell_{2},v|=-c|v,\ell_{2}|.

The sum yields the area
A_{\square}
,

\begin{split}A_{\square}&=\frac{1}{2}(c|w-v,\ell_{2}|+d|w-v,m_{2}|+cd|m_{2},\ell_{2}|)\\
&=\left(\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle}{2\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle}\right)|w-v,\ell_{2}|+\left(\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle}{2\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}\right)|w-v,m_{2}|\\
&+\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle)(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle)}{2\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}|m_{2},\ell_{2}|.\end{split}

By
(\ref{eqn:equality-tri-quad})
,
A_{\triangle}=A_{\square}
that reads

\begin{split}\left(\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle)^{2}}{\langle\ell_{1},\xi\rangle\langle m_{1},\xi\rangle}\right)|m_{1},\ell_{1}|=&\left(\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle}{\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle}\right)|w-v,\ell_{2}|\\
&+\left(\frac{h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle}{\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}\right)|w-v,m_{2}|\\
&+\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle)(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle)}{\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}|m_{2},\ell_{2}|.\end{split}

(8)

Let
u=\alpha e_{1}+h_{M}(e_{2})e_{2}
,
v=\beta e_{1}+h_{M}(e_{2})e_{2}
, and
w=\gamma e_{1}+h_{M}(e_{2})e_{2}
, where
\alpha<0
,
\beta>0,\gamma>0
. Since
h(\phi)=h_{M}(\xi(\phi))
is real analytic on
S^{1}
, then
h_{M}
is equal to its power series around
e_{2}
(
\phi=0
) on
\Omega
,

h_{M}(\xi)=h_{M}(\sin\phi,\cos\phi)=h(0)+\frac{dh}{d\phi}(0)\phi+o(\phi^{2}).

By the chain rule for
\xi\in\Omega
,
x_{1}=\sin\phi
,
x_{2}=\cos\phi
,

\displaystyle\frac{dh}{d\phi}(0)=\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial x_{1}}{\partial\phi}+\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{\partial x_{2}}{\partial\phi}=\left.\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{1}}\cos\phi\right|_{\phi=0}-\left.\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{2}}\sin\phi\right|_{\phi=0}=\left.\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{1}}\right|_{\phi=0}=h_{x_{1}}(0).

Hence, the Taylor expansion of
h
about
\phi=0
is

h(\phi)=h(0)+h_{x_{1}}(0)\phi+o(\phi^{2}).

(9)

Also consider the Taylor expansion of
\langle x,\xi(\phi)\rangle
for sufficiently small
\phi
, where
x=\lambda e_{1}+h(e_{2})e_{2}
,

\begin{split}\langle x,\xi(\phi)\rangle&=\lambda\sin\phi+h_{M}(e_{2})\cos\phi=\lambda(\phi+o(\phi^{3}))+h_{M}(e_{2})(1+o(\phi^{2}))\\
&=h_{M}(e_{2})+\lambda\phi+o(\phi^{2}).\end{split}

Then

h_{M}(\xi(\phi))-\langle x,\xi(\phi)\rangle=(h_{x_{1}}(0)-\lambda)\phi+o(\phi^{2})=-\tilde{\lambda}\phi+o(\phi^{2}).

Since the above holds for all points
x
on the horizontal line
\langle x,e_{2}\rangle=h_{M}(e_{2})
, for
x=u,v,w
, we have

\displaystyle h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=-\tilde{\alpha}\phi+o(\phi^{2}),\quad h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle=-\tilde{\beta}\phi+o(\phi^{2}),

\displaystyle h_{M}(\xi)-\langle w,\xi\rangle=-\tilde{\gamma}\phi+o(\phi^{2}).

Furthermore, for
m_{i}=(m_{i_{1}},m_{i_{2}})
,
\ell_{i}=(\ell_{i_{1}},\ell_{i_{2}})
the denominators in (
8
) can also be expanded around
e_{2}
,

\displaystyle\langle\ell_{i},\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\ell_{i_{1}}\sin\phi+\ell_{i_{2}}\cos\phi=\ell_{i_{2}}+\ell_{i_{1}}\phi+o(\phi^{2}),

\displaystyle\langle m_{i},\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=m_{i_{1}}\sin\phi+m_{i_{2}}\cos\phi=m_{i_{2}}+m_{i_{1}}\phi+o(\phi^{2}).

For the reciprocal of
\langle\ell_{i},\xi\rangle
and
\langle m_{i},\xi\rangle
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\ell_{i},\xi\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell_{i_{2}}+\ell_{i_{1}}\phi+o(\phi^{2})}=\dfrac{1}{\ell_{i_{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\epsilon(\phi)}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\ell_{i_{2}}}\left(1-\frac{\ell_{i_{1}}}{\ell_{i_{2}}}\phi+o(\phi^{2})\right),

where
\epsilon(\phi)=\frac{\ell_{i_{1}}}{\ell_{i_{2}}}\phi+o(\phi^{2})
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\langle\ell_{i},\xi\rangle}=\frac{1}{\langle\ell_{i},e_{2}\rangle}\left(1-\frac{\langle\ell_{i},e_{1}\rangle}{\langle\ell_{i},e_{2},\rangle}\phi+o(\phi^{2})\right),\,\frac{1}{\langle m_{i},\xi\rangle}=\frac{1}{\langle m_{i},e_{2}\rangle}\left(1-\frac{\langle m_{i},e_{1}\rangle}{\langle m_{i},e_{2},\rangle}\phi+o(\phi^{2})\right).

Consequently, for sufficiently small
\phi
, the expansions of the left-hand side (LHS) and the three terms of the right-hand side (RHS) in (
8
) are,

\displaystyle LHS

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\alpha}^{2}}{\langle\ell_{1},e_{2}\rangle\langle m_{1},e_{2}\rangle}\phi^{2}+o(\phi^{3}),\quad RHS_{(1)}=\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{\langle\ell_{2},e_{2}\rangle}|v-w,\ell_{2}|\phi+o(\phi^{2}),

\displaystyle RHS_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{\langle m_{2},e_{2}\rangle}|v-w,m_{2}|\phi+o(\phi^{2}),\quad RHS_{(3)}=\frac{\tilde{\beta}\tilde{\gamma}}{\langle\ell_{2},e_{2}\rangle\langle m_{2},e_{2}\rangle}|m_{2},\ell_{2}|\phi^{2}+o(\phi^{3}).

Equating the expansions of the two sides and collecting linear terms in
\phi
yields

\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{\langle\ell_{2},e_{2}\rangle}|v-w,\ell_{2}|+\frac{\tilde{\gamma}}{\langle m_{2},e_{2}\rangle}|v-w,m_{2}|=0.

(10)

Note
v-w=(\beta-\gamma)e_{1}
, then

|v-w,\ell_{2}|=(\beta-\gamma)\langle\ell_{2},e_{2}\rangle.

Analogously for
m_{2}
,
|v-w,m_{2}|=(\beta-\gamma)\langle m_{2},e_{2}\rangle
. Then
(\ref{eqn:lin-terms})
gives

(\beta-\gamma)(\tilde{\beta}+\tilde{\gamma})=0.

If
\beta=\gamma
, then
v=w
. Otherwise,
\tilde{\beta}=-\tilde{\gamma}
, then by the definition of
\tilde{\beta}
and
\tilde{\gamma}
, this yields

h_{x_{1}}(e_{2})=\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}.
Furthermore, note that the midpoint of
v
and
w
is then given by
\left(\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2},h_{M}(e_{2})\right)=\left(h_{x_{1}}(e_{2}),h(e_{2})\right)
.

By (
1
), a support point
X_{\xi}\in M
for
\xi=(\sin\phi,\cos\phi)
is

X_{\xi}=h_{M}(\xi)\xi+\frac{dh_{M}(\xi)}{d\phi}\xi^{\perp}
. Note that
\xi^{\perp}=(\cos\phi,-\sin\phi)
.
If
\xi=e_{2}
(that is
\phi=0
), then
\xi^{\perp}=e_{1}
. Using the chain rule,
\frac{dh}{d\phi}(e_{2})=h_{x_{1}}(e_{2})
. This yields that

X_{e_{2}}=h(e_{2})e_{2}+h_{x_{1}}(e_{2})e_{1}=(h_{x_{1}}(e_{2}),h(e_{2}))
.
Thus, the support point
X_{e_{2}}
of
M
is the midpoint between
v
and
w
. In particular, that means
X_{e_{2}}\in M
,
X_{e_{2}}\not\in P\cap Q
, which is impossible since
M\subset P\cap Q
, so
\tilde{\beta}\neq-\tilde{\gamma}
.

Under the assumption
\beta=\gamma
(or equivalently
v=w
),
(\ref{eqn:bigone})
results in

\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle u,\xi\rangle)^{2}}{\langle\ell_{1},\xi\rangle\langle m_{1},\xi\rangle}|m_{1},\ell_{1}|=\frac{(h_{M}(\xi)-\langle v,\xi\rangle)^{2}}{\langle\ell_{2},\xi\rangle\langle m_{2},\xi\rangle}|m_{2},\ell_{2}|.

By Theorem
2.1
both sides are analytic, and by Theorem
2.2
their analytic extensions are unique and coincide beyond the intersected sides of the polygons. Then, the points of non-analyticity of the left and right hand side must coincide. Thus,
\{\ell_{1}^{\perp},m_{1}^{\perp}\}=\{\ell_{2}^{\perp},m_{2}^{\perp}\}
.
Furthermore, it implies that the pair of vectors
\{\ell_{1},m_{1}\}
must have the same orientation as
\{\ell_{2},m_{2}\}
(otherwise, the signs of the left side and the right side are distinct).
Then the only possible outcome is
\ell_{1}=-\ell_{2}
and
m_{1}=-m_{2}
. To conclude the proof, we show that this is impossible.

Since
P
and
Q
are symmetric in origin,
-u\in P\cap Q
if
u\in P\cap Q
. Then the sides of
P
and
Q
containing
-u
have directions
\ell_{1},m_{1}
, respectively. Hence,
u,v,-u
belong to the pairwise parallel sides of
P
and
Q
. As a convex polygon cannot have three distinct parallel sides, then
u
and
-v
belong to the same sides of
P
and
Q
. Therefore, the side of
P
that is adjacent to
-u
and parallel to
\ell_{1}
should be the
same
as the side of
P
that is adjacent to
v
and parallel to
\ell_{1}
. Thus,
-u
lies on the straight line that passes through
v
and is parallel to
\ell_{1}
. Similarly,
-u
lies on the straight line that passes through
v
and is parallel to
m_{1}
. Since
\ell_{1}
is not parallel to
m_{1}
, this gives
v=-u
. This is not possible and implies
P=Q
.
∎

Note that the above proof relies on the fact that a supporting line
H(\xi)
that defines a slab and contains a common point of
P
and
Q
corresponds to an analytical point
\xi
of
h_{M}
.

Corollary 3.0.1
.

Theorem
1.2
still holds if
h_{M}(\xi)
is piecewise analytic on
S^{1}
and analytic for
\xi
such that
H(\xi)
contains a common point of
P
and
Q
.
