On Positivity of the Limit F F -signature
Yuchen Liu, Suchitra Pande

Abstract
. We study a conjecture of Carvajal-Rojas, Schwede and Tucker which states that for a complex KLT singularity ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}) , the F F -signatures of the reductions of R R to characteristic p ≫ 0 p\gg 0 remain bounded away from zero as p → ∞ p\to\infty . We prove that this conjecture holds for three-dimensional non-weakly exceptional singularities by an inductive argument. We also prove that the conjecture holds for smooth hypersurfaces of very low degree by constructing isotrivial normal toric degenerations. By considering the version of this conjecture for the Frobenius-alpha invariant, our techniques are inspired by K-stability theory and involve using degenerations and birational geometry.



1.
Introduction

The
F
-signature
is a numerical invariant of singularities of local rings in positive characteristics that measures the
asymptotic
flatness of the
Frobenius
morphism. For an
F
-finite local ring
R
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(meaning that the Frobenius map on
\Spec(R)
is finite), the
F
-signature
{s}(R)
is a real number between
0
and
1
; it equals
1
exactly when
R
is a regular ring
[
31
]
,
[
66
]
. This latter fact is a strengthening of the classical theorem of Kunz that characterizes regular rings via the flatness of the Frobenius map
[
36
]
. Similarly, the
F
-signature is positive exactly when
R
has only
strongly
F
-regular
singularities
[
1
]
, a mild class of singularities that implies that
R
is normal and Cohen-Macaulay
[
30
]
. The numerical measurement encoded by the
F
-signature has found important applications, notably to bounding the local fundamental groups and torsion in divisor class groups of singularities
[
14
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
47
]
.

Although strongly
F
-regular singularities are defined via splittings of the Frobenius map of
R
, they turn out to be deeply connected to a class of complex singularities arising in the minimal model program, namely,
Kawamata log terminal (KLT)
singularities. Indeed, it follows from the works
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
62
]
that a normal,
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein ring
R
of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
is KLT if and only if the reduction of
R
to characteristic
p\gg 0
is strongly
F
-regular for all
p\gg 0
. Since the
F
-signature is positive for strongly
F
-regular rings, this suggests a connection between the properties of a KLT singularity
R
over
\mathbb{C}
and the
F
-signature of its reductions to positive characteristics. In this direction, Carvajal-Rojas, Schwede and Tucker made the following conjecture in
[
14
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a KLT singularity that is essentially of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. For any prime
p\gg 0
, let
{s}_{p}(R)
denote the
F
-signature of the reduction of
R
to characteristic
p
(see
3.3
for a more precise formulation). Then, there is a constant
C>0
(independent of
p
) such that for all
p\gg 0
, we have
{s}_{p}(R)>C
. In other words, we have

\liminf_{p\to\infty}{s}_{p}(R)>0.

This conjecture has been established in the following cases: toric singularities
[
58
]
,
[
67
]
, finite quotient singularities
[
31
]
, quadric hypersurfaces
[
65
]
, diagonal hypersurfaces
[
13
]
, full flag varieties
[
49
]
and reductive quotient singularities
[
61
]
. In this paper, we develop several tools to address this conjecture and apply them to prove it in many new cases. Our first observation is that
1.1
for KLT singularities can be reduced to a similar conjecture about the
Frobenius-alpha invariant
for log Fano pairs (
2.18
):

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
(
2.3
). There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
p\gg 0
and reductions
(X_{p},\Delta_{p})
, we have
\alpha_{F}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\geq C
.

Relating the two conjectures, we prove:

Theorem 1.3
.

(
3.10
,
3.7
)

1.1
holds for all KLT pairs
(R,\Delta)
of dimension
n\geq 2
if and only if
1.2
holds for log Fano pairs in dimension
n-1
.

This result was also independently obtained by Takagi and Yamaguchi in
[
61
]
. The Frobenius-alpha invariant of a log Fano pair in positive characteristics was introduced in
[
49
]
as an analog of Tian’s alpha-invariant from complex geometry. Thus, it is more amenable to tools involving degenerations and birational geometry, which we exploit. We also note that
1.2
for a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X
is equivalent to
1.1
for the
cone over
X
with respect to the embedding defined by any
-rK_{X}
(for any
r>0
).

Furthermore, we establish a partial inductive approach to proving
1.1
. For a projective log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
, we let
\alpha(X,\Delta)
denote Tian’s alpha-invariant, or equivalently, the global log canonical threshold (
2.4
).

Theorem 1.4

(
6.1
)
.

Suppose
1.2
is true for all complex log Fano pairs of dimension
n\geq 1
. Then,
1.2
also holds for all log Fano pairs
(X,\Delta)
of dimension
n+1
with
\alpha(X,\Delta)<1
.

We now discuss our results in more detail.

1.1.
Threefold singularities.

We apply
1.4
to complex threefold KLT singularities that are not
weakly exceptional
. A weakly-exceptional singularity is defined as a KLT singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
that admits a unique PLT blow-up; see
[
55
]
. Weakly exceptional singularities are local versions of log Fano pairs
(S,\Delta_{S})
such that
\alpha(S,\Delta_{S})>1
.

1.4
and
1.3
together reduce
1.1
for non-weakly exceptional threefold singularities to the case of pairs
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\Delta)
where
\Delta
has at most three components (see
6.1
). The latter has been established in
[
12
]
and thus we obtain:

Theorem 1.5
.

1.1
holds for all non-weakly exceptional threefold KLT singularities
(R,\mathfrak{m})
over
\mathbb{C}
. Similarly,
1.2
holds for all log del Pezzo surfaces
S
satisfying
\alpha(S)<1
.

This theorem covers the case of smooth del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least
2
and is new for all the non-toric and non-diagonal ones. In forthcoming work, we use results of Monsky
[
48
]
to extend this result to all non-weakly exceptional pairs
x\in(X,\Delta)
in dimension
3
. We also obtain uniform versions of
1.1
for non-weakly exceptional threefold singularities by comparing the
F
-signature to the
normalized volume
(
7.1
) defined by Li
[
41
]
. See Section
7
for the details.

Our techniques also allow us to prove an interesting result about
F
-regularity (resp.
+
-regularity) of non-weakly exceptional three-dimensional singularities in positive and mixed characteristics when the residue characteristic is larger than
5
. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.6
.

(
5.14
)
Let
X=\Spec(R)
be a three-dimensional KLT singularity over a perfect field

of characteristic

(resp. a complete DVR

with residue field

). Assume that

is non-weakly exceptional (

). Then,

is
F
-regular (resp.

/BCM-regular).

By the examples constructed in
[
18
]
, it is known that the above theorem fails without the non-weakly exceptional assumption.

1.2.
Toric degenerations.

We also consider another method for proving
1.2
which involves using an
isotrivial degeneration
of a Fano variety
X
to a
normal toric variety

X_{0}
(see
3.13
). An isotrivial degeneration
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to C
over a pointed curve
0\in C
has the special property that all fibers of
\pi
over the punctured curve
C\setminus\{0\}
are isomorphic to
X
. Combined with the semi-continuity properties of the
F
-signature or the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant, this provides the required control while reducing modulo
p\gg 0
:

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose a complex
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X
admits an isotrivial degeneration
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to C
to a normal toric variety
X_{0}
over a pointed curve
0\in C
. Assume that
-K_{\mathcal{X}/C}
is
\pi
-ample. Then,
1.2
holds for
X
.

This theorem readily implies
1.2
for spherical
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano varieties over
\mathbb{C}
(
3.18
) thanks to the normal toric degenerations constructed by
[
2
]
. This includes Grassmannians and partial flag varieties. Furthermore, we construct new isotrivial degenerations to normal toric varieties for smooth low-degree hypersurfaces:

Theorem 1.8

(
4.1
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
n\geq\binom{2d-1}{d}+d-1
. Then there is an isotrivial degeneration of
X
to
X_{0}
(over some pointed curve) such that
X_{0}
is isomorphic to the hypersurface defined by
(x_{0}^{d}=x_{1}\dots x_{d})
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Thus,
1.2
holds for all such

.

1.3.
Proof strategies

The strategy for proving
1.4
is to use the theory of
F
-adjunction (
[
57
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
), along with the techniques from the minimal model program. The key idea is that the condition
\alpha(X,\Delta)<1
on a log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
implies the existence of a
special divisor
over
(X,\Delta)
(
[
10
]
), which by a result of Zhuang, provides a lower-dimensional log Fano pair
(E,\Delta_{E})
on a birational model of
X
. Then, the proof proceeds by a careful reduction modulo
p
argument and lifting
F
-splittings from
(E,\Delta_{E})
. See Section
6
for the details.

The same idea also allows us to show
1.6
, where we deduce global
F
-regularity from the results of Watanabe
[
68
]
which deal with the case of pairs on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. This is discussed in Section
5
.

Constructing
normal
toric degenerations as in
1.8
involves exploiting the projectivity of appropriate moduli spaces of degree
d
hypersurfaces along with a result of Starr from
[
60
]
. Starr shows that when the degree of a smooth hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is very low relative to
n
, then any general hypersurface of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{d}
can be realized as a linear section of
X
(up to isomorphism). Since
X
degenerates to the cone over any of its linear sections,
1.8
follows from a limiting argument. In Section
4
, we provide two different proofs using the GIT and K-moduli spaces respectively.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Singularities of MMP and Negativity Lemma

We recall the relevant notions of singularities of the MMP. Throughout this subsection a pair
(X,\Delta)
will refer to a normal variety over an algebraically closed field
k
and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that some multiple of
K_{X}+\Delta
is Cartier, where
K_{X}
denotes a canonical divisor of
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

A divisor
E

over

X
refers to some prime divisor
E\subset Y\xrightarrow{\pi}X
, where
Y
is a normal variety over
k
and
\pi:Y\to X
is a projective, birational morphism to
X
. The
log discrepancy
of such a divisor
E
with respect to the pair
(X,\Delta)
is a rational number defined by

A_{(X,\Delta)}(E)=\ord_{E}(K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+E-\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta_{X}))

where
\Delta_{Y}
is the strict transform of
\Delta
and
K_{Y}
is a canonical divisor of
Y
that is compatible with
K_{X}
on the locus where
\pi
is an isomorphism.

We use log discrepancies to define the following class of singularities.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair
(X,\Delta)
is defined to be

(1)

Kawamata log terminal (KLT)
if
A_{(X,\Delta)}(E)>0
for every divisor
E
over
X
.

(2)

log canonical (LC)
if for every divisor
E
over
X
, we have
A_{(X,\Delta)}(E)\geq 0
. Any divisor
E
over
X
with
A_{(X,\Delta)}(E)=0
is called an
LC-place
of
(X,\Delta)
.

(3)

purely log terminal (PLT)
if
(X,\Delta)
is log canonical,
S=\lfloor\Delta\rfloor
is a prime divisor on
X
and
S
is the only LC-place of
(X,\Delta)
.

We also have the following global versions for projective varieties:

Definition 2.3
.

A pair
(X,\Delta)
is called a
log Fano
pair if:

•

X
is a normal, projective variety,

•

(X,\Delta)
is KLT and

•

the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
-K_{X}-\Delta
is ample.

A projective variety
X
is said to be of
Fano type
if there exists some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
such that
(X,\Delta)
is a log Fano pair.

The following numerical invariants of singularities will play a central role in this paper:

Definition 2.4
.

Given a log canonical pair
(X,\Delta)
and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D\geq 0
on
X
, the
log canonical threshold
of
D
with respect to
(X,\Delta)
is defined to be

\lct(X,\Delta;D):=\sup\{t\geq 0\,|\,(X,\Delta+tD)\,\text{is log canonical}\}.

Globally, given a log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
, the
\alpha
-invariant
or the
global log canonical threshold
of
(X,\Delta)
is defined to be the infimum:

\alpha(X,\Delta):=\inf\{\lct(X,\Delta;D)\ |\ \text{$D\geq 0$ is any $\mathbb{Q}$-divisor with}\,-(K_{X}+\Delta)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}D\}.

We will also repeatedly use the negativity lemma, which holds over algebraically closed fields of arbitrary characteristic:

Lemma 2.5
.

[
6
, Section 2.3]
Let
f:Y\to X
be a projective, birational morphism between normal projective varieties over
k
and suppose we have an
f
-exceptional,
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
E
on
Y
. If
E
is
f
-nef, then
E\leq 0
. In particular, if
E
is numerically trivial over
X
, then
E=0
.

As a consequence, we have:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
be normal projective varieties over an algebraically closed field
k
, and
f:Y\to Y^{\prime}
be a birational contraction. Let
\Delta\geq 0
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
and let
\Delta^{\prime}=f_{*}\Delta
be the corresponding divisor on
Y^{\prime}
. Assume that
K_{Y}+\Delta
and
K_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
are both
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Then, we have

(1)

If
K_{Y}+\Delta\sim 0
, then
K_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}\sim 0
as well. Moreover, in this case, if
(Y,\Delta)
is KLT then so is
(Y^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
.

(2)

Assume that
K_{Y}+\Delta
is nef over
Y^{\prime}
. Then,

(2.1)

K_{Y}+\Delta\leq f^{*}(K_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}).

In this case, if
(Y^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is KLT, then so is
(Y,\Delta)
.

Proof.

Write

(2.2)

K_{Y}+\Delta+E=f^{*}(K_{Y^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})

for some
f
-exceptional divisor
E
on
Y
. Moreover, let
\mu:Z\to Y
be a log resolution of
(Y,\Delta+E)
. Then, note that for any prime divisor
F\subset Z
, we have
A_{Y,\Delta+E}(F)=A_{Y^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}}(F)
.

(1)

We now apply the negativity lemma for
f
. Thus, if
K_{Y}+\Delta\sim 0
,
E
is numerically trivial over
Y^{\prime}
and hence must be zero. This proves part (1).

(2)

For part (2), note that if
K_{Y}+\Delta
is nef over
Y^{\prime}
, then
-E
is nef over
Y^{\prime}
which means that

must be effective. Since

is effective, we also have
A_{Y,\Delta}(F)\geq A_{Y,\Delta+E}(F)=A_{Y^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}}(F)
for any prime divisor
F\subset Z
. This proves
(2)
. ∎

2.2.
F
-signature

For any prime number

, let

be a ring of characteristic

r\cdot F_{*}^{e}s:=F_{*}^{e}(r^{p^{e}}s)\textrm{\quad for any $r\in R$ and $F_{*}^{e}s\in F_{*}^{e}R$}.

Now let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
denote a normal local ring of Krull dimension
d>0
and
X
denote the normal scheme
\Spec(R)
. Throughout, we will assume that

is the localization of a finitely generated
k
-algebra at a maximal ideal, which also makes it

-finite (i.e.,
F_{*}^{e}R
is a finitely generated
R
-module for any

), with the rank of

over
R
being

. Let
\Delta
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

. Since

R\subset R(\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\rceil)

. Thus, applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\,\textunderscore\,,\,R)

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}\big(F_{*}^{e}R(\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\rceil),R\big)\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(F_{*}^{e}R,R).

Thus, given any element
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}\big(F_{*}^{e}R(\lceil(p^{e}-1)\Delta\rceil),R\big)

-free rank

.

[

Let

3.
Lim-inf

-signature

In this section, we will define the lim-inf
F
-signature of a complex KLT singularity and formulate the precise versions of the conjecture on the positivity of the lim-inf
F
-signature. We will focus on the case of log Fano pairs and show that the global case implies the local case of KLT singularities (

3.1.
Definition of the lim-inf
F
-signature

Definition 3.1

(Reduction to characteristic
p\gg 0
of a log Fano pair)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. A
family of characteristic
p\gg 0
models
of
(X,\Delta)
, denoted by
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})\to\Spec(A)
, consists of the following data:

(1)

A finitely generated smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
A\subset\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

A normal projective scheme
X_{A}
, that is flat over
\Spec(A)
and such that the fiber
X_{A}\times_{A}\Spec(\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to
X
. Let
\phi:X_{A}\times_{A}\mathbb{C}\to X
be an isomorphism.

(3)

An effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta_{A}
on
X_{A}
that is flat over
A
and such that we have
\phi(\Delta_{A}\times_{A}\mathbb{C})=\Delta
. We also assume that
K_{X_{A}}+\Delta_{A}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.

(4)

Assume that
-(K_{X_{A}}+\Delta_{A})
is ample over
\Spec(A)
.

Given a family of characteristic
p\gg 0
models of
(X,\Delta)
, for any prime
p\gg 0
, the
reduction to characteristic
p
of
(X,\Delta)
refers to a fiber

(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}):=(X_{A}\times_{A}\Spec(A/\mathfrak{m}),\Delta_{A}\times_{A}\Spec(A/\mathfrak{m})),

where
\mathfrak{m}\subset A
is any maximal ideal whose residue characteristic is
p
. Recall that in that case
A/\mathfrak{m}
is a finite field of characteristic
p
.

Remark 3.2
.

Given
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})
, a family of characteristic
p\gg 0
models of a log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
, we may assume, using
[
56
, Theorem 5.1]
that (after possibly replacing
A
by a finitely generated extension) for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}\subset A
, the fiber
(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})
is a globally
F
-regular log Fano pair. In particular, we may assume that all fibers of
X_{A}\to\Spec(A)
are geometrically normal.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
and let
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})\to\Spec(A)
be a family of characteristic
p\gg 0
models of
(X,\Delta)
. Note that if the ring
A
is not a one dimensional ring, then the reduction to characteristic
p\gg 0
of
(X,\Delta)
over
A
is not necessarily uniquely defined. Rather, for all prime numbers
p\gg 0
, we get a family of globally
F
-regular log-Fano pairs
(X_{p},\Delta_{p}):=(X_{A}\times_{A}\Spec{(A/pA)},\Delta|_{X_{p}})
over
\Spec(A/pA)
where
\Delta_{p}
denotes the restriction of
\Delta
to the normal subscheme
X_{p}
. For any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
A
that lies over
p
, we call the fiber
X_{\kappa(\mathfrak{m})}=X\times_{A}\Spec{(A/\mathfrak{m})}
a
characteristic
p

of
X
.

Definition 3.3

(The lim-inf
F
-signature and
\alpha_{F}
-invariant of complex log Fano pairs)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider a family of characteristic
p
models
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})\to\Spec(A)
of
(X,\Delta)
. Then, for each prime number
p\gg 0
,

•

we define the
F
-signature in characteristic
p
, denoted by
{s}_{p}(X,\Delta)
as

{s}_{p}(X,\Delta)\,:=\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A/pA\,\text{maximal}}{s}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}).

And we define the
lim-inf
F
-signature
of
(X,\Delta)
to be:

{s}_{-}(X,\Delta)=\liminf_{p\to\infty}{s}_{p}(X,\Delta).

•

Similarly, we define the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant in characteristic
p
, denoted by
\alpha_{p}(X,\Delta)
as

\alpha_{p}(X,\Delta)\,:=\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A/pA\,\text{maximal}}\alpha_{F}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}).

And we define the
lim-inf
\alpha_{F}
-invariant
of
(X,\Delta)
to be:

\alpha_{-}(X,\Delta)=\liminf_{p\to\infty}\alpha_{p}(X,\Delta).

Note that by definition, the lim-inf
F
-signature and the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant are both computed using a chosen family of characteristic
p
-models of
(X,\Delta)
. We now check that these numerical invariants are indeed independent of the models chosen.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider two families of characteristic
p
models
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})\to\Spec(A)
and
(X_{B},\Delta_{B})\to\Spec(B)
of
(X,\Delta)
over finitely generated smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras
A
and
B
. Then, for any prime
p\gg 0
,

(1)

we have

\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A/pA\,\text{maximal}}{s}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})=\sup_{\mathfrak{n}\subset B/pB\,\text{maximal}}{s}(X_{\mathfrak{n}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{n}}).

(2)

Similarly,

\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A/pA\,\text{maximal}}\alpha_{F}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})=\sup_{\mathfrak{n}\subset B/pB\,\text{maximal}}\alpha_{F}(X_{\mathfrak{n}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{n}}).

Proof.

By finding a common enlargement for
A
and
B
if necessary, it is sufficient to prove the lemma when
A
is a subring of
B
. Furthermore, by
[
59
,
Tag 00TF
]
and generic smoothness (applied over
\text{Frac}(A)
, which is a field of characteristic zero), there exist localizations
A_{f}
and
B_{g}
such that
A_{f}\subset B_{g}
and the induced map
\Spec(B_{g})\to\Spec(A_{f})
is a smooth map of schemes. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the lemma when
\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)
is a dominant and smooth map of smooth schemes over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Fix a prime
p\gg 0
such that the induced map
\Spec(B/pB)\to\Spec(A/pA)
is dominant and smooth. Let
\Spec(A_{p})
and
\Spec(B_{p})
be fixed connected components of
\Spec(A/pA)
and
\Spec(B/pB)
respectively, such that there is an induced dominant map
\Spec(B_{p})\to\Spec(A_{p})
. Note that
A_{p}
and
B_{p}
are regular domains of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Moreover, we may assume that the map between the fraction fields

K:=\text{Frac}(A_{p})\subset L:=\text{Frac}(B_{p})

is separable.

(1)

First we claim that

(3.1)

\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A_{p}\,\text{maximal}}{s}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})={s}(X_{K},\Delta_{K})={s}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}}),

and similarly,

(3.2)

\sup_{\mathfrak{n}\subset B_{p}\,\text{maximal}}{s}(X_{\mathfrak{n}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{n}})={s}(X_{L},\Delta_{L})={s}(X_{L^{\infty}},\Delta_{L^{\infty}}).

By symmetry, it is enough to prove the claim for
A_{p}
. To see this, note that since
A_{p}
is regular, each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset A_{p}
is a complete intersection (i.e., generated by a regular sequence). Thus, by
[
64
, Corollary 6.1]
, for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset A_{p}
, we have
{s}(X_{B_{\mathfrak{m}}},\Delta_{B_{\mathfrak{m}}})\geq{s}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})
. Moreover, by the lower semicontinuity of the
F
-signature
[
53
, Theorem 5.7]
, we have
{s}(X_{K},\Delta_{K})\geq{s}(X_{B_{\mathfrak{m}}},\Delta_{B_{\mathfrak{m}}})
. Lastly,
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
implies that

{s}(X_{K},\Delta_{K})={s}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})

since the induced map on the section rings of
X_{K^{\infty}}
and
X_{K}
has a regular fiber (isomorphic to
\Spec(K^{\infty})
).
This shows that the RHS is at least the LHS in
Equation

3.1
and
Equation

3.2
.

For the reverse inequality, we use
[
15
, Theorem 4.10]
to see that for any
0<\lambda<{s}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})
, there exists a dense open set
U_{\lambda}\subset\Spec(A_{p})
such that for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in U_{\lambda}
, we have
{s}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})>\lambda
. Then, the reverse inequality follows by taking
\lambda\to{s}(X_{L^{\infty}},\Delta_{L^{\infty}}).
This completes the proof of the claim.

Now, to prove Part (1) of the lemma using the above claim, it suffices to show that
{s}(X_{K},\Delta_{K})={s}(X_{L},\Delta_{L})
. But this follows again from
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
.

(2)

Arguing as in the proof of Part (1), we first show that

(3.3)

\alpha_{F}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})=\sup_{\mathfrak{m}\subset A_{p}\,\text{maximal}}\alpha_{F}(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}).

First suppose that
\alpha_{F}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})=0
. Then, by
[
49
, Theorem 3.10]
,
(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})
is not globally
F
-regular. By
Equation

3.1
, we know that
(X_{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}})
is not globally
F
-regular for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\subset A_{p}
. Therefore, by
[
49
, Theorem 3.10]
again, the LHS of
Equation

3.3
is zero as well. Therefore, we may assume that the locus

U=\{y\in\Spec(A_{p})\,|\,(X_{y^{\infty}},\Delta_{y^{\infty}})\,\text{is globally $F$-regular}\}

is non-empty. But this locus is also open in
\Spec(A_{p})
. Therefore, it is sufficient to prove
Equation

3.3
when
U=\Spec(A_{p})
. This follows immediately from the lower semi-continuity of the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant proved in
[
50
, Theorem V.2.1]
.

Finally, we use
[
49
, Corollary 4.14]
to conclude that

\alpha_{F}(X_{K^{\infty}},\Delta_{K^{\infty}})=\alpha_{F}(X_{L^{\infty}},\Delta_{L^{\infty}}).

This completes the proof of the lemma. ∎

3.2.
Statements of the main conjectures.

We now formulate two precise versions of the question first raised by Carvajal-Rojas, Schwede and Tucker
[
14
, Question 5.9]
using the lim-inf
F
-signature and the lim-inf
\alpha_{F}
-invariant introduced above (
3.3
):

Conjecture 3.5
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a KLT log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Then, both
{s}_{-}(X,\Delta)
and
\alpha_{-}(X,\Delta)
are positive.

There is a natural strengthening of the above conjecture that takes into account the
F
-signature of the family of characteristic
p
reductions of
(X,\Delta)
.

Conjecture 3.6
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a KLT log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose
(X_{A},\Delta_{A})
is a family of characteristic
p\gg 0
models of
(X,\Delta)
. Then, there are positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
and a dense open set
U\subset\Spec(A)
such that for any closed point
y\in U
, we have

{s}(X_{y},\Delta_{y})>C_{1},

and similarly,

\alpha_{F}(X_{y},\Delta_{y})>C_{2}.

Note that the characteristic of the residue field (and hence the meaning of the
F
-signature and the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant) depends on the closed point
y
.

Now, we observe that in both
3.5
and
3.6
, the version for the
F
-signature is equivalent to the version for the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant. This follows immediately from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a log Fano pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Set
d=\dim(X)
and
V=\vol(-K_{X}-\Delta)
. Then there exist positive real-valued functions
f
and
g
depending only on
d
and
V
such that
for any
p\gg 0
and
(X_{p},\Delta_{p})
a characteristic
p
-model of
(X,\Delta)
, we have

(1)

If
{s}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\geq C
then
\alpha_{F}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\geq f(C)
.

(2)

And conversely, if
\alpha_{F}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\geq C
then
{s}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\geq g(C)
.

Proof.

For a log Fano pair
(X_{p},\Delta_{p})
over a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
, let
{s}_{p}
and
\alpha_{p}
denote the
F
-signature and the
\alpha_{F}
-invariant of
(X_{p},\Delta_{p})
respectively. Then, following
[
49
, Theorem 4.7]
we have:

\frac{2\,\alpha_{p}^{d+1}\,V}{(d+1)!}\leq{s}_{p}\leq\frac{2\,\big((\frac{1}{2})^{d+1}-(\frac{1}{2}-\alpha_{p})^{d+1}\big)\,V}{(d+1)!}.

Note that here we have used the fact that the dimension of
X_{p}
is also
d
and the volume of
-K_{X_{p}}-\Delta_{p}
is
V
. Thus, we may take

g(C)=\frac{2VC^{d+1}}{(d+1)!}

and

f(C)=\frac{1}{2}-\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d+1}-\frac{C(d+1)!}{2V}\right)^{1/d+1}.

Note that the function
f(C)
makes sense for all
0\leq C\leq\frac{V}{2^{d}\,(d+1)!}
. This is sufficient because by
[
49
, Corollary 4.8]
, we always have
{s}_{p}(X_{p},\Delta_{p})\leq\frac{V}{2^{d}(d+1)!}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

.

Three-point configurations on

3.3.
Local to global reduction.

Now we explain how
3.5
and
3.6
for log Fano pairs imply the corresponding versions for arbitrary KLT singularities
(X,\Delta,x)
. For this, we recall the construction of degeneration to the orbifold cone over a Kollár component:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a closed point, where
(X,\Delta)
is a KLT pair over
\mathbb{C}
. Then, a proper birational map
\pi:Y\to X
provides a Kollár component
S
of
(X,\Delta)
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

S\subset Y
is the unique, prime
\pi
-exceptional divisor on
Y
and
\pi(S)=\{x\}
.

(2)

\pi
is an isomorphism over
X\setminus\{x\}
.

(3)

The pair
(Y,S+\Delta_{Y})
is PLT, where
\Delta_{Y}
denotes the strict transform of
\Delta
on
Y
(
2.2
).

(4)

-S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and ample over
X
.

Now assume that
X=\Spec(R)
is an affine variety. Given a Kollár component
S
of
(X,\Delta)
, the pair
(S,\Delta_{S})
is a projective, log Fano pair of one dimension less than the dimension of
(X,\Delta)
. Here
\Delta_{S}
is defined by the adjunction formula

(K_{Y}+S+\Delta_{Y})|_{S}=K_{S}+\Delta_{S}.

Furthermore, let
v
denote the valuation centered at
x
defined by the prime divisor
S
, and let
\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v)=\{f\in R\,|\,v(f)\geq m\}
be the
m^{\text{th}}
-valuation ideal. Following
[
40
, Section 2.4]
(also see
[
45
, Section 2.4]
), let
R_{*}
denote the associated graded ring

R_{*}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathfrak{a}_{m}(v)/\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}(v).

Then, there is a natural effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta_{C}
on
C=\Spec(R_{*})
so that
(C,\Delta_{C})
is a KLT pair. In particular,
R_{*}
is a Noetherian normal domain. Moreover, if
d
is a positive integer such that
dS
is Cartier and
-d(K_{Y}+S+\Delta_{Y})
is Cartier, then there is an associated degree
d
finite map
h:C\to C_{d}=C(S;-d(K_{S}+\Delta_{S}))
such that

(3.4)

h^{*}(K_{C_{d}}+\Delta_{C_{d}})=K_{C}+\Delta_{C}.

Here,
C_{d}:=C(S;-d(K_{S}+\Delta_{S}))
denotes the affine cone over
S
with respect to the ample divisor
-d(K_{S}+\Delta_{S})
and
\Delta_{C_{d}}
denotes the cone over
\Delta_{S}
. Thus, we say that
(C,\Delta_{C})
is the
orbifold cone
over the log Fano pair
(S,\Delta_{S})
.

Again assuming
X=\Spec(R)
is affine, any Kollár component
S
of the KLT pair
(X,\Delta,x)
defines a degeneration of
(X,\Delta)
to the orbifold cone
C=\Spec(R_{*})
. Following
[
40
, Section 4.1]
, this is obtained by considering the extended Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}
which is the graded subring
\mathcal{R}\subset R[t,t^{-1}]
defined as

\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{a}_{k}(v)t^{-k}.

Then let
\mathcal{X}=\Spec(\mathcal{R})
denote the total space and we have the following properties for
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{X}
:

(1)

The map
\varphi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
corresponding to the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[t]\subset\mathcal{R}
is flat.

(2)

We have
\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[t]}\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\cong R[t,t^{-1}]
. In other words, the restriction
\mathcal{X}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}}
is just the product
X\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\})
.

(3)

\mathcal{R}/t\mathcal{R}\cong R_{*}
, i.e., the Cartier divisor defined by
t
is isomorphic to
C=\Spec(R_{*})
.

(4)

If
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the maximal ideal of
R
corresponding to
x
, the ideal

\mathfrak{p}=\bigoplus_{k>0}\mathfrak{a}_{k}(v)t^{-k}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}t^{k}

defines a section
i:\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathcal{X}
such that
\varphi\circ i=\text{id}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
. Thus, under this degeneration of
X
to
C
, the point
x
degenerates to the vertex of
C
defined by the homogeneous maximal ideal of
R_{*}
.

(5)

Let
K_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the canonical divisor of
\mathcal{X}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
denote the closure of the pull-back of
\Delta
in
\mathcal{X}
. Then
K_{\mathcal{X}}+\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and we have

(K_{\mathcal{X}}+\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})|_{C}=K_{C}+\Delta_{C},

where
\Delta_{C}
is as defined above and satisfies
Equation

3.4
.

Using this construction, we prove the following theorem:

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a KLT pair over
\mathbb{C}
, and
x\in X
be a closed point. Suppose
(S,\Delta_{S})
is a Kollár component of
(X,\Delta)
(
3.9
). Assume that
3.6
holds for the pair
(S,\Delta_{S})
, then for any model
(X_{A},\Delta_{A},x_{A})
of
(X,\Delta,x)
over a finitely generated smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra
A
, there exists a dense open set
U\subset\Spec(A)
and a positive constant
C>0
such that we have

{s}(X_{y},\Delta_{y},x_{y})\geq C

for all closed points
y\in U
.

Proof.

By enlarging
A
if necessary, we may assume that we also have a model for
Y,\pi,S,\Delta_{S},C
and
\Delta_{C}
over
A
. Moreover, we may also assume that properties (1) to (5) listed above hold over each closed point of
\Spec(A).

In this situation, by our assumption there exists a constant
C>0
and a dense open set
U\subset\Spec(A)
such that for each closed point
y\in U
, we have

{s}(S_{y},\Delta_{S_{y}})\geq C
. Therefore, to prove the theorem it is sufficient to justify that for each closed point
y\in U
, we have
{s}(X_{y},\Delta_{y},x_{y})\geq A_{X,\Delta}(S)\ {s}(S_{y},\Delta_{S_{y}})
.

To see this, we first note that applying
[
64
, Corollary 1.2]
, we have
{s}(\mathcal{X}_{y},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_{y}},\mathfrak{n})\geq{s}(C_{y},\Delta_{C_{y}},\mathfrak{m}_{0,y})
. Here
\mathfrak{m}_{0,y}
denotes the closed point corresponding to the homogeneous maximal ideal of
R_{*,y}
, and
\mathfrak{n}
corresponds to the same point in
\mathcal{X}_{y}
. Then using
[
52
, Theorem 4.9]
, we have

{s}(\mathcal{X}_{y},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_{y}},\mathfrak{n})\leq{s}(\mathcal{X}_{y},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_{y}},\mathfrak{p}_{y})\leq{s}(\mathcal{X}_{y}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_{y}}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}},(\mathfrak{p}_{y},t^{-1}))

where
\mathfrak{p}_{y}
denotes the reduction of the ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\bigoplus_{k>0}\mathfrak{a}_{k}(v)t^{-k}\oplus\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}t^{k}
. But we note that
{s}(\mathcal{X}_{y}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}_{y}}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}},(\mathfrak{p}_{y},t^{-1}))
is just the
F
-signature
{s}(R_{y}\otimes_{k_{y}}k_{y}[t,t^{-1}],\Delta_{R_{y}},\mathfrak{m}_{y}[t,t^{-1}])
, which in turn is equal to
{s}(R_{y},\Delta_{R_{y}},\mathfrak{m}_{y})
by Theorem 3.6 of
[
15
]
. Thus, we have shown that
{s}(X_{y},\Delta_{X_{y}},x_{y})\geq{s}(C_{y},\Delta_{C_{y}},\mathfrak{m}_{0,y})
.

Now we will show that
{s}(C_{y},\Delta_{C_{y}},\mathfrak{m}_{0,y})
is equal to
A_{(X,\Delta)}(S)\ {s}(S_{y},\Delta_{S_{y}})
which will complete the proof. Note that by definition,
{s}(S_{y},\Delta_{S_{y}})=r\,{s}(\oplus_{j\geq 0}(H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(-rj(K_{S}+\Delta_{S})))),\Delta_{r})
for any sufficiently divisible
r
. But at the same time,
we have
C=\Spec(R_{*})\cong\Spec\oplus_{j\geq 0}H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}(\lfloor-jS|_{S}\rfloor))
by
[
45
, Proposition 2.10]
and we have

K_{S}+\Delta_{S}=(K_{X}+\Delta+S)|S\sim A_{X,\Delta}(S)S|S.

Therefore, choosing a
d
such that
r=\frac{d}{A_{X,\Delta}(S)}
is integral, we have the degree
r
map
h_{r}:C\to C_{r}
such that
h^{*}_{r}(K_{C_{r}}+\Delta_{C_{r}})=K_{C}+\Delta_{C}
. Then, applying
[
14
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have
{s}(C,\Delta_{C})=r\,{s}(C_{r},\Delta_{C_{r}})=A_{X,\Delta}(S)\,{s}(S,\Delta_{S})
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

In the above proof, we have shown the following useful statement that we record for future reference:

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
and suppose that we have a normal Kollár component
(S,\Delta_{S})
of a KLT singularity

. Then, we have

{s}(X,\Delta,x)\geq{A_{X,\Delta}(S)}\,{s}(S,\Delta_{S}).

.

was also independently obtained by Takagi and Yamaguchi in

]
.

3.4.
Isotrivial degenerations

We now prove that we may use isotrivial degenerations to prove
3.5
.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Suppose
C
is a smooth affine curve over
\mathbb{C}
and let
c
be a fixed point on
C
. Set
C^{\circ}=C\setminus\{c\}
. Then an
isotrivial degeneration
of
X
over
(c\in C)
to a normal projective variety
X_{0}
is given by a flat, projective map
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to C
, and a (
\pi
-)ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that:

(1)

\pi
is trivial over
C^{\circ}
:

\mathcal{X}^{\circ}:=\pi^{-1}(C^{\circ})=\mathcal{X}\times_{C}C^{\circ}\cong X\times_{\mathbb{C}}C^{\circ}.

(2)

the fiber over
c
is isomorphic to
X_{0}
:

\mathcal{X}_{c}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{C}\Spec(\kappa(c))\cong X_{0}.

(3)

The restriction (as a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor)
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
to
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
is linearly equivalent to
-K_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
, the anti-canonical divisor on
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Assume that
X
has an isotrivial degeneration to a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X_{0}
. Then, if either
3.5
or
3.6
is true for
X_{0}
, then the same is true for
X
. Moreover, we have
{s}_{-}(X)\geq{s}_{-}(X_{0})

and

\alpha_{-}(X)\geq\alpha_{-}(X_{0})
.

We first note some technical properties of isotrivial degenerations to normal varieties.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose we have an isotrivial degeneration
\pi:(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\to C
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Fano variety
X
to a normal projective variety
X_{0}
over a smooth, affine pointed curve
(c\in C)
. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{X}
is a normal variety over
\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

Both
\mathcal{X}
and
X_{0}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein with ample the anti-canonical divisor. Moreover, we have
\mathcal{L}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{\mathcal{X}|_{C}}
.

(3)

For any
m\geq 0
, every divisor
\mathcal{D}^{\circ}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
that is linearly equivalent to
m\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
extends uniquely to a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
\mathcal{D}
is linearly equivalent to
m\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{D}
does not vanish along
X_{0}
.

Proof.

First, we note that since
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
is isomorphic to the product
X\times_{\mathbb{C}}C^{\circ}
, we may shrink
C
to a neighbourhood of
c
to prove the theorem. Hence, without loss of generality, we may assume that the point
c
is a principal divisor on
C
. Therefore,
X_{0}
is a principal divisor on
\mathcal{X}
.

(1)

Since
X_{0}
is a principal divisor on
\mathcal{X}
and
X_{0}
is assumed to be normal it follows that
\mathcal{X}
is normal.

(2)

Since
\mathcal{X}
is normal, let
K_{\mathcal{X}}
denote a canonical Weil-divisor on
\mathcal{X}
. We claim that
\mathcal{L}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{\mathcal{X}}
. Since by assumption,
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-K_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
, taking closures in
\mathcal{X}
(as
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisors), we must have

-K_{\mathcal{X}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{L}+aX_{0},\quad\text{for some $a\in\mathbb{Q}$}

since
X_{0}
is a prime divisor. But the claim follows from the fact that
X_{0}
is actually principal. This shows that
\mathcal{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein and
-K_{\mathcal{X}}
is ample.

Next, let
U\subset X_{0}
denote the smooth locus of
X_{0}
, and
V\subset\mathcal{X}_{\text{sm}}
be an open set such that
V|_{X_{0}}=U
. Note that
U
is a principal divisor on
V
. Then, applying the adjunction formula, we have

K_{U}\sim K_{V}+U|_{U}\sim K_{V}|_{U}.

Taking closures and using the normality of
X_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}
, we get that

-K_{X_{0}}\sim-K_{\mathcal{X}}|_{X_{0}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{L}|_{X_{0}}.

This proves that
X_{0}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein as well, and since
\mathcal{L}
is ample on
\mathcal{X}
,
-K_{X_{0}}
is ample on
X_{0}
.

(3)

Given a divisor
\mathcal{D}^{\circ}
on
\mathcal{X}^{\circ}
, we may take its closure
\mathcal{D}
, which is a Weil-divisor on
\mathcal{X}
. Since
\mathcal{D}|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}\sim\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{X}^{\circ}}
, we must again have

\mathcal{D}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{L}+aX_{0},\quad\text{for some $a\in\mathbb{Q}$}.

Now the claim follows from the fact that
X_{0}
is a principal divisor. Note that this automatically makes
\mathcal{D}
a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor. ∎

Proof of
3.14
.

Given an isotrivial degeneration
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to C
of
X
to
X_{0}
over a pointed curve
(c\in C)
, we may choose models for
\mathcal{X}
,
X_{0}
,
\pi
,

and

over a smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra

. Furthermore, we may assume that the isomorphisms in the properties (1) - (3) in
3.13
hold over

and hence hold over every closed fiber over

. Thus, for any

, we may assume that we have an isotrivial degeneration of the characteristic

to the characteristic

and relies on the semicontinuity properties of the

∎

In this section, we work over
\mathbb{C}
. For notions regarding K-stability and K-moduli spaces, we refer to
[

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be an arbitrary smooth hypersurface of degree

\Phi:\mathbb{G}(k,n)\dashrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{k,d}
by taking linear sections of

with a

\mathbb{P}^{k}
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then, Starr in
[

showed that the map

is dominant if

, and we set

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
n\geq n_{0}
. Then there exists an isotrivial degeneration

(
3.13
) such that
X_{0}
is a toric hypersurface of degree

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with Gorenstein canonical singularities. Moreover,

has the equation

.

Combining this with
3.14
, we obtain:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a smooth hypersurface of degree
d
over
\mathbb{C}
. Assume that

3.6
holds for

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are two projective subvarieties of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with the same Hilbert polynomial. Then, there is an
embedded
isotrivial degeneration of
X_{1}\rightsquigarrow X_{2}
if and only if the orbit closure
\overline{\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}\cdot X_{1}}
of
X_{1}
intersects the
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}
-orbit of
X_{2}
in the corresponding Hilbert scheme.

Here, an embedded isotrivial degeneration is a family
\mathcal{X}\to C
as in
3.13
that arises from a subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times C\to C
.

Proof.

The proof relies on the existence and projectivity of the Hilbert scheme parametrizing subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
with a fixed Hilbert polynomial. Let
P
be the Hilbert polynomial of
X_{1}
and let
Z:=\overline{\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}\cdot[X_{1}]}\subset\Hilb_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(P)

under the natural
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}
-action on
\Hilb_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(P)
. First, suppose there is a flat subscheme

over some pointed curve
(0\in C)
that defines an isotrivial degeneration of
X_{1}\rightsquigarrow X_{2}
. Then, by considering the corresponding map
\pi:C\to\Hilb_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(P)
, we note that the image
\pi(C\setminus\{0\})
is contained in the orbit of

, while
\pi(0)
is contained in the orbit of
X_{2}
. Since
\Hilb_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(P)
is proper and the orbit closure

is a closed subscheme of
\Hilb_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(P)
,
\pi(0)
must also be contained in the subscheme

. Therefore,
S
intersects the orbit of
X_{2}
.

Conversely, suppose
z\in Z\cap(\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}\cdot X_{2})
. Then, since
Z
is a projective scheme, there is a map from a smooth curve
\pi:C\to Z
that joins
z
to some point of
\mathrm{PGL}_{n+1}\cdot X_{1}
in
Z
. Let
0\in C
be the point mapping to

. Since the orbit of
X_{1}
is dense in

, there must be an open set

that maps into the orbit of
X_{1}
. Now, since

is finite, we may replace

by

so that
\pi:C\to Z
now defines an embedded isotrivial degeneration of
X_{1}
to
X_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For subschemes

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose

with

. Let

be a linear section of

by a

For each fixed

Remark 4.7
.

We note that Ilten and Lautsch
[
32
]
prove that if
d\geq 2n-1
then a general hypersurface
X
admits a degeneration via a test configuration to a toric hypersurface. However, their construction of toric degeneration is in general not normal (hence not KLT).

5.
Non-weakly exceptional singularities

5.1.
Special divisors over non-weakly exceptional log del Pezzo surfaces.

First, we will study non-weakly exceptional log del Pezzo surfaces over
k
.

Definition 5.1
.

A log Fano pair
(X,\Delta)
over
k
is weakly exceptional if for every effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(K_{X}+\Delta)
, the pair
(X,\Delta+\Gamma)
is log canonical. In other words, we have
\alpha(X,\Delta)\geq 1
.

The following theorem on extracting special divisors over surfaces in characteristic
p>0
was obtained by Harold Blum and Joe Waldron and we thank them for pointing us to its proof. It is an analog of a theorem due to Zhuang in the complex case, and we include a proof for completeness.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(S,\Delta)
be a log del Pezzo surface over
k
and assume that
(S,\Delta)
is not weakly exceptional (i.e.,
\alpha(S,\Delta)<1
). Then, there exists a projective birational map
\mu:Z\to S
and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D^{*}
on
S
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

There is a unique prime divisor
E\subset Z
and
-E
is
\mu
-ample.

(2)

We have
D^{*}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(K_{S}+\Delta)
and
(S,\Delta+D^{*})
is log canonical.

(3)

E
is the unique log canonical place of
(S,\Delta+D^{*})
, so that the pair
(Z,E+\Delta_{Z}+D^{*}_{Z})
is PLT (where
\Delta_{Z},D_{Z}^{*}
denote the strict transforms of
\Delta,D^{*}
respectively).

(4)

The divisor
-K_{Z}-\Delta_{Z}-E
is big.

(5)

The surface
Z
is of Fano type, i.e., there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}\geq 0
on
Z
such that
-K_{Z}-\Delta^{\prime}
is ample and
(Z,\Delta^{\prime})
is KLT.

Proof.

Since
(S,\Delta)
is not weakly exceptional, we can find an effective divisor
D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(K_{S}+\Delta)
such that
(S,\Delta+tD)
is log canonical but not KLT for some
t\in(0,1)
. Let
E
denote an LC-place of
(S,\Delta+tD)
. Consider a projective log resolution
h:W\to S
of
(S,\Delta+D)
containing
E
and pick
0<\delta\ll 1
and write

(5.1)

K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}=h^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta+(t-\delta)D)+G_{2},

where
\Delta_{W},D_{W}
denote the strict transforms of
\Delta,D
respectively and
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are effective exceptional divisors such that: the support of
G_{1}
contains all divisors with log discrepancy over
(S,\Delta+tD)
less than
1
and
G_{2}
contains all divisors with log discrepancy larger than
1
. We may assume that
E
appears in
G_{1}
(since
E
is an LC-place) and that every exceptional divisor of
h
appears in either
G_{1}
or
G_{2}
.

Let
F
denote the reduced divisor containing the support of
G_{1}\setminus E
. It follows from
Equation

5.1
that if we pick
0<\varepsilon\ll\delta
then,
(W,\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}+\varepsilon F)
is KLT and moreover, since
\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}+\varepsilon F
is effective, it is big over
S
(since
h
is a projective, birational map). Therefore, by
[
63
, Theorem 6.5]
we may run a
K_{W}+\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}+\varepsilon F
- MMP over
S
which ends in a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal model
W\overset{g}{\to}Z\overset{\mu}{\to}S
such that
K_{Z}+g_{*}(\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}+\varepsilon F)
is nef over
S
. Using
Equation

5.1
again, we have

K_{Z}+g_{*}(\Delta_{W}+(t-\delta)D_{W}+G_{1}+\varepsilon F)=\mu^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta+(t-\delta)D)+g_{*}(G_{2}+\varepsilon F).

Since
G_{2}
and
F
are effective divisors, by the negativity lemma, we must have that
g_{*}(G_{2}+\varepsilon F)=0
, so that
g
contracts all
h
-exceptional divisors except
E
. Since we know that
E
is an LC-place of
(S,\Delta+tD)
, we have

(5.2)

K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+tD_{Z}+E=\mu^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta+tD),

and hence we know that
-E^{2}>0
, and hence
-E
is ample over
S
. This shows part (1). Moreover, since
-(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+E)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(1-t)\mu^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta)+tD_{Z}
,
we see that
-(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+E)
is big (since
-(K_{S}+\Delta_{S})
was big and
t\in(0,1)
), which proves part (4).

Next, set
m:=\text{ord}_{E}(D)
and note that for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, by
Equation

5.2
we have
K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+(t-\varepsilon)D_{Z}+(1-m\varepsilon)E=\mu^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta+(t-\varepsilon)D)
. Therefore,
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+(t-\varepsilon)D_{Z}+(1-m\varepsilon)E)
is KLT since
(S,\Delta+(t-\varepsilon)D)
is.
Moreover,
-(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+(t-\varepsilon)D_{Z}+(1-m\varepsilon)E)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(1-t+\varepsilon)\mu^{*}(K_{S}+\Delta)
is big and nef. Therefore, since
-E
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
\mu
-ample, we see that
-(K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}+(t-\varepsilon)D_{Z}+(1-m\varepsilon-\delta)E)
is ample for
0<\delta\ll 1
, which shows that
Z
is of Fano type (Part (5)).

Next, we run a
(-K_{Z}-\Delta_{Z}-E)
-MMP which terminates to
Z\to Z^{\rm m}
such that, if
E^{\rm m}
,
\Delta_{Y^{\rm m}}
denotes the strict transform of
E
and
\Delta_{Z}
on

, we have
-K_{Z^{\rm m}}-\Delta_{Z^{\rm m}}-E^{\rm m}
is big and nef. We can run such an MMP because

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial surface by
[
63

. Moreover, by
[
63
, Proposition 3.29]
,
-K_{Z^{\rm m}}-\Delta_{Z^{\rm m}}-E^{\rm m}
is big and semi-ample, and hence admits a birational ample model
\pi:Z\to Z^{\rm m}\to Z^{\prime}
. Moreover, we see that

is not contracted by

: first note that we may pick a rational number

and a general divisor

that avoids the singular points of

and the center of

, so that
(S,\Delta+(t-\lambda)D+H)
is KLT. Moreover, since

, we see that the crepant pull-backs of
(S,\Delta+(t-\lambda)D+H)
to

,

and

are all KLT as well. In particular, we see that
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
,
(Z^{\mathfrak{m}},\Delta_{Z^{\rm m}})

is contracted on

Let
\Delta_{Z^{\prime}},E^{\prime}
denote the strict transforms of

is dlt and since
