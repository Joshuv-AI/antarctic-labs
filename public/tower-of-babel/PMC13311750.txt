Impact of dexmedetomidine administration timing on postoperative delirium in elderly patients with lower limb fractures: a study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Meilan Chen, Yingmei Ren, Xuan Wu, Wei Yuan, Shijie Ji, Xiaolin Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative delirium (POD) is a prevalent perioperative complication among geriatric orthopaedic patients, leading to extended hospitalisation, escalated medical expenditures and persistent cognitive impairment. Although existing studies suggest that dexmedetomidine can reduce the risk of POD, the optimal timing of administration remains undetermined.Methods and analysisThis is a prospective, randomised controlled trial. Patients aged ≥65 years undergoing lower limb fracture surgery under neuraxial anaesthesia will be randomised into a preoperative administration group, an intraoperative administration group or a control group. The study employed a double-dummy technique, with all patients receiving an intravenous infusion at two time points: 15 min prior to neuraxial anaesthesia puncture (T1) and 15 min prior to skin incision (T2). The preoperative group will receive dexmedetomidine at T1 and placebo at T2; the intraoperative group will receive placebo at T1 and dexmedetomidine at T2 and the control group will receive placebo at both T1 and T2. The dexmedetomidine dose is 0.5 µg/kg (infused over ≥10 min), and the placebo is an equivalent volume of 0.9% saline. Background

The accelerating ageing of the global population has led to a consistent rise in the volume of geriatric patients undergoing surgery.1 Due to osteoporosis, decline in physical function and other factors, lower limb fractures are particularly common among elderly patients,2,4 and surgery is the primary treatment method.5 However, postoperative delirium (POD) represents a frequent and debilitating complication in these patients, with an incidence of up to 30%.6 POD not only increases the length of hospital stays but is also closely associated with long-term cognitive decline and mortality, imposing a substantial burden on healthcare systems.7

According to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition,8 POD is defined as an acute disturbance in attention and awareness accompanied by a change in cognition, which is reversible. Clinically, it can be classified into hyperactive, hypoactive or mixed subtypes, with the hypoactive form being particularly prevalent yet easily overlooked in geriatric patients.9 10 Established independent risk factors include advanced age (≥65 years), preoperative comorbidities, hypoalbuminaemia, pre-existing dementia and an American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status of III or higher.11 Despite the identification of these risk factors, there is currently a lack of effective and specific intervention strategies to minimise POD risk in this vulnerable population.

Current pathophysiological evidence suggests that neuroinflammation,12 neuronal injury13 and sleep–wake cycle disruption14 play pivotal roles in the development of POD. These pathological processes are often accompanied by elevated levels of brain injury biomarkers (eg, S100 calcium-binding protein B (S100β), neuron-specific enolase (NSE)) and inflammatory cytokines (eg, C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6)).15 Dexmedetomidine, a highly selective α₂-adrenergic receptor agonist, has demonstrated the potential to attenuate these pathological responses.16 17 Mechanistically, it activates presynaptic α₂-receptors to inhibit norepinephrine release for analgesia and postsynaptic receptors to inhibit sympathetic tone for sedation.18 Furthermore, it acts on the locus coeruleus to induce a state resembling physiological sleep (often termed ‘biomimetic sleep’). Accumulating evidence indicates that dexmedetomidine exerts protective effects on the brain, heart and kidneys19 20 and improves patient outcomes through its opioid-sparing21 and anti-inflammatory properties.22

However, some studies have also shown that intravenous infusion of dexmedetomidine can increase the incidence of hypotension and bradycardia. The occurrence of these haemodynamic adverse events may be closely related to the dosage and, crucially, the timing of administration.23 24 Although current evidence supports the efficacy of dexmedetomidine in reducing POD, the optimal timing of administration remains undetermined. Consequently, the objective of this trial is to evaluate the preventive effectiveness and safety profile of various dexmedetomidine administration schedules in mitigating POD among older adults receiving lower limb fracture surgery.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study is designed as a single-centre, prospective, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The primary objective is to evaluate how different dexmedetomidine administration timings (preoperative vs intraoperative) affect the development of POD in patients aged ≥65 years, thereby identifying the optimal clinical window.

Ethical authorisation for this research was granted by the Rugao People’s Hospital Medical Ethics Committee (Approval No. 2025-YJ-011-01), with prospective registration completed at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2500114299). The design of this study follows the relevant standards for randomised trials.25 26 The study flow and schedule are detailed in figure 1 and table 1, respectively. Recruitment is scheduled to commence in March 2026, with study completion expected by December 2027. Eligible patients who provide written informed consent will be randomised in a 1:1:1 ratio into the preoperative administration group, intraoperative administration group or control group.

Written informed consent was obtained from all patients preoperatively. No patients were enrolled intraoperatively. After hospital admission, eligible patients with stable vital signs and adequate decision-making capacity were fully informed by an independent researcher and provided voluntary written consent. For patients who were illiterate or unable to write due to low educational level, consent was obtained from their legally authorised representative under the approved ethical protocol.

The study is set to take place within the Department of Anesthesiology and Department of Orthopedics, Rugao People’s Hospital, Jiangsu Province, China.

Randomisation and blinding

The randomisation sequence will be generated by an independent statistician using a computer-generated random number list, and patients were randomly assigned to three groups in a 1:1:1 ratio. The randomisation results were sealed in opaque envelopes, and patients, the anaesthesiologists in charge and individuals responsible for outcome assessment remained unaware of the treatment allocation. Unblinding by the anaesthesiologist is permitted only if a severe adverse event requiring emergency management occurs intraoperatively (eg, severe hypotension or bradycardia refractory to conventional treatment). To maintain blinding integrity despite the potential physiological effects of dexmedetomidine, a strict separation of clinical and research roles will be implemented. Study drugs will be prepared in identical syringes by an independent research assistant not involved in anaesthesia management or data collection. While intraoperative haemodynamic fluctuations may be observed by the attending anaesthesiologist, the outcome assessors responsible for POD screening and secondary assessments will be completely blinded to the intraoperative grouping and anaesthesia records. These assessors will be physically isolated from the operating environment and are strictly prohibited from accessing any real-time or recorded vital signs data until the conclusion of the study.

Intervention and grouping

The study drug is dexmedetomidine hydrochloride injection (80 µg/20 mL; Sichuan Beite Pharmaceutical, China). Patients in the experimental groups will receive a single intravenous infusion at a dose of 0.5 µg/kg, with the infusion duration controlled at ≥10 min. To maintain strict blinding, a double-dummy technique will be employed where all patients receive infusions at two distinct time points: Time point 1 (T1), commencing 15 min prior to neuraxial anaesthesia puncture; and Time point 2 (T2), commencing 15 min prior to skin incision. The preoperative group will receive dexmedetomidine at T1 and an equivalent volume of 0.9% saline (placebo) at T2; the intraoperative group will receive placebo at T1 and dexmedetomidine at T2; and the control group will receive placebo at both T1 and T2. Infusion rates and durations remain identical across groups, and a 3-month postoperative follow-up will be conducted via telephone interview to assess long-term outcomes.

Standardised haemodynamic management will be strictly enforced during the infusion and perioperative period: if the heart rate drops below 50 bpm, 0.5 mg of intravenous atropine will be administered; if the mean arterial pressure decreases by >20% from baseline, phenylephrine or ephedrine will be used. All infusion volumes will remain identical across groups. A 3-month postoperative follow-up will be conducted via telephone interview by a blinded researcher to assess long-term recovery outcomes.

Anaesthesia

On arrival in the operating theatre, routine peripheral venous access will be established, and patients will undergo combined spinal-epidural anaesthesia (CSEA) in the lateral decubitus position. To facilitate the lateral decubitus position for CSEA without resorting to systemic sedation, all patients will receive an ultrasound-guided fascia iliaca compartment block. The block will be performed using a mixture of 10 mL 2% lidocaine and 10 mL (113 mg) bupivacaine liposome, diluted with 0.9% normal saline to a total volume of 30 mL. No additional systemic sedatives or opioids will be administered for positioning, thereby minimising pharmacological interference with the assessment of POD. CSEA was selected as the sole anaesthetic technique for this trial for two reasons. First, neuraxial anaesthesia is routinely used for lower limb fracture surgery in older adults at our centre and therefore reflects local clinical practice. Second, restricting enrolment to a single anaesthetic technique reduces heterogeneity and minimises confounding related to differential anaesthetic exposure, thereby allowing a clearer evaluation of the effect of dexmedetomidine administration timing on POD.

The L2–3 or L3–4 interspace will be selected for puncture using the needle-through-needle technique, with an epidural catheter advanced 3–4 cm cephalad. Following confirmation of subarachnoid placement, 1.6–2.2 mL of 0.75% isobaric bupivacaine will be injected (with the needle bevel cephalad) at a rate of 1 mL/5 s, targeting a sensory block level of T8. In cases of puncture failure or inadequate block (sensory level <T10), patients will be converted to general anaesthesia (induction with propofol and remifentanil; tracheal intubation) to ensure surgical safety. Continuous monitoring of ECG, non-invasive blood pressure, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO₂), temperature, regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO2) and Bispectral Index (BIS) will be performed for all patients. Oxygen will be delivered via a face mask at 2–4 L/min to maintain SpO₂ ≥95%. rSO₂ and BIS values will be recorded at baseline (room entry), immediately after anaesthesia and at 10, 30 and 60 min postanaesthesia, as well as at the end of surgery.

Baseline and perioperative data

Demographics and baseline characteristics will be recorded, including age, gender, height, weight, body mass index, education level, age-adjusted Charlson Comorbidity Index, type of surgery and preoperative baseline scale scores (MMSE, LSEQ, ADL, VAS, QoR-15). Perioperative data include intraoperative haemodynamics, heart rate, duration of surgery, intraoperative blood loss, fluid administration, BIS and rSO₂, nausea and vomiting, intensive care unit (ICU) admission, length of hospital stay, etc.

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the incidence of POD within the first three postoperative days. To capture the fluctuating nature of delirium, assessments will be conducted two times per day: once in the morning (08:00–10:00) and once in the afternoon (14:00–16:00). Evaluations will be performed by trained research personnel using the 3D-CAM, a tool specifically validated for the Chinese surgical population.27 28

Data management and monitoring

Clinical data for all participants will be initially recorded on case report forms and subsequently transcribed into a password-protected electronic database. To ensure data accuracy and integrity, a double data entry method will be employed for verification. All study-related documents will be retained by the principal investigator (PI) for at least 5 years after study completion.

Trial monitoring and safety oversight

Given the established safety profile of dexmedetomidine and the low-risk, single-centre nature of this study, a Trial Management Group (TMG) will be responsible for oversight in lieu of a formal Data Monitoring Committee. The TMG, comprising the PI, an independent statistician (unaffiliated with data collection), and a senior anaesthesiologist, operates independently of the funding body with no financial conflicts of interest. This group will convene every 6 months or after the recruitment of every 50 patients to evaluate cumulative safety data, recruitment milestones and data quality. The TMG holds the authority to recommend trial suspension or termination if an unexpected increase in the rate of serious adverse events is observed. All monitoring reports will be submitted to the Medical Ethics Committee of Rugao People’s Hospital for final review.

Sample size estimation and statistical analysis

The sample size was estimated using PASS 2021 software based on the primary outcome of POD incidence. We estimated the baseline POD rate in our control group at 30% for several reasons. First, our study focuses on acute lower limb fractures, a population experiencing significantly higher levels of surgical stress, acute pain and sleep deprivation compared with elective cohorts. Second, a recent study specifically investigating elderly Chinese patients undergoing hip replacement for fractures reported a POD incidence of 33.3%,33 which aligns with the high-risk profile of our emergency orthopaedic setting. Furthermore, our internal pilot audit of 20 patients at Rugao People’s Hospital observed an incidence of 20%. Given these data and the high diagnostic sensitivity of the 3D-CAM tool, a 30% baseline is a robust and conservative estimate to ensure the trial is not underpowered. Based on a meta-analysis indicating that dexmedetomidine could reduce POD incidence by 54%,34 we anticipate the incidence in the intervention groups will decrease to approximately 10–15%. To achieve 80% power at a two-sided significance level of 0.05, a total of 200 participants is required. Accounting for a potential 10% dropout rate, we aim to recruit 75 patients per group, totalling 225 patients across the three groups.

Statistical analyses will be performed on an intention-to-treat basis using SPSS V.26.0 (IBM, USA). Continuous variables will be described as mean±SD or median (IQR) based on normality (assessed via the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test) and compared among the three groups using one-way analysis of variance or the Kruskal-Wallis H test, respectively, with post hoc pairwise comparisons where appropriate. Categorical variables will be presented as n (%) and analysed via the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. The primary outcome will be assessed using the χ² test with relative risk calculation, and a binary multivariate logistic regression model will be employed to adjust for potential confounders, with a p value <0.05 indicating statistical significance.

Discussion

This randomised, placebo-controlled, prospective study was designed to evaluate the prophylactic capacity of dexmedetomidine administered at different timings (preoperative vs intraoperative) on POD among older individuals with lower limb fractures, with the goal of establishing the optimal intervention window.

POD is a common and serious complication in elderly patients, with a complex pathogenesis. The potential of dexmedetomidine in reducing the risk of POD in aged patients has received widespread attention. Evidence suggests a strong correlation between the occurrence of delirium and circadian rhythm dysregulation,35 while dexmedetomidine promotes physiological sleep by increasing the proportion of N2 sleep and reducing the sleep fragmentation index.36 However, the α₂-receptor agonist effect of the drug also brings dose-dependent haemodynamic side effects,37 and high-dose infusion often causes bradycardia and hypotension. Therefore, on the premise of ensuring efficacy, exploring the optimal timing of administration rather than simply increasing the dosage has important clinical significance for achieving the best balance between realising neuroprotective benefits and maintaining perioperative haemodynamic stability.

A previous study published in The Lancet indicated that postoperative continuous infusion of low-dose dexmedetomidine in an ICU setting effectively reduced the incidence of POD in elderly patients undergoing non-cardiac surgery, a mechanism primarily attributed to the drug’s improvement of nocturnal sleep architecture.38 However, the applicability and safety of this regimen are significantly limited for patients returning to general wards postoperatively. Considering that the target population of this study consists of elderly patients with lower limb fractures who routinely return to general wards after surgery, continuous vital sign monitoring comparable to that in the ICU is lacking. If dexmedetomidine were infused continuously at night, the potential risks of bradycardia and hypotension would be difficult to detect and manage in a timely manner. Based on safety priorities, this study shifts the intervention window forward, designing it as a single preoperative or intraoperative administration, aiming to use the pharmacological after-effects of the drug to prevent delirium while avoiding the risks of medication use in an unmonitored postoperative state.

An epidemiological analysis from the UK indicates that neuraxial anaesthesia is the preferred anaesthetic method for lower limb fracture surgery in the elderly,39 which aligns with the population included in this study. Indeed, in many regions, including our own centre, neuraxial anaesthesia remains the established standard of care for geriatric fracture surgery. Our exclusive focus on CSEA is a deliberate methodological choice to enhance the study’s internal validity: by restricting enrolment to a single neuraxial anaesthesia technique, we effectively eliminate confounding related to general anaesthesia, as volatile anaesthetics and higher perioperative opioid doses are well-established independent risk factors for POD. Regarding dosage, previous studies have reported ranges varying from 0.1 to 1.4 µg/kg/h.38 40 41 Considering the significant individual variability in the duration of fracture surgery, the use of continuous infusion would result in uncontrollable total doses. To achieve standardised drug exposure, we uniformly adopted a single bolus infusion of 0.5 μg/kg.24 Notably, while prior work by Liu et al42 has linked 0.5 µg/kg dexmedetomidine to haemodynamic instability in older adults, these findings were observed with a ‘loading dose plus maintenance infusion’ regimen. In contrast, our protocol uses a one-time bolus infused over a minimum of 10 min, with no postoperative maintenance infusion. This design significantly reduces total cumulative drug exposure, while still retaining the neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory effects of dexmedetomidine, and supports a more manageable haemodynamic profile for this vulnerable cohort. This dosage was ultimately selected to preserve the drug’s targeted biological effects, while keeping haemodynamic fluctuations within a range safely tolerable for elderly patients.

The main strength of this study lies in its prospective, randomised, placebo-controlled design. In addition to using multidimensional scales such as MMSE, VAS, LSEQ, ADL and QoR-15 for cognitive and functional assessment, we also collected serum biomarkers before and after surgery to analyse neuronal injury markers (S100β protein, NSE) and inflammatory factors (CRP, IL-6), which can reflect the pathological mechanism of POD from an objective level.

In conclusion, this study aims to determine the optimal timing of dexmedetomidine administration for the prevention of POD in elderly patients. It is expected to provide high-quality evidence for optimising perioperative anaesthesia management strategies for elderly fracture patients, thereby improving long-term patient outcomes while alleviating the economic burden on the healthcare system.

Our study excludes patients with pre-existing dementia, which may limit the generalisability of our findings to this high-risk population. This exclusion was necessary to ensure the diagnostic accuracy of POD assessments; however, future studies should explore whether dexmedetomidine timing offers similar benefits in patients with established cognitive decline.
