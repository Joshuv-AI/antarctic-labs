Higher order perturbative and nonperturbative QCD corrections on the proton structure functions and parity violating electron asymmetry
F. Zaidi, M. Sajjad Athar, S. K. Singh

Abstract
We study the nonperturbative and higher order perturbative corrections on the electromagnetic ( F 1 ​ p , 2 ​ p γ F_{1p,2p}^{\gamma} ) and electromagnetic-weak interference ( F 1 ​ p , 2 ​ p , 3 ​ p γ ​ Z F_{1p,2p,3p}^{\gamma Z} ) structure functions and their impact on the parity violating electron asymmetry in the deep inelastic scattering of longitudinally polarized electron off an unpolarized proton target. The numerical results for them are presented by including the perturbative corrections beyond the leading order (LO) up to the next-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) and nonperturbative QCD corrections due to the target mass corrections (TMC) and the higher twist (HT: twist-4) effects. We also present the numerical results for the electron beam spin asymmetry A P ​ V ( e ) ​ ( x , Q 2 ) A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2}) corresponding to the JLab energies of 6 GeV, 12 GeV and 22 GeV and discuss the feasibility of determining the d / u d/u quark distribution ratio. The results obtained in this work may be useful for the analysis of future measurements at the Electron Ion Collider(EIC) in USA, and the Electron ion collider in China(EicC) aimed at studying parity violating effects in the deep inelastic scattering of polarized electrons from unpolarized proton targets.

Using Eq.

in Eq.

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{dxdy}

For the numerical calculations, we use the MMHT PDFs parameterization
Harland-Lang:2014zoa

(19)

where

for

II.2
Electron spin asymmetry
A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

The parity violating spin asymmetry for
the longitudinally polarized electron beam off unpolarized proton target is defined as
Hobbs:2008mm
:

A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})-\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})}{\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})+\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})},

(21)

where the differential scattering cross sections for the scattering of right-handed (
\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})
) and left-handed (
\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})
) electron off an unpolarized
proton target are given by:

\sigma_{R(L)}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{dxdy}\Big(\lambda_{e}=+1(-1)\Big),

(22)

where average is taken over the proton spin states
\lambda_{p}
.

\frac{d^{2}\sigma^{0}}{dxdy}=\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})+\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})

(23)

We evaluate the unpolarized differential scattering cross section (
\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})+\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})
) using Eq.
18
and
summing over all possible spin orientations of the charged lepton beam and the target nucleon as well as
the averaged differential cross sections (
\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})-\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})
)
for the longitudinally polarized electron beam scattering off unpolarized nucleon target
Boughezal:2022pmb
, and by taking their ratio
we obtain the electron asymmetry
A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

Hobbs:2008mm
:

\displaystyle A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})-\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})}{\sigma_{R}(x,Q^{2})+\sigma_{L}(x,Q^{2})}=\frac{{\cal A}(x,Q^{2})}{{\cal B}(x,Q^{2})},

(24)

\displaystyle\textrm{where}~~{\cal A}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P_{e}|\Big[\eta^{\gamma Z}\Big\{g_{A}^{e}\big(xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})\big)-g_{V}^{e}xy\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)\;F_{3p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\eta^{\gamma Z})^{2}\Big\{-2g_{V}^{e}g_{A}^{e}\big(xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})\big)+\big((g_{V}^{e})^{2}+(g_{A}^{e})^{2}\big)xy\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)\;F_{3p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})\Big\}\Big],

\displaystyle{\cal B}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})\Big\}

\displaystyle-\eta^{\gamma Z}\Big\{g_{V}^{e}\big(xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})\big)-g_{A}^{e}xy\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)\;F_{3p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})\Big\}

\displaystyle+(\eta^{\gamma Z})^{2}\Big\{\big((g_{V}^{e})^{2}+(g_{A}^{e})^{2}\big)\big(xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})\big)-2g_{V}^{e}g_{A}^{e}xy\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)\;F_{3p}^{Z}(x,Q^{2})\Big\},

where
|P_{e}|
is the polarization vector for the longitudinally polarized electron beam.
It is important to note that in polarization experiments
like PVDIS experiments at JLab
Accardi:2023chb
;
Meziani:2024leh
;
JeffersonLabSoLID:2022iod
, EIC
AbdulKhalek:2021gbh
;
Boughezal:2022pmb
and
EicC
Anderle:2021wcy
, the polarization of electron beam is about 80%, i.e.,
|P_{e}|=0.8
. Therefore, we have taken

|P_{e}|=0.8
for the numerical calculations of the electron asymmetry.

For
Q^{2}<<M_{Z}^{2}
, the contribution from the pure
Z-
exchange is suppressed by a factor of

(\eta^{\gamma Z})^{2}
(Eq.
2
), which is of the order of
10^{-6}
for
Q^{2}\simeq 10
GeV
2
, therefore, here on-wards, we will not
consider the contribution from the pure weak
Z-
channel.
The denominator, on the other hand, contains all the contributions, but is
dominated by the purely electromagnetic component.
Hence, the electron spin asymmetry (Eq.
24
) can be written as
Hobbs:2008mm
:

\displaystyle A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P_{e}|\frac{\eta^{\gamma Z}\Big\{g_{A}^{e}\big(xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})\big)-g_{V}^{e}xy\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)\;F_{3p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})\Big\}}{\;\Big\{xy^{2}\;F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})+(1-y)\;F_{2p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})\Big\}},

(25)

Alternatively, the above expression of the electron spin asymmetry given in Eq.
25
may also be expressed in terms of the ratio of the
longitudinal
F_{Lp}(x,Q^{2})

\Big(=\Big[1+\frac{4M^{2}x^{2}}{Q^{2}}\Big]F_{2p}(x,Q^{2})-2xF_{1p}(x,Q^{2})\Big)

to the transverse
F_{Tp}(x,Q^{2})
(
=2xF_{1p}(x,Q^{2})
) structure functions as:

\displaystyle A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P_{e}|\eta^{\gamma Z}\Big[g_{A}^{e}\Big(\frac{F_{1p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})}{F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}\Big)\;\frac{y^{2}+\frac{2}{r^{2}}(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2}))}{y^{2}+\frac{2}{r^{2}}(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2}))}

(26)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{V}^{e}\Big(\frac{F_{3p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})}{F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}\Big)\;\frac{y\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)}{y^{2}+\frac{2}{r^{2}}(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2}))}\Big],

where
R_{p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})
is given by

R_{p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{F_{Lp}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}{2xF_{1p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}=\frac{(1+\gamma^{2})\;F_{2p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})-2xF_{1p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}{2xF_{1p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})},

(27)

with
r^{2}=1+\gamma^{2}
.

The above expression (Eq.
26
) in a more compact form is given by
Hobbs:2008mm
:

\displaystyle A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|P_{e}|\eta^{\gamma Z}\Big\{Y_{1p}(x,Q^{2})a_{1p}(x,Q^{2})-Y_{3p}(x,Q^{2})a_{3p}(x,Q^{2})\Big\},

(28)

where

Y_{1p}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{y^{2}+2(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2}))/r^{2}}{y^{2}+2(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2}))/r^{2}};\;\;\;Y_{3p}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{y\big(1-\frac{y}{2}\big)}{y^{2}+2(1-y)(1+R_{p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2}))/r^{2}},

(29)

and

\displaystyle a_{1p}(x,Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{A}^{e}\Big(\frac{F_{1p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})}{F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}\Big),\hskip 8.53581pta_{3p}(x,Q^{2})=g_{V}^{e}\Big(\frac{F_{3p}^{\gamma Z}(x,Q^{2})}{F_{1p}^{\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}\Big).

(30)

In the LO of QCD,
a_{1p}(x)
and
a_{3p}(x)
are given by (using Eq.
30
)
Hobbs:2008mm
:

\displaystyle a_{1p}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i}\;C_{1i}e_{i}(q_{i}(x)+\bar{q}_{i}(x))}{\sum_{i}e_{i}^{2}(q_{i}(x)+\bar{q}_{i}(x))},\hskip 8.53581pta_{3p}(x)=\frac{\sum_{i}\;C_{2i}e_{i}(q_{i}(x)-\bar{q}_{i}(x))}{\sum_{i}e_{i}^{2}(q_{i}(x)+\bar{q}_{i}(x))},

where
C_{1i}=2g_{A}^{e}g_{V}^{i}
and
C_{2i}=2g_{V}^{e}g_{A}^{i}
are the effective weak coupling constants
ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
. In the
standard model, the vector (
g_{V}^{i}
) and axial-vector (

) couplings of quarks are given by
Hobbs:2008mm

(32)

leading to

C_{1u}=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4}{3}\;\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\Big)=0.19173,~~~C_{1d}=\Big(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{3}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\Big)=-0.34586

(33)

and

C_{2u}=\Big(-\frac{1}{2}+2\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\Big)=-0.0376,~~~C_{2d}=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-2\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\Big)=0.0376

(34)

using
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=0.2312

ParticleDataGroup:2024cfk
.

It is important to note that in the standard model, the electron asymmetry

is dominated by the first term containing

In the Bjorken limit of

holds, the longitudinal structure function (

\gamma\to 0,~r\to 1~\textrm{and}~R_{p}^{\gamma/\gamma Z}\to 0\;\;\textrm{(following Eqs.~\ref{cgmod} and \ref{rlp})}.

For low and moderate values of

and

The determination of

directly reflects how momentum is shared between these quarks as a function of Bjorken

. In recent years, the study of the parity violating

x

ratio.
Traditionally, the
d(x)/u(x)
ratio has been determined from the electromagnetic EMC measurements of
\frac{F_{2A}(x,Q^{2})}{F_{2p}(x,Q^{2})}

on nuclei like
A=2
(

3
(
{}^{3}_{1}H
,
{}^{3}_{2}He
)
Cocuzza:2026vey

ambiguities in defining precise cuts on the center of mass energy
W
and the four momentum transfer square
Q^{2}

SajjadAthar:2020nvy
. In the case of proton
target, there are no nuclear medium corrections, however, uncertainties related to the proper definition of the boundary of the transition region still
present. Owing to the absence of nuclear effects, studies of parity violating electron asymmetry for proton targets have been
proposed as a cleaner method to determine the

in the region of high

in which sea quarks contribution can be neglected
Brady:2011uy
;
Cocuzza:2026vey

IV
Summary and conclusion

In this work, we have studied the effects of perturbative and nonperturbative QCD corrections to the QPM in the evaluation of the
proton structure functions in the electromagnetic as well as in the weak-electromagnetic interference sectors. We have also studied the impact of these
corrections on the Callan-Gross relation
Callan:1969uq
by calculating the values of the ratios

r^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{F_{2p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}{2xF_{1p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}
and

R_{p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})=\frac{F_{Lp}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}{2xF_{1p}^{\gamma Z/\gamma}(x,Q^{2})}
. The differential scattering cross
sections
\frac{d\sigma}{dx}
and the parity violating electron beam asymmetry
A_{PV}^{(e)}(x,Q^{2})
have also been studied. The higher order perturbative
corrections are calculated up to NNLO using MMHT PDFs in the 3-flavor
\overline{\textrm{MS}}
scheme. The
nonperturbative effects, namely, the target mass corrections and the higher twist corrections have been incorporated. The effect of
these QCD corrections on the ratio
d(x)/u(x)
in the region of high
x
has also been discussed.

We conclude that:

•

The effect of higher order perturbative corrections are quantitatively significant on the differential cross sections and the parity violating electron asymmetry only in the
region of high
x
, i.e.,
x\geq 0.6
.

•

The TMC effect is found to be significant in the differential scattering cross sections as well as
the parity violating electron asymmetry in the region of intermediate-to-high
x~(\gtrsim 0.4)
while the higher twist corrections are observed to be important in the
kinematic region of high
x~(\gtrsim 0.6)
for the projectile beam energies considered in the present work.

•

The violation of the Callan-Gross relation due to nonperturbative QCD corrections is significant in the region of intermediate-to-high
x

for all the values of
Q^{2}
in the range of
2\leq Q^{2}\leq 20
GeV
2
considered in this work. The violation of Callan-Gross relation is found to be quantitatively different
in the electromagnetic and weak-electromagnetic structure functions of the proton.

•

The present study of
a_{1p}(x,Q^{2})
with QCD corrections may be helpful in understanding the behavior of
d(x)/u(x)
ratio
as well as in interpreting the relevant experimental data, especially, from the upcoming experiments at JLab.

Thus to summarize, the higher order QCD corrections on the proton structure functions are quite significant for both the
\gamma-
exchange and
\gamma Z-
exchange channels
in the present kinematic region of
0.\leq x\leq 0.8
and
2\leq Q^{2}\leq 20
GeV
2
relevant to the ongoing and upcoming experiments at JLab, EIC and EicC, and
a better theoretical understanding is required in the region of high
x
, where nonperturbative effects are important.
