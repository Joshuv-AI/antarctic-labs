The variation of zeros of the Miller basis
Liubomir Chiriac and Andrei Jorza

Abstract
. We exhibit a connection between the variation of zeros in the Miller basis of modular forms q m + O ⁡ ( q ℓ + 1 ) q^{m}+O(q^{\ell+1}) and a logarithmic version 𝒮 δ \mathcal{S}_{\delta} of the Szegő curve, where δ = m / ℓ \delta=m/\ell . When δ < 0.6194 \delta<0.6194 we show that all the zeros are on the unit arc for k ≫ 0 k\gg 0 , while if δ \delta is asymptotically close to 1, we show that all the zeros lie on 𝒮 δ \mathcal{S}_{\delta} . In general, we posit that for all δ \delta , the zeros are located on the union of the unit arc and the log Szegő curve, obtaining a partial result, and find conjectural thresholds for m / ℓ m/\ell with all zeros on the unit arc, and no zeros on the arc. Finally, we enumerate all algebraic zeros of Miller forms up to ℓ − m ≤ 25 \ell-m\leq 25 .

Introduction

Denote by
M_{k}
the space of all holomorphic modular forms of even weight

and level one on the upper half-plane
\mathfrak{H}
. For any element of

we consider its zeros in the standard fundamental domain

[

]

of the unit circle joining

by an elementary function on
\mathcal{A}
, continues to serve as a blueprint for most subsequent results on the subject.

Building on this foundational idea, Duke and Jenkins
[
DJ08
]
incorporated the circle method as a crucial new component to show that the zeros of certain weakly holomorphic modular forms also lie on the unit circle. Raveh
[
Rav25
]
recently adapted their proof to study the zeros of certain elements of the Miller basis of
M_{k}
denoted by

(1.1)

g_{k,m}=q^{m}+O\left(q^{\ell+1}\right),\text{ where }\ell+1=\dim M_{k},0\leq m\leq\ell.

He showed that for fixed
m\geq 1
, once

exceeds a certain linear bound in

lie on
\mathcal{A}
, a property that always holds true for

. In contrast, when the difference

is fixed, Rudnick
[
Rud24
]
used Faber polynomials to show that the zeros of

cluster near vertical lines as

One of the main motivations for this paper is to provide a framework that encompasses the entire family of Miller basis. Our first result provides a lower-bound for the proportion of non-elliptic zeros on the arc depending on the ratio

Beyond their geometric location, the arithmetic nature of these zeros is also of significant interest. Kohnen

2.
Approximating the Miller forms

If
k\geq 4
is an even weight and
\ell=\dim M_{k}-1
, we can write

k=12\ell+k^{\prime}
for some

k^{\prime}\in\{0,4,6,8,10,14\}.

Consider the Miller basis of
M_{k}
given by
g_{k,m}=q^{m}+O(q^{\ell+1}).

Since each
g_{k,m}
has integer Fourier coefficients, it is clear that
\overline{g_{k,m}(z)}=g_{k,m}(-\overline{z}).
Setting
z=e^{i\theta}
we obtain

\overline{g_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})}=g_{k,m}(-e^{-i\theta})=g_{k,m}(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&-1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)e^{i\theta})=e^{ik\theta}g_{k,m}(e^{i\theta}).

Therefore
g_{k,m}
can be rescaled along the arc to obtain the
real-valued function
e^{ik\theta/2}g_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})
. We denote

\overline{g}_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})=e^{ik\theta/2+2\pi m\sin\theta}g_{k,m}(e^{i\theta}).

One can show (see, for example,
[
Rav25
, Lemma 3.7]
) that for each
R>0
there exists an
A>1
such that for all
z\in\mathcal{F}
with
|j(z)|<R
we have

g_{k,m}(z)=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iA}^{\frac{1}{2}+iA}G(z,\tau)d\tau,

where

G(z,\tau)=\frac{\Delta^{\ell}(z)E_{k^{\prime}}(z)E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\tau)}{\Delta^{\ell+1}(\tau)(j(\tau)-j(z))}e^{2\pi im\tau}.

Since we are primarily interested in roots on
\mathcal{A}
, we may take
R>1728
and a corresponding
A
, so that the above integral formula holds. Duke-Jenkins and Raveh use the circle method to find good
estimates of the above integral. Abstracting their process, for
each
B>0
, we will integrate on the following contour

Figure 3
.
Contour of integration

This yields

(2.1)

g_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iB}^{\frac{1}{2}+iB}G(\tau,e^{i\theta})d\tau-2\pi i\sum_{\gamma}\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=\gamma e^{i\theta}}G(\tau,e^{i\theta}),

where the sum is over all
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such
that
\operatorname{Re}\gamma e^{i\theta}=-\frac{1}{2}
and
\operatorname{Im}\gamma e^{i\theta}>B
, as well as the identity matrix
I_{2}
and
S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&-1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. The main idea is that the residues at
I_{2}
and
S
control the size of
\overline{g}_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})
, the other terms being errors that one can estimate.

Lemma 1
.

For any
e^{i\theta}\in\mathcal{F}
, the function
j(\tau)-j(e^{i\theta})
has finitely many roots inside the contour above. At any root
\gamma e^{i\theta}
, which corresponds to a simple pole of
G(\tau,e^{i\theta})
, we have

\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=\gamma e^{i\theta}}G(\tau,e^{i\theta})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}(ce^{i\theta}+d)^{-k}e^{2\pi im(\gamma e^{i\theta})},

giving

|-2\pi i\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=\gamma e^{i\theta}}G(\tau,e^{i\theta})|=|ce^{i\theta}+d|^{-k}e^{-2\pi m\sin\theta|ce^{i\theta}+d|^{-2}}.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=\gamma e^{i\theta}}G(\tau,e^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{\ell}(e^{i\theta})E_{k^{\prime}}(e^{i\theta})E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\gamma e^{i\theta})}{\Delta^{\ell+1}(\gamma e^{i\theta})\frac{dj}{d\tau}(\gamma e^{i\theta})}e^{2\pi im\gamma e^{i\theta}}

\displaystyle=(ce^{i\theta}+d)^{-k}\frac{\Delta^{\ell}(\gamma e^{i\theta})E_{k^{\prime}}(\gamma e^{i\theta})E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\gamma e^{i\theta})}{\Delta^{\ell+1}(\gamma e^{i\theta})\frac{dj}{d\tau}(\gamma e^{i\theta})}e^{2\pi im\gamma e^{i\theta}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}(ce^{i\theta}+d)^{-k}e^{2\pi im(\gamma e^{i\theta})},

as
E_{k^{\prime}}(\tau)E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\tau)=E_{14}(\tau)
and
\frac{dj}{d\tau}=-2\pi i\frac{E_{14}(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau)}
.
∎

In particular, the residues at
z
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&-1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)z
have a combined contribution of

\displaystyle-2\pi i\left(\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=e^{i\theta}}+\operatorname{Res}_{\tau=-e^{-i\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ime^{i\theta}}+e^{-ik\theta}e^{-2\pi ime^{-i\theta}}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\pi m\sin\theta-ik\theta/2}2\cos\left(2\pi m\cos(\theta)+k\theta/2\right).

The technical core of this section, and of the zeros counting argument in the next, relies on estimating each of the remaining terms in (
2.1
). First, it is clear that the error satisfies

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=|\overline{g}_{k,m}(e^{i\theta})-2\cos\left(2\pi m\cos(\theta)+k\theta/2\right)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{2\pi m\sin\theta}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iB}^{\frac{1}{2}+iB}|G(\tau,e^{i\theta})|d\tau+e^{2\pi m\sin\theta}\sum_{\gamma\neq I_{2},S}|ce^{i\theta}+d|^{-k}e^{-2\pi m\sin\theta|ce^{i\theta}+d|^{-2}}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq\frac{2\pi}{3}
.
For each
B>0
such that
\operatorname{Im}\gamma e^{i\theta}\neq B
for any
\gamma
as above and all
\theta\in[\alpha,\beta]
, there is a constant

such that

\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iB}^{\frac{1}{2}+iB}|G(\tau,e^{i\theta})|d\tau\leq e^{-2\pi mB+h_{\alpha,\beta,B}}\left(\frac{|\Delta(e^{i\theta})|}{|\Delta(iB)|}\right)^{\ell}.

Proof.

We follow Duke-Jenkins in estimating the integral:

\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iB}^{\frac{1}{2}+iB}|G(\tau,e^{i\theta})|d\tau\leq e^{-2\pi mB}\max_{|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{|\Delta(e^{i\theta})|}{|\Delta(x+iB)|}\right)^{\ell}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+iB}^{\frac{1}{2}+iB}\frac{|E_{k^{\prime}}(e^{i\theta})E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\tau)|}{|\Delta(\tau)(j(\tau)-j(e^{i\theta})|}d\tau.

Remark that
\min_{|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}}|\Delta(x+iB)|=|\Delta(iB)|
. Indeed, the

q

. Plugging in

\frac{e^{-2\pi B}}{|\Delta(x+iB)|}\leq\sum c_{n}e^{-2\pi nB}=\frac{e^{-2\pi B}}{|\Delta(iB)|}.

Finally, the function
f(\theta,\tau)=\frac{|E_{k^{\prime}}(e^{i\theta})E_{14-k^{\prime}}(\tau)|}{|\Delta(\tau)(j(\tau)-j(e^{i\theta})|}
is analytic on the compact set
[\sigma,\beta]\times\{x+iB\mid x\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]\}
and thus achieves a maximum
e^{h_{\alpha,\beta,B}}
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
as above and
B\in(0,\sin\alpha)
.
There exists a constant

\frac{m}{\ell}<I(\alpha,\beta,B)

Proof.

Let

be the finite set of elements

and

.
From Lemma

Writing

When

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta(\sin\theta-B)-(\ln|\Delta(iB)|-\ln|\Delta(e^{i\theta})|)

\displaystyle<0

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta\sin\theta(1-|ce^{i\theta}+d|^{-2})-12\ln|ce^{i\theta}+d|

\displaystyle<0\ \ \ \forall\gamma\in\mathcal{R}.

(2.3)

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta(\sin\alpha-B)-(\ln|\Delta(iB)|-\ln|\Delta(e^{i\beta})|)

\displaystyle<0

\displaystyle<0\ \ \ \forall\gamma\in\mathcal{R}\ \ cd\geq 0

\displaystyle 2\pi\delta\sin\alpha(1-|ce^{i\beta}+d|^{-2})-12\ln|ce^{i\alpha}+d|

Proof of Theorem B

Consider again the Miller basis
g_{k,m}=q^{m}+O(q^{\ell+1})
of weight

, with

. For some monic polynomial
P
of degree
D=\ell-m
we have

\frac{g_{k,m}}{\Delta^{\ell}E_{k^{\prime}}}=P(j),

where
j=E_{4}^{3}/\Delta
is the usual
j
-invariant.
We refer to
P
as the Faber polynomial of
g_{k,m}
. Writing

P(X)=\sum_{d=0}^{D}y_{d}x^{D-d}\quad y_{0}=1,

we will be concerned with estimating the coefficients
y_{d}
when
D
grows with
k
, albeit more slowly.
For all

we also set

j^{r}=\sum_{n=-r}^{\infty}c_{r,n}q^{n},

so that

P(j)=\sum_{d=0}^{D}y_{d}j^{D-d}=\sum_{d=0}^{D}\sum_{n\geq-(D-d)}y_{d}c_{D-d,n}q^{n}.

Since

\frac{g_{k,m}}{\Delta^{\ell}E_{k^{\prime}}}=q^{-D}\frac{1+O(q^{D+1})}{(q^{-1}\Delta)^{\ell}E_{k^{\prime}}},

it follows that

(q^{-1}\Delta)^{-\ell}(E_{k^{\prime}})^{-1}=\sum_{d=0}^{D}\sum_{n=-(D-d)}^{0}y_{d}c_{D-d,n}q^{D+n}+O(q^{D+1}).

On the other hand, we may expand the power series formally as

(q^{-1}\Delta)^{-\ell}(E_{k^{\prime}})^{-1}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{\ell,d}q^{d}.

Lemma 10
.

(1)

If
d=O(|k|^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon})
for some
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
then

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\ell,d},\mathcal{D}_{\ell,d}=\frac{(2|k|)^{d}}{d!}\left(1+O\left(|k|^{-2\varepsilon}\right)\right).

(2)

If

then

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\ell,d},\mathcal{D}_{\ell,d}=O\left(\left(\frac{2\alpha|k|}{d}\right)^{d}\right),

where
\alpha=\left(\frac{25}{24}\right)^{25}
.

Proof.

Suppose
k>0
, as the proof when
k<0
is almost identical.
As in
[
Rud24
, §4]
, we have

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{\ell,d}=\sum_{\sum ir_{i}=d}\prod_{i}\binom{2k+r_{i}-1}{r_{i}}.

By the Stirling approximation, we have

\displaystyle\binom{2k+r-1}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k}{2k+r}\binom{2k+r}{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{2k}{2k+r}\frac{(2k+r)^{2k+r}}{r!(2k)^{2k}}\frac{1}{e^{r}}\sqrt{\frac{2k+r}{2k}}\left(1+\frac{1}{24k}+O(k^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k)^{r}}{r!}\left[\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{r}{2k}\right)^{1+\frac{2k}{r}-\frac{1}{2r}}\right]^{r}\left(1+\frac{1}{24k}+O(k^{-2})\right).

(1) If
r\leq d=O(k^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon})
, we see that

\displaystyle\log\left[\frac{1}{e}\left(1+\frac{r}{2k}\right)^{1+\frac{2k}{r}-\frac{1}{2r}}\right]^{r}

\displaystyle=(r+2k-\frac{1}{2})\log\left(1+\frac{r}{2k}\right)-r

\displaystyle=(r+2k-\frac{1}{2})\left(\frac{r}{2k}-\frac{r^{2}}{8k^{2}}+O\left(\frac{r^{3}}{k^{3}}\right)\right)-r

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{4k}-\frac{r}{4k}+\frac{r^{2}}{16k^{2}}+O\left(\frac{r^{3}}{k^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{4k}+O\left(\frac{r}{k}\right)=O(k^{-2\varepsilon}).

We conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum ir_{i}=d}\prod_{i}\binom{2k+r_{i}-1}{r_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sum ir_{i}=d}\prod_{i}\frac{(2k)^{r_{i}}}{r_{i}!}\left(1+\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{4k}+O\left(\frac{r}{k}\right)\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{24k}+O(k^{-2})\right)^{d^{\prime}},

where
d^{\prime}<\sqrt{2d}
is the number of nonzero
r_{i}
. Thus

\left(1+\frac{1}{24k}+O(k^{-2})\right)^{d^{\prime}}=1+O(k^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}})
,
while
\prod\left(1+\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{4k}+O\left(\frac{r}{k}\right)\right)=1+O(k^{-2\varepsilon})
for any choice of
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{d})
. Therefore, it suffices to verify that the term with

dominates the sum.

Note that the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum ir_{i}=d}\prod_{i}\frac{(2k)^{r_{i}}}{r_{i}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\binom{d-1}{j-1}\frac{(2k)^{j}}{j!}

can be computed using the Laguerre polynomial

L_{d}^{-1}(-2k)
. The terms in the latter sum are increasing in

as

\displaystyle\sum_{\sum ir_{i}=d}\prod_{i}\frac{(2k)^{r_{i}}}{r_{i}!}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{d^{d}}{k^{d-r}r^{r}}(1728-e^{2\pi})^{d-r}\frac{(D-r)^{D-r}}{(D-d)^{D-d}(d-r)^{d-r}}\right),
