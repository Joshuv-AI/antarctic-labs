Accuracy Certificates for Convex Optimization at Accelerated Rates via Primal-Dual Averaging
Matthew X. Burns, Jiaming Liang

Abstract
Many works in convex optimization provide rates for achieving a small primal gap. However, this quantity is typically unavailable in practice. In this work, we show that solving a regularized surrogate with algorithms based on simple primal-dual averaging provides non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for a computable optimality certificate. We first analyze primal and dual methods based on one average, namely modified dual averaging and generalized conditional gradient, and establish 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ε − 1 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-1}) certificate complexities. Motivated by asymmetries in the one-average case, we analyze a self-dual, two-average method that preserves symmetry while losing certificate guarantees. To recover certificate convergence, we propose a three-average method that achieves an accelerated 𝒪 ~ ( ε − 1 / 2 ) \tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-1/2}) certificate complexity. Furthermore, we prove primal-dual algorithm correspondences for the one, two, and three-average cases. In particular, the primal three-average accelerated method mirrors the well-known gradient extrapolation method in the dual. By interpreting our results through the lens of zero-sum matrix games and Fisher markets, we further connect primal-dual averaging methods to game theory and market dynamics. Key words. primal-dual averaging, conditional gradient method, accelerated method, gradient extrapolation method, accuracy certificate

Our contributions
In this work, we characterize primal-dual pairs in three algorithmic classes targeting the regularized problem (
2
), methods with one, two, and three averages, along with their complexity and optimality certificate guarantees. The algorithms considered are summarized in Table

While the algorithm possesses appealing symmetry, it does not admit the same straightforward primal-dual certificate convergence as the one-average case. Finally, we propose a three-average accelerated (TAA) method. TAA recovers the computable primal-dual certificate of the one-average case with an accelerated

The motivation for the algorithmic progression MDA

We consider a smoothed variation of the Fisher market equilibrium with linear utility functions proposed by
[

]

5

MDA (One-Average)
. In each round, the market generates provisional prices

by a best-fit approximation to smoothed demand

AggGCG (Two-Averages)
. Algorithm
3
remedies the asymmetry in MDA market dynamics, but loses convergence guarantees for optimality certificates. In the MDA results, the ACP model included the latest buyer allocation
\nabla f(x_{k+1})

. The technical motivation for a third average was primarily geometric as visualized in Figure

. Each round

7
Conclusion

In this work, we explore primal-dual correspondences in regularized convex optimization with a focus on certifiable optimality. Motivated by primal-dual correspondences in one and two averages, we propose the TAA method which leverages three averages to obtain near-optimal complexity for solving (
1
). While TAA is new, we show that its dual counterpart is the well-studied GEM. We show that both TAA and the GEM obtain computable
\varepsilon
-certificates for primal-dual gap convergence with
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-1/2})
complexity, a novel result for the GEM to our knowledge. Our certificate guarantees further enhance the utility for the GEM as a subsidiary solver by providing upper bounds on a computable termination condition. Furthermore, we provide in-depth intuition and motivation for our results with concrete examples in Fisher markets and zero-sum matrix games.

There are a number of directions for future work. Our analysis relies on knowledge of the problem smoothness
L
. However,
L
may be unknown at runtime, particularly for large-scale problem instances. Future work on universal variants of TAA would substantially improve its scope of application. Similarly, our analysis focused on
smooth
problems, whereas an increasing number of methods target the class of
relatively smooth
problems
[
26
]
. Recent work
[
22
]
has generalized the typical smoothness/strong-convexity duality relation to the relative case, suggesting that algorithmic correspondences may exist under these generalized relations.

Lemma A.4

(Three-Points Inequality)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
and
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be closed, proper, and convex functions with
\mathrm{dom}\,\Phi\subseteq\mathrm{dom}\,\omega
and
\mathrm{dom}\,\Phi
has a nonempty relative interior.
Assume that
\Phi
is
\mu
-strongly convex relative to
\omega
, that is,
\Phi(\cdot)-\mu\omega(\cdot)
is a convex function. Fix
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\Phi
,
\beta>0
and define

x^{+}=\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathrm{argmin}\,}\left\{\Phi(x)+\beta\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(x\|x_{0})\right\},

(39)

where
\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(x\|x_{0})
is defined using an arbitrary
\omega^{\prime}(x_{0})\in\partial\omega(x_{0})
.
Then, for any

,

\Phi(x^{+})+\beta\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(x^{+}\|x_{0})+(\beta+\mu)\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(u\|x^{+})\leq\Phi(u)+\beta\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(u\|x_{0}),

(40)

where
\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(\cdot\|x^{+})
is defined using some suitable
\omega^{\prime}(x^{+})\in\partial\omega(x^{+})
.

Proof
: Define
\varphi(\cdot)=\Phi(\cdot)+\beta\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(\cdot\|x_{0})
. By the optimality condition on
x^{+}
, we have
0\in\partial\varphi(x^{+})
. Since
\Phi(\cdot)-\mu\omega(\cdot)
is convex, we have that
\varphi(\cdot)-(\beta+\mu)\omega(\cdot)
is convex. By
[
4
, Theorem 3.40]
, we have for all

(\beta+\mu)\partial\omega(x)+\partial[\varphi(\cdot)-(\beta+\mu)\omega(\cdot)](x)=\partial\varphi(x).

Then, using that

and rearranging, we have

-(\beta+\mu)\omega^{\prime}(x^{+})\in\partial(\varphi(\cdot)-(\beta+\mu)\omega(\cdot))(x^{+})

(41)

for some
\omega^{\prime}(x^{+})\in\partial\omega(x^{+})
. It follows from the definition of the subdifferential that

\varphi(x^{+})-(\beta+\mu)\omega(x^{+})-(\beta+\mu)\langle\omega^{\prime}(x^{+}),u-x^{+}\rangle\leq\varphi(u)-(\beta+\mu)\omega(u).

(42)

Substituting the definition of
\varphi(\cdot)
and noting

\omega(u)-\omega(x^{+})-\langle\omega^{\prime}(x^{+}),u-x^{+}\rangle=\operatorname{D}_{\omega}(u\|x^{+})

(43)

gives the result.

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:xz_gem}}}{{=}}(h^{\alpha})^{*}(-z_{k})-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|v_{k}-v_{k+1}\|^{2}.

(100)

It thus follows from Lemma
D.2
(b) that

A_{k}\psi^{\alpha}(z_{k})-\frac{\alpha a_{k}^{2}}{2\tau_{k+1}}\|v_{k}-v_{k+1}\|^{2}=A_{k}\left[\psi^{\alpha}(z_{k})-\frac{\alpha}{2}\|v_{k}-v_{k+1}\|^{2}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:hlam_smooth}}}{{\geq}}A_{k}\left[f^{*}(z_{k})+\ell_{(h^{\alpha})^{*}}(z_{k};z_{k+1})\right].

The following lemma provides a convenient algebraic identity resulting from the definition of the extrapolation point
\hat{v}_{k}
.

Lemma D.6
.

For every
k\geq 0
, define

s_{k}:=a_{k}(v_{k+1}-v_{k})

(101)

with
s_{-1}:=0
. Then, for all
g\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}\langle v_{i+1}-\hat{v}_{i},g_{i+1}-g\rangle=\langle s_{k-1},g_{k}-g\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\langle s_{i-1},g_{i+1}-g_{i}\rangle.

(102)

Proof
: By the definitions of
s_{k}
and
\hat{v}_{k}
in (
101
) and (
25
), respectively, we have

s_{k}-s_{k-1}=a_{k}(v_{k+1}-v_{k})-a_{k-1}(v_{k}-v_{k-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:gem_hatx}}}{{=}}a_{k}(v_{k+1}-\hat{v}_{k}).

Then, summing from
0
to
k-1
and using
s_{-1}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}

\displaystyle\langle v_{i+1}-\hat{v}_{i},g_{i+1}-g\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\langle s_{i},g_{i+1}-g\rangle-\langle s_{i-1},g_{i+1}-g\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\langle s_{k-1},g\rangle+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\langle s_{i}-s_{i-1},g_{i+1}\rangle=\langle s_{k-1},g_{k}-g\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\langle s_{i-1},g_{i+1}-g_{i}\rangle.

We therefore prove the claim.
