On the Cauchy problem for the multi-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system: Global strong solutions with arbitrarily large initial data
Xiangdi Huang, Muxi Lei, Huitao Zhou a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China;

Abstract
Since the pioneering work of Korteweg (1901) and the subsequent refinement of capillary fluid models by Dunn and Serrin (1985), the global existence of strong solutions to the multi-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes–Korteweg (NSK) system with arbitrarily large initial data has stood as a formidable open problem in fluid mechanics. This challenge was recently addressed by [Gu-Huang-Meng-Zhou, arXiv:2603.11762], who established the global existence of strong solutions for arbitrarily large initial data on the periodic domain 𝕋 N \mathbb{T}^{N} ( N = 2 , 3 N=2,3 ), provided that the viscosity coefficients satisfy a BD-type algebraic relation ( μ ⁡ ( ρ ) = ν ​ ρ α , λ ⁡ ( ρ ) = 2 ​ ν ​ ( α − 1 ) ​ ρ α \mu(\rho)=\nu\rho^{\alpha},\lambda(\rho)=2\nu(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha} ) and the Korteweg stress tensor complies with a generalized Bohm identity ( κ ⁡ ( ρ ) = ε 2 ​ α 2 ​ ρ 2 ​ α − 3 \kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3} ). However, the existence of global strong solutions for the Cauchy problem under these conditions has remained an open question. In this paper, we resolve this problem by proving the global existence of strong solutions for the Cauchy problem ( ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , N = 2 , 3 N=2,3 ) with arbitrarily large initial data and non-vacuum far-field density. By employing a refined truncation analysis combined with an original modified Nash-Moser type iteration scheme, we overcome the difficulties arising from the lack of integrability for the density in the whole space. This result extends the large-data theory of compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg equations from bounded torus 𝕋 N \mathbb{T}^{N} to unbounded whole space ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , thus applicable to more general physical settings. Keywords: compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system; density-dependent viscosity; global strong solutions; large initial data; Cauchy problem. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020): 35D35; 35Q30; 35Q35; 35Q40; 76N10.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem for the Navier–Stokes–Korteweg system in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N=2,3
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&(\rho u)_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla\rho^{\gamma}=\operatorname{div}\bigl(2\mu(\rho)\mathbb{D}u\bigr)+\nabla\bigl(\lambda(\rho)\operatorname{div}u\bigr)+\operatorname{div}\mathbb{K},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
\gamma\geq 1
, and
\rho=\rho(x,t)
,
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})^{\top}=u(x,t)
and
P=\rho^{\gamma}(\gamma\geq 1)
denote the fluid density, velocity and pressure, respectively. Moreover,

\mathbb{D}u=\frac{\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}}{2},

is the deformation tensor. The Korteweg stress tensor
\mathbb{K}
is defined by

\mathbb{K}=\Bigl(\rho\operatorname{div}\bigl(\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho\bigr)-\frac{\rho\kappa^{\prime}(\rho)-\kappa(\rho)}{2}\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Bigr)I-\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho,

(2)

where
I
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix. A direct computation yields

\operatorname{div}\mathbb{K}=\nabla\Bigl(\rho\kappa(\rho)\Delta\rho+\frac{\kappa(\rho)+\rho\kappa^{\prime}(\rho)}{2}\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Bigr)-\operatorname{div}\bigl(\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho\bigr).

(3)

System (
1
) is based on the capillarity model originally formulated by Korteweg
[
17
]
, where the stress tensor is augmented with density gradient terms. This formulation was subsequently modernized and generalized by Dunn and Serrin
[
9
]
. The well-posedness of this equation has received extensive attention recently, leading to several important breakthroughs.

With the choice of viscosity and capillarity coefficients satisfying

\mu(\rho)=\nu_{0}\rho,\quad\lambda(\rho)=0,\quad\kappa(\rho)=\frac{\kappa_{0}^{2}}{\rho},

(4)

the compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg equations degenerate into the quantum Navier-Stokes system. In the one-dimensional setting, some advancements have been achieved concerning the well-posedness of the system. For the case where the viscosity and capillarity coefficients are balanced (
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}
), Jüngel
[
15
]
established the global existence of smooth solutions in the whole space
\mathbb{R}
, provided the initial density is away from vacuum. Recently, this result was extended by Chen and Zhao
[
7
]
to a broader regime where
\nu_{0}\geq\kappa_{0}
and
\gamma\geq 1
. They obtained the global classical solutions and further investigated the large-time behavior of these solutions in the absence of vacuum. For the multi-dimensional case, the exploration of global-in-time solutions has primarily focused on weak or renormalized solutions, particularly when vacuum states are involved. In the periodic setting
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{N}
, Jüngel
[
16
]
first established the global existence of weak solutions allowing vacuum for the case
\nu_{0}<\kappa_{0}
, under the technical constraints
\gamma\geq 1
for
N=2
and
\gamma>3
for
N=3
. Subsequently, Antonelli and Spirito
[
3
]
constructed global weak solutions in the absence of vacuum for various regimes of
\nu_{0}
and
\kappa_{0}
, providing a standard framework for
N=2,3
. Later, Lacroix-Violet and Vasseur
[
18
]
proved the existence of global renormalized solutions for
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{N}
with
\gamma>1
and
\kappa_{0}\geq 0
, a result that allowed vacuum. More recently, the focus has shifted toward the Cauchy problem in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Notably, Antonelli, Hientzsch and Spirito
[
2
]
obtained global weak solutions allowing vacuum for the multi-dimensional system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, provided that the viscosity and capillarity coefficients satisfy
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}
. However, the global-in-time existence of strong solutions to the multi-dimensional generalized NSK system remained a long-standing open problem. This challenge was first addressed by Huang, Meng and Zhang
[
14
]
in the periodic setting
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. They established the global existence of strong solutions away from vacuum for the case
\nu_{0}=\kappa_{0}
, covering the adiabatic exponent range
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
for
N=2
and
\gamma\in[1,7/3)
for
N=3
. Subsequently, by introducing a truncation mechanism, Huang, Gu and Lei
[
13
]
successfully extended these results to the Cauchy problem in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
in the absence of vacuum, while further improving the range of
\gamma
to
[1,8/3)
in the three-dimensional case.

However, the situation becomes much more delicate when the system (
1
) is considered with general coefficient structure. For the one-dimensional space case, the theory has been relatively fruitful. Tsyganov
[
19
]
proved the global existence and asymptotic behaviors of weak solutions with large data away from vacuum. For systems with density-dependent coefficients, Germain and LeFloch
[
11
]
established the global existence of finite energy weak solutions for the Cauchy problem and their convergence toward entropy solutions of the Euler system. Furthermore, Burtea and Haspot
[
6
]
explored the capillarity vanishing limit, while Antonelli, Bresch and Spirito
[
1
]
obtained global weak solutions for the periodic problem with large data under certain conditions on
\mu(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha}
and
\kappa(\rho)=\rho^{\beta}
. However, when moving to high-dimensional problems, the available results for the general NSK system are much more constrained. For density-dependent cases, Danchin and Desjardins
[
8
]
obtained global smooth solutions only for data close to a stable equilibrium. A major milestone for weak solutions was achieved by Bresch, Desjardins and Lin
[
4
]
, who established the global existence of weak solutions allowing for vacuum by introducing a novel a priori entropy estimate (the BD entropy). This framework was further extended to viscous shallow water models with capillarity
[
5
]
. More recently, Haspot
[
12
]
investigated both local and global strong solutions for various density-dependent coefficients, yet these results still largely rely on smallness assumptions on the initial data. The transition from the ’balanced’ quantum coefficients to general coefficients often results in the loss of certain analytical structures (such as the effective velocity), making the global existence of strong solutions with large data an even more demanding problem. Recently, a groundbreaking advancement was achieved by Gu, Huang, Meng and
Zhou
[
10
]
, who resolved the long-standing open problem regarding the global existence of strong solutions for the multi-dimensional NSK model with general coefficients. In their work, they provided an affirmative answer to this challenge by considering a generalized algebraic structure for the coefficients. Specifically, the authors operated under the assumption that the viscosity coefficients satisfy a BD-type algebraic relation of the form
\mu(\rho)=\nu\rho^{\alpha}
and
\lambda(\rho)=2\nu(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha}
. Furthermore, they assumed that the Korteweg stress tensor complies with a generalized Bohm identity of the form
\kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3}
. Under these structural conditions, they successfully established the global existence of strong solutions for the 2D and 3D systems on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
with arbitrarily large regular initial data.

Despite the aforementioned progress in establishing the global existence of strong solutions with arbitrarily large initial data in the periodic domain for a broad class of general NSK coefficients, the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem remains a more fundamental and pressing issue. The primary mathematical challenges arise from the unbounded measure of the whole space and the non-vanishing density at infinity, which lead to severe difficulties regarding the integrability of the solutions. Addressing these critical issues in the context of the Cauchy problem is the central objective of the present paper.

Throughout the paper, we work under the following structural assumptions on the coefficients:

\mu(\rho)=\nu\rho^{\alpha},\qquad\lambda(\rho)=2\nu(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha},\qquad\kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3},\qquad\nu\geq\varepsilon>0.

(5)

In particular,

\lambda(\rho)=2\rho\mu^{\prime}(\rho)-2\mu(\rho),

so that the viscosity coefficients satisfy the BD algebraic relation. Moreover,

2\mu(\rho)+N\lambda(\rho)=2\nu\bigl(N\alpha-N+1\bigr)\rho^{\alpha}.

Hence the physical constraints

\mu(\rho)\geq 0,\qquad 2\mu(\rho)+N\lambda(\rho)\geq 0

require that

\alpha\geq\frac{N-1}{N}.

To focus on the essential difficulties, we prescribe the far-field equilibrium state

\rho(x,t)\to 1,\qquad u(x,t)\to 0,\qquad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.

(6)

The initial data are given by

\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\qquad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(7)

and are assumed to satisfy

0<\underline{\rho_{0}}\leq\rho_{0}\leq\overline{\rho_{0}},\qquad\rho_{0}-1\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(8)

where
\underline{\rho_{0}}
and
\overline{\rho_{0}}
are positive constants.

For later use, we introduce

\beta=\sqrt{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\nu^{2}}}\in[0,1).

(9)

Assumption 1.1

(Parameter conditions)
.

Assume that
(\alpha,\gamma,\beta)
satisfies one of the following conditions:

\displaystyle N=2,\quad\alpha\in\left(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},1\right),\quad\gamma\in[1,\infty),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta_{2}^{+}(\alpha));

(10)

or

N=3,\quad\alpha\in\left(\frac{9\sqrt{3}-4\sqrt{2}}{9\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{2}},1\right),\quad\gamma\in\left[1,\frac{15\alpha-7}{3}\right),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta_{3}^{+}(\alpha)).

(11)

Here,
\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)

(N=2,3)
is defined as the unique positive solution to the quadratic equation:

\left(\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)\right)^{2}+\frac{2(1-\alpha)(N\alpha^{2}+\sqrt{N}\alpha+1-N)}{\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}}\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)+\frac{(1-\alpha)(-N\alpha^{2}-N\alpha+2N-2)}{\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}}=0.

We now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\in\{2,3\}
and assume that Assumption
1.1
holds. Then the Cauchy problem (
1
), (
5
)-(
8
) admits a unique global strong solution
(\rho,u)
. More precisely, for any
0<T<\infty
, there exists a constant
C(T)>0
such that

(C(T))^{-1}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq C(T)\qquad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T],

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho-1\in C([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),\\
&\rho_{t}\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),\\
&u\in C([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})),\\
&u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})).\end{aligned}\right.

Here the constant
C(T)
depends only on
T
, the parameters of the system, and the norms of the initial data.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
can be regarded as the extension of the periodic domain results Gu, Huang, Meng and
Zhou
[
10
]
to the whole space, achieving the same range of indices. In comparison, obtaining the same indices for
\alpha
and
\gamma
in the whole space entails significantly more challenges, primarily due to the far-field behavior at infinity and the lack of compactness of the domain. However, according to the
\gamma
-range
\gamma\in[1,8/3)
for the quantum NSK system established by Huang, Gu and Lei
[
13
]
, we observe that the limit of
(15\alpha-7)/3
as
\alpha\to 1
is precisely
8/3
. This provides strong justification for the conjecture that
\gamma
can reach the same range as in the periodic case.

Remark 1.2
.

For
N=2
, the lower bound of
\alpha
arises from the constraints required to establish the density lower bound. In contrast, for
N=3
, the lower bound of
\alpha
is dictated by the requirements of higher-order estimates, which impose more stringent restrictions than those stemming from the density lower bound. In the specific case where
\alpha=1

[
13
]
.

2.2
Reformulation

Before deriving the a priori estimates, we recast (
1
) in terms of an effective velocity, following the arguments presented in the appendix of
[
10
]
. Set

c_{\pm}=\nu(1\pm\beta)=\nu\pm\sqrt{\nu^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad v_{\pm}=u+c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho.

By (
5
) and (
9
), the constants
c_{\pm}
satisfy

c_{\pm}^{2}-2\nu c_{\pm}+\varepsilon^{2}=0.

A straightforward computation based on (
3
) and (
1
) shows that
(\rho,u)
solves (
1
) if and only if
(\rho,v_{\pm})
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho v_{\pm})-c_{\pm}\Delta\rho^{\alpha}=0,\\
&\rho(v_{\pm})_{t}+\rho\bigl(v_{\pm}-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\bigr)\cdot\nabla v_{\pm}+\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\\
&\qquad=\nu\operatorname{div}(\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v_{\pm})+(\nu-c_{\pm})\operatorname{div}\bigl(\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v_{\pm})^{\top}\bigr)+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c_{\pm})\nabla\bigl(\rho^{\alpha}\operatorname{div}v_{\pm}\bigr).\end{aligned}\right.

(12)

Moreover, once
(\rho,v_{\pm})
is known, the original velocity is recovered uniquely from

u=v_{\pm}-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho.

(13)

In the rest of the paper we work with the positive branch and write

v:=v_{+},\qquad c:=c_{+}=\nu(1+\beta).

Then (
12
) becomes

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho v)-c\Delta\rho^{\alpha}=0,\\
&\rho v_{t}+\rho\bigl(v-c\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\bigr)\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\\
&\qquad=\nu\operatorname{div}(\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v)+(\nu-c)\operatorname{div}\bigl(\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{\top}\bigr)+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\nabla\bigl(\rho^{\alpha}\operatorname{div}v\bigr).\end{aligned}\right.

(14)

Observe that

c-\nu=\nu\beta,\qquad 2\nu-c=\nu(1-\beta)>0.

We next translate the initial conditions for (
1
) into those for (
14
). Since
N\in\{2,3\}
, the Sobolev embedding

H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

combined with (

v_{0}:=u_{0}+c\alpha\rho_{0}^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Hence the initial data for (
14

0<\underline{\rho_{0}}\leq\rho_{0}\leq\overline{\rho_{0}},\qquad\rho_{0}-1\in H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad v_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(15)

where
\underline{\rho_{0}}
and
\overline{\rho_{0}}
are the positive constants appearing in (
8

\rho(x,t)\to 1,\qquad v(x,t)\to 0,\qquad\text{as }|x|\to\infty.

(16)

2.3
Outline of the Proof

We now present the outline of the proof for the main results. The strategy is divided into the following key steps:

•

Local Well-posedness:
First, we establish the local-in-time existence of solutions to the Cauchy problem. Since this process is relatively standard for such systems, the details are omitted for brevity.

•

Integrability of Momentum:
Second, as a key to improving the integrability of density, we derive the integrability estimates for
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx
. Given a fixed value of
\alpha
, obtaining these estimates involves determining the range of the critical index
\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)
(for
N=2,3
). In particular, the three-dimensional case necessitates certain restrictions on the range of the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
.

•

Upper Bound of Density:
Third, equipped with the integrability of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx
for
q>N-2
, we apply a carefully designed new Nash-Moser iteration to the density equation to establish a uniform upper bound for the density. It is worth noting that as
q
approaches
N-2
, the permissible lower bound for
\alpha
becomes broader.

•

Lower Bound of Density:
Fourth, by leveraging the newly established density upper bound, we can refine the integrability of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx
. Re-applying another refined Nash-Moser iteration then allows us to derive a strictly positive lower bound for the density, thereby ruling out vacuum formation.

•

Global Existence:
Finally, with the uniform upper and lower bounds of density, we obtain the remaining higher-order estimates. By employing a contradiction argument regarding the maximal existence time
T^{*}
, we conclude the existence of a global-in-time strong solution.

In the periodic case, the methods for estimating the upper and lower bounds of the density rely heavily on the integrability of the density and the finite measure of the domain, thus completely inapplicable to the whole space. The improvements achieved in this work primarily stem from four technical advancements:

§2.3.1 Piecewise analysis of
\gamma
:
We implement a piecewise investigation of the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
to achieve a
\gamma
-improvement
for the weighted integrability of the effective velocity.

§2.3.2 Truncation procedure for the whole space:
We develop a
truncation procedure
specifically adapted to the whole-space setting, which effectively addresses the difficulties arising from the lack of boundary constraints and compactness.

§2.3.3 Refined Nash-Moser iteration for the lower bound of density:

Establishing the density lower bound is more technically demanding than obtaining the upper bound. For the lower bound analysis, we must construct suitable reverse Hölder inequalities for the equation governing the density’s lower bound and guarantee that the iteration converges.

§2.3.4 Decoupling of the strong coupling in the first-level higher-order estimates:
This decoupling method is particularly evident in the case
N=3
. Using the dissipation generated by the estimate of
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}
as an auxiliary source of damping, we establish a large-scale Gronwall inequality involving
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}
,
\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
, and
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}
.

2.3.1
Piecewise analysis of
\gamma
when
N=3

Prior to establishing the upper bound of the density, it is necessary to obtain the density-weighted estimates for the effective velocity. Testing the momentum equation in (
14
) by
|v|^{q}v
yields

\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx+\delta_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq C\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|.

For different ranges of
\gamma
, we employ distinct sets of known integrability conditions to establish a closed-loop weighted estimate for the effective velocity. Under Assumption
1.1
, the following two cases are sufficient to cover the entire range of the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
.

•

Case I:
\gamma\in[\frac{3}{2}\alpha-\frac{1}{6},\frac{15\alpha-7}{3})
. In this case, we choose
1<q<2
such that the following condition is satisfied:

\frac{3}{2}\alpha-\frac{1}{6}\leq\gamma<3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{q+2}.

In this step, we exploit the integrability condition

\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}-1\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C,

and by splitting the pressure term according to the domain decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\Omega_{1}(t)\cup\Omega_{2}(t)
, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|,

where the disjoint subsets
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
are defined as

\Omega_{1}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)\leq 4\},\quad\Omega_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)>4\}.

(17)

We establish the density-weighted integrability of the effective velocity within this
\gamma
-interval.

•

Case II:
\gamma\in[1,\frac{9}{2}\alpha-2)
. In this case, the main challenge arises from the high-density region
\Omega_{2}(t)
. By defining
\zeta^{\prime}:=\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}
and employing interpolation techniques, we utilize an additional integrability condition

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}dt\leq C,

to establish the density-weighted integrability of the effective velocity within this
\gamma
-range.

2.3.2
Truncation procedure for the whole space

Since
\rho\to 1
at infinity, one cannot iterate directly on
\rho
or
\rho^{-1}
. Therefore, we introduce suitable truncations of the large-density and low-density parts and performs a modified Nash–Moser iteration only on these truncated quantities. The relative entropy estimate guarantees that their supports have finite measure, which makes the iteration possible. For example, when establishing the upper bound of the density, we choose
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta(s)=0\ \text{for }0\leq s\leq 2,\qquad\eta(s)=1\ \text{for }s\geq 4,

and define

f:=\eta(\rho)\rho.

By testing against appropriately chosen smooth-truncated density functions, we focus on improving the
L^{p}
integrability of the density within the high-density region, which is also a domain of finite measure. Through a refined Nash-Moser iteration technique, we successfully establish the upper bound for the density.

2.3.3
Refined Nash-Moser iteration for the lower bound of density

The lower bound of the density is the most delicate part of the whole-space analysis and the point at which the argument departs decisively from the periodic theory. The difficulty is not simply to iterate on the inverse density. Once one passes to
\tau:=\rho^{-1}
, the low-density regime becomes singular, while the transport generated by the effective velocity remains fully nonlinear. On
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where no background integrability for
\tau-1
is available, a direct iteration on
\rho^{-1}
cannot be closed.

The argument begins with a truncation of the inverse density,
f:=\eta(\tau)\tau
,
which localizes the analysis to the vacuum region. Since
\rho\to 1
at infinity, the relevant obstruction is the concentration of
\tau
near vacuum rather than its behavior at spatial infinity. The relative entropy bound then yields the crucial support control: the truncated inverse density is supported on a set of uniformly finite measure in time.

This geometric input must be combined with the refined weighted estimate for the effective velocity, available only after the upper bound of the density has been established. The lower bound is therefore not a formal consequence of Nash–Moser iteration alone. What closes the argument is a carefully designed new Nash–Moser scheme for the truncated inverse density, stable in a singular regime where diffusion and transport remain strongly coupled.

The outcome is a strictly positive lower bound for the density on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
. This is the decisive step in the whole-space theory: it excludes vacuum formation and makes the global higher-order analysis possible.

2.3.4
Decoupling of the strong coupling in the first-level higher-order estimates

To establish the first-level higher-order estimates, we construct a large-scale Gronwall inequality of the form:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla^{2}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle+\left(\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||\nabla\rho||\nabla^{2}\rho|\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq\mathcal{R}(t).

Specifically, the dissipation term
\||\nabla\rho||\nabla^{2}\rho|\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
generated by the estimate of
\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
plays a crucial role in canceling the nonlinear terms on the right-hand side. The constant
\frac{9\sqrt{3}-4\sqrt{2}}{9\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{2}}
in Assumption
1.1
is precisely the threshold required to ensure that
\||\nabla\rho||\nabla^{2}\rho|\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
provides the necessary positive dissipation. By coupling these three independent estimates, we establish a comprehensive, closed-loop Gronwall inequality. It is worth noting that without the inclusion of this specific dissipation, the system involving only
\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}
remains strongly coupled and cannot be decoupled. This represents the core technical difficulty encountered in establishing the higher-order estimates. It is precisely what prevents the three-dimensional strong coupling from propagating to higher levels and makes the global higher-order theory available.

Proposition 3.1
.

C=C(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0})

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\rho|u|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)+\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C.

(24)

Proof.

Multiply the momentum equation in (
1
) by
u
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. By the continuity equation,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl((\rho u)_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho u\otimes u)\Bigr)\cdot u\,dx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}\,dx.

Using (
20

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot u\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho\nabla\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\cdot u\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\operatorname{div}(\rho u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho_{t}\,dx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi(\rho)\,dx.

Furthermore, by (
5
) and the identity

\mathbb{D}u:\nabla u=|\mathbb{D}u|^{2},

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{div}\bigl(2\mu(\rho)\mathbb{D}u\bigr)\cdot u\,dx=-2\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\,dx,

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\bigl(\lambda(\rho)\operatorname{div}u\bigr)\cdot u\,dx=-2\nu(\alpha-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}(\operatorname{div}u)^{2}\,dx.

For the Korteweg term, it follows from (
22

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{div}\mathbb{K}\cdot u\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\,\operatorname{div}(\rho u)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\,\rho_{t}\,dx.

Noting that

\partial_{t}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2\alpha-1}{2}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\rho_{t},

we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{div}\mathbb{K}\cdot u\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\,\partial_{t}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\,dx.

Collecting the above identities yields (
23
).

Integrating (
23
) over
(0,t)
and using (
21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right)(x,t)\,dx

\displaystyle+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}\Bigl(|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}+(\alpha-1)(\operatorname{div}u)^{2}\Bigr)\,dx\,ds=E_{0}.

On the other hand, since

it follows that

Because

, the coefficient

is strictly positive. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle+2\nu(N\alpha-N+1)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq E_{0}.

Absorbing the fixed positive coefficients into the constant gives (
24

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
N\in\{2,3\}
, let
\gamma\geq 1
, and assume that

\alpha\in\left(\frac{N-1}{N},\,1\right).

Let
\Pi(\rho)
and
E_{0}
be defined by (
19
) and (
21
), respectively. Let
(\rho,v)
be a sufficiently smooth solution to (
14
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]
such that
\rho>0
and the far-field condition (
16
) holds. Then, for every
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)\right)(x,t)\,dx+c\gamma\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx\,ds

(25)

\displaystyle+\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,ds\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\Pi(\rho_{0})\right)\,dx,

where

\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}:=(2\nu-c)(N\alpha-N+1)=\nu(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)>0.

(26)

Consequently, there exists a positive constant

C=C(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0})

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C.

(27)

More precisely, one may take

C=\frac{2}{1-\beta}\max\left\{2,\,\frac{1}{c\gamma\alpha},\,\frac{1}{\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}}\right\}E_{0}.

(28)

Proof.

Since

\rho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho u)=0,

(29)

we test the momentum equation in (
14
) against
v
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Owing to (
29
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(\rho v_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla v\bigr)\cdot v\,dx=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx.

On the other hand, by (
20
) and the first equation in (
14

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi(\rho)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho_{t}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\operatorname{div}(\rho v)\,dx+c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho v\cdot\nabla\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\,dx-c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\Pi^{\prime}(\rho)\cdot\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\cdot\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\,dx-c\gamma\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Integrating by parts in the viscous terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)\right)\,dx+c\gamma\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}Q(\nabla v)\,dx=0,

where

Q(A):=\nu|A|^{2}+(\nu-c)A:A^{\top}+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)(\operatorname{tr}A)^{2}.

We now estimate the quadratic form
Q
. For any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, set

A_{s}:=\frac{A+A^{\top}}{2},\qquad A_{a}:=\frac{A-A^{\top}}{2},\qquad A_{0}:=A_{s}-\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{N}I.

Then

A=A_{a}+A_{0}+\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{N}I,\qquad|A|^{2}=|A_{a}|^{2}+|A_{0}|^{2}+\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^{2}}{N},

and

A:A^{\top}=-|A_{a}|^{2}+|A_{0}|^{2}+\frac{(\operatorname{tr}A)^{2}}{N}.

Hence

\displaystyle Q(A)

\displaystyle=c|A_{a}|^{2}+(2\nu-c)|A_{0}|^{2}+\frac{(2\nu-c)(N\alpha-N+1)}{N}(\operatorname{tr}A)^{2}.

Since

0<N\alpha-N+1<1,\qquad 2\nu-c=\nu(1-\beta)>0,\qquad c=\nu(1+\beta)\geq 2\nu-c,

it follows that

Q(A)\geq\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}|A|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }A\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},

with
\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}
given by (
26

, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\Pi(\rho)\right)\,dx+c\gamma\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx+\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

Integrating this inequality over
(0,t)
yields (
25

It remains to estimate the initial term on the right-hand side. Set

w_{0}:=c\alpha\rho_{0}^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho_{0},\qquad v_{0}=u_{0}+w_{0}.

For any
\theta>0
, Young’s inequality gives

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\leq(1+\theta)\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+(1+\theta^{-1})\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|w_{0}|^{2}.

\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|w_{0}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}c^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho_{0}^{2\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho_{0}|^{2}=\frac{c^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\left|\nabla\rho_{0}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}.

we infer that

1+\theta=(1+\theta^{-1})\frac{c^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{2}{1-\beta}.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{2}{1-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\left|\nabla\rho_{0}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\Pi(\rho_{0})\right)\,dx\leq\frac{2}{1-\beta}E_{0}.

Finally, we derive (

) and (

\Pi(\rho)\geq 0
by (

then (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{2}(x,t)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle\leq M\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}+\Pi(\rho_{0})\right)\,dx\leq\frac{2M}{1-\beta}E_{0}.

Taking the supremum over

completes the proof.

For later use, we record two elementary estimates for the relative internal energy density
\Pi(\rho)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Pi(\rho)
be defined by (
19
). Then there exist positive constants

c_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma)>0,\qquad C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma)>0,

depending only on
\gamma
, such that for every
\rho\in[0,4]
,

c_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma)(\rho-1)^{2}\leq\Pi(\rho)\leq C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma)(\rho-1)^{2}.

(30)

Proof.

Define

H_{\gamma}(\rho):=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\Pi(\rho)}{(\rho-1)^{2}},&\rho\neq 1,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{1}{2}\Pi^{\prime\prime}(1),&\rho=1.\end{cases}

Clearly,
H_{\gamma}
is continuous on
[0,4]\setminus\{1\}
. Moreover, by Taylor’s theorem,

\Pi(\rho)=\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{\prime\prime}(1)(\rho-1)^{2}+o\bigl((\rho-1)^{2}\bigr)\qquad(\rho\to 1),

and hence
H_{\gamma}
is also continuous at
\rho=1
. Therefore,

H_{\gamma}\in C([0,4]).

On the other hand,

\Pi^{\prime\prime}(\rho)=\begin{cases}\gamma\rho^{\gamma-2},&\gamma>1,\\[2.84526pt]
\dfrac{1}{\rho},&\gamma=1,\end{cases}\qquad\rho>0,

so
\Pi
is strictly convex on
(0,\infty)
. Since

\Pi(1)=0,\qquad\Pi^{\prime}(1)=0,

it follows that
\rho=1
is the unique minimizer of
\Pi
on
(0,\infty)
. Noting also that

\Pi(0)=1

(with the usual convention
0\log 0=0
when
\gamma=1
), we obtain

\Pi(\rho)>0\qquad\text{for all }\rho\in[0,4]\setminus\{1\}.

Since

H_{\gamma}(1)=\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{\prime\prime}(1)>0,

we conclude that

H_{\gamma}(\rho)>0\qquad\text{for all }\rho\in[0,4].

By the compactness of
[0,4]
, we may define

c_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma):=\min_{0\leq\rho\leq 4}H_{\gamma}(\rho)>0,\qquad C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\gamma):=\max_{0\leq\rho\leq 4}H_{\gamma}(\rho)<\infty.

This gives (
30
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Pi(\rho)
be defined by (
19
). Then, for every
\rho\geq 4
,

\Pi(\rho)\geq c_{\mathrm{high}}(\gamma)\rho^{\gamma},

(31)

where

c_{\mathrm{high}}(\gamma):=\frac{\Pi(4)}{4^{\gamma}}=\begin{cases}\log 4-\dfrac{3}{4},&\gamma=1,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{4^{\gamma}-1-3\gamma}{(\gamma-1)4^{\gamma}},&\gamma>1.\end{cases}

(32)

In particular, for every measurable set

A\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho(x)\geq 4\},

one has

\int_{A}\rho^{\gamma}\,dx\leq c_{\mathrm{high}}(\gamma)^{-1}\int_{A}\Pi(\rho)\,dx.

(33)

Proof.

For
\rho>0
, define

G_{\gamma}(\rho):=\frac{\Pi(\rho)}{\rho^{\gamma}},

where, in the case
\gamma=1
, this simply means
G_{1}(\rho)=\Pi(\rho)/\rho
. A direct computation from (
19
) shows that, in both cases
\gamma=1
and
\gamma>1
,

G_{\gamma}^{\prime}(\rho)=\gamma\rho^{-\gamma-1}(\rho-1),\qquad\rho>0.

Hence
G_{\gamma}
is strictly increasing on
[1,\infty)
. Therefore, for every
\rho\geq 4
,

\frac{\Pi(\rho)}{\rho^{\gamma}}=G_{\gamma}(\rho)\geq G_{\gamma}(4)=\frac{\Pi(4)}{4^{\gamma}}=c_{\mathrm{high}}(\gamma),

which proves (
31
). Formula (
32
) follows immediately from (
19
), and moreover

c_{\mathrm{high}}(\gamma)=G_{\gamma}(4)>0.

Finally, integrating (
31
) over any measurable set

A\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho(x)\geq 4\}

yields (
33
).
∎

Proof.

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\rho^{m}\right|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}E_{0}=\frac{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}{2\nu^{2}(1-\beta^{2})\alpha^{2}}E_{0},

On the other hand, on

\rho\geq 4
. Since

2m=2\alpha-1<1\leq\gamma,

it follows that

|\rho^{m}-1|^{2}\leq\rho^{2m}\leq 4^{\,2m-\gamma}\rho^{\gamma}.

Therefore, by Lemma
3.2
and (
35
),

\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}|\rho^{m}-1|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}\,dx\leq C\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\Pi(\rho)\,dx\leq CE_{0}.

Combining the estimates on
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\Omega_{2}(t)
, we obtain

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq CE_{0}.

Together with (
36
), this yields

\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq CE_{0}^{1/2},

which proves (
34
).

For later use, we also record that
\Pi
is increasing on
[1,\infty)
, and hence, on
\Omega_{2}(t)
,

\Pi(\rho)\geq\Pi(4).

Therefore,

|\Omega_{2}(t)|\leq\Pi(4)^{-1}\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\Pi(\rho)\,dx\leq\Pi(4)^{-1}E_{0}.

(37)

∎

In the three-dimensional case, we next derive a higher-order spacetime estimate for suitable powers of the density. Combined later with Proposition
3.3
, this bound will provide the regularity input needed for the derivation of the uniform upper bound in the next subsection.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
N=3
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T]
such that
\rho>0
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
).
Then there exists a positive constant

C=C(\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0})

such that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}}\right|^{4}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C.

(38)

Proof.

All integrations by parts below are justified by the smoothness of the solution together with the far-field conditions (
6
) and (
16
). Throughout the proof,
C
denotes a positive constant depending only on
\alpha
,
\nu
,
\varepsilon
, and
E_{0}
.

Set

\phi:=\rho^{\alpha-1},\qquad\theta:=\frac{c\alpha}{1-\alpha}.

By (
13
),

u=v+\theta\nabla\phi,\qquad\nabla v=\nabla u-\theta\nabla^{2}\phi.

Since
\nabla^{2}\phi
is symmetric, we have

\nabla u:\nabla^{2}\phi=\mathbb{D}u:\nabla^{2}\phi.

Hence

\displaystyle\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}+\theta^{2}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}-2\theta\rho^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}u:\nabla^{2}\phi

(39)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\theta^{2}}{2}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}-2\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2},

where we used Young’s inequality in the last step. Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

By (
24
) and (
27
),

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C.

Therefore,

M:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C.

We now convert this weighted Hessian bound into (
38
). Set

w:=\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1},\qquad z:=\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}}.

Since

\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla w=\frac{3\alpha-2}{2(\alpha-1)}\nabla\phi,

we infer that

\left(\frac{3\alpha-2}{2(1-\alpha)}\right)^{2}M=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}\left|\nabla\!\left(\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla w\right)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Expanding the square gives

\left(\frac{3\alpha-2}{2(1-\alpha)}\right)^{2}M=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}\,dx\,dt+A+I,

where

A:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}\left|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right|^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\,dt,

and

I:=2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\nabla^{2}w\cdot\nabla w\,dx\,dt.

A direct computation yields

A=\frac{16\alpha^{2}}{(3\alpha-2)^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla z|^{4}\,dx\,dt.

It remains to estimate the cross term
I
. Since

\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho,

an integration by parts gives

I=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname{div}(\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho)\,|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname{div}(\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla\phi)\,|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Moreover,

\nabla\rho^{1-\alpha}\cdot\nabla\phi=-(\alpha-1)^{2}\rho^{-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\leq 0.

Since
\alpha<1
, the contribution of this term is nonnegative, and therefore

I\geq\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{1-\alpha}\Delta\phi\,|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Hence, for any
\delta>0
, Young’s inequality yields

I\geq-C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{1-\alpha}|\Delta\phi|\,|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx\,dt

and

\rho^{1-\alpha}|\Delta\phi|\,|\nabla w|^{2}\leq\delta\,\rho^{\alpha}|\Delta\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\delta}\rho^{2-3\alpha}|\nabla w|^{4}.

In dimension three,

|\Delta\phi|^{2}\leq 3|\nabla^{2}\phi|^{2},

while

\rho^{2-3\alpha}|\nabla w|^{4}=\frac{(3\alpha-2)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\rho^{\alpha}\left|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right|^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}.

Consequently,

I\geq-C_{\alpha}\delta\,M-\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\delta}\,A,

where
C_{\alpha}>0
depends only on
\alpha
. Choosing
\delta=\delta(\alpha)
so that

\frac{C_{\alpha}}{\delta}=\frac{1}{2},

we obtain

I\geq-C_{\alpha}M-\frac{1}{2}A.

Substituting this estimate into the preceding expansion, we arrive at

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}A\leq C_{\alpha}M\leq C.

Recalling the expression for
A
and the definitions

w=\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1},\qquad z=\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}},

we conclude that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}}\right|^{4}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C,

which is exactly (
38
). The proof is complete.
∎

As a direct consequence of Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
, we obtain the following spacetime
H^{2}
-estimate for
\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}-1
in dimension three.

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume that
N=3
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
).
Then, for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C(\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\beta,E_{0},T^{\ast})>0

such that

\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}-1\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

(40)

Proof.

Set

m:=\alpha-\frac{1}{2},\qquad\sigma:=\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1.

Since
N=3
, Assumption
1.1
yields

\alpha\in\left(\frac{2}{3},1\right),

and hence

0<\sigma<m.

Let

\theta:=\frac{\sigma}{m}\in(0,1).

Then
\rho^{\sigma}=(\rho^{m})^{\theta}
, and for every
y\geq 0
one has

|y^{\theta}-1|\leq|y-1|.

Therefore,

|\rho^{\sigma}-1|\leq|\rho^{m}-1|\qquad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).

Combining this with (
34
) in Proposition
3.3
, we obtain

\|\rho^{\sigma}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

On the other hand, Proposition
3.4
gives

\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\sigma}-1)\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Set

f:=\rho^{\sigma}-1.

Then, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
, Plancherel’s theorem yields the standard interpolation inequality

\|\nabla f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla^{2}f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Integrating in time and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}f(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(T^{\ast})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq C.

Moreover,

\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq T^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq(T^{\ast})^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Collecting the above bounds, we conclude that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C,

where
C
depends only on
\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\beta,E_{0}
, and
T^{\ast}
.
Recalling that
f=\rho^{\sigma}-1
and
\sigma=\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1
, we obtain (
40
).
By (
9
), the same dependence may equivalently be expressed in terms of
\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0}
, and
T^{\ast}
.
∎

3.2
Upper bound for the density

To initiate the proof of the upper bound for the density, we first derive a weighted
L^{q+2}
-estimate for the effective velocity
v
. This estimate will be a key ingredient in the argument below. For the sake of narrative convenience, let

q_{N,\ast}:=\frac{4(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)}{\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2}}.

(41)

In fact, under Assumption
1.1
, one has the bound

q_{N,\ast}>2.

Indeed, regarding
q_{N,\ast}
as a function of
\beta
, we write

q_{N,\ast}(\beta)=\frac{4(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)}{\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2}}=\frac{4(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)}{\bigl(\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)+(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))\beta\bigr)^{2}}.

Since Assumption
1.1
implies
\alpha>\frac{N-1}{N}
, we have

N\alpha-N+1>0.

Moreover, Assumption
1.1
also yields
\alpha>1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
, and therefore

\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)<1.

A direct differentiation gives

\frac{d}{d\beta}q_{N,\ast}(\beta)=-\frac{4(N\alpha-N+1)\bigl(2-\beta-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)}{\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{3}}<0\qquad\text{for all }\beta\in[0,1).

Hence
q_{N,\ast}(\beta)
is strictly decreasing with respect to
\beta
.

Next, let

P_{N}(\beta;\alpha):=\beta^{2}+\frac{2(1-\alpha)(N\alpha^{2}+\sqrt{N}\alpha+1-N)}{\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}}\,\beta+\frac{(1-\alpha)(-N\alpha^{2}-N\alpha+2N-2)}{\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}}.

Then, by Assumption
1.1
,
\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)
is the unique positive root of

P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)=0,

and the admissible parameter range is precisely
0\leq\beta<\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)
. Furthermore,

P_{N}(0;\alpha)=\frac{(1-\alpha)(-N\alpha^{2}-N\alpha+2N-2)}{\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}}<0.

Indeed, for
N=2
, the condition
\alpha>\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}
gives

2\alpha^{2}+2\alpha-2>0,

while for
N=3
, the condition

\alpha>\frac{9\sqrt{3}-4\sqrt{2}}{9\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{2}}

implies

3\alpha^{2}+3\alpha-4>0.

Consequently, since
P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)
has leading coefficient
1
, has a unique positive root
\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)
, and satisfies
P_{N}(0;\alpha)<0
, we infer that

P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)<0\qquad\text{for all }\beta\in[0,\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)).

We now use the identity

\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2}\bigl(q_{N,\ast}-2\bigr)=2(2\alpha-1)(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)-\alpha(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha))^{2}P_{N}(\beta;\alpha),

which follows from a straightforward algebraic computation. Under Assumption
1.1
, we have
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
,
1-\beta>0
,
N\alpha-N+1>0
, and
-P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)>0
. Therefore the right-hand side is strictly positive, and thus

q_{N,\ast}-2>0.

This proves that

q_{N,\ast}>2.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
N\in\{2,3\}
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
).

For
N=2
, let
q
be any exponent satisfying

2\leq q<q_{2,\ast}.

(42)

For
N=3
, assume in addition that

1\leq\gamma<\frac{9}{2}\alpha-2,

(43)

and choose
q
such that

2\leq q<q_{3,\ast},\qquad\gamma<3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{q+2}.

(44)

Then, for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C\!\left(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\beta,E_{0},T^{\ast},q,\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)>0

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C.

(45)

Equivalently,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v\right\|_{L^{q+2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C.

(46)

Proof.

It is enough to establish (
45
). We shall derive a differential inequality for

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx.

(47)

Set
\Psi=|v|^{q}v
. Testing the second equation in (
14
) against
\Psi
and using (
29
), we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigl(\rho v_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla v\bigr)\cdot\Psi\,dx=\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx.

(48)

For the pressure term, an integration by parts together with

\bigl|\operatorname{div}(|v|^{q}v)\bigr|\leq C_{N,q}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|

gives

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot\Psi\,dx\right|\leq C_{N,q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|\,dx.

(49)

For the viscous contribution, write
e=v/|v|
and
\xi=\nabla|v|
on
\{v\neq 0\}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v:\nabla\Psi\,dx+(\nu-c)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{\top}:\nabla\Psi\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}(\operatorname{div}v)\operatorname{div}\Psi\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}\Bigl(\nu|\nabla v|^{2}+(\nu-c)\nabla v:(\nabla v)^{\top}+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)(\operatorname{div}v)^{2}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+q\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}\Bigl(\nu|\xi|^{2}+(\nu-c)(\nabla v\,e)\cdot\xi+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)(\operatorname{div}v)\,e\cdot\xi\Bigr)\,dx.

(50)

By the argument already used in Proposition
3.2
, the quadratic form in the first integral on the right-hand side of (
50
) is bounded from below by
\mu_{\mathrm{GE}}|\nabla v|^{2}
. For the second integral we use

|(\nabla v\,e)\cdot\xi|\leq|\nabla v|\,|\xi|,\qquad|\operatorname{div}v|\leq\sqrt{N}\,|\nabla v|,

together with
c-\nu=\nu\beta
and
2\nu-c=\nu(1-\beta)
, and complete the square. This gives

\nu|\xi|^{2}+(\nu-c)(\nabla v\,e)\cdot\xi+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)(\operatorname{div}v)\,e\cdot\xi\geq-\frac{\bigl(c-\nu+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(2\nu-c)\bigr)^{2}}{4\nu}|\nabla v|^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\nu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v:\nabla\Psi\,dx+(\nu-c)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{\top}:\nabla\Psi\,dx

(51)

\displaystyle+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}(\operatorname{div}v)\operatorname{div}\Psi\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx,

where

\delta_{q}:=\nu\left[(1-\beta)(N\alpha-N+1)-\frac{q}{4}\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2}\right].

(52)

Since
q<q_{N,\ast}
, by (
41
) we have
\delta_{q}>0
.
Combining (
48
), (
49
), and (
51
), and then applying Young’s inequality to the pressure term, we arrive at

\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx+\frac{\delta_{q}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx.

(53)

Fix
t\in[0,T]
and decompose

\Omega_{1}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho(x,t)\leq 4\},\qquad\Omega_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho(x,t)>4\}.

Write

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t),

(54)

where

I_{1}(t):=\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx,\qquad I_{2}(t):=\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx.

On
\Omega_{1}(t)
one has
\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}\leq C\rho
, since
2\gamma-\alpha>1
. Therefore,

I_{1}(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q}\,dx.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}},

with the usual convention that the second factor is
1
when
q=2
. Using (
27
), we infer

I_{1}(t)\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right).

(55)

We first consider the case
N=2
. Let

m:=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\qquad\lambda:=\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(q+2)-q}{2}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

I_{2}(t)\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}.

On
\Omega_{2}(t)
we have
\rho^{m}\leq C(\rho^{m}-1)
, hence

\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\leq C\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{L^{\lambda/m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\lambda/m}.

Moreover,

\frac{\lambda}{m}=\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(q+2)-q}{2\alpha-1}=\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha-1)q+4\gamma-2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}\geq\frac{4-2\alpha}{2\alpha-1}>2.

Thus (
34
) and the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all finite
r\geq 2
yield

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\leq C.

Consequently,

I_{2}(t)\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right).

(56)

Combining (
53
), (
55
), and (
56
), we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right).

Gronwall’s inequality gives (
45
) in dimension two.

It remains to treat the case
N=3
. Set

m:=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\in\left(\frac{1}{6},\frac{1}{2}\right),\qquad\sigma:=\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right),\qquad\lambda:=\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(q+2)-q}{2}.

As above,

I_{2}(t)\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}.

(57)

Choose
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta(s)=0\ \text{for }0\leq s\leq 2,\qquad\eta(s)=1\ \text{for }s\geq 4,

and define

g:=\eta(\rho)\rho^{\sigma}.

Then
g=\rho^{\sigma}
on
\Omega_{2}(t)
. Let also

\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)>2\}.

Since
\Pi
is increasing on
[1,\infty)
,

|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|\leq\Pi(2)^{-1}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)}\Pi(\rho)\,dx\leq\Pi(2)^{-1}E_{0}.

(58)

In particular,
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\ g(x,t)\neq 0\}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)
for every
t
.

Now let

p_{0}:=\frac{6m}{\sigma}=\frac{12\alpha-6}{3\alpha-2}>6.

On
\{\rho>2\}
we have
\rho^{m}\leq C(\rho^{m}-1)
, and therefore

|g|^{p_{0}}\leq C|\rho^{m}-1|^{6}.

Hence, by (
34
) and the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

(59)

Further, (
58
) and
p_{0}>6
imply

\|g(t)\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\|g(t)\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

so
\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C
. On the other hand,

\nabla g=\eta^{\prime}(\rho)\rho^{\sigma}\nabla\rho+\eta(\rho)\nabla\rho^{\sigma}.

Since
\eta^{\prime}(\rho)\neq 0
only when
2\leq\rho\leq 4
, where
\rho
is uniformly bounded above and below, we have
|\nabla g|\leq C|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}|
. Therefore, by (
40
) and the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Thus

\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

(60)

We next interpolate between (
59
) and (
60
). Choose
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\zeta>1
so that

\frac{1}{\zeta}=\frac{\theta}{p_{0}}-\frac{1-\theta}{6}.

(61)

Then the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality gives

\|g\|_{L^{\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\theta}\|g\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{1-\theta}.

(62)

We now impose

\frac{2}{q+2}\zeta=\frac{2}{1-\theta}.

(63)

With this choice, (
59
), (
60
), (
62
), and Hölder’s inequality in time imply

\|g\|_{L^{\frac{2}{q+2}\zeta}(0,T;L^{\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

(64)

Solving (
61
) and (
63
), we find

\zeta=\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{3\alpha-2}.

Set

\zeta^{\prime}:=\sigma\zeta=\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}.

(65)

Since
g=\rho^{\sigma}
on
\Omega_{2}(t)
, (
64
) yields

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}dt\leq C.

(66)

A short computation shows that (
44
) is equivalent to
\lambda<\zeta^{\prime}
. Using (
37
) and Hölder’s inequality on the set
\Omega_{2}(t)
, we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}.

Substituting this into (
57
), we obtain

I_{2}(t)\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}.

(67)

Combining (
53
), (
55
), and (
67
), we infer

\frac{d}{dt}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)+C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}.

Equivalently,

\frac{d}{dt}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\leq C\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}.

(68)

Since
\lambda<\zeta^{\prime}
, the exponent on the right is strictly smaller than
2/(q+2)
; therefore (
66
) implies

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\zeta^{\prime}}}dt\leq C.

Gronwall’s inequality applied to (
68
) now yields (
45
) in dimension three as well. The proof is complete.
∎

The previous proposition treats the case
q\geq 2
. To cover the remaining range
1<q<2
in dimension three, we next establish the corresponding weighted
L^{q+2}
-estimate for the effective velocity
v
, using the additional regularity provided by Corollary
3.1
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
N=3
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
).

Assume in addition that

\frac{3}{2}\alpha-\frac{1}{6}\leq\gamma<5\alpha-\frac{7}{3}.

(69)

Choose
q
such that

1<q<2,\qquad q<q_{3,\ast},\qquad\gamma<3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{q+2}.

(70)

Then, for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C\!\left(\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},q,\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)>0

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C.

(71)

Equivalently,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v\right\|_{L^{q+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C.

(72)

Proof.

It is enough to prove (
71
). Arguing exactly as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, namely by testing the second equation in (
14
) against
|v|^{q}v
, one finds that

\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx+\delta_{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq C\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|,

where
\delta_{q}
is given by (
52
). Since
q<q_{3,\ast}
, (
41
) yields
\delta_{q}>0
.

Let

\Omega_{1}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)\leq 4\},\qquad\Omega_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)>4\},\qquad\sigma:=\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1.

By Assumption
1.1
, in particular
\alpha\in(\frac{2}{3},1)
, hence
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The lower bound in (
69
) gives
\gamma\geq\sigma+\frac{5}{6}
, and therefore
\frac{6}{5}(\gamma-\sigma)\geq 1
. Splitting the pressure term according to
\Omega_{1}(t)\cup\Omega_{2}(t)
, we obtain

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|.

On
\Omega_{1}(t)
, using
\nabla\rho^{\gamma}=\frac{\gamma}{\sigma}\rho^{\gamma-\sigma}\nabla\rho^{\sigma}
and Hölder’s inequality,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq C\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}(t)\|_{L^{6}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\rho^{\frac{6}{5}(\gamma-\sigma)}|v|^{\frac{6}{5}(q+1)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}.

Since
\rho\leq 4
on
\Omega_{1}(t)
and
\frac{6}{5}(\gamma-\sigma)\geq 1
, the last integral is bounded by

C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{\frac{6}{5}(q+1)}\,dx\right)^{\frac{5}{6}}.

Now
\frac{6}{5}(q+1)\in(2,q+2)
because
q\in(1,2)
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{\frac{6}{5}(q+1)}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{4-q}{5q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{6q-4}{5q}}.

In view of (
27
), this yields

\left|\int_{\Omega_{1}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq C\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}(t)\|_{L^{6}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{2}{3q}}.

Moreover, by (
40
) and the embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow W^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq T^{1/2}\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

For the contribution of
\Omega_{2}(t)
, integrating by parts gives

-\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho^{\gamma}-4^{\gamma})_{+}\operatorname{div}(|v|^{q}v)\,dx,

whence

\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq C\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|\,dx.

By Young’s inequality,

\left|\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot|v|^{q}v\,dx\right|\leq\frac{\delta_{q}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx.

Set

\lambda:=\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(q+2)-q}{2}.

From the lower bound in (
70
),

2\gamma-\alpha\geq 2\alpha-\frac{1}{3}>1,

so that
\lambda>0
. Hence Hölder’s inequality gives

\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}.

It remains to control the last factor. Let

m:=\alpha-\frac{1}{2},\qquad p_{0}:=\frac{6m}{\sigma}=\frac{12\alpha-6}{3\alpha-2}>6,

and choose
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta\equiv 0\ \text{on }[0,2],\qquad\eta\equiv 1\ \text{on }[4,\infty).

Write

g:=\eta(\rho)\rho^{\sigma},\qquad\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\rho(x,t)>2\}.

Since
\Pi
is increasing on
[1,\infty)
, (
23
) implies

|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|\leq\Pi(2)^{-1}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)}\Pi(\rho)\,dx\leq\Pi(2)^{-1}E_{0}.

On
\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)
one has
\rho^{m}\leq C(\rho^{m}-1)
, and therefore

|g|^{p_{0}}\leq C|\rho^{m}-1|^{6}\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)}.

Using (
34
) and the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we infer that

\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

The uniform bound on
|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|
and the fact that
p_{0}>6
then imply

\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Furthermore,

\nabla g=\eta^{\prime}(\rho)\rho^{\sigma}\nabla\rho+\eta(\rho)\nabla\rho^{\sigma},

and since
\eta^{\prime}
is supported in
[2,4]
, one has
|\nabla g|\leq C|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}|
. Hence (
40
) gives

\|g\|_{L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Choose
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\zeta>1
so that

\frac{1}{\zeta}=\frac{\theta}{p_{0}}-\frac{1-\theta}{6},\qquad\frac{\zeta}{q+2}=\frac{1}{1-\theta}.

Then the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality yields

\|g\|_{L^{\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\theta}\|g\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{1-\theta},

and therefore, by Hölder’s inequality in time,

\|g\|_{L^{\frac{2\zeta}{q+2}}(0,T;L^{\zeta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

A direct computation gives

\zeta=\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{3\alpha-2},\qquad\sigma\zeta=\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}.

Since
g=\rho^{\sigma}
on
\Omega_{2}(t)
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}dt\leq C.

The upper bound in (
70
) is equivalent to

\lambda<\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}.

Hence, using again the uniform bound on
|\Omega_{2}(t)|
from (
37
),

\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2\lambda}{(q+2)\bigl((5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12\bigr)/2}}.

Since

0<\frac{\lambda}{\frac{(5\alpha-3)q+22\alpha-12}{2}}<1,

the elementary inequality
a^{\vartheta}\leq 1+a
for
a\geq 0
and
\vartheta\in(0,1)
gives

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}dt\leq C.

Collecting the preceding bounds, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}(t)\|_{L^{6}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{1-\frac{2}{3q}}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{q+2}}.

Let
s:=\frac{2}{3q}\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3})
. Multiplying by

s\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{s-1},

we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{s}\leq C\|\nabla\rho^{\sigma}(t)\|_{L^{6}}+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2}(t)}\rho^{\lambda}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{s-1+\frac{q}{q+2}}.

Now

s-1+\frac{q}{q+2}=-\frac{4(q-1)}{3q(q+2)}<0,

so the last factor is bounded by
1
. Integrating over
(0,t)
and using the preceding
L^{1}(0,T)
bounds, we conclude that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\right)^{s}\leq C\left(1+\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{q+2}\right).

Since
s>0
, this implies (
71
). The proof is complete.
∎

3.5
and

), (

), (

), (

q_{2,\ast}>2
, Proposition
3.5
applies with

; interpolating the resulting
L^{4}
-bound with the basic BD entropy estimate yields the following family of weighted integrability estimates for the effective velocity, which will serve as the two-dimensional input for the upper-bound argument below.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
N=2
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
)
.

Then, for every
h\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
and every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C_{h}=C_{h}\!\left(\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},\left\|\rho_{0}^{1/4}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{4}}\right)>0

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-h}}(x,t)\,dx\leq C_{h}.

(74)

Equivalently,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\rho^{\frac{1-h}{2}}v\right\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-h}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C_{h}.

(75)

Proof.

By the BD entropy estimate (
27
), there exists a positive constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\leq C_{2}.

Since
q_{2,\ast}>2
, Proposition
3.5
applies with
q=2
. Hence there exists a positive constant
C_{4}>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}\,dx\leq C_{4}.

Fix
h\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
.

When
0<h<\frac{1}{2}
, set

\vartheta:=\frac{h}{1-h}\in(0,1).

Then

\frac{2}{1-h}=2+2\vartheta=2(1-\vartheta)+4\vartheta.

Therefore, for each fixed
t\in[0,T]
, Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-h}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigl(\rho|v|^{2}\bigr)^{1-\vartheta}\bigl(\rho|v|^{4}\bigr)^{\vartheta}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1-\vartheta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}\,dx\right)^{\vartheta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{1-\vartheta}C_{4}^{\vartheta}.

When
h=\frac{1}{2}
, we have
\frac{2}{1-h}=4
, and thus directly from the above
L^{4}
bound,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-h}}\,dx=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{4}\,dx\leq C_{4}.

Combining the two cases, we conclude that for every
h\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
, there exists a positive constant
C_{h}>0
, with the dependence stated above, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-h}}\,dx\leq C_{h}.

This proves (
74
).

Finally, since

\left\|\rho^{\frac{1-h}{2}}v\right\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-h}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\frac{2}{1-h}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-h}}\,dx,

estimate (
75
) follows immediately after relabeling the constant.
∎

We are now in a position to establish the uniform upper bound for the density. The weighted integrability estimates for the effective velocity obtained above—namely, Corollary
3.2
in three dimensions and Lemma
3.3
in two dimensions—provide the key input for the following proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that
N\in\{2,3\}
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by (
13
). Then, for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C\!\left(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},\overline{\rho_{0}},\|\rho_{0}^{1/4}v_{0}\|_{L^{4}}\right)>0

such that

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)\leq C.

(76)

Proof.

Fix
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
. Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
be nondecreasing, with

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta\equiv 0\ \text{on }[0,2],\qquad\eta\equiv 1\ \text{on }[4,\infty),

and set

f:=\eta(\rho)\rho,\qquad\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho(x,t)>2\}.

Exactly as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
,

|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|\leq\Pi(2)^{-1}E_{0}=:K_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

Hence

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t),\qquad|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{0}\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

We next choose
\theta
. If
N=2
, we set

\theta:=\frac{2\alpha-1}{8}\in\Bigl(0,\frac{1}{2}\Bigr).

If
N=3
, we choose
q_{\sharp}\in(1,2]
as follows. When
\gamma<\frac{9\alpha-4}{2}
, let
q_{\sharp}=2
. When

\frac{9\alpha-4}{2}\leq\gamma<\frac{15\alpha-7}{3},

the function

q\longmapsto 3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{q+2}

is continuous and strictly decreasing on
(1,2]
, with limit
(15\alpha-7)/3
as
q\to 1+
; since
q_{3,\ast}>2
, one may therefore choose
q_{\sharp}\in(1,2)
such that

\gamma<3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{q_{\sharp}+2}.

We then set

\theta:=\frac{q_{\sharp}}{q_{\sharp}+2}\in\Bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\Bigr].

In either case,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-\theta}}\,dx\leq C_{\theta}.

(77)

Indeed, in two dimensions this is Lemma
3.3
. In three dimensions, if
q_{\sharp}=2
this is Proposition
3.5
; if
1<q_{\sharp}<2
, then by Hölder’s inequality, (
27
) at
t=0
, and
\rho_{0}\leq\overline{\rho_{0}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{q_{\sharp}+2}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-q_{\sharp}}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{4}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{\sharp}}{2}}\leq C,

and Corollary
3.2
applies.

For
p\geq 0
, define

\lambda_{p}:=p+\alpha+1,\qquad l:=p+2+\theta-\alpha,\qquad\Phi_{p}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\eta(y)^{\lambda_{p}}y^{p+1}\,dy.

Then

\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(s)=\eta(s)^{\lambda_{p}}s^{p+1},\qquad(\eta(s)s)^{p+2}\leq(p+2)\Phi_{p}(s)+4^{p+2}\mathbf{1}_{\{s>2\}}.

Testing the first equation in (
14
),

\rho_{t}+\operatorname{div}(\rho v)-c\Delta\rho^{\alpha}=0,

against
\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(\rho)
gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\rho)\,dx+c\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha-1}\Phi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho v\cdot\nabla\!\bigl(\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+1}\bigr)\,dx.

Since
\eta^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\Phi_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\rho)\geq(p+1)\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p}.

Writing the right-hand side as

(p+1)\int\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+1}v\cdot\nabla\rho+\lambda_{p}\int\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}-1}\eta^{\prime}(\rho)\rho^{p+2}v\cdot\nabla\rho=:I_{1}+I_{2},

we estimate
I_{1}
by Young’s inequality:

|I_{1}|\leq\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{4}\int\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+C(p+1)\int\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+3-\alpha}|v|^{2}.

Since
p+3-\alpha=l+1-\theta
and

\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{l}\leq f^{l}+4^{l}\mathbf{1}_{\{2<\rho<4\}},

Hölder’s inequality together with (
77
) yields

\displaystyle\int\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+3-\alpha}|v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\bigl(\rho^{\frac{1-\theta}{2}}|v|\bigr)^{2}\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{l}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\rho|v|^{\frac{2}{1-\theta}}\,dx\right)^{1-\theta}\left(\int\bigl(\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{l}\bigr)^{1/\theta}\,dx\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int f^{l/\theta}\,dx\right)^{\theta}+C4^{p+2},

where we also used
l\leq p+2
and
|\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)|\leq K_{0}
.

For
I_{2}
, let
m:=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}>0
. Since
\operatorname{supp}\eta^{\prime}\subset[2,4]
and

|\nabla\rho|=\frac{1}{m}\rho^{1-m}|\nabla(\rho^{m}-1)|\leq C|\nabla(\rho^{m}-1)|\qquad\text{on }\{2<\rho<4\},

Cauchy–Schwarz, (
27
), and Proposition
3.3
give

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C(p+1)4^{p+2}\int_{\{2<\rho<4\}}|v||\nabla\rho|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(p+1)4^{p+2}\left(\int\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int|\nabla(\rho^{m}-1)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(p+1)4^{p+2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\rho(x,t))\,dx+c_{1}(p+1)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\rho_{0})\,dx+C(p+1)\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{l/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{l}+C(p+1)4^{p+2},

(78)

for some
c_{1}>0
depending only on
\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon
.

The initial term is estimated by

\Phi_{p}(s)\leq\frac{s^{p+2}}{p+2}\mathbf{1}_{\{s>2\}},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\rho_{0})\,dx\leq C\overline{\rho_{0}}^{\,p+2}.

Moreover,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C(p+2)\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\rho(x,t))\,dx+C4^{p+2},

and, since
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{0}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+\alpha+1}\,dxdt\leq CT^{\ast}\left(1+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+2}(x,t)\,dx\right).

If

g_{p}:=f^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}},

then, using
\operatorname{supp}\eta^{\prime}\subset\{2<\rho<4\}
,

|\nabla g_{p}|\leq C(p+2)\eta(\rho)^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\rho^{\frac{p+\alpha-1}{2}}|\nabla\rho|+C(p+2)4^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\{2<\rho<4\}}|\nabla\rho|.

Hence,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla g_{p}|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C(p+2)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\rho)^{\lambda_{p}}\rho^{p+\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dxdt+C(p+2)^{2}4^{p+2},

where Proposition
3.3
was used again to control the integral over
\{2<\rho<4\}
. Combining these estimates with (
78
), and enlarging the constants if necessary, we obtain: there exist
C_{0}\geq 1
and
\Lambda\geq 8+\overline{\rho_{0}}
, depending only on

N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},\overline{\rho_{0}},\|\rho_{0}^{1/4}v_{0}\|_{L^{4}},

such that, for every
p\geq 0
,

\left\|f^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}+\left\|f^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{2}\leq C_{0}(p+2)^{2}\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{l/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{l}+C_{0}\Lambda^{p+2}.

(79)

We now derive the reverse Hölder inequality. If
N=2
, let

r:=\frac{8}{\theta},\qquad a:=\theta-\frac{2}{r}=\frac{3\theta}{4}>0.

Since
\theta=\frac{2\alpha-1}{8}<\alpha
,

\alpha(2+a)-1-\theta(1+a)=2\alpha-1-\theta+a(\alpha-\theta)>0.

If
N=3
, let

r:=6,\qquad a:=\theta-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2(q_{\sharp}-1)}{3(q_{\sharp}+2)}>0.

A direct computation gives

\alpha(2+a)-1-\theta(1+a)=\frac{2\bigl(4\alpha q_{\sharp}^{2}+13\alpha q_{\sharp}+10\alpha-4q_{\sharp}^{2}-8q_{\sharp}-6\bigr)}{3(q_{\sharp}+2)^{2}}.

The numerator is concave in
q_{\sharp}
and, since
\alpha\geq\frac{7}{9}
, its values at
q_{\sharp}=1
and
q_{\sharp}=2
are

27\alpha-18>0,\qquad 52\alpha-38>0.

Hence

a=\theta-\frac{2}{r}>0,\qquad\alpha(2+a)-1-\theta(1+a)>0.

(80)

For
p\geq 0
, define

s:=(1+a)p+\alpha+1+2a,\qquad\vartheta:=\frac{2}{s},\qquad\xi:=\frac{2a}{s}.

Then

\frac{\xi(p+2)}{2}+\frac{\vartheta(p+\alpha+1)}{2}=1,\qquad\frac{\vartheta s}{2}=1,\qquad\frac{\xi}{2}+\frac{\vartheta}{r}=\frac{\theta}{s}.

Therefore, Hölder’s inequality in time and space yields

\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{s/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq\left\|f^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{\xi}\left\|f^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{r})}^{\vartheta}.

By the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and (
79
),

\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{s/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq C_{r}^{\vartheta}\left(C_{0}(p+2)^{2}\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{l/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}^{l}+C_{0}\Lambda^{p+2}\right)^{\frac{\xi+\vartheta}{2}}.

(81)

Moreover,

s-(1+a)l=\alpha(2+a)-1-\theta(1+a)>0,

hence

\frac{\xi+\vartheta}{2}\,l=\frac{(1+a)l}{s}\leq 1.

Since

\frac{l}{\theta}-(p+2)=\frac{(1-\theta)(p+2)+\theta-\alpha}{\theta}>0,

we also have
p+2\leq l/\theta
, and therefore
\Lambda^{p+2}\leq(\Lambda^{1/\theta})^{l}
. Define

\Upsilon(\lambda):=\max\left\{\|f\|_{L^{\lambda}(0,T;L^{\lambda/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))},\Lambda^{1/\theta}\right\},\qquad\lambda\geq l_{0}:=2+\theta-\alpha.

Then (
81
) implies

\Upsilon(s)\leq(2C_{r}^{2}C_{0})^{\frac{1+a}{s}}(p+2)^{\frac{2(1+a)}{s}}\Upsilon(l).

(82)

We now iterate. Set
l_{0}:=2+\theta-\alpha
, and define

p_{k}:=l_{k}-2-\theta+\alpha,\qquad l_{k+1}:=(1+a)p_{k}+\alpha+1+2a\qquad(k\geq 0).

Then

l_{k+1}=(1+a)l_{k}+\alpha(2+a)-1-\theta(1+a),

so that, by (
80
),

l_{k+1}\geq(1+a)l_{k},\qquad l_{k}\geq(1+a)^{k}l_{0},\qquad l_{k}\to\infty.

Applying (
82
) with
p=p_{k}
and
l=l_{k}
gives

\Upsilon(l_{k+1})\leq(2C_{r}^{2}C_{0})^{\frac{1+a}{l_{k+1}}}(p_{k}+2)^{\frac{2(1+a)}{l_{k+1}}}\Upsilon(l_{k}).

Since

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l_{k}}<\infty,\qquad p_{k}+2=l_{k}+\alpha-\theta\leq 2l_{k},

the corresponding infinite product converges. It remains to show that
\Upsilon(l_{0})
is finite.

If
N=2
, let again
m=\alpha-\frac{1}{2}>0
. On
\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)
one has
\rho^{m}\leq C(\rho^{m}-1)
, hence

f\leq C(\rho^{m}-1)^{1/m}\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{2}(t)}.

Since
l_{0}/(\theta m)<\infty
, the embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{l_{0}/(\theta m)}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and Proposition
3.3
imply

\|f(t)\|_{L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{L^{l_{0}/(\theta m)}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1/m}\leq C\|\rho^{m}-1\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1/m}\leq C

uniformly for
t\in[0,T]
. Therefore

\|f\|_{L^{l_{0}}(0,T;L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq(T^{\ast})^{1/l_{0}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\leq C,

and thus
\Upsilon(l_{0})\leq C
.

If
N=3
, define

p_{\star}:=\frac{l_{0}}{\theta}-2>0,\qquad l_{\star}:=p_{\star}+2+\theta-\alpha,\qquad F_{\star}:=f^{\frac{p_{\star}+\alpha+1}{2}}.

Applying (
79
) with
p=p_{\star}
,

\left\|f^{\frac{p_{\star}+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}+\|F_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}\leq C_{0}(p_{\star}+2)^{2}\|f\|_{L^{l_{\star}}(0,T;L^{l_{\star}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{l_{\star}}+C_{0}\Lambda^{p_{\star}+2}.

(83)

Moreover,

\|f(t)\|_{L^{l_{\star}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{l_{\star}}=\|F_{\star}(t)\|_{L^{s_{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\mu_{\star}},\qquad s_{\star}:=\frac{2l_{\star}}{\theta(p_{\star}+\alpha+1)},\qquad\mu_{\star}:=\frac{2l_{\star}}{p_{\star}+\alpha+1}.

A direct computation yields

\mu_{\star}<2\iff\alpha>\frac{q_{\sharp}+1}{q_{\sharp}+2},

and the right-hand side is at most
3/4<7/9\leq\alpha
, so
0<\mu_{\star}<2
. Likewise,

s_{\star}\geq 2\iff(1-\alpha)q_{\sharp}^{2}+(3-2\alpha)q_{\sharp}+(4-2\alpha)\geq 0,

while

s_{\star}\leq 6\iff(\alpha+1)q_{\sharp}^{2}+q_{\sharp}+2\alpha-4\geq 0.

The latter quadratic is strictly increasing for
q_{\sharp}>0
, and its value at
q_{\sharp}=1
is
3\alpha-2>0
; hence

2\leq s_{\star}\leq 6,\qquad 0<\mu_{\star}<2.

Therefore, by
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{s_{\star}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\|f\|_{L^{l_{\star}}(0,T;L^{l_{\star}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{l_{\star}}\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\|F_{\star}(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\mu_{\star}}\,dt\leq\delta\|F_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}+C_{\delta}

for every
\delta>0
. Choosing

\delta=\frac{1}{2C_{0}(p_{\star}+2)^{2}}

and returning to (
83
), we obtain

\left\|f^{\frac{p_{\star}+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}+\|F_{\star}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{2}\leq C.

Since
p_{\star}+2=l_{0}/\theta
, this yields

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C,

and hence

\|f\|_{L^{l_{0}}(0,T;L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq(T^{\ast})^{1/l_{0}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{l_{0}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C.

Thus in both dimensions

\Upsilon(l_{0})\leq C,

and consequently

\sup_{k\geq 0}\Upsilon(l_{k})\leq C.

Let
Q_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
. Since
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{0}
,

\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(Q_{T})}\leq K_{0}^{\frac{1-\theta}{l_{k}}}\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(0,T;L^{l_{k}/\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))}\leq C\qquad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

Fix
\lambda>C
. Then Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\lambda^{l_{k}}|\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:f(x,t)>\lambda\}|\leq\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(Q_{T})}^{l_{k}}\leq C^{\,l_{k}}.

Letting
k\to\infty
and using
l_{k}\to\infty
, we infer that

|\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:f(x,t)>\lambda\}|=0.

Hence
f\in L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, with

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C.

Since
f=\rho
on
\{\rho\geq 4\}
and
\rho\leq 4
on
\{\rho<4\}
, we have

\rho\leq 4+f\qquad\text{on }Q_{T},

and therefore

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)}\rho(x,t)\leq C.

Finally,
\rho_{0}\leq\overline{\rho_{0}}
, so enlarging
C
if necessary we obtain

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)\leq C.

This proves (
76
).
∎

3.3
Positive lower bound for the density

In this subsection, building on the upper bound (
76
) established in Proposition
3.7
, we first recast the density-weighted
L^{q+2}
estimate for the effective velocity obtained in the previous subsection in a form that no longer requires the additional three-dimensional restriction on
\gamma
. We then use this refined estimate to derive a positive lower bound for the density. We continue to use the notation (
41
).

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume that
N\in\{2,3\}
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
), (
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to (
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be given by (
13
).

Assume in addition that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.7
are satisfied, so that (
76
) holds for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
.
Fix any
q\geq 2
with
q<q_{N,\ast}
, Then, for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C\!\left(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},\overline{\rho_{0}},\|\rho_{0}^{1/4}v_{0}\|_{L^{4}},q,\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}}\right)

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C.

(84)

Proof.

Fix
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
. All integrals below are taken over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
C
denotes a positive constant depending only on the quantities displayed in the statement.

Repeating the argument leading to (
53
) in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, we obtain

\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx+\frac{\delta_{q}}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{q}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{q}\,dx.

(85)

Since
q<q_{N,\ast}
, (
41
) yields
\delta_{q}>0
.

By Proposition
3.7
, there exists
\overline{C}_{T}>0
such that

0<\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{C}_{T}\qquad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T].

Moreover, Assumption
1.1
gives
\gamma\geq 1
and
\alpha<1
, hence
2\gamma-\alpha>1
. Therefore

\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(x,t)\leq\overline{C}_{T}^{\,2\gamma-\alpha-1}\rho(x,t)\qquad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T],

and (
85
) reduces to

\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\leq C\int\rho|v|^{q}\,dx.

(86)

If
q=2
, then (
27
) gives

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\leq C.

If
q>2
, Hölder’s inequality together with (
27
) yields

\int\rho|v|^{q}\,dx\leq\left(\int\rho|v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\left(\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\leq C\left(\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\leq C\left(1+\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right),

(87)

since
\frac{q-2}{q}\in(0,1)
. In the case
q=2
, the same bound is immediate from (
27
). Consequently, (
86
) implies

\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\leq C\left(1+\int\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)\qquad\text{for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

Gronwall’s lemma therefore gives

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho|v|^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C\left(1+\int\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{q+2}\,dx\right),

this proves (
84
).
∎

Combining the upper bound (
76
) from Proposition
3.7
with the refined
density-weighted
L^{q+2}
-estimate for the effective velocity obtained in Proposition

3.8
, we next derive a positive lower bound for the density by estimating its
reciprocal. Before doing so, we record that the exponent required in the argument below
can indeed be chosen within the admissible range.

Since Assumption
1.1
implies
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, the condition

\alpha<\frac{q}{q+2}

is equivalent to

q>\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha},

which is stronger than
q\geq 2
. On the other hand, using the definition of

q_{N,\ast}
in (
41
), and denoting by
P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)
the quadratic
polynomial introduced above whose positive zero is
\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)
, we have the
identity

\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\bigl(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\bigr)^{2}\left(q_{N,\ast}-\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\bigl(1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)\bigr)^{2}P_{N}(\beta;\alpha).

By Assumption
1.1
, one has

1-\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)>0,\qquad P_{N}(\beta;\alpha)<0\qquad\text{for }\beta\in[0,\beta_{N}^{+}(\alpha)).

Therefore (
88
) gives

q_{N,\ast}>\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}.

Consequently, the interval

\left(\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha},\,q_{N,\ast}\right)

is nonempty, and we may choose once and for all an exponent
q
satisfying

\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}<q<q_{N,\ast}.

(89)

For such a choice of
q
, both
q\geq 2
and
\alpha<q/(q+2)
hold automatically.
With this admissible exponent fixed, we prove the following
L^{\infty}
-bound for

\rho^{-1}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume that
N\in\{2,3\}
, and that (
5
), (
6
), (
7
),
(
8
), and Assumption
1.1
hold.
Let
(\rho,u)
be a sufficiently smooth strictly positive local strong solution to
(
1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T^{\ast})
, and let
v
be defined by
(
13
).

Assume moreover that the hypotheses of Proposition
3.7
hold, so that
(
76
) is available for every
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
.
Let
q
be any exponent satisfying (
89
). Then, for every

T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, there exists a positive constant

C=C\!\left(N,\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{\ast},\overline{\rho_{0}},\|\rho_{0}^{1/4}v_{0}\|_{L^{4}},q,\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{q+2}},\left\|\rho_{0}^{-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)>0

such that

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T]}\rho(x,t)^{-1}\leq C.

(90)

Proof.

Fix
T\in(0,T^{\ast})
, and write

\tau:=\rho^{-1},\qquad Q_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\qquad\Theta_{T}:=\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}+1.

All constants below depend only on the quantities displayed in the statement. From the first equation in (
14
),

\partial_{t}\tau-c\alpha\operatorname{div}\!\bigl(\tau^{1-\alpha}\nabla\tau\bigr)+2c\alpha\tau^{-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}+v\cdot\nabla\tau-\tau\operatorname{div}v=0.

Choose
\eta\in C^{\infty}([0,\infty))
nondecreasing such that

0\leq\eta\leq 1,\qquad\eta\equiv 0\ \text{on }[0,2],\qquad\eta\equiv 1\ \text{on }[4,\infty),

and set

f:=\eta(\tau)\tau.

By (
30
) and the basic energy inequality,

\bigl|\{\tau(\cdot,t)>2\}\bigr|=\bigl|\{\rho(\cdot,t)<1/2\}\bigr|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Pi(\rho(x,t))\,dx\leq CE_{0}=:K_{-}\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

Hence

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}\subset\{\tau(\cdot,t)>2\},\qquad|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{-},\qquad\tau\leq f+4.

We also introduce

H(t):=\int_{\{2<\tau<4\}}\bigl(|v|^{2}+|\nabla\tau|^{2}\bigr)(x,t)\,dx.

Since
\{2<\tau<4\}=\{1/4<\rho<1/2\}
, one has

|\nabla\tau|=\rho^{-2}|\nabla\rho|\leq C\,|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}-1)|\qquad\text{on }\{2<\tau<4\},

and therefore, by (
34
) and Proposition
3.8
,

\int_{0}^{T}H(t)\,dt\leq C.

Let

\delta:=\frac{2}{q+2},\qquad a:=\frac{q+2}{q},

and fix
p\geq 0
. We write

\Lambda_{p}:=p+3-\alpha,\qquad l:=p+1+\alpha+\delta,\qquad\Phi_{p}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\eta(\sigma)^{\Lambda_{p}}\sigma^{p+1}\,d\sigma.

Testing the equation for
\tau
against
\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(\tau)=\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1}
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\tau)\,dx+c\alpha(p+3)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\,dx\leq I_{1}+I_{2},

where

I_{1}:=(p+3)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1}v\cdot\nabla\tau\,dx\right|,

and

I_{2}:=\Lambda_{p}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}-1}\eta^{\prime}(\tau)\tau^{p+2}v\cdot\nabla\tau\,dx\right|.

Then, after one application of Young’s inequality,

I_{1}\leq\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\,dx+C(p+3)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1+\alpha}|v|^{2}\,dx,

and, since
\operatorname{supp}\eta^{\prime}\subset[2,4]
,

I_{2}\leq C(p+3)4^{p+2}H(t).

Now

\tau^{p+1+\alpha}|v|^{2}=\bigl(\rho|v|^{q+2}\bigr)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\tau^{l},\qquad\Lambda_{p}-l=2-2\alpha-\delta>0

by (
89
). Hence, using
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and Proposition
3.8
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1+\alpha}|v|^{2}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{q+2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{a\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{la}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\leq C\|f(t)\|_{L^{la}}^{l}.

Consequently,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{p}(\tau)\,dx+\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\,dx\leq C(p+3)\|f(t)\|_{L^{la}}^{l}+C(p+3)4^{p+2}H(t).

(91)

On the other hand,

f^{p+2}\leq C\Bigl((p+2)\Phi_{p}(\tau)+4^{p+2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau>2\}}\Bigr).

Integrating (
91
) over
(0,T)
and using the preceding bound, the estimate on
\int_{0}^{T}H
, and the fact that
\tau_{0}\in L^{\infty}
, we find a constant
C_{0}\geq 1
, depending only on
K_{-}
and
\|\tau_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+2}(x,t)\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}}\tau^{p+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C(p+3)^{2}\Bigl(\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{la})}^{l}+C_{0}^{p+2}\Bigr).

(92)

Set

g_{p}:=f^{\frac{p+3-\alpha}{2}}.

Since
\operatorname{supp}\eta^{\prime}\subset[2,4]
, the difference between
|\nabla g_{p}|
and
\eta(\tau)^{\Lambda_{p}/2}\tau^{\frac{p+1-\alpha}{2}}|\nabla\tau|
is supported in
\{2<\tau<4\}
and is controlled by
C(p+3)H(t)^{1/2}
. It follows from (
92
) that

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla g_{p}|^{2}\,dxdt\leq C(p+3)^{2}\Bigl(\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{la})}^{l}+C_{0}^{p+2}\Bigr).

Moreover,

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{p}^{2}\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+3-\alpha}\,dxdt\leq T^{\ast}\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{p+2}(x,t)\,dx.

Hence

\left\|f^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}+\left\|f^{\frac{p+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2}\leq C\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}(p+3)^{2}\Bigl(\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{la})}^{l}+C_{0}^{p+2}\Bigr).

(93)

We now choose
r
so that

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\qquad\theta:=\frac{q}{q+2}-\frac{2}{r}>\frac{1}{q}.

For
N=2
this is immediate with
r<\infty
large. For
N=3
, Assumption
1.1
gives
\alpha>\frac{2}{3}
; since
\alpha<\frac{q}{q+2}
, we have
q>\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}>4
, and then one may choose

r\in\left(\frac{2q(q+2)}{q^{2}-q-2},\,6\right].

Now define

s:=(p+2)\theta+p+3-\alpha,\qquad\xi:=\frac{2\theta}{s},\qquad\zeta:=\frac{2}{s}.

Then

\frac{\xi}{2}(p+2)+\frac{\zeta}{2}(p+3-\alpha)=1,\qquad\frac{1}{as}=\frac{\xi}{2}+\frac{\zeta}{r},

so that Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding yield

\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{as})}\leq C_{r}^{\zeta}\left\|f^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{\xi}\left\|f^{\frac{p+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{\zeta}.

Combining this with (
93
), we get

\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{as})}\leq C^{\frac{1+\theta}{s}}\Theta_{T}^{(1-\alpha)\frac{1+\theta}{s}}(p+3)^{\frac{2(1+\theta)}{s}}\Bigl(\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{al})}^{l}+C_{0}^{p+2}\Bigr)^{\frac{1+\theta}{s}}.

By (
89
),

1-\alpha-\delta>0,\qquad 2-2\alpha-\delta>0,

and therefore

s-(1+\theta)l=2-2\alpha-\delta+\theta(1-\alpha-\delta)>0.

In particular,

l\frac{1+\theta}{s}\leq 1.

Since also
p+2\leq C_{\ast}l
for some constant
C_{\ast}=C_{\ast}(\alpha,q)
, we may rewrite the preceding estimate as

\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{as})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}\leq C^{\frac{1+\theta}{s}}\Theta_{T}^{(1-\alpha)\frac{1+\theta}{s}}(p+3)^{\frac{2(1+\theta)}{s}}\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{l}(0,T;L^{al})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}.

(94)

Set

l_{0}:=q,\qquad p_{k}:=l_{k}-1-\alpha-\delta,\qquad l_{k+1}:=(p_{k}+2)\theta+p_{k}+3-\alpha.

Then

l_{k+1}=(1+\theta)l_{k}+A,\qquad A:=2-2\alpha-\delta+\theta(1-\alpha-\delta)>0.

Hence
l_{k}\to\infty
, more precisely

l_{k+1}\geq(1+\theta)^{k+1}q,\qquad p_{k}+3=l_{k}+2-\alpha-\delta\leq C(1+\theta)^{k}.

Iterating (
94
) with
p=p_{k}
, and using

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1+\theta}{l_{k+1}}<\infty,\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\log(p_k+3)}{l_{k+1}}<\infty,

we infer that

\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(0,T;L^{al_{k}})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}\leq C\,\Theta_{T}^{\sigma}\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q+2})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}

(95)

for all
k\geq 0
, where

\sigma:=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1+\theta}{l_{k+1}}.

Moreover,

0<\sigma\leq(1-\alpha)\frac{1+\theta}{q\theta}<(1-\alpha)\Bigl(1+\frac{1}{q}\Bigr)<1.

Indeed,
\theta>\frac{1}{q}
,
q\geq 2
, and Assumption
1.1
implies
\alpha>\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2}>\frac{1}{2}
.

Since
|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{-}
, (
95
) gives

\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(Q_{T})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{l_{k}}(0,T;L^{al_{k}})}\leq C\,\Theta_{T}^{\sigma}\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q+2})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}.

As
l_{k}\to\infty
and
|\{(x,t)\in Q_{T}:f(x,t)\neq 0\}|\leq K_{-}T<\infty
, letting
k\to\infty
yields

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C\,\Theta_{T}^{\sigma}\max\Bigl\{\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q+2})},\,C_{0}^{C_{\ast}}\Bigr\}.

(96)

It remains to control the initial norm on the right-hand side. To this end we return to (
91
) with
p=q
. Set

l_{q}:=q+1+\alpha+\delta<q+2,\qquad g_{q}:=f^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}},

and

m_{q}:=\frac{2(q+2)l_{q}}{q(q+3-\alpha)},\qquad r_{q}:=\frac{2l_{q}}{q+3-\alpha},\qquad b_{q}:=\frac{2(q+2)}{q+3-\alpha}.

Then

\|f\|_{L^{al_{q}}}^{l_{q}}=\|g_{q}\|_{L^{m_{q}}}^{r_{q}},\qquad\|g_{q}\|_{L^{b_{q}}}^{b_{q}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{q+2}\,dx.

Since
r_{q}<2
, and
m_{q}<\infty
for
N=2
while
m_{q}<6
for
N=3
, the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality and Young’s inequality give

\|f\|_{L^{al_{q}}}^{l_{q}}\leq\eta\|\nabla g_{q}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\eta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\nu_{q}},

where

\nu_{q}=\frac{q^{2}+\bigl(3-(N-1)\alpha\bigr)q+4-(N-2)\alpha-2N}{q^{2}+\bigl(4-N\alpha\bigr)q+4-N-2N\alpha}.

A direct computation yields

1-\nu_{q}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{q(1-\alpha)+2-4\alpha}{q^{2}+(4-2\alpha)q+2-4\alpha},&N=2,\\[8.53581pt]
\dfrac{q(1-\alpha)+3-5\alpha}{q^{2}+(4-3\alpha)q+1-6\alpha},&N=3,\end{cases}

so that
0<\nu_{q}<1
because (
89
) implies
q>\dfrac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}
.
Using again the transition-layer estimate,

\|\nabla g_{q}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta(\tau)^{q+3-\alpha}\tau^{q+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}\,dx+CH(t).

Substituting these bounds into (
91
) with
p=q
and taking
\eta
sufficiently small, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{q}(\tau)\,dx\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{q+2}\,dx\right)^{\nu_{q}}+CH(t).

Since

f^{q+2}\leq C\,\Phi_{q}(\tau)+C\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau>2\}},\qquad|\{\tau(\cdot,t)>2\}|\leq K_{-},

we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{q}(\tau)\,dx\leq C\left(1+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{q}(\tau)\,dx\right)^{\nu_{q}}\right)+CH(t).

The nonlinear Gronwall inequality, together with
\int_{0}^{T}H(t)\,dt\leq C
, now gives

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{q}(\tau(x,t))\,dx\leq C,

and therefore

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f^{q+2}(x,t)\,dx\leq C.

In particular,

\|f\|_{L^{q}(0,T;L^{q+2})}\leq(T^{\ast})^{1/q}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t)\|_{L^{q+2}}\leq C.

Returning to (
96
), we conclude that

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C\,\Theta_{T}^{\sigma},\qquad 0<\sigma<1.

Since
\tau\leq f+4
, it follows that

\Theta_{T}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}+5\leq C\,\Theta_{T}^{\sigma}+5.

Because
\sigma<1
, Young’s inequality implies
\Theta_{T}\leq C
. Equivalently,

\|\tau\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq C.

This is exactly (
90
).
∎

3.4
Higher-order estimates

As a preparation for the higher-order estimates, we first derive an auxiliary bound on
\nabla^{3}\rho
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
N\in\{2,3\}
, suppose
0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\bar{\rho}<\infty
and Assumption
1.1
hold. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\underline{\rho},\bar{\rho},N)>0
such that

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx.

(97)

Moreover, for
N=3
, it holds that

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(98)

For
N=2,
assuming that
\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\|\nabla\rho^{\alpha}(s)\|_{L^{2}}
is bounded, then the estimate simplifies to

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}.

(99)

Proof.

A direct calculation leads to

\displaystyle\nabla\Delta\rho=

\displaystyle\nabla\left[\alpha^{-1}\rho^{1-\alpha}\left(\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})-\alpha(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha-2}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}\right)\right]

(100)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left|\nabla\rho\right|\left|\nabla^{2}\rho\right|+\left|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\right|+\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{3}\right).

By Plancherel’s theorem, we obtain

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\left|\nabla\rho\right|\left|\nabla^{2}\rho\right|\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}.

(101)

For
N=3
with
\alpha<1
, it follows from integration by parts and Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{6}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-2}\sum^{3}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{i}(\rho^{\alpha-2})\partial_{i}\rho|\nabla\rho|^{4}dx

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-2}\left(\Delta\rho|\nabla\rho|^{4}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}4\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}\partial_{i}\rho\partial_{j}\rho\partial_{ij}\rho\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\left|\left(|\nabla\rho|^{2}I+4\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho\right):\nabla^{2}\rho\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{4}|\nabla^{2}\rho|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}dx+\frac{27}{2(2-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\rho\right|^{2}dx.

From the uniform boundedness of
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{6}\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}^{3-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{6}dx\leq\frac{27\,\overline{\rho}^{3-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(103)

For
N=2
, by (
24
), integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, Plancherel’s theorem, together with the boundedness of
\rho
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\rho\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{6}}^{6}\leq C\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

(104)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right|\left|\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right|dx\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using (
104
), Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left|\nabla\rho\right|\left|\nabla^{2}\rho\right|\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\left|\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right|\left|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right|\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{6}_{L^{6}}

(105)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{4}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{6}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{6}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{4}+C\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{6}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left\lVert\nabla^{3}\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\left\lVert\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{6}_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It follows from (
101
), (
104
) and (
105
) that (
99
) holds, which completes the proof.
∎

The preceding lemmas, combined with the uniform positive upper and lower bounds on the density, allow us to establish the higher-order estimates for the solution.

Proposition 3.10
.

, and assume that (

Assume further that the conditions of Proposition

. Fix the exponent

3.9
, and denote

r>N
, and there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{2}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)

(106)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{3}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)\,dt\leq C.

Furthermore, when
N=3

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}\leq C.

(107)

Proof.

Step1: Some lower-order estimates

Combining (
46

\rho

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|v(t)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C.

(108)

(where

for

and

for

.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt=

(109)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}dt.

Here (
24
), (
27
) and (
39
) implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})|^{2}dxdt

(110)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

Furthermore, for

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}dt\leq C\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C,

(111)

whereas for

we deduce

Thus, we have established that

\int^{T}_{0}\left\lVert\nabla^{2}\rho(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}dt\leq C.

(112)

Step2: Second-order bounds on the density

The continuity equation
\eqref{sys of v}_{1}
can be recast into the form

\rho_{t}-c\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\Delta\rho=-\text{div}\left(\rho v\right)+c\alpha(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}.

(113)

Multiplying by
\Delta\rho_{t}
, and integrating by parts yields

\displaystyle\frac{c\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}(\Delta\rho)^{2}dx+\int|\nabla\rho_{t}|^{2}dx

(114)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\rho_{t}(\Delta\rho)^{2}dx-\int\nabla(\text{div}(\rho v))\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}dx+c\alpha(\alpha-1)\int\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\text{div}\left(\rho v\right)(\Delta\rho)^{2}dx+\frac{c^{2}\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})(\Delta\rho)^{2}dx

\displaystyle-\int\nabla(\text{div}(\rho v))\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}dx+c\alpha(\alpha-1)\int\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2})\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}+K_{4}.

Integration by parts, combined with boundness of
\rho
, Young’s inequality and (
97
), we have

\displaystyle\left|K_{1}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{c\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}(v\cdot\nabla\rho)|\Delta\rho|^{2}dx+c\alpha(\alpha-1)\int\rho^{\alpha-1}v\cdot\nabla\Delta\rho\Delta\rho dx\right|

(115)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int|v|\,|\nabla\rho|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx+C\int|v|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|\,|\nabla^{3}\rho|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{32}\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\Bigl(|v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{6}\Bigr)\,dx,

and

\displaystyle|K_{2}|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{c^{2}\alpha^{2}(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}{2}\int\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\Delta\rho|^{2}dx+c^{2}\alpha^{2}(\alpha-1)\int\rho^{2\alpha-3}\Delta\rho\nabla\Delta\rho\cdot\nabla\rho dx\right|

(116)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{32}\|\nabla\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\Bigl(|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{6}\Bigr)\,dx.

Moreover, using Young’s inequality and boundness of the density, it directly follows that

\displaystyle|K_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int|\nabla\rho_{t}|^{2}dx+C\int|\nabla(\text{div}(\rho v))|^{2}dx

(117)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\nabla\rho_{t}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}+\left|v\right|^{2}\left|\nabla^{2}\rho\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla^{2}v\right|^{2}dx

and

\displaystyle|K_{4}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int|\nabla\rho_{t}|^{2}dx+C\int\left|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2})\right|^{2}dx
