Small Sample Machine Learning for Predicting Composition
Changes in Chemically Pretreated Kapok Fiber

Yitong Niu, Ireland LaBass, Ying Ying Tye, Tristan Smith, Sicheng Wang, Yunxiang Li, Ting Han, Cheu Peng Leh

Abstract

Kapok is a lightweight
hollow tropical fiber with potential as
a renewable feedstock, but its waxy surface and lignin-containing
matrix complicate aqueous processing. This study evaluated whether
limited-data machine learning could predict kapok fiber composition
after aqueous chemical pretreatment using only three process descriptors.
Kapok fibers were pretreated using dilute H2SO4, hydrothermal water, and NaOH across a shared window of residence
time, temperature and pH-associated pretreatment environment. A data
set of 33 pretreatment runs was constructed, with cellulose, hemicellulose
and lignin contents used as composition targets after correction with
solid yield. Single-output and wrapper-based multitarget regression
models were screened and then evaluated using leave-one-out cross-validation
to reduce dependence on a single train–test split. The LOOCV
results showed target-dependent predictability. In single-output modeling,
cellulose was the most predictable component, with GBDT giving the
strongest performance (R
2 = 0.782; RMSE
= 4.185 percentage points), whereas hemicellulose and lignin showed
weaker prediction. In wrapper-based multitarget modeling, GBDT improved
simultaneous prediction of cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin, giving R
2 values of 0.909, 0.653, and 0.672, respectively.
Feature-importance analysis indicated that temperature was the dominant
descriptor for polysaccharide-related composition changes, whereas
the pH-associated pretreatment environment contributed more strongly
to lignin-related variation. These findings position limited-data
machine learning as a useful exploratory tool for modeling pretreatment–composition
relationships in kapok fiber, while also highlighting the need for
cautious validation when sample size is small.

Introduction

Lignocellulosic fibers
are gaining increasing attention as renewable
feedstocks for biobased chemicals, fuels, and functional materials,
yet their conversion efficiency is limited by the native arrangement
of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin in the plant cell wall.
,
 Among nonwood tropical fibers, kapok is distinctive for its hollow
lumen, low density, and wax-rich cuticle, attributes that enable applications
such as absorbents and insulation but also amplify mass-transfer limitations
and enzymatic inaccessibility. A hydrophobic
surface layer, comparatively high lignin fraction, and lumen collapse
upon drying render untreated kapok highly recalcitrant to aqueous
processing.
,
 Consequently, effective valorization requires
pretreatments that disrupt or redistribute the lignin–hemicellulose
matrix while preserving sufficient cellulose for downstream use.
,
 A clearer understanding of how different pretreatment chemistries
reshape kapok’s composition is therefore essential for rational
process design.

Dilute acid, hydrothermal autohydrolysis, and
alkaline pretreatment
constitute three widely adopted aqueous approaches for reducing lignocellulosic
recalcitrance. Dilute acid conditions
catalyze hemicellulose solubilization and generate a cellulose-rich
solid with limited lignin removal. Hydrothermal
pretreatment removes hemicellulose through autohydrolytic reactions
and partially opens the fiber structure in water-based pretreatment
systems, with lignin typically redistributed rather than solubilized. Alkaline pretreatment creates a contrasting
environment in which lignin and lignin–carbohydrate linkages
are cleaved, promoting delignification and fiber swelling and enhancing
cellulose accessibility at elevated severity.
−

 Studies on
kapok and related fibers have documented structural and digestibility
changes under these regimes, yet systematic, condition-resolved comparisons
across acid, hydrothermal, and alkaline spaces remain limited.
−

Much of the available evidence is fragmented across studies
that
examine only narrow regions of time–temperature space or treat
pH in a simplified manner, making it difficult to generalize compositional
trends across pretreatment types. Severity
indices provide some unification but do not reflect the fundamentally
different chemistries operating under acidic, hydrothermal and alkaline
pretreatment environments. As a result, reported “optimal conditions”
for kapok are often protocol-specific and cannot be readily transferred
or interpolated. An integrated pretreatment–composition
landscape showing how cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin evolve
across a common time–temperature–pH-associated pretreatment
space is still lacking.

Machine learning (ML) offers a means
to extract such integrated
relationships from compact but systematically designed data sets. ML is increasingly being applied in research
fields such as chemical materials.
−

 Previous studies have
discussed lignocellulose for predicting sugar yield, digestibility,
and energy parameters, with nonlinear methods capturing interactive
effects beyond empirical correlations.
−

 However, ML studies
rarely target the chemical composition of the pretreated solid itself,
and applications to kapok and related hollow fibers are particularly
scarce. No prior work has examined dilute acid, hydrothermal, and
alkaline conditions within a unified ML framework driven by time,
temperature, and pH.

In this study, kapok fiber was used as
a model lignocellulosic
feedstock to examine how aqueous chemical pretreatment conditions
affect cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin contents. Hydrothermal,
dilute-acid and alkaline pretreatments were conducted within a shared
experimental window, and the resulting data set was used to develop
machine-learning models based on residence time, temperature and pH-associated
pretreatment environment. Single-output models were first evaluated
to determine the predictability of each composition fraction, followed
by multioutput models for simultaneous composition prediction. To
improve the reliability of model assessment under the limited data
set size, retained models were evaluated using LOOCV-based out-of-fold
prediction rather than relying only on a single train–test
split. Feature-importance analysis was further used to interpret the
relative contribution of pretreatment descriptors to the predicted
composition responses. This study provides a data-driven assessment
of pretreatment–composition relationships in kapok fiber and
clarifies the extent to which simple experimental descriptors can
support composition prediction after aqueous chemical pretreatment.
The overall experimental design and machine-learning framework are
summarized in .

Materials and Methods

Biomass Feedstock

Kapok (Ceiba pentandra) fiber was sourced from Kelantan,
Malaysia. Fibers were manually separated from the seedpods, gently
shaken to remove adhering pith and loose debris, and visually cleaned
without any chemical purification. A composite batch was prepared
by pooling and mixing multiple subsamples to ensure representativeness,
after which the material was stored in sealed polyethylene bags at
room temperature in the dark. All masses and compositional values
reported in this study refer to the dry basis unless otherwise specified.

Chemical Pretreatment

Kapok fibers
were subjected to three aqueous chemical pretreatment routes: hydrothermal,
dilute-acid and alkaline pretreatment. All pretreatments were conducted
in a laboratory-scale stainless-steel digester equipped with microprocessor-controlled
heating and an internal thermocouple. The solid-to-liquor ratio was
maintained at 1:12. Hydrothermal pretreatment
was conducted with deionized water, whereas dilute-acid and alkaline
pretreatments were conducted using 1–2% H2SO4 and 1–3% NaOH, respectively. These routes generated three pH-associated pretreatment environments,
corresponding to strongly acidic dilute-acid pretreatment, hydrothermal
water with a mildly alkaline measured pH, and strongly alkaline pretreatment.

Across the three pretreatment routes, the target temperature ranged
from 100 to 180 °C and the residence time ranged from 45 to 90
min. Residence time was counted from the point at which the digester
reached the target temperature. After pretreatment, the digester was
rapidly cooled, and the slurry was discharged. The pretreated solids
were recovered by filtration, washed thoroughly with deionized water
until approximately neutral pH, and oven-dried to constant mass before
chemical composition analysis.

Chemical Composition Analysis

Untreated
and pretreated kapok fibers were air-dried, gently milled and passed
through a 2.0 mm sieve prior to analysis. For the untreated material,
extractives were removed following TAPPI T 204 (ethanol–toluene),
whereas pretreated solids were treated as effectively extractives-free
owing to prior liquor contact and subsequent water washing. Acid-insoluble
(Klason) lignin was determined according to TAPPI (T222 om-02), and acid-soluble lignin was quantified spectrophotometrically
following TAPPI UM 250. Holocellulose
was determined using the sodium chlorite method, and α-, β-
and γ-cellulose fractions were determined using the graded-alkali
procedure described in JIS 8101. In this study, cellulose was defined
as α-cellulose, hemicellulose was calculated as the sum of β-
and γ-cellulose, and lignin was calculated as the sum of acid-insoluble
and acid-soluble lignin.

Solid yield was calculated from the
oven-dried mass before and after pretreatment according to . Cellulose and hemicellulose
contents in the recovered solid were calculated from their fractions
in holocellulose according to . The final component contents used for modeling were then
obtained by combining the measured composition of the recovered solid
with the corresponding solid yield according to . This calculation was applied consistently
to cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin. The resulting values therefore
represent solid-yield-corrected component contents expressed relative
to the initial oven-dried biomass rather than the composition of the
recovered solids.

Data Set Construction and
Exploratory Analysis

The modeling data set comprised 33 pretreatment
runs. The liquor
pH used as an input descriptor was measured from the prepared pretreatment
liquor before each pretreatment run at room temperature. This pH value
therefore represented the initial liquor condition associated with
each pretreatment route and was interpreted as a route-associated
descriptor rather than as an isolated continuous mechanistic variable.
Before modeling, the compiled data set was checked for missing entries,
inconsistent units and transcription errors, and all 33 records were
retained. The complete data set is provided as Supporting Information Data File S1.

Machine-Learning
Modeling and Validation

As shown in , the machine-learning workflow was organized
into preliminary algorithm
screening followed by LOOCV-based validation of the retained models.
In the preliminary screening stage, eight regression algorithms were
evaluated for single-output prediction of cellulose, hemicellulose
and lignin, including multiple linear regression, ridge regression,
decision tree, random forest, gradient boosting decision tree, support
vector regression, multilayer perceptron and Gaussian process regression.
−

 These algorithms were selected to cover linear baselines, tree-based
models, ensemble learners, kernel-based regression, neural-network
regression and probabilistic regression. A common 80/20 train–test
split was used at this stage only to identify candidate algorithms
for each target variable, and the complete screening results are reported
in the Supporting Information.

The retained single-output models were subsequently evaluated using
leave-one-out cross-validation. In each LOOCV iteration, one sample
was held out as the validation sample, while the remaining 32 samples
were used for model training. For models requiring hyperparameter
optimization, parameter tuning was performed within the training portion
of each LOOCV iteration using inner 3-fold cross-validation. The held-out
sample in the outer LOOCV loop was therefore not used for hyperparameter
selection. Final model performance was calculated from the aggregated
out-of-fold predictions obtained across all 33 LOOCV iterations.

Wrapper-based multitarget modeling was also conducted to predict
cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin simultaneously from the same pretreatment
descriptors. Random forest, decision tree, gradient boosting decision
tree and multilayer perceptron were evaluated as candidate models
within a wrapper-based multitarget regression framework. In this implementation,
one target-specific estimator was fitted for each composition output
using the same input descriptors and validation procedure. Therefore,
the approach was interpreted as simultaneous multitarget prediction
rather than as explicit modeling of correlations among cellulose,
hemicellulose and lignin. The retained wrapper-based multitarget models
were evaluated using the same LOOCV-based validation procedure. Model
performance was quantified using the coefficient of determination
(R
2), root mean squared error (RMSE),
mean absolute error (MAE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE).
RMSE and MAE were expressed in percentage points, whereas MAPE was
expressed as a percentage.

All machine-learning analyses were
performed in Python 3.13.5 using
scikit-learn version 1.8.0 for model implementation, cross-validation,
hyperparameter tuning and performance evaluation. NumPy version 2.3.5
and pandas version 2.2.3 were used for numerical processing and data
handling. The main model classes included LinearRegression, Ridge,
DecisionTreeRegressor, RandomForestRegressor, GradientBoostingRegressor,
SVR, MLPRegressor and GaussianProcessRegressor. Wrapper-based multitarget
RF, DT, GBDT, and MLP models were implemented using MultiOutputRegressor,
which fits one estimator per output target. Random states were fixed
at 42 for stochastic algorithms where applicable to improve reproducibility.
The searched hyperparameter ranges and selected configurations of
the retained models are provided in the Supporting Information.

Feature-Importance Analysis

Permutation
importance was calculated for the retained models after LOOCV validation.
Each input descriptor was randomly permuted independently, and the
resulting increase in prediction error was used as its importance
value. The permutation procedure was repeated 100 times for each descriptor.
RMSE was used as the error function for calculating the increase in
prediction error after permutation. For single-output models, permutation
importance was calculated separately for cellulose, hemicellulose
and lignin. For wrapper-based multitarget models, permutation importance
was calculated from the normalized prediction error across the three
outputs. The final values were normalized within each model so that
the summed importance of residence time, temperature, and pH-associated
pretreatment environment equaled one, and were reported as mean ±
SD across permutation repeats.

Results
and Discussion

Data Set Overview and Exploratory
Composition
Patterns

The compiled data set contained 33 pretreatment
condition combinations covering residence times of 45–90 min
and temperatures of 100–180 °C. The pH values were distributed
into three route-associated regimes, corresponding to acid pretreatment
with 1–2% H2SO4 (pH 0.39–0.69),
hydrothermal pretreatment (pH ≈ 8.40) and alkaline pretreatment
with 1–3% NaOH (pH 13.40–13.88). Across this experimental
space, the solid-yield-corrected cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin
contents on an initial dry biomass basis ranged from 13.99–48.78%,
5.22–36.94%, and 4.70–18.90%, respectively.

As
shown in (a),
temperature showed the clearest association with the composition variables
among the three pretreatment descriptors. Cellulose and hemicellulose
were negatively associated with temperature, indicating that stronger
thermal exposure was linked to greater polysaccharide loss or redistribution.
Time showed the same general direction but with weaker correlations,
suggesting that residence time provided less linear explanatory information
within the tested range. In contrast, pH showed weak global linear
correlations with the individual composition variables when all samples
were pooled. This result does not necessarily indicate a negligible
chemical effect of pH, but rather reflects that pH in this data set
represented route-associated pretreatment environments instead of
a continuous independent gradient. Therefore, pH-related effects were
expected to appear through route-specific and interaction-dependent
responses rather than through a single global linear trend.

The composition distributions
in (b–d)
further show that cellulose,
hemicellulose and lignin responded differently across the time–temperature–pH
space. Cellulose-rich samples were mainly observed under moderate
thermal conditions, whereas higher temperatures, especially under
hydrothermal conditions, were associated with marked cellulose reduction.
,
 Hemicellulose showed broader sensitivity to the combined effects
of temperature, time and pH-associated pretreatment environment, consistent
with its lower thermal stability and greater susceptibility to solubilization
during aqueous pretreatment.
−

 Lignin varied over a narrower
numerical range but displayed a clearer dependence on pretreatment
route, with lower lignin contents mainly observed under alkaline conditions
at elevated temperature, consistent with alkaline cleavage of lignin–carbohydrate
linkages and lignin solubilization.
,
 Localized
higher lignin values under severe acid or hydrothermal conditions
may also reflect lignin condensation, redeposition or pseudolignin
formation.
,
 These exploratory patterns indicate
that the final composition was not governed by a single monotonic
severity axis, but by coupled thermal and route-associated chemical
effects, supporting the use of machine-learning models to capture
nonlinear and interaction-dependent relationships between pretreatment
descriptors and fiber composition.

Predictive
Performance of Single-Output Machine
Learning Models on Fiber Composition

An initial screening
was conducted using eight single-output algorithms to identify candidate
models for each composition target. The complete train–test
screening results are provided in Table S1, and the corresponding graphical comparison is shown in Figure S1. The screening results showed that
cellulose was generally more responsive to nonlinear learning than
to linear baselines, with GBDT and GPR giving lower test errors than
Linear and Ridge models. For hemicellulose, the model ranking was
less stable, although MLP gave the strongest test-set performance
in the initial screening. For lignin, DT gave the lowest test error
among the screened models. Therefore, Ridge, GBDT, and GPR were retained
for cellulose, Ridge and MLP were retained for hemicellulose, and
DT was retained for lignin. These screened models were then re-evaluated
using LOOCV-based out-of-fold prediction to reduce the dependence
of performance assessment on a single train–test partition.

After the initial screening, the retained models were further evaluated
using LOOCV out-of-fold prediction. This step was used as the main
performance assessment because each sample served once as an independent
validation point, which reduced the sensitivity of the results to
a single train–test split. For models requiring hyperparameter
selection, tuning was performed within each LOOCV training fold using
inner 3-fold cross-validation, with RMSE as the optimization criterion.
The searched hyperparameter ranges and selected configurations are
summarized in Table S2, including the fold-wise
selection frequencies for GBDT, GPR, Ridge and MLP. The final LOOCV
metrics calculated from the out-of-fold predictions are reported in .

As shown in , cellulose showed the strongest
LOOCV predictability among the three
composition targets. Ridge gave an LOOCV R
2 of 0.427, with RMSE, MAE and MAPE values of 6.787 percentage points,
5.351 percentage points and 19.09%, respectively, indicating that
the linear baseline captured only part of the variation in cellulose
content. This result suggests that the relationship between pretreatment
conditions and cellulose composition was not fully additive across
time, temperature and pH-associated pretreatment environment, which
is consistent with the expected nonlinear and interaction-dependent
nature of lignocellulosic pretreatment responses.
,
 Introducing nonlinear models improved the prediction, with GBDT
giving the best cellulose performance, as reflected by an R
2 of 0.782, RMSE of 4.185 percentage points
and MAPE of 10.76%. GPR also improved over Ridge, giving an R
2 of 0.564 and MAPE of 15.15%, but it remained
less accurate than GBDT. a shows that GBDT provided closer agreement between
measured and LOOCV-predicted cellulose values compared with Ridge
and GPR. This improvement indicates that the cellulose response involved
nonlinear curvature and variable interactions that the linear baseline
could not represent. Nevertheless, the
comparison between the LOOCV results and the stronger apparent performance
observed during the initial train–test screening indicates
that model flexibility should be interpreted cautiously under the
limited and clustered experimental design.

For hemicellulose, the LOOCV results indicated
weaker and less
stable predictability than cellulose. Ridge gave an R
2 of 0.122, with RMSE, MAE and MAPE values of 6.738 percentage
points, 5.141 percentage points and 41.46%, respectively, showing
that the three macroscopic descriptors captured only a limited fraction
of the hemicellulose response. MLP did not improve the prediction
after LOOCV-based evaluation and hyperparameter tuning, giving a lower R
2 of 0.026 and a higher MAPE of 46.73%. This
result differs from the initial train–test screening, where
MLP appeared more favorable, and indicates that its apparent advantage
was not retained under the more conservative validation scheme. The
scatter distribution in (b) also shows a compressed predicted range relative to the
measured hemicellulose values, which is consistent with weak target-specific
signal recovery from time, temperature and pH-associated pretreatment
environment alone. Such limited predictability is reasonable because
hemicellulose removal is affected not only by bulk pretreatment conditions
but also by localized fiber accessibility, diffusion constraints,
xylan substitution patterns and structural heterogeneity that were
not explicitly included in the input descriptors.
,

For lignin, the selected DT configuration produced a weaker
but
still positive LOOCV performance compared with cellulose. The model
gave an R
2 of 0.216, with RMSE, MAE and
MAPE values of 2.990 percentage points, 2.365 percentage points and
19.88%, respectively. This result indicates that the selected pretreatment
descriptors captured part of the residual lignin variation, but the
predictive structure was less reproducible than that observed for
cellulose. (c) shows that several lignin predictions still deviated substantially
from the measured values, suggesting that the DT model mainly recovered
broad composition trends rather than precise pointwise lignin responses.
This limited predictability is consistent with the complex behavior
of lignin during pretreatment, where delignification can occur together
with lignin redeposition, pseudolignin formation and condensation
reactions, depending on local mass transfer, lignin–carbohydrate
associations and liquor chemistry.
−

 Therefore, the lignin model should
be interpreted as a qualitative indicator of residual-lignin tendency
within the explored pretreatment space rather than as a reliable optimization
model for lignin content alone.

Wrapper-Based Multitarget Prediction of Fiber
Composition

Wrapper-based multitarget modeling was further
conducted to predict cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin contents
simultaneously from the same pretreatment descriptors. This analysis
was used to compare the three composition targets under a consistent
modeling and validation framework. Because the wrapper-based implementation
fits one estimator per output target, the approach was interpreted
as simultaneous multitarget prediction rather than explicit learning
of correlations among cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin.

RF,
DT, GBDT, and MLP were first evaluated as candidate wrapper-based
multitarget models to identify suitable algorithms for simultaneous
composition prediction. The complete screening results are provided
in Table S3, and the corresponding graphical
comparison is shown in Figure S2. Based
on the screening results, RF and GBDT were retained for subsequent
LOOCV validation. RF was retained as a stable ensemble baseline, whereas
GBDT was retained because of its stronger nonlinear learning capacity
and favorable prediction performance across the three composition
outputs. The hyperparameter settings of the retained models are summarized
in Table S4.

reports
the LOOCV performance of the retained wrapper-based multitarget models.
Because cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin differed in content range
and response behavior, the prediction metrics were reported separately
for each output rather than averaged across outputs. RF produced positive R
2 values for all three outputs, with the strongest
performance observed for cellulose (R
2 = 0.690; RMSE = 4.994 percentage points; MAPE = 13.47%). Its performance
was weaker for hemicellulose and lignin, with R
2 values of 0.117 and 0.112, respectively, indicating stable
but conservative simultaneous prediction. GBDT showed stronger LOOCV
performance across the three outputs, with R
2 values of 0.909, 0.653, and 0.672 for cellulose, hemicellulose
and lignin, respectively. The corresponding MAPE values were 6.38%,
21.37% and 11.67%, showing that GBDT provided more accurate wrapper-based
simultaneous prediction than RF across the three composition targets.

a visual comparison between the measured and LOOCV-predicted values
for the retained wrapper-based multitarget models. For RF, the cellulose
predictions showed better agreement with the measured values than
the hemicellulose and lignin predictions, consistent with the higher R
2 and lower MAPE reported for cellulose in . The hemicellulose
predictions showed greater dispersion, indicating that RF only partially
captured the variation in hemicellulose content under simultaneous
prediction. For GBDT, the predicted values were more closely aligned
with the measured values across all three outputs, especially for
cellulose and lignin. The reduced dispersion in d–f supports the quantitative results
in and indicates
that GBDT provided closer agreement between measured and predicted
values under the wrapper-based multitarget framework.

The comparison between
RF and GBDT indicates that multioutput prediction
benefited from boosting-based nonlinear learning. Although RF provided
a stable ensemble baseline, its weaker performance for hemicellulose
and lignin suggests that bagging-based averaging may smooth part of
the target-specific variation in the composition-response patterns.
In contrast, GBDT showed stronger prediction across all three outputs,
indicating that the boosting-based estimator was more effective for
the target-specific nonlinear relationships between pretreatment descriptors
and fiber composition. Similar observations have been reported in
other data-driven decision-support contexts, where nonlinear intelligent-computing
approaches improved the modeling of systems governed by interacting
operational variables. Cellulose remained
the most predictable target, whereas hemicellulose was still associated
with relatively higher error, reflecting its sensitivity to route-dependent
solubilization and structural heterogeneity. The improved lignin prediction
obtained by GBDT further suggests that the model better represented
residual-lignin patterns within the explored pretreatment space. Therefore,
GBDT was selected as the preferred wrapper-based multitarget model
for simultaneous composition prediction, while RF was retained as
a comparative ensemble baseline.

Feature-Importance
Analysis of Pretreatment
Descriptors

Feature importance was evaluated using LOOCV-derived
permutation importance to interpret how the retained models used time,
temperature and pH-associated pretreatment environment for composition
prediction. The importance values were normalized within each model
so that the three descriptor contributions summed to one, allowing
comparison among retained single-output and multioutput models. Because
pH values were clustered by pretreatment route, pH-related importance
was interpreted as the contribution of the pH-associated pretreatment
environment rather than as an isolated continuous pH effect.

As shown in (a–c) and Table S5, the retained
single-output models showed target-dependent feature-importance patterns.
For cellulose, temperature was the dominant descriptor across all
retained models, with normalized importance values of 1.000 in Ridge,
0.839 in GBDT and 0.852 in GPR. The pH-associated pretreatment environment
showed a secondary contribution in the nonlinear cellulose models,
whereas time contributed little. This pattern indicates that cellulose
enrichment within the explored pretreatment space was mainly associated
with thermal exposure and route-dependent chemical modification. For hemicellulose, temperature also remained
the leading descriptor, with importance values of 0.761 in Ridge and
0.627 in MLP, although time and the pH-associated pretreatment environment
contributed more clearly than in cellulose prediction. This broader
distribution is consistent with hemicellulose being more sensitive
to solubilization, depolymerization and route-specific reactions during
aqueous pretreatment.
,
 In contrast, lignin prediction
showed a different hierarchy, with DT assigning the highest importance
to the pH-associated pretreatment environment (0.613), followed by
temperature (0.371) and time (0.015). This result agrees with the
chemical-route dependence of lignin removal and redistribution during
pretreatment, including delignification, condensation and lignin redeposition
under different pretreatment environments.
−

The retained multioutput models
showed a more integrated feature-importance
pattern for simultaneous composition prediction. As shown in (d) and Table S5, temperature remained the dominant shared
descriptor in both RF and GBDT, with normalized importance values
of 0.768 and 0.707, respectively. The pH-associated pretreatment environment
was consistently the second most important descriptor, contributing
0.232 in RF and 0.268 in GBDT, whereas time contributed little to
the simultaneous prediction. This result indicates that, when cellulose,
hemicellulose and lignin were predicted simultaneously under the wrapper-based
framework, the models relied mainly on thermal exposure and route-level
chemical environment rather than residence time alone. The dominance
of temperature in the multioutput models is consistent with the broad
influence of thermal severity on biomass fractionation, carbohydrate
solubilization and fiber-structure modification during aqueous pretreatment. Meanwhile, the secondary but consistent contribution
of the pH-associated pretreatment environment reflects the route-dependent
nature of compositional redistribution, especially for lignin-related
changes under acid, hydrothermal and alkaline conditions.

Overall,
the feature-importance results indicate that the composition
models mainly extracted predictive information from temperature and
the pH-associated pretreatment environment, but their relative roles
differed among composition targets. Temperature was more influential
for cellulose and hemicellulose prediction, suggesting that polysaccharide-related
composition changes were primarily associated with thermal exposure
and thermally driven fractionation within the investigated pretreatment
range. By contrast, the stronger contribution of the pH-associated
pretreatment environment to lignin prediction indicates that lignin-related
variation was more closely linked to route-dependent chemistry than
to residence time alone.
,
 The low importance
of time should not be interpreted as evidence that residence time
is chemically irrelevant, because permutation importance reflects
predictive dependence within the current data set rather than an independent
causal effect. Within the present experimental design, residence time
provided less additional predictive information than temperature and
the route-associated chemical environment, probably because the composition
responses were more strongly structured by thermal and chemical-route
contrasts.

Conclusion

This
study evaluated limited-data machine learning for predicting
the cellulose, hemicellulose and lignin contents of kapok fiber after
aqueous chemical pretreatment. A data set of 33 pretreatment runs
was constructed from hydrothermal, dilute-acid and alkaline pretreatments
using residence time, temperature and pH-associated pretreatment environment
as input descriptors. To address the limited sample size, model assessment
was based on LOOCV out-of-fold prediction after preliminary algorithm
screening. The results showed target-dependent predictability. In
the single-output models, cellulose was the most predictable component,
with GBDT giving the best LOOCV performance, whereas hemicellulose
and lignin showed weaker prediction from the same three descriptors.
In the wrapper-based multitarget setting, GBDT improved simultaneous
prediction across the three composition targets, indicating that target-specific
composition responses could be predicted more accurately using boosting-based
nonlinear estimators than using the other retained models.

Feature-importance
analysis further showed that the models relied
mainly on temperature and the pH-associated pretreatment environment,
but their relative contributions differed among composition targets.
Temperature was the dominant descriptor for cellulose and hemicellulose
prediction, supporting the importance of thermal exposure in polysaccharide-related
composition changes. By contrast, lignin prediction was more strongly
associated with the pH-associated pretreatment environment, reflecting
the route-dependent nature of lignin removal, redistribution and secondary
reactions under acid, hydrothermal and alkaline conditions. Residence
time showed lower importance within the present experimental range,
indicating that it provided less additional predictive information
than temperature and route-associated chemical environment in this
data set.

The main limitation of this study is the compact data
set size
and the use of only three macroscopic pretreatment descriptors. Therefore,
the models should be interpreted as limited-data predictive and exploratory
tools rather than as general-purpose optimization models for kapok
pretreatment. The clustered pH distribution also means that pH-related
importance reflects route-associated pretreatment environment rather
than an isolated continuous pH effect. Future work should enlarge
the experimental matrix, include independent external validation,
and incorporate additional descriptors such as solid yield, liquor
composition, severity-related indices, fiber morphology, crystallinity
and lignin-structure information. Linking these enriched descriptors
to downstream responses, such as enzymatic digestibility, sugar release
or material performance, would strengthen the use of data-driven modeling
for sustainable kapok fiber valorization.
